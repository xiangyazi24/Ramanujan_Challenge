# Audit of the fixed-row p-adic carrier

## Verdict

The proposed carrier

\[
D_n=B_n-5S_K(n)
\]

has the universal factor \(p\) for every prime \(3n/4<p\le n\), but the target condition \(p\mid B_u\) does not force a second factor \(p\). The first jet contains an independent local correction term coming from the head block, so target-selectivity fails in general.

## First-order expansion

Write

\[
T_k(n)=\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]

For \(p=n-u\), the head terms satisfy a first-order lifting

\[
T_k(p+u)\equiv T_k(u)\left(1+pL_k(u)\right)\pmod {p^2},
\]

for \(k<p\), where

\[
L_k(u)=2\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac1{u-j}+\frac1{u+j+1}\right)
\]

with the usual interpretation that denominators are reduced modulo \(p\) and only unit terms occur. Summing the head gives

\[
S_K(p+u)-B_u
\equiv
p\sum_{k\le K}T_k(u)L_k(u)\pmod {p^2}.
\]

The middle range contributes multiples of \(p^2\) by hypothesis. The tail is the reflected copy and gives the known zeroth-order term \(5B_u\), but its first jet is another independent harmonic correction.

Hence

\[
\frac{D_n}{p}\equiv
5\frac{B_u}{p}+\mathcal H_u(n)
\pmod p,
\]

where \(\mathcal H_u(n)\) is the explicit finite harmonic-binomial sum obtained from the head and tail first jets. It is not divisible by \(B_u\) as an integer identity.

Therefore

\[
p\mid B_u
\]

only removes the first term; it leaves \(\mathcal H_u(n)\), which is generally nonzero modulo \(p\).

## Consequence

The implication

\[
p\mid B_u\Longrightarrow p^2\mid D_n
\]

is false. The failure is not a valuation accident: it is a genuine support issue. The universal congruence records the mod-p digit lift, while the second digit depends on a new deformation parameter.

## Continuant form

The primitive continuant identity

\[
N_p(u)\equiv-B_u^2\pmod p
\]

is only a zeroth-order identity. Lifting it gives

\[
N_p(u)+B_u^2=p\,M_p(u)
\]

with \(M_p(u)\) containing the same type of harmonic deformation terms. There is no factorization

\[
M_p(u)=B_u\cdot Q_p(u)
\]

forced by the recurrence or Casoratian.

## Common-gcd/Wronskian check

The Wronskian

\[
H[n]B[n-1]-n^3H[n-1]B[n]=((n-1)!)^3
\]

implies that a prime dividing both \(B_n\) and \(H_n\) must divide the factorial side. For \(p>3n/4\), this side has exactly one forced power only when the relevant factorial contains \(p\); it does not create an additional selective support condition. The localized ideal generated by \(B_n,H_n\) therefore has no extra generator cutting out the roots of \(B_u\) at the next p-adic digit.

## Additional block carriers

Any linear combination of head/tail/middle blocks can manufacture a universal factor \(p^a\) by cancellation, but the next quotient digit again contains independent harmonic sums. A second exact target detector would require a new identity forcing the first jet into the ideal generated by \(B_u\), which is absent from the recurrence.

## Narrow residual

The actual remaining Apéry statement is not a universal p-adic carrier theorem. It is the weaker support problem:

> For \(p>3n/4\), among primes satisfying \(p\mid B_{n-p}\), prove that the first-jet obstruction \(\mathcal H_{n-p}(n)\) is nonzero for almost all candidates, or otherwise bound the simultaneous vanishing of the pair \((B_u,\mathcal H_u(n))\).

This is a genuine two-condition arithmetic problem. Finite verification can check the first-jet formula and search for exceptions, but it cannot replace a proof of support saving.
