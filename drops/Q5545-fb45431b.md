# The top-half pair energy: exact collision algebra, a new degenerate-pair bound, and the pure-cross obstruction

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
B_m=\sum_{k=0}^m\binom mk^2\binom{m+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:B_r\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]

and

\[
T_n=\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n,\ n-p\in Z_p\},
\qquad K(n)=|T_n|.
\]

The ordered top-half pair energy on a dyadic shell is

\[
\mathcal E_1(N)=\sum_{N<n\le2N}(K(n))_2.
\]

I do **not** obtain a fixed power saving

\[
\mathcal E_1(N)\ll N^{2-\delta}.
\]

The hostile audit does, however, produce two rigorous advances and a sharper description of the missing input.

1. **Strongest unconditional full-energy bound from the present vertical input.** From `|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}` and prime counting,
   \[
   \boxed{\mathcal E_1(N)\ll \frac{N^{8/3}}{\log^2N}.}
   \]
   Reflection and the no-adjacent-zero theorem do not improve this exponent.

2. **A genuine fixed-gap theorem.** For a collision with prime gap `h`, call it *degenerate* when either prime also divides the Apéry value belonging to the other prime. For every fixed `n,h`,
   \[
   \boxed{M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll h.}
   \]
   The proof uses the single nonzero integer
   \[
   N_h(n-h)N_h(n),
   \qquad
   \log|N_h(n-h)N_h(n)|\ll h\log n,
   \]
   where `N_h` is the Apéry continuant. Thus the hoped-for low-height mechanism really exists for the pairs that become two zeros in one characteristic.

3. **The pairs left over are exactly the different-characteristic ones.** Writing a collision as
   \[
   p+(s+h)=(p+h)+s=n,
   \]
   the target conditions are
   \[
   p\mid B_{s+h},\qquad p+h\mid B_s.
   \]
   A *pure-cross* pair additionally satisfies
   \[
   p\nmid B_s,\qquad p+h\nmid B_{s+h}.
   \]
   For such a pair the continuant `N_h(s)` is a unit modulo **both** primes. The transfer equation leaves two uncontrolled quotient residues
   \[
   B_{s+h}/p\pmod{p+h},
   \qquad
   B_s/(p+h)\pmod p.
   \]
   There is no same-prime root to count.

4. **Every natural `p(p+h)` certificate saturates.** The low-height Casoratian has logarithmic height `O(h\log n)` but is not divisible by either moving prime. Cross-weighting it to force both primes introduces an exponential term of size `B_sB_{s+h}`. Central-binomial or other factorial carriers either retain the whole positive-density candidate primorial and have linear height, or cancel their bulk Kummer valuations together with their linear height. Generic Newton--Padé, Smith, Fitting, resultant, and modular-Siegel constructions reproduce the same saturation.

5. **The exact remaining lemma can be made strictly weaker than HC2.** Sort the hits at one `n`, retain only adjacent hit primes, and retain only pure-cross gaps at most `A\log N`. It is enough to prove
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sup_{N<n\le2N}A_{A\log N}^{\times}(n)
      =o_A\!\left(\frac N{\log N}\right)
   }
   \tag{PCLG}
   \]
   for every fixed `A>0`. Here `A_H^\times(n)` counts adjacent pure-cross hit pairs with prime gap at most `H`. This is strictly weaker than HC2: it ignores all nonadjacent pairs, all large gaps, and all degenerate pairs. Nevertheless it rules out a linear-weight top-half spike.

The precise missing arithmetic input is therefore not another same-prime continuant identity. It is a horizontal theorem saying that the distinguished Apéry initial state cannot support a near-prime-gap chain of pure-cross zeros in different residue characteristics.

## 2. Exact collision coordinates

Take two distinct hits `p,\ell\in T_n`, and order them as `p<\ell`. Put

\[
h=\ell-p>0,
\qquad
s=n-\ell,
\qquad
r=n-p=s+h.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\ell=p+h,
\quad
n=p+s+h=\ell+s,
\quad
p\mid B_{s+h},
\quad
\ell\mid B_s.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The top-half inequalities give

\[
0\le s<s+h<p<\ell,
\tag{2.2}
\]

so every recurrence coefficient and factorial occurring between `s` and `s+h` is a unit modulo both primes. For large `N`, both primes are odd, hence `h` is even.

Let `M_h(n)` be the number of **unordered** pairs `p<\ell` satisfying (2.1). Then

\[
(K(n))_2=2\sum_{h\ge1}M_h(n),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_1(N)
 =2\sum_{h\ge1}\sum_{N<n\le2N}M_h(n).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Equivalently, for fixed `h`,

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}M_h(n)
 =\sum_{\substack{p,\ p+h\ {m prime}}}
   \#\left\{
   \begin{array}{l}
   s:\ s\in Z_{p+h},\ s+h\in Z_p,\\
   N<p+s+h\le2N,\ s+h<p
   \end{array}
   \right\}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

This formula exposes the quantifiers. A same-prime theorem can control multiplicity in the shifted intersection

\[
Z_{p+h}\cap(Z_p-h),
\]

but the energy also contains the singleton event that this intersection is merely nonempty. There are `N^{2-o(1)}` possible prime pairs, so controlling only multiplicities cannot yield a fixed power saving.

## 3. Strongest unconditional bounds from the current inputs

### 3.1 Incidence times maximum degree

Let

\[
\mathcal I(N)=\sum_{N<n\le2N}K(n).
\]

Each hit has a unique representation `n=p+r` with `r\in Z_p` and `p>r`, so the proved vertical estimate gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I(N)
&\le \sum_{N/2<p\le2N}|Z_p|\\
&\ll \sum_{N/2<p\le2N}p^{2/3}
 \ll \frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Also

\[
K(n)\le \pi(n)-\pi(n/2)\ll \frac N{\log N}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_1(N)
 \le \bigl(\max K(n)\bigr)\mathcal I(N)
 \ll \frac{N^{8/3}}{\log^2N}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is the strongest full-shell exponent obtained from the vertical zero count by direct counting. Reflection changes constants and parity; no-adjacent zeros remove gap one inside one `Z_p`; neither changes `8/3`.

### 3.2 Fixed-gap prime-pair sieve

For a fixed even `h`, (2.4) and `|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}` give

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}M_h(n)
 \ll N^{2/3}
 \#\{N/2<p\le2N:p,p+h\text{ prime}\}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The Selberg upper-bound sieve yields

\[
\#\{p\asymp N:p,p+h\text{ prime}\}
 \ll \mathfrak S(h)\frac N{\log^2N},
\tag{3.5}
\]

where `\mathfrak S(h)=0` for odd `h` in the large-prime range and

\[
\sum_{h\le H}\mathfrak S(h)\ll H.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Hence the energy from gaps at most `H` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{1,\le H}(N)
 :=2\sum_{h\le H}\sum_{N<n\le2N}M_h(n)
 \ll \frac{H N^{5/3}}{\log^2N}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

In particular, for every fixed `\eta>0`,

\[
H=N^{1/3-\eta}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E_{1,\le H}(N)\ll N^{2-\eta+o(1)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

This is a genuine power saving for the **truncated** energy. It does not prove a pointwise theorem: one exceptional diagonal with `K\asymp N/\log N` can have only `O(K)` adjacent gaps below `H`, far below the allowance in (3.7).

Summing (3.7) to `H\asymp N` reproduces (3.3).

### 3.3 The pointwise adjacent-gap split reaches exactly the critical scale

Order the hits

p_1<p_2<\cdots<p_K.

Their total span is less than `n/2`. If `g_i=p_{i+1}-p_i`, then

\[
K\le1+\frac n{2H}

## 4. The Apéry continuant and a new `O(h)` theorem

Put

\[
P(x)=34x^3+51x^2+27x+5,
\]

and define the cleared continuants

\[
N_0(x)=0,
\qquad N_1(x)=1,
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For any Apéry solution, the exact transfer formula is

\[
Q_h(s)B_{s+h}
 =N_h(s)B_{s+1}
  -(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)B_s,
\tag{4.2}
\]

where

\[
Q_h(s)=\prod_{j=2}^h(s+j)^3
\quad(Q_1=1).
\]

### 4.1 Degenerate versus pure-cross collisions

For a collision (2.1), call it **degenerate** if

\[
p\mid B_s
\quad\text{or}\quad
\ell\mid B_{s+h}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

It is **pure cross** if

\[
\boxed{
p\nmid B_s,\qquad \ell\nmid B_{s+h}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The terminology is exact: in (4.3), one characteristic sees zeros at both endpoints; in (4.4), each characteristic sees only its own endpoint.

### 4.2 Proposition: degenerate pairs are `O(h)` for fixed `n,h`

Let `M_h^{\rm deg}(n)` count degenerate gap-`h` pairs at `n`. Then

\[
\boxed{M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll h.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

Suppose first that `p\mid B_s` as well as the target condition `p\mid B_{s+h}`. The no-consecutive-zero theorem gives `p\nmid B_{s+1}`. Every factor in `Q_h(s)` is a unit modulo `p`, so (4.2) yields

\[
p\mid N_h(s).
\]

Since

\[
s=n-h-p\equiv n-h\pmod p,
\]

we obtain

\[
p\mid N_h(n-h).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Similarly, if `\ell\mid B_{s+h}` in addition to `\ell\mid B_s`, then

\[
\ell\mid N_h(s),
\qquad
s=n-\ell\equiv n\pmod\ell,
\]

and hence

\[
\ell\mid N_h(n).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The tridiagonal determinant representation of `N_h(x)` has order `h-1`, diagonal entries `P(x+j)`, and off-diagonal entries of size `(x+j)^3`. For `x=n-h` or `x=n`, with `0<h<n/2`, Hadamard gives

\[
0<N_h(x)\le(Cn^3)^{h-1}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Positivity follows also from the exact Apéry Casoratian formula in `Q338.md`. Therefore

\[
\omega_{>n/2}(N_h(x))
 \le \frac{\log N_h(x)}{\log(n/2)}
 \ll h.
\tag{4.9}
\]

For fixed `n,h`, a prime `p` determines `s=n-h-p` and hence determines the pair. Equations (4.6)--(4.9) prove (4.5). ∎

Thus the explicit low-height integer

\[
\boxed{D^{\rm deg}_{n,h}=N_h(n-h)N_h(n)}
\tag{4.10}
\]

captures every prime that turns a collision into a same-characteristic two-zero event, and

\[
\log D^{\rm deg}_{n,h}\ll h\log n.
\tag{4.11}
\]

This is precisely the hoped-for height scale, but it captures only the degenerate part.

Summing (4.5),

\[
\sum_{h\le H}M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll H^2.
\tag{4.12}
\]

For logarithmic `H`, this is negligible compared with `n/\log n`.

## 5. Exact quotient obstruction for pure-cross pairs

Assume now (4.4). Write

\[
B_{s+h}=pu,
\qquad
B_s=\ell v,
\qquad \ell=p+h,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and abbreviate

\[
A=N_h(s),
\qquad
V=(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1),
\qquad
Q=Q_h(s).
\]

Equation (4.2) becomes

\[
\boxed{A B_{s+1}=pQu+\ell Vv.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Reducing in the two different characteristics gives

\[
A B_{s+1}\equiv hVv\pmod p,
\tag{5.3}
\]

\[
A B_{s+1}\equiv-hQu\pmod\ell.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The opposite quotients are units:

\[
v\not\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad
u\not\equiv0\pmod\ell,
\tag{5.5}
\]

where the second symbol is `u` in (5.1).

### 5.1 The continuant is a unit modulo both primes

One has

\[
\boxed{p\nmid N_h(s),\qquad \ell\nmid N_h(s).}
\tag{5.6}
\]

For `\ell`, if `\ell\mid A`, then (4.2), `\ell\mid B_s`, and the fact that `Q` is an `\ell`-unit imply `\ell\mid B_{s+h}`, contradicting pure-crossness.

For `p`, suppose `p\mid A`. Since `p\mid B_{s+h}` and `p\nmid B_s`, (4.2) forces

\[
p\mid N_{h-1}(s+1).
\]

For `h\ge2`, the Dodgson identity

\[
N_h(s)N_{h-2}(s+1)
 -N_{h-1}(s)N_{h-1}(s+1)
 =-\prod_{j=2}^{h-1}(s+j)^6
\tag{5.7}
\]

then gives a contradiction, because every factor on the right is a `p`-unit. For `h=1`, `N_1=1` directly.

Thus the basic continuant is not merely “not known to vanish”; it is forced to be nonzero in both fields on the pure-cross locus.

Equations (5.3)--(5.5) identify the missing data exactly:

```plain text
same-prime gap theory controls N_h(s)=0;
pure-cross collisions force N_h(s) != 0 and leave
    B_(s+h)/p mod (p+h),
    B_s/(p+h) mod p
as free quotient residues.
```

No root count for `N_h` sees these pairs.

### 5.2 Reflection does not repair the field mismatch

Reflection gives

\[
s+h\in Z_p
 \Longrightarrow p-1-s-h\in Z_p,
\]

\[
s\in Z_\ell
 \Longrightarrow \ell-1-s\in Z_\ell.
\]

The two reflected indices differ by `2h`, but they still lie in different fields. The first reflected index equals `s` precisely when

\[
p=2s+h+1,
\]

which makes `p\mid B_s` and hence is degenerate. The second reflected index equals `s+h` precisely when

\[
\ell=2s+h+1,
\]

which again is degenerate. Outside these central coincidences, reflection creates no same-prime pair relevant to the original collision.

## 7. Casoratian tradeoff: low height or selective divisibility

Let `a_m` be the rational Apéry companion. The exact normalized Casoratian is

\[
\Delta_{s,h}
 :=a_{s+h}B_s-a_sB_{s+h}
 =\frac{6N_h(s)}{\prod_{j=1}^h(s+j)^3}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Its rational height is excellent:

\[
h(\Delta_{s,h})\ll h\log n.
\tag{7.2}
\]

But a pure-cross pair does not force `p` or `\ell` to divide its numerator; (5.6) says the opposite for the natural normalization.

The natural crossed expression that forces both hit primes is

\[
R_{n,s,h}
 :=p\,a_{s+h}B_s-\ell\,a_sB_{s+h}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

After clearing the companion denominators, whose prime support is below `p` and `\ell`, every pure-cross pair gives

\[
p\ell\mid R_{n,s,h}^{\rm int}.
\]

However,

\[
R_{n,s,h}=p\Delta_{s,h}-h\,a_sB_{s+h}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

The first term is the desired low-gap Casoratian. The second is the different-modulus defect and has full exponential height.

Indeed, put `x_m=a_m/B_m`. Then

\[
R_{n,s,h}=B_sB_{s+h}
 \bigl(p(x_{s+h}-x_s)-h x_s\bigr).
\tag{7.5}
\]

The Wronskian gives

\[
x_{m+1}-x_m
 =\frac6{(m+1)^3B_mB_{m+1}},
\tag{7.6}
\]

while `x_s\ge6/5`. Once `s\gg\log n`, the first term in the parenthesis in (7.5) is smaller than `(h/2)x_s`. Therefore

\[
|R_{n,s,h}|\gg hB_sB_{s+h},
\tag{7.7}
\]

and

\[
\log|R_{n,s,h}|\asymp 2s+h.
\tag{7.8}
\]

The `O(\log n)` endpoint indices excluded from this estimate are already harmless by elementary height.

There is also an exact coefficient-lattice explanation. Consider

\[
X_{\alpha,\beta}
 =\alpha a_{s+h}B_s-\beta a_sB_{s+h}.
\]

To force divisibility modulo `p` from only the pure-cross endpoint conditions, the universal ansatz needs `p\mid\alpha`; modulo `\ell` it needs `\ell\mid\beta`. Cancellation of the common dominant mode `a_m\sim\zeta(3)B_m` requires `\alpha=\beta`. The first point satisfying all three requirements is

\[
\alpha=\beta\in p\ell\mathbb Z,
\]

which gives the universal multiple `p\ell\Delta_{s,h}`. Its factor `p\ell` is present independently of badness; dividing it out removes all selective divisibility. Every primitive selective point has `\alpha\ne\beta` and retains the exponential defect.

Thus the natural two-solution/Casoratian lattice is exactly saturated.

## 8. Factorial carriers: bulk Kummer factors either stay tall or cancel

Let

\[
C_m=\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}.
\]

For an odd prime `q`,

\[
v_q(C_m)=
\begin{cases}
1,&q\le m\le2q-2,\\
0,&m=2q-1,
\end{cases}
\tag{8.1}
\]

in the relevant one-carry range.

Consider a bounded shift window `|t|\le H=o(n)`. For every prime `q` in the bulk intersection of the Kummer blocks for all `n+t`,

\[
v_q(C_{n+t})=1
\quad\text{for every }t.
\tag{8.2}
\]

There are two possibilities.

### 8.1 Additive combinations

Every integer linear combination

\[
L_n=\sum_{|t|\le H}c_t C_{n+t}
\]

is divisible by the whole bulk candidate primorial. If `L_n\ne0`, this alone forces

\[
\log|L_n|\gg n-O(H).
\tag{8.3}
\]

The first-order central-binomial recurrence can make some such combinations exactly zero, but a zero certificate is unusable. Any nonzero additive or determinant carrier retaining all bulk primes has linear height before Apéry divisibility is used.

### 8.2 Multiplicative ratios and finite differences of valuations

For

\[
\mathcal C_n=\prod_{|t|\le H}C_{n+t}^{c_t},
\]

cancellation of the linear Archimedean term requires

\[
\sum_t c_t=0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

But (8.2) then gives

\[
v_q(\mathcal C_n)=\sum_t c_t=0
\tag{8.5}
\]

for every bulk candidate prime. Only primes crossing a Kummer-block boundary survive, and those are endpoint/central slices rather than the pure-cross bulk.

Thus factorial carriers exhibit an exact height-versus-selectivity saturation:

```plain text
retain the universal bulk carries  => linear logarithmic height;
cancel the linear height          => cancel the bulk carries.
```

The same problem affects a residue block `J`. The universal pair carrier

\[
\prod_{s\in J}(n-s)(n-s-h)
\tag{8.6}
\]

contains every candidate pair, good or bad, and has logarithmic height `\asymp|J|\log n`. A useful block determinant would have to compress this to `|J|^{1-\delta}\log n` by an Apéry-specific relation. Generic interpolation cannot do so.

## 9. Newton--Padé, resultants, and saturation

The current repository has already proved the relevant generic obstructions.

1. A Newton polynomial interpolating the Apéry values sends both marked residues to the single evaluation point `n`; every target prime divides that value. Its height is exponential. This is just the one-point carrier again.

2. Ordinary resultants detect that *some* prefix zero exists but forget whether it is the marked residue `n-p`.

3. Marked subresultants and Fitting data contract to the evaluated two-entry ideal; they do not create a smaller integer.

4. For the primitive Padé family, the degree-dependent maximal-minor divisors are the exact saturation obstruction. Exterior-power identities produce mixed minors rather than a closed low-height recurrence for the saturated values.

5. A hit-adaptive binomial-basis lattice satisfies an exact identity: its index is the unknown target radical, and every vector outside the zero-evaluation hyperplane already has evaluation at least that radical. Siegel or Minkowski vectors can all lie in the zero hyperplane.

For a pair gap `h`, the most tempting new candidate would be one common integer `D_{n,h}` independent of `s`. The audited constructions give only:

```plain text
N_h(n-h) N_h(n):
    low height, but captures only degenerate pairs;

crossed Casoratian:
    captures pure-cross pairs, but depends on s and is exponentially tall;

factorial/block product:
    common and explicit, but universal and linear in block length;

marked interpolation/resultant:
    common, but its saturation index is the target product itself.
```

This is the precise point at which the proposed determinant route currently stops.

## 10. Bilinear/Fourier and large-sieve audit

Write the periodic zero indicator as

\[
1_{Z_p}(m)-\frac{|Z_p|}{p}
 =\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}c_{p,a}e(am/p).
\tag{10.1}
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}|c_{p,a}|^2
 \le \frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

After dyadic smoothing of the moving condition `n/2<p\le n`, the classical large sieve for the fractions `a/p`, with `p\asymp N`, yields at best

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}
 \left|\sum_p\left(1_{Z_p}(n)-\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\right)\right|^2
 \ll (N+N^2)\sum_{p\asymp N}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Using `|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}`,

\[
\sum_{p\asymp N}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}
 \ll \frac{N^{2/3}}{\log N},
\]

so (10.3) is

\[
\ll \frac{N^{8/3}}{\log N},
\tag{10.4}
\]

which is even weaker by one logarithm than (3.3). The `N^2` large-sieve term is forced by Farey spacing `\asymp N^{-2}`.

Even under the conjectural truth `|Z_p|\asymp1`, the same black-box large sieve gives only

\[
N^2/\log N=N^{2-o(1)},
\]

not a fixed power saving.

For a fixed gap `h`, expanding

\[
\sum_s1_{Z_{p+h}}(s)1_{Z_p}(s+h)
\]

produces phases modulo both `p` and `p+h`. Completion introduces prime-dependent inverse twists. There is no common scalar frequency and no bounded-conductor trace function over one fixed field. This is the same inverse-twisted obstruction recorded in the working notes: the two residual fields and the marked Fourier coefficients move together.

A bilinear theorem would have to prove cancellation after summing the actual zero-set transforms over both primes. Neither the ordinary large sieve nor an individual Weil bound supplies it.

## 11. The different-characteristic and singleton obstructions

### 11.1 Elimination over `\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_\ell`

The interval transfer matrix from `s` to `s+h` is one integer matrix. Reducing it modulo `p` and modulo `\ell` gives two componentwise systems. In

\[
\mathbb Z/p\ell\mathbb Z
 \cong \mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_\ell,
\]

the pair conditions are

\[
B_{s+h}=0\quad\text{in the }\mathbb F_p\text{ component},
\]

\[
B_s=0\quad\text{in the }\mathbb F_\ell\text{ component}.
\]

Each is one nonzero linear equation on a two-dimensional state space and has a one-dimensional solution line. The two state choices are independent in the product ring. Eliminating the state variables therefore gives the zero ideal. Universal transfer identities base-change componentwise and cannot manufacture an off-diagonal relation between the two times in the two fields.

Any nonzero resultant must insert one of:

- the moving primes themselves;
- the quotient residues `u,v` from (5.1);
- a global arithmetic relation special to the actual initial state `(1,5)`.

The first is tautological, the second is uncontrolled, and the third is exactly what is missing.

### 11.2 A hostile recurrence model

Put

\[
W_m=(m!)^3B_m.
\]

The cleared recurrence is

\[
W_{m+1}=P(m)W_m-m^6W_{m-1}.
\tag{11.1}
\]

For `r<p`, the transfer from an arbitrary integral initial vector `(W_1,W_0)` to the state at time `r` is invertible modulo `p`, because its determinant is a product of sixth powers of integers below `p`.

Fix finitely many primes `p_i` and indices `r_i<p_i`. Over each `\mathbb F_{p_i}`, choose a nonzero initial vector whose orbit has `W_{r_i}=0` and neighboring value nonzero. The Chinese remainder theorem gives one integral initial vector satisfying all these local conditions simultaneously.

Taking

\[
r_i=n_0-p_i
\]

plants an arbitrary finite set of top-half hits on one diagonal `n_0`. The resulting solution still satisfies the same recurrence, no-adjacent-zero theorem, continuant bounds, and—using the repository's general reflection theorem—the same first-block reflection.

The initial vector depends on the chosen finite set, so this is not a counterexample to the distinguished Apéry sequence. It proves a formal limitation:

> The recurrence operator, reflection, no-adjacent zeros, and the vertical `p^{2/3}` bound do not imply any fixed-power pair-energy saving uniformly over the recurrence class. A successful proof must use arithmetic special to the fixed Apéry initial direction.

### 11.3 Singleton barrier

Suppose one could prove that every shifted intersection

\[
Z_{p+h}\cap(Z_p-h)
\]

has size at most one. This would be an extremely strong same-prime multiplicity theorem. It would still give only

\[
\mathcal E_1(N)\ll \#\{p<\ell:p,\ell\asymp N\}
 \asymp \frac{N^2}{\log^2N}=N^{2-o(1)},
\]

not `N^{2-\delta}`.

The hard configuration is one fresh singleton alignment for each participating prime pair. Same-prime continuants begin only at the second alignment for a fixed modulus pair; the first alignment is invisible. This is the sharp reason that further resultant identities among the `N_h` do not address the full energy.

## 12. A lemma strictly weaker than HC2 that closes the pointwise problem

Order the hits at `n` as

\[
p_1<\cdots<p_K.
\]

For an adjacent pair `p_i,p_{i+1}`, put

\[
h_i=p_{i+1}-p_i,
\qquad
s_i=n-p_{i+1}.
\]

Call it pure cross if

\[
p_i\nmid B_{s_i},
\qquad
p_{i+1}\nmid B_{s_i+h_i}.
\]

Let `A_H^\times(n)` be the number of adjacent pure-cross pairs with `h_i\le H`.

### Pure-cross logarithmic-gap lemma (PCLG)

For every fixed `A>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{N<n\le2N}A_{A\log N}^{\times}(n)
 =o_A\!\left(\frac N{\log N}\right).
}
\tag{PCLG}
\]

A convenient stronger quantitative form would be

\[
A_{A\log N}^{\times}(n)
 \ll_A \frac N{\log^{1+\eta}N}
\tag{12.1}
\]

for one fixed `\eta>0`.

### PCLG implies the top-half theorem

Assume, toward a contradiction, that for some fixed `\varepsilon>0` there are arbitrarily large `n` with

\[
W(n)\ge\varepsilon n.
\]

By (1.1),

\[
K(n)\ge\frac{\varepsilon n}{\log n}.
\tag{12.2}
\]

Choose a fixed

\[
A>2/\varepsilon,
\qquad H=A\log n.
\]

By (3.9), at most

\[
\frac n{2H}<\frac{\varepsilon n}{4\log n}
\]

adjacent gaps exceed `H`. Hence `\gg_\varepsilon n/\log n` adjacent hit pairs have gap at most `H`.

By the new proposition (4.5), the total number of degenerate pairs with such gaps is

\[
\sum_{h\le H}M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll H^2\ll\log^2n
 =o(n/\log n).
\]

Therefore

\[
A_H^\times(n)\gg_\varepsilon n/\log n,
\]

contradicting PCLG. Thus `W(n)=o(n)` for every `n`.

### Why PCLG is strictly weaker than HC2

HC2 controls every ordered pair on every diagonal after summing an entire shell:

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}(K(n))_2
 =o(N^2/\log^2N).
\]

PCLG ignores:

- all nonadjacent pairs;
- all gaps larger than `A\log N`;
- all degenerate pairs;
- the energy on all other diagonals.

The distinction is strict even combinatorially. An abstract configuration with

\[
K(n)=N^{2/3}
\]

at every `n`, with hits spaced by `N^{1/3}`, has `A_{A\log N}^\times(n)=0` and satisfies the desired pointwise `o(N/\log N)` bound, but its shell energy is `\asymp N^{7/3}`, so HC2 fails badly.

Thus PCLG is a genuinely smaller theorem targeted only at the configuration that can violate the Apéry conclusion.

## 13. A precise low-height certificate version of PCLG

A direct algebraic reopening lemma is the following.

### Pure-cross gap certificate (PCGC)

For every fixed `A>0` and every `h\le A\log n`, construct a nonzero integer `D_{n,h}`, independent of the unknown pair location `s`, such that every pure-cross adjacent gap-`h` pair with primes `p,p+h` satisfies

\[
p(p+h)\mid D_{n,h},
\tag{13.1}
\]

and, for one fixed constant `C`,

\[
\log|D_{n,h}|\ll h(\log n)^C.
\tag{13.2}
\]

For fixed `h`, the graph on hit primes with edges of gap `h` is a union of paths. Hence its number of edges is at most its number of vertices. Every vertex prime is `>n/2` and divides `D_{n,h}`, so

\[
M_h^\times(n)
 \ll \omega_{>n/2}(D_{n,h})
 \ll h(\log n)^{C-1}.
\tag{13.3}
\]

Summing over `h\le A\log n` gives only a polylogarithmic number of pure-cross short-gap pairs, which is much stronger than PCLG.

The audit above explains exactly why the current objects do not establish PCGC:

- `N_h(n-h)N_h(n)` satisfies the height bound but misses every pure-cross pair;
- the crossed Casoratian sees pure-cross pairs but depends on `s` and has exponential height;
- factorial and node-product carriers are common but universal and have linear height;
- generic interpolation and Fitting constructions have saturation divisor equal to the unknown target product;
- classical large-sieve completion gives no fixed power because the frequencies have denominator spacing `N^{-2}`.

A proof of PCGC would therefore be a genuinely new cross-characteristic consequence of the Apéry hypergeometric initial state, not a reformulation of the existing continuant algebra.

## 14. Final assessment

The q=1 pair-energy route does not close with the present tools.

The strongest unconditional statements established in this audit are:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_1(N)\ll N^{8/3}/\log^2N,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{1,\le N^{1/3-\eta}}(N)
 \ll N^{2-\eta+o(1)},
}
\]

and the new pointwise fixed-gap theorem

\[
\boxed{M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll h.}
\]

The first two are insufficient for one exceptional diagonal. The third proves that the recurrence already controls every collision that becomes a two-zero event in one field. What remains is not vague “correlation”: it is the pure-cross singleton alignment

\[
p\mid B_{s+h},
\qquad
p+h\mid B_s,
\qquad
p\nmid B_s,
\qquad
p+h\nmid B_{s+h}.
\]

On this locus the Apéry continuant is a unit in both characteristics, divisor switching introduces uncontrolled exponential co-divisors, and every known common carrier is saturated.

The weakest clean next target is PCLG, or constructively PCGC. Either one is strictly more focused than HC2 and would finish the top-half problem. Existing recurrence, reflection, Kummer, interpolation, and large-sieve tools miss it because they are respectively same-characteristic, universally nonselective, saturated, or limited by `N^{-2}` frequency spacing.
