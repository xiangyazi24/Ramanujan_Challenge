## 2. Rank 1 — one-sided upper-prime cut decorrelation

### 2.1 Exact lemma

Define

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak U_+(T,J)
 :=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_T}\bigl(\delta_q(C)\bigr)_+

\tag{2.1}

Here `Q_T` is the exact upper-prime shell with all owner masks retained. The smallest robust theorem I would put into the canonical proof is:

> **UP-CUT+ lemma.** For every fixed integer `J`,
> \[

```text
for every fixed J and every epsilon>0,

  0,
```

No uniformity in a growing `J(T)` is asserted or needed.

### 2.2 Closure proof

\[

\le\sum_q(\delta_q(C))_+

and `M_{R,0}>=0`, (1.7) gives

\[
M^\vee
\le M_{A,0}+M_{R,z}+\mathfrak U_+(T,J).

Equations (1.9) and (2.2) imply

\[
M^\vee=o_J(B),

The threshold is linear, not quadratic:

\[
\boxed{T^2L^{-2}\times o_J(1).}

No fixed power saving in `T` is necessary. Either of the stronger estimates

\[
\mathfrak U_+(T,J)\ll_JT^{2-\eta}L^A

\[

This is the owner PF2 object. TSO is the theorem intended to bound the two-shift expansion of (4.2); it is not `V_R` and it is not the post-selected factorial.

## 5. Routes that are now rejected

### 5.1 Full WAUD from the existing abstract interfaces

The robust fixed-width rectangle has both sign masks equal to one for every lower prime and every `(q,t)` in the rectangle. The union-corrected `R_q(t)` is constant there. The symmetric spaced-zero countermodel attains

\[
\sum_{q,t}|R_q(t)-\overline R_q|^2
\asymp T^{12/5}L^{-11/5}
\]

in the relevant abstract normalization, whereas WAUD needs

\[
T^{12/5}L^{-16/5}.
\]

Thus the abstract interface misses one full logarithm in energy. Equivalently, any one-prime rectangle-mass rescue must save a half-log before squaring. Window occupancy, nesting, small `t`-variation, reflection symmetry, and the current zero moments permit the countermodel and cannot prove WAUD.

This does not invalidate WAUD as a sufficient theorem. It says WAUD cannot be obtained from the present abstract banks without a new actual-Apéry property. More importantly, the corrected owner consumer already pays `R` directly, so proving a global `V_R` theorem is not the shortest route.

### 5.2 Cubic overlap or Pollard affine closure as the missing log

For `N_p=Z(p)` and the exact cubic count `T_p=p^2 tau_p`, the affine-closure defect theorem gives, for every `p>13`,

\[
\frac14(N_p)_2
\le p^2(\rho_{2,p}-\tau_{3,p})
\le(N_p)_2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The same comparison holds under every nonnegative prime weight. Hence `rho_2-tau_3` is the same scale as the second factorial zero mass up to an absolute factor four. It cannot provide an additional `1/L`, let alone the PF2 fifth-power gain. The actual two-zero primes attaining the extremal obstruction show this is not merely a synthetic defect.

The only surviving cubic possibility is a **distinct, diagonal-subtracted cubic correlation with the actual moving q6 weights**. Once stated correctly, that is part of a PF2 or UP-CUT proof, not a rescue by the scalar `tau_3` average.

### 5.3 PAOD/PAUC or another projection without new correlation

The global lower-plane, fixed-`(p,d)` line, and upper-`q`-fibre projections have separately subcritical deterministic means. Their centered covariances differ by only `o_J(B)`, so at C4 scale they are equivalent gates. The fixed-`d` line projection is closest to the existing same-characteristic factorial theorem, but that theorem controls line totals, not the alignment of starts with the moving upper zero set `Z_{p+d}`.

An abstract countermodel preserves every line count and places all starts on upper-marked residues. Therefore changing projections does not remove the missing theorem. PAOD/PAUC are useful coordinate systems for proving UP-CUT+, not conclusions that close C4 by themselves.

### 5.4 Bounded-J plus geometry or a moving cutoff

The sharp plus analysis gives a genuine transversal threshold, not disappearance:

```text
J<=44: no boundary-tight plus state;
J=45: unique boundary geometry;
J>=46: a positive interval of boundary states.
```

At `J=45`, genuine primality collapses the unique diagonal ray to the irrelevant small case, but this deletes only that ray. It does not remove the other plus carrier.

The proof quantifiers also forbid choosing `J(T)` to stay forever inside a favorable bounded sector. For fixed `J`, the far estimate is `o_J(B)`; the near error becomes small only after the outer limit `J->infinity`. The unresolved positive plus slab merely changes owner between near and far when `J` crosses a bounded threshold. No asymptotic gain results without a new arithmetic estimate on that slab.

### 5.5 More finite zero enumeration as a proof route

The exact `T<=300000` all-`J` zero census is valuable for validation:

- it checks floors, reflections, inverse maps, Boolean union, and activation intervals;
- together with nonempty pre-upper first hits, it points to upper-mark avoidance;
- it can falsify a proposed local mixing law.

It has no direct asymptotic force. The first-hit staircase is an extreme-value statistic, and zero marked counts in a sparse range are compatible with many asymptotic behaviors. Extending the same census without a power calculation is not theorem-bearing.

### 5.6 Marginal zero counts, support occupancy, or same-characteristic resultants alone

The current first and second zero moments, no-consecutive-zero theorem, reflection, subinterval `2/3` bound, continuants, and same-characteristic resultants all survive the sharp rectangle and the PAOD/PAUC alignment countermodels. They may supply inputs to a cross-characteristic argument, but none multiplies the lower-pair density by the upper density against the adaptive moving mask. Repackaging them cannot close C4.
