# Executive assessment

The no-go results have eliminated the **wrong representations of the problem**, but they have not eliminated every geometric or cohomological route.

The most concrete remaining bridge is:

> Construct the arithmetic Mellin transform of the fixed Apéry K3 system as an **integral difference \(F\)-crystal in the Mellin exponent**, retain its full marked Frobenius state rather than only its trace, and prove a **residual shrinking-target/large-sieve theorem** for that difference system.

There is already a bounded-rank object very close to this. Let `P_p` be the perverse sheaf coming from the fixed primitive Apéry K3 family on the geometric parameter line and let

$$
χ_r=ω_p^{-r}.
$$

The Mellin cohomology

$$
C_{p,r}:=RΓ_c\bigl(G_{m,\overline{F}_p},P_p\otimes L_{χ_r}\bigr)
\tag{0.1}
$$

has uniformly bounded cohomological dimension. In the repository's normalization, the relevant generic cohomology has dimension \(6\). Its Frobenius trace is the geometric Mellin sum \(M_p(r)\), and the exact repository comparison gives, after the prescribed normalization and boundary corrections,

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid C_{p,r})
=M_p(r)\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.
\tag{0.2}
$$

This does **not** contradict Q7799. The object in (0.1) is not a rank-three K3 fiber \(V_r\). It is cohomology over the entire geometric \(t\)-line after twisting by the character indexed by \(r\). Thus \(r\) remains a Mellin exponent, exactly as Q7799 requires.

It also does not contradict Q7808. Q7808 showed that the exact trace function of the already-formed zero indicator

$$
r\longmapsto 1_{b_r=0}
$$

has a tautological direct-sum realization of rank \(Z(p)\). The repository proves only the worst-case bound \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\), not the asymptotic \(Z(p)\sim p^{2/3}\); numerically \(Z(p)\) is usually tiny. The object (0.1) represents the **value \(b_r\)** with bounded rank, before applying the nonlinear zero detector. The missing theorem is precisely the passage from bounded-rank values to rare residual zeros.

My overall judgment is:

- **No standard conjecture such as GRH, ordinary Langlands, Sato–Tate, or \(abc\) presently implies the required estimate.**
- The nearest known framework is finite-field Mellin equidistribution, but it gives complex/unitary weak equidistribution over characters, whereas the Apéry event is a microscopic congruence at the moving defining prime.
- A genuinely new ingredient is needed, but it need not be a completely new motive. It should be a new **integral/residual theory of the existing Mellin object**, coupled to the Apéry recurrence as a difference equation.
- Building the correct object is a plausible one-year project. Proving the first useful residual mixing theorem is a five-year-class project. With current tools, the unconditional full all-index theorem is closer to the twenty-year category than to the one-year category, although the exceptional rigidity of the Apéry recurrence leaves a credible five-year shortcut.

# 1. What the no-go results actually say

The four failures isolate four requirements for any successful replacement.

| Failed route | Exact failure | Requirement on the replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Zero-set trace sheaf | Rank \(Z(p)\), reducible into rank-one phases, conductor unbounded in the proved worst case | Work with the bounded-rank Mellin **value object before zero detection** |
| Hasse-root indicator | \(1_{H_p(t)=0}\) is the wrong observable; those roots contribute zero to the value Mellin transform | Retain all values \(H_p(t)\), not merely their divisor |
| Direct \(t\)-versus-\(v\) factorization | Additive zero detection places the entire Mellin coefficient inside one phase | Treat the Mellin exponent \(r\) as an essential variable |
| Hypothetical K3 fiber \(V_r\) | \(r\) is a character/coefficient index, not a point of the K3 base | Use Mellin cohomology or a difference module, not a fiber on the \(t\)-line |

The common lesson is not “geometry is useless.” It is:

> The category has to change from sheaves on the geometric parameter line to an **arithmetic Mellin/difference category on the character exponent**.

The complex analogue is classical: the Mellin transform of a holonomic \(D\)-module on \(G_m\) is a difference system in the Mellin variable. What is missing here is an integral \(p\)-adic/crystalline form with enough residual monodromy and quantitative mixing.

# 2. The concrete object I would try to construct

## 2.1 The bounded-rank Mellin cohomology

Let \(U\subset G_m\) be the smooth parameter locus of the Apéry K3 pencil, and let

$$
P_ℓ=j_{!*}V_ℓ[1]
$$

be the normalized perverse middle extension of its primitive rank-three system. For a good prime \(p\), a multiplicative character \(χ\) of \(F_p^\times\), and the corresponding Kummer sheaf \(L_χ\), define

$$
C_{p,χ}=RΓ_c\bigl(U_{\overline{F}_p},P_{ℓ,p}\otimes L_χ\bigr).
\tag{2.1}
$$

For generic \(χ\), generic vanishing concentrates this in one cohomological degree. Its dimension is controlled by the fixed rank and fixed singular divisor of \(P\), hence is \(O(1)\); in the present Apéry normalization the generic dimension is \(6\). Grothendieck–Lefschetz gives

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid C_{p,χ})
=\sum_{t\in U(F_p)}θ_p(t)χ(t)
+\text{explicit boundary terms}.
\tag{2.2}
$$

Specializing to \(χ=ω_p^{-r}\) and using the repository's exact Mellin comparison yields (0.2). Therefore

$$
\textbf{there is a bounded-rank object indexed by }r,
\textbf{ but it is Mellin cohomology, not a K3 fiber.}
\tag{2.3}
$$

Katz's finite-field Mellin formalism, the Forey–Fresán–Kowalski arithmetic Fourier-transform category, and the new Bah–Shuddhodan uniform theorem all live around (2.1). They explain the excellent complex equidistribution seen when \(χ\) ranges over all characters.

## 2.2 Why the existing Mellin object is still insufficient

The desired event is

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid C_{p,ω^{-r}})
\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.
\tag{2.4}
$$

Existing Mellin Sato–Tate theorems normalize the complex Frobenius conjugacy class and test it against fixed continuous functions. The set in (2.4) has residual probability about \(1/p\). It is a **shrinking target whose scale tends to zero with the field**. Weak complex equidistribution cannot see it.

A useful analogy is the difference between:

- proving Sato–Tate for normalized traces; and
- proving a Lang–Trotter-type asymptotic for one exact trace value.

The Apéry problem is harder still because the coefficient prime at which the trace is reduced is the same prime as the base characteristic, and the character also varies with that prime.

## 2.3 The required integral enhancement

Kedlaya's companion theorem supplies a crystalline companion to the fixed \(ℓ\)-adic system at each \(p\), with matching Frobenius characteristic polynomials. That is necessary but not sufficient. One needs an integral lattice and a simultaneous character-space construction.

A first model is

$$
Λ_p=O_p[F_p^\times]
$$

and a universal tame Kummer local system \(K_{\mathrm{univ}}\). Formally, one would like a perfect complex

$$
\mathscr M_p
:=RΓ_{\mathrm{rig},c}\bigl(
 U,P_{p,\mathrm{cris}}\otimes K_{\mathrm{univ}}\bigr)
\tag{2.5}
$$

over \(Λ_p\), with Frobenius \(φ\), such that specialization at the idempotent corresponding to \(ω_p^{-r}\) gives the crystalline version of \(C_{p,r}\).

There is an important trap. Since \(p\nmid p-1\), after adjoining the relevant roots of unity the tame group algebra is finite étale and splits into \(p-1\) idempotent factors. Therefore merely saying that (2.5) has bounded rank as a \(Λ_p\)-module does **not** couple the \(p-1\) character specializations. It can hide \(p-1\) unrelated objects in a semisimple package.

The missing extra structure is a **difference connection in the exponent**.

## 2.4 The Apéry recurrence is the scalar shadow of the needed difference structure

The Apéry Picard–Fuchs operator is

$$
θ^3-t(2θ+1)(17θ^2+17θ+5)+t^2(θ+1)^3.
$$

Its coefficient Mellin transform is the recurrence

$$
(r+1)^3b_{r+1}
-\bigl(34r^3+51r^2+27r+5\bigr)b_r
+r^3b_{r-1}=0.
\tag{2.6}
$$

Put

$$
P(r)=34r^3+51r^2+27r+5
$$

and represent the projective transfer by

$$
A(r)=\bigl[\,[P(r),-r^3],[(r+1)^3,0]\,\bigr].
\tag{2.7}
$$

Then

$$
A(r)[b_r:b_{r-1}]=[b_{r+1}:b_r].
\tag{2.8}
$$

Equivalently, with \(x_r=b_r/b_{r-1}\),

$$
x_{r+1}=\frac{P(r)x_r-r^3}{(r+1)^3x_r}.
\tag{2.9}
$$

Thus the sequence is the orbit of the fixed skew-product birational map

$$
Φ(r,x)=\left(r+1,\frac{P(r)x-r^3}{(r+1)^3x}\right)
\tag{2.10}
$$

on \(A^1\times P^1\), and \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\) is an intersection of its reduction with the divisor \(x=0\).

The scalar recurrence is not yet the full cohomological object. The concrete construction target is a lift of this scalar difference equation to Mellin cohomology:

$$
\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{Ap}}
=(M,σ,φ,\mathscr L,\langle\ ,\ \rangle),
\tag{2.11}
$$

where:

1. \(M\) is a fixed-rank difference module in the exponent variable;
2. \(σ\) implements \(r\mapsto r+1\) and lifts (2.6);
3. \(φ\) is the crystalline Frobenius inherited from the K3 system;
4. \(
\mathscr L\) is an integral lattice stable enough to reduce modulo \(p\);
5. polarization, reflection \(r\mapsto p-1-r\), and boundary/Tate pieces are explicit;
6. specialization at \(r\) has Frobenius trace congruent to \(-b_r\).

I would call (2.11) the **Apéry integral Mellin–difference crystal**.

The classical \(D\)-module Mellin transform explains why this object is natural: differential systems on the torus become difference systems in the Mellin variable. What is not currently available in the repository—or, as far as this audit found, in the literature in the needed form—is the integral crystalline construction together with a uniform residual monodromy theorem.

## 2.5 Why the full marked state matters

All previous scalar certificate arguments suffer from “certificate parity”: one divisibility event supplies one scalar equation, while useful algebraic carriers need two or more coincidences at the same prime.

The full object (2.11) supplies more than the trace. At a trace-zero specialization it canonically supplies, for example:

- the entire residual Frobenius matrix;
- invariant-line or flag data when present;
- Mellin difference transport from \(r\) to \(r+1\);
- finite Cartier/Frobenius jets.

Schematically one obtains a marked residual state

$$
\mathfrak s_{p,r}
=\bigl(\overline Φ_{p,r},
       ∇_σ\overline Φ_{p,r},
       ∇_σ^2\overline Φ_{p,r},\ldots\bigr)
\tag{2.12}
$$

in a fixed-dimensional algebraic space. The earlier coordinates \(J_1,J_2\) are plausibly the first visible shadows of this marked state. The data that the two common \((b_r,J_1)\)-zeros have \(J_2\ne0\) is exactly the kind of transversality one would hope to prove inside (2.12).

Jets alone do not solve the problem: a simple zero can still occur many times. Their value is that they may turn a large Palm correlation into an algebraic relation between **marked** states, allowing a large-monodromy or expansion theorem to rule it out without multiplying separate \(O(r)\)-height carriers term by term.

# 3. The genuinely new theorem needed

## 3.1 A residual Mellin shrinking-target theorem

Assume the construction produces residual matrices

$$
\overline Φ_{p,r}\in G(F_p)
$$

for a fixed reductive group scheme \(G\) and fixed representation \(ρ\), with

$$
b_r\equiv τ(\overline Φ_{p,r})\pmod p,
\qquad τ=\operatorname{Tr}ρ.
\tag{3.1}
$$

For a random element of a large classical group over \(F_p\), a nondegenerate trace equation has density on the order of \(1/p\). With \(p\) Mellin exponents, one expects \(O(1)\) zeros and Poisson factorial moments. For two exponent configurations not related by polarization or reflection, one expects density on the order of \(1/p^2\). This predicts:

- \(Z(p)\) of constant mean;
- near-Sidon ratio sets;
- Poisson cumulants;
- tiny connected \(E_2\)-weighted ratio dispersion.

A useful theorem would be a local-limit or large-sieve statement at the \(1/p\) scale, not merely weak equidistribution.

### Residual Mellin shrinking-target principle

For every fixed \(k\le8\), every family of exponent transformations \(γ_1,\ldots,γ_k\) not related by the forced reflection/duality strata, and every completed \(E_2\)-admissible weight \(w_p\), require a bound of the schematic form

$$
\sum_{p\asymp X}\left|
 \sum_{\mathbf r}w_p(\mathbf r)\left(
  \prod_{i=1}^k
  1_{τ(\overline Φ_{p,γ_i(\mathbf r)})=0}
  -\text{forced terms}-p^{-k}
 \right)\right|
 \ll X^{-δ}\mathcal D_{k,X}
\tag{RMST}
$$

for some fixed \(δ>0\), where \(\mathcal D_{k,X}\) is the corresponding completed diagonal scale.

One does not need (RMST) for all \(k\). The current paper architecture needs only a bounded order—at most the fixed order required for \((HM)_7\) or \((HM)_8\). This is a major advantage over the abandoned logarithmic-order tensor program.

At order two, (RMST) should specialize to the actual \((ARD_{E_2})\) form from Q7798. At order three it should imply the first pair-Palm contraction \((PP3)\).

## 3.2 Why Deligne/Katz equidistribution does not give (RMST)

Ordinary trace-function equidistribution controls test functions of bounded complexity. The indicator

$$
1_{τ(g)=0\text{ in }F_p}
$$

is a shrinking residual target. Expressing it by additive orthogonality introduces \(p\) character modes. This is the same \(p\)-sized complexity that appeared in the zero-detector and Gross–Koblitz audits.

Thus even perfect complex Sato–Tate distribution permits the exact residual zero set to be anomalous. The missing theorem must be a **local limit**, **expansion**, or **large sieve for residual conjugacy classes**, not another Riemann-hypothesis estimate for a fixed trace sheaf.

## 3.3 A concrete analytic implementation: expansion of the transfer cocycle

For a block length \(L\), define

$$
B_L(r)=A(r+L-1)\cdots A(r)\in PGL_2(F_p).
\tag{3.2}
$$

A plausible Apéry-specific theorem is that, after removing exact reflection and singular strata, the block maps \(B_L(r)\) have quantitative expansion on \(P^1(F_p)\) when \(r\) ranges over a long interval and \(L\) is polylogarithmic or a small power of \(p\).

The proof technology would likely combine:

1. computation of the generic difference-Galois group of (2.6);
2. classification of proper algebraic subgroups and rank-one quotients;
3. sum-product/product-growth estimates in \(PGL_2(F_p)\);
4. van der Corput or dispersion to pass from block-product mixing to \(k\)-point orbit correlations;
5. Brun or Chen–Stein factorial-moment arguments to obtain Poisson-scale rare events.

This is not a standard Bourgain–Gamburd application: the generators vary deterministically with time, and the target is one orbit rather than a random walk. The new theorem would be a **non-autonomous projective-cocycle expansion theorem**. But it is concrete, testable, and exactly matched to the recurrence.

## 3.4 A second implementation: an inverse theorem for large \(E_2\) excess

A potentially more economical route is not to prove full residual equidistribution. Prove an inverse theorem:

> If the \(E_2\)-weighted connected ratio term is larger than the diagonal scale, then the zero indicators possess a coherent rank-one additive/Kummer phase, or the marked Mellin states concentrate in a proper algebraic subgroup or correspondence.

The anchored star exhibits precisely such a coherent phase. The arithmetic half would then show that the Apéry Mellin–difference crystal has no corresponding rank-one difference subquotient after removing reflection and polarization.

This route has the right division of labor:

- harmonic analysis and additive combinatorics classify a large dispersion excess;
- difference Galois theory and marked residual monodromy exclude the structured alternatives.

It also explains why the numerical value \(ARD_{E_2}<0.1\%\) is more informative than unweighted Sidon energy: it says the actual completion weights see no coherent low-rank phase.

# 4. Is this reducible to a known conjecture?

## 4.1 GRH: no

GRH estimates zeros of global \(L\)-functions and yields cancellation in sums of their complex coefficients. Here the finite-field Riemann hypothesis needed for every fixed bounded-conductor trace function is already supplied by Deligne. That part of the technology is not missing.

The unresolved event is

$$
M_p(ω_p^{-r})\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p},
$$

where both the base characteristic and the reduction prime are \(p\). A nonzero algebraic integer of generic complex size can vanish modulo the chosen prime above \(p\). GRH does not control this microscopic residual event.

Thus “GRH for the relevant \(L\)-functions” would not by itself imply the Apéry estimate.

## 4.2 Langlands or automorphy: no, not by itself

The function-field Langlands correspondence is already an ingredient in the existence of crystalline companions. It helps identify compatible coefficient objects and matching Frobenius polynomials. It does not prove that one selected residual trace—or all residual traces in a shrinking target—equidistributes modulo the moving defining prime.

Even granting full automorphy of the fixed K3 system leaves two missing steps:

1. an integral family in the Mellin exponent with a controlled lattice;
2. a diagonal residual large sieve/local limit for its specializations.

So the problem is not waiting for “Langlands” in the usual sense. It is waiting for a new quantitative theorem after Langlands/companions have constructed the objects.

## 4.3 Sato–Tate: essentially available in the wrong topology

Katz and Forey–Fresán–Kowalski give Mellin equidistribution as the character varies. Bah–Shuddhodan now provide a uniform varying-characteristic theorem for bounded-complexity perverse sheaves with common Tannakian group. This is strikingly close to the Apéry setup.

But those results concern normalized complex/unitary Frobenius classes and average over essentially all characters. They do not give a local limit modulo the defining characteristic. The proposed new statement is best described as

> a residual, shrinking-target Mellin Sato–Tate theorem.

That is a natural conjecture, but it is not currently a standard named conjecture with an established implication chain.

## 4.4 Standard compatible-system large sieves: no

Large-sieve-for-Frobenius results use reductions at many auxiliary primes \(ℓ\ne p\) while the base finite field has characteristic \(p\). The Apéry event lies on the forbidden diagonal \(ℓ=p\). Crystalline companions put an object on that diagonal but do not supply the independence across \(p\) needed by the sieve.

FKMS-type Type-II theorems can handle the semiprime variable once a bounded-conductor irreducible trace kernel is present. Q7808 shows that the exact zero-indicator kernel is not such a kernel. The proposed difference-crystal construction is intended to create a different input, not to reapply FKMS unchanged.

## 4.5 \(abc\), Mason–Stothers, or Vojta: no clean reduction

The recurrence skew product (2.10) makes an arithmetic-dynamical reformulation possible. One could ask for a Vojta-style truncated counting estimate for orbit intersections with the divisor \(x=0\). Existing conditional gcd theorems in arithmetic dynamics typically concern polarized iterates of fixed rational maps or simultaneous divisor hits of independent orbits. The Apéry map is a non-polarized skew product with a translating base coordinate, and the required cutoff uses the same prime as the orbit period modulo \(p\).

A sufficiently strong, uniform Vojta conjecture tailored to this skew product might imply an \(o(n)\) gcd estimate. At present that would be more a reformulation than a reduction to a recognized standard consequence. Ordinary \(abc\) tends to force large radicals or primitive divisors; it does not automatically show that the part of \(b_n\) supported on primes \(p\le n\) has logarithm \(o(n)\).

## 4.6 The closest familiar analogy

The closest classical analogy is not GRH but **Lang–Trotter plus a large sieve**, with three extra complications:

- the representation changes with the Mellin character;
- the coefficient prime equals the base characteristic;
- the target is a fixed-order joint distribution needed by \(E_2\) dispersion.

One can formulate a “diagonal residual Mellin Lang–Trotter conjecture” that implies the theorem. That conjecture would be new and would encode almost exactly the missing arithmetic.

# 5. Why the numerical evidence points to this construction

The four main numerical observations are not generic signs of “randomness”; they match specific consequences of large residual monodromy.

## 5.1 Poisson \(Z(p)\)

If \(p\) marked Frobenius states sample a fixed-dimensional group and the trace-zero hypersurface has density \(1/p+O(p^{-2})\), then the expected number of hits is \(1+O(1/p)\). Fixed-order independence gives Poisson factorial moments.

## 5.2 Near-Sidon zero sets

Two unrelated trace-zero events have expected joint density \(1/p^2\). Among \(p\) indices, a prescribed ratio relation should therefore occur only exceptionally. Reflection supplies the forced relation; the almost complete absence of any other repeated ratio is the predicted two-point behavior.

## 5.3 Tiny \(ARD_{E_2}\)

A rank-one additive or Kummer constituent would survive completion and align with semiprime products, exactly as in the anchored star. A tiny weighted connected ratio term is evidence that no such constituent is present in the actual marked system.

## 5.4 Sub-Poisson Palm excess

The pair-Palm experiment conditions on two CRT hits and sees no amplified third-prime load. This is the fixed-order connected correlation that a residual product-monodromy theorem should control.

These observations do not prove the theorem, but they tell us what to compute next: not more root counts, but the residual monodromy and transition data of the full Mellin state.

# 6. A concrete research program

## Phase I: construct and falsify quickly — approximately one year

### I.1 Build the integral Mellin cohomology

Construct the overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal companion of the primitive K3 sheaf with all boundary/Tate corrections explicit. Produce a Frobenius-stable integral lattice on the Mellin cohomology for tame characters and prove

$$
\operatorname{Tr}Φ_{p,r}\equiv-b_r\pmod p.
\tag{6.1}
$$

The repository identities should make the trace comparison feasible; the genuine work is the integral lattice and simultaneous character bookkeeping.

### I.2 Lift the recurrence to a difference connection

Prove that the Mellin cohomology admits a shift operator whose trace shadow is (2.6). Determine whether the full system has fixed rank and rational transition matrices of bounded degree independent of \(p\).

A scalar recurrence alone is not enough. The go/no-go question is whether the **full Frobenius state** is coupled by a bounded-complexity difference connection.

### I.3 Compute the difference-Galois and residual groups

Determine the generic difference-Galois group after removing polarization and reflection. The desired signal is a large non-solvable group and no rank-one subquotients in the connected tensor constructions needed through order \(8\).

A solvable group, a persistent Kummer quotient, or a decomposition whose number of factors grows with \(p\) would kill this route.

### I.4 Compute marked states, not just traces

For moderate primes, compute

$$
\overline Φ_{p,r},\qquad
∇_σ\overline Φ_{p,r},\qquad
J_1,\qquad J_2
$$

and test:

- residual group size and subgroup concentration;
- trace-zero state distribution;
- pair distributions under \(r\mapsto hr\) and reflection;
- whether large \(E_2\) weights correlate with any matrix coefficient or jet;
- whether the two exceptional \((b,J_1)\)-zeros are explained by a visible proper stratum.

This phase should produce a decisive answer even if it does not prove the conjecture.

## Phase II: first arithmetic theorem — approximately two to five years

The first target should not be the full theorem. Choose one of:

1. a fixed-\(h\), averaged-\(p\) residual two-point estimate;
2. an averaged bound for the number of non-forced repeated ratios;
3. an inverse theorem showing that failure of \(ARD_{E_2}\) forces a rank-one difference quotient;
4. a block-product expansion theorem for \(B_L(r)\) on \(P^1(F_p)\).

A theorem of this kind would be the first genuine bridge from the bounded-rank value object to the sparse zero set. It would also be independently publishable as a new result on arithmetic difference equations.

## Phase III: pair-Palm and fixed moments — approximately five years if Phase II succeeds

Use the first residual theorem inside the exact Q7798 completion, keeping the actual joint \(E_2\) weights. Prove \((PP3)\), then extend the connected construction only through the fixed tensor order required by \((HM)_7\) or \((HM)_8\).

Because the tensor order is bounded, ranks and exceptional strata may depend on that order. This is much more realistic than uniform control through \(c\log X\).

## Phase IV: full all-index theorem

If the residual theorem is stable under fixed products and quotient-cell uniformity, the existing moment/quotient reductions should assemble the full result. If each order requires a bespoke new argument, the project becomes much longer.

A concise project blueprint is:

```text
AMDC-1  Integral Mellin cohomology and exact trace congruence
AMDC-2  Difference connection lifting the Apéry recurrence
AMDC-3  Generic/residual difference-monodromy classification
AMDC-4  Inverse theorem or block expansion for trace-zero hits
AMDC-5  E2-weighted two-point estimate / PP3
AMDC-6  Fixed connected powers through order 7 or 8
AMDC-7  Quotient-cell uniformity and final assembly
```

# 7. Honest time estimate

## What is plausibly a one-year problem

A focused collaboration involving one expert in overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystals or \(p\)-adic cohomology and one expert in difference equations could plausibly do the following within a year:

- construct the candidate object in a precise category;
- establish the trace specialization;
- identify the shift/difference structure or prove it does not exist in bounded form;
- compute the generic difference-Galois group;
- produce a strong residual-monodromy data set;
- state the exact first new theorem with no category errors.

A complete proof of the Apéry conjecture in one year would require a hidden identity or unexpectedly strong rigidity theorem. I would not plan on it.

## What is plausibly a five-year problem

A five-year program is credible for:

- proving the first residual mixing/inverse theorem;
- closing \(ARD_{E_2}\) or \(PP3\);
- possibly extending it to all fixed orders needed by the manuscript, if one general monodromy/expansion theorem handles the tensor powers uniformly.

This is the optimistic but serious scenario. The fact that only fixed order is needed, together with the exceptional order drop of the Apéry recurrence at the genuine Apéry parameter, is the strongest reason not to classify the problem automatically as twenty-year.

## Why the full problem may be twenty-year-class

If the Apéry-specific difference system does not admit a direct expansion theorem, one would need a general theory of:

- integral Mellin transforms of \(F\)-isocrystals;
- residual monodromy in a varying tame-character family;
- local limits for targets of density \(1/p\);
- the diagonal coefficient-prime/base-characteristic regime;
- weighted Type-II estimates for deterministic sections.

That is not one missing lemma. It is a new interface among \(p\)-adic cohomology, difference Galois theory, expansion in finite groups, and sparse sieve theory. Developing it generally is a twenty-year-scale mathematical program.

### Forced choice

If forced to assign the labels in the question:

- **the correct-object construction:** one-year category;
- **a decisive bridge theorem such as PP3:** five-year category;
- **the unconditional full all-index theorem, from today's toolkit:** closer to the twenty-year category than the five-year category.

My practical recommendation is to organize it as a **five-year high-risk program with a one-year kill gate**. At the end of year one, the difference-monodromy computation should say whether an Apéry-specific shortcut exists. If the group is large and the only low-rank strata are the already known reflection/polarization ones, a five-year proof becomes believable. If the integral Mellin family splits or carries hidden rank-one pieces, stop trying geometric refinements and return to a direct recurrence-cocycle/inverse-sieve attack.

# 8. Bottom line

> **The missing bridge is an integral arithmetic Mellin transform that lands in a bounded-rank difference \(F\)-crystal, together with a residual shrinking-target or expansion theorem for its marked Frobenius states. Existing Mellin geometry supplies bounded rank and complex equidistribution; the Apéry recurrence supplies a fixed difference equation. What is absent is the same-characteristic local limit that turns those values into Poisson residual zeros and controls their \(E_2\)-weighted correlations.**

This is genuinely new, but it is not directionless. It has a canonical object, a precise first theorem, a falsifiable monodromy test, and a fixed-order endpoint. That is the route I would pursue before any further attempt to package \(Z_p\), the roots of \(H_p\), or a fictitious \(r\)-fiber as an ordinary bounded-conductor trace function.
