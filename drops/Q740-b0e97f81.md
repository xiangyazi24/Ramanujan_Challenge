# Q740: hostile audit of the q=1 tail-defect route

## 1. Exact orientation of the radical

Let
\[
m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\qquad D=\operatorname{lcm}(m+1,\ldots,n),
\]
and
\[
T=\sum_{k=m+1}^n\frac{b_{n-k}}k.
\]

Write the reduced denominator of T as den(T). For a prime p in (n/2,n], p occurs in D with valuation exactly one, because p is larger than every possible multiple 2p>n. Hence
\[
v_p(D)=1.
\]

The p-part of D/den(T) survives exactly when the p-denominator is not cancelled in T. Since
\[
T=\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\frac{b_{n-k}}k,
\]
modulo p only the term k=p has denominator divisible by p. Therefore the residue of pT is
\[
pT\equiv b_{n-p}\pmod p.
\]

Thus:

- if p divides b_{n-p}, the p-denominator is not cancelled and p divides the reduced denominator of T;
- if p does not divide b_{n-p}, the p-denominator cancels.

Therefore the correct orientation is
\[
R_1=\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}(D/\operatorname{den}(T)),
\]
not the radical of den(T). The earlier statement with den(T) had the sign reversed.

## 2. Audit of the proposed bounded-degree recurrence

The claimed identity was that for
\[
L_n(x)=\sum_{k>n/2}\frac{b_{n-k}}{k+x}
\]
there exist bounded-degree polynomials A_n,B_n,P_n such that
\[
A_n(x)L_n(x+1)-B_n(x)L_n(x)=P_n(x).
\]

This claim is not established by the Apéry recurrence, and in the stated form it is essentially false as a nontrivial bounded-degree relation.

The reason is a pole-counting obstruction. The function L_n(x) has simple poles at
\[
x=-k,\qquad k=m+1,\ldots,n,
\]
with residues
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{x=-k}L_n(x)=b_{n-k}.
\]
There are about n/2 independent poles.

Assume A,B have degree bounded by a constant C independent of n. Consider the difference
\[
A(x)L_n(x+1)-B(x)L_n(x).
\]
At a generic pole x=-k of L_n, the second term has residue
\[
-B(-k)b_{n-k}.
\]
The first term has a pole only when x+1=-(k-1), i.e. from the neighboring index. Hence cancellation of all poles imposes a first-order relation among consecutive Apéry coefficients. A bounded-degree polynomial multiplier cannot cancel an arbitrary block of n/2 poles unless the recurrence coefficients are explicitly encoded into A and B with degree growing with n.

The actual Apéry recurrence has polynomial coefficients in the index k:
\[
(k+1)^3b_{k+1}=a(k)b_k-k^3b_{k-1},
\]
where a(k) has degree three. Translating this recurrence to the pole variable x necessarily introduces factors depending on every shifted pole. It does not produce bounded-degree A_n,B_n in x.

Hence the previous "bounded-degree recurrence in x" was a conflation of two different facts:

1. the sequence b_n is holonomic in n;
2. the finite partial fraction sum L_n(x) has a rational shift relation in x with bounded degree.

The first does not imply the second.

## 3. What can actually be extracted from the recurrence

A valid weaker statement is available. Multiplying by the full denominator
\[
Q_n(x)=\prod_{k=m+1}^n(x+k)
\]
gives
\[
Q_n(x)L_n(x)=\sum_{k=m+1}^n b_{n-k}\frac{Q_n(x)}{x+k},
\]
which is a polynomial of degree n-m-1.

The Apéry recurrence can give recurrences for this polynomial family, but the degree necessarily grows with n. This yields no direct subexponential bound on the denominator defect.

The surviving concrete statement is therefore:

> A successful q=1 proof needs a theorem controlling cancellations among the n/2 residues of this growing-degree rational function. A fixed-degree Padé/G-function denominator theorem cannot be invoked because the required bounded-degree shift equation is absent.

---

## 4. Final obstruction

The tail-defect formulation remains an exact reformulation of R_1, but the proposed mechanism is not a proof route. The missing ingredient is not a bounded-degree recurrence; it would have to be a new cancellation theorem for the Apéry partial-fraction polynomial
\[
Q_n(x)L_n(x).
\]

Any valid future route must exploit additional arithmetic structure of the Apéry recurrence beyond holonomicity. Generic G-function denominator bounds do not apply to this object and would only recover exponential height estimates, not the required o(n) logarithmic radical bound.
