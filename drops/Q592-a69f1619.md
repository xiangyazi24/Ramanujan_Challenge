# Cocycle return times and bounded multiplicity for Apéry

## Executive summary

The proposed cocycle/Bézout route identifies the right geometric object, but the final claim is false in its stated form. A second-order holonomic recurrence over \(\mathbf F_p\) does **not** imply an \(O(1)\) bound on zeros over a full period. The missing input is not holonomicity; it is a strong arithmetic monodromy or irreducibility theorem for the finite-field family.

The correct hierarchy is:

* The eigenvector condition is a quadratic hypersurface condition.
* Naive degree growth gives a bound of size \(O(h)\) for gap \(h\).
* Holonomicity reduces the problem to a finite-dimensional differential/difference module, but its finite-field zero count is still typically \(O(p)\), not \(O(1)\).
* To obtain bounded multiplicity one needs additional input: large monodromy, independence of shifted evaluations, or a trace-function correlation estimate.

## (A) Fixed matrix versus varying projective point

For a fixed \(A\in GL_2(\mathbf F_p)\), the fixed locus in \(\mathbf P^1\) is indeed at most two points unless \(A\) is scalar.

However the collision condition is

\[
P_{s,r}x_r=x_r,
\qquad x_r=[f_r:g_r],
\]

and both entries vary with \(r\). This is not a fixed-point problem for one matrix.

The useful reformulation is instead:

\[
\det(P_{s,r}v_r,v_r)=0.
\]

This is one scalar recurrence expression. The question becomes the zero count of a new holonomic sequence.

## (B) Degree of the eigenvector variety

The variety

\[
V=\{(A,[x:y]):Ax=\lambda x\}
\]

is correctly described by

\[
(a-d)xy+by^2-cx^2=0.
\]

It is a quadratic hypersurface in the projective coordinate variables.

The problem is the degree of the parametrized curve \(\Phi(r)\). It is not bounded independently of the time gap.

For a fixed gap \(h=s-r\), the transfer product is

\[
M(r+h-1)\cdots M(r).
\]

After clearing denominators, the entries have degree approximately

\[
3h
\]

in \(r\). Therefore Bézout gives only

\[
O(h)
\]

zeros, exactly as observed in the question.

This is a real bound, but it is not enough after summing over all gaps.

## (C) Why holonomicity does not immediately give O(1)

The Apéry recurrence is a rank-two difference module with polynomial coefficients of degree three. This gives a holonomic object.

But the general finite-field zero theory is closer to:

* rational functions: zeros bounded by degree;
* algebraic functions: bounded by complexity;
* differential/difference equations: complexity bounds usually scale with the finite-field size or conductor.

There is no theorem of the form:

> order \(d\) + coefficient degree \(c\) implies at most \(f(d,c)\) zeros modulo every prime.

Such a theorem would contradict many examples of holonomic sequences with many finite-field zeros.

The known Apéry fact that \(|Z_p|\) is empirically close to one is therefore extra arithmetic information, not a consequence of holonomicity alone.

## (D) The sharper recurrence structure

The useful object is not the individual polynomial degree but the transfer cocycle.

For a gap \(h\), define

\[
C_h(r)=\det(P_{r+h,r}v_r,v_r).
\]

Then \(C_h\) is itself a solution/coordinate of a finite-rank difference module.

The desired statement is something like:

\[
\#\{r:C_h(r)=0\}=O(1)
\]

uniformly in \(h\).

This would follow from a strong statement that the corresponding shifted module has geometric monodromy with no exceptional invariant subspace.

At present, no such theorem is available for the Apéry cocycle.

## (E) What could actually prove bounded multiplicity?

Three possible routes remain.

### 1. Large monodromy

If the family generated by the transfer matrices has full geometric monodromy (for example a large subgroup of \(SL_2\)), then collision conditions become rare orbit intersections.

One would need an analogue of:

\[
\#\{r:P_{r+h,r}v_r\parallel v_r\}\ll 1+o(p).
\]

This is an orbit-intersection theorem, not a generic holonomic theorem.

### 2. Trace-function bounds

After expressing the collision indicator as a trace function, one could seek square-root cancellation in sums such as

\[
\sum_r \psi(C_h(r)).
\]

This is analogous to the missing Mellin anti-concentration results elsewhere in P3.2.

### 3. Gap-polynomial irreducibility

The project already has a more arithmetic route: same-prime collisions imply divisibility by gap polynomials \(N_h(r)\).

If the family \(N_h\) is irreducible/coprime with controlled height, then a prime cannot divide too many shifts simultaneously.

This gives a global substitute for the desired cocycle multiplicity theorem.

## Final answers

1. **Can the cocycle condition be bounded geometrically?** Yes, but naive Bézout gives only \(O(h)\), not a constant.

2. **What is the degree of \(\Phi\)?** For fixed gap \(h\), about \(3h\) after clearing denominators.

3. **Can holonomicity imply bounded zeros?** No. Holonomicity gives finite complexity, not uniform finite-field zero bounds.

4. **Is there a sharper Apéry-specific route?** Yes: use monodromy/orbit expansion, trace-function cancellation, or the existing gap-polynomial/resultant machinery.

The precise missing theorem is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Uniform orbit-intersection bound for the Apéry transfer cocycle over }\mathbf F_p.
}
\]

It is stronger than holonomicity and is the cocycle analogue of the missing TCMA/HM3 anti-concentration estimates in the rest of P3.2.
