# Q449 — What Katz–Sarnak actually gives, and the theorem P3.2 really needs

## Executive verdict

There is a strong Katz/Dwork interpretation behind the new `alpha` experiments, but the proposed argument currently conflates three different local systems.

| Object | Rank | Varying parameter | Connected Zariski monodromy | Relation to `b_r` |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| The Apéry K3 Picard–Fuchs system for `A(x)=sum b_n x^n` | 3 | the geometric coordinate `x` | `SO_3(C) ≅ PSL_2(C)`; it is a symmetric square | the `b_n` are Taylor coefficients at `x=0` |
| The classical `4F3(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2;1,1,1;x)` system | 4 | the geometric coordinate `x` | `Sp_4(C)`; compact form `USp(4)` | at the special fiber `x=1`, its truncation gives the central coefficient `b_{(p-1)/2}` modulo `p` |
| The character-aspect hypergeometric object in the current P3.2 repository | rank 4 generically in `t`, rank 3 at `t=1` | a Kummer character `A=omega_p^r`, plus the hypergeometric coordinate `t` | generically symplectic in the relevant self-dual cases; the `t=1` stalk has a Tate line plus a rank-2 weight-3 factor | its trace at character exponent `r` is congruent to `b_r mod p` |

The answers to the four questions are therefore:

1. **There is no literal continuous evaluation-point family `alpha -> M_alpha` whose geometric coordinate is `alpha` and whose Frobenius trace is `b_{floor(alpha p)}`.** The correct parameter is the discrete Kummer-character exponent `r`. Katz's finite-field Mellin-transform framework is indeed the right ambient theory, but ordinary Katz equidistribution does not locate the exact mod-`p` zeros in character space.
2. **For the rank-3 Apéry K3 equation, the group is `SO_3`, equivalently `PSL_2`, with simply connected cover `SL_2` acting through `Sym^2`.** Thus candidates (b) and (c) are two descriptions of the same connected group. It is not `Sp_4`.
3. **For the separate rank-4 classical `4F3(1/2^4;1^3;x)` equation, Beukers–Heckman gives Zariski closure `Sp_4(C)`.** Its Sato–Tate compact form is `USp(4)`.
4. **The heuristic `Pr(p divides trace) ~ 1/p`, hence an expected `O(1)` number of zero labels among `p` characters, is reasonable as a local-limit heuristic. It is not a consequence of standard Katz–Sarnak equidistribution.** Moreover, it predicts bounded mean or a Poisson-scale distribution, not a uniform bound `|Z_p|=O(1)`; and even a uniform vertical bound would not imply the all-`n` P3.2 statement without cross-prime horizontal decorrelation.

The exact missing theorem is a **defining-characteristic local-limit theorem in the Kummer-character aspect, upgraded to a two-prime weighted dispersion estimate**. Big monodromy is important evidence for that theorem, but is not itself that theorem.

## 0. Four corrections that must be made before applying Katz

### 0.1 The Apéry numbers are coefficients, not values at `x=1`

Let

```text
A(x) = sum_{n>=0} b_n x^n.
```

The Apéry differential operator can be written

```text
L_Ap = theta^3
       - x (2 theta + 1)(17 theta^2 + 17 theta + 5)
       + x^2 (theta + 1)^3,
       theta = x d/dx.
```

Thus `b_n` is the `n`-th Taylor coefficient of the holomorphic solution at `x=0`. Evaluating the period at `x=1` does not produce the sequence `(b_n)`. In fact the power series has radius `17-12 sqrt(2)<1`, so its defining Taylor series does not converge at `x=1`.

In the standard `x` normalization, the finite nonzero singularities of the Apéry equation are the roots of

```text
1 - 34x + x^2 = 0,
```

namely `17 ± 12 sqrt(2)`, not `1/34^2` and `1`. A rational pullback can of course move singularities, but the list in the question mixes normalizations and mixes the Apéry equation with a different hypergeometric equation.

### 0.2 The rank-4 `4F3` is a different Picard–Fuchs system

The classical hypergeometric series

```text
H(x) = _4F_3(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2;1,1,1;x)
```

satisfies

```text
[theta^4 - x(theta+1/2)^4] H = 0
```

and has singularities `0,1,infinity`. This is the Picard–Fuchs system associated with a hypergeometric Calabi–Yau **threefold**, not the rank-3 Apéry K3 Picard–Fuchs system.

The central Apéry coefficient enters because

```text
b_n = _4F_3(-n,-n,n+1,n+1;1,1,1;1),
```

and for `n=(p-1)/2` all four numerator parameters reduce to `1/2 mod p`. Hence the central coefficient is congruent modulo `p` to a truncation of the half-parameter `4F3` at `x=1`. This is the special arithmetic bridge behind the weight-4 level-8 modular form. It does **not** identify the entire moving row `r -> b_r mod p` with the geometric fibers `x -> H(x)`.

### 0.3 The moving index `r` is a character exponent

The current repository already contains the correct object in
[`problems/3.2/lemmaBprime_result.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/lemmaBprime_result.tex). For a nontrivial Kummer character

```text
A = chi_{p,r} = omega_p^r,
```

it defines a finite-field Mellin/Kummer trace

```text
S_{p,r} = - sum_{x in U(F_p)} A(Lambda(x))
```

with

```text
S_{p,r} = b_r mod p.
```

After Jacobi convolution, the relevant generic hypergeometric object is written there as

```text
Hyp(epsilon,epsilon,epsilon,epsilon;
    A,A,A^{-1},A^{-1})
```

in a separate hypergeometric coordinate `t`. At the balanced singular value `t=1`, the manuscript obtains the effective Frobenius factor

```text
P_{p,r}(X) = (X-p)(X^2-a_{p,r}X+p^3),
qquad a_{p,r} = S_{p,r}-p,
```

and consequently

```text
a_{p,r} = b_r mod p.
```

This is the precise motive-like statement available in the project:

- there is a Tate eigenvalue `p`;
- the remaining rank-2 factor has determinant `p^3` and is of weight 3;
- `b_r mod p` is the Hasse invariant / residual trace of that rank-2 factor.

So `b_r` is not literally a weight-3 Frobenius trace as an integer. Its residue modulo `p` agrees with one.

### 0.4 Haar measure of the exact trace-zero set is zero

The relevant compact group for a rank-4 symplectic system is `USp(4)`, not the noncompact complex algebraic group `Sp_4(C)`. The trace map on `USp(4)` has a continuous distribution. Therefore

```text
Haar{g in USp(4) : tr(g)=0} = 0,
```

not a positive constant. What can be positive is the **density** of the trace distribution at zero, or the measure of a fixed interval around zero.

The congruence `a_{p,r}=0 mod p` is not the same event as normalized trace exactly zero. It is a fine arithmetic lattice event involving every multiple of `p` in the Weil interval.

## 1. Does a family `alpha -> M_alpha` fit Katz's framework?

## 1.1 Not as a continuous geometric family in `alpha`

At a fixed prime `p`, the index set is the character group

```text
Hom(F_p^*, C^*) ≅ Z/(p-1)Z.
```

Writing

```text
alpha = r/(p-1)
```

puts an archimedean coordinate on this discrete group, but it does not turn it into the `F_p`-points of a fixed algebraic parameter line carrying a bounded-conductor sheaf. Katz's Mellin-transform theory varies the character `A`; it does not reinterpret the exponent `r` as the geometric evaluation coordinate of the original K3 family.

This distinction is decisive. Ordinary conjugacy-class equidistribution of the multiset

```text
{Frob(A) : A a good character of F_p^*}
```

is invariant under permuting the character labels. It can describe the distribution of trace values, but it cannot by itself say whether the zero labels occur near `r/(p-1)=0.1`, `0.5`, or `0.9`. The new `alpha_p` statistic is precisely a statement about the **correlation between trace and character label**.

A theorem proving the observed location law would need twisted sums such as

```text
sum_{r mod p-1} 1_{p|b_r} exp(2 pi i m r/(p-1)),
```

not merely moments of the normalized traces.

## 1.2 What happens for one fixed rational `alpha=a/b`

There is a useful partial salvage. Assume `0<a<b`, `(a,b)=1`, and restrict to primes

```text
p = 1 mod b.
```

Then

```text
floor(ap/b) = a(p-1)/b,
```

and the character

```text
A_{p,alpha} = omega_p^{a(p-1)/b}
```

has fixed order `b`. In this restricted sense, a fixed rational `alpha` can plausibly be packaged as a compatible hypergeometric system with cyclotomic coefficient field. The rank-2 weight-3 quotient of the `t=1` stalk is a reasonable candidate for a motive `M_alpha`, after all local normalizations and exceptional primes are handled.

But four qualifications are essential:

1. The theorem would identify a Frobenius trace only **modulo `p`** with `b_{floor(alpha p)}`.
2. The coefficient field is generally cyclotomic, not necessarily `Q`.
3. For primes in other residue classes modulo `b`, the floor introduces finitely many residue-class-dependent shifts; these must be treated separately.
4. Scanning all `r=0,...,p-1` means denominators and character orders grow with `p`. That is no longer a fixed compatible system of bounded complexity.

Thus fixed rational slices are meaningful, but they do not form the claimed continuous family and they do not by themselves control `Z_p`.

## 1.3 Can different fixed rational slices be proved independent?

For a **fixed finite list** of rational parameters, there is a standard strategy in Katz's framework:

1. construct the corresponding compatible sheaves or motives;
2. prove each has connected big monodromy;
3. rule out geometric isomorphisms, dualities, rank-one twists, and common quotient systems;
4. apply a Goursat–Kolchin–Ribet product-monodromy criterion.

If these checks succeed, the product monodromy group is the product of the individual groups, and one obtains joint equidistribution for fixed continuous class functions.

This is the correct conceptual meaning of “independent motives.” The small observed correlations in the question are supportive, but correlations of a few raw residue sequences do not prove Tannakian independence. They are also not sensitive to rare exact divisibility events.

Even full product Sato–Tate for a finite collection of fixed rational `alpha` values would still not imply either of the following growing statements:

```text
# {0<=r<p : p|b_r} = O(1),
```

or

```text
uniform pair dispersion over all primes p,q and all moving labels r.
```

Those statements involve `p` character labels at characteristic `p`, not a fixed finite list of motives.

## 1.4 What Katz does give in the present setting

Katz's finite-field Mellin theorem is extremely relevant. Roughly, once the input trace function is geometrically suitable and its Tannakian monodromy group is known, normalized Mellin transforms over good characters become equidistributed in conjugacy classes of a maximal compact group as the finite fields grow.

This gives, for fixed algebraic representations or fixed continuous class functions, asymptotics of the form

```text
(1/# good characters) sum_A Phi(Frob_A)
    -> integral_K Phi(g) dg.
```

It is excellent for:

- fixed trace moments;
- polynomial statistics of eigenvalues;
- macroscopic intervals in normalized trace;
- proving lack of a gross bias among character twists;
- product equidistribution after product monodromy is proved.

It does **not** automatically handle the test function

```text
Phi_p(Frob_A) = 1_{trace(Frob_A)=0 mod p},
```

because `Phi_p` depends on `p`, oscillates on the defining-characteristic lattice scale, and is not a fixed continuous class function.

### Answer to Question 1

The correct statement is:

> The moving Apéry coefficients fit naturally into Katz's **character-aspect Mellin-transform framework**, not into the geometric evaluation-point family proposed in the question. Big monodromy can explain macroscopic trace randomness and fixed-moment independence, but a new defining-characteristic local-limit theorem is required to count the exact Hasse zeros and to locate them in the character group.

## 2. What is the precise monodromy group for the Apéry K3 equation?

## 2.1 The order-3 operator is a symmetric square

The Apéry generating function satisfies

```text
x^2(1-34x+x^2)y'''
 + x(3-153x+6x^2)y''
 + (1-112x+7x^2)y'
 + (x-5)y = 0.
```

This third-order equation is the symmetric square of a second-order equation. A convenient form of the latter is

```text
x(x^2-34x+1)u''
 + (2x^2-51x+1)u'
 + (x-10)u/4 = 0.
```

Consequently the rank-3 monodromy representation is the symmetric-square representation of a rank-2 monodromy representation.

## 2.2 `SO_3`, `PSL_2`, and `SL_2` are the same answer at three levels

The symmetric-square map is

```text
Sym^2 : SL_2(C) -> SO_3(C),
```

with kernel `{±I}`. Hence

```text
SO_3(C) ≅ PSL_2(C).
```

A recent differential-Galois computation by Dreyfus–Rivoal states that an equivalent gauged rank-3 Apéry equation has differential Galois group `PSL_2(C)`. Geometrically, the rank-3 transcendental local system of a Picard-rank-19 K3 family carries a nondegenerate symmetric intersection form, so its connected monodromy lies in `SO_3`; nonisotriviality and the symmetric-square description give the full connected group.

Therefore:

- in the actual rank-3 representation, the connected Zariski closure is `SO_3(C)`;
- abstractly it is `PSL_2(C)`;
- its simply connected cover is `SL_2(C)` acting through `Sym^2`.

So candidates (b) and (c) in the question are not alternatives. Candidate (a), `Sp_4`, belongs to a different rank-4 system.

The compact Sato–Tate form is correspondingly `SO(3)`, or equivalently `SU(2)/{±1}` in the symmetric-square parametrization.

### Answer to Question 2

For the **Apéry K3 Picard–Fuchs equation**, the correct connected algebraic monodromy is

```text
G_Ap^0 = SO_3(C) ≅ PSL_2(C),
```

with `SL_2(C)` as its double cover. It is a rank-3 orthogonal system, not a rank-4 symplectic system.

## 3. Monodromy of `4F3(1/2^4;1^3;x)`

## 3.1 Beukers–Heckman gives `Sp_4`

For the classical order-4 hypergeometric equation, Beukers–Heckman use four numerator and four denominator eigenvalues. The implicit fourth denominator parameter is `1`. Thus

```text
alpha_i = 1/2  for i=1,...,4,
beta_i  = 1    for i=1,...,4,
```

and the corresponding local eigenvalue sets are

```text
{a_i} = {-1,-1,-1,-1},
{b_i} = { 1, 1, 1, 1}.
```

The system is irreducible because no numerator eigenvalue equals a denominator eigenvalue. It is self-dual. The special eigenvalue of the reflection at `x=1` is `c=+1`. Beukers–Heckman, Theorem 6.5, then says that an infinite primitive self-dual hypergeometric group with `c=+1` has Zariski closure

```text
Sp_4(C).
```

The corresponding compact group for Sato–Tate statistics is

```text
USp(4).
```

This is the precise **connected/Zariski monodromy** answer. Beukers–Heckman do not, merely from Theorem 6.5, determine the exact index of the integral monodromy subgroup inside an integral symplectic group; arithmeticity versus thinness is a separate question and is not needed for the Sato–Tate heuristic here.

## 3.2 The special fiber `x=1`

The half-parameter `4F3` belongs to the fourteen rigid hypergeometric Calabi–Yau threefold cases studied by Long–Tu–Yui–Zudilin. At the rigid fiber `x=1`, the associated arithmetic is governed by the weight-4 level-8 modular form

```text
f_8(tau) = eta(2 tau)^4 eta(4 tau)^4.
```

Modulo `p`, the central Apéry coefficient is identified with the corresponding modular trace because its four terminating parameters become `1/2` modulo `p`.

This special modular fiber should not be used to identify every moving coefficient `b_r` with a geometric fiber of the same `x`-family. The full moving row belongs to the Kummer-character family described in Section 0.3.

## 3.3 Relation to the repository's generic rank-4 object

For the central quadratic character `A=A^{-1}`, the character-aspect hypergeometric parameters collapse to the familiar half-parameter pattern after exchanging Katz's two parameter lists and applying the standard normalization. For general `A`, however, the parameters are

```text
A,A,A^{-1},A^{-1},
```

and vary with the character exponent. The generic object is rank 4, while its balanced `t=1` middle-extension stalk in the manuscript has rank 3 and factors as a Tate line plus a rank-2 weight-3 piece.

Thus the following groups must not be conflated:

```text
Apéry K3 period system:                   SO_3 ≅ PSL_2,
classical half-parameter 4F3 system:       Sp_4,
generic Kummer-character hypergeometric:  rank 4, character-dependent,
t=1 special stalk:                        Tate rank 1 + weight-3 rank 2.
```

### Answer to Question 3

Yes. The general Beukers–Heckman monodromy theorem applies directly and gives

```text
G_geom = Sp_4(C)
```

for the irreducible `4F3(1/2^4;1^3;x)` local system, with compact form `USp(4)`. Katz's hypergeometric-sheaf theory provides the finite-field/ell-adic counterpart. This result concerns the rank-4 Calabi–Yau-threefold system, not the rank-3 Apéry K3 system.

## 4. Does Katz–Sarnak predict `|Z_p|` of constant order?

## 4.1 The corrected local-limit heuristic

Suppose for a moment that, as the Kummer character `A` varies, the rank-2 weight-3 trace `a_{p,A}` has a smooth normalized density

```text
rho(t),
qquad t = a_{p,A}/p^(3/2),
qquad |t| <= 2.
```

The event `p|a_{p,A}` means

```text
a_{p,A} = m p
```

for one of `O(sqrt p)` integers `m` in the Weil interval. A lattice local-limit model gives

```text
Pr(a_{p,A}=mp)
   approximately p^(-3/2) rho(m/sqrt p).
```

Summing over the admissible multiples gives

```text
Pr(p|a_{p,A})
  approximately
  sum_{|m|<=2 sqrt p} p^(-3/2) rho(m/sqrt p)
  approximately
  (1/p) integral_{-2}^2 rho(t) dt
  = 1/p.
```

Thus among approximately `p` character labels one expects

```text
E |Z_p| approximately 1,
```

up to local lattice, parity, inverse-character, and exceptional-character factors. Because the palindrome pairs noncentral labels, the more refined heuristic is naturally

```text
|Z_p| approximately 2 * Poisson(1/2)
```

at ordinary primes, exactly as observed in the larger computations already recorded in the repository.

For a rank-4 `USp(4)` trace, the support and density differ, but the same Riemann-sum calculation still gives probability of order `1/p`: there are `O(sqrt p)` multiples of `p`, each with point mass heuristically `O(p^{-3/2})`.

So the **order-of-magnitude prediction** in the question is correct after replacing the Haar-zero-set statement by a lattice local-limit statement.

## 4.2 Why Haar equidistribution alone cannot prove it

Standard Katz–Sarnak equidistribution controls fixed continuous test functions. In a family with about `p` parameters, its natural square-root error is typically of order

```text
O(sqrt p)
```

in an unnormalized count. The expected number of exact mod-`p` zeros is only `O(1)`. The equidistribution error is therefore much larger than the proposed main term.

Equivalently, detecting one congruence class modulo `p` requires a Fourier expansion with defining-characteristic frequencies. The complexity of the test function grows with `p`, so the bounded-conductor estimates underlying ordinary equidistribution no longer apply uniformly.

This is the same logical gap as the distinction between:

```text
normalized traces have a Sato-Tate distribution,
```

and

```text
integer traces are equidistributed in residue classes modulo p.
```

The latter is a local-limit theorem and is strictly stronger.

## 4.3 Constant mean is not a uniform `O(1)` theorem

Even the ideal independent model does **not** predict a deterministic constant upper bound for all primes. A Poisson distribution has unbounded support. It predicts:

- bounded mean and bounded fixed moments;
- exponentially small tails;
- rare primes with `|Z_p|=4,6,8,...`;
- a slowly growing record maximum as the prime range increases.

This agrees with the repository's larger scan, where `Z(p)` takes even values through `12`, and with its `2*Poisson(1/2)` model. Thus the intended Katz–Sarnak prediction should be

```text
average_p |Z_p| = O(1)
```

or convergence of factorial moments, not

```text
sup_p |Z_p| < infinity.
```

## 4.4 A vertical bound would still not prove all-`n` P3.2

Suppose, much more strongly than presently known, that

```text
|Z_p| <= C
```

for every prime. This does not imply

```text
D(n) = # {p in (sqrt n,n] : n mod p in Z_p} = O(1).
```

The first statement is vertical: each prime marks at most `C` residue classes. The second is horizontal: for one fixed integer `n`, how many different primes have marked the particular residue `n mod p`?

An adversarial family can have `|Z_p|=1` for every prime and choose

```text
Z_p = {n_0 mod p}.
```

Then every prime marks the same integer `n_0`. Cardinality and one-prime equidistribution do not exclude such an anchored star. The current P3.2 project has already isolated precisely this cross-prime obstruction.

A uniform `O(1)` bound would substantially sharpen the density-one and first-moment results, but the all-index theorem still requires a horizontal dispersion input.

### Answer to Question 4

The corrected prediction is:

> Big monodromy plus an additional defining-characteristic local-limit principle predicts `Pr(p|b_r)~1/p` for a typical character label, hence `|Z_p|` has constant mean and Poisson-scale fluctuations.

It is **not** obtained by assigning positive Haar measure to the trace-zero locus, and it does not yield a uniform bound or all-`n` P3.2 by itself.

## 5. The exact theorem that would be enough

There are two logically separate missing theorems.

## 5.1 Vertical character-aspect local limit

A one-prime theorem should control the Hasse-zero indicators

```text
H_p(r) = 1_{a_{p,r}=0 mod p} = 1_{b_r=0 mod p}.
```

A useful conjectural hierarchy is:

### V1. Fixed-frequency zero-location equidistribution

For every fixed nonzero integer `m`,

```text
sum_{r mod p-1} H_p(r) exp(2 pi i m r/(p-1))
    = o(|Z_p|)
```

in an averaged prime sense. This would support the observed uniform `alpha` law, but it is not enough for P3.2.

### V2. Local-limit/factorial-moment law

For every fixed `k`, averaged over primes,

```text
(|Z_p|/2)_k -> (1/2)^k
```

away from the exceptional central character, with suitable quantitative errors. This is the `2*Poisson(1/2)` prediction.

### V3. Growing-frequency control

The Fourier estimates for `H_p(r)` must remain nontrivial for frequencies growing with `p`. This is far stronger than weak equidistribution and is the level at which a large-sieve argument becomes possible.

Katz monodromy is a natural source of V1-style macroscopic evidence. A genuinely new `p`-adic local-limit theorem is needed for V2 and V3.

## 5.2 Horizontal two-prime dispersion

For an interval `I`, define

```text
X_p(n) = 1_{n mod p in Z_p},
mu_p   = |Z_p|/p,
g_p(n)  = X_p(n)-mu_p.
```

Let `P<p<=2P` be a dyadic prime block and let `w_p` be either `1` or the logarithmic weight used in the gcd reduction. The exact robust hypothesis is a signed covariance estimate of diagonal size:

```text
| sum_{p != q} w_p w_q
    sum_{n in I} g_p(n) g_q(n) |

    <= C
       sum_p w_p^2
       sum_{n in I} |g_p(n)|^2,
```

uniformly in the P3.2 ranges of `I` and `P`.

Equivalently,

```text
sum_{n in I} |sum_p w_p g_p(n)|^2
    <= (1+C)
       sum_p w_p^2 ||g_p||_2^2.
```

This is the pair-dispersion theorem already visible in the repository's diagonal/off-diagonal decomposition. Together with the vertical mean bounds, the trivial `L^2`-to-`L^infinity` inequality gives a pointwise load far below `n/log n`, hence the all-index P3.2 conclusion.

In the proposed `alpha_p` coordinates for the dominant `|Z_p|=2` primes, this becomes a denominator-sensitive bilinear estimate for

```text
cos(pi h alpha_p) cos(pi k alpha_q)
```

against the Farey/Dirichlet kernel. Ordinary marginal equidistribution of the `alpha_p` does not imply it because the weights depend strongly on `p,q,h,k` and the frequencies grow with the prime scale.

## 5.3 A concise statement of the real target

The theorem one would like to prove is therefore not merely:

```text
"the hypergeometric sheaf has monodromy Sp_4".
```

It is:

> **Character-local-limit plus pair-dispersion theorem.** The Hasse-zero labels of the Apéry Kummer-twist family have Poisson-scale vertical moments and satisfy a uniform two-prime large-sieve/dispersion estimate in incomplete CRT intervals.

That theorem would indeed close P3.2. It would also be a substantial new result in arithmetic geometry and analytic number theory, well beyond standard Katz–Sarnak equidistribution.

## 6. Direct answers in compact form

### Question 1

**No, not as a continuous geometric evaluation family in `alpha`.** The exact moving parameter is a Kummer character exponent. For a fixed rational `alpha=a/b` and primes `p=1 mod b`, one can plausibly define a fixed-order hypergeometric compatible system with cyclotomic coefficients, but the full row uses character orders growing with `p`. Katz's Mellin framework applies conceptually, yet ordinary equidistribution does not count or locate exact mod-`p` zeros.

### Question 2

For the rank-3 Apéry K3 Picard–Fuchs equation,

```text
G_mono^0 = SO_3(C) ≅ PSL_2(C),
```

and the system is the symmetric square of a rank-2 system with simply connected group `SL_2(C)`. Thus `SO(3)` and `SL(2) via Sym^2` are the same answer at different representation levels. `Sp_4` is not the Apéry K3 group.

### Question 3

For

```text
_4F_3(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2;1,1,1;x),
```

Beukers–Heckman gives connected Zariski monodromy

```text
Sp_4(C),
```

with compact form `USp(4)`. The rigid fiber at `x=1` is related to the weight-4 level-8 modular form. This is a rank-4 Calabi–Yau-threefold system distinct from the rank-3 Apéry K3 equation.

### Question 4

The exact trace-zero hypersurface has Haar measure zero. A separate lattice local-limit heuristic gives

```text
Pr(p divides weight-3 trace) ~ 1/p,
```

because there are `O(sqrt p)` admissible multiples of `p`, each heuristically having mass `O(p^{-3/2})`. Hence one predicts constant mean for `|Z_p|`, plausibly `2*Poisson(1/2)`. Standard Katz equidistribution has error much larger than the `O(1)` main term and does not prove this. Nor would a uniform vertical bound alone imply the fixed-`n` divisor bound.

## Bottom line

The new idea points to the correct hidden variable—**the Kummer-character label**—and Katz's Mellin-transform theory is the correct conceptual umbrella. But the decisive implication

```text
big monodromy
  => exact mod-p zero count of constant order
  => all-n P3.2
```

has two invalid arrows.

The defensible chain is

```text
big monodromy
  => macroscopic character-aspect Sato-Tate heuristic
  + new defining-characteristic local limit
  => Poisson-scale vertical zero statistics
  + new two-prime CRT dispersion
  => all-n P3.2.
```

The two new statements after big monodromy—not the monodromy computation itself—are the genuine frontier.
