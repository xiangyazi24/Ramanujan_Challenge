# Q84: Does the Goldbach second moment become automatic?

## Short answer

No. The Goldbach reformulation is genuinely a major simplification, but the second moment is **not automatically bounded** by CRT independence. The subtle point is that the events

\[
X_r=1_{p_r\mid b_r},\qquad p_r=m-r,
\]

are not random variables living on independent probability spaces. The primes \(p_r\) and \(p_s\) are different, but the quantities \(b_r\) and \(b_s\) are deterministic values of the same Apéry recurrence. There is no literal probabilistic independence theorem coming from \(p_r\neq p_s\).

The missing input is weaker than the original cross-prime problem, but it is still a genuine arithmetic statement.

---

## 1. Why the argument looks automatic

The heuristic is very compelling.

For
\[
p=m-r,
\qquad q=m-s,
\]
with \(r\neq s\), we want
\[
\Pr(p\mid b_r,\ q\mid b_s).
\]

The expected sizes are
\[
\Pr(p\mid b_r)\approx \frac{|Z_p|}{p},
\]
and similarly
\[
\Pr(q\mid b_s)\approx \frac{|Z_q|}{q}.
\]

Since \(p\neq q\), it is tempting to write
\[
\Pr(p\mid b_r,q\mid b_s)
\approx
\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\frac{|Z_q|}{q}.
\]

If this were true, then
\[
\sum_{r<s}E(X_rX_s)
\ll
\sum_{r<s}\frac1{(m-r)(m-s)}
=O(1),
\]
and the second moment would follow.

But the word "probability" hides the difficulty.

---

## 2. CRT independence is not enough

CRT says that for a **single integer** \(N\), the conditions
\[
N\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad
N\equiv0\pmod q
\]
are independent when \(p\neq q\) if \(N\) is sampled uniformly modulo \(pq\).

Here we have instead:

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad
b_s\equiv0\pmod q.
\]

The pair \((b_r,b_s)\) is not uniformly distributed modulo \(pq\). It is generated by a deterministic second-order recurrence.

The recurrence gives a map
\[
(b_0,b_1)\mapsto(b_r,b_s),
\]
but there is no theorem saying this map is equidistributed in
\[
\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_q.
\]

That is exactly the missing independence statement.

---

## 3. Why the large common gcd issue is not the main obstruction

The observation about
\[
\gcd(b_r,b_s)
\]
is important but not decisive.

A common prime divisor \(\ell\mid b_r,b_s\) means
\[
r,s\in Z_\ell.
\]

This can happen because \(|Z_\ell|>1\).

However the Goldbach events use
\[
p=m-r,
\qquad q=m-s,
\]
not the common divisor \(\ell\).

So large gcds between Apéry numbers do not directly destroy the Goldbach reduction.

The relevant question is instead:

> For distinct primes \(p,q\), how correlated are the two recurrence orbits
> \(b_r\pmod p\) and \(b_s\pmod q\)?

---

## 4. What would be sufficient

A sufficient statement is:

\[
\sum_{\substack{r,s<m/2\\r\neq s}}
\left|
\Pr(p_r\mid b_r,p_s\mid b_s)
-\Pr(p_r\mid b_r)\Pr(p_s\mid b_s)
\right|
=o(1).
\]

This is a two-prime correlation estimate.

It is much weaker than the original Mellin independence conjecture because:

* the tested primes are tied to the indices by \(p=m-r\);
* each prime occurs only once;
* we only need second moments, not arbitrary CRT tuples.

But it is not automatic.

---

## 5. A more realistic route

The correct replacement for naive CRT independence is a **two-character correlation theorem**.

Define
\[
Y_p(r)=1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}.
\]

Use additive characters:

\[
Y_p(r)=\frac1p\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}e_p(tb_r).
\]

Then the pair correlation becomes a double exponential sum:

\[
\frac1{pq}\sum_{u,v}
\sum_{?}
 e_p(ub_r)e_q(vb_s).
\]

The required theorem would be a bound showing cancellation whenever both frequencies are nonzero.

This is a genuinely smaller version of the Mellin/crystalline problem.

---

## 6. The promising Apéry-specific ingredient

The feature unavailable for the central binomial coefficients is the second-order dynamical system.

Modulo a prime \(p\), the Apéry sequence is an orbit in
\[
\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_p)
\]
under transfer matrices
\[
M(n)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(n)/(n+1)^3&-n^3/(n+1)^3\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For different primes \(p,q\), the two systems are different finite dynamical systems.

The plausible theorem is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The product orbit on }\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_p)\times\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_q)
\text{ is equidistributed for large }p,q.
}
\]

Large monodromy should imply this.

This is exactly the Goldbach analogue of the Mellin independence conjecture, but only for pairs of primes.

---

## 7. Final conclusion

The Goldbach reduction is **not solved by CRT alone**.

The correct status is:

* The reduction is substantially easier than the original problem.
* Adjacent indices have genuine independence from the Casoratian.
* Small-prime gcd phenomena are irrelevant for the top-window primes.
* Distinct primes do not automatically imply independent Apéry recurrence values.
* What remains is a two-prime orbit-correlation theorem.

The likely breakthrough is not a bound on \(\gcd(b_r,b_s)\), but proving that the pair of finite-field Apéry recurrence orbits has square-root cancellation.

A successful theorem of the form

\[
\left|
\#\{(r,s):b_r=0\pmod p,b_s=0\pmod q\}
-rac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}N^2
\right|
\ll N^{1+o(1)}
\]

would immediately give the Goldbach second moment and would be a realistic first target for the full cross-prime independence program.
