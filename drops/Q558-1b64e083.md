# Q558 — Palindromic symmetry and the factor 2 in the Apéry transfer-matrix zero density

## Summary

The proposed explanation is essentially correct, but the precise statement is slightly different: the factor 2 is not caused by the sign in the Apéry reflection formula (the sign is irrelevant for zeros), but by the fact that the involution on ordered zero pairs exchanges the two half-intervals. The full T12 zero count is the union of two copies of the forward fundamental domain, with only O(p) possible fixed points/boundary effects.

## 1. Palindromic doubling

Let

\[
S_p=\{(r,h):1\le r\le p-2,\ 2\le h\le p-1-r,\ K_{h-1}(r+1)=0\pmod p\}.
\]

Since

\[
T_{12}(r,r+h)=-B(r)K_{h-1}(r+1),
\]

and B(r) is nonzero for 1 <= r <= p-2, this is exactly the set of pairs of Apéry zeros

\[
b_r=b_{r+h}=0.
\]

The reflection

\[
\iota(r,h)=(p-1-r-h,h)
\]

maps a zero pair to another zero pair with the same gap h. Therefore the full range decomposes into reflection orbits. Away from fixed points, every orbit has size 2.

The fixed point condition is

\[
r=p-1-r-h,
\]

or

\[
2r+h=p-1.
\]

These contribute only a lower-order correction. Hence

\[
|S_p|=2|S_p^+|+O(p),
\]

where S_p^+ is one representative from each reflection orbit.

Therefore the observed ratio 2.007 is exactly what the palindromic symmetry predicts.

## 2. Correct forward fundamental domain

Q543 used the equivalent endpoint formulation:

\[
1\le r<s\le (p-1)/2.
\]

Writing s=r+h gives

\[
1\le r,
\qquad r+h\le (p-1)/2.
\]

The upper endpoint depends on parity. For odd p,

\[
(p-1)/2
\]

is an integer. This is the clean forward half.

It is not merely the condition r<s<(p-3)/2. The correct condition is that both endpoints lie in the same reflected half:

\[
S_p^+=\{(r,h):1\le r<r+h\le (p-1)/2\}.
\]

This matches Q543's clean forward pairs.

## 3. Exact Apéry reflection sign

For the Apéry numbers

\[
b_n=\sum_k\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\]

one has the congruence

\[
\boxed{b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p}.
\]

There is no factor (-1)^r for this sequence.

The frequently occurring sign twist belongs to other hypergeometric families where the Frobenius eigenvalue has a nontrivial determinant character. The Apéry zeta(3) sequence has the untwisted palindromic symmetry.

For the zero set, even a sign twist would not matter because

\[
(-1)^r b_r=0 \iff b_r=0.
\]

## 4. Correct density statement

The proposed theorem needs a small correction in the normalization. The valid pair count is

\[
N_p=\sum_{r=1}^{p-2}(p-2-r)=\frac{(p-2)(p-3)}2.
\]

The empirical full-range theorem should be written as

\[
\boxed{|S_p|=\frac{2}{p+1}N_p+O(\sqrt p\,p^{o(1)})}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{|S_p|=\frac{(p-2)(p-3)}{p+1}+O(\sqrt p\,p^{o(1)})}.
\]

The forward half is

\[
\boxed{|S_p^+|=\frac{1}{p+1}N_p^++O(\sqrt p\,p^{o(1)})}
\]

where

\[
N_p^+=\#\{(r,h):1\le r<r+h\le(p-1)/2\}
=\frac{(p-3)(p-1)}8+O(1).
\]

Thus

\[
|S_p^+|\sim \frac{p^2}{8(p+1)}\sim\frac p8,
\]

and the full count is twice this:

\[
|S_p|\sim\frac p4.
\]

## 5. Expected error term from monodromy

The O(\sqrt p) term is the natural expectation if the continuant family is controlled by a geometrically irreducible l-adic sheaf with bounded conductor.

More precisely, Deligne-type estimates for trace functions give square-root cancellation in one-dimensional parameter sums:

\[
O(C\sqrt p).
\]

However, proving the stated theorem requires more than the pointwise trace estimate because the parameter space is two-dimensional (r,h). One needs a two-variable equidistribution statement for the continuant/Frobenius family.

Therefore the rigorous target should be phrased as

\[
|S_p|-\frac{2N_p}{p+1}=O(p^{1/2+\epsilon})
\]

rather than claiming a proved O(\sqrt p) before the monodromy and independence estimates are established.

## Conclusion

The empirical factor of 2 has a clean explanation:

1. Q543 counted only the forward half of the palindromic orbit space.
2. The full T12 range contains both halves.
3. The reflection involution

\[
(r,h)\mapsto(p-1-r-h,h)
\]

pairs them.
4. Fixed points contribute only lower-order corrections.

The correct density is therefore:

\[
\text{forward: }\frac1{p+1},
\qquad
\text{full: }\frac2{p+1}.
\]
