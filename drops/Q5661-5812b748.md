# Apéry large-prime vanishing: exact modular/Sturm audit and the two-characteristic barrier

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
R(n)=\prod_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}p.
\]

I do **not** obtain a proof that

\[
\log R(n)=o(n)
\tag{0.1}
\]

for every `n`. I do obtain an exact modular/Sturm formulation and a precise no-go for the standard Cartier, Hasse, theta-cycle, Sturm, and one-field trace-function routes.

The strongest conclusions are these.

1. For `p>sqrt(n)`, write `n=qp+r`. The p-Lucas congruence gives
   \[
   b_n\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
   \]
   The total logarithmic weight of the upper-digit channel `p|b_q` is
   \[
   O\!\left(n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3}+n^{1/2}\log n\right)=o(n).
   \]
   Hence the hard channel is `p|b_{n mod p}`.

2. The classical level-6 parametrization makes `b_n` the constant term of a weakly holomorphic weight-4 modular form with pole order `n`. Clearing the pole gives a holomorphic form of weight `2n+4` and safe `Gamma_0(6)` Sturm bound `2n+4`. However, this holomorphic form always has constant coefficient `1`; `p|b_n` is one moving linear functional of its coefficients, not a Sturm-prefix condition.

3. For every prime `p>=5` and residue `0<=r<p`, there is an exact p-integral modular form
   \[
   Q_{p,r}\in M_{2(p-1)}(\Gamma_0(6);\mathbb Z_{(p)})
   \]
   such that
   \[
   Q_{p,r}\equiv4b_r\Phi^rZ^{p-1-r}\pmod p.
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
   p\mid b_r
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   Q_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod p,
   \]
   and the exact safe Sturm bound is `2(p-1)`.

4. This local Sturm criterion does **not** amplify targetness: every coefficient of `Q_{p,r} mod p` is a scalar multiple of the same residue `b_r`, and the coefficient of `q^r` is exactly `4b_r`.

5. The required theta projector has provably minimal order `p-1`. Thus no bounded-order theta cycle can select an arbitrary coefficient of the first Hasse block.

6. Cartier and Dwork are locally rank one:
   \[
   \Lambda_{p,r}F\equiv b_rF\pmod p.
   \]
   Since `F(0)=1`, the ideal generated by the complete Cartier section is exactly `(b_r)`. It supplies no second local target equation.

7. Directly batching the p-specific forms has total weight
   \[
   \sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}2(p-1)
   =(1+o(1))\frac{n^2}{\log n},
   \]
   and total Hauptmodul degree
   \[
   \left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac{n^2}{\log n}.
   \]
   Standard Sturm, determinant, and resultant height bounds are therefore far above `exp(o(n))`.

8. The exact two-characteristic theorem sufficient for (0.1) is the shell pair-energy estimate
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sum_{N<n\le2N}(K(n))_2
   =o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right),
   }
   \tag{PE}
   \]
   where
   \[
   K(n)=\#\{\sqrt n<p\le n:b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p\}.
   \]
   The diagonal first moment is already `N^(5/3+o(1))` from the proved vertical bound `|Z_p|<<p^(2/3)`. Thus `(PE)` isolates the missing cross-characteristic correlation.

9. Katz varies characters in one fixed finite field. Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel fix one modulus and sum a trace function over prime arguments. Here the summation prime is itself the changing residue characteristic and only one moving character is sampled in each field. Even assuming the conjecturally optimal `|Z_p|=O(1)`, the classical additive large sieve gives only `O(N^2)`, missing the required `log^2 N` saving.

So the modular parametrization does not fail for lack of structure. It fails because the available structure is one-characteristic and coefficient-local, whereas P3.2 requires two-characteristic nonalignment.

## 1. Reduce to the moving lower digit

The Apéry numbers satisfy the p-Lucas law

\[
b_{ap+r}\equiv b_ab_r\pmod p
\qquad(0\le r<p).
\tag{1.1}
\]

For `p>sqrt(n)`, write

\[
n=qp+r,
\qquad
1\le q<\sqrt n,
\qquad
0\le r<p.
\]

Then

\[
p\mid b_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_qb_r.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The two factors have very different pointwise behavior.

### Proposition 1.1 — the upper-digit channel is harmless

Let

\[
U(n)=\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}}}\log p.
\]

Then

\[
U(n)
\ll n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3}+n^{1/2}\log n
=o(n).
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Proof

For fixed `q`, the primes with `floor(n/p)=q` lie in

\[
I_q=\left(\frac n{q+1},\frac nq\right].
\]

Their logarithmic weight subject to `p|b_q` is at most

\[
\log b_q\ll q,
\tag{1.4}
\]

using the standard Apéry growth `log b_q=O(q)`. It is also at most

\[
\left(\frac n{q(q+1)}+1\right)\log n.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Choose

\[
Q=(n\log n)^{1/3}.
\]

Use (1.4) for `q<=Q` and (1.5) for `Q<q<sqrt(n)`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
U(n)
&\ll \sum_{q\le Q}q
 +\log n\sum_{Q<q<\sqrt n}
 \left(\frac n{q^2}+1\right)\\
&\ll Q^2+\frac{n\log n}{Q}+\sqrt n\log n,
\end{aligned}
\]

which is (1.3). ∎

Define the remaining count

\[
K(n)=\#\{\sqrt n<p\le n:p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Since

\[
\frac12\log n<\log p\le\log n
\]

throughout this interval, (0.1) is equivalent, after Proposition 1.1, to

\[
K(n)=o\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right).
\tag{1.7}
\]

## 2. Exact modular parametrization and coefficient extraction

Set `q=e^(2 pi i tau)` and

\[
t(\tau)=
\left(
\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau)}
     {\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau)}
\right)^{12},
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
Z(\tau)=
\frac{\eta(2\tau)^7\eta(3\tau)^7}
     {\eta(\tau)^5\eta(6\tau)^5}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The classical Apéry parametrization is

\[
Z(\tau)=F(t(\tau))
       =\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt(\tau)^n.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Here `t` is a modular function for `Gamma_0(6)+w_6`, `Z` is a weight-2 modular form, and

\[
t=q+O(q^2),
\qquad
Z=1+O(q).
\]

Write

\[
D=q\frac d{dq}.
\]

The eta quotient gives

\[
D\log t
=\frac12\bigl(
E_2(\tau)+6E_2(6\tau)
-2E_2(2\tau)-3E_2(3\tau)
\bigr).
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is a meromorphic weight-2 modular form: it can have a logarithmic pole at the unique pole of `t`. The zero of `Z` at that point cancels this pole in the products used below.

### 2.1 Constant-term formula

Changing residues from `t` to `q` gives

\[
\boxed{
 b_n=\operatorname{CT}_q
 \left(
  Z(\tau)\frac{Dt(\tau)}{t(\tau)^{n+1}}
 \right).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Define

\[
H_n(\tau)=Z(\tau)\frac{Dt(\tau)}{t(\tau)^{n+1}}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Then `H_n` is weakly holomorphic of weight `4`, has pole order exactly `n` at infinity, and

\[
b_n=\operatorname{CT}_qH_n.
\tag{2.7}
\]

This is a genuine characteristic-zero modular representation of the fixed coefficient, but its distinguished scalar is still `b_n` itself.

### 2.2 Clear the pole and test Sturm

Put

\[
\Phi(\tau)=t(\tau)Z(\tau)
=\frac{\eta(\tau)^7\eta(6\tau)^7}
       {\eta(2\tau)^5\eta(3\tau)^5}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

It is a holomorphic weight-2 modular form on `Gamma_0(6)` with

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty\Phi=1,
\qquad
\Phi=q+O(q^2).
\]

It need not vanish at every cusp; only its order at infinity is used here.

Now

\[
J_n:=\Phi^nH_n
=Z^{n+1}D\log t
\in M_{2n+4}(\Gamma_0(6)).
\tag{2.9}
\]

Holomorphy at the pole of `t` follows because one factor of `Z` cancels the logarithmic pole of `D log t`; the remaining factors are holomorphic.

The index is

\[
[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_0(6)]
=6\left(1+\frac12\right)\left(1+\frac13\right)
=12.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Thus the safe Sturm bound for weight `k` on `Gamma_0(6)` is `k`, and

\[
\boxed{B_6(J_n)=2n+4.}
\tag{2.11}
\]

This does not turn `p|b_n` into a Sturm-prefix condition. Indeed,

\[
J_n=1+O(q)
\tag{2.12}
\]

for every `n`, while

\[
\boxed{
 b_n=[q^n]\left[
 J_n(q)\left(\frac q{\Phi(q)}\right)^n
 \right].
}
\tag{2.13}
\]

The factor `(q/Phi)^n` is an integral formal series but is not a holomorphic modular form. Therefore `p|b_n` is one moving linear functional on the coefficients of `J_n`; it is not vanishing of its first `2n+5` Fourier coefficients.

### 2.3 A genuine fixed-weight obstruction

If a nonzero holomorphic modular form `M` on `Gamma_0(6)` of weight `k` vanishes to order at least `n` at infinity, the valence formula gives

\[
n\le
\frac{k}{12}[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_0(6)]
=k.
\tag{2.14}
\]

Hence any holomorphic pole-clearing multiplier must have weight at least `n`. The explicit `Phi^n` has weight `2n`.

This rules out a fixed-weight Sturm proof. It does not rule out a new signed cancellation in a growing weight space.

## 3. The exact p-dependent Hasse/Frobenius polynomial

For every prime `p>=5`, Beukers–Tsai–Ye define

\[
G_p(\tau)=
\frac{
 \sum_{d\parallel6}d^{p-1}E_{p-1}(d\tau)^2
}{Z(\tau)^{p-1}},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `d` runs through the Hall divisors of `6`. It is a polynomial in `t`:

\[
G_p(\tau)=P_p(t(\tau)),
\qquad
\deg P_p\le p-1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For `p>=5`, the number of Hall divisors contributes the unit factor `4`, and

\[
G_p\equiv4Z^{1-p}\pmod p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Dwork/Lucas gives

\[
Z^{1-p}\equiv
\frac{F(t)}{F(t^p)}
\equiv
A_p(t):=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j
\pmod p.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
P_p(t)\equiv4A_p(t)
=4\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j
\pmod p.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

The Hasse/Frobenius object controls the whole first block. The target event is a coefficient zero of `P_p`, not a root of `P_p(t)` in the modular parameter.

## 4. A degree-optimal theta projector and an exact local Sturm theorem

Let

\[
\Theta_t=t\frac d{dt}.
\]

For `0<=r<p`, set

\[
\Pi_{p,r}(X)=1-(X-r)^{p-1}\in\mathbb F_p[X].
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `0<=j<p`, Fermat gives

\[
\Pi_{p,r}(j)=
\begin{cases}
1,&j=r,\\
0,&j\ne r.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently, for every polynomial of degree at most `p-1`,

\[
\Pi_{p,r}(\Theta_t)
\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}c_jt^j\right)
=c_rt^r.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Apply this to `P_p`. Define the characteristic-zero p-integral lift

\[
Q_{p,r}(\tau)
=Z(\tau)^{p-1}
 \Pi_{p,r}(\Theta_t)P_p(t(\tau)).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since `Theta_t` preserves polynomial degree, this is a linear combination of

\[
t^jZ^{p-1}=\Phi^jZ^{p-1-j},
\qquad0\le j\le p-1.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus

\[
Q_{p,r}\in
M_{2(p-1)}(\Gamma_0(6);\mathbb Z_{(p)}).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Modulo `p`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_{p,r}\equiv
4b_rt^rZ^{p-1}
=4b_r\Phi^rZ^{p-1-r}\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

The second factor is nonzero, so

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Its safe `Gamma_0(6)` Sturm bound is

\[
\boxed{B_6(Q_{p,r})=2(p-1).}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This is a fully explicit use of Sturm, not a dismissal of it.

### 4.1 Sturm supplies no second equation

Because

\[
\Phi^rZ^{p-1-r}=q^r+O(q^{r+1}),
\]

the coefficient of `q^r` in `Q_{p,r} mod p` is exactly `4b_r`. Every later coefficient is another fixed scalar multiple of `b_r`.

Hence the ideal generated by all Fourier coefficients in the Sturm range is exactly the one-dimensional target ideal `(b_r)`. The Sturm test repackages the coefficient condition perfectly, but does not strengthen it.

### 4.2 The projector order is minimal

#### Lemma 4.1

If `R(X) in F_p[X]` satisfies

\[
R(r)=1,
\qquad
R(j)=0\quad(j\ne r),
\]

for all `j in F_p`, then

\[
\deg R\ge p-1.
\]

#### Proof

`R` has `p-1` distinct roots and is nonzero. ∎

Thus `Pi_(p,r)` is degree-optimal. Any universal polynomial in `Theta_t` that selects one arbitrary coefficient of the Hasse block must have order at least `p-1`.

This is the exact obstruction to a bounded theta cycle.

## 5. Cartier operators and Dwork congruences are locally rank one

For a formal series `f(t)=sum a_mt^m`, define

\[
\Lambda_{p,r}f
=\sum_{m\ge0}a_{pm+r}t^m.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The p-Lucas law yields

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{p,r}F\equiv b_rF\pmod p.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `F(0)=1`, the ideal generated by all coefficients of the complete Cartier section is

\[
\left(\operatorname{coeffs}\Lambda_{p,r}F\right)
=(b_r)
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The ordinary Cartier operator is the `r=0` case:

\[
\mathcal C_pF\equiv F\pmod p.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The Dwork quotient is

\[
\frac{F(t)}{F(t^p)}\equiv A_p(t)\pmod p,
\tag{5.5}
\]

and Mellit–Vlasenko refine such quotients modulo higher powers of `p`. These are powerful one-characteristic congruences, but (5.2) proves that their first layer contains only the same scalar target condition.

### 5.1 The mod-p differential equation does not mark a coefficient

The Apéry differential operator is

\[
\mathcal L
=\theta^3
-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3,
\qquad
\theta=t\frac d{dt}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mathcal L
=\theta^3
-t(34\theta^3+51\theta^2+27\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The Hasse block is the normalized first Frobenius quotient and its interior coefficients satisfy the Apéry recurrence. But `b_r=0` is one coordinate hyperplane in that polynomial solution. It does not force a new singularity, a common root, reducibility, or a dimension drop.

A root resultant for `P_p(t)` therefore detects the wrong event. A coefficient resultant must first implement the degree-`p-1` projector from Section 4.

## 6. Hasse invariants and theta cycles: precise growth

The ordinary modular theta operator is `D=q d/dq`. Modulo `p`, applying `D` to a form of weight `k` has a modular lift of weight `k+p+1`, subject to the usual filtration drops. Iterating `p-1` times has formal weight increase

\[
(p-1)(p+1)=p^2-1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The optimized construction in Section 4 avoids this quadratic formal weight by acting on the degree-`p-1` modular function `P_p(t)` and clearing the denominator once. It reaches weight `2(p-1)`. Nevertheless, Lemma 4.1 shows that the interpolation order remains linear in `p`.

Filtration drops cannot evade the algebraic lower bound: separating one eigenvalue from all `p-1` others requires a degree-`p-1` polynomial in the eigenvalue.

### 6.1 Explicit p-dependent Hasse polynomial

The repository’s audited Gross–Koblitz reduction gives

\[
\mathscr H_p(X)
=\sum_{m=0}^{(p-1)/2}
 \frac{
  \prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(X-j(j+1))^2
 }{(m!)^4}
\in\mathbb F_p[X]
\tag{6.2}
\]

with

\[
b_r\equiv\mathscr H_p(r(r+1))\pmod p.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The displayed polynomial has degree at most `p-1`; it changes with `p`. This converts targetness into an evaluation/root condition, but not one for a fixed characteristic-zero polynomial.

Generic ordinarity does not imply a bounded nonordinary locus. The classical Legendre family is generically ordinary while its Deuring Hasse polynomial has degree `(p-1)/2`. Therefore Hasse-invariant theory alone gives neither a bounded zero fibre nor cross-prime coupling.

## 7. Standard characteristic-zero batching has the wrong height

### 7.1 The fixed weakly holomorphic carrier

The direct carrier is

\[
\operatorname{CT}H_n=b_n.
\]

The Apéry asymptotic gives

\[
\log b_n
=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus the obvious characteristic-zero modular scalar has positive linear logarithmic height.

A signed Faber-basis combination in the `Theta(n)`-dimensional pole space could in principle cancel the leading growth, but ordinary Sturm, determinant, and coefficient estimates do not prove such a cancellation. It would be a new theorem about the distinguished Apéry initial state.

### 7.2 Product of all local projector forms

The total weight is

\[
\begin{aligned}
W(n)
&=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}2(p-1)\\
&=(1+o(1))\frac{n^2}{\log n},
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

using

\[
\sum_{p\le x}p\sim\frac{x^2}{2\log x}.
\]

The total polynomial degree is

\[
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}(p-1)
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac{n^2}{\log n}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Every coefficient of the product is divisible by each target prime, but standard fixed-level modular-form bounds now operate at weight `Theta(n^2/log n)`. They do not yield a nonzero integer of height `exp(o(n))`.

### 7.3 Resultants

The polynomials `P_p` and `H_p` live in different residue characteristics and have degree `Theta(p)`. Choosing characteristic-zero lifts and multiplying or taking resultants reproduces the degree scale (7.3). Moreover, a root resultant misses coefficient vanishing; including the coefficient projector restores the minimal degree `p-1`.

### Scope

This is a no-go for the **standard constructions**:

- fixed-pole clearing plus Sturm;
- universal Hasse root counting;
- bounded theta cycles;
- the universal coefficient projector;
- direct products of local projector forms;
- and ordinary determinant/resultant height estimates.

It is not a theorem excluding a new Apéry-specific modular cancellation.

## 8. Mellin traces and the changing-character obstruction

The diagonal/finite-hypergeometric realization gives, for each fixed `p`, a finite Mellin trace representation

\[
b_r
=\operatorname{Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)(\omega_p^r)
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p,
\tag{8.1}
\]

up to an explicit nonzero normalization. Here `omega_p` is the Teichmüller character and `K_p` is obtained from the fixed Apéry diagonal/K3 object.

The repository’s geometric analysis identifies the primitive Mellin cohomology as rank `2`, weight `3`, and symplectic. The negative quantifier analysis below needs only the exact finite Mellin identity and bounded local complexity.

If `n=qp+r`, then

\[
r=n-qp\equiv n-q\pmod{p-1},
\tag{8.2}
\]

so the selected character is

\[
\chi_{p,n,q}=\omega_p^{n-q}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

For fixed `n,q`, its order is

\[
d_{p,n,q}
=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-q)}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

This order and its coefficient field generally grow with `p`.

### 8.1 Katz has vertical, not horizontal, quantifiers

Katz fixes one finite field and sheaf, then varies the character. This controls

\[
r\mapsto
\operatorname{Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)(\omega_p^r)
\]

for one `p`. Our problem fixes `n`, selects one character, and changes the field:

\[
p\mapsto
\operatorname{Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)
 (\omega_p^{n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor}).
\]

Perfect equidistribution in every row does not prevent the selected zero in every row from aligning on one integer column.

Exact vanishing is also a shrinking p-adic condition, not a fixed continuous class function of the complex Sato–Tate class. Over `F_p`,

\[
\mathbf1_{x=0}=1-x^{p-1},
\tag{8.5}
\]

so the exact zero detector itself has degree growing with `p`.

### 8.2 Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel has the wrong arity

Their prime-sum trace-function theorem fixes a field `F_P` and a trace function `K` on it, then sums

\[
\sum_{\substack{\ell\le X\\\ell\text{ prime}}}K(\ell).
\]

The modulus `P` is fixed while the prime argument `ell` varies.

Here the sampled prime is the modulus:

\[
p\mapsto K_p(n\bmod p).
\]

There is one selected point in each of many fields, not many arguments in one field. The completion step and Deligne bound do not cross this arity mismatch.

Even a hypothetical fixed compatible system leaves a second wall: the condition is reduction of the trace modulo the same varying prime `p`, not a conjugacy condition in a fixed finite quotient suitable for ordinary Chebotarev.

## 9. Exact two-characteristic theorem sufficient for P3.2

Define

\[
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}
\tag{9.1}
\]

and

\[
K(n)=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}
\mathbf1_{Z_p}(n\bmod p).
\tag{9.2}
\]

The exact requested pair theorem is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{N<n\le2N}(K(n))_2
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
}
\tag{PE}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\ne\ell}}
\#\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
N<n\le2N,\\
\sqrt n<p,\ell\le n,\\
n\bmod p\in Z_p,\\
n\bmod\ell\in Z_\ell
\end{array}
\right\}
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{9.3}
\]

In trace language, the last two conditions are the simultaneous congruences

\[
\operatorname{Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)
 (\omega_p^{n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor})\equiv0\pmod p
\]

and the analogous congruence at `ell`.

### Proposition 9.1 — `(PE)` closes the all-n problem

For any `n in (N,2N]`,

\[
(K(n))_2
\le
\sum_{N<m\le2N}(K(m))_2
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\]

Hence

\[
K(n)=o(N/\log N)=o(n/\log n)
\]

uniformly. The lower-digit radical has logarithm at most `K(n) log n=o(n)`. Proposition 1.1 handles the upper-digit channel. ∎

### 9.1 The diagonal term is already proved

The repository proves

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

For each `p`, a residue in `Z_p` occurs at most `N/p+1` times in a shell of length `N`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{N<n\le2N}K(n)
&\ll
\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le2N}
\left(\frac Np+1\right)|Z_p|\\
&\ll N^{5/3+o(1)}\\
&=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.5}
\]

Thus the diagonal contribution in `sum K(n)^2` is harmless. The missing input is exactly the cross-prime sum (9.3).

## 10. The classical large sieve stops at `O(N^2)`

Let

\[
\delta_p(x)=\mathbf1_{Z_p}(x\bmod p),
\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{|Z_p|}{p},
\qquad
\delta_p^\circ=\delta_p-\rho_p.
\]

Its Fourier expansion is

\[
\delta_p^\circ(x)
=\frac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}
\widehat\delta_p(a)e_p(ax),
\tag{10.1}
\]

where

\[
\widehat\delta_p(a)=\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(-az).
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}
\left|\frac{\widehat\delta_p(a)}p\right|^2
=rac{|Z_p|}{p}-\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p^2}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

The additive large sieve for the reduced fractions `a/p`, with `p<=Q` and `Q asymp N`, gives only

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}
\left|
 \sum_{p\le Q}\delta_p^\circ(n)
\right|^2
\ll
(N+Q^2)
\sum_{p\le Q}
\left(
\frac{|Z_p|}{p}-\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p^2}
\right).
\tag{10.3}
\]

Even if one assumes the conjecturally optimal vertical bound

\[
|Z_p|=O(1),
\]

the coefficient norm over `sqrt(N)<p<=2N` is `O(1)`. Thus (10.3) gives only

\[
O(N^2),
\tag{10.4}
\]

where `(PE)` needs `o(N^2/log^2 N)`.

So the ordinary large sieve misses a full `log^2 N` factor even under ideal vertical input. The current `p^(2/3)` vertical bound makes the generic estimate much worse.

The geometric source of the `Q^2` barrier is also exact: for `p,ell>sqrt(N)`, the CRT modulus `p ell` is at least the shell scale. A residue pair is sampled zero or only a bounded number of times, rather than over a complete CRT period.

Any successful two-characteristic theorem must therefore exploit arithmetic special to the Apéry zero sets, not merely spacing of rational frequencies.

## 12. What would genuinely reopen the route

### 12.1 Characteristic-zero modular cancellation

One needs an initial-state-specific construction of nonzero integers `C_n` such that every target prime divides `C_n` and

\[
\log|C_n|=o(n).
\]

The new theorem cannot be another universal Hasse projector, ordinary Sturm prefix, or direct resultant: their exact degree and weight costs are already determined above. It would have to prove an Archimedean cancellation in a growing level-6 modular-form space tied to the distinguished Apéry solution `b_0=1,b_1=5`.

A concrete possible shape is a signed Faber-basis functional whose modular value has subexponential height and to which all selected local projector ideals map. No such identity is presently known or present in the repository.

### 12.2 Two-characteristic Apéry large sieve

Prove `(PE)`, or directly

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}
\left|
 \sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}
 \mathbf1_{{\rm Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)
 (\omega_p^{n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor})\equiv0\ (p)}
\right|^2
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{12.1}
\]

The exact novelty required is a logarithmic saving beyond the classical `Q^2` barrier in a family where:

- the residue characteristic varies with the summation prime;
- the selected character order is unbounded;
- the exact zero detector has degree `p-1`;
- and the shell is shorter than the pairwise CRT moduli.

No standard Katz, Deligne, Chebotarev, Fouvry–Michel, or Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel theorem has these quantifiers.

## 13. Final assessment

The modular route reaches a sharp boundary:

- **Locally**, targetness has an exact weight-`2(p-1)` Sturm test, but the entire Sturm ideal is principal and generated by `b_r`.
- **Globally**, the fixed coefficient has an exact weight-4 weak modular representation, but pole clearing forces growing weight and does not convert one coefficient into a Sturm prefix.
- **Across primes**, the Hasse polynomial, theta projector, Mellin character, coefficient field, and residue characteristic all vary with `p`.
- **Analytically**, the classical large sieve is critical even under ideal vertical zero counts.

The strongest exact progress here is therefore not a claimed proof of P3.2, but the combination of:

1. the proved `o(n)` upper-digit pruning;
2. the explicit fixed-`n` modular coefficient formula;
3. the exact local Hasse-projector Sturm theorem;
4. the degree-`p-1` projector lower bound;
5. the principal Cartier/Sturm target ideal;
6. the `Theta(n^2/log n)` cost of direct modular batching;
7. and the precise two-characteristic pair theorem `(PE)`, with every diagonal intermediate already controlled.

A complete proof still requires a new Apéry-specific cross-prime cancellation theorem.
