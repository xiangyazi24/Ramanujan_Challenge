# Apéry large-prime vanishing: exact modular/Sturm audit and the two-characteristic barrier

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
R(n)=\operatorname{rad}_{\sqrt n<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n}(b_n).
\]

I do **not** obtain a proof of

\[
\log R(n)=o(n)
\tag{0.1}
\]

for every `n`. The modular and finite-field structures can, however, be pushed to an exact stopping point substantially sharper than “the weight varies” or “Sturm does not apply.”

The main conclusions are:

1. **The companion digit is harmless.** For `p>sqrt(n)`, writing `n=qp+r`, the Lucas law gives
   \[
   b_n\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
   \]
   The total logarithmic weight of primes coming from `p|b_q` is
   \[
   O\!\left(n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3}+n^{1/2}\log n\right)=o(n).
   \]
   Thus the true obstruction is the moving lower digit `p|b_{n mod p}`.

2. **There is an exact characteristic-zero modular coefficient formula.** With the classical level-6 Apéry parametrization, `b_n` is the constant term of a weakly holomorphic weight-4 modular form with pole order exactly `n`. Multiplying by a canonical level-6 cusp form gives a holomorphic form of weight `2n+4`, whose safe Sturm bound on `Gamma_0(6)` is `2n+4`. But divisibility of the one constant term does not imply the Sturm prefix vanishes; the holomorphic form has constant coefficient `1` for every `n`.

3. **There is also an exact local Sturm criterion for each pair `(p,r)`.** Beukers–Tsai–Ye construct a degree-`p-1` polynomial Hasse/Frobenius quotient `P_p(t)` satisfying
   \[
   P_p(t)\equiv4\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j\pmod p.
   \]
   Applying the spectral projector
   \[
   \Pi_{p,r}(X)=1-(X-r)^{p-1}
   \]
   to `Theta_t=t d/dt` and multiplying by `Z^{p-1}` produces a holomorphic level-6 modular form `Q_{p,r}` of weight `2(p-1)` such that
   \[
   Q_{p,r}\equiv4b_rt^rZ^{p-1}\pmod p.
   \]
   Hence
   \[
   p\mid b_r
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   Q_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod p,
   \]
   and the exact Gamma_0(6) Sturm bound is `2(p-1)`.

   This is the strongest clean Sturm formulation I find. It also proves the obstruction: modulo `p`, **every Sturm coefficient of `Q_{p,r}` is a scalar multiple of the same single residue `b_r`**. Sturm supplies no second equation.

4. **The theta projector has provably minimal order.** A polynomial over `F_p` that is `1` at `r` and `0` at all other residues must have degree at least `p-1`. Thus no bounded-order theta cycle can select an arbitrary Apéry coefficient from the first Hasse block. The projector above is degree-optimal.

5. **Cartier and Dwork are locally rank one.** If
   \[
   \Lambda_{p,r}\!\left(\sum a_mt^m\right)=\sum_{m\ge0}a_{pm+r}t^m,
   \]
   then the p-Lucas law gives the exact identity
   \[
   \Lambda_{p,r}F\equiv b_rF\pmod p.
   \]
   Since `F(0)=1`, the ideal generated by all coefficients of this Cartier section is exactly `(b_r)`. Higher local identities can refine the p-adic digit, but the first Cartier block contains no independent target equation.

6. **The p-specific modular forms cannot be batched by the standard product/Sturm construction at subexponential cost.** The sum of their weights over `sqrt(n)<p<=n` is
   \[
   \sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}2(p-1)
   =(1+o(1))\frac{n^2}{\log n}.
   \]
   The corresponding total polynomial degree is asymptotic to `n^2/(2 log n)`. Standard coefficient or determinant bounds are therefore vastly larger than `exp(o(n))`. This does not rule out an unknown Apéry-specific cancellation, but it rules out the direct Hasse-projector product and ordinary Sturm/resultant estimates.

7. **The exact two-characteristic theorem that would close the problem is a shell pair-energy bound.** Put
   \[
   Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},
   \qquad
   K(n)=\#\{\sqrt n<p\le n:n\bmod p\in Z_p\}.
   \]
   It is enough to prove
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sum_{N<n\le2N}(K(n))_2
   =o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
   }
   \tag{PE}
   \]
   The diagonal first moment is already
   \[
   \sum_{N<n\le2N}K(n)\ll N^{5/3+o(1)}
   \]
   from the repository’s proved `|Z_p|<<p^(2/3)`. Thus `(PE)` isolates exactly the missing cross-characteristic correlations.

8. **No standard trace-function large sieve has the right arity.** Katz varies characters in one fixed finite field. Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel fix one modulus/field and sum a trace function over prime arguments. Here the summation prime is itself the residue characteristic, and only one character is sampled in each changing field. Even under the conjecturally optimal vertical bound `|Z_p|=O(1)`, the ordinary additive large sieve gives only the critical `O(N^2)` second moment, missing the required factor `log^2 N`.

Accordingly, neither a standard modular/Sturm theorem nor an existing one-field trace-function theorem closes (0.1). The two genuinely viable completion shapes are:

- an **initial-state-specific characteristic-zero modular cancellation** producing a nonzero integer of height `exp(o(n))` divisible by every target prime; or
- a **two-residue-characteristic large sieve** proving `(PE)`, with a logarithmic saving beyond the classical `Q^2` barrier.

## 1. First reduction: isolate the moving lower digit

The Apéry numbers satisfy the p-Lucas congruence

\[
b_{ap+r}\equiv b_ab_r\pmod p
\qquad(0\le r<p).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Fix `p>sqrt(n)` and write

\[
n=qp+r,
\qquad
1\le q<\sqrt n,
\qquad
0\le r<p.
\]

Then

\[
p\mid b_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_qb_r.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The two factors should not be conflated. The upper factor `b_q` is pointwise manageable.

### Proposition 1.1 — the upper-digit channel is `o(n)`

Let

\[
U(n)=\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}}}\log p.
\]

Then

\[
U(n)
\ll n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3}+n^{1/2}\log n
=o(n).
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Proof

For fixed `q`, the primes with `floor(n/p)=q` lie in

\[
I_q=\left(\frac n{q+1},\frac nq\right].
\]

The logarithmic weight of those also dividing `b_q` has two independent bounds:

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in I_q\\p\mid b_q}}\log p
\le \log b_q
\ll q,
\tag{1.4}
\]

using the standard Apéry growth `log b_q=O(q)`, and

\[
\sum_{p\in I_q}\log p
\ll\left(\frac n{q(q+1)}+1\right)\log n.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Choose

\[
Q=(n\log n)^{1/3}.
\]

For `q<=Q`, sum (1.4). For `Q<q<sqrt(n)`, sum (1.5):

\[
\begin{aligned}
U(n)
&\ll \sum_{q\le Q}q
 +\log n\sum_{Q<q<\sqrt n}\left(\frac n{q^2}+1\right)\\
&\ll Q^2+\frac{n\log n}{Q}+\sqrt n\log n\\
&\ll n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3}+n^{1/2}\log n.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is `o(n)`. ∎

Therefore define the hard count

\[
K(n)=\#\{\sqrt n<p\le n:p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Since every such prime satisfies

\[
\frac12\log n<\log p\le\log n,
\]

(0.1) is equivalent, after the already proved estimate (1.3), to

\[
K(n)=o\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right).
\tag{1.7}
\]

This is the exact horizontal target used below.

## 2. The classical modular parametrization with exact weights

Set `q=e^{2 pi i tau}` and define

\[
t(\tau)=
\left(
\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau)}
     {\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau)}
\right)^{12},
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
Z(\tau)=
\frac{\eta(2\tau)^7\eta(3\tau)^7}
     {\eta(\tau)^5\eta(6\tau)^5}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The classical Apéry parametrization is

\[
Z(\tau)=F(t(\tau))
       =\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt(\tau)^n.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Here:

- `t` is a modular function on `Gamma_0(6)+w_6`, with `t=q+O(q^2)`;
- `Z` is a weight-2 modular form on that group, with `Z=1+O(q)`;
- after restriction, both are objects on `Gamma_0(6)`.

Let

\[
D=q\frac d{dq}.
\]

The eta-product gives the exact logarithmic derivative

\[
D\log t
=\frac12\bigl(
E_2(\tau)+6E_2(6\tau)
-2E_2(2\tau)-3E_2(3\tau)
\bigr).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Although the individual `E_2` terms are quasimodular, the displayed combination is the logarithmic derivative of a modular unit and is a holomorphic weight-2 modular form on `Gamma_0(6)`.

### 2.1 Exact coefficient extraction

Changing residues from the `t` coordinate to the `q` coordinate gives

\[
\boxed{
 b_n=\operatorname{CT}_q
 \left(
  Z(\tau)\frac{Dt(\tau)}{t(\tau)^{n+1}}
 \right).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Define

\[
H_n(\tau)=Z(\tau)\frac{Dt(\tau)}{t(\tau)^{n+1}}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Then `H_n` is a weakly holomorphic modular form of weight `4`, with a pole of order exactly `n` at infinity, and

\[
b_n=\operatorname{CT}_qH_n.
\tag{2.7}
\]

This is the most direct characteristic-zero modular carrier. Its distinguished coefficient is exactly the original integer `b_n`; no height has yet been saved.

### 2.2 Clearing the pole and testing Sturm exactly

Put

\[
\Phi(\tau)=t(\tau)Z(\tau)
=\frac{\eta(\tau)^7\eta(6\tau)^7}
       {\eta(2\tau)^5\eta(3\tau)^5}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

It is a weight-2 cusp form on `Gamma_0(6)` with

\[
\Phi=q+O(q^2).
\]

Multiplying (2.6) by `Phi^n` gives

\[
J_n:=\Phi^nH_n
=Z^{n+1}D\log t
\in M_{2n+4}(\Gamma_0(6)).
\tag{2.9}
\]

The index is

\[
[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_0(6)]
=6\left(1+\frac12\right)\left(1+\frac13\right)
=12.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Hence the safe Sturm bound for weight `k` on `Gamma_0(6)` is

\[
B_6(k)=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{12}\cdot12\right\rfloor=k.
\]

For `J_n`, this is

\[
\boxed{B_6(J_n)=2n+4.}
\tag{2.11}
\]

This does **not** turn `p|b_n` into a Sturm-prefix condition. Indeed,

\[
J_n=1+O(q)
\tag{2.12}
\]

for every `n`, while

\[
\boxed{
 b_n=[q^n]\left[J_n(q)\left(\frac q{\Phi(q)}\right)^n\right].
}
\tag{2.13}
\]

The factor `(q/Phi)^n` is an integral formal power series but is not a holomorphic modular form. Thus `p|b_n` is one linear functional of the first `n+1` coefficients of `J_n`; it is not vanishing of the first `2n+5` coefficients.

This is the precise reason the ordinary Sturm theorem does not apply to the fixed coefficient.

### 2.3 A weight lower bound for the pole-clearing route

There is also a clean valence obstruction to keeping the weight fixed. If a nonzero holomorphic modular form `M` of level `6` and weight `k` vanishes to order at least `n` at infinity, the valence formula gives

\[
n\le \frac{k}{12}
[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_0(6)]
=k.
\tag{2.14}
\]

Therefore any holomorphic multiplier that clears an order-`n` pole at infinity has weight at least `n`. The explicit multiplier `Phi^n` has weight `2n`.

This proves a genuine no-go for a **fixed-weight** Sturm argument. It does not prove a height lower bound for every possible signed combination in a growing weight space.

## 3. The p-dependent Hasse/Frobenius polynomial

For every prime `p>=5`, Beukers–Tsai–Ye construct a modular function

\[
G_p(\tau)=
\frac{
 \sum_{d\parallel6}d^{p-1}E_{p-1}(d\tau)^2
}{Z(\tau)^{p-1}},
\tag{3.1}
\]

with the normalization in which `d` runs through the exact divisors of `6`. It is a polynomial in `t`:

\[
G_p(\tau)=P_p(t(\tau)),
\qquad
\deg P_p\le p-1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Since `E_{p-1}=1 mod p` and `d^{p-1}=1 mod p` for `d|6`,

\[
G_p\equiv4Z^{1-p}\pmod p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Dwork/Lucas gives

\[
Z^{1-p}\equiv
\frac{F(t)}{F(t^p)}
\equiv
A_p(t):=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j
\pmod p.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
P_p(t)\equiv4A_p(t)
=4\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j
\pmod p.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the correct Hasse/Frobenius object. Notice that targetness is a **coefficient zero of `P_p`**, not a root of `P_p(t)` in the modular parameter.

## 4. An exact coefficient projector and an exact Sturm criterion

Let

\[
\Theta_t=t\frac d{dt}.
\]

For `0<=r<p`, define

\[
\Pi_{p,r}(X)=1-(X-r)^{p-1}\in\mathbb F_p[X].
\tag{4.1}
\]

For every `0<=j<p`, Fermat’s theorem gives

\[
\Pi_{p,r}(j)=
\begin{cases}
1,&j=r,\\
0,&j\ne r.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore, for every polynomial of degree at most `p-1`,

\[
\Pi_{p,r}(\Theta_t)
\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}c_jt^j\right)
=c_rt^r.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Applying this to (3.5) yields

\[
\Pi_{p,r}(\Theta_t)P_p(t)
\equiv4b_rt^r\pmod p.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Define the p-integral characteristic-zero lift

\[
Q_{p,r}(\tau)
=Z(\tau)^{p-1}
 \Pi_{p,r}(\Theta_t)P_p(t(\tau)).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because `Theta_t` preserves polynomial degree, every term in (4.5) is a linear combination of

\[
t^jZ^{p-1}=\Phi^jZ^{p-1-j},
\qquad0\le j\le p-1.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Hence

\[
Q_{p,r}\in M_{2(p-1)}(\Gamma_0(6);\mathbb Z_{(p)}).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Modulo `p`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_{p,r}\equiv4b_rt^rZ^{p-1}
=4b_r\Phi^rZ^{p-1-r}\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Since the second factor is a nonzero modular form, this gives the exact equivalence

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

The exact `Gamma_0(6)` Sturm bound is

\[
\boxed{B_6(Q_{p,r})=2(p-1).}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus a target can indeed be tested by a Sturm bound.

### 4.1 Why this Sturm test does not amplify targetness

Equation (4.8) is stronger than (4.9). It says that the complete reduction of `Q_{p,r}` is the scalar `4b_r` times one fixed nonzero form. Since

\[
\Phi^rZ^{p-1-r}=q^r+O(q^{r+1}),
\]

the first potentially nonzero coefficient of `Q_{p,r}` is already `4b_r`.

Therefore the ideal generated by all the Sturm coefficients is exactly

\[
(b_r)\subset\mathbb F_p.
\tag{4.11}
\]

The `2(p-1)+1` Sturm coefficients are not independent target equations. They are all copies of the same one-dimensional obstruction.

### 4.2 Minimality of the theta projector

The order `p-1` is not an artifact of the chosen formula.

#### Lemma 4.1

If `R(X) in F_p[X]` satisfies

\[
R(r)=1,
\qquad
R(j)=0\quad(j\in\mathbb F_p,\ j\ne r),
\]

then

\[
\deg R\ge p-1.
\]

#### Proof

`R` has `p-1` distinct roots. A nonzero polynomial of degree below `p-1` cannot have that many roots. ∎

Thus `Pi_{p,r}` is degree-optimal. Any universal polynomial in `Theta_t` that isolates one arbitrary coefficient in the Hasse block has order at least `p-1`.

This explicitly rules out a bounded theta cycle as a coefficient selector.

## 5. Cartier operators and Dwork congruences: exact local rank one

For a formal series `f(t)=sum a_mt^m`, define the Cartier section

\[
\Lambda_{p,r}f
=\sum_{m\ge0}a_{pm+r}t^m.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The p-Lucas congruence gives

\[
b_{pm+r}\equiv b_mb_r\pmod p,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{p,r}F\equiv b_rF\pmod p.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because `F(0)=b_0=1`, the coefficient ideal of the entire section is exactly

\[
\left(	ext{coefficients of }\Lambda_{p,r}F\right)
=(b_r)
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is the Cartier analogue of (4.11): the full first-block operator contains only one target scalar.

The ordinary Cartier operator is the `r=0` case:

\[
\mathcal C_pF=\Lambda_{p,0}F\equiv F\pmod p.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The Dwork quotient is

\[
\frac{F(t)}{F(t^p)}\equiv A_p(t)\pmod p,
\tag{5.5}
\]

and Mellit–Vlasenko’s higher congruences refine this quotient modulo `p^s`. These are strong congruences inside one characteristic, but (5.2) shows why the zeroth layer does not create a second condition on a target coefficient.

### 5.1 The mod-p differential equation does not mark a coefficient

The generating function satisfies

\[
\mathcal LF=0,
\tag{5.6}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal L
=\theta^3
-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3,
\qquad
\theta=t\frac d{dt}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mathcal L
=\theta^3
-t(34\theta^3+51\theta^2+27\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Modulo `p`, the Hasse block `A_p` is the normalized Frobenius quotient and its interior coefficients obey the same recurrence. But `b_r=0` is merely one coordinate hyperplane in that polynomial solution. It does not force:

- a new singularity of `mathcal L`;
- a common root of `A_p` and another fixed polynomial;
- reducibility of the differential operator;
- or a drop in the dimension of the normalized solution space.

A resultant that detects roots of `A_p(t)` is therefore testing the wrong event. A coefficient resultant must first implement the degree-`p-1` projector of Section 4, returning the same p-dependent complexity.

## 6. Hasse invariants, theta cycles, and the exact obstruction

There are two distinct theta operators:

- `D=q d/dq`, natural for modular forms;
- `Theta_t=t d/dt=(D log t)^{-1}D`, natural for selecting a coefficient in the Apéry parameter.

For a mod-p modular form of weight `k`, the ordinary q-theta operator has a modular lift of weight `k+p+1` (up to the usual filtration drops). Iterating it `p-1` times has formal weight increase

\[
(p-1)(p+1)=p^2-1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The optimized construction of Section 4 avoids this quadratic formal weight by acting on the degree-`p-1` modular function `P_p(t)` first and clearing the denominator only once. It reaches weight `2(p-1)`. But Lemma 4.1 shows that the **operator order still must grow linearly with `p`**.

Filtration drops in a theta cycle cannot remove this interpolation lower bound: distinguishing all `p` eigenvalues `0,1,...,p-1` requires a degree-`p-1` polynomial in the eigenvalue.

### 6.1 The explicit p-dependent Hasse polynomial

The repository’s audited Gross–Koblitz reduction also gives a Hasse-type polynomial

\[
\mathscr H_p(X)
=\sum_{m=0}^{(p-1)/2}
 \frac{
  \prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(X-j(j+1))^2
 }{(m!)^4}
\in\mathbb F_p[X]
\tag{6.2}
\]

such that

\[
b_r\equiv\mathscr H_p(r(r+1))\pmod p.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The displayed formula has degree at most `p-1` in `X`; its degree grows linearly with the characteristic. It converts targetness into a root/evaluation statement, but the polynomial itself changes with `p`.

Generic ordinarity does not imply that this rational root locus is bounded. The classical Legendre/Deuring Hasse polynomial already has degree `(p-1)/2`, despite generic ordinarity of the family. Thus Hasse-invariant theory by itself supplies neither a uniform root bound nor horizontal coupling across different primes.

## 7. Why the standard characteristic-zero batching has the wrong height

There are three natural modular batching attempts.

### 7.1 Use the fixed weakly holomorphic form `H_n`

Its target coefficient is exactly

\[
\operatorname{CT}H_n=b_n.
\]

The Apéry asymptotic is

\[
\log b_n
=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus this direct characteristic-zero integer has positive linear logarithmic height. It is not a subexponential carrier.

One may take signed combinations in the `Theta(n)`-dimensional space of weakly holomorphic forms with pole order at most `n`, but ordinary Faber-basis, determinant, or Sturm estimates do not prove the leading exponential cancellation needed to turn (7.1) into `o(n)`.

### 7.2 Multiply the p-specific Hasse-projector forms

For each candidate prime use `Q_{p,n mod p}`. The total weight is

\[
\begin{aligned}
W(n)
&=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}2(p-1)\\
&=(1+o(1))\frac{n^2}{\log n},
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

because

\[
\sum_{p\le x}p\sim\frac{x^2}{2\log x}.
\]

The total degree in the Hauptmodul is

\[
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}(p-1)
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac{n^2}{\log n}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The product is a legitimate way to force every target prime into every coefficient, but standard fixed-level modular coefficient bounds now operate at weight `Theta(n^2/log n)`. They give no `exp(o(n))` integer.

### 7.3 Take resultants of the Hasse polynomials

The objects `P_p` and `H_p` live in different residue characteristics and have degrees `Theta(p)`. To form one characteristic-zero resultant, one must choose separate lifts and then combine them. The natural total degree is again (7.3), and ordinary resultant-height bounds are at least on a quadratic-in-`n` scale as upper estimates.

More importantly, a root resultant of `P_p(t)` does not detect a coefficient zero. Incorporating the coefficient projector reintroduces the minimal degree `p-1` from Lemma 4.1.

### Scope of this no-go

These calculations do **not** prove that no unknown modular identity can compress the target radical. They prove that the following standard mechanisms do not do so:

- clearing the fixed pole and applying Sturm;
- Hasse-invariant root counting;
- bounded-order theta cycles;
- the universal coefficient projector;
- products of the p-specific projector forms;
- and ordinary degree/height bounds for their resultants.

A successful characteristic-zero route would need a new, Apéry-initial-state-specific cancellation not visible in these universal modular constructions.

## 8. Mellin traces: exact parameter dependence

The diagonal/finite-hypergeometric realization gives, for every fixed prime `p`, a finite Mellin trace representation of `b_r mod p`. Schematically,

\[
b_r
=\operatorname{Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)(\omega_p^r)
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p,
\tag{8.1}
\]

up to an explicit nonzero normalization, where `omega_p` is the Teichmüller character and `K_p` is obtained from the fixed Apéry diagonal/K3 object.

The repository’s geometric analysis identifies the primitive Mellin cohomology as a rank-2, weight-3 symplectic family; the negative conclusions below need only the exact finite Mellin identity and bounded local geometric complexity.

For `n=qp+r`,

\[
r=n-qp\equiv n-q\pmod{p-1}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus the selected character can be written

\[
\chi_{p,n,q}=\omega_p^{n-q}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

For fixed `n,q`, the exponent is a fixed integer, but its character order is

\[
d_{p,n,q}
=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-q)}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

This order generally grows with `p`, and so do the coefficient field and the relevant prime above `p`.

### 8.1 Why Katz’s vertical theorem has the wrong quantifiers

Katz’s Mellin equidistribution fixes one finite field and one sheaf, then varies the character in that field. It can describe the vertical family

\[
r\longmapsto
\operatorname{Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)(\omega_p^r)
\qquad(r\in\mathbb Z/(p-1)\mathbb Z).
\]

Our horizontal problem selects one moving character and then changes the field:

\[
p\longmapsto
\operatorname{Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)(\omega_p^{n-q}).
\]

These are different quantifiers. Even perfect equidistribution of all characters for every fixed `p` permits the selected zero in each row to align at one fixed `n`.

There is a second obstruction: exact vanishing modulo the residue characteristic is not a fixed continuous class function of a complex Sato–Tate conjugacy class. Over `F_p`, the zero indicator is

\[
\mathbf1_{x=0}=1-x^{p-1},
\tag{8.5}
\]

whose algebraic degree grows with `p`.

### 8.2 Why Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel has the wrong arity

The prime-sum trace-function theorems of Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel have the form

\[
\sum_{\substack{\ell\le X\\\ell\text{ prime}}}K(\ell),
\]

where `K` is a trace function on one fixed field `F_P`; the modulus `P` is fixed while the prime argument `ell` varies.

Here the sampled prime is itself the modulus:

\[
p\mapsto K_p(n\bmod p).
\]

There is one point in each of many fields, not many arguments in one field. Completion and Deligne’s Riemann hypothesis cannot be applied across those changing fields.

Even a hypothetical fixed compatible system would leave a separate wall: the event is

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

with reduction modulo the same moving prime. That is not a conjugacy condition in any fixed finite quotient suitable for ordinary Chebotarev.

## 9. The exact two-characteristic theorem that suffices

Define

\[
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}
\tag{9.1}
\]

and

\[
K(n)=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}
\mathbf1_{Z_p}(n\bmod p).
\tag{9.2}
\]

The requested two-characteristic statement can be given with no heuristic language.

### Pair-energy theorem `(PE)`

For every `N->infinity`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{N<n\le2N}(K(n))_2
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\ne\ell}}
\#\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
N<n\le2N,\\
\sqrt n<p,\ell\le n,\\
n\bmod p\in Z_p,\\
n\bmod\ell\in Z_\ell
\end{array}
\right\}
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{9.4}
\]

In Mellin language, replace the last two conditions by

\[
\operatorname{Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)
 (\omega_p^{n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor})\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

and the analogous condition at `ell`.

### Proposition 9.1 — `(PE)` proves the all-n theorem

Assume (9.3). Then

\[
K(n)=o\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)
\]

uniformly, and hence `log R(n)=o(n)`.

### Proof

For any `n in (N,2N]`,

\[
(K(n))_2
\le
\sum_{N<m\le2N}(K(m))_2
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
K(n)=o(N/\log N)
=o(n/\log n).
\]

The lower-digit radical has logarithm at most `K(n) log n=o(n)`. Add the upper-digit estimate (1.3). ∎

### 9.1 Every intermediate diagonal term is already controlled

The repository proves

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}.
\tag{9.5}
\]

For a fixed `p`, each residue in `Z_p` occurs at most `N/p+1` times in a shell of length `N`. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{N<n\le2N}K(n)
&\ll
\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le2N}
\left(\frac Np+1\right)|Z_p|\\
&\ll N^{5/3+o(1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.6}
\]

Since

\[
N^{5/3+o(1)}
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right),
\]

the diagonal part of `sum K(n)^2` is harmless. The only missing component is the cross-prime sum (9.4).

This is why a stronger fixed-p zero-fibre theorem is not by itself the horizontal theorem: (9.4) asks how zeros in two different residue characteristics align on one integer diagonal.

## 10. The classical additive large sieve stops at the critical scale

The obstruction can be quantified without invoking sheaf terminology.

Let

\[
\delta_p(x)=\mathbf1_{Z_p}(x\bmod p),
\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{|Z_p|}{p},
\]

and define the centered function

\[
\delta_p^\circ(x)=\delta_p(x)-\rho_p.
\]

Its finite Fourier expansion is

\[
\delta_p^\circ(x)
=\frac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}
\widehat\delta_p(a)e_p(ax),
\tag{10.1}
\]

where

\[
\widehat\delta_p(a)
=\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(-az).
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}
\left|\frac{\widehat\delta_p(a)}p\right|^2
=rac{|Z_p|}{p}-\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p^2}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Apply the classical additive large sieve to all reduced fractions `a/p` with `p<=Q`, `Q asymp N`. It yields only

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}
\left|
 \sum_{p\le Q}\delta_p^\circ(n)
\right|^2
\ll
(N+Q^2)
\sum_{p\le Q}
\left(
\frac{|Z_p|}{p}-\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p^2}
\right).
\tag{10.3}
\]

Even under the conjecturally optimal uniform input

\[
|Z_p|=O(1),
\]

the coefficient norm over `sqrt(N)<p<=2N` is `O(1)`, so (10.3) gives only

\[
O(N^2).
\tag{10.4}
\]

The required threshold is

\[
o(N^2/\log^2N).
\]

Thus the ordinary large sieve misses a full `log^2 N` factor even in the ideal vertical regime. With the currently proved `|Z_p|<<p^(2/3)`, its bound is much worse.

A successful two-characteristic theorem must therefore exploit arithmetic cancellation special to the Apéry zero sets; spacing of the rational frequencies alone is insufficient.

There is also a geometric reason for the critical `Q^2` term. For `p,ell>sqrt(N)`, the CRT modulus `p ell` exceeds the shell length. A residue pair supplies at most one or a bounded number of integers in the shell, so there is no complete-period averaging available.

## 12. What would genuinely reopen the modular route

The analysis leaves two precise possibilities.

### 12.1 A characteristic-zero modular cancellation theorem

One would need to construct, from the distinguished Apéry modular parametrization, nonzero integers `C_n` with

\[
p\mid C_n
\quad
\text{for every }\sqrt n<p\le n\text{ with }p\mid b_n,
\tag{12.1}
\]

and prove

\[
\log|C_n|=o(n).
\tag{12.2}
\]

The new content cannot merely be another Hasse projector, Sturm prefix, or universal resultant: Sections 4 and 7 show their exact degree and weight costs. It must prove an Archimedean cancellation in a growing level-6 modular-form space that uses the specific initial state `b_0=1,b_1=5`.

A plausible precise form would be a signed Faber-basis functional of support `o(n/log n)` or with exponentially accurate leading-symbol cancellation, together with a proof that every selected local projector value maps into that one functional. No such identity is currently present in the repository or in the standard modular-form literature audited here.

### 12.2 A two-characteristic Apéry large sieve

Prove `(PE)`, equivalently (9.4). An analytic strengthening that would suffice is

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}
\left|
 \sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}
 \mathbf1_{{\rm Mellin}(\mathcal K_p)
 (\omega_p^{n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor})\equiv0\ (p)}
\right|^2
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{12.3}
\]

The exact novelty required is a logarithmic saving over the `Q^2` large-sieve barrier for a family in which:

- the residue characteristic varies with the summation prime;
- the selected tame-character order is unbounded;
- the zero detector has degree `p-1`;
- and the interval is shorter than the pairwise CRT moduli.

No standard Katz, Deligne, Chebotarev, Fouvry–Michel, or Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel theorem has these quantifiers.

## 13. Final assessment

The modular parametrization is highly informative, but it separates into two layers:

1. **Vertical/local layer.** At each `p`, the Hasse quotient, Cartier sections, theta projector, mod-p differential equation, and Mellin sheaf describe the whole first block. The coefficient event `p|b_r` has an exact modular Sturm test.

2. **Horizontal layer.** For fixed `n`, the test form, selected coefficient, character, coefficient field, and residue characteristic all change with `p`. The local test ideal is principal and generated by `b_r`; it carries no second local equation. Standard batching pays either linear candidate-primorial height or total modular weight `Theta(n^2/log n)`.

Thus the route does not fail because modularity is absent. It fails because the available modularity is **one-characteristic and coefficient-local**, while P3.2 requires **two-characteristic nonalignment**.

The strongest exact outputs of this audit are:

- the weight-`2n+4` fixed-`n` coefficient extraction and its tested Sturm failure;
- the weight-`2(p-1)` local Hasse-projector Sturm criterion;
- the degree-`p-1` lower bound for every universal theta coefficient selector;
- the principal Cartier/Sturm target ideal `(b_r)`;
- the `Theta(n^2/log n)` cost of direct modular batching;
- and the exact sufficient pair theorem `(PE)`, with its diagonal part already proved and its classical large-sieve stopping point quantified.

A complete proof of `log R(n)=o(n)` still requires a new Apéry-specific cross-prime cancellation theorem.
