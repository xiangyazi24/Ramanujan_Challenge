# Executive verdict

The all-\(k\) irreducibility conjecture is very plausible, but none of the five proposed routes currently gives a uniform proof. More importantly, **irreducibility is not the lemma needed for the stated application**.

The repository already contains two stronger structural ingredients in separate places:

1. the renewal/addition identity for the gap continuants; and
2. the strict complex root strip
   \[
   -h<\Re z<-1\qquad (N_h(z)=0).
   \]

Together they give, directly and unconditionally,

\[
\boxed{\gcd_{\mathbf Q[r]}(\widetilde W_h,\widetilde W_k)=1
       \quad\text{for every }h\ne k.}
\]

Thus the proposed implication

\[
\text{irreducibility}+\text{one nonvanishing value}
\Longrightarrow \text{pairwise coprimality}
\]

can be replaced by a short direct proof. This removes the alleged irreducibility gate from the \(G_n\) argument. The all-\(k\) irreducibility/Galois-group statement remains an interesting independent problem, but it should no longer sit on the critical path.

# 1. Normalize the sign

The repository uses

\[
N_0=0,\qquad N_1=1,
\]

with

\[
N_{h+1}(r)=A(r+h)N_h(r)-(r+h)^6N_{h-1}(r),
\]

whereas the question uses \(\widetilde W_1=-1\). Hence simply

\[
\widetilde W_h=-N_h
\]

for every \(h\). Signs do not affect roots, irreducibility of the primitive part, or polynomial gcds, so it is cleaner to prove the result for \(N_h\).

# 2. The direct pairwise-coprimality theorem

## 2.1 The two repository identities

The renewal theorem in `FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex` states, in its \(U_m\)-notation,

\[
U_{m+g+1}(s)
=U_{m+1}(s)U_g(s+m+1)
 -(s+m+1)^6U_m(s)U_{g-1}(s+m+2).
\]

Using the exact dictionary

\[
N_h(r)=U_{h-1}(r+1)
\]

and substituting \(m=h-1\), \(g=d-1\), \(s=r+1\), one obtains the gap-addition law

\[
\boxed{
N_{h+d}(r)
=N_{h+1}(r)N_d(r+h)
 -(r+h+1)^6N_h(r)N_{d-1}(r+h+1).}
\tag{A}
\]

The root-strip proposition in `meso_result.tex` says that, for every \(j\ge2\),

\[
\boxed{N_j(z)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad -j<\Re z<-1.}
\tag{B}
\]

Both statements are proved in the repository over characteristic zero.

## 2.2 Adjacent indices

The repository already proves

\[
\gcd_{\mathbf Q[r]}(N_h,N_{h+1})=1.
\]

There is also a very short proof from the recurrence and the strip. Suppose \(\alpha\) were a common root. Then

\[
0=N_{h+1}(\alpha)
=A(\alpha+h)N_h(\alpha)-(\alpha+h)^6N_{h-1}(\alpha),
\]

so

\[
(\alpha+h)^6N_{h-1}(\alpha)=0.
\]

The strict strip for \(N_h\) excludes \(\alpha=-h\). Hence \(N_{h-1}(\alpha)=0\). Repeating descends to \(N_1(\alpha)=1=0\), a contradiction.

## 2.3 Arbitrary indices

Let \(1\le h<k\), and put \(d=k-h\). Suppose \(\alpha\) is a common complex root of \(N_h\) and \(N_k\). The case \(h=1\) is impossible because \(N_1=1\), so take \(h\ge2\).

Evaluate (A) at \(r=\alpha\). Since \(N_h(\alpha)=N_k(\alpha)=0\),

\[
N_{h+1}(\alpha)N_d(\alpha+h)=0.
\]

Adjacent coprimality gives \(N_{h+1}(\alpha)\ne0\). Therefore

\[
N_d(\alpha+h)=0.
\tag{C}
\]

If \(d=1\), this already contradicts \(N_1=1\). If \(d\ge2\), the strip for the original root gives

\[
\Re(\alpha+h)>0,
\]

whereas (B) applied to (C) requires

\[
\Re(\alpha+h)<-1.
\]

This is impossible. Thus no common root exists.

Here is the whole argument in drop-in form.

```latex
\begin{theorem}[Pairwise coprimality of all Apéry gap continuants]
Let
\[
N_0=0,\quad N_1=1,\quad
N_{j+1}(x)=P(x+j)N_j(x)-(x+j)^6N_{j-1}(x).
\]
Then for every pair of distinct positive integers $h,k$,
\[
\gcd_{\mathbf Q[x]}(N_h,N_k)=1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\gcd_{\mathbf Q[x]}(\widetilde W_h,\widetilde W_k)=1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume $1\le h<k$ and put $d=k-h$.  The renewal identity gives
\[
N_k(x)=N_{h+1}(x)N_d(x+h)
 -(x+h+1)^6N_h(x)N_{d-1}(x+h+1).
\]
Suppose that $\alpha$ is a common root of $N_h$ and $N_k$.
Adjacent coprimality gives $N_{h+1}(\alpha)\ne0$, and hence
$N_d(\alpha+h)=0$.  If $d=1$ this contradicts $N_1=1$.
If $d\ge2$, the root-strip theorem gives
$-h<\Re\alpha<-1$, hence $\Re(\alpha+h)>0$; but every root
of $N_d$ has real part strictly between $-d$ and $-1$.
This contradiction proves the theorem.
\end{proof}
```

## 2.4 Immediate consequences

For all \(h\ne k\),

\[
\operatorname{Res}_r(N_h,N_k)\ne0.
\]

The same is true for \(\widetilde W_h,\widetilde W_k\). In particular, no irreducible factor of an odd centered polynomial can recur in another gap polynomial, and no noncentral factor of an even polynomial can recur either.

The central-value identity

\[
\widetilde W_{2n+1}(-(n+1))
=(-1)^{n+1}(n!)^6b_n^2
\]

is therefore not needed to establish pairwise coprimality. It remains useful for arithmetic valuations and endpoint formulas, but not for this logical step.

One should state the conclusion over \(\mathbf Q[r]\), or for primitive parts in \(\mathbf Z[r]\). Integer contents are separate from nonconstant polynomial factors and can create bad-characteristic reductions without changing the characteristic-zero gcd theorem.

# 3. What this does—and does not—settle for the \(G_n\) proof

According to the dependency chain in the question, the only role assigned to all-\(k\) irreducibility was to deduce

\[
\gcd(\widetilde W_j,\widetilde W_k)=1\qquad(j\ne k).
\]

That role is now discharged unconditionally by the theorem above. Therefore the proof should be reorganized as

\[
\boxed{
\text{renewal identity}+\text{root strips}
\Longrightarrow
\text{pairwise coprimality},}
\]

not through the Galois groups of the individual centered factors.

This does not automatically prove every later quantitative step. In particular:

- pairwise coprimality over \(\mathbf Q\) says each pairwise resultant is a nonzero integer, but one must still control the total prime-divisor/height budget;
- the raw shifted-resultant budget from Q314 is \(\asymp K^4\log K\), not literally \(O(K^4)\); the extra logarithm is \(K^{o(1)}\) and should be absorbable in a \(p^\varepsilon\) margin, but it must be carried honestly;
- if some later argument uses irreducibility for a purpose stronger than pairwise coprimality—such as transitivity of a reduction, a uniform Frobenius orbit, or a multiplicity assertion—that use needs a separate audit.

For the use stated in Q320, however, **the decisive lemma is already proved**.

# 4. Why the obvious irreducibility routes do not presently close

The following obstructions are specific to this family, not generic pessimism.

## 4.1 The prime \(3\): exact Frobenius-cube degeneration

Modulo \(3\),

\[
A(t)=(2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5)
\equiv t^3-1=(t-1)^3.
\]

Define \(C_0=0\), \(C_1=1\) over \(\mathbf F_3[r]\) by

\[
C_{j+1}(r)
=(r+j-1)C_j(r)-(r+j)^2C_{j-1}(r).
\]

Then induction using Frobenius gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{N_j(r)\equiv C_j(r)^3\pmod3.}
\tag{D}
\]

Indeed, if it holds at two consecutive indices, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{j+1}
&\equiv (r+j-1)^3C_j^3-(r+j)^6C_{j-1}^3\\
&=\bigl((r+j-1)C_j-(r+j)^2C_{j-1}\bigr)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Affine centering preserves being a cube. Reflection shows that the cube root is even for odd \(j\) and odd for even \(j\). Consequently, after a nonzero primitive reduction:

- for odd \(j\), the centered factor is a perfect cube in \(\mathbf F_3[u]\);
- for even \(j\), after removing the forced central linear factor, the residual reduction has the form
  \[
  u\,D_j(u)^3
  \]
  up to a nonzero scalar.

Thus reduction modulo \(3\) can never be the desired irreducible reduction. It is maximally inseparable.

This degeneration may still be useful through *higher* \(3\)-adic Newton polygons: it could produce wild inertia acting in 3-blocks. But that is only a source of local 3-cycles. A second argument would still be needed to connect all residual blocks and prove transitivity.

## 4.2 The central value is hostile to ordinary Eisenstein

For \(j=2m+1\), the constant coefficient of the odd centered factor is

\[
f_{2m+1}(0)=\pm(m!)^6b_m^2.
\]

Hence every prime valuation of this constant coefficient is even:

\[
v_p(f_{2m+1}(0))=6v_p(m!)+2v_p(b_m).
\]

In particular, ordinary Eisenstein at \(u=0\) is impossible for every prime: the constant coefficient can never have valuation exactly one.

Nor does a prime divisor of \(b_m\) force all the other coefficients to be divisible by that prime. The first nontrivial example is

\[
f_3(u)=25-219u+765u^2-1155u^3.
\]

At \(p=5\), the constant term has valuation \(2\), but the coefficient \(-219\) is a 5-adic unit. Its Newton polygon has a length-one first edge rather than one Eisenstein edge spanning the full degree.

The central identity is therefore excellent for proving nonvanishing and exact valuations, but by itself it is not an irreducibility criterion.

## 4.3 Primitive divisors of the leading coefficient give only a one-root edge

Let \(\ell_j\) be the leading coefficient. The repository proves

\[
\ell_{j+1}=34\ell_j-\ell_{j-1}
\]

and gives the next centered coefficient

\[
u_j=
\frac{-(j-1)(32j^2-64j-11)\ell_j+5j\ell_{j-1}}{256}.
\tag{E}
\]

Suppose an odd prime \(q\) divides \(\ell_j\), while

\[
q\nmid 10j\ell_{j-1}.
\]

Then (E) gives

\[
u_j\not\equiv0\pmod q.
\]

Thus the centered factor drops by exactly one degree modulo \(q\): its leading coefficient vanishes, but its next coefficient does not. In the reciprocal \(q\)-adic Newton polygon, this creates only a length-one terminal edge. It isolates one local root; it does not create a long inertia cycle and does not prove transitivity.

The natural primitive divisors of the Pell/Lucas sequence \(\ell_j\) therefore explain the degree-drop lattice seen in `meso_resultants.md`, but they are not an all-degree irreducibility mechanism.

## 4.4 “Find one irreducible reduction” is a certificate, not yet a uniform theory

For a fixed \(j\), an irreducible reduction modulo one good prime proves irreducibility over \(\mathbf Q\). This is exactly why the DDF computations through \(j=50\), and at \(60,70,80\), are rigorous finite certificates.

The missing theorem is the existence of such a prime for every \(j\), with a pattern that can be proved uniformly. That statement is stronger than a generic appeal to Chebotarev: asking for an irreducible reduction asks the Galois group to contain a full \(n\)-cycle, and not every transitive permutation group contains one. Chebotarev cannot supply transitivity before transitivity has been proved.

The current tables should therefore be mined for a *deterministic congruence pattern* in the witness primes, rather than merely extended to larger \(j\).

## 4.5 Proving \(S_n\) first is logically stronger than the needed result

A proof that

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(f_j/\mathbf Q)=S_{\deg f_j}
\]

would of course imply irreducibility, but its first group-theoretic input is already transitivity, which is precisely irreducibility.

The observed DDF distributions for \(f_3,f_5,f_7\) are strong evidence. Exact factorization types at good primes can also be assembled into a finite group-theoretic certificate for each fixed \(j\). But a distribution “matching \(S_n\)” over a finite sample is not a uniform monodromy proof.

The standard efficient order is:

1. prove transitivity/irreducibility;
2. produce a suitably long prime cycle by a Newton polygon or a good reduction;
3. invoke a Jordan-type criterion to obtain \(A_n\);
4. exhibit odd Galois action, for example through a nonsquare discriminant or simple tame ramification, to obtain \(S_n\).

For this family, Step 1 remains the hard part. Starting with the full \(S_n\) target makes the problem harder than necessary.

## 4.6 The recurrence does not propagate irreducibility

Three-term continuant recurrences frequently have reducible members even when adjacent members are coprime. The relation

\[
N_{j+1}=A(r+j)N_j-(r+j)^6N_{j-1}
\]

therefore does not imply that irreducibility of \(N_{j-1}\) and \(N_j\) propagates to \(N_{j+1}\).

What *does* propagate is the renewal identity (A). That identity, combined with root geometry, propagates exactly the property needed here: nonsharing of roots between different indices.

## 4.7 Analytic root distribution cannot by itself force arithmetic transitivity

The root strip is powerful enough to prove the pairwise-coprimality theorem. Finer localization may place three roots near each unit cell. But a rational factor could, in principle, select a Galois-stable union of such root clusters. Separation in the complex plane does not rule that out.

An analytic/hypergeometric identification would become decisive only if it supplied an arithmetic theorem—such as a known irreducible classical family, a differential-Galois monodromy theorem, or a coefficient formula to which a uniform irreducibility criterion applies. No such identification is presently recorded in the repository.

# 5. Most promising route if all-\(k\) irreducibility is pursued independently

The best realistic route is **not** to begin with full Galois monodromy. It is to derive a coefficient formula sufficiently explicit for a Filaseta/Hajir-style Newton-polygon argument.

## 5.1 Start from the exact matching expansion

Because \(N_j\) is a tridiagonal continuant, it has the exact path-matching expansion

\[
N_j(r)=
\sum_{M}
(-1)^{|M|}
\prod_{i\text{ unmatched}}A(r+i)
\prod_{(i,i+1)\in M}(r+i+1)^6,
\tag{F}
\]

where \(M\) ranges over matchings of the path on \(\{1,\ldots,j-1\}\).

Formula (F) is completely uniform, but in its present form it has too much cancellation to read off coefficient valuations. The key missing lemma is a transformation of the primitive centered factor into a factorial/binomial basis

\[
F_j(u)=\sum_{t=0}^{n_j} c_{j,t}\,\Phi_{j,t}(u)
\]

for which \(v_p(c_{j,t})\) and the denominators of \(\Phi_{j,t}\) are explicit.

## 5.2 Use several primes to exclude every possible factor degree

A viable Newton-polygon proof need not find one Eisenstein prime. It can use a family of primes, each excluding an interval of possible factor degrees. The intended architecture is:

```text
for every possible factor degree d with 1 <= d <= floor(n_j/2):
    choose a prime p in an interval depending on j and d;
    prove the p-adic Newton polygon has no factor of degree d;
conclude that no proper factor degree is possible.
```

The factorial \((m!)^6\) in the central coefficient supplies many primes in controlled ranges. What is missing is matching control of *all intermediate coefficients*. The first two centered-coefficient formulas in `nv_theorem.tex` are not enough; one needs either a closed formula or uniform valuation inequalities through the whole coefficient array.

This is the same general technology that succeeds for Bessel and generalized Laguerre polynomials: coefficient formulas first, Newton polygons second, large Galois groups only after irreducibility.

## 5.3 Then upgrade to \(A_n\) or \(S_n\)

Once transitivity is known, the computational evidence suggests the upgrade should be much easier. A practical target is to prove that, for each sufficiently large \(j\), one auxiliary prime produces a Newton-polygon edge whose denominator is a prime \(q\) in a Jordan range. That gives a long \(q\)-cycle. A second prime with one simple double root, or a uniform nonsquare-discriminant statement, should then distinguish \(S_n\) from \(A_n\).

This separates the tasks correctly:

\[
\text{global coefficient arithmetic}
\Longrightarrow \text{irreducibility}
\Longrightarrow \text{large local cycle}
\Longrightarrow A_n/S_n.
\]

## 5.4 A secondary, higher-risk route: wild \(3\)-adic blocks

Identity (D) makes \(p=3\) special enough to merit a separate experiment. One can write schematically

\[
F_j(u)=G_j(u)^3+3H_j(u)
\]

and run a higher-order Montes/Newton analysis at every irreducible factor of \(G_j\bmod3\). The desired outcome would be cubic ramification above each residual root plus a second prime whose Frobenius joins the cubic blocks transitively.

This route exploits genuine family structure, but it has two unresolved layers:

1. the factorization of the cube root \(G_j\bmod3\) itself varies with \(j\);
2. local cubic inertia does not connect distinct residual factors.

It is worth computing, but it is less likely to give the first uniform proof than a factorial-basis coefficient theorem.

# 6. Recommended computational campaign

The present DDF campaign should be changed from “more degrees” to “search for a theorem-shaped pattern.” For each primitive centered factor \(F_j\), record the smallest exact witnesses for:

1. **transitivity:** a prime for which \(F_j\bmod p\) is irreducible, or a minimal collection of cycle types ruling out every intransitive subgroup;
2. **a long prime cycle:** a factorization type containing a prime cycle in the Jordan range;
3. **odd action:** a prime giving a transposition after taking a suitable power of Frobenius, or a certified nonsquare discriminant;
4. **local coefficient geometry:** complete Newton polygons at \(p=2,3,5\), at primes dividing \(b_{(j-1)/2}\), and at primitive divisors of \(\ell_j\).

Then regress the witness primes against congruence data such as

\[
p\bmod j,\qquad p\bmod(2j\pm1),\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_p(17+12\sqrt2),
\]

and against the ranks of apparition of \(\ell_j\). A repeatable congruence class is potentially provable; a merely small random witness prime is not.

The first symbolic target should be the coefficient array, not another hundred factorizations:

```text
INPUT:  path-matching formula (F)
GOAL:   explicit centered coefficient c_{j,t}
        as factorial products times a p-integral sum
OUTPUT: uniform lower hull of (t, v_p(c_{j,t}))
```

# 7. Final answer to “what is the most promising route?”

There are two different questions, and they have different answers.

## For the Ramanujan-Challenge proof

The most promising route is already complete:

\[
\boxed{
\text{renewal/addition law}
+
\text{strict root strips}
\Longrightarrow
\gcd(\widetilde W_h,\widetilde W_k)=1
\quad(h\ne k).}
\]

Do not make all-\(k\) irreducibility or \(S_n\)-Galois groups a dependency of the \(G_n\) proof.

## For the independent irreducibility conjecture

The most promising route is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{continuant matching formula}
\to
\text{factorial-basis coefficients}
\to
\text{multi-prime Newton polygons}
\to
\text{irreducibility},}
\]

followed only then by a Jordan/discriminant upgrade to \(A_n\) or \(S_n\).

The simple Eisenstein variants are blocked by the square central value; the leading-coefficient primes give only length-one edges; modulo \(3\) is an exact cube; and a Galois-first proof is stronger than, and logically contains, the missing transitivity statement.
