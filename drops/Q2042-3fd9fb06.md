## 2. The companion numerator theorem

Let \(p\) be strict-top and assume only

\[
p^3\parallel b_n,\qquad n=p+r,\quad1\le r\le p-2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Put

\[
c:=b_n/p^3\in\mathbf Z_p^\times.
\]

By Lucas,

\[
b_n\equiv5b_r\pmod p,
\]

so \(p\mid b_r\).  No-consecutive-zeros gives

\[
p\nmid b_{r-1}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The same Lucas law at \(n-1=p+r-1\) gives

\[
b_{n-1}\equiv5b_{r-1}\pmod p,
\tag{2.3}
\]

hence \(b_{n-1}\) is a \(p\)-unit.

Apply the pair law (1.2) to \(n-1\):

\[
p^3a_{n-1}\equiv6b_{r-1}
\equiv \frac65 b_{n-1}\pmod p.
\tag{2.4}
\]

In particular

\[
v_p(a_{n-1})=-3.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Now insert \(b_n=p^3c\) into the exact Casoratian (1.1):

\[
a_nb_{n-1}-(p^3a_{n-1})c=\frac6{n^3}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Modulo \(p\), (2.4) gives

\[
a_nb_{n-1}
\equiv
\frac6{n^3}+\frac65b_{n-1}c
\pmod p.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Since \(b_{n-1}\) is a unit, we have the exact equivalence

\[
\boxed{
 a_n\equiv0\pmod p
 \iff
 n^3b_{n-1}c+5\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

The left side is well-defined: (2.6) shows \(a_n\in\mathbf Z_p\) under
(2.1).  Writing \(a_n=R_n/Q_n\) in lowest terms, (2.8) becomes

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid R_n
 \iff
 n^3b_{n-1}(b_n/p^3)+5\equiv0\pmod p,
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

because \(p\nmid Q_n\).

Thus, under exact cube multiplicity, the corrected endpoint gate is exactly
**companion-numerator divisibility**.

For a seam prime the right side of (2.9) holds by hypothesis, hence

\[
p\mid R_n.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Meanwhile (2.5) implies

\[
v_p(Q_{n-1})=3,\qquad p\nmid R_{n-1}.
\tag{2.11}
\]

This proves (0.4).

### Integer-continuant form

Since \(p<n<2p\),

\[
v_p(n!)=v_p((n-1)!)=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
v_p(X_{n-1})=3+v_p(a_{n-1})=0,
\]

while (2.10) gives \(v_p(a_n)\ge1\), hence

\[
v_p(X_n)=3+v_p(a_n)\ge4.
\]

This proves the unit-to-fourth jump (0.3).

## 3. The fixed Bézout identity and what it does not prove

Write

\[
a_m=R_m/Q_m
\]

in lowest terms.  Clearing denominators in the Casoratian (1.1) gives the
exact integer identity

\[
\boxed{
 n^3\bigl(R_nQ_{n-1}b_{n-1}-R_{n-1}Q_nb_n\bigr)
 =6Q_nQ_{n-1}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let

\[
g_n:=\gcd(R_n,b_n).
\]

Since \((R_n,Q_n)=1\), also \((g_n,Q_n)=1\).  Both terms on the left of
(3.1) are divisible by \(g_n\), so

\[
g_n\mid6Q_nQ_{n-1}.
\]

Cancelling the coprime factor \(Q_n\) yields

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(R_n,b_n)\mid6Q_{n-1}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is a genuine fixed-\(n\) Bézout relation between the two Apéry solutions.
For a seam prime \(p\ge7\), (2.10) and \(p^3\parallel b_n\) imply

\[
p\mid\gcd(R_n,b_n),
\]

and (3.2) recovers \(p\mid Q_{n-1}\); the stronger pair law gives the exact
exponent \(3\).

However, (3.2) is not yet a height theorem.  Since

\[
Q_{n-1}\mid\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,n-1)^3,
\]

we only get

\[
\log\gcd(R_n,b_n)\le3\psi(n-1)+O(1)=3n+o(n).
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is linear and therefore insufficient.

## 4. Why the obvious denominator-extractor gate is target-blind

For an integer \(N\), keep the prompt's exact definition

\[
D_3(N)=\sum_{\ell^e\parallel N}
 e(e-1)(e-2)\frac{N}{\ell^3}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Suppose \(p\) is strict-top and

\[
v_p(Q_{n-1})=3.
\]

Write

\[
Q_{n-1}=p^3q,\qquad p\nmid q.
\]

Then

\[
D_3(Q_{n-1})\equiv6q\pmod p.
\tag{4.2}
\]

But

\[
p^3a_{n-1}=R_{n-1}/q\pmod p.
\]

Combining this with (2.4) gives

\[
R_{n-1}\equiv6qb_{r-1}\pmod p,
\qquad
b_{n-1}\equiv5b_{r-1}\pmod p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 b_{n-1}D_3(Q_{n-1})-5R_{n-1}\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Crucially, no condition on \(b_n\), \(H_n\), or the seam was used beyond
\(v_p(Q_{n-1})=3\).  Thus

\[
K_n=b_{n-1}D_3(Q_{n-1})-5R_{n-1}
\]

is a **target-blind gate**: it detects the ordinary top denominator skeleton
of the companion sequence, not the endpoint seam.

This explains why combining the known \(H_n\) with an analogous denominator
extractor does not add codimension.

Its height is also still linear.  Since

\[
\log Q_{n-1}\le3n+o(n)
\]

and \(D_3(Q_{n-1})=Q_{n-1}\,n^{O(1)}\) on a logarithmic scale,

\[
\log|D_3(Q_{n-1})|\le3n+o(n).
\]

Using

\[
\log b_{n-1}=\Lambda n+O(\log n),
\qquad
\Lambda=\log(17+12\sqrt2)=3.525494348\ldots,
\]

and

\[
\log|R_{n-1}|\le(\Lambda+3)n+o(n),
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{
\log|K_n|\le(\Lambda+3)n+o(n)
=6.525494348\ldots n+o(n).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

So this route is both target-blind and linearly tall.

## 5. Rigorous no-go for bounded multiplicity-extractor collections

The obstruction can be stated and proved in a general form.

### 5.1 One carrier

For \(m\ge1\), define

\[
\mathcal D_m(N)
=\sum_{\ell^e\parallel N}(e)_m\frac N{\ell^m},
\qquad
(e)_m=e(e-1)\cdots(e-m+1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let \(p^3\parallel N\), and write

\[
N=p^3c,\qquad c\in\mathbf Z_p^\times.
\]

For every prime \(\ell\ne p\), the \(\ell\)-term in (5.1) still contains the
factor \(p^3\), hence vanishes modulo \(p\).  The \(p\)-term is

\[
(3)_m p^{3-m}c.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D_m(N)\equiv
\begin{cases}
6c,&m=3,\\
0,&m\ne3,
\end{cases}
\pmod p.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For \(m<3\), a factor \(p\) remains; for \(m>3\), \((3)_m=0\).

Hence every integer polynomial in

\[
N,\mathcal D_1(N),\ldots,\mathcal D_M(N)
\]

reduces at an exact-cube prime to a polynomial in the **single variable**
\(c=N/p^3\pmod p\).

This includes the gate \(H_n\): at an exact cube prime,

\[
H_n\equiv6n^3b_{n-1}c+30\pmod p,
\]

so the gate fixes one value of \(c\), namely

\[
c\equiv-5(n^3b_{n-1})^{-1}\pmod p.
\]

No formula for an arithmetic derivative is being used here; (5.1) is a
separately defined falling-factorial extractor.

### 5.2 CRT transparency

Let \(S\) be any finite set of primes, put

\[
P_S=\prod_{p\in S}p,
\qquad M_S=\sum_{p\in S}\log p.
\]

Suppose a bounded family of multiplicity-extractor gates has, for each
\(p\in S\), a common nonzero local solution \(c_p\in\mathbf F_p^\times\).
Choose \(C\) modulo \(P_S\) by

\[
C\equiv c_p\left(\frac{P_S}{p}\right)^{-3}\pmod p
\qquad(p\in S).
\tag{5.3}
\]

CRT gives a representative \(1\le C\le P_S\), and every residue in (5.3)
is nonzero, so \((C,P_S)=1\).  Put

\[
N=P_S^3C.
\]

Then for every \(p\in S\),

\[
p^3\parallel N,
\qquad
N/p^3\equiv c_p\pmod p,
\]

so **all** the bounded extractor gates hold simultaneously, while

\[
\boxed{
\log N<4M_S.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus linear logarithmic prime mass is fully compatible with
\(\exp(O(n))\) height for every bounded collection in this extractor class.

### 5.3 Several free carriers

The same proof works verbatim for finitely many carrier integers
\(N_1,\ldots,N_s\).  If carrier \(j\) is required to have exact exponent
\(e_j\) at each \(p\in S\), then modulo \(p\) its entire extractor algebra
has only one surviving cofactor variable

\[
c_{j,p}=N_j/p^{e_j}.
\]

Choose the cofactors independently by CRT and set

\[
N_j=P_S^{e_j}C_j.
\]

Then

\[
\log N_j<(e_j+1)M_S.
\tag{5.5}
\]

So adding finitely many further multiplicity extractors to finitely many
otherwise free height-bounded integers does not escape the model.

**What can escape:** an actual recurrence, determinant, or geometric relation
which couples those carriers across indices.  The jump (0.3) is exactly of
this type.

## 6. Why finitely many companion solutions do not add a gate

The recurrence is second order.  Over \(\mathbf Q\), every solution is

\[
u_m=\alpha a_m+\beta b_m
\]

for fixed \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf Q\).

At a seam, (2.9) gives

\[
a_n\in p\mathbf Z_p,
\qquad
b_n\in p^3\mathbf Z_p.
\]

For every seam prime not dividing the fixed denominators of
\(\alpha,\beta\),

\[
u_n\in p\mathbf Z_p.
\]

Therefore **every bounded collection of fixed companion solutions vanishes at
the same seam prime automatically**.  The two-dimensional solution space
cannot provide another independent mod-\(p\) equation at index \(n\).

This is a second scoped no-go, separate from the multiplicity-extractor CRT
no-go.

## 7. The adjacent recurrence also gives no new equation

The \(b\)-recurrence at \(n\) gives

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\]

Modulo \(p\), since \(p\mid b_n\),

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}\equiv-n^3b_{n-1}\pmod p.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Multiplying by \(c=b_n/p^3\) and using (0.1),

\[
\boxed{
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}c\equiv5\pmod p.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus the right-neighbor version is exactly the old gate with the recurrence
substituted.  Every bounded collection of further \(b\)-neighbors modulo
\(p\) is obtained by iterating the same two-dimensional state and adds no
new condition.

The companion neighbors behave the same way after the natural normalization
of \(a_{n-1}\): the pair

\[
(a_n,\ p^3a_{n-1})\pmod p
\]

is already fixed by (2.8), (2.4), and the low digit.  A new local condition
must therefore use a **higher normalized digit**, not another first-depth
neighbor.

This closes the plain adjacent-endpoint route.

## 8. The exact escaping support carrier

Let

\[
\operatorname{CubRad}(N)
:=\prod_{\ell:\,v_\ell(N)=3}\ell,
\]

and let

\[
T_n:=\prod_{(n+1)/2<p<n}p.
\]

Define the fixed integer

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_n
:=\gcd\bigl(|R_n|,\operatorname{CubRad}(b_n),T_n\bigr).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

By (2.9), every corrected exact-cubic endpoint prime divides \(R_n\); by
hypothesis it occurs to exponent exactly three in \(b_n\); and it is
strict-top.  Hence every seam prime divides \(\mathcal C_n\).

Moreover, on strict-top exact-cube primes, (2.9) says precisely that

\[
p\mid H_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid R_n.
\tag{8.2}
\]

So (8.1) is not merely a necessary relaxation: it is the clean fixed-integer
carrier for the **entire corrected exact-cube + endpoint-gate package**.
The continuant seam may still be a proper subset, but the multiplicity
extractor \(H_n\) has disappeared from the carrier.

A sufficient theorem is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal C_n=o(n).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This is a genuinely cross-sequence gcd problem involving the Apéry companion
numerator and the Apéry denominator sequence.  It is not a one-carrier
multiplicity-extractor problem.

The integer-continuant version of the same support is the UT4 set:

\[
\{p:(n+1)/2<p<n,\ p\nmid X_{n-1},\ p^4\mid X_n\}.
\]

Indeed, in the strict-top range,

\[
p\nmid X_{n-1}\iff v_p(Q_{n-1})=3,
\qquad
p^4\mid X_n\iff p\mid R_n.
\tag{8.4}
\]

For seam primes the first equivalence is automatic by (2.5).

Thus UT4 is the smallest recurrence-level theorem isolated by this attack.

## 9. Height accounting: the remaining exponent is still linear

Let

\[
\Lambda=\log(17+12\sqrt2)=3.525494348\ldots.
\]

The standard Apéry asymptotics give

\[
\log b_n=\Lambda n+O(\log n),
\qquad
\log|a_n|=\Lambda n+O(\log n).
\]

Since

\[
Q_n\mid\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,n)^3,
\]

we have

\[
\log Q_n\le3n+o(n).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log|R_n|\le(\Lambda+3)n+o(n)
=6.525494348\ldots n+o(n).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

For the known fixed gate,

\[
\log|H_n|\le2\Lambda n+o(n)
=7.050988696\ldots n+o(n).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Exact cube multiplicity alone gives, for seam product
\(P_{\rm seam}\),

\[
P_{\rm seam}^3\mid b_n,
\]

hence

\[
\log P_{\rm seam}
\le\frac{\Lambda}{3}n+o(n)
=1.175164782\ldots n+o(n).
\tag{9.3}
\]

The companion denominator improves the unrestricted cube-height constant:
seam primes also occur to exponent three in \(Q_{n-1}\), so

\[
\log P_{\rm seam}\le n+o(n).
\tag{9.4}
\]

But in the strict-top problem the strongest automatic bound is simply the
support interval:

\[
\log P_{\rm seam}
\le\sum_{(n+1)/2<p<n}\log p
=(1/2+o(1))n.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Thus after all identities proved here, the best unconditional exponential
exponent is still

\[
\boxed{1/2.}
\]

This is exactly why (8.3) or UT4 is the correct next theorem: any height-only
argument remains compatible with linear mass.

## 10. Two terminal obstructions and one falsified overreach

### 10.1 The companion numerator is not a new local residue

Equation (2.9) is an equivalence.  Once exact cube multiplicity is known,
\(p\mid R_n\) and \(p\mid H_n\) are the same local condition.  Therefore the
companion does not give a second independent residue class at the first
normalized depth.

### 10.2 The denominator derivative is target-blind

Equation (4.3) holds for every strict-top exact denominator cube, without
using \(b_n\).  Thus the most obvious second derivative-style gate from
\(Q_{n-1}\) merely detects the universal denominator skeleton.

### 10.3 Global numerator coprimality is false

Exact computation disproves the tempting global statement
\((R_n,n!)=1\).  In fact

\[
11\mid R_{113},\qquad 11^2\mid R_{117},
\]

with \(11\nmid Q_{113}Q_{117}\).  So a proof must use the **strict-top**
geometry; there is no global numerator-prime exclusion theorem of this form.

For reconnaissance only, the exact integer recurrence (0.2) shows no
strict-top factor \(p^4\mid X_{p+r}\) for primes \(p\le5000\).  This finite
absence is not used as a theorem.

## 11. Standalone exact checker

The following script uses only Python's exact integers and `Fraction`.  It
checks:

1. the Apéry and companion recurrences;
2. the exact Casoratian;
3. the integer companion recurrence (0.2);
4. the strict-top pair block residues used in the proof;
5. the equivalence (2.9) whenever an exact cube is encountered;
6. target-blindness of (4.3);
7. the extractor residue law (5.2) and the CRT construction;
8. the exact counterexamples \(11\mid R_{113}\), \(11^2\mid R_{117}\);
9. a finite, explicitly labelled reconnaissance scan for UT4.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
from fractions import Fraction
from math import gcd, factorial, isqrt


def P(n):
    return 34*n**3 + 51*n**2 + 27*n + 5


def primes_upto(N):
    sieve = bytearray(b"\x01") * (N+1)
    sieve[:2] = b"\x00\x00"
    for q in range(2, isqrt(N)+1):
        if sieve[q]:
            sieve[q*q:N+1:q] = b"\x00" * (((N-q*q)//q)+1)
    return [q for q in range(2, N+1) if sieve[q]]


def vp(z, p):
    z = abs(z)
    if z == 0:
        return 10**9
    e = 0
    while z % p == 0:
        z //= p
        e += 1
    return e


def vp_frac(x, p):
    return vp(x.numerator, p) - vp(x.denominator, p)


def mod_frac(x, p):
    x = Fraction(x)
    den = x.denominator % p
    assert den != 0
    return (x.numerator % p) * pow(den, -1, p) % p


def factorint(n):
    n = abs(n)
    out = []
    q = 2
    while q*q <= n:
        if n % q == 0:
            e = 0
            while n % q == 0:
                n //= q
                e += 1
            out.append((q, e))
        q = 3 if q == 2 else q + 2
    if n > 1:
        out.append((n, 1))
    return out


def Dm(N, m):
    ans = 0
    for ell, e in factorint(N):
        ff = 1
        for j in range(m):
            ff *= e-j
        if ff:
            ans += ff * (N // ell**m)
    return ans


def D3(N):
    return Dm(N, 3)


def build(N):
    a = [Fraction(0) for _ in range(N+1)]
    b = [0]*(N+1)
    X = [0]*(N+1)
    a[0], a[1] = Fraction(0), Fraction(6)
    b[0], b[1] = 1, 5
    X[0], X[1] = 0, 6

    for m in range(1, N):
        a[m+1] = (P(m)*a[m] - m**3*a[m-1]) / (m+1)**3
        num = P(m)*b[m] - m**3*b[m-1]
        assert num % (m+1)**3 == 0
        b[m+1] = num // (m+1)**3
        X[m+1] = P(m)*X[m] - m**6*X[m-1]
        assert Fraction(X[m+1], factorial(m+1)**3) == a[m+1]

    for n in range(1, N+1):
        assert a[n]*b[n-1] - a[n-1]*b[n] == Fraction(6, n**3)
    return a, b, X


def crt(residues):
    # residues = [(a_i, m_i)] with pairwise coprime m_i
    M = 1
    for _, m in residues:
        M *= m
    x = 0
    for a, m in residues:
        Mi = M // m
        x += a * Mi * pow(Mi, -1, m)
    return x % M


def check_extractor_crt():
    S = [11, 13, 17]
    local_c = {11: 2, 13: 3, 17: 5}
    PS = 1
    for p in S:
        PS *= p
    residues = []
    for p in S:
        target = local_c[p] * pow((PS//p)**3, -1, p) % p
        residues.append((target, p))
    C = crt(residues)
    if C == 0:
        C += PS
    N = PS**3 * C
    for p in S:
        assert vp(N, p) == 3
        c = (N // p**3) % p
        assert c == local_c[p]
        for m in range(1, 8):
            want = (6*c) % p if m == 3 else 0
            assert Dm(N, m) % p == want


def main():
    LIMIT = 240
    a, b, X = build(LIMIT)
    ps = primes_upto(LIMIT)

    # Exact global counterexamples to the overstrong gcd(R_n,n!)=1 claim.
    assert vp(a[113].numerator, 11) == 1
    assert vp(a[113].denominator, 11) == 0
    assert vp(a[117].numerator, 11) == 2
    assert vp(a[117].denominator, 11) == 0

    # Pair-block and target-blind denominator gate checks in the strict-top range.
    for p in ps:
        if p < 7:
            continue
        for r in range(1, min(p-1, LIMIT-p+1)):
            n = p+r
            if n > LIMIT:
                break

            # q=1 pair block at n-1 = p+(r-1)
            lhs = mod_frac(p**3 * a[n-1], p)
            assert lhs == (6*b[r-1]) % p
            assert b[n-1] % p == (5*b[r-1]) % p

            Qprev = a[n-1].denominator
            Rprev = a[n-1].numerator
            if vp(Qprev, p) == 3:
                K = b[n-1]*D3(Qprev) - 5*Rprev
                assert K % p == 0

            # When an exact cube occurs, endpoint gate <=> companion numerator.
            if vp(b[n], p) == 3:
                assert a[n].denominator % p != 0
                c = (b[n] // p**3) % p
                gate = (pow(n, 3, p) * (b[n-1] % p) * c + 5) % p == 0
                companion = a[n].numerator % p == 0
                assert gate == companion
                if gate:
                    assert vp(Qprev, p) == 3
                    assert X[n-1] % p != 0
                    assert X[n] % p**4 == 0

    check_extractor_crt()

    # Finite reconnaissance only: not used as proof.
    top_jumps = []
    for p in ps:

            continue
        for r in range(1, min(p-1, LIMIT-p+1)):
            n = p+r
            if n > LIMIT:

            if X[n-1] % p != 0 and X[n] % p**4 == 0:
                top_jumps.append((p, r, n))

    print("all exact identities passed")
    print("global numerator counterexamples: n=113 (11^1), n=117 (11^2)")
    print("UT4 reconnaissance up to index", LIMIT, ":", top_jumps)
```

## 12. Bottom line

The companion/Casoratian attack produces a real structural reorganization but
not yet a second local seam equation.

The exact result is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
 p^3\parallel b_n\text{ and corrected endpoint gate}\\[2mm]
 \Updownarrow\\[-1mm]
 p\mid\operatorname{num}(a_n)
\end{array}
}
\qquad\text{(strict top)}.
\]

Thus every seam prime is a **unit-to-fourth-order jump prime** of the independent
integer companion continuant \(X_m=(m!)^3a_m\):

\[
p\nmid X_{n-1},\qquad p^4\mid X_n.
\]

This is the first Apéry-specific relation outside the one-variable
multiplicity-extractor CRT model.

Three tempting ways of turning it into another bounded gate are now closed:

1. the companion numerator is primewise equivalent to the known gate once
   exact cube multiplicity is fixed;
2. the \(D_3(Q_{n-1})\) gate is target-blind;
3. any bounded family of multiplicity extractors, even on finitely many free
   carriers, is CRT-transparent, and any bounded family of fixed companion
   solutions lies in the same two-dimensional solution space.

The smallest genuinely escaping theorem is therefore the recurrence-level
UT4 mass estimate

\[
\sum_{\substack{p>(n+1)/2\\p\nmid X_{n-1}\\p^4\mid X_n}}\log p=o(n),
\]

or equivalently the cross-sequence fixed-carrier estimate

\[
\log\gcd\bigl(|R_n|,\operatorname{CubRad}(b_n),T_n\bigr)=o(n).
\]

The pointwise version forbidding a unit-to-fourth jump would prove seam
impossibility outright.  Current algebra and height alone leave exponent
\(1/2\), so a proof of UT4 must use the actual Apéry recurrence (or a higher
normalized concatenation digit), not another bounded multiplicity extractor.
