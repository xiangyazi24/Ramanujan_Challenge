# Arithmetic randomness and Erdos-Kac for Apéry numbers

## Executive conclusion

The proposed Erdős–Kac theorem for the Apéry numbers
\[
 b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n {n\choose k}^2{n+k\choose k}^2
\]
should be regarded as a major open problem. Existing theorems on linear recurrence sequences do not prove either
\[
\omega(b_n)\sim \log\log b_n
\]
or a Gaussian law. They also do not directly give
\[
\omega_{(N,2N]}(b_n)=O(1)
\]
for almost all n.

However, the Apéry sequence has much more structure than a generic recurrence: it is a G-function, has a Picard–Fuchs/K3 interpretation, and satisfies strong Lucas-type congruences. These give a plausible route to an "arithmetic randomness" theorem, but a proof would require new input: essentially a prime-by-prime independence theorem for Frobenius conditions.

---

## 1. Why the classical Erdos-Kac proof fails

For integers m≤x, Hardy–Ramanujan/Erdős–Kac studies
\[
\omega(m)=\sum_p 1_{p\mid m}
\]
by showing that the variables 1_{p|m} are sufficiently independent when m ranges over an interval.

For a sequence b_n we instead study
\[
X_p(n)=1_{p|b_n}.
\]
The required input would be estimates such as
\[
\sum_{n\le N}X_p(n)=\frac{N}{p}+o(N/p)
\]
and, more importantly, joint estimates
\[
\sum_{n\le N}\prod_{i=1}^k X_{p_i}(n)
\approx
N\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{1}{p_i}.
\]

For Apéry numbers this becomes a moving-modulus problem because the period modulo p is not simply p.

## 2. What is known from recurrence theory

### Evertse

Evertse proved finiteness and quantitative restrictions for prime divisors of non-degenerate linear recurrence sequences.

A typical statement: for a non-degenerate recurrence sequence u_n, the set of indices n for which u_n has unusually many prime divisors from a fixed finite set S is controlled by an exponential-polynomial argument.

This is an S-unit theorem application. It does NOT imply
\[
\omega(u_n)=O(\log n)
\]
or an Erdos-Kac law.

Reason: Evertse controls recurrence values avoiding primes, not the distribution of all prime divisors.

Reference:

- J.-H. Evertse, "On sums of S-units and linear recurrences", Compositio Math. 53 (1984).

---

### Stewart

Stewart proved lower bounds for the largest prime factor of recurrence terms.

For non-degenerate binary recurrences, one obtains results of the form
\[
P(u_n)>n^{1+\epsilon}
\]
for large n outside exceptional sets.

This is orthogonal to the present question. A single large prime factor does not imply control of the number of prime factors.

---

### Corvaja-Zannier

Corvaja-Zannier proved deep gcd results for recurrence sequences, including results of the shape
\[
\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)=\exp(o(n))
\]
for multiplicatively independent a,b.

Their method uses the Subspace Theorem and S-unit equations.

The Apéry GCD conjecture is philosophically similar, but their theorems do not apply directly because b_n is not an exponential recurrence with multiplicative independence. It is a Picard-Fuchs period sequence.

Reference:

- P. Corvaja and U. Zannier, "On the Diophantine equation f(a^m,x)=b^n", Acta Arith.

## 3. What can probably be proved: upper bounds

A weak target is
\[
\omega(b_n)=O(\log n).
\]

This is consistent with the size bound
\[
\log b_n=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
\]

Trivially
\[
\omega(b_n)\le \frac{\log b_n}{\log2}=O(n),
\]
which is useless.

To improve this, one needs exclude concentration on many small primes.

The Apéry congruence
\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p
\]
shows that for fixed p the zero set
\[
Z_p=\{r<p:p|b_r\}
\]
has small density. Numerically and geometrically one expects
\[
|Z_p|=O(1)
\]
on average.

If one could prove the average bound
\[
\sum_{p\le x}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\ll \log\log x,
\]
then a first moment argument would give
\[
\omega(b_n)=O(\log n)
\]
for almost all n.

This is currently unavailable in the required strength.

## 4. Restricted primes p in (N,2N]

Define
\[
\omega_{(N,2N]}(b_n)=\#\{p\in(N,2N]:p|b_n\}.
\]

This is the quantity closest to the GCD conjecture reduction.

For p>N and n∈(N,2N], write
\[
n=p+r.
\]
The Lucas congruence gives
\[
p|b_n \iff r\in Z_p.
\]

Hence
\[
\omega_{(N,2N]}(b_n)=\sum_{p\in(N,2N]}1_{n-p\in Z_p}.
\]

The expected value is
\[
\sum_{p\sim N}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\approx O(1).
\]

So the conjectural answer is indeed
\[
\omega_{(N,2N]}(b_n)=O(1)
\]
for almost all n.

But proving this requires controlling correlations:

\[
\#\{n:p|b_n,q|b_n\}
\]
for distinct primes p,q.

This is exactly the cross-prime independence problem appearing in the LGTQ-3/twin-atom formulation.

---

## 5. Arithmetic randomness interpretation

The correct probabilistic model is not random integers but random Frobenius conditions.

For each prime p, define
\[
I_p(n)=1_{b_n\equiv0\pmod p}.
\]
The desired theorem requires:

1. Marginal equidistribution:
\[
P(I_p=1)\approx \frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

2. Independence:
\[
P(I_{p_1}=\cdots=I_{p_k}=1)
\approx \prod_i P(I_{p_i}=1).
\]

The K3 interpretation suggests these are Frobenius trace conditions on a compatible family of Galois representations.

A proof would likely use:

- Chebotarev density in large composita,
- monodromy groups of the Apéry K3 family,
- large sieve for Frobenius conjugacy classes.

This is analogous to the work of Kowalski on probabilistic behavior of arithmetic sequences.

---

## 6. Is a Gaussian law plausible?

The expected theorem would be
\[
\frac{\omega(b_n)-\log\log b_n}{\sqrt{\log\log b_n}}
\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\]

A sufficient condition would be a Turán-Kubilius theorem for the sequence b_n.

One would need:

\[
\sum_{n\le x}(\omega(b_n)-A(x))^2\sim B(x)
\]
with
\[
A(x)\sim\log\log x,
\quad B(x)\sim\log\log x.
\]

No such theorem is currently known for Apéry numbers.

---

## 7. Relation to the Apéry GCD conjecture

The GCD conjecture only needs a much weaker statement:

\[
\max_n\omega_{(\sqrt n,n]}(b_n)=n^{o(1)}.
\]

A full Erdos-Kac theorem would imply this immediately by Gaussian tails.

The hierarchy is:

\[
\text{Erdos-Kac}
\Rightarrow
\omega_{(N,2N]}=O(1)\text{ a.a. }n
\Rightarrow
\max K(n)=N^{o(1)}.
\]

The last implication is the actual GCD target.

---

## Final assessment

1. Gaussian Erdős-Kac for Apéry numbers: **open; far beyond current recurrence methods.**

2. \(\omega(b_n)=O(\log n)\) almost surely: **plausible but not currently proved.**

3. Restricted count \(\omega_{(N,2N]}(b_n)=O(1)\) for almost all n: **the most realistic arithmetic-randomness target, but still requires new cross-prime independence estimates.**

The relevant missing theorem is not a generic linear recurrence theorem. It is a Frobenius large sieve statement for the Apéry K3 motive:

\[
\#\{n\le N:p_i|b_n\ \forall i\}
\approx
N\prod_i\frac{|Z_{p_i}|}{p_i}.
\]

Proving this would simultaneously attack the restricted prime factor problem and provide the probabilistic foundation for the Apéry GCD conjecture.
