# Q1999 — Ore-adjoint attack on the full Padé-minor carrier

## Verdict

The new Ore factorization **does repair the algebraic defect that killed the second-order Green attack**, but it does **not** produce a sublinear primitive carrier.

The first-order orientation proposed in the question is correct. With

\[
P(j)=34j^3+51j^2+27j+5,
\qquad g_j=(2j+1)^3,
\]

and

\[
(Af)_j=(j+1)^3f_{j+1}-P(j)f_j+j^3f_{j-1},
\]

define

\[
(Bh)_j=g_jh_{j+1}-g_{j+1}h_j.
\]

Then

\[
A1=-4g,\qquad Bg=0,\qquad L:=BA,
\]

so

\[
Lb=0,\qquad L1=0,\qquad L(b_n-b_j)=0.
\]

Thus the old unavoidable bulk term `b_n A1` really is gone.

However, the factorization exposes a different exact obstruction. There is a canonical adjoint construction using the second Apéry solution `a_j`. Before integral normalization it gives the spectacularly small identity

\[
\sum_j c_j\lambda_j(b_n-b_j)=-6.
\]

But the only nonzero coefficients are

\[
c_0=-\frac{3}{2\lambda_0},
\qquad
c_1=\frac{3}{2\lambda_1}.
\]

After clearing denominators and dividing coefficient content, the primitive integer vector is

\[
(u_0,u_1)=
\frac1{\gcd(|\lambda_0|,|\lambda_1|)}
(-\lambda_1,\lambda_0),
\]

and its value is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_j u_j\lambda_j(b_n-b_j)
=-4\,\frac{\lambda_0\lambda_1}
        {\gcd(|\lambda_0|,|\lambda_1|)}
}
\]

up to the harmless overall sign convention for the cardinal weights. Hence

\[
\boxed{
|S_{\rm prim}|=4\,\operatorname{lcm}(|\lambda_0|,|\lambda_1|).
}
\]

For `M=floor(n/2)`,

\[
|\lambda_0|=\binom{n-1}{M-1},
\]

so

\[
\log |S_{\rm prim}|=n\log2+O(\log n).
\]

This is an **exact normalization obstruction**, not an estimate from finite data. The Ore multiplication removes the exponential Apéry factor `b_n` but replaces it with an exponential cardinal/Smith denominator.

I also obtain two broader no-go statements.

1. For the full sequence-blind polynomial conjugated-adjoint class, no nonzero polynomial can be an exact global adjoint solution. If a degree-`m` polynomial is homogeneous at `H` regular interval sites, then
   \[
   H\le m+10.
   \]
   Thus an adjoint with only `e=o(M)` defects across a length-`M` block must have degree `M-o(M)`.

2. For a single fixed-ratio hypergeometric multiplier `h_{j+1}/h_j=R(j)` with rational quotient degree `d`, either it is the unique exact rank-one kernel, whose Green boundary difference is identically zero, or it can be homogeneous at at most
   \[
   4d+17
   \]
   regular sites. Hence `d=o(M)` together with `o(M)` exceptional sites is impossible.

Finally, for a hard right cutoff, any nonzero endpoint flux whose `b_n` coefficient is killed leaves the exact factor

\[
(2M-1)^3q_{M-1}
\left[M^3b_{M-1}-(M^3+4(2M+1)^3)b_M\right],
\]

whose bracket has magnitude greater than `4(2M+1)^3 b_M`. Since

\[
b_M\ge \binom{2M}{M}^2,
\]

that endpoint is exponentially large unless the normalization denominator absorbs the same exponential scale. Again, the exact primitive-normalization formula below shows where such a cancellation would have to live.

So the answer is **NO CONSTRUCTION** in the requested natural classes. The precise scoped no-go is:

> The Ore-adjoint route has one rationally tiny Casoratian certificate, but its primitive cardinal normalization is exponentially large. Every exact fixed-ratio hypergeometric global adjoint is either the zero-flux kernel or impossible; every sublinear-degree polynomial/fixed-ratio finite-interval adjoint has too few homogeneous sites to localize its defect. The only unclosed zone is linear-degree, potentially exponentially-contented adjoints. Controlling that content is no longer an Ore identity; it is a Smith/divisibility problem of essentially the same arithmetic difficulty as the original full-minor gcd.

No finite pattern is used as a theorem.

# 1. The first-order operator and the exact third-order left multiple

Put

\[
g_j=(2j+1)^3.
\]

The proposed orientation

\[
\boxed{
(Bh)_j=g_jh_{j+1}-g_{j+1}h_j
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

is the correct one, because

\[
(Bg)_j=g_jg_{j+1}-g_{j+1}g_j=0.
\]

For the Apéry operator

\[
(Af)_j=(j+1)^3f_{j+1}-P(j)f_j+j^3f_{j-1},
\]

a direct expansion gives

\[
(A1)_j=(j+1)^3+j^3-P(j)=-4(2j+1)^3=-4g_j.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
L:=BA
\]

annihilates both the Apéry solution `b` and the constant sequence.

The fully expanded operator is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
(Lf)_j={}&g_j(j+2)^3 f_{j+2}\\
&-\bigl(g_jP(j+1)+g_{j+1}(j+1)^3\bigr)f_{j+1}\\
&+\bigl(g_j(j+1)^3+g_{j+1}P(j)\bigr)f_j\\
&-g_{j+1}j^3f_{j-1}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Hence, for the full-minor difference sequence

\[
d_j=b_n-b_j,
\]

we really have

\[
\boxed{Ld=0.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

This is the genuine improvement over the second-order attack.

# 2. Formal adjoint and exact Green concomitant

The second-order operator is self-adjoint:

\[
A^*=A.
\]

From (1.1),

\[
\boxed{
(B^*q)_j=g_{j-1}q_{j-1}-g_{j+1}q_j.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{L^*=A B^*.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Set

\[
y_j=(B^*q)_j.
\]

For any two sequences `q,f`, define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_j(q,f)
=g_jq_j(Af)_{j+1}
+(j+1)^3\bigl(y_jf_{j+1}-f_jy_{j+1}\bigr).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then exact expansion gives the third-order discrete Green identity

\[
\boxed{
q_j(Lf)_j-f_j(L^*q)_j
=\mathcal K_j(q,f)-\mathcal K_{j-1}(q,f).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Since `Ld=0`, summing from `a` through `b` yields

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=a}^b d_j(L^*q)_j
=\mathcal K_{a-1}(q,d)-\mathcal K_b(q,d).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Also

\[
(Ad)_j=-4b_ng_j,
\]

so the boundary is explicitly

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K_j(q,d)
={}&-4b_ng_jg_{j+1}q_j\\
&+(j+1)^3
\bigl(y_j(b_n-b_{j+1})-(b_n-b_j)y_{j+1}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

This formula is the correct place to audit both `b_n` cancellation and primitive content.

# 3. Exact primitive-normalization theorem

Suppose the adjoint is conjugated into the full-minor coordinates:

\[
(L^*q)_j=\lambda_j c_j,
\qquad c_j\in\mathbf Q.
\]

Let `Delta` be any common positive denominator for the `c_j` on the selected finite interval, put

\[
v_j=\Delta c_j\in\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\kappa=\gcd_j |v_j|,
\qquad
u_j=v_j/\kappa.
\]

Then `u=(u_j)` is primitive, and because

\[
D_n\mid \lambda_jd_j
\]

for every `j`, one has

\[
\boxed{
D_n\mid S_u(n),
\qquad
S_u(n)=
\frac{\Delta}{\kappa}
\left(
\mathcal K_{a-1}(q,d)-\mathcal K_b(q,d)
\right).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is the exact content formula. The normalization cost is

\[
\boxed{\Delta/\kappa,}
\]

not `Delta` alone and not the coefficient gcd alone.

Any claim of a low Green boundary that ignores this quotient is therefore incomplete.

# 4. The Ore factor produces a rational `-6` certificate — and its exact primitive value is exponential

This is the most important calculation in the audit.

Let `a_j` be the second Apéry solution,

\[
a_0=0,\qquad a_1=6,
\]

satisfying `Aa=0` for `j>=1`.

For any solution `y` of the Apéry recurrence on `j>=1`, define

\[
\boxed{
q_j[y]
=\frac{(j+1)^3(y_j-y_{j+1})}
{4g_jg_{j+1}}
\qquad(j\ge0).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The recurrence gives the exact identity

\[
\frac{j^3(y_{j-1}-y_j)}4
-rac{(j+1)^3(y_j-y_{j+1})}4
=g_jy_j
\qquad(j\ge1),
\tag{4.2}
\]

because

\[
P(j)-j^3-(j+1)^3=4g_j.
\]

Thus

\[
(B^*q[y])_j=y_j
\qquad(j\ge1).
\tag{4.3}
\]

At the left edge,

\[
(B^*q[y])_0=\frac{y_1-y_0}{4}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Write a general recurrence solution as

\[
y=\alpha b+\beta a.
\]

Since `b_1=5b_0` and `(a_0,a_1)=(0,6)`, (4.4) differs from `y_0` by

\[
\delta=\frac{y_1-5y_0}{4}=\frac{3\beta}{2}.
\]

A direct application of `A` now gives

\[
\boxed{
(L^*q[y])_0=-\frac{3\beta}{2},
\qquad
(L^*q[y])_1=\frac{3\beta}{2},
\qquad
(L^*q[y])_j=0\quad(j\ge2).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The `b` component (`beta=0`) gives no certificate. Every nonzero `a` component gives exactly the same two-point direction.

Taking `beta=1`, after cardinal conjugation we obtain

\[
\boxed{
c_0=-\frac3{2\lambda_0},\qquad
c_1=\frac3{2\lambda_1},\qquad c_j=0\ (j\ge2).}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore, **over the rationals**, the full-minor combination is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_jc_j\lambda_jd_j
&=-\frac32(b_n-b_0)+\frac32(b_n-b_1)\\
&=\frac32(1-5)\\
&=\boxed{-6}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}
\]

So the Ore idea does exactly what one would hope before integrality is imposed.

## 4.1 Exact content calculation

Put

\[
G=\gcd(|\lambda_0|,|\lambda_1|),
\qquad
L_0=\operatorname{lcm}(|\lambda_0|,|\lambda_1|).
\]

A convenient clearing denominator is

\[
\Delta=2L_0.
\]

Then the cleared vector is

\[
\left(
-3\frac{\lambda_1}{G},
3\frac{\lambda_0}{G}
\right)
\]

up to the cardinal signs, and its content is **exactly `3`**. Dividing that content gives the unique primitive direction

\[
\boxed{
(u_0,u_1)=
\left(-\frac{\lambda_1}{G},
\frac{\lambda_0}{G}\right).
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Its integer value is

\[
\boxed{
S_{\rm prim}
=-4\frac{\lambda_0\lambda_1}{G},
\qquad
|S_{\rm prim}|=4L_0.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Thus the attractive rational `-6` becomes an exponentially large primitive integer.

Using

\[
|\lambda_0|=\binom{n-1}{M-1}
\]

and `M=floor(n/2)`, elementary central-binomial estimates give, uniformly for large `n`,

\[
|\lambda_0|\ge \frac{2^{n-2}}{n+1}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\log |S_{\rm prim}|
\ge n\log2-O(\log n).
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

This is the cleanest death certificate for the new Ore idea.

## 4.2 Why the normalization is target-saturated

For a prime `p>M` and a residue `r=n-p` with `0<=r<M`, cardinal interpolation gives

\[
\lambda_j(n)\equiv\delta_{jr}\pmod p.
\]

Thus if `r>=2`, then

\[
p\mid\lambda_0,\qquad p\mid\lambda_1.
\]

For the Apéry target primes `p>=7`, the cases `r=0,1` cannot be zero nodes because

\[
b_0=1,\qquad b_1=5.
\]

Therefore every ordinary top-window target prime divides the normalization factor in (4.9). The small rational Casoratian has not escaped the target radical; it has merely moved it into the cardinal denominator.

# 5. Conjugating the full third-order adjoint by the cardinal weights

Write

\[
q_j=\lambda_jh_j
\]

and set

\[
r_j=\frac{\lambda_{j+1}}{\lambda_j}
=-\frac{(n-j)(M-1-j)}{(j+1)(n-j-1)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Define

\[
s_j=\frac{(B^*q)_j}{\lambda_j}.
\]

Using (0.2), one gets the exact first-order conjugated expression

\[
\boxed{
 s_j
=-g_{j-1}\frac{j(n-j)}{(n-j+1)(M-j)}h_{j-1}
-g_{j+1}h_j.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(\mathcal C^*h)_j
:=\frac{(L^*(\lambda h))_j}{\lambda_j}
=(j+1)^3r_js_{j+1}-P(j)s_j+\frac{j^3}{r_{j-1}}s_{j-1}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is the exact cardinal-conjugated third-order adjoint.

After one `Together` reduction, its universal affine denominator is

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_j=
(j-M-1)(j-M)(j-n-2)(j-n-1)(j-n+1)
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

up to an irrelevant overall sign convention.

Thus the new third-order conjugation has five forced endpoint/outer-evaluation factors. They are outside the safe interior `0<=j<M`, but their products govern the integer clearing when a polynomial/rational ansatz is imposed.

For a general sequence `h`, write

\[
\Omega_j(\mathcal C^*h)_j=F_h(j).
\]

The exact numerator is the following four-shift expression:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_h(j)={}&
(j-1)j^4(2j-3)^3
(j-n-1)(j-n)(j-n+1)h_{j-2}\\
&-j(2j+1)Q_5(j)
(j-M-1)(j-n-2)(j-n)(j-n+1)h_{j-1}\\
&+(2j+1)R_5(j)
(j-M-1)(j-M)
(j-n-2)(j-n-1)(j-n+1)h_j\\
&-(j+1)^2(2j+5)^3
(j-M-1)(j-M)(j-M+1)\\
&\hspace{32mm}\cdot
(j-n-2)(j-n-1)(j-n)h_{j+1},
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

where

\[
Q_5(j)=-5+13j+25j^2-61j^3-64j^4+140j^5,
\tag{5.6}
\]

and

\[
R_5(j)=136+736j+1576j^2+1595j^3+764j^4+140j^5.
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is useful because it makes the polynomial obstruction completely algebraic.

# 6. Full denominator-free polynomial class: exact global kernel and finite-window degree obstruction

Let `h(j)` be a nonzero polynomial of degree `m`.

Substituting `h(j+k)` into (5.5), every summand initially has degree at most `m+11`, but the `j^(m+11)` terms cancel identically. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\deg F_h\le m+10.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

More sharply, if the leading coefficient of `h` is `a`, the next coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
[j^{m+10}]F_h
=-256a(M-m-5).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The exceptional apparent degree `m=M-5` does **not** give a global polynomial adjoint solution.

## 6.1 There is no nonzero global polynomial kernel

The exact rational-hypergeometric kernel of `L^*` is determined below. After dividing by the cardinal weights, its shift quotient would have to be

\[
\boxed{
R_0(j)=
-\frac{(j+1)(n-j-1)(2j+1)^3}
{(n-j)(M-1-j)(2j+5)^3}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If a nonzero polynomial `h` satisfied this ratio identically, then

\[
(n-j)(M-1-j)(2j+5)^3h(j+1)
=-(j+1)(n-j-1)(2j+1)^3h(j).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Set `j=-5/2`. The left coefficient vanishes while the right coefficient is nonzero for positive integer `n,M`; therefore

\[
h(-5/2)=0.
\]

Then set `j=-7/2`; all coefficient factors are nonzero and (6.4) forces

\[
h(-7/2)=0.
\]

Inductively,

\[
h(-5/2-k)=0
\qquad(k=0,1,2,\ldots),
\]

so `h` has infinitely many zeros and is identically zero, contradiction.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C^*h\equiv0,\ h\in\mathbf Q[j]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=0.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This closes the exact denominator-free global polynomial kernel class.

## 6.2 Finite-window theorem

Suppose a degree-`m` polynomial adjoint is homogeneous at `H` distinct regular sites, i.e.

\[
(\mathcal C^*h)_j=0
\]

at those sites. Since the denominator (5.4) is nonzero in the safe interval, every such site is a zero of `F_h`. By (6.5), `F_h` is a nonzero polynomial. Therefore

\[
\boxed{H\le m+10.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Consequently, on a length-`M` block with only `e` exceptional/defect sites,

\[
\boxed{m\ge M-e-10.}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Thus a polynomial with `m=o(M)` cannot be homogeneous except at `o(M)` boundary sites. Any localization strong enough to leave only a bounded or sublinear number of Green defects forces essentially full interpolation degree.

This is a theorem for the whole polynomial input class; it does not depend on a particular coefficient fit.

# 7. Fixed-ratio rational/hypergeometric adjoints

There is an equally clean rank-one theorem.

First observe that

\[
q^{(0)}_j=\frac{C}{g_jg_{j+1}}
\tag{7.1}
\]

satisfies

\[
B^*q^{(0)}=0,
\qquad
L^*q^{(0)}=0.
\]

Its ratio is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{q^{(0)}_{j+1}}{q^{(0)}_j}
=\frac{g_j}{g_{j+2}}
=\left(\frac{2j+1}{2j+5}\right)^3.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For this kernel, `y=B^*q=0`, and (2.6) gives

\[
\mathcal K_j(q^{(0)},d)
=-4Cb_n
\]

for every `j`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_{a-1}-\mathcal K_b=0.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

So the unique obvious low-complexity adjoint is a **zero certificate**.

## 7.1 Uniqueness among rational-hypergeometric exact adjoints

Assume `q` has rational shift quotient

\[
S(j)=\frac{q_{j+1}}{q_j}\in\mathbf Q(j)
\]

and satisfies `L^*q=0` identically.

Put

\[
y=B^*q.
\]

If `y=0`, (2.1) immediately gives (7.2), so `q` is (7.1).

If `y\ne0`, then `y` is again hypergeometric with rational quotient. Indeed

\[
y_j=q_j\left(\frac{g_{j-1}}{S(j-1)}-g_{j+1}\right).
\]

But `Ay=0`. A rational-hypergeometric solution of the Apéry recurrence would have a rational Riccati quotient `T(j)` satisfying

\[
(j+1)^3T(j)-P(j)+\frac{j^3}{T(j-1)}=0.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Write `T=P_1/Q_1` with rational polynomials. Comparing degrees at infinity forces equal numerator/denominator degree. If `c` is their rational leading-coefficient ratio, the coefficient of `j^3` in (7.4) gives

\[
c-34+c^{-1}=0,
\]

so

\[
c^2-34c+1=0.
\]

Its roots are

\[
17\pm12\sqrt2,
\]

not rational. Contradiction.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
q\text{ rational-hypergeometric and }L^*q=0
\Longrightarrow
q_j=C/(g_jg_{j+1}).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

And that unique exact solution has zero Green boundary difference.

## 7.2 Finite-window quantitative version

Let the conjugated sequence `h` have rational consecutive quotient

\[
R(j)=\frac{h_{j+1}}{h_j}=\frac{P(j)}{Q(j)},
\qquad
 d=\max(\deg P,\deg Q).
\]

Then `q=lambda h` has quotient

\[
S(j)=r_jR(j)
\]

of degree at most `d+2`.

Write `D=deg S<=d+2`. If `B^*q` is not identically zero, its quotient has rational degree at most

\[
2D+3.
\]

Applying the Apéry Riccati numerator to that quotient gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most

\[
4D+9\le4d+17.
\]

Therefore, away from explicitly declared zero/pole/endpoint exceptional sites, `L^*q=0` can hold at no more than

\[
\boxed{4d+17}
\tag{7.6}
\]

sites unless `q` is the exact kernel (7.1).

Thus a length-`M` fixed-ratio hypergeometric adjoint with `e` irregular/defect sites satisfies the dichotomy

\[
\boxed{
\text{either }R=R_0\text{ and the Green certificate is zero,}
\quad\text{or}\quad
 d\ge\frac{M-e-17}{4}.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

In particular, bounded-degree or sublinear-degree factorial/binomial/hypergeometric gauges cannot produce a nonzero boundary-localized certificate across a linear-size block.

# 8. Exact hard-endpoint formula

The remaining possibility is to use a finite supported adjoint and engineer its endpoint flux directly.

Take the right endpoint `M-1` and impose the hard cutoff

\[
q_M=0.
\]

Then

\[
y_M=g_{M-1}q_{M-1},
\]

and

\[
y_{M-1}=g_{M-2}q_{M-2}-g_Mq_{M-1}.
\]

From (2.6), the right boundary is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K_{M-1}
={}&-4b_ng_{M-1}g_Mq_{M-1}\\
&+M^3\bigl(y_{M-1}(b_n-b_M)
-(b_n-b_{M-1})y_M\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
\]

To remove the `b_n` coefficient one must have

\[
y_{M-1}-y_M
=\frac{4g_{M-1}g_M}{M^3}q_{M-1}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

If `q_{M-1}=0`, (8.2) forces `y_M=y_{M-1}=0`, and the boundary is zero.

If `q_{M-1}\ne0`, substituting (8.2) into (8.1) gives the exact nonzero boundary

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_{M-1}
=g_{M-1}q_{M-1}
\left[
M^3b_{M-1}-(M^3+4g_M)b_M
\right].
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Since `b_M>b_{M-1}>0`,

\[
\left|
M^3b_{M-1}-(M^3+4g_M)b_M
\right|
>4g_Mb_M.
\tag{8.4}
\]

And the `k=M` summand in Apéry's positive binomial formula gives

\[
b_M\ge\binom{2M}{M}^2
\ge\frac{16^M}{(2M+1)^2}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus the raw nonzero `b_n`-free endpoint is exponentially large on the full-minor scale `M~n/2`.

The only possible way to turn (8.3) into a sublinear **primitive** value is for the denominator/content quotient `Delta/kappa` in (3.1) to cancel the same exponential scale. That is not a new Green identity: it is exactly a large Smith-content assertion about the adjoint coefficient vector.

The explicit `-6 -> 4 lcm(lambda_0,lambda_1)` calculation in Section 4 shows this transfer of size into content happens in the simplest Ore-adjoint case, and happens in the wrong direction.

---

# 9. What is actually closed

The results above rigorously close the following classes.

## 9.1 Canonical factor-adjoint class

Take an `A`-solution, lift it through `B^*`, and use the resulting `L^*` defect as the full-minor coefficient vector.

- The `b` component gives zero.
- Every nonzero `a` component gives the same rational two-point certificate `-6`.
- Its primitive integer normalization is exactly
  \[
  4\operatorname{lcm}(|\lambda_0|,|\lambda_1|),
  \]
  of linear logarithmic height.

This class is completely dead.

## 9.2 Exact denominator-free global polynomial adjoints

There are no nonzero ones: (6.5).

## 9.3 Finite-window polynomial adjoints with sublinear defect support

If only `e=o(M)` sites are allowed to fail the homogeneous adjoint equation, degree must be `M-o(M)` by (6.7). Hence no bounded/sublinear-degree polynomial Green construction can give the desired localization.

## 9.4 Fixed-ratio rank-one hypergeometric adjoints

The unique exact low-complexity kernel gives zero flux. Every other one with `o(M)` exceptional sites has rational quotient degree `Omega(M)` by (7.7).

## 9.5 Hard-cut endpoint cancellation

A nonzero endpoint after removing `b_n` contains an Apéry-size factor by (8.3)–(8.5), unless an exponential normalization/content cancellation is proved separately.

---

# 10. What is not closed

I do **not** claim a no-go for arbitrary linear-degree adjoints with carefully engineered large content.

At degree `Theta(M)`, polynomial/rational interpolation can encode a substantial fraction of an arbitrary finite coefficient vector. In that regime, a statement such as

\[
\frac{\Delta}{\kappa}
\left|\mathcal K_{a-1}-\mathcal K_b\right|
=\exp(o(n))
\]

would be a genuinely new Smith/divisibility theorem. Ore algebra alone does not decide it.

This is the exact remaining boundary:

> **Missing high-complexity lemma.** Construct a linear-degree cardinal-conjugated adjoint whose cleared coefficient vector has enough common content to make the primitive boundary subexponential, while the primitive boundary is nonzero.

But proving such a statement is essentially the arithmetic compression that the full-minor carrier was introduced to obtain. It cannot be inferred merely from `Ld=0`.

So the new Ore multiplication is valuable as a diagnosis: it proves that the second-order `b_n` term was not the deepest obstruction. Once that term is killed, the obstruction moves into the primitive cardinal denominator/content.

# 11. Executable exact symbolic verifier

The following Wolfram Language script verifies the operator orientation, the expanded left multiple, the adjoint, the Green identity, the unique rank-one kernel, the conjugated denominator, the polynomial leading-degree cancellation for a range of symbolic degrees, and the hard-endpoint formula. All arithmetic is exact.

```wl
ClearAll["Global`*"];

pp[x_] := 34 x^3 + 51 x^2 + 27 x + 5;
gg[x_] := (2 x + 1)^3;

Aop[z_, x_] :=
  (x + 1)^3 z[x + 1] - pp[x] z[x] + x^3 z[x - 1];

Bop[z_, x_] :=
  gg[x] z[x + 1] - gg[x + 1] z[x];

Bstar[z_, x_] :=
  gg[x - 1] z[x - 1] - gg[x + 1] z[x];

Lop[z_, x_] := Bop[Function[t, Aop[z, t]], x];
Lstar[z_, x_] := Aop[Function[t, Bstar[z, t]], x];

assert[cond_, tag_] :=
  If[! TrueQ[FullSimplify[cond]],
    Print["FAIL: ", tag]; Exit[1],
    Print["PASS: ", tag]
  ];

(* 1. A1=-4g and Bg=0. *)
assert[
  Expand[Aop[Function[t, 1], j] + 4 gg[j]] == 0,
  "A sends constants to -4g"
];

assert[
  Expand[Bop[Function[t, gg[t]], j]] == 0,
  "B annihilates g"
];

(* 2. Expanded L=BA. *)
expectedL =
    gg[j] (j + 2)^3 f[j + 2]
  - (gg[j] pp[j + 1] + gg[j + 1] (j + 1)^3) f[j + 1]
  + (gg[j] (j + 1)^3 + gg[j + 1] pp[j]) f[j]
  - gg[j + 1] j^3 f[j - 1];

assert[
  Expand[Lop[f, j] - expectedL] == 0,
  "expanded L=BA"
];

(* 3. Exact Green identity. *)
y[x_] := Bstar[q, x];
K[x_] :=
  gg[x] q[x] Aop[f, x + 1]
  + (x + 1)^3 (y[x] f[x + 1] - f[x] y[x + 1]);

green = Together[
  q[j] Lop[f, j] - f[j] Lstar[q, j] - (K[j] - K[j - 1])
];

assert[
  Numerator[green] == 0,
  "third-order Green identity"
];

(* 4. Unique obvious B* kernel. *)
q0[x_] := 1/(gg[x] gg[x + 1]);

assert[
  Numerator[Together[Bstar[q0, j]]] == 0,
  "B* q0 = 0"
];

assert[
  Numerator[Together[Lstar[q0, j]]] == 0,
  "L* q0 = 0"
];

(* 5. Local right-inverse identity for B* on an A-solution. *)
yp = (pp[j] y0 - j^3 ym)/(j + 1)^3;
tPrev = j^3 (ym - y0)/4;
tCur  = (j + 1)^3 (y0 - yp)/4;

assert[
  Numerator[Together[tPrev - tCur - gg[j] y0]] == 0,
  "canonical B* right inverse for j>=1"
];

(* 6. The a-component left defect is (-3/2,+3/2). *)
a0 = 0; a1 = 6; a2 = 351/4;
z0 = 3/2; z1 = a1; z2 = a2;

Lq0 = z1 - 5 z0;
Lq1 = 8 z2 - pp[1] z1 + z0;

assert[Lq0 == -3/2, "left defect j=0"];
assert[Lq1 ==  3/2, "left defect j=1"];

(* 7. Cardinal conjugation. *)
rr[x_] :=
  -((n - x) (M - 1 - x))/((x + 1) (n - x - 1));

ss[x_] :=
  -gg[x - 1] x (n - x)/((n - x + 1) (M - x)) h[x - 1]
  -gg[x + 1] h[x];

Cstar = Together[
  (j + 1)^3 rr[j] ss[j + 1]
  - pp[j] ss[j]
  + j^3/rr[j - 1] ss[j - 1]
];

expectedDen =
  (j - M - 1) (j - M)
  (j - n - 2) (j - n - 1) (j - n + 1);

assert[
  Expand[Denominator[Cstar] - expectedDen] == 0,
  "conjugated universal denominator"
];

(* 8. Polynomial numerator degree/leading coefficient regression.
   The prose proof gives the all-degree theorem; this loop is a finite
   exact regression only. *)

cstarPoly[m_Integer] := Module[{hp, sp},
  hp[x_] := x^m;
  sp[x_] :=
    -gg[x - 1] x (n - x)/((n - x + 1) (M - x)) hp[x - 1]
    -gg[x + 1] hp[x];
  Together[
    (j + 1)^3 rr[j] sp[j + 1]
    - pp[j] sp[j]
    + j^3/rr[j - 1] sp[j - 1]
  ]
];

Do[
  num = Expand[Numerator[cstarPoly[m]]];
  assert[
    Exponent[num, j] <= m + 10,
    "degree bound m=" <> ToString[m]
  ];
  assert[
    Factor[Coefficient[num, j, m + 10]
      + 256 (M - m - 5)] == 0,
    "leading coefficient m=" <> ToString[m]
  ];
,
{m, 0, 12}];

(* 9. Hard right endpoint after killing the b_n coefficient. *)
yM = gg[M - 1] qMm1;
yMm1Kill = yM (1 + 4 gg[M]/M^3);

Kright = Expand[
  -4 bn gg[M - 1] gg[M] qMm1
  + M^3 (
      yMm1Kill (bn - bM)
      - (bn - bMm1) yM
    )
];

expectedKright =
  gg[M - 1] qMm1
  (M^3 bMm1 - (M^3 + 4 gg[M]) bM);

assert[
  Expand[Kright - expectedKright] == 0,
  "hard-endpoint formula"
];

(* 10. Finite primitive-cardinal regression for the -6 certificate. *)
lambda[nn_, MM_, jj_] :=
  (-1)^(MM - 1 - jj)
  Binomial[nn, jj]
  Binomial[nn - jj - 1, MM - 1 - jj];

Do[
  MM = Floor[nn/2];
  l0 = lambda[nn, MM, 0];
  l1 = lambda[nn, MM, 1];
  gc = GCD[Abs[l0], Abs[l1]];
  u0 = -l1/gc;
  u1 =  l0/gc;
  (* d1-d0=(b_n-5)-(b_n-1)=-4 exactly. *)
  Sprim = Expand[u0 l0 (bn - 1) + u1 l1 (bn - 5)];
  assert[
    Abs[Sprim] == 4 LCM[Abs[l0], Abs[l1]],
    "primitive -6 normalization n=" <> ToString[nn]
  ];
,
{nn, 10, 40}];

Print["Q1999 exact symbolic audit: PASS"];
```

# 12. Final decision

The left Ore multiple is mathematically the right correction:

\[
\boxed{L=BA\text{ annihilates }b,1,\text{ and therefore }d_j=b_n-b_j.}
\]

But it does not produce the required sublinear full-minor carrier.

The strongest new positive identity is the rational `-6` certificate (4.7). Its exact primitive normalization is already

\[
4\operatorname{lcm}(|\lambda_0|,|\lambda_1|)
=\exp((\log2+o(1))n).
\]

That is the new obstruction: **cardinal denominator pollution replaces the old Apéry-value pollution**.

The polynomial and fixed-ratio hypergeometric enlargements do not repair it at sublinear complexity: exact polynomial kernels are zero; sparse-defect polynomial degree is forced to be essentially linear; the unique fixed-ratio exact adjoint has zero Green flux; every other fixed-ratio adjoint needs linear quotient degree on a long interval.

A future escape would have to live in the unclosed linear-degree/high-content zone and prove a genuinely new Smith cancellation

\[
\Delta/\kappa=\exp(-\Theta(n)+o(n))
\]

against an exponentially large raw boundary. That is not supplied by Ore factorization or Green summation. It is essentially the missing arithmetic theorem itself.

So this third-order Ore-adjoint direction should be recorded as:

**ALGEBRAIC REPAIR CONFIRMED; CANONICAL PRIMITIVE CERTIFICATE EXPONENTIALLY POLLUTED; SUBLINEAR-COMPLEXITY POLYNOMIAL/FIXED-RATIO CLASSES CLOSED; LINEAR-DEGREE SMITH-CONTENT ESCAPE REMAINS OPEN.**
