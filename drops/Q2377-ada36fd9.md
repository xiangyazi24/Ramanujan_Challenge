# P3.2 CFVZ square root versus the Delannoy/Racah kernel

## Verdict

There **is** an exact connection, but it is a diagonal/Hadamard connection, not a polarization of the kernel.

Let

\[
D(n,k)=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k},
\qquad
K(r,s)=\sum_{k\ge0}D(r,k)D(s,k),
\]

and let

\[
\mathscr K(X,Y)=\sum_{r,s\ge0}K(r,s)X^rY^s.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr K(X,Y)=
\frac{1}{(1-X)(1-Y)}
{}_2F_1\!\left(
\begin{matrix}\frac12,\frac12\\1\end{matrix};
\frac{16XY}{(1-X)^2(1-Y)^2}
\right).
}
\tag{V1}
\]

If `Diag` denotes coefficient-diagonal extraction,

\[
\operatorname{Diag}_{X,Y}\mathscr K(t)
:=\sum_{n\ge0}[X^nY^n]\mathscr K(X,Y)t^n,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Diag}_{X,Y}\mathscr K(t)
=\sum_{n\ge0}K(n,n)t^n
=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n
=C_+(t)^2.
}
\tag{V2}
\]

Thus the CFVZ square root is exactly a square root of the **coefficient diagonal** of the full bivariate kernel.

What is false is the stronger interpretation

\[
K(r,s)\stackrel{?}{=}q_rq_s
\]

or any of the natural shifted Toeplitz-Gram variants made from the single coefficient sequence `q_n`.  The square

\[
B_p(t)^2
\]

uses **Cauchy convolution in one variable**, whereas `K(r,s)` is the Euclidean Gram pairing of two different Delannoy rows.  These are different quadratic constructions.

There is nevertheless an exact off-diagonal formula in terms of the CFVZ coefficients.  Combining the Q2337 Racah recurrence with

\[
b_n=(q*q)_n:=\sum_{j=0}^nq_jq_{n-j}
\]

in the raw-square branch gives, for `s=r+h`,

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)=
\frac{
\mathcal S_h(r)(q*q)_r
-\dfrac{r^3}{6}\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)(q*q)_{r-1}
}{(r+1)_h^3}.
}
\tag{V3}
\]

Here `\mathcal R` is exactly the Racah gap continuant from Q2337, and `\mathcal S` is an explicitly defined companion continuant below.  Therefore, when `b_r=0`,

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)=
-\frac{r^3}{6(r+1)_h^3}
\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)
\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}q_jq_{r-1-j}.
}
\tag{V4}
\]

The no-consecutive-zero theorem makes the convolution in (V4) nonzero.  The unresolved factor is still precisely

\[
\mathcal R_{h-1}(r).
\]

So the CFVZ square root does **not** by itself remove the Q2337 obstruction.  It rewrites the already-known nonzero factor `b_{r-1}` as a `q`-convolution, but supplies no reason that `\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)` cannot vanish when the second convolution `(q*q)_{r+h}` vanishes.

The most promising new output is (V1): the entire two-variable kernel is itself an elliptic `{}_2F_1` period.  A genuinely useful “polarized CFVZ theorem” would have to factor or algebraically pull back this **two-variable** period, not merely factor its coefficient diagonal.

## 1. The two quadratic forms are genuinely different

Pad each Delannoy row by zeros and write

\[
\mathbf d_n=(D(n,0),D(n,1),\ldots).
\]

Then

\[
K(r,s)=\mathbf d_r^{\mathsf T}\mathbf d_s,
\qquad
b_n=K(n,n)=\mathbf d_n^{\mathsf T}\mathbf d_n.
\tag{1.1}
\]

On the CFVZ side, for `n\le M`, put

\[
\mathbf q^{(n)}=(q_0,q_1,\ldots,q_n)^{\mathsf T}
\]

and let `J_n` be the reversal matrix

\[
(J_n)_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&i+j=n,\\0,&i+j\ne n.\end{cases}
\]

Then (0.3) is

\[
\boxed{
b_n=(\mathbf q^{(n)})^{\mathsf T}J_n\mathbf q^{(n)}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus the same scalar `b_n` has two representations:

\[
\mathbf d_n^{\mathsf T}I\mathbf d_n
=(\mathbf q^{(n)})^{\mathsf T}J_n\mathbf q^{(n)}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

But (1.3) does not identify the vectors, and the form `J_n` changes with `n`.  It therefore cannot be polarized between different indices to recover

\[
\mathbf d_r^{\mathsf T}\mathbf d_s=K(r,s).
\]

This is the precise linear-algebraic meaning of the obstruction:

- `b_n=0` says that the Delannoy row `\mathbf d_n` is isotropic for the standard dot product over `\mathbf F_p`;
- the CFVZ factorization says that the prefix `\mathbf q^{(n)}` is isotropic for the reversal form `J_n`;
- there is no checked-in isometry, correspondence, or common ambient quadratic space carrying one family to the other.

The equality of the diagonal scalar values alone does not determine the cross pairings.

## 2. Exact double generating function for `K(r,s)`

The useful elementary identity is

\[
D(n,k)
=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}
=\binom{2k}{k}\binom{n+k}{2k}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For fixed `k`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n\ge k}D(n,k)X^n
&=\binom{2k}{k}
  \sum_{n\ge k}\binom{n+k}{2k}X^n\\
&=\binom{2k}{k}\frac{X^k}{(1-X)^{2k+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr K(X,Y)
&=\sum_{k\ge0}
 \left(\sum_{r\ge k}D(r,k)X^r\right)
 \left(\sum_{s\ge k}D(s,k)Y^s\right)\\
&=\frac1{(1-X)(1-Y)}
  \sum_{k\ge0}\binom{2k}{k}^2
  \left(\frac{XY}{(1-X)^2(1-Y)^2}\right)^k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{(1/2)_k}{k!}=\frac1{4^k}\binom{2k}{k},
\]

(2.3) becomes (V1):

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr K(X,Y)=
\frac{1}{(1-X)(1-Y)}
{}_2F_1\!\left(
\frac12,\frac12;1;
\frac{16XY}{(1-X)^2(1-Y)^2}
\right).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This identity is over `\mathbb Z[[X,Y]]`; no reduction modulo `p` is involved.

Taking coefficient diagonal gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Diag}_{X,Y}\mathscr K(t)
&=\sum_{n\ge0}K(n,n)t^n\\
&=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n\\
&=C_+(t)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
\]

In a raw CFVZ class this reduces to the exact polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{
B_p(t)^2
=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}[X^nY^n]\mathscr K(X,Y)t^n
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[t].
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Equation (2.6) is the cleanest exact answer to “how are `K` and the CFVZ square root related?”

### Two operations that must not be confused

There are two different diagonal-looking operations here.

1. Coefficient diagonal of the bivariate kernel:

   \[
   \operatorname{Diag}_{X,Y}\mathscr K(t)
   =\sum_n[X^nY^n]\mathscr K\,t^n.
   \]

2. Ordinary one-variable product:

   \[
   B_p(t)^2
   =\sum_n\left(\sum_jq_jq_{n-j}\right)t^n.
   \]

The first selects entries `(n,n)` from the matrix `K(r,s)`.  The second sums products of `q`-coefficients along an anti-diagonal `j+(n-j)=n`.  Their equality is a nontrivial scalar identity, but it is not a coefficientwise identification of the two underlying bilinear forms.

Also,

\[
\mathscr K(t,t)
=\sum_{r,s}K(r,s)t^{r+s}
\]

is not the Apéry series.  Its coefficient of `t` is `K(0,1)+K(1,0)=2`, while the coefficient of `t` in `C_+(t)^2` is `b_1=5`.

## 3. The natural `q`-Gram candidates fail

### 3.1 Rank-one candidate

The matrix

\[
(q_rq_s)_{0\le r,s\le M}
\]

has rank at most one.  The Delannoy Gram matrix has full rank.

Indeed, let

\[
\mathcal D_M=(D(n,k))_{0\le n,k\le M}.
\]

It is lower triangular and

\[
D(n,n)=\binom{2n}{n}\ne0\pmod p
\qquad(0\le n\le M),
\]

because `2n\le p-1`.  Hence

\[
\mathcal K_M:=(K(r,s))_{0\le r,s\le M}
=\mathcal D_M\mathcal D_M^{\mathsf T}
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf F_p}\mathcal K_M=M+1.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Therefore `K(r,s)=q_rq_s` is impossible except in a one-point truncation.

### 3.2 Canonical shifted Toeplitz candidate

The scalar sequence `q_n` supplies no natural two-index family `q_j(r)`.  The most obvious choice is the shift family

\[
q_j(r)=q_{r-j},
\qquad q_m=0\text{ for }m<0,
\]

which gives

\[
L_q(r,s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,s)}q_{r-j}q_{s-j}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Its double generating function is

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr L_q(X,Y)
=\sum_{r,s\ge0}L_q(r,s)X^rY^s
=\frac{Q(X)Q(Y)}{1-XY}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This cannot equal `\mathscr K`.  The boundary already separates them:

\[
K(0,s)=1\quad\text{for every }s,
\]

so

\[
\mathscr K(0,Y)=\frac1{1-Y}.
\]

But

\[
L_q(0,s)=q_s,
\qquad
\mathscr L_q(0,Y)=Q(Y).
\]

Since `Q(Y)\ne(1-Y)^{-1}`, the natural shifted Gram is not `K`.

More generally, every construction of the form

\[
Q(X)Q(Y)W(XY)
\]

has boundary `W(0)Q(Y)` at `X=0`, so it cannot equal `\mathscr K(0,Y)=1/(1-Y)` unless `Q` is geometric.  The CFVZ square root is not geometric.

### 3.3 Bounded-rank corrections also cannot work

A formula involving a fixed number of separable terms,

\[
K(r,s)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{J}F_\nu(r)G_\nu(s)
\]

with `J` independent of `p`, would give rank at most `J`.  Equation (3.1) forces `J\ge M+1`.

Thus a true `q`-based Gram realization would need an entire `M+1`-dimensional family of transforms of `q`, not one square-root vector or a bounded number of corrections.  The CFVZ theorem supplies only the scalar polynomial `B_p`; it does not supply those `M+1` vectors.

## 4. Exact off-diagonal expression through two `q`-convolutions

Although no direct `q`-Gram identity exists, the Q2337 Racah recurrence gives an exact reconstruction of each row from two adjacent diagonal data.

Fix `r` and write

\[
Y_h=K(r,r+h).
\]

Set

\[
\mathcal C_m(r)
=(2r+2m+1)
\bigl(3r^2+2rm+m^2+3r+m+1\bigr).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The Racah recurrence is

\[
(r+m+1)^3Y_{m+1}
=\mathcal C_m(r)Y_m-(r+m)^3Y_{m-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define

\[
Z_m=(r+1)_m^3Y_m.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
Z_{m+1}
=\mathcal C_m(r)Z_m-(r+m)^6Z_{m-1}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The initial values are

\[
Z_0=b_r
\]

and, using the proved adjacent identity

\[
K(r,r-1)=\frac{b_r+b_{r-1}}6,
\]

one gets

\[
Z_1
=\left(\mathcal C_0(r)-\frac{r^3}{6}\right)b_r
 -\frac{r^3}{6}b_{r-1}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Now define the two continuants

\[
\mathcal R_0(r)=1,
\qquad
\mathcal R_1(r)=\mathcal C_1(r),
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_m(r)
=\mathcal C_m(r)\mathcal R_{m-1}(r)
 -(r+m)^6\mathcal R_{m-2}(r)
\qquad(m\ge2),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal S_0(r)=1,
\qquad
\mathcal S_1(r)=\mathcal C_0(r)-\frac{r^3}{6},
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{m+1}(r)
=\mathcal C_m(r)\mathcal S_m(r)
 -(r+m)^6\mathcal S_{m-1}(r)
\qquad(m\ge1).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Induction in (4.3) gives

\[
\boxed{
Z_h
=\mathcal S_h(r)b_r
 -\frac{r^3}{6}\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)b_{r-1}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Dividing by `(r+1)_h^3` proves

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)
=\frac{
\mathcal S_h(r)b_r
 -\dfrac{r^3}{6}\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)b_{r-1}
}{(r+1)_h^3}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This slightly extends the conditional formula recorded in Q2337: it keeps the `b_r` term instead of setting it to zero at the outset.

### Substitute the CFVZ square

In a raw-square class and in the folded range `0\le r<r+h\le M`, substitute

\[
b_r=(q*q)_r,
\qquad
b_{r-1}=(q*q)_{r-1}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)=
\frac{
\mathcal S_h(r)\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r}q_jq_{r-j}
-\dfrac{r^3}{6}\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)
 \displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}q_jq_{r-1-j}
}{(r+1)_h^3}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This is the exact `q`-coefficient relation requested in the question.

It is not of the form `q_rq_s+small correction`.  It uses two complete convolution coefficients, indexed by `r` and `r-1`, and it contains no distinguished `q_s` term.

When `b_r=0`, (4.9) becomes (V4):

\[
K(r,r+h)=
-\frac{r^3\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)}{6(r+1)_h^3}
(q*q)_{r-1}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

If `b_r=0`, no-consecutive-zero gives

\[
(q*q)_{r-1}=b_{r-1}\ne0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)=0
\iff
\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)=0
\qquad(b_r=0),
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

exactly as in Q2337.

If also `b_{r+h}=0`, the CFVZ information is simply

\[
(q*q)_{r+h}=0.
\tag{4.12}
\]

To close the gap one would need the new implication

\[
\boxed{
(q*q)_r=(q*q)_{r+h}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)\ne0.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

But, because `(q*q)_n=b_n`, (4.13) is just the desired noncollision theorem rewritten in the rank-two square-root coordinates.  It is not a formal consequence of the CFVZ factorization.

## 5. Why `b_n=0` does not mean `q_n=0`

For `1\le n\le M`, `q_0=1` gives

\[
b_n=2q_n+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}q_jq_{n-j}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
b_n=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q_n=-\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}q_jq_{n-j}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

There is no reason for the right side to vanish.  The zero condition makes `q_n` a specific quadratic expression in earlier coefficients; it does not annihilate `q_n`.

Conversely, `q_n=0` does not force `b_n=0`, because the interior convolution terms in (5.1) can remain nonzero.

This already makes a formula led by `q_rq_s` unsuitable as a nonvanishing mechanism: under two Apéry-zero conditions, `q_rq_s` itself becomes a product of two sums of lower cross terms, with no unit or sign control in `\mathbf F_p`.

## 8. Why the rank-two recurrence does not add an independent zero constraint

The coefficient recurrence (0.1) is rank two.  Its symmetric square is exactly the rank-three Apéry differential equation, as checked in `hasse_franel_descent.tex`.

Consequently, after forming

\[
b_n=(q*q)_n,
\]

the sequence `b_n` satisfies the same Apéry recurrence already used to define the gap continuant `N_h(r)`.

Thus, conditional on

\[
b_r=b_{r+h}=0,
\]

working with the rank-two `q` recurrence reproduces the same Apéry condition

\[
N_h(r)=0.
\]

On the kernel side, `K(r,r+h)=0` under `b_r=0` reproduces

\[
\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)=0.
\]

So the CFVZ descent returns exactly the same common-root problem

\[
N_h(r)=\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)=0.
\]

It does not create a third independent equation.  Any proof that uses only “`q` satisfies the rank-two equation and `q*q=b`” but no new two-variable structure is therefore expected to repackage, rather than eliminate, the existing `N_h/\mathcal R_{h-1}` obstruction.

## 9. What a genuinely useful polarized CFVZ statement would look like

Equation (2.4) shows that the full kernel is already an elliptic period:

\[
\mathscr K(X,Y)
=\frac1{(1-X)(1-Y)}
{}_2F_1\!\left(
\frac12,\frac12;1;
\frac{16XY}{(1-X)^2(1-Y)^2}
\right).
\]

This suggests three concrete, non-tautological targets.

### Target A: an algebraic pullback to the CFVZ rank-two period

Seek algebraic functions `\tau(X,Y)` and `J(X,Y)`, normalized at `(0,0)`, such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr K(X,Y)=J(X,Y)C_+(\tau(X,Y)).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

or such an identity after a finite modular/isogeny cover.

If (9.1) exists, then

\[
K(r,s)
=[X^rY^s]
J(X,Y)\sum_{n\ge0}c_n\tau(X,Y)^n,
\]

which would express `K(r,s)` as an explicit **linear transform of the square-root coefficients**.  This would be a real two-variable connection, unlike the scalar diagonal identity.  It would still require a coefficient nonvanishing theorem, but it would put both objects in one rank-two elliptic system.

No identity of the form (9.1) is proved in the connector-visible project state.

### Target B: a finite-characteristic addition theorem

A stronger possibility is a two-point identity with several square-root sections,

\[
\boxed{
K(r,s)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{M}
Q_{\nu,p}(r)Q_{\nu,p}(s),
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

where the `M+1` functions `Q_{\nu,p}` are generated functorially from `B_p` by Cartier, divided derivatives, or an explicit connection matrix.

The number `M+1` is forced by the full-rank calculation (3.1).  A single `B_p`, or a fixed number of its transforms, cannot suffice.

This is the finite-field analogue of the high-rank jet-crystalline realization identified in Q2329.

### Target C: a two-variable Cartier/Frobenius identity for `\mathscr K`

Instead of factoring only

\[
\operatorname{Diag}\mathscr K=A_p,
\]

seek a Frobenius relation for the whole bivariate period, for example a relation schematically of the form

\[
\mathscr K(X,Y)
=H_p(X,Y)\,
\mathscr K(X^p,Y^p)
\pmod p,
\tag{9.3}
\]

with an explicit bivariate Hasse polynomial `H_p`.  Its off-diagonal coefficients could retain information about `K(r,s)` that is destroyed by diagonal extraction.

A scalar CFVZ factorization of `A_p` cannot substitute for such an identity.

## 11. Final answer to the proposed strategy

1. **Is there a relation between `K(r,s)` and the CFVZ square root?**

   Yes.  The exact global relation is

   \[
   C_+(t)^2=\operatorname{Diag}_{X,Y}\mathscr K(t),
   \]

   with `\mathscr K` given explicitly by the elliptic hypergeometric formula (2.4).

2. **Is `K(r,s)` a Gram form directly built from the single sequence `q_n`?**

   No.  `q_rq_s` is rank one, the natural shifted Toeplitz Gram has the wrong boundary values, and the actual kernel has rank `(p+1)/2`.

3. **Can `K(r,s)` be written exactly using `q`?**

   Yes, after using the Racah recurrence:

   \[
   K(r,r+h)=
   \frac{\mathcal S_h(q*q)_r-rac{r^3}{6}\mathcal R_{h-1}(q*q)_{r-1}}
        {(r+1)_h^3}.
   \]

4. **Does `b_r=b_s=0` make the remaining correction explicitly nonzero?**

   No.  It makes `(q*q)_r=(q*q)_s=0`; no-consecutive-zero makes `(q*q)_{r-1}\ne0`; the unresolved factor is still `\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)`.

5. **Does a zero of `b_n` force `q_n=0`?**

   No.  At `p=11`, `b_5=0` but `q_5=1`.  Conversely, at `p=29`, `q_7=0` but `b_7=24`.

6. **What should be attacked next?**

   Not another scalar manipulation of `B_p^2`.  The concrete new target is a **two-variable elliptic pullback/addition theorem for `\mathscr K(X,Y)`**, or a bivariate Cartier/Frobenius factorization retaining the off-diagonal coefficients.  That is the level at which the CFVZ rank-two structure could genuinely interact with `K(r,s)`.

So the CFVZ square root is structurally relevant, but the existing scalar square is not yet the missing P3.2 nonvanishing theorem.  Its exact contribution is the diagonal identity (V2) and the convolution rewrite (V3)/(V4); the last obstruction remains the Racah gap continuant.
