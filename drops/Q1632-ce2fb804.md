# P3.2 punctured Hermite inverse: three-hole Riccati descent

## Verdict

There is a rigorous structural divisor theorem here, but the hoped-for closure through the punctured leading coefficient is false.

Let

\[
p=2m+1,\qquad \lambda_j=j(j+1),\qquad a=\lambda_r,
\]

with the canonical degree-\(2m\) polynomial

\[
P(Y)=\sum_{k=0}^m\phi_k(Y)^2
\]

and assume \(a=\lambda_r\) is the unique noncentral simple folded-grid zero. Put

\[
G=\prod_{j=0}^m(Y-\lambda_j),\qquad
H=\frac{G}{Y-a},\qquad
T=\prod_{j=r+1}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_j),\qquad
L=HT.
\]

For \(r\le m-2\),

\[
L=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(Y-\lambda_j)
  \prod_{j=r+1}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_j)^2
  (Y-\lambda_m),
\qquad
n:=\deg L=2m-r-1.
\]

Let

\[
\widehat V\equiv P^{-1}\pmod L,\qquad \deg\widehat V<n,
\]

and define \(v_z=\widehat V(\lambda_z)\). Then

\[
R_{\rm raw}(z)
=(z+1)^3v_{z-1}v_z
-(2z+1)(17\lambda_z+5)v_{z-1}v_{z+1}
+z^3v_zv_{z+1}
\]

is anti-invariant under \(z\mapsto-1-z\), hence uniquely

\[
R_{\rm raw}(z)=(2z+1)\widehat R(\lambda_z).
\]

The strongest divisor forced solely by the punctured Hermite contact plus the exact Apéry WZ source is, for the non-gap-one branch,

\[
\boxed{
D(Y)=
\prod_{j=0}^{r-2}(Y-\lambda_j)
\prod_{j=r+2}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_j)^2
(Y-\lambda_m)
\mid \widehat R(Y).
}
\tag{D}
\]

Thus the structural holes are exactly \(r-1,r,r+1\). The two outer holes are genuinely nonremovable:

\[
\boxed{
\widehat R(\lambda_{r-1})
=\frac{r^3}{(2r-1)b_{r-2}b_{r-1}}\ne0,
}
\tag{H-}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\widehat R(\lambda_{r+1})
=\frac{(r+1)^3}{(2r+3)b_{r+1}b_{r+2}}\ne0.
}
\tag{H+}
\]

At the middle hole,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat R(a)
=-\frac{17a+5}{b_{r-1}b_{r+1}}.
}
\tag{H0}
\]

Therefore \(Y-a\) is an additional recordwise factor exactly when \(17a+5=0\) at the value level; that is an arithmetic exceptional factor, not part of the universal structural divisor. Any still higher multiplicity there, or any extra multiplicity at a factor already appearing in \(D\), is an accidental quotient zero/jet and requires a new calculation.

For the gap-one branch \(r=m-1\), the center factor is **not** structurally forced. The correct guaranteed divisor is only

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm gap}(Y)=\prod_{j=0}^{m-3}(Y-\lambda_j).
}
\tag{Dgap}
\]

The three holes are then \(m-2,m-1,m\). If

\[
w=\widehat V(\lambda_{m-1}),\qquad
w'=\widehat V'(\lambda_{m-1}),\qquad
c_m=\widehat V(\lambda_m)=b_m^{-1},
\]

then the exact midpoint value is

\[
\boxed{
\widehat R(\lambda_m)
=\frac14\bigl(3c_mw-c_mw'-3w^2\bigr).
}
\tag{C}
\]

Hence a center factor occurs in gap one iff

\[
\boxed{c_mw'=3w(c_m-w).}
\tag{C0}
\]

That equation is not imposed by \(P\widehat V\equiv1\pmod L\), because \(\lambda_{m-1}=a\) is the puncture. So the proposed universal center factor in gap one is false unless one proves the genuinely new missing-target jet equation (C0).

The more important no-go is at infinity. If

\[
s=\deg\widehat V,\qquad c=\operatorname{lc}(\widehat V),
\]

then exactly

\[
\boxed{
\deg\widehat R=2s+1,\qquad
\operatorname{lc}(\widehat R)=-16c^2.
}
\tag{LC}
\]

Thus, for \(Q=\widehat R/D\) in the non-gap-one branch,

\[
\boxed{
\deg Q=2s+1-(2m-r-4),\qquad
\operatorname{lc}(Q)=-16c^2.
}
\tag{Qdeg}
\]

If

\[
\widehat q:=[Y^{n-1}]\widehat V\ne0,
\]

then \(s=n-1=2m-r-2\), so

\[
\boxed{
\deg Q=2m-r+1,\qquad
\operatorname{lc}(Q)=-16\widehat q^{\,2}.
}
\tag{Qfull}

This leading coefficient is quadratic in \(\widehat q\), hence is not a nonzero linear trace certificate. Worse, \(\widehat q\) is not the repository trace scalar \(q_0\). If \(A=P/(Y-a)\), \(M=(Y-a)L=GT\), and \(V_A\) is the full inverse of \(A\) modulo \(M\) used in Q1612/Q1613, then

Since \(L\) is monic,

\boxed{
q_0=[Y^{\deg M-1}]V_A

For \(r<m\), the folded-grid derivative is \(G'(a)=-1/2\), so

\kappa=-\frac{2}{P'(a)T(a)}.

This is the decisive obstruction. Under hypothetical trace failure \(q_0=0\), one gets

\[
\widehat q=-\kappa\ne0.

So the punctured inverse has **full** degree, not a degree drop, and

\deg Q=2m-r+1,\qquad
\operatorname{lc}(Q)=-16\kappa^2\ne0.

## 2. Why \(R_{\rm raw}\) is the right reciprocal Riccati numerator

For a polynomial representative \(V\), put

\[
f_V(z)=\frac1{V(\lambda_z)}.
\]

Then multiplication by the three neighboring values gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm raw}(z)
=v_{z-1}v_zv_{z+1}\,\mathcal L(f_{\widehat V})(z).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Indeed the first term of \(\mathcal L(f_{\widehat V})\) contributes

\[
(z+1)^3\frac{v_{z-1}v_zv_{z+1}}{v_{z+1}}
=(z+1)^3v_{z-1}v_z,
\]

and similarly for the other two terms.

Under \(z\mapsto-1-z\),

\[
\lambda_z\mapsto\lambda_z,\qquad
\lambda_{z-1}\leftrightarrow\lambda_{z+1},\qquad
2z+1\mapsto-(2z+1),
\]

and the first and third terms interchange with a minus sign. Hence

\[
R_{\rm raw}(-1-z)=-R_{\rm raw}(z).
\]

Because \(p\) is odd, the anti-invariant polynomials for this involution are exactly

\[
(2z+1)\,K[z(z+1)].
\]

This proves existence and uniqueness of \(\widehat R\).

## 3. Punctured Hermite contact and the Bezout error

Define

\[
\boxed{
B(Y)=\frac{P(Y)\widehat V(Y)-1}{L(Y)}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then wherever \(\widehat V\) is nonzero,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{\widehat V}-P
=-\frac{LB}{\widehat V}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

At every root of \(L\), \(P\widehat V=1\), so both \(P\) and \(\widehat V\) are units there. Consequently the order of reciprocal contact is at least the root multiplicity of \(L\):

- simple contact at \(\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_{r-1},\lambda_m\);
- double contact at \(\lambda_{r+1},\dots,\lambda_{m-1}\).

This is the exact source of the three-hole pattern. It is also the reason one must not treat all roots of \(L\) uniformly: the midpoint is ramified under \(z\mapsto\lambda_z\), and the punctured node \(a\) is not a root at all.

## 4. Proof of the non-gap-one divisor

Assume \(r\le m-2\). In the canonical singleton shell this implies \(r\ge2\): \(b_0=1\), and \(b_1=5\) can vanish only at \(p=5\), where \(m=2,r=1=m-1\) is gap one.

### 4.1 Lower block: simple factors \(0\le j\le r-2\)

At \(z=j\), all three folded arguments \(\lambda_{j-1},\lambda_j,\lambda_{j+1}\) are roots of \(L\) (with the usual fold \(\lambda_{-1}=\lambda_0\) at \(j=0\)). Hence

\[
(1/\widehat V-P)(\lambda_{j-1})
=(1/\widehat V-P)(\lambda_j)
=(1/\widehat V-P)(\lambda_{j+1})=0.
\]

Also the WZ source vanishes at every field point. Thus

\[
\mathcal L(1/\widehat V)(j)=0,
\]

and therefore \(R_{\rm raw}(j)=0\). Since \(2j+1\ne0\) and \(z\mapsto\lambda_z\) is unramified there,

\[
Y-\lambda_j\mid\widehat R.
\]

No second order is structurally forced: the reciprocal error has only simple Hermite contact at these lower nodes.

### 4.2 Interior tail: double factors \(r+2\le j\le m-2\)

Now all three neighboring folded nodes belong to the doubled part of \(L\). Equation (3.2) therefore gives second-order vanishing of each reciprocal-error term after pullback to \(z=j\). The exact WZ source has a double zero there because

\[
z^p-z=-(z-j)+O((z-j)^p),\qquad 2j+1\ne0.
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal L(1/\widehat V)(z)=O((z-j)^2).
\]

The three \(v\)-factors are units at the node, so

\[
R_{\rm raw}(z)=O((z-j)^2).
\]

Since \(\lambda_z-\lambda_j=(2j+1)(z-j)+O((z-j)^2)\), this gives

\[
(Y-\lambda_j)^2\mid\widehat R.
\]

### 4.3 Last tail center \(j=m-1\)

When \(r\le m-3\), this index is part of the doubled range in (D). The only apparent problem is the right shift, because \(\lambda_m\) is only a simple root of \(L\). But at \(z=m-1+t\),

\[
\lambda_{z+1}-\lambda_m=t^2.
\]

Thus the simple reciprocal error at \(\lambda_m\) pulls back with order two. The other two shifted errors have genuine double Hermite contact, and the WZ source again has order two at \(m-1\). Therefore

\[
R_{\rm raw}(z)=O((z-(m-1))^2)
\]

and \(\lambda_z\) is unramified at \(m-1\), giving

\[
(Y-\lambda_{m-1})^2\mid\widehat R.
\]

This is the endpoint exception that a naive multiplicity count in the \(Y\)-variable misses.

### 4.4 Midpoint \(j=m\)

At \(z=m+t\),

\[
2z+1=2t,\qquad \lambda_z-\lambda_m=t^2.
\]

The central reciprocal error has simple \(Y\)-contact at \(\lambda_m\), hence order two in \(t\). Both shifted values land at \(\lambda_{m-1}\), where \(L\) has multiplicity two, so those errors also have order at least two. The WZ source itself has order three:

\[
-16(2t)(z^p-z)^2=O(t^3).
\]

Therefore \(R_{\rm raw}=O(t^2)\). But \(R_{\rm raw}\) is anti-invariant, and the involution is exactly \(t\mapsto-t\) at the fixed midpoint. Hence its order is odd, so in fact

\[
R_{\rm raw}=O(t^3).
\]

Since

\[
R_{\rm raw}=2t\,\widehat R(\lambda_m+t^2),
\]

we obtain

\[
Y-\lambda_m\mid\widehat R.
\]

Combining 4.1--4.4 proves (D).

## 5. The three holes are not bookkeeping artifacts

Put \(b_j=P(\lambda_j)\). For \(j\ne r\),

\[
v_j=b_j^{-1}.
\]

The punctured value

\[
w:=\widehat V(a)
\]

is not fixed by the congruence. Nevertheless it cancels from all three hole values by the Apéry recurrence.

### 5.1 Left hole

At \(z=r-1\), the terms containing \(w\) have coefficient

\[
-\frac{(2r-1)(17\lambda_{r-1}+5)}{b_{r-2}}
+\frac{(r-1)^3}{b_{r-1}}.
\]

The recurrence at \(r-1\), together with \(b_r=0\), says this is zero. Therefore

\[
R_{\rm raw}(r-1)
=\frac{r^3}{b_{r-2}b_{r-1}},
\]

which yields (H-). Every denominator is a unit and \(r,2r-1\ne0\), so the value is nonzero.

### 5.2 Target hole

At \(z=r\), the two \(w\)-terms cancel because the recurrence at the zero gives

\[
(r+1)^3b_{r+1}+r^3b_{r-1}=0.
\]

Thus

\[
R_{\rm raw}(r)
=-(2r+1)(17a+5)(b_{r-1}b_{r+1})^{-1},
\]

which is (H0).

So the target is a genuine hole except on the explicit arithmetic locus

\[
17\lambda_r+5=0\pmod p.
\]

If that locus occurs, \(Y-a\) is a new factor. A second factor at \(a\) would require the next target jet and is not implied by the value equation.

### 5.3 Right hole

At \(z=r+1\), the \(w\)-terms cancel by the recurrence at \(r+1\), since again \(b_r=0\). The surviving term gives (H+). Its numerator and denominator are units for \(r\le m-2\), so it is always nonzero.

This proves that the outer two holes cannot be filled by any hidden consequence of the same recurrence.

## 6. Gap one: why the center factor fails

Take \(r=m-1\), so \(a=\lambda_{m-1}\) is omitted from \(L\). The lower simple factors through \(m-3\) survive exactly as before. But at the midpoint the shifted arguments both land at the puncture, not at a doubled Hermite node.

Write \(z=m+t\). Then

\[
\lambda_z=\lambda_m+t^2,
\]

\[
\lambda_{z-1}=a-2t+t^2,\qquad
\lambda_{z+1}=a+2t+t^2.
\]

With

\[
w=\widehat V(a),\quad w'=\widehat V'(a),\quad c_m=\widehat V(\lambda_m),
\]

we have

\[
\widehat V(\lambda_{z-1})=w-2w't+O(t^2),
\]

\[
\widehat V(\lambda_{z+1})=w+2w't+O(t^2),
\]

and

\[
\widehat V(\lambda_z)=c_m+O(t^2).
\]

In \(\mathbf F_p\), \(m=-1/2\), so

\[
(m+1+t)^3=\frac18+\frac34t+O(t^2),
\]

\[
(m+t)^3=-\frac18+\frac34t+O(t^2).
\]

Also \(\lambda_m=-1/4\), hence

\[
17\lambda_m+5=\frac34.
\]

Substituting gives

\[
R_{\rm raw}(m+t)
=\frac12\bigl(3c_mw-c_mw'-3w^2\bigr)t+O(t^3).
\]

Since \(2z+1=2t\), division gives exactly (C). There is no reason for (C) to vanish. The missing equation (C0) is therefore the exact gap-one center obstruction.

## 7. Exact degree and leading coefficient

Let

\[
\widehat V(Y)=cY^s+O(Y^{s-1}),\qquad c\ne0.
\]

Then

\[
\widehat V(\lambda_{z+k})=c z^{2s}+O(z^{2s-1})
\]

for each fixed \(k\in\{-1,0,1\}\). The first and third terms of \(R_{\rm raw}\) contribute

\[
2c^2z^{4s+3}.
\]

The leading part of the middle term contributes

\[
-34c^2z^{4s+3}.
\]

This formula is valid even at \(p=17\): there the middle degree-\(4s+3\) coefficient is zero because \(34=0\), and \(-32=2\) in \(\mathbf F_{17}\). Therefore for every odd \(p\),

\[
R_{\rm raw}(z)=-32c^2z^{4s+3}+O(z^{4s+2}).
\]

Since \(R_{\rm raw}=(2z+1)\widehat R(\lambda_z)\),

\[
\deg\widehat R=2s+1,
\qquad
2\operatorname{lc}(\widehat R)=-32c^2,
\]

which proves (LC).

For non-gap one,

\[
\deg D=(r-1)+2(m-r-2)+1=2m-r-4.
\]

This gives (Qdeg). In particular, if \(\widehat q\ne0\), then \(s=n-1\), and (Qfull) follows.

For gap one,

\[
\deg D_{\rm gap}=m-2.
\]

If \(\widehat q\ne0\) then \(n=m\), \(s=m-1\), and

\[
\boxed{
\deg(\widehat R/D_{\rm gap})=m+1,\qquad
\operatorname{lc}(\widehat R/D_{\rm gap})=-16\widehat q^2.
}
\]

Again the leading coefficient is quadratic and therefore cannot by itself supply the desired linear trace contradiction.

## 8. Exact Bezout-cofactor formula for \(Q=\widehat R/D\)

Use the shorthand

\[
Y_0=\lambda_z,\qquad
Y_+=\lambda_{z+1},\qquad
Y_-=\lambda_{z-1},
\]

\[
V_\bullet=\widehat V(Y_\bullet),\qquad
L_\bullet=L(Y_\bullet),\qquad
B_\bullet=B(Y_\bullet),
\]

and \(u=2z+1\). From (3.2),

\[
\frac1{V_\bullet}=P(Y_\bullet)-\frac{L_\bullet B_\bullet}{V_\bullet}.
\]

Apply the Apéry operator and multiply by \(V_-V_0V_+\). The WZ part is

\[
-16u\,\Phi(Y_0)V_-V_0V_+.
\]

The Bezout-error part is

\[
\mathcal E_B(z)=
(z+1)^3L_+B_+V_-V_0
-u(17Y_0+5)L_0B_0V_-V_+
+z^3L_-B_-V_0V_+.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm raw}(z)
=-16u\Phi(Y_0)V_-V_0V_+-\mathcal E_B(z).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Dividing by \(uD(Y_0)\) gives the requested exact quotient formula

\[
\boxed{
Q(\lambda_z)
=-16\frac{\Phi(Y_0)}{D(Y_0)}V_-V_0V_+
-\frac{\mathcal E_B(z)}{uD(Y_0)}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

The second displayed term is written in pulled-back \(z\)-coordinates, but the divisor theorem proves its combined numerator is divisible by \(uD(\lambda_z)\); anti-invariance then makes the quotient an invariant polynomial, hence a polynomial in \(Y=\lambda_z\).

For reference,

\[
\boxed{
L=D\,(Y-\lambda_{r-1})(Y-\lambda_{r+1})^2
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

in the non-gap branch, and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Phi}{D}
=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{r-2}(Y-\lambda_j)\right)
(Y-\lambda_{r-1})^2(Y-a)^2(Y-\lambda_{r+1})^2.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Formula (8.2) is useful because it shows exactly why a quotient value or jet is not automatically a trace coefficient: after the forced divisor is removed, the surviving data are values and jets of the global Bezout cofactor \(B\), not merely the coefficient at infinity of \(\widehat V\).

## 9. The affine bridge \(\widehat q=q_0-\kappa\)

This is the main closure-killer.

Set

\[
A=\frac{P}{Y-a},\qquad M=GT=(Y-a)L.
\]

Let \(V_A\) be the unique reduced inverse of \(A\) modulo \(M\), the object used in Q1612/Q1613. Since

\[
A(Y-a)\widehat V=P\widehat V\equiv1\pmod L,
\]

both \(V_A\) and \((Y-a)\widehat V\) are inverses of \(A\) modulo \(L\). Their difference is divisible by \(L\). Both have degree at most \(n\), while \(\deg L=n\), so the difference is a scalar multiple:

\[
V_A=(Y-a)\widehat V+\kappa L.
\]

Evaluate at \(Y=a\). The first term vanishes, while

\[
V_A(a)=A(a)^{-1}=P'(a)^{-1}.
\]

Thus

\[
\kappa L(a)=P'(a)^{-1},
\]

which proves (bridge).

The simplification (kappa) follows from

\[
L(a)=H(a)T(a)=G'(a)T(a)
\]

and \(G'(\lambda_r)=-1/2\) for \(r<m\). One quick derivation is to write

\[
K(Y)=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_j),
\]

use

\[
z^p-z=\frac{2z+1}{2}K(\lambda_z),
\]

and differentiate at \(z=r\). This gives

\[
K'(\lambda_r)=-\frac{2}{(2r+1)^2}.
\]

Since

\[
\lambda_r-\lambda_m=\frac{(2r+1)^2}{4},
\]

one gets

\[
G'(\lambda_r)
=(\lambda_r-\lambda_m)K'(\lambda_r)
=-\frac12.
\]

Now compare the coefficient of \(Y^n\) in the bridge. The coefficient from \((Y-a)\widehat V\) is \(\widehat q=[Y^{n-1}]\widehat V\); the coefficient from \(\kappa L\) is \(\kappa\). Q1612 identifies the left coefficient with \(q_0\). Hence

\[
q_0=\widehat q+\kappa.
\]
