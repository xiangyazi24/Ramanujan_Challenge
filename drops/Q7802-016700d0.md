# Verdict

Take the cubic in the question as the defining hypothesis and put

\[
b=b_r,\qquad c=p\chi(p)b,\qquad d=p^3,
\]
\[
f(X):=\det(X-\Phi)=X^3-bX^2+cX-d.
\]

At an ordinary label, \(p\nmid b\), there is a unique unit root
\(\alpha\in\overline{\mathbf Q}_p^\times\), characterized by

\[
f(\alpha)=0,\qquad \alpha\equiv b\pmod p.
\]

The exact unit-root projector is the quadratic polynomial in Frobenius

\[
\boxed{
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{p,r}
=
\frac{
 \Phi^2-(b-\alpha)\Phi+(p^3/\alpha)I
}{
 3\alpha^2-2b\alpha+p\chi(p)b
}.}
\tag{1}
\]

The denominator is a \(p\)-adic unit. Formula (1) is the canonical answer in
**any specified crystalline basis**: substitute the actual \(3\times3\)
Frobenius matrix in that basis. The characteristic polynomial alone does not
determine the nine numerical entries of the matrix, because it does not give
the change of basis from a cyclic/eigenbasis to the chosen crystalline basis.

In the cyclic companion basis \((e,\Phi e,\Phi^2e)\), however, both \(\Phi\)
and the projector are completely explicit. One has

\[
F_c=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&p^3\\
1&0&-p\chi(p)b\\
0&1&b
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_c
=
\frac1{f'(\alpha)}
\begin{pmatrix}
 p^3/\alpha\\[2pt]
 \alpha-b\\[2pt]
 1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
 1&\alpha&\alpha^2
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus it is visibly an idempotent of rank one.

At \((p,r)=(11,5)\),

\[
b_5=819005=11\cdot74455,\qquad 11\nmid74455.
\]

The Newton polygon of the stated cubic has **three slopes exactly equal to
\(1\)**, not merely three unspecified positive slopes. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{11,5}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{11,5})=0\) as well.

There is also an important rank correction to the proposed tensor step:

- \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)\) for the full rank-three crystal has rank \(6\);
- removing its polarization/Tate line gives the traceless symmetric square
  \(\operatorname{Sym}^2_0(V)\), of rank \(5\);
- but \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})\) is the symmetric square of a
  **rank-one projector**, so it has rank \(1\), not rank \(5\);
- its image is already orthogonal to the duality line, so deleting the duality
  invariant does not lower that rank;
- reflection is an external correspondence between the \(r\)- and
  \(p-1-r\)-fibres, not an additional universal invariant inside a single
  \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_r)\). On the reflected diagonal its projected graph
  is at most the same one-dimensional unit-root line.

Therefore the phrase
“connected \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})\) minus
 duality/reflection invariants” does not define a new rank-five object. The
rank-five object is \(\operatorname{Sym}^2_0(V)\); its slope-zero projector is
still rank one. If “connected” additionally means deleting the decomposable
unit-root square itself, the fibrewise remainder is rank zero.

# 1. Exact ordinary projector

## 1.1 Spectral formula

Let the three Frobenius eigenvalues be

\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,
\]

ordered so that

\[
v_p(\alpha)=0,\qquad v_p(\beta)=1,\qquad v_p(\gamma)=2.
\]

The coefficient identities are

\[
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=b,
\qquad
\alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma+\beta\gamma=p\chi(p)b,
\qquad
\alpha\beta\gamma=p^3.
\tag{5}
\]

Since \(b\) is a unit, reduction modulo \(p\) gives

\[
f(X)\equiv X^2(X-b)\pmod p.
\]

Hence \(X=b\) is a simple root modulo \(p\), and Hensel gives the unique unit
root \(\alpha\). It may be computed to arbitrary precision by

\[
\alpha_0=b,
\qquad
\alpha_{n+1}
=\alpha_n-rac{f(\alpha_n)}{f'(\alpha_n)}.
\tag{6}
\]

For example,

\[
\alpha\equiv b-p\chi(p)\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{7}
\]

The Lagrange spectral idempotent is

\[
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}
=rac{(\Phi-\beta I)(\Phi-\gamma I)}
       {(\alpha-\beta)(\alpha-\gamma)}.
\tag{8}
\]

Using

\[
\beta+\gamma=b-\alpha,
\qquad
\beta\gamma=p^3/\alpha,
\]

and

\[
(\alpha-\beta)(\alpha-\gamma)
=f'(\alpha)
=3\alpha^2-2b\alpha+p\chi(p)b,
\]

gives formula (1). Modulo \(p\),

\[
f'(\alpha)\equiv b^2\not\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

so no denominator problem occurs on the ordinary locus.

The identities

\[
(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})^2=\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}},
\qquad
\Phi\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}=\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}\Phi
=\alpha\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}},
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}=1
\tag{9}
\]

follow immediately.

## 1.2 Entrywise matrix in an arbitrary crystalline basis

Let

\[
F=(F_{ij})_{1\le i,j\le3}
\]

be the actual Frobenius matrix in the chosen crystalline basis. Then (1) says
entry by entry

\[
\boxed{
(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})_{ij}
=
\frac{
 \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^3F_{ik}F_{kj}
 -(b-\alpha)F_{ij}
 +(p^3/\alpha)\delta_{ij}
}{
 3\alpha^2-2b\alpha+p\chi(p)b
}.}
\tag{10}
\]

This is as explicit as the data in the question allow. A characteristic
polynomial fixes the eigenvalues but not the matrix entries in a named basis.
If the repository supplies a change-of-basis matrix \(S\) from the companion
basis to a crystalline basis, then

\[
F_{\mathrm{crys}}=SF_cS^{-1},
\qquad
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{\mathrm{crys}}
=S\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_cS^{-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

Without \(S\), no further numerical \(3\times3\) matrix is canonical.

## 1.3 Companion-basis matrix

In the cyclic basis, define

\[
u_\alpha=
\begin{pmatrix}
p^3/\alpha\\ \alpha-b\\1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\ell_\alpha=
\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha&\alpha^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
F_cu_\alpha=\alpha u_\alpha,
\qquad
\ell_\alpha F_c=\alpha\ell_\alpha,
\qquad
\ell_\alpha u_\alpha=f'(\alpha).
\tag{12}
\]

Therefore (3) follows. Expanded, it is

\[
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_c
=rac1{f'(\alpha)}
\begin{pmatrix}
 p^3/\alpha&p^3&p^3\alpha\\
 \alpha-b&\alpha^2-b\alpha&\alpha^3-b\alpha^2\\
 1&\alpha&\alpha^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{13}
\]

The middle entries may equivalently be rewritten using \(f(\alpha)=0\):

\[
\alpha^2-b\alpha=p^3/\alpha-p\chi(p)b,
\]

\[
\alpha^3-b\alpha^2=p^3-p\chi(p)b\alpha.
\]

## 1.4 Relation with the Dwork limit

For a linear Frobenius over \(\mathbf Q_p\), the exact spectral formula also
satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}
=\lim_{N\to\infty}
 \Phi^{(p-1)p^N}.}
\tag{14}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\alpha^{(p-1)p^N}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\beta^{(p-1)p^N},\gamma^{(p-1)p^N}\longrightarrow0.
\]

The factorial version \(\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi^{n!}\) gives the same limit.
If crystalline Frobenius is initially \(\sigma\)-semilinear over a nontrivial
unramified coefficient extension, one must first pass to the corresponding
linearized \(q\)-Frobenius; then \(q-1\), not blindly \(p-1\), is the relevant
Teichmüller exponent. Formula (1) already presupposes the linear operator whose
characteristic polynomial is the displayed cubic.

# 2. Tensor order two: the exact rank ledger

Let

\[
V=U_0\oplus U_1\oplus U_2
\]

be the slope decomposition on the ordinary locus, with each \(U_i\) a line of
slope \(i\). Then

\[
\operatorname{im}\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}=U_0.
\]

## 2.1 Literal symmetric square of the projector

The induced endomorphism on \(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\) is

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})(x\odot y)
=\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}x\odot\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}y.
\tag{15}
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{im}\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})
=\operatorname{Sym}^2(U_0)=U_0^{\otimes2},
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})=1.}
\tag{16}
\]

Using the companion-basis vectors in (12), one can write it without a
six-by-six expansion:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})(x\odot y)
=
\frac{\ell_\alpha(x)\ell_\alpha(y)}{f'(\alpha)^2}
\,u_\alpha\odot u_\alpha.}
\tag{17}
\]

This formula makes rank one immediate.

## 2.2 The rank-five object is different

The full symmetric square has

\[
\dim\operatorname{Sym}^2V=6.
\]

Its slope decomposition is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{summand}&\text{slope}&\text{rank}\\ \hline
U_0^2&0&1\\
U_0U_1&1&1\\
U_0U_2\oplus U_1^2&2&2\\
U_1U_2&3&1\\
U_2^2&4&1.
\end{array}
\tag{18}
\]

Assume the expected orthogonal polarization

\[
q:V\otimes V\longrightarrow L
\]

with similitude/Tate line \(L\) of slope \(2\). The inverse-polarization
(coevaluation) tensor spans one line

\[
\mathbf q^\vee\subset\operatorname{Sym}^2V\otimes L^{-1}.
\]

In a slope basis it has the form, up to nonzero scalars,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf q^\vee
=u_0\odot u_2+\kappa\,u_1\odot u_1.}
\tag{19}
\]

It lies in slope \(2\). Removing this line from the **full** symmetric square
gives

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2V
=L\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2_0V,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\dim\operatorname{Sym}^2_0V=5.}
\tag{20}
\]

For geometric monodromy \(\mathrm{SO}_3\), this is the familiar irreducible
five-dimensional traceless symmetric-square representation.

But

\[
q(u_0,u_0)=0
\]

because the polarization pairs slope \(0\) with slope \(2\), not with itself.
Consequently \(u_0^2\) is already traceless, and

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})(\mathbf q^\vee)=0.
\]

If \(\pi_0\) denotes the projection to \(\operatorname{Sym}^2_0V\), then

\[
\boxed{
\pi_0\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})\pi_0
=\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}),}
\tag{21}
\]

so deleting the duality line leaves rank \(1\), not rank \(0\) or rank \(5\).
Equivalently, the unit-root projector of the rank-five crystal
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2_0V\) is precisely the rank-one operator in (17).

## 2.3 Forced tensors at order two

The forced tensor ledger is as follows.

### (a) Duality/identity tensor

In \(V\otimes V^*\), the invariant is

\[
\operatorname{id}_V=\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i^*.
\]

After identifying \(V^*\) with a Tate twist of \(V\) through the polarization,
this is the same one-dimensional line as \(\mathbf q^\vee\) in (19). It is the
only universal order-two invariant for generic \(\mathrm{SO}_3\) monodromy.

### (b) Reflection graph

Let

\[
\iota(r)=p-1-r.
\]

A genuine lifted reflection is an isomorphism

\[
R_r:V_r\longrightarrow V_{\iota(r)}.
\]

Its graph tensor is

\[
\Gamma_{R_r}
=\sum_i e_i^*\otimes R_r(e_i)
\in V_r^*\otimes V_{\iota(r)}.
\tag{22}
\]

This spans a correspondence line on the **external reflected diagonal**
\(s=\iota(r)\). It is not a universal subspace of a single
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_r)\). After unit-root projection,

\[
(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_r)^*\otimes
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{\iota(r)}(\Gamma_{R_r})
\]

has rank at most one and is the graph of the identification between the two
unit-root lines.

At a fixed point of reflection, such as \(r=(p-1)/2\), the unique ordinary
unit-root line would be stable under \(R_r\). Reflection acts on that line by a
scalar, and its square \(U_0^2\) is reflection-even. If one elects to delete
that entire reflected unit-root graph as a disconnected diagonal term, the
rank-one image is killed and the remainder is zero. This is a stratified
external-correlation subtraction, not a second internal invariant forced by
the cubic.

### (c) Volume/determinant tensor

The volume tensor is

\[
\varepsilon_V\in\bigwedge^3V.
\]

It is forced by the determinant and spans a Tate/determinant line, but it is a
**tensor-order-three** invariant. It does not occur in
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\) and must not be subtracted at tensor order two.

## 2.4 What “connected” can consistently mean

There are three different objects that should not be conflated.

1. **Full traceless symmetric square:**
   \[
   \operatorname{Sym}^2_0V,
   \qquad \text{rank }5.
   \]
2. **Its slope-zero projector:**
   \[
   \operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}),
   \qquad \text{rank }1\text{ on the ordinary locus}.
   \]
3. **A cumulant/connected external tensor:** remove constant, duality and
   reflected-diagonal subobjects from a family over two parameter variables.
   Its rank depends on the actual family and on the reflection map; it is not
   determined by one fibre’s characteristic polynomial.

If “connected” means subtracting the decomposable square of the sole unit-root
line from item 2, the result is tautologically zero. A rank-one line has no
nontrivial fibrewise second cumulant.

This is the central correction to the proposed Q7786 geometric step. A
nontrivial primitive object can live in the varying rank-five crystal
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2_0V\), or in an external two-point tensor
\(V_r\boxtimes V_s\) after diagonal stratification. It cannot be manufactured
by symmetrically squaring a rank-one projector and then expecting a new
higher-rank connected representation.

The further assertion needed for the Apéry proof—no rank-one Kummer or
translation subquotient after all forced strata are removed—is a monodromy
statement about that varying/external object. It does not follow from the
cubic or from the rank calculation.

# 3. The target \(p=11,r=5\)

## 3.1 Exact cubic

The fifth Apéry number is

\[
b_5=819005=11\cdot74455.
\]

Therefore

\[
\det(1-\Phi t)
=1-819005t
+9{,}009{,}055\,\chi(11)t^2
-1331t^3,
\tag{24}
\]

and

\[
f_{11,5}(X)
=X^3-819005X^2
+9{,}009{,}055\,\chi(11)X
-1331.
\tag{25}
\]

At a good prime, \(\chi(11)\) is a \(11\)-adic unit. The coefficient
valuations of \(f_{11,5}\), ordered from constant term to leading term, are

\[
(3,2,1,0).
\tag{26}
\]

Thus the four Newton points are

\[
(0,3),(1,2),(2,1),(3,0),
\]

all lying on one segment of slope \(-1\). Hence the three roots of
\(f_{11,5}\) all have valuation \(1\).

Equivalently, writing \(X=11Y\),

\[
\boxed{
11^{-3}f_{11,5}(11Y)
=Y^3-74455Y^2+74455\chi(11)Y-1.}
\tag{27}
\]

The three roots in \(Y\) are \(11\)-adic units; the Frobenius eigenvalues are
\(11\) times those units.

More generally, under the stated cubic, any target \(p\mid b_r\) has all
three slopes exactly \(1\). Indeed, if \(v_p(b_r)=m\ge1\), the two interior
Newton points are

\[
(1,m+1),\qquad(2,m),
\]

which lie on or above the segment from \((0,3)\) to \((3,0)\).

## 3.2 Explicit matrices

The companion representative is

\[
F_{c,11,5}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&1331\\
1&0&-9{,}009{,}055\,\chi(11)\\
0&1&819005
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{28}
\]

There is no unit root \(\alpha\), so the ordinary formula (1) must not be
specialized by inventing one. The slope-zero summand is the zero space, and
its spectral idempotent is exactly (4). In the limit formulation,

\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{c,11,5}^{\,10\cdot11^N}=0.
\tag{29}
\]

The slopes of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\) are all \(2\), because every pairwise
sum of the three slope-one eigenvalues is \(2\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{11,5})=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}=0.}
\tag{30}
\]

The same is true after passing to the rank-five traceless symmetric square:
it has no slope-zero line at this target.

# 4. Consequence for the proposed proof architecture

The local detector survives:

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{p,r}
=
\begin{cases}
1,&p\nmid b_r,\\
0,&p\mid b_r,
\end{cases}
\]

under the Q7802 slope dichotomy. But the tensor-order-two step needs to be
rephrased.

A mathematically coherent target is one of the following.

1. Construct the varying rank-five crystal
   \(\operatorname{Sym}^2_0V\), classify its one-dimensional Tate/reflection
   strata, and prove primitivity for the remaining external two-point sheaf.
   Its ordinary slope-zero projector is (17).
2. Work directly with
   \(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_r\boxtimes\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_s\) over a two-parameter
   base, remove the ordinary diagonal and reflected diagonal as geometric
   strata, and prove a Type-II estimate for the complement.

What is **not** available is a nonzero higher-rank connected fibre obtained
from \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}})\) alone. Fibrewise that object
has only one line to begin with. This rank obstruction should be resolved
before invoking bilinear trace-function technology or claiming that all
rank-one Kummer/translation constituents have been removed.
