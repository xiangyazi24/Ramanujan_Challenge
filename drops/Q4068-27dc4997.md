# Verdict

**No useful additional small carrier or common valuation is forced.** There is one exact characteristic-zero consequence:

$$
pq\mid N_{6q}(6q+u),
$$

where $N_h(X)$ is the standard Apéry transfer continuant. However,

$$
\log N_{6q}(6q+u)=18q\log q+O(q),
$$

so this carrier is larger, not smaller, than the original endpoint carrier $b_{6q+u}$, whose logarithmic height is only $O(q)$. Reverse transfer gives the same continuant up to sign. Passing to a nonzero resultant raises the logarithmic height to $\Theta(q^2\log q)$. Finally, an exact affine-prime specialization has all four $p$- and $q$-adic endpoint valuations equal to one, so there is no forced lift to $p^2q$, $pq^2$, or $(pq)^2$.

Thus recurrence, Lucas/reflection, the Wronskian, and the banked resultants only **repackage** the plus event; they do not yield aggregate compression.

# 1. The exact long-transfer carrier

Write

$$
P(m)=34m^3+51m^2+27m+5
$$

and define

$$
N_1(X)=1,\qquad N_2(X)=P(X+1),
$$

$$
N_{h+1}(X)=P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X).
$$

Set

$$
D_h(X)=\prod_{j=2}^{h}(X+j)^3.
$$

A direct induction on the Apéry recurrence gives the exact integer identity

$$
\boxed{
D_h(X)b_{X+h}
 =N_h(X)b_{X+1}-(X+1)^3N_{h-1}(X+1)b_X.
}
\tag{1}
$$

Let $\ell>3$ be prime and suppose $\ell\mid b_X$ and $\ell\mid b_{X+h}$. The project’s adjacent-coprimality/Wronskian consequence gives $\ell\nmid b_{X+1}$. Reducing (1) modulo $\ell$ therefore yields

$$
\ell\mid N_h(X).
$$

For the plus event $X=n=6q+u$ and $h=M-n=6q$, both $p$ and $q$ divide the two endpoints, hence

$$
\boxed{pq\mid N_{6q}(6q+u).}
\tag{2}
$$

This is the strongest uniform characteristic-zero carrier supplied by the bare recurrence.

## Exact height

For $X\ge0$, put $R_j=N_j(X)/N_{j-1}(X)$. Since

$$
P(t)-(t+1)^3=33t^3+48t^2+24t+4>0,
$$

and

$$
P(t)-t^3-(t+1)^3=32t^3+48t^2+24t+4>0,
$$

induction in the Riccati recurrence

$$
R_{j+1}=P(X+j)-\frac{(X+j)^6}{R_j}
$$

gives

$$
(X+j)^3\le R_j\le P(X+j-1)\le117(X+j-1)^3.
$$

Consequently

$$
\prod_{j=2}^{h}(X+j)^3
 \le N_h(X)
 \le117^{h-1}\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(X+j)^3.
\tag{3}
$$

For $h=6q$, $X=6q+u$, and $0\le u<q$, (3) specializes to the completely explicit bounds

$$
(6q)^{18q-3}
 \le N_{6q}(6q+u)
 \le \bigl(117(13q)^3\bigr)^{6q-1}.
\tag{4}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\log N_{6q}(6q+u)=18q\log q+O(q).}
\tag{5}
$$

This is not an aggregate-compressing height. A divisor $pq\asymp q^2$ costs only $2\log q+O(1)$ logarithmic size, whereas (5) can accommodate $\Theta(q)$ such prime pairs even under an unrealistically favorable common-carrier interpretation. In reality the continuant also depends on the event parameter $u$. Moreover, the elementary binomial bound

$$
b_m\le(m+1)64^m
$$

already gives $\log b_{6q+u}=O(q)$, so the new transfer carrier is asymptotically worse than the original endpoint integer.

# 2. Lucas and reflection supply only one root condition per prime orbit

For a prime $\ell$ and $m=a\ell+r$, $0\le r<\ell$, Lucas gives

$$
b_m\equiv b_a b_r\pmod\ell.
$$

At the $q$-side of the displayed plus event,

$$
b_{6q+u}\equiv b_6b_u\pmod q,
\qquad
b_{12q+u}\equiv b_{12}b_u\pmod q.
\tag{6}
$$

Outside the finite set of primes dividing $b_6b_{12}$, the two endpoint conditions in (6) are exactly the **same** condition $q\mid b_u$. They are not two equations whose resultant could be taken.

Reflection likewise does not add an equation:

$$
b_{\ell-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod\ell.
$$

It identifies two representatives of one root orbit. In the reverse-shift plus geometry, moving a root between a low and a high base-$p$ digit, or replacing it by its reflected digit, still consumes only one Lucas zero.

For valuation lifting, define the exact Lucas quotient

$$
L^{(\ell)}_{a,r}
 =\frac{b_{a\ell+r}-b_ab_r}{\ell}\pmod\ell.
$$

If $\ell\mid b_r$, then

$$
\frac{b_{a\ell+r}}{\ell}
 \equiv b_a\frac{b_r}{\ell}+L^{(\ell)}_{a,r}\pmod\ell.
\tag{7}
$$

Neither ordinary Lucas nor reflection controls the right side of (7). In particular, for the two $q$-blocks $a=6,12$, no named identity forces either quotient to vanish, much less both. A mod-$\ell^2$ cross-digit supercongruence would be genuinely new input.

# 3. The Wronskian is first-order, not a multiplicity statement

For the standard companion $a_m$,

$$
a_m b_{m+1}-a_{m+1}b_m=-\frac{6}{(m+1)^3}.
\tag{8}
$$

Equation (8) proves that an endpoint zero has a nonzero adjacent state at primes $>3$. This is exactly what permits the deduction $\ell\mid N_h(X)$ from (1). It does **not** say that the corresponding transfer-matrix entry has a repeated zero. Projectively, one line returning to itself is one scalar condition on a nonsingular $2\times2$ transfer; the nonzero determinant does not turn that condition into a square.

Thus the Wronskian justifies the first power in (2), but supplies no second power.

# 4. Reverse transfer and resultants do not improve the height

The banked reflection identity for the continuants is

$$
N_h(-X-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(X).
\tag{9}
$$

Since $h=6q$ is even, reverse transfer gives exactly $-N_h(X)$. Therefore the forward and reverse carriers are not algebraically independent; their resultant is identically zero.

The first genuinely nonzero eliminant in the banked package is the adjacent resultant

$$
\boxed{
\left|\operatorname{Res}_X(N_h,N_{h+1})\right|
 =\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}\bigl(j!^3b_j\bigr)^6.
}
\tag{10}
$$

Its height is already supercritical. Taking logarithms,

$$
\log|\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+1})|
 =18\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}\log(j!)+6\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}\log b_j.
$$

The elementary estimate $1\le b_j\le(j+1)64^j$ shows that the second sum is $O(h^2)$, while Stirling/Barnes gives

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}\log(j!)
 =\frac12h^2\log h-\frac34h^2+O(h\log h).
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\log|\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+1})|
 =9h^2\log h+O(h^2),
}
$$

and at $h=6q$,

$$
\boxed{
\log|\operatorname{Res}(N_{6q},N_{6q+1})|
 =324q^2\log q+O(q^2).
}
\tag{11}
$$

So resultants make the carrier much larger. There is also a basic logical obstruction: a resultant eliminates a common root modulo the **same** prime. In the plus event, $p$ and $q$ support separate Lucas-root conditions. The affine relation between the numerical primes does not convert them into one prime dividing two coprime characteristic-zero polynomials.

# 5. Exact affine-prime specialization: no valuation lift

The following specialization satisfies the displayed common-endpoint geometry and a neighboring affine-prime relation:

$$
p=97,\qquad q=193=2p-1,\qquad u=128,
$$

$$
n=6q+u=1286,\qquad M=12q+u=2444.
$$

Its base-$p$ and base-$q$ decompositions are

$$
1286=6\cdot193+128=13\cdot97+25,
$$

$$
2444=12\cdot193+128=25\cdot97+19.
$$

The exact one-digit residues are

$$
b_{128}\equiv0\pmod{193},\quad
b_6\equiv190,\quad b_{12}\equiv18\pmod{193},
$$

and

$$
b_{25}\equiv0\pmod{97},\quad
b_{13}\equiv32,\quad b_{19}\equiv68\pmod{97}.
$$

Thus Lucas gives $97\cdot193\mid b_{1286}$ and $97\cdot193\mid b_{2444}$. The $p$-root $25$ merely moves from the low digit of $n$ to the high digit of $M$; the $q$-root $128$ remains the common low digit.

Now compute modulo the prime squares:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_{1286}&\equiv8245=85\cdot97 &&\pmod{97^2},\\
b_{2444}&\equiv8439=87\cdot97 &&\pmod{97^2},\\
b_{1286}&\equiv1158=6\cdot193 &&\pmod{193^2},\\
b_{2444}&\equiv965=5\cdot193 &&\pmod{193^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Every displayed cofactor is a unit modulo the corresponding prime. Therefore

$$
v_{97}(b_{1286})=v_{97}(b_{2444})
=v_{193}(b_{1286})=v_{193}(b_{2444})=1.
$$

The exact gcd is

$$
\boxed{
\gcd(b_{1286},b_{2444})
 =2{,}340{,}125
 =5^3\cdot97\cdot193.
}
\tag{12}
$$

The fixed factor $5^3$ has no aggregate value; crucially, neither event prime lifts.

This is a direct finite specialization, not a bounded search. The following exact checker evaluates only the two prescribed Apéry numbers:

```python
from math import comb, gcd

def B(n: int) -> int:
    return sum(comb(n, k)**2 * comb(n + k, k)**2
               for k in range(n + 1))

p, q, u = 97, 193, 128
n, M = 6*q + u, 12*q + u
x, y = B(n), B(M)

assert (n, M) == (1286, 2444)
assert gcd(x, y) == 5**3 * p * q
assert (x % p**2, y % p**2) == (85*p, 87*p)
assert (x % q**2, y % q**2) == (6*q, 5*q)
```

A smaller checksum is $(p,q,u)=(17,19,8)$, for which $(n,M)=(122,236)$ and

$$
\gcd(b_{122},b_{236})=17\cdot19,
$$

with all four endpoint valuations again equal to one.

# Scoped algebraic no-go

From the named package one can rigorously extract only:

1. one Lucas-root orbit per event prime;
2. the long continuant divisibility $pq\mid N_{6q}(6q+u)$;
3. no independent reverse carrier;
4. nonzero resultants of logarithmic height $\Theta(q^2\log q)$;
5. no forced common $p$- or $q$-adic lift, by (12).

Therefore there is **no aggregate compression from Apéry recurrence + Lucas/reflection + Wronskians + the current resultant package**. A successful algebraic repair would have to add genuinely new information, such as a uniform mod-$\ell^2$ cross-digit identity or a cross-prime characteristic-zero relation of logarithmic height $O(q)$ or smaller that is shared by many events. Neither is a consequence of the audited identities, and the explicit specialization above rules out a universal valuation-lift claim.
