# Audit result

The second-order recurrence structure gives a useful exact dictionary, but it does not by itself give a mean-in-G saving strong enough to replace the degree bound. The strongest unconditional statement from the recurrence alone is the exact factorization of each fixed-G count through a continuant return sequence and the same degree bound already present in the repository.

A genuine improvement would require an additional family estimate (for example a growing-G Chebotarev/trace theorem), not just the fact that the objects are generated by a second-order recurrence.

# 1. Exact return-sequence identity

Use the renormalized recurrence

$$
v_{n+1}=P(n)v_n-n^6v_{n-1},
$$

and define

$$
U_0(s)=1,\qquad U_1(s)=P(s),
$$

$$
U_{m+1}(s)=P(s+m)U_m(s)-(s+m)^6U_{m-1}(s).
$$

The repository dictionary gives

$$
N_h(x)=U_{h-1}(x+1).
$$

For fixed $u=x+s$, the long return condition is therefore

$$
N_G(u)=0 \iff U_{G-1}(u+1)=0.
$$

Thus the desired quantity is

$$
\sum_{G>K}\#\{x:N_s(x)=0,\ U_{G-1}(x+s+1)=0\}.
$$

This is not an arbitrary polynomial family: it is the orbit of the single transfer cocycle.

# 2. Transfer-matrix form

For

$$
M_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&(t+1)^6\\-1&P(t)\end{pmatrix},
$$

one has

$$
M_{u+G-2}\cdots M_u
$$
with bottom-right entry $U_{G-1}(u+1)$.

Hence a zero means that the projective transfer sends the initial point to the distinguished return point. Equivalently, the condition is a repeated hitting event of a non-autonomous projective dynamical system.

This does not make the sequence periodic: the matrices depend on $u+j$. The singular step at $-1$ (equivalently the original $n=0$ step modulo $p$) prevents replacing this by a fixed matrix power problem.

# 3. What follows for fixed s

For every fixed short gap $s$,

$$
N_s(x)=0
$$

has at most

$$
\deg N_s=3(s-1)
$$

roots.

For each such root $x$, putting $u=x+s$, the number of $G$ in a range for which

$$
N_G(u)=0
$$

is the zero count of the return sequence $U_m(u+1)$.

The unconditional pointwise statement remains

$$
\#\{G:K<G<p-s\}\le 3(G-1)
$$

when applied termwise, giving the already-known quadratic-scale obstruction after summing over $s,G$.

The recurrence relation alone does not improve this because it only controls each individual $U_m$.

# 4. Why no automatic mean-in-G cancellation appears

A tempting argument would be:

- $U_m$ has degree $3m$;
- a second-order recurrence should behave like a linear recurrence;
- therefore the total number of zeros over many $m$ should be small.

The last implication is false for non-autonomous recurrences.

The sequence

$$
U_m(t)
$$

is a polynomial sequence in the initial parameter $t$, not a scalar recurrence sequence in $m$ with constant coefficients. Standard Skolem-Mahler-Lech conclusions do not apply.

Even over finite fields, a non-autonomous second-order recurrence can have long zero runs or many returns because the coefficient sequence itself can encode the state evolution.

# 5. Reflection and singular indices

Reflection gives

$$
\pi(p-1-n)=\pi(n),
$$

and the continuant identity gives the root pairing

$$
U_m(-s-m)=(-1)^mU_m(s).
$$

This explains the forced reflected returns. It does not reduce the number of forward long returns beyond the existing reflection quotient.

The singular indices must be removed before any matrix inversion. In particular, one cannot form a full-period monodromy matrix and diagonalize it, because the step through the singular point is rank one, not an invertible element of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)$.

# 6. Obstruction model

A second-order recurrence framework by itself allows the following obstruction.

Choose a finite field and a non-autonomous matrix sequence

$$
A_j(t)\in\mathrm{Mat}_2(\mathbf F_p)
$$

with occasional rank-one steps, and arrange that a chosen vector is returned at a prescribed set of times $G$.

The entries of the resulting return determinants still satisfy second-order continuant recurrences and reflection can be imposed by taking a palindromic matrix word.

Therefore the abstract properties

1. second-order transfer;
2. reflection pairing;
3. singular full-cycle step;

are insufficient to force a small return count.

The missing ingredient must use special arithmetic of the Apéry coefficients $P(n)$.

# 7. Consequence for P32 far incidence

The far incidence quantity satisfies the repository reduction

$$
A_p^{nw}(K)
=\sum_{s\le\sqrt p}\sum_{G>K}
\#\{x:N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\}.
$$

The recurrence reformulation changes the second condition into a return sequence condition, but the currently proved estimates remain:

$$
\#\{x:N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\}
\le 3(s-1).
$$

No unconditional replacement by $O(1)$, $O(p^\epsilon)$, or an averaged $G$-saving follows from the second-order identity alone.

A successful theorem would need a new statement of the form

$$
\sum_{G>K}\#\{u:U_{G-1}(u)=0,\ u\in S_s\}
\ll p^{o(1)}|S_s|
$$

or an equivalent trace/large-sieve estimate for the non-autonomous Apéry cocycle.

# Final verdict

**Proved:** the $G$-direction is a genuine return sequence and admits the exact $U_m$ continuant formulation.

**Not proved:** any mean-in-$G$ saving sufficient for the P32 target.

**Obstruction:** second-order recurrence structure, reflection, and singular full-cycle behavior alone admit countermodels; the required improvement must exploit arithmetic special to the Apéry polynomial $P(n)$.
