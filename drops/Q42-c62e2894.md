# Certificate-parity bypass: the exact Bezout specialization, its height, and the cross-prime obstruction

## Terminal verdict

Let \(p<q\) be primes in \((N,2N]\), let \(m\le 2N\) be a top-window column hit by both primes, and put

\[
r=m-p,\qquad s=m-q,\qquad h=q-p=r-s,\qquad r=s+h.
\]

Thus \(0\le s<r<N<p<q\), with

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad q\mid b_s.
\]

The proposed bypass does **not** work.

The current continuant Bezout identity does have an exact specialization linking \(b_s\) and \(b_r\):

\[
\boxed{
(s!)^3N_r(0)b_s-(r!)^3N_s(0)b_r=(s!)^6N_h(s).
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

However, the first term is killed modulo \(q\), while the second term is killed modulo \(p\). Their difference is therefore not forced to vanish modulo either prime. In particular, the twin-atom hypotheses do **not** imply

\[
p\mid N_h(s),\qquad q\mid N_h(s),\qquad\text{or}\qquad pq\mid N_h(s).
\]

After removing the cut-edge factor \((s!)^6\), which is a unit modulo \(pq\), the remaining Bezout value is \(N_h(s)\). For \(2\le h\le s+1\), its logarithmic value height satisfies the matching bounds

\[
\boxed{
\log N_h(s)=3(h-1)\log(s+1)+O(h).
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Thus the saturated certificate has height \(\Theta(h\log r)\), not \(O(h)\). The unsaturated integer in (0.1) is much larger:

\[
\log\bigl((s!)^6N_h(s)\bigr)
=6s\log s-6s+3(h-1)\log s+O(h+\log s).
\tag{0.3}
\]

There is also an exact algebraic obstruction. Once the recurrence is written in a two-dimensional state, the two local hypotheses define one linear condition modulo \(p\) and a different linear condition modulo \(q\). The formal certificate ideal is

\[
\boxed{
(p,L_h)\cap(q,X)=(pq,\ pX,\ qL_h,\ XL_h).
}
\tag{0.4}
\]

Hence a recurrence-only cross-prime certificate is assembled from an explicit factor \(pq\), a \(p\)-weighted \(q\)-zero, a \(q\)-weighted \(p\)-zero, or the product of the two zero values. The continuant identities do not create a fifth small generator.

A trivial nonzero cross-prime certificate exists:

\[
W_{\mathrm{triv}}=q\,b_r+p\,b_s,
\qquad pq\mid W_{\mathrm{triv}}.
\tag{0.5}
\]

But the Apéry growth law gives

\[
\log W_{\mathrm{triv}}
=\log b_r+O(\log N)
=r\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log r+\log N),
\tag{0.6}
\]

so its height is \(\Theta(r)\) when \(r\asymp N\).

Finally, even a hypothetical \(O(h)\)-height certificate, which would yield only \(h\gg\log N\), would **not** prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). A \(c\log N\)-spaced subset of an interval of length \(N\) can still have \(\asymp N/\log N\) elements. Moreover, the sieve count of prime pairs at a fixed even gap \(h\) has scale \(N/\log^2N\), not \(N/h\). Excluding gaps below \(c\log N\) removes only a negligible fraction of all shell-prime pairs.

The exact conclusion is:

> **The current Apéry continuant/Bezout algebra does not bypass certificate parity. Its best saturated value height is \(\Theta(h\log r)\), and more fundamentally the twin-atom hypotheses do not make that value divisible by \(p\), \(q\), or \(pq\). A successful bypass requires new cross-characteristic information about the fixed Apéry initial state, not another identity inside the one-characteristic recurrence algebra.**

## 2. Exact \(h\)-step transfer

Put

\[
B_n=(n!)^3b_n.
\]

The Apéry recurrence becomes

\[
B_{n+1}=P(n)B_n-n^6B_{n-1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

With \(N_0=0\), \(N_1=1\), and

\[
N_{j+1}(x)=P(x+j)N_j(x)-(x+j)^6N_{j-1}(x),
\]

induction gives, for \(r=s+h\),

\[
\boxed{
B_r=N_h(s)B_{s+1}-(s+1)^6N_{h-1}(s+1)B_s.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

In terms of the original integer sequence,

\[
\boxed{
\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3\right)b_r
=N_h(s)b_{s+1}-(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)b_s,
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

with the empty product equal to \(1\) for \(h=1\).

Every integer in the products is below \(r<p<q\), so all displayed factors are units modulo both primes.

### Modulo \(q\)

Since \(q\mid b_s\), no-consecutive-zeros gives \(q\nmid b_{s+1}\). Reducing (2.3) modulo \(q\),

\[
\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3\right)b_r
\equiv N_h(s)b_{s+1}\pmod q.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Both the product and \(b_{s+1}\) are units, so

\[
\boxed{
q\mid N_h(s)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\mid b_r.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

The twin atom supplies \(p\mid b_r\), not \(q\mid b_r\). Therefore it imposes no \(q\)-divisibility on \(N_h(s)\).

### Modulo \(p\)

Since \(p\mid b_r\), (2.3) yields only

\[
N_h(s)b_{s+1}
\equiv(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)b_s\pmod p.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Neither side is forced to vanish. Thus \(p\mid b_r\) alone does not imply \(p\mid N_h(s)\).

The usual implication

\[
b_s\equiv b_{s+h}\equiv0\pmod\ell
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_h(s)\equiv0\pmod\ell
\tag{2.7}
\]

requires both endpoints to vanish modulo the **same** prime \(\ell\). A twin atom supplies one endpoint modulo \(q\) and the other modulo \(p\).

### Transfer-matrix form

With \(v_n=(B_n,B_{n-1})^T\),

\[
v_{n+1}=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(n)&-n^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}v_n.
\]

The determinant of the transfer from \(s\) to \(r\) is a product of sixth powers of integers below \(r\), hence a unit modulo \(pq\). The recurrence therefore transports projective lines invertibly in each characteristic; it does not collapse two different local hyperplanes into one scalar condition.

For primes avoiding the fixed integer \(N_h(s)\), a generic recurrence solution can be chosen by CRT so that \(B_s\equiv0\pmod q\) and \(B_r\equiv0\pmod p\) while \(N_h(s)\) remains a unit modulo both primes. This proves that no identity valid for every solution of the recurrence can infer \(pq\mid N_h(s)\) from the two cross-prime zeros. Any theorem specific to the actual Apéry sequence must use more than the transfer algebra.

## 3. Exact specialization of `prop:bezout`

The proposition defines

\[
S_d(x)=\prod_{j=2}^{d}(x+j)
\]

and proves, for \(2\le d<e\),

\[
\boxed{
N_{e-1}(x+1)N_d(x)-N_{d-1}(x+1)N_e(x)
=S_d(x)^6N_{e-d}(x+d).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

After saturation by \(S_d\),

\[
(N_d,N_e):S_d^\infty
=(N_d,N_{e-d}(x+d)):S_d^\infty.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is useful when one prime divides two gap-polynomial values. It is not a cross-characteristic identity.

Set

\[
x=-1,\qquad d=s+1,\qquad e=r+1=s+h+1.
\]

Then

\[
S_{s+1}(-1)=s!,\qquad e-d=h,\qquad x+d=s.
\]

Using

\[
N_{t+1}(-1)=t!^3b_t,
\tag{3.3}
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
(s!)^3N_r(0)b_s-(r!)^3N_s(0)b_r
=(s!)^6N_h(s).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is the precise Bezout identity linking the two Apéry values.

Write

\[
A=(s!)^3N_r(0)b_s,\qquad
B=(r!)^3N_s(0)b_r,\qquad
C=(s!)^6N_h(s).
\]

Then

\[
A-B=C,\qquad q\mid A,\qquad p\mid B.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
C\equiv A\pmod p,\qquad
C\equiv-B\pmod q,
\tag{3.6}
\]

and neither residue is forced to be zero.

If one prime \(\ell>r\) divided both \(b_s\) and \(b_r\), then both \(A\) and \(B\) would vanish modulo \(\ell\), and (3.4) would imply \(\ell\mid N_h(s)\). That is exactly the same-prime gap argument. Different primes destroy the conclusion.

### Forcing \(pq\) restores the large terms

The weighted combination

\[
W_1=pA-qB
\tag{3.7}
\]

is divisible by \(pq\). Since \(q=p+h\),

\[
W_1=p(A-B)-hB=pC-hB.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The small Bezout difference \(C\) is accompanied by the full-size term \(hB\). Removing \(hB\) removes the \(q\)-divisibility. This is the exact failure of the proposed cancellation.

## 4. Exact height of \(N_h(s)\)

The manuscript realizes \(N_h(x)\) as the determinant of an \((h-1)\times(h-1)\) symmetric tridiagonal matrix \(\mathsf A_h(x)\), with

\[
(\mathsf A_h)_{ii}=P(x+i),\qquad
(\mathsf A_h)_{i,i+1}=(x+i+1)^3.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For \(x\ge0\), this matrix is positive definite. The identity

\[
P(n)-n^3-(n+1)^3=4(2n+1)^3
\tag{4.2}
\]

gives explicit determinant bounds.

### Proposition 4.1

For \(h\ge2\) and \(x\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{
\bigl(4(2x+3)^3\bigr)^{h-1}
\le N_h(x)
\le\bigl(119(x+h)^3\bigr)^{h-1}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

In the row indexed by \(i\), put \(n=x+i\). The diagonal entry minus the sum of the absolute off-diagonal entries is at least

\[
P(n)-n^3-(n+1)^3
=4(2n+1)^3
\ge4(2x+3)^3.
\]

By Gershgorin, every eigenvalue is at least \(4(2x+3)^3\), proving the lower determinant bound.

For the upper bound, \(P(n)\le117(n+1)^3\) for \(n\ge0\), and each possible off-diagonal entry is at most \((x+h)^3\). Thus every eigenvalue is at most the maximum row sum, bounded by \(119(x+h)^3\). Multiplying the eigenvalue bounds proves (4.3).

### Corollary 4.2

If \(2\le h\le s+1\), then

\[
\boxed{
3(h-1)\log(s+1)+(h-1)\log32
\le\log N_h(s)
\le3(h-1)\log(s+1)+(h-1)\log952.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed,

\[
4(2s+3)^3\ge32(s+1)^3,\qquad
119(s+h)^3\le952(s+1)^3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log N_h(s)=3(h-1)\log(s+1)+O(h).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is a matching upper and lower bound. There is no hidden positive-\(s\) cancellation that can reduce \(h\log s\) to \(h\).

The leading coefficient supplies the same diagnosis. If \(\ell_h=\operatorname{lc}N_h\), then

\[
\ell_{h+1}=34\ell_h-\ell_{h-1},\qquad
\ell_h=U_{h-1}(17)\asymp(17+12\sqrt2)^h.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Even if the coefficient height were only exponential in \(h\), evaluating a degree \(3(h-1)\) polynomial at \(s\) adds the unavoidable term \(3(h-1)\log s\).

For the first possible prime gap \(h=2\),

\[
N_2(s)=P(s+1)
=(2s+3)(17s^2+51s+39),
\tag{4.7}
\]

so

\[
\log N_2(s)=3\log s+\log34+o(1).
\]

This fixed-\(h\) example alone disproves any \(O(h)\) value-height bound independent of \(s\).

## 5. Height of the general Bezout value

Define

\[
\mathcal B_{d,e}(x)
=N_{e-1}(x+1)N_d(x)-N_{d-1}(x+1)N_e(x).
\]

For \(x\ge0\), (3.1) gives the positive factorization

\[
\mathcal B_{d,e}(x)=S_d(x)^6N_{e-d}(x+d).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence exactly

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal B_{d,e}(x)
=6\sum_{j=2}^{d}\log(x+j)
+\log N_{e-d}(x+d).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Put \(e=d+h\). When \(d,h\ll x\), (4.5) yields

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal B_{d,d+h}(x)
=\bigl(6(d-1)+3(h-1)\bigr)\log x+O(d+h).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the smallest fixed cut \(d=2\),

\[
\mathcal B_{2,h+2}(x)=(x+2)^6N_h(x+2),
\tag{5.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal B_{2,h+2}(x)
=3(h+1)\log x+O(h).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

After saturation by \((x+2)^6\), the remaining value still has height \(3(h-1)\log x+O(h)\).

At the endpoint specialization (3.4), the unsaturated integer is \((s!)^6N_h(s)\). Stirling and (4.5) give

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\log\bigl((s!)^6N_h(s)\bigr)
&=6\log(s!)+\log N_h(s)\\
&=6s\log s-6s+3(h-1)\log s+O(h+\log s).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Because \(p,q>r>s\), \(s!\) is a unit modulo both primes. Saturation removes the factorial term but leaves \(N_h(s)\), whose exact height remains \(\Theta(h\log s)\).

## 6. The gap Casoratian: small real value, large arithmetic height

Let \(a_n\) be the companion Apéry solution normalized by

\[
a_n b_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.
\]

The exact \(h\)-step Casoratian is

\[
\boxed{
a_r b_s-a_s b_r
=\frac{6N_h(s)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{h}(s+j)^3},
\qquad r=s+h.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

For \(h=1\), this is \(6/(s+1)^3\).

The real value can be small because the numerator and denominator contain nearly the same powers of \(s\). But the arithmetic numerator is \(6N_h(s)\), and the denominator has \(h\) factors of size \(s\). Their natural logarithmic heights are of order \(h\log s\). A modular argument sees the numerator after the denominator is inverted, not merely the small real magnitude.

More decisively, the cross-prime hypotheses do not kill this determinant. Modulo \(p\),

\[
a_r b_s-a_s b_r\equiv a_r b_s\pmod p,
\]

while modulo \(q\),

\[
a_r b_s-a_s b_r\equiv-a_s b_r\pmod q.
\]

Neither is forced to vanish. The adjacent Wronskian proves no-consecutive-zeros because one prime kills both terms; two different primes kill one term each.

One may force a \(pq\)-multiple by clearing a denominator \(D_r\), coprime to \(pq\), and considering

\[
D_r\bigl(q\,a_s b_r-p\,a_r b_s\bigr).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since \(q=p+h\),

\[
q\,a_s b_r-p\,a_r b_s
=p(a_s b_r-a_r b_s)+h\,a_s b_r.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The first term is the controlled Casoratian; the second is a full-size Apéry product. Deleting the second term deletes the cross-prime divisibility.

## 7. Exact ideal-theoretic obstruction

Let

\[
X=b_s,\qquad Y=b_{s+1},
\]

and define

\[
L_h(X,Y)
=N_h(s)Y-(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)X.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equation (2.3) says

\[
L_h(b_s,b_{s+1})
=\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3\right)b_r.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Work over

\[
R=\mathbb Z\left[\frac1{1\cdot2\cdots r}\right][X,Y].
\]

The two local hypotheses are represented by

\[
I_p=(p,L_h),\qquad I_q=(q,X).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since \(p\) and \(q\) are coprime, \(I_p+I_q=R\), and therefore

\[
\boxed{
I_p\cap I_q=I_pI_q=(pq,\ pX,\ qL_h,\ XL_h).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus every formal polynomial certificate generated from the two local hypotheses is assembled from:

1. \(pq\), which is tautological;
2. \(pX=p\,b_s\);
3. \(qL_h=q(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3)b_r\);
4. \(XL_h=(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3)b_sb_r\).

There is no small fifth generator involving only \(N_h(s)\). A Bezout identity can rewrite combinations of \(L_h\) and \(X\), but cannot change the ideal (7.4).

This is certificate parity in algebraic form:

- one zero gives one hyperplane modulo \(p\);
- the other zero gives a different hyperplane modulo \(q\);
- because the characteristics are coprime, the joint formal ideal is the product ideal;
- a small determinant arises only when two hyperplanes are imposed in the same residue field.

The equality \(q-p=h\) is an equality of integers; it does not identify \(\mathbb F_p\) with \(\mathbb F_q\).

This is a no-go theorem for certificates derived from the recurrence identities and the two local zero hypotheses. It does not rule out a future theorem using genuinely global information special to the fixed Apéry initial state, such as a new Frobenius or motivic coupling across \(p\) and \(q\). No such coupling is contained in `prop:bezout`.

## 8. Trivial cross-prime certificates have linear height

The positive integer

\[
W_{\mathrm{triv}}=q\,b_r+p\,b_s
\tag{8.1}
\]

is divisible by \(pq\). The dominant characteristic root of the Apéry recurrence is

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
\]

and the standard Poincare asymptotic is

\[
b_n=C\alpha^n n^{-3/2}(1+O(1/n)).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\log W_{\mathrm{triv}}
=\log b_r+O(\log N)
=r\log\alpha+O(\log r+\log N).
\tag{8.3}
\]

For \(r\asymp N\), this is \(\Theta(N)\). The product certificate \(b_rb_s\) has the same defect.

The signed combination \(q\,b_r-p\,b_s\) is also divisible by \(pq\), but does not cancel at a fixed positive gap: \(b_{s+h}/b_s	o\alpha^h\), whereas \(p/q	o1\).

Thus existence is easy; small nonzero height is the entire issue.

## 9. Why an \(O(h)\)-height certificate would still not prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

Assume hypothetically that every twin atom gave a nonzero \(W\) with

\[
pq\mid W,\qquad \log|W|\le Ch.
\]

Then

\[
2\log N+O(1)\le\log(pq)\le Ch,
\]

so \(h\gg\log N\).

This is not enough.

### Pointwise packing

If one row contains hit primes

\[
N<p_1<\cdots<p_T\le2N
\]

with every pair gap at least \(c\log N\), then

\[
N\ge p_T-p_1\ge(T-1)c\log N.
\]

Hence

\[
T\le1+\frac{N}{c\log N}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

This is still the full prime-counting scale. The Fable collapse needs \(T\le\lambda_NN^{o(1)}\), much smaller.

### Fixed-gap prime-pair counts

For fixed even \(h\), the upper-bound sieve gives

\[
\#\{N<p\le2N:p,\ p+h\text{ prime}\}
\ll\mathfrak S(h)\frac{N}{\log^2N}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

There is no factor \(1/h\). Summing over the \(O(N)\) possible gaps gives the natural total shell-pair scale \(N^2/\log^2N\). Removing the \(O(\log N)\) gaps below \(c\log N\) changes this by only about \(N/\log N\), negligible compared with the total.

For actual twin atoms in a short interval, one still needs the CRT witness for a pair to land in that interval and in both Apéry zero sets. The expected \(1/N\) factor is exactly the missing fixed-origin cross-prime equidistribution. A lower bound on \(q-p\) provides none of it.

Therefore

\[
h\gg\log N
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
(\mathrm{HM})_3.
\]

## 10. Reflection does not repair the construction

The Apéry zero sets satisfy \(Z_p=p-1-Z_p\). A hit at \(r\) therefore supplies the same-prime reflected zero \(p-1-r\), with reflection gap

\[
d_p=|p-1-2r|.
\]

This does not bypass the obstruction:

- \(d_p\) depends on \(p\) and is generally of order \(N\), so its gap-polynomial value has very large height;
- the \(p\)- and \(q\)-reflection certificates still live in different characteristics;
- projective reflection forces the corresponding gap root on every reflected orbit, not only on the zero fiber, so the reflected anchored-star model already satisfies this necessary local structure.

Reflection supplies a second local coincidence, but at a prime-dependent long gap and without a common small integer across primes.

## 11. What a useful certificate must do

A pairwise integer of height \(O(h\log N)\) is insufficient. A certificate capable of proving the Fable anti-tail bound would need an aggregation property such as:

> For each column \(m\), construct a nonzero integer \(\mathscr W(m)\) satisfying
> 
> \[
> \prod_{\substack{N<p\le2N\\m\bmod p\in Z_p}}p
> \mid\mathscr W(m)
> \]
> 
> and
> 
> \[
> \log|\mathscr W(m)|\le N^{o(1)}\log N.
> \]

Then

\[
K_N(m)\log N\le\log|\mathscr W(m)|
\]

would give \(K_N(m)\le N^{o(1)}\).

The current recurrence certificates do not have this shape. They require a second incidence at each individual prime, and their value heights contain degree times \(\log m\).

The strictly narrower residual is therefore:

> **Fixed-initial-state cross-characteristic certificate problem.** Find a global Apéry object, not generated by the local continuant ideal alone, whose specialization at one column is simultaneously annihilated by every hit prime and whose nonzero integer height is \(N^{o(1)}\log N\).

No such object is currently present in the repository.

## 12. Direct answers

### Does a nonzero \(pq\)-divisible \(W(r,h)\) exist?

Yes, trivially:

\[
q\,b_r+p\,b_{r-h}.
\]

Its logarithmic height is \(\Theta(r)\), so it is useless.

The smaller Bezout difference (3.4) is not divisible by \(p\), \(q\), or \(pq\) under the twin-atom hypotheses.

### What is the exact height of the Bezout certificate?

For the endpoint specialization linking \(b_s\) and \(b_r\):

- unsaturated:
  
  \[
  (s!)^6N_h(s),
  \]
  
  with
  
  \[
  \log=6s\log s-6s+3(h-1)\log s+O(h+\log s);
  \]
- saturated:
  
  \[
  N_h(s),
  \]
  
  with
  
  \[
  \boxed{\log N_h(s)=3(h-1)\log(s+1)+O(h).}
  \]

The best natural scale is therefore \(\Theta(h\log r)\), not \(O(h)\).

### Does the \(h\)-step Casoratian help?

Its exact value is

\[
a_r b_s-a_s b_r
=\frac{6N_h(s)}{\prod_{j=1}^h(s+j)^3}.
\]

It has small real magnitude but arithmetic height \(\Theta(h\log r)\). More importantly, different primes kill different determinant terms, so it has no forced cross-prime divisibility.

### Would \(O(h)\) height prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)?

No. It would imply only \(h\gg\log N\), leaving \(\asymp N/\log N\) possible hit primes in one row and excluding only a negligible fraction of shell-prime pairs. The asserted \(O(N/h)\) count for prime pairs at gap \(h\) is not valid.

## Final conclusion

The proposed route reaches two exact terminal obstructions:

1. **Divisibility obstruction:** the specialized Bezout remainder is not a multiple of either prime because the two endpoint terms vanish in different characteristics.
2. **Height obstruction:** after every legitimate saturation, \(N_h(s)\) has logarithmic height \(3(h-1)\log(s+1)+O(h)\), not \(O(h)\).

The certificate-parity barrier therefore survives intact.
