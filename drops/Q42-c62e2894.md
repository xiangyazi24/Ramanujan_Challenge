# Certificate-parity bypass: exact Bezout specialization, height, and the cross-prime obstruction

## Terminal verdict

Let \(p<q\) be primes in \((N,2N]\), let \(m\le 2N\) be a top-window column hit by both primes, and write

\[
 r=m-p,\qquad s=m-q,\qquad h=q-p=r-s,\qquad r=s+h.
\]

Thus \(0\le s<r<N<p<q\), \(p\mid b_r\), and \(q\mid b_s\).

The proposed bypass does **not** work. There are three separate reasons.

1. The continuant Bezout identity does have a very useful specialization linking \(b_s\) and \(b_r\):
   
   \[
   oxed{
   (s!)^3N_r(0)b_s-(r!)^3N_s(0)b_r=(s!)^6N_h(s).
   }
   	ag{0.1}
   \]
   
   But the first term is killed modulo \(q\), whereas the second is killed modulo \(p\). Their difference is therefore not forced to vanish modulo either prime. In particular, the twin-atom hypotheses do **not** imply
   
   \[
   p\mid N_h(s),\qquad q\mid N_h(s),\qquad	ext{or}\qquad pq\mid N_h(s).
   \]
   
   The same-prime gap argument works only because a single prime kills both endpoint terms.

2. After removing the cut-edge factor \((s!)^6\), which is a unit modulo \(pq\), the remaining Bezout value is \(N_h(s)\). Its exact logarithmic scale is
   
   \[
   oxed{
   \log N_h(s)=3(h-1)\log(s+1)+O(h)
   }
   	ag{0.2}
   \]
   
   uniformly when \(2\le h\le s+1\). Thus the saturated certificate has height \(\Theta(h\log r)\), not \(O(h)\). The unsaturated integer in (0.1) is much larger:
   
   \[
   \logigl((s!)^6N_h(s)igr)
   =6s\log s-6s+3(h-1)\log s+O(h+\log s).
   	ag{0.3}
   \]

3. There is an exact ideal-theoretic obstruction. After the \(h\)-step recurrence is written in a two-dimensional state, the local hypotheses are one linear condition modulo \(p\) and a different linear condition modulo \(q\). The formal certificate ideal is
   
   \[
   (p,L_h)\cap(q,X)
   =(pq,\ pX,\ qL_h,\ XL_h).
   	ag{0.4}
   \]
   
   Consequently a recurrence-only cross-prime certificate is built from one of four things: an explicit factor \(pq\), a \(p\)-weighted \(q\)-zero, a \(q\)-weighted \(p\)-zero, or the product of the two large zero values. The continuant Bezout identities do not create a fifth, small generator.

A trivial nonzero cross-prime certificate certainly exists:

\[
W_{\mathrm{triv}}=q\,b_r+p\,b_s,\qquad pq\mid W_{\mathrm{triv}}.
	ag{0.5}
\]

However,

\[
\log W_{\mathrm{triv}}=\log b_r+O(\log N)=\Theta(r),
	ag{0.6}
\]

because \(b_n\) grows like \((17+12\sqrt2)^n n^{-3/2}\). It has exactly the forbidden linear height.

There is one further correction to the proposed implication. Even a hypothetical certificate of height \(O(h)\), giving \(h\gg\log N\), would **not** prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). A subset of \((N,2N]\) with mutual spacing \(c\log N\) can still contain \(\asymp N/\log N\) primes. Moreover, the number of prime pairs at a fixed even gap \(h\) is not \(O(N/h)\); the sieve scale is essentially \(N/\log^2N\), with no \(1/h\) saving. Excluding gaps below \(c\log N\) removes only a negligible fraction of all shell-prime pairs.

The exact conclusion is therefore:

> **The current Apéry continuant/Bezout algebra does not bypass certificate parity. Its best saturated value height is \(\Theta(h\log r)\), and more fundamentally the twin-atom hypotheses do not make that value divisible by \(p\), \(q\), or \(pq\). A successful bypass would require genuinely new cross-characteristic information about the fixed Apéry initial state, not another identity inside the one-characteristic recurrence algebra.**

## 2. The exact \(h\)-step transfer identity

It is useful first to write the recurrence in a denominator-free form. Put

\[
B_n=(n!)^3b_n.
\]

Then

\[
B_{n+1}=P(n)B_n-n^6B_{n-1}.
	ag{2.1}
\]

For \(r=s+h\), induction on \(h\) gives

\[
oxed{
B_r=N_h(s)B_{s+1}-(s+1)^6N_{h-1}(s+1)B_s.
}
	ag{2.2}
\]

Equivalently, in the original Apéry numbers,

\[
oxed{
\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3ight)b_r
=N_h(s)b_{s+1}-(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)b_s,
}
	ag{2.3}
\]

where the product is \(1\) for \(h=1\).

All integers \(1,\ldots,r\) are smaller than both \(p\) and \(q\). Hence every factorial and every product in (2.2)--(2.3) is a unit modulo both primes.

### Reduction modulo \(q\)

Since \(q\mid b_s\), the no-consecutive-zeros theorem gives

\[
q
mid b_{s+1}.
\]

Reducing (2.3) modulo \(q\),

\[
\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3ight)b_r
\equiv N_h(s)b_{s+1}\pmod q.
	ag{2.4}
\]

Both the product and \(b_{s+1}\) are units. Therefore

\[
oxed{
q\mid N_h(s)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\mid b_r.
}
	ag{2.5}
\]

But the twin atom gives \(p\mid b_r\), not \(q\mid b_r\). Thus it gives no \(q\)-divisibility of \(N_h(s)\).

### Reduction modulo \(p\)

Since \(p\mid b_r\), (2.3) gives only

\[
N_h(s)b_{s+1}\equiv
(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)b_s\pmod p.
	ag{2.6}
\]

There is no reason for either side to vanish. In particular, \(p\mid b_r\) alone does not imply \(p\mid N_h(s)\).

This is the certificate-parity obstruction in its most elementary form. The implication

\[
b_s\equiv b_{s+h}\equiv0\pmod\ell
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_h(s)\equiv0\pmod\ell
	ag{2.7}
\]

requires **both endpoints to vanish modulo the same prime \(\ell\)**. The twin atom supplies one endpoint modulo \(q\) and the other endpoint modulo \(p\).

### Transfer-matrix interpretation

With the state vector \(v_n=(B_n,B_{n-1})^T\), one step is

\[
v_{n+1}=
egin{pmatrix}
P(n)&-n^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}v_n.
\]

The determinant of the transfer from \(s\) to \(r\) is a product of sixth powers of integers below \(r\), and is therefore a unit modulo \(pq\). Thus the recurrence transports one projective line to another invertibly in each characteristic. It does not collapse the two different local hyperplanes into a common scalar condition.

Indeed, for a generic initial state \((B_s,B_{s+1})\), CRT lets one impose

\[
B_s\equiv0\pmod q,\qquad B_r\equiv0\pmod p
\]

independently while keeping \(N_h(s)\) a unit modulo both primes. Hence no identity valid for every solution of the recurrence can deduce \(pq\mid N_h(s)\) from the two cross-prime zero conditions. Any theorem for the actual Apéry solution must use additional information about its fixed initial state, not just the transfer algebra.

## 3. What `prop:bezout` says, and its exact specialization

The current proposition writes

\[
S_d(x)=\prod_{j=2}^{d}(x+j)
\]

and proves, for \(2\le d<e\),

\[
oxed{
N_{e-1}(x+1)N_d(x)-N_{d-1}(x+1)N_e(x)
=S_d(x)^6N_{e-d}(x+d).
}
	ag{3.1}
\]

After saturation by the cut-edge divisor \(S_d\), this says

\[
(N_d,N_e):S_d^\infty
=(N_d,N_{e-d}(x+d)):S_d^\infty.
	ag{3.2}
\]

This is powerful when one prime divides two gap-polynomial values. It is not a cross-characteristic identity.

### The relevant specialization

Set

\[
x=-1,\qquad d=s+1,\qquad e=r+1=s+h+1.
\]

Then

\[
S_{s+1}(-1)=s!,\qquad e-d=h,\qquad x+d=s.
\]

The endpoint formula

\[
N_{t+1}(-1)=t!^3b_t
	ag{3.3}
\]

turns (3.1) into

\[
oxed{
(s!)^3N_r(0)b_s-(r!)^3N_s(0)b_r
=(s!)^6N_h(s).
}
	ag{3.4}
\]

This is the precise integer identity anticipated in the question.

Put

\[
A=(s!)^3N_r(0)b_s,\qquad
B=(r!)^3N_s(0)b_r,\qquad
C=(s!)^6N_h(s).
\]

Then

\[
A-B=C,\qquad q\mid A,\qquad p\mid B.
	ag{3.5}
\]

But from these facts one obtains only

\[
C\equiv A\pmod p,\qquad
C\equiv-B\pmod q.
	ag{3.6}
\]

Neither residue is forced to vanish. Thus (3.4) is not a \(pq\)-certificate.

If a **single** prime \(\ell>r\) divided both \(b_s\) and \(b_r\), then both \(A\) and \(B\) would vanish modulo \(\ell\), and (3.4) would give

\[
\ell\mid N_h(s).
\]

That is exactly the ordinary gap-polynomial argument. Replacing that one prime by two different primes destroys the conclusion.

### Forcing \(pq\) reintroduces the large terms

One can force divisibility by weighting the two terms with the opposite primes:

\[
W_1=pA-qB.
	ag{3.7}
\]

Because \(q\mid A\) and \(p\mid B\), one has \(pq\mid W_1\). Since \(q=p+h\),

\[
W_1=p(A-B)-hB=pC-hB.
	ag{3.8}
\]

The small Bezout difference \(C\) is accompanied by the large term \(hB\). Removing \(hB\) would remove the \(q\)-divisibility. This is the exact algebraic reason the hoped cancellation cannot be retained in a cross-prime certificate.

## 4. Exact height of the gap polynomial

The manuscript realizes \(N_h(x)\) as the determinant of an \((h-1)	imes(h-1)\) real symmetric tridiagonal matrix \(\mathsf A_h(x)\), with

\[
(\mathsf A_h)_{ii}=P(x+i),\qquad
(\mathsf A_h)_{i,i+1}=(x+i+1)^3.
	ag{4.1}
\]

For \(x\ge0\), this matrix is positive definite. The identity

\[
P(n)-n^3-(n+1)^3=4(2n+1)^3
	ag{4.2}
\]

gives explicit two-sided determinant bounds.

### Proposition 4.1 — uniform value-height bounds

For \(h\ge2\) and \(x\ge0\),

\[
oxed{
igl(4(2x+3)^3igr)^{h-1}
\le N_h(x)
\leigl(119(x+h)^3igr)^{h-1}.
}
	ag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

For the row indexed by \(i\), put \(n=x+i\). The diagonal entry minus the sum of the absolute off-diagonal entries is at least

\[
P(n)-n^3-(n+1)^3=4(2n+1)^3\ge4(2x+3)^3.
\]

Gershgorin's theorem for the symmetric matrix therefore puts every eigenvalue above \(4(2x+3)^3\), proving the lower bound for the determinant.

For the upper bound, \(P(n)\le117(n+1)^3\) for \(n\ge0\), and the two possible off-diagonal entries are each at most \((x+h)^3\). Every eigenvalue is therefore at most the maximum row sum, which is at most \(119(x+h)^3\). Multiplying the eigenvalue bounds proves (4.3).

### Corollary 4.2 — exact scale for \(x=s\)

If \(2\le h\le s+1\), then

\[
oxed{
3(h-1)\log(s+1)+(h-1)\log32
\le\log N_h(s)
\le3(h-1)\log(s+1)+(h-1)\log952.
}
	ag{4.4}
\]

Indeed,

\[
4(2s+3)^3\ge32(s+1)^3,\qquad
119(s+h)^3\le952(s+1)^3.
\]

Consequently

\[
oxed{
\log N_h(s)=3(h-1)\log(s+1)+O(h).
}
	ag{4.5}
\]

This is a matching upper and lower bound. There is no hidden cancellation capable of changing \(h\log s\) to \(h\).

The leading coefficient gives the same conclusion from another angle. If \(\ell_h=\operatorname{lc}N_h\), then

\[
\ell_{h+1}=34\ell_h-\ell_{h-1},\qquad
\ell_h=U_{h-1}(17)\asymp(17+12\sqrt2)^h.
	ag{4.6}
\]

Even if the coefficient height were only exponential in \(h\), evaluating a degree \(3(h-1)\) polynomial at \(s\) contributes the unavoidable term \(3(h-1)\log s\). Positivity and (4.3) rule out cancellation among the coefficients at positive \(s\).

### The fixed-gap test already disproves \(O(h)\)

For \(h=2\),

\[
N_2(s)=P(s+1)
=(2s+3)(17s^2+51s+39).
	ag{4.7}
\]

Hence

\[
\log N_2(s)=3\log s+\log34+o(1).
\]

Thus even the first possible prime gap, \(h=2\), produces a saturated value whose height grows like \(3\log r\). An \(O(h)\) bound independent of \(r\) is impossible.

## 5. Exact height of the Bezout certificate

For the general Bezout identity, define

\[
\mathcal B_{d,e}(x)
=N_{e-1}(x+1)N_d(x)-N_{d-1}(x+1)N_e(x).
\]

Equation (3.1) gives the exact positive factorization, for \(x\ge0\),

\[
\mathcal B_{d,e}(x)
=S_d(x)^6N_{e-d}(x+d).
	ag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
oxed{
\log\mathcal B_{d,e}(x)
=6\sum_{j=2}^{d}\log(x+j)
+\log N_{e-d}(x+d).
}
	ag{5.2}
\]

Put \(e=d+h\). When \(d,h\ll x\), (4.5) gives

\[
oxed{
\log\mathcal B_{d,d+h}(x)
=igl(6(d-1)+3(h-1)igr)\log x+O(d+h).
}
	ag{5.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\log\mathcal B_{d,d+h}(x)
=3(h+2d-3)\log x+O(d+h).
\]

The smallest fixed cut \(d=2\) gives

\[
\mathcal B_{2,h+2}(x)=(x+2)^6N_h(x+2)
	ag{5.4}
\]

and hence

\[
oxed{
\log\mathcal B_{2,h+2}(x)
=3(h+1)\log x+O(h).
}
	ag{5.5}
\]

After saturation by \((x+2)^6\), the remaining value is \(N_h(x+2)\), still of height \(3(h-1)\log x+O(h)\).

### Height at the endpoint specialization

For the actual \(b_s,b_r\) identity (3.4), the unsaturated integer is

\[
C=(s!)^6N_h(s).
\]

By Stirling and (4.5),

\[
oxed{
egin{aligned}
\log C
&=6\log(s!)+\log N_h(s)\\
&=6s\log s-6s+3(h-1)\log s+O(h+\log s).
\end{aligned}

	ag{5.6}
\]

This is \(\Theta(s\log s)\).

Since \(p,q>r>s\), the integer \(s!\) is a unit modulo \(pq\). One may therefore saturate by \((s!)^6\) in a modular argument. The best resulting integer is \(N_h(s)\), and its height is exactly the \(\Theta(h\log s)\) scale in (4.5). Saturation removes the factorial height but cannot remove the evaluation height.

## 6. The gap Casoratian has small real size but large arithmetic height

Let \(a_n\) be the companion Apéry solution used in `proof.tex`, normalized so that

\[
a_n b_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=rac6{n^3}.
\]

The exact \(h\)-step Casoratian is

\[
oxed{
a_r b_s-a_s b_r
=rac{6N_h(s)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{h}(s+j)^3},\qquad r=s+h.
}
	ag{6.1}
\]

At \(h=1\), this is precisely \(6/(s+1)^3\).

There are two different notions of size here.

- Archimedean size: for fixed \(h\) and large \(s\), the powers of \(s\) nearly cancel, and the value is roughly a constant depending exponentially on \(h\), divided by \(s^3\).
- Arithmetic height: the natural numerator is \(6N_h(s)\), and the denominator contains \(h\) factors of size \(s\). Both have logarithmic scale \(h\log s\).

A modular divisibility argument sees the numerator after the denominator is inverted. Thus the apparent small real value does not supply an integer of height \(O(h)\).

More importantly, (6.1) is not killed by the cross-prime hypotheses. Modulo \(p\),

\[
a_r b_s-a_s b_r\equiv a_r b_s\pmod p,
\]

and modulo \(q\),

\[
a_r b_s-a_s b_r\equiv-a_s b_r\pmod q.
\]

Neither is forced to vanish. The adjacent Wronskian proves no-consecutive-zeros because one prime kills **both** terms. Two different primes kill one term each and give no small common numerator.

One can force a \(pq\)-multiple by taking a common denominator \(D_r\), coprime to \(pq\), and forming

\[
D_rigl(q\,a_s b_r-p\,a_r b_sigr).
	ag{6.2}
\]

But since \(q=p+h\),

\[
q\,a_s b_r-p\,a_r b_s
=p(a_s b_r-a_r b_s)+h\,a_s b_r.
	ag{6.3}
\]

The first term is the small Casoratian; the second is a full-size Apéry product. Deleting the second term deletes the cross-prime divisibility. This is the companion-solution version of (3.8).

## 7. Exact ideal-theoretic obstruction

The obstruction can be stated without asymptotics.

Let

\[
X=b_s,\qquad Y=b_{s+1},
\]

and define the recurrence linear form

\[
L_h(X,Y)
=N_h(s)Y-(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)X.
	ag{7.1}
\]

By (2.3),

\[
L_h(b_s,b_{s+1})
=\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3ight)b_r.
	ag{7.2}
\]

Work in the coefficient ring

\[
R=\mathbb Z\left[rac1{1\cdot2\cdots r}ight][X,Y].
\]

The localization only inverts integers below \(p,q\), so reduction modulo either prime remains valid. The two local hypotheses are encoded by

\[
I_p=(p,L_h),\qquad I_q=(q,X).
	ag{7.3}
\]

Since \(p\) and \(q\) are coprime, \(I_p+I_q=R\). Hence

\[
oxed{
I_p\cap I_q=I_pI_q=(pq,\ pX,\ qL_h,\ XL_h).
}
	ag{7.4}
\]

This classifies every certificate obtained by formal polynomial manipulation of the two local hypotheses.

After substitution, the four generators are:

\[
egin{array}{c|c}
	ext{generator}&	ext{meaning}\\
\hline
pq&	ext{tautological prime product}\\
pX&p\,b_s\\
qL_h&q\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3ight)b_r\\
XL_h&\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3ight)b_sb_r.
\end{array}
	ag{7.5}
\]

There is no small fifth generator involving only \(N_h(s)\). A Bezout identity may rewrite combinations of \(L_h\) and \(X\), but it cannot change the ideal (7.4).

This is the precise algebraic form of certificate parity:

- one local zero gives one hyperplane modulo \(p\);
- one different local zero gives a second hyperplane modulo \(q\);
- because the characteristics are coprime, their joint formal ideal is the product ideal;
- a small determinant appears only when two hyperplanes are imposed in the **same** residue field.

The relation \(q-p=h\) does not alter this decomposition. It is an equality of integers, not a map identifying \(\mathbb F_p\) with \(\mathbb F_q\).

### Scope of the obstruction

Equation (7.4) is a no-go theorem for certificates derived from the recurrence identities and the two local divisibility hypotheses. It does not logically rule out every possible theorem special to the fixed Apéry initial state. A future cross-prime theorem could use, for example, a genuinely global Frobenius, automorphic, or motivic relation coupling the reductions at \(p\) and \(q\). But that information is absent from `prop:bezout` and from the continuant algebra.

## 8. Trivial cross-prime certificates and their height

Because \(p\mid b_r\) and \(q\mid b_s\),

\[
oxed{
W_{\mathrm{triv}}=q\,b_r+p\,b_s
}
	ag{8.1}
\]

is a positive nonzero integer divisible by \(pq\).

The Apéry recurrence has dominant characteristic root

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
\]

and the standard Poincare asymptotic is

\[
b_n=C\alpha^n n^{-3/2}(1+O(1/n)).
	ag{8.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\log W_{\mathrm{triv}}
=\log b_r+O(\log N)
=r\log\alpha+O(\log r+\log N).
	ag{8.3}
\]

For \(r\asymp N\), this is \(\Theta(N)\). The equally natural product certificate \(b_rb_s\) has the same defect.

The signed combination

\[

These examples show that existence of a cross-prime integer is trivial. The entire problem is small **nonzero height**, and the recurrence does not supply it.

## 9. Even an \(O(h)\)-height certificate would not prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

Suppose hypothetically that every twin atom produced a nonzero integer \(W\) with

\[
pq\mid W,\qquad \log|W|\le Ch.
\]

Then

\[
2\log N+O(1)\le\log(pq)\le Ch,
\]

so \(h\gg\log N\).

This is only a natural prime-spacing condition. It does not provide the extra factor \(1/\log N\) required by the third moment.

### Pointwise packing

If a row contains \(T\) hit primes

\[
N<p_1<\cdots<p_T\le2N
\]

and every pair gap is at least \(c\log N\), then

\[
N\ge p_T-p_1\ge(T-1)c\log N.
\]

Hence

\[
T\le1+rac{N}{c\log N}.
	ag{9.1}
\]

This is still the full prime-counting scale. The Fable collapse needs \(T\le\lambda_NN^{o(1)}\), which is vastly smaller.

### Counting prime pairs at a fixed gap

For fixed even \(h\), the standard upper-bound sieve has scale

\[
\#\{N<p\le2N:p,\ p+h	ext{ prime}\}
\ll \mathfrak S(h)rac{N}{\log^2N}.
	ag{9.2}
\]

There is no factor \(1/h\). Summing (9.2) over the \(O(N)\) possible gaps gives the natural total shell-pair scale \(N^2/\log^2N\). Removing the \(O(\log N)\) gaps below \(c\log N\) changes this by only about \(N/\log N\), negligible relative to the total.

For the actual twin-atom count in a short interval, one still needs the CRT witness for a prime pair to land in that interval and in the two Apéry zero sets. The expected factor \(1/N\) is precisely the missing fixed-origin cross-prime equidistribution. A lower bound on \(q-p\) supplies none of it.

Thus the proposed line

\[
	ext{“}h\gg\log N	ext{”}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
	ext{\((\mathrm{HM})_3\)}
\]

is false even before the certificate construction is considered.

## 10. Reflection does not repair the bypass

The actual Apéry zero sets satisfy

\[
Z_p=p-1-Z_p.
\]

Thus a hit at \(r\) supplies a second same-prime zero \(p-1-r\), and hence a gap-polynomial certificate with reflection gap

\[
d_p=|p-1-2r|.
\]

This does not solve the cross-prime problem.

- The gap \(d_p\) depends on \(p\) and is usually of order \(N\), so the corresponding value has very large height.
- For two primes \(p,q\), the two reflection certificates still live modulo different primes.
- Projective reflection forces the associated \(N_{d_p}\)-root for every reflected orbit, not only for the zero fiber. This is why the reflected anchored-star model can satisfy all such necessary local certificates while retaining a high-load atom.

The reflection spray creates the second local coincidence needed to invoke \(N_d\), but it creates a **prime-dependent long gap** and no common small integer across different characteristics.

## 11. What a useful certificate would have to look like

A pairwise integer of height \(O(h\log N)\) is insufficient. A certificate capable of proving the Fable anti-tail bound would need a different aggregation property.

One genuinely sufficient shape would be:

> For each column \(m\), construct a nonzero integer \(\mathscr W(m)\) such that
> 
> \[
> \prod_{\substack{N<p\le2N\\mmod p\in Z_p}}p\mid\mathscr W(m)
> \]
> 
> and
> 
> \[
> \log|\mathscr W(m)|\le N^{o(1)}\log N.
> \]

Then

\[
K_N(m)\log N\le\log|\mathscr W(m)|
\]

would give \(K_N(m)\le N^{o(1)}\).

The current recurrence certificates do not have this form. They require a second incidence at each individual prime, and their value heights accumulate a degree times \(\log m\). A successful construction must couple the prime reductions before taking a determinant, or exploit a global object whose reduction at every hit prime vanishes simultaneously.

This identifies the strictly narrower residual:

> **Fixed-initial-state cross-characteristic certificate problem.** Find a global Apéry object, not generated by the local continuant ideal alone, whose specialization at one column is simultaneously annihilated by every hit prime and whose nonzero integer height is \(N^{o(1)}\log N\).

No such object is currently present in the repository.

## 12. Final answers to the explicit questions

### Does the proposed \(W(r,h)\) exist?

A nonzero \(pq\)-divisible integer exists trivially, for example

\[
q\,b_r+p\,b_{r-h}.
\]

Its logarithmic height is \(\Theta(r)\), so it is useless.

The continuant Bezout identity produces the exact smaller difference (3.4), but that difference is not divisible by \(p\), \(q\), or \(pq\) under the twin-atom hypotheses. Hence the proposed useful \(W\) does not arise from `prop:bezout`.

### What is the exact height of the Bezout certificate?

For the endpoint specialization linking \(b_s\) and \(b_r\):

- unsaturated integer:
  
  \[
  (s!)^6N_h(s),\qquad
  \log=6s\log s-6s+3(h-1)\log s+O(h+\log s);
  \]
- saturated integer:
  
  \[
  N_h(s),\qquad
  oxed{\log N_h(s)=3(h-1)\log(s+1)+O(h)}.
  \]

Thus the best natural scale is \(O(h\log r)\), with a matching lower bound. It is not \(O(h)\).

### Does the h-step Casoratian help?

Its exact value is

\[
a_r b_s-a_s b_r
=rac{6N_h(s)}{\prod_{j=1}^h(s+j)^3}.
\]

It has small real magnitude after numerator/denominator cancellation, but arithmetic height \(\Theta(h\log r)\). More decisively, different primes kill different terms, so it has no forced cross-prime divisibility.

### Would an \(O(h)\)-height certificate prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)?

No. It would imply only \(h\gg\log N\), which leaves \(\asymp N/\log N\) possible hit primes in one row and excludes only a negligible fraction of shell-prime pairs. The asserted \(O(N/h)\) count for prime pairs at gap \(h\) is not valid.

## Final conclusion

The proposed route reaches two exact terminal obstructions:

1. **Divisibility obstruction:** `prop:bezout` compares two endpoints in one recurrence algebra, but the twin atom places those endpoints in different characteristics. The specialized Bezout remainder is not a multiple of either prime.
2. **Height obstruction:** after every legitimate saturation, the remainder \(N_h(s)\) has logarithmic height \(3(h-1)\log s+O(h)\), not \(O(h)\).

The certificate-parity barrier therefore survives intact.
