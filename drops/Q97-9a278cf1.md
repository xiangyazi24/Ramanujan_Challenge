# Q97 — Polynomial cocycle expansion: verification of the shifted Mellin-sheaf argument

## Short answer

The proposed argument identifies the correct obstruction, but the final step is not valid as stated. The pair of Mellin traces at consecutive characters is **not automatically independent** from Katz equidistribution. The map

\[
n\mapsto (n,n-1)
\]

is a shifted diagonal inside \(\mathbb A^2\), and pulling back \(\mathcal F\boxtimes\mathcal F\) to this curve does not preserve the product monodromy in general. One must prove a genuine monodromy statement for the pullback sheaf. This is the missing theorem.

The idea is still the right reduction: the polynomial cocycle problem becomes a rank-4 (or tensor-square) sheaf monodromy problem. But the Goursat argument alone is insufficient.

---

# 1. What is correct

Let

\[
\Pi_n=M(n-1)\cdots M(0).
\]

The recurrence

\[
\Pi_{n+1}=M(n)\Pi_n
\]

means the cocycle is generated by a polynomial coefficient system. Writing

\[
\Pi_n=\begin{pmatrix}a_n&b_n\\c_n&d_n\end{pmatrix},
\]

one gets a rank-4 linear system

\[
v_{n+1}=A(n)v_n,
\]

where \(A(n)\) is polynomial. This is exactly the kind of object that can be attacked by Katz's formalism.

For the Apéry cocycle, the identification

\[
\Pi_n=\begin{pmatrix}b_n&a_n\\b_{n-1}&a_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}
\]

is especially useful because the entries are Mellin-type traces.

The reduction from polynomial cocycle expansion to joint distribution of

\[
(T_p(n),T_p(n-1),U_p(n),U_p(n-1))
\]

is therefore legitimate.

---

# 2. The fatal gap: shifted diagonal is not generic product

The claim

> Katz equidistribution for \(\mathcal F\) implies independence of Frobenius at \(n\) and \(n-1\)

is false in this generality.

The relevant sheaf is

\[
\mathcal G=(x\mapsto(x,x-1))^*(\mathcal F\boxtimes\mathcal F).
\]

Its geometric monodromy is not automatically

\[
G\times G.
\]

The reason is geometric: the two factors are no longer independent variables. They are tied by the translation relation

\[
y=x-1.
\]

A sheaf and its translate can have common subquotients. For example, if

\[
\mathcal F\simeq[+1]^*\mathcal F
\]

or if \(\mathcal F\) has translation symmetries, the pullback monodromy can be diagonal or a proper subgroup.

The correct statement is:

\[
\operatorname{Mon}(\mathcal G)
\subseteq G\times G
\]

and one must prove the inclusion is equality (or large enough) by excluding geometric relations between \(\mathcal F\) and its translate.

---

# 3. What theorem is actually needed

For the Mellin sheaf \(\mathcal F\), one needs a translation independence theorem:

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\pi_1^{geom}(\mathbb A^1)}
(\mathcal F,\tau_1^*\mathcal F)=0
\]

up to the obvious dual/twist exceptions.

Equivalently, prove that \(\mathcal F\) and \(\tau_1^*\mathcal F\) have no common irreducible constituent.

Then Goursat's lemma gives

\[
\operatorname{Mon}(\mathcal G)=G\times G.
\]

After this, Katz equidistribution applies and the pair of traces is genuinely independent.

So the missing lemma is not a character sum estimate. It is a geometric irreducibility/translation lemma.

---

# 4. Apéry-specific situation

The Apéry Mellin sheaf is not an arbitrary sheaf. It comes from the K3/hypergeometric family. This helps:

* the geometric monodromy is expected to be large;
* the singularities are explicit;
* local monodromy at the singular points can distinguish \(\mathcal F\) from \(\tau_1^*\mathcal F\).

A possible proof route is:

1. Compute the singular support of \(\mathcal F\).
2. Compute the singular support of \(\tau_1^*\mathcal F\).
3. Show the local monodromy representations cannot match.
4. Conclude no common irreducible summand exists.
5. Apply Goursat.

This is a realistic Katz-style proof target.

---

# 5. Does this prove polynomial cocycle expansion?

Not yet.

Even after proving

\[
(T_p(n),T_p(n-1))
\]

equidistribution, one must still pass from trace statistics to the full matrix element distribution in \(SL_2(\mathbb F_p)\).

Trace alone does not determine an element of \(SL_2\). One needs either:

* all matrix coefficients of the standard representation;
* equidistribution of the Frobenius conjugacy classes together with enough representations;
* or directly the rank-4 representation monodromy.

The rank-4 sheaf is actually the better object because its Frobenius trace gives the required matrix-coordinate functions.

---

# Final verdict

The proposed route is the closest correct sheaf-theoretic formulation so far.

The statement

\[
\text{``same Mellin sheaf at }n\text{ and }n-1\Rightarrow\text{independent''}
\]

is the one invalid step.

The repaired theorem is:

> If the Mellin sheaf \(\mathcal F\) has no geometric overlap with its translate \(\tau_1^*\mathcal F\), then the shifted-pair sheaf has product monodromy, and Katz equidistribution gives the required joint distribution.

Thus the polynomial cocycle expansion problem reduces to a concrete Katz problem:

\[
\boxed{\text{prove translation-independence of the Apéry Mellin sheaf.}}
\]

This is substantially sharper than the original formulation and appears to be the true remaining geometric bottleneck.
