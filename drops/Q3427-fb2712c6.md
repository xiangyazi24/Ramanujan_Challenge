I do **not** use this as the main obstruction because the question defines `C47=J4-5J7` at an aligned endpoint and does not itself state block-independence of that particular normalization. The gauge theorem in Section 7 does not need that extra assumption.

> **Theorem.** Let
> \[
> P_J(x)=\sum_{r=0}^J(-1)^r\binom JrF_rx^r,
> \qquad
> h_n(J)=[x^n]\theta\log P_J(x).
> \]
> Then
> \[
> h_1=-2J,
> \quad
> h_2=6J^2-10J,
> \quad
> h_3=-6J^3+54J^2-56J,
> \]
> \[
> h_4=-3J^4-102J^3+435J^2-346J.
> \]
> Consequently, for fixed bounded shifts `r,s`, every
> \[
> D_n(7u+r,4u+s),\qquad n\le4,
> \]
> is a cubic polynomial in `u`, and the unshifted `D_1,...,D_4` form a basis of `Q[u]_{<=3}`. Hence every fixed-coefficient shifted `n<=4` Dwork ansatz for `C47` is equivalent to one fixed cubic congruence
> \[
> C_{47}\equiv Q(u)\pmod p.
> \]
> The current Franel-Dwork theorem supplies integrality and `O(log m)` height for the right side, but no theorem identifies it with the sampled-Apéry interpolation scalar `C47=J4-5J7`. Moreover the current local Newton/Lucas/Dwork law admits a target-preserving additive tail gauge which fixes every pure Dwork scalar and moves `C47` arbitrarily modulo `p`; therefore no such identity is a formal consequence of that local law. Any positive theorem must use an additional global gauge-fixing normalization connecting the Franel-Dwork family to the canonical Apéry interpolation derivative.

This is the strongest exact conclusion justified by the current state without a finite scan or an invented canonical counterexample family.
