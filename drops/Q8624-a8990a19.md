# Q8624 audit: attainable bounds for the target-regular fibre

## 1. What the available inputs actually give

The proposed ingredients are not sufficient to prove

\[
J_X=o(X/\log^3 X).
\]

The obstruction is not the short CRT step. The obstruction is that the Apéry root condition does not currently supply three independent logarithmic savings.

For a fixed translation parameter t, the three conditions have the shape

\[
p_i\mid B_{u_i(t)},\qquad N-d_i=c_i p_i,
\]

where \(u_i(t)\) is affine before folding. The shell-prime/cofactor decomposition gives at most the usual divisor/sieve constraints on the integers \(N-d_i\).

The exact short CRT statement is useful: after fixing \((p_2,p_3)\), the congruence determines \(c_1\) modulo \(p_2p_3\), and the admissible interval has length \(O(X)\). Since \(p_2p_3\asymp X^2\), this gives a genuine factor

\[
O(X/(p_2p_3)+1)=O(1)
\]

for the coefficient window. It does not create logarithmic savings by itself.

## 2. Prime divisors of Apéry values

The elementary height estimate is

\[
\log B_u=O(u).
\]

Hence the number of distinct prime divisors of one value satisfies only

\[
\omega(B_u)\ll u/\log u.
\]

For shell primes this gives no better than a proportion comparable to a logarithm for a single root index. More explicitly, if \(u\asymp X\), then the possible shell primes dividing \(B_u\) are bounded by

\[
\#\{p\in(X,2X]:p\mid B_u\}\leq \omega(B_u)\ll X/\log X.
\]

After normalizing by the total shell prime count \(\asymp X/\log X\), this is only an O(1) density statement. The height bound does not prove that a random shell prime occurs with probability \(1/\log X\).

Therefore it does not yield three independent factors

\[
(1/\log X)^3.
\]

At present the rigorous saving supplied by the root-divisor height is at most one global divisor-count saving, and even that must be combined carefully with the cofactor equations.

## 3. Sieve on the three shifted products

Ignoring Apéry roots, the conditions

\[
N,\ N-d_2,\ N-d_3
\]

having shell prime factors have a shifted-prime-factor sieve interpretation. A Brun/Selberg sieve can estimate the number of N with prescribed large prime divisors, but it treats the prime divisors as independent local conditions only after averaging over N and residue classes.

The missing input is a uniform correlation estimate of the form

\[
\sum_t 1_{p_1\mid B_{u_1(t)}}1_{p_2\mid B_{u_2(t)}}1_{p_3\mid B_{u_3(t)}}
\]

with savings beyond the trivial divisor bound.

The Apéry recurrence gives congruences such as Lucas factorization and reflection, but these describe the location of zeros modulo a fixed prime. They do not imply that zeros at three unrelated shell primes behave independently as t varies.

## 4. Best rigorous bound from the stated tools

A crude combination is:

- \(O(X)\) choices of t;
- each shifted integer has at most \(O(X/\log X)\) possible shell prime divisors from the height estimate;
- the CRT window removes a coefficient multiplicity but does not add a logarithmic factor.

This leaves a bound of polynomial size in X, not

\[
o(X/\log^3X).
\]

The desired estimate requires an additional theorem, for example a Bombieri--Vinogradov type statement for the Apéry zero sets, a large-sieve estimate for the trace functions controlling \(B_u\bmod p\), or an algebraic independence/resultant statement forcing simultaneous roots to be rare.

## 5. Exact missing resultant/S-unit gate

A sufficient characteristic-zero statement would have to control the equations

\[
B_{a_1t+b_1}\equiv0\pmod {p_1},
\]
\[
B_{a_2t+b_2}\equiv0\pmod {p_2},
\]
\[
B_{a_3t+b_3}\equiv0\pmod {p_3}.
\]

One would need a bound showing that the simultaneous integer compatibility locus has height much smaller than the ambient O(X) translations.

A possible gate is a resultant estimate: if two affine root conditions could be lifted to characteristic zero polynomial conditions

\[
F_i(t)=0,
\]

then a nonzero resultant

\[
\operatorname{Res}(F_i,F_j)
\]

with controlled height would bound common solutions by divisor estimates. However the current Apéry congruence information does not produce such fixed characteristic-zero polynomials of bounded degree. The degrees grow with the indices.

The continuant/Casoratian identities similarly control propagation once a prime is fixed; they do not yet provide a three-prime collision bound.

## Conclusion

The strongest conclusion obtainable from the listed ingredients is:

1. The short CRT window is a real multiplicity reduction.
2. Shifted-prime sieve estimates can control the arithmetic progression part after averaging.
3. The height \(\log B_u=O(u)\) does **not** supply three independent \(1/\log X\) factors.
4. A uniform bound \(J_X=o(X/\log^3X)\) remains beyond these tools.

The missing ingredient is a genuine recurrence-aware anti-correlation theorem for Apéry roots across three shifted shell primes. Without it, the proposed fibre estimate cannot be derived unconditionally.
