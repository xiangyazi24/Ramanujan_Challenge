# Fourth moment of the Apéry shell-prime count

## Executive conclusion

The fourth moment is not the missing theorem.  A CRT expansion does give a strong global moment estimate, essentially of Poisson size.  The obstruction is that any fixed moment only controls the measure of large values, not the maximum.  The route to LGTQ-3 requires either:

1. a uniform upper-tail estimate for all n (large sieve/exponential moment type), or
2. a structural theorem preventing simultaneous shell hits.

The fourth moment is therefore a useful diagnostic, but it does not close the conjecture by itself.

## 1. Exact fourth moment expansion

Write

\[
S(n)=\sum_{p\in(P,2P]}X_p(n),\qquad X_p(n)=1_{n\bmod p\in Z_p}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{n\le N}S(n)^4=\sum_{p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4}\sum_{n\le N}\prod_iX_{p_i}(n).
\]

Separate according to the partition type of the four primes.

For four distinct primes, CRT gives

\[
\sum_{n\le N}\prod_iX_{p_i}(n)
=N\prod_i\frac{|Z_{p_i}|}{p_i}+O(1).
\]

Hence the main distinct-prime contribution is

\[
N\left(\sum_p\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\right)^4+O(\text{CRT error}).
\]

Since the shell density is about one zero per prime, this is O(N).

The diagonal terms are larger but still harmless.  For example p_1=p_2 and p_3=p_4 contributes

\[
N\sum_{p\ne q}\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p}\frac{|Z_q|^2}{q}.
\]

Using |Z_p|=O(1) heuristically gives

\[
N(\sum_{p\sim P}1/p)^2=O(N),
\]

and even the weaker known bounds only produce polylogarithmic losses.

Therefore the natural fourth moment is

\[
\sum_{n\le N}S(n)^4=N\,E(S^4)+o(N),
\]

with E(S^4) of constant size.

## 2. Poisson comparison

For a Poisson variable of mean lambda,

\[
E(X^4)=\lambda+7\lambda^2+6\lambda^3+\lambda^4.
\]

For lambda approximately 0.5 this is a small constant (~3-4).  Thus the numerical Poisson law is exactly consistent with the CRT calculation.

The fourth moment is not unexpectedly large; it is behaving like an independent rare-event model.

## 3. Why this does not prove the maximum bound

Markov gives

\[
\#\{n:S(n)>V\}\leq V^{-4}\sum_nS(n)^4.
\]

Therefore

\[
\#\{n:S(n)>V\}\ll N/V^4.
\]

This gives excellent density-one statements.  However, to prove

\[
\max_{n\le N}S(n)=o(N/\log N)
\]

one needs information at the extreme tail.  No fixed k works.

A moment of order k gives only

\[
\#\{S>V\}\ll N V^{-k}.
\]

Taking V=N/log N gives

\[
N(N/\log N)^{-k}=N^{1-k}(\log N)^k,
\]

which is already tiny for k>1, but this does not apply because the shell range P and the required uniformity vary with n.  More importantly, the LGTQ-3 reduction asks for a deterministic upper bound over every n, not merely a density estimate.

## 4. Can moments beat CRT?

Not at the fourth moment level.  CRT predicts the correct independent model.

Any improvement would require showing negative correlations between different primes:

\[
\Pr(p_1,\ldots,p_k\mid b_{n\bmod p_i})
<\prod_i|Z_{p_i}|/p_i.
\]

There is no known mechanism forcing such anticorrelation.  In fact the observed Poisson distribution indicates that the independent model is close to optimal.

The desired gain is not smaller moments; it is stronger control of rare simultaneous configurations.

## 5. Critical moment index

There is no finite critical k.

Even knowing all fixed moments

\[
E(S^k)=O_k(1)
\]

only implies tightness of the family S(n).  It does not exclude rare n with S(n) growing slowly.

To control the maximum one needs either:

- moments with k growing with N, or
- an exponential moment bound.

## 6. Exponential moments

The ideal statement is

\[
E(e^{tS})\ll_t1
\]

uniformly.

For a Poisson model,

\[
E(e^{tS})=\exp(\lambda(e^t-1)).
\]

Then Chernoff gives

\[
\#\{S>V\}\ll N\exp(-V\log V+O(V)).
\]

This is strong enough to rule out dangerous shell clusters.

However proving this requires controlling all factorial moments:

\[
\sum_n(S(n))_k
\]

uniformly for k growing, which is equivalent to controlling k-fold CRT intersections.  The fourth moment is only the k=4 case.

## 7. What is the next realistic target?

The hierarchy is:

1. L2: already proved.
2. Fixed higher moments: likely accessible by CRT and current zero-set bounds.
3. Growing moments k≈c log log N: potentially enough for subpolynomial tails.
4. Exponential moments: essentially equivalent to the missing large-sieve theorem.

The next meaningful target is not the fourth moment but a growing factorial moment estimate:

\[
\sum_{n\le N}(S(n))_k\ll N C^k
\]

for k up to a slowly growing range.

## 8. Relation to the Apéry structure

The only place where Apéry arithmetic enters is the correlation estimate

\[
\#\{n:n\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i}\ \forall i\}.
\]

For fixed distinct primes CRT solves this.  The hard case is not average intersections but exceptional aligned tuples of primes and residues.

This matches the previous LGTQ-3 obstruction: the missing theorem is an anti-clustering theorem for shell primes.

## Final verdict

The fourth moment is essentially already available from the CRT model and cannot be the breakthrough.  It confirms that shell hits behave like a Poisson process on average.

The decisive missing result is a uniform high-moment/exponential-moment estimate, or an equivalent theorem excluding structured multi-prime alignments.  The research target should move from

\[
E(S^4)
\]

to

\[
E((S)_k),\qquad k\to\infty,
\]

or directly to a large-values estimate of the form

\[
\#\{n:S(n)>V\}\ll N\exp(-cV\log V).
\]

That is the correct bridge from the density-one theorem to the all-n Apéry GCD conjecture.
