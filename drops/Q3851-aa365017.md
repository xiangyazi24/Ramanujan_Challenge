\]

The first moment is even weaker at this threshold. The common-value q-mate theorem controls the total number of extensions, but allows them to be concentrated into a much smaller set of high-degree `(p,z)` stars. No banked theorem controls that concentration.

The fixed-gap resultant also does not help on this tail: once the shifts vary, the pair `(h,k)` varies, and the only available aggregate height is the supercritical sum (6.7). The lower bound `d_j-d_i>D_c` supplies no deterministic inverse-gap saving; a set of shifts can have two dense clusters separated by more than `D_c`, leaving a positive proportion of all cross-cluster pairs.

This is the precise failure point.

# 10. Narrow formal obstruction to a first-degree carrier proof

Let `G_1,...,G_K` be the squarefree integers `G(z,d)` at one fixed `(p,z)`, restricted to the shifts for which `p|G(z,d)`. Then

\[
v_p\left(\prod_{i=1}^KG_i\right)=K,
\tag{10.1}
\]

whereas

\[
\boxed{
v_p\left(
\prod_{1\le i<j\le K}\gcd(G_i,G_j)
\right)=\binom K2.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

Consequently no bounded power of the first-degree product can pay the pair carrier uniformly in `K`. One needs an exponent at least `(K-1)/2` at that star.

The extra arithmetic conditions do not alter this exponent ledger:

- `pq_i|b_(z+7d_i)` pays each edge once;
- `p product_i q_i | b_(7p-1-z)` pays the common lower prime once and each upper prime once;
- the adjacent shifted resultant pays one `p`-adic unit per marked pair, but it is a different determinant as the pair geometry varies.

None of the current statements forces one prescribed integer of subcritical aggregate height to contain

\[
p^{\binom K2}.
\]

This is a scoped obstruction to the proposed proof mechanism, not an assertion that the Apéry-specific excess inequality is false.

# 11. Corrected theorem obtainable now

## Theorem — minus-minus paired common-value contraction

Fix `J`, and let `G(z,d)` be exactly the squarefree-filtered carrier in the question. Assume the currently proved first and second zero moments, the q-mate bound (3.2), the continuant recurrence/nonzero-content theorem, and the separated-resultant height theorem.

Then:

1. The exact pair carrier is

   \[
   C=\sum_{p,z}\binom{K_{p,z}}2\log p.
   \]

2. Unconditionally,

   \[
   C\ll_JT^{43/15}\ell^{-3/5}+T^{8/3}.
   \]

3. For every `D>=1`,

   \[
   C_{\le D}\ll_JD^2T^{8/5}\ell^{1/5}.
   \]

4. With `D_c` and `Lambda` defined by (V5) and (V8),

   \[
   C
   \ll_J
   \frac{\mathcal H_0(T)}{\eta_1(T)^2}
   +
   \frac{\mathcal H_0(T)}{\eta_2(T)}
   +
   \ell\,\mathfrak X_{\Lambda,D_c}(T).
   \tag{11.1}
   \]

Therefore the single Apéry-specific inequality `(MM-excess)` implies

\[
\boxed{
C=o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{\log T}\right).
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

The new inequality is a truncated far-pair codegree estimate, not a support radical estimate and not the full target restated. It retains only the genuinely unresolved simultaneous concentration of many distinct shifted upper-prime/upper-zero events at one lower `(p,z)` star.
