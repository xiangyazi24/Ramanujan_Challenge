# Pointed anti-concentration for the Apéry projective orbit

## Main conclusion

The correct framework is not ordinary mixing of a random walk on $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)$ and not global $L^2$ discrepancy.  The required statement is a **pointed return theorem for a non-autonomous algebraic cocycle**.

Full monodromy $G_p\supseteq \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)$ explains why the orbit looks random, but by itself it does not imply bounded visits to a point.  The missing input is a statement controlling the correlation between the specific cocycle orbit and the point stabilizer.

---

## (A) Random matrix model

Let

$$
M_r=g_r\cdots g_1,
$$

where the $g_i$ are independent uniform elements of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)$.  Because the uniform measure is stationary, each $M_r$ is itself uniform in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)$.

Therefore

$$
\Pr(M_r v=w)=\frac1{p+1}.
$$

For $n\approx p$ steps,

$$
N_w=\#\{r:M_rv=w\}
$$

has approximately a Poisson distribution with mean

$$
\lambda=\frac{n}{p+1}\approx1.
$$

Hence:

- expectation is $O(1)$;
- all fixed moments are bounded;
- $N_w=O(\log p/\log\log p)$ with overwhelming probability;
- $N_w=O(1)$ with positive probability tending to a constant, but not uniformly with probability $1-o(1)$.

The random model therefore predicts the observed Poisson law, but it does not give a deterministic theorem.

---

## (B) Deterministic condition needed

A sufficient algebraic condition would be a **uniform pointed mixing estimate**:

$$
\left|\sum_r 1_{M(r)v=w}-\frac n{p+1}\right|\le C.
$$

Representation-theoretically this is equivalent to bounding all nontrivial matrix coefficients:

$$
\sum_r \langle \delta_w,\rho(M(r))\delta_v\rangle=O(1).
$$

Full monodromy only gives irreducibility of the representation.  It does not bound this sum.

A stronger condition would be that the cocycle has a **spectrally expanding transfer operator** on the orthogonal complement of constants.  For a fixed random walk this follows from property (T)-type arguments.  For the Apéry cocycle the matrices vary with $r$, so this theory does not directly apply.

---

## (C) Variety viewpoint

The condition

$$
5M_{11}(r)+M_{12}(r)=0
$$

is indeed a hypersurface condition in $\mathrm{GL}_2$.

However, the difficulty is that $M(r)$ is not a random point of the group variety.  It is a one-parameter product:

$$
T(r-1)\cdots T(1).
$$

The degree of the resulting matrix entries grows with $r$.  A naive Lang-Weil or Bézout estimate therefore gives bounds much larger than $O(1)$.

The correct algebraic-geometric formulation is a **pullback intersection problem**:

$$
\gamma(r)\in V,
$$

where $\gamma$ is the Apéry cocycle section and $V$ is the point stabilizer variety.  One needs bounded complexity of this pullback.  This is exactly the missing AFP theorem.

---

## (D) Variable Möbius maps

The affine coordinate satisfies

$$
x_{r+1}=c(r)-\frac{d(r)}{x_r}.
$$

This is a non-autonomous Riccati equation.

The classical theory of iterated Möbius maps studies

$$
x_{r+1}=g(x_r)
$$

for fixed $g$.  Then one has eigenvalue formulas, periodicity, and explicit diagonalization.

The Apéry case is different:

$$
x_r=M(r)x_0
$$

with changing $M(r)$.  There is no single matrix to diagonalize.

The closest theories are:

- $p$-adic differential equations;
- difference Galois theory;
- algebraic cocycles over finite fields;
- $\ell$-adic sheaves attached to the recurrence.

The right analogue of eigenvalues is the monodromy representation of the cocycle.

---

## (E) Fibonacci comparison

For Fibonacci,

$$
f_r=\frac{\phi^r-\psi^r}{\phi-\psi},
$$

because the transfer matrix is constant.  The zero condition becomes

$$
(\phi/\psi)^r=1.
$$

Thus zeros are controlled by multiplicative order in a finite field.

For Apéry there is no analogous closed formula of the form $\alpha^r-\beta^r$.  The best replacement would be a Frobenius-period formula:

$$
M(r)=\mathcal P\exp\left(\int A(t)dt\right)
$$

in the $p$-adic/differential-equation sense.

The Apéry recurrence is therefore a variable-coefficient analogue of Fibonacci where the constant eigenvalues are replaced by monodromy.

# What the known structure implies

The three strongest observations:

1. $G_p\supseteq \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)$ gives randomness of the cocycle.
2. $M(p-1)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ gives a global involutive symmetry.
3. The Poisson occupancy strongly suggests a random model.

But none alone proves AFP.

The missing theorem should look like:

**Pointed anti-concentration theorem.** For the Apéry cocycle,

$$
\#\{r:M(r)v=w\}=\frac{p}{p+1}+O(1).
$$

Equivalently, the Steinberg matrix coefficient has bounded partial sums:

$$
\sum_r \mathrm{St}_{v,w}(M(r))=O(1).
$$

This is stronger than Deligne's square-root cancellation.  Deligne controls arbitrary trace functions:

$$
O(\sqrt p),
$$

while AFP requires an additional arithmetic rigidity coming from the special Apéry cocycle (reflection law, swap closure, and possibly a hidden geometric symmetry).

The most promising route is not a generic monodromy argument, but identifying an additional structure that turns the Steinberg coefficient into a coboundary or telescoping sum.
