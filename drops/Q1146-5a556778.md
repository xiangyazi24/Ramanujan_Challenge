# Universal Lucas-content audit

## Conclusion

The broad assertion is false: the p-Lucas property alone does not force

`gcd_{j<M}(L_j(n)u_j) | u_n`.

The Newton coefficients encode a Kummer divisibility pattern, while Lucas only controls residues after reduction modulo each prime. It does not connect the zero set of the sequence to the particular low indices selected by the Newton kernel.

## Countermodel mechanism

A Lucas sequence can be modeled by compatible digit data. Such models satisfy

`u_{pN+r}=u_Nu_r (mod p)`

because the base-p digit decomposition separates the low digit from the higher digits.

However this leaves freedom to arrange a prime p and an index n such that

`u_n = 0 (mod p)`

while some Newton index `j<M` has

`p ∤ L_j(n)` and `u_j` is a p-unit.

Then the gcd of the Newton products need not contain the p-factor appearing in `u_n`. Hence the claimed divisibility cannot be a Lucas-only theorem.

## Mod-p^2 lift

The stronger relation

`u_(pN+r)=u_N(u_r+pN B_r) mod p^2`

still only controls the first p-adic derivative layer. It does not imply that all Newton-minimizing indices have the same valuation behavior.

The remaining obstruction is a simultaneous condition:

1. `j` minimizes `v_p(L_j(n))+v_p(u_j)`;
2. `u_j` has lower p-valuation than `u_n`;
3. the first p-adic correction term does not repair the difference.

A proof for Apéry numbers therefore needs Apéry-specific p-adic information, not just Lucas lifting.

## Missing structural lemma

A sufficient additional statement would be:

For every prime p and n, if `v_p(u_n)=a`, then every Newton-minimizing index j satisfies

`v_p(L_j(n))+v_p(u_j) >= a`.

For Apéry numbers this would have to come from the recurrence, the hypergeometric representation, or a derivative/Wronskian identity. It is not implied by Lucas.

Therefore the observed Apéry gcd-content divisibility is genuinely sequence-specific; finite verification cannot establish a universal Lucas theorem.
