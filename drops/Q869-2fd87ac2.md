# Q869: characteristic-zero carrier audit for J_n

## Verdict

The proposed carrier \(J_n=\gcd(L_n/n,b_n,X_n)\) is a correct exact reformulation of the large-prime obstruction, but the suggested recurrence/Wronskian route does not produce a sublinear bound. The obstruction is not a missing factor of \(L_n\); it is that the Casoratian identity already gives the optimal available integer height.

## 1. Exact setup and immediate divisibility

Let

\[
X_n=L_n^3a_n\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
J_n=\gcd(L_n/n,b_n,X_n).
\]

For a prime \(p>\sqrt n\), \(p\mid L_n\) occurs with exponent exactly one. The banked local law gives

\[
v_p(J_n)=1 \iff p\mid b_{n\bmod p}.
\]

Hence the large-prime part of 

\[
\log J_n
\]

is exactly

\[
W(n)=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n,\ p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}\log p.
\]

The remaining primes contribute only \(O(\sqrt n)\) by the valuation bounds coming from \(L_n\).

Therefore any proof of

\[
\log J_n=o(n)
\]

must eliminate this large-prime contribution.

## 2. Attempt: Wronskian clearing

The Casoratian is

\[
a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.
\]

Multiplying by \(L_n^3\) gives

\[
X_nb_{n-1}-X_{n-1}b_n=6\frac{L_n^3}{n^3}.
\]

If a divisor \(D\) divides \(J_n\), then \(D\mid b_n\) and \(D\mid X_n\). Reducing the identity modulo \(D\) gives

\[
D\mid 6\frac{L_n^3}{n^3}
\]

provided \(D\) also divides the corresponding adjacent term. This is the only unconditional Bezout-type output.

However for the large primes \(p>\sqrt n\), the right hand side contains exactly one factor of \(p\) whenever \(p\mid L_n\). It therefore gives no saving over the already known \(L_n\) contribution.

The height is

\[
\log(6L_n^3/n^3)=3\psi(n)+O(\log n)=\Theta(n),
\]

not \(o(n)\).

Thus Wronskian clearing is saturated.

## 3. Attempt: adjacent-index gcd

One might try to combine

\[
J_n\mid X_n,b_n
\]

with

\[
J_{n-1}\mid X_{n-1},b_{n-1}.
\]

The recurrence gives

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\]

But a prime \(p>\sqrt n\) satisfying

\[
p\mid b_{n\bmod p}
\]

is not forced to divide a neighboring residue. The zero condition lives at the Cartier block index modulo \(p\), not at adjacent integer indices.

Therefore no deterministic amplification

\[
J_n^\alpha\mid\text{small-height integer}
\]

follows.

## 4. Attempt: primitive divisor mechanism

Primitive divisor results would need a statement of the form:

"large primes dividing \(b_{n-r}\) cannot repeatedly divide the same recurrence orbit."

The required primes are not divisors of one fixed term \(b_m\); they are primes varying with \(m=n\bmod p\). Primitive divisor theorems do not address this moving-modulus condition.

No usable inequality results.

## 5. Exact obstruction model

The missing step would need a new theorem controlling the set

\[
\{p>\sqrt n:p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}.
\]

The recurrence and Casoratian only control valuations after a prime is known to divide a term. They do not control the occurrence of these moving residue zeros.

In particular, an identity of the desired form

\[
J_n^\alpha\mid H_n
\]

with

\[
\log H_n=o(\alpha n)
\]

would imply a nontrivial upper bound on \(W(n)\), because

\[
\alpha W(n)\le \log H_n.
\]

No such \(H_n\) is produced by the existing characteristic-zero identities.

## 6. L_n versus L_{n-1}

The exact relation is

\[
L_n=L_{n-1}\prod_{\substack{p\le n\\p\text{ prime power}\ p^e=n}}p
\]

in the usual prime-power update form. For a prime \(p>\sqrt n\), the update exponent is exactly one because \(p^2>n\). Hence replacing \(L_n\) by \(L_{n-1}\) only removes the endpoint prime factors and cannot affect the moving large-prime carrier.

The large-prime obstruction survives unchanged.

## 7. Counterexample to the proposed mechanism

The proposed mechanism would require the recurrence to turn a congruence

\[
b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p
\]

into a small-height integer divisibility statement. But the congruence is compatible with the recurrence because the recurrence itself is modulo \(p\) and the coefficient singularities occur at indices crossing multiples of \(p\).

The recurrence supplies propagation inside a fixed characteristic. It does not supply cancellation between different characteristics \(p\).

Therefore the mechanism cannot be completed without an additional horizontal distribution theorem.

## Final line-by-line verdict

| Proposed tool | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Recurrence alone | Insufficient: controls fixed-modulus propagation only |
| Casoratian | Exact but saturated; gives height Θ(n) |
| Adjacent indices | No transfer from \(b_{n\bmod p}\) to neighbors |
| Primitive divisors | Wrong direction; primes are moving with the index |
| p-adic valuation identities | Already encoded in the banked local law |
| Bezout identity | Produces only \(6L_n^3/n^3\), whose height is linear |

The exact conclusion is therefore: \(J_n\) is a valid characteristic-zero carrier, but the known characteristic-zero identities cannot prove 

\[
\log J_n=o(n).
\]

The missing ingredient is a genuinely new horizontal theorem on Apéry residues \(b_{n\bmod p}\pmod p\), not a stronger use of the Wronskian.
