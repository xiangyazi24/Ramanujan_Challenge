# Arithmetic-intersection audit of the aligned content invariant

## Executive conclusion

The product-formula/arithmetic-Bezout route does not currently prove
\[
\sum_{a+b+c\le H}\log C^*_{a,b,c}=H^{3+o(1)}.
\]
The obstruction is not a missing citation to arithmetic Bezout: the available
height inequalities bound the height of the *whole resultant configuration*,
while the desired invariant is the finite-place intersection of two moving
translated Apéry factors after structural saturation.  The known root-strip
lemma controls the archimedean side (separation of complex roots), but it does
not control non-archimedean coincidences of algebraic roots modulo many primes.

The strongest realistic product-formula statement is a conditional reduction:
if one can prove a subquadratic height bound for the primitive intersection
carrier of each aligned triple, then AC^tr follows.  That missing estimate is
essentially the arithmetic version of the aligned-content problem itself.

## 1. What arithmetic Bezout actually gives

Let
\[
F=N_a(x),\qquad G=N_b(x+a),\qquad J=N_{b+c}(x+a),
\]
with the central palindromic factor removed where required.  Put
\[
R(T)=\operatorname{Res}_x(F,G+TJ).
\]
The content
\[
C=\operatorname{cont}_T R(T)
\]
is the primitive common divisor of all coefficients of the resultant pencil.
Locally at a prime p, it is the Fitting ideal of the torsion module
\[
M_p=\operatorname{coker}(A_p\xrightarrow{(G,J)}A_p^2),
\quad A_p=\mathbf Z_p[x]/(F),
\]
after removing the structural leading-coefficient factors.

Arithmetic intersection theory therefore identifies
\[
v_p(C^*)=\operatorname{length}_{\mathbf Z_p}(\text{primitive intersection module})
\]
not with an archimedean quantity, but with a finite-place intersection multiplicity.

An arithmetic Bezout theorem (for example Philippon/Bost-Gillet-Soulé type
height inequalities) has schematic form
\[
 h(Z\cap W)\le d_1d_2(h(Z)+h(W)+O(\log d_1d_2)).
\]
For the zero-dimensional intersections here this bounds the *global height* of
an intersection cycle.  It does not remove the finite-place term
\[
\sum_p v_p(C^*)\log p.
\]
That term is exactly the quantity to be bounded.

## 2. Why root-strip separation is insufficient

The First Lemma gives a statement of the following type:

- roots of the continuant polynomials lie in disjoint complex strips;
- therefore certain resultants do not vanish over C;
- therefore a pair of algebraic factors has nonzero complex separation.

This implies a lower bound on the archimedean absolute value of a resultant:
\[
|\operatorname{Res}(F,G)|_\infty>0.
\]
But the product formula is
\[
\sum_v \log |\alpha|_v=0,
\]
so a small p-adic value can be compensated by the archimedean value.  Root
separation gives only one place in this sum.

A family illustrating the failure is:
\[
F_n(x)=x^n-2,
\qquad G_n(x)=x^n-2+p_n.
\]
The complex roots remain separated by approximately
\(p_n/n\), while modulo p_n the two polynomials acquire a complete common
root set.  Thus complex separation alone does not bound
\(v_{p_n}(\operatorname{Res}(F_n,G_n))\).

The Apéry situation is more rigid, but the missing rigidity must be proved
arithmetically; it does not follow from the root-strip theorem.

## 3. Why the naive triangular height has the wrong scale

The available continuation/cut-edge carrier has size roughly
\[
\prod_{a+b+c\le H} \Delta_{a,b,c},
\]
where each local edge factor has logarithmic size comparable to its degree.
The resulting height is
\[
\log \Delta_{\rm naive}=\Theta(H^3\log H).
\]

Arithmetic Bezout applied directly to this carrier reproduces this bound. It
cannot explain why the primitive saturated content should lose the logarithm.

At the critical dyadic step, the exceptional-prime contribution is weighted by
local zero multiplicity. Therefore a spare \(\log H\) is not automatically
absorbed.

## 4. Subspace theorem / BCZ-type gcd estimates

A Corvaja-Zannier/Bugeaud-Corvaja-Zannier style gcd estimate would require a
sequence of algebraic points with a fixed ambient torus or semiabelian
structure and a non-degeneracy condition excluding infinitely many points in
proper subvarieties.

The aligned family is instead
\[
(N_a(x),N_b(x+a),N_{b+c}(x+a))
\]
with three independently moving indices.  The parameter space dimension grows
with H and the translated continuants are not a fixed finitely generated
multiplicative orbit.

The required hypothesis would be something like:

\[
\sum_{a+b+c\le H} \sum_p
\operatorname{mult}_p(F_{a,b,c},G_{a,b,c},J_{a,b,c})\log p
\ll H^{3+o(1)},
\]
which is a reformulation of AC^tr rather than a consequence of current gcd
technology.

## 5. Smallest additional input that would close the route

The minimal missing theorem is not full AC^tr; it is a primitive height-loss
lemma:

**Primitive intersection height conjecture.** After removing the structural
prime set \(\mathfrak U_H\), the primitive common-root divisor satisfies
\[
\log C^*_{a,b,c}
\le O((a+b+c)^\epsilon)
\bigl(a+b+c\bigr)^2
\]
with enough average saving that
\[
\sum_{a+b+c\le H}\log C^*_{a,b,c}=H^{3+o(1)}.
\]

Equivalently, one needs to prove that non-structural finite intersections
cannot accumulate with the full triangular height.

## 6. Verdict on proposed tools

| Tool | Status |
|---|---|
| Arithmetic Bezout | Gives a global height upper bound, but retains the H^3 log H carrier |
| Adelic intersection | Correct language for C, but no compression without a primitive height theorem |
| Subspace theorem | Wrong ambient geometry unless a fixed algebraic dynamical structure is found |
| BCZ gcd estimates | No fixed recurrence/orbit hypothesis currently available |
| Root-strip separation | Proves transversality over C, not finite-place sparsity |

The correct strategic conclusion is therefore:

- AC^tr remains the right invariant.
- Arithmetic intersection theory explains what it measures.
- Existing global height technology does not prove its required H^{3+o(1)} bound.
- A successful arithmetic route needs a new theorem controlling the loss of
  primitive height after structural saturation, not a stronger generic Bezout
  inequality.

A promising falsifiable target is a finite-place analogue of the root-strip
lemma: prove that every non-structural prime dividing a saturated aligned
content forces a new algebraic constraint on the continuant transfer matrix,
with bounded total rank. Such a theorem would directly bridge the complex and
p-adic sides and would be genuinely stronger than the current archimedean
separation result.
