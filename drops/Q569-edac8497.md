# Q569 — Equidistribution of Apéry values and the gap between collision bounds and M1

## Summary

The collision-count route is useful, but it does not by itself reach M1.  It proves that the zero set cannot be much larger than square-root size, whereas M1 requires an averaged constant-size zero set.  The missing ingredient is not ordinary Weil cancellation; it is a mechanism preventing the special value 0 from having exceptional multiplicity.

## (A) Weil bound and C₂

The Parseval identity is correct. If

\[
S(a)=\sum_{r<p} e_p(a b_r),
\]

then

\[
C_2(p)=\frac1p\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}|S(a)|^2.
\]

The zero frequency contributes p. A uniform Weil estimate

\[
|S(a)|\ll \sqrt p
\]

for a≠0 gives

\[
C_2(p)\ll p.
\]

This is the natural Deligne/Weil level of equidistribution.

However,

\[
|Z_p|^2=m(0)^2\le C_2(p)
\]

only implies

\[
|Z_p|\ll \sqrt p.
\]

This is exactly the usual character-sum barrier.

## (B) Why full equidistribution is too strong

A statement such as

\[
m(v)=1+O(p^{-1/2})
\]

for every v is impossible for Apéry zeros because the observed palindromic zeros force multiplicities greater than one infinitely often.

The correct target is weaker:

\[
\mathbb E_p |Z_p|=O(1),
\]

not pointwise equidistribution of every value.

The zero value is not special algebraically, but the event "Fourier transform vanishes" is a nonlinear condition.  Deligne bounds control Fourier amplitudes, not the probability that an amplitude equals exactly zero.

## (C) The real barrier

There is a large gap:

\[
|S(a)|\ll \sqrt p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|Z_p|\ll\sqrt p,
\]

while M1 needs

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|\ll \pi(X).
\]

Breaking this gap likely requires averaging over p.  The right philosophy is:

- Weil/Deligne gives cancellation inside each fixed finite field.
- A large sieve or monodromy argument must average the exceptional zero events over varying p.

## (D) Large sieve over primes

The Mellin expression

\[
b_r=\sum_t H_p(t)t^{-r}
\]

is promising because r indexes multiplicative characters.  But a standard large sieve does not immediately apply because:

1. The coefficient system H_p(t) depends on p.
2. The condition b_r=0 is nonlinear.
3. Different primes live in different character groups.

The missing theorem would be something like:

"For the family of Apéry trace functions, the number of vanishing Mellin coefficients averaged over p is bounded."

This is closer to a crystalline/monodromy anti-concentration statement than ordinary large sieve.

## Minimal ingredient beyond Weil

The needed upgrade is not stronger square-root cancellation.  It is an anti-concentration theorem for a family of trace functions:

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\#\{r:b_r(p)=0\}=O(\pi(X)).
\]

Possible routes:

1. A p-adic/crystalline family argument showing the zero locus has bounded average size.
2. A geometric monodromy statement showing the Mellin transforms behave like generic random variables.
3. A sieve over primes combined with a rigidity theorem for the Apéry recurrence.

## Conclusion

The collision count theorem is a useful intermediate result:

\[
C_2(p)=O(p)
\]

is consistent with random behavior and rules out large concentration.  But it cannot prove M1.  The decisive missing theorem is not equidistribution in the usual Fourier sense; it is **zero anti-concentration for the Apéry Mellin family**.

The large sieve viewpoint may help only after this nonlinear zero-detection step is converted into a linear statistic.  Until then, Weil bounds remain one square-root barrier short of the required O(1) average zero count.
