# Q1691: corrected half-integer Apéry solution attack

## 1. First correction: Q1687's candidate is impossible

The recurrence anchors are decisive. Any proposed closed form must satisfy

\[
u_0=1,\qquad u_1=0,\qquad u_2=-\frac1{27},\qquad u_3=-\frac{88}{135},\qquad u_4=-\frac{129763}{9261}.
\]

In particular, the value $u_2=4/27$ contradicts the recurrence and rejects that branch immediately. No later endpoint argument can repair a wrong normalization.

## 2. Relation with the Apéry recurrence

The recurrence

\[
a_tu_{t+1}-b_tu_t+c_tu_{t-1}=0
\]

with

\[
a_t=(2t+1)^3,\qquad b_t=4t(68t^2+3),\qquad c_t=(2t-1)^3
\]

becomes the classical Apéry recurrence after the half-integer shift

\[
n=t-\frac12 .
\]

Indeed,

\[
2t+1=2(n+1),\qquad 2t-1=2n,
\]

so after removing the common factor $8$ the coefficients are the standard Apéry coefficients

\[
(n+1)^3A_{n+1}-(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)A_n+n^3A_{n-1}=0.
\]

Therefore $u_t$ is a solution of the half-shifted Apéry equation.

## 3. What can be stated exactly about the two Apéry solutions

Let $A(n)$ and $B(n)$ denote any fixed fundamental pair of solutions of the classical Apéry equation, for example the standard holomorphic solution and its logarithmic companion obtained from the Frobenius basis at the regular singular point.

The half-integer solution space is two-dimensional, hence

\[
u_t=C_1A(t-1/2)+C_2B(t-1/2).
\]

The constants are uniquely determined by

\[
C_1A(-1/2)+C_2B(-1/2)=1,
\]

and

\[
C_1A(1/2)+C_2B(1/2)=0.
\]

Equivalently, using the Wronskian normalization,

\[
C_1=\frac{B(1/2)}{W(-1/2,1/2)},\qquad
C_2=-\frac{A(1/2)}{W(-1/2,1/2)}.
\]

This is the precise linear combination. A more explicit hypergeometric/Meijer/Wilson expression requires choosing a specific normalization of the Apéry basis; different published normalizations differ by triangular transformations.

## 4. Anchor verification

The correct verification route is not numerical fitting but substitution into the recurrence.

For the proposed basis expression define

\[
v_t=C_1A(t-1/2)+C_2B(t-1/2).
\]

Because both basis functions satisfy the shifted Apéry equation,

\[
a_tv_{t+1}-b_tv_t+c_tv_{t-1}=0.
\]

The constants above force

\[
v_0=1,\qquad v_1=0.
\]

The recurrence then gives uniquely

\[
v_2=-\frac1{27},
\]

\[
v_3=-\frac{88}{135},
\]

and

\[
v_4=-\frac{129763}{9261}.
\]

Thus the four anchors are consequences of the initial normalization plus the recurrence. Any candidate special-function formula failing one of these four values is the wrong branch or wrong shift.

## 5. Endpoint expression

The desired endpoint is

\[
u_{m-1}-5u_m .
\]

Modulo

\[
p=2m+1,
\]

one has the half-integer symmetry

\[
 t\mapsto m-t
\]

coming from reflection of the recurrence coefficients. This is the source of the reciprocal-trace phenomena, but it does not by itself imply the endpoint is nonzero.

The endpoint is a linear functional on the two-dimensional Apéry solution space:

\[
L(u)=u_{m-1}-5u_m.
\]

To prove $L(u)\ne0$ one needs either:

1. an explicit evaluation of the half-integer Apéry solution modulo $p$; or
2. a WZ/adjoint certificate converting $L(u)$ into a nonzero reciprocal trace.

The recurrence alone only gives the two-dimensional solution space and cannot exclude accidental vanishing.

## 6. Reciprocal trace issue

The expression

\[
\sum_t \frac{t^2-1}{u_t}
\]

or its equivalent reciprocal trace requires a separate adjoint identity. It is not obtained merely by identifying $u_t$ with an Apéry solution.

The missing theorem is an exact coefficient identity of the form

\[
\Delta(F_t)=w_t/u_t,
\]

where $F_t$ is built from a second adjoint solution. Without that identity, the claimed trace reduction is unsupported.

## 7. Primary-source citation status

A citation can legitimately be attached only to the exact theorem being used: namely the classical Apéry recurrence and its standard two-dimensional solution space. A citation to a general statement that "Apéry numbers have hypergeometric forms" is insufficient for this argument because it does not specify the half-integer analytic continuation and normalization.

Therefore the rigorous status is:

- the shift to the Apéry recurrence is valid;
- the normalized solution is uniquely the above linear combination;
- the four anchors are mandatory and verify the branch;
- endpoint nonvanishing and reciprocal-trace evaluation require an additional explicit arithmetic identity, not just the Apéry representation.

No formula should be accepted unless it passes the four anchor checks exactly.
