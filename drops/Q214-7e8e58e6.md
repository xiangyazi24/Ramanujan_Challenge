# The fractional moment between \((HM)_2\) and \((HM)_3\)

## Verdict

There is a clean continuous interpolation, but it is **not** the ordinary moment \(\sum K^{2+\delta}\), the Gamma-function falling factorial, or
\(K(K-1)|K-\lambda|^\delta\). Those observables have the wrong sparse-Poisson scale.

The correct interpolation is based on the number of **additional hits after an ordered pair has already occurred**:

\[
L_{pq}(m)=K_X(m)-2
\qquad
(m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q).
\]

For \(0<\delta<1\), define

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{\delta,\lambda}(K)
  =(K)_2\bigl(\lambda+(K-2)_+\bigr)^\delta.
}
\]

Then the strong fractional hypothesis is

\[
\boxed{
(HM)_{2+\delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}:
\qquad
\sum_{m<M}\Phi_{\delta,\lambda_X}(K_X(m))
 \ll_\delta M\lambda_X^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{FHM}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_2
 \left(1+\frac{(K_X(m)-2)_+}{\lambda_X}\right)^\delta
 \ll_\delta M\lambda_X^2.
\tag{FHM-norm}
\]

This has the correct endpoints:

\[
\delta=0:\quad F_2,
\qquad
\delta=1:\quad F_3+\lambda_XF_2.
\]

Since \((HM)_2\) is already proved, the **exact minimal new cross-prime input** is the nonnegative fractional pair-Palm excess

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}\ \sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\left[
 \bigl(L_{pq}(m)+\lambda_X\bigr)^\delta
 -\lambda_X^\delta
\right]
\ll_\delta M\lambda_X^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{FPP\(_\delta\)}
\]

This statement is equivalent to `(FHM)` up to the already-proved second-moment term. It is strictly weaker than \((HM)_3\): it is a weighted triple count in which a row containing many third primes receives the discount
\((K-2)^{-(1-\delta)}\).

There is **no soft deduction from \((HM)_2\) alone**. In fact, exact pairwise independence together with the uniform bound \(K\le3\) still permits

\[
F_2\asymp M\lambda^2,
\qquad
F_{2+\delta}\asymp M\lambda^2,
\]

which misses the required \(M\lambda^{2+\delta}\) by the factor \(\lambda^{-\delta}\). Thus any proof must add a genuine, although very weak, three-prime anti-clustering input.

In one line:

```text
The smallest new theorem is not full three-prime independence.
It is a lambda^delta saving for the pair-incidence mass that supports
at least one additional prime, with an integrated fractional tail for
rows supporting many additional primes.
```

## 1. Setup and the exact second-moment input

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p:X<p\le2X\},
\qquad M=X^2,
\]

and let

\[
\Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad
K(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\mathbf 1_{\Omega_p}(m).
\]

Write

\[
\lambda=\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

The exact finite-interval mean is

\[
\mu_X=\frac1M\sum_{m<M}K(m)
     =\sum_p\frac{|\Omega_p|}{M}.
\]

The endpoint errors relating \(\mu_X\) and \(\lambda_X\) are harmless at the scales below. Every exact identity may be written with \(\mu_X\) instead of \(\lambda_X\). We use \(\lambda\) to match the project notation. If \(\lambda=0\), all statements are trivial, so assume \(0<\lambda\le1\).

For integers \(r\ge1\), put

\[
(K)_r=K(K-1)\cdots(K-r+1),
\qquad
F_r(X)=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_r.
\]

For an ordered pair of distinct primes define

\[
J_{pq}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|.
\]

The proved CRT pair estimate is

\[
\boxed{
F_2=\sum_{p\ne q}J_{pq}\ll M\lambda^2.
}
\tag{HM2}
\]

The question is what extra information, strictly weaker than

\[
F_3=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_3\ll M\lambda^3,
\tag{HM3}
\]

is enough to obtain a genuine fractional improvement over `(HM2)`.

## 2. Why the naive fractional moments have the wrong scale

### 2.1 The ordinary moment \(K^{2+\delta}\)

In the sparse regime, a Poisson variable with mean \(\lambda\ll1\) satisfies

\[
\mathbb P(K=1)\sim\lambda.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E K^{2+\delta}\asymp\lambda,
\]

not \(\lambda^{2+\delta}\). Ordinary moments are dominated by singleton hits and do not isolate cross-prime coincidences.

### 2.2 The proposed centered observable

Consider

\[
K(K-1)|K-\lambda|^\delta.
\]

At \(K=2\), this is

\[
2|2-\lambda|^\delta\asymp1.
\]

Since a sparse Poisson variable has \(\mathbb P(K=2)\asymp\lambda^2\), the expectation is already \(\asymp\lambda^2\). It cannot be bounded by \(O(\lambda^{2+\delta})\). The centering must occur **after subtracting the two primes already selected**, not after subtracting the global mean from \(K\).

### 2.3 The Gamma-function falling factorial

The analytic continuation

\[
(K)_{2+\delta}^{\Gamma}
 =\frac{\Gamma(K+1)}{\Gamma(K-1-\delta)}
\]

also has the wrong behavior. At \(K=2\),

\[
(2)_{2+\delta}^{\Gamma}
 =\frac{2}{\Gamma(1-\delta)}\ne0.
\]

Thus double hits again contribute at scale \(\lambda^2\), rather than the desired \(\lambda^{2+\delta}\). Moreover, this analytic continuation has no direct incidence-counting interpretation.

### 2.4 The right residual variable

Once an ordered pair \((p,q)\) is fixed and
\(m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q\), the relevant random variable is

\[
\boxed{
L_{pq}(m)=\sum_{r\ne p,q}\mathbf1_{\Omega_r}(m)=K(m)-2.
}
\]

The integer moments use

\[
(K)_2L_{pq}=(K)_3.
\]

The fractional interpolation must therefore use a fractional power of
\(L_{pq}\), not of \(K-\lambda\).

## 3. The canonical continuous interpolation

Let \((x)_+=\max(x,0)\). For \(0<\delta<1\), define the regularized integrand

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{\delta,\lambda}(K)
  =(K)_2\bigl(\lambda+(K-2)_+\bigr)^\delta.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

There are three useful descriptions of the same object.

### 3.1 Geometric interpolation of the two integer endpoints

For every integer \(K\ge0\),

\[
\Phi_{\delta,\lambda}(K)
 =[(K)_2]^{1-\delta}
  [(K)_3+\lambda(K)_2]^\delta.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\Phi_{0,\lambda}(K)=(K)_2,
\]

while

\[
\Phi_{1,\lambda}(K)=(K)_3+\lambda(K)_2.
\]

After summing over \(m\), the \(\delta=1\) endpoint is equivalent to `(HM3)` because `(HM2)` already controls the additional term \(\lambda F_2\).

The expression suggested in the question,

\[
[(K)_3]^{1-\varepsilon}[(K)_2]^\varepsilon,
\]

is the **unregularized** version with

\[
\delta=1-\varepsilon:
\qquad
[(K)_3]^{1-\varepsilon}[(K)_2]^\varepsilon
 =(K)_2(K-2)_+^\delta.
\]

That is the correct killed fractional coincidence moment, but adding
\(\lambda\) is preferable because it restores a genuinely continuous
\(\delta=0\) endpoint.

### 3.2 Normalized pair-Palm form

Dividing by \(\lambda^\delta\),

\[
\lambda^{-\delta}\Phi_{\delta,\lambda}(K)
 =(K)_2
 \left(1+\frac{(K-2)_+}{\lambda}\right)^\delta.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus `(FHM)` is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_2
 \left(1+\frac{(K(m)-2)_+}{\lambda}\right)^\delta
 \ll_\delta M\lambda^2.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This says: under the measure obtained by choosing an ordered incident pair, the normalized additional multiplicity

\[
1+\frac{K-2}{\lambda}
\]

has a uniformly bounded \(\delta\)-moment.

### 3.3 Killed form

Define

\[
\Psi_\delta(K)=(K)_2(K-2)_+^\delta.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Since \(0<\delta<1\), subadditivity gives

\[
(K-2)_+^\delta
 \le \bigl(\lambda+(K-2)_+\bigr)^\delta
 \le (K-2)_+^\delta+\lambda^\delta.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\delta(K)
 \le \Phi_{\delta,\lambda}(K)
 \le \Psi_\delta(K)+\lambda^\delta(K)_2.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

After summing and using `(HM2)`, the two hypotheses

\[
\sum_m\Phi_{\delta,\lambda}(K(m))\ll M\lambda^{2+\delta}
\]

and

\[
\sum_m\Psi_\delta(K(m))\ll M\lambda^{2+\delta}
\tag{3.7}
\]

are equivalent up to constants.

### 3.4 Poisson sanity check

For \(K\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\) with \(\lambda\to0\), the \(K=2\) atom gives

\[
\mathbb E\Phi_{\delta,\lambda}(K)
 =\lambda^{2+\delta}+O_\delta(\lambda^3).
\]

By contrast,

\[
\mathbb E\Psi_\delta(K)
 =\lambda^3+O_\delta(\lambda^4),
\]

because it vanishes at \(K=2\) and its first contribution is \(K=3\).

Thus the target \(\lambda^{2+\delta}\) is deliberately weaker than full Poisson three-hit behavior whenever \(0<\delta<1\). It really is an intermediate condition, not a disguised form of complete triple independence.

### 3.5 The resulting tail bound

For every integer \(R\ge3\), `(FHM)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\#\{m<M:K(m)\ge R\}
 \le
 \frac{C_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta}}
 {(R)_2(R-2+\lambda)^\delta}
 \ll_\delta
 \frac{M\lambda^{2+\delta}}{R^{2+\delta}}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

This is the precise Markov estimate that a downstream finite-exception argument must use.

## 4. Exact pair-Palm characterization

For any function \(G:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\to\mathbb R\), there is the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_2G(K(m)-2)
 =\sum_{p\ne q}
   \sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}G(L_{pq}(m)).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, a point with \(K(m)=k\) is counted once for each of its
\((k)_2\) ordered incident pairs.

Taking

\[
G(L)=(L+\lambda)^\delta
\]

gives

\[
F_{2+\delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}
 :=\sum_m\Phi_{\delta,\lambda}(K(m))
 =\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
   (L_{pq}(m)+\lambda)^\delta.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Subtract the known pair baseline. Define

\[
\mathcal E_\delta(X)
 =\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
 \left[
  (L_{pq}(m)+\lambda)^\delta-\lambda^\delta
 \right].
\tag{4.3}
\]

Every summand is nonnegative, and

\[
\boxed{
F_{2+\delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}
 =\lambda^\delta F_2+\mathcal E_\delta(X).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since `(HM2)` gives

\[
\lambda^\delta F_2\ll M\lambda^{2+\delta},
\]

we obtain the exact equivalence

\[
\boxed{
(HM)_{2+\delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E_\delta(X)\ll_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta},
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

up to changing the constant by the known `(HM2)` constant.

This is the requested **minimal aggregate cross-prime input**. It is not merely sufficient: after removing the proved pair term, it is exactly the remainder that must be bounded.

In normalized form, `(4.5)` is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\left[
 \left(1+\frac{L_{pq}(m)}{\lambda}\right)^\delta-1
\right]
\ll_\delta M\lambda^2.
}
\tag{FPP\(_\delta\)-norm}
\]

This is the cleanest formulation of the fractional pair-Palm theorem.

### Pair-specific baseline

The natural independent conditional mean for an anchored pair is

\[
\lambda_{pq}
 =\sum_{r\ne p,q}\frac{|Z_r|}{r},
\]

or, on the exact finite interval,

\[
\mu_{pq}=\sum_{r\ne p,q}\frac{|\Omega_r|}{M}.
\]

A pair-adapted version is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_\delta^{\mathrm{loc}}
 =\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
 \left[
  (L_{pq}(m)+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
  -\lambda_{pq}^\delta
 \right]
 \ll_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is equivalent, under `(HM2)`, to the killed fractional moment `(3.7)`. Indeed, subadditivity gives

\[
0\le (L+\lambda_{pq})^\delta-\lambda_{pq}^\delta
 \le L^\delta,
\]

while

\[
L^\delta
 \le (L+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 =\bigl[(L+\lambda_{pq})^\delta-\lambda_{pq}^\delta\bigr]
  +\lambda_{pq}^\delta.
\]

The total baseline obeys

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{pq}\lambda_{pq}^\delta
 \le \lambda^\delta F_2
 \ll M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
\]

Thus `(4.6)` is the most intrinsic pair-Palm formulation.

## 5. Two other exact forms of the minimal input

### 5.1 A weighted triple-intersection count

From `(3.7)` and `(4.1)`,

\[
\sum_m\Psi_\delta(K(m))
 =\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
   L_{pq}(m)^\delta.
\]

For \(L\ge1\), write

\[
L^\delta=L\,L^{\delta-1}
\]

and expand

\[
L_{pq}(m)=\sum_{r\ne p,q}\mathbf1_{\Omega_r}(m).
\]

This gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_m\Psi_\delta(K(m))
 =\sum_{\substack{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}}
   \sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q\cap\Omega_r}
   (K(m)-2)^{\delta-1}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

At \(\delta=1\), the weight is \(1\) and `(5.1)` is the usual third factorial moment. For \(0<\delta<1\), the exponent \(\delta-1<0\), so a row with many primes is discounted.

Therefore another exact statement of the weakest new input is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}}
   \sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q\cap\Omega_r}
   \frac{1}{(K(m)-2)^{1-\delta}}
 \ll_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{WT\(_\delta\)}
\]

This is literally a fractional triple moment: it counts every triple intersection, but discounts a triple lying inside a high-multiplicity atom.

For the regularized excess, define for \(L\ge1\)

\[
w_{\delta,\lambda}(L)
 =\frac{(L+\lambda)^\delta-\lambda^\delta}{L}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal E_\delta(X)
 =\sum_{\substack{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}}
   \sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q\cap\Omega_r}
   w_{\delta,\lambda}(L_{pq}(m)).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The mean-value theorem gives

\[
\delta(L+\lambda)^{\delta-1}
 \le w_{\delta,\lambda}(L)
 \le \delta\lambda^{\delta-1},
\]

and for \(L\ge1\), \(\lambda\le1\), one has

\[
w_{\delta,\lambda}(L)\asymp_\delta L^{\delta-1}.
\]

Thus the exact weights really are small on very heavy atoms, as suggested in the question.

### 5.2 Integrated pair-Palm tail

Define

\[
A_h(X)
 =\sum_{p\ne q}
  \#\{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q:L_{pq}(m)\ge h\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
A_h(X)=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_2
       \mathbf1_{\{K(m)\ge h+2\}}.
\]

Discrete layer-cake gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_\delta(X)
 =\sum_{h\ge1}
  \left[(h+\lambda)^\delta-(h-1+\lambda)^\delta\right]
  A_h(X).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For \(0<\lambda\le1\),

\[
(1+\lambda)^\delta-\lambda^\delta\asymp_\delta1,
\]

and, for \(h\ge2\),

\[
(h+\lambda)^\delta-(h-1+\lambda)^\delta
 \asymp_\delta h^{\delta-1}.
\]

Hence `(FPP_delta)` is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
A_1(X)+\sum_{h\ge2}h^{\delta-1}A_h(X)
 \ll_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This makes the new content transparent.

The first necessary layer is

\[
\boxed{
A_1(X)
 =\sum_m(K(m))_2\mathbf1_{\{K(m)\ge3\}}
 \ll M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

At the natural pair scale \(F_2\asymp M\lambda^2\), this says that only an
\(O(\lambda^\delta)\) fraction of the ordered pair-incidence mass may sit on rows supporting any third prime.

Full Poisson independence predicts the stronger estimate

\[
A_1\asymp M\lambda^3.
\]

For \(0<\delta<1\),

\[
M\lambda^3=o(M\lambda^{2+\delta}),
\]

so the fractional theorem leaves substantial room for non-Poisson behavior.

If one separately proved a uniform bound \(K(m)\le B\), then `(5.6)` alone would be equivalent to `(FPP_delta)` up to a constant depending on \(B\). Without such a bound, the integrated tail in `(5.5)` is necessary.

When empirically \(\lambda\asymp1/\log X\), the first-layer saving is only

\[
\lambda^\delta\asymp(\log X)^{-\delta}
\]

over the pair bound. This logarithmic fractional saving is the precise amount of genuinely new cross-prime information being requested.

## 6. A pair-only weighted excess using \(J_{pq}\) and \(T_{pq}\)

The exact condition depends on the full distribution of \(L_{pq}(m)\) on each pair locus. A stronger but more compressed sufficient condition uses only its mean.

Define

\[
T_{pq}
 =\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}L_{pq}(m)
 =\sum_{r\ne p,q}|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q\cap\Omega_r|,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and, when \(J_{pq}>0\),

\[
a_{pq}=\frac{T_{pq}}{J_{pq}}.
\]

Set \(a_{pq}=0\) if \(J_{pq}=0\).

Because \(x\mapsto x^\delta\) is concave,

\[
\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
 (L_{pq}(m)+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 \le J_{pq}(a_{pq}+\lambda_{pq})^\delta.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore the following is sufficient for `(FPP_delta)`:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{pq}
 \left[
  (a_{pq}+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
  -\lambda_{pq}^\delta
 \right]
 \ll_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{MEAN-FPP\(_\delta\)}
\]

This is stronger than the exact row-level condition because Jensen may lose information, but it involves only the pair-Palm totals \(J_{pq}\) and \(T_{pq}\).

To isolate the actual excess, note that the independent conditional prediction is

\[
a_{pq}\approx\lambda_{pq}.
\]

The baseline at equality is

\[
J_{pq}\left[(2\lambda_{pq})^\delta-\lambda_{pq}^\delta\right],
\]

whose sum is already

\[
O_\delta(\lambda^\delta F_2)
 =O_\delta(M\lambda^{2+\delta}).
\]

It therefore suffices to prove

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{pq}
 \left[
  (a_{pq}+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
  -(2\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 \right]_+
 \ll_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This can be written exactly in the weighted-excess form proposed in the question. For \(a_{pq}>\lambda_{pq}\), set

\[
\omega_{pq,\delta}
 =\frac{
 (a_{pq}+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 -(2\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 }{a_{pq}-\lambda_{pq}},
\tag{6.4}
\]

and set \(\omega_{pq,\delta}=0\) otherwise. Then `(6.3)` is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}
 \omega_{pq,\delta}
 (T_{pq}-J_{pq}\lambda_{pq})_+
 \ll_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The secant-slope bounds are

\[
\boxed{
\delta(a_{pq}+\lambda_{pq})^{\delta-1}
 \le \omega_{pq,\delta}
 \le \delta(2\lambda_{pq})^{\delta-1}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Because \(\delta-1<0\), pairs with a very large Palm mean receive a smaller weight. Near the expected mean the weight is large, of order
\(\lambda_{pq}^{\delta-1}\), but the positive excess itself is then small.

Two distinctions are important:

1. The **exact minimal** weight is row-dependent, as in `(5.2)`.
2. The pair-only weight `(6.4)` is a sufficient compression obtained by Jensen; it is not logically necessary.

Thus the pair-Palm proposal is correct after replacing an unspecified weight by the precise concave secant slope `(6.4)` and retaining only positive excess.

## 7. Relation to \((HM)_3\) and to intermediate triple bounds

### 7.1 Hölder interpolation from the two integer endpoints

From `(3.2)` and Hölder,

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{2+\delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}
&=\sum_m[(K(m))_2]^{1-\delta}
       [(K(m))_3+\lambda(K(m))_2]^\delta\\
&\le F_2^{1-\delta}
      (F_3+\lambda F_2)^\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
(HM)_2+(HM)_3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(HM)_{2+\delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}
\]

for every \(0<\delta<1\).

More generally, if a fractional endpoint \((HM)_{2+\eta}\) is known for some \(\eta>\delta\), then

\[
F_{2+\delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}
 \le F_2^{1-\delta/\eta}
      (F_{2+\eta}^{\mathrm{reg}})^{\delta/\eta}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

So the hierarchy is genuinely continuous **once two endpoints are available**. There is no interpolation theorem from the single endpoint \(\delta=0\).

### 7.2 A simple but stronger intermediate input

Since \(L\) is an integer and \(L^\delta\le L\) for \(L\ge1\),

\[
\sum_m\Psi_\delta(K(m))\le F_3.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus the intermediate triple estimate

\[
\boxed{
F_3\ll M\lambda^{2+\delta}
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

is sufficient for `(FHM)`. It is weaker than full `(HM3)`, because for \(0<\lambda<1\),

\[
\lambda^{2+\delta}>\lambda^3.
\]

However, `(7.4)` is still stronger than the exact weighted condition `(WT_delta)`: it gives weight \(1\) to every triple, whereas `(WT_delta)` discounts triples lying inside high-multiplicity rows.

The hierarchy of sufficient inputs is therefore

\[
(HM)_3
\Longrightarrow
F_3\ll M\lambda^{2+\delta}
\Longrightarrow
(WT_\delta)
\Longleftrightarrow
(FHM),
\]

where neither reverse implication is formal.

## 8. Why no soft argument from \((HM)_2\) can work

There are three independent certificates.

### 8.1 Exact pairwise independence with \(K\le3\)

This is the cleanest probabilistic obstruction.

Fix \(N\ge3\) and \(0<\lambda<1\). Put

\[
\theta=\frac{\lambda}{N}.
\]

Construct Bernoulli variables \(X_1,\ldots,X_N\) as follows.

- With probability
  \[
  \alpha=\frac{\theta^2N(N-1)}6
         =\frac{\lambda^2(N-1)}{6N},
  \]
  choose a uniformly random three-element subset of \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\) and set precisely those three variables equal to \(1\).
- With probability
  \[
  \beta=\lambda-3\alpha
       =\lambda-\frac{\lambda^2(N-1)}{2N},
  \]
  choose a uniformly random singleton and set precisely that variable equal to \(1\).
- With the remaining probability, set all variables equal to \(0\).

For every \(i\),

\[
\mathbb P(X_i=1)=\frac{3\alpha+\beta}{N}
 =\frac\lambda N=\theta.
\]

For distinct \(i,j\),

\[
\mathbb P(X_i=X_j=1)
 =\alpha\frac{6}{N(N-1)}
 =\theta^2.
\]

Thus the variables are **exactly pairwise independent**.

Let

\[
K=\sum_{i=1}^NX_i.
\]

Then \(K\in\{0,1,3\}\), so in particular

\[
\max K=3.
\]

Nevertheless,

\[
\mathbb E(K)_2=6\alpha
 =\lambda^2\frac{N-1}{N}
 \asymp\lambda^2,
\tag{8.1}
\]

while

\[
\mathbb E\Psi_\delta(K)
 =6\alpha
 \asymp\lambda^2,
\tag{8.2}
\]

because the only multiple-hit atom has \(K=3\) and
\((K-2)^\delta=1\).

The fractional target would require

\[
\mathbb E\Psi_\delta(K)\ll\lambda^{2+\delta},
\]

which fails by the factor \(\lambda^{-\delta}\).

After realizing this distribution on a sufficiently large finite sample space and multiplying by \(M\), it gives exactly the same obstruction for finite incidence sets.

Therefore none of the following is enough:

```text
exact pairwise independence;
the optimal second factorial moment;
a uniform bound max K <= 3;
soft interpolation, truncation, or smoothing.
```

The missing statement is precisely that the mass of the \(K=3\) atom is smaller than the pair scale by a factor \(\lambda^\delta\).

### 8.2 The repository's anchored-star obstruction

`problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` constructs an abstract Apéry-compatible anchored star with

\[
L_X\asymp\frac{X}{\log X}
\]

prime sets all passing through one point \(m_0\). It retains the pair CRT scale and a substantial list of the currently banked local constraints.

At the common atom,

\[
K(m_0)=L_X.
\]

With

\[
M=X^2,
\qquad
\lambda\asymp\frac1{\log X},
\]

the contribution to the proved pair moment is

\[
(K(m_0))_2
 \asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2}
 \asymp M\lambda^2,
\]

so `(HM2)` tolerates it. But its fractional contribution is

\[
(K(m_0))_2(K(m_0)-2)^\delta
 \asymp\frac{X^{2+\delta}}{(\log X)^{2+\delta}},
\]

whereas the target is

\[
M\lambda^{2+\delta}
 \asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^{2+\delta}}.
\]

The ratio is \(X^\delta\). Thus the present pairwise and local structural inputs do not formally imply even an arbitrarily small positive fractional moment.

This is not a counterexample to the actual Apéry sets. It is a death certificate for any proof using only the currently abstracted pair/local axioms.

### 8.3 Why truncation fails at the first new atom

Suppose one splits into \(K\le R\) and \(K>R\). On the first part,

\[
\Psi_\delta(K)
 \le (R-2)^\delta(K)_2.
\]

Using `(HM2)` gives only

\[
\sum_{K\le R}\Psi_\delta(K)
 \ll (R-2)^\delta M\lambda^2.
\]

To reach \(M\lambda^{2+\delta}\), one would need

\[
R-2\ll\lambda.
\]

For \(\lambda<1\), this excludes even \(K=3\). Thus the obstruction is not an extreme tail that can be truncated away: it begins at the very first genuinely three-prime atom.

The part \(K>R\) is even less controllable from `(HM2)` alone. Markov gives a count of such points, but no upper bound for their contribution to a higher moment without a separate uniform-integrability or tail hypothesis. Such a hypothesis is simply another formulation of the missing cross-prime anti-clustering.

## 9. The weakest theorem to try to prove first

The exact target should be named and isolated as follows.

### Fractional pair-Palm anti-clustering

For some fixed \(0<\delta<1\), prove uniformly in the relevant dyadic prime windows that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\left[
 (L_{pq}(m)+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 -\lambda_{pq}^\delta
\right]
 \le C_\delta M\lambda_X^{2+\delta}.
}
\tag{FPP\(_\delta\)}
\]

Then

\[
(FPP_\delta)+(HM)_2
\Longrightarrow
(HM)_{2+\delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}.
\]

This theorem requires neither an asymptotic formula for every triple nor individual product independence. It only requires that, after conditioning on an incident pair, the fractional mass of the additional-hit count is small on average over the anchoring pair.

The precise logical chain is

```text
proved pair CRT / (HM)_2
        +
fractional pair-Palm excess (FPP_delta)
        |
        v
regularized fractional factorial moment (HM)_{2+delta}
        |
        v
Markov tail  M lambda^(2+delta) / R^(2+delta)
        |
        v
whatever finite-exception consequence is established in Q209.
```

### More accessible but stronger first target

If the available machinery naturally produces only \(J_{pq}\) and \(T_{pq}\), target `(MEAN-FPP_delta)` or the weighted positive-excess estimate

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}
 \omega_{pq,\delta}
 (T_{pq}-J_{pq}\lambda_{pq})_+
 \ll_\delta M\lambda^{2+\delta},
\]

with the explicit secant weight `(6.4)`.

This is the correct implementation of the weighted pair-Palm idea. It is weaker than unweighted control of the full third moment because

\[
\omega_{pq,\delta}\asymp
(a_{pq}+\lambda_{pq})^{\delta-1}
\]

decays on heavily clustered pairs.

### First-layer target under a bounded-multiplicity theorem

If a separate argument proved

\[
K_X(m)\le B
\]

uniformly, then it would be enough to establish

\[
\boxed{
\sum_m(K(m))_2\mathbf1_{\{K(m)\ge3\}}
 \ll M\lambda^{2+\delta}.
}
\]

The empirical observation \(\max K\le3\) would make this exactly a bound for the total \(K=3\) mass. At present that bounded-multiplicity input is empirical, so the rigorous theorem must retain the weighted tail `(5.5)`.

## 10. Two project-level caveats

### 10.1 The downstream hierarchy is not yet the theorem recorded on public `main`

The pinned public repository currently records an integer high-moment criterion with a fixed \(k>6\) as the sufficient route to the all-\(n\) result. The statement in Q209 that every \((HM)_{2+\delta}\) gives a finite exceptional set and that \((HM)_3\) gives all \(n\) is a newer proposed refinement, not yet a banked theorem in `main`.

The combinatorial equivalences proved above are independent of that issue. For the final implication, one should insert the exact Q209 deterministic reduction and verify that the Markov bound `(3.8)` has the required summability.

### 10.2 Logarithmic savings require quantitative losses to be explicit

If

\[
\lambda_X\asymp\frac1{\log X},
\]

then the entire gain beyond `(HM2)` is only

\[
\lambda_X^\delta=(\log X)^{-\delta}.
\]

Consequently a bound of the form

\[
X^{o(1)}M\lambda_X^{2+\delta}
\]

is not automatically enough: a generic \(X^{o(1)}\) loss can dominate every fixed power of \(\log X\). The fractional theorem should be stated with an absolute \(C_\delta\), or with an explicitly controlled logarithmic loss small enough for the downstream summability argument.

Likewise, the interpretation as a sparse interpolation assumes \(\lambda_X\to0\). That behavior is strongly supported by the computations and the average-zero model, but it does not follow merely from the unconditional pointwise bound \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\).

## 11. Direct answers

### What is the precise \((HM)_{2+\delta}\)?

Use

\[
\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_2
 \bigl(\lambda_X+(K(m)-2)_+\bigr)^\delta
 \ll_\delta M\lambda_X^{2+\delta}.
\]

Equivalently, normalize by \(\lambda_X^\delta\) as in `(FHM-norm)`. Under `(HM2)`, one may remove the regularizer and use

\[
\sum_m(K(m))_2(K(m)-2)_+^\delta
 \ll_\delta M\lambda_X^{2+\delta}.
\]

### What is the weakest cross-prime input?

The exact minimal aggregate input is `(FPP_delta)`:

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\left[
 (L_{pq}(m)+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 -\lambda_{pq}^\delta
\right]
 \ll M\lambda_X^{2+\delta}.
\]

Equivalently, it is the weighted triple count `(WT_delta)` or the integrated pair-Palm tail `(5.5)`.

Its first unavoidable component is the \(\lambda_X^\delta\)-saving

\[
A_1\ll M\lambda_X^{2+\delta}
\]

for pair incidences that admit at least one third hit.

### Can it be expressed using weighted pair-Palm excess?

Yes. A pair-only sufficient form is

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}
 \omega_{pq,\delta}
 (T_{pq}-J_{pq}\lambda_{pq})_+
 \ll M\lambda_X^{2+\delta},
\]

where the correct weight is the concave secant slope

\[
\omega_{pq,\delta}
 =\frac{
 (T_{pq}/J_{pq}+\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 -(2\lambda_{pq})^\delta
 }{T_{pq}/J_{pq}-\lambda_{pq}}
\]

on pairs with \(T_{pq}>J_{pq}\lambda_{pq}\), and zero otherwise.

### Can \((HM)_{2+\delta}\) be proved from \((HM)_2\) by a soft argument?

No. Exact pairwise independence and even \(K\le3\) do not imply it. The explicit construction in Section 8.1 has the optimal second factorial moment but a fractional moment larger than the target by \(\lambda^{-\delta}\). Truncation fails already at \(K=3\).

Any successful proof must therefore insert a genuinely new three-prime statement. The good news is that it may be much weaker than full triple independence: only a fractional \(\lambda^\delta\) suppression of pair-Palm third-hit mass, together with its integrated high-multiplicity tail, is required.

## Final assessment

The continuous hierarchy is real after the correct normalization:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_m(K)_2
 \left(1+\frac{K-2}{\lambda}\right)^\delta
}
\]

interpolates exactly between the second factorial moment and the third factorial moment divided by \(\lambda\).

But there is a sharp phase transition in information: the moment \(\delta=0\) sees only pairs, while every fixed \(\delta>0\) demands that pair incidences supporting a third prime occupy a vanishing \(O(\lambda^\delta)\) fraction of the pair mass. Pairwise CRT, even in its ideal exact-independence form, does not force that vanishing.

Therefore the smallest viable new theorem is:

```text
FRACTIONAL PAIR-PALM ANTI-CLUSTERING:
condition on an ordered Apéry pair hit, and prove a positive fractional
moment bound for the number of additional prime hits, averaged over the
anchoring pair, with defining-prime scale lambda^(2+delta).
```

That theorem is strictly weaker than \((HM)_3\), exactly equivalent to the desired fractional moment once \((HM)_2\) is inserted, and cannot be replaced by a soft probabilistic interpolation.
