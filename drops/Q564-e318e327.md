# Q564 — Strassmann bounds for the Apéry zero set

## Executive verdict

The proposed Strassmann route does **not** give a bounded or bounded-average estimate for \(|Z_p|\) in its present form.

There are three separate obstructions.

1. **There is no continuous, hence no analytic, interpolation of the full sequence \(n\mapsto b_n\) on \(\mathbf Z_p\).** Indeed, \(p^m\to0\) in \(\mathbf Z_p\), whereas the Apéry–Lucas congruence gives
   \[
   b_{p^m}\equiv b_1=5\not\equiv b_0=1\pmod p.
   \]
   Thus the sequence is already discontinuous at zero.

2. The finite Mahler polynomial
   \[
   P_p(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}c_k\binom{s}{k},
   \qquad c_k=\Delta^k b_0,
   \]
   certainly interpolates \(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-2}\), but this is a degree-\(p-2\), prime-dependent polynomial, not a bounded-complexity analytic family. Its Newton polygon is almost completely flat. For every prime \(11\le p\le101\), its Strassmann index is either \(p-2\) or \(p-3\).

3. The Mellin expression does **not** define a one-variable analytic function \(s\mapsto\omega(t)^{-s}\) on \(\mathbf Z_p\). A nontrivial Teichmüller root has prime-to-\(p\) order, so its integer powers cannot extend continuously to the pro-\(p\) group \(\mathbf Z_p\). Iwasawa weight space instead has \(p-1\) separate tame branches. The indices \(j=0,\ldots,p-2\) select one point on each branch, precisely the horizontal branch obstruction already recorded in `proof.tex`.

The computation requested in part (D) is strongly negative for the proposed mechanism:

- 22 primes, \(11\le p\le101\);
- 1,122 Mahler coefficient/prime pairs;
- 1,090 of 1,122 Mahler coefficients are \(p\)-adic units (97.15%);
- 1,093 of 1,122 ordinary power coefficients are units (97.42%);
- no tested Mahler or ordinary coefficient has valuation \(\ge2\);
- for 11 primes the Strassmann index is \(p-2\);
- for the other 11 it is \(p-3\).

So the Newton polygon is not steep. It has only one or two lower segments, but their horizontal length is \(\asymp p\). This also corrects a premise in the question: **having \(O(1)\) Newton-polygon vertices does not imply \(O(1)\) zeros**. Strassmann is controlled by the horizontal index of the last coefficient of maximal norm, not by the number of vertices.

The current project conclusion therefore remains unchanged: M1 is a same-characteristic **crystalline Mellin anti-concentration** problem, not an ordinary one-variable Strassmann/Iwasawa problem.

# (A) What is the Newton polygon of \(F_p(s)\)?

## 2. There is no intrinsic \(F_p\)

There are two different interpolation questions.

### 2.1 Interpolating the entire sequence

Suppose that a continuous function
\[
F_p:\mathbf Z_p\longrightarrow\mathbf Z_p
\]
satisfied \(F_p(n)=b_n\) for all nonnegative integers \(n\). Since \(p^m\to0\), continuity would give
\[
b_{p^m}=F_p(p^m)\longrightarrow F_p(0)=b_0=1.
\]
But the Apéry–Lucas congruence gives
\[
b_{p^m}\equiv b_1b_0^{m}=5\pmod p
\]
for every \(m\ge1\). Hence
\[
v_p(b_{p^m}-1)=0
\]
for all \(m\), a contradiction.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{The full Apéry sequence has no continuous interpolation on }\mathbf Z_p.}
\]

By Mahler's theorem, the infinite finite-difference coefficients of a continuous function must tend to zero \(p\)-adically. The contradiction above shows that the global coefficients \(\Delta^k b_0\) cannot have the required decay.

### 2.2 Interpolating only \(0,\ldots,p-2\)

There is a unique polynomial of degree at most \(p-2\),
\[
P_p(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}c_k\binom{s}{k},
\qquad c_k=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}b_j,
\]
with
\[
P_p(r)=b_r\qquad(0\le r\le p-2).
\]
It is finite, hence analytic. But it is merely a finite interpolation convention. If
\[
V_p(s)=\prod_{r=0}^{p-2}(s-r),
\]
then for every restricted power series \(H(s)\),
\[
P_p(s)+V_p(s)H(s)
\]
has exactly the same prescribed values. Its Newton polygon can be quite different.

Thus the values \(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-2}\) do not determine an intrinsic Newton polygon. The degree-minimal polynomial \(P_p\) is a reasonable object to test, but its degree grows linearly with \(p\).

## 3. Correct Strassmann index

Write the degree-minimal polynomial in ordinary powers:
\[
P_p(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-2}a_{p,n}s^n.
\]
For a restricted power series \(f(s)=\sum a_ns^n\), Strassmann's theorem uses
\[
N(f)=\max\left\{n:v_p(a_n)=\min_m v_p(a_m)\right\}.
\]
It gives at most \(N(f)\) zeros in \(\mathbf Z_p\), counted with multiplicity in the usual strengthened formulation.

Since \(P_p(0)=1\),
\[
\min_n v_p(a_{p,n})=0.
\]
Moreover, because \(k<p\), every \(k!\) is a \(p\)-adic unit, and
\[
\binom{s}{k}=\frac{s(s-1)\cdots(s-k+1)}{k!}
\]
has \(p\)-integral ordinary coefficients. The conversion from the Mahler basis to powers is therefore computable modulo \(p^2\) without denominators divisible by \(p\).

The computation gives
\[
\boxed{
N(P_p)=
\begin{cases}
p-2,&11\text{ tested primes},\\
p-3,&11\text{ tested primes}.
\end{cases}}
\]

More precisely:

- If \(p\in\{11,17,19,41,43,59,67,73,83,89,97\}\), the top ordinary coefficient is a unit. The lower Newton polygon is the horizontal segment
  \[
  (0,0)\longrightarrow(p-2,0),
  \]
  and \(N(P_p)=p-2\).
- If \(p\in\{13,23,29,31,37,47,53,61,71,79,101\}\), the top coefficient has valuation exactly 1 and the coefficient of degree \(p-3\) is a unit. The lower Newton polygon is
  \[
  (0,0)\longrightarrow(p-3,0)\longrightarrow(p-2,1),
  \]
  and \(N(P_p)=p-3\).

This is the exact lower Newton polygon in the tested range. Interior coefficients lying above the horizontal segment do not change it.

### Important correction

A polygon with two vertices can have a horizontal segment of length \(p-2\). Thus
\[
\#\{\text{vertices}\}=O(1)
\]
does **not** imply a bounded Strassmann index. What must be bounded is the relevant horizontal coordinate/Weierstrass degree.

## 8. Structural identity for the top Mahler coefficient

Let
\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j.
\]
For \(0\le j\le p-2\),
\[
\binom{p-2}{j}\equiv(-1)^j(j+1)\pmod p.
\]
Since \(p-2\) is odd,
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{p-2}
&=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}(-1)^{p-2-j}\binom{p-2}{j}b_j\\
&\equiv-\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}(j+1)b_j\\
&\equiv-\bigl(A_p(1)+A_p'(1)\bigr)\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\]
The project proves the reciprocal symmetry
\[
t^{p-1}A_p(1/t)=A_p(t).
\]
Differentiating at \(t=1\) gives
\[
2A_p'(1)=(p-1)A_p(1)\equiv-A_p(1)\pmod p.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{c_{p-2}\equiv-\frac12A_p(1)\pmod p.}
\]

So the final Newton point is detecting ordinarity at the **single evaluation point** \(t=1\) of the Hasse polynomial. It is not detecting the number of vanishing coefficients of \(A_p\).

In the tested range, \(v_p(c_{p-2})=1\) occurs exactly for \(p\equiv5,7\pmod8\), while it is 0 for \(p\equiv1,3\pmod8\). That observed fixed-fiber pattern may merit a separate CM/Hasse calculation, but it does not provide a bound for \(Z_p\).

# (C) Mellin interpolation and Iwasawa theory

## 9. Why \(\omega(t)^{-s}\) is not analytic in \(s\in\mathbf Z_p\)

Let \(\zeta=\omega(t)\in\mu_{p-1}\) be nontrivial. There is no continuous function on \(\mathbf Z_p\) extending \(n\mapsto\zeta^n\).

One proof is group-theoretic: every continuous homomorphism
\[
\mathbf Z_p\longrightarrow\mu_{p-1}
\]
is trivial, since the source is pro-\(p\) and the target has order prime to \(p\).

Concretely, \(p^m\to0\), but
\[
\zeta^{p^m}=\zeta
\]
because \(p^m\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\). This does not tend to \(1=\zeta^0\).

Nor does the usual formula help:
\[
\zeta^s=\exp(s\log\zeta).
\]
The \(p\)-adic logarithm of a Teichmüller root is zero, so this expression is identically 1 and does not interpolate its integer powers.

Consequently the proposed expression
\[
F_p(s)=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\operatorname{Ha}_p(t)\omega(t)^{-s}
\]
is well-defined for integer exponents modulo \(p-1\), but it is **not** a one-variable analytic function on \(\mathbf Z_p\).

## 10. Correct Iwasawa-space picture

A continuous character of \(\mathbf Z_p^\times\) has the form
\[
\chi(x)=\omega(x)^a\langle x\rangle^s,
\qquad a\in\mathbf Z/(p-1),
\]
where the wild variable \(s\) lies on a \(p\)-adic open disc. Thus weight space is a disjoint union of \(p-1\) discs.

The finite Mellin characters used here are
\[
\chi_j=\omega^{-j},
\qquad 0\le j\le p-2.
\]
They are the points
\[
(a,s)=(-j,0),
\]
one point on each of the \(p-1\) components.

Iwasawa/Weierstrass theory can control zeros as \(s\) varies **inside one fixed tame component**. It gives no relation between the values at \(s=0\) on \(p-1\) different components. Even a uniform \(O(1)\) Weierstrass degree on each component would sum to an \(O(p)\) horizontal bound.

This is exactly the branch obstruction already stated in `proof.tex`:

- the vertical tower
  \[
  j,\ j+(p-1),\ j+(p-1)p,\ldots
  \]
  lies in one branch and is where Dwork/Iwasawa congruences can act;
- the set \(j=0,\ldots,p-2\) meets every branch once.

Packaging all branches in the group ring of \(\mu_{p-1}\) does not remove the issue: the coefficient algebra has rank \(p-1\), so its complexity grows linearly with \(p\).

## 11. Same-characteristic problem

There is an additional mismatch. The event of interest is
\[
b_j\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
This is divisibility by the residue characteristic of a crystalline/Hasse coefficient. Complex or \(\ell\)-adic equidistribution of normalized Mellin transforms, with \(\ell\ne p\), does not control this event.

A useful Iwasawa-type theorem would therefore have to be new and two-dimensional in spirit: it would need a uniform relation **across tame branches** together with same-characteristic slope or unit-root control. Ordinary one-branch \(\lambda\)-invariants do not supply it.

# (D) Reproducible computation

## 12. Method

The computation did the following.

1. Generate exact integer Apéry numbers using
   \[
   (n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
   \]
2. Form the exact forward-difference table and record
   \[
   c_k=\Delta^k b_0.
   \]
3. For every prime \(11\le p\le101\), reduce \(c_0,\ldots,c_{p-2}\) modulo \(p^2\) and compute their exact valuations.
4. Convert
   \[
   \sum c_k\binom{s}{k}
   \]
   to the ordinary basis modulo \(p^2\), using falling factorials and the fact that \(k!\) is invertible for \(k<p\).
5. Compute the largest degree carrying a unit ordinary coefficient: the actual Strassmann index.
6. Independently compute \(Z_p\).

## 13. Standalone code

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
from __future__ import annotations


def is_prime(n: int) -> bool:
    if n < 2:
        return False
    if n % 2 == 0:
        return n == 2
    d = 3
    while d * d <= n:
        if n % d == 0:
            return False
        d += 2
    return True


def vp(x: int, p: int) -> int:
    if x == 0:
        return 10**9
    x = abs(x)
    v = 0
    while x % p == 0:
        x //= p
        v += 1
    return v


def apery_numbers(nmax: int) -> list[int]:
    b = [1, 5]
    for n in range(1, nmax):
        Pn = 34*n**3 + 51*n**2 + 27*n + 5
        num = Pn*b[n] - n**3*b[n-1]
        den = (n+1)**3
        assert num % den == 0
        b.append(num // den)
    return b


def mahler_coefficients(values: list[int]) -> list[int]:
    row = values[:]
    ans = []
    while row:
        ans.append(row[0])
        row = [row[i+1] - row[i] for i in range(len(row)-1)]
    return ans


def power_coefficients_mod_p2(cvals: list[int], p: int) -> list[int]:
    """Convert sum c_k binom(s,k) to ordinary powers modulo p^2."""
    q = p*p
    deg = len(cvals)-1
    ans = [0]*(deg+1)
    falling = [1]                 # s^(underline 0)
    fact = 1

    for k, ck in enumerate(cvals):
        if k > 0:
            fact = fact*k % q
        scalar = (ck % q)*pow(fact, -1, q) % q
        for j, coeff in enumerate(falling):
            ans[j] = (ans[j] + scalar*coeff) % q

        # falling <- falling*(s-k)
        nxt = [0]*(len(falling)+1)
        for j, coeff in enumerate(falling):
            nxt[j] = (nxt[j] - k*coeff) % q
            nxt[j+1] = (nxt[j+1] + coeff) % q
        falling = nxt

    return ans


PMAX = 101
primes = [p for p in range(11, PMAX+1) if is_prime(p)]
b = apery_numbers(PMAX)
c_all = mahler_coefficients(b[:PMAX])

for p in primes:
    q = p*p
    c = c_all[:p-1]              # k=0,...,p-2
    ordinary = power_coefficients_mod_p2(c, p)

    c_v = [vp(x, p) for x in c]
    a_v = [0 if x % p else (1 if x % q else 2) for x in ordinary]
    zeros = [r for r in range(p) if b[r] % p == 0]
    strassmann = max(i for i, v in enumerate(a_v) if v == 0)

    print(f"p={p}")
    print("  zeros:", zeros)
    print("  c mod p^2:", [x % q for x in c])
    print("  v_p(c):", c_v)
    print("  ordinary mod p^2:", ordinary)
    print("  ordinary valuations (2 means >=2):", a_v)
    print("  Strassmann index:", strassmann)
```

# 14. Consequence for M1 and the pair-zero reduction

The finite-interpolation Strassmann estimate gives only
\[
|Z_p|\le N(P_p)\in\{p-3,p-2\}
\]
for the tested primes. This is essentially the trivial scale and is much weaker than the project's unconditional \(O(p^{2/3})\) gap-polynomial bound.

The experiment also explains why the small observed \(|Z_p|\) can coexist with a flat Newton polygon. A polynomial over \(\mathbf F_p\) may have degree \(\asymp p\), unit coefficients throughout, and only \(O(1)\) rational roots. Strassmann sees its Weierstrass degree, not the arithmetic rarity of its \(\mathbf F_p\)-roots.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{This Mahler/Newton-polygon route does not establish M1.}}
\]

A viable future \(p\)-adic route would need an additional Apéry-specific normalization that does one of the following:

1. places all relevant tame characters into a bounded number of analytic components;
2. produces a bounded-degree algebraic function of the tame character after Gauss/Jacobi normalization; or
3. proves an average same-characteristic unit-root theorem across the \(p-1\) branches.

The raw Mellin coefficients do not have any of these properties. The linear-complexity and branch obstructions in `proof.tex`, together with the flat coefficient data above, show that the required new input is genuinely stronger than ordinary Strassmann or classical Iwasawa theory.

## Bottom line

- **(A)** There is no canonical Newton polygon for the finite data. For the degree-minimal Mahler interpolant, the lower polygon is horizontal to degree \(p-2\) or \(p-3\).
- **(B)** The Mahler coefficients are almost all units; their valuations do not grow with \(k\).
- **(C)** The Teichmüller exponent is not analytic on \(\mathbf Z_p\); Iwasawa theory works branchwise, while \(Z_p\) samples one point from every tame branch.
- **(D)** The complete calculation through \(p=101\) gives Strassmann index \(p-2\) or \(p-3\) in every case, decisively ruling out the hoped-for steep Newton polygon in this range.
