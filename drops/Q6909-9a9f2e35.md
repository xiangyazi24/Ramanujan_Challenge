# P25 structural reconstruction: exact Lima coupling and a formal no-go for a rational gauge/Bauer–Muir source

## Verdict

There is one short exact identity coupling the printed positive challenge cocycle to the two-step Lima–Guillera partial-sum transition:

\[
\boxed{E_{n+1}=K_n E_n M_n.}
\]

Here `M_n` is exactly `positiveMatrix n` from `Problem25.lean`, `K_n` is a strictly totally positive `2 x 2` rational matrix coming from the nested Lima intervals, and `E_n` is the `2 x 3` matrix of the upper and lower endpoint errors.

This identity is verified below, including the exact initial alignment. However, it is **not** a total-positivity proof of the desired bracket. Two exact obstructions are decisive:

1. the printed `3 x 3` challenge step is not totally nonnegative, even after any fixed row/column permutation or diagonal sign change: one of its `2 x 2` minors changes sign between `n=11` and `n=12`, while another is always negative;
2. the normalized challenge difference module cannot be rationally gauge-equivalent to the two-step Lima partial-sum module, nor be a rank-one triangular extension of its reduced rank-two module. Their formal exponential characters are incompatible: the Lima module contains a pair with ratio `64`, while the challenge characters are
   
   \[
   1,\quad 17+12\sqrt2,\quad 17-12\sqrt2,
   \]
   
   and no pair has ratio `64`.

Consequently there is no direct rational gauge, finite rational Bauer–Muir transform, or positive elementary-matrix factorization of the requested kind. The numerical interlacing is real, but proving it requires an orbit-specific invariant stronger than total positivity and stronger than the existing positive-Grassmannian cone. A larger contiguous hypergeometric module is not ruled out, but it would be a genuinely new module, not an extension or rational transformation of the raw two-step Lima partial-sum transition.

## 1. Exact Lima two-step module

Use the same convention as `lean/p25_lima_bracket.py`:

\[
c_m=\frac{(-1)^m(3m+2)8^m}
 {2(2m+1)^3\binom{2m}{m}^3},
\qquad
A_M=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}c_m.
\]

The term quotient is

\[
\rho_m:=\frac{c_{m+1}}{c_m}
=-\frac{(m+1)^3(3m+5)}{(2m+3)^3(3m+2)}.
\tag{1}
\]

For the bracket observed in the script, define

\[
a_n=A_{2n+4},\qquad b_n=A_{2n+5},\qquad h_n=c_{2n+4}>0.
\]

Thus `b_n=a_n+h_n`. Put

\[
\mu_n=1+\rho_{2n+4},\qquad
R_n=\rho_{2n+4}\rho_{2n+5},\qquad
\lambda_n=\mu_n+R_n.
\tag{2}
\]

Then the two-step partial-sum transition is exactly

\[
a_{n+1}=a_n+\mu_nh_n,\qquad
b_{n+1}=a_n+\lambda_nh_n,\qquad
h_{n+1}=R_nh_n.
\tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, for the row state `z_n=(1,a_n,h_n)`,

\[
\boxed{
z_{n+1}=z_nL_n,\qquad
L_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&\mu_n&R_n
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{4}
\]

The exact factorizations of the relevant rational functions are

\[
\rho_{2n+4}
=-\frac{(2n+5)^3(6n+17)}
 {2(3n+7)(4n+11)^3},
\tag{5}
\]

\[
\rho_{2n+5}
=-\frac{16(n+3)^3(3n+10)}
 {(4n+13)^3(6n+17)},
\tag{6}
\]

\[
\mu_n=
\frac{336n^4+3568n^3+14184n^2+25014n+16509}
 {2(3n+7)(4n+11)^3},
\tag{7}
\]

\[
1-\mu_n=
\frac{(2n+5)^3(6n+17)}
 {2(3n+7)(4n+11)^3},
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
R_n=
\frac{8(n+3)^3(2n+5)^3(3n+10)}
 {(3n+7)(4n+11)^3(4n+13)^3}.
\tag{9}
\]

Furthermore,

\[
1-\lambda_n=
\frac{(2n+5)^3
 (336n^4+4240n^3+20040n^2+42042n+33029)}
 {2(3n+7)(4n+11)^3(4n+13)^3},
\tag{10}
\]

and `lambda_n-mu_n=R_n`. Every displayed factor is positive for `n>=0`, hence

\[
0<\mu_n<\lambda_n<1.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the Lima intervals are strictly nested:

\[
a_n<a_{n+1}<b_{n+1}<b_n.
\]

### Endpoint transition

Let

\[
B_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
-b_n&1\\
-a_n&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For any scalar `r`, `B_n(1,r)^T=(r-b_n,r-a_n)^T`. Equations (3) give

\[
\boxed{B_{n+1}=K_nB_n,\qquad
K_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda_n&1-\lambda_n\\
\mu_n&1-\mu_n
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{12}
\]

All four entries of `K_n` are positive and

\[
\det K_n=\lambda_n-\mu_n=R_n>0.
\tag{13}
\]

Therefore `K_n` is strictly totally positive. This is the genuine total-positivity structure on the Lima side.

## 2. Exact coupling to the challenge cocycle

Let

\[
C_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
q_{n,0}&q_{n,1}&q_{n,2}\\
p_{n,0}&p_{n,1}&p_{n,2}
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where the rows are the positive denominator and numerator rows used by `p25_lima_bracket.py`. By `positiveNumerator_succ` and `positiveDenominator_succ` in `Problem25.lean`,

\[
C_{n+1}=C_nM_n,
\qquad M_n=\operatorname{positiveMatrix}(n).
\tag{14}
\]

Define the endpoint-error matrix

\[
E_n=B_nC_n
=
\begin{pmatrix}
p_n-b_nq_n\\
p_n-a_nq_n
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{15}
\]

Combining (12) and (14) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
E_{n+1}=K_nE_nM_n.}
\tag{16}
\]

This is the shortest exact reconstruction connecting the two systems. No guessed constants or numerical identification occur.

The desired inequalities are two entries of (15):

\[
A_{2n+5}\le r_{n,0}
\iff (E_n)_{0,0}\ge0,

\[
r_{n,2}\le A_{2n+4}

The numerical experiment actually observes the stronger interlacing cell

\[
r_{n,0}>b_n>r_{n,1}>a_n>r_{n,2},

\]

which is equivalent to

\[

## 4. Why total positivity does not propagate the sign cell

The matrix `K_n` is strictly totally positive, but the right factor `M_n` is not totally nonnegative.

For the printed positive challenge matrix, the northwest `2 x 2` minor factors as

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^{12}_{12}(M_n)
={}&(n+2)^3(n+3)^3(2n+3)(2n+5)^2(2n+7)^2\\
&\times(8n^3-44n^2-478n-801).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

The final cubic has values

\[
8(11)^3-44(11)^2-478(11)-801=-735,
\]

\[
8(12)^3-44(12)^2-478(12)-801=951.
\]

Hence this minor changes sign between `n=11` and `n=12`.

A second minor is always negative:

\[
\Delta^{13}_{12}(M_n)
=-10(n+2)^3(n+3)^3(2n+3)(2n+5)^2(2n+7)^2(16n+41).
\tag{25}
\]

Therefore:

- `M_n` is not totally nonnegative;
- no fixed row/column permutation and no fixed diagonal sign change can make all `M_n` totally nonnegative for every `n`, because a fixed monomial gauge only permutes the minors and multiplies each by a fixed sign;
- consequently `M_n` cannot be a product of totally nonnegative Jacobi/elementary matrices with positive diagonal factors.

This is not merely a failure of a convenient factorization. The desired sign cell is not invariant under the abstract action (16), even if one assumes the correct sign pattern and positive maximal minors.

At `n=0`,

\[
K_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{36810273}{40938898}&\frac{4128625}{40938898}\\
\frac{16509}{18634}&\frac{2125}{18634}
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and take

\[
\widetilde E=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-\frac12&-2\\
2&\frac12&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This matrix has exactly the target sign cell (20), and its three maximal minors are

\[
\frac32,\quad3,\quad\frac32,
\]

all positive. Nevertheless exact multiplication gives

\[
K_0\widetilde E M_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\frac{2210333295915}{5848414}&
-\frac{1423123315857}{5848414}&
-\frac{7555046955}{224939}\\
-\frac{974977695}{2662}&
-\frac{628580781}{2662}&
-\frac{43506195}{1331}
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{26}
\]

whose six entries are negative.

Thus neither the sign cell nor positivity of all Plücker coordinates is sufficient. Any successful induction must use additional orbit-specific inequalities involving the actual `E_n`, not total positivity alone.

## 5. Formal-spectrum obstruction to a rational gauge

The raw three-dimensional Lima two-step system (4) has limiting characteristic polynomial

\[
\chi_L(x)=(x-1)^2\left(x-\frac1{64}\right),
\tag{27}
\]

because `R_n -> 1/64`. Its reduced affine rank-two module has characters

\[
1,\quad\frac1{64}.
\tag{28}
\]

The exact Poincaré polynomial recorded in `Problem25.lean` is

\[
c^3+560c^2+8960c+4096
=(c+16)(c^2+544c+256).
\]

After removing the common scalar `-16`, the challenge characters are

\[
\boxed{
1,\quad\Lambda=17+12\sqrt2,\quad\Lambda^{-1}=17-12\sqrt2.}
\tag{29}
\]

A rational gauge over `Q(n)` preserves formal exponential characters; an outer hypergeometric scalar normalization multiplies all characters by one common constant. Therefore a direct rational gauge equivalence would require

\[
\{\alpha,\alpha,\alpha/64\}
=\{1,\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1}\},
\]

which is impossible because the left side has a repeated character and the right side does not.

More generally, a rank-one triangular extension of the reduced Lima module would force two challenge characters to have ratio `64`. The possible nontrivial ratios in (29) are

\[
\Lambda,\qquad \Lambda^2,
\]

and neither equals `64`. This is exact:

\[
64^2-34\cdot64+1=1921\ne0,
\]

so `Lambda != 64`, while

\[
8^2-34\cdot8+1=-207\ne0,
\]

so `Lambda^2 != 64`.

This rules out all of the following constructions when their matrices are rational functions of `n`:

1. a `3 x 3` rational gauge from the full Lima state (4) to the normalized challenge system;
2. a triangular rank-one extension of the reduced two-dimensional Lima partial-sum module;
3. any finite chain of rational gauge changes and rational scalar normalizations;
4. a finite rational Bauer–Muir transformation of the Lima numerator/denominator recurrence, followed by a rank-one extension.

A Bauer–Muir transformation with rational modifying factors is an invertible rational change of the two-dimensional numerator/denominator state, so it cannot remove the ratio-`64` formal character invariant.

## 6. What remains possible, and what would count as a certificate

The obstruction is deliberately scoped.

It does **not** rule out a genuinely larger hypergeometric construction—for example a contiguous module whose rank is at least four and whose challenge quotient has characters (29). But such a construction is not a rational extension of the raw two-step partial-sum transition. It must introduce new exponential characters before taking a quotient.

To count as the requested source reconstruction, such a proposal would need all three of the following exact objects:

1. a concrete rational first-order matrix `H_n` for the larger contiguous module;
2. an explicit rational invariant submodule or quotient with Poincaré characters (29);
3. an exact gauge identity
   
   \[
   G_n^{-1}H_nG_{n+1}=s_nM_n
   \]
   
   together with an initial-vector identity producing (22)–(23).

No such object is present in `Problem25.lean` or `p25_lima_bracket.py`, and the direct module generated by `A_M,c_M` cannot provide it by the formal-spectrum argument above.

Accordingly, the correct conclusion from the current exact data is:

> The Lima partial sums give a strictly totally positive moving interval and satisfy the exact coupling `E_(n+1)=K_n E_n M_n`, but the printed challenge cocycle is not a total-positive realization or rational Bauer–Muir transform of that two-step module. The observed bracket requires a new orbit-specific cone or a genuinely larger contiguous hypergeometric module.
