# 6. Why Deshouillers--Iwaniec / Kuznetsov is not a direct black-box match

The original Deshouillers--Iwaniec technology is a theorem about **averages of complete Kloosterman sums** with smooth or specially structured weights.  Bettin--Chandee explicitly contrast their fraction theorem with that situation: DI is especially efficient when one already has sums

\[
  S(a,b;c)
  =\sum_{x\bmod c}^{*}
   e\!\left(\frac{a\bar x+bx}{c}\right)

and a smooth/special weight in the Kloosterman variables.

The literal PS14 endpoint after actual-zero expansion contains instead a **single Kloosterman fraction**

\[

A modern DI-style theorem can allow a fairly general coefficient `b_{n,r,s}` **once an actual Kloosterman sum and a smooth multi-variable test function are already present**.  That is not the current form.  I therefore do not quote a DI/Kuznetsov exponent as though it applied directly.

# 11. `omega_{p,q}(d)`: PS8 mismatch versus actual source structure

## 11.1 PS8 as stated

Q4806/PS8 deliberately proves a robust completed statement with

\[
  |\omega_{p,q}(d)|\le1
\]

and no assumption of factorization, realness, transpose symmetry, or smooth dependence.

This is **not** a Bettin--Chandee coefficient.  The theorem permits

\[
  \alpha_p\beta_q\nu_d,
\]

not

\[
  \alpha_p\beta_q\nu_d\omega_{p,q}(d)
\]

for arbitrary `omega`.

Likewise DFI permits arbitrary `alpha_p,beta_q` but not an arbitrary joint pair matrix at fixed `d`.

A generic bounded joint matrix can have large nuclear/projective norm, so `|omega|<=1` alone gives no hidden `N^{o(1)}` separable expansion.  Therefore **PS8 cannot be fed as a black box into Bettin--Chandee**.

## 11.2 The literal pre-PS8 analytic multiplier is much smoother

The canonical Farey form before robustification contains

\[
  \widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right)
\]

with the prime shell weights already in `u_p,u_q` and the determinant cutoff in the one-variable `d` weight.

On the present shell

\[
  \left|\frac{Nd}{pq}\right|
  \ll \frac DN
  =N^{-59/100}.
\tag{11.1}
\]

Since `widehat w` is smooth, Taylor to first order gives uniformly

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right)
 ={}&c_0
 +c_1\frac DN
      \left(\frac dD\right)
      \left(\frac Np\right)
      \left(\frac Nq\right)
 +R_{p,q}(d),
 \\
 |R_{p,q}(d)|\ll{}&N^{-118/100}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{11.2}
\]

The first two terms are exactly separable in `(p,q,d)` and have bounded factors on fixed ratio boxes.

For the remainder, absolute zero mass gives for each `d`

\[
  |\Delta B_d|
  \ll B_N^2 N^{-118/100}
  \ll N^{1/50+o(1)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
  \sum_{|d|\asymp D}|\Delta B_d|^2
  \ll N^{41/100+1/25+o(1)}
  =N^{9/20+o(1)}.}
\tag{11.3}
\]

This is negligible.

Hence, after unwinding PS8 back to the actual smooth source multiplier and carrying the already-controlled completion errors, `omega` itself can be made Bettin--Chandee compatible at constant rank.

**Conclusion on `omega`:**

```text
PS8 black-box omega:          genuinely too general for BC/DFI
actual smooth source omega:   separable to constant rank + negligible error
intrinsic final obstruction:  zero-label / cross-characteristic coefficient geometry
```

This distinction is load-bearing.  It is incorrect both to say “BC applies to arbitrary PS8 omega” and to say “the original smooth Farey multiplier itself blocks BC.”

# 12. Four-distinctness does not change the theorem match

The shell square asks for four distinct characteristics across two rows.  This is a predicate on a pair of `(p,q)` rows, not on one coefficient entry.

Q4648 gives the exact constant-cost four-color identity.  Color primes independently with four colors and isolate

```text
first row:   p color 1, q color 2
second row:  p' color 3, q' color 4.
```

Averaging over the coloring recovers the all-four-distinct contribution with factor `4^4=256`.

For each fixed coloring, any Bettin--Chandee/DFI bound on the corresponding first/second row `d`-vectors can be paired by Cauchy.  The color projections preserve all one-variable coefficient norms and cost no power of `N`.

Thus four-distinctness is not the external-theorem mismatch.

# 14. Minimal coefficient-compression lemma that would actually close the shell

To make Bettin--Chandee not merely applicable but sufficient, one needs a projective decomposition of the **whole actual-zero reciprocal tensor** which preserves most of the native Hilbert mass.

For a dual shell vector `nu=(nu_d)` with `||nu||_2=1`, call a BC atom a form

\[
  \sum_{d,p,q}
   \alpha_{j,p}\beta_{j,q}\nu_{j,d}
   e\!\left(
    \vartheta_j d\frac{\bar p}{q}
    +f_j(d;p,q)
   \right),
\]

with `vartheta_j != 0` and the Bettin--Chandee Remark 1 derivative bounds.

Define the projective coefficient norm

\[
  \mathfrak P_D
  :=\inf
  \sum_j
   \|\alpha_j\|_2
   \|\beta_j\|_2
   \|\nu_j\|_2,
\tag{14.1}
\]

where the infimum is over decompositions of the literal completed actual-zero shell, after the negligible endpoint and smooth-twist remainders are removed.

Bettin--Chandee gives

\[
  \|B\|_{\ell^2(d)}
  \ll N^{2187/2000+o(1)}\mathfrak P_D.
\]

The target

\[
  \|B\|_{\ell^2(d)}^2
  \ll N^{159/100-\eta+o(1)}
\]

is therefore implied by

\[
\boxed{
  \mathfrak P_D
  \ll N^{-597/2000-\eta/2+o(1)}.}
\tag{14.2}
\]

Since

\[
  E_N\ll N^{-2/5+o(1)}=N^{-800/2000+o(1)},
\]

it is enough to lose at most

\[
\boxed{
  N^{203/2000-\eta/2+o(1)} }
\tag{14.3}
\]

when converting the native Hilbert coefficient geometry into a BC projective decomposition.

By contrast, the direct scalar actual-zero decomposition has

\[
  \mathfrak P_D
  \ll N^{3/5+o(1)},
\]

which misses (14.2) by the enormous factor

\[
  N^{1797/2000+\eta/2-o(1)}.
\]

This is the narrow theorem-matching target I would carry forward.

A source-native name for it could be

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{BCP}_{203/2000}:}
\]

> the centered actual-Apéry zero tensor admits a fixed-gap Bettin--Chandee atomic decomposition whose projective norm is at most `E_N N^(203/2000-o(1))`.

No current gap-polynomial, first-zero, common-index, affine-frame, periodic fourth-moment, or PS8 completion theorem implies it.

# 15. Why the existing Parseval endpoint and BC do not interpolate automatically

It is tempting to combine

\[
  \sum_d|B_d|^2\ll N^{161/100+o(1)}
\]

with the very strong scalar BC cancellation on each fixed zero-label slice and claim a small interpolation gain.  There is no such automatic interpolation.

The two estimates control different norms of the zero-label tensor:

- fixed-`d` Parseval uses native characteristic Fourier orthogonality and sees `E_N`-type Hilbert mass;
- scalar BC sees a sum of fixed-label rank-one slices and pays the projective mass `(sum_k w_k)^2`.

There is no complex interpolation theorem converting an `E_N` Hilbert estimate and a projective-slice oscillatory estimate into a better estimate for the same operator without a common analytic family or a factorization theorem.  Q4668's exact cross-`q` low-mode saturation is a concrete warning: mean-zero and complete Parseval do not themselves give short-band restriction.

Thus “interpolate Parseval with BC” is not a proof.
