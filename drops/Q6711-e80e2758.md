\tag{9.2}

Comparing valuations in (9.1)–(9.2) proves only

which is weaker than \(V^{2/3}\).  Taking a gcd or radical loses the adjacent-pair multiplicity; taking the product retains it but pays the whole span.  Simple \(\ell_i\)-adic valuation in every factor is fully compatible with all current inputs.

The successful step is different: for each short adjacent gap, (3.9) limits the number of possible **lower-prime pairs** at fixed \((X,d)\), and the exact inverse then permits summation over keys.  There is no pointwise power gain in \(C(K)\).

### 9.2 CRT: injective but not sparse

Let \(x_i(q)\) be the moving zero in characteristic \(\ell_i\).  Its slope in \(q\) is \(6\) or \(7\).  The shell excludes the fixed primes dividing those slopes.  If two colours have the same CRT residue pair, then

\[
6(q-q')\equiv0
\quad\text{or}\quad
7(q-q')\equiv0
\pmod{\ell_1\ell_2}.
\]

Since \(|q-q'|<V<\ell_1\ell_2\), this forces \(q=q'\).  Thus

\[
q\longmapsto(x_1(q)\bmod\ell_1,\ x_2(q)\bmod\ell_2)
\tag{9.3}
\]

is injective.

The failed implication is

```text
CRT injectivity + two zero-set memberships
    => product-density or O(1) intersection.
```

An injective line can pass through \(V^{2/3}\) selected pairs if the two zero sets are correlated along that line.  No current theorem supplies equidistribution of either zero set against this CRT line.  The fact that the q interval is shorter than \(\ell_1\ell_2\) proves uniqueness of the CRT representative, not scarcity of representatives.

The endpoint condition \(q\mid b_J\) also does not repair this.  Height gives only

\[
\#\{q\asymp T:q\mid b_J\}\ll T/\mathcal L,

which is much weaker than the chart cap.

### 9.4 Complementary endpoints

The complementary endpoint map (3.3) is affine and injective once \(J\) is fixed.  It is indispensable for the inverse formulas (4.1)–(4.2).  Each atom has

\[
qP\mid b_J,

qP\mid b_{K(q)}

outside the fixed structural-prime set.

However, the complements cannot simply be relabelled as new bottom-short designated endpoints.  Equation (9.5) shows that they are macroscopically far from \(m\).  The previously proved designated-endpoint theorem operates on the original bottom-short endpoint coordinate and does not automatically transpose to these complements.

Distinct complements also carry distinct primes \(q\); they do not create \(q^2\) in a fixed value.  Endpoint radicals therefore count support but do not pay colour multiplicity.

What the complements **do** provide is the short adjacent gap

\]

which is exactly the successful carrier in Section 3.
