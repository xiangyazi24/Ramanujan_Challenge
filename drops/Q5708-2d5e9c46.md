# Newton residual and the two-dimensional carrier family

## Verdict

Let

\[
Y_d=C_M(d),\qquad
G_{d,L}=\sum_{i=0}^L(-1)^i\binom{d+i}{i}\binom{d+L+1}{L-i}Y_{d+i}.
\]

There are three exact conclusions.

1. **The bound needed for (46.12) is a uniform total-family height bound, not a per-pair bound.** If every possible short-gap pair in the block is assigned a nonzero target-blind carrier \(K_{d,L}\), both endpoint targets force \((d+1)(d+L+1)\mid K_{d,L}\), and
   \[
   \sum_{(d,L)\text{ in the whole candidate family}}
      \log\max(2,|K_{d,L}|)=o_A(H),
   \]
   then the adjacent-collision estimate follows. Merely proving
   \(\log |K_{d,L}|=o(H)\) separately for each realized pair does not suffice.

2. **The four values requested in the question have an exact primitive Pascal relation and an exact Smith form, but they do not eliminate the residual.** Put
   \[
   A=\binom{d+L}{L},\qquad
   C=\binom{d+L}{L-1},\qquad
   B=\binom{d+L+1}{L}=A+C,
   \]
   and \(g=\gcd(A,C)\). The \(4\times3\) evaluation matrix has Smith form
   \[
   \operatorname{diag}(1,g,B).
   \]
   For a prime endpoint pair
   \[
   q=d+1,\qquad \ell=d+L+1=q+L,
   \]
   one has the stronger exact factorization
   \[
   A=qg,\qquad C=Lg,\qquad B=\ell g,
   \]
   and the primitive Pascal relation is
   \[
   \boxed{L G_{d,L}+qG_{d+1,L-1}=\ell G_{d,L-1}.}
   \]
   After all target-unit Smith content is saturated, the last invariant is still \(\ell\). Dividing it removes the right-hand target factor. Thus the four-value construction has rank three, one primitive relation, and one unavoidable endpoint invariant; it does **not** furnish a target-preserving unit pivot for the high difference.

3. **After all formal content is removed, the actual-array residual remains a genuinely distinguished scalar.** With
   \[
   X=G_{d,L-1},\qquad U=\Delta^L Y_d,\qquad V=\Delta^L Y_{d+1},
   \]
   and \(s=(-1)^L\),
   \[
   G_{d,L}=X+sAU,\qquad
   \Delta^{L+1}Y_d=V-U.
   \]
   Therefore the smallest exact normal form of the residual is
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathfrak r_{M;d,L}
   =\gcd\bigl(X+sAU,\,V-U\bigr)
   =\gcd\bigl(G_{d,L},\Delta^{L+1}Y_d\bigr).
   }
   \]
   Primitive radial Möbius packets have coefficient content one, the cyclic-binomial filters contribute no common scalar, and the Euler coefficient recurrence does not close on these three quantities. No further universal factor remains to strip.

Consequently this route does **not yet prove (46.12)**. The missing theorem is an actual-shell, family-level statement: a target-safe divisor of the residual/common core must remove essentially all of the total height over the entire \((d,L)\)-family, while remaining coprime to both endpoint targets.

## 1. The exact uniform-family criterion for (46.12)

Let \(H=n^{1/3}\), let \(I\) be one quotient block, and for fixed \(A>0\) put

\[
\mathcal D_A(n,I)=
\left\{(d,L):
\begin{array}{l}
q=d+1,\ \ell=d+L+1\text{ are primes in }I,\\
1\le L\le A\log n
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

This is the **whole possible pair family**. It is defined before the target subset is known.

### Uniform total-height carrier theorem

Assume that for every \((d,L)\in\mathcal D_A(n,I)\) there is a nonzero integer \(K_{n,I}(d,L)\) such that:

1. \(K_{n,I}(d,L)\) is constructed uniformly from \(n,I,d,L\), not by first selecting the actual target pairs;
2. whenever both endpoint conditions hold,
   \[
   q\mid C_M(d),\qquad \ell\mid C_M(d+L),
   \]
   one has
   \[
   q\ell\mid K_{n,I}(d,L);
   \]
3. uniformly for fixed \(A\),
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sum_{(d,L)\in\mathcal D_A(n,I)}
       \log\max(2,|K_{n,I}(d,L)|)=o_A(H).
   }
   \tag{1.1}
   \]

Then the adjacent-collision estimate (46.12) follows:

\[
C_A(n,I)=o_A\!\left(\frac{H}{\log n}\right).
\]

### Proof

For every actual short adjacent target pair, condition 2 gives

\[
\log |K_{n,I}(d,L)|\ge \log q+\log\ell.
\]

In the quotient sectors used in Section 46, \(a\le H\), hence all candidate primes satisfy \(q,\ell\gg n^{2/3}\). Therefore

\[
\log q+\log\ell\ge \left(\frac43+o(1)\right)\log n.
\]

Summing over the actual adjacent pairs and then enlarging to the whole family gives

\[
\left(\frac43+o(1)\right)C_A(n,I)\log n
\le
\sum_{\text{actual pairs}}\log |K_{n,I}(d,L)|
\le
\sum_{\mathcal D_A(n,I)}\log\max(2,|K_{n,I}(d,L)|).
\]

Now use (1.1).

The constant \(4/3\) is not important; any uniform lower bound
\(\log q\ge c\log n\) gives the same order.

### Why a separate small carrier is insufficient

A statement of the form

\[
\log |K_{d,L}|=o(H)
\quad\text{for every actual pair separately}
\]

allows the sum over \(C_A\) pairs to be as large as \(C_Ao(H)\). That is circular: it gives no upper bound on \(C_A\). Even a uniform estimate

\[
\log |K_{d,L}|=O_A((\log n)^2)
\]

only yields (46.12) if it holds for a **preassigned whole family** and the sum over that family is \(o(H)\). A maximum bound by itself is not the required input.

A slightly more general usable formulation permits finitely many carriers per pair and a bounded-incidence assignment, but the invariant quantity is still the total logarithmic height over all carriers made available before the target set is revealed.

## 2. Closed constant-term, binomial, and hypergeometric formulas

The live normalization in Sections 48--51 is

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)
=\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz},
\]

with

\[
\Lambda^M=\sum_{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb Z^3}c_M(u,v,w)x^uy^vz^w.
\]

An exact one-fold coefficient formula, equivalent to the formula used by the Q5703 scanner, is

\[
\boxed{
 c_M(u,v,w)
 =\sum_{t=0}^M
   \binom Mt\binom M{t+u}
   \binom{M+t}{t+v}
   \binom{M+t}{t+w}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

All binomial coefficients outside their natural range are zero.

### 2.1 Shell formula

Let

\[
a_d=\left\lfloor\frac Md\right\rfloor,
\qquad
D_a(T)=\sum_{j=-a}^aT^j.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
Y_d=C_M(d)
=\operatorname{CT}_{x,y,z}
 \Lambda(x,y,z)^M
 D_{a_d}(x^d)D_{a_d}(y^d)D_{a_d}(z^d).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Define the cyclic-binomial packets

\[
X_{M,d}(t)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb Z}\binom M{t+du},
\qquad
Z_{M,d}(t)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z}\binom{M+t}{t+dv}.
\]

Then (2.1) factorizes exactly to

\[
\boxed{
Y_d=\sum_{t=0}^M\binom Mt X_{M,d}(t)Z_{M,d}(t)^2.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The root filters are

\[
X_{M,d}(t)
=\frac1d\sum_{\zeta^d=1}\zeta^{-t}(1+\zeta)^M,
\]

\[
Z_{M,d}(t)
=\frac1d\sum_{\eta^d=1}\eta^{-t}(1+\eta)^{M+t}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus \(Y_d\) is simultaneously a finite constant term, a one-fold binomial sum of three cyclic packets, and a moment over two \(d\)-th-root filters.

### 2.2 The Newton kernel

Write

\[
\omega_i(d,L)=(-1)^i\binom{d+i}{i}\binom{d+L+1}{L-i}.
\]

Its generating polynomial has the closed beta/hypergeometric form

\[
\boxed{
W_{d,L}(z)
:=\sum_{i=0}^L\omega_i(d,L)z^i
=\binom{d+L+1}{L}(d+1)
  \int_0^1t^d(1-zt)^L\,dt
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

and hence

\[
W_{d,L}(z)
=\binom{d+L+1}{L}
\,{}_2F_1(-L,d+1;d+2;z).
\tag{2.6}
\]

In particular \(W_{d,L}(1)=1\), which is the exact origin coefficient of the primitive carrier.

Combining (2.3) with the weights gives

\[
\boxed{
G_{d,L}
=\sum_{i=0}^L\omega_i(d,L)
  \sum_{t=0}^M\binom Mt
  X_{M,d+i}(t)Z_{M,d+i}(t)^2.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
G_{d,L}
=\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^M
\sum_{i=0}^L\omega_i(d,L)
\prod_{\xi\in\{x,y,z\}}
D_{a_{d+i}}(\xi^{d+i}).
\tag{2.8}
\]

### 2.3 The high difference

The high difference is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta^{L+1}Y_d
=\sum_{i=0}^{L+1}(-1)^{L+1-i}
  \binom{L+1}{i}Y_{d+i}.
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

Its constant-term kernel is obtained from (2.8) by replacing the Newton weights with the coefficients in (2.9). Since their sum is zero, the common origin coefficient \(b_M=c_M(0,0,0)\) cancels exactly.

## 3. Minimal joint recurrence system for \(G\) and the high difference

Newton's forward formula gives

\[
\boxed{
G_{d,L}
=\sum_{j=0}^L(-1)^j\binom{d+j}{j}\Delta^jY_d.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

From this one obtains three exact identities.

### Vertical extension

\[
\boxed{
G_{d,L}-G_{d,L-1}
=(-1)^L\binom{d+L}{L}\Delta^LY_d.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Adjacent-stencil difference

\[
\boxed{
G_{d,L}-G_{d+1,L}
=(-1)^{L+1}\binom{d+L+1}{L}\Delta^{L+1}Y_d.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the coefficient in the question is exactly

\[
\boxed{B_{d,L}=\binom{d+L+1}{L}.}
\]

### Minimal triangular two-dimensional system

Set

\[
H_{d,L}=\Delta^{L+1}Y_d.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
H_{d,L+1}=H_{d+1,L}-H_{d,L},
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
G_{d,L+1}
=G_{d,L}
+(-1)^{L+1}\binom{d+L+1}{L+1}H_{d,L},
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

with initial data

\[
G_{d,0}=Y_d,
\qquad
H_{d,0}=Y_{d+1}-Y_d.
\]

Equations (3.4)--(3.5) are the minimal universal first-order joint system: \(H\) propagates by the Pascal difference equation and \(G\) is a triangular extension of \(H\). Eliminating \(H\) recovers (3.3) and the scalar Pascal identity below.

A lower-order scalar recurrence in \(d\) with coefficients independent of \(M\) would require additional structure of the actual shell sequence. The cyclic filters and Euler coefficient equations considered in Section 7 do not provide such a closure.

## 4. Exact factorization of the adjacent gcd

Let

\[
H=\Delta^{L+1}Y_d,
\qquad
R=\gcd(G_{d,L},H),
\qquad
B=\binom{d+L+1}{L}.
\]

Write

\[
G_{d,L}=Rx,
\qquad
H=Ry,
\qquad
\gcd(x,y)=1.
\]

By (3.3),

\[
G_{d+1,L}=R\bigl(x-(-1)^{L+1}By\bigr).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(G_{d,L},G_{d+1,L})
=R\,\gcd\!\left(\frac{G_{d,L}}R,B\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the exact nuisance factorization requested in the question.

It separates two mechanisms:

* \(R\) is the **intrinsic actual-array residual**;
* the second factor is a **pure endpoint-binomial nuisance**.

For a prime endpoint pair \(q=d+1\), \(\ell=d+L+1\), \(L<q\),

\[
v_q(B)=0,
\qquad
v_\ell(B)=1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence the adjacent gcd can acquire a forced right-endpoint factor through \(B\), even when \(\ell\nmid R\). Dividing the full adjacent gcd therefore loses target information.

The target valuations of \(R\) themselves are exactly

\[
v_q(R)=\min(v_q(G_{d,L}),v_q(H)),
\]

\[
v_\ell(R)=\min(v_\ell(G_{d,L}),v_\ell(H)).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since a target pair forces \(q\ell\mid G_{d,L}\), a target-safe division by \(R\) requires the genuinely new unit assertions

\[
q\nmid H,
\qquad
\ell\nmid H.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Neither assertion follows from interpolation, the packet decomposition, or the coefficient recurrence.

## 5. The four-value Pascal relation and Smith form

Put

\[
X=G_{d,L-1},
\qquad
U=\Delta^LY_d,
\qquad
V=\Delta^LY_{d+1},
\qquad
s=(-1)^L,
\]

and

\[
A=\binom{d+L}{L},
\qquad
C=\binom{d+L}{L-1},
\qquad
B=A+C=\binom{d+L+1}{L}.
\]

Then the four values are exactly

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
G_{d,L-1}\\
G_{d+1,L-1}\\
G_{d,L}\\
G_{d+1,L}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
1&-sC&0\\
1&sA&0\\
1&-sC&sB
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}X\\U\\V\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### 5.1 Primitive Pascal relation

The first three rows give

\[
C G_{d,L}+A G_{d+1,L-1}=B G_{d,L-1}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let

\[
t=\gcd(d+1,L),
\qquad
u=\frac{d+1}{t},
\qquad
v=\frac{L}{t}.
\]

Since \(A:C=(d+1):L=\nu:v\), if \(g=\gcd(A,C)\), then

\[
A=g\nu,
\qquad
C=gv,
\qquad
B=g(\nu+v).
\]

Dividing (5.2) by \(g\) gives the primitive relation

\[
\boxed{
vG_{d,L}+\nu G_{d+1,L-1}
=(\nu+v)G_{d,L-1}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

For distinct odd prime endpoints, \(t=\gcd(q,L)=1\), so

\[
\boxed{
L G_{d,L}+qG_{d+1,L-1}=\ell G_{d,L-1}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

There is no unit coefficient in the large-prime regime: \(L=1\) would be the only possibility, and two distinct odd primes cannot differ by one.

### 5.2 Smith form

The gcd of all entries in the matrix in (5.1) is one. The gcd of all \(2\times2\) minors is \(g=\gcd(A,C)\). The nonzero \(3\times3\) minors are, up to sign,

\[
CB,\qquad AB,\qquad B^2,
\]

whose gcd is \(gB\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{SNF}(5.1)=\operatorname{diag}(1,g,B).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

For prime endpoints,

\[
\boxed{
A=qg,\qquad C=Lg,\qquad B=\ell g,
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
g
=\frac1q\binom{\ell-1}{L}
=\frac1L\binom{\ell-1}{L-1}
=\frac1\ell\binom\ell L.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Thus the Smith form specializes to

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{diag}(1,g,\ell g).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

The factor \(g\) is coprime to both \(q\) and \(\ell\). It is harmless target-unit content. After saturating it, however, the last invariant is still \(\ell\). Full primitive saturation divides by \(\ell\) and deletes the right target.

### 5.3 Exact four-value gcd and the smallest saturated coordinates

Unimodular row operations transform the four values to

\[
X,\qquad gU,\qquad BV,\qquad0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(G_{d,L-1},G_{d+1,L-1},G_{d,L},G_{d+1,L})
=\gcd(X,gU,BV).
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

At a prime pair this is

\[
\gcd(X,gU,\ell gV).
\tag{5.10}
\]

There are two natural primitive cores:

* full algebraic saturation:
  \[
  \boxed{
  \rho_{M;d,L}=\gcd(X,U,V);
  }
  \tag{5.11}
  \]
* target-safe saturation, in which one may remove \(g\) but not \(\ell\):
  \[
  \boxed{
  \rho^{\mathrm{safe}}_{M;d,L}=\gcd(X,U,\ell V).
  }
  \tag{5.12}
  \]

The first is smaller, but extracting \(V\) integrally from the four values divides by \(B=\ell g\). The second is the smallest scalar exposed by target-safe Smith saturation.

Neither scalar determines the original residual

\[
R=\gcd(X+sAU,V-U).
\]

In particular, \(R\) can contain a congruential coincidence between \(X+sAU\) and \(V-U\) that is absent from the common core \(ho\). The four-value package rewrites \(R\) in primitive coordinates but does not eliminate it.

## 7. Three attempted structural eliminations

### 7.1 Primitive radial Möbius packets

Define

\[
P_M(m)=
\sum_{\gcd(|u|,|v|,|w|)=m}c_M(u,v,w),
\qquad m\ge1.
\]

Then

\[
Y_d=b_M+\sum_{k\ge1}P_M(kd),
\tag{7.1}
\]

and finite Möbius inversion gives

\[
\boxed{
P_M(m)=\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)(Y_{km}-b_M).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

In the grid-disjoint range used in Section 49, for example under

\[
d>a(L+1),
\]

the nonorigin packet sets entering \(Y_d,Y_{d+1},\ldots,Y_{d+L+1}\) are disjoint. Therefore the packet-coordinate vector of

\[
\Delta^{L+1}Y_d
\]

has coefficients

\[
(-1)^{L+1-i}\binom{L+1}{i}.
\]

The two endpoint coefficients are \(\pm1\). Hence the primitive packet content is exactly one.

This gives a sharp negative result: no universal radial Möbius factor divides the high difference. Any nontrivial \(R\) comes from the numerical values of the actual packet sums, not from common packet support or formal content.

### 7.2 Cyclic-binomial factorization

The root filters (2.4) separate each shell into \(d\)-th-root packets, but neighboring shells use different conductors \(d,d+1,\ldots,d+L+1\). After the common origin is removed, the disjoint-grid statement again leaves endpoint packet coefficients \(\pm1\). There is therefore no common cyclotomic factor across the high difference.

The exact binomial factorization is valuable computationally, but it does not produce a target-safe scalar divisor of \(R\).

### 7.3 Euler coefficient recurrence

Let \(	heta_j=x_j\partial_{x_j}\). From

\[
\Lambda\theta_j(\Lambda^M)-M(\theta_j\Lambda)\Lambda^M=0
\]

one obtains the exact coefficient recurrence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{supp}\Lambda}
\lambda_\alpha
\bigl(\nu_j-(M+1)\alpha_j\bigr)
 c_M(\nu-\alpha)=0.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

At a shell boundary \(
u_j=ad\), with \(M=ad+r\), \(1\le r<d\), the exposed coefficient for \(\alpha_j=1\) is

\[
ad-(M+1)=-(r+1).
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is a unit modulo \(q=d+1\), but it is not an integer unit unless the excluded divisor case \(r=0\) occurs. More importantly, (7.3) moves from a shell point to \(
u-\alpha\), which is generally on no neighboring \((d+i)\mathbb Z^3\)-grid. Summing over a shell leaves uncancelled boundary faces. Thus the recurrence does not close on

\[
Y_d,\quad \Delta^LY_d,\quad \Delta^LY_{d+1}.
\]

The local modular pivot is real, but it is an off-grid coefficient pivot, not a scalar unit pivot after the high difference.

## 8. Nuisance classification

The exact analysis separates the following phenomena.

1. **Endpoint-binomial nuisance.** The factor \(B=\ell g\) in (4.1) contributes a right target even when \(\ell\nmid R\).
2. **Vertical endpoint nuisance.** The coefficient \(A=qg\) in (3.2) contains the left target.
3. **Target-unit Pascal content.** The common factor \(g\) is coprime to \(q\ell\), but removing it only saturates one lattice direction.
4. **Actual-array residual.** The scalar
   \[
   \mathfrak r_{M;d,L}=\gcd(X+sAU,V-U)
   \]
   has no remaining universal factorization.
5. **Quotient-cell boundary.** If \(a_{d+L+1}\ne a_d\), the constant-term formulas remain exact with their individual \(a_j\), but the simplest common-packet picture crosses a cell wall.
6. **Grid overlap.** For small \(d\le a(L+1)\), primitive radial packet sets can overlap. This is a finite/small-parameter nuisance, not the asymptotic short-gap regime.
7. **Divisor endpoint.** The exact integer Euler pivot \(\pm1\) appears only at \(r=0\), excluded by the interior target hypotheses.
8. **Zero carrier.** If \(G_{d,L}=0\), divisibility is vacuous and logarithmic height arguments must treat the pair separately. No nonvanishing theorem is supplied by the present identities.

## 10. The smallest unresolved theorem

The formal algebra is now exhausted. In primitive coordinates,

\[
R
=\gcd\left(

\right).
\tag{10.1}
\]

For a prime endpoint pair this is

\[
\boxed{
R

V-U
\right),
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

with \(g\) a \(q\ell\)-unit and with no universal packet content.

A theorem strong enough to close the route would have to produce, uniformly over the whole family, divisors \(S_{d,L}\mid G_{d,L}\) such that on every target pair

\[
\gcd(S_{d,L},q\ell)=1
\tag{10.3}
\]

\tag{10.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{The four-value Pascal/Smith package does not eliminate }R.

It removes the explicit target-unit content \(g\), exposes the unavoidable endpoint invariant \(\ell\), and reduces the remaining problem to the distinguished scalar (10.2) together with the uniform total-height requirement (10.4).
