# Exact reduced denominators of Apéry's \(\zeta(3)\) companion

## Verdict

Let

\[
D_n:=d_n^3,\qquad a_n=\frac{A_n}{C_n},\qquad (A_n,C_n)=1,
\]

and put

\[
U_n:=D_n a_n\in\mathbf Z.
\]

Then there is an exact identity, valid without any coprimality assumption involving \(b_n\):

\[
\boxed{
C_n=\frac{D_n}{\gcd(D_n,U_n)},
\qquad
\frac{D_n}{C_n}=\gcd(D_n,U_n).
}
\]

Consequently, if \(Q_n=D_nb_n\) and \(G_n=\gcd(U_n,Q_n)\), then

\[
\boxed{
G_n=\frac{D_n}{C_n}\,\gcd(A_n,b_n).
}
\]

Thus the experimentally observed “denominator-clearing part” is not merely heuristic: it is exactly \(D_n/C_n\), and equality \(G_n=D_n/C_n\) holds exactly when \(\gcd(A_n,b_n)=1\).

For every prime \(p\le n\), write

\[
e_p(n):=v_p(d_n)=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor.
\]

The exact valuation formula is

\[
\boxed{
v_p(C_n)=3e_p(n)-\min\{3e_p(n),v_p(U_n)\},
}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
v_p\!\left(\frac{D_n}{C_n}\right)
=\min\{3e_p(n),v_p(U_n)\}.}
\]

This is the complete valuationwise answer.  An explicit finite harmonic-sum formula for \(U_n\) is given below.  In general there is no known cancellation-free closed criterion for \(v_p(U_n)\); the valuation is genuinely the valuation of a structured sum.

For the large-prime range \(p>\sqrt n\), however, the current repository's audited block congruence gives a sharp first-digit criterion.  Write

\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 1\le q<p,\quad 0\le r<p.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{p^3a_{qp+r}\equiv a_qb_r\pmod p.}
\]

Because \(p>q\), the denominator of \(a_q\) is a \(p\)-adic unit.  Since \(v_p(D_n)=3\), it follows that

\[
\boxed{
p\mid \frac{D_n}{C_n}
\iff
p\mid a_qb_r
\quad (p>\sqrt n).}
\]

There are therefore two exact large-prime defect channels:

1. the companion channel \(p\mid a_q\);
2. the moving Apéry-zero channel \(p\mid b_r\).

In the top-half range \(n/2<p\le n\), one has \(q=1\), \(a_1=6\), and hence, for \(p\ge7\),

\[
\boxed{
p\mid \frac{D_n}{C_n}
\iff p\mid b_{n-p}.}
\]

Using the Apéry Lucas congruence

\[
b_{p+r}\equiv b_1b_r=5b_r\pmod p,
\]

this is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
p\mid \frac{D_n}{C_n}
\iff p\mid b_n
\qquad (n/2<p\le n,\ p\ge7).}
\]

This answers Task 3 exactly in the asymptotically decisive range: the top-half denominator defects are precisely the top-half prime divisors of the single Apéry number \(b_n\).

The requested subexponential estimate is presently open.  In fact,

\[
\prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}p
\mid \frac{D_n}{C_n}
\]

up to the fixed primes \(2,3,5\).  Therefore

\[
\log\frac{D_n}{C_n}=o(n)
\]

would prove the unresolved top-half radical estimate

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)=o(n).
\]

An \(O(\sqrt n\,\mathrm{polylog}\,n)\) bound would be much stronger still.  No argument in the current literature or repository proves either statement pointwise for every \(n\).

The strongest honest conclusion is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{d_n^3}{C_n}=o(n)
\text{ is open and already contains the main q=1 obstruction of P3.2.}
}
\]

## 1. The explicit companion formula

Put

\[
B_{n,k}:=\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
H_n^{(3)}:=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1{j^3}.
\]

The classical Apéry companion with \(a_0=0,a_1=6\) is

\[
\boxed{
a_n=
\sum_{k=0}^n B_{n,k}
\left(
H_n^{(3)}+
\sum_{m=1}^k
\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}
{2m^3\binom nm\binom{n+m}{m}}
\right).}
\]

For \(n=1\), the \(k=0\) term is \(1\), while the \(k=1\) term is

\[
4\left(1+\frac14\right)=5,
\]

so \(a_1=6\), as required.

Swapping the finite sums gives the form

\[
a_n=b_nH_n^{(3)}+
\frac12\sum_{m=1}^n
\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m^3\binom nm\binom{n+m}{m}}
\sum_{k=m}^n B_{n,k}.
\]

Hence the exact integer whose valuation controls the reduced denominator is

\[
\boxed{
U_n=D_nb_nH_n^{(3)}+
\frac{D_n}{2}\sum_{m=1}^n
\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m^3\binom nm\binom{n+m}{m}}
\sum_{k=m}^n B_{n,k}.}
\]

The standard Apéry integrality theorem states that \(U_n\in\mathbf Z\).  The formula above is useful for exact valuation computation, but it does not turn \(v_p(U_n)\) into the minimum of the term valuations: different \(m\)-terms can cancel modulo \(p\), and such cancellation is exactly what occurs in the moving-zero problem.

Thus a proof-grade valuation formula is

\[
v_p(C_n)=
\max\{0,-v_p(a_n)\}
=3e_p(n)-\min\{3e_p(n),v_p(U_n)\},
\]

with \(U_n\) given by the displayed finite sum.

## 2. Proof of the exact denominator identities

Let \(D=D_n\), \(a_n=A/C\) in lowest terms, and assume \(Da_n\in\mathbf Z\).  Since \((A,C)=1\), integrality of \(DA/C\) implies \(C\mid D\).  Write

\[
D=CE.
\]

Then

\[
U_n=Da_n=EA.
\]

Because \((A,C)=1\),

\[
\gcd(D,U_n)=\gcd(CE,EA)=E\gcd(C,A)=E.
\]

Therefore

\[
E=\frac DC=\gcd(D,U_n),
\]

which proves

\[
C_n=\frac{D_n}{\gcd(D_n,U_n)}.
\]

For the full Apéry gcd,

\[
Q_n=D_nb_n=CEb_n,
\]

so

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_n
&=\gcd(EA,CEb_n)\\
&=E\gcd(A,Cb_n)\\
&=E\gcd(A,b_n),
\end{aligned}
\]

because \((A,C)=1\).  Hence

\[
G_n=\frac{D_n}{C_n}\gcd(A_n,b_n).
\]

This identity is exact at every prime and every index.

## 3. Prime-by-prime defect criterion

Define the denominator defect exponent

\[
\delta_p(n):=v_p\!\left(\frac{D_n}{C_n}\right).
\]

Since \(v_p(D_n)=3e_p(n)\), the preceding identity gives

\[
\boxed{
\delta_p(n)=\min\{3e_p(n),v_p(U_n)\}.}
\]

Therefore:

- \(p\) creates no defect iff \(v_p(U_n)=0\);
- \(p\) creates a defect iff \(U_n\equiv0\pmod p\);
- the exact defect multiplicity is the truncated valuation of \(U_n\).

This is valid for every \(p\le n\).

For small primes the harmonic formula is currently the honest exact criterion.  There is no known theorem reducing all such defects to divisibility of one Apéry number.

For \(p>\sqrt n\), the block formula gives a much cleaner support theorem.  Since \(e_p(n)=1\),

\[
\delta_p(n)=\min\{3,v_p(p^3a_n)\}.
\]

Writing \(n=qp+r\), the audited congruence

\[
p^3a_n\equiv a_qb_r\pmod p
\]

implies

\[
\delta_p(n)\ge1
\iff p\mid a_qb_r.
\]

This is a support statement, not a complete higher-valuation formula.  Determining whether \(\delta_p(n)=1,2,3\) requires the next \(p\)-adic digits of \(p^3a_n\).

### Top half

If \(n/2<p\le n\), then \(q=1\), and for \(p\ge7\), \(a_1=6\) is a unit.  Thus

\[
\delta_p(n)\ge1
\iff p\mid b_{n-p}.
\]

Lucas gives

\[
b_n=b_{p+(n-p)}\equiv b_1b_{n-p}=5b_{n-p}\pmod p,
\]

so

\[
\delta_p(n)\ge1
\iff p\mid b_n.
\]

Hence the top-half defect support is exactly

\[
\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n\},
\]

apart from the finite primes dividing \(30\).

### Middle large primes

For \(\sqrt n<p\le n/2\), one has \(q\ge2\), and

\[
\delta_p(n)\ge1
\iff
\bigl(p\mid a_q\bigr)
\ 	ext{or}\
\bigl(p\mid b_r\bigr).
\]

The first is the companion channel and the second is the moving lower-digit Apéry channel.  The repository's current pointwise analysis controls the companion channel by height and the short interval attached to fixed \(q\); the lower channel is the surviving obstruction.

## 4. What can actually be bounded unconditionally

The trivial exact divisibility

\[
\frac{D_n}{C_n}\mid D_n
\]

gives

\[
\log\frac{D_n}{C_n}
\le 3\log d_n
=3\psi(n)
=3n+o(n).
\]

This is only a linear bound.

The small-prime part is harmless.  Indeed,

\[
\sum_{p\le\sqrt n}3e_p(n)\log p
=O(\sqrt n)
\]

using the prime number theorem, or \(O(\sqrt n\log n)\) elementarily.  Therefore all possible linear growth comes from primes \(p>\sqrt n\).

For those primes, let \(n=q_pp+r_p\) and define

\[
L_b(n):=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_{r_p}}}\log p,
\qquad
L_a(n):=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid a_{q_p}}}\log p.
\]

Since every large-prime defect exponent is at most three,

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{D_n}{C_n}
\le O(\sqrt n)+3L_a(n)+3L_b(n).}
\]

The current P3.2 work controls \(L_a(n)\) sublinearly; the unresolved term is \(L_b(n)\).  In particular the q=1 part of \(L_b(n)\) is

\[
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}\log p.
\]

Thus no proof of \(o(n)\) for the denominator defect is presently available independently of the main moving-zero problem.

An \(O(\sqrt n\,\mathrm{polylog}\,n)\) estimate would imply the same bound for the top-half radical of \(b_n\), which is far stronger than the currently open \(o(n)\) target.

## 5. Why the Casoratian does not force large reduced denominators

The exact adjacent Casoratian is

\[
\boxed{
a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.}
\]

This proves that the two rational solutions are linearly independent and gives strong restrictions on simultaneous divisibility after denominator clearing.  It does not give a lower bound on \(C_n\).

Indeed, after writing \(a_j=A_j/C_j\), the identity becomes

\[
A_nb_{n-1}C_{n-1}-A_{n-1}b_nC_n
=\frac{6C_nC_{n-1}}{n^3}.
\]

A prime removed from \(C_n\) can be absorbed by the numerator term involving \(b_n\), and the equation only relates two consecutive indices.  In the top-half range the block congruence shows explicitly what happens: whenever \(p\mid b_{n-p}\), the same prime becomes a denominator defect of \(a_n\).  The Casoratian is compatible with this; it does not exclude it.

Consequently neither the recurrence nor the adjacent determinant yields a pointwise lower bound

\[
C_n\ge d_n^3e^{-o(n)}.
\]

Any such theorem would already solve the top-half radical problem.

## 6. Literature status

The classical Apéry and Beukers arguments prove the integrality

\[
d_n^3a_n\in\mathbf Z
\]

and the exponential quality of \(a_n/b_n\) as an approximation to \(\zeta(3)\).  They do not prove that the reduced denominator \(C_n\) is close to \(d_n^3\), nor do they prove a subexponential upper bound for \(d_n^3/C_n\).

The exact companion harmonic formula is classical and is routinely used to establish \(d_n^3a_n\in\mathbf Z\).  What is not present in the standard irrationality proofs is a cancellation theorem controlling

\[
\gcd(d_n^3,d_n^3a_n).
\]

No attributable theorem currently known in the project literature implies

\[
\log(d_n^3/C_n)=o(n)
\]

for every \(n\).  This agrees with the repository's prior searches of van der Poorten, Beukers, Zudilin, and related Padé treatments: they provide integrality and approximation estimates, not a pointwise reduced-denominator theorem.

## 7. Concrete obstruction

The obstruction is not merely that the harmonic sum is difficult to estimate.  It is structurally identical to the remaining q=1 P3.2 problem.

For every prime \(p\) with

\[
\frac n2<p\le n,
\]

one has

\[
p\mid \frac{d_n^3}{C_n}
\iff p\mid b_n
\]

apart from \(2,3,5\).  Therefore a fixed \(n\) may exhibit a “rainbow” collection of isolated primes, each arising from one different residue \(n-p\).  Same-prime zero-spacing, continuant, or resultant arguments do not couple these different primes.

The existing example \(n=321\) has the three top-half targets

\[
p=179,193,211.
\]

Their residues are

\[
142,128,110,
\]

and all three primes divide the denominator defect \(d_{321}^3/C_{321}\).  This is not a counterexample to an \(o(n)\) theorem, but it is a concrete demonstration that denominator defects include the same multiple-prime pile-ups already seen in P3.2.

## 8. Strongest theorem that follows now

The proof-grade unconditional statement is:

### Theorem

For every \(n\ge1\), let \(a_n=A_n/C_n\) in lowest terms and \(D_n=d_n^3\).  Then

\[
\frac{D_n}{C_n}=\gcd(D_n,D_na_n),
\]

and for every prime \(p\le n\),

\[
v_p(C_n)=3\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor-
\min\!\left(3\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor,
 v_p(D_na_n)\right).
\]

Moreover, if \(p>\sqrt n\) and \(n=qp+r\), then

\[
p\mid \frac{D_n}{C_n}
\iff p\mid a_qb_r.
\]

In particular, for \(n/2<p\le n\) and \(p\ge7\),

\[
p\mid \frac{D_n}{C_n}
\iff p\mid b_{n-p}
\iff p\mid b_n.
\]

Finally,

\[
\log\frac{D_n}{C_n}
\le O(\sqrt n)+3L_a(n)+3L_b(n),
\]

where the companion part \(L_a(n)\) is sublinearly controlled by the current repository arguments and the moving Apéry part \(L_b(n)\) is the remaining pointwise obstruction.

### Consequence

Neither

\[
\log(d_n^3/C_n)=o(n)
\]

nor the stronger

\[
\log(d_n^3/C_n)=O(\sqrt n\,\mathrm{polylog}\,n)
\]

is presently proved.  Any proof of the former would settle the top-half radical estimate for \(b_n\); any proof of the latter would settle it with a much stronger quantitative bound.

That is the exact current status: the denominator-defect problem is not an easier denominator bookkeeping problem.  It is another exact formulation of the main cross-prime arithmetic obstruction.
