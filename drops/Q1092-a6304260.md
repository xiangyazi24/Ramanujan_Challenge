# Q1092: Smith/Fitting audit of the fixed-q Padé carriers versus Newton determinantal carriers

## Conclusion

The fixed-q Padé gcds `Gplus` and `Gminus` are not, in general, choice-independent determinantal divisors of the interpolation spaces used to define the pairs `(P_a,Q_a)`. They become canonical only after replacing the individual primitive Padé pairs by the Fitting ideal of the full evaluation module. That canonical ideal is related to the Newton minors, but there is no exact transport of the form `Gplus | D_N` or `D_N | Gplus` at a single affine index `N(n,q)` without an additional normalization factor.

The obstruction is already visible at the level of maximal minors.

## 1. Padé interpolation matrix

Fix `H` and `a`. Write

\[
Q(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{H-a}q_i s^i,
\qquad
P(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{a}p_i s^i .
\]

The equations

\[
P(s)-b_sQ(s)=0,\qquad 0\le s\le H
\]

form the integer matrix

\[
\mathcal P_a=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&\cdots&-b_0&-b_0 0&\cdots\\
1&1&\cdots&-b_1&-b_1&\cdots\\
\vdots&&&\vdots\\
1&H&\cdots&-b_H&-b_HH&\cdots
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The primitive solution is a generator of the one-dimensional integer kernel. Its value at `n` is

\[
P_a(n)=\ell_n(v_a),
\]

where `v_a` is the kernel generator and `\ell_n` is evaluation at `n` on the numerator block.

Therefore the ideal generated by all possible Padé values is not the gcd of one chosen kernel vector. It is the Fitting ideal

\[
I_n(a)=\operatorname{Fitt}_0(\operatorname{coker}(\mathcal P_a,n)).
\]

The gcd of all `P_a(n)` over choices of primitive kernels equals this Fitting ideal only after all kernel choices are included.

## 2. Newton matrix comparison

The Newton matrix is

\[
\mathcal N_N=
\left( L_j(N)(b_N-b_j)\right)_{0\le j<M},
\]

with

\[
L_j(N)=(-1)^{M-1-j}\binom Nj\binom{N-j-1}{M-1-j}.
\]

The last determinantal divisor is

\[
D_N=\gcd_j L_j(N)(b_N-b_j).
\]

The parallel one-block content is

\[
\Gamma_N=\gcd_j L_j(N)b_j.
\]

These are genuine determinantal divisors because the Newton basis triangularizes the evaluation map. In Smith normal form language they are the first nonzero invariant factors of the corresponding one-row presentation.

## 3. Exact ideal containment direction

The Padé module maps into the Newton module by restriction of the same evaluation functional. Hence there is a natural homomorphism

\[
\operatorname{coker}(\mathcal N_N)\longrightarrow \operatorname{coker}(\mathcal P_a).
\]

Functoriality of Fitting ideals gives the direction

\[
\operatorname{Fitt}(\operatorname{coker}(\mathcal P_a))
\supseteq
\operatorname{Fitt}(\operatorname{coker}(\mathcal N_N)) .
\]

Thus the canonical Padé ideal contains the Newton determinantal ideal. The reverse containment would require the Padé constraints to generate the complete Newton evaluation lattice, which fails because the denominator block introduces extra lattice directions.

## 4. Explicit minor obstruction

Take the smallest nontrivial case `H=2`. The Padé matrix for `a=1` has columns

\[
(1,s,-b_s,-sb_s),\qquad s=0,1,2.
\]

A maximal minor involving the numerator columns and two denominator columns contains terms proportional to

\[
b_0b_1-b_0b_2-b_1b_2,
\]

whereas the Newton minor is generated by differences

\[
L_j(N)(b_N-b_j).
\]

The former is not in the principal ideal generated by the latter for arbitrary integer data `b_s`. Therefore equality of determinant ideals cannot hold formally. The Apéry sequence may satisfy extra divisibilities, but they are arithmetic input rather than consequences of the interpolation construction.

## 5. Consequence for fixed-q transport

The correct possible bridge is therefore not

\[
G^\pm_q(n)=D_{N(n,q)}
\]

but rather

\[
D_{N(n,q)}\mid I_q(n)\mid G^\pm_q(n),
\]

where `I_q(n)` is the Fitting ideal of the full fixed-q Padé family.

To obtain a height bound one must bound the index

\[
[I_q(n):D_{N(n,q)}]
\]

or equivalently the saturation defect between the Padé lattice and Newton lattice. The present determinantal calculation shows this defect is real and cannot be removed by simply declaring `Gplus,Gminus` to be Newton contents.

## 6. Scoped obstruction result

The dependency-map edge is therefore:

- valid: Newton determinantal divisors provide a lower ideal bound for the canonical Padé Fitting ideal;
- invalid: individual primitive Padé values are choice-independent determinantal divisors;
- open: bounding the saturation quotient by subexponential height.

A successful q-transfer theorem must introduce this saturation quotient explicitly. The q=1 `D_N/Gamma_N` mechanism does not automatically propagate to all fixed q by an affine index substitution alone.
