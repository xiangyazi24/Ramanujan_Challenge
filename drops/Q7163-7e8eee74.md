# Q7163: weak-DPLS central attack — off-diagonal chain pairs

## Executive conclusion

The separated off-diagonal case cannot be eliminated to bounded-gap equations using only the two short chains. Reflection, Wronskian identities, transfer composition, and the addition law all reduce the long return to a product involving an uncontrolled transfer segment of length `L`; they do not remove that segment. There is an exact algebraic obstruction: two equal projective states impose only a rank-one condition on the monodromy, while a second short return condition at a distant location requires information about the intervening matrix in `PGL_2(F_p)`. The intervening matrix ranges over a large family even after fixing both endpoint short-chain constraints.

Therefore the proposed weak-DPLS variance target cannot be proved by a pure two-short-chain elimination. A successful proof needs an additional averaged monodromy/energy input or a different counting parameter.

## 1. Variance expansion

Write

\[
V_p=\sum_q(C_p(q)-M_p/(p+1))^2.
\]

The diagonal contribution is the number of chains. The off-diagonal contribution counts pairs

\[
(A,B),\qquad A\neq B,
\]

of carrier-clean primitive short four-return chains with the same projective state.

Let a chain be encoded by a start `x` and gaps `(a,b,c)` with total span

\[
s=a+b+c\le H=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor.
\]

The four-return condition is a collection of continuant zeros

\[
N_a(x)=N_b(x+a)=N_c(x+a+b)=0
\]

(up to the exact normalization used by the verifier). The question is whether two such systems at starts `x,y` imply enough extra equations when `L=y-x` is large.

## 2. Case split

### 2.1 Overlap case

If

\[
|x-y|\le O(H),
\]

then the two chains share a bounded interval. Primitive/carrier-clean conditions are useful here. The union of two chains has only `O(H)` possible local gap patterns.

This case is compatible with the desired estimate. The number of local configurations is polynomial in `H`, and one can attack it by exact continuant gcd/resultant arguments.

### 2.2 Reflection case

The Apéry reflection law gives relations between indices `r` and `p-1-r`. This controls pairs linked by the involution. However, reflection is an involution on the whole recurrence orbit; it does not identify arbitrary separated starts.

A reflected chain at `x` determines a chain near `p-1-x`, but a generic second chain at distance `L` is not reflected. The exceptional reflected contribution is therefore a separate structured family, not a mechanism for eliminating all separated pairs.

### 2.3 Separated case

Assume

\[
H\ll L\ll p-H.
\]

Let `T_n(x)` be the transfer matrix carrying the recurrence state from `x` to `x+n`. The equality of projective states is

\[
T_L(x)v_x \sim v_y .
\]

Equivalently, one scalar determinant vanishes:

\[
\det(v_y,T_L(x)v_x)=0.
\]

This is only one equation on the long transfer matrix.

## 3. Why the addition law does not eliminate the long return

The identity

\[
N_{x+1+h}(-1)=N_{x+2}(-1)N_h(x)
 -(x+1)^6N_{x+1}(-1)N_{h-1}(x+1)
\]

is a composition identity. It splits a long continuant into two pieces, but one piece still has length `h`.

Suppose a short chain forces a zero near `x`. Substitution may kill one term, giving something like

\[
N_{x+1+h}(-1)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_h(x)=0
\]

under a nonvanishing coefficient. But `h` is essentially `L`, the separation. This replaces the original long return by another long return; it does not create bounded gaps.

The same phenomenon occurs with transfer matrices:

\[
T_{L+s}(x)=T_s(y)T_L(x).
\]

The right factor `T_L(x)` remains. Knowing two short endpoint relations only constrains its action on one projective vector.

## 4. Exact algebraic obstruction

Fix two short chains around `x` and `y`. Their constraints determine

\[
v_x\in P^1(F_p),\qquad v_y\in P^1(F_p).
\]

The separated compatibility condition is

\[
T_L(x)v_x=v_y.
\]

Choose any matrix

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_2(F_p)
\]

with

\[
M v_x=v_y.
\]

There are `p-1` free choices for such matrices. Hence the endpoint projective condition leaves a positive-dimensional family of possible long monodromies.

The recurrence transfer matrices are not all of `SL_2(F_p)`, but the argument shows the required missing fact: short-chain equations do not algebraically determine the long segment. Any claimed elimination would need an additional theorem controlling the image of long transfer products.

In particular, a putative bounded-gap polynomial relation

\[
F(a,b,c,a',b',c')=0
\]

for every separated pair would imply that all admissible long monodromies mapping one endpoint state to the other lie in a bounded algebraic subset. This contradicts the transfer freedom unless one proves a new rigidity theorem about the Apéry monodromy group.

## 5. Wronskian viewpoint

The discrete Wronskian is preserved (up to the known normalization factor). It detects linear dependence of two solutions. A projective equality gives

\[
W(v_y,T_Lv_x)=0.
\]

But this is exactly the same single determinant condition. The Wronskian cannot recover the missing two-dimensional transfer data.

Thus Wronskian arguments and addition formulas are equivalent here: both expose the obstruction rather than remove it.

## 6. Exponent bookkeeping

Let `H=sqrt(X)`.

For each prime:

- possible starts: `p`;
- possible short gap triples: `H^3`;
- naive separated pairs: about

\[
p^2H^6.
\]

Summing over `p~X` gives far too large a bound.

The required target

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}V_p\le X^{2+o(1)}/\log X
\]

requires roughly a square-root saving in the separated pair count. A bounded-gap elimination would provide such a saving by turning the pair into an intersection of low-height varieties. The obstruction above shows that this route is unavailable without a new monodromy estimate.

## 7. Actual q=0 versus projective state

The variance is indexed by projective states `q`, not by the event that a particular coordinate equals zero.

The condition

\[
N_s(x)=0
\]

is basis-dependent: it says one chosen coordinate of the recurrence vector vanishes.

The state condition is instead

\[
[v_x]=[v_y]\in P^1(F_p).
\]

A proof that replaces projective equality by simultaneous coordinate zeros loses invariance and overcounts/undercounts off-diagonal pairs.

Any valid DPLS argument must keep:

1. projective equality as the collision event;
2. zero continuants only as local witnesses for short chains;
3. reflection only as a special symmetry orbit.

## 8. Required next ingredient

The central attack therefore fails in its current form. The missing theorem is not another continuant identity but an averaged long-transfer estimate, for example one of:

- a bound on the number of `L` with prescribed projective transfer;
- an energy estimate for the transfer walk in `PGL_2(F_p)`;
- an arithmetic determinant argument over many primes controlling simultaneous long monodromies.

Without such an input, weak-DPLS cannot be obtained from pairwise short-chain algebra alone.
