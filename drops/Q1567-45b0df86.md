# Second-jet attack on the Problem 3.2 trace lemma

## Verdict

The proposed second-derivative route does **not** force \(q_0\neq0\).

There is one real and useful leading-term fact: if the trace failure holds in its extremal form

\[
\deg V=N-2,
\qquad c:=\operatorname{lc}(V)\ne0,
\]

then for the natural folded Riccati numerator \(R_V\),

\[
M\mid R_V,\qquad W:=R_V/M,
\]

one has

\[
\deg W=N-2,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}(W)=-16c^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{U:=W+16cV}
\]

really does satisfy

\[
\boxed{\deg U\le N-3.}
\]

So the suggested cancellation is genuine.

What fails is the next step. The second WZ jet is **inhomogeneous**. After the already-known factor \(M\mid R_V\) has been removed, the second derivative does not give one more zero; it gives the value of the quotient \(W\). At the first doubled folded node that value contains a nonzero multiple of

\[
\boxed{\tau:=S(y),\qquad y=\lambda_r,}
\]

where

\[
P_1V-1=MS.
\]

Equivalently, \(\tau\) is the unconstrained first-order reciprocal mismatch at the removed factor \(Y-y\):

\[
\boxed{
\tau
=\frac{P_1'(y)V(y)+P_1(y)V'(y)}{M'(y)}
=\frac{P_1'(y)/P_1(y)+P_1(y)V'(y)}{M'(y)}.}
\]

The congruence \(P_1V\equiv1\pmod M\) fixes \(V(y)=P_1(y)^{-1}\), but because \(y\) is only a **simple** root of \(M\), it does not fix \(V'(y)\). The second WZ derivative transports exactly this missing terminal slope into \(W(\lambda_{r+1})\); it does not kill it.

Thus \(W+16cV\) does not acquire the required zeros from the second jet. More generally, no parameter-free combination obtained from the same two differentiated identities can remove this obstruction without an additional theorem controlling \(\tau\) (equivalently the missing second-order inverse condition at \(y\)).

There is also a separate degree-count obstruction: even in the fictitiously favorable situation where \(U\) vanished at **every distinct folded node**, that gives only \(m+1\) ordinary zeros. Since

\[
\deg U\le N-3=2m-r-3,
\]

those zeros do not force \(U=0\) for \(r\le m-4\). To exploit the doubled factors of \(M\), one would need derivative zeros of \(U\) at the doubled nodes. Those first appear one jet later: a third derivative is needed to see \(W'\) when \(R_V=MW\) and \(M\) has a double zero.

So the exact obstruction is twofold, but the decisive local one is the terminal slope \(S(y)\).

## 1. Folded field polynomial and the exact second-order source

Put

\[
p=2m+1,
\qquad \lambda_z=z(z+1),
\qquad a_z:=2z+1.
\]

The folding involution is

\[
z\longmapsto -1-z,
\]

and

\[
\lambda_{-1-z}=\lambda_z,
\qquad a_{-1-z}=-a_z.
\]

For \(0\le j\le m\), the values \(\lambda_j\) are pairwise distinct in \(\mathbf F_p\). Indeed,

\[
\lambda_i-\lambda_j=(i-j)(i+j+1),
\]

and for \(0\le i,j\le m\), the second factor can vanish modulo \(p=2m+1\) only when \(i=j=m\), already covered by \(i=j\).

Define

\[
K(Y):=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_j).
\]

The roots of \(K(\lambda_z)\) are all field points except the midpoint \(z=m\). Since both sides below are monic of degree \(p\),

\[
\boxed{
z^p-z=\frac{2z+1}{2}K(\lambda_z).}
\tag{1.1}
\]

A direct check of the leading coefficient also proves (1.1): \(K(\lambda_z)\) is monic of degree \(2m=p-1\) in \(z\), and \((2z+1)/2\) supplies the final factor \(z\).

Now define the full folded field polynomial

\[
\Phi(Y):=\prod_{z\in\mathbf F_p}(Y-\lambda_z).
\]

Pairing the two preimages of each non-midpoint folded value gives

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(Y)
=(Y-\lambda_m)\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_j)^2
=(Y-\lambda_m)K(Y)^2.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Since

\[
4\lambda_m+1=(2m+1)^2=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p,
\]

we also have

\[
4Y+1=4(Y-\lambda_m).
\tag{1.3}
\]

### The Apéry operator

Use the recurrence operator already underlying the repository's WZ proof:

\[
(\mathcal Lf)(z)
=(z+1)^3f(z+1)
-(2z+1)(17z(z+1)+5)f(z)
+z^3f(z-1).
\tag{1.4}
\]

For a folded polynomial \(f(z)=P(\lambda_z)\), \(\mathcal Lf\) is anti-invariant under \(z\mapsto-1-z\). Therefore there is a unique polynomial \(\widehat{\mathcal L}P\in\mathbf F_p[Y]\) such that

\[
\mathcal L(P(\lambda_z))=(2z+1)(\widehat{\mathcal L}P)(\lambda_z).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The given WZ identity is

\[
\mathcal L(P(\lambda_z))
=-16(2z+1)(z^p-z)^2.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Divide by \(2z+1\) as a polynomial identity and use (1.1)--(1.3):

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\widehat{\mathcal L}P)(\lambda_z)
&=-16(z^p-z)^2\\
&=-16\frac{(2z+1)^2}{4}K(\lambda_z)^2\\
&=-4(4\lambda_z+1)K(\lambda_z)^2\\
&=-16(\lambda_z-\lambda_m)K(\lambda_z)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence the exact folded identity is

\[
\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal L}P=-16\Phi.}
\tag{1.7}
\]

This already explains why the second jet is not an additional homogeneous vanishing condition: after the exact field polynomial has been factored, the first nonzero jet is the value of its quotient.

### Original \(z\)-jet

Let

\[
h(z):=z^p-z,
\qquad G(z):=-16(2z+1)h(z)^2.
\]

In characteristic \(p\),

\[
h'(z)=-1,
\qquad h''(z)=0.
\]

Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
G'(z)
&=-32h(z)^2+32(2z+1)h(z),\\
G''(z)
&=128h(z)-32(2z+1).
\end{aligned}
\]

At every \(j\in\mathbf F_p\), \(h(j)=0\), so

\[
\boxed{
G(j)=0,\qquad G'(j)=0,\qquad G''(j)=-32(2j+1).}
\tag{1.8}
\]

Therefore \(G''(j)\ne0\) for every \(j\ne m\), while \(G''(m)=0\).

### Folded \(Y\)-jet

For \(j<m\), (1.7) has a double zero at \(Y=\lambda_j\). Its second derivative is also completely explicit.

Differentiate (1.1) at \(z=j\). Because \(K(\lambda_j)=0\),

\[
-1
=h'(j)
=\frac{(2j+1)^2}{2}K'(\lambda_j),
\]

so

\[
K'(\lambda_j)=-\frac{2}{(2j+1)^2}.
\tag{1.9}
\]

Using

\[
\lambda_j-\lambda_m=\frac{(2j+1)^2}{4},
\]

we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi''(\lambda_j)
&=2(\lambda_j-\lambda_m)K'(\lambda_j)^2\\
&=\frac{2}{(2j+1)^2}
=\frac{2}{4\lambda_j+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
(-16\Phi)''(\lambda_j)
=-\frac{32}{4\lambda_j+1}\ne0
\qquad(j<m).}
\tag{1.10}
\]

This is the folded form of the nonzero second-order source.

## 2. Exact multiplicities in \(M\)

In this Newton-tail formulation,

\[
\phi_{z+1}(Y)=Y-\lambda_z,
\qquad
J(Y)=\prod_{z=r}^{p-1}(Y-\lambda_z),
\]

and the removed factor is

\[
\phi_{r+1}(Y)=Y-y,
\qquad y=\lambda_r.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
M(Y)=\frac{J(Y)}{Y-y}
=\prod_{z=r+1}^{p-1}(Y-\lambda_z),}
\tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
N=\deg M=p-r-1=2m-r.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Assume first \(r<m\), which is the case in which \(y\) has a distinct reflected preimage. Folding (2.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
M(Y)
=(Y-\lambda_m)
\prod_{j=0}^{r}(Y-\lambda_j)
\prod_{j=r+1}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_j)^2.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus:

- \(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_r,\lambda_m\) are simple roots of \(M\);
- \(\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_{m-1}\) are double roots.

The degree count is

\[
(r+2)+2(m-r-1)=2m-r=N.
\]

Also, from the full product,

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(Y)=M(Y)H_r(Y),
\qquad
H_r(Y):=\prod_{j=0}^{r}(Y-\lambda_j).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently the exact folded WZ source is

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathcal L}P=-16M H_r.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

This factorization is conceptually decisive. At every doubled node \(\lambda_j\), \(r<j<m\),

\[
H_r(\lambda_j)\ne0.
\]

So the source has **exactly the same \(M\)-multiplicity** there, not one more. After dividing by \(M\), the source is nonzero. The second derivative is therefore expected to prescribe a quotient value, not to produce a new zero.

## 3. What the extremal trace failure says about \(P_1,V,S\)

The WZ identity also fixes the degree and leading coefficient of \(P\), without any scan.

Let

\[
P(Y)=a_tY^t+\cdots.
\]

Since \(\lambda_z\sim z^2\), the three terms in (1.4), applied to the top monomial, have leading coefficients

\[
1,\qquad -34,\qquad 1
\]

at degree \(z^{2t+3}\). Hence

\[
\mathcal L(P(\lambda_z))
=-32a_tz^{2t+3}+\text{lower powers of }z.
\]

The right side of (1.6) is

\[
-32z^{2p+1}+\text{lower powers}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\deg P=p-1,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}(P)=1.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since

\[
P=(Y-y)P_1
\]

and the zero at \(y\) is simple,

\[
\boxed{
\deg P_1=p-2,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}(P_1)=1,
\qquad P_1(y)\ne0.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Now assume the trace failure in its extremal case

\[
\boxed{\deg V=N-2.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Write

\[
c:=\operatorname{lc}(V)\ne0.
\]

From

\[
P_1V-1=MS,
\tag{3.4}
\]

we obtain

\[
\deg(P_1V)=p+N-4.
\]

Since \(M\) is monic of degree \(N\), there is no leading cancellation with the constant \(1\), and hence

\[
\boxed{
\deg S=p-4,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}(S)=c.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

So the Bezout identity itself already gives a perfectly consistent leading coefficient for \(S\). There is no contradiction here.

## 4. Exact folded Riccati numerator and its leading coefficient

For clarity, define the natural numerator directly from the Apéry recurrence. Put

\[
D(Y):=Y-y,
\]

and abbreviate

\[
Y_0=\lambda_z,
\quad Y_+=\lambda_{z+1},
\quad Y_-=\lambda_{z-1},
\]

\[
V_0=V(Y_0),
\quad V_+=V(Y_+),
\quad V_-=V(Y_-).
\]

Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}(z):={}&
(z+1)^3D(Y_+)V_0V_-\\
&-(2z+1)(17Y_0+5)D(Y_0)V_+V_-\\
&+z^3D(Y_-)V_+V_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Under \(z\mapsto-1-z\), the plus and minus terms exchange with a sign and the middle term changes sign. Hence

\[
\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}(-1-z)=-\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}(z).
\]

Every anti-invariant polynomial is \((2z+1)\) times a polynomial in \(Y=z(z+1)\). Therefore there is a unique \(R_V\in\mathbf F_p[Y]\) such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}(z)=(2z+1)R_V(\lambda_z).}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the folded Riccati numerator in the normalization for which the candidate \(W+16cV\) is natural. The previously proved value/first-jet argument gives

\[
\boxed{M\mid R_V.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let \(d=\deg V\) and \(c=\operatorname{lc}V\). The top \(z\)-degree in (4.1) is \(4d+5\). Again the three principal coefficients are

\[
1,-34,1,
\]

so

\[
\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}(z)
=-32c^2z^{4d+5}+\cdots.
\]

Divide by \(2z+1\sim2z\), and use \(Y\sim z^2\):

\[
\boxed{
\deg R_V=2d+2,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}(R_V)=-16c^2.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Under \(d=N-2\),

\[
\deg R_V=2N-2.
\]

Writing

\[
R_V=MW,
\tag{4.5}
\]

with \(M\) monic of degree \(N\), we get

\[
\boxed{
\deg W=N-2,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}(W)=-16c^2.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore the suggested cancellation is exact:

\[
\boxed{
U:=W+16cV,
\qquad \deg U\le N-3.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is a genuine one-degree descent. The rest of the question is whether the second jet supplies enough zero conditions on \(U\). It does not.

## 5. Bezout jets: where the terminal slope lives

Set

\[
B(Y):=P_1(Y)V(Y)-1=M(Y)S(Y).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let \(x\) be a root of \(M\).

### 5.1 Simple root of \(M\)

If \(M(x)=0\) and \(M'(x)\ne0\), then

\[
B(x)=0,
\]

\[
B'(x)=M'(x)S(x),
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
B''(x)=M''(x)S(x)+2M'(x)S'(x).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the first two jets determine \(S(x)\) and then \(S'(x)\), but neither is forced to vanish.

### 5.2 Double root of \(M\)

If \(M(x)=M'(x)=0\) and \(M''(x)\ne0\), then

\[
B(x)=B'(x)=0
\]

and

\[
\boxed{B''(x)=M''(x)S(x).}
\tag{5.4}
\]

So the second jet determines a **value** of \(S\) at a doubled node. It does not give \(S'(x)\); that requires one more derivative.

### 5.3 The removed factor \(y=\lambda_r\)

By (2.3), \(y\) is a simple root of \(M\) when \(r<m\). Since \(P_1(y)\ne0\), (5.1) gives

\[
V(y)=P_1(y)^{-1}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

But there is no derivative condition. Define

\[
\boxed{\tau:=S(y).}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Using (5.2),

\[
M'(y)\tau
=P_1'(y)V(y)+P_1(y)V'(y).
\]

Substitute (5.5):

\[
\boxed{
\tau
=\frac{P_1'(y)/P_1(y)+P_1(y)V'(y)}{M'(y)}.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
V'(y)
=-\frac{P_1'(y)}{P_1(y)^2}
+\frac{M'(y)}{P_1(y)}\tau.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

The first term is the derivative of the true reciprocal \(P_1^{-1}\). The second term is the missing terminal slope.

There is a useful exact reformulation. Since

\[
J=(Y-y)M,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
\tau=0
\iff (Y-y)\mid S
\iff J\mid(P_1V-1).}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus controlling \(\tau\) is exactly the problem of upgrading the inverse congruence from \(M\) to the one missing copy of \(Y-y\) in \(J\). Nothing in the assumption \(q_0=0\), i.e. the degree drop of the inverse modulo \(M\), supplies this derivative upgrade.

## 6. Exact second-differentiated WZ/Bezout identity

This is the cleanest place to see why the nonzero source does not help.

From (5.1),

\[
P_1(Y)=\frac{1+B(Y)}{V(Y)}
\]

as an identity in the fraction field. Hence

\[
P(Y)=D(Y)P_1(Y)
=D(Y)\frac{1+B(Y)}{V(Y)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Put

\[
T(z):=V_+V_0V_-.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Substituting (6.1) into the exact Apéry recurrence and multiplying by \(T\) gives

\[
\boxed{
G(z)T(z)=\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}(z)+\mathcal E(z),}
\tag{6.3}
\]

where \(G(z)=-16(2z+1)(z^p-z)^2\), \(\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}\) is (4.1), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E(z)={}&
(z+1)^3D(Y_+)B(Y_+)V_0V_-\\
&-(2z+1)(17Y_0+5)D(Y_0)B(Y_0)V_+V_-\\
&+z^3D(Y_-)B(Y_-)V_+V_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

No congruence has been used in (6.3); it is an exact identity.

At a field point \(j\), every folded neighbor \(Y_-,Y_0,Y_+\) is a root of \(M\) in the main case \(r<m\), hence

\[
B(Y_-)=B(Y_0)=B(Y_+)=0.
\]

Also \(G(j)=G'(j)=0\). Differentiating (6.3) twice therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
(\mathcal R_V^{\sharp})''(j)+\mathcal E''(j)
=G''(j)T(j)
=-32(2j+1)V_{j+1}V_jV_{j-1}.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Here and below \(V_k:=V(\lambda_k)\), with folded indices understood.

### A uniform expression for the correction jet

Write the three summands of \(\mathcal E\) as

\[
Q_\sigma(z)B(Y_\sigma(z)),
\qquad \sigma\in\{-,0,+\}.
\]

At a field point, \(B(Y_\sigma)=0\), so

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E''(j)
=\sum_{\sigma}
\left(
2Q_\sigma'(j)\frac{d}{dz}B(Y_\sigma(j))
+Q_\sigma(j)\frac{d^2}{dz^2}B(Y_\sigma(j))
\right).}
\tag{6.6}
\]

For \(x_k=\lambda_k\), put \(a_k=2k+1\). Since

\[
\frac{d}{dz}\lambda_{z+s}\bigg|_{z=j}=a_{j+s},
\qquad
\frac{d^2}{dz^2}\lambda_{z+s}=2,
\]

we have

\[
\frac{d}{dz}B(\lambda_{z+s})\bigg|_{z=j}
=a_{j+s}B_Y'(x_{j+s}),
\tag{6.7}
\]

\[
\frac{d^2}{dz^2}B(\lambda_{z+s})\bigg|_{z=j}
=2B_Y'(x_{j+s})+a_{j+s}^2B_Y''(x_{j+s}).
\tag{6.8}
\]

Equations (5.2)--(5.4), (6.6)--(6.8) are the requested exact second-jet interpolation conditions on \(S\).

They show immediately why a nonzero source does not imply one more factor: at a simple root the correction contains \(S'\), while at a double root it contains the value \(S\). These are legitimate quotient jets.

## 7. The terminal-slope obstruction appears with a nonzero coefficient

Assume

\[
r\le m-2,
\]

so that the first doubled node exists. Set

\[
j:=r+1,
\qquad x:=\lambda_j,
\qquad a_j=2j+1=2r+3,
\qquad a_r=2r+1.
\]

Then \(x\) is a double root of \(M\), while

\[
Y_-\big|_{z=j}=\lambda_r=y
\]

is the simple terminal root.

Because \(R_V=MW\) and \(x\) is a double root,

\[
R_V(x)=R_V'(x)=0,
\qquad
R_V''(x)=M''(x)W(x).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Using

\[
\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}(z)=(2z+1)R_V(\lambda_z),
\]

and differentiating twice, the terms containing \(R_V\) and \(R_V'\) vanish at \(z=j\), leaving

\[
\boxed{
(\mathcal R_V^{\sharp})''(j)
=a_j^3M''(x)W(x).}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Now isolate the minus summand of (6.4):

\[
\mathcal E_-(z)
=z^3D(Y_-)B(Y_-)V_+V_0.
\]

At \(z=j\), both

\[
D(Y_-)=Y_--y=0,
\qquad B(Y_-)=0.
\]

Thus in its second derivative the only surviving product-rule term is

\[
2(D(Y_-))'(B(Y_-))'\,j^3V_{j+1}V_j.
\]

Now

\[
(D(Y_-))'=a_r,
\]

and by (5.2), (5.6),

\[
(B(Y_-))'=a_r B_Y'(y)
=a_rM'(y)\tau.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_-''(j)
=2j^3a_r^2V_{j+1}V_jM'(y)\tau.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

This coefficient is nonzero:

- \(j=r+1\ne0\) in \(\mathbf F_p\);
- \(a_r=2r+1\ne0\) because \(r<m\);
- \(V_j,V_{j+1}\ne0\) because \(P_1V=1\) at roots of \(M\);
- \(M'(y)\ne0\) because \(y\) is a simple root.

Let \(\Xi_j\) denote the remaining two contributions to \(\mathcal E''(j)\), from the central and plus slots. They are explicitly obtained from (6.6)--(6.8); at doubled slots they involve \(M''S\), and if the plus slot is the midpoint its ramification \(a_m=0\) leaves the corresponding simple-root \(M'S\) term. Crucially, none of these is the terminal quantity \(\tau=S(y)\).

Insert (7.2) and (7.3) into (6.5):

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
a_j^3M''(x)W(x)
&+\Xi_j
+2j^3a_r^2V_{j+1}V_jM'(y)\tau\\
&=-32a_jV_{j+1}V_jV(y).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
W(x)
={}&-\frac{32V_{j+1}V_jV(y)}{a_j^2M''(x)}
-\frac{\Xi_j}{a_j^3M''(x)}\\
&-\frac{2j^3a_r^2V_{j+1}V_jM'(y)}{a_j^3M''(x)}\tau.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is the exact boundary interpolation equation for \(W\).

Define

\[
\kappa_r
:=\frac{2j^3a_r^2V_{j+1}V_jM'(y)}{a_j^3M''(x)}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

All factors above are nonzero, so

\[
\boxed{\kappa_r\ne0.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Consequently the degree-descended polynomial

\[
U=W+16cV
\]

satisfies at the first doubled node an equation of the form

\[
\boxed{
U(\lambda_{r+1})=A_r-\kappa_r\tau,}
\tag{7.8}
\]

where \(A_r\) is the completely explicit expression consisting of the first two terms of (7.5), \(16cV(\lambda_{r+1})\), and the nonterminal pieces inside \(\Xi_j\).

The important point is not the name \(A_r\); it is the nonzero coefficient (7.7). The second-jet identities do **not** imply

\[
U(\lambda_{r+1})=0.
\]

They imply instead that such a zero would be an **additional terminal-slope equation**

\[
\tau=A_r/\kappa_r.
\tag{7.9}
\]

No leading-coefficient calculation gives (7.9).

This is the exact obstruction requested in the question.

## 8. Why the leading coefficients are consistent rather than contradictory

There are two leading comparisons, and both close tautologically.

### 8.1 Bezout leading term

We already found

\[
\operatorname{lc}(S)=c.
\]

Nothing in the second jet changes this.

### 8.2 Global WZ/Bezout numerator identity

The exact identity (6.3) also shows why no hidden contradiction appears at infinity.

If \(d=\deg V\), then

\[
T=V_+V_0V_-
\]

has leading \(z\)-term

\[
c^3z^{6d}.
\]

The WZ source \(G\) has leading term \(-32z^{2p+1}\), so

\[
GT=-32c^3z^{2p+6d+1}+\cdots.
\tag{8.1}
\]

On the correction side,

\[
B=P_1V-1
\]

has degree \(p+d-2\) and leading coefficient \(c\). In the three terms of \(\mathcal E\), the same principal recurrence coefficients \(1,-34,1\) occur, while the remaining two \(V\)-factors contribute \(c^2\). Hence

\[
\mathcal E=-32c^3z^{2p+6d+1}+\cdots.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus the top equation in (6.3) is simply

\[
-32c^3=-32c^3.
\]

The low-degree Riccati remainder \(\mathcal R_V^{\sharp}\) starts far below this scale. So the WZ identity is completely compatible with \(\operatorname{lc}S=c\); the nonzero second-order source does not force \(c=0\) or any inconsistent top coefficient.

Similarly, (4.6) gives the consistent quotient coefficient

\[
\operatorname{lc}W=-16c^2.
\]

The polynomial \(U=W+16cV\) removes that one coefficient, but nothing forces its next coefficient to vanish.

## 9. Degree and interpolation counts

There are two independent reasons the second jet cannot provide a general strict descent.

### 9.1 One jet short at every double root

Suppose \(x\) is a double root of \(M\). With \(R_V=MW\),

\[
R_V''(x)=M''(x)W(x).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Thus the second derivative determines \(W(x)\).

But

\[
R_V'''(x)
=M'''(x)W(x)+3M''(x)W'(x).
\tag{9.2}
\]

So \(W'(x)\) does not appear until the **third** derivative.

To prove that a polynomial such as \(U=W+16cV\) is divisible by the doubled part of \(M\), one needs

\[
U(x)=0,
\qquad U'(x)=0
\]

at each doubled root. A twice-differentiated WZ identity simply cannot supply the second of these Hermite conditions. It is structurally one jet short.

### 9.2 Even ordinary zeros at every folded point are usually insufficient

There are only

\[
m+1
\]

distinct folded points \(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_m\).

Under the extremal trace failure,

\[
\deg U\le N-3=2m-r-3.
\]

Even if, contrary to (7.8), one granted the optimistic statement

\[
U(\lambda_j)=0
\qquad(0\le j\le m),
\]

those \(m+1\) distinct zeros force \(U=0\) only when

\[
m+1>2m-r-3,
\]

i.e.

\[
r\ge m-3.
\]

For

\[
r\le m-4,
\]

a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(N-3\) can vanish at all \(m+1\) folded points. For example, its distinct-root factor can have degree \(m+1\) while still fitting inside the allowed degree.

Thus a uniform proof would have to use the doubled Hermite multiplicities in \(M\), and those require third-jet information for \(U'\).

### 9.3 The same count on \(S\)

From (3.5),

\[
\deg S=p-4=2m-3,
\]

so \(S\) has \(2m-2\) coefficients.

Through second order, the Bezout quotient supplies at most:

- two pieces \(S(x),S'(x)\) at each of the \(r+2\) simple roots \(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_r,\lambda_m\);
- one piece \(S(x)\) at each of the \(m-r-1\) double roots.

That is

\[
2(r+2)+(m-r-1)=m+r+3
\]

jet values. Even after fixing \(\operatorname{lc}S=c\), a raw interpolation count leaves genuine freedom whenever \(r\) is sufficiently below \(m\). More importantly, (7.4) identifies the actual boundary datum that survives: \(S(y)\), equivalently \(V'(y)\).

The count is therefore consistent with the explicit local obstruction; it is not hiding an overdetermination.

---

## 10. Why \(W+16cV\) is the only linear leading cancellation, and why alternatives do not help

Suppose one looks only for a combination

\[
W+\alpha V
\]

with scalar \(\alpha\) that lowers the degree. Since

\[
\operatorname{lc}W=-16c^2,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}V=c,
\]

the leading coefficient is

\[
-16c^2+\alpha c.
\]

Because \(c\ne0\), cancellation forces

\[
\boxed{\alpha=16c.}
\]

So \(W+16cV\) is not merely a plausible choice; it is the unique scalar linear combination of \(W\) and \(V\) that gives this one-degree descent.

Equation (7.8) then shows exactly why it stops:

\[
U(\lambda_{r+1})=A_r-\kappa_r\tau,
\qquad \kappa_r\ne0.
\]

Adding lower-degree expressions in \(V,V',\ldots\) can only hide the same issue. To cancel the terminal term uniformly, the new combination must somehow encode

\[
\tau=S(y)
\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad V'(y).
\]

But that is precisely the datum not fixed by the inverse congruence modulo the simple factor at \(y\). Once such a terminal datum is inserted by hand, the argument has not proved a trace lemma; it has assumed the missing trace-adjacent relation in another form.

So there is no parameter-free second-jet combination giving a uniform strict descent unless one first proves an additional identity for the terminal slope.

## 11. Edge cases

### \(r=m-1\)

There are no doubled roots in (2.3). The polynomial \(M\) is squarefree on all folded nodes. Then the first derivative of \(R_V=MW\) already gives \(W(x)\), while the second derivative gives

\[
R_V''(x)=M''(x)W(x)+2M'(x)W'(x).
\]

On the Bezout side, simple roots similarly give

\[
B''(x)=M''(x)S(x)+2M'(x)S'(x).
\]

Thus the second WZ jet is a slope equation involving \(W'\) and \(S'\), not a new zero condition. The local obstruction is even more transparent: there is no doubled \(M\)-factor on which a second derivative could create another multiplicity.

### \(r=m\)

The removed point is the midpoint, where

\[
2r+1=0
\]

and the original WZ second derivative (1.8) vanishes. Therefore the proposed nonzero-source mechanism is absent at the terminal point itself. This case cannot be rescued by the argument under discussion; it has strictly less second-jet information.

---

## 12. What additional statement would actually close the route

There are two qualitatively new inputs that could make a higher-jet proof work.

### A. A terminal-slope theorem

One needs an independent formula for

\[
\boxed{\tau=S(y)}
\]

or equivalently for

\[
V'(y).
\]

The strongest simple possibility is

\[
\tau=0,
\]

which, by (5.9), upgrades

\[
P_1V\equiv1\pmod M
\]

to

\[
P_1V\equiv1\pmod J.
\]

But the second WZ jet does not imply this; its boundary equation has a nonzero coefficient of \(\tau\) and simply solves for a quotient value in terms of it.

For the specific polynomial \(U=W+16cV\), it would already suffice to prove the exact value (7.9) required to make \(U(\lambda_{r+1})=0\). That is still an independent terminal-slope identity.

### B. One more jet at the doubled roots

Even after controlling the value conditions, a divisibility/descent argument using the full multiplicity of \(M\) needs \(U'(\lambda_j)=0\) at the doubled roots. By (9.2), this requires the third derivative of the Riccati identity and the corresponding third Bezout jet.

A successful higher-jet strategy therefore has to do both:

1. close the missing terminal slope at the removed factor;
2. propagate one further derivative through every doubled folded root.

The second derivative alone does neither.

---

# Final conclusion

Assume the extremal trace failure

\[
q_0=0,
\qquad \deg V=N-2,
\qquad c=\operatorname{lc}V\ne0.
\]

Then the exact algebra gives

\[
\deg S=p-4,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}S=c,
\]

and

\[
R_V=MW,
\qquad
\deg W=N-2,
\qquad
\operatorname{lc}W=-16c^2.
\]

Hence

\[
U=W+16cV
\]

has the genuine degree descent

\[
\deg U\le N-3.
\]

But the folded WZ source is

\[
-16\Phi=-16M H_r,
\]

with \(H_r(\lambda_j)\ne0\) at the doubled nodes above \(r\). Its second derivative is therefore the **first nonzero quotient jet**, not an extra homogeneous zero.

At the first doubled node \(j=r+1\), differentiating the exact WZ/Bezout identity twice gives

\[
a_j^3M''(\lambda_j)W(\lambda_j)
+\Xi_j
+2j^3(2r+1)^2V_{j+1}V_jM'(y)\,S(y)
=-32a_jV_{j+1}V_jV(y),
\]

and the coefficient of \(S(y)\) is nonzero. Therefore

\[
(W+16cV)(\lambda_{r+1})
=A_r-\kappa_rS(y),
\qquad \kappa_r\ne0,
\]

rather than \(0\).

That is the exact obstruction. The nonzero second-order source does not contradict the degree-\(N-2\) inverse; it consumes the next jet by fixing a quotient value while leaving the missing terminal reciprocal slope as a free boundary datum. Leading coefficients are fully consistent, and a twice-differentiated identity is also one derivative short of the Hermite multiplicities needed for a uniform zero-count descent.

So this second formulation does **not** prove \(q_0\ne0\). The next honest target is a separate terminal-slope identity (or an equivalent upgrade from \(M\) to \(J\)), followed, if one wants an \(M\)-multiplicity descent, by a third-jet analysis.
