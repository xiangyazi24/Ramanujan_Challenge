# Third moment sieve and the atom obstruction

## Short answer

The proposed third moment sieve does not close with the current inputs. The obstruction is genuine: the expansion of the third factorial moment reduces to a short-interval CRT anti-concentration problem. The per-prime separation estimate

```text
A_p(h) <= 3(h-1)
```

controls geometry inside one fiber Z_p, but it does not control how triples of independent residue conditions are distributed among CRT atoms.

## Third factorial moment

Let

```text
K(m)=sum_{p in (X,2X]} 1_{m mod p in Z_p}.
```

Then

```text
sum_{m<X^2} (K(m))_3
```

is a sum over triples of distinct primes and triples of zeros

```text
r_p in Z_p, r_q in Z_q, r_l in Z_l.
```

For fixed data, CRT gives one residue modulo pq l. Since

```text
p q l > X^3 > X^2,
```

there is at most one representative below X^2.

## The factor X loss

The number of possible marked triples is approximately

```text
(sum_p |Z_p|)^3 = (X lambda)^3.
```

A random CRT representative would land in [0,X^2) with probability about

```text
X^2/(p q l) ~ 1/X.
```

This heuristic gives the desired main term

```text
X^2 lambda^3.
```

But the trivial upper bound counts all atoms and gives

```text
X^3 lambda^3,
```

which loses exactly the factor X.

## Why pair separation is insufficient

The estimate

```text
A_p(h) <= 3(h-1)
```

says that two bad residues cannot cluster too much for a single prime p. It controls configurations

```text
b_r = b_{r+h} = 0 (mod p).
```

The third moment requires a statement about

```text
m = r_p (mod p),
 m = r_q (mod q),
 m = r_l (mod l).
```

This is a statement about the distribution of CRT lifts in Z/(pql)Z. The vertical spacing of Z_p gives no direct information about this horizontal CRT placement.

Therefore the factor X cannot be removed from the third moment using only A_p(h).

## What would be enough

A sufficient new theorem would be a CRT anti-concentration estimate:

```text
#{(r_p,r_q,r_l): CRT(r_p,r_q,r_l)<X^2}
 <= X^2/(p q l) |Z_p||Z_q||Z_l| + error.
```

Summing over triples would imply

```text
sum_m (K(m))_3 << X^2 lambda^3.
```

Then

```text
n_T <= sum_m(K(m))_3 / (T(T-1)(T-2))
     << X^2 lambda^3/T^3.
```

## Possible routes

1. Large sieve: express the indicators by Fourier expansion and seek cancellation in mixed frequencies a/p+b/q+c/l.

2. Triple resultant geometry: control simultaneous compatibility of three gap conditions using the recurrence algebra.

3. Direct atom exclusion: prove that a fixed integer m cannot occupy too many CRT atoms. This is essentially the desired pointwise theorem.

## Conclusion

The third moment sieve is the right probabilistic mechanism, but the missing ingredient is not a refinement of pair correlations. The barrier is the CRT atom problem:

```text
three primes => modulus ~ X^3, counting interval ~ X^2.
```

A new three-prime anti-concentration theorem is needed to pass from the known T^{-2} Chebyshev bound to the desired T^{-3} tail.
