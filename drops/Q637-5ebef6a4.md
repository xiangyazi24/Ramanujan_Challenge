# The rounded Kloosterman toy is not the Kloosterman sheaf modulo \(p\)

## Executive verdict

There are **three different objects** being conflated:

1. the exact cyclotomic Kloosterman value
   
   \[
   K_p(t)=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
   \zeta_p^{\,x+t/x}\in \mathbf Z[\zeta_p],
   \]

2. its nearest rational integer
   
   \[
   R_p(t)=\operatorname{round}\bigl(\iota_\infty K_p(t)\bigr)\in\mathbf Z,
   \]

3. a \(p\)-adic or crystalline reduction of the exact algebraic integer \(K_p(t)\).

The first is the trace function of the Kloosterman sheaf. The second is an **archimedean rounding operation** and is not a sheaf trace, a Frobenius trace, a Hasse invariant, or a reduction of the first in any algebraic sense.

This is already visible at \(p=5\):

\[
K_5(1)=2+\zeta_5^2+\zeta_5^3
      =\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}
      \approx0.381966,
\]

so the exact Kloosterman sum is an algebraic integer but not a rational integer, while rounding replaces it by \(0\).

The precise conclusions are:

- If the computation uses \(R_p(t)=\operatorname{round}(K_p(t))\), then its Mellin transform is a new, ad hoc finite-field transform. It has **no Gauss-square identity**. Mean zero-count about \(1\) is exactly the random-vector baseline.
- If one keeps the exact \(K_p(t)\) and uses least-residue weights \(t^r\bmod p\), then after choosing the \(p\)-adic embedding one has
  
  \[
  \sum_t (t^r\bmod p)K_p(t)
  \equiv g(\omega^r)^2 \pmod{p\mathcal O}.
  \]
  
  Thus the ordinary residue weights do **not** destroy the Gauss-square relation modulo the rational ideal \((p)\).
- However, the quotient \(\mathcal O/(p)\) is not \(\mathbf F_p\). It is a nonreduced ramified ring of length \(p-1\). The canonical residue-field map sends \(\zeta_p\mapsto1\), so \(K_p(t)\mapsto-1\) for every \(t\). Consequently direct reduction to \(\mathbf F_p\) makes **all nontrivial Mellin coefficients vanish**.
- The correct Dwork-normalized \(p\)-adic Mellin coefficient is
  
  \[
  \pi^{-2a}g(\omega^{-a})^2
  =\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac a{p-1}\right)^2,
  \]
  
  a \(p\)-adic unit for every \(a\). Its first-Hasse reduction has **no zeros at all**.

So the apparent gap

```text
complex Mellin: no zeros
rounded integer Mellin mod p: about one zero
```

is not an arithmetic mystery. The second statistic is not a reduction of the first. The nearest-integer step has replaced the Kloosterman trace function by a different function.

This also means that the rounded Kloosterman experiment is **not a valid model for AFP**. In the Apéry problem, the \(b_r\bmod p\) are canonically defined integral/Hasse--Witt Mellin coefficients. No archimedean rounding is involved.

# 1. The exact Kloosterman sum is not integer-valued

Fix

\[
\psi(u)=\zeta_p^u,
\qquad
K_p(t)=\sum_{x\ne0}\psi\!\left(x+\frac tx\right).
\]

The standard facts are:

- \(K_p(t)\) is an algebraic integer in the real subfield of \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_p)\);
- it is real because complex conjugation is absorbed by \(x\mapsto-x\);
- in general it is not rational, hence not an integer;
- the Weil bound is \(|K_p(t)|\le2\sqrt p\).

Thus the terminology

```text
Kl_int(t) = round(K_p(t)) is the integer Kloosterman sum
```

should be replaced by

```text
R_p(t) is the nearest-integer rounding of one complex embedding
of the cyclotomic Kloosterman sum.
```

That distinction is structural. Rounding is not Galois-equivariant, not \(p\)-adically continuous, and not compatible with an integral Frobenius lattice.

A tiny archimedean error can be a \(p\)-adic unit. If

\[
E_p(t)=R_p(t)-K_p(t),
\]

then \(|\iota_\infty E_p(t)|\le1/2\), but typically

\[
v_\pi(E_p(t))=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_t(t^r\bmod p)R_p(t)
-
\sum_t(t^r\bmod p)K_p(t)
=
\sum_t(t^r\bmod p)E_p(t)
\]

is archimedean-small termwise but completely uncontrolled modulo \(p\).

# 2. Exact complex and \(p\)-adic Mellin transforms

Let \(\chi:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\overline{\mathbf Q}^{\times}\) be a multiplicative character and define

\[
g(\chi)=\sum_{u\ne0}\chi(u)\psi(u).
\]

Then the Mellin transform of \(K_p\) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{t\ne0}\chi(t)K_p(t)=g(\chi)^2.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t\ne0}\chi(t)K_p(t)
&=\sum_{t,x\ne0}\chi(t)\psi(x)\psi(t/x)\\
&=\sum_{x\ne0}\psi(x)
  \sum_{u\ne0}\chi(ux)\psi(u)
  \qquad(t=ux)\\
&=g(\chi)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Depending on whether the sheaf trace is \(K_p(t)\) or \(-K_p(t)\), and whether Mellin uses \(\chi\) or \(\chi^{-1}\), there may be an overall sign or inverse character. None affects zeros or valuations.

Cohomologically, multiplicative convolution sends the two Artin--Schreier Mellin lines to their tensor product, so the Kloosterman Mellin fiber is one-dimensional with Frobenius eigenvalue \(g(\chi)^2\).

# 3. Least-residue powers versus Teichmüller characters

The statement in part (B) is only half correct.

It is true that the integer-valued function

\[
t\longmapsto [t^r]_p\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
\]

is not a complex multiplicative character. Thus there is no exact characteristic-zero factorization

\[
\sum_t[t^r]_pK_p(t)=g(\chi_r)^2.
\]

But modulo \(p\), the least-residue lift is congruent to the Teichmüller character, so the Gauss-square identity **does survive modulo the rational ideal \((p)\)**.

Choose a \(p\)-adic embedding and let

\[
\omega:\mathbf F_p^\times\longrightarrow\mu_{p-1}\subset\mathbf Z_p^\times
\]

be the Teichmüller character. Put

\[
C_r=\sum_{t\ne0}[t^r]_pK_p(t)
\]

inside the ring of integers \(\mathcal O\) of \(\mathbf Q_p(\zeta_p)\). Since

\[
[t^r]_p\equiv\omega(t)^r\pmod p,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
C_r\equiv
\sum_{t\ne0}\omega(t)^rK_p(t)
=g(\omega^r)^2
\pmod{p\mathcal O}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, for some \(D_r\in\mathcal O\),

\[
C_r=g(\omega^r)^2+pD_r.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore the discrepancy between ordinary residue powers and Teichmüller powers is **not** what produces the observed \(O(1)\) zero count. If exact cyclotomic Kloosterman values are retained, the two weights give the same class modulo \(p\mathcal O\).

## 3.1 Exact character expansion of the residue weight

There is also an exact formula, not merely a congruence. Expand the function \([t^r]_p\) in the Teichmüller character basis:

\[
[t^r]_p
=\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}A_{r,k}\omega(t)^k,
\]

where

\[
A_{r,k}
=\frac1{p-1}
 \sum_{t\ne0}[t^r]_p\omega(t)^{-k}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
C_r=\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}A_{r,k}g(\omega^k)^2.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Because \(p-1\) is a \(p\)-adic unit and \([t^r]_p\equiv\omega(t)^r\pmod p\), character orthogonality gives

\[
A_{r,k}\equiv\delta_{r,k}\pmod p.
\]

Equation (3.3) therefore reduces to (3.1). This is the precise algebraic relation requested in part (A).

# 4. Why “modulo \(p\)” is ambiguous for cyclotomic Kloosterman values

Let \(\pi\) be a Dwork uniformizer satisfying

\[
\pi^{p-1}=-p
\]

up to the standard choice of unit. Then

\[
(p)=(\pi)^{p-1}
\]

in \(\mathcal O\).

There are two different quotients:

\[
\mathcal O/(\pi)\cong\mathbf F_p,
\]

and

\[
\mathcal O/(p)
=\mathcal O/(\pi^{p-1}),
\]

which is a nonreduced local ring of length \(p-1\), modeled by

\[
\mathbf F_p[\pi]/(\pi^{p-1}).
\]

Thus

```text
reduce modulo the prime above p
```

and

```text
reduce modulo the rational ideal (p)
```

are not the same operation.

## 4.1 Direct reduction to the residue field

Every primitive \(p\)-th root of unity reduces to \(1\), because in characteristic \(p\)

\[
X^p-1=(X-1)^p.
\]

Therefore

\[
K_p(t)\equiv\sum_{x\ne0}1=p-1\equiv-1\pmod\pi
\]

for every \(t\ne0\). Hence

\[
\sum_{t\ne0}t^rK_p(t)
\equiv-\sum_{t\ne0}t^r\pmod\pi.
\]

It follows that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{t\ne0}t^rK_p(t)\equiv0\pmod\pi
\quad(1\le r\le p-2).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

So the canonical \(\mathbf F_p\)-valued reduction of the exact Kloosterman trace has \(p-2\) nontrivial Mellin zeros, not about one.

All nontrivial information is stored in the nilpotent \(\pi\)-adic layers of \(\mathcal O/(p)\), not in the residue field alone.

## 4.2 Divisibility by the rational ideal \((p)\)

Now use Gross--Koblitz. With the convention

\[
\chi_a=\omega^{-a},
\qquad 0\le a\le p-2,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
g(\omega^{-a})
=-\pi^a\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac a{p-1}\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The gamma value is a unit. Thus

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\pi g(\omega^{-a})^2=2a.
\]

Since \(\operatorname{ord}_\pi(p)=p-1\),

\[
g(\omega^{-a})^2\in p\mathcal O
\iff
2a\ge p-1.
\]

Among \(a=1,\ldots,p-2\), this occurs for exactly

\[
\boxed{\frac{p-1}{2}}
\]

characters. By (3.1), the same is true for the exact least-residue-weighted sum \(C_r\), after translating between \(r\) and \(a=p-1-r\).

Thus the canonical exact statistics are:

| object | nontrivial zero count |
|---|---:|
| characteristic-zero Mellin \(g(\chi)^2\) | \(0\) |
| residue-field reduction mod \((\pi)\) | \(p-2\) |
| raw divisibility mod \((p)\) | \((p-1)/2\) |
| Dwork-normalized first-Hasse reduction | \(0\) |

None has mean one.

# 5. What nearest-integer rounding actually computes

Define

\[
R_p(t)=\operatorname{round}(K_p(t))\in\mathbf Z
\]

using one fixed complex embedding, and reduce it modulo \(p\). Then

\[
M_r^{\mathrm{round}}
=\sum_{t\ne0}R_p(t)t^r\in\mathbf F_p
\]

is the finite-field Mellin transform of the arbitrary function

\[
R_p:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\mathbf F_p.
\]

There is no Gauss-square formula for this function. The exact identity is only the tautology

\[
M_r^{\mathrm{round}}
=C_r+
\sum_{t\ne0}[t^r]_p\bigl(R_p(t)-K_p(t)\bigr),
\]

and the second term is \(p\)-adically uncontrolled.

Indeed, modulo \(\pi\),

\[
R_p(t)-K_p(t)\equiv R_p(t)+1.
\]

Thus unless every rounded value happens to be \(-1\bmod p\), the rounding error is visible already in the first residue-field layer.

## 5.1 Why a mean zero-count near one is the random baseline

For any function \(R:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\mathbf F_p\), define

\[
M_r=\sum_{t\ne0}R(t)t^r,
\qquad 0\le r\le p-2.
\]

This transform is invertible. Multiplicative orthogonality gives

\[
\boxed{
R(t)=-\sum_{r=0}^{p-2}M_rt^{-r}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore the map

\[
(R(t))_{t\ne0}\longmapsto(M_r)_{0\le r\le p-2}
\]

is an invertible linear transformation of \(\mathbf F_p^{p-1}\).

If the rounded vector behaves like a generic vector in \(\mathbf F_p^{p-1}\), then its Mellin transform is equally generic. Each coordinate is zero with probability \(1/p\), so

\[
\mathbf E\#\{r:M_r=0\}
=\frac{p-1}{p}
\longrightarrow1.
\]

The zero count tends to a Poisson\((1)\)-type random baseline. Hence the reported mean about \(1\) and maximum \(3\) for small primes are exactly what one expects from an arbitrary rounded vector. They are not evidence for a Kloosterman-sheaf theorem.

# 6. The correct crystalline interpretation

For a \(p\)-adic trace function, “mod-\(p\) trace” is meaningful only after specifying:

1. a \(p\)-adic realization;
2. an integral Frobenius lattice;
3. the expected Hodge/Stickelberger factor;
4. the graded piece in which the leading Frobenius term is reduced.

For Kloosterman Mellin, Gross--Koblitz performs this normalization explicitly.

For \(\chi_a=\omega^{-a}\), define

\[
M_a^{\mathrm{Teich}}
=\sum_{t\ne0}\omega(t)^{-a}K_p(t)
=g(\omega^{-a})^2.
\]

Then

\[
M_a^{\mathrm{Teich}}
=\pi^{2a}
 \Gamma_p\!\left(\frac a{p-1}\right)^2.
\]

The expected first Hodge slope is \(2a/(p-1)\). Remove it:

\[
\widetilde M_a
=\pi^{-2a}M_a^{\mathrm{Teich}}
=\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac a{p-1}\right)^2
\in\mathcal O^\times.
\]

Thus the initial form in the relevant graded piece is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{in}_{2a}(M_a^{\mathrm{Teich}})
=\overline{\Gamma_p(a/(p-1))^2}
\in\mathbf F_p^\times.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Every normalized Mellin fiber is ordinary at the first break. There is one minimal-slope Frobenius eigenvalue and it is a unit after normalization. No cancellation is possible because the Mellin fiber is one-dimensional.

This is the correct \(p\)-adic answer to part (D): the Kloosterman Mellin zero count can be determined exactly, and the normalized first-Hasse zero count is \(0\).

## 6.1 General Kloosterman and hypergeometric systems

For the \(n\)-variable Kloosterman sheaf, the same convolution calculation gives schematically

\[
\mathcal M(\mathrm{Kl}_n)(\chi)=g(\chi)^n.
\]

Gross--Koblitz then gives a single gamma-unit after removal of the predicted \(\pi\)-power. Again there are no normalized first-Hasse zeros.

For a bare Katz hypergeometric sheaf, the Mellin transform is a product of Gauss sums, up to explicit constants and exceptional-character corrections. Gross--Koblitz converts this to

```text
predicted pi-power × product of p-adic gamma units.
```

A pure product has no normalized zeros. Zeros arise only when the relevant geometric object produces a **sum** of several equal-minimal-valuation terms, or when pullback, primitive projection, or boundary corrections create additive interference.

That is precisely the structural difference between the Kloosterman toy and the Apéry problem.

# 7. What framework addresses genuine mod-\(p\) Mellin zeros?

Suppose one has a canonically defined function

\[
\theta_p:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\mathbf F_p.
\]

Its finite-field Mellin coefficients are

\[
\widehat\theta_p(r)
=\sum_{t\ne0}\theta_p(t)t^{-r}.
\]

If

\[
P_p(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}c_jT^j
\]

is the unique degree-\(\le p-2\) polynomial representing \(\theta_p\) on \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), then orthogonality gives

\[
\widehat\theta_p(r)=-c_r
\]

up to the indexing convention. Thus zero counting is exactly a **coefficient anti-concentration problem** for the canonical mod-\(p\) Hasse/Cartier function.

The available frameworks are:

- Katz/Gabber--Loeser multiplicative Mellin theory for characteristic-zero \(\ell\)-adic transforms;
- rigid or crystalline cohomology for \(p\)-adic Frobenius;
- Hasse--Witt and Cartier operators for the first mod-\(p\) Frobenius layer;
- Dwork/Gross--Koblitz formulas for the expected slopes and normalized unit factors.

What these frameworks do **not** provide in general is a theorem of the form

\[
\#\{r:\widehat\theta_p(r)=0\}=O(1)
\]

from bounded rank or conductor. Rank-one characters, Legendre/Deuring polynomials, Jacobi sums, and pullback symmetries give fixed-complexity counterexamples with \(\gg p\) zero Mellin coefficients.

The missing theorem in the current Apéry proof remains what `proof.tex` calls **crystalline Mellin anti-concentration**: control of the residue of the normalized slope-zero trace across all tame Kummer branches.

# 8. Why the Apéry situation is genuinely different

For Apéry, the scalar Hasse polynomial is canonically

\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r\in\mathbf F_p[t],
\]

and

\[
b_r=-\sum_{t\ne0}A_p(t)t^{-r}
\]

for interior exponents. The values \(b_r\bmod p\) are therefore genuine integral/Hasse--Witt Mellin coefficients. No complex embedding or rounding is involved.

In the current project’s Morita/Gross--Koblitz form, the normalized first-Hasse scalar for an interior Apéry exponent is a sum

\[
\Theta_{p,r}
=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(r,p-1-r)}
 \overline U_{p,r,k}^{\,2}.
\]

There are several minimal-valuation unit terms, and

\[
p\mid b_r
\iff
\Theta_{p,r}=0.
\]

Thus Apéry zeros are genuine cancellation among several normalized units.

For Kloosterman Mellin, by contrast,

\[
\widetilde M_a
=\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac a{p-1}\right)^2
\]

is a single unit. There is nothing to cancel.

So the valid comparison is:

```text
Kloosterman:
    one normalized unit
    => zero first-Hasse failures.

Apéry:
    sum of several normalized units
    => rare but nontrivial cancellations.
```

The rounded Kloosterman statistic sits outside this comparison.

Work in \(\mathbf Z[\zeta_p]/(1-\zeta_p)\cong\mathbf F_p\).

all p-2 nontrivial Mellin coefficients are zero.

Work in the nonreduced ring

\[
\mathbf Z_p[\zeta_p]/(p)
\cong\mathbf F_p[\pi]/(\pi^{p-1}).
\]

Expected result: the least-residue-weighted moment agrees with \(g(\omega^r)^2\), and exactly half the nontrivial exponents give zero in this ring.

## Experiment IV: normalized Dwork Mellin

Compute

\[
\pi^{-2a}g(\omega^{-a})^2
\pmod\pi.
\]

Expected result: no zeros.

These four tests are mutually incompatible numerically because they are four different invariants. A program returning mean \(1\) is performing Experiment I, not II--IV.

# 10. Direct answers to the four questions

## (A) Exact relationship

If `Kl_int` means nearest-integer rounding, there is no Gauss-square relationship. The rounding error is \(p\)-adically uncontrolled.

If `Kl_int` means the exact cyclotomic algebraic integer and only the weight \(t^r\) is represented by its least residue, then after choosing the \(p\)-adic embedding

\[
\sum_t[t^r]_pK_p(t)
\equiv g(\omega^r)^2\pmod{p\mathcal O}.
\]

The target is \(\mathcal O/(p)\), not \(\mathbf F_p\).

## (B) Modular powers versus complex characters

Yes, they are not equal as characteristic-zero functions. But the modular power is congruent to the Teichmüller character modulo \(p\), so the exact cyclotomic moment still agrees with the Gauss square modulo \(p\mathcal O\). The observed discrepancy comes from rounding and from collapsing a ramified nonreduced ring to \(\mathbf F_p\), not merely from using \(t^r\bmod p\).

## (C) Framework for mod-\(p\) zeros

The correct framework is an integral overconvergent \(F\)-crystal or rigid cohomology with Kummer twists, together with its Hasse--Witt/Cartier initial form. One must specify the Frobenius lattice and remove the character-dependent Hodge factor before reducing. There is no general conductor-only zero-count theorem; the required Apéry input is crystalline Mellin anti-concentration.

## (D) \(p\)-adic Mellin transform

For Kloosterman it is completely explicit:

\[
\mathcal M_p(\mathrm{Kl}_2)(\omega^{-a})
=g(\omega^{-a})^2
=\pi^{2a}\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac a{p-1}\right)^2.
\]

After normalization, the gamma square is a unit and the exact first-Hasse zero count is \(0\). Raw mod-\((p)\) divisibility gives half the characters, while raw residue-field reduction gives all nontrivial characters.

# Final conclusion for the campaign

The “complex Mellin never vanishes versus integer Mellin has about one mod-\(p\) zero” phenomenon is not the AFP gap. It is an artifact of applying nearest-integer rounding to a cyclotomic trace.

The correct Kloosterman crystalline Mellin transform is easier than Apéry:

\[
\text{Gauss square}
=\text{known Hodge factor}\times\text{one gamma-unit square},
\]

so its normalized first-Hasse invariant never vanishes.

The actual AFP gap remains:

\[
\text{Apéry normalized Mellin trace}
=\text{sum of several minimal-slope units},
\]

and one must prove that their residues cancel for only bounded average mass across the tame-character branches. That is exactly the crystalline Mellin anti-concentration problem already isolated in the current `proof.tex`.
