# Lucas apparition calibration for the moving top-prime window

## 0. Executive verdict

Let

\[
U_0=0,\qquad U_1=1,\qquad U_{m+2}=P U_{m+1}-Q U_m,
\qquad Q\in\{\pm1\},
\]

be nondegenerate, let

\[
\Delta=P^2-4Q,
\qquad
U_m=\frac{\alpha^m-\beta^m}{\alpha-\beta},
\qquad \alpha\beta=Q,
\]

and, for a good prime \(p\nmid 2Q\Delta\), let \(\rho(p)\) denote its rank of apparition. I use \(\rho\), rather than \(\alpha(p)\), to avoid confusing the rank with the Binet root \(\alpha\). Put

\[
\varepsilon_p=\left(\frac{\Delta}{p}\right)\in\{\pm1\},
\qquad
\iota(p)=\frac{p-\varepsilon_p}{\rho(p)}.
\]

The two calibration examples are

- Fibonacci: \((P,Q,\Delta)=(1,-1,5)\);
- the non-unit-discriminant sequence \(U_{m+2}=4U_{m+1}-U_m\): \((P,Q,\Delta)=(4,1,12)\), with roots \(2\pm\sqrt3\).

The main conclusions are:

```text
HEURISTIC:
  E Z(n) -> a positive sequence-dependent constant lambda_U;
  max_{n<=X} Z(n) should be of order log X / log log X.

PROVED UNCONDITIONALLY HERE, from standard Lucas tools:
  Z(n) <<_U [tau(n-1)+tau(n+1)] sqrt(n)/log n + O_U(1),
  hence max_{n<=X} Z(n) <<_U X^(1/2+o(1));

  sum_{n<=X} Z(n) <<_U X^(3/2)/log X;
  sum_{n<=X} Z(n)^2 <<_U X^2.

ROW-PAIR CALIBRATION:
  for the canonical row window [p,2p),
  M_p = iota(p) + 1_{epsilon_p=+1},
  M_p(M_p-1) = iota(p)^2 + epsilon_p iota(p).

  The fixed-index Artin model predicts
  sum_{X<p<=2X} M_p(M_p-1) asymp_U X^2/(log X)^2,
  not Poisson scale. This is a heavy-tail row statistic.
```

The strongest honest verdict is therefore:

> **No, \(\max_n Z(n)=O(1)\) is not known, even in the fully structured Lucas setting. More importantly, the standard Artin/Poisson model predicts that it is false:** the expected maximum is \(\asymp \log X/\log\log X\). The closest classical unresolved input is the Erdős conjecture on the number of primes having a prescribed multiplicative order, in the form studied by Felix--Murty, together with an additional cross-rank alignment problem. Even that conjecture by itself would naturally yield only \(Z(n)=n^{o(1)}\), not an absolute constant.

All finitely many bad primes are absorbed in \(O_U(1)\) below.

## 1. The exact torus reduction

### 1.1 Rank as an order — **CITED / standard**

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt\Delta)\) and

\[
\gamma=\alpha/\beta\in K^\times.
\]

Then \(N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)=1\), and for every good prime \(p\), \(\rho(p)\) is the multiplicative order of the reduction of \(\gamma\) modulo either prime of \(K\) over \(p\). This is Lemma 3.4 of Sanna's index paper, referring back to his rank-divisibility paper.

If \(\varepsilon_p=+1\), the torus splits and the order divides \(p-1\). If \(\varepsilon_p=-1\), the reduction lies in the norm-one subgroup of \(\mathbb F_{p^2}^\times\), of order \(p+1\). Hence

\[
\boxed{\rho(p)\mid p-\varepsilon_p.}\tag{1.1}
\]

Also

\[
\boxed{p\mid U_m\iff \rho(p)\mid m}\tag{1.2}
\]

for every good \(p\). This is the exact group-law apparition property.

### 1.2 The moving event collapses to a divisor of \(n\pm1\) — **PROVED**

Suppose \(p\in(n/2,n]\). By (1.1),

\[
p-\varepsilon_p=\iota(p)\rho(p).
\]

Therefore

\[
\rho(p)\mid n-p
\iff
\rho(p)\mid (n-p)+(p-\varepsilon_p)
\iff
\rho(p)\mid n-\varepsilon_p.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
Z(n)=\#\left\{p\in(n/2,n]:\rho(p)\mid n-\varepsilon_p\right\}+O_U(1).
}\tag{1.3}
\]

Equivalently,

- split primes have \(\rho(p)\mid n-1\);
- inert primes have \(\rho(p)\mid n+1\).

This is the first major simplification unavailable in the general Apéry zero set: every moving zero is assigned to a divisor of one of two fixed neighboring integers.

### 1.3 Exact parameterization inside one divisor class — **PROVED**

If \(d=\rho(p)\), write

\[
p-\varepsilon_p=i d,
\qquad
n-p=k d.
\]

Then

\[
n-\varepsilon_p=(i+k)d.
\]

For fixed \(n,\varepsilon,d\mid n-\varepsilon\), every possible prime is of the form

\[
p=n-kd=\varepsilon+i d,
\qquad
0\le k<\frac{n}{2d}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The primality, exact-rank, and interval conditions are all that remain.

## 2. Heuristic size: constant mean and logarithmic extremes

### 2.1 The correct direct density theorem — **CITED, GRH**

The closest direct theorem is not merely a Kurlberg--Pomerance density-one statement. It is Sanna's theorem for the **index of appearance**.

For a fixed positive integer \(t\), define

\[
\mathcal P_U(t,x)=\{p\le x:p\nmid2Q\Delta,\ \iota(p)=t\}.
\]

Sanna proves under GRH, for nondegenerate Lucas sequences with nonsquare discriminant, that

\[
\#\mathcal P_U(t,x)
=
\delta_U(t)\frac{x}{\log x}
+O_U\!\left(
\frac{x}{\log^2x}
+
\frac{x\log(2\log x)}{\varphi(t)\log^2x}
\right)
\tag{2.1}
\]

uniformly for

\[
\boxed{x>t^3.}\tag{2.2}
\]

Moreover, outside two exceptional quadratic discriminants, he gives

\[
\delta_U(t)=A\,F_{2h}(t)G_U(t),
\tag{2.3}
\]

where \(A\) is Artin's constant, \(F_{2h}\) is explicit multiplicative, and \(G_U\) is an explicitly computable periodic rational function. On residue classes where \(G_U(t)\ne0\), the scale is

\[
\delta_U(t)\asymp_U \frac{1}{t\varphi(t)}
\tag{2.4}
\]

up to fixed local factors. This is the Lucas-torus analogue of the density of primes for which a fixed integer is a near-primitive root of index \(t\).

For Fibonacci, Cubre--Rouse compute the fixed-divisibility densities by realizing the entry point as the order of a point on the torus \(x^2-5y^2=1\). Sanna treats fixed divisibility of general Lucas ranks under mild hypotheses, and the 2026 preprint of Cera da Conceição gives explicit fixed-\(d\) Dirichlet densities for all Lucas sequences and all \(d\).

### 2.2 What Kurlberg's matrix theorem does and does not say — **CITED, GRH**

Kurlberg proves, under GRH, that a fixed hyperbolic unimodular matrix has order greater than \(p^{1-\eta}\) for a density-one set of primes. For \(Q=1\), the Lucas companion matrix is in \(\mathrm{SL}_2\); for \(Q=-1\), its square is. Its matrix order and \(\rho(p)\) differ by at most a bounded factor. Hence one obtains, under GRH,

\[
\rho(p)>p^{1-\eta}
\]

for density-one many primes.

This is useful typical-prime information, but it is not enough for the present moments. The quantities

\[
\sum\frac1{\rho(p)},
\qquad
\sum\frac1{\rho(p)^2},
\qquad
\sum \iota(p)^2
\]

are sensitive to the sparse small-rank tail. A density-zero exceptional set may dominate them completely.

Pappalardi's unconditional and GRH-conditional results for finitely generated subgroups of \(\mathbb Q^\times\) are the rational split-torus precedent. They do not directly cover a fixed element in a quadratic norm-one torus, though Kurlberg's matrix formulation does cover the hyperbolic Lucas examples on GRH.

### 2.3 Mean index and mean incidence — **HEURISTIC**

From (2.4),

\[
t\delta_U(t)\asymp_U\frac1{\varphi(t)}.
\]

Since

\[
\sum_{t\le T}\frac1{\varphi(t)}\asymp \log T,
\]

the fixed-index model predicts

\[
\mathbb E_{p\asymp x}\iota(p)\sim \kappa_U\log x
\tag{2.5}
\]

for a positive constant \(\kappa_U\) determined by the local factors in (2.3).

For a random integer \(n\) near \(x\), a row with period \(\rho(p)\) hits with frequency \(1/\rho(p)\). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E Z(n)
&\approx
\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\frac1{\rho(p)}\\
&=
\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\frac{\iota(p)}{p-\varepsilon_p}\\
&\sim
\kappa_U\log n
\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\frac1p\\
&\sim \kappa_U\log 2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Here

\[
\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\frac1p\sim\frac{\log2}{\log n}.
\]

Therefore the natural prediction is

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E Z(n)\to\lambda_U:=\kappa_U\log2,}\tag{2.7}
\]

up to a negligible contribution from the automatic event \(n=p\) when \(n\) itself is prime.

### 2.4 Extreme values — **HEURISTIC**

If the cross-prime incidences are approximately Poisson with bounded mean \(\lambda_U\), then

\[
\Pr(Z(n)\ge k)\approx \left(\frac{e\lambda_U}{k}\right)^k.
\]

Setting \(X\Pr(Z\ge k)\asymp1\) gives

\[
k\log k\sim\log X,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\max_{n\le X}Z(n)
\sim \frac{\log X}{\log\log X}
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

at first order.

Thus the Lucas calibration agrees with the Apéry Poisson picture:

- bounded average;
- slowly diverging, logarithmic-type extremes;
- not a bounded maximum.

### 2.5 Quantifier warning — **PROVED ABOUT THE CITED THEOREM**

Equation (2.1) is uniform only for \(t<x^{1/3}\). The mean in (2.5), and especially the second index moment below, receives contributions from indices as large as

\[
t\asymp \frac{x}{\log x}.
\]

Therefore the complete constant-mean and extreme-value predictions do **not** follow rigorously from Sanna's published GRH theorem. They require extrapolating the Artin density through a much larger moving range.

## 3. Cyclotomic atoms and primitive divisors

### 3.1 Homogeneous cyclotomic factorization — **PROVED / standard**

For \(d\ge2\), set

\[
\Psi_d:=\Phi_d(\alpha,\beta)
=
\prod_{\substack{1\le j\le d\\(j,d)=1}}
(\alpha-\zeta_d^j\beta).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Up to the conventional harmless signs at the first two indices, \(\Psi_d\in\mathbb Z\), and

\[
\boxed{
U_m=\prod_{\substack{d\mid m\\d>1}}\Psi_d.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Since the sequence is nondegenerate, \(\Psi_d\ne0\). Also

\[
\log|\Psi_d|
\le
\varphi(d)\log(|\alpha|+|\beta|)
\ll_U\varphi(d).
\tag{3.3}
\]

If \(p\) is good and \(\rho(p)=d\), then \(p\) is a primitive prime divisor of \(U_d\), hence

\[
\boxed{p\mid\Psi_d.}\tag{3.4}
\]

### 3.2 The crucial multiplicity bound in a large-prime window — **PROVED**

Fix \(n\), \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\), and \(d\mid n-\varepsilon\). Let

\[
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)
=
\#\{p\in(n/2,n]:\rho(p)=d,\ \varepsilon_p=\varepsilon\}.
\]

All such distinct primes divide \(\Psi_d\), so

\[
\prod_{p\text{ counted}}p\mid |\Psi_d|.
\]

Consequently

\[
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)\log(n/2)
\le \log|\Psi_d|
\ll_U\varphi(d),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)
\ll_U\frac{\varphi(d)}{\log n}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the upper-bound role of cyclotomic factorization. It is stronger than merely knowing that \(p\mid U_{n-\varepsilon}\), because it assigns the prime to one rank atom of logarithmic size \(O(\varphi(d))\).

### 3.3 What BHV and Stewart contribute — **CITED, but wrong direction for this statistic**

Bilu--Hanrot--Voutier prove that every Lucas or Lehmer term of index \(d>30\) has a primitive divisor. Thus, apart from the classified defective terms, every atom \(\Psi_d\) contains at least one new prime.

Stewart proves that the greatest prime factor of a Lucas term grows faster than its index; Bilu--Hong--Gun give an explicit uniform version.

These are **existence/lower-bound** theorems. They show that rank classes are populated and that some primitive divisor is large. They do not upper-bound

\[
\#\{p:\rho(p)=d\},
\]

nor do they prevent many primitive prime divisors of the same atom from lying in a prescribed dyadic interval. Hence neither BHV nor Stewart improves (3.5) for the present upper-bound problem.

The elementary Binet growth already gives the useful lower bound

\[
p\mid U_{\rho(p)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\le |U_{\rho(p)}|\le C_U\Lambda_U^{\rho(p)},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\rho(p)\gg_U\log p.}\tag{3.6}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\iota(p)\ll_U \frac p{\log p}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

## 4. Pointwise upper bound

### 4.1 Three independent bounds for one rank class

For \(d\mid n-\varepsilon\), there are three bounds on \(N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)\).

**Cyclotomic size:** by (3.5),

\[
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)\ll_U \frac{\varphi(d)}{\log n}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

**Integer positions:** by (1.4),

\[
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)\le 1+\frac n{2d}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

**Brun--Titchmarsh:** all primes in the class satisfy

\[
p\equiv\varepsilon\pmod d,
\]

and \((\varepsilon,d)=1\). When \(d<n/2\), the short-interval Brun--Titchmarsh theorem gives

\[
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)
\ll
\frac{n}{\varphi(d)\log(n/(2d))}
\tag{4.3}
\]

whenever the denominator is positive.

### 4.2 Uniform per-divisor estimate — **PROVED**

Let

\[
q=\frac{n-\varepsilon}{d}.
\]

If

\[
q\le \frac{4\sqrt n}{\log n},
\]

then (4.2) gives

\[
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)\ll \frac{\sqrt n}{\log n}.
\]

Otherwise \(d\ll\sqrt n\log n\), and for large \(n\),

\[
\log\frac{n}{2d}\gg\log n.
\]

Combining (4.1) and (4.3) by the geometric-mean inequality gives

\[
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)
\ll_U
\min\left(
\frac{\varphi(d)}{\log n},
\frac{n}{\varphi(d)\log n}
\right)
\ll_U \frac{\sqrt n}{\log n}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus every possible divisor rank contributes at most \(O_U(\sqrt n/\log n)\) primes.

### 4.3 Pointwise theorem — **PROVED**

Summing over divisors of \(n-1\) and \(n+1\),

\[
\boxed{
Z(n)
\ll_U
1+
\bigl(\tau(n-1)+\tau(n+1)\bigr)
\frac{\sqrt n}{\log n}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Using the classical maximal order of the divisor function,

\[
\tau(m)\le
\exp\!\left(
(\log2+o(1))\frac{\log m}{\log\log m}
\right),
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\max_{n\le X}Z(n)
\ll_U X^{1/2+o(1)}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

More precisely, (4.5) retains the saving \(1/\log n\), though that logarithm is swallowed by the conventional \(n^{o(1)}\) divisor factor.

### 4.4 Status of “best”

I did not locate a published theorem tailored to this moving statistic. Among the standard general Lucas inputs listed in the question, (4.5) is the strongest clean unconditional composite bound: BHV and Stewart are lower-bound tools, while the available Chebotarev/order theorems do not have the moving uniformity needed to improve the square-root tradeoff.

## 5. First moment

### 5.1 Exact incidence identity — **PROVED**

For a fixed good prime \(p\le X\), the integers \(n\le X\) counted by its row are

\[
n=p+j\rho(p),
\qquad
0\le j\rho(p)<p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{p\le X\\p\text{ good}}}
\left(
1+\left\lfloor
\frac{\min(p-1,X-p)}{\rho(p)}
\right\rfloor
\right)
+O_U(1).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, \(\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)\gg X/\log X\), simply from \(n=p\).

### 5.2 Dyadic rank multiplicity — **PROVED**

For \(Y<p\le2Y\), let

\[
A_d(Y)=\#\{Y<p\le2Y:\rho(p)=d\}.
\]

By (3.4), all such primes divide \(\Psi_d\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
A_d(Y)\ll_U\frac{\varphi(d)}{\log Y}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

A row has at most

\[
1+\frac{2Y}{d}
\]

points in its natural interval. Let \(D=\sqrt Y\). For \(d\le D\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{d\le D}A_d(Y)\left(1+\frac{2Y}{d}\right)
&\ll_U
\frac1{\log Y}
\sum_{d\le D}\left(\varphi(d)+Y\frac{\varphi(d)}d\right)\\
&\ll_U \frac{D^2+YD}{\log Y}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

For \(d>D\), use the prime number theorem upper bound

\[
\sum_{d>D}A_d(Y)\ll\frac{Y}{\log Y}
\]

and the row-length bound \(1+2Y/D\). This gives

\[
\sum_{Y<p\le2Y}M_p
\ll_U\frac{Y^{3/2}}{\log Y}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Dyadic summation yields

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)
\ll_U\frac{X^{3/2}}{\log X}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The Poisson/Artin prediction is much smaller:

\[
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)\sim\lambda_U X.
\]

The gap between \(X\) and \(X^{3/2}/\log X\) is exactly the absence of a sharp uniform small-rank distribution theorem.

## 6. Second moment over \(n\)

### 6.1 A divisor majorant — **PROVED**

Fix a dyadic interval \(N<n\le2N\), and put \(L=\log N\). For every rank divisor \(d\mid n\pm1\), combine the atom bound and the elementary position bound:

\[
N_{d,\varepsilon}(n)
\ll_U
\min\left(\frac dL,\frac Nd\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define

\[
w_N(d)=C_U\min\left(\frac dL,\frac Nd\right),
\qquad
R_N(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}w_N(d).
\]

Then

\[
Z(n)\ll_U1+R_N(n-1)+R_N(n+1).
\tag{6.2}
\]

It suffices to bound the mean square of \(R_N\).

### 6.2 GCD-sum calculation — **PROVED**

Expanding and counting common multiples,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{m\le3N}R_N(m)^2
&\le
3N\sum_{d,e\le3N}
\frac{w_N(d)w_N(e)}{\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Write

\[
a_d=\frac{w_N(d)}d
\ll_U
\min\left(\frac1L,\frac N{d^2}\right).
\]

Since

\[
\gcd(d,e)=\sum_{k\mid d,\,k\mid e}\varphi(k),
\]

we have

\[
\sum_{d,e}
\frac{w_N(d)w_N(e)}{\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)}
=
\sum_{k\ge1}\varphi(k)
\left(\sum_{m\ge1}a_{km}\right)^2.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Put \(D=\sqrt{NL}\). Then

\[
\sum_{m\ge1}a_{km}
\ll_U
\begin{cases}
D/(kL),&k\le D,\\
N/k^2,&k>D.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k\le D}\varphi(k)\frac{D^2}{k^2L^2}
&\ll \frac{D^2}{L^2}\sum_{k\le D}\frac1k
\ll N,\\
\sum_{k>D}\varphi(k)\frac{N^2}{k^4}
&\ll N^2\sum_{k>D}\frac1{k^3}
\ll \frac NL.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Substituting into (6.3),

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}Z(n)^2\ll_U N^2.
\]

Dyadic summation gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)^2\ll_U X^2.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

The Poisson prediction is instead

\[
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)^2
\sim (\lambda_U^2+\lambda_U)X.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus even in the Lucas model there remains a full power between a clean unconditional second-moment bound and the random-model value.

### 6.3 Cross-prime interpretation

Let

\[
A_p=\{n:p\le n<2p,\ n\equiv p\pmod{\rho(p)}\}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_n (Z(n))_2
=
\sum_{p\ne q}|A_p\cap A_q|.
\tag{6.9}
\]

For two rows, the congruences are compatible precisely when

\[
\gcd(\rho(p),\rho(q))\mid p-q.
\]

Using \(p\equiv\varepsilon_p\pmod{\rho(p)}\), this condition is automatic for equal signs, while for opposite signs it forces

\[
\gcd(\rho(p),\rho(q))\mid2.
\]

This is real Lucas-specific structure, but without a sharp distribution of the ranks and their gcds it does not currently produce the Poisson-scale bound.

## 7. The row-pair statistic requested in Question 3

### 7.1 Exact floor formula — **PROVED**

For any integer interval of offsets

\[
J=[a,a+H),
\]

the number of multiples of \(\rho(p)\) in \(J\) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
M_p(J)
=
\left\lfloor\frac{a+H-1}{\rho(p)}\right\rfloor
-
\left\lfloor\frac{a-1}{\rho(p)}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For the canonical full first-block window \(J=[0,p)\), equivalently \(n\in[p,2p)\),

\[
M_p=1+\left\lfloor\frac{p-1}{\rho(p)}\right\rfloor.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For all but finitely many good primes, \(\rho(p)>2\). Since

\[
p-\varepsilon_p=\iota(p)\rho(p),
\]

we get the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
M_p=\iota(p)+\mathbf1_{\varepsilon_p=+1}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
M_p(M_p-1)=\iota(p)^2+\varepsilon_p\iota(p).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

If

\[
\pi_{\varepsilon,t}(X)
=
\#\{X<p\le2X:\varepsilon_p=\varepsilon,\ \iota(p)=t\},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X}M_p(M_p-1)
=
\sum_{t\ge1}
\bigl[t(t+1)\pi_{+,t}(X)+t(t-1)\pi_{-,t}(X)\bigr]
+O_U(1).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is the exact answer under any supplied index-distribution theorem.

### 7.2 A sharp small-rank counting function

Define

\[
\mathcal A_X(y)
=
\#\{X<p\le2X:\rho(p)\le y\}.
\]

The cyclotomic atoms immediately give the unconditional bound

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_X(y)\log X
&\le
\sum_{d\le y}\log|\Psi_d|\\
&\ll_U \sum_{d\le y}\varphi(d)
\ll_U y^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_X(y)\ll_U\frac{y^2}{\log X}.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Together with \(\rho(p)\gg_U\log X\), partial summation gives

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac1{\rho(p)^2}\ll_U1,
\qquad
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac1{\rho(p)}\ll_U\frac X{\log X}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Since \(M_p\ll1+X/\rho(p)\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X}M_p(M_p-1)\ll_U X^2.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

This is unconditional.

### 7.3 Artin-density prediction: a heavy-tailed second row moment — **HEURISTIC**

The fixed-index density has scale

\[
\delta_U(t)\asymp_U\frac1{t\varphi(t)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{t\le T}t\delta_U(t)\asymp_U\log T,
\qquad
\sum_{t\le T}t^2\delta_U(t)\asymp_U T.
\tag{7.9}
\]

By (3.7), the natural cutoff is

\[
T\asymp_U\frac X{\log X}.
\]

If (2.1) were available with sufficient aggregate uniformity throughout this full range, then (7.5) would give

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X}M_p(M_p-1)
\asymp_U\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2}.
}
\tag{7.10}
\]

This large value is not a contradiction to the constant-mean Poisson prediction for \(Z(n)\). It counts **two points in the same prime row**, and is dominated by rare primes with unusually small rank and very long rows.

By contrast,

\[
\sum_n(Z(n))_2
\]

counts **two distinct prime rows meeting at the same integer**. These are transposed Gram statistics and have very different tails.

### 7.4 The minimal distribution theorem needed

The Artin model predicts

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_X(y)\asymp_U\frac y{\log X}
}
\tag{7.11}
\]

for \(\log X\ll_U y\ll X^{1-o(1)}\), with local corrections. Indeed, \(\rho(p)\le y\) is equivalent to \(\iota(p)\gtrsim X/y\), and the density tail of \(\iota\) is \(\asymp1/t\).

A clean sufficient upper-bound theorem is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_X(y)
\ll_U
\frac{y\,X^{o(1)}}{\log X}
\quad
(c_U\log X\le y\le2X).
}
\tag{7.12}
\]

Partial summation would then yield

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}M_p(M_p-1)
\ll_U
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{(\log X)^2}.
\tag{7.13}
\]

Equivalently, one could assume a fixed-index estimate uniform through the whole natural range:

\[
\#\{X<p\le2X:\iota(p)=t\}
\ll_U
\frac{X}{t\varphi(t)\log X}
\quad
\left(1\le t\ll_U\frac X{\log X}\right).
\tag{7.14}
\]

No theorem cited here supplies (7.12) or (7.14), unconditionally or on the usual GRH. Sanna's uniformity \(t<X^{1/3}\) only reaches ranks \(\rho(p)>X^{2/3}\), while (7.10) is controlled by much larger indices and ranks down to logarithmic size.

Kurlberg's density-one theorem is also insufficient: a sparse exceptional set can dominate the square-weight \(\iota(p)^2\).

## 8. Chebotarev and large-sieve audit

### 8.1 What Chebotarev actually encodes — **CITED**

Let

\[
K_m=K(\zeta_m,\gamma^{1/m}).
\]

Sanna proves that

\[
m\mid\iota(p)
\]

is equivalent to a specified Frobenius condition in \(K_m/\mathbb Q\). The relevant fields satisfy, uniformly,

\[
[K_m:\mathbb Q]\gg_U m\varphi(m),
\qquad
\log|\Delta_{K_m}|\ll_U m\varphi(m)\log(2m).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Hooley-style inclusion--exclusion plus effective Chebotarev under GRH then gives the fixed-index theorem (2.1).

### 8.2 The condition \(\rho(p)\mid m\) is not a positive-density Chebotarev condition

For fixed \(m\),

\[
\rho(p)\mid m
\iff
p\mid U_m.
\]

Thus only finitely many primes satisfy it. It is a prime-divisor condition on one fixed algebraic integer, not a splitting condition of positive density in a fixed extension.

This distinction matters. Chebotarev is naturally suited to

- \(d\mid\iota(p)\);
- \(d\mid\rho(p)\);
- \(\iota(p)=t\);
- maximal or near-maximal order.

The moving condition \(\rho(p)\mid n-\varepsilon_p\) is instead controlled by the factorization of the terms \(U_{n\pm1}\), and more sharply by their cyclotomic atoms.

### 8.3 Why a large sieve for Frobenius does not presently close the gap

A large sieve over the Kummer fields could average several divisibility conditions, but the degrees and discriminants in (8.1) grow with the moving parameter. To reach the row-pair range one needs indices up to \(X/\log X\), far beyond the range in which termwise effective Chebotarev errors can be summed.

For the pointwise statistic, one additionally needs simultaneous control over all divisor ranks of \(n-1\) and \(n+1\), not merely the density of one fixed index. Thus even a strong fixed-\(t\) theorem does not automatically control the worst \(n\).

### Transfer summary

The Lucas pointwise bound (4.5) uses two ingredients simultaneously:

1. the rank group law, to reduce every event to a divisor of \(n\pm1\);
2. the cyclotomic atom, to bound how many large primes can realize one rank.

The first has only partial Apéry substitutes, and the second is structurally absent. Therefore the Lucas square-root proof does not currently transfer.

## 10. Final calibration verdict

### 10.1 What is proved today

For every fixed nondegenerate unit-determinant Lucas sequence of the stated type,

\[
Z(n)\ll_U
\bigl(\tau(n-1)+\tau(n+1)\bigr)rac{\sqrt n}{\log n}+O_U(1),
\]

and hence

\[
\max_{n\le X}Z(n)\ll_U X^{1/2+o(1)}.
\]

Also

\[
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)\ll_U\frac{X^{3/2}}{\log X},
\qquad
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)^2\ll_U X^2.
\]

These are unconditional consequences of the fixed-torus, rank, cyclotomic, prime-distribution, and divisor technologies.

### 10.2 What is expected

The direct Lucas Artin-density model predicts

\[
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)\sim\lambda_U X,
\qquad
\sum_{n\le X}Z(n)^2\sim(\lambda_U+\lambda_U^2)X,
\]

and

\[
\max_{n\le X}Z(n)\asymp\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}.
\]

Thus \(O(1)\) for the maximum is not the expected truth.

### 10.3 The classical open problem exposed by the calibration

For a fixed integer \(g\), Erdős conjectured that

\[
\#\{p:\operatorname{ord}_p(g)=r\}\ll_\eta r^\eta
\]

for every \(\eta>0\). Felix--Murty analyze this conjecture and its relation to Artin's primitive-root conjecture, GRH, and ABC.

The natural Lucas-torus analog is

\[
\#\{p:\rho_U(p)=d\}\ll_{U,\eta}d^\eta.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Even assuming (10.1), the divisor collapse gives only

\[
Z(n)
\le
\sum_{d\mid n-1}d^\eta+
\sum_{d\mid n+1}d^\eta
=n^{o(1)},
\]

after letting \(\eta\downarrow0\). It does not give an absolute constant, because many different ranks may align at one \(n\).

So the fully structured Lucas problem contains two layers:

1. **fixed-rank multiplicity**, already in the orbit of the classical Erdős fixed-order conjecture;
2. **cross-rank alignment along the two divisor lattices \(n-1,n+1\)**, which is additional.

Artin's primitive-root conjecture is only the first, much weaker, density question: it concerns index \(1\). Hooley/Sanna GRH methods settle fixed indices but not the moving high-index tail and not the worst integer \(n\).

A useful neighboring warning is the shifted-prime divisor function

\[
\omega^*(m)=\#\{p:p-1\mid m\},
\]

whose maximal order is known to be unbounded and very large; see Fan--Pollack, arXiv:2510.14167. Although \(\omega^*\) is not identical to \(Z\), it shows that even highly structured “prime order divides one integer” functions need not have bounded maxima.

> **Final verdict:** the Lucas structure converts the moving zero problem into an explicit classical order/divisor problem and yields genuine unconditional square-root and moment bounds. It does not make the pointwise extreme problem trivial. An \(O(1)\) maximum is neither proved nor heuristically correct. The expected target is logarithmic extreme size, and proving even a strong polylogarithmic or \(n^{o(1)}\) pointwise bound uniformly would require order-multiplicity and cross-rank distribution input beyond current Artin/GRH technology.
