# All-degree moving-zero saturation: an exact pointwise no-go for universal direct carriers

## Executive verdict

I do **not** obtain the desired unconditional estimate

\[
 \log \operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}(A_n)=o(n).
\]

I do obtain a new exact theorem that closes a substantially broader carrier class than the previously ruled-out bounded tensor/exterior-power constructions.

For each fixed \(n\), form the universal algebraic ideal expressing, simultaneously for every candidate prime \(p\in(n/2,n]\), only the local implication

\[
 p\mid A_{n-p}.
\]

Then evaluation at the actual Apéry coefficients has image **exactly**

\[
 R_n\mathbf Z,
 \qquad
 R_n:=\prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid A_{n-p}}}p.
\]

The same exact image survives after quotienting by the full Apéry recurrence and reducing an arbitrary finite window to the two initial-state coordinates. This is an all-degree statement: polynomial degree, resultant size, and recurrence window may depend arbitrarily on \(n\). In particular, slowly growing finite-difference, creative-telescoping, determinant, resultant, Smith, Fitting, symmetric-power, and exterior-power constructions are all included whenever their divisibility proof is formal from the one target equation and universal recurrence identities.

Thus the natural direct-carrier formulation is not merely missing a clever saturation: its fully saturated evaluation quotient is the unknown radical itself. Local algebra can recover \(R_n\) exactly, but it supplies no independent height bound for it.

There is also an explicit target-blind identity:

\[
 \boxed{
 R_n=\gcd\!\left(
     \Pi_n,
     \left\{\frac{\Pi_n}{q}A_{n-q}:q\in\mathcal P_n\right\}
     \right),
 }
 \tag{E}
\]

where

\[
 \mathcal P_n=\{q\text{ prime}:n/2<q\le n\},
 \qquad
 \Pi_n=\prod_{q\in\mathcal P_n}q.
\]

Every integer in (E) has logarithmic height \(\Theta(n)\); after exact gcd saturation, the remaining integer is precisely \(R_n\). Consequently (E) is an exact deterministic carrier and a rigorous linear-height audit, but not an \(o(n)\) proof.

This answers the question in the sense of option **(iii)**, together with the exact carrier identity requested in option **(ii)**. It does **not** rule out a genuinely new identity special to the distinguished binomial sum that supplies a second target-selective equation or an independently proved Archimedean cancellation.

## 2. Direct binomial reduction in the top half

Write

\[
 A_m=\sum_{k=0}^m\binom{m}{k}^2\binom{m+k}{k}^2.
\]

Fix a prime \(p\), let \(0\le r<p\), and put \(m=p+r\). Splitting the summation index as

\[
 k=ap+b,
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\},\quad 0\le b<p,
\]

and applying Lucas term by term gives

\[
 \binom{p+r}{ap+b}\equiv \binom1a\binom rb\pmod p.
\]

For the second binomial factor, the two nonvanishing blocks have leading digits \(1\) and \(2\), so their squared weights add to

\[
 1^2+2^2=5.
\]

The terms whose lower digit creates a carry vanish on both sides. Hence

\[
 \boxed{A_{p+r}\equiv5A_r\pmod p.}
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Now fix \(n>10\) and \(p\in\mathcal P_n\). Then \(p\ge7\), so \(5\) is a unit, and with \(r_p=n-p\) one has

\[
 \boxed{
 p\mid A_n
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p\mid A_{r_p}.
 }
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The possible odd boundary \(n=2p-1\), for which \(r_p=p-1\), is automatically absent. Indeed, for \(1\le k\le p-1\),

\[
 p\mid\binom{p-1+k}{k},
\]

so every nonzero-\(k\) summand in \(A_{p-1}\) is divisible by \(p^2\), while the \(k=0\) term is \(1\). Thus

\[
 A_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^2}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
 R_n=\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}(A_n)
    =\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_n\\p\mid A_{r_p}}}p.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

No averaging or zero-count estimate has entered.

## 3. The moving-zero carrier ideal

Let

\[
 M=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 \mathscr R_n=\mathbf Z[X_0,\ldots,X_M].
\]

For each candidate prime \(p\in\mathcal P_n\), define

\[
 \mathfrak j_p=(p,X_{r_p})\subset\mathscr R_n,
 \qquad r_p=n-p.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

A polynomial belongs to \(\mathfrak j_p\) exactly when its reduction modulo \(p\) vanishes formally after imposing the single target equation \(X_{r_p}=0\). Define the simultaneous carrier ideal

\[
 \mathfrak I_n=\bigcap_{p\in\mathcal P_n}\mathfrak j_p.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Let

\[
 \varepsilon_A:\mathscr R_n\longrightarrow\mathbf Z,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_A(X_j)=A_j
 \tag{3.3}
\]

be evaluation at the actual Apéry coefficients.

### Theorem 3.1 — exact all-degree evaluation image

For every integer \(n>10\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \varepsilon_A(\mathfrak I_n)=R_n\mathbf Z.
 }
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbf Z/\varepsilon_A(\mathfrak I_n)
 \cong \mathbf Z/R_n\mathbf Z.
 }
 \tag{3.5}
\]

There is no restriction on polynomial degree.

### Proof

For distinct candidate primes \(p\ne q\), the ideals \(\mathfrak j_p\) and \(\mathfrak j_q\) are comaximal, because integers \(a,b\) exist with

\[
 ap+bq=1.
\]

Hence

\[
 \mathfrak I_n
 =\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}\mathfrak j_p.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

Evaluation is a ring homomorphism, and the image of a product of ideals is the product of their image ideals. Therefore

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \varepsilon_A(\mathfrak I_n)
 &=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}
      \varepsilon_A(\mathfrak j_p)\\
 &=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}(p,A_{r_p})\mathbf Z.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.7}
\]

Because \(p\) is prime,

\[
 (p,A_{r_p})\mathbf Z
 =
 \begin{cases}
 p\mathbf Z,&p\mid A_{r_p},\\
 \mathbf Z,&p\nmid A_{r_p}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{3.8}
\]

Multiplying (3.8) over all candidates gives exactly \(R_n\mathbf Z\). ∎

### Interpretation

The theorem says more than “every target prime divides every carrier.” It computes the completely saturated arithmetic content of **all** such carriers. The smallest positive generator of the evaluation image is exactly the radical one is trying to bound.

Thus arbitrary gcds of minors, Smith normalization, Fitting ideals, elimination, resultants, discriminants, or degree-growing polynomial combinations cannot produce a new arithmetic invariant merely by exploiting the equations \(X_{r_p}=0\): their final primitive evaluation ideal is already \(R_n\mathbf Z\).

## 4. An explicit deterministic carrier identity

Put

\[
 \Pi_n=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}p.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The product description (3.6) contains the following target-blind generators:

\[
 \Pi_n,
 \qquad
 \frac{\Pi_n}{q}X_{r_q}\quad(q\in\mathcal P_n).
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Evaluating (4.2) gives

\[
 \Pi_n,
 \qquad
 G_{n,q}:=\frac{\Pi_n}{q}A_{n-q}.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

For a candidate prime \(p\):

- if \(p\mid A_{n-p}\), then \(p\) divides \(\Pi_n\) and every \(G_{n,q}\); for \(q=p\), the missing factor \(p\) is supplied by \(A_{n-p}\);
- if \(p\nmid A_{n-p}\), then
  \[
  p\nmid G_{n,p}=\frac{\Pi_n}{p}A_{n-p}.
  \]

Since the first entry \(\Pi_n\) has no prime factors outside \(\mathcal P_n\), this proves the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{
 R_n=\gcd\left(\Pi_n,\{G_{n,q}:q\in\mathcal P_n\}\right).
 }
 \tag{4.4}
\]

A direct symbolic audit is therefore:

```python
P = [q for q in primes if n / 2 < q <= n]
Pi = product(P)
carrier = gcd(Pi, *(Pi // q * apery(n - q) for q in P))
target = product(q for q in P if apery(n - q) % q == 0)
assert carrier == target
```

This code is only a transcription of the prime-by-prime proof; the identity itself is exact for every \(n>10\).

## 5. Exact recurrence saturation, with arbitrary degree and window

The preceding theorem used independent coefficient variables. One might hope that imposing every exact Apéry recurrence relation creates additional low-height elimination content. It does not.

Let

\[
 P(m)=34m^3+51m^2+27m+5.
\]

For an arbitrary solution \(u_m\) of the Apéry recurrence, put

\[
 U_m=(m!)^3u_m.
\]

Then

\[
 U_{m+1}=P(m)U_m-m^6U_{m-1}.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Define integral linear forms in two state variables by

\[
 L_0(S,T)=S,
 \qquad
 L_1(S,T)=T,
\]

\[
 L_{m+1}(S,T)=P(m)L_m(S,T)-m^6L_{m-1}(S,T).
 \tag{5.2}
\]

For the distinguished Apéry state \(s_A=(1,5)\),

\[
 L_m(1,5)=(m!)^3A_m.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Write

\[
 L_m=\alpha_mS+\beta_mT.
\]

The adjacent determinant is exact:

\[
 \boxed{
 \alpha_m\beta_{m+1}-\alpha_{m+1}\beta_m=(m!)^6.
 }
 \tag{5.4}
\]

Indeed, the determinant at \(m=0\) is \(1\), and (5.2) multiplies it by \(m^6\) at the next step. Since \(r_p<p\), equation (5.4) shows that \(L_{r_p}\) is a nonzero linear form modulo every candidate \(p\).

Define the recurrence-saturated target ideal

\[
 \mathfrak K_n
 =\bigcap_{p\in\mathcal P_n}(p,L_{r_p})
 \subset\mathbf Z[S,T].
 \tag{5.5}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — full recurrence quotient remains exact

For every \(n>10\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{ev}_{(1,5)}(\mathfrak K_n)=R_n\mathbf Z.
 }
 \tag{5.6}
\]

Moreover, for every degree cap \(D\ge1\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{ev}_{(1,5)}
 \bigl(\mathfrak K_n\cap\mathbf Z[S,T]_{\le D}\bigr)
 =R_n\mathbf Z.
 }
 \tag{5.7}
\]

For every homogeneous degree \(d\ge1\), the same statement holds with homogeneous binary forms of degree \(d\).

### Proof

The ideals \((p,L_{r_p})\) are pairwise comaximal because their integer primes are distinct. Therefore their intersection is their product. Evaluation gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{ev}_{(1,5)}(\mathfrak K_n)
 &=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}
    (p,L_{r_p}(1,5))\mathbf Z\\
 &=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}
    (p,(r_p!)^3A_{r_p})\mathbf Z.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5.8}
\]

Since \(r_p<p\), the factorial \(r_p!\) is a \(p\)-unit. Thus

\[
 (p,(r_p!)^3A_{r_p})=(p,A_{r_p}),
\]

and (5.6) follows exactly as in Theorem 3.1.

For the degree-capped statement, the ideal contains

\[
 \Pi_n
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \frac{\Pi_n}{q}L_{r_q}(S,T)
 \qquad(q\in\mathcal P_n),
 \tag{5.9}
\]

which already have degree at most one and whose evaluations have gcd \(R_n\). Every evaluation is divisible by \(R_n\), so (5.7) follows.

For homogeneous degree \(d\), multiply the two expressions in (5.9) by \(S^d\) and \(S^{d-1}\), respectively. Since \(S(1,5)=1\), their evaluations are unchanged. ∎

### Consequence for growing windows

Any polynomial expression in an arbitrary finite recurrence window

\[
 U_{j_1},\ldots,U_{j_w}
\]

reduces by (5.2) to a polynomial in \(S,T\). The window length \(w=w(n)\) and total degree \(d=d(n)\) may grow arbitrarily. If the proposed divisibility is proved universally from

\[
 U_{r_p}=0\pmod p,
\]

then the reduced polynomial lies in \((p,L_{r_p})\) for every candidate \(p\), hence in \(\mathfrak K_n\). Theorem 5.1 applies with no bounded-order hypothesis.

This is the precise extension beyond the already closed bounded tensor/exterior-power route.

## 6. Height ledger

The explicit carrier family (4.4) is selective but linearly tall.

By the prime number theorem,

\[
 \log\Pi_n
 =\vartheta(n)-\vartheta(n/2)
 =\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Also \(A_r\ge1\), so for every candidate \(q\),

\[
 G_{n,q}\ge\frac{\Pi_n}{q},
\]

and consequently

\[
 \log G_{n,q}
 \ge\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n-O(\log n).
 \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus none of the canonical target-blind generators has sublinear logarithmic height.

For an elementary upper bound, use

\[
 \binom rk\le2^r,
 \qquad
 \binom{r+k}{k}\le2^{r+k}\le2^{2r}.
\]

Then

\[
 A_r\le(r+1)2^{6r}.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

Because \(r=n-q<n/2\), equations (6.1) and (6.3) give

\[
 \log G_{n,q}
 \le
 \left(\frac12+3\log2+o(1)\right)n.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

Using the standard Apéry asymptotic

\[
 \log A_r=r\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log(r+2)),
\]

one can sharpen (6.4) to

\[
 \log G_{n,q}
 \le
 \left(
   \frac12+rac12\log(17+12\sqrt2)+o(1)
 \right)n.
 \tag{6.5}
\]

So the exact deterministic family has height \(\exp(\Theta(n))\), and exact saturation reduces it to \(R_n\), whose height is the original unknown.

For a general homogeneous binary carrier

\[
 F(S,T)=\sum_{j=0}^d c_jS^{d-j}T^j,
 \qquad
 H(F)=\max_j|c_j|,
\]

one has

\[
 |F(1,5)|
 \le(d+1)H(F)5^d.
 \tag{6.6}
\]

If \(F\in\mathfrak K_n\) and \(F(1,5)\ne0\), then Theorem 5.1 gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \log R_n
 \le
 \log H(F)+d\log5+O(\log(d+1)).
 }
 \tag{6.7}
\]

Therefore a construction with

\[
 \log H(F)=o(n),
 \qquad
 d=o(n),
 \qquad
 F(1,5)\ne0
\]

would indeed solve the top-half problem. The saturation theorem identifies exactly what is missing: neither target-line ideal membership nor the full recurrence provides the required coefficient bound and nonzero cancellation. Such a bound would be new arithmetic information about the distinguished state.

## 7. What is ruled out, and what remains open

### Covered by the no-go theorem

The following constructions are covered whenever their target divisibility is proved only by the formal equation \(A_{n-p}=0\pmod p\), together with identities valid for every recurrence state:

1. polynomial and integer-valued-polynomial carriers of arbitrary degree;
2. determinants, minors, Smith/Fitting divisors, exterior and symmetric powers;
3. resultants, discriminants, subresultants, and elimination ideals;
4. finite differences and creative-telescoping certificates after the summation variables have been eliminated;
5. windows and orders growing slowly, or even arbitrarily, with \(n\);
6. arbitrary gcd and Bézout saturation of all such carriers.

For degree less than \(p\), even a merely functional statement that a polynomial vanishes on every state with \(L_{r_p}=0\) forces algebraic divisibility by \(L_{r_p}\): after a linear change of coordinates, the restriction is a one-variable polynomial of degree \(<p\) vanishing at all \(p\) points. Thus every slowly growing degree \(d=o(n)\) is automatically inside the algebraic ideal formulation for all top-half candidates once \(n\) is large.

### Not ruled out

The theorem deliberately does not exclude:

1. a second congruence special to the actual binomial summand, involving for example \(A_{n-p}/p\), a derivative, or another independently nonzero coordinate;
2. a cross-prime identity coupling different residues \(n-p\) before taking products or gcds;
3. an actual-state-specific signed cancellation whose smallness is proved independently of the target radical;
4. a genuinely new diagonal/Frobenius identity that does more than recover the one Mellin coefficient equation \(A_{n-p}=0\pmod p\).

Any successful direct attack must enter one of these four categories. Merely increasing the order of a universal resultant or telescope does not escape Theorems 3.1 and 5.1.
