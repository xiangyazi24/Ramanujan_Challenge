# CPZD fixed polynomial for Apéry numbers

## Summary

The CPZD target is extremely strong and there is no known example among non-degenerate Picard-Fuchs coefficient sequences where a fixed polynomial detects all large prime divisors of coefficients. For Apéry numbers the current evidence points away from a fixed polynomial detector: the zeros are governed by varying Frobenius data rather than a fixed algebraic divisor in the index variable.

## 1. Known precedents

No standard Picard-Fuchs sequence is known to satisfy such a law. Classical examples behave differently. For Lucas sequences arising from algebraic groups, divisibility is controlled by periods/ranks of apparition. Fibonacci has a detector because the rank condition forces a divisibility relation in the index. This is exceptional group structure, not generic Picard-Fuchs behavior.

## 2. Central binomial coefficients

For $u_n=\binom{2n}{n}$, Kummer gives a digit criterion. The set of n modulo p for which p divides $u_n$ is large and varies with p. A fixed degree-d polynomial has at most d roots modulo a prime p (outside finitely many bad primes), so it cannot detect a set of size proportional to p. Hence no fixed polynomial detector exists.

## 3. Size of $R_r^+$ for Apéry numbers

The observed small number of large prime factors is compatible with heuristics such as random Frobenius trace behavior, but it does not imply a CPZD law. A bound like $\log R_r^+=O(r)$ would be much weaker and plausible from general height heuristics. CPZD would require those primes to lie in a fixed algebraic set in the index variable, which is a qualitatively different phenomenon.

## 4. Finite collections of Apéry sequences

Replacing F by a finite product of related sequences is more plausible, but the Casoratian shows the obvious companion does not work: at a hit $p|b_r$ one has

$$a_r b_{r-1}\equiv 6/r^3\pmod p,$$

so p cannot divide $a_r$ or $b_{r-1}$. A finite-dimensional recurrence module is unlikely to contain a universal detector unless there is additional motivic structure.

## 5. Algebraic functions

A norm from a fixed number field reduces to a polynomial condition after taking norms. Function-field dependence would only help if the detector were allowed to vary with the prime or with Frobenius, which is precisely outside CPZD.

## 6. Third independent solution

The recurrence has a two-dimensional solution space. There is no third linearly independent scalar solution. Any new detector must come from an extension, a derivative/jet, or extra geometric structure, not another recurrence solution.

## 7. Moving zero d(p)

The reflection pair $d,p-1-d$ explains the symmetry of $Z_p$, but d(p) is a Frobenius-dependent quantity. There is no evidence that d(p) satisfies a fixed polynomial equation over $\mathbb F_p$. It is better viewed as a root of a characteristic polynomial of a varying Frobenius operator.

## Conclusion

CPZD is likely false in its literal fixed-polynomial form. The more realistic replacement is an AQJS-type statement: the Frobenius/jet data at hits satisfy enough cross-prime relations to prevent concentration. The missing structure is therefore not a polynomial detector but a geometric coupling of moving Frobenius zeros.
