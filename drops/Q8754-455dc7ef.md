# Apéry quotient concentration: the displayed hyperplane is universal, not probabilistic

## Executive verdict

The proposed concentration route does **not** presently yield LGTQ-3. Its main observable is saturated one level earlier than the question assumes:

\[
\Delta_{s,t}p_0\beta_0-\Delta_{0,t}D_s p_1\beta_1+
\Delta_{0,s}D_t p_2\beta_2=0
\]

is not an unlikely relation among three generic large integers. After substituting
\(p_j\beta_j=b_{u_j}\), it is exactly the universal order-two
Plücker/transfer identity among the three Apéry values. It holds for every
base index and every candidate affine denominator, whether or not any
\(p_j\mid b_{u_j}\). The hit conditions merely make the three rational
quotients integral. Thus the conditional probability of this hyperplane
relation is **one**, not a small factor.

There are two further exact corrections.

1. The quotients are not uniformly distributed modulo fixed small primes in
   even the weakest literal sense: every \(b_m\) is odd, so every
   \(b_d/p\) at an odd hit prime is odd; modulo \(3\) the quotient is always
   a unit and has an explicit base-three digit formula.
2. The asserted shift
   \(b_{d+p}\equiv b_d\pmod {p^3}\) is false for general \(d\).
   The arbitrary-digit theorem is only a first-jet congruence modulo
   \(p^2\):
   \[
   b_{d+pm}\equiv (b_d+pm\dot b_d)b_m\pmod {p^2}.
   \]
   At a hit it exposes a new free coordinate \(\dot b_d\), rather than
   propagating \(b_d/p\) unchanged.

Accordingly the answers are:

| Question | Status | Exact reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Distribution of \(\beta\) | **PARTIAL / open** | Fixed-auxiliary residues have explicit digital biases; no theorem controls the own-prime first digit conditional on \(p\mid b_d\). |
| 2. Use the linear identity | **NO as stated** | The identity is recurrence-universal and creates no additional codimension. |
| 3. Propagate \(p^2\)-data | **NO from current Dwork theory** | The quoted \(p^3\) shift is false; the correct lift introduces the independent first jet. |
| 4. Smooth variation in \(u_0\) | **NO by itself** | The smooth rational extension lies identically in the same hyperplane; simultaneous integrality is discontinuous. |
| 5. Convex/lattice count | **NO by itself** | The hyperplane slice contains exponentially many positive lattice points; imposing the Apéry curve restores the original divisibility problem. |
| 6. Positivity and size | **NO from these inputs** | The signed balance and its asymptotic sizes hold for every Apéry state, not specifically for hits. |

The constructive replacement is a two-stage program:

1. produce an **Apéry-specific quotient-jet separation law** not contained in
   the saturated recurrence module;
2. prove a **cross-characteristic quotient-jet large sieve** for that new
   law.

Concentration is potentially useful only in stage 2. At present stage 1 is
missing.

## 1. The universal-hyperplane saturation

Fix one gap pattern and write

\[
u_0=u,\qquad u_1=u+s,\qquad u_2=u+t.
\]

Q8740's elimination gives an identity of the form

\[
A_0(u)b_{u_0}+A_1(u)b_{u_1}+A_2(u)b_{u_2}=0,
\tag{1.1}
\]

where

\[
A_0=\Delta_{s,t},\qquad
A_1=-\Delta_{0,t}D_s,\qquad
A_2=\Delta_{0,s}D_t.
\]

This is the unique rank-two transfer/Plücker dependence among three
solution evaluations. It is an identity in the recurrence module.

For arbitrary nonzero integers \(m_0,m_1,m_2\), define rational numbers

\[
\widetilde\beta_j=\frac{b_{u_j}}{m_j}.
\]

Substitution in (1.1) gives

\[
A_0m_0\widetilde\beta_0+A_1m_1\widetilde\beta_1+
A_2m_2\widetilde\beta_2=0
\tag{1.2}
\]

with no divisibility assumption. Taking \(m_j=p_j\) shows that the displayed
quotient identity already holds throughout the entire candidate set. A
three-hit constellation is distinguished only by

\[
\widetilde\beta_j\in\mathbb Z\quad(j=0,1,2).
\]

### Consequence

The heuristic

> “three enormous generic integers rarely satisfy one bounded-coefficient
> relation”

uses the wrong ambient measure. The Apéry vector is never sampled from a
three-dimensional box. It is confined deterministically to the rank-two
solution plane, and the coefficients in (1.1) are exactly its annihilator.
The appropriate generic model would already be conditioned on (1.2), in
which case this relation contributes no saving.

This remains true after reduction modulo an auxiliary prime, after an
Archimedean normalization, or after restricting to positive values. Each is
only another image of the same identity.

## 2. What is known about \(\beta_{p,d}=b_d/p\)

There are three different distribution questions, and they should not be
conflated.

### 2.1 Archimedean size

Put

\[
\lambda=17+12\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^4.
\]

The standard Poincaré asymptotic is

\[
b_d=\kappa\lambda^d d^{-3/2}(1+O(d^{-1}))
\]

for a positive constant \(\kappa\). Hence, for a hit prime \(p\asymp d\),

\[
\beta_{p,d}\asymp \frac{\lambda^d}{p\,d^{3/2}}.
\]

For gaps \(s=O(\log n)\),

\[
\frac{\beta_{p',d+s}}{\beta_{p,d}}
=\lambda^s\frac{p}{p'}
\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\right),
\tag{2.1}
\]

so the nearby quotients lie near a deterministic exponential ray. They are
not three comparably distributed independent samples. Since (1.1) annihilates
the exact solution, its coefficients automatically match this dominant-mode
behavior.

### 2.2 Reduction modulo a fixed auxiliary prime \(\ell\ne p\)

Gessel's Apéry--Lucas theorem gives, for the base-\(\ell\) digits
\(d=\sum d_i\ell^i\),

\[
b_d\equiv\prod_i b_{d_i}\pmod\ell.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{p,d}\equiv p^{-1}\prod_i b_{d_i}\pmod\ell.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

This is a digital/automatic law, not an a priori uniform random variable.
Two exact biases are immediate.

#### Modulo 2

Since

\[
b_0\equiv b_1\equiv1\pmod2,
\]

(2.2) gives \(b_d\equiv1\pmod2\) for every \(d\). Every relevant hit
prime is odd, hence

\[
\boxed{\beta_{p,d}\equiv1\pmod2.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

So uniformity modulo \(2\) is maximally false.

#### Modulo 3

The digit values are

\[
b_0\equiv1,\qquad b_1\equiv-1,\qquad b_2\equiv1\pmod3.
\]

If \(\nu_1(d)\) is the number of base-three digits of \(d\) equal to
\(1\), then

\[
b_d\equiv(-1)^{\nu_1(d)}\pmod3.
\]

In particular no Apéry number is divisible by \(3\), and for a hit prime
\(p\ne3\),

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{p,d}\equiv p^{-1}(-1)^{\nu_1(d)}\pmod3,}
\tag{2.5}
\]

which is always a unit. Again there is an exact bias.

For a general fixed \(\ell\), the unconditional residue sequence is
\(\ell\)-automatic through (2.2). What is unknown is the **mixed-prime
conditional correlation** obtained by restricting \(d\) to

\[
Z_p=\{0\le d<p:p\mid b_d\}.
\]

Neither Lucas theory nor the present K3/Frobenius description proves that
this restriction is equidistributed among the digitally allowed classes.

### 2.3 The own-prime first digit \(\beta_{p,d}\bmod p\)

This is the genuinely new local datum. Conditional uniformity in
\(\mathbb F_p\) is a reasonable random-map heuristic, but no theorem of
this strength is known. It can be zero: for example

\[
b_3=1445=5\cdot17^2,
\]

so at \((p,d)=(17,3)\), \(b_d/p\equiv0\pmod p\).

The exact binomial expansion expresses this quotient through a divided
Frobenius polynomial and a growing family of Fermat quotients. Its literal
complexity is \(\Theta(p)\) in the bulk; it does not collapse to a fixed
bounded-degree trace function. Thus current K3 mod-\(p\) information controls
the zero event, but not the first lift after that zero.

Most importantly, even perfect marginal uniformity of
\(\beta_{p,d}\bmod p\) would not by itself reduce LGTQ-3: the three quotients
live in three different residue fields, while the integer identity involves
their reductions at the *other* carrier primes.

## 3. Why the quotient identity gives no density saving

Suppose one reduces the exact identity modulo a fixed small \(\ell\). Equation
(2.3) simply converts it back into the Apéry recurrence identity modulo
\(\ell\). It is true for every candidate \(u\), not only hit triples.
There is no factor \(1/\ell\).

Now reduce it modulo one carrier, say \(p_0\). The first term vanishes and one
gets

\[
A_1p_1\left(\frac{b_{u_1}}{p_1}\right)
+A_2p_2\left(\frac{b_{u_2}}{p_2}\right)
\equiv0\pmod {p_0}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because \(p_1,p_2\ne p_0\), this is a relation between the **off-diagonal**
quotient residues

\[
X_{01}:=\frac{b_{u_1}}{p_1}\pmod {p_0},
\qquad
X_{02}:=\frac{b_{u_2}}{p_2}\pmod {p_0}.
\]

But (3.1) is just what the universal transfer identity says after the one
condition \(p_0\mid b_{u_0}\) is imposed. It is not a second condition.
The corresponding reductions modulo \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) give the other two
one-hit transfer relations. Q8740's saturation theorem is precisely the
statement that scalar recurrence elimination produces no independent
residual beyond these hit equations.

Thus a concentration theorem for three independent \(\beta_j\)'s would be
false as a model, and a concentration theorem correctly conditioned on the
recurrence plane would give no gain.

## 4. The proposed \(p^3\) shift is false

The arbitrary-digit prime-power theorem is the first-jet congruence of
Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler:

\[
\boxed{
 b_{d+pm}\equiv
 (b_d+pm\dot b_d)b_m\pmod {p^2},
 \qquad 0\le d<p.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Here \(\dot b_d\) is the derivative of the entire Apéry interpolation. One
explicit formula is

\[
\dot b_d=
2\sum_{k=0}^d
\binom dk^2\binom{d+k}{k}^2
\bigl(H_{d+k}-H_{d-k}\bigr).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Primary reference:
[Eric Rowland, Reem Yassawi, and Christian Krattenthaler,
“Lucas congruences for the Apéry numbers modulo \(p^2\),”
*Integers* 21 (2021), A20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04801).

Taking \(m=1\), since \(b_1=5\), gives

\[
b_{d+p}\equiv5(b_d+p\dot b_d)\pmod {p^2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

At a hit \(b_d=p\beta_{p,d}\), division by \(p\) yields only

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_{d+p}}p
\equiv5(\beta_{p,d}+\dot b_d)\pmod p.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

It neither preserves \(\beta_{p,d}\) nor determines it modulo \(p^2\).
For instance,

\[
b_1=5,\qquad b_6=21460825,
\]

and

\[
b_6\equiv75\not\equiv5=b_1\pmod {5^3}.
\]

The familiar Apéry supercongruence

\[
b_{pn}\equiv b_n\pmod {p^3}
\]

is a special zero-digit statement, not a freely shifted statement. The
general projective Dwork congruences of Mellit--Vlasenko also retain quotient
coordinates rather than giving the asserted translation invariance; see
[Anton Mellit and Masha Vlasenko, “Dwork's congruences for the constant terms
of powers of a Laurent polynomial”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5811).

### Reflection shows the same free jet

At an interior hit one also has

\[
\frac{b_{p-1-d}}p\equiv\beta_{p,d}-\dot b_d\pmod p,
\tag{4.5}
\]

while (4.4), after division by \(5\), gives

\[
\frac{b_{p+d}}{5p}\equiv\beta_{p,d}+\dot b_d\pmod p
\qquad(p\ne5).
\tag{4.6}
\]

The matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
\]

has determinant \(2\). Hence the two lifted endpoint quotients are merely an
invertible change of coordinates on

\[
(\beta_{p,d},\dot b_d)\in\mathbb F_p^2.
\]

No nonzero scalar condition appears. Q376's exact deflation calculation
shows the same phenomenon at every deeper layer: dividing one more forced
factor introduces one more normalized jet coordinate.

### Why this does not propagate through a transverse triple

Dwork information at \(p_j\) concerns \(\beta_j\) and its jets modulo its
**own** prime \(p_j\). Reducing the triple identity modulo \(p_0\) instead
requires \(\beta_1,\beta_2\bmod p_0\). These are off-diagonal,
cross-characteristic residues. Current Dwork congruences at \(p_1\) and
\(p_2\) say nothing about them. Thus there is no route

```text
own-prime p² lift
    -> triple identity
    -> extra common congruence
```

without a genuinely new mixed-prime theorem.

## 5. Smoothness in the starting residue does not count hits

For a fixed gap pattern, define for every candidate integer \(u\), not only
hits,

\[
\widetilde\beta_j(u)=
\frac{b_{u+s_j}}{p_j(u)},
\qquad
p_j(u)=\frac{n-u-s_j}{q}
\]

on the appropriate affine branch. Through the entire Apéry interpolation,
these have a smooth real extension away from a denominator zero. Their
magnitudes obey (2.1).

However:

1. the linear combination proposed in the question vanishes identically on
   this smooth curve, by (1.2);
2. the hit condition is exactly
   \(\widetilde\beta_j(u)\in\mathbb Z\), a discontinuous divisibility
   condition;
3. primality of the three affine forms \(p_j(u)\) is also discontinuous.

A zero-count theorem for a smooth function cannot help because the relevant
function is identically zero. What remains is a simultaneous-integrality
problem for a P-recursive/exponential sequence along affine denominators.
No standard equidistribution theorem for the real parameter \(u\) controls
that arithmetic event.

This also explains why ordinary archimedean concentration is misaligned with
the all-\(n\) objective. An average over \(u\), \(p\), or \(n\) needs a
proved discrepancy theorem before it has a pointwise consequence; Q8746's
countermodel shows that excellent local sparsity and all current same-prime
certificates can coexist with maximal deterministic spikes.

## 6. The convex-body/lattice-point formulation is too large

Ignore the Apéry origin temporarily and count positive integer solutions of

\[
A_0p_0x_0+A_1p_1x_1+A_2p_2x_2=0
\tag{6.1}
\]

inside boxes having the actual quotient sizes. Since each side length is
exponential in the base index, a two-dimensional hyperplane section contains
exponentially many lattice points, even after positivity and polynomial-size
ratio restrictions are imposed. An upper bound from the covolume of this
lattice is enormously larger than \(n/(\log n)^3\).

If one now restricts to the one-dimensional Apéry trajectory

\[
(x_0,x_1,x_2)
=\left(\frac{b_u}{p_0},
       \frac{b_{u+s}}{p_1},
       \frac{b_{u+t}}{p_2}\right),
\]

then (6.1) is automatic and the condition that this point be integral in all
three coordinates is exactly the original three-hit problem. The lattice
formulation has therefore made one of two choices:

- discard the recurrence trajectory, yielding a hopelessly large count; or
- retain it, yielding LGTQ-3 unchanged.

There is no intermediate convex-body saving supplied by the known size
bounds.

## 7. Positivity supplies no new codimension

All Apéry numbers and all hit quotients are positive. In an admissible
transverse orientation, the Plücker relation consequently has a signed
balance of the form

\[
\text{positive term}
=	ext{positive term}+\text{positive term}.
\]

This can exclude a sign-incompatible formal pattern. It cannot bound the
number of surviving bases, because the equality is an identity for every
positive Apéry state. The transfer minors were constructed precisely so that
they annihilate both independent solutions of the recurrence; the dominant
\(\lambda^u\) mode and the recessive mode are both cancelled exactly.

More generally, any property using only

- positivity of \(b_m\),
- log-convexity or neighboring ratio bounds,
- the Poincaré asymptotic,
- and the universal three-value identity

holds before the divisibility conditions are imposed. It may classify gap
signs, but it cannot distinguish a hit base from a non-hit base inside one
fixed gap pattern.

Also, “logarithmic height \(O((\log n)^2)\)” should not be read as literal
coefficients \(O(1)\): it permits quasi-polynomial absolute size. This does
not affect the saturation conclusion—even bounded coefficients would still
annihilate the recurrence trajectory identically.

## 8. The corrected constructive program

The quotient idea can become useful only after introducing a new observable
outside the recurrence module.

### 8.1 Separate diagonal and crossed quotient data

At a hit define the own-prime first jet

\[
J_{p,d}:=\left(
\frac{b_d}{p}\bmod p,
\dot b_d\bmod p
\right)\in\mathbb F_p^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For a transverse triple define the crossed quotient matrix

\[
X_{ij}:=rac{b_{u_j}}{p_j}\pmod {p_i},
\qquad i\ne j.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The present theory gives:

- an invertible parametrization of each diagonal jet \(J_{p,d}\), but no
  restriction on it;
- one recurrence-linear relation in each row of \(X\), but no second row
  condition;
- no theorem coupling the diagonal jet at \(p_j\) to its off-diagonal
  reductions at \(p_i\).

This is the exact information gap.

### 8.2 Stage 1: Apéry quotient-jet separation

A useful new theorem must produce, for actual Apéry data, a relation

\[
\Phi_{q;s,t}
\bigl(u;J_{p_0,u_0},J_{p_1,u_1},J_{p_2,u_2},X\bigr)=0
\tag{AQJS}
\]

such that:

1. \(\Phi\) is **not** in the saturated ideal generated by the order-two
   recurrence, the three hit equations, and the universal Plücker relation;
2. its degree/conductor and height are at most polylogarithmic in \(n\) for
   \(s,t=O(\log n)\);
3. it remains nonzero on the generic one-hit recurrence state line.

This is the missing non-scalar, non-recurrence input requested in Question 6.
A distinguished mod-\(p^2\) K3/Hasse--Witt correction is a plausible source,
but the existing reciprocal-square factorization is insufficient:
`hasse_square_no_go.tex` proves triangular freedom of its early coefficients.
The lift must use the arithmetically distinguished Apéry square root or
Frobenius extension class, not reciprocity and squareness alone.

### 8.3 Stage 2: cross-characteristic quotient-jet large sieve

Once a nontrivial \(\Phi\) exists, one can ask for a concentration theorem:
for fixed \(q\), uniformly in \(s,t\le C\log n\), the number of candidate
bases whose three affine forms are prime, whose three hit conditions hold,
and whose quotient jets lie on the proper locus \(\Phi=0\), should have a
power or logarithmic saving over \(n/(\log n)^3\).

That would be a genuine **cross-characteristic quotient-jet large sieve**
(CQJLS). Deligne/Katz methods might then become relevant if \(\Phi\) has a
bounded-complexity geometric realization. Without AQJS, however, CQJLS has no
proper locus to sieve: the only available locus is the universal hyperplane,
which contains every candidate Apéry vector.

## 9. Recommended next exact experiment

A computation should not test whether the Q8740 linear identity holds; it is
guaranteed. The informative test is:

1. enumerate actual fully transverse triples;
2. record the jets \(J_{p_j,u_j}\) and crossed matrix \(X_{ij}\);
3. quotient out the three universal row relations and all reflection/transfer
   factors;
4. search for a residual low-degree rank defect or polynomial relation;
5. compare against recurrence-compatible local states with independently
   chosen quotient jets, not against independent random integers.

A reproducible residual relation surviving that control ensemble would be a
candidate AQJS invariant. Absence of such a relation at increasing ranges
would be evidence that quotient concentration is not the missing Apéry input.

## Final conclusion

The proposed idea correctly identifies the quotient layer as the first place
where actual Apéry arithmetic could escape Q8740/Q8746. But the particular
linear identity does not exploit that layer: it is exactly the relation that
the recurrence already forces.

The strongest rigorous conclusion is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{marginal distribution of }b_d/p\text{ is presently insufficient;}\\
\text{the Q8740 hyperplane gives no density loss;}\\
\text{the general }d+p\text{ mod-}p^3\text{ premise is false;}\\
\text{a new cross-characteristic quotient-jet separation law must come first.}
\end{gathered}}
\]

That AQJS law—followed by CQJLS—is the smallest constructive version of the
concentration route that is not already ruled out by recurrence-elimination
saturation and the Q8746 countermodel.
