# Repeated-folded-anchor audit

## 1. Equal folded anchor forces the opposite-sign configuration

Let a hit at the row \(m\) be written as

\[
m=c_pp+z_p,
\qquad z_p=s_pu-\tau_p,
\]

where the folded representative is \(u\). The two possible unfolded residues attached to the same fold are the pair

\[
\{u,-u-1\} \pmod p.
\]

For two distinct shell hits with the same folded anchor \(u\), equality of the folded data but distinct primes forces the two signs to select the two complementary representatives. Thus, after the normalization of the fixed shift τ, the two congruences are

\[
m\equiv u\pmod p,
\qquad
m\equiv -1-u\pmod q .
\]

Equivalently,

\[
p\mid m-u,
\qquad q\mid m+1+u .
\]

The Apéry condition is independent of the sign choice, hence both primes satisfy

\[
p,q\mid B_u.
\]

The same-sign alternative would require two distinct primes to use the same unfolded residue. That is an ordinary anchor collision and is excluded by the folded-root convention except in the equal-anchor exceptional sector.

## 2. Product defect

Write the two anchor contributions as

\[
n_p=m-u,
\qquad n_q=m+1+u .
\]

The difference between the two affine products is therefore

\[
n_q-n_p=2u+1.
\]

Depending on which ordering convention is used for the full defect vector, the defect label is

\[
\boxed{d=\pm(2u+1)}.
\]

Hence a fixed nondegenerate defect determines the anchor:

\[
 u=\frac{|d|-1}{2},
\]

provided the parity condition is satisfied. There is no remaining \(O(X)\) freedom in \(u\).

## 3. Translation parameter

With \(t=z_1\), the first anchor is

\[
z_1=t.
\]

The equal-anchor pair fixes the two translations because

\[
z_p-z_q=2u+1.
\]

Thus for a fixed sign pattern and full defect label the only remaining choices are the shell divisors of the fixed Apéry integer \(B_u\), together with the third hit.

More explicitly, let

\[
\mathcal P_u(X)=\{r:X<r\le 2X,\ r\ {m prime},\ r\mid B_u\}.
\]

The repeated-anchor fibre is contained in the finite set of triples

\[
(p,q,\ell)
\in \mathcal P_u(X)^2\times \mathcal P_{u_3(t)}(X),
\]

subject to

\[
m\equiv u\pmod p,
\quad
m\equiv -1-u\pmod q,
\quad
m-d_3\equiv z_3(t)\pmod \ell .
\]

For fixed \(p,q\), the first two congruences determine a unique CRT class modulo \(pq\). Since

\[
pq>X^2>m,
\]

there is at most one possible row \(m<X^2\). Therefore the remaining multiplicity is bounded by the number of admissible pairs of shell divisors of \(B_u\) and compatible shell divisors of the third value.

## 4. Divisor bound obtainable from the Apéry height

The elementary height estimate gives

\[
\log B_u=O(u).
\]

Therefore the total number of prime divisors satisfies

\[
\omega(B_u)\ll \frac{u}{\log u}
\]

(up to the standard loss from small prime factors). Consequently

\[
|\mathcal P_u(X)|\ll \frac{u}{\log X}.
\]

For the repeated-anchor pair this gives only

\[
|\mathcal P_u(X)|^2
\ll \frac{u^2}{\log^2 X}.
\]

The third condition does not automatically contribute another independent \(1/\log X\). The prime \(\ell\) divides a different Apéry value \(B_{u_3(t)}\), and no unconditional independence between the divisor sets of two Apéry values follows from the height bound alone.

Hence the rigorous bound from the available inputs is

\[
J_{\rm rep}(X;d,s)
\ll \max_{u\ll X}\omega(B_u)^2
\ll X^2/\log^2 X
\]

before using any additional recurrence-specific cancellation.

This is far weaker than the required \(o(X/\log^3X)\).

## 5. What the recurrence would need to supply

To improve this, one needs an additional statement of one of the following forms:

1. A gcd/resultant estimate

\[
\gcd(B_u,B_{u'})=X^{o(1)}
\]

for the relevant affine pairs \(u,u'\).

2. A shell-divisor sparsity theorem

\[
\#\{p\in(X,2X]:p\mid B_u\}=X^{o(1)}
\]

uniformly for \(u\ll X\).

3. A first-jet or continuant relation showing that the third congruence eliminates all but \(o(X/\log^3X)\) translations.

The current height estimate alone does not prove any of these.

## 6. Low rows

Rows with \(m<X\) must not appear. In the target-regular construction the shell condition requires \(p\le m\) with \(p>X\), so every such row is empty. Counting them would introduce artificial solutions with invalid coefficients \(c_p\).

## Conclusion

The exact repeated-anchor reduction is algebraic: equal folded anchors force opposite signs, the pair defect is exactly \(\pm(2u+1)\), and CRT gives a unique row once the shell divisors are chosen. However, the available Apéry height estimate supplies at most the divisor bound for one fixed value and does not create three independent logarithmic savings. A proof of the desired fibre estimate requires a genuinely new recurrence-aware divisor/resultant input; it does not follow from height, CRT uniqueness, or shell-prime counting alone.
