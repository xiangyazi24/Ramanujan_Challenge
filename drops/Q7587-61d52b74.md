## Verdict

There **are** genuinely different safe rational slopes whose dominant algebraic bases are multiplicatively independent. The cleanest example is

\[
\theta_1=\frac13,\qquad \theta_2=\frac12.
\]

For the direct prefixes their per-`n` bases are

\[
B_D(1/3)=16,\qquad B_D(1/2)=27,
\]

which are multiplicatively independent. For the Strehl--Franel prefixes they are

\[
B_S(1/3)=8,\qquad B_S(1/2)=6\sqrt6,
\]

which are also multiplicatively independent.

This does **not** currently yield a subexponential gcd theorem. There are two independent rigorous obstructions.

1. **The fixed-slope sequences are not fixed power sums / constant-coefficient linear recurrences.** Along each floor-residue class they have an endpoint-saddle asymptotic of the form
   \[
   C_r m^{-2}\Lambda^m(1+O(m^{-1})),
   \]
   with `m` the residue-class parameter. A nonzero fixed power sum has, after passing to a further residue class, leading form `m^d lambda^m` with integer `d>=0`, not `m^{-2} lambda^m`. Thus the usual BCZ/Corvaja--Zannier power-sum hypotheses are not met. Merely knowing the dominant bases are independent is not an admissible substitute.

2. **There is an exact Apéry local rank-one obstruction that survives linearly separated slopes.** For every top-half prime and every two safe cutoffs, all four numbers
   \[
   D_{n,K_1},S_{n,K_1},D_{n,K_2},S_{n,K_2}
   \]
   have the same reduction `b_j mod p`. A non-target prime therefore gives a nonzero rank-one vector, while a target prime gives the zero vector. Every pairwise difference and every `2x2` determinant vanishes modulo **every** top-half prime, target or not. The target information lives in the first determinantal divisor / row gcd, exactly the quantity one is trying to bound.

So the two-slope idea gives an exact and attractive *reduction* of the q=1 target radical to a same-index gcd with independent real bases, but neither the Subspace theorem nor the current resultant/content machinery supplies the missing height estimate. The failure is not just “we have not tried the right slopes”: the obvious fixed-polynomial Bezout/resultant constructions are ruled out below.

# 1. Exact common target-safe interval and endpoint audit

Let

\[
A(n,k)=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k},\qquad
F_k=\sum_j\binom kj^3,
\]

and

\[
D_{n,K}=\sum_{k=0}^K A(n,k)^2,
\qquad
S_{n,K}=\sum_{k=0}^K F_kA(n,k).
\]

Fix a prime

\[
\frac n2<p\le n,
\qquad n=p+r,\quad 0\le r<p,
\]

and put the folded index

\[
j=\min(r,p-1-r).
\]

The maximum possible folded index is

\[
H=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{3}\right\rfloor.
\]

Indeed `j=min(r,n-1-2r)`, whose integer maximum is `H`.

For every `k<p`, Lucas gives the following support dichotomy.

- If `k>r`, then `binom(r,k)=0`, hence `A(n,k)=0 mod p`.
- If `r>j=p-1-r` and `k>j`, then `r+k>=p`; the low Lucas digit in `binom(r+k,k)` is `r+k-p<k`, hence that factor is zero mod `p`.
- For `k<=j`, no carry occurs and `A(n,k)=A(r,k) mod p`.

Therefore every term with `j<k<p` vanishes modulo `p`, and for every cutoff satisfying

\[
j\le K<p
\]

one has

\[
D_{n,K}\equiv b_r\equiv b_j\pmod p,
\]

and, using the exact Strehl identity from `q32_strehl_gcd.py`,

\[
S_{n,K}\equiv b_r\equiv b_j\pmod p.
\]

Consequently the **uniform common safe interval for the full folded top-half problem** is

\[
\boxed{
H\le K\le M:=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The lower inequality guarantees `K>=j` for every top-half prime. The upper inequality guarantees `K<p` because `p>n/2` strictly.

This explains the two intervals in `q32_multicutoff_content_intersection.py`:

- `[H,M]` is the full common folded interval;
- the larger direct-only interval `[H,n-H-1]` applies after restricting to the unreflected direct q=1 candidates `p=n-j` with `j<=H`, and is not the common safe interval for all top-half folded candidates.

### Endpoint exceptions

There is **no target-preservation exception** at either `K=H` or `K=M`.

There is one relevant valuation exception already recorded in `q32_strehl_gcd.py`: at the lower boundary carrier,

\[
v_p\!\left(\binom n{H+1}\binom{n+H+1}{H+1}\right)=2
\]

when

\[
2n+1=3p,
\]

and it is `1` generically for the tested `p>=7` situation. This affects content saturation, not the congruence above.

For a rational slope `theta in [1/3,1/2]`, `K=floor(theta n)` satisfies (1.1) for every `n`:

\[
H\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor\le\lfloor\theta n\rfloor\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor.
\]

Thus two distinct rational slopes in this interval are genuinely simultaneously target-safe.

# 2. Exact dominant algebraic bases

A useful exact simplification is

\[
A(n,k)=\frac{(n+k)!}{(n-k)!(k!)^2}.
\]

For `k=xn+O(1)`, Stirling gives

\[
A(n,k)
= c_A(x,r)n^{-1}\alpha(x)^n(1+O(n^{-1}))
\]

on each floor-residue class, where `c_A(x,r)>0` is residue dependent and

\[
\boxed{
\alpha(x)=
\frac{(1+x)^{1+x}}
{(1-x)^{1-x}x^{2x}}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The exponent

\[
a(x)=\log\alpha(x)
\]

satisfies

\[
a'(x)=\log\frac{1-x^2}{x^2}.
\]

Hence `a` is strictly increasing throughout `[1/3,1/2]`.

For the Franel numbers, the elementary central-term bounds already give

\[
\log F_k=k\log8+O(\log k),
\]

and the standard one-dimensional Laplace expansion gives the sharper form

\[
F_k=\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt3}\frac{8^k}{k}
(1+O(k^{-1})).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore the endpoint exponents are

\[
\boxed{B_D(x)=\alpha(x)^2}
\tag{2.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{B_S(x)=8^x\alpha(x).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

The derivative for the Strehl exponent is

\[
\frac d{dx}\log B_S(x)
=
\log\frac{8(1-x^2)}{x^2}>0
\]

on the whole safe interval. Thus both prefixes are exponentially dominated by their upper endpoint.

This endpoint dominance is quantitatively strong. The exact adjacent ratio is

\[
\frac{A(n,k-1)}{A(n,k)}
=
\frac{k^2}{(n-k+1)(n+k)}
\to
\frac{x^2}{1-x^2}.
\]

Hence the previous/current ratios tend to

\[
q_D(x)=\left(\frac{x^2}{1-x^2}\right)^2\le\frac19
\]

and, by (2.2),

\[
q_S(x)=\frac1{8}\frac{x^2}{1-x^2}\le\frac1{24}.
\]

So summing the prefix changes only the positive prefactor, not the base. More precisely, on every rational-slope residue class,

\[
D_{n,K}=C_{D,r}n^{-2}B_D(\theta)^n(1+O(n^{-1})),
\tag{2.5}
\]

\[
S_{n,K}=C_{S,r}n^{-2}B_S(\theta)^n(1+O(n^{-1})),
\tag{2.6}
\]

with positive constants `C_{D,r},C_{S,r}`.

## Rational slopes: exact algebraic base on a residue class

Write the slope in lowest terms

\[
\theta=\frac uv,\qquad 0<u<v,
\]

and fix `n=vm+r`. Then

\[
K=um+\left\lfloor\frac{ur}{v}\right\rfloor.
\]

Define

\[
R_{u,v}
=
\frac{(v+u)^{v+u}}
{(v-u)^{v-u}u^{2u}}.
\]

The exact algebraic relations are

\[
\alpha(u/v)^v=R_{u,v},
\]

\[
\boxed{B_D(u/v)^v=R_{u,v}^2,}
\qquad
\boxed{B_S(u/v)^v=8^uR_{u,v}.}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus, as a sequence in `m`, the dominant bases are the explicit rational numbers

\[
\Lambda_D(u,v)=R_{u,v}^2,
\qquad
\Lambda_S(u,v)=8^uR_{u,v},
\]

while the residue `r` changes only the nonzero constant prefactor.

### The two endpoint slopes

For `theta=1/3`, `R_{1,3}=64`, so

\[
B_D(1/3)=16,\qquad B_S(1/3)=8.
\]

For `theta=1/2`, `R_{1,2}=27`, so

\[
B_D(1/2)=27,\qquad B_S(1/2)=\sqrt{216}=6\sqrt6.
\]

Therefore

\[
16^a=27^b\Longrightarrow a=b=0,
\]

and

\[
8^a=(6\sqrt6)^b\Longrightarrow a=b=0.
\]

So multiplicatively independent dominant bases are available without any numerical guesswork.

There is also a useful global ordering. On the safe interval,

\[
B_D(\theta)\ge16,
\]

whereas

\[
B_S(\theta)\le6\sqrt6<16.
\]

Thus for `theta_1<theta_2` one has

\[
B_S(\theta_1)<B_S(\theta_2)<B_D(\theta_1)<B_D(\theta_2).
\tag{2.8}
\]

There is no cross-coordinate equality of dominant bases anywhere in the common safe interval.

# 3. Exact two-slope target reduction

Choose any two safe slopes and let `K_i=floor(theta_i n)`. For every top-half prime `p`, with folded target index `j`, Section 1 gives

\[
D_{n,K_1}\equiv S_{n,K_1}
\equiv D_{n,K_2}\equiv S_{n,K_2}
\equiv b_j\pmod p.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Hence for any two chosen coordinates `X_n,Y_n` among these four,

\[
\boxed{
p>n/2:\quad
p\mid\gcd(X_n,Y_n)
\iff
b_j\equiv0\pmod p.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the two-slope construction is an **exact target carrier**. For example,

\[
X_n=D_{n,\lfloor n/3\rfloor},
\qquad
Y_n=D_{n,\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
\]

have multiplicatively independent bases `16` and `27`, and their top-half gcd support is exactly the q=1 bad-prime support.

A theorem

\[
\log\gcd(X_n,Y_n)=o(n)
\]

would therefore close the q=1 top-half carrier immediately (and a theorem only for the top-half radical of the gcd would suffice).

The rest of this note explains why the standard gcd machinery does not presently prove it.

# 4. Why BCZ / Corvaja--Zannier cannot be invoked

The fixed-slope residue subsequences are not constant-coefficient linear recurrences.

From (2.5)--(2.6), after fixing `n=vm+r`, one has

\[
U_m=C_r m^{-2}\Lambda^m(1+O(m^{-1})),
\qquad C_r\ne0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

By contrast, every nonzero constant-coefficient recurrence sequence has a representation

\[
U_m=\sum_{i=1}^s P_i(m)\lambda_i^m
\]

with polynomial `P_i`. After passing to a further residue class to remove root-of-unity ratios among dominant roots, a nonzero dominant contribution has the form

\[
c\,m^d\lambda^m(1+o(1)),
\qquad d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]

The exponent `-2` in (4.1) is impossible. Therefore the exact sequences under discussion are **not** fixed power sums.

This is enough to block a direct BCZ/Corvaja--Zannier application. Replacing the exact power-sum hypothesis by the statement “the real asymptotic bases are multiplicatively independent” is not legitimate.

Even more strongly, dominant-base independence alone is false as a gcd principle **inside the power-sum class itself**. Consider

\[
x_n=6^n-3^n=3^n(2^n-1),
\]

\[
y_n=10^n-5^n=5^n(2^n-1).
\]

The dominant bases `6` and `10` are multiplicatively independent, and both sequences have rational generating functions and are fixed power sums. Nevertheless

\[
\gcd(x_n,y_n)\ge2^n-1,
\]

so

\[
\log\gcd(x_n,y_n)\ge n\log2+o(n).
\]

The missing hypothesis is visible in the full root data:

\[
6/3=10/5=2.
\]

There is a common torus factor invisible to the dominant roots. Standard Subspace-theorem gcd results require precisely this kind of full toric/common-factor degeneracy to be excluded. For the Apéry prefixes there is not even a finite characteristic-root set on which such a hypothesis can currently be checked.

# 5. Fixed diagonals / G-functions do not by themselves repair the argument

For a fixed rational slope and a fixed residue class, the direct prefix is a proper hypergeometric sum with a linear boundary; the Strehl prefix is the corresponding finite binomial multisum. It is reasonable, and standard holonomic closure supports, treating these as fixed holonomic/G-function-type objects after residue splitting.

But this is not enough for a gcd theorem. The counterexample in Section 4 already lies in the much smaller class of **rational generating functions**, hence also in any broad class containing rational diagonals/G-functions. Therefore no theorem of the form

> distinct or multiplicatively independent dominant singularities imply `log gcd=o(n)`

can hold for that class without an additional arithmetic coprimality condition on the *entire* object.

The present repository does not contain such a coprimality theorem for the two slope-prefix diagonals. In fact the exact mod-`p` coupling in the next section points in the opposite direction: the two objects are intentionally correlated on every top-half prime.

# 6. The exact local rank-one obstruction

Let

\[
M_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
D_{n,K_1}&S_{n,K_1}\\
D_{n,K_2}&S_{n,K_2}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For every top-half prime, (3.1) gives

\[
M_n\equiv
b_j
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
\pmod p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Therefore:

- if `b_j != 0 mod p`, the reduction has rank exactly `1`;
- if `b_j = 0 mod p`, the reduction has rank `0`.

The determinant vanishes in **both** cases. Hence

\[
p\mid\det M_n
\]

for every top-half prime, target or not. All `2x2`-minor information is universal. The target is the additional drop from rank one to rank zero, which is detected by the first determinantal divisor

\[
\gcd(D_{n,K_1},S_{n,K_1},D_{n,K_2},S_{n,K_2}).
\]

That is exactly the desired gcd again.

This is the two-slope analogue of the selector-matrix phenomenon already seen elsewhere in the project: higher minors can be nearly trivial or universally forced while the marked information lives in row/entry gcds.

## Exponential universal difference content

Define the top-half primorial

\[
\Pi_n=\prod_{n/2<p\le n}p.
\]

Because all four entries are equal modulo every such `p`, one has the exact divisibilities

\[
\Pi_n\mid D_{n,K_i}-S_{n,K_i},
\]

\[
\Pi_n\mid D_{n,K_2}-D_{n,K_1},
\]

\[
\Pi_n\mid S_{n,K_2}-S_{n,K_1},
\]

and

\[
\Pi_n\mid D_{n,K_1}S_{n,K_2}-S_{n,K_1}D_{n,K_2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

By the prime number theorem,

\[
\log\Pi_n=\frac n2+o(n).
\tag{6.3}
\]

So every nonzero difference/determinant certificate of these standard forms is already exponentially large for a **universal** reason.

More generally, if a fixed integer polynomial `P(X1,X2,X3,X4)` satisfies

\[
P(T,T,T,T)\equiv0,
\]

then

\[
\Pi_n\mid
P(D_{K_1},S_{K_1},D_{K_2},S_{K_2}).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus any nonzero polynomial certificate that kills the universal diagonal line has size at least `exp((1/2+o(1))n)`.

### Why one cannot simply divide out the universal content

At a target prime, the first selective information is that the common residue `b_j` itself becomes zero. Dividing a universal difference by its first `p`-factor can erase exactly the target prime one hopes to retain.

The lower-cutoff audit in `q32_strehl_gcd.py` makes this issue concrete: the relevant boundary carrier has `p`-valuation exactly `1` generically, with the exceptional second copy only when `2n+1=3p`. There is no uniform theorem that a target zero contributes an additional `p`-adic copy to the saturated difference.

This is also why `q32_all_cutoff_determinant.py` finds large universal cutoff content, and why `q32_primitive_cutoff_smith.py` has to divide exact row content before even asking for selectivity. The latter still has explicit finite contamination examples after saturation; those examples are evidence only, not an asymptotic no-go theorem.

# 7. A fixed-polynomial Bezout/resultant no-go for independent-base coordinates

Take the clean pair

\[
X_n=D_{n,\lfloor n/3\rfloor},
\qquad
Y_n=D_{n,\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
\]

Their dominant bases are `16` and `27`, multiplicatively independent.

Let

\[
P(X,Y)=\sum_{a,b}c_{a,b}X^aY^b\in\mathbb Z[X,Y]
\]

be a fixed nonzero polynomial with zero constant term, so that

\[
\gcd(X_n,Y_n)\mid P(X_n,Y_n).
\]

Because `16` and `27` are multiplicatively independent, distinct monomials have distinct exponential bases

\[
16^a27^b.
\]

Hence there is a unique largest exponential monomial among those with nonzero coefficient. The asymptotics in Section 2 imply

\[
\log|P(X_n,Y_n)|=c_Pn+O(\log n)
\]

for some `c_P>0` unless `P(X_n,Y_n)` is eventually zero.

The same argument permits coefficients of height `exp(o(n))`: a subexponential coefficient cannot cancel a unique exponential leader.

Thus **no fixed-degree polynomial/resultant/Bezout certificate in two independent-base prefix values can have size `exp(o(n))`**.

For all four coordinates the ordering (2.8) is even stronger: with two distinct slopes the largest active base in any nontrivial linear combination is unique. A small linear Bezout identity cannot arise from leading-term cancellation unless one uses exponentially large `n`-dependent coefficients, which defeats the desired height bound.

This does not prove the gcd itself is exponential. It proves that the most direct “multiplicative independence + small explicit Bezout remainder” strategy cannot work.

# 8. Asymptotic resultants of recurrences do not give the needed same-index integer

After fixing a rational slope/residue class, creative telescoping can in principle produce polynomial-coefficient recurrences for the hypergeometric sums. Even if one proves two such Ore operators are coprime, an Ore-Bezout identity relates **shifted values**

\[
U_m,U_{m+1},\ldots,
\]

and

\[
V_m,V_{m+1},\ldots,
\]

with polynomial coefficients in `m`.

That is not a same-index gcd certificate. The top-half candidate prime attached to the original integer `n` is a moving modulus; there is no reason it divides the corresponding prefixes at `n+1,n+2,...`. Thus a recurrence resultant can be nonzero and small while saying nothing about

\[
\gcd(U_m,V_m).
\]

The “characteristic resultant at infinity” is weaker still: it merely records that the real exponential bases differ. Sections 4 and 7 show that such information does not control arithmetic common factors.

# 9. What the current multicutoff/content machinery really says

`q32_multicutoff_content_intersection.py` proves an exact useful ideal identity for the Legendre--Euler transforms:

\[
I_n=\gcd(\Gamma_{n,H},\kappa_{n,H+1},\ldots,\kappa_{n,M}),
\]

with

\[
\kappa_{n,J}
=
\gcd_{J\le k\le n}
\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}\binom{k}{J}.
\]

Every q=1 bad prime survives this intersection, and the implementation shows that intersecting many cutoffs can remove substantial nuisance content in examples.

But this is not a height theorem. The same file says explicitly that proving

\[
\log I_n=o(n)
\]

would already solve the q=1 slice. Nothing in the exact ideal reduction proves that estimate.

The all-cutoff determinant audit explains the structural reason: after row subtraction, candidate primes divide many difference rows universally, independently of the Apéry zero. Determinants amplify the universal content rather than the selective bit. Primitive row saturation is necessary, but the existing primitive Smith audit still does not isolate the target.

Linearly separating two slopes does not remove this phenomenon; equation (6.1) shows it persists at zeroth order for every pair of safe slopes.

# 10. Could a genuine Subspace/S-unit route still exist?

Only after proving substantially more structure than dominant-base independence.

A legitimate positive route would need an **exact** representation of the selected two prefix sequences in a fixed finitely generated torus, or a fixed power-sum representation, together with a proof that the two full objects have no common toric factor. The present sequences fail the fixed power-sum test in Section 4.

Alternatively, one could seek a new gcd theorem for the particular globally bounded diagonal/holonomic sequences arising from rational-slope binomial sums. Such a theorem would need a hypothesis strong enough to exclude the rational-function counterexample

\[
6^n-3^n,\qquad10^n-5^n,
\]

and would also have to confront the exact rank-one congruence (6.1). No theorem of that strength is currently present in the repository, and it is not a formal consequence of G-function/diagonal status.

So the route is not killed by a theorem saying “all fixed-slope G-function gcds can be large.” Rather:

- **BCZ/CZ is inapplicable as stated** because the exact hypotheses fail;
- **base independence alone is rigorously insufficient**, by an explicit fixed-power-sum counterexample;
- **the obvious Apéry determinant/resultant/Bezout certificates are rigorously obstructed** by universal rank-one content and by their exponential size.

# 11. Smallest possible positive theorem

The most economical two-slope target is already the direct/direct pair

\[
X_n=D_{n,\lfloor n/3\rfloor},
\qquad
Y_n=D_{n,\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
\]

It has exact target preservation and independent bases `16,27`. The theorem one actually needs is

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}_{p>n/2}\gcd(X_n,Y_n)=o(n).
}
\tag{11.1}
\]

A stronger whole-gcd statement

\[
\log\gcd(X_n,Y_n)=o(n)
\tag{11.2}
\]

would also suffice but is not necessary.

Any proof via a concrete Bezout integer must produce integers `U_n,V_n` and a nonzero

\[
E_n=U_nX_n+V_nY_n
\]

such that

\[
\log|U_n|+\log|V_n|=o(n),
\qquad
\log|E_n|=o(n).
\tag{11.3}
\]

The spectral separation `16<27` shows that a two-term linear cancellation with subexponential coefficients cannot satisfy (11.3). Fixed polynomial combinations fail for the same reason. Pairwise-difference or determinant constructions additionally carry the universal primorial `Pi_n` and are already of height `exp(Theta(n))` before target selectivity enters.

Therefore a successful proof must be **genuinely arithmetic**, not just asymptotic. It must control the common prime divisors directly—e.g. by a new local-global theorem for the two fixed-slope binomial diagonals, or by a target-selective higher `p`-adic observable that survives universal-content saturation.

# 12. Final route assessment

## What is genuinely positive

1. The full folded target is preserved simultaneously for every rational slope in `[1/3,1/2]`; there is no hidden endpoint failure.
2. Exact dominant bases are available in closed algebraic form:
   \[
   B_D(u/v)^v=R_{u,v}^2,
   \qquad
   B_S(u/v)^v=8^uR_{u,v}.
   \]
3. `theta=1/3` and `theta=1/2` give multiplicatively independent direct bases `16,27` and independent Strehl bases `8,6sqrt(6)`.
4. Any two such safe coordinates give an exact same-index gcd carrier for the top-half q=1 target radical.

## What fails

1. The fixed-slope sequences are not fixed power sums, so BCZ/Corvaja--Zannier cannot be cited from dominant-base independence.
2. Diagonal/G-function status alone is far too weak; even rational power sums with independent dominant bases can have exponential gcd.
3. Two slopes do not create a second local equation: modulo every top-half prime they still lie on the same one-dimensional line `b_j*(1,1,1,1)`.
4. Determinants and pairwise differences are universally divisible by the top-half primorial, of logarithmic height `Theta(n)`.
5. Dividing that universal content is not target-safe: the generic target prime may have only the same first `p`-adic copy.
6. A fixed-degree polynomial Bezout/resultant in independent-base coordinates is necessarily exponentially large by its unique dominant monomial.
7. Recurrence/Ore resultants concern shifted indices and do not preserve the same moving candidate prime.

## Bottom line

The two-slope idea is **not a dead reduction**: it gives an unusually clean exact reformulation of the q=1 carrier as the gcd of two sequences whose dominant real bases can be made independent. But multiplicative independence is not the missing theorem. The missing input is a new arithmetic gcd theorem for these specific fixed-slope binomial/Franel prefixes, or an additional target-selective `p`-adic coordinate beyond the common `b_j` reduction.

At present I would not bank a Subspace-theorem or asymptotic-resultant closure. The strongest rigorous statement is the exact two-slope carrier plus the no-go results above. No subexponential integer gcd/Bezout bound is proved here, and nothing in the finite multicutoff experiments is used as asymptotic evidence.
