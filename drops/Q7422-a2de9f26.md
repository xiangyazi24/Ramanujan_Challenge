# P32 exact quartic interpolation attack

## 1. Endpoint normalization and the operator identity

Write

\[
P_a(z)=\sum_{u=1}^a b_{a-u}\frac{Q_a(z)}{z-u}.
\]

The only poles in the defining rational expression are removable.  The shifted Apéry recurrence gives, after multiplying by the common denominator, the polynomial identity

\[
\mathcal D_aP_a=H_aR_a,
\qquad H_a(z)=\prod_{u=2}^a(z-u).
\]

No division by a polynomial modulo \(q\) is used here; this is an identity in \(\mathbb Z[z]\).

At \(z=0\), the first and third summands of \(\mathcal D_aP_a\) contribute only through the values at \(1\) and \(-1\).  Substitution of the interpolation values

\[
P_a(u)=b_{a-u}(-1)^{a-u}(u-1)!(a-u)!
\]

and cancellation with \(H_a(0)=(-1)^{a-1}(a-1)!\) gives

\[
R_a(0)=a^3b_{a-1}.
\]

Similarly, evaluating at \(z=1\) gives

\[
R_a(1)=a^3b_a.
\]

These two values are exact and sign-sensitive.  They do not determine the remaining three coefficients of the quartic.

## 2. Why the triple-zero hypothesis does not immediately contradict the quartic

Assume

\[
P_a(0)=P_a'(0)=P_a''(0)=0\pmod q.
\]

Since \(q>a\), all factors \(1,\ldots,a\) are nonzero modulo \(q\), hence

\[
Q_a(0),Q_a'(0),Q_a''(0)
\]

are well-defined and the hypothesis is equivalent to

\[
\sum_{u=1}^a b_{a-u}u^{-s}=0\pmod q,
\qquad s=1,2,3.
\]

The operator contains a shift.  The vanishing of the first three Taylor coefficients of \(P_a\) does not imply the same vanishing for \(P_a(z+1)\) or \(P_a(z-1)\).  Therefore applying \(\mathcal D_a\) at zero cannot eliminate all unknown terms.

More explicitly, if

\[
P_a(z)=z^3T(z)\pmod q,
\]

then

\[
\mathcal D_aP_a(0)=a^4P_a(1),
\]

not zero.  The value \(P_a(1)\) is the boundary interpolation value

\[
P_a(1)=b_{a-1}( -1)^{a-1}(a-1)!.
\]

Hence the operator produces boundary data rather than a contradiction.

## 3. Reduced determinant formulation

The quartic may be written

\[
R_a(z)=r_0+r_1z+r_2z^2+r_3z^3+r_4z^4.
\]

The endpoint equations give

\[
 r_0=a^3b_{a-1},
\]

and

\[
 r_0+r_1+r_2+r_3+r_4=a^3b_a.
\]

The remaining information obtainable from the triple-zero assumption is a linear system in \(r_1,\ldots,r_4\) whose coefficients come from the three first derivatives of \(H_a^{-1}\mathcal D_aP_a\) at zero.  Equivalently, define

\[
M_a=(\partial^i[z^j] (H_a^{-1}\mathcal D_a(z^3z^j)))_{0\le i\le2,\ 0\le j\le4}.
\]

Then triple vanishing implies

\[
M_a(r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4)^T
 =v_a(b_{a-1},b_a,b_{a+1})^T\pmod q,
\]

where \(v_a\) is obtained by moving the known endpoint terms to the right side.  This is a strict reduction: the original problem is reduced to a rank/resultant computation involving a 3 by 4 matrix and Apéry boundary values.

## 4. Current obstruction to an impossibility proof

A contradiction would require proving that the above linear system has no solution whenever \(q>a\).  The endpoint values alone are insufficient: they only constrain two affine combinations of the five coefficients of \(R_a\).  A quartic has enough freedom to absorb three Taylor constraints.

The missing ingredient would have to be an additional arithmetic identity, for example

\[
\det M'_a=\pm c_a\neq0\pmod q
\]

with \(c_a\) forced to have all prime divisors \(\le a\), or a recurrence relation forcing the remaining coefficients to be incompatible with the Apéry boundary values.

Neither follows from the displayed operator identity alone.

## Conclusion

The exact identities

\[
R_a(0)=a^3b_{a-1},\qquad R_a(1)=a^3b_a
\]

are verified reductions, but they do not prove impossibility of

\[
z^3\mid P_a(z)\pmod q.
\]

The strongest unconditional reduction from the given data is the lower-degree resultant/determinant formulation above.  A complete proof or counterexample requires evaluating the remaining determinant against the actual Apéry recurrence; generic rank arguments are insufficient.
