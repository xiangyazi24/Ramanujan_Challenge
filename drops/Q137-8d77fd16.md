# Cross-characteristic independence via motivic monodromy

## Conclusion

The motivic/automorphic viewpoint explains the single-prime distribution very well, but it does not currently prove the required CRT independence. The missing estimate is a genuinely stronger horizontal independence theorem: it concerns defining-characteristic reductions of one motive at many unrelated primes, not the ordinary distribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes.

The three proposed routes clarify exactly where the gap lies.

---

## 1. Symmetric powers and Rankin--Selberg

The Apéry modular form f gives a compatible system of two-dimensional Galois representations

```
rho_l : Gal(Qbar/Q) -> GL_2(Q_l).
```

Rankin--Selberg methods for

```
L(Sym^a f x Sym^b f,s)
```

control sums of Hecke eigenvalues such as

```
a_p(f)^i a_q(f)^j
```

and correlations of Frobenius conjugacy classes.

This is information about

```
Frob_p in GL_2,
```

where p is the characteristic of the Frobenius element.

The Apéry zero event is different:

```
p | b_r
```

means that a coefficient sequence is zero after reducing the motive modulo the same prime p. It is not a condition on the Hecke eigenvalue a_p(f).

Therefore Rankin--Selberg does not directly imply

```
Pr_p(m mod p in Z_p and q condition) = product.
```

A successful argument would require a new interpretation of the zero indicator as a trace function in a family where the prime itself varies.

---

## 2. Sato--Tate and Hecke independence

For distinct primes p and q, the Frobenius classes

```
Frob_p, Frob_q
```

are independent in the usual automorphic sense. More precisely, the Euler product factorization of automorphic L-functions implies the expected statistical independence of local parameters when averaged over primes.

Thus the pair

```
(theta_p, theta_q)
```

has the product Sato--Tate distribution at the level of Frobenius angles.

However, this is not the needed independence.

The event in the Apéry problem is

```
m mod p in Z_p.
```

Here Z_p is a subset of F_p defined by reduction of a recurrence/coefficient system in characteristic p. It is an internal mod-p condition.

Sato--Tate describes the distribution of the geometric Frobenius at p acting on cohomology. It does not describe the distribution of all coefficient positions r where a reduction vanishes.

So Sato--Tate independence is the wrong independence statement.

---

## 3. The defining-characteristic obstruction

This is the central obstruction.

There are two different uses of a prime p:

1. p as a Frobenius place of a characteristic-zero motive.
2. p as the characteristic in which the entire recurrence is reduced.

Automorphic theory controls (1).

The zero set Z_p belongs to (2).

A bridge would require a theorem of the form:

```
zero statistics of the mod-p Mellin sheaf
are controlled by the characteristic-zero automorphic representation.
```

No such general theorem is known.

Even the implication

```
a_p(f) behaves randomly
=> {r : b_r=0 mod p} behaves randomly
```

is false in general; the defining-characteristic structure contains much finer information.

---

## 4. What a motivic solution would have to prove

The desired estimate is

```
Σ_{p != q} | #{m<X^2 : m mod p in Z_p,
                 m mod q in Z_q}
          - |Z_p||Z_q|X^2/(pq) |
=o(X^4/(log X)^3).
```

A sufficient geometric formulation would be a two-prime large sieve for the family

```
(M_p,M_q)
```

of Mellin sheaves in defining characteristic.

One would need cancellation for the tensor product zero indicator

```
1_{Tr(Frob_m,M_p)=0} * 1_{Tr(Frob_m,M_q)=0}.
```

This is closer to a horizontal large sieve for compatible systems than to Rankin--Selberg.

---

## 5. Most promising route

The likely route is:

1. Express b_r mod p through the Cartier/Mellin sheaf description.
2. Replace the zero indicator by an algebraic expansion in trace functions.
3. Prove a uniform Deligne-type bound for the resulting mixed-characteristic sums.

The required theorem would be a new ``large sieve for defining-characteristic Frobenius traces''.

Existing tools provide:

* SL(2) monodromy for one Mellin sheaf.
* Katz equidistribution for fixed p.
* Sato--Tate for the modular form.
* Rankin--Selberg bounds for Hecke data.

They do not provide the cross-prime CRT independence needed for (HM)_2 or (HM)_3.

Therefore the motivic picture identifies the correct object, but proving the displayed estimate remains a new theorem rather than a consequence of known automorphic functoriality.
