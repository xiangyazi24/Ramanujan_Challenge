# P3.2 M1 attack: what controls cancellation beyond Cauchy--Schwarz?

## Executive summary

The computation \(C(p)/p\to3\) is exactly the obstruction to a second-moment proof. Parseval sees the total \(L^2\) mass of the Fourier coefficients \(S(u)\), but M1 asks for a **signed first moment** of the nonzero Fourier modes:

\[
|Z_p|=1+\frac1p\sum_{u\ne0}S(u),
\qquad S(u)=\sum_r e_p(u b_r).
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives only

\[
\left|\sum_{u\ne0}S(u)\right|
\le \sqrt{p\sum_{u\ne0}|S(u)|^2},
\]

which is controlled by the collision energy \(C(p)\). Since \(C(p)\sim3p\), this gives the observed \(O(\sqrt p)\) barrier.

To get average \(O(1)\) zeros, one needs a **higher-order cancellation theorem** for the family \(S(u)\), not a better estimate of its individual size.

---

## 1. Why Parseval is the wrong tool

The Fourier identity is

\[
1_{b_r=0}=\frac1p\sum_u e_p(u b_r),
\]

so

\[
|Z_p|=\frac1p\sum_u\sum_r e_p(u b_r).
\]

The \(u=0\) term gives \(1\). The problem is

\[
T_p=\sum_{u\ne0}S(u).
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\sum_u|S(u)|^2
=p\#\{(r,s):b_r=b_s\}.
\]

Because of the palindrome

\[
b_{p-1-r}=b_r,
\]

there are already \(2p\) collision contributions (diagonal plus reflection). The remaining random collisions contribute another approximately \(p\). Thus

\[
\sum_u|S(u)|^2\asymp p^2.
\]

Hence

\[
|T_p|\le O(p^{3/2}),
\]

and

\[
|Z_p|\le O(\sqrt p).
\]

This is optimal information from the second moment. It cannot see the sign cancellation.

---

# 2. The missing object: the additive Fourier transform of a trace function

The sequence

\[
r\mapsto b_r\pmod p
\]

should be viewed as a finite-field trace distribution. The sums

\[
S(u)=\sum_r e_p(u b_r)
\]

are the additive Fourier transform of this distribution.

The usual Katz machinery controls sums of the form

\[
\sum_x\chi(f(x))
\]

when \(f\) is a fixed algebraic function and the sheaf has bounded conductor. Here the difficulty is different:

* \(b_r\) is itself a Frobenius trace of a varying hypergeometric motive;
* the additive character is applied to the trace value;
* the resulting sheaf is an Artin--Schreier pullback by a trace function.

This is a **second Fourier layer**.

The first layer is the Apéry motive with geometric monodromy \(Sp_4\). The second layer asks about the distribution of its trace values in ℓF_p.

Sp(4) monodromy controls the first layer but does not automatically give the second.

---

# 3. What methods go beyond Cauchy--Schwarz?

## (A) Van der Corput differencing

A possible route is to study

\[
S(u)=\sum_r e_p(u b_r)
\]

by differencing:

\[
|S(u)|^2
=\sum_h\sum_r e_p(u(b_{r+h}-b_r)).
\]

The inner correlation is

\[
C_h(u)=\sum_r e_p(u(b_{r+h}-b_r)).
\]

If one proves cancellation for all nonzero shifts:

\[
C_h(u)\ll p^{1-\delta},
\]

then iterated van der Corput gives stronger bounds for \(S(u)\).

However, for M1 we do not actually need each \(S(u)\) small. We need cancellation after summing over \(u\). Therefore the better target is a mixed estimate:

\[
\sum_{u\ne0}\sum_h C_h(u).
\]

This is closer to a correlation theorem for the Apéry sequence than to a classical Weyl bound.

---

## (B) Large sieve

A large sieve inequality for the family \(S(u)\) would control

\[
\sum_u|S(u)|^2,
\]

but that is exactly Parseval again.

To exploit signs one needs a **one-sided large sieve** or a spectral decomposition that detects the special vector

\[
\sum_{u\ne0}S(u).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{u\ne0}S(u)=
\sum_r\sum_{u\ne0}e_p(ub_r)
\]

is not a generic large-sieve problem; it is a projection onto the zero trace fiber.

The relevant analogy is not the large sieve but the study of exceptional fibers of trace maps.

---

## (C) Deligne/Katz equidistribution

Suppose the normalized traces behaved like independent samples from the \(USp(4)\) trace distribution.

Then the number of exact zeros would have bounded mean. The reason is simple:

\[
\Pr(\operatorname{tr}(g)=0)\asymp \frac1p
\]

for a random matrix in a continuous compact group reduced modulo \(p\).

Hence among \(p\) samples one expects

\[
p\cdot\frac1p=O(1)
\]

zeros.

This matches the observed Poisson law.

But Katz equidistribution requires a geometric parameter \(x\in\mathbb A^1(\mathbb F_p)\). Here the parameter \(r\) is a Kummer/Mellin parameter, and the trace values are the output of a varying character family. A new Mellin-side sheaf statement is required.

---

# 4. Is there a Vinogradov-type method?

The closest analogy is a polynomial exponential sum:

\[
\sum_x e_p(f(x)).
\]

There, Weil bounds apply because \(f\) is fixed algebraic data.

For Apéry:

\[
\sum_r e_p(u b_r)
\]

has \(b_r\) itself as a complicated trace sequence. There is no known algebraic polynomial \(f(r)\) with bounded degree representing it.

A successful Vinogradov-style argument would need a statement like:

\[
\sum_r e_p(u(b_{r+h}-b_r))\ll p^{1-\delta}
\]

uniformly in \(u,h\).

This would be a major new theorem about the additive randomness of the Apéry trace sequence.

---

# 5. The likely correct route: additive trace anti-concentration

The computational evidence suggests:

* individual \(S(u)\) have square-root size;
* the family \(\{S(u)\}\) has strong sign cancellation;
* the collision energy is exactly what a palindromic random model predicts.

Therefore the missing theorem is not

\[
|S(u)|\ll\sqrt p.
\]

That is already true and insufficient.

The needed theorem is something like:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{u\ne0}S(u)=O(p)
}
\]

or, more structurally,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{u\ne0}\widehat{\mu_p}(u)=O(p)
}
\]

where \(\mu_p\) is the trace-value distribution of the Apéry motive.

This is an anti-concentration theorem for one specific fiber of the trace map.

---

# Final answer

**What goes beyond Cauchy--Schwarz?**

Not another \(L^2\) estimate. One needs a higher-order or geometric cancellation theorem.

The possible frameworks are:

1. **Van der Corput/Weyl differencing:** study correlations \(b_{r+h}-b_r\). Promising, but requires new Apéry-specific exponential sum estimates.

2. **Large sieve:** standard large sieve only reproduces Parseval. A useful version would need extra geometry of the Apéry family.

3. **Katz/Deligne:** the correct philosophy, but the present problem is a second Fourier layer. Sp(4) monodromy alone does not imply the needed additive trace equidistribution.

4. **Trace-function methods:** the ideal theorem would show that the additive Fourier transform of the Apéry trace distribution has cancellation comparable to a random \(Sp_4\) trace family.

Therefore the M1 bottleneck is not a failure of existing \(L^2\) methods. It is precisely the missing theorem that the Apéry Frobenius trace values are additively equidistributed in \(\mathbb F_p\) at the \(1/p\) fiber scale.
