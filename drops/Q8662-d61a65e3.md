# Q8662 rc5 audit: heavy-row two-surplus-prime certificate

## Conclusion

The two-surplus-prime condition on a heavy row does **not** by itself yield a bounded-multiplicity certificate from HM2 and the stated local equations. The available information is second-moment information on the row load and local CRT compatibility. It does not prevent a single fixed fibre key from occupying a linear-size family of heavy rows/primes with the same two surplus primes reused.

Therefore the desired bound
\[
J_{\rm dist,\ge5}=o(X/\log^3X)
\]
cannot be deduced from the stated hypotheses alone.

## Why the natural charging attempt fails

Suppose a distinguished triple \(T\) on row \(m\) has surplus primes \(q,r\). A possible charge is
\[
T\mapsto \{q,r\}.
\]
To use HM2 one would need a uniform multiplicity bound
\[
\#\{T:T\mapsto\{q,r\}\}\ll 1
\]
or at least \(X^{o(1)}\).

However HM2 only gives
\[
\sum_m (K_m)_2\leq 5X^2\lambda^2.
\]
This controls the number of ordered pairs of hits on rows. It gives no control on how many distinct triples can share one pair of surplus hits while preserving the same fibre key.

The missing statement would be a genuine pair-rigidity lemma:

> Given two surplus primes \(q,r\) on a row, there are at most \(O(1)\) all-distinct full-defect triples compatible with the same key.

No such implication follows from HM2.

## Abstract countermodel

Construct an abstract incidence system satisfying all stated constraints.

Choose a fixed row \(m_0\), five primes
\[
p_1,\ldots,p_L,q,r
\]
in the shell, with \(L\asymp X\). Declare:

* every \(p_i\) is a regular target hit at \(m_0\);
* the two surplus primes are always \(q,r\);
* every triple \((p_i,p_j,p_k)\) chosen from the distinguished subset satisfies the same fibre key equations
\[
c_i p_i=N-d_i,
\qquad z_i=t+d_i.
\]

The CRT constraints only require existence of the relevant coefficients and residues. They do not impose that a surplus pair identifies a unique third prime.

Set all other rows to have load at most one. Then
\[
\sum_m(K_m)_2=(K_{m_0})_2
\asymp X^2,
\]
which is compatible with the HM2 inequality after choosing \(\lambda\) with constant-size upper envelope. Yet the fixed fibre can contain
\[
\binom{L}{3}\gg X^3
\]
formal triples, and in particular a subfamily of size \(cX\).

Thus the local equations plus HM2 do not exclude a linear heavy fibre.

## What extra certificate would suffice

Any successful reduction must add one of the following genuinely new inputs:

1. **Pair rigidity:** for every regular pair \((q,r)\), the number of compatible full-defect triples is bounded by \(X^{o(1)}\).

2. **Fourth-prime gate:** every heavy fibre triple determines a new prime \(s\) outside the chosen triple, and the map to \(s\) has bounded multiplicity. Then HM2 or a higher-energy estimate could become applicable.

3. **Five-prime relation:** a proved short-CRT identity involving the three fibre primes and the two surplus primes, with a divisor/height contradiction.

The current hypotheses provide none of these.

## Quantifier audit

The heavy-row statement is:

\[
\forall T\in\mathcal F,\quad K(m(T))\ge5\Rightarrow \exists q,r.
\]

The desired conclusion requires:

\[
\forall(q,r),\quad \#\{T:(q,r)\text{ are surplus}\}\text{ is small}.
\]

The first existential statement cannot be inverted into the second bounded-multiplicity statement.

Hence the two-surplus-prime fact is information-theoretically insufficient. A new arithmetic rigidity theorem, not another application of HM2, is required.
