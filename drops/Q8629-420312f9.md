# Cold audit of the repeated folded-anchor decomposition

## 1. The local anchor dichotomy

Let the folded anchor be
\[
 u=\min(r,p-1-r),\qquad 0\leq u\leq (p-1)/2.
\]
A hit at row m has one of the two forms
\[
 m=c p+z,
\]
with the reflected choices encoded as
\[
 z= u\quad(+),\qquad z=-1-u\quad(-).
\]
Equivalently the two congruences are
\[
 p\mid m-u \quad(+),\qquad p\mid m+1+u\quad(-).
\]

If two shell hits at the same row have the same non-central folded anchor u, then the same-sign possibility would require two shell primes dividing the same integer m-u or m+1+u. Since the coefficient range gives \(m<X^2\) and the relevant repeated-anchor sector fixes the anchor, the claimed multiplicity bound is not a consequence of the anchor alone; it requires the target support condition that only one shell factorization with the allowed coefficient survives. Without that condition the unfiltered statement \(\mu_m(u)\le2\) is false in general.

For the intended target-regular sector, two surviving hits with the same u must have opposite signs:
\[
p\mid m-u,\qquad q\mid m+1+u,
\]
with
\[
p,q\mid B_u.
\]
Subtracting the two anchored representatives gives
\[
(m-u)-(m+1+u)=-1-2u,
\]
so the associated product defect is exactly
\[
\pm(2u+1),
\]
with the sign determined by the ordering convention for the defect.

## 2. Central endpoint m=u

The endpoint case is different. If \(m=u\), then a positive hit requires
\[
u=c p+u,
\]
hence
\[
(c p)=0
\]
and therefore only the coefficient-zero solution exists. It is outside the positive target range. Thus repeated positive anchors at \(m=u\) do not occur for target-regular rows.

The formula
\[
\nu_u=\#\{p\in(X,2X]:p>2u,\ p\mid B_u\}
\]
counts potential unfiltered endpoint divisors, but these rows are removed by the condition \(p\le m\) (and by the coefficient range) when \(m<X\). Therefore they cannot contribute to the pointwise theorem.

The threshold is equivalently
\[
p>2u \iff p\ge 2u+1
\]
for integral primes. The strict form is harmless but the endpoint exclusion must be stated explicitly.

## 3. Ordered triple coefficient audit

For a row with a double anchor, an ordered triple counted in \(F_3\) chooses the repeated-anchor pair and then a third hit. The proposed local factor
\[
(\nu_u)_2(3\kappa_u-2\nu_u-2)
\]
comes from choosing an ordered pair of distinct endpoint divisors and then a third hit. This coefficient is not exact without a further disjointness statement: the third hit can itself lie in the same endpoint divisor set or can share the same anchor pattern.

Thus the displayed identity
\[
F_{3,rep}=\sum_{u<X}(\nu_u)_2(3\kappa_u-2\nu_u-2)
 +6\sum_{X\le m<X^2}c(m)(K_X(m)-2)
\]
is an upper-bound decomposition after removing overlaps, not an unconditional exact identity.

The second term has the correct combinatorial shape for a high row with exactly one double anchor: choose the repeated pair in three positions and order the remaining choice. But if a row has two distinct double-anchor classes, the same ordered triple may be represented more than once. Such rows must be separated.

## 4. Inequalities

The inequality
\[
F_{3,\pm}\le 3F_{2,high}
\]
is valid only for triples whose repeated structure forces a unique opposite-sign pair. It fails as a statement about arbitrary unfiltered triples.

Likewise
\[
F_{3,rep}\le 9(M_X-2)_+A_2
\]
requires
\[
A_2=\sum_u(\nu_u)_2
\]
and a uniform bound on the number of third hits extending a fixed double anchor. The factor 9 is a valid bookkeeping constant after such a separation, but it is not obtained from the anchor definition alone.

## 5. Target-regular high-row statement

The only statement relevant for the pointwise theorem is:

For \(m\ge X\), every repeated folded-anchor contribution is either

1. an opposite-sign pair determined by a divisor pair of \(B_u\), with defect \(\pm(2u+1)\), or
2. a higher collision requiring an additional shell divisor of another Apéry value.

All rows \(m<X\) are absent because a shell prime satisfies \(p>X>m\), so the required condition \(p\le m\) cannot hold.

The remaining problem is therefore a divisor-count problem for shell prime divisors of the fixed Apéry values \(B_u\), together with CRT compatibility for the third hit. No APG/lambda normalization enters this reduction.

## 6. Missing patterns and corrections

The exact overcounts are:

- endpoint divisors \(p\mid B_u\) with \(m=u<X\) counted by \(\nu_u\) but forbidden by target support;
- rows with two different repeated-anchor pairs, where the second summand can count the same ordered triple multiple times;
- triples where the third hit shares the same anchor class, requiring a separate multiplicity term.

The corrected conclusion is that the proposed formulas describe the target-regular high-row repeated-anchor sector only after imposing the coefficient and support filters and after partitioning multiple-collision rows. They are not exact identities for the unfiltered \(K_X\).
