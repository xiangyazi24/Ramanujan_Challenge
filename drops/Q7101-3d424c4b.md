# Audit of the corrected consecutive-quadruple resultant argument

The corrected argument fixes the two main categorical issues from Q7095:

1. `S_{a,b}=Res_X(N_a(X),N_b(X+a))` is an integer. The relevant invariant is the ordinary integer
\[
\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3}),
\]
not a gcd of polynomial reductions and not a resultant of the resultants.

2. The correct quadruple count is linear in the number of zeros in each block, not \(\binom m4\).

The resulting linear route is valid, subject to the usual exclusions of bad primes (degree drops, vanishing leading coefficients, and identically-zero resultants).

## 1. Consecutive quadruple counting

Partition \([0,p-1]\) into blocks of length \(H\). Let a block contain
\[
z_1<\cdots<z_m
\]
zeros of the Apéry sequence modulo \(p\).

Select only the consecutive quadruples
\[
(z_i,z_{i+1},z_{i+2},z_{i+3}),\qquad 1\le i\le m-3.
\]
Their number is
\[
(m-3)_+\ge m-3.
\]
Summing over all blocks gives
\[
Q_4(p,H)\ge Z(p)-3B_p,
\]
where \(B_p\le \lceil p/H\rceil\) is the number of blocks.

Because zeros are nonconsecutive,
\[
h_1,h_2,h_3\ge2,
\]
and for a selected quadruple in one block,
\[
h_1+h_2+h_3=z_{i+3}-z_i<H.
\]

Hence every selected quadruple contributes to a gap triple with
\[
h_1+h_2+h_3\le H.
\]

## 2. Fixed gap triple and Smith normal form

Fix \((h_1,h_2,h_3)\). Let \(t\) be the number of selected starts \(x\) with this gap pattern.

The conditions imply
\[
N_{h_1}(x)=0,
\qquad
N_{h_2}(x+h_1)=0,
\qquad
N_{h_3}(x+h_1+h_2)=0
\pmod p.
\]

Therefore the same \(t\) values of \(x\) are common roots of
\[
N_{h_1}(X),\quad N_{h_2}(X+h_1).
\]

Assume the generic-prime hypotheses:

* leading coefficients of the two polynomials are nonzero modulo \(p\);
* the resultant is not the zero integer.

Then the Sylvester matrix represents multiplication in the standard resultant module. Over \(\mathbb F_p\), each distinct common root contributes one dimension to the kernel. Therefore its corank is at least \(t\).

For an integer Sylvester matrix \(M\), Smith normal form gives
\[
U M V=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n),
\]
with
\[
S_{h_1,h_2}=\pm\prod_i d_i.
\]
The number of invariant factors divisible by \(p\) is exactly the nullity of \(M\bmod p\). Hence
\[
v_p(S_{h_1,h_2})\ge t.
\]

Applying the same argument to
\[
N_{h_2}(X),\quad N_{h_3}(X+h_2),
\]
and using starts shifted by \(h_1\), gives
\[
v_p(S_{h_2,h_3})\ge t.
\]

Therefore
\[
t\le \min(v_p(S_{h_1,h_2}),v_p(S_{h_2,h_3}))
=v_p(\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3})).
\]

This is the exact invariant needed.

## 3. Summing over gap triples

Let
\[
G_H=\sum_{h_1+h_2+h_3\le H}
\log\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3}).
\]

For a fixed prime \(p\), summing the bound over gap triples gives
\[
Z(p)-3B_p
\le
\sum_{h_1+h_2+h_3\le H}
 v_p(\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3})).
\]

Now sum over generic primes \(X<p\le2X\):
\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}^{gen} Z(p)
\le
3\sum_p B_p
+
\sum_{h_1+h_2+h_3\le H}
\sum_{X<p\le2X}^{gen}
 v_p(\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3})).
\]

Since \(B_p\ll X/H\),
\[
\sum_p B_p\ll \pi(2X)X/H
\ll \frac{X^2}{H\log X}.
\]

For the valuation term, every prime contribution satisfies
\[
v_p(n)\log p\le \log |n|.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}^{gen}
 v_p(\gcd(S_1,S_2))
\le
\frac{\log\gcd(S_1,S_2)}{\log X}.
\]

Hence the global inequality is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X}^{gen} Z(p)
\le
O\!\left(\frac{X^2}{H\log X}\right)
+
\frac1{\log X}
\sum_{h_1+h_2+h_3\le H}
\log\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3})
}
\]
up to the excluded exceptional primes.

## 4. Does GM* imply the desired bound?

Assume
\[
\sum_{h_1+h_2+h_3\le H}
\log\gcd(S_1^*,S_2^*)
\le H^{3+o(1)}.
\]

Take
\[
H=X^{1/2}.
\]

The block term is
\[
\frac{X^2}{H\log X}
=\frac{X^{3/2}}{\log X}.
\]

The gcd term is
\[
\frac{H^{3+o(1)}}{\log X}
=
\frac{X^{3/2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
\]

Therefore the linear route gives
\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}^{gen}Z(p)
\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

So GM* is exactly sufficient for the claimed generic-prime average bound.

## 5. Remaining hidden issues

### (a) Zero resultants

If
\[
S_{a,b}=0
\]
as an integer, the valuation statement is meaningless. This corresponds to the two polynomials having a common factor over \(\mathbb Q\), not merely occasional modular intersections.

Such pairs must be removed from the generic set or treated separately.

### (b) Degree drops

If the leading coefficient of one polynomial vanishes modulo \(p\), the ordinary Sylvester matrix has artificial nullity coming from the dropped degree. The Smith-normal-form argument above only applies after excluding these primes or using a saturated resultant.

The generic hypothesis in the question is precisely what removes this problem.

### (c) Sharing only the middle polynomial

No Macaulay determinant is required. The two integer resultants are not being combined by a multivariate elimination argument. The middle polynomial merely supplies the shift relation:

\[
N_{h_2}(X+h_1)
\]

appears in both pairwise intersections. The same collection of starts gives valuations in two separate integer resultants, and taking their gcd records the shared valuation.

There is no need to prove a three-polynomial common-root resultant statement.

## Conclusion

After the corrections, the linear Smith-normal-form route is mathematically coherent:

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}^{gen}Z(p)
\ll
\frac{X^2}{H\log X}
+
\frac{G_H}{\log X}.
\]

With
\[
G_H=H^{3+o(1)},\qquad H=\sqrt X,
\]
this gives
\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}^{gen}Z(p)
\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

The only genuine remaining work is GM*: proving the gcd-content estimate after removing structural factors and handling zero-resultant/degree-drop exceptional pairs. A Macaulay determinant would add no value unless one needs to handle those exceptional algebraic dependencies.
