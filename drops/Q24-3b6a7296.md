# Short-interval (HM)_2 for Apéry zeros: analysis

## Summary

The full-interval (HM)_2 estimate does **not** by itself imply the short-interval estimate needed for the GCD problem. The obstruction is exactly an equidistribution problem for a sparse CRT-generated set. Among the three proposed approaches, the most promising is a hybrid of Approach 2 (Fourier/Erdos–Turan) and a Halberstam–Richert style weighted sieve argument. A direct transfer from Selberg's upper bound sieve is not available.

## 1. Why the naive CRT estimate loses a factor N

Let

\[
A_{p,q}=\{m:m\bmod p\in Z_p,\ m\bmod q\in Z_q\}.
\]

For p,q in (N,2N], pq>N^2, hence every CRT class has at most one representative in an interval of length N. Therefore

\[
|A_{p,q}\cap(N,2N]|\le |Z_p||Z_q|.
\]

Summing gives the trivial estimate

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}|A_{p,q}\cap I|\ll (\sum_p |Z_p|)^2.
\]

This is of size roughly N^2/log^2 N, whereas the Poisson prediction is N/log^2 N.

The missing saving is not sieve dimension; it is cancellation/equidistribution of CRT representatives.

## 2. Full interval information is insufficient

The known estimate

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}|A_{p,q}\cap[0,N^2)|\ll N^2\lambda_N^2
\]

only controls the total mass. It allows pathological concentration: all twin atoms could cluster in one interval of length N while preserving the global count.

Thus one needs an additional theorem of the form

\[
\sup_{I:|I|=N}\sum_{p\ne q}|A_{p,q}\cap I|
\ll N\lambda_N^2.
\]

This is a discrepancy theorem, not a consequence of (HM)_2 alone.

## 3. Fourier/Erdos-Turan approach (most natural)

Write the discrepancy for a short interval through exponential sums:

\[
D(I)=\sum_{p,q}\sum_{m\in A_{p,q}}1_I(m)-\frac{|I|}{N^2}|A_{p,q}|.
\]

A Fourier expansion reduces this to bounding

\[
\sum_{h\ne0}\widehat{1_I}(h)
\sum_{p,q}\sum_{m\in A_{p,q}}e(hm/N^2).
\]

Therefore the required theorem is a large-sieve-type estimate for CRT atoms:

\[
\sum_{p,q}|\sum_{m\in A_{p,q}}e(hm/N^2)|^2
\ll N^{2+o(1)}\lambda_N^2.
\]

The special Apéry input would need to enter through cancellation in the zero sets Z_p. CRT alone does not provide this cancellation.

## 4. Why Halberstam-Richert is more promising than Selberg reversal

Selberg sieve gives upper bounds for

\[
\#\{n:K(n)=0\}
\]

or more generally sifted sets. It controls averages of products of local conditions. It does not control the maximum multiplicity K(n).

The relevant object is a higher moment:

\[
\sum_n K(n)(K(n)-1).
\]

For max K one needs estimates for all moments:

\[
\sum_n (K(n))_r.
\]

This is closer to a dimension-r combinatorial sieve problem. A Halberstam-Richert framework could work if one proves uniform distribution of the r-fold CRT atoms in short intervals.

## 5. Character sum formulation

A possible target theorem is:

For every nonzero h,

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}\left|\sum_{z_p\in Z_p,z_q\in Z_q}
 e(h m(z_p,z_q)/(N^2))\right|
\ll N^{1+o(1)}.
\]

Here m(z_p,z_q) is the CRT solution.

This resembles bilinear Kloosterman-type estimates. The Apéry problem needs an arithmetic input beyond CRT: Frobenius/K3 structure or p-adic constraints on Z_p.

## 6. Assessment of the three approaches

### Approach 1: equidistribution

Correct formulation, but proving it is essentially the main missing theorem. CRT gives uniformity modulo pq, not uniformity in the embedding of CRT classes into the real interval.

### Approach 2: Erdos-Turan

Most promising. It converts the problem into explicit exponential sums. It identifies exactly what cancellation is missing.

### Approach 3: Halberstam-Richert

Useful as an organizational framework, but it requires the same local distribution estimates. It cannot manufacture the missing saving.

## 7. Relation to Bombieri-Vinogradov

The analogy is imperfect. In BV, averaging over moduli creates cancellation. Here the moduli are essentially fixed and the variable is the translate interval. Therefore there is no automatic complementary modulus mechanism.

The analogue would be a large sieve for the family of CRT embeddings:

\[
\sum_h |\sum_{atoms}e(hm/Q)|^2.
\]

Such a theorem would play the role of BV.

## 8. Conclusion

The short interval (HM)_2 statement is a new theorem, not a corollary of the existing full interval bound.

The likely route is:

1. Express twin atoms using CRT phases.
2. Prove Fourier cancellation for the Apéry zero sets Z_p.
3. Deduce short-interval equidistribution by Erdos-Turan.
4. Feed this into a Halberstam-Richert/combinatorial sieve moment argument.

The decisive missing ingredient is therefore a CRT large-sieve/discrepancy estimate exploiting genuine Apéry structure. Pure sieve theory and pure CRT independence are insufficient.
