# Verdict

The observed value near \(1\) has a clean explanation, but it is **not** the one stated in the question.

The quantity called \(L^2\) in the prompt is evidently the normalized Pearson statistic

\[
D_p^2:=\frac{\chi_p^2}{p+1}.
\]

For \(p-1\) approximately independent uniform samples in a state space of size \(p+1\), one has **exactly**

\[
\mathbf E\,\chi_p^2=p,
\qquad
\mathbf E\,D_p^2=\frac{p}{p+1}\longrightarrow 1.
\]

Thus \(D_p\approx1\) is the ordinary **random-occupancy/birthday-paradox scale**. It does not mean that the orbit is close to a bijection, and it does not confirm “exact uniform distribution.” In fact, a nearly bijective traversal of \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\) would have \(D_p\to0\).

The most important conclusions are:

1. The exponent \(p^{0.03}\) is consistent with a constant plus finite-size noise.
2. Bourgain--Gamburd random-walk theory was compared using a different normalization and, separately, endpoint mixing was confused with occupation statistics.
3. Deligne would imply a bounded normalized \(L^2\) statistic **if** the orbit Fourier modes were trace functions of uniformly bounded-conductor sheaves. The recurrence matrix \(T(r)\) does not currently provide such sheaves.
4. The Grothendieck--Ogg--Shafarevich conductor proposed in the question is not defined for this prefix-product orbit. \(T(r)\) defines a difference equation, not an \(\ell\)-adic local system on the \(r\)-line.
5. Even a theorem \(D_p=O(1)\) gives only \(Z(p)=O(\sqrt p)\), not AFP. It is a global second-moment statement and cannot isolate the distinguished projective state \(0=[0:1]\).

This is fully consistent with the current repository state: `AVENUES.md` treats distinguished projective-orbit coupling as essential and records that formal projective returns and generic codegree arguments admit phantoms; the existing transfer algebra does not by itself control the distinguished Apéry orbit.

# 1. Normalize the statistic first

Put

\[
x_r=M(r)[5:1]=[b_r:b_{r-1}]\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p),
\qquad 1\le r\le p-1.
\]

Let

\[
n=p-1,\qquad m=p+1,\qquad
N_x:=\#\{1\le r\le n:x_r=x\},
\qquad \mu=\frac nm.
\]

Thus \(N_x\) is the occupation number of the projective state \(x\). Define the Pearson statistic

\[
\chi^2=\sum_{x\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)}
\frac{(N_x-\mu)^2}{\mu}
\]

and the reported normalized \(L^2\) quantity

\[
D^2:=\frac{\chi^2}{m}
=\frac1n\sum_x(N_x-\mu)^2.
\]

There are three common normalizations which should not be mixed:

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\rm count}^2&=\sum_x(N_x-\mu)^2,\\
D^2&=\frac1nD_{\rm count}^2=\frac{\chi^2}{m},\\
\|\nu-u\|_2^2&=\frac1{n^2}D_{\rm count}^2,
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(\nu(x)=N_x/n\) and \(u(x)=1/m\). At the random-occupancy scale these are respectively

\[
D_{\rm count}\asymp\sqrt p,\qquad D\asymp1,\qquad
\|\nu-u\|_2\asymp p^{-1/2}.
\]

So the statements “random discrepancy is \(\sqrt p\)” and “the observed discrepancy is constant” describe the **same fluctuations in different units**.

# 2. Exact collision identity

Let

\[
C_p:=\#\{(r,s):1\le r<s\le p-1,\ x_r=x_s\}
=\sum_x\binom{N_x}{2}.
\]

Since

\[
\sum_xN_x^2=n+2C_p,
\]

we obtain the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_p^2
&=\frac1n\left(\sum_xN_x^2-\frac{n^2}{m}\right)\\
&=1-\frac nm+\frac{2C_p}{n}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{2}{p+1}+\frac{2C_p}{p-1}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
D_p=O(1)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C_p=O(p).
\]

The computation is measuring a **linear projective-return energy**. The values near \(1\) say more specifically

\[
C_p\approx\frac{p}{2}.
\]

That is exactly the birthday-paradox prediction for \(p-1\) samples in \(p+1\) boxes.

For independent uniform samples,

\[
\mathbf E C_p=\frac{\binom{p-1}{2}}{p+1},
\]

and hence

\[
\mathbf E D_p^2
=\frac{2}{p+1}
+\frac{2}{p-1}\frac{\binom{p-1}{2}}{p+1}
=\frac{p}{p+1}\to1.
\]

Equivalently, for a multinomial sample the standard identity gives

\[
\mathbf E\chi^2=m-1=p.
\]

This expectation identity is exact; it does not rely on the classical chi-square limiting distribution, whose usual large-expected-cell hypothesis is absent here because \(\mu\sim1\).

## What genuine near-uniformity would look like

Because \(n=m-2\), the most evenly distributed integral occupancy pattern has \(p-1\) states occupied once and two states unoccupied. For this pattern,

\[
C_p=0,\qquad \chi^2=2,\qquad
D_p^2=\frac{2}{p+1}\to0.
\]

Thus:

- \(D_p\to0\): almost injective / almost permutation-like traversal;
- \(D_p\to1\): random Poisson-occupancy traversal;
- \(D_p\asymp\sqrt p\): extreme concentration, such as nearly all times in one state.

Consequently, the sentence “\(\chi^2/(p+1)\to1\), confirming exact uniform distribution” should be replaced by:

> The projective orbit has the second-moment occupancy statistics of a random map with load approximately one.

That is interesting, but much weaker than pointwise equidistribution.

# 3. Random-walk comparison: endpoint versus occupation measure

There are two different questions for a Markov chain \(X_t\):

1. **Endpoint mixing:** how close is the law of \(X_t\) to stationarity?
2. **Occupation mixing:** how large are the fluctuations of
   \(N_x=\#\{t\le n:X_t=x\}\)?

Bourgain--Gamburd expansion addresses the first through convolution powers on the group. It does not say that the occupation count of every state is almost deterministic.

For a stationary uniform Markov chain on \(m\) states,

\[
\mathbf E C
=\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}(n-h)q_h,
\qquad
q_h:=\Pr(X_{t+h}=X_t).
\]

For independent samples, \(q_h=1/m\), giving the birthday term \(\binom n2/m\). For a chain with a uniform spectral gap \(1-|\lambda_2|\ge\varepsilon>0\),

\[
q_h=\frac1m+O(|\lambda_2|^h),
\]

so

\[
\mathbf E C=\frac{\binom n2}{m}+O_\varepsilon(n)=O(p)
\]

when \(n\asymp m\asymp p\). Hence its normalized \(D\) is also \(O(1)\).

Two corrections to the prompt are therefore needed.

First, the Bourgain--Gamburd theorem gives a **uniform positive spectral gap** for the relevant fixed-generator expander families under its hypotheses; it is not the estimate \(1-c/p\). Second, even perfect endpoint mixing leaves order-one normalized occupation noise when one takes only about one sample per state.

The Apéry sequence is not a homogeneous random walk, so this is a model rather than a proof. But it explains why \(D\sim1\), rather than \(D\sim\sqrt p\), is the correct random benchmark.

# 4. What Deligne would imply, conditionally

Suppose temporarily that the finite projective coordinate can be written as a function

\[
\rho_p(r)\in\mathbf F_p
\]

and define additive Fourier sums

\[
S_p(a)=\sum_r e_p(a\rho_p(r)),
\qquad a\in\mathbf F_p.
\]

Ignoring the single point at infinity for the moment, Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{y\in\mathbf F_p}
\left(N_y-\frac np\right)^2
=\frac1p\sum_{a\ne0}|S_p(a)|^2.
\]

If one had the uniform Deligne-type estimate

\[
|S_p(a)|\le C\sqrt p
\qquad(a\ne0),
\]

with \(C\) independent of \(p\) and \(a\), then

\[
\sum_y\left(N_y-\frac np\right)^2=O(C^2p),
\]

and hence the normalized occupation discrepancy would indeed satisfy

\[
D_p=O(C).
\]

So the proposed sheaf-theoretic mechanism is logically correct in the following **conditional** form:

> A uniformly bounded-conductor trace-sheaf realization of all nontrivial Fourier modes of the orbit would imply bounded normalized \(L^2\) discrepancy.

But this does not yet exist for the Apéry coefficient orbit.

Moreover, even this strong uniform square-root estimate gives by Fourier inversion only

\[
N_0=\frac np+\frac1p\sum_{a\ne0}S_p(a)=O(\sqrt p)
\]

after the triangle inequality. This is precisely the familiar additive-Fourier loss. It cannot prove AFP.

# 5. Why the transfer matrix does not have the claimed GOS conductor

The equation

\[
v_{r+1}=T(r)v_r
\]

is a **difference equation** for the shift \(r\mapsto r+1\). The product

\[
M(r)=T(r-1)\cdots T(1)
\]

is a path-ordered prefix product. This is not the same object as a lisse \(\ell\)-adic sheaf on \(\mathbf A^1_r\), and the entries of \(M(r)\) are not Frobenius traces obtained by evaluating one fixed sheaf at \(r\).

The Grothendieck--Ogg--Shafarevich formula applies to an actual constructible or lisse sheaf \(\mathcal F\) on an algebraic curve. In a common conductor convention,

\[
\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal F)
=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal F)
+\sum_{x\in\mathbf P^1\setminus U}
\bigl(\operatorname{drop}_x(\mathcal F)
+\operatorname{Swan}_x(\mathcal F)\bigr).
\]

A rational transition matrix does not specify the inertia representations, drops, or Swan conductors needed in this formula. Therefore there is no legitimate numerical conductor

\[
2+\operatorname{cond}_{-1}+\operatorname{cond}_\infty
\]

attached to the orbit merely from the displayed \(T(r)\).

There are also two elementary warnings about the proposed pole count:

- \(T(r)\) has a pole at \(r=-1\);
- \(\det T(r)=r^3/(r+1)^3\), so it ceases to be \(\mathrm{GL}_2\)-valued at \(r=0\);
- at \(r=\infty\), however,
  
  \[
  T(r)\longrightarrow
  \begin{pmatrix}34&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
  \]
  
  which is invertible. Thus infinity is not a pole of this particular rational matrix.

More fundamentally, low degree of \(T(r)\) does not imply low complexity of its solution. Even a scalar recurrence

\[
y_{r+1}=g(r)y_r
\]

with \(g\) of degree one produces a factorial/Gamma-type prefix product. In characteristic \(p\), singularities of a difference equation propagate along the additive-shift orbit; the shift orbit of one residue is the whole of \(\mathbf F_p\). This is radically different from a fixed finite singular set for a lisse sheaf.

If one interpolates the function \(r\mapsto M(r)[5:1]\) by a rational or polynomial function over \(\mathbf F_p\), its degree can be of order \(p\). An Artin--Schreier sheaf built from that interpolation would then have conductor growing with \(p\), and Deligne would yield no constant-strength conclusion.

## Do not confuse the two Apéry variables

The geometric Apéry Picard--Fuchs/K3 sheaf has a geometric parameter usually denoted \(z\) or \(t\). It is a rank-three weight-two primitive object (or, after an appropriate symmetric-square description, related to a rank-two root object). Its bounded geometric conductor controls trace sums in the **fiber parameter** \(t\).

Here \(r\) is the **coefficient index** of the recurrence. The two variables are not interchangeable. Bounded conductor of the K3 family in \(t\) does not supply bounded conductor for the orbit \(r\mapsto[b_r:b_{r-1}]\).

# 6. Large monodromy is not quantitative mixing of this orbit

The statement that the difference Galois/monodromy group contains \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) is an algebraic irreducibility statement. It says that there is no small invariant algebraic structure of the expected kind. It does not imply that one distinguished ordered prefix product

\[
T(r-1)\cdots T(1)[5:1]
\]

visits \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\) with random occupancy.

Similarly, the determinant observation does not prove mixing:

\[
\det T(r)=\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^3.
\]

The projective action is unchanged by scalar multiplication, so determinant distribution contains very little information about the orbit on \(\mathbf P^1\). At most it records which component or determinant class a chosen lift occupies.

Standard Deligne/Lang--Weil arguments apply to evaluation of a **fixed bounded-degree algebraic map** \(f:\mathbf A^1\to G\), or to trace functions of a fixed sheaf. The prefix product \(M(r)\) has growing word length and is not such a bounded-degree map. There is no general theorem saying

\[
\text{full monodromy of a rational difference cocycle}
\Longrightarrow D_p=O(1)
\]

for every distinguished solution.

Katz--Sarnak likewise supplies the correct philosophy only after one has produced a geometric family of Frobenius conjugacy classes with fixed monodromy and bounded complexity. It predicts the distribution of those Frobenius classes; it does not by itself geometrize the Apéry index orbit.

# 7. The exact projective-return target

The constant-discrepancy assertion can be reformulated without any probability language:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{x\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)}N_x^2=O(p)
}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
C_p=\#\{r<s:x_r=x_s\}=O(p).
}
\]

The random prediction is more precise:

\[
\sum_xN_x^2\sim2p,
\qquad C_p\sim\frac p2,
\qquad D_p^2\sim1.
\]

Decompose returns by their gap:

\[
C_p=\sum_{h=1}^{p-2}R_p(h),
\qquad
R_p(h)=\#\{r:x_{r+h}=x_r\}.
\]

For a fixed \(h\), the block

\[
A_h(r)=T(r+h-1)\cdots T(r)
\]

has rational complexity growing linearly with \(h\), and \(x_{r+h}=x_r\) says that the distinguished state \(x_r\) is a fixed point of \(A_h(r)\). A fixed-gap Bézout or Deligne estimate therefore has a constant growing with \(h\). Summing the resulting bounds over all \(h<p\) loses the required factor.

To prove \(C_p=O(p)\), one needs an **average-in-gap return theorem** or genuine cancellation across \(h\), not merely bounded complexity for each fixed gap.

This is exactly where the current repository analysis lands. `AVENUES.md` records that:

- generic transfer-matrix codegree bounds do not force the required saving;
- formal projective returns have primitive phantom solutions;
- the marked companion identities reduce to cocycle identities and do not add an independent equation;
- the actual distinguished orbit must be coupled to the arithmetic initial vector.

Thus the numerical \(D_p\approx1\) is excellent evidence for the projective collision-energy conjecture, but it is not currently explained by an available bounded-conductor theorem.

# 8. Why this does not prove AFP

Because no two consecutive Apéry values vanish, the projective state satisfies

\[
x_r=[0:1]
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_r=0,
\]

up to harmless endpoint conventions. Hence \(Z(p)\) is the occupation of one distinguished state:

\[
Z(p)=N_{[0:1]}.
\]

From the global energy bound one gets only

\[
Z(p)^2\le\sum_xN_x^2=O(p),
\]

so

\[
Z(p)=O(\sqrt p).
\]

This is the sharp information obtainable from a global \(L^2\) estimate alone. One state may have occupancy \(c\sqrt p\) while the total normalized \(L^2\) statistic remains \(O(1)\).

AFP requires a **pointed anti-concentration theorem** for the specific state \([0:1]\), not merely white-noise energy averaged over all projective states. Possible sufficient forms are:

\[
N_{[0:1]}=O(1),
\]

or a horizontal averaged analogue strong enough for the project, or a family of high-moment estimates with constants controlled uniformly in the moment. The present \(L^2\) computation is a useful diagnostic but not a new route around the AFP barrier.

To verify the interpretation decisively, the orbit script should output, for each \(p\):

1. \(C_p=\sum_x\binom{N_x}{2}\);
2. the identity
   
   \[
   D_p^2=\frac{2}{p+1}+\frac{2C_p}{p-1};
   \]
3. the ratio
   
   \[
   \frac{C_p}{\binom{p-1}{2}/(p+1)};
   \]
4. the empty-state proportion, whose random prediction is approximately \(e^{-1}\);
5. the occupancy histogram \(\#\{x:N_x=j\}\), whose random load-one model is approximately Poisson with mean \(1\);
6. separately, the distinguished occupancies \(N_0=Z(p)\) and \(N_\infty\), rather than hiding them in the global energy.

If the collision ratio, empty fraction, and occupancy histogram all converge to the load-one random predictions, then the correct empirical statement is substantially stronger and cleaner than a log--log fit \(p^{0.03}\):

> The Apéry projective orbit has Poisson(1) state occupancy and linear collision energy.

That would be a natural conjecture, but its proof still requires distinguished-orbit arithmetic beyond present sheaf and transfer-matrix technology.

# Direct answers to the three proposed explanations

## (a) Deligne equidistribution

**Conditionally yes, presently no.** If every nontrivial projective Fourier mode of the orbit came from a geometrically nontrivial sheaf of conductor \(O(1)\), Deligne plus Parseval would give \(D_p=O(1)\). The missing step is precisely the construction of such a sheaf. The rational matrix \(T(r)\) does not provide it.

## (b) Katz--Sarnak

**A good heuristic, not a theorem for this orbit.** Large monodromy predicts random-looking values once the orbit is realized as Frobenius in a bounded-complexity family. Here the coefficient index orbit is not presently such a family. Katz--Sarnak explains what one should expect, not why the prefix product satisfies it.

## (c) Polynomial-cocycle or expander results

**Random occupation theory explains the scale; no standard polynomial-cocycle theorem proves it here.** A constant-gap random walk has \(D_p=O(1)\) at time \(p\), while its unnormalized count discrepancy is \(O(\sqrt p)\). Bourgain--Gamburd concerns random products/endpoints and fixed-generator expanders, not this deterministic inhomogeneous prefix product. Full difference monodromy alone is insufficient.

## Conductor

There is no valid rank-two GOS conductor computable as

\[
2+\operatorname{cond}_{-1}+\operatorname{cond}_\infty
\]

for the sequence \(r\mapsto M(r)[5:1]\). The displayed recurrence is a difference module; it is not an \(\ell\)-adic sheaf on the \(r\)-line. Even at the naive rational-matrix level, \(r=0\) is a noninvertible point and infinity is regular. The bounded conductor of the geometric Apéry/K3 sheaf concerns the geometric fiber variable, not the coefficient index.
