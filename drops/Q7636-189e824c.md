\tag{2.3}

Also `F in 1+t Z[[t]]`, so its formal inverse and every integral power of that inverse lie in `Z[[t]]`. Hence

## 2.3 `mathcal G` is not D-finite

This can be proved; it is not merely the observation that Q7621 found only a growing-width recurrence.

Assume for contradiction that `mathcal G` is D-finite over `C(t)`. Since `sqrt(Delta)` is algebraic and D-finite, closure of D-finite functions under products would give

\[
Y:=\mathcal G\sqrt\Delta=F^{-2}
\]

D-finite. Its reciprocal

\[
Y^{-1}=F^2
\]

is also D-finite because `F` is D-finite and D-finite functions are closed under products.

The theorem of Harris--Sibuya on reciprocals of solutions of linear ODEs now applies: if a nonzero function and its reciprocal are both D-finite over a characteristic-zero differential field, its logarithmic derivative is algebraic. Therefore

\[
\frac{Y'}Y=-2\frac{F'}F
\]

would be algebraic over `C(t)`.

But an algebraic logarithmic derivative would force the line generated by the period `F` to become a rank-one sub-local-system after a finite algebraic base change. Indeed, pass to a finite algebraic cover on which `F'/F` is single-valued. Analytic continuation of `F` by any loop on that cover has the same logarithmic derivative, so the continued solution differs from `F` by a constant scalar. Hence a finite-index subgroup of the Picard--Fuchs monodromy preserves the line `C F`.

That contradicts the Apéry monodromy. Beukers--Peters and Peters identify the rank-three transcendental Picard--Fuchs system with the irreducible symmetric-square variation attached to the non-isotrivial elliptic family. The current repository makes the same geometry explicit in `toric_fiber_sym2.tex` through

\[
E_x:\quad Y^2+(1-2x)XY+x^2Y=X^3
\]

and its six-isogenous partner. The corresponding elliptic `j`-map is nonconstant; the connected algebraic monodromy is the `SL_2` elliptic monodromy, and its symmetric square is the irreducible rank-three `SO_3` representation. Passing to a finite-index subgroup does not create an invariant line.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal G\text{ is not D-finite}.}
\tag{2.12}
\]

Consequences:

\[
\boxed{(g_r)\text{ is not P-recursive}.}
\tag{2.13}
\]

Since a G-function is, by definition, D-finite in addition to its arithmetic height conditions,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal G\text{ is not a G-function}.}
\tag{2.14}
\]

And since a diagonal of a rational function is D-finite,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal G\text{ is not a rational diagonal}.}
\tag{2.15}
\]

The only external geometric input in this non-D-finiteness proof is the standard irreducible Apéry monodromy result; the reciprocal step itself is exactly Harris--Sibuya.

\Xi_r=-1-5\sum_{m=1}^r g_mb_{m-1}.

For the Hadamard product

(A\odot B)(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nb_nz^n,

the coefficient of `z^r` in `zF(z)` is `b_{r-1}`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
(1-z)X(z)=-1-5(\mathcal G\odot zF)(z).

\tag{4.3}

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
X(z)=-\frac1{1-z}\left[

\left(
\mathcal G(w)\frac zwF(z/w)
\right)
\right].

\tag{4.4}
\]

There is also the `kappa`-Casoratian form from Q7621,

\[
\boxed{
X(z)+1
=\theta_z^3\Bigl((zF)\odot K-F\odot(zK)\Bigr).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

These are exact generating functions. They show why ordinary holonomic closure arguments fail: the operation is coefficientwise/Hadamard, not ordinary multiplication.

## 4.1 What is rigorously classified

The sequence `Xi_r` is integral. Q7621 proves the stronger determinant identity

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_r=r^3(\kappa_rb_{r-1}-\kappa_{r-1}b_r)
\in\mathbb Z.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

It also gives the analytic size

\[
\boxed{
\log|\Xi_r|=2r\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log r).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus `X` has integer coefficients of exponential size.

## 4.2 What is *not* rigorously classified

I do not know a theorem from the available structure that proves `X` D-finite or non-D-finite.

The tempting argument is:

- if `X` were D-finite, then `(Xi_r-Xi_{r-1})` would be P-recursive;
- `(b_{r-1})` is P-recursive and never zero over the positive integers;
- by (4.2),
  \[
  g_r=-\frac{\Xi_r-\Xi_{r-1}}{5b_{r-1}};
  \]
- perhaps a termwise quotient theorem would make `(g_r)` P-recursive, contradicting (2.13).

The last step is precisely unavailable. The classical Hadamard Quotient Theorem concerns **constant-coefficient linear recurrences**, equivalently coefficients of rational generating functions. Here the denominator sequence `b_{r-1}` is not of that class. Its generating function is D-finite/G but nonrational. Generalizing coefficientwise quotient rigidity beyond the rational/linear-recurrence Hadamard ring is a separate hard problem; the existing Hadamard-quotient theorems do not supply this implication.

Similarly, although `mathcal G` is differentially algebraic, I do not use an unsupported claim that differentially algebraic series are closed under the constant-term/Hadamard operation (4.4). Therefore I do **not** promote `X` to “differentially algebraic” without a proof.

The exact classification is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{series} & \text{proved class}\\ \hline
F & \text{rational diagonal, D-finite, G-function}\\
\Delta^{-1/2} & \text{algebraic, D-finite, G-function}\\
\mathcal G=1/(F^2\sqrt\Delta)
 & \text{differentially algebraic, not D-finite}\\
K & \text{differentially algebraic, not D-finite}\\
X & \text{integral Hadamard/partial-sum transform; D-finite status open here}
\end{array}
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

If `X` were D-finite, then because of its integral coefficients and exponential growth it would automatically satisfy the arithmetic height part of the definition of a G-function. Thus in this specific case

\[
X\text{ D-finite}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\text{ is a G-function}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

No rational-diagonal representation of `X` is currently produced; nor is one excluded without first settling D-finiteness.

# 5. Exact arithmetic target and the all-index reduction

Put

\[
d_r=\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r),
\qquad
R(T)=\operatorname{rad}\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le r\le T}d_r.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Split prime divisors of `R(T)` at `T`.

Every prime `p<=T` can occur only once in the radical, so its total logarithmic contribution is at most

\[
\vartheta(T)=\sum_{p\le T}\log p=O(T).
\]

If `p>T` divides `R(T)`, then it divides `d_r` for some `r<=T`, and therefore `p>r`. Hence

\[
\prod_{\substack{p>T\\p\mid R(T)}}p
\mid
\prod_{r\le T}\operatorname{rad}_{>r}d_r.
\]

Taking logarithms gives the exact inequality

\[
\boxed{
\log R(T)
\le\vartheta(T)
+\sum_{r\le T}\log\operatorname{rad}_{>r}\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore either of the following is sufficient:

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>r}\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)=o(r)
\quad\text{pointwise},
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

or directly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r\le T}\log\operatorname{rad}_{>r}\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)=o(T^2).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The pointwise statement (5.3) implies (5.4) by summation, and (5.2) then gives exactly the requested

\[
\log R(T)=o(T^2).
\]

This formulation also explains why small primes are not the issue: they cost only `O(T)` at the radical-lcm level.

# 6. Exact large-prime reduction and counterexamples

For every prime `p>r`, Q7621 proves that the reduced denominator of `kappa_r` is a `p`-unit. The no-two-consecutive-zeros theorem for `b` gives

\[
p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow p\nmid b_{r-1}.
\]

Reducing the determinant (4.6) modulo `p` therefore gives

\[
\Xi_r\equiv r^3b_{r-1}\kappa_r\pmod p.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_r
\Longrightarrow
\bigl(p\mid\Xi_r\iff\kappa_r\equiv0\pmod p\bigr).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

If `kappa_r=A_r/D_r` is reduced and every prime factor of `D_r` is at most `r`, then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rad}_{>r}\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)
=\operatorname{rad}_{>r}\gcd(b_r,A_r).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is a genuine denominator saturation theorem. It does **not** make the numerator small.

Also

\[
\Xi_{r+1}=\Xi_r-5g_{r+1}b_r,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_r,\ p\mid\Xi_r
\Longrightarrow p\mid\Xi_{r+1}.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus a transverse common prime becomes a pair of consecutive zeros of `Xi`. This is again an equivalent manifestation, not an independent equation.

The exact same-project audit gives two actual `p>r` examples through `r=5000`:

\[
\boxed{(r,p)=(13,17),\ (492,2237).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

At both pairs the valuations of `b_r` and `Xi_r` at `p` are one. In particular:

- universal exclusion `p>r` is false;
- universal exclusion `p>4r` is false;
- universal exclusion `p>(9/2)r` is false.

No asymptotic density or independence conclusion is drawn from the finite scan.

# 7. Why the natural resultant/Casoratian construction gives no smaller carrier

Let `a_r` be the homogeneous companion with

\[
a_0=0,\qquad a_1=6
\]

and the same Apéry recurrence. Its exact Casoratian is

\[
\boxed{
r^3(a_rb_{r-1}-a_{r-1}b_r)=6.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

That constant unit might suggest adjoining `kappa` and taking resultants. But Q7621 computes the relevant determinants exactly:

\[
\boxed{
r^3(\kappa_rb_{r-1}-\kappa_{r-1}b_r)=\Xi_r,}
\tag{7.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{
r^3(\kappa_ra_{r-1}-\kappa_{r-1}a_r)=-5\Psi_r,}
\tag{7.3}
\]

where

\[
\Psi_r=\sum_{m=1}^rg_ma_{m-1},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{6\kappa_r=a_r\Xi_r+5b_r\Psi_r.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

At a large-prime common zero of `b_r` and `Xi_r`, (7.4) simply says

\[
\kappa_r\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

which is exactly (6.1). There is no second independent vanishing condition.

Likewise, (6.3) does not create a second resultant: the difference

\[
\Xi_{r+1}-\Xi_r=-5g_{r+1}b_r
\]

shows that two consecutive `Xi` zeros are forced by the same target zero.

Therefore the first natural state-space Bezout determinant is not a new low-height integer. It is exactly the transverse integer whose gcd is being studied.

This is the key scoped resultant no-go.

# 8. Audit of existing arithmetic/Diophantine sequence theorems

## 8.1 Bugeaud--Corvaja--Zannier type gcd bounds

The prototype theorem says that for multiplicatively independent integers `a,b>1`,

\[
\log\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)=o(n).
\]

The proof is a Subspace-Theorem argument on a fixed algebraic-torus orbit.

**Why it does not apply:** neither `b_r` nor `Xi_r` is known to be a fixed Laurent polynomial evaluated at a point `(alpha_1^r,...,alpha_s^r)` of a finitely generated multiplicative group. The Apéry recurrence has polynomial coefficients in `r`, and `g` is not P-recursive at all.

## 8.2 Corvaja--Zannier gcd bounds for S-units

The applicable setup is a fixed finitely generated subgroup

\[
\Gamma\subset\mathbb G_m^N(\overline{\mathbb Q})
\]

and fixed coprime polynomials evaluated at points of `Gamma`, outside a proper exceptional subvariety.

**Why it does not apply:** there is no fixed finite set `S` for which the prime supports of `b_r` and `Xi_r` lie in the corresponding S-unit group. Integrality of the sequences does not help; it removes denominators but says nothing about the moving numerator prime support.

## 8.3 Results for linear recurrence sequences / power sums

Corvaja--Zannier and later refinements treat sequences of the form

\[
U_n=\sum_iP_i(n)\alpha_i^n,
\]

i.e. constant-coefficient linear recurrence sequences (possibly with polynomial multiplicity factors).

**Why it does not apply:** `b_r` is P-recursive but not a constant-coefficient linear recurrence. Its generating function is not rational; its asymptotic

\[
b_r\asymp c\alpha^r r^{-3/2}
\]

already has the fractional power `r^{-3/2}`, incompatible with coefficients of a rational generating function. More decisively, `g_r` is not P-recursive, and `Xi` is not known to lie in the constant-coefficient recurrence class.

A useful warning is that “P-recursive” alone would not imply a small gcd theorem anyway: two identical P-recursive sequences can have arbitrarily large gcd (for example `u_n=v_n=n!`). What matters in the Subspace-Theorem results is the algebraic-torus/exponential-polynomial structure plus independence, not merely holonomy.

## 8.4 Classical Hadamard Quotient Theorem

The classical theorem says, roughly, that a termwise quotient of two **linear recurrence** sequences which stays in a finitely generated ring is again a linear recurrence sequence (under the standard formulation/field enlargement).

It cannot be applied to

\[
g_r=-\frac{\Xi_r-\Xi_{r-1}}{5b_{r-1}}
\]

even under the hypothetical assumption that `Xi` were P-recursive, because `b_{r-1}` is not a constant-coefficient linear recurrence. This precisely blocks the most obvious route from the proved non-P-recursiveness of `g` to non-P-recursiveness of `Xi`.

There are generalizations of Hadamard quotient rigidity in algebraic/rational settings, but no theorem in the natural classical package used here turns the quotient of two arbitrary P-recursive/G-function coefficient sequences into a P-recursive sequence. Thus that step cannot be smuggled into the argument.

## 8.5 G-function theory

`F` is a G-function; `mathcal G` is not. If `X` were D-finite, it would also be a G-function because its coefficients are integral and exponentially bounded.

But G-function theory by itself is not a coefficientwise gcd theorem. It controls differential equations, denominators, local exponents, and arithmetic continuation. It does not say that coefficients of two unrelated G-functions have subexponential gcd. Even the class statement cannot imply such a conclusion: taking the same G-function twice gives the full coefficient as the gcd.

Therefore proving `X` to be a G-function would not by itself prove (5.3) or (5.4).

## 8.6 Fixed polynomial resultants

For fixed coprime `P,Q in Z[X]`, one has the familiar fixed resultant restriction on common prime divisors of `P(n)` and `Q(n)`.

Here there are no fixed polynomials `P(r),Q(r)` whose values are `b_r,Xi_r`. The natural state resultant is (7.2), namely `Xi_r` itself, and its size grows exponentially. The current identities therefore do not produce a fixed nonzero integer resultant and hence do not produce a fixed finite exceptional prime set.

The actual counterexamples show that any claimed universal unit resultant is impossible. They do **not** prove that the set of common large primes is infinite; a hypothetical finite exceptional set would at least have to contain `17` and `2237`, and no theorem here establishes finiteness or infinitude.

## 8.7 Fixed toric/elliptic geometry

The repository does have a fixed toric realization for `b_r mod p`, and `toric_fiber_sym2.tex` reduces nonzero fibers to an elliptic symmetric-square trace. That is powerful one-sequence information.

For `Xi`, however, the generating construction inserts the reciprocal period `mathcal G` and then a Hadamard extraction. I do not find a fixed algebraic variety or fixed-rank l-adic trace function whose `r`th coefficient is `Xi_r` and whose simultaneous vanishing with `b_r` could be attacked by a fixed resultant/Chebotarev theorem. Inventing such a realization would be genuinely new structure.

# 9. The exact Archimedean height barrier

Let

\[
\rho=17-12\sqrt2,
\qquad
\alpha=\rho^{-1}=17+12\sqrt2.
\]

Q7621 gives the local exponents `0,1/2,1` of the Apéry Picard--Fuchs operator at `t=rho` and derives

\[
\boxed{b_r\asymp c_b\alpha^r r^{-3/2}.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Since `F(rho)` is finite and nonzero while `sqrt(Delta)` has exponent `1/2`,

\[
\boxed{g_r\asymp c_g\alpha^r r^{-1/2}.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
g_rb_{r-1}\asymp c\alpha^{2r}r^{-2},
\]

and the terminal terms dominate the partial sum defining `Xi`, giving

\[
\boxed{|\Xi_r|\asymp c_\Xi\alpha^{2r}r^{-2}.}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\log b_r=r\log\alpha+O(\log r),
\tag{9.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{\log|\Xi_r|=2r\log\alpha+O(\log r).}
\tag{9.5}
\]

Since the natural state determinant equals `Xi_r`, its aggregate characteristic-zero height is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r=1}^T\log|\Xi_r|
&=2\log\alpha\sum_{r=1}^Tr+O(T\log T)\\
&=\boxed{\log\alpha\,T^2+O(T\log T).}
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.6}
\]

This is exactly the scale that the requested theorem must beat.

The same issue affects any proof that, for each index separately, merely manufactures a nonzero integer `H_r` divisible by the target primes and proves only

\[
\log|H_r|=O(r).
\]

Multiplying these carriers yields only

\[
\log\left|\prod_{r\le T}H_r\right|=O(T^2).
\]

So a **termwise exponential-height carrier plus multiplication is structurally incapable of proving the requested `o(T^2)` by height alone**. To improve the exponent one needs at least one of:

1. a local carrier with `log|H_r|=o(r)`;
2. a cross-index divisibility law showing massive reuse of the same primes/carriers;
3. a fixed finite exceptional-prime theorem;
4. an arithmetic distribution theorem directly bounding the sum in (5.4).

None is supplied by the current Picard--Fuchs/Hadamard/S-unit package.

# 10. Reproducible exact computations

The following PARI/GP script reproduces the coefficient formulas, the first `g` and `Xi` values, the determinant identity, and the exact `p>r` counterexample scan from Q7621. It uses exact integer/formal power-series arithmetic; no floating-point independence inference is involved.

```gp
\\ Q7636 exact structural/transverse audit
N = 5000;
P(n) = 34*n^3 + 51*n^2 + 27*n + 5;

\\ b[k+1] = b_k
b = vector(N+1);
b[1] = 1;
b[2] = 5;
for(n=1,N-1,
  b[n+2] = (P(n)*b[n+1] - n^3*b[n])/(n+1)^3
);
assert(vector(6,k,b[k]) == [1,5,73,1445,33001,819005]);

x = 'x;
F = Ser(b);
Delta = 1 - 34*x + x^2 + O(x^(N+1));
G = 1/(F^2*sqrt(Delta));
g = vector(N+1,k,polcoef(G,k-1));
assert(vector(6,k,g[k]) == [1,7,192,5520,165168,5068320]);

\\ Independent integrality identity for Delta^(-1/2):
ell(n) = sum(k=0,n, binomial(n,k)^2 * 8^k * 9^(n-k));
for(n=0,30,
  assert(polcoef(1/sqrt(Delta),n) == ell(n))
);

\\ xi[k+1] = Xi_k
xi = vector(N+1);
xi[1] = -1;
for(r=1,N,
  xi[r+1] = xi[r] - 5*g[r+1]*b[r]
);
assert(vector(5,k,xi[k]) == [-1,-36,-4836,-2019636,-1195358436]);

\\ kappa and exact Casoratian identity
kap = vector(41);
kap[1] = 0;
kap[2] = -36;
for(r=2,40,
  kap[r+1] =
    (P(r-1)*kap[r] - (r-1)^3*kap[r-1] - 5*g[r+1])/r^3
);
for(r=1,40,
  assert(r^3*(b[r]*kap[r+1]-b[r+1]*kap[r]) == xi[r+1])
);

\\ Exhaustive exact p>r scan through r=5000.
\\ If p>r divides both values, gcd(b_r,Xi_r)>r, so only those gcds need factoring.
d = vector(N,r,gcd(abs(b[r+1]),abs(xi[r+1])));
inds = vector(N,r,r);
cand = select(r->d[r] > r, inds);
print(apply(r->[r,d[r],factor(d[r])],cand));

\\ valuation certificates for the two p>r events
print([valuation(b[14],17), valuation(xi[14],17)]);
print([valuation(b[493],2237), valuation(xi[493],2237)]);
print([b[493] % 2237^2, xi[493] % 2237^2]);
```

The decisive finite output is

```text
[[13,17,17],
 [223,3281,17*193],
 [224,289,17^2],
 [492,2237,2237],
 [3961,6443,17*379]]

v_17(b_13), v_17(Xi_13)       = 1, 1
v_2237(b_492), v_2237(Xi_492) = 1, 1
```

Only the two rows `(13,17)` and `(492,2237)` contain a prime factor exceeding the index. This is an exact theorem for the scanned range and is used only to refute overly strong pointwise claims.

# 11. What a successful theorem would have to add

The most economical new theorem is not a holonomy theorem. It is an arithmetic statement about simultaneous prime support.

A sufficient pointwise target is

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>r}\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)=o(r).
}
\tag{11.1}
\]

A sufficient and strictly more flexible aggregate target is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r\le T}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>r}\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)=o(T^2).
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

By (5.2), (11.2) directly implies the HCF bound requested in the question.

The exact reduction (6.2) says this is equivalently a large-prime gcd theorem for

\[
(b_r,A_r),
\qquad
\kappa_r=A_r/D_r,
\qquad
\operatorname{Supp}(D_r)\subseteq\{p:p\le r\}.
\]

So one concrete research direction is:

> Prove a Subspace-Theorem-like gcd estimate for a homogeneous P-recursive period `b_r` and the numerator of the **differentially algebraic, non-D-finite inhomogeneous period** `kappa_r`, exploiting their common Picard--Fuchs geometry rather than treating them as arbitrary integer sequences.

That is outside the current linear-recurrence/S-unit theorems but has exactly the right target implication.

A second genuinely new route would be to construct a fixed algebraic/etale realization of the pair `(b_r,Xi_r) mod p` for `r<p`, with a fixed nonzero geometric resultant controlling simultaneous zeros. The repository already has such a fixed toric realization for `b_r`; it does not yet have one for `Xi_r`.

Either would be substantive. By contrast, producing another integer of size `exp(c r)` divisible by `gcd(b_r,Xi_r)` and multiplying over `r` merely reproduces the quadratic height budget and should not be counted as progress.

# 12. Literature scope actually used

The following results are the relevant external theorems; I list them because their hypotheses are exactly what is being audited.

1. **W. A. Harris Jr. and Y. Sibuya**, *The reciprocals of solutions of linear ordinary differential equations*, Advances in Mathematics **58** (1985), 119--132, DOI `10.1016/0001-8708(85)90113-6`.
   - Used for: `y` and `1/y` both D-finite => `y'/y` algebraic.

2. **F. Beukers and C. A. M. Peters**, *A family of K3 surfaces and zeta(3)*, J. reine angew. Math. **351** (1984), 42--54, DOI `10.1515/crll.1984.351.42`.

3. **C. Peters**, *Monodromy and Picard-Fuchs equations for families of K3-surfaces and elliptic curves*, Ann. Sci. ENS **19** (1986), 583--607, DOI `10.24033/asens.1519`.
   - Used for: the irreducible/infinite projective monodromy input in the Apéry K3 variation.

4. **Y. Bugeaud, P. Corvaja, U. Zannier**, *An upper bound for the G.C.D. of `a^n-1` and `b^n-1`*, Math. Z. **243** (2003), 79--84.
   - Scope: multiplicative-torus/exponential orbit, not P-recursive periods.

5. **P. Corvaja and U. Zannier**, *A lower bound for the height of a rational function at S-unit points*, Monatsh. Math. **144** (2004), 203--224, DOI `10.1007/s00605-004-0273-0`.
   - Scope: fixed finitely generated multiplicative group and fixed polynomial/rational functions.

6. **P. Corvaja and U. Zannier**, *Finiteness of integral values for the ratio of two linear recurrences*, Invent. Math. **149** (2002), 431--451, DOI `10.1007/s002220200221`.
   - Scope: constant-coefficient linear recurrences/Hadamard ring, not arbitrary P-recursive sequences.

7. **V. Dimitrov**, *A note on a generalization of the Hadamard quotient theorem*, arXiv:1309.1920.
   - Relevant warning: extending coefficientwise quotient rigidity beyond the classical rational/linear-recurrence setting is itself a nontrivial problem; it cannot be assumed for the `b_r` denominator in (4.2).

No theorem in this list has hypotheses matching the pair `(b_r,Xi_r)` strongly enough to imply (11.1) or (11.2).

# Final scoped conclusion

The transverse HCF problem does **not** collapse into classical arithmetic of P-recursive sequences.

The hierarchy is now precise:

\[
F:
\quad
\text{rational diagonal}
\Rightarrow\text{G-function/D-finite/P-recursive};
\]

\[
\mathcal G=\frac1{F^2\sqrt\Delta}:
\quad
\text{integral + differentially algebraic, but not D-finite};
\]

\[
K:
\quad
\text{differentially algebraic, but not D-finite};
\]

\[
X:
\quad
\text{integral Hadamard transform of the reciprocal period, with D-finite status not settled by the natural theory}.
\]

The exact large-prime arithmetic is

\[
p\mid b_r,\ p\mid\Xi_r
\iff
p\mid b_r,\ \kappa_r\equiv0\pmod p
\qquad(p>r),
\]

not a universal exclusion. The actual events `(13,17)` and `(492,2237)` prove that a pointwise `p>r` theorem is impossible.

The natural Casoratian/resultant is exactly `Xi_r`, whose logarithmic height is `2r log(17+12 sqrt(2))+O(log r)`. Aggregating that carrier costs

\[
\log(17+12\sqrt2)T^2+O(T\log T),
\]

so the characteristic-zero height method lands exactly on the forbidden quadratic scale.

Accordingly, the clean theorem still worth proving is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r\le T}\log\operatorname{rad}_{>r}\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)=o(T^2),
}
\]

or the stronger pointwise `o(r)` version. Achieving it requires new arithmetic control of common prime support (or a new fixed geometric realization/resultant for the pair), not another exponential-height determinant.
