# F-isocrystal equidistribution in defining characteristic

## Main conclusion

There is currently no crystalline analogue of Katz equidistribution that gives the required cross-prime independence of the Apéry zero sets. The obstruction is structural: Katz equidistribution studies Frobenius conjugacy classes in a fixed l-adic representation as points vary, whereas the condition

```
p | b_r
```

is a mod-p condition on the same characteristic in which the Frobenius object is defined.

This is a defining-characteristic problem, and it is substantially harder.

---

## 1. Is there a p-adic analogue of Katz equidistribution?

For a fixed p, the crystalline theory is powerful. The relevant objects are:

* F-crystals and overconvergent F-isocrystals (Berthelot);
* p-adic differential equations;
* unit-root F-isocrystals;
* Cartier-Manin operators.

Dwork theory describes Frobenius eigenvalues p-adically. For example, the unit-root part of the zeta function of a family can often be described by a p-adic analytic function.

However, this does not produce a Sato-Tate theorem in characteristic p.

The reason is that the p-adic topology and the finite-field variation are different. Katz's theorem uses Deligne's theory of weights and l-adic monodromy with l different from p. In defining characteristic the representation is no longer semisimple in the same way, and weights do not give the same compact-group equidistribution statement.

Known results:

* Katz, *Slope filtration of F-crystals* and related work: p-adic monodromy and slopes.
* Crew, Kedlaya: p-adic differential equations and semistable reduction.
* Chiarellotto-Tsuzuki: rigid cohomology and Frobenius structures.

None gives the distribution of zeros of Frobenius traces modulo p as the parameter varies through F_p.

---

## 2. Can Dwork theory improve zero-density bounds?

Dwork theory is relevant, but it attacks a different invariant.

The unit-root method studies the characteristic polynomial

```
T^2 - a(t)T + p^w
```

of Frobenius and especially the p-adic valuation of its roots.

The Apéry zero condition is

```
a(t) ≡ 0 mod p.
```

This is a condition on the trace modulo the residue field, not on slopes.

A supersingularity-type analogy exists: for elliptic curves, the vanishing of the Hasse invariant detects supersingular points. Crystalline theory controls those loci geometrically.

For the Apéry K3 family, the identity

```
H_p(t)=B_p(t)^2
```

is precisely a Hasse invariant phenomenon. Dwork theory can explain why this polynomial appears, but existing theory does not provide bounds on correlations of the roots of B_p for different p.

Therefore Dwork may refine the single-prime estimate

```
|Z_p|=O(p^{2/3}),
```

but does not currently imply CRT independence.

---

## 3. Independence of Hasse invariants B_p across p

The desired statement would be something like:

```
(B_p mod p, B_q mod q)
```

behaves independently for p != q.

No theorem of this form is known.

The reason is that B_p and B_q are not reductions of one polynomial over Z. They are different characteristic-p shadows of the same motive.

There are strong characteristic-by-characteristic results:

* Ogus crystalline Torelli describes the period map of K3 surfaces in characteristic p.
* Nygaard-Ogus study ordinary loci and canonical lifts.
* Katz studies Hasse invariants and their geometry.

But these theories compare objects inside one characteristic. They do not give a horizontal independence theorem as p varies.

The missing theorem would be a kind of "horizontal crystalline monodromy": a statement that the reductions of the same motive at different primes behave like independent random fibers.

Such a theorem is not presently available.

---

## 4. Does Ogus crystalline Torelli give horizontal independence?

No, not directly.

Ogus's crystalline Torelli theorem identifies K3 surfaces in characteristic p from their crystalline period data. It gives a period map

```
X -> crystalline period domain.
```

The theorem is local in p. It controls deformation theory and the geometry of the ordinary/supersingular strata.

The required statement would involve the family of period maps

```
Per_p : X(F_p) -> D_p
```

for infinitely many primes p and would require proving that these maps have no arithmetic correlation across characteristics.

No such horizontal theorem is known.

---

## 5. What would actually solve the Apéry CRT problem?

The desired estimate is

```
Σ_{p != q}
| #{m<X^2: m mod p in Z_p, m mod q in Z_q}
 - |Z_p||Z_q|X^2/(pq) |
=o(X^4/(log X)^3).
```

A sufficient route would be one of the following:

### Route A: a motivic large sieve

Construct a global object whose reductions at p encode B_p and prove a large sieve inequality for its defining-characteristic fibers.

This would generalize Kowalski's large sieve for Frobenius to p-adic/finite-field reductions.

### Route B: a two-prime crystalline correlation theorem

Prove that for p != q:

```
Z_p x Z_q
```

has the expected density among CRT classes.

This is exactly the missing independence statement.

### Route C: return to l-adic geometry

Avoid defining characteristic by expressing b_r mod p through a fixed l-adic sheaf and then applying Deligne/Katz machinery. The difficulty is that the zero indicator is nonlinear and the modulus equals the parameter prime.

---

## Final assessment

1. A p-adic analogue of Katz equidistribution for this problem is not known.
2. Dwork theory explains the crystalline Frobenius and Hasse invariant structure but does not currently give cross-prime zero-density.
3. Independence of the Hasse polynomials B_p across characteristics is a genuinely new horizontal arithmetic statement.
4. Ogus crystalline Torelli is powerful but has no known horizontal independence consequence.

The missing estimate is therefore not a hidden consequence of existing motivic theory. It appears to require a new "horizontal crystalline monodromy" or defining-characteristic large sieve theorem.
