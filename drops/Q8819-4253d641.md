# Row degree versus the Apéry projective recurrence: a definitive audit

## Executive verdict

The local Möbius/transfer-matrix structure does **not**, with current technology, give a nontrivial uniform upper bound for

\[
K_X(m)=\#\{X<p\le 2X:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad 0\le m<X^2.
\]

The obstruction is a quantifier change:

* for one fixed prime \(p\), the projective recurrence controls the orbit as the index \(d\) varies in \(\mathbf F_p\);
* the row-degree problem fixes one integer \(m\) and varies the residue characteristic \(p\), sampling one moving index \(mmod p\) from each unrelated finite field.

No theorem turns prime-by-prime orbit equidistribution into uniform control of that cross-characteristic diagonal.

There are also two important corrections to the proposed setup.

1. **The Apéry dynamics is nonautonomous.** It is a product of matrices \(T_d\) depending on \(d\); it is not the orbit of one element of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\). Consequently the statement that the relevant orbit period divides \(|\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)|=p(p^2-1)\), or that there is a single generator of order approximately \(p\), is not the structure proved in the repository.
2. If \(m=qp+r<p^2\), Lucas gives
   
   \[
   b_m\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
   \]
   
   Hence
   
   \[
   p\mid b_m
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   q\in Z_p\ 	ext{or}\ r\in Z_p,
   \]
   
   not merely \(r=mmod p\in Z_p\). The lower-digit condition counted by \(K_X(m)\) nevertheless implies \(p\mid b_m\), so
   
   \[
   \boxed{K_X(m)\le \omega_X(b_m)},
   \qquad
   \omega_X(N):=\#\{X<p\le2X:p\mid N\}.
   \]

Thus a uniform shell-factor theorem

\[
\omega_X(b_m)\le X^{o(1)}
\qquad(m<X^2)
\tag{UDR}
\]

would rule out the anchored star and would prove the Apéry GCD conjecture. But no theorem remotely this strong is known for Apéry numbers, for general holonomic sequences, or as a consequence of \(abc\), Faltings, Bombieri--Lang, the Subspace Theorem, primitive-divisor theory, or the affine sieve.

The strongest elementary information is only

\[
K_X(m)\log X
\le \log\!\prod_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_m}}p
\le \log b_m=O(m),
\]

and therefore, for \(m<X^2\),

\[
K_X(m)\le
\min\left\{\pi(2X)-\pi(X),\frac{O(X^2)}{\log X}\right\}
\ll\frac X{\log X}.
\tag{0.1}
\]

The prime-counting term is the useful one. It is exactly the critical scale saturated by the anchored-star model.

The correct live route is therefore still an aggregate cross-prime statement such as \((\mathrm{HM})_8\), its exact pair--Palm form, or LGTQ-MTD. A direct pointwise bound such as (UDR) would be stronger than what is needed, not a consequence of the presently known recurrence structure.

## 1. The local projective dynamics is not a cyclic group orbit

### 1.1 The exact transfer system

In the gauge used in `FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex`, set

\[
x_n=[n^3b_{n-1}:b_n]\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p).
\]

For \(u\ne-1\), define

\[
M_u=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&(u+1)^6\\
-1&P(u)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P(u)=34u^3+51u^2+27u+5.
\]

Then

\[
x_{n+1}=M_nx_n,
\qquad
\det M_u=(u+1)^6.
\]

Thus the state at time \(d\) is obtained from the partial product

\[
M_{d-1}M_{d-2}\cdots M_0,
\]

not from a power \(M^d\) of one fixed matrix.

At the seam \(u=-1\pmod p\), the determinant vanishes. Therefore the regular projective transport itself is not one invertible \(p\)-periodic cyclic action through the entire index circle. The Lucas congruence supplies a digit law beyond the seam, but it does not turn the first-\(p\)-digit dynamics into powers of one element of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\).

Consequently:

* Lagrange's theorem about the order of a **fixed** element of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\) is inapplicable to the time-dependent word;
* the observed order of a total monodromy product, even if large, does not control the number of target visits among its partial products;
* the claim that the Apéry orbit has a single generator of order approximately \(p\) is not a theorem in the current source.

One may augment the state by the phase \(dmod p\), obtaining a finite nonautonomous dynamical system. Finiteness gives eventual periodicity of the augmented state in a formal sense. It gives no useful bound on how often the first \(p\) states meet one marked point.

### 1.2 What the local recurrence really proves

The projective formalism proves strong same-characteristic statements:

* reflection symmetry;
* no adjacent zeros;
* exact gap-polynomial criteria;
* degree bounds for fixed gaps;
* energy and fiber estimates;
* restart identities after a zero.

For instance, a pair of projective returns at distance \(h\) is governed by a polynomial \(N_h(r)\) of degree \(3(h-1)\). Such information constrains the geometry of one set \(Z_p\) or one projective fiber.

It does not relate the selected points

\[
mmod p\in\mathbf F_p
\]

as \(p\) varies. The values \(mmod p\) live in different fields, the transfer words have different lengths, and the target incidence is in the defining characteristic each time.

### 1.3 The vertical-to-horizontal quantifier gap

The numerical statement

> for a typical \(p\), the orbit visits about \(1-e^{-1}\) of the projective points and the target fiber has Poisson-sized multiplicity

is a **vertical** statement: \(p\) is fixed and \(d\) varies.

The row-degree statement

\[
K_X(m)=\sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathbf1_{Z_p}(mmod p)
\]

is **horizontal and diagonal**: \(m\) is fixed and both the field and the marked index vary.

Even perfect equidistribution for every individual orbit would not by itself exclude a conspiracy in which the one prescribed index \(mmod p\) is a zero for many \(p\). A family of uniformly distributed sets can all contain one externally prescribed point. Spatial discrepancy relative to the prescribed section is additional information.

This is exactly the distinction isolated in Q8775 as LGTQ-MTD and in Q8811 as the short-interval CRT landing problem.

## 2. The exact relation between row degree and prime factors of \(b_m\)

Let \(p>X\) and \(m<X^2\). Since \(p^2>X^2>m\), write

\[
m=q_pp+r_p,
\qquad 0\le q_p,r_p<p.
\]

The Apéry--Lucas congruence gives

\[
b_m\equiv b_{q_p}b_{r_p}\pmod p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
r_p\in Z_p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_m.
\tag{2.2}
\]

It follows that

\[
\boxed{
K_X(m)\le\omega_X(b_m)\le\pi(2X)-\pi(X).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The converse in (2.2) is false in general: \(p\mid b_{q_p}\) may be the reason that \(p\mid b_m\). This is the leading-digit channel. For the GCD problem that channel is neutralized by the numerator congruence, but a bound on the entire shell factor count \(\omega_X(b_m)\) is still a valid, stronger sufficient condition.

Define the shell radical

\[
R_X(m)=\prod_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_m}}p.
\]

Then

\[
X^{\omega_X(b_m)}<R_X(m)\le |b_m|,
\]

so

\[
\omega_X(b_m)\lerac{\log|b_m|}{\log X}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The elementary Apéry bound used in the repository is

\[
b_m\le(m+1)64^m,
\]

and the precise asymptotic growth has base

\[
17+12\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^4.
\]

Hence

\[
\log b_m=O(m).
\]

For \(m\asymp X^2\), equation (2.4) gives only \(O(X^2/\log X)\), worse than the prime-counting cap \(O(X/\log X)\).

This proves a useful negative statement:

> **The exponential height of a single Apéry number contains far too much room to control its shell factors at \(p\asymp\sqrt m\).**

## 3. Why the anchored star is not contradicted by the size of \(b_m\)

The anchored-star obstruction marks one row \(m_0\asymp X^2\) for essentially every shell prime. If this exact pattern occurred for Apéry, then

\[
\prod_{X<p\le2X}p\mid b_{m_0}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

By the prime number theorem,

\[
\log\prod_{X<p\le2X}p
=artheta(2X)-artheta(X)
=(1+o(1))X.
\tag{3.2}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
\log b_{m_0}=\Theta(m_0)=\Theta(X^2).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the hypothetical product of **all** shell primes uses only an \(O(1/X)\) fraction of the available logarithmic height. It remains compatible even if every shell prime occurs with valuation exactly one.

This height budget is decisive. Neither integrality nor the known growth asymptotic can rule out the star.

The comparison in `pairpalm_result.tex` is apt. The sequence

\[
c_m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,m)
\]

has

\[
\log c_m=O(m),
\]

but every prime in \((X,2X]\) divides \(c_m\) as soon as \(m\ge2X\). Exponential height and a common integer sequence can coexist with maximal shell alignment.

Of course \(c_m\) is not Apéry. The point is logical: any proof using only

* a common integer \(b_m\),
* exponential height,
* sparse local zero sets,
* reflection and no adjacency,
* second moments or bounded pair codegrees,

cannot exclude the anchored star. The repository construction makes this no-go exact.

## 4. Why a Lucas-sequence rank-of-apparition theorem would help—and why Apéry does not have one

For a classical constant-coefficient Lucas sequence \(U_n\), divisibility is controlled by powers of one fixed \(2\times2\) matrix. Under standard hypotheses there is a rank of apparition \(z(p)\) such that

\[
p\mid U_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z(p)\mid n
\]

up to familiar exceptional cases. Primitive-divisor theorems, such as Bilu--Hanrot--Voutier, exploit this autonomous algebraic-group structure.

If an analogous theorem held for Apéry numbers, then many shell primes dividing one \(b_m\) would force many ranks to divide one integer \(m\). Divisor-function bounds might then lead to \(m^{o(1)}\)-type row control.

But the required hypotheses fail:

1. the recurrence coefficients depend on \(n\);
2. the transfer is a nonautonomous word, not powers of one matrix;
3. the sequence is not a strong divisibility sequence;
4. the zero set \(Z_p\) is not a subgroup or a coset in the index variable.

Already

\[
b_1=5,\qquad b_2=73
\]

shows that the most elementary divisibility pattern \(b_1\mid b_2\) fails. There is no proved identity

\[
\gcd(b_m,b_n)=b_{\gcd(m,n)}
\]

or any replacement yielding a rank-of-apparition calculus.

The known primitive-divisor theorems therefore point in the wrong direction and apply to the wrong class of recurrences. They prove the **existence** of new prime factors in autonomous divisibility sequences; they do not upper-bound the number of prime factors in one dyadic shell, and they do not apply to the Apéry P-recursive sequence.

## 5. Part (a): can group theory constrain \(K_X(m)\)?

### Definitive answer

**Not from the presently known group-theoretic structure.**

The useful finite-group statements are all local in one characteristic. To bound \(K_X(m)\), one would need a theorem of the following genuinely new form:

> **Cross-characteristic marked-orbit large sieve.** The marked partial products of the Apéry transfer cocycle, evaluated at the moving indices \(mmod p\), do not concentrate on the zero divisor for more than \(X^{1-\delta}\), or preferably \(X^{o(1)}\), primes \(p\in(X,2X]\), uniformly for \(m<X^2\).

This is not a consequence of:

* large geometric monodromy;
* expansion in one finite group;
* the order of a local monodromy element;
* Sato--Tate or Chebotarev for conjugacy classes;
* projective occupancy statistics.

There are three separate barriers.

#### Barrier 1: the condition is marked, not conjugacy invariant

The event that a specified vector is sent to a specified line is a matrix-coefficient incidence, not a class function. Ordinary Chebotarev sees a conjugacy class of Frobenius. It loses the frame and the marked vector.

#### Barrier 2: the representation characteristic moves with the prime

The event is \(b_{mmod p}=0\pmod p\). It lives in the defining characteristic \(p\). Standard compatible-system arguments fix an auxiliary prime \(\ell\) and vary Frobenius primes \(p\ne\ell\). Here one is on the diagonal \(\ell=p\).

#### Barrier 3: one samples a deterministic section

Expansion or equidistribution normally controls an average over many group elements, many orbit points, or many starting points. Here one selected partial product is taken from each unrelated group. No averaging variable remains inside a fixed group.

The Peter--Weyl calculation of Q8785 compresses the local incidence to a Steinberg matrix coefficient, but then the desired theorem becomes a mixed-prime correlation estimate for moving Steinberg representations of dimensions \(p\). That is a cleaner formulation, not a proof.

## 6. Part (b): Faltings, Bombieri--Lang, Vojta, and the Subspace Theorem

### 6.1 Why Faltings does not apply

Faltings's theorem controls rational points on one fixed curve of genus at least two. The statement

\[
p_1p_2\cdots p_k\mid b_m
\]

for many primes is not the assertion that one obtains many rational points on a fixed curve. It is a statement about the vertical divisor of one integer \(b_m\) on

\[
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z.
\]

More geometrically, a prime divisor of \(b_m\) is a place where one integral section meets a divisor after reduction. One rational point can have a coordinate divisible by arbitrarily many primes. Faltings does not bound the number of places at which one fixed rational point meets a divisor.

Encoding \(k\) chosen prime divisibilities into Diophantine equations introduces the primes, their residue classes, and usually \(k\) auxiliary variables. The resulting varieties change with the primes and their complexity grows with \(k\). There is no fixed genus-\(\ge2\) curve to which uniform Faltings can be applied.

### 6.2 Why Bombieri--Lang does not apply

Bombieri--Lang predicts that rational points are not Zariski dense on a fixed variety of general type. It does not give a mesoscopic upper bound for the support of the vertical divisor of a single integer.

A Bombieri--Lang strategy would require a **uniform, fixed-complexity algebraization** of the statement that \(k\) shell primes divide \(b_m\). No such algebraization is known. The direct transfer word representing \(b_m\) has length \(m\), and its polynomial complexity grows with \(m\).

### 6.3 What Vojta's conjecture would say

Vojta's conjecture with truncated counting functions is powerful for primitive-divisor and dynamical Zsigmondy problems. It relates the radical-type contribution

\[
N^{(1)}(D,P)=\sum_p\min\{1,v_p(D(P))\}\log p
\]

to heights of a varying rational point \(P\).

In the present scale, however, the total height is \(\Theta(m)=\Theta(X^2)\), whereas the logarithmic mass of all shell primes is only \(\Theta(X)\). A height inequality of order \(X^2\) leaves the anchored star completely untouched.

Vojta could become relevant only after finding an auxiliary geometric object of height \(o(X)\) whose divisor contains every shell hit. The current object \(b_m\) has height \(\Theta(X^2)\).

### 6.4 Why the Subspace Theorem does not apply

The strongest GCD theorems derived from the Subspace Theorem—such as the Bugeaud--Corvaja--Zannier estimate for

\[
\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)
\]

with multiplicatively independent \(a,b\)—depend on a fixed finitely generated multiplicative group or fixed \(S\)-unit data.

For the Apéry row problem:

* the relevant set \(S=\{p:X<p\le2X\}\) grows with \(X\);
* the values \(b_m\) do not lie in a fixed finitely generated multiplicative group;
* one has one sequence value, not two independent multiplicative coordinates whose common factors force a subspace relation;
* only some factors of \(b_m\) are constrained to lie in \(S\).

This is precisely why the previously audited Subspace-Theorem route does not close.

### 6.5 Arithmetic-intersection formulation of the missing theorem

Let

\[
\operatorname{div}(b_m)=\sum_pv_p(b_m)[p]
\]

be the arithmetic divisor on \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z\). Its total Arakelov degree is

\[
\sum_pv_p(b_m)\log p=\log|b_m|=\Theta(m).
\]

The desired result asks for a strong upper bound on the number of support points with norm in one short multiplicative interval \((X,2X]\). General height and Diophantine-geometry theorems control total degree; they do not equidistribute that degree by prime norm.

That norm-localization is the extra arithmetic content.

## 7. Part (c): what \(abc\) and the radical can and cannot do

### 7.1 The stated \(\omega\)-bound does not require \(abc\)

For an arbitrary integer \(N\), the maximal-order estimate is

\[
\omega(N)\le(1+o(1))\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}.
\]

Since \(\log b_m=\Theta(m)\), this gives

\[
\omega(b_m)=O\left(\frac m{\log m}\right).
\]

This is an elementary maximal-order bound, not a consequence of \(abc\). It is also much larger than the trivial number of shell primes when \(m\asymp X^2\).

### 7.2 \(abc\) does not imply \(\operatorname{rad}(b_m)=b_m^{1-o(1)}\)

The \(abc\) conjecture applies to a coprime additive equation

\[
A+B=C.
\]

The Apéry recurrence supplies

\[
(m+1)^3b_{m+1}+m^3b_{m-1}=P(m)b_m.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Even after dividing common factors, an \(abc\) estimate for (7.1) controls the radical of the **product** of the three neighboring terms and polynomial coefficients. It does not isolate the radical of \(b_m\) at every index.

There is currently no theorem, conditional only on standard \(abc\), that gives

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}(b_m)=\log b_m+o(m)
\]

for every Apéry number.

### 7.3 Even the strongest radical statement would not rule out the star

Assume, far beyond what is known, that

\[
\operatorname{rad}(b_m)=b_m^{1-o(1)}
\]

or even that \(b_m\) is squarefree. Then the logarithmic radical is still \(\Theta(m)=\Theta(X^2)\). The product of every prime in \((X,2X]\) has logarithm only \(\Theta(X)\).

Thus the anchored-star pattern is fully compatible with:

* squarefreeness of all shell factors;
* \(v_p(b_m)=1\) for almost every prime factor;
* maximal possible radical;
* an \(abc\)-quality multiplicity theorem.

The radical controls multiplicities. The row-degree problem concerns how many distinct primes fall into one mesoscopic norm window. These are orthogonal questions at the present height scale.

## 8. Part (d): the Erdős--Kac heuristic and why no effective theorem applies

### 8.1 Correction to the random-integer scale

For a random integer \(N\) of size approximately \(e^{cm}\), the Erdős--Kac theorem predicts

\[
\omega(N)\approx\log\log N=\log m+O(1),
\]

with fluctuations of order \(\sqrt{\log m}\).

It does **not** predict \(cm/\log m\). The latter is the maximal-order scale for the number of distinct prime factors of an integer of that size.

For one dyadic shell,

\[
\mathbf E\,\omega_X(N)
\approx\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac1p
=\frac{\log2+o(1)}{\log X}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus a random integer usually has no prime factor in the shell and occasionally has one. Across \(X^2\) independent rows, the maximum shell count would heuristically be only

\[
O\left(\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}ight),
\]

well within \(X^{o(1)}\).

This is excellent evidence for the desired conclusion. It is not an available theorem for the deterministic sequence \(b_m\).

### 8.2 What an Erdős--Kac theorem for \(b_m\) would require

A sieve proof normally starts from uniform estimates

\[
\#\{m<M:d\mid b_m\}
=g(d)M+r_d
\]

for squarefree \(d\), together with a level-of-distribution bound for the summed remainders \(r_d\).

For Apéry numbers, Lucas gives the local density candidate

\[
g(p)\approx\frac{|Z_p|}{p},
\]

but the required multiplicativity and remainder control for products of distinct shell primes are exactly unknown.

At the critical scale \(M=X^2\), already

\[
pq>M
\qquad(p,q\in(X,2X]),
\]

so a pair of residue conditions has at most one representative below \(M\). For eight primes, the modulus is \(\asymp X^8\), and the expected probability that the unique CRT representative lands below \(X^2\) is \(X^{-6}\). Recovering that landing probability on aggregate is precisely \((\mathrm{HM})_8\).

Therefore an “effective Erdős--Kac theorem for Apéry numbers” strong enough to rule out the anchored star would not bypass the central problem. It would contain the missing high-order cross-prime discrepancy as its main sieve axiom.

### 8.3 Almost-all versus uniform

Erdős--Kac is an almost-all theorem. Even a perfect Apéry Erdős--Kac law could allow rare exceptional rows. The GCD conjecture needs control of every row.

To deduce a uniform maximum bound, one would need an exponential upper tail such as

\[
\#\{m<X^2:K_X(m)\ge t\}
\le X^2\left(\frac{C\lambda_X}{t}\right)^t
\tag{8.2}
\]

uniformly up to

\[
t\asymp\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}.
\]

This corresponds to factorial moments of growing order, not merely a fixed central-limit theorem. The fixed eighth moment is sufficient for the Apéry GCD target because the target threshold is much larger, but it is still unproved.

### 8.4 The strongest published Apéry prime-factor results point the other way

Luca and Shparlinski proved that, for a density-one set of indices,

\[
\omega(b_m)>c_0\log\log\log m
\]

and

\[
P(b_m)>c_0(\log m\log\log m)^{1/2},
\]

where \(P(N)\) denotes the largest prime factor. These are lower bounds for the abundance or size of prime divisors. They do not give an upper bound for the number of prime divisors in \((X,2X]\).

Florian Luca's theorem on prime divisors of binary holonomic sequences gives a cumulative lower bound: the product of the numerators and denominators of the first \(N\) nonzero terms has at least \(c\log N\) distinct prime factors. Again this is a lower, cumulative statement, not a pointwise dyadic upper bound.

No published Apéry theorem supplies a Sathe--Selberg law, Erdős--Kac law, or uniform shell large-deviation estimate for \(b_m\).

## 9. Part (e): can one prove \(\omega_X(b_m)\le X^arepsilon\)?

### Definitive answer

**Not with any known theorem.** Such a result would be a major new theorem about prime factors of Apéry numbers.

A useful formulation is:

> **Uniform dyadic radical sparsity (UDR).** For every \(arepsilon>0\), uniformly for \(0\le m<X^2\),
> 
> \[
> \omega_X(b_m)\ll_arepsilon X^arepsilon.
> \]

This would immediately give

\[
K_X(m)\ll_arepsilon X^arepsilon.
\]

For the master GCD sum, only shells \(X>\sqrt n\) need this estimate. Since \(n<X^2\) on such a shell,

\[
\sum_{\substack{X\text{ dyadic}\\\sqrt n<X\le n}}
K_X(n)\log(2X)
\ll n^arepsilon(\log n)^2=o(n)
\]

for any fixed \(arepsilon<1\). Thus UDR proves the all-\(n\) conjecture with enormous room.

In fact UDR is far stronger than necessary. Either of the following uniform statements would already close the GCD conjecture:

\[
K_X(m)\ll X^{1-\delta}
\qquad(\delta>0),
\tag{9.1}
\]

or

\[
K_X(m)\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1+\delta}}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

The current trivial bound is

\[
K_X(m)\ll X/\log X,
\]

so any fixed power or logarithmic saving from the prime-counting scale would be decisive.

### 9.1 Why this is stronger than \((\mathrm{HM})_8\)

The pointwise UDR assertion rules out every high-degree row directly. The eighth factorial moment is aggregate:

\[
F_8(X)=\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_8
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^8.
\]

If one row has degree \(T\), then

\[
(T)_8\le F_8(X).
\]

Using only the proved bound

\[
\lambda_X\ll X^{2/3}/\log X,
\]

HM\(_8\) gives the pointwise scale

\[
T\ll X^{11/12+o(1)},
\]

which is exactly sufficient for the GCD theorem but much weaker than \(X^arepsilon\). Under the empirical scale \(\lambda_X\asymp1/\log X\), HM\(_8\) would instead give roughly

\[
T\ll X^{1/4+o(1)}.
\]

A true \(X^{o(1)}\) maximum bound would require substantially higher moments or a separate arithmetic factorization theorem.

### 9.2 The exact anchored-star implication

The anchored star has

\[
K_X(m_0)\asymp X/\log X.
\]

If it were an actual Apéry configuration, equation (2.2) would imply that essentially every shell prime divides \(b_{m_0}\). UDR, (9.1), or (9.2) would all exclude it.

But the current recurrence identities do not exclude it because they act primewise. The anchored-star model was designed to satisfy:

* \(|Z_p|\le2\);
* reflection symmetry;
* no adjacency;
* interval sparsity;
* the proved second moment;
* bounded pair codegrees;
* the known fiber and collision-energy bounds;
* necessary gap-polynomial certificates.

Thus any proof of UDR must use genuinely new Apéry-specific **cross-prime** information, not a stronger repackaging of the existing local orbit facts.

---

## 10. Why affine sieve and expansion do not solve the problem

The Bourgain--Gamburd--Sarnak affine sieve studies a fixed orbit

\[
\mathcal O=\Gamma v\subset\mathbf Z^n
\]

of a finitely generated linear group and a fixed polynomial \(F\), using expansion of congruence quotients to count orbit points for which \(F\) has few prime factors.

The Apéry row problem has the wrong architecture:

1. the transfer matrices depend on the time \(d\), so there is no fixed finitely generated semigroup orbit whose \(m\)-th point is the Apéry state;
2. the quantity \(b_m\) is not a fixed polynomial evaluated on a bounded-complexity orbit point with word length \(O(\log m)\);
3. the question fixes one orbit point and asks how its prime divisors are distributed, whereas affine sieve averages over many orbit points;
4. the defining prime \(p\) also determines the finite field and the marked time \(mmod p\).

An autonomous, bounded-degree lift of the Apéry recurrence to a fixed algebraic-group orbit would be transformative. `oracleC_result.tex` instead shows that the direct transfer word has length \(m\), and the degrees of its entries grow linearly with the word length. No bounded-complexity lift is currently known.

## 11. What the Kummer--Mellin and K3 descriptions do provide

The repository proves the fixed toric identity

\[
b_j
=-\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^*)^3}
\Lambda(x,y,z)^j
\pmod p
\qquad(0\le j\le p-2),
\]

and interprets the corresponding cyclotomic lift as a Frobenius supertrace of a uniformly bounded-complexity Kummer sheaf on a fixed toric threefold.

This is real geometric structure. It gives a plausible route to:

* one-prime value distribution;
* Mellin-transform monodromy;
* fixed-characteristic correlation bounds;
* better estimates for \(|Z_p|\) or local collision statistics.

It does not presently give the row bound. The exact zero condition is

\[
b_{mmod p}\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

which is the reduction modulo the **same prime \(p\)** of a cyclotomic trace whose character and coefficient field also vary with \(p\). For eight shell primes one needs a correlation of eight such defining-characteristic divisibility events, each in a different finite field, together with localization of the CRT representative to \([0,X^2)\).

Deligne's theorem is a single-field theorem. Tensoring eight sheaves over eight different residue characteristics is not an available geometric operation that produces the required short-interval integer count.

The Kummer--Mellin description would become decisive if one could prove an adelic statement such as:

\[
\sum_{m<X^2}\prod_{i=1}^k
\left(\mathbf1_{Z_{p_i}}(m)-\frac{|Z_{p_i}|}{p_i}ight)
\]

has sufficient aggregate cancellation uniformly over shell prime tuples. That is a new mixed-characteristic large-sieve theorem—essentially HM\(_k\) or LGTQ-MTD in geometric language.

## 12. The most promising precise approaches

### 12.1 The current best target: pair--Palm HM\(_8\)

The exact repository reduction is

\[
F_8(X)=\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}^{[6]},
\]

where

\[
T_{p,q}^{[6]}
=\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}(K_X(m)-2)_6.
\]

Writing

\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|,
\qquad
\lambda_{p,q}=\sum_{\ell\notin\{p,q\}}rac{|Z_\ell|}{\ell},
\]

HM\(_8\) is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(T_{p,q}^{[6]}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}^6\right)_+
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^8.}
\tag{12.1}
\]

This asks for the correct sixth factorial extension after conditioning on an actual two-prime intersection. It directly attacks the anchored-star mechanism: in the star, conditioning on any active pair leaves approximately \(X/\log X\) additional active primes at the same row.

### 12.2 A low-height global detector

A direct pointwise proof would follow if every lower-digit shell hit forced

\[
p\mid D_{m,X}
\]

for one nonzero integer \(D_{m,X}\) satisfying

\[
\log|D_{m,X}|=o(X)
\]

or even \(O(X^{1-\delta})\). Then the product of too many shell primes could not divide \(D_{m,X}\).

The obvious detector is \(D_{m,X}=b_m\), but

\[
\log b_m=\Theta(X^2),
\]

which is too large by a factor \(X\). The fixed-polynomial-detector route was audited in Q8763 and appears incompatible with the moving Frobenius nature of \(Z_p\).

A successful detector would be a genuine breakthrough: it would compress a length-\(m\) recurrence word to an arithmetic certificate of height \(o(\sqrt m)\).

### 12.3 A rank-of-apparition or algebraic-group law

If one could prove that \(p\mid b_m\) forces an order or rank \(z(p)\) to divide one of \(O(m^{o(1)})\) explicit integers, then divisor bounds might give UDR. No such order law is known, and the nonautonomous recurrence makes the classical Lucas mechanism unavailable.

### 12.4 A mixed-characteristic Frobenius large sieve

A theorem controlling the moving marked Frobenius incidences across primes could prove either HM\(_8\) or a direct row bound. It would need to retain the marked vector, handle defining characteristic, and see the short integer interval inside a product modulus much larger than the interval. Existing Chebotarev, Katz equidistribution, Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel trace-function theory, and Kowalski's Frobenius large sieve do not have this combination of quantifiers.

### 12.5 A uniform Apéry Erdős--Kac theorem with large deviations

One could conjecture a Poisson-binomial law for

\[
K_X(m),\qquad m<X^2,
\]

with mean \(\lambda_X\), plus a uniform high-tail estimate. This would rule out the star. Proving it would require exactly the multi-prime divisibility estimates currently named HM\(_k\). It is a clean conjectural language, not a shortcut.

## 13. Direct answers to the five questions

### (a) Can the group-theoretic Möbius structure constrain \(K(m)\)?

Not with current results. The Apéry projective dynamics is a nonautonomous cocycle \(M_{d-1}\cdots M_0\), not powers of one element of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\). Local orbit size, element order, monodromy, reflection, and same-prime energy do not control the cross-prime diagonal \(p\mapsto mmod p\).

A useful theorem would have to be a new cross-characteristic marked-orbit large sieve or a rank-of-apparition law. Neither exists.

### (b) Is there a Faltings/Bombieri--Lang analogue?

There is no direct applicable analogue. Many prime divisors of one integer are many vertical intersections of one section, not many rational points on a fixed high-genus curve. Encoding the primes produces a moving, growing-complexity family rather than one fixed variety. Bombieri--Lang and Faltings therefore do not yield a shell-factor bound.

Vojta's truncated counting framework can prove primitive-divisor phenomena in autonomous dynamics, but its natural height scale is \(m\asymp X^2\), while the complete shell has logarithmic mass only \(X\). It leaves the anchored star far below the available height budget.

### (c) Does \(abc\) or a large radical help?

No. The bound \(\omega(b_m)=O(m/\log m)\) is an elementary maximal-order bound, not an \(abc\) consequence. Standard \(abc\) does not imply \(\operatorname{rad}(b_m)=b_m^{1-o(1)}\) for every Apéry number. Even granting squarefreeness or maximal radical, all shell primes can divide \(b_m\) once each because their product has logarithm \(\Theta(X)\), whereas \(\log b_m=\Theta(X^2)\).

### (d) Is there an effective Erdős--Kac theorem for \(b_m\)?

No. A random integer predicts total \(\omega\) of order \(\log m\) and shell mean \((\log2)/\log X\), not \(m/\log m\). No effective Erdős--Kac or Sathe--Selberg theorem is known for Apéry values. To prove one, one would need uniform estimates for \(d\mid b_m\) over products of shell primes; at the critical interval length this is precisely the HM/LGTQ short-CRT obstruction.

The existing Luca--Shparlinski theorem gives density-one lower bounds on \(\omega(b_m)\) and the largest prime factor, not shell-wise upper bounds.

### (e) Can we prove that \(b_m\) has at most \(X^arepsilon\) prime factors in \((X,2X]\)?

No known method proves this. It is a strong and natural conjecture—uniform dyadic radical sparsity—and it would immediately rule out the anchored star and solve the GCD conjecture. But it is substantially beyond current recurrence, sieve, \(abc\), Subspace-Theorem, and monodromy technology.

The weakest currently credible decisive route is not this pointwise \(X^arepsilon\) theorem; it is the fixed eighth factorial moment HM\(_8\), equivalently the aggregate pair--Palm bound (12.1).

---

## Final conclusion

The same rational recurrence appearing modulo every prime is genuine global structure, but the currently extracted consequences are all **local or pairwise**. They constrain each \(Z_p\), same-prime gaps, and intersections of two columns. They do not constrain the support of the vertical divisor of one value \(b_m\) inside the shell \((X,2X]\).

The anchored star identifies the exact missing feature: many individually sparse local fibers can align on one integer row. For actual Apéry numbers such an alignment would mean that many shell primes divide one \(b_m\), but the exponential height of \(b_m\) leaves room for every shell prime. Neither maximal radical nor squarefreeness changes that.

Therefore the definitive diagnosis is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Local projective recurrence structure alone does not bound row degree.}
}
\]

A proof must add one of the following genuinely cross-prime ingredients:

1. HM\(_8\) / the pair--Palm sixth-extension estimate;
2. LGTQ-MTD or its Steinberg form;
3. a mixed-characteristic marked-Frobenius large sieve;
4. a new low-height detector for shell hits;
5. a previously unknown rank-of-apparition/divisibility law for the Apéry recurrence.

At present, none of items 3--5 is available. HM\(_8\) remains the narrowest explicit route already integrated into the repository.
