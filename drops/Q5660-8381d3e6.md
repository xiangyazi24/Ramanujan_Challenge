# Mixed denominator curvature for the two Apéry rational sequences

## Verdict

Let

\[
T_n=\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_{n-p}\}.
\]

For every sufficiently large \(n\), and every prime \(p\in(n/2,n]\), the two positive-curvature conditions in the question are **the same local equation**. More precisely,

\[
 p\mid \kappa_n\iff p\mid b_{n-p}\iff p\mid\delta_n,
\]

and in fact

\[
 v_p(\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n))=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&p\in T_n,\\
 0,&p\notin T_n.
 \end{cases}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n)
 =\prod_{p\in T_n}p.}
\]

Thus a bound

\[
 \log\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n)=o(n)
\]

would prove the unresolved top-half statement, but it is not a compression of that statement: its top-half radical is exactly the target radical. The gcd can remove lower- and middle-prime false positives, but it cannot remove even one genuine top-half target.

I also find no mixed Casoratian or resultant which creates a second target-selective condition. The natural mixed determinant reduces to the ordinary Apéry Wronskian plus a term carrying a central-binomial factor; at a candidate prime it is divisible for a purely universal carry reason, whether or not the target equation holds. Locally, after imposing the explicit Apéry initial states, the two curvature equations generate the same principal ideal \((b_r)\) over \(\mathbf Z_p\). The local Smith rank is one.

The strongest exact conclusion is therefore negative but sharp:

> The two rational \(G\)-functions provide two characteristic-zero realizations of the same large-prime residue condition. Their intersection removes irrelevant primes outside the target range but gives no second independent equation and no proved sublinear-height carrier.

## 1. Notation and the two curvature carriers

Write the companion coefficient in lowest terms as

\[
 a_m=\frac{A_m}{C_m},\qquad \gcd(A_m,C_m)=1,
\]

and define

\[
 \kappa_n=
 \frac{\gcd(C_{n-1},C_{n+1})}
      {\gcd(C_{n-1},C_n,C_{n+1})}.
\]

For the central-binomial normalization put

\[
 K_m=\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor},\qquad
 \frac{b_m}{K_m}=\frac{u_m}{q_m}
\]

in lowest terms, so

\[
 q_m=\frac{K_m}{\gcd(K_m,b_m)},
\]

and

\[
 \delta_n=
 \frac{\gcd(q_{n-1},q_{n+1})}
      {\gcd(q_{n-1},q_n,q_{n+1})}.
\]

Prime by prime,

\[
 v_p(\delta_n)=
 \max\{0,\min(v_p(q_{n-1}),v_p(q_{n+1}))-v_p(q_n)\}.
\]

The repository already proves for the central-binomial carrier that for \(n>10\)

\[
 \operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}\delta_n
 =\prod_{p\in T_n}p.
\]

It also proves the exact local top-half pattern

\[
 (v_p(q_{n-1}),v_p(q_n),v_p(q_{n+1}))=(1,0,1)
\]

at a target.

## 2. Exact top-half local comparison

Let

\[
 p\in(n/2,n],\qquad n=p+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

The endpoint residues \(r=0,p-2,p-1\) are nontargets for \(p\ge7\), so a genuine target lies in

\[
 1\le r\le p-3.
\]

### 2.1 Central-binomial curvature

In this interior range, the carry law gives

\[
 v_p(K_{n-1})=v_p(K_n)=v_p(K_{n+1})=1.
\]

Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
 b_{n+j}\equiv b_1b_{r+j}=5b_{r+j}\pmod p,
 \qquad j=-1,0,1.
\]

Consecutive Apéry zeros are impossible modulo \(p\): the Casoratian of the two fundamental solutions is a \(p\)-unit at all indices \(<p\). Hence

\[
 p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow p\nmid b_{r-1}b_{r+1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
 v_p(q_{n+j})=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&p\mid b_{r+j},\\
 1,&p\nmid b_{r+j},
 \end{cases}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{v_p(\delta_n)=1_{p\mid b_r}.}
\]

The valuation is exactly one because each of the three central binomial coefficients contains \(p\) only once.

### 2.2 Companion-denominator curvature

The companion block congruence for \(p>\sqrt n\), with

\[
 n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p,
\]

has the form that the low digit \(b_r\) controls the denominator hole unless the fixed upper companion coordinate vanishes. In the notation of the question, when

\[
 p\nmid a_q,
\]

positive companion curvature is equivalent to

\[
 p\mid b_r.
\]

For the top half, \(q=1\) and \(a_1=6\), so every \(p\ge7\) is automatically outside the exceptional upper-digit locus. The exact proved valuation is

\[
 \boxed{v_p(\kappa_n)=\min(3,v_p(b_n)).}
\]

Since Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
 b_n=b_{p+r}\equiv5b_r\pmod p,
\]

we obtain

\[
 v_p(\kappa_n)>0\iff p\mid b_r.
\]

Combining the two boxes,

\[
 \boxed{
 v_p(\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n))
 =\min(v_p(\kappa_n),v_p(\delta_n))
 =1_{p\mid b_r}.}
\]

This proves the exact radical identity announced in the verdict.

## 3. The local ideal and Smith form

The preceding equivalence can be made stronger than a support comparison.

Fix an interior top-half candidate \(p\), put \(r=n-p\), and work in the local ring \(\mathbf Z_p\). The explicit upper digit is \(a_1=6\in\mathbf Z_p^\times\), while the central carry multiplicity is one. After removing these fixed units, the two curvature defects have local defining functions

\[
 F_1=u_1 b_r,
 \qquad
 F_2=u_2 b_r,
 \qquad u_1,u_2\in\mathbf Z_p^\times.
\]

Therefore

\[
 (F_1,F_2)=(b_r)
\]

as ideals of \(\mathbf Z_p\). Equivalently, the \(2\times1\) local equation matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}u_1\\u_2\end{pmatrix}
\]

has Smith normal form

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

There is one equation, not two. The use of the actual Apéry initial states is crucial here:

- the companion upper coordinate is the explicit unit \(a_1=6\);
- the Gessel upper digit is the explicit unit \(b_1=5\);
- the neighboring nonvanishing is supplied by the exact Apéry Casoratian.

Thus this is not a generic-recurrence countermodel. It is a computation for the distinguished Apéry solution.

At higher \(p\)-adic order the two valuations retain different truncations—\(\kappa_n\) can see up to three powers, while \(\delta_n\) sees only the single central carry—but their common part is still

\[
 v_p(\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n))
 =\min(1,v_p(b_r)).
\]

So the gcd deliberately discards all higher target multiplicity and retains exactly the radical channel.

## 4. Lower and middle primes

Let \(p>\sqrt n\) and write

\[
 n=qp+r,\qquad 1\le q<\sqrt n.
\]

The companion carrier has the exact qualification

\[
 p\nmid a_q
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 p\mid\kappa_n\iff p\mid b_r.
\]

If \(p\mid a_q\), the upper digit is singular and companion curvature may have an additional contribution or may fail to distinguish the low-digit zero. This is a genuine finite-upper-digit exceptional locus, not a top-half issue.

For the central normalization, the local condition is controlled by the three carry exponents

\[
 c_j=v_p(K_{n+j})
\]

and the low-digit values supplied by Lucas. In general

\[
 v_p(q_{n+j})=c_j-\min(c_j,v_p(b_{n+j})).
\]

Hence

\[
 v_p(\delta_n)=
 \max\{0,
 \min(c_{-1}-e_{-1},c_1-e_1)-(c_0-e_0)
 \},
\]

where

\[
 e_j=\min(c_j,v_p(b_{n+j})).
\]

Outside the top half, the carry vector \((c_{-1},c_0,c_1)\) is no longer uniformly \((1,1,1)\). Thus \(\delta_n\) may contain carry-boundary primes unrelated to an isolated low-digit zero, and it may miss a low-digit zero if the neighboring carry exponents do not exceed the middle one.

Taking \(\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n)\) can indeed cancel some such lower/middle false positives. But this cancellation has no bearing on the required top-half estimate, because there the two supports coincide exactly. Formally, if

\[
 G_n=\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n),
\]

then

\[
 \log G_n
 \ge
 \sum_{p\in T_n}\log p.
\]

Therefore any proof of \(\log G_n=o(n)\) already contains a proof of the target radical estimate. No estimate for the discarded lower-prime part can provide the missing saving.

## 5. The natural mixed Casoratian

The ordinary Apéry Casoratian is exact. For the two solutions \(a_n,b_n\), define

\[
 W_n=a_n b_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n.
\]

The recurrence gives

\[
 W_{n+1}=\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}W_n,
\]

and \(W_1=6\), hence

\[
 \boxed{W_n=\frac6{n^3}.}
\]

Now put

\[
 y_n=\frac{b_n}{K_n}.
\]

The most immediate mixed determinant is

\[
 \mathcal M_n=a_ny_{n+1}-a_{n+1}y_n.
\]

After clearing the central denominators,

\[
 K_nK_{n+1}\mathcal M_n
 =K_n a_n b_{n+1}-K_{n+1}a_{n+1}b_n.
\]

Using

\[
 a_n b_{n+1}-a_{n+1}b_n=-\frac6{(n+1)^3},
\]

we obtain the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{
 K_nK_{n+1}\mathcal M_n
 =(K_n-K_{n+1})a_n b_{n+1}
 -\frac{6K_{n+1}}{(n+1)^3}.}
\]

This is the sharp natural mixed identity. It does not help.

At an interior top-half candidate, both \(K_n\) and \(K_{n+1}\) are divisible by \(p\). Therefore the cleared mixed determinant is divisible by \(p\) for **every candidate prime**, independently of whether \(p\mid b_n\). Its divisibility is caused by the universal central-binomial carry, not by the target equation.

If instead one divides out the carry factors, then on the target locus the ordinary Wronskian shows that the remaining factors \(a_n\) and \(b_{n+1}\) are units. The normalized determinant is therefore a unit or a predetermined pole, not a second zero.

The same calculation applies to the shifted determinants involving \(n-1,n\). Every adjacent mixed Casoratian decomposes into

1. the fixed rational Apéry Wronskian, and
2. a gauge-difference term involving \(K_{m+1}/K_m\).

Neither term creates a second target-selective congruence.

## 6. Resultants and larger mixed windows

The sequence \(a_n\) and the sequence \(b_n/K_n\) do not satisfy the same scalar recurrence: the latter is a gauge transform by \(K_n\). Conjugating the companion matrix by the diagonal gauge records exactly the central carry factors. Thus any bounded mixed-window resultant separates into:

- an Apéry transfer determinant, whose only state-independent invariant is the known Casoratian;
- powers and adjacent ratios of \(K_n\), which encode candidate-prime carries;
- state coordinates generated from \(b_r\) and one complementary unit coordinate.

On an interior top-half candidate, the transfer matrix is nonsingular modulo \(p\), and the target condition is one regular hyperplane

\[
 b_r=0.
\]

The companion denominator hole and the central-binomial denominator hole are two gauges of that same hyperplane. Eliminating the complementary state coordinate therefore leaves a power of \(b_r\), multiplied by units and universal carry factors. It cannot produce a second independent factor without an additional Apéry identity not implied by the recurrence, Lucas, reflection, or the Wronskian.

This explains why a higher mixed resultant does not improve the local Smith rank. Increasing the window adds transfer-equivalent coordinates, not new equations.

## 7. Height comparison

There are elementary one-sided bounds:

\[
 G_n=\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n)\mid\delta_n
\]

and the repository proves

\[
 \delta_n\mid2\gcd(b_n,K_n),
\]

with the factor \(2\) absent for even \(n\). Hence

\[
 G_n\mid2\gcd(b_n,K_n).
\]

This is not sublinear: the right side is exactly another established carrier for the same top-half radical, and no pointwise theorem gives

\[
 \log\gcd(b_n,K_n)=o(n).
\]

Likewise \(G_n\mid\kappa_n\), but the best general height control for the companion denominators is exponential. Taking the gcd does not by itself improve either estimate because the common top-half radical can already have logarithmic weight of order \(n\) under present knowledge.

The exact lower bound

\[
 \log G_n\ge\sum_{p\in T_n}\log p
\]

shows the logical equivalence:

\[
 \log G_n=o(n)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{p\in T_n}\log p=o(n).
\]

Conversely, after separately controlling lower/middle common primes, the target estimate would imply the same bound for \(G_n\). Thus the mixed gcd is a clean repackaging, not an independent route.

## 8. Precise negative conclusion and next missing ingredient

The requested mixed construction fails for a structural, Apéry-specific reason:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{At every interior top-half candidate, both curvatures generate the same local ideal }(b_{n-p}).}
\]

The actual initial states make the multipliers units: \(a_1=6\), \(b_1=5\). The Wronskian makes the neighboring coordinate a unit. The central carry has multiplicity one. Therefore the common local Smith module has one target coordinate and no torsion beyond that coordinate.

A positive mixed argument would require one genuinely new ingredient, for example:

\[
 p\mid b_r
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 p\mid H(n,r)
\]

where \(H\) is not a unit multiple of \(b_r\) modulo \(p\), and where the characteristic-zero values \(H(n,n-p)\) for all target primes are contained in an integer of logarithmic height \(o(n)\). Neither denominator curvature supplies such an \(H\).

So the final verdict is:

> **No proved sublinear mixed height is obtained.** The integer \(\gcd(\kappa_n,\delta_n)\) has exactly the unresolved top-half radical. Natural mixed Casoratians are either universal candidate-prime multiples or reduce to the fixed Apéry Wronskian. Bounded mixed resultants have local rank one. Any successful continuation must discover a second Apéry-specific horizontal invariant, not another normalization of the same low-digit zero.
