# P3.2 — Overconvergent modular forms and branch-coupling for ZM₂

## Short verdict

The proposed overconvergent modular-form route is **conceptually related but not currently the correct object for the Apéry zero problem**. Coleman/Buzzard eigenvarieties control variation of Frobenius eigenvalues in a genuine p-adic automorphic family. The Apéry branch-coupling problem instead concerns the simultaneous vanishing of twisted unit-root classes

\[
H_{p,j}^{\mathrm{unit}}=0,\qquad j\in \mathbf Z/(p-1),
\]

for many tame Kummer twists of one hypergeometric motive.

There may be an eigenvariety-like object behind this, but it is not presently a classical overconvergent modular-form family. The relevant geometry is closer to a **Dwork unit-root F-crystal over weight space** than to the Coleman eigencurve.

---

## (A) Does an overconvergent eigenform encode the branch coupling?

Not directly.

The Apéry motive is a rank-4 hypergeometric motive with symplectic monodromy. The crystalline Frobenius has slopes compatible with the Hodge polygon

\[
(0,1,2,3).
\]

At ordinary primes there is a unique slope-zero eigenvalue

\[
\alpha_p\in \mathbf Z_p^\times .
\]

After twisting by a tame character \(\omega^j\), one obtains a family of Frobenius operators

\[
\Phi_j=\Phi\otimes \omega^j.
\]

The question defining ZM₂ is whether the unit root disappears for particular j. This is a question about the zero locus of a **unit-root section**:

\[
u_p(j)=0.
\]

A Coleman family instead starts with a family of automorphic forms

\[
f_k=\sum a_n(k)q^n
\]

whose Hecke eigenvalues vary analytically in weight. It controls

\[
a_p(k),
\]

not directly the zero set of unit-root periods of a hypergeometric F-crystal.

Therefore there is no known overconvergent eigenform whose Hecke eigenvalues are exactly the branch-coupling data.

---

## (B) Is the ghost-conjecture picture applicable?

Only after a major reinterpretation.

The ghost conjecture describes the Newton polygon of Fredholm determinants of compact operators on overconvergent modular forms:

\[
P(T,k)=\det(1-TU_p).
\]

Slope jumps occur when zeros of this Fredholm series collide.

The Apéry analogue would be a Dwork Fredholm determinant

\[
P_{Ap}(T,j)=\det(1-T\Phi_j).
\]

Then branch coupling becomes:

"How many j in a p-adic weight disc satisfy that the first Newton slope of P_{Ap}(T,j) is positive?"

This analogy is real. It is the correct p-adic functional-analytic framework.

However, the missing theorem is an Apéry-specific ghost theorem: bounded complexity of the Newton polygon of the Dwork operator.

No such theorem is currently known.

---

## (C) Can Gouvêa–Mazur/Buzzard–Calegari bound bad tame characters?

Not immediately.

The Gouvêa–Mazur philosophy says that in a fixed eigenvariety component, slopes are locally constant under suitable weight congruence conditions.

For modular forms this gives statements of the form:

\[
k_1\equiv k_2 \pmod {p^m}
\Longrightarrow
\text{slope data agrees}.
\]

For Apéry twists one would need an analogous statement:

\[
j_1\equiv j_2\pmod {p^m}
\Longrightarrow
v_p(\nu_p(j_1))=v_p(\nu_p(j_2)).
\]

That is exactly the desired branch coupling.

But it has not been established because the Apéry object is not known to arise from a finite-slope automorphic eigenvariety.

---

## (D) Does weight-space degree imply \(|Z_p|\le C\)?

This is the most promising reformulation, but the relevant degree is unknown.

Suppose there existed a rigid analytic function

\[
U_p(W)
\]

on weight space \(W=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf Z_p^\times,\mathbf C_p^\times)\) such that

\[
U_p(j)=\text{Apéry unit-root coefficient for }\omega^j.
\]

Then zeros of U_p would satisfy a Weierstrass preparation theorem:

\[
U_p(W)=\pi^m P(W) V(W),
\]

where P is a distinguished polynomial.

The number of bad characters in a residue disc would then be bounded by

\[
\deg P.
\]

This would prove the desired AFP bound if

\[
\deg P=O(1).
\]

The problem is that no bound of this form is known. The Newton polygon degree may grow with p because the underlying cohomology varies with the whole Kummer character space.

The relevant object is not the classical modular Newton polygon but the Newton polygon of the Dwork unit-root F-crystal.

---

## (E) Known literature connection

The closest frameworks are:

### Dwork

Dwork's unit-root theory constructs p-adic analytic functions whose zeros detect supersingular/nonordinary phenomena. This is closest to the Apéry situation.

The desired theorem would be a Dwork-style uniformity result over the Kummer character variable.

### Katz

Katz's theory of p-adic differential equations and hypergeometric motives gives the correct F-crystal and variation of Frobenius.

### Coleman

Coleman's theory gives analytic families of modular eigenforms and slope control, but Apéry is not known to lie on such an eigenvariety.

### Gouvêa–Mazur / Buzzard–Calegari

These provide slope variation principles in automorphic families. The analogy suggests the right theorem but does not prove it for Apéry.

### Bergdall–Pollack ghost conjecture

This suggests that Newton polygons of p-adic families can have bounded combinatorial complexity. An Apéry version would be exactly the needed branch-coupling statement.

---

## Relation to ZM₂

The desired statement is roughly:

\[
\#\{j: \nu_p(j)=0\}=O(1).
\]

A successful eigenvariety/Dwork theorem would give this because zeros of a bounded-degree analytic function are bounded.

The missing chain is:

\[
\text{Apéry motive}
\rightarrow
\text{Dwork unit-root family in j}
\rightarrow
\text{bounded Newton degree}
\rightarrow
|Z_p|=O(1).
\]

At present only the first arrow is established.

Thus:

- Coleman eigenforms: useful analogy, not the actual object.
- Ghost conjecture: likely the correct philosophy.
- Dwork/Katz unit-root families: the technically correct framework.
- Relevant Newton polygon degree: unknown; proving it bounded would essentially solve AFP.
