# 3. Global partial fractions: the higher Taylor remainder disappears

The rational function `G_n` has denominator `(Y+1)_n^3`; hence its only possible finite poles are

\[
Y=-1,-2,\ldots,-n,
\]

all of order at most three.

The numerator `U_n` and denominator `(Y+1)_n^3` have the same degree `3n`.  If `ell_n` is defined by

\[
\ell_0=0,\quad\ell_1=1,\quad
\ell_{a+1}=34\ell_a-\ell_{a-1},
\tag{3.1}
\]

then a leading-term induction in `(1.1)` gives

\[
\lim_{Y\to\infty}G_n(Y)=\ell_{n+1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Now subtract from `G_n` the constant `ell_(n+1)` and all principal parts `(2.4)` at the poles.  The difference has no finite pole and tends to zero at infinity.  It is therefore the zero rational function.

This proves `(PF)`:

\[
\boxed{
G_n(Y)=\ell_{n+1}
 +\sum_{j=1}^n\left(
 \frac{A_1(n,j)}{Y+j}
 +\frac{A_2(n,j)}{(Y+j)^2}
 +\frac{A_3(n,j)}{(Y+j)^3}
 \right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is the key exact step.  The first two jets are not being extrapolated from `Y=0` to `Y=1/2`.  They are used to determine the finite principal parts of a rational function whose pole divisor is already known; rational-function uniqueness then gives the whole function.

That is why the nonzero `Y^3` Taylor remainder observed in Q2202 is harmless here.

# 5. Exact half-step formula

\[
O_n=\prod_{a=1}^n(2a+1)=3\cdot5\cdots(2n+1),

\[
\boxed{
m_{n+1}=O_n^3G_n(1/2).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Insert `(3.3)`.  Since

\[
\frac1{j+1/2}=\frac2{2j+1},

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
m_{n+1}
=O_n^3\left[
\ell_{n+1}
+\sum_{j=1}^n\left(
\frac{2A_1(n,j)}{2j+1}

\right].

\tag{5.3}

Substituting `(2.4)` and collecting `C_0,C_1,C_2` gives the compact summand `(Phi)`:

\boxed{

\Phi_{n,j}
={}&-\frac{2j^3}{2j+1}C_2(n,j)\\
&+\frac{2j^2(4j+3)}{(2j+1)^2}C_1(n,j)\\

\end{aligned}}
\tag{5.4}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
m_{n+1}=O_n^3\left(\ell_{n+1}+\sum_{j=1}^n\Phi_{n,j}\right).

\tag{5.5}

There is a convenient differential form.  From `(2.2)`,

C_0=F_{n,j}(0),\quad

\mathcal D_jF
:=-\frac{2j(4j^2+6j+3)}{(2j+1)^3}F(0)

\boxed{
\Phi_{n,j}=\mathcal D_j\bigl(G_j(-t)G_{n-j}(t)\bigr).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This form is tailored for a contiguous-relation attack in the outer index `j`.

# 7. Expand `(M-PF)` into an explicit terminating binomial-harmonic sum

For `a>=0` and `0<=k<=a`, put

\[
T_{a,k}=\binom ak^2\binom{a+k}{k}^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Define

\[
u_{a,k}=H_{a+k}-H_{a-k},
\tag{7.2}

\[

\qquad

\tag{7.3}

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
b_a&=\sum_{k=0}^aT_{a,k},\\
g_a&=2\sum_{k=0}^aT_{a,k}u_{a,k},\\
\eta_a&=2\sum_{k=0}^aT_{a,k}

\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Fix `j`, put `q=n-j`, and expand the products in `(2.1)`.  One obtains exactly

\[
\boxed{
C_0(n,j)
=\sum_{k=0}^j\sum_{l=0}^{q}
T_{j,k}T_{q,l},
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

\[
\boxed{
C_1(n,j)
=2\sum_{k=0}^j\sum_{l=0}^{q}
T_{j,k}T_{q,l}
\bigl(u_{q,l}-u_{j,k}\bigr),
}
\tag{7.6}

and

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_2(n,j)
=2\sum_{k=0}^j\sum_{l=0}^{q}
T_{j,k}T_{q,l}\bigl[&
 (u_{j,k}-u_{q,l})^2\\
&-v_{j,k}-w_k-v_{q,l}-w_l
\bigr].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.7}
\]

The last identity is just

```text
b_j*eta_q + eta_j*b_q - g_j*g_q
```

expanded and the quadratic terms collected.

Insert `(7.5)--(7.7)` into `(5.4)`.  Define `d=2j+1`, `q=n-j`, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{n;j,k,l}:={}&
-\frac{4j^3}{d}
\left[(u_{j,k}-u_{q,l})^2

&-\frac{2j(4j^2+6j+3)}{d^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Then the half-step integer has the fully explicit terminating formula

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
m_{n+1}=O_n^3\Bigg[\ell_{n+1}
+\sum_{j=1}^{n}
 \sum_{k=0}^{j}
 \sum_{l=0}^{n-j}

 K_{n;j,k,l}\Bigg].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.9}
\]

This is a finite binomial/hypergeometric weight decorated by harmonic functions of orders at most two.  All summation bounds are explicit.  There is no infinite series and no remainder term.

The denominator proof should still be performed through the collapsed `C_0,C_1,C_2` form `(2.5)`, because Q2124's `Lambda^2` cancellation uses the binomial weights and should not be destroyed by taking crude denominators term-by-term in `(7.9)`.
