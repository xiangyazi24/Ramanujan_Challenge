# Q459 — Function-field transplant to \(\mathbb F_q[T]\)

## Executive verdict

The proposed transplant is a **high-value diagnostic**, but it is not presently a direct route to P3.2. The central issue is that three distinct function-field theories are being conflated:

1. **Carlitz/Anderson–Thakur zeta arithmetic**, where \(\zeta_A(3)\) is encoded by tensor powers of the Carlitz module and Frobenius-semilinear difference equations;
2. **a fixed geometric family over a fixed finite field**, where a Hasse invariant is a section of a fixed line bundle and divisor-degree arguments really can bound the nonordinary locus;
3. **the horizontal, self-characteristic problem with a moving prime \(P\subset A=\mathbb F_q[T]\)**, which is the faithful analogue of \(a_p\equiv0\pmod p\) and is not settled merely by Deligne equidistribution or purity.

The answers to the five questions are therefore:

| Part | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **(A) Existence** | Carlitz zeta values, Anderson–Thakur motives, rational partial-sum approximants, and Frobenius difference equations exist. I did **not** find a standard or canonical function-field Apéry pair \(A_n/B_n\to\zeta_A(3)\) with an Apéry-style fixed low-order recurrence and Carlitz-binomial denominator. An ad hoc Carlitz-binomial Apéry sequence can be defined, but no known link to \(\zeta_A(3)\) comes with it. |
| **(B) Translation** | The literal reduction of the integer Apéry sequence modulo \(P\) is isotrivial and collapses to reduction modulo the constant characteristic \(p\). The faithful variable is a **Kummer-character label** \(j\in\mathbb Z/(|P|-1)\), not an additive element of \(A/P\). Deligne–Katz controls off-characteristic Frobenius distributions; exact divisibility by the same moving \(P\) is a local-shtuka/Hasse-invariant problem. |
| **(C) Ordinary density** | For a **fixed** family over a fixed curve in characteristic \(p\), generic ordinarity plus a fixed Hasse divisor gives a known finite/bounded exceptional set. For the **varying-\(P\)** analogue of weight 4, purity does not imply finiteness. Naive Drinfeld-modular-form formulations can even have Hecke eigenvalue divisible by \(P\) for every \(P\). |
| **(D) Pilot** | Do not use \(q=2,3\) as a genuine \(\zeta(3)\) pilot: \(q=2\) is Euler–Carlitz, while \(q=3\) is Frobenius-imprimitive. The first clean value is \(q=5\). Run two controls: the known Legendre-Drinfeld Deuring recurrence, which should **not** be Poisson, and an explicitly labelled ad hoc Carlitz-Apéry sequence, whose behavior is genuinely unknown. |
| **(E) Mechanism** | The useful mechanism is not “big monodromy alone.” It is: a nonzero Hasse section on a parameter space of bounded complexity, together with a **uniform bound on the degree/intersection number of its zero divisor**. The possible number-field shadow is a bounded-degree algebraization of the Kummer-character aspect. That is precisely the missing structure in the present Apéry problem. |

The most important strategic conclusion is:

> **Function fields separate generic equidistribution from self-characteristic anti-concentration. They make the first accessible, but they do not automatically solve the second.**

This conclusion agrees with the warning already recorded in `HANDOFF_P32_SESSION2.md`: the naive function-field route collapses by Lucas behavior and monodromy acts in the wrong variable. The analysis below makes those two obstructions precise and identifies a corrected pilot.

## 1. First correction: there are three inequivalent “function-field analogues”

Let

\[
A=\mathbb F_q[\theta],\qquad K=\mathbb F_q(\theta),\qquad
K_\infty=\mathbb F_q((1/\theta)).
\]

A monic irreducible \(P\in A\) of degree \(d\) has residue field

\[
k_P=A/P\cong\mathbb F_Q,\qquad Q=q^d.
\]

### 1.1 Carlitz zeta arithmetic

The Carlitz zeta value is

\[
\zeta_A(s)=\sum_{a\in A_+}\frac1{a^s}\in K_\infty,
\]

where \(A_+\) denotes the monic polynomials. Anderson and Thakur interpret \(\zeta_A(s)\) through the tensor power \(C^{\otimes s}\) of the Carlitz module: it is a coordinate of the logarithm of a special algebraic point. The natural equations are **Frobenius-semilinear \(\tau\)-difference equations**, not ordinary differential equations and not scalar three-term recurrences in an integer index.

This is the right world for part (A), but by itself it contains no varying prime \(P\) and no Kummer-character row of length \(|P|\).

### 1.2 Fixed-characteristic geometric families

Take a variety or an \(F\)-crystal over a curve \(C/\mathbb F_q\). Here Deligne’s theory, monodromy, trace formulas, and Hasse invariants apply in their cleanest form. If the Hasse invariant is a fixed nonzero section of a fixed line bundle on \(C\), its zero locus has bounded degree.

This is the world in which “the nonordinary locus is a proper divisor” is a theorem with strong consequences.

### 1.3 Horizontal equal-characteristic arithmetic

The faithful analogue of the project’s condition

\[
a_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod p
\]

must let the characteristic prime itself vary:

\[
P\subset A,\qquad a_{P,j}\equiv0\pmod P.
\]

This requires an integral compatible system of \(A\)-motives/local shtukas and a canonical motivic normalization of \(a_{P,j}\). It is a **moving-characteristic** problem. An auxiliary \(\ell\)-adic sheaf with \(\ell\neq\operatorname{char}k_P\) does not by itself see divisibility by \(P\).

The present repository is already in exactly this third configuration over \(\mathbb Q\): `problems/3.2/lemmaBprime_result.tex` identifies \(r\) with a Kummer-character exponent and gives, schematically,

\[
a_{p,r}\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\]

Therefore a faithful transplant has to preserve **both moving legs**: the moving characteristic prime and the moving Kummer character.

# (A) Does a Carlitz/Anderson–Thakur Apéry sequence exist?

## 2. What definitely exists

For every positive integer \(s\):

1. \(\zeta_A(s)\) exists in \(K_\infty\).
2. The partial sums
   \[
   R_N(s)=\sum_{\substack{a\in A_+\\ \deg a<N}}a^{-s}\in K
   \]
   give rational approximations \(R_N(s)\to\zeta_A(s)\) in the nonarchimedean absolute value.
3. Anderson–Thakur theory attaches an explicit \(t\)-module/\(t\)-motive and a Frobenius difference equation to \(\zeta_A(s)\).
4. Anderson–Thakur polynomials encode the degree-by-degree power sums entering \(\zeta_A(s)\).
5. Transcendence and algebraic-relation questions are much more completely understood than over \(\mathbb Q\): the Euler–Carlitz and Frobenius relations account for the standard algebraic relations among the depth-one zeta values.

Thus there is no shortage of function-field analogues of the **period/motive** side of Apéry’s proof.

## 3. What I did not find

I did not find in the standard Carlitz/Anderson–Thakur literature a canonical pair

\[
A_n,B_n\in A,\qquad \frac{A_n}{B_n}\longrightarrow\zeta_A(3),
\]

having all of the following simultaneously:

1. a fixed-order scalar recurrence analogous to
   \[
   (n+1)^3b_{n+1}=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)b_n-n^3b_{n-1};
   \]
2. an Apéry/Carlitz-binomial closed form for \(B_n\);
3. an integral Casoratian controlling common factors;
4. a non-isotrivial family whose reduction modulo a varying \(P\) produces a row of \(|P|\) genuinely new residues;
5. a direct relation \(A_n/B_n\to\zeta_A(3)\).

This is a literature assessment, not a theorem that such a sequence cannot exist. The closest established structure is a finite-dimensional **semilinear Frobenius system**, not a scalar Apéry recurrence.

Recent motivic Mellin-transform formulas for Drinfeld modules likewise express special Goss \(L\)-values through periods and rigid analytic trivializations. They do not currently supply an Apéry-number denominator sequence of the required type.

## 4. Why the small fields \(q=2,3\) are misleading for \(\zeta_A(3)\)

Two standard identities are

\[
(q-1)\mid s
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\zeta_A(s)}{\widetilde\pi^s}\in K,
\]

and, if \(p=\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q\),

\[
\zeta_A(ps)=\zeta_A(s)^p.
\]

For \(s=3\):

| \(q\) | Degeneracy of \(\zeta_A(3)\) |
|---:|---|
| \(2\) | \(q-1=1\mid3\): Euler–Carlitz. |
| \(3\) | \(p=3\mid3\): \(\zeta_A(3)=\zeta_A(1)^3\), Frobenius-imprimitive. |
| \(4\) | \(q-1=3\mid3\): Euler–Carlitz. |
| \(5\) | Neither relation applies: this is the first clean non-Eulerian, non-Frobenius-imprimitive case. |

Therefore the first serious \(\zeta(3)\) pilot should be over

\[
\boxed{A=\mathbb F_5[\theta].}
\]

Computations over \(\mathbb F_2[T]\) or \(\mathbb F_3[T]\) may still be useful as software checks, but they should not be interpreted as evidence for the primitive \(\zeta_A(3)\) situation.

## 5. A useful trilemma

There are three immediately available constructions, but each drops one essential feature.

### 5.1 Base-change the classical Apéry recurrence

Reduce the integer recurrence into \(A\). Then every \(b_n\) lies in the constant field \(\mathbb F_p\subset A\). Reduction modulo different irreducibles \(P\) sees no new arithmetic: it only sees \(b_n\bmod p\).

This preserves the Apéry recurrence but loses non-isotriviality and the Carlitz zeta value.

### 5.2 Use partial sums of \(\zeta_A(3)\)

The degree truncations \(R_N(3)\) converge to \(\zeta_A(3)\), and Anderson–Thakur theory gives highly structured formulas for their degree pieces.

This preserves the zeta value and motive but does not provide the known Apéry recurrence or a canonical integer-index denominator sequence with the required congruence row.

### 5.3 Replace binomial coefficients by Carlitz binomial coefficients

Define

\[
\mathcal B_n
 =\sum_{k=0}^{n}
   {n\choose k}_C^2 {n+k\choose k}_C^2\in A.
\]

This is a perfectly legitimate \(A\)-valued sequence and is the most obvious computational analogue of the Apéry numbers. But at present it is **ad hoc**:

- no convergence \(\mathcal A_n/\mathcal B_n\to\zeta_A(3)\) is known;
- no fixed-order Apéry recurrence is known;
- no K3/Anderson-motive interpretation is known;
- Carlitz Lucas congruences impose strong digital structure that may dominate the statistics.

It is still the best inexpensive exploratory sequence, provided it is explicitly labelled as a stress test rather than “the” function-field Apéry sequence.

# (B) What is the correct translation of \(Z_p\)?

## 6. The literal translation fails exactly by the repository’s Lucas kill shot

Let \(q=p^e\), let \(P\) have degree \(d\), and put

\[
Q=|A/P|=q^d=p^{ed}.
\]

If the ordinary integer Apéry numbers are merely embedded in \(A\), then reduction modulo \(P\) equals reduction modulo the constant characteristic \(p\). The Apéry Lucas congruence gives, for the base-\(p\) expansion

\[
n=n_0+n_1p+\cdots+n_{ed-1}p^{ed-1},
\]

that

\[
b_n\equiv\prod_{i=0}^{ed-1}b_{n_i}\pmod p.
\]

Let

\[
z_p=\#\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

Among \(0\le n<Q\), the product is nonzero exactly when every digit avoids the \(z_p\) bad digits. Consequently the naive function-field zero count is **exactly**

\[
\boxed{
 z_P^{\mathrm{naive}}
 =Q-(p-z_p)^{ed}.
}
\]

Hence:

- if \(z_p=0\), then \(z_P^{\mathrm{naive}}=0\) for every \(P\);
- if \(z_p>0\), then \(z_P^{\mathrm{naive}}\asymp Q\), not \(O(1)\).

For the first informative characteristic \(p=q=5\),

\[
(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)\equiv(1,0,3,0,1)\pmod5,
\]

so \(z_5=2\), and for every degree-\(d\) irreducible \(P\in\mathbb F_5[T]\),

\[
z_P^{\mathrm{naive}}=5^d-3^d.
\]

The first values are

\[
2,\ 16,\ 98,\ 544,\ldots
\]

for \(d=1,2,3,4,\ldots\). Thus the naive transplant does not merely fail subtly: it gives the opposite of anti-concentration.

This is the precise content of the “Lucas collapses to a fixed list” warning in the P3.2 handoff.

## 7. The coefficient/root mismatch

Suppose, as in the Apéry diagonal model, that

\[
b_r=\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda^r)
\]

for a fixed Laurent polynomial \(\Lambda\). Over a finite field of cardinality \(Q\), form

\[
H_P(x)=\operatorname{CT}\bigl((1-x\Lambda)^{Q-1}\bigr).
\]

Because

\[
{Q-1\choose r}(-1)^r\equiv1
\]

in characteristic \(p\) for \(0\le r<Q\), one gets

\[
\boxed{
H_P(x)\equiv\sum_{r=0}^{Q-1}b_r x^r.
}
\]

This identity explains both the attraction and the limitation of Hasse geometry:

- a Hasse-divisor theorem controls the **roots in the geometric parameter \(x\)** of \(H_P(x)\);
- P3.2 asks for the **zero coefficients** \(b_r\).

A polynomial can have a bounded number of roots and an arbitrarily large number of zero coefficients, or conversely. Deligne–Katz equidistribution of \(H_P(x)\) as \(x\) varies therefore acts in the wrong direction for the coefficient-zero problem.

This is the exact geometric form of the repository’s second warning: “monodromy equidistributes the wrong variable.”

## 8. The faithful character-aspect formulation

The repository’s Mellin identity shows that the original index \(r\) is best viewed as a Kummer-character exponent. The function-field replacement should therefore be:

1. choose a prime \(P\subset A\) with \(k_P\cong\mathbb F_Q\);
2. choose a Teichmüller character
   \[
   \omega_P:k_P^\times\longrightarrow\mu_{Q-1};
   \]
3. let
   \[
   \chi_{P,j}=\omega_P^j,
   \qquad j\in\mathbb Z/(Q-1)\mathbb Z;
   \]
4. construct a twisted \(A\)-motive or local shtuka whose canonically normalized Frobenius trace is \(a_{P,j}\);
5. define
   \[
   \boxed{
   Z_P^{\mathrm{char}}
   =\{j\bmod(Q-1):a_{P,j}\equiv0\pmod P\}.
   }
   \]

This is the faithful analogue of the current \(a_{p,r}\equiv b_r\pmod p\) formulation.

The hard missing object is not Deligne’s theorem. It is the **integral family \(a_{P,j}\)** with:

- bounded conductor as \(P,j\) vary;
- a canonical motivic normalization;
- a \(P\)-adic/local-shtuka realization;
- a Hasse invariant whose vanishing is equivalent to \(P\mid a_{P,j}\).

Once that object exists, off-characteristic monodromy can help with irreducibility and correlations, but it still does not automatically give exact self-characteristic anti-concentration.

## 9. Why Deligne–Katz does not by itself prove \(|Z_P|=O(1)\)

Deligne–Katz equidistribution controls continuous class functions of normalized Frobenius conjugacy classes in an auxiliary \(\ell\)-adic realization, \(\ell\neq p\). The event

\[
a_{P,j}\equiv0\pmod P
\]

is not such a continuous class function. It is a lattice event in the **same residue characteristic as the varying prime**.

This is the function-field version of the distinction already isolated in `drops/Q449-e9a30cbd.md`:

- big monodromy controls macroscopic Frobenius distribution;
- exact divisibility needs a defining-characteristic local-limit or Hasse theorem;
- moving across two primes requires a further horizontal decorrelation statement.

Infinite/Zariski-dense monodromy remains a valuable discriminator: it rules out the central-binomial finite/solvable mechanism. But it is a necessary structural hypothesis, not the desired anti-concentration theorem.

# (C) Is the ordinary-density problem known over function fields?

## 10. The answer depends on which of two problems is meant

### 10.1 Fixed characteristic and a fixed parameter curve: yes

Let \(C/\mathbb F_q\) be a smooth projective curve and let a family of crystals or motives over \(C\) have a Hasse invariant

\[
\operatorname{Ha}\in H^0(C,\mathcal L)
\]

for a fixed line bundle \(\mathcal L\). If \(\operatorname{Ha}\not\equiv0\), then

\[
D=\deg\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{Ha})<\infty.
\]

Therefore, for every extension \(\mathbb F_{q^m}\),

\[
\#\{x\in C(\mathbb F_{q^m}):\operatorname{Ha}(x)=0\}\le D.
\]

Equivalently, the nonordinary closed points lie in a fixed finite set. This is a completely known geometric mechanism. It uses:

1. generic ordinarity, or merely one ordinary fiber, to show \(\operatorname{Ha}\not\equiv0\);
2. the fact that the base is a curve;
3. a fixed line bundle and a fixed section.

It is **not purity alone**.

On a base of dimension \(s>1\), a proper Hasse divisor normally has about \(O(q^{m(s-1)})\) rational points over \(\mathbb F_{q^m}\). Properness gives density zero, not an \(O(1)\) absolute bound.

### 10.2 Varying characteristic prime \(P\): no general finiteness theorem

In the horizontal problem the special fiber and Hasse section change with \(P\). Their degrees may grow with \(|P|\). There is then no fixed divisor whose degree gives a uniform bound.

The Legendre-Drinfeld family makes this explicit. Bassa and Beelen define universal polynomials

\[
u_{-1}(s)=0,\qquad u_0(s)=1,
\]

\[
 u_{i+1}(s)
 =(s+\theta^q)^{q^i}u_i(s)
 -(\theta^{q^i}-\theta)s^{q^i}u_{i-1}(s).
\]

If \(P\neq(\theta)\) has degree \(d\), the reduction \(u_d^{(P)}(s)\) is the Deuring polynomial for a Legendre family of rank-2 Drinfeld modules. It is separable and has degree

\[
\boxed{
\deg u_d^{(P)}=\frac{q^d-1}{q-1}.
}
\]

Thus the number of geometric supersingular parameters grows on the order of \(|P|\). This is a rigorous counterexample to the expectation that “large monodromy + a Hasse invariant” should automatically produce \(O(1)\) zeros when the characteristic prime varies.

## 11. Purity does not settle the weight-4 analogue

Suppose a canonically normalized trace \(a_P\in A\) comes from a pure object of weight \(w\), so that, schematically,

\[
|a_P|_\infty\ll |P|^{w/2}.
\]

In degree language this gives

\[
\deg a_P\le \frac w2\deg P+O(1).
\]

If \(P\mid a_P\) and \(a_P\neq0\), then \(\deg a_P\ge\deg P\). Hence:

- for \(w<2\), divisibility eventually forces \(a_P=0\);
- for \(w=2\), multiples comparable to \(P\) remain possible;
- for \(w=3\), the analogue of a classical weight-4 form, one may have
  \[
  a_P=P c_P,\qquad \deg c_P\le\frac12\deg P+O(1),
  \]
  so purity leaves exponentially many possible nonzero multiples.

Therefore purity does not turn \(P\mid a_P\) into a finite or impossible event in weight 4.

This is exactly the same limitation as over \(\mathbb Q\): an archimedean/Weil bound is too coarse to control divisibility by the residue characteristic once the motivic weight is at least 2.

## 12. Two further warnings from Drinfeld theory

### 12.1 “Drinfeld modular form of weight 4” is not automatically the faithful analogue

Drinfeld modular forms have characteristic-\(p\) phenomena absent classically, and Hecke normalizations can differ by powers of \(P\). Before asking whether \(P\mid\lambda_P\), one must select a canonical **motivic** normalization. Divisibility of an arbitrarily normalized Hecke eigenvalue is not invariant.

Petrov constructs infinitely many cuspidal Drinfeld eigenforms with \(A\)-expansions whose Hecke eigensystem, in his normalization, is

\[
\lambda_P=P^n
\]

for every prime \(P\). Thus \(P\mid\lambda_P\) for **every** prime. In a cohomologically rescaled normalization the same system may become a unit system. This shows that the raw Drinfeld-modular-form formulation is not the right direct analogue of the classical ordinary-prime question.

### 12.2 Even rank-2 Drinfeld modules do not have a universal supersingular-prime law

For a rank-2 Drinfeld module with Frobenius polynomial

\[
X^2-a_PX+\mu_P P,
\]

the Weil bound gives \(\deg a_P\le\tfrac12\deg P\). Therefore \(P\mid a_P\) forces \(a_P=0\), the supersingular condition.

Nevertheless the horizontal distribution is arithmetic, not automatic geometry:

- Chantal David proved distribution results and average Lang–Trotter-type theorems under suitable hypotheses;
- Poonen constructed rank-2 and higher Drinfeld modules over \(\mathbb F_q(T)\) with **no supersingular primes at all**.

So even in the easier weight-1 setting, purity identifies the event but does not impose a universal density or infinitude theorem.

## 13. Direct answer to part (C)

The correct statement is:

> **Fixed-family geometric ordinarity over a fixed finite-field curve is settled by Hasse-divisor geometry. The horizontal same-prime divisibility problem for a varying \(P\), especially in weight 4, is not settled by purity and has no general finiteness theorem.**

Indeed, naive Drinfeld-form analogues may have divisibility at every prime, while rank-2 Drinfeld modules may have none. The category and normalization must be fixed before the question has a meaningful analogue.

# (D) The simplest useful pilots

I recommend a three-level pilot, with the first two serving as negative controls.

## 14. Pilot 0A: verify the Lucas collapse with the original Apéry numbers

Use \(q=5\). For every irreducible \(P\) of degree \(d\), enumerate

\[
0\le n<5^d
\]

and reduce the ordinary Apéry number \(b_n\) into \(A/P\). The theorem predicts exactly

\[
\#\{n<5^d:b_n=0\text{ in }A/P\}=5^d-3^d.
\]

This computation is almost free because one need only use the base-5 digits and the row

\[
(1,0,3,0,1).
\]

Purpose: confirm that the literal transplant is isotrivial and non-Poisson.

## 15. Pilot 0B: implement the known Legendre-Drinfeld Deuring recurrence

For \(q=2\) or \(3\), and monic irreducibles \(P\neq T\) of small degree \(d\):

1. compute \(u_d(s)\bmod P\) using the Bassa–Beelen recurrence;
2. factor it over \(A/P\) and small extensions;
3. verify separability;
4. verify total geometric root count
   \[
   (q^d-1)/(q-1);
   \]
5. record Frobenius orbit degrees of the roots.

This is probably the simplest genuine function-field Hasse computation available. It should **not** show Poisson\((1/2)\). It is the control demonstrating that Hasse geometry counts parameter roots and that its divisor degree grows with \(P\).

## 16. Pilot 1: an ad hoc Carlitz-Apéry sequence over \(\mathbb F_5[T]\)

Define the Carlitz factorial from the base-\(q\) expansion

\[
n=\sum_i n_iq^i,
\qquad
n!_C=\prod_i D_i^{n_i},
\]

where

\[
D_i=\prod_{r=0}^{i-1}(T^{q^i}-T^{q^r}),
\]

and

\[
{n\choose k}_C=\frac{n!_C}{k!_C(n-k)!_C}\in A.
\]

Then set

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_n
 =\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}_C^2{n+k\choose k}_C^2.
}
\]

For a degree-\(d\) prime \(P\), put \(Q=5^d\) and compute

\[
Z_P^C=\{0\le n<Q:\mathcal B_n\equiv0\pmod P\}.
\]

This is not yet attached to \(\zeta_A(3)\), but it tests whether the most obvious Carlitz combinatorics reproduces the empirical Apéry statistics.

### 16.1 Efficient local computation

Thakur’s Carlitz-Lucas theorem uses base \(Q=q^d\). For \(0\le n,k<Q\), if \(n+k\ge Q\), then

\[
{n+k\choose k}_C\equiv0\pmod P
\]

because the low base-\(Q\) digit of \(n+k\) is smaller than \(k\). Thus one never has to divide by the vanishing factor \(D_d\) in \(A/P\).

The following is Sage-style pseudocode, intended as a specification rather than syntax guaranteed for every Sage version:

```python
q = 5
F = GF(q)
A.<T> = PolynomialRing(F)

# P is monic irreducible of degree d.
def carlitz_apery_row(P):
    d = P.degree()
    Q = q**d
    K.<alpha> = GF(Q, modulus=P)

    # D_i(alpha) is nonzero for i < d.
    D = [K(1)]
    for i in range(1, d):
        Di = K(1)
        for r in range(i):
            Di *= alpha**(q**i) - alpha**(q**r)
        D.append(Di)

    def cfac_small(n):
        # Valid for 0 <= n < Q.
        ans = K(1)
        i = 0
        while n:
            digit = n % q
            ans *= D[i]**digit
            n //= q
            i += 1
        return ans

    def cbinom_small(n, k):
        # Valid for 0 <= n < Q.
        if k < 0 or k > n:
            return K(0)
        return cfac_small(n) / (cfac_small(k) * cfac_small(n-k))

    def B(n):
        total = K(0)
        for k in range(n+1):
            c1 = cbinom_small(n, k)
            # Carlitz-Lucas: the second factor vanishes after a Q-carry.
            c2 = K(0) if n+k >= Q else cbinom_small(n+k, k)
            total += c1**2 * c2**2
        return total

    row = [B(n) for n in range(Q)]
    zeros = [n for n, value in enumerate(row) if value == 0]
    return row, zeros
```

For \(d=1\), all relevant Carlitz factorials equal 1 and the row is immediately

\[
(\mathcal B_0,\ldots,\mathcal B_4)=(1,2,3,2,1),
\]

so there are no zeros. Degree \(2\) is the first nontrivial test.

### 16.2 What to measure

For all degree-\(d\) primes \(P\), record:

1. \(z_P^C=|Z_P^C|\);
2. whether the exact reflection
   \[
   \mathcal B_{Q-1-n}\equiv\mathcal B_n\pmod P
   \]
   holds for every \(n\);
3. locations of zero pairs, if reflection holds;
4. the first three factorial moments of
   \[
   Y_P=z_P^C/2;
   \]
5. dependence on the degree \(d\), the polynomial \(P\), and Carlitz carry profiles.

Do **not** assume Poisson\((1/2)\) before checking reflection. The comparison should be:

- if exact reflection holds and fixed points contribute negligibly,
  \[
  Y_P\stackrel{?}{\sim}\operatorname{Poisson}(1/2);
  \]
- if no pairing exists,
  \[
  z_P^C\stackrel{?}{\sim}\operatorname{Poisson}(1).
  \]

For Poisson\((1/2)\), the factorial moments are

\[
\mathbb E[Y]=\frac12,
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y(Y-1)]=\frac14,
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y(Y-1)(Y-2)]=\frac18.
\]

Recommended scale:

- degrees \(2,3\): exhaustive correctness tests;
- degree \(4\): exhaustive distribution over all irreducibles is still plausible;
- degree \(5\) and above: sample primes or optimize the row computation before attempting all primes.

### 16.3 A decisive interpretation rule

There are three possible outcomes:

1. **Large/digital zero sets.** Then the Carlitz-binomial construction is another Lucas-type false analogue and should be discarded.
2. **Bounded zeros but no \(\zeta_A(3)\) link.** Then it is an interesting combinatorial sequence, not yet a function-field P3.2 model.
3. **Bounded reflected-Poisson zeros plus a discovered Anderson–Thakur motive/recurrence.** This would justify a serious research program.

## 17. Pilot 2: the faithful but more expensive character-aspect experiment

The eventual experiment should mimic the repository’s Mellin transform, not the Carlitz binomial sum. One wants a trace function \(t_P(x)\) on \(k_P^\times\) and Mellin coefficients

\[
M_P(j)=\sum_{x\in k_P^\times}t_P(x)\omega_P(x)^j
\]

with an integral local-shtuka realization satisfying

\[
M_P(j)\bmod P=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{loss of the unit-root piece at }j.
\]

No off-the-shelf construction currently identifies these \(M_P(j)\) with a Carlitz \(\zeta(3)\) Apéry sequence. Building that construction is the real theoretical task; running finite-field character sums alone would test complex equidistribution, not the desired same-prime divisibility.

# (E) What mechanism would prove a function-field P3.2?

## 18. The fixed-family mechanism

For a genuine fixed family over a curve, the proof would have the following form.

### Step 1: integral crystalline object

Construct an \(F\)-crystal, local shtuka, or integral \(A\)-motive \(\mathcal E\) over a curve \(C\), with a Hodge filtration and Frobenius.

### Step 2: Hasse section

The induced map on the lowest Hodge piece gives a Hasse invariant

\[
\operatorname{Ha}\in H^0(C,\mathcal L^{\otimes N}).
\]

Vanishing means that the unit-root/ordinary piece disappears.

### Step 3: generic ordinarity

Exhibit one ordinary fiber, or use monodromy to show that the Hasse section is not identically zero.

### Step 4: degree/intersection bound

Prove

\[
\deg\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{Ha})\le D.
\]

On a curve, this immediately gives at most \(D\) geometric zeros.

### Step 5: Frobenius orbits and correlations

If one needs counts over extension fields or joint conditions in several families, analyze the Frobenius orbits of the finite zero set. Deligne’s bounds then become useful for auxiliary correlation sums on fiber products.

The decisive step is Step 4. “The Hasse locus is proper” is not enough unless the base is one-dimensional and the degree is uniformly bounded.

## 19. Why the moving-\(P\) version is harder

For the horizontal problem, one has a family of sections

\[
\operatorname{Ha}_P\in H^0(C_P,\mathcal L_P^{\otimes N_P}),
\]

and typically

\[
\deg\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{Ha}_P)\to\infty
\]

with \(\deg P\). The Bassa–Beelen degree \((q^d-1)/(q-1)\) is an explicit instance.

A function-field proof of the desired \(O(1)\) statement would therefore require a much stronger theorem:

> There is a bounded-complexity **character parameter space** and a Hasse section in the Kummer-character aspect whose divisor degree is bounded independently of \(P\).

A useful conditional theorem would be the following.

### Conditional character-Hasse theorem

Assume that for every good \(P\):

1. the character labels \(j\) are realized as points of a curve \(X_P\) obtained from a fixed bounded-complexity model \(X\);
2. the congruence \(a_{P,j}\equiv0\pmod P\) is equivalent to vanishing of a section \(\operatorname{Ha}_P\);
3. \(\operatorname{Ha}_P\not\equiv0\);
4. \(\deg\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{Ha}_P)\le D\), with \(D\) independent of \(P\).

Then

\[
|Z_P^{\mathrm{char}}|\le D
\]

for every good \(P\).

This implication is elementary. The hard theorem is the uniform bounded-degree algebraization in hypotheses 1–4.

## 20. Role of monodromy

Large monodromy can supply several indispensable ingredients:

- geometric irreducibility;
- exclusion of a finite/solvable factorization like the central-binomial example;
- non-isotriviality;
- generic ordinarity or nonvanishing of the Hasse section;
- square-root cancellation for off-characteristic trace correlations;
- independence on fiber products.

But it does **not** by itself supply:

- a bounded-degree Hasse divisor in a moving character space;
- a local limit for divisibility by the same characteristic prime;
- coefficient anti-concentration;
- horizontal two-prime independence of the self-characteristic events.

Thus the monodromy discriminator identified in the question is real, but the implication

\[
\text{Zariski-dense monodromy}
\Longrightarrow
|Z_P|=O(1)
\]

is false without an additional bounded-complexity Hasse statement.

## 21. The number-field shadow to target

The most promising shadow is not a generic Sato–Tate theorem. It is a **uniform algebraization of the character aspect**.

For the Apéry problem over \(\mathbb Q\), one would seek:

1. a fixed curve or torus \(X\) parameterizing the Kummer twists;
2. a bounded-degree integral section \(\operatorname{Ha}_p\) on \(X_{\mathbb F_p}\);
3. an embedding of the labels \(r\) into the relevant torsion/character points of \(X\);
4. an equivalence
   \[
   b_r\equiv0\pmod p
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \operatorname{Ha}_p(x_r)=0;
   \]
5. a degree bound independent of \(p\).

Then \(|Z_p|=O(1)\) would follow from intersection theory rather than probabilistic equidistribution.

This formulation also explains the current obstacle. The labels \(r\) live in a cyclic character group whose order grows with \(p\), and no fixed bounded-degree Hasse section in that character coordinate is currently known. Katz’s Mellin-transform theory organizes the characters representation-theoretically, but it does not automatically produce the required defining-characteristic section of uniformly bounded degree.

A second possible shadow is a torsion-intersection theorem: the Hasse locus is a proper subvariety of a fixed character torus and the points \(x_r\) are torsion points. A uniform bound would then resemble a Manin–Mumford/unlikely-intersection statement. In characteristic \(p\), however, Frobenius and isotrivial factors can create large torsion intersections, so one would need exactly the kind of non-isotrivial, large-monodromy hypotheses highlighted by the central-binomial litmus test.

## 22. Recommended research decision

### What to do now

1. **Record the conceptual correction**: Deligne equidistribution does not solve same-prime divisibility; it acts off-characteristic and usually in the geometric parameter rather than the coefficient/character index.
2. **Run the \(q=5\) negative controls**:
   - ordinary Apéry base-change, checking \(5^d-3^d\);
   - Legendre-Drinfeld Deuring polynomials, checking degree \((5^d-1)/4\).
3. **Run the ad hoc Carlitz-Apéry pilot** for degree \(2,3,4\) primes, testing reflection before testing Poisson\((1/2)\).
4. **Pursue theory only if the pilot is exceptional**: bounded zeros, reflected pair law, and evidence of a low-order Frobenius recurrence.
5. In parallel, formulate the true geometric target as a **moving-character Hasse divisor of uniformly bounded degree**.

### What not to claim

At present one should not claim that:

- an Anderson–Thakur Apéry sequence for \(\zeta_A(3)\) is standard;
- Deligne/Katz proves \(|Z_P|=O(1)\);
- purity settles the weight-4 ordinary-prime problem over function fields;
- a proper Hasse locus automatically has bounded cardinality as \(P\) varies;
- \(q=2\) or \(q=3\) is a faithful primitive \(\zeta_A(3)\) model.

## Final assessment

The function-field route is valuable precisely because it reveals the missing theorem with unusual clarity:

\[
\boxed{
\text{P3.2 needs a bounded-complexity Hasse theory in the moving Kummer-character aspect.}
}
\]

A fixed geometric family over \(\mathbb F_q\) has such a theory and is easy. A varying-prime Drinfeld/Anderson family generally does not; its Hasse degree can grow like \(|P|\). Big monodromy distinguishes Apéry from central binomial coefficients and may prove generic ordinarity, but only a uniform divisor/intersection bound can turn that distinction into \(|Z_P|=O(1)\).

So the transplant is not currently a proof of P3.2. It is, however, an excellent way to isolate the exact geometric structure that a successful number-field proof would have to discover.
