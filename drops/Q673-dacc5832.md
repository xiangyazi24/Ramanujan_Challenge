# Polynomial cocycle on PGL₂ and AFP: arithmetic dynamics audit

## Executive verdict

The cocycle viewpoint is the correct fixed algebraic object, but the proposed
bounded-degree argument does not exist in its naive form.

The Apéry transfer matrix

\[
T(r)=\begin{pmatrix}P(r)/(r+1)^3&-r^3/(r+1)^3\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

is a fixed rational map to PGL₂ over \(\mathbf Z\).  This is valuable: it
moves the problem from a changing interpolation polynomial \(Q_p\) to a fixed
rank-two difference module.  However, the orbit

\[
x_r=T(r-1)\cdots T(1)x_0=[b_r:b_{r-1}]
\]

is not an algebraic orbit of bounded degree in the parameter \(r\).  The
iteration length itself grows with \(r\).  The missing AFP theorem is therefore
a horizontal anti-concentration theorem for a deterministic polynomial cocycle,
not a direct application of Pink-Zilber, Schwartz-Zippel, or Furstenberg.

---

## (A) Is this an unlikely intersection problem?

Not in the standard Pink-Zilber sense.

The map

\[
\Phi_N:\mathbb A^N\to\mathrm{PGL}_2,
\qquad
(r_1,\ldots,r_N)\mapsto T(r_N)\cdots T(r_1)
\]

is algebraic.  The diagonal point

\[
(1,2,\ldots,N)
\]

is a special sequence of points.  But the AFP condition is the intersection of
this sequence with

\[
\{g:g x_0\in W\},\qquad W=[0:1].
\]

Pink-Zilber applies to a subvariety intersecting a subgroup or orbit in an
algebraic group when there is an ambient algebraic dynamical structure.  Here
the varying product

\[
T(r-1)\cdots T(1)
\]

is not an iterate of a fixed endomorphism of PGL₂.  It is a non-autonomous
cocycle.

The closest frameworks are:

- arithmetic dynamics of polynomial cocycles;
- difference algebra / difference Galois theory;
- exponential-polynomial zero estimates.

A direct unlikely-intersection theorem would require a new theorem for
non-autonomous matrix products.

---

## (B) Effective degree: why Schwartz-Zippel fails

The tempting argument is:

\[
r\mapsto x_r\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)
\]

has bounded degree because \(T\) has degree 3.

This is false.

After \(n\) steps,

\[
M_n=T(n-1)\cdots T(1)
\]

has numerator and denominator degrees growing roughly linearly with \(n\).  This
is not a single rational map of bounded degree; it is a sequence of rational
maps with increasing complexity.

The effective degree of

\[
r\mapsto M(r)x_0
\]

is therefore not bounded independently of \(p\).  Modulo \(p\), every function
on \(\mathbf F_p\) can be represented by a polynomial of degree at most
\(p-1\), but this is only the interpolation degree.  It gives no useful
uniform bound.

A bounded effective degree theorem would indeed imply AFP by Schwartz-Zippel,
but the Apéry cocycle does not satisfy such a hypothesis.

---

## (C) Projective Strassmann

There is a partial analogue, but it does not solve AFP.

The ratio

\[
y_r=\frac{b_r}{b_{r-1}}
\]

is a projective coordinate of the recurrence.  Over a p-adic domain one can
study the associated Riccati equation

\[
y_{r+1}=A(r)-\frac{B(r)}{y_r}.
\]

This is often simpler than the two-dimensional linear system, and p-adic
analytic methods study such functions using:

- Frobenius structures;
- overconvergent unit-root functions;
- p-adic differential/difference equations.

However, ordinary Strassmann applies to a single analytic function on a fixed
p-adic disk.  Here the relevant object changes with the prime and the analytic
radius/number of zeros grows with the length of the cocycle.

There is no general theorem saying that a projective coordinate of a linear
recurrence has bounded Strassmann index when the matrix coefficients vary.

The correct possible target would be:

\[
\text{bounded zero count for a Frobenius-compatible projective unit-root
function.}
\]

This is stronger and more geometric than ordinary Strassmann.

---

## (D) Is there a polynomial derandomization theorem?

The proposed theorem is too strong and is false in this generality.

A statement of the form

\[
T:\mathbb A^1\to\mathrm{PGL}_2,
\quad \deg T=d
\]

implying

\[
\#\{r:T(r-1)\cdots T(1)x_0=w\}\le C(d)
\]

cannot hold without additional hypotheses.

Reason: one can construct low-degree cocycles with long prescribed orbit
segments.  Bounded degree of each matrix does not bound the complexity of the
ordered product.

Random matrix products use independence at every step.  The Apéry cocycle has
no independence; it has arithmetic correlations.

The closest positive results are:

1. sum-product theorems in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\);
2. expansion for genuinely generated subgroups;
3. Deligne/Katz bounds for trace functions of fixed complexity.

The missing ingredient is to show that the deterministic sequence

\[
T(1),T(2),\ldots,T(p-1)
\]

has enough cancellation to imitate independence.

---

## (E) Does the Apéry set of matrices generate PGL₂(F_p)?

This is plausible for almost all primes, but it is not the needed theorem.

The determinant is

\[
\det T(r)=\frac{r^3}{(r+1)^3}.
\]

Thus after projectivization the matrices are generically non-degenerate.  The
family is not contained in a Borel subgroup because the lower-left entry is 1,
and it is not generically contained in a torus because the matrices for
neighboring values do not commute.

Therefore the generic Zariski closure over \(\mathbf Q\) should be large
(typically PGL₂).

For finite fields this suggests:

\[
\langle T(1),\ldots,T(p-1)\rangle=\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)
\]

for all sufficiently large primes outside a finite exceptional set.

But even this only gives subgroup generation.  It does not imply that the
ordered product walk

\[
T(r-1)\cdots T(1)x_0
\]

mixes like a random walk.

Generation is a necessary condition, not an AFP proof.

---

# Correct arithmetic-dynamics formulation

The cocycle should be viewed as a rank-two difference module:

\[
Y(r+1)=T(r)Y(r).
\]

The relevant Galois object is the difference Galois group of this module.
Large difference monodromy explains why the recurrence has no algebraic
collapse.  But AFP asks a finer question:

\[
\#\{r\bmod p:Y(r)e_0\in W\}.
\]

This is a zero-counting problem for a deterministic Frobenius reduction of a
large-monodromy difference equation.

The likely route is not bounded degree, but:

\[
\boxed{
\text{large difference monodromy}
+
\text{finite-field trace-function cancellation}
\Longrightarrow
\text{bounded average zero count}
}
\]

This aligns with the previous Mellin framework: the coefficient index is a
character exponent, and the zero event is a defining-characteristic
anti-concentration problem.

---

# Bottom line

(A) Pink-Zilber unlikely intersections do not directly apply because the Apéry
object is a non-autonomous cocycle, not an algebraic group orbit.

(B) The effective degree of the orbit map grows with the iteration length; no
prime-independent degree bound is available.

(C) A projective p-adic formulation is meaningful, but no bounded Strassmann
index theorem exists for this setting.

(D) A general polynomial derandomization theorem of the proposed form is false;
bounded-degree generators do not imply bounded visits.

(E) The matrices likely generate PGL₂ for generic primes, but subgroup
generation is far weaker than orbit equidistribution.

The cocycle viewpoint is still useful: it identifies the correct fixed
arithmetic object.  The missing AFP theorem is a deterministic analogue of
random-walk cancellation for a polynomial PGL₂ cocycle, not a degree argument.
