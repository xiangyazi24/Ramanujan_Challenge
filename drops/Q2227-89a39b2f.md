\[

## 10. Why `(POL2)` is a real new lemma, not a normalization cleanup

\[

\[

\]

That explains why `V` has four denominator factors.

By contrast, the second Duhamel bracket `(T-C)` is causal/oriented:

\[

So turning `[t^2]V` into `mathcal T` requires a **polarization plus triangular projection**.  A proof must show exactly how the symmetric four-denominator coefficient splits into

1. the strict ordered region `v<u` in `(T-C)`,
2. the diagonal `v=u`,
3. the reversed region `u<v`, and
4. the two boundary faces.

The diagonal/reversed/boundary pieces are precisely where the `CD2`, `Outer-s`, and endpoint terms above have to appear.  Dropping any one of them is unsafe; `(cross-test)` already kills the simplest mutant.

This is why I do not identify `[t^2]V` with `mathcal T` merely from the fact that both contain four denominator factors.

But the second inversion alone does not prove `K(r,s)=0`.  After retaining every crossing and applying the exact Racah Lagrange identity, it reduces to

\[
\mathcal T_{r,s}\equiv-\frac{K(r,s)}{6c_s}\pmod{(b_r,b_s)}.
\]

So the next theorem should be stated narrowly as `(POL2)`:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{r,s}
\in
(b_r,b_s,[t^2]V_{(1/2,r+1,s-r-1)}).
}
\]

This is the smallest exact remaining atom.  It is a **symmetric-resolvent to causal-triangle polarization identity**.  Proving it closes the desired conclusion immediately; ordinary reflected WZ/Duhamel cannot replace it, because its ordered crossing cancels back to the already-known boundary current.
