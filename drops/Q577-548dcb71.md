# TCMA₂ and branch-coupling for Apéry Kummer twists

## Executive summary

The proposed branch-coupling idea is the correct conceptual direction, but no standard theorem currently gives the needed bound. The existing geometry gives a family of related twists, not a single fixed-rank matrix whose zero locus controls all twists.

The precise missing theorem is:

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\binom{|Z_p|/2}{2}\ll \pi(X),
\]

or equivalently a two-character anti-concentration statement preventing two distinct tame characters from simultaneously producing Hasse/unit-root failure.

## (A) Is there a tame parameter algebra coupling all H_{p,j}?

There are three related constructions, but none immediately solves TCMA₂.

### 1. Group-algebra Frobenius

A natural object is the Mellin package:

\[
\mathcal H_p=H^1_{rig,c}(U,V\otimes \mathbf Z_p[\mu_{p-1}]).
\]

After specializing the group-algebra variable by a character

\[
\mathbf Z_p[\mu_{p-1}]\to \mathbf Z_p,\qquad [g]\mapsto \omega^j(g),
\]

one recovers the individual twisted modules H_{p,j}.

This is the closest object to the desired "master Frobenius matrix". However, the specialization parameter is a finite character group of size p-1, not an algebraic parameter line with bounded degree. Therefore a bound on the number of specializations with zero trace does not follow from ordinary determinant-degree arguments.

### 2. Total-space cohomology

The full Kummer cover

\[
U\times_{\mathbf G_m}\mathbf G_m\to U
\]

does see all characters simultaneously. Fourier decomposition of the deck group recovers the H_{p,j}.

But the decomposition is semisimple only after adjoining the tame characters. It gives a direct sum:

\[
H=\bigoplus_j H_{p,j},
\]

not a coupling relation between traces of different summands.

### 3. Universal deformation rings

A deformation-theoretic object can interpolate tame characters, but the required statement is not a deformation-theoretic smoothness statement. It is a mod-p exceptional-zero count. A universal ring would encode the branches but does not automatically bound how many branches have vanishing unit root.

Therefore: a parameter algebra exists, but the needed anti-concentration theorem does not follow formally.

## (B) Can the rank-4 K3 Frobenius matrix bound zero twists?

The rank-4 Picard-Fuchs object is important, but the proposed formula is too optimistic.

The Mellin identity is

\[
b_j\equiv \sum_t Ha_p(t)\omega(t)^{-j}\pmod p.
\]

This is a Fourier coefficient of a rank-4 trace function.

However it is not generally of the form

\[
b_j=\operatorname{Tr}(M_pD_j)
\]

for one fixed 4x4 matrix M_p with D_j varying diagonally.

The reason is that Frobenius acts on the geometric fiber, while Mellin projection acts on the parameter t. These are different directions:

- Frobenius rank controls the cohomological dimension;
- the Mellin index j controls the character decomposition.

A fixed-rank determinant argument would require a single algebraic family in j. That object has not been constructed.

The repository's Frobenius factorization gives a useful one-character statement: the zero condition b_j=0 corresponds to loss of the unit-root contribution (Hasse/nonordinary behavior). This identifies the event, but does not couple two different j values.

## (C) Small-prime Frobenius computation

The relevant expected slope picture is:

For an ordinary twist:

\[
\text{slopes}=(0,1,3)
\]

for the effective rank-3 K3 factor (equivalently the rank-2 transcendental factor plus Tate piece).

At a Hasse zero:

\[
\text{slope }0\text{ disappears},
\]

and the Newton polygon lies strictly above the Hodge polygon.

The unit-root dimension is therefore not constant across twists j for a fixed p. It is exactly the variation we need to count.

For TCMA₂, the required computation is not the eigenvalues for one fiber t=1. It is the correlation table:

\[
(j,k)\mapsto \bigl(\mathbf 1_{b_j=0},\mathbf 1_{b_k=0}\bigr).
\]

The theorem needed is a bound on the number of simultaneous Hasse failures among characters.

## (D) Transfer matrix formulation

The transfer matrix viewpoint is valid but has the same obstruction.

The recurrence gives a nonautonomous cocycle:

\[
P(r)=M(r-1)\cdots M(0).
\]

Then

\[
b_r=0\pmod p
\]

is a rank-drop condition on a marked entry of P(r).

This gives:

- a projective orbit problem;
- a nonautonomous SL_2 cocycle;
- a finite-field hitting-time problem.

It does not reduce to zeros of a fixed polynomial matrix product because the matrices vary with r.

A fixed transfer matrix order argument (like Fibonacci/Pisano) fails because

\[
M(r+1)\neq M(r)
\]

and the cocycle is not generated by powers of one element.

## What would prove TCMA₂?

A sufficient theorem would be a cross-character correlation estimate. For example, define

\[
g_p(j)=1_{b_j\equiv0}-\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

It would suffice to prove a bound of the form

\[
\sum_{j,k}g_p(j)g_p(k)K(j,k)\ll p^{1+o(1)},
\]

where K detects distinct tame characters.

Geometrically this should come from one of:

1. independence of Frobenius conjugacy classes in the Mellin family;
2. a large sieve for Kummer sheaves;
3. a monodromy theorem showing the joint Mellin sheaf has sufficiently large geometric monodromy.

The existing Sp(4)/rank-4 structure is evidence for such independence, but not yet a proof.

## Precise gap

Known:

- Apéry zero sets are Hasse/unit-root failure sets.
- Individual twists have controlled crystalline cohomology.
- Mellin transforms package the twists together.
- Monodromy information exists for the underlying geometric family.

Missing:

A theorem controlling the joint distribution of two different tame-character specializations:

\[
\Pr(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\ \&\ b_s\equiv0\pmod p)
\]

with r != s.

That is exactly the Two-Twist Crystalline Mellin Anti-concentration theorem. It is a genuine horizontal equidistribution theorem, not a consequence of rank alone.
