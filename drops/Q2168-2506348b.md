## Scope and verdict

I read the live P3.2 state read-only.  In particular, `README.md` still marks Problem 3.2 as partial/open; `problems/3.2/full_cycle_bridge_reciprocity.tex` uses exactly
\[
N_{h+1}(X)=P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X),
\]
and `problems/3.2/atom_tail_section.tex` uses the continuant/determinant realization and \(\deg N_h=3(h-1)\).  Nothing below assumes a stronger unproved P3.2 statement.

The answers are:

1. **Yes, \(\beta_h\) is even.**  There is a completely explicit coprime \(\mathbf Z[T]\) presentation.  For odd \(h\), the central denominator contributes \(T^3\), but exactly **one** \(T\) cancels; the reduced denominator retains \(T^2\).  No other denominator factor cancels, because every boundary value \(N_h(-j)\), \(1\le j\le h\), is a nonzero signed product of two positive Apéry numbers.
2. After normalizing by the endpoint value \(\beta_h(h)\), the cross numerator is an even integral polynomial vanishing at \(T=h\), hence also at \(T=-h\); in fact it lies in \((T^2-h^2)\mathbf Z[T]\).
3. For a two-variable corner \(u(T)+v(S)-1\), exact denominator clearing preserves the **coordinatewise** degree bounds (hence a bidegree/max-coordinate statement), but in ordinary total degree it generically pays the **sum**.  This is a scoped statement: if the top mixed coefficient cancels, the total degree can fall.  For \(h=k=2\), it does not cancel: the primitive cleared numerator has bidegree \((4,4)\), total degree \(8\), and \(T^4S^4\)-coefficient \(22\).  Thus \(h=k=2\) is already a counterexample to a blanket primitive-total-degree \(\le\max(\deg_h,\deg_k)\) claim.

## 1. Preparatory exact facts about \(N_h\)

Put
\[
d_h:=3(h-1).
\]
The continuant attached to the recurrence is
\[
N_h(X)=\det K_{h-1}(X),
\]
where \(K_{h-1}(X)\) is the symmetric tridiagonal \((h-1)\times(h-1)\) matrix
\[
K_{h-1}(X)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(X+1)&(X+2)^3&&\\
(X+2)^3&P(X+2)&(X+3)^3&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&& (X+h-1)^3&P(X+h-1)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The empty determinant is \(N_1=1\).  Expanding the last row gives exactly the stated recurrence.

### Degree and leading coefficient

Let \(\lambda_h=\operatorname{lc}N_h\).  Then
\[
\lambda_0=0,\qquad \lambda_1=1,\qquad
\lambda_{h+1}=34\lambda_h-\lambda_{h-1}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\lambda_h=
\frac{(17+12\sqrt2)^h-(17-12\sqrt2)^h}{24\sqrt2}>0.}
\]
So there is no leading cancellation and
\[
\boxed{\deg N_h=3(h-1).}
\]

### Exact values at every denominator boundary \(X=-j\)

Define the ordinary Apéry numbers
\[
a_n:=\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr^2\binom{n+r}{r}^2,
\]
so \(a_0=1,a_1=5\), all \(a_n>0\), and
\[
(n+1)^3a_{n+1}=P(n)a_n-n^3a_{n-1}.
\]
Set \(A_m:=N_m(-1)\).  Substituting \(X=-1\) in the gap recurrence and comparing with the Apéry recurrence gives
\[
\boxed{A_m=((m-1)!)^3a_{m-1}\qquad(m\ge1).}
\]

Now fix \(1\le j\le h\).  At \(X=-j\), the off-diagonal entry \((X+j)^3\) is zero, so the continuant splits into a left block and a right block.  For \(2\le j\le h-1\),
\[
N_h(-j)=N_j(-j)\,N_{h-j+1}(-1).
\]
The given reflection identity, evaluated at \(X=-1\), gives
\[
N_j(-j)=(-1)^{j-1}N_j(-1).
\]
The same formula also covers \(j=1,h\) if an empty block is assigned determinant \(1=A_1\).  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
N_h(-j)=(-1)^{j-1}A_jA_{h-j+1}
=(-1)^{j-1}((j-1)!)^3((h-j)!)^3a_{j-1}a_{h-j}.}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{N_h(-j)\ne0\quad(1\le j\le h).}
\tag{2}
\]
The endpoint cases are
\[
N_h(-1)=((h-1)!)^3a_{h-1},\qquad
N_h(-h)=(-1)^{h-1}((h-1)!)^3a_{h-1}.
\]
If \(h=2m+1\) is odd, the central value is especially useful:
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
N_{2m+1}(-(m+1))=(-1)^m(m!)^6a_m^2\ne0.}
\tag{3}
\]
This is what prevents the central \(T^3\) denominator from disappearing completely.

## 2. Exact coprime form of \(\beta_h\) and parity

Let
\[
x=\frac{T-h-1}{2}.
\]
First cancel the single displayed factor \(x+h\) in the definition of \(\beta_h\):
\[
\beta_h(T)
=\frac{T\,N_h(x)}{(x+1)^2(x+h)^2\prod_{j=2}^{h-1}(x+j)^3}.
\tag{4}
\]
Define the integral centered polynomial
\[
\boxed{M_h(T):=2^{d_h}N_h\!\left(\frac{T-h-1}{2}\right)\in\mathbf Z[T].}
\tag{5}
\]
Integrality is immediate termwise because \(d_h=\deg N_h\): if \(a_rX^r\) is a term of \(N_h\), then after multiplying by \(2^{d_h}\) its contribution is \(a_r2^{d_h-r}(T-h-1)^r\).

The reflection gives
\[
\boxed{M_h(-T)=(-1)^{h-1}M_h(T).}
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(M_h\) is even for odd \(h\) and odd for even \(h\), and
\[
\operatorname{lc}M_h=\lambda_h.
\]

Put
\[
D_h(T):=(T^2-(h-1)^2)^2
\prod_{j=2}^{h-1}(T+2j-h-1)^3.
\tag{7}
\]
Equation (4), with all powers of \(2\) accounted for, is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\beta_h(T)=\frac{2T\,M_h(T)}{D_h(T)}.}
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed the total exponent in the denominator of (4) is
\(2+2+3(h-2)=3h-2\), whereas \(M_h=2^{3h-3}N_h(x)\), leaving the factor \(2\) in (8).

Every zero of \(D_h\) has the form
\[
T_j=h+1-2j\qquad(1\le j\le h),
\]
which corresponds exactly to \(x=-j\).  By (1),
\[
M_h(T_j)=2^{d_h}N_h(-j)\ne0.
\tag{9}
\]
Therefore the only possible cancellation in (8) is the *external* factor \(T\) when \(T=0\) itself is one of the denominator roots.

Let
\[
\varepsilon_h:=
\begin{cases}
0,&h\text{ even},\\
1,&h\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then the reduced integral pair is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
p_h(T)=2T^{1-\varepsilon_h}M_h(T),\qquad
q_h(T)=\frac{D_h(T)}{T^{\varepsilon_h}},\qquad
\beta_h(T)=\frac{p_h(T)}{q_h(T)}.}
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(q_h\) is monic.  Equations (2)--(3) show that
\[
\boxed{\gcd_{\mathbf Z[T]}(p_h,q_h)=1.}
\tag{11}
\]
More explicitly:

- If \(h\) is even, \(0\) is not a denominator root, so **nothing cancels**.
- If \(h=2m+1\) is odd, the central index \(j=m+1\) contributes \(T^3\) to \(D_h\).  Because \(M_h(0)\ne0\) by (3), the raw numerator \(2TM_h\) has exactly one factor \(T\).  Thus the gcd is exactly \(T\), and the reduced denominator still contains \(T^2\).  There is no \(T^3\)-cancellation.

The reduced denominators can be written entirely in even factors:
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
q_h(T)=
\begin{cases}
(T^2-(h-1)^2)^2
\displaystyle\prod_{\substack{1\le r\le h-3\\r\text{ odd}}}(T^2-r^2)^3,
&h\text{ even},\\[4mm]
T^2(T^2-(h-1)^2)^2
\displaystyle\prod_{\substack{2\le r\le h-3\\r\text{ even}}}(T^2-r^2)^3,
&h\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{12}
\]
The empty products are \(1\).

By (6), the reduced numerator is also even:

- for even \(h\), \(M_h\) is odd and \(T M_h\) is even;
- for odd \(h\), \(M_h\) is even.

Hence
\[
\boxed{\beta_h(-T)=\beta_h(T).}
\tag{13}
\]
So item (i) is verified.

The numerator and denominator are balanced in degree.  Define
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\delta_h:=3h-2-\varepsilon_h=
\begin{cases}
3h-2,&h\text{ even},\\
3h-3,&h\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\deg p_h=\deg q_h=\delta_h,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}p_h=2\lambda_h,
\qquad \operatorname{lc}q_h=1.}
\tag{15}
\]
In particular
\[
\beta_h(\infty)=2\lambda_h.
\tag{16}
\]

## 3. Endpoint normalization and the factor \(T^2-h^2\)

Normalize at the positive endpoint by
\[
U_h(T):=\frac{\beta_h(T)}{\beta_h(h)}.
\tag{17}
\]
First, this is legitimate: \(\beta_h(h)\ne0\).

Indeed \(T=h\) means \(x=-1/2\).  In the tridiagonal matrix for \(N_h(-1/2)\), row \(r\) has positive diagonal
\[
P\left(r-\frac12\right)
\]
and, for an interior row, sum of absolute off-diagonal entries
\[
\left(r-\frac12\right)^3+\left(r+\frac12\right)^3.
\]
Using the exact identity
\[
P(n)-n^3-(n+1)^3=4(2n+1)^3
\]
at \(n=r-1/2\) gives
\[
P\left(r-\frac12\right)
-\left(r-\frac12\right)^3
-\left(r+\frac12\right)^3
=32r^3>0.
\]
At the two boundary rows the radius is smaller.  Thus the real symmetric matrix is strictly diagonally dominant with positive diagonal, hence positive definite; therefore
\[
N_h(-1/2)>0.
\]
All explicit factors in (4) at \(T=h\) are also nonzero.  Thus \(\beta_h(h)>0\).

For reference, its exact form is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\beta_h(h)
=\frac{2h(2h-1)M_h(h)}{\prod_{j=2}^h(2j-1)^3}
=\frac{2h\,M_h(h)}{(2h-1)^2\prod_{j=2}^{h-1}(2j-1)^3}.}
\tag{18}
\]

Using the coprime pair (10),
\[
U_h(T)=\frac{q_h(h)p_h(T)}{p_h(h)q_h(T)}.
\tag{19}
\]
Consequently the numerator of \(U_h(T)-1\) before any harmless scalar-content reduction is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
C_h(T):=q_h(h)p_h(T)-p_h(h)q_h(T)\in\mathbf Z[T].}
\tag{20}
\]
Both \(p_h\) and \(q_h\) are even, hence \(C_h\) is even.  Also
\[
C_h(h)=0.
\]
Therefore \(C_h(-h)=C_h(h)=0\).  Equivalently, write
\[
p_h(T)=P_h(T^2),\qquad q_h(T)=Q_h(T^2)
\]
with \(P_h,Q_h\in\mathbf Z[Y]\).  Then
\[
C_h(T)=H_h(T^2),
\quad
H_h(Y)=q_h(h)P_h(Y)-p_h(h)Q_h(Y),
\]
and \(H_h(h^2)=0\).  Since \(Y-h^2\) is monic,
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
C_h(T)=(T^2-h^2)R_h(T),\qquad R_h(T)\in\mathbf Z[T].}
\tag{21}
\]
In fact \(R_h\) is itself even.

If one subsequently divides numerator and denominator of (19) or of \(U_h-1\) by a common integer content, the resulting integral numerator is still even and still vanishes at \(\pm h\), so the same monic-factor argument shows that the *primitive* cross numerator is still divisible by \(T^2-h^2\).

Thus item (ii) is proved exactly.

## 4. Two-characteristic denominator clearing: what max-degree statement survives

Here is the precise algebraic statement needed for item (iii).

### Scoped two-variable clearing theorem

Let
\[
u(T)=\frac{A(T)}{B(T)}\in\mathbf Q(T),\qquad
v(S)=\frac{C(S)}{D(S)}\in\mathbf Q(S),
\]
with \(A,B\in\mathbf Z[T]\), \(C,D\in\mathbf Z[S]\), \(B,D\ne0\), and assume the one-variable pairs are reduced over \(\mathbf Q\):
\[
\gcd_{\mathbf Q[T]}(A,B)=1,
\qquad
\gcd_{\mathbf Q[S]}(C,D)=1.
\]
Exact clearing gives
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
u(T)+v(S)-1
=\frac{F(T,S)}{B(T)D(S)},
\quad
F=A(T)D(S)+B(T)C(S)-B(T)D(S).}
\tag{22}
\]
Then:

1. **No nonconstant polynomial cancellation occurs:**
   \[
   \boxed{\gcd_{\mathbf Q[T,S]}(F,B(T)D(S))=1.}
   \tag{23}
   \]
   Proof: if an irreducible factor \(\phi(T)\mid B(T)\) also divided \(F\), then modulo \(\phi\),
   \[
   F\equiv A(T)D(S).
   \]
   But \(\phi\nmid A\) by reducedness, and a nonconstant \(T\)-polynomial cannot divide the nonzero \(S\)-polynomial \(D(S)\).  The argument for factors of \(D(S)\) is symmetric.  Thus an integral presentation can only admit a **scalar/content** cancellation; this cannot change any degree.

2. Put
   \[
   m:=\max(\deg A,\deg B),\qquad
   n:=\max(\deg C,\deg D).
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \boxed{\deg_TF\le m,\qquad \deg_SF\le n,\qquad
   \deg_{\rm tot}F\le m+n.}
   \tag{24}
   \]
   Thus exact clearing really does preserve a coordinatewise/max-coordinate compression:
   \[
   \max(\deg_TF,\deg_SF)\le\max(m,n).
   \]
   What it does **not** preserve in general is ordinary total degree.

3. Suppose the two fractions are balanced,
   \[
   \deg A=\deg B=m,
   \qquad
   \deg C=\deg D=n,
   \]
   and write their leading coefficients as \(a_m,b_m,c_n,d_n\).  The coefficient of the top mixed monomial \(T^mS^n\) is
   \[
   \boxed{a_md_n+b_mc_n-b_md_n
   =b_md_n\left(\frac{a_m}{b_m}+\frac{c_n}{d_n}-1\right).}
   \tag{25}
   \]
   Therefore, under the explicit noncancellation condition
   \[
   \frac{a_m}{b_m}+\frac{c_n}{d_n}\ne1,
   \]
   one has
   \[
   \boxed{\operatorname{bideg}F=(m,n),\qquad
   \deg_{\rm tot}F=m+n.}
   \tag{26}
   \]
   Scalar primitive reduction leaves these degrees unchanged.  If the quantity in (25) is zero, the top corner cancels and the total degree may fall; no stronger conclusion is asserted.  This is the promised scoped theorem rather than a broad impossibility statement.

Applied to the endpoint-normalized gap functions \(U_h\), the balanced degree is exactly \(\delta_h\) from (14), and
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
U_h(\infty)=\frac{2\lambda_h}{\beta_h(h)}.}
\tag{27}
\]
Thus for the \((h,k)\) corner, if
\[
\frac{2\lambda_h}{\beta_h(h)}+
\frac{2\lambda_k}{\beta_k(k)}\ne1,
\]
the exactly cleared numerator has, up to scalar primitive reduction,
\[
\operatorname{bideg}=(\delta_h,\delta_k),
\qquad
\deg_{\rm tot}=\delta_h+\delta_k.
\]
So if both characteristics are evaluated at size \(\asymp X\), the top mixed term is of size \(X^{\delta_h+\delta_k}\), not merely \(X^{\max(\delta_h,\delta_k)}\), unless a specific algebraic cancellation such as (25) intervenes.

## 5. Full explicit audit for \(h=k=2\)

For \(h=2\),
\[
N_2(X)=P(X+1).
\]
Since
\[
P(n)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5),
\]
and \(x=(T-3)/2\),
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
N_2(x)=P\left(\frac{T-1}{2}\right)
=\frac{T(17T^2+3)}4.}
\tag{28}
\]
Substitution in the definition of \(\beta_2\), or (8), gives
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\beta_2(T)=
\frac{4T^2(17T^2+3)}{(T^2-1)^2}.}
\tag{29}
\]
This is already reduced: at \(T=\pm1\) the numerator is \(80\ne0\).
The degree from (14) is
\[
\delta_2=4.
\]
At the endpoint,
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\beta_2(2)=\frac{1136}{9}.}
\tag{30}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
U_2(T)=\frac{\beta_2(T)}{\beta_2(2)}
=\frac{9T^2(17T^2+3)}{284(T^2-1)^2}.}
\tag{31}
\]
Write
\[
A(T)=9T^2(17T^2+3)=153T^4+27T^2,
\qquad
B(T)=284(T^2-1)^2.
\]
For \(u(T)=U_2(T)\) and \(v(S)=U_2(S)\), the raw exactly cleared numerator is
\[
F_{\rm raw}(T,S)=A(T)B(S)+B(T)A(S)-B(T)B(S).
\tag{32}
\]
Every term has a factor \(284\), and in fact
\[
F_{\rm raw}=284F_{2,2},
\]
where
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
F_{2,2}(T,S)
={}&9(17T^4+3T^2)(S^2-1)^2\\
&+9(17S^4+3S^2)(T^2-1)^2\\
&-284(T^2-1)^2(S^2-1)^2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{33}
\]
Expanding,
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
F_{2,2}(T,S)
={}&22T^4S^4
+289(T^4S^2+T^2S^4)
-131(T^4+S^4)\\
&-1244T^2S^2
+595(T^2+S^2)
-284.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{34}
\]
The coefficient gcd is \(1\) (already \(\gcd(22,289)=1\)), so (34) is primitive.  After the common scalar \(284\) is removed from (32) and the product denominator, the exact reduced integral form is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
U_2(T)+U_2(S)-1
=\frac{F_{2,2}(T,S)}
{284(T^2-1)^2(S^2-1)^2}.}
\tag{35}
\]
There is no polynomial cancellation with \((T^2-1)^2(S^2-1)^2\); for example at \(T=\pm1\),
\[
F_{2,2}(\pm1,S)=180(S^2-1)^2\not\equiv0,
\]
and symmetrically in \(S\).  The general gcd argument (23) gives the same conclusion.

Now the requested degree/leading-coefficient check is immediate:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{bideg}F_{2,2}=(4,4),\qquad
\deg_{\rm tot}F_{2,2}=8,\qquad
[T^4S^4]F_{2,2}=22.}
\tag{36}
\]
For comparison, before primitive scalar cancellation the top coefficient is
\[
153\cdot284+284\cdot153-284^2=6248=284\cdot22.
\]
Equivalently
\[
U_2(\infty)=\frac{153}{284},
\qquad
2U_2(\infty)-1=\frac{22}{284}\ne0,
\]
which is exactly the noncancellation criterion (25).

Thus, with \(\deg_2=\delta_2=4\), (36) gives the concrete counterexample
\[
\boxed{8=\deg_{\rm tot}F_{2,2}
>\max(4,4)=4,
\qquad
8=4+4.}
\]
Along the diagonal \(T=S=H\),
\[
F_{2,2}(H,H)=22H^8+O(H^6),
\]
so the same example refutes a max-degree claim interpreted as ordinary evaluation height: exact clearing really pays the sum here.

## Final distilled statements

### (i) Evenness and the exact reduced pair

With \(M_h\) from (5), \(D_h\) from (7), and \(\varepsilon_h=1\) for odd \(h\), \(0\) for even \(h\),
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\beta_h(T)=\frac{p_h(T)}{q_h(T)},\quad
p_h=2T^{1-\varepsilon_h}M_h,\quad
q_h=D_h/T^{\varepsilon_h},\quad
\gcd(p_h,q_h)=1.}
\]
Both \(p_h,q_h\) are even.  For odd \(h\), only one of the central three powers of \(T\) cancels.  The exact nonvanishing certificate at every boundary is (1).

### (ii) Endpoint cross factor

For \(U_h=\beta_h/\beta_h(h)\),
\[
U_h(T)-1=
\frac{q_h(h)p_h(T)-p_h(h)q_h(T)}{p_h(h)q_h(T)},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{T^2-h^2\mid q_h(h)p_h(T)-p_h(h)q_h(T)\quad\text{in }\mathbf Z[T].}
\]
The same factor survives primitive scalar normalization.

### (iii) What exact two-characteristic clearing costs

For reduced one-variable rationals of balanced degrees \(m,n\), exact clearing of \(u(T)+v(S)-1\) always has coordinate degrees at most \((m,n)\).  If the explicit top-corner coefficient (25) is nonzero, its bidegree is exactly \((m,n)\) and its ordinary total degree is exactly \(m+n\).  Thus a max-coordinate statement survives, but a generic total-degree \(\le\max(m,n)\) statement does not.  The primitive \(h=k=2\) numerator (34), with bidegree \((4,4)\), total degree \(8\), and leading mixed coefficient \(22\), is an exact counterexample to such a max-total-degree claim.
