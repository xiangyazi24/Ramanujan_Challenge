# Q8657 rc5 audit: same-sign full-defect fibre

## 1. Determinant normalization

Write
\[
D_i=A_i'B_{u_i}-A_iB_{u_i+h},\qquad A_i=N-d_i,
\]
with \(A_i'=N'-d_i\). Since the same-sign hypothesis gives the same affine shifts, the only varying part in the Apéry index is the translation by \(h\).

The determinant can be rewritten as
\[
D_i=(N'-d_i)B_{u_i}-(N-d_i)B_{u_i+h}
\]
\[
=N'B_{u_i}-NB_{u_i+h}+d_i(B_{u_i+h}-B_{u_i}).
\]
Thus the dependence on the defect is exactly affine.

## 2. Sign pattern from log-convexity

The Apéry numbers satisfy strict log-convexity:
\[
B_n^2<B_{n-1}B_{n+1}.
\]
Equivalently the ratios
\[
R_n=\frac{B_{n+1}}{B_n}
\]
are strictly increasing. Hence for fixed positive shift \(h\),
\[
\frac{B_{u+h}}{B_u}
\]
is strictly increasing in \(u\).

The recurrence
\[
(n+1)^3B_{n+1}=P(n)B_n-n^3B_{n-1}
\]
with positive initial data preserves positivity and strict increase of these ratios. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(D_i)=\operatorname{sgn}\left((N'-d_i)-(N-d_i)\frac{B_{u_i+h}}{B_{u_i}}\right).
\]
The defect enters only through the affine prefactors; changing \(d_i\) changes the linear interpolation but cannot change the monotone ordering forced by the ratios.

For ordered indices \(u_1<u_2<u_3\), the quantities
\[
\frac{B_{u_i+h}}{B_{u_i}}
\]
are strictly ordered, so the three determinants have at most one sign transition. They cannot oscillate. In particular the pattern is one of
\[
+++,\ ++-,\ +--,\ ---
\]
(up to reversing all signs), never \(+ - +\) or \(- + -\).

## 3. Failure of exponential cancellation

Any linear combination
\[
\sum_i c_iD_i
\]
is a combination of the four exponential scales
\[
B_{u_1},B_{u_1+h},B_{u_2},B_{u_2+h},B_{u_3},B_{u_3+h}.
\]
Cancellation of the dominant terms requires coefficient vectors satisfying the same adjoint recurrence relation as the Apéry companion matrix. The determinant coefficients coming from defects are only affine vectors
\[
(N'-d_i,-(N-d_i)),
\]
which do not satisfy the adjoint transport equations except in the trivial proportional case.

The required relation would be an identity of the form
\[
(c_{n+1},c_n)M_n=(c_n,c_{n-1})
\]
for the recurrence matrices \(M_n\). The defect coefficients contain no such recurrence propagation; they are fixed affine data. Therefore the exponential main term cannot be removed nontrivially.

## 4. Primitive saturation cannot repair height

After primitive saturation, a remaining nonzero combination still has size comparable to the largest surviving Apéry term:
\[
|L|\ge \exp(cu)
\]
for some positive constant \(c\), unless the full recurrence-adjoint cancellation occurs. Hence it cannot become a polynomial-height integer carrying three shell primes.

## 5. No-go theorem for +++/--- sector

**Theorem.** In the all-same-sign full-defect fibre, parallel shifts \(u_i(t')=u_i(t)+h\) admit no nonzero defect-only determinant combination of polynomial or subexponential height after primitive saturation. Consequently the determinant route cannot prove
\[
J_{\mathrm{dist}}=o(X/\log^3X).
\]

**Reason.** Strict ratio monotonicity forces a one-transition sign pattern among the determinants, while the only possible exponential cancellation mechanism is an adjoint-recurrence relation. The affine defect vectors fail this relation. Therefore every nontrivial combination retains an Apéry exponential component.

The obstruction is specific to the +++/--- parallel-index geometry: it is not an endpoint-content issue, but the absence of an available recurrence-compatible coefficient vector.
