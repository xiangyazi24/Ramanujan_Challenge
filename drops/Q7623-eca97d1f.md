# Full Laurent \(p^3\) theorem for the Apéry level-6 Hecke packet

## Verdict

The stronger claim is **true**.  For every prime \(p\ge 5\), every \(Q\ge 0\), every \(0\le r<p\), and

\[
n=pQ+r,
\]

one has the coefficientwise congruence of complete Laurent series

\[
U_p\Omega_n \equiv u_0\,\Omega_Q \pmod{p^3}
\qquad\text{in }\mathbf Z((q)),
\]

where

\[
u_0=[q^{-Q}]U_p\Omega_n=[q^r]EHA^n.
\]

Equivalently, for every \(j\ge0\), not merely \(0\le j\le Q\),

\[
[q^{-Q+j}]U_p\Omega_n
\equiv
u_0[q^{-Q+j}]\Omega_Q
\pmod{p^3},
\]

that is,

\[
[q^{r+pj}]EHA^n
\equiv
u_0[q^j]EHA^Q
\pmod{p^3}.
\]

Thus the Q7616 principal-part theorem was a genuine truncation of a stronger global theorem.  The short positive-weight proof works, but only after filling two gaps that are easy to miss:

1. one must prove that every Fricke-odd weight-4 form is divisible by the simple interior zeros of
   \(\Psi:=EH\), so that division by \(\Psi\) creates no interior poles; and
2. one must prove the cusp-filtration statement for \(T_p\) at \(0,1/2,1/3\), using commutation with the normalized Atkin-Lehner involutions and the exact \(U_p/V_p\) order inequalities.

Once those two points are supplied, the whole Laurent congruence follows from an integral finite expansion.

The connected GitHub `main` still does not contain a Q7614 file or the newer scan that motivated the claim, so nothing below uses a finite scan.  The current `problems/3.2/proof.tex` supplies the ambient Apéry framework; the argument here is self-contained from the displayed modular parametrization.

# 1. Modular objects and exact cusp orders

Put

\[
t(\tau)=
\left(
\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau)}
     {\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau)}
\right)^{12},
\]

\[
E(\tau)=
\frac{\eta(2\tau)^7\eta(3\tau)^7}
     {\eta(\tau)^5\eta(6\tau)^5},
\]

\[
D=q\frac d{dq}=\frac1{2\pi i}\frac d{d\tau},
\qquad
H=\frac{Dt}{t},
\qquad
\Psi=EH,
\]

and

\[
\Omega_m=\Psi t^{-m}=EHt^{-m}
=q^{-m}EHA^m,
\qquad
A=\frac qt.
\]

The eta-quotient exponent vectors, indexed by \(1,2,3,6\), are

\[
(12,-12,-12,12)
\]

for \(t\), and

\[
(-5,7,7,-5)
\]

for \(E\).  Direct Ligozat substitution, with the cusps labelled as

\[
\infty,\quad 0,\quad \frac12,\quad \frac13,
\]

gives

\[
\operatorname{ord}(t)
=(1,1,-1,-1),
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{ord}(E)
=(0,0,1,1).
\]

So the label correction in the question is correct.

To compute \(H\), let \(c\) be any cusp and choose a normalized scaling coordinate \(q_c\).  Since \(t\) has order \(e_c\in\{1,-1\}\) at every cusp,

\[
(t|_0\sigma_c)(\tau)=q_c^{e_c}u_c(q_c),
\qquad
u_c(0)\ne0.
\]

By the chain rule for a weight-2 logarithmic derivative,

\[
(H|_2\sigma_c)(\tau)
=q_c\frac d{dq_c}
\log(t|_0\sigma_c)
=e_c+O(q_c).
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{ord}(H)=(0,0,0,0).
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{ord}(\Psi)
=(0,0,1,1),
\]

and, for every \(m\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ord}(\Omega_m)
=(-m,-m,m+1,m+1)
}
\]

at

\[
\infty,\quad0,\quad\frac12,\quad\frac13,
\]

respectively.

The integral \(q\)-expansions at infinity are also exact.  From the eta product,

\[
A(q)
=\frac q{t(q)}
=\prod_{3\nmid a}(1+q^a)^{12}
\in1+q\mathbf Z[[q]].
\]

Also

\[
E\in1+q\mathbf Z[[q]],
\qquad
H=1-D\log A\in1+q\mathbf Z[[q]].
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_m\in q^{-m}\mathbf Z[[q]],
\qquad
\Omega_m=q^{-m}+O(q^{-m+1}).
}
\]

# 2. Fricke parity and the two interior zeros of \(\Psi\)

Use the determinant-normalized slash operator

\[
(f|_kW)(\tau)
=(\det W)^{k/2}(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(W\tau).
\]

For

\[
W_6=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
6&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]

the eta transformation formula and the chain rule give

\[
t|_0W_6=t,
\]

\[
E|_2W_6=-E,
\]

and

\[
H|_2W_6=H.
\]

Hence

\[
\Psi|_4W_6=-\Psi,
\qquad
\Omega_m|_4W_6=-\Omega_m.
\]

The divisor of \(t\) is

\[
\operatorname{div}(t)
=(\infty)+(0)-\left(\frac12\right)-\left(\frac13\right).
\]

Therefore the map

\[
t:X_0(6)\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1
\]

has degree \(2\).  Since \(t\) is \(W_6\)-invariant and \(W_6\) is a nontrivial involution,

\[
\mathbf C(X_0(6))^{W_6}=\mathbf C(t).
\]

The involution \(W_6\) swaps

\[
\infty\leftrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac12\leftrightarrow\frac13,
\]

so its two fixed points are interior points.  Because \(t\) is the degree-two quotient coordinate, both fixed points are simple ramification points of \(t\).

Now let \(x\) be one of these fixed points.  Choose a local coordinate \(z\) centered at \(x\) such that

\[
W_6:z\longmapsto-z.
\]

A weight-4 modular form \(f\) is locally a quadratic differential

\[
\alpha_f=f(\tau)(d\tau)^2.
\]

If \(f|_4W_6=-f\), then

\[
W_6^*\alpha_f=-\alpha_f.
\]

Write

\[
\alpha_f=a(z)(dz)^2.
\]

Since

\[
W_6^*(dz)^2=d(-z)^2=(dz)^2,
\]

anti-invariance gives

\[
a(-z)=-a(z).
\]

Therefore

\[
a(z)=zA(z^2)
\]

for a holomorphic \(A\).  Thus **every Fricke-odd weight-4 form vanishes at every interior \(W_6\)-fixed point**.

For \(\Psi\) we get more: the zero is simple.  Since \(x\) is a simple branch point of the quotient map,

\[
t=t(x)+cz^2+O(z^4),
\qquad
c\ne0,
\qquad
t(x)\ne0,\infty.
\]

The weight-2 differential attached to \(H\) is

\[
H(\tau)d\tau
=\frac1{2\pi i}\frac{dt}{t}.
\]

Hence locally

\[
H(\tau)d\tau
=\frac1{2\pi i}
\left(
\frac{2c}{t(x)}z+O(z^3)
\right)dz.
\]

So \(H\) has a simple zero at \(x\).  The eta function has no zeros in the upper half-plane, hence \(E(x)\ne0\).  Therefore \(\Psi=EH\) also has a simple zero at \(x\).

Consequently, for every Fricke-odd holomorphic weight-4 form \(f\),

\[
\frac f\Psi
\]

is regular at both interior fixed points: in the coordinate \(z\), the numerator is \(z\) times a holomorphic function and the denominator is \(z\) times a unit.

This is the interior regularity needed for the positive-weight basis theorem.  Merely saying “odd forms vanish at fixed points” is not enough; one needs the **simple** zero of \(\Psi\).

# 3. The exact cusp-filtered space \(G(M,L)\)

For integers

\[
M\ge L\ge0,
\]

define \(G(M,L)\) to be the space of weakly holomorphic weight-4 forms \(f\) on \(\Gamma_0(6)\) such that

\[
f|_4W_6=-f,
\]

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(f)\ge-M,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_0(f)\ge-M,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{1/2}(f)\ge L+1,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_{1/3}(f)\ge L+1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
G(M,L)
=igoplus_{m=L}^M\mathbf C\,\Omega_m.
}
\]

Moreover, on the integral \(q\)-lattice at infinity,

\[
\boxed{
G(M,L)\cap\mathbf Z((q))
=igoplus_{m=L}^M\mathbf Z\,\Omega_m.
}
\]

## Proof

Take \(f\in G(M,L)\) and put

\[
R=\frac f\Psi.
\]

Both \(f\) and \(\Psi\) are Fricke odd, so

\[
R|_0W_6=R.
\]

The preceding local quadratic-differential argument proves that \(R\) is regular at the two interior zeros of \(\Psi\).  Away from those points, \(\Psi\) has no interior zeros: \(E\) has none, and the zeros of \(H\) are precisely the two simple ramification points of the degree-two map \(t\).  Thus \(R\) has no interior poles.

Since

\[
\mathbf C(X_0(6))^{W_6}=\mathbf C(t),
\]

we have

\[
R\in\mathbf C(t).
\]

At the two cusps above \(t=0\), \(\Psi\) has order \(0\), so \(R\) has pole order at most \(M\).  At the two cusps above \(t=\infty\), \(\Psi\) has order \(1\), so \(R\) has zero order at least \(L\).  Hence a rational function \(R(t)\) has no finite poles except possibly at \(t=0\), with pole order at most \(M\), and vanishes at \(t=\infty\) to order at least \(L\).  Therefore

\[
R(t)=\sum_{m=L}^M c_m t^{-m}.
\]

Multiplying by \(\Psi\) gives

\[
f=\sum_{m=L}^M c_m\Omega_m.
\]

The reverse inclusion follows immediately from the cusp-order table for \(\Omega_m\).

For the integral statement, use

\[
\Omega_m=q^{-m}+O(q^{-m+1})
\]

with all coefficients integral.  Starting with the coefficient of \(q^{-M}\) and descending, the transition matrix from

\[
\Omega_M,\Omega_{M-1},\ldots,\Omega_L
\]

to the principal monomials

\[
q^{-M},q^{-M+1},\ldots,q^{-L}
\]

is unitriangular over \(\mathbf Z\).  Hence an integral \(q\)-series in \(G(M,L)\) has every \(c_m\in\mathbf Z\).  This proves both assertions.

# 4. Hecke order transport at all four cusps

Let \(p\nmid6\) be prime.  For weight \(4\), normalize

\[
T_p=U_p+p^3V_p,
\]

where, for a Laurent series

\[
f(q)=\sum_{a\in\mathbf Z}c_aq^a,
\]

we set

\[
U_pf=\sum_{a\in\mathbf Z}c_{pa}q^a,
\]

and

\[
V_pf=f(q^p)=\sum_{a\in\mathbf Z}c_aq^{pa}.
\]

If

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(f)=a,
\]

then

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(V_pf)=pa,
\]

exactly, while

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(U_pf)
\ge
\left\lceil\frac ap\right\rceil.
\]

The latter may be a strict inequality if the first coefficient selected by \(U_p\) happens to vanish; the lower bound is the exact filtration statement needed below.

Choose normalized Atkin-Lehner operators \(W_2,W_3,W_6\) so that

\[
W_2\infty=\frac13,
\qquad
W_3\infty=\frac12,
\qquad
W_6\infty=0.
\]

For example one may take determinant-\(2\) and determinant-\(3\) representatives

\[
W_2=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1\\
6&-2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
W_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&1\\
6&3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(d\mid6\) and \(p\nmid6\), the normalized Hecke operator commutes with the normalized Atkin-Lehner operator:

\[
(T_pf)|_4W_d=T_p(f|_4W_d).
\]

This follows directly from the double-coset description: \(W_d\) normalizes \(\Gamma_0(6)\), and because \(p\nmid6\), conjugation by \(W_d\) preserves the local \(p\)-double coset defining \(T_p\).  The normalization is the one whose expansion at infinity is exactly

\[
T_p=U_p+p^3V_p.
\]

Now fix

\[
n=pQ+r,
\qquad
0\le r<p.
\]

At infinity,

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(\Omega_n)=-n.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(U_p\Omega_n)
\ge
\left\lceil-\frac np\right\rceil
=-Q,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(V_p\Omega_n)=-pn.
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(T_p\Omega_n)\ge-pn.
\]

Because \(\Omega_n\) is Fricke odd and \(T_p\) commutes with \(W_6\), the same bound holds at \(0\):

\[
\operatorname{ord}_0(T_p\Omega_n)\ge-pn.
\]

At \(1/3\), transport by \(W_2\).  Since

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{1/3}(\Omega_n)=n+1,
\]

we have

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty(\Omega_n|_4W_2)=n+1.
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty
U_p(\Omega_n|_4W_2)
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{p}\right\rceil
=Q+1,
\]

because

\[
n+1=pQ+r+1
\]

with \(0\le r<p\).  Also

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\infty
V_p(\Omega_n|_4W_2)
=p(n+1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{1/3}(T_p\Omega_n)
\ge Q+1.
\]

The identical argument with \(W_3\) gives

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{1/2}(T_p\Omega_n)
\ge Q+1.
\]

Finally, \(T_p\Omega_n\) is again Fricke odd because \(T_p\) commutes with \(W_6\).  Consequently

\[
\boxed{
T_p\Omega_n\in G(pn,Q).
}
\]

This is the exact positive-weight cusp-filtration statement.

# 5. Integral finite expansion and descent of the principal coefficients

By the basis theorem,

\[
T_p\Omega_n
=\sum_{m=Q}^{pn}C_m\Omega_m.
\]

Since \(\Omega_n\in\mathbf Z((q))\) and

\[
T_p=U_p+p^3V_p
\]

preserves integral Laurent coefficients, the integral part of the basis theorem gives

\[
\boxed{C_m\in\mathbf Z.}
\]

We now descend from \(m=pn\) to \(m=Q\).

Write

\[
\Omega_n=\sum_{a\ge-n}a_aq^a.
\]

For every integer \(m\),

\[
[q^{-m}]T_p\Omega_n
=a_{-pm}
+p^3\mathbf 1_{p\mid m}\,a_{-m/p}.
\]

If \(m>Q\), then

\[
pm\ge p(Q+1)>pQ+r=n.
\]

Hence

\[
-pm<-n,
\]

so

\[
a_{-pm}=0.
\]

Thus for every \(m>Q\),

\[
\boxed{
[q^{-m}]T_p\Omega_n\in p^3\mathbf Z.
}
\]

The \(U_p\)-contribution is exactly zero; the only possible contribution is the \(p^3V_p\) term.

Now compare with

\[
T_p\Omega_n
=\sum_{\ell=Q}^{pn}C_\ell\Omega_\ell.
\]

Since

\[
\Omega_m=q^{-m}+O(q^{-m+1}),
\]

the coefficient of \(q^{-m}\) is

\[
[q^{-m}]T_p\Omega_n
=C_m+
\sum_{\ell>m}C_\ell[q^{-m}]\Omega_\ell.
\]

All coefficients of every \(\Omega_\ell\) are integral.  Descending induction on \(m\), starting at \(m=pn\), therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
C_m\equiv0\pmod{p^3}
\qquad(m>Q).
}
\]

At \(m=Q\),

\[
[q^{-Q}]T_p\Omega_n
=a_{-pQ}
+p^3\mathbf1_{p\mid Q}\,a_{-Q/p}.
\]

The first term is

\[
a_{-pQ}
=[q^{-pQ}]\Omega_n
=[q^{n-pQ}]EHA^n
=[q^r]EHA^n
=u_0.
\]

Hence

\[
[q^{-Q}]T_p\Omega_n
\equiv u_0\pmod{p^3}.
\]

All terms with index \(m>Q\) in the finite \(\Omega_m\)-expansion are already divisible by \(p^3\), and the leading coefficient of \(\Omega_Q\) at \(q^{-Q}\) is \(1\).  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
C_Q\equiv u_0\pmod{p^3}.
}
\]

We have proved the sharper integral expansion statement

\[
\boxed{
T_p\Omega_n
=\sum_{m=Q}^{pn}C_m\Omega_m,
\quad
C_Q\equiv u_0\pmod{p^3},
\quad
C_m\equiv0\pmod{p^3}\ (m>Q).
}
\]

# 6. The whole Laurent congruence

Because every \(\Omega_m\) has integral Laurent coefficients, the finite expansion immediately gives

\[
T_p\Omega_n-u_0\Omega_Q
\in p^3\mathbf Z((q)).
\]

That is,

\[
\boxed{
T_p\Omega_n
\equiv
u_0\Omega_Q
\pmod{p^3}
}
\]

coefficientwise for **every** exponent of \(q\), negative, zero, and positive.

Finally,

\[
T_p\Omega_n
=U_p\Omega_n+p^3V_p\Omega_n.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
U_p\Omega_n
\equiv
u_0\Omega_Q
\pmod{p^3}
\qquad\text{in }\mathbf Z((q)).
}
\]

This is the full Laurent theorem claimed in Q7614.

There is no remaining hidden flaw in the finite-expansion reduction.  The potential flaw would have been an unproved identity

\[
G(M,L)=\operatorname{span}\{\Omega_L,\ldots,\Omega_M\},
\]

because \(\Psi\) has interior zeros.  Once the local quadratic-differential argument proves that every Fricke-odd weight-4 numerator vanishes at those same points and that the zeros of \(\Psi\) are simple, \(f/\Psi\) is regular there and the basis theorem is valid.  The other potential flaw would have been using the infinity \(U_p/V_p\) order calculation at \(1/2,1/3\) without transporting through \(W_2,W_3\); the commutation argument above supplies that missing step.

# 7. Relation with the Q7616 negative-weight/Bol proof

The negative-weight proof used the dual basis

\[
\psi_m=\frac{t^m}{E}
\]

and the exact constant-term pairing

\[
\operatorname{CT}_q(\Omega_n\psi_m)=\delta_{nm}.
\]

Its integral Hecke operator in weight \(-2\) was

\[
\widetilde T_p^{(-2)}
=p^3T_p^{(-2)}
=p^3U_p+V_p,
\]

and Bol's operator gave

\[
D^3\widetilde T_p^{(-2)}
=T_p^{(4)}D^3.
\]

That argument naturally controlled a finite principal-part quotient: pairing against finitely many \(\psi_m\) detects the coefficients of the nonpositive principal part, but by itself it does not determine the positive Fourier tail of an arbitrary weakly holomorphic weight-4 form.

The positive-weight theorem above is therefore strictly stronger **as a conclusion**.  It uses additional global information that the negative-weight pairing did not encode: after applying \(T_p\), the form has strong zero conditions at both \(t=\infty\) cusps.  Those conditions force the entire form into the finite-dimensional space

\[
G(pn,Q),
\]

where the principal coefficients determine the whole modular form through the finite basis

\[
\Omega_Q,\Omega_{Q+1},\ldots,\Omega_{pn}.
\]

So the Q7616 proof stopped at principal parts because of the scope of its dual lattice, not because the full statement is false.

The strongest theorem is:

> **Full Laurent Hecke congruence.**  Let \(p\ge5\) be prime, \(Q\ge0\), \(0\le r<p\), and \(n=pQ+r\).  Then
>
> \[
> U_p\Omega_n\equiv
> \left([q^r]EHA^n\right)\Omega_Q
> \pmod{p^3}
> \]
>
> coefficientwise in \(\mathbf Z((q))\).

No hypothesis \(Q<p\) is needed.

In particular, defining

\[
u_j=[q^{r+pj}]EHA^n
\]

and

\[
d_j(Q)=[q^j]EHA^Q,
\]

we get for every \(j\ge0\)

\[
\boxed{
\nu_j\equiv\nu_0d_j(Q)\pmod{p^3}.
}
\]

The old restriction \(0\le j\le Q\) was only the range corresponding to the nonpositive principal part.

# Publication-quality lemma

**Lemma (full Laurent \(p^3\) Hecke descent at level \(6\)).**
Let

\[
\Psi=EH,
\qquad
\Omega_m=\Psi t^{-m},
\]

with \(t,E,H\) as above.  Let \(p\ge5\) be prime and write

\[
n=pQ+r,
\qquad
Q\ge0,
\qquad
0\le r<p.
\]

Set

\[
u_0=[q^r]EHA^n.
\]

Then there exist integers

\[
C_Q,C_{Q+1},\ldots,C_{pn}
\]

such that

\[
T_p\Omega_n
=\sum_{m=Q}^{pn}C_m\Omega_m,
\]

with

\[
C_Q\equiv u_0\pmod{p^3},
\]

and

\[
C_m\equiv0\pmod{p^3}
\qquad(m>Q).
\]

Consequently

\[
T_p\Omega_n\equiv u_0\Omega_Q\pmod{p^3}
\]

and

\[
U_p\Omega_n\equiv u_0\Omega_Q\pmod{p^3}
\]

coefficientwise in \(\mathbf Z((q))\).

**Proof.**  The cusp orders are

\[
\operatorname{ord}(\Omega_m)=(-m,-m,m+1,m+1)
\]

at

\[
\infty,0,1/2,1/3.
\]

The two interior zeros of \(\Psi\) are exactly the simple ramification points of the degree-two Fricke quotient \(t\).  Every Fricke-odd weight-4 quadratic differential is odd in a local coordinate \(z\mapsto-z\) at such a point, hence vanishes there, so division by \(\Psi\) is regular.  Therefore

\[
G(M,L)=\bigoplus_{m=L}^M\mathbf C\Omega_m,
\]

and its integral \(q\)-lattice is the corresponding \(\mathbf Z\)-span.

Hecke commutes with \(W_2,W_3,W_6\).  At a cusp whose transported order is \(a\), \(V_p\) changes the order to \(pa\), while \(U_p\) changes it to at least

\[
\left\lceil\frac ap\right\rceil.
\]

Since

\[
n=pQ+r,
\qquad0\le r<p,
\]

these bounds give

\[
T_p\Omega_n\in G(pn,Q).
\]

Hence

\[
T_p\Omega_n=\sum_{m=Q}^{pn}C_m\Omega_m
\]

with \(C_m\in\mathbf Z\).  For \(m>Q\), the coefficient of \(q^{-m}\) in \(U_p\Omega_n\) is the coefficient of \(q^{-pm}\) in \(\Omega_n\), which is zero because \(pm>n\).  Thus

\[
[q^{-m}]T_p\Omega_n\in p^3\mathbf Z.
\]

Descending through the unitriangular integral basis \(\Omega_{pn},\ldots,\Omega_Q\) gives

\[
C_m\in p^3\mathbf Z
\qquad(m>Q).
\]

At \(m=Q\),

\[
[q^{-Q}]U_p\Omega_n
=[q^{-pQ}]\Omega_n
=[q^r]EHA^n
=u_0,
\]

while the \(V_p\)-term and all already-descended higher basis terms are multiples of \(p^3\).  Hence

\[
C_Q\equiv u_0\pmod{p^3}.
\]

Reducing the finite expansion modulo \(p^3\) proves

\[
T_p\Omega_n\equiv u_0\Omega_Q\pmod{p^3}.
\]

Since

\[
T_p-U_p=p^3V_p,
\]

the same congruence holds with \(U_p\).  This proves the lemma.  \(\square\)

## Caveat

The theorem is stated for primes \(p\nmid6\), hence for primes \(p\ge5\).  No claim is made here for \(p=2\) or \(p=3\), where the level divides the Hecke prime and the operator/cusp transport changes.  There is **no** restriction \(Q<p\), and there is **no** restriction to the principal part or to \(j\le Q\).
