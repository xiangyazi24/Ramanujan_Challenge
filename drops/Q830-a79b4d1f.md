# Q830: exact nonduplicate attack on the remaining disjoint-edge C4 term

## 1. Setup and exact decomposition

Let
\[
S_{C4}=\sum_n\sum_{e\ne e',\ e\cap e'=\varnothing}X_e(n)X_{e'}(n).
\]
The previous reductions remove the diagonal sector and the one-shared-prime sector. Therefore every surviving ordered pair has four distinct primes
\[
(p,\ell,p',\ell').
\]
The only structural information available from the definition of the canonical Apery masks is that the four divisibility events are generated by the common sequence \(b_n\); they are not independent arbitrary masks.

Write
\[
d=p-\ell,\qquad d'=p'-\ell'.
\]
The requested split is

* equal gap: \(d=d'\);
* unequal gap: \(d\neq d'\).

No unconditional cancellation follows merely from this split.

## 2. Folded-index affine relations

For a fixed q-cell, write the folded index in the usual form
\[
j_p=n-qp,
\]
with the corresponding reflected index in the RR branch
\[
j_p^*=qp-(n+1)
\]
(up to the fixed endpoint convention used in the code). The important fact is that changing the prime by a short gap gives an affine relation.

For a DD pair, both edges use direct folded coordinates:
\[
j_p-j_\ell=(n-qp)-(n-q\ell)=-q(p-\ell)=-qd.
\]
Similarly
\[
j_{p'}-j_{\ell'}=-qd'.
\]
Hence:

* equal gap gives two translates with the same displacement:
\[
(j_p,j_\ell,j_{p'},j_{\ell'})
=(u,u+qd,v,v+qd),
\]
up to orientation;
* unequal gap gives two unrelated translations.

For RR the reflection reverses orientation, so
\[
j_p^*-j_\ell^*=qd.
\]
Thus RR has the same relation with opposite sign.

For mixed DR:
\[
j_p-j_{\ell'}^*
\]
is an affine expression containing the cell parameter and the folded origin. It is not a pure fixed-gap rectangle relation. The only fixed part is the coefficient of the gap, namely \(\pm qd\).

Therefore the four branches are:

| branch | relation available |
|---|---|
| DD | fixed translation by \(-qd\) |
| RR | fixed translation by \(+qd\) |
| DR/RD | affine relation with reflection offset |

No branch yields a genuine additive rectangle of four independent Apery-zero indicators.

## 3. Equal gaps do not close by rectangle extraction

Suppose \(d=d'\). Then DD becomes a pair of parallel translates:
\[
1_{Z_p}(j)1_{Z_\ell}(j+qd)1_{Z_{p'}}(k)1_{Z_{\ell'}}(k+qd).
\]
A rectangle estimate would require control of
\[
\sum_{j,k}F(j)F(j+qd)G(k)G(k+qd),
\]
where the functions arise from different primes but from the same Apery sequence.

The available codegree estimate (Q8740 type input) controls configurations where two prime constraints meet. It does not control four distinct prime constraints with a common n after CRT expansion. The equal-gap condition reduces one parameter but does not create a two-prime collision.

Thus equal gaps do not reduce to matching-block or rectangle counts.

## 4. Unequal gaps and CRT frequencies

After CRT/Fourier expansion, a four-prime term has frequencies
\[
\alpha_p/p+\alpha_\ell/\ell+\alpha_{p'}/p'+\alpha_{\ell'}/\ell'.
\]
The n-sum gives a Weyl factor
\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}e(n\theta).
\]
The weak low-frequency route only gives savings when the denominator structure prevents near-zero frequencies.

For four unrelated primes, there are near-collisions:
\[
\frac{a}{p}+\frac{b}{\ell}\approx-rac{c}{p'}-\frac{d}{\ell'}.
\]
The short-gap condition does not exclude these. Equal gaps are actually the most dangerous because the denominators have correlated sizes.

Therefore CRT-Weyl gives no proved saving at the target scale
\[
o(N^2/\log^2N).
\]

## 5. Matching-block and Q8740 status

Q8740-style codegree bounds remain useful for:

* diagonal terms;
* one shared prime;
* two constraints collapsing to the same local residue.

They do not apply to four distinct primes. The missing input is a four-way correlation theorem for canonical Apery masks.

A matching-block extraction also fails: a matching only removes shared vertices. The remaining C4 sector is already a matching of prime vertices, so the combinatorial obstruction survives.

## 6. Canonical-compatible obstruction

A generic arbitrary-mask counterexample is insufficient, but the following canonical-compatible obstruction identifies exactly what is missing.

The existing hypotheses only constrain finite collections of events involving at most two prime coordinates. Consider a hypothetical Apery-compatible model in which the four-prime joint correlation contains an additional hidden component
\[
\Delta(p,\ell,p',\ell')
\]
that is invisible after every one- and two-coordinate projection:
\[
\sum_{p'}\Delta=\sum_{\ell'}\Delta=0,
\]
while
\[
\sum_{p,\ell,p',\ell'}\Delta
\]
is of the C4 scale.

Every lower-order input remains unchanged, but the C4 term survives. Hence no proof using only the existing pairwise codegree, CRT low-frequency, or matching information can close the theorem.

This is not a construction of the Apery sequence; it is a logical obstruction showing that the currently available lemmas do not imply the desired estimate.

## Conclusion

No existing reduction closes the disjoint-edge C4 term. The exact affine folded-index relations are known, but they do not imply an additive rectangle, matching-block estimate, or CRT-Weyl saving. The remaining obstruction is specifically a four-prime correlation statement for the canonical Apery zero sets.
