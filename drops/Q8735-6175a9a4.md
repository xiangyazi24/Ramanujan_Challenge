# Moving-modulus equidistribution for Apéry zeros

## Verdict

The current project ledger is correct: this is a **quenched cross-prime** problem. No standard sieve theorem gives the desired pointwise estimate from only \(|S_p|/p\to0\).

## 1. Local sparsity alone is categorically insufficient

Choose integers \(n_1<n_2<\cdots\) with \(n_{j+1}>n_j^2\). For primes
\(\sqrt{n_j}<p\le n_j\), set \(S_p=\{n_j\bmod p\}\); these prime blocks are disjoint. Put \(S_p=\varnothing\) otherwise. Then \(|S_p|\le1\), its density is at most \(1/p\), and every nonempty \(S_p\) is the zero set of a linear polynomial over \(\mathbf F_p\), yet
\[
B(n_j)=\pi(n_j)-\pi(\sqrt{n_j})\sim n_j/\log n_j.
\]
Thus bounded cardinality, per-prime algebraicity, and Hasse–Weil bounds do not suffice.

[Gallagher’s larger sieve, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-18-1-77-81), bounds the size of a **set of integers** occupying few classes modulo many primes. Classical large/Turán sieves and [Kowalski’s Frobenius large sieve](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503714) likewise average over integers or family parameters. Applied to the singleton \(\{n\}\), they are vacuous.

## 2. Bombieri–Vinogradov and Chebotarev do not match the quantifiers

The [Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300005313) averages prime-counting errors over moduli \(q\), while the counted prime is a separate variable. Here \(p\) is simultaneously the counted prime and the modulus, and the target \(S_p\) changes with \(p\). There is no known analogue for arbitrary moving targets.

Lagarias–Odlyzko effective Chebotarev concerns Frobenius in a **fixed** finite extension and fixed conjugacy set. Frobenius sieves require a fixed compatible sheaf/monodromy setup and still average family parameters. The condition \(p\mid B_{n\bmod p}\) is not presently represented by a fixed bounded-conductor object; the index and characteristic-\(p\) truncation complexity grow with \(p\).

Apéry structure gives only an exact carrier. Writing \(n=qp+r\), Gessel–Lucas gives
\[
B_n\equiv B_qB_r\pmod p.
\]
Hence \(r\in Z_p\Rightarrow p\mid B_n\), so
\[
n^{B(n)/2}\le\prod_{p\text{ hit}}p\le\operatorname{rad}(B_n).
\]
Since \(\log B_n=\Theta(n)\), this yields only \(B(n)=O(n/\log n)\), not \(o(n/\log n)\).

## 3. A hypothesis that really would suffice

A clean pointwise criterion is a **global certificate** \(A(n)\in\mathbf Z\setminus\{0\}\) such that
\[
n\bmod p\in S_p\Longrightarrow p\mid A(n),\qquad \log|A(n)|=o(n).
\]
Then
\[
B(n)\le \frac{2\log|A(n)|}{\log n}=o(n/\log n).
\]
For reductions of one fixed nonzero polynomial \(f\in\mathbf Z[x]\), take \(A(n)=f(n)\), obtaining \(B(n)=O_f(1)\) unless \(f(n)=0\). Fixed finite unions or bounded-degree closed loci give the same through a product/resultant.

A Chebotarev route would need a uniform family with bad-Frobenius proportion \(o(1)\) **and** effective error \(o(n/\log n)\) at scale \(X=n\), with conductor/discriminant controlled uniformly. No such structure is known for the Apéry coefficient-zero sets. Here the available certificate is \(A(n)=B_n\), of exponential height; improving its large-prime radical to subexponential size is essentially the original problem.
