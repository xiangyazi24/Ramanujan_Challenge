# Q6689 qHATCH audit: union sieve versus collision energy

## Verdict

The addition-law cascade is the only part of the proposed qHATCH route that has a genuine new mechanism. The certified square-root bounds for individual gap polynomials do not close (4.9), and a direct Selberg detector optimization over the gap family does not produce the required L^2 log D saving without an additional correlation input.

The sharp unconditional statement from the present toolkit is:

P_D <= sum_{d<=D} C_d << D^2

with the improved first-return formulation

P_D = sum_{d<=D} P_d^prim,

where

P_d^prim = |Z(N_d) \ union_{e<d} Z(N_e)|.

The missing term is an overlap theorem for the first-return sieve.

## (a) Addition-law cascade

Assume

N_d(r)=0 and N_e(r)=0, 0<e<d.

Equivalently,

pi(r)=pi(r+d)=pi(r+e).

The transfer composition gives

pi(r+e)=pi(r+d)

inside the shifted window, hence

pi(r+e)=pi(r+e+(d-e)).

Therefore

N_{d-e}(r+e)=0.

This is an exact cascade identity. It implies that every multiple return is witnessed by a shorter return after translation.

However, it does NOT by itself imply that

|Z(N_d) intersect later-return bases|

is a positive proportion of |Z(N_d)|. The obstruction is primitive periodic returns: a base can have first return d and no shorter return at the same base. The cascade moves the secondary collision to r+e; it does not put it back into the original zero set.

Hence the desired estimate

sum_d P_d^prim << D/(L^2 log D)

cannot be derived from the cascade alone.

## First-return sieve reformulation

The correct quantity is

P_D = |union_{d<=D} Z(N_d)| = sum_{d<=D} |Z(N_d) \setminus U_{d-1}|,

U_{d-1}=union_{e<d}Z(N_e).

Using the degree bound gives

|Z(N_d)| <= 3(d-1),

but this only gives

P_D << D^2.

To improve to near D one needs a theorem of the form

|Z(N_d) \cap U_{d-1}| >= (1-o(1))|Z(N_d)|

on average over d. No such theorem follows from adjacent coprimality, reflection, or Chebotarev fixed-h statistics.

## (b) Mollified detector attempt

Let R_d(r) be a zero detector for N_d(r)=0. A weighted union bound gives

P_D <= sum_r min(1, sum_{d<=D} w_d R_d(r)).

The certified per-d estimate gives

sum_r R_d(r) = O(d),

and the Weil-type square-root error controls each fixed d.

Optimizing only with these inputs reduces to a linear programming problem whose best possible scale remains

sum_{d<=D} d = D^2.

The reason is that the detectors have no proven negative covariance. A Selberg sieve needs pair correlations

#{r:N_d(r)=N_e(r)=0}

with cancellation beyond the diagonal. The available curve estimates give only

O(de) main size plus O((d+e)sqrt(p))

and after summing over d,e the family error dominates.

Therefore the mollified detector does not prove (4.9).

## (c) What would actually close qHATCH

The needed new lemma is not a single-gap estimate but a primitive-return suppression statement:

For D=sqrt(N)L,

sum_{d<=D} P_d^prim << N/(L^2 log D).

Equivalent useful forms are:

1. A first-return density theorem:

average_d |Z(N_d) \setminus U_{d-1}| << 1/(L^2 log D).

2. A pair-overlap theorem:

sum_{d,e<=D}|Z(N_d) intersection Z(N_e)| >> (sum_d |Z(N_d)|)^2 / N

at the strength required by the Selberg sieve.

3. A family cancellation theorem for the difference curves

Delta_{r,d}=Delta_{r',e}

showing cancellation of the Weil error terms over d,e <= D.

The last formulation is the most natural geometric target.

## Final lemma statement

OPEN LEMMA (qHATCH primitive-return suppression).

Let

P_D = |{r: exists d<=D with N_d(r)=0 in F_p}|.

For D=sqrt(p)L, prove

P_D << p/(L^2 log D).

Equivalently, prove that the primitive return mass satisfies

sum_{d<=D}|Z(N_d) minus union_{e<d}Z(N_e)| << p/(L^2 log D).

Current toolkit proves the cascade identity and individual square-root cancellation, but neither implies this lemma. The breakthrough must be a family-averaged correlation theorem for the growing difference-curve family.
