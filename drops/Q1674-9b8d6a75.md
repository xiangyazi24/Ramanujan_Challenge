# P3.2 canonical target-jet evaluation: all-one-shell collapse

## Verdict

There is a genuine collapse of the source sum.  For the canonical all-one shell, the target jet is the universal finite harmonic sum

\[
\boxed{
D_r=
\sum_{k=1}^{r}
\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2
\bigl(H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}\bigr).
}
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently, at a simple zero \(b_r=0\),

\[
\boxed{
N_r=r^3b_{r-1}
\sum_{k=1}^{r}
\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2
\bigl(H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}\bigr).
}
\tag{2}
\]

This is stronger than the variation-of-parameters formula in the question: the inhomogeneous source sum \(\sum_{j<r}b_js_j\) has disappeared completely.  In fact \(D_r\) is independent of the cutoff \(m\) as soon as \(m\ge r\).

There is also an exact, quadratic-independent \(p^2\)-reflection formula:

\[
\boxed{
A_{p-1-r}\equiv A_r-2pD_r\pmod{p^2},
}
\tag{3}
\]

where \(A_n=\sum_k\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2\) is the integer Apéry number.  Thus, whenever \(p\mid A_r\),

\[
\boxed{
D_r\equiv
\frac{A_r-A_{p-1-r}}{2p}\pmod p.
}
\tag{4}
\]

The quotient is an ordinary integer quotient before reduction mod \(p\), since the two Apéry values are congruent mod \(p\).  This is the sharpest useful new restriction I can get from the all-one shell: it determines the target jet itself from the first antisymmetric \(p\)-adic reflection digit, rather than merely putting it in a character class.

It excludes both supplied quadratic roots in both anchors:

- \(p=17,r=3\): \(D_r=9\), roots \(2,7\); neither equals \(9\).
- \(p=31,r=8\): \(D_r=29\), roots \(10,16\); neither equals \(29\).

However, **quadratic character alone cannot be the universal closing invariant**.  At \(p=17\), both \(D_r=9\) and the bad candidate root \(2\) are squares.  At \(p=31\), character happens to work: \(D_r=29\) is a nonsquare while \(10,16\) are both squares.  Therefore a one-bit square/nonsquare rule loses information already at the \(p=17,r=3\) anchor; the exact reflection digit (4) is the right object.

## 1. Exact all-one-shell derivation

Put

\[
\lambda_s=s(s+1),\qquad
\phi_k(Y)=\frac{\prod_{s=0}^{k-1}(Y-\lambda_s)}{(k!)^2}.
\]

For an integer index \(n\),

\[
\phi_k(\lambda_n)
=\frac{\prod_{s<k}(n-s)(n+s+1)}{(k!)^2}
=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}.
\]

Hence the canonical all-one shell is

\[
P_m(Y)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\phi_k(Y)^2,
\]

and on the grid \(b_n=P_m(\lambda_n)=A_n\) for \(0\le n\le m\).

Fix \(r\le m\).  For \(k>r\), \(\phi_k(\lambda_r)=0\), so \(\phi_k(Y)^2\) has a double zero at \(Y=\lambda_r\); therefore its first derivative contributes zero.  Thus

\[
P_m'(\lambda_r)
=2\sum_{k=1}^{r}
\phi_k(\lambda_r)\phi_k'(\lambda_r).
\]

For \(1\le k\le r\), logarithmic differentiation gives

\[
\frac{\phi_k'(\lambda_r)}{\phi_k(\lambda_r)}
=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\frac1{\lambda_r-\lambda_s}
=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\frac1{(r-s)(r+s+1)}.
\]

Use

\[
\frac{2r+1}{(r-s)(r+s+1)}
=\frac1{r-s}+\frac1{r+s+1}.
\]

Summing in \(s\),

\[
(2r+1)\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}
\frac1{\lambda_r-\lambda_s}
=H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}.
\]

Since

\[
D_r=\frac{2r+1}{2}P_m'(\lambda_r),
\]

we obtain (1):

\[
D_r=
\sum_{k=1}^{r}
\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2
(H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}).
\]

This is an exact characteristic-zero identity.  Modulo an odd prime \(p=2m+1\), with \(r<m\), every harmonic denominator is at most \(2r\le p-3\), so reduction modulo \(p\) is legitimate term-by-term.

A useful equivalent formulation is the index derivative.  Let

\[
\mathcal A_m(x)=P_m(x(x+1)).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_m'(r)=2D_r.}
\tag{5}
\]

Again, all terms with \(k>r\) have a double zero at \(x=r\), so this derivative is independent of \(m\ge r\).

## 2. Actual collapse of \(N_r\)

The question gives, at a simple zero,

\[
D_r=\frac{N_r}{r^3b_{r-1}},\qquad
N_r=\sum_{j<r}b_js_j.
\]

Substituting (1) produces (2), namely

\[
N_r=r^3b_{r-1}
\sum_{k=1}^{r}
\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2
(H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}).
\]

So this is not a repackaged variation-of-parameters formula: the \(j\)-source sum has been evaluated in terms of one finite hypergeometric/harmonic shell at the target itself.

For comparison, direct expansion of the source sum only gives the adjoint quadratic form

\[
N_r=
\frac12\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}C'(j)b_j^2
-3\sum_{j=0}^{r-2}(j+1)^2b_jb_{j+1},
\]

because the \(b_r\) boundary term vanishes.  Formula (2) is independent of that quadratic form and is strictly more target-local.

## 3. The stronger \(p^2\) reflection-digit formula

The all-one shell also explains the reflection formula in `q32_apery_reflection_p2.py`.

The grid map is invariant under \(x\mapsto-1-x\):

\[
(-1-r)(-r)=r(r+1)=\lambda_r.
\]

Now move from \(-1-r\) to \(p-1-r=-1-r+p\).  To first order,

\[
(p-1-r)(p-r)
=\lambda_r-p(2r+1)+p^2.
\]

Taylor expansion of the shell therefore gives

\[
P_m(\lambda_r-p(2r+1)+p^2)
\equiv b_r-p(2r+1)P_m'(\lambda_r)
=b_r-2pD_r\pmod{p^2}.
\]

For the full integer Apéry sum \(A_{p-1-r}\), terms beyond the lower branch have at least a squared factor of \(p\), so they vanish modulo \(p^2\).  Hence

\[
A_{p-1-r}\equiv A_r-2pD_r\pmod{p^2}.
\]

At a mod-\(p\) zero this becomes the exact first-digit extraction (4):

\[
D_r\equiv\frac{A_r-A_{p-1-r}}{2p}\pmod p.
\]

This gives a very small independent gate for any proposed quadratic value of \(D_r\): compute two Apéry numbers modulo \(p^2\), take one divided difference, and compare with the two roots.  No adjoint quadratic is used to derive the value.

Equivalently, if

\[
\delta_r:=\frac{A_{p-1-r}-A_r}{p}\pmod p,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{\delta_r=-2D_r.}
\tag{6}
\]

For a candidate quadratic \(\alpha X^2+\beta X+\gamma\), the independent exclusion test can therefore be written directly as

\[
\alpha\left(-\frac{\delta_r}{2}\right)^2
+\beta\left(-\frac{\delta_r}{2}\right)+\gamma\ne0\pmod p.
\]

## 6. What this does and does not buy for the square-discriminant gate

Suppose a square-discriminant record gives

\[
\alpha X^2+\beta X+\gamma=0,
\qquad
X\in\{x_+,x_-\}.
\]

The new independent gate is

\[
\boxed{
W_r:=
\sum_{k=1}^{r}
\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2
(H_{r+k}-H_{r-k})
\notin\{x_+,x_-\}.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Or, without harmonic sums,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A_r-A_{p-1-r}}{2p}
\notin\{x_+,x_-\}\pmod p.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Equation (8) is particularly attractive computationally because it only needs the Apéry recurrence modulo \(p^2\).  It is independent of the derivation of the adjoint quadratic and therefore is a true cross-check rather than circular reuse of the same quadratic identity.

What is still missing is a theorem showing that (7) or (8) can never equal either root for **all** square-discriminant target records.  The pushed reflection audit supplies strong evidence that the target zero is simple through its tested range, but it does not prove a fixed residue class or fixed quadratic character for \(D_r\).  The two anchors also show why a universal character theorem would be too weak: the \(p=17\) root \(2\) has the same quadratic character as the actual jet \(9\).

Thus the best next theorem is not “\(D_r\) is always a square/nonsquare.”  It is a direct incompatibility between the reflection digit

\[
\delta_r=(A_{p-1-r}-A_r)/p
\]

and the adjoint quadratic after substituting \(D_r=-\delta_r/2\).  Any proof of that incompatibility would combine two genuinely independent structures: the all-one-shell \(p^2\) reflection law and the adjoint quadratic.

## 7. Minimal mechanical verifier for the record bank

No source-sum reconstruction is needed.  For each square-discriminant record one can check the following logic:

```text
input: p, r, alpha, beta, gamma
require: p odd, 0 < r < (p-1)/2, A_r == 0 mod p

D_shell = sum_{k=1}^r
    C(r,k)^2 C(r+k,k)^2 (H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}) mod p

Alo = A_r mod p^2
Ahi = A_{p-1-r} mod p^2
D_reflection = ((Alo-Ahi)/p) * inverse(2) mod p

assert D_shell == D_reflection
assert alpha*D_shell^2 + beta*D_shell + gamma != 0 mod p
```

The first assertion cross-checks two independent evaluations of the canonical jet; the second is the desired root exclusion.  For the two supplied anchors, the first value is respectively \(9\) and \(29\), and the quadratic residuals are respectively \(14\) and \(30\), both nonzero.

## Bottom line

The requested source sum really does collapse:

\[
\boxed{D_r=W_r}
\]

with \(W_r\) the explicit binomial/harmonic shell in (1), and hence

\[
\boxed{N_r=r^3b_{r-1}W_r.}
\]

The sharper independent congruence is

\[
\boxed{A_{p-1-r}-A_r\equiv-2pD_r\pmod{p^2}.}
\]

This exact first reflection digit excludes both quadratic roots at \((17,3)\) and \((31,8)\).  A mere quadratic-character restriction cannot be the general proof because at \(p=17\) the actual jet \(9\) and the spurious root \(2\) lie in the same square class.  The strongest credible next attack is therefore to substitute the **reflection digit** \(D_r=(A_r-A_{p-1-r})/(2p)\) into the square-discriminant quadratic and prove its residual nonzero directly, rather than trying to squeeze \(D_r\) into a one-bit character class.
