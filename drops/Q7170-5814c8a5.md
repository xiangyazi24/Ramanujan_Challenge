## Conclusion

The proposed global reflection argument gives a rigorous deterministic inequality, but it does **not** by itself force centered adjacency. Reflection and consecutiveness only impose a geometric exclusion interval around the reflected points. They do not contradict `p > s^2`.

The strongest unconditional inequality from the stated global facts is the following.

Let the four globally consecutive zeros be

\[
 z_0<z_1<z_2<z_3,
\]

with gaps

\[
 a=z_1-z_0,\qquad b=z_2-z_1,\qquad c=z_3-z_2,
\]

and span

\[
 s=a+b+c.
\]

Let the reflection center be

\[
 m=(p-1)/2.
\]

If the window is off-center, no reflected point belongs to the same four-zero window. Since the reflected zeros are also zeros and the original four zeros are consecutive, the reflected block must lie entirely outside the open interval `(z_0,z_3)`.

Therefore either

\[
 z_3 < 2m-z_3 < 2m-z_2 < 2m-z_1 < 2m-z_0
\]

or the symmetric reversed ordering. Equivalently, after translating by the center,

\[
 |z_i-m|+|z_j-m| \ge p-1
\]

for the nearest original/reflected boundary pair. In particular, if

\[
 d=\min_i |z_i-m|,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{d\ge (p-1-s)/2.}
\]

This is the sharp deterministic distance-to-center inequality available from reflection plus consecutiveness alone.

## 1. Why it does not prove the short-window reflection principle

The desired contradiction would require showing that a block of four zeros of span

\[
s\le \sqrt p
\]

cannot fit in one half while its reflected copy fits in the other half.

But the inequality above only says that the window is far from the center:

\[
 d\ge (p-1-s)/2.
\]

For `s << p`, this is perfectly compatible: the block simply sits near an endpoint and its mirror sits near the opposite endpoint.

No contradiction with `p>s^2` follows.

The missing ingredient would need to show that actual Apéry zeros cannot have such endpoint-separated reflected blocks.

## 2. Pair-cap consequence

For a zero pair at gap `h`, the roots are roots of `N_h` and hence

\[
\#\{x:z_x=0,\ z_{x+h}=0\}\le 3(h-1).
\]

A four-zero window contributes the three pairs

\[
(a,b,c),
\]

so summing pair caps gives only

\[
\#\text{windows with gaps }(a,b,c)
\le \min(3(a-1),3(b-1),3(c-1)).
\]

This is a local bound. It does not see that the same four zeros have reflections and does not couple the three gaps.

The total reflected contribution can therefore be bounded, but not eliminated:

\[
\sum_{a+b+c=s}
\min(a,b,c)
=O(s^3).
\]

There is no extra factor forcing one of `a,b,c` to be a centered pair.

## 3. Test against the p=3727 long off-center window

The known long off-center actual window has

\[
p=3727
\]

and satisfies the reflection geometry. Since its span is much smaller than `p`, the distance inequality predicts only that it lies far from the midpoint.

That is exactly what occurs: reflection gives another block near the opposite side; it does not create an impossible interlacing order.

Thus the `p=3727` example is not a counterexample to the inequality. It is evidence that the inequality is the strongest consequence of reflection ordering alone.

## 5. Exact missing Apéry input

The missing statement is not another continuant identity. It must couple the orbit structure with reflection.

Equivalent possible forms are:

### (A) Actual short-window reflection principle

There exists fixed `\eta>0` such that every actual consecutive four-zero window with

\[
s\le \eta\sqrt p
\]

contains a centered adjacent pair.

### (B) Distinguished-orbit coupling

A primitive short return chain cannot occur in a non-central reflection orbit unless it lies in the structural carrier.

### (C) Stronger than pair caps

The triple return condition

\[
N_a(z_0)=N_b(z_1)=N_c(z_2)=0
\]

must have a global incompatibility with its reflected triple, not visible in any single `N_h` degree bound.

## Final statement

The rigorous deterministic geometry is:

\[
\boxed{|z_i-(p-1)/2|\ge (p-1-s)/2\text{ for an off-center consecutive window}.}
\]

Combined with pair caps this gives only cubic counting bounds in the span parameters. It does **not** imply a contradiction when `p>s^2`.

The short-window reflection principle therefore requires a genuinely Apéry-specific input: a coupling between the distinguished orbit and its reflection, not merely the global zero-set axioms.
