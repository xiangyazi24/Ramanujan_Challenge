# P3.2 — defining-prime slope Fourier L2 / DSLS2 audit

## Verdict

There is a real unconditional theorem here, but it is weaker than the natural “random-scale” DSLS2 and it exposes the exact remaining wall.

For a fixed Apéry zero index \(q\), let
\[
\mathcal D_q(P)=\{p\text{ prime}:P<p\le 2P,\ p\mid b_q,\ q\le (p-1)/2\}.
\]
The banked reflection/palindrome law forces the second zero \(p-1-q\). Hence the normalized mirror gap (“defining-prime slope”) is
\[
\theta_{p,q}=\frac{p-1-2q}{p}
=1-\frac{c_q}{p},
\qquad c_q:=2q+1.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus for every integer Fourier mode \(k\),
\[
e(k\theta_{p,q})=e\!\left(-\frac{k c_q}{p}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
This common numerator \(c_q\) is the usable Apéry-specific structure.

If
\[
S_q(k;P)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal D_q(P)}w_p e(k\theta_{p,q}),
\qquad
V=\sum_{p\in\mathcal D_q(P)}|w_p|^2,
\]
then the ordinary large sieve, applied to the reciprocal points \(c_q/p\), gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{1\le |k|<K}
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{K}\right)|S_q(k;P)|^2
\ll
\left(K+\frac{P^2}{c_q}\right)V
\asymp
\left(K+\frac{P^2}{q}\right)V.
}
\tag{LS-q}
\]
This improves the generic varying-modulus \(K+P^2\) barrier by a factor \(q\). In particular, the full random/diagonal-scale DSLS2
\[
L_K\ll K V\,P^{o(1)}
\tag{DSLS2-opt}
\]
follows from spacing alone only in the range
\[
\boxed{K\gtrsim P^2/q}
\tag{3}
\]
(up to subpowers/logarithms).

However, **full DSLS2 is stronger than what Fejér needs if the downstream goal is merely to rule out a macroscopic slope cluster.** The exact Fejér implication is
\[
W(I)
\le \frac{\pi}{2}
\sqrt{\frac{W^2+L_K}{K}},
\qquad |I|\le \frac1{2K},
\tag{F}
\]
where \(w_p\ge0\),
\[
W=\sum_p w_p,
\qquad
W(I)=\sum_{\theta_{p,q}\in I}w_p,
\]
and
\[
L_K=\sum_{1\le |k|<K}
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{K}\right)|S_q(k;P)|^2.
\]
Consequently it is enough for \(W(I)=o(W)\) that
\[
\boxed{K\to\infty,\qquad L_K=o(KW^2).}
\tag{4}
\]
For equal weights, with \(M=|\mathcal D_q(P)|\), the large-sieve theorem (LS-q) therefore already gives no macroscopic cluster provided
\[
\boxed{
K\to\infty,\qquad M\to\infty,\qquad
\frac{qKM}{P^2}\to\infty.
}
\tag{5}
\]
This is weaker than \(K\gtrsim P^2/q\) by the factor \(M\).

There is an even more useful absolute version when the fixed-\(q\) ledger only needs the clustered defining-prime **log mass** to be \(o(q)\). Since
\[
\sum_{p\mid b_q}\log p\le \log b_q=O(q),
\tag{6}
\]
take \(w_p=\log p\). Then \(V\ll (\log P)W\ll q\log P\), and (F)+(LS-q) gives
\[
\boxed{
W(I)
\ll
\frac{q}{\sqrt K}
+\sqrt{q\log P}
+P\sqrt{\frac{\log P}{K}}.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Hence in polynomial/subexponential \(P\)-ranges where \(\log P=o(q)\), it is enough that
\[
\boxed{
K\gg \frac{P^2\log P}{q^2}\,\omega(q),
\qquad \omega(q)\to\infty,
}
\tag{8}
\]
to obtain \(W(I)=o(q)\). This is weaker than optimal DSLS2 by essentially another factor \(q/\log P\).

Below the threshold in (5), or below the absolute threshold (8) if that is the actual ledger target, I do **not** find an unconditional proof from the banked Apéry structure. The obstruction is exact: after duality/dispersion, one must control short gaps among the prime divisors \(p\mid b_q\) in a dyadic block, on the scale
\[
\boxed{\Delta_P\asymp \frac{P^2}{qK}.}
\tag{9}
\]
The centered recurrence and quasiperiod/reflection identify the phase (1), but they are one-characteristic statements. They do not control how two distinct defining primes \(p,\ell\mid b_q\) are spaced. Trace-function completion is also at the wrong level: there is no single fixed finite field or bounded-conductor trace function whose variable is the defining prime and whose support is \(p\mid b_q\). A dispersion expansion simply rewrites DSLS2 as this same short-gap pair-correlation problem.

So the sharp conclusion is:

1. **Proved unconditionally:** the Apéry common-numerator large sieve (LS-q).
2. **Full DSLS2 not proved by the available structure:** the first missing gain is precisely over the \(P^2/q\) reciprocal-spacing term.
3. **Likely DSLS2 is over-strong for the intended Fejér step:** use (4), (5), or the weighted absolute criterion (8), depending on the actual downstream target.
4. **Precise circularity:** any proposed “dispersion proof” of the missing range that estimates the off-diagonal by saying defining primes do not cluster on intervals of length \(P^2/(qK)\) has assumed the Fejér conclusion/DSLS2 in its dual form.

# 1. The Apéry-specific slope is reciprocal with a common numerator

The current banked palindrome/reflection law says, for \(0\le r<p\),
\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore, if \(p\mid b_q\) and \(q<p\), then
\[
p\mid b_{p-1-q}.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(q\le(p-1)/2\), the two forced zeros are ordered as
\[
q<p-1-q,
\]
and their gap is
\[
h_{p,q}=p-1-2q.
\tag{12}
\]
The normalized slope is exactly
\[
\theta_{p,q}=\frac{h_{p,q}}p
=1-\frac{2q+1}{p}.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus, with \(c_q=2q+1\),
\[
e(k\theta_{p,q})=e(-kc_q/p).
\tag{14}
\]

This is the key structural simplification. The defining-prime family is not a generic collection of fractions \(a_p/p\) with unrelated numerators. Every member has the **same numerator** \(c_q\).

The historical `oracleA_result.tex` already contains the compatible doublet Fourier identity: if a zero set is a reflected doublet
\[
\{r,p-1-r\},
\]
then with \(h=p-1-2r\),
\[
F_p(a)=2e_p\!\left(\frac{a(p-1)}2\right)
\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi ah}{p}\right).
\tag{15}
\]
After removing the harmless center phase, the only oscillating parameter is \(h/p\), exactly (13) when \(r=q\).

The centered recurrence is useful for explaining this reflection internally: centering at \((p-1)/2\) turns \(r\mapsto p-1-r\) into \(t\mapsto-t\), and the identity \(P(-x-1)=-P(x)\) is the algebraic reflection behind the transfer symmetry. The quasiperiod/Lucas structure explains how the mod-\(p\) recurrence repeats or rescales across \(p\)-blocks. But for the present Fourier problem, all that structure has already been compressed into the single reciprocal phase (14).

That observation is both the opportunity and the limit: once (14) is reached, a low-frequency mean square is a question about the spacing/correlation of the defining primes \(p\mid b_q\).

# 2. Exact Fejér implication

Let
\[
\mathcal D=\mathcal D_q(P),
\qquad
\theta_p=\theta_{p,q},
\qquad
w_p\ge0,
\]
and define
\[
S(k)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal D}w_p e(k\theta_p).
\tag{16}
\]
Set
\[
W=\sum_{p\in\mathcal D}w_p,
\qquad
W(I)=\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal D\\\theta_p\in I}}w_p.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(K\ge2\), use the standard Fejér kernel
\[
\mathcal F_K(x)
=\sum_{|k|<K}\left(1-\frac{|k|}{K}\right)e(kx)
=\frac1K\left|\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}e(jx)\right|^2.
\tag{18}
\]
It is nonnegative. If \(\|x\|\le1/(2K)\), then
\[
\mathcal F_K(x)
=\frac1K\left(\frac{\sin \pi Kx}{\sin\pi x}\right)^2
\ge \frac{4K}{\pi^2}.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed \(|\sin \pi Kx|\ge2K\|x\|\) and \(|\sin\pi x|\le\pi\|x\|\) in that range.

Define the Fejér energy
\[
\mathscr E_K
:=\sum_{|k|<K}\left(1-\frac{|k|}{K}\right)|S(k)|^2.
\tag{20}
\]
Expanding and using (18),
\[
\mathscr E_K
=\sum_{p,\ell\in\mathcal D}
 w_p w_\ell\,
 \mathcal F_K(\theta_p-\theta_\ell).
\tag{21}
\]
Because all terms are nonnegative, if \(I\subset\mathbb T\) has length at most \(1/(2K)\), then every pair \(p,\ell\) in \(I\) has
\[
\|\theta_p-\theta_\ell\|\le1/(2K),
\]
so (19) gives
\[
\mathscr E_K
\ge\frac{4K}{\pi^2}W(I)^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
W(I)\le \frac\pi2\sqrt{\frac{\mathscr E_K}{K}}.
}
\tag{23}
\]

Now separate the zero Fourier mode. Since \(S(0)=W\), put
\[
L_K
:=\sum_{1\le|k|<K}
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{K}\right)|S(k)|^2.
\tag{24}
\]
Then
\[
\mathscr E_K=W^2+L_K,
\]
and hence the **exact DSLS2-to-arc implication** is
\[
\boxed{
W(I)
\le \frac\pi2
\sqrt{\frac{W^2}{K}+\frac{L_K}{K}}.
}
\tag{25}
\]

Two consequences matter.

### 2.1 Optimal \(L^2\) does not give Poisson-scale pointwise arcs

If unit weights are used and \(M=|\mathcal D|\), then \(W=M\). Even the ideal random-scale estimate
\[
L_K\ll K M^{1+o(1)}
\tag{26}
\]
only gives
\[
\boxed{
\#\{p:\theta_p\in I\}
\ll
\frac{M}{\sqrt K}+M^{1/2+o(1)}.
}
\tag{27}
\]
It does **not** by itself give the expected pointwise arc count \(M/K\). To obtain \(M/K\) uniformly in \(I\) one would need a stronger maximal/higher-moment input. Thus if Q2730's Fejér step claims \(M/K\) directly from a second moment, that implication is too strong.

### 2.2 Full DSLS2 is stronger than needed for nonconcentration

To prove merely
\[
W(I)=o(W)
\tag{28}
\]
uniformly for arcs of length \(1/(2K)\), (25) shows that it is enough that
\[
\boxed{
K\to\infty,
\qquad
L_K=o(KW^2).
}
\tag{29}
\]
There is no need for \(L_K\ll KV\) if the downstream theorem only needs (28).

More quantitatively, for unit weights, if one wants
\[
\#\{p:\theta_p\in I\}\ll M^{1-\delta+o(1)},
\qquad 0<\delta\le\tfrac12,
\tag{30}
\]
then (25) shows that the exact sufficient conditions are
\[
\boxed{
K\ge M^{2\delta-o(1)},
\qquad
L_K\ll K M^{2-2\delta+o(1)}.
}
\tag{31}
\]
The first condition comes from the unavoidable zero mode \(M^2/K\). The second is the weakest \(L^2\) exponent compatible with the desired arc exponent through Fejér.

That is the clean answer to “if a weaker exponent suffices”: the relevant exponent is set by the **actual arc-count target**, not by the random-scale benchmark.

# 3. Apéry-specific large sieve: an unconditional theorem

We now exploit (14).

Let \(p\ne\ell\) lie in \(\mathcal D_q(P)\), so \(P<p,\ell\le2P\), and put \(c=c_q=2q+1\). On the forced-mirror branch we have \(c\le p,\ell\). Then
\[
\theta_p-\theta_\ell
=c\left(\frac1\ell-\frac1p\right)
=\frac{c(p-\ell)}{p\ell}.
\tag{32}
\]
Since distinct odd primes differ by at least \(2\), the ordinary real difference has size
\[
|\theta_p-\theta_\ell|
\ge \frac{2c}{(2P)^2}
=\frac{c}{2P^2}.
\tag{33}
\]
One must check torus wrap. If the real difference is at most \(1/2\), (33) is already the torus separation. If it exceeds \(1/2\), then because \(c\le\min(p,\ell)\), the complementary distance to the nearest integer is at least \(c/(2P)\), which is larger than \(c/(2P^2)\). Thus in all cases
\[
\boxed{
\|\theta_p-\theta_\ell\|\gg \frac{c_q}{P^2}\asymp\frac q{P^2}.
}
\tag{34}
\]

The standard large sieve for \(\delta\)-spaced points \(\alpha_j\in\mathbb T\) gives
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\left|\sum_j a_j e(k\alpha_j)\right|^2
\le (K+\delta^{-1}+O(1))\sum_j|a_j|^2.
\tag{35}
\]
Applying this to the \(	heta_p\), and doubling for positive/negative \(k\), yields
\[
\boxed{
L_K
\ll
\left(K+\frac{P^2}{c_q}\right)
\sum_{p\in\mathcal D_q(P)}|w_p|^2.
}
\tag{36}
\]
This is (LS-q).

## 3.1 Comparison with the generic varying-modulus barrier

If the numerators were arbitrary \(a_p\), the Farey spacing between \(a_p/p\) and \(a_\ell/\ell\) is only \(\gg P^{-2}\), so one gets
\[
K+P^2.
\]
The defining-prime mirror law replaces that by
\[
K+P^2/q.
\]
This factor \(q\) is real and comes directly from Apéry reflection.

The earlier repository `oracleA_result.tex` is the generic version of exactly this phenomenon. Its Gallagher/Fejér reduction can use the ordinary large sieve only in the range where the interval length dominates the \(P^2\) spacing term. DSLS2's defining-prime slopes improve the spacing, but do not remove the same mechanism.

## 3.2 When does this prove random-scale DSLS2?

From (36),
\[
L_K\ll KV
\]
follows as soon as
\[
K\gg P^2/q.
\tag{37}
\]
Below this, spacing alone leaves the excess factor
\[
\boxed{
\frac{P^2}{qK}.
}
\tag{38}
\]
This is the first sharp exponent obstruction for the strong DSLS2 premise.

# 4. The weaker Fejér threshold: full DSLS2 is often unnecessary

Let
\[
W=\sum_p w_p,
\qquad
V=\sum_p w_p^2,
\qquad
M_{\rm eff}:=\frac{W^2}{V}.
\tag{39}
\]
For equal weights, \(M_{\rm eff}=M\). Combining (25) and (36),
\[
\frac{W(I)}{W}
\ll
\frac1{\sqrt K}
+\frac1{\sqrt{M_{\rm eff}}}
+\sqrt{\frac{P^2}{qK M_{\rm eff}}}.
\tag{40}
\]
Therefore:

> **Unconditional Fejér nonconcentration criterion.**  The Apéry common-numerator large sieve alone gives \(W(I)=o(W)\) uniformly on arcs \(|I|\le1/(2K)\) provided
> \[
> K\to\infty,
> \qquad M_{\rm eff}\to\infty,
> \qquad \frac{qK M_{\rm eff}}{P^2}\to\infty.
> \tag{41}
> \]

For unit weights this is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{qKM}{P^2}\to\infty.
}
\tag{42}
\]
So if Q2730 only uses DSLS2 to rule out an arc carrying a positive proportion of the defining primes, the premise can be weakened substantially: the needed cutoff is
\[
K\gg \frac{P^2}{qM}\,\omega,
\qquad \omega\to\infty,
\tag{43}
\]
not \(K\gg P^2/q\).

## 4.1 Weighted absolute target from the height of \(b_q\)

For the actual fixed-\(q\) arithmetic, logarithmic weights are more natural. Let
\[
w_p=\log p.
\]
Since every \(p\in\mathcal D_q(P)\) divides the fixed integer \(b_q\),
\[
W=\sum_{p\in\mathcal D_q(P)}\log p
\le \log\operatorname{rad}(b_q)
\le \log b_q.
\tag{44}
\]
The standard Apéry asymptotic gives
\[
\log b_q=O(q),
\tag{45}
\]
so \(W\ll q\). Also \(\log p\asymp\log P\) on the dyadic block, hence
\[
V=\sum(\log p)^2
\ll (\log P)W
\ll q\log P.
\tag{46}
\]
Insert this in (25)+(36):
\[
\begin{aligned}
W(I)
&\ll \frac{W}{\sqrt K}
 +\sqrt{V}
 +\sqrt{\frac{P^2V}{qK}}\\
&\ll
\frac{q}{\sqrt K}
+\sqrt{q\log P}
+P\sqrt{\frac{\log P}{K}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{47}
\]
Thus if the fixed-\(q\) ledger merely needs
\[
W(I)=o(q),
\tag{48}
\]
then, in any range with \(\log P=o(q)\), it is enough that
\[
\boxed{
K\gg \frac{P^2\log P}{q^2}\,\omega(q),
\qquad \omega(q)\to\infty.
}
\tag{49}
\]
This can be much weaker than strong DSLS2's \(K\gg P^2/q\).

Equivalently, for unweighted counts the elementary height bound
\[
M\log P\le \log b_q\ll q
\tag{50}
\]
gives
\[
M\ll q/\log P,
\tag{51}
\]
and the same substitution in the Fejér/large-sieve inequality gives
\[
\#\{p:\theta_p\in I\}
\ll
\frac{q}{\sqrt K\log P}
+\sqrt{\frac q{\log P}}
+\frac{P}{\sqrt{K\log P}}.
\tag{52}
\]
To make this \(o(q/\log P)\), condition (49) again appears.

This is the most useful “weaker exponent” conclusion I can derive without seeing Q2730's exact downstream normalization.

# 5. Duality does not remove the wall; it identifies it exactly

The large sieve is already a duality theorem. Writing out the Gram matrix shows what any improvement must control.

For simplicity take real nonnegative \(w_p\). From (18),
\[
L_K
=\sum_{p,\ell}w_pw_\ell
\left(\mathcal F_K(\theta_p-\theta_\ell)-1\right).
\tag{53}
\]
Using (32),
\[
\theta_p-\theta_\ell
=c_q\frac{p-\ell}{p\ell}.
\tag{54}
\]
The Fejér core is large when
\[
\left\|c_q\frac{p-\ell}{p\ell}\right\|
\lesssim \frac1K.
\tag{55}
\]
On \(p,\ell\asymp P\) this is exactly
\[
\boxed{
|p-\ell|\lesssim \frac{P^2}{qK}.
}
\tag{56}
\]
Thus the missing low-frequency gain is a theorem about how the **prime divisors of one fixed Apéry number \(b_q\)** are distributed in short intervals inside \((P,2P]\).

This is sharper than saying “cross-prime cancellation is needed.” It identifies the precise correlation scale.

Generic prime-distribution theorems do not solve (56), because \(\mathcal D_q(P)\) is a thin deterministic subset selected by \(p\mid b_q\). The height bound controls only its total cardinality/log mass, not its local spacing.

# 6. Direct dispersion identity: exact circularity

Use the unsmoothed positive-frequency sum to make the obstruction transparent:
\[
\Sigma_K
:=\sum_{1\le k\le K}|S(k)|^2.
\tag{57}
\]
Expanding,
\[
\Sigma_K
=K\sum_p w_p^2
+\sum_{p\ne\ell}w_pw_\ell
\sum_{k=1}^{K}e\!\left(kc_q\left(\frac1\ell-\frac1p\right)\right).
\tag{58}
\]
The inner geometric progression satisfies
\[
\left|\sum_{k=1}^{K}e(kx)\right|
\ll \min(K,\|x\|^{-1}).
\tag{59}
\]
Hence, away from wrap,
\[
\Sigma_K
\ll KV
+\sum_{p\ne\ell}w_pw_\ell
\min\!\left(K,\frac{P^2}{q|p-\ell|}\right).
\tag{60}
\]

If one now takes absolute values and only uses the ordering of the defining primes, there is no gain. For unit weights, if
\[
p_1<\cdots<p_M,
\]
then \(|p_i-p_j|\ge2|i-j|\), so
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}\frac1{|p_i-p_j|}
\ll M\log M.
\tag{61}
\]
This yields only
\[
\Sigma_K
\ll KM+\frac{P^2}{q}M\log M,
\tag{62}
\]
which is the large-sieve barrier with an extra logarithm.

To improve (62) in the bad range, one must show either:

- cancellation in the signed geometric sums in (58), or
- fewer defining-prime pairs than the worst-case spacing model at the critical scale (56).

But a proposed proof that says “the defining primes should not have many pairs with \(|p-\ell|\le P^2/(qK)\)” has simply assumed the dual content of DSLS2. That is the circularity.

More formally, a positive majorant for the Fejér kernel converts the off-diagonal into a short-gap pair count. Conversely, a short-gap pair bound at every dyadic gap scale feeds back into (53) or (60) and proves DSLS2. They are two coordinate systems for the same missing cross-prime statement.

# 7. Why the centered recurrence / quasiperiod does not by itself close the off-diagonal

The centered recurrence is powerful inside one characteristic. Modulo a single defining prime \(p\), it gives:

- the reflection \(q\leftrightarrow p-1-q\);
- the centered \(t\leftrightarrow-t\) symmetry;
- transfer-matrix identities and Casoratian nondegeneracy;
- gap/return constraints by the \(N_h\);
- via Lucas, block/quasiperiod relations across indices differing by multiples of \(p\).

All of these can identify or constrain the **zero geometry modulo one prime**.

DSLS2, however, contains terms with two different characteristics:
\[
p\ne\ell,\qquad p\mid b_q,\qquad \ell\mid b_q,
\]
and the Fourier phase is
\[
e\!\left(kc_q\left(\frac1\ell-\frac1p\right)\right).
\tag{63}
\]
There is no common field in which the \(p\)-transfer and the \(\ell\)-transfer interact. The fact that both primes divide \(b_q\) gives the integer divisibility
\[
p\ell\mid b_q
\tag{64}
\]
for distinct primes, but height only turns this into
\[
\sum_{p\mid b_q}\log p\le\log b_q=O(q).
\tag{65}
\]
It does not constrain \(|p-\ell|\).

Attempting to use gap polynomials or Casoratians simultaneously at \(p\) and \(\ell\) has the same issue. They give two local congruences, one modulo \(p\) and one modulo \(\ell\). To get a saving in (56), one needs a new theorem coupling those congruences across characteristics. This is exactly the type of cross-prime input that the project's previous common-unit / pair-mass / atom-tail audits have repeatedly isolated as missing.

So the recurrence contributes the common numerator \(c_q\)—which yields the factor-\(q\) improvement in (36)—but I do not see a second recurrence identity that removes the remaining \(P^2/(qK)\) term.

# 8. The per-prime Fourier nonconcentration theorem is the wrong \(L^2\)

The current atom-tail Fourier lemma controls, for one prime \(p\),
\[
F_p(a)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal Z_p}e_p(ar)
\]
and gives a short-frequency estimate of the shape
\[
\sum_{1\le|a|\le A}|F_p(a)|^2
\ll
A Z(p)+\frac{p^2}{A}
\quad
\text{(up to the recorded logarithmic/constant variant).}
\tag{66}
\]
This is an **intra-\(p\)** statement: it says the entire zero set of one recurrence modulo \(p\) does not concentrate too strongly in ordinary frequencies.

DSLS2 is different. It selects one distinguished mirror slope per defining prime and then sums **across \(p\)**:
\[
\sum_{p\mid b_q}w_p e(-kc_q/p).
\tag{67}
\]
Summing (66) over \(p\) cannot create the cross-prime cancellation in (67), because (66) is nonnegative prime by prime. The \(p^2/A\) remainder also survives separately for each modulus. Thus the existing Fourier nonconcentration theorem cannot prove DSLS2 by Cauchy/Parseval without reintroducing precisely the unwanted \(P^2/q\)-type cost.

This is a level mismatch, not just a bad constant.

# 9. Trace-function route: why standard Deligne technology does not apply directly

There are two different trace-function problems one could imagine.

## 9.1 Fix \(p\), vary an index/residue

This is the setting where the project already has genuine algebraic geometry: rational transfer functions, gap polynomials, fiber products, and fixed-\(h\) Weil/Chebotarev estimates. One can often realize an additive twist in a residue variable over \(\mathbb F_p\) and obtain square-root cancellation with conductor depending on the degree/lag.

That is not DSLS2.

## 9.2 Fix \(q\), vary the defining prime \(p\mid b_q\)

The desired sum is essentially
\[
\sum_{p\sim P}
1_{p\mid b_q}\,w_p\,e(-kc_q/p).
\tag{68}
\]
Here:

- the modulus of the additive character varies with \(p\);
- the selector \(1_{p\mid b_q}\) asks whether \(p\) divides a fixed integer;
- there is no single finite field \(\mathbb F_P\) in which \(p\) is a variable;
- there is no banked bounded-conductor sheaf on the prime parameter whose trace is (68).

One could dream of an adelic/compatible-system reformulation in which the condition \(p\mid b_q\) is a Frobenius condition and the reciprocal phase is incorporated into a global trace. But no such object is present in the project, and for a fixed coefficient \(b_q\), “\(p\mid b_q\)” is fundamentally a prime-divisor condition, not a standard Chebotarev condition of bounded conductor as \(q\) grows.

Applying Deligne/Bombieri separately for each \(p\) loses the cross-\(p\) phase and returns to a sum of absolute values. Therefore the trace-function route does not currently improve (36).

# 10. Varying-modulus large sieve: this is the one route that does give a theorem

The generic large sieve in the historical Oracle A calculation sees fractions \(a/p\) and has the familiar spacing \(1/P^2\). That is why its clean regime ends near \(P\sim\sqrt K\).

For the defining-prime mirror slopes, \(a\) is not generic: it is the common integer \(c_q=2q+1\). Consequently the spacing is \(q/P^2\), and the clean regime extends to
\[
P\lesssim\sqrt{qK}.
\tag{69}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
K\gtrsim P^2/q.
\]
This is the unconditional extension that is actually available.

There is no reason to hide this behind an unproved DSLS2 label: it should be stated as a direct lemma. If Q2730's required low-frequency range lies wholly in (69), then the DSLS2 premise is unnecessary there.

If its range extends beyond (69), the residual factor is exactly (38), and one must compare that residual with the weaker Fejér requirement (29)/(31)/(49) before declaring a new conjectural input necessary.

# 11. Exact exponent ledger for possible downstream requirements

Because Q2730 itself is not visible, here is the complete conversion table in formula form.

Let unit weights be used and \(M=|\mathcal D_q(P)|\).

Suppose the desired arc bound is
\[
R_K:=\max_{|I|\le1/(2K)}\#\{p:\theta_p\in I\}
\ll M^{1-\delta+o(1)}.
\tag{70}
\]
Fejér requires
\[
K\ge M^{2\delta-o(1)}
\tag{71}
\]
for the zero mode, and it suffices to have
\[
L_K\ll K M^{2-2\delta+o(1)}.
\tag{72}
\]

The unconditional large sieve gives
\[
L_K\ll \left(K+\frac{P^2}{q}\right)M.
\tag{73}
\]
Therefore (70) follows from spacing alone whenever
\[
\boxed{
K\ge M^{2\delta-o(1)},
\qquad
\frac{P^2}{q}
\le K M^{1-2\delta+o(1)}.
}
\tag{74}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
qK M^{1-2\delta}\ge P^{2-o(1)}.
}
\tag{75}
\]

Special cases:

- \(\delta=0^+\) (only \(R_K=o(M)\)): \(qKM/P^2\to\infty\).
- \(\delta=1/2\) (square-root arc count): one is back at the strong spacing threshold \(qK\gtrsim P^2\).
- intermediate \(0<\delta<1/2\): the required DSLS2 exponent interpolates exactly by (75).

For weighted logarithmic mass, replace \(M\) by the effective cardinality \(M_{\rm eff}=W^2/V\), or use the absolute height-budget formula (47).

# 12. What would genuinely prove the missing range?

A theorem strictly stronger than spacing would need one of the following.

### A. Defining-prime short-gap theorem

For
\[
\Delta=P^2/(qK),
\]
show uniformly that
\[
\sum_{x\asymp P}
\binom{
 \#\{p\mid b_q:x<p\le x+\Delta\}
}{2}
\]
is smaller than the worst-case spacing prediction by the factor required in (75).

This is essentially DSLS2 in physical space.

### B. Signed reciprocal dispersion

Prove cancellation in
\[
\sum_{\substack{p\ne\ell\\p,\ell\mid b_q}}
 w_pw_\ell
 D_K\!\left(c_q\left(\frac1p-\frac1\ell\right)\right),
\tag{76}
\]
where \(D_K\) is the Dirichlet kernel, beyond what follows from absolute spacing.

This is DSLS2 in dual pair form.

### C. A genuinely adelic Apéry theorem

Construct a bounded-complexity object varying with the prime that simultaneously encodes \(p\mid b_q\) and the reciprocal phase \(c_q/p\), and prove a large-sieve/trace theorem for that family.

Nothing in the currently visible recurrence, gap-polynomial, Cartier, or fixed-\(h\) Chebotarev package supplies such an object.

Absent A/B/C, I do not see an unconditional path below the thresholds above.

# 13. Circularity map

The circularity can be stated precisely enough to use as a proof-audit checklist.

1. **Fejér side:** DSLS2 controls low modes \(S(k)\); Fejér turns that into small slope arcs.
2. **Dual side:** expanding those same modes produces the kernel \(\mathcal F_K(c_q(1/p-1/\ell))\).
3. **Physical side:** that kernel is supported at scale \(|p-\ell|\lesssim P^2/(qK)\).
4. Therefore a “proof” of DSLS2 that assumes defining primes have the expected number of neighbours in those intervals has used the desired Fejér conclusion in dual form.
5. A proof using only the total height \(\log b_q=O(q)\) controls \(M\), not these neighbour counts.
6. A proof using the recurrence separately modulo \(p\) and modulo \(\ell\) supplies no joint distribution theorem.
7. A proof using the current one-prime Fourier \(L^2\) lemma controls frequencies *inside* each zero set and has no cross-prime cancellation.
8. A proof invoking “trace functions” must exhibit a single varying-prime trace family; otherwise applying Weil separately is again only a one-prime absolute bound.

That is the exact place where the argument becomes circular.

# 14. Final theorem package

The following is the clean unconditional replacement I would bank in place of an unnecessarily strong DSLS2 premise.

> **Theorem (defining-prime reciprocal large sieve).**  Fix \(q\ge1\) and let \(c_q=2q+1\). Let \(\mathcal D_q(P)\) be any set of primes \(P<p\le2P\) such that \(p\mid b_q\) and \(q\le(p-1)/2\). Put
> \[
> \theta_{p,q}=1-c_q/p.
> \]
> Then for arbitrary complex weights \(w_p\) and \(K\ge2\),
> \[
> \sum_{1\le|k|<K}
> \left(1-\frac{|k|}{K}\right)
> \left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal D_q(P)}w_p e(k\theta_{p,q})\right|^2
> \ll
> \left(K+\frac{P^2}{q}\right)
> \sum_{p\in\mathcal D_q(P)}|w_p|^2.
> \]
> The implied constant is absolute.

> **Corollary (Fejér arc bound).**  If \(w_p\ge0\), \(W=\sum w_p\), \(V=\sum w_p^2\), and \(I\subset\mathbb T\) has \(|I|\le1/(2K)\), then
> \[
> W(I)
> \ll
> \frac{W}{\sqrt K}
> +\sqrt V
> +\frac{P}{\sqrt{qK}}\sqrt V.
> \tag{77}
> \]

> **Corollary (weak threshold).**  With effective cardinality \(M_{\rm eff}=W^2/V\), one has \(W(I)=o(W)\) if
> \[
> K\to\infty,
> \quad M_{\rm eff}\to\infty,
> \quad qKM_{\rm eff}/P^2\to\infty.
> \]

> **Corollary (log-weight height threshold).**  For \(w_p=\log p\), the Apéry height \(\log b_q=O(q)\) gives
> \[
> W(I)
> \ll
> qK^{-1/2}+\sqrt{q\log P}+P\sqrt{\log P/K}.
> \]
> Hence, if \(\log P=o(q)\), the condition
> \[
> K\gg (P^2\log P/q^2)\,\omega(q),\qquad \omega(q)\to\infty,
> \]
> suffices for \(W(I)=o(q)\).

These statements are unconditional consequences of the visible reflection theorem, the growth bound for \(b_q\), Fejér, and the classical large sieve. No finite scan enters.

The remaining range is not a matter of improving constants. It requires a new cross-characteristic theorem on the short-gap distribution or signed reciprocal correlation of the defining primes of \(b_q\).
