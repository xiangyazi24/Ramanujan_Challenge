# 7. Direct comparison with Q4772 TDW

Q4772 expands Q4704's joint collision after the legal outer Cauchy.  Two source rows \(x_i\), with \(P_i=p_iq_i\), share the same moving prime \(\ell\), outer prime \(p'\), and outer zero \(r'\in\mathcal Z_{p'}\).  Their signed lifts are

\[
 \delta_i=t_i(p'\ell)-r'P_i+n_iP_ip',
 \qquad i=1,2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Every pair satisfies

\[
 P_1\delta_2\equiv P_2\delta_1\pmod{p'},
\tag{7.2}

and, with \(h=n_2-n_1\),

\[
 P_1\delta_2-P_2\delta_1
 =p'\left\{\ell(P_1t_2-P_2t_1)+P_1P_2h\right\}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The already-proved pieces are

\[
 \mathcal C_{x_1=x_2}\ll N^{79/100+o(1)},
\tag{7.4}
\]

and

\[
 \mathcal C_{x_1\ne x_2,\,\delta_1=\delta_2}
 \ll N^{139/100+o(1)}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

TDW concerns the remaining \(\delta_1\ne\delta_2\) positive incidence

\[
 \mathfrak T_{2,\ne},
\tag{7.6}
\]

with the two literal tents, both sets of zero tests, common \(\ell,p',r'\), both circular branches and all masks.  Its sufficient form is

\[
 \mathfrak T_{2,\ne}
 \ll N^{44/25-\varepsilon+o(1)},
\tag{TDW$_\varepsilon$}
\]

which gives every \(\eta<\varepsilon/2\).

## 7.1 Why WSR2E does not imply TDW

WSR2E fixes one first-row outer block and one determinant, then averages the square of the number of possible moving fourth primes against that determinant's moving-prime energy.  TDW fixes the moving prime and outer zero, then pairs two different first rows and two different determinants.

To force TDW into WSR2E one would have to define, for fixed \(\ell,p',r'\), a determinant coefficient

\[
 A_{\ell,p',r'}(\delta)
 =\sum_{x,n:\delta_{x;\ell,p',r'}(n)=\delta}
 b_x\tau_x(n)m_x(n)
 \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}(r'-np').
\tag{7.7}
\]

But (7.7) already contains the moving-\(\ell\) zero selector and is indexed by the fixed moving prime.  Multiplying it by a reciprocal fibre degree counts that selector again.  Removing the selector makes the coefficient depend on the second determinant and first row; the resulting squared energy is precisely the TDW mixed term.  Thus the conversion is circular.

## 7.2 Why TDW does not imply WSR2E

TDW omits the same-determinant part and requires two first rows sharing one moving prime and one outer zero.  WSR2E can be large because one determinant in one fixed outer block has many admissible moving primes, even if no second first row reuses them in a related determinant.  Q4772's TDW statement has no term that bounds this one-row Palm degree.

## 7.3 Cauchy order

Applying Cauchy to one side of TDW produces

\[
 \left(\sum_{\delta,\ell}|u_\ell|I_1(\delta,\ell)\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{\delta,\ell}|u_\ell|I_1(\delta,\ell)
       |F_2(\delta,\ell)|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

The first factor is controlled by the one-determinant SRG theorem.  The second is a two-determinant selected continuation energy, not WSR2E.  Reversing the Cauchy order either pays the first-row \(L^1\) mass or changes Q4704's optimal outer normalization \(U_NB_N\) to the larger \(B_N^2\).

Therefore

```text
TDW_epsilon => WSR2E_beta:    not proved and structurally false as a formal implication
relationship:                 INCOMPARABLE ALTERNATIVE SUFFICIENT HYPOTHESES
```

This is an index-level conclusion from the literal formulas, not an arbitrary formal packet.

```latex
\begin{proposition}[Prime-weighted reciprocal-fibre squares]
\label{prop:ad-reciprocal-fibre-weighted-squares}
Fix pairwise distinct shell primes $p,q,p'\asymp N$, a sign
$\sigma\in\{\pm1\}$, and an interval $J$ of length
$N^{359/100+o(1)}$.  Put
\[
 I(\delta,\ell)
 =\mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_p}
   \!\left(\sigma\delta\overline{p'\ell}\pmod p\right)
  \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}
   \!\left(-\sigma\delta\overline{pq}\pmod\ell\right),
\]
with the usual distinct-prime restriction, and for
$\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}$ define
\[
 R_\alpha(\delta)
 =\sum_{\ell\asymp N}|u_\ell|^\alpha I(\delta,\ell).
\]
Then, uniformly after arbitrary additional deletion masks,
\[
 \sum_{\delta\in J}R_\alpha(\delta)^2
 \ll |J|N^{13/15-2\alpha+o(1)}.
\tag{WRF2.1}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \sum_{\delta\in J}R_1(\delta)^2
 \ll |J|N^{-17/15+o(1)},
 \qquad
 \sum_{\delta\in J}R_2(\delta)^2
 \ll |J|N^{-47/15+o(1)}.
\tag{WRF2.2}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expand the square into ordered pairs $(\ell_1,\ell_2)$.  If
$\ell_1\ne\ell_2$, the two fixed-$p$ tests occupy at most $Z(p)$
classes modulo $p$, while the two moving tests occupy
$Z(\ell_i)$ classes modulo $\ell_i$.  CRT gives at most
$Z(p)Z(\ell_1)Z(\ell_2)$ classes modulo $p\ell_1\ell_2$, and every
class has at most $|J|/(p\ell_1\ell_2)+1$ representatives in $J$.
The equal-moving-prime diagonal is counted similarly.  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\delta\in J}R_\alpha(\delta)^2
 \ll{}&
 |J|\frac{Z(p)}p
 \left(\sum_\ell\frac{|u_\ell|^\alpha Z(\ell)}\ell\right)^2
 +Z(p)\left(\sum_\ell|u_\ell|^\alpha Z(\ell)\right)^2\\
 &+|J|\frac{Z(p)}p
 \sum_\ell\frac{|u_\ell|^{2\alpha}Z(\ell)}\ell
 +Z(p)\sum_\ell|u_\ell|^{2\alpha}Z(\ell).
\end{aligned}
\]
Using
\[
 Z(p)\ll N^{2/3+o(1)},\qquad
 \sum_\ell |u_\ell|^\alpha Z(\ell)
 \ll N^{8/5-\alpha+o(1)},
\]
and the same estimate with an additional factor $\ell^{-1}$ gives
respectively
\[
 |J|N^{13/15-2\alpha+o(1)},\quad
 N^{58/15-2\alpha+o(1)},\quad
 |J|N^{4/15-2\alpha+o(1)},\quad
 N^{34/15-2\alpha+o(1)}.
\]
Since $|J|=N^{359/100+o(1)}$, the first term dominates.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Three-test determinant square]
\label{prop:ad-three-test-determinant-square}
Under the same hypotheses, define
\[
 S_\alpha(\delta)
 =\sum_{\ell\asymp N}|u_\ell|^\alpha I(\delta,\ell)
  \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_q}
   \!\left(\sigma\delta\overline{p'\ell}\pmod q\right).
\]
Then, for $\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}$,
\[
 \sum_{\delta\in J}S_\alpha(\delta)^2
 \ll |J|N^{283/300-2\alpha+o(1)}.
\tag{3TS2.1}
\]
The estimate remains valid after imposing the actual $p'$ zero test
and every current deletion mask.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For distinct moving primes, the fixed tests occupy at most
$Z(p)Z(q)$ classes modulo $pq$, and the moving tests contribute
$Z(\ell_1)Z(\ell_2)$ classes modulo $\ell_1\ell_2$.  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\delta\in J}S_\alpha(\delta)^2
 \ll{}&
 |J|\frac{Z(p)Z(q)}{pq}
 \left(\sum_\ell\frac{|u_\ell|^\alpha Z(\ell)}\ell\right)^2\\
 &+Z(p)Z(q)
 \left(\sum_\ell|u_\ell|^\alpha Z(\ell)\right)^2
 +\text{the equal-prime diagonal}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The off-diagonal main and endpoint terms are
\[
 |J|N^{8/15-2\alpha+o(1)},
 \qquad
 N^{68/15-2\alpha+o(1)}.
\]
The endpoint dominates, and
\[
 \frac{68}{15}-\frac{359}{100}=\frac{283}{300}.
\]
The diagonal is smaller.  Further selectors only delete terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Absolute high-fibre tent energy]
\label{lem:ad-high-fibre-tent-energy}
Retain the hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ad-reciprocal-fibre-weighted-squares}, and let

\]
\end{proof}
```
