# Q1936: exact Q1918 cofactor continuation

I anchored this to the current `main` state at commit `efd768c30c8d7a5ee2dc31ff91b2324d8d77fe18` and to the exact centered-operator normalization used in Q1933/Q1934.  The conclusion is negative but quite sharp:

> There is an exact bordered-determinant recurrence for `det M_C/K_*`, but after one Schur step its normalized increment is exactly `-2(3-theta)/k_j`.  Thus the recurrence accumulates the same reciprocal trace as `S`.  The centered equation `H C=-(theta/2) delta_0` and the raw lift do not supply a second boundary evaluation of that accumulator.  Modulo `P=t^p-t`, the raw-lift term involving `c_{p-3}` disappears; before reduction, the desired reciprocal coefficient is merely transferred into the unknown `P`-quotient.

So there is no new `S`-free evaluation from the three natural routes requested here (bordered/continuant, displacement, raw coefficient descent).  Any such evaluation would be a genuinely new identity: equivalently, an independent formula for the top coefficient of the reciprocal of `C`, or a telescoper for `sum 1/k_t`.

---

## 1. Normalizations

Write

\[
a:=3-\theta.
\]

The even cyclic kernel has values

\[
k_0=-1,\qquad k_{\pm1}=3,
\qquad k_{\pm t}=k_t\quad(2\le t\le m),
\]

and the low even correction is

\[
C=k-\theta d,
\qquad d=\delta_1+\delta_{-1}.
\]

Thus

\[
C(0)=-1,\qquad C(\pm1)=a,
\qquad C(\pm t)=k_t\quad(2\le t\le m).
\]

Because `C` has degree at most `p-3`, it has mean zero, hence

\[
0=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}C(x)
=-1+2a+2\sum_{t=2}^m k_t.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\theta=\frac52+\sum_{t=2}^m k_t.}
\]

The Q1918 reciprocal trace is

\[
\boxed{
S=\frac13-2\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t}.
}
\]

Let

\[
K_*:=\prod_{t=2}^m k_t,
\]

and assume the all-unit case `K_* != 0`, as in the Q1918 cofactor calculation.

Q1918 gives

\[
\frac{\det M_C}{K_*}
=\frac{\theta-9}{3}+(3-\theta)S.
\]

Substituting the definition of `S` already puts this into the form needed for the recurrence:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\det M_C}{K_*}
&=\frac{\theta-9}{3}
+a\left(\frac13-2\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t}\right)\\
&=-2-2a\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\det M_C}{K_*}
=-2\left(1+a\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t}\right).
}
\tag{1}
\]

This form is more informative than the original affine expression in `S`.

A useful trivial special case is

\[
a=0\quad(\theta=3).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\det M_C/K_*=-2,}
\]

so the cofactor is automatically nonzero.  All of the reciprocal discussion below is only needed for `a != 0`.

## 2. Exact rank-one / bordered determinant normal form

Let

\[
D_m:=\operatorname{diag}(k_2,\ldots,k_m),
\qquad \mathbf 1=(1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

The matrix determinant lemma gives

\[
\det(D_m+a\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T})
=K_*\left(1+a\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t}\right).
\]

Therefore Q1918's cofactor has the exact determinant representation

\[
\boxed{
\det M_C
=-2\det(D_m+a\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}).
}
\tag{2}
\]

No approximation or sample-prime observation is being used here: (2) is algebraically identical to the Q1918 closed formula.

Equivalently one can use the border

\[
B_m=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-a\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\\
\mathbf1&D_m
\end{pmatrix},
\]

for which

\[
\det B_m
=K_*\left(1+a\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t}\right),
\]

so

\[
\det M_C=-2\det B_m.
\]

This is the cleanest bordered form of the Q1918 minor.

## 3. The exact bordered recurrence, and where `S` reappears

For `j>=2`, define

\[
K_j:=\prod_{t=2}^j k_t,
\]

and

\[
G_j:=-2K_j\left(1+a\sum_{t=2}^j\frac1{k_t}\right).
\]

Use the empty initial values

\[
K_1=1,\qquad G_1=-2.
\]

Appending the `j`th diagonal entry gives the exact first-order pair

\[
\boxed{
K_j=k_jK_{j-1},
}
\tag{3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
G_j=k_jG_{j-1}-2aK_{j-1}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
k_jG_{j-1}-2aK_{j-1}
&=-2k_jK_{j-1}
 \left(1+a\sum_{t=2}^{j-1}\frac1{k_t}\right)
 -2aK_{j-1}\\
&=-2K_j\left(1+a\sum_{t=2}^j\frac1{k_t}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

The two equations can be written as a `2 x 2` transfer:

\[
\boxed{
\binom{G_j}{K_j}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
k_j&-2a\\0&k_j\end{pmatrix}
\binom{G_{j-1}}{K_{j-1}}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

After dividing by `K_j`, put

\[
g_j:=G_j/K_j.
\]

Then (4) becomes

\[
\boxed{
g_j=g_{j-1}-\frac{2a}{k_j},
\qquad g_1=-2.}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus

\[
g_m=-2-2a\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t}
=\frac{\det M_C}{K_*}.
\]

This is already the requested exact recurrence.  It also proves the main obstruction:

> The first normalized Schur step introduces `1/k_j`; the terminal bordered determinant is the accumulated reciprocal trace.  There is no intermediate continuant scalar that removes it.

In nilpotent form, each normalized transfer is

\[
\frac1{k_j}
\begin{pmatrix}k_j&-2a\\0&k_j\end{pmatrix}
=I-\frac{2a}{k_j}N,
\qquad
N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad N^2=0.
\]

Therefore the whole product is exactly

\[
\prod_{j=2}^m
\left(I-\frac{2a}{k_j}N\right)
=I-2a\left(\sum_{j=2}^m\frac1{k_j}\right)N.
\]

So the reciprocal sum is not an artifact of one particular expansion: it is the unique nilpotent coefficient of the bordered transfer product.

## 4. Injecting the centered `H` recurrence does not close (6)

For the centered operator, write

\[
A_t=(t+\tfrac12)^3(t+2),
\quad
B_t=(34t^2+\tfrac32)(t^2-1),
\quad
D_t=(t-\tfrac12)^3(t-2),
\]

so

\[
(Hf)(t)=A_tf(t+1)-B_tf(t)+D_tf(t-1).
\]

Away from `t=0`, the corrected kernel satisfies `HC=0`.  For `2<=t<m`, since `C(t)=k_t`, this gives the exact tail recurrence

\[
\boxed{
(2t+1)^3(t+2)k_{t+1}
-4(68t^2+3)(t^2-1)k_t
+(2t-1)^3(t-2)k_{t-1}=0.
}
\tag{7}
\]

There are two important seam zeros:

* at `t=1`, the diagonal coefficient contains `t^2-1`, so `C(1)=a` is absent;
* at `t=2`, the backward coefficient contains `t-2`, so `C(1)=a` is again absent.

Thus `a=3-theta` is a genuine seam variable: the interior recurrence generating `k_2,...,k_m` never propagates it.

Now put

\[
r_t:=1/k_t.
\]

Taking reciprocals of (7) does **not** give a linear continuant.  It gives the quadratic relation

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&(2t+1)^3(t+2)r_tr_{t-1}\\
&\quad-4(68t^2+3)(t^2-1)r_{t+1}r_{t-1}\\
&\quad+(2t-1)^3(t-2)r_{t+1}r_t=0.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{8}
\]

But the bordered recurrence is precisely

\[
g_j-g_{j-1}=-2a r_j.
\]

Substituting `r_j=-(g_j-g_{j-1})/(2a)` (when `a != 0`) into (8) produces a recurrence only for the **increments** of `g`:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&(2t+1)^3(t+2)\,\Delta g_t\Delta g_{t-1}\\
&\quad-4(68t^2+3)(t^2-1)\,\Delta g_{t+1}\Delta g_{t-1}\\
&\quad+(2t-1)^3(t-2)\,\Delta g_{t+1}\Delta g_t=0,
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{9}
\]

where `Delta g_t=g_t-g_{t-1}`.

Equation (9) is exactly the original recurrence rewritten in reciprocal variables.  It does not determine the terminal accumulated value by an endpoint continuant.  The additive accumulation

\[
g_m=-2+\sum_{j=2}^m\Delta g_j
\]

is still the Q1918 sum.

So the direct continuant route fails in the strongest possible elementary sense: after normalization, its state variable is `1/k_j` itself.

## 5. Exact displacement identity: `HC` being rank one does **not** make multiplication by `C` low-displacement-rank

Let `T_+` be cyclic translation `f(t) -> f(t+1)` on functions on `F_p`, and let `M_f` denote multiplication by a function `f`.  Then

\[
H=M_A T_+-M_B+M_D T_+^{-1}.
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
[H,M_C]
=M_{A(T_+C-C)}T_+
 +M_{D(T_+^{-1}C-C)}T_+^{-1}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

This is the correct displacement identity.

The equation

\[
HC=-\frac\theta2\delta_0
\]

controls only the single linear combination

\[
A(T_+C)-BC+D(T_+^{-1}C).
\]

It does **not** say that either multiplier

\[
A(T_+C-C),\qquad D(T_+^{-1}C-C)
\]

is supported at the seam.  Generically those are nonzero across the entire tail.  Thus the rank-one source `HC` does not imply a rank-one displacement for `M_C`.

This rules out the most obvious displacement-rank shortcut.  The raw lift cannot repair it inside the cyclic algebra, because its extra term is a multiple of `P=t^p-t` and hence represents zero as a function.

## 6. The cofactor ratio is the top reciprocal coefficient

Assume now `a != 0`, so `C` is a unit on the full even function set whenever all `k_t` are units.  Let `R` be the unique reduced even polynomial of degree at most `p-1` representing the pointwise reciprocal

\[
R(x)=1/C(x).
\]

Write

\[
R(t)=r_0+r_2t^2+\cdots+r_{p-1}t^{p-1}.
\]

Since `C(0)=-1`,

\[
r_0=R(0)=-1.
\]

For any reduced polynomial `F` of degree at most `p-1`,

\[
[t^{p-1}]F=-\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}F(x).
\]

Therefore, with

\[
\rho:=[t^{p-1}]R,
\]

we have

\[
\rho
=-\left(-1+2\sum_{t=1}^m\frac1{C(t)}\right)
=1-2\sum_{t=1}^m\frac1{C(t)}.
\]

Since

\[
\sum_{t=1}^m\frac1{C(t)}
=\frac1a+\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t},
\]

(1) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\det M_C}{K_*}=a(\rho-1).
}
\tag{11}
\]

This is a completely `S`-free formula, but it is only a reparameterization: `rho` is exactly the same reciprocal-trace scalar.

Indeed

\[
\rho
=\frac23+S-\frac2a,
\]

and substituting this into (11) recovers Q1918.

Thus the genuinely missing independent evaluation can be stated cleanly as:

> compute the top coefficient `rho=[t^{p-1}] C^{-1}` from the recurrence/source data without first computing the reciprocal trace.

The given `H` identities do not do this.

## 7. The raw lift transfers `rho` into the unknown `P`-quotient

Now use the exact raw polynomial identity supplied in the question:

\[
\boxed{
\widehat H C
=-\frac\theta2(1-t^{p-1})
-32c_{p-3}\,t(t^p-t).
}
\tag{12}
\]

Put

\[
P=t^p-t,
\qquad
\delta(t)=1-t^{p-1}.
\]

For the reciprocal `R` above, `R(0)=-1`.  There is an exact polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{
R\delta
=-\delta-P\frac{R+1}{t}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Proof: `R+1` has zero constant term, and for every positive monomial

\[
t^j(1-t^{p-1})
=t^j-t^{j+p-1}
=-t^{j-1}(t^p-t)
=-t^{j-1}P.
\]

Multiplying (12) by `R` and using (13) gives the exact raw identity

\[
\boxed{
R\,\widehat H C
=
\frac\theta2\delta
+P\left[
\frac\theta2\frac{R+1}{t}
-32c_{p-3}\,tR
\right].
}
\tag{14}
\]

Equation (14) is the decisive raw-lift calculation.

Modulo `P`, it collapses to

\[
RHC=\frac\theta2\delta,
\]

which is just

\[
R(0)\left(-\frac\theta2\delta_0\right)
=\frac\theta2\delta_0.
\]

The `c_{p-3}` term has vanished completely in the cyclic algebra.

Before reducing modulo `P`, however, the desired reciprocal polynomial appears in the quotient

\[
\boxed{
Q_R(t)
=
\frac\theta2\frac{R+1}{t}
-32c_{p-3}\,tR.
}
\tag{15}
\]

In particular, the top reciprocal coefficient `rho` is not determined; it is simply promoted to a coefficient of the lift quotient.  For example,

\[
[t^p]Q_R=-32c_{p-3}\rho.
\]

If `c_{p-3}=0`, the same phenomenon moves one step down: `rho` remains in the `(R+1)/t` part.  Thus coefficient descent does not create an extra equation for `rho`; it starts with `rho` as quotient data.

This is the exact reason the raw lift does not independently evaluate (11).

## 8. The extra raw scalar `c_{p-3}` is a direct moment, not a reciprocal moment

There is also a simple value-space interpretation of the raw coefficient.  Since `C` has degree at most `p-3`,

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}x^2C(x)=-c_{p-3}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
c_{p-3}
=-2\left(a+\sum_{t=2}^m t^2k_t\right).}
\tag{16}
\]

So the two explicit scalars in the raw source are

* `theta`, a direct zeroth moment of the kernel tail;
* `c_{p-3}`, a direct quadratic moment of the corrected kernel.

The cofactor ratio instead uses the inverse zeroth moment

\[
\sum_{t=2}^m1/k_t.
\]

Nothing in (12) converts the direct moments in (16) into this inverse moment.

## 9. Correct product identity under the hypothetical `S=0`

There is one further exact check that is useful because it prevents the false product rule that appeared in earlier variants of this attack.

If `S=0`, then the pointwise reciprocal of the cyclic kernel,

\[
r(x)=1/k(x),
\]

has mean zero and therefore has a low even representative of degree at most `p-3`.  Pointwise,

\[
Cr=(k-\theta d)r
=1-\frac\theta3d,
\]

because `r(±1)=1/3`.

One must **not** write `H(Cr)=rHC+CHr`.  The exact one-sided discrete Leibniz identity is

\[
\boxed{
H(Cr)-rHC
=A\,(T_+C)(T_+r-r)
+D\,(T_+^{-1}C)(T_+^{-1}r-r).
}
\tag{17}
\]

Now

\[
Hd=\frac12\delta_0,
\qquad
HC=-\frac\theta2\delta_0,
\qquad
r(0)=-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
H(Cr)=H1-\frac\theta6\delta_0,
\]

and

\[
rHC=\frac\theta2\delta_0.
\]

So (17) becomes

\[
\boxed{
A\,(T_+C)(T_+r-r)
+D\,(T_+^{-1}C)(T_+^{-1}r-r)
=H1-\frac{2\theta}{3}\delta_0.
}
\tag{18}
\]

Here

\[
\boxed{H1=-32t^4+40t^2+2.}
\]

Equation (18) is the correct recurrence-specific equation for the reciprocal.  It is an equation for **differences** of `r`, not for its mean.  Recovering the low-degree condition requires exactly the missing scalar

\[
\sum_x r(x)=0,
\]

which is equivalent to `S=0`.

Thus the properly corrected product calculation also fails to generate an independent test of `S`; it uses the reciprocal-mean condition as the integration constant.

## 10. Final conclusion

The requested three natural continuations all terminate at the same scalar:

### Bordered / continuant route

\[
\frac{\det M_C}{K_*}=g_m,
\qquad
g_j-g_{j-1}=-\frac{2(3-\theta)}{k_j}.
\]

So the recurrence literally accumulates the reciprocal trace.

### Displacement route

\[
[H,M_C]
=M_{A(T_+C-C)}T_+
+M_{D(T_+^{-1}C-C)}T_+^{-1},
\]

which is not low rank merely because `HC` is supported at zero.

### Raw-lift route

\[
R\widehat HC
=\frac\theta2(1-t^{p-1})
+P\left[
\frac\theta2\frac{R+1}{t}
-32c_{p-3}tR
\right].
\]

Modulo `P` the extra raw term vanishes; before reduction the target top reciprocal coefficient is part of the unknown quotient.

Accordingly, the sharp no-`S` restatement is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\det M_C}{K_*}
=(3-\theta)\left([t^{p-1}]C^{-1}-1\right)
}
\]

when `3-theta != 0`, while for `theta=3` the ratio is simply `-2`.

But `[t^{p-1}]C^{-1}` is exactly the same reciprocal-trace obstruction in coefficient language.  The given recurrence and raw lift do not independently determine it.

So Q1936 does **not** produce a second determinant evaluation to compare with Q1918.  The smallest genuinely new lemma would have to be one of the following equivalent kinds:

```text
(1) an endpoint formula for [t^(p-1)] C^{-1};
(2) a telescoper for sum_{t=2}^m 1/k_t;
(3) a Green/adjoint identity whose source is the constant reciprocal weight;
(4) a new structural relation coupling the raw P-quotient in (14) to endpoint data.
```

Without such an extra identity, the natural determinant recurrences reintroduce `S` immediately rather than eliminating it.
