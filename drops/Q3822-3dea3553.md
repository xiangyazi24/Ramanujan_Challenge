# 1. Exact lower-primary star dictionary

Fix a lower prime `p` and its canonical reflected primary zero

\[
z\in Z_p,
\qquad
r:=p-1-z\in Z_p.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Every extension from `(p,z)` has the same row

\[
\boxed{n=6p+r=7p-1-z.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Write one extension as

\[
q=p+d,
\qquad
n=6q+u,
\qquad
u=p-1-z-6d.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The true row conditions are

\[
p\mid b_r,
\qquad
q\mid b_u.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Reflection gives `p|b_z` iff `p|b_r`. Apéry--Lucas then gives

\[
b_n\equiv b_6b_r\pmod p,
\qquad
b_n\equiv b_6b_u\pmod q,
\]

and hence

\[
pq\mid b_n.
\tag{1.5}
\]

## 1.1 Plus secondary

The plus secondary lower zero is

\[
a^+=L_+=r+6d-p=6d-z-1.
\tag{1.6+}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
6d=z+a^++1,
\qquad
d_+(a)=\frac{z+a+1}{6},
\qquad
q_+(a)=p+\frac{z+a+1}{6}.
}
\tag{1.7+}
\]

Its canonical upper digit and reflection are

\[
\boxed{
 u_+(a)=p-a-2z-2,
\qquad
 \bar u_+(a)=q_+(a)-1-u_+(a)
             =\frac{13z+7a+7}{6}.
}
\tag{1.8+}
\]

Q3806 also supplies the translated plus pair. Intersections involving those additional indices are proper affine coincidences; sharing `(p,z)` does not force one.

## 1.2 Minus secondary

The minus secondary lower zero is

\[
a^-=L_-=d-1-u=7d+z-p.
\tag{1.6-}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
7d=p-z+a^-,
\qquad
d_-(a)=\frac{p-z+a}{7},
\qquad
q_-(a)=p+\frac{p-z+a}{7}.
}
\tag{1.7-}
\]

The canonical upper digit and its reflection are

\[
\boxed{
 u_-(a)=\frac{p-z-6a-7}{7},
\qquad
 \bar u_-(a)=q_-(a)-1-u_-(a)=p+a.
}
\tag{1.8-}
\]

The actual reflected minus support edge is oriented

\[
 a\longrightarrow r,
\qquad
 a<d,
\qquad
 r=7d-1-a=p-1-z.
\tag{1.9-}
\]

This last orientation distinguishes the incoming star in the present problem from Q3806's outgoing fork.

# 2. Sector-by-sector common-index and fork audit

Take two extensions from the same `(p,z)` star with distinct secondaries

\[
a_1\ne a_2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For every extension, because `n` is fixed,

\[
u(q)=n-6q,
\qquad
\bar u(q)=q-1-u(q)=7q-1-n.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus equality of two low digits, or equality of two reflected high digits, is equivalent to equality of the corresponding upper primes. The q6 low/high ranges are disjoint, so a low digit cannot equal a reflected high digit in the macroscopic shell.

## 2.1 `++`

The map

\[
a\mapsto d_+(a)=\frac{z+a+1}{6}
\]

is injective. Therefore distinct secondaries imply

\[
d_1\ne d_2,
\qquad
q_1\ne q_2,
\qquad
u_1\ne u_2,
\qquad
\bar u_1\ne\bar u_2.
\tag{2.3++}
\]

So `++` forces only the common row (1.2). It forces neither a common upper index nor a minus fork.

## 2.2 `--`

The map

\[
a\mapsto d_-(a)=\frac{p-z+a}{7}
\]

is also injective. Hence distinct secondaries give distinct `d_i`, `q_i`, low upper digits, and reflected upper digits `p+a_i`.

The two minus support edges are

\[
a_1\longrightarrow r,
\qquad
a_2\longrightarrow r,
\qquad r=p-1-z.
\tag{2.3--}
\]

This is a co-oriented **incoming** fork with common head `r`. Q3806's carrier `b_{p+v}/p` requires a common smaller tail

\[
v\longrightarrow r_1,
\qquad
v\longrightarrow r_2.
\]

Here the tails are `a_1!=a_2`. Thus the complete `--` sector has the common-row carrier, but not the Q3806 short fork carrier.

## 2.3 `+-`

Let `a` be the plus secondary and `b` the minus secondary. A common upper prime occurs exactly when

\[
d_+(a)=d_-(b),
\]

i.e.

\[
\boxed{
7(z+a+1)=6(p-z+b),
}
\tag{2.3+-}
\]

or

\[
7a-6b+13z+7-6p=0.
\tag{2.4+-}
\]

Because `n` is common, (2.3+-) is equivalent to equality of the canonical low upper digits and to equality of their reflections.

But equal `p,z,d` means that the two signs describe the same physical q6 row. The deterministic OR convention retains that row once, in one sign class. Therefore this affine diagonal is absent from a genuine post-OR `+-` pair family.

If pre-OR bookkeeping retains both sign records, the repeated prime contributes only one factor `q`; one may not replace it by `q^2` in a carrier.

Off (2.3+-), the upper primes and canonical upper indices are distinct. With only one minus edge, no minus fork occurs.

# 3. Exact common-value carriers

## 3.1 Common row carrier

For a valid post-OR pair in any sector, `q_1` and `q_2` are distinct primes larger than `p`. From (1.5),

\[
pq_1q_2\mid b_n,
\qquad n=7p-1-z.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
q_1q_2\mid \frac{b_{7p-1-z}}p.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The elementary estimate used in Q3806,

\[
b_m\le(m+1)64^m,
\]

gives

\[
\log\left(\frac{b_{7p-1-z}}p\right)=O_J(T).
\tag{3.2}
\]

## 3.2 Common upper index

If two distinct upper primes happen to select the same integer index `N`, then

\[
\boxed{q_1q_2\mid b_N.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

No complete sign sector forces this. In `+-`, the only forced equality among the canonical row digits is the OR-duplicate diagonal above; all further intersections in the enlarged Q3806 upper-zero sets require extra affine equations.

## 3.3 Q3806 outgoing-minus carrier

If several minus rows share the same smaller endpoint `v`, Q3806 gives

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{e\in E^-_{\rm out}(v)}q_e
\mid \frac{b_{p+v}}p.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

A distinct-secondary same-primary `--` pair has different smaller endpoints, so (3.4) is not its carrier. The correct carrier is (3.1).

# 4. Exact capacity-`R` bound

Let `mathcal M_{epsilon eta}` denote one post-OR sign-sector family of unordered distinct-secondary pairs. Regard each pair as an edge joining its two tagged upper extension occurrences

\[
\xi=(n,q,u,\text{row occurrence}).
\]

For `F subseteq mathcal M_{epsilon eta}`, let `mathcal U(F)` be its set of tagged endpoint occurrences. Define

\[
\boxed{
D_{\epsilon\eta,R}
=\max_{F\subseteq\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}}
 \bigl(|F|-R|\mathcal U(F)|\bigr)_+.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The clone form of Hall's theorem says that deleting exactly `D_{epsilon eta,R}` pairs permits an assignment of every remaining pair to one of its two upper occurrences with load at most `R`.

Let `mathcal N_{J,T}` be the possible common row indices. In the q6 shell

\[
|\mathcal N_{J,T}|\ll_JT,
\qquad
n\ll_JT.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Use the squarefree aggregate row carrier

\[
\mathcal C_{\rm row}(T)
:=\prod_{n\in\mathcal N_{J,T}}\operatorname{rad}(b_n).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
\log\mathcal C_{\rm row}(T)
\le\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_{J,T}}\log b_n
\ll_JT^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Assign every Hall-matched pair to one endpoint. The product of the assigned upper primes divides `mathcal C_row(T)^R`. Since each upper prime is `asymp_J T`,

\[
\bigl(|\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}|-D_{\epsilon\eta,R}\bigr)
\log(c_JT)
\le R\log\mathcal C_{\rm row}(T)
\ll_JRT^2.
\]

This proves

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}|
\ll_J\frac{RT^2}{\log T}
+D_{\epsilon\eta,R}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Using both factors `q_1q_2` under a maximum-degree hypothesis changes only a constant. Each pair costs two prime logarithms rather than one, not a product of two logarithmic savings.

For reference, a same-sign star with `k` extensions contains `K_k`; its local capacity obstruction is

\[
\left(\binom{k}{2}-Rk\right)_+.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus a complete same-sign star is `R`-orientable only for `k<=2R+1`. In the mixed complete bipartite envelope, the corresponding local obstruction is

\[
\bigl(k_+k_- -R(k_++k_-)\bigr)_+,
\]

before deleting equal-secondary and OR-duplicate edges. Globally, (4.1) is the exact deficiency because one upper occurrence can participate in more than one lower star.

# 5. Exponent ledger

Substituting

\[
R=\frac{T^{2/5}}{L^{6/5}\Omega(T)}
\]

into (4.5), even with `D_{epsilon eta,R}=0`, gives

\[
|\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}|
\ll_J
\frac{T^{12/5}}
 {\Omega(T)L^{11/5}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The desired estimate is

\[
o_J\!\left(
\frac{T^{12/5}}
 {\Omega(T)L^{16/5}}
\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence no complete sign sector closes from the common-value carriers plus capacity alone.

This is the sharp height-only ceiling. For one fixed row `n`, the elementary height bound permits `O_J(T/L)` prime divisors of size `asymp T`. Capacity `R` can therefore pay `O_J(RT/L)` pair assignments on that row. There are `O_J(T)` possible rows, giving exactly `O_J(RT^2/L)`.

A common-upper-index subfamily has possible indices in an `O_J(T)` interval, each with carrier height `O_J(T)`. The outgoing-minus-fork indices `p+v` likewise occupy `O_J(T)` integers. Thus both special mechanisms still have aggregate log height `O_J(T^2)` and the same bound (5.1). The shorter Q3806 index improves constants, not the missing logarithm.

# 6. Q3815 deletion and the exact leftover

For a distinct-secondary star pair, sort the three actual lower p-zero positions as

\[
x<x+h<x+h+k.
\]

Q3815 proves

\[
W_{\rm gap}(\Psi)
\ll_J
\frac{\Psi^2(\log(2\Psi))^2}{\log T}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

With

\[
\Psi=
\frac{T^{6/5}}
 {L^{21/10}\sqrt{\Omega(T)}\,\omega(T)},
\qquad
\omega(T)\to\infty,
\tag{6.2}
\]

one has, sector by sector,

\[
W_{\epsilon\eta}(hk\le\Psi)
=o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L^2}\right).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Let `mathcal M^>_{epsilon eta}` be the residual `hk>Psi` family and let `D^>_{epsilon eta,R}` be its exact capacity deficiency. Combining (4.5) and (6.3) yields

\[
\boxed{
W_{\epsilon\eta}(T)
\le
 o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L^2}\right)
 +O_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L}\right)
 +D^>_{\epsilon\eta,R}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The exact large-product remnants are:

```text
++:
  hk>Psi; distinct q_1,q_2 and distinct canonical upper digits;
  common-row carrier only.

+-:
  hk>Psi; same-q affine diagonal removed by deterministic OR;
  distinct q_+,q_- and no forced common upper digit;

  hk>Psi; distinct smaller minus tails and one common head;
  incoming fork, not the Q3806 common-tail fork;
  common-row carrier only.
```

Same-secondary outgoing minus forks are outside this distinct-secondary theorem and must be handled separately by (3.4).

# 7. Smallest missing estimate

The missing input is not a universal mixed-circuit carrier. It is one extra logarithm in the actual large-product common-row divisor-pair count.

A precise sufficient statement for one complete sector is

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal M^>_{\epsilon\eta}|
-D^>_{\epsilon\eta,R}
=o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L^2}\right).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equivalently, for an `R`-bounded orientation `phi`, one needs

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{e\in\mathcal M^>_{\epsilon\eta}}
\log q_{\phi(e)}
=o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L}ight).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The common-row carrier proves only `O_J(RT^2)` for the left side of (7.2). Thus (7.2) is exactly a `1/log T` improvement beyond height.

A sieve statement would have to be conditioned on the moving divisor sets

\[
\{q\asymp T:q\mid b_n,\ q\text{ is a legal q6 extension of }(p,z)\},
\]

while retaining `hk>Psi` and the sign sector. The ordinary two-linear-form upper sieve does not by itself provide this moving common-row divisor-pair estimate.

No universal mixed theta/handcuff carrier or cross-characteristic identity is asserted.

# 8. Capacity-admissibility caveat

The counting argument above is valid for every integer `R`. If the same `R` is also used in the older amplified occurrence carrier of height `O_J(RT^{5/3})`, its separate admissibility condition remains

\[
R=o\!\left(\frac{T^{1/3}}{\log T}\right).
\]

For the displayed `R`, this is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T^{1/15}}
 {L^{1/5}\Omega(T)}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

A merely slowly divergent `Omega` is therefore insufficient for simultaneous use in that older carrier. This is independent of the one-logarithm obstruction above.
