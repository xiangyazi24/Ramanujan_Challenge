# Verdict

Let `L=log T` and

\[
R=\frac{T^{2/5}}{L^{6/5}\Omega(T)}
\]

(up to the harmless integer part). For an unordered, distinct-secondary, post-OR lower-primary star pair sharing `(p,z)`, **all three sign sectors `++`, `+-`, and `--` have the same row key**

\[
\rho=p-1-z,
\qquad
n=6p+\rho=7p-1-z,
\]

and every upper prime of either extension divides `b_n`. Thus every valid pair with distinct upper primes has the exact common-row carrier

\[
\boxed{
q_1q_2\mid C^{\rm row}_{p,z},
\qquad
C^{\rm row}_{p,z}:=\frac{b_{7p-1-z}}p.
}
\tag{V1}
\]

The sign refinements are:

- `++`: the two upper primes and their canonical low/reflected upper digits are automatically distinct. No common upper digit and no minus fork is forced.
- `+-`: a common upper prime/digit occurs **only** on one explicit affine diagonal, where the two sign witnesses are attached to the same physical q6 row. The deterministic OR convention retains that row once, so it is not a genuine post-OR star pair. Off that diagonal, only the common-row carrier is forced.
- `--`: the two minus edges have the same **head** `r=p-1-z` and distinct smaller tails. They form a co-oriented **incoming** minus fork, not the common-tail/outgoing fork to which Q3806's shorter carrier applies. Hence the full distinct-secondary `--` sector does not force a common `b_{p+v}/p` carrier.

Q3806's short carrier

\[
\prod q_e\mid \frac{b_{p+v}}p
\]

applies when several minus rows share the same smaller endpoint `v`; in the present star-pair problem that means the same secondary, not distinct secondaries. It is therefore a complementary configuration, not a carrier for the full `--` sector.

Even in the most favorable capacity-`R` situation—zero Hall deficiency—the common-row carrier gives only

\[
\boxed{
W_{\epsilon\eta}^{(R)}(T)
\ll_J \frac{RT^2}{\log T}
=rac{T^{12/5}}
       {\Omega(T)(\log T)^{11/5}}
}
\tag{V2}
\]

for each full sector `epsilon eta in {++, +-, --}`. The desired scale is

\[
\frac{RT^2}{(\log T)^2}
=rac{T^{12/5}}
       {\Omega(T)(\log T)^{16/5}}.
\]

Thus the exact common-value method misses by one factor of `log T`:

\[
\frac{RT^2/\log T}{RT^2/(\log T)^2}=\log T.
\tag{V3}
\]

This remains true for a special common-upper-index subfamily or for Q3806's short outgoing-minus-fork subfamily: the possible carrier indices occupy only `O_J(T)` integers, each carrier has logarithmic height `O_J(T)`, so the aggregate carrier height is still `O_J(T^2)`.

\boxed{

\tag{V4}

# 1. Exact lower-primary star dictionary

Fix a lower prime `p` and its canonical reflected primary zero

\[
z\in Z_p,
\qquad
r:=p-1-z\in Z_p.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Every extension from this star has the same lower row key `r`, hence the same integer row

\[
\boxed{
n=6p+r=7p-1-z.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

For one extension write

\[
q=p+d,
\qquad
n=6q+u,
\qquad
u=p-1-z-6d.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The true q6 row conditions give

\[
p\mid b_r,
\qquad
q\mid b_u.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Reflection at `p` gives `p|b_z` iff `p|b_r`, and Apéry--Lucas applied to (1.2)--(1.3) gives

\[
p\mid b_n,
\qquad
q\mid b_n.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Indeed

\[
b_n\equiv b_6b_r\pmod p,
\qquad
b_n\equiv b_6b_u\pmod q.
\]

The two signs produce different secondary lower zeros.

## 1.1 Plus extension

The plus secondary is the exact reflected-plus endpoint

\[
a^+=L_+=r+6d-p=6d-z-1.
\tag{1.6+}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
6d=z+a^++1,
\qquad
d_+(a)=\frac{z+a+1}{6},
\qquad
q_+(a)=p+\frac{z+a+1}{6}.
}
\tag{1.7+}
\]

The canonical upper digit and its q-reflection are

\[
\boxed{
 u_+(a)=p-a-2z-2,
\qquad
 \bar u_+(a)=q_+(a)-1-u_+(a)
             =\frac{13z+7a+7}{6}.
}
\tag{1.8+}
\]

Q3806 supplies two further translated plus zeros, but their equality with a zero from a second edge is an additional affine hyperplane; it is not forced by sharing `(p,z)`.

## 1.2 Minus extension

The minus secondary is

\[
a^-=L_-=d-1-u=7d+z-p.
\tag{1.6-}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
7d=p-z+a^-,
\qquad
d_-(a)=\frac{p-z+a}{7},
\qquad
q_-(a)=p+\frac{p-z+a}{7}.
}
\tag{1.7-}
\]

The canonical upper digit and its reflection are

\[
\boxed{
 u_-(a)=\frac{p-z-6a-7}{7},
\qquad
 \bar u_-(a)=q_-(a)-1-u_-(a)=p+a.
}
\tag{1.8-}
\]

The actual reflected minus support edge is canonically oriented as

\[
 a\longrightarrow r,
\qquad
 a<d,
\qquad
 r=7d-1-a=p-1-z.
\tag{1.9-}
\]

This orientation is load-bearing in the fork audit below.

# 2. Exact sector classification

Take two extensions `e_1,e_2` from the same `(p,z)` star, with distinct secondary lower zeros

\[
a_1\ne a_2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

All sectors share (1.2), hence all share the row carrier. What differs is the upper-index and fork geometry.

## 2.1 Sector `++`

The map

\[
a\mapsto d_+(a)=\frac{z+a+1}{6}
\]

is injective. Hence `a_1!=a_2` implies

\[
d_1\ne d_2,
\qquad
q_1\ne q_2.
\tag{2.2++}
\]

The two low upper digits are distinct because

\[
u_+(a_1)-u_+(a_2)=-(a_1-a_2),
\]

and the reflected high digits are distinct because

\[
\bar u_+(a_1)-\bar u_+(a_2)
 =\frac76(a_1-a_2).
\]

The q6 low/high ranges are disjoint, so a low digit on one row cannot equal a reflected high digit on the other. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
++:\quad
\text{common row key yes; common canonical upper index no; minus fork no.}
}
\tag{2.3++}
\]

Any intersection involving Q3806's additional plus translated zeros is a proper affine coincidence, not a sector identity.

## 2.2 Sector `--`

Likewise

\[
a\mapsto d_-(a)=\frac{p-z+a}{7}
\]

is injective, so distinct secondaries give distinct `d_i` and distinct upper primes. The low digits satisfy

\[
u_-(a_1)-u_-(a_2)
=-\frac67(a_1-a_2),
\]

while the reflected high digits are `p+a_1` and `p+a_2`; both pairs are distinct, and the low/high ranges do not cross.

The two actual minus support edges are

\[
a_1\longrightarrow r,
\qquad
a_2\longrightarrow r,
\qquad r=p-1-z.
\tag{2.2--}
\]

Thus they form a co-oriented **incoming** fork at the common head `r`. Q3806's short carrier is for an outgoing fork

\[
v\longrightarrow r_1,
\qquad
v\longrightarrow r_2,
\]

with a common smaller tail `v`. Here the tails are the distinct secondaries `a_1,a_2`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
--:\quad
\text{common row key yes; common upper index no;}
\text{ common-head in-fork yes, but Q3806 short fork no.}
}
\tag{2.3--}
\]

The Q3806 carrier `b_{p+v}/p` belongs to the complementary same-secondary/different-primary configuration.

## 2.3 Sector `+-`

Let `a` be the plus secondary and `b` the minus secondary. A common upper prime occurs exactly when

\[
d_+(a)=d_-(b),
\]

i.e.

\[
\boxed{
7(z+a+1)=6(p-z+b),
}
\tag{2.2+-}
\]

or equivalently

\[
7a-6b+13z+7-6p=0.
\tag{2.3+-}
\]

Because `n` is common, the following are equivalent:

\[
q_+(a)=q_-(b),
\qquad
u_+(a)=u_-(b),
\qquad
\bar u_+(a)=\bar u_-(b).
\tag{2.4+-}
\]

But equality of `p,z,d` means that these are the plus and minus witnesses of **one physical q6 row**. Under the deterministic OR convention that row is retained once, in one sign class. Therefore the affine diagonal (2.2+-) is absent from a genuine post-OR pair family.

If a pre-OR bookkeeping system keeps both sign records, the repeated upper prime contributes only one factor `q`, not `q^2`; it must not be counted as a two-prime common-value event.

Off (2.2+-), the upper primes and the canonical upper digits are distinct. With only one minus edge there is no minus fork. Thus

\[
\boxed{
+-:\quad
\text{common row key yes; common upper index only on the OR-duplicate diagonal;}
\text{ no minus fork.}
}
\tag{2.5+-}
\]

No other upper-index equality is forced. Extra equalities among the larger Q3806 selected-index sets are additional proper affine hyperplanes.

# 3. Exact common-value carriers

## 3.1 Common row

For a valid post-OR pair in any sign sector, the two upper primes are distinct and exceed `p`. From (1.5),

\[
pq_1q_2\mid b_n,
\qquad n=7p-1-z.
\]

Since the three primes are pairwise coprime,

\[
\boxed{
q_1q_2\mid \frac{b_n}{p}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is a genuine prescribed integer carrier. It is not the tautological additive-prime carrier of a circuit relation.

The elementary Apéry estimate used in Q3806,

\[
b_m\le (m+1)64^m,
\]

gives

\[
\log\left(\frac{b_n}{p}\right)=O_J(T)
\tag{3.2}
\]

on the macroscopic shell.

## 3.2 Common upper index

If two distinct upper primes happen to select one common integer index `N`, then

\[
\boxed{q_1q_2\mid b_N.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

No full sign sector forces (3.3). The only canonical low/reflective equality in the mixed sector is the same-row diagonal (2.2+-), which has one prime rather than two after OR.

## 3.3 Q3806 outgoing minus fork

If distinct minus rows share the same smaller tail `v`, Q3806 proves

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{e\in E^-_{\rm out}(v)}q_e
\mid \frac{b_{p+v}}p.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

For the present distinct-secondary same-primary pair, the smaller tails are the distinct secondaries. Thus (3.4) is not available for the full `--` sector. The common-head row carrier (3.1) is the correct carrier there.

# 4. Capacity-`R` counting with the row carrier

Let `mathcal M_{epsilon eta}` be one post-OR sign-sector family of unordered distinct-secondary star pairs. Regard its members as edges of a graph whose vertices are the tagged upper extension occurrences

\[
\xi=(n,q,u,\text{row occurrence}).
\]

A pair is incident to its two extension occurrences. For `F subset mathcal M_{epsilon eta}`, let `mathcal U(F)` be the set of tagged endpoint occurrences.

The exact capacity deficiency is

\[
\boxed{
D_{\epsilon\eta,R}
=\max_{F\subseteq\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}}
\bigl(|F|-R|\mathcal U(F)|\bigr)_+.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

By the clone form of Hall's theorem, after deleting exactly `D_{epsilon eta,R}` pairs, every remaining pair can be assigned to one of its two upper occurrences so that no occurrence receives more than `R` pairs.

Let `mathcal N_{J,T}` be the possible common row indices. The q6 shell gives

\[
|\mathcal N_{J,T}|\ll_J T,
\qquad
n\ll_J T.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define the squarefree row-value carrier

\[
\mathcal C_{\rm row}(T)
:=\prod_{n\in\mathcal N_{J,T}}\operatorname{rad}(b_n).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Every tagged upper occurrence `(n,q,u)` contributes its prime `q` to the factor `rad(b_n)`. By the Apéry height estimate,

\[
\log\mathcal C_{\rm row}(T)
\le\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_{J,T}}\log b_n
\ll_J T^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Assign every Hall-matched pair to one endpoint. The product of the assigned upper primes divides `mathcal C_row(T)^R`. Since every upper prime is `asymp_J T`,

\[
\bigl(|\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}|-D_{\epsilon\eta,R}\bigr)
\log(c_JT)
\le R\log\mathcal C_{\rm row}(T)
\ll_J RT^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}|
\ll_J \frac{RT^2}{\log T}
+D_{\epsilon\eta,R}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Using both prime factors `q_1q_2` for each pair under a maximum-degree rather than orientation hypothesis changes only the absolute constant. It cannot turn one logarithmic prime cost into a `(log T)^2` saving.

## 4.1 Local meaning of the deficiency

If one star has `k_+` plus extensions, its full `++` pair graph is `K_{k_+}`. The star alone violates capacity `R` by

\[
\left(\binom{k_+}{2}-Rk_+\right)_+.
\tag{4.6++}
\]

The same formula holds for `--` with `k_-`. In particular, a full same-sign star is `R`-orientable only when

\[
k_\pm\le 2R+1.
\]

For the mixed sector, the complete bipartite upper envelope gives the local obstruction

\[
\bigl(k_+k_- -R(k_++k_-)\bigr)_+,
\tag{4.6+-}
\]

before removing the equal-secondary and OR-duplicate diagonals. Globally, occurrences can be shared across lower stars, so (4.1), not a sum of the local formulas, is the exact deficiency.

# 5. Exponent ledger for the proposed capacity

Put

\[
R=\frac{T^{2/5}}{L^{6/5}\Omega(T)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Even with

\[
D_{\epsilon\eta,R}=0,
\]

(4.5) gives

\[
|\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}|
\ll_J
\frac{T^{12/5}}
 {\Omega(T)L^{11/5}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The requested target is

\[
o_J\!\left(
\frac{RT^2}{L^2}
\right)
=
 o_J\!\left(
\frac{T^{12/5}}
 {\Omega(T)L^{16/5}}
\right).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore no full sign sector closes from the exact carriers and capacity alone. The common-row carrier is short enough to save all powers of `T` required here, but not the second logarithm.

This is the sharp height-only ceiling. For one fixed row `n`, the elementary bound permits as many as

\[
O_J(T/\log T)
\]

prime divisors of size `asymp T`. An `R`-bounded assignment can therefore pay

\[
O_J(RT/\log T)
\]

pairs on that row. There are `O_J(T)` possible rows, reproducing exactly `O_J(RT^2/log T)`. No named input supplies an additional `1/log T` distribution factor for the large prime divisors of these moving Apéry values.

## 5.1 Common-index and short-fork subfamilies do not change the ledger

For a common upper index `N`, all possible `N` lie in an `O_J(T)` interval and `log b_N=O_J(T)`. Thus the product of all common-index carriers has log height `O_J(T^2)`.

For Q3806's outgoing minus forks, the short indices are `p+v=O_J(T)` and again occupy only `O_J(T)` integers. Hence

\[
\log\prod_m\operatorname{rad}(b_m)=O_J(T^2).
\]

Both mechanisms therefore give the same capacity bound (5.2). The word “shorter” improves constants and local geometry, but not the power/logarithm ledger needed for (5.3).

# 6. Interaction with the Q3815 shifted-resultant deletion

For a distinct-secondary star pair, sort its three actual lower p-zero positions as

\[
x<x+h<x+h+k.

\[
W_{\rm gap}(\Psi)
\ll_J
\frac{\Psi^2(\log(2\Psi))^2}{\log T}.
\tag{6.1}

With

\[

\frac{T^{6/5}}
 {L^{21/10}\sqrt{\Omega(T)}\,\omega(T)},
\qquad \omega(T)\to\infty,
\tag{6.2}
\]

this becomes

\[
W_{\rm gap}(\Psi)
=o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L^2}\right).
\tag{6.3}

Each individual sign sector is a subset, so (6.3) applies separately to `++`, `+-`, and `--`.

Define `mathcal M^>_{epsilon eta}` by `hk>Psi` and let `D^>_{epsilon eta,R}` be its capacity deficiency. The complete unconditional ledger is then

\[
\boxed{
W_{\epsilon\eta}(T)
\le
 o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L^2}\right)
 +O_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L}\right)


# 7. The exact smallest missing estimate

The required new statement is not a universal mixed-circuit carrier. It is a sectorwise, large-product, common-row divisor-pair estimate.

One exact formulation is: for at least one full sector `epsilon eta`, prove

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal M^>_{\epsilon\eta}|
-D^>_{\epsilon\eta,R}
=o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L^2}\right).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equivalently, after an `R`-bounded orientation `phi` of the surviving pair graph, one needs the logarithmic mass improvement

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{e\in\mathcal M^>_{\epsilon\eta}}
 \log q_{\phi(e)}
=o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{L}ight).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The carrier identity proves only the `O_J(RT^2)` bound for the left side of (7.2). Thus (7.2) is exactly one factor `1/log T` beyond the present common-value height argument.

A prime-pair/Selberg statement could supply that factor only if it is formulated on the actual moving divisor sets

\[
\{q\asymp T:q\mid b_n,\ q\text{ is a legal q6 extension of the fixed }(p,z)\},
\]

with the `hk>Psi` and sign constraints retained. The ordinary two-linear-form upper sieve does not by itself give this conditional divisor-pair estimate: the two secondaries are independent variables and the divisibility sets move with `b_n`.

No assertion is made here about a universal mixed theta/handcuff carrier, and no cross-characteristic identity is inferred.

# 8. Capacity-admissibility caveat

The counting theorem above is valid for every integer `R`. If the same numerical `R` is also inserted into the older amplified occurrence carrier with height `O_J(RT^{5/3})`, its separate admissibility condition remains

\[
R=o\!\left(\frac{T^{1/3}}{\log T}\right).
\]

For (5.1), this is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T^{1/15}}
 {L^{1/5}\Omega(T)}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus a merely slowly divergent `Omega` is not enough to use this `R` simultaneously in that older carrier. This does not repair or worsen the one-logarithm obstruction (V3); it is an independent consistency condition.
