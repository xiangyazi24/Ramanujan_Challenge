# 7. Why projectivizing at the anti index does not add a constraint

The flag \(p\nmid b_r\), with \(r=v-p\), allows one to normalize the Apéry state in \(\mathbf F_p\) by \(b_r^{-1}\). This is the only direct algebraic advantage of the flag.

Let

\[
Y_t:=\begin{pmatrix}b_{t+1}\\b_t\end{pmatrix}
\]

and let \(T_{s\leftarrow t}\) be the Apéry transfer matrix from \(Y_t\) to \(Y_s\). On a nonwrapping interval all recurrence pivots are \(p\)-units, so

\[
T_{s\leftarrow t}\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_p).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The P row has zeros at

\[
x
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x+h.
\]

Starting from any base index \(r\), the two zero conditions say that two row functionals

\[
L_x:=e_2^TT_{x\leftarrow r},
\qquad
L_{x+h}:=e_2^TT_{x+h\leftarrow r}
\tag{7.2}
\]

annihilate the nonzero state \(Y_r\). Therefore

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}L_x\\L_{x+h}\end{pmatrix}=0
\pmod p.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Changing the base from \(r\) to \(x\) multiplies this determinant by

\[
\det T_{x\leftarrow r},
\]

a \(p\)-unit. The remaining determinant is, up to the standard unit denominator product, exactly the coefficient \(N_h(x)\) in the transfer from \(x\) to \(x+h\). Hence `(7.3)` is equivalent to

\[
p\mid N_h(x),
\tag{7.4}
\]

which is already equivalent to the two P endpoint zeros.

Thus the projective use of \(b_r\ne0\) gives no third congruence. It merely chooses a private slope

\[
\frac{b_{r+1}}{b_r}\in\mathbf F_p.
\]

For two lower primes \(p_1\ne p_2\), these slopes belong to different fields. An ordinary resultant or determinant over one characteristic cannot couple them. A characteristic-zero construction obtained by multiplying the private equations restores the full product height and gives no codegree saving.

This is the first exact algebraic obstruction to a proof based solely on the nonzero flag and the existing transfer identity.
