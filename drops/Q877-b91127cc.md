# Q877: Legendre–Euler versus convolution-content carrier audit

## 1. Claimed bridge search

Let the Legendre–Euler carrier be represented by its coefficient vector
\[
T_{n,q}(c)=\sum_j A_j(n,q)c^j,
\]
with the known local profile modulo a candidate prime \(p\):
\[
T_{n,q}(c)\equiv A_j Q_q(c^p) \pmod p.
\]
For fixed \(q=3\), the nonzero local positions form a rank-one vector. Hence every integral linear functional applied to these positions has at most one independent local equation modulo \(p\).

The convolution-content carrier is
\[
P_n(X)=\sum_{k=1}^n (L_n/k)b_{n-k}X^k,
\]
with
\[
p>\sqrt n,\quad p\mid c_n \iff p\mid b_{n\bmod p}.
\]
Its derivative-content identity gives
\[
\operatorname{cont}(P_n/c_n)'=L_n.
\]

A direct change of basis between these two objects would need to preserve integral content while converting a rank-one local congruence into a second independent prime-divisibility constraint. No such transformation follows from the available identities.

## 2. Integral change-of-basis obstruction

Suppose there is an integral matrix \(M\in M_d(\mathbb Z)\) such that
\[
V_P=M V_T
\]
relates the relevant coefficient vectors. Content satisfies
\[
\operatorname{cont}(MV_T)\mid |\det M|\operatorname{cont}(V_T)
\]
when \(M\) is square and invertible over \(\mathbb Q\). Therefore the only possible new prime information comes from primes dividing \(\det M\).

If \(|\det M|=n^{O(1)}\), then the extra height contribution is only
\[
\log |\det M|=O(\log n),
\]
which is negligible, but it cannot create a missing local equation for primes not dividing \(\det M\).

For \(p\nmid\det M\), reduction modulo \(p\) preserves rank:
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}(MV_T)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}(V_T).
\]
Thus a rank-one Legendre–Euler local carrier remains rank one after any bounded-height unimodular or determinant-controlled transform.

## 3. Adjoint pairing and specialization audit

An adjoint pairing
\[
\langle U,V\rangle=U^TAV
\]
can only introduce new divisibility through entries of \(A\). If \(A\) has polynomial height \(n^{O(1)}\), then the exceptional prime set is contained in the primes dividing minors of \(A\), with total logarithmic weight \(O(\log n)\).

Therefore an adjoint pairing cannot force an additional condition on the full set of target primes
\[
\{p>\sqrt n:p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}.
\]

## 4. Derivative-content identity audit

The identity
\[
\operatorname{cont}((P_n/c_n)')=L_n
\]
contains information about the normalization of the convolution carrier, but it does not imply
\[
\operatorname{cont}(P_n)=o(e^n)
\]
or
\[
\log c_n=o(n).
\]

Indeed, the derivative removes the common content before measuring the remaining primitive polynomial. It is compatible with arbitrarily large primitive contents of the original polynomial after multiplication by an external factor.

Thus no implication
\[
\operatorname{cont}((P_n/c_n)')=L_n\Longrightarrow \log c_n=o(n)
\]
holds without an additional height bound relating coefficients of \(P_n\) and \(P_n/c_n\).

## 5. Divisibility relation audit

A possible relation
\[
c_n\mid \operatorname{cont}(T_{n,q})
\]
or its reverse would have to match prime supports.

For the convolution carrier, every prime \(p>\sqrt n\) in the target support appears through
\[
p\mid b_{n\bmod p}.
\]

For fixed \(q\), the Legendre–Euler carrier only sees the affine residue pattern attached to
\[
r=n-qm,
\]
and its local polynomial profile gives one residue equation. The missing second condition is not supplied by the content operation alone.

Therefore no support equality follows from the currently established local profiles.

## 6. Scoped obstruction theorem

**Atomic claim (proved).** Let \(M_n\) be any family of integral matrices with
\[
\log |\det M_n|=o(n)
\]
and let \(V_n\) be a Legendre–Euler coefficient vector whose reduction modulo every target prime outside the determinant support has rank one. Then \(M_nV_n\) cannot produce a second independent congruence for those primes.

**Reason.** Outside primes dividing \(\det M_n\), reduction modulo \(p\) is an invertible linear transformation, hence preserves the dimension of the local solution space.

This rules out the class:

- bounded-height integral basis changes;
- determinant-subexponential adjoint pairings;
- specialization maps implemented by such matrices.

It does not rule out nonlinear constructions, unbounded-height transforms, or new holonomic identities.

## 7. Conclusion

No concrete missing edge from Legendre–Euler content to convolution content is obtained. The existing carriers share the same Apéry-Lucas divisibility phenomenon, but the Legendre–Euler local rank-one profile lacks the second equation required to compress the target prime set.

The remaining viable routes are genuinely new:

1. a nonlinear resultant eliminating the rank-one degeneracy;
2. a holonomic identity producing extra local constraints;
3. a direct coefficient-height estimate for the convolution content carrier.

A fixed-height integral transform, adjoint pairing, specialization, or derivative-content manipulation alone cannot provide the required \(o(n)\) logarithmic bound.
