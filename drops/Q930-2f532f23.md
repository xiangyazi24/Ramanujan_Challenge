# Q930: uniform Newton--Pade degree audit

## Verdict

The requested uniform-in-d lower bound is not currently derivable from the fixed-d theorem, and the available structural information does not produce a growing-degree escape construction. The sharp statement that can be proved from the Edgar Stieltjes-density structure is a transition theorem: the fixed-degree regime is saturated, while the only unresolved window is the high-codimension edge where the denominator degree approaches H.

In particular, finite scans through H<=24 are consistent with this transition but do not prove it.

## 1. Determinant representation

Let the Newton--Pade system of denominator degree d be written in the Stieltjes moment basis. The primitive numerator coefficients are signed cofactors of the moment matrix

M_d(H)=(mu_{i+j})_{0<=i,j<d},

with Edgar's density rows giving

mu_k=integral_0^1 x^k rho_H(x) dx,

where rho_H is positive on (0,1).

For fixed d, total positivity gives strict sign regularity of all minors. Hence the primitive cofactor vector has one sign and the determinant ratio gives

log |P(n)| >= c n+o(n), c=0.450561.

This argument uses d fixed: the constants in the variation-diminishing estimates depend on d.

## 2. Growing d regimes

Write H=floor((n-1)/3).

### Regime d=o(H)

Here the moment matrix probes only the first o(H) rows of the Stieltjes density. The total positivity constants can be expected to remain stable only if uniform bounds on the relevant minors are supplied. The existing theorem does not contain such bounds.

No escape construction is obtained: the determinant size remains exponential because the principal minors retain positive mass.

### Regime d~sqrt(H)

The determinant is no longer controlled by fixed-dimensional asymptotics. A Neville elimination argument would require a uniform lower bound for every pivot

Delta_{j+1}/Delta_j

for j up to sqrt(H). Such a bound is not presently established. Therefore this range remains open rather than disproved.

### Regime d=theta H, 0<theta<1

The matrix samples a positive fraction of the density moments. Sign changes may occur because high-order orthogonality begins to compete with total positivity. However, no subexponential primitive cofactor vector is obtained automatically: the determinant scale and the gcd cancellation must both be controlled.

The observed mixed signs near d=H are therefore not a certificate of small height.

### Codimension H-d fixed

This is the genuinely different boundary regime. Here the Pade system is almost diagonal in the complementary moment basis. A possible cancellation mechanism could occur through large gcds of cofactors, but proving

log(det/gcd)=o(H)

requires arithmetic information not contained in positivity.

## 3. Gcd obstruction

The primitive normalization is the critical missing piece. Let v_d be the integer cofactor vector before division and g_d=gcd(v_d). The desired escape would require

log ||v_d||-log g_d=o(n).

Total positivity controls ||v_d|| but gives no lower bound for g_d. Conversely, the existence of a large gcd is an arithmetic phenomenon invisible to the Stieltjes moment matrix.

Therefore neither positivity nor Neville elimination alone closes the growing-degree problem.

## 4. Support preservation obstruction

A successful carrier must satisfy two independent conditions:

1. the evaluation functional at q=1 must survive, giving a nonzero singular value after projection;
2. the primitive coefficient vector must retain target-prime support.

Near d=H, cancellations that could reduce height also threaten the support law. No verified construction satisfies both requirements.

## 5. Transition theorem supported by current information

The strongest justified statement is:

For every degree sequence d(H) satisfying d(H)=o(H) with a uniform minor lower-bound hypothesis on the first d(H) Stieltjes pivots, the fixed-sign Newton--Pade lower-bound mechanism extends and gives

log|P(n)| >= c n-o(n).

Without that hypothesis, all ranges d(H)->infinity remain unresolved. The only plausible escape window is

H-d(H)=O(1)

or more generally H-d(H)=o(H), where gcd cancellation and complementary moment coordinates must be analyzed.

## 6. What additional input would close the problem

A sufficient new theorem would be either:

* a uniform Neville pivot estimate

  inf_{1<=j<=d(H)} log(Delta_{j+1}/Delta_j)>-O(1)

  for all d(H)<=H-1;

or

* an arithmetic gcd bound

  log gcd(v_d)=o(n)

  uniformly in the transition range.

Either would rule out subexponential primitive height.

## Final answer

The fixed-d theorem cannot be extrapolated. No explicit growing-degree subexponential carrier with verified q=1 support is known from the stated data. The correct sharply scoped result is a transition theorem: all degrees with independently controlled minors are saturated; the remaining possible escape is the near-full-degree regime H-d=o(H), especially bounded codimension. The obstruction is not determinant growth alone but the unresolved interaction between determinant size, primitive gcd cancellation, and support preservation.
