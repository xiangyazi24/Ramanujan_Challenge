# The affine C4 barrier theorem

Let `p` be an odd lower-shell prime and let

\[
0\le a<b<c<d<p
\]

be four actual lower `p`-zeros supporting one of the ordered surviving C4 topologies below. For a natural directed row from tail `x` to head `y`, use the exact upper-state maps

\[
\Phi_P(x,y)
:=\left(
\frac{7p-y+x}{6},\ y-2x-1
\right),
\tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
\Phi_M(x,y)
:=\left(
\frac{8p-x+y}{7},\ \frac{p-x-6y}{7}-1
\right).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Formula (1.2) covers both minus orientations: the branch label records whether `x<y` (`M-`) or `x>y` (`M+`), but the natural-arrow formula is the same.

The three ordered topologies are:

```text
AD1:
  a --P--> c --M+--> b --P--> d --M+--> a

AD2:
  a --P--> d --M+--> b --P--> c --M+--> a

PD:
  a --P--> b --M---> c --P--> d --M+--> a
```

Here `M-` on `b -> c` and `M+` on `d -> a` are distinct inverse branches. Then:

1. No one of AD1, AD2, or PD has an ordinary cycle-local `q/u` parallelogram.
2. AD has two formal same-sign parallelograms, but each relates AD1 to AD2 rather than closing inside one orientation.
3. Every topology satisfies the weighted identity (V1).
4. The source shift has the exact transformation law (V2), with fixed plus common indices and moving minus common indices.
5. These identities, even augmented by any fixed-dimensional prime sieve, do not improve the `T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}` ceiling to the required `o(T^2/L^2)` scale.

The proof occupies the next four sections.

# 1. Which topologies actually have a `q/u` parallelogram?

## 1.1 A cycle-local opposite-edge parallelogram fails for all three types

Number the four directed perimeter edges so that edges `1,3` are plus and edges `2,4` are minus. Put

\[
Q_P=q_1+q_3,\qquad U_P=u_1+u_3,
\]

and

\[
Q_M=q_2+q_4,\qquad U_M=u_2+u_4.
\]

Let `L_1,L_2` be the two plus tails and `H_1,H_2` the two plus heads, and abbreviate

\[
A:=L_1+L_2,\qquad B:=H_1+H_2.
\]

For AD1 and AD2,

\[
\{L_1,L_2\}=\{a,b\},\qquad
\{H_1,H_2\}=\{c,d\}.
\tag{1.3AD}
\]

For PD, the correct alternating classes are

\[
\{L_1,L_2\}=\{a,c\},\qquad
\{H_1,H_2\}=\{b,d\}.
\tag{1.3PD}
\]

In every case the two minus arrows run from the `H` class back to the `L` class. Summing (1.1) over the two plus rows gives

\[
\boxed{6Q_P=14p+A-B,}
\tag{1.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{U_P=B-2A-2.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

Summing (1.2) over the two minus rows gives

\[
\boxed{7Q_M=16p+A-B,}
\tag{1.6}
\]

\[
\boxed{U_M=\frac{2p-B-6A}{7}-2.}
\tag{1.7}
\]

The ordinary upper-state parallelogram around the cycle would be

\[
(q_1,u_1)+(q_3,u_3)=(q_2,u_2)+(q_4,u_4),
\tag{1.8}
\]

i.e. `Q_P=Q_M` and `U_P=U_M`. But (1.4) and (1.6) give

\[
\boxed{
Q_M-Q_P=\frac{B-A-2p}{42}.
}
\tag{1.9}
\]

Since every endpoint lies in `[0,p)`, one has `B-A<2p`; hence (1.9) is never zero.

Likewise, (1.5) and (1.7) give

\[
\boxed{
U_M-U_P=\frac{2p-8(B-A)}7.
}
\tag{1.10}
\]

Equality here would imply

\[
p=4(B-A),
\]

which is impossible because `p` is odd. Thus neither the `q` coordinates nor the `u` coordinates close into an opposite-edge parallelogram for any individual AD1, AD2, or PD cycle.

This is the first exact obstruction to using a cycle-local parallelogram as an extra codimension-two condition.

## 1.2 The two genuine AD affine parallelograms are cross-orientation identities

For a low endpoint `x in {a,b}` and high endpoint `y in {c,d}`, write

\[
\Psi_P(x,y):=\Phi_P(x,y),
\]

and write the `M+` state with natural arrow `y -> x` as

\[
\Psi_{M+}(x,y)
:=\Phi_M(y,x)
=\left(
\frac{8p-y+x}{7},\ \frac{p-y-6x}{7}-1
\right).
\tag{1.11}
\]

Both maps are affine-separable in `x` and `y`. Consequently the following identities hold formally in `Q^2`:

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_P(a,c)+\Psi_P(b,d)
=\Psi_P(a,d)+\Psi_P(b,c),
}
\tag{1.12P}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_{M+}(a,c)+\Psi_{M+}(b,d)
=\Psi_{M+}(a,d)+\Psi_{M+}(b,c).
}
\tag{1.12M}
\]

Their actual-row scope is the key point:

```text
AD1 actual plus diagonal:   P(a,c), P(b,d)
AD2 actual plus diagonal:   P(a,d), P(b,c)

AD1 actual M+ diagonal:     M+(c,b), M+(d,a)
AD2 actual M+ diagonal:     M+(d,b), M+(c,a)
```

Thus (1.12P) equates the AD1 plus diagonal with the AD2 plus diagonal, while (1.12M) equates the two complementary `M+` diagonals. A lone AD1 or AD2 does not supply the opposite diagonal.

Moreover, actualness of one orientation does not force the four complementary states to be integer candidates. For example, from integrality of

\[
\frac{p-c+a}{6},\qquad \frac{p-d+b}{6}
\]

one gets only

\[
d-c\equiv b-a\pmod 6;
\]

it does not follow that `(p-d+a)/6` or `(p-c+b)/6` is integral. The analogous statement holds modulo `7` for the complementary `M+` rows. Even after integrality, primality and the upper selectedness conditions remain independent arithmetic requirements not supplied by the affine identity.

Therefore the exact scope is:

- **AD1 alone:** no all-actual `q/u` parallelogram;
- **AD2 alone:** no all-actual `q/u` parallelogram;
- **AD1 and AD2 simultaneously actual on the same quadruple:** both typed parallelograms are all-actual, but they are automatic identities of the affine maps;
- **PD:** no typed `K_{2,2}` parallelogram, because its minus edges use different branches and its plus edges are the two perimeter edges `a -> b` and `c -> d`.

If the C4 packing forgets sign and direction, AD1 and AD2 use the same four unordered endpoint pairs and cannot be charged as two edge-disjoint support cycles. If it retains physical signed rows, they can coexist, but (1.12P)--(1.12M) still impose no rare condition: they hold identically once all eight typed rows exist.

# 2. The exact weighted `q` identities for AD and PD

For a plus natural arrow `x -> y`, (1.1) gives

\[
y-x=7p-6q_P.
\tag{2.1P}
\]

For a minus natural arrow `x -> y`, (1.2) gives

\[
y-x=7q_M-8p.
\tag{2.1M}
\]

Summing the four directed displacements around any of the three perimeters gives zero. Since there are exactly two plus and two minus rows,

\[
(7p-6q_{P,1})+(7q_{M,1}-8p)
 +(7p-6q_{P,2})+(7q_{M,2}-8p)=0,
\]

which is precisely

\[
\boxed{
7(q_{M,1}+q_{M,2})
-6(q_{P,1}+q_{P,2})=2p.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

In the ordered notation this is:

### AD1

```text
P(a,c), M+(c,b), P(b,d), M+(d,a)
```

\[
\boxed{
7\bigl(q^{M+}_{c\to b}+q^{M+}_{d\to a}\bigr)
-6\bigl(q^P_{a\to c}+q^P_{b\to d}\bigr)
=2p.
}
\tag{2.3AD1}
\]

### AD2

```text
P(a,d), M+(d,b), P(b,c), M+(c,a)
```

\[
\boxed{
7\bigl(q^{M+}_{d\to b}+q^{M+}_{c\to a}\bigr)
-6\bigl(q^P_{a\to d}+q^P_{b\to c}\bigr)
=2p.
}
\tag{2.3AD2}
\]

### PD

```text
P(a,b), M-(b,c), P(c,d), M+(d,a)
```

\[
\boxed{
7\bigl(q^{M-}_{b\to c}+q^{M+}_{d\to a}\bigr)
-6\bigl(q^P_{a\to b}+q^P_{c\to d}\bigr)
=2p.
}
\tag{2.3PD}
\]

Equations (1.4) and (1.6) give another derivation. For AD put

\[
S_{AD}:=a+b-c-d;
\]

for PD put

\[
S_{PD}:=a+c-b-d.
\]

Then in either case

\[
6Q_P=14p+S,
\qquad
7Q_M=16p+S,
\tag{2.4}
\]

and subtraction gives (2.2).

This relation is useful classification bookkeeping, but it is not an additional Diophantine constraint. After the exact row formulas are substituted, its left side is the zero polynomial obtained by telescoping the four endpoint differences. It therefore cannot be used as:

- a nonzero resultant whose prime divisors can be counted;
- a second independent affine equation cutting the endpoint lattice;
- a new prime divisibility carrier; or
- an additional linear-form sieve condition.

It simply reconstructs the already-selected lower prime `p` from the four upper primes.

# 3. Exact audit of the source-reuse shift

## 3.1 The correct endpoint classes

The shift must use the plus-tail/plus-head partition, not always the two numerically smallest versus the two largest vertices.

For AD1 and AD2:

\[
L=\{a,b\},\qquad H=\{c,d\}.
\tag{3.1AD}
\]

For PD:

\[
L=\{a,c\},\qquad H=\{b,d\}.
\tag{3.1PD}
\]

The PD distinction is load-bearing. Shifting the sorted pair `{a,b}` by `7t` and `{c,d}` by `14t` would not freeze both PD plus rows. The alternating classes (3.1PD) are the unique natural choice for the proposed reuse.

For every `ell in L` and `h in H`, define

\[
p_t:=p+t,\qquad
\ell_t:=\ell+7t,\qquad
h_t:=h+14t.
\tag{3.2}
\]

All statements below are restricted to the interval of `t` for which the shifted digits remain in `[0,p_t)`, the required endpoint order remains valid, and the branch legality inequalities hold. The affine calculation itself does not assert that this interval is macroscopically long.

## 3.2 Plus rows are exactly reused

For a plus edge `ell -> h`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
q^P_t
&=\frac{7(p+t)-(h+14t)+(\ell+7t)}6\\
&=\frac{7p-h+\ell}6
=q^P,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
u^P_t
&=(h+14t)-2(\ell+7t)-1\\
&=h-2\ell-1
=u^P.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The two characteristic-zero common indices of a plus row are

\[
X_P=7p-\ell-1,
\qquad
Y_P=14p-h-1.
\tag{3.5}
\]

They are fixed term by term:

\[
X_{P,t}=7(p+t)-(\ell+7t)-1=X_P,
\tag{3.6}
\]

\[
Y_{P,t}=14(p+t)-(h+14t)-1=Y_P.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus the two plus edges of AD or PD reuse:

- the same two upper primes;
- the same upper zero digits;
- the same four common Apéry indices; and
- the same stitched plus carrier.

A shifted lower prime is not automatically an actual source. It must still divide the fixed plus carrier; under the usual fixed-quotient unit exclusions, that divisibility recovers the shifted lower endpoint zeros by Lucas and reflection. The carrier has logarithmic height `O(T)`, so for one fixed fibre it gives at best

\[
\#\{p_t\text{ dividing the fixed carrier}\}
\ll \frac{T}{L},
\tag{3.8}
\]

which is the ordinary prime-counting scale of a length-`T` source interval. No current aggregate theorem controls the number or overlap of these stitched carriers strongly enough to turn (3.8) into a global power saving.

## 3.3 Minus rows move, rather than reuse

Every minus arrow in all three topologies runs from some `h in H` to some `ell in L`. Applying (1.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
q^M_t
&=\frac{8(p+t)-(h+14t)+(\ell+7t)}7\\
&=q^M+\frac t7,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.9}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
u^M_t
&=\frac{(p+t)-(h+14t)-6(\ell+7t)}7-1\\
&=u^M-\frac{55t}{7}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.10}
\]

The minus common indices are

\[
X_M=7p-h-1=6q_M+u_M,
\qquad
Y_M=p+\ell=q_M-1-u_M.
\tag{3.11}
\]

They transform as

\[
\boxed{X_{M,t}=X_M-7t,}
\qquad
\boxed{Y_{M,t}=Y_M+8t.}
\tag{3.12}
\]

Thus no fixed characteristic-zero minus carrier is reused.

To preserve integrality one may take `t in 7Z`; to preserve odd-prime parity and even prime gaps, `t in 14Z` suffices. The safe sublattice

\[
t=42r
\tag{3.13}
\]

also preserves the small modulus-`3`/`6` residue classes used by the q6 lattice. On this sublattice,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
p_r&=p+42r,\\
(q_P,u_P)_r&=(q_P,u_P),\\
q_{M,i,r}&=q_{M,i}+6r,\\
u_{M,i,r}&=u_{M,i}-330r.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.14}
\]

The weighted identity remains invariant:

\[
\begin{aligned}
7\bigl((q_{M,1}+6r)+(q_{M,2}+6r)\bigr)-6Q_P
&=2p+84r\\
&=2(p+42r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.15}
\]

Likewise, the formal AD same-sign parallelograms remain true: the plus states are fixed, and every `M+` candidate state translates by the same vector `(6r,-330r)`.

But actualness requires, among other things,

\[
q_{M,i}+6r\mid b_{u_{M,i}-330r},
\tag{3.16}
\]

as well as the shifted lower-zero conditions modulo `p+42r`. Reflection and Lucas operate at one fixed modulus; they do not transport (3.16) from `r=0` to another `r`. Therefore (3.14) is an exact **plus-source reuse symmetry**, not an actual-cycle translation theorem.

This is the second exact obstruction: the affine map has a one-dimensional kernel on the plus data, while the two missing minus selectedness conditions move together along that kernel.

# 4. Quantitative comparison with the C4 ceiling

The current C4-packing reduction gives, up to an absolute multiplicity constant,

\[
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}\nu_4(p)
\ll
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}Z(p)(Z(p)-1)
\ll_J T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The required unweighted scale is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}\nu_4(p)
=o_J(T^2/L^2).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently, since `log p` is comparable with `L` on the shell, the weighted cycle-excess target is

\[
\sum_p\nu_4(p)\log p=o_J(T^2/L).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The ratio of the present ceiling to the target is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}}{T^2L^{-2}}
=T^{1/5}L^{7/5}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence the missing improvement is a genuine power, not a logarithm.

## 4.1 The most generous prime-form sieve still misses by a power

Along the safe source fibre (3.14), the two plus upper primes are fixed and already prime. The moving primality conditions are at most the three affine forms

```text
p+42r,
q_(M,1)+6r,
q_(M,2)+6r.
```

If two minus forms coincide in a special orientation, the sieve dimension is smaller. Give the proposed route its strongest possible interpretation and grant an additional `L^{-3}` factor anyway. Applying it directly to the entire second-moment ceiling—already a stronger independence step than the current theorems justify—would yield only

\[
T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}L^{-3}
=T^{11/5}L^{-18/5}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

But

\[
\frac{T^{11/5}L^{-18/5}}{T^2L^{-2}}
=T^{1/5}L^{-8/5}\to\infty.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The moving upper selectedness conditions (3.16) are not primality conditions and cannot be assigned another independent logarithmic density without a new theorem.

More generally, even if a fixed collection of affine identities or carrier-height arguments supplied `A` additional logarithms, for any fixed `A`

\[
\frac{T^{11/5}L^{-3/5-A}}{T^2L^{-2}}
=T^{1/5}L^{7/5-A}\to\infty.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus no fixed-dimensional sieve can close (4.2).

## 4.2 Exact size of the missing arithmetic input

Relative to (4.1), reaching (4.2) requires a factor at most

\[
\boxed{
T^{-1/5}L^{-7/5}
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

and a little-o improvement beyond it. In qualitative terms, the missing theorem must save

\[
T^{1/5-o(1)}
\]

on the family of source-fibre completions.

A sufficient new input would be an ordered, topology-sensitive mixed completion estimate controlling the simultaneous events

\[
\begin{cases}
p+42r\text{ completes both fixed plus carriers},\\
q_{M,1}+6r\mid b_{u_{M,1}-330r},\\
q_{M,2}+6r\mid b_{u_{M,2}-330r},\\
\text{all shifted lower endpoint zeros and branch inequalities hold},
\end{cases}
\tag{4.9}
\]

with an average `T^{-1/5+o(1)}` power saving over the lower two-zero second-moment mass. This is a genuine cross-characteristic Apéry codegree/selectedness theorem. Neither the AD formal parallelograms, the PD weighted identity, nor the fixed plus stitched carrier proves it.

# Final obstruction

The requested route stops at the following exact point:

```text
AD1/AD2 same-sign q/u parallelogram:
  valid only as a formal identity joining complementary orientations;
  not four actual states of one C4.

PD q/u parallelogram:
  absent; the two minus edges are M- and M+.

Cycle-local affine information:
  exactly 7 sum(q_M) - 6 sum(q_P) = 2p;
  identically telescoping, hence no new codimension or divisor.

Source shift:
  freezes the two plus states and their stitched common indices;
  moves both minus selectedness conditions on one affine parameter.

Quantitative outcome:
  at best finitely many logarithmic sieve gains;
  the current deficit is T^(1/5) up to logarithms.
```

Therefore the ordered sign classification and all exact affine upper-state identities yield **no unconditional power saving beyond the existing `T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}` ceiling**. The first exact obstruction is the non-cycle-local scope of the AD parallelogram; the source-reuse kernel and the power-versus-log comparison show that repairing only the scope still would not reach `T^2/L^2`.
