# 1. Exact row maps and ordered topologies

Let `p` be an odd lower-shell prime and let

\[
0\le a<b<c<d<p
\]

be four actual lower `p`-zeros.

For a natural plus arrow from tail `x` to head `y`, the exact upper state is equivalently characterized by

\[
6q_P(x,y)=7p-y+x,
\qquad
u_P(x,y)=y-2x-1.
\tag{1.1P}
\]

For a natural minus arrow from tail `x` to head `y`, both inverse branches are covered by

\[
7q_M(x,y)=8p-x+y,
\qquad
7(u_M(x,y)+1)=p-x-6y.
\tag{1.1M}
\]

The branch label records the endpoint order:

```text
x<y  gives M-,
x>y  gives M+.
```

The three ordered surviving topologies are

```text
AD1:
  a --P--> c --M+--> b --P--> d --M+--> a

AD2:
  a --P--> d --M+--> b --P--> c --M+--> a

PD:
  a --P--> b --M---> c --P--> d --M+--> a
```

The PD minus rows are genuinely different inverse branches: `b -> c` is `M-`, while `d -> a` is `M+`.

For later use, the common indices of a plus row `x -> y` are

\[
X_P=7p-x-1,
\qquad
Y_P=14p-y-1,
\tag{1.2P}
\]

whereas the common indices of a minus row `x -> y` are

\[
X_M=7p-x-1,
\qquad
Y_M=p+y.
\tag{1.2M}
\]

# 2. First obstruction: the `q/u` parallelogram has the wrong scope

## 2.1 No individual AD1, AD2, or PD cycle has an opposite-edge parallelogram

Number the directed perimeter edges so that edges `1,3` are plus and edges `2,4` are minus. Put

\[
Q_P=q_1+q_3,
\qquad
U_P=u_1+u_3,
\]

\[
Q_M=q_2+q_4,
\qquad
U_M=u_2+u_4.
\]

Let `L_1,L_2` be the two plus tails and `H_1,H_2` the two plus heads, and set

\[
A=L_1+L_2,
\qquad
B=H_1+H_2.
\]

For AD1 and AD2,

\[
\{L_1,L_2\}=\{a,b\},
\qquad
\{H_1,H_2\}=\{c,d\}.
\tag{2.1AD}
\]

For PD, the correct alternating classes are

\[
\{L_1,L_2\}=\{a,c\},
\qquad
\{H_1,H_2\}=\{b,d\}.
\tag{2.1PD}
\]

In all three topologies, the minus arrows run from the `H` class back to the `L` class. Summing (1.1P) gives

\[
6Q_P=14p+A-B,
\qquad
U_P=B-2A-2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Summing (1.1M) gives

\[
7Q_M=16p+A-B,
\qquad
7(U_M+2)=2p-B-6A.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The ordinary upper-state parallelogram around one alternating C4 would require

\[
(q_1,u_1)+(q_3,u_3)=(q_2,u_2)+(q_4,u_4),
\tag{2.4}
\]

i.e. `Q_P=Q_M` and `U_P=U_M`.

From (2.2)--(2.3),

\[
\boxed{
42(Q_M-Q_P)=B-A-2p.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Since all endpoints lie in `[0,p)`, one has `B-A<2p`; hence the right side of (2.5) is nonzero.

Likewise,

\[
\boxed{
7(U_M-U_P)=2p-8(B-A).
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

If this vanished, then `p=4(B-A)`, impossible for odd `p`. Therefore neither the `q` coordinates nor the `u` coordinates close into an opposite-edge parallelogram for any individual AD1, AD2, or PD cycle.

This is the first exact obstruction to treating a cycle as a codimension-two affine upper-state rectangle.

## 2.2 The genuine AD parallelograms join AD1 to AD2

For a low endpoint `x in {a,b}` and a high endpoint `y in {c,d}`, let

```text
Psi_P(x,y)  = upper state of P(x -> y),
Psi_M(x,y)  = upper state of M+(y -> x).
```

Both maps are affine-separable in `x,y`. Hence, as identities in `Q^2`,

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_P(a,c)+\Psi_P(b,d)
=\Psi_P(a,d)+\Psi_P(b,c),
}
\tag{2.7P}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_M(a,c)+\Psi_M(b,d)
=\Psi_M(a,d)+\Psi_M(b,c).
}
\tag{2.7M}
\]

Their exact actual-row scope is

```text
AD1 plus diagonal:  P(a,c), P(b,d)
AD2 plus diagonal:  P(a,d), P(b,c)

AD1 M+ diagonal:    M+(c,b), M+(d,a)
AD2 M+ diagonal:    M+(d,b), M+(c,a)
```

Thus (2.7P) equates the AD1 plus diagonal with the AD2 plus diagonal, and (2.7M) equates the two complementary `M+` diagonals. A lone orientation supplies only one side of each identity.

Actualness of one orientation does not even force integrality of the opposite diagonal. For example, integrality of

```text
(p-c+a)/6,
(p-d+b)/6
```

implies only

\[
d-c\equiv b-a\pmod 6;
\]

it does not imply integrality of `(p-d+a)/6` or `(p-c+b)/6`. The analogous failure occurs modulo `7` for the complementary `M+` rows. Even when those candidates are integral, their primality and upper selectedness remain separate arithmetic conditions.

Therefore:

- **AD1 alone:** no all-actual `q/u` parallelogram;
- **AD2 alone:** no all-actual `q/u` parallelogram;
- **AD1 and AD2 both actual on the same quadruple:** both typed parallelograms are all-actual, but automatic;
- **PD:** no typed same-sign parallelogram at all.

If the C4 packing forgets signs/directions, AD1 and AD2 use the same four unordered endpoint pairs and cannot be charged as two edge-disjoint support cycles. If physical signed rows are retained, both orientations may coexist, but (2.7P)--(2.7M) still impose no rare equation.

# 3. Exact weighted `q` identity for AD and PD

For a plus natural arrow `x -> y`, (1.1P) gives

\[
y-x=7p-6q_P.
\tag{3.1P}
\]

For a minus natural arrow `x -> y`, (1.1M) gives

\[
y-x=7q_M-8p.
\tag{3.1M}
\]

The four directed displacements telescope around the perimeter. With two plus and two minus rows,

\[
(7p-6q_{P,1})+(7q_{M,1}-8p)
 +(7p-6q_{P,2})+(7q_{M,2}-8p)=0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
7(q_{M,1}+q_{M,2})
-6(q_{P,1}+q_{P,2})=2p.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Explicitly:

### AD1

\[
\boxed{
7\bigl(q^{M+}_{c\to b}+q^{M+}_{d\to a}\bigr)
-6\bigl(q^P_{a\to c}+q^P_{b\to d}\bigr)=2p.
}
\tag{3.3AD1}
\]

### AD2

\[
\boxed{
7\bigl(q^{M+}_{d\to b}+q^{M+}_{c\to a}\bigr)
-6\bigl(q^P_{a\to d}+q^P_{b\to c}\bigr)=2p.
}
\tag{3.3AD2}
\]

### PD

\[
\boxed{
7\bigl(q^{M-}_{b\to c}+q^{M+}_{d\to a}\bigr)
-6\bigl(q^P_{a\to b}+q^P_{c\to d}\bigr)=2p.
}
\tag{3.3PD}
\]

For AD set `S=a+b-c-d`; for PD set `S=a+c-b-d`. Equations (2.2)--(2.3) become

\[
6Q_P=14p+S,
\qquad
7Q_M=16p+S,
\tag{3.4}
\]

and subtraction gives (3.2).

This identity is useful bookkeeping, but it is not a new Diophantine restriction. After substituting the row maps, its left side minus `2p` is the zero polynomial obtained by telescoping endpoint differences. Consequently it cannot provide:

- a nonzero resultant;
- a second independent affine equation;
- a new prime-divisibility carrier; or
- an additional linear-form sieve condition.

It only reconstructs the already-selected lower prime `p` from the four upper primes.

# 4. Exact source-reuse shift

## 4.1 The endpoint classes are topology-dependent

The shift must use plus tails versus plus heads, not always the two numerically smallest versus the two largest vertices.

For AD1 and AD2,

\[
L=\{a,b\},
\qquad
H=\{c,d\}.
\tag{4.1AD}
\]

For PD,

\[
L=\{a,c\},
\qquad
H=\{b,d\}.
\tag{4.1PD}
\]

The PD distinction is load-bearing. Shifting sorted classes `{a,b}` and `{c,d}` would not freeze both PD plus rows.

For `ell in L` and `h in H`, put

\[
p_t=p+t,
\qquad
\ell_t=\ell+7t,
\qquad
h_t=h+14t.
\tag{4.2}
\]

All conclusions are restricted to values of `t` for which the shifted digits remain in `[0,p_t)`, the required endpoint order persists, and all branch inequalities remain legal. The affine calculation alone does not guarantee a long legal interval.

## 4.2 Plus states and common indices are fixed

For a plus edge `ell -> h`, direct substitution gives

\[
6q^P_t=7(p+t)-(h+14t)+(\ell+7t)=6q^P,
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
u^P_t=(h+14t)-2(\ell+7t)-1=u^P.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The plus common indices (1.2P) satisfy

\[
7(p+t)-(\ell+7t)-1=X_P,
\tag{4.5}
\]

\[
14(p+t)-(h+14t)-1=Y_P.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus both plus edges reuse the same two upper states, the same four common indices, and the same stitched plus carrier.

A shifted lower prime is not automatically actual. It must divide the fixed carrier; with the usual fixed-quotient unit exclusions, Lucas/reflection then recover the shifted lower zeros. A fixed carrier has logarithmic height `O(T)`, so one fibre gives at best `O(T/L)` distinct shell-prime divisors—the ordinary prime-counting scale of a length-`T` interval. No existing aggregate estimate controls the number or overlap of such stitched carriers strongly enough to produce a global power saving.

## 4.3 Minus states move

Every minus arrow runs from some `h in H` to some `ell in L`. Substitution into (1.1M) gives

\[
7(q^M_t-q^M)=t,
\tag{4.7}
\]

\[
7(u^M_t-u^M)=-55t.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The minus common indices (1.2M) satisfy

\[
X_{M,t}=X_M-7t,
\qquad
Y_{M,t}=Y_M+8t.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Hence no fixed minus carrier is reused.

Integrality is preserved on `t in 7Z`; odd-prime parity and even prime gaps are preserved on `t in 14Z`. The safe sublattice

\[
t=42r
\tag{4.10}
\]

also preserves the small modulus-`3`/`6` q6 residue classes. There

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
p_r&=p+42r,\\
(q_P,u_P)_r&=(q_P,u_P),\\
q_{M,i,r}&=q_{M,i}+6r,\\
u_{M,i,r}&=u_{M,i}-330r.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4.11}
\]

The weighted identity is invariant:

\[
7\bigl((q_{M,1}+6r)+(q_{M,2}+6r)\bigr)-6Q_P
=2(p+42r).
\tag{4.12}
\]

The formal AD parallelograms are also invariant: plus states are fixed and every `M+` candidate translates by the same vector `(6r,-330r)`.

Actualness, however, requires the moving selectedness conditions

\[
q_{M,i}+6r\mid b_{u_{M,i}-330r},
\tag{4.13}
\]

plus the shifted lower-zero conditions modulo `p+42r`. Reflection and Lucas operate at one fixed modulus and do not transport (4.13) from `r=0` to another `r`.

Therefore the shift is an exact **plus-source reuse symmetry**, not an actual-cycle translation theorem. It displays a one-dimensional kernel of the affine plus data, while the two missing minus selectedness conditions move together along that kernel.
