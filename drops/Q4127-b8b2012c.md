# 3. Reduction (A): proof of the fibre-one triple map

Define

\[
\Phi_p:

\[
\Phi_p(\{C_1,C_2\})=(z,\{x_1,x_2\}),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where \(x_i\) is defined by (2.6).

## 3.1 Legality and distinctness

Because \(f_i\) is a support edge in the lower-zero graph,

\[
x_i\in Z_p.
\]

Since \(C_i\) is simple,

\[
x_i\ne z.
\]

If \(x_1=x_2\), then the simple support edges \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) would both equal \(\{z,x_1\}\), contradicting support-edge-disjointness of the packing.  Hence

\[
z,x_1,x_2\quad\text{are pairwise distinct.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

## 3.2 Injectivity

Suppose

\[
\Phi_p(\{C_1,C_2\})
=
\Phi_p(\{C'_1,C'_2\}).
\]

Then the unordered sets of support edges

\[
\{\{z,x_1\},\{z,x_2\}\}
\]

are equal.  In one fixed support-edge-disjoint packing, every support edge belongs to at most one packed cycle.  Therefore those two edges recover the same unordered pair of indexed cycles:

\[
\{C_1,C_2\}=\{C'_1,C'_2\}.
\]

The canonical map \(C\mapsto r_C\) then recovers the selected rows and their common sign.  Thus \(\Phi_p\) is injective.  In particular, forgetting \(\sigma\) causes no factor \(2\): a second sign with the same endpoint triple would have to use the same two packed cycles, whose canonical selected rows already have fixed signs.

Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{C4\mathrm{sel}}(p)
&\le
\sum_{z\in Z_p}
\binom{Z(p)-1}{2}\\
&=
\frac12 Z(p)(Z(p)-1)(Z(p)-2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Summing over \(p\) proves (V1).  If the ledger is weighted by \(\log p\), the identical proof gives

\[
\widetilde W_{C4\mathrm{sel}}
\le
\frac12\sum_p(\log p)
Z(p)(Z(p)-1)(Z(p)-2).
\tag{3.4}
\]

## 3.3 Exact scope failure for an all-row energy

For an all-row ledger, two recovered cycles need not recover the two physical witnesses.  For fixed \(s=(p,z,\sigma)\), the fibre over one endpoint triple is exactly bounded by

\[
m_{C_1}(s)m_{C_2}(s).
\tag{3.5}

Thus a uniform cap \(m_C(s)\le\mu\) would give only

and without such a cap there is no pure zero-moment bound.  Indexed fibre-two occurrences must remain distinct even when they have the same numerical upper prime.

# 8. Exact comparison with minimum collision energy

For a full selector

\[
\phi:\mathcal P^{\rm deep}\to\mathcal S,
\qquad
\phi(C)\in N(C),

\[

\[
X_h(\phi):=\sum_s(n_s(\phi)-h)_+.
\tag{8.1}

## 8.1 Equality with minimum excess

For any \(\phi\), retaining at most \(h\) cycles from each star fibre gives a capacity-\(h\) matching covering

\[
\sum_s\min(n_s(\phi),h)
=|
\mathcal P^{\rm deep}|-X_h(\phi)

cycles.  Hence

\[
D_h(G)\le X_h(\phi).

Conversely, take a maximum capacity-\(h\) matching, which leaves exactly \(D_h(G)\) cycles unmatched.  Assign every unmatched cycle arbitrarily to one of its nonempty candidate stars. \[
X_h(\phi)\le D_h(G).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D_h(G)=\min_{\phi}X_h(\phi).

\tag{8.2}

For every integer \(k\ge0\) and \(h\ge1\),

\[
(k-h)_+
\le
\frac{\binom{k}{2}}{2h-1}.
\tag{8.3}

For \(k\ge h+1\), the difference factors as

\[
\binom{k}{2}-(2h-1)(k-h)

\frac{(k-(2h-1))(k-2h)}2

\tag{8.4}

# 10. Why the remaining six support edges give no stronger forced characteristic-zero carrier

There are three separate levels, and they should not be conflated.

## 10.1 Support-zero level

The collision pair supplies at least the three distinct zeros

\[
z,x_1,x_2.
\]

In fact the four distinct support edges at \(z\) supply four distinct neighbours of \(z\), so one can retain a richer five-zero tagged configuration.  But the smaller map (3.1) is already fibre one.  Adding the selected-edge endpoints cannot reduce a fibre below one; it only enlarges the ambient zero tuple and asks for a higher factorial moment.  Thus the triple carrier is the optimal support-level compression furnished by edge-disjointness alone.

## 10.2 Physical-row level

The other six support edges carry the variable occurrence family (2.3).  Any carrier that chooses one physical witness from \(k\) of those fibres acquires the corresponding product of fibre sizes.  If it records only that a witness exists, it forgets indexed row multiplicity and cannot pay an occurrence-sensitive sum.

This is exactly why the Hall graph uses cycles as left vertices, deduplicates all row witnesses producing the same candidate star, and selects one witness only after the capacitated matching has been found.  A raw pair or tuple moment multiplies one cycle's marginal across many partner rows; Hall deficiency does not.

## 10.3 Characteristic-zero coefficient level

A physical support edge supplies its own exact pair of Apéry coefficient slots.  Even for one \(C_4\), the literal stitched support consists in general of eight distinct slots rather than four cyclically shared values. ```text
