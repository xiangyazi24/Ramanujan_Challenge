# 1. Exact object and ranges

Fix a real smooth function

\[
v\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty)),
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}v\subset[\alpha,\beta]
\quad(0<\alpha<\beta<\infty).
\]

For the Farey consumer one takes `v >= 0`, as in Q4507. Put

\[
\mathscr P_N=\{p\text{ prime}:v(p/N)\ne0\},
\qquad
u_p=\frac{\log p}{p}v(p/N),
\qquad
M_N=|\mathscr P_N|.
\]

For each integer `r >= 0`, define

\[
P_N(r)=\{p\in\mathscr P_N:r<p,\ p\mid b_r\},
\qquad
m_N(r)=|P_N(r)|.
\]

The restriction `r<p` is part of the definition. Since

\[
\mathcal Z_p=\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\},
\]

we have the exact incidence identities

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}m_N(r)
=\sum_{p\in\mathscr P_N}Z(p)=:A_N,
\tag{1.1}
\]

and, for distinct primes,

\[
|\mathcal Z_p\cap\mathcal Z_q|
=\#\{r<\min(p,q):p q\mid b_r\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

All nonempty columns lie in

\[
0\le r<\beta N.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The common-index moment is

\[
\mathcal H_N(v)
=\sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathscr P_N\\p\ne q}}
 \nu_p\nu_q\,|\mathcal Z_p\cap\mathcal Z_q|.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Expanding in the integer index gives the exact **weighted** identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_N(v)
=\sum_{r\ge0}
 \sum_{\substack{p,q\in P_N(r)\\p\ne q}}\nu_p\nu_q
=\sum_{r\ge0}\left[
 \left(\sum_{p\in P_N(r)}\nu_p\right)^2
 -\sum_{p\in P_N(r)}\nu_p^2
 \right].}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The corresponding unweighted ordered-pair identity is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r\ge0}m_N(r)(m_N(r)-1)
=\sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathscr P_N\\p\ne q}}
 |\mathcal Z_p\cap\mathcal Z_q|.}
\tag{1.6}
\]

Thus `H_N(v)` is not literally equal to (1.6) unless the weights are constant. What is always true, and is the step used in Q4524, is

\[
|\mathcal H_N(v)|
\le U_N^2\sum_r m_N(r)(m_N(r)-1),
\qquad
U_N:=\max_{p\in\mathscr P_N}|\nu_p|
\ll_v\frac{\log N}{N}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

For `v >= 0`, `\mathcal H_N(v) >= 0` as well.

# 3. Audit of the metric codegree deduction

Define, for distinct ordinary positions,

\[
c_N(r,s)=|P_N(r)\cap P_N(s)|.

## Lemma 3.1 — uniform short metric codegree

There are constants `C_v,c_v>0` such that, for all sufficiently large `N`,

\[
\boxed{
1\le s-r\le c_v\frac{N}{\log N}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_N(r,s)\le C_v(s-r).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof and quantifier audit

Partition the fixed prime box `[\alpha N,\beta N]` into a fixed number of **disjoint** pieces, each contained in a dyadic interval `(X_j,2X_j]`, with

\[
X_j\asymp_v N.
\]

Put `h=s-r`. For the displayed range and large `N`,

\[
h<X_j,
\qquad
0\le r<s<\beta N<X_j^2.
\]

Therefore `thm:atomtail-twin` applies literally to `(m,m')=(r,s)` in every block. Every prime counted by `P_N(r)\cap P_N(s)` is counted by the corresponding `W_{X_j}(r,s)`, so

\[
c_N(r,s)
\le\sum_jW_{X_j}(r,s)
\ll_v h.
\]

There is no wrap-around issue hidden here. Indeed, for a prime in `P_N(r)\cap P_N(s)` one has `r,s<p` by definition, so this is actually the source dichotomy's type-(A) sector. The looser `W_X` bound is still valid and sufficient. The finite dyadic split contributes only a constant depending on `v`, not a logarithm of `N`. This proves (3.1).

# 4. Audit of the high-column lemma

For an integer `t>=1`, put

\[
S_t=\{0\le r<\beta N:m_N(r)\ge t\}.
\]

## Lemma 4.1 — high horizontal columns

There is a constant `C_v'>0` such that, for

\[
t\ge C_v'\sqrt{M_N},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
|S_t|\ll_v\frac{N M_N^2}{t^3}+\frac{M_N}{t}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The set is empty for `t>M_N`.

## Proof

Let `C_0` be a constant in (3.1), and choose

\[
L_t=\left\lfloor\frac{t^2}{8C_0M_N}\right\rfloor.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Increase `C_v'` so that the number inside the floor is at least one. Then

\[
L_t\ge\frac{t^2}{16C_0M_N}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If `S_t` is nonempty then `t<=M_N`, and hence

\[
L_t\le\frac{M_N}{8C_0}\ll_v\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the metric codegree bound applies to every pair in an interval of `L_t` consecutive integers.

Fix one such interval `I`, put

\[
S=S_t\cap I,
\qquad s=|S|,
\]

and for each prime `p\in\mathscr P_N` define

\[
z_p=\#\{r\in S:p\in P_N(r)\},
\qquad
E=\sum_pz_p=\sum_{r\in S}m_N(r).
\]

Because every `r\in S` has degree at least `t`,

\[
E\ge ts.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz over the `M_N` primes gives

\[
\sum_pz_p(z_p-1)
=\sum_pz_p^2-E
\ge\frac{E^2}{M_N}-E.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The same quantity has the exact ordered-pair expansion

\[
\sum_pz_p(z_p-1)
=\sum_{\substack{r,r'\in S\\r\ne r'}}c_N(r,r').
\tag{4.7}
\]

There is no missing factor `2`: both sides count ordered triples `(p,r,r')` with `r\ne r'` and `p\in P_N(r)\cap P_N(r')`.

Since the positions lie in one interval,

\[
|r-r'|<L_t,
\]

so (3.1) and (4.2) give

\[
\sum_pz_p(z_p-1)
\le C_0L_t s(s-1)
\le\frac{t^2s^2}{8M_N}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Suppose `E>=2M_N`. Then (4.6) is at least

\[
\frac{E^2}{2M_N}
\ge\frac{t^2s^2}{2M_N},
\]

contradicting (4.8). Therefore

\[
E<2M_N,
\qquad
s<\frac{2M_N}{t}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Partition `[0,\beta N)` into intervals of `L_t` consecutive integers. By (4.3), their number is

\[
\ll_v\frac{NM_N}{t^2}+1.
\]

Multiplying by (4.9) proves

\[
|S_t|\ll_v
\left(\frac{NM_N}{t^2}+1\right)\frac{M_N}{t}
\ll_v\frac{NM_N^2}{t^3}+\frac{M_N}{t}.
\]

## Edge-case audit

### Floor effects

The threshold `t>=C_v' sqrt(M_N)` ensures the floor in (4.2) is at least one. For every real `x>=1`, `floor(x)>=x/2`, which gives (4.3). No zero-length interval occurs.

### Interval cover

The intervals are a partition, not an overlapping cover. The last interval may be shorter; this only improves (4.8). The count is exactly `ceil(beta N/L_t)` and has the stated order.

### Isolated saturated columns

The argument does not exclude one position carrying all `M_N` primes. For `t=M_N`, the term `M_N/t` in (4.1) permits such a column. Hence no hidden anti-star or randomness hypothesis is present.

### The case `M_N=0`

Every quantity vanishes. For `M_N>0`, all divisions above are legitimate.

### The case `t>M_N`

`S_t` is empty by definition. The layer-cake sum stops at `M_N`.

The high-column lemma is therefore valid as stated.

# 5. Audit of the layer-cake estimate

Set

\[
B_N=\sum_{0\le r<\beta N}m_N(r)^2.
\]

The exact discrete layer-cake identity is

\[
\boxed{
B_N=\sum_{t=1}^{M_N}(2t-1)|S_t|,}
\tag{5.1}
\]

because `m^2=sum_{t=1}^m(2t-1)`.

Choose

\[
T=N^{7/10}(\log N)^{-1/2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Integer floors or ceilings change nothing below.

## Low levels

For every `t`, first-moment Markov gives

\[
t|S_t|\le A_N.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{t\le T}(2t-1)|S_t|
\le2A_NT.
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is an unweighted column-counting use of Markov; there is no Palm size-bias issue in this problem.

## The case `M_N<T`

Then the whole layer-cake sum is low-level, and more directly

\[
B_N\le M_NA_N<TA_N.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the high-column lemma is not invoked outside its range.

## High levels when `M_N>=T`

From (2.3),

\[
\frac{T}{\sqrt{M_N}}\gg_v N^{1/5},
\]

so for large `N` every integer `t>T` satisfies the threshold in Lemma 4.1. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{T<t\le M_N}(2t-1)|S_t|
&\ll_v NM_N^2\sum_{t>T}\frac1{t^2}
     +M_N\sum_{T<t\le M_N}1\\
&\ll_v\frac{NM_N^2}{T}+M_N^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Combining (5.4)--(5.6), then inserting (2.2), (2.3), and (5.2), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_NT
&\ll_v
\frac{N^{8/5}}{\log N}
\frac{N^{7/10}}{(\log N)^{1/2}}
=rac{N^{23/10}}{(\log N)^{3/2}},\\
\frac{NM_N^2}{T}
&\ll_v
\frac{N^3}{(\log N)^2}
\frac{(\log N)^{1/2}}{N^{7/10}}
=rac{N^{23/10}}{(\log N)^{3/2}},\\
M_N^2&\ll_v\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
B_N=\sum_r m_N(r)^2
\ll_v\frac{N^{23/10}}{(\log N)^{3/2}}.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

All exponents and logarithms in Q4524 are correct.

A useful abstract form of the calculation is

\[
B_N\ll_v A_NT+\frac{NM_N^2}{T}+M_N^2,
\]

valid for any admissible cutoff above the high-column threshold. The chosen `T` balances the first two terms using the canonical bounds.

\label{ssec:ad-common-index}

Fix a real function $v\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty))$, with
$\operatorname{supp}v\subset[\alpha,\beta]$ for some
$0<\alpha<\beta<\infty$.  Put

 \mathscr P_N=\{p\text{ prime}:v(p/N)\ne0\},\qquad

\[
 A_N=\sum_{r\ge0}m_N(r)=\sum_{p\in\mathscr P_N}Z(p).

 \mathcal H_N(v)

\begin{lemma}[Short horizontal codegree]
\label{lem:ad-common-index-codegree}
There is a constant $C_v$ such that, for all sufficiently large $N$,
\[
 |P_N(r)\cap P_N(s)|\le C_v|r-s|
\]
whenever
\[
 0\le r<s<\beta N,
 \qquad 1\le s-r\ll_v N/\log N.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Partition $[\alpha N,\beta N]$ into $O_v(1)$ disjoint pieces,
each contained in a dyadic interval $(X_j,2X_j]$ with
$X_j\asymp_vN$.  Put $h=s-r$.  In the displayed range,
$h<X_j$ and $r,s<X_j^2$ for all sufficiently large $N$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:atomtail-twin}, applied with $(m,m')=(r,s)$,
gives at most $O(h)$ primes from each dyadic piece.  Summing the
fixed number of pieces proves the claim.  Notice that a prime in
$P_N(r)\cap P_N(s)$ has $r,s<p$, so only the nonwrapping case of
the source dichotomy can occur.
\end{proof}

For $t\ge1$ put
\[
 S_t=\{0\le r<\beta N:m_N(r)\ge t\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[High horizontal columns]
\label{lem:ad-common-index-tail}
Uniformly for $t\ge C_v\sqrt{M_N}$,
\[
 |S_t|\ll_v\frac{NM_N^2}{t^3}+\frac{M_N}{t}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $C_0$ be a constant in
Lemma~\ref{lem:ad-common-index-codegree} and put
\[
 L_t=\left\lfloor\frac{t^2}{8C_0M_N}\right\rfloor.
\]
Increasing the threshold constant makes $L_t\ge1$, and then
$L_t\ge t^2/(16C_0M_N)$.  If $S_t\ne\varnothing$, then
$t\le M_N$, so $L_t\ll_v N/\log N$ and the short-codegree lemma
applies inside every interval of $L_t$ consecutive integers.

Fix such an interval $I$, write $S=S_t\cap I$ and $s=|S|$, and put
\[
 z_p=\#\{r\in S:p\in P_N(r)\},

\]

\[
 \sum_pz_p(z_p-1)

\]
On the other hand, ordered-pair expansion and the codegree lemma give
\[

$O_v(N/L_t+1)=O_v(NM_N/t^2+1)$ intervals, which proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Horizontal common-index moment]
\label{prop:ad-common-index-moment}
One has
\[
 \sum_{r\ge0}m_N(r)^2
 \ll_v\frac{N^{23/10}}{(\log N)^{3/2}}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathcal H_N(v)|
 \ll_v N^{3/10}(\log N)^{1/2}.}
\]
For $v\ge0$, the absolute-value signs on $\mathcal H_N(v)$ may be omitted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The unconditional first zero moment and Chebyshev's estimate give
\[
 A_N\ll_v\frac{N^{8/5}}{\log N},
 \qquad M_N\ll_v\frac{N}{\log N}.
\]
The layer-cake identity is
\[
 \sum_rm_N(r)^2=\sum_{t=1}^{M_N}(2t-1)|S_t|.
\]
Take $T=N^{7/10}(\log N)^{-1/2}$.  For $t\le T$,
$t|S_t|\le A_N$, and hence the low levels contribute $O(A_NT)$.
If $M_N<T$, this handles the entire sum.  Otherwise
$T\gg_v\sqrt{M_N}$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:ad-common-index-tail} gives
\[
 \sum_{T<t\le M_N}(2t-1)|S_t|
 \ll_v\frac{NM_N^2}{T}+M_N^2.
\]
Both $A_NT$ and $NM_N^2/T$ are
$O_v(N^{23/10}(\log N)^{-3/2})$, while $M_N^2$ is smaller.
This proves the first assertion.

The exact weighted identity is
\[
 \mathcal H_N(v)
 =\sum_r\sum_{\substack{p,q\in P_N(r)\\p\ne q}}u_pu_q.
\]
Since $\max_{p\in\mathscr P_N}|u_p|\ll_v(\log N)/N$,
\[
 |\mathcal H_N(v)|
 \le\frac{C_v(\log N)^2}{N^2}
      \sum_rm_N(r)(m_N(r)-1),
\]
and the result follows from the first assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Deletion of the equal-index Farey subdiagonal]
\label{cor:ad-common-index-band}

\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
