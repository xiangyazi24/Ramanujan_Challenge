The observation is correct and admits an exact algebraic saturation theorem.
Let

\[
 t_k(n)=\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}k^2,
 \qquad
 S_K(n)=\sum_{k=0}^K t_k(n),
 \]

and put

\[
 H=\left\lfloor\frac n3\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 L=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_n=\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n\}.
\]

Define the top-half primorial and the lower subinterval primorial

\[
 U_n=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}p,
 \qquad
 V_n=\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_n\\p\le2H+1}}p.
\]

The main conclusions are as follows.

1. **Exact universal congruence.** For every \(H\le K\le L\),
   \[
   S_K(n)\equiv S_H(n)\pmod{U_n^2}.
   \]
   Thus the entire long block has only one free residue coordinate modulo every candidate prime squared.

2. **Exact tail valuations.** If \(p\in\mathcal P_n\), then
   \[
   \min_{H<k\le L}v_p(t_k(n))
   =
   \begin{cases}
   4,&p\le2H+1,\\
   2,&p>2H+1.
   \end{cases}
   \]
   Hence the top-half part of the ordinary tail gcd is exactly
   \[
   U_n^2V_n^2.
   \]

3. **Exact Smith saturation.** After dividing the successive differences by their maximal universal top-prime content while retaining one copy of every candidate prime, the tail ideal is exactly \((U_n)\). Adding the prefix coordinate gives
   \[
   (S_H,\text{saturated differences})=(S_H,U_n)
   \]
   in the semilocal ring at the primes in \(\mathcal P_n\). Consequently its single Smith invariant is
   \[
   R_n:=\gcd(S_H,U_n)
   =\prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_{n-p}}}p.
   \]
   Locally at a candidate prime, the invariant is \(p\) for a target and \(1\) for a non-target. There is no second selective elementary divisor.

4. **Unavoidable linear tail factor.** Any tail-only order-\(K\) construction obtained from the saturated tail ideal lies in \((U_n^K)\), and therefore every nonzero such integer satisfies
   \[
   \log|C_n|\ge K\log U_n
   =\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)Kn.
   \]
   Thus the requested \(o(Kn)\) height is impossible for a tail-only carrier, independently of the choice of finite differences, Bézout coefficients, minors, or exterior powers.

5. **One genuine positive carrier, but with linear height.** A normalized adjacent Hankel/Dodgson minor gives an explicit nonzero integer divisible by every target prime. For the first admissible index it has
   \[
   \log|C_n^{\rm adj}|=n\log16+O(\log n),
   \]
   so it is a real selective certificate but not a height improvement. Products give target order \(K\) with height \(\Theta(Kn)\), not \(o(Kn)\).

6. **No bounded hypergeometric boundary collapse.** The summand has the exact first-order ratio
   \[
   \frac{t_{k+1}}{t_k}
   =\left(\frac{(n-k)(n+k+1)}{(k+1)^2}\right)^2.
   \]
   There is no rational Gosper certificate over \(\mathbf Q(n)(k)\) telescoping the unweighted sum to \(O(1)\) boundary values. For fixed integer \(n\), any rational certificate, if it exists at all, must have a denominator containing a shift chain of length at least \(n\). Therefore every such bespoke boundary elimination has linear degree before heights are considered.

7. **No cross-prime consequence from the long block.** For any prescribed subset \(T\subseteq\mathcal P_n\), CRT produces a positive fake prefix block with the *actual* successive differences \(t_{H+1},\ldots,t_L\), the exact second-order prefix recurrence, all the same universal valuations and derivative data, but with precisely the primes in \(T\) dividing every prefix. Hence the long-block structure by itself permits an arbitrary target column.

The net result is therefore a sharp saturation theorem, not the desired sublinear carrier. The only remaining selective quantity is the early-prefix residue itself:

\[
 \boxed{
 \log\gcd\!\left(S_{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}(n),
                 \prod_{n/2<p\le n}p\right)=o(n).
 }
\]

This is exactly the top-half target-radical theorem in a new prefix form. The long tail supplies no additional codimension or height gain.

## 1. Exact valuation of one Apéry summand at a top-half prime

It is useful to take the integral square root of the summand:

\[
 q_k(n):=\binom nk\binom{n+k}k
 =\frac{(n+k)!}{k!^2(n-k)!},
 \qquad t_k(n)=q_k(n)^2.
\]

Fix \(p\in\mathcal P_n\). Since \(p>n/2\), write uniquely

\[
 n=p+r,
 \qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

For every \(k\le L<p\), the denominator \(k!^2\) is a \(p\)-unit and

\[
 q_k(n)
 =\frac1{k!^2}\prod_{j=n-k+1}^{n+k}j.
\]

The interval \([n-k+1,n+k]\) has length \(2k<2p\), so it contains at most the two multiples \(p\) and \(2p\). They occur under the exact conditions

\[
 p\in[n-k+1,n+k]
 \iff k>r,
\]

and

\[
 2p\in[n-k+1,n+k]
 \iff k>p-1-r.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 v_p(q_k(n))
 =\mathbf1_{\{k>r\}}+\mathbf1_{\{k>p-1-r\}},
 }
\tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 v_p(t_k(n))
 =2\mathbf1_{\{k>r\}}+2\mathbf1_{\{k>p-1-r\}}.
 }
\tag{1.2}
\]

This is an equality, not merely a lower bound.

Put

\[
 m_p=\min(r,p-1-r),
 \qquad
 M_p=\max(r,p-1-r).
\]

Then

\[
 v_p(t_k)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&k\le m_p,\\
 2,&m_p<k\le M_p,\\
 4,&k>M_p.
 \end{cases}
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Exact nonvanishing of the normalized derivative

Formula (1.1) also settles the proposed first-derivative refinement. If

\[
 \alpha_p(k)=v_p(q_k(n)),
\]

then

\[
 \frac{q_k(n)}{p^{\alpha_p(k)}}
 \equiv
 2^{\mathbf1_{\{k>p-1-r\}}}
 \frac{
  \displaystyle\prod_{\substack{n-k+1\le j\le n+k\\j\notin\{p,2p\}}}j
 }{(k!)^2}
 \pmod p.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Every factor on the right is a \(p\)-unit. Hence

\[
 \frac{q_k(n)}{p^{\alpha_p(k)}}\not\equiv0\pmod p,
 \qquad
 \frac{t_k(n)}{p^{2\alpha_p(k)}}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Equivalently, regarding the numerator product as a polynomial in the outer variable, the reduction at \(n\equiv r\pmod p\) has root multiplicity exactly \(\alpha_p(k)\), and the square has root multiplicity exactly \(2\alpha_p(k)\). There is no hidden extra derivative zero available for selectivity.

## 2. Why the folded index is always reached by \(H=\lfloor n/3\rfloor\)

The claimed bound \(m_p\le H\) is floor-safe.

If \(r\le H\), it is immediate. If \(r\ge H+1\), then

\[
 p-1-r=n-2r-1
 \le n-2(H+1)-1.
\]

Since \(n\le3H+2\), the right side is at most \(H-1\). Thus in all cases

\[
 \boxed{m_p\le H.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

For \(k\le m_p\), Lucas reduction applies to both binomial factors and gives

\[
 t_k(n)\equiv t_k(r)\pmod p.
\]

For \(k>m_p\), equation (1.2) gives \(p^2\mid t_k(n)\). If \(m_p<r\), then the corresponding terms of \(b_r\) also vanish modulo \(p\), because \(r+k\ge p\) makes \(\binom{r+k}{k}\) divisible by \(p\). Consequently, for every \(K\) with \(m_p\le K<p\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 S_K(n)
 &\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{m_p}t_k(n)\\
 &\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{m_p}t_k(r)\\
 &\equiv b_r\pmod p.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 p\mid b_{n-p}
 \iff p\mid S_H(n)
 \iff p\mid S_K(n)\quad(H\le K\le L).
 }
\tag{2.3}
\]

The boundary case \(n=2p-1\) has \(r=p-1\), \(m_p=0\), and (2.2) gives
\(S_H\equiv b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p\), so it is automatically non-target.

## 3. The exact universal square and the ordinary tail gcd

For every \(p\in\mathcal P_n\) and every \(H<k\le L\), (2.1) and (1.2) give

\[
 p^2\mid t_k(n).
\]

Since the primes are distinct,

\[
 \boxed{U_n^2\mid t_k(n)\qquad(H<k\le L).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

It follows at once that

\[
 \boxed{
 S_K(n)\equiv S_H(n)\pmod{U_n^2}
 \qquad(H\le K\le L).
 }
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is the global version of the local statement that every target prefix has the same quotient modulo \(p\).

### Exact minimum valuations

At the first tail index \(k=H+1\), both inequalities in (1.1) hold exactly when

\[
 r\le H
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 p-1-r\le H.
\]

Their sum is \(p-1\), so this is equivalent to

\[
 p\le2H+1.
\]

Since the valuation is nondecreasing in \(k\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \mu_p:=\min_{H<k\le L}v_p(t_k)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 4,&p\le2H+1,\\
 2,&p>2H+1.
 \end{cases}
 }
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let

\[
 \Gamma_n=\gcd(t_{H+1},t_{H+2},\ldots,t_L).
\]

Then the exact part supported on \(\mathcal P_n\) is

\[
 \boxed{
 (\Gamma_n)_{\mathcal P_n}=U_n^2V_n^2.
 }
\tag{3.4}
\]

The prime number theorem gives the height ledger

\[
 \log U_n
 =\vartheta(n)-\vartheta(n/2)
 =\frac n2+o(n),
\tag{3.5}
\]

\[
 \log V_n
 =\vartheta(2H+1)-\vartheta(n/2)
 =\frac n6+o(n),
\tag{3.6}
\]

and hence

\[
 \log (U_n^2V_n^2)
 =\frac{4n}{3}+o(n).
\tag{3.7}
\]

This explains quantitatively why the unnormalized tail gcd is worse than the single top-half primorial.

## 4. Exact Smith normal form after maximal universal normalization

Let

\[
 \mathcal O_n
 =\mathbb Z\bigl[\,a^{-1}:\gcd(a,U_n)=1\,\bigr].
\]

This is the semilocal PID whose maximal ideals are the \((p)\) with
\(p\in\mathcal P_n\).

The lower-bidiagonal difference matrix is unimodular over \(\mathbb Z\), and sends

\[
 (S_H,S_{H+1},\ldots,S_L)
\]

to

\[
 (S_H,t_{H+1},\ldots,t_L).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus ordinary finite differencing does not change the generated ideal.

### A common explicit saturation

Put

\[
 W_n=U_nV_n^2.
\]

By (3.3), \(W_n\mid t_k\) for every \(H<k\le L\). Define

\[
 d_k=\frac{t_k}{W_n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

At \(k=H+1\), equation (3.3) gives, for every \(p\in\mathcal P_n\),

\[
 v_p(d_{H+1})=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Hence \(d_{H+1}/U_n\) is a unit of \(\mathcal O_n\), and therefore

\[
 (d_{H+1},\ldots,d_L)\mathcal O_n
 =U_n\mathcal O_n.
\tag{4.4}
\]

In fact one normalized difference already generates the saturated tail ideal.

### Per-term maximal saturation

One may also remove the exact universal content term by term. For \(H<k\le L\), define

\[
 W_{n,k}
 =\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}p^{v_p(t_k)-1},
 \qquad
 e_k=\frac{t_k}{W_{n,k}}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Formula (1.2) makes \(W_{n,k}\) completely explicit, and

\[
 v_p(e_k)=1
 \qquad(p\in\mathcal P_n).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus this more aggressive normalization has the same tail ideal \((U_n)\).
Dividing by one additional copy of any candidate prime makes the corresponding normalized difference a unit and therefore destroys, rather than strengthens, the target divisor.

### The Smith theorem

Let

\[
 I_n=(S_H,d_{H+1},\ldots,d_L)\mathcal O_n.
\]

By (4.4),

\[
 I_n=(S_H,U_n)\mathcal O_n.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since \(U_n\) is squarefree and (2.3) identifies its prime divisors which divide \(S_H\),

\[
 \boxed{
 I_n=R_n\mathcal O_n,
 \qquad
 R_n=\prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_{n-p}}}p.
 }
\tag{4.8}
\]

Equivalently, the row matrix formed by the prefix and saturated differences has Smith form

\[
 [R_n,0,\ldots,0]
\]

over \(\mathcal O_n\).

Locally, for \(p\in\mathcal P_n\),

\[
 I_n\otimes\mathbb Z_p
 =
 \begin{cases}
  \mathbb Z_p,&p\nmid b_{n-p},\\
  p\mathbb Z_p,&p\mid b_{n-p}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This remains true even if \(p^2\mid S_H\), because the saturated difference has valuation exactly one. Thus the primitive ideal is automatically squarefree in the candidate interval.

For comparison, without saturation the ordinary prefix ideal has local valuation

\[
 v_p\bigl((S_H,\ldots,S_L)\bigr)
 =\min\{v_p(S_H),\mu_p\},
\tag{4.10}
\]

with \(\mu_p\) from (3.3). Those possible higher powers are not independent target conditions; they are capped by the universal tail multiplicity.

## 5. Why finite differences and Bézout coefficients add no invariant

Every higher finite difference satisfies

\[
 \Delta^jS_K=\Delta^{j-1}t_{K+1}.
\]

It is therefore an integral linear combination of the first differences. Adding all higher differences leaves the ordinary ideal unchanged. After universal saturation, the tail ideal is already principal and equal to \((U_n)\); no higher-difference matrix can create a second elementary divisor.

Bézout likewise gives an exact but tautological conclusion. From (4.4), there are integers, after clearing factors prime to \(U_n\), whose combination of the normalized differences equals \(U_n\). Adding \(S_H\) permits a Bézout combination equal to

\[
 \gcd(S_H,U_n)=R_n.
\]

This does not bound \(R_n\). Producing that Bézout combination is equivalent to computing the desired primitive ideal generator, and proving its logarithm is \(o(n)\) is exactly the original target-radical problem.

### Higher target order

The saturated tail ideal is \((U_n)\), so its \(K\)-th power is exactly

\[
 (U_n)^K=(U_n^K).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every nonzero tail-only order-\(K\) carrier therefore obeys

\[
 \log|C_n|
 \ge K\log U_n
 =\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)Kn.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is an unconditional impossibility result for the requested
\(o(Kn)\) height within the tail ideal.

Once \(S_H\) is admitted, the selective ideal is \((R_n)\) and its
\(K\)-th power is \((R_n^K)\). There is still only one local target coordinate. Any proof that a generator of this ideal has \(o(Kn)\) logarithmic height is precisely a proof that \(\log R_n=o(n)\); the long block has supplied no extra rank.

## 6. The exact coefficient-index recurrence and its free additive constant

The hypergeometric ratio is

\[
 \boxed{
 (k+1)^4t_{k+1}
 =(n-k)^2(n+k+1)^2t_k.
 }
\tag{6.1}
\]

Put

\[
 A_k=(n-k)^2(n+k+1)^2,
 \qquad
 B_k=(k+1)^4.
\]

Since \(t_k=S_k-S_{k-1}\), equation (6.1) is equivalent to the exact second-order prefix recurrence

\[
 \boxed{
 B_kS_{k+1}-(A_k+B_k)S_k+A_kS_{k-1}=0.
 }
\tag{6.2}
\]

The coefficient sum is zero, so the constant sequence is a solution. Hence

\[
 S_k\longmapsto S_k+C
\tag{6.3}
\]

preserves the recurrence and every successive difference.

This identifies the structural obstruction: the long tail determines the prefix sequence only up to one additive constant, and the complete target set is encoded in that single constant modulo \(U_n\).

### Arbitrary-subset CRT countermodel

Let \(T\subseteq\mathcal P_n\) be arbitrary. By CRT choose an integer \(X_T\), with \(1\le X_T\le U_n^2\), such that

\[
 X_T\equiv
 \begin{cases}
  p\pmod{p^2},&p\in T,\\
  1\pmod{p^2},&p\notin T.
 \end{cases}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Define on the long block

\[
 \widetilde S_H=X_T,
 \qquad
 \widetilde S_K
 =X_T+\sum_{j=H+1}^K t_j(n)
 \quad(H<K\le L).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Then:

- \(\widetilde S_K-\widetilde S_{K-1}=t_K(n)\) exactly;
- the exact recurrence (6.2) holds, because \(\widetilde S-S\) is constant;
- all finite differences and all normalized derivative data are the actual ones;
- every \(\widetilde S_K\) is positive;
- for every \(p\in T\), all prefixes have valuation exactly one;
- for every \(p\notin T\), all prefixes are \(p\)-units.

Thus every subset of the candidate primes is compatible with the complete long-block structure. This is not a counterexample to the actual Apéry early prefix; it proves that no argument using only the long block, its recurrence, its differences, universal content, or local derivatives can produce a forbidden target configuration or cross-prime moment bound. The missing input must use the arithmetic value of the integration constant \(S_H\).

## 7. No bounded Gosper/WZ boundary telescoper

A hoped-for q-binomial or finite-difference escape would be a rational function \(R(n,k)\) satisfying

\[
 t_k=\Delta_k(R(n,k)t_k),
\]

or equivalently

\[
 R(k+1)\rho(k)-R(k)=1,
\tag{7.1}
\]

where

\[
 \rho(k)=\frac{t_{k+1}}{t_k}
 =\frac{(N-k(k+1))^2}{(k+1)^4},
 \qquad N=n(n+1).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Such an identity would collapse every prefix to two hypergeometric boundary terms. It does not exist.

### Lemma 7.1

There is no \(R\in\mathbf Q(n)(k)\) satisfying (7.1).

### Proof: poles

Suppose \(R\) has a pole. In one translation orbit, choose a terminal pole \(\alpha\), meaning \(\alpha+1\) is not a pole. At \(k=\alpha\), the right side of

\[
 R(k+1)\rho(k)=R(k)+1
\]

has a pole, while \(R(k+1)\) is regular. Hence \(\rho\) must have a pole at \(\alpha\). Its only pole is \(k=-1\), so every terminal pole is \(-1\).

Choose an initial pole \(\beta\), meaning \(\beta-1\) is not a pole. At \(k=\beta-1\), the factor \(R(k+1)\) has a pole while the right side is regular. Therefore \(\rho(\beta-1)=0\). Its zeros are

\[
 k=n,
 \qquad k=-n-1.
\]

Thus

\[
 \beta=n+1
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \beta=-n.
\]

But in \(\mathbf Q(n)\), neither point lies in the same integral translation orbit as \(-1\). This contradicts the existence of the finite pole chain. Hence \(R\) is a polynomial in \(k\).

### Proof: degree

At infinity,

\[
 \rho(k)=1-\frac2k+O(k^{-2}).
\]

If \(R(k)\sim ck^d\), then

\[
 R(k+1)\rho(k)-R(k)
 \sim c(d-2)k^{d-1},
\]

except for the exceptional degree \(d=2\). Therefore only degrees \(1\) and \(2\) can possibly produce the constant 1; degree 0 tends to 0 and degrees above 2 leave a nonconstant leading term.

At the two zeros of \(\rho\), equation (7.1) gives

\[
 R(n)=R(-n-1)=-1.
\tag{7.3}
\]

If \(R=ak+b\), (7.3) forces \(a=0\) and \(R=-1\), which does not satisfy (7.1).

If \(R=ak^2+bk+c\), (7.3) forces \(b=a\) and

\[
 R(k)=a(k(k+1)-N)-1.
\]

Set \(u=k+1\). Substitution into (7.1), followed by cancellation of the nonzero polynomial \(N-k(k+1)\), gives

\[
 [a(2N+1)+1]u^2-u-(aN^2+N)=0
\]

as a polynomial identity. The coefficient of \(u\) is \(-1\), a contradiction. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

### Fixed-\(n\) complexity lower bound

For a fixed positive integer \(n\), the symbolic orbit obstruction disappears only because \(-n,-n+1,\ldots,-1\) lie in one integral shift orbit. The same pole propagation shows that any nonpolynomial rational certificate, if one exists, must contain poles at every point

\[
 -n,-n+1,\ldots,-1.
\]

Its denominator degree is therefore at least \(n\). A bounded number of exceptional summation indices can shorten this chain only by \(O(1)\). Thus a bespoke finite-range certificate necessarily has linear algebraic complexity.

Finally, any fixed-width linear concomitant involving adjacent terms
\(t_{k-j},\ldots,t_{k+j}\) reduces to \(R(k)t_k\), because all adjacent terms are rational multiples of \(t_k\). Hence Lemma 7.1 rules out every uniformly bounded-width linear Green/Gosper/WZ boundary collapse of the unweighted prefix, not merely the simplest ansatz.

Creative telescoping in the outer variable can and does produce holonomic recurrences; it does not produce a product evaluation or a low-height boundary integer. The additive constant in (6.3) remains.

## 8. A genuine normalized Dodgson carrier

Although the long block gives no sublinear carrier, it does give one clean nonzero selective integer.

Choose \(k\) with

\[
 H+1\le k<L.
\]

Let

\[
 g_k=\gcd(t_k,t_{k+1}).
\]

Define the adjacent prefix minor

\[
 D_k=S_{k-1}S_{k+1}-S_k^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Writing \(x=t_k\), \(y=t_{k+1}\), and \(S=S_{k-1}\), one has

\[
 D_k=S(y-x)-x^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since \(g_k\mid x,y\), it follows that \(g_k\mid D_k\). Put

\[
 C_{n,k}^{\rm adj}=-\frac{D_k}{g_k}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

### Explicit integral formula

Reduce the ratio in (6.1):

\[
 A_k=(n-k)^2(n+k+1)^2,
 \qquad B_k=(k+1)^4,
\]

\[
 d_k=\gcd(A_k,B_k),
 \qquad a_k=A_k/d_k,
 \qquad b_k=B_k/d_k.
\]

Then \((a_k,b_k)=1\),

\[
 t_k=b_kg_k,
 \qquad t_{k+1}=a_kg_k,
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 C_{n,k}^{\rm adj}
 =b_kt_k-S_{k-1}(a_k-b_k).
 }
\tag{8.4}
\]

This proves integrality without any denominator convention.

### Target divisibility

Let \(p\) be a target. Then \(p\mid S_{k-1}\) and \(p^2\mid t_k,t_{k+1}\). If

\[
 \nu=\min\{v_p(t_k),v_p(t_{k+1})\}=v_p(g_k),
\]

then the first term in (8.2) has valuation at least \(\nu+1\), while
\(x^2\) has valuation at least \(\nu+2\). Hence

\[
 p^{\nu+1}\mid D_k,
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{R_n\mid C_{n,k}^{\rm adj}.}
\tag{8.5}
\]

This is genuinely selective: the extra factor beyond the universal gcd comes from the target prefix.

### Nonvanishing and sign

Let

\[
 \rho_j=t_{j+1}/t_j.
\]

For \(j<n\),

\[
 \sqrt{\rho_j}
 =\frac{N}{(j+1)^2}-1+\frac1{j+1},
\]

which is strictly decreasing in \(j\). In the range \(j\le L\), it is greater than 1. Thus \(\rho_j>1\) and is strictly decreasing.

Moreover,

\[
 \frac{S_{k-1}}{t_k}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{t_j}{t_k}
 <\frac1{\rho_{k-1}-1}
 <\frac1{\rho_k-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
 S_{k-1}(t_{k+1}-t_k)<t_k^2,
\]

so \(D_k<0\) and

\[
 C_{n,k}^{\rm adj}>0.
\tag{8.6}
\]

### Exact height scale at the first index

Take \(k=H+1\). Stirling's formula gives

\[
 \log t_{H+1}
 =n\log16+O(\log n).
\tag{8.7}
\]

Equation (8.4) and \(a_k,b_k\ll n^4\) give the matching upper bound

\[
 \log C_{n,H+1}^{\rm adj}
 \le n\log16+O(\log n).
\]

The preceding geometric-series estimate also gives

\[
 C_{n,k}^{\rm adj}
 \ge b_kt_k
 \frac{\rho_{k-1}-\rho_k}{\rho_{k-1}-1}.
\]

At \(k=n/3+O(1)\), the ratio difference is \(\asymp1/n\), while the denominator is bounded away from zero. Since \(b_k\ge1\),

\[
 \log C_{n,H+1}^{\rm adj}
 \ge n\log16-O(\log n).
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \log C_{n,H+1}^{\rm adj}
 =n\log16+O(\log n).
 }
\tag{8.8}
\]

The carrier is nonzero and explicit, but decisively linear. Its \(K\)-fold product supplies target order \(K\) with height

\[
 K n\log16+O(K\log n),
\]

not \(o(Kn)\).

## 9. General Hankel/Dodgson minors have only one selective factor

Equation (3.2) permits a general determinant calculation. Write

\[
 S_{H+j}=X+U_n^2Y_j,
 \qquad X=S_H,
 \qquad Y_0=0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Take any \(d\times d\) matrix of entries from the long prefix block of the form

\[
 M_{ij}=S_{H+\lambda_i+\mu_j}.
\]

Then

\[
 M=X\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\!T}+U_n^2A.
\]

The rank-one determinant identity gives, without any nonsingularity assumption on \(A\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \det M
 =U_n^{2(d-1)}
 \left(
  X\,\mathbf1^{\!T}\operatorname{adj}(A)\mathbf1
  +U_n^2\det A
 \right).
 }
\tag{9.2}
\]

After removing the universal factor \(U_n^{2(d-1)}\), the dependence on the selective coordinate \(X=S_H\) is **linear**, irrespective of the determinant size.

Therefore:

- every target prime divides the normalized determinant;
- no determinant gives two guaranteed selective powers of the same target prime;
- increasing \(d\) only accumulates universal powers already present for every candidate prime;
- Dodgson condensation and Plücker relations cannot change this rank-one fact.

The adjacent carrier of Section 8 is the first nontrivial \(d=2\) instance. Higher minors do not amplify the target order.

More generally, modulo \(U_n^2\), every polynomial expression in the prefix variables reduces to its diagonal specialization

\[
 F(S_H,\ldots,S_L)
 \equiv F(X,\ldots,X)\pmod{U_n^2}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

To force target order \(K\) from this local information, the diagonal polynomial must vanish to order \(K\) at \(X=0\), unless the whole expression lies in the universal difference ideal. Thus the target condition remains one principal coordinate; higher order is obtained only by taking powers of it.

## 10. Two targets, moments, and cross-prime use

Suppose \(p\) and \(q\) are both targets for the same outer \(n\). The exact global statement supplied by the long block is simply

\[
 pq\mid S_H,
 \qquad
 p^2q^2\mid S_K-S_H
 \quad(H<K\le L).
\tag{10.1}
\]

The normalized adjacent minor gives

\[
 pq\mid C_{n,k}^{\rm adj},
\]

but this is obtained independently at the two primes from the same single coordinate \(S_H\). There is no mixed resultant, common residue field, or off-diagonal relation between \(p\) and \(q\).

The arbitrary-subset countermodel (6.4)--(6.5) proves that the long-prefix axioms allow all column multiplicities, all factorial moments, and all cross-prime alignments. Consequently they yield none of the following by themselves:

- a new power exponent below the existing recurrence bounds;
- a forbidden long target configuration;
- a nontrivial fixed-\(n\) pair-energy estimate;
- a horizontal cancellation theorem;
- a subexponential common certificate.

A product of \(M=L-H+1\) prefixes contains \(R_n^M\), but its logarithmic height is \(\Theta(Mn)\), giving only \(\log R_n=O(n)\). Determinants remove universal powers but, by (9.2), retain only one selective power. No moment amplification survives saturation.

## 11. Sanity check at \(n=16\)

Here

\[
 H=5,
 \qquad L=8,
 \qquad \mathcal P_{16}=\{11,13\},
\]

so

\[
 U_{16}=11\cdot13,
 \qquad V_{16}=11,
 \qquad W_{16}=U_{16}V_{16}^2.
\]

At the first tail term \(k=6\):

- for \(p=11\), \(r=5\) and \(p-1-r=5\), so \(v_{11}(t_6)=4\);
- for \(p=13\), \(r=3\) and \(p-1-r=9\), so \(v_{13}(t_6)=2\).

Therefore

\[
 v_{11}(t_6/W_{16})=v_{13}(t_6/W_{16})=1,
\]

exactly as (4.3) predicts.

The Apéry values are

\[
 b_3=1445\not\equiv0\pmod{13},
 \qquad
 b_5=819005\equiv0\pmod{11}.
\]

Hence the local Smith invariant is \(11\) at the target prime and a unit at \(13\), and the global candidate-prime invariant is exactly \(R_{16}=11\).

## 12. Final theorem package and reopen condition

### Proved here

```text
1. Exact valuation:
   v_p(t_k(n)) = 2*[k>n-p] + 2*[k>2p-n-1].

2. Exact block congruence:
   S_K(n) = S_H(n) mod U_n^2 for H <= K <= L.

3. Exact ordinary tail content on top primes:
   gcd(t_(H+1),...,t_L) has top part U_n^2 V_n^2.

4. Exact saturated Smith form:
   (S_H, divided differences) = (S_H,U_n) = (R_n)
   in the top-prime semilocal ring.

5. Exact local rank:
   one selective elementary divisor, never two.

6. Tail-only order-K obstruction:
   every nonzero such carrier has log height at least
   (1/2+o(1))*K*n.

7. No rational bounded-width Gosper/WZ boundary telescoper.

8. Explicit nonzero adjacent-minor carrier:
   R_n divides C_n^adj and
   log C_n^adj = (log 16)*n + O(log n).

9. General Hankel/Dodgson minors are linear in the one selective
   prefix coordinate after universal saturation.

10. Arbitrary target subsets are compatible with the entire long-block
    recurrence and exact tail data after changing only the additive
    integration constant.
```

### Not obtained

```text
- no C_n with log|C_n| = o(n);
- no target order K with height o(K*n);
- no new power exponent;
- no nontrivial moment or pair-energy bound;
- no forbidden target configuration;
- no cross-prime certificate.
```

### Narrowest genuine reopen condition

The early prefix must satisfy an Apéry-specific anti-cancellation theorem against the top-half primorial:

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid S_{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}(n)}}\log p=o(n).
 }
\tag{12.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \log\gcd\!\left(
 S_{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}(n),
 \prod_{n/2<p\le n}p
 \right)=o(n).
 }
\tag{12.2}
\]

A useful stronger input would be a second global observable whose reduction is transverse to the free additive constant in (6.3). Its target-prime divisibility must not factor through \((S_H,U_n)\), and its Archimedean height must be sublinear. Neither the hypergeometric tail ratio, finite differences, normalized derivatives, Bézout saturation, nor Dodgson minors provide such an observable.

Thus the long-prefix escape hatch is now exhausted at the algebraic level: it gives an exact rank-one Smith saturation and one explicit linear-height selective minor, but no route past the unavoidable Chebyshev factor.
