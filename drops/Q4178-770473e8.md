# 2. A partner-independent horizontal Apéry carrier

The key observation is that one does not need to use the full common values \(b_n,b_N,b_s\).  Their **initial binomial prefixes already know any sufficiently small base-\(p\) remainder**.

## 2.1 Truncated-prefix Lucas lemma

Recall the Apéry formula
\[
b_r
=
\sum_{k=0}^{r}
\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2.
\tag{2.1}
\]
For \(R\ge0\), define
\[
\mathscr K_R(M)
:=
\sum_{k=0}^{R}
\binom Mk^2\binom{M+k}{k}^2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Lemma 2.1
Let \(\ell\) be prime, let
\[
M=c\ell+r,
\qquad
0\le r<\ell,
\qquad c<\ell,
\]
and suppose
\[
r\le R<\ell.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr K_R(M)\equiv b_r\pmod\ell.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Proof
For \(0\le k<\ell\), ordinary Lucas for binomial coefficients gives
\[
\binom Mk
\equiv
\binom rk
\pmod\ell.
\tag{2.4}
\]
For the second binomial there are two cases.

If \(r+k<\ell\), Lucas gives
\[
\binom{M+k}{k}
\equiv
\binom{r+k}{k}
\pmod\ell.
\tag{2.5}
\]
If \(r+k\ge\ell\), then the low base-\(\ell\) digit of \(M+k\) is
\[
r+k-\ell<k,
\]
so Lucas gives
\[
\binom{M+k}{k}\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\tag{2.6}
\]
The ordinary integer \(\binom{r+k}{k}\) is also divisible by \(\ell\) in this one-carry case, so (2.5) remains valid modulo \(\ell\).

Hence each summand of (2.2) is congruent to the corresponding Apéry summand at index \(r\).  For \(k>r\), \(\binom rk=0\), so all terms between \(r+1\) and \(R\) vanish modulo \(\ell\).  Summing gives (2.3).  QED.

This proves the needed congruence directly from ordinary binomial Lucas.  No stronger p-adic or Gessel theorem is being imported.

# 4. Why the obvious horizontal finite difference does not extend it

A natural idea is to reduce the height of a deep prefix by taking consecutive differences.  The first difference is exact:
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr K_R(M)-\mathscr K_{R-1}(M)
=
\binom MR^2\binom{M+R}{R}^2.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Suppose \(M=c\ell+r\), \(0\le r<\ell\), and
\[
r<R<\ell.
\]
Ordinary Lucas gives
\[
\binom MR\equiv\binom rR=0\pmod\ell.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\ell^2
\mid
\mathscr K_R(M)-\mathscr K_{R-1}(M)
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
**whether or not \(\ell\mid b_r\)**.

Thus the first cancellation after crossing the remainder cutoff is entirely a universal base-\(\ell\) carry factor.  It contains no primitive information about the Apéry zero.  Dividing/saturating that factor destroys precisely the divisibility one hoped to use.

This is the horizontal-prefix analogue of the Q4166 phenomenon
\[
N_h(X)N_h(X+1)
-N_{h+1}(X)N_{h-1}(X+1)
=S_h(X)^6,
\]
where the first exterior cancellation is only the restart boundary factor.

I emphasize the scope: (4.2) rules out the **naive scalar finite-difference localization** of the prefix carrier.  It is not a lower bound for every linear-size growing-prefix syzygy.  That larger class remains open and is exactly where a successful continuation can live.

There is a reflected version of Lemma 2.1 as well.  If the low remainder is
\[
r=\ell-1-z,
\]
then for \(k\le z\), the identities
\[
\binom{\ell-1-z}{k}
\equiv(-1)^k\binom{z+k}{k},
\]
\[
\binom{\ell-1-z+k}{k}
\equiv(-1)^k\binom zk
\pmod\ell

show that a prefix of length at least \(z\) already reproduces \(b_z\) modulo \(\ell\).  But along one q6 partner block

so a single reflected prefix covering all \(D\le\Delta<2D\) has to reach length \(z+14D\).  The available height estimate is therefore linear in \(D\), giving only \(O(D)\) shell-prime divisors.  Reflection alone does not create the missing power.

\ll_J
\frac{T Z^2}{L^2}.
}
\tag{5.8-}

The minus formula is Q4151's row theorem; (5.8+) is the plus analogue with the sharper Q4065 \(h^{2/3}\) local capacity inserted.

Fix a constant \(C_0\ge32\), and let \(E_{\sigma,\rm sh}(D;C_0)\) count exact same-state raw partner pairs with \(D\le p'-p<2D\) for which at least one endpoint has reflected gap at most \(C_0D\).

Choose a shallow endpoint.  For a fixed actual shallow row, the same-state partner is still required to be a prime in an interval of length \(D\).  Dropping its Apéry and raw predicates only enlarges the count, and Brun--Titchmarsh gives, for macroscopic polynomial \(D\),
\[
\#\{p'\in[p+D,p+2D):p'\text{ prime}\}
\ll \frac D{\log D}
\asymp_J\frac DL.
\tag{5.9}
\]
A pair has at most two shallow endpoints.  Therefore (5.8)--(5.9), with \(Z=C_0D\), give
\[
E_{+,\rm sh}(D;C_0)
\ll_J
\frac{T D^{5/3}}{L^2}\frac DL
=
\boxed{
O_J\!\left(\frac{T D^{8/3}}{L^3}\right),
}
\tag{5.10+}
\]
\[
E_{-,\rm sh}(D;C_0)
\ll_J
\frac{T D^2}{L^2}\frac DL
=
\boxed{
O_J\!\left(\frac{T D^3}{L^3}\right).
}
\tag{5.10-}
\]
Both lower primalities are preserved: \(p\) is part of the actual shallow row counted by (5.8), and \(p'\) is retained through Brun--Titchmarsh.  The partner zero conditions are dropped only in the final enlarging inequality.

Use the corrected lower-zero factorial envelope
\[
S_2(T)\ll_J\overline S_2(T)
:=T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}.
\tag{5.11}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{E_{+,\rm sh}}{D\overline S_2}
\ll_J
T^{-6/5}D^{5/3}L^{-12/5}.
\tag{5.12+}
\]
If \(D\le T^{3/5+o(1)}\), the right side is
\[
T^{-1/5+o(1)}.
\]
Likewise

\frac{E_{-,\rm sh}}{D\overline S_2}
\ll_J
T^{-6/5}D^2L^{-12/5}.
\tag{5.12-}

If \(D\le T^{3/5-\delta_0}\), this is
\[
\ll T^{-2\delta_0}L^{-12/5}.

This proves (V13+)--(V13-).

\tag{5.14}

So every cross-order minus pair is already contained in the shallow family for \(C_0\ge16\).  The genuinely unresolved deep minus family has a fixed order type.

# 6. Exact deep-overlap geometry

Remove the reflected-shallow family with \(C_0=32\).  The surviving pairs have reflected gap \(h>32D\) at both endpoints.  Their normalized recurrence intervals overlap almost completely.

The exact Q4169 transports are:

### Plus
If the base sorted pair is \([x,x+h]\), then
\[
[x',x'+h']
=
[x+7\Delta,\ x+h+14\Delta],
\tag{6.1+}
\]
so
\[
h'=h+7\Delta.
\]
The common interval has length at least
\[
h-7\Delta>h-14D,
\]
and the total boundary symmetric difference is \(21\Delta=O(D)\).

### Minus `M+`
\[
[x',x'+h']
=
[x-\Delta,\ x+h+7\Delta],
\tag{6.1M+}
\]
so the partner interval contains the base interval and adds exactly \(8\Delta\) boundary steps.

### Minus `M-`
As long as the order is retained,
\[
[x',x'+h']
=
[x+7\Delta,\ x+h-\Delta],
\tag{6.1M-}
\]
and the partner interval is obtained by removing exactly \(8\Delta\) boundary steps.  By (5.14), every deep pair has stable order, so this is the only deep `M-` case.

Thus every deep pair has one characteristic-zero transfer block common to the two recurrences, with only \(O(D)\) boundary transfer matrices changing.

This observation is real but does **not** itself give a cross-characteristic equation.  The base endpoint-return condition is imposed modulo \(p\); the partner endpoint-return condition is imposed modulo \(p'\ne p\).  If one treats the common transfer matrix only through its formal recurrence identities and determinant-unit law, the two congruences occupy independent CRT components
\[
\mathbb Z/(pp')\mathbb Z
\simeq
\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_{p'}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Therefore a boundary-only identity obtained solely from formal transfer factorization cannot force a new common-root relation.  Any successful use of the long common block has to exploit the **actual arithmetic value** of that Apéry transfer block across varying defining characteristics.  This is precisely the arithmetic content requested by `IPRD`, not something supplied by ordinary resultant theory.

This also explains why the Q3956/Q4155 bounded-aspect resultant cannot be imported here.  That theorem concerns two gap continuants in **one fixed characteristic** and a resultant such as
\[
\operatorname{Res}_X(N_d(X),N_r(X+d)).
\]
APD has one return modulo \(p\) and a translated return modulo \(p'\).  Using the bounded-aspect ledger here would repeat the already-known characteristic-quantifier error.

# 7. Audit of the four proposed Apéry-specific routes

## 7.1 Horizontal large-prime divisor cancellation

**New positive result:** the local two-prefix carrier (V8) is fixed across the whole partner block, and it proves the boundary-prefix power theorem.

**Remaining obstruction:** in the deep affine interior,
\[
B_6,B_\sigma\asymp T
\]
is possible.  The height bound (2.13) then returns to the \(O_J(T)\) scale of the saturated full gcd.  The first finite difference is the universal carry factor (4.2).  Thus a power in the interior requires nontrivial cancellation in the **large-prime radical** of the two prefixes, not their generic archimedean height.

## 7.2 Defining-characteristic bilinear geometry

The exact common-index geometry is already two-channel:
\[
(n,m_+)=(6p+\rho,13p+\alpha_+)
\]
or
\[
(n,m_-)=(6p+\rho,p+\alpha_-).
\]
This is what makes (V8) possible.  But for two partners the defining fields are \(\mathbb F_p\) and \(\mathbb F_{p'}\).  Ordinary bilinear/resultant elimination does not identify them.  A useful new theorem would have to be a variable-modulus large-prime divisor correlation for the **actual pair of truncated Apéry values**, which is exactly one natural formulation of `IPRD`.

## 7.3 Growing-prefix syzygies

Q4166 proves that bounded recurrence rows, bounded subresultants, bounded exterior/symmetric powers, companion minors, norms and contents do not lower the moving degree: the first tangent polynomial has exact degree \(3h\), and the first exterior cancellation is only the restart factor \(S_h^6\).

The prefix calculation in Section 4 adds a horizontal analogue: the first scalar truncation difference is only a universal Lucas carry.  Therefore the next algebraic class really is a **linear-size saturated prefix module**.  A success theorem sufficient for APD would be:

```text
for each deep base leaf (u,p,D), construct a nonzero primitive C(u,p,D)
from the two prefix/transfer modules such that

  every shell-prime divisor in Q_sigma(u,p,D) divides C(u,p,D),

and

  sum_(u,p deep) log |C(u,p,D)|
      <<_J (log T) D^(1-delta) M_sigma(T)

for some fixed delta>0,

after stripping every restart, Lucas-carry, q-primary,
fixed-coefficient and denominator factor.
```

This immediately implies `IPRD_delta`.  Conversely, proving that every such saturated primitive row has degree/height \(\gg D\) would close this whole growing-prefix route.

## 7.4 p-adic compatibility

The exact first-jet/Newton identity from Q2962 is
\[
b_r=K_r(r(r+1)),
\qquad
 g_r=(2r+1)K_r'(r(r+1)).
\]
After the central ramification is stripped, the strongest natural same-characteristic statement is the still-open simple-root condition
\[
p>2r+1,\quad p\mid b_r
\Longrightarrow p\nmid g_r.
\]
Even if that theorem were proved, it would say that a large prime occurs **simply** in one defining characteristic.  APD counts distinct prime divisors of a fixed marked gcd/prefix family, not their valuations.  Simplicity does not prevent many different primes from lying in one short horizontal interval.  Thus first-jet transversality alone cannot imply APD; one needs a genuinely cross-characteristic p-adic law coupling the two defining primes.

\]
Used termwise to prove Lemma 2.1.  No stronger Apéry-Lucas theorem is needed there.

### B. Q4065 local-capacity theorem
For an actual normalized reflected pair

\qquad p\mid b_x,b_{x+h},

the transfer is conjunctive:
\[
(p\mid b_x\ \&\ p\mid b_{x+h})
\iff
(p\mid b_x\ \&\ p\mid N_h(x)).
\]
At fixed \((p,h)\), the number of actual q6 rows is

\ll\min\{h,w^{2/3}\}.

For plus \(w=\min(h,p-h)\), giving \(O(h^{2/3})\); for shallow minus gaps in the present range, \(O(h)\) is the relevant bound.

Used only after fixing an actual shallow q6 row, and only to retain the primality of the possible partner.  All partner Apéry predicates are dropped at that last enlarging step.
