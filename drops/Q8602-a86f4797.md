# Q8602 — exact \(p\)-adic normalization of the factorial-cleared \(p\)-block transfer

## Verdict

The congruence \(A_u^2=0\) is only the zeroth-order statement.  By itself it proves that a product of at least two based \(p\)-blocks is divisible by \(p\), and nothing close to the exact content.

For the actual Apéry matrices the surviving \(X=1024\) packet has a much larger exact content:

\[
 v_p\!\left(\gcd\text{ of all four entries of }M_{u+cp-1}\cdots M_u\right)=3(c-1)
\]

at all three positive hits.  After division by \(p^{3(c-1)}\), the first nonzero matrix modulo \(p\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of the same rank-one nilpotent \(A_u\).  Numerically,

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
p&c&u&e& p^{-e}\mathcal T_c\pmod p\\ \hline
1069&14&502&39&824E_{21}\\
1381&11&277&30&933E_{21}\\
1847&8&692&21&1789E_{21}.
\end{array}
\]

This does **not** produce a target-selective or cross-characteristic carrier.  The normalized matrix is determined by the recurrence matrices, not by the extra condition \(p\mid b_u\); at a root it annihilates the actual Apéry state.  Recovering a scalar mod-\(p^2\) Lucas/Gessel quotient requires the next, solution-specific jet.

The rigorous route closure is therefore:

* the correct normalization is governed by a third-order full-period pole, not by \(A_u^2=0\);
* a general theorem giving the exact exponent \(3(c-1)\) is an additional full-period lattice theorem, not a consequence of the audited mod-\(p\) formula;
* even granting that theorem, its leading matrix supplies no new target condition and no common small-height integer shared by three different target primes.

## 1. Exact transfer and the off-by-one indices

Put

\[
C_n=(n!)^3b_n,
\qquad
V_n=\binom{C_n}{C_{n-1}}.
\]

The factorial-cleared recurrence is

\[
C_{n+1}=P(n)C_n-n^6C_{n-1},
\qquad
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5,
\]

so

\[
V_{n+1}=M_nV_n,
\qquad
M_n=\begin{pmatrix}P(n)&-n^6\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(1\le u<p\), define the \(j\)-th based full block by

\[
\mathcal B_j(p,u)
 =M_{u+(j+1)p-1}\cdots M_{u+jp}.
\]

It contains exactly \(p\) matrices.  If

\[
m=cp+u,
\]

then the transfer from \(V_u\) to \(V_m\) is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_c(p,u)
 =M_{u+cp-1}\cdots M_u
 =\mathcal B_{c-1}\cdots\mathcal B_0 .
}
\]

There is no extra block and no omitted endpoint: \(M_u\) first sends \(V_u\) to \(V_{u+1}\), and \(M_{m-1}\) last sends \(V_{m-1}\) to \(V_m\).

Since \(M_{n+p}\equiv M_n\pmod p\), every block has the same reduction

\[
\mathcal B_j\equiv A_u\pmod p.
\]

Consequently

\[
\mathcal T_c\equiv A_u^c\pmod p.
\]

For \(c\ge2\), this is zero because \(A_u^2=0\).

At a regular root \(p\mid b_u\), \(u!\) is a \(p\)-unit and hence \(C_u\equiv0\pmod p\).  The audited rank-one formula becomes

\[
A_u
 =\frac{1}{((u-1)!)^6}
 \binom{C_u}{C_{u-1}}
 \begin{pmatrix}-C_{u-1}&C_u\end{pmatrix}
 \equiv a_uE_{21}\pmod p,
\]

where

\[
a_u=-\frac{C_{u-1}^2}{((u-1)!)^6}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Thus the target root merely puts the generic nilpotent into the \(E_{21}\) direction.

## 2. The exact factorial gauge

Introduce the rational Apéry transfer matrix

\[
L_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(n)/(n+1)^3&-n^3/(n+1)^3\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]

and

\[
F_n=\operatorname{diag}\bigl((n!)^3,((n-1)!)^3\bigr).
\]

A direct multiplication gives the exact gauge identity

\[
\boxed{M_n=F_{n+1}L_nF_n^{-1}.}
\]

Hence, with

\[
\mathcal R_c(p,u)=L_{u+cp-1}\cdots L_u,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{\mathcal T_c=F_{u+cp}\mathcal R_cF_u^{-1}.}
\]

Because \(1\le c<p\) and \(1\le u<p\), the integers \(p,2p,\ldots,cp\) are the only multiples of \(p\) in \([1,cp+u]\), and none is divisible by \(p^2\).  Therefore

\[
v_p((cp+u)!)=v_p((cp+u-1)!)=c.
\]

Write

\[
F_{u+cp}=p^{3c}E_c,
\qquad E_c\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_p),
\]

while \(F_u\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_p)\).  If

\[
\nu_{p,u}(c)=
\min_{r,s}v_p\bigl((\mathcal R_c)_{rs}\bigr),
\]

then multiplication by the two diagonal unit matrices does not alter entrywise valuations, and the exact matrix-content exponent is

\[
\boxed{
 e_{p,u}(c)=3c+\nu_{p,u}(c).
}
\]

This is the precise normalization formula.  It isolates the real issue: the pole order of the rational full-period transfer.

For one block, the nonzero reduction of \(\mathcal B_0\) gives

\[
\nu_{p,u}(1)=-3,
\qquad e_{p,u}(1)=0.
\]

A general identity

\[
\nu_{p,u}(c)\ge-3
\]

would imply

\[
p^{3(c-1)}\mid\mathcal T_c.
\]

Exact equality would additionally require

\[
\nu_{p,u}(c)=-3.
\]

Thus the commonly suggested exponent \(3(c-1)\) is equivalent to a **single \(p^3\)-pole theorem for the complete rational transfer**.  It is not implied by periodicity modulo \(p\) or by \(A_u^2=0\).

The determinant check is consistent but does not prove the single-pole theorem:

\[
\det M_n=n^6,
\]

and the interval \(u\le n\le u+cp-1\) contains precisely \(p,2p,\ldots,cp\).  Hence

\[
v_p(\det\mathcal T_c)=6c.
\]

Therefore every common-content exponent satisfies

\[
e_{p,u}(c)\le3c.
\]

The determinant leaves the four possibilities \(3c-3,3c-2,3c-1,3c\), and many smaller exponents, open.

## 3. What \(A_u^2=0\) actually proves

Write

\[
\mathcal B_j=A_u+pE_j+p^2F_j+p^3G_j+\cdots .
\]

For two blocks,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_1\mathcal B_0
={}&A_u^2\\
&+p(A_uE_0+E_1A_u)\\
&+p^2(A_uF_0+E_1E_0+F_1A_u)\\
&+p^3(A_uG_0+E_1F_0+F_1E_0+G_1A_u)+\cdots .
\end{aligned}
\]

Nilpotence kills only the first line.  Thus it proves

\[
p\mid\mathcal B_1\mathcal B_0.
\]

To obtain the observed Apéry normalization \(p^3\), one must also prove the two independent identities

\[
A_uE_0+E_1A_u\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

and

\[
A_uF_0+E_1E_0+F_1A_u\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Only then is the first candidate matrix

\[
\boxed{
J_2=
A_uG_0+E_1F_0+F_1E_0+G_1A_u
\pmod p.
}
\]

If \(J_2\ne0\), then

\[
e_{p,u}(2)=3,
\qquad
p^{-3}\mathcal T_2\equiv J_2\pmod p.
\]

If \(J_2=0\), the exact exponent is larger.  Hence the first nonzero normalized object for \(c=2\) is a **third block jet**.  A mod-\(p^2\) calculation does not determine it; the individual blocks must be known modulo \(p^4\).

The same point can be expressed in the rational gauge.  Each one-period rational block has a Laurent expansion beginning at \(p^{-3}\).  In a product of two periods, \(A_u^2=0\) removes the coefficient of \(p^{-6}\), but the coefficients of \(p^{-5}\) and \(p^{-4}\) require additional cancellations.  The single-pole theorem is exactly the assertion that all poles below \(p^{-3}\) cancel.

## 4. Exact countermodel: nilpotence does not determine the exponent

The insufficiency is not merely logical wording.  For any odd prime \(p\), let

\[
B(x)=
\begin{pmatrix}
p x+p^5&p^2x\\
1&p
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
B(x)\equiv E_{21}\pmod p,
\qquad
\det B(x)=p^6.
\]

Thus every \(B(x)\) has the same mod-\(p\) nilpotent block and the same determinant valuation as an Apéry \(p\)-block.

Take \(B_1=B(1)\).

* If \(B_0=B(0)\), then the lower-left entry of \(B_1B_0\) is
  \[
  p(1+p^4),
  \]
  so the common content is exactly \(p^1\).

* If \(B_0=B(-1+p^2)\), then
  \[
  v_p(B_1B_0)=
  \begin{pmatrix}4&5\\3&4\end{pmatrix},
  \]
  so the common content is exactly \(p^3\).

Both examples have identical reductions \(E_{21}\), identical nilpotence, and determinant \(p^6\) for each block.  They differ only in higher \(p\)-adic jets.

For a target root the cleared state satisfies

\[
V_u\equiv (0,C_{u-1})^T\pmod p,
\]

which lies in the kernel of \(E_{21}\).  Both countermodels are therefore compatible with the same zeroth-order target-state condition.  This proves that the audited \(A_u\) formula cannot determine either the exact exponent or the normalized matrix.

## 5. General \(c\): the exact missing full-period statement

The non-tautological theorem needed to promote the observed normalization is the following.

### FP3 single-pole theorem

For \(1\le u<p\), \(1\le c<p\), put

\[
\mathcal R_c=L_{u+cp-1}\cdots L_u.
\]

Prove

\[
\boxed{p^3\mathcal R_c\in M_2(\mathbb Z_p)}
\]

and identify its reduction.  In the strongest based form one would have

\[
\boxed{
 p^{-3(c-1)}\mathcal T_c
 \equiv \alpha_{p,u}(c)A_u\pmod p
}
\]

for a scalar \(\alpha_{p,u}(c)\in\mathbb F_p\).

If \(\alpha_{p,u}(c)\ne0\), then

\[
\boxed{e_{p,u}(c)=3(c-1).}
\]

If \(\alpha_{p,u}(c)=0\), the content is larger and one must continue to the next weighted jet.  Thus no universal “first nonzero matrix” exists without a nonvanishing theorem for \(\alpha_{p,u}(c)\).

For general \(c\), this is not a first-order expansion.  If gauged integral blocks are written

\[
S_j=S_{j,0}+pS_{j,1}+p^2S_{j,2}+\cdots,
\]

then divisibility by \(p^{3(c-1)}\) means that every coefficient of total degree below \(3(c-1)\) in

\[
S_{c-1}\cdots S_0
\]

vanishes.  The candidate leading matrix is

\[
\sum_{r_0+\cdots+r_{c-1}=3(c-1)}
S_{c-1,r_{c-1}}\cdots S_{0,r_0}\pmod p.
\]

The mod-\(p\) nilpotence checks only the total-degree-zero term.  It does not supply the remaining \(3(c-1)-1\) cancellations.

## 7. The normalized scalar is not the quotient digit

For the same packet, the short quotient Apéry values are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
p&c&\alpha_{p,u}(c)&b_c&(c!)^3b_c\\ \hline
1069&14&725&353&336\\
1381&11&1276&1098&793\\
1847&8&251&1595&299.
\end{array}
\]

Thus the scalar is neither \(b_c\) nor the factorial-cleared \(C_c\).  It is a prime-, residue-, and block-length-dependent monodromy jet.

More fundamentally, \(\mathcal T_c\) is built only from the matrices \(M_n\).  It is independent of which solution of the recurrence is later inserted.  The condition \(p\mid b_u\) only selects a residue and makes

\[
A_u\equiv a_uE_{21}.
\]

It does not impose a new equation on \(\alpha_{p,u}(c)\).

The regular target state satisfies

\[
V_u\equiv C_{u-1}e_2\pmod p.
\]

Since

\[
E_{21}e_2=0,
\]

one has

\[
\left(p^{-3(c-1)}\mathcal T_c\right)V_u\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Therefore even a nonzero normalized matrix is invisible on the actual target state at first order.

## 8. Relation to mod-\(p^2\) Lucas/Gessel quotients

The scalar first-digit quotient for the Apéry solution is

\[
\Phi_p(c,u)=
\frac{b_{cp+u}-b_cb_u}{p}\pmod p,
\]

whenever the numerator is divided using Lucas modulo \(p\).

This is not the same object as \(\alpha_{p,u}(c)\).  The latter is the leading pole coefficient of the **matrix transfer after factorial gauge normalization**.  At a root \(b_u\equiv0\pmod p\), its leading matrix annihilates the zeroth-order state.  The next scalar term in the propagated solution has the form

\[
J_cV_u^{(1)}+J_c^{(1)}V_u^{(0)},
\]

where \(V_u^{(1)}\) contains the lift \(C_u/p\), and \(J_c^{(1)}\) is the next matrix jet.  Those are the data from which a mod-\(p^2\) Lucas/Gessel quotient can be recovered.

Hence:

1. \(A_u^2=0\) is below the mod-\(p^2\) scalar quotient;
2. the \(p^{3(c-1)}\)-normalized leading matrix is still solution-independent;
3. target selectivity first appears only after adjoining \(C_u/p\) and the next block jet;
4. the target condition in this problem is only \(p\mid b_m\), not \(p^2\mid b_m\), so it imposes no vanishing condition on that next quotient.

Indeed regularity gives \(b_c\not\equiv0\pmod p\), while Lucas already yields

\[
b_{cp+u}\equiv b_cb_u\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Thus the common-row target adds no first-jet equation at all on the positive branch.

## 9. Negative branch audit

The analysis above is for the exact positive representation

\[
m=cp+u.
\]

For a negative folded hit,

\[
m=cp-u-1
 =(c-1)p+(p-u-1).
\]

Put

\[
r=p-u-1.
\]

The correct based transfer is then from residue \(r\), through \(c-1\) full blocks:

\[
M_{m-1}\cdots M_r.
\]

Therefore the positive exponent \(3(c-1)\) cannot simply be copied.  Under an FP3 theorem at the reflected residue, the corresponding candidate exponent would be

\[
3((c-1)-1)=3(c-2).
\]

In particular, the allowed negative endpoint \(c=2\) contains only one full block and has no nilpotent block product.  Reflection gives the zeroth-order root equivalence \(b_r\equiv b_u\pmod p\), but it does not transport the higher monodromy jet without a separate reflected-jet identity.

Thus this route cannot be summed over both signs by treating the coefficient \(c\) uniformly.

## 10. Why there is no cross-characteristic carrier

Even the strongest packet-supported congruence

\[
\mathcal T_c
\equiv p^{3(c-1)}\alpha_{p,u}(c)A_u
\pmod {p^{3(c-1)+1}}
\]

is prime-local:

* the matrix length is \(cp\);
* its starting residue is \(u\);
* its scalar is \(\alpha_{p,u}(c)\);
* the normalization exponent depends on that prime's quotient coefficient;
* a different target prime on the same row produces a different transfer matrix.

There is no single integer here that all three target primes divide.

The raw integral transfer is also far too large to serve as a small carrier.  Since \(m< X^2\) and the entries of \(M_n\) are \(O(n^6)\), a crude bound gives

\[
\log\|\mathcal T_c\|=O(m\log m)=O(X^2\log X).
\]

Dividing by \(p^{3(c-1)}\) removes only \(O(X\log X)\) from this logarithmic height.  The resulting entries are not of \(X^{o(1)}\) height, and the three primes do not divide the same entry or the same resultant.

The normalized leading matrix also contains no target condition: it is nonzero on the packet, while its action on the target state is zero for the generic kernel reason.  Converting it to a divisor statement would either be tautological or require the unproved next-jet correlation across different characteristics.

## 11. Exact route status

The strongest honest theorem exposed by this audit is the following conditional normalization statement:

> If the FP3 single-pole theorem holds for a based residue \(u\), then
> \[
> p^{3(c-1)}\mid M_{u+cp-1}\cdots M_u.
> \]
> If its leading pole scalar \(\alpha_{p,u}(c)\) is nonzero, the exponent is exact and the normalized matrix is a nonzero rank-one residue operator.

The \(X=1024\) positive packet verifies both clauses for its three positive hits.

However, FP3 is a higher-jet full-period theorem; it is not proved by \(A_u^2=0\).  More importantly, even a complete proof of FP3 would not prove the cubic target estimate.  A usable continuation would need a strictly stronger, target-selective statement controlling the next scalar quotient, for example a cross-characteristic correlation theorem for

\[
\left(C_u/p,\ J_c^{(1)},\ \Phi_p(c,u)\right)
\]

that produces one nonzero integer of \(X^{o(1)}\) height for all primes in a fixed target fibre.  No such identity follows from periodicity, reflection, Lucas, the Wronskian, or the normalized nilpotent block.

## Final conclusion

The monodromy route does not close DSEL3.

The unnormalized equality \(A_u^2=0\) is pure factorial-cleared singular-lattice content and is not target-selective.  The actual Apéry packet shows that the correct first surviving transfer occurs after division by \(p^{3(c-1)}\), not merely by \(p\), but the resulting matrix is still a generic scalar multiple of \(A_u\) and annihilates the target state.  Its scalar is neither \(b_c\) nor \(C_c\), and it varies with \(p,u,c\).

Therefore there is no new common integer congruence of subexponential height.  The named blocker is:

\[
\boxed{\text{TARGET-SELECTIVE NEXT-JET CROSS-CHARACTERISTIC CORRELATION}.}
\]

Without that additional theorem, the first normalized monodromy jet is a correct local \(p\)-adic refinement but not a three-prime carrier.
