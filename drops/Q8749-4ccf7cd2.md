# LGTQ-3: Can Apéry quotient arithmetic give anti-concentration?

## Executive summary

The quotient identity is a genuine arithmetic constraint, but by itself it is not an anti-concentration mechanism. The recurrence module has already been saturated: there is no hidden scalar divisor or low-height invariant left to exploit. The plausible saving must come from the fact that the Apéry solution is a distinguished hypergeometric period, not from generic second-order recurrence arithmetic.

The current evidence points away from a K3 cross-characteristic correlation argument and toward a local-global sieve mechanism: Dwork-type congruences, Frobenius-controlled zero sets, and the exceptional algebraic structure of the Apéry motive may reduce the density of simultaneous transverse hits. No known theorem currently turns this into the required uniform LGTQ-3 bound.

## 1. Size and arithmetic of beta_j

For u approximately n/q,

\[
 b_u \sim \frac{(1+\sqrt2)^{4u}}{\pi^{3/2}u^{3/2}},
\]

so

\[
\log_2 \beta_j = u\log_2((1+\sqrt2)^4)+O(\log n).
\]

Thus beta_j has linear size in n bits. This huge size alone is not useful: a random integer of this size has no obstruction to being a member of a short linear relation.

Modulo a prime ell, however, beta_j is not random in the usual sense. The Apéry recurrence gives Frobenius-periodic restrictions, and the binomial formula gives stronger Dwork congruences. These are local restrictions, not a direct global description of beta_j.

## 2. The quotient identity

The identity

\[
\Delta_{s,t}p_0\beta_0-\Delta_{0,t}D_sp_1\beta_1+\Delta_{0,s}D_tp_2\beta_2=0
\]

is strong because the coefficients have height only polylogarithmic in n while the beta_j are exponentially large.

If beta_0,beta_1,beta_2 were independent random integers of size exp(cn), the chance of satisfying one exact linear equation would be about exp(-cn). Therefore the identity cannot be explained by accidental cancellation.

However, it does not immediately reduce the count of triples: every triple in the construction already comes from the same Apéry recurrence, and the identity is a deterministic consequence of that recurrence. To gain a saving, one needs to show that the map from prime triples to possible quotient triples has substantially smaller image than the generic sieve predicts.

The missing statement is an anti-concentration theorem for these quotient values.

## 3. Transversality probability

The transfer relation gives

\[
b_{u+h}\equiv N_h(u)b_{u+1}\pmod p
\]

when b_u is zero modulo p.

Hence failure of transversality is exactly the event

\[
N_h(u)\equiv0\pmod p
\]

or

\[
N_{h_1+h_2}(u)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

For fixed h and varying p, the gap polynomial has bounded degree O(h). If u behaved randomly, each condition would occur with probability O(h/p), so both continuants being nonzero would have probability

\[
1-O((h_1+h_2)/p)=1-o(1).
\]

Thus transversality removes exceptional degeneracies but is not itself the source of a power saving.

## 4. K3 Frobenius and cross-characteristics

The K3 interpretation controls the distribution of b_u modulo a fixed prime p. It describes the Frobenius orbit in one characteristic.

LGTQ-3 involves

\[
(b_{u_0}\bmod p_0,b_{u_1}\bmod p_1,b_{u_2}\bmod p_2)
\]

with three different characteristics. There is no direct Frobenius action relating these three residue fields.

A K3 argument could help indirectly by proving strong uniform estimates for each local zero set Z_p or by controlling exceptional primes, but a direct joint distribution theorem across varying characteristics is not currently available.

## 5. Casoratian

The identity

\[
a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=6/n^3
\]

is already part of the recurrence structure. It gives linear independence of the Apéry solutions and controls denominators, but it does not impose a new independent condition on beta_j.

A possible use is indirect: if combined with p-adic information on a_n and b_n it can rule out certain simultaneous high valuations. It does not by itself create anti-concentration.

## 6. Actual mechanism for a saving

The likely answer is not (b) or (c).

### (a) Dwork/p-adic structure: partially yes

The Apéry binomial sums are not generic recurrence solutions. They are diagonal/hypergeometric periods. Dwork congruences give extra compatibility between blocks:

\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p
\]

and stronger p-adic congruences. These restrict which quotient patterns can occur.

A successful proof would likely combine these with the moving-prime condition p|b_u to show that simultaneous quotient constraints have fewer solutions than a random model.

### (b) K3 Frobenius: indirect only

Frobenius explains individual prime behavior and zero-set geometry. It is unlikely to directly correlate p_0,p_1,p_2 because they are different characteristics.

### (c) Irrationality of zeta(3): no direct route

The irrationality proof uses the same Apéry periods, but irrationality of the limiting ratio a_n/b_n is far too weak to control finite congruence coincidences among many primes.

### (d) Most plausible mechanism

The missing mechanism is a local-to-global rigidity theorem:

1. The Apéry solution has very small p-adic complexity compared with a generic recurrence solution.
2. The zero condition p|b_u places the prime inside a Frobenius-controlled exceptional set.
3. Three nearby primes with independent transverse zero conditions would require three simultaneous exceptional configurations.
4. Dwork congruences and hypergeometric structure should make the number of such configurations smaller than the generic sieve prediction.

The needed theorem is therefore an anti-concentration estimate for Apéry zero patterns, not a new linear relation among beta_j.

A realistic intermediate target is:

\[
\#\{p_0: p_0,p_1,p_2\text{ form a transverse LGTQ triple}\}
\ll \frac{n}{(\log n)^3} \eta(n)
\]

for some explicit \(\eta(n)\to0\). At present the arithmetic identity identifies the rigidity but does not supply the saving.
