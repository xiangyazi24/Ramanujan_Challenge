# Finite-field audit of the quadratic-root claim

## Statement

Let
\[
A_u=\sum_{k=0}^u {u\choose k}^2 {u+k\choose k}^2
\]
and suppose
\[
Q(u)=17u^2+51u+39\equiv0\pmod p,
\]
with p>=5 and 0<=u<=p-3. The proposed implication is
\[
Q(u)=0\Longrightarrow A_u\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The available algebraic identities do not prove this implication by themselves.

## Hypergeometric reformulation

Using
\[
{u\choose k}{u+k\choose k}=(-1)^k\frac{(-u)_k(u+1)_k}{(k!)^2},
\]
we obtain the truncated hypergeometric form
\[
A_u={}_4F_3\left(\begin{matrix}-u,-u,u+1,u+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1\right).
\]
For u<p-1 this truncation is valid in F_p.

The condition Q(u)=0 is the discriminant-type quadratic condition attached to the Apéry recurrence coefficient, but it is not a parameter identity for the above hypergeometric value. No standard finite-field transformation forces the value away from zero on this locus.

## Recurrence obstruction

The Apéry recurrence is
\[
(n+1)^3A_{n+1}=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)A_n-n^3A_{n-1}.
\]
The polynomial Q(n)=17n^2+51n+39 differs from the recurrence coefficient and arises after shifting/combining adjacent terms. Therefore Q(u)=0 only annihilates a recurrence coefficient; it does not annihilate a term of the sequence.

Consequently a proof would require an additional identity relating the zero locus of A_n modulo p to this shifted coefficient. Such an identity is not supplied by Lucas congruence alone.

## Exact checks and proof status

The logically valid deductions are:

1. Lucas congruences give
\[
A_{mp+r}\equiv A_mA_r\pmod p
\]
for the standard Apéry sequence, but with m=0 this only controls indices below p and does not prohibit zeros.

2. Reflection relations for the zero set likewise do not imply exclusion of a quadratic residue class.

3. Hypergeometric transformations preserve the possibility of vanishing finite-field values.

Thus the claim remains an independent finite-field zero-exclusion statement.

## Counterexample status

No explicit counterexample is established in this audit. A valid refutation would require an exact prime p and integer u satisfying
\[
0\le u\le p-3,
\quad17u^2+51u+39\equiv0\pmod p,
\quad A_u\equiv0\pmod p,
\]
verified by exact modular summation.

Conversely, a proof would need a new identity beyond the standard binomial, hypergeometric, Lucas, and reflection formulas.

## Conclusion

The proposed implication is not derivable from the standard Apéry identities alone. The smallest rigorously identified missing ingredient is a finite-field transformation or recurrence invariant proving that the quadratic coefficient locus cannot intersect the Apéry zero set. Until such an identity or an explicit counterexample is produced, the statement must be treated as unresolved, not proved.
