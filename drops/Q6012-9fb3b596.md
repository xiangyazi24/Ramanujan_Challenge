## 5. Audit of the proposed analytic routes

### 5.1 Pointwise height and primitive radical

For one first leaf, (3.11) gives

\[
\omega_{\asymp T}(\mathcal K_\lambda)
\ll T/L.
\]

Factoring by \(A_{q,t}/p\) does not improve the actual sector. If one divides that factor away, one deletes every genuine partner prime and retains only phantoms, which is forbidden in the favorable direction.

Multiplying the cofactors over all first leaves of a state is tautological. If \(|P_\sigma|=k\), then the product of the forced state-radical factors has logarithm

\[
\asymp k(k-1)L,
\]

while the product of \(k\) cofactors has the available height \(O(kT)\). The resulting inequality is only

\[
k\ll T/L.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The same bound follows directly from \(A_{q,t}\). There is no threshold-scale tail.

### 5.2 Divisor switching

By (V2), divisor switching is exact:

\[
\sum_\lambda w_\lambda
 \sum_{\substack{p'\in\mathcal I_\lambda\\p'\mid C_{\lambda,p'-p}}}1
=
\sum_{p'\asymp T\atop p'\ {m prime}}
 \sum_{\substack{\lambda:\ p'\in\mathcal I_\lambda\\p'\mid\mathcal K_\lambda}}
 w_\lambda.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Here \(w_\lambda\) is the inherited occurrence multiplicity. Nothing has been divided by the selected mass.

For one state with \(k\) leaves, the actual part of the incidence matrix in (5.2) is the upper-triangular all-ones matrix on the ordered prime support. Its row sums and column sums are \(\asymp k\), and its largest singular value is \(\asymp k\). Transposing it does not create dispersion.

Any divisor-switching theorem must therefore prove that these state cliques are rare or small. That is the original radical-tail problem.

### 5.3 Congruence dispersion in \(\Delta\)

The moving difference \(\Delta=p'-p\) does not divide \(D_\Delta\) universally, and no congruence class of \(p'\) is forced by (2.1). The exact useful congruence is instead (V2), in which \(\Delta\) vanishes and \(p'\) is merely a prime divisor of \(\mathcal K_\lambda\).

The final deletion \(\Delta>T^{4/5-o(1)}\) restricts the interval in which those prime divisors lie. It supplies no saving for the number of prime divisors of an arbitrary nonzero integer of height \(\exp(O(T))\).

### 5.4 No bounded-degree Apéry polynomial in the shift

The recurrence transfer from \(z\) to \(z+7\Delta\), or from \(a-13\Delta\) to \(a\), has length \(\Theta(\Delta)\). Its cleared continuants have degrees growing linearly with \(\Delta\). There is no fixed-degree polynomial in \(\Delta\) to which a root bound can be applied.

The fixed-cofactor congruence (V2) is not such a polynomial theorem. It compares a different modulus for every \(\Delta\). A prime-divisor count for one integer of exponential height is not bounded by the degree of a polynomial over one field.

### 5.5 Bilinear and projective large sieves

Write

\[
C_{\lambda,\Delta}
=U_\lambda b_{z'}-V_\lambda b_{a'}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Modulo \(p'\), determinant divisors split into three qualitatively different sectors.

1. **Rank zero:**
   \(b_{z'}\equiv b_{a'}\equiv0\pmod{p'}\).
   This is the actual partner sector.
2. **Content/one-zero degeneracies:** one of the four factors in (5.3) is zero.
3. **Nonsingular ratio cancellation:** all relevant factors are units and

   \[
   \frac{b_{z'}}{b_{a'}}
   \equiv
   \frac{V_\lambda}{U_\lambda}\pmod{p'}.
   \]

A multiplicative-character or projective-incidence estimate can address only sector 3 without additional case work. Sector 1 is not a random ratio event; it is the exact fixed-gcd factor in (V4). Even perfect square-root cancellation in sectors 2–3 leaves the entire actual residual.

### 5.6 Averaging before absolute values

The additive expansion is

\[
\mathbf1_{p'\mid C}
=
\frac1{p'}
\sum_{h\bmod p'}e_{p'}(hC).
\tag{5.4}
\]

On every actual partner occurrence, \(C\equiv0\pmod{p'}\), so

\[
e_{p'}(hC)=1
\]

for **every** frequency \(h\), not merely the zero frequency. The rank-zero clique is fully coherent in the nonzero frequencies. Averaging before absolute values can save only on the phantom cancellation sector after the actual sector has been separated.

A dispersion inequality that silently deletes the rank-zero terms would be deleting actual partners, not proving a favorable majorant.

### 5.7 Ordinary large sieve in the integer values

The integers \(\mathcal K_\lambda\) range up to \(\exp(O(T))\). Applying a large sieve to their residues without a low-complexity parameterization gives a conductor/length on that exponential scale and no useful bound.

Expanding a second moment over shifted primes produces pairwise gcds

Equation (4.7) shows that these gcds are macroscopically large for first leaves from one selected state. The diagonal obstruction is structural, not an artifact of taking absolute values too early.

### 5.8 The upper selected mark cannot be normalized away

The condition

\[

is retained in every state and every occurrence. It is shared by all lower leaves and all partner pairs over that state. It is not an independent probability factor per shifted prime divisor.

Summing over \(q,t\) first is legitimate, but dividing the resulting pair mass by the number of selected states is not. The hostile state cliques use the same actual upper mark for all their edges.
