# Repeated-folded-anchor sector audit

## 1. Normalization

Let
\[
B_n=\sum_k {n\choose k}^2{n+k\choose k}^2,
\qquad Z_p=\{u<p:B_u\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]
For a shell hit at a prime \(p\in(X,2X]\), write the folded digit as \(u_p\in[0,X]\) with the reflection sign recorded separately. The ordered triple contribution with two equal folded anchors is therefore indexed by
\[
(p,q,\ell,m),\qquad u_p=u_q=u,\quad u_\ell=v.
\]
The falling-third normalization is ordered: each unordered triple of distinct primes gives \(3!\) orderings. Hence the repeated-anchor sector is a subset of the ordered third moment, not an additional multiplicity factor.

## 2. Same-anchor primes are divisors of one fixed Apéry value

If \(u_p=u_q=u\), then
\[
p\mid B_u,\qquad q\mid B_u.
\]
Thus the two same-anchor primes are distinct shell divisors of the single integer \(B_u\). The reflection-only countermodel fails here: it permits arbitrary many primes at the same artificial anchor, while the actual Apéry condition forces all such primes into the divisor set of \(B_u\).

However, no unconditional bound of the required strength follows merely from this observation. The trivial divisor bound for \(B_u\) is far too weak because \(B_u\) has exponential size.

## 3. Continuant reduction

For \(0\le u<v\), the exact Casoratian identity gives
\[
H_vB_u-(v!/u!)^3H_uB_v=(u!)^3N_{v-u}(u).
\]
If a prime \(r\) divides both \(B_u\) and \(B_v\), then after reduction modulo \(r\), the left side vanishes and therefore
\[
r\mid N_{v-u}(u)
\]
(up to the stated factorial valuation corrections when \(r\le v\)). For shell primes and \(u,v<X\), the factorials are invertible, so the clean divisor implication holds.

This controls collisions between two different anchors. It does not control multiple shell divisors of one fixed \(B_u\).

## 4. Exact residual reduction

The repeated-anchor triple count can be reduced to the fixed-index divisor moment
\[
\sum_{u<X}\binom{\omega_X(B_u)}2\,\#\{\ell\in(X,2X]:u_\ell\in Z_\ell\text{ at the same row}\},
\]
where \(\omega_X(B_u)\) counts distinct shell primes dividing \(B_u\).

This is strictly smaller than the original triple problem because the first two coordinates are frozen to a single Apéry value. It is not equivalent to the total third moment.

## 5. Obstruction to completion

The available recurrence, reflection, and Wronskian identities prove:

* no two different anchors can share a shell prime except through the explicit continuant divisor condition;
* repeated anchors reduce to shell divisor multiplicities of one fixed \(B_u\).

They do not prove the missing estimate
\[
\sum_{u<X}\binom{\omega_X(B_u)}2\ll X^{1+o(1)}\lambda_X^2
\]
or its weighted row analogue needed for
\[
X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\]
A claim that the third coordinate is lifted by recurrence/reflection alone is therefore only a restatement of the original DSEL problem.

## Conclusion

The unconditional result is the fixed-index divisor-moment reduction above. The additional arithmetic datum required is a genuine bound on shell-prime divisor clustering of individual Apéry values \(B_u\) (with valuations if needed). The all-distinct-anchor sector remains separate and requires the independent three-coordinate argument.
