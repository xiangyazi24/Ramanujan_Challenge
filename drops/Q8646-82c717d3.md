# Q8646 audit: repeated-anchor divisor gate

## 1. Exact formulation

Fix `u,v<X` and fixed signed offsets. We count triples of distinct shell primes

\[
X<p,q,r\le 2X
\]

with coefficients

\[
1\le \alpha\le X-1,\qquad 2\le\beta\le X-1,
\]

and the appropriate signed range for `\gamma`, satisfying

\[
p,q\mid B_u,\qquad r\mid B_v,
\]

and

\[
\beta q-\alpha p=2u+1,
\]

\[
\gamma r-\alpha p=u-z_k.
\]

The first equation is the repeated-anchor constraint. The second is the third-prime completion constraint. The question is whether these two affine relations alone force the desired

\[
o(X/\log ^3X)
\]

bound.

The answer is: no. The affine geometry gives a genuine saving over the unrestricted three-prime count, but it does not by itself produce a logarithmic power saving. The missing input is an Apéry-specific restriction on large prime divisors of the fixed sequences `B_u` and on their correlations.

## 2. What the affine equations actually imply

For a fixed ordered pair `(p,q)`, the equation

\[
\beta q-\alpha p=h,
\qquad h=2u+1,
\]

has at most one coefficient pair `(\alpha,\beta)` in the allowed rectangle.

Indeed, if two solutions exist then

\[
(\beta-\beta')q=(\alpha-\alpha')p.
\]

Since `p,q` are distinct primes, `p|\beta-\beta'` and `q|\alpha-\alpha'`. But

\[
|\beta-\beta'|<X<p,
\]

so `\beta=\beta'`, and similarly `\alpha=\alpha'`.

Therefore the first gate gives only

\[
\#\{(p,q)\}\le \omega_X(B_u)^2,
\]

where

\[
\omega_X(B_u)=\#\{X<p\le2X:p\mid B_u\}.
\]

The second affine equation then gives at most one admissible `\gamma` for a fixed `(p,r)`, by the same determinant argument.

Hence the exact geometric reduction is

\[
N(u,v)\le \sum_{p\mid B_u}\sum_{q\mid B_u}1_{\beta q-\alpha p=2u+1}\,\omega_X(B_v).
\]

No factor of `\log^{-3}X` appears.

## 3. Determinant/Farey geometry

Rewrite the first relation as

\[
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\frac{h}{\beta q}=\frac{p}{q}.
\]

The determinant identity is

\[
\beta q-\alpha p=h.
\]

This means the pair `(p,q)` lies on one of finitely many short Farey arcs. The area heuristic suggests sparsity, but the available conclusion is only a divisor-count estimate.

The number of possible coefficient pairs is at most

\[
(X-1)(X-2)=O(X^2),
\]

and each gives at most one prime pair. Thus the affine system alone gives the trivial upper bound

\[
O(X^2),
\]

before the Apéry divisibility conditions are imposed.

The divisibility conditions replace this by

\[
O(\omega_X(B_u)^2\omega_X(B_v)).
\]

This is the sharp unconditional consequence of the two affine equations.

## 4. Why large-prime divisor bounds are the missing lemma

To obtain

\[
o(X/\log^3X),
\]

one would need something like

\[
\omega_X(B_u)^2\omega_X(B_v)=o(X/\log^3X)
\]

uniformly for every `u,v<X`.

Nothing in the affine equations implies this.

A standard divisor bound gives only

\[
\omega_X(N)\le \frac{\log |N|}{\log X},
\]

and since Apéry numbers satisfy

\[
\log B_u\asymp u,
\]

this gives

\[
\omega_X(B_u)=O(1),
\]

only when `u=O(\log X)`. For the full range `u<X`, this becomes

\[
O(X/\log X),
\]

which is far too weak.

Thus a needed additional statement is an Apéry-specific large-prime divisor theorem.

A sufficient form is:

> For every `u<X`, the number of shell primes dividing `B_u` and participating in a repeated-anchor relation is `X^{o(1)}` uniformly, with a saving after averaging over the anchor.

This is essentially the missing repeated-anchor gate `A_2`.

## 5. Explicit adversarial coefficient-feasible model

The affine equations permit the following abstract construction.

Choose a set

\[
S\subset(X,2X]
\]

of size `M`. Define an abstract sequence value with

\[
p\mid B_u\quad(p\in S).
\]

Choose a prime `p_0\in S`. For each `q\in S` define

\[
\alpha_q=1,
\qquad
\beta_q=(2u+1+p_0)/q,
\]

whenever this is integral and in the coefficient range. There is no contradiction with the frozen affine conditions. The model can be arranged by choosing admissible pairs in a finite residue class construction.

The resulting incidence graph has many repeated-anchor pairs while preserving:

* distinct shell primes;
* coefficient ranges;
* the two affine equations;
* fixed indices `u,v`.

Therefore the affine constraints alone cannot imply the required asymptotic.

## 6. Minimal missing Apéry statement

The smallest genuinely Apéry-specific lemma needed is not an S-unit theorem for the affine equations. It is a correlation bound on large prime divisors of one fixed Apéry term:

\[
\boxed{
\#\{(p,q):p,q\in(X,2X],\ p,q\mid B_u,\ \beta q-\alpha p=2u+1\}
=o(X/\log^3X).
}
\]

Any theorem implying this is sufficient. Examples would be:

1. a strong large-prime divisor sparsity theorem for `B_u`;
2. an S-unit theorem applied to the actual Apéry sequence values;
3. an independent correlation estimate for the zero sets `Z_p`.

The affine geometry itself is insufficient.

## Conclusion

The two affine equations provide uniqueness of coefficients and reduce the repeated-anchor gate to a fixed-index divisor-correlation problem. They do not yield a power saving or a logarithmic saving on their own. The exact obstruction is the possible concentration of many shell prime divisors inside one fixed Apéry number `B_u`. The missing ingredient is therefore an Apéry-specific large-prime divisor/correlation lemma, not further manipulation of the affine equations.
