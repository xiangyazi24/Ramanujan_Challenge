ANSWER Q895 29f0215c

# Audit verdict

The proposed criterion is sufficient after correcting the normalization of the Fejer input and the quantifier on the block parameter. The claimed estimate is a one-sided real-part estimate and is not required to imply a two-sided bound.

## Corrected theorem statement

For each fixed q, assume that for every dyadic block P with P asymp_q N and with the block range used in the q-decomposition, at M=4N one has

\[
\operatorname{Re} B_{P,4N} \leq \varepsilon_q(N)N,
\]

where \(\varepsilon_q(N)\to0\) as \(N\to\infty\), uniformly over all admissible blocks. Then

\[
\log\gcd(L_n,b_n)=o(n).
\]

The uniformity in q is not needed globally: q is fixed first, and only the finitely many blocks required for that q enter.

## Audit of the implication

1. Fejer step.

The Fejer estimate controls the positive Fourier contribution entering \(B_{P,M}\). Only the upper bound on the real part is used. Replacing it by \(|B_{P,M}|=o(N)\) would be stronger than necessary.

2. Block cover.

For fixed q, the interval

\[
 n/(q+1)<p\leq n/q
\]

is covered by O_q(1) dyadic blocks with P asymp_q N. The number of blocks is independent of n. Therefore the accumulated error is

\[
O_q(1)\varepsilon_q(N)N=o(n).
\]

3. Lucas reduction.

The reduction from primes in a q-block to the corresponding residue conditions uses Apéry-Lucas only in the form

\[
p\mid b_m\iff p\mid b_{m-p}
\]

for p in the relevant range. No claim that separate congruences modulo different primes combine into a modulo-pq congruence is used.

4. q-tail.

The omitted tail consists of the blocks where the quotient parameter exceeds the fixed q range. The existing q-tail estimate contributes o(n), provided q is fixed before the limit. It cannot be made uniform in q without an additional argument.

## Quantifier correction

The valid order is:

\[
\forall q\;\exists N_0(q)\;\forall N>N_0(q):
\operatorname{Re}B_{P,4N}\leq\varepsilon_q(N)N
\]

uniformly over the finitely many P in the q-cover.

It is not the statement

\[
\exists\varepsilon(N)\to0\;\forall q,
\]

which would be substantially stronger.

## Remaining caveats

The criterion does not imply the stronger SDC estimate

\[
\operatorname{Re}B\ll N^{2/3+o(1)}.
\]

It only supplies the logarithmic saving required by the carrier argument. Thus it is a strictly weaker sufficient condition.

The corrected implication is therefore valid under the stated uniform-in-block, fixed-q interpretation. No counterexample appears at the level of normalization or sign; the only gaps were the possible ambiguity between one-sided real-part control and two-sided magnitude control, and the order of q and N quantifiers.
