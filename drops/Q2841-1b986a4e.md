# 1. The affine constant and what it records

Put
\[
F=N_h(x),\qquad G=N_k(x+h),
\]
with
\[
m=\deg F=3(h-1),\qquad n=\deg G=3(k-1).
\]
The separated root strips proved in `meso_result.tex` imply
\(\gcd_{\mathbb Q[x]}(F,G)=1\). Hence
\[
(F,G)\cap\mathbb Z=c_{h,k}\mathbb Z
\]
for a unique positive integer \(c_{h,k}\).

The first basic fact is exactly affine, not projective.

## Proposition 1: affine support criterion

For every rational prime \(p\),
\[
\boxed{
 p\mid c_{h,k}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \gcd_{\mathbb F_p[x]}(\bar F,\bar G)\ne1.
}
\]
Here the gcd is taken after the actual reductions are trimmed to their
true degrees. Thus:

- a common projective point only at infinity does **not** put \(p\) into
  \(c_{h,k}\);
- an irreducible nonsplit common factor of degree \(d>1\) **does** put
  \(p\) into \(c_{h,k}\);
- the assertion records only the existence of an affine algebraic common
  component, not its degree or multiplicity.

### Proof

If \(p\nmid c_{h,k}\), reduce an integral Bezout identity for
\(c_{h,k}\) modulo \(p\); the nonzero scalar \(\bar c_{h,k}\) is a unit,
so \((\bar F,\bar G)=\mathbb F_p[x]\).

Conversely, suppose \((\bar F,\bar G)=\mathbb F_p[x]\). Lift a modular
Bezout identity to obtain
\[
 A F+B G=1+pH
\]
with \(A,B,H\in\mathbb Z[x]\). In
\[
R=\mathbb Z_{(p)}[x]/(F,G)
\]
this says that \(p\) is a unit. But \(c_{h,k}=0\) in \(R\), and
\(c_{h,k}=p^a u\) with \(u\in\mathbb Z_{(p)}^\times\). Therefore
\(c_{h,k}\) is a unit in \(R\), so \(R=0\). Clearing denominators gives
an integer in \((F,G)\) not divisible by \(p\), hence \(p\nmid c_{h,k}\).
This proves the equivalence. \(\square\)

Since the ordinary Sylvester resultant is an integral combination of
\(F\) and \(G\), one always has
\[
 c_{h,k}\mid |\operatorname{Res}(F,G)|.
\]
The quotient is precisely where pure infinity factors and the Smith
multiplicity lost by the exponent of the affine quotient can live.

# 2. Correct Sylvester coordinates, and why raw adjugation is not enough

Write
\[
F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}f_i x^i,
\qquad
G(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}g_j x^j.
\]
Use ascending coefficient columns in the basis
\[
(1,x,\ldots,x^{m+n-1}).
\]
Define the square Sylvester map
\[
\Phi_{m+n-1}:\mathbb Z[x]_{<n}\oplus\mathbb Z[x]_{<m}
 \longrightarrow \mathbb Z[x]_{<m+n},
\qquad (A,B)\longmapsto AF+BG.
\]
Its matrix \(S(F,G)\) has, in this order, the coefficient columns of
\[
F,xF,\ldots,x^{n-1}F,
\quad
G,xG,\ldots,x^{m-1}G.
\]
With the standard root-product convention for the resultant,
\[
\boxed{
 \det S(F,G)=(-1)^{mn}\operatorname{Res}(F,G).
}
\]
Let
\[
e_0=(1,0,\ldots,0)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad D=\det S,
\qquad w=\operatorname{adj}(S)e_0.
\]
The least positive constant obtainable with the displayed degree bounds is
\[
\boxed{
 c_{\rm Syl}(F,G)
 =\frac{|D|}{\gcd(|D|,w_1,\ldots,w_{m+n})}.
}
\]
Indeed, Cramer's rule gives
\[
S^{-1}(a e_0)=\frac{a}{D}\operatorname{adj}(S)e_0,
\]
so the displayed quotient is exactly the least \(a>0\) for which all
coordinates are integral.

## Critical audit point: \(c_{\rm Syl}\) need not equal \(c\)

Take
\[
F=2x-1,\qquad G=2x+1.
\]
Then
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\2&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad |D|=4,
\qquad \operatorname{adj}(S)e_0=(2,-2)^{\mathsf T},
\]
so \(c_{\rm Syl}=2\). But
\[
1=xF+(1-x)G,
\]
so the true affine constant is \(c=1\). Modulo \(2\), both leading terms
disappear and the reduced polynomials are nonzero constants; the factor
\(2\) is purely at infinity. This is exactly the failure mode that the
question asks us not to conceal.

## The exact saturated formula

For every \(D\ge m+n-1\), let
\[
\Phi_D:
 \mathbb Z[x]_{\le D-m}\oplus\mathbb Z[x]_{\le D-n}
 \longrightarrow \mathbb Z[x]_{\le D},
\qquad (A,B)\longmapsto AF+BG,
\]
and let \(M_D(F,G)\) be its matrix in ascending monomial bases. Its
columns are
\[
x^iF\ (0\le i\le D-m),
\qquad
x^jG\ (0\le j\le D-n).
\]
It has \(D+1\) rows and full row rank over \(\mathbb Q\). For a full-row-
rank integer matrix \(M\) with \(r\) rows, write \(\Delta_r(M)\) for the
positive gcd of all \(r\times r\) minors. Put
\[
\delta_D=\Delta_{D+1}(M_D),
\qquad
\delta_D^+=\Delta_{D+1}([M_D\mid e_0]).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 c_D(F,G)=\frac{\delta_D}{\delta_D^+}
}
\]
is the least positive constant admitting a Bezout representation whose
products have degree at most \(D\), and
\[
\boxed{
 c(F,G)=c_D(F,G)\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }D.
}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 c(F,G)=\gcd_{D\ge m+n-1}c_D(F,G).
\]

### Proof

Let \(L_D\subseteq\mathbb Z^{D+1}\) be the column lattice of \(M_D\).
Smith normal form gives
\[
[\mathbb Z^{D+1}:L_D]=\delta_D,
\qquad
[\mathbb Z^{D+1}:L_D+\mathbb Ze_0]=\delta_D^+.
\]
Therefore the order of the class of \(e_0\) modulo \(L_D\), which is the
least represented positive constant, is \(\delta_D/\delta_D^+\).
As \(D\) increases the allowed identities increase, so
\(c_{D+1}\mid c_D\). Any representation of the true generator \(c(F,G)\)
has finite degree, so eventually \(c_D=c(F,G)\). \(\square\)

At \(D=m+n-1\), this determinantal-divisor formula is exactly the square
adjugate formula above. The larger \(D\)'s perform the required affine
saturation.

Prime-locally there is a clean cutoff. If at least one of \(f_m,g_n\) is
a \(p\)-adic unit, then polynomial division by that polynomial is integral
over \(\mathbb Z_p\), and every constant identity reduces to the standard
Sylvester degree bounds. Hence
\[
\boxed{
 p\nmid\gcd(f_m,g_n)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 v_p(c)=v_p(c_{\rm Syl}).
}
\]
Only common leading-coefficient primes require the higher Macaulay
saturation. Those are exactly the primes at which projective infinity can
pollute the raw resultant.

# 3. The exact good-prime Smith formula

Assume first that \(p\nmid f_m\). Normalize
\[
\widehat F=f_m^{-1}F\in\mathbb Z_p[x],
\]
which is monic, and put
\[
\mathcal O_{F,p}=\mathbb Z_p[x]/(\widehat F).
\]
This is free of rank \(m\), with ordered basis
\[
1,x,\ldots,x^{m-1}.
\]
Let \(T_G\in M_m(\mathbb Z_p)\) be the multiplication matrix whose
\(j\)-th column is the coefficient vector of
\[
x^jG\bmod\widehat F,
\qquad 0\le j<m.
\]
Write its Smith exponents as
\[
0\le\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_m,
\]
so its invariant factors are units times \(p^{\lambda_i}\).

## Proposition 2: last invariant factor

One has
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(c_{h,k})=\lambda_m.
}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i
 =v_p(\det T_G)
 =v_p(\operatorname{Res}(F,G)).
}
\]
If
\[
r_p=\deg\gcd_{\mathbb F_p[x]}(\bar F,\bar G),
\]
then exactly \(r_p\) of the \(\lambda_i\)'s are positive. Consequently,
for \(r_p>0\),
\[
\boxed{
 \left\lceil\frac{v_p(\operatorname{Res}(F,G))}{r_p}\right\rceil
 \le v_p(c_{h,k})
 \le v_p(\operatorname{Res}(F,G))-r_p+1.
}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(c_{h,k})-1
 \le v_p(\operatorname{Res}(F,G))-r_p.
}
\]

### Proof

The quotient
\[
\mathcal O_{F,p}/G\mathcal O_{F,p}
 \simeq \mathbb Z_p[x]/(F,G)
\]
is a ring. The least \(e\) for which \(p^e\) kills its element \(1\) is
also the least \(e\) killing the whole additive module, because
\(p^ea=(p^e1)a\). Thus \(v_p(c)\) is the exponent of the cokernel of
\(T_G\), namely its largest Smith exponent \(\lambda_m\).

The determinant is the norm of \(G\) in the monic algebra, hence the
resultant up to the leading-coefficient unit. Reduction modulo \(p\) gives
\[
\operatorname{coker}(T_G)\otimes\mathbb F_p
 \simeq \mathbb F_p[x]/(\bar F,\bar G),
\]
whose dimension is \(r_p\). This is the number of positive Smith
exponents. The two inequalities follow by comparing the maximum of
\(r_p\) positive integers with their sum. \(\square\)

If \(p\nmid g_n\) instead, swap \(F\) and \(G\). Thus the proposition
covers every prime except those dividing both leading coefficients. The
saturated Macaulay formula in Section 2 is the exact replacement at those
remaining primes.

This proposition gives the requested distinctions in one line:

- the raw resultant records the **sum** of all local Smith exponents;
- \(c_{h,k}\) records only their **maximum**;
- \(r_p\) records how many are positive;
- nonsplit irreducible factors contribute to \(r_p\) and hence to \(c\);
- pure infinity contributes to the raw Sylvester determinant but disappears
  after affine saturation.

# 4. What the continuant identities actually do to Smith modules

Set
\[
H=N_{h+k}(x),
\quad
A=N_{k+1}(x+h-1),
\quad
Q=(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x),
\]
\[
U=N_{h+k-1}(x+1),
\quad
C=N_{h-1}(x+1),
\quad
R=S_h(x)^6.
\]
The two identities are
\[
H=FA-QG,
\qquad
UF-CH=RG.
\]
Work at a prime for which \(F\) is monic after multiplication by a
\(p\)-adic unit, and put
\[
B=\mathbb Z_p[x]/(F).
\]
Bars are omitted from classes in \(B\). Then
\[
H=-QG.
\]
The maps given by multiplication by \(G\) and \(Q\) are injective on the
free \(\mathbb Z_p\)-module \(B\), because each is nonzero in
\(B\otimes\mathbb Q_p\). Hence there is an exact sequence
\[
\boxed{
0\longrightarrow B/GB
 \xrightarrow{\;\cdot Q\;}
 B/HB
 \longrightarrow B/QB
 \longrightarrow0.
}
\]
Combining the two continuant identities gives
\[
(CQ-R)G=0\quad\text{in }B.
\]
Injectivity of multiplication by \(G\) therefore gives the exact
saturation relation
\[
\boxed{CQ=R=S_h^6\quad\text{in }B.}
\]

Let
\[
\ell_p(T)=\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_p}(B/TB),
\qquad
e_p(T)=v_p(\operatorname{exponent}(B/TB)).
\]
The exact sequence yields
\[
\boxed{
\ell_p(H)=\ell_p(G)+\ell_p(Q),
}
\]
and only the sharp inequalities
\[
\boxed{
\max\{e_p(G),e_p(Q)\}
 \le e_p(H)
 \le e_p(G)+e_p(Q).
}
\]
The length identity is the resultant/norm multiplication. The exponent is
the last Smith factor, and it has no exact additive or max recurrence.
Cauchy--Binet expresses the codimension-one minors of a product as sums of
products of minors; their gcd depends on alignment, not only on the two
input determinantal divisors.

## Off the boundary, restart preserves the full local algebra

Let \(\mathfrak m\) be a maximal ideal of \(B\) containing \(p\) and
\(G\), and suppose
\[
S_h\notin\mathfrak m.
\]
Then \(R=S_h^6\) is a unit in \(B_{\mathfrak m}\). Since \(CQ=R\), both
\(C\) and \(Q\) are units there. Hence
\[
(H)=(G)\quad\text{in }B_{\mathfrak m},
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
(B/GB)_{\mathfrak m}\simeq(B/HB)_{\mathfrak m}.
}
\]
This is stronger than support equivalence: all local lengths, nilpotents,
ramification, and Smith exponents are transported unchanged. The restart
map sends the separated pair \((h,k)\) to the same-column pair
\((h,h+k)\); it is a relabelling to a larger level, not a strict-height
charge.

At the boundary, the discrepancy is exactly the \(Q\)-module in the short
exact sequence. Thus the identities separate affine restart mass from
boundary mass, but they do not make the affine mass smaller.

# 5. The Smith bounds are sharp even with a sixth-power saturation relation

The failure is not a peculiarity of noncommuting matrices. Let
\[
B=\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_p
 \simeq \mathbb Z_p[x]/(x(x-1)),
\]
a finite etale monogenic algebra. Fix \(a,b\ge1\).

## Aligned model

Take
\[
g=(p^a,1),
\qquad
s=(p^b,1),
\qquad
q=s^6=(p^{6b},1),
\qquad
h=-qg.
\]
With \(c=1\) and \(r=s^6=q\), the exact quotient identities are
\[
h=-qg,
\qquad
-ch=rg.
\]
The exponents are
\[
e(g)=a,\qquad e(q)=6b,\qquad e(h)=a+6b.
\]
The upper bound is attained.

## Transverse model

Take instead
\[
g=(p^a,1),
\qquad
s=(1,p^b),
\qquad
q=s^6=(1,p^{6b}),
\qquad
h=-qg.
\]
The same two identities hold, but now
\[
e(h)=\max\{a,6b\}.
\]
The lower bound is attained.

Thus even in a reduced, finite etale, commutative algebra, and even with
\(q\) literally a sixth power and \(cq=s^6\), the continuant quotient
relations do not determine the last Smith factor. They determine the total
length and the extension, not its invariant-factor alignment.

# 6. Full-scale affine counterfamily

The following family rules out any generic inequality derived only from
primitivity, monicity, degrees, coefficient heights, affine saturation, and
the resultant.

## Proposition 3: same resultant, opposite Smith concentration

Let \(n\ge m\ge2\), let \(p\) be prime, and let \(L\ge1\). Put
\[
F_m(x)=x^m,
\]
\[
G_{\rm flat}(x)=x^n+p^L,
\qquad
G_{\rm conc}(x)=x^n+x+p^L.
\]
All three polynomials are monic and primitive, and both pairs are coprime
over \(\mathbb Q\). One has
\[
\operatorname{Res}(F_m,G_{\rm flat})
 =\operatorname{Res}(F_m,G_{\rm conc})
 =p^{Lm}.
\]
But
\[
\boxed{
(F_m,G_{\rm flat})\cap\mathbb Z=p^L\mathbb Z,
}
\]
whereas
\[
\boxed{
(F_m,G_{\rm conc})\cap\mathbb Z=p^{Lm}\mathbb Z.
}
\]

### Proof

In \(\mathbb Z[x]/(x^m)\), \(x^n=0\). Thus multiplication by
\(G_{\rm flat}\) is the scalar \(p^L\), with Smith exponents
\[
(L,L,\ldots,L).
\]
Its quotient has exponent \(p^L\) and order \(p^{Lm}\).

For the concentrated pair,
\[
(x^m,G_{\rm conc})=(x^m,x+p^L).
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb Z[x]/(x^m,G_{\rm conc})
 \simeq \mathbb Z/(p^{Lm}),
\]
by sending \(x\) to \(-p^L\). Its Smith exponents are
\[
(0,\ldots,0,Lm).
\]
The two resultants are \(G(0)^m=p^{Lm}\). \(\square\)

Modulo \(p\), the flat pair has affine gcd \(x^m\), while the concentrated
pair has affine gcd exactly \(x\). Thus the concentrated example has one
distinct split affine common factor, no infinity degeneration at all, and
still
\[
 c=|\operatorname{Res}|.
\]
Taking \(L=n\) gives
\[
\log c=mn\log p,
\]
while the logarithmic coefficient height of \(G_{\rm conc}\) is only
\(n\log p\). This saturates the usual bilinear resultant-height scale.

The example is deliberately not the fixed Apéry recurrence: it proves the
logical no-go. Indeed, any pair \((F,G)\) can be embedded into the two
identity templates by taking
\[
Q=R=1,
\quad C=1,
\quad A=U=0,
\quad H=-G.
\]
Then
\[
H=FA-QG,
\qquad
UF-CH=RG,
\]
with \(R=1^6\). Hence the displayed identities as polynomial equations do
not exclude the counterfamily. The finite-etale models of Section 5 show
that even the sixth-power Smith alignment remains undetermined after one
keeps the saturation relation.

By choosing independent primes and these concentrated pairs on a triangular
index set, the aggregate
\[
\sum_{h+k\le H}\log c_{h,k}
\]
can have the full \(H^4\log H\) scale in the class of identity-compatible
polynomial systems. This is not a counterexample to the one fixed Apéry
sequence; it proves that any saving for that sequence must use arithmetic
special to
\[
P(t)=(2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5),
\]
not merely the universal continuant identities.

\[
\boxed{v_{4787}(c_{4,4})=1.}
\]
Thus \(c\) carries the nonsplit prime but forgets the degree-two common

## 7.4 A falsifiable next computation

At \((8,8,3109)\), the current census records two distinct split affine

by \(3109\) or has a higher lift. Any proposed universal transversality
statement is falsified by the first case with \(\lambda_{\max}>1\).

# 8. What is and is not obtained for the aggregate radical

For good primes define
\[
L_{h,k}(p)=v_p(\operatorname{Res}(F,G)),
\qquad
r_{h,k}(p)=\deg\gcd(\bar F,\bar G),
\qquad
\lambda_{h,k}(p)=v_p(c_{h,k}).
\]
The exact Smith inequality gives
\[
\lambda_{h,k}(p)
 \le 1+L_{h,k}(p)-r_{h,k}(p)
\]
on affine support. Therefore
\[
\log c_{h,k}^{\rm good}
\le
\log\operatorname{rad}(c_{h,k}^{\rm good})
+
\sum_p\bigl(L_{h,k}(p)-r_{h,k}(p)\bigr)\log p.
\]
This is the clean decomposition into

1. affine radical mass, and
2. excess \(p\)-adic intersection multiplicity.

The continuant identities control neither term downward. Away from the
boundary they preserve the complete local quotient, so both the radical
and the excess multiplicity are unchanged. At the boundary they append the
explicit \(Q\)-module. Thus no aggregate saving follows by summing the
restart identities.

It is also important to specify what “aggregate radical” means. The useful
quantity for the sum over pairs is
\[
\mathfrak R(H)=
\sum_{\substack{h,k\ge2\\h+k\le H}}
 \sum_{p\mid c_{h,k}}\log p,
\]
not merely
\(\log\operatorname{rad}\prod c_{h,k}\), which forgets how many pairs use
the same prime. Even a bound for \(\mathfrak R(H)\) does not control
\(\sum\log c_{h,k}\) unless the Smith-excess term is also bounded.

# 9. The smallest genuinely new Apéry-specific Smith statement

At a good prime, let \(T_{h,k,p}\) be the multiplication matrix of Section
3. Write
\[
\Delta_m(T)=|\det T|,
\qquad
\Delta_{m-1}(T)=\gcd\{\text{all }(m-1)\times(m-1)\text{ minors of }T\}.
\]
If the invariant factors are \(s_1\mid\cdots\mid s_m\), then
\[
\Delta_m=s_1\cdots s_m,
\qquad
\Delta_{m-1}=s_1\cdots s_{m-1},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
p^{v_p(c_{h,k})}
 =p^{v_p(\Delta_m(T))-v_p(\Delta_{m-1}(T))}.
}
\]
In Fitting-ideal language, the raw resultant is the zeroth Fitting ideal,
whereas the missing datum is the first Fitting ideal. The continuant
identities determine the zeroth Fitting ideal multiplicatively but do not
determine the first.

Therefore the narrowest new uniform statement that would suffice is the
following codimension-one determinantal-divisor gain.

## Apéry affine Fitting gain

After using the saturated Macaulay presentation at common-leading primes,
prove uniformly that
\[
\boxed{
\log\Delta_{m-1}^{\rm aff}(h,k)
\ge
\log\Delta_m^{\rm aff}(h,k)
-o\bigl(hk\log(h+k)\bigr).
}
\]
Equivalently, the last affine Smith invariant satisfies
\[
\log s_m^{\rm aff}(h,k)
=o\bigl(hk\log(h+k)\bigr).
\]
An aggregate power-saving version is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{h,k\ge2\\h+k\le H}}
 \left(
  \log\Delta_m^{\rm aff}(h,k)
 -\log\Delta_{m-1}^{\rm aff}(h,k)
 \right)
 \ll H^{4-\delta}
}
\]
for some \(\delta>0\). Either statement beats the raw
\(O(H^4\log H)\) ledger.

This is “smallest” in a literal Smith sense: \(\Delta_m\), the determinant,
is already the known resultant/norm. The first genuinely missing
arithmetic datum is \(\Delta_{m-1}\). The concentrated counterfamily has
\(\Delta_{m-1}=1\) and fails maximally; the flat counterfamily has
\(\Delta_{m-1}=p^{L(m-1)}\) and exhibits maximal compression. Thus the
statement is directly falsifiable by exact Smith computations.

If an aggregate radical theorem is proved first, the strictly smaller Smith
upgrade needed is
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak X(H)=
\sum_{\substack{h,k\ge2\\h+k\le H}}
 \sum_{p\mid c_{h,k}}
 \bigl(v_p(c_{h,k})-1\bigr)\log p
=o(H^4\log H).
}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\sum_{h+k\le H}\log c_{h,k}=\mathfrak R(H)+\mathfrak X(H).
\]
A stronger but especially clean falsifiable form would say that, outside an
explicit exceptional integer of aggregate logarithmic height
\(o(H^4\log H)\), every nonzero affine intersection module is killed by
\(p\); equivalently every positive Smith exponent is one. Nothing in the
current restart identities proves this.

# 10. Final conclusion

The exact continuant machinery gives:

\[
\text{determinant lengths add},
\qquad
\text{affine local modules are transported off the boundary},
\]
not
\[
\text{last Smith factors contract}.
\]

The raw square Sylvester adjugate must first be saturated at common-leading
primes. At good primes, \(c_{h,k}\) is exactly the last Smith invariant of
the affine multiplication matrix. The restart identities place this module
as a submodule of a larger-height module and preserve it locally away from
\(S_h=0\). Sharp finite-etale models realize both extremal Smith alignments,
and the monic concentrated family realizes
\(c=|\operatorname{Res}|\) at the full bilinear height scale with only one
affine common factor modulo \(p\).

Therefore no uniform or aggregate compression theorem follows from the
present continuant identities alone. A successful theorem for the actual
Apéry family must prove new arithmetic about the codimension-one minors of
the multiplication matrix, or equivalently about the first Fitting ideal
of the affine intersection module. That is the precise missing
Apéry-specific Smith statement.
