\[
a=a_n(p):=\min(r,p-1-r).
\]

Then the defining-characteristic bad event has the exact equivalent forms

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r

 \Theta_{p,r}=0
 \iff
 \mathcal H_p(a)=0
 \iff
 p\mid b_a,
}
\tag{V1}
\]

where `Theta_{p,r}` is the explicit slope-zero Morita unit sum and

\[
\mathcal H_p(X)=
\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/2}
\left(\frac{(-X)_k(X+1)_k}{(k!)^2}\right)^2
\in\mathbf F_p[X].
\tag{V2}
\]

At integral `0<=a<=(p-1)/2` the series truncates automatically at `k=a`, so `mathcal H_p(a)=b_a mod p`. In the K3/Mellin normalization already established in the repository, this is exactly the first Hasse invariant/unit-root obstruction for the corresponding character eigenspace.

\[
I_q(n)=\left(\frac n{q+1},\frac nq\right],

the switch occurs at

\[
p_q^*=\frac{2n+1}{2q+1}.
\]

On the upper half of the cell,

\[
a=n-qp,\qquad p=\frac{n-a}{q},
\tag{V3+}
\]

and on the lower half,

\[
a=(q+1)p-n-1,\qquad p=\frac{n+1+a}{q+1}.
\tag{V3-}
\]

Thus every horizontal bad prime is a prime divisor of `b_a` lying on one of the two rational lines `n-a=qp` or `n+1+a=(q+1)p`. This is the cleanest deterministic form I can obtain.

## 2. Exact Morita-Gamma formula, including the sign

Use Morita's integer normalization

\[
\Gamma_p(s)=(-1)^s
\prod_{\substack{1\le j<s\\p\nmid j}}j
\qquad(s\in\mathbf Z_{>0}).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `0<=j<=2p-2`, there is at most one multiple of `p` in `j!`, so

\[
j!
=
p^{\mathbf 1_{j\ge p}}
(-1)^{j+1}\Gamma_p(j+1).
\tag{2.2}
\]

For `0<=k<=r<=p-1`, set

\[
A_{r,k}:=\binom rk\binom{r+k}k
=\frac{(r+k)!}{(k!)^2(r-k)!}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Only `(r+k)!` can contain `p`. Applying (2.2), the total sign exponent is

\[
(r+k+1)-2(k+1)-(r-k+1)=-2,
\]

so the sign is **exactly +1**. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
A_{r,k}
=p^{\mathbf1_{r+k\ge p}}
U_{p,r,k},
\qquad
U_{p,r,k}:=
\frac{\Gamma_p(r+k+1)}
{\Gamma_p(k+1)^2\Gamma_p(r-k+1)}
\in\mathbf Z_p^\times.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Squaring gives the exact Apéry decomposition

\[
\boxed{
b_r=
\sum_{k=0}^r
p^{2\mathbf1_{r+k\ge p}}U_{p,r,k}^2.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Let

\[
a=\rho_p(r):=\min(r,p-1-r).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Then `r+k<p` exactly for `k<=a`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_{p,r}:=\sum_{k=0}^{a}\overline{U}_{p,r,k}^{\,2}
\in\mathbf F_p,
\qquad
p\mid b_r\iff\Theta_{p,r}=0.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

More strongly, because every omitted term is divisible by `p^2`,

\[
\boxed{
b_r\equiv\sum_{k=0}^aU_{p,r,k}^2\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{2.8}
\]

This is the exact slope-zero packet; there is no valuation-one packet.

### Endpoints

The Mellin congruence is an interior-character statement, but the Apéry endpoints are harmless and in fact stronger p-adically.

- `r=0`: `a=0`, `b_0=1`, `Theta=1`.
- `r=p-1`: `a=0`; every `k>=1` has `r+k>=p`, hence is divisible by `p^2`, so
  \[
  \boxed{b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^2}.}
  \tag{2.9}
  \]

Thus neither endpoint is bad.

## 3. Hypergeometric form of the slope-zero packet

For an integer `a>=0`,

\[
\binom ak\binom{a+k}k
=(-1)^k\frac{(-a)_k(a+1)_k}{(k!)^2}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The sign disappears on squaring, so

\[
\boxed{
b_a
={}_4F_3\!\left[
\begin{matrix}-a,-a,a+1,a+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1
\right]
=
\sum_{k=0}^a
\left(\frac{(-a)_k(a+1)_k}{(k!)^2}\right)^2.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For each odd prime define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_p(X)=
\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/2}
\left(\frac{(-X)_k(X+1)_k}{(k!)^2}\right)^2
\in\mathbf F_p[X].}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Every denominator is a p-unit. If `0<=a<=(p-1)/2`, then `(-a)_k=0` for `k>a`, hence

\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_p(a)=b_a\pmod p.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The degree bound from the displayed definition is

\[
\deg\mathcal H_p\le2(p-1),
\tag{3.5}
\]

so this is **not** a bounded-degree polynomial family in `p`. Merely saying “the Hasse polynomial is nonzero” gives no useful horizontal power saving; even a degree bound gives only `O(p)` roots, weaker than the manuscript's proved `Z(p)=O(p^(2/3))`.

### Exact finite-field all-character form

For completeness, the finite-field/Jacobi transform from the established normalization is as follows. Put `N=p-1`, let `T` generate the multiplicative character group compatibly with the Teichmüller choice, set

\[
\varphi=T^{N/2},\qquad\chi_r=T^r,
\]

extend characters by zero at `0`, and write

\[
J(A,B)=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}A(x)B(1-x).
\]

For `1<=r<=p-2`,

\[
\boxed{
 b_r\equiv-
 \frac1{p-1}\sum_{\eta}
 J(\varphi\eta\chi_r,\varphi\eta^{-1})^2
 J(\varphi\eta\chi_r^{-1},\varphi\eta^{-1})^2
 \pmod{\mathfrak p_p},}
\tag{3.6}
\]

where `eta` runs over **all `p-1` multiplicative characters**. The minus sign and `1/(p-1)` factor are part of this Jacobi normalization; they are not the sign in the Morita factorial identity (2.4), whose sign is +1.

Gross--Koblitz/Stickelberger gives valuations `0,2,4` for these Jacobi summands and exactly `a+1` valuation-zero summands. Thus neither representation produces `O(1)` summands: the Jacobi transform has `p-1`, while the minimum-slope Morita packet has `a+1`.

## 4. Reflection collapses the slope-zero sum exactly

Suppose first that `r<=(p-1)/2`. Then `a=r`, all Apéry terms have slope zero, and

\[
\Theta_{p,r}=b_r\pmod p=b_a\pmod p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now suppose `r>(p-1)/2` and put `a=p-1-r`. For `0<=k<=a`, reduction modulo `p` gives

\[
-r\equiv a+1,
\qquad
r+1\equiv-a.
\]

Therefore

\[
(-r)_k(r+1)_k
\equiv
(a+1)_k(-a)_k\pmod p,
\]

and term by term

\[
U_{p,r,k}^2
\equiv
\left(\binom ak\binom{a+k}k\right)^2
\pmod p.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Summing,

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_{p,r}\equiv b_a\pmod p,
\qquad a=\min(r,p-1-r).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{b_r\equiv b_{p-1-r}\pmod p.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This gives (V1).

### Unit-root/Hasse interpretation

In the rank-three K3/Mellin normalization already established in Q7690/Q7699, the residue `Theta_{p,r}` is the first Hasse invariant for the `r`-character Mellin eigenspace. Therefore, for interior `r`,

\[
\boxed{
 p\nmid b_r
 \iff \text{a slope-zero/unit-root direction is present},
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r
 \iff \mathcal H_p(a)=0
 \iff \text{that first Hasse invariant vanishes}.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is an exact conceptual restatement, not a horizontal estimate.

## 5. Uniformity in every quotient `q`

Let

\[
n=qp+r,\qquad0\le r\le p-1,
\qquad m=n-q.
\]

Then identically

\[
\boxed{m=q(p-1)+r,}
\tag{5.1}
\]

so `m==r mod p-1` for **every** `q`. On the interior range the given Mellin theorem therefore gives

\[
M_p(n-q)\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Combining with (4.3),

\[
\boxed{
M_p(n-q)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}
\iff
\mathcal H_p(a_n(p))=0,
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
a_n(p)=\min(n-qp,(q+1)p-n-1).}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The endpoints `r=0,p-1` are nonzero by Section 2 and need no Mellin interpolation.

### The two linear branches

The inequality selecting the unreflected branch is

\[
r\le\frac{p-1}{2}
\iff
(2q+1)p\ge2n+1.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the exact switch in `I_q(n)` is

\[
p_q^*=\frac{2n+1}{2q+1}.
\]

If `p>=p_q^*`,

\[
a=r=n-qp,
\qquad n-a=qp.
\tag{5.6}
\]

If `p<p_q^*`,

\[
a=p-1-r=(q+1)p-n-1,
\qquad n+1+a=(q+1)p.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Hence a bad prime can be reparameterized exactly by

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_a,
\qquad
 p\mid(n-a)(n+1+a),
\qquad
0\le a\le\frac{p-1}{2},}
\tag{5.8}
\]

together with the branch/quotient condition specifying whether the divisor is `(n-a)/q` or `(n+1+a)/(q+1)`.

This is potentially useful because the moving Mellin character has disappeared from the event. What remains is a transverse divisibility condition along two moving linear forms.

## 6. Search for a theorem with the correct quantifiers

### 6.1 What the checked Dwork/Hasse theorems actually give

I checked the following primary sources.

- Mellit--Vlasenko, *Dwork's congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial*, arXiv:1306.5811: prime-power congruences and p-adic analytic continuation for constant-term sequences.
- Adolphson--Sperber, *A-hypergeometric series and a p-adic refinement of the Hasse-Witt matrix*, arXiv:2001.07280: p-adic unit roots of a hypersurface over a finite field of characteristic `p`, expressed through an A-hypergeometric matrix.
- Delaygue, *Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers*, arXiv:1310.4131: p-adic valuation results and p-Lucas criteria for Apéry-like factorial ratios.

These sources justify the **prime-by-prime** Hasse/unit-root interpretation, congruences, and Lucas structure. None of the displayed theorems supplies a bound of the form

\[
\#\{X<p\le2X:\mathcal H_p(a_n(p))=0\}
\ll X^{1-\delta}
\tag{6.1}
\]

for a fixed integer `n` as the defining characteristic varies.

The important distinction is not cosmetic. Here both the residue field and the character eigenspace move with `p`; `a_n(p)` is one arithmetically selected point in each characteristic. A theorem that describes the ordinary locus **inside one characteristic** does not count this diagonal across characteristics.

### 6.2 Why the FKM/compatible-system large sieve still has the wrong axis

Perret-Gentil, *Exponential sums over finite fields and the large sieve*, arXiv:1703.06965, proves zero-density estimates for values `t(x)` as

\[
x\in\mathbf F_q,
\qquad q=p^e\to\infty,
\]

for coherent systems over a fixed base characteristic; the paper explicitly notes that in the Larsen--Pink route the implicit constant depends on `p`, forcing one to fix `p` and let `e->infinity`. Even in examples where integral monodromy makes constants uniform in `p`, the averaging variable is still `x` in each finite field, not a single adversarial/arithmetic point `x=a_n(p)` chosen as `p` varies.

So bounded FKM conductor is necessary geometric bookkeeping but does not imply (6.1). To use a compatible-system sieve here one would need an additional **prime-aspect coupling theorem** converting the family of defining-characteristic Hasse zeros into a compatible global Galois/Frobenius condition with the correct diagonal evaluation. That coupling is precisely what is absent.

### 6.3 Scoped conclusion

**[SCOPED NO-THEOREM]** In the checked primary sources, I find no theorem whose stated quantifiers imply `DDHdelta`, or even `DDH0`, for the Apéry diagonal. This does not assert bibliographic impossibility; it says the standard Dwork/Gross--Koblitz/Hasse-Witt and auxiliary-prime large-sieve results cannot be cited as the missing proof without adding a genuinely new prime-aspect theorem.

## 7. The smallest new arithmetic lemma that closes all quotient cells

Let

\[
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\}.
\]

For a dyadic block define exactly as in the manuscript

\[
K_X(m)=\#\{X<p\le2X:m\bmod p\in Z_p\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

By Sections 4--5 this is equally

\[
K_X(m)=
\#\{X<p\le2X:\mathcal H_p(\min(m\bmod p,p-1-m\bmod p))=0\}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

### DDH0

The smallest non-tautological pointwise density statement I see is

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le m<X^2}K_X(m)
=o\!\left(\frac X{\log X}\right).
}
\tag{DDH0}
\]

One can weaken the supremum to only the `m=n` and blocks occurring after quotient reduction, but (DDH0) is the clean standalone arithmetic lemma.

### Deterministic reduction from DDH0

Take the manuscript's

\[
Q_n=f(n)\log n,
\qquad f(n)\to\infty,
\qquad f(n)\le\log n
\]

eventually. Quotient reduction has already disposed of

\[
p\le n/Q_n.
\]

Cover the surviving interval `(n/Q_n,n]` by dyadic blocks `(X,2X]`. Their lower endpoints satisfy

\[
X\ge \frac{n}{2Q_n}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since `Q_n<=log^2 n`, for large `n`,

\[
n<X^2
\]

on every surviving block, so (DDH0) applies with `m=n`.

Write

\[
\varepsilon(Y)=
\sup_{X\ge Y}
\sup_{m<X^2}
\frac{K_X(m)\log X}{X}.
\]

Then `epsilon(Y)->0`. The log-weight of bad primes in one block is

\[
W_X(n)
:=\sum_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}\log p
\le K_X(n)\log(2X)
\ll\varepsilon(X)X.
\tag{7.4}
\]

The dyadic scales form a geometric progression, so

\[
\sum_X X\ll n,
\tag{7.5}
\]

not `n log Q_n`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p>n/Q_n\atop p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}\log p
\ll
\varepsilon\!\left(\frac{n}{2Q_n}\right)n
=o(n).}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Multiplying by the existing safe valuation cap `6` leaves `o(n)`. Together with quotient reduction, this proves the all-index target.

**There is no `Q_n` loss in (7.6).** The pointwise dyadic lemma has already aggregated all quotient cells represented in each prime block.

### Fixed power saving

A stronger but very clean target is: for some fixed `delta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
K_X(m)\ll X^{1-\delta}
\quad(0\le m<X^2).}
\tag{DDHdelta}
\]

Then

\[
W_X(n)\ll X^{1-\delta}\log X,
\]

and the geometric dyadic sum gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p>n/Q_n\atop p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}\log p
\ll n^{1-\delta}\log n.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Again there is no `Q_n^delta` factor. That factor appears only if one first proves a separate `P_q^(1-delta)` estimate in every quotient cell and then sums over `q`; the blockwise DDH lemma has already done the cross-`q` aggregation.

### Relation to the current `(HM)_8`

The manuscript has

\[
\lambda_X\ll X^{2/3}/\log X
\]

and under `(HM)_k`,

\[
\max_{m<X^2}K_X(m)
\ll X^{2/k+o(1)}\lambda_X+1
\ll\frac{X^{2/3+2/k+o(1)}}{\log X}+1.
\tag{7.8}
\]

For `k=8`, this is

\[
K_X(m)\ll X^{11/12+o(1)},
\]

so `(HM)_8` implies `DDHdelta` for every fixed `delta<1/12` eventually. Conversely, DDH0 says nothing about factorial moments averaged over all `m`; it only rules out a dense prime diagonal at each point. Thus DDH0 is strictly the smaller target suggested by the horizontal Mellin reduction.

## 8. What the slope break does give: a useful exceptional-strip theorem

The slope-zero packet length is

\[
a+1=1+\min(r,p-1-r).
\]

A very short packet is rare for purely geometric reasons in the quotient cells.

### Proposition 8.1 — short-prefix primes have small total mass

Fix `L>=0`. In one quotient cell `q`, the condition `a<=L` puts `p` in one of two endpoint intervals:

\[
\frac n{q+1}<p\le\frac{n+1+L}{q+1}
\tag{8.1-}
\]

or

\[
\frac{n-L}{q}\le p\le\frac nq.
\tag{8.1+}
\]

Their total number of integer points is

\[
O\!\left(\frac Lq+\frac{L+1}{q+1}+1\right).
\]

Summing `1<=q<Q_n`,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{p>n/Q_n:a_n(p)\le L\}
=O(L\log Q_n+Q_n).}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Hence their logarithmic prime mass is

\[
\boxed{
O((L\log Q_n+Q_n)\log n).}
\tag{8.3}
\]

For example, taking

\[
L=\frac{n}{\log n\,(\log Q_n)^2}
\]

makes (8.3) `o(n)` because `Q_n<=log^2 n`. Thus the unresolved central set may be assumed to have a **long** slope-zero packet:

\[
a_n(p)\gg\frac{n}{\log n(\log Q_n)^2}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

This is a genuine deterministic gain, but it does not solve the central range.

### A second height proof for very short `a`

The Apéry recurrence and positivity give a simple exponential bound. Since

\[
\frac{P(j)}{(j+1)^3}<34
\]

for `j>=0`, induction gives

\[
b_a<34^a.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Therefore all bad primes with `a<=L` divide

\[
\prod_{a\le L}b_a,
\]

so their distinct-prime log weight is at most

\[
\sum_{a\le L}\log b_a
\le\frac{\log34}{2}L(L+1).
\tag{8.6}
\]

Thus `L=o(sqrt n)` is another unconditional negligible strip. The interval-count proof (8.3) permits much larger `L`, so it is the more useful one here.

## 9. Reflection-gap stratification and the exact gap-polynomial no-go

Define the reflection gap

\[
h=p-1-2a=|p-1-2r|.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Using `r=n-qp`,

\[
\boxed{
h=|(2q+1)p-(2n+1)|.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Thus `h` measures distance from the quotient-cell switch point `p_q^*`.

### Proposition 9.1 — small reflection gap is negligible

If `h<=H`, then

\[
\left|p-\frac{2n+1}{2q+1}\right|
\le\frac H{2q+1}.
\]

Hence, over all `q<Q_n`,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{p>n/Q_n:h_n(p)\le H\}
=O(H\log Q_n+Q_n),}
\tag{9.3}
\]

and the log weight is

\[
O((H\log Q_n+Q_n)\log n).
\tag{9.4}
\]

So the same choice

\[
H=\frac{n}{\log n(\log Q_n)^2}
\]

removes the small-gap midpoint strips with `o(n)` mass.

After Sections 8--9, the genuinely unresolved primes can be restricted to

\[
a>L,
\qquad h>H,
\qquad2a+h=p-1.
\tag{9.5}
\]

There is no contradiction: a positive-fraction central range has both quantities large.

### The reflected-pair gap certificate is automatically zero

This is the important exact no-go.

Let `N_h(x)` be the cleared gap numerator from the manuscript, with

\[
N_1=1,
\qquad N_2=P(x+1),
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x).
\tag{9.6}
\]

Since

\[
P(-u-1)=-P(u),
\]

induction in (9.6) gives

\[
\boxed{N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).}
\tag{9.7}
\]

If `h` is even, (9.7) says `N_h` is odd around `x=-(h+1)/2`, hence

\[
\boxed{2x+h+1\mid N_h(x).}
\tag{9.8}
\]

For the reflection pair `a` and `p-1-a`,

\[
h=p-1-2a
\]

is automatically even, and

\[
2a+h+1=p.
\tag{9.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{N_h(a)\equiv0\pmod p}
\tag{9.10}
\]

**for every such reflected pair, independently of whether `b_a` is zero**.

The usual implication

\[
p\mid b_a,\ b_{a+h}\Longrightarrow p\mid N_h(a)
\]

therefore collapses to `0=0` on the exact reflection supplied by the Morita analysis. Dividing out the structural factor cannot be justified from a merely mod-p zero pair: the recurrence gives divisibility of `N_h(a)` by `p`, and that divisibility is already consumed by the factor (9.9).

This closes one tempting route completely at the present modulus.

## 10. The exact second p-adic digit of reflection

The reflection congruence has a clean first defect modulo `p^2`.

Let

\[
r=p-1-a,
\qquad0\le a\le\frac{p-2}{2}.
\]

For `0<=k<=a`, direct factorial products give

\[
\frac{A_{r,k}}{A_{a,k}}
=
\prod_{j=1}^k
\left(1-\frac p{a+j}\right)
\left(1-\frac p{a+1-j}\right).
\tag{10.1}
\]

All denominators are p-units. With

\[
H_t=\sum_{j=1}^t\frac1j,
\qquad H_0=0,
\]

(10.1) gives

\[
A_{r,k}
\equiv
A_{a,k}
\left(1-p(H_{a+k}-H_{a-k})\right)
\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Squaring,

\[
A_{r,k}^2
\equiv
A_{a,k}^2
\left(1-2p(H_{a+k}-H_{a-k})\right)
\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Every `k>a` term of `b_r` is already divisible by `p^2`. Therefore define

\[
D_{p,a}
:=\sum_{k=0}^a
\left(\binom ak\binom{a+k}k\right)^2
(H_{a+k}-H_{a-k})
\in\mathbf Z_{(p)}.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
b_{p-1-a}\equiv b_a-2pD_{p,a}\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{10.5}
\]

If `p|b_a`, write

\[
c_{p,a}:=b_a/p\pmod p.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
p^2\mid b_{p-1-a}
\iff
c_{p,a}\equiv2D_{p,a}\pmod p.}
\tag{10.6}
\]

This is a real second-digit invariant. It can potentially control higher multiplicity. But it gives **no saving for the radical**, because (10.5) reduces modulo `p` to exactly

\[
p\mid b_{p-1-a}\iff p\mid b_a.
\]

So a second-digit theorem cannot replace DDH0 unless it also proves a first-digit horizontal zero-density statement.

## 11. Explicit counterexamples to tempting implications

### 11.1 `q=1` does not control all quotients

**[EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE]** Take `n=11`.

- `p=5` has `q=2`, `r=1`, and
  \[
  b_1=5\equiv0\pmod5.
  \]
- The `q=1` primes are `p=7,11`. For `p=7`, `r=4` and
  \[
  b_4=33001\equiv3\pmod7.
  \]
  For `p=11`, `r=0` and `b_0=1`.

Thus the top cell has no bad prime while the `q=2` cell does.

### 11.2 Unit summands do not imply a unit sum

**[EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE]** For `p=5,r=1`, the slope-zero terms are

\[
T_{1,0}=1,
\qquad T_{1,1}=4,
\]

both p-adic units, but

\[
1+4=5\equiv0\pmod5.
\]

Hence “there is a slope-zero term” or “all lowest terms are units” says nothing about their cancellation.

### 11.3 Reflection mod `p` is not reflection mod `p^2`

**[EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE]** For `p=5,a=1,r=3`,

\[
b_1=5\equiv5\pmod{25},
\]

while

\[
b_3=1445\equiv20\pmod{25}.
\]

Here `D_{5,1}=6`, and (10.5) gives

\[
5-2\cdot5\cdot6=-55\equiv20\pmod{25}.
\]

So one may not silently lift `b_r==b_{p-1-r} mod p` to `p^2`.

### 11.4 Small row zero-count does not imply horizontal sparsity

**[MODEL COUNTEREXAMPLE]** Fix an integer `m_0` and define abstract sets

\[
Z_p=\{m_0\bmod p\}.
\]

Then `|Z_p|=1` for every prime, yet

\[
K_X(m_0)=\pi(2X)-\pi(X),
\]

the maximum possible horizontal density. If one insists on reflection symmetry, use

\[
Z_p=\{r_p,p-1-r_p\},
\qquad r_p=m_0\bmod p;
\]

then `|Z_p|<=2` and the same diagonal saturation persists.

Therefore even a hypothetical uniform `Z(p)=O(1)` would not, by itself, prove the all-`n` theorem. The missing information is cross-prime placement of the zeros.

### 11.5 A nonzero bounded-degree Hasse polynomial per prime is not enough

**[MODEL COUNTEREXAMPLE]** Let

\[
H_p(X)=X-(m_0\bmod p).
\]

Every `H_p` is a nonzero degree-one polynomial, but

\[
H_p(m_0\bmod p)=0
\]

for every prime. Generic nonvanishing, bounded degree, or an `O(1)` root count inside each field therefore does not imply prime-aspect diagonal sparsity without arithmetic coupling between the polynomials across `p`.

### 11.6 Bounded conductor does not force nonzero defining-characteristic trace

The actual `p=5,r=1` Apéry/Mellin event already has zero Hasse residue. Uniformly bounded geometric conductor controls complexity of the sheaf; it does not prevent individual trace/Hasse values from vanishing.

### 11.7 Fixed positive density is not enough

If a fixed positive fraction `c` of the prime log mass were bad in every surviving region, then the aggregate would be `c theta(n)+o(n)=Theta(n)`, not `o(n)`. One needs vanishing density/log weight, or a power saving.

### 11.8 The second digit does not remove first-digit radical primes

Again `p=5,a=1`: the second digit is nontrivial, but `5` remains a radical divisor. Thus an estimate only for `v_p>=2` cannot close the first-power horizontal mass.

## 13. What would constitute an actual proof advance

The exact arithmetic target can now be stated without sheaf ambiguity.

### Target A — weakest useful theorem

Prove

\[
\sup_{m<X^2}
\frac{\log X}{X}
\#\left\{X<p\le2X:
\mathcal H_p(\min(m\bmod p,p-1-m\bmod p))=0
\right\}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13.1}
\]

This is DDH0 and closes the theorem by Section 7.

### Target B — fixed power theorem

Prove for some fixed `delta>0`

\[
\#\left\{X<p\le2X:
\mathcal H_p(\min(m\bmod p,p-1-m\bmod p))=0
\right\}
\ll X^{1-\delta}
\tag{13.2}
\]

uniformly for `m<X^2`. This gives `O(n^(1-delta)log n)` high-prime mass.

### Target C — arithmetic two-line version

Equivalently, prove a power saving for primes for which there exists

\[
0\le a\le(p-1)/2
\]

such that

\[
p\mid b_a
\]

and one of

\[
n-a=qp,
\qquad n+1+a=(q+1)p
\]

holds in the quotient-reduced range. This version may be more suitable for a genuine number-field/Chebotarev or resultant attack because it removes the p-dependent Kummer notation entirely.

The key requirement on any proposed theorem is now testable: **does it control one arithmetically selected Hasse zero in each varying defining characteristic, uniformly in the external integer `n`?** If not, its quantifiers do not close P3.2.

## 15. Final answer

The p-adic route is not dead, but its remaining content is now sharply delimited.

1. The exact Morita/Gross--Koblitz calculation reduces every quotient cell to the same first-Hasse zero
   \[
   \mathcal H_p(a_n(p))=0,
   \qquad
   a_n(p)=\min(n-qp,(q+1)p-n-1).
   \]
2. Reflection is exact modulo `p`, and its `p^2` defect is explicit. The latter only controls multiplicity, not radical support.
3. Short slope-zero packets and small reflection gaps occupy only `o(n)` log mass after choosing mesoscopic thresholds; hence any successful theorem may work entirely in the central long-packet/long-gap regime.
4. The reflected pair makes the current gap-polynomial certificate **automatically divisible by `p`** through the factor `2a+h+1=p`; that route supplies no hidden second constraint.
5. The checked Dwork/Hasse-Witt and trace-function large-sieve theorems do not have the required varying-defining-characteristic diagonal quantifiers.
6. The smallest clean new arithmetic input is DDH0, the pointwise vanishing-density bound `K_X(m)=o(X/log X)`. It is strictly weaker than `(HM)_8` and deterministically gives the required aggregate `o(n)` with no quotient-count loss.

So the next proof attack should not be “more conductor” or “more Newton polygon.” It should target **cross-prime arithmetic coupling of the Hasse zeros** in (13.1), preferably through the two linear divisibility forms (5.6)-(5.7). Until such a coupling is proved, the full all-index theorem remains open.
