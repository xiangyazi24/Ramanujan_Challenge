# Apéry two-coordinate Newton determinant audit

## 1. The two transformed coordinates

Let
\[
F^B_{n,K}(c)=\sum_{i=0}^{K}(-1)^{K-i}B_i\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i}c^{K-i},
\]
and define the analogous transform for the companion solution
\[
F^A_{n,K}(c)=\sum_{i=0}^{K}(-1)^{K-i}A_i\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i}c^{K-i}.
\]
Since the Apéry companion has denominators dividing \(d_K=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)^3\), put
\[
\widetilde F^A=d_KF^A.
\]

For a shell prime \(p\in(3n/4,n]\), let \(z=n-p\). The Newton coefficient argument gives
\[
p\mid [c^j]F^B_{n,K}(c)
\]
for all coefficients. Thus
\[
p\mid F^B_{n,K}(c)
\]
for every integral evaluation of \(c\).

The second coordinate does not have the same automatic divisibility.

## 2. Companion divisibility obstruction

The pair/block law gives the congruence of the global pair
\[
(A_n,B_n)\equiv (0,0)\pmod p
\]
only in the projective sense relevant to the shell hit. It does not imply
\[
p\mid A_i
\]
or
\[
p\mid F^A_{n,K}(c).
\]

The coefficient module generated by \(A_i\) has a different local lattice. Clearing denominators multiplies it by \(d_K\), so the correct statement is
\[
 p\mid \widetilde F^A(c)
\]
only if the Smith class of the cleared companion vector contains the hit prime. This is not forced by the block law.

Hence a common determinant built from \(F^B\) and \(F^A\) cannot automatically contain the shell radical.

## 3. Smallest possible determinant

Suppose nevertheless that a prime divides both coordinates at two evaluations. The minimal determinant is
\[
\Delta(c_1,c_2)=
\begin{vmatrix}
F^B(c_1)&F^B(c_2)\\
\widetilde F^A(c_1)&\widetilde F^A(c_2)
\end{vmatrix}.
\]
Equivalently one can use
\[
F^B(c)\widetilde F^{A\prime}(c)-F^{B\prime}(c)\widetilde F^A(c).
\]

If both rows vanish modulo \(p\), then \(p\mid\Delta\). However the premise is already stronger than the known Apéry congruence.

## 4. Denominator and height audit

The clearing factor satisfies
\[
\log d_K^{}=3\psi(K)=3K+o(K).
\]
With \(K=\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), this contributes
\[
\log d_K=3n/4+o(n).
\]

A 2 by 2 determinant therefore inherits at least the companion clearing height unless there is a separate cancellation theorem. The desired threshold is below
\[
2K=n/2,
\]
but the unavoidable lcm contribution alone is larger:
\[
3K>2K.
\]

Therefore the naive cleared Casoratian route cannot reach the required radical height.

## 5. Why zeta(3) decay does not repair this

The estimate
\[
A_i-\zeta(3)B_i\to0
\]
is analytic and irrational. It does not imply an integral divisibility statement for
\[
A_i-\lambda B_i
\]
with \(\lambda=\zeta(3)\).

A valid determinant bound must be a rational identity involving only the recurrence, Casoratian, or exact continuants. Replacing the rational pair by the limiting irrational direction does not produce an integer of small height.

## 6. Scoped saturation theorem

Let \(M_n\) be the local integer module generated by the transformed pair
\[
\{(F^B(c),\widetilde F^A(c)):c\in S\}.
\]
If the Smith normal form of this module has elementary divisors \(s_1|s_2\), then every determinant from evaluations in \(S\) is divisible by \(s_1s_2\). The hit prime appears only when it divides both elementary divisors.

The present route would require:

1. \(p\mid s_1\) and \(p\mid s_2\) for every shell hit;
2. \(\log(s_1s_2)<n/2-o(n)\).

The lcm clearing gives an elementary divisor contribution of size at least \(d_K\) in the companion direction unless a new cancellation theorem is proved. Thus the natural two-coordinate determinant is saturated before amplification.

## 7. Conclusion

The two-coordinate idea does not currently improve the Problem 3.2 radical bound. The obstruction is exact: shell divisibility lies in the integer Apéry coordinate, while the companion coordinate requires a stronger local Smith divisibility statement. After minimal denominator clearing, the lcm height \(3K+o(K)\) already exceeds the determinant budget \(2K\).

The genuinely new invariant needed is the local Smith class of the cleared companion Newton lattice. A proof that its second elementary divisor carries the shell primes with subexponential quotient would revive the route; absent that, the scoped saturation theorem above gives the obstruction to the naive Casoratian amplification.
