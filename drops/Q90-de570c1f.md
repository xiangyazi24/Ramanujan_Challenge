# Q90: Bypassing the full cocycle theorem via Mellin trace methods

## Conclusion

The Mellin-sheaf approach does bypass the full polynomial cocycle expansion theorem, but not by proving equidistribution of the cocycle. The correct object for the Goldbach second moment is a different correlation problem: a moving-prime trace function on the diagonal family

\[
 p=m-r,\qquad r=m-p.
\]

The available Mellin sheaf technology controls variation in the character parameter for fixed characteristic. The Goldbach sum requires simultaneous variation of the characteristic and the character. This is a new mixed-modulus trace-function problem.

## 1. Why the naive additive Fourier expansion fails

The expansion

\[
1_{b_r\equiv0(p)}=\frac1p\sum_t\psi_p(tb_r)
\]

is formally correct, but it is the wrong normalization. The nonzero Fourier modes require bounds for

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}\psi_p(t b_{m-p}).
\]

Deligne/Katz gives square-root cancellation for sums where one finite field and one sheaf are fixed. Here both change with p. The quantity is not a classical trace-function sum over one fixed finite field.

The Sato-Tate heuristic does not help enough because

\[
|T_p(r)|\sim p^{3/2}
\]

and the additive character sees $T_p(r)/p$, which is of size $p^{1/2}$ rather than small. The Taylor expansion of the additive character is therefore invalid as a uniform cancellation mechanism.

## 2. The correct geometric reformulation

The data are

\[
F(p,r)=b_r\pmod p,
\]

restricted to the diagonal

\[
r+p=m.
\]

For fixed p, $r\mapsto b_r\pmod p$ is a Mellin trace function. But the Goldbach problem samples the family

\[
\{\mathcal F_p(r): p=m-r\}.
\]

This is not a single trace function over $\mathbb F_p$; it is an arithmetic family over varying residue fields.

The natural framework is closer to a compatible system of sheaves over $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb Z)$ with a moving parameter. One needs control of Frobenius traces along the arithmetic curve

\[
\{(p,r):p+r=m\}.
\]

## 3. What product monodromy gives

The proven statement

\[
G_{p,q}=SL_2(\mathbb F_p)\times SL_2(\mathbb F_q)
\]

is valuable for the two-prime correlation

\[
1_{p|b_r}1_{q|b_s},
\]

because it eliminates a hidden diagonal obstruction.

However, it does not immediately provide the required estimate because the pair $(r,s)$ is coupled to $(p,q)$ through

\[
r=m-p,\quad s=m-q.
\]

The issue is not independence of two fixed finite-field systems; it is independence along a moving arithmetic family.

## 4. Possible bypass routes

### Route A: vertical trace-function method

For fixed m, construct a global compatible system whose Frobenius trace at p is

\[
\tau_m(p)=b_{m-p}\pmod p.
\]

Then prove a large-sieve estimate

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}|\tau_m(p)|^2\ll X.
\]

This would imply the needed second moment. The missing ingredient is constructing this global object.

### Route B: Katz-style stratification

One can attempt to place the family $(p,r)$ inside an algebraic family of Mellin sheaves and analyze the geometric monodromy of the total space. The needed theorem would be a diagonal specialization result:

full geometric monodromy of the two-variable family implies cancellation after restricting to $p+r=m$.

This is plausible but is not a direct application of Goursat-Kolchin-Ribet.

### Route C: exploit the special endpoint window

Here

\[
p>m/2,\qquad r=m-p<p.
\]

Therefore the Lucas reduction is especially rigid:

\[
p|b_r\iff r\in Z_p.
\]

A direct zero-density theorem for the moving family $Z_{m-r}$ would be enough. This may be substantially weaker than full cocycle equidistribution.

## 5. Final assessment

The full cocycle theorem is probably stronger than necessary. The Goldbach second moment only needs a two-point estimate along the arithmetic diagonal, not equidistribution of the entire Apéry transfer orbit.

The promising replacement theorem is:

**Moving Mellin diagonal theorem.** For fixed m,

\[
\sum_{\substack{p,q\sim m\\p\ne q}}
1_{b_{m-p}\equiv0(p)}1_{b_{m-q}\equiv0(q)}\ll m^{o(1)}.
\]

This is a mixed-characteristic trace-function estimate. Existing Katz equidistribution, ordinary Sato-Tate, and product monodromy do not prove it. The remaining mathematics is not the full cocycle expansion problem, but a weaker arithmetic large-sieve theorem for a compatible Mellin family restricted to a diagonal.

Therefore the bypass is real: the target theorem can be weakened substantially. But it still requires a new input: a moving-prime Mellin trace estimate rather than cocycle mixing.
