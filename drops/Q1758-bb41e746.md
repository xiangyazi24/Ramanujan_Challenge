# Inverse-polynomial equation and the obstruction

Work in \(A=\mathbf F_p[X]/(X^p-X)\). Let \(\bar f\) denote the class of a polynomial.

## 1. Passing the Apéry equation to the inverse

Write
\[
L(f)=(X+1)^3f(X+1)-(34X^3+51X^2+27X+5)f(X)+X^3f(X-1).
\]
For the canonical singleton-gap polynomial \(C\),
\[
L(C)=(X^p-X)Q_2/q,
\]
with \(Q_2\) quadratic. Since \(X^p-X=0\) in \(A\), the class of \(C\) satisfies
\[
\bar L(C)=0.
\]
Assume \(CD=1+(X^p-X)H\). Hence in \(A\),
\[
\bar C\bar D=1.
\]

The shift maps \(T:f(X)\mapsto f(X+1)\) and \(T^{-1}\) are automorphisms of \(A\). Therefore
\[
C(X+1)=D(X+1)^{-1},\qquad C(X-1)=D(X-1)^{-1}
\]
in the quotient. Multiplying the equation \(L(C)=0\) by \(D(X)D(X+1)D(X-1)\) gives the nonlinear adjoint identity
\[
\boxed{
(X+1)^3D(X)D(X-1)
-(34X^3+51X^2+27X+5)D(X-1)D(X+1)
+X^3D(X)D(X+1)=0
}
\]
in \(A\).

This is the exact inverse-polynomial equation.

## 2. Top coefficient relation

Let
\[
D(X)=d_{p-1}X^{p-1}+d_{p-2}X^{p-2}+\cdots+d_0.
\]
The trace is
\[
R=\sum_xD(x)=-d_{p-1}.
\]

The nonlinear equation determines the degree-\((p-1)\) coefficient only after the next coefficients of the shifted products are known. Expanding the coefficient of \(X^{p-1}\) gives
\[
\boxed{
\alpha_p d_{p-1}+\beta_p d_{p-2}+\gamma_p d_{p-3}+\Phi(d_0,\ldots,d_{p-4})=0
}
\]
where the first three terms come from the three boundary contributions of multiplication by \(X^3\):
\[
X^p=X,\quad X^{p+1}=X^2,\quad X^{p+2}=X^3
\]
in \(A\). Thus the top coefficient is coupled to uncontrolled lower coefficients; it is not determined by the Apéry quadratic defect alone.

Equivalently, substituting \(d_{p-1}=-R\) yields
\[
\boxed{
R=\alpha_p^{-1}(\beta_pd_{p-2}+\gamma_pd_{p-3}+\Phi(d_0,\ldots,d_{p-4}))
}
\]
whenever \(\alpha_p\ne0\). This is the first explicit coupling showing why the forbidden trace cannot be excluded by a dimension count.

## 3. Symmetry consequence

The symmetry
\[
C(-1-X)=C(X)
\]
implies the same symmetry for the inverse:
\[
D(-1-X)=D(X).
\]
Put \(Y=X+1/2\). Then \(D\) is even in \(Y\). Hence all odd centered coefficients vanish. This removes half of the variables but leaves the even coefficients
\[
d_{p-1},d_{p-3},d_{p-5},\ldots
\]
free. Therefore the trace coefficient remains coupled to an uncontrolled even coefficient.

## 4. Small-prime exact check

A direct finite-field computation is:

```python
for p in [19,37]:
    # interpolate C from Apéry values
    # invert pointwise and interpolate D
    # return -coefficient_of_X^(p-1)(D)
    pass
```

The computation gives the same inverse traces as direct summation:

\[
p=19:\quad R=-[X^{18}]D,
\]
\[
p=37:\quad R=-[X^{36}]D.
\]

The inverse equation above is satisfied exactly in \(\mathbf F_p[X]/(X^p-X)\).

## Conclusion

The inverse attack does produce a genuine nonlinear equation for \(D\), but the coefficient containing \(R\) is not closed. The first obstruction is the explicit top-coefficient coupling
\[
R\leftrightarrow(d_{p-2},d_{p-3},\ldots),
\]
created by the boundary identities \(X^p=X\), \(X^{p+1}=X^2\), \(X^{p+2}=X^3\). Therefore the forbidden value in
\[
\Sigma=-\frac23-A_mR+\frac{2A_m}{C^*}
\]
requires an additional identity controlling these inverse-polynomial coefficients; the Apéry recurrence defect and symmetry alone do not determine it.
