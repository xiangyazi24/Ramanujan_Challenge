# Hostile audit of the fixed-gap multiplicity route

## Verdict

The observed statement that every scanned fixed triple `(n,q,h)` has at most one fully transverse edge is strong evidence, but it does not currently imply an unconditional theorem

\[
P_{q;h}(n)=O_q(1)
\]

or even

\[
P_{q;h}(n)=O_q(h^A).
\]

The first exact obstruction is that all available identities are either:

1. local divisibility statements for individual characteristics, or
2. pairwise cross-prime constraints,

and neither produces a row-level algebraic relation coupling an arbitrary number of transverse edges.

The missing ingredient would be a genuinely multi-hit identity controlling the intersection
\[
\bigcap_{i=1}^R\{p_i\mid b_{u_i},\ p_i\mid b_n\}.
\]

## 1. Fixed-gap graph does not force bounded multiplicity

For fixed q and h, the transverse edges form a graph on residues u with edges

\[
(u,u+h).
\]

Because vertices are ordered by residue, connected components are paths. This gives matching and rectangle consequences, but a path can have arbitrarily many edges.

A graph-theoretic bound such as degree <=2 does not imply the number of edges in one row is bounded. A path of length m is compatible with all local graph restrictions.

## 2. Why direct/reflected carrier separation is insufficient

The direct and reflected Apéry carriers give relations of the form

\[
p\mid b_u,\qquad p\mid b_{u+h}
\]

under their respective characteristic conditions.

For different primes p_i, these become congruences modulo different prime ideals. They do not combine into one congruence modulo

\[
\prod_i p_i
\]

without an additional compatibility identity.

CRT only says that independent residues can be simultaneously realized. It does not create a contradiction.

## 3. Countermodel showing local identities permit many transverse edges

Consider a formal model with a row n and residues

\[
u_i=a+ih,\qquad i=0,\ldots,m.
\]

Assign distinct primes

\[
p_i=(n-u_i)/q
\]

and impose:

\[
p_i\mid b_{u_i},\qquad p_i\mid b_n.
\]

For every characteristic separately impose the known Lucas-type congruences. Choose the local correction terms so that each congruence is satisfied modulo p_i but no relation is imposed between different i.

Then:

* every edge is fully transverse;
* the same canonical row n contains arbitrarily many edges;
* every pairwise congruence statement remains valid;
* no contradiction appears because all constraints are separated by the moduli p_i.

This is not a claim that Apéry numbers realize the model. It is a saturation model proving that the currently banked identities do not exclude it.

## 4. What would be needed to kill the model

A sufficient missing statement would be a multi-characteristic determinant identity of the form:

For every fixed q there exists C(q) such that for every row n and every transverse set U of size T,

\[
\prod_{i=1}^T p_i
\mid F_q(U,n)
\]

where F_q has height

\[
\log |F_q(U,n)|=o(T\log n).
\]

Since the divisor has size approximately

\[
\exp(T\log n),
\]

this would force F_q(U,n)=0 for large T and yield bounded multiplicity.

No such identity is present in the cited fixed-gap reports.

## 5. Relation to the existing reports

The fixed-gap compression and cross-prime attacks obtain strong restrictions on:

* one characteristic;
* one pair of characteristics;
* bounded-gap configurations.

They do not establish a uniform row theorem because the quantifier order remains:

\[
\forall p\ \exists \text{local relation},
\]

rather than

\[
\exists \text{one global relation controlling all }p_i.
\]

## 6. Minimal exact new target

The smallest upgrade that would imply LGTQ-2 is not necessarily full LGTQ-RECT. It is the fixed-row multiplicity theorem:

\[
\forall q,C\ \exists A,C_q:
\]

\[
\max_{n}\max_{\substack{h\le C\log n\\q\mid h}}P_{q;h}(n)\le C_q(\log n)^A.
\]

The stronger desired form is

\[
\max_{n,h}P_{q;h}(n)=O_q(1).
\]

The current data and local congruences do not prove either statement.

Therefore the correct audit result is a scoped no-go theorem: finite scans and existing fixed-gap identities eliminate many configurations, but they do not contain the global characteristic-zero coupling required for an unconditional multiplicity bound.
