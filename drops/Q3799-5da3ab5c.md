# 3. Graph matching theorem: PASS

For a finite loopless multigraph `G`, the following are equivalent:

1. every edge can be assigned to a distinct incident vertex;
2. every edge subset `F` satisfies `|F|<=|V(F)|`;
3. every connected component has at most one independent cycle, i.e. `G` is a pseudoforest.

This is exactly Hall's theorem applied to the edge--endpoint incidence bipartite graph.  The multigraph qualification causes no problem: two parallel edges on two vertices form a unicyclic component and can be assigned one to each endpoint; three parallel edges already violate Hall.

Thus the graph-matching lemma used by the cycle-excess reduction is correct.

## 5.1 Explicit logical counterexample to exactness

\[

The reason is that `p`-adic valuation is fungible across components.  The tree component has one unit of slack, which pays the bicyclic component's one-unit deficit.

# 13. The smallest useful remaining lemma

The cycle theorem asks for too much.  A sufficient theorem that is strictly weaker and matches the actual carrier capacity is the following.

Let

\[

\frac{T^{1/3}}{\log T\,\Omega(T)}
\right\rfloor,
\qquad \Omega(T)\to\infty
\]

slowly.  Prove

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{J,T}}
 (M_p-R(T)Z(p))_+\log p
=o_J(T^2/\log T).
}
\tag{Dense}
\]

Then `(18)` closes the entire surviving far matched diagonal.

This target has several advantages over pseudoforestness:

- it is purely primewise multiplicity bookkeeping, with no artificial component label;
- it allows average row degree as large as almost `T^(1/3)/log T` per `p`-zero before asking for any new theorem;
- it automatically discards every prime with `Z(p)<=R(T)+1`;
- it preserves plus orientation multiplicity and the deterministic OR sign partition exactly;
- it is strictly implied by the proposed cycle-excess bound because
  \[
  (M_p-RZ(p))_+\le(M_p-Z(p))_+\le\delta_p.
  \]

A slightly stronger but more structurally targeted version is the primary-star tail

\[
\sum_p\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_p}
 (D_p(z)-R(T))_+\log p
=o_J(T^2/\log T),
\tag{StarTail}
\]

because

\[
(M_p-RZ(p))_+
\le

\]

The exact cross-characteristic content of `D_p(z)` is given by `(23)`: it counts upper primes `q` for which

\[
q\mid b_{7p-1-z}
\]

and one of the prescribed affine secondary indices is an actual `p`-zero, with the corresponding `q`-zero `u=7p-1-z-6q`.

I do not find a proof of `(Dense)` or `(StarTail)` in the pushed source.  Conversely, the present one-characteristic identities do not refute them.

# Bottom line

The proposed cycle-excess reduction contains good mathematics, but the phrase **“exact remaining weighted cycle excess” is false**.  Its Hall/pseudoforest step imposes an endpoint-local matching condition that the actual `p`-adic carrier does not need.

The smallest exact repair is to forget components and work primewise.  The one-copy residual is

\[
(M_p-Z(p))_+,
\]

and a much stronger unconditional carrier permits

\[
R(T)=o(T^{1/3}/\log T)
\]

copies of every zero occurrence while still having `o_J(T^2/log T)` height.  Thus the first genuinely hard residual is `(Dense)`, supported only on primes with an unusually large zero set and unusually high q6 row density.

The direct affine attack yields the exact cross-characteristic bistar `(27-)`/`(27+)`, but no current pushed theorem turns those common-divisor relations into the required dense-star tail bound.  That cross-characteristic high-multiplicity estimate—not one more same-characteristic continuant identity—is the first missing arithmetic lemma.
