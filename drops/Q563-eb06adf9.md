# Q563: Difference Galois theory for the Apéry recurrence — G_diff = SL₂?

## Executive summary

The correct conclusion is:

* The Picard–Fuchs differential monodromy being large (SL₂/Sp-type after reduction) strongly suggests that the associated **difference Galois group** is also large, but it does not by itself prove it.
* For the Apéry recurrence, the expected statement `G_diff = SL₂` is very plausible and can be attacked by the standard rank-two classification of difference Galois subgroups.
* However, even a proof of `G_diff = SL₂` is not the missing M1 theorem. Difference Galois theory controls algebraic symmetries of the recurrence, not the distribution of Frobenius traces or finite-field orbits.

The missing bridge is not group-theoretic largeness but an arithmetic equidistribution theorem for the shift dynamics.

---

## (A) Can one prove G_diff = SL₂ explicitly?

Write the recurrence as

\[
Y_{n+1}=A(n)Y_n,
\]

where

\[
Y_n=(b_n,b_{n-1})^T,
\]

and

\[
A(n)=\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)}{(n+1)^3}&-\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

After the standard determinant normalization this defines a rank-two difference module over \(K=\mathbb Q(n)\) with automorphism \(\sigma(f(n))=f(n+1)\).

For a rank-two module with determinant one, proper algebraic subgroups of SL₂ are essentially:

1. reducible (Borel): a one-dimensional invariant subspace;
2. imprimitive (normalizer of a torus): a quadratic decomposition;
3. finite subgroups.

Therefore proving SL₂ reduces to excluding these cases.

### 1. Excluding the Borel case

A reducible difference module would imply a hypergeometric solution

\[
y_{n+1}=r(n)y_n, \qquad r(n)\in\overline{\mathbb Q(n)}.
\]

Equivalently the associated Riccati equation would have an algebraic solution.

For Apéry's recurrence this is incompatible with the known asymptotic structure. The two independent solutions have different exponential behaviors:

\[
b_n\sim C\frac{34^n}{n^{3/2}},
\]

while the second solution has the recessive exponential rate. A hypergeometric algebraic-ratio solution would force the dominant solution to have a rational logarithmic derivative and hence a much more rigid asymptotic expansion. The Apéry recurrence has no such solution.

A fully formal proof would use the Abramov–Petkovsek type criterion: solve the rational Riccati equation and show no rational candidate exists after local analysis at the singularities \(0,-1,\infty\).

### 2. Excluding the torus normalizer

The imprimitive case means that after a quadratic extension the module splits into two rank-one modules. Equivalently the second symmetric power has a one-dimensional invariant factor.

For Apéry this would imply an algebraic relation between the two independent solutions. But the differential Picard–Fuchs operator has irreducible SL₂-type monodromy, and the recurrence is its arithmetic specialization. The same irreducibility obstruction rules out the quadratic decomposition.

A direct difference proof would again use the Riccati equation over a quadratic extension.

### 3. Excluding finite groups

A finite difference Galois group would make every solution algebraic over \(\mathbb Q(n)\). In particular the growth of solutions would be constrained to algebraic-function growth.

The Apéry dominant solution satisfies

\[
b_n\asymp 34^n n^{-3/2},
\]

which is incompatible with finite monodromy/difference symmetry.

Therefore the expected classification gives:

\[
\boxed{G_{\rm diff}=SL_2.}
\]

The caveat is that the above is a proof blueprint, not yet a line-by-line published certificate for Apéry. The missing technical piece is an explicit Riccati obstruction computation.

## (B) What does G_diff = SL₂ imply mod p?

Very little directly.

The partial products

\[
T_{1\to r}=M(r-1)\cdots M(1)
\]

are finite-field evaluations of the difference module. Knowing the generic difference Galois group says that there are no algebraic invariants over characteristic zero.

It does **not** imply that the sequence

\[
T_{1\to1},T_{1\to2},\ldots,T_{1\to p}
\]

is equidistributed in SL₂(\mathbb F_p).

The analogy with differential equations is misleading:

* differential equation + lisse sheaf + Frobenius = Deligne theory;
* difference equation + shift n→n+1 has no automatic Frobenius sheaf.

The shift orbit is deterministic. There is no trace function of a Frobenius conjugacy class to which Deligne's theorem applies.

Therefore:

* `G_diff = SL₂` does not bound \(|Z_p|\);
* it does not prove M1;
* it does not control pair correlations \(D_p\).

The obstruction identified in Q555 remains.

## Is there a difference-Galois analogue of Deligne?

Not in this generality.

There are partial analogues:

* difference Galois theory gives algebraic group structure;
* Hrushovski-type theories give point-counting for certain difference varieties;
* Dreyfus–Hardouin–Roques give algorithms and structural results.

But none says that the deterministic orbit of a single recurrence modulo p behaves like Haar-random SL₂(\mathbb F_p).

The missing ingredient is an arithmetic dynamics theorem.

## (C) Dreyfus–Hardouin–Roques–Singer and Apéry

The Dreyfus–Hardouin–Roques/Singer framework provides algorithms for computing difference Galois groups of linear difference equations.

The Apéry recurrence is exactly the kind of rank-two equation to which these algorithms apply. However, there does not appear to be a standard database entry saying "Apéry recurrence has G_diff = SL₂".

The practical route would be:

1. normalize the recurrence into SL₂ form;
2. run a difference Galois algorithm;
3. certify absence of Riccati solutions;
4. certify irreducibility.

The output would be valuable because it would separate the algebraic symmetry question from the finite-field distribution question.

## (D) What is the weakest statement that actually gives M1?

M1 needs a statement about zeros:

\[
|Z_p|=O(1)\quad\text{on average over }p.
\]

This requires cancellation in the finite-field trace sequence

\[
b_r\pmod p.
\]

A sufficient statement would be something like:

For almost all p, the normalized orbit

\[
T_{1\to r}\in SL_2(\mathbb F_p)
\]

has square-root cancellation against all nontrivial class functions.

Equivalently one wants a bound of the form

\[
\sum_r \psi(b_r) \ll p^{1/2+o(1)}
\]

for suitable additive/multiplicative characters.

This is much closer to Deligne/Katz theory than to ordinary difference Galois theory.

The real target is therefore not:

\[
G_{\rm diff}=SL_2,
\]

but:

\[
\textbf{the Apéry recurrence modulo p defines a family of trace functions with geometric monodromy SL₂ and Deligne-type bounds.}
\]

That requires realizing the recurrence as a genuine lisse sheaf or crystalline object.

# Final conclusion

The difference Galois route is still useful, but only as a structural lemma:

\[
\boxed{G_{\rm diff}=SL_2\quad\text{is plausible and likely provable.}}
\]

It proves that no hidden algebraic symmetry explains the Apéry sequence.

However:

\[
\boxed{G_{\rm diff}=SL_2\not\Rightarrow |Z_p|=O(1).}
\]

The missing theorem is an arithmetic equidistribution statement, not a difference-Galois statement.

For P3.2 the hierarchy is therefore:

1. Difference Galois: prove generic SL₂ symmetry.
2. Geometric realization: upgrade recurrence to a sheaf/crystal.
3. Deligne/Katz-type cancellation: obtain M1.
4. Tensor-product independence: obtain AKD₃.

The bottleneck remains step 2→3, not step 1.
