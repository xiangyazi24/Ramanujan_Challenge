# 1. Exact gate and accounting ledger

Let

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
\]

be the Apéry numbers, and let

\[
G_m=\gcd(d_ma_m,d_mb_m),
\qquad d_m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,m)^3.
\]

For \(P\ge3\), define the literal support block

\[
\mathcal P(m;P)
 =\{p\ {m prime}:P<p\le\min(2P,m),\ p>\sqrt m,\ p\mid b_m\},
\]

and

\[
R(m;P)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P(m;P)}p.
\]

All products below are squarefree products of **numerical prime divisors of an actual Apéry integer**.  Empty products equal \(1\).

The requested statement is

\[
\exists\delta>0\ \exists C,m_0\ \forall m\ge m_0\ \forall P:
\qquad
\log R(m;P)\le C\frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}},
\tag{UDBRD}
\]

whenever the block intersects \((\sqrt m,m]\).

## 1.1 Why UDBRD closes P3.2

Take powers of two \(P\) for which \((P,2P]\) meets \((\sqrt m,m]\).  They are disjoint and

\[
\sum_P P\ll m,
\qquad
\log P\ge\tfrac12\log m-O(1).
\]

Therefore UDBRD gives

\[
\sum_P\log R(m;P)
 \ll \frac{m}{(\log m)^{1+\delta}}=o(m).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The canonical source supplies the remaining exact ledger:

- primes \(p\le\sqrt m\) contribute \(O(\sqrt m)\);
- the companion/leading-coordinate block contributes \(O(m^{2/3})\) pointwise;
- for \(p>\sqrt m\), \(v_p(G_m)\le3\);
- every other contributing prime satisfies the lower-digit condition
  
  \[
  p\mid b_{m\bmod p}.
  \]

Hence, with the multiplicity retained,

\[
\log G_m
 \le C m^{2/3}
   +3\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt m\\p\mid b_{m\bmod p}}}\log p.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Gessel--Lucas implies every prime in the last sum divides \(b_m\), so (1.1) proves \(\log G_m=o(m)\).  No normalization by an unknown mass occurs.

The factor \(3\) in (1.2) is load-bearing: because \(p>\sqrt m\), the source bound

\[
v_p(G_m)\le3\lfloor\log_p m\rfloor
\]

gives exactly \(v_p(G_m)\le3\), not multiplicity one.

## 1.2 A weaker block theorem would already suffice

For \(p>\sqrt m\), write

\[
m=q_pp+r_p,
\qquad q_p=\lfloor m/p\rfloor,
\qquad0\le r_p<p.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Define the lower-digit block radical

\[
R_{\rm low}(m;P)
 =\prod_{\substack{P<p\le\min(2P,m)\\p>\sqrt m\\p\mid b_{r_p}}}p.
\tag{1.4}
\]

A uniform UDBRD estimate only for \(R_{\rm low}\) is enough for P3.2, because the source already disposes of the other actual-gcd channels.  Thus it is legitimate to separate the stronger support-blind \(R(m;P)\) from the exact consumer \(R_{\rm low}(m;P)\).

# 2. Exact Lucas splitting and a complete pointwise leading-support theorem

Since \(p>\sqrt m\), one has \(q_p<p\).  Gessel--Lucas therefore gives the exact congruence

\[
b_m\equiv b_{q_p}b_{r_p}\pmod p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Put

\[
\mathcal P_{\rm lead}(m;P)
 =\{p\in\mathcal P(m;P):p\mid b_{q_p}\},
\]

and

\[
\mathcal P_{\rm low}(m;P)
 =\{p\in\mathcal P(m;P):p\mid b_{r_p}\}.
\]

Then, with overlap allowed,

\[
\mathcal P(m;P)
 =\mathcal P_{\rm lead}(m;P)
  \cup\mathcal P_{\rm low}(m;P).
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is an equality of actual numerical prime sets, not a carrier inclusion.

## Proposition 2.1 — pointwise dyadic leading-support bound

For all \(m\ge4\) and every relevant \(P\),

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm lead}(m;P)}\log p
 \ll
 \min\!\left\{
 P,
 \left(\frac mP\right)^2
 +\frac mP\log\!\left(2+\frac mP\right)
 \right\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(q\).  Every distinct prime counted with \(q_p=q\) divides the one positive integer \(b_q\).  Hence

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm lead}(m;P)\\q_p=q}}\log p
 \le\log b_q.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The binomial formula gives the elementary pointwise height

\[
0<b_q\le(q+1)64^q,
\tag{2.5}
\]

because each of the \(q+1\) summands is at most \(64^q\).  Since \(p>P\), all possible quotients satisfy \(q<m/P\).  Summing (2.4)--(2.5),

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm lead}(m;P)}\log p
 \le
 \sum_{1\le q<m/P}\bigl(q\log64+\log(q+1)\bigr),
\]

which is the second quantity in (2.3).  The first is the ordinary Chebyshev bound for all primes in \((P,2P]\).  ∎

## Corollary 2.2 — the entire leading support is \(O(m^{2/3})\)

Uniformly for every \(m\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt m<p\le m\\p\mid b_{\lfloor m/p\rfloor}}}
 \log p\ll m^{2/3}.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

### Proof

Primes \(p\le m^{2/3}\) have total logarithmic weight \(O(m^{2/3})\).  If \(p>m^{2/3}\), then \(q_p<m^{1/3}\).  Grouping by \(q_p\) and applying (2.4)--(2.5),

\[
\sum_{\substack{p>m^{2/3}\\p\mid b_{q_p}}}\log p
 \le\sum_{q<m^{1/3}}\log b_q
 \ll\sum_{q<m^{1/3}}q
 \ll m^{2/3}.
\]

∎

This already proves a uniform \(o(m)\) theorem for one entire half of the support-blind Lucas decomposition.  In particular, the support-blind strengthening introduced by UDBRD does not create a new obstruction in the leading branch.

For a single block, (2.3) is at the requested UDBRD scale whenever

\[
P^3\gg m^2(\log P)^{1+\delta}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

A stronger exact vacancy statement is also immediate: if

\[
(q+1)64^q<P,
\tag{2.8}
\]

then no prime \(p>P\) with quotient \(q\) can divide \(b_q\).  Thus the leading branch is empty for \(P\ge C m/\log m\) with any fixed \(C>\log64\), once \(m\) is large.

# 3. Primitive-divisor methods point in the wrong direction

The Lucas splitting gives a clean theorem that rules out a tempting route.

## Proposition 3.1 — every target prime is Lucas-imprimitive

Let \(p>\sqrt m\), \(p\le m\), and \(p\mid b_m\).  Then \(p\) divides an earlier Apéry number.  More precisely, either

\[
p\mid b_{q_p},
\qquad q_p<m,
\]

or

\[
p\mid b_{r_p},
\qquad r_p<m.
\]

In the second case reflection gives

\[
p\mid b_{s_p},
\qquad
s_p:=\min(r_p,p-1-r_p)\le\frac{p-1}{2}<m.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

Equation (2.1) gives the first dichotomy.  The source's reflection theorem gives \(b_{p-1-r_p}\equiv b_{r_p}\pmod p\), proving (3.1).  ∎

Consequently, a primitive-prime-divisor theorem for \(b_m\) would not restrict the primes in \(R(m;P)\): by construction, none of them is primitive at index \(m\).  This is an exact direction-of-implication failure, not merely absence of a known theorem.

# 4. A pointwise low-character-order theorem

The toric/Mellin representation associates to the lower residue \(r_p\) the multiplicative character \(x\mapsto x^{r_p}\) on \(\mathbb F_p^	imes\).  Its order is

\[
d_p:=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(r_p,p-1)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a lower-digit prime, \(r_p\ne0,p-1\), because \(b_0=1\) and reflection gives \(b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p\).

The key point is that the order has a completely elementary divisor parametrization.

Since \(r_p=m-q_pp\) and \(p\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\),

\[
\gcd(r_p,p-1)=\gcd(m-q_p,p-1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Put

\[
g_p:=\gcd(m-q_p,p-1)=\frac{p-1}{d_p}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
g_p\mid m-q_p,
\qquad
p=d_pg_p+1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus, for fixed \(q\), the pair \((d_p,g_p)\) determines \(p\) injectively.

Let \(	au(n)\) be the divisor function and define the exact short divisor sum

\[
Q(m;P):=\left\lceil\frac mP\right\rceil,
\qquad
\mathcal T(m;P):=
 \sum_{1\le q\le Q(m;P)}\tau(m-q).
\tag{4.5}
\]

For relevant blocks and large \(m\), \(Q(m;P)<m\), so every term is defined.

## Proposition 4.1 — low-order numerical-prime bound

For every \(D\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm low}(m;P)\\d_p\le D}}
 \log p
 \le D\,\mathcal T(m;P)\log(2P).}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(q\).  By (4.4), a prime with \(q_p=q\) and \(d_p\le D\) is determined by a choice

\[
1\le d\le D,
\qquad g\mid m-q,
\qquad p=dg+1.
\]

We deliberately ignore primality, the quotient-cell inequalities, and the Apéry-zero condition; this only enlarges the set.  Hence there are at most \(D\tau(m-q)\) candidates for fixed \(q\).  Summing over \(q\) and weighting each numerical prime by at most \(\log(2P)\) proves (4.6).  ∎

This estimate is pointwise in \(m\), contains no Palm measure, and does not infer a prime from a formal Mellin root.  The Mellin structure is used only to define a partition of the already selected numerical prime divisors.

## Corollary 4.2 — the UDBRD scale for low orders

Fix \(\delta>0\), put \(L_P=\log(2P)\), and define

\[
D_\delta(m;P)
 :=\left\lfloor
 \frac{P}{16\,\mathcal T(m;P)L_P^{2+\delta}}
 \right\rfloor.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm low}(m;P)\\
                 d_p\le D_\delta(m;P)}}\log p
 \ll_\delta\frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The statement remains valid when the floor in (4.7) is zero, in which case the selected set is empty.

## Size of the cutoff

Let

\[
\Delta(x)=\max_{1\le n\le x}\tau(n).
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal T(m;P)
 \le Q(m;P)\Delta(m).
\tag{4.9}
\]

The classical maximal-order divisor bound gives

\[
\Delta(m)=
 \exp\!\left(O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{\log\log m}\right)\right)
 =m^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Therefore, in the quotient-reduced range

\[
P\ge\frac{m}{F(m)\log m},
\qquad F(m)=m^{o(1)},
\tag{4.11}
\]

one has

\[
\mathcal T(m;P)=m^{o(1)},
\qquad
D_\delta(m;P)=m^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

So the unconditional theorem removes **all but near-primitive character orders** in precisely the top blocks where the all-index conjecture lives.

An entirely elementary alternative to (4.10), useful away from very short quotient ranges, is

\[
\sum_{M<n\le M+Q}\tau(n)
 \ll Q\log M+\sqrt M,
\tag{4.13}
\]

obtained by counting divisors up to \(\sqrt M\).  Taking the better of (4.9) and (4.13) gives a uniform explicit numerical cutoff at every scale.

# 5. Endpoint and reflection-centre pruning

For a lower-digit prime set

\[
s_p:=\min(r_p,p-1-r_p),
\qquad
h_p:=|p-1-2r_p|=p-1-2s_p.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Reflection gives \(p\mid b_{s_p}\).

## Proposition 5.1 — exact endpoint bound

For every \(E\ge0\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm low}(m;P)\\s_p\le E}}
 \log p
 \ll (E+1)\log(2m).
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(s\).  If \(r_p=s\), then

\[
m=q_pp+s,
\qquad p\mid m-s.
\]

If \(r_p=p-1-s\), then

\[
m=(q_p+1)p-1-s,
\qquad p\mid m+s+1.
\]

Therefore the squarefree product of all primes belonging to the first branch divides \(m-s\), and the product in the second branch divides \(m+s+1\).  Their total logarithmic weight is \(O(\log(2m))\) for each \(s\).  Sum over \(s\le E\).  ∎

This also gives exact vacancy near the endpoint.  Since \(b_s\le(s+1)64^s\), no prime \(p>P\) can occur if

\[
(s+1)64^s<P.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus a fixed positive multiple of \(\log P\) is empty, but only a logarithmic strip; the deep regime is unaffected.

## Proposition 5.2 — exact reflection-centre bound

For every \(H\ge0\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm low}(m;P)\\h_p\le H}}
 \log p
 \ll (H+1)\log(3m).
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

For fixed \(h\), the two possible signs in (5.1) give

\[
(2q_p+1)p=2m+1+h
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
(2q_p+1)p=2m+1-h.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Hence every such prime divides one of the two fixed integers \(2m+1\pm h\).  The squarefree product for fixed \(h\) therefore has logarithm \(O(\log(3m))\).  Sum over \(h\le H\).  ∎

Because a relevant block has \(2P>\sqrt m\), one has

\[
\log m\le2\log(2P)+O(1).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Consequently, with

\[
E_\delta(P)=H_\delta(P)
 :=\left\lfloor\frac{P}{16(\log(2P))^{2+\delta}}\right\rfloor,
\tag{5.7}
\]

the union of (5.2) and (5.4) is bounded by

\[
\ll_\delta\frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

# 6. The sharp unconditional resolved-versus-terminal theorem

Define the literal terminal lower-prime set

\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal H_\delta(m;P):=\{p:\;&P<p\le\min(2P,m),\ p>\sqrt m,\\
 &p\mid b_{r_p},\quad
 d_p>D_\delta(m;P),\\
 &s_p>E_\delta(P),\quad
 h_p>H_\delta(P)\},
\end{split}
\tag{6.1}
\]

and its numerical radical

\[
R_{\rm hard,\delta}(m;P)
 :=\prod_{p\in\mathcal H_\delta(m;P)}p.
\tag{6.2}
\]

## Theorem 6.1 — pointwise UDBRD decomposition

For every fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly in \(m\) and every relevant dyadic block,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\log R(m;P)
\le{}&\log R_{\rm hard,\delta}(m;P)\\
&+C_\delta\frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}}\\
&+C\left[
 \left(\frac mP\right)^2
 +\frac mP\log\!\left(2+\frac mP\right)
 \right].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof

Use the exact union (2.2).  Proposition 2.1 bounds the leading branch.  Every lower prime not in \(\mathcal H_\delta\) lies in at least one of the low-order, endpoint, or centre sectors.  Corollary 4.2 and (5.8) bound their union at the displayed logarithmic-deficit scale.  No multiplicity is lost because all products are radicals.  ∎

The last line of (6.3) is itself at the UDBRD scale in the range (2.7).  More importantly, it is globally harmless even when it is not blockwise small, by Corollary 2.2.

## Corollary 6.2 — global pointwise reduction to the terminal radical

Let \(P\) run over powers of two whose blocks meet \((\sqrt m,m]\).  Then

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(\sqrt m,m]}(b_m)
 \le
 C_\delta m^{2/3}
 +C_\delta\frac{m}{(\log m)^{1+\delta}}
 +\sum_P\log R_{\rm hard,\delta}(m;P).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof

Use Corollary 2.2 globally for all leading primes.  Sum the resolved lower-prime bounds over disjoint dyadic blocks.  Since \(\sum_PP\ll m\) and \(\log P\gg\log m\), their total is \(O(m/(\log m)^{1+\delta})\).  ∎

For the actual gcd, the source's small-prime, companion-block, and multiplicity results give the sharper consumer

\[
\boxed{
\log G_m
 \le C_\delta m^{2/3}
 +C_\delta\frac{m}{(\log m)^{1+\delta}}
 +3\sum_P\log R_{\rm hard,\delta}(m;P).}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus **P3.2 would be fully unconditional if and only if this audit were supplemented by a uniform \(o(m)\) estimate for the terminal sum in (6.5)**.  No such estimate is proved here or in the canonical source.

The phrase “if and only if” in the previous sentence is only relative to decomposition (6.5): it does not assert that terminal radical smallness is the unique possible proof of P3.2.

## The true remaining geometry

After the source's quotient reduction, take

\[
P\ge\frac{m}{F(m)\log m},
\qquad F(m)=m^{o(1)}.
\]

Then every unresolved prime has simultaneously

\[
q_p\le F(m)\log m,
\tag{6.6}
\]

\[
d_p=m^{1-o(1)},
\tag{6.7}
\]

and, from (5.7),

\[
s_p,h_p\gg
 \frac{m}{F(m)(\log m)^{3+\delta}}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

So the remaining individual-integer frontier is not “all divisors of \(b_m\)”.  It is the near-primitive, deep, off-centre defining-characteristic diagonal.

\label{ssec:udbrd-pruning}

For $m\ge4$ and $P\ge3$, put
\[
 \mathcal P(m;P)=
 \{p\text{ prime}:P<p\le\min(2P,m),\ p>\sqrt m,\ p\mid b_m\}.

\[

Finally, let

 Q(m;P)=\left\lceil\frac mP\right\rceil,
 \qquad

\begin{proposition}[Lucas-leading radical]
\label{prop:udbrd-leading}
Uniformly for every $m$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\sqrt m<p\le m\\p\mid b_{\lfloor m/p\rfloor}}}
 \log p\ll m^{2/3}.
\]
More locally,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P(m;P)\\p\mid b_{q_p}}}\log p
 \ll

\]
\end{proposition}

For fixed $q$, the squarefree product of the primes with $q_p=q$
and $p\mid b_q$ divides $b_q$.  The binomial formula gives
$b_q\le(q+1)64^q$.  Sum over $q<m/P$.  Globally, primes below
$m^{2/3}$ have Chebyshev weight $O(m^{2/3})$, while primes above
$m^{2/3}$ have $q_p<m^{1/3}$, and
$\sum_{q<m^{1/3}}\log b_q=O(m^{2/3})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Low Mellin-order pruning]
\label{prop:udbrd-low-order}
For every $D\ge1$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{P<p\le\min(2P,m)\\p>\sqrt m\\
                  p\mid b_{r_p},\ d_p\le D}}
 \log p
 \le D\,\mathcal T(m;P)\log(2P).
\]
\end{proposition}

\[

$p=d_pg_p+1$.  For fixed $q$, the pair $(d_p,g_p)$ determines $p$;
there are at most $D\tau(m-q)$ possibilities.  Sum over $q$.
\end{proof}

\label{prop:udbrd-edge-centre}
For all $E,H\ge0$,
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{\substack{P<p\le\min(2P,m)\\p>\sqrt m\\
                  p\mid b_{r_p},\ s_p\le E}}\log p
 &\ll(E+1)\log(2m),\\

                  p\mid b_{r_p},\ h_p\le H}}\log p
 &\ll(H+1)\log(3m).
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Reflection gives $p\mid b_{s_p}$.  For fixed $s$, the two possible
orientations imply respectively $p\mid m-s$ or $p\mid m+s+1$.
For fixed $h$, the two signs imply
$(2q_p+1)p=2m+1\pm h$.  The squarefree products therefore divide
fixed integers of size $O(m)$; sum their logarithms.
\end{proof}

Fix $\delta>0$, write $L_P=\log(2P)$, and set
\[
 D_\delta(m;P)=
 \left\lfloor\frac{P}{16\mathcal T(m;P)L_P^{2+\delta}}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 E_\delta(P)=H_\delta(P)=
 \left\lfloor\frac{P}{16L_P^{2+\delta}}\right\rfloor.
\]
Let $R_{\rm hard,\delta}(m;P)$ be the product of the primes satisfying
\[
 p\mid b_{r_p},\quad
 d_p>D_\delta(m;P),\quad
 s_p>E_\delta(P),\quad h_p>H_\delta(P).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Resolved versus terminal individual radical]
\label{thm:udbrd-terminal}
Uniformly in $m$ and every relevant dyadic block,
\[
\begin{aligned}

 \le{}&\log R_{\rm hard,\delta}(m;P)

 &+O\left(\left(\frac mP\right)^2
 +\frac mP\log\left(2+\frac mP\right)\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[

 \le O_\delta(m^{2/3})
 +O_\delta\left(\frac{m}{(\log m)^{1+\delta}}\right)
 +3\sum_P\log R_{\rm hard,\delta}(m;P),
\]
where $P$ runs over the dyadic blocks meeting $(\sqrt m,m]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Gessel--Lucas gives $b_m\equiv b_{q_p}b_{r_p}\pmod p$.
Apply Propositions~\ref{prop:udbrd-leading},
\ref{prop:udbrd-low-order}, and
\ref{prop:udbrd-edge-centre}.  In a relevant block,
$\log m\ll\log P$, and $\sum_PP\ll m$.
For the gcd, use the manuscript's small-prime and companion-block bounds,
the lower-digit reduction, and $v_p(G_m)\le3$ for $p>\sqrt m$.
\end{proof}

# 8. The exact irreducible subcase

The low-order theorem is substantial, but it cannot close the problem by order classification alone.

## Proposition 8.1 — full-order obstruction when \(m-1\) is prime

Assume \(m-1\) is prime.  For every prime

\[
\frac m2<p<m,
\]

one has \(q_p=1\), \(r_p=m-p\), and

\[
\boxed{d_p=p-1.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Proof

Modulo \(p-1\),

\[
r_p=m-p\equiv m-1\pmod{p-1}.
\]

Since \(m-1\) is prime and \(0<p-1<m-1\),

\[
\gcd(r_p,p-1)=\gcd(m-1,p-1)=1.
\]

Thus (8.1) follows from (4.1).  ∎

In the same range, Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
b_m=b_{p+(m-p)}\equiv b_1b_{m-p}=5b_{m-p}\pmod p.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Therefore, for \(p>5\),

\[
p\mid b_m\iff p\mid b_{m-p},
\tag{8.3}
\]

and hence the exact identity (B):

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(m/2,m)}(b_m)
 =\sum_{\substack{0<r<m/2\\m-r\ {m prime}\\m-r\mid b_r}}
 \log(m-r).}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This subcase survives every low-order cutoff, no matter how close to \(p\) the cutoff is.  It is also already enough to contain the original top-half radical problem.  Consequently:

> A proof that only discards character-order sectors can never finish P3.2.  It must use the defining-prime Apéry zero condition inside the full-order sector.

The endpoint and centre theorems delete explicit subranges of (8.4), but leave a macroscopic interior.

# 9. Why the remaining advertised mechanisms do not cross the logarithmic threshold

## 9.1 Archimedean size and product formula

The actual asymptotic is

\[
\log b_m=m\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log m),
\qquad
\log(17+12\sqrt2)=3.52549\ldots.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Meanwhile

\[
\log\prod_{\sqrt m<p\le m}p
 =\vartheta(m)-\vartheta(\sqrt m)=m+o(m).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Thus \(b_m\) has enough Archimedean capacity to contain every prime in the entire target interval once, with a linear margin.  On one block, the rigorous height ledger is only

\[
\log R(m;P)
 \le\min\{\log b_m,\vartheta(2P)-\vartheta(P)\}
 \le(1+o(1))P,
\tag{9.3}
\]

with the obvious truncation at \(m\).  This is exactly the trivial prime-capacity scale and contains no fixed logarithmic deficit.

A product-formula reformulation does not improve (9.3): it merely restates that the finite-place contribution of the selected primes is paid for by the known Archimedean height of \(b_m\).  The requested saving would require a new Apéry-specific statement about **which** finite places occur, not another upper bound for the same height.

This explicitly avoids the forbidden inference “\(b_m\) is exponentially large, therefore its large-prime radical is subexponential.”  The conclusion does not follow.

## 9.2 \(p\)-adic valuations and the Wronskian

The Wronskian theorem is indispensable for multiplicities in \(G_m\), but UDBRD is squarefree.  A bound

\[
v_p(G_m)\le3
\]

cannot restrict the number of distinct primes with \(v_p(b_m)>0\).  Even a hypothetical exact formula \(v_p(b_m)=1\) for every target prime would leave \(\log R(m;P)\) unchanged.

The same-prime supercongruences compare indices along a \(p\)-power tower.  They do not correlate the support as the defining prime itself varies through \((P,2P]\).  The exact first failed implication is

\[
\text{uniform control of }v_p(b_{ap^k})
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\#\{P<p\le2P:p\mid b_m\}=o(P/\log P).
\]

## 9.3 Binomial and diagonal representations

The binomial formula supplied (2.5) and the endpoint vacancy (5.3), but in the deep range its only uniform consequence is the exponential height (9.1).

The toric formula gives an exact defining-characteristic Mellin coefficient

\[
b_r=-\sum_{a\ne0}\mu_p(a)a^r\pmod p.
\tag{9.4}
\]

The K3 theorem improves every individual nonzero-fiber error to \(O(p)\), but (9.4) has \(p-1\) summands.  An \(O(p)\) pointwise trace bound does not prevent exact cancellation modulo \(p\).  The source records the literal cancellation at \((p,r)=(11,5)\).

The weighted-square identity is also not a positive norm:

\[
b_r=-\sum_{t\ne0}t^r(1-t)^{p-1-2r}A_r(t)^2.
\tag{9.5}
\]

The source expands (9.5) coefficientwise back into the original Apéry binomial sum.  It is an exact reformulation, not a second congruence.

Likewise, the reciprocal-square/Hasse factorization does not constrain early coefficient-zero patterns: the source proves the coefficient map is triangular and surjective in the free half-range.  Therefore no per-prime deficit follows from squareness or reciprocity alone.

## 9.4 Congruence classes of prime divisors

A restriction to a fixed positive-density collection of residue classes would give at best

\[
\log R(m;P)\le cP+o(P)
\]

for a fixed \(c>0\), still much larger than \(P/(\log P)^{1+\delta}\).  One needs a family of allowed classes whose density tends to zero uniformly with \(m\).  The current source proves no such law.

The order parametrization (4.4) is the strongest available moving-modulus restriction.  It supplies the proved low-order theorem, but \(g_p=1\) imposes no congruence restriction at all; Proposition 8.1 shows that this full-order case is unavoidable.

## 9.5 Restricted-denominator irrationality

UDBRD counts every large prime divisor of \(b_m\), including primes that need not divide the numerator side of the Apéry approximant.  Such primes are invisible to gcd reduction, so an irrationality lower bound cannot prove the support-blind UDBRD statement.

Even for the actual gcd, the standard rational-approximation ledger is too weak.  One has

\[
b_m\asymp\alpha^m,
\qquad
|b_m\zeta(3)-a_m|\asymp\alpha^{-m},
\qquad
\log d_m=3m+o(m),
\]

with \(\alpha=17+12\sqrt2\).  If \(g_m=G_m\), the reduced denominator has size

\[
B_m\asymp \frac{d_mb_m}{g_m}.
\]

Even gifting the optimal irrationality exponent \(2\), comparison with

\[
\left|\zeta(3)-\frac{a_m}{b_m}\right|\asymp\alpha^{-2m}
\]

gives only

\[
\log g_m\le3m+o(m),
\tag{9.6}
\]

not \(o(m)\).  The linear term is the denominator-clearing cost.  A “restricted-denominator” theorem sharp enough to remove it would have to distinguish exactly the large-prime support now under study; that would be genuinely new arithmetic, not an existing irrationality consequence.

## 9.6 Formal carriers, resultants, and primitive roots

If an actual zero pair produces a gap-polynomial or resultant divisor, the converse need not hold.  The canonical source contains primitive phantom carrier roots for primes with no corresponding Apéry zero.  Therefore replacing

\[
p\mid b_{r_p}
\]

by

\[
p\mid N_h(r_p),
\quad p\mid\operatorname{Res}(\cdots),
\quad\text{or}\quad p\mid\operatorname{cont}(\cdots)
\]

would enlarge the support and would not prove UDBRD for actual divisors.  Any product-formula height attached to those formal carriers also pays for false positives.

# 11. Standing equivalence, packaging, and quantifier checks

## 11.1 Equivalence check

The low-order theorem is not UDBRD in disguise.  It is true after deleting all large-order primes and remains true in a world where every full-order candidate divides \(b_m\).  Proposition 8.1 shows that a complete prime interval can lie in the deleted full-order sector.  Hence the premise is strictly weaker than the target and does not collapse to it.

The endpoint and centre bounds are likewise independent exact exclusions based on divisibility by \(O(m)\)-sized integers.

By contrast, the statement

\[
\sum_P\log R_{\rm hard,\delta}(m;P)=o(m)
\]

is only the residual target after the proved deletions.  I do not present it as a new mechanism.

## 11.2 Packaging check

Every prime in Propositions 2.1, 4.1, 5.1, 5.2, and Theorem 6.1 is a numerical prime satisfying a literal divisibility condition involving \(b_m\), \(b_{q_p}\), or \(b_{r_p}\).  No resultant root, gap-polynomial root, K3 fiber, or Hasse evaluation root is counted as an Apéry divisor.

The character order \(d_p\) is used only as an attribute of an already selected lower-digit prime.  The proof of Proposition 4.1 actually counts a larger explicit numerical candidate set, so no lost marking is hidden.

## 11.3 Quantifier check

All proved estimates are uniform and pointwise in \(m\).  There is no average over \(m\), no density-one exception, no Palm normalization, and no division by a possibly zero selected mass.

The known polylogarithmic exceptional-set theorem cannot be inserted here: a single exceptional \(m\) can carry the entire pointwise obstruction.

## 11.4 Multiplicity check

The block radicals are squarefree.  Multiplicity is reintroduced only at the actual-gcd consumer, where the source gives \(v_p(G_m)\le3\) for \(p>\sqrt m\).  This is why (6.5) has the explicit factor \(3\).

## 11.5 Finite-evidence check

No scan, maximum observed factor count, fitted smoothness law, or random-divisor heuristic is used in any proof.  Such evidence may favor UDBRD, but it does not enter the verdict.
