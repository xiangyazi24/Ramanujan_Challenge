# 1. Exact gate and accounting ledger

\[

and

\[
G_m=\gcd(d_ma_m,d_mb_m),
\qquad d_m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,m)^3.

For \(P\ge3\), define the literal support block

\[
\mathcal P(m;P)
 =\{p\ \text{prime}:P<p\le\min(2P,m),\ p>\sqrt m,\ p\mid b_m\},
\]

and

\[
R(m;P)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P(m;P)}p.
\]

All products below are squarefree products of numerical prime divisors of actual Apéry integers.  Empty products equal \(1\).

The requested theorem is

\[
\exists\delta>0\ \exists C,m_0\ \forall m\ge m_0\ \forall P:
\quad
\log R(m;P)\le C\frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}}
\tag{UDBRD}
\]

for every dyadic block meeting \((\sqrt m,m]\).

## 1.1 Exact dyadic consumer

Let \(P\) run over powers of two for which \((P,2P]\) meets \((\sqrt m,m]\).  Then

\[
\sum_P P\ll m,
\qquad
\log P\ge\tfrac12\log m-O(1).
\]

Thus UDBRD would give

\[
\sum_P\log R(m;P)
 \ll\frac{m}{(\log m)^{1+\delta}}=o(m).
\tag{1.1}

The canonical manuscript supplies the exact remaining ledger:

Consequently

\[
\log G_m
 \le C m^{2/3}
 +3\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt m\\p\mid b_{m\bmod p}}}\log p.
\tag{1.2}

The factor \(3\) is exact at this level: \(p>\sqrt m\) gives

\[
v_p(G_m)\le3\lfloor\log_p m\rfloor=3.

# 2. Exact Lucas splitting and the complete leading-support theorem

Since \(p>\sqrt m\), one has \(q_p<p\).  Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
b_m\equiv b_{q_p}b_{r_p}\pmod p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Put

\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{lead}}(m;P)
 =\{p\in\mathcal P(m;P):p\mid b_{q_p}\},
\]

and

\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{low}}(m;P)
 =\{p\in\mathcal P(m;P):p\mid b_{r_p}\}.
\]

Then, with overlap allowed,

\[
\mathcal P(m;P)
 =\mathcal P_{\mathrm{lead}}(m;P)
  \cup\mathcal P_{\mathrm{low}}(m;P).
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is an equality of actual prime sets.

## Proposition 2.1 — dyadic leading support

For every \(m\ge4\) and every relevant \(P\),

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{lead}}(m;P)}\log p
 \ll
 \min\!\left\{
 P,
 \left(\frac mP\right)^2
 +\frac mP\log\!\left(2+\frac mP\right)
 \right\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(q\).  The squarefree product of all primes with \(q_p=q\) and \(p\mid b_q\) divides the positive integer \(b_q\).  Therefore

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{lead}}(m;P)\\q_p=q}}
 \log p\le\log b_q.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The binomial formula gives

\[
0<b_q\le(q+1)64^q,
\tag{2.5}
\]

because each of its \(q+1\) summands is at most \(64^q\).  Since \(p>P\), all quotients satisfy \(q<m/P\).  Summing (2.4)--(2.5) proves the second bound in (2.3).  The first is Chebyshev's bound for all primes in \((P,2P]\).  ∎

## Corollary 2.2 — all leading support is \(O(m^{2/3})\)

Uniformly for every \(m\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt m<p\le m\\p\mid b_{\lfloor m/p\rfloor}}}
 \log p\ll m^{2/3}.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

### Proof

Primes \(p\le m^{2/3}\) have total logarithmic weight \(O(m^{2/3})\).  If \(p>m^{2/3}\), then \(q_p<m^{1/3}\), so

\[
\sum_{\substack{p>m^{2/3}\\p\mid b_{q_p}}}\log p
 \le\sum_{q<m^{1/3}}\log b_q
 \ll\sum_{q<m^{1/3}}q
 \ll m^{2/3}.
\]

∎

For one block, (2.3) is at the UDBRD scale whenever

\[
P^3\gg m^2(\log P)^{1+\delta}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

There is also exact vacancy: if

\[
(q+1)64^q<P,
\tag{2.8}
\]

then no prime \(p>P\) with quotient \(q\) divides \(b_q\).  Hence the leading branch is empty for \(P\ge C m/\log m\), for any fixed \(C>\log64\) and all sufficiently large \(m\).

# 4. Pointwise pruning by multiplicative-character order

For a lower-digit prime, define

\[
d_p:=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(r_p,p-1)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the order of the defining character \(x\mapsto x^{r_p}\) on \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).  A lower-digit prime has \(r_p\ne0,p-1\), since \(b_0=1\) and reflection gives \(b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p\).

Because \(r_p=m-q_pp\) and \(p\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\),

\[
\gcd(r_p,p-1)=\gcd(m-q_p,p-1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Put

\[
g_p:=\gcd(m-q_p,p-1)=\frac{p-1}{d_p}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
g_p\mid m-q_p,
\qquad
p=d_pg_p+1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For fixed \(q\), the pair \((d_p,g_p)\) determines \(p\) injectively.

Let \(	au(n)\) be the divisor function and define

\[
Q(m;P):=\left\lceil\frac mP\right\rceil,
\qquad
\mathcal T(m;P)
 :=\sum_{1\le q\le Q(m;P)}\tau(m-q).
\tag{4.5}
\]

For relevant blocks and large \(m\), \(Q(m;P)<m\).

## Proposition 4.1 — low-order numerical-prime bound

For every \(D\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{low}}(m;P)\\d_p\le D}}
 \log p
 \le D\,\mathcal T(m;P)\log(2P).}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(q\).  By (4.4), a prime with \(q_p=q\) and \(d_p\le D\) is determined by

\[
1\le d\le D,
\qquad g\mid m-q,
\qquad p=dg+1.
\]

Ignoring primality, the quotient-cell inequalities, and the Apéry-zero condition only enlarges the candidate set.  There are at most \(D\tau(m-q)\) candidates for fixed \(q\).  Summation over \(q\), with weight at most \(\log(2P)\) per numerical prime, proves (4.6).  ∎

This proof is pointwise in \(m\).  The character order only partitions primes already satisfying literal divisibility; no formal Mellin root is promoted to an Apéry divisor.

## Corollary 4.2 — UDBRD scale for low orders

Fix \(\delta>0\), put \(L_P=\log(2P)\), and define

\[
D_\delta(m;P)
 :=\left\lfloor
 \frac{P}{16\,\mathcal T(m;P)L_P^{2+\delta}}
 \right\rfloor.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{low}}(m;P)\\
                 d_p\le D_\delta(m;P)}}\log p
 \ll_\delta\frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

If the floor is zero, the selected set is empty.

## Size of the cutoff

Let

\[
\Delta(x)=\max_{1\le n\le x}\tau(n).
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal T(m;P)\le Q(m;P)\Delta(m).
\tag{4.9}
\]

The classical maximal-order divisor bound is

\[
\Delta(m)
 =\exp\!\left(O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{\log\log m}\right)\right)
 =m^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Therefore, in the quotient-reduced range

\[
P\ge\frac{m}{F(m)\log m},
\qquad F(m)=m^{o(1)},
\tag{4.11}
\]

one has

\[
\mathcal T(m;P)=m^{o(1)},
\qquad
D_\delta(m;P)=m^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Thus only near-primitive orders remain in the top blocks.

For an elementary alternative away from very short quotient ranges,

\[
\sum_{M<n\le M+Q}\tau(n)
 \ll Q\log M+\sqrt M,
\tag{4.13}
\]

by counting divisors up to \(\sqrt M\).  One may use the better of (4.9) and (4.13).

# 5. Endpoint and reflection-centre pruning

For a lower-digit prime set

\[
s_p:=\min(r_p,p-1-r_p),
\qquad
h_p:=|p-1-2r_p|=p-1-2s_p.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Reflection gives \(p\mid b_{s_p}\).

## Proposition 5.1 — endpoint bound

For every \(E\ge0\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{low}}(m;P)\\s_p\le E}}
 \log p
 \ll(E+1)\log(2m).
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(s\).  If \(r_p=s\), then \(p\mid m-s\).  If \(r_p=p-1-s\), then \(p\mid m+s+1\).  The squarefree product in each orientation divides the corresponding fixed integer.  Its logarithm is \(O(\log(2m))\) for fixed \(s\).  Sum over \(s\le E\).  ∎

There is exact endpoint vacancy whenever

\[
(s+1)64^s<P,
\tag{5.3}
\]

because then \(0<b_s<P<p\).

## Proposition 5.2 — reflection-centre bound

For every \(H\ge0\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{low}}(m;P)\\h_p\le H}}
 \log p
 \ll(H+1)\log(3m).
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

For fixed \(h\), the two signs in (5.1) give

\[
(2q_p+1)p=2m+1+h
\quad\text{or}\quad
(2q_p+1)p=2m+1-h.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus every such prime divides one of two fixed integers of size \(O(m)\).  Sum over \(h\le H\).  ∎

A relevant block satisfies \(2P>\sqrt m\), hence

\[
\log m\le2\log(2P)+O(1).
\tag{5.6}
\]

With

\[
E_\delta(P)=H_\delta(P)
 :=\left\lfloor\frac{P}{16(\log(2P))^{2+\delta}}\right\rfloor,
\tag{5.7}
\]

the union of (5.2) and (5.4) contributes

\[
\ll_\delta\frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

# 6. Resolved versus terminal radical

Define the terminal set

\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal H_\delta(m;P):=\{p:\;&P<p\le\min(2P,m),\ p>\sqrt m,\\
 &p\mid b_{r_p},\quad d_p>D_\delta(m;P),\\
 &s_p>E_\delta(P),\quad h_p>H_\delta(P)\},
\end{split}
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
R_{\mathrm{hard},\delta}(m;P)
 :=\prod_{p\in\mathcal H_\delta(m;P)}p.
\tag{6.2}
\]

## Theorem 6.1 — pointwise UDBRD decomposition

For every fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly in \(m\) and every relevant block,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\log R(m;P)
\le{}&\log R_{\mathrm{hard},\delta}(m;P)\\
&+C_\delta\frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}}\\
&+C\left[
 \left(\frac mP\right)^2
 +\frac mP\log\!\left(2+\frac mP\right)
 \right].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof

Use (2.2).  Proposition 2.1 bounds the leading branch.  Every lower prime outside \(\mathcal H_\delta\) lies in the low-order, endpoint, or centre sector.  Corollary 4.2 and (5.8) bound their union.  ∎

The final line of (6.3) is at UDBRD scale in range (2.7).  Globally it is harmless at every scale by Corollary 2.2.

## Corollary 6.2 — global pointwise terminal reduction

Let \(P\) run over powers of two whose blocks meet \((\sqrt m,m]\).  Then

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(\sqrt m,m]}(b_m)
 \le
 C_\delta m^{2/3}
 +C_\delta\frac{m}{(\log m)^{1+\delta}}
 +\sum_P\log R_{\mathrm{hard},\delta}(m;P).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof

Apply Corollary 2.2 globally to the leading primes.  Sum the resolved lower bounds; \(\sum_PP\ll m\) and \(\log P\gg\log m\).  ∎

For the actual gcd, the source's small-prime, companion-block, lower-digit, and multiplicity theorems give

\[
\boxed{
\log G_m
 \le C_\delta m^{2/3}
 +C_\delta\frac{m}{(\log m)^{1+\delta}}
 +3\sum_P\log R_{\mathrm{hard},\delta}(m;P).}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore a uniform \(o(m)\) estimate for the terminal sum would finish P3.2.  It is a sufficient repair, not a logically necessary reformulation: the terminal support is deliberately support-blind and may contain primes excluded by the finer actual-gcd masks.

After quotient reduction, if

\[
P\ge\frac{m}{F(m)\log m},
\qquad F(m)=m^{o(1)},
\]

then every unresolved prime satisfies

\[
q_p\le F(m)\log m,
\tag{6.6}
\]

\[
d_p=m^{1-o(1)},
\tag{6.7}
\]

and

\[
s_p,h_p\gg
 \frac{m}{F(m)(\log m)^{3+\delta}}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The remaining object is thus the near-primitive, deep, off-centre defining-characteristic diagonal.

# 8. Exact irreducible full-order subcase

## Proposition 8.1

Assume \(m-1\) is prime.  For every prime \(m/2<p<m\),

\[
q_p=1,
\qquad r_p=m-p,
\qquad d_p=p-1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Proof

Modulo \(p-1\),

\[
r_p=m-p\equiv m-1\pmod{p-1}.
\]

Since \(m-1\) is prime and \(0<p-1<m-1\),

\[
\gcd(r_p,p-1)=\gcd(m-1,p-1)=1.
\]

Use (4.1).  ∎

In this range, Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
b_m=b_{p+(m-p)}\equiv b_1b_{m-p}=5b_{m-p}\pmod p.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For \(p>5\),

\[
p\mid b_m\iff p\mid b_{m-p}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Reparameterizing \(r=m-p\) yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(m/2,m)}(b_m)
 =\sum_{\substack{0<r<m/2\\m-r\ \text{prime}\\m-r\mid b_r}}

\tag{8.4}
\]

This subcase survives every low-order cutoff.  Therefore an argument that merely discards character-order sectors cannot finish P3.2; it must use the Apéry zero condition in the full-order sector.

# 9. Audit of the remaining advertised mechanisms

## 9.1 Archimedean size and product formula

The actual asymptotic is

\[
\log b_m=m\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log m),
\qquad
\log(17+12\sqrt2)=3.52549\ldots.
\tag{9.1}
\]

The available prime capacity is

\[
\log\prod_{\sqrt m<p\le m}p
 =\vartheta(m)-\vartheta(\sqrt m)=m+o(m).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Thus \(b_m\) has Archimedean room for every prime in the target interval once, with a linear margin.  On one block the rigorous bound is only

\[
\log R(m;P)
 \le\min\{\log b_m,\vartheta(2P)-\vartheta(P)\}
 \le(1+o(1))P,
\tag{9.3}
\]

with truncation at \(m\).  This is full prime capacity, not a logarithmic deficit.

A product-formula reformulation does not improve (9.3); it charges the same finite places against the same Archimedean height.  A new theorem about which primes occur is required.

## 9.2 Valuations, Wronskians, and supercongruences

The Wronskian controls multiplicity in \(G_m\), whereas UDBRD is radical.  Even the hypothetical exact law \(v_p(b_m)=1\) at every target prime would not change \(R(m;P)\).

Supercongruences compare indices in one fixed \(p\)-power tower.  They do not suppress support while the defining prime varies.  The exact failed implication is

\[
\text{uniform same-prime valuation control}
\not\Longrightarrow
\#\{P<p\le2P:p\mid b_m\}=o(P/\log P).
\]

## 9.3 Binomial, diagonal, K3, and hypergeometric formulas

The binomial formula proves the height (2.5) and endpoint vacancy (5.3), but in the deep range only gives (9.1).

The toric formula gives

\[
b_r=-\sum_{a\ne0}\mu_p(a)a^r\pmod p.
\tag{9.4}
\]

The K3 theorem bounds each nonzero-fiber residual by \(O(p)\), but (9.4) has \(p-1\) summands.  It does not prevent exact cancellation modulo \(p\); the source records the actual cancellation \((p,r)=(11,5)\).

The weighted-square formula

\[
b_r=-\sum_{t\ne0}t^r(1-t)^{p-1-2r}A_r(t)^2
\tag{9.5}
\]

is an indefinite finite-field pairing.  The source expands it exactly back to the original Apéry binomial sum, so it supplies no second congruence.

The reciprocal-square/Hasse factorization also does not restrict coefficient-zero patterns in its free half-range: the source proves the relevant coefficient map is triangular and surjective.

## 9.4 Congruence classes

A fixed positive-density restriction on prime residue classes yields at best \(cP+o(P)\), not \(P/(\log P)^{1+\delta}\).  One needs an allowed density tending to zero.  The order parametrization (4.4) is the strongest proved moving-modulus restriction; the full-order case \(g_p=1\) imposes none.

## 9.5 Restricted-denominator irrationality

UDBRD counts factors of \(b_m\) that need not lie in the actual gcd, so irrationality estimates cannot prove the support-blind statement.

Even for \(G_m\), with \(\alpha=17+12\sqrt2\),

\[
b_m\asymp\alpha^m,
\qquad
|b_m\zeta(3)-a_m|\asymp\alpha^{-m},
\qquad
\log d_m=3m+o(m).
\]

If \(g_m=G_m\), the reduced denominator is \(B_m\asymp d_mb_m/g_m\).  Even gifting irrationality exponent \(2\), comparison with

\[
\left|\zeta(3)-\frac{a_m}{b_m}\right|\asymp\alpha^{-2m}
\]

gives only

\[
\log g_m\le3m+o(m),
\tag{9.6}
\]

not \(o(m)\).  A restricted-denominator theorem removing this \(3m\) cost would have to detect precisely the large-prime support now under study; it is new arithmetic, not an existing consequence.

## 9.6 Formal carriers and resultants

Gap polynomials and resultants provide necessary carriers for some actual zero configurations, not equivalences.  The source contains primitive phantom carrier roots at primes with no corresponding Apéry zero.  Replacing \(p\mid b_{r_p}\) by divisibility of such a carrier loses the actual marking and cannot prove UDBRD.

# 11. Standing checks

## Equivalence

The low-order theorem is not UDBRD restated.  It remains true in a world where every full-order candidate divides \(b_m\); Proposition 8.1 shows that an entire top-half interval can lie in the omitted order sector.

The endpoint and centre results are independent exact divisibility arguments.

By contrast, an \(o(m)\) terminal-radical estimate is only the residual sufficient premise after the proved deletions.  It is not presented as a new mechanism or as equivalent to P3.2.

## Packaging

Every counted object is a numerical prime satisfying a literal divisibility condition involving \(b_m\), \(b_{q_p}\), or \(b_{r_p}\).  No formal resultant root, gap-polynomial root, K3 fiber, or Hasse evaluation root is counted as an Apéry divisor.

The character order is only an attribute of an already selected lower-digit prime.  Proposition 4.1 deliberately counts a larger explicit numerical candidate set, so no marking is lost.

## Quantifiers

Every proved estimate is uniform and pointwise in \(m\).  There is no average over \(m\), density-one exception, Palm normalization, or division by a possibly zero mass.

## Multiplicity

The block products are radicals.  Multiplicity re-enters only in (6.5), through the source bound \(v_p(G_m)\le3\) for \(p>\sqrt m\).

## Finite evidence

No scan, observed maximum, fitted smoothness law, or random-divisor heuristic appears in a proof.
