# Q873 audit: top-prime-block Diagonal AMTD

## Verdict

No unconditional proof of the required block estimate \(F=O(T)\) follows from the currently banked inputs. The exact reduction is correct, but every proposed characteristic-zero route still stops at the same missing two-large-prime correlation statement. A valid weakest intermediate lemma is an explicit gap-restricted pair estimate stated below.

## 1. Normalization audit

For \(I_N=(N,2N]\), define

\[
X_p(m)=1_{p\le m,\ p\mid b_m},\qquad N<m\le2N.
\]

Since \(p>N\), write \(m=pq+r\). In this interval only \(q=1\), so Apéry--Lucas gives

\[
b_m\equiv b_1b_r\equiv 5b_r\pmod p.
\]

Therefore the top-block event is equivalent to \(p\mid b_r\), with \(0\le r<p\). This identification is exact.

The quantities

\[
A_p=\sum_{m\in I_N}X_p(m),\quad
T=\sum_p A_p,
\]

and

\[
F=\sum_m\lambda(m)(\lambda(m)-1)
\]

satisfy

\[
F=\sum_{p\ne q}|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|.
\]

Thus \(F=O(T)\) is precisely a second-moment sparsity assertion for simultaneous divisibility by two primes in \((N,2N]\).

## 2. Direct gap summation

For \(p,q>N\), a simultaneous hit means

\[
b_m\equiv b_{m-p}\equiv b_{m-q}\equiv0
\pmod{pq}
\]

because the lower digits are \(m-p\) and \(m-q\).

The recurrence can propagate one zero forward, but it does not independently control two different backward residues. The no-consecutive-zero and gap-polynomial statements give restrictions for a single modulus \(p\); they do not provide a joint bound for

\[
\#\{m: b_m\equiv b_{m-p}\equiv0\pmod{pq}\}.
\]

Hence gap summation alone gives no bound below the ambient pair count.

## 3. q=1 transfer / continuant route

The top block has only one quotient digit, so the transfer is from \(m\) to \(r=m-p\). A recurrence matrix gives

\[
(b_{r+1},b_r)^T=M_r(b_r,b_{r-1})^T.
\]

A second prime imposes another transfer at a different residue. To obtain \(F=O(T)\), one needs a non-degeneracy statement that the two transfer constraints are rarely compatible modulo \(pq\).

The needed statement is not supplied by the determinant/Casoratian because the determinant controls two solutions at the same index, whereas here the two constraints are on shifted indices.

## 4. Resultant / gcd route

A possible exact lemma would be:

For distinct primes \(p,q\in(N,2N]\), let

\[
H_{p,q}=\{m\in(N,2N]:p\mid b_m,\ q\mid b_m\}.
\]

Then prove

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}|H_{p,q}|\ll\sum_p A_p.
\]

Equivalently, one needs a uniform bound on the resultant/gcd obstruction generated by the two residue conditions.

No such resultant identity is currently derived. The ordinary recurrence determinant has insufficient height: it only sees the two-dimensional solution space, not the independent prime shifts.

## 5. Fourier/dispersion route

Writing indicators by additive characters produces complete sums involving

\[
\sum_m e_{pq}(u b_m)
\]

or equivalent recurrence phases. Without a proven cancellation estimate for these Apéry exponential sums, this is only a reformulation of the missing correlation problem.

## Weakest exact intermediate lemma

A sufficient statement strictly below the final AMTD conclusion is the following gap-pair lemma.

For every dyadic \(N\), every distinct \(p,q\in(N,2N]\), define

\[
C_{p,q}=\#\{r<pq:\ N< p+r\le2N,\ p\mid b_r,\ q\mid b_{r+p-q}\}.
\]

Prove

\[
\sum_{N<p,q\le2N\atop p\ne q} C_{p,q}
\ll
\sum_{N<p\le2N} A_p.
\]

This is exactly the needed input after the Apéry--Lucas reduction and is weaker than proving a full independence model or \(Z(p)=O(1)\).

## Final verdict

- The physical interval \(p,q>N\) is handled correctly.
- The lower-digit translation is exact.
- No random-model assumption is used.
- Gap continuants, resultants, and Fourier dispersion each identify the same missing object: a genuine two-prime shifted-zero correlation estimate.
- The proposed Diagonal AMTD route is therefore not proved; the minimal remaining theorem is the explicit pair-correlation lemma above.
