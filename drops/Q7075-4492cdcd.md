# CRT unlikely intersections for Apéry atoms

The Bombieri--Masser--Zannier/Pila--Wilkie philosophy is suggestive, but it does not directly apply to the Apéry atom problem. Classical BMZ requires a fixed algebraic variety of bounded complexity; here the zero sets vary with the prime.

For each p one has the finite set
\[
Z_p=\{r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]
and the family is indexed by changing finite fields. There is no single curve in a fixed torus whose rational points are the atoms. The CRT height condition
\[
\operatorname{CRT}(r_p,r_q,r_\ell)<X^2
\]
is an Archimedean inequality after passing between unrelated residue rings, not an algebraic subgroup condition.

A direct Pila--Wilkie argument therefore cannot give an H^\epsilon bound.

The correct analogy is an adelic unlikely-intersection problem: the Apéry recurrence defines a family of finite dynamical systems, and one asks whether simultaneous zero hits can have unexpectedly small CRT representatives. A theorem here would need uniform control over a varying-prime family.

The Apéry-specific substitutes for transcendence are:

* gap-polynomial sparsity of each zero set, giving |Z_p| bounds;
* reflection and no-consecutive-zero structure;
* codegree estimates for simultaneous hits;
* possible Fourier cancellation for CRT lifts.

The dimension heuristic is misleading because the ambient object is not a fixed three-dimensional torus. The coordinates live in \(\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_q\times\mathbb F_\ell\) with varying moduli and varying defining equations.

A realistic route is to prove an arithmetic equidistribution theorem: zero choices from different primes should have CRT representatives distributed across \([0,pq\ell)\). Combined with local sparsity this would give the missing atom-tail estimate. Such a theorem would be a new finite-field/adelic analogue of unlikely intersections, rather than an application of BMZ or Pila--Wilkie.
