# Verdict

The elementary Hilbert-space estimate is **correct and already proves the stated fixed-`g`, unweighted dyadic mean-square saving**, provided the summation in

\[
S(h)=\sum_N f_N(h)
\]

is understood as

\[
\boxed{S(h)=\sum_{N\in\mathcal P} f_N(h).}
\]

Under that intended indexing,

\[
\boxed{
V\ll
\frac{g^{8/3}X^{8/3}}
{\varphi(g)^2\log^2X}
=o(M)
}
\]

for every family `g=g(X)` with `g=X^{o(1)}`, assuming—as is standard in the stated available theorems—that the implied constants in the bounds for `|P|` and `z_N` are uniform over this allowed `g`-range.

There is no endpoint defect, no overlap defect, and no hidden covariance term: the Hilbert-space inequality dominates all off-diagonal correlations at once.

The only literal notation failure would be this: if `sum_N` really ranges over **all** integers `X<N<=2X`, while `f_N` is nonzero for nonprime candidates too, then the first factor `|P|` is unjustified. One would have to replace it by the number of all active `N`, which is `asymp X`. In the intended prime-candidate model, where the sum is over `N in P` (or equivalently `f_N` contains the factor `1_{N in P}`), the argument is valid.

What it proves is exactly a **single-`g` unweighted mean-square estimate over the `h`-rows**. It does **not** prove the all-`n` pointwise theorem, does not eliminate a sparse set of exceptional rows, does not combine different `g`-slices, and does not by itself give a log-weighted cross-`g` estimate.

# 1. Exact support and norm

Fix one `N in P`. By definition

\[
f_N(h)=F_N(h-qN)
\]

when

\[
qN<h<(q+1)N,
\]

and is zero otherwise.

Because `qN` and `(q+1)N` are integers, the integer values of `h` in this open interval are exactly

\[
qN+1,qN+2,\ldots,(q+1)N-1.
\]

Therefore the translation

\[
a=h-qN
\]

is a bijection from the support of `f_N` onto

\[
\{1,2,\ldots,N-1\}.
\]

There is no missing endpoint and no extra endpoint. Hence, with the ambient Hilbert space `ell^2(Z)`, exactly

\[
\|f_N\|_2^2
=\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}|F_N(a)|^2.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Now `Z_N` is a subset of the reduced residues. On the `phi(N)` reduced residues,

\[
F_N(a)=
\begin{cases}
1-\rho_N,&a\in Z_N,\\
-\rho_N,&(a,N)=1,\ a\notin Z_N,
\end{cases}
\]

while on nonreduced residues `F_N(a)=0`.

Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|f_N\|_2^2
&=z_N(1-\rho_N)^2
 +(\varphi(N)-z_N)\rho_N^2\\
&=z_N-2z_N\rho_N+\varphi(N)\rho_N^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since

\[
\rho_N=\frac{z_N}{\varphi(N)},
\]

we have

\[
\varphi(N)\rho_N^2=z_N\rho_N.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|f_N\|_2^2=z_N(1-\rho_N).
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

This equality is exact.

In particular, because `0<=rho_N<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\|f_N\|_2^2\le z_N.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The centering is used only for the sharper exact formula (1.2); inequality (1.3) is immediate from it.

# 2. Hilbert-space estimate

Assume from now on that

\[
S=\sum_{N\in\mathcal P}f_N.
\]

For every fixed `h`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\left|\sum_{N\in\mathcal P}f_N(h)\right|^2
\le
|\mathcal P|
\sum_{N\in\mathcal P}|f_N(h)|^2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

One could replace `|P|` here by the smaller number of supports containing that particular `h`, but that refinement is unnecessary.

Summing (2.1) over all integers `h`, all terms being nonnegative and finitely supported,

\[
\begin{aligned}
V
:=\sum_h|S(h)|^2
&\le
|\mathcal P|
\sum_h\sum_{N\in\mathcal P}|f_N(h)|^2\\
&=
|\mathcal P|
\sum_{N\in\mathcal P}\|f_N\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
V\le |\mathcal P|
\sum_{N\in\mathcal P}z_N(1-\rho_N).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Using `1-rho_N<=1`,

\[
V
\le |\mathcal P|\sum_{N\in\mathcal P}z_N
\le |\mathcal P|^2\max_{N\in\mathcal P}z_N.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Every step is exact or one-sided in the displayed direction. No assumption about pairwise support overlap or covariance is present.

This is the important structural point: expanding `V` into diagonal and off-diagonal terms is unnecessary if this crude Hilbert-space bound already lies below the target scale.

# 3. Insert the two available theorems

The first available theorem is

\[
|\mathcal P|
\ll
\frac{gX}{\varphi(g)\log X}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The second is

\[
z_N\ll(gN)^{2/3}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For `X<N<=2X`,

\[
(gN)^{2/3}\le (2gX)^{2/3}\ll g^{2/3}X^{2/3},
\]

so uniformly on the dyadic block

\[
\max_{N\in\mathcal P}z_N
\ll g^{2/3}X^{2/3}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Substituting (3.1) and (3.3) into (2.4),

\[
\begin{aligned}
V
&\ll
\left(
\frac{gX}{\varphi(g)\log X}
\right)^2
(gX)^{2/3}\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{g^{8/3}X^{8/3}}
{\varphi(g)^2\log^2X}.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

So the power of `g`, the power of `X`, and both `phi(g)` and logarithmic denominators in the proposed estimate are all correct.

---

# 4. Exact comparison with the target scale

The target is

\[
M=
\frac{g^2X^3}
{\varphi(g)^2\log^2X(\log\log X)^2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Divide the bound (3.4) by `M`. The `phi(g)^{-2}` and `log^{-2}X` factors cancel exactly, leaving

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{
 g^{8/3}X^{8/3}/(\varphi(g)^2\log^2X)
}{
 g^2X^3/(\varphi(g)^2\log^2X(\log\log X)^2)
}
&=
\boxed{
 g^{2/3}X^{-1/3}(\log\log X)^2.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Now assume

\[
g=X^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently, along the allowed family,

\[
\frac{\log g}{\log X}\to0.
\]

For example, for all sufficiently large `X` we may take

\[
g\le X^{1/4}.
\]

Then

\[
g^{2/3}\le X^{1/6},
\]

and hence

\[
g^{2/3}X^{-1/3}(\log\log X)^2
\le
X^{-1/6}(\log\log X)^2
\to0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{V=o(M).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

More intrinsically,

\[
g^{2/3}X^{-1/3}=X^{-1/3+o(1)},
\]

so (4.2) is

\[
X^{-1/3+o(1)}(\log\log X)^2=o(1).
\]

There is a very large power margin; the two logarithms in `M` are harmless.

---

# 5. Uniformity caveat on the `<<` constants

The conclusion above uses the usual interpretation of the two supplied theorems: their implied constants are uniform over the allowed `g=X^{o(1)}` range (and, if relevant, may depend on the fixed `q`).

If instead one allowed the hidden constants in

\[
|P|\ll \frac{gX}{\varphi(g)\log X},
\qquad
z_N\ll(gN)^{2/3}
\]

to depend arbitrarily on `g=g(X)`, then the notation would not by itself imply a uniform `o(M)` statement. An uncontrolled constant could grow faster than the power saving in (4.2).

That is not the standard reading of an “available theorem” in this model, but it is the only genuine quantifier caveat in the asymptotic step.

For each **fixed numerical `g`**, no such issue exists at all: (4.2) is simply

\[
O_g(X^{-1/3}(\log\log X)^2)=o(1).
\]

For a varying subpolynomial `g(X)`, uniform theorem constants are required.

# 6. The only indexing ambiguity that can make the argument false

The model says:

- `P` is the set of `N` with `1+gN` prime;
- for each `N`, `F_N` and `f_N` are defined;
- then `S(h)=sum_N f_N(h)`.

There are two possible literal readings.

## Intended reading

\[
S(h)=\sum_{N\in\mathcal P}f_N(h).
\]

Then Sections 1--5 prove the claimed result.

## Literal all-`N` reading

If instead

\[
S(h)=\sum_{X<N\le2X}f_N(h)
\]

with nonzero `f_N` also for `N\notin P`, then (2.1) only gives

\[
|S(h)|^2
\le
\#\{N:X<N\le2X\}
\sum_N|f_N(h)|^2,
\]

so the factor is `asymp X`, not `|P|`.

That is the **first precise failure** under the literal all-`N` interpretation. The prime-count theorem for `|P|` would then be irrelevant to those extra functions.

This is repaired either by declaring the sum to be over `N in P`, or by defining

\[
f_N(h)
:={\bf1}_{N\in P}F_N(h-qN)
\]

from the start.

# 7. What the estimate proves

Under the intended indexing, the theorem is:

> **Fixed-`g` Hilbert mean-square lemma.**  For one `g`-slice and one dyadic block `X<N<=2X`, if
> \[
> |P|\ll\frac{gX}{\varphi(g)\log X},
> \qquad
> z_N\ll(gN)^{2/3},
> \]
> then
> \[
> V
> \ll
> \frac{g^{8/3}X^{8/3}}
> {\varphi(g)^2\log^2X}.
> \]
> Uniformly for `g=X^{o(1)}`, this is `o(M)`.

This is an **unweighted mean square over `h` for a single `g`-slice**.

It also trivially controls any unweighted subset of rows, since for `E subset Z`,

\[
\sum_{h\in E}|S(h)|^2\le V.
\]

But that observation still does not turn the statement into a pointwise theorem.

By Chebyshev, for any threshold `T>0`,

\[
\#\{h:|S(h)|\ge T\}
\le \frac VT^2.
\]

At the natural PTMA-size threshold this can show that large rows are sparse. It cannot show that there are no such rows.

---

# 8. What it does not prove

The estimate does **not** establish any of the following without additional work.

1. **All-`n` / pointwise control.**  A mean square can be tiny compared with `M` while one or a few rows remain exceptional. Thus this does not prove PTMA or the final all-`n` theorem.

2. **Cross-`g` control.**  The proof fixes one `g` inside the sum. If the actual object is
   \[
   S_{\rm total}(h)=\sum_g S_g(h),
   \]
   then
   \[
   \|S_{\rm total}\|_2^2
   \]
   contains cross-`g` terms. One cannot simply add the fixed-`g` estimates and call that the norm of the total sum. A further Cauchy factor involving the number/weight of `g`-slices, or an actual cross-`g` argument, is required.

3. **The actual row-dependent divisor condition `g|m`.**  In the fixed-`n` packet geometry, the admissible `g` are tied to the row integer. The present Hilbert model freezes `g` first and averages over rows. Passing back to the union of row-dependent divisor slices is a separate bookkeeping/analytic step.

4. **Log-weighted prime mass.**  Here every `N` contributes with coefficient `1`. If the application weights a target by `log p_N`, then one must insert those weights into the Hilbert norm and compare against the correspondingly weighted target scale. The unweighted theorem does not automatically state that result.

5. **Adaptive weighted exceptional-set estimates.**  Restricting to an unweighted subset `E` is harmless, as noted above, but a nontrivial row weight or a normalization depending on `g,h,N` is a different quadratic form and must be audited separately.

---

# Final audit

So, in the self-contained model as intended, there is **no hidden off-diagonal obstruction at all**: the crude Hilbert-space inequality is stronger than the target scale by the factor

\[
\boxed{
X^{1/3-o(1)}/(\log\log X)^2.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac VM
\ll
 g^{2/3}X^{-1/3}(\log\log X)^2=o(1).
}
\]

The calculation is rigorous.

The first and only literal failure is if `S=sum_N f_N` includes nonprime-candidate `N`; then `|P|` cannot be used in Cauchy. With `S=sum_{N in P}f_N`—or with the prime indicator absorbed into `f_N`—the proposed conclusion is valid.
