# Apéry large-prime radical: exact reduction, literature audit, and the sharp remaining carrier

## Verdict

I do **not** know an unconditional proof of

\[
 \log \operatorname{rad}_{\sqrt n<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n}p=o(n)
 \qquad(n\to\infty)
 \tag{LR}
\]

for every integer \(n\), and I found no published theorem that implies it.
The available theorems on Apéry numbers, diagonals of rational functions,
\(G\)-functions, Lucas congruences, or holonomic sequences do not give an
every-\(n\) upper bound for the moving large-prime part of one coefficient.
The closest direct Apéry paper, Luca--Shparlinski, gives density-one **lower**
bounds for the number and size of prime factors.  Delaygue gives local
\(p\)-adic valuation and Lucas-property theorems.  Fischler--Rivoal control
denominators of \(G\)-function coefficients, which is vacuous here because
\(b_n\in\mathbb Z\).  None controls the numerator radical in a moving interval.

The reduction in the question is correct, with one harmless qualification:
for \(p>\sqrt n\), write

\[
 n=q_p p+r_p,\qquad 0\le r_p<p,\qquad q_p<\sqrt n.
\]

Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
 b_n\equiv b_{q_p}b_{r_p}\pmod p.
 \tag{1}
\]

Thus a prime \(p>\sqrt n\) dividing \(b_n\) either satisfies
\(p\mid b_{r_p}\), or belongs to the exceptional quotient channel
\(p\mid b_{q_p}\).  Splitting at \(Q=n^{1/3}\), the logarithmic weight of
the latter is

\[
 \ll \sum_{q\le Q}\log b_q+\vartheta(n/Q)
 \ll Q^2+n/Q
 \ll n^{2/3}.
 \tag{2}
\]

Consequently (LR) is equivalent, up to \(O(n^{2/3})=o(n)\), to the original
moving-remainder channel.

What remains is genuinely horizontal: several distinct characteristics must
be prevented from simultaneously selecting their own unrelated zero indices
\(r_p=n-q_pp\).  All currently available Apéry identities are vertical
(one prime at a time), or have characteristic-zero height \(\asymp n\).

## 1. Exact verification of the reduction

Let

\[
 C(n)=\{p>\sqrt n:p\mid b_n\},\qquad
 B(n)=\{p>\sqrt n:p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}.
\]

For \(p>\sqrt n\), \(q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor<p\) and \(r=n-qp<p\).  The
one-digit Gessel--Lucas congruence is (1).  Hence \(B(n)\subseteq C(n)\),
because \(p\mid b_r\) forces \(p\mid b_n\).

If \(p\in C(n)\setminus B(n)\), then \(p\mid b_q\).  Split these primes into
\(q\le Q\) and \(q>Q\).

For \(q\le Q\), every such prime divides \(\prod_{j\le Q}b_j\), so

\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\in C(n)\setminus B(n)\\q_p\le Q}}\log p
 \le \sum_{j\le Q}\log b_j
 =O(Q^2),
 \tag{3}
\]

using \(\log b_j=j\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log j)\).

For \(q>Q\), one has \(p\le n/Q\), and therefore

\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\in C(n)\setminus B(n)\\q_p>Q}}\log p
 \le \vartheta(n/Q)=O(n/Q).
 \tag{4}
\]

Taking \(Q=n^{1/3}\) proves (2).  Thus

\[
 \sum_{p\in C(n)}\log p
 =\sum_{p\in B(n)}\log p+O(n^{2/3}).
 \tag{5}
\]

Since \(B(n)\subseteq C(n)\), (LR) and
\(\sum_{p\in B(n)}\log p=o(n)\) are equivalent.

## 2. Literature audit: no applicable published theorem

### 2.1 Luca--Shparlinski

Florian Luca and Igor E. Shparlinski, **Arithmetic properties of Apéry
numbers**, *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* (2) 78 (2008),
545--562, DOI `10.1112/jlms/jdn031`, prove that for a density-one set of
integers \(n\),

\[
 \omega(b_n)>c_0\log\log\log n,
 \qquad
 P(b_n)>c_0(\log n\log\log n)^{1/2}.
\]

These are lower bounds, for almost all \(n\).  They do not give an upper bound
for the number or logarithmic weight of primes in \((\sqrt n,n]\), and they
do not hold in the required pointwise form.

### 2.2 Delaygue and Lucas-property theorems

Éric Delaygue, **Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers**,
*Compositio Mathematica* 154 (2018), 249--274,
DOI `10.1112/S0010437X17007552`, proves lower bounds for \(p\)-adic
valuations and effective criteria for \(p\)-Lucas properties of factorial-ratio
multisums.  These results justify and generalize reductions such as (1), but
they do not bound how many different primes can simultaneously satisfy the
resulting residue-index conditions for one fixed \(n\).

Rowland--Yassawi's finite-automaton results for diagonals modulo \(p^\alpha\)
and Straub's multivariate supercongruences have the same limitation: they
describe each characteristic separately.  The automaton or Cartier operator
changes with \(p\), and no cross-prime height compression is supplied.

### 2.3 \(G\)-function denominator theorems

Fischler--Rivoal, **On the denominators of the Taylor coefficients of
G-functions**, *Kyushu Journal of Mathematics* 71 (2017), 287--298,
DOI `10.2206/kyushujm.71.287`, bound common denominators of initial
coefficient segments of a \(G\)-function.  The Apéry coefficients are already
integers.  Their theorem contains no assertion about prime divisors of the
numerators, let alone the radical supported on \((\sqrt n,n]\).

### 2.4 Why no theorem for the ambient classes can suffice

The obstruction is structural.  The central binomial coefficient

\[
 c_n=\binom{2n}{n}=\operatorname{Diag}\frac1{1-x-y}
\]

is hypergeometric, P-recursive, an integral \(G\)-function coefficient, and a
diagonal of a fixed rational function.  Yet every prime \(n<p\le2n\) divides
\(c_n\), so

\[
 \log\operatorname{rad}_{n<p\le2n}c_n
 =\vartheta(2n)-\vartheta(n)\sim n.
 \tag{6}
\]

Therefore no result based only on being holonomic, a diagonal, a constant-term
sequence, globally bounded, or a \(G\)-function can prove (LR).  A successful
theorem must use an Apéry-specific horizontal feature absent from factorial
carry sequences.

## 3. What the standard Apéry structures actually give

### 3.1 Constant-term/diagonal representation

A fixed Laurent-polynomial model gives

\[
 b_n=\operatorname{CT}\Phi^n
\]

for an Apéry Laurent polynomial \(\Phi\), and hence the generating series is a
rational diagonal.  Modulo a fixed prime, Cartier decomposition explains the
Lucas congruence.  In characteristic zero, however, the natural coefficient
carrier is still \(b_n\) itself, whose logarithmic height is

\[
 \log b_n=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
 \tag{7}
\]

No known constant-term identity replaces this by an integer of height \(o(n)\)
that contains all primes in \(B(n)\).

### 3.2 Modular parametrization

The Beukers--Peters modular/K3 parametrization controls the generating
function as a function of its geometric parameter.  The condition
\(p\mid b_{r_p}\) is a **coefficient** condition in characteristic \(p\), not
the vanishing of a fixed modular form value at a fixed algebraic point.
Fourier/Mellin inversion converts it into a vanishing Mellin coefficient of a
Hasse--Witt function, but exact zero detection uses correlations whose order
grows with \(p\).  Existing Deligne/Katz equidistribution theorems for a fixed
bounded-conductor sheaf do not apply uniformly to this growing-complexity
coefficient selector.

### 3.3 Dwork congruences

Dwork/Gessel congruences factor a coefficient according to base-\(p\) digits.
For \(p>\sqrt n\), this is exactly (1), which has already been fully exploited
in (2).  Higher \(p\)-adic lifts refine the condition inside one
characteristic.  They do not relate two distinct primes \(p\) and \(\ell\),
because the Frobenius lifts, residue indices, and divided coordinates are
all characteristic-dependent.

### 3.4 Holonomic recurrence

The Apéry recurrence propagates a zero at one index modulo one prime and gives
continuant/Casoratian constraints between two indices **in the same field**.
For a fixed index gap \(h\), these yield explicit polynomial or integer
certificates.  But when two primes \(p,\ell\mid b_n\) are reduced by (1), their
zero indices are

\[
 r_p=n-q_pp,\qquad r_\ell=n-q_\ell\ell,
 \tag{8}
\]

and both the quotient and the gap vary.  Summing fixed-gap certificates over
the full range restores linear height.  The recurrence has no known invariant
that is simultaneously integral, nonzero, independent of \(p,\ell\), and of
sublinear height.

## 4. The strongest exact deterministic coupling currently available

There is a clean exact coupling, but its height is precisely the forbidden
linear scale.

Define

\[
 R_n=\prod_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}p.
\]

Then, tautologically but importantly,

\[
 R_n\mid b_n,
 \qquad
 \log R_n\le n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
 \tag{9}
\]

Using Gessel--Lucas, remove the quotient-exceptional factor

\[
 E_n=\prod_{\substack{p>\sqrt n,\ p\mid b_n\\p\mid b_{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}}}p.
\]

Equation (2) gives \(\log E_n=O(n^{2/3})\).  Hence the genuinely unresolved
carrier

\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{main}}=R_n/E_n
\]

satisfies

\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{main}}\mid b_n,
 \qquad
 p\mid b_{n-q_pp}\quad(p\mid R_n^{\mathrm{main}}),
 \qquad
 \log R_n^{\mathrm{main}}\le cn+O(n^{2/3}).
 \tag{10}
\]

This is the sharp current deterministic identity coupling **all** distinct
large prime divisors of the same coefficient.  It is not enough because its
height is linear.

A somewhat more structured pair formulation is the following.  If distinct
\(p,\ell>\sqrt n\) lie in the main channel, then

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 n&=q_pp+r_p=q_\ell\ell+r_\ell,\\
 p&\mid b_{r_p},\qquad \ell\mid b_{r_\ell},\\
 p\ell&\mid b_n.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]

Reflection adds

\[
 p\mid b_{p-1-r_p},\qquad
 \ell\mid b_{\ell-1-r_\ell},
 \tag{12}
\]

but these reflected indices correspond to different outer integers and do not
create a second common characteristic-zero divisor.  The Apéry Wronskian
prevents simultaneous vanishing of the two fundamental solutions modulo one
prime, but for a pure-cross pair it reduces to unit relations separately
modulo \(p\) and modulo \(\ell\).  It does not imply that \(p\ell\) divides a
new integer smaller than \(b_n\).

This is exactly the point where all bounded-order tensor, fixed-gap
continuant, and finite Dwork-lift proposals fail: after imposing the local
relations, each prime retains one independent target coordinate.  Their
product is coupled only through the original coefficient \(b_n\).

## 5. A precise missing lemma that would close the route

Any one of the following genuinely Apéry-specific statements would prove
(LR).

### Sublinear carrier form

Construct nonzero integers \(H_n\) such that

\[
 \prod_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}p\mid H_n,
 \qquad
 \log|H_n|=o(n).
 \tag{13}
\]

The point is that \(H_n=b_n\) has height \(cn\), and no known symbolic
transformation lowers it.

### Dyadic horizontal saving

It suffices to prove, uniformly for \(\sqrt n<X\le n\),

\[
 \sum_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_n}}\log p
 \ll \frac{X}{L(n)}
 \tag{14}
\]

for some \(L(n)\to\infty\), with the obvious truncation at \(n\).  Summing
\(O(\log n)\) dyadic intervals requires a correspondingly strong uniform
saving; an isolated fixed-gap or fixed-quotient estimate is not enough.

### Cross-prime zero nonalignment

After deleting the quotient-exceptional channel, prove

\[
 \sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt n\\p\mid b_{n-p\lfloor n/p\rfloor}}}\log p=o(n).
 \tag{15}
\]

This is essentially (LR) restated, but it identifies the exact new ingredient:
a theorem that correlates the mod-\(p\) zero sets of the Apéry sequence across
changing primes along the affine sections \(r=n-q p\).

No theorem cited above supplies (13), (14), or (15).

## 6. Why an interpolation/resultant construction does not automatically help

For a fixed quotient \(q\), one can interpolate the values

\[
 X\longmapsto b_{n-qX}
\]

on the candidate integer interval.  If an integral polynomial \(F_{n,q}(X)\)
represents these values, then

\[
 p\mid F_{n,q}(p)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid F_{n,q}(0).
\]

But the natural constant term is \(F_{n,q}(0)=b_n\).  Thus the
"modulus equals the variable" specialization recovers exactly the original
linear-height carrier.  Reducing the interpolation degree by recurrence
elimination introduces denominators or leading minors whose product has
linear logarithmic height.  A resultant can only improve the argument if an
Apéry-specific factorization removes the \(b_n\) component while retaining all
target primes.  No such factorization is known.

This is a useful no-go, not merely a failed calculation: ordinary polynomial
interpolation of the pulled-back recurrence cannot by itself create a
sublinear carrier, because evaluation at \(X=p\) modulo \(p\) sees only the
constant term.

## 7. Final answer

1. The stated Gessel--Lucas reduction and the \(O(n^{2/3})\) exceptional
   quotient estimate are correct.
2. I found no precise published theorem proving the every-\(n\) large-prime
   radical estimate for Apéry coefficients.
3. The known Apéry, diagonal, modular, Dwork, holonomic, and \(G\)-function
   theorems are vertical or average-density results and do not provide the
   required cross-prime compression.
4. The sharpest unconditional deterministic coupling is

   \[
   \prod_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}p\mid b_n,
   \]

   together with the Apéry-specific removal of an \(O(n^{2/3})\)-weight
   quotient-exceptional factor.  Its remaining carrier still has linear
   logarithmic height.
5. The earliest missing ingredient is a genuinely horizontal Apéry theorem:
   a sublinear-height common carrier, or an equivalent uniform dyadic saving,
   for the varying conditions \(p\mid b_{n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor p}\).

So the proposed reformulation is valuable—it isolates a clean pointwise
large-prime-radical statement—but it is not currently closed by known
\(G\)-function or modular technology.
