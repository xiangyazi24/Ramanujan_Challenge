# 1. Exact star-mate lemma

Fix a lower prime `p` and write `barGamma_p` for the simple reflected-zero support graph. Its vertices are actual lower `p`-zero occurrences. Hence
\[
V(\bar\Gamma_p)\subseteq Z_p,
\qquad
|Z_p|=Z(p).
\]

Let `P` be the fixed edge-disjoint simple-C4 packing. For a packed cycle `C`, let
\[
e(C)
\]
be its canonical selected support edge and `r(C)` the canonical retained physical row above it. Restrict to those cycles whose selected row survives the two width deletions.

Suppose
\[
s(r(C))=(p,z,\varepsilon).
\]
Then `e(C)` is incident with the support vertex `z`. Since `C` is a simple C4, there is exactly one **other** support edge of `C` incident with `z`; denote it
\[
m_z(C).
\]
Call this the star mate of `e(C)` in `C`.

## Lemma 1

For distinct surviving selected cycles `C,D` anchored at the same unsigned lower vertex `(p,z)`, the four support edges
\[
e(C),\ m_z(C),\ e(D),\ m_z(D)
\]
are all distinct.

### Proof

Inside one simple C4 the two edges incident with a vertex are distinct. Between two different cycles in the packing, every support edge is distinct because the packing is edge-disjoint. That proves all four pairwise distinctions. `m_z(C)` also cannot be the selected edge of some third packed cycle, because it already belongs to `C`. QED.

Let
\[
k_{p,z,\varepsilon}
:=\#\{C:s(r(C))=(p,z,\varepsilon)\},
\qquad
k_{p,z}:=\sum_\varepsilon k_{p,z,\varepsilon}.
\]
The deterministic sign assignment is binary, but it is important that we **sum the two signs before using the mate edges**: all cycles, independent of the sign of their selected row, consume disjoint incident support edges at `z`.

Lemma 1 therefore gives `2 k_{p,z}` distinct support edges incident with `z`, whence
\[
2k_{p,z}
\le d_{\bar\Gamma_p}(z).
\tag{1.1}
\]
Because `barGamma_p` is simple and loopless on lower zero positions,
\[
d_{\bar\Gamma_p}(z)\le Z(p)-1.
\tag{1.2}
\]
Combining (1.1) and (1.2) proves (V1):
\[
\boxed{
k_{p,z}\le\left\lfloor\frac{Z(p)-1}{2}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

This theorem does not even require maximality of the packing; edge-disjointness is enough. Maximum/lexicographic choice does not strengthen (1.3) in general.
