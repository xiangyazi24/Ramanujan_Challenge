# Pointwise sieve audit for Apéry hits

## Conclusion

No unconditional proof of \(B(n)=o(n/\log n)\) is currently obtained from the stated facts. The remaining gap is a genuine pointwise correlation problem for the Apéry zero sets. The available algebraic information gives strong average control, but no known theorem gives the required fixed-\(n\) bound.

## 1. Pointwise bounds for \(B(n)\)

A bound \(B(n)=O(1)\) is not presently provable from \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\). The obstruction is that the conditions

\[
n\bmod p\in Z_p
\]

are indexed by varying primes and are not independent congruence conditions.

Even if every set \(Z_p\) had size 1, an adversarial family of residue sets could force many primes to hit the same integer. Therefore a pointwise estimate requires additional structure beyond cardinality bounds.

No standard sieve gives \(B(n)=O(n^{1-\epsilon})\) in this setting without a new correlation input.

## 2. Does the Cartier diagonal force \(Z(p)=O(1)\)?

Not known. The diagonal interpretation implies that \(B_d\bmod p\) is controlled by a geometric object, and Deligne theory gives bounds on trace sums. It does not imply that the zero locus of the sequence has bounded cardinality.

The known estimate

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}
\]

comes from this geometry, but upgrading it to \(O(1)\) would require a much stronger statement about exact vanishing of a Frobenius trace.

Even assuming the conjectural strengthening

\[
|Z_p|=O(1),
\]

one still needs a pointwise argument. It would make the average easier, but the fixed-\(n\) problem remains a simultaneous moving-modulus problem.

## 3. Existing sieve tools

Large sieve: no. It gives bounds after averaging over \(n\). It cannot control one specified integer.

Selberg sieve: no. Selberg requires a sequence with controllable local densities and aggregate distribution. Here the sequence is a single point \(n\), with moduli \(p\asymp n\).

Montgomery--Vaughan type inequalities: no. They control exponential sums over ranges, not a single CRT-selected integer.

The missing input is a pointwise correlation estimate of the form:

\[
\#\{p_1,\dots,p_k:n\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i}\}
\]

for a fixed \(n\), not an average over \(n\).

## 4. Does Deligne/Weil give extra equidistribution?

Only partially. The trace interpretation gives cancellation for sums such as

\[
\sum_{d\in\mathbf F_p}\psi(d)B_d,
\]

or other bounded-complexity trace sums.

The desired quantity uses the exact indicator

\[
1_{B_{n\bmod p}=0}.
\]

Turning this into a Fourier delta detector introduces conductor comparable to \(p\). Therefore bounded-conductor sheaf estimates do not apply directly.

The missing theorem is an exact-zero incidence theorem for the Apéry trace function with moving evaluation point.

## 5. Direct \(n^{5/6}\) fallback

No. The bound

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}
\]

controls the number of possible residues for each prime, but \(B(n)\) asks for primes whose residue matches one specific \(n\). There is no summation over residues available.

A counting argument over all \(n\le N\) gives the known density-one result, but reversing it to a uniform bound loses the needed information.

## Exact remaining gap

The problem reduces to the following new statement:

**Apéry moving-modulus zero-correlation conjecture.** For the Apéry sequence,

\[
\max_n \#\{p\in(n/2,n]:B_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p\}=o(n/\log n).
\]

This is not implied by current trace bounds, large sieve, or the known \(p^{2/3}\) zero-set estimate.

The computational observations \(Z(p)\le8\) and \(B(n)\le3\) for \(n\le10000\) strongly suggest a much stronger hidden theorem, but they do not provide a proof.

Therefore the unconditional terminal condition is not reached: the remaining gap is a new pointwise Apéry zero-set correlation theorem, rather than a missing application of a standard sieve.
