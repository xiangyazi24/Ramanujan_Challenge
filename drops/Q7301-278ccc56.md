# Simultaneous Apéry congruences: determinant-method and larger-sieve feasibility audit

## Verdict

There is one unconditional power saving over the raw \(k\)-tuple CRT bound, but it is exactly the already available lift of the second moment:

\[
\boxed{
F_k(X)\ll_k \frac{X^{k+4/3}}{(\log X)^k}
}
\qquad (k\ge 3),
\]

and in particular

\[
\boxed{F_3(X)\ll \frac{X^{13/3}}{(\log X)^3}.}
\]

For \(k=3\), this saves \(X^{2/3}\) over the universal tuple bound \(X^5/(\log X)^3\), but it still misses the independence scale \(X^4/(\log X)^3\) by \(X^{1/3}\). For general fixed \(k\), it misses \(X^2\lambda_X^k\) by as much as \(X^{(k-2)/3}\) under the proved bound \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\). In particular,

\[
F_7(X)\ll \frac{X^{25/3}}{(\log X)^7},
\qquad
X^2\lambda_X^7\ll \frac{X^{20/3}}{(\log X)^7},
\]

so the present gap at \(k=7\) is \(X^{5/3}\).

The determinant/larger-sieve verdict is negative for the presently available Apéry algebraizations:

1. **For fixed primes**, the exact algebraic model is a union of
   \(\prod_i Z(p_i)\) rational CRT lines. Its dimension is only \(1\), but its degree and number of geometric components are exactly the universal residue-tuple count. Each component contributes \(0\) or \(1\) relevant integer point because \(\prod_i p_i>X^2\). A determinant method gives the \(O(1)\)-per-component error, not the desired average \(X^2/\prod_i p_i\).
2. **If the primes are variables**, writing a congruence as
   \(B(r)=p\,y\) does not impose an algebraic codimension: \(y=B(r)/p\) over \(\mathbb Q\). The variety remains high-dimensional, and all scarcity is hidden in integrality of the quotient.
3. **The natural exact certificates have an unavoidable complexity tradeoff.** The binomial sum or recurrence gives bounded-degree equations but \(\Theta(p)\) state/selector/quotient variables. Eliminating them gives degree growing with \(p\). The projective orbit has reduced degree \(\Theta(p)\) in the project audit. The gap-continuant model has \(\deg N_h=3(h-1)\); optimizing the short-gap disjunction gives total degree \(H^2+p/H\), minimized at \(H\asymp p^{1/3}\), exactly \(p^{2/3}\). The Hasse--Franel model turns \(b_r=0\) into a coefficient-convolution cancellation involving \(\Theta(p)\) coefficients, not the vanishing of a bounded-degree function evaluated at \(r\).
4. **Gallagher's larger sieve is formally vacuous in the hard regime.** For a fixed \(k\)-tuple its denominator is positive only when
   \(\sum_i 1/Z(p_i)>2+o(1)\). If \(Z(p_i)\asymp X^{2/3}\), one needs \(k\gg X^{2/3}\), not one fixed \(k>6\).
5. **The common integer \(n\) does provide a genuine Lucas coupling**:
   every hit at \(p\) implies \(p\mid b_n\). But \(\log b_n=\Theta(n)\), whereas a fixed product of dyadic primes has logarithm \(O_k(\log X)\). Even the product of all primes in \((X,2X]\) has logarithm only \(O(X)\), still far below the \(O(X^2)\) height available when \(n<X^2\). This divisibility has no useful archimedean contradiction.

Thus the missing factor is not presently a determinant-method problem. It is a **short-prefix CRT discrepancy problem**: the unique CRT representatives attached to Apéry zero tuples must be shown to land in \([0,X^2)\) with frequency \(X^2/\prod p_i\) on average. None of the recurrence, continuant, orbit, or Hasse encodings supplies that distribution. A successful future route would need a genuinely new mixed-prime Fourier/trace estimate or another bounded-complexity cross-characteristic relation; merely repackaging the known one-prime structures does not do it.

## 1. Normalization and the three relevant scales

Put

\[
N=X^2,
\qquad
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},
\qquad
z_p=|Z_p|,
\]

\[
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{z_p}{p},
\qquad
K(n)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
  1_{\{n\bmod p\in Z_p\}}.
\]

I use the ordered convention

\[
F_k(X)=\sum_{0\le n<N}(K(n))_k.
\]

For distinct ordered primes \(\mathbf p=(p_1,\ldots,p_k)\), define

\[
A(\mathbf p)=
\#\{0\le n<N:n\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i}\text{ for every }i\}.
\]

Then

\[
F_k(X)=
\sum_{\substack{p_1,\ldots,p_k\in\mathcal P_X\\
                 p_i\text{ pairwise distinct}}}
A(p_1,\ldots,p_k).
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(Q=\prod_i p_i\). Since \(Q>X^k\ge N\) for \(k\ge2\), every residue vector in
\(Z_{p_1}\times\cdots\times Z_{p_k}\) has at most one representative below \(N\). Hence

\[
A(\mathbf p)\le\prod_{i=1}^k z_{p_i}.
\tag{2}
\]

This gives the universal tuple bound

\[
F_k(X)\le
\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}z_p\right)^k
\le (2X\lambda_X)^k.
\tag{3}
\]

Under \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\), prime counting gives

\[
\lambda_X\ll \frac{X^{2/3}}{\log X},
\tag{4}
\]

so (3) is

\[
F_k(X)\ll_k\frac{X^{5k/3}}{(\log X)^k}.
\tag{5}
\]

The independent/full-period prediction is

\[
F_k(X)\asymp N\lambda_X^k,
\tag{6}
\]

whose largest permitted scale under (4) is

\[
N\lambda_X^k
\ll_k\frac{X^{2+2k/3}}{(\log X)^k}.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the raw CRT bound misses a factor \(X^{k-2}\). Equivalently, for one fixed prime tuple it misses the density

\[
\frac{N}{Q}\asymp X^{2-k}.
\]

The question is whether the special nature of \(Z_p\) recovers this factor on average.

## 2. A concrete nontrivial bound for \(F_3\) and every higher fixed moment

The following is the sharp deterministic envelope already compatible with
`problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex`.

### Proposition 2.1

Let

\[
L_X=\#\{p\in\mathcal P_X:z_p>0\}.
\]

For every fixed \(k\ge3\),

\[
F_k(X)\le (L_X-2)_{k-2}F_2(X),
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
F_2(X)\le
\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}z_p\right)^2
\le4X^2\lambda_X^2.
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently

\[
F_k(X)\ll_k
X^k\lambda_X^2
\min\{\lambda_X,1/\log X\}^{k-2},
\tag{10}
\]

and, using \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\),

\[
\boxed{
F_k(X)\ll_k \frac{X^{k+4/3}}{(\log X)^k}.}
\tag{11}
\]

### Proof

For distinct \(p,q\in\mathcal P_X\), \(pq>N\). A fixed pair
\((a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q\) therefore has at most one simultaneous representative in \([0,N)\). Hence

\[
|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|\le z_pz_q,
\]

and summing over ordered \(p\ne q\) gives the first inequality in (9). Since \(p\le2X\),

\[
\sum_pz_p\le2X\sum_p\frac{z_p}{p}=2X\lambda_X,
\]

which proves the second.

Pointwise,

\[
(K(n))_k=(K(n))_2(K(n)-2)_{k-2}
\le (K(n))_2(L_X-2)_{k-2}.
\]

Summing proves (8). Every active prime contributes at least one zero, so

\[
L_X\le\sum_pz_p\le2X\lambda_X,
\]

while \(L_X\ll X/\log X\). Substitution into (8)--(9) gives (10). Finally (4) gives (11). \(\square\)

For \(k=3\), (11) is

\[
F_3(X)\ll \frac{X^{13/3}}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{12}
\]

The comparison is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{bound for }F_3&\text{scale under }z_p\ll p^{2/3}\\ \hline
\text{raw residue tuples}&X^5/(\log X)^3\\
\text{Proposition 2.1}&X^{13/3}/(\log X)^3\\
\text{independence target}&X^4/(\log X)^3.
\end{array}
\]

So there is a genuine \(X^{2/3}\) power saving over the universal tuple bound, but no determinant method is involved, and the final \(X^{1/3}\) for \(F_3\) remains missing. In general (11) saves

\[
X^{2(k-2)/3}
\]

relative to (5), but still misses (7) by

\[
X^{(k-2)/3}.
\tag{13}
\]

## 3. The exact CRT-offset problem

For a fixed tuple \(\mathbf p\), let

\[
R_{\mathbf p}(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\in[0,Q)
\]

be the least nonnegative CRT representative of

\[
R_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf a)\equiv a_i\pmod {p_i}.
\]

Then there is an exact identity

\[
\boxed{
A(\mathbf p)=
\sum_{\mathbf a\in Z_{p_1}\times\cdots\times Z_{p_k}}
1_{\{R_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf a)<N\}}.}
\tag{14}
\]

The desired estimate is therefore

\[
A(\mathbf p)\approx
\frac{N}{Q}\prod_i z_{p_i}
\tag{15}
\]

on average over prime tuples. Nothing about the number of roots alone controls the positions of the CRT representatives.

There is also an exact Fourier form. Let

\[
Q_i=Q/p_i,
\qquad
\bar Q_iQ_i\equiv1\pmod{p_i},
\]

and write

\[
E_{p_i}(h)=
\sum_{a\in Z_{p_i}}e_{p_i}(h\bar Q_i a).
\]

If

\[
\widehat I_Q(h)=\sum_{0\le n<N}e_Q(-hn),
\]

then finite Fourier inversion on \(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z\) gives

\[
\boxed{
A(\mathbf p)=
\frac1Q\sum_{h\bmod Q}
\widehat I_Q(h)
\prod_{i=1}^kE_{p_i}(h).}
\tag{16}
\]

The \(h=0\) term is exactly

\[
\frac{N}{Q}\prod_i z_{p_i}.
\]

Thus recovering the missing probability means controlling the nonzero-frequency part of (16), averaged over distinct primes. Parseval gives only the individual identity

\[
\sum_{h\bmod p}
\left|\sum_{a\in Z_p}e_p(ha)\right|^2=pz_p.
\tag{17}
\]

That is second-moment information and is compatible with Proposition 2.1. It gives no mixed-prime cancellation in the product in (16). This is the exact analytic obstruction behind the determinant audit below.

## 4. The canonical algebraic variety: dimension \(1\), but \(\prod z_p\) components

The cleanest algebraic normal form already shows what a determinant method sees.

For fixed distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), define the exact selector

\[
S_p(T)=\prod_{a\in Z_p}(T-a)\in\mathbb Z[T].
\tag{18}
\]

Consider the affine variety with coordinates

\[
(n,q_1,r_1,\ldots,q_k,r_k)
\]

and equations

```text
n - p_i q_i - r_i = 0,
S_{p_i}(r_i) = 0,                 i = 1,...,k.
```

Call it \(V_{\mathbf p}\).

### Proposition 4.1: CRT-line decomposition

Over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q\),

\[
V_{\mathbf p}
=
\bigcup_{\mathbf a\in Z_{p_1}\times\cdots\times Z_{p_k}}
L_{\mathbf a},
\tag{19}
\]

where

\[
L_{\mathbf a}:
\quad
r_i=a_i,
\qquad
q_i=\frac{n-a_i}{p_i}
\]

is an affine line. Consequently,

\[
\dim V_{\mathbf p}=1,
\qquad
\#\{\text{geometric components}\}
=\deg V_{\mathbf p}
=\prod_i z_{p_i}.
\tag{20}
\]

The integer points on \(L_{\mathbf a}\) have

\[
n=R_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf a)+tQ,
\qquad t\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{21}
\]

Since \(Q>N\), the line contributes at most one point with \(0\le n<N\), and it contributes one exactly when

\[
R_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf a)<N.
\]

### Proof

Each \(S_{p_i}\) is squarefree and splits as the product of the distinct linear factors \(T-a_i\). Choosing one factor in every block fixes all \(r_i\), after which the \(k\) linear CRT equations express every \(q_i\) in terms of the free parameter \(n\). This gives (19)--(20). Integrality of all \(q_i\) is equivalent to the simultaneous congruences \(n\equiv a_i\pmod{p_i}\), whose solutions are (21). \(\square\)

The selector height is already nonuniform:

\[
H(S_p)\le (2p)^{z_p},
\qquad
\log H(S_p)\ll z_p\log p
\ll X^{2/3}\log X.
\tag{22}
\]

More importantly, (20) is not a bad choice of coordinates. It is the arithmetic itself. The lattice-point count on one line is

\[
\frac{N}{Q}+O(1).
\tag{23}
\]

Summing (23) over all components gives

\[
A(\mathbf p)
\le
\frac{N}{Q}\prod_i z_{p_i}
+O\!\left(\prod_i z_{p_i}\right).
\tag{24}
\]

Because \(N/Q<1\), the error term is exactly the universal tuple bound and dominates the desired main term. A global determinant method can see the dimension \(1\); it cannot remove an \(O(1)\) error from each of \(\prod z_{p_i}\) components. Removing it is equivalent to proving that the intercepts \(R_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf a)\) are equidistributed in the short prefix \([0,N)\).

This gives a precise obstruction:

> **The missing \(N/Q\) is a distribution factor among components, not a codimension factor inside a component.**

There is also no standard \(p\)-adic determinant gain. For a fixed residue vector modulo all \(p_i\), the fiber in \([0,N)\) contains at most one point. The usual determinant method gains \(p\)-adic valuation from several points coalescing in one residue fiber; here there is no nontrivial combined fiber on which to build such a determinant. Across varying prime tuples, the rows do not share one modulus, so their congruences do not create a common determinant valuation.

## 5. Why adding congruence quotients does not create algebraic codimension

Suppose one replaces the selector \(S_p(r)=0\) by an equality

\[
B(r)=p\,y,
\tag{25}
\]

where \(B(r)\) is any exact representation of \(b_r\). If \(p\) and \(r\) are variables, the affine hypersurface (25) is birational over \(\mathbb Q\) to the unrestricted \((p,r)\)-plane: for \(p\ne0\), simply put

\[
y=B(r)/p.
\]

Thus the condition \(p\mid B(r)\) is not an algebraic dimension drop over characteristic zero. Its content is that the rational value \(B(r)/p\) is an integer in a prescribed box.

For \(k\) hits, the natural system is

```text
n - p_i q_i - r_i = 0,
B(r_i) - p_i y_i = 0,             i = 1,...,k.
```

It has \(1+4k\) displayed variables and \(2k\) equations, hence expected dimension \(1+2k\). Primality only contributes logarithmic sieve weights; it is not an algebraic equation. There is no geometric codimension corresponding to the probability \(1/p_i\).

This is the fixed-prime/variable-prime dichotomy:

* fixing the primes turns integrality into the union of \(\prod z_p\) CRT lines;
* varying the primes keeps the equations low-degree, but the variety is high-dimensional and the divisibility lives entirely in quotient integrality.

The natural Apéry certificates merely choose how to represent \(B(r)\); they do not change this dichotomy.

## 6. Audit of the four natural Apéry certificates

The only variables shared by different primes are \(n\) and the linear relations

\[
r_i=n-p_iq_i.
\tag{26}
\]

All recurrence, summation, collision, or Hasse witnesses occur in disjoint prime-indexed blocks. Consequently, unless one discovers a new equation relating two different blocks, their degrees/components multiply.

### 6.1 Binomial-summation certificate

Write

\[
T_j(r)=\binom rj^2\binom{r+j}j^2.
\]

For \(0\le j<r<p\), the exact hypergeometric ratio is

\[
(j+1)^4T_{j+1}
=(r-j)^2(r+j+1)^2T_j.
\tag{27}
\]

With partial sums \(C_0=T_0=1\) and

\[
C_{j+1}=C_j+T_{j+1},
\]

one has \(C_r=b_r\). Modulo \(p\), an integer certificate can use representatives \(0\le T_j,C_j<p\) and quotient variables \(u_j,v_j\):

\[
(j+1)^4T_{j+1}-(r-j)^2(r+j+1)^2T_j=p\,u_j,
\tag{28}
\]

\[
C_{j+1}-C_j-T_{j+1}=p\,v_j,
\qquad C_r=0.
\tag{29}
\]

For a fixed \(r\), this uses \(\Theta(r)\) witnesses and equations. To let \(r\) vary, one must either

* pad to length \(p\) and add \(\Theta(p)\) active/one-hot selectors, or
* eliminate the trace and replace it by a polynomial lookup in \(r\).

The first option has bounded equation degree (degree \(5\) as written, or quadratic after introducing multiplication gates), polynomial-size coordinates, but \(\Theta(p)\) variables and constraints per prime. The second option has few variables but degree growing with \(p\), and its zero selector has degree at least \(z_p\).

No variable in (28)--(29) is shared between two different primes. Substituting (26) only changes the input \(r_i\); it does not couple the traces.

### 6.2 Direct Apéry-recurrence certificate

The project recurrence is

\[
(j+1)^3b_{j+1}=P(j)b_j-j^3b_{j-1},
\qquad
P(j)=34j^3+51j^2+27j+5.
\tag{30}
\]

For fixed \(p\), let \(x_j\in[0,p)\) be the residue of \(b_j\). Then

\[
(j+1)^3x_{j+1}-P(j)x_j+j^3x_{j-1}=p\,u_j,
\tag{31}
\]

with \(x_0=1,x_1=5\), gives a triangular trace up to \(j=p-1\). To select the variable index \(r\), introduce one-hot variables \(\delta_j\):

\[
\delta_j^2-\delta_j=0,
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\delta_j=1,
\qquad
r=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}j\delta_j,
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\delta_jx_j=0.
\tag{32}
\]

Equations (31)--(32) have degree at most \(2\) if \(p\) is fixed, and at most \(2\) with \(p\) variable as well because only \(p\,u_j\) is bilinear. The state and quotient coordinates have polynomial height in \(X\). But there are \(\Theta(p)=\Theta(X)\) state, quotient, and selector variables for every prime block.

Eliminating those variables returns a lookup relation in \(r\). At minimum, a single nonzero polynomial whose zero set contains all of \(Z_p\) has degree at least \(z_p\). In the projective formulation the obstruction is stronger: the audit in `drops/Q7258-44c112fd.md` identifies the reduced orbit map

\[
r\longmapsto[b_r:c_r]
\]

as having generic degree \(\Theta(p)\), even after the Frobenius determinant carrier is removed. Thus the recurrence circuit is low-degree only because its length grows linearly with \(p\).

### 6.3 Projective collisions and gap continuants

The exact project notation in
`problems/3.2/FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex` is

\[
N_h(r)=U_{h-1}(r+1),
\qquad
\deg N_h=3(h-1).
\tag{33}
\]

If \(r\) and \(r+h\) are two nonwrapping zeros, then

\[
N_h(r)=0.
\tag{34}
\]

This can certify a hit only after adding a second zero and a gap witness. Such a witness need not exist when \(z_p=1\), and for \(z_p>1\) its length is not uniformly bounded.

The exact restart inequality is, up to harmless endpoint conventions,

\[
z_p
\le
1+\sum_{m=1}^{H}
  \#\{s:U_m(s)=0\}
+rac{p}{H+1}.
\tag{35}
\]

Since \(\deg U_m=3m\),

\[
z_p\ll H^2+rac pH.
\tag{36}
\]

The optimum \(H\asymp p^{1/3}\) gives \(p^{2/3}\). This same calculation is a determinant-method obstruction, not merely a root-count proof.

Indeed, the short-gap certificate is a disjoint union over \(m\le H\) of the hypersurfaces

\[
U_m(s)=0,
\qquad \deg_sU_m=3m.
\]

Its total degree budget is

\[
\sum_{m\le H}3m\asymp H^2.
\tag{37}
\]

For \(k\) independent prime blocks, summing the complete-intersection degree over all \((m_1,\ldots,m_k)\) gives

\[
\sum_{m_1,\ldots,m_k\le H}
\prod_{i=1}^k 3m_i
=
\left(\sum_{m\le H}3m\right)^k
\asymp H^{2k}.
\tag{38}
\]

At \(H\asymp X^{1/3}\), this is

\[
X^{2k/3},
\]

exactly the product of the one-prime \(X^{2/3}\) budgets. The long-gap term \(p/H\) is of the same size. The common \(n\) turns each certified residue vector into one CRT line, but it creates no equation relating \(m_i\) to \(m_j\).

Encoding \(m\) as another algebraic variable does not avoid (37). The family \(m\mapsto U_m(s)\) is a nonautonomous recurrence in the discrete length \(m\), not one bounded-degree bivariate polynomial. One must either use \(H\) selectors/recurrence states or interpolate in \(m\), which introduces degree \(H\); together with the \(s\)-degree \(3H\), the same \(H^2\) complexity returns.

Thus the gap-continuant route is quantitatively closed at the existing \(p^{2/3}\) exponent unless one proves a genuinely joint saving for

\[
\#\{(s,m):m\le H,\ U_m(s)=0\}
\]

beyond the sum of the column degrees. That is precisely the horizontal incidence theorem that remains open in the project; the determinant method does not supply it automatically.

### 6.4 Hasse--Franel coefficient certificate

The project Hasse polynomial is

\[
H_p(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jT^j.
\tag{39}
\]

In the Sym\(^2\)/Franel realization audited in
`drops/Q7259-3ce2efee.md`, after the discriminant factor is separated one has

\[
H_p(T)=\Delta(T)^\varepsilon B_p(T)^2,
\qquad
B_p(T)=\sum_{u=0}^{d_p}\beta_uT^u,
\tag{40}
\]

with \(d_p\) growing linearly with \(p\). If

\[
\Delta(T)^\varepsilon=\sum_ed_eT^e,
\]

then

\[
b_r=
\sum_{u+v+e=r}d_e\beta_u\beta_v.
\tag{41}
\]

Equation (41) is quadratic, but it is a coefficient convolution involving \(\Theta(p)\) coefficients. Crucially,

\[
b_r=0
\]

is **not** the statement that a bounded-degree polynomial in a parameter has a root at \(T=r\). It is the statement that the \(r\)-th coefficient of a degree-\(\Theta(p)\) polynomial vanishes.

There are only two exact algebraic implementations:

* hard-code the \(eta_u\), then use \(\Theta(p)\) selectors to extract coefficient \(r\); or
* expose the coefficient vector, adding \(\Theta(p)\) variables and the convolution equations.

Elimination again gives a lookup polynomial in \(r\) of growing degree. The square-root factorization lowers the rank of the geometric Frobenius object, but it does not lower the number of coefficient coordinates or impose a support restriction on the coefficient-zero set. This is exactly the obstruction recorded in the project audit.

### 6.5 Summary table

| certificate | equation degree before elimination | witnesses per prime | eliminated degree/complexity | cross-prime link beyond \(n=pq+r\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| binomial sum | at most \(5\), or quadratic circuit | \(\Theta(p)\) | grows with \(p\) | none |
| Apéry recurrence | \(2\) with quotient/selectors | \(\Theta(p)\) | at least \(z_p\), orbit degree generically \(\Theta(p)\) | none |
| gap continuants | \(3(h-1)\) in \(r\) | gap or \(O(H)\) trace | total \(H^2+p/H\), optimum \(p^{2/3}\) | none |
| Hasse--Franel | quadratic convolution | \(\Theta(p)\) coefficients/selectors | coefficient lookup of degree growing with \(p\) | none |
| exact selector \(S_p\) | \(z_p\) | none | \(\prod z_p\) CRT-line components | none |

The tradeoff is therefore precise: **bounded degree requires linearly many witnesses; boundedly many variables requires degree/number of components growing at least at the known zero-set scale.**

## 7. Gallagher's larger sieve: exact denominator obstruction

Let \(A\subset[0,N)\) occupy at most \(
u(p)\) residue classes modulo each prime in a finite set \(\mathcal S\). Gallagher's larger sieve gives

\[
|A|\le
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal S}\log p-\log N}
     {\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal S}\frac{\log p}{\nu(p)}-\log N},
\tag{42}
\]

provided the denominator is positive.

Apply this to

\[
A=A(p_1,\ldots,p_k)
=\{n<N:n\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i}\},
\]

with \(\mathcal S=\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}\) and \(
u(p_i)=z_{p_i}\). Since all \(p_i\asymp X\) and \(\log N=2\log X\), positivity requires

\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\frac1{z_{p_i}}>2+o(1).
\tag{43}
\]

This is the exact obstruction. In the hard regime

\[
z_{p_i}\asymp X^{2/3},
\]

one has

\[
\sum_i\frac1{z_{p_i}}\asymp kX^{-2/3}=o(1)
\]

for fixed \(k\), so (42) is vacuous. To force positivity uniformly at the permitted scale would require

\[
k\gg X^{2/3}.
\tag{44}
\]

Even \(k=7\) or \(8\) is nowhere near this threshold.

When several \(z_{p_i}\) are \(1,2,\) or \(3\), the denominator can be positive, but then the raw product \(\prod z_{p_i}\) is already tiny. More fundamentally, since \(N/Q<1\), no uniform per-tuple integer bound can equal the fractional expectation \(N\prod z_i/Q\): a nonempty tuple contributes at least one. The missing factor must come from proving that most tuples are empty, and (42) does not count empty prime tuples.

One might try to apply the larger sieve to a set of rows sharing many primes. At the \(z_p\asymp X^{2/3}\) scale, however, it needs roughly \(X^{2/3}\) **common** moduli before its denominator becomes positive. A fixed factorial moment counts \(k\)-stars in the row--prime incidence graph; it does not force a biclique with \(X^{2/3}\) common columns. Anchored/rainbow-star configurations in the project show exactly why such an extraction is not a deterministic consequence of a high fixed moment.

Therefore Gallagher's larger sieve contributes no new fixed-\(k\) power saving here.

## 8. Polynomial large sieve and determinant variants

### 8.1 What the classical large sieve can see

The Fourier expansion (16) shows that the independent main term is easy; all difficulty is in mixed products of the transforms of \(Z_p\). The classical large sieve at length \(N=X^2\) and moduli \(p\asymp X\) is exactly at its natural \(Q^2\asymp N\) threshold. Combined with (17), it controls quadratic averages and is consistent with the proved \(F_2\) bound.

Repeated Cauchy--Schwarz without new arithmetic information replaces higher mixed products by a maximum column/row multiplicity and returns the lift

\[
F_k\le (L_X-2)_{k-2}F_2.
\]

It does not generate the missing \(X^{-(k-2)/3}\).

Primewise Fourier decay would not be enough in any event. The obstacle is a mixed phase across distinct primes and the short interval \([0,X^2)\), whose length is a fraction \(X^{2-k}\) of the full CRT period. The project already records synthetic columns with individually tiny Fourier sums but a common anchor row. Thus a useful polynomial large sieve would need a theorem about the joint product in (16), not separate estimates for each \(Z_p\).

### 8.2 Why the known polynomial families do not meet fixed-degree hypotheses

A polynomial-large-sieve or trace-function argument needs a family of bounded degree/conductor as \(p\) varies. The present candidates have:

* selector degree \(z_p\), potentially \(p^{2/3}\);
* gap-polynomial degree \(3(h-1)\), with a necessary union over \(h\le p^{1/3}\) and total degree mass \(p^{2/3}\);
* projective orbit degree \(\Theta(p)\);
* Hasse/Franel coefficient length \(\Theta(p)\).

Applying a theorem whose constants depend on degree while suppressing this \(p\)-dependence would be invalid. More structurally, the selector and short-gap varieties split into as many components as their root budgets, so an irreducible-curve determinant estimate has nothing to act on.

### 8.3 Row-dependent primes do not create a determinant valuation

A standard \(p\)-adic determinant argument gains a high valuation because many points reduce to one low-dimensional configuration modulo the **same** prime. Here:

* for fixed \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), a combined residue fiber contains at most one \(n<N\);
* across different prime tuples, the relevant primes vary from row to row;
* factoring one row-specific vanishing quantity \(b_{r_j}\), divisible by \(p_j\), gives at most one obvious \(p_j\)-factor per row and simultaneously introduces an entry of exponential height.

There is therefore no valuation surplus over the archimedean determinant. To obtain one, one would first need a new relation making many simultaneous-hit points coalesce modulo a common auxiliary prime or on a bounded-degree common reduction. None of the four Apéry certificates does this.

## 9. A precise no-go lemma for a uniform auxiliary polynomial

The most direct auxiliary-polynomial idea can be ruled out sharply.

### Lemma 9.1

Let

\[
A_X(P,R)\in\mathbb Z[P,R],
\qquad
\deg_R A_X\le D.
\]

Suppose that for every \(p\) in a set \(\mathcal S\subset\mathcal P_X\),

\[
A_X(p,r)\equiv0\pmod p
\qquad
\text{for every }r\in Z_p.
\tag{45}
\]

If \(z_p>D\), then every coefficient of \(A_X(0,R)\) is divisible by \(p\). Hence, if

\[
\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal S\\z_p>D}}p
>
H(A_X(0,R)),
\tag{46}
\]

where \(H\) is the maximum absolute coefficient, then

\[
A_X(0,R)=0,
\]

so \(P\mid A_X(P,R)\). In that case

\[
A_X(p,R)\equiv0\pmod p
\]

for every residue \(R\), and the auxiliary congruence is tautological.

### Proof

Modulo \(p\), evaluation at \(P=p\) kills every positive power of \(P\), so

\[
A_X(p,R)\equiv A_X(0,R)\pmod p.
\]

If \(z_p>D\), the degree-\(\le D\) polynomial \(A_X(0,R)\bmod p\) has more roots than its degree and is therefore the zero polynomial. Thus \(p\) divides every coefficient of \(A_X(0,R)\). The product in (46) then divides every coefficient; the height inequality forces every coefficient to be zero. A polynomial in \(P\) with zero constant term is divisible by \(P\). \(\square\)

This gives the following dichotomy for any uniform vanishing-polynomial route:

* either \(D\ge z_p\) on the hard primes, so the degree is already as large as the zero-set complexity;
* or the \(P=0\) specialization vanishes and the congruence is identically true modulo \(p\).

The same lemma applies to \(A_X(P,n)\), because \(n\bmod p=r\). Allowing a determinant-generated polynomial of enormous height evades (46), but then the archimedean determinant is too large to be forced to zero by a fixed product of dyadic primes. Thus fixed degree and controlled height cannot both be retained.

The lemma is scoped to a uniform polynomial that vanishes on the whole zero relation. An entirely new bounded-complexity existential finite-field description could in principle behave differently; none of the binomial, recurrence, gap, projective, or Hasse witnesses supplies such a description. Their exact eliminations are the high-degree selectors audited above.

## 10. The genuine common-\(n\) relation: Lucas divisibility and its height obstruction

The \(p\)-Lucas congruence is

\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\tag{47}
\]

For \(n<X^2\) and \(p>X\), write \(n=qp+r\). If \(r\in Z_p\), then

\[
p\mid b_n.
\tag{48}
\]

Thus a simultaneous \(k\)-hit gives the genuine cross-prime relation

\[
Q=\prod_{i=1}^kp_i\mid b_n.
\tag{49}
\]

This is the strongest currently available link through the common integer \(n\). It is nevertheless far too weak by height.

The defining sum gives elementary exponential bounds. The \(j=n\) term yields

\[
b_n\ge\binom{2n}{n}^2
\ge \frac{16^n}{(2n+1)^2},
\tag{50}
\]

while

\[
\binom nj\le2^n,
\qquad
\binom{n+j}j\le2^{n+j}\le4^n
\]

imply

\[
b_n\le(n+1)64^n.
\tag{51}
\]

Hence

\[
\log b_n=\Theta(n).
\tag{52}
\]

For fixed \(k\),

\[
\log Q=k\log X+O_k(1),
\]

whereas \(n\) may be of order \(X^2\). There is no size contradiction in (49). In fact the simple consequence

\[
K(n)\log X\le\log b_n=O(n)
\]

is weaker than the trivial bound \(K(n)\ll X/\log X\) when \(n\asymp X^2\).

Trying to convert (49) into an algebraic curve also fails at fixed complexity. There is no nonzero fixed polynomial

\[
F(T,Y)\in\mathbb C[T,Y]
\]

with

\[
F(n,b_n)=0
\]

for all sufficiently large \(n\). Indeed, write

\[
F(T,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^df_j(T)Y^j,
\qquad f_d\ne0.
\]

After division by \(b_n^d\), the lower-\(j\) terms tend to \(0\) exponentially by (50), while the nonzero polynomial \(f_d(n)\) cannot tend to \(0\) along all large integers. This is a contradiction.

Therefore an exact algebraization of the graph \(n\mapsto b_n\) on \(n<X^2\) must have growing degree, a recurrence trace of length \(\Theta(X^2)\), or exponentially large coordinates. The Lucas reformulation is arithmetically genuine but determinant-method hostile.

## 11. Feasibility classification

### What is proved now

1. The concrete nontrivial estimate
   \[
   F_3(X)\ll X^{13/3}/(\log X)^3
   \]
   and, more generally, (11).
2. An exact algebraic model for each fixed prime tuple, with dimension \(1\), degree and component count \(\prod_i z_{p_i}\), and one CRT progression per component.
3. A precise larger-sieve threshold: fixed \(k\) is useless when the \(z_{p_i}\) grow; at the \(X^{2/3}\) scale one needs \(k\gg X^{2/3}\).
4. A precise degree/witness obstruction for each listed Apéry certificate.
5. A uniform auxiliary-polynomial dichotomy, Lemma 9.1.
6. A genuine Lucas common-\(n\) relation whose available height is quantitatively insufficient.

### What is not obtained

No determinant method, polynomial large sieve, larger sieve, or presently available auxiliary polynomial improves (11). In particular, none gives

\[
F_3(X)\ll X^{4+o(1)}
\]

or

\[
F_k(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k
\]

for any fixed \(k>6\).

### The exact missing lemma

A successful route must prove a mixed-prime short-prefix discrepancy estimate of the following kind: for one fixed \(k>6\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{p_1,\ldots,p_k\in\mathcal P_X\\
                 p_i\text{ distinct}}}
\left|
A(\mathbf p)-
\frac{X^2}{p_1\cdots p_k}
\prod_{i=1}^kz_{p_i}
\right|
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k,
\tag{53}
\]

or a one-sided/averaged variant strong enough to imply the same bound for \(F_k\). By (16), this is equivalently a cancellation theorem for the nonzero mixed Fourier modes. Any algebraic approach would have to produce a bounded-complexity cross-characteristic object controlling those modes.

The present recurrence state, projective orbit, gap continuants, and Hasse coefficients are all **one-prime** structures of complexity growing with \(p\). Their fiber product over the common \(n\) remains block-diagonal and reduces to the CRT-line union. Therefore they do not meet (53).

## Final conclusion

The determinant/larger-sieve attack is **not feasible with the current Apéry structures**. The obstruction is not a vague concern about constants: it is the exact combination of

\[
\boxed{
\text{one CRT line per zero tuple}
+
\text{degree/component count }\prod z_p
+
\text{no common residue fiber}
+
\text{fixed-}k\text{ larger-sieve denominator }\le0.
}
\]

The only unconditional power saving is the pair-moment lift (11). To reach the all-index GCD target at fixed \(k>6\), one needs a new arithmetic theorem governing the distribution of the CRT intercepts or the mixed Fourier product in (16), not another algebraic presentation of the existing one-prime recurrence data.
