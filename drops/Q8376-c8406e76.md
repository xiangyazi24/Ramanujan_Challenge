# 1. The cross-ray quotient is exactly one-dimensional

Let

\[
\ell_s:\mathbf Z^r\longrightarrow\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\ell_s(c)=c\cdot s.
\]

Put

\[
g=\gcd(s_1,\ldots,s_r)>0.\]

Choose an integer Bézout row \(a\in\mathbf Z^r\) with

\[
a\cdot s=g.\]

## Theorem 1.1 — integral cross-ray decomposition

Every \(c\in\mathbf Z^r\) has the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
 c=q a+z,
 \qquad
 q=\frac{c\cdot s}{g}\in\mathbf Z,
 \qquad
 z\cdot s=0.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf Z^r/\ker\ell_s\cong g\mathbf Z\cong\mathbf Z.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

### Proof

Every integer linear combination of the \(s_i\) is divisible by \(g\), so \(q\in\mathbf Z\). Set \(z=c-qa\). Then

\[
z\cdot s=c\cdot s-qg=0.\]

The image of \(\ell_s\) is \(g\mathbf Z\) by Bézout, proving (1.2). ∎

## Corollary 1.2 — all nonorthogonal rows are the same quotient class

Let \(\mathcal P_M\) be the full safe candidate primorial. Use the banked zero-ray theorem in the exact form supplied in Q8376:

\[
z\cdot s=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal P_M\mid z\cdot E.\]

Then (1.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
 c\cdot E\equiv q(a\cdot E)\pmod{\mathcal P_M}.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Thus adding a second, third, or dense collection of nonorthogonal rows cannot create another transverse Smith coordinate. After zero-ray saturation, every such row is an integer multiple of one quotient-one anchor.

This observation matters strategically. The remaining task is not “find many nonorthogonal rows.” It is to evaluate **one** quotient class by an independently small actual-Apéry boundary.

# 3. A uniform \(16^M\) bound for the nonorigin upper band

The coefficient formula verified in `q32_cartier_packet_audit.py` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_M(u,v,w)
&:=[x^uy^vz^w]\Lambda^M\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{M}
 \binom Mk
 \binom M{k-u}
 \binom{2M-k}{M-v}
 \binom{2M-k}{M-w},
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

with the zero-binomial convention.

Let

\[
B_M=\binom{2M}{M}\le4^M.
\]

## Lemma 3.1 — one top-band packet coefficient

Let \(d=M-j>M/2\), and let

\[
(x,y,z)\in P\cap\mathbf Z^3\setminus\{0\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
A_M(dx,dy,dz)
\le
(j+1)(2M)^{4j+4}B_M^2.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

There are three cases for \(x\).

### Case \(x=-1\)

The factor

\[
\binom M{k+d}
\]

forces \(0\le k\le j\). Hence there are at most \(j+1\) terms, and

\[
\binom Mk,\ \binom M{k+d}\le M^j.
\]

Every binomial with upper index at most \(2M\) is at most \(B_M\). Thus this case is bounded by

\[
(j+1)M^{2j}B_M^2.
\]

### Case \(x=1\)

Now

\[
\binom M{k-d}
\]

forces \(M-j\le k\le M\). The same endpoint estimate applies.

### Case \(x=0\)

At least one of \(y,z\) is nonzero.

- If, say, \(y=1\), then
  
  \[
  \binom{2M-k}{M-d}=
  \binom{2M-k}{j}\le(2M)^j.
  \]
  
  The remaining long binomial is at most \(B_M\), and
  
  \[
  \sum_k\binom Mk^2=B_M.
  \]

- If \(y=-1\), then
  
  \[
  \binom{2M-k}{M+d}
  \]
  
  forces \(k\le j\), and after complementing its lower index it is at most \((2M)^j\). The first two binomials contribute at most \(M^{2j}\). The same argument applies if the nonzero coordinate is \(z\).

The deliberately generous common upper bound in (3.2) covers every subcase. ∎

## Corollary 3.2 — nonorigin shell bound

There are 21 nonorigin points in the packet. Hence

\[
\boxed{
0\le C_M^{\circ}(M-j)
\le
21(j+1)(2M)^{4j+4}16^M.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

If \(J=o(M/\log M)\), this becomes uniformly for \(j\le J\),

\[
\boxed{
C_M^{\circ}(M-j)
\le \exp\bigl(M\log16+o(M)\bigr).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

No cancellation, recurrence, or prime input is used here. It is a positive coefficient estimate for the literal Apéry Laurent polynomial.

# 4. Upper-band cross-ray dominance

Use the banked Apéry asymptotic

\[
\boxed{
 b_M=\kappa\alpha^M M^{-3/2}
 \left(1+O(M^{-1})\right),
 \quad
 \alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
 \quad \kappa>0.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

In particular \(\alpha>16\).

## Theorem 4.1 — raw upper-band dominance

Let \(J=o(M/\log M)\), and let \(d_0,\ldots,d_J\in\mathbf Z\) satisfy

\[
\log\max_j(1,|d_j|)=o(M).
\]

Put

\[
q=\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_j,
\qquad
R_M(d)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_j C_M(M-j).
\]

If \(q\ne0\), then

\[
\boxed{
R_M(d)=q b_M+
O\!\left(16^M e^{o(M)}\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{R_M(d)}{q b_M}
=1+e^{-\delta M+o(M)},
\quad
\delta=\log(\alpha/16)>0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular \(R_M(d)\ne0\) eventually and

\[
\boxed{
\log|R_M(d)|=M\log\alpha+o(M).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Insert \(C_M(M-j)=b_M+C_M^{\circ}(M-j)\). The origin terms sum to \(qb_M\). By (3.3),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\sum_{j=0}^{J}d_jC_M^{\circ}(M-j)\right|
&\le
21(J+1)^2\max_j|d_j|
(2M)^{4J+4}16^M\\
&=16^M e^{o(M)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(q\) is a nonzero integer, \(|q|\ge1\). Divide by (4.1). ∎

## Theorem 4.2 — derived Turán/Smith row

Let

\[
\mathbf C_M^{\rm top}
=(C_M(M),C_M(M-1),\ldots,C_M(M-J))^T,
\]

and let \(A_M\) be an integral matrix with

\[
\log\|A_M\|_\infty=o(M),
\qquad
\log(\#\text{entries of }A_M)=o(M).
\]

Define

\[
E=A_M\mathbf C_M^{\rm top},
\qquad
E^{\circ}=A_M( C_M^{\circ}(M-j))_{j=0}^{J},
\qquad
s=A_M\mathbf1.
\]

Let \(c_M\) be an integral row of subexponential height and size. If

\[
c_M\cdot s\ne0,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
 c_M\cdot E
 =(c_M\cdot s)b_M
 \left(1+e^{-\delta M+o(M)}\right).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
|c_M\cdot E^{\circ}|
\le16^M e^{o(M)}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

The pulled-back raw row is \(d=c_MA_M\). Its coefficient sum is

\[
d\cdot\mathbf1=c_M\cdot A_M\mathbf1=c_M\cdot s.
\]

All size and height assumptions remain subexponential under the matrix product. Apply Theorem 4.1. ∎

The left side has exponential base at most \(16\), while the first term on the right has base \(\alpha>16\). Hence no such identity exists under the hypotheses of Theorem 4.2.

This covers:

# 6. Fixed-order self-adjoint recurrence rows

The Apéry recurrence operator is

\[
L=(n+1)^3S^2-P(n)S+n^3,
\qquad
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The project has proved that its difference Galois group is \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), equivalently here:

- the determinant is a coboundary;
- there is no rational Riccati solution;
- the two exponential roots are unequal,
  
  \[
  \alpha=17+12\sqrt2,\qquad \beta=\alpha^{-1}.
  \]

## Theorem 6.1 — fixed rational Ore trichotomy

Let \(T\in\mathbf Q(n)[S]\) have fixed order. Right-divide by the monic normalization of \(L\):

\[
T=QL+r_0(n)+r_1(n)S.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then exactly one of the following holds.

1. \(r_0=r_1=0\). Then \(T\) annihilates the full two-dimensional homogeneous solution space. It cannot produce a transverse inhomogeneous numerator.
2. The remainder is nonzero. Then, after clearing its fixed rational denominator,
   
   \[
   \boxed{
   \log|Tb_n|=n\log\alpha+O(\log n)
   }
   \tag{6.3}
   
   for all sufficiently large regular \(n\).

### Proof

In the second case,

\[
Tb_n=b_n\left(r_0(n)+r_1(n)\frac{b_{n+1}}{b_n}\right).
\tag{6.4}
\]

The ratio \(b_{n+1}/b_n\) has a complete formal expansion at infinity with leading term \(\alpha\). If the parenthesis in (6.4) vanished to every asymptotic order, then

\[
\frac{b_{n+1}}{b_n}=-\frac{r_0(n)}{r_1(n)}
\]

would be a rational Riccati solution for the Apéry operator. The proved \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) difference Galois group excludes this. Hence the parenthesis has a first nonzero Laurent term, changing only a power of \(n\). Combining with (4.1) proves (6.3). ∎

This sharpens the banked recurrence-only no-go. A fixed rational self-adjoint operator is either homogeneous or exponentially tall. It cannot generate the required cross-ray cancellation.

## 6.2 Why the exact Green numerator does not contradict the theorem

Let \(u_0=0,u_1=1\) be the canonical second homogeneous solution. Then

\[
\boxed{
 n^3\bigl(b_{n-1}u_n-b_nu_{n-1}\bigr)=1.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The numerator is perfect, but the row coefficients are the two global solutions themselves. Their cleared heights are exponential; Q8319 already records the same obstruction in the normalization with numerator \(6\).

Thus the self-adjoint recurrence supplies a small **value** only by paying linear logarithmic height in the coefficients. It does not satisfy the Turán–Smith budget.

# 7. Modular/Eichler seams are not target-preserving

Let \(v_n\) be any canonical extension coordinate, and consider its first Green seam

\[
W_n(v)=b_nv_{n+1}-b_{n+1}v_n.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let \(p>n+1\) and suppose \(p\mid b_n\). Two consecutive Apéry zeros are impossible, so \(p\nmid b_{n+1}\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
W_n(v)\equiv-b_{n+1}v_n\pmod p.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
p\mid W_n(v)\iff p\mid v_n.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

A modular parametrization or framed extension does not by itself imply the new condition \(p\mid v_n\).

## 7.1 Exact actual-Apéry counterexample

For the canonical Golyshev–Zagier Eisenstein–Eichler coordinate \(\kappa\), Q8318 proves the exact finite-field data

\[
\boxed{
 p=31,\quad n=8,\quad
 b_8=0,\quad
 \kappa_8=13,\quad
 \Xi_8=28
 \quad\text{in }\mathbf F_{31}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus both the \(\kappa\)-seam and its Casoratian coordinate are \(31\)-units at an actual target. This is a counterexample to universal target preservation, not a statistical observation.

For the homogeneous companion \(u\), (6.5) gives even more:

\[
p\mid b_n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\nmid u_n.

So the absolute Green seam is deliberately transverse to every target.

The parameter-derivative/Gessel extension is a third distinct extension. Q8318 proves a nonzero exterior separation from \(\kappa\), but neither zero condition forces the other. Its fixed transfer also retains linear logarithmic height.

## 7.2 Scope

\[
p\mid b_n\Longrightarrow p\mid v_n
\]

for a genuinely different coordinate. Such a theorem would be new arithmetic input; it is not supplied by modularity alone.

# 8. Combined scoped impossibility

## Theorem 8.1 — Turán cross-ray trichotomy

Consider a proposed integer row obtained from any finite composition of the following interfaces:

1. an integral top-cell shell operator supported in
   
   \[
   d=M-J,\ldots,M,
   \qquad J=o(M/\log M),
   \]
   
   with subexponential matrix and row coefficients;
2. a fixed-order rational Apéry recurrence or self-adjoint Ore operator;
3. a fixed modular/Eichler extension used through a first Green/Casoratian seam, without an additional target-vanishing theorem.

Then a row with \(c\cdot s\ne0\) cannot be a nonzero target-preserving object of logarithmic height \(o(M)\). More precisely, it falls into one of these terminal classes:

- **upper-band dominance:** its value is \((c\cdot s)b_M(1+e^{-\delta M+o(M)})\);
- **homogeneous collapse:** it lies in the recurrence/zero-ray module and gives no new forcing;
- **exponential coefficient height:** a small Green numerator is paid for by global Apéry-solution coefficients;
- **target failure:** the modular seam is a unit at an actual Apéry zero.

### Proof

Apply Theorem 4.2 to interface 1, Theorem 6.1 and (6.5) to interface 2, and (7.2)–(7.4) to interface 3. ∎

This theorem is deliberately scoped. It does not assert that no conceivable nonlocal Apéry identity exists. It does prove that the unique cross-ray quotient is not accessible through the absolute endpoint, the fixed self-adjoint recurrence, or the currently canonical modular seams.

# 9. The remaining arithmetic task

A viable row must now satisfy all of the following concrete requirements.

1. **Primitive cross-ray class.** After zero-ray saturation it should represent a generator of
   
   \[
   \mathbf Z^r/\ker\ell_s,

   so \(c\cdot s=\pm g\), not zero.
2. **Genuine nonlocality.** Its raw shell pullback must reach depth at least \(M/\log M\) along an infinite subsequence, or leave the raw top-cell shell module entirely.
3. **Subexponential coefficient clearing.** It may not encode \(b_M\), the companion solution, or the candidate primorial in its coefficients.
4. **Outside the fixed Ore module.** A fixed rational recurrence operator is terminal by Theorem 6.1.
5. **Target-selective first-primary seam.** If a modular extension appears, a proved specialization theorem must force its value to vanish at every relevant Apéry zero.
6. **Independent nonzero evaluation.** The boundary must be evaluated without defining it as the unknown Turán gcd or smooth radical.

The last two conditions are where the arithmetic—not the Smith algebra—must enter. The zero-ray theorem and Theorem 1.1 have reduced the whole question to one quotient coordinate. The upper-band theorem now proves that this coordinate cannot be evaluated locally at the absolute endpoint.

Thus the desired Apéry cancellation is impossible at width \(o(M/\log M)\).

### Theorem D — fixed-order recurrence trichotomy

A fixed rational Apéry Ore row is either homogeneous or has logarithmic value height \(M\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log M)\).

### Theorem E — modular seam failure

A first Green seam preserves an Apéry target iff its extension coordinate also vanishes there. The canonical \(\kappa\) and \(\Xi\) coordinates fail this at \((p,n)=(31,8)\).

# Final ruling

No explicit viable nonorthogonal row exists among the four requested source classes.

The absolute upper endpoint is not merely unhelpful: throughout every band of width \(o(M/\log M)\), the nonorthogonal quotient is asymptotic to the full Apéry origin and therefore has linear logarithmic height. The fixed self-adjoint recurrence either annihilates homogeneously or retains the same dominant exponential. The canonical modular/Eichler seams are transverse units at actual targets.

Accordingly, the only surviving Turán–Smith route is a **genuinely nonlocal first-primary cross-ray identity** whose raw support reaches beyond the \(M/\log M\) endpoint depth, whose coefficients remain subexponential, and whose arithmetic specialization preserves every target. Producing such an identity would be new Apéry arithmetic; it is not contained in the existing endpoint, recurrence, modular, Green/WZ, Plücker, or Smith interfaces.
