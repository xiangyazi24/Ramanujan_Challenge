# Stage C: horizontal dispersion for Apéry LGTQ-3

## 1. Closest analogue

The closest framework is the Friedlander-Iwaniec dispersion method combined with large-sieve ideas, not classical BDH/BV. BDH and BV average over fixed arithmetic progressions; here the sparse sets Z_p move with p and are defined by Frobenius/recurrence data. Hooley's square sieve is philosophically closer (detecting rare events by second moments), but the incidence family is not a quadratic-sieve family. The obstruction is the absence of a usable independent family of characters: 1_{Z_p} is not naturally a single trace function of bounded conductor.

## 2. The N+P^2 barrier

A raw large sieve cannot beat the P^2 term when P~N. Breaking it requires additional algebraic cancellation. The possible source is that Z_p is not an arbitrary subset of size p^{2/3}; it is a zero locus of a K3 Picard-Fuchs trace function. One needs a geometric large sieve for a compatible family of sheaves, where the conductor is controlled uniformly and cross-prime correlations are bounded.

## 3. Multiplicative Fourier structure

The identity expressing b_j as a multiplicative Fourier coefficient is the correct entry point. Expanding products of X_p leads to sums of K3 trace functions over F_p^*. If one can show Deligne-type square-root cancellation after averaging the primes, this becomes a dispersion estimate. The missing ingredient is independence of the local systems attached to different primes.

## 4. Possible Apéry-specific cancellation

Supercongruences alone mainly control vertical p-power behavior and do not directly decorrelate different primes. More promising are: (i) monodromy of the Picard-Fuchs local system, (ii) nontrivial Frobenius conjugacy distribution, and (iii) the recurrence forcing low-complexity relations among shifted zeros. These are the genuinely Apéry-specific inputs.

## 5. Hybrid strategy

A hybrid proof is plausible and has precedents. Large sieve gives density-one L^2 control; geometric input can bound structured exceptional configurations; high moments amplify this into tail estimates. This resembles modern sieve arguments using average equidistribution plus exceptional-set elimination.

Conclusion: Stage C is not a missing elementary estimate. The required theorem is a geometric dispersion theorem for a moving family of Frobenius-defined sparse sets. Existing analytic tools provide the amplification mechanism, but a new Apéry/K3-specific cross-prime independence statement is the decisive missing step.
