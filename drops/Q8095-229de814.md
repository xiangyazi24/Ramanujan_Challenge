# Verdict

The proposed raw-gcd theorem is **false**, and it fails inside the exact Apéry–Franel system, not in a synthetic model. There is an entire exact shell family on which the two cutoffs coincide with the full Apéry number:

\[
0\le m\le X \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_{m,X}=S_{m,X}=b_m.
\]

Consequently, at `m=X`,

\[
\gcd(D_{X,X},S_{X,X})=b_X\ge \binom{2X}{X}^2
\ge \frac{16^X}{(2X+1)^2},
\]

so

\[
\sup_{0\le m<X^2}\log\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X})
\ge X\log16-O(\log X).
\]

In particular the supremum is not `o(X)`, and no bound `O(X^{1-\delta})` can hold. This is a sharply scoped counterexample preserving the **actual** Franel coefficient vector, the exact Strehl identity, the cutoff recurrences, and the stated WZ identity.

There is a second, independent obstruction to the proposed WZ route. Put `Y=m(m+1)` and

\[
y_h=h(h+1).
\]

The exact factorization

\[
A(m,k)=\frac1{(k!)^2}\prod_{h=0}^{k-1}(Y-y_h)
\]

shows that for every target prime `X<p\le2X`, the boundary factor in the WZ identity is already divisible by `p` **for every residue m mod p** (with the harmless special case `p=X+1` absorbed by the front factor `(X+1)^2`). Thus reduction of

\[
(X!)^2(D-S)=(X+1)^2A(m,X+1)P_X(Y)
\]

modulo a target prime is identically `0=0`: it does not give a second equation at first p-adic order. Only repeated common divisibility, typically `p^2`, can sometimes force `P_X(Y)\equiv0\pmod p`.

The correct window-prime part of the construction is therefore not a stronger substitute for the moving-zero problem; it is **exactly the moving-zero problem**. If

\[
\Pi_X=\prod_{X<p\le2X}p,
\]

then the assumed exact congruences give the identity

\[
\boxed{
\gcd\bigl(D_{m,X},S_{m,X},\Pi_X\bigr)
=
\prod_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_{m\bmod p}}}p.
}
\]

Hence the corrected theorem

\[
\sup_{m<X^2}
\log\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X},\Pi_X)=o(X)
\]

is not a bypass of HM7/I5; it is precisely the desired dyadic radical estimate in another notation.

What the direct attack *does* yield is a clean structural diagnosis: the raw gcd contains large genuine shell mass that must be removed, while every first-order WZ/Bezout attempt that keeps all target primes also inherits the universal folded shell and loses transversality. A successful direct approach needs a genuinely new **transverse first-power Apéry quantity**, not merely another derivative, resultant, or holonomic relation of the same shell pair.

# 2. Exact shell counterexample to the claimed uniform theorem

Recall

\[
A(m,k)=\binom mk\binom{m+k}{k},
\qquad
F_k=\sum_{i=0}^k\binom ki^3,
\]

\[
D_{m,X}=\sum_{k=0}^X A(m,k)^2,
\qquad
S_{m,X}=\sum_{k=0}^X A(m,k)F_k.
\]

The first sum is the usual Apéry summand. The second is Strehl's Apéry–Franel transform. In the present normalization the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
 b_m=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom mk\binom{m+k}{k}F_k.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

It is the identity used in the repository's cutoff-transform files; a primary source is Volker Strehl, *Binomial identities — combinatorial and algorithmic aspects*, Discrete Mathematics 136 (1994), 309–346, DOI `10.1016/0012-365X(94)00118-3`.

## Theorem 2.1 — exact saturated-shell obstruction

For every pair of integers `X\ge1` and `0\le m\le X`,

\[
D_{m,X}=S_{m,X}=b_m.
\]

### Proof

If `k>m`, then `\binom mk=0`. Hence

\[
D_{m,X}
=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom mk^2\binom{m+k}{k}^2
=b_m.
\]

The same truncation and (2.1) give

\[
S_{m,X}
=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom mk\binom{m+k}{k}F_k
=b_m.
\]

No congruence, asymptotic estimate, or exceptional-prime deletion is involved. ∎

## Corollary 2.2 — linear lower bound for the supremum

At `m=X`,

\[
\gcd(D_{X,X},S_{X,X})=b_X.
\]

The `k=X` term in the defining Apéry sum is

\[
A(X,X)^2=\binom{2X}{X}^2.
\]

Since the central binomial coefficient is the largest of the `2X+1` coefficients in

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{2X}\binom{2X}{j}=4^X,
\]

we have

\[
\binom{2X}{X}\ge\frac{4^X}{2X+1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log\gcd(D_{X,X},S_{X,X})
\ge X\log16-2\log(2X+1).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

So the proposed `o(X)` theorem is false.

The obstruction is not confined to the single endpoint. For every fixed `0<\delta\le1`, take `m=\lfloor\delta X\rfloor`; then

\[
\log\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X})
\ge \delta X\log16-O(\log X).
\]

Thus deleting finitely many exceptional `m`, or only the point `m=X`, does not repair the theorem. One must at least remove the whole saturated range `m\le X` or divide away its irrelevant non-window arithmetic.

## Audit of the claimed `X\le2048` diagnostic

If a computation really took

\[
\max_{0\le m<X^2}\operatorname{bitlength}\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X}),
\]

then it cannot be only tens of bits. At `X=2048,m=2048`, (2.2) alone gives

\[
\gcd(D,S)\ge \frac{2^{8192}}{4097^2}>2^{8166}.
\]

So even this crude analytic lower bound forces more than eight thousand bits. The reported small gcd data must therefore have excluded `m\le X`, measured only a prime-window part, divided out a shell contribution, or computed a different pair. That distinction matters to the theorem statement.

# 3. The exact corrected object is the original target radical

Let

\[
\Pi_X=\prod_{X<p\le2X}p.
\]

For a target prime the hypothesis of the question is the exact congruence

\[
D_{m,X}\equiv S_{m,X}\equiv b_{m\bmod p}\pmod p.
\]

Because `\Pi_X` is squarefree, for every `p\mid\Pi_X`,

\[
p\mid\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_{m\bmod p}.
\]

Multiplying over target primes proves the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\gcd\bigl(\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X}),\Pi_X\bigr)
=
R_X(m),
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
R_X(m)=\prod_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_{m\bmod p}}}p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sup_{m<X^2}\log R_X(m)=o(X)
\]

is exactly the corrected `D,S` statement. It no longer has the false shell mass, but it no longer bypasses the original arithmetic problem either.

This gives a useful logical separation:

- **Raw gcd:** stronger than needed and false.
- **Window-prime part of raw gcd:** exactly the desired radical and open.
- **Off-shell raw gcd**, for example `X<m<X^2`: computationally interesting and plausibly small, but not currently a theorem; even if proved, the shell range would still require control of (3.1).

# 4. Why the WZ identity is first-order tautological on the target window

Put

\[
Y=m(m+1),\qquad y_h=h(h+1),
\]

and for `k\ge0`

\[
\Phi_k(Y)=\prod_{h=0}^{k-1}(Y-y_h).
\]

Then directly from the two binomial products,

\[
\boxed{
A(m,k)=\frac{\Phi_k(m(m+1))}{(k!)^2}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed,

\[
(m-h)(m+h+1)=m(m+1)-h(h+1).
\]

Take `k=X+1` in (4.1). Fix a prime `X<p\le2X` and a residue `r=m\bmod p`. Write its fold

\[
s=\min(r,p-1-r).
\]

For odd target primes,

\[
0\le s\le\frac{p-1}{2}<X.
\]

Also

\[
r(r+1)\equiv s(s+1)=y_s\pmod p.
\]

### Case 1: `p>X+1`

Then `(X+1)!` is a p-adic unit, and the factor `h=s` occurs in `\Phi_{X+1}`. Hence

\[
p\mid A(m,X+1)
\]

for **every** integer `m`, independently of whether `p\mid b_r`.

### Case 2: `p=X+1`

If this is prime, the WZ front factor `(X+1)^2` already contributes `p^2`.

Thus for every target prime,

\[
p\mid (X+1)^2A(m,X+1)
\]

identically in `m`. Reducing the exact WZ identity

\[
(X!)^2(D-S)=(X+1)^2A(m,X+1)P_X(Y)
\tag{4.2}
\]

modulo `p` therefore yields no condition on `P_X(Y)`.

This is not an unfortunate choice of normalization. It is the same geometric fact that makes the cutoff work: because `p\le2X`, the cutoff already reaches every folded residue `s`. **Uniform Lucas saturation and WZ loss of transversality are two sides of the same shell factorization.**

# 5. Exact p-adic valuation of the WZ boundary factor

There is still information at the next p-adic order, but it controls repeated prime factors rather than the squarefree radical.

Fix `p>X+1`, put `k=X+1`, and assume `m\ge k`. Since

\[
m<X^2<p^2,
\]

write

\[
m=ap+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

Because `k<p`, Legendre's formula gives

\[
v_p\binom mk
=\left\lfloor\frac mp\right\rfloor
-\left\lfloor\frac{m-k}{p}\right\rfloor
=\mathbf1_{r<k},
\]

and

\[
v_p\binom{m+k}{k}
=\left\lfloor\frac{m+k}{p}\right\rfloor
-\left\lfloor\frac mp\right\rfloor
=\mathbf1_{r+k\ge p}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
v_p A(m,X+1)
=\mathbf1_{r\le X}+\mathbf1_{r\ge p-X-1}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since `p\le2X`, these two intervals cover all residues. Hence

\[
v_pA(m,X+1)=
\begin{cases}
2,&p-X-1\le r\le X,\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For `m<X+1`, the binomial `\binom m{X+1}` is zero exactly, which is the saturated-shell obstruction already discussed.

Now suppose `p>X+1` and `p^2` divides both `D_{m,X}` and `S_{m,X}`. Then `p^2\mid D-S`. Since `p\nmid X!(X+1)`, (4.2) gives

\[
v_pA(m,X+1)+v_pP_X(Y)\ge2.
\]

Thus in the residue region where `v_pA=1`, one obtains

\[
p\mid P_X(Y).
\]

But where `v_pA=2`, even `p^2\mid D-S` is automatic from the boundary factor. More importantly, the target radical asks only for the first power `p\mid D,S`. Formula (5.1) shows that this first power is already swallowed by the universal shell. So p-adic lifting does not solve the first-power problem unless one proves a separate theorem that forces many target primes to occur with higher common valuation, which is not known and is not suggested by the current setup.

# 6. Small-prime factorial content is genuinely non-uniform

For primes `\ell\le X`, `(X!)^2` in (4.2) is not invertible. Off the exact zero case the valuation equality is

\[
2v_\ell(X!)+v_\ell(D-S)
=2v_\ell(X+1)+v_\ell A(m,X+1)+v_\ell P_X(Y).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The term

\[
2v_\ell(X!)
\]

is of size `\asymp X/(\ell-1)` for fixed `\ell`, and globally

\[
\log (X!)^2=2X\log X-2X+O(\log X).
\]

Therefore any argument that clears the Newton denominators and then treats the resulting factorial content as an error immediately incurs `\Theta(X\log X)`, far above an `o(X)` target. The small-prime contribution must be **saturated algebraically**, not bounded by its raw height.

The next section shows that even after this saturation, a constant Bezout certificate is forced to remember the large-prime parts of the shell Apéry values.

# 7. Shell resultant and a Bezout/Smith no-go

Define

\[
\Phi_X(Y)=\prod_{h=0}^{X-1}(Y-y_h)
\]

and the degree-`X-1` Franel cutoff polynomial

\[
\mathcal S_{X-1}(Y)
=\sum_{j=0}^{X-1}
F_j\frac{\Phi_j(Y)}{(j!)^2}.
\]

Put the integral clearing

\[
C_X(Y)=(X!)^2\mathcal S_{X-1}(Y)\in\mathbb Z[Y].
\]

At a shell node `Y=y_h`, all terms with `j>h` vanish and the surviving Strehl sum is the full Apéry number. Therefore

\[
\boxed{C_X(y_h)=(X!)^2b_h\qquad(0\le h<X).}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Since `\Phi_X` is monic with the distinct roots `y_0,\ldots,y_{X-1}`, (7.1) immediately gives

\[
\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_X,C_X)
=(X!)^{2X}\prod_{h=0}^{X-1}b_h.
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is the tall resultant identified in the question.

The important point is that the factorial factor is not merely an artifact of choosing the full resultant.

## Proposition 7.1 — integral Bezout constants necessarily contain `(X!)^2`

Suppose

\[
U(Y)\Phi_X(Y)+V(Y)C_X(Y)=c,
\qquad U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y],\quad c\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.
\]

Evaluating at `Y=y_0=0` and using `b_0=1` gives

\[
c=V(0)(X!)^2.
\]

Hence

\[
(X!)^2\mid c.
\]

So **every** integral constant Bezout/Smith certificate already has logarithmic height `\gg X\log X` before it sees a single nontrivial Apéry value.

## Proposition 7.2 — after factorial saturation, large-prime shell factors remain

Let

\[
R_X=\mathbb Z[1/X!].
\]

Suppose

\[
U\Phi_X+VC_X=c,
\qquad U,V\in R_X[Y],\quad 0\ne c\in R_X.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then for every `0\le h<X` and every prime `q>X`,

\[
v_q(c)\ge v_q(b_h).
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Proof

Evaluate (7.3) at `y_h`. By (7.1),

\[
c=V(y_h)(X!)^2b_h.
\]

For `q>X`, both `(X!)^2` and every allowed denominator in `R_X` are q-adic units, while `V(y_h)` is q-adically integral. Hence (7.4). ∎

Equivalently, the prime-`>X` part of

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_{X-1})
\]

must divide any factorial-saturated constant certificate. Thus a miraculous small Smith invariant over `\mathbb Z[1/X!]` would already contain a theorem controlling precisely the large-prime shell arithmetic we are trying to understand. A constant Bezout certificate cannot make that information disappear.

This does **not** prove that every more sophisticated `Y`-dependent certificate is useless. It proves the precise no-go for the most tempting uniform constant certificate: either one keeps factorials and pays `X\log X`, or one inverts them and is left with the large-prime Apéry shell values.

# 8. Why first derivative/discriminant saturation produces no new target equation

The shell differences have the exact factorization

\[
y_h-y_j=(h-j)(h+j+1).
\]

Hence

\[
\Phi_X'(y_h)
=(-1)^{X-1-h}\frac{(X-1-h)!(X+h)!}{2h+1}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For a prime `p>X`, the factor `h-j` can never vanish modulo `p` when `0\le h,j<X` and `h\ne j`. Therefore

\[
p\mid\Phi_X'(y_h)
\]

can occur only when there is a distinct shell index

\[
j=p-1-h\in[0,X-1].
\]

That is exactly the reflection partner. If `2h+1=p`, then `j=h`; this is the fixed fold and is excluded from the derivative product, so it is not a multiple-root collision.

Thus discriminant saturation at target primes removes only the already-known reflection collision of shell nodes. It does not create a second independent condition on `C_X` or on `b_h`. Away from that collision a bad prime simply gives a **simple** common root of `\Phi_X` and `C_X` modulo `p`. Differentiating does not make it a multiple common root. This is the algebraic reason that first-derivative saturation returns the same resultant information rather than a new target equation.

# 9. Exact cutoff recurrence: parameter substitution and the height dead end

The repository theorem `Q32_ALL_CUTOFF_BLOCK_AND_LOSS_PROOF.md`, Theorem 2.1, writes an arbitrary index and cutoff as

\[
n=qp+r,\qquad J=Ap+B,\qquad 0\le r,B<p,
\]

and proves coefficientwise modulo `p`

\[
T_{qp+r,Ap+B}(c)
\equiv
A_rT_{q,A-1}(c^p)
+K_{q,A}(c^p)g_A(c^p)T_{r,B}(c).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Now substitute the present dyadic parameters:

\[
n=m,\qquad J=X,\qquad X<p\le2X.
\]

Since `J=X<p`, its base-p high digit is

\[
A=0,\qquad B=X.
\]

Also `m<X^2<p^2`, so `q<p`, exactly within the two-digit range of the theorem. Equation (9.1) reduces to the low-digit transform

\[
T_{m,X}(c)\equiv T_{r,X}(c)\pmod p.
\]

Fold `r` to

\[
j=\min(r,p-1-r)<X.
\]

The cutoff `B=X` is already beyond the effective folded degree `j`, so the repository's saturation formula gives

\[
T_{r,X}(c)\equiv b_j\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\]

This exactly recovers the stated Lucas/reflection congruence. It is not a second restriction.

The same repository proof becomes more informative when `J` is varied: it identifies precisely where a bad prime is lost or regained by a cutoff and proves the loss-count formula

\[
v_p(P_n)=\min(q,p-1-q).
\]

But Section 5 of that proof explicitly records the remaining statement as a **height** problem:

\[
\log\operatorname{Once}(P_n)=o(n),
\]

with the warning that no known bound on individual cutoff contents or on the raw loss product implies it. This is exactly the phenomenon encountered here: cutoff algebra can label the desired support very efficiently, but it does not make the product of those primes small.

# 10. Franel recurrence and Dwork congruences preserve the exact model — and still do not prove the raw bound

The Franel vector in this problem is not arbitrary. It satisfies the fixed second-order recurrence

\[
(n+1)^2F_{n+1}
=\bigl(7n(n+1)+2\bigr)F_n+8n^2F_{n-1}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

It also has the relevant Lucas/Dwork structure. The repository's `Q32_FRANEL_TAIL_DWORK_PROOF.md` uses the constant-term Laurent polynomial

\[
\Lambda(u,v)=(1+u)(1+v)(1+1/(uv)),
\]

with

\[
F_m=\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^m,
\]

and proves the exact tail-lattice integrality by a coefficient-ring version of Dwork congruences. The primary congruence inputs are:

- Kira Samol and Duco van Straten, *Dwork congruences and reflexive polytopes*, Annales Mathématiques du Québec 39 (2015), 185–203, DOI `10.1007/s40316-015-0031-9`.
- Anton Mellit and Masha Vlasenko, *Dwork's congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial*, International Journal of Number Theory 12 (2016), 313–321, DOI `10.1142/S1793042116500184`, arXiv `1306.5811`.

Mellit–Vlasenko's Theorem 1 assumes that the origin is the unique interior integral point of the Newton polyhedron and obtains the prime-power Dwork congruence for the constant terms. Those hypotheses are exactly of the type used in the repository proof.

But the shell counterexample of Section 2 already uses the actual recurrence (10.1), the actual Dwork-compatible `F_n`, and the actual Strehl transform. Therefore no theorem whose only new assumptions are “Franel recurrence”, “Franel is a G-function/D-finite sequence”, or “Dwork congruences hold” can imply the false raw-gcd bound. To become relevant to (3.1), the p-adic input has to control **cross-characteristic first-power alignment**, not only a single-prime coefficient congruence or integrality lattice.

The exact Hasse–Franel pullback in `hasse_franel_descent.tex`,

\[
K_p(x)^2=(1+x)^{p-1}A_p\!\left(\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x}\right),
\]

is similarly Apéry-specific and exact, but its coordinates are the individual `b_r`. It reformulates the bad residue condition; it does not bound the product of varying primes for which those coordinates vanish.

# 11. Precise audit of Subspace-Theorem and G-function gcd machinery

Several powerful gcd theorems look superficially relevant because they prove `o(height)` common divisors. Their fixed-data hypotheses fail here in a way that cannot be hidden in an `X^{o(1)}` constant.

## 11.1 Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier

Bugeaud, Corvaja and Zannier, *An upper bound for the G.C.D. of a^n-1 and b^n-1*, Math. Z. 243 (2003), 79–84, DOI `10.1007/s00209-002-0449-z`, prove for **fixed multiplicatively independent integers** `a,b` that for every `\varepsilon>0`,

\[
\log\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)\le \varepsilon n+O_{a,b,\varepsilon}(1).
\]

There is no valid substitution here:

1. For fixed `X`, `D_{m,X}` and `S_{m,X}` are polynomials in `m` (equivalently in `Y=m(m+1)`), not two fixed-base exponential sequences.
2. If one forces a linear-recurrence viewpoint, a polynomial sequence has only characteristic root `1` with multiplicity, so the multiplicative-independence mechanism of the BCZ theorem disappears.
3. More importantly, the degree/order of the polynomial recurrences grows with `X`; BCZ's constants are for fixed `a,b`, not a family of increasing complexity.

So BCZ supplies no uniform estimate in the present moving-degree regime.

## 11.2 Corvaja–Zannier S-unit rational-function theorem

Corvaja and Zannier, *A lower bound for the height of a rational function at S-unit points*, Monatsh. Math. 144 (2005), 203–224, arXiv `math/0311030`, assume:

- a **fixed** finitely generated subgroup `\Gamma\subset\mathbb G_m^2(\overline{\mathbb Q})`;
- two **fixed** coprime polynomials `p(X,Y),q(X,Y)` not both vanishing at `(0,0)`;
- points `(u,v)\in\Gamma`, outside a proper fixed Zariski-closed exceptional set.

The theorem compares the height of `p(u,v)/q(u,v)` with the projective height and yields gcd consequences.

Trying `u=m,v=m+1` fails immediately: these pairs do not lie in one fixed finitely generated S-unit subgroup as `m` varies. Trying to use the actual cutoff polynomials fails independently because their degree and coefficients depend on `X`. Enlarging `S` with the prime divisors of `m(m+1)` makes the group itself move. Thus both essential fixed-data hypotheses fail.

## 11.3 Fischler–Rivoal on G-function values

Fischler and Rivoal, *Rational approximation to values of G-functions, and their expansions in integer bases*, arXiv `1512.06534` (current revision dated March 22, 2026), Theorem 2, fix a single non-rational G-function `F` with rational Taylor coefficients. They obtain effective constants `c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4` depending only on that fixed `F` (and `t` where stated), and for rational points `a/b` satisfying

\[
b>(c_1|a|)^{c_2},
\]

prove restricted rational-approximation lower bounds for `F(a/b)`.

The present gcd is not a rational approximation to a value of one fixed G-function:

- `m` is a **coefficient/index variable**, not a rational analytic argument `a/b`.
- `D_{m,X}` and `S_{m,X}` are moving finite cutoff polynomials of degree growing with `X`.
- If one packages a cutoff into a G-function `F_X`, then the function itself changes with `X`, and the theorem's constants become `c_i(F_X)`. No published bound makes those constants subexponential, let alone uniform enough for `m<X^2`.
- If instead one fixes the Apéry generating function, its value at `a/b` is not the integer pair whose gcd is under study.

Thus the theorem cannot be parameter-substituted into an `o(X)` gcd estimate.

## 11.4 Arithmetic dynamics / generalized gcd

Silverman, *Generalized Greatest Common Divisors, Divisibility Sequences, and Vojta's Conjecture for Blowups*, Monatsh. Math. 145 (2005), 333–350, arXiv `math/0407415`, obtains powerful gcd estimates from **Vojta's conjecture** for fixed varieties, blowups/divisors and algebraic-group data.

Even ignoring its conditional status, the present family has moving divisors/polynomials of degree `X`. One could view `m\mapsto m+1` as a fixed dynamical map, but the two divisors cut out by `D_X` and `S_X` change with `X`; the constants and exceptional subvarieties in the fixed-data theorem are not uniform in this moving family. Hence this does not presently imply the desired estimate.

# 12. Ordinary holonomicity is structurally insufficient

Both the Franel and Apéry sequences are holonomic, and for fixed `X` the cutoff polynomials satisfy many finite recurrences. But holonomicity alone cannot control a gcd of integer values uniformly when the order/degree of the family moves. The exact shell example is the cleanest witness: the two holonomic constructions are literally equal to `b_m` on `m\le X`, and `b_m` is exponentially large.

Any argument advertised as “the two sums satisfy recurrences, therefore their gcd is small” must contain an additional arithmetic nonalignment theorem. Without it, the recurrence merely transports the same common factor.

# 13. What the WZ identity actually controls

Let

\[
g_{m,X}=\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X}).
\]

For a prime `q>X`, the factorial `(X!)^2` is a q-adic unit. Therefore from (4.2), any q-adic part of `g_{m,X}` not already explained by

\[
(X+1)^2A(m,X+1)
\]

must occur in `P_X(Y)`. Symbolically, the q-primary part of

\[
\frac{g_{m,X}}
{\gcd(g_{m,X},(X+1)^2A(m,X+1))}
\]

is bounded by the q-primary part of `P_X(Y)`.

This is a valid transverse statement about **unexpected** large-prime common factors. But it removes precisely the wrong part for the Apéry target: every target prime `X<p\le2X` already divides the universal boundary factor. So the WZ identity is well suited to proving that the experimentally tiny *extra* gcd is small, while being blind at first order to the target radical itself.

That distinction may explain the diagnostic data: if the experiment divided out the universal shell/boundary contribution, a gcd of only tens of bits is entirely compatible with the analysis above.

# 14. Strongest theorem-grade conclusion available from this attack

The following statements are rigorous.

## Theorem A — raw theorem is false

\[
\sup_{m<X^2}\log\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X})
\ge X\log16-O(\log X).
\]

This uses the actual Franel vector and exact identities.

## Theorem B — window restriction is exactly the original radical

\[
\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X},\Pi_X)
=
\prod_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_{m\bmod p}}}p.
\]

Thus an `o(X)` theorem for the window restriction is equivalent to the desired direct dyadic radical bound, not a stronger independent theorem from which it follows for free.

## Theorem C — first-order WZ transversality fails identically

For every target prime, the WZ boundary coefficient is already divisible by that prime for every physical `m`. Hence the WZ identity supplies no new equation modulo `p`. Formula (5.1) gives the exact multiplicity and shows where a `p^2` common divisor can force `P_X(Y)\equiv0\pmod p`.

## Theorem D — constant Smith/Bezout certificates have a forced-height barrier

Over `\mathbb Z[Y]`, every constant certificate lies in `(X!)^2\mathbb Z`. After saturating by `X!`, every prime `q>X` dividing any shell Apéry value `b_h`, `h<X`, must still divide the constant certificate with at least the same valuation.

These four points close the most direct versions of the proposed route without appealing to heuristic evidence.

# 15. The minimal plausible repair

There are two different repaired research questions.

## 15.1 Off-shell raw gcd

One may ask whether

\[
\sup_{X<m<X^2}\log\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X})=o(X).
\tag{15.1}
\]

The exact shell counterexample no longer applies. The existing diagnostic data may well concern this quantity. I do not know a published theorem proving (15.1), and none of the fixed-data Subspace/G-function results audited above has the required uniformity. Moreover the first-order WZ degeneracy **still persists** for target primes, because it depends on the folded residue modulo `p`, not on `m\le X`.

Even a proof of (15.1) would not by itself finish the all-index conjecture: one must still control the window-prime part for the saturated rows `m\le X`.

## 15.2 A genuinely transverse first-power quantity

A real bypass would need a third exact Apéry-specific integer or polynomial `T_{m,X}` with both properties:

1. every target prime with `p\mid b_{m\bmod p}` divides `T_{m,X}`;
2. unlike `(X+1)^2A(m,X+1)`, `T_{m,X}` does **not** vanish modulo every target prime for purely shell-geometric reasons.

Then a small Bezout/resultant between `T` and one of the existing cutoffs could attack first powers. First derivatives and discriminant saturation do not satisfy (2); the analysis of Section 8 shows that they only remove reflection multiplicity. The existing cutoff-loss construction does satisfy a support-selection property, but its unresolved height is precisely the hard part.

So the smallest missing mechanism is not another recurrence. It is **first-power transverse arithmetic across the folded shell**.

# 16. Reproducible exact diagnostics

The following Python uses only integer arithmetic. It is not needed for any proof above; it is a regression test for the theorem statement and the p-adic carrier calculation.

```python
from math import comb, gcd


def A(m, k):
    if k < 0 or k > m:
        return 0
    return comb(m, k) * comb(m + k, k)


def franel(k):
    return sum(comb(k, i) ** 3 for i in range(k + 1))


def apery(m):
    return sum(A(m, k) ** 2 for k in range(m + 1))


def D(m, X):
    return sum(A(m, k) ** 2 for k in range(X + 1))


def S(m, X):
    return sum(A(m, k) * franel(k) for k in range(X + 1))

# Exact saturated-shell regression.
for X in range(1, 25):
    for m in range(X + 1):
        b = apery(m)
        assert D(m, X) == b
        assert S(m, X) == b
        assert gcd(D(m, X), S(m, X)) == b

# The m=X lower-bound term alone already has thousands of bits at X=2048.
X = 2048
central_term = comb(2 * X, X) ** 2
print("central-term lower-bound bits:", central_term.bit_length())


def vp_factorial(n, p):
    ans = 0
    while n:
        n //= p
        ans += n
    return ans


def vp_binom(n, k, p):
    if k < 0 or k > n:
        return 10**9  # stand-in for infinity for this diagnostic
    return (vp_factorial(n, p)
            - vp_factorial(k, p)
            - vp_factorial(n-k, p))

# For p > X+1 and X+1 <= m < X^2, verify equation (5.1).
def check_boundary_valuation(X, p, m):
    k = X + 1
    assert p > k and m >= k and m < X * X
    r = m % p
    lhs = vp_binom(m, k, p) + vp_binom(m + k, k, p)
    rhs = int(r <= X) + int(r >= p - X - 1)
    assert lhs == rhs
    assert lhs in (1, 2)

# Example exact finite regression, with supplied prime lists in (X,2X].
for X, ps in [(10, [13, 17, 19]), (20, [23, 29, 31, 37])]:
    for p in ps:
        if p <= X + 1:
            continue
        for m in range(X + 1, X * X):
            check_boundary_valuation(X, p, m)
```

A scan intended to test the raw supremum should include the assertion at `m=X`; a scan intended to test the off-shell or window-restricted conjecture should label that restriction explicitly.

# Final conclusion

The attractive statement

\[
\sup_{m<X^2}\log\gcd(D_{m,X},S_{m,X})=o(X)
\]

cannot be the direct bridge to the all-index Apéry conjecture, because the exact system itself has

\[
D_{m,X}=S_{m,X}=b_m
\]

throughout the saturated shell `m\le X`, giving an exponential common divisor. After restricting to the dyadic prime window, the common divisor becomes **exactly** the original target radical, so the apparent bypass disappears.

The WZ identity nevertheless gives a useful negative theorem: the cutoff required for uniform Lucas/reflection is exactly large enough that its boundary factor contains every folded shell root modulo every target prime. Hence first-order WZ is tautological on the target support. The p-adic valuation formula (5.1) shows precisely what survives at second order. Likewise, factorial-saturated Bezout constants are forced to retain the large-prime parts of the actual shell values `b_h`.

Accordingly, a genuinely new direct proof must introduce a **transverse first-power Apéry certificate** whose target-prime vanishing is not caused by the universal shell. Neither ordinary holonomicity, the existing resultant, first derivative/discriminant saturation, fixed-data Subspace-Theorem gcd results, nor current G-function value theorems provide that mechanism.
