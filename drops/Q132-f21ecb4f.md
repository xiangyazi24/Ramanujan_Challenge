# The companion-denominator route: an exact local theorem, and why it is still the shell-radical problem

## 5. What the companion viewpoint really buys

The companion route does not improve the numerical target, but it does give three genuine structural gains.

### 5.1 It identifies the local mechanism exactly

A top hit is precisely a failure of the companion denominator to contain the full \(p^3\) layer. The amount of failure is

\[
\min(v_p(b_n),3).
\]

So the top radical is not merely correlated with the denominator defect; it is its support.

### 5.2 It separates support from multiplicity

The empirical law \(v_p(G_n)=1\) is equivalent to simple hits. A mod-\(p^2\) theorem for \(b_{p+r}\) could potentially prove that repeated top hits are rare. This would settle the local weight, although not the support count.

### 5.3 It suggests a new p-adic observable

At a hit, the first coefficient (2.12) vanishes. The next coefficient

\[
rac{p^3a_{p+r}}p\pmod p
\]

is a tangent or extension-class observable at the zero \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\). Controlling its vanishing could prove simple-hit or nonresonance statements. This is a legitimate Dwork/Mellin refinement.

But none of these facts couples **different primes**. The product over top primes remains a horizontal support problem.

A useful name for the exact residual is:

```text
(TWDS)  Top-window denominator saturation:

log product_{n/2<p<=n} p^(3-v_p(D_n)) = o(n).
```

By (3.12), this is

\[
\log\prod_{n/2<p\le n}p^{\min(v_p(b_n),3)}=o(n),
\]

which is equivalent to the shell-radical theorem.

Standard denominator theorems for \(G\)-functions or recurrence solutions run in the opposite direction: they construct a common denominator such as \(\Delta_n\), hence give an **upper bound** on \(D_n\). TWDS requires a near-maximal **lower bound** on the reduced denominator after all cancellations. No general theorem supplies that lower bound.

## 6. Why small-prime saturation is not an equivalent route

Define the small-prime logarithmic mass

\[
S_{\le n/2}(b_n)
=\sum_{p\le n/2}v_p(b_n)\log p.
	ag{6.1}
\]

The proposed theorem is

\[
S_{\le n/2}(b_n)\ge(\log(17+12\sqrt2)-o(1))n.
	ag{6.2}
\]

Because

\[
\log b_n=\log(17+12\sqrt2)n+O(\log n),
\]

(6.2) says that the entire cofactor of \(b_n\) supported on primes \(>n/2\) is subexponential. This controls not only the dangerous shell \((n/2,n]\), but every prime above \(n\), including prime factors of exponential size.

It is therefore much stronger than the required statement.

A simple logical example shows the gap. Suppose an integer \(N_n\) of size \(\exp(cn)\) is itself prime and exceeds \(n\). Then

\[
\omega_{(n/2,n]}(N_n)=0,
\]

so the shell conclusion holds perfectly, while

\[
\sum_{p\le n/2}v_p(N_n)\log p=0.
\]

Thus shell evacuation does not require small-prime saturation; its logarithmic mass may escape upward rather than downward.

### 6.1 The random-integer model predicts the opposite

For a random integer \(X\) much larger than \(n\),

\[
\mathbb E\,v_p(X)\approx\sum_{j\ge1}rac1{p^j}=rac1{p-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E\sum_{p\le n/2}v_p(X)\log p
\approx\sum_{p\le n/2}rac{\log p}{p-1}
=\log n+O(1).
	ag{6.3}
\]

This is logarithmic, not linear.

On the other hand,

\[

\approx\sum_{n/2<p\le n}rac{\log p}{p}

So the random model simultaneously predicts:


This is exactly why (6.2) is the wrong target. The desired theorem is a **local evacuation of one shell**, not a global smoothness theorem.

## 7. Why Kummer carries of the summands cannot give a lower bound

For each summand

\[
W_{n,k}=inom nk^2inom{n+k}{k}^2,
\]

Kummer's theorem gives an exact carry formula for \(v_p(W_{n,k})\). But a lower bound on the valuations of individual summands does not pass to their sum.

The most basic obstruction is

\[
W_{n,0}=1.
	ag{7.1}
\]

Hence for every prime \(p\),

\[
\min_{0\le k\le n}v_p(W_{n,k})=0.
\]

The ultrametric inequality therefore yields only

\[
v_p(b_n)\ge0.
\]

To get \(v_p(b_n)>0\), the \(p\)-adic unit summands must cancel modulo \(p\). Determining that cancellation is exactly the condition

\[
b_n\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Stratifying the summands by their carry patterns merely reconstructs the finite-field Apéry sum. It does not create an independent lower bound for the small-prime part.

The factorial factors inside a term are thus not inherited by \(b_n\). Addition can erase every one of them. The model example is

\[
N!+1:
\]

although \(N!\) contains every prime at most \(N\), no prime at most \(N\) divides \(N!+1\). A tiny positive remainder can completely destroy all small-prime divisibility of a dominant factorial term.

### 8.1 More importantly, the proposed prime dichotomy is false

Write

\[
b_n=M_n+R_n.
\]

If \(p\mid b_n\), then

\[
R_n\equiv-M_n\pmod p.
\]

There are two cases:

- if \(p\mid M_n\), then \(p\mid R_n\);
- if \(p
mid M_n\), then \(p

Thus the generic cancellation prime divides **neither** \(M_n\) nor \(R_n\). It is not true that every prime divisor of \(b_n\) must divide the chosen term or the tail.

Consequently one cannot bound

\[
\omega_{>n/2}(b_n)
\]

by the sum of the corresponding prime-factor counts of \(M_n\) and \(R_n\).

### 8.2 Relative smallness is irrelevant modulo \(p\)

Even if one chose a saddle block \(M_n\) capturing \(1-o(1)\) of the positive real mass, the remainder \(R_n\) would still be exponentially large in absolute value. A congruence

\[
M_n\equiv-R_n\pmod p
\]

has no difficulty occurring for a prime \(p\asymp n\). To rule it out by size one would need \(|R_n|<p\), or a subexponential product bound, not merely \(R_n=o(M_n)\).

Positivity is archimedean information; reduction modulo \(p\) forgets order and sign.

## 9. Could the mass escape to primes above \(n\) instead?

A logically valid alternative sufficient condition would be to prove that almost all of \(\log b_n\) is carried by primes **above** \(n\), leaving little room for the shell. For example, a sufficiently strong statement of the form

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>n}(b_n)\ge
(\log(17+12\sqrt2)-o(1))n
	ag{9.1}
\]

would force shell evacuation after controlling small prime powers.

This direction is more compatible with the random-integer model than small-prime saturation. But it is not currently easier:

- it is a lower bound on the large squarefree part of one Apéry value;
- largest-prime-factor or primitive-divisor theorems produce one factor, not almost all logarithmic mass;
- \(abc\) gives lower bounds on radicals in suitable coprime sums, but does not localize that radical to \(p>n\), and the radicals of the Apéry summands/remainder are not controlled;
- no cyclotomic or gamma-product factorization of \(b_n\) is known.

Thus “mass escapes upward” is a plausible heuristic picture, not an available proof.

## 10. Why a global denominator estimate does not automatically help

The exact identity (4.1) connects the companion denominator to the reduced Apéry approximation. Indeed,

\[
rac{a_n}{b_n}=rac{A_n}{D_nb_n},
\]

whose reduced denominator is

\[
q_n=rac{D_nb_n}{\gcd(A_n,b_n)}.
	ag{10.1}
\]

Equation (4.1) is equivalently

\[
q_n=rac{\Delta_nb_n}{G_n}.
	ag{10.2}
\]

So a lower bound for \(q_n\) gives an upper bound for \(G_n\). But ordinary irrationality-measure arguments are far too coarse. They control only the total exponential size of \(q_n\); they do not force \(D_n\) to retain almost every top-window prime layer.

Even an optimal irrationality exponent for \(\zeta(3)\) would not by itself identify which primes were cancelled from \(\Delta_n\). Prime support is finer information than denominator magnitude.

This is the same distinction already isolated in `Q115-f1049731.md`:

```text
Diophantine approximation controls how small the reduced denominator can be.
The GCD conjecture asks which moving primes are missing from that denominator.
```

## 12. Final answer

The companion sequence does give an explicit and rigorous top-window valuation theorem:

\[
oxed{
 p^3a_{p+r}\equiv6b_r\pmod p,\qquad
 v_p(D_n)=\max(3-v_p(b_n),0).
}
\]

In particular, a simple hit has

\[
v_p(a_n)=-2,\qquad v_p(G_n)=1.
\]

But this is not true merely from the indicator \(p\mid b_n\); it depends on the hit multiplicity. The correct denominator defect is the truncated valuation \(\min(v_p(b_n),3)\).

Therefore the companion approach transforms the conjecture into

\[
	ext{“the reduced denominator of }a_n	ext{ loses only }e^{o(n)}
	ext{ in the top prime window.”}
\]

That statement is exactly equivalent to the shell-radical bound. No generic denominator theorem proves it.

The proposed small-prime saturation strategy should not be pursued as the main route. It asks for near-total \(n/2\)-smoothness of \(b_n\), is far stronger than shell evacuation, and contradicts the natural random-factorization heuristic. Kummer carry information for the individual summands cannot force it because \(W_{n,0}=1\) and divisibility of a sum is governed by cancellation among \(p\)-adic units.

The dominant-term strategy also fails twice: \(k=n/2\) is exponentially subdominant, and a cancellation prime of \(M+R\) generically divides neither \(M\) nor \(R\).

The best new theorem exposed by Q132 is therefore the **top-window companion denominator law**. The remaining named residual is the **horizontal denominator-saturation theorem**, which is a clean p-adic restatement—but not a simplification—of the top-window Apéry zero-density problem.
