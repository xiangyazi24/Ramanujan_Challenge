# Stationary phase for the Apéry Mellin transform: exact rank, and the category error

## Verdict

There are three different transforms in play, and they must not be identified.

1. The object proved in the repository is the **rank-three transcendental K3 local system** on the Apéry parameter line. Its singularities are
   \[
   0,\quad \infty,\quad \alpha=17+12\sqrt2,\quad \beta=17-12\sqrt2.
   \]
   The two finite points \(\alpha,\beta\) are essential.

2. The character-by-character **multiplicative Mellin cohomology** of the middle-extension K3 sheaf has generic dimension
   \[
   \boxed{2}.
   \]
   This rank two comes from the two finite Picard--Lefschetz reflections. It is **not** obtained by taking a \(\mu _6\)-eigenspace.

3. Katz--Laumon local Fourier transform and stationary phase concern the **additive Fourier--Deligne transform on \(\mathbb A^1\)**. That is not the Gabber--Loeser multiplicative Mellin transform. The latter is a coherent complex over a character algebra, not a lisse sheaf on an algebraic curve \(\mathbb G_m^\vee\). Consequently it has no canonically defined inertia representations at ``\(0^\vee\)'' and ``\(\infty^\vee\)'' whose Jordan forms can be read off by Laumon's stationary-phase theorem.

There is also a useful sharp correction to the calculation in the question:

> A rank-two sheaf lisse on all of \(\mathbb G_m\), tame and unipotent only at \(0,\infty\), has **generic Mellin rank zero**, not two.

The drop terms at \(0,\infty\) were inserted into the wrong Euler-characteristic formula.

Finally, none of these rank computations proves
\[
S_c=\sum_j e_p(cb_j)=O(\sqrt p).
\]
The values \(b_j\) are Mellin traces indexed by characters; exponentiating those traces is a nonlinear operation that is not the Fourier transform of the rank-two Mellin cohomology.

## 1. What the repository actually constructs

The fixed Laurent polynomial is

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz},
\]

with

\[
\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda^n)=b_n.
\]

The file

```text
problems/3.2/toric_fiber_k3.tex
```

identifies the non-Tate primitive part of the toric K3 fibers as a generic rank-three system. The associated Picard--Fuchs operator is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L
=
\theta^3
-a(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+a^2(\theta+1)^3,
\qquad \theta=a\frac d{da}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Its regular singular points are

\[
0,\quad \infty,\quad
\alpha,\beta,
\qquad
\alpha,\beta\text{ the roots of }a^2-34a+1.
\]

The point \(a=1\), although it has a special point-count correction in the toric model, is a Noether--Lefschetz/specialization point rather than an extra singular point of the rank-three Picard--Fuchs equation.

The repository's symmetric-square file

```text
problems/3.2/toric_fiber_sym2.tex
```

constructs the quadratic cover

\[
8x^2+(a-1)x+a=0
\]

and the elliptic curve

\[
E_x:\quad Y^2+(1-2x)XY+x^2Y=X^3,
\]

with a rational point of exact order six and a six-isogeny. On the Kummer tensor product one gets a rank-four piece; the graph of the isogeny supplies one Tate line, and its orthogonal complement is the rank-three transcendental K3 system.

This is **not** a decomposition of the K3 system into \(\mu _6\)-eigenspaces. The datum of a rational point of order six gives a cyclic isogeny, not a generic automorphism by \(\mu _6\). Translation by a torsion point acts trivially on \(H^1\) of an elliptic curve. Thus the statement

\[
\text{``take the }\mu _6\text{-eigenspace and obtain a rank-two sheaf }M\text{''}
\]

is not a theorem presently established in the repository.

There is a rank-two elliptic local system on the quadratic cover, whose symmetric square/Asai descent produces the rank-three K3 system. That rank-two system is a different object, on a different base, and it is not the character-indexed Mellin transform of the Apéry coefficients.

## 2. Exact local monodromy of the rank-three K3 system

The Riemann scheme of (1) is

\[
\left\{
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &0&\alpha&\beta&\infty\\ \hline
 &0&0&0&1\\
 &0&\tfrac12&\tfrac12&1\\
 &0&1&1&1
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently the geometric local monodromy types are:

| point | local type on the rank-three system \(\mathcal V\) | invariant dimension | drop |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(0\) | one principal unipotent block \(J_3(1)\) | \(1\) | \(2\) |
| \(\infty\) | one principal unipotent block \(J_3(1)\) | \(1\) | \(2\) |
| \(\alpha\) | Picard--Lefschetz reflection, eigenvalues \((1,1,-1)\) | \(2\) | \(1\) |
| \(\beta\) | Picard--Lefschetz reflection, eigenvalues \((1,1,-1)\) | \(2\) | \(1\) |

All four points are tame at good primes. Thus every Swan conductor is zero.

The two MUM points are indeed \(0\) and \(\infty\), but the two reflection points cannot be discarded in a Mellin rank calculation. They are exactly what produces the nonzero generic Mellin rank.

## 3. The correct generic Mellin-rank formula

Let

\[
U=\mathbb G_m-\{\alpha,\beta\},
\qquad
j:U\hookrightarrow\mathbb G_m,
\]

and let

\[
K=(j_*\mathcal V)[1]=j_{!*}(\mathcal V[1])
\]

be the middle-extension perverse sheaf on \(\mathbb G_m\).

For a multiplicative character \(\chi\), let \(\mathcal L_\chi\) be the corresponding Kummer sheaf. The Mellin fiber is

\[
\mathfrak M(K)_\chi
=
R\Gamma_c\!\left(
\mathbb G_m,
K\otimes\mathcal L_\chi
\right).
\tag{3}
\]

For a general rank-\(r\) middle-extension sheaf \(\mathcal F\) on \(\mathbb G_m\), lisse away from a finite set \(S\subset\mathbb G_m\), Grothendieck--Ogg--Shafarevich gives the generic Mellin rank

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{grank}\mathfrak M(\mathcal F[1])
=
\operatorname{Swan}_0(\mathcal F)
+
\operatorname{Swan}_\infty(\mathcal F)
+
\sum_{x\in S}
\left(
\operatorname{Swan}_x(\mathcal F)
+r-\dim \mathcal F^{I_x}
\right).
}
\tag{4}
\]

The important feature is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{drops at }0,\infty\text{ do not occur in (4); only their Swan conductors do.}
}
\]

The reason is transparent. On

\[
U=\mathbb G_m-S
\]

one has

\[
\chi_c(U,\mathcal F)
=r\chi_c(U)-\sum_{x\in S\cup\{0,\infty\}}\operatorname{Swan}_x(\mathcal F),
\]

and \(\chi_c(U)=-|S|\). Passing from extension by zero on \(U\) to the middle extension on \(\mathbb G_m\) adds the invariant stalk \(\mathcal F^{I_x}\) at each finite point \(x\in S\). This yields (4).

Equivalently, after a generic Kummer twist, one can compactify to \(\mathbb P^1\). The twist kills the invariant vectors at \(0,\infty\), while the two finite reflection stalks retain dimension two.

## 4. Applying the formula to the Apéry K3 sheaf

For \(\mathcal V\):

- \(r=3\);
- \(S=\{\alpha,\beta\}\);
- all Swan conductors are zero;
- \(3-\dim \mathcal V^{I_\alpha}=3-2=1\);
- \(3-\dim \mathcal V^{I_\beta}=3-2=1\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{grank}\mathfrak M(K)=1+1=2.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Here is the same calculation directly from Euler characteristic. Since \(U\) is the projective line minus four points,

\[
\chi_c(U)=2-4=-2.
\]

The lisse rank-three cohomology on \(U\) gives

\[
\chi_c(U,\mathcal V\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)=3(-2)=-6.
\]

At \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), middle extension adds invariant stalks of dimensions two and two. Hence

\[
\chi_c(\mathbb G_m,j_*\mathcal V\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
=-6+2+2=-2.
\]

For a generic character there are no \(H_c^0\) or \(H_c^2\) terms, so

\[
\dim H_c^1
=-\chi_c
=2.
\]

This also identifies the overcount in the earlier fixed-exponent Mellin note in the repository: the number

\[
3(4-2)=6
\]

is the cohomological dimension before restoring the two middle-extension stalks. The complete middle-extension Mellin trace loses four dimensions:

\[
\boxed{6-(2+2)=2.}
\]

Thus the exact linear Mellin bound is, before Tate normalization,

\[
\left|
\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
\theta_p(a)\chi(a)
\right|
\le 2p^{3/2}
\tag{6}
\]

for a generic nonresonant character, up to the usual normalization/sign convention. After twisting the weight-two K3 system by \((1)\), this becomes a rank-two weight-one bound of size \(2\sqrt p\).

That is the valid square-root statement supplied by rank two. It concerns a **linear Mellin transform of the K3 trace function**.

## 5. Why the rank-two/two-MUM calculation in the question is not correct

Suppose, literally, that \(M\) were a rank-two sheaf lisse on all of \(\mathbb G_m\), tame at \(0,\infty\), and with one unipotent block at each boundary. There would be no finite singular set \(S\).

Formula (4) would then give

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{grank}\mathfrak M(M[1])=0.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed, the correct open-curve GOS formula is

\[
\chi_c(\mathbb G_m,M)
=2\chi_c(\mathbb G_m)
-\operatorname{Swan}_0(M)
-\operatorname{Swan}_\infty(M)
=0.
\]

For a generic Kummer twist, \(H_c^0=H_c^2=0\), so \(H_c^1=0\).

The formula used in the question,

\[
2\chi_c(\mathbb G_m)-\operatorname{drop}_0-\operatorname{drop}_\infty,
\]

mixes two different Euler characteristics:

- for \(\chi_c(U,\mathcal F)\) on an open curve, GOS subtracts **Swan conductors**, not tame drops;
- the full Artin conductors \(\operatorname{Swan}+\operatorname{drop}\) occur in the Euler characteristic of the middle extension on a compactification.

So the proposed rank-two source with only two tame MUM points cannot have generic Mellin rank two. The actual Apéry rank two in (5) is produced by the two finite reflection singularities of the rank-three K3 system.

## 6. There is no \(\mu _6\)-eigenspace rank-two Apéry Mellin sheaf in the current construction

The order-six geometry in `toric_fiber_sym2.tex` consists of:

1. an elliptic curve with a rational point of exact order six;
2. a cyclic six-isogeny;
3. an algebraic correspondence whose graph supplies a Tate class in a rank-four Kummer tensor product;
4. a rank-three orthogonal complement.

A rational cyclic subgroup does not provide a generic \(\mu _6\)-action on the transcendental cohomology. In particular, translation by the torsion section acts trivially on the elliptic \(H^1\). Therefore no nontrivial \(\mu _6\)-eigenspace of dimension two is produced by this argument.

The rank-two elliptic local system on the quadratic cover is real and useful, but:

- it is on the \(x\)-cover, not on the character index line;
- its symmetric square/Asai descent is the rank-three K3 object;
- its singular set is not captured merely by saying ``unipotent at \(0\) and \(\infty\)'';
- its Mellin transform is not the sequence \(j\mapsto b_j\).

Thus any separate rank-two object called \(M\) needs a new definition and proof before its Fourier rank or local monodromy can be computed.

## 7. Additive local Fourier transform is not multiplicative Mellin transform

Laumon's Fourier--Deligne transform is

\[
\operatorname{FT}_\psi(K)
=
Rp_{2!}
\left(
 p_1^*K\otimes\mathcal L_{\psi(xx')}
\right)[1]
\tag{8}
\]

on the additive affine line. Its local stationary-phase functors are

\[
\mathfrak F^{(0,\infty')},
\qquad
\mathfrak F^{(\infty,0')},
\qquad
\mathfrak F^{(\infty,\infty')}.
\]

These are the local Fourier transforms studied by Laumon and calculated explicitly by Fu. Their kernel is the additive Artin--Schreier sheaf \(\mathcal L_{\psi(xx')}\).

The multiplicative Mellin fiber (3), by contrast, uses Kummer twists

\[
K\longmapsto R\Gamma_c(\mathbb G_m,K\otimes\mathcal L_\chi).
\]

Gabber--Loeser's universal Mellin transform packages these fibers as a coherent complex over a universal character algebra. It is not, in general, a lisse \(\ell\)-adic sheaf on a finite-type dual curve.

Therefore the phrase

\[
\text{``local monodromy of }\operatorname{FT}_{\rm mult}(M)
\text{ at }0^\vee,\infty^\vee\text{''}
\]

has no canonical meaning for the actual Mellin transform used here.

One may fix characters of a bounded order \(N\), obtaining a finite zero-dimensional character scheme. Such a scheme has no boundary points. Letting \(N\) vary gives a pro-character space, not an ordinary algebraic \(\mathbb G_m^\vee\) to which Laumon's inertia formalism applies.

The source monodromy eigenvalues do determine **resonant characters** and cohomology-jump loci. In the Apéry K3 case, the boundary monodromy at \(0,\infty\) has only eigenvalue one, so the trivial Kummer character is the obvious resonance. This is the closest legitimate analogue of boundary information on the Mellin side. It is not a pair of dual-boundary Jordan forms.

## 8. What happens if one really applies the additive Fourier transform?

For comparison, let \(M\) be a rank-two sheaf on \(\mathbb G_m\), tame at \(0,\infty\), and consider the extension-by-zero additive transform. At a nonzero dual parameter \(c\), its fiber is controlled by

\[
R\Gamma_c
\left(
\mathbb G_m,
M\otimes\mathcal L_{\psi(cx)}
\right).
\]

The Artin--Schreier factor has slope one at \(\infty\). Tensoring with a tame rank-two sheaf gives Swan conductor two there. Therefore, under the usual absence of \(H_c^0,H_c^2\),

\[
\dim H_c^1=2.
\]

So an additive Fourier transform of an extension-by-zero rank-two tame sheaf can indeed have generic rank two. But this rank comes from

\[
\operatorname{Swan}_\infty
\left(M\otimes\mathcal L_{\psi(cx)}\right)=2,
\]

not from the two tame drops in the question.

Its local monodromy is governed by Laumon's local Fourier functors, and depends on:

- whether one uses \(j_!\), \(j_*\), or \(j_{!*}\);
- the complete local representation, not just the number of invariant vectors;
- the separation of invariant/punctual summands for the eigenvalue-one unipotent blocks;
- Fourier normalization, inversion, Gauss-sum twist, and Tate twist.

This additive object is not the Apéry character-index Mellin transform and does not have trace function \(j\mapsto b_j\).

## 9. Why the rank-two Mellin result does not imply \(S_c=O(\sqrt p)\)

The linear Mellin traces are

\[
M_p(\chi_j)
=
\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
\theta_p(a)\chi_j(a),
\]

and their reductions are related to \(b_j\). For each \(j\), rank two describes the two-dimensional cohomology space producing this scalar.

But

\[
S_c
=
\sum_j e_p\!\left(c b_j\right)
	ag{9}
\]

performs two operations not covered by that rank computation:

1. it varies through all Kummer characters \(\chi_j\);
2. it applies an additive character to the resulting Frobenius trace.

The spaces

\[
H_c^1
\left(
\mathbb G_m,
K\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_j}
\right)
\]

do not currently assemble in the repository into a lisse rank-two sheaf on a fixed algebraic curve whose \(\mathbb F_p\)-points are the exponents \(j\).

More fundamentally, even when \(t_{\mathcal M}(x)\) is the trace function of a lisse sheaf, it is merely a function on finite-field points. It is not generally the evaluation of a regular morphism

\[
t_{\mathcal M}:X\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1.
\]

Thus there is no canonical pullback

\[
t_{\mathcal M}^*\mathcal L_\psi
\]

whose trace is \(\psi(t_{\mathcal M}(x))\). Earlier answers in this wave used the phrase ``Artin--Schreier pullback along the trace morphism''; that phrase is only legitimate after an actual bounded-complexity algebraic trace morphism has been constructed. No such morphism is presently available for \(j\mapsto b_j\).

One can always sheafify an arbitrary finite function at the cost of complexity growing with \(p\). That is precisely what does not yield a uniform Deligne bound.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{grank}\mathfrak M(K)=2
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
|S_c|\ll\sqrt p.
}
\tag{10}
\]

The missing statement would be a new bounded-complexity sheafification of the defining-characteristic phase

\[
j\longmapsto e_p(cb_j),
\]

or a direct complete-sum theorem for that phase.

## 10. Even a square-root bound for every \(S_c\) would give only \(O(\sqrt p)\) zeros

Let

\[
Z_p=\#\{0\le j<p:b_j\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

Additive orthogonality gives

\[
pZ_p
=
\sum_{c\in\mathbb F_p}S_c
=
p+\sum_{c\ne0}S_c.
\tag{11}
\]

Therefore a uniform estimate

\[
|S_c|\le C\sqrt p
\qquad(c\ne0)
\]

implies only

\[
Z_p\le 1+C\sqrt p.
\tag{12}
\]

It does not imply \(Z_p=O(1)\) without additional cancellation in the outer \(c\)-sum. This still improves the current pointwise \(p^{2/3}\)-scale bound, but it does not by itself reach the Poisson-size zero count or solve the cross-prime problem.

|---|---:|---|---|

## Final conclusion

The exact rank relevant to the repository's Apéry Mellin coefficient is

\[
\boxed{2},
\]

but the correct source is the rank-three K3 middle-extension sheaf, and the rank is

\[
\boxed{
(3-2)+(3-2)=2
}
\]

from the two finite Picard--Lefschetz reflection points.

The two MUM boundary drops do **not** produce this rank. A literal rank-two tame sheaf with only the two MUM points would have generic Mellin rank zero.

There is no presently defined lisse sheaf \(\operatorname{FT}_{\rm mult}(M)\) on an algebraic \(\mathbb G_m^\vee\) with local monodromy at \(0^\vee,\infty^\vee\). Applying Katz--Laumon stationary phase here confuses additive Fourier transform with multiplicative Mellin transform.

Most importantly, the exact rank-two Mellin computation does not furnish the nonlinear phase sheaf required for

\[
S_c=\sum_j e_p(cb_j).
\]

The strictly narrower residual is:

> Construct, uniformly in \(p\) and \(c\ne0\), a bounded-complexity geometric object whose trace function on the character-index set is \(j\mapsto e_p(cb_j)\), or prove an equivalent complete-sum estimate directly.

Until that new construction exists, stationary phase for the K3 Mellin transform does not prove \(S_c=O(\sqrt p)\).
