# Q382: Modular Turán–Kubilius for additive modular functions

## Executive verdict

There is a clean answer, but it has an important negative part.

1. **There is no universal Turán–Kubilius inequality of the desired strength based only on centering and small support.** Even singleton sets \(Z_p\) can be arranged so that all congruence conditions meet at one integer, giving a second moment of order \(|\mathcal P|^2\).

2. The natural unconditional analogue is the **dual large sieve**:
   
   \[
   \sum_{n\le N}\left|\sum_{p\le Q}h_p(n\bmod p)\right|^2
   \ll (N+Q^2)\sum_p \frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}|h_p(a)|^2.
   \]
   
   This gives a genuine modular Turán–Kubilius theorem when \(Q\lesssim \sqrt N\), but it is useless in the present range \(p\in(\sqrt N,N]\). The \(Q^2\) barrier is not merely a defect of the proof: a sparse aligned example forces a constant of size nearly \(N^2\).

3. The signed cancellation can be formalized **exactly**. However, after expanding the centered indicators, all positive/negative-background terms reduce to harmless one-modulus boundary errors. The remaining hard term is the aggregate CRT occupancy of the actual positive supports \(Z_p\times Z_q\). Thus the sign pattern alone gives no cancellation.

4. Selberg–Delange and Halász are not directly applicable because the functions here are periodic-coordinate sums, not multiplicative functions of \(n\). The related tool is the large sieve, and possibly the Hilbert-space Halász–Montgomery inequality, but those tools only reorganize the same correlation problem.

5. The problem is naturally a **restricted second-order discrepancy / spectral discrepancy / hypergraph codegree** problem. It is not standard signing discrepancy. For P3.2, the cleanest minimal target is a one-vector CRT-dispersion estimate, or even the positive pair-incidence bound
   
   \[
   \sum_{p<q}R_{pq}\ll N,
   \]
   
   where \(R_{pq}\) is the number of \(n\le N\) hitting both \(Z_p\pmod p\) and \(Z_q\pmod q\).

The repository's current description of the missing “occupancy-one CRT discrepancy” is therefore exactly the right diagnosis. There is no off-the-shelf modular TK theorem that removes it.

# 1. Exact notation

Let

\[
I_N=\{1,\dots,N\},\qquad
X_p(n)=\mathbf 1_{\,n\bmod p\in Z_p},
\]

and put

\[
\alpha_p=\frac{z_p}{p},\qquad
h_p(n)=X_p(n)-\alpha_p,\qquad
\lambda=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\alpha_p.
\]

Thus

\[
B(n)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}X_p(n),
\qquad
f(n)=B(n)-\lambda=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}h_p(n).
\]

Define the one-prime counts and boundary errors by

\[
A_p=\sum_{n\le N}X_p(n)=|\{n\le N:n\bmod p\in Z_p\}|,
\]

\[
d_p=A_p-N\alpha_p.
\]

Every residue class modulo \(p\) occurs either \(\lfloor N/p\rfloor\) or \(\lceil N/p\rceil\) times in \(I_N\), so

\[
|d_p|\le z_p.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For \(p\ne q\), define

\[
R_{pq}=\sum_{n\le N}X_p(n)X_q(n)
       =|\{n\le N:n\bmod p\in Z_p,\ n\bmod q\in Z_q\}|,
\]

and its product-density discrepancy

\[
\Delta_{pq}=R_{pq}-N\alpha_p\alpha_q.
\tag{1.2}
\]

In the range \(p,q>\sqrt N\), one has \(pq>N\), so each pair of residues \((a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q\) contributes either zero or one integer to \(R_{pq}\).

# 2. The exact modular second-moment identity

The key calculation is elementary but decisive.

## Lemma 2.1: exact pair covariance

For \(p\ne q\),

\[
\sum_{n\le N}h_p(n)h_q(n)
=\Delta_{pq}-\alpha_qd_p-\alpha_pd_q.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{n\le N}h_p(n)h_q(n)
=R_{pq}-\alpha_qA_p-\alpha_pA_q+N\alpha_p\alpha_q.
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Expand the product:

\[
(X_p-\alpha_p)(X_q-\alpha_q)
=X_pX_q-\alpha_qX_p-\alpha_pX_q+\alpha_p\alpha_q.
\]

Summing over \(n\le N\) gives (2.2), and substituting
\(A_p=N\alpha_p+d_p\) and \(A_q=N\alpha_q+d_q\) gives (2.1). ∎

There is also a useful empirical-marginal form:

\[
\sum_{n\le N}h_p(n)h_q(n)
=\left(R_{pq}-\frac{A_pA_q}{N}\right)+\frac{d_pd_q}{N}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the pair term is the ordinary empirical covariance of the two sets in \(I_N\), plus a small correction caused by centering with \(z_p/p\) rather than the exact finite-interval density \(A_p/N\).

## Lemma 2.2: exact total identity

Let

\[
D_1=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}d_p.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le N}f(n)^2
=N\sum_p\alpha_p(1-\alpha_p)
 +(1-2\lambda)D_1
 +2\sum_{p<q}\Delta_{pq}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

The off-diagonal part alone is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p<q}\sum_{n\le N}h_p(n)h_q(n)
=\sum_{p<q}\Delta_{pq}
 -\sum_p d_p(\lambda-\alpha_p).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

### Proof

For the diagonal,

\[
\sum_{n\le N}h_p(n)^2
=N\alpha_p(1-\alpha_p)+(1-2\alpha_p)d_p.
\]

Sum this over \(p\), add twice (2.1), and use

\[
\sum_{p<q}(\alpha_qd_p+\alpha_pd_q)
=\sum_p d_p(\lambda-\alpha_p).
\]

The coefficient of \(d_p\) becomes

\[
(1-2\alpha_p)-2(\lambda-\alpha_p)=1-2\lambda.
\]

This proves (2.4) and (2.5). ∎

These identities are the precise form of the proposed signed cancellation.

# 3. What happens to the positive and negative values of \(h_p\)?

For \(pq>N\), write

\[
K_{pq}(a,b)
=\#\{n\le N:n\equiv a\pmod p,\ n\equiv b\pmod q\}.
\]

Then \(K_{pq}(a,b)\in\{0,1\}\), and

\[
\sum_{n\le N}h_p(n)h_q(n)
=\sum_{a\bmod p}\sum_{b\bmod q}
 h_p(a)h_q(b)K_{pq}(a,b).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because \(h_p\) and \(h_q\) are centered, one may equivalently replace \(K_{pq}\) by

\[
K_{pq}(a,b)-\frac{N}{pq}.
\]

Now expand \(h_p=\mathbf 1_{Z_p}-\alpha_p\) and
\(h_q=\mathbf 1_{Z_q}-\alpha_q\). One obtains

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a,b}h_p(a)h_q(b)K_{pq}(a,b)
={}&\sum_{a\in Z_p,b\in Z_q}K_{pq}(a,b)\\
&-\alpha_q\sum_{a\in Z_p,b}K_{pq}(a,b)\\
&-\alpha_p\sum_{a,b\in Z_q}K_{pq}(a,b)\\
&+\alpha_p\alpha_q\sum_{a,b}K_{pq}(a,b).
\end{aligned}
\]

The four terms are exactly

\[
R_{pq}-\alpha_qA_p-\alpha_pA_q+N\alpha_p\alpha_q.
\]

Therefore:

- the positive-positive cells produce \(R_{pq}\);
- the positive-negative and negative-positive cells produce only the one-prime marginals \(A_p,A_q\);
- the negative-negative background produces the constant \(N\alpha_p\alpha_q\).

After the one-prime counts are written as \(A_p=N\alpha_p+d_p\), all of the negative-background contribution becomes the boundary correction

\[
-\alpha_qd_p-\alpha_pd_q.
\]

So the sign pattern does **not** supply a free probabilistic cancellation. It performs a deterministic algebraic centering, after which the hard term is

\[
\Delta_{pq}=R_{pq}-\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq}.
\]

This is important for the project: trying to extract further cancellation merely from the facts

\[
h_p>0\text{ on }Z_p,\qquad h_p<0\text{ off }Z_p
\]

cannot work. The same sign pattern occurs in the counterexample below, where every pair correlation is large and positive.

# 4. A positive-incidence form that avoids signed cancellation

Let

\[
\mathcal I_2(N)=\sum_{p<q}R_{pq}.
\]

Since \(R_{pq}=|S_p\cap S_q|\), where

\[
S_p=\{n\le N:n\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\]

one has the exact double-counting identity

\[
\mathcal I_2(N)
=\sum_{n\le N}\binom{B(n)}2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le N}(B(n)-\lambda)^2
=2\mathcal I_2(N)+N\lambda(1-\lambda)+(1-2\lambda)D_1.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This follows directly from

\[
B(n)^2=B(n)+2\binom{B(n)}2
\]

and

\[
\sum_{n\le N}B(n)=N\lambda+D_1.
\]

Equation (4.2) gives a particularly clean sufficient condition.

## Positive HM2 criterion

Suppose

\[
\lambda=O(1),\qquad \sum_pz_p=o(N),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_2(N)=\sum_{p<q}R_{pq}\ll N.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{n\le N}(B(n)-\lambda)^2\ll N.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For \(p\in(\sqrt N,N]\) and \(z_p\le Z\), standard prime counting gives

\[
\sum_pz_p\ll_Z\frac{N}{\log N},
\qquad
\lambda=\sum_p\frac{z_p}{p}=O_Z(1),
\]

so the boundary term in (4.2) is already \(o(N)\).

Thus, for an \(O(N)\) second moment, one need not prove square-root signed cancellation. It is enough to prove that the **total number of pair collisions** is \(O(N)\). This is a nonnegative statement.

The empirical observation that the centered cross-term is \(O(\sqrt N)\) is stronger. By (2.5), its exact formulation is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p<q}\left(R_{pq}-\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq}\right)
-\sum_p d_p\left(\lambda-\frac{z_p}{p}\right)
=O(\sqrt N).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

If this is what the computation measures, the two quantities on the left should be recorded separately. Otherwise a cancellation between the aggregate CRT discrepancy and the one-prime boundary term can be mistaken for pairwise equidistribution.

# 5. Why no universal small-support modular TK theorem can hold

Here is a definitive counterexample.

Fix an integer \(n_0\in[1,N]\). For every \(p\in\mathcal P\), let

\[
Z_p=\{n_0\bmod p\}.
\]

Then \(z_p=1\) for every \(p\), yet all the conditions meet at \(n=n_0\). Hence

\[
B(n_0)=|\mathcal P|
\]

and

\[
f(n_0)=|\mathcal P|-\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac1p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{n\le N}f(n)^2
\ge
\left(|\mathcal P|-\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac1p\right)^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Take \(\mathcal P=\{p:\sqrt N<p\le N\}\). Then

\[
|\mathcal P|\asymp\frac{N}{\log N},
\qquad
\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le N}\frac1p\asymp 1.
\]

Thus

\[
\sum_{n\le N}f(n)^2
\gg\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

On the other hand, the proposed TK diagonal parameter is

\[
N\sum_p\frac1p\left(1-\frac1p\right)=O(N).
\]

So an inequality of the form

\[
\sum_{n\le N}f(n)^2
\le C N\sum_p\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}|h_p(a)|^2
\]

cannot hold uniformly, even when every support has cardinality one.

In this example,

\[
R_{pq}=1
\]

for every pair \(p<q\), and hence

\[
\mathcal I_2(N)=\binom{|\mathcal P|}{2}.
\]

This also proves that the positive/negative sign pattern of the centered indicators cannot force cancellation.

## Contrast with the classical divisibility case

For the classical event \(p\mid n\), the target residue is \(0\pmod p\). If \(p,q>\sqrt N\), then no positive \(n\le N\) is divisible by \(pq\), so

\[
R_{pq}=0.
\]

That exclusion is much stronger than approximate independence. It is a special consequence of the common zero residue and multiplicative divisibility. For arbitrary shifted residue sets, the unique CRT solution can lie anywhere in \([1,pq]\), including inside \([1,N]\), and many prime-indexed conditions may be deliberately aligned at the same integer.

This is the fundamental reason classical Turán–Kubilius does not transfer formally to arbitrary modular summands.

# 6. The actual universal theorem: the dual large sieve

Define the normalized Fourier transform on \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) by

\[
\widehat h_p(r)
=\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}h_p(a)e(-ra/p),
\qquad e(x)=e^{2\pi i x}.
\]

Since \(h_p\) is centered,

\[
\widehat h_p(0)=0,
\]

and Fourier inversion gives

\[
h_p(n\bmod p)
=\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}\widehat h_p(r)e(rn/p).
\]

Consequently,

\[
f(n)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}
\widehat h_p(r)e(rn/p).
\tag{6.1}
\]

If every \(p\le Q\), the reduced fractions \(r/p\) appearing in (6.1) are distinct modulo one and are separated by at least \(Q^{-2}\). The classical large sieve therefore yields

\[
\sum_{n\le N}|f(n)|^2
\ll (N+Q^2)
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}|\widehat h_p(r)|^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

By Parseval,

\[
\sum_{r\bmod p}|\widehat h_p(r)|^2
=\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}|h_p(a)|^2.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le N}|f(n)|^2
\ll (N+Q^2)
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}|h_p(a)|^2.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For a centered indicator,

\[
\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}|h_p(a)|^2
=\alpha_p(1-\alpha_p)
=\frac{z_p}{p}\left(1-\frac{z_p}{p}\right).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{n\le N}|f(n)|^2
\ll (N+Q^2)
\sum_p\frac{z_p}{p}\left(1-\frac{z_p}{p}\right).
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is the correct general “modular Turán–Kubilius inequality.”

- If \(Q\le \sqrt N\), then \(N+Q^2\ll N\), and (6.5) has exactly the desired TK scale.
- If \(Q\asymp N\), then \(N+Q^2\asymp N^2\), which is fatal.

The square-root threshold is therefore structural: below it, the ordinary large sieve solves the modular problem; above it, the interval is shorter than a full pair modulus and the occupancy-one CRT geometry appears.

## Why small support does not improve the Fourier bound

For \(r\ne0\), the constant term in \(h_p\) has zero Fourier transform, so

\[
\widehat h_p(r)
=\frac1p\sum_{a\in Z_p}e(-ra/p).
\tag{6.6}
\]

A small support in residue space generally gives a **dense** Fourier transform. In the singleton case \(Z_p=\{a_p\}\),

\[
|\widehat h_p(r)|=\frac1p
\]

for every \(r\ne0\). Thus physical-space sparsity is not frequency-space sparsity; it does not remove the family of nearly \(Q^2\)-spaced rational frequencies.

The aligned-singleton example shows more: any universal support-sensitive large-sieve constant for the present class must be at least

\[
\gg \frac{N^2}{\log^2N}
\]

in the range \(p\in(\sqrt N,N]\). Hence logarithmic refinements of a sparse-moduli large sieve cannot reach the required \(O(N)\) scale without an additional arithmetic hypothesis on the sets \(Z_p\).

# 7. Fourier form of the desired signed cancellation

For the Apéry indicators, define

\[
S_p(r)=\sum_{a\in Z_p}e(ra/p).
\]

By (6.6), for \(r\ne0\),

\[
\widehat h_p(r)=\frac{S_p(-r)}{p}.
\]

Let

\[
K_N(\theta)=\sum_{n\le N}e(n\theta).
\]

Then the exact pair correlation is

\[
\sum_{n\le N}h_p(n)h_q(n)
=\frac1{pq}
\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1}^{q-1}
S_p(-r)S_q(-s)
K_N\left(\frac rp+\frac sq\right).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The Dirichlet kernel satisfies

\[
|K_N(\theta)|\ll \min\left(N,\frac1{\|\theta\|}\right).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Formula (7.1) is a rigorous analytic realization of the signed cancellation. However:

- taking absolute values in \(r,s,p,q\) loses the cancellation and returns to the large-sieve barrier;
- pointwise bounds for \(S_p(r)\) alone generally do not control the many near-resonances
  
  \[
  \frac rp+\frac sq\approx 0\pmod1;
  \]
- what is needed is an **averaged bilinear estimate** over \(p,q,r,s\), or an equivalent spectral estimate for the whole block Gram matrix.

Thus a trace-function, Poisson, Kuznetsov, or spectral route would have to exploit genuine structure of the Apéry zero-set transforms \(S_p(r)\). The generic Fourier formalism identifies the target but does not prove the required cancellation.

Under a fully independent Bernoulli model for the \(X_p(n)\), the aggregate cross-term has mean zero and \(O(\sqrt N)\) is the natural fluctuation scale when \(\lambda=O(1)\). That explains the empirical observation heuristically. The actual modular system has strong deterministic dependence in \(n\), so this random-model prediction is not itself an argument.

# 8. What a true modular TK theorem would look like

There are two different possible targets.

## 8.1 The coefficient-uniform operator inequality

A full analogue of the dual Turán–Kubilius inequality would assert that for every set of coefficients \(c_p\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le N}
\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}c_ph_p(n\bmod p)\right|^2
\ll
N\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}|c_p|^2\alpha_p(1-\alpha_p).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

For arbitrary small-support sets, (8.1) is false by the aligned-singleton example. For the fixed Apéry sets, it is a meaningful **restricted large-sieve conjecture**.

But (8.1) is stronger than P3.2 presently needs: it controls every direction \(c=(c_p)\), while the second moment in the question uses only \(c_p=1\).

## 8.2 The one-vector inequality

The minimal statement is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le N}
\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}h_p(n\bmod p)\right|^2
\ll N.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

By (2.4), this is equivalent, up to the already controlled boundary term, to the single aggregate quadratic-form estimate

\[
\sum_{p<q}\Delta_{pq}\ll N.
\tag{8.3}
\]

By (4.2), a particularly robust sufficient form is the positive incidence estimate

\[
\sum_{p<q}R_{pq}\ll N.
\tag{8.4}
\]

For the current project, (8.4) is the cleanest HM2 target. It neither asks for a uniform operator theorem nor relies on delicate cancellation between positive and negative pair discrepancies.

The stronger empirical square-root assertion is

\[
\sum_{p<q}\Delta_{pq}
-\sum_p d_p(\lambda-\alpha_p)
\ll N^{1/2+o(1)}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

This would give the random-model asymptotic for the cross-term, but it is more than is needed for an \(O(N)\) second moment.

A compact project formulation is:

```text
AMTK-SS(N), minimal one-vector form:

Let P_N = {p prime : sqrt(N) < p <= N},
alpha_p = z_p/p,
d_p = #{n <= N : n mod p in Z_p} - N alpha_p,
and
R_pq = #{n <= N : n mod p in Z_p and n mod q in Z_q}.

It suffices to prove either

    sum_{p<q} R_pq << N,

or, equivalently at the required scale,

    sum_{p<q} (R_pq - N alpha_p alpha_q) << N.

The observed square-root cancellation is the stronger statement

    sum_{p<q} (R_pq - N alpha_p alpha_q)
      - sum_p d_p (lambda - alpha_p)
    << N^(1/2+o(1)).
```

# 9. Selberg–Delange and Halász

## 9.1 Selberg–Delange

Selberg–Delange applies to Dirichlet series whose coefficients are multiplicative, with an Euler-product singularity that can be analyzed near \(s=1\). The standard model is something such as

\[
z^{\omega(n)},
\]

whose Dirichlet series has an Euler product because the condition \(p\mid n\) is multiplicative.

Here

\[
X_p(n)=\mathbf 1_{\,n\bmod p\in Z_p}
\]

is not a multiplicative condition in \(n\), and neither \(B(n)\) nor \(e^{itB(n)}\) is generally multiplicative. There is therefore no natural Euler product to which Selberg–Delange applies.

There is a formal moment expansion. For any \(y\),

\[
y^{B(n)}
=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}\left(1+(y-1)X_p(n)\right),
\]

so

\[
\sum_{n\le N}y^{B(n)}
=\sum_{J\subseteq\mathcal P}(y-1)^{|J|}A_J(N),
\tag{9.1}
\]

where

\[
A_J(N)=\#\{n\le N:n\bmod p\in Z_p\text{ for every }p\in J\}.
\]

If one already knew

\[
A_J(N)\approx N\prod_{p\in J}\alpha_p
\]

for the relevant \(J\), then (9.1) would resemble an Euler-product probabilistic model. But in the present range, already for \(|J|=2\) the product modulus exceeds \(N\), and \(A_J(N)\) is an occupancy-zero-or-one CRT quantity. Proving the needed approximation for \(A_J\) is exactly the missing discrepancy theorem. Selberg–Delange does not supply it.

The **Selberg sieve**, as opposed to Selberg–Delange, can sometimes give upper bounds for high-multiplicity sets. But it likewise requires suitable local intersection estimates; it cannot infer them from \(z_p=O(1)\) alone.

## 9.2 Halász

Halász's mean-value theorem concerns bounded multiplicative functions. The function

\[
n\longmapsto e^{itB(n)}
\]

is not multiplicative for general \(Z_p\), so the usual pretentious/Halász machinery does not apply.

There is a differently named tool, the **Halász–Montgomery inequality**, which is a Hilbert-space quasi-orthogonality estimate and is closely related to the dual large sieve. It can organize a proof in terms of the Gram matrix

\[
G_{pq}=\sum_{n\le N}h_p(n)h_q(n).
\]

But it still requires bounds for the off-diagonal entries or their square average. In other words, it can turn pair-correlation information into an operator bound; it does not create the pair-correlation information.

So the correct connection is:

- not Selberg–Delange;
- not the multiplicative Halász theorem;
- yes to large-sieve/Hilbert-space duality, conditional on an Apéry-specific correlation input.

# 10. Discrepancy-theory formulations

Yes, there are several exact discrepancy formulations.

## 10.1 Product-space discrepancy

Let

\[
\Omega=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z
\]

with product uniform measure \(\mu\), and let

\[
\mu_N=\frac1N\sum_{n\le N}
\delta_{(n\bmod p)_{p\in\mathcal P}}.
\]

Under the product measure \(\mu\), centered functions on different coordinates are exactly orthogonal:

\[
\int_\Omega h_p(x_p)h_q(x_q)\,d\mu=0
\qquad(p\ne q).
\]

Hence

\[
\int_\Omega\left|\sum_ph_p(x_p)\right|^2d\mu
=\sum_p\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}|h_p(a)|^2.
\]

Under the empirical measure,

\[
\int_\Omega\left|\sum_ph_p(x_p)\right|^2d\mu_N
=\frac1N\sum_{n\le N}f(n)^2.
\]

A modular TK theorem therefore says that \(\mu_N\) behaves like the product measure on the degree-two test class generated by the selected coordinate functions \(h_p\).

This is a restricted approximate-orthogonality or approximate-two-design statement. It cannot hold for all small-support coordinate tests, because the aligned-singleton example selects a cylinder through one atom of \(\mu_N\).

## 10.2 Bipartite CRT graph discrepancy

For each pair \(p,q\), form the \(p\times q\) matrix

\[
K_{pq}(a,b)
=\#\{n\le N:n\equiv a\pmod p,\ n\equiv b\pmod q\}.
\]

When \(pq>N\), this is the adjacency matrix of a bipartite graph with \(N\) edges and no repeated CRT edge. Then

\[
R_{pq}=\mathbf 1_{Z_p}^{\,T}K_{pq}\mathbf 1_{Z_q},
\]

and

\[
\Delta_{pq}
=\mathbf 1_{Z_p}^{\,T}
\left(K_{pq}-\frac{N}{pq}J\right)
\mathbf 1_{Z_q}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Thus \(\Delta_{pq}\) is the edge discrepancy of the small rectangle \(Z_p\times Z_q\).

This resembles an expander-mixing or cut-norm problem. But a separate per-pair cut-norm estimate is generally too expensive when summed over roughly \(N^2/\log^2N\) pairs. The project needs an **average block discrepancy** or a global bilinear estimate across \(p,q\).

## 10.3 Hypergraph degree discrepancy

On the vertex set \(I_N\), take one hyperedge

\[
S_p=\{n\le N:n\bmod p\in Z_p\}
\]

for each prime \(p\). Then

\[
B(n)=\deg(n),
\]

and

\[
R_{pq}=|S_p\cap S_q|.
\]

The desired second moment is the \(L^2\)-regularity of the degree sequence, while

\[
\sum_{p<q}R_{pq}=\sum_n\binom{\deg(n)}2
\]

is the total codegree mass. The aligned-singleton obstruction is a hub of degree \(|\mathcal P|\).

This makes the missing hypothesis very concrete: the Apéry residue hypergraph must have no excessive concentration of incidences at a small set of integers.

## 10.4 Operator/spectral discrepancy

Let

\[
T:\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P}L_0^2(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow \ell^2(I_N)
\]

be the synthesis operator

\[
T((g_p)_p)(n)=\sum_pg_p(n\bmod p).
\]

A coefficient-uniform modular TK theorem is a restricted bound on \(\|T\|_{op}\). The one-vector P3.2 question asks only for the quadratic form of the block Gram matrix \(T^*T\) at the particular vector \((h_p)_p\).

This distinction matters strategically: proving the full operator norm may be much harder than proving the single quadratic-form estimate actually required.

## Relation to classical discrepancy theory

This is not primarily Beck–Fiala/Spencer discrepancy, where one is allowed to choose signs to balance a set system. Here the coefficients are fixed, and the question is whether a deterministic arithmetic incidence system is already pseudorandom. The closest terminology is:

- restricted spectral discrepancy;
- edge/cut discrepancy of CRT graphs;
- large-sieve quasi-orthogonality;
- dispersion;
- hypergraph degree/codegree regularity.

# 11. Direct answers to the four questions

## 1. Is there a modular Turán–Kubilius inequality for small-support \(h_p\)?

There is a general large-sieve inequality

\[
\sum_{n\le N}|f(n)|^2
\ll(N+Q^2)\sum_p\|h_p\|_{2,p}^2.
\]

It gives the desired TK scale only for moduli \(p\lesssim\sqrt N\). In the range \(p>\sqrt N\), no \(O(N)\)-scale theorem follows from centering and \(z_p=O(1)\) alone; the aligned-singleton example disproves it.

For the fixed Apéry sets, one can formulate an Apéry-specific restricted large-sieve or modular-TK conjecture, but that conjecture is essentially the missing CRT-dispersion theorem rather than a known general result.

## 2. Can the signed cancellation be formalized?

Yes, exactly, through (2.1)–(2.5) or through the Fourier formula (7.1). But the sign pattern alone gives only algebraic centering. After that centering, the hard quantity is

\[
R_{pq}-\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq},
\]

summed over pairs, together with a small explicit marginal correction. Any genuine gain must come from distributional structure of the Apéry sets \(Z_p\), not from the mere fact that \(h_p\) has positive and negative values.

For an \(O(N)\) second moment, the positive bound

\[
\sum_{p<q}R_{pq}\ll N
\]

is already sufficient and avoids relying on cancellation.

## 3. Is there a connection to Selberg–Delange or Halász?

Not directly. Selberg–Delange and Halász exploit multiplicativity and Euler products, which are absent here. The formal subset expansion of \(y^{B(n)}\) exposes the CRT intersection numbers \(A_J(N)\), but estimating them is precisely the unresolved problem.

The relevant classical framework is the large sieve and Hilbert-space quasi-orthogonality. The Halász–Montgomery inequality may be useful organizationally, but only after one proves nontrivial Apéry-specific pair-correlation estimates.

## 4. Is this a discrepancy problem?

Yes. It is a restricted second-order discrepancy problem for the orbit

\[
n\mapsto(n\bmod p)_{p\in\mathcal P},
\]

or equivalently an edge-discrepancy problem for the CRT bipartite graphs, or a degree-regularity problem for the incidence hypergraph \(S_p\subseteq[1,N]\).

The correct target is not worst-case discrepancy over all sparse residue sets—that is false—but discrepancy for the specific algebraically defined Apéry zero sets, averaged globally over \(p,q\).

# 12. Project-level conclusion

For P3.2, “modular Turán–Kubilius” should be treated as a **name for the desired output**, not as an available black-box theorem.

The clean hierarchy is:

1. **General theorem:**
   
   \[
   V\ll(N+Q^2)\sum_p\alpha_p(1-\alpha_p).
   \]
   
   This stops at the \(Q^2\) barrier.

2. **Minimal Apéry-specific HM2 theorem:**
   
   \[
   \sum_{p<q}R_{pq}\ll N.
   \]
   
   Together with \(z_p=O(1)\), this gives the desired \(O(N)\) second moment.

3. **Stronger random-model theorem:**
   
   \[
   \sum_{p<q}\Delta_{pq}
   -\sum_pd_p(\lambda-\alpha_p)
   \ll N^{1/2+o(1)}.
   \]
   
   This matches the empirical cross-term but is not necessary merely to obtain \(O(N)\).

4. **Full operator theorem:**
   
   \[
   \sum_{n\le N}\left|\sum_pc_ph_p(n)\right|^2
   \ll N\sum_p|c_p|^2\alpha_p.
   \]
   
   This would be a true restricted large sieve for the Apéry zero sets, but it is stronger than the current one-vector need.

Therefore the honest answer is:

> **No generic modular TK inequality closes the argument. The exact centered algebra reduces the question to an Apéry-specific occupancy-one CRT discrepancy/codegree estimate. Signed cancellation is rigorously expressible, but not automatic; Selberg–Delange and Halász do not bypass it.**
