# Literature audit: cross-characteristic high moments for Apéry zero sets

## Verdict

I found **no published theorem in the searched literature that implies the requested fixed high factorial-moment estimate**

\[
F_k(X):=\sum_{n<X^2}\left(\#\{X<p\le 2X:\ b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p\}\right)_k
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\left(\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\right)^k
\tag{T}
\]

for one fixed \(k>6\), where \(Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}\).

This is not merely because the available estimates are quantitatively weak. The closest bodies of theory operate on the wrong object or in the wrong varying direction:

* Apéry/Lucas/Dwork/automaticity results describe the sequence **one characteristic at a time** and give no relation between the transition data \(Z_p\) and \(Z_q\).
* Frobenius large sieves obtain independence while varying an **auxiliary coefficient prime** over one fixed base finite field; here the base characteristic itself is the varying prime.
* Horizontal Sato--Tate and fixed-motive Chebotarev give one bounded-complexity Frobenius random variable per prime, whereas the present problem uses the coefficient with a prime-dependent index \(r=n\bmod p\) from a length-\(p\) coefficient vector.
* The classical and larger sieves accept arbitrary local residue sets, so anchored residue stars are genuine extremizers unless some additional arithmetic coupling is supplied.
* Prime-divisor theorems for Apéry or holonomic sequences give lower bounds for the number or size of prime divisors for typical indices, not upper-tail estimates for the number of divisors in \((X,2X]\) at indices \(n\le X^2\).

The **closest theorem that genuinely keeps the same integer specialization while varying base primes** is Zywina's larger-sieve quantitative Hilbert irreducibility theorem, Theorem 1.4 of *Hilbert's irreducibility theorem and the larger sieve*. It applies only when all local conditions arise from a single fixed finite cover over a number field, with fixed Galois group and fixed geometric complexity. No such fixed-cover realization of

\[
(r,p)\longmapsto \mathbf 1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}
\]

is known; the universal polynomial interpolation has degree \(O(p)\), and the natural Hasse-invariant/generating-polynomial realizations concern values in a geometric parameter, not zero coefficients indexed by \(r\).

The remainder gives the precise audit and the exact missing hypothesis.

## 1. What a theorem would actually have to prove

Put \(\mathcal P_X=\{p:X<p\le2X\}\), \(z_p=|Z_p|\), and

\[
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{z_p}{p}.
\]

Expanding the falling factorial gives the exact identity

\[
F_k(X)
 =\sum_{\substack{(p_1,\ldots,p_k)\in\mathcal P_X^k\\p_i\ {m distinct}}}
 N(p_1,\ldots,p_k),
\tag{1.1}
\]

where

\[
N(p_1,\ldots,p_k)
 =\#\{0\le n<X^2:n\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i}\ (1\le i\le k)\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The independence prediction for a tuple is

\[
N(p_1,\ldots,p_k)
 \approx X^2\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{z_{p_i}}{p_i}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

For \(k>2\), the joint CRT period

\[
Q=p_1\cdots p_k\asymp X^k
\]

is much larger than the common interval length \(X^2\). Thus (1.3) cannot be obtained by taking complete periods. It is a short-interval occupancy statement for the CRT image of

\[
Z_{p_1}\times\cdots\times Z_{p_k}\subseteq
\prod_i\mathbf F_{p_i}.
\]

A theorem adequate for (T) therefore needs all of the following.

1. **Diagonal coupling:** the same integer \(n\) must be retained across the different reductions \(n\bmod p_i\).
2. **Base-characteristic uniformity:** \(p_i\) themselves vary through \((X,2X]\), rather than serving as auxiliary \(\ell\)-adic coefficient primes over a fixed finite field.
3. **Critical range:** it must work for modulus \(p\asymp X=(X^2)^{1/2}\), exactly the large-sieve transition range.
4. **Fixed high correlation:** it must control a fixed \(k\)-fold correlation, not only a one-prime count or an \(L^2\) average.
5. **Product-density scale:** an additive discrepancy such as \(O(X^{2-\delta})\) per tuple is not enough. The desired right side can be very small when \(\lambda_X\) is small, and some \(z_p\) may be zero. One needs a relative product-density estimate, or a globally summable error whose total is \(o(X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k)\).
6. **Bounded complexity:** any sheaf, cover, polynomial, or automaton used to encode the event must have rank/degree/conductor bounded independently of \(p\), or grow slowly enough that its aggregate error remains below the right side of (T).

None of the results below satisfies this full list.

## 2. The strongest elementary cross-characteristic coupling already available

Gessel's Lucas congruence says that, for every prime \(p\), every \(q\ge0\), and \(0\le r<p\),

\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For \(n<X^2\) and \(p\in(X,2X]\), write \(n=q_pp+r_p\). Then \(0\le q_p<X\), and

\[
p\mid b_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q_p\in Z_p\ \text{or}\ r_p\in Z_p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
r_p=n\bmod p\in Z_p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid b_n,
\tag{2.3}
\]

so

\[
K_X(n):=\#\{p\in\mathcal P_X:r_p\in Z_p\}
 \le \omega_{(X,2X]}(b_n).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Equation (2.4) is a genuine same-index, varying-prime statement. It reduces (T) to a sufficiently strong upper-tail theorem for medium-sized prime divisors of the single integer \(b_n\). No such theorem is known for Apéry numbers.

The size bound alone is far too weak. For example, from the binomial formula one obtains

\[
b_n\le (n+1)64^n,
\]

and hence only

\[
\omega_{(X,2X]}(b_n)\ll \frac{n}{\log X},
\tag{2.5}
\]

which is useless for \(n\asymp X^2\).

There is also an important structural warning. Iterating (2.1), if

\[
n=n_0+n_1p+\cdots+n_sp^s,
\]

then

\[
b_n\equiv\prod_{j=0}^s b_{n_j}\pmod p.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Thus the zero language in base \(p\) is simply: *at least one digit belongs to \(Z_p\)*. This is a two-state acceptance rule once membership in \(Z_p\) is built into the digit transitions, but the transition table contains the entire unconstrained subset \(Z_p\subset\mathbf F_p\). Lucas congruences therefore propagate a local zero into an anchored \(p\)-ary star; they do not decorrelate stars belonging to different bases.

## 3. Direct Apéry, Lucas, valuation, and automaticity results

### 3.1 Gessel's Lucas theorem and Malik--Straub's extension

I. Gessel, *Some congruences for Apéry numbers*, J. Number Theory **14** (1982), 362--368, Theorem 1, proves (2.1). His Theorem 4 gives the first-order lift

\[
b_{d+pn}\equiv (b_d+pn b'_d)b_n\pmod{p^2},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where \(b'_d\) is the explicit harmonic-weighted companion used in that paper.

A. Malik and A. Straub, [*Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-016-0036-8), Research in Number Theory **2** (2016), Article 5:

* **Theorem 3.1:** every sporadic Apéry-like sequence in their Tables 1 and 2 satisfies Lucas congruences modulo every prime (with the listed minor exceptional-prime conventions for the general framework).
* **Lemma 6.2:** for the Apéry numbers,
  \[
  b_r\equiv b_{p-1-r}\pmod p,\qquad 0\le r<p.
  \tag{3.2}
  \]
* **Conjecture 6.3:** the proportion of primes that divide no Apéry number is conjectured to be \(e^{-1/2}\). By Lucas, this is equivalent to \(Z_p=\varnothing\).

**Uniformity and failure for (T).** Equations (2.1), (2.6), and (3.2) hold for every \(p\) and every index, so the index range \(n\le X^2\) is no issue. But all information is intra-characteristic. Reflection only pairs two residues inside the same \(Z_p\). The fact that even the frequency of \(Z_p=\varnothing\) is conjectural is direct evidence that these theorems do not provide the prime-aspect statistics required in (T).

### 3.2 Delaygue's valuation theorems

É. Delaygue, [*Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X17007552), Compositio Math. **154** (2018), 249--274, gives the strongest general digitwise valuation framework located in this search.

In the notation of that paper, \(\alpha_p(A,n)\) counts the base-\(p\) digits of \(n\) that fall in the set of digits at which the relevant sequence vanishes modulo \(p\).

* **Theorem 1:** under the stated factorial-ratio and differential-operator hypotheses,
  \[
  S_{e,f}(n)\in p^{\alpha_p(S_{e,f},n)}\mathbf Z,
  \tag{3.3}
  \]
  with the corresponding twisted multisum satisfying the stated \(p^{\alpha_p-1}\mathbf Z_p\) bound. The Apéry differential operator is one of the type-I operators covered by the theorem.
* **Theorem 2:** if \(A(n)=\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda^n)\), the origin is the unique interior lattice point of the Newton polytope, and the generating series is annihilated by a type-I or type-II operator, then
  \[
  A(n)\in p^{\alpha_p(A,n)}\mathbf Z_p.
  \tag{3.4}
  \]
* **Theorem 3:** gives an effective criterion for factorial-ratio sequences to have the \(p\)-Lucas property for all primes outside an explicit finite set.

**Uniformity and failure for (T).** These statements are uniform in the index and can be applied prime by prime. However, the quantity \(\alpha_p(A,n)\) is defined from the very local zero set whose cross-prime behavior is at issue. The theorems amplify a known zero digit into a valuation lower bound; they neither bound \(z_p\) nor compare \(Z_p\) for different primes. Summing (3.3) over varying \(p\) gives only the elementary product-divisibility observation behind (2.4).

### 3.3 Automatic congruences and linear \(p\)-schemes

E. Rowland and R. Yassawi, [*Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational functions*](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.901), J. Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux **27** (2015), 245--288:

* **Theorem 2.1:** if a multivariate series is the diagonal of a rational function \(R/Q\) with \(Q(0)=1\), then its coefficient sequence modulo \(p^\alpha\) is \(p\)-automatic, and an automaton can be constructed by Cartier operators.
* **Remark 2.2:** with maximum input degree \(L\), \(k\) variables, and \(m\le p^{\alpha-1}L\), their general construction has at most
  \[
  p^{\alpha (p^{\alpha-1}L+1)^k}
  \tag{3.5}
  \]
  states.
* Their Apéry section supplies an explicit rational-diagonal representation and poses **Question 3.26**, asking whether infinitely many primes divide no Apéry number.

J. Henningsen and A. Straub, [*Generalized Lucas congruences and linear \(p\)-schemes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2022.102409), Adv. Appl. Math. **141** (2022), 102409, prove that ordinary Lucas congruences are exactly one-state linear \(p\)-schemes and construct finite-state generalizations for constant-term sequences.

F. Beukers, [*\(p\)-Linear schemes for sequences modulo \(p^r\)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15240), constructs, for fixed \(r\), schemes for broad constant-term sequences with a number of states bounded independently of \(p\).

**Uniformity and failure for (T).** The later uniform state-count result removes one possible objection, but not the decisive one. The input alphabet has \(p\) digits, the transition matrices depend on \(p\), and for ordinary Apéry Lucas congruences the transition labels encode all values \(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1}\pmod p\), hence all of \(Z_p\). A uniformly bounded number of states does not give a uniformly bounded algebraic description of the digit subset. There is no theorem in this literature comparing automata with different bases \(p\), let alone bounding the diagonal correlation \(n\mapsto(\mathcal A_p(n))_{p\sim X}\).

### 3.4 Lucas congruences modulo \(p^2\)

E. Rowland, R. Yassawi, and C. Krattenthaler, [*Lucas congruences for the Apéry numbers modulo \(p^2\)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04801), Integers **21** (2021), A20, define

\[
D(p)=\{0\le d<p:b_d\equiv b_{p-1-d}\pmod{p^2}\}.
\]

Their **Theorem 2** states:

\[
b_{d+pn}\equiv b_db_n\pmod{p^2}\quad\text{for every }n\in\mathbf Z
\]

if and only if \(d\in D(p)\). Consequently, if every base-\(p\) digit of \(n\) belongs to \(D(p)\), then the full digit product holds modulo \(p^2\).

A. Straub, [*Gessel--Lucas congruences for sporadic sequences*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-023-01894-3), Monatsh. Math. **203** (2024), 883--898, proves for every sporadic Apéry-like sequence:

* **Theorem 1.1:** for \(p\ge3\),
  \[
  b_{p^rn}\equiv b_{p^{r-1}n}\pmod{p^{2r}};
  \tag{3.6}
  \]
* **Theorem 1.2:** the ordinary Lucas congruence;
* **Theorem 1.3:** for \(0\le d<p\),
  \[
  b_{pn+d}\equiv b_db_n+pn b'_d b_n\pmod{p^2}.
  \tag{3.7}
  \]

The author's [official corrigendum](https://arminstraub.com/pub/gessel-lucas) notes that the published inequalities in Theorems 1.2, 1.3, and 5.1 must read \(0\le d<p\), not \(d<n\).

**Uniformity and failure for (T).** These are uniform, exact, and stronger than the mod-\(p\) input. Yet \(D(p)\) and \(b'_d\pmod p\) vary with \(p\), and no theorem bounds their cross-prime joint behavior. Modulo \(p^2\) information cannot by itself create independence modulo distinct primes.

### 3.5 Partial Lucas theorems

A. Straub, [*Partial Lucas-type congruences*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-025-01275-4), Ramanujan J. **69** (2026), 1--14, **Theorem 1.2**, states the following. If \(P\) is an integral Laurent polynomial and \(M\ge1\) is such that \(M^{-1}\operatorname{Newt}(P)\) has no interior lattice point other than the origin, then for

\[
A(n)=\operatorname{CT}(P^n)
\]

one has, for every prime \(p\),

\[
A(pn+d)\equiv A(n)A(d)\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le d<p/M.
\tag{3.8}
\]

This is a broad and genuinely uniform theorem, but Apéry already enjoys the stronger full range \(d<p\). Like the full Lucas property, (3.8) factors digits within one characteristic and gives no relation among the local digit-zero sets for different primes.

## 4. Dwork congruences, \(G\)-functions, and modular forms

### 4.1 Mellit--Vlasenko's Dwork congruences

A. Mellit and M. Vlasenko, [*Dwork's congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5811), Int. J. Number Theory **12** (2016), 313--321, fix a prime \(p\), a Laurent polynomial \(\Lambda\in\mathbf Z_p[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_d^{\pm1}]\), and

\[
a_n=\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda^n),\qquad
f_s(X)=\sum_{0\le n<p^s}a_nX^n.
\]

If the Newton polytope has the origin as its only interior lattice point, their **Theorem 1** gives

\[
\frac{f(X)}{f(X^p)}
 \equiv
\frac{f_s(X)}{f_{s-1}(X^p)}
\pmod{p^s},
\tag{4.1}
\]

or equivalently

\[
f_{s+1}(X)f_{s-1}(X^p)
 \equiv f_s(X)f_s(X^p)\pmod{p^s}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The coefficient form includes the Samol--van Straten congruence

\[
a_{n+mp^s}a_{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}
 \equiv
 a_na_{\lfloor n/p\rfloor+mp^{s-1}}
 \pmod{p^s}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

**Uniformity and failure for (T).** These congruences are excellent in the index and \(p\)-adic depth but are proved after fixing \(p\). They concern Frobenius in the series variable \(X\), not statistical independence as the base characteristic varies. They yield no mixed congruence involving two distinct primes \(p\) and \(q\).

### 4.2 Modular forms: only special coefficients or Lucas factorization

S. Ahlgren and K. Ono, [*A Gaussian hypergeometric series evaluation and Apéry number congruences*](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.2000.093), J. reine angew. Math. **518** (2000), 187--212, let

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}a(n)q^n
 =q\prod_{m\ge1}(1-q^{2m})^4(1-q^{4m})^4.
\]

Their **Theorem 5** gives, for odd primes \(p\),

\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a(p)\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a genuine horizontal modular relation, but it covers only the single prime-dependent index \((p-1)/2\), not all \(0\le r<p\) and certainly not \(r=n\bmod p\) for a free common \(n\).

F. Beukers, W.-L. Tsai, and D. Ye, [*Lucas congruences using modular forms*](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.13182), Bull. London Math. Soc. **57** (2025), 69--78, **Theorem 1.1**, works with a genus-zero group \(\Gamma_0(N)\) or an Atkin--Lehner extension, a uniformizer \(t\) with its prescribed unique zero and pole, and a weight-2 modular form \(Z\) with the stated multiplier and zero conditions. Writing

\[
Z=\sum_{n\ge0}T(n)t^n,
\]

the theorem proves Lucas congruences for \(T(n)\) modulo every prime admitting the required embedding of the coefficient ring.

**Uniformity and failure for (T).** This supplies a conceptually different proof of a local digit law, but the conclusion is still (2.6). It contains no estimate for \(Z_p\), no mixed-characteristic correlation, and no uniform bounded-conductor object whose Frobenius trace at the residue \(r\) equals \(b_r\).

### 4.3 \(G\)-functions and global nilpotence

The Apéry generating series is a \(G\)-function satisfying a fixed Picard--Fuchs equation, and its reductions have strong algebraicity/Frobenius properties. Results of Chudnovsky--André--Katz on global nilpotence control the differential equation and its \(p\)-curvature. B. Adamczewski, J. Bell, and É. Delaygue, [*Algebraic independence of \(G\)-functions and congruences à la Lucas*](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.2392), Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Supér. **52** (2019), 515--559, study classes with functional congruences of the form

\[
f(x)\equiv A_p(x)f(x^{p^k})\pmod p
\tag{4.5}
\]

and use them for algebraic-independence results.

None of these theorems concerns the distribution, over varying base primes, of the *coefficient positions* at which a reduction vanishes. Global nilpotence bounds the complexity of the differential module, but coefficient extraction at order \(r\asymp p\) is not a bounded-conductor trace operation.

## 5. The recent Galois classification modulo \(p\): strong, but on the wrong statistic

X. Caruso, F. Fürnsinn, D. Vargas-Montoya, and W. Zudilin, [*Galois groups of Apéry-like series modulo primes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23298), Bull. Austral. Math. Soc. **114** (2026), 65--78, consider

\[
f_p(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}(b_n\bmod p)t^n
\quad\text{and}\quad
A_p(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}(b_n\bmod p)t^n.
\]

For the Apéry series, their **Theorem 1** determines the Galois group of
\(\mathbf F_p(t,f_p)/\mathbf F_p(t)\): it is the subgroup of squares in \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) for

\[
p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},
\]

and all of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) for

\[
p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.
\]

Their **Theorem 2** gives the corresponding factorization

\[
A_p(t)=B_p(t)^2
\tag{5.1}
\]

in the first four residue classes, and

\[
A_p(t)=(t^2-34t+1)B_p(t)^2
\tag{5.2}
\]

in the second four.

This is the strongest genuinely horizontal structural theorem found in the direct Apéry-series literature. It still does **not** control \(Z_p\), because

\[
Z_p=\{r:\ [t^r]A_p(t)=0\},
\]

whereas (5.1)--(5.2) and the Galois group concern the polynomial/series as a function of \(t\). Coefficients of a square are convolution sums

\[
[t^r]B_p(t)^2=\sum_{i+j=r}[t^i]B_p(t)[t^j]B_p(t),
\]

and their cancellations are not governed by the zero locus of \(B_p(t)\). Moreover, \(\deg B_p\asymp p\), so treating its coefficients by a generic algebraic-geometry estimate introduces degree/conductor comparable to the characteristic.

This coefficient-versus-value distinction also blocks a direct use of monodromy for a Hasse-invariant polynomial: geometry may control the values \(A_p(t_0)\) as \(t_0\in\mathbf F_p\) varies, while the problem asks whether the \(r\)-th coefficient of \(A_p\) is zero.

## 6. Prime divisors and residue classes of Apéry/holonomic sequences

### 6.1 Luca--Shparlinski on prime divisors of Apéry numbers

F. Luca and I. Shparlinski, [*Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers*](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jdn031), J. London Math. Soc. **78** (2008), 545--562, prove the following principal density-one assertions: there is an absolute \(c_0>0\) such that, for a set of positive integers \(n\) of asymptotic density one,

\[
\omega(b_n)>c_0\log\log\log n
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
P(b_n)>c_0(\log n\log\log n)^{1/2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Their method applies more generally to second-order polynomial-coefficient recurrences with suitable Lucas congruences.

**Uniformity and failure for (T).** These are lower bounds on the total prime-factor complexity of a typical term. For \(n\asymp X^2\), (6.2) reaches only polylogarithmic primes, far below \(p\asymp X\). The desired result is an upper factorial moment for primes in one dyadic interval, uniformly including exceptional \(n\). Neither (6.1) nor (6.2) localizes prime divisors to \((X,2X]\), controls multiplicity there, or supplies an upper tail.

### 6.2 Luca on binary holonomic sequences

F. Luca, [*Prime divisors of binary holonomic sequences*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2006.12.001), Adv. Appl. Math. **40** (2008), 168--179, proves that if a rational sequence satisfies a second-order recurrence with polynomial coefficients and is not eventually a constant-coefficient binary recurrence, then the product of the nonzero numerators and denominators of the terms up to \(N\) has at least \(c\log N\) distinct prime factors.

This controls the union of prime divisors over many indices. It gives no information about how many primes in a prescribed interval divide one fixed term, and no mixed moment over the same index.

### 6.3 Garaev--Luca--Shparlinski on residue classes

M. Garaev, F. Luca, and I. Shparlinski, [*Catalan and Apéry numbers in residue classes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2005.08.003), J. Combin. Theory Ser. A **113** (2006), 851--865, prove, for polynomial-recurrence sequences including Apéry numbers, that the set of sequence values modulo a sufficiently large fixed prime is a finite additive basis of \(\mathbf F_p\).

This is a fixed-characteristic additive-combinatorial statement about the value set \(\{b_n\bmod p\}\). It does not locate the indices at which the value is zero, and additive-basis coverage gives no cross-characteristic decorrelation.

### 6.4 Exact near miss: Calkin's Franel divisibility theorem

N. Calkin, [*Factors of sums of powers of binomial coefficients*](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-86-1-17-26), Acta Arith. **86** (1998), 17--26, writes

\[
f_{n,a}=\sum_{j=0}^n\binom nj^a.
\]

His **Proposition 3** states that if \(m\ge1\) and a prime \(p\) satisfies

\[
\frac nm<p<\frac{2a(n+1)-1}{2ma-1},
\tag{6.3}
\]

then

\[
p\mid f_{n,2a}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

His **Lemma 4** is the digit-product Lucas congruence for these Franel sums, and **Corollary 5** says that if \(\ell<p\) and \(p\mid f_{\ell,a}\), then

\[
p\mid f_{\ell+jp,a}\qquad(j\ge1).
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is a theorem that genuinely couples the same index range with varying primes. However, it is derived from a special cancellation identity for pure powers \(\binom nj^{2a}\); no analogue for
\(\binom nj^2\binom{n+j}j^2\) is known. More importantly, (6.3)--(6.5) create deterministic intervals and rays of divisibility. They illustrate that Lucas structure can generate anchored stars rather than rule them out. Thus Calkin is a close *type* match but points in the opposite statistical direction.

## 7. Positive-characteristic zero-set theorems

H. Derksen, [*A Skolem--Mahler--Lech theorem in positive characteristic and finite automata*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-006-0031-0), Invent. Math. **168** (2007), 175--224:

* **Theorem 1.8:** if a sequence over a field of characteristic \(p\) satisfies a constant-coefficient linear recurrence of order \(d\), its zero set is \(p\)-normal of order at most \(d-2\).
* **Theorems 1.9 and 1.10:** give the module version and effectivity.
* **Corollary 1.11:** if the zero set contains no infinite arithmetic progression, then the number of zeros in an interval of length \(N\) is
  \[
  O((\log N)^{d-2}).
  \tag{7.1}
  \]

B. Adamczewski and J. Bell, *On vanishing coefficients of algebraic power series over fields of positive characteristic*, Invent. Math. **187** (2012), 343--393, give corresponding automatic/structural descriptions for zero sets of algebraic power series.

**Why neither applies.** The Apéry recurrence has polynomial coefficients in the index; it is P-recursive, not a fixed constant-coefficient recurrence over \(\mathbf F_p\) on the entire line. Its reduction is algebraic/automatic because of Lucas congruences, but the algebraic equation and automaton are considered after fixing \(p\). Even a sparse description of each individual zero language would not compare languages in different bases. In the range \(0\le r<p\), automaticity alone permits an arbitrary first-digit subset \(Z_p\).

## 8. Large sieve for Frobenius and trace functions

### 8.1 Kowalski's large sieve for Frobenius

E. Kowalski, [*The large sieve, monodromy and zeta functions of curves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503714), J. reine angew. Math. **601** (2006), 29--69, fixes a finite field \(\mathbf F_q\), a variety \(U/\mathbf F_q\), and a compatible family of sheaves indexed by auxiliary primes \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbf F_q\), with linearly disjoint monodromy.

In the notation of **Theorem 3.1**, for coefficients \(\alpha(u)\),

\[
\sum_{\ell\le L}\sum_{\pi\ne1}^{*}
\left|\sum_{u\in U(\mathbf F_q)}
 \alpha(u)\operatorname{Tr}((\pi\circ\rho_\ell)(\operatorname{Fr}_u))\right|^2
\le
\bigl(\kappa q^d+Cq^{d-1/2}L^A\bigr)
\sum_u|\alpha(u)|^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For a compatible system on a curve, the theorem gives \(A=1+s+t/2\); in the stated prime-to-characteristic generality it gives \(A=1+5s/2+t/2\). **Proposition 3.3** converts this to a variance estimate

\[
\sum_{u\in U(\mathbf F_q)}(P(u,L)-P(L))^2
 \le(\kappa q^d+Cq^{d-1/2}L^A)P(L).
\tag{8.2}
\]

C. Perret-Gentil, [*Exponential sums over finite fields and the large sieve*](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rny202), IMRN **2020**, 7139--7174, develops zero-density estimates for trace values lying in algebraic subsets in compatible systems. Algebraic trace-function large sieves such as P. Xi, *Large sieve inequalities for algebraic trace functions*, IMRN **2017**, and Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *Algebraic trace functions over the primes*, Duke Math. J. **163** (2014), likewise require trace functions of uniformly bounded conductor.

**Exact mismatch.** In (8.1), the base field and its characteristic are fixed; \(\ell\) varies only as the coefficient/monodromy prime. The target varies the base field \(\mathbf F_p\) itself and evaluates at the diagonal residue \(u=n\bmod p\). Moreover, no bounded-conductor trace function \(t_p(r)\) is known with

\[
t_p(r)=\mathbf1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Even if one existed, (8.1) is fundamentally an \(L^2\) result. Reaching (T) would require a mixed \(k\)-fold number-field/short-interval version with constants uniform in the family.

### 8.2 Horizontal Sato--Tate

Horizontal Sato--Tate fixes a motive, elliptic curve, or modular form and studies one normalized Frobenius trace \(a_p\) as \(p\) varies. Vertical Sato--Tate fixes \(p\) and varies a parameter over \(\mathbf F_p\). Neither setup supplies the triangular array

\[
\{b_r\bmod p:0\le r<p\}_{p}.
\]

Ahlgren--Ono's (4.4) identifies one distinguished entry of that array with a modular coefficient. There is no theorem identifying every \(b_r\), uniformly for \(r<p\), as the trace of a fixed-rank family over \(\mathbf Z\). The obvious point-count realization of a coefficient \(b_r\) has degree/weight growing with \(r\), so at \(r\asymp p\) the conductor is not bounded.

## 9. Classical large sieve, Gallagher's larger sieve, and Hilbert irreducibility

### 9.1 Classical large sieve

The classical inequality

\[
\sum_{q\le Q}\sum_{a\bmod q}^{*}
\left|\sum_{n\le N}c_ne(an/q)\right|^2
 \le (N+Q^2)\sum_{n\le N}|c_n|^2
\tag{9.1}
\]

is at its transition point when \(N=X^2\) and \(Q=X\). It provides only an \(L^2\) aggregate. Fourier-expanding arbitrary indicators \(1_{Z_p}\) introduces their full unrestricted spectra. A family with a common anchor can saturate the incidence count, so (9.1) cannot prove (T) without an Apéry-specific spectral estimate that already couples different \(p\).

### 9.2 Gallagher's larger sieve

P. X. Gallagher, [*A larger sieve*](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-18-1-77-81), Acta Arith. **18** (1971), 77--81, **Theorem 1**, states: if \(A\subset[1,N]\) occupies at most \(g(q)\) residue classes modulo each prime power \(q\) in a set \(\mathcal S\), then

\[
|A|\le
\frac{\sum_{q\in\mathcal S}\Lambda(q)-\log N}
 {\sum_{q\in\mathcal S}\Lambda(q)/g(q)-\log N},
\tag{9.2}
\]

provided the denominator is positive.

For one fixed \(k\)-tuple in (1.2), take \(A\) to be the integers satisfying all \(k\) local restrictions and \(g(p_i)=z_{p_i}\). Since \(\log N=2\log X\), the denominator is roughly

\[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^k\frac1{z_{p_i}}-2\right)\log X.
\tag{9.3}
\]

It is often nonpositive, and even when positive the resulting bound is reciprocal-linear in the \(z_{p_i}\), not the product density \(N\prod z_{p_i}/p_i\). Summing (9.2) over tuples loses the required scale. The larger sieve therefore does not defeat anchored stars.

### 9.3 Zywina's quantitative Hilbert irreducibility theorem

D. Zywina, [*Hilbert's irreducibility theorem and the larger sieve*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6465), fixes a monic irreducible

\[
f(x,T)\in k(T_1,\ldots,T_n)[x]
\]

with geometric splitting field \(L/k(T)\) and fixed Galois group \(G\). For a conjugacy-stable subset \(C\subset G\), his **Theorem 1.4** gives

\[
\#\{t\in\mathcal O_k^n-\Omega_f:\|t\|\le B,\ G_t\subseteq C\}
 \ll_{n,f,k}
 B^{[k:\mathbf Q](n-1+|C|/|G|)}\log B,
\tag{9.4}
\]

and, for rational points of height at most \(B\),

\[
\#\{t\in k^n-\Omega_f:H(t)\le B,\ G_t\subseteq C\}
 \ll_{n,f,k}
 B^{[k:\mathbf Q](n+|C|/|G|)}\log B.
\tag{9.5}
\]

This is the closest abstract theorem found: it keeps one integer specialization \(t\) while reading its reductions at many varying prime ideals. Chebotarev supplies fixed local densities, and the larger sieve converts them into a global count.

It still cannot be applied here for three exact reasons.

1. The cover \(f\), group \(G\), bad locus, and implicit constants are fixed before \(B\) grows. No fixed cover is known whose Frobenius class at \((r,p)\) detects \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\) for every \(r<p\).
2. Interpolating the table gives only
   \[
   B_p(T)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r
   \prod_{s\ne r}\frac{T-s}{r-s}\in\mathbf F_p[T],
   \tag{9.6}
   \]
   with \(\deg B_p\le p-1\). No degree bound independent of \(p\) is known. Feeding (9.6) into a geometric sieve makes the cover and constants grow at least linearly with the modulus.
3. Theorem 1.4 counts specializations constrained at all relevant primes by one fixed Galois condition. It is not a \(k\)-fold incidence moment with a relative product-density main term. Even a hypothetical fixed cover would still require a sharpened moment version adapted to the short interval \(n<X^2\).

Thus (9.4) is a precise blueprint for what is missing, not an applicable result.

## 10. GCD, resultants, and fixed algebraic-group orbit theorems

A representative strong same-index GCD theorem is Y. Bugeaud, P. Corvaja, and U. Zannier, [*An upper bound for the G.C.D. of \(a^n-1\) and \(b^n-1\)*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-002-0448-z), Math. Z. **243** (2003), 79--84, **Theorem 1**: for multiplicatively independent integers \(a,b>1\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
\log\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)\le \varepsilon n+O_{a,b,\varepsilon}(1).
\tag{10.1}
\]

Subspace-theorem extensions treat fixed recurrences or fixed semiabelian-group orbits. Their power comes from a fixed finite-dimensional torus/orbit and fixed exceptional subvarieties. Apéry numbers arise from a polynomial-coefficient recurrence, not a fixed multiplicative orbit, and no strong-divisibility identity comparable to
\(\gcd(U_m,U_n)=U_{\gcd(m,n)}\) is available.

Likewise, a resultant can force common prime divisors when two quantities are values of fixed polynomials of bounded degree. Here the most direct polynomial representing \(r\mapsto b_r\pmod p\) is (9.6), of degree \(O(p)\). Continuant/resultant encodings derived from the three-term recurrence have degree and height growing with the gap or index. Standard Bézout, resultant, determinant-method, and Ekedahl-sieve bounds therefore carry constants of size at least a power of \(p\), which consumes the entire square-root-range budget. I found no published Apéry-specific GCD or resultant theorem that bounds

\[
\#\{p\sim X:p\mid b_n\}
\]

uniformly for every \(n\le X^2\), much less its fixed high moments.

## 12. The exact missing hypothesis

A theorem in the existing Frobenius-sieve paradigm would become relevant if one first proved an Apéry-specific geometric realization of the following form.

> **Missing bounded-complexity realization.** There is a finite-type object \(\mathcal U/\mathbf Z[1/S]\), a compatible system of fixed rank and uniformly bounded conductor on \(\mathcal U\), and uniformly bounded class functions \(\Phi_p\) such that for every \(p\notin S\) and every \(r\in\mathbf F_p\),
> \[
> \mathbf1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}
> =\Phi_p(\operatorname{Frob}_{(r,p)}),
> \tag{12.1}
> \]
> with product geometric monodromy for distinct base primes.

That alone would not quite prove (T). One would also need a number-field diagonal large-sieve theorem of the schematic form

\[
\sum_{n<N}\prod_{i=1}^k
\left(\Phi_{p_i}(\operatorname{Frob}_{n\bmod p_i})
      -\frac{z_{p_i}}{p_i}\right)
\]

with an error that is summable over \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\sim N^{1/2}\) and is small relative to the product densities, uniformly when some densities are tiny.

No realization (12.1) is currently known. The obstacle is not merely that the generating function lacks geometry; it has abundant geometry. The obstacle is that \(b_r\) is a **coefficient indexed by \(r\)**. Existing geometry controls values, point counts, Frobenius traces, or the whole Hasse-invariant polynomial. Extracting its \(r\)-th coefficient for \(r\asymp p\) has complexity growing with \(p\). The interpolation (9.6) makes this growth explicit.

An alternative sufficient input, closer to prime-divisor theory, would be a uniform medium-prime Turán--Kubilius theorem for Apéry values:

\[
\sum_{n<X^2}
\left(\omega_{(X,2X]}(b_n)\right)_k
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k,
\tag{12.2}
\]

or even the corresponding bound restricted to the residue-digit channel in (2.2). No theorem of this kind is known for polynomial-coefficient recurrences or \(G\)-function coefficients.

## 13. Bottom line for the project

1. **There is no off-the-shelf theorem proving the required high moment.** In particular, citing monodromy, horizontal Sato--Tate, Chebotarev, automaticity, or Dwork congruences without constructing (12.1) leaves the main quantifier mismatch untouched.
2. **The best unconditional same-index bridge is (2.3):** every hit prime divides the actual integer \(b_n\). The unsolved input is an upper-tail theorem for medium prime divisors of \(b_n\), not another one-prime zero bound.
3. **Zywina Theorem 1.4 is the closest general theorem in logical form.** Its exact missing hypothesis is a fixed finite cover/bounded-conductor Frobenius encoding of coefficient zeros. The known Apéry generating-polynomial and Galois theorems have degree \(\asymp p\) and concern polynomial values rather than coefficient positions.
4. **Calkin Proposition 3 is the closest sequence-specific precedent.** It shows that a binomial-sum identity can genuinely couple \(n\) and varying \(p\), but for Franel sums it produces structured divisibility stars. An Apéry-specific identity of comparable strength would have to prove anti-clustering, not merely Lucas propagation.
5. Therefore the requested moment should be treated as a **new arithmetic-statistics lemma**, not as a routine consequence of an existing large sieve or compatible-system theorem.
