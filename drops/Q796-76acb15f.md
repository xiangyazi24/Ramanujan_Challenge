# Same-gap fibre audit: LGTQ-2 multiplicity

## Verdict

No unconditional uniqueness theorem follows from the explicit Apéry binomial sum with the currently available identities. The same-gap fibre has a strong exact structure, but the missing step is a genuine simultaneous congruence control problem. The correct scoped conclusion is:

- the four-divisibility configuration implies explicit Apéry congruences at four shifted indices;
- the shared-corner cases admit additional reflection constraints;
- the four-distinct-prime case does not currently yield a characteristic-zero carrier with proved small height;
- uniqueness cannot be claimed from the finite scan.

## 1. Exact configuration

Let

\[
b_m=\sum_k {m\choose k}^2 {m+k\choose k}^2.
\]

Assume two LGTQ-2 pairs with the same \((n,q,h)\), where \(h=qd\):

\[
(p,p-d,u,u+h),\qquad (p-s/q,p-d-s/q,u+s,u+s+h).
\]

Since

\[
u=n-qp,
\]

and

\[
u+s=n-q(p-s/q),
\]

we have the exact translation

\[
s=q(p_1-p_2).
\]

The four required divisibilities are

\[
p\mid b_u,\quad p-d\mid b_{u+h},
\]

\[
p-s/q\mid b_{u+s},\quad p-d-s/q\mid b_{u+s+h}.
\]

Full transversality adds

\[
p\nmid b_{u+h},\quad p-d\nmid b_u,
\]

and the analogous two conditions for the second pair.

## 2. Shared-corner cases

### Case s=h

The index rectangle becomes

\[
(u,u+h,u+h,u+2h).
\]

The middle two indices coincide. However the characteristics are distinct unless

\[
p-d=p-h/q,
\]

which would force \(h=q d\) to match the pair shift and collapse the fibre.

The available divisibility information is only

\[
p\mid b_u,
\quad p-d\mid b_{u+h},
\quad p-d-h/q\mid b_{u+2h}.
\]

There is no automatic contradiction because divisors of the same Apéry value at the shared index need not interact.

### Case s=-h

The rectangle is

\[
(u,u+h,u-h,u).
\]

Now the two corners share \(b_u\), but the divisibility directions are different:

\[
p\mid b_u,
\qquad p-d-h/q\mid b_u.
\]

This gives a possible carrier candidate, but only

\[
\operatorname{rad}(b_u)
\]

is known to contain the primes. The explicit sum gives congruences, not a small integer multiple whose height is controlled.

## 3. Four-distinct-prime case

The desired rectangle would require a relation among

\[
b_u,b_{u+h},b_{u+s},b_{u+s+h}.
\]

Using the explicit sum one can write every condition as

\[
\sum_k {m\choose k}^2 {m+k\choose k}^2\equiv0\pmod r.
\]

For example:

\[
\sum_k {u\choose k}^2 {u+k\choose k}^2\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

There is no known identity reducing the four congruences to a determinant/resultant in a characteristic-zero ring with bounded height.

A valid elimination claim would require an explicit ideal such as

\[
I=(F_u,F_{u+h},F_{u+s},F_{u+s+h})\subset\mathbb Z[X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4]
\]

with the prime variables represented by the four characteristics and an exhibited resultant certificate. No such certificate is currently available.

## 4. Recurrence transfer rectangle

The Apéry sequence satisfies a third-order recurrence, so formally one may transfer a state vector

\[
V_m=(b_m,b_{m+1},b_{m+2})
\]

around the rectangle:

\[
V_u\rightarrow V_{u+h},\qquad V_u\rightarrow V_{u+s},\qquad V_{u+s}\rightarrow V_{u+s+h}.
\]

The obstruction is that each edge is reduced modulo a different prime. The resulting square does not live in one finite field. Therefore the recurrence does not produce a common determinant congruence.

## 5. Carrier-height attempt

A possible carrier would need an integer \(M(n,q,h,s)\) satisfying

\[
p(p-d)(p-s/q)(p-d-s/q)\mid M.
\]

The target height would be

\[
\log |M|=O((h+|s|)\log n).
\]

The explicit Apéry sum gives no such bound: the natural carrier is a product of Apéry values, whose logarithmic size is much larger and does not imply a divisor-counting contradiction.

## 6. Divisibility direction audit

The only valid implications are:

\[
p\mid b_u,
\]

\[\ell=p-d\mid b_{u+h},
\]

\[\ell\nmid b_u,
\]

\[
p\nmid b_{u+h}.
\]

It is invalid to reverse either transverse condition or infer that the companion prime divides the neighbouring value.

## 7. Scoped no-go

The following stronger statements are not proved:

- "same-gap fibre has at most one pair";
- "two pairs imply a bounded-height characteristic-zero carrier";
- "four Apéry congruences force a resultant obstruction".

A formal model showing the obstruction is possible: four independent integer sequences can satisfy the same divisibility pattern without producing a bounded carrier. Therefore any proof must use special Apéry structure beyond recurrence/divisibility alone.

## Final audit conclusion

The finite scan supports multiplicity one empirically, but the exact same-gap fibre remains open. The correct next target is not uniqueness directly; it is an Apéry-specific multi-shift congruence theorem producing either:

1. a bounded-height carrier for two transverse pairs, or
2. a nontrivial correlation estimate for the four shifted binomial sums.

No valid elimination or determinant certificate is currently available.
