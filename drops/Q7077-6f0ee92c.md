# Palindromic orbit averaging for the CRT error

The full reflection orbit is the right object, but palindromy alone does not imply the cancellation required for the third factorial moment.

For each triple of primes, the reflection involution preserves the product zero set. Pairing a triple with its full reflection gives no useful cancellation: the two terms are

```text
1_{m* < X^2}+1_{m** < X^2}-2X^2/(p q l).
```

Since the threshold X^2 is much larger than p+q+l, the two reflected lifts are often simultaneously below X^2. Thus two-point pairing tends to double the contribution.

The natural object is instead the full 8-element orbit obtained by independently reflecting each coordinate. Define

```text
D(O)= sum_{m in O} 1_{m<X^2} - 8 X^2/(p q l).
```

A proof of cancellation would require showing that these orbit discrepancies have strong average bounds. This is essentially an exponential-sum discrepancy problem for the structured CRT orbit.

Writing the interval indicator with additive characters suggests that reflection may cancel some odd character components, leaving even components. But those remaining sums require new input beyond the known bounds on |Z_p|.

Conclusion:

* pairwise reflection: no cancellation mechanism;
* full 8-point orbit averaging: the correct framework;
* symmetry alone: insufficient;
* possible breakthrough: prove square-root-type discrepancy bounds for palindromic CRT orbits.
