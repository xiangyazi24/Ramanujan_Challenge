# Corrected fixed-q proof audit

The two observations remove the alleged defects. The remaining proof obligation is exactly the fixed-q estimate, not a hidden tail problem.

## 1. Definitions

Let
\[
G_n=\gcd(b_n,\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,n)^6)
\]
(or equivalently the valuation form used in the project). For a prime p|G_n, the contribution is bounded by a constant multiple of \(v_p(b_n)\log p\). The factor 6 comes from the chosen lcm power; it is not structurally important.

If one instead defines \(G_n\) with \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)\), the factor 6 disappears. Keeping it only weakens the estimate by a harmless constant.

## 2. Large quotient tail

Write
\[
q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor.
\]
For q>Q,
\[
p\le \frac{n}{Q+1}.
\]
Therefore all such primes contribute at most
\[
6\sum_{p\le n/(Q+1)}\log p
=6\vartheta(n/(Q+1))
=O(n/Q).
\]
The implied constant is absolute. No uniform fixed-q estimate is required here.

## 3. Fixed q part

For each fixed q, the target primes satisfy
\[
\frac{n}{q+1}<p\le \frac nq.
\]
The fixed-q theorem gives
\[
\sum_{p: \lfloor n/p\rfloor=q}v_p(b_n)\log p=o_q(n).
\]
There is no extra saturation from small primes dividing b_q: if p divides the fixed integer b_q, then p is bounded independently of n, while the interval lower endpoint
\[
\frac n{q+1}\to\infty
\]
for fixed q. Hence eventually no such p lies in the interval.

## 4. Quantifier order

Given epsilon > 0:

1. Choose Q so large that the tail bound satisfies \(O(n/Q)<\epsilon n/2\).
2. For this finite set q\le Q, choose N so that for every n>N,
\[
\sum_{q\le Q}\sum_{\lfloor n/p\rfloor=q}v_p(b_n)\log p<\epsilon n/2.
\]

The finite sum over fixed q is legitimate because Q is chosen first.

Hence
\[
\log G_n=o(n).
\]

## 5. Is there a counterexample?

No counterexample remains under the stated fixed-q theorem. The earlier objection that the tail required uniformity in q was incorrect: the tail is handled by the elementary bound \(p\le n/(Q+1)\). The earlier concern about fixed divisors of b_q was also incorrect: those primes eventually fall below the moving interval.

The only necessary clarification is the convention for \(G_n\): whether the project uses the sixth-power lcm normalization or the first-power lcm normalization. The proof works in either case; the factor 6 is a harmless multiplicative constant.
