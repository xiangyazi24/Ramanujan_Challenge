# Step-line Hermite–Padé for \(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)\): the corrected construction and why it is not P2.7

## Executive verdict

The proposed two-measure Hermite–Padé idea is mathematically natural, and the corrected step-line Type II polynomials do satisfy a four-term recurrence. However, the construction in the question does **not** produce the P2.7 denominator sequence.

There are four decisive points.

1. The approximation orders in the question are overdetermined. For a step-line multi-index
   \[
   \boldsymbol n=(n_2,n_3),\qquad n_2+n_3=N,
   \]
   the correct Type II remainder orders are
   \[
   P_{\boldsymbol n}(z)f_j(z)-Q_{\boldsymbol n,j}(z)
   =O(z^{-n_j-1}),
   \]
   not \(O(z^{-N-1})\) for both functions.
2. The zeta values occur at \(z=1\), not at \(z=0\):
   \[
   f_2(1)=\zeta(2),\qquad f_3(1)=\zeta(3).
   \]
   In fact, the defining integrals for \(f_2(0)\) and \(f_3(0)\) diverge.
3. The first corrected step-line polynomials can be computed exactly. Neither their values at \(0\) nor their values at \(1\) agree with the P2.7 values, even up to one constant normalization. Direct substitution also shows that neither evaluation sequence satisfies the P2.7 recurrence.
4. The correct Hermite–Padé error contains the factor \((1-t)^{-1}\). The error proposed in the question without that factor is identically zero for every step-line index with \(n_2,n_3\ge1\), by the defining orthogonality. At the endpoint \(z=1\), standard Markov/Padé asymptotics do not supply the P2.7 geometric relative rate \(0.001227\ldots\).

Thus this particular step-line system is a legitimate simultaneous approximation scheme for \(\zeta(2)\) and \(\zeta(3)\), but it is a different scheme from P2.7 and does not prove \(c_0(e)=0\) for P2.7.

## 1. Correct Type II formulation

Put
\[
d\mu_2(t)=-\log t\,dt,
\qquad
d\mu_3(t)=\frac12\log^2t\,dt,
\qquad 0<t<1.
\]
Their moments are
\[
m_k^{(2)}=\int_0^1t^k\,d\mu_2(t)=\frac1{(k+1)^2},
\qquad
m_k^{(3)}=\int_0^1t^k\,d\mu_3(t)=\frac1{(k+1)^3}.
\tag{1}
\]
The corresponding Markov functions are
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_2(z)&=\int_0^1\frac{d\mu_2(t)}{z-t}
      =\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{-k-1}}{(k+1)^2}
      =\operatorname{Li}_2(1/z),\\
f_3(z)&=\int_0^1\frac{d\mu_3(t)}{z-t}
      =\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{-k-1}}{(k+1)^3}
      =\operatorname{Li}_3(1/z),
\end{aligned}
\qquad |z|>1.
\tag{2}
\]

For a multi-index \(\boldsymbol n=(n_2,n_3)\), let
\[
N=|\boldsymbol n|=n_2+n_3.
\]
The monic Type II polynomial \(\Pi_{\boldsymbol n}\) is the degree-\(N\) polynomial satisfying
\[
\int_0^1 t^k\Pi_{\boldsymbol n}(t)\,d\mu_2(t)=0,
\qquad 0\le k<n_2,
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\int_0^1 t^k\Pi_{\boldsymbol n}(t)\,d\mu_3(t)=0,
\qquad 0\le k<n_3.
\tag{4}
\]
Define the numerator polynomials
\[
Q_{\boldsymbol n,j}(z)
=\int_0^1
\frac{\Pi_{\boldsymbol n}(z)-\Pi_{\boldsymbol n}(t)}{z-t}
\,d\mu_j(t),
\qquad j=2,3.
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(\deg Q_{\boldsymbol n,j}\le N-1\), and
\[
\Pi_{\boldsymbol n}(z)f_j(z)-Q_{\boldsymbol n,j}(z)
=\int_0^1\frac{\Pi_{\boldsymbol n}(t)}{z-t}\,d\mu_j(t)
=O(z^{-n_j-1}).
\tag{6}
\]
The last equality follows by expanding \((z-t)^{-1}\) at infinity and using (3)–(4).

### Why the orders in the question cannot hold

Requiring both remainders to be \(O(z^{-N-1})\) imposes \(N\) moment equations for each measure, hence \(2N\) conditions on one polynomial of degree at most \(N\). It is generically inconsistent.

Already for \(N=1\), write \(P(t)=t+c\). The \(\mu_2\) condition gives
\[
\frac14+c=0,
\]
whereas the \(\mu_3\) condition gives
\[
\frac18+c=0.
\]
No nonzero polynomial satisfies both.

### Normality and the four-term recurrence

The two weights form an AT/extended-Chebyshev system. After setting \(t=e^{-x}\), the relevant type-I linear combinations reduce to confluent exponential polynomials built from
\[
e^{-kx},\qquad xe^{-kx}.
\]
Such a nonzero exponential polynomial has the required zero bound, so all the step-line indices are normal.

Along the alternating step line
\[
(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(2,1),(2,2),(3,2),\ldots,
\tag{7}
\]
write \(\Pi_N=\Pi_{\lceil N/2\rceil,\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\). General multiple-orthogonality theory then gives
\[
t\Pi_N(t)
=\Pi_{N+1}(t)+\beta_N\Pi_N(t)
 +\gamma_N\Pi_{N-1}(t)+\delta_N\Pi_{N-2}(t).
\tag{8}
\]
This is the expected four-term recurrence for two measures. Equation (8) is structural; it does **not** imply that its recurrence coefficients coincide with those of P2.7.

## 2. Exact computation of the first six step-line polynomials

Write
\[
\Pi_N(t)=t^N+\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}c_{N,r}t^r.
\]
Equations (3)–(4) become the rational confluent-Cauchy system
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{N} \frac{c_{N,r}}{(r+k+1)^2}=0,
\quad 0\le k<n_2,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{N} \frac{c_{N,r}}{(r+k+1)^3}=0,
\quad 0\le k<n_3,
\tag{10}
\]
where \(c_{N,N}=1\).

Solving (9)–(10) gives:

\[
\boxed{\Pi_0(t)=1.}
\tag{11}
\]

\[
\boxed{\Pi_1(t)=t-\frac14.}
\tag{12}
\]

\[
\boxed{\Pi_2(t)=t^2-\frac{16}{27}t+\frac1{27}.}
\tag{13}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_3(t)=t^3-\frac{4779}{4600}t^2
+\frac{1107}{4600}t-\frac{133}{18400}.}
\tag{14}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_4(t)=t^4-\frac{12928}{8883}t^3
+\frac{25104}{41125}t^2
-\frac{2960}{41125}t
+\frac{1253}{1110375}.}
\tag{15}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_5(t)={}&t^5-\frac{88959375}{46704448}t^4
+\frac{1158533750}{965468511}t^3\\
&-\frac{40569875}{143032372}t^2
+\frac{2935095}{143032372}t
-\frac{1775657}{8827140672}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{16}
\]

Their relevant evaluations are:

| \(N\) | multi-index | \(\Pi_N(0)\) | \(\Pi_N(1)\) | l.c.m. of monic coefficient denominators |
|---:|:---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | \((0,0)\) | \(1\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| 1 | \((1,0)\) | \(-1/4\) | \(3/4\) | \(4\) |

| 5 | \((3,2)\) | \(-1775657/8827140672\) | \(164353085/5149165392\) | \(61789984704\) |

The first step-line recurrence coefficients begin with
\[
\beta_0=\frac14,
\qquad
\beta_1=\frac{37}{108},
\qquad
\gamma_1=\frac7{144},
\tag{17}
\]
and
\[
\beta_2=\frac{55433}{124200},
\qquad
\gamma_2=\frac{8165}{134136},
\qquad
\delta_2=0.005915637860\ldots .
\tag{18}
\]
These are the coefficients of the genuine multiple-orthogonal recurrence (8).

## 3. The special value is \(z=1\), not \(z=0\)

The identities
\[
f_2(1)=\int_0^1\frac{-\log t}{1-t}\,dt=\zeta(2),
\qquad
f_3(1)=\int_0^1\frac{\frac12\log^2t}{1-t}\,dt=\zeta(3)
\tag{19}
\]
show that the canonical Hermite–Padé denominator and numerator for the target constant are
\[
q_N^{\mathrm{HP}}=\Pi_N(1),
\qquad
p_N^{\mathrm{HP}}=Q_{N,2}(1)+Q_{N,3}(1).
\tag{20}
\]
By contrast,
\[
\int_0^1\frac{-\log t}{t}\,dt
\quad\text{and}\quad
\int_0^1\frac{\frac12\log^2t}{t}\,dt
\]
diverge. Hence \(f_2(0)\), \(f_3(0)\), and the proposed evaluation formula at \(z=0\) are not defined.

One can reformulate the problem using reversed polynomials and a transformed variable so that a constant term is used, but that is a different normalization and does not rescue the comparison below.

## 4. Direct comparison with P2.7

The P2.7 values satisfy
\[
\frac{q_1}{q_0}=0.859569944180336\ldots,
\qquad
\frac{q_2}{q_0}=0.738550482000391\ldots .
\tag{21}
\]
Under the monic HP normalization,
\[
\frac{\Pi_1(0)}{\Pi_0(0)}=-\frac14,
\qquad
\frac{\Pi_2(0)}{\Pi_0(0)}=\frac1{27},
\tag{22}
\]
and, at the correct zeta evaluation point,
\[
\frac{\Pi_1(1)}{\Pi_0(1)}=\frac34,
\qquad
\frac{\Pi_2(1)}{\Pi_0(1)}=\frac49.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus neither sequence agrees with \(q_N\) up to one global normalization.

### Direct P2.7 recurrence test

Using the degree-12 P2.7 recurrence in the current proof file, the first usable step is
\[
u_3=0.8596385499333346\,u_2
 -0.0005023606000926333\,u_1
 +0.000001030687114800318\,u_0.
\tag{24}
\]
For \(u_N=\Pi_N(1)\), the right-hand side of (24) is
\[
0.381685837985194\ldots,
\]
whereas
\[
\Pi_3(1)=0.194510869565217\ldots .
\]
For \(u_N=\Pi_N(0)\), (24) predicts
\[
0.031965085649484\ldots,
\]
whereas
\[
\Pi_3(0)=-0.007228260869565\ldots .
\]
Therefore neither evaluation sequence is annihilated by P2.7. Since the degree-18 operator is another minimal/desingularized presentation of the same P2.7 solution module, this also excludes that presentation.

The denominator pattern is also different. For example, the monic HP polynomials introduce the factors
\[
23,\quad47,\quad14893
\]
already by degrees \(3,4,5\), while the displayed P2.7 values have denominators \(905\) and \(6071\). Denominators depend on normalization, so this is secondary evidence; the value and recurrence tests above are normalization-independent once the monic HP convention is fixed.

## 5. The correct error identity

From (6), evaluated at \(z=1\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
E_N^{\mathrm{HP}}
&:=\Pi_N(1)\bigl(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)\bigr)
 -\bigl(Q_{N,2}(1)+Q_{N,3}(1)\bigr)\\
&=\int_0^1\frac{\Pi_N(t)}{1-t}
\left(-\log t+\frac12\log^2t\right)dt.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{25}
\]
This is the valid simultaneous remainder.

The expression proposed in the question,
\[
\int_0^1\Pi_N(t)\left(-\log t+\frac12\log^2t\right)dt,
\tag{26}
\]
is not the HP error. For every \(N\ge2\), the step-line index has \(n_2,n_3\ge1\), so the \(k=0\) conditions in both (3) and (4) imply that (26) is exactly zero.

Using the geometric identity and the orthogonality, (25) may also be written
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_N^{\mathrm{HP}}
={}&\int_0^1\frac{t^{n_2}\Pi_N(t)}{1-t}(-\log t)\,dt\\
&+\int_0^1\frac{t^{n_3}\Pi_N(t)}{1-t}\frac12\log^2t\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
This is the usual useful HP remainder form.

### First simultaneous approximants

For a polynomial \(\Pi_N(t)=\sum_{r=0}^Nc_rt^r\), one has the exact rational formula
\[
p_N^{\mathrm{HP}}
=\sum_{r=1}^Nc_r
\left(H_r^{(2)}+H_r^{(3)}\right).
\tag{28}
\]
The first approximants are:

| \(N\) | \(q_N^{\mathrm{HP}}=\Pi_N(1)\) | \(p_N^{\mathrm{HP}}/q_N^{\mathrm{HP}}\) | \(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)-p_N^{\mathrm{HP}}/q_N^{\mathrm{HP}}\) | \(E_N^{\mathrm{HP}}\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | \(0.75\) | \(2.666666666667\) | \(0.180324303341\) | \(0.135243227506\) |
| 2 | \(0.444444444444\) | \(2.677083333333\) | \(0.169907636674\) | \(0.075514505189\) |
| 3 | \(0.194510869565\) | \(2.760946053626\) | \(0.086044916382\) | \(0.016736671507\) |
| 4 | \(0.084218845323\) | \(2.790274011587\) | \(0.056716958421\) | \(0.004776713348\) |
| 5 | \(0.031918393077\) | \(2.808599979947\) | \(0.038390990061\) | \(0.001225378711\) |

This is a genuine approximation to the target constant, but it is visibly not the P2.7 approximation.

### Why the P2.7 rate does not follow

For P2.7 the two relevant rates are
\[
|e_N|^{1/N}\longrightarrow |r_\pm|=0.001053785\ldots,
\tag{29}
\]
and
\[
\left|\frac{e_N}{q_N}\right|^{1/N}
\longrightarrow \frac{|r_\pm|}{r_0}=0.00122703\ldots .
\tag{30}
\]
These are different quantities; the question conflates them.

The Type II identities alone do not determine either asymptotic rate. One needs strong asymptotics for the multiple orthogonal polynomials and their remainders. Moreover, the evaluation point \(z=1\) lies at the endpoint of the common support \([0,1]\). The usual geometric Padé rate outside the support is governed by a Green function; that Green function vanishes at the boundary. Thus the standard HP mechanism at \(z=1\) supplies no fixed relative factor smaller than one. Endpoint convergence is normally subgeometric, with algebraic exponents determined by the local weight singularities.

The numerical table is consistent with this endpoint behavior and is incompatible with a per-step factor as small as \(0.001227\). Orthogonality therefore does not automatically place this HP error in the P2.7 recessive plane.

## 6. The proposed Pochhammer gauge fails

The determinant term found in the P2.7 companion matrix is
\[
h_N=2^{-20N}\frac{(3)_N^4(4)_N^6}
{(5/2)_N^4(7/2)_N^3(9/2)_N^3},
\tag{31}
\]
with
\[
\frac{h_{N+1}}{h_N}
=\frac{(N+3)^4(N+4)^6}
{1024(2N+5)^4(2N+7)^3(2N+9)^3}.
\]
At \(N=0\),
\[
h_1=\frac4{1929375}.
\tag{32}
\]
Therefore the specific proposal
\[
q_N=C\,\frac{h_N}{64^N}\Pi_N(1)
\]
would give
\[
\frac{q_1}{q_0}
=\frac{h_1}{64}\frac{\Pi_1(1)}{\Pi_0(1)}
=\frac1{41160000},
\tag{33}
\]
not \(0.859569944\ldots\). Using \(\Pi_N(0)\) gives
\[
\frac{q_1}{q_0}=-\frac1{123480000}.
\tag{34}
\]
So the proposed gauge fails at the first index.

There is also a conceptual reason. Stirling's formula gives
\[
h_N\sim C_h\,2^{-20N}N^2.
\tag{35}
\]
The power \(2\) comes from
\[
4\cdot3+6\cdot4
-4\cdot\frac52-3\cdot\frac72-3\cdot\frac92=2.
\]
Thus this scalar factor changes both the exponential base and the formal power in a way incompatible with the observed P2.7 asymptotics.

More importantly, \(h_{N+1}/h_N\) was extracted from the **determinant of a rank-three companion matrix**. It is a volume-scaling invariant, not a scalar solution gauge. If a scalar sequence gauge \(u_N=g_Nv_N\) is applied to an order-three recurrence, then the companion determinant changes by
\[
\frac{g_{N+3}}{g_N},
\tag{36}
\]
not by \(g_{N+1}/g_N\). The determinant factor (31) therefore does not justify multiplying each scalar solution by \(h_N\).

Finally, the exact formal-power comparison is
\[
\frac{q_N}{64^{-N}b_N}
\sim C\,N^{\sigma_{27}+3/2},
\tag{37}
\]
not exactly \(C N^{3/2}\). The correction \(\sigma_{27}\) is algebraic irrational. A factorial/Pochhammer gauge with rational parameters shifts a Birkhoff power by a rational number, so it cannot account for this irrational correction. This agrees with the proved GCRD and Ore-intertwiner obstructions in the project.

## 7. What the calculation does and does not establish

### What is true

* The two moment measures form a natural perfect multiple-orthogonality system.
* Its step-line Type II polynomials satisfy a four-term recurrence.
* Evaluating the associated Hermite–Padé approximants at \(z=1\) gives rational approximations to \(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)\).
* The exact remainder is the integral (25).

### What is false for this system

* The orders \(O(z^{-N-1})\) for both functions.
* Evaluation at \(z=0\).
* Identification of \(\Pi_N(0)\) or \(\Pi_N(1)\) with the P2.7 sequence.
* The stated Pochhammer scalar gauge.
* Automatic decay with the P2.7 rate.

### A viable Hermite–Padé route would need additional structure

A successful HP explanation of P2.7 would have to modify at least one of the following:

1. **Vary the measures with \(N\).** The P2.7 half-integer endpoint factors suggest Jacobi/Beta weights whose exponents depend affinely on \(N\), rather than two fixed logarithmic measures.
2. **Use a mixed-type or multiple-orthogonality problem.** A type-I/type-II coupled system can introduce the additional accessory data absent from the fixed step-line construction.
3. **Move the evaluation off the support and then apply a nonlocal transform.** This can produce a genuine geometric rate before a binomial, Mellin, or Hadamard transform maps it to P2.7.
4. **Construct the recurrence first.** Starting from the full P2.7 operator, solve the inverse nearest-neighbor/step-line recurrence problem and then test whether the resulting recurrence coefficients come from positive measures. Merely choosing measures with the right moments is underdetermined.

The present computation is therefore a useful negative result: it removes the simplest two-measure HP model while preserving Hermite–Padé as a broader possible framework.
