# Katz/Deligne Mellin equidistribution versus defining-characteristic vanishing

## Executive verdict

**[FACT]** Standard Katz--Deligne Mellin equidistribution is an \(\ell\)-adic theory with \(\ell\neq p\).  After choosing an embedding \(\iota:\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\hookrightarrow\mathbf C\), it equidistributes normalized Frobenius conjugacy classes, or their complex traces, against Haar measure.  It excludes finitely many "bad" multiplicative characters and controls averages of fixed continuous class functions.  Katz has both:

- an extension-field theorem over a fixed finite field (Theorem 7.2/7.3 and Corollary 7.4), and
- a genuine horizontal theorem allowing prime fields \(\mathbf F_p\) with \(p\to\infty\), under a uniform common-monodromy hypothesis (Theorem 28.1).

**[DEDUCTION]** Such equidistribution can imply zero density for **literal algebraic/complex equality** \(M_p(\chi)=0\), provided the limiting trace measure has no atom at zero.  This follows from the Portmanteau theorem, even though the indicator of \(\{0\}\) is not continuous.

**[NEGATIVE RESULT]** It does **not** imply zero density for

\[
M_p(\chi)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p},\qquad \mathfrak p\mid p,
\]

or for the equivalent \(\mathbf F_p\)-valued condition in the question.  The complex embedding and the reduction at the moving defining prime are different places of an algebraic number field.  Weak complex equidistribution carries no information about the \(\mathfrak p\)-adic valuation.  A pure bounded-conductor Gauss-sum family gives an explicit counterexample: its normalized complex Mellin values are Haar-equidistributed on the unit circle while **every** value is divisible by \(p\).

**[SEARCH VERDICT]** I found no verified primary-source theorem of the required defining-characteristic form: no general \(\mathbf F_p\)-valued, integral-crystalline, overconvergent-\(F\)-isocrystal, or \(p\)-adic Mellin equidistribution theorem that bounds the atom

\[
\#\{\chi:M_p(\chi)=0\text{ in }\mathbf F_p\}.
\]

This is a report of the targeted primary-source search, not a claim that no such result can exist.  The Will Sawin paper whose title contains "Witt vector Dirichlet characters" is still explicitly an \(\ell\)-adic/complex unitary equidistribution theorem with \(\ell\nmid q\); "Witt vector" describes the character parameter/conductor, not the coefficient characteristic.  Sawin's work on wild Kloosterman sums gives stationary-phase evaluations for particular sums over local rings, not a general character-aspect residual atom theorem.

**[GAP FOR THIS PROJECT]** Even if the current Franel--Mellin object is promoted to a compatible pure \(\ell\)-adic system and its common horizontal monodromy group is proved, Katz would yield an archimedean Sato--Tate law, not residual anti-concentration.  A separate theorem of one of the following kinds is needed:

1. a sublinear-degree/fiber bound for the function of the character index;
2. square-root cancellation for the nonlinear additive sums \(\sum_r e_p(uM_p(r))\);
3. a defining-characteristic residual monodromy/Chebotarev theorem; or
4. a uniform local-limit/maximal-atom estimate in \(\mathbf F_p\).

None is standard or currently proved for the Franel--Apéry Mellin object.

# 1. What Katz's standard Mellin theorems actually say

## 1.1 Coefficient characteristic and normalization

**Primary source:** Nicholas M. Katz, *Convolution and Equidistribution: Sato--Tate Theorems for Finite-Field Mellin Transforms*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 180, Princeton University Press, 2012.

- Author PDF: https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/mellin398.pdf
- Publisher/DOI: https://doi.org/10.23943/princeton/9780691153308.001.0001

**[FACT--KATZ]** In the overview Katz fixes the characteristic \(p\) of the finite field, then chooses a prime \(\ell\neq p\) and an embedding

\[
\iota:\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\hookrightarrow\mathbf C.
\]

He realizes additive and multiplicative characters as \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times\)-valued characters, forms Artin--Schreier and Kummer sheaves, and uses \(\iota\) to discuss weights and complex absolute values.  See the author PDF, pp. 12--13 (printed pp. 8--9), especially the paragraph beginning "Fixing the characteristic \(p\)" and the half-Tate normalization immediately following it.

For a perverse object \(N\) on a form of \(\mathbf G_m\), the Mellin sum is

\[
S(N,E,\rho)
 =\sum_{t\in G(E)}\rho(t)
   \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{E,t}\mid N).
\]

The object is normalized to weight zero before the compact-group conjugacy class is formed.  Thus the theorem concerns algebraic \(\ell\)-adic traces and their complex images, not a sheaf with coefficient field \(\mathbf F_p\).

A crucial algebraic point is already visible here:

> There is no unital reduction map \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\to\mathbf F_p\) when \(\ell\neq p\), because \(p\) is a unit in every \(\ell\)-adic field but maps to zero in \(\mathbf F_p\).

To speak about reduction modulo \(p\), one needs a common algebraic number field/integral model and a **different** place \(\mathfrak p\mid p\).

## 1.2 Good and exceptional characters

**[FACT--KATZ]** Katz calls a multiplicative character \(\rho\) good for \(N\) when the canonical forget-supports map

\[
Rj_!(N\otimes\mathcal L_\rho)
 \longrightarrow
Rj_*(N\otimes\mathcal L_\rho)
\]

is an isomorphism.  See the author PDF, p. 16 (printed p. 12), and Lemma 3.3 on pp. 25--26.

For a good character, the relevant Mellin cohomology is pure of weight zero after normalization, and Frobenius determines a conjugacy class in a maximal compact subgroup.  The bad characters come from tame characters occurring in local monodromy at \(0\) or \(\infty\).  Katz states in the overview that there are at most

\[
2\,\operatorname{rk}(N)
\]

bad characters; see author PDF p. 17 (printed p. 13).

**[DEDUCTION]** In a bounded-rank family, deleting \(O(1)\) bad characters cannot affect a density statement among \(p-1\) characters.  It can, however, matter for individual special characters and must be respected in any exact formula.

## 1.3 Extension-field equidistribution

**[FACT--KATZ]** The general main theorem is Theorem 7.2, author PDF p. 44 (printed p. 40).  In the common simpler case

\[
G_{\rm geom,N}=G_{\rm arith,N},
\]

Theorem 7.3 says that, as \(E/k\) runs through larger finite extensions, the good-character Frobenius conjugacy classes become equidistributed for Haar measure on the conjugacy classes of a maximal compact group \(K\).  Corollary 7.4 pushes this measure forward by the trace map and gives equidistribution of the complex Mellin sums.  See author PDF p. 47 (printed p. 43).

The exact limiting statement is weak convergence.  Equivalently, for every fixed continuous central function \(F\) on \(K\),

\[
\frac{1}{\#\operatorname{Good}(E,N)}
\sum_{\rho\in\operatorname{Good}(E,N)}F(\theta_{E,\rho})
 \longrightarrow
\int_K F(k)\,dk.
\]

Remark 7.5, author PDF pp. 47--48 (printed pp. 43--44), gives an \(O(\#E^{-1/2})\) estimate for the character of a **fixed irreducible representation** of the Tannakian group.  The constant involves the generic rank and Tannakian dimension of the corresponding tensor construction.

This is important for interpreting what is controlled:

- fixed continuous class functions: yes;
- fixed irreducible representation characters and fixed moments: yes, effectively;
- fixed Borel continuity sets: yes, by weak convergence;
- a \(p\)-dependent congruence class in an integral lattice: no;
- a test function whose frequency/complexity grows with \(p\): not from Remark 7.5 without new uniform estimates.

## 1.4 Katz also has a horizontal theorem across primes

**[FACT--KATZ]** The introduction explicitly says that prime-field equidistribution is addressed in Chapter 28.  The general result is Theorem 28.1, author PDF pp. 177--179 (printed pp. 173--175).

It fixes:

- one prime \(\ell\);
- one reductive group \(G/\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\), one faithful \(n\)-dimensional representation, and one compact form \(K\);
- finite fields \(k_i\), all of characteristic different from \(\ell\), with \(\#k_i\to\infty\);
- pure, arithmetically semisimple perverse objects \(N_i\) satisfying
  \[
  G_{\rm geom,N_i}=G_{\rm arith,N_i}=G;
  \]
- a uniform bound on generic rank and on \(\#\operatorname{Bad}(N_i)\).

Then the good-character conjugacy classes over \(k_i\) equidistribute in \(K^\#\).  Katz explicitly lists a sequence of prime fields \(\mathbf F_{p_i}\) as an allowed example immediately before the theorem.

**[FACT--EFFECTIVITY]** The proof gives, for each fixed irreducible nontrivial representation \(\Lambda\) occurring in tensor degree \(a+b\), a bound of shape

\[
O_{\Lambda,C}(\#k_i^{-1/2}).
\]

Again, \(\Lambda\) is fixed.  This does not approximate an indicator that changes on a mesh of size \(1/p\) or \(p^{-1/2}\).

**[GAP--APPLICATION]** For the Franel--Apéry object, Chapter 28 is relevant to the complex horizontal problem, but the current repository has not proved all of its hypotheses.  In particular, local monodromy and absence of Kummer self-twists do not alone prove one common arithmetic Tannakian group for every prime.

## 1.5 Earlier Gauss-sum equidistribution

**Primary source:** Nicholas M. Katz, *Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 116, Princeton University Press, 1988.

- Author PDF: https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/Katz-GKM.pdf

Chapter 9, Theorems 9.3 and 9.5, author PDF pp. 82--83 (printed pp. 158--160), prove weak convergence of normalized \(r\)-tuples of Gauss sums to Haar measure on \((S^1)^r\).  The variable multiplicative character omits the finite list for which one of the shifted characters becomes trivial.  The theorem is explicitly stated against every continuous complex-valued test function.

This is the cleanest model for the scope of Mellin Sato--Tate: it describes complex angles of normalized Gauss sums.  It does not describe their reduction at the prime defining the finite field.

## 1.6 Recent generalization: Forey--Fresán--Kowalski

**Primary source:** Arthur Forey, Javier Fresán, and Emmanuel Kowalski, *Arithmetic Fourier transforms over finite fields: generic vanishing, convolution, and equidistribution*.

- arXiv v6: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.11961v6

The paper generalizes Katz from \(\mathbf G_m\) to connected commutative algebraic groups.  Its sheaf-theoretic setup again fixes \(\ell\) invertible in the base characteristic.  Section 4.7, Theorem 4.19, PDF pp. 95--96, gives a horizontal equidistribution statement under a stated uniform-complexity hypothesis.  Its output is again unitary/complex conjugacy-class equidistribution.

There is a genuinely mod-\(p\) result in the same paper, Proposition 6.8, PDF pp. 120--121.  It proves uniform distribution modulo \(p\) of values

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{k_n/\mathbf F_p}
 f(y_n^{v_1},\ldots,y_n^{v_d})
\]

as exponent vectors range in large boxes, using additive characters.  This is worth separating carefully from the present problem:

- it starts with an actual morphism \(f:\mathbf G_m^d\to\mathbf A^r\) in defining characteristic;
- its value is a field trace of a polynomial/morphism value;
- it is not the reduction modulo \(p\) of an \(\ell\)-adic Mellin Frobenius trace.

Thus Proposition 6.8 shows that defining-characteristic equidistribution is possible when one has a low-complexity algebraic formula in the exponent variables.  It does not provide such a formula for the Franel Mellin trace.

# 2. What complex equidistribution does and does not imply

## 2.1 It can count literal complex zeros

Let

\[
\mu_p=\frac1{N_p}\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p}
 \delta_{z_{p,\chi}}
\]

be empirical measures of normalized complex Mellin values, and suppose

\[
\mu_p\Rightarrow\mu
\]

weakly.  The singleton \(\{0\}\) is closed, so the Portmanteau theorem gives

\[
\limsup_{p\to\infty}\mu_p(\{0\})
 \leq \mu(\{0\}).
\]

Therefore:

**[DEDUCTION--LITERAL ZERO]** If the limiting trace measure has no atom at zero, then

\[
\#\{\chi:z_{p,\chi}=0\}=o(N_p).
\]

Since normalization is by a nonzero scalar, this is the same as literal equality of the algebraic Mellin trace to zero.

For a connected compact group and a nonzero analytic trace function, the zero locus normally has Haar measure zero; for example the standard \(SU(2)\) trace has the continuous semicircle law.  One must nevertheless check the actual representation and disconnected components: a whole component can, in principle, have identically zero trace and create an atom.

This is the exact positive consequence of Katz that should be retained.

## 2.2 Residual zero is a different event

The desired event is

\[
M_{p}(\chi)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p},
\qquad \mathfrak p\mid p,
\]

where \(M_p(\chi)\) is an algebraic integer in some number field containing the character values and Frobenius trace.

The complex datum is

\[
\iota_\infty(M_p(\chi))\in\mathbf C,
\]

whereas the congruence datum is

\[
v_{\mathfrak p}(M_p(\chi))>0.
\]

There is no continuity relation between these two places.  A nonzero algebraic integer can be arbitrarily large or perfectly generic at every complex embedding and still lie in \(\mathfrak p\).

**[DEDUCTION--NO FORMAL IMPLICATION]** Weak convergence of the complex embeddings alone cannot bound the proportion of \(\mathfrak p\)-divisible values.  The set

\[
\mathfrak p\subset\mathcal O_K
\]

has no image as a fixed continuity set in the compact Sato--Tate space.

## 2.3 A pure bounded-conductor counterexample

This gives a direct sheaf-theoretic counterexample, not merely an abstract sequence.

For each prime \(p\), choose the standard nontrivial additive character

\[
\psi_p(t)=e^{2\pi i t/p}
\]

and for every nontrivial multiplicative character \(\chi\) define the Gauss sum

\[
G_p(\chi)=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\psi_p(t)\chi(t).
\]

Katz's Chapter 9 theorem gives Haar equidistribution of

\[
\frac{G_p(\chi)}{\sqrt p}\in S^1
\]

as \(\chi\) varies and \(p\to\infty\).  The input is the rank-one Artin--Schreier sheaf, of uniformly bounded conductor.

Now tensor that sheaf by the constant rank-one \(\ell\)-adic representation on which geometric Frobenius acts by \(p\) (equivalently, a Tate twist with the convention chosen so the trace is multiplied by \(p\)).  Its geometric conductor is unchanged, it remains pure after the corresponding weight shift, and its Mellin values are

\[
M'_p(\chi)=pG_p(\chi).
\]

After the correct normalization,

\[
\frac{M'_p(\chi)}{p^{3/2}}
 =\frac{G_p(\chi)}{\sqrt p},
\]

so the normalized complex Mellin values have exactly the same Haar limit.  But \(G_p(\chi)\) is an algebraic integer and hence

\[
M'_p(\chi)\in p\mathcal O_K
\]

for every \(\chi\).  Thus the residual zero proportion is \(1\), while the limiting complex distribution is continuous and has no atom at zero.  Moreover, nontrivial Gauss sums are nonzero as algebraic/complex numbers.

This proves:

\[
\boxed{\text{pure bounded conductor + complex Mellin equidistribution does not imply residual zero density.}}
\]

A second illustration shows that even the entire residual behavior can be changed by a term invisible to the limiting complex distribution.  Add the punctual trace function \(\delta_1(t)\):

\[
M''_p(\chi)=pG_p(\chi)+1.
\]

Then

\[
M''_p(\chi)/p^{3/2}
 -M'_p(\chi)/p^{3/2}\to0
\]

uniformly, so the weak complex limit is unchanged, whereas

\[
M''_p(\chi)\equiv1\pmod p
\]

for every \(\chi\).  This second family is mixed rather than pure, so the first example is the load-bearing counterexample; the perturbation simply makes the non-determinacy vivid.

## 2.4 Why the Franel weight makes the scale mismatch concrete

Suppose, as in the expected rank-two Mellin realization, the integral Mellin trace has Weil weight \(3\), so that typically

\[
|M_p(\chi)|\asymp p^{3/2}.
\]

For a rational integer value, divisibility by \(p\) means

\[
\frac{M_p(\chi)}{p^{3/2}}
 \in p^{-1/2}\mathbf Z.
\]

The grid spacing \(p^{-1/2}\) tends to zero.  The target set is therefore a \(p\)-dependent, increasingly fine lattice inside the fixed compact Sato--Tate interval.  Weak convergence at fixed macroscopic scale does not resolve such a lattice.  A local-limit theorem at scale \(p^{-1/2}\), not ordinary Sato--Tate, is required.

By contrast, there is one useful special bridge:

**[SUFFICIENT BUT INAPPLICABLE BRIDGE]** If \(M_p(\chi)\in\mathbf Z\) and

\[
|M_p(\chi)|<p
\]

for all but \(o(p)\) characters, then \(p\mid M_p(\chi)\) forces \(M_p(\chi)=0\).  In that special low-height regime, a non-atomic complex trace law would imply residual zero density.  The expected \(p^{3/2}\) size in this project is outside that regime.

## 2.5 Why auxiliary-\(\ell\) Frobenius large sieves do not repair the gap

**Primary source:** Corentin Perret-Gentil, *Exponential Sums over Finite Fields and the Large Sieve*, International Mathematics Research Notices 2020, no. 20, 7139--7174.

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.06965
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rny202

The abstract and Section 1 formulate zero-density estimates for \(\ell\)-adic trace functions taking values in fixed algebraic subsets of cyclotomic integers, using reductions of compatible systems at auxiliary primes \(\lambda\) and finite monodromy groups.  This can detect literal algebraic zero or membership in a fixed thin set.

For the moving condition \(M\in p\mathcal O_K\), however, every auxiliary \(\lambda\nmid p\) sees no restriction at all:

\[
p\mathcal O_K\pmod\lambda
 =\mathcal O_K/\lambda,
\]

because \(p\) is invertible modulo \(\lambda\).  Taking \(\lambda\mid p\) destroys the auxiliary-prime setup and collides with the requirement that the sheaf coefficient characteristic be different from the base characteristic.

**[DEDUCTION]** The auxiliary-prime large sieve is literally blind to defining-prime divisibility.  Its zero-density results do not imply the desired statement.

# 3. Defining-characteristic and \(p\)-adic literature audit

## 3.1 Overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystals do not intrinsically remember reduction modulo \(p\)

An overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal is an isogeny object: its coefficient field has \(p\) inverted.  Multiplication by \(p\) is an automorphism.  Consequently the statement "the Frobenius trace is divisible by \(p\)" is not intrinsic to the isocrystal alone.

To formulate the target event one needs additional integral data:

- an \(F\)-crystal or Frobenius-stable lattice;
- a chosen integral comparison with the integer coefficient or Hasse--Witt scalar; and
- control that remains valid on the nonordinary locus.

Changing the lattice within one isocrystal can change its mod-\(p\) reduction.  Therefore an equidistribution theorem for isocrystal conjugacy classes, even if available, would not automatically control the atom of an integral Hasse invariant.

## 3.2 Dwork/Hasse--Witt/unit-root results: relevant structure, not a zero-count theorem

**Primary source:** Alan Adolphson and Steven Sperber, *A-hypergeometric series and a \(p\)-adic refinement of the Hasse--Witt matrix*.

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07280

The paper studies a family of projective hypersurfaces with variable geometric coefficients \(\lambda\).  In the introduction:

- equation (1.2) defines the Hasse--Witt matrix;
- Proposition 1.3 and Corollary 1.6 give its mod-\(p\) relation to the unit-root factor;
- Theorem 1.10 gives a \(p\)-adic analytic unit-root formula using a matrix \(F(\Lambda^p)^{-1}F(\Lambda)\), under the nonvanishing condition \(\overline D(\lambda)\neq0\).

See PDF pp. 1--4.

This is powerful defining-characteristic structure, but it has the wrong counting variable and the wrong locus for the present question:

1. \(\lambda\) varies in a geometric parameter space; here the geometric family is fixed and a tame multiplicative character varies.
2. The unit-root formula uses inversion of the Hasse--Witt determinant and is stated on the ordinary locus \(\overline D(\lambda)\neq0\).  The desired zero event is precisely where the first Hasse invariant fails to be invertible.
3. No theorem there gives anti-concentration of a Hasse--Witt entry as a character index ranges through \(\mathbf Z/(p-1)\).

A division-free integral Frobenius module valid at zero could be a useful reformulation, but sparse dependence on the character index would still be a new theorem.

## 3.3 What Will Sawin's Witt-vector theorem actually says

**Primary source:** Will Sawin, *The equidistribution of L-functions of twists by Witt vector Dirichlet characters over function fields*.

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04330

Theorems 1.2 and 1.3, PDF pp. 2--3, explicitly assume:

\[
\ell\nmid q_0,
\qquad
\iota:\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\hookrightarrow\mathbf C,
\]

and start from continuous \(\ell\)-adic representations or middle-extension \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\)-sheaves that are \(\iota\)-pure.  For a set of Witt-vector Dirichlet characters of size \((1-O(1/q))q^d\), the normalized Frobenius classes lie in unitary groups, and the theorem averages **continuous conjugacy-invariant functions** of those classes.

Thus:

**[FACT--SAWIN]** This is an \(\ell\)-adic/complex equidistribution theorem.  "Witt vector" refers to the ray-class/character parameterization and high conductor at infinity.  It does not mean that the Frobenius traces are \(W(\mathbf F_p)\)-valued and then equidistributed modulo \(p\), and it does not control a residual atom at zero.

## 3.4 Other Sawin work found in the targeted search

**Primary source:** Will Sawin, *The size of wild Kloosterman sums in number fields and function fields*.

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.02170

The introduction and Theorem 1.1/Proposition 1.2 study hyper-Kloosterman sums over quotients of discrete valuation rings using stationary phase, including cases where the rank is divisible by the residue characteristic.  The paper gives upper/lower bounds and, for particular phases, evaluations or vanishing information.

It is not a theorem that fixes a bounded-conductor geometric trace function on \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), varies all tame characters, and proves anti-concentration of the resulting \(\mathbf F_p\)-valued Mellin transform.

Recent quantitative sheaf theory involving Sawin likewise works with \(\ell\)-adic sheaves with \(\ell\) invertible on the base; it supplies uniform complexity bounds for cohomology and horizontal complex equidistribution, not a defining-prime reduction theorem.

## 3.5 Gross--Koblitz does not supply equidistribution at zero

**Primary source:** Benedict H. Gross and Neal Koblitz, *Gauss sums and the \(p\)-adic \(\Gamma\)-function*, Annals of Mathematics 109 (1979), 569--581.

- Article/DOI: https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1979/109-3/p06

Gross--Koblitz gives an exact \(p\)-adic formula for Gauss sums in terms of the \(p\)-adic gamma function.  It is a conversion/evaluation theorem.  Applied term by term, it can expose valuations and carry strata; it does not state that a family of sums is equidistributed modulo \(p\), nor does it bound the number of character indices at which a long combination vanishes.

## 3.6 Search conclusion

**[SEARCH VERDICT, CAREFULLY LIMITED]** In the primary sources above and targeted searches through recent work on:

- Katz Mellin/convolution sheaves;
- arithmetic Fourier transforms;
- Witt-vector Dirichlet twists;
- wild \(p\)-adic Kloosterman sums;
- overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystals and Hasse--Witt/unit-root formulas; and
- Frobenius large sieves,

I found no theorem whose conclusion has the required form

\[
\#\left\{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbf F_p^\times}:
 \sum_t f_p(t)\chi(t)=0\text{ in }\mathbf F_p\right\}=o(p)
\]

for a general fixed bounded-conductor trace-like family \(f_p\).

The closest genuinely mod-\(p\) statement located is Forey--Fresán--Kowalski Proposition 6.8, but it requires an explicit defining-characteristic algebraic morphism in the exponent variables, which is exactly the additional structure not yet available here.

# 4. Exact coefficient-field and character mismatch

## 4.1 \(\mathbf F_p\)-valued characters do exist, but they are power maps

There is a small correction to one possible reading of the prompt.

Because \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) is cyclic of order \(p-1\), every group homomorphism

\[
\chi:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\mathbf F_p^\times
\]

is a power map

\[
\chi_r(t)=t^r,\qquad r\in\mathbf Z/(p-1)\mathbf Z.
\]

The map \(r\mapsto(t\mapsto t^r)\) is canonical; no generator is needed to write it.  A generator is needed only if one wants to identify the index \(r\) with a point \(g^r\in\mathbf F_p^\times\).

Thus the defining-characteristic discrete Mellin transform is simply

\[
\widehat f_p(r)
 =\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}f_p(t)t^{-r}
 \in\mathbf F_p.
\]

## 4.2 Katz's character is a root-of-unity-valued lift

Katz instead uses

\[
\rho:\mathbf F_p^\times\to
 \mu_{p-1}\subset\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times,
\qquad \ell\neq p,
\]

and then applies \(\iota:\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\to\mathbf C\).

The \(p\)-adic Teichmüller lift is

\[
\omega:\mathbf F_p^\times\to
 \mu_{p-1}\subset W(\mathbf F_p)^\times=\mathbf Z_p^\times,
\qquad
\omega(t)\equiv t\pmod p.
\]

Hence, for an integral \(p\)-adic lift \(\widetilde f_p(t)\),

\[
\sum_t\widetilde f_p(t)\omega(t)^{-r}\pmod p
 =\sum_tf_p(t)t^{-r}.
\]

This equality lives at the \(p\)-adic place.

To compare it with an \(\ell\)-adic Frobenius trace, one needs all of the following:

1. the trace and character values lie in a common algebraic number field;
2. they are integral at a chosen prime \(\mathfrak p\mid p\);
3. the chosen \(\mathfrak p\) identifies the algebraic \((p-1)\)-st roots of unity with \(\mathbf F_p^\times\); and
4. the geometric trace reduces to the intended \(f_p(t)\) at every relevant stalk.

A complex embedding and a \(p\)-adic embedding are independent choices.  The first gives Sato--Tate; the second gives divisibility.

## 4.3 If only the \(\mathbf F_p\)-function is known, there is no canonical complex lift

An arbitrary \(f_p:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\mathbf F_p\) has many algebraic or \(p\)-adic lifts.  Two lifts can have the same reduction but completely different complex sizes and distributions.  Conversely, two bounded-complexity algebraic trace functions can have the same normalized complex limit but opposite residual behavior, as in the Gauss-sum example above.

Therefore the statement

> "\(M_p(\omega^{-r})\) is equidistributed by Katz, so \(M_p(t^{-r})\neq0\) modulo \(p\) for almost all \(r\)"

contains an invalid change of coefficient field and topology.

# 5. Precise sufficient hypotheses that would prove zero density

Write

\[
M_p(r)=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}f_p(t)t^{-r}\in\mathbf F_p,
\qquad 1\le r\le p-2,
\]

and

\[
Z_p=\#\{1\le r\le p-2:M_p(r)=0\}.
\]

The following are genuine sufficient conditions.

## 5.1 A sublinear-degree fiber bound

Fix a generator \(g_p\) of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\).  Suppose there is a nonzero polynomial \(P_p(X)\in\mathbf F_p[X]\) such that

\[
M_p(r)=P_p(g_p^r)
\]

for all relevant \(r\), with

\[
\deg P_p=d_p=o(p).
\]

Then distinct \(r\bmod p-1\) give distinct \(g_p^r\), so

\[
Z_p\le d_p=o(p).
\]

The same conclusion holds for a rational function if the total numerator degree plus the number of poles is \(o(p)\), or more generally for a map whose zero fiber has \(o(p)\) points.

**Status for this project:** not known.  The repository's interpolation experiments find degree \(p-1\) or \(p-3\) for the direct polynomial interpolation of \(r\mapsto b_r\bmod p\), so the naive low-degree route is empirically unavailable.  A low-dimensional matrix recursion or stratified formula could still exist, but it has not been proved.

## 5.2 Additive anti-concentration in the Mellin value

Let \(e_p(x)=e^{2\pi i x/p}\).  The exact additive-character identity is

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_p
&=\sum_{r=1}^{p-2}\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(uM_p(r))\\
&=\frac{p-2}{p}
 +\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p^\times}S_p(u),
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
S_p(u)=\sum_{r=1}^{p-2}e_p(uM_p(r)).
\]

Therefore the uniform bound

\[
\max_{u\neq0}|S_p(u)|=o(p)
\]

implies \(Z_p=o(p)\).  A square-root bound

\[
|S_p(u)|\ll p^{1/2}
\]

would give

\[
Z_p\ll p^{1/2}.
\]

**Status for this project:** this is a precise attractive target, but it is not a standard corollary of sheaf theory for the original Mellin object.  The phase

\[
r\longmapsto e_p(u\,\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_r))
\]

is an additive character of a **value of a trace**.  It is not obtained by tensoring the original sheaf with an Artin--Schreier sheaf on its geometric parameter unless the trace value itself has first been realized as a bounded-complexity algebraic function of \(r\).  That realization is the missing step.

## 5.3 A residual monodromy/Chebotarev theorem

Suppose one constructs, on a genuine algebraic parameter space whose \(\mathbf F_p\)-points encode the characters, a residual representation

\[
\overline\rho_p:\pi_1(X_p)\to G_p
\]

such that

\[
M_p(r)=\operatorname{tr}\overline\rho_p(\operatorname{Frob}_r),
\]

and proves an effective Chebotarev estimate

\[
\#\{r:M_p(r)=0\}
 =|X_p(\mathbf F_p)|
   \frac{|\{g\in G_p:\operatorname{tr}g=0\}|}{|G_p|}
  +o(p).
\]

If additionally

\[
\frac{|\{g\in G_p:\operatorname{tr}g=0\}|}{|G_p|}\to0,
\]

then \(Z_p=o(p)\).

**Status for this project:** this is the correct shape of a mod-\(p\) monodromy/sieve theorem, but no such residual character-parameter sheaf has been constructed.  The usual \(\ell\)-adic Mellin Tannakian group is not this object.

## 5.4 A local-limit or maximum-atom bound

Any estimate

\[
\max_{a\in\mathbf F_p}
\#\{r:M_p(r)=a\}=o(p)
\]

immediately gives zero density.  A stronger uniform local limit such as

\[
\#\{r:M_p(r)=a\}
 =\frac{p-2}{p}+O(p^{1/2})
\]

would give \(Z_p=O(p^{1/2})\).

This is essentially equivalent to the additive-character target in §5.2.  It is not part of standard Katz Sato--Tate.

## 5.5 What would be enough from a division-free crystalline construction

A division-free integral Mellin Frobenius module would become a counting mechanism if it supplied, uniformly in \(p\), one of:

- a first Hasse coefficient that is a nonzero polynomial of degree \(o(p)\) in a character coordinate;
- a bounded-size matrix product with only \(p^{o(1)}\) carry/valuation strata and a proven zero-fiber bound;
- an additive trace formula giving \(o(p)\) bounds for every nontrivial additive Fourier coefficient; or
- a residual monodromy group with an effective Chebotarev theorem.

A mere identity

\[
p\mid M_p(r)
\iff \text{first Hasse invariant at }r\text{ vanishes}
\]

is tautological unless accompanied by one of these complexity/anti-concentration statements.

# 6. Final project-specific adjudication

## Facts

1. Katz's Mellin theory is \(\ell\)-adic with \(\ell\neq p\), then complex via \(\iota\).
2. It excludes finitely many bad characters; bounded rank gives a uniformly negligible exceptional set.
3. It controls fixed continuous class functions and fixed tensor-character moments.
4. Katz Theorem 28.1 genuinely permits horizontal prime-field limits.
5. Literal complex zero has density zero when the limiting trace law has no atom at zero.
6. Sawin's Witt-vector twist theorem is also \(\ell\)-adic/complex, not defining-characteristic.
7. Dwork/Hasse--Witt formulas and Gross--Koblitz identities provide arithmetic structure but no character-aspect zero-count theorem.

## Deductions

1. Reduction at \(\mathfrak p\mid p\) is invisible to the complex place without an additional local-limit or height theorem.
2. The pure Gauss-sum/Tate-twist counterexample rules out any general implication from bounded conductor plus complex equidistribution to residual zero density.
3. Auxiliary-\(\ell\) large sieves cannot detect divisibility by the moving defining prime.
4. For a weight-three Franel Mellin trace, the residual event lives on a \(p^{-1/2}\)-mesh after normalization, below the resolution of ordinary weak Sato--Tate.

## Gaps

1. The repository still needs the arithmetic compatible-system comparison at all relevant stalks if it wants a literal \(\ell\)-adic lift of the exact \(\mathbf F_p\) identity.
2. It still needs the common horizontal Tannakian group and Katz Theorem 28.1 hypotheses for an archimedean theorem.
3. Most importantly, even after 1--2, it needs a new defining-characteristic anti-concentration theorem.  This is logically independent of Katz's complex equidistribution.

## Bottom line

\[
\boxed{
\text{Katz/Deligne Mellin equidistribution does not, by itself, yield }
\#\{r:M_p(r)=0\text{ in }\mathbf F_p\}=o(p).
}
\]

It can justify an archimedean Sato--Tate law and, under a non-atomic trace law, zero density of **literal algebraic zeros**.  The desired congruence is a local defining-characteristic event and requires a residual/local-limit input not found in the standard or recent primary literature audited here.
