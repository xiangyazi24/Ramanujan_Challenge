# Zero-set sizes of Apéry-like sequences and monodromy

## Executive summary

The proposed heuristic is essentially correct, but with an important refinement:

- Infinite **geometric monodromy** generally predicts that a Frobenius trace/unit-root section behaves like a genuinely varying object over \(\mathbf F_p\), so a zero condition has codimension one probability about \(1/p\). Hence \(|Z_p|\) should be bounded or slowly growing (often experimentally Poisson-like).
- Finite monodromy means the solution space comes from a finite algebraic covering. The sequence is then controlled by finitely many residue classes/periodic patterns, and a positive density of residues can vanish, giving \(|Z_p|=\Theta(p)\).
- Intermediate cases are governed by the dimension of the monodromy orbit of the relevant section, not simply finite versus infinite.

The prediction is therefore a geometric Lang--Chebotarev heuristic rather than a theorem in general.

---

## (A) \(\zeta(2)\) Apéry sequence

Definition:

\[
a_n=\sum_{k=0}^n {n\choose k}^2{n+k\choose k}.
\]

Initial values:

\[
a_0=1,\qquad a_1=3.
\]

Recurrence:

\[
(n+1)^2a_{n+1}=(11n^2+11n+3)a_n+n^2a_{n-1}.
\]

The generating function is a rank-2 Picard--Fuchs solution attached to a family of elliptic curves. The differential Galois group is infinite; generically it is an elliptic modular type group (a subgroup of \(SL_2\), not finite).

Prediction:

\[
|Z_p|=\#\{0\le r<p:a_r\equiv0\pmod p\}=O(1)
\]

is the expected behavior.

There is no known Kummer-style digit criterion. Modulo \(p\), the sequence has a Lucas-type decomposition, but the zero condition is controlled by elliptic Frobenius data rather than carries.

---

## (B) Zagier sporadic sequence

For the sporadic sequence:

\[
(n+1)^2u_{n+1}=(7n^2+7n+2)u_n+8n^2u_{n-1},
\]

with the standard Apéry-like initial conditions.

It has a rank-2 Picard--Fuchs equation. The associated differential equation is modular/elliptic and has infinite monodromy.

Prediction:

\[
|Z_p|=O(1).
\]

Again, there is no analogue of Kummer's theorem. The zeros are expected to correspond to exceptional Frobenius conditions on the underlying elliptic modular object.

---

## (C) Domb numbers

Definition:

\[
D_n=\sum_k {n\choose k}^2{2k\choose k}{2n-2k\choose n-k}.
\]

These count abelian squares.

The generating function is a Calabi--Yau type period. The Picard--Fuchs equation has rank 4 and the monodromy is infinite. In the AESZ classification the Domb operator is one of the standard Calabi--Yau operators with symplectic structure.

Expected monodromy:

\[
G\subset Sp_4,
\]

with Zariski-dense infinite image.

Prediction:

\[
|Z_p|=O(1)
\]

(or at worst a very slowly growing function of \(p\)).

No Kummer criterion is known. The natural replacement is a Frobenius condition on the four-dimensional period vector.

---

## (D) Franel numbers

Definition:

\[
f_n=\sum_k {n\choose k}^3.
\]

The recurrence is

\[
(n+1)^2f_{n+1}=(7n^2+7n+2)f_n+8n^2f_{n-1}.
\]

This is the same recurrence as the Zagier sporadic sequence after normalization. The alternative cubic recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3f_{n+1}=(2n+1)(7n^2+7n+2)f_n-8n^3f_{n-1}
\]

is **not** the recurrence for the ordinary Franel numbers; it belongs to a different Apéry-like normalization.

The associated Picard--Fuchs equation has infinite monodromy (elliptic/hypergeometric type).

Prediction:

\[
|Z_p|=O(1).
\]

There is no known carry criterion comparable to Kummer's theorem.

---

## (E) Almkvist--Zudilin / AESZ rank-4 sequences

A representative AZ sequence is

\[
Z_n=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k{3n+3k\choose n-k}{3k\choose k}{2k\choose k}.
\]

Many AZ sequences have rank-4 Calabi--Yau Picard--Fuchs equations.

The generic monodromy is

\[
G\subset Sp_4(\mathbf Z),
\]

and in the interesting cases is Zariski-dense in \(Sp_4\).

Prediction:

\[
|Z_p|=O(1).
\]

The mechanism is the same as for Apéry \(\zeta(3)\): the zero condition is a codimension-one condition on a varying Frobenius orbit.

# (F) Finite versus infinite monodromy

Among (A)--(E), none are genuinely finite-monodromy examples.

Finite-monodromy examples are instead hypergeometric algebraic sequences, for example:

\[
\binom{2r}{r}
\]

or more generally Schwarz hypergeometric cases.

For

\[
c_r={2r\choose r},
\]

Kummer gives

\[
v_p(c_r)=\text{number of carries when adding }r+r\text{ in base }p.
\]

Thus

\[
p\mid c_r
\]

iff there is a base-\(p\) carry. For \(0\le r<p\), this never happens, but for longer digit ranges the zero set has positive density. More generally finite monodromy gives periodic algebraic conditions and hence

\[
|Z_p|=\Theta(p)
\]

when the zero locus is nonempty of positive density.

# (G) Intermediate monodromy

Yes. Apéry-like operators can have monodromy that is infinite but not Zariski dense in the ambient symplectic group.

Examples include:

1. Operators arising from symmetric powers of elliptic curves.
2. Tensor products with extra endomorphisms.
3. Calabi--Yau operators with CM or extra Hodge cycles.

The prediction depends on the orbit dimension.

If the relevant Frobenius section moves on a variety of dimension \(d\), while the zero condition has codimension \(c\), then the heuristic probability is roughly

\[
p^{-c}
\]

only when the orbit is sufficiently large. If the orbit is constrained by monodromy, the density can increase.

A useful classification is:

```
finite monodromy:
    periodic/algebraic values
    |Z_p| ~ p

Zariski-dense infinite monodromy:
    random Frobenius values
    |Z_p| ~ constant

intermediate monodromy:
    depends on orbit dimension
    p^alpha behavior possible
```

For the Ramanujan Challenge Apéry \(\zeta(3)\) sequence, the observed Poisson(1) law is exactly what the last regime predicts for a rank-4 \(Sp_4\)-type motive with a one-dimensional zero condition.
