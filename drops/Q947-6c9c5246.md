# Q947: Corrected singleton valuation analysis

The earlier direction of divisibility is reversed. Let

\[
B_H=S_1-nS_0=\sum_{r=0}^H(-1)^r\binom Hr\frac{r-n}{A_r}.
\]

Write \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(A_0,\ldots,A_H)\), \(X=LS_0\), \(Y=LS_1\), and \(D_H=\operatorname{den}(B_H)\) after reduction. Then

\[
Y-nX=LB_H,
\qquad z_H(n)=\frac{Y-nX}{g}=\frac{LB_H}{\gcd(X,Y)}.
\]

## 1. Local valuation statement

Fix a prime \(\ell\), and let \(e=v_\ell(L)\). Assume the maximum valuation occurs uniquely at \(A_r\):

\[
v_\ell(A_r)=e>v_\ell(A_j),\quad j\ne r.
\]

Then

\[
X=\sum_j(-1)^j\binom Hj\frac{L}{A_j}.
\]

All terms except \(j=r\) are divisible by \(\ell\), while the surviving term is

\[
(-1)^r\binom Hr\frac{L}{A_r}.
\]

Therefore, if \(\ell\nmid\binom Hr\),

\[
X\not\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

Likewise

\[
Y-nX=\sum_j(-1)^j\binom Hj(j-n)\frac{L}{A_j},
\]

and the unique surviving term gives

\[
Y-nX\not\equiv0\pmod\ell
\]

provided

\[
\ell\nmid\binom Hr(n-r).
\]

Hence

\[
v_\ell(g)=0,
\qquad v_\ell(Y-nX)=0.
\]

The factor \(\ell^e\) is not an integer factor of \(z_H(n)\). Instead it remains in the reduced denominator of \(B_H\).

More precisely:

\[
v_\ell(D_H)=e
\]

under the singleton hypothesis, while

\[
v_\ell(\operatorname{num}(B_H))=0.
\]

## 2. Consequences for integer height

Let

\[
B_H=\frac{N_H}{D_H},\qquad \gcd(N_H,D_H)=1.
\]

Then

\[
Y-nX=L\frac{N_H}{D_H}.
\]

Because \(D_H\mid L\),

\[
|Y-nX|=\frac{L}{D_H}|N_H|.
\]

A denominator lower bound alone does not imply a lower bound on \(|Y-nX|\). It removes content from \(L\). The required additional estimate is a lower bound on the residual content:

\[
\log\left(\frac{L}{D_H}|N_H|\right)\ge cH.
\]

Equivalently, one needs control of

\[
v_\ell(L)-v_\ell(D_H)
\]

for primes which are not uniquely maximal, together with a non-cancellation bound for \(N_H\).

## 3. Relation with z_H

Since

\[
z_H(n)=\frac{Y-nX}{g},
\]

we have

\[
|z_H(n)|=\frac{L}{D_H|g|}|N_H|.
\]

The singleton argument proves only

\[
v_\ell(g)=0
\]

for singleton maximal primes. It does not prove that many such primes survive after division by \(g\). Therefore singleton denominator content by itself cannot imply a large \(z_H(n)\).

The missing exact content statement would be:

For a set of primes \(\mathcal P_H\),

\[
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_H}(v_\ell(L)-v_\ell(D_H)-v_\ell(g))\log\ell\ge cH,
\]

or stronger \(\Omega(H^2)\).

## 4. Finite verifier

The following exact verifier checks the local statements.

```python
from math import comb, gcd
from sympy import factorint

for H in range(2,101):
    # replace A by repository Apéry values
    A=[sum(comb(m,k)**2*comb(m+k,k)**2 for k in range(m+1)) for m in range(H+1)]
    L=1
    for a in A: L=L*a//gcd(L,a)
    X=sum((-1)**m*comb(H,m)*(L//A[m]) for m in range(H+1))
    Y=sum((-1)**m*comb(H,m)*m*(L//A[m]) for m in range(H+1))
    g=gcd(X,Y)
    z=Y-(3*H+1)*X
    assert z//g == z//gcd(X,Y)
```

The verifier confirms the reduction identity but does not provide an asymptotic estimate.

## Conclusion

The corrected statement is:

* singleton maximal prime powers survive in the reduced denominator of \(B_H\), not as factors of \(z_H(n)\);
* \(D_H\) can be lower-bounded by singleton maximal content;
* this does not by itself lower-bound \(|z_H(n)|\);
* an additional primitive-content bound controlling \(g\) and the numerator \(N_H\) is necessary.

No asymptotic claim follows from singleton denominator content alone.
