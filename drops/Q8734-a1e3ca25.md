# Pointwise sieve, attempt 3

## Verdict

No complete all-\(n\) proof emerges from the three routes. However, the companion channel can be closed **unconditionally**, without any prime-factor-spreading theorem:

\[
B_{\rm comp}(n)\ll \frac{n}{(\log n)^2}.
\]

Consequently the sole remaining pointwise obstruction is the lower-digit moving-modulus incidence.

## 1. Corvaja–Zannier: **PARTIAL; no direct holonomic extension**

The Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier argument places \((a^n,b^n)\) in a fixed finitely generated subgroup of \(\mathbf G_m^2\). Extensions to algebraic linear recurrences retain a Binet form
\(\sum_j f_j(n)\alpha_j^n\), hence fixed multiplicative rank and a fixed finite set \(S\) of places; see [Grieve–Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09109).

For the Apéry recurrence the state is instead
\[
T(q-1)\cdots T(1)v_1,
\]
with genuinely varying transfer matrices. After clearing denominators, new primes up to \(q\) enter, so there is neither a fixed torus orbit nor a fixed \(S\)-unit group. The moving-target Subspace Theorem permits moving *forms*, not arbitrary moving arguments of increasing multiplicative rank. The Casoratian controls the determinant of this non-autonomous evolution but does not produce an exponential-polynomial representation.

The closest genuine holonomic theorem is Luca’s [prime-divisor theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2006.12.001), which gives only cumulative information for the product of terms through \(N\), not a pointwise gcd or short-interval factor distribution. A tractable function-field surrogate is the Ailon–Rudnick theorem: for multiplicatively independent \(A,B\in\mathbf C[T]\), \(\gcd(A^n-1,B^n-1)\) divides a fixed polynomial ([paper](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa113-1-3)). That models a fixed torus power map, not the Apéry P-recursive system.

## 2. Nilpotent \(p\)-curvature: **NO for prime counting**

The premise “nilpotent \(p\)-curvature iff successive extension of rank-one objects” is false. Katz’s theorem concerns nilpotent connections and local monodromy, not triangularization into rank-one systems; globally nilpotent irreducible systems with differential Galois group \(G_2\) are known ([Dettweiler–Reiter](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605383); [Katz](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1970__39__175_0/)).

More decisively, even **zero** \(p\)-curvature does not force sparse coefficient zeros. The algebraic solution
\[
(1-4x)^{-1/2}=\sum_{m\ge0}\binom{2m}{m}x^m
\]
has zero \(p\)-curvature at good primes, yet Kummer gives
\(p\mid\binom{2m}{m}\) for every \((p+1)/2\le m<p\): roughly half the coefficient block vanishes. Thus Beukers’s global nilpotence can explain the local Frobenius/block structure already obtained, but cannot bound how many characteristics hit one moving index \(n\).

## 3. Companion prime factors: **YES, by quotient geometry rather than spreading**

Use the established vanishing range: no contribution occurs for
\(q\le q_0:=\lfloor(\log n)/4\rfloor\). For every remaining counted pair,
\[
q>q_0,\qquad p\in\left(\frac n{q+1},\frac nq\right]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\le \frac n{q_0}<\frac{(4+o(1))n}{\log n}.
\]
The quotient intervals are disjoint, so each prime is counted at most once. Chebyshev’s bound \(\pi(x)\ll x/\log x\) therefore yields
\[
B_{\rm comp}(n)
 \le \pi\!\left(\frac{(4+o(1))n}{\log n}\right)
 \ll \frac{n}{(\log n)^2}.
\]
Hence its logarithmic contribution is \(O(n/\log n)=o(n)\). The Casoratian itself only prevents simultaneous divisibility of the two solution coordinates when \(p\nmid6q\); it gives no short-interval spreading, but none is needed here.

## Exact residual

With small primes already contributing \(O(\sqrt n)\), the all-\(n\) theorem is now reduced to
\[
\boxed{\;B_{\rm low}(n)=
\#\{p>\sqrt n:n\bmod p\in Z_p\}=o(n/\log n)\;}. 
\]
Even \(Z(p)\ll(\log p)^{1+o(1)}\) does not imply this pointwise anti-alignment. The nearest named framework is Kowalski’s [large sieve for Frobenius](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503714), but it averages over a parameter family and retains the short-range \(P^2\) barrier. In the repository’s terminology, the sufficient missing input remains the mixed-characteristic short parabolic rainbow-variance statement \((PV_\eta)\).
