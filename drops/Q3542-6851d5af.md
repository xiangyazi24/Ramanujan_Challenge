## 2. Why two different path determinants do not factor the wedge

The valid exterior-transport identity is the **common transport** identity

\[
(\lambda T)\wedge(\mu T)
=(\det T)(\lambda\wedge\mu).
\]

It does not extend to two unrelated matrices as

\[
(\lambda T_7)\wedge(\mu T_4)
\stackrel{\text{false}}=
(\det T_7)(\det T_4)(\lambda\wedge\mu).
\]

If `T_4` is invertible, the exact rewrite is instead

\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda T_7\\
\mu T_4
\end{pmatrix}
=
\det(T_4)
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda T_7T_4^{-1}\\
\mu
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The relative matrix

\[
T_7T_4^{-1}
\]

remains. Its contribution is precisely the cross-slope information one was trying to compute; it cannot be discarded and replaced by a product of determinants.

There is a second obstruction. A Newton charge is not generally one endpoint row transported along one path. It is a linear combination of many evaluations:

\[
\mathcal C_s
=N_{s,R_s}
\sum_{t=0}^{R_s}a_{s,t}\,
 e_1^T\Phi_{\sigma_s+st},
\]

where `Phi_n` is a fundamental transfer matrix and the coefficients `a_{s,t}` come from the two forward differences. Therefore

\[
\mathcal C_7\wedge\mathcal C_4
=N_{7,R_7}N_{4,R_4}
\sum_{t,u}a_{7,t}a_{4,u}
\det\left(
 e_1^T\Phi_{\sigma_7+7t},
 e_1^T\Phi_{\sigma_4+4u}
\right).
\]

This is a double sum of cross-minors. It is not a single product of local transfer determinants unless a new telescoping or rank-one identity collapses that sum.

Finally, the scan's slope-4 row is the associated-grade seam row `h_p`, not an ordinary homogeneous recurrence solution across the whole sampled interval. Thus ordinary two-dimensional recurrence transport cannot by itself produce the exact scan matrix.

This is the precise logical status:

- `det_bc = U W_j` is formally true once a common rank-two filtered lattice and its state matrix have been constructed.
- Q3537 proposed doing that construction.
- Q3531/Q3538 compute the specialized triangular residue matrix.
- The claimed product-of-path-determinants formula for `U` is not present and does not follow from determinant multiplicativity.

## 5. Comparison with the proposed transfer-determinant explanation

For the ordinary recurrence, legal transport from state index `j` to the seam input at `p-1` has

\[
\begin{aligned}
\det T(j\to p-1)
&=\prod_{n=j}^{p-2}\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}\\
&=\left(\frac{j}{p-1}\right)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The two fixtures give

\[
\det T(8\to18)
=\left(\frac8{18}\right)^3
\equiv1\pmod{19},
\]

\[
\det T(25\to96)
=\left(\frac{25}{96}\right)^3
\equiv89\pmod{97}.
\]

These do not match

\[
U_{19,8}^{\mathrm{scan}}=6,
\qquad
U_{97,25}^{\mathrm{scan}}=41.
\]

This is not surprising. Q3529 already showed that bare recurrence transport of the genuine Apéry zero state sends every route ending at the same seam input to

\[
(b_{p-1},b_{p-2})=(1,5)\pmod p.
\]

Thus the bare cross-slope coefficient wedge collapses to zero. The nonzero scan determinant appears only after adding the divided/associated-grade seam coordinate. It cannot be recovered from the determinant of the bare legal transfer alone.

A path determinant may still occur as one factor after both filtered rows have been transported to a common state, but then a **relative terminal wedge** remains. In the two examples that unresolved factor is exactly what contributes the residues `6` and `41`.

## 7. The exact next lemma needed for Q3537

The determinant-line route remains viable, but the correct target is filtered rather than ordinary.

Construct a rank-two `Z_p` lattice `L` with basis represented by the Apéry solution and the unit companion, together with the first-seam filtration for which

\[
p^3c_{p+q}\equiv b_q\pmod p.
\]

Then prove that the two charge rows extend to honest elements of the same filtered dual lattice and, after transport to the target state, factor as

\[
M_{bc}^{\mathrm{scan}}
=\Lambda_{p,j}\Phi_j,
\]

where

\[
\det\Phi_j=W_j.
\]

Only then does one obtain a basis-invariant identity

\[
\det_{bc}^{\mathrm{scan}}
=(\det\Lambda_{p,j})W_j.
\]

The required all-prime statement becomes

\[
\det\Lambda_{p,j}\in\mathbf Z_p^\times.
\]

The two fixtures say that its reductions would be

\[
\det\Lambda_{19,8}=6,
\qquad
\det\Lambda_{97,25}=41.
\]

But the proof must compute the **relative filtered row wedge**, not merely telescope the determinants of two different ordinary transfer products.
