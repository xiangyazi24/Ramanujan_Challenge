# Q871: canonical companion-denominator audit

## Verdict

No proved fixed power saving for `log D_n` follows from the listed global identities alone. The strongest rigorous reduction is a weaker exact statement: controlling `D_n` is equivalent to controlling the surviving denominator of the single characteristic-zero companion solution, and the telescoping/Pade-style candidates reduce to explicit denominator-cancellation lemmas. The aggregate lcm identity does not imply pointwise retention bounds.

## 1. Telescoping formula audit

The identity

\[
\frac{a_n}{b_n}=\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{6}{k^3b_kb_{k-1}}
\]

is exact from the Casoratian:

\[
\frac{a_k}{b_k}-\frac{a_{k-1}}{b_{k-1}}
=\frac{a_kb_{k-1}-a_{k-1}b_k}{b_kb_{k-1}}
=\frac{6}{k^3b_kb_{k-1}}.
\]

However, reducing this rational sum to information about `delta_n` requires cancellation between denominators of all previous terms. No monotone divisibility relation between `b_k` and `b_n` exists. Therefore the formula alone does not imply a lower bound on the retained denominator of `a_n`.

The exact weaker lemma obtained is:

> If a prime power `p^e` survives in `delta_n`, then the p-adic valuation of the truncated telescoping numerator cannot cancel the p-adic denominator contribution coming from the k-th summand for all k simultaneously.

This is only a local obstruction statement, not a sublinear bound.

## 2. Growing-window determinant attempt

For any window ending at n, determinants of consecutive pairs satisfy

\[
 a_i b_{i-1}-a_{i-1}b_i=6/i^3.
\]

Taking determinants of larger matrices built from `(a_i,b_i)` gives only products of the same Wronskian factors. The height is therefore controlled by powers of `L_n` and does not isolate `delta_n`.

The exact obstruction is:

\[
\operatorname{den}(a_n)\mid L_n^3
\]

is already the output of determinant clearing. Every determinant obtained from the second-order recurrence introduces the same denominator scale. No extra factor with height `o(n)` has been produced.

## 3. p-adic uniqueness attempt

Let

\[
a_n=A_n/\delta_n,\qquad (A_n,\delta_n)=1.
\]

A surviving prime p dividing delta_n means

\[
v_p(a_n)<0.
\]

The recurrence can propagate this valuation only together with the companion initial conditions. Since the recurrence coefficients contain factors of n and n+1, there is no general uniqueness principle forcing valuation loss at every large prime.

The known congruence information identifies the bad primes through the residue classes of b_{n mod p}; it does not provide cancellation of those primes.

## 4. Padé/G-function denominator theory

The Apéry generating functions are G-functions, so general denominator theorems provide exponential upper bounds. They do not provide

\[
\log \delta_n=\log L_n^3-o(n),
\]

which is a near-maximal denominator-retention statement. A new theorem of this strength would require an arithmetic irrationality-measure type input beyond standard G-function theory.

## 5. Dense Bezout identity attempt

A possible route would be an identity

\[
U_n A_n+V_n b_n=1
\]

with coefficients whose height is sublinear. Such an identity would force cancellation in `gcd(A_n,b_n)` and hence improve `D_n`.

But the known exact bound

\[
gcd(A_n,b_n)\mid 6D_n
\]

is already the determinant Bezout information after clearing denominators. Any stronger Bezout relation would be an additional arithmetic statement, not a consequence of the recurrence and Casoratian alone.

## 6. Aggregate lcm identity

The identity

\[
\operatorname{lcm}_{j\le N}\delta_j=d_N/6
\]

only says every denominator prime power appears somewhere up to N. It permits all of the lcm mass to concentrate on sparse indices. It gives no pointwise estimate for a fixed `delta_n`.

A countermodel at the logical level is any sequence of divisors `delta_n|d_n` satisfying the same aggregate lcm but with infinitely many small `delta_n`; the aggregate condition alone cannot rule this out.

## Final result

The proposed global mechanisms do not currently yield

\[
\log D_n\le (1-\varepsilon)n+o(n).
\]

The precise reduced target is:

**Canonical denominator-retention lemma.** For every epsilon > 0,

\[
\#\{p>\sqrt n:p\mid d_n/\delta_n\text{ and }p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}
\]

weighted by `log p` must be `o(n)`.

Equivalently, one still needs a pointwise control on the moving characteristic-zero denominator. The local propagation obstruction is not the issue; the missing ingredient is a global statement preventing simultaneous p-adic cancellation patterns in the canonical initial-state solution.
