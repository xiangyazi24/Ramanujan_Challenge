# 7. Why the fixed-\(h\) divisor theorem does not repair the pair count

The fixed-\(h\) carrier is exact and useful:

\[
\#\{\ell:I_x(\ell)=1,\ h_x(\ell)=h\}\ll_v1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

But the adjacent-label count sums over many different displacement values. A configuration may use one candidate at each of \(N^{2/3}\) distinct \(h\)'s and still satisfy (7.1) perfectly.

The algebra shows why small label gaps do not reduce this freedom. Put

\[
C_x=\left\langle\Delta_x\overline{p'}\right\rangle_P.
\]

For each candidate there is a unique \(k_\ell\) such that

\[
t_\ell\ell=C_x+Pk_\ell,
\qquad
h_x(\ell)=k_\ell-k_0.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Modulo \(p\),

\[
r_x(\ell)\equiv C_x\overline\ell\pmod p.
\tag{7.3}
\]

If two labels differ by \(g\), then their primes satisfy the Möbius congruence

\[
\ell_2
\equiv
\frac{C_x\ell_1}{C_x+g\ell_1}\pmod p,
\tag{7.4}
\]

whenever the denominator is nonzero. Equation (7.4) controls one residue class for \(\ell_2\), but it imposes no bound on

\[
|k_{\ell_2}-k_{\ell_1}|
=|h_x(\ell_2)-h_x(\ell_1)|.
\]

The missing \(q\)-zero test on the unselected copies is exactly what prevents the full-\(P\) completions \(t_{\ell_1},t_{\ell_2}\) from becoming two actual selected first rows. Thus a small same-\(p\) gap can occur anywhere in the ultra-long displacement range.

This is why PS15--PS17 does not enlarge the pure displacement sector.

# 8. Prime weights, Palm size bias, and Cauchy normalization

The only weight in the rooted average is the already selected atom \(w_x\), which remains untouched.

## 8.2 The prime-averaged PS consequence is in the wrong measure

Canonical first-moment bookkeeping gives

\[
\sum_{p\asymp N}|u_p|M_p(G)
\ll
N^{3/5+o(1)}+N^{3/10+o(1)}G^{4/3}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Let

\[
\mu_p:=\sum_{x:\,p(x)=p}w_x.
\]

The Palm quantity would require control of

\[
\sum_p\mu_pM_p(G).
\tag{8.2}

There is no source theorem comparing the normalized measure

\[
\mu_p/M_0(F)

with the base measure proportional to \(|u_p|\). The selected roots are size-biased by all four zero tests, the determinant kernel, and every mask inside \(J\). An exceptional \(p\) may carry a large share of \(M_0(F)\) even if it is negligible in (8.1).

Thus (8.1) cannot be divided by an upper bound for \(M_0(F)\) or substituted into (8.2).

Moreover, even granting an ideal measure comparison would not close the exponent: combining \(N/G\) with the second term of (8.1) optimizes no better than the existing \(G^2\) route. The normalization failure is real, but the numerical failure occurs first.

The canonical factorial energy does not estimate the weighted square in (8.3). It counts same-\(p\) gap columns before the moving-prime selectors and under a different averaging law. A second Cauchy cannot manufacture the missing normalization.
