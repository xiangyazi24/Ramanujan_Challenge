# Problem 3.2 — fixed-`n` modular/Hecke attack on the moving Apéry coefficient

## Verdict

I do **not** obtain a proof of
\[
 \sum_{\substack{p\le n\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}\log p=o(n)
\]
or of the equivalent `gcd(lcm(1,...,n),b_n)` statement.

I do obtain two exact theorems that sharpen the route selection.

1. **Cartier saturation theorem.**  Put
   \[
   \Theta=q\frac d{dq},\qquad
   \mathcal H_n(q):=(\Theta E(q))\,t(q)^{-n}.
   \]
   If `n=Qp+r`, `1<=r<=p-1`, then the moving coefficient is exactly
   \[
   C_{r-1}(n)=[q^{-Qp}]\mathcal H_n=[q^{-Q}]U_p\mathcal H_n,
   \]
   and, modulo `p`,
   \[
   \boxed{
   U_p\mathcal H_n
   \equiv U_p\bigl((\Theta E)t^{-r}\bigr)t^{-Q},
   \qquad
   [q^{-Q}]U_p\mathcal H_n\equiv r b_r\pmod p.
   }
   \]
   More strongly, `U_p((Theta E)t^{-r})` has no negative powers, so
   `r b_r` is literally the **leading principal coefficient** of the
   Cartier image.  The quotient `Q=floor(n/p)` survives only as the pole
   shift `t^{-Q}`; it disappears from the leading coefficient.

   This proves a sharp no-go for the natural Frobenius/Cartier/leading-principal-part class.  It also explains, in one formula, why the first divided jet and other fixed-`p` refinements found in the existing audits do not become a horizontal theorem when `p` changes.

2. **Diagonal Newton--Pascal block theorem.**  Inside a fixed quotient cell
   `Q=floor(n/p)`, a whole short interval of different moving coefficient
   coordinates can be compressed into one signed integer
   \[
   \mathcal N_{n,Q;D,L}
   =\sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^i
     \binom{D-1+i}{i}\binom{D+L}{L-i}
     C_{\,n-Q(D+i)-1}(n).
   \]
   If `D>L>=1` and `(D,D+L]` lies inside one quotient-`Q` cell, then
   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{rad}_{(D,D+L]}
   \gcd\!\left(\mathcal N_{n,Q;D,L},\binom{D+L}{L}\right)
   =
   \prod_{\substack{D<p\le D+L\\p\text{ prime}\\
        p\mid C_{n-Qp-1}(n)}}p .
   }
   \]
   This is a genuinely cross-coordinate arithmetic carrier for the fixed-`n`
   series: the left side contains one signed convolution of `L+1` distinct
   coefficients of `G_n`, not one private integer for each prime.

The clean remaining target is therefore **not** “prove the original conjecture again”.  A sufficient mesoscopic statement is a uniform primitive-content saving for these explicit pairs:
\[
 \max_{B\in\mathscr B_n}
 \frac1{L_B}
 \log\operatorname{rad}_{I_B}
 \gcd\!\left(\mathcal N_B,\binom{D_B+L_B}{L_B}\right)
 \longrightarrow0,
 \tag{DNPC}
\]
where `mathscr B_n` is a canonical cover of the quotient cells for
`p>n^(2/3)` by blocks of length `asymp n^(1/3)` (with at most
`O(n^(2/3))` total uncovered boundary length).  `(DNPC)` gives the desired
`o(n)` bound immediately.  It is an explicit structured-gcd estimate between a
Newton convolution of the fixed-`n` modular coefficients and one Pascal
coefficient; it is a plausible target for a new Padé/Bezout/modular-symbol
identity and is not a one-prime restatement.

I also record a second exact no-go for the “single convolution polynomial”
carrier: for the natural product-of-linear-factors realization, Gauss's content
lemma factorizes the content exactly into the old coordinatewise gcds.  Thus the
mere act of convolving those linear factors creates **zero** arithmetic
cross-coordinate cancellation.

# 1. Formal-series setup

Write
\[
 t(q)=\left(\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau)}
                 {\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau)}\right)^{12},
 \qquad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.
\]
Then `t=q+O(q^2)` and
\[
 A(q)=\frac q{t(q)}
 =\prod_{3\nmid m}(1+q^m)^{12}\in1+q\mathbf Z[[q]].
\]
The factor `q` in `A=q/t` is important: `t` is the level-6 modular
function, while `A` itself is `q` times its inverse.  One should therefore not
silently treat `A^n` as a weight-zero modular function and apply Hecke theory to
`G_n=E'A^n` directly.

Let
\[
 G_n(q):=E'(q)A(q)^n=\sum_{j\ge0}C_j(n)q^j.
\]
Lagrange inversion gives, for `r>=1`,
\[
 C_{r-1}(r)=r b_r.
 \tag{1.1}
\]
For a prime `p`, write
\[
 n=Qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]
Since
\[
 A(q)^{Qp}\equiv A(q^p)^Q\equiv1\pmod{(p,q^p)},
\]
one has the familiar vertical saturation
\[
 \boxed{
 G_n(q)\equiv G_r(q)\pmod{(p,q^p)}.
 }
 \tag{1.2}
\]
Hence, when `1<=r<=p-1`,
\[
 C_{r-1}(n)\equiv C_{r-1}(r)=r b_r\pmod p.
 \tag{1.3}
\]
This proof is completely fixed-`p`: the quotient ring, the Frobenius map and the
residue `r` all depend on `p`.  Equation (1.3) is not a relation between the
values obtained from two different primes for the same `n`.

# 2. Modularize the moving coefficient before applying Cartier

The useful observation is that the `q/t` factor can be removed **exactly**.
Let
\[
 \mathcal H_n(q):=(\Theta E(q))t(q)^{-n},
 \qquad \Theta=q\frac d{dq}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]
Because `E'=q^{-1}Theta E` and `A^n=q^nt^{-n}`,
\[
 G_n=q^{n-1}\mathcal H_n.
\]
Therefore
\[
 C_{r-1}(n)
 =[q^{r-1}]G_n
 =[q^{r-n}]\mathcal H_n
 =[q^{-Qp}]\mathcal H_n.
 \tag{2.2}
\]
This is already a genuinely fixed-`n` reformulation: as `p` varies, all targets
are principal-part coefficients of the **one Laurent series** `H_n`, at the
indices `-p floor(n/p)`.

Define the Cartier operator on Laurent series bounded below by
\[
 U_p\left(\sum_m a_mq^m\right)=\sum_m a_{pm}q^m.
\]
Then (2.2) is
\[
 C_{r-1}(n)=[q^{-Q}]U_p\mathcal H_n.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

# 3. Cartier saturation theorem

## Theorem 3.1

Let `p` be prime, `n=Qp+r`, `1<=r<=p-1`.  In
`F_p((q))`,
\[
 \boxed{
 U_p\mathcal H_n
 \equiv
 U_p\bigl((\Theta E)t^{-r}\bigr)t^{-Q}.
 }
 \tag{3.1}
\]
Moreover
\[
 U_p\bigl((\Theta E)t^{-r}\bigr)\in\mathbf F_p[[q]],
 \tag{3.2}
\]
and its constant term is `r b_r`.  Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 U_p\mathcal H_n
 = r b_r\,q^{-Q}+O(q^{-Q+1})\pmod p,
 }
 \tag{3.3}
\]
so
\[
 C_{r-1}(n)\equiv r b_r\pmod p.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

Since `t=q/A` and `A in 1+q Z[[q]]`,
`t^{-1}=q^{-1}A` is an integral Laurent series.  Freshman's dream gives
\[
 t(q)^{-p}=q^{-p}A(q)^p
 \equiv q^{-p}A(q^p)=t(q^p)^{-1}\pmod p.
\]
Hence
\[
 t^{-Qp}\equiv t(q^p)^{-Q}\pmod p.
\]
Writing
\[
 \mathcal H_n=(\Theta E)t^{-r}t^{-Qp}
\]
and using the exact Cartier product identity
\[
 U_p(F(q)K(q^p))=U_p(F(q))K(q)
\]
proves (3.1).

Now `Theta E` has zero constant term and begins in degree at least `1`, while
`t^{-r}=q^{-r}(1+O(q))`.  Therefore
\[
 (\Theta E)t^{-r}\in q^{1-r}\mathbf Z[[q]].
\]
Since `1-r>=2-p`, every negative exponent lies strictly between `-p` and `0`.
None is divisible by `p`.  Cartier therefore kills every negative term, proving
(3.2).

The coefficient of `q^{-Q}` in the right side of (3.1) can consequently only
come from the constant term of the first factor times the leading term
`q^{-Q}` of `t^{-Q}`.  Thus
\[
 [q^{-Q}]U_p\mathcal H_n
 \equiv [q^0](\Theta E)t^{-r}.
\]
Finally
\[
 [q^0](\Theta E)t^{-r}
 =[q^0]qE'(q)q^{-r}A(q)^r
 =[q^{r-1}]E'(q)A(q)^r
 =r b_r
\]
by Lagrange inversion.  This proves (3.3)--(3.4).  QED.

# 4. Sharp no-go: the natural modular/Cartier class is vertical

Theorem 3.1 is more informative than (1.2).  The moving coefficient is not an
arbitrary internal coefficient after modularization; it is the **leading pole
coefficient** of the natural Cartier image.

Define the following explicitly limited method class.

> **Cartier-principal-part class (CPP).**  A proof is CPP if, for each candidate
> prime `p`, it rewrites the target using `H_n`, uses only reduction modulo `p`,
> Frobenius substitutions `F(q)^p=F(q^p)`, Cartier `U_p`, identities of the
> level-6 modular functions/forms valid before coefficient extraction, and then
> detects targetness from the pole order and leading principal coefficient of
> the resulting `U_p H_n`.

This includes the most immediate “turn the Lagrange series into a modular form
and apply `U_p`/Frobenius/Hecke-at-`p`” proposal.  It does **not** claim to
include every imaginable Hecke argument; in particular a theorem using a new
global trace over many varying primes is outside CPP.

## Corollary 4.1 (CPP no-go)

Within CPP, after `n=Qp+r` the entire target criterion reduces to
\[
 \operatorname{lead}(U_p\mathcal H_n)=r b_r\pmod p.
 \tag{4.1}
\]
The quotient `Q` affects only the pole order.  Thus CPP supplies no second
arithmetic condition on a target prime and no relation between two different
candidate primes for the same `n`.

This is a theorem, not a heuristic: it is precisely (3.3).

### Why a fixed-`p` Hecke congruence is not a horizontal statement

For one `p`, (3.1) is an identity in `F_p((q))` involving the operator `U_p`.
For another prime `ell`, the ambient field and operator are `F_ell` and
`U_ell`.  There is no common equality from which one may multiply the two
vanishing statements.  A horizontal theorem would have to produce one
**integer/rational object independent of the candidate prime** whose local
valuation sees all the targets.  CPP never creates such an object.

### Direct `p`-jet methods are an even smaller no-go class

Let
\[
 J_p(F)=F\bmod(p,q^p).
\]
Equation (1.2) says exactly
\[
 J_p(G_n)=J_p(G_r).
 \tag{4.2}
\]
Therefore every invariant that factors through `J_p` is independent of `Q`.
This includes arbitrary algebraic operations, `Theta`, Hasse derivatives, and
coefficient recurrences performed entirely inside the first `p` coefficients.
It also includes any Hecke-coefficient manipulation whose queried indices all
remain below `p`.  It does **not** include a general `T_l` formula which samples
coefficients beyond that truncation, so the no-go is not overstated.

The known “first divided jet is nonzero at targets” result is consistent with
this: it says the zero is usually transverse in the **same** characteristic.
Transversality prevents a second local equation; it does not bound how many
different characteristics can have their one transverse zero aligned with the
same external `n`.

# 5. Endpoints

## 5.1 `r=0`

Here the formal target would be `C_{-1}(n)`, which is not a coefficient of
`G_n`; neither (1.3) nor Theorem 3.1 should be stated with `r=0`.

These primes satisfy `p|n`.  Whatever their behavior in `b_n`, their total
logarithmic weight is harmless:
\[
 \sum_{p\mid n}\log p=\log\operatorname{rad}(n)\le\log n=o(n).
 \tag{5.1}
\]
Thus removing the `r=0` sector is legitimate in the all-index estimate.

## 5.2 `r=p-1`

This endpoint is **inside** the Lagrange range.  The target index is `p-2`, and
\[
 C_{p-2}(n)\equiv(p-1)b_{p-1}\equiv-b_{p-1}\pmod p.
 \tag{5.2}

The project’s proved reflection theorem for `p`-integral Apéry solutions gives

endpoint is in fact not a bad Apéry residue.  Even without using reflection,
there is no indexing defect here: Theorem 3.1 applies verbatim.

The fixed primes `2,3,5` may be removed at `O(1)` logarithmic cost throughout.

# 6. Exact single convolution-polynomial carrier, and why it gives no gain

There is a useful exact way to encode all moving coordinates into the content
of one polynomial.  It also exposes a no-go.

For `0<=j<=n-2`, let
\[
 M_{n,j}:=\operatorname{rad}_{>j+1}(n-j-1),
 \tag{6.1}
\]
i.e. the product of prime divisors `p` of `n-j-1` satisfying `p>j+1`.
Define
\[
 \mathscr P_n(T)
 :=\prod_{j=0}^{n-2}\bigl(M_{n,j}+C_j(n)T\bigr)\in\mathbf Z[T].
 \tag{6.2}
\]
Its coefficients are literal convolution sums of the coordinate factors.

## Proposition 6.1

For any prime `p` with `p not| n`, let `r=n mod p`, `1<=r<=p-1`.  Then
`p` divides the content of `P_n` iff
\[
 p\mid C_{r-1}(n).
 \tag{6.3}
\]
Moreover `p` can occur through only the factor `j=r-1`.

### Proof

If `p|M_{n,j}`, then
\[
 p\mid n-j-1,\qquad p>j+1.
\]
Put `s=j+1`.  Then `1<=s<p` and `s congruent n (mod p)`, hence `s=r` and
`j=r-1`.  Conversely `j=r-1` gives `p|n-r` and `p>r`, so `p|M_{n,r-1}`.
Thus the indicated factor is unique.

Over the field `F_p`, the product polynomial in (6.2) is identically zero iff
one of its linear factors is identically zero.  By the uniqueness just proved,
that is equivalent to `p|M_{n,r-1}` and `p|C_{r-1}(n)`.  QED.

Equivalently, Gauss's content lemma gives the stronger integer identity
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{cont}(\mathscr P_n)
 =\prod_{j=0}^{n-2}\gcd(M_{n,j},C_j(n)),
 }
 \tag{6.4}
\]
(up to sign), and every `M_{n,j}` is squarefree.  Hence, after the harmless
`p|n` sector,
\[
 \operatorname{cont}(\mathscr P_n)
 =\prod_{p\in\mathcal B(n)}p,
 \tag{6.5}
\]
where `B(n)` is the moving bad-prime set.

### No-go consequence

Although `P_n` is a single convolution polynomial, **its content has no
cross-coordinate cancellation at all**: content is multiplicative.  Any attack
which only bounds the content of the product by looking at its factor contents
has exactly repackaged the original pointwise gcds.

This is why a genuinely useful cross-coordinate carrier must use a
nonmultiplicative operation--a determinant, signed interpolation sum, resultant
with a fixed auxiliary polynomial, structured Bezout relation, etc.--before
taking the gcd/content.

# 7. A genuinely cross-coordinate fixed-`n` theorem

The repository already contains the generic Newton--Pascal identity in the
form used in §65 of the Problem 3.2 ledger.  Applying it to the **diagonal
coefficient sequence of `G_n`** gives the following.

Fix `n` and an integer `Q>=1`.  Define
\[
 Y^{(n,Q)}_d:=C_{\,n-Q(d+1)-1}(n),
 \tag{7.1}
\]
with any fixed convention outside the nonnegative coefficient range (those
values will not be used when the block is contained in a quotient cell).

Let `D>L>=1` and suppose every integer `m in (D,D+L]` lies in the same quotient
cell
\[
 Q=\left\lfloor\frac nm\right\rfloor
 \tag{7.2}
\]
and the block avoids an `m|n` endpoint.  Put
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal N_{n,Q;D,L}
 =\sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^i
   \binom{D-1+i}{i}\binom{D+L}{L-i}
   Y^{(n,Q)}_{D-1+i}.
 }
 \tag{7.3}
\]
Also put
\[
 B_{D,L}=\binom{D+L}{L}.
 \tag{7.4}
\]

## Theorem 7.1 (diagonal Newton--Pascal block identity)

For every prime `p in (D,D+L]`,
\[
 \mathcal N_{n,Q;D,L}
 \equiv
 Y^{(n,Q)}_{p-1}
 =C_{n-Qp-1}(n)
 \pmod p.
 \tag{7.5}
\]
Furthermore `v_p(B_{D,L})=1`.  Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{rad}_{(D,D+L]}
 \gcd\!\left(\mathcal N_{n,Q;D,L},B_{D,L}\right)
 =
 \prod_{\substack{D<p\le D+L\\p\ {m prime}\\
        p\mid C_{n-Qp-1}(n)}}p .
 }
 \tag{7.6}
\]

### Proof

The generic Newton--Pascal carrier from the repository is
\[
 G_{d,L}(Y)=
 \sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^i\binom{d+i}{i}
 \binom{d+L+1}{L-i}Y_{d+i}.
\]
Its exact interpolation congruence is
\[
 G_{D-1,L}(Y)\equiv Y_{p-1}\pmod p
 \qquad(D<p\le D+L),
 \tag{7.7}
\]
provided `D>L`.  Taking `Y=Y^(n,Q)` gives (7.5).

For such a prime `p`, the numerator interval of `binom(D+L,L)` contains
exactly one multiple of `p`, namely `p` itself, because `D+L<2D<2p`;
`L!` is a `p`-unit since `p>D>L`.  Hence `v_p(B)=1`.  Restricting the gcd to
this prime interval proves (7.6).  QED.

This theorem is the requested kind of object: one integer uses simultaneous
information from many **different moving coordinates of the same fixed-`n`
series**.  There is no changing-modulus operation in its definition.

# 8. The minimal remaining estimate

Small primes `p<=n^(2/3)` contribute at most
\[
 \vartheta(n^{2/3})=O(n^{2/3}),
 \tag{8.1}
\]
so only `p>n^(2/3)` matter.  There
\[
 Q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\le n^{1/3}.
\]
The quotient cells
\[
 I_Q=\left(\frac n{Q+1},\frac nQ\right]
 \tag{8.2}
\]
can be split into blocks of length
\[
 H\asymp n^{1/3}
\]
with `D>L`; split the `Q=1` cell once more if necessary.  Keep one remainder of
length `<H` per quotient cell.  There are `O(n^(1/3))` cells, so all remainders
together have length `O(n^(2/3))` and therefore only `O(n^(2/3))` prime
log-weight by Chebyshev.

There are `O(n/H)=O(n^(2/3))` full blocks.

Thus the following is sufficient.

> **Diagonal Newton primitive-content estimate (DNPC).**  Uniformly over the
> canonical full blocks `B=(D,D+L]` above,
> \[
> \log\operatorname{rad}_{B}
> \gcd\!\left(
>   \mathcal N_{n,Q;D,L},\binom{D+L}{L}
> \right)
> =o(H),
> \tag{8.3}
> \]
> where the `o(H)` is uniform as `n->infinity`.

Indeed, if the right side is `epsilon(n) H` with `epsilon(n)->0`, summing over
`O(n/H)` full blocks gives `O(epsilon(n)n)=o(n)`, and the discarded sectors
are `O(n^(2/3)+log n)`.

A power-saving version
\[
 \ll H^{1-\delta}(\log n)^A
 \tag{8.4}
\]
for any fixed `delta>0` (with `A` small enough, or after the usual epsilon
adjustment) is more than sufficient: summation gives a power saving from `n`.

This is the estimate I would attack next.  Unlike the original conjecture, it
presents an explicit pair of integers with strong algebraic structure:

- the first is a **signed Newton convolution** of nearby moving coefficients of
  one `G_n`;
- the second is one Pascal coefficient with completely understood prime
  support and valuations;
- only their common interval-prime radical matters;
- absolute height of the first integer is allowed to be `exp(Theta(n))`.

So a proof may exploit cancellation/Bezout structure without needing a
sublinear archimedean bound for the raw coefficients themselves.

This is closely analogous to the repository's equation (65.23), but here the
sequence fed to the generic Newton carrier is directly the diagonal coefficient
sequence (7.1) of the fixed-`n` Lagrange series.

# 9. Why standard Hecke theory still does not prove (DNPC)

There are three distinct obstructions.

### 9.1 `G_n` itself is not the fixed-level modular object

`A=q/t`; the explicit `q^n` factor prevents treating `A^n` as a modular
function.  The correct Laurent modularization is `H_n=(Theta E)t^{-n}`.

### 9.2 The natural `U_p` theorem is exactly saturated

Theorem 3.1 shows that the target is the leading pole coefficient and that
Frobenius removes the quotient `Q`.  Thus the most natural Hecke-at-`p`
operation converts the fixed-`n` diagonal back into the old residue coordinate.
It does not produce the signed block interaction in (7.3).

### 9.3 The modular spaces grow with the pole order

Even after replacing `Theta E` by any legitimate modular/quasimodular
combination, multiplication by `t^{-n}` gives pole order growing linearly with
`n`.  One is not in a fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module in which a bounded
number of eigenvalue recurrences controls all principal coefficients.  A
standard coefficient recurrence can therefore have macroscopic height, exactly
as observed in the audits.

A successful Hecke route would need a **new trace identity across the whole
Newton block**, for example an identity of the schematic form
\[
 \mathcal N_{n,Q;D,L}
 =X_{n,Q;D,L}\,R_{D,L}+Y_{n,Q;D,L}\,B_{D,L}
 \tag{9.1}
\]
where the interval-prime radical of `R_{D,L}` has logarithm `o(H)`.  Merely
rewriting each summand with its own `U_p` is useless, because `p` varies across
the block.

# 10. Resultants and theta decompositions: what would count as progress

The exact block theorem suggests three genuinely horizontal possibilities.

1. **Structured Bezout/Padé identity.**  Express the Newton convolution
   `N_{n,Q;D,L}` modulo the Pascal polynomial data by a determinant of size
   `O(1)` or `O(log H)` whose primitive part has `exp(o(H))` interval-prime
   radical.

2. **A fixed auxiliary resultant.**  Produce polynomials `F_B(X),J_B(X)` over
   `Z` from the level-6 modular equation such that every common interval prime
   in (7.6) contributes to
   \[
   \operatorname{cont}_T\operatorname{Res}_X(F_B,G_B+TJ_B),
   \]
   while the local intersection lengths admit total `o(H)` height after the
   already-known structural saturation.  This is the genuinely useful version
   of a resultant; taking one resultant separately for each prime is again
   vertical.

3. **Theta decomposition with a common characteristic-free coefficient.**  A
   decomposition is useful only if the bad prime divides the same bounded
   family of integer Fourier coefficients for all `p` in the block.  If the
   theta component or marked coefficient itself depends on `p`, CRT recombination
   pays the candidate primorial and returns the original radical, as the
   existing adelic audit already found.

Any of these would attack `(DNPC)` rather than merely furnish another local
nonvanishing jet.

# 11. Relation to the existing low-Kummer / first-divided-jet obstruction

The current project has repeatedly found the following local pattern: after the
mod-`p` target equation is imposed, the first divided residue is generally a
free/nonzero coordinate.  In the modular language above this is not an
accident.  The target equation is the leading coefficient in (3.3).  A next
principal coefficient belongs to
\[
 [q^1]U_p((\Theta E)t^{-r})
\]
together with the first coefficient of `t^{-Q}`.  Nothing in the Frobenius
factorization forces it to vanish when the leading coefficient does.

Thus a first-jet construction can prove that each bad prime is a simple local
intersection.  But simple local intersections at many different primes can
still have total logarithmic mass `Theta(n)`.  To get `o(n)` one needs either

- a common global height object whose local intersection lengths sum
  sublinearly, or
- a cross-coordinate cancellation theorem such as `(DNPC)`.

This is exactly why the nonvanishing divided-jet result and the observed linear
height of macroscopic raw coefficients are compatible rather than contradictory.

# 12. Final theorem/no-go ledger

### Proved here

1. Exact coefficient reindexing:
   \[
   C_{r-1}(n)=[q^{-Qp}](\Theta E)t^{-n}.
   \]
2. Cartier saturation (3.1)--(3.3).
3. `p`-jet saturation `G_n congruent G_r mod (p,q^p)`.
4. Exact convolution-content carrier (6.2)--(6.5), together with the Gauss
   multiplicativity no-gain theorem.
5. Exact diagonal Newton--Pascal block identity (7.5)--(7.6).
6. Endpoint handling: `r=0` costs at most `log n`; `r=p-1` is a valid Lagrange
   endpoint and is nonzero by the project reflection theorem.

### Rigorously ruled out as a complete route

- Any proof depending only on the first `p`-jet of `G_n`.
- The natural Frobenius/`U_p`/leading-principal-part route applied to the
  modularized fixed-`n` series.
- A product-of-linear-coordinate “single convolution polynomial” whose only
  invariant is ordinary integer content: Gauss's lemma factorizes it back into
  the pointwise gcds.

These no-go statements are deliberately scoped.  They do **not** rule out a
new Hecke trace involving several different coefficients before reduction, a
new theta lift with a characteristic-independent coefficient, or a new
resultant/Bezout identity.

### Minimal next arithmetic estimate

Prove `(DNPC)` in (8.3), or an averaged version
\[
 \sum_{B\in\mathscr B_n}
 \log\operatorname{rad}_{I_B}
 \gcd\!\left(\mathcal N_B,\binom{D_B+L_B}{L_B}\right)=o(n)
 \tag{12.1}
\]
by an argument that genuinely uses the signed Newton convolution rather than
expanding it back into `L+1` pointwise conditions.

The uniform block form (8.3) is preferable because it is a falsifiable,
characteristic-free arithmetic statement about an explicit pair of integers.
It is the smallest target I see that is both strong enough for the all-index
conjecture and structurally different from the original moving-prime sentence.

## Bottom line

The modular parametrization does give a beautiful fixed-`n` reformulation, but
the first natural Hecke operation is **too exact**: it recovers the old residue
zero with no quotient information.  The new place to seek a breakthrough is
one level later, after several moving coordinates have been combined over
`Z` by signed interpolation.  A Padé/resultant/theta theorem that proves small
**primitive interval content of the diagonal Newton carrier** would be genuinely
horizontal and would close the conjecture; a further fixed-`p` jet identity
will not.
