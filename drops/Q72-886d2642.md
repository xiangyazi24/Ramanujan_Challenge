# I5: exact isolated five-star specification and attack plan

## Executive verdict

The exact I5 input is **not** a weakened version of \((\mathrm{HM})_3\), and it is not merely a triple-correlation estimate. It is a weak-type fifth-factorial-moment estimate for the part of the Apéry zero sets that remains after folding and deleting all roots having a nearby folded companion.

With the notation defined below, the minimal statement is

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{0\le m<X^2\\K_{\mathrm{iso},H_X}(m)\ge L_X}}
       \bigl(K_{\mathrm{iso},H_X}(m)\bigr)_5
 \ \ll_\eta\ X^{o(1)}
       \left(1+X^2\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H_X)^5\right),
}
\tag{I5-tail}
\]

where

\[
 H_X=\left\lfloor X^{7/15}(\log X)^{-1/3}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 L_X=\left\lceil X^{14/15+\eta}\right\rceil,
\]

and \((k)_5=k(k-1)(k-2)(k-3)(k-4)\).

There is an exact additive-character formulation. If

\[
 B_L=\{m<X^2:K_{\mathrm{iso},H}(m)\ge L\},
\]

\[
 F_{p,H}(h)=\sum_{r\in Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}}e_p(-hr),
 \qquad
 D_{B_L}(\alpha)=\sum_{m\in B_L}e(\alpha m),
\]

then, up to the harmless zero-frequency main term, I5-tail is equivalent to the one-sided five-characteristic coherence bound

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \Bigg(&\sum_{(p_1,\ldots,p_5)\in\mathcal P_X^{\underline 5}}
 \frac1{p_1\cdots p_5}
 \sum_{\substack{h_i\bmod p_i\\(h_1,\ldots,h_5)\ne\mathbf0}}
 D_{B_L}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^5\frac{h_i}{p_i}\right)
 \prod_{i=1}^5F_{p_i,H}(h_i)
 \Bigg)_+
 \\
 &\hspace{8em}\ll_\eta
 X^{o(1)}\left(1+X^2\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H)^5\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{I5-coh}
\]

Here \(\mathcal P_X^{\underline5}\) denotes ordered five-tuples of distinct shell primes. This is the precise analytic meaning of “five-characteristic coherence.” A sum of absolute values, a uniform estimate for every five-tuple, or full independence of all five zero sets would be substantially stronger than necessary.

The most important correction to the proposed intuition is:

> **Isolation does not imply that the five folded zero positions belonging to five different primes are pairwise more than \(H\) apart.**

Isolation is a condition inside each individual folded set. It gives packing, removes the deterministic near contribution, and improves one-prime Fourier energy. It supplies no cross-prime gap by itself. An anchored-star family with one folded root per prime is perfectly isolated and can still make every prime hit one common integer. Thus the word **actual-Franel** is essential: the missing estimate must use the arithmetic fact that the local zero coordinates are those of the genuine Franel/Apéry polynomial, not arbitrary separated sets.

The rank-two, \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-monodromy Mellin result is useful structural information, but the existing Katz-type theorems do not prove I5-coh. They control normalized characteristic-zero/\(\ell\)-adic Mellin traces averaged over characters; I5 asks for defining-prime residual divisibility, additive ordering of character indices, a sparse endogenous isolated subset, and a joint CRT condition across five different characteristics.

## 2. Folded, near, and isolated zero sets

Put

\[
 \mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
 \qquad M=X^2,
\]

\[
 Z_p=\{0\le r\le p-1:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},
 \qquad z_p=|Z_p|.
\]

The proved palindrome gives

\[
 b_r\equiv b_{p-1-r}\pmod p.
\]

Define the folding map

\[
 \sigma_p(r)=\min(r,p-1-r)
 \in\left[0,\frac{p-1}{2}\right]
\]

and the folded zero set

\[
 Y_p=\sigma_p(Z_p).
\]

For an integer \(H\ge1\), define

\[
 Y_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}
 =\left\{y\in Y_p:
    Y_p\cap[y-H,y+H]=\{y\}
  \right\},
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
 Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}
 =\{r\in Z_p:\sigma_p(r)\in Y_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}\},
 \qquad
 Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{near}}=Z_p\setminus Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The central root \((p-1)/2\), when present, has one lift; every other folded root has the two reflected lifts \(y\) and \(p-1-y\).

For \(\star\in\{\mathrm{iso},\mathrm{near}\}\), put

\[
 f_{p,H}^{\star}(m)
 =\mathbf1_{\{m\bmod p\in Z_{p,H}^{\star}\}},
 \qquad
 K_{\star,H}(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}f_{p,H}^{\star}(m).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then

\[
 K_X(m)=K_{\mathrm{near},H}(m)+K_{\mathrm{iso},H}(m).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Finally define the periodic isolated density

\[
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H)
 =\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}|}{p}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

### 2.1 Packing of isolated roots

The points of \(Y_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}\) are more than \(H\) apart in an interval of length \((p-1)/2\). Hence

\[
 |Y_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}|
 \le1+\frac{p-1}{2(H+1)},
 \qquad
 |Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}|
 \le2+\frac{p}{H+1}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H)
 &\le \lambda_X,\\
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H)
 &\ll \sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac2p
       +\frac1H\#\mathcal P_X\\
 &\ll \frac1{\log X}+\frac{X}{H\log X}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H)
 \ll\min\left(
   \lambda_X,
   \frac1{\log X}+\frac{X}{H\log X}
 \right).
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

This packing estimate is the principal quantitative gain supplied by isolation.

### 2.2 The unconditional near bound

If \(p\) contributes to \(K_{\mathrm{near},H}(m)\), the folded root hit by \(m\) has another folded zero at distance some \(1\le h\le H\). Unfolding the two points produces two genuine zeros in characteristic \(p\) separated by \(h\). The repaired wrap dichotomy and gap-polynomial identity in `atom_tail_section.tex` then charge \(p\) to either

- a nonzero gap value \(N_h(t)\), with \(t\) an integer translate determined by \(m\); or
- one of the small-index endpoint values \(b_j\), \(j<h\), in the wrap case.

The height of a fixed gap value gives \(O(h)\) shell-prime divisors, uniformly in \(m<X^2\), and the endpoint terms have the same total scale. Summing over \(h\le H\) yields

\[
\boxed{
 \max_{m<M}K_{\mathrm{near},H}(m)\ll H^2.
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

This part is deterministic and unconditional. I5 is solely about the retained isolated part.

## 3. The precise isolated fifth-factorial tail

Fix \(\eta>0\) and set

\[
 H_X=\max\left(1,
 \left\lfloor X^{7/15}(\log X)^{-1/3}\right\rfloor\right),
 \qquad
 L_X=\left\lceil X^{14/15+\eta}\right\rceil.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let

\[
 B_{X,\eta}
 =\{0\le m<M:K_{\mathrm{iso},H_X}(m)\ge L_X\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### I5-tail, fully quantified

For every fixed \(\eta>0\), there should exist a function
\(\epsilon_\eta(X)\to0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(X\),

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{m\in B_{X,\eta}}
   \bigl(K_{\mathrm{iso},H_X}(m)\bigr)_5
 \ll_\eta
 X^{\epsilon_\eta(X)}
 \left(1+M\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H_X)^5\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Only this single choice of \(H_X\) and this high threshold \(L_X\) is required. A theorem uniform for all \(H\), all \(L\), or all centers would be stronger.

### 3.1 Why this proves the \(14/15+\eta\) atom bound

At the selected scale,

\[
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H_X)
 \ll X^{8/15}(\log X)^{-2/3}+\frac1{\log X},
\tag{3.4}
\]

so

\[
 M\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H_X)^5
 \ll X^{14/3}(\log X)^{-10/3}+X^{2+o(1)}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

If some \(m\) had \(K_{\mathrm{iso},H_X}(m)\ge L_X\), then for large \(X\)

\[
 \sum_{u\in B_{X,\eta}}
 (K_{\mathrm{iso},H_X}(u))_5
 \ge(L_X)_5
 \gg L_X^5
 \gg X^{14/3+5\eta}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

This contradicts (3.3), whose right side is \(X^{14/3+o(1)}\). Therefore

\[
 \max_{m<M}K_{\mathrm{iso},H_X}(m)<L_X.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Together with (2.9),

\[
 \max_{m<M}K_X(m)
 \le L_X+O(H_X^2)
 \ll X^{14/15+\eta}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

### 3.2 Why the fifth factorial moment is the first useful one

A fifth-tail estimate heuristically gives

\[
 \max_m K_X(m)
 \lesssim H^2+M^{1/5}\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}
 =H^2+X^{2/5}\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Using the packing term \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}\ll X/(H\log X)\), this becomes

\[
 H^2+\frac{X^{7/5}}{H\log X}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Balancing the two terms gives

\[
 H^3=\frac{X^{7/5}}{\log X},
 \qquad
 H=X^{7/15}(\log X)^{-1/3},
\tag{3.11}
\]

and both terms have size

\[
 X^{14/15}(\log X)^{-2/3}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

More generally, a \(k\)-star tail would lead to the optimized power

\[
 \frac{2}{3}\left(1+\frac2k\right).
\tag{3.13}
\]

For \(k=4\) this is exactly \(1\), so a four-star argument gives no sublinear power. For \(k=5\) it is \(14/15\). Thus five is the minimal factorial order at which this near/isolated decomposition beats the linear barrier.

## 4. Exact expansion as a five-prime CRT incidence

For brevity write

\[
 f_p(m)=f_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}(m),
 \qquad K(m)=K_{\mathrm{iso},H}(m),
 \qquad B=B_L=\{m<M:K(m)\ge L\}.
\]

Pointwise,

\[
 (K(m))_5
 =\sum_{(p_1,\ldots,p_5)\in\mathcal P_X^{\underline5}}
   \prod_{i=1}^5f_{p_i}(m).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal T_{5}^{\mathrm{iso}}(X;H,L)
 :=\sum_{m\in B}(K(m))_5
 =\sum_{\mathbf p\in\mathcal P_X^{\underline5}}
   J_{\mathbf p}(B),
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

where

\[
 J_{\mathbf p}(B)
 =\#\{m\in B:m\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}
                 \text{ for }1\le i\le5\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

There is no missing factor of \(5!\): both \((K)_5\) and
\(\mathcal P_X^{\underline5}\) use ordered distinct choices.

### 4.1 Least CRT representative

For \(\mathbf p=(p_1,\ldots,p_5)\), let

\[
 P=\prod_{i=1}^5p_i,
 \qquad P_i=P/p_i,
 \qquad \overline P_iP_i\equiv1\pmod{p_i}.
\]

For \(\mathbf r=(r_1,\ldots,r_5)\), define the least representative

\[
 c_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf r)
 =\left[\sum_{i=1}^5r_iP_i\overline P_i\right]_P
 \in[0,P).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since \(P>X^5>M=X^2\), each residue vector has at most one representative below \(M\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
 J_{\mathbf p}(B)
 =\sum_{r_i\in Z_{p_i,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
   \mathbf1_B\bigl(c_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf r)\bigr).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equations (4.2) and (4.5) are the exact combinatorial five-characteristic coherence problem.

The least-residue operation is essential:

\[
 c_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf r)
 =\sum_i r_iP_i\overline P_i
 -P\left\lfloor
   \frac{\sum_i r_iP_i\overline P_i}{P}
  \right\rfloor.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The floor and the cutoff couple all five primes and all five roots. Replacing (4.4) by the un-reduced idempotent sum destroys its residue modulo any auxiliary modulus and gives the wrong witness, exactly as in the two-prime audit in Q55.

### 4.2 Quotient-cell and sign expansion

The folded geometry can also be written without CRT idempotents. If \(m\) is hit by \(p\), put

\[
 q=\left\lfloor\frac mp\right\rfloor,
 \qquad y=\sigma_p(m\bmod p).
\]

Choose \(s=+1\) when \(m\bmod p=y\), and \(s=-1\) when
\(m\bmod p=p-1-y\). Then

\[
 m=\Phi_{q,s}(p,y),
\tag{4.7}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
 \Phi_{q,s}(p,y)
 =\left(q+\frac{1-s}{2}\right)p
  -\frac{1-s}{2}+sy.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Explicitly,

\[
 \Phi_{q,+}(p,y)=qp+y,
 \qquad
 \Phi_{q,-}(p,y)=(q+1)p-1-y.
\tag{4.9}
\]

After removing the duplicate sign at a central root, (4.2) becomes the exact equal-intercept count

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T_{5}^{\mathrm{iso}}(X;H,L)
 =\sum_{\mathbf q,\mathbf s}
 \#\Bigl\{&(p_i,y_i)_{i=1}^5:
 p_i\in\mathcal P_X\text{ distinct},\ 
 y_i\in Y_{p_i,H}^{\mathrm{iso}},\\
 &\Phi_{q_1,s_1}(p_1,y_1)=\cdots
 =\Phi_{q_5,s_5}(p_5,y_5)=m\in B_L
 \Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus the exact geometry is: five genuine isolated folded zeros, in five different characteristics, lie on five affine quotient/sign rays having one common integer intercept \(m\).

Again, (4.10) does **not** assert that the numbers \(y_1,\ldots,y_5\) are mutually separated. Each \(y_i\) is separated only from other roots in \(Y_{p_i}\).

## 5. Exact additive-character form

Let

\[
 e(t)=e^{2\pi it},
 \qquad e_p(t)=e(t/p),
\]

\[
 F_{p,H}(h)
 =\sum_{r\in Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}}e_p(-hr),
 \qquad h\bmod p,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and for any \(B\subseteq[0,M)\),

\[
 D_B(\alpha)=\sum_{m\in B}e(\alpha m).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Finite Fourier inversion gives, for every ordered distinct five-tuple,

\[
\boxed{
 J_{\mathbf p}(B)
 =\frac1P
  \sum_{h_i\bmod p_i}
  D_B\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^5\frac{h_i}{p_i}\right)
  \prod_{i=1}^5F_{p_i,H}(h_i).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The term \(h_1=\cdots=h_5=0\) is

\[
 |B|\prod_{i=1}^5\frac{|Z_{p_i,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}|}{p_i}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

After summing over ordered distinct tuples,

\[
 \mathcal D_0(B)
 :=|B|\sum_{\mathbf p\in\mathcal P_X^{\underline5}}
     \prod_{i=1}^5
     \frac{|Z_{p_i,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}|}{p_i}
 \le |B|\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H)^5
 \le M\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H)^5.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Define the nonzero-frequency aggregate

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak C_{5}^{\mathrm{iso}}(B;X,H)
 :=&\sum_{\mathbf p\in\mathcal P_X^{\underline5}}
 \frac1P
 \sum_{\substack{h_i\bmod p_i\\\mathbf h\ne\mathbf0}}
 D_B\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^5\frac{h_i}{p_i}\right)
 \prod_{i=1}^5F_{p_i,H}(h_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal T_{5}^{\mathrm{iso}}(X;H,L)
 =\mathcal D_0(B_L)
  +\mathfrak C_{5}^{\mathrm{iso}}(B_L;X,H).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The aggregate in (5.6) is real by conjugate symmetry. Moreover, because the denominators \(p_i\) are distinct primes, an exact relation

\[
 \sum_i h_i/p_i\in\mathbb Z,
 \qquad 0\le h_i<p_i,
\]

forces every \(h_i=0\). Thus every term in (5.6) is a genuinely nontrivial additive phase.

Since the zero-frequency term is already bounded by the desired main scale, I5-tail is equivalent, up to an absolute constant, to

\[
\boxed{
 \left(
  \mathfrak C_{5}^{\mathrm{iso}}(B_{X,\eta};X,H_X)
 \right)_+
 \ll_\eta X^{o(1)}
 \left(1+M\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}(X,H_X)^5\right).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is the exact character-sum formulation requested in the question.

### 5.1 Stronger but cleaner variants

The following full isolated fifth moment is sufficient but stronger:

\[
 \sum_{m<M}(K_{\mathrm{iso},H}(m))_5
 \ll X^{o(1)}\left(1+M\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}^5\right).
\tag{5.9}
\]

Its advantage is that

\[
 D_{[0,M)}(\alpha)
 =\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}e(\alpha m)
\]

is the explicit Dirichlet kernel, with

\[
 |D_{[0,M)}(\alpha)|\le
 \min\left(M,\frac1{2\|\alpha\|}\right).
\tag{5.10}
\]

For the minimal tail theorem, \(D_{B_L}\) is endogenous and may have no a priori minor-arc decay. Thus a sensible analytic program is to attack (5.9) first; if moderate-load centers make (5.9) false or too expensive, return to the weak-type form (5.8) using a level-set or density-increment argument.

The following are all stronger than I5 requires:

- a power-saving estimate for every fixed five-tuple \(\mathbf p\);
- a bound for the sum of absolute values of all nonzero-frequency terms;
- a product-distribution formula for every residue box;
- full equidistribution of the isolated roots inside every \(Z_p\).

Only the positive part of the aggregate (5.6), at one threshold and one scale \(H_X\), is needed.

## 6. The exact Franel object

The repository proves the characteristic-\(p\) identity

\[
 K_p(x)^2=(1+x)^{p-1}A_p(\phi(x))
 =\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r\Psi_{p,r}(x),
\tag{6.1}
\]

where

\[
 A_p(a)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_ra^r,
 \qquad
 K_p(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}f_rx^r,
 \qquad
 f_r=\sum_k\binom rk^3,
\tag{6.2}
\]

\[
 \phi(x)=\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x},
 \qquad
 \Psi_{p,r}(x)=x^r(1-8x)^r(1+x)^{p-1-r}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The \(\Psi_{p,r}\) are linearly independent. Let

\[
 \ell_{p,r}(P)=[P]_{\Psi_{p,r}}
\]

be the corresponding coordinate functional. Then

\[
\boxed{
 r\in Z_p
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ell_{p,r}(K_p^2)=0
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Using reflection, a folded point \(y\) is isolated exactly when

\[
 \ell_{p,y}(K_p^2)=0
\tag{6.5}
\]

and

\[
 \ell_{p,y'}(K_p^2)\ne0
 \quad
 \text{for every }y'\in[0,(p-1)/2],\quad
 0<|y'-y|\le H.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Consequently, the actual-Franel five-characteristic incidence is the disjoint union, over prime, quotient, and sign data, of the sets

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak I(\mathbf p,\mathbf q,\mathbf s)
 =\bigl\{(m,y_1,\ldots,y_5):
 &\ \ell_{p_i,y_i}(K_{p_i}^2)=0,\\
 &\ \ell_{p_i,y'}(K_{p_i}^2)\ne0
       \text{ for }0<|y'-y_i|\le H,\\
 &\ m=\Phi_{q_i,s_i}(p_i,y_i)
       \text{ for all }i,\\
 &\ m<M,\ K_{\mathrm{iso},H}(m)\ge L
 \bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.7}
\]

After the central-sign multiplicity convention, the total cardinality of (6.7) is exactly the left side of I5-tail.

This identifies the geometric object as precisely as the current theory permits:

- locally, one has five coordinate-zero/nonzero conditions on five genuine Franel squares \(K_{p_i}^2\);
- globally, the five local conditions are glued by the integer equal-intercept/CRT condition;
- the cutoff \(m<X^2\) and high-load condition are arithmetic, not Zariski conditions over one finite field.

There is **no single finite-type variety over one finite field** whose ordinary Frobenius trace is (6.7). It is a mixed-characteristic arithmetic incidence correspondence. Isolation is an open nonvanishing restriction; it is not an additional equation and does not automatically contribute algebraic codimension.

That is what “actual-Franel five-characteristic coherence” must mean: prove that these particular coordinate-zero loci cannot make too many fivefold equal-intercept stars.

## 7. Exactly how I5 differs from \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

The full third factorial moment is

\[
 F_3(X)=\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_3,
\]

and \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) asks for

\[
 F_3(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The exact result in `hm3_result.tex` says that this is equivalent to controlling the positive pair-Palm overload: after fixing a two-prime CRT witness, third primes may not be systematically overrepresented on that witness locus.

The differences are structural.

| Feature | \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) | I5 |
|---|---|---|
| roots used | every root in every \(Z_p\) | only folded \(H\)-isolated roots |
| centers used | every \(m<X^2\) | only \(K_{\mathrm{iso}}(m)\ge L\) |
| factorial order | 3 | 5 |
| deterministic preprocessing | none | near part removed with \(K_{\mathrm{near}}\ll H^2\) |
| density parameter | \(\lambda_X\) | smaller \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}\) |
| exact obstruction | pair witness overloaded by third primes | high-load five-star CRT coherence |
| behavior permitted | no large total third moment | arbitrary low/moderate-load behavior is allowed |

Thus I5 is easier in the precise sense that it permits:

1. every clustered root to be discarded and handled by the gap-polynomial bound;
2. every center below the very high threshold \(L_X\) to be ignored;
3. signed cancellation after summing all five-tuples, rather than tuplewise independence;
4. an \(X^{o(1)}\) loss on a main term already reduced by isolated-root packing.

It is harder only superficially because the factorial order is five rather than three. At a high-load center, five is an advantage: one center contributes \((K)_5\asymp K^5\), so a weak global fifth-star bound rules out that center.

### 7.1 The claimed cross-prime gap advantage is false

For \(p\ne q\), the numbers

\[
 y_p=\sigma_p(m\bmod p),
 \qquad
 y_q=\sigma_q(m\bmod q)
\]

belong to different folded zero sets. The statement

\[
 |y_p-y_q|>H
\]

is not implied by either root being isolated. They may even be equal as integers.

A decisive abstract countermodel is the anchored isolated star. Fix one integer \(m_0<M\), and for each shell prime, after deleting the finitely many endpoint degeneracies, set

\[
 Z_p^*=\{m_0\bmod p,\ p-1-(m_0\bmod p)\}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The folded set \(\sigma_p(Z_p^*)\) has one point, hence is \(H\)-isolated for every \(H\). Nevertheless every active prime hits \(m_0\), so

\[
 K_{\mathrm{iso}}^*(m_0)\asymp\frac{X}{\log X}.
\]

Therefore folding, reflection, no-neighbor conditions, and packing alone do not imply I5. The proof must distinguish the actual Franel coordinates from arbitrary isolated coordinates.

### 7.2 What isolation genuinely gives analytically

For each fixed \(p\), \(Y_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}\) is \(H\)-separated. Standard large-sieve duality therefore gives, for coefficients \(c_y\) and \(A<p/2\), a bound of the form

\[
 \sum_{|a|\le A}
 \left|\sum_{y\in Y_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 c_y e_p(ay)\right|^2
 \ll\left(A+\frac pH\right)
 \sum_y|c_y|^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The transform of the unfolded reflected set is a cosine transform of this separated set, up to the central-root correction. Thus (7.3) supplies a genuine one-prime low-frequency energy improvement. It is useful in a CRT dispersion argument, but it does not replace the missing five-prime arithmetic input.

## 8. Can the rank-two Mellin sheaf help more here?

### 8.1 Exact local Mellin interpretation

Under the normalization used in Q59, let \(\omega_p\) be the Teichmüller character and let

\[
 T_p(r)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^*}\theta_p(a)\omega_p(a)^r
\tag{8.1}
\]

be the Mellin transform of the primitive K3 trace. For \(1\le r\le p-2\), the exact toric identity gives

\[
\boxed{
 T_p(r)\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p},
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

for the chosen prime \(\mathfrak p_p\) above \(p\) in the coefficient field. The punctual \(a=1\) correction between the literal deflated trace and the canonical middle-extension trace is a multiple of \(p\), so it does not change (8.2).

The Q59 calculation identifies the canonical generic Mellin cohomology as rank two, with normalized geometric Tannakian group \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) and compact form \(\mathrm{SU}_2\), after excluding the bounded exceptional set and making the stated middle-extension normalization.

Therefore

\[
 r\in Z_p
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathfrak p_p\mid
 \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid V_{p,r}),
\tag{8.3}
\]

where \(V_{p,r}\) is the rank-two Mellin cohomology at the character indexed by \(r\).

### 8.2 Why existing Katz equidistribution still does not prove I5

Katz, Forey–Fresán–Kowalski, and the new varying-characteristic theorem of Bah–Shuddhodan control normalized characteristic-zero/\(\ell\)-adic Frobenius conjugacy classes as multiplicative characters are averaged. I5 needs four additional operations not supplied by those theorems.

1. **Defining-prime reduction.** The predicate is
   \[
   \mathfrak p_p\mid T_p(r),
   \]
   not \(T_p(r)=0\) as a complex number. Haar measure on \(\mathrm{SU}_2\) does not see reduction at the same prime as the base characteristic.

2. **Ordered index geometry.** Mellin characters are naturally indexed modulo \(p-1\). The CRT uses the same integer label as a residue modulo \(p\), and then folds it by \(r\mapsto p-1-r\). Character equidistribution is invariant under permuting the character labels; I5 is not.

3. **Endogenous sparse selection.** The isolated subset is selected by asking which neighboring Mellin traces are also divisible by \(p\). It is not an independently chosen set of characters to which ordinary equidistribution may be conditioned without bias.

4. **Five different characteristics.** The five local sheaves live over five different finite fields and are coupled by the integer CRT/equal-intercept relation. There is no existing single-base tensor-product sheaf whose monodromy theorem gives this joint law.

Bah–Shuddhodan materially improves the horizontal theory by allowing bounded-complexity sheaves and the characteristic to vary while the common Tannakian group remains fixed. But its empirical measure still averages essentially all multiplicative characters in each field and records unitary conjugacy classes. It does not control the selected residual trace-zero indices or their CRT alignment.

### 8.3 In what sense Mellin helps more for I5 than for HM3

It helps **structurally**, but not yet as an unconditional estimate.

I5 asks only for:

- one fixed tensor order, five;
- a high-tail aggregate rather than a full distribution theorem;
- a subset whose total density has already been reduced by \(H\)-packing;
- an \(X^{o(1)}\)-accurate upper bound, not a pointwise asymptotic for every tuple.

Thus a future residual large sieve need not prove full zero equidistribution. It would suffice to prove that the rank-two residual trace-zero events have no fivefold excess on the CRT-compatible isolated locus.

The natural hoped-for group is a product

\[
 \mathrm{SL}_2^5,
\]

with one codimension-one residual trace condition in each factor. But this slogan is not a theorem: the factors have different residue characteristics, and the characters are coupled by (4.8). The missing technology is a mixed-characteristic, defining-prime, ordered-character large sieve.

A precise name for the residual is:

> **Residual isolated Mellin five-sieve (RIM5):** prove I5-coh after replacing each local zero indicator by the equivalent residual rank-two Mellin trace condition (8.3), uniformly over the quotient/sign CRT strata.

RIM5 is genuinely narrower than a general crystalline Katz theorem, but no current Katz-type theorem implies it.

## 9. A complete attack program

### Phase A. Land the deterministic I5 reduction

Write a self-contained section proving, with exact constants or uniform \(O(1)\) losses:

1. the definitions (2.1)–(2.5);
2. the packing estimate (2.8);
3. the near bound (2.9), including every wrap and central-root case;
4. the factorial identity (4.2);
5. the CRT identity (4.5);
6. the Fourier identity (5.7);
7. the implication I5-tail \(\Rightarrow\) (3.8);
8. the optimizer (3.11).

This phase is unconditional and should be formalizable independently of the unresolved coherence theorem.

A useful theorem statement is:

```text
I5 reduction theorem.
For every eta>0, I5-tail at H=X^(7/15)(log X)^(-1/3)
and L=X^(14/15+eta) implies
max_{m<X^2} K_X(m) << X^(14/15+eta).
```

### Phase B. Exact numerical census of the target statistic

For dyadic \(X\) within the existing zero database, compute exactly:

- \(|Y_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}|\), \(|Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{near}}|\), and \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}\);
- \(K_{\mathrm{near},H}(m)\), \(K_{\mathrm{iso},H}(m)\), and their maxima;
- the level counts \(\#\{m:K_{\mathrm{iso},H}(m)\ge L\}\);
- the exact tail \(\sum_{K_{\mathrm{iso}}\ge L}(K_{\mathrm{iso}})_5\);
- the ratio of this tail to \(1+M\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}^5\);
- the quotient/sign signatures \((q_i,s_i)\) of the largest five-stars;
- the distribution of near-resonant phases \(\|\sum h_i/p_i\|\) in (5.6).

The purpose is not to infer a proof from Poisson behavior. It is to decide whether the stronger full moment (5.9) is viable and to identify the exact major-arc strata carrying any excess.

### Phase C. Prove the deterministic isolated Fourier lemmas

The first analytic deliverables should be:

1. the separated-set large-sieve estimate (7.3), including the reflected cosine transform;
2. bounds for higher local energies of \(F_{p,H}\) using the absence of folded differences \(1\le|d|\le H\);
3. a uniform treatment of the central folded root;
4. average versions over \(p\in(X,2X]\) that retain the actual sizes \(|Z_{p,H}^{\mathrm{iso}}|\), rather than replacing them by the worst-case packing bound.

These are the precise unconditional gains from isolation.

### Phase D. Attack the cleaner full-moment Fourier problem

For (5.9), insert the Dirichlet-kernel bound (5.10) and split according to

\[
 \left\|\sum_{i=1}^5\frac{h_i}{p_i}\right\|
 \le\frac1M
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 >\frac1M.
\tag{9.1}
\]

- **Minor arcs:** combine (5.10), the isolated large-sieve energy, Cauchy–Schwarz/Hölder, and spacing of rational sums with distinct prime denominators.
- **Major arcs:** classify near-relations among the five rational frequencies. Exact equality is only the zero mode, but there are many possible \(1/M\)-near-relations because the product denominator is of order \(X^5\). This is where generic Fourier estimates are least likely to suffice.

A successful absolute-value minor-arc estimate would leave a signed major-arc residual. The major-arc theorem should be formulated only as strongly as needed for the positive aggregate in (5.8).

If the full moment (5.9) is too strong, use the resulting minor-arc machinery inside a restricted weak-type or density-increment argument for \(B_L\). One must not silently apply Dirichlet-kernel decay to \(D_{B_L}\); unlike \(D_{[0,M)}\), the high-load level set has no automatic Fourier decay.

### Phase E. Use the actual Franel structure on the major arcs

Stratify by \((\mathbf q,\mathbf s)\) in (4.10). The exact target becomes an averaged bound for five systems

\[
 \ell_{p_i,y_i}(K_{p_i}^2)=0,
 \qquad
 \Phi_{q_i,s_i}(p_i,y_i)=m.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Potential subroutes are:

1. **Coefficient-coordinate rigidity.** Use the fact that the vanishing coordinates come from one universal Franel sequence, not arbitrary polynomials, to derive a cross-characteristic determinant, resultant, or compatible-system obstruction for five equal-intercept points.

2. **Quotient-cell dispersion.** For fixed \(\mathbf q,\mathbf s\), treat (9.2) as a five-linear incidence problem in \((p_i,y_i)\), exploiting the affine dependence of \(y_i\) on \(p_i\) and the local isolation large sieve.

3. **Residual Mellin sieve.** Replace (9.2) by the five residual trace conditions (8.3), prove large residual monodromy away from a bounded exceptional set, and develop a large sieve whose sampling set is the CRT-compatible ordered exponent locus.

4. **Carrier/exceptional-locus theorem.** Show that any major-arc fivefold coherence forces the primes or quotient data onto an explicitly bounded-height carrier; sum the shell-prime divisors of that carrier. This would parallel the gap-polynomial philosophy but must genuinely cross characteristics.

The terminal theorem of this phase is not “the roots are equidistributed.” It is exactly the positive aggregate I5-coh.

### Phase F. Assemble and optimize

Once I5-coh is proved:

1. add the zero-frequency term using (5.5);
2. obtain I5-tail from (5.7);
3. use (3.6) to eliminate the high-load isolated set;
4. add the unconditional near contribution (2.9);
5. use the optimizer (3.11) to obtain \(X^{14/15+\eta}\).

## 11. Answers to the four numbered questions

### 1. Precise isolated fifth-factorial tail

It is (3.3): for every fixed \(\eta>0\), at

\[
 H_X=X^{7/15}(\log X)^{-1/3},
 \qquad
 L_X=X^{14/15+\eta},
\]

bound the fifth factorial moment only over centers with
\(K_{\mathrm{iso},H_X}(m)\ge L_X\) by

\[
 X^{o(1)}\left(1+X^2\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}^5\right).
\]

### 2. Exact CRT/character sum/geometric object

The exact CRT count is (4.2)–(4.5). The exact additive-character expansion is (5.3)–(5.8). The exact actual-Franel object is the mixed-characteristic incidence (6.7): five isolated cover-adapted coordinate zeros of the genuine Franel squares, tied to one common integer by the quotient/sign equations (4.8).

### 3. Difference from \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

I5 discards all near roots, ignores every center below a high threshold, and asks only for a fifth-star upper tail. It is therefore strictly weaker in the directions that matter for the atom bound. But isolation does **not** impose cross-prime pairwise gaps; its actual gains are packing and one-prime separation. The anchored isolated-star model proves that actual Apéry/Franel arithmetic is still indispensable.

### 4. Role of the Mellin sheaf

The rank-two/\(\mathrm{SL}_2\) theorem identifies a bounded-complexity local object and suggests a product-monodromy framework for a future fivefold residual sieve. Existing Katz, Forey–Fresán–Kowalski, and Bah–Shuddhodan equidistribution do not control the defining-prime residual trace-zero predicate, ordered exponent positions, isolation conditioning, or five-characteristic CRT coupling. Thus they presently give no unconditional I5 saving, although I5 is a substantially narrower target for any new residual Mellin technology than full \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) or complete zero equidistribution.

## Final residual

The I5 route is reduced exactly to the following named problem:

> **Actual-Franel isolated five-characteristic coherence:** bound the positive nonzero-frequency aggregate (5.6) on the high-load level set, at \(H=X^{7/15}(\log X)^{-1/3}\), by \(X^{o(1)}(1+X^2\Lambda_{\mathrm{iso}}^5)\).

This is neither an ordinary Katz equidistribution theorem nor a consequence of pairwise folded gaps. It is a mixed-characteristic residual incidence theorem for five genuine Franel coordinate zeros under an integer CRT constraint.
