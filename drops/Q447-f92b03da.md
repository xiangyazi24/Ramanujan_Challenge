# Q447: Family-averaged Lang--Trotter for Apéry zeros

## Executive summary

The reformulation is very promising, but the direct Fouvry--Murty / David--Pappalardi theorem does **not** apply in its present form. The reason is structural:

\[
D_a(n)=\#\{p:\ a_p(M_{n/p})\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]

is not an average over a fixed geometric family. The parameter of the motive changes with the prime. This is closer to a **diagonal trace-function problem** than to classical averaged Lang--Trotter.

The right framework is indeed Katz--Sarnak / l-adic monodromy, but the needed theorem is a new type of "moving-parameter Lang--Trotter" estimate. If one could prove the expected local estimate

\[
\#\{\alpha\in\mathcal A_p: a_p(M_\alpha)=0\}\ll 1,
\]

with enough uniformity, then summing over primes would give the desired bounded (or logarithmic) result.

---

# 1. Does Fouvry--Murty / David--Pappalardi apply?

## (a) The parameter alpha=n/p varies with p

Classical averaged Lang--Trotter has the shape:

\[
\sum_{E\in\mathcal F(X)}\#\{p\le x:a_p(E)=0\}.
\]

The family \(\mathcal F\) is fixed first, and primes are averaged second.

Your problem is instead:

\[
\sum_{p\le n}1_{a_p(M_{n/p})=0}.
\]

The two quantifiers are reversed. The motive is selected after seeing p.

This is not covered by Fouvry--Murty.

The closest analogy is not a family average but a "thin diagonal" in the two-dimensional space

\[
(p,\alpha).
\]

The set being sampled is

\[
\alpha=n/p,
\]

which has only one point per prime.

---

## (b) Vanishing mod p versus a_p=0

This is a major difference.

For an elliptic curve over \(\mathbb Q\), Lang--Trotter studies

\[
a_p(E)=0
\]

where Hasse gives \(|a_p|\le2\sqrt p\).

For Apéry motives the condition is instead

\[
a_p(M_\alpha)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

This means the trace in \(\mathbb F_p\) vanishes. It is closer to asking for zeros of a trace function:

\[
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p,x)=0.
\]

The expected probability is about \(1/p\), not \(1/\sqrt p\).

This is actually stronger probabilistically. It explains the observed bounded D_a(n).

---

## (c) Continuous alpha parameter

The continuous parameter is not directly useful.

Arithmetic geometry does not average over real alpha. One needs a mod-p parameter space:

\[
\alpha\in \mathbb F_p \quad\text{or}\quad \mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p).
\]

For rational alpha=a/b, the relevant object is a family of hypergeometric sheaves over a parameter variety.

The correct family is not the real interval [0,1], but the algebraic parameter space whose fibers specialize to the motives M_alpha.

---

# 2. What Fouvry--Murty actually requires

The Fouvry--Murty theorem concerns elliptic curves in a box family such as

\[
E_{a,b}:y^2=x^3+ax+b.
\]

One averages over integral parameters (a,b) and obtains an asymptotic for supersingular primes.

The ingredients are:

1. A genuine algebraic family.
2. Enough independent parameters.
3. Control of Frobenius distributions.
4. Sieve estimates.

For modern formulations, the key condition is usually expressed as large geometric monodromy.

For higher-dimensional motives, one expects an analogous condition:

\[
G_{geom}=Sp_d,\ SO_d,\ SL_d,
\]

or another maximal classical group.

A fixed rational parameter alpha is fine. A real parameter is not the relevant notion.

The error terms are family-dependent; roughly they come from square-root cancellation in character sums plus sieve losses. They do not immediately imply anything for a one-dimensional moving diagonal.

# 3. Katz--Sarnak viewpoint

The Katz--Sarnak intuition is the correct one.

Suppose there is a family

\[
\mathcal M\rightarrow X
\]

of hypergeometric motives with large monodromy.

For each p, we have a trace function

\[
t_p(x)=a_p(M_x)\in\mathbb F_p.
\]

The desired local statement is:

\[
\#\{x\in X(\mathbb F_p):t_p(x)=0\}\approx \frac{|X(\mathbb F_p)|}{p}.
\]

This follows heuristically from equidistribution of Frobenius in the monodromy group.

Then

\[
\sum_{p\in(\sqrt n,n]}\frac1p\approx\log2,
\]

so the expected size is O(1).

This is exactly the mechanism suggested by computation.

However, the missing theorem is:

\[
t_p(x)\text{ has enough cancellation uniformly on the diagonal }x=n/p.
\]

That is not automatic from Katz--Sarnak.

---

# 4. What is the actual family?

The candidate family is the hypergeometric family behind Apéry numbers.

Apéry numbers arise from a hypergeometric motive of type roughly

\[
{}_4F_3
\]

with special fibers giving modular forms.

The parameter alpha should enter through a shifted hypergeometric sheaf:

\[
M_\alpha \leftrightarrow \mathcal H(\alpha).
\]

The parameter space is expected to be an algebraic curve or higher-dimensional variety over \(\mathbb Q\).

The monodromy should be a classical group determined by the weight and polarization:

- weight 3 Apéry-type motives: often orthogonal/symplectic depending on realization;
- rank 4 hypergeometric motives frequently have \(Sp_4\)-type monodromy.

The precise group must be computed for the actual sheaf. It cannot be assumed from numerical independence alone.

---

# 5. Has this been done for hypergeometric motives?

Related pieces exist:

* Katz developed the theory of hypergeometric sheaves and their monodromy groups.
* Rodriguez-Villegas studied hypergeometric motives and point counting.
* Long--Tu--Yui studied modularity of Apéry-like recurrences.
* Ahlgren--Ono proved modular congruences for Apéry numbers.
* Roberts--Rodriguez-Villegas studied hypergeometric supercongruences.

However, a theorem of the form

\[
\#\{p:a_p(M_{n/p})=0\}=O(1)
\]

for a moving hypergeometric family does not appear to exist.

---

# 6. Best route to close P3.2

The target should not be classical Lang--Trotter. The more natural theorem is:

## Moving-parameter trace-function anti-concentration

Let \(t_p(x)\) be the Apéry trace function. Prove:

\[
\#\{x\in I_p:t_p(x)=0\}\ll p^{1-\delta}
\]

for every interval I_p of length comparable to p.

Then for the special set

\[
x=n/p
\]

one obtains strong anti-concentration.

Even the weaker estimate

\[
\#\{x:t_p(x)=0\}=O(p^{1-\delta})
\]

would be enough after combining with the large-prime range.

The expected random-model bound is actually much stronger:

\[
\#\{x:t_p(x)=0\}=O(1).
\]

---

# Conclusion

1. Fouvry--Murty / David--Pappalardi do not directly apply because the family parameter moves with p.
2. The condition mod p is a trace-function zero problem, not classical Lang--Trotter.
3. Katz--Sarnak is the correct conceptual framework.
4. The needed object is an algebraic family of hypergeometric motives with large monodromy, probably Sp_4-type.
5. The missing ingredient is a uniform anti-concentration theorem for Apéry hypergeometric trace functions on moving rational parameters.

This reformulation is nevertheless important: it changes P3.2 from a problem about prime divisors of individual Apéry numbers into a geometric monodromy/trace-function problem, where an O(1) bound is the natural conjecture.
