# Crystalline Mellin anti-concentration: the M1 target

## Executive summary

The formulation in terms of the Kummer Mellin transform is the right one:

\[
 b_r=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^*}\mathrm{Ha}_p(t)t^{-r}.
\]

The missing theorem is **not** a monodromy theorem. It is an anti-concentration theorem for the Mellin transform of a special crystalline function. SL₂/Sp₄ monodromy controls geometric irreducibility and trace statistics, but it does not by itself control how often a Fourier coefficient vanishes.

The realistic routes are:

1. A Kummer large sieve / trace-function argument in the character variable \(r\).
2. A Dwork-crystalline argument showing the Mellin transform is not concentrated on a large character subspace.
3. A pair-correlation estimate for the zero indicator.

The unit-root and slope filtrations provide structural information, but alone they do not give M1.

---

# (A) Generic Mellin coefficients versus Apéry Hasse invariant

Let

\[
H(t)=\mathrm{Ha}_p(t)
\]

and define

\[
\widehat H(r)=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^*}H(t)t^{-r}.
\]

For a random function

\[
H:\mathbf F_p^*\rightarrow \mathbf F_p,
\]

all Fourier coefficients are approximately independent random variables. Since a random element of \(\mathbf F_p\) vanishes with probability \(1/p\), one expects

\[
\mathbb E|Z_p|\approx (p-1)/p\approx1.
\]

This heuristic is exactly the observed Poisson(1) behavior.

However Apéry \(H(t)\) is highly structured. It is a Hasse invariant of a K3 family, hence:

- it satisfies Picard-Fuchs differential constraints;
- it comes from a crystalline cohomology class;
- its coefficients satisfy Dwork congruences.

Therefore the generic random-function model cannot be used directly.

The correct target is a statement of the form:

\[
\#\{r:\widehat H(r)=0\}=O(1)
\]

on average over primes.

Equivalently, the Mellin transform must have no exceptional concentration on characters.

---

# (B) Dwork congruences

Dwork congruences give relations such as

\[
a_{mp^k}\equiv a_{mp^{k-1}}\pmod {p^k}.
\]

For the Apéry sequence this reflects Frobenius stability of the unit-root part.

However M1 concerns

\[
b_r\pmod p,
\]

with

\[
0\le r<p.
\]

The first digit range is exactly the range where Dwork lifting has the least leverage: there is no previous base-p digit to constrain.

Dwork gives strong information for indices related by multiplication by \(p\), but inside one residue block it does not directly prevent

\[
b_r=0\pmod p.
\]

A possible useful consequence would be a theorem of the form:

\[
b_r=0\Rightarrow r\text{ belongs to a Frobenius-stable exceptional set},
\]

where the exceptional set has bounded crystalline complexity. Such a statement is currently the missing ingredient.

---

# (C) Slope filtration

For an ordinary K3 fiber, the crystalline slopes are

\[
0,1,1,2.
\]

The slope-zero piece corresponds to the unit-root F-crystal.

The Hasse invariant detects whether the slope-zero piece survives. Thus

\[
\mathrm{Ha}_p(t)=0
\]

is a statement about a fiber becoming nonordinary.

But the M1 condition is different:

\[
b_r=0
\]

is a Mellin coefficient vanishing condition.

The slope filtration controls the values of \(H(t)\) as \(t\) varies. It does not immediately control Fourier cancellation among the Kummer characters.

The missing bridge is a theorem saying that the unit-root function has no large Fourier spectrum.

In harmonic-analysis language:

slope theory controls the function;
M1 needs uncertainty-principle control of its Mellin transform.

---

# (D) Kummer large sieve

This is the most promising rigorous direction.

The characters

\[
\chi_r(t)=t^r
\]

form the dual group of \(\mathbf F_p^*\).

The Mellin coefficients are

\[
M(r)=\sum_t H(t)\chi_r(t).
\]

A standard large sieve gives

\[
\sum_r |M(r)|^2
=(p-1)\sum_t |H(t)|^2
\]

by Parseval.

This is true but insufficient: it controls the total energy, not the number of exact zeros.

To prove M1, one needs a small-ball estimate:

\[
\#\{r:M(r)=0\}\ll 1
\]

on average over \(p\).

A useful intermediate target is a fourth moment bound:

\[
\sum_r |M(r)|^4 \ll p^2.
\]

Then the additive energy of the Mellin spectrum is bounded, preventing too many zero frequencies.

For the pair-zero estimate one wants the stronger correlation statement:

\[
\sum_p \binom{|Z_p|/2}{2}=O(\pi(N)).
\]

This is a second moment theorem in the Kummer variable.

---

# Why monodromy alone fails

SL₂ monodromy says the geometric representation is large. It implies Frobenius matrices are distributed in the algebraic group.

But the condition

\[
M(r)=0
\]

is not a trace condition. It is a Fourier coefficient condition.

Different characters \(\chi_r\) probe the same Frobenius object in different directions. Trace equidistribution does not automatically imply coefficient anti-concentration.

The missing theorem is analogous to:

"A trace function with large geometric monodromy has Fourier coefficients with bounded zero multiplicity."

Such a theorem is not available in this generality.

---

# Most realistic proof target

The strongest plausible first theorem is not directly M1. It is:

## Kummer fourth moment theorem

For Apéry Hasse invariants:

\[
\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p^*}
\left|
\sum_t \mathrm{Ha}_p(t)t^{-r}
\right|^4
\ll p^2.
\]

Consequences:

1. bounded Mellin concentration;
2. pair-zero estimate;
3. M1 and M2 simultaneously.

This matches the observed data:

\[
D_p=\binom{|Z_p|/2}{2}
\]

has mean about \(1/8\), consistent with a Poisson(1/2) pair model.

---

# Final verdict

(A) Generic polynomials have about one zero Mellin coefficient. The Apéry Hasse invariant should behave similarly, but this requires proving a crystalline anti-concentration theorem.

(B) Dwork congruences explain Frobenius self-similarity but do not directly constrain the first digit zeros.

(C) Slope filtrations explain the Hasse invariant geometry but do not control Mellin zeros.

(D) The Kummer large sieve is the correct framework. Parseval is easy; the missing ingredient is a fourth-moment/small-ball estimate for the Apéry Hasse invariant.

The precise missing statement for P3.2 is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The Mellin transform of the Apéry K3 Hasse invariant has bounded zero multiplicity on average over }p.
}
\]

This is a crystalline analogue of Fourier anti-concentration, not a consequence of monodromy alone.
