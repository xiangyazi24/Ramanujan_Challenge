# Q753: Proportional prime-factor slice audit

## Verdict

**Verdict: no existing general theorem for nondegenerate P-recursive, G-function, diagonal, or Apéry sequences gives the required bound**
\[
\#\{p\in(n/(q+1),n/q]:p\mid b_n\}=o_q(n/\log n)
\]
for Apéry numbers. The obstruction is not the lack of a recurrence; it is the lack of a theorem controlling the density of large prime divisors in a prescribed proportional interval for a single term of a holonomic sequence.

The strongest applicable general results only give structural information (finiteness of prime divisors of a term, gcd bounds, height bounds, or growth of the sequence), not the required local prime-factor sparsity.

---

## 1. What is needed

For fixed q, define
\[
P_q(n)=\#\{p: n/(q+1)<p\le n/q,\ p\mid b_n\}.
\]
The trivial estimate is
\[
P_q(n)\le \pi(n/q)-\pi(n/(q+1))\asymp_q n/\log n.
\]
The target requires a strict saving:
\[
P_q(n)=o_q(n/\log n).
\]
This asks that a positive proportion of primes in a macroscopic interval fail to divide one very special integer b_n.

---

# 2. Candidate theorem audit

## 2.1 P-recursive sequence results

A P-recursive sequence satisfies
\[
\sum_{i=0}^d P_i(n)a_{n+i}=0
\]
with polynomial coefficients.

Known closure and arithmetic results imply:

- asymptotic expansions;
- holonomic closure under sums/products/transforms;
- differential equation descriptions of generating functions.

They do **not** imply bounds of the form
\[
\#\{p\in I_n:p\mid a_n\}=o(|I_n|/\log n).
\]

Reason: P-recursiveness is compatible with extremely different arithmetic behaviors. The recurrence controls additive evolution, not the distribution of prime divisors of individual values.

**Status:** does not apply.

---

## 2.2 G-functions

Apéry's generating function is a G-function: it satisfies a differential equation over Q and has controlled coefficient growth.

Relevant results:

- André's theory of G-functions;
- Bombieri-Dwork conjectural framework;
- Chudnovsky-André arithmetic properties.

These give:

- differential equations;
- global nilpotence phenomena;
- denominator and height control.

They do not provide estimates for
\[
\#\{p\sim n:p\mid a_n\}.
\]

A G-function may have coefficients with highly non-random prime support.

**Status:** does not apply.

---

## 2.3 Diagonal sequences

Apéry numbers admit diagonal/hypergeometric interpretations. Diagonal sequences of rational functions are G-functions.

Theorems of Furstenberg, Lipshitz, Christol, Denef-Lipshitz imply holonomicity and arithmetic properties of diagonals.

They do not imply large-prime-factor sparsity.

**Status:** does not apply.

---

## 2.4 Results on prime divisors of recurrence sequences

Classical results for nondegenerate linear recurrences (e.g. S-unit methods, Zsigmondy-type theorems, Corvaja-Zannier theory) concern sequences such as
\[
u_n=\alpha^n-\beta^n.
\]

They exploit multiplicative group structure.

Apéry numbers satisfy a polynomial-coefficient recurrence, not a constant-coefficient recurrence. There is no known reduction to an S-unit equation controlling prime divisors.

**Status:** does not apply.

---

# 3. Testing the hypotheses against Apéry numbers

The Apéry sequence
\[
b_n=\sum_k\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
\]
satisfies a third-order polynomial recurrence.

It has:

- integral coefficients;
- exponential growth \(\log b_n\sim c n\);
- G-function generating series;
- diagonal representation;
- Picard-Fuchs origin.

Missing hypotheses:

- no known multiplicative independence statement for prime divisors;
- no primitive divisor theorem;
- no sieve-level equidistribution of valuations;
- no random model theorem for reductions modulo varying primes.

Therefore none of the existing prime-divisor theorems can be invoked.

---

# 4. What weakest extra property would suffice?

A sufficient additional arithmetic statement would be a local non-divisibility estimate:

For every fixed q,
\[
\sum_{p\in(n/(q+1),n/q]}1_{p\mid b_n}=o_q(n/\log n).
\]

This is exactly the desired statement, so it is not useful as a hypothesis.

A more structural sufficient property would be:

> The reductions of the Apéry Galois representation modulo p have uniformly bounded fixed-vector probability for p in proportional intervals.

Concretely, if one could prove
\[
\Pr(b_n\equiv0\pmod p)=O(p^{-1-\delta})
\]
uniformly for p in a proportional interval, then summing over \(\asymp n/\log n\) primes gives
\[
P_q(n)=O(n^{-\delta}/\log n)=o(n/\log n).
\]

This would be an arithmetic monodromy/equidistribution theorem, not a recurrence theorem.

# 5. Relation to Ramanujan Challenge 3.2

The fixed-quotient reduction is therefore valid:

\[
\log G_n=o(n)
\]
reduces to proving every fixed slice satisfies
\[
\log R_q(n)=o_q(n).
\]

But the missing step is precisely a prime-factor sparsity theorem for Apéry values. Holonomicity, G-function structure, and recurrence growth do not provide it.

The correct research target is not a stronger recurrence manipulation. It is an arithmetic theorem controlling the density of primes p for which
\[
b_n\equiv0\pmod p
\]
when \(p\asymp n\).

**Final verdict: no currently available general theorem applies; the weakest meaningful missing ingredient is a uniform mod-p equidistribution/large-sieve statement for the Apéry motive, not another consequence of P-recursiveness.**
