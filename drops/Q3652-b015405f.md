# Verdict

**No tail-only characteristic-zero carrier survives inside the linear/rational two-jet module.** There is an exact common-prefix normalization, and it is useful algebraically: after anchoring at `s`, the first and second Frobenius jets split into explicit Green tails supported on `[s,r]` plus a finite-dimensional homogeneous prefix state. But the prefix state does not disappear for free.

The exact structure is:

1. before any normalization, the regular two-jet transfer from `s` to `r` has a rank-four invertible linear part;
2. after quotienting the obvious homogeneous `b`-gauges by discrete Wronskians and anchoring at `s`, the remaining nuisance module has rank **two**;
3. at the two later selected points `u,w`, those two nuisance coordinates enter through a `2 x 2` coefficient matrix
   \[
   M_{u,w}=\begin{pmatrix}C_u&D_u\\ C_w&D_w\end{pmatrix},
   \qquad
   \Delta_{u,w}:=C_uD_w-D_uC_w;
   \]
4. in the normalization stated in the question, this determinant is exactly
   \[
   \boxed{\Delta_{u,w}=\widehat E/q=D_wC_u-D_uC_w}
   \]
   up to the harmless sign convention for ordering the columns;
5. eliminating both common-prefix coordinates over characteristic zero requires inverting `M`, hence dividing by `Delta_{u,w}`. But `Delta_{u,w}` is precisely the characteristic-zero scalar whose selected specialization carries the extra factor `q`. **The normalization that removes the prefix therefore divides away the selected `q`-factor.**
6. if one refuses to divide by `Delta_{u,w}`, the adjugate identities retain `Delta_{u,w}` multiplied by a global prefix/projective coordinate. # 8. Exact scoped no-go: eliminating both prefix coordinates consumes the selected determinant

Let `K` be the characteristic-zero coefficient field generated by the fixed Apéry/Green data on `[s,w]`, after inverting only regular transfer denominators. Treat `C_u,D_u,C_w,D_w,T_u,T_w` as elements of `K` and assume

\[
\Delta_{u,w}\ne0
\quad\text{in characteristic zero}.
\]

Consider

\[
\mathcal R
=K[\alpha,\beta,R_u,R_w]
/
(R_u-C_u\alpha-D_u\beta,
 R_w-C_w\alpha-D_w\beta).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Because `M_{u,w}` is invertible over `K`,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R\simeq K[\alpha,\beta].}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Equivalently, projection to `(R_u,R_w)` is an isomorphism after localizing at `Delta_{u,w}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(R_u-C_u\alpha-D_u\beta,
 R_w-C_w\alpha-D_w\beta)
\cap K[R_u,R_w]=(0).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

There is **no nonzero polynomial tail relation** left after eliminating the two common-prefix coordinates. For rational functions the statement is even more direct: the action

\[
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto
(R_u,R_w)
\]

is transitive on the two endpoint coordinates whenever `Delta_{u,w}` is nonzero, so every rational invariant of `(R_u,R_w)` under the full prefix action is constant.

To recover the prefix explicitly one must invert the matrix:

\[
\boxed{
\alpha
=\frac{D_wR_u-D_uR_w}{\Delta_{u,w}},
\qquad
\beta
=\frac{-C_wR_u+C_uR_w}{\Delta_{u,w}}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Now specialize to the selected prime `q`. By the input from the three-zero eliminant, the characteristic-zero determinant `Delta_{u,w}=Ehat/q` is the carrier with the desired extra `q`-divisibility. Thus

\[
q\mid\Delta_{u,w}
\]

on the exceptional/selected locus under discussion.

But (8.4) is exactly the forbidden operation: it inverts the element carrying `q`. In the local ring `Z_(q)`, `Delta_{u,w}` is not a unit. Therefore:

- **invert `Delta`:** the common prefix disappears, but the selected `q` factor is divided away;
- **do not invert `Delta`:** (7.6)--(7.7) retain `Delta alpha` or `Delta beta`, hence a global prefix/projective coordinate.

This proves the requested scoped module-rank no-go.

A useful way to state it is:

> **Proposition (common-prefix two-jet no-go).** On a regular interval `0<s<u<w<q`, within the category generated by the Apéry two-jet state by linear operations and rational operations with `q`-unit denominators, the anchor-normalized two-endpoint module has rank two. If its coefficient determinant `Delta` is the selected characteristic-zero `q`-carrier, then no nonconstant tail-only rational function can eliminate both prefix coordinates while remaining `q`-integral. Any such elimination either localizes at `Delta` and destroys the selected `q`-factor, or retains at least one global projective prefix coordinate.

This is stronger than a height objection: it says the desired scalar does not exist in the stated module before one asks how small its height is.

# 9. What happens on the rank-drop chart `Delta=0`

One might hope that the selected congruence itself, where

\[
\Delta_{u,w}\equiv0\pmod q,
\]

creates the missing tail invariant. It creates a **modular** rank drop, but not a new characteristic-zero tail scalar.

Modulo `q`, the two coefficient rows are dependent. A left null vector gives one congruence among the actual endpoint residuals. Lifting that congruence back to characteristic zero gives precisely (7.6) or (7.7): the lift is `Delta` times a prefix coordinate.

Thus the modular relation has two possible characteristic-zero lifts:

1. keep `Delta alpha` or `Delta beta`; then it is a valid `q`-divisible integer/rational carrier but retains the global prefix state and its absolute-height cost;
2. divide by `Delta`; then it is prefix-free but no longer carries the selected `q`.

There is no third option inside the two-row linear/rational module.

If `Delta` vanishes **identically in characteristic zero**, then the two tail tracks are already globally dependent. In that singular reuse locus only one nuisance direction is observable, so one invariant can survive. But this is not the generic three-zero carrier: it is an additional characteristic-zero rank-drop relation, i.e. exactly the kind of new theorem that would have to be proved independently. It cannot be inferred from the selected congruence `q|Delta`.

# 10. Consequence for the proposed height saving

The Green expressions (4.5)--(4.7) genuinely remove all recurrence history before `s`; after clearing regular denominators their complexity is controlled by the gap length and the local indices. So **as formal tails**, they have the kind of `[s,w]` support sought in Q3652.

What fails is target preservation.

The selected `q`-divisibility in the natural characteristic-zero lift is the determinant of the two remaining prefix coefficient tracks. Resetting the prefix replaces the actual two-jet by a different solution and removes precisely the rank-drop event that generated the factor `q`. Restoring the actual solution reintroduces `(alpha,beta)`, equivalently the projective data in (5.5)--(5.7).

Therefore one cannot promote the formal tail height saving to a new carrier bound of the form

\[
\log|F_{s,u,w}|
=O((w-s)\log q)
\]

with

\[
q\mid F_{s,u,w}
e0
\]

by common-prefix Wronskian/Green normalization alone. The known natural carrier remains at the stated

\[
O\bigl(w+(w-s)\log q\bigr)
\]

scale unless some *additional* arithmetic identity removes the surviving projective coordinate without dividing by `Delta`.

# 11. Smallest possible escape from the no-go

Within this exact algebra, a positive result needs genuinely new rank, not a different presentation of the same two rows. The minimum possibilities are:

1. **a third independent endpoint equation after anchoring at `s`** with no new private jet coordinate; then three rows can eliminate the two prefix coordinates and leave a nontrivial minor relation;
2. **an independent arithmetic equation fixing one of `alpha,beta` or `xi_s`** by a tail-supported quantity, again without introducing a new private quotient digit;
3. **a nonlinear identity outside the linear/rational two-jet transfer module** whose characteristic-zero lift retains the selected `q`-factor after prefix saturation.

Merely adding another Green summation, another Wronskian of the same two-jet, or another rational basis change does not help: those operations stay inside the rank-two quotient module (6.7)--(8.3).

The common-prefix normalization exists explicitly, but it proves a **no-go**, not a height improvement.

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{actual two-jet}

\[
D_wR_u-D_uR_w=(\widehat E/q)\alpha,

\]

Hence **cancel all prefix coordinates** `iff` one divides by the determinant on the generic characteristic-zero chart; doing so cancels the selected `q`-carrier. Refusing that division leaves `alpha` or `beta`, and at the selected anchor these contain the explicit global projective coordinate

So, within linear/rational functions of the Frobenius two-jet state, there is **no** nonzero characteristic-zero `q`-divisibility carrier supported only on `[s,w]` obtained by common-prefix normalization of `Ehat/q`. A new independent relation or a genuinely nonlinear invariant is required.
