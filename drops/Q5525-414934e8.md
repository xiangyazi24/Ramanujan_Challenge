# Hostile audit: reflection, K3/Frobenius structure, and Apéry zero sets

**Literature and repository state checked through 29 July 2026.** I use

\[
B_r=\sum_{k=0}^r\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:B_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

The conclusion is negative and fairly sharp.

> **Verdict.** Reflection is an exact two-to-one bookkeeping symmetry, not a zero-count mechanism. The canonical Apéry K3 Hasse invariant is the polynomial in the *geometric parameter*
> \[
> \mathcal A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}B_rt^r,
> \]
> whereas membership in \(Z_p\) is the vanishing of one *coefficient* of \(\mathcal A_p\), equivalently one moving multiplicative Fourier/Mellin coefficient. The degree-\(p-1\) polynomial in \(X=r(r+1)\) that interpolates these coefficients is a different, spectral object. None of the available K3 ordinarity, Hasse-invariant, monodromy, Sato--Tate, or Chebotarev theorems gives an absolute, polylogarithmic, or even new power-saving bound for \(|Z_p|\), and none gives the required fixed-\(n\) cross-prime estimate.
>
> The only unconditional power saving presently available in the project is the recurrence/continuant bound \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\). It is not an algebraic-geometric consequence. Even the hypothetical optimal bound \(|Z_p|\le2\), together with reflection, would not imply a pointwise bound for the number of primes \(p\) with \(B_{n-p}\equiv0\pmod p\). A separate cross-prime nonalignment theorem is logically necessary.

A compact status table is:

| Proposed input | What is actually a theorem | What it does **not** imply |
|---|---|---|
| Reflection \(r\mapsto p-1-r\) | \(B_{p-1-r}\equiv B_r\pmod p\) | Any upper bound beyond pairing/parity; any correlation between distinct primes |
| Apéry K3 Hasse invariant | \(\mathcal A_p(t)=\sum_{r<p}B_rt^r\) is the first Hasse--Witt scalar, up to the usual trivialization | A bound on the number of zero **coefficients** \(B_r\) |
| Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin square factorization | \(\mathcal A_p(t)\) is a square, or the discriminant times a square, according to \(p\bmod24\) | A bound on the zero coefficients of \(\mathcal A_p\), or on roots of the spectral interpolant |
| Fixed K3 ordinary-reduction theorems | Density-one ordinary reduction for each fixed K3 surface after finite extension | Uniformity in a moving fiber; short intervals; moving coefficient/Mellin conditions |
| Big monodromy / finite-field Mellin Sato--Tate | Equidistribution of normalized Frobenius classes for fixed sheaf data and character averages/extension fields | Exact divisibility by the residue characteristic; an all-\(p\) bound for exceptional characters |
| Global Sato--Tate for the central eta-product | Continuous equidistribution of \(a_p/(2p^{3/2})\) | Density zero for \(p\mid a_p\); control of any off-center root |
| Chebotarev | Counts fixed conjugacy conditions in a fixed finite extension or fixed residual quotient | A condition modulo the same prime being counted, for a character/motive whose order changes with \(p\) |

## 1. Exact reflection and the degree-\(p-1\) spectral interpolant

To avoid confusing two different Hasse-type polynomials, I call the polynomial in the spectral variable \(X\) by the name \(I_p(X)\), reserving \(\mathcal A_p(t)\) for the genuine K3 Hasse polynomial in the geometric parameter.

For an indeterminate \(\nu\), put \(X=\nu(\nu+1)\). The elementary identity

\[
(-\nu)_k(\nu+1)_k
=(-1)^k\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\bigl(X-j(j+1)\bigr)
\]

gives

\[
B_r={}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}-r,-r,r+1,r+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1
\right)
=\sum_{k=0}^r
\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(r(r+1)-j(j+1))^2}{(k!)^4}.
\]

Let \(p\) be odd, \(M=(p-1)/2\), and define

\[
I_p(X)=
\sum_{k=0}^{M}
\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(X-j(j+1))^2}{(k!)^4}
\in\mathbf F_p[X].
\tag{1.1}
\]

### Proposition 1.1

For every \(r\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\),

\[
B_r\equiv I_p(r(r+1))\pmod p.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\deg I_p=p-1
\tag{1.3}
\]

and

\[
B_{p-1-r}\equiv B_r\pmod p.
\tag{1.4}
\]

### Proof

If \(0\le r\le M\), every term of (1.1) with \(k>r\) contains the factor with \(j=r\), hence vanishes exactly at \(X=r(r+1)\). The surviving terms are precisely the defining sum for \(B_r\).

For general \(r\), set \(s=p-1-r\) when \(r>M\). Then

\[
r(r+1)\equiv s(s+1)\pmod p.
\]

In the hypergeometric expression for \(B_r\), all terms with \(k>s\) contain the factor \(X-s(s+1)\) and vanish modulo \(p\); the remaining terms agree with the defining terms for \(B_s\). This proves both (1.2) and (1.4).

The top summand in (1.1), \(k=M\), has degree \(2M=p-1\) and nonzero leading coefficient \((M!)^{-4}\in\mathbf F_p^\times\); all earlier summands have lower degree. Hence (1.3). ∎

Let

\[
\mathcal Q_p=\{r(r+1):r\in\mathbf F_p\}.
\]

The map \(r\mapsto r(r+1)\) is two-to-one except at the fixed point \(r=M\), because

\[
4r(r+1)+1=(2r+1)^2.
\]

If

\[
N_p=\#\{x\in\mathcal Q_p:I_p(x)=0\},
\qquad
\varepsilon_p=\mathbf 1_{B_M\equiv0\ (p)},
\]

then exactly

\[
|Z_p|=2N_p-\varepsilon_p.
\tag{1.5}
\]

This is the complete numerical content of reflection. The degree bound in (1.3) is of order \(p\), so it supplies no sublinear bound. Reflection supplies no more than paired roots plus the possible central fixed root.

### Reflection alone cannot imply any fixed-\(n\) bound

This is not merely a lack of technique; there is a formal logical obstruction. Fix \(n\), and choose any set of primes \(\mathcal P\subset(n/2,n)\). For each \(p\in\mathcal P\), define the abstract set

\[
\Omega_p=\{n-p,\;2p-n-1\}\subset\{0,\ldots,p-1\}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

It is reflection invariant, has size at most two, and contains \(n-p\) for every selected prime. Thus the hypotheses

```text
reflection symmetry + a uniform bound |Z_p| <= 2
```

are logically compatible with **every** prime in \((n/2,n)\) hitting the same fixed integer \(n\). This abstract construction is not asserted to be the Apéry zero set; it proves that any successful pointwise proof must use additional cross-prime arithmetic, not merely per-prime geometry and reflection.

The reflected residue also makes the mismatch explicit. If \(r=n-p\), its reflection is

\[
r'=p-1-r=2p-n-1.
\]

It corresponds to the different outer integer

\[
n'=p+r'=3p-n-1.
\]

One has \(n'=n\) only at the central point

\[
p=\frac{2n+1}{3}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

So reflection does not produce a second condition at the same \(n\), except for the unique central candidate.

## 2. The three objects that must not be conflated

The algebraic-geometric discussion contains three genuinely different objects.

### 2.1 The genuine K3 Hasse polynomial in the geometric parameter

A standard constant-term model for the Apéry numbers is

\[
\Phi(x,y,z)=
\frac{(x+y)(z+1)(x+y+z)(y+z+1)}{xyz},
\qquad
B_m=\operatorname{CT}(\Phi^m).
\]

The toric pencil

\[
\mathcal X_t:\quad 1-t\Phi=0
\]

compactifies and resolves to the Beukers--Peters Apéry K3 variation. At a good prime, in the standard local trivialization of the Hodge line, the first Hasse--Witt scalar is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{CT}\bigl((1-t\Phi)^{p-1}\bigr)
&=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{m}(-t)^mB_m\\
&\equiv\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}B_mt^m\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus define

\[
\mathcal A_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}B_mt^m.
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is the actual K3 Hasse polynomial/section.

At a smooth fiber \(t=t_0\), the statement supplied by Stienstra--Beukers and by the general Hasse--Witt/period formalism is

\[
\mathcal A_p(t_0)\ne0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal X_{t_0}\text{ is ordinary (formal Brauer height }1).
\tag{2.2}
\]

If \(\mathcal A_p(t_0)=0\), the fiber is nonordinary: its formal Brauer height is at least two, possibly infinite. The first Hasse invariant alone does **not** say that the K3 surface is Artin-supersingular.

The key distinction is

\[
B_r=[t^r]\mathcal A_p(t),
\qquad
\mathcal A_p(t_0)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}B_mt_0^m.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
B_r=0
\quad\text{is not the condition}\quad
\mathcal A_p(r(r+1))=0.
\]

The former is a zero coefficient; the latter is a nonordinary geometric fiber. Substituting \(t=n(n+1)\) into the canonical K3 pencil produces a genuine K3 surface, but its Hasse invariant is \(\mathcal A_p(n(n+1))\), not \(I_p(n(n+1))\) and not \(B_{n-p}\).

There is an exact multiplicative Fourier formulation. For \(1\le r\le p-2\),

\[
B_r=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\mathcal A_p(t)t^{-r}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus the event \(r\in Z_p\) is cancellation in one Mellin coefficient of the entire Hasse section. Pointwise ordinarity of fibers does not control the zero set of its Fourier transform.

### 2.2 The Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin factorization

Their 2026 Theorem 1.2 proves that there is \(Q_p(t)\in\mathbf F_p[t]\) such that

\[
\mathcal A_p(t)=Q_p(t)^2
\quad\text{if }p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},
\tag{2.5}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal A_p(t)=(t^2-34t+1)Q_p(t)^2
\quad\text{if }p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

This is a strong and relevant structural theorem about the reduction of the **generating series** and its function-field Galois group. It is not a theorem about the degree-\(p-1\) polynomial \(I_p(X)\), and it is not a theorem about zero coefficients of \(\mathcal A_p(t)\).

A square polynomial may have arbitrarily many zero coefficients, and knowing that its geometric roots have even multiplicity imposes no useful anti-concentration on its coefficient vector. Equations (2.5)--(2.6) therefore do not bound \(|Z_p|\).

The factor \(t^2-34t+1\) is also the finite discriminant/singular locus of the Apéry Picard--Fuchs equation. It must be removed before interpreting roots as smooth nonordinary fibers. It is not an exceptional pair of coefficient indices.

### 2.3 The spectral interpolant \(I_p(X)\)

The polynomial \(I_p(X)\) in (1.1) is an exact terminating hypergeometric interpolation on the quadratic Casimir lattice. The variable \(X\) changes the upper parameters \(-\nu,-\nu,\nu+1,\nu+1\), while the ordinary hypergeometric differential variable has already been specialized to \(1\). Hence \(X\) is naturally a spectral/contiguous variable, not automatically a moduli coordinate of a fixed algebraic family.

No theorem follows from the notation \({}_4F_3\) that constructs a fixed finite-type family over a fixed \(X\)-line, with fixed-rank crystalline cohomology, whose first Hasse invariant is \(I_p(X)\) for every \(p\). Such a construction would require, at minimum:

1. a fixed integral model over \(\mathbf Z[1/N]\);
2. a fixed-rank cohomology group or overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal;
3. an algebraic \(X\)-parameter of bounded degree/height complexity;
4. a Cartier/Frobenius matrix whose specified entry or determinant reduces to \(I_p(X)\);
5. compatibility across primes.

The interpolation identity proves none of these.

There is a legitimate character-side cohomological interpretation: \(B_r\) is a finite Mellin coefficient of the fixed K3 trace function, so it can be realized through a Kummer twist whose character is \(\omega_p^r\). But this is a discrete character family whose character order and coefficient field vary with \(p\). It is not a fixed \(X\)-family over \(\mathbf Q\). Even granting a rank-two weight-three primitive Mellin factor for every generic character, generic ordinarity still does not count the exceptional characters; see Section 3.3.

## 3. Audit of the applicable algebraic-geometric theorems

## 3.1 K3 ordinarity and Hasse-invariant theorems

### What applies

Stienstra--Beukers identify the truncated period with the first formal-Brauer/Hasse--Witt invariant for the Apéry K3 pencil. Huang--Lian--Yau--Yu give a modern general Hasse--Witt/truncated-period framework for toric Calabi--Yau hypersurfaces. Dwork-crystal and higher Hasse--Witt theories then describe Frobenius and the unit root on the ordinary locus.

Their quantifier pattern is, schematically,

\[
\text{fix }p,\quad \text{vary the geometric parameter }t_0,\quad
\mathcal A_p(t_0)\ne0\Longleftrightarrow\text{ordinary fiber}.
\]

They identify the locus and provide Frobenius on its complement. They do not bound the number of zero coefficients of \(\mathcal A_p\), nor the number of zeros of a separate spectral interpolation polynomial.

### Why generic ordinarity gives no small root count

Generic ordinarity says that the Hasse section is not identically zero, or that the ordinary locus is Zariski open and nonempty. On a one-dimensional base, the Hasse section naturally has degree proportional to \(p-1\). A nonzero polynomial of degree proportional to \(p\) can have proportionally many geometric zeros.

The Legendre family is the decisive warning. It has large \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) monodromy and is generically ordinary, while its Deuring Hasse polynomial has degree \((p-1)/2\) and cuts out the supersingular locus. Thus

\[
\text{generic ordinarity + large monodromy}
\not\Longrightarrow
O(1)\text{ or polylogarithmically many nonordinary parameters}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This logical failure occurs in the most classical rank-two family. It cannot be repaired by invoking generic K3 ordinarity abstractly.

### Fixed K3 surfaces: the Bogomolov--Zarhin theorem has the wrong object and quantifiers

Bogomolov and Zarhin prove:

\[
\forall X/K\text{ a fixed K3 surface},\quad
\exists L/K\text{ finite such that }X_L
\text{ is ordinary outside a density-zero set of places of }L.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This theorem is powerful, but it does not match the problem in four independent ways.

1. It concerns one fixed K3 surface, whereas \(B_{n-p}\) is a moving Mellin coefficient/character.
2. It gives natural-density one, not a uniform estimate in a short interval \(p\asymp n\).
3. It has no stated uniformity as the height of a moving fiber grows with \(n\).
4. The canonical fiber \(t=n(n+1)\) has Hasse invariant \(\mathcal A_p(n(n+1))\), not \(B_{n-p}\).

Recent theorems on supersingular reductions or Picard-rank jumps of fixed K3/K3-type motives have the same mismatch and usually address a stronger, different event than first-Hasse vanishing. They do not count the slope jump relevant to \(p\mid B_r\) in the moving Mellin object.

## 3.2 Symmetric-square geometry does not change the zero-count quantifier

The third-order Apéry Picard--Fuchs variation is related to the symmetric square of a rank-two modular/elliptic local system. This explains the orthogonal rank-three K3 monodromy and is compatible with the square pattern (2.5)--(2.6).

Even an integral, Frobenius-compatible theorem identifying the reduced K3 Hasse divisor with the pullback of an elliptic supersingular divisor would still concern roots in the geometric parameter \(t\). It would not convert a coefficient condition \([t^r]\mathcal A_p=0\) into a fiber condition. Furthermore, elliptic supersingular divisors themselves are not uniformly bounded: their geometric degree is linear in \(p\), and their \(\mathbf F_p\)-rational part is governed by class-number phenomena of roughly square-root scale, not by an absolute constant.

Thus a symmetric-square factorization may explain multiplicities and reduce a geometric rank, but it does not supply the required anti-concentration in the spectral/character index.

## 3.3 Monodromy and Katz's finite-field Mellin Sato--Tate theorem

The canonical K3 variation has fixed geometric monodromy in the \(t\)-parameter. After a Mellin transform against a generic Kummer character, one expects—and in the standard cohomological construction obtains after removing boundary pieces—a bounded-rank weight-three object. The repository's numerical and structural work points to a rank-two symplectic factor with \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) geometric monodromy.

For this audit, one may grant the strongest favorable version of that statement. It still does not prove a zero count.

Katz's main finite-field Mellin equidistribution theorem has a precise quantifier pattern. One fixes a finite base field \(k\) and a pure arithmetically semisimple object \(N\). As extensions \(E/k\) grow through prescribed degree classes, the Frobenius conjugacy classes attached to all good multiplicative characters of \(E^\times\) equidistribute in the relevant compact group.

The Apéry horizontal problem instead does this:

```text
for each varying prime p:
    use the field F_p;
    select one p-dependent character chi_{p,n};
    ask whether a p-adic Frobenius/Hasse coordinate is 0 modulo p.
```

This differs from Katz's theorem in all three variables: the base field varies, only one character is selected, and the event is exact residue-characteristic divisibility rather than a continuous class function of a normalized complex conjugacy class.

Even a vertical Sato--Tate theorem as \(r\) ranges through all characters at one large \(p\) would control continuous test functions and fixed moments. The indicator

\[
\mathbf 1_{B_r\equiv0\ (p)}
\]

is a shrinking arithmetic target. In a weight-three rank-two lift with integral trace \(T_{p,r}\), one has schematically

\[
B_r\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
T_{p,r}=p\,m_{p,r},
\qquad |m_{p,r}|\ll\sqrt p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

After normalization by \(p^{3/2}\), the relevant points form a \(p\)-dependent lattice of mesh \(p^{-1/2}\). Weak Sato--Tate equidistribution does not resolve individual lattice points at this scale. A local-limit theorem uniform at residue-characteristic resolution would be a new theorem.

Large geometric monodromy is therefore consistent with the observed mean \(|Z_p|\approx1\), but it proves neither that mean for exact zeros nor any all-prime upper bound.

## 3.4 The central eta-product: a genuine fixed motive, but only one residue

Let

\[
f(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
=\sum_{m\ge1}a_mq^m\in S_4(\Gamma_0(8)).
\]

Ahlgren and Ono prove, for \(p\ge5\),

\[
B_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f)\pmod{p^2},
\tag{3.4}
\]

and Kilbourn strengthens the supercongruence by one further power of \(p\). This is a genuine fixed rank-two weight-three motive.

For fixed \(n\), however, the central residue can occur only when

\[
n-p=\frac{p-1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p=\frac{2n+1}{3}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Hence the central modular-form slice contributes at most one candidate prime to the fixed-\(n\) problem. It is already pointwise harmless without any density theorem.

The global Sato--Tate theorem for non-CM holomorphic newforms does apply to

\[
\frac{a_p(f)}{2p^{3/2}}.
\]

But the nonordinary condition is

\[
p\mid a_p(f)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a_p(f)=p\,m_p,
\qquad |m_p|\le2\sqrt p.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Again this is a growing union of exact lattice targets, not a fixed interval or fixed trace. Sato--Tate does not prove

\[
\#\{p\le x:p\mid a_p(f)\}=o(x/\log x).
\tag{3.7}
\]

I found no theorem proving (3.7) for this non-CM weight-four form. Suh's higher-weight result requires potentially abelian \(\ell\)-adic realizations on a finite-index subgroup; the generic \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) motive here is not of that type. Wang--Zhang's 2026 positive-density ordinary-prime theorem concerns \(\mathrm{GL}_2\)-type abelian varieties and weight-two modular forms, not weight four.

There is also a weight distinction. The local polynomial is

\[
1-a_p(f)T+p^3T^2.
\]

The generic nonordinary case \(v_p(a_p)=1\) has slopes \(\{1,2\}\), while \(a_p=0\) has slopes \(\{3/2,3/2\}\). This is not the weight-two K3 supersingularity condition. Fixed-K3 supersingular-prime theorems therefore cannot be imported to count (3.6).

## 3.5 Chebotarev: exact scope and exact failure

Chebotarev counts primes whose Frobenius lies in a fixed conjugacy subset of a fixed finite Galois group. In compatible-system applications, one may fix an auxiliary prime \(\ell\) and count a fixed condition in a residual representation modulo \(\ell\).

The Apéry condition is diagonal in the residue characteristic:

\[
\text{prime being counted}=p,
\qquad
\text{modulus imposing the condition}=p.
\]

It is therefore not a condition in any one fixed finite quotient. Letting \(\ell=p\) after applying a theorem for fixed \(\ell\) is an invalid quantifier swap.

The moving character is an independent obstruction. For fixed \(n\), put \(r=n-p\). On \(\mathbf F_p^\times\),

\[
\chi_{p,n}=\omega_p^r=\omega_p^{n-1},
\]

but its order is

\[
d_{p,n}=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-1)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

This order is generally comparable with \(p\), so the Kummer twist and its natural cyclotomic coefficient field vary with the prime being counted. These are not reductions of one fixed compatible system over \(\mathbf Q\).

The bounded-order slices can be quantified. Suppose \(0<r<p-1\) and \(\chi_{p,n}\) has order \(d\). Then \(p-1\mid dr\); write

\[
j=\frac{dr}{p-1},
\qquad 1\le j\le d-1.
\]

Since \(r=n-p\),

\[
d(n-p)=j(p-1),
\]

so

\[
p=\frac{dn+j}{d+j}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For each pair \((d,j)\), there is at most one candidate \(p\). Therefore, uniformly in \(n\),

\[
\#\{p:n/2<p<n,\ d_{p,n}\le D\}=O(D^2).
\tag{3.10}
\]

Every fixed finite collection of finite-order hypergeometric/Kummer motives is thus automatically negligible. The hard primes are precisely those with unbounded character order, where there is no fixed Chebotarev extension.

A second tempting Chebotarev use is to fix a recurrence gap \(h\), form a characteristic-zero continuant polynomial, and use its splitting field to study roots modulo \(p\). For each fixed \(h\), this is legitimate and gives an average-over-primes theorem. It cannot yield an all-\(p\) bound when the necessary gap \(h\) grows with \(p\). Indeed, for every fixed finite range of gaps, infinitely many primes split completely in the compositum of the corresponding splitting fields. Fixed-gap Chebotarev has the opposite quantifier from a uniform zero-fiber theorem.

Finally, the function-field Galois group computed by Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin is the Galois group of the mod-\(p\) generating series over \(\mathbf F_p(t)\). It is not a number-field extension in which rational primes encode roots of \(I_p\), and it does not create a horizontal Chebotarev problem.

## 4. The fixed-\(n\) problem is a horizontal prime-divisor problem

For \(n/2<p\le n\), write \(n=p+r\), so \(0\le r<p\). Gessel's \(p\)-Lucas congruence for the Apéry numbers gives

\[
B_n=B_{p+r}\equiv B_1B_r=5B_r\pmod p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus, apart from the single prime \(p=5\),

\[
p\mid B_{n-p}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid B_n.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently, since \((n-p)(n-p+1)\equiv n(n+1)\pmod p\),

\[
p\mid B_{n-p}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
I_p\bigl(n(n+1)\bigr)=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equation (4.3) can look like a fixed-fiber condition because \(n(n+1)\) is one fixed integer. It is not: the polynomial \(I_p\) changes with \(p\), and no fixed \(X\)-family realizing all \(I_p\) is known. Equation (4.2) shows the elementary arithmetic content more honestly: the target primes are the top-half prime divisors of the single exponentially large integer \(B_n\).

The asymptotic

\[
\log B_n=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n)
\]

gives only

\[
\#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid B_{n-p}\}
\ll\frac{n}{\log n}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The desired pointwise result needs a little-oh improvement, equivalently

\[
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid B_{n-p}}}\log p=o(n).
\tag{4.5}
\]

No fixed-fiber K3 theorem controls the large-prime radical of one Taylor coefficient \(B_n\). Encoding \(B_n\) tautologically into the rank, conductor, discriminant, or a splitting field merely moves the original exponential height into the auxiliary object and gives no gain.

### A vertical theorem would still not prove (4.5)

Suppose, far more strongly than known, that \(|Z_p|\le C\) for every prime. This controls the average number of incidences when \(n\) varies, because each residue \(r\in Z_p\) selects one value \(n=p+r\). It does not control the maximum over \(n\). The reflection-invariant construction (1.6) shows that all the bounded sets can align on one integer.

Therefore the fixed-\(n\) problem has two logically separate layers:

1. **vertical anti-concentration:** bound \(|Z_p|\) for each prime;
2. **horizontal nonalignment:** prevent the chosen roots \(n-p\in Z_p\) from aligning for many different primes at one \(n\).

Monodromy and Sato--Tate at one prime address, at best, the first layer in an average sense. They do not supply the second.

## 5. What real bounds were found

The literature search and the repository audit give the following answer.

### 5.1 Absolute \(O(1)\) bound

No theorem found. Neither generic ordinarity, square factorization, K3 monodromy, finite-field Mellin equidistribution, nor Gross--Koblitz/Dwork unit-root formulas imply it.

### 5.2 Polylogarithmic bound

No theorem found. A Strassmann argument would require a \(p\)-independent bound on the Weierstrass degree of a global analytic interpolation in the character variable. The known interpolation has degree \(p-1\), and the Gross--Koblitz leading coefficient is a genuine sum of many unit terms whose cancellation is exactly the problem.

### 5.3 A power saving

There is one unconditional, mathematically valid power saving in the project:

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

It comes from the Apéry recurrence, transfer matrices, short-gap continuants, and polynomial degree counting. It is not a consequence of K3/Frobenius theory. No algebraic-geometric theorem found improves (5.1) for this coefficient-zero set.

A hypothetical bounded-conductor additive realization would give a square-root bound, but that realization is precisely missing. Indeed, by additive-character orthogonality,

\[
|Z_p|=p^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}\psi(aB_r).
\tag{5.2}
\]

If one could prove, uniformly for every \(a\ne0\),

\[
\left|\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}\psi(aB_r)\right|\le C\sqrt p
\tag{5.3}
\]

from a fixed-rank sheaf of conductor \(O(1)\), then (5.2) would give

\[
|Z_p|=1+O(\sqrt p).
\tag{5.4}
\]

The implication (5.3) \(\Rightarrow\) (5.4) is valid. What is not available is (5.3). The naive Artin--Schreier sheaf attached to the polynomial \(I_p(r(r+1))\) has conductor growing on the order of its degree, hence on the order of \(p\), and Deligne's estimate becomes useless. Constructing a bounded-complexity compression of this function would itself be a major new theorem.

### 5.4 Horizontal cancellation for fixed \(n\)

No theorem found. Standard Chebotarev, Sato--Tate, trace-function, and large-sieve results do not handle one unbounded-order character over each varying field \(\mathbf F_p\), with divisibility tested modulo that same \(p\), uniformly in the external integer \(n\).

The central fixed form does not help: it accounts for at most the one prime in (3.5), and even its own density-zero nonordinary-prime assertion is open.

---

## 6. The exact new theorems that would be required

There is no presently valid algebraic-geometric route that can be completed using only known theorems. The missing inputs can nevertheless be stated precisely.

## 6.1 A vertical residue-characteristic local-limit theorem

An \(O(1)\) or polylogarithmic result would require an Apéry-specific theorem of the form

\[
\boxed{
\#\{r\in\mathbf F_p:I_p(r(r+1))=0\}
\ll (\log p)^A
}
\tag{V}
\]

for all primes \(p\), or at minimum a new exponent \(\theta<2/3\) in place of the polylogarithm.

Calling \(I_p\) a Hasse polynomial does not prove (V). A geometric proof would need one of the following genuinely new structures, together with a zero-count theorem:

- a fixed finite-type parameter space and fixed-rank Frobenius crystal whose first Hasse section restricts to \(I_p\) through a map of uniformly bounded degree;
- a bounded-conductor two-variable trace-function model for \((a,r)\mapsto\psi(aB_r)\), yielding at least (5.3);
- a fixed arithmetic tower in which roots of \(I_p\) are Frobenius-fixed points and whose genus/conductor grows subpolynomially;
- or an explicit second Frobenius minor/difference-equation invariant whose common-zero locus with \(I_p\) has uniformly bounded complexity.

No such theorem is currently available. Generic ordinarity is strictly weaker than all of them.

## 6.2 A separate horizontal nonalignment theorem

Even (V) with \(A=0\) would not prove the fixed-\(n\) result. One needs a theorem correlating different residue characteristics. A sufficient quantitative statement is: for some fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly in \(n\),

\[
\boxed{
\#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ B_{n-p}\equiv0\pmod p\}
\ll \frac{n}{(\log n)^{1+\delta}}.
}
\tag{H1}
\]

The exact weakest target is the weighted form

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\B_{n-p}\equiv0\ (p)}}\log p=o(n)
}
\tag{H2}
\]

uniformly for every \(n\).

An algebraic-geometric version would be a **horizontal local-limit/Chebotarev theorem for unbounded-order Kummer twists in moving residue characteristic**, controlling

\[
I_p(n(n+1))=0
\]

as \(p\) varies, uniformly in the high-height parameter \(n\). Such a theorem would have to allow simultaneously:

1. the field \(\mathbf F_p\) to vary;
2. the Kummer-character order \(d_{p,n}\) in (3.8) to grow with \(p\);
3. the residual modulus to equal the prime being counted;
4. one selected character per prime rather than an average over all characters;
5. uniformity in \(n\asymp p\).

No current Chebotarev or Sato--Tate theorem has these quantifiers.

One can also formulate a centered dyadic version. For a dyadic block \(P<p\le2P\), restricted to the admissible primes \(n/2<p\le n\), a theorem of the shape

\[
\sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\n/2<p\le n}}
\left(
\mathbf 1_{\,n-p\in Z_p}-\frac{|Z_p|}{p}
\right)
\ll \frac{P}{(\log P)^{1+\delta}}
\tag{H3}
\]

uniformly in the corresponding \(n\)-range would provide the missing cross-prime cancellation. This is much stronger than vertical Sato--Tate and is not implied by independence of fixed compatible systems.

## 7. Final hostile verdict

The following statements are defensible.

```text
PROVED / APPLICABLE
-------------------
1. B_{p-1-r} = B_r mod p.
2. Z_p is the zero set of the degree-(p-1) spectral interpolant I_p
   on the quadratic image r(r+1).
3. The geometric Apéry K3 Hasse scalar is A_p(t)=sum_{r<p} B_r t^r.
4. A_p(t) is a square or discriminant times a square, according to p mod 24.
5. The central value is governed by the fixed weight-4 eta-product.
6. When its hypotheses and monodromy are verified, fixed-sheaf
   Mellin Sato-Tate controls normalized Frobenius distributions.
7. The project has the recurrence-theoretic bound |Z_p| << p^(2/3).

NOT PROVED / INVALID INFERENCE
------------------------------
1. Reflection forces |Z_p|=O(1) or polylog(p).
2. K3 generic ordinarity bounds zero coefficients of its Hasse polynomial.
3. The spectral variable X is a fixed K3 moduli coordinate.
4. Large monodromy or Sato-Tate controls exact mod-p vanishing.
5. The central modular form represents generic off-center roots.
6. Fixed-extension Chebotarev applies when both the character and modulus move with p.
7. Any per-prime bound on |Z_p| alone yields a pointwise fixed-n bound.
```

The algebraic geometry is real and valuable: it explains the period, the Hasse section, the symmetric-square phenomenon, the central modular slice, and the bounded-rank Mellin objects. What it does **not** presently supply is the required anti-cancellation theorem for the spectral Hasse values, nor the cross-characteristic nonalignment theorem for one fixed \(n\).

Accordingly, this route should not be cited as a proof strategy with a missing routine lemma. It is blocked at two exact new theorems, (V) and (H2)/(H3). The first is a residue-characteristic local-limit theorem for the Apéry Mellin/Hasse family; the second is a horizontal local-limit theorem for unbounded-order twists. Neither is contained in existing K3 ordinarity, monodromy, Sato--Tate, or Chebotarev theory.
