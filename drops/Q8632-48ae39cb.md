# Q8632 audit: repeated folded anchors and all-distinct fibres

## 1. Correction: no polynomial resultant in the index

The Apéry sequence $B_u$ is a sequence in the integer index $u$, not a polynomial variable. Therefore expressions such as
\[
\operatorname{Res}_U(B_U,F(U))
\]
are not defined in the algebraic sense used for polynomial resultants. Any elimination argument must use actual finite integer identities, recurrence relations, or height bounds.

The relevant structure is instead the folded-anchor map inside one fixed full-defect fibre.

## 2. Repeated folded anchors in one full-defect triple

Fix signs and nondegenerate defects
\[
d_2d_3(d_2-d_3)\neq0.
\]
The folded indices are
\[
u_i(t)=s_i(t+d_i+\tau_i),
\]
with folding modulo the corresponding shell prime. Equality of folded anchors between two positions requires an affine relation between the corresponding signed translations.

For two positions with the same sign the equality is impossible except for the already excluded degenerate defect coincidences. For opposite signs one obtains
\[
t+d_i+\tau_i\equiv -(t+d_j+\tau_j)-1\pmod{p},
\]
so after eliminating the bounded translations there are at most two explicit possible values of $t$ for which a repeated folded anchor can occur.

Thus repeated-anchor configurations are not a large fibre phenomenon. They are concentrated on finitely many translations.

## 3. Exact fixed-index S-unit/divisor problem

At such a translation the two repeated-anchor primes satisfy
\[
p\mid B_u,
\qquad q\mid B_u,
\]
for one fixed folded index $u<X$.

The two products have the form
\[
\alpha p=n_i,
\qquad \beta q=n_j
\]
with bounded coefficients
\[
1\leq \alpha,eta<X.
\]
The anchor difference gives the exact linear relation
\[
\beta q-\alpha p=2u+1.
\]

Therefore the repeated sector is bounded by the number
\[
J_{\rm rep}(u)=\#\{(p,q,\alpha,\beta):p,q\in(X,2X],\ p,q\mid B_u,\ 1\leq\alpha,eta<X,\ \beta q-\alpha p=2u+1\}.
\]
This is the smallest natural fixed-index divisor count. The third prime contributes independently: the remaining shifted product is the unique shell divisor of the corresponding integer $m-z_k$, and its folded index $v$ must satisfy
\[
p_3\mid B_v.
\]

A standard divisor bound gives only
\[
\omega(B_u)\ll \frac{\log B_u}{\log X},
\]
which is far too large because $B_u$ has exponential size in $u$. Even a bound of the form
\[
J_{\rm rep}(u)\leq X^{o(1)}
\]
would not imply
\[
\sum_{u<X}J_{\rm rep}(u)=o(X/\log^3 X).
\]
One would need an additional saving, for example
\[
\sum_{u<X}J_{\rm rep}(u)\ll X^{1-\delta}
\]
or a logarithmically weighted analogue. No currently stated Apéry congruence, recurrence, or Casoratian identity supplies such a fixed-index S-unit estimate.

Hence the repeated sector is reduced to an explicit S-unit problem but not closed by the frozen inputs.

## 4. Two all-distinct fibres and cross determinants

Suppose two fibre solutions exist:
\[
(t,N),\qquad (t',N').
\]
For each defect position define
\[
D_i=(N'-d_i)B_{u_i(t)}-(N-d_i)B_{u_i(t')}.
\]
Because both representations use shell primes $p_i,p_i'$, the determinant is divisible by
\[
p_ip_i'.
\]

The useful observation is that $D_i$ is a genuine integer determinant, unlike a formal resultant. However its usefulness depends on controlling its height and gcd structure.

## 5. Why the Casoratian does not immediately close the problem

The Apéry recurrence gives a companion sequence and an exact Casoratian, schematically
\[
A_vB_u-A_uB_v=\text{controlled rational factor}.
\]
This controls two independent solutions of the recurrence.

The determinants above instead involve
\[
(N'-d_i)B_{u_i(t)}-(N-d_i)B_{u_i(t')},
\]
where the coefficients $N-d_i$ are not recurrence companions. They are CRT/product parameters depending on the fibre.

To use the Casoratian one would need an identity expressing these coefficients as linear combinations of the canonical Apéry companion values at the same indices. No such identity follows from the recurrence.

Equivalently, the obstruction is:

1. the indices $u_i(t)$ and $u_i(t')$ vary with $t$;
2. the multipliers $N-d_i$ are external CRT variables;
3. the recurrence controls index evolution but not arbitrary linear forms in two moving indices.

Therefore no second independent determinant of polynomial height is presently available.

## 6. Precise obstruction statement

The remaining missing input is one of the following equivalent-strength statements:

### Repeated sector
A weighted fixed-index divisor correlation:
\[
\sum_{u<X}\sum_{\substack{p,q>X\\p,q\mid B_u}}
 w(p,q,u)
=o(X/\log^3X)
\]
for the exact coefficients satisfying
\[
\beta q-\alpha p=2u+1.
\]

### All-distinct sector
A fibre-separation theorem:
for distinct fibre solutions the product
\[
\prod_i p_ip_i'
\]
dividing the three determinants must exceed their possible gcd/height unless the fibres coincide.

Neither follows from the frozen Apéry recurrence facts alone.

## Conclusion

The valid reduction is:

- repeated folded anchors reduce exactly to a finite family of fixed-index S-unit counts $J_{\rm rep}(u)$;
- all-distinct multiplicity reduces to bounding genuine integer determinants $D_i$;
- the Apéry companion/Casoratian does not provide the missing independent determinant because its coefficients are not the CRT fibre coefficients.

Thus there is no legitimate resultant-in-index argument. The remaining gap is a concrete arithmetic correlation estimate on divisors of $B_u$ or a fibre-height separation theorem; without one of these, the full nondegenerate fibre bound is not proved.
