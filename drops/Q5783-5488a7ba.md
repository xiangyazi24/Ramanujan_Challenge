# 3. Every proposed moving-degree collapse

## 3.1 Centering fixes the evaluation point but not the family index

For odd `h`, the involution (2.10) makes `N_h` invariant under

 \tag{3.1}

 \deg_T\mathcal K_h=\frac{3(h-1)}2.
 \tag{3.2}

For the moving packet,

\[
 2y_1+g+1=q+1+2\eta=D,
 \tag{3.3}

so

\[
 s\mid U_g
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 s\mid \mathcal K_g(D^2).
 \tag{3.4}

This is a useful normalization: the evaluation point `D^2` is fixed.  However, `g` still selects a different polynomial of degree `3(g-1)/2` for every `s`.

The broad interval gives

## 3.2 Leading-coefficient saturation

The leading coefficient of `N_h` is the Lucas sequence

\[
 V_h=U_{h-1}(17),\qquad V_{h+1}=34V_h-V_{h-1}.
\]

A degree drop modulo `s` can occur when `s\mid V_g`.  This is structural, but it does not save a power.

Let `\chi_s=(2/s)` and let `\rho_V(s)` be the rank of apparition of `s` in `V`.  The canonical rank lemma gives

\[
 \rho_V(s)\mid g,
 \qquad
 \rho_V(s)\mid s-\chi_s.

Using `g+2s=3q`, one obtains

\[
 \rho_V(s)\mid g+2(s-\chi_s)=3q-2\chi_s.

Consequently

\[
 s\mid V_{3q-2}\,V_{3q+2}.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

Both fixed Lucas values have logarithmic height `O(N)`, so their shell-prime support is still only bounded by `O(N/\log N)`.  It gives no fixed power.  Outside (3.6), the centered degree in (3.2) is the full characteristic-zero degree.

The content restriction is cleaner: because `g<s`, the canonical `h\ge2s` condition for a prime to divide the full content of `N_h` is impossible.  Thus the target short-gap polynomial is never identically zero modulo `s`.

Finally, `g` is odd, so the even-gap structural factor `2X+g+1` is absent.  The target root is not a reflection-center root.

This is not a new carrier.  The extension has crossed the polluted cutoff `c`; all later levels already contain the anchor factor `B_a`.  At the fixed length `3q`, it contains exactly the two accepted fixed anchors and factorial cut-edge content.

## 3.4 Restart identity: why there is no strong divisibility law

At a primitive return,

\[
 U_g\equiv0\pmod s,\qquad U_{g+1}\not\equiv0\pmod s.
\]

The canonical restart identity gives, for every `d\ge0`,

\[
 U_{g+d}
 \equiv U_{g+1}N_d(x_0+g)\pmod s.
 \tag{3.10}
\]

The base point has shifted.  Therefore later return times are zeros of a **different column**; they are not multiples of `g` and there is no Lucas-sequence-style implication `g\mid h`.

Here

\[
 x_0+g=-m-1,
 \qquad
 c-g=m+1.
\]

At the pollution cutoff,

\[
 N_{m+1}(-m-1)=(-1)^mB_m(m!)^3\equiv0\pmod s,
 \tag{3.11}
\]

which is exactly the reflected endpoint zero.  Equation (3.10) explains the later pollution; it does not create an independent low-height factor.

This failure of a fixed base point is the precise obstruction to a cyclotomic or primitive-divisor factorization

\[
 U_n=\prod_{d\mid n}\Phi_d
\]

of the kind available for autonomous divisibility sequences.  The “primitive part at level `g`” is currently only a primewise rank label, not a characteristic-zero quotient of controlled height.

# 4. The exact averaged radical and why it saturates

## 4.1 Rank partition of the fixed anchor

Let `I_s` be the refined moving interval

\[
 I_s=(1.052r,1.056r),
\]

or the broad interval `(21r/20,11r/10)` if no compact refinement is made.  Define

\[
 \mathcal P_a
 =\{s\in I_s:s\text{ prime and }s\mid B_a\}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

For every `s\in\mathcal P_a`, the polluted cutoff gives `\rho_s(x_0)\le c`.  Put

\[
 \mathcal P_h
 =\{s\in\mathcal P_a:\rho_s(x_0)=h\},
 \qquad 2\le h\le c.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
 \mathcal P_a=\bigsqcup_{h=2}^{c}\mathcal P_h.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

The primitive condition does not shrink the total shell radical; it labels each prime factor by its first level.

The target is the diagonal subset

\[
 \mathcal S(q,r,\eta)
 =\{s\in\mathcal P_a:\rho_s(x_0)+2s=3q\}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Since `s\mapsto g_s=3q-2s` is injective, every gap row is sampled at most once.

## 4.2 Exact radical mass

The rank partition gives

\[
 \sum_{h=2}^{c}\sum_{s\in\mathcal P_h}\log s
 =\log\operatorname{rad}_{I_s}(B_a).
 \tag{4.5}
\]

The elementary Apéry bound

\[
 B_a\le(a+1)64^a
\]

gives

\[
 \log\operatorname{rad}_{I_s}(B_a)\le\log B_a=O(N).
 \tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
 |\mathcal S(q,r,\eta)|\ll\frac N{\log N}.
 \tag{4.7}
\]

This is exactly the old anchored height scale.  The least-return condition has reorganized the same radical into rank fibers; it has not reduced its total mass.

## 4.3 Why the canonical row and column bounds do not average the diagonal

For one fixed prime `s`, the column theorem bounds the number of zero levels in an interval of length `L` by `O(L^{2/3})` before pollution.  But the affine diagonal asks about only one level

\[
 h=3q-2s
\]

for that prime.  There is no multiplicity to average.

Conversely, for one fixed `h`, the degree bound controls the number of roots in the **starting variable** `X`.  The diagonal tests only the single fixed residue `x_0` (or its centered value `D^2`).  Since `g_s` is injective, one candidate per polynomial row is far below the degree capacity `3(h-1)`.

A naive sum

\[
 \sum_h\omega_{I_s}(\gcd(B_a,U_h))
\]

is worse: one prime divisor of `B_a` may occur at many later polluted levels.  Restricting to first ranks repairs the overcount and returns exactly (4.5), not a saving.

## 4.4 Structural versus target-selective content

The complete saturation ledger is:

```text
short target N_g(x0):
  denominator/cut edge        absent, because 0 < a-g < a < s;
  full coefficient content    absent, because g < s < 2s;
  reflection-center factor    absent, because g is odd;
  leading coefficient V_g     possible, but lies in fixed V_{3q-2}V_{3q+2};
  target root                  genuine split root when it occurs.

fixed lift N_{3q}(x0):
  endpoint factor B_a          forced;
  endpoint factor B_j          accepted second anchor;
  factorial/cut-edge support   forced, and j! contains every candidate s;
  residual after saturation    1.

primitive condition:
  one divisibility U_g=0;
  earlier conditions U_h != 0;
  no new positive integer factor.
```

Thus the obstruction is not an unremoved diagonal factor.  After all known structural content is removed, the remaining question is whether the actual Apéry cocycle places a genuine first return at one prescribed affine level for many different characteristics.

## 4.5 Fixed-`h` Galois information does not apply

The hyperoctahedral/Chebotarev results in `proof.tex` vary the prime for a **fixed** gap polynomial `N_h`.  Here

\[
 h=g_s\asymp s
\]

and changes with the characteristic.  The canonical source explicitly separates this fixed-`h` prime aspect from the orbit aspect at one growing prime.  No effective Chebotarev range in the source reaches the diagonal `h\asymp s`.

## 4.6 Prime-form masks save at most logarithms

If the source-prime masks are restored, then with `t=2u` one additionally asks that the three linear forms in (1.6) be prime.  A standard dimension-three upper-bound sieve on a fixed locally admissible residue cell can save logarithmic factors, of the shape

\[
 N^{1+o(1)}(\log N)^{-3},
\]

but gives no exponent below `1`.  This is only an upper bound; it proves neither simultaneous-prime existence nor any actual-zero realization.  It does not alter the RR68 power ledger.

# 6. Hostile tests

## 6.1 Reflection-invariant surrogate zero sets

Fix a nonalias subcell with `|D-s|>g`.  For each chosen moving prime `s`, define the surrogate support

\[
 \mathcal Z_s^*
 =\{m,a,s-1-a,s-1-m\}
 =\{m,a,y_1,y_2\}.
 \tag{6.1}

After deleting the finitely many affine endpoint/midpoint hyperplanes, this support:

- is reflection-invariant;
- has no adjacent points;

- has only four points, so it satisfies every `O(|I|^{2/3})` interval bound;

This is a zero-set interface model only.  It does **not** assert that `p,p',s` are simultaneously prime, that the genuine `N_g` vanishes, or that these are actual Apéry zeros.

## 6.2 Degree/reflection interface model

Injectivity makes the polynomial interface equally permissive.  For each used gap row `h=g_s`, only one characteristic requests one reflected root pair

\[
 x_0,\quad -x_0-h-1.
\]

The available degree is `3(h-1)\gg2`.  One may build abstract reflection-symmetric nonzero polynomials with exactly that pair and choose every earlier row to avoid `x_0`.  Thus degree, reflection, nonvanishing, and least-return logic alone allow `N^{1-o(1)}` diagonal occupancy.

This does not reproduce the exact Apéry recurrence.  Its role is to identify the missing input: any successful proof must use arithmetic specific to the genuine transfer matrices, not just their degree and involution.

## 6.3 Universal continuant identities remain saturated

The canonical `A_1` tiling example shows that Dodgson singularity confinement permits dense zeros.  The affine-plane model in `gcdtail_result.tex` satisfies row degrees, local column estimates, and even codegree one while retaining quadratically many deep pairs.  The primitive-projective audits in `atom_tail_section.tex` and `primitive_projective_prime_verify.py` exhibit genuine continuant common roots and exact primitive return chains in nondistinguished fibers.

These are not counterexamples to the actual Apéry-zero assertion.  They show that:

```text
reflection + degree + restart + local column bounds
+ primitive projective returns + scalar resultants
```

still do not isolate the distinguished zero fiber or force a power-saving affine diagonal.

## 6.4 Why injectivity does not help by itself

The map

\[
 s\longmapsto g_s=3q-2s

is injective, but this only prevents two moving primes from sharing one gap row.  It removes the very collisions that row-degree and resultant-energy estimates know how to exploit.  A first-return theorem must control the **location** of one rank label per prime, not the multiplicity of roots in one polynomial.
