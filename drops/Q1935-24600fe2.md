# Q1935: theta=3 exceptional branch

## 1. Separate the exceptional eigenvalue

Let
\[
C=k-\theta(\delta_1+\delta_{-1})
\]
in \(A=\mathbf F_p[x]/(x^m-1)\). Evaluation at \(x=1\) gives
\[
C(1)=k-2\theta=3-\theta.
\]
For \(\theta=3\), the constant mode is killed:
\[
C(1)=0.
\]
Therefore one must not use the formula
\[
R(1)=\frac{1+\mu m}{C(1)},
\]
which is invalid in this branch.

Instead decompose
\[
A=\mathbf F_p\mathbf 1\oplus A_0,
\]
where
\[
A_0=\{f:f(1)=0\}
\]
is the augmentation ideal. The operator \(C\) preserves both summands. For \(\theta=3\), its restriction to \(A_0\) is the only possible inverse.

## 2. The equation for R

The required equation is
\[
CR=1+\mu(1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1}),
\qquad \mu=2.
\]
The right hand side has augmentation
\[
(1+\mu m).
\]
Since \(C\) annihilates the constant vector, solvability requires the right hand side to have zero constant projection:
\[
1+\mu m=0\pmod p.
\]
With \(p=2m+1\) and \(\mu=2\), this becomes
\[
1+2m=p=0,
\]
so the compatibility condition is automatically satisfied.

Hence the exceptional branch is not ruled out by the constant eigenvalue alone.

## 3. Augmentation inverse

Write
\[
R=\frac13\mathbf 1+R_0,
\qquad R_0\in A_0.
\]
The value \(R(1)=1/3\) is fixed because the original polynomial normalization fixes the constant component; it is not obtained by dividing by \(C(1)\).

The remaining equation is
\[
C|_{A_0}R_0=\pi_0(1+2S),
\]
where
\[
S=1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1}
\]
and \(\pi_0\) denotes projection to the augmentation ideal.

Thus existence is equivalent to
\[
\det(C|_{A_0})\neq0
\]
or, if the restriction is singular, to the corresponding Fredholm compatibility condition.

## 4. Relation with H C=-(3/2)delta_0

The sparse source equation
\[
HC=-\frac32\delta_0
\]
can also be projected:
\[
H|_{A_0}\,C|_{A_0}= -\frac32\pi_0(\delta_0).
\]
Therefore if \(C|_{A_0}\) is invertible,
\[
H|_{A_0}= -\frac32\pi_0(\delta_0)(C|_{A_0})^{-1}.
\]
The exceptional information is contained entirely in the augmentation determinant
\[
\Delta_0=\det(C|_{A_0}).
\]

## 5. Reduction to endpoint continuant scalar

Using the cyclic determinant factorization,
\[
\det(C)=C(1)\Delta_0.
\]
For \(\theta=3\), the first factor vanishes. The remaining obstruction is exactly
\[
\Delta_0.
\]
The endpoint relation \(E_m=0\) identifies the cyclic cofactor with the continuant endpoint:
\[
\operatorname{cof}_{0,0}(C|_{A_0})
=\lambda_m\big((2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m\big),
\]
for the explicit normalization factor \(\lambda_m\) coming from the cyclic-to-path minor conversion.

Hence under \(E_m=0\), the exceptional branch reduces to the single scalar condition
\[
\Delta_0=0.
\]

## Conclusion

The \(\theta=3\) case cannot be dismissed by dividing by \(C(1)\); that argument is invalid. The correct reduction is:

\[
\boxed{\theta=3\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{only the augmentation determinant }\Delta_0=\det(C|_{A_0})\text{ remains}.}
\]

If \(\Delta_0\neq0\), the augmentation inverse exists and the branch survives the linear algebra test. If \(\Delta_0=0\), the sparse-source equation reduces to one explicit endpoint continuant scalar, whose vanishing must be checked separately. No automatic contradiction follows without proving this final determinant is nonzero.
