# Two-zero dispersion and the Apéry-specific route to M1

## Summary

The two-zero count is a much more promising target than the one-zero count, but not because a generic codimension argument automatically works. The key is that the Apéry zero condition is not an arbitrary moving target: it comes from a rank-4 geometric object with symplectic monodromy. The strongest combination is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cartier diagonal + Sp(4) monodromy + recurrence structure}}
\]

with palindromicity used to remove the forced reflection symmetry.

## (1) Is D_p easier than N_p?

Yes, heuristically and structurally.

The first moment counts

\[
N_p=\#\{r:b_r=0\}/2.
\]

A single zero is a codimension-one condition. Controlling the average number of zeros requires understanding the distribution of a one-parameter family of Frobenius traces.

After removing the involution

\[
r\leftrightarrow p-1-r,
\]

an additional zero requires two independent points satisfying the same Apéry congruence. The event

\[
b_r\equiv b_s\equiv0,\qquad s\neq p-1-r
\]

should behave like a codimension-two intersection.

Thus one expects

\[
D_p\approx N_p(N_p-1)/2
\]

much smaller than the first moment. A bound

\[
\sum_{p\le X}D_p=O(\pi(X))
\]

would imply bounded second factorial moment and hence strong control on the average of $N_p$.

However, this is not true for arbitrary target sets: the Q520 counterexample shows that geometry of the target family is essential.

## (2) Cartier diagonal and dimension drop

The Cartier diagonal representation is the most promising algebraic route.

The Apéry numbers arise from a diagonal of a rational function. Modulo p, the congruence

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\]

can be interpreted through Frobenius action on a cohomological space attached to the diagonal variety.

A pair of zeros gives two simultaneous constraints:

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_{r})=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_{s})=0.
\]

The relevant parameter space is therefore a fiber product of two trace-zero loci. If the two Frobenius conditions are sufficiently independent, the dimension drops by two rather than one.

The desired estimate would come from a statement of the form:

\[
\#V(\mathbb F_p)=O(p^{\dim V-2+\epsilon})
\]

for the two-zero incidence variety.

This is the geometric analogue of the desired Kloosterman dispersion estimate.

## (3) Bombieri-Lang style argument?

The analogy is useful, but the correct tool is probably closer to Deligne/Bombieri bounds than Bombieri-Lang.

A possible proof strategy:

1. Construct an algebraic incidence variety

\[
\mathcal V=\{(r,s): b_r=b_s=0,\ s\neq p-1-r\}.
\]

2. Use the diagonal representation to realize this as a family over the finite field with a controlled l-adic sheaf.

3. Show that the two trace-zero conditions correspond to independent sections of the sheaf.

4. Apply Deligne's Riemann hypothesis over finite fields to obtain cancellation in the Frobenius trace sums.

The expected outcome is

\[
\sum_p D_p\ll \pi(X)
\]

or even stronger.

## Role of the five known properties

### (1) Palindromicity

Important but not sufficient. It removes the trivial paired zeros:

\[
(r,p-1-r).
\]

It converts the problem from counting zeros to counting independent zero orbits.

### (2) Gap polynomial degree bound

Useful but probably secondary. A bound

\[
\deg B\le p^{2/3}
\]

controls clustering, but does not explain cancellation between different primes.

### (3) Sp(4) monodromy

Very important. It predicts that independent trace conditions behave generically and prevents hidden algebraic relations forcing many simultaneous zeros.

### (4) Recurrence

Provides explicit control. It allows one to express shifted zeros and correlations as exponential sums involving the Apéry dynamical system.

### (5) Cartier diagonal

The most likely source of a proof. It turns the congruence problem into a question about algebraic varieties and l-adic sheaves, where Weil/Deligne bounds apply.

## Expected proof architecture for AKD_3

A realistic proof would look like:

1. Interpret $b_r$ as Frobenius traces of the Apéry diagonal motive.

2. Build the two-zero incidence variety excluding the reflection diagonal.

3. Prove geometric irreducibility and expected codimension two using Sp(4) monodromy.

4. Apply Deligne bounds to the associated exponential sums.

5. Deduce

\[
\sum_{p\le X}D_p=O(\pi(X)).
\]

Therefore the most plausible route is not a direct Kloosterman estimate from the recurrence, but a geometric dispersion theorem arising from the Apéry motive itself.

The critical missing theorem is essentially:

\[
\boxed{\text{The two-point Apéry trace-zero incidence variety has square-root cancellation.}}
\]

That is the Apéry-specific ingredient absent from arbitrary moving target models.
