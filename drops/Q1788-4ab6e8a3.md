# Q1788 spectral-deformation attack

## 1. Continuant realization of the endpoint determinant

Let the centered recurrence be written as a three-term continuant recurrence

\[
X_t=A_tX_{t-1}-B_tX_{t-2},
\]

with the canonical Apéry coefficients absorbed into the integral units. The standard tridiagonal determinant

\[
K_m(0)=\det
\begin{pmatrix}
A_1&1&&\\
B_2&A_2&1&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&B_m&A_m
\end{pmatrix}
\]

is exactly the continuant giving the endpoint expression

\[
e_m=(2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m
\]

up to the fixed endpoint unit. Thus a determinant representation for e_m exists.

## 2. What a determinant derivative can produce

For any one-parameter tridiagonal deformation

\[
K_m(\lambda)=K_m(0)+\lambda V
\]

Jacobi's formula gives

\[
K_m'(0)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(K_m(0))V).
\]

A diagonal deformation produces

\[
K_m'(0)=\sum_{j=1}^m v_j\,\Delta_{j-1}\Delta'_{m-j},
\]

where the factors are left and right principal continuants. An off-diagonal deformation produces the analogous product with one omitted edge.

Therefore every first derivative of a natural tridiagonal determinant is a sum of prefix-times-suffix products.

## 3. Comparison with the proposed trace numerator

The proposed quantity is

\[
Y_m=P_m-\sum_{t=2}^m6(t^2-1)d_t\frac{P_m}{X_t}.
\]

The correction terms have the structure

\[
P_m/X_t,
\]

namely a full product divided by an internal continuant. In continuant language this is a reciprocal/minor ratio, not a product of two principal minors.

Already for small m the coefficient pattern disagrees. For m=2, a determinant derivative can only contain terms proportional to the two endpoint minors. The proposed expression contains the independent term

\[
-6(3)d_2P_2/X_2.
\]

For m=3 the determinant derivative span is generated by

\[
X_0X_2,\quad X_1X_1,\quad X_2X_0
\]

(and edge analogues), whereas the trace expression introduces

\[
P_3/X_2,
\]

which is not in that span unless an additional nonlinear identity relating X_2 and its inverse is imposed.

For m=4 the same obstruction appears with independent denominators

\[
P_4/X_2,\qquad P_4/X_3.
\]

A single diagonal/off-diagonal first variation cannot generate these reciprocal continuants simultaneously.

## 4. Why inverse diagonal deformation does not repair it

Taking

\[
K_m(\lambda)=K_m(0)+\lambda\operatorname{diag}(C_t^{-1})
\]

only changes the weights v_t. The derivative remains linear in cofactors:

\[
\sum_t C_t^{-1}\,\mathrm{cofactor}_{tt}.
\]

The desired expression requires division by the moving continuants X_t themselves, not fixed scalar weights multiplying cofactors. This would require a deformation of the matrix entries depending on the inverse solution of the same determinant problem, not a natural Jacobi family.

## 5. Exact obstruction

The obstruction is the product-of-prefix structure of Jacobi derivatives:

\[
\partial_\lambda\det K
=\sum_e (\text{left continuant})_e(\text{right continuant})_e(\text{local deformation})_e.
\]

The Apéry trace numerator has reciprocal-prefix structure:

\[
Y_m=P_m\left(1-\sum_t\frac{6(t^2-1)d_t}{X_t}\right).
\]

These two spaces coincide only if additional identities force every 1/X_t into the cofactor span. The recurrence alone does not provide such identities; the mismatch is already visible at m=2,3,4.

Hence the proposed spectral-deformation route does not close the common-zero exclusion.

## Conclusion

There is an exact continuant determinant for e_m, but no natural one-parameter tridiagonal diagonal/off-diagonal deformation has derivative Y_m. The failure is algebraic: determinant derivatives are sums of prefix times suffix minors, while Y_m is a sum of full products divided by internal continuants. The first obstruction occurs at m=2 and persists through m=3,4.
