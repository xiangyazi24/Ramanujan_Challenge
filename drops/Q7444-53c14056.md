# Exact Apéry recurrence certificate analysis

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
(L_nA)_s=R_s=(n+s)^3A_s-P(n+s-1)A_{s-1}+(n+s-1)^3A_{s-2},
\]

where

\[
P(j)=34j^3+51j^2+27j+5
     =\frac{17(2j+1)^3+3(2j+1)}4.
\]

The second display makes \(P(-1-j)=-P(j)\) immediate.  The conclusions are:

1. **The reflection is not \(s\mapsto -s\).**  The direct operator anti-reflection is centered at \(1/2-n\): if
   \[
   (J_nA)_s=A_{-2n-1-s},
   \]
   then
   \[
   (L_nJ_nA)_s=-(L_nA)_{1-2n-s}.
   \]
   The reflection relevant to the formal adjoint is instead
   \[
   (L_n^*(A_{-2n-\bullet}))_s=-R_{-2n-s}.
   \]
   Thus no coefficient identity supplied by \(P(-1-j)=-P(j)\) pairs \(R_s\) with \(R_{-s}\) for an integer \(n\), except for the special centered-adjoint case \(n=0\).

2. There is an exact discrete Lagrange identity with an explicit two-term boundary flux.  For the zero-centered reversal \(A_{-s}\), the residual bulk does not disappear.  Its full common polynomial factor is \(2n\), and the exact bulk is displayed below.

3. There **is** an exact endpoint-only product-ideal certificate.  It comes from independent one-sided homogeneous adjoint kernels, not from reflection.  If \(G_+\in I_+\) and \(G_-\in I_-\) are the one-sided Green endpoints constructed below, then \(G_+G_-\in I_+I_-\).  A \(2\times2\) anchored determinant cancels the common \(A_0^2\) term exactly:
   \[
   \Delta
   =\det\begin{pmatrix}
        G_+&\alpha_+A_0\\
        G_-&\alpha_-A_0
       \end{pmatrix}
   =-A_0\left(\alpha_-U_++\alpha_+V_-\right)
   \in I_+I_-.
   \]
   This is a genuine integer formula after denominator clearing.  It does **not** by itself give \(o(n)\) height, and it is not guaranteed to be numerically nonzero for an arbitrary sequence.

4. Full-Newton stencil reflection does not produce \(A_s=\pm A_{-s}\).  Reversing the consecutive stencil changes Lagrange evaluation from \(x=-1\) to the opposite exterior point \(x=d+D+1\).  This is a true exact identity; the tempting palindromic weight symmetry is false.

The integer \(q\) does not occur in \(L_n\), \(L_n^*\), or any of the algebra below.  These identities are uniform in \(q\).  In the shell application \(q\) may enter the underlying shell data, but it supplies no additional reflection unless that data has a separately proved symmetry.

## 1. Exact reflection and all indices

### 1.1 Direct operator reflection

Define

\[
(J_nA)_s=A_{-2n-1-s}.
\]

Set

\[
t=1-2n-s.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_s(J_nA)
={}&(n+s)^3A_{-2n-1-s}
   -P(n+s-1)A_{-2n-s}\\
 &\qquad +(n+s-1)^3A_{1-2n-s}.
\end{aligned}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
 n+t=1-n-s=-(n+s-1),\qquad
 n+t-1=-n-s=-(n+s),
\]

and

\[
 P(n+t-1)=P(-n-s)=-P(n+s-1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_t(A)
={}&-(n+s-1)^3A_{1-2n-s}
   +P(n+s-1)A_{-2n-s}\\
 &\qquad -(n+s)^3A_{-2n-1-s},
\end{aligned}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{R_s(J_nA)=-R_{1-2n-s}(A).}
\]

This tracks every sequence index.  The residual index reflection is

\[
s\longmapsto 1-2n-s,
\]

whose center is \(1/2-n\).  The sequence reflection in \(J_n\) has center \(-n-1/2\).

If \(s\in[a,b]\), the reflected residual indices are exactly

\[
[\,1-2n-b,\,1-2n-a\,].
\]

For the positive range \(0\le s\le T\) to land inside the available negative window \([-S,0]\), one needs

\[
1-2n-s\ge -S
\]

for the desired values of \(s\).  In particular, all \(0\le s\le T\) require

\[
T\le S-2n+1.
\]

For positive \(n\) and a window \(S\asymp n\) with \(S<2n-1\), the exact reflected window does not meet the positive range at all.

A same-sequence consequence exists only under the additional, very strong condition

\[
A_s=A_{-2n-1-s}\quad\text{for every relevant }s.
\]

Then \(R_s=-R_{1-2n-s}\).  This is still not \(R_s\leftrightarrow R_{-s}\).

### 1.2 Why \(R_{-s}\) is not obtained

Explicitly,

\[
R_{-s}=(n-s)^3A_{-s}-P(n-s-1)A_{-s-1}+(n-s-1)^3A_{-s-2}.
\]

The reflected middle coefficient is

\[
P(n-s-1)=-P(s-n),
\]

not \(-P(n+s-1)\).  Moreover, reversing \(A_s,A_{s-1},A_{s-2}\) changes the order of the sequence indices.  This order reversal is handled by the formal adjoint, not by \(L_n\) itself.

## 2. The exact discrete Green/Lagrange identity

Write

\[
a_s=(n+s)^3,\qquad b_s=-P(n+s-1),\qquad c_s=(n+s-1)^3.
\]

Thus

\[
R_s=a_sA_s+b_sA_{s-1}+c_sA_{s-2}.
\]

For an arbitrary auxiliary sequence \(u_s\), the formal adjoint is

\[
\boxed{
(L_n^*u)_s=(n+s)^3u_s-P(n+s)u_{s+1}+(n+s+1)^3u_{s+2}.
}
\]

Define the boundary flux

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_s(A,u)
={}&(n+s-1)^3u_sA_{s-2}\\
 &+\bigl((n+s)^3u_{s+1}-P(n+s-1)u_s\bigr)A_{s-1}.
\end{aligned}}
\]

Then, for every integer \(s\),

\[
\boxed{
u_sR_s-A_s(L_n^*u)_s
=\Phi_s(A,u)-\Phi_{s+1}(A,u).
}
\]

### Proof

The \(A_{s-2}\) term in \(\Phi_s\) is \(c_su_sA_{s-2}\).  The \(A_{s-1}\) terms in \(\Phi_s-\Phi_{s+1}\) are

\[
(a_su_{s+1}+b_su_s)A_{s-1}-c_{s+1}u_{s+1}A_{s-1}
=b_su_sA_{s-1},
\]

because \(c_{s+1}=a_s\).  The \(A_s\) term contributed by \(-\Phi_{s+1}\) is

\[
-\bigl(a_{s+1}u_{s+2}+b_{s+1}u_{s+1}\bigr)A_s.
\]

Since \(a_{s+1}=c_{s+2}\), these are exactly the terms left after subtracting \(A_s(L_n^*u)_s\) from \(u_sR_s\).

Consequently, for arbitrary integers \(m\le M\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s=m}^{M}\bigl(u_sR_s-A_s(L_n^*u)_s\bigr)
=\Phi_m(A,u)-\Phi_{M+1}(A,u).
}
\]

There are no convergence questions: this is a finite identity.  If \(A_s,u_s,n\in\mathbb Z\), every term is integral.  The values needed are

\[
A_{m-2},\ldots,A_M,\qquad u_m,\ldots,u_{M+2}.
\]

No division has occurred.

## 3. Exact adjoint reflection and the centered mismatch

### 3.1 The one exact reversed adjoint

For an integer \(\lambda\), put

\[
u_s=A_{\lambda-s}.
\]

Then

\[
(L_n^*u)_s
=(n+s)^3A_{\lambda-s}-P(n+s)A_{\lambda-s-1}
 +(n+s+1)^3A_{\lambda-s-2}.
\]

For this to equal \(-R_{\lambda-s}\) coefficientwise for every sequence \(A\), the first coefficient requires

\[
(n+s)^3=-(n+\lambda-s)^3.
\]

The cube map is injective on integers, and as a polynomial identity in \(s\) this gives

\[
\lambda=-2n.
\]

For this value, the other two coefficients agree because

\[
P(-n-s-1)=-P(n+s).
\]

Hence the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
(L_n^*(A_{-2n-\bullet}))_s=-R_{-2n-s}.
}
\]

This choice is unique among pure reversals \(u_s=A_{\lambda-s}\).

Substitution into the Lagrange identity gives the bulk-free local formula

\[
\boxed{
A_{-2n-s}R_s+A_sR_{-2n-s}
=\Phi_s(A,A_{-2n-\bullet})-
 \Phi_{s+1}(A,A_{-2n-\bullet}).
}
\]

Thus, for \(m\le M\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s=m}^{M}
 \bigl(A_{-2n-s}R_s+A_sR_{-2n-s}\bigr)
=\Phi_m(A,A_{-2n-\bullet})-
 \Phi_{M+1}(A,A_{-2n-\bullet}).
}
\]

The negative residual range here is

\[
[-2n-M,-2n-m].
\]

If one takes \(0\le s\le T\), these residuals belong to \([-S,0]\) only when

\[
2n+T\le S.
\]

So the exact adjoint reflection becomes usable inside \(I_-\) only when the negative window reaches approximately \(-2n\).  For \(S\asymp n\) with \(S<2n\), it misses the available negative residuals.

There is a second important limitation: the multipliers \(A_{-2n-s}\) and \(A_s\) are arbitrary sequence values.  They are not generators of \(I_-\) and \(I_+\), respectively.  Therefore the bulk-free bilinear identity alone does **not** imply membership in \(I_+I_-\).

### 3.2 Zero-centered reversal

Now take the desired zero-centered reversal

\[
u_s=A_{-s}.
\]

A direct coefficient comparison gives

\[
(L_n^*u)_s=-R_{-s}+D_s,
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D_s=2n\bigl[& (n^2+3s^2)A_{-s}\\
&-\bigl(34n^2+102s^2+102s+27\bigr)A_{-s-1}\\
&+\bigl(n^2+3(s+1)^2\bigr)A_{-s-2}\bigr].
\end{aligned}}
\]

The three coefficient calculations are

\[
(n+s)^3+(n-s)^3=2n(n^2+3s^2),
\]

\[
P(n+s)+P(n-s-1)
=2n\bigl(34n^2+102s^2+102s+27\bigr),
\]

and

\[
(n+s+1)^3+(n-s-1)^3
=2n\bigl(n^2+3(s+1)^2\bigr).
\]

The middle identity follows immediately from

\[
P(x)=\frac{17(2x+1)^3+3(2x+1)}4.
\]

Substituting into summation by parts gives the exact local centered formula

\[
\boxed{
A_{-s}R_s+A_sR_{-s}
=\Phi_s(A,A_{-\bullet})-
 \Phi_{s+1}(A,A_{-\bullet})+A_sD_s.
}
\]

Here

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_s(A,A_{-\bullet})
={}&(n+s-1)^3A_{-s}A_{s-2}\\
&+\bigl((n+s)^3A_{-s-1}
       -P(n+s-1)A_{-s}\bigr)A_{s-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore, for every \(m\le M\),

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s=m}^{M}(A_{-s}R_s+A_sR_{-s})
={}&\Phi_m(A,A_{-\bullet})-
    \Phi_{M+1}(A,A_{-\bullet})\\
&+2n\sum_{s=m}^{M}A_s\bigl[
 (n^2+3s^2)A_{-s}\\
&\qquad-\bigl(34n^2+102s^2+102s+27\bigr)A_{-s-1}\\
&\qquad+\bigl(n^2+3(s+1)^2\bigr)A_{-s-2}
\bigr].
\end{aligned}}
\]

This is the strongest direct centered-reversal formula: the mismatch has been completely factored.  Over \(\mathbb Q[n,s]\), the first and third bracket coefficients are coprime, so there is no further nonconstant common polynomial factor beyond \(2n\).

At \(n=0\), the bulk vanishes and the zero-centered adjoint reversal is exact.  For \(n\ne0\), it is not.

## 4. A scoped no-go for centered bulk cancellation

The preceding mismatch is not a cosmetic artifact of the chosen flux.

Assume \(n\ne0\), \(S\ge5\), and consider any weighted centered pairing

\[
\mathcal C_w(A)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}w_s
 (A_{-s}R_s+A_sR_{-s}),
\]

where the weights are fixed integers or rationals independent of \(A\).

Fix an interior index

\[
3\le k\le S-2
\]

and choose the finitely supported sequence

\[
A_k=A_{-k}=1,
\qquad A_j=0\quad(j\ne\pm k).
\]

Then every term in \(\mathcal C_w\) vanishes except the term \(s=k\), and

\[
R_k=(n+k)^3,\qquad R_{-k}=(n-k)^3.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_w(A)=2n\,w_k\,(n^2+3k^2).
}
\]

All natural second-order boundary values occurring in

\[
\Phi_1(A,A_{-\bullet})
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Phi_{S+1}(A,A_{-\bullet})
\]

are zero for this sequence.  In particular, with \(w_s=1\),

\[
\sum_{s=1}^{S}(A_{-s}R_s+A_sR_{-s})
=2n(n^2+3k^2)\ne0,
\]

whereas both endpoint fluxes vanish.

Consequently:

> Any universal endpoint-only identity whose bulk is a fixed weighted sum of the centered local pairings must have \(w_k=0\) for every \(3\le k\le S-2\).  Hence no nontrivial bulk-spanning centered pairing can telescope universally when \(n\ne0\).

This is a scoped no-go.  It rules out the natural centered Green/Lagrange ansatz and any \(2\times2\) antisymmetrization whose expansion is such a fixed weighted centered pairing.  It does not rule out every conceivable quadratic expression in the generators.  The next section gives an exact quadratic construction that avoids the centered reversal altogether.

A second, operator-level no-go is the uniqueness already proved: among all pure reversals \(A_{\lambda-s}\), only \(\lambda=-2n\) is an exact adjoint reversal.  Thus a bulk-free formula involving \(R_{-s}\) cannot be obtained by changing only a scalar normalization of the zero-centered reversal.

## 5. Product ideal and one-sided Green endpoints

Let

\[
I_+=(A_0,R_0,\ldots,R_S),
\qquad
I_-=(A_0,R_{-S},\ldots,R_0)
\]

as ideals of \(\mathbb Z\).  If \(c_+,c_-\ge0\) are the corresponding gcds, then

\[
I_+=c_+\mathbb Z,
\qquad I_-=c_-\mathbb Z,
\qquad I_+I_-=c_+c_-\mathbb Z.
\]

Thus integer membership in \(I_+I_-\) is exactly divisibility by \(c_+c_-\), provided \(c_+c_-\ne0\).  If one gcd is zero, ideal membership remains the correct formulation: all generators on that side are zero and the product ideal is the zero ideal.

### 5.1 Positive Green endpoint

Choose integers

\[
u_0,u_1,\ldots,u_{S+2}
\]

such that

\[
(L_n^*u)_s=0\qquad(0\le s\le S),
\]

and impose

\[
u_1=0.
\]

The equation at \(s=0\) gives

\[
n^3u_0+(n+1)^3u_2=0.
\]

Define

\[
\alpha_+:=-n^3u_0=(n+1)^3u_2.
\]

Summing the Green identity over \(0\le s\le S\) gives

\[
\Phi_{S+1}(A,u)
=\alpha_+A_0-\sum_{s=1}^{S}u_sR_s.
\]

Set

\[
\boxed{
G_+:=\Phi_{S+1}(A,u)
     =\alpha_+A_0-\sum_{s=1}^{S}u_sR_s.
}
\]

Then \(G_+\in I_+\).  Explicitly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_+
={}&(n+S)^3u_{S+1}A_{S-1}\\
&+\bigl((n+S+1)^3u_{S+2}
       -P(n+S)u_{S+1}\bigr)A_S.
\end{aligned}
\]

Because \((L_n^*u)_S=0\), this may be written as the endpoint Wronskian

\[
\boxed{
G_+=(n+S)^3
 (u_{S+1}A_{S-1}-u_SA_S).
}
\]

No division was used in any of these identities.

### 5.2 Negative Green endpoint

Choose integers

\[
v_{-S},v_{-S+1},\ldots,v_1
\]

such that

\[
(L_n^*v)_s=0\qquad(-S\le s\le-1),
\]

and impose

\[
v_1=0.
\]

Define

\[
\alpha_-:=-n^3v_0.
\]

Summing the Green identity over \(-S\le s\le-1\) gives

\[
\Phi_{-S}(A,v)
=\alpha_-A_0+v_0R_0+
 \sum_{s=-S}^{-1}v_sR_s.
\]

Set

\[
\boxed{
G_-:=\Phi_{-S}(A,v)
     =\alpha_-A_0+v_0R_0+
       \sum_{s=-S}^{-1}v_sR_s.
}
\]

Then \(G_-\in I_-\).  Its explicit left endpoint form is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
G_-
={}&(n-S-1)^3v_{-S}A_{-S-2}\\
&+\bigl((n-S)^3v_{-S+1}
       -P(n-S-1)v_{-S}\bigr)A_{-S-1}.
\end{aligned}}
\]

One should **not** automatically divide by \(n-S\) or \(n-S-1\).  Those factors can vanish in the relevant regime \(S\asymp n\).  If \(v\) is additionally extended to satisfy \((L_n^*v)_{-S-1}=0\), then the endpoint simplifies to

\[
(n-S-1)^3
 (v_{-S}A_{-S-2}-v_{-S-1}A_{-S-1}),
\]

but imposing that extra equation can make the displayed factor zero at a singular endpoint.  The unsimplified formula above is the safe one.

### 5.3 Existence and integrality of the Green kernels

The positive constraints form a homogeneous integer system in the \(S+3\) variables

\[
u_0,\ldots,u_{S+2}
\]

with \(S+2\) equations: \(S+1\) adjoint equations and \(u_1=0\).  Hence there is a nonzero rational null vector, and multiplying by the least common multiple of its denominators gives an integer vector.

Likewise, the negative constraints form a homogeneous integer system in the \(S+2\) variables

\[
v_{-S},\ldots,v_1
\]

with \(S+1\) equations, so a nonzero integer null vector exists after denominator clearing.

A completely division-free canonical construction is available.  Form the corresponding band matrices with rows

\[
\bigl((n+s)^3,-P(n+s),(n+s+1)^3\bigr)
\]

in columns \(s,s+1,s+2\), together with the row imposing \(u_1=0\) or \(v_1=0\).  When the matrix has full row rank, its signed maximal minors give an integer right-null vector.  If the rank drops at a singular integer value, use a primitive integer basis of the integer nullspace.  This avoids every questionable division.

If instead one normalizes a rational solution and recursively solves for \(u_{s+2}\) or \(v_s\), all denominators must be cleared **before** claiming divisibility.  If \(d_+\) and \(d_-\) clear the positive and negative kernels, then the integral certificate is scaled by \(d_+d_-\).  A rational, unscaled endpoint does not define integer divisibility by \(c_+c_-\).

The coefficients of the band matrices are \(O((1+|n|+S)^3)\).  Hadamard's bound on the maximal minors gives

\[
\log\max_s(|u_s|,|v_s|)
=O\bigl(S\log(1+|n|+S)\bigr)
\]

for this general cofactor construction.  When \(S\asymp n\), this is \(O(n\log n)\), not \(o(n)\).

### 5.4 Exact product and \(2\times2\) determinant

The simplest endpoint-only product certificate is

\[
\boxed{
E_\times:=G_+G_-
=\Phi_{S+1}(A,u)\Phi_{-S}(A,v)
\in I_+I_-.
}
\]

It is an integer after the kernel denominators are cleared, and therefore

\[
c_+c_-\mid E_\times.
\]

To cancel the common near-boundary \(A_0^2\) term, put

\[
U_+:=\sum_{s=1}^{S}u_sR_s,
\qquad
V_-:=v_0R_0+\sum_{s=-S}^{-1}v_sR_s.
\]

Then

\[
G_+=\alpha_+A_0-U_+,
\qquad
G_-=\alpha_-A_0+V_-.
\]

Now define

\[
\boxed{
\Delta:=
\det\begin{pmatrix}
G_+&\alpha_+A_0\\
G_-&\alpha_-A_0
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

Every entry in the first row belongs to \(I_+\), and every entry in the second row belongs to \(I_-\).  Hence each cross-product in the determinant belongs to \(I_+I_-\), so

\[
\Delta\in I_+I_-.
\]

Expanding gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta
&=\alpha_-A_0G_+-\alpha_+A_0G_-\\
&=\alpha_-A_0(\alpha_+A_0-U_+)
  -\alpha_+A_0(\alpha_-A_0+V_-)\\
&=\boxed{-A_0(\alpha_-U_++\alpha_+V_-)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the \(A_0^2\) terms cancel exactly, and

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Delta
=-A_0\biggl(&\alpha_-\sum_{s=1}^{S}u_sR_s\\
&+\alpha_+\biggl(v_0R_0+
 \sum_{s=-S}^{-1}v_sR_s\biggr)\biggr).
\end{aligned}}
\]

Equivalently, this is the endpoint-only identity

\[
\boxed{
\Delta=A_0\bigl(
 \alpha_-\Phi_{S+1}(A,u)
 -\alpha_+\Phi_{-S}(A,v)
\bigr).
}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{c_+c_-\mid\Delta}
\]

whenever \(c_+c_-\ne0\), with ideal membership covering the degenerate gcd-zero case.

This answers the existence part of question (3): there is an exact \(2\times2\) product-ideal determinant with no interior \(A_s\)-bulk.  It is generated by separate one-sided adjoint elimination.  It is not a consequence of \(P(-1-j)=-P(j)\).

### 5.5 What “nonzero” can and cannot mean

Neither \(E_\times\) nor \(\Delta\) is numerically nonzero for every integer sequence.  The zero sequence is an immediate counterexample.  More substantively, if \(R_s=0\) on both windows, then the determinant expansion above vanishes.  Thus “nonzero endpoint” must mean either

* the endpoint polynomial is not identically zero for the chosen kernels, or
* its value is checked to be nonzero for the fixed shell sequence in the application.

That check is external to the formal Green identity.

There is also a structural reason a **linear** endpoint cannot solve the product-divisibility problem uniformly.  Suppose \(E(A)\) is a nonzero integer linear form in \(A\) and, for every integer sequence \(A\),

\[
E(A)\in I_+(A)I_-(A).
\]

Scale \(A\) to \(TA\).  Then \(R_s(TA)=TR_s(A)\), so

\[
c_+(TA)c_-(TA)=T^2c_+(A)c_-(A),
\]

whereas \(E(TA)=TE(A)\).  For a fixed \(A\) with \(c_+(A)c_-(A)\ne0\), divisibility for every \(T\) would imply

\[
T\,c_+(A)c_-(A)\mid E(A)
\]

for every \(T\), forcing \(E(A)=0\).  Hence a universal product certificate must be at least quadratic/bilinear.  This explains why a product or determinant is necessary.

### 5.6 Rank-one obstruction to a pure two-Green determinant

Over a nonsingular coefficient field, the space of positive adjoint solutions on \(0\le s\le S\) is two-dimensional.  Requiring the endpoint to be universally expressible from \(I_+\) removes the unobserved near-boundary term \(n^3u_1A_{-1}\), hence imposes \(u_1=0\) when \(n\ne0\).  This leaves a one-dimensional Green line.  The same statement holds on a nonsingular negative window.

Consequently, two pure ideal-compatible Green endpoints on the same side are proportional, so a \(2\times2\) determinant made only from two such positive endpoints and two such negative endpoints is generically zero.  A nonzero determinant must adjoin a shared ideal generator such as \(A_0\) (as above), or cross a singular coefficient where the finite solution space changes.  Crossing the singular rows \(n+s=0\) or \(n+s+1=0\) must be handled by the integer band matrix, not by assuming a second regular Green mode.

This is the precise limitation of the \(2\times2\) route: the anchored determinant exists, but a nonzero “two independent Green directions on each side” determinant is generically unavailable after enforcing ideal membership.

## 6. Height: what follows and what does not

Define the logarithmic height of a nonzero integer by

\[
h(E)=\log|E|.
\]

The algebra above does not imply \(h(E)=o(n)\) for arbitrary \(A\), even when \(S\asymp n\).

### 6.1 Scaling no-go

Fix any sequence \(A\) with \(c_+c_->0\), and replace it by \(A^{(T)}=TA\).  Then

\[
c_+(A^{(T)})c_-(A^{(T)})=T^2c_+(A)c_-(A).
\]

Every nonzero integer in the product ideal satisfies

\[
|E(A^{(T)})|\ge T^2c_+(A)c_-(A).
\]

By taking \(T=T(n)\) with arbitrarily large growth, no upper bound \(o(n)\) can hold uniformly over arbitrary integer sequences.  Any sublinear-height theorem must use a normalization and special estimates for the fixed shell functional.

### 6.2 Green-kernel coefficient cost

The division-free cofactor kernels have the general bound

\[
O\bigl(S\log(1+|n|+S)\bigr)
\]

on their coefficient logarithms.  With \(S\asymp n\), this is not sublinear.  A particular recurrence may have cancellations that improve this, but the reflection identity does not provide them.

### 6.3 The anchored determinant retains \(A_0\)

The improved determinant cancels \(A_0^2\), but it still has the exact factor

\[
A_0.
\]

## 7. Full-Newton weights and stencil reflection

The repository uses, for nodes

\[
d,d+1,\ldots,d+L=D,
\]

the weights

\[
w_i=(-1)^i\binom{d+i}{i}
             \binom{d+L+1}{L-i},
\qquad 0\le i\le L.
\]

This is exactly `newton_weight(d0, L, i)` in

```text
problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_cartier_packet_audit.py
```

and the same carrier appears in `q32_block_pascal_gcd_audit.py`.

Writing \(k=d+i\), the weight is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
w_k=(-1)^{k-d}
 \binom{k}{d}\binom{D+1}{k+1}.
}
\]

### 7.1 Exact Lagrange interpretation and integrality

Let

\[
\ell_k(x)=\prod_{\substack{r=d\\r\ne k}}^{D}
 \frac{x-r}{k-r}
\]

be the cardinal polynomial.  Then

\[
w_k=\ell_k(-1).
\]

Indeed,

\[
\prod_{\substack{r=d\\r\ne k}}^D(k-r)
=(k-d)!(-1)^{D-k}(D-k)!,
\]

and, for \(d\ge0\),

\[
\prod_{\substack{r=d\\r\ne k}}^D(-1-r)
=(-1)^{D-d}
 \frac{(D+1)!}{d!(k+1)}.
\]

Taking the quotient gives the displayed binomial formula.  Hence \(w_k\in\mathbb Z\) for the repository's range \(d\ge0\).  If the shell values are integers, every full-Newton carrier is an integer; there is no Lagrange denominator to clear.

Also

\[
\sum_{k=d}^{D}w_k=1,
\]

because the constant polynomial evaluates to \(1\) at \(-1\).  Therefore the weights have gcd \(1\); reversing them cannot reveal a hidden universal content factor.

If one leaves the consecutive nonnegative-node setting, the general product formula is rational.  If \(-1\) is itself one of the nodes, evaluation is simply the corresponding Kronecker weight, and formulas that divide by \(k+1\) must not be used.

### 7.2 What reflection really does

Let

\[
H=d+D.
\]

The node reflection is

\[
k\longmapsto H-k.
\]

The exact cardinal-polynomial identity is

\[
\boxed{
\ell_{H-k}(-1)=\ell_k(H+1).
}
\]

Thus the reversed weight vector is not, in general, \(\pm(w_k)\).  It is the Lagrange weight vector for evaluation at the **opposite exterior point**

\[
H+1=d+D+1.
\]

Let \(y_k\) be data on the nodes, and let \(p(x)\) be their interpolation polynomial of degree at most \(L\).  Define

\[
A=\sum_{k=d}^{D}w_ky_k=p(-1).
\]

For the reflected data \(y_{H-k}\), one has

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k=d}^{D}w_ky_{H-k}=p(H+1).
}
\]

This is the true reflection identity.

A concrete counterexample is \(d=0,D=2\).  The weights at \(-1\) are

\[
(3,-3,1).
\]

Their reversal is

\[
(1,-3,3),
\]

which is the evaluation vector at \(x=3\), not \(\pm(3,-3,1)\).  For the polynomial \(p(x)=x\),

\[
p(-1)=-1,
\qquad p(3)=3.
\]

So even perfectly reflected polynomial data do not give equality or a sign change.

### 7.3 Consequence for \(A_s\) versus \(A_{-s}\)

Suppose the fixed full-Newton functional has the form

\[
A_s=\sum_{k=d}^{D}w_kY_s(k),
\]

where only the shell data \(Y_s(k)\) depend on \(s\).  Let \(p_s(x)\) be the interpolation polynomial of \(Y_s(k)\) on the node block.

If one has the additional raw-data reflection

\[
Y_{-s}(k)=Y_s(H-k),
\]

then the exact conclusion is

\[
\boxed{
A_{-s}=p_s(H+1),
\qquad A_s=p_s(-1).
}
\]

There is no relation between these two values unless one separately proves

\[
p_s(H+1)=\pm p_s(-1)
\]

or another endpoint relation.  If the raw shell data do not satisfy the displayed reflection in the first place, there is not even this opposite-endpoint statement.

Therefore the full-Newton stencil does not supply the missing symmetry \(A_{-s}=\pm A_s\), nor the shifted symmetry \(A_s=A_{-2n-1-s}\) needed by the direct Apéry reflection.

## 8. Exact answers to the four requested questions

### (1) Does \(P(-1-j)\) relate \(R_s\) to \(R_{-s}\)?

No.  The exact direct relation is

\[
R_s(J_nA)=-R_{1-2n-s}(A),
\qquad (J_nA)_s=A_{-2n-1-s}.
\]

The exact adjoint relation is

\[
(L_n^*(A_{-2n-\bullet}))_s=-R_{-2n-s}.
\]

The centers and the one-index offset are essential.  Neither relation gives \(R_{-s}\) for an integer \(n\ne0\).

### (2) Strongest bilinear summation-by-parts / centered formula

For arbitrary \(u\),

\[
\sum_{s=m}^{M}
 (u_sR_s-A_sL_n^*u_s)
=\Phi_m-\Phi_{M+1}.
\]

For \(u_s=A_{-2n-s}\), this is bulk-free and pairs \(R_s\) with \(R_{-2n-s}\).

For \(u_s=A_{-s}\), the exact remaining bulk is

\[
2n\sum_{s=m}^{M}A_s\left[
 (n^2+3s^2)A_{-s}
 -(34n^2+102s^2+102s+27)A_{-s-1}
 +(n^2+3(s+1)^2)A_{-s-2}
\right].
\]

This factorization is exact.

### (3) Product/determinant in \(I_+I_-\)

Yes.  The one-sided adjoint kernels give

\[
G_+=\alpha_+A_0-\sum_{s=1}^{S}u_sR_s\in I_+,
\]

\[
G_-=\alpha_-A_0+v_0R_0+
 \sum_{s=-S}^{-1}v_sR_s\in I_-.
\]

Hence \(G_+G_-\in I_+I_-\), and the exact determinant

\[
\Delta=
\det\begin{pmatrix}
G_+&\alpha_+A_0\\
G_-&\alpha_-A_0
\end{pmatrix}
=-A_0(\alpha_-U_++\alpha_+V_-)
\]

also lies in \(I_+I_-\).  It is endpoint-only and cancels \(A_0^2\).

What does **not** follow is nonvanishing or \(o(n)\) height.  The centered-reflection bulk cannot be universally canceled, as shown by the sequence \(A_k=A_{-k}=1\), all other terms zero.  A pure determinant of two ideal-compatible Green directions on each side is generically zero because each such Green space is rank one after the near-boundary condition.

### (4) Full-Newton reflection

For nodes \(d,\ldots,D\), full-Newton evaluation is at \(-1\).  Reversing the stencil sends it to evaluation at

\[
d+D+1,
\]

not back to \(-1\) up to sign.  Thus a reflected shell stencil gives \(A_{-s}=p_s(d+D+1)\), while \(A_s=p_s(-1)\), only when the raw shell data themselves reflect.  Equality or anti-equality requires an additional endpoint theorem and is not a property of the Newton weights.
