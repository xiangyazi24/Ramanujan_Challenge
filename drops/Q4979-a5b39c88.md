\[

\[

For fixed \(0\le i<j\le3\), the same argument shows

## 3.3 Structural primes

For the gap \(h\), use the local carrier
\[
\mathfrak u_h=((h-1)!)^3b_{h-1}V_h.
\]
Define the structural six-pair mass by
\[
\mathfrak S_{6,\mathrm{str}}(H)
=\sum_{0\le i<j\le3}\sum_p\log p\;\#\{Q\in\mathcal Q_p(H):p\mid\mathfrak u_{h_{ij}(Q)}\}.
\]
Grouping by \(h\), using (2.1), and then using prime-divisor weight gives
\[
egin{aligned}
\mathfrak S_{6,\mathrm{str}}(H)
&\le6\sum_{2\le h<H}3(h-1)\sum_{p\mid\mathfrak u_h}\log p\
&\le18\sum_{2\le h<H}(h-1)\log\mathfrak u_h\
&\ll\sum_{h<H}h^2\log(2h)\ll H^3\log(2H).
\end{aligned}
	ag{3.7}
\]
This is completely unconditional from the literal source. For an actual contribution \(p>h\), the factorial factor is in fact a \(p\)-unit, so one can sharpen the estimate, but the stated \(O(H^3\log H)\) is sufficient.

It is important not to replace \(p\mid\mathfrak u_h\) in (3.7) by the unrelated condition \(p\mid\mathfrak U_H\) for every \(h\). A prime can divide the ambient product because of one index \(j\) and would then be charged to all \(H\) gaps. The direct argument would deteriorate by an extra \(H\). The singular/structural carrier must be localized to the realized gap.

## 3.4 Centered pairs and deflation

The literal fully-deflated lemma proves that among the **three adjacent** pairs, the unique centered adjacent pair can affect at most three selected windows. This cannot be copied to all six pairs. Reflection pairs every zero with \(p-1-z\), so a zero set can have many centered nonadjacent pairs. In a symmetric quadruple one can have simultaneously
\[
z_0+z_3=p-1,\qquad z_1+z_2=p-1.
\]
Hence the all-six insertion should use raw \(N_h\), as in (3.1)--(3.6), and let \(N_h'\) detect actual multiple roots. If one insists on \(N_h^\circ\), all centered nonadjacent pairs must be restored as a separate term; they are not an \(O(1)\)-per-prime deletion by the adjacent-pair argument.

\[
egin{aligned}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\log p\;\#\{Q:ho_p(Q)\le R\}

A zero can support more than one selected window when its slot is allowed to vary, but at most four; (4.1) explicitly pays the four slots. This is why the unqualified uniqueness claim must not be used.

# 5. Why the first/last-block corollary is not justified

The literal source does not prescribe a reflection-symmetric partition or even a specific origin; it only asks for consecutive blocks of cardinality at most \(H\). Thus endpoint depth does not imply membership in a named first or last block.

Even under the usual origin-zero convention
\[
[0,H-1],[H,2H-1],\ldots,
\]
the final block generally has a short remainder. For example, with \(p=11\), \(H=4\), the blocks are
\[
[0,3],\ [4,7],\ [8,10].
\]
The upper endpoint strip of depth \(R=3<H\) is \([7,10]\), which meets both the penultimate and final blocks. Thus an origin-zero convention alone would still not prove “upper shallow implies last block”; one would additionally need \(R\) smaller than the terminal remainder, or a specially constructed reflection-compatible partition.

The endpoint proof (4.1)--(4.3) is global and avoids this unnecessary issue. The optional first/last-block corollary should be omitted from the insertion.

The following is source-safe. The first lemma isolates the purely combinatorial transfer. The discriminant corollary is explicitly conditional until a squarefreeness theorem is imported.

```tex
\begin{lemma}[Fixed-slot and fixed-pair-slot injectivity]
\label{lem:qc-slot-injectivity}
Fix an odd prime $p$, partition $[0,p-1]$ into disjoint consecutive
intervals, and in every interval select all sliding quadruples of
consecutive elements of $\mathcal Z_p$.  Write the selected family as
$\mathcal Q_p$.  For each $i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ the map
$Q=(z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3)\mapsto z_i$ is injective on $\mathcal Q_p$.
For each $0\le i<j\le3$ the map $Q\mapsto(z_i,z_j)$ is injective.
Consequently, for every integer $h\ge1$,
\[
 \#\{Q\in\mathcal Q_p:z_j-z_i=h\}\le A_p(h)\le3(h-1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write the full ordered zero set as $u_1<\cdots<u_{Z(p)}$.  Every
selected quadruple is $(u_t,u_{t+1},u_{t+2},u_{t+3})$ for one index
$t$ whose four entries lie in one block.  For fixed $i$, the rank of
$z_i=u_{t+i}$ determines $t$; the pair assertion follows a fortiori.
A block boundary can delete this unique global window but cannot create
a second one.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Six-pair transfer of a one-gap singular mass]
\label{lem:qc-six-pair-transfer}
For $h\ge2$ and an odd prime $p$, let
$\operatorname{Sing}_p(h)$ be a subset of the roots of $N_h$ modulo
$p$.  Suppose
\[
 \sum_p(\log p)\,|\operatorname{Sing}_p(h)|
 \ll h^2\log(2h)
 \qquad(h\ge2).
\]
Let $\mathcal Q_p(H)$ be the selected sliding quadruples of span less
than $H$.  Then
\[
 \sum_p\log p\sum_{Q\in\mathcal Q_p(H)}
 \#\{(i,j):0\le i<j\le3,\ z_i\in
       \operatorname{Sing}_p(z_j-z_i)\}
 \ll H^3\log(2H).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $(i,j)$ and $h$, Lemma~\ref{lem:qc-slot-injectivity}
maps the counted windows injectively to
$\operatorname{Sing}_p(h)$.  Sum first over the six pairs of slots
and then over $2\le h<H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Six-pair discriminant singular mass]
\label{cor:qc-six-pair-discriminant}
Assume that $N_h$ is squarefree over $\mathbb Q$ for every $h\ge2$.
Put
\[
 \Delta_h=\operatorname{Res}_x(N_h,N_h'),\qquad
 \operatorname{Sing}_p(h)=
 \{x\in\mathbb F_p:N_h(x)=N_h'(x)=0\}.
\]
Then the conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:qc-six-pair-transfer} holds.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The fixed Sylvester-corank argument gives
$|\operatorname{Sing}_p(h)|\le v_p(\Delta_h)$.  Hence
\[
 \sum_p(\log p)|\operatorname{Sing}_p(h)|
 \le\log|\Delta_h|\ll h^2\log(2h),
\]
the last estimate following from the standard resultant-height bound
for the degree-$3(h-1)$ continuant $N_h$ and its derivative.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Six-pair structural mass]
\label{lem:qc-six-pair-structural}
Put
\[
 \mathfrak u_h=((h-1)!)^3b_{h-1}V_h.
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_{0\le i<j\le3}\sum_p(\log p)
 \#\{Q\in\mathcal Q_p(H):p\mid\mathfrak u_{z_j-z_i}\}
 \ll H^3\log(2H).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $(i,j)$ and $h$, Lemma~\ref{lem:qc-slot-injectivity} and
$A_p(h)\le3(h-1)$ bound the number of windows by $3(h-1)$.  Therefore
the left side is at most
\[
 18\sum_{2\le h<H}(h-1)\sum_{p\mid\mathfrak u_h}\log p
 \le18\sum_{2\le h<H}(h-1)\log\mathfrak u_h
 \ll H^3\log(2H).
\]
\end{proof}
```

The endpoint insertion can be unconditional immediately:

```tex
\begin{lemma}[Prime-weighted endpoint depth of selected quadruples]
\label{lem:qc-endpoint-depth}
Let $X\ge3$, let $\mathcal P_X=\{p:X<p\le2X\}$, and for each
$p\in\mathcal P_X$ let $\mathcal Q_p(H)$ be the sliding quadruples
selected from one partition of $[0,p-1]$ into disjoint consecutive
intervals.  For
\[
 \rho_p(Q)=\min_{0\le i\le3}\min(z_i,p-1-z_i)
\]
and every real $R\ge0$,
\[
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}(\log p)
 \#\{Q\in\mathcal Q_p(H):\rho_p(Q)\le R\}
 \le 8\sum_{1\le r\le\lfloor R\rfloor}\log b_r
 \ll R^2.
\]
More precisely, the left side is zero for $0\le R<1$ and is at most
$28.8R^2$ for $R\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose deterministically a witnessing slot $i$, endpoint side, and
integer depth $0\le r\le\lfloor R\rfloor$.  For fixed
$(p,i,\text{side},r)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:qc-slot-injectivity} permits at
most one selected window.  Since $p\ge5$, palindrome gives
$b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p$.  Hence the number at $p$ is at most
$8\sum_{r\le\lfloor R\rfloor}\mathbf1_{p\mid b_r}$.  The $r=0$
term vanishes because $b_0=1$.  Summing with weight $\log p$ gives
\[
 8\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor R\rfloor}
   \sum_{p\mid b_r}\log p
 \le8\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor R\rfloor}\log b_r
 \le14.4\lfloor R\rfloor(\lfloor R\rfloor+1),
\]
using $\log b_r\le3.6r$.
\end{proof}
```
