# Q465: The HM_3 frontier

## Summary

(HM)_3 is not a missing elementary estimate; it is a genuine three-modulus equidistribution theorem for the family of Apéry zero sets. The required statement is equivalent to showing that the CRT images

\[
Z_{p_1}\times Z_{p_2}\times Z_{p_3}\to \mathbf Z/(p_1p_2p_3)\mathbf Z
\]

have discrepancy on intervals of length \(X^2\) comparable with the random model. The expected count is

\[
|Z_{p_1}||Z_{p_2}||Z_{p_3}|\frac{X^2}{p_1p_2p_3},
\]

and the theorem needs this expectation after summing over primes. Thus the missing input is a power-saving cancellation statement for the triple CRT distribution.

The most plausible route is not a direct Deligne intersection count, but a hybrid of:

1. Fourier expansion of the interval condition;
2. trace-function estimates for the individual Apéry zero indicators;
3. a three-fold large sieve / bilinear form estimate.

---

# (A) Existing cross-prime equidistribution techniques

The closest classical analogues are:

* Bombieri--Vinogradov: averages over moduli for arithmetic progressions.
* Barban--Davenport--Halberstam: mean square discrepancy.
* Large sieve inequalities for sparse sets.
* Gallagher's larger sieve.

The difficulty is that here the set modulo p is not a fixed residue class. We have

\[
Z_p=\{r:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

A useful reformulation is to introduce

\[
f_p(r)=1_{Z_p}(r)-\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

Then HM_3 reduces to proving cancellation in

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}\sum_{m<X^2} f_p(m)f_q(m)f_r(m).
\]

This is a cubic correlation of sparse trace functions. The analog of Bombieri--Vinogradov would have to treat the family \(f_p\), not residue classes.

The key missing theorem is therefore a "Bombieri--Vinogradov for Apéry zero trace functions".

---

# (B) Toric representation and Deligne

The toric/K3 description is highly relevant, but not in the naive intersection form.

Using the Mellin identity,

\[
b_r=-\sum_x \Lambda(x)^r\pmod p,
\]

one can express the indicator of a zero by additive character orthogonality:

\[
1_{b_r=0}=\frac1p\sum_t e_p(tb_r).
\]

Then the triple correlation becomes a sum over three primes and three character parameters. The inner sums are no longer arbitrary: they are exponential sums attached to the Mellin sheaf.

If one can prove that the relevant tensor product sheaf

\[
\mathcal H_{p_1}\otimes \mathcal H_{p_2}\otimes \mathcal H_{p_3}
\]

has sufficiently large geometric monodromy and no exceptional invariant pieces, Deligne's Riemann Hypothesis gives square-root cancellation.

However, there is a serious arithmetic issue: the three primes correspond to three different finite fields. Deligne applies naturally after fixing a finite field. The desired estimate averages across varying characteristics. One needs a compatible family argument, not just a single-variety point count.

Therefore the toric route supplies the local square-root input but not the global prime-average step.

---

# (C) Palindromic symmetry

The involution

\[
r\mapsto p-1-r
\]

is useful but insufficient by itself.

It gives

\[
Z_p=p-1-Z_p,
\]

and therefore every CRT collision has a mirror collision. This removes possible first-order bias and implies the distribution is centered under the affine involution

\[
m\mapsto -m-1.
\]

However, HM_3 is a magnitude estimate. Pairing two bad solutions does not make them cancel because the counting function is positive.

The symmetry may help only after Fourier expansion: the odd Fourier modes vanish or pair, reducing the number of frequencies that need control. It is a useful structural constraint, not the main estimate.

---

# (D) Why the binomial coefficient counterexample is different

For

\[
\binom{2r}{r},
\]

Kummer gives a large zero set which is essentially an interval. Its Fourier transform is concentrated at low frequencies, so CRT combinations concentrate.

For Apéry numbers the observed sets satisfy:

* |Z_p| is tiny;
* reflection symmetry only;
* no visible arithmetic progression structure;
* experimentally almost Poisson factorial moments.

The conjectural explanation is monodromy. The Mellin sheaf has large monodromy (expected SL_2), so the zero condition behaves like a random codimension-one condition.

The precise statement needed is a quantitative version:

For nontrivial additive frequencies \(h\), prove

\[
\sum_{r<p}1_{Z_p}(r)e_p(hr)=O(p^{1/2+o(1)}).
\]

Together with a prime-average large sieve this would imply HM_3.

This is the natural meaning of "random-looking zeros via monodromy".

---

# (E) Three-fold large sieve

There is no standard cubic large sieve that directly gives HM_3.

Classical large sieve is fundamentally quadratic:

\[
\sum_n\left|\sum_q a_q e(qn/N)\right|^2.
\]

Higher moment inequalities exist, but they normally reduce to repeated Cauchy--Schwarz and lose exactly the powers needed here.

Possible tools:

## 1. Multilinear large sieve

Bourgain, Demeter, Guth style multilinear estimates show that independent frequency packets gain savings unavailable from quadratic estimates.

A successful formulation would look like:

\[
\sum_m F_{p_1}(m)F_{p_2}(m)F_{p_3}(m)
\ll X^{o(1)}\prod_i \|F_{p_i}\|_2.
\]

## 2. Trace-function large sieve

Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel type results are closer. They control sums of trace functions over varying moduli using monodromy hypotheses.

The Apéry problem appears to need exactly such a theorem.

---

# (F) Vinogradov mean value theory status

VMVT is unlikely to apply directly.

VMVT controls equations such as

\[
x_1^j+\cdots+x_s^j=y_1^j+\cdots+y_s^j.
\]

The Apéry condition is not a polynomial phase condition; it is a zero condition for a recurrence modulo p.

The closest analogues are:

* exponential sums along algebraic dynamical systems;
* trace functions over finite fields;
* Bourgain--Gamburd type expansion methods;
* Ostafe--Shparlinski bounds for polynomial/rational orbits.

A future proof would probably replace VMVT by a "mean value theorem for Apéry trace functions".

---

# Concrete route to HM_3

A realistic proof strategy is:

## Step 1: Fourier expansion

Write

\[
1_{Z_p}(m)=\alpha_p+\sum_{h\neq0}c_p(h)e_p(hm).
\]

Need bounds for \(c_p(h)\).

## Step 2: Monodromy input

Prove square-root cancellation:

\[
|c_p(h)|\ll p^{-1/2+o(1)}|Z_p|^{1/2}.
\]

This should come from the Mellin/K3 sheaf.

## Step 3: Triple CRT Fourier sum

The interval \(m<X^2\) has Fourier transform

\[
\widehat{1_{[0,X^2)}}(\xi).
\]

Insert the expansions and estimate the off-diagonal terms by a multilinear large sieve.

The diagonal term gives exactly

\[
X^2\lambda_X^3.
\]

The off-diagonal must be shown to be \(X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3\) as well.

---

# Final assessment

HM_3 is the first genuinely arithmetic step beyond CRT counting. The obstruction is not the size of Z_p; it is proving that the small sets Z_p do not synchronize across independent characteristics.

The most promising theorem would be:

> A Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem for the Apéry zero trace functions, obtained from Mellin-sheaf monodromy plus a multilinear large sieve.

Palindromic symmetry helps organize the Fourier analysis, and toric geometry supplies the local square-root estimates, but neither alone proves HM_3. The missing ingredient is a global cross-characteristic decorrelation theorem.
