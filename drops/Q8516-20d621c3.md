## claim
For ordered pairs, write

F_2 = sum_{p != q} J(p,q),
W_j = sum_{2^j lambda < delta <= 2^{j+1} lambda} J.

Since the shells partition the positive-delta contribution,

sum_{p != q} J delta^2/lambda^2 = sum_j W_j 2^{2j+O(1)}.

If

W_j <= X^{o(1)} 2^{-(1+epsilon)j} F_2,

then the dyadic contribution is bounded by

X^{o(1)} F_2 sum_j 2^{(1-epsilon)j}.

Thus this particular exponent alone does not automatically close the desired estimate unless combined with the available upper cutoff for delta or an additional decay/range argument. The proposed layer-cake statement therefore requires the exact truncation step; without it, the displayed shell inequality is insufficient by itself.

The claimed implication cannot be accepted as written without that missing normalization.

The asserted X=512 packet

m=98926, anchors 857 and 953, with a third label 659,

would indeed refute only the specific shell inequality if the following were independently verified:

- 659,857,953 are primes in (512,1024];
- the three residues of 98926 modulo those primes are in the corresponding actual Apery zero sets;
- the anchor pair contributes J=T=1;
- the computed delta belongs to shell j=3;
- the total ordered F_2 is 2190.

Those arithmetic checks were not reproduced here from the recurrence, so no numerical acceptance is claimed.

Even if verified, the conclusion would be narrow: it would disprove that particular dyadic atomic route with that shell exponent. It would not disprove all ACF3 approaches, all L2 approaches, or aggregate moment methods.
