# Problem 3.2 — exact fully marked seven-zero word audit

## Verdict

There is a real improvement over the *projection* used in Q7151, but not yet a new `OV-SETF` power saving.

For the generic off-diagonal, off-centre cell in which

```text
z1,z2,z3,J,K,m,m'
```

are seven distinct lower-row zero locations, the full marked seven-zero word has:

- **absolute renewal rank 6** in each lower characteristic: after saturation the six primitive equations are the six adjacent-return equations of the ordered word;
- **incremental renewal rank exactly 2** over the fixed five-zero skeleton `{z1,z2,z3,J,K}`: inserting `m` adds one primitive direction and inserting `m'` adds one more, irrespective of whether the two prefix intervals are disjoint, overlap, or both prefixes fall in the same skeleton gap;
- **inverse fibre exactly 1** from the fully marked ordered gap word plus the frozen endpoint/lift data back to `(m,m')` on a fixed order/reflection cell.  If reflection is deliberately forgotten the fibre is at most `2`; if even the mark order is forgotten but `J,K` are retained, the loss is still only an absolute constant.  The `Theta(u)` inverse fibre in Q7151 appears only after projecting the seven-zero word to one relative gap such as `m'-m`.

So the earlier inverse-load obstruction is **not intrinsic to the fully marked object**.

However, the second conclusion is equally sharp:

\[
\boxed{\text{rank }2\text{ by itself gives no new }T\text{-power beyond }OV\text{-}SETF\text{-}PAIR\text{-}K.}
\]

For fixed surviving token `t`, fixed `P={ell,p}`, and fixed ordered endpoint pair `(J,K)`, every pair

\[
(m,m')\in A_J\times A_K
\]

is already an actual seven-zero marked word.  On the dyadic block `|A_J|,|A_K|\asymp u`, there are genuinely

\[
\asymp u^2
\]

of them, and the fibre-one gap-word encoding simply remembers all `u^2`; it does not compress them.  The renewal/Bezout identities only replace long returns by restarted adjacent returns.  They do not prove a subquadratic two-return incidence theorem.  Nor do two primitive equations force two units of shell-prime valuation: the normalization-length statement in `atom_tail_section.tex` gives one unit at a simple common branch, not one unit per equation written down.

Thus the currently provable incremental exponent vector is still

\[
\boxed{(\Delta_T,\Delta_{\log})=(0,0)}.
\]

A positive power would require a genuinely new **occurrence-preserving two-insertion / two-return codegree theorem** (or an equivalent marked seven-zero moment) which is not contained in the present continuant identities or in `OV-SETF-PAIR-K`.

The centre, reflection, coincidence, and overlap cells do not rescue the generic case.  They all have at most one free prefix after the degeneracy is imposed and therefore fall to the six-zero scale `O(u)` per endpoint pair (or smaller).  They should be peeled off and charged to the already banked lower-order/PAIR-K terms, not used as evidence that the generic `u^2` cell is smaller.

# 4. Repeated renewal gives the adjacent-return normal form

This is the key algebraic calculation.

Let

\[
D_j=d_1+\cdots+d_j=x_{j+1}-x_1,
\qquad 1\le j\le6.
\]

If one roots the word at `x_1`, the naive six long-return equations are

\[
N_{D_j}(x_1)\equiv0\pmod r,
\qquad j=1,\ldots,6.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Writing them this way makes them look much more independent than they are.  Apply the connected source identity

\[
(N_d,N_e):S_d^\infty
=(N_d,N_{e-d}(x+d)):S_d^\infty
\tag{4.2}
\]

successively with `d=d_1`, then after restarting at `x_2`, and so on.  On the nonconsecutive/cut-edge-unit locus this yields the exact saturated normal form

\[
\boxed{
I(W_r)
\sim
\bigl(
N_{d_1}(x_1),
N_{d_2}(x_2),
\ldots,
N_{d_6}(x_6)
\bigr),
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

where `~` denotes equality after the same denominator/cut-edge saturation used in `proof.tex`.

Thus the primitive data of a seven-zero word are the six **adjacent restarted returns**.  Every longer return is generated by these together with structural cut-edge factors.

This immediately rules out the false count

```text

+ 15 two-step returns
+ ...
= many independent equations.
```

There are only the adjacent restarted blocks after renewal.

# 6. Do not confuse renewal rank 2 with valuation rank 2

Equation (5.7) is a genuine statement, but it is not the statement needed for a free shell-prime square.

After all gaps are fixed, an aligned return system is still being evaluated at one normalized recurrence branch in one characteristic.  The normalization-length proposition in `atom_tail_section.tex` says schematically

\[
v_r(\operatorname{content})
=\sum_{\text{branches}}
\operatorname{length}
\bigl(\mathcal O/(G(\alpha),J(\alpha))\bigr).
\tag{6.1}
\]

On a simple transverse branch the length is `1`.  Writing two extra return equations at that same branch does **not** imply

\[
r^2\mid\text{primitive carrier}.
\]

The same warning already appeared in Q7037: one common root gives one guaranteed unit of aligned-content valuation; repeated valuation comes from multiple branches/multiple roots or a genuinely nontransverse local intersection, not from the number of equations displayed on paper.

Therefore the implication

```text
incremental renewal rank = 2
    => two independent r-adic valuations
    => an extra T^{-1} or similar power
```

is false.

# 8. Why the real rank two still gives no new `T` power

Now freeze exactly the data in the question: `P`, the `z` triple, `J,K`, and the finite cell.  Let

\[
A_J=\{\text{actual common-}P\text{ prefixes attached to }J\},
\qquad
A_K=\{\text{actual common-}P\text{ prefixes attached to }K\}.
\]

On the heavy dyadic block,

\[
|A_J|,|A_K|\asymp u.
\]

Every ordered pair

\[
(m,m')\in A_J\times A_K
\tag{8.1}
\]

already gives seven lower zeros in each characteristic: the fixed three from the token, the two fixed endpoints, and the two chosen prefix zeros.  After deleting the coincidence/centre cells of Section 9, the map (7.4) is injective.  Therefore the number of generic fully marked words is itself

\[
\boxed{
\asymp u^2.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This observation is decisive.  A fibre-one encoding cannot simultaneously be a pointwise compression of the same `u^2` actual pairs.

## 8.1 One-gap degree bounds only recover the two factors already present

For one prefix insertion, the gap polynomial/root theorem bounds the corresponding actual zero set by the banked `u` scale.  Applying it to the second prefix gives another `u`.  This is the literal Cartesian product (8.1):

\[
 u\cdot u=u^2.
\]

There is no legitimate step that replaces this by `u^{2-kappa}` merely because the two restarted equations have rank two.

## 8.2 The two scalar prefix carriers do not create a height/valuation gain

For the two endpoint-prefix pairs one can form primitive evaluated short carriers

\[
F_{J,m},
\qquad
F_{K,m'},
\]

and both lower primes in `P` divide each appropriate common-prefix carrier.  Taking products or powers merely raises logarithmic height in the same proportion as forced valuation, exactly as in Q7037's `K_r | U_y^r` calculation.  Taking a gcd would be useful only with a new theorem showing that two independently moving prefix carriers have unusually small common shell-prime support.  No such cross-prefix gcd/codegree theorem is currently banked.

At the stage of this question `P` is already fixed, so even the ordinary divisor-count benefit of a small gcd cannot reduce the `u^2` number of prefix pairs.

## 8.3 Renewal gives factorization, not sparsity

The restart identity is actually hostile to the hoped-for automatic gain: it says that the full seven-zero condition factors into local adjacent returns once the previous zero is reached.  Two prefixes in different skeleton gaps are literally two restarted local insertions.  Two prefixes in the same skeleton gap give two restarted insertions after quotienting by the old enclosing return.  No extra long-return equation survives as a third transverse constraint.

Thus rank two is the **correct structural rank**, but no universal continuant identity turns that rank into a subquadratic incidence count.

## 8.4 Relation to `OV-SETF-PAIR-K`

`OV-SETF-PAIR-K` already pays for the endpoint-pair layer.  The fully marked seven-zero construction says that, over one such endpoint pair, the two prefix marks can be retained with bounded inverse fibre.  What it does **not** say is that there are fewer than the actual `u^2` prefix pairs.

Therefore the strongest conclusion available from the current source is

\[
\boxed{
\text{extra power beyond }OV\text{-}SETF\text{-}PAIR\text{-}K
=T^{0+o(1)}(\log T)^0.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

In the symbolic Q7151 notation

\[
u=T^{a_4+o(1)}L^{b_4},
\]

the generic marked numerator retains the whole

\[
T^{2a_4+o(1)}L^{2b_4}
\]

prefix-pair load.  There is no inverse `u` loss any more, but there is also no new upper-bound saving to subtract from it.  The improvement is therefore **architectural** (a lossless target exists), not yet **quantitative**.

# 9. Centre factors and every relevant degeneracy

The generic rank-two cell is not the whole event.  The exceptional cells must be removed explicitly before claiming two primitive directions.  None of them is a new obstruction: each has at most one free prefix and hence is at the six-zero/PAIR-K scale.

## 9.1 Prefix diagonal `m=m'`

This is allowed by raw interval overlap unless separately excluded by a source mask.  It gives two marked prefix occurrences but only one new zero location.  Thus

\[
R_{\rm new}(r)=1
\]

rather than `2`, and the number of such choices for fixed `(t,J,K)` is

\[
|A_J\cap A_K|\le \min(|A_J|,|A_K|)=O(u).
\tag{9.1}
\]

This is exactly six-zero scale.

## 9.2 A prefix equals another fixed zero

The defining attachment already excludes

```text
m=J,
m'=K
```

because the prefix gap is positive.  But a priori one can encounter cross coincidences such as

```text
m=K,
m'=J,
m=z_i,
m'=z_i.
```

For fixed skeleton each equality specifies at most one prefix value.  With the other prefix free, the contribution is `O(u)`; if both prefixes are fixed by such coincidences it is `O(1)`.  These cells again have incremental rank at most one.

If the current local ledger already masks any of these equalities, they are simply empty.

## 9.3 Residue collision caused by different lifts

Distinct absolute marks can coincide modulo `r` only on an affine equality

\[
a-b=(\lambda_a-\lambda_b)r.
\tag{9.2}
\]

On a frozen absolute configuration and a fixed mark pair this selects at most one candidate defining characteristic.  Such a collision reduces `n_r` in (5.6) and must be treated as a lower-rank cell, not as a seven-distinct word.

## 9.4 Consecutive zero gap

If an order cell gives

\[
d_i=1,
\]

it is empty by the no-consecutive-zero theorem.  There is no exceptional contribution to bound.

## 9.5 Reflection-centre factor

For an even adjacent gap `d`, the source factorization is

\[
N_d(x)=(2x+d+1)N_d^\circ(x).
\tag{9.3}
\]

For the adjacent endpoints `x<y=x+d`, the structural branch is

\[
2x+d+1\equiv0\pmod r
\iff
x+y\equiv r-1\pmod r.
\tag{9.4}
\]

That is exactly the palindrome/reflection pairing `y=R_r(x)`.  On this branch the second zero is forced by reflection from the first; it is **not** a new primitive return.  The raw factor must be deflated before any rank/transversality argument.

Two useful facts keep this harmless.

1. In one ordered word, at most one adjacent interval can straddle the single reflection centre `(r-1)/2`.  Hence at most one adjacent primitive block can be purely central in a given characteristic.
2. For a fixed other endpoint, (9.4) determines the reflected prefix value uniquely.  Thus a centre-forced prefix contributes `O(1)` choices; with the other prefix free the total is `O(u)`.

Equivalently, at the scalar-carrier level the central factor is one `O(T)` integer and has only `O(1)` shell-prime divisors per frozen word, exactly as audited in Q7037.

## 9.6 The two new blocks are reflection conjugates

A slightly broader degeneracy occurs when the two new adjacent-return presentations are mirror images rather than each individually central.  The reflection identity then identifies their primitive polynomial values up to sign.  This imposes an affine relation determining one prefix from the other, so there are at most `O(u)` marked pairs on this locus.  Again it is a rank-one exceptional cell.

## 9.7 Overlapping intervals without equality

This is **not** a degeneracy.

Even in the extremal clone case

\[
A_J=A_K=A,
\qquad |A|\asymp u,
\]

the off-diagonal pairs

\[
(m,m')\in A^2,
\qquad m\ne m',
\]

produce `Theta(u^2)` distinct fully marked words.  Their order may be `m<m'` or `m'>m`, possibly with other fixed zeros between them, but Section 5 still gives incremental rank two and Section 7 still gives fibre one.

Thus interval overlap cannot be used to remove a raw factor `u`.

## 9.8 Repeated gap lengths / arithmetic-progression faces

Equal numerical gaps do not automatically make two restarted equations the same: the starts are generally different.  However the source's aligned-resultant machinery has known special faces when several gap parameters coincide, and the reflection centre can propagate through those faces.  One must use the same single-resultant/central-deflation treatment as in `atom_tail_section.tex`, rather than multiply nominally repeated resultants.

When the equality of gap parameters imposes a relation between `m` and `m'`, it removes one free prefix parameter and is therefore at most `O(u)` for fixed skeleton.  When it does not impose such a relation, it gives no rank reduction and no automatic power saving.

## 9.9 Wrap/boundary/cut-edge cells

The gap theorem is applied only after the finite quotient/borrow/nonwrap split.  Wrapped prefix intervals are cut into finitely many linear pieces.  Cut-edge denominators are saturated exactly as in (4.2).  These operations can delete cells or change an absolute constant, but they do not change the generic incremental rank `2`.

# 11. What theorem would actually convert rank two into a power saving?

The next statement cannot be another deterministic renewal identity; those are exhausted by (4.3).  It must be a quantitative theorem saying that two restarted primitive insertions are globally sparse **while retaining both prefix marks**.

A suitable schematic form is a heavy, occurrence-preserving two-return codegree bound.  On each finite order/reflection cell, let

\[
\mathscr W(t,P,J,K;m,m')
=(\mathcal G_\ell,\mathcal G_p,J,K)
\]

be the fully marked double-characteristic word.  The map in `(m,m')` has bounded fibre.  One now needs an estimate over the varying heavy skeletons of the form

\[
\sum_{\text{heavy }t,P,J\ne K}
\#\{(m,m')\in A_J\times A_K:
       \text{generic two-return cell}\}
\le
T^{-\delta+o(1)}\,\mathcal B_{\rm PAIR}
\tag{11.1}
\]

for some `delta>0`, where `B_PAIR` is the exact occurrence envelope supplied by the surrounding `OV-SETF-PAIR-K` bookkeeping.

Equation (11.1) is not meant as a tautological restatement of the target: the proof ingredient has to be a concrete weighted codegree / split-gcd / aligned-content estimate for the **two distinct restarted blocks**.  Examples of genuinely sufficient inputs would be:

```text
- a weighted split-gcd multiplicity tail for the two prefix carriers;
- a two-insertion aligned-content theorem with a transverse average
  saving after central deflation;
- an averaged factorial/codegree bound for the joint representation
  of the two adjacent primitive returns.
```

What is *not* sufficient is:

```text
- writing both long-return equations;
- multiplying two one-gap root bounds;
- multiplying two PAIR-K marginal savings;
- counting the original and reflected carriers separately;
- claiming valuation 2 because two primitive equations are present.
```

The fully marked word is therefore the correct **target object** for the next theorem: it removes the inverse `u`.  But the theorem itself is still missing.

# 12. Final answer to the question asked

1. **Ordered word modulo `ell` and modulo `p`.**  Use (1.1), sort separately in the two characteristics, and parameterize every order by one of the six endpoint/prefix shuffles followed by one of the 35 merges with `z1<z2<z3`.  The merge may differ between `ell` and `p`.  Reflection reverses the word and the six gaps and gives no new primitive equation.

2. **Renewal normal form.**  For a seven-distinct word, repeated continuant Bezout reduces all long returns to the six adjacent restarted equations `(4.3)`.

3. **Rank.**  The fixed five-zero skeleton has four adjacent primitive returns.  Each genuinely new prefix increases the rank by exactly one.  Hence the generic fully marked word has

\[
R_{\rm abs}=6,
\qquad
\boxed{R_{\rm new}=2}
\]

in both lower characteristics.  This remains true for every interval ordering, including two prefixes in the same skeleton gap.

4. **Exact inverse fibre.**  On a fixed marked order/lift/reflection cell,

\[
(m,m')\mapsto(\text{full marked gap word},J,K)
\]

has **fibre exactly one**.  Forgetting reflection costs at most `2`; forgetting finite mark-order data still costs only an absolute constant.  The `Theta(u)` fibre occurs only after projecting to a single relative gap.

5. **Does rank 2 give a new power?**  **No, not from the current source.**  The rank-two word faithfully parametrizes the actual `u^2` prefix pairs.  Renewal gives no subquadratic incidence estimate, and a simple common branch supplies only one guaranteed content valuation.  Therefore there is presently

\[
\boxed{T^{0+o(1)}(\log T)^0}
\]

additional saving beyond `OV-SETF-PAIR-K`.

6. **Centres and degeneracies.**  Prefix diagonal, cross coincidences, reflection-conjugate insertions, and centre-forced returns have at most one free prefix and contribute `O(u)` per endpoint pair; `d=1` cells are empty.  Repeated-gap/resultant faces require the already-known deflation/single-resultant split and give no free extra valuation.  These lower-order cells can be peeled; they do not affect the generic no-gain conclusion.

So the sustained attack ends at a very specific boundary:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the seven-zero marked inverse is good (bounded fibre),
 but a new two-return codegree/moment theorem is still required.}
}

That is strictly stronger and more useful than the Q7151 projection no-go: the missing object is now localized to a quantitative **generic rank-two marked** theorem, not to reconstruction or bookkeeping.
