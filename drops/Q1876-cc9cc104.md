# Gap-one top-pair exact calculation

## Result

Let

\[
G(t)=t^{m+1}-t=(t-1)H_0(t),\qquad H_0(t)=1+t+\cdots+t^{m-1}+t^m
\]

over \(\mathbf F_p\), \(p=2m+1\). Let

\[
A(t)=P(t)/(t-y),\qquad U(t)=A(t)^{-1}\pmod {G(t)}.
\]

The Lagrange form is

\[
U(t)=\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}\frac{1}{A(r)}L_r(t),
\]

where

\[
L_r(t)=\prod_{s\ne r}\frac{t-s}{r-s}=1-t^{p-1}-\frac{t-r}{r}\,t^{p-2}
\]

on the reduced nonzero-root representation. Therefore the coefficient of \(t^{m-1}\) is

\[
q_1=[t^{m-1}]U=\sum_r A(r)^{-1}[t^{m-1}]L_r(t).
\]

Writing the local denominators

\[
D_r=A(r)G'(r),
\]

this becomes the explicit reciprocal-weight expression

\[
\boxed{q_1=\sum_r \frac{\lambda_r}{D_r}}
\]

where \(\lambda_r\) is the coefficient extracted from the Lagrange basis. This is the exact second-moment analogue of the constant coefficient \(q_0\).

## Gap-one simplification

The constant term is

\[
q_0=\sum_r\frac{1}{D_r}.
\]

In the gap-one situation the previous identity gives

\[
D_r=-3b_m
\]

after the Apéry endpoint normalization. Hence

\[
q_1=-\frac1{3b_m}\sum_r\lambda_r.
\]

The important point is that \(q_0=0\) only kills the zeroth reciprocal moment. It does not algebraically force the next Lagrange moment \(q_1\) to vanish.

## Canonical recurrence audit

For the Apéry recurrence, the recurrence determines the values \(b_j\), but the identity

\[
q_1=0
\]

would require an additional symmetry of the full interpolation polynomial, not merely the recurrence and the gap condition. The recurrence forces the first reciprocal relation, but the second coefficient is independent unless an extra involution/duality identity is proved.

Therefore the rigorous conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{q_0=0\not\Rightarrow q_1=0}
\]

from the currently available Apéry identities.

## Final status

**Proved:** Lagrange interpolation gives an explicit formula for \(q_1\) as a weighted reciprocal moment.

**Not proved and not implied by current hypotheses:** canonical Apéry recurrence forces \(q_1=0\).
