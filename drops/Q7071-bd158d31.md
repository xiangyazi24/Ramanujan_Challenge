# CRT error term and whether local separation helps

The proposed mechanism is worth checking carefully, but the answer is essentially **no in its current form**: the intra-prime separation of each zero set $Z_p$ does not interact strongly enough with the CRT error term to give the missing factor $T$.

## 1. What the error term really counts

For a triple of distinct primes $p,q,\ell\sim X$, the CRT conditions

$$m\equiv r_p\pmod p,\quad m\equiv r_q\pmod q,\quad m\equiv r_\ell\pmod \ell$$

have one residue class modulo $P=pq\ell$. Since $P>X^3$, the interval $[0,X^2)$ contains at most one representative of this class.

The main term replaces this indicator by its density $X^2/P$. The error is therefore a discrepancy problem:

$$1_{m_*<X^2}-\frac{X^2}{pq\ell}.$$

The local structure of $Z_p$ only enters through the number of choices for $r_p,r_q,r_\ell$.

## 2. Why separation inside $Z_p$ does not save the CRT sum

The no-consecutive property gives

$$|r-r'|\ge 2$$

for two elements of the same $Z_p$. This improves a one-dimensional spacing estimate for exponential sums over one fixed prime.

However, in the triple CRT error we are not comparing two points of the same modulus. We are comparing the unique CRT lift

$$m_*=r_p+pt_p$$

with the cutoff $X^2$.

The conditions involving $q$ and $\ell$ determine $t_p$ pseudo-randomly modulo $q\ell$. There is no polynomial relation forcing $r_p$ and $r_q$ to be close, or forcing two elements of the same $Z_p$ to interact.

Thus the implication

"$Z_p$ is separated" $\Rightarrow$ "few triples have $m_*<X^2$"

has no direct route.

## 3. The twin-atom/codegree bound cannot be inserted here

The gap-polynomial argument applies when the same prime divides two Apéry values:

$$p\mid b_r,\qquad p\mid b_s.$$

Then $r,s\in Z_p$, and the continuant structure gives a constraint on $|r-s|$.

But in the CRT triple we have

$$p\mid b_{r_p},\quad q\mid b_{r_q},\quad \ell\mid b_{r_\ell},$$

with different primes. The corresponding Apéry indices live in different zero sets:

$$r_p\in Z_p,\quad r_q\in Z_q.$$

There is no common polynomial whose zero set contains both. The codegree estimate therefore does not apply.

## 4. What would actually be needed

To gain the extra factor $T$ one needs a genuine third-moment saving, something like

$$\sum_{p,q,\ell}\#\{(r_p,r_q,r_\ell):m_*<X^2\}\ll X^2\lambda^3.$$

This requires cancellation or equidistribution of the CRT representatives.

Possible routes:

### (a) Large sieve for the family of CRT points

One would need a bound for the set

$$\left\{\frac{r_p}{p}:p\in(X,2X],\ r_p\in Z_p\right\}$$

which exploits structure across different denominators, not merely spacing inside each denominator. The known Selberg/Montgomery--Vaughan separated-set bounds are mostly intra-family bounds and do not provide this.

### (b) Correlation between different zero sets

A useful theorem would resemble:

$$\#\{(p,q,r,s):p\ne q,\ r\in Z_p,s\in Z_q,\ |pr-qs|<Y\}
$$

being smaller than the random estimate.

This is a genuine bilinear incidence statement. The current $|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}$ bound is not enough.

### (c) Algebraic deformation

The $λ$-polynomial coprimality is promising for same-prime intersections:

$$\gcd(b_r(\lambda),b_s(\lambda))=1.

But after specializing $\lambda=1$ this only says that a prime cannot divide two polynomial values through a common root in $\lambda$. It does not prevent unrelated integers $b_r(1)$ and $b_s(1)$ from sharing many prime divisors.

A height argument would require controlling the arithmetic surface

$$b_r(\lambda)\equiv0\pmod p$$

uniformly in both $r$ and $p$. Pairwise coprimality alone is too weak.

## Conclusion

The combined CRT + local-gap idea does not appear to close the third moment. The obstruction is structural:

- gap polynomials control **same prime, different residues**;
- CRT errors involve **different primes, different residues**;
- separation gives at most constant-factor improvements in exponential sums, not a factor proportional to $T$.

The missing $T^{-1}$ likely requires a new cross-prime input: a bilinear/trilinear incidence theorem for the Apéry zero sets, or a genuine equidistribution theorem for the CRT lifts. The intra-prime separation is a useful ingredient for second moments but is not by itself the source of the required third-moment saving.
