# Covariance-by-sharing audit for the third factorial moment

## Executive verdict

The sharing-pattern decomposition is a useful diagnostic, but it does **not** currently give a direct proof of

$$
|F₃(X)/6-B₃(X)| ≪ √B₃(X).
$$

There are four decisive points.

1. For the actual Apéry zero sets on the single interval `[0,M)`, the numbers `J_S` and `d_S` are deterministic. Thus

   $$
   (Σ_S d_S)² = Σ_S d_S² + 2Σ_{S<T} d_Sd_T
   $$

   is an algebraic identity, not a variance decomposition. Calling the grouped products “covariances” requires a separately specified probability space.
2. In the most natural probability space—uniformly translating the interval modulo the product of the shell primes—the exact signs are the opposite of two proposed mechanisms:
   * disjoint triples have covariance exactly `0`;
   * triples sharing one or two primes have covariance `≥ 0`.
3. Even the diagonal estimate `Σ_S d_S² ≪ B₃` is not a consequence of CRT. A prime triple can have up to `|Z_p||Z_q||Z_r|` witnesses. The repository's anchored-star construction makes the diagonal larger than `B₃` by a factor of order `X`, while satisfying all currently banked local Apéry-style constraints.
4. A variance estimate over random translates would imply a square-root bound for **most translates**, not for the distinguished translate `t=0`. Chebyshev cannot turn an averaged statement into a deterministic bound for `[0,M)`.

There is nevertheless a clean positive result hidden in the proposed route. Let

$$
Σ₂(X)=Σ_{X<p≤2X}|Z_p|².
$$

Under the natural second-zero-mass hypothesis

$$
Σ₂(X) ≪ X/log X,
$$

the random-translate variance of the triple count is

$$
≪ X²/(log X)³.
$$

This is the desired independence scale in the Poisson regime. It is an average-over-translates theorem. The missing deterministic input is an **origin-typicality theorem** showing that the Apéry interval beginning at `0` is not exceptional.

A normalization point is also important. In the current repository, `(HM)₃` means the upper bound

$$
F₃(X) ≪ X^{2+o(1)} λ_X³,
\qquad
λ_X=Σ_{X<p≤2X}|Z_p|/p,
$$

or equivalently the positive pair-Palm excess bound in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex`. The central-limit-scale estimate

$$
|F₃/6-B₃| ≪ √B₃
$$

is substantially stronger than the repository's `(HM)₃`.

## 1. Exact deterministic setup

Put

$$
P_X={p prime : X<p≤2X},
\qquad M=X²,
$$

and

$$
f_p(m)=1_{m mod p ∈ Z_p},
\qquad s_p=|Z_p|,
\qquad ρ_p=s_p/p.
$$

For an unordered triple `S={p,q,r}⊂P_X`, define

$$
J_S=Σ_{0≤m<M} Π_{u∈S} f_u(m).
$$

With the ordered convention used by the repository,

$$
F₃(X)/6=Σ_{|S|=3}J_S.
$$

The complete-period independent baseline is

$$
B₃=M Σ_{|S|=3} Π_{p∈S}ρ_p.
$$

Set

$$
μ_S=M Π_{p∈S}ρ_p,
\qquad d_S=J_S-μ_S.
$$

For the fixed Apéry data, each `d_S` is a number. There is no random variable and hence no literal `Var(d_S)` or `Cov(d_S,d_T)`.

One may average over the prime labels and call the resulting empirical products “correlations,” but that does not make `Σ_S d_S` a random sum to which Chebyshev applies.

The numerical identity

$$
2Σ_{S<T}d_Sd_T=(Σ_Sd_S)²-Σ_Sd_S²
\tag{1.1}
$$

also gives an important warning: once the total discrepancy happens to be small, the total cross term is automatically negative. Thus observing a negative cross term is not an independent explanation of the small total; it may simply restate the small total through (1.1).

## 2. The natural probability space: random translation

There is a canonical way to make the quantities random without changing the zero sets.

Let

$$
Q=Π_{p∈P_X}p
$$

and choose `t` uniformly in `Z/QZ`. Define

$$
J_S(t)=Σ_{0≤m<M} Π_{p∈S}f_p(t+m).
$$

By CRT, the coordinates `t mod p`, for `p∈P_X`, are independent and uniform. Consequently

$$
E_t J_S(t)=M Π_{p∈S}ρ_p=μ_S.
$$

For any subset `U⊂P_X`, write

$$
N_U(t)=Σ_{0≤m<M}Π_{p∈U}f_p(t+m),
\qquad N_∅(t)=M,
$$

and put `ρ_A=Π_{p∈A}ρ_p`.

### Exact covariance theorem

Let `S,T` be two prime triples and put `U=S∩T`. Then

$$
\boxed{
Cov_t(J_S(t),J_T(t))
=ρ_{S∖U}ρ_{T∖U} Var_t(N_U(t)).
}
\tag{2.1}
$$

#### Proof

Condition on the residues `t mod p` for `p∈U`. Given those shared coordinates, the private coordinates belonging to `S∖U` and `T∖U` are independent. Moreover,

$$
E_t[J_S(t)\mid(t mod p)_{p∈U}]
=ρ_{S∖U}N_U(t),
$$

and similarly for `T`. The conditional covariance is zero, so the law of total covariance gives (2.1). ∎

This elementary identity completely determines the signs in the translation ensemble.

| Sharing pattern | Exact covariance |
|---|---|
| `S=T` | `Var N_S ≥ 0` |
| `S∩T={p,q}` | `ρ_rρ_{r'} Var N_{p,q} ≥ 0` |
| `S∩T={p}` | `ρ_qρ_rρ_{q'}ρ_{r'} Var N_p ≥ 0` |
| `S∩T=∅` | `0` exactly |

Thus:

* there is no negative share-0 “global budget” covariance in this ensemble;
* there is no negative share-2 “pair competition” covariance;
* all overlap covariances are nonnegative.

The observed negative share-0 and share-2 blocks at `t=0` are therefore properties of that single realized arithmetic block, not generic covariance signs.

### Equivalent autocorrelation formula

Define

$$
γ_p(h)=p^{-1}Σ_{x mod p}f_p(x)f_p(x+h)
=|Z_p∩(Z_p-h)|/p.
$$

Then

$$
Var_t N_U(t)
=Σ_{|h|<M}(M-|h|)
\left(
Π_{p∈U}γ_p(h)-Π_{p∈U}ρ_p²
\right).
\tag{2.2}
$$

Equations (2.1) and (2.2) show exactly where an Apéry-specific theorem would enter: it would have to control the horizontal autocorrelations of the zero sets, not merely their cardinalities.

## 3. Why the proposed “budget” mechanisms are not valid

### 3.1 A fixed first-moment budget does not impose negative association

It is true that

$$
Σ_{m<M}K(m)=Σ_p|Ω_p|
$$

is fixed once the columns are fixed. But a fixed total number of hits does not make the triple counts negatively associated. Triple counts are convex clustering statistics: placing many hits on one row creates many triples simultaneously.

One hit is not “used up” when it participates in a triple. If a row supports six primes, it supports `C(6,3)=20` triples at once, including many pairs of disjoint triples.

### 3.2 Pair witnesses do not compete for extension primes

If `m₀` is a witness for an anchor pair `(p,q)`, it may simultaneously satisfy

$$
f_r(m₀)=f_{r'}(m₀)=1
$$

for many distinct extension primes. There is no sampling-without-replacement mechanism. The same pair witness can support every extension prime.

The exact shift covariance

$$
Cov_t(J_{pqr},J_{pqr'})
=ρ_rρ_{r'} Var_t N_{pq}
≥0
$$

is the rigorous opposite of the proposed pair-budget negativity.

## 4. The anchored-star obstruction refutes all universal sign claims

The construction in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` chooses an interior row `m_*` and, for `L≈X/log X` active primes, takes reflection pairs

$$
Z_p^*={m_* mod p,\ p-1-(m_* mod p)}.
$$

After deleting only finitely many exceptional shell primes, these sets satisfy the currently banked local restrictions: reflection, no consecutive zeros, endpoint avoidance, the subinterval bound, the universal pair CRT estimate, bounded row codegrees for distinct rows, and the recorded fiber/energy consequences.

Every active prime contains the row `m_*`. Hence every active triple has

$$
J_S^*≥1.
$$

Its periodic baseline satisfies

$$
μ_S^*=M Π_{p∈S}(2/p)≤8/X.
$$

For large `X`, therefore,

$$
d_S^*=J_S^*-μ_S^*>0
$$

for **every** active triple. Consequently `d_S^*d_T^*>0` for every pair of distinct triples, whether they share zero, one, or two primes.

This proves:

* share-0 cross terms need not be nonpositive;
* share-2 cross terms need not be nonpositive;
* share-1 need not be the only positive contribution;
* the proposed sign pattern cannot follow from the present local Apéry input.

It also refutes the claimed automatic diagonal scale. The anchored star has

$$
Σ_S(d_S^*)² ≫ C(L,3) ≈ X³/(log X)³,
$$

whereas

$$
B₃^*≈X²/(log X)³.
$$

The diagonal is larger than `B₃` by a factor of order `X`.

## 5. The diagonal is not a free CRT estimate

For a prime triple `S={p,q,r}`, CRT gives at most one witness below `M` for each residue tuple

$$
(a,b,c)∈Z_p×Z_q×Z_r,
$$

because `pqr>M`. It does **not** say that the prime triple itself has at most one witness. In general

$$
0≤J_S≤s_ps_qs_r.
\tag{5.1}
$$

Actual Apéry zero sets are not restricted to sizes `0,1,2`. For example, `problems/3.2/centered_residual_verify.py` records

$$
Z_{499}={67,170,203,295,328,431},
$$

which has size `6`.

In the random-translation ensemble, put `R_S=s_ps_qs_r`. Since `J_S(t)²≤R_SJ_S(t)`,

$$
Var_t J_S(t)
≤R_S E_tJ_S(t)
=M s_p²s_q²s_r²/(pqr).
\tag{5.2}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
Σ_{|S|=3}Var_tJ_S(t)
≤MΣ_{p<q<r}s_p²s_q²s_r²/(pqr).
}
\tag{5.3}
$$

The right side involves the **second mass** of the zero counts. It is not bounded by `B₃` from CRT alone.

Thus statement (i) in the question is already a new arithmetic assertion unless one assumes, for example, a uniform bound on `s_p` or an averaged second-mass estimate.

## 6. Share-1: the exact bound and why five-prime CRT is insufficient

Consider

$$
S={p,q,r},
\qquad T={p,q',r'},
$$

with the four private primes distinct. Formula (2.1) gives

$$
\boxed{
Cov_t(J_S,J_T)
=ρ_qρ_rρ_{q'}ρ_{r'} Var_tN_p(t).
}
\tag{6.1}
$$

Write `M=k_pp+u_p`, with `0≤u_p<p`. Every zero residue occurs exactly `k_p` times in a translated interval of length `M`, plus possibly once in the final arc of length `u_p`. Hence

$$
N_p(t)=k_ps_p+Y_p(t),
\qquad 0≤Y_p(t)≤s_p.
$$

By the elementary range bound for variance,

$$
Var_tN_p(t)≤s_p²/4.
\tag{6.2}
$$

Consequently

$$
Cov_t(J_S,J_T)
≤(s_p²/4)ρ_qρ_rρ_{q'}ρ_{r'}.
\tag{6.3}
$$

After summing over all share-1 pairs,

$$
\boxed{
Σ_{share\ 1}Cov_t(J_S,J_T)
≪(Σ_ps_p²)λ_X⁴.
}
\tag{6.4}
$$

This explains why share-1 is very small in a bounded-zero random-translate model: a single-prime column has almost no interval-count fluctuation because `M≫p` contains many complete periods.

### Why a five-prime overlap does not control the fixed cross product

The product is

$$
J_SJ_T
=Σ_{m,n<M}
\left(Π_{u∈S}f_u(m)\right)
\left(Π_{v∈T}f_v(n)\right).
\tag{6.5}
$$

The five-prime CRT intersection controls only the diagonal part `m=n`. The off-diagonal terms `m≠n` require the shared prime `p` to hit both rows, while the four private primes split between the two rows. These are not a single five-prime CRT condition.

Thus a bound of the form

$$
M²/(p²qq'rr')
$$

cannot be justified merely by invoking a five-prime common witness. One must control the two-row autocorrelation of the shared `p`-column. In the random-translate model that autocorrelation is exactly packaged by `Var N_p` in (6.1); for the fixed origin it is a new deterministic correlation problem.

## 7. Share-2: positive variance, not negative competition

For

$$
S={p,q,r},
\qquad T={p,q,r'},
$$

formula (2.1) yields

$$
\boxed{
Cov_t(J_S,J_T)
=ρ_rρ_{r'} Var_tN_{p,q}(t)≥0.
}
\tag{7.1}
$$

Since `pq>M`, a translated interval of length `M` contains at most one representative of each residue pair. Therefore

$$
0≤N_{p,q}(t)≤s_ps_q,
$$

and hence

$$
Var_tN_{p,q}(t)≤s_p²s_q²/4.
\tag{7.2}
$$

Summing gives

$$
\boxed{
Σ_{share\ 2}Cov_t(J_S,J_T)
≪(Σ_ps_p²)²λ_X².
}
\tag{7.3}
$$

Again, this is nonnegative. The negative share-2 block in the fixed `X=300` table is realized cancellation among the centered numbers `d_S(0)`, not covariance generated by a finite pair budget.

## 8. What FKG and negative association actually say

FKG does not give the desired share-0 negativity.

Under an independent Bernoulli model for the elementary hit variables, each triple count

$$
J_S=Σ_mΠ_{p∈S}f_p(m)
$$

is an increasing function. The Harris-FKG principle gives nonnegative, not negative, correlation between increasing functions. Rewriting in terms of absence variables makes the same functions decreasing; two decreasing functions are also positively associated.

Negative association is a different property. It must be proved for the underlying measure, and its standard conclusion applies to monotone functions of **disjoint coordinate sets**. No negative-association or strongly-Rayleigh structure has been established for the deterministic CRT-prefix measure here. Moreover, triples sharing one or two primes do not involve disjoint coordinate sets in any case.

Reimer/BK inequalities concern disjoint occurrence of events. They do not imply ordinary negative covariance for the AND-counts `J_S`.

Relevant primary references are:

* C. M. Fortuin, P. W. Kasteleyn, and J. Ginibre, “Correlation inequalities on some partially ordered sets,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 22 (1971), 89–103, DOI 10.1007/BF01651330.
* K. Joag-Dev and F. Proschan, “Negative Association of Random Variables with Applications,” *Annals of Statistics* 11 (1983), 286–295, DOI 10.1214/aos/1176346079.
* J. Borcea, P. Brändén, and T. M. Liggett, “Negative dependence and the geometry of polynomials,” *Journal of the AMS* 22 (2009), 521–567, DOI 10.1090/S0894-0347-08-00618-8.

## 9. A rigorous average-over-translates theorem

The sharing decomposition does prove something useful under the second-zero-mass hypothesis.

Assume

$$
Σ₂(X)=Σ_{p∈P_X}s_p²≪X/log X.
\tag{9.1}
$$

Prime counting and Cauchy-Schwarz then give

$$
Σ_ps_p≤√(#P_X·Σ₂(X))≪X/log X,
$$

so

$$
λ_X≪1/log X.
\tag{9.2}
$$

Let

$$
D(t)=Σ_{|S|=3}\left(J_S(t)-MΠ_{p∈S}ρ_p\right).
$$

### Diagonal

By (5.3), using `p,q,r>X`,

$$
Σ_SVar J_S
≤(M/X³)Σ_{p<q<r}s_p²s_q²s_r²
≪Σ₂(X)³/X
≪X²/(log X)³.
\tag{9.3}
$$

### Share-2

By (7.3),

$$
Σ_{share\ 2}Cov(J_S,J_T)
≪Σ₂(X)²λ_X²
≪X²/(log X)⁴.
\tag{9.4}
$$

### Share-1

By (6.4),

$$
Σ_{share\ 1}Cov(J_S,J_T)
≪Σ₂(X)λ_X⁴
≪X/(log X)⁵.
\tag{9.5}
$$

### Share-0

It is exactly zero.

Combining these bounds gives the rigorous conditional theorem

$$
\boxed{
Var_tD(t)≪X²/(log X)³.
}
\tag{9.6}
$$

In the Poisson-count regime,

$$
B₃≈X²/(log X)³,
$$

so (9.6) is precisely an `O(B₃)` variance bound.

This theorem also predicts the generic random-translate hierarchy when `s_p=O(1)` and the number of active primes is `L≈X/log X`:

| Component | Expected order |
|---|---:|
| diagonal | `X²/(log X)³` |
| share-2 | `X²/(log X)⁴` |
| share-1 | `X/(log X)⁵` |
| share-0 | `0` exactly |

Thus share-1 being small is natural in this averaged bounded-zero model. Its smallness in the fixed `X=300` table is compatible with that model, but the negative share-0 and share-2 terms are not predicted by the covariance theorem.

The hypothesis (9.1) is not currently proved unconditionally for the Apéry zero sets. It is the “natural second-mass condition” already discussed elsewhere in the project.

## 10. Chebyshev does not give the fixed interval

From (9.6), Chebyshev yields, for every `A>0`,

$$
P_t\left(|D(t)|>A X/(log X)^{3/2}\right)≪1/A².
\tag{10.1}
$$

This says that most translates are good when `A` is large. It does **not** say:

* that every translate is good;
* that the translate `t=0` is good;
* that the bound holds “with probability one over all intervals.”

A single exceptional translate can be arbitrarily bad while contributing negligibly to an average over the enormous period `Q`.

To recover the actual Apéry interval, one would need an additional deterministic theorem such as

$$
\boxed{
|D(0)|²
≪X^{o(1)}Q^{-1}Σ_{t mod Q}|D(t)|².
}
\tag{10.2}
$$

No such point-evaluation inequality is true for arbitrary periodic functions. In Fourier language, the variance controls the `ℓ²` mass of the nonzero frequencies, whereas evaluating at `t=0` sums those frequencies with no phase oscillation and requires an `ℓ¹`-type bound. Establishing (10.2) would be a new Apéry-specific cross-prime dispersion theorem.

Borel-Cantelli does not repair this. It would require a specified random model and summable failure probabilities, and would still prove a statement for almost every random realization, not for the deterministic Apéry zero sets.

## 11. Direct answers to the sub-questions

### (a) Does FKG imply share-0 negativity for the intersection indicators?

No. FKG gives positive association for increasing events and also for two decreasing events. Under a product model, AND-events are increasing. Under the natural random-translation model, disjoint triples are even stronger: they depend on disjoint CRT coordinates and are exactly independent, so their covariance is exactly zero.

There is no established negatively-associated measure whose coordinates are the relevant fixed-interval Apéry events.

### (b) Can the share-1 contribution be bounded by a five-prime CRT overlap?

Not directly. A five-prime common intersection controls only the `m=n` portion of `J_SJ_T`. The `m≠n` terms require a two-row autocorrelation in the shared prime and are not a single CRT point.

In the random-translate ensemble the exact answer is

$$
Cov(J_{pqr},J_{pq'r'})
=ρ_qρ_rρ_{q'}ρ_{r'}Var N_p,
$$

with `Var N_p≤s_p²/4`. This yields the aggregate bound (6.4), conditional on zero-count second mass. It does not bound the fixed products `d_S(0)d_T(0)`.

### (c) Is share-1 generically the smallest contribution?

In the bounded-zero random-translate model, yes in the asymptotic sense displayed in Section 9: share-1 is much smaller than the diagonal and share-2, while share-0 is exactly zero.

For the fixed deterministic interval, no universal ordering exists. The anchored star makes every sharing class positive and large. The ratios `0.055,-0.468,-0.462` at `X=300` are informative numerical data, not consequences of the sharing pattern alone.

A useful computational check would be to repeat the table after many independent CRT translations. One need not construct `Q`: independently choose a shift modulo each shell prime and translate each `Z_p`. The exact full-shift average must converge to:

* share-0: `0`;
* share-1: nonnegative;
* share-2: nonnegative.

### (d) Does `Var≤CB₃` give the deterministic square-root bound?

No. It gives a probability estimate for the ensemble over which the variance was taken. Chebyshev proves a statement for most intervals/translates, not for all intervals and not for the single interval beginning at zero.

The deterministic fixed-origin transfer is an additional theorem, not a consequence of variance.

## 12. Final assessment for the project

The covariance-by-sharing computation should be retained as a valuable diagnostic, but the proposed direct proof does not close.

What can be banked is:

1. **Exact shift covariance formula:**

   $$
   Cov_t(J_S,J_T)=ρ_{S∖U}ρ_{T∖U}Var_tN_U,
   \qquad U=S∩T.
   $$
2. **Exact signs under random translation:** share-0 is zero; share-1 and share-2 are nonnegative.
3. **Conditional average theorem:** the second-zero-mass estimate `Σ_p|Z_p|²≪X/log X` implies random-translate variance `O(X²/log³X)`.
4. **No deterministic transfer:** none of this controls the distinguished translate `0`.

The smallest new named residual for this route is therefore:

> **Fixed-origin triple-dispersion / origin-typicality.** Prove that the actual Apéry translate `t=0` obeys a pointwise bound comparable to the random-translate `L²` scale.

That residual is a genuine cross-prime arithmetic theorem. It is not supplied by FKG, negative association, CRT injectivity, the pair budget, or the sharing-pattern decomposition itself.
