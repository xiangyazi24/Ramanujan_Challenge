# Q372 — Is the Apéry zero-set Fourier transform a trace function?

## Executive verdict

The useful answer separates **literal trace-function status** from the much stronger **cross-prime dispersion theorem** needed by P3.2.

1. For every prime \(p\),

   \[
   S_p(u)=\sum_{r\in Z_p} e_p(ru)
   \]

   is exactly a sum of \(z_p=|Z_p|\) rank-one Artin–Schreier trace functions. If \(z_p=O(1)\), it is already a sum of \(O(1)\) bounded-conductor trace functions. No Apéry \(p\)-curvature is needed for this literal realization.

2. This does **not** give the needed Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel mechanism. The natural summands are exceptional linear additive characters; more importantly, a conductor bound at one prime says nothing about how the root phases \(r\in Z_p\) align with roots in \(Z_q\). That cross-prime alignment is exactly the unresolved CRT-incidence problem.

3. The interpolation polynomial is the wrong complexity measure. The raw polynomial \(1-f_p^{p-1}\) reduces modulo \(X^p-X\) to degree at most \(p-1\), but the reduced indicator polynomial has degree at least

   \[
   \max(z_p,p-z_p).
   \]

   Thus no bounded-degree exact indicator exists, even when \(z_p=1\). Nevertheless a singleton Fourier transform is one rank-one trace function, so high interpolation degree does not imply high sheaf conductor.

4. The proposed \(p\)-curvature identification is not correct. The displayed operator involving \(T\) is a **difference operator** in \(n\), not the Apéry Picard–Fuchs differential operator. Its natural \(2\times2\) transfer-cycle determinant telescopes to \(1\). The actual generating-function differential operator has order \(3\), and neither notion of \(p\)-curvature has determinant zero set equal to the zeros of one selected coefficient sequence.

5. The numerical paragraph is internally inconsistent with the project state. `drops/Q359-4a66b8d3.md` records, through \(p\le2{,}000{,}000\), mean \(z_p=1.001222\ldots\) and maximum \(z_p=12\). Parseval therefore gives typical total Fourier energy about \(p\), not \(0.91p^2\). The latter would force \(z_p\approx0.91p\).

6. The proposed fourth-moment substitute

   \[
   \sum_u |S_p(u)|^4\ll p^2z_p^2
   \]

   is automatic for every subset \(Z_p\subseteq\mathbf F_p\), with constant \(1\). It contains no arithmetic information.

7. The displayed Dirichlet-kernel estimate loses a full factor \(pq\). From

   \[
   \sum_{h=1}^{pq-1}|D_N(h/pq)|\ll pq\log(2N)
   \]

   and \(|S_p(u)|\le C\sqrt p\), absolute-value completion gives

   \[
   |\epsilon_{pq}^{\mathrm{mixed}}|
   \ll C^2\sqrt{pq}\log(2N),
   \]

   not \(C^2\log(pq)/\sqrt{pq}\). For \(p,q\asymp N\), this is \(O(N\log N)\), hence useless after the trivial \(O(N)\) cap.

8. Square-root cancellation for every individual \(S_p\) cannot imply ALPDD. If abstractly \(Z_p=\{a\bmod p\}\) for one common \(a\le N\), then \(|S_p(u)|=1\) for every \(p,u\), each \(S_p\) has rank one and bounded conductor, and all one-prime Fourier moments are optimal. Yet every pair \((p,q)\) contains the common representative \(a\), so \(\epsilon_{pq}=1-N/(pq)\asymp1\) and the pair sum has anchored-star size \(N^2/\log^2N\).

The decisive conclusion is therefore:

> **The missing input is not a Deligne bound for each \(S_p\). It is a two-modulus short-interval dispersion theorem retaining the cross-prime phases of the roots, plus control of the three Lucas channels not covered by suffix/suffix CRT.**

## 1. First correct the Fourier normalization

Write

\[
e_p(x)=e^{2\pi i x/p},\qquad
S_p(u)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(ur),\qquad
z_p=|Z_p|.
\]

The exact identities are

\[
S_p(0)=z_p
\tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{u\bmod p}|S_p(u)|^2=pz_p.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{u\ne0}|S_p(u)|^2=pz_p-z_p^2.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The current P3.2 repository analysis records

\[
\operatorname{mean}_p z_p=1.001222\ldots,
\qquad
\max_{p\le2{,}000{,}000}z_p=12.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Thus the actual zero sets are sparse in the available computation. In that regime,

\[
|S_p(u)|\le z_p
\tag{1.5}
\]

is usually far stronger than \(3.5\sqrt p\). The reported normalized maximum is therefore not evidence for Deligne cancellation; it is mostly weaker than the trivial cardinality bound.

### If \(0.91p^2\) was intended

If instead \(z_p=\delta p+o(p)\) with \(\delta=0.91\), then the zero mode contributes

\[
|S_p(0)|^2=z_p^2\sim0.8281p^2.
\]

The nonzero-frequency energy is only

\[
pz_p-z_p^2
\sim\delta(1-\delta)p^2
=0.0819p^2.
\]

Hence the average over \(u\ne0\) is \(0.0819p+o(p)\), not \(0.91p\). The peak-to-nonzero-average ratio arising from \(|S_p(u)|\le3.5\sqrt p\) would be at most

\[
\frac{3.5^2}{0.0819}\approx149.6,
\]

not \(13.5\).

The computation should first verify exactly, prime by prime,

```text
S_p(0)^2 = z_p^2,
sum_u |S_p(u)|^2 = p*z_p,
sum_{u != 0} |S_p(u)|^2 = p*z_p-z_p^2.
```

As written, the stated density, Parseval value, repository scan, and peak statistic cannot all refer to the same \(Z_p\).

## 2. Literal trace-function realization

Fix a nontrivial additive character

\[
\psi_p:\mathbf F_p\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^{\times}
\]

whose chosen complex realization is \(e_p\). For each \(r\in\mathbf F_p\), the function

\[
u\longmapsto e_p(ru)
\]

is the trace function of the rank-one Artin–Schreier sheaf

\[
\mathcal L_{\psi_p(rX)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
S_p(u)=t_{\mathcal G_p}(u),
\qquad
\mathcal G_p=\bigoplus_{r\in Z_p}\mathcal L_{\psi_p(rX)}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

A nonconstant linear Artin–Schreier summand has conductor at most \(3\) in the FKM normalization, independently of \(p\) and \(r\). The direct sum has

\[
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal G_p)=z_p,
\qquad
\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal G_p)\ll z_p+1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

This gives the precise answer to “is \(S_p\) a trace function?”

- In the broad sheaf-theoretic sense: **yes**.
- As one geometrically isotypic FKM trace weight: generally **not by this direct-sum realization**, because distinct linear frequencies give nonisomorphic rank-one summands.
- As a sum of \(z_p\) rank-one FKM trace weights: **yes, exactly**.
- If \(z_p=O(1)\) uniformly: it is a sum of \(O(1)\) uniformly bounded-conductor trace weights.

The summands are, however, FKM-exceptional additive-character sheaves. Classifying them is easy; obtaining cancellation between their frequencies across different primes is not.

### The indicator and \(f_p\)

The indicator \(1_{Z_p}\) is the trace function of a direct sum of punctual sheaves if general constructible complexes are allowed. Its finite Fourier transform is \(S_p\), up to normalization and sign conventions.

The interpolation function

\[
f_p:\mathbf F_p\to\mathbf F_p,
\qquad f_p(n)=b_n\pmod p,
\]

is \(\mathbf F_p\)-valued and is therefore not itself a complex FKM trace weight without composition with a character. For example,

\[
x\longmapsto e_p(vf_p(x))
\]

is an Artin–Schreier trace function. If the reduced interpolation polynomial has degree \(d_p<p\), the standard pullback has conductor \(O(d_p)\), usually \(d_p+2\). There is no reason for \(d_p\) to be \(O(1)\).

The identity

\[
1_{f_p(x)=0}
=\frac1p\sum_{v\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(vf_p(x))
\tag{2.3}
\]

uses \(p\) trace functions and has growing complexity. When \(z_p\) is small, the direct root description (2.1) is much cheaper.

For \(u\ne0\), one also has

\[
S_{Z_p}(u)=-S_{\mathbf F_p\setminus Z_p}(u),
\tag{2.4}
\]

so the elementary representation at nonzero frequencies uses at most

\[
\min(z_p,p-z_p)
\]

linear additive-character summands.

## 3. No bounded-degree exact indicator polynomial

Every function \(\mathbf F_p\to\mathbf F_p\) has a unique polynomial representative of degree at most \(p-1\), modulo \(X^p-X\). Thus the formal degree \((p-1)^2\) of

\[
1-f_p(X)^{p-1}
\]

is not the reduced degree: it can always be reduced to at most \(p-1\).

But there is no bounded-degree exact indicator. Let \(P_p\) be the unique reduced polynomial satisfying

\[
P_p(x)=1_{Z_p}(x)
\qquad(x\in\mathbf F_p),
\]

and assume \(0<z_p<p\). Since \(P_p\) vanishes on the \(p-z_p\) points outside \(Z_p\),

\[
\deg P_p\ge p-z_p.
\]

Since \(P_p-1\) vanishes on all \(z_p\) points of \(Z_p\),

\[
\deg P_p\ge z_p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\deg P_p\ge\max(z_p,p-z_p).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus:

- for a positive-density set and positive-density complement, the degree is \(\asymp p\);
- for a sparse set with \(z_p=O(1)\), the degree is at least \(p-O(1)\).

The sparse case shows why degree is not the right invariant: a singleton indicator has reduced degree \(p-1\), while its Fourier transform is one rank-one bounded-conductor Artin–Schreier trace function.

So a lower-degree expression is neither available nor necessary. What would matter is a bounded-complexity **geometric description** of the set or of its centered indicator.

## 4. The recurrence and the two notions of \(p\)-curvature

The operator in the question,

\[
(n+1)^3T-(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)+n^3T^{-1},
\]

is a difference operator in the index \(n\).

### 4.1 The \(2\times2\) transfer system

Put

\[
P(n)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5),
\qquad
v_n=\binom{b_n}{b_{n-1}}.
\]

Then, away from \(n=-1\),

\[
v_{n+1}=A(n)v_n,
\]

where

\[
A(n)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{P(n)}{(n+1)^3}&-\dfrac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}\\[6pt]
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\det A(n)=\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The natural characteristic-\(p\) cycle product, considered as a rational matrix, is

\[
M_p(n)=A(n+p-1)\cdots A(n+1)A(n).
\]

Its determinant telescopes:

\[
\det M_p(n)
=\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{(n+j)^3}{(n+j+1)^3}
=\frac{n^3}{(n+p)^3}
=1
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p(n).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Every finite \(p\)-orbit meets the pole at \(-1\), so pointwise evaluation requires care. But (4.3) already rules out the proposed interpretation

\[
Z_p=\{n:\det M_p(n)=0\}.
\]

After clearing denominators, determinant zeros are singularity artifacts, not the condition \(b_n=0\).

More fundamentally, \(b_n=0\) is a matrix-coefficient condition depending on the chosen initial vector:

\[
b_n=e_1^TA(n-1)\cdots A(1)v_1
\tag{4.4}
\]

for \(1\le n<p\). A determinant or conjugacy class of the operator does not remember which coordinate of which selected solution vanishes. Even \(\det(M_p-I)=0\) would mean that \(1\) is an eigenvalue, not that \(b_n=0\).

### 4.2 The actual Picard–Fuchs differential operator

Let

\[
B(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nx^n,
\qquad
\theta=x\frac{d}{dx}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\left[
\theta^3
-x(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+x^2(\theta+1)^3
\right]B(x)=0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is an order-\(3\) Picard–Fuchs equation, so its natural differential connection has rank \(3\) and its differential \(p\)-curvature is represented by a \(3\times3\) matrix. There may be a related rank-\(2\) modular system whose symmetric square yields this operator, but that does not make the displayed index recurrence a \(2\times2\) differential \(p\)-curvature statement.

Differential \(p\)-curvature concerns the generating/evaluation variable \(x\). The set

\[
Z_p=\{n:[x^n]B(x)\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]

concerns the coefficient index \(n\). These are dual-space conditions. A truncated Hasse polynomial

\[
A_p(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}b_nx^n
\]

may have genuine geometric meaning, but its roots in \(x\) are not the indices of its zero coefficients.

Accordingly, the statement

\[
Z_p=\{n:\det\Psi_p(n)=0\}\quad\text{“roughly”}
\]

should not be used. It confuses the difference and differential operators, an operator invariant with a chosen solution, and evaluation roots with coefficient zeros.

## 5. What fixed Apéry geometry gives uniformly in \(p\)

There is real fixed geometry. The Apéry numbers are diagonal coefficients of the fixed rational function

\[
\frac{1}
{(1-x_1-x_2)(1-x_3-x_4)-x_1x_2x_3x_4},
\tag{5.1}
\]

and the Picard–Fuchs operator (4.5) is defined over the integers. This can produce bounded-rank geometric objects in an evaluation parameter, with fixed singularity data outside finitely many bad primes.

It does not automatically produce a sheaf on \(\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf F_p}\) whose trace at \(n\) is \(1_{b_n=0}\). The passage

1. from a fixed period or rational diagonal to its \(n\)-th coefficient,
2. to reduction in the varying characteristic \(p\), and
3. to the nonlinear predicate “this coefficient is zero”

is \(p\)-dependent.

Automatic-sequence results for rational diagonals show that \(b_n\bmod p\) can be generated by a finite automaton for each \(p\), and they explain Lucas-type congruences. They do not imply an \(O(1)\) state count, an \(O(1)\) conductor, or a fixed trace family in the index variable as \(p\to\infty\).

### What uniformity would actually be useful?

For many one-modulus FKM estimates, a compatible system over all primes is not formally required. One may choose a different sheaf for each \(p\), provided the conductors are uniformly bounded and the relevant exceptional cases are excluded.

The present problem needs more. A geometric route would have to establish a representation such as

\[
1_{Z_p}(n)-\frac{z_p}{p}
=\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_{j,p}\,t_{\mathcal F_{j,p}}(\phi_j(n))
\tag{5.2}
\]

with all of the following controlled independently of \(p\):

- \(J\);
- ranks, singularities, and Swan conductors;
- degrees of the maps \(\phi_j\);
- coefficient sizes;
- geometric nonexceptionality and nonisomorphism sufficient for correlation bounds;
- a mechanism that remains nontrivial on intervals of length \(N\approx\sqrt{pq}\).

The recurrence and bare \(p\)-curvature do not provide (5.2).

If \(z_p=O(1)\), then \(S_p\) already has the elementary representation (2.1). In that sparse regime the hard question is not constructing a sheaf; it is proving that its simple linear frequencies do not align across many characteristics.

## 6. Fourth moments: exact identity and the triviality of the proposed bound

The fourth moment is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{u\bmod p}|S_p(u)|^4=pE^+(Z_p),
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

where

\[
E^+(Z_p)=
\#\{(a,b,c,d)\in Z_p^4:a+b=c+d\pmod p\}
\]

is the additive energy. Removing the zero mode gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{u\ne0}|S_p(u)|^4=pE^+(Z_p)-z_p^4.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The proposed estimate

\[
\sum_u|S_p(u)|^4\le Cp^2z_p^2
\tag{6.3}
\]

holds with \(C=1\) for every set. Indeed,

\[
\sum_u|S_p(u)|^4
\le
\left(\max_u|S_p(u)|^2\right)
\sum_u|S_p(u)|^2
\le z_p^2\cdot pz_p
=pz_p^3
\le p^2z_p^2.
\tag{6.4}
\]

A meaningful random-scale one-prime target would instead be

\[
\sum_{u\ne0}|S_p(u)|^4\ll pz_p^2,
\tag{6.5}
\]

or equivalently

\[
E^+(Z_p)=\frac{z_p^4}{p}+O(z_p^2).
\tag{6.6}
\]

For dense sets, (6.5) saves a factor \(p\) over (6.3). But even (6.5) does not control cross-prime anchoring: a singleton satisfies it optimally while singleton roots may coincide at one integer across every prime.

Thus no moment involving one \(Z_p\) alone can, by itself, solve a two-characteristic CRT-placement problem.

## 7. Centering exposes the zero-frequency axes

For the suffix event, define

\[
I_p(n)=1_{Z_p}(n\bmod p),
\qquad
a_p=\frac{z_p}{p},
\qquad
g_p(n)=I_p(n)-a_p.
\]

Then

\[
R_{pq}=\sum_{n\le N}I_p(n)I_q(n)
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
R_{pq}-Na_pa_q
=\sum_{n\le N}g_p(n)g_q(n)
+a_p\sum_{n\le N}g_q(n)
+a_q\sum_{n\le N}g_p(n).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The first term is the mixed covariance. The last two are one-prime boundary discrepancies. In the Fourier expression:

- mixed frequencies have \(p\nmid h\) and \(q\nmid h\);
- \(p\mid h\) forces the \(p\)-frequency to be \(0\), hence contributes \(S_p(0)=z_p\);
- \(q\mid h\) similarly contributes \(S_q(0)=z_q\).

Therefore a hypothesis only for nonzero \(u\) cannot be applied to every \(h=1,\ldots,pq-1\). Centering removes the axes from the mixed term, but it does not repair the main completion loss.

## 8. Correct Dirichlet-kernel completion

Let

\[
m=pq,
\qquad
D_N(\alpha)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(n\alpha).
\]

Up to harmless signs or conjugates,

\[
\epsilon_{pq}
=\frac1m\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}
S_p(h\bar q)S_q(h\bar p)D_N(h/m).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Since \(p,q>\sqrt N\), one has \(N<m\). The standard estimate

\[
|D_N(h/m)|
\le\min\left(N,\frac{1}{2\|h/m\|}\right)
\]

implies

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}|D_N(h/m)|\ll m\log(2N).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Indeed, for \(1\le h\le m/2\), split at \(h\asymp m/N\). The first \(m/N\) terms contribute \(O(m)\), and the harmonic tail contributes \(O(m\log N)\).

### Mixed frequencies

Assume

\[
|S_p(u)|\le C\sqrt p,
\qquad
|S_q(v)|\le C\sqrt q
\]

for nonzero frequencies. On \(p\nmid h\), \(q\nmid h\),

\[
|S_p(h\bar q)S_q(h\bar p)|\le C^2\sqrt{pq}=C^2\sqrt m.
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\epsilon_{pq}^{\mathrm{mixed}}|
&\le
\frac{C^2\sqrt m}{m}
\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}|D_N(h/m)|\\
&\ll C^2\sqrt m\log(2N).
\end{aligned}
\]

That is,

\[
\boxed{
|\epsilon_{pq}^{\mathrm{mixed}}|
\ll C^2\sqrt{pq}\log(2N).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

The proposed derivation divided by \(pq\) twice. Its own estimate

\[
\sum_h|D_N(h/pq)|\asymp pq\log N
\]

multiplied by

\[
\frac{C^2\sqrt{pq}}{pq}
=\frac{C^2}{\sqrt{pq}}
\]

gives \(C^2\sqrt{pq}\log N\), not \(C^2\log N/\sqrt{pq}\).

For \(p,q\asymp N\), the modulus is \(m\asymp N^2\) and the interval length is \(N\asymp\sqrt m\). Complete square-root cancellation therefore yields the classical Pólya–Vinogradov-scale incomplete bound \(\sqrt m\log m\asymp N\log N\), which does not beat the trivial interval length.

This is the exact short-interval barrier: a Deligne bound for complete local transforms is not enough at length \(\sqrt{pq}\). One needs averaging over \(p,q\), Burgess-type input, Type-II/Type-III dispersion, spectral structure, or another genuinely short-sum theorem.

### Axis frequencies

For \(h=pk\), \(1\le k<q\), the \(p\)-frequency is zero. The contribution is bounded by

\[
\frac{z_p}{pq}
\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}|S_q(k)|\,|D_N(k/q)|.
\]

Using \(|S_q(k)|\le C\sqrt q\) and

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}|D_N(k/q)|\ll q\log(2q),
\]

one gets

\[
\boxed{
|\epsilon_{pq}^{(p\text{-axis})}|
\ll C\frac{z_p\sqrt q}{p}\log(2q).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Similarly,

\[
\boxed{
|\epsilon_{pq}^{(q\text{-axis})}|
\ll C\frac{z_q\sqrt p}{q}\log(2p).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

These may be small in the sparse regime, but they are not covered by a nonzero-frequency hypothesis and must be handled separately.

### Parseval and the direct sparse bound

The CRT map

\[
h\bmod pq
\longmapsto
(h\bar q\bmod p,h\bar p\bmod q)
\]

is a bijection, so

\[
\sum_{h\bmod pq}
|S_p(h\bar q)S_q(h\bar p)|^2
=pqz_pz_q.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Also, because \(N<pq\),

\[
\sum_{h\bmod pq}|D_N(h/pq)|^2=pqN.
\tag{8.7}
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz gives only

\[
|\epsilon_{pq}|\ll\sqrt{Nz_pz_q},
\tag{8.8}
\]

which is still far too large to sum over all pairs.

In the sparse regime a direct combinatorial estimate is better. Since \(pq>N\), each CRT class has at most one representative in \([1,N]\), and there are only \(z_pz_q\) root pairs. Hence

\[
R_{pq}\le z_pz_q
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
|\epsilon_{pq}|
\le R_{pq}+\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq}
<2z_pz_q.
}
\tag{8.9}
\]

For bounded \(z_p,z_q\), this is \(O(1)\), but summing \(O(1)\) over all prime pairs gives the full forbidden scale. The unresolved issue is how often one of those \(z_pz_q\) CRT classes lands in the very short initial segment \([1,N]\).

## 9. Why even perfect one-prime Fourier bounds do not imply ALPDD

Fix \(a\in[1,N]\), and define abstract sets

\[
Z_p=\{a\bmod p\}
\]

for every \(p>\sqrt N\). Then \(z_p=1\) and

\[
S_p(u)=e_p(au),
\qquad
|S_p(u)|=1
\]

for every \(u\). Thus:

- the pointwise bound is far stronger than \(C\sqrt p\);
- \(S_p\) is one rank-one bounded-conductor trace function;
- the family even comes from the same fixed integer frequency \(a\);
- Parseval is optimal: \(\sum_u|S_p(u)|^2=p\);
- the fourth moment is optimal: \(\sum_u|S_p(u)|^4=p\).

Nevertheless, for every pair \(p<q\), the common CRT class contains \(a\le N\). Because \(pq>N\), it is the unique representative in the interval, so

\[
R_{pq}=1
\]

and, for \(p,q\asymp N\),

\[
\epsilon_{pq}=1-\frac{N}{pq}=1-o(1).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{p<q}|\epsilon_{pq}|
\asymp
\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)^2.
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is an abstract logical counterexample, not a claim about the actual Apéry roots. It shows that trace status, bounded conductor, pointwise Fourier bounds, \(L^2\), and \(L^4\) can all be perfect while pair dispersion fails maximally.

For Apéry numbers, a common anchor \(a\) corresponds to many primes dividing the same integer \(b_a\). Excluding that possibility is exactly the all-\(n\) content of P3.2. The magnitudes \(|S_p(u)|\) discard the phases that record this anchor.

## 10. The hypothetical small pair bound would suffice—but it is not derived

If one somehow proved, for \(p,q\asymp N\),

\[
|\epsilon_{pq}|\ll\frac{\log N}{N},
\]

then the final summation in the question would be correct:

\[
\sum_{p<q}|\epsilon_{pq}|
\ll
\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\cdot\frac{\log N}{N}
\ll\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Across the full prime window the same scale follows from

\[
\sum_{p\le N}p^{-1/2}\asymp\frac{\sqrt N}{\log N}.
\]

Thus the **implication** from the hypothetical pair bound to ALPDD is fine. The error lies entirely in deriving that pair bound from square-root estimates for \(S_p\).

There is also an integrality obstruction to a uniform tiny pair estimate in the sparse regime. If \(z_p=z_q=1\) and \(p,q\asymp N\), then

\[
R_{pq}\in\{0,1\},
\qquad
\frac{N}{pq}\asymp\frac1N.
\]

Whenever the CRT class hits \([1,N]\), one has

\[
|\epsilon_{pq}|=1+O(1/N),
\]

not \(O(\log N/N)\). The realistic theorem must permit exceptional hitting pairs and bound their aggregate number. It cannot make every pair individually pseudorandom.

---

## 11. The correct weaker substitute

For \(r\in Z_p\), \(s\in Z_q\), let

\[
[r,s]_{p,q}\in[0,pq)
\]

be the CRT representative satisfying

\[
[r,s]_{p,q}\equiv r\pmod p,
\qquad
[r,s]_{p,q}\equiv s\pmod q.
\]

Since \(pq>N\), the suffix/suffix count is exactly

\[
R_{pq}
=\sum_{r\in Z_p}\sum_{s\in Z_q}
1_{[r,s]_{p,q}\in[1,N]}.
\tag{11.1}
\]

The natural aggregate target is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p<q}
\sum_{r\in Z_p}\sum_{s\in Z_q}
1_{[r,s]_{p,q}\le N}
=O(N),
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

or the centered positive-covariance form

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p<q}
\left(
R_{pq}-\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq}
\right)_+
=O(N).
}
\tag{11.3}
\]

These retain the cross-prime phases/root positions that \(|S_p(u)|\) loses.

An analytic version would be a two-modulus large-sieve or dispersion theorem for the centered functions \(g_p\), strong enough at interval length \(N\approx\sqrt{pq}\) to control

\[
\sum_{p<q}
\left(
\sum_{n\le N}g_p(n)g_q(n)
\right)_+.
\tag{11.4}
\]

A sheaf-theoretic proof would need both:

1. cross-prime geometric nonalignment or monodromy information; and
2. a short-interval mechanism beyond ordinary complete-sum Deligne bounds.

Merely recognizing each \(S_p\) as a trace function gives neither.

### The other Lucas channels

For the full P3.2 event and \(p>\sqrt N\), write

\[
n=a_pp+r_p,
\qquad 0\le a_p,r_p<p.
\]

The Apéry Lucas congruence gives

\[
p\mid b_n
\iff
a_p\in Z_p\quad\text{or}\quad r_p\in Z_p.
\]

Thus a two-prime intersection has four channels:

1. suffix/suffix;
2. suffix/leading;
3. leading/suffix;
4. leading/leading.

The Fourier sum in the question addresses only the suffix variable. Even a proof of (11.2) would not control low roots \(a\in Z_p\) that generate intervals of length about \(p\). This is the four-channel obstruction already identified in `drops/Q359-4a66b8d3.md` and `drops/Q375-563302f3.md`.

Only in the top-half model \(p>n/2\) is the leading digit fixed at \(1\); for \(p\ge7\), \(1\notin Z_p\) because \(b_1=5\), so the event is then suffix-only.

## 12. Direct answers to the five questions

### 1. Is \(f_p\) a trace function? Is there a lower-degree expression?

- \(f_p\) is \(\mathbf F_p\)-valued, not itself a complex FKM trace weight.
- \(e_p(vf_p(x))\) is an Artin–Schreier trace function, but the natural conductor grows with the interpolation degree.
- \(1_{Z_p}\) is a trace function of a punctual constructible complex in the broad sense.
- \(S_p\) is exactly a sum of \(z_p\) rank-one Artin–Schreier trace functions.
- If \(z_p=O(1)\), this is already an \(O(1)\)-term bounded-conductor decomposition.
- No bounded-degree exact indicator polynomial exists; its reduced degree is at least \(\max(z_p,p-z_p)\).
- High polynomial degree does not preclude a more economical sheaf realization.

### 2. Does the Apéry differential/difference equation make \(Z_p\) a bounded-complexity \(p\)-curvature zero locus?

No, not by the stated argument.

- The \(T\)-operator is a difference operator.
- Its natural \(2\times2\) cycle determinant telescopes to \(1\).
- The actual Picard–Fuchs differential operator has order \(3\).
- Operator \(p\)-curvature does not encode zero coefficients of a selected solution.
- Any valid identity cutting out \(Z_p\) from a curvature matrix would be a new theorem, not a routine consequence of the recurrence.

### 3. Is there a family of uniformly bounded conductor as \(p\) varies?

The fixed Apéry geometry provides uniform complexity in an evaluation parameter, but no known uniform bounded-conductor realization of the coefficient-zero indicator in the index variable.

For many FKM estimates, separate sheaves of uniformly bounded conductor at each \(p\) are enough; a compatible system is not always required. Here even uniform conductor would still be insufficient without cross-prime nonalignment. The singleton-anchor example is already a uniformly bounded-conductor compatible linear-character family and fails pair dispersion maximally.

### 4. What weaker substitute is useful?

- If \(z_p=O(1)\), \(S_p\) is already a sum of \(O(1)\) trace functions.
- The proposed fourth moment \(O(p^2z_p^2)\) is trivial.
- A meaningful one-prime fourth moment is \(O(pz_p^2)\) after removing \(u=0\), but it still cannot prevent anchoring.
- The useful substitute is an aggregate CRT-incidence or cross-prime centered large-sieve estimate such as (11.2)–(11.4).

### 5. Is the proposed bilinear estimate correct, and does square-root cancellation alone suffice?

- If the claimed per-pair estimate \(O(\log N/N)\) were true, its sum would indeed be \(O(N/\log N)\).
- That estimate does not follow from \(|S_p(u)|\ll\sqrt p\).
- Absolute completion gives \(O(\sqrt{pq}\log N)\), not \(O(\log N/\sqrt{pq})\).
- The original uncentered formula also has zero-frequency axes.
- Even \(|S_p(u)|=1\) for every \(p,u\) can coexist with anchored-star discrepancy.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{Square-root cancellation of each }S_p\text{ alone does not suffice for ALPDD.}
}
\]

---

## 13. Recommended next theorem

The most focused next step is not to prove that \(S_p\) is a trace function—it already is one in the elementary sense. The relevant new theorem should be one of:

1. **Suffix CRT anti-anchor:** prove (11.2), perhaps first in dyadic blocks and with a controlled exceptional set of pairs.
2. **Cross-prime phase large sieve:** bound the Gram matrix of the centered functions \(g_p\) on \([1,N]\) at length \(N\approx\sqrt{pq}\).
3. **Structural root transport:** place the roots \(r\in Z_p\) in finitely many algebraic families with enough nonexceptional monodromy to rule out coherent anchors.
4. **Direct all-channel incidence:** control all four Lucas channels simultaneously.

A theorem merely asserting

\[
|S_p(u)|\ll\sqrt p
\]

is neither the minimum ingredient nor a sufficient ingredient.
