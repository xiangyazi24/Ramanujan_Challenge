# Explicit Steinberg matrix coefficient for the Apéry transfer orbit

## Summary

The proposed formula is essentially correct. The Steinberg matrix coefficient coming from the permutation model is exactly a centered indicator of a projective-line hit:

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{St}_{w,v}(g)=1_{g v=w}-\frac1{p+1}.}
\]

There is no additional hidden character expansion that makes the AFP estimate automatic. The hard part remains bounding the number of visits of the transfer orbit to the point \([0:1]\).

The Steinberg representation packages the zero count, but does not remove the dynamical difficulty.

---

## (A) Explicit formula

Let

\[
X=\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p),\qquad |X|=p+1.
\]

The permutation representation is

\[
\mathbf C[X]=\operatorname{span}\{\delta_x:x\in X\}.
\]

It decomposes as

\[
\mathbf C[X]=\mathbf 1\oplus \mathrm{St},
\]

where the trivial vector is

\[
\mathbf 1=\sum_{x\in X}\delta_x.
\]

Define the projected basis vector

\[
e_x=\delta_x-\frac1{p+1}\mathbf 1.
\]

Then

\[
\langle e_y,g e_x\rangle
=
\left\langle
\delta_y-\frac1{p+1}\mathbf1,
\delta_{gx}-\frac1{p+1}\mathbf1
\right\rangle .
\]

Expanding:

\[
=\delta_{gx=y}
-\frac1{p+1}
-\frac1{p+1}
+\frac{p+1}{(p+1)^2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\langle e_y,g e_x\rangle
=
\delta_{gx=y}-\frac1{p+1}.
}
\]

For the Apéry case:

\[
v=[5:1],\qquad w=[0:1],
\]
so

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{St}_{[0:1],[5:1]}(g)
=
1_{g[5:1]=[0:1]}-rac1{p+1}.
}
\]

---

## (B) Formula in matrix entries

Write

\[
g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
g[5:1]=[5a+b:5c+d].
\]

Therefore

\[
g[5:1]=[0:1]
\]

iff the first coordinate vanishes:

\[
5a+b=0
\]

and the second coordinate is nonzero:

\[
5c+d\neq0.
\]

Hence the explicit formula is

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{St}_{[0:1],[5:1]}(g)
=
\begin{cases}
\frac{p}{p+1},&5a+b=0,\\
-\frac1{p+1},&5a+b\neq0.
\end{cases}}
\]

(The determinant condition guarantees the second coordinate is nonzero when the first is zero.)

---

## (C) Relation to AFP

For the Apéry transfer matrices

\[
M(r)=T(r-1)\cdots T(1),
\]

we have

\[
M(r)[5:1]=[b_r:b_{r-1}].
\]

Therefore

\[
M(r)[5:1]=[0:1]
\iff b_r=0.
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_{r=1}^{p-2}
\mathrm{St}_{[0:1],[5:1]}(M(r))
=
Z_p-rac{p-2}{p+1}.
\]

So bounded Steinberg sum is equivalent to AFP up to an additive constant.

This is not too simple: it is exactly the standard spherical matrix coefficient of the Steinberg representation realized inside the permutation representation.

The fact that the representation has dimension \(p\) does not mean every matrix coefficient has a complicated formula. The permutation model already diagonalizes the relevant coefficient.

---

## (D) Can it be expanded into character sums?

There is no useful expansion over characters of a fixed subgroup that gives cancellation automatically.

The identity

\[
1_{gv=w}-\frac1{p+1}
\]

is already the full Steinberg coefficient.

One can write the delta function by Fourier expansion after choosing an affine coordinate on \(\mathbf P^1\):

\[
1_{x=0}=\frac1p\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(tx).
\]

Thus formally,

\[
1_{M(r)[5:1]=[0:1]}
\]

can be written as an additive character sum in the numerator

\[
5a_r+b_r.
\]

However this gives

\[
\frac1p\sum_t e_p(t(5a_r+b_r))
\]

and the problem becomes bounding exponential sums along the nonautonomous transfer orbit.

No cancellation theorem follows merely from the Steinberg decomposition.

---

## The real obstruction

The representation-theoretic reformulation is:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r \mathrm{St}(M(r))
}
\]

is a matrix coefficient sum along the orbit of a polynomial cocycle in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\).

For a constant matrix this reduces to powers of one group element and character theory applies.

For Apéry:

\[
M(r)=T(r-1)\cdots T(1)
\]

is a varying cocycle. There is no general theorem saying bounded-degree rational cocycles have O(1) hyperplane hits.

A counterexample exists in general: one can choose a bounded-degree cocycle whose product encodes an arbitrary prescribed orbit in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\).

Therefore the missing input must use special Apéry structure:

1. geometric monodromy of the Picard-Fuchs system;
2. p-adic overconvergence / Strassmann control;
3. a crystalline interpretation of the orbit hitting condition;
4. algebraic independence of transfer products.

The Steinberg formulation is valuable because it identifies AFP as a matrix coefficient problem, but the representation theory alone does not supply the O(1) bound.
