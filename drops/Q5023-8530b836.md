# 2. Exact two-pair decomposition

Fix \(s,\lambda\ne0\). The equations
\[
uv=s,
\qquad
\frac{1-u-v}{uv}=\lambda
\]
are equivalent to
\[
u+v=1-s\lambda,
\qquad uv=s.
\]
Thus \(u,v\) are the two roots, with order remembered by the choice of the first root, of
\[
X^2-(1-s\lambda)X+s=0.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Its discriminant is
\[
\Delta(s,\lambda)=(1-s\lambda)^2-4s.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Because \(s\ne0\), every root is nonzero. A quadratic over \(\mathbb F_p\) has
\(1+\chi(\Delta)\) roots when \(\chi(0)=0\); each choice of \(u\) fixes
\(v=s/u\). This proves (CM2), including the repeated-root case.

Now let \(F=0\), put
\[
s=uv,
\qquad q=zw=t/s,
\]
and define
\[
\lambda=\frac{1-u-v}{uv},
\qquad
\mu=\frac{1-z-w}{zw}.
\]
Then
\[
(1-u-v)(1-z-w)=s\lambda\,q\mu=t\lambda\mu.
\]
Since \(F=0\) says that the left side equals \(t\ne0\),
\[
\boxed{\lambda\mu=1.}
\tag{2.3}
\]
In particular neither parameter can vanish.

Conversely, choose
\[
s\ne0,\quad\lambda\ne0,
\]
choose an ordered pair \((u,v)\) counted by \(A_p(s,\lambda)\), and choose
\((z,w)\) counted by \(A_p(t/s,\lambda^{-1})\). Then
\[
uvzw=t,
\qquad
(1-u-v)(1-z-w)=t,
\]
so \(F=0\). The constructions are inverse. This proves (CM3) with no multiplicity factor, no unordered-pair division, and no missing discriminant-zero case.

A useful check is the exceptional parameter \(\lambda=-1\). There
\[
\Delta(s,-1)=(s-1)^2,
\]
so
\[
A_p(s,-1)=
\begin{cases}1,&s=1,\\2,&s\ne1.\end{cases}
\tag{2.4}
\]
This agrees with
\[
1-u-v=-uv
\iff(1-u)(1-v)=0:
\]
the fibre is the union of the two lines \(u=1\) and \(v=1\), meeting at \((1,1)\).

As a second check,
\[
\sum_{s\ne0}A_p(s,\lambda)
=\begin{cases}
p-3,&\lambda\ne-1,\\2p-3,&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.5}
\]
Consequently the exact torus point count is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{t\ne0}C_p(t)
=(p-2)(p-3)^2+(2p-3)^2
=p^3-4p^2+9p-9.}
\tag{2.6}
\]
This also confirms why one must not use the nonzero-character Mellin formula at \(r=0\): modulo \(p\), the right side of (2.6) is \(-9\), not the Cartier coefficient \(b_0=1\).

# 4. Teichmüller lift and Jacobi diagonalization

Let \(G=\mathbb F_p^\times\), and extend every multiplicative character by zero at \(0\). Since \(p\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\), the prime \(p\) splits completely in \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{p-1})\). Choose a prime \(\mathfrak p\) and a Teichmüller character \(\omega\) such that
\[
\omega(a)\equiv a\pmod{\mathfrak p}
\qquad(a\in G).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Put
\[
\rho=\omega^{-r}\ne1.
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)=\sum_{s\ne0}A_p(s,\lambda)\rho(s).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)\equiv K_{p,r}(\lambda)\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

There is a direct one-variable parametrization. The equation at fixed \(\lambda\) is
\[
1-u-v=\lambda uv.
\tag{4.4}
\]
For \(\lambda\ne-1\), solve
\[
v=\frac{1-u}{1+\lambda u}.
\]
After extending characters by zero, the forbidden values \(u=0,1,-\lambda^{-1}\) contribute zero, so
\[
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)
=\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_p}
 \rho(u)\rho(1-u)\bar\rho(1+\lambda u).
\tag{4.5}
\]
For \(\lambda=-1\), (4.4) is \((1-u)(1-v)=0\). The exceptional line \(u=1\) contributes
\(\sum_{v\ne0}\rho(v)=0\), and (4.5) remains valid. In fact
\[
\boxed{\mathcal K_\rho(-1)=-1}
\qquad(\rho\ne1).
\tag{4.6}
\]
Thus (4.5) holds uniformly for all \(\lambda\ne0\).

Let
\[
J(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}
 \alpha(x)\beta(1-x).
\]
For a character \(\eta\), Mellin-transform (4.5) in \(\lambda\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\mathcal K}_\rho(\eta)
&=\sum_u\rho(u)\rho(1-u)
 \sum_{\lambda\ne0}\eta(\lambda)
 \bar\rho(1+\lambda u).
\end{aligned}
\]
The outer factor kills \(u=0\). For \(u\ne0\), set
\(x=-\lambda u\). Then
\[
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}\eta(\lambda)\bar\rho(1+\lambda u)
=\eta(-1)\bar\eta(u)J(\eta,\bar\rho).
\]
The remaining \(u\)-sum is
\[
J(\rho\bar\eta,\rho),
\]
proving (CM7).

With the transform convention in (CM7), inversion is
\[
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)
=\frac1{p-1}\sum_\eta
 \widehat{\mathcal K}_\rho(\eta)\bar\eta(\lambda).
\tag{4.7}
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda^{-1})
=\frac1{p-1}\sum_\eta
 \widehat{\mathcal K}_\rho(\eta)^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Since \(\eta(-1)^2=1\), (CM8)--(CM9) follow.

Two points are important.

1. This is a **bilinear square**, not a Hermitian norm. There is no complex conjugate in (CM8).
2. The congruence in (CM9) is at the chosen prime \(\mathfrak p\) above \(p\). The Jacobi sums are cyclotomic algebraic integers; an archimedean estimate for them is not a statement about their \(\mathfrak p\)-adic divisibility.

# 5. Exact exceptional-character and Parseval audit

The standard exact Jacobi identities are
\[
J(1,\alpha)=-1\quad(\alpha\ne1),
\qquad
J(\alpha,\bar\alpha)=-\alpha(-1),
\tag{5.1}
\]
and if \(\alpha,\beta,\alpha\beta\) are all nontrivial, then
\[
|J(\alpha,\beta)|=\sqrt p.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Apply these to (CM7).

## 5.1 Nonquadratic \(\rho\)

Assume \(\rho^2\ne1\). Then
\[
\left|\widehat{\mathcal K}_\rho(\eta)\right|^2
=\begin{cases}
p,&\eta=1,\\
1,&\eta=\rho,\\
p,&\eta=\rho^2,\\
p^2,&\eta\notin\{1,\rho,\rho^2\}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]
In particular **none** of the Mellin coefficients vanishes. Multiplicative Parseval gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}|\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)|^2
&=\frac{(p-4)p^2+2p+1}{p-1}\\
&=\boxed{p^2-3p-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

## 5.2 Quadratic \(\rho=\chi\)

Now \(\rho^2=1\), so the exceptional characters coalesce:
\[
\left|\widehat{\mathcal K}_\chi(\eta)\right|^2
=\begin{cases}
1,&\eta=1\text{ or }\eta=\chi,\\
p^2,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}|\mathcal K_\chi(\lambda)|^2
=\frac{(p-3)p^2+2}{p-1}
=\boxed{p^2-2p-2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is the first hostile saturation result. The local \(\lambda\)-trace has bounded geometric complexity, but its multiplicative spectrum is not sparse: it has support exactly all \(p-1\) characters, and \(p-O(1)\) spectral coefficients have the maximal generic size \(p\).

There is an equally explicit frame identity in the other direction. From (CM2), for \(\lambda\ne-1\),
\[
\sum_{s\ne0}A_p(s,\lambda)=p-3,
\]
\[
\sum_{s\ne0}A_p(s,\lambda)^2
=2p-7-\chi(\lambda+1).
\]
Character Parseval in \(s\), after removing the trivial character, gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\rho\ne1}|\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)|^2
=p^2-3p-2-(p-1)\chi(\lambda+1)
\qquad(\lambda\ne-1).}
\tag{5.7}
\]
At the exceptional fibre,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\rho\ne1}|\mathcal K_\rho(-1)|^2=p-2,}
\tag{5.8}
\]
which is simply (4.6) summed over the \(p-2\) nontrivial characters.

Thus neither side of the \((s,\lambda)\) transform contains a hidden low-rank support which a large sieve could exploit for a power saving.

# 6. Mod-\(p\) reduction: the Jacobi sum returns to Apéry term by term

This is the decisive audit against converting a complex hypergeometric bound into actual-fibre sparsity.

Let \(n=p-1\), write
\[
\eta=\omega^{-j},\qquad0\le j\le n-1,
\qquad
\rho=\omega^{-r},\quad1\le r\le n-1.
\]
Modulo \(\mathfrak p\), direct finite-field summation gives
\[
J(\omega^{-j},\omega^r)
\equiv
\begin{cases}
(-1)^{j+1}\binom rj,&0\le j\le r,\\
0,&j>r.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Indeed the reduction is
\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}x^{n-j}(1-x)^r,
\]
and after expanding \((1-x)^r\), the only exponent divisible by \(n\) is the term \(k=j\).

For \(j\le r\), the second Jacobi factor reduces to
\[
J(\omega^{j-r},\omega^{-r})
\equiv
\begin{cases}
-\displaystyle\binom{2r-j}{r-j},
&2r-j\le n,\\
0,&2r-j>n.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.2}
\]
The same computation uses
\[
\binom{n-r}{k}\equiv(-1)^k\binom{r+k}{k}\pmod p.
\]
Therefore the product in (CM9) is nonzero modulo \(\mathfrak p\) only when
\[
\max(0,2r-n)\le j\le r.
\tag{6.3}
\]
There are exactly
\[
\boxed{\min(r,n-r)+1}
\tag{6.4}
\]
such terms. In the bulk this is still order \(p\); the \(p\)-adic reduction is not bounded-rank either.

Since
\[
-\frac1{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p,
\]
substituting (6.1)--(6.2) into (CM9), and setting \(k=r-j\), gives
\[
 b_r\equiv
 \sum_{k=0}^{\min(r,n-r)}
 \binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2\pmod p.
\tag{6.5}
\]
For \(k>n-r\), one has \(r+k\ge p\) while \(k<p\), so Lucas/Kummer gives
\[
p\mid\binom{r+k}{k}.
\]
Hence (6.5) is exactly the original Apéry formula modulo \(p\), proving (CM12).

This is not a defect of the derivation: it is an exact hostile certificate that the new Jacobi coordinates are a **unitary/algebraic reparametrization of the actual fibre**, not an independent second constraint on it.

# 7. Hypergeometric transformations and self-duality

The character sum (4.5) is, up to the conventional normalization and the change of variable \(x=-\lambda\), the finite-field \({}_2F_1\)-type trace with parameters
\[
(\rho,\rho;\rho^2).
\]
The following transformations can be proved directly, so no convention for finite-field hypergeometric functions is needed.

## 7.1 Pfaff involution

For \(\lambda\ne-1\), substitute \(u\mapsto1-u\) in (4.5):
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)
=\bar\rho(1+\lambda)
\mathcal K_\rho\!\left(-\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right).}
\tag{7.1}
\]
This is the exact finite-field Pfaff symmetry of the present family.

## 7.2 Inversion

A direct substitution \(u=-\lambda v\) gives, for \(\lambda\ne0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda^{-1})
=\rho(-\lambda)
\sum_v
 \rho(v)\rho(1+\lambda v)\bar\rho(1-v).}
\tag{7.2}
\]
Thus inversion does **not** return the same trace family for a general \(\rho\): it interchanges one \(\rho\) with \(\bar\rho\).

For the quadratic character \(\chi=\bar\chi\), this becomes the literal self-duality
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_\chi(\lambda^{-1})
=\chi(-\lambda)\mathcal K_\chi(\lambda).}
\tag{7.3}
\]
The nontrivial quadratic character corresponds to the centered exponent
\(r=(p-1)/2\). Thus the most obvious exact inversion self-duality is the already-structural centered character, not the generic balanced-start sector.

I do **not** promote (7.2) into a claim that quadraticity is the only possible geometric isomorphism class after all hypergeometric transformations; that would require a complete local-monodromy classification. It is enough for the present audit that the generic family is not literally invariant under inversion, while the one literal self-dual character supplies no generic actual-start saving.

A Deligne/Katz correlation theorem could, after the relevant no-constant-summand verification, produce square-root cancellation for a **complex** normalized \(\lambda\)-correlation. Even granting the strongest expected generic outcome
\[
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)
\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda^{-1})
\ll p^{3/2},
\tag{7.4}
\]
would not prove the desired actual-fibre statement, for the reason in the next section. I therefore do not use (7.4) as an unconditional source lemma.

No Dixon/Saalschuetz-type collapse of (CM8) to a fixed finite product is supplied by the canonical source, and the elementary transformations (7.1)--(7.3) do not produce one.

# 8. The mod-\(p\) versus complex obstruction

CC2ac is a congruence statement. If \(r\in\mathcal Z_p\), then (CM9) says
\[
\mathscr J_p(\rho)
:=\frac1{p-1}\sum_\eta
 J(\eta,\bar\rho)^2J(\rho\bar\eta,\rho)^2
\in\mathfrak p.
\tag{8.1}
\]
This is a \(\mathfrak p\)-adic divisibility condition on a cyclotomic algebraic integer.

An archimedean Weil/Deligne bound such as
\[
|\mathscr J_p(\rho)|\ll p^{3/2}
\]
under a complex embedding does **not** imply
\(\mathscr J_p(\rho)\notin\mathfrak p\). Even a uniform polynomial bound under every complex conjugate is compatible with divisibility by \(\mathfrak p\): the algebraic degree grows with \(p\), and the norm bound is vastly larger than the single factor \(p\) forced by \(\mathfrak p\)-divisibility.

There is no legal implication
\[
\text{complex square-root cancellation}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
b_r\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{8.2}
\]
To obtain such an implication one would need an independent \(p\)-adic unit/nonvanishing theorem, or an integer-valued trace with a sufficiently small absolute bound plus a proof that the integer is not exactly zero. Neither is supplied here.

No Hasse estimate is used in this answer. In particular, I do not identify \(b_r\) with an integer Frobenius trace and then infer nonvanishing from an archimedean interval.

The termwise reduction in Section 6 is stronger evidence for the obstruction: the Jacobi-sum expression modulo \(\mathfrak p\) is exactly the original Apéry binomial sum. The complex trace formalism has not removed the \(p\)-adic cancellation responsible for actual zeros.

# 9. Why the rank/conductor information does not yield CC2w

The low-conductor statement is real but lives in the wrong variable.

For a **fixed** nontrivial \(\rho\), (4.5) is the complete sum of a rank-one Kummer trace on the \(u\)-line with a bounded number of moving tame singularities. Its \(\lambda\)-family therefore has bounded local geometric complexity. This explains the square-root scale of an individual \(\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)\).

CC2w, however, sums actual starting indices. With
\[
\rho_x=\omega^{-x},
\]
a shift by the first two gaps becomes
\[
\rho_{x+a}=\rho_x\omega^{-a},
\qquad
\rho_{x+a+b}=\rho_x\omega^{-(a+b)}.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Thus horizontal motion in \(x\) changes the **Kummer character itself**. The exact spectrum (5.3)--(5.6) shows that this is not a small-rank motion: almost all multiplicative spectral coordinates remain at full size.

After reduction modulo \(\mathfrak p\), the situation is even more explicit. The function
\[
x\longmapsto Q_p(x)=b_x\pmod p
\]
is represented by a Jacobi sum with \(\asymp p\) surviving unit terms for bulk \(x\). The bounded conductor in \(\lambda\) has become conductor/degree of order \(p\) in the horizontal exponent parameter. There is no fixed-conductor sheaf in \(x\) to which a standard trace-function correlation theorem can be applied.

This is precisely the place where a one-prime complex hypergeometric estimate fails to become an actual-fibre anti-clustering theorem.

# 10. Large-sieve audits

## 10.1 Large sieve over \(\lambda\)

For fixed \(\rho\), exact Parseval already gives
\[
\|\mathcal K_\rho\|_{\ell^2(\lambda)}^2
=p^2+O(p).
\]
The Mellin support is all \(p-1\) characters, with all but \(O(1)\) coefficients of maximal generic modulus. Thus a multiplicative large sieve in \(\lambda\) returns the same \(p^2\) Hilbert norm. There is no support or rank factor to save.

The singular parameter \(\lambda=-1\) is not the obstruction: by (4.6) it contributes only \(1\) to the square norm. Removing finitely many exceptional \(\lambda\)'s changes no power.

## 10.2 Large sieve over the character parameter \(\rho\)

For fixed generic \(\lambda\), (5.7) gives
\[
\sum_{\rho\ne1}|\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)|^2
=p^2+O(p).
\]
So the dual character large sieve is also saturated. There is no hidden \(p^{-\sigma}\) in switching the order of the \(\rho\)- and \(\lambda\)-sums.

## 10.3 Large sieve over primes

Across different primes, the fields, the Teichmüller groups, and the characters
\(\rho_x=\omega_p^{-x}\) all vary. The exact coefficients in (CM7) are Jacobi sums in those varying fields. They are not values of one fixed Dirichlet character or one fixed bounded-conductor compatible sheaf to which a standard prime large sieve can be applied with a power gain.

A prime large sieve applied only to abstract coefficient norms again sees the saturated norms above. A genuine horizontal theorem would have to use arithmetic compatibility of the **actual Apéry family across changing \(p\)**; no such theorem is present in the canonical source.

# 11. Additive-energy / first-gap audit

The same-characteristic gap machinery and the new Cartier--Mellin machinery control different coordinates.

The canonical gap energy bounds columns of return levels
\[
k_x(H)=\#\{2\le h\le H:N_h(x)=0\},
\]
and its factorial energy controls pairs of such local return levels. It does not estimate the mixed moment
\[
\sum_x \mathbf1_{\{b_x=0\}}\,W_{p,H}(x)
\tag{11.1}
\]
for the balanced \((a,b)\)-weight \(W_{p,H}\). The marginal estimate \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\) cannot be multiplied into (11.1): the nonnegative weight may concentrate on the actual zero set.

The new identity rewrites the one actual predicate exactly as
\[
\mathbf1_{\{b_x=0\}}
=\frac1p\sum_{h\in\mathbb F_p}e_p(hQ_p(x)).
\tag{11.2}
\]
Thus the missing object is precisely a **mixed Fourier correlation between the local balanced-gap weight and the actual Cartier--Mellin value**. Neither the old factorial gap energy nor the local rank-two \(\lambda\)-sheaf estimates that mixed correlation.

This is why (CMC$_\sigma$) is a smaller and more faithful target than a fresh prime-tail or Poisson-spacing assumption.

# 12. Minimal non-circular CC2w target

Let \(W_{p,H}(x)\) be the literal nonnegative coefficient obtained from the CC2w balanced \((a,b)\) sum after removing **one** distinguished actual predicate \(\mathbf1_{\{b_x=0\}}\), while retaining every other actual zero predicate, first-return mask, simplicity mask, boundary mask, centered-sector deletion, and source multiplicity exactly as it occurs. If the literal coefficient is a sum over \((a,b)\), this means
\[
W_{p,H}(x)=\sum_{(a,b)\in\mathcal B_H}W_{p,H}(x;a,b).
\]
No projective root is inserted in place of an actual start.

Then
\[
\mathcal A_{p,H}
:=\sum_xW_{p,H}(x)\mathbf1_{\{b_x=0\}}
\]
is the actual-fibre mass to be saved at this stage. By (11.2),
\[
\mathcal A_{p,H}
=\frac1p\sum_xW_{p,H}(x)
+\frac1p\sum_{h\ne0}
 \sum_xW_{p,H}(x)e_p(hQ_p(x)).
\tag{12.1}
\]
Therefore the single estimate
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{h\ne0}
 \left|
   \sum_xW_{p,H}(x)e_p(hQ_p(x))
 \right|
 \ll
 p^{1-\sigma+o(1)}\sum_xW_{p,H}(x)}
\tag{12.2}
\]
implies
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{p,H}
\ll p^{-\sigma+o(1)}\sum_xW_{p,H}(x)}
\tag{12.3}
\]
for every fixed \(0<\sigma<1\). Thus the exact CC2w threshold is met if (12.2) holds with
\[
\boxed{\sigma>25/492.}
\]

This target is non-circular:

* its phase is the explicit actual Cartier--Mellin/Jacobi value \(Q_p(x)\);
* it contains no indicator \(1_{Q_p(x)=0}\);
* it does not assume random spacing;
* it preserves the literal balanced-gap coefficient;
* it asks only for cancellation of one additive Fourier transform.

It is also strictly weaker than demanding pointwise equidistribution of all Apéry values, because it is needed only against the one CC2w weight.

No current theorem proves (12.2) with any fixed positive \(\sigma\).

# 13. Hostile saturation audit

The following exact facts prevent the new identity from being mistaken for a proof of (12.2).

### 13.1 Bounded pair multiplicity does not mean sparse Mellin support

For every \((s,\lambda)\),
\[
A_p(s,\lambda)\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]
Nevertheless (5.3) shows that for nonquadratic \(\rho\), \(p-4\) of the \(p-1\) Mellin coefficients have squared modulus exactly \(p^2\). The transform is maximally broad.

### 13.2 Low local sheaf rank does not mean low horizontal rank

The fixed-\(\rho\) \(\lambda\)-trace is rank two, but
\[
\sum_{\lambda}|\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)|^2=p^2+O(p),
\qquad
\sum_{\rho}|\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)|^2=p^2+O(p).
\]
Both primal and dual frames sit at endpoint scale.

### 13.3 The exceptional self-dual character is only one horizontal point

The exact inversion self-duality (7.3) occurs for \(\rho=\chi\), i.e. the centered exponent. For fixed shifts \(a,b\), requiring any of
\[
\rho_x,\quad\rho_x\omega^{-a},\quad\rho_x\omega^{-(a+b)}
\]
to be trivial or quadratic removes only \(O(1)\) values of \(x\). This is a finite exceptional-character deletion, not a power saving on the generic starts.

### 13.4 The \(\mathfrak p\)-adic unit support is still long

Equation (6.4) gives
\[
\min(r,p-1-r)+1
\]
surviving Jacobi summands modulo \(\mathfrak p\). In the central/bulk range this is \(\asymp p\), so the mod-\(p\) formula is not a short sum after exceptional characters are removed.

### 13.5 Complex Weil cancellation is orthogonal to divisibility

Even an optimal non-self-dual complex correlation estimate does not control whether the cyclotomic correlation belongs to \(\mathfrak p\). The actual zero condition is a residue-field event, not a large-complex-value event.

### 13.6 The exact reduction closes the loop back to Apéry

The final congruence (CM12) is the original Apéry binomial sum with precisely the Lucas-zero tail deleted. Thus any argument that uses only formal algebraic equivalence of the Jacobi representation and then asserts independent cancellation has double-counted the same arithmetic information.

These are method-level saturation statements. They do not prove that (CMC$_\sigma$) is false for the actual Apéry family; they show that it is not a consequence of rank, conductor, self-duality, Parseval, or the standard complex Weil bound alone.

```latex
\begin{proposition}[Two-pair Cartier--Mellin decomposition]
\label{prop:cc2-two-pair-mellin}
Let $p$ be an odd prime and
\[
 F(u,v,z,w)=(1-u-v)(1-z-w)-uvzw.
\]
For $t\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ put
\[
 C_p(t)=\#\{(u,v,z,w)\in(\mathbb F_p^\times)^4:
 F(u,v,z,w)=0,\ uvzw=t\}.
\]
For $s,\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ put
\[
 A_p(s,\lambda)=
 \#\left\{(u,v)\in(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2:
 uv=s,\ \frac{1-u-v}{uv}=\lambda\right\}.
\]
Then, with the quadratic character extended by $\chi(0)=0$,
\[
 A_p(s,\lambda)
 =1+\chi\!\left((1-s\lambda)^2-4s\right),
\]
and
\[
 C_p(t)=\sum_{s,\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
 A_p(s,\lambda)A_p(t/s,\lambda^{-1}).
\]
If $1\le r\le p-2$ and
\[
 K_{p,r}(\lambda)=
 \sum_{s\in\mathbb F_p^\times}A_p(s,\lambda)s^{-r},
\]
then
\[
 K_{p,r}(\lambda)
 =\sum_{s\in\mathbb F_p^\times}s^{-r}
 \chi\!\left((1-s\lambda)^2-4s\right)
\]
and
\[
 b_r\equiv-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
 K_{p,r}(\lambda)K_{p,r}(\lambda^{-1})\pmod p.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $(s,\lambda)$, the ordered pair $(u,v)$ is determined by
choosing the first root of
\[
 X^2-(1-s\lambda)X+s,
\]
whose discriminant is $(1-s\lambda)^2-4s$.  This gives the formula
for $A_p$.  On $F=0$, writing $s=uv$, $q=zw$ and
\[
 \lambda=\frac{1-u-v}{uv},\qquad
 \mu=\frac{1-z-w}{zw},
\]
one has $uvzw\,\lambda\mu=uvzw$, hence $\lambda\mu=1$.
This proves the convolution formula for $C_p$.

For $1\le r\le p-2$, the Cartier diagonal and
$\mathbf1_{F=0}=1-F^{p-1}$ give
\[
 \sum_{F=0}(uvzw)^{-r}\equiv-b_r\pmod p:
\]
the constant torus sum vanishes, while multiaffinity of $F$ makes the
second torus sum select only
$[u^rv^rz^rw^r]F^{p-1}$.  Mellin-transforming the convolution in $t$
therefore gives the stated bilinear identity.  Finally
$\sum_{s\ne0}s^{-r}=0$, so the constant term in $A_p$ disappears.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Jacobi diagonalization of the two-pair transform]
\label{prop:cc2-jacobi-diagonal}
Choose a Teichmuller character
$\omega:\mathbb F_p^\times\to\mu_{p-1}$ and a prime
$\mathfrak p\mid p$ with $\omega(a)\equiv a\pmod{\mathfrak p}$.
For $1\le r\le p-2$ put $\rho=\omega^{-r}$, and extend all
multiplicative characters by zero at zero.  Define
\[
 \mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)
 =\sum_{s\ne0}A_p(s,\lambda)\rho(s).
\]
Then
\[
 \mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)
 =\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}
 \rho(x)\rho(1-x)\bar\rho(1+\lambda x),
\]
and, for every multiplicative character $\eta$,
\[
 \sum_{\lambda\ne0}\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)\eta(\lambda)
 =\eta(-1)J(\eta,\bar\rho)J(\rho\bar\eta,\rho).
\]
Consequently
\[
 b_r\equiv-rac1{p-1}\sum_\eta
 J(\eta,\omega^r)^2
 J(\omega^{-r}\bar\eta,\omega^{-r})^2
 \pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]
If $\rho^2\ne1$, then
\[
 \sum_{\lambda\ne0}|\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)|^2
 =p^2-3p-1;
\]
if $\rho$ is quadratic, the corresponding identity is
\[
 \sum_{\lambda\ne0}|\mathcal K_\rho(\lambda)|^2
 =p^2-2p-2.
\]
Moreover, for $\rho^2\ne1$ every multiplicative Mellin coefficient is
nonzero; its squared modulus is $p$ at $\eta=1,\rho^2$, is $1$ at
$\eta=\rho$, and is $p^2$ otherwise.
\end{proposition}
```

I would insert the first proposition unconditionally. The second is also exact, but its role should be described as a structural diagonalization/saturation lemma, **not** as an anti-clustering theorem.

# Conclusion

The two-pair idea is valid and worth making canonical. It converts the actual Cartier torus fibre into an exact multiplicative convolution and then diagonalizes that convolution by a pair of Jacobi sums. It also exposes the correct exceptional geometry: the only literal inversion-self-dual nontrivial character is the quadratic/centered one, while the generic \(\lambda\)-trace is a rank-two bounded-conductor hypergeometric object.

But the same calculation supplies its own no-go audit. The rank-two trace has full Mellin support and exact \(p^2\) energy; after reduction at \(\mathfrak p\), its Jacobi formula becomes the original Apéry binomial sum with \(\asymp p\) surviving terms in the bulk. Thus local low conductor does not become low complexity in the actual-start exponent. Even an optimal complex sheaf correlation estimate would not imply a mod-\(p\) nonvanishing statement.

Accordingly I obtain no unconditional \(p^{-\sigma}\) factor in CC2w. The sharp new exact identity is (CM9), together with the saturation formulas (CM10)--(CM12). The smallest next theorem is the weighted additive correlation (CMC$_\sigma$) for the literal balanced-gap CC2w coefficient, with \(\sigma>25/492\). That target is genuinely about the actual Cartier--Mellin value and is not a repackaged projective-root or zero-count hypothesis.
