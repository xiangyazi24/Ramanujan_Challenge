## Executive verdict

1. **The alleged citation is misattributed.** arXiv:2510.23298 is by **Xavier Caruso, Florian Fürnsinn, Daniel Vargas-Montoya, and Wadim Zudilin**. The acronym CFVZ happens still to fit, but the authors are not Chen–Feng–Vsemirnov–Zhang. Their Theorem 2 proves a polynomial square-factorization of the Apéry truncation. It does **not** construct a rank-2 Frobenius-trace function called \(S_p\), an l-adic sheaf, or an overconvergent F-isocrystal.

2. **The Franel differential equation is genuinely geometric and elliptic.** The Franel series is an algebraic pullback of the Hesse-pencil elliptic Picard–Fuchs equation

   ```text
   theta_z^2 - z (theta_z + 1/3)(theta_z + 2/3).
   ```

   Equivalently, after the Beukers degree-two pullback, it is the Picard–Fuchs local system of the modular elliptic family for Gamma_1(6). Thus there is a natural fixed rank-2 elliptic local system/sheaf of bounded conductor behind the characteristic-zero Franel series.

3. **\(H_p(x)\) is a Hasse–Witt scalar, not an l-adic trace.** With the Hodge trivialization whose period is the Franel series, \(H_p\) is the Hasse–Witt polynomial of the pulled-back Hesse family. This is a semilinear Frobenius matrix entry modulo \(p\). On smooth \(\mathbf F_p\)-fibres it is congruent, after fixing Frobenius conventions, to the elliptic Frobenius trace modulo \(p\); over extension fields the correct invariant is a Frobenius-orbit product, not the single value \(H_p(x)\). The rank-1 unit-root object exists only on the ordinary locus and therefore is not automatically a fixed bounded-conductor object.

4. **A square of traces is not a symmetric-square trace.** For a rank-2 sheaf \(\mathcal V\) with trace \(a\) and determinant \(d\),

   ```text
   Tr(V tensor V) = a^2,
   Tr(Sym^2 V)    = a^2 - d,
   V tensor V     = Sym^2 V direct-sum det(V).
   ```

   The exact trace-square object is rank 4. A rank-3 symmetric square agrees only modulo \(p\) on degree-one elliptic fibres because \(d=p\) up to a finite character. Consequently the mod-\(p\) data do not determine whether “FranelSquare” means a rank-1 unit-root square, a rank-3 symmetric square, or a rank-4 tensor square.

5. **The Apéry rank-2 differential local system is explicit.** Beukers gives the rank-2 equation whose symmetric square is the Apéry third-order equation, its four singularities, its local exponents, and the quadratic pullback to the Gamma_1(6) elliptic family. Its two normalized formal branches

   ```text
   tau   = sqrt(F),
   sigma = sqrt(F/q)
   ```

   correspond to rank-2 local systems differing by the fixed quadratic Kummer sheaf attached to \(q(t)\). This does not make the finite polynomial \(B_p\) a Frobenius trace.

6. **The fixed geometric pushforward is reducible in a completely explicit way.** Let \(\mathcal V_	au\) be the Apéry rank-2 local system, let

   ```text
   K_q = quadratic Kummer sheaf of y^2 = q(t),
   A   = Sym^2(V_tau).
   ```

   Then the Franel symmetric-square pushforward satisfies

   ```text
   phi_* Sym^2(V_Franel)  =  A  direct-sum  (A tensor K_q)
   ```

   on the common lisse open, followed by middle extension. The exact tensor-square version is

   ```text
   phi_* (V_Franel tensor V_Franel)
       = (V_tau tensor V_tau)
         direct-sum
         (V_tau tensor V_tau tensor K_q).
   ```

   Hence the \(q\)-quadratic companion is a canonical constituent of the pushforward, not an unrelated sheaf.

7. **For the standard Mellin Kummer twists \(\mathcal L_\chi(t)\), every relevant constituent has trivial self-twist group.** In particular, no character of unbounded order can stabilize one of these fixed sheaves. More generally, determinant already forces the order of a self-twisting Kummer character to divide the rank.

8. **There is nevertheless one fixed rational-function Kummer symmetry.** The full pushforward is stabilized by \(\mathcal K_q\), which swaps its two summands. This is a quadratic Kummer sheaf ramified at the two roots of \(q\); it is **not** a standard Mellin sheaf \(\mathcal L_\chi(t)\), which is ramified only at \(0\) and infinity.

9. **The main unresolved assertion remains arithmetic, not monodromic.** No verified source used here proves that \(A_p(t)\), the CFVZ polynomial \(B_p(t)\), or the repository’s \(S_p(t)\) is the exact Frobenius trace of the fixed sheaves just described. The local monodromy and twist classification can be completed for the natural candidate sheaves, but connecting those sheaves to the finite polynomials requires an additional Hasse–Witt/crystalline comparison theorem with explicit normalizations.

[PROVED-SOURCE] Official arXiv metadata records the initial submission on 27 October 2025 and the authors Xavier Caruso, Florian Fürnsinn, Daniel Vargas-Montoya, and Wadim Zudilin. See the source list at the end.


The paper calls this polynomial \(B_p\), not a Frobenius trace \(S_p\). It studies Kummer extensions of the mod-\(p\) generating series. It contains no construction of:

- an l-adic rank-2 sheaf whose trace is \(B_p(t)\);
- a compatible system over varying \(p\);
- an overconvergent F-isocrystal with Frobenius trace \(B_p(t)\);

Thus the claim “CFVZ express \(A_p\) as square(s) of a rank-2 trace \(S_p\)” is **not** a statement of that paper.

### 1.2 An apparent inversion typo in the published proof

Let \(F(t)=\sum b_nt^n\) be the Apéry series, \(h(x)\) the Franel series, and \(H_p(x)\) its truncation. CFVZ use

```text
F(phi(x)) = (1+x) h(x)^2,
F(t)      = A_p(t) F(t)^p,
h(x)      = H_p(x) h(x)^p
```

in characteristic \(p\). Therefore, as an identity in \(\mathbf F_p(x)\),

```text
A_p(phi(x))
  = F(phi(x))^(1-p)
  = (1+x)^(1-p) H_p(x)^2
  = H_p(x)^2 / (1+x)^(p-1).                 (1.1)
```

[DEDUCTION] This follows immediately from the three displayed identities and is also the factor used in the repository’s `FABLE_SECTION_apparition_tower.tex`.

The arXiv proof of Theorem 2, printed p. 7, and the published proof of Theorem 1.2 instead write \(f^e=(1+x)^eH\) and \(A_p=(1+x)^{p-1}H^2\). This is inconsistent with the paper’s preceding relation \(h=Hh^p\), from which it correctly records \(h^2=H^{-1/e}\). The algebraically consistent statements are

```text
f^e = (1+x)^e / H,
A_p = H^2 / (1+x)^(p-1).
```

This apparent inversion typo does **not** affect CFVZ’s square-factor theorem: both \((1+x)^{p-1}\) and its inverse are squares in \(\mathbf F_p(x)\), because \(p-1\) is even. It also does not affect the repository’s degree-one pointwise tests, because for \(x\in\mathbf F_p\setminus\{-1\}\), Fermat gives \((1+x)^{p-1}=1\). It matters decisively over extension fields and therefore matters for any sheaf-theoretic claim.

In particular,

```text
A_p(phi(x)) = H_p(x)^2
```

is a valid identity of functions on the indicated \(\mathbf F_p\)-points, but it is not the exact rational-function identity and it is not an all-closed-points trace identity.

### 1.3 What can be deduced about \(B_p\), and what cannot

Normalize \(B_p(0)=1\). Formal square-root uniqueness gives

```text
B_p = truncation of tau = sqrt(F) through degree (p-1)/2
      in the classes 1,5,7,11 mod 24;

B_p = truncation of sigma = sqrt(F/q) through degree (p-3)/2
      in the classes 13,17,19,23 mod 24.                 (1.2)
```

This deduction is legitimate because \(A_p\) agrees coefficientwise with \(F\) below degree \(p\), \(q(0)=1\), and \(p\) is odd. It is a statement about formal power-series coefficients modulo \(p\). It is not a construction of a Frobenius trace.

Two different quadratic characters must not be conflated:

- \(({-6}/p)\) is a constant depending on the prime and decides which branch in (1.2) occurs;
- \(\chi_2(q(t))\) is the trace function of a fixed quadratic Kummer sheaf in the variable \(t\).

## 2. Framing: Hasse–Witt scalar, unit root, and l-adic trace are different objects

### 2.1 The concrete elliptic family behind the Franel series

[PROVED-SOURCE] The Hesse pencil is

```text
E_psi: X^3 + Y^3 + Z^3 - 3 psi XYZ = 0.
```

With \(z=\psi^{-3}\), its rank-2 Picard–Fuchs operator is

```text
L_Hesse = theta_z^2 - z (theta_z + 1/3)(theta_z + 2/3).     (2.1)
```

Jie Zhou gives the pencil and its Picard–Fuchs operator in equations (1.1), (1.2), and (1.9), and records the period

```text
G(z) = 2F1(1/3, 2/3; 1; z)                                (2.2)
```

and the cusp/orbifold singularities in equation (2.12) and the surrounding text.

[PROVED-SOURCE] CFVZ record the classical Franel pullback

```text
z(x) = 27 x^2 / (1-2x)^3,
h(x) = G(z(x)) / (1-2x).                                  (2.3)
```

Thus the Franel local system is an elliptic Picard–Fuchs local system, not intrinsically a K3 local system. Squaring it produces a weight-2 tensor or symmetric-square object, but an actual K3 realization requires an additional algebraic construction and is not supplied by CFVZ.

### 2.2 \(H_p\) is the Hasse–Witt scalar in a specified Hodge basis

[PROVED-SOURCE] Huang–Lian–Yau–Yu treat the Dwork family

```text
X_0^(n+1) + ... + X_n^(n+1) - (n+1)t X_0...X_n = 0
```

and show that its period is the hypergeometric series in their equation (1.3), while the Hasse–Witt matrix is its truncation, equation (1.4). For \(n=2\), this is the Hesse cubic and the period is (2.2).

Let \(G_p(z)\) denote the corresponding truncation. CFVZ derive, in the proof of their Lemma 7 (published Lemma 2.3),

```text
H_p(x) = (1-2x)^(p-1) G_p(z(x)).                           (2.4)
```

This factor is exactly the Hodge-basis transformation law. Indeed, if a Hodge section is changed from \(\omega\) to \(\omega'=c\omega\), then its dual changes by \(c^{-1}\). Because Frobenius is \(p\)-semilinear, a Hasse–Witt scalar \(a\) changes as

```text
a' = c^(1-p) a.                                            (2.5)
```

Taking \(c=(1-2x)^{-1}\) gives (2.4). Therefore:

[DEDUCTION] **On the smooth Franel base and with the Hodge trivialization whose period is \(h(x)\), \(H_p(x)\) is the Hasse–Witt scalar of the pulled-back Hesse elliptic family.**

That is a precise and useful statement. It is not the statement that \(H_p\) is an l-adic trace function.

### 2.3 Why a Hasse invariant is not a trace function

A Hasse–Witt scalar is:

- defined in characteristic \(p\);
- a matrix entry of a semilinear Frobenius/Cartier map;
- dependent on a Hodge trivialization as in (2.5);
- only the first mod-\(p\) approximation to a crystalline Frobenius eigenvalue.

An l-adic trace function is:

- the trace of linear geometric or arithmetic Frobenius on an l-adic stalk;
- invariant under a choice of Hodge basis;
- defined compatibly at every closed point and over every finite extension;
- valued in a fixed characteristic-zero coefficient field.

On a smooth elliptic fibre over \(\mathbf F_p\), let \(a_x\) be the Frobenius trace. After fixing the usual sign/Frobenius convention,

```text
a_x = H_p(x) mod p.                                        (2.6)
```

This congruence is basis-independent at degree one because \(c(x)^{p-1}=1\) for \(c(x)\in\mathbf F_p^	imes\). It does not turn the polynomial \(H_p\) into the exact trace \(a_x\).

For a closed point of degree \(r\), the semilinear coefficient must be multiplied around the Frobenius orbit. Huang–Lian–Yau–Yu’s equations (1.5)–(1.8), and more generally Theorem 1.5 and equation (1.12), give the unit root as a product of Dwork ratios at the Teichmüller lifts of

```text
x, x^p, ..., x^(p^(r-1)).
```

Modulo \(p\), this is an orbit product of Hasse–Witt factors. It is not the single polynomial value \(H_p(x)\). This is the precise extension-field obstruction missed by a test that loops only over \(x\in\mathbf F_p\).

### 2.4 The unit-root line is not automatically the desired bounded object

On the ordinary locus \(H_p
e0\), crystalline cohomology has a rank-1 unit-root subobject. Its Frobenius eigenvalue reduces to the Hasse invariant. However:

- the ordinary locus depends on \(p\);
- its complement is the moving supersingular locus;
- the unit-root line need not extend as a rank-1 overconvergent object across that locus;
- deleting all supersingular points does not give a fixed singular set or an a priori uniform conductor.

Thus “take the unit-root sheaf whose trace is \(H_p\)” does not solve the fixed bounded-conductor problem. The fixed object is the full rank-2 elliptic cohomology sheaf/isocrystal; \(H_p\) is only its mod-\(p\) Hasse datum.

### 2.5 Square versus symmetric square

Let \(\mathcal V\) be a rank-2 Frobenius object. At a closed point write

```text
trace(V) = a,
det(V)   = d.
```

Then

```text
trace(V tensor V) = a^2,
trace(Sym^2 V)    = a^2 - d.                               (2.7)
```

For standard elliptic \(H^1\), \(d\) is the residue-field cardinality, possibly multiplied by a finite character after descent. Hence at an \(\mathbf F_p\)-point

```text
trace(Sym^2 V) = a^2 mod p,
```

but not exactly in characteristic zero. Three different candidates therefore share the same first mod-\(p\) shadow:

1. the square of the rank-1 unit-root object, only on the ordinary locus;
2. the rank-3 object \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal V\);
3. the rank-4 object \(\mathcal V\otimes\mathcal V\), whose trace is exactly \(a^2\).

The polynomial \(H_p^2\) by itself does not choose among them.

### 2.6 Extra data required before the project may say “trace sheaf”

A rigorous normalization must specify all of the following.

1. **Integral algebraic family.** Give a smooth proper elliptic family, or a precise cohomological summand, over a fixed open of a model over \(\mathbf Z[1/6]\). For the Franel side one may use the Hesse pencil with descent data or the Gamma_1(6) modular elliptic family.

2. **Realization.** Define \(\mathcal V_\ell=R^1\pi_*\mathbf Q_\ell\) for \(\ell
e p\), or the corresponding relative rigid/crystalline cohomology object, including coefficient field and integral lattice.

3. **Frobenius convention.** State geometric versus arithmetic Frobenius, Tate twists, and any geometrically constant character introduced by descent.

4. **Hodge trivialization.** Specify the differential whose period is \(h\), \(	au\), or \(F\), and prove the claimed Hasse–Witt polynomial in that basis.

5. **Exact lift of the square.** Choose rank 1, 3, or 4 as explained above and include the determinant correction if rank 3 is used.

6. **All-closed-points compatibility.** Prove a trace or trace-congruence formula at every closed point, not only at \(\mathbf F_p\)-rational points. A degree-one polynomial identity is insufficient.

7. **Finite pushforward and middle extension.** Restrict to the finite étale locus of \(\phi\), take finite direct image there, and state the middle-extension convention at \(0,\infty\), and the two roots of \(q\).

8. **p-adic ordinary/supersingular treatment.** If a unit-root subobject is used, prove its overconvergence and control its extension or conductor at the moving supersingular points.

9. **Apéry comparison.** Prove that \(A_p(t)\) is the Hasse scalar or the reduction of the trace of the chosen fixed rank-3/rank-4 object. CFVZ’s polynomial square-factorization does not prove this.

## 5. Pullback and pushforward: the fixed geometric decomposition

### 5.1 Rank-2 pullback relation

The characteristic-zero identity

```text
F(phi(x))=(1+x)h(x)^2
```

and normalized square roots give

```text
tau(phi(x))=sqrt(1+x) h(x).                               (5.1)
```

Let \(\mathcal M\) be the quadratic Kummer sheaf of \(1+x\) on the \(x\)-line. Comparing the two irreducible second-order differential modules, or directly comparing the local monodromy after Beukers’s pullback, gives

```text
phi^* V_tau = V_Franel tensor M.                           (5.2)
```

This is a statement about the fixed geometric local systems. Arithmetic realizations may differ by a geometrically constant character unless descent conventions are fixed.

Because \(\mathcal M^2=1\), both symmetric and tensor squares eliminate this auxiliary twist:

```text
phi^* Sym^2(V_tau)        = Sym^2(V_Franel),
phi^* (V_tau tensor V_tau)= V_Franel tensor V_Franel.      (5.3)
```

### 5.2 Symmetric-square pushforward

Define

```text
A   = Sym^2(V_tau),
A_q = A tensor K_q,
W   = Sym^2(V_Franel).                                    (5.4)
```

On

```text
U_t = P^1_t minus {0, infinity, alpha_+, alpha_-},
```

the map \(\phi\) is finite étale of degree 2 and its covering character is \(\mathcal K_q\). Hence

```text
phi_* 1 = 1 direct-sum K_q.                               (5.5)
```

Using (5.3) and the projection formula,

```text
P_sym := phi_* W
       = A tensor (phi_*1)
       = A direct-sum A_q.                                (5.6)
```

This is the central fixed-sheaf identity. The direct image is then middle-extended across the four boundary points.

For every closed point in the lisse open,

```text
Tr(P_sym,t) = (1 + Tr(K_q,t)) Tr(A,t).                    (5.7)
```

At \(t\in\mathbf F_p\) with \(q(t)
e0\), this is

```text
Tr(P_sym,t) = (1+chi_2(q(t))) Tr(A,t).                    (5.8)
```

This is the sheaf-theoretic version of the fibre count. It is valid at all closed points once the arithmetic realization of \(\mathcal A\) is fixed.

### 5.3 Local monodromy of the rank-3 constituents and the rank-6 pushforward

For \(A=\operatorname{Sym}^2V_	au\):

- \(\operatorname{Sym}^2J_2(1)=J_3(1)\);
- \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(-J_2(1))=J_3(1)\);
- \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) has eigenvalues \(1,1,-1\).

Twisting by \(\mathcal K_q\) changes the signs at each \(\alpha_\pm\). Therefore:

| point | \(A\) | \(A_q\) | \(P_{m sym}=A\oplus A_q\) |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(0\) | \(J_3(1)\) | \(J_3(1)\) | \(J_3(1)\oplus J_3(1)\) |
| \(\alpha_+\) | \(1,1,-1\) | \(1,-1,-1\) | \(1^3\oplus(-1)^3\) |
| \(\alpha_-\) | \(1,1,-1\) | \(1,-1,-1\) | \(1^3\oplus(-1)^3\) |
| \(\infty\) | \(J_3(1)\) | \(J_3(1)\) | \(J_3(1)\oplus J_3(1)\) |

All inertia is tame. The sums of local Artin conductors are

```text
sum a_s(A)      = 6,
sum a_s(A_q)    = 8,
sum a_s(P_sym)  = 14.                                    (5.9)
```

Thus the ranks and all standard conductor measures are absolute constants.

The rank-3 sheaf \(A\) is geometrically irreducible because the symmetric-square representation of the Zariski-dense \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) monodromy is irreducible.

### 5.4 Exact trace-square pushforward

If “FranelSquare” is intended to have exact trace \(a_x^2\), the correct object is

```text
T_Fr = V_Franel tensor V_Franel,
B    = V_tau tensor V_tau,
B_q  = B tensor K_q.                                      (5.10)
```

Then

```text
P_tensor := phi_* T_Fr = B direct-sum B_q.                (5.11)
```

Moreover

```text
B = A direct-sum det(V_tau).                               (5.12)
```

Geometrically, \(\det(V_	au)=K_q\), while arithmetically it carries the Tate factor in (4.15). Hence, suppressing only Tate/constant notation,

```text
P_tensor = A direct-sum A_q direct-sum K_q direct-sum 1.  (5.13)
```

The two rank-1 summands have Frobenius traces divisible by \(p\) in the elliptic arithmetic normalization, so they vanish in the degree-one mod-\(p\) shadow. This is exactly why a computation modulo \(p\) cannot distinguish (5.6) from (5.11).

Local monodromy of \(B\) is \(J_3(1)\oplus J_1(1)\) at \(0,\infty\), and has eigenvalues \(1,1,-1,-1\) at each \(\alpha_\pm\). Thus

```text
sum a_s(B)        = 8,
sum a_s(B_q)      = 8,
sum a_s(P_tensor) = 16.                                   (5.14)
```

Again this is uniformly bounded.

### 5.5 The companion is a summand, and the virtual pair cancels

Suppose a fixed sheaf \(\mathcal C\) on \(U_t\) has been chosen as the exact arithmetic lift of the desired Apéry-side function, and the source “FranelSquare” is \(\phi^*\mathcal C\). Then, formally and exactly,

```text
phi_* phi^* C = C direct-sum (C tensor K_q).               (5.15)
```

The putative companion \(\chi_2(q(t))A_p(t)\) is the trace shadow of \(\mathcal C\otimes\mathcal K_q\). Therefore the repository’s signed pair has virtual sheaf

```text
-[phi_* phi^* C] + [C tensor K_q] = -[C].                 (5.16)
```

For the symmetric-square choice, this is

```text
-[A direct-sum A_q] + [A_q] = -[A].                       (5.17)
```

For the exact tensor-square choice, replace \(A\) by \(B\).

This is not merely a correlation: the companion is literally one of the two direct summands of the fixed pushforward. Consequently the pair should be decomposed and cancelled before invoking any irreducible-sheaf equidistribution theorem. The full pushforward is geometrically reducible.

This geometric identity does **not** fill the arithmetic gap that \(A_p\) must first be identified with the trace or Hasse reduction of \(\mathcal A\) or \(\mathcal B\).

## 6. Complete Kummer self-/mutual-twist classification for the candidate sheaves

### 6.1 Two different meanings of “Kummer twist”

For Mellin character aspect, the standard Kummer sheaf is

```text
L_chi(t),
```

with tame local character \(\chi\) at \(0\), \(\chi^{-1}\) at infinity, and no ramification at finite nonzero points.

The companion uses instead

```text
K_q = L_chi2(q(t)),
```

which is ramified at \(\alpha_+\) and \(\alpha_-\), and unramified at \(0,\infty\). It is a rational-function Kummer sheaf, not a Mellin twist \(\mathcal L_\chi(t)\).

This distinction must be retained in every “self-twist” statement.

### 6.2 Standard Mellin Kummer twists

[DEDUCTION] Every sheaf in the following list has **no nontrivial standard Mellin self-twist**:

```text
V_tau, V_sigma,
A, A_q,
B, B_q,
P_sym, P_tensor.
```

Reason: at \(t=0\), each object has only eigenvalue \(1\) with the displayed nontrivial unipotent Jordan structure. Tensoring by \(\mathcal L_\chi(t)\) multiplies every inertia eigenvalue at \(0\) by \(\chi\). Preservation of the local conjugacy class forces \(\chi=1\).

There is also no standard Mellin mutual twist

```text
V_tau  -> V_sigma,
A      -> A_q,
B      -> B_q.
```

For \(A\) and \(A_q\), standard Mellin twists are unramified at \(\alpha_\pm\), while the inertia multiplicities \(2:1\) and \(1:2\) differ there. For \(V_	au,V_\sigma\) and \(B,B_q\), the condition at \(0\) forces the Mellin character to be trivial, after which the two globally distinct sheaves remain distinct.

Therefore no high-order Mellin character can stabilize or identify these fixed constituents.

### 6.3 Rational-function quadratic twists

The complete finite tame rank-1 classification on the fixed singular set is:

| object | geometric self-twist group |
|---|---|
| \(\mathcal V_{m Franel}\) | trivial |
| \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal V_{m Franel}\) | trivial |
| \(\mathcal V_{m Franel}\otimes\mathcal V_{m Franel}\) | trivial |
| \(\mathcal V_	au\) | trivial |
| \(\mathcal V_\sigma\) | trivial |
| \(A\) | trivial |
| \(A_q\) | trivial |
| \(B\) | trivial |
| \(B_q\) | trivial |
| \(P_{m sym}=A\oplus A_q\) | \(\{1,\mathcal K_q\}\) |
| \(P_{m tensor}=B\oplus B_q\) | \(\{1,\mathcal K_q\}\) |

The mutual twists are uniquely

```text
V_sigma = V_tau tensor K_q,
A_q     = A tensor K_q,
B_q     = B tensor K_q.                                   (6.1)
```

There are no others.

### 6.4 Proof that \(\mathcal V_	au\) has no quadratic self-twist

Local inertia leaves only two possible finite tame rank-1 candidates:

- the trivial sheaf;
- \(\mathcal K_q\), which changes sign at both \(\alpha_\pm\).

Suppose

```text
V_tau tensor K_q = V_tau.                                 (6.2)
```

Let \(T\) be an intertwiner. On the index-two subgroup \(\ker K_q\), \(T\) commutes with the restriction of \(\mathcal V_	au\). That restriction is the pullback to the Beukers cover and has Zariski-dense \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) monodromy. By Schur’s lemma, \(T\) is scalar. For an element \(g\) outside \(\ker K_q\), the intertwining relation would then say

```text
T rho(g) T^(-1) = -rho(g),
```

which is impossible for scalar \(T\). Hence (6.2) does not occur. The same argument applies to \(\mathcal V_\sigma\).

### 6.5 Rank-3 constituents and full pushforward

At either \(\alpha_\pm\), \(A\) has eigenvalue multiplicities \(+1:2\), \(-1:1\). Multiplying by \(-1\) swaps these multiplicities and does not preserve the local conjugacy class. Therefore \(A\) has no \(\mathcal K_q\)-self-twist; similarly for \(A_q\).

On the other hand,

```text
P_sym tensor K_q
  = (A tensor K_q) direct-sum A
  = P_sym,                                                 (6.3)
```

so the full pushforward has the canonical order-two self-twist that exchanges the two irreducible rank-3 constituents. Local inertia shows it is the only nontrivial finite tame self-twist: a twist must be trivial at \(0,\infty\), and at the two \(\alpha\)-points it may only change both signs simultaneously, giving precisely \(1\) or \(\mathcal K_q\).

The same proof applies to the rank-8 exact tensor-square pushforward.

### 6.6 What this means for the project

The following two assertions are simultaneously true and must not be conflated:

```text
(1) No nontrivial Mellin Kummer L_chi(t) fixes a relevant constituent.
(2) The full pushforward has the fixed quadratic self-twist K_q.
```

Thus the character-aspect self-twist obstruction is stronger than merely “bounded order”: for standard Mellin characters it is absent. But the two sheaf summands are linked by a fixed, non-Mellin quadratic twist, so treating the pushforward and companion as independent geometrically irreducible objects would be false.

## 7. General determinant and local-inertia lemma

### Lemma 7.1: self-twists

Let \(U\subset\mathbf G_m\) be a nonempty open over an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(p\), and let \(\mathcal F\) be a geometrically irreducible middle-extension sheaf of rank \(r\). Let \(\mathcal L\) be a finite-order rank-1 Kummer sheaf. If

```text
F tensor L = F,                                           (7.1)
```

then

```text
L^r = 1.                                                  (7.2)
```

**Proof.** Taking determinants in (7.1) gives

```text
det(F tensor L) = det(F) tensor L^r = det(F).
```

Cancel \(\det(F)\). This proves (7.2). No conductor hypothesis is needed. In particular,

```text
order(L) divides r.                                       (7.3)
```

Consequences:

- nonconstant Kummer self-twists of unbounded order cannot occur at fixed rank;
- fixed conductor is more than enough;
- the bound is sharp in general for representations induced from a cyclic degree-\(r\) cover.

If one asks only for a **geometric** isomorphism and allows geometrically constant arithmetic characters, such constant characters disappear after base change to the algebraic closure and can have unrelated arithmetic order. They are not variable Kummer sheaves in \(t\). For an exact arithmetic self-isomorphism, the determinant argument again gives (7.2).

### Lemma 7.2: local restrictions

At every boundary point \(s\), write \(\lambda_s\) for the local tame character of \(\mathcal L\). An isomorphism (7.1) requires

```text
F|I_s tensor lambda_s = F|I_s.                            (7.4)
```

Therefore:

1. the multiset of tame inertia eigencharacters must be invariant under multiplication by \(\lambda_s\);
2. Jordan-block sizes for each shifted eigencharacter must agree;
3. twisting by a tame Kummer sheaf does not change wild slopes or Swan conductors;
4. if the local representation is one nontrivial unipotent block with a single eigencharacter, then \(\lambda_s=1\).

For standard \(\mathcal L_\chi(t)\),

```text
lambda_0 = chi,
lambda_infinity = chi^(-1),
lambda_s = 1 at every finite nonzero s.                    (7.5)
```

This frequently forces \(\chi=1\) immediately.

For a rational-function Kummer sheaf \(\mathcal L_\chi(f(t))\),

```text
lambda_s = chi^(ord_s f).                                 (7.6)
```

Thus its possible ramification and cancellation of scalar local monodromy are read directly from the divisor of \(f\).

At fixed rank and fixed finite singular set, (7.4) gives only finitely many possible local scalars: each must be a ratio of inertia eigencharacters. Together with the global product relation for rank-1 tame monodromy on \(\mathbf P^1\), this bounds not only the order but also the number of possible self-twists in terms of the rank and conductor.

### Lemma 7.3: mutual twists

Let \(\mathcal F,\mathcal G\) be geometrically irreducible middle-extension sheaves of the same rank \(r\). If

```text
F tensor L = G,                                           (7.7)
```

then

```text
L^r = det(G) tensor det(F)^(-1).                           (7.8)
```

Moreover, at every \(s\),

```text
G|I_s = F|I_s tensor lambda_s.                             (7.9)
```

This gives the following classification procedure.

1. **Compare ranks.** Unequal ranks rule out a mutual twist.
2. **Compare singular sets carefully.** A new ramification point of \(\mathcal L\) normally creates a scalar singularity. A singularity can disappear only when the original local monodromy is exactly the inverse scalar. Singular sets alone are therefore not sufficient; full inertia is required.
3. **Compare wild slopes and Swan conductors.** Tame Kummer twisting preserves them.
4. **Solve the local eigencharacter shifts and Jordan-block constraints.**
5. **Impose (7.8) and the global product/divisor relation.**
6. **Verify the remaining global candidate.** For a rigid local system, local data may suffice. Otherwise one needs an explicit geometric isomorphism or equality of Frobenius characteristic polynomials on a Chebotarev-dense set.

If \(\mathcal L_1\) and \(\mathcal L_2\) both satisfy (7.7), then

```text
F tensor (L_1 tensor L_2^(-1)) = F.
```

Hence the set of mutual twists, when nonempty, is a torsor under the self-twist group of \(\mathcal F\). In particular, once the self-twist group is trivial, a mutual twist is unique.

The pair \(\mathcal V_	au,\mathcal V_\sigma\) is a useful warning: their local inertia conjugacy classes are the same at all four points, but a global calculation is still needed. The explicit gauge by \(q^{-1/2}\) supplies the mutual twist, while dense monodromy rules out its being a self-twist.

## Final assessment

The local monodromy and Kummer-twist part of the project is substantially computable and, for the natural geometric candidate sheaves, completely rigid enough to classify. The answer is stronger than “only bounded-order Mellin self-twists”: there are no nontrivial standard Mellin self-twists at all. The only nontrivial fixed symmetry is the quadratic rational-function Kummer sheaf \(\mathcal K_q\), which exchanges the two constituents of the degree-two pushforward.

The remaining obstacle is the initial trace normalization. CFVZ prove a mod-\(p\) polynomial factorization, not a rank-2 trace formula. Until an explicit cohomological/Frobenius construction identifies \(A_p\) and the chosen square object with reductions of exact traces, the project may use the monodromy computation only as a theorem about candidate fixed sheaves, not as a theorem about the raw finite polynomials.
