# Q562 — AKD₃ via Bombieri–Vinogradov averaging

## Executive conclusion

A Bombieri–Vinogradov strategy is a useful diagnostic decomposition, but it does not by itself remove the main obstruction. The obstruction is not the prime-pair averaging; it is that the fixed Apéry phases are not currently known to be trace functions with enough uniformity after the nonlinear zero detector.

The Q554 near-injection argument is substantially stronger than a generic BV input for the RMS/phase-averaged problem, but it does not automatically imply the fixed-phase first absolute moment required by AKD₃.

## (A) Separating BV and non-BV pieces

The AKD₃ expression naturally splits into:

1. A geometric/combinatorial part:

\[
(q,r)\mapsto (qr)^{-1}\pmod {pp'}.
\]

Q554 controls this because the map from ordered semiprimes is almost injective. This gives Parseval control after averaging the phases.

2. A coefficient part:

\[
\widehat Z_p(a)=\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(az).
\]

The problem is that \(Z_p\) is defined by the nonlinear condition \(b_r=0\pmod p\). It is not the trace function of a fixed lisse sheaf. Therefore standard BV machinery cannot simply be applied to \(\widehat Z_p\).

A realistic BV decomposition would require first replacing the zero indicator by a linearized object (for example, a family of trace functions with controlled conductor). This is exactly the missing AKD₃ ingredient.

## (B) Can near-injection replace character cancellation?

Only partially.

The near-injection lemma gives the correct RMS estimate:

\[
\mathbb E_c\left|\sum_{q<r}e_{pp'}(c(qr)^{-1})\right|^2\ll P.
\]

This is a second-moment statement in the phase parameter \(c\).

AKD₃ fixes the coefficients

\[
c=z p'-z'p
\]

coming from actual Apéry zeros. There is no remaining average over \(c\). Thus one needs either:

* equidistribution of the Apéry phases among the allowed \(c\)'s; or
* a direct bound for the exceptional Fourier coefficients.

Near-injection alone cannot provide this upgrade.

## (C) Is there an Apéry BV theorem?

Not in the required form.

Known results on Apéry numbers include congruences, modular parametrizations, and some supercongruence phenomena. They do not provide a Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem for the nonlinear sequence

\[
1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}.
\]

Even if the underlying Apéry differential equation has automorphic realizations, this controls linear trace data, not the zero locus indicator.

Symmetric-power automorphy would become relevant only after a successful linearization of the zero detector.

## (D) Minimal extra ingredient beyond Q554

The target is not a full automorphic BV theorem. The minimal missing statement is a Fourier-flatness estimate:

\[
\sum_{p\in(X,2X]}
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^*}
|\widehat Z_p(a)|^4
\ll X^{2+o(1)},
\]

or an equivalent pair-correlation bound for the Fourier transforms of the zero sets.

Together with the Q554 near-injection lemma, such a fourth-moment estimate would convert phase averaging into fixed Apéry phases.

This is weaker than proving full equidistribution of Apéry zeros.

## Relation to Heath-Brown/Fouvry–Iwaniec methods

Heath-Brown identities and Fouvry–Iwaniec dispersion methods are designed for sums where coefficients are already arithmetic functions with controlled convolution structure. They can handle bilinear Kloosterman sums, but they do not create cancellation for an arbitrary sparse set \(Z_p\).

The promising route is therefore:

1. prove a Fourier moment theorem for Apéry zero sets;
2. combine it with Q554 near-injection;
3. derive AKD₃ by Cauchy–Schwarz/large sieve.

A direct BV theorem for the fixed nonlinear zero detector is likely stronger than necessary.

## Paper-level formulation

The clean conjectural statement should be:

> The Apéry zero Fourier transforms satisfy sufficient fourth-moment cancellation uniformly in p. Combined with the semiprime near-injection property, this implies AKD₃.

The remaining research bottleneck is therefore not prime-pair averaging, but proving Fourier pseudorandomness of the Apéry zero sets themselves.
