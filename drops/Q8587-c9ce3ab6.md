## Claim

## Fibre calculation

Write the three hit equations as

  c_i p_i - c_j p_j = d_ij.

After fixing signs and (d12,d13), the variables p_i are constrained by two affine relations. The target-filtered shell gives

  1 <= c_i <= X-1

for positive coefficients and

  2 <= c_i <= X-1

for negative coefficients. The excluded zero-defect case removes the degenerate diagonal fibres.

The translated folded zero-root conditions add congruence constraints on the corresponding Apéry residues. They reduce the number of admissible coefficient choices by at most subpolynomial factors unless a new independence statement is supplied. Thus the strongest unconditional fibre estimate obtainable from these equations is

  N(d12,d13,sign) <= X^(o(1)) lambda_X^3.

This is a local theorem only.

## Summation obstruction

The number of full defect pairs is naturally O(X^2), since each defect lies in an interval of length O(X). Therefore the direct summation gives

  total <= X^2 * X^(o(1)) lambda_X^3,

which is one full factor X too large for the scalar-span DSEL3 target.

A valid completion would require a theorem of the following form:

  sum_{compatible (d12,d13)} N(d12,d13,sign)
      <= X^(1+o(1)) lambda_X^3.

Equivalently, one needs a quantified aggregation statement such as

  #{compatible defects weighted by fibre multiplicity} <= O(X)

or an energy estimate proving cancellation/concentration beyond independent fibre counting.

## Strongest non-tautological candidate theorem

The natural target is an incidence-energy theorem:

For every sign vector,

  E = sum_d (sum_{triples in fibre d} 1)^2

satisfies

  E <= X^(1+o(1)) lambda_X^6.

The affine determinant route is unavailable because the determinant vanishes identically. Scalar span injectivity is also false at X=1024, so no replacement based on unique defect encoding is valid.
