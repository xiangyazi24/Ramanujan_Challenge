# Audit of the proposed mod-64 recurrence

## Conclusion

The displayed recurrence is not proved by the binomial sum from the information available, and the proposed route hides the main missing step. The recurrence may be a true empirical congruence, but a finite scan through 2048 is not a proof. I cannot certify it as an all-m proof without an independent 2-adic section identity.

## 1. What would be needed

Write

\[
b_n=\sum_k A(n,k),\qquad A(n,k)=\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]

A proof of

\[
b_{2m+e}\equiv c_e(m\bmod8)b_m\pmod {64}
\]

requires an identity after splitting the sum into even and odd k. The required statement is not merely a valuation estimate; it must give

\[
\sum_{k\equiv r(2)}A(2m+e,k)\equiv u_r(m)\,b_m\pmod {64}
\]

with all odd correction factors controlled modulo 64.

Kummer's theorem alone only determines when individual summands vanish modulo 64. It does not determine the surviving unit factors.

## 2. The proposed stratification gap

The suggestion to discard terms with large 2-adic valuation is valid only after proving a uniform bound of the form

\[
v_2\left(\binom{2m+e}{k}\binom{2m+e+k}{k}\right)\ge 3
\]

for the discarded classes. Such a bound is not automatic: the two binomial valuations can cancel independently, and surviving terms can have valuation exactly below the required threshold.

For the remaining classes one needs explicit congruences for the odd parts of the binomial coefficients modulo 64. Those congruences are the missing input.

## 3. Closed form of the coefficient table

The table is a function only of e and m modulo 8, but observing this does not establish that the dependence is periodic. A proof needs either:

* a 2-adic factorial congruence giving the period-8 unit factor; or
* a finite companion recurrence with a proved invariant.

No such invariant is supplied by the numerical table.

## 4. Status of possible counterexample search

A counterexample would require exact modular recomputation beyond the supplied range. The recurrence cannot be accepted or rejected from the stated data alone. No independently verified least counterexample is produced here.

## 5. Narrow lemma sufficient for completion

A sufficient theorem would be:

For every m and e in {0,1}, there is an identity of integer sums

```
b_(2m+e) = u_e(m) b_m + 64 R_e(m)
```

where u_e(m) is an explicitly evaluated odd 2-adic unit satisfying

```
u_e(m+8)=u_e(m) mod 64.
```

The remaining task is therefore a genuine 2-section identity for Apéry numbers, not a finite automaton extrapolation.
