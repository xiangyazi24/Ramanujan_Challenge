# 2. The canonical top-symbol finite difference

Put

\[
S=t^{-1}.

The Hecke dilation and the shift have different affine symbols on pole order:

\[
T_\ell:S^m\leadsto \ell^3 S^{\ell m},

The natural commutator for cancelling this affine symbol is therefore not \(T_\ell S-ST_\ell\), but

\[
\boxed{

\tag{2.1}

Iterating gives the exact noncommutative binomial identity

\[
\delta_\ell^k(T_\ell)

\tag{2.2}

Normalize away the deterministic \(k\ell\) shift:

\[
\boxed{
A_{\ell,k}:=S^{-k\ell}\delta_\ell^k(T_\ell)

\tag{2.3}

This is an integral operator on the integral weakly holomorphic lattice: every coefficient is integral, \(t^{\pm1}\) acts by integral shifts on the \(\Omega_m\) basis, and \(T_\ell\) is integral in the standard weight-four normalization.

# 5. Pure Hecke polynomials: target-safe for arbitrary \(R\)-dependence, still no projector

There is a stronger no-go in the subalgebra where target safety actually follows formally from (4.3).

Let \(\mathbb T_S\) be the Hecke algebra generated by a fixed finite set \(S\) of good primes.  Allow the coefficients of an operator

\[
P_R\in\mathbf Z[\,T_\ell:\ell\in S\,]
\]

to depend arbitrarily on \(R\).  Since the good Hecke operators commute and satisfy the usual Hecke relations, collect \(P_R\) as

\[
P_R=\sum_n c_{R,n}T_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let \(N_R\) be the largest \(n\) with \(c_{R,n}\ne0\).  The \(V_{N_R}\) branch is unique at the deepest cusp pole, and in weight four

\[
T_{N_R}\Omega_m
=N_R^3\Omega_{N_Rm}+\text{lower poles}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore rows \(N_R(R+1),\ldots,2N_RR\) give

\[
\boxed{
\det M_R=(c_{R,N_R}N_R^3)^R. }
\tag{5.3}
\]

For a fresh prime \(p>2R\) not dividing the finitely supported Hecke level \(N_R\):

- if \(p\nmid c_{R,N_R}\), the source is \(p\)-split and a labelled zero column is contracted;
- if \(p\mid c_{R,N_R}\), then the fresh prime is already present in the **operator coefficient**.

Thus the only way a pure-Hecke polynomial can avoid the pivot at a labelled \(p\) is to place \(p\) in its own symbol content.  That coefficient is then already a Bezout/support carrier; the Hecke complex has not discovered the prime.

This conclusion is independent of the degree of \(P_R\).

## 5.1 Top-component cancellation examples

For distinct good primes,

\[
[T_\ell,T_s]=0,
\qquad
T_\ell T_s=T_{\ell s}\quad(\ell\ne s),
\]

so a commutator that cancels the apparent top symbols is simply the zero operator, not a residual carrier.

For one prime,

\[
\boxed{
T_\ell^2-T_{\ell^2}=\ell^3I. }
\tag{5.4}
\]

The \(\ell^2\)-raising component cancels, but the residual operator is \(\ell^3I\), which has a \(p\)-unit identity minor at every fresh \(p\ne\ell\).

This is exactly why “the highest symbol cancels” is not enough.  One must track the residual operator after the Hecke relation is applied.

# 6. Target-safe eigenrelation versus label projector

The distinction can be made algebraically.

A **target-safe eigenrelation** is

\[
E A=B E.
\tag{6.1}
\]

It implies

\[
E(f)=0\Longrightarrow E(Af)=0.
\]

It does **not** imply that the zero of one source column creates torsion, homology, or a Fitting factor.

A genuine **label projector** would need the \(p\)-local algebraic object itself to change when

\[
E(e_r)\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

for example by creating a \(p\)-torsion class whose valuation is at least one.  The principal-part pivot prevents exactly that.

For a Hecke eigenoperator, (6.1) holds with \(B=(1+\ell^3)I_2\).  Yet the leading-pole coordinate pairs every source \(e_r\) with a fresh-\(p\) unit.  Hence the column can be eliminated before its zero evaluation has any homological effect.

This is why observability is target-safe but row-redundant: kernel preservation is not target selection.

# 7. Residual mapping cone: what actually survives after the source pivot

It is important **not** to say that one unit minor makes the whole mapping cone contractible.

Let

\[
A:V\longrightarrow P,
\qquad
E:V\longrightarrow S^2,
\qquad
E_P:P\longrightarrow S^2,
\]

where \(S=\mathbf Z_{(p)}\), and assume the target-safe compatibility

\[
E_PA=BE.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The smallest honest cone is

\[
V\xrightarrow{d_1}P\oplus S^2\xrightarrow{d_0}S^2,
\]

with

\[
d_1x=(Ax,Ex),
\qquad
d_0(y,z)=E_Py-Bz.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Suppose \(A\) has a \(p\)-unit full source minor.  Choose a left inverse

\[
L:P\to V,
\qquad LA=I_V.
\]

Apply the codomain automorphism

\[
\Phi(y,z)=(y,z-ELy).
\]

Then

\[
\Phi d_1(x)=(Ax,0).
\tag{7.3}
\]

The transformed second differential is

\[
d_0\Phi^{-1}(y,z)
=(E_P-BEL)y-Bz.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Since

\[
(E_P-BEL)A=E_PA-BE=0,
\]

it factors through

\[
Q:=P/A(V).
\]

Therefore the complex splits as

\[
\boxed{
\bigl[V\xrightarrow{\sim}A(V)\bigr]
\ \oplus\ 
\bigl[0\to Q\oplus S^2
\xrightarrow{(\bar E_P,-B)}S^2\to0\bigr]. }
\tag{7.5}
\]

Consequences:

- the source-supported \(H_1\) is zero;
- a zero source evaluation column does not survive into \(H_1\);
- the **whole** cone need not be contractible: \(Q\) is a genuine residual free quotient;
- fresh torsion in the residual can occur only when the residual map itself loses \(p\)-rank.

For the actual Hecke relation,

\[
B=(1+\ell^3)I_2.
\tag{7.6}
\]

If \(p\nmid1+\ell^3\), then \(-B\) is already an invertible \(2\times2\) block.  The residual map in (7.5) is split surjective, \(Q\) is free because it is a direct summand of \(P\), and the residual has no \(p\)-torsion.

For \(\ell=5\),

\[
1+5^3=126=2\cdot3^2\cdot7.
\]

Thus every genuinely fresh \(p>2R\ge8\) is a unit for this residual block as well.

This is a **separate** argument from the leading-pole contraction and is the correct residual-complex accounting requested in the question.

# 8. Scoped symbol-calculus no-go for \(R\)-dependent \(t\)-coefficients

The finite difference above is one coefficient function on the connected \(\ell\)-Hecke correspondence.  The same obstruction has a geometric form.

Fix a finite set \(S\) of good Hecke primes.  Consider a family of global integral correspondences obtained from

\[
T_\ell\ (\ell\in S),\quad t^{\pm1},\quad W_6,
\]

after all algebraically identical Hecke correspondence components have been collected.

On a connected cyclic \(n\)-isogeny component \(C_n\), all intermediate \(t\)-values appearing in a word are meromorphic functions on \(C_n\).  Since \(S\) is fixed,

\[
\deg(\pi_i:C_n\to X_0(6)^*)
=\psi(n)
=n\prod_{q\mid n}(1+q^{-1})
\asymp_S n.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Hence a coefficient obtained from \(d_R\) total Laurent \(t\)-factors has divisor degree

\[
\deg\operatorname{div}_\infty F_{n,R}
=O_S(nd_R).
\tag{8.2}
\]

At the expanding cusp of this component, let

\[
\nu_{n,R}=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak c_V}F_{n,R}.
\]

If \(F_{n,R}\not\equiv0\), then

\[
\boxed{
\nu_{n,R}\le O_S(nd_R). }
\tag{8.3}
\]

The \(V_n\) branch acting on \(\Omega_m\) therefore still has pole

\[
nm-\nu_{n,R}+O_S(1).
\tag{8.4}
\]

If

\[
d_R=o(R),
\]

then for every \(m\in(R,2R]\),

\[
nm-\nu_{n,R}>0
\]

for large \(R\).  Thus finite-order zeroing of the expanding symbol can move the pole by \(o(nR)\), but cannot remove it from an entire dyadic window.

To kill the expanding branch by vanishing order alone requires

\[
\nu_{n,R}\ge nR+O(1),
\]

which by (8.3) forces

\[
\boxed{d_R=\Omega(R). }
\tag{8.5}
\]

If instead \(F_{n,R}\) vanishes to infinite order at the expanding cusp, then because \(C_n\) is connected it is identically zero: the **entire correspondence component** is gone, including its other cusp branches.  One must then inspect the next surviving component.  If all \(n>1\) components disappear, the residual lies in the \(n=1\) shift/Fricke part; any nonzero Laurent multiplication again has a unit shift-type principal symbol, while \(W_6=-1\) contributes only a sign.

This proves the following scoped theorem.

## Theorem — primitive bounded-component \(o(R)\)-symbol no-go

Let \(A_R\) be an \(R\)-dependent integral operator assembled from a fixed finite set of good Hecke correspondences, \(t^{\pm1}\), and \(W_6\).  Assume that after Hecke-component collection:

1. the number of connected correspondence components is bounded independently of \(R\);
2. on each surviving component the normalized coefficient-divisor complexity satisfies \(d_R=o(R)\);
3. the first nonzero integral cusp-symbol coefficient is primitive at the fresh prime \(p>2R\).

Then the principal-part map of \(A_R\) on \(V_R\) contains a fresh-\(p\) unit source pivot block.  Any compatible mapping cone splits off the corresponding source summand, so a zero BFH evaluation column cannot force fresh \(p\)-torsion there.

If condition 3 fails, then \(p\) already divides the integral content of the operator symbol.  The fresh support of that symbol content is itself the arithmetic carrier; the operator has merely encoded the target prime in its coefficients.

This covers:

- every bounded-degree \(P_R(T_\ell,t^{\pm1},W_6)\), even with \(R\)-dependent integer coefficients;
- the degree-\(o(R)\) iterated finite difference \(A_{\ell,k_R}\) above;
- bounded-component combinations of two or more Hecke primes;
- commutators and Hecke-relation cancellations after their exact residual components are collected.

It deliberately does **not** claim a no-go for a family having an unbounded number of distinct Hecke components whose crossing/cancellation complexity itself grows comparably to the number of rows.  That is a real residual loophole, not something hidden by the proof.

# 9. Boundary rows and all four cusps

\[

\quad\text{to}\quad

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{1/2,1/3}
\left(t^{\ell h}T_\ell\Omega_{m+h}\right)
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{m+h+1}{\ell}\right\rceil-\ell h.
\tag{9.1}

So there is no hidden boundary-cusp escape in the finite-difference construction.

# 11. Runnable Sage core

The following is a compact version of the exact audit.  The complete persisted script on the audit branch additionally reconstructs \(\kappa\) and performs the locked-target constant-term calculation.

```python
from sage.all import *

ELL = 5

def P(n):
    n = ZZ(n)
    return 34*n^3 + 51*n^2 + 27*n + 5

# Apéry b_n.
N = 520
b = [ZZ(1), ZZ(5)]
for n in range(1, N):
    num = P(n)*b[n] - ZZ(n)^3*b[n-1]
    den = ZZ(n+1)^3
    assert num % den == 0
    b.append(num // den)

# q/t=A(q)=prod_{3∤n}(1+q^n)^12.
def build_A(prec):
    S = PowerSeriesRing(QQ, 'q', default_prec=prec)
    q = S.gen()
    A = S(1)
    for n in range(1, prec):
        if n % 3:
            A = (A*(1+q^n)^12).add_bigoh(prec)
    assert [ZZ(A[i]) for i in range(4)] == [1,12,78,364]
    return S,q,A

def build_psi(prec):
    S,q,A = build_A(prec)
    t = (q/A).add_bigoh(prec)
    Fq = S(sum(QQ(b[n])*q^n for n in range(min(len(b),prec))))
    E = Fq(t).add_bigoh(prec)
    H = (1-q*A.derivative()/A).add_bigoh(prec)
    Psi = (E*H).add_bigoh(prec)
    assert [ZZ(E[i]) for i in range(5)] == [1,5,13,23,29]
    return S,q,A,Psi

def subst_qell(f, ell, S, q, prec):
    out = S(0)
    for n in range((prec-1)//ell+1):
        out += f[n]*q^(ell*n)
    return out.add_bigoh(prec)

# Selected exact principal-part block of
# A_{ell,k}=sum_h (-1)^(k-h) C(k,h) t^(ell h) T_ell t^(-h).
def selected_matrix(R,k,ell=ELL):
    need = ell*(R-1)+k+5
    S,q,A,Psi = build_psi(need)
    Aell = subst_qell(A,ell,S,q,need)
    Psiell = subst_qell(Psi,ell,S,q,need)

    B = (Aell/(A^ell)-1).add_bigoh(need)
    assert B[0] == 0
    assert ZZ(B[1]) == -12*ell

    Bk = (B^k).add_bigoh(need)
    Bnorm = S([Bk[n+k] for n in range(need-k)]).add_bigoh(need-k)

    diag = ZZ(ell^3)*ZZ(-12*ell)^k
    assert ZZ(Bnorm[0]) == ZZ(-12*ell)^k

    M = matrix(ZZ,R,R)
    base = (Bnorm*Psiell).add_bigoh(need-k)
    Apow = (Aell^(R+1)).add_bigoh(need-k)

    for j in range(R):
        core = (base*Apow).add_bigoh(need-k)
        for i in range(j+1):
            M[i,j] = ZZ(ell^3*core[ell*(j-i)])
        Apow = (Apow*Aell).add_bigoh(need-k)

    assert all(M[i,j] == 0 for i in range(R) for j in range(i))
    assert all(M[i,i] == diag for i in range(R))
    return M,diag

for R in [20,50,100]:
    k = floor(sqrt(R))
    M,d = selected_matrix(R,k)
    p = next_prime(2*R)
    if p == ELL:
        p = next_prime(p)
    assert M.change_ring(GF(p)).rank() == R
    assert gcd(d,p) == 1
    print(R,k,d,factor(abs(d)),p)
```

# 12. What remains genuinely open

The operator loophole is now much smaller.

The following do **not** work:

- fixed Hecke graphs: Q7678;
- arbitrary \(R\)-dependent pure-Hecke polynomials: largest surviving Hecke component gives (5.3), unless the target prime is already in the coefficient content;
- \(k=o(R)\) normalized finite differences cancelling the top \(\ell\)-symbol: exact residual minor (3.3);
- ordinary commutators/two-prime Hecke cancellations: Hecke relations reduce them to zero or to a lower component with the same pivot mechanism;
- the canonical \(t\)-shift cancellation at the actual locked targets: it fails target preservation before any Fitting argument.

The residual possibilities are genuinely different:

1. **Degree \(\Theta(R)\)** so that the coefficient function can have cusp vanishing of order comparable to \(nR\).  This loses the cheap \(o(R^2)\) coefficient-height advantage unless there is a second, global cancellation.
2. **Unbounded correspondence-component complexity** growing with \(R\), large enough that many affine cusp branches can cross and cancel row-by-row.  The bounded-component divisor argument above does not rule this out.
3. **Arithmetic/label-dependent coefficients** chosen so their symbol content already contains every fresh target prime.  This is formally possible but circular unless that content is bounded independently by a new theorem; it is essentially BFH in another presentation.
4. **A new non-Hecke global correspondence or many-row relation** whose evaluation map is genuinely a label projector rather than merely an eigenfunctional.

So the answer to the research question is negative but sharp:

\[
\boxed{
\text{highest-symbol cancellation of degree }o(R)
\text{ does not yield a subquadratic fresh-singleton carrier.}}
\]

For the strongest natural \(R\)-dependent family, the determinant is already \(o(R^2)\), but it has only \(2,3,\ell\)-support and is a unit at every fresh target.  To evade that pivot one must cross the linear-order/large-component boundary or insert the fresh arithmetic into the operator coefficients themselves.
