# Q572: The Strassmann route — p-adic zero counting for Apéry Mellin coefficients

## Main conclusion

The Strassmann idea is conceptually very close to the desired M1 theorem, but the naive finite Mahler expansion over $\mathbf F_p$ cannot give bounded zero counts. The missing ingredient is not finite-difference decay modulo $p$; it is a genuine $p$-adic analytic continuation of the Apéry sequence with a radius of overconvergence $>1$.

The correct statement to aim for is:

> The Apéry unit-root crystal produces a $p$-adic analytic interpolation $F_p(s)$ whose overconvergence radius is uniformly bounded below, and Strassmann then bounds the number of zeros in the residue disk.

This would directly imply AFP/M1.

---

# (A) Mahler coefficients $c_k=\Delta^k b(0)$

The coefficients are

$$
c_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}b_j.
$$

For an arbitrary sequence this has no useful $p$-adic decay. The recurrence alone does not imply

$$
v_p(c_k)\to \infty.
$$

The reason is that the Apéry recurrence is a recurrence in the index $r$ over the integers, but the Mahler expansion asks for regularity of the function

$$
r\mapsto b_r
$$

as a function on $\mathbf Z_p$.

These are different notions.

A linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients does not automatically give a bounded-degree Mahler expansion modulo $p$.

In particular, the implication

$$
v_p(c_k)\ge 1 \quad(k\ge K)
$$

is far too strong. It would mean that the reduction of $F_p$ is literally a polynomial of bounded degree over $\mathbf F_p$, which is not expected for a generic irreducible hypergeometric recurrence with large monodromy.

The empirical boundedness of $|Z_p|$ therefore cannot come from small finite Mahler degree.

---

# (B) Dwork congruences and Mahler decay

The Dwork congruences

$$
b_{mp^s}\equiv b_{mp^{s-1}}\pmod {p^{3s}}
$$

are very strong, but they control a different direction.

They imply compatibility along the tower

$$
m,mp,mp^2,\ldots
$$

and describe Frobenius self-similarity.

Mahler coefficients instead measure

$$
\Delta^k b(0),
$$

which mixes values at

$$
0,1,\ldots,k.
$$

There is no direct implication

$$
\text{Dwork congruence}\Rightarrow v_p(\Delta^k b(0))>0.
$$

A counterintuition is that many Frobenius-compatible sequences have complicated first-level residue behavior.

Therefore Dwork congruences should enter through the crystalline realization, not through elementary finite differences.

---

# (C) Concrete Mahler computation expectation

For small primes $p=11,13,17,19,23$, one can compute

$$
c_k=\Delta^k b(0)\pmod p
$$

for $0\le k\le p-2$.

The expected pattern is:

1. There is no small universal cutoff $K$ with

$$
c_k=0\quad(k>K).
$$

2. The effective Mahler degree remains close to $p$, not bounded.

3. The bounded zero count comes from cancellation/geometry, not polynomial degree.

This is analogous to exponential sums: a trace function can have only few zeros without being represented by a low-degree polynomial.

Thus the computation is useful as a falsification test for the bounded-degree hypothesis, but it is unlikely to reveal the M1 mechanism.

---

# (D) Overconvergence and the unit-root crystal

This is the promising part.

The Apéry differential equation is a Picard-Fuchs equation. At ordinary primes, the crystalline realization has a unit-root subcrystal with Frobenius eigenvalue

$$
\alpha_p,\qquad |\alpha_p|_p=1.
$$

The unit-root splitting gives a $p$-adic analytic object. The relevant question is whether this analytic continuation has a uniform overconvergence radius.

If one obtains

$$
F_p(s)\in \mathbf C_p\langle p^{-\rho}s\rangle
$$

for some $\rho>0$ independent of $p$, then Strassmann applies on residue disks.

The number of zeros is bounded by the dominant Newton polygon coefficient:

$$
\#\{s\in\mathbf Z_p:F_p(s)=0\}\leq \operatorname{Strassmann}(F_p).
$$

This is the correct route to $|Z_p|=O(1)$.

---

# Important distinction: finite Mahler vs overconvergent Mahler

There are two different objects:

## Finite interpolation

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}c_k\binom{s}{k}\pmod p.
$$

This always exists, but gives only degree $p-2$.

## Infinite $p$-adic interpolation

$$
F_p(s)=\sum_{k\ge0}c_k\binom{s}{k}
$$

with

$$
v_p(c_k)\to\infty.
$$

Only the second object can use Strassmann effectively.

The open theorem is precisely proving that the Apéry recurrence admits this second interpretation uniformly in $p$.

---

# Relation to AFP

This gives a sharper formulation of Apéry Fourier Pseudorandomness:

AFP is not merely Fourier cancellation of the finite vector

$$
(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-2}).
$$

It should follow from the fact that this vector is the reduction of an overconvergent $p$-adic analytic function.

The zero set is then a $p$-adic analytic zero locus, not a random subset.

---

# Final assessment

- (A) Bounded Mahler degree is almost certainly false; the finite-difference route is a dead end.
- (B) Dwork congruences do not directly kill high Mahler coefficients.
- (C) Small-prime computations should show no bounded effective degree.
- (D) The overconvergent unit-root crystal route is the correct Strassmann route.

The missing theorem becomes:

**Uniform overconvergence of the Apéry unit-root $F$-crystal implies bounded Strassmann index for the Mellin coefficient interpolation.**

This is a plausible geometric replacement for the missing AFP theorem and is substantially stronger than a classical large sieve statement.
