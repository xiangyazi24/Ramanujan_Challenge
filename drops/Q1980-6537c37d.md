# Strategic audit: far-gap Apéry target primes

## Verdict

**No sublinear-height carrier follows from the stated odd-quotient geometry plus the pointwise Apéry zero condition alone.** More sharply, if

\[
\mathcal T_G(n)=\{p:\sqrt n<p\le n,\ p\text{ is a target},\ g(p)>G\}
\]

and

\[
W_G(n):=\sum_{p\in\mathcal T_G(n)}\log p,
\]

then the existence of any nonzero integer carrier \(C_G(n)\) divisible by every prime in \(\mathcal T_G(n)\) and satisfying \(\log|C_G(n)|=o(n)\) is **equivalent, at the level of arithmetic height, to**

\[
\boxed{W_G(n)=o(n).}
\]

Indeed \(\prod_{p\in\mathcal T_G(n)}p\mid C_G(n)\), so

\[
\log|C_G(n)|\ge W_G(n),
\]

while the squarefree target product itself is a carrier of height exactly \(W_G(n)\). Thus products, determinants, norms, resultants, or symbolic compression cannot lower the logarithmic cost below the total logarithmic mass of the distinct target primes. Any successful \(o(n)\) resultant would already contain, implicitly, the missing horizontal zero-incidence theorem.

This matches the current repository reduction in `problems/3.2/ORACLE_COMM/chatgpt_q32_fixed_exponent_mellin_attack.md`: after folding, the moving problem is a prime-divisor correlation for the fixed characteristic-zero Apéry sequence along two affine rays, and the stated sufficient closer is a weighted vanishing-density estimate in quotient cells. The current `problems/3.2/proof.tex` supplies strong *vertical* Apéry structure (reflection, gap polynomials, \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\), projective-orbit identities, adjacent resultants), but none of those presently yields the needed deterministic horizontal anti-alignment.

There is one especially important additional no-go: the most natural reciprocal/gap-polynomial resultant is **tautological** at a folded zero. For the reflected pair separated by \(h=2g\), the canonical recurrence polynomial \(N_{2g}(x)\) already contains the factor \(2x+2g+1\); at \(x=s\) that factor is exactly \(p\). Hence its mod-\(p\) vanishing does not use \(p\mid b_s\) at all.

## 1. Exact fixed-\(q\) coordinates

Put

\[
N:=2n+1,\qquad D:=2q+1.
\]

For \(p>\sqrt n\), one has \(q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor<\sqrt n<p\). In the direct branch,

\[
n=qp+s,
\]

and in the reflected branch,

\[
n=(q+1)p-1-s.
\]

Since \(s=(p-1)/2-g\), the two odd-quotient equations are

\[
Dp=N+2g\qquad(+),
\]

and

\[
Dp=N-2g\qquad(-).
\]

Eliminating \(p\) gives the exact affine index formulas

\[
\boxed{s_+(g)=\frac{n-q-2qg}{2q+1}},
\qquad
\boxed{s_-(g)=\frac{n-q-2(q+1)g}{2q+1}}.
\]

Equivalently, parametrizing by the prime,

\[
\boxed{s_+(p)=n-qp},
\qquad
\boxed{s_-(p)=(q+1)p-1-n}.
\]

Thus the far-gap target mass decomposes exactly as

\[
W_G(n)=\sum_{q<\sqrt n}\bigl(W^+_{n,q}(G)+W^-_{n,q}(G)\bigr),
\]

where

\[
W^+_{n,q}(G)=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\
\lfloor n/p\rfloor=q\\
Dp>N+2G\\
p\mid b_{n-qp}}}\log p,
\]

and

\[
W^-_{n,q}(G)=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\
\lfloor n/p\rfloor=q\\
Dp<N-2G\\
p\mid b_{(q+1)p-1-n}}}\log p.
\]

This is the right object to audit. Any claimed carrier must beat this weighted mass, not merely count quotient cells or describe the set of gaps succinctly.

## 2. Height lemma: no arithmetic packaging can compress distinct prime mass

### Lemma

Let \(\mathcal P\) be any finite set of distinct primes. If \(C\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\) and \(p\mid C\) for every \(p\in\mathcal P\), then

\[
\boxed{\log|C|\ge\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\log p.}
\]

### Proof

The primes are pairwise coprime, so

\[
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p\mid C.
\]

Taking logarithms proves the claim.

The same obstruction survives all standard variants. If the carrier is rational, take its primitive numerator. If it is an algebraic integer, take a nonzero rational norm. If it is a determinant or resultant, it is still a nonzero integer after the stated denominator clearing. A zero resultant is not a finite-height carrier; divisibility of zero is vacuous.

Therefore there is no distinction, for the purpose of the desired \(o(n)\) height, between an explicit product and a clever resultant: **both must first prove that the target prime mass itself is \(o(n)\).**

## 3. The existing global carrier \(b_n\) is only linear-height

The repository uses Gessel's Apéry-Lucas congruence

\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
\]

A target has \(p\mid b_r\), equivalently \(p\mid b_s\) after folding. Hence every target prime \(p>\sqrt n\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_n.}
\]

So the squarefree target product already divides \(\operatorname{rad}(b_n)\). This is a genuine global arithmetic carrier, but it has only the useless linear scale

\[
\log b_n=\Theta(n).
\]

An elementary bound suffices: the \(j=n\) summand gives

\[
b_n\ge \binom{2n}{n}^2,
\]

so

\[
\log b_n\ge n\log 16-O(\log n),
\]

while \(\binom nj\le2^n\), \(\binom{n+j}{j}\le 2^{n+j}\le4^n\) gives \(b_n\le(n+1)64^n\).

For \(q=1\) this is especially sharp: \(b_n\equiv5b_{n-p}\pmod p\), so for every sufficiently large candidate prime in the direct branch,

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_{n-p}\iff p\mid b_n.}
\]

Thus even the first quotient cell asks for a nontrivial statement about where the large prime divisors of the single integer \(b_n\) can lie. The size of \(b_n\) permits a linear logarithmic mass there; size alone cannot give \(o(n)\).

## 4. Fixed-\(q\) products do not close

There are two natural fixed-cell products, and they fail for opposite reasons.

### 4.1 Geometry-only product: already linear in the \(q=1\) cell

Take the direct \(q=1\) cell. Then

\[
3p=N+2g,
\qquad
s=n-p,
\qquad
 g=\frac{3p-2n-1}{2}.
\]

For primes

\[
\frac34n\le p\le\frac78n,
\]

we have

\[
\frac18n-\frac12\le g\le\frac{5}{16}n-\frac12.
\]

Hence for every \(G=o(n)\), and therefore for every \(G=o(n/\log n)\), this entire fixed positive-proportion prime interval is eventually in the far-gap region. The geometry-only prime product over this interval has

\[
\sum_{3n/4\le p\le7n/8}\log p
=\vartheta(7n/8)-\vartheta(3n/4)
=\frac18n+o(n)
\]

by the prime number theorem. So even one fixed quotient cell contains geometrically admissible far gaps of linear total prime mass.

The map \(p\mapsto g=(3p-N)/2\) is injective here. Thus there is no hidden many-primes-per-gap compression in this cell.

### 4.2 Product of Apéry values: far too large

If one uses the zero condition by multiplying the relevant \(b_s\)'s, the carrier becomes much larger. In the same interval,

\[
s=n-p\in[n/8,n/4].
\]

For each such \(s\),

\[
\log b_s\ge 2\log\binom{2s}{s}=s\log16-O(\log s)=\Omega(n).
\]

There are \(\asymp n/\log n\) primes in the interval, so a product of the corresponding Apéry values has logarithm at least \(\Omega(n^2/\log n)\) if one includes all candidate positions. Multiplying over all integer positions is even worse.

The only exact stripping is

\[
K^+_{n,q}(G)
:=\prod_{\substack{p\text{ prime in the direct }q\text{-cell}\\g(p)>G}}\gcd\bigl(p,b_{n-qp}\bigr),
\]

and similarly

\[
K^-_{n,q}(G)
:=\prod_{\substack{p\text{ prime in the reflected }q\text{-cell}\\g(p)>G}}\gcd\bigl(p,b_{(q+1)p-1-n}\bigr).
\]

Since \(p\) is prime, each gcd is \(1\) or \(p\), so these are **exactly** the squarefree target products in the two cells. Therefore

\[
\log K^+_{n,q}=W^+_{n,q},\qquad
\log K^-_{n,q}=W^-_{n,q}.
\]

This is not a new carrier estimate; it is the target statement rewritten as a product.

## 5. Reciprocal pairing and the gap polynomial: the obvious resultant is tautological

This is the strongest concrete no-go supplied by the current Apéry machinery.

The current `problems/3.2/proof.tex` defines the cleared gap-polynomial numerator \(N_h(x)\) by

\[
N_1(x)=1,
\qquad
N_2(x)=P(x+1),
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x),
\]

with

\[
P(t)=(2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5).
\]

If \(b_x\equiv b_{x+h}\equiv0\pmod p\) in the nonsingular range, then \(N_h(x)\equiv0\pmod p\).

The same file records the exact reflection identity

\[
N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).
\]

For even \(h\), this forces the integer polynomial factor

\[
\boxed{2x+h+1\mid N_h(x).}
\]

Now a folded Apéry zero has the reflected zero at

\[
p-1-s=s+2g,
\]

so \(h=2g\). Consequently

\[
2s+h+1=2s+2g+1=p.
\]

Writing

\[
N_{2g}(x)=(2x+2g+1)\,Q_g(x),
\qquad Q_g(x)\in\mathbb Z[x],
\]

we obtain the **integer identity**

\[
\boxed{N_{2g}(s)=p\,Q_g(s).}
\]

Thus \(p\mid N_{2g}(s)\) is automatic for every symmetric pair \((s,p-1-s)\), whether or not \(p\mid b_s\). The reciprocal collision has used up all of its divisibility in the forced central factor.

Equivalently, in the projective-orbit language of `proof.tex`, \(N_h(x)\equiv0\) detects the collision \(\pi_x=\pi_{x+h}\). But the reflection symmetry already makes \(\pi_s=\pi_{p-1-s}\) a palindrome. The Apéry zero asks for the common projective point to have the specific zero coordinate \([B_s:D_s]=[0:1]\); the collision determinant forgets that coordinate information.

This kills the naive reciprocal/resultant plan. Saturating by \(2x+h+1\) leaves \(Q_g(x)\), but the hypothesis \(p\mid b_s\) gives no presently proved implication

\[
p\mid Q_g(s)
\]

(or equivalently \(p^2\mid N_{2g}(s)\)). Such a second-order/seam statement, if true in a useful form, would be genuinely new Apéry input rather than a consequence of folding.

### Adjacent resultants are also the wrong scale

The same repo file gives

\[
\left|\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+1})\right|
=\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(j!^3b_j)^6.
\]

Hence already the factorial part gives

\[
\log\left|\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+1})\right|
\ge18\sum_{j<h}\log(j!)
=9h^2\log h+O(h^2).
\]

For the far-gap \(q=1\) subwindow above, \(h=2g=\Theta(n)\), so this canonical resultant is vastly larger than \(e^{o(n)}\). More importantly, different target primes correspond to different \(h\)'s and different moduli; they are not common roots of a fixed adjacent pair \((N_h,N_{h+1})\). The adjacent resultant therefore gives no cross-\(g\) aggregation of the target set.

## 6. Generic resultants do not evade the height barrier

Suppose, for a fixed \(q\) and branch, one constructs an integer polynomial \(B_{n,q,\pm}(X)\) which encodes the values \(b_{s_\pm(g)}\) at all admissible gap points, perhaps by interpolation after clearing denominators. A target gives a common root modulo \(p\) of

\[
L_+(X)=N+2X
\]

and \(B_{n,q,+}(X)\), or of

\[
L_-(X)=N-2X
\]

and \(B_{n,q,-}(X)\), because \(L_\pm(g)=Dp\).

Then \(p\) divides the corresponding resultant. But for a degree-\(d\) polynomial \(B\), the linear resultant is merely

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X(N+2X,B)=2^dB(-N/2),
\]

or

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X(N-2X,B)=(-2)^dB(N/2).
\]

So this construction just evaluates a high-degree interpolation polynomial at the remote point \(\mp N/2\). There is no automatic small-height gain. The Apéry sequence is holonomic, not a bounded-degree polynomial in its index; a finite interpolation over the required moving indices has degree growing with the number of sampled positions, and denominator clearing introduces its own large factors.

But the decisive objection is independent of interpolation estimates: **if the resultant is a nonzero integer divisible by all far target primes, its logarithm is at least \(W_G(n)\)** by the height lemma. Therefore a proof that the resultant is \(e^{o(n)}\) is already a proof of the missing weighted zero-incidence bound. Resultant language does not weaken that arithmetic burden.

## 7. Compression across \(g\): symbolic compression is not height compression

One can package long products over \(g\) by factorials, double factorials, gamma quotients, product trees, or polynomial evaluations. This can make the **description length** small. It cannot make the absolute value of a nonzero integer containing distinct target primes smaller than their squarefree product.

The central-gap mechanism works because it uses only \(O(G)\) explicitly indexed geometric factors. In the far region there is no analogous bound on the number of distinct \(g\)'s. The \(q=1\) direct cell already has an injective prime-to-gap map, so a positive proportion interval of candidate primes produces a positive-proportion interval of distinct far gaps.

Therefore any genuine far-gap compression must prove that the actual Apéry zero predicate deletes almost all of those geometrically admissible primes. That is a horizontal arithmetic statement, not an algebraic repackaging of the odd-quotient relation.

## 8. Precise countermodel: the stated inputs allow linear target mass even with perfect reciprocal sparsity

Here is a falsifiable impossibility theorem for any argument that uses only:

1. the exact odd-quotient geometry;
2. a pointwise local-zero predicate at the folded index;
3. reciprocal pairing of zero positions; and even
4. a uniform vertical bound of two zeros per prime.

It does **not** replace the Apéry sequence by another sequence in the actual theorem; rather, it proves that those formal properties alone cannot imply an \(o(n)\) carrier. Some further Apéry-specific relation is logically indispensable.

Take

\[
n_j=8^j
\]

and let

\[
\mathcal P_j=\left\{p\text{ prime}:\frac34n_j\le p\le\frac78n_j\right\}.
\]

For every \(p\in\mathcal P_j\), set

\[
q=1,
\qquad
s=n_j-p,
\qquad
 g=\frac{3p-2n_j-1}{2}.
\]

Then

\[
3p=2n_j+1+2g,
\]

and

\[
s\in[n_j/8,n_j/4],
\qquad
 g\in[n_j/8-1/2,5n_j/16-1/2].
\]

Thus every one of these primes is a far-gap prime for any \(G(n)=o(n)\).

Let the reflected index be

\[
t=p-1-s=2p-n_j-1\in[n_j/2-1,3n_j/4-1].
\]

Because the scale factor from \(n_j\) to \(n_{j+1}\) is \(8\), all the \(s\)- and \(t\)-bands for different \(j\)'s are disjoint. Define one fixed integer sequence \((c_k)_{k\ge0}\) by

\[
c_s=c_t=p
\]

for every \(j\) and every \(p\in\mathcal P_j\), and set \(c_k=1\) at every unassigned index. There are no conflicting assignments.

For each designated prime \(p\), its zero set in \(0\le k<p\) is exactly

\[
\{s,p-1-s\}.
\]

So this surrogate has perfect folded reciprocal symmetry and the extremely strong vertical bound

\[
Z_c(p)\le2
\]

for every prime, yet all primes in \(\mathcal P_j\) satisfy the local-zero condition \(p\mid c_s\) on the exact direct \(q=1\) odd-quotient ray.

By the prime number theorem,

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_j}\log p
=\vartheta(7n_j/8)-\vartheta(3n_j/4)
=\frac18n_j+o(n_j).
\]

Therefore every nonzero integer carrier for these targets has

\[
\log|C_{n_j}|\ge\frac18n_j+o(n_j),
\]

not \(o(n_j)\).

So even **reciprocal zero pairing plus \(Z(p)\le2\)** cannot force a sublinear carrier. In particular, the actual unconditional vertical bound \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\) in `proof.tex` is structurally incapable, by itself, of solving the deterministic far-gap problem.

The surrogate deliberately does not satisfy the Apéry recurrence/binomial formula. That is exactly the point: any proof for the true \(b_s\) must use some property that excludes this adversarial horizontal alignment.

## 9. What additional Apéry structure is indispensable

The missing information must control the **selected zero coordinate across changing primes**, not merely the number or reciprocal arrangement of zeros inside each fixed \(\mathbb F_p\).

A minimal exact formulation is the weighted horizontal anti-alignment statement

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{q<\sqrt n}\left(W^+_{n,q}(G)+W^-_{n,q}(G)\right)=o(n).
}
\]

A stronger, clean sufficient form already identified in the repo's fixed-exponent audit is: for the surviving quotient cells,

\[
W^{\pm}_{n,q}(G)
\le \varepsilon(n)\,\Theta^{\pm}_{n,q}(G),
\qquad \varepsilon(n)\to0,
\]

uniformly in \(q\), where \(\Theta^{\pm}_{n,q}\) is the full Chebyshev/logarithmic mass of candidate primes in that cell. Since the quotient cells are disjoint,

\[
\sum_q\Theta^{\pm}_{n,q}\le O(n),
\]

and this gives the required \(o(n)\) mass immediately.

In recurrence/projective language, the extra theorem has to retain the condition

\[
[B_s:D_s]=[0:1]
\]

(or the equivalent \(p\mid b_s\)), rather than collapsing it to the automatic palindrome collision

\[
\pi_s=\pi_{p-1-s}.
\]

One possible algebraic form would be a **new saturated seam condition**: after removing the forced factor \(2s+2g+1=p\) from every reciprocal determinant, the Apéry zero would have to force some additional nonzero low-height integer congruence. For example, a theorem of the schematic type

\[
p\mid b_s
\Longrightarrow
p\mid F(s,g)
\]

with \(F\) not divisible identically by \(2s+2g+1\), together with a global resultant/norm for \(F\) of logarithmic height \(o(n)\), would genuinely close the carrier route. No such saturated low-height condition is supplied by the current gap-polynomial identities; proving one would be new substance.

Alternatively, a many-prime correlation/dispersion theorem for the actual affine section \(p\mapsto s_\pm(p)\) would supply exactly the needed structure. The current vertical zero-count, reflection, fixed-\(h\) Chebotarev information, and all-character Mellin averages do not have the necessary deterministic diagonal quantifier.

## 10. A single falsifiable necessary test

The first quotient cell gives a particularly clean test that every proposed far-gap carrier must pass. For any \(G=o(n)\), all primes in \([3n/4,7n/8]\) are eventually far-gap and direct with \(s=n-p\). Hence a necessary condition for an \(o(n)\) carrier is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{3n/4\le p\le7n/8\\p\mid b_{n-p}}}\log p=o(n).
}
\]

For \(p>5\), Apéry-Lucas with \(q=1\) makes this equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{3n/4\le p\le7n/8\\p\mid b_n}}\log p=o(n).
}
\]

Thus a computational or theoretical subsequence with

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac1n
\sum_{\substack{3n/4\le p\le7n/8\\p\mid b_{n-p}}}\log p
>0
\]

would **disprove the existence of any nonzero integer far-gap carrier of logarithmic size \(o(n)\)**, regardless of whether that carrier is presented as a product, determinant, norm, or resultant.

Conversely, proving this \(q=1\) estimate is only a necessary first step, not sufficient for all quotient cells.

```text
FALSIFIABLE CARRIER CLAIM

For every G(n)=o(n), any nonzero integer C_G(n) divisible by all
far-gap target primes satisfies

    log |C_G(n)| >= W_G(n).

Hence an e^{o(n)} carrier exists only if W_G(n)=o(n).
A necessary fixed-cell test is

    sum_{3n/4 <= p <= 7n/8, p | b_{n-p}} log p = o(n).

The reciprocal gap-polynomial condition does not improve this:
N_{2g}(s) already contains the forced factor 2s+2g+1=p.
```

## Bottom line

There is **no unconditional sublinear global carrier obtained from the exact odd-quotient relation plus the local Apéry zero predicate by fixed-\(q\) products, reciprocal pairing, the current gap-polynomial resultants, or symbolic compression across gaps**. The natural global carrier \(b_n\) has linear logarithmic height; stripped fixed-cell products are exactly the target prime product; reciprocal gap determinants lose the zero coordinate and vanish for the tautological factor \(p\); and any nonzero resultant divisible by all targets is subject to the same squarefree height lower bound.

What is indispensable is a genuinely **horizontal Apéry theorem** excluding adversarial alignment of the affine selected index with the zero set as \(p\) varies—equivalently a weighted zero-incidence estimate in these fixed-\(q\) rays, or a new saturated seam/transfer-matrix congruence that retains the zero coordinate and has a provably sublinear global norm. Without that extra structure, the q=1 countermodel above shows that the stated inputs are compatible with linear target mass even under reciprocal symmetry and the much stronger vertical bound \(Z(p)\le2\).
