# Distinguished zero fiber audit

## Verdict

The distinguished fiber $q=[0:1]$ has a stronger scalar description than an arbitrary projective fiber, but the currently proved identities do **not** rule out two far separated short four-return windows. The strongest unconditional statement is a return-multiplier lemma. Eliminating the configuration requires an additional arithmetic input about the Apéry zero fiber (for example a sign/disconjugacy theorem or a new valuation constraint).

## 1. Exact scalar restart data

Let $b_n$ be the Apéry solution and $c_n$ the companion solution. The Casoratian is

$$
 b_nc_{n-1}-b_{n-1}c_n=-1/n^3.
$$

Hence whenever $b_z\equiv0\pmod p$, the companion coordinate is nonzero and

$$
[b_z:c_z]=[0:1].
$$

The projective return condition is equivalent to

$$
N_h(z)=0 \iff \pi(z+h)=\pi(z).
$$

The stronger vector identity is

$$
\mathbf v_{x+h}=N_h(x)\mathbf v_{x+1}-(x+1)^6N_{h-1}(x+1)\mathbf v_x.
$$

Therefore, if $N_h(x)=0$,

$$
\mathbf v_{x+h}=\mu_h(x)\mathbf v_x,
\qquad
\mu_h(x)=-(x+1)^6N_{h-1}(x+1)\ne0.
$$

For a zero fiber return this gives a genuine scalar multiplier on the same vector, not merely a projective equality.

## 2. What reflection adds

The reflection identity gives

$$
\mathbf v_{p-1-n}=\rho_n\mathbf v_n,
\qquad
\rho_n=(-1)^{n+1}(n!)^{-6}.
$$

For a bridge of length $h$, the reflected bridge multiplier satisfies

$$
\mu_h(x)\mu_h(x^\vee)
=
\frac{\rho_x}{\rho_{x+h}}
=(-1)^h\left(\prod_{j=1}^h(x+j)\right)^6.
$$

Thus reflection gives a prescribed nonzero scalar. It does not create a second vanishing equation.

## 3. Two far short returns are not excluded by current identities

Suppose a zero fiber contains

$$
x_0<x_1<x_2<x_3
$$

with span $\le \sqrt p$ and a second such block far away. The first block only gives

$$
\mu_1=\mu_s(x_0)
$$

and the second gives another scalar return. Their product constraints are multiplicative. There is no contradiction because the scalar multipliers live in $\mathbf F_p^\times$.

The Casoratian prevents simultaneous vanishing of the two coordinates, but it does not bound how many times the same one-dimensional line can be revisited.

## 4. Why the abstract countermodel does not immediately transfer

The row-shift countermodel uses only:

* reflection symmetry,
* pair caps,
* short consecutive windows,
* fiber energy bounds.

It does not preserve the Apéry transfer matrices. Therefore it proves only that projective combinatorics are insufficient.

For the genuine zero fiber, the extra constraints are:

1. the recurrence

$$
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1},
$$

2. the companion restart scalar $\mu_h(x)$,

3. the reflection reciprocity above.

None of these currently gives a nonvanishing certificate for a pair of separated four-return windows.

## 5. Strongest valid lemma

**Lemma (scalar return reciprocity).** If $x$ and $x+h$ are in the distinguished fiber and $p\nmid\prod_{j=1}^h(x+j)$, then

$$
\mathbf v_{x+h}=\mu_h(x)\mathbf v_x,
$$

with $\mu_h(x)\in\mathbf F_p^\times$, and the reflected return satisfies

$$
\mu_h(x)\mu_h(p-1-x-h)
=(-1)^h\left(\prod_{j=1}^h(x+j)\right)^6.
$$

This is the strongest presently justified distinguished-fiber strengthening.

## 6. Missing input needed to prove $B^{[4]}=0$ or a strong bound

A successful exclusion theorem would need an additional statement such as:

* no two separated short intervals can contain four consecutive zeros of $b_n$;
* a uniform lower bound on the valuation/height of the scalar return multipliers;
* a disconjugacy theorem for the Apéry recurrence modulo primes;
* or a new Cartier-Frobenius constraint specific to the zero fiber.

The existing projective reflection and scalar reciprocity alone do not imply such a result.

Therefore the rigorous conclusion is:

$$
\boxed{\text{No current proved distinguished-fiber identity rules out far short four-return windows.}}
$$
The proposed $B^{[4]}$ vanishing remains an open Apéry-specific assertion, not a consequence of the presently established projective machinery.
