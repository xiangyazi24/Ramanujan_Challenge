# Q8706 audit: Apéry p^3 shift congruence

## Claim status

The checked congruence is consistent with the known Apéry block congruence, but the stated universal formula is not obtained merely from the ordinary Lucas congruence. The required statement is a genuine mod p^3 refinement.

Let
\[
B_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]
For p>=7 and 0<=u<p/2, split the defining sum for B_{p+u} into k<p and k>=p. The k>=p terms are not automatically negligible mod p^3; the standard proof requires a p-adic expansion of the factorial ratios.

## Expansion mechanism

For k<p write
\[
\binom{p+u}{k}=\binom uk\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{p+u-j}{u-j}
\]
when the denominator is nonzero, and expand the product to second order:
\[
\prod_j(1+p/(u-j))
\equiv 1+pH_1(u,k)+p^2H_2(u,k)\pmod {p^3},
\]
where
\[
H_1=\sum_j(u-j)^{-1},\qquad
H_2=\sum_{i<j}(u-i)^{-1}(u-j)^{-1}.
\]
The required harmonic identities are the standard finite-field relations
\[
\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}r^{-1}\equiv0\pmod p,
\]
\[
\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}r^{-2}\equiv0\pmod p,
\]
and their shifted versions obtained by translating the summation variable. These remove the first-order terms after the WZ telescoping identity is applied.

## WZ cancellation

A direct termwise comparison is insufficient. The Apéry summand admits a WZ decomposition whose telescoping boundary terms give the factor 5. After summing the p-adic expansions one obtains
\[
B_{p+u}\equiv 5\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\binom{p+u}{k}^2\binom{p+u+k}{k}^2\pmod {p^3}.
\]
The observed range is therefore evidence for this standard p^3 block law, not a new root-selection phenomenon.

## Root-selectivity audit

The congruence is universal in u. It says that the value at index p+u is determined modulo p^3 by a truncated block around u. It does not distinguish whether
\[
p\mid B_u.
\]
Indeed, if p divides B_u, the congruence remains valid for the same reason as for every other u. The quotient
\[
(B_{p+u}-5T_u)/p^3
\]
therefore contains information about the next p-adic layer of the recurrence, but no known argument makes it a selective detector for Apéry roots.

A root-selective statement would require an additional implication such as
\[
p\mid B_u\Longleftrightarrow Q_{p,u}\equiv0\pmod p
\]
for the quotient Q, and this does not follow from the universal block congruence.

## Conclusion

The congruence is a valid p-adic block phenomenon when proved with the full factorial/WZ machinery, but the requested harmonic-only derivation must include the telescoping identity; second-order expansion alone leaves uncancelled terms. The resulting p^3 quotient is a higher congruence invariant, not a root selector. No divisor information about p|B_u follows from universality alone.
