# 5. What information is actually equivalent to q-selectedness?

There is an important distinction between **full integer data** and **p-local data**.

From

\[
B_{p,q}=b_s-qv

\[
B_{p,q}\equiv b_s\pmod q.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
q\mid b_s\iff q\mid B_{p,q}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
q\mid b_s
\iff pq\mid A_{p,q}
\iff A_{p,q}/(pq)\in\mathbf Z.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

So as a statement in `\mathbf Z`, the scalar `A/p` **does** retain the q-hit: one tests its divisibility by q.

q\in\mathbf Z_{(p)}^\times,

\[
q\mathbf Z_{(p)}=\mathbf Z_{(p)}.

The condition

\[

A useful slogan is:

> **Q3684 has moved selectedness from `q\mid b_s` to `q\mid A/p`; it has not converted q-selectedness into p-selectedness.**

This is why `A/(pq)` looks attractive: its integer existence is equivalent to the edge condition. But using that existence as a p-fiber witness is circular unless the q-divisibility is established by a separate arithmetic theorem.

# 6. No residue-level criterion can recover the integer q-divisibility from these data alone

The failure is not tied to one formula. It is a basic localization obstruction.

Fix distinct primes p,q and fix residues

\[
\bar x,\bar v\in\mathbf F_p.

There are integers `x` in **both** q-divisibility classes with the same residue `\bar x\pmod p`:

- a q-divisible lift is obtained by choosing `\nu` with
  \[
  q\nu\equiv\bar x\pmod p
  \]
  and setting `x=q\nu`;
- a q-nondivisible lift with the same p-residue exists because among the arithmetic progression
  \[
  x_0+kp
  \]
  exactly one residue class of `k\pmod q` is divisible by q; choose any other class.

Thus the reduction map to `\mathbf F_p` identifies integers from the selected and nonselected q-classes.

Equivalently, no function of only

(b_s\bmod p,\ v\bmod p,\ q\bmod p)

can be equivalent, as a formal arithmetic predicate, to

\[
q\mid b_s.

For the actual Apéry orbit it is logically possible that some **additional Apéry-specific cross-prime theorem** cuts down the possible tuples so strongly that a p-residue criterion becomes valid. This is the exact scope of the no-go: it refutes the claimed deduction from the single source, not every conceivable Apéry cross-characteristic identity.

# 9. Exterior/Fitting constructions do not restore information erased by localization

This can be stated cleanly at the module level.

Let `R=\mathbf Z_{(p)}` or `\mathbf Z_p`. Since `q\in R^\times`, the relation

\[
q\nu=b_s
\]

is isomorphic over R to simply adjoining the coordinate

\[
\nu=q^{-1}b_s.
\]

Thus a presentation with this quotient variable and the presentation after eliminating it define isomorphic R-modules.

Fitting ideals, ranks, kernels after tensoring with `\mathbf F_p`, and exterior powers are invariants of this R-module up to the usual unit changes. They cannot recover the global sublattice condition

\[
b_s\in q\mathbf Z
\]

because that condition has already become

\[
b_s\in qR=R.
\]

For the source itself there is an even simpler statement: (2.3) says all source vectors generate the same rank-one R/pR source line.

Therefore the following operations do **not**, by themselves, evade the obstruction:

- replacing the source by a Green response to it;
- wedging that response with fixed homogeneous Apéry/companion columns;
- taking determinants or exterior powers linear/homogeneous in the single source column;
- adjoining `b_s/q` as a quotient variable and then reducing modulo p;
- multiplying or dividing by q in any p-local formula;
- passing to Smith/Fitting rank after q has become a unit.

All such operations can reorganize the p-local data, but they do not reinstate the lost q-integrality predicate.

# 10. Could a divided/exterior construction from this source still work?

Only with **additional information not contained in the single-source p-fiber**.

There are three logically distinct possibilities.

## 10.1 A genuine q-adic/global-integrality datum

Keep the condition

\[
q\mid B_{p,q}
\]

as an integral or q-adic valuation statement **before** localizing at p. This is exactly equivalent to q-selectedness by (5.1). It is valid, but by itself it is not a compression or a new p-rank mechanism; it simply carries the original q-hit along.

To become useful, one would need a universal integer presentation whose global Smith/Fitting invariant couples the p- and q-adic conditions without paying one independent q-divisibility row per edge.

## 10.2 A nonlinear cross-prime identity

For example, a universally defined scalar `C_{p,q}` built from Apéry/Bockstein data could conceivably satisfy

\[
p\mid C_{p,q}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q\mid b_s
\]

on the p-selected locus. But this must be proved from a new identity whose reduction modulo p is genuinely nonlinear or otherwise retains cross-characteristic arithmetic. It cannot follow from merely writing `b_s=q\nu` and then dividing by the p-unit q.

## 10.3 A second independent source/constraint

A second universal source whose p-reduction is not in the same line `\mathbf F_p\delta_r`, and whose compatibility depends on the q-hit through a non-unit mechanism, could create codimension two. This is precisely what the one-source construction lacks.

Without one of these additions, no divided/exterior manipulation of the single source supplies the missing selected-neighbor multiplicity.

A particularly important clarification is that **p-adic integrality at p is not enough**. Because q is a p-adic unit,

for every candidate q once `B_{p,q}\in\mathbf Z_p`. The relevant extra integrality is q-adic/global integrality, or a genuinely cross-prime nonlinear relation.

# 11. Strongest exact scoped theorem

Here is a formulation suitable for reuse.

## Theorem — one selected endpoint makes the Q3684 source p-degenerate for every neighbor

Let `p\ne q` be primes, let `s,r` be indices, and let `b_s,b_r\in\mathbf Z`. Assume

\[
p\mid b_r,
\qquad v=b_r/p.
\]

Define

\[
f_{p,q}=p\delta_s-q\delta_r,
\qquad
A_{p,q}=p b_s-q b_r.
\]

Then:

1. `A_{p,q}=p(b_s-qv)`, hence `p\mid A_{p,q}` without any hypothesis on `b_s`.
2. In `\mathbf F_p`,
   \[
   f_{p,q}=-q\delta_r,
   \]
   so all `f_{p,q}` with `q\ne p` span the same one-dimensional source line.
3. The first divided moment
   \[
   B_{p,q}=A_{p,q}/p=b_s-qv
   \]
   is integral for every q.
4. Over `\mathbf F_p`, with `\widetilde\nu_q=q^{-1}b_s`,
   \[
   q^{-1}B_{p,q}=\widetilde\nu_q-v.
   \]
   This identity requires no q-divisibility of `b_s`.
5. If `q\mid b_s` and `\nu=b_s/q\in\mathbf Z`, then `\widetilde\nu_q\equiv\nu\pmod p`; no new p-equation results.
6. The q-hit is characterized integrally by
   \[
   q\mid b_s
   \iff q\mid B_{p,q}
   \iff A_{p,q}/(pq)\in\mathbf Z,
   \]
   and this condition becomes vacuous after localization at p because q is a unit.
7. Hence any p-rank defect arising from the undivided single source occurs for every candidate q once p is selected. Adjoining the q-quotient linearly cannot turn that defect into selected-neighbor multiplicity.

### Proof

Items 1--6 are the displayed calculations above. For item 7, reducing the source modulo p gives a unit multiple of `\delta_r`; therefore every linear image of the source is a unit multiple of the image of `\delta_r`, and every homogeneous determinant/exterior expression has the same zero/nonzero status. A quotient row `q\nu-b_s=0` has a unit pivot modulo p and is eliminable. Thus neither construction adds a q-selected p-rank condition. ∎

# 12. Final assessment of Q3684

The identity

\[
\frac{A}{pq}=\nu-v
\]

on a true edge is algebraically correct.

The claimed interpretation is not.

The decisive distinction is:

```text
integer/global statement:
    q | b_s
    <=> q | (A/p)
    <=> A/(pq) is an integer

p-local statement:
    q is a unit
    q^{-1}(A/p) = q^{-1} b_s - v
    exists for every candidate q
```

So the two-spike source does **not** make the q-endpoint selectedness visible at the p-fiber. Before division, it gives a p-rank defect for every q. After one p-division, it gives an ordinary p-local scalar for every q. The second division by q is exactly where the original q-divisibility is being reintroduced, and modulo p that division is a unit operation.

Accordingly:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Q3684 does not escape the unit-witness no-go.}
}
\]

The source is still useful as a bookkeeping identity and perhaps as one row inside a genuinely global two-prime lattice construction. But **by itself** it cannot support the needed neighbor-count multiplicity theorem. The next positive object must carry something that is not annihilated by localization at p: a nonprincipal cross-prime identity, a second independent source, or explicit q-adic/global-integrality information that is then compressed nontrivially rather than merely restating `q\mid b_s`.
