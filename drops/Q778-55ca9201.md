# Q778: fixed-q pair energy and logarithmic-gap concentration audit

## Verdict

The logarithmic-gap decomposition is a valid reduction, but the currently banked aligned-pair estimate does not close the energy bound. The missing object is not a pointwise prime-gap estimate; it is an aggregate bound on **moving Apéry divisibility intersections along a fixed gap**.

The strongest unconditional statement obtained from the proposed route is a decomposition plus a conditional gate. It does not improve the known vertical bound.

---

## 1. Exact pair-energy decomposition

Fix q and write

\[
I_q(n)=(n/(q+1),n/q]\cap \mathbb P.
\]

For \(p\in I_q(n)\), define

\[
\epsilon_p(n)=1_{p\mid b_n}.
\]

Then

\[
T_q(n)=\sum_{p\in I_q(n)}\epsilon_p(n),
\]

and

\[
E_q(N)=\sum_{N<n\le 2N}\binom{T_q(n)}2
\]

becomes exactly

\[
E_q(N)=\frac12\sum_{p\ne r}\#\{n:N<n\le2N,
 p,r\in I_q(n),
 p\mid b_n,
 r\mid b_n\}.
\]

Write the ordered difference as

\[
h=r-p.
\]

Hence

\[
E_q(N)=\frac12\sum_{h\ne0}E_q(h;N),
\]

where

\[
E_q(h;N)=\sum_p\#\{n: p,p+h\in I_q(n),
 p\mid b_n,
 p+h\mid b_n\}.
\]

The conditions split into three cases.

### Interior transverse pairs

Both primes are strictly inside the interval. Then

\[
 n\in(qp,q(p+h)]\cap((q+1)p,(q+1)(p+h)]
\]
with the corresponding reflected boundary convention. These are the genuine pair-energy terms.

### Endpoint collisions

If \(p=n/q\) or \(p=n/(q+1)\), the prime lies on a moving boundary. Such terms have at most one degree of freedom and contribute an endpoint error bounded by the number of boundary primes, not by the bulk energy.

### Non-transverse collisions

If the same prime is counted twice, the falling factorial definition removes it. Thus no diagonal \(p=r\) contribution occurs.

This gives an exact gap identity:

\[
E_q(N)=\sum_{1\le h\ll N}\mathcal E_q(h,N)+O(\mathcal B_q(N)),
\]

where \(\mathcal B_q\) is the boundary correction.

---

## 2. Natural scale per gap

There are about

\[
N/\log N
\]

primes in a dyadic block. A target bound

\[
E_q(N)=o(N^2/\log^2N)
\]

requires the average fixed-gap contribution to satisfy

\[
\mathcal E_q(h,N)=o(N/\log^2N)
\]

on average over the relevant gaps.

This is the natural scale. Any estimate merely of size

\[
O(N/\log N)
\]

per gap is too weak by one logarithm.

---

## 3. Does the O(h) aligned-pair count close the problem?

No.

The banked statement says that for a fixed small gap h the number of aligned prime pairs is bounded by roughly

\[
O(h).
\]

Even granting this in the strongest possible form, summing

\[
\sum_{h\le C\log N}O(h)=O((\log N)^2)
\]

only controls the number of prime pairs. It does not control the Apéry conditions

\[
p\mid b_n,\qquad p+h\mid b_n.
\]

The missing multiplicative factor is the moving-target correlation

\[
\#\{n:p\mid b_n,\ p+h\mid b_n\}.
\]

The recurrence does not currently turn this into a small quantity.

For large gaps the number of possible h is large enough that even a weak bound gives no saving.

Therefore the logarithmic-gap split is a valid organization, but not an estimate.

---

## 4. Attempted Apéry recurrence improvement

The order-two recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}
\]

implies a two-dimensional state evolution modulo p. For one prime p this gives a finite-state constraint. For two primes p and p+h it gives a product state modulo p(p+h).

The obstacle is that the state spaces are different for every pair. There is no fixed finite carrier whose height is independent of h.

The first-jet formulas have the same limitation: they describe a single boundary unit. They do not supply a uniform estimate for simultaneous divisibility by two moving primes.

---

## 5. Weakest aggregate statement that would close E_q

A sufficient statement strictly weaker than LGTQ-3 is:

> **Pair-energy gate.** For every fixed q,
> \[
> \sum_{1\le h\le N}
> \#\{(p,n):p,p+h\in I_q(n),
> p\mid b_n,(p+h)\mid b_n\}
> =o_q(N^2/\log^2N).
> \]

This is essentially the desired energy statement rewritten by gap, but it is genuinely aggregate: it allows exceptional gaps and does not demand any individual gap bound.

It is weaker than LGTQ-3 because LGTQ-3 extracts a fixed small gap vector occurring in a dense row and controls every transverse triple. The pair-energy gate only controls the total second moment.

It is stronger than current moving-target discrepancy because it concerns actual Apéry divisibility intersections, not just locations of prime targets.

---

## 6. Height audit

No height improvement follows from the gap decomposition alone.

- Counting prime pairs gives at best combinatorial savings of logarithmic size.
- The missing divisibility intersection may still have size comparable to the number of candidate n.
- The recurrence state modulo p(p+h) has height depending on the modulus.
- Scalar elimination over a finite packet again produces a carrier whose height is essentially the product of packet primes.

Therefore no \(o(N^2/\log^2N)\) estimate follows without a new arithmetic input.

---

## Final classification

**Proved:** exact gap decomposition and the natural per-gap target scale.

**Conditional:** the pair-energy gate implies the fixed-q slice bound.

**Finite evidence:** recurrence and first-jet formulas show structure on tested diagonals but no aggregate two-prime cancellation.

**Scoped no-go:** logarithmic prime-gap concentration plus the O(h) aligned-pair count cannot by itself control Apéry divisibility intersections.

The missing arithmetic gate is an aggregate moving-target two-prime correlation estimate, not a stronger prime-gap theorem.
