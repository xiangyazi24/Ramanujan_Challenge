# Q772: exact diagonal aggregate and what current inputs can prove

## 1. Exact diagonal formulation

Let the fixed-q window be
\[
\frac n{q+1}<p\le \frac nq,
\qquad n=qp+r,
\]
and let
\[
J=\left\lfloor\frac{n-q}{2q+1}\right\rfloor .
\]
The boundary valuation computation gives, up to the single possible midpoint overlap, the exact carrier
\[
R_q(n)=\prod_{0\le j\le J} \operatorname{rad}\left(\gcd(b_j,L_D(n,j)L_R(n,j))\right),
\]
where
\[
L_D(n,j)=\frac{n-j}{q},
\qquad
L_R(n,j)=\frac{n+1+j}{q+1},
\]
with the convention that only integral prime values are included.

A convenient aggregate is therefore
\[
H_q(n)=\prod_{j=0}^{J}\gcd(b_j,L_D(n,j))\gcd(b_j,L_R(n,j)),
\]
with duplicate factors removed when the two linear forms give the same prime. Because the fixed-q boundary factors are squarefree except for O_q(1) midpoint residues,
\[
\log R_q(n)=\log H_q(n)+O_q(\log n).
\]

Thus the question reduces exactly to bounding the diagonal gcd aggregate.

## 2. Reversing the summation over n

Sum over \(N\le n\le2N\). For the D-form, a prime p contributes when
\[
n=qp+j.
\]
Hence for fixed p and j there is at most one n. The total contribution is controlled by
\[
\sum_{j\le J}\sum_{\substack{p\text{ prime}\ p\asymp N/q}}
\mathbf 1_{p\mid b_j}\log p.
\]
The R-form is identical after the change
\[
n=(q+1)p-j-1.
\]
Therefore reversal does not create extra averaging in b_j; it merely rewrites the problem as a weighted count of large prime divisors of the initial segment
\[
b_0,b_1,\dots,b_{\asymp N}.
\]

## 3. What follows from only log b_j=O(j)

The trivial estimate is
\[
\sum_{p\mid b_j}\log p\le \log |b_j|=O(j).
\]
Summing over \(j\le cN\) gives
\[
\sum_{N\le n\le2N}\log H_q(n)
\ll_q \sum_{j\le cN}j
=O_q(N^2).
\]
After division by the number of n, this gives only
\[
\frac1N\sum_{n\in[N,2N]}\log H_q(n)=O_q(N),
\]
which is the full trivial scale and is useless for proving \(o(N)\) pointwise.

## 4. Adding Brun/Selberg sieve or large sieve

A sieve can estimate the number of indices j for which a fixed collection of linear forms are simultaneously prime. It does not control the event
\[
p\mid b_j.
\]
The sequence \(b_j\) is not random from the viewpoint of available theorems, and Apéry-Lucas/reflection only gives congruence transport:
\[
b_{p-1-j}\equiv b_j\pmod p.
\]
It gives no upper bound for the number of large prime divisors of b_j.

Consequently Brun/Selberg applied only to the linear forms gives at best the density of possible carriers, not the size of the gcd factors. The resulting bound remains compatible with
\[
\log H_q(n)=\Theta(n)
\]
for a sparse exceptional subsequence.

## 5. Resultant and P-recursive approaches

For a P-recursive sequence one can form resultants involving the recurrence, but the available recurrence has polynomial coefficients of size O(j). Eliminating a fixed prime p through a recurrence segment of length m produces coefficients whose height is at least exponential in m in the worst case, giving logarithmic size O(m), not o(m).

The recurrence therefore cannot by itself prove that large primes dividing b_j are rare. The obstruction is that divisibility modulo p propagates through the recurrence, but the modulus p changes with j through
\[
p=(n-j)/q\quad\text{or}\quad p=(n+1+j)/(q+1).
\]
There is no fixed characteristic-zero resultant whose value is forced to contain all these varying primes with sublinear height.

## 6. Weakest missing estimate

A sufficient additional theorem would be a large-prime divisor mean estimate of the form:

For every fixed q and every epsilon > 0,
\[
\sum_{j\le X}
\sum_{\substack{p>X/(q+1)\\p\mid b_j}}
\log p
\le \epsilon X^2.
\]

This is exactly the averaged statement needed, because reversal gives
\[
\sum_{N\le n\le2N}\log H_q(n)
\le
\sum_{j\le cN}
\sum_{p\mid b_j,\ p\asymp N}\log p.
\]
To upgrade from average n to every n, one needs the stronger pointwise form:
\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in(n/(q+1),n/q]\\p\mid b_j\text{ for some diagonal }j}}
\log p=o_q(n).
\]

Equivalently, it is enough to prove that the diagonal large-prime divisor mass of every Apéry prefix is sublinear.

## 7. Status of possible inputs

Known:

- Apéry recurrence and integrality.
- Lucas congruences and reflection identities.
- Linear-form sieve estimates for primes.
- General growth bounds \(\log b_j=O(j)\).

Not known from these inputs:

- Independence of large primes dividing different b_j.
- A Bombieri-Vinogradov type theorem for Apéry divisibility.
- A pointwise large-prime-factor sparsity theorem for b_j.
- A variance estimate strong enough to remove exceptional n.

Therefore the diagonal aggregate reformulation is exact and useful, but elementary sieve plus P-recursive structure alone stops at the trivial O(n) logarithmic scale. The missing ingredient is precisely a large-prime divisor sparsity estimate for Apéry numbers, not a further manipulation of the prime diagonal.
