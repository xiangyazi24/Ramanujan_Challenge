# Large-sieve variance for CRT discrepancy: exact correction, no-go theorem, and the viable signed route

I inspected the current project state at main commit `1fbbc40a58ba9faf4a123b7a2276eaaa1acd090c`, in particular:

- `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` (`thm:hm3-palm-characterization`, `prop:hm3-anchored-star`),

- `problems/3.2/hm3_exploration.md`.

The decisive conclusion is that the proposed target with absolute discrepancies is false, including in the ideal independent model. The numerical `z`-scores are evidence for cancellation in the **signed** aggregate, not for a small sum of absolute tuple discrepancies.

```text
Correct target:

  delta_S = N_S - mu_S,
  E_r     = sum_{|S|=r} delta_S = F_r - M e_r(rho),
  |E_r|   should be O(sqrt(M e_r(rho))).

Incorrect replacement:

  E_r = sum D_S,  where D_S = |delta_S|.
```

The rest of this answer proves the obstruction, audits the five proposed approaches, and gives the exact signed pair-anchored route that remains viable.

## 1. Exact notation and the first correction

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},\qquad
z_p=|Z_p|,\qquad \rho_p=\frac{z_p}{p},\qquad M=X^2.
\]

For an unordered set \(S\subset\mathcal P_X\) of size \(r\), put

\[
P_S=\prod_{p\in S}p,\qquad
L_S=|A_S|=\prod_{p\in S}z_p,\qquad
\mu_S=\frac{M L_S}{P_S}=M\prod_{p\in S}\rho_p,
\]

and

\[
N_S=\#\{a\in A_S:a<M\},\qquad
\delta_S=N_S-\mu_S,\qquad D_S=|\delta_S|.
\]

Then, exactly,

\[
F_r=\sum_{|S|=r}N_S,\qquad
B_r:=M e_r((\rho_p)_p)=\sum_{|S|=r}\mu_S,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{F_r=B_r+\sum_{|S|=r}\delta_S.}
\]

Only the inequality

\[
|F_r-B_r|\le \sum_{|S|=r}D_S
\]

is true. Replacing the signed error by \(\sum D_S\) destroys the cancellation seen numerically.

## 2. A rigorous no-go theorem for \(\sum_S D_S\)

### Proposition 2.1 — rare-event absolute-discrepancy identity

Let

\[
\mu_*:=\max_{|S|=r,\,L_S>0}\mu_S,
\]

and suppose \(\mu_*<1\). Let

\[
\mathcal H=\{S:|S|=r,\ N_S>0\}
\]

be the family of tuples having at least one CRT representative below \(M\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|S|=r}D_S
=B_r+F_r-2\sum_{S\in\mathcal H}\mu_S.}
\]

Consequently,

\[
B_r+(1-2\mu_*)F_r
\le \sum_{|S|=r}D_S
\le B_r+F_r.
\]

In particular, if \(\mu_*\le 1/2\), then

\[
\boxed{\sum_{|S|=r}D_S\ge B_r.}
\]

If additionally

\[
F_r=B_r+O(\sqrt{B_r})
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mu_*=o(1),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|S|=r}D_S
=2B_r+O(\sqrt{B_r}+\mu_*B_r).}
\]

#### Proof

If \(S\notin\mathcal H\), then \(N_S=0\), hence \(D_S=\mu_S\). If \(S\in\mathcal H\), then \(N_S\ge1>\mu_S\), hence \(D_S=N_S-\mu_S\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_S D_S
&=\sum_{S\notin\mathcal H}\mu_S
  +\sum_{S\in\mathcal H}(N_S-\mu_S)\\
&=\sum_S\mu_S+\sum_SN_S-2\sum_{S\in\mathcal H}\mu_S\\
&=B_r+F_r-2\sum_{S\in\mathcal H}\mu_S.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(|\mathcal H|\le F_r\), one has

\[
0\le\sum_{S\in\mathcal H}\mu_S\le\mu_*F_r,
\]

which gives all displayed bounds. ∎

### Interpretation

For fixed \(r\ge3\), bounded \(z_p\), and \(p\asymp X\),

\[
\mu_S\asymp X^{2-r}=o(1).
\]

Thus this is exactly the rare-event regime. If the signed count has the conjectured square-root fluctuation, the absolute-discrepancy sum is not \(O(\sqrt{B_r})\); it is asymptotic to \(2B_r\).

The ideal Bernoulli model says the same thing. For one uniformly random CRT point, \(N_S\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\mu_S)\), so

\[
\mathbb E|N_S-\mu_S|=2\mu_S(1-\mu_S),
\qquad
\mathbb E(N_S-\mu_S)^2=\mu_S(1-\mu_S).
\]

Therefore the model predicts

\[
\sum_S D_S\asymp 2B_r,\qquad
\sum_S\delta_S^2\asymp B_r,\qquad
\sum_S\delta_S\text{ fluctuates on scale }\sqrt{B_r}.
\]

These are three different statistics. The numerical `z`-scores test the third one.

## 3. Why the observed sparsity does not supply the missing power of \(X\)

Tuples containing a prime with \(Z_p=\varnothing\) contribute exactly zero to every quantity, so the active-prime sparsity is already built into \(e_r(\rho)\). It does not give an additional saving after the baseline has been formed.

Let

\[
N_+=\#\{p\in\mathcal P_X:z_p>0\}.
\]

In the empirical regime where \(N_+\asymp cX/\log X\) and the nonzero \(z_p\)'s are bounded above and below by constants,

\[
\#\{S:A_S\ne\varnothing\}
=\binom{N_+}{r}
\asymp \frac{X^r}{(\log X)^r},
\]

while

\[
\mu_S\asymp X^{2-r}.
\]

Hence

\[
B_r=\sum_S\mu_S
\asymp \frac{X^2}{(\log X)^r}.
\]

The factor \((47/135)^r\), or any limiting active proportion \(c^r\), is only a constant for fixed \(r\). The missing factor in the trivial CRT bound is the landing probability

\[
\frac{M}{P_S}\asymp X^{2-r},
\]

not the probability that all selected zero sets are nonempty.

There is also an exact scale comparison. Since \(X<p\le2X\),

\[
2^{-r}X^{2-r}e_r((z_p)_p)
\le B_r
\le X^{2-r}e_r((z_p)_p).
\]

The tuplewise trivial error is bounded by

\[
\sum_{|S|=r}|A_S|=e_r((z_p)_p),
\]

so it loses precisely a factor \(\asymp X^{r-2}\). This loss is constant for \(r=2\), is \(X\) for \(r=3\), and is \(X^4\) for \(r=6\).

Thus \(r=3\), not \(r=4\) or \(5\), is the first genuinely obstructed moment.

## 4. The correct Fourier formula

There are two useful forms.

### 4.1 Per-tuple CRT completion

For \(p\in S\), write

\[
P_{S,p}=P_S/p,
\qquad
u_{S,p}P_{S,p}\equiv1\pmod p,
\]

and define

\[
S_p(t)=\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(-tz),
\qquad
V_M(h;P)=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}e_P(hm).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\delta_S
=\frac1{P_S}\sum_{h=1}^{P_S-1}
V_M(h;P_S)
\prod_{p\in S}S_p(h\nu_{S,p}).}
\]

The multiplier \(\nu_{S,p}=(P_S/p)^{-1}\pmod p\) is essential. The local frequency is not simply \(h\bmod p\); it depends on every other prime in \(S\).

An Erdős–Turán truncation gives, up to an absolute constant,

\[
D_S
\ll \frac{L_S}{H+1}
+\sum_{1\le h\le H}\frac1h
\left|\prod_{p\in S}S_p(h\nu_{S,p})\right|.
\]

This form is unsuitable for the desired cancellation because it inserts absolute values before summing over \(h\) and before summing over \(S\).

### 4.2 Exact centered support decomposition

Let

\[
f_p(m)=\mathbf1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p},
\qquad
g_p(m)=f_p(m)-\rho_p.
\]

Define the normalized local Fourier transform

\[
\widehat f_p(t)
=\frac1p\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(-tz),
\qquad
\widehat f_p(0)=\rho_p.
\]

For nonempty \(T\subset\mathcal P_X\), put

\[
C_T(M)=\sum_{m<M}\prod_{p\in T}g_p(m).
\]

Then

\[
C_T(M)
=\sum_{(t_p)\in\prod_{p\in T}\mathbb F_p^\times}
\left(\prod_{p\in T}\widehat f_p(t_p)\right)
V_M\left(\sum_{p\in T}\frac{t_p}{p}\right),
\]

where \(V_M(\alpha)=\sum_{m<M}e(m\alpha)\).

Expanding \(f_p=\rho_p+g_p\), summing over \(S\), and then over \(m\), gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
F_r-B_r
=\sum_{j=1}^r
\sum_{\substack{T\subset\mathcal P_X\\|T|=j}}
 e_{r-j}((\rho_q)_{q\notin T})\,C_T(M).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the correct dual formulation. It keeps every sign and groups the error by the number of genuinely nonzero local Fourier frequencies.

### The one-prime support is already harmless

For \(T=\{p\}\),

\[
C_{\{p\}}(M)
=\#\Omega_p-M\rho_p,
\]

and residue counting gives

\[
|C_{\{p\}}(M)|\le z_p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
|E_{r,1}|
&:=\left|
\sum_p e_{r-1}(\rho_{\ne p})C_{\{p\}}(M)
\right|\\
&\le \sum_p z_p e_{r-1}(\rho_{\ne p})\\
&\le 2X\sum_p\rho_p e_{r-1}(\rho_{\ne p})\\
&=2rX e_r(\rho)
=\frac{2r}{X}B_r.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the first genuinely new term in (4.1) is the two-prime centered correlation.

### Primewise Parseval has the right coefficient scale

Parseval gives the exact identity

\[
\sum_{t\ne0}|\widehat f_p(t)|^2
=\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
\]

For

\[
a_{T,\mathbf t}
=e_{r-|T|}(\rho_{\notin T})
 \prod_{p\in T}\widehat f_p(t_p),
\qquad
\alpha_{T,\mathbf t}=\sum_{p\in T}\frac{t_p}{p},
\]

one obtains

\[
F_r-B_r-E_{r,1}
=\sum_{2\le|T|\le r}\sum_{\mathbf t}a_{T,\mathbf t}V_M(\alpha_{T,\mathbf t}).
\]

Moreover,

\[
\sum_{|T|=j}\sum_{\mathbf t}|a_{T,\mathbf t}|^2
\le
\frac{\lambda_X^{r-j}}{(r-j)!}
\binom rj e_r(\rho),
\]

because

\[
\sum_{|T|=j}
\left(\prod_{p\in T}\rho_p\right)
 e_{r-j}(\rho_{\notin T})
=\binom rj e_r(\rho).
\]

Hence, in the sparse regime \(\lambda_X\le1\),

\[
\sum_{2\le|T|\le r}\sum_{\mathbf t}|a_{T,\mathbf t}|^2
\ll_r e_r(\rho).
\]

So the local Fourier coefficients have exactly the correct total energy. The missing theorem is cancellation between those coefficients and the interval kernels \(V_M(\alpha)\).

## 5. Why Gallagher/Montgomery–Vaughan does not provide that theorem

### 5.1 A mean-square bound over tuples is not the desired signed bound

If a large sieve proved the ideal estimate

\[
\sum_{|S|=r}\delta_S^2\ll B_r,
\]

then for \(T_r=\#\{S:|S|=r,A_S\ne\varnothing\}\), Cauchy only gives

\[
\left|\sum_{|S|=r}\delta_S\right|
\le \sqrt{T_r}\left(\sum_S\delta_S^2\right)^{1/2}
\ll\sqrt{T_rB_r}.
\]

This is larger than \(\sqrt{B_r}\) by \(\sqrt{T_r}\). Equivalently, for a uniformly random tuple \(S\), a bound on \(\mathbb E_S\delta_S^2\) controls the RMS of one tuple, not the mean \(\mathbb E_S\delta_S\). The latter is exactly the global signed error divided by the number of tuples.

To obtain the numerical scale one must control the off-diagonal covariance:

\[
(F_r-B_r)^2
=\sum_S\delta_S^2
+2\sum_{S<T}\delta_S\delta_T.
\]

The near-vanishing of the off-diagonal aggregate is the new arithmetic statement.

### 5.2 Erdős–Turán has already discarded the needed cancellation

The proposed Erdős–Turán inequality uses

\[
\sum_h\frac1h\left|\prod_pS_p(\cdots)\right|.
\]

After this absolute value is inserted, expanding the square cannot recover cancellation among \(h\)'s. A large-sieve attempt must start from the exact signed formula, not from the Erdős–Turán upper bound.

### 5.3 The standard dual large sieve loses a full factor \(M\), even before spacing

Let \(i=(T,\mathbf t)\), and abbreviate \(a_i=a_{T,\mathbf t}\), \(\alpha_i=\alpha_{T,\mathbf t}\). Then

\[
L:=\sum_i a_iV_M(\alpha_i)
=\sum_{m<M}\sum_i a_ie(m\alpha_i).
\]

If the frequencies are \(\delta\)-spaced, Montgomery–Vaughan gives a bound of the shape

\[
\sum_{m<M}\left|\sum_i a_ie(m\alpha_i)\right|^2
\le (M-1+\delta^{-1})\sum_i|a_i|^2.
\]

Cauchy in \(m\) therefore yields only

\[
|L|^2
\le M(M-1+\delta^{-1})\sum_i|a_i|^2.
\]

The desired random-phase scale would be

\[
|L|^2\ll M\sum_i|a_i|^2.
\]

Thus even in the unrealistically favorable case \(\delta^{-1}\ll M\), the standard large sieve is too weak by a factor \(M\). It controls an \(L^2\) norm over \(m\); the problem asks for cancellation in the zero-frequency sum over \(m\).

### 5.4 The actual spacing parameter is also enormous

For a reduced frequency

\[
\alpha_{T,\mathbf t}=\sum_{p\in T}\frac{t_p}{p}\pmod1,
\qquad t_p\ne0,
\]

the pair \((T,\mathbf t)\) is uniquely determined by \(\alpha\). Indeed, equality of two such sums, after multiplication by the product of all involved primes and reduction modulo a prime appearing on only one side, is impossible; after the supports agree, reduction modulo each prime forces the coefficients to agree.

For top support \(|T|=r\), the number of such frequencies is

\[
R_r=\sum_{|T|=r}\prod_{p\in T}(p-1)
\asymp_r \frac{X^{2r}}{(\log X)^r}.
\]

Any \(R_r\) points on the circle have minimum spacing at most \(1/R_r\), so the large-sieve spacing constant satisfies

\[
\delta^{-1}\ge R_r
\asymp_r \frac{X^{2r}}{(\log X)^r}
\]

for a family in which these coefficients are nonzero. For a two-point zero set at an odd prime, every nonzero local Fourier coefficient is nonzero, so this is not a merely formal worst case.

Hence the Montgomery–Vaughan constant is vastly larger than \(M=X^2\).

### 5.5 Gallagher's variants do not fix the issue

Gallagher's 1967 large sieve is another density/mean-square formulation; it does not produce the coefficient-specific off-diagonal cancellation above. If “Gallagher” means the 1971 **larger sieve**, that tool bounds the cardinality of a set of integers occupying few residue classes. It does not control the factorial multiplicity \(\binom{K(m)}r\), and it permits a single integer to lie in the selected class for a very large number of primes.

The current repository already contains exactly such a countermodel.

## 6. The repository's anchored-star proposition is a direct no-go theorem

`problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex`, Proposition `prop:hm3-anchored-star`, constructs for nearly all shell primes

\[
Z_p^*=\{m_0\bmod p,\ p-1-(m_0\bmod p)\}
\]

with a fixed \(m_0<M\). These sets have size two, are reflection-invariant, avoid endpoints and consecutive roots, satisfy the interval sparsity condition, satisfy the universal second-moment bound, have natural centered second moment, and have uniformly bounded row codegrees.

For every active \(r\)-tuple \(S\), the residue choice \(m_0\bmod p\) for every \(p\in S\) has CRT representative exactly \(m_0\). Thus

\[
N_S\ge1.
\]

If \(r\ge3\), then

\[
\mu_S=\frac{2^rM}{P_S}\ll_r X^{2-r}=o(1),
\]

so

\[
\delta_S\ge1-o(1)
\]

with the same sign for every active tuple. If \(L\asymp X/\log X\) primes are active, then

\[
F_r^*\ge\binom Lr
\asymp_r\frac{X^r}{(\log X)^r},
\]

whereas

\[
B_r^*=M e_r((2/p)_p)
\asymp_r\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^r}.
\]

Therefore

\[
F_r^*/B_r^*\asymp X^{r-2},
\]

and also

\[
\sum_S(\delta_S^*)^2
\gg\frac{X^r}{(\log X)^r},
\]

not \(O(B_r^*)\).

This proves that no large-sieve theorem using only:

- \(z_p=O(1)\),
- active-prime sparsity,
- reflection,
- primewise Fourier Parseval,
- interval sparsity of each \(Z_p\),
- the proved second moment, or
- bounded row codegrees

can establish the desired variance. A successful theorem must use an Apéry-specific cross-prime fact that excludes coherent common-row alignment. That is precisely the missing incidence theorem, not a consequence of the classical large sieve.

## 7. Efron–Stein does not apply to the deterministic statistic

There is no probability space in the given \(F_r\). The prime sets \(Z_p\), the interval, and \(F_r\) are fixed. Efron–Stein requires a function of independent random coordinates and an independent resampling of one coordinate.

The deterministic leave-one-prime difference is nevertheless explicit. Let \(F_r^{(-p)}\) denote the statistic with prime \(p\) removed. Then

\[
\Delta_pF_r
:=F_r-F_r^{(-p)}
=\sum_{m\in\Omega_p}\binom{K(m)-1}{r-1}.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{\sum_p\Delta_pF_r=rF_r.}
\]

Every contributing \(r\)-set is counted once for each of its \(r\) primes. Thus these deterministic differences encode the size of \(F_r\) itself; they are not variance increments.

One can introduce independent Bernoulli selectors \(\xi_p\) and define a randomly thinned statistic

\[
G_r(\xi)
=\sum_{|S|=r}N_S\prod_{p\in S}\xi_p.
\]

If \(\mathbb P(\xi_p=1)=\theta\), then

\[
\mathbb EG_r(\xi)=\theta^rF_r.
\]

Thus the unknown deterministic \(F_r\) is already in the mean. Efron–Stein can bound fluctuations around \(\theta^rF_r\), but it does not compare \(F_r\) with \(B_r\). At \(\theta=1\), the randomized variance is zero.

A random shift of the interval or random independent shifts of the zero sets can reproduce the heuristic variance, but then one still needs a derandomization theorem proving that the actual Apéry configuration at shift zero is typical. That derandomization is the missing arithmetic.

## 8. The moment method does not bypass the obstruction

For the unordered factorial moments

\[
F_j=\sum_{m<M}\binom{K(m)}j,
\]

one has the exact Stirling expansion

\[
\sum_{m<M}K(m)^k
=\sum_{j=1}^k j!\,S(k,j)F_j,
\]

where \(S(k,j)\) is a Stirling number of the second kind. In particular,

\[
\sum_mK(m)^3=6F_3+6F_2+F_1.
\]

This identity is correct, but it does not estimate \(F_3\).

For a triple \(p,q,s\), CRT gives a unique residue modulo \(pqs\) for each root triple. Since

\[
\frac{M}{pqs}\asymp\frac1X,
\]

one must prove that only an \(O(1/X)\) fraction of all root triples land below \(M\), on aggregate. CRT itself supplies no information about the position of the canonical representative in \([0,pqs)\).

The tuplewise \(O(1)\) error therefore sums to

\[
O(N_+^3),
\]

which is asymptotically larger than the expected \(X^2/(\log X)^3\) scale by a factor \(X\). The same calculation gives loss \(X^{k-2}\) at the \(k\)-prime level.

Accordingly, a “three-prime CRT bound” of the needed strength is exactly

\[
F_3=M e_3(\rho)+O(\sqrt{M e_3(\rho)}),
\]

or at least \(F_3\ll M e_3(\rho)\). It is the target, not an input supplied by CRT.

This matches the current repository. `thm:hm3-palm-characterization` identifies the third factorial moment with the aggregate conditional overload of third primes on actual two-prime CRT loci, and `prop:hm3-anchored-star` proves that all presently available primewise and pairwise coarse inputs do not imply it.

## 9. Weil–Deligne over the composite modulus gives no new cross-prime cancellation

There are three separate obstructions.

### 9.1 There is no single finite field of size \(P_S\)

For squarefree composite \(P_S\),

\[
\mathbb Z/P_S\mathbb Z\cong\prod_{p\in S}\mathbb F_p.
\]

Any complete sum over the simultaneous zero set factors into the local sums:

\[
\sum_{a\in A_S}e_{P_S}(ha)
=\prod_{p\in S}S_p(h\nu_{S,p}).
\]

There is no new geometrically irreducible object coupling the different characteristics. Complete composite-modulus harmonic analysis is exactly the product already displayed.

### 9.2 The interval condition is Archimedean, not algebraic

The condition that the canonical representative satisfy \(0\le a<M\) is an order condition inside \([0,P_S)\), not a polynomial equation over \(\mathbb Z/P_S\mathbb Z\). Completion converts it into the interval kernel \(V_M(h;P_S)\), and the entire problem returns as cancellation among the product Fourier coefficients.

### 9.3 For small zero sets, a Weil bound is worse than the trivial bound

If one writes a local polynomial having \(Z_p\) as its roots, then

\[
|S_p(t)|\le z_p
\]

trivially. When \(z_p=O(1)\), this is much stronger than a generic \(O(\sqrt p)\) Weil bound. The difficulty is not estimating a single complete local sum; it is the coherent phase relation across many varying primes and the short initial interval.

Representing the function \(m\mapsto b_m\pmod p\) by an interpolating polynomial does not help: its degree can grow with \(p\), and the zero-indicator is not presently supplied by a bounded-conductor sheaf uniform in \(p\). Even a uniform local Deligne theorem would still leave the cross-prime interval correlation untouched.

Thus Weil–Deligne does not turn this into a standard complete character-sum problem.

## 10. The exact viable route: signed pair anchoring

The direct \(r\)-tuple discrepancy sum uses the wrong objects. The natural critical modulus is a **pair**, because \(pq\asymp M\). One should first anchor on actual two-prime landings and then study extensions.

For \(p<q\), define

\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|,
\]

and for \(r\ge2\),

\[
T_{p,q}^{(r-2)}
=\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\binom{K(m)-2}{r-2}.
\]

Every contributing \(r\)-set has \(\binom r2\) anchor pairs, hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p<q}T_{p,q}^{(r-2)}
=\binom r2F_r.}
\]

Set

\[
b_{p,q}=e_{r-2}((\rho_\ell)_{\ell\notin\{p,q\}}).
\]

The elementary-symmetric identity

\[
\sum_{p<q}\rho_p\rho_q b_{p,q}
=\binom r2e_r(\rho)
\]

gives the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\binom r2(F_r-B_r)
={}&\sum_{p<q}
\left(T_{p,q}^{(r-2)}-J_{p,q}b_{p,q}\right)\\
&+\sum_{p<q}
\left(J_{p,q}-M\rho_p\rho_q\right)b_{p,q}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{10.1}
\]

This separates two genuinely different tasks.

### Residual A: weighted signed pair landing

\[
(\mathrm{P2}^{\pm}_r):\qquad
\left|
\sum_{p<q}
(J_{p,q}-M\rho_p\rho_q)b_{p,q}
\right|
\ll_r\sqrt{B_r}.
\]

This is the signed two-prime CRT discrepancy. A large-sieve-style tool could plausibly enter here, but the exact Fourier form is a bilinear reciprocal-modulus expression

\[
\frac1{pq}\sum_{h\ne0}
V_M(h;pq)S_p(h\bar q)S_q(h\bar p),
\]

not a direct instance of the Gallagher or Montgomery–Vaughan inequalities. It would require a specialized bilinear estimate for reciprocal/Kloosterman-type phases with the actual Apéry root coefficients.

### Residual B: signed pair-Palm extension

\[
(\mathrm{PP}^{\pm}_r):\qquad
\left|
\sum_{p<q}
\left(T_{p,q}^{(r-2)}-J_{p,q}b_{p,q}\right)
\right|
\ll_r\sqrt{B_r}.
\]

This asks whether the remaining primes extend the **actual landed pair loci** at the independent Bernoulli rate. It is precisely the signed finite-density version of the pair-Palm hierarchy already isolated in `pairpalm_result.tex`.

By (10.1), \((\mathrm{P2}^{\pm}_r)\) and \((\mathrm{PP}^{\pm}_r)\) imply

\[
F_r=B_r+O_r(\sqrt{B_r}).
\]

This route uses sparsity correctly: it sums over actual pair anchors, whose total mass is at the proved second-moment scale, rather than paying one absolute discrepancy for every active \(r\)-tuple.

For \(r=3\), the second residual becomes

\[
\sum_{p<q}
\left(
\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}(K(m)-2)
-J_{p,q}\sum_{\ell\ne p,q}\rho_\ell
\right),
\]

which is exactly the signed conditional-third-prime statistic suggested by the numerical data. The current `hm3_exploration.md` diagnostics—small aggregate signed pair error and conditional extension rate close to \(\lambda_X\)—support these two residuals, but do not prove either uniformly in \(X\).

## 11. A second exact formulation: the Apéry-specific signed Fourier theorem needed

Equation (4.1) shows another precise sufficient theorem. Define

\[
\mathfrak C_r(X)
=\sum_{2\le|T|\le r}
 e_{r-|T|}(\rho_{\notin T})
 \sum_{m<M}\prod_{p\in T}(f_p(m)-\rho_p).
\]

Then

\[
F_r-B_r=E_{r,1}+\mathfrak C_r(X),
\qquad
|E_{r,1}|\le 2rB_r/X.
\]

Therefore the exact new theorem would be

\[
\boxed{
|\mathfrak C_r(X)|
\ll_r\sqrt{B_r}.}
\tag{AP-SC\(_r\)}
\]

In Fourier form, this is a coefficient-specific off-diagonal cancellation theorem for

\[
\sum_{T,\mathbf t}
 e_{r-|T|}(\rho_{\notin T})
 \left(\prod_{p\in T}\widehat f_p(t_p)\right)
 V_M\left(\sum_{p\in T}\frac{t_p}{p}\right).
\]

Primewise Parseval proves that the coefficient energy is of size \(e_r(\rho)\), but classical large-sieve inequalities do not control this particular linear form at square-root scale. A proof of `(AP-SC_r)` must exploit an Apéry-specific incompatibility with the anchored-star phase alignment.

The pair-anchored decomposition (10.1) is preferable for the project because it localizes that incompatibility into the already named pair-Palm residual and the two-prime landing residual.

## 12. Direct answers to the five questions

### 1. Can one bound \(\mathbb E[D_S^2]\) for a random tuple by a large sieve?

Possibly for the actual Apéry data, but not from the current generic inputs. In the ideal model, \(\sum_S D_S^2\asymp B_r\) is the correct scale. However:

1. this does not imply \(|\sum_S\delta_S|\ll\sqrt{B_r}\), and
2. `prop:hm3-anchored-star` gives a family satisfying all current coarse conditions for which \(\sum_S D_S^2\gg X^r/(\log X)^r\), larger than \(B_r\) by \(X^{r-2}\).

Thus a mean-square theorem already requires new Apéry-specific cross-prime input.

### 2. Does Gallagher or Montgomery–Vaughan apply to the dual form?

Only formally, with a useless bound. The correct local frequency contains the CRT inverse \((P_S/p)^{-1}\), the Erdős–Turán absolute value removes the desired cancellation, the standard dual inequality loses a factor \(M\), and the spacing term is at least of order \(X^{2r}/(\log X)^r\) on the full support-frequency family. No off-the-shelf Gallagher/Montgomery–Vaughan inequality yields the conjectured variance.

### 3. Does Efron–Stein apply to deterministic \(F_r\)?

No. There are no independent random coordinates. Random thinning or random shifts produce a different ensemble and control fluctuations around a mean containing the unknown \(F_r\); they do not bound the deterministic bias \(F_r-B_r\).

### 4. Can the third ordinary moment or a three-prime CRT bound close the problem?

No shortcut occurs. The Stirling identity reduces the ordinary third moment to \(F_3,F_2,F_1\), and estimating \(F_3\) at the required scale is exactly the three-prime landing problem. CRT loses a factor \(X\) already at \(r=3\). The current repository's HM3 characterization identifies this as the first anchored-star obstruction.

### 5. Can Weil–Deligne on a composite-modulus polynomial improve the discrepancy?

Not in the proposed form. The complete sum factors over the prime fields, the initial interval is not algebraic, and for small \(Z_p\) the trivial local bound \(|S_p(t)|\le z_p\) is stronger than a square-root Weil bound. A successful algebraic theorem would have to be a new cross-characteristic correlation theorem for the actual Apéry zero indicators, not a standard complete character-sum estimate modulo \(P_S\).

## 13. Final route assessment

The proposed statement

\[
\sum_{|S|=r}D_S=O(\sqrt{M e_r(\rho)})
\]

is false and should be removed from the proof plan. In the intended sparse regime, the independent model predicts

\[
\sum_SD_S\sim2M e_r(\rho).
\]

The numerical evidence is instead consistent with

\[
\sum_S\delta_S
=O(\sqrt{M e_r(\rho)}).
\]

The correct next theorem is not a generic large-sieve variance bound. It is either:

1. the pair of signed residuals \((\mathrm{P2}^{\pm}_r)\) and \((\mathrm{PP}^{\pm}_r)\) in (10.1), preferably attacked at the pair-anchor level; or
2. the equivalent Apéry-specific signed centered-correlation estimate `(AP-SC_r)`.

Of these, the pair-anchored route is the source-faithful one already aligned with `thm:hm3-palm-characterization` and `thm:pairpalm-hierarchy`. A classical large sieve might contribute to the weighted two-prime residual after it is rewritten as a reciprocal-phase bilinear form, but it cannot by itself establish the higher pair-Palm extension cancellation. The latter is the genuine remaining arithmetic theorem.

A useful numerical audit is now to record, for each \(r\), all three quantities

\[
L_{1,r}=\sum_SD_S,
\qquad
L_{2,r}^2=\sum_S\delta_S^2,
\qquad
E_r=\sum_S\delta_S.
\]

The independent prediction is

\[
L_{1,r}\asymp2B_r,
\qquad
L_{2,r}^2\asymp B_r,
\qquad
E_r/\sqrt{B_r}=O(1).
\]

That experiment would directly confirm the distinction on which the proof route depends.
