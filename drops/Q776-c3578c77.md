# Q776: audit of affine-lift elimination

## Summary

The two affine lifts do give a useful paired linear relation, but they do **not** eliminate the term \(b_j/p\) into a characteristic-zero carrier attached to the original outer index \(n\). The obstruction is local and algebraic: the lifts determine only affine residue classes modulo p, and the unknown quotient \(b_j/p\) can be chosen to force arbitrary cancellation patterns while preserving all stated inputs.

Therefore the construction requested in A fails from the banked hypotheses alone.

## A. Attempted construction

For a target prime \(p\), write \(n=qp+j\). The two lifts give

\[
X_q:=D_{q,qp+j}/p \equiv c+2qd_j \pmod p,
\]

and on the reflected branch

\[
Y_q:=D_{q,(q+1)p-1-j}/p \equiv c-2(q+1)d_j \pmod p,
\]

where

\[
c=b_j/p \pmod p.
\]

Adding eliminates c:

\[
X_q+Y_q\equiv -2d_j\pmod p.
\]

Subtracting gives

\[
X_q-Y_q\equiv (4q+2)d_j+2c\pmod p.
\]

The only c-free quantity is therefore a multiple of \(d_j\). It does not retain divisibility by p coming from \(p\mid b_j\), because p has disappeared from the congruence. The resulting characteristic-zero integer candidate would have to be built from the actual prefixes, but the known congruence only says that one linear combination has residue \(-2d_j\), not that p divides it.

Hence no direct carrier

\[
C_n\in\mathbb Z,\qquad p\mid C_n\quad(p\mid b_{n-p})
\]

follows.

## B. Local CRT countermodel

Fix a finite set of target primes \(P\subset (n/(q+1),n/q]\). For each p choose a residue variable

\[
c_p=b_j/p\pmod p
\]

and choose \(d_p\) with the required reflection constraint

\[
d_{p-1-j}=-d_j.
\]

The affine lifts require only

\[
X_p=c_p+2qd_p,
\]

\[
Y_p=c_p-2(q+1)d_p.
\]

For every p these equations can be satisfied independently. By CRT, the corresponding characteristic-zero integers can be chosen simultaneously modulo

\[
M=\prod_{p\in P}p.
\]

The dyadic condition does not restrict this construction: exact
\(v_2(d_j)=1-\lfloor\log_2 j\rfloor\) only requires a nonzero rational representative, and multiplying by odd local CRT adjustments preserves the required 2-adic valuation after clearing denominators.

The sparse exceptional theorem

\[
\#\{n\le N:\log G_n>\epsilon n\}=O_\epsilon(\log^2N)
\]

also does not prevent the model. It controls whole gcd spikes, not the distribution of the individual affine parameters. One can place the exceptional behavior on a polylogarithmic set of n and leave all other n unchanged.

Thus the listed facts are compatible with arbitrary cancellation patterns such as

\[
c_p+2qd_p=0\pmod p
\]

for many target primes.

## C. Weakest additional statement needed

A sufficient extra statement is not the full fixed-q slice bound. The minimal useful statement is a uniform control on affine cancellation classes:

> For every fixed q and every \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_p\), the number of primes \(p\) in the q-slice satisfying
> \[
> b_{n-p}/p+\lambda d_{n-p}\equiv0\pmod p
> \]
> is \(o(n/\log n)\).

This is strictly weaker than controlling all primes with \(p\mid b_{n-p}\), because it only controls the affine hyperplane selected by the lift.

However, it is not enough merely to prove nonvanishing of d_j. The example
\((p,j,q)=(97,25,1)\) shows that cancellation survives even when d_j is nonzero.

## D. Height audit

A hypothetical carrier obtained by multiplying prefixes has height at least

\[
\log |D_{q,n}|=O(n),
\]

because the binomial terms have exponential size.

Eliminating c by addition produces expressions involving d_j. The known estimate for harmonic numbers gives at best

\[
\log |d_j|=O(j\log j),
\]

which is much larger than the required o(n) when summed over macroscopic ranges.

The polylog exceptional-set theorem gives no height saving; it only says large gcd events are rare.

Therefore the affine lifts are a genuine structural refinement, but they do not by themselves create a subexponential characteristic-zero carrier. The missing ingredient is an affine cancellation-count theorem, not another elimination identity.
