# Companion-block pointwise bound audit

The available structural input does not currently imply \(B_{comp}(n)=o(n/\log n)\) pointwise. The standard divisor-count argument is essentially sharp without a new theorem on the numerators of the companion sequence.

For \(q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\), the relevant primes lie in
\[
 n/(q+1)<p\le n/q,
\]
with \(q<\sqrt n\). Every such prime divides \(\operatorname{num}(a_q)\). Hence
\[
B_{comp}(n)\le \sum_{q<\sqrt n}\omega(\operatorname{num}(a_q)).
\]
The generic height bound gives
\[
\log |\operatorname{num}(a_q)|=O(q),
\]
so only
\[
\omega(\operatorname{num}(a_q))\ll q/\log q
\]
is available from size alone. Summing gives
\[
B_{comp}(n)\ll \sum_{q\le\sqrt n}q/\log q
=O(n/\log n),
\]
not \(o(n/\log n)\).

The Casoratian
\[
a_qb_{q-1}-a_{q-1}b_q=6/q^3
\]
forces coprimality information between adjacent companion and Apéry values, but it does not bound the number of large prime divisors of a single numerator \(\operatorname{num}(a_q)\). A prime dividing \(a_q\) need not divide any controlled Apéry zero. Thus the recurrence relation alone does not sharpen the above estimate.

The stronger claim \(B_{comp}(n)=O(\sqrt n)\) would require an additional statement such as
\[
\omega(\operatorname{num}(a_q))=O(1)
\]
or an averaged large-prime divisor theorem for the companion sequence. Neither follows from the stated inputs.

For small \(n\), the observed bound \(B_{comp}(n)\le3\) is explained by interval sparsity: each fixed \(q\) has a short prime interval \((n/(q+1),n/q]\), and simultaneously requiring a large shell prime divisor of \(\operatorname{num}(a_q)\) is restrictive. The phenomenon is empirical, not a consequence of the current recurrence identities.

Therefore the correct theorem-level statement is:

**Unconditional baseline.** For all \(n\),
\[
B_{comp}(n)=O(n/\log n).
\]

Improving this to \(o(n/\log n)\) requires a genuinely new arithmetic input controlling the large prime factors of \(\operatorname{num}(a_q)\); the Apéry recurrence and Casoratian alone are insufficient.
