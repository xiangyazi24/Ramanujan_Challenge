# Moving-modulus short-gap chains: a continuant dichotomy and a fixed-jet CRT no-go

## Verdict

**PARTIAL POSITIVE RESULT + PRECISE NO-GO.**

Let

\[
\mathcal H(n)=\{p\in(n/2,n]:p\mid b_n\},
\qquad j_p=n-p.
\]

Take as established the corrected top-shell facts

\[
p\mid b_n\iff p\mid b_{n-p}
\quad(p>n/2),
\]

and

\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\]

If the ordered hits have a short consecutive gap

\[
j'=j+h,
\qquad p=n-j,
\qquad \ell=n-j'=p-h,
\]

then the Apéry recurrence gives an exact integer identity

\[
N_h(j)b_{j+1}
 =p\,C_h(j)\frac{b_j}{p}
  +\ell\,D_h(j)\frac{b_{j+h}}\ell.
\tag{1}
\]

There is a sharp dichotomy.

1. If either endpoint prime divides the continuant \(N_h(j)\), then that prime sees an **extra same-characteristic zero** at both endpoints. All such short gaps with \(h\le H\) are supported on one explicit integer
   \[
   \mathcal W_H(n)=\prod_{2\le h\le H}N_h(n)N_h(n-h),
   \]
   and their number is \(O(H^2)\). For \(H=O_\varepsilon(\log n)\), this is only
   \[
   O_\varepsilon((\log n)^2)=o(n/\log n).
   \]

2. On every remaining, **continuant-regular** gap, \(N_h(j)\) is a unit modulo both \(p\) and \(p-h\). Then (1) does not relate the local quotients
   \[
   \frac{b_j}{p}\pmod p,
   \qquad
   \frac{b_{j+h}}{p-h}\pmod{p-h}.
   \]
   Its two reductions involve instead the *cross-residues* of those quotients in the other characteristic. Those cross-residues are freely CRT-compatible.

The same obstruction survives the Gessel mod-\(p^2\) first jet and reflection. If

\[
s_i=\frac{b_n}{p_i}\pmod{p_i},
\qquad P=\prod_i p_i,
\]

then \(b_n=PM\) and

\[
s_i\equiv \frac{P}{p_i}M\pmod{p_i}.
\]

For **every** tuple \((s_i)_i\), CRT gives a unique \(M\pmod P\). Thus the requirement that all local jets come from the **same integer \(b_n\)** and the **same fixed index \(n\)** imposes no compatibility condition. For one pair \(\ell=p-h\), this is explicitly

\[
M\equiv-h^{-1}s_p\pmod p,
\qquad
M\equiv h^{-1}s_\ell\pmod\ell.
\tag{2}
\]

Smallness of \(h\) is invisible here: modulo both primes, \(h\) is simply a unit.

Consequently, the recurrence, reflection, gap continuants, and any fixed collection of prime-wise Gessel jets do **not** yield the desired bound

\[
|\mathcal H(n)|=o(n/\log n).
\]

They rigorously dispose of only the continuant-singular subfamily. A successful argument must add a genuinely cross-characteristic, non-prime-separable input: for example, one common low-height integer carrier for many regular gaps, or an exact global identity using the fixed Apéry initial state that cannot be reconstructed coordinatewise by CRT.

This is a theorem-level obstruction, not a claim that the desired hit bound is false.

# 1. Dense hits force many short consecutive gaps

Order the hit digits as

\[
0\le j_1<j_2<\cdots<j_t<n/2,
\qquad p_i=n-j_i.
\]

Then

\[
p_1>p_2>\cdots>p_t>n/2,
\qquad h_i=j_{i+1}-j_i=p_i-p_{i+1}.
\]

For any \(H\ge1\),

\[
\#\{i:h_i>H\}\,H
 <\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}h_i
 =j_t-j_1
 <\frac n2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\#\{i:h_i\le H\}
 \ge t-1-\frac{n}{2H}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

If

\[
t\ge \varepsilon\frac n{\log n}
\]

and

\[
H=\left\lceil\frac{2\log n}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil,
\]

then

\[
\frac n{2H}\le \frac{\varepsilon n}{4\log n}\le\frac t4,
\]

so at least

\[
\frac{3t}{4}-1
\tag{4}
\]

consecutive gaps have length \(O_\varepsilon(\log n)\).

The issue is therefore whether a short gap in the **indices** forces a relation across the two changing characteristics. The next sections give the exact answer.

# 2. Exact transfer identity across one moving-modulus gap

Put

\[
P(x)=34x^3+51x^2+27x+5
     =(2x+1)(17x^2+17x+5).
\]

The Apéry recurrence is

\[
(m+1)^3b_{m+1}=P(m)b_m-m^3b_{m-1}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the integer gap continuants

\[
N_0(x)=0,
\qquad N_1(x)=1,
\]

\[
N_{r+1}(x)
 =P(x+r)N_r(x)-(x+r)^6N_{r-1}(x).
\tag{6}
\]

Also put

\[
D_h(x)=\prod_{r=2}^{h}(x+r)^3,
\qquad D_1(x)=1,
\]

and

\[
C_h(x)=(x+1)^3N_{h-1}(x+1).
\]

## Lemma 2.1: exact endpoint transfer

For every \(x\ge0\) and \(h\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
D_h(x)b_{x+h}
 =N_h(x)b_{x+1}-C_h(x)b_x.
}
\tag{7}
\]

### Proof

For \(h=1\), this is

\[
b_{x+1}=b_{x+1}.
\]

Assume it for \(h\) and \(h-1\). Apply (5) at \(m=x+h\) and multiply by \(D_h(x)\):

\[
D_{h+1}(x)b_{x+h+1}
 =P(x+h)D_h(x)b_{x+h}
  -(x+h)^6D_{h-1}(x)b_{x+h-1}.
\]

Substitute the two induction hypotheses. The coefficient of \(b_{x+1}\) is

\[
P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x)=N_{h+1}(x),
\]

and the coefficient of \(b_x\) is

\[
-(x+1)^3
\left(
P(x+h)N_{h-1}(x+1)
 -(x+h)^6N_{h-2}(x+1)
\right)
=-C_{h+1}(x).
\]

This proves (7). ∎

Now take consecutive aligned hits

\[
p=n-j,
\qquad \ell=p-h=n-j-h,
\]

with

\[
p\mid b_j,
\qquad \ell\mid b_{j+h}.
\]

Because both primes are in \((n/2,n]\),

\[
0\le j<j+h<\ell<p.
\tag{8}
\]

Thus every factor occurring in \(D_h(j)\) is a unit modulo both primes.

Set the exact integer quotients

\[
A=\frac{b_j}{p},
\qquad A'=\frac{b_{j+h}}{\ell}.
\]

Equation (7) becomes

\[
\boxed{
N_h(j)b_{j+1}
 =pC_h(j)A+\ell D_h(j)A'.
}
\tag{9}
\]

This is the exact relation requested in the question. Its content is subtler than a relation between \(A\bmod p\) and \(A'\bmod\ell\).

# 3. The continuant-singular case gives a real common carrier

Call the gap \((j,p)\to(j+h,\ell)\) **singular** if

\[
p\ell\not\perp N_h(j),
\]

and **regular** otherwise.

## Lemma 3.1: a singular gap forces an extra same-characteristic return

Under (8):

1. if \(p\mid N_h(j)\), then
   \[
   p\mid b_j
   \quad\text{and}\quad
   p\mid b_{j+h};
   \]
2. if \(\ell\mid N_h(j)\), then
   \[
   \ell\mid b_j
   \quad\text{and}\quad
   \ell\mid b_{j+h}.
   \]

### Proof

If \(p\mid N_h(j)\), reduce (7) modulo \(p\). Since \(p\mid b_j\) and \(D_h(j)\) is a unit,

\[
D_h(j)b_{j+h}\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

so \(p\mid b_{j+h}\).

If \(\ell\mid N_h(j)\), reduce (7) modulo \(\ell\). Since \(\ell\mid b_{j+h}\),

\[
C_h(j)b_j\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

The two coefficients \(-C_h(j)/D_h(j)\) and \(N_h(j)/D_h(j)\) are the second row of the invertible transfer matrix carrying \((b_j,b_{j+1})\) to \((b_{j+h-1},b_{j+h})\). Every step is invertible modulo \(\ell\) because all indices are below \(\ell\). Hence these two coefficients cannot both vanish. Since \(N_h(j)\equiv0\pmod\ell\), one has

\[
C_h(j)\not\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

Therefore \(\ell\mid b_j\). ∎

This is the only immediate way the short moving-modulus gap turns into a same-characteristic gap continuant.

## Lemma 3.2: common-\(n\) horizontalization

For a singular gap,

\[
p\mid N_h(j)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid N_h(n),
\tag{10}
\]

because \(j=n-p\), while

\[
\ell\mid N_h(j)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell\mid N_h(n-h),
\tag{11}
\]

because \(j=n-\ell-h\).

This is the useful place where the common relation \(p+j=n\) genuinely creates one integer carrier. It is not being discarded or replaced by independent local data.

# 4. Singular short gaps are only \(O(H^2)\)

Define

\[
\mathcal W_H(n)
 =\prod_{h=2}^{H}N_h(n)N_h(n-h).
\tag{12}
\]

Every singular consecutive gap of size at most \(H\) has at least one endpoint prime dividing \(\mathcal W_H(n)\), by (10)--(11).

We need an explicit height bound.

## Lemma 4.1: positivity and size of the continuants

For \(x\ge0\) and \(h\ge1\),

\[
N_h(x)>0.
\tag{13}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
N_h(x)
 \le 35^{h-1}(x+h+1)^{3(h-1)}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

### Proof of positivity

The exact identity

\[
P(t)=t^3+(t+1)^3+4(2t+1)^3
\tag{15}
\]

implies

\[
P(t)>t^3+(t+1)^3.
\]

For \(h\ge2\), set

\[
R_h(x)=\frac{N_h(x)}{N_{h-1}(x)}.
\]

The base value satisfies

\[
R_2(x)=P(x+1)>(x+2)^3.
\]

If \(R_h(x)>(x+h)^3\), then from (6)

\[
R_{h+1}(x)
 =P(x+h)-\frac{(x+h)^6}{R_h(x)}
 >P(x+h)-(x+h)^3
 >(x+h+1)^3.
\]

Thus all \(R_h\), and hence all \(N_h\), are positive.

### Proof of the upper bound

Also

\[
P(t)\le34(t+1)^3
\qquad(t\ge0).
\tag{16}
\]

Fix a target \(h\), put \(T=x+h+1\), and induct on \(r\le h\). The cases \(r=1,2\) are immediate. If

\[
N_r(x)\le35^{r-1}T^{3(r-1)},
\qquad
N_{r-1}(x)\le35^{r-2}T^{3(r-2)},
\]

then, using positivity and (6),

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{r+1}(x)
&\le34T^3\,35^{r-1}T^{3(r-1)}
   +T^6\,35^{r-2}T^{3(r-2)}\\
&=35^{r-2}(34\cdot35+1)T^{3r}\\
&\le35^rT^{3r},
\end{aligned}
\]

because

\[
34\cdot35+1=1191<1225=35^2.
\]

This proves (14). ∎

Applying (14) at \(x=n\) and \(x=n-h\) gives

\[
\log\mathcal W_H(n)
\le
H(H-1)
\left(
\log35+3\log(n+H+1)
\right).
\tag{17}
\]

Let \(\omega_{>n/2}(M)\) denote the number of distinct prime divisors of \(M\) exceeding \(n/2\). Then

\[
\omega_{>n/2}(M)
\le\frac{\log M}{\log(n/2)}.
\tag{18}
\]

One endpoint prime can certify at most the gap immediately before it and the gap immediately after it. Therefore:

## Theorem 4.2: singular-gap bound

For \(n>4\) and \(2\le H<n/2\), the number \(S_H(n)\) of singular consecutive hit gaps with \(h_i\le H\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{
S_H(n)
\le
\frac{
2H(H-1)
\left(
\log35+3\log(n+H+1)
\right)
}{
\log(n/2)
}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

In particular,

\[
S_H(n)=O(H^2).
\tag{20}
\]

For

\[
H=\left\lceil\frac{2\log n}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil,
\]

this becomes

\[
S_H(n)=O_\varepsilon((\log n)^2).
\tag{21}
\]

Combining (4) and (21), the contradiction hypothesis forces at least

\[
\boxed{
\frac{3t}{4}-1-O_\varepsilon((\log n)^2)
}
\tag{22}
\]

short **regular** gaps. If \(t\ge\varepsilon n/\log n\), this is still

\[
\gg_\varepsilon n/\log n.
\]

Thus the gap-continuant carrier mechanism can explain only a negligible subfamily of the alleged dense chain. The bulk is necessarily the regular case.

# 5. On a regular gap, the exact quotient relation has two free cross-residues

Return to (9):

\[
N_h(j)b_{j+1}=pC_h(j)A+\ell D_h(j)A',
\]

where

\[
A=\frac{b_j}{p},
\qquad A'=\frac{b_{j+h}}\ell,
\qquad \ell=p-h.
\]

Suppose

\[
\gcd(N_h(j),p\ell)=1.
\]

Reducing modulo \(p\) gives

\[
\boxed{
N_h(j)b_{j+1}
\equiv-hD_h(j)A'\pmod p,
}
\tag{23}
\]

while reducing modulo \(\ell\) gives

\[
\boxed{
N_h(j)b_{j+1}
\equiv hC_h(j)A\pmod\ell.
}
\tag{24}
\]

The local zero tangents are

\[
\alpha=A\pmod p,
\qquad
\alpha'=A'\pmod\ell.
\]

But (23) uses

\[
A'\pmod p,
\]

not \(A'\pmod\ell\), and (24) uses

\[
A\pmod\ell,
\]

not \(A\pmod p\).

These are two independent **cross-residues**. The exact recurrence therefore does not eliminate to a relation between \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha'\).

## Proposition 5.1: pairwise first-jet compatibility

Fix a regular pair \((p,\ell=p-h)\). For arbitrary prescribed values

\[
\alpha\in\mathbf F_p,
\qquad
\alpha'\in\mathbf F_\ell,
\]

there exist integers \(A,A',X\) such that

\[
A\equiv\alpha\pmod p,
\qquad
A'\equiv\alpha'\pmod\ell,
\]

and

\[
N_h(j)X
\equiv pC_h(j)A+\ell D_h(j)A'
\pmod{p^2\ell^2}.
\tag{25}
\]

### Proof

Choose the own-characteristic residues

\[
A\pmod p,
\qquad A'\pmod\ell
\]

as prescribed. Choose their cross-components

\[
A\pmod{\ell^2},
\qquad A'\pmod{p^2}
\]

arbitrarily, and combine each pair by CRT. Since \(N_h(j)\) is a unit modulo both \(p^2\) and \(\ell^2\), define

\[
X\equiv
N_h(j)^{-1}
\left(
 pC_h(j)A+\ell D_h(j)A'
\right)
\pmod{p^2}
\]

and independently modulo \(\ell^2\). A final CRT lift gives one integer \(X\) satisfying (25). ∎

The same argument works to every fixed \(p\)-adic depth. Increasing the depth only increases the two coprime CRT moduli; it does not create a cross-characteristic equation.

This does **not** assert that the actual Apéry quotients are arbitrary. It proves the narrower and relevant no-go statement:

> The short-gap recurrence identity by itself imposes no compatibility condition on the two local quotient labels. Any relation between the actual labels must use additional global information not contained in the prime-wise recurrence jets.

# 6. The Gessel first jet remains compatible with one common \(b_n\)

For the top shell, the outer base-\(p\) quotient is exactly \(1\). The checked first-order Gessel congruence is

\[
b_{p+j}
\equiv5b_j+5p\dot b_j
\pmod{p^2},
\tag{26}
\]

where \(\dot b_j\) is the formal index derivative modulo \(p\). At a hit,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_n}{p}
\equiv
5\left(
\frac{b_j}{p}+\dot b_j
\right)
\pmod p.
}
\tag{27}
\]

For the canonical lower-half representative \(\rho\), the exact reflection lift is

\[
b_{p-1-\rho}
\equiv b_\rho-p\dot b_\rho
\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{28}
\]

Thus the reflection defect

\[
\varepsilon_\rho
 =\frac{b_{p-1-\rho}-b_\rho}{p}
\pmod p
\]

is simply

\[
\varepsilon_\rho\equiv-\dot b_\rho\pmod p.
\tag{29}
\]

Normalizing by the nonzero local slope

\[
u_\rho=(\rho+1)^3b_{\rho+1}\pmod p
\]

to form \(\tau\) and \(\eta\) changes no compatibility issue: it only multiplies local quantities by a unit in \(\mathbf F_p\).

Combining Gessel and reflection also gives the valid prime-local identity already isolated in the preceding audit,

\[
b_n+5b_{p-1-j}-10b_j\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{30}
\]

For the neighboring prime \(\ell=p-h\), one gets a second congruence

\[
b_n+5b_{\ell-1-(j+h)}-10b_{j+h}
\equiv0\pmod{\ell^2}.
\tag{31}
\]

The reflected indices differ by \(2h\), but (30) and (31) still live in coprime characteristics. Overlap of the index windows is not a compatibility condition; it is handled by coordinatewise CRT, as formalized below.

## Theorem 6.1: common-cofactor CRT theorem

Let \(p_1,\ldots,p_t\) be distinct primes, put

\[
P=\prod_{i=1}^t p_i,
\]

and prescribe arbitrary depth-\(d\) normalized common-index jets

\[
s_i\in\mathbf Z/p_i^d\mathbf Z.
\]

Then there exists an integer \(B\), divisible by every \(p_i\), such that

\[
\frac{B}{p_i}\equiv s_i\pmod{p_i^d}
\qquad(1\le i\le t).
\tag{32}
\]

Moreover, \(B\) is unique modulo \(P^{d+1}\).

### Proof

Write

\[
B=PM.
\]

Condition (32) is

\[
\frac{P}{p_i}M\equiv s_i\pmod{p_i^d}.
\]

Since \(P/p_i\) is a unit modulo \(p_i^d\), this specifies one residue

\[
M\equiv
s_i\left(\frac{P}{p_i}\right)^{-1}
\pmod{p_i^d}.
\]

The moduli \(p_i^d\) are pairwise coprime, so CRT gives a unique \(M\pmod{P^d}\), hence a unique \(B=PM\pmod{P^{d+1}}\). ∎

For one short-gap pair \(\ell=p-h\), this specializes at depth one to

\[
\frac{B}{p}=\ell M\equiv-hM\pmod p,
\]

\[
\frac{B}{\ell}=pM\equiv hM\pmod\ell.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
M\equiv-h^{-1}s_p\pmod p,
\qquad
M\equiv h^{-1}s_\ell\pmod\ell.
}
\tag{33}
\]

There is no relation between \(s_p\) and \(s_\ell\). The common integer \(B\) is exactly what CRT constructs.

Applying this with

\[
s_i=5\left(\frac{b_{j_i}}{p_i}+\dot b_{j_i}\right)
\pmod{p_i}
\]

shows that the complete tuple of Gessel first jets is compatible with one common integer \(b_n\), regardless of the short-gap chain.

This directly preserves, rather than discards, the common \(n\) relation.

# 7. Overlapping fixed-depth recurrence/reflection windows are also CRT-compatible

The following elementary statement is the precise logical no-go.

## Theorem 7.1: global-table CRT lifting

Fix the single integer \(n\), the primes \(p_i\), and the actual positions

\[
j_i=n-p_i.
\]

For each \(i\), let \(S_i\) be any finite set of **global integer indices**. It may include, for example,

\[
j_i-D,\ldots,j_i+D,
\]

\[
p_i-1-j_i-D,\ldots,p_i-1-j_i+D,
\]

and the common index \(n\). The sets \(S_i\) may overlap heavily because the gaps \(h_i\) are small.

Suppose that for each \(i\) one has a locally admissible assignment

\[
x_m^{(i)}\in\mathbf Z/p_i^d\mathbf Z
\qquad(m\in S_i)
\]

satisfying any desired collection of polynomial congruences modulo \(p_i^d\), including cleared recurrence equations, reflection equations, gap-continuant identities, and Gessel jet equations.

Then there is one integer \(X_m\) for every

\[
m\in\bigcup_iS_i
\]

such that

\[
X_m\equiv x_m^{(i)}\pmod{p_i^d}
\qquad(m\in S_i),
\tag{34}
\]

simultaneously for every \(i\). Hence all local systems hold in one common global integer table.

### Proof

For each fixed global index \(m\), collect the congruences (34) over the primes whose sets contain \(m\). Their moduli are pairwise coprime, so CRT gives one integer \(X_m\). Repeat independently for each global index. Every polynomial congruence in the \(i\)-th local system remains true after replacing its local variables by the corresponding \(X_m\), because those integers have the same residues modulo \(p_i^d\). ∎

This theorem does **not** introduce independent copies \(n_i\). There is one fixed \(n\), one actual integer index \(m\) for every overlapping window entry, and one common variable at index \(n\). The only independence is the mathematically unavoidable independence of coprime residue characteristics.

Therefore no contradiction can arise merely from placing finitely many prime-wise jets beside each other. A contradiction must use an equation that is not a conjunction of separate congruences—typically an exact low-height integer identity shared by many characteristics.

The depth \(D\) need not be constant for this statement. It may be \(O(\log n)\), as in the short-gap chain. The obstruction persists for every finite collection of prime-separable equations.

# 8. Exact recurrence-only countermodel with one global initial state

The preceding theorem is a congruence-model no-go. There is also a stronger countermodel for the recurrence and continuants themselves.

Use the cleared recurrence

\[
Y_{m+1}=P(m)Y_m-m^6Y_{m-1}.
\tag{35}
\]

For every integer initial pair \((Y_0,Y_1)\), this defines one exact integer sequence.

## Proposition 8.1: arbitrary aligned zero positions are compatible with one exact cleared-recurrence sequence

Fix distinct primes \(p_i\) and positions

\[
0\le j_i<p_i.
\]

Then there is an integer initial pair \((Y_0,Y_1)\), not both divisible by any \(p_i\), such that the exact sequence (35) satisfies

\[
p_i\mid Y_{j_i}
\qquad(1\le i\le t).
\tag{36}
\]

The positions may in particular be the common-\(n\) positions

\[
j_i=n-p_i.
\]

### Proof

Modulo \(p_i\), the transfer map

\[
(Y_0,Y_1)\longmapsto(Y_{j_i},Y_{j_i+1})
\]

is invertible. Indeed, every one-step determinant is \(m^6\), and all steps used have \(m<j_i<p_i\). Therefore the evaluation functional

\[
L_{j_i}:(Y_0,Y_1)\longmapsto Y_{j_i}
\]

is nonzero over \(\mathbf F_{p_i}\). Its kernel is a one-dimensional subspace. Choose a nonzero vector

\[
v_i\in\ker L_{j_i}.
\]

CRT on the two coordinates produces one integer pair \((Y_0,Y_1)\) reducing to \(v_i\) modulo each \(p_i\). The exact integer sequence generated from this pair then satisfies (36). ∎

The first zero quotient can also be prescribed.

## Proposition 8.2: arbitrary first tangent for the recurrence countermodel

After choosing a nonzero kernel vector modulo \(p_i\), the lift modulo \(p_i^2\) can be chosen so that

\[
\frac{Y_{j_i}}{p_i}\pmod{p_i}
\]

is any prescribed element of \(\mathbf F_{p_i}\). These choices can be made simultaneously for all \(i\) by CRT on the initial pair modulo

\[
\prod_i p_i^2.
\]

### Proof

Choose one lift \(\widetilde v_i\) of the kernel vector. Since

\[
L_{j_i}(\widetilde v_i)\equiv0\pmod{p_i},
\]

write

\[
L_{j_i}(\widetilde v_i)=p_ic_i\pmod{p_i^2}.
\]

Replacing the lift by

\[
\widetilde v_i+p_iw
\]

changes the normalized quotient by

\[
L_{j_i}(w)\pmod{p_i}.
\]

Because \(L_{j_i}\) is nonzero, this map is surjective. Thus the quotient can be prescribed arbitrarily. The simultaneous global lift is again coordinatewise CRT. ∎

At a zero, \(Y_{j_i+1}\not\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\), or else invertibility of the transfer would force the initial vector to be zero. Hence one may normalize by the local slope and prescribe an arbitrary recurrence analogue of \(\tau_i\) as well.

This is one exact global recurrence sequence, not separate local sequences. It proves that recurrence order, Wronskian nondegeneracy, gap continuants, and first zero tangents alone cannot bound the aligned load.

The countermodel deliberately does **not** retain the fixed Apéry initial state \((1,5)\), and it need not satisfy the Apéry-specific Gessel seam law. That is exactly the point: a successful proof must use an Apéry-specific global feature that survives neither this initial-state CRT construction nor the global-table CRT theorem.

# 9. Even the crude height of the common \(b_n\) does not break first-jet CRT

For first jets, the conditions

\[
p_i\mid B,
\qquad
\frac{B}{p_i}\equiv s_i\pmod{p_i}
\]

fix one residue class

\[
B\pmod{P^2}.
\tag{37}
\]

Could the actual size of \(b_n\) make that CRT class impossible? Not at the available scale.

By the prime number theorem,

\[
t\le\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)
 =\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac n{\log n}.
\]

Since every \(p_i\le n\),

\[
P\le n^t
 =\exp\left(\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n\right),
\]

and therefore

\[
P^2\le\exp((1+o(1))n).
\tag{38}
\]

On the other hand, the \(k=n\) term in the Apéry sum gives

\[
b_n\ge\binom{2n}{n}^2.
\]

Using

\[
\binom{2n}{n}\ge\frac{4^n}{2n+1},
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{
 b_n\ge\frac{16^n}{(2n+1)^2}
 =\exp((\log16+o(1))n).
}
\tag{39}
\]

Since

\[
\log16=2.77258\ldots>1,
\]

every first-jet CRT class modulo \(P^2\) has exponentially many positive representatives below the crude Apéry scale (39).

This does not mean the actual fixed integer \(b_n\) may be replaced by another representative. It means that **height plus the prime-wise first-jet congruences alone** cannot force a contradiction. One needs a much sharper identity identifying the actual representative or a carrier substantially smaller than \(P^2\).

# 10. Final theorem and obstruction

Collecting the preceding results gives the exact answer to the proposed route.

## Theorem 10.1: moving-modulus short-gap dichotomy

Assume

\[
t=|\mathcal H(n)|
\ge\varepsilon\frac n{\log n}.
\]

Let

\[
H=\left\lceil\frac{2\log n}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil.
\]

Then:

1. at least
   \[
   \frac{3t}{4}-1
   \]
   consecutive hit gaps satisfy \(h_i\le H\);

2. at most
   \[
   O_\varepsilon((\log n)^2)
   \]
   of these are continuant-singular;

3. every singular gap is certified by a large prime divisor of the explicit common carrier
   \[
   \mathcal W_H(n)
   =\prod_{2\le h\le H}N_h(n)N_h(n-h);
   \]

4. the remaining
   \[
   \gg_\varepsilon n/\log n
   \]
   short gaps are continuant-regular;

5. on a regular gap, the exact recurrence relation (9) contains two free cross-residues and imposes no relation between the two own-characteristic normalized quotients;

6. any fixed-depth Gessel/reflection jets at all hit primes are compatible with one common integer at the one common index \(n\), by Theorems 6.1 and 7.1.

### Consequence

The listed local tools cannot prove

\[
|\mathcal H(n)|=o(n/\log n)
\]

by forcing short-gap incompatibility. They can bound only the singular subfamily, which is already negligible under the contradiction hypothesis.

A successful continuation must produce at least one of the following genuinely new kinds of input:

- an exact integer carrier shared by a positive proportion of the **regular** gaps, with logarithmic height \(o(t\log n)\);
- a cross-characteristic identity that determines one quotient's cross-residue in another characteristic;
- an exact elimination using the fixed Apéry initial state that is not expressible as separate congruences modulo the \(p_i^d\);
- a sharp representative/height theorem for the common cofactor \(b_n/P\), far stronger than ordinary CRT reconstruction and the known exponential scale of \(b_n\).

Without such an input, the moving-modulus chain is a CRT-compatible configuration, not a contradiction.

---

## Bottom line

The common relation

\[
p_i+j_i=n
\]

does yield one useful theorem: it horizontalizes every **singular** short-gap continuant into the common carriers \(N_h(n)\) and \(N_h(n-h)\), giving only \(O_\varepsilon((\log n)^2)\) such gaps.

But the contradiction hypothesis forces \(\gg n/\log n\) **regular** short gaps. For those, the recurrence quotient identity, the mod-\(p^2\) Gessel jet, reflection defects, and all overlapping fixed-depth windows remain freely compatible by CRT while retaining the same fixed \(n\) and one common integer \(b_n\).

Therefore the rigorous verdict is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{NO FULL HIT BOUND FROM FIXED-DEPTH MOVING-MODULUS JETS;}
\quad
\text{ONLY THE }O(\log^2 n)\text{ SINGULAR SUBFAMILY IS CONTROLLED.}
}
\]

No proof of the all-index Apéry GCD conjecture is claimed here.
