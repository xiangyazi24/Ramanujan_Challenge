# P3.2 saturated terminal lemma: direct proof attack

## Executive verdict

I do **not** close the saturated theorem.

What I do get is a substantially sharper exact normalization of the remaining arithmetic problem. Under the desired counterexample hypotheses

\[
\ell\nmid 3d_mP_m,\qquad e_m\equiv H_m\equiv0\pmod\ell,
\]

all homogeneous transfer variables can be eliminated completely. The problem reduces to one triangular rational dynamical system over \(\mathbf F_\ell\):

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{n+1}
=\frac{\alpha_n}{\beta_n-\gamma_n\eta_n},
\qquad
D_n=\frac{D_{n-1}}{\eta_n}-1,
}
\tag{T}
\]

with exact initial data

\[
\boxed{
\eta_3=\frac5{33},\qquad D_2=-\frac{487}{486},
}
\tag{B}
\]

and

\[
\alpha_n=(2n+1)^3(n+2),
\]

\[
\beta_n=4(68n^2+3)(n^2-1),
\]

\[
\gamma_n=(2n-1)^3(n-2).
\]

The two terminal conditions are exactly

\[
\boxed{
\eta_m=\frac{5(m^2-1)}{m(m-2)},
\qquad
D_m=0.
}
\tag{E}
\]

for \(m\ge3\).

The first equation in `(T)` is autonomous in \(\eta\): the endpoint condition \(e_m=0\) only constrains this Riccati orbit. The second equation is an affine cocycle driven by that orbit and **does not feed back into it**. Thus the first exact scalar not controlled by the homogeneous/Casoratian dynamics is the affine endpoint

\[
D_m,
\]

equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{H_m}{\sigma_2}
=487-\sum_{n=3}^m\frac{h_n}{X_n}
=1-\sum_{n=2}^m\frac1{A_n},
}
\tag{S}
\]

where

\[
A_n:=\frac{X_n}{h_n}.
\]

So the exact missing arithmetic lemma can be stated without \(\lambda,\mu,\rho,\sigma,d,P,Y\):

> **Reduced saturated lemma.** Let \(m\ge3\) and let \(\ell>2m-1\) be prime. Start `(T)` from `(B)`. If all denominators in the orbit are nonzero and
> \[
> \eta_m=\frac{5(m^2-1)}{m(m-2)},
> \]
> then
> \[
> D_m\ne0.
> \]

This is an exact reformulation under the unit hypotheses, not a proof of nonvanishing.

I also obtain a clean first-zero law for the affine variable,

\[
\boxed{D_n=0\iff D_{n+1}=-1,}
\tag{Z}
\]

but it does not force a contradiction. The ordinary continuant Casoratian similarly collapses exactly to the already-known endpoint condition and supplies no second scalar.

The strongest honest conclusion is therefore:

1. the homogeneous/projective part is completely solved;
2. the source term reduces to one explicit affine cocycle;
3. no transfer-determinant or Casoratian argument alone can kill that cocycle;
4. a proof now has to exploit a genuinely new arithmetic identity for the specific coefficients \(\alpha_n,\beta_n,\gamma_n\), not merely their nonvanishing.

I give a sharply scoped countermodel below showing this point. It is **not** a counterexample to the theorem: it preserves all transfer recurrences and unit conditions but changes exactly one arithmetic datum.

---

## 1. Unit regime and the small edge case

Assume

\[
\ell\nmid 3d_mP_m.
\]

If \(m\ge3\), then \(2\mid X_3\), so \(\ell=2\) cannot occur because \(2\mid P_m\). Every odd prime \(q\le2m-1\) occurs among the odd factors

\[
3,5,\ldots,2m-1
\]

of \(d_m\). Hence for \(m\ge3\),

\[
\boxed{\ell>2m-1.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Consequently all of

\[
2,3,5,\ldots,m+1,
\qquad
c_n=(2n-1)^3,
\qquad
h_n=6(n^2-1)d_n
\]

are nonzero modulo \(\ell\) for \(2\le n\le m\), and by hypothesis every

\[
X_2,\ldots,X_m
\]

is nonzero.

The case \(m=2\) is direct:

\[
X_2=-1,\qquad e_2=5,\qquad H_2=487,
\]

so

\[
\gcd(e_2,H_2)=1.
\]

Thus only \(m\ge3\) needs discussion below.

---

## 2. Exact homogeneous projective consequences of \(e_m=0\)

Write

\[
a_t:=4t(68t^2+3),\qquad c_t:=(2t-1)^3.
\]

The Q1937 concomitant is

\[
\mathcal E_n
:=\mu_nc_nX_{n-1}-\lambda_nX_n.
\]

A one-line substitution gives

\[
\mathcal E_{n-1}=\mathcal E_n,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_n=e_m\qquad(2\le n\le m).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Now assume

\[
e_m=0.
\]

Because the transfer matrices have determinant

\[
c_nc_{n-1}
e0,
\]

the backward pair \((\lambda_n,\mu_n)\) is never the zero pair. Equation `(2.1)` and the unit assumptions then force, for \(3\le n\le m\),

\[
\lambda_n\ne0,\qquad \mu_n\ne0,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
q_n:=\frac{\lambda_n}{\mu_n}
=c_n\frac{X_{n-1}}{X_n}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

At the ends,

\[
q_m=5,
\qquad
\lambda_2=e_m=0,
\qquad
\mu_2\ne0.
\]

The projective recurrence is therefore

\[
\boxed{
q_{n-1}
=\frac{a_{n-1}q_n-c_n}{c_{n-1}q_n}
=\frac{a_{n-1}}{c_{n-1}}
-\frac{c_n}{c_{n-1}q_n}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

This is all the homogeneous endpoint condition says.

### Matrix/Casoratian interpretation

Set

\[
v_n:=\binom{X_n}{c_nX_{n-1}},
\qquad
M_n:=
\begin{pmatrix}
a_{n-1}&-c_{n-1}\\ c_n&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
v_n=M_nv_{n-1}.
\]

Set also

\[
w_n:=(-\lambda_n,\mu_n).
\]

The backward recurrence is exactly

\[
w_{n-1}=w_nM_n.
\]

Therefore

\[
w_nv_n=w_{n-1}v_{n-1}=e_m.
\]

Under \(e_m=0\), this pairing merely says that \(w_n\) lies in the one-dimensional annihilator of the nonzero vector \(v_n\). In dimension two that is perfectly consistent. The Casoratian has no second scalar left to spend.

This is why a generic statement of the form “the transfer determinants are units, hence the terminal gcd is supported there” is invalid.

## 3. Normalize the affine transfer

Because \(\sigma_m=1\) and

\[
\sigma_{n-1}=c_n\mu_n\sigma_n,
\]

all \(\sigma_n\) are units under the present hypotheses. Define

\[
z_n:=\frac{\rho_n}{\sigma_n}.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
z_{n-1}
&=\frac{\lambda_n}{c_n\mu_n}(z_n+h_n)\\
&=\frac{X_{n-1}}{X_n}(z_n+h_n),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the second equality uses `(2.2)`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
z_{n-1}
=\frac{X_{n-1}}{X_n}(z_n+h_n),
\qquad z_m=0.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Divide by \(X_{n-1}\):

\[
\boxed{
\frac{z_{n-1}}{X_{n-1}}
=\frac{z_n}{X_n}+\frac{h_n}{X_n}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Telescoping from \(m\) down to \(2\), and using \(X_2=-1\), gives

\[
\frac{z_2}{X_2}
=\sum_{n=3}^m\frac{h_n}{X_n},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
z_2=-\sum_{n=3}^m\frac{h_n}{X_n}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since

\[
H_m=\rho_2+487\sigma_2
=\sigma_2(z_2+487),
\]

and \(\sigma_2\) is a unit,

\[
\boxed{
H_m=0
\iff
487-\sum_{n=3}^m\frac{h_n}{X_n}=0.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is the first exact scalar not determined by the homogeneous projective orbit.

---

## 4. Remove \(d_n\) and \(h_n\) completely

The previous scalar becomes much cleaner after one more normalization.

For \(n\ge2\), define

\[
\boxed{A_n:=\frac{X_n}{h_n}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Every \(A_n\) is a unit.

Also put

\[
u_n:=\frac{X_n}{d_n}.
\]

Then

\[
u_n=6(n^2-1)A_n.
\]

The integral recurrence for \(X\) is equivalent to

\[
(2n+1)^3u_{n+1}
-4n(68n^2+3)u_n
+(2n-1)^3u_{n-1}=0.
\]

Substitute \(u_j=6(j^2-1)A_j\). For \(n\ge2\), the factor \(n\) cancels and one obtains

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_nA_{n+1}
-\beta_nA_n
+\gamma_nA_{n-1}=0,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

with

\[
\alpha_n=(2n+1)^3(n+2),
\]

\[
\beta_n=4(68n^2+3)(n^2-1),
\]

\[
\gamma_n=(2n-1)^3(n-2).
\]

At \(n=2\), the last coefficient is zero, so no \(A_1\) is needed.

The first value is

\[
\boxed{A_2=-\frac1{486}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Endpoint condition

Since

\[
\frac{h_m}{h_{m-1}}
=c_m\frac{m^2-1}{m(m-2)},
\]

the equation

\[
c_mX_{m-1}=5X_m
\]

becomes

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A_{m-1}}{A_m}
=\frac{5(m^2-1)}{m(m-2)}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Affine scalar

Equation `(3.4)` becomes

\[
H_m=0
\iff
487-\sum_{n=3}^m\frac1{A_n}=0.
\]

But \(A_2^{-1}=-486\), hence

\[
\boxed{
H_m=0
\iff
1-\sum_{n=2}^m\frac1{A_n}=0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is already a very compact statement of the exact remaining lemma.

---

## 5. The clean triangular projective system

Define

\[
\boxed{
\eta_n:=\frac{A_{n-1}}{A_n}
\qquad(n\ge3).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because all \(A_n\) are units, every \(\eta_n\) is a unit.

From `(4.2)`, for \(n\ge3\),

\[
\frac{\alpha_n}{\eta_{n+1}}
-\beta_n
+\gamma_n\eta_n=0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{n+1}
=\frac{\alpha_n}{\beta_n-\gamma_n\eta_n}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

At \(n=2\), since \(\gamma_2=0\),

\[
\eta_3=\frac{\alpha_2}{\beta_2}
=\frac{500}{3300}
=\boxed{\frac5{33}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the entire homogeneous endpoint problem is one explicit Riccati orbit starting at \(5/33\).

The endpoint equation `(4.4)` is simply

\[
\boxed{
\eta_m
=\frac{5(m^2-1)}{m(m-2)}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Now define the partial reciprocal defect

\[
R_n:=1-\sum_{j=2}^n\frac1{A_j},
\]

and scale it by

\[
\boxed{D_n:=A_nR_n.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Then

\[
R_n=R_{n-1}-\frac1{A_n},
\]

so

\[
D_n
=A_nR_{n-1}-1
=\frac{A_n}{A_{n-1}}D_{n-1}-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D_n=\frac{D_{n-1}}{\eta_n}-1.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The bottom datum is

\[
R_2=1-(-486)=487,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{D_2=-\frac{487}{486}.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Finally `(4.5)` is exactly

\[
\boxed{H_m=0\iff D_m=0.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Combining `(5.2)`, `(5.3)`, `(5.6)`, `(5.7)`, `(5.4)`, `(5.8)` gives the triangular system `(T)`--`(E)` stated at the beginning.

### Why this reduction is useful

The map is triangular:

\[
\eta\longmapsto\eta'
\]

is independent of \(D\), while

\[
D\longmapsto D'
\]

is affine once \(\eta\) is known.

So:

- \(e_m=0\) is a pure projective condition on the \(\eta\)-orbit;
- \(H_m=0\) is a second scalar condition on an affine cocycle over that orbit;
- no invertibility statement about the \(\eta\)-transfer automatically constrains the cocycle endpoint.

That is the precise location where all determinant-support-only arguments stop.

## 6. First-zero propagation in the affine coordinate

Equation `(5.6)` gives an exact zero law:

\[
D_{n+1}=\frac{D_n}{\eta_{n+1}}-1.
\]

Since \(\eta_{n+1}\) is a unit,

\[
\boxed{D_n=0\iff D_{n+1}=-1.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus zeros of the reciprocal-defect coordinate are followed immediately by \(-1\), and conversely.

This is the sharp first-zero statement I obtain in the unit regime. It does **not** close the theorem. The desired terminal condition is itself

\[
D_m=0,
\]

which would merely force

\[
D_{m+1}=-1
\]

if one extends the affine recursion one step. There is no contradiction inside \(2,\ldots,m\).

One can also eliminate \(A\) and obtain a closed polynomial recurrence for \(D\). From

\[
A_nD_{n-1}=A_{n-1}(D_n+1)
\]

and `(4.2)`,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_n(D_{n+1}+1)(D_n+1)
-\beta_nD_n(D_n+1)
+\gamma_nD_{n-1}D_n=0.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This identity is exact, but it is weaker than the triangular \((\eta,D)\) system at an internal zero, because solving `(6.2)` backward divides by \(D_n\). The pair `(5.2),(5.6)` is therefore the safer formulation.

---

## 7. No closure from the ordinary Casoratian

The homogeneous matrix pairing in §2 is the natural continuant Casoratian. Under \(e_m=0\) it gives exactly one statement:

\[
w_nv_n=0.
\]

Since \(v_n\ne0\), this determines the projective class of \(w_n\), which is exactly `(2.2)`. There is no additional independent bilinear scalar.

After that normalization, \(H_m\) appears only through the affine cocycle `(3.1)` or equivalently `(5.6)`. Any new bilinear pairing capable of proving the theorem would therefore have to mix the homogeneous orbit with the **specific source values** \(h_n\); a pairing depending only on the invertible transfer matrices cannot see the difference between \(D_m=0\) and \(D_m\ne0\).

This is a structural statement, not a proof that no more sophisticated identity exists.

## 8. Smallest endpoint example: \((\ell,m)=(29,3)\)

This example is useful because the homogeneous endpoint condition really occurs with every \(X_t\) a unit, but the affine condition misses by one residue.

Modulo \(29\):

\[
X_2=-1\equiv28,
\qquad
X_3=-2200\equiv4,
\]

so

\[
P_3\equiv25\ne0.
\]

Also

\[
e_3\equiv0.
\]

In the normalized system,

\[
\eta_3=\frac5{33}\equiv23,
\]

while the endpoint value is

\[
\frac{5(3^2-1)}{3(3-2)}
=\frac{40}{3}
\equiv23.
\]

Thus the projective endpoint condition is met exactly.

But

\[
D_2=-\frac{487}{486}\equiv24,
\]

and

\[
D_3=\frac{D_2}{\eta_3}-1\equiv24\ne0.
\]

So the affine scalar survives. This is the smallest concrete demonstration that the homogeneous orbit alone does not force the desired contradiction.

## 9. Sharply scoped countermodel to a transfer-only proof

There is no known countermodel preserving **all arithmetic coefficient definitions**; such a model would be an actual counterexample to the theorem.

There is, however, a minimal countermodel to any argument which uses only transfer recurrences and unit hypotheses and does not use the exact source/bottom arithmetic.

Keep the actual \((m,\ell)=(3,29)\) homogeneous data:

\[
a_2\equiv25,
\qquad c_2\equiv27,
\qquad c_3\equiv9,
\]

\[
X_2\equiv28,
\qquad X_3\equiv4,
\]

so all units remain units and

\[
\lambda_2=0.
\]

The actual source is

\[
h_3=162000\equiv6\pmod{29}.
\]

Replace only this source value by

\[
h_3':=487X_3\equiv5\pmod{29}.
\]

Run the **same** backward recurrences

\[
\rho_2=\lambda_3(\rho_3+h_3'\sigma_3),
\qquad
\sigma_2=c_3\mu_3\sigma_3.
\]

Then

\[
H_3'=0.
\]

Thus all transfer recurrences and every unit condition are compatible with simultaneous homogeneous and affine terminal vanishing. What fails is exactly one arithmetic specialization:

\[
h_3'=5\ne6=h_3.
\]

This is **not** a theorem counterexample. It proves only the following sharply scoped point:

> Invertibility/Casoratian/projective dynamics alone cannot prove the theorem. The proof must use the exact arithmetic source \(h_n=6(n^2-1)d_n\), equivalently the exact coefficients \(\alpha_n,\beta_n,\gamma_n\) and bottom value \(-487/486\) in `(T)`.

An equivalent bottom-data countermodel is even simpler. For the same exact projective orbit modulo \(29\), top vanishing \(D_3=0\) would require

\[
D_2=\eta_3=23,
\]

whereas the true bottom datum is

\[
D_2=24.
\]

Changing exactly that one bottom residue makes the triangular recurrence close.

## 12. Exact status: theorem / reduction / evidence

### Proved here

Under

\[
\ell\nmid3d_mP_m,
\qquad m\ge3,
\]

and \(e_m=0\):

1. \(\ell>2m-1\).
2. All \(X_n,c_n,h_n,\lambda_n,\mu_n,\sigma_n\) needed above are units for \(3\le n\le m\).
3. The homogeneous projective identity is
   \[
   q_n=c_nX_{n-1}/X_n.
   \]
4. The affine normalized recursion is
   \[
   z_{n-1}=(X_{n-1}/X_n)(z_n+h_n).
   \]
5. The terminal scalar is exactly
   \[
   H_m/\sigma_2=487-\sum_{n=3}^m h_n/X_n.
   \]
6. With \(A_n=X_n/h_n\), the exact recurrence is `(4.2)`.
7. With \(\eta_n=A_{n-1}/A_n\), the projective dynamics is `(5.2)` with
   \[
   \eta_3=5/33.
   \]
8. With \(D_n=A_n(1-\sum_{j=2}^nA_j^{-1})\), the affine cocycle is
   \[
   D_n=D_{n-1}/\eta_n-1,
   \qquad D_2=-487/486.
   \]
9. The simultaneous counterexample conditions are exactly the two terminal equations `(E)`.
10. The first-zero law is
    \[
    D_n=0\iff D_{n+1}=-1.
    \]

### Exact reduction, not proof

The desired theorem for \(m\ge3\) is reduced to:

> For prime \(\ell>2m-1\), the orbit `(T)` from `(B)` cannot simultaneously satisfy
> \[
> \eta_m=5(m^2-1)/(m(m-2)),
> \qquad D_m=0.
> \]

This is sharper than merely restating the localized ideal equivalence: every localization variable and every backward auxiliary sequence has disappeared.

### Evidence

- Exact saturated \((e_m,H_m)\) scan: no violation for \(2\le m\le300\).
- Direct modular scan: no \(P_m\)-unit common \((e_m,Y_m)\) zero for primes \(\ell\le10000\).
- The smallest unit endpoint hit \((29,3)\) satisfies the homogeneous terminal condition but misses the affine terminal condition.

None of these is promoted to an all-\(m\) theorem.

## 13. What would actually finish the proof

The reduction shows exactly what kind of new identity is needed.

A successful proof must establish a nonvanishing statement for the affine cocycle over the specific Riccati orbit. Equivalent useful forms are:

\[
D_m\ne0
\]

under the endpoint condition,

or

\[
1-\sum_{n=2}^m\frac1{A_n}\ne0,
\]

where \(A_n\) satisfies `(4.2)`, or a bilinear/conservation law for `(T)` whose value at `(B)` is incompatible with `(E)`.

What is **not** enough:

- transfer-matrix invertibility;
- the ordinary continuant Casoratian;
- a statement that terminal gcds inherit transfer-determinant support;
- positivity or real Sturm theory;
- the already-known localized ideal equality.

The triangular system `(T)` makes the obstruction explicit: the homogeneous coordinate \(\eta\) never sees \(D\). Therefore any closing invariant must genuinely couple the affine source to the arithmetic coefficients. I do not currently have such an invariant.

That is the first exact unresolved scalar and the narrowest proof target I can justify from the present recurrence data.
