# P2.7 Kummer twist: explicit recurrence and exact no-intertwiner theorem

## Result

Put

\[
Q(n)=946n^2-731n+153,
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
M(n)={}&104060n^6+127710n^5+12788n^4-34525n^3\\
&-8482n^2+3298n+1071,
\end{aligned}
\]

and

\[
N(n)=3784n^5-1032n^4-1925n^3+853n^2+328n-184.
\]

For Zudilin's sequence \(b_n\), define

\[
k_n=64^{-n}\frac{(5/2)_n}{n!},
\qquad h_n=k_nb_n.
\]

Then \(h_n\) satisfies the exact shift-order-three recurrence

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&2^{22}Q(n)(2n+1)(n+1)^4h_{n+1}\\
&\quad-2^{15}(2n+5)M(n)h_n\\
&\quad+2^8(2n+3)(2n+5)N(n)h_{n-1}\\
&\quad-Q(n+1)(n-1)^2(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+5)h_{n-2}=0,
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1}
\]

valid for \(n\ge2\).

Its characteristic polynomial is

\[
\boxed{
\rho^3-\frac{55}{64}\rho^2
+\frac{1}{2048}\rho-\frac{1}{2^{20}}
=\frac{4(64\rho)^3-220(64\rho)^2+8(64\rho)-1}{2^{20}}.}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus its roots are exactly the P2.7 roots. Moreover,

\[
k_n\sim \frac{64^{-n}n^{3/2}}{\Gamma(5/2)},
\]

so the Zudilin power \(-3/2\) becomes \(0\), exactly as proposed.

The root-and-power match is nevertheless insufficient. There is no nonzero rational Ore intertwiner:

> **Theorem.** There are no \(U,V\in\mathbb Q(n)\langle S\rangle\) with \(U\ne0\), \(\operatorname{ord}U<3\), and
> \[
> L_{27}U=V L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}.
> \]
> Consequently no rational functions \(u_0,u_1,u_2\), of degree at most \(10\) or of any degree, satisfy
> \[
> q_n=u_0(n)h_n+u_1(n)h_{n+1}+u_2(n)h_{n+2}
> \]
> for all \(n\).

The obstruction is the determinant line of the two rank-three difference modules. Their determinant quotient has divisor sum \(+12\) on the integer shift orbit and \(-12\) on the half-integer shift orbit. Such a function cannot be \(g(n+1)/g(n)\) for rational \(g\).

The code in §5 derives (1), verifies (2), proves the determinant obstruction exactly, and performs a direct degree-\(10\) polynomial-intertwiner nullspace calculation.

# 1. Derivation of the twisted recurrence

Zudilin's recurrence is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&2Q(n)(2n+1)(n+1)^3b_{n+1}-2M(n)b_n\\
&\quad+2nN(n)b_{n-1}
-Q(n+1)n(n-1)^3b_{n-2}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The twist ratio is

\[
\frac{k_{n+1}}{k_n}=\frac{2n+5}{128(n+1)}.
\tag{4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{k_{n+1}}{k_{n-1}}
=\frac{(2n+3)(2n+5)}{2^{14}n(n+1)},
\tag{5}
\]

and

\[
\frac{k_{n+1}}{k_{n-2}}
=\frac{(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+5)}
{2^{21}(n-1)n(n+1)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Substitute \(b_j=h_j/k_j\) into (3), multiply by \(k_{n+1}\), and then by \(2^{21}(n+1)\). This gives (1).

The four coefficients in (1) have degree \(7\). Their leading coefficients, in the order \(h_{n+1},h_n,h_{n-1},h_{n-2}\), are

\[
2^{23}\cdot946,\qquad
-2^{16}\cdot104060,\qquad
2^{10}\cdot3784,\qquad
-8\cdot946.
\]

Using \(104060=110\cdot946\) and \(3784=4\cdot946\) gives (2).

If

\[
b_n^{(j)}\sim c_j\mu_j^n n^{-3/2},
\]

then

\[
h_n^{(j)}
\sim \frac{c_j}{\Gamma(5/2)}
\left(\frac{\mu_j}{64}\right)^n,
\]

so both formal invariants at infinity agree with P2.7.

# 2. Positive-shift operators

Shift (1) by \(n\mapsto n+2\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
z_0(n)={}&-Q(n+3)(n+1)^2(2n+5)(2n+7)(2n+9),\\
z_1(n)={}&2^8(2n+7)(2n+9)N(n+2),\\
z_2(n)={}&-2^{15}(2n+9)M(n+2),\\
z_3(n)={}&2^{22}Q(n+2)(2n+5)(n+3)^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Then

\[
z_0(n)h_n+z_1(n)h_{n+1}+z_2(n)h_{n+2}+z_3(n)h_{n+3}=0,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}
=\frac{z_0(n)}{z_3(n)}
+\frac{z_1(n)}{z_3(n)}S
+\frac{z_2(n)}{z_3(n)}S^2+S^3.}
\tag{8}
\]

The question's P2.7 monic recurrence, shifted by \(n\mapsto n+2\), is represented by

\[
\boxed{
L_{27}
=-\frac{D(n)}{A(n)}
+\frac{C(n+1)}{A(n+1)}S
-\frac{B(n+2)}{A(n+2)}S^2+S^3.}
\tag{9}
\]

This rational operator is the exact translation of the stated monic convention. One must not clear its four terms by four different denominators.

Every Ore operator \(U\) can be right-divided by \(L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}\):

\[
U=H L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}+U_0,
\qquad \operatorname{ord}U_0<3.
\]

The first term acts as zero on source solutions. Hence the ansatz

\[
U=u_0(n)+u_1(n)S+u_2(n)S^2
\]

is the general reduced scalar ansatz, not an additional restriction.

# 3. Determinant obstruction

For a monic cubic

\[
L=\ell_0(n)+\ell_1(n)S+\ell_2(n)S^2+S^3,
\]

the companion matrix on \((u_n,u_{n+1},u_{n+2})^T\) has determinant

\[
\det C_L(n)=-\ell_0(n).
\]

## 3.1 Source determinant

From (7)–(8),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_Z(n)
=\frac{Q(n+3)(n+1)^2(2n+7)(2n+9)}
{2^{22}Q(n+2)(n+3)^4}.}
\tag{10}
\]

## 3.2 P2.7 determinant

Put

\[
R(n)=946n^2+4515n+5399.
\]

The quadratic in \(A(n)\) is \(R(n+1)\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{27}(n)
=\frac{(n+3)^4(n+4)^6R(n)}
{2^{10}(2n+5)^4(2n+7)^3(2n+9)^3R(n+1)}.}
\tag{11}
\]

Dividing (11) by (10) gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta_{27}(n)}{\Delta_Z(n)}
={}&\frac{(n+3)^8(n+4)^6}
{(n+1)^2
 (n+\frac52)^4(n+\frac72)^4(n+\frac92)^4}\\
&\times\frac{R(n)}{R(n+1)}
\frac{Q(n+2)}{Q(n+3)}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{12}
\]

The two quadratic ratios are shift coboundaries. Indeed, with

\[
g_R(n)=\frac1{R(n)},
\qquad
g_Q(n)=\frac1{Q(n+2)},
\]

one has

\[
\frac{R(n)}{R(n+1)}=\frac{g_R(n+1)}{g_R(n)},
\qquad
\frac{Q(n+2)}{Q(n+3)}=\frac{g_Q(n+1)}{g_Q(n)}.
\]

The remaining linear divisor has total multiplicity

\[
8+6-2=12
\]

on the integer shift orbit, and total multiplicity

\[
-4-4-4=-12
\]

on the half-integer shift orbit.

## Orbit-sum lemma

For rational \(g(n)\ne0\), the divisor of \(g(n+1)/g(n)\) has total multiplicity zero on each orbit under integer translation. This follows by writing the valuations of \(g\) along an orbit as a finitely supported sequence \(v_k\): the quotient has valuations \(v_{k-1}-v_k\), whose sum telescopes to zero.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta_{27}(n)}{\Delta_Z(n)}
\ne\frac{g(n+1)}{g(n)}
\quad(g\in\mathbb Q(n)^\times).}
\tag{13}
\]

Both operators have one horizontal Newton edge, with irreducible edge polynomial

\[
4x^3-220x^2+8x-1.
\]

A factorization of either rank-three operator over \(\mathbb Q(n)\) would factor this cubic; hence both difference modules are irreducible.

If a reduced nonzero \(U\) satisfied \(L_{27}U=V L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}\), it would induce an isomorphism of the two irreducible rank-three modules. A companion-basis matrix \(G(n)\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb Q(n))\) would satisfy

\[
G(n+1)C_Z(n)=C_{27}(n)G(n).
\]

Taking determinants would force

\[
\frac{\Delta_{27}(n)}{\Delta_Z(n)}
=\frac{\det G(n+1)}{\det G(n)},
\]

contradicting (13). This proves the theorem.

# 4. Consequence for the particular sequence \(q_n\)

The twisted denominator starts from \(b_0,b_1,b_2=1,7,163\). Its dominant ratio is a degree-three algebraic number. It therefore cannot satisfy a rational recurrence of order at most two, whose limiting characteristic polynomial would have degree at most two. Thus (8) is its minimal order-three annihilator.

If

\[
q_n=u_0(n)h_n+u_1(n)h_{n+1}+u_2(n)h_{n+2}
\]

held for all \(n\), then \(L_{27}U\) would annihilate \(h\), so minimality would make it right-divisible by \(L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}\). The no-intertwiner theorem rules this out.

Therefore the exact output of the requested degree-\(10\) rational search is

\[
\boxed{\text{no solution}.}
\]

This is an all-degree theorem, not merely a failed bounded ansatz.

#!/usr/bin/env sage
from sage.all import *

Rn.<n> = PolynomialRing(QQ)
K = Rn.fraction_field()
Rx.<x> = PolynomialRing(QQ)


def sh(f, j):
    f = K(f)
    return K(f.numerator()(n+j)) / K(f.denominator()(n+j))


def Q(t):
    return 946*t**2 - 731*t + 153


def M6(t):
    return (104060*t**6 + 127710*t**5 + 12788*t**4
            - 34525*t**3 - 8482*t**2 + 3298*t + 1071)


def N5(t):
    return (3784*t**5 - 1032*t**4 - 1925*t**3
            + 853*t**2 + 328*t - 184)


# ---------- Task 1: derive the twist ----------
A0 = Rn(2*Q(n)*(2*n+1)*(n+1)**3)
B0 = Rn(-2*M6(n))
C0 = Rn(2*n*N5(n))
D0 = Rn(-Q(n+1)*n*(n-1)**3)

r10 = K((2*n+5)/(128*(n+1)))
r1m1 = K((2*n+3)*(2*n+5)/(128**2*n*(n+1)))
r1m2 = K((2*n+1)*(2*n+3)*(2*n+5)
          /(128**3*(n-1)*n*(n+1)))

clear = K(2**21*(n+1))

Ah = Rn(2**22*Q(n)*(2*n+1)*(n+1)**4)
Bh = Rn(-2**15*(2*n+5)*M6(n))
Ch = Rn(2**8*(2*n+3)*(2*n+5)*N5(n))
Dh = Rn(-Q(n+1)*(n-1)**2*(2*n+1)*(2*n+3)*(2*n+5))

assert K(clear*A0) == K(Ah)
assert K(clear*B0*r10) == K(Bh)
assert K(clear*C0*r1m1) == K(Ch)
assert K(clear*D0*r1m2) == K(Dh)

def b_terms(count):

    for m in range(2, count-1):
        num = (2*M6(m)*b[m] - 2*m*N5(m)*b[m-1]

bt = b_terms(20)

for m in range(2, len(ht)-1):
    assert (Ah(m)*ht[m+1] + Bh(m)*ht[m]
            + Ch(m)*ht[m-1] + Dh(m)*ht[m-2]) == 0


# ---------- Positive-shift source operator ----------
z0 = Rn(Dh(n+2))
z1 = Rn(Ch(n+2))
z2 = Rn(Bh(n+2))
z3 = Rn(Ah(n+2))
LZ = [K(z0/z3), K(z1/z3), K(z2/z3), K(1)]

    return (1024*(2*t+5)**4*(2*t+7)**3*(2*t+9)**3
            *(946*t**2+6407*t+10860))


def B27(t):
    return (128*(2*t+7)**3*(2*t+9)**3
            *(104060*t**6+1745370*t**5+12145238*t**4
              +44886481*t**3+92943995*t**2
              +102256019*t+46709052))


def C27(t):
    return (16*(t+3)**4*(2*t+9)**3
            *(3784*t**5+57792*t**4+351019*t**3
              +1059230*t**2+1587211*t+944620))

                *R(n+1)*Q(n+3)))

def linear_orbit_sum(f, residue):
    f = K(f)
    total = ZZ(0)
    for sign, pol in [(1, Rn(f.numerator())),
                      (-1, Rn(f.denominator()))]:
        for fac, exponent in pol.factor():
            if fac.degree() == 1:
                a = QQ(fac.monic()[0])  # fac.monic() = n+a
                if (a-QQ(residue)).denominator() == 1:
                    total += sign*exponent
    return total

# ---------- Minimal custom Ore arithmetic ----------
ZERO = K(0)


def trim(P):
    P = [K(c) for c in P]
    while len(P) > 1 and P[-1] == 0:
        P.pop()
    return P


def op_add(P,Q):
    out = [ZERO]*max(len(P),len(Q))
    for i in range(len(out)):
        if i < len(P): out[i] += P[i]
        if i < len(Q): out[i] += Q[i]
    return trim(out)


def op_sub(P,Q):
    return op_add(P,[-c for c in Q])


def op_mul(P,Q):
    P,Q = trim(P),trim(Q)
    out = [ZERO]*(len(P)+len(Q)-1)
    for i,pi in enumerate(P):
        for j,qj in enumerate(Q):
            out[i+j] += pi*sh(qj,i)
    return trim(out)


def right_quo_rem(P,L):
    P,L = trim(P),trim(L)
    r = len(L)-1
    quo = [ZERO]*max(1,len(P)-r)
    while len(P)-1 >= r and not (len(P)==1 and P[0]==0):
        d = len(P)-1-r
        c = P[-1]/sh(L[-1],d)
        if d >= len(quo): quo += [ZERO]*(d+1-len(quo))
        quo[d] += c
        P = op_sub(P,op_mul([ZERO]*d+[c],L))
    return trim(quo),trim(P)


# Sanity check.
quo,rem = right_quo_rem(op_mul(L27,LZ),LZ)
assert all(c==0 for c in rem)
assert trim(quo)==trim(L27)


def coefficient_matrix(columns):
    rows=[]
    for j in range(3):
        common=Rn(1)
        for col in columns:
            common=lcm(common,Rn(col[j].denominator()))
        polys=[]
        for col in columns:
            val=K(common)*col[j]
            assert val.denominator().degree()==0
            polys.append(Rn(val))
        d=max((p.degree() for p in polys if p!=0),default=-1)
        for e in range(d+1):
            row=[QQ(p[e]) if e<=p.degree() else QQ(0) for p in polys]
            if any(row): rows.append(row)
    return matrix(QQ,rows) if rows else matrix(QQ,0,len(columns))


def search_fixed_denominator(max_degree,denominator=1):
    denominator=K(denominator)
    columns=[]
    labels=[]
    for i in range(3):
        for k in range(max_degree+1):
            U=[ZERO]*i+[K(n**k)/denominator]
            _,rem=right_quo_rem(op_mul(L27,U),LZ)
            rem=trim(rem)+[ZERO]*(3-len(trim(rem)))
            columns.append(rem[:3])
            labels.append((i,k))
    mat=coefficient_matrix(columns)
    ker=mat.right_kernel()
    print("denominator =",denominator,
          "; numerator degree <=",max_degree,
          "; nullity =",ker.dimension())
    if ker.dimension():
        v=ker.basis()[0]
        nums=[Rn(0),Rn(0),Rn(0)]
        for c,(i,k) in zip(v,labels): nums[i]+=c*n**k
        print("candidate numerators:",nums)
    return ker


# Direct degree-10 polynomial check. The determinant theorem above

# 6. Interpretation

The \(64^{-n}(5/2)_n/n!\) twist is the correct formal preconditioner: it matches all three roots and all three powers. The failure occurs at the next invariant, the determinant class in

\[
\mathbb Q(n)^\times/
\{g(n+1)/g(n):g\in\mathbb Q(n)^\times\}.
\]

A viable bridge must therefore leave the requested rational scalar Ore category—for example, a hypergeometric-coefficient matrix gauge, a nonlocal Mellin/binomial transform, or a normal-function/Barnes construction attached directly to the P2.7 initial vectors.
