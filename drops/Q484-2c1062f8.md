# Q484: CRT equidistribution for (HM)_3

## Executive summary

The Erdos–Turan route is not the right normalization. The issue is that the CRT set

\[
S=Z_{p_1}\times Z_{p_2}\times Z_{p_3}\subset \mathbf Z/(p_1p_2p_3)\mathbf Z
\]

has bounded cardinality when |Z_p|=O(1), while the ambient modulus is of size X^3. Classical discrepancy is an additive error notion, not a relative-density statement. The desired estimate is a short-interval hitting estimate for a sparse set.

The correct target is not full equidistribution but:

\[
\#(S\cap[0,X^2])\ll |S|X^{-1}.
\]

This is a small-scale anti-clustering statement.

## (A) Erdos–Turan diagnosis

The factorization of Fourier coefficients is correct:

\[
\widehat{1_S}(h)=\prod_i \sum_{r_i\in Z_{p_i}}e(h_ir_i/p_i).
\]

However, the conclusion from Erdos–Turan is being interpreted incorrectly.

For a set of N points in a circle, discrepancy is usually normalized as

\[
D_N=\sup_I\left|\frac{\#(S\cap I)}N-|I|\right|.
\]

For |S|=O(1), even the optimal discrepancy is generally O(1). This is not a failure of the estimate; it is the correct scale. A discrepancy bound of O(1) says nothing about a count of size O(1/X).

The desired statement asks for a relative improvement below the natural one-point scale. It requires arithmetic information about the locations of the points.

## (B) Fourier cancellation from palindromy

Palindromy alone does not give enough cancellation.

For a pair

\[
Z_p=\{r,p-1-r\},
\]

one obtains

\[
\widehat{1_{Z_p}}(h)=e(hr/p)(1+e(-h(2r+1)/p)).
\]

The magnitude can be close to 2. Therefore the involution

\[
r\mapsto p-1-r
\]

only creates symmetry, not Fourier decay.

The missing ingredient would be a bound of the form

\[
\left|\sum_{r\in Z_p}e(hr/p)\right|\ll p^{-\delta}|Z_p|,
\]

uniformly for h not divisible by p. Palindromy does not imply this.

## (C) What actually needs proving

For fixed p_1,p_2,p_3, write the CRT element explicitly:

\[
R(r_1,r_2,r_3)=r_1M_1u_1+r_2M_2u_2+r_3M_3u_3 \pmod M,
\]

where M=p_1p_2p_3.

The HM_3 estimate is equivalent to showing that this linear form does not map the small zero sets into a short interval unusually often.

Thus the correct statement is:

> The restricted CRT map has no short-interval concentration on the product of Apéry zero sets.

This is weaker than equidistribution and is the right theorem.

## (D) Better techniques than Erdos–Turan

### 1. Additive energy / Balog–Szemeredi viewpoint

A concentration in an interval of length X^2 would imply many additive relations among CRT images. Therefore one can bound the second additive energy

\[
E(S)=\#\{a+b=c+d:a,b,c,d\in S\}.
\]

If E(S) is close to the random value |S|^4/M, then clustering is impossible.

This fits the fact that the sets Z_p are not intervals or arithmetic progressions.

### 2. Geometry of numbers

The condition

\[
R(r_1,r_2,r_3)<X^2
\]

is a lattice-point condition in a three-dimensional box with one linear inequality. If the product set has no aligned directions, Minkowski/geometry-of-numbers estimates can give the required saving.

### 3. Trace-function cancellation

The strongest route is to use the origin of Z_p. The condition

\[
r\in Z_p
\]

is not arbitrary; it is the vanishing locus of a finite-field trace function (unit-root section of the Apéry sheaf).

Then the triple correlation becomes a product trace-function sum. Deligne-type bounds are exactly designed for this situation.

The desired HM_3 estimate should follow from a trilinear correlation bound:

\[
\sum_m g_{p_1}(m)g_{p_2}(m)g_{p_3}(m)
\ll X^{-\eta}.
\]

## (E) What prevents clustering?

The strongest structural obstruction is that Z_p is an algebraic zero set, not an arbitrary sparse set.

For Apéry sequences:

* Z_p comes from Frobenius conditions.
* The Mellin transform has large monodromy.
* Different primes correspond to independent Frobenius parameters.
* The product monodromy is expected to be independent:

\[
SL_2(\mathbf F_{p_1})\times SL_2(\mathbf F_{p_2})\times SL_2(\mathbf F_{p_3}).
\]

Therefore a simultaneous CRT alignment should behave like three independent random conditions.

This is exactly the heuristic behind the Poisson model.

## (F) Recommended proof strategy for HM_3

The optimal route is likely:

1. Encode

\[
1_{Z_p}(r)
\]

as a trace function of the Apéry/K3 sheaf.

2. Expand the triple correlation by finite-field Fourier transform.

3. Apply Deligne/Katz bounds to nontrivial Fourier modes.

4. Handle exceptional modes using monodromy independence.

This avoids requiring individual Fourier decay of Z_p.

## Relation to P3.2

The new universal twist and palindromic symmetry help structurally:

\[
Z_p=p-1-Z_p.
\]

They remove one possible source of bias, namely asymmetric zero distributions. However, they do not by themselves solve HM_3. The obstruction is not one-dimensional symmetry; it is three-prime CRT concentration.

The frontier theorem is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Large monodromy }+\text{ trace-function independence }\Rightarrow HM_3.}
\]

The remaining missing ingredient is a quantitative three-prime correlation estimate, not an Erdos–Turan discrepancy bound.
