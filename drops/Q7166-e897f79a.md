# P32 toric/Hasse route audit

## 1. The constant-term degree argument

Let the repository Laurent polynomial be the nonnegative Laurent polynomial Lambda with

\[
 b_n=CT(\Lambda^n),\qquad \Lambda(1,1,1)=40.
\]

Assume the reduction of the sequence modulo p has a polynomial representative f_p(r) of degree d_p<p-1 on F_p. Then for every m>d_p,

\[
\Delta^m b_0=\sum_{j=0}^m(-1)^{m-j}{m\choose j}b_j\equiv 0\pmod p.
\]

Using the constant term representation,

\[
\Delta^m b_0=CT((\Lambda-1)^m).
\]

Because Lambda has nonnegative coefficients and CT(Lambda^j) counts nonnegative terms, the value is a nonnegative integer. The upper bound is

\[
0\le CT((\Lambda-1)^m)\le CT((|\Lambda|+1)^m),
\]

and the crude bound coming from evaluation at (1,1,1) gives

\[
CT((\Lambda-1)^m)\le (\Lambda(1,1,1)-1)^m=39^m.
\]

Therefore, if m=d_p+1 and the integer is nonzero, divisibility by p implies

\[
p\le CT((\Lambda-1)^m)\le39^m.
\]

Hence

\[
d_p+1=m>\frac{\log p}{\log39}
\]

and

\[
d_p\ge \frac{\log p}{\log39}-1.
\]

Endpoint correction: if CT((Lambda-1)^m)=0, the argument gives no information. Thus one must prove positivity for the chosen m. For m=0 the statement is irrelevant. Exceptional primes are exactly those for which the first nonzero difference at or after d_p+1 has size divisible by p; the logarithmic conclusion applies only after excluding zero constant terms.

## 2. Does this imply a useful zero bound?

No. A lower bound of order log(p) on the degree of the Hasse polynomial is far below what is needed.

The number of zeros of a nonzero polynomial over F_p is at most its degree. Thus the degree estimate gives only

\[
Z(p)\le p-1-d_p
\]

for the complementary nonzero information, and the logarithmic lower bound on d_p does not improve the trivial O(p) scale.

Even if one obtains the stronger square/twisted-square Hasse polynomial identities, the conclusion remains a structural factorization statement. To reach

\[
\sum_{p\sim X} Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}/\log X
\]

one needs either:

1. a degree bound with cancellation information on the zero divisor, or
2. an equidistribution/monodromy theorem controlling the Frobenius traces.

The square identity alone does not supply such cancellation.

## 3. Precise obstruction

The obstruction is parameter count. The toric constant-term method gives a one-variable polynomial degree statement. It sees the recurrence sequence as a function on F_p, but it does not distinguish a polynomial of degree O(log p) from one having a highly structured exceptional zero set. A zero set of size p^{1-o(1)} is compatible with the available degree information.

Likewise, Hasse squaring controls congruence classes and Frobenius symmetries but does not bound the number of F_p-points on the zero locus without additional geometric input.

Therefore the route is valid as a logarithmic nondegeneracy certificate, but cannot reach the square-root variance scale by itself.

## Conclusion

The corrected theorem is:

> If the reduced representative of b_r mod p has degree d_p<p-1 and CT((Lambda-1)^{d_p+1}) is positive, then
> \[
> d_p\ge \frac{\log p}{\log39}-1.
> \]

The argument is rigorous after the positivity exception is stated. The Hasse polynomial refinements do not currently yield the required P32 square-root average bound; a new monodromy, equidistribution, or genuinely higher-dimensional arithmetic estimate is required.
