# Q496: The explicit Kummer/Mellin object for the Apéry zero set

## Executive verdict

There is an explicit and uniformly bounded-complexity \(\ell\)-adic construction, but it is **not** a rank-four lisse sheaf on the additive parameter line

\[
r\in \mathbb A^1(\mathbb F_p)
\]

whose trace at the point \(r\) is \(b_r\). The natural parameter is the **multiplicative character**

\[
A_j=\omega_p^j\in \widehat{\mathbb F_p^\times},
\qquad 1\le j\le p-2,
\]

not the additive residue \(j\in\mathbb F_p\). The exact construction is

\[
\mathcal L_{p,j}=\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{A_j}
\]

on a fixed three-dimensional toric arrangement complement \(U\), and

\[
\widetilde b_{p,j}
:=-\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm alt}
 \bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
 R\Gamma_c(U_{\overline{\mathbb F}_p},\mathcal L_{p,j})\bigr)
\]

is a cyclotomic integer satisfying

\[
\boxed{\widetilde b_{p,j}\equiv b_j\pmod{\mathfrak p}}.
\]

Equivalently, \(\widetilde b_{p,j}\) is the finite-field **Mellin transform** of the fixed direct-image complex \(R\Lambda_!\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\), evaluated at the character \(A_j\).

After an exact Jacobi/Gauss convolution, this same number is the value at \(t=1\) of the balanced Katz hypergeometric object

\[
\operatorname{Hyp}
 (\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon;
  A_j,A_j,A_j^{-1},A_j^{-1}).
\]

That hypergeometric sheaf has generic rank four in the **geometric variable \(t\)**, but \(t=1\) is its singular balanced point. Its middle-extension stalk there has rank three, with semisimplified factor

\[
\boxed{
P_{p,j}(X)=(X-p)(X^2-a_{p,j}X+p^3),
\qquad a_{p,j}=\widetilde b_{p,j}-p.
}
\]

Thus, modulo \(p\), the Apéry zero condition is controlled by the trace of the rank-two quotient:

\[
b_j\equiv a_{p,j}\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]

This is not the trace-zero locus of a generic rank-four \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) family.

There is a second major correction. The minimal Picard--Fuchs operator for the \(\zeta(3)\) Apéry generating function is order three,

\[
\mathcal L_{\rm Ap}
=\theta^3-z(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
 +z^2(\theta+1)^3,
\]

not order four. The corresponding K3 transcendental local system has rank three and orthogonal monodromy (the symmetric-square image of a rank-two system), not \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\). A rank-four hypergeometric object does occur in the Mellin calculation, but it is a different local system in a different geometric parameter.

These distinctions are essential for the proposed \((HM)_3\) architecture.

# (A) The explicit \(\ell\)-adic construction

## A.1 The fixed toric variety and its Kummer twists

Let

\[
T=(\mathbb G_m)^3,
\qquad
U=T\setminus V(\Lambda),
\]

so that

\[
\Lambda:U\longrightarrow\mathbb G_m
\]

is a regular morphism.

Fix a prime \(p\ge5\), an auxiliary prime \(\ell\ne p\), and put

\[
E_p=\mathbb Q(\mu_{p-1}).
\]

Choose a prime \(\mathfrak p\mid p\) of \(E_p\), together with the Teichmüller character

\[
\omega_p:\mathbb F_p^\times\longrightarrow\mu_{p-1}
\]

normalized by

\[
\omega_p(a)\equiv a\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]

For \(1\le j\le p-2\), define

\[
A_j=\chi_{p,j}=\omega_p^j.
\]

Choose an embedding \(E_p\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\), and let

\[
\mathcal K_{A_j}
\]

be the rank-one Kummer sheaf on \(\mathbb G_m/\mathbb F_p\) whose Frobenius trace at \(a\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) is \(A_j(a)\). Pull it back along \(\Lambda\):

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{p,j}=\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{A_j}
}
\tag{A.1}
\]

on \(U\).

The Grothendieck trace formula gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde b_{p,j}
&:=-\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm alt}
 \left(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
 R\Gamma_c(U_{\overline{\mathbb F}_p},\mathcal L_{p,j})\right)\\
&=-\sum_{u\in U(\mathbb F_p)}A_j(\Lambda(u)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.2}
\]

This is an algebraic integer in \(E_p\). Reducing it at \(\mathfrak p\), one has

\[
A_j(\Lambda(u))\equiv\Lambda(u)^j\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]

Every exponent of every variable in \(\Lambda^j\) lies in \([-j,j]\). For \(j\le p-2\), the only multiple of \(p-1\) in this range is zero. Three applications of torus orthogonality therefore give

\[
\sum_{u\in T(\mathbb F_p)}\Lambda(u)^j
=(-1)^3\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^j=-b_j.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde b_{p,j}\equiv b_j\pmod{\mathfrak p},
\qquad 1\le j\le p-2.
}
\tag{A.3}
\]

This is exactly the construction proved in `oracleC_result.tex`, equations `eq:oracleC-Kummer-object` and `eq:oracleC-Frobenius-coordinate`.

### Important meaning of “gives \(b_j\bmod p\)”

The characteristic-zero Frobenius trace does **not** generally equal the integer \(b_j\), and it need not vanish when \(b_j\equiv0\pmod p\). What is true is reduction at the chosen prime above \(p\):

\[
\widetilde b_{p,j}\pmod{\mathfrak p}=b_j\pmod p.
\]

The repository records the concrete example

\[
(p,j)=(11,5),\qquad
\widetilde b_{11,5}=-33\ne0,\qquad
b_5\equiv0\pmod{11}.
\]

Thus the Apéry zero set is a **\(p\)-adic divisibility/Hasse-zero condition**, not a characteristic-zero trace-zero condition. This distinction prevents a direct application of ordinary Sato--Tate trace density.

## A.2 The two endpoints

The nontrivial-character formula above naturally covers \(1\le j\le p-2\). The endpoints require care.

The exponents \(j=0\) and \(j=p-1\) give the same multiplicative character, namely the trivial character, but the raw extension-by-zero Kummer sum over \(U\) gives

\[
-\#U(\mathbb F_p)\equiv14\pmod p,
\]

not \(b_{p-1}=1\). At \(p=7\), it is even zero.

For both endpoints one instead uses the constant sheaf on the full torus \(T\). Its negative Frobenius supertrace is

\[
-(p-1)^3\equiv1\pmod p,
\]

which agrees with

\[
b_0=b_{p-1}=1\pmod p.
\]

This endpoint discontinuity is another reason there is no single natural lisse sheaf on an additive \(j\)-line whose point traces are all \(b_j\).

## A.3 The clean conceptual form: a finite-field Mellin transform

Define the fixed direct-image complex

\[
\mathcal G:=R\Lambda_!\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell
\]

on \(\mathbb G_m\). The projection formula gives

\[
R\Gamma_c(U,\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{A_j})
\simeq
R\Gamma_c\left(\mathbb G_m,
 \mathcal G\otimes\mathcal K_{A_j}\right).
\tag{A.4}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde b_{p,j}
=-\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm alt}\left(
\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
R\Gamma_c(\mathbb G_m,
 \mathcal G\otimes\mathcal K_{A_j})\right).
}
\tag{A.5}
\]

This is the finite-field Mellin transform of the fixed sheaf complex \(\mathcal G\), evaluated at \(A_j\).

If

\[
g_p(t)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm alt}
 \left(\operatorname{Frob}_t\mid\mathcal G_{\bar t}\right),
\]

then schematically

\[
\widetilde b_{p,j}
=-\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p^\times}g_p(t)A_j(t).
\tag{A.6}
\]

Thus the fibers

\[
X_t=\Lambda^{-1}(t)
\]

do enter the construction, but \(b_j\) is a **multiplicative Fourier coefficient of their point-count function**, not the trace of the fiber at \(t=j\).

This is precisely the framework of Katz's finite-field Mellin transforms.

## A.4 Relation to the Dwork/K3 family

The geometric one-parameter family naturally attached to the constant-term period is

\[
Y_z:\ 1-z\Lambda(x,y,z)=0
\]

inside the three-torus, followed by an appropriate compactification and resolution. Its holomorphic period is

\[
\Phi(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nz^n.
\]

The variable \(z\) is a genuine geometric deformation parameter. The index \(n\) is the coefficient number in the period expansion. They are not interchangeable.

In characteristic \(p\), the Hasse invariant of the K3 family is represented, after the usual coordinate/gauge normalization, by a truncation of the period,

\[
H_p(z)\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}b_nz^n\pmod p.
\]

Again, \(b_n\) is a coefficient of the Hasse polynomial, not its value at the point \(z=n\).

Therefore:

* Dwork/crystalline cohomology of \(Y_z\) explains the period, formal Brauer group, unit root, and Hasse invariant;
* it does **not** by itself produce a sheaf on \(\mathbb A^1_j\) with point trace \(b_j\);
* the bridge from the coefficient index \(j\) to cohomology is the Kummer/Mellin transform in (A.1)--(A.6).

## A.5 The exact hypergeometric realization

The repository goes further and evaluates the Mellin object by an exact Jacobi convolution.

For \(q=p^f\), put

\[
A=A_j\circ N_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_p}
\]

and

\[
S_{p,j}^{(f)}
=-\sum_{u\in U(\mathbb F_q)}A(\Lambda(u)).
\]

Then

\[
S_{p,j}^{(f)}
=-\frac1{q-1}\sum_\rho
J_q(\bar\rho,A)^2J_q(\bar\rho,A\rho)^2,
\tag{A.7}
\]

where all \(q-1\) multiplicative characters \(\rho\) occur. In Gauss-sum form,

\[
J_q(\bar\rho,A)^2J_q(\bar\rho,A\rho)^2
=q^{-2}g_q(\bar\rho)^4
 g_q(A\rho)^2g_q(A^{-1}\rho)^2.
\tag{A.8}
\]

This is the trace formula for the balanced hypergeometric object

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_A
=\operatorname{Hyp}
 (\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon;
 A,A,A^{-1},A^{-1}).
}
\tag{A.9}
\]

With the normalization used in the repository,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}\mid(\mathcal H_A)_{t=1})
=S_{p,j}^{(f)}.
}
\tag{A.10}
\]

So the answer to “Kloosterman-type or not?” is:

* it is not a classical Kloosterman sheaf;
* after Jacobi convolution it is a Katz balanced hypergeometric sheaf, hence genuinely in the Kloosterman/hypergeometric sheaf world;
* the relevant value is the singular stalk \(t=1\), while the exponent \(j\) changes the character parameters \(A,A,A^{-1},A^{-1}\).

A direct additive exponential-sum treatment of the phase \(t\Lambda\) is misleading here. The phase is degenerate; the naive Newton polytope would manufacture rank/volume \(38\). The correct nondegenerate GKZ circuit appears only after the Jacobi convolution. This correction is proved in

```text
problems/3.2/lemmaBprime_result.tex
```

under `The corrected Adolphson--Sperber object`.

## A.6 What the unit-root crystal contributes

The unit-root/crystalline interpretation is real and important, but it is the final arithmetic layer rather than the source of an additive \(j\)-parameter sheaf.

At the special point \(t=1\), the effective Frobenius factor is

\[
P_{p,j}(X)
=(X-p)(X^2-a_{p,j}X+p^3),
\qquad
 a_{p,j}=S_{p,j}^{(1)}-p.
\tag{A.11}
\]

Since

\[
a_{p,j}\equiv S_{p,j}^{(1)}\equiv b_j\pmod{\mathfrak p},
\]

the quadratic roots have slopes

\[
(0,3),\quad(1,2),\quad\text{or}\quad(3/2,3/2)
\]

according as \(v_{\mathfrak p}(a_{p,j})=0,1,\ge2\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 b_j\not\equiv0\pmod p
 \iff \text{there is one unit root},
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 b_j\equiv0\pmod p
 \iff \text{the special Mellin object is nonordinary}.
}
\tag{A.12}
\]

This is the correct unit-root formulation of the Apéry zero set. It does not turn \(j\) into a geometric point of \(\mathbb A^1\); it turns the zero condition into a slope/Hasse condition for a family indexed by tame characters.

## A.7 Why there is no natural bounded-rank sheaf on the additive \(j\)-line

As a set, \(\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}\) can of course be identified with \(\mathbb F_p\). Geometrically, however, the construction uses

\[
j\pmod{p-1}\longmapsto A_j=\omega_p^j.
\]

This map has the following features:

1. it uses the cyclic character group of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\), not the additive group of \(\mathbb F_p\);
2. it depends on a Teichmüller/character normalization;
3. \(j=0\) and \(j=p-1\) give the same character;
4. it is not an algebraic morphism \(\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb F_p}\to\) a connected character variety;
5. the reflection \(j\mapsto p-1-j\) is character inversion \(A_j\mapsto A_j^{-1}\), which is the natural symmetry of the Mellin problem.

One can package the \(p-1\) Kummer twists over a finite, zero-dimensional character scheme, or over the group algebra of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\). But that base is a disjoint finite set, not a curve carrying useful geometric monodromy.

Tautologically, one could interpolate the values \(b_j\) by a degree-\(<p\) polynomial or build a direct sum of skyscraper sheaves. Such a construction has conductor/complexity growing like \(p\), has no explanatory monodromy, and is useless for \((HM)_3\).

Therefore the honest answer to the literal first question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{There is no known natural uniformly bounded-conductor rank-four
lisse sheaf on }\mathbb A^1_j
\text{ with trace }b_j\bmod p.
}
\]

The natural object is the Kummer-indexed Mellin family above.

# (B) What is the rank?

There is no single rank because four different objects have been conflated.

| Object | Genuine parameter | Rank/dimension |
|---|---|---:|
| Kummer sheaf \(\mathcal K_{A_j}\) on \(\mathbb G_m\) | point of \(\mathbb G_m\), with \(A_j\) fixed | 1 |
| Pullback \(\mathcal L_{p,j}=\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{A_j}\) on \(U\) | torus point, with \(j\) fixed | 1 |
| Compactly supported complex \(R\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal L_{p,j})\) | \(j\) is a discrete character index | not a sheaf rank; total Betti number is uniformly \(O(1)\) |
| Generic balanced hypergeometric sheaf \(\mathcal H_A\) in \(t\ne1\) | geometric hypergeometric variable \(t\) | 4 |
| Middle-extension stalk of \(\mathcal H_A\) at \(t=1\) | fixed singular point | 3 |
| Non-Tate quotient at \(t=1\) | character \(A_j\) discrete | 2 |
| Minimal Apéry K3 Picard--Fuchs local system | geometric deformation variable \(z\) | 3 |
| Full \(H^2\) of a K3 surface | geometric deformation variable \(z\) | 22, mostly algebraic/constant; transcendental part rank 3 |

## The Apéry Picard--Fuchs operator is order three

From the recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}
=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)b_n-n^3b_{n-1},
\]

the generating function \(B(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nz^n\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\left[
\theta^3-z(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+z^2(\theta+1)^3
\right]B(z)=0.
}
\tag{B.1}
\]

This is the minimal K3 Picard--Fuchs equation. It is the symmetric square of an order-two equation. Some mirror-symmetry treatments adjoin an integration/extension and write a related fourth-order operator, but that derived rank-four system is not the K3 transcendental local system and is not the sheaf whose point traces are \(b_j\).

Therefore the inference

```text
Apéry ODE has order 4, so the desired r-sheaf should have rank 4
```

is not valid.

# (C) What is the geometric monodromy group?

Again, the answer depends on which object is meant.

## C.1 There is no geometric monodromy “as \(j\) varies” on \(\mathbb A^1_j\)

The index \(j\) varies through the finite character group

\[
\widehat{\mathbb F_p^\times}.
\]

This is the Mellin aspect. It is not the set of rational points of a fixed connected algebraic curve over which \(\mathcal L_{p,j}\) forms a lisse \(\ell\)-adic local system of fixed rank. Consequently a phrase such as

\[
G_{\rm geom}(\mathcal F_j)=\mathrm{Sp}_4
\]

is not presently defined in the proposed additive-parameter sense.

Katz's *Convolution and Equidistribution* develops equidistribution theorems for Mellin transforms as the character varies. That is the correct conceptual replacement for ordinary pointwise monodromy on an \(r\)-line. However, the present problem has two additional difficulties:

1. \(p\) itself varies, so this is a horizontal/two-characteristic problem;
2. the event is \(\mathfrak p\)-divisibility of an algebraic trace, not a real/complex trace interval.

Neither follows from ordinary Deligne equidistribution.

## C.2 The K3 Picard--Fuchs monodromy is orthogonal, not symplectic

For a K3 surface, the cup-product form on \(H^2\) is symmetric. In the Apéry pencil the generic Picard rank is \(19\), so the varying transcendental part has rank

\[
22-19=3.
\]

Its monodromy preserves a nondegenerate symmetric form. The order-three Picard--Fuchs operator is a symmetric square of a rank-two operator, so the connected algebraic monodromy is the symmetric-square image

\[
\mathrm{SL}_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{SO}_3.
\]

Thus the standard formulation is

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm geom}^{\circ}\simeq\mathrm{SO}_3
\simeq\mathrm{PGL}_2,
}
\]

up to the usual finite-index/arithmetic distinctions. It is not \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\).

The expression “Apéry K3 sheaf with \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) monodromy” mixes the K3 local system with the separate rank-four hypergeometric Mellin object.

## C.3 The generic hypergeometric \(t\)-sheaf is the rank-four symplectic candidate

For fixed nontrivial \(A\), the two parameter multisets in

\[
\operatorname{Hyp}(\varepsilon^4;A,A,A^{-1},A^{-1})
\]

are disjoint, so the generic hypergeometric sheaf is geometrically irreducible of rank four. Its Hodge degrees are

\[
0,1,2,3,
\]

and, after the standard Tate/determinant normalization, its weight-three polarization is alternating. Hence its geometric monodromy is contained in a symplectic group:

\[
G_{\rm geom}\subseteq\mathrm{Sp}_4.
\]

At \(t=1\), local monodromy is a nontrivial rank-one unipotent pseudoreflection, i.e. a symplectic transvection.

These facts make \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) the natural generic candidate. A theorem that the group is exactly \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) requires the remaining Katz-classification checks:

1. geometric primitivity rather than Kummer induction;
2. exclusion of finite and imprimitive cases;
3. exclusion of a proper connected irreducible subgroup such as a symmetric-power image;
4. treatment of small-order exceptional characters \(A\);
5. determinant and constant Kummer-twist normalization.

The current repository proves irreducibility, the balanced transvection at \(t=1\), the Hodge data, and the special-stalk factorization. It does **not** yet state or prove a uniform theorem

\[
G_{\rm geom}=\mathrm{Sp}_4
\quad\text{for every }(p,j).
\]

It would therefore be premature to use exact \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) monodromy as an established input.

Most importantly, even an exact \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) theorem for the generic \(t\)-sheaf would not by itself govern \(Z_p\), because all Apéry values occur at the singular point \(t=1\), where the middle-extension stalk has rank three and semisimplifies as a Tate line plus a rank-two quotient:

\[
P_{p,j}(X)=(X-p)(X^2-a_{p,j}X+p^3).
\]

Modulo \(p\), it is the rank-two trace \(a_{p,j}\), not a generic rank-four trace, that equals \(b_j\).

# Corrected architecture for \((HM)_3\)

The proposed four-step plan should be rewritten.

## Original Step 1

> Construct a rank-four sheaf on \(\mathbb A^1_r\) whose trace at \(r\) is \(b_r\bmod p\).

This is not the natural geometry and is presently unavailable with bounded conductor.

## Corrected Step 1: already achieved

Construct the fixed toric Mellin object

\[
\mathcal G=R\Lambda_!\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell
\]

and its Kummer twists, with

\[
\widetilde b_{p,j}
=-\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm alt}R\Gamma_c
 (\mathbb G_m,\mathcal G\otimes\mathcal K_{A_j}),
\qquad
\widetilde b_{p,j}\equiv b_j\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]

This has uniform geometric complexity and is proved in the repository.

## Corrected Step 2

Develop a theorem for the **character aspect**

\[
A\in\widehat{\mathbb F_p^\times}
\longmapsto
R\Gamma_c(U,\Lambda^*\mathcal K_A),
\]

rather than monodromy of an additive \(r\)-line. Katz's finite-field Mellin-transform theory is the correct model, but the needed theorem must be horizontal in \(p\) and compatible with the integral structure at \(\mathfrak p\mid p\).

## Corrected Step 3

Count nonordinary characters:

\[
Z_p
=\{j:v_{\mathfrak p}(a_{p,j})\ge1\}.
\]

This is a \(p\)-adic slope/Hasse-zero problem, not a characteristic-zero trace-zero problem. Compact Sato--Tate density at trace zero does not answer it.

## Corrected Step 4

Prove cross-prime dispersion for these character-indexed Hasse zeros. This requires a genuinely horizontal independence theorem or a bilinear trace/Mellin estimate. Product Chebotarev for one fixed \(\ell\)-adic representation does not automatically provide it because both the base characteristic and the reduction prime vary.

Thus the explicit sheaf construction is not the missing piece anymore. The missing piece is a **horizontal arithmetic equidistribution theorem for the reductions/unit-root failures of finite-field Mellin transforms**.

# Direct answers

### (A)

The explicit object is

\[
\mathcal L_{p,j}=\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{\omega_p^j}
\]

on the fixed toric complement \(U\), and its compactly supported Frobenius supertrace is a cyclotomic lift reducing to \(b_j\). Equivalently, it is the Mellin transform of \(R\Lambda_!\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\). The Dwork/K3 fiber family enters through this direct image, the Kloosterman-style avatar is a balanced Katz hypergeometric sheaf, and the unit-root crystal detects the mod-\(p\) zero as nonordinarity. None of these gives a natural bounded-rank sheaf on the additive \(j\)-line.

### (B)

The Kummer coefficient sheaf has rank one. The generic hypergeometric \(t\)-sheaf has rank four. Its middle-extension stalk at the relevant point \(t=1\) has rank three, with a Tate line plus a rank-two quotient. The minimal K3 Picard--Fuchs local system also has rank three. Therefore “the desired sheaf should have rank four” is not correct.

### (C)

There is no geometric monodromy group in an additive \(j\)-variable because \(j\) is a character exponent. The K3 Picard--Fuchs monodromy is orthogonal, with connected group \(\mathrm{SO}_3\), not \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\). The generic rank-four hypergeometric \(t\)-sheaf is symplectically self-dual and has a transvection, so \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) is the natural generic candidate, but an exact uniform all-character theorem still requires a Katz classification argument and is not currently proved in the repository. In any case, the Apéry values are obtained at its singular rank-three stalk, and the mod-\(p\) zero is controlled by the rank-two quotient.

### (D)

The exact in-project sources are `oracleC_result.tex`, `lemmaA_result.tex`, and `lemmaBprime_result.tex`. The external foundations are Katz's books on exponential sums, rigid local systems, and finite-field Mellin transforms; Adolphson--Sperber and Fu for GKZ sheaves; Beukers--Peters, Stienstra--Beukers, and Peters for the K3 Picard--Fuchs geometry; and Ahlgren--Ono for special finite-field hypergeometric Apéry congruences.

## Bottom line

The construction exists, but its correct slogan is

\[
\boxed{
\text{Apéry coefficients are reductions of Kummer--Mellin Frobenius traces,
not point traces of a rank-four sheaf on the coefficient-index line.}
}
\]

That correction identifies the true next frontier for \((HM)_3\): horizontal distribution and cross-prime independence of the **\(p\)-adic Hasse-zero characters** of a fixed toric Mellin object.
