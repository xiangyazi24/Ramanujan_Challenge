# Verdict

The estimate

\[
C(p)=\#\{(r,s)\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^2:b_r\equiv b_s\pmod p\}
\le 3p+o(p)
\]

is extremely plausible, and the constant \(3\) is exactly the prediction of the palindromic birthday model. But I do **not** know a rigorous proof from currently available methods. In fact, even the weaker bound

\[
C(p)=O(p)
\]

is still the real open frontier. It is equivalent to an averaged square-root-cancellation, or local-limit, theorem for the defining-characteristic Mellin transforms attached to the Apéry family.

The Cartier-diagonal representation is structurally relevant, because it places all coefficients in one fixed algebraic family. It does **not**, by itself, make the collision predicate \(b_r=b_s\) a bounded-complexity point-counting problem. After coefficient extraction and interpolation, the relevant conductor or degree still grows linearly with \(p\). Standard Weil--Deligne, Katz, or Mellin-equidistribution theorems therefore do not give \(C(p)=O(p)\).

There are three important positive conclusions.

1. The constant \(3\) and the forced/random decomposition can be made exact.
2. If \(C(p)\le 3p+o(p)\), the zero bound is actually a little stronger than the one stated in the question:
   \[
   |Z_p|\le 1+\sqrt{p+2+o(p)}.
   \]
3. The current bordered-certificate theorem in the repository gives a short proof of the partial aggregate bound
   \[
   C(p)\ll p^{5/3}.
   \]
   This is still far from linear and does not improve the already proved \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\), but it identifies precisely where two powers of the lag parameter are being lost.

Thus the proposed \(3p+o(p)\) theorem would be a genuinely new and substantial result.

# 1. The exact constant \(3\)

Let

\[
f_v=\#\{0\le r<p:b_r=v\},
\qquad
C(p)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb F_p}f_v^2.
\]

For odd \(p\), the reflection

\[
\iota(r)=p-1-r
\]

has one fixed point \(c=(p-1)/2\) and \((p-1)/2\) two-cycles. The Apéry reflection identity gives

\[
b_{\iota(r)}=b_r.
\]

Collapse the index set into reflection orbits. Assign weight \(2\) to every two-cycle and weight \(1\) to the central fixed point. If the orbit values are denoted \(y_i\) and their weights by \(w_i\in\{1,2\}\), then

\[
C(p)=\sum_iw_i^2+
     \sum_{i\ne j}w_iw_j\,\mathbf 1_{y_i=y_j}.
\]

The first term is exact:

\[
\sum_iw_i^2
=4\frac{p-1}{2}+1
=2p-1.
\]

This consists of

- \(p\) diagonal ordered pairs;
- \(p-1\) off-diagonal reflection pairs.

Define the nonforced energy

\[
C_{\mathrm{nf}}(p)=C(p)-(2p-1).
\]

If the \((p+1)/2\) orbit values are independent uniform samples from \(\mathbb F_p\), with the displayed weights, then

\[
\mathbb E C_{\mathrm{nf}}(p)
=\frac{(\sum_iw_i)^2-\sum_iw_i^2}{p}
=\frac{p^2-(2p-1)}p
=p-2+\frac1p.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E C(p)=3p-3+\frac1p.}
\]

So \(C(p)/p\to3\) is not merely a heuristic order of magnitude: \(3p-3+1/p\) is the exact mean in the independent palindromic model.

There is no deterministic lower bound near \(3p\). Reflection only forces \(C(p)\ge2p-1\); the orbit values could in principle all be distinct. The extra \(p+O(1)\) is a random-pair-correlation assertion.

# 2. Consequence for the zero fiber

Let

\[
Z=|Z_p|=f_0,
\]

and let \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) indicate whether the central fixed point satisfies \(b_{(p-1)/2}=0\). Write \(Z=2g+\varepsilon\), where \(g\) is the number of two-cycles in the zero fiber.

The forced contribution inside the zero fiber is

\[
4g+\varepsilon=2Z-\varepsilon.
\]

Hence its contribution to the nonforced energy is

\[
Z^2-(2Z-\varepsilon).
\]

If \(C(p)\le3p+o(p)\), then

\[
C_{\mathrm{nf}}(p)\le p+1+o(p),
\]

so

\[
Z^2-2Z+\varepsilon\le p+1+o(p).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
(Z-1)^2\le p+2-\varepsilon+o(p).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{|Z_p|\le1+\sqrt{p+2+o(p)}.}
\]

The bound \(1+\sqrt{2p}\) is valid but leaves unused part of the forced reflection contribution. The natural sharp constant from the collision theorem is \(1\), not \(\sqrt2\), in front of \(\sqrt p\).

For P3.2, however, the constant is secondary: any uniform bound \(C(p)\ll p\) already gives \(Z(p)\ll\sqrt p\).

# 3. Exact Fourier formulation

Let

\[
S_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}e_p(tb_r),
\qquad
 e_p(x)=e^{2\pi i x/p}.
\]

Additive orthogonality gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{C(p)=\frac1p\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}|S_p(t)|^2.}
\]

The zero frequency contributes \(p\), since \(S_p(0)=p\). Thus

\[
C(p)\le3p+o(p)
\]

is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{t\ne0}|S_p(t)|^2\le(2+o(1))p^2.}
\]

This is an averaged square-root-cancellation statement: the average nonzero-frequency square should be \(2p+o(p)\), the factor \(2\) coming from reflection.

It is useful to remove reflection completely. Put

\[
R_p=\{0,1,\ldots,(p-3)/2\},
\qquad m=|R_p|=(p-1)/2,
\]

and

\[
M_v=\#\{r\in R_p:b_r=v\}.
\]

Then

\[
f_v=2M_v+\mathbf1_{b_{(p-1)/2}=v}
\]

and hence

\[
C(p)=4\sum_vM_v^2+4M_{b_{(p-1)/2}}+1.
\]

Since the repository already proves a power-saving bound for the maximum fiber, the central correction is \(o(p)\). Therefore the sharp collision conjecture is essentially equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\sum_vM_v^2=\frac34p+o(p).}
\]

Equivalently, the ordered off-diagonal pair correlation on the half row is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{r,s\in R_p\\r\ne s}}
\left(
 \mathbf1_{b_r=b_s}-\frac1p
\right)=o(p).}
\tag{VCLL}
\]

This is the cleanest formulation of the missing theorem: a **vertical coefficient local-limit law** for one Apéry row.

There is also a projective orbit

\[
\pi(r)=[b_r:c_r]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)
\]

for a companion solution \(c_r\). Its collision energy is also empirically \(3p+o(p)\), and the gap polynomial detects projective returns exactly. This is structurally valuable, but it is a different statistic. Equality \(b_r=b_s\) is equality of one affine coordinate; it does not imply \([b_r:c_r]=[b_s:c_s]\). A point set can have nearly minimal radial/projective energy and still be concentrated on a vertical line. Thus projective energy does not by itself bound \(C(p)\).

# 5. A rigorous partial aggregate bound: \(C(p)\ll p^{5/3}\)

The latest nonvanishing theorem for the bordered certificate yields a short aggregate argument that is worth recording explicitly.

For \(2\le H<p\), define

\[
L_H(r)=\#\{1\le h\le H:r+h\le p-1,\ b_{r+h}=b_r\},
\]

and put

\[
T_H=\sum_rL_H(r),
\qquad
Q_H=\sum_r\binom{L_H(r)}2.
\]

If \(h<k\le H\) both occur in \(L_H(r)\), then

\[
b_r=b_{r+h}=b_{r+k}.
\]

The bordered certificate \(\Delta_{h,k}(r)\) therefore vanishes. The repository proves that \(\Delta_{h,k}\) is a nonzero polynomial modulo \(p\) for every \(1\le h<k<p\), and its degree is at most \(3(k-1)\). Hence

\[
Q_H
\le\sum_{k=2}^H\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}3(k-1)
=3\sum_{j=1}^{H-1}j^2
=\frac{H(H-1)(2H-1)}2
<H^3.
\]

Since

\[
\sum_rL_H(r)^2=T_H+2Q_H,
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
T_H^2\le p(T_H+2Q_H).
\]

Therefore

\[
T_H\le\frac{p+\sqrt{p^2+8pH^3}}2.
\]

In particular, if \(H^3\le p\), then

\[
T_H\le2p.
\]

Now partition \(\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\) into

\[
B=\left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil
\]

consecutive blocks of length at most \(H\). If \(n_{I,v}\) is the multiplicity of \(v\) in a block \(I\), then

\[
\sum_I\sum_vn_{I,v}^2
=p+2\cdot\#\{\text{equal-value pairs lying in one block}\}
\le p+2T_H.
\]

For each \(v\), Cauchy--Schwarz over the \(B\) blocks gives

\[
f_v^2\le B\sum_In_{I,v}^2.
\]

Summing over \(v\), and choosing \(H=\lfloor p^{1/3}\rfloor\), yields

\[
C(p)
\le B(p+2T_H)
\le5p\left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil
\ll p^{5/3}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{C(p)\ll p^{5/3}}
\]

is a direct consequence of the present bordered-certificate theorem. This exponent already appears in the project research ledger, although the main fiber-energy corollary still advertises the simpler \(p^{7/4}\) estimate.

This does **not** improve the zero bound: Cauchy--Schwarz would only give \(Z(p)\ll p^{5/6}\), weaker than the direct gap-polynomial theorem \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\). Its value is diagnostic. The argument loses:

- one factor from summing the degrees \(3(k-1)\) over all lag pairs;
- one factor from patching short intervals into the whole row.

To reach \(C(p)=O(p)\), the aggregate triple-return count must be close to its random scale, not merely bounded by total polynomial degree.

# 6. Correction to the displayed Cartier formula

The Cartier identity verified in the current repository is

\[
\mathscr F(x,y,z,w)=(1-x-y)(1-z-w)-xyzw,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 b_r\equiv[x^ry^rz^rw^r]\mathscr F(x,y,z,w)^{p-1}\pmod p
}
\]

for \(0\le r<p\). See [`new_sections.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/42d8cddded1b3f36c57aed24098642d012f74a3b/problems/3.2/new_sections.tex) and Straub's [multivariate diagonal theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0854).

The expression

\[
xyzw(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)(1+w)
\]

raised to the \((p-1)\)-st power cannot have coefficient \(x^ry^rz^rw^r\) for \(r<p-1\), because every variable already occurs to exponent at least \(p-1\). Thus the formula in the question needs either a Laurent normalization and shifted exponent, or replacement by the verified \(\mathscr F\) above.

# 7. Why Cartier does not presently prove the collision theorem

## 7.1 Coefficients are not evaluations of a fixed trace function

The Cartier formula represents every \(b_r\) as a coefficient of one fixed polynomial power. Collision counting applies the nonlinear predicate

\[
\mathbf1_{[x^r\cdots]\mathscr F^{p-1}=[x^s\cdots]\mathscr F^{p-1}}.
\]

Deligne's theory handles sums of trace functions evaluated at algebraic points. It does not turn equality of two coefficient-extraction functionals into a fixed bounded-conductor sheaf.

To see the complexity growth concretely, let \(P_p(X)\in\mathbb F_p[X]\), \(\deg P_p\le p-1\), interpolate the function

\[
P_p(r)=b_r
\qquad(r\in\mathbb F_p).
\]

Reflection implies

\[
P_p(-1-X)=P_p(X),
\]

so

\[
P_p(X)=Q_p(X(X+1))
\]

for a polynomial \(Q_p\) of degree at most \((p-1)/2\). Then

\[
P_p(X)-P_p(Y)
\]

has the two forced factors

\[
X-Y,
\qquad
X+Y+1.
\]

The residual collision curve has degree \(\Theta(p)\), not bounded degree. Absolute irreducibility alone would give a main term of size \(p\), but the ordinary Weil error is of order roughly

\[
O(\deg^2\sqrt p)=O(p^{5/2}),
\]

which is useless here. One would need a special low-conductor or low-genus structure not supplied by the Cartier identity.

## 7.2 The K3/Mellin reduction reaches the correct object but the wrong topology

The repository's toric/K3 calculation gives, for \(1\le r\le p-2\),

\[
b_r=-\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}\mu_p(a)a^r,
\]

where \(\mu_p(a)\) is the fiber count of a fixed Laurent polynomial. After removing the constant baseline,

\[
b_r=-\sum_{a\ne0}\theta_p(a)a^r,
\]

with \(\theta_p(a)\) a bounded-rank weight-two K3 trace function. See [`toric_mellin_square.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/42d8cddded1b3f36c57aed24098642d012f74a3b/problems/3.2/toric_mellin_square.tex) and [`toric_fiber_k3.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/42d8cddded1b3f36c57aed24098642d012f74a3b/problems/3.2/toric_fiber_k3.tex).

Thus \(b_r\) is a multiplicative Fourier/Mellin coefficient of a fixed geometric trace function. This is the best geometric formulation currently available.

Katz's finite-field Mellin theory, Forey--Fresán--Kowalski's [arithmetic Fourier-transform theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11961), and Bah--Shuddhodan's 2026 [uniform horizontal equidistribution theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00304) control normalized complex or \(\ell\)-adic Frobenius conjugacy classes as the multiplicative character varies. They do not count pairs for which

\[
b_r-b_s\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

That is a reduction at the **defining prime**. Two Mellin sums can be distinct algebraic integers of generic complex size and still differ by a multiple of \(p\). Weak or fixed-resolution complex equidistribution cannot detect this microscopic \(p\)-adic congruence.

Equivalently, after choosing a generator of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\), the Mellin transform is an invertible discrete Fourier transform over \(\mathbb F_p\). For an arbitrary input vector it can realize an arbitrary output vector, so no collision bound follows from Fourier inversion alone. The special K3 geometry must be used through a new residual anti-concentration theorem.

## 7.3 The square-factorization results do not remove the growth

Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin compute the Galois structure of Apéry-like generating series modulo \(p\); see [arXiv:2510.23298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23298). The square or quadratic-times-square phenomena are important structural information about the generating series, but they concern a polynomial in the generating variable. They do not make the coefficient map \(r\mapsto b_r\) a bounded-degree evaluation map. The relevant degree remains \(\Theta(p)\).

# 8. Three precise sufficient theorems

Any one of the following would prove \(C(p)=O(p)\).

## 8.1 Defining-characteristic Mellin local limit

Prove the half-row pair correlation

\[
\sum_{\substack{r,s\in R_p\\r\ne s}}
\left(
\mathbf1_{b_r=b_s}-\frac1p
\right)=O(p),
\]

and prove it is \(o(p)\) for the sharp \(3p+o(p)\) asymptotic.

This is the most direct statement, but it is genuinely \(p\)-adic/crystalline rather than ordinary Sato--Tate equidistribution.

## 8.2 Additive Weyl-energy theorem

Prove

\[
\sum_{t\ne0}
\left|
 \sum_{r\in R_p}e_p(tb_r)
\right|^2
\ll p^2.
\]

The sharp constant is

\[
\sum_{t\ne0}
\left|
 \sum_{r\in R_p}e_p(tb_r)
\right|^2
=\frac{(p-1)^2}{2}+o(p^2).
\]

A pointwise bound \(\ll\sqrt p\) for every nonzero \(t\) would be more than enough, but no current trace-function realization of \(r\mapsto e_p(tb_r)\) has bounded conductor.

## 8.3 Aggregate bordered-return energy

Let

\[
\mathcal T_p
=\#\{(r,s,t):r<s<t<p,\ b_r=b_s=b_t\}.
\]

If

\[
\mathcal T_p=O(p),
\]

then \(C(p)=O(p)\). Indeed, fibers of size \(1\) or \(2\) contribute only \(O(p)\) in total, while for \(f\ge3\), \(f^2\ll1+\binom f3\).

The bordered polynomials \(\Delta_{h,k}\) encode these triples. The current argument sums their individual degrees and pays \(O(p^3)\) at full lag range. A theorem showing that their roots overlap or cancel so that the total root mass is \(O(p)\) would close the collision problem. This is an arithmetic family-compatibility or gcd-energy theorem, far stronger than mere nonvanishing of each \(\Delta_{h,k}\).

# 9. Most realistic research program

I would separate the goals as follows.

1. **First target: \(C(p)\ll p\), not the constant \(3\).** This is already enough for \(Z(p)\ll\sqrt p\).
2. **Work on the half row.** Reflection should be quotiented out at the start; otherwise the forced \(p-1\) pairs obscure the genuinely random statistic.
3. **Use the K3/Mellin identity as the conceptual object**, but formulate the missing input explicitly as a defining-prime residual pair-correlation theorem.
4. **In parallel, attack the bordered family globally.** The concrete target is an \(O(p)\) bound for total triple-return mass, or an average gcd/resultant theorem for the \(\Delta_{h,k}\).
5. **Try an average-over-primes theorem first.** A statement such as
   \[
   \sum_{p\le X}C(p)
   =(3+o(1))\sum_{p\le X}p
   \]
   may be more accessible than the pointwise theorem, although the moving lag range still prevents a direct fixed-polynomial Chebotarev argument.
6. **Audit the residual collision curve computationally.** Interpolate \(P_p\), divide \(P_p(X)-P_p(Y)\) by the two forced factors, and record irreducible components and geometric genera. A bounded-genus residual component would revive the Cartier route; linear degree with growing genus would confirm the present conductor barrier.

# 10. Final answer

- The prediction \(C(p)=3p+o(p)\) is mathematically coherent and its constant is exactly explained by reflection plus birthday collisions.
- It is **not currently proved**. Even \(C(p)=O(p)\) remains open.
- The Cartier-diagonal theorem does not directly imply it, because collision of coefficient extractions is a nonlinear, growing-conductor condition.
- The current repository machinery rigorously reaches \(C(p)\ll p^{5/3}\) by combining bordered-certificate nonvanishing with short-interval energy, but this is still far from the required linear scale.
- The missing result should be advertised as an **Apéry defining-characteristic Mellin local-limit theorem**, or equivalently a sharp pair-correlation theorem for the half-row values.
- If proved, it would immediately improve the zero bound to
  \[
  |Z_p|\le1+\sqrt{p+o(p)},
  \]
  and would be a genuinely new theorem beyond the existing Cartier, K3, and Mellin-equidistribution literature.
