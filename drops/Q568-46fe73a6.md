# Q568 — Direct CRT triple-product Parseval: can it bypass M1+AKD₃?

## Summary

The triple-product CRT formulation is a useful reformulation, but it does **not** remove the fundamental difficulty. It packages M1+M2+AKD₃ into one higher-dimensional Fourier statement. Parseval gives a strong averaged bound, but the missing step is again control of the fixed Apéry Fourier phases.

The route is promising because the third moment naturally has a triple Fourier expansion, but the required saving is not automatic from CRT near-injection alone.

## (A) Signed CRT errors

For a fixed triple `(p,q,r)` one has

\[
E_{pqr}=\sum_{(z_p,z_q,z_r)}\left(\#\{m\le X^2:m\equiv CRT(z_p,z_q,z_r)\}-\frac{X^2}{pqr}\right).
\]

The O(1) error per residue class is a worst-case bound. Summing absolute values gives the useless estimate

\[
(\sum_p |Z_p|)^3\approx \pi(X)^3.
\]

There is no general reason for these interval-discrepancy signs to cancel. The CRT residues are highly structured, not random independent points. A cancellation theorem would itself be a new AKD-type theorem.

Therefore signed cancellation is possible, but cannot be assumed as a free consequence of the CRT map.

## (B) Triple exponential expansion

The Fourier identity is the correct global viewpoint:

\[
E_{pqr}=\sum_{h\ne0\pmod{pqr}}
G_X(h;pqr)\,
\widehat Z_p(h)\widehat Z_q(h)\widehat Z_r(h)/(pqr),
\]

where

\[
G_X(h;pqr)=\sum_{m\le X^2}e(hm/(pqr)).
\]

The CRT factorization

\[
\sum_z e(h\,CRT(z)/(pqr))
=\widehat Z_p(h)\widehat Z_q(h)\widehat Z_r(h)
\]

is exactly the triple analogue of the AKD₃ expansion.

However, the hard part remains: the three Fourier transforms are evaluated at the same frequency h. This is a fixed-phase correlation problem.

## (C) Parseval over h

For a single triple, Parseval gives

\[
\sum_h |\widehat Z_p(h)|^2|\widehat Z_q(h)|^2|\widehat Z_r(h)|^2.
\]

If one averages over all phases independently, this collapses cleanly and gives the Q554-type RMS bound. But the Apéry sets are fixed sets. Parseval only controls the L2 mass:

\[
\sum_h |\widehat Z_p(h)|^2=p|Z_p|,
\]

not the triple aligned product at identical h.

A generic bound gives losses comparable to replacing L1 by L2, which is exactly the gap between averaged AKD₃ and pointwise AKD₃.

## (D) Relation to M1+AKD₃

The triple Parseval method is not genuinely independent. Expanding the triple Fourier expression and grouping two primes together gives the same pair correlation terms:

\[
\widehat Z_p(h)\widehat Z_q(h)
\]

that appear in AKD₃. The M1+M2+AKD₃ decomposition is essentially the second-order marginalization of this full triple Fourier identity.

The direct method may give cleaner notation, but it does not avoid the nonlinear zero-set Fourier problem.

## Role of Q554 near-injection

The near-injection of

\[
(z_p,z_q,z_r)\mapsto CRT(z_p,z_q,z_r)
\]

is valuable. It proves that collisions are rare and gives excellent RMS/phase-averaged estimates. But near-injection is a counting statement; it does not imply cancellation of a fixed exponential phase.

The missing ingredient is one of:

1. Fourier pseudorandomness of the Apéry zero sets;
2. a spectral/automorphic estimate controlling the fixed Fourier coefficients;
3. a dispersion estimate converting the fixed phases into averaged phases.

## Conclusion

The direct CRT triple-product route is a good reformulation, but not a bypass:

- (A) signed error cancellation is the desired theorem, not an automatic fact;
- (B) the triple Fourier expansion is correct;
- (C) Parseval proves the phase-averaged version but not fixed Apéry phases;
- (D) the method collapses back to AKD₃ after marginalization.

The minimal missing theorem remains a fixed-phase Fourier pseudorandomness statement for Apéry zero sets. Q554's near-injection provides the geometric half; AKD₃ is the arithmetic cancellation half.
