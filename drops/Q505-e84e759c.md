# Q505: Factorial moment formulation of (HM)_3

## Executive summary

The corrected formulation is important: \((HM)_3\) is **not** a pointwise bound on the number of primes hitting a single integer. It is a third factorial moment estimate.

Let

\[
K_X(m)=\#\{p\in(X,2X]:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\]

and

\[
F_3(X)=\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_3.
\]

The conjectured Poisson/random model gives

\[
F_3(X)\sim X^2\lambda_X^3,
\qquad
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in(X,2X]}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

The meaning of \((HM)_3\) is therefore:

> triple prime coincidences occur with the same frequency as independent random residues.

It is an averaged independence statement.

---

# (A) Relation between \((HM)_3\) and the bound on \(D(n)\)

Recall the P3.2 target:

\[
D(n)=\#\{p\in(\sqrt n,n]:p\mid b_n\}.
\]

Using the Lucas property,

\[
p\mid b_n
\Longleftrightarrow
\exists r\in Z_p:\ n\equiv r\pmod p.
\]

Thus \(D(n)\) is exactly the number of primes hitting the residue class of \(n\):

\[
D(n)=K_X(n)
\]

with an appropriate dyadic decomposition of primes.

The third factorial moment controls the tail. For any integer \(T\ge3\),

\[
\mathbf 1_{K\ge T}
\leq \frac{(K)_3}{(T)_3}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\#\{m<X^2:K_X(m)\ge T\}
\leq
\frac{F_3(X)}{(T)_3}.
\]

If

\[
F_3(X)\leq(1+o(1))X^2\lambda_X^3,
\]

then

\[
\#\{m:K_X(m)\ge T\}
\ll
\frac{X^2\lambda_X^3}{T^3}.
\]

Since

\[
\lambda_X\approx \sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}
\approx O(1),
\]

choosing a slowly growing threshold gives that almost all integers have boundedly many large prime divisors.

To prove the all-\(n\) statement

\[
D(n)\le \frac{n}{3\log n}+o(n/\log n),
\]

one combines the factorial-moment bound with the standard splitting argument:

1. small primes are handled by smooth-number estimates;
2. large primes are controlled by the moment estimate;
3. exceptional integers with many hits are sparse.

Thus \((HM)_3\) is a key input, but not literally equivalent to the final bound. It is the triple-collision estimate needed to eliminate the high multiplicity tail.

# (B) Is \((HM)_3\) equivalent to Poisson distribution?

No. It is a necessary low-order consequence of Poisson behavior, but not equivalent.

For a Poisson random variable with parameter \(\lambda_X\),

\[
\mathbb E(K)_3=\lambda_X^3.
\]

Hence the prediction

\[
F_3(X)=X^2\lambda_X^3
\]

is exactly the third factorial moment identity.

However, many different distributions share the same first three factorial moments. A third moment estimate alone does not prove

\[
K_X(m)\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_X).
\]

Full Poisson convergence would require

\[
\frac1{X^2}\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_j
\to \lambda_X^j
\]

for every fixed \(j\).

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{(HM)_3\text{ is the }j=3\text{ Poisson moment, not Poisson convergence.}}
\]

The computational observation

\[
\max K_X(m)=O(1)
\]

is much stronger than what the third moment alone gives.

# (C) Relation with Selberg--Delange

The analogy is very close.

In the classical Selberg--Delange setting one studies

\[
\omega(n)=\sum_p 1_{p\mid n}.
\]

The factorial moments satisfy

\[
\sum_{n\le N}(\omega(n))_k
\]

and asymptotically match the Poisson model:

\[
\sim N(\log\log N)^k.
\]

The proof works because divisibility conditions at different primes are approximately independent:

\[
\Pr(p_1\cdots p_k\mid n)
\approx
\frac1{p_1\cdots p_k}.
\]

The Apéry problem has exactly the same formal structure:

ordinary integers:

\[
p\mid n
\]

versus

Apéry:

\[
n\bmod p\in Z_p.
\]

The difficulty is that the local sets \(Z_p\) are not fixed congruence classes. They are moving Frobenius-defined sets.

Thus the missing theorem is an analogue of Selberg--Delange for **moving residue sets**:

\[
\{Z_p\}_{p}
\]

rather than ordinary divisibility.

# (D) Large sieve and third moments

The ordinary large sieve gives a second moment:

\[
\sum_m\left|\sum_p f_p(m)\right|^2
\leq
(X^2+P^2)\sum_p\|f_p\|_2^2.
\]

For the third factorial moment we expand:

\[
F_3(X)=
\sum_{p_1<p_2<p_3}
\sum_{r_i\in Z_{p_i}}
\#\{m<X^2:m\equiv r_i\pmod {p_i}\}.
\]

The inner count is a CRT counting problem:

\[
\#\{m<X^2:m\equiv r_i\pmod {p_i}\}
=
\frac{X^2}{p_1p_2p_3}+\text{error}.
\]

The main term is exactly

\[
X^2\lambda_X^3.
\]

The problem is controlling the accumulated CRT error.

There is no direct universal "third moment large sieve" analogous to the quadratic inequality. The available higher-moment tools are:

## 1. Large sieve for products

One can apply the large sieve after expanding pairs, but the loss is typically

\[
X^2+P^2
\]

at each stage and becomes too large near \(P\sim X\).

## 2. Higher correlation estimates

The desired estimate is closer to a triple correlation bound:

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}
\sum_{a\in Z_p,b\in Z_q,c\in Z_r}
1_{CRT(a,b,c)<X^2}
\]

with cancellation in the off-diagonal terms.

This is a problem of the type treated by:

- Gallagher large sieve methods;
- Linnik dispersion;
- bilinear trace-function estimates.

But the Apéry sets are moving targets, so the standard fixed-modulus large sieve does not immediately apply.

# The correct hierarchy

The logical implications are:

\[
\text{Poisson convergence}
\Longrightarrow
(HM)_3
\Longrightarrow
\text{control of triple collisions}
\Longrightarrow
\text{tail bounds for }D(n).
\]

The reverse implications fail.

The most realistic analytic route is therefore not to prove a full Poisson theorem, but directly prove the third factorial moment estimate by showing that the CRT triple correlations of the Frobenius zero sets behave randomly.

This matches the current computational picture:

- \(|Z_p|\) is already tiny;
- \(\lambda_X\) is bounded;
- the obstruction is not local density;
- the obstruction is the absence of a moving-target Selberg--Delange/large-sieve theorem for the family \(Z_p\).
