# Referee verdict

The proposed growth-plus-supercongruence route does **not** prove R2. The main reason is structural:

> R2 is a statement about the **dyadic radical** of one Apéry number—how many distinct primes in `(P,2P]` divide it—whereas the Apéry supercongruences control **multiplicity at one fixed prime along a `p`-power index tower**.

Those are almost orthogonal kinds of information. In fact, the standard supercongruence usually **freezes** a valuation of `1` or `2`; it does not amplify it.

There is also an important normalization issue. The literal quantity in the question,

$$
C_P(n):=\#\{P<p\le 2P:p\mid b_n\},
\tag{0.1}
$$

is not the same as the low-digit AMTD variable used in the current project,

$$
K_P(n):=\#\{P<p\le 2P:n\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\}.
\tag{0.2}
$$

For `n<p^2`, Gessel's Lucas congruence gives

$$
\boxed{
 p\mid b_n
 \iff
 \lfloor n/p\rfloor\in Z_p
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 n\bmod p\in Z_p.
}
\tag{0.3}
$$

Thus literal divisibility has a **high-digit branch** in addition to the low-digit branch. The CRT second-moment estimate in the project applies directly to (0.2), not to (0.1).

The answers to the five questions are:

1. **First-row valuation:** no theorem is known that every first-row zero is simple, and it is false literally: `v_11(b_5)=1`, whereas `b_3=1445=5·17^2`, so `v_17(b_3)=2`. The random `p`-adic heuristic predicts that, conditional on `p|b_r`, valuation `1` occurs with probability `1-O(1/p)`, but this is not proved uniformly over the moving first row.
2. **Using higher multiplicity in the height bound:** an average valuation of `2` only improves the constant by a factor of two. To obtain `N^{o(1)}` distinct primes from size alone would require the average selected valuation to grow roughly like `N^{1-o(1)}/log N`, far beyond every known or expected supercongruence.
3. **CFVZ:** the factorization is modulo `p` in the evaluation variable. It neither supplies a canonical lift modulo `p^2` nor forces a zero coefficient to be divisible by `p^2`.
4. **Dickman/smoothness:** random-integer heuristics strongly support R2, but smooth-number theorems do not transfer to this exponentially sparse recurrence sequence and do not control concentration inside one dyadic prime block.
5. **Average R2:** for the intended low-digit variable, the CRT calculation is valid and is essentially already the project's proved `(HM)_2` estimate. At the natural range `n<P^2`, the desired average bound follows from the much weaker first-moment input
   $$
   \sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p\le P^{1+o(1)}.
   $$
   This remains open. For literal `p|b_n`, the proposed estimate `#{n:pq|b_n}\le N/(pq)+\cdots` is not justified because of the two moving base-`p` digit branches.

# 1. Growth controls weighted multiplicity, not the dyadic radical

The dominant characteristic root of the Apéry recurrence is

$$
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^4=33.970562\ldots,
$$

and

$$
\log b_n=n\log\alpha+O(\log n),
\qquad
\log\alpha=3.525494\ldots.
\tag{1.1}
$$

Let

$$
S_P(n)=\{p:P<p\le2P,\ p\mid b_n\},
\qquad C_P(n)=|S_P(n)|,
$$

and, when `C_P(n)>0`, define the average selected valuation

$$
\bar e_P(n)
=\frac1{C_P(n)}\sum_{p\in S_P(n)}v_p(b_n).
$$

Then

$$
\sum_{p\in S_P(n)}v_p(b_n)\log p
\le\log b_n,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{
 C_P(n)
 \le
 \frac{(3.526+o(1))n}
      {\bar e_P(n)\log P}.
}
\tag{1.2}
$$

This is the complete information obtainable from size after multiplicities are included.

If every selected prime divided to order at least two, then (1.2) would become

$$
C_P(n)\le\frac{(3.526+o(1))n}{2\log P}.
$$

That is only a constant-factor improvement. Suppose, for orientation, that `n≈N` and `P=N^θ` with fixed `θ>0`. To force

$$
C_P(n)=N^{o(1)}
$$

from (1.2), one would need

$$
\bar e_P(n)
\ge \frac{N^{1-o(1)}}{\log N}.
\tag{1.3}
$$

No Apéry supercongruence predicts anything remotely of this size. Typical divisibility should instead have valuation one.

Equivalently, R2 is a bound on

$$
\operatorname{rad}_{(P,2P]}(b_n)
:=\prod_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\p\mid b_n}}p.
$$

Since every selected prime lies between `P` and `2P`,

$$
C_P(n)\log P
\le
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(P,2P]}(b_n)
\le
C_P(n)\log(2P).
\tag{1.4}
$$

Thus R2 is, up to a bounded factor, precisely

$$
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(P,2P]}(b_n)
\le N^{o(1)}\log P.
\tag{1.5}
$$

The radical deliberately forgets all higher valuations. This is why multiplicity theorems are poorly matched to R2.

Even averaging the height inequality over `n` gives only

$$
\sum_{n\le N}\sum_{P<p\le2P}v_p(b_n)\log p
\le\sum_{n\le N}\log b_n
=O(N^2),
\tag{1.6}
$$

hence at best `O(N^2/log P)` unweighted incidences. This misses an `N^{1+o(1)}` target by essentially a full factor of `N` in the range `P=N^{Θ(1)}`.

# 2. First-row valuations: what is actually known?

## 2.1 There is no uniform simplicity theorem

For `0≤r<p`, the recurrence is nonsingular modulo `p`, and the project proves that two consecutive coefficients cannot both vanish modulo `p`. That is a statement about the **location** of mod-`p` zeros. It does not control whether a single zero lifts modulo `p^2`.

Both possibilities already occur in very small exact examples:

$$
b_5=819005=11\cdot74455,
\qquad v_{11}(b_5)=1,
\tag{2.1}
$$

whereas

$$
b_3=1445=5\cdot17^2,
\qquad v_{17}(b_3)=2.
\tag{2.2}
$$

Thus the statement

$$
p\mid b_r,\ r<p \Longrightarrow p\parallel b_r
$$

is false.

The natural random `p`-adic heuristic says that, after conditioning on one congruence `b_r≡0 mod p`, the next digit should be random. It therefore predicts

$$
\Pr(v_p(b_r)=1\mid p\mid b_r)\approx1-\frac1p,
$$

and

$$
\Pr(v_p(b_r)\ge2\mid p\mid b_r)\approx\frac1p.
\tag{2.3}
$$

This is compatible with higher-order zeros being exceptional, but it is not a theorem in the simultaneous moving range `r<p`, `p→∞`.

## 2.2 The center is a special one-index exception

For the central index, Ahlgren--Ono give the stronger congruence

$$
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod{p^2},
\qquad
f_8=\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4.
\tag{2.4}
$$

Consequently, if `p|a_p(f_8)` but `a_p(f_8)≠0`, then Deligne's bound

$$
|a_p(f_8)|\le2p^{3/2}<p^2\qquad(p\ge5)
$$

forces

$$
v_p\bigl(b_{(p-1)/2}\bigr)=1.
\tag{2.5}
$$

A central valuation at least two can occur only in the exceptional case `a_p(f_8)=0`. This is a useful exact statement, but it concerns one index per prime and supplies no information about the remaining `p-1` first-row positions.

# 3. What the Apéry supercongruence really does to valuations

For primes `p≥5`, the Coster--Beukers supercongruence, in the form emphasized by Straub, is

$$
\boxed{
 b_{mp^k}\equiv b_{mp^{k-1}}\pmod{p^{3k}}
}
\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{3.1}
$$

Set

$$
A_k=b_{mp^k}.
$$

Then

$$
A_k=A_{k-1}+p^{3k}u_k
$$

for some integer `u_k`. Therefore

$$
\boxed{
 v_p(A_{k-1})<3k
 \Longrightarrow
 v_p(A_k)=v_p(A_{k-1}).
}
\tag{3.2}
$$

Indeed, the two summands have different valuations, so the non-Archimedean triangle law gives the smaller one exactly.

This has the following concrete consequences.

- If `p∤b_m`, then every `b_{mp^k}` remains a `p`-adic unit.
- If `v_p(b_m)=1`, then
  $$
  v_p(b_{mp^k})=1
  $$
  for every `k≥0`.
- If `v_p(b_m)=2`, then
  $$
  v_p(b_{mp^k})=2
  $$
  for every `k≥0`.

Thus (3.1) generally **preserves** a small valuation rather than increasing it. For instance, (2.1) and (2.2) imply

$$
v_{11}(b_{5\cdot11^k})=1,
\qquad
v_{17}(b_{3\cdot17^k})=2
\qquad(k\ge0).
\tag{3.3}
$$

Moreover, (3.1) says nothing directly about a first-row index `0<r<p`; such an index is not obtained by multiplying a smaller positive integer by `p`.

There are broader digit-valuation results. Delaygue proves Beukers' valuation conjectures and, for the classical Apéry sequence, obtains a lower bound in terms of the number of base-`p` digits lying in `Z_p`. Schematically,

$$
v_p(b_n)\ge
\#\{\text{base-`p` digits of `n` that belong to }Z_p\}.
\tag{3.4}
$$

This is important for one fixed prime, but it still does not help R2:

1. in the AMTD range `p>\sqrt N`, an index `n≤N` has at most two base-`p` digits, so (3.4) gives at most a bounded multiplicity;
2. the digit decomposition changes with `p`, so it supplies no constraint on how many different primes can occur;
3. a bounded multiplicity only changes the constant in (1.2).

The modulo-`p^2` Lucas congruences of Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler are likewise restricted to indices whose digits lie in a specified admissible set. They are not a theorem that every mod-`p` zero acquires another factor of `p`.

# 4. Why the CFVZ square does not force square divisibility of coefficients

CFVZ prove in `F_p[t]` that

$$
A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r
      =\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
\qquad
\Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1.
\tag{4.1}
$$

This is an identity after reduction modulo `p`. It is powerful for evaluation roots of the truncated polynomial, but the event relevant here is

$$
[t^r]A_p(t)=b_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{4.2}
$$

Three obstructions prevent a valuation conclusion.

## 4.1 There is no canonical lift modulo `p^2`

The polynomial `B_p` in (4.1) is defined over `F_p`. Different integral lifts differ by multiples of `p`, and their squares differ modulo `p^2`. Thus (4.1) does not determine the next `p`-adic digit of any coefficient.

## 4.2 A square coefficient can have valuation exactly one

Even an exact square over the integers would not suffice. For any odd prime `p`, put

$$
B(t)=1+t+\frac{p-1}{2}t^2\in\mathbb Z[t].
$$

Then

$$
[t^2]B(t)^2
=1+2\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}
=p,
\tag{4.3}
$$

which is divisible by `p` but not by `p^2`.

The reason is that coefficient extraction from a square is a convolution. A mod-`p` zero is cancellation among convolution terms, not double vanishing of one factor.

## 4.3 Evaluation roots and coefficient valuations remain dual notions

The full multiset of roots determines the coefficients through Newton--Vieta, but for a typical coefficient the required elementary symmetric function has order `Θ(p)`. That exact information-theoretic bridge does not turn a root multiplicity statement into a coefficient valuation statement.

Hence CFVZ does not improve (1.2), either pointwise or on average.

# 5. Smooth numbers and the correct random model

Let `x=b_n≈exp(cn)` with `c=log α`. If one sets `y=n^β`, then

$$
u=rac{\log x}{\log y}
\asymp\frac{n}{\log n}.
\tag{5.1}
$$

Dickman's function predicts that a random integer of size `x` is `y`-smooth with astronomically small probability. Thus the random model strongly predicts that `b_n` has a prime factor much larger than any fixed power of `n`.

But this is not the question in R2. An integer can have one enormous prime factor and simultaneously have many prime factors in `(P,2P]`. Smoothness controls the **largest** prime factor or the absence of primes above `y`; it does not control clustering in one intermediate dyadic interval.

The more relevant Kubilius-style model treats the events `p|b_n` as Bernoulli variables of mean `1/p`. It predicts

$$
\mathbb E C_P(n)
\approx\sum_{P<p\le2P}\frac1p
\sim\frac{\log2}{\log P}.
\tag{5.2}
$$

For `N` independent samples, standard extreme-value heuristics would put the maximum at most on a polylogarithmic scale, hence certainly at `N^{o(1)}`. This explains the numerical observations.

It does not constitute a proof for the sparse deterministic sequence `(b_n)`. Classical smooth-number theorems concern all or almost all integers in intervals. The Apéry numbers form an exponentially sparse holonomic sequence, so those density theorems cannot simply be restricted to it.

The closest published arithmetic result is due to Luca--Shparlinski. For a density-one set of indices they prove lower bounds for the total number of distinct prime factors and for the largest prime factor of `b_n`. Those results point in the opposite direction—they show that prime factors exist—and give no upper bound on the number lying in one prescribed dyadic block.

# 6. The averaged square: what the CRT argument actually proves

## 6.1 The low-digit AMTD variable

Let

$$
\mathcal P_P=\{p:P<p\le2P\},
\qquad
Z_1(P)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_P}z_p,
$$

and, for `0≤m<M`, define

$$
K_P(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_P}
\mathbf1_{\{m\bmod p\in Z_p\}}.
\tag{6.1}
$$

Put

$$
\Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p\}.
$$

For one prime,

$$
|\Omega_p|
\le z_p\left(\frac Mp+1\right).
\tag{6.2}
$$

For distinct primes `p,q`, every pair `(r,s)∈Z_p×Z_q` determines one residue class modulo `pq`, so

$$
|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|
\le z_pz_q\left(\frac M{pq}+1\right).
\tag{6.3}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{m<M}K_P(m)^2
\le
\left(\frac MP+1\right)Z_1(P)
+
\left(\frac M{P^2}+1\right)Z_1(P)^2.
}
\tag{6.4}
$$

Constants have been suppressed only through the inequalities `p>P`.

At the natural AMTD scale `M=P^2`, this becomes

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{m<P^2}K_P(m)^2
\ll PZ_1(P)+Z_1(P)^2.
}
\tag{6.5}
$$

Equivalently, with

$$
\lambda_P=\sum_{P<p\le2P}\frac{z_p}{p}
\asymp\frac{Z_1(P)}P,
$$

one has

$$
\sum_{m<P^2}K_P(m)^2
\ll P^2(\lambda_P+\lambda_P^2).
\tag{6.6}
$$

This is exactly the diagonal term plus the project's proved factorial second-moment estimate. In the notation of `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex`,

$$
\sum_{m<P^2}(K_P(m))_2
\le4P^2\lambda_P^2.
\tag{6.7}
$$

Thus the CRT stage is already complete.

The target

$$
\sum_{m<P^2}K_P(m)^2\le P^{2+o(1)}
\tag{6.8}
$$

follows from

$$
\boxed{
Z_1(P)\le P^{1+o(1)},
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
\lambda_P\le P^{o(1)}.
}
\tag{6.9}
$$

Conversely, the diagonal contribution satisfies

$$
\sum_{m<P^2}K_P(m)\gg PZ_1(P),
$$

so (6.8) itself forces (6.9), up to `P^{o(1)}`. Therefore the averaged low-digit R2 is essentially **equivalent to a weak first-moment zero-count bound**.

The stronger R1 hypothesis

$$
Z_1(P)\ll\frac P{\log P}
\tag{6.10}
$$

would give

$$
\sum_{m<P^2}K_P(m)^2
\ll\frac{P^2}{\log P}.
\tag{6.11}
$$

The current per-prime theorem `z_p≪P^{2/3}` gives only

$$
Z_1(P)\ll\frac{P^{5/3}}{\log P},
$$

and hence, from (6.5), approximately

$$
\sum_{m<P^2}K_P(m)^2
\ll P^{10/3+o(1)}
=(P^2)^{5/3+o(1)},
\tag{6.12}
$$

which is far above (6.8).

So the average version is a meaningful weakening of R1, but it is not obtained from growth or supercongruences. Its missing input is still average control of `z_p`.

## 6.2 Literal divisibility is not a one-residue-class CRT problem

For the literal indicator `1_{p|b_n}`, Gessel's congruence gives, whenever `0≤n<p^2`,

$$
n=ap+r,\qquad
b_n\equiv b_ab_r\pmod p.
\tag{6.13}
$$

Hence (0.3). Over the complete two-digit box `0≤n<p^2`, the exact number of divisible terms is

$$
\boxed{
\#\{0\le n<p^2:p\mid b_n\}
=p^2-(p-z_p)^2
=2pz_p-z_p^2.
}
\tag{6.14}
$$

The low-digit zeros form residue classes modulo `p`, but the high-digit zeros form intervals

$$
\bigcup_{a\in Z_p}[ap,(a+1)p).
\tag{6.15}
$$

For two different primes, these interval families can align at a common integer. Therefore there is no deterministic estimate of the form

$$
\#\{n\le N:pq\mid b_n\}
\le\frac N{pq}+O(1)
\tag{6.16}
$$

without additional information. Even for the low-low branch, the correct CRT upper bound contains the factor `z_pz_q`:

$$
\#\{n<N:n\bmod p\in Z_p,\ n\bmod q\in Z_q\}
\le z_pz_q\left(\frac N{pq}+1\right).
\tag{6.17}
$$

Mixed and high-high branches encode the same moving-anchor phenomenon that AMTD is designed to control. Treating them as independent residue classes would assume the desired conclusion.

Thus:

- if the intended variable is the project's low-digit `K_P`, use (6.4)--(6.7);
- if the intended variable is literal `p|b_n`, a separate high-digit analysis is required, and the proposed pair count is not presently proved.

# 7. What would actually constitute progress on R2?

For the pointwise maximum, the clean target is a dyadic-radical theorem:

$$
\boxed{
\sup_{n\le N}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(P,2P]}(b_n)
\le N^{o(1)}\log P.
}
\tag{7.1}
$$

None of the following is enough:

- a bounded lower bound on `v_p(b_n)`;
- the `p^3` supercongruence along `mp^k`;
- the mod-`p` CFVZ square;
- a theorem that `b_n` has one very large prime factor;
- the individual zero-count bound `z_p≪p^{2/3}`.

A successful theorem would need genuinely cross-prime information, for example one of:

1. a large-prime-radical upper bound for the fixed integers `b_n`;
2. a selected-character, defining-characteristic Mellin anti-concentration theorem uniform in `p` and `n`;
3. a pointwise anti-tail theorem for the moving zero sets `Z_p`;
4. for the averaged target only, the weak first moment `Z_1(P)≤P^{1+o(1)}`.

The strongest honest conclusion from the present growth and congruence inputs is therefore:

> **Supercongruences can explain how an already existing `p`-divisibility propagates through base-`p` digits and `p`-power index dilation. They do not make dyadic prime support sparse. The averaged low-digit second moment reduces cleanly to a weak average bound for `z_p`; the maximum R2 remains a separate radical/anti-tail problem.**
