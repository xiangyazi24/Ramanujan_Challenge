# Q116 — Supercongruences and the lower quotient ranges

## Summary

The top-window computation is essentially correct: every top-window hit contributes exactly a bounded local factor (in the generic case exactly \(p^3\)) to the denominator-cleared gcd. Therefore the whole conjecture reduces to controlling the number of top-window hits.

For lower quotient ranges the situation is different. The same mechanism shows that a prime hit contributes at most a bounded \(p\)-adic amount, but the contribution is not automatically zero. The obstruction is again the distribution of Apéry zeros, now for several base-\(p\) digits.

---

## 1. General Lucas decomposition

Write

\[
n=q p+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

The Lucas-type congruence gives

\[
b_n\equiv b_q b_r\pmod p.
\]

Hence

\[
p\mid b_n
\]

occurs when either

\[
p\mid b_q
\]

or

\[
r\in Z_p.
\]

For the second quotient range,

\[
\frac n3<p\le \frac n2,
\]

we have \(q=2\), so

\[
n=2p+s,
\qquad s=n-2p.
\]

Therefore

\[
b_n\equiv b_2 b_s\pmod p.
\]

Since

\[
b_2=73,
\]

there are two cases.

---

## 2. Generic second-window primes

If

\[
p\nmid 73,
\]

then

\[
p\mid b_n \iff p\mid b_s.
\]

Thus the hit condition is again a zero-set condition, but at the smaller index

\[
s=n-2p < p.
\]

The supercongruence/block law does not directly say

\[
b_{2p+s}\equiv b_2b_s\pmod {p^3},
\]

with the same strength as the \(q=1\) top window unless one has the corresponding higher block congruence for \(q=2\). The known block law gives the first-order structure, but the \(p^3\) valuation requires additional input about the \(q=2\) block.

Therefore the statement

\[
v_p(b_n)=v_p(b_s)
\]

is not automatic in this range.

---

## 3. What can be said about \(v_p(G_n)\)

Recall

\[
v_p(G_n)=\min(v_p(d_na_n),v_p(d_nb_n)).
\]

For primes

\[
p\in(n/3,n/2],
\]

we still have

\[
v_p(d_n)=3
\]

because \(p\le n\) but \(p^2>n\), so \(p\) appears once in the lcm.

The Casoratian implies that away from the exceptional primes dividing \(6n\),

\[
p\mid b_n\implies p\nmid a_n.
\]

Hence

\[
v_p(d_na_n)=3.
\]

Consequently,

\[
v_p(G_n)=\min(3,3+v_p(b_n))=3
\]

whenever \(p\mid b_n\) and the Casoratian applies.

So the second window does **not** disappear: a hit there also normally contributes a factor \(p^3\).

---

## 4. The exceptional prime \(p=73\)

The only new phenomenon is the factor

\[
b_2=73.
\]

If \(p=73\), then

\[
b_n\equiv0\pmod {73}
\]

can occur because of the quotient digit \(q=2\), independently of the final digit.

This is a finite exceptional phenomenon and does not affect asymptotic estimates.

---

## 5. Higher quotient ranges

The same pattern continues. If

\[
n=qp+r,
\]

then

\[
b_n\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
\]

For fixed small \(q\), the possible sources of divisibility are:

1. a zero of \(b_q\), which is a finite exceptional condition;
2. a zero of \(b_r\), giving a moving zero-set problem.

The denominator exponent remains bounded because \(p^2>n\) in all ranges with \(p>\sqrt n\).

Thus the large-prime part of \(G_n\) is controlled by counting hits, not by large \(p\)-adic valuations.

---

## 6. Consequence for the conjecture

The supercongruence analysis confirms the following reduction:

\[
\log G_n^{(p>\sqrt n)}
\asymp
3\sum_{p>\sqrt n,\ p\mid b_n}\log p.
\]

Therefore proving

\[
\omega_{p>\sqrt n}(b_n)=o(n/\log n)
\]

is essentially the remaining task.

The top window is the sharpest because each prime corresponds to one digit

\[
r=n-p.
\]

The second window corresponds to

\[
r=n-2p,
\]

and higher windows correspond to more complicated base-\(p\) digit constraints. They do not vanish, but they have the same qualitative structure.

So the conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Supercongruences bound the local }p\text{-adic contribution; they do not remove the hit-counting problem.}}
\]

The irreducible core remains a density/anti-concentration theorem for the Apéry zero sets \(Z_p\).
