# P2.7 versus AESZ #209: the exact Hadamard relations, the normalization correction, and why none of (A)–(D) presently gives \(q_n\)

## Executive conclusion

There is an exact and useful operation involving AESZ #209, but it stops one step before the P2.7 denominator.

Let

\[
a_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{n}\binom{n+2k}{n},
\qquad
A_n=\binom{2n}{n}a_n,
\]

and write

\[
A_Z(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n,
\qquad
F_{209}(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}A_nz^n.
\]

Then the exact AESZ construction is the Hadamard product

\[
\boxed{
F_{209}(z)=A_Z(z)\star(1-4z)^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

It can be inverted coefficientwise:

\[
\boxed{
A_Z(z)=F_{209}(z)\star
{}_2F_1\!\left(\begin{matrix}1,1\\[1mm]\tfrac12\end{matrix};\frac z4\right),
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

because the coefficient of \(z^n\) in the second factor is
\(1/\binom{2n}{n}\).

Thus the natural rank-three object behind the order-four AESZ period is the **inner Zudilin sequence** \(a_n\), not a divisor restriction of a Calabi–Yau threefold.

After the correct exponential rescaling, the exact source to compare with P2.7 is

\[
\boxed{
A_Z(z/64)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{a_n}{64^n}z^n.
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

No exact transformation from (0.3) to the printed P2.7 generating function is currently known. In particular:

- the Hadamard quotient always exists tautologically, but it has not been identified as a useful D-finite series;
- a finite Euler/differential post-operator would amount to a finite Ore relation, and no such relation has been found;
- the P2.7 cubic is **not** a symmetric-square spectrum;
- the level-11 identity is an exact quadratic **spectral trace**, not a symmetric-square or a proof of equality of sequences;
- a geometric fiber restriction would require an explicit correspondence or invariant rank-three subquotient, which has not been exhibited.

The most important correction is the scaling. For the recurrence actually committed in `problems/2.7/proof.tex`, the P2.7 shift ratios are

\[
r_j=\frac{\mu_j}{64},
\qquad
4\mu^3-220\mu^2+8\mu-1=0,
\]

whereas the full AESZ coefficients \(A_n\) have exponential bases \(4\mu_j\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{4\mu_j}{\mu_j/64}=256.
}
\]

Accordingly, the generating-function dilation that aligns actual singularities is

\[
\boxed{F_{209}(z/256),}
\]

not \(F_{209}(z/4)\). The factor \(4\) is correct only after replacing the P2.7 sequence by the artificially rescaled sequence \(64^nq_n\).

# 1. The normalization error behind the proposed pullback

Let \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) be the roots of

\[
P_{27}(x)=4x^3-220x^2+8x-1.
\]

There are three related but different normalizations.

## 1.1 Inner AESZ/Zudilin sequence

The sequence \(a_n\) has Poincaré bases \(x_j\). Its dominant term has the standard saddle form

\[
a_n\sim C_a x_0^n n^{-3/2}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

## 1.2 Full AESZ #209 period

Since

\[
\binom{2n}{n}\sim\frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}},
\]

we have

\[
A_n\sim C_A(4x_0)^n n^{-2}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus the full AESZ bases are \(4x_j\).

## 1.3 The P2.7 recurrence as printed

The coefficient recurrence in the repository has actual bases

\[
r_j=\frac{x_j}{64}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

This is stated explicitly in the current proof: its edge polynomial is

\[
946P_{27}(64r).
\]

Therefore the singular points of the two ordinary generating functions satisfy

\[
z_j^{209}=\frac1{4x_j},
\qquad
z_j^{27}=\frac1{r_j}=\frac{64}{x_j}=256z_j^{209}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently,

\[
F_{209}(z/256)
=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{A_n}{256^n}z^n
\]

has the same exponential bases as the P2.7 sequence.

If instead one defines

\[
\widehat q_n:=64^nq_n,
\qquad
\widehat G(z)=G(64z),
\]

then \(\widehat q_n\) has bases \(x_j\), and it is true that the AESZ bases are four times the bases of \(\widehat q_n\). In that rescaled convention the matching pullback is \(F_{209}(z/4)\). But the quotient to test is then

\[
\frac{\widehat q_n}{A_n/4^n}
=\frac{256^nq_n}{A_n},
\]

not the quantity \(4^nq_n/A_n\) listed in the question.

# 2. Why the displayed quotient decreases near \(1/64\), not \(1/4\)

Define

\[
h_n^{(4)}:=\frac{4^nq_n}{A_n},
\qquad
H_n:=\frac{256^nq_n}{A_n}=64^nh_n^{(4)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The numerical values in the question have successive ratios

\[
0.0492,\ 0.0324,\ 0.0266,\ 0.0238,\ 0.0221,\ 0.0210,\ldots,
\]

which are moving toward

\[
\frac1{64}=0.015625,
\]

not toward \(1/4\).

For the recurrence normalization currently committed in the project, a direct first-order Birkhoff calculation gives, at a root \(x\) of \(P_{27}\),

\[
\sigma_{27}(x)
=
\frac{24(4x-1)}{220x^2-16x+3}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

At the dominant root \(x_0=54.963695098\ldots\),

\[
\sigma_0=0.007913449316\ldots.
\]

Combining (1.2) with the P2.7 asymptotic gives

\[
H_n\sim C_Hn^{2+\sigma_0},
\tag{2.3}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{h_{n+1}^{(4)}}{h_n^{(4)}}
=
\frac1{64}
\left(1+\frac{2+\sigma_0}{n}+O(n^{-2})\right).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

At \(n=7\), the right side is already about \(0.0201\), in agreement with the observed \(0.0210\ldots\).

If a later normalization of the target recurrence makes the formal power exactly zero, the same conclusion remains valid with \(2+\sigma_0\) replaced by \(2\). The scaling correction and all conclusions below are unaffected.

The nonintegrality of \(h_n\) is irrelevant: D-finite and hypergeometric sequences may certainly be rational. What matters is that the quotient of two P-recursive sequences is **not** closed under P-recursiveness. A recurrence for \(H_n\) must be found and verified; it does not follow from those of \(q_n\) and \(A_n\).

# 3. The exact Hadamard and Euler-integral formulas for AESZ #209

Let

\[
B(z)=(1-4z)^{-1/2}
=\sum_{n\ge0}\binom{2n}{n}z^n.
\]

Then (0.1) is immediate coefficientwise. It also has the standard contour representation

\[
F_{209}(z)
=
\frac1{2\pi i}\oint
A_Z(u)B(z/u)\,\frac{du}{u}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

After the correct scaling,

\[
\boxed{
F_{209}(z/256)
=
\frac1{2\pi i}\oint
A_Z(u)
\left(1-\frac{z}{64u}\right)^{-1/2}
\frac{du}{u}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

There is also an elementary real integral. Since

\[
\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}
=
\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^{2n}\theta\,d\theta,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
F_{209}(z/256)
=
\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}
A_Z\!\left(\frac{z\sin^2\theta}{64}\right)d\theta.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is the exact integral implementation of the outer central-binomial factor.

## 3.1 Exact Hadamard deconvolution

The reciprocal central-binomial sequence is itself hypergeometric:

\[
\frac1{\binom{2n}{n}}
=
\frac{(1)_n}{4^n(\tfrac12)_n}.
\]

Hence

\[
D(z):=
{}_2F_1\!\left(
\begin{matrix}1,1\\[1mm]\tfrac12\end{matrix};\frac z4
\right)
=
\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{\binom{2n}{n}},
\tag{3.4}
\]

and coefficientwise

\[
\boxed{A_Z=F_{209}\star D.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the actual order-four-to-rank-three reduction behind AESZ #209. It is a Hadamard deconvolution, not a fiber restriction.

After (3.5), the spectral normalization relevant to P2.7 is simply \(z\mapsto z/64\). Thus the exact comparison chain is

\[
\boxed{
F_{209}
\xrightarrow{\ \star D\ }
A_Z
\xrightarrow{\ z\mapsto z/64\ }
A_Z(z/64).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The missing arrow is

\[
A_Z(z/64)\dashrightarrow G_{27}(z).
\]

No formula for that arrow is currently known.

# 4. Option (A): Hadamard quotient

With the correct scaling, one can always define

\[
H_n:=\frac{256^nq_n}{A_n},
\qquad
H(z):=\sum_{n\ge0}H_nz^n,
\]

and then write the formal identity

\[
\boxed{G_{27}(z)=F_{209}(z/256)\star H(z).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is exact but tautological. It becomes useful only after independently identifying \(H\).

The following facts are important.

1. **Rational values do not imply D-finiteness.**  \(H_n\in\mathbb Q\), but the quotient of two P-recursive sequences need not be P-recursive.

2. **A rational function or a scalar Pochhammer quotient is too small.**  Such a sequence has an asymptotic of the form \(C\kappa^nn^\tau\) with rational \(	au\). In the current recurrence normalization, (2.3) has exponent \(2+\sigma_0\), which is algebraic irrational. Thus \(H_n\) is not a balanced scalar Pochhammer quotient with rational parameters.

3. **A general D-finite \(H\) is not excluded.**  A rational-coefficient D-finite series can have algebraic local exponents. But no annihilator for \(H\) is presently known, and eight or fifteen numerical values cannot establish one.

4. **Hadamard order can fall only through cancellation.**  Even if \(H\) is D-finite, the generic Hadamard product has rank roughly the product of the ranks. Obtaining the target module would require exact cancellation of a large annihilator, which must be exhibited algebraically.

A legitimate success for option (A) would therefore consist of:

- an explicit independent formula or recurrence for \(H_n\);
- an exact proof of (4.1);
- a factorization of the Hadamard-product annihilator showing that the P2.7 operator is the relevant factor;
- exact initial-value matching.

At present, option (A) is a useful computational inverse problem, not an identified transformation.

# 5. Option (B): local differential and Euler transforms

## 5.1 The proposed local differential transform

Let

\[
T(z)=z^{-a}\left(\frac d{dz}\right)^b
\left[z^cF_{209}(z/s)\right].
\]

A direct coefficient calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
[z^n]T(z)
=
(n+a+b)_{\underline b}\,
 s^{-(n+a+b-c)}
 A_{n+a+b-c},
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where \((x)_{\underline b}\) is a falling factorial.

A finite linear combination of expressions (5.1) is exactly a finite-width relation

\[
q_n=\sum_{j=0}^d p_j(n)\frac{A_{n+j}}{256^{n+j}}
\tag{5.2}
\]

with polynomial or rational \(p_j(n)\). Thus the proposed operation is not a new nonlocal mechanism; it is a finite Ore/differential post-operator. The negative intertwiner searches in the project test precisely this class.

A finite search does not prove that no relation of arbitrarily high degree exists, but it does prove that none of the small derivative formulas suggested in the question works. More importantly, no geometric or hypergeometric reason predicts a high-order relation.

## 5.2 A genuine Euler beta transform

The standard nonlocal Euler transform is

\[
\mathcal E_{\alpha,\beta}F(z)
=
\frac{\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta-\alpha)}
\int_0^1
 t^{\alpha-1}(1-t)^{\beta-\alpha-1}F(tz)\,dt.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Coefficientwise,

\[
[z^n]\mathcal E_{\alpha,\beta}F
=
\frac{(\alpha)_n}{(\beta)_n}[z^n]F.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus it is a scalar Pochhammer gauge. It multiplies every characteristic root by the same exponential constant and shifts every formal power by the same number \(\alpha-\beta\). It cannot, by itself, manufacture three root-dependent accessory exponents.

Generalized Euler transforms and middle convolutions can change rank, but they require the full Riemann scheme and an explicit kernel; the mere scaling of three Poincaré roots does not determine such a transform. The construction of Doran–Malmendier is a relevant template, not an automatic identification.

Therefore option (B), in its stated form, does not produce P2.7.

# 6. Option (C): symmetric-square root

This option is ruled out by an exact spectral invariant already recorded in the project.

If a rank-two recurrence has characteristic roots \(\alpha,\beta\), then, after a common scalar gauge \(g\), its symmetric-square roots are

\[
g\alpha^2,\qquad g\alpha\beta,\qquad g\beta^2.
\]

The middle root \(r=g\alpha\beta\) satisfies

\[
r^3=(g\alpha^2)(g\alpha\beta)(g\beta^2),
\]

i.e. its cube equals the product of all three roots.

In the \(P_{27}\)-normalization the product of the roots is \(1/4\). Hence a symmetric-square spectrum would contain a root \(r\) satisfying

\[
4r^3=1.
\]

Combining this with \(P_{27}(r)=0\) gives

\[
P_{27}(r)=1-220r^2+8r-1=r(8-220r)=0,
\]

so \(r=2/55\). But

\[
(2/55)^3\ne1/4.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{P_{27}\text{ is not a symmetric-square spectrum.}}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The level-11 identity

\[
16P_{27}\!\left(\frac{(t-2)^2}{4}\right)
=-C_{11}(t)C_{11}(4-t)
\tag{6.2}
\]

has a different meaning. It is a quadratic norm under the involution

\[
t\longleftrightarrow4-t.
\]

At the sequence level, the corresponding exact transform on a pure exponential is the centered even-binomial transform

\[
\boxed{
(\mathcal KT)_n
=
\frac1{256^n}
\sum_{k=0}^{2n}
\binom{2n}{k}(-2)^{2n-k}T_k.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For \(T_k=t^k\),

\[
(\mathcal KT)_n
=
\left(\frac{(t-2)^2}{256}\right)^n
=
\left(\frac{\mu}{64}\right)^n,
\qquad
\mu=\frac{(t-2)^2}{4}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus (6.3) explains the P2.7 **edge polynomial exactly**. It does not determine the lower-order coefficients, accessory singularities, or connection constants. To promote it to an identity of sequences, one would still have to prove by creative telescoping that the transformed Cooper sequence satisfies the complete P2.7 recurrence and then match three initial values. The polynomial identity (6.2) alone does not do this.

So option (C) fails as a symmetric-square construction, while (6.3) remains a valuable spectral clue.

# 7. Option (D): fiber restriction or cohomological projection

There are two separate issues.

## 7.1 The alleged order \(4\to3\) mismatch is based on a category error

The P2.7 recurrence has **shift order three**—four consecutive terms. That does not mean its ordinary generating function satisfies a third-order differential equation.

If

\[
\sum_{j=0}^3P_j(n)q_{n+j}=0,
\]

then, up to the initial-value polynomial, its generating function satisfies the Euler differential equation

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=0}^3 z^{3-j}P_j(\theta-j)G(z)=\text{initial polynomial},
\qquad
\theta=z\frac d{dz}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The differential order of (7.1) is controlled by

\[
\max_j\deg P_j,
\]

which is \(12\) for the displayed recurrence, not \(3\). A homogeneous annihilator may have still higher order after the initial polynomial is removed.

Therefore one cannot compare “AESZ differential order four” with “P2.7 differential order three” and infer a rank-one projection. They are different notions of order.

## 7.2 No degree-four cover is present

The coordinate change required by the actual recurrence is

\[
z\longmapsto z/256.
\]

This is a degree-one dilation, not a degree-four étale cover. The factor \(4\) arises from the central-binomial Hadamard factor and from the rescaled spectral variable, not from the degree of a map of parameter curves.

## 7.3 What a genuine fiber restriction would require

Restricting a Calabi–Yau family to a divisor does not canonically produce a rank-three quotient of its \(H^3\) variation. One would need an explicit algebraic correspondence, a relative cohomology sequence, or a normal-function extension and would have to prove that its Picard–Fuchs module is the target module.

No such divisor or correspondence is presently known for P2.7. The exact relation that **is** known is the Hadamard deconvolution (3.5), which recovers the rank-three inner Zudilin system from the rank-four AESZ period. That recovered system is still not the P2.7 system.

Thus option (D) is not currently supported by an explicit geometric construction.

# 8. What can be stated exactly now

The complete exact diagram currently justified is

\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
F_{209}(z)
&\xrightarrow{\star\,\sum z^n/\binom{2n}{n}}&
A_Z(z)
&\xrightarrow{z\mapsto z/64}&
A_Z(z/64)
\\[2mm]
&&&&\ \dashrightarrow\ G_{27}(z).
\end{array}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The first two arrows are explicit. The dashed arrow is the missing mathematical content.

Separately, the level-11 identity gives the exact spectral operation

\[
T_k\longmapsto
\frac1{256^n}\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{k}(-2)^{2n-k}T_k,
\tag{8.2}
\]

but no verified equality with \(q_n\).

Hence the answer to the four proposed possibilities is:

| Proposal | Exact status |
|---|---|
| **(A) Hadamard quotient** | Always definable by \(H_n=256^nq_n/A_n\), but presently tautological; \(H\) is not identified. |
| **(B) Euler/differential transform** | The local derivative form is a finite Ore post-operator and does not match. A generalized Euler/middle convolution would need new explicit kernel data. |
| **(C) Symmetric square** | Ruled out exactly by the cubic product invariant. The level-11 identity is a quadratic trace, not a symmetric square. |
| **(D) Fiber restriction** | No explicit correspondence exists; the apparent rank comparison is invalid because shift order is not differential order. |

No option currently supplies the desired exact formula for \(G_{27}\).

# 9. A reproducible exact diagnostic for the Hadamard quotient

The following Sage-compatible code computes both quotient normalizations exactly and searches for a low-order polynomial recurrence. A guessed recurrence is not a proof; any output must be verified on all indices by an Ore identity or by deriving it from a closed form.

```python
from sage.all import QQ, ZZ, binomial, matrix, PolynomialRing

N = 80


def A209(n):
    inner = sum(
        binomial(n, k)^2 * binomial(n + k, n) * binomial(n + 2*k, n)
        for k in range(n + 1)
    )
    return ZZ(binomial(2*n, n) * inner)


def A(n):
    return QQ(1024)*(2*n+5)^4*(2*n+7)^3*(2*n+9)^3 \
        *(946*n^2+6407*n+10860)


def B(n):
    return QQ(128)*(2*n+7)^3*(2*n+9)^3 * (
        104060*n^6+1745370*n^5+12145238*n^4+44886481*n^3
        +92943995*n^2+102256019*n+46709052
    )


def C(n):
    return QQ(16)*(n+3)^4*(2*n+9)^3 * (
        3784*n^5+57792*n^4+351019*n^3+1059230*n^2
        +1587211*n+944620
    )


def D(n):
    return QQ(n+3)^4*(n+4)^6*(946*n^2+4515*n+5399)


q = [
    QQ(-215040420000),
    QQ(-167282265043404)/905,
    QQ(-964185327658080)/6071,
]
for n in range(2, N-1):
    q.append(
        B(n)/A(n)*q[n]
        - C(n-1)/A(n-1)*q[n-1]
        + D(n-2)/A(n-2)*q[n-2]
    )

AA = [QQ(A209(n)) for n in range(N)]
h4 = [q[n]*4^n/AA[n] for n in range(N)]
H  = [q[n]*256^n/AA[n] for n in range(N)]

for n in range(1, 12):
    print(n, "h4 ratio =", h4[n]/h4[n-1],
          "H ratio =", H[n]/H[n-1])

# Search sum_{j=0}^r p_j(n) H[n+j] = 0,
# with deg p_j <= d. Hold back terms for exact verification.
R.<x> = PolynomialRing(QQ)

def guess_recurrence(seq, max_order=6, max_degree=16, holdout=20):
    for r in range(1, max_order+1):
        for d in range(max_degree+1):
            unknowns = (r+1)*(d+1)
            available = len(seq)-r
            train = min(available-holdout, unknowns+5)
            if train < unknowns-1:
                continue
            rows = []
            for n in range(train):
                rows.append([
                    QQ(n)^k * seq[n+j]
                    for j in range(r+1)
                    for k in range(d+1)
                ])
            for v in matrix(QQ, rows).right_kernel().basis():
                p = []
                i = 0
                for j in range(r+1):
                    p.append(sum(v[i+k]*x^k for k in range(d+1)))
                    i += d+1
                if all(
                    sum(p[j](n)*seq[n+j] for j in range(r+1)) == 0
                    for n in range(available)
                ):
                    return r, d, p
    return None

print(guess_recurrence(H))
```

The first sanity check should be that the `h4 ratio` approaches \(1/64\), while the `H ratio` approaches \(1\).

# 10. Best next route toward the proof

The most promising precise program is not another raw pullback guess.

1. **Use the exact de-Hadamard source.** Work with \(A_Z(z/64)\), not the full AESZ period.
2. **Construct the true target Euler/Mellin operator** from (7.1), including its initial polynomial and desingularization.
3. **Compare full local data**, not only the Poincaré cubic: singularities, exponents, logarithmic blocks, and accessory parameters.
4. Search for a genuine nonlocal functor—Euler/middle convolution, Cauchy convolution, or a mixed-period transform—with an explicit kernel and exact action on local bases.
5. Independently, continue the target-specific Beukers/Hermite–Padé route. An integral that satisfies the P2.7 recurrence, matches three initial values, and has strict subdominant decay would prove the required connection coefficient directly.

The factor-four observation is real in the rescaled spectral variable, but it does not determine a generating-function pullback. The exact information currently extracted from it is the centered trace transform (6.3), not an equality with the P2.7 denominator.
