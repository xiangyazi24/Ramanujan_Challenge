# 5. Audit of the named mechanisms

## 5.1 Cleared gap polynomials and cross-characteristic resultants

At p, the two zeros live in F_p. At P=p+Δ, the shifted two zeros live in F_P. A resultant controls a common root after reduction modulo **one and the same prime**. It does not turn

```text
p | F(p),   P | G(P)
```

into a prime divisor of `Res(F,G)`.

After confluence, diagonal evaluation modulo the foreign prime produces the fixed companion values listed above. But the forced Smith factor is exactly the singular factor that makes raw diagonal divisibility automatic. Removing it produces optional residuals, and an actual support pair need not make them vanish. Resultants therefore control the excess channel and stop at CPS.

## 5.2 Companion/Frobenius matrices

The reductions modulo p and P lie in different DVRs. Over

```text
Z/(pP) = F_p x F_P,
```

all current determinant and rank conditions split componentwise. The Smith defects are respectively `(1,p^6)` and `(1,P^6)`; there is no banked cross-component minor coupling their primitive coordinates. Keeping raw torsion preserves an occurrence majorant but recounts the same staircase Smith tails. Removing it gives state-free optional excess and loses the occurrence implication.

This is a scoped no-go for the present matrix identities, not a claim that no new mixed-characteristic representation can exist.

## 5.3 Reciprocity and reflection

Reflection explains the offsets 2Δ and 7Δ; the unreflected comparison explains Δ and 6Δ. It does not move all four zeros into one characteristic. Applying reflection again only permutes the same four optional companion residues.

## 5.4 Same-characteristic twin theorem

The canonical twin-atom theorem counts primes ell for which two specified indices are both zeros modulo that same ell, then charges a gap polynomial modulo ell. Here the endpoint tests are split between p and P. Replacing P by p in order to invoke the theorem changes the event. It cannot be inserted into C4Z.
