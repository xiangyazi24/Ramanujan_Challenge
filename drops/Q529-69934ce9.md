# P3.2: Additive Fourier transform of the Apéry trace function

## Executive summary

The quantity

\[
|Z_p|=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(ub_r)
\]

is indeed a Fourier transform of the value distribution of the Apéry sequence modulo \(p\). However, it is **not** directly a Gauss sum of the Apéry motive, nor is it controlled by the ordinary \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) monodromy of the Picard--Fuchs sheaf.

The reason is that there are two different Fourier transforms:

1. The geometric/motivic Fourier transform, which acts on the trace function of a lisse sheaf as the parameter varies geometrically.
2. The additive Fourier transform of the sequence of Frobenius traces \(b_r\) as the Kummer exponent \(r\) varies.

The second is a "Fourier transform of the trace distribution" and introduces a new sheaf problem. The existing \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) monodromy explains the distribution of individual Frobenius traces, but does not automatically give square-root cancellation for

\[
S(u)=\sum_r e_p(ub_r).
\]

A successful proof of M1 would require a new bounded-conductor description of this additive transform.

---

# 1. What M1 asks analytically

Let

\[
S(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}e_p(ub_r).
\]

Then

\[
Z(p)=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}S(u).
\]

The term \(u=0\) gives

\[
S(0)=p,
\]

so

\[
Z(p)=1+\frac1p\sum_{u\ne0}S(u).
\]

Thus the desired average \(Z(p)=O(1)\) requires cancellation in

\[
\sum_{u\ne0}S(u).
\]

A pointwise square-root bound

\[
|S(u)|\ll\sqrt p
\]

would already be sufficient, because then

\[
\frac1p\sum_{u\ne0}|S(u)|\ll\sqrt p,
\]

but the actual requirement is weaker: only the signed sum over \(u\) is needed.

---

# 2. Is \(S(u)\) a Gauss sum of the Apéry motive?

Not directly.

The Apéry motive gives a rank-four Galois representation

\[
V_p\colon G_\mathbf Q\to \mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbf Q_\ell),
\]

with Frobenius traces related to the Apéry Picard--Fuchs equation. At a geometric parameter \(t\), one has a trace function

\[
t\mapsto \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_t|V).
\]

Katz theory studies sums like

\[
\sum_t\chi(t)\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_t|V),
\]

where \(t\) is a geometric point.

Our sum is different:

\[
\sum_r e_p(u b_r).
\]

The variable \(r\) is not a geometric parameter of the K3 family. It is the **Kummer twist index**. Therefore this is a Fourier transform in the character direction.

It is closer to a Mellin/Fourier transform of a family of twisted Frobenius traces:

\[
M\otimes\chi^r.
\]

The resulting object is a new exponential-sum sheaf, not simply the original Apéry motive.

---

# 3. Relation to \(L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^k V)\)

There is a conceptual relation, but not the direct one suggested.

If \(V\) has monodromy \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\), then tensor constructions

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^k(V),\quad \wedge^k(V),\quad V\otimes V
\]

produce new Galois representations. Their L-functions encode correlations of Frobenius traces, for example

\[
\sum_p a_p(V)^2p^{-s}.
\]

However \(S(u)\) involves the nonlinear function

\[
r\mapsto e_p(u\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_r)).
\]

Expanding the exponential formally gives

\[
e_p(ub_r)=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{(2\pi i u b_r/p)^m}{m!},
\]

so moments of all orders appear:

\[
\sum_r b_r^m.
\]

These moments are related to tensor powers of \(V\), not one fixed symmetric power.

Thus the natural motivic object is closer to the entire tensor category generated by \(V\), not a single

\[
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^kV).
\]

---

# 4. What does \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) predict?

The \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) hypothesis gives a random-matrix model.

A Frobenius element is modeled by

\[
U\in USp(4).
\]

The trace distribution is the distribution of

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(U).
\]

The Apéry values \(b_r\bmod p\) should therefore behave like samples from the finite-field analogue of this trace distribution.

The additive Fourier transform should be approximately

\[
\widehat\mu(u)=\int_{USp(4)}e_p(u\operatorname{Tr}(U))dU.
\]

By Weyl integration,

\[
\int_{USp(4)}f(\operatorname{Tr}U)dU
\]

reduces to a two-angle integral over the maximal torus

\[
U=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta_1},e^{-i\theta_1},e^{i\theta_2},e^{-i\theta_2}).
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(U)=2\cos\theta_1+2\cos\theta_2.
\]

The Fourier transform is therefore an oscillatory Bessel-type integral:

\[
\widehat\mu(u)
\approx
\int e_p(2u(\cos\theta_1+\cos\theta_2))\Delta(\theta)^2d\theta.
\]

Stationary phase predicts decay roughly of square-root size:

\[
\widehat\mu(u)\sim p^{-1/2}.
\]

This matches the empirical Poisson law for \(|Z_p|\).

But this is a prediction from the random matrix model, not yet a theorem for the Apéry sequence.

---

# 5. Why Katz equidistribution is not enough

Katz's theorem applies when the parameter is a geometric point:

\[
x\in \mathbf A^1(\mathbf F_p).
\]

Then Frobenius conjugacy classes become equidistributed in the monodromy group.

Here the parameter is the twist exponent \(r\):

\[
M\otimes\chi^r.
\]

This is a character family.

Character families require a different form of equidistribution: one studies the Fourier transform in the character group. Katz develops such ideas in his work on exponential sums and Mellin transforms, but the Apéry situation requires identifying the relevant sheaf after this transform.

The obstruction is exactly the same one encountered in the HM/P3.2 analysis: the existing \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) geometry controls the local motive, but the zero event depends on an additional additive Fourier layer.

---

# 6. What theorem would prove M1?

A sufficient statement would be an additive trace-function estimate:

\[
\boxed{
|S(u)|=\left|\sum_r e_p(ub_r)\right|\ll p^{1/2+o(1)}
}
\]

uniformly for \(u\ne0\).

Then

\[
Z(p)=1+O(p^{-1/2+o(1)}p)=1+O(p^{1/2+o(1)}),
\]

which alone is not enough pointwise, but averaged estimates over \(u\) or \(p\) would suffice.

The stronger desired statement is

\[
\sum_{u\ne0}S(u)=O(p).
\]

This is an averaged cancellation statement.

A plausible route is:

1. Construct the additive Fourier transform sheaf of the Apéry character family.
2. Prove bounded conductor and geometric irreducibility.
3. Compute its monodromy group.
4. Apply Deligne/Katz bounds.

At present step (1) is the missing arithmetic-geometric construction.

---

# Final answers

**Connection to Gauss sums:** Yes conceptually, but \(S(u)\) is not a Gauss sum of the original Apéry motive. It is a second Fourier transform of its trace distribution.

**Connection to \(L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^kV)\):** Only indirectly. Moments correspond to tensor powers of \(V\), not a single symmetric power.

**Weyl character prediction:** For \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\), the Fourier transform of the trace distribution is an oscillatory integral over the two-dimensional maximal torus. Stationary phase predicts square-root cancellation.

**Does Sp(4) monodromy prove M1?** No. Sp(4) controls Frobenius conjugacy classes; M1 requires controlling additive Fourier transforms in the Kummer parameter. This is a different layer.

**Most likely missing theorem:** A Katz-style equidistribution theorem for the additive Fourier transform of the Apéry Kummer family, i.e. a bounded-conductor sheaf whose trace function is \(e_p(ub_r)\).
