# P3.2 Apéry resultant attack

## Executive result

The resultant reduction is valid, but the requested final nonvanishing theorem does not follow from the currently available hypotheses alone. I computed the structural form of the resultant and isolated the remaining arithmetic lemma.

Let

\[
E_m(\xi)=e_m=(2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m,
\]

and

\[
Y_m(\xi)=\Pi_m-\sum_{t=2}^m6(t^2-1)d_t\frac{\Pi_m}{X_t}.
\]

After clearing the denominators one obtains a polynomial in \(\xi\). The resultant

\[
R_m=\operatorname{Res}_{\xi}(E_m,Y_m)
\]

is the elimination invariant for the central gap condition.

The known identity

\[
R_m=u_m\Sigma
\]

with a unit factor \(u_m\) is stronger than an equivalence statement: it says the only possible vanishing comes from the reciprocal weighted sum. However, it does not itself imply that \(R_m\) is prime to \(p\).

## Normalization

The obvious factors are produced by

\[
d_t=\prod_{s<t}(2s+1)^3.
\]

Define the normalized resultant

\[
\widetilde R_m=R_m/\prod_{t=2}^m d_t^{\alpha_t}
\]

where the exponents \(\alpha_t\) are the multiplicities introduced when clearing the denominators in \(Y_m\).

The remaining factor is the genuine Apéry part.

## Exact computation script

A reproducible Sage/Python implementation is:

```python
from sage.all import *

R.<xi> = PolynomialRing(QQ)

def compute(m):
    X=[None]*(m+1)
    X[0]=R(1)
    X[1]=xi
    for t in range(1,m):
        X[t+1]=4*t*(68*t*t+3)*X[t]-(2*t-1)^6*X[t-1]

    Pi=prod(X[t] for t in range(2,m+1))
    ds=1
    Y=Pi
    for t in range(2,m+1):
        ds*= (2*t-1)^3
        Y-=6*(t*t-1)*ds*Pi//X[t]

    e=(2*m-1)^3*X[m-1]-5*X[m]
    return e.resultant(Y)

for m in range(2,10):
    print(m, factor(compute(m)))
```

This computes over characteristic zero before reduction, avoiding accidental modular cancellation.

## Observed pattern

The factorization separates into:

1. powers of the predictable odd factors from \(d_t\);
2. a residual factor attached to the Apéry recurrence.

No verified recurrence of the residual factor was found from the tested range. In particular, the data does not justify a claim such as

\[
\widetilde R_m=\prod_i f_i(m)
\]

with simple linear factors.

## Attempted proof of prime-to-p property

Suppose

\[
p=2m+1.
\]

The hypotheses give

\[
X_0,\ldots,X_{m-2},X_m\in\mathbf F_p^\times,
\]

and

\[
X_{m-1}=0.
\]

The recurrence then forces

\[
(2m-1)^6X_{m-2}=4(m-1)(68(m-1)^2+3)X_{m-1}-X_m,
\]

so the endpoint relation constrains the last transition.

However, the resultant vanishing condition is equivalent to the existence of a common root of \(E_m\) and \(Y_m\), or equivalently a zero of the reciprocal weighted functional. The unit hypotheses only control the denominators:

\[
X_t^{-1}\quad(0\le t\le m-2).
\]

They do not exclude cancellation in

\[
\Sigma=1-\sum_{t=2}^m6(t^2-1)d_t/X_t.
\]

Therefore the missing theorem is:

> If the Apéry recurrence has the central gap \(X_{m-1}=0\) modulo \(2m+1\) and all other required terms are units, then the weighted reciprocal functional \(\Sigma\) is nonzero.

This is strictly stronger than the unit hypotheses.

## Theorem/conjecture separation

### Proven

- The resultant eliminates \(\xi\) exactly.
- Clearing denominators gives a polynomial resultant with predictable \(d_t\)-factors.
- The normalized resultant detects precisely the weighted reciprocal obstruction.

### Conjectural

The normalized resultant is always nonzero modulo \(2m+1\) in the singleton Apéry gap case.

A proof would require an additional Apéry-specific identity, likely one of:

1. a parameter derivative identity for the recurrence;
2. a Casoratian involving the second independent solution;
3. a spectral derivative of the three-term transfer matrix.

The ordinary adjacent Casoratian does not suffice because it produces local determinant weights, not the global reciprocal weights in \(\Sigma\).

## Conclusion

The resultant attack narrows P3.2 to a single missing arithmetic lemma. Current hypotheses prove elimination but not nonvanishing. The next target is an Apéry-specific derivative/Casoratian identity whose specialization at the central zero converts the weighted reciprocal sum into a nonzero neighboring solution value.
