# Q1698: finite-field reflection/norm audit of the endpoint trace

## Summary

The cycle/reflection formalism gives an exact transfer-matrix identity, but by itself it does **not** force the endpoint trace
\[
S=\frac13-2\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{t^2-1}{u_t}
\]
to be nonzero. The obstruction is an isotropic kernel possibility: the trace is a matrix coefficient of a product in \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\), and the same Jordan/monodromy data permits vanishing of that coefficient.

Therefore a proof of \(S\neq0\) needs an extra arithmetic input (for example a non-isotropic invariant form or a simple-root statement for the continued fraction), not merely the existence of a p-cycle.

## Transfer matrix identity

Write
\[
A_t=\begin{pmatrix}
4t(68t^2+3)&-(2t-1)^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The recurrence vector
\[
v_t=(u_t,u_{t-1})^T
\]
satisfies
\[
v_{t+1}=A_t v_t.
\]
Hence
\[
v_{m+1}=M v_1,\qquad M=A_mA_{m-1}\cdots A_1.
\]
The seam condition \(u_{m-1}=5u_m\) says
\[
(1,-5)v_m=0.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
(1,-5)A_{m-1}\cdots A_1v_1=0.
\]
This is the exact endpoint constraint.

## Trace interpretation of S

For the continuant, the logarithmic derivative of the transfer product gives
\[
\frac{d}{d\lambda}\log \det(M(\lambda))\bigg|_{\lambda=0}
=\sum_t \operatorname{tr}(A_t^{-1}A'_t).
\]
After normalizing the recurrence by the pivots \(u_t\), the coefficient appearing is exactly
\[
\frac13-2\sum_{t=2}^{m}\frac{t^2-1}{u_t}.
\]
Thus \(S\) is a first variation of the endpoint functional, not a trace invariant of the unperturbed matrix alone.

## Why monodromy rank is insufficient

Suppose the product matrix has eigenvalue 1 with a fixed vector. There are two cases:

1. The fixed line is non-isotropic for the induced bilinear form. Then a trace-type obstruction may exist.

2. The fixed line is isotropic. Then the same endpoint functional can vanish while the monodromy remains nontrivial.

The second case is the concrete obstruction. In \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\), a unipotent matrix
\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
has rank-one nilpotent part but can have zero pairing against a chosen endpoint vector. Therefore Jordan type alone cannot imply \(S\neq0\).

## Reflection extension across the seam

Extending by the formal involution \(t\mapsto -t\) gives paired factors
\[
A_{-t}\sim J A_t^{-1}J^{-1}
\]
for a suitable exchange matrix \(J\). Consequently the full cycle product is conjugate to a norm element
\[
N(M)=MJMJ^{-1}.
\]
This gives a determinant constraint
\[
\det N(M)=1,
\]
but determinant one does not exclude a zero endpoint matrix coefficient.

## Failure model

A valid countermodel to the claimed implication is any \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\) norm cycle with
\[
N(M)=I+N,\qquad N^2=0,
\]
and endpoint vectors chosen so that
\[
w^TN(M)v=0
\]
while \(N\neq0\). It has the same cycle/Jordan structure but vanishing trace functional.

Thus the desired implication
\[
\text{cycle structure}\Longrightarrow S\neq0
\]
is false without additional arithmetic restrictions.

## Required missing ingredient

A successful proof would need one of:

* a non-isotropicity theorem for the endpoint line;
* a simple-root theorem for the endpoint polynomial;
* a positivity/denominator identity forcing the first variation coefficient to be nonzero.

The finite-field reflection/norm attack narrows the target but does not close the endpoint valuation claim by itself.
