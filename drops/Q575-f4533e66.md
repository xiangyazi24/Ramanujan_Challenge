# Q575 — Katz equidistribution and vanishing Mellin coefficients

## Executive summary

The short answer is:

\[
\boxed{\text{SL}_2\text{ monodromy of the Apéry sheaf is not enough to prove }|Z_p|=O(1).}
\]

Katz equidistribution controls the distribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes and hence moments of trace functions. The Apéry zero set

\[
Z_p=\{r:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]

is a **zero locus of a Mellin transform**, not a trace-function value distribution. The operation

\[
\text{trace function}\longrightarrow\text{indicator of exact vanishing}
\]

is nonlinear and is not controlled by geometric monodromy alone.

The missing theorem is therefore not a standard Katz equidistribution theorem. It would require an additional statement controlling the rank-drop locus of the Mellin transform sheaf.

---

# (A) Direct route: can Katz equidistribution bound the number of vanishing Mellin coefficients?

## Setup

Let \(\mathcal F\) be the Apéry sheaf with geometric monodromy

\[
G_{\rm geom}=SL_2.
\]

For a multiplicative character \(\chi_r\), the Mellin coefficient is

\[
M(\chi_r)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p^\times}t_{\mathcal F}(t)\chi_r(t).
\]

The desired estimate is

\[
\#\{r:M(\chi_r)=0\}=O(1)\quad\text{on average over }p.
\]

Katz gives equidistribution of

\[
\operatorname{Fr}_t\in SU(2)
\]

as \(t\) varies. This implies moment estimates such as

\[
\sum_t |\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Fr}_t)|^{2k}
\]

and Deligne bounds for exponential sums.

However, \(M(\chi_r)=0\) is the statement that a **Fourier coefficient** of the trace function vanishes exactly.

There is no contradiction with Katz if many coefficients vanish. For example, a function can have a sparse Fourier spectrum while its values are perfectly equidistributed.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{SL_2\text{ monodromy}\not\Rightarrow \#\{r:M(\chi_r)=0\}=O(1).}
\]

The missing input is a theorem about the Fourier transform object itself.

# (B) Fourier–Möbius mixing on the character group

The Kummer characters form

\[
\widehat{\mathbb F_p^\times}\cong\mathbb Z/(p-1)\mathbb Z.
\]

One might hope for a spectral-gap theorem saying that the sequence

\[
1_{M(\chi_r)=0}
\]

mixes on this group.

There is no such general theorem.

The reason is structural: the character group is not carrying a natural random walk generated by the monodromy group. The action

\[
\chi\mapsto M(\chi)
\]

is a finite Fourier transform, not a Markov process.

A spectral gap would require an additional dynamical structure, for example:

* a convolution operator whose eigenfunctions are Kummer characters;
* an expanding correspondence on the dual torus;
* a trace-function interpretation of the zero indicator.

None is currently available for Apéry Mellin zeros.

---

# (C) Katz bounds on moments versus exact zeros

Katz's work gives many powerful results on moments of exponential sums, for example:

* N. Katz, **Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups**;
* N. Katz, **Exponential Sums and Differential Equations**;
* Katz's Fourier transform theory for lisse sheaves.

These results control quantities like

\[
\sum_r |M(\chi_r)|^{2k}
\]

or the geometric monodromy of the Fourier transform sheaf.

They do not generally control

\[
\#\{r:M(\chi_r)=0\}.
\]

A moment bound gives an upper bound on large values, not on exact zeros.

For example, a family may satisfy perfect square-root cancellation for every nonzero coefficient and still have many coefficients equal to zero because of hidden symmetry.

Therefore no known Katz theorem directly gives average \(|Z_p|=O(1)\).

# (D) Convolution sheaf and a possible rank theorem

The correct geometric object is indeed the Mellin transform sheaf.

The Mellin transform of \(\mathcal F\) is another sheaf on the character torus:

\[
\operatorname{Mell}(\mathcal F).
\]

The question becomes:

> How large can the zero locus of the Mellin transform sheaf be?

For a generic irreducible sheaf one expects few zeros. However, the known theory gives statements about:

* rank;
* conductor;
* singular support;
* Fourier transform complexity.

It does not give a theorem of the form

\[
\#\{\chi:\operatorname{Mell}(\mathcal F)(\chi)=0\}\le C\cdot \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal F).
\]

Why not?

Because the Mellin transform values are not functions on a fixed algebraic variety over an algebraically closed field in the usual sense. They are Frobenius traces of fibers indexed by finite-field characters. Exact vanishing is arithmetic, not geometric.

The closest possible analogue would be a theorem saying:

> The Mellin transform sheaf has bounded conductor and its Frobenius trace vanishes at only boundedly many points of the character torus.

No such general theorem is known.

# (E) Literature status

## Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel

FKM developed the theory of trace functions over finite fields and powerful large-sieve estimates.

Relevant philosophy:

\[
\text{bounded conductor trace family}\Rightarrow\text{square-root cancellation}.
\]

The obstruction is that

\[
1_{b_r=0\pmod p}
\]

is not known to be a bounded-conductor trace function.

The Apéry sheaf gives the trace

\[
b_r\pmod p,
\]

but the zero detector is nonlinear.

Thus FKM does not directly apply.

---

## Rojas-León and Mellin transforms

Rojas-León and related work study Fourier and Mellin transforms of lisse sheaves, including preservation of irreducibility and monodromy properties.

These results describe the transformed sheaf:

\[
\mathcal F\mapsto\operatorname{Mell}(\mathcal F).
\]

They do not provide a bound on the number of Frobenius traces of the transformed sheaf that vanish.

---

## Katz

Katz proves very strong statements about:

* geometric monodromy;
* equidistribution;
* moments;
* Fourier transforms of sheaves.

But the implication

\[
G_{\rm geom}=SL_2
\Longrightarrow
\#\{\chi:M(\chi)=0\}=O(1)
\]

is not a theorem.

# The precise missing theorem for P3.2

The desired statement should be formulated as a Mellin anti-concentration theorem:

## Apéry Mellin zero anti-concentration conjecture

Let \(\mathcal F\) be the Apéry sheaf and

\[
M_p(\chi)=\sum_t \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Fr}_t|\mathcal F)\chi(t).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{\pi(X)}\sum_{p\le X}
\#\{\chi:M_p(\chi)=0\}
=O(1).
}
\]

A stronger version would be a second moment:

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|^2\ll \pi(X),
\]

which is exactly the zero-mode input needed for M1/M2.

---

# Why SL₂ still matters

The proven SL₂ monodromy is not useless. It rules out obvious algebraic degeneracies:

* reducible sheaf;
* finite monodromy;
* toric decomposition.

It makes the expected distribution random-like.

But the logical chain is:

\[
SL_2\text{ monodromy}
\]

only gives

\[
\text{generic Frobenius randomness}
\]

and the missing step is

\[
\text{Frobenius randomness}
\Rightarrow
\text{Fourier zero anti-concentration}.
\]

That implication is not available in current Katz/FKM theory.

---

# Recommended research target

The realistic next theorem is not "Katz proves AFP" but:

## Mellin zero sieve theorem

For the Apéry Mellin transform sheaf,

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\sum_{\chi}
1_{M_p(\chi)=0}
\ll \pi(X),
\]

or equivalently

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|\ll\pi(X).
\]

A stronger form:

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|^2\ll\pi(X)
\]

would immediately give the M1/M2 part of the GCD conjecture.

At present this is a new arithmetic anti-concentration theorem for Mellin transforms, not a direct corollary of Katz equidistribution.
