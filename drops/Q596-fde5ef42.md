# Effective Stickelberger for the Apéry crystal

## Overview

The Stickelberger/Gross-Koblitz mechanism is the correct local framework, but the Jacobi rank-1 argument does not transfer directly. The key obstruction is cancellation among several equal-weight hypergeometric terms. For the Apéry motive, the relevant object is not a single valuation but the Newton polygon of the twisted hypergeometric motive.

The likely conclusion is:

- a unique minimal Stickelberger term implies nonvanishing mod p;
- vanishing occurs precisely at collision loci of minimal fractional-part weights;
- these collision loci are controlled by the Hodge polygon of the rank-3 crystal.

This gives a geometric reformulation of AFP/HTO, but not an immediate proof of M1.

---

## (A) Individual Gamma-product valuations

For a hypergeometric motive, Gross-Koblitz writes each Frobenius contribution as a product of p-adic Gamma factors. The valuation of a term is determined by a fractional-part sum

$$
v_p(T_m(r)) = s(r,m)=\sum_i\langle \alpha_i(m)+r/(p-1)\rangle-\sum_j\langle\beta_j(m)+r/(p-1)\rangle.
$$

The Apéry parameters are of hypergeometric type

$$
{}_4F_3(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2;1,1,1).
$$

For a fixed twist r, if there exists a unique m minimizing s(r,m), and the minimum is zero, then the corresponding Gamma product is a p-adic unit and no cancellation can remove it. Hence

$$
b_r\not\equiv0\pmod p.
$$

This is the exact analogue of the Jacobi argument.

The difference is that the Apéry Frobenius trace is a rank-3 object, so several m's can lie on the same Newton slope.

---

## (B) When can cancellation occur?

Cancellation requires at least two minimal terms:

$$
 s(r,m_1)=s(r,m_2)=v_{min}(r).
$$

Thus the exceptional set is the set of twists where the Stickelberger polygon has a horizontal face of multiplicity greater than one.

For rank one Jacobi sums this happens on large intervals because the fractional-part function has long flat regions.

For the Apéry 4F3 parameters, the four numerator parameters 1/2 produce a much more symmetric but higher-dimensional fractional-part pattern. The expected behavior is different:

- generic r: one slope-zero contribution dominates;
- exceptional r: several Frobenius terms share the first slope;
- these exceptional r correspond to nonordinary twists.

Therefore the desired bound is not a direct divisor estimate but an ordinarity statement:

$$
|\{r:H_{p,r}=0\}|=O(1)\quad\text{on average over }p.
$$

---

## (C) Computing the minimal Stickelberger valuation

The formal computation is:

$$
v_{min}(r)=\min_m s(r,m).
$$

For the Apéry motive this should be interpreted as the first Newton slope of the Kummer-twisted Frobenius crystal.

The expected relation is:

$$
v_{min}(r)>0 \Longleftrightarrow H_{p,r}=0 \Longleftrightarrow b_r\equiv0\pmod p.
$$

However, the last implication requires excluding cancellation between equal-slope terms. The valuation alone gives a necessary condition, not always sufficient.

The right object is therefore:

$$
\text{initial form of Frobenius at slope }0.
$$

If the slope-zero part is one-dimensional, its Hasse scalar is the surviving leading coefficient.

---

## (D) Shape of the fractional-part sums for Apéry

For parameters

$$
(\alpha_i)=(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2),\qquad(\beta_j)=(1,1,1),
$$

one obtains repeated terms of the form

$$
4\langle 1/2+x\rangle-3\langle x\rangle.
$$

The half-integer symmetry implies a reflection symmetry in x. This explains the observed palindromic zero symmetry.

But unlike Jacobi sums, the numerator multiplicity four means the slope-zero piece is not automatically unique. The correct invariant is the multiplicity of the lowest Newton slope.

The prediction is:

- ordinary twists: slope sequence begins with 0 and multiplicity one;
- exceptional twists: first slope increases or multiplicity jumps.

These exceptional twists are exactly the HTO obstruction.

---

## (E) Existing theory

Yes. The relevant theories are:

1. Dwork's theory of p-adic hypergeometric functions.
2. Adolphson-Sperber Newton polygons of hypergeometric exponential sums.
3. Miyatani's work on Hodge/Newton polygons of hypergeometric motives.

They provide explicit formulas for generic Newton polygons and Hodge polygons, but not automatically the exact zero set of the finite-field trace coefficient.

The missing bridge is:

$$
\text{Newton polygon information}\Rightarrow\text{zero-fiber bound for Mellin coefficients}.
$$

That bridge is essentially the AFP theorem.

---

# Consequence for Apéry GCD

The effective Stickelberger route refines the target:

Instead of proving directly

$$
|Z_p|=O(1),
$$

prove:

1. the twisted Apéry crystal is ordinary for almost all tame characters;
2. the nonordinary twist set has bounded average size;
3. the slope-zero Hasse scalar equals the Mellin coefficient b_r mod p.

Then

$$
Z_p\subseteq E_p^{sl},
$$

and HTO implies M1.

So the Stickelberger approach does not replace AFP; it gives an explicit crystalline description of the AFP exceptional set.
