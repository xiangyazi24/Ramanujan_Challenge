# Cold audit: one-blow-up K3-family local model

## Scope

This audit concerns only the local semistable/regular-model assertion for

\[
F=uv(w-1)(1-uvw)-aw(u-1)(v-1)=0
\]

near a henselian trait whose closed point satisfies \(a^2-34a+1=0\). It does **not** imply any endpoint middle-extension theorem, Frobenius trace identification, or global sheaf statement.

## Executive conclusion

The statement “blowing up the seven fixed singular sections once gives a proper flat regular total space” is too strong without additional chart computations. The weakest publication-ready proposition is:

> After explicit local verification at every singular section, the resulting blow-up is a proper flat relative Cartier model. Regularity follows only if every residual surface singularity in the exceptional charts is checked to be resolved.

The currently stated ingredients do not by themselves prove this proposition. In particular, an A2 surface singularity along a section is not automatically resolved by one ordinary blow-up of that section.

## 1. Local singularity audit

Let \(R\) be the henselian DVR and let \(t\) be a uniformizer at the chosen root of \(a^2-34a+1\). A regular total space requires that in every affine chart the completed local ring after blow-up have no non-regular maximal ideals.

A single blow-up of a section removes a transversal ordinary double point only in the A1 case. For an A1 model

\[
xy=t,
\]

blowing up the singular section separates the two branches and gives the expected regular chart structure.

For an A2 surface singularity, the normal form is instead

\[
xy-z^3=0
\]

(or an equivalent ADE form). Blowing up the singular point/section once produces an exceptional configuration which generally still contains a singular chart. The minimal resolution requires two exceptional (-2)-curves. Therefore:

**Claim:** “persistent A2 resolves after one blow-up” is false unless the relative embedding has additional non-generic structure and that structure is explicitly exhibited.

## 2. Cartier and flatness checks

The strict transform is a relative Cartier divisor only if:

1. the blow-up center is contained in the hypersurface;
2. the total transform has multiplicity one along the exceptional divisor, or the strict transform equation is a non-zero-divisor;
3. no exceptional component becomes an embedded component.

Flatness over \(R\) follows from a miracle-flatness argument only after checking:

- the total space is Cohen--Macaulay;
- the morphism has equidimensional fibers;
- the parameter \(t\) is a non-zero-divisor.

A formal hypersurface equation alone is insufficient after blow-up because exceptional components can appear in the special fiber.

## 3. Boundary divisors and Gram matrix

The asserted list

- 14 boundary strict transforms,
- E1,...,E4,
- one chosen A2 component,

being integral horizontal Cartier divisors with constant Gram matrix 48 is not a consequence of the first blow-up.

Required checks:

* each divisor remains irreducible over the henselian base;
* horizontalness is preserved;
* no divisor splits after passing to the strict transform;
* intersection numbers are computed on the resolved surface, not the singular model.

A constant Gram matrix requires a verified relative intersection pairing. It cannot be inferred from the combinatorial boundary labels.

## 4. Derivative normalization audit

The raw derivative is

\[
\partial_a F=-w(u-1)(v-1).
\]

If the normalized parameter is \(\Lambda=a+a^{-1}\), then

\[
\frac{d\Lambda}{da}=1-a^{-2}=\frac{a^2-1}{a^2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\partial_\Lambda F
=
\frac{da}{d\Lambda}\partial_aF
=
\frac{a^2}{a^2-1}(-w(u-1)(v-1)),
\]

where defined.

The two derivatives differ by a scalar factor. They are not interchangeable in a trace calculation unless the normalization factor is carried through the Gauss--Manin connection. A claim proved using raw \(dF/da\) does not automatically prove the normalized \(dF/d\Lambda\) statement.

## 5. What is actually proved by the local blow-up calculation

A valid local proposition would be:

> Given explicit affine charts for the blow-up and assuming the residual ADE singularities are resolved, the strict transform is a relative Cartier hypersurface. Under the additional Cohen--Macaulay and fiber-dimension hypotheses it is flat over the trait.

This is weaker than “one blow-up gives a regular proper flat model”.

## 6. Remaining hypotheses required for publication

The following must be supplied:

1. Complete affine chart list for all seven singular sections.
2. Jacobian computation before blow-up identifying A1 versus A2 loci.
3. Explicit exceptional-chart equations after blow-up.
4. Proof that no A2 chart remains singular, or a further resolution step.
5. Proof that the strict transform is Cartier everywhere.
6. Proof that the special fiber has no embedded components.
7. Flatness proof over the henselian DVR.
8. Irreducibility/horizontalness proof for all claimed divisors.
9. Direct intersection computation giving the claimed Gram matrix 48.
10. Separate argument connecting the resolved geometric model to any middle-extension theorem.

## Final audit verdict

The one-blow-up regular-model statement is not established from the stated information. The main obstruction is the A2 locus: ordinary one-step blow-up is not a general resolution of an A2 surface singularity. The correct publication claim must be weakened to a conditional local-model proposition until the exceptional charts and intersection theory are explicitly verified.

No endpoint middle-extension conclusion follows from this local audit.
