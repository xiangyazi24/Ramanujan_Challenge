# Long-transfer estimate after short endpoint conditioning: status audit

## 1. Target statement

The desired bound is not another estimate for
\[
E_p^{sep}=\#\{(x,s,G,t):N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=N_t(x+s+G)=0,\ s,t\leq \sqrt p\},
\]
because this is only a rewriting of the original separated-chain problem.

The missing ingredient is a genuine non-concentration statement after conditioning on the two short returns:

> For most short endpoint pairs \((x,s,t)\), the intermediate transfer length \(G\) cannot hit a second zero of the Apéry continuant at the expected \(1/p\) rate.

A usable form would be
\[
\sum_{\substack{x,s,t\\s,t\le H}}
\left(
R(x,s,t)-\frac{L}{p}
\right)^2
\ll X^{2+o(1)}/p,
\]
where
\[
R(x,s,t)=\#\{G\in I:N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=N_t(x+s+G)=0\}
\]
and \(|I|=L\) is the long-transfer range.

This is the missing large-sieve input. No currently standard incidence theorem gives this estimate.

---

## 2. Why additive/multiplicative character expansion does not close

The condition
\[
N_G(y)=0
\]
is equivalent to equality of projective states
\[
\pi_y=\pi_{y+G}.
\]
Writing the PGL\(_2\) transition matrix as \(M_G(y)\), this is a rational equation of bounded degree in the parameters, but the parameter \(G\) is not an independent additive variable: it is an iterate length.

A character expansion would require control of sums such as
\[
\sum_{y,G}\chi(F_G(y)),
\]
where \(F_G\) has degree growing with \(G\). For \(G\leq p^{1/2}\), the degree is already of order \(p^{1/2}\), so Deligne-type bounds are unavailable in their standard fixed-conductor form.

The recurrence identity
\[
N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x)
\]
does not linearize the dependence on \(h\). Thus there is no currently available square-root cancellation theorem for these character sums.

---

## 3. PGL2 incidence route: precise obstruction

A possible reduction is to view the state evolution as a projective action
\[
\pi_{n+1}=T(n)\pi_n,
\]
with \(T(n)\in \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\).

The desired estimate would follow from a point-plane incidence bound for the set
\[
\mathcal P=\{(\pi_x,\pi_{x+s},\pi_{x+s+G})\}
\]
with the third coordinate restricted by a short endpoint condition.

However, standard Rudnev/Vinh bounds give only a schematic estimate
\[
I(P,\Pi)\ll |P|^{3/4}|\Pi|^{3/4}+|P|+|\Pi|,
\]
or in the balanced case an error of size approximately
\[
p^{3/2}.
\]

Here the desired saving is one full factor of \(p\): after fixing the two short endpoints, the number of surviving long transfers should be \(L/p\), not the incidence error scale \(p^{1/2}\).

Therefore generic incidence gives the wrong exponent.

---

## 4. Quotient-ring bridge formulation

The strongest clean reduction is through the bridge residue algebra.

Fix \((x,s)\) with \(N_s(x)=0\). Then the state at \(x+s\) is fixed relative to \(x\). The long return condition becomes
\[
N_G(x+s)=0.
\]
Modulo the short-return ideal generated by \(N_s(x)\), the transfer matrices live in a finite quotient algebra.

The missing estimate is equivalent to:

**Long-return nonconcentration lemma.**  Let \(A\) be the quotient algebra obtained after imposing a nondegenerate short endpoint relation. For any interval \(I\subseteq[1,p]\),
\[
\#\{G\in I: \rho_G=1\text{ in }A\}
\leq \frac{|I|}{p}+O(p^{1/2+o(1)}),
\]
uniformly for all admissible short endpoints outside an exceptional set of size \(p^{o(1)}\).

This lemma is exactly the additional theorem required. It is not a reformulation of \(E_p^{sep}\), because the conditioning has already removed the two short-chain constraints.

---

## 5. Prime averaging does not automatically supply the missing factor

Averaging over primes could help if one had a trace-function description with bounded conductor:
\[
\sum_{p\sim X}\sum_G \mathrm{Tr}(\rho_p(G)).
\]

But the recurrence transfer of length \(G\) has conductor depending on \(G\). In the required range
\[
G>\sqrt{2X}/\sqrt{\log X},
\]
the conductor growth destroys the standard Bombieri--Vinogradov style averaging range.

Thus prime averaging alone does not produce the needed \(1/p\) gain.

---

## 6. What has actually been proved versus what remains

The existing resultant argument handles
\[
G\leq \sqrt{2X}/\sqrt{\log X}.
\]
It works because two bounded-degree continuants cannot have too many simultaneous roots unless the resultant vanishes.

For larger \(G\), the polynomial degree of the transfer condition is too large, and the resultant method loses its bounded-complexity advantage.

The remaining theorem is therefore equivalent to the following new arithmetic input:

```
Short-conditioned Apéry long-return nonconcentration:
After fixing a nondegenerate pair of short return endpoints,
long return lengths G behave with probability 1/p up to X^{o(1)}.
```

This is stronger than:

* Hasse squareness,
* reciprocity,
* palindromicity,
* generic PGL2 incidence,
* generic finite-field point-plane bounds.

Those structural facts permit too many artificial reciprocal trajectories. They do not detect that the actual Apéry vector is one special orbit generated by the recurrence.

---

## Conclusion

No unconditional long-transfer theorem follows from currently available generic incidence machinery. The exact remaining bottleneck is a short-conditioned nonconcentration theorem for Apéry transfer iterates. Any successful completion of P32 must prove a new statement of this form (probably using additional arithmetic structure of the specific Apéry recurrence), not merely apply Hasse squareness or reciprocity.
