# 4. Cartier–Mellin and the rank-one selector

The canonical Cartier–Mellin representation writes the Apéry value, in the source normalization, as a Mellin transform of a bounded-rank trace function:

\[
b_x\equiv-\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
             \vartheta_p(u)u^x\pmod p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is linear information about the value `b_x`.  The distinguished actual-zero selector is nonlinear:

\[
z_p(x)=\mathbf1_{\{b_x=0\}}
 =\frac1p\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb F_p}
    e_p(\lambda b_x).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Substituting (4.1) into (4.2) gives

\[
e_p\!\left(
 -\lambda\sum_u\vartheta_p(u)u^x
\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Standard Deligne bounds for the trace function `vartheta_p` do not give a bounded-conductor estimate for the exponential of its full Mellin transform.  Equivalently, the polynomial identity

Summing (4.2) one `lambda` at a time costs `p` frequencies.  Cauchy in `lambda` returns the Parseval mass in Section 3 and its physical diagonal. # 5. The selected fourth endpoint does not create an independent codegree

Let `B_n=(n!)^3b_n` and let `D_n` be the canonical companion solution.  On `0<=n<p`, the Casoratian is a unit, so the projective state

\[
\pi_n=[B_n:D_n]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)
\tag{5.1}
\]

is defined.  The canonical projective-orbit identity is

\[
N_h(x)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\pi_x=\pi_{x+h}
\tag{5.2}
\]

on the nonwrapping regular locus.

Therefore, if

\[
K^{(4)}_{p;a,b,c}(x)\ne0,
\]

then

\[
\pi_x=\pi_{x+a}=\pi_{x+a+b}=\pi_{x+a+b+c}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The actual zero fibre is

\[
\mathcal Z_p=\pi^{-1}([0:1]).
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
z_p(x)=z_p(x+a)=z_p(x+a+b)=z_p(x+a+b+c).}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus

\[
z_p(x)z_p(x+a)z_p(x+a+b)z_p(x+a+b+c)=z_p(x).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Even two centered endpoint selectors do not become an independent second moment.  On a return chain,

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_p(x)f_p(x+a)
 &=f_p(x)^2\\
 &=(1-2\rho_p)f_p(x)+\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The last term is a positive diagonal.  The first term is the original one-selector discrepancy.  Higher products give the same phenomenon.

Hence the correct candidate baseline for a selected quadruple is still

\[
\rho_p\times\#\{\text{candidate quadruple starts}\},
\]

not `rho_p^2`, `rho_p^3`, or `rho_p^4` times that count.

# 8. The first exact failed implication

The second arrow is true only at the candidate-incidence level.  The third is false.  Equations (5.3)–(5.6) show that the two occupied cells carry the same projective-state bit.  Their product has a linear distinguished term and a positive diagonal.

No presently standard candidate-root theorem supplies it.

# 9. Weakest sufficient new lemma

Let

\[
\mathscr Q^{\rm hi,simp}_p(P,H)
\]

be the literal multiset of selected quadruple occurrences `(x,a,b,c)` satisfying:

```text

(a,b) in L_H;
ab > H^2 P^(-4/159)     [or the slightly smaller strict-theta cutoff];
first returns a,b,c at the same actual start chain;
nonwrapping/headroom/fourth-endpoint masks;
the two unresolved first-return roots are simple;
every remaining literal selector;
full occurrence multiplicity retained.
```

A concrete stronger-than-necessary arithmetic lemma is:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
  |\mathscr Q^{\rm hi,simp}_p(P,H)|_{\rm occ}
 \ll P^{8/5-\gamma+o(1)},
\qquad
\gamma>\frac{41}{3975}.}
\tag{DASR$_\gamma$}
\]

Here `|.|_occ` is the literal occurrence mass, not the number of numerical supports.  Since a positive centered discrepancy is at most its actual occupied mass, `(DASR_gamma)` plus the already-proved low-product and singular estimates closes the selected `d=2`/quadruple consumer.

The exactly weakest centered version is `(ASTD_gamma)` in Section 2.  It permits cancellation against the candidate expectation and exceptional dense lines.  But as a logical statement it is essentially the unresolved selected consumer written in Fourier form.

`(DASR_gamma)` is narrower and more arithmetic than a generic CAR or resultant hypothesis: it asks only for distinguished, literal, high-product, simple, selected first-return starts.  Nevertheless, with current tools it is still a new first-moment theorem rather than a consequence of a known large sieve.

A mechanism-level theorem that would imply it is a growing-family actual-state large sieve:

\[
\sup_{0\le\lambda\le1}
\operatorname{Re}
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\frac{\log p}{p}
 \sum_{k\ne0}F_p(k)
  \widehat G^{(4)}_{p,\lambda}(-k)
 \ll P^{8/5-\gamma+o(1)}.
\tag{ASLS$_\gamma$}
\]

Unlike the ordinary additive large sieve, `(ASLS_gamma)` must exploit the arithmetic relation between the Mellin-transformed Apéry sheaf and the growing continuant first-return cylinders.  No currently standard bounded-conductor or fixed-gap theorem supplies that relation.  It is plausibly a genuine new monodromy/nonconcentration theorem, not a routine application; absent such a mechanism, `(ASTD_gamma)` is merely an equivalent reduction.

A weighted all-line form may replace the left side by the exact outer/source/host occurrence weights from the selected consumer.  The same exponent `gamma>41/3975` is required after those already-proved weights are factored.  No normalization by selected mass is allowed.

# 11. Final conclusion

The selected fourth endpoint is useful only as an additional **candidate geometry** condition.  It does not raise the number of independent actual-state tests, because every return endpoint lies in the same projective fibre.  Once low-product and singular cells are removed, the residual obstruction is a collection of simple, high-product, distinguished singleton starts.  Such starts may form a matching of selected quadruples and saturate

\[
P^{8/5+o(1)}
\]

while obeying every currently stated variance, degree, first-return, and codegree constraint.

Therefore the missing `41/3975` is not available from ordinary gcd height, generic candidate roots, a scalar resultant, a routine two-coordinate large sieve, or the fourth endpoint alone.  The first genuinely new input must be a prime-aspect power saving for the **distinguished actual projective state inside growing first-return cylinders**, in the exact form `(ASTD_gamma)` or a concrete stronger form such as `(DASR_gamma)`.
