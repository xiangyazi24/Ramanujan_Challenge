## 1. **HOLDS, with a necessary domain correction:** the projective transfer and restart

For \(u\ne-1\), write the map in homogeneous coordinates as

\[
M_u=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&(u+1)^6\\
-1&P(u)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_u[X:Z]=[(u+1)^6Z:-X+P(u)Z].
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\det M_u=(u+1)^6.
\]

Thus \(M_u\in\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\) exactly when \(u\ne-1\). On the allowed domain,

\[
M_u(\infty)=M_u[1:0]=[0:-1]=0.
\]

For \(1\le n\le p-2\), the state is

\[
x_n=\left[n^3b_{n-1}:b_n\right],
\]

or \(x_n=n^3b_{n-1}/b_n\) on the affine chart. The recurrence gives

\[
x_{n+1}=\frac{(n+1)^6}{P(n)-x_n}=F_{p,n}(x_n).
\]

Since \(n\ne0\pmod p\) and consecutive Apéry zeros do not occur, \(b_n=0\) is equivalent to \(x_n=\infty\). Therefore

\[
b_{z_i}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_{z_i}=\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_{z_i+1}=0.
\]

This part is sound.

The correction is that the sentence “\(F_u(\infty)=0\) for every \(u\)” must say **for every allowed \(u\ne-1\)**. At \(u=-1\),

\[
M_{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\-1&-5\end{pmatrix}
\]

has determinant zero and has a base point at \(x=-5\). It is not a Möbius transformation. The true orbit never uses this parameter, but cyclic test windows can.

## 2. **FIXABLE but dangerous:** the proof currently overloads \(N_m\) with two different index conventions

This is not cosmetic. It changes the degree, the parity of the forced center factor, and every off-by-one in the restart identity.

### 2.1 The exact \(m\)-step denominator

Define

\[
U_{-1}(s)=0,\qquad U_0(s)=1,\qquad U_1(s)=P(s),
\]

and

\[
U_{m+1}(s)=P(s+m)U_m(s)-(s+m)^6U_{m-1}(s).
\tag{2.1}
\]

A direct multiplication gives, for \(m\ge1\),

\[
M_{s+m-1}\cdots M_s[0:1]
=
\left[(s+m)^6U_{m-1}(s):U_m(s)\right].
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus, on an admissible window,

\[
\boxed{
\text{the state after exactly \(m\) maps is \(\infty\)}
\iff U_m(s)=0.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Over \(\mathbf Q\),

\[
\deg U_m=3m.
\]

Indeed the leading coefficients satisfy

\[
c_0=1,\qquad c_1=34,\qquad c_{m+1}=34c_m-c_{m-1},
\]

and are positive.

### 2.2 The banked gap polynomial in `proof.tex`

The existing project polynomial \(N_h(r)\) encodes two zero positions separated by \(h\). It has

\[
N_1=1,\qquad N_2(r)=P(r+1),\qquad \deg N_h=3(h-1).
\]

The exact relation is

\[
\boxed{N_h(r)=U_{h-1}(r+1),
\qquad U_m(s)=N_{m+1}(s-1).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently:

* first zero \(z_1\):
  \[
  U_{z_1}(0)=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  N_{z_1+1}(-1)=0;
  \]
* consecutive zeros \(z_i<z_{i+1}\), with positional gap
  \(h=z_{i+1}-z_i\):
  \[
  N_h(z_i)=0;
  \]
* the number of maps after the restart is
  \[
  m=h-1=z_{i+1}-z_i-1,
  \]
  and the exact \(m\)-step statement is
  \[
  U_m(z_i+1)=0.
  \]

The symmetry also shifts:

\[
N_h(-r-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(r),
\]

whereas

\[
\boxed{U_m(-s-m)=(-1)^mU_m(s).}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Therefore the forced center root occurs for **even \(h\)** in the old \(N_h\) notation, but for **odd \(m\)** in the exact-map \(U_m\) notation.

The current ledger’s phrases “\(N_m=U_m\), degree \(3m\), center for odd \(m\)” silently switch from the established \(N_h\) convention to the exact-map convention. A formal document must not do this. Rename the exact-map polynomial \(U_m\) or \(D_m\) and retain \(N_h\) for the established gap polynomial.

## 3. **HOLDS:** the short-gap injection and the \(p/H\) tail

Let

\[
1\le z_1<z_2<\cdots<z_k\le p-2
\]

be the zeros. For \(1\le i\le k-1\), define

\[
m_i=z_{i+1}-z_i-1,\qquad s_i=z_i+1.
\]

No consecutive zeros implies \(m_i\ge1\). The restart identity and (2.3) give

\[
U_{m_i}(s_i)=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Moreover the window is genuinely nonwrapping:

\[
s_i+m_i-1=z_{i+1}-1\le p-3.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The map \(i\mapsto(s_i,m_i)\) is injective. Hence every inter-zero gap with \(m_i\le H\) contributes a distinct root pair to any count containing all such admissible starts.

The long-gap estimate is exact:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}m_i
=z_k-z_1-(k-1)
\le p-3.
\tag{3.3}
\]

If \(m_i>H\), then \(m_i\ge H+1\), so

\[
\#\{i:m_i>H\}\le\frac{p-3}{H+1}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The first zero is handled by the additive \(1\). Thus, defining for example

\[
R_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,H)
=
\sum_{m=1}^H
\#\left\{
1\le s\le p-m-1:\ U_m(s)=0
\right\},
\tag{3.5}
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
|Z_p|
\le
1+R_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,H)
+\left\lfloor\frac{p-3}{H+1}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The actual restart starts satisfy the stricter bounds \(s_i\ge2\) and \(s_i\le p-m_i-2\), so (3.5) is a harmless enlargement.

There is no leakage at \(p-1\): \(b_{p-1}\equiv b_0\equiv1\pmod p\), so \(p-1\) is not a zero.

## 4. Attack point (a): **HOLDS on admissible windows; FIXABLE globally**

### 4.1 Intermediate \(\infty\)-hits do not corrupt the polynomial condition

Put

\[
G_m(s)=M_{s+m-1}\cdots M_s.
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\Delta_m(s)=\prod_{j=1}^m(s+j)^6.
\tag{4.1}
\]

If \(\Delta_m(s)\ne0\), every factor is a genuine Möbius map and \(G_m(s)\) is invertible. By (2.2), if \(U_m(s)=0\), then the top coordinate

\[
(s+m)^6U_{m-1}(s)
\]

cannot also vanish. Therefore the final projective state is genuinely \(\infty\); there is no removable \(0/0\), no cancellation, and no spurious root.

Intermediate hits are automatically handled. If \(U_a(s)=0\) on an admissible window, then the state after \(a\) maps is \(\infty\), after the next map it is \(0\), and subsequent maps resume from the restart state. Algebraically,

\[
U_{a+1+b}(s)
\equiv
U_{a+1}(s)\,U_b(s+a+1)
\pmod{U_a(s)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently, at any admissible root of \(U_a\),

\[
U_{a+1+b}(s)=0
\iff
U_b(s+a+1)=0,
\tag{4.3}
\]

because \(U_{a+1}(s)\ne0\).

This is the exact renewal identity. It proves that an iterator which **continues after every \(\infty\)-hit** counts the same \(m\)-th-step event as \(U_m(s)=0\).

### 4.2 The honest algebraic object is locally closed, not just \(U_m=0\)

The full polynomial root set is

\[
\{s:U_m(s)=0\}.
\]

The actual projective-dynamics root set is

\[
\boxed{
\{s:U_m(s)=0,\ \Delta_m(s)\ne0\}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Scheme-theoretically, one should restrict to the open set \(\Delta_m\ne0\), or saturate by \(\Delta_m\). Any common factor of the two coordinates in (2.2) can only lie over \(\Delta_m=0\); an adjugate/determinant argument shows that their gcd divides a power of the determinant product.

Thus:

* **intermediate \(\infty\)** is not a problem;
* **specialization at a window containing \(u=-1\)** is the only source of base-locus/boundary ambiguity.

### 4.3 Degree and non-identical vanishing

The degree bound is

\[
\deg U_m=3m
\]

over \(\mathbf Q\), and at most \(3m\) modulo \(p\). A leading-coefficient drop modulo \(p\) can occur, but only lowers the degree.

More importantly, \(U_m\) is not the zero polynomial modulo \(p\) in the intended range. The banked nonvanishing theorem for \(N_h\) says that for \(p\ge7\) and \(h\le p\), \(N_h\not\equiv0\pmod p\). Since

\[
U_m(s)=N_{m+1}(s-1),
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{U_m\not\equiv0\pmod p\qquad(0\le m\le p-1).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The proposed \(H\asymp\sqrt p\) lies safely inside this range.

## 5. Attack point (b): **FIXABLE; the inequality direction survives, but two different statistics are being conflated**

A length-\(m\) window starting at the standard representative \(s\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\) avoids \(u=p-1=-1\) exactly when

\[
0\le s\le p-m-1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every genuine restart window satisfies the stronger (3.2), so discarding wrapping starts does **not** discard any root used in the short-gap injection. Therefore the combinatorial inequality may legitimately use the restricted count computed by a break-at-\(-1\) iterator.

The logical error would be to define

\[
R_{\mathrm{alg}}(p,H)=
\sum_{m\le H}\#\{s\in\mathbf F_p:U_m(s)=0\}
\tag{5.2}
\]

and then substitute an experimental number produced from only nonwrapping starts. These are not the same statistic.

Use distinct names:

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{alg}}(p,H)
&=\sum_{m\le H}\#\{s\in\mathbf F_p:U_m(s)=0\},\\
R_{\mathrm{nw}}(p,H)
&=\sum_{m\le H}\#\{0\le s\le p-m-1:U_m(s)=0\},\\
R_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,H)
&=\sum_{m\le H}\#\{1\le s\le p-m-1:U_m(s)=0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
R_{\mathrm{rel}}\le R_{\mathrm{nw}}\le R_{\mathrm{alg}},
\]

and (3.6) uses \(R_{\mathrm{rel}}\) or any larger count.

A theorem for \(R_{\mathrm{alg}}\) is sufficient. An experiment for \(R_{\mathrm{nw}}\) is relevant to the combinatorial bound. But empirical smallness of \(R_{\mathrm{nw}}\) is not automatically evidence that the full algebraic count \(R_{\mathrm{alg}}\) has the same value.

### 5.1 The wrapping roots are arithmetically structured, not random noise

For an invalid start \(s=-j-1\), \(0\le j\le m-1\), the endpoint evaluation in `proof.tex` gives

\[
\boxed{
U_m(-j-1)
=(-1)^{j+1}
 b_{j+1}b_{m-j-1}
 ((j+1)!)^3((m-j-1)!)^3.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For \(m<p\), the factorials are units modulo \(p\). Thus a wrapping specialization is a root precisely when one of two shorter Apéry numbers vanishes modulo \(p\).

This has two consequences:

1. wrap contamination can be measured exactly and should be reported, not silently mixed into the main statistic;
2. for growing \(m\), full-root boundary contamination is tied to the same unknown zero process and can become circular.

For fixed \(m\), these boundary values are nonzero integers, so only finitely many primes see them vanish; hence they do not change the fixed-\(m\) Chebotarev mean. Uniformly for \(m\) growing with \(p\), that finite-exception observation is useless.

## 6. Attack point (c): **HOLDS**

The proposed \(p/H\) tail is valid, and the exact accounting is cleaner than stated.

For inter-zero map gaps \(m_i=z_{i+1}-z_i-1\),

\[
\sum_i m_i\le p-3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\#\{i:m_i>H\}
\le\frac{p-3}{H+1}
<\frac pH.
\]

If one also includes the first gap \(z_1\), then

\[
z_1+\sum_i m_i=z_k-k+1\le p-k-1,
\]

so the same conclusion holds. The additive \(1\) in (3.6) makes including the first gap unnecessary.

No boundary zero at \(n=p-1\) exists, and \(n=p-2\) causes no wrap: the last map used to reach it is \(u=p-3\).

## 8. Attack point (d): **FATAL to the interpretation of the constant \(1.36\)**

### 8.1 The exact forced baseline is \(H/2\)

From

\[
U_m(-s-m)=(-1)^mU_m(s),
\]

odd \(m\) has the forced center root

\[
s_c=-m/2=(p-m)/2\in\mathbf F_p.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For \(m\le p-2\), this representative satisfies

\[
1\le s_c\le p-m-1,
\]

so it is nonwrapping and belongs to the relevant interior count. Therefore

\[
R_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,H)\ge\left\lceil\frac H2\right\rceil.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The interval \(1\le s\le p-m-1\) is invariant under \(s\mapsto -s-m\), so all noncenter roots occur in pairs. Hence

\[
\boxed{
r_m^{\mathrm{rel}}
=\mathbf 1_{m\text{ odd}}+2q_m(p)}
\tag{8.3}
\]

for an integer \(q_m(p)\ge0\).

Thus the useful experimental statistic is not merely \(R/H\), but

\[
\frac1H\sum_{m\le H}q_m(p)
=
\frac{R_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,H)-\lceil H/2\rceil}{2H}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

The reported \(1.36H\) corresponds to approximately \(0.43H\) noncenter root pairs.

### 8.2 Conditional Chebotarev predicts \(3H/2\), not \(1.36H\)

The current ledger’s factor law is only machine-verified for \(m\le12\):

* odd \(m\): center linear factor times one primitive irreducible factor;
* even \(m\): one primitive irreducible factor.

Assume, as a heuristic, that this persists for all \(m\), and that bad discriminant primes are excluded. For a fixed polynomial, the prime-average number of roots is the number of irreducible factors over \(\mathbf Q\). Therefore the fixed-\(m\) prediction is

\[
\mathbb E_p r_m=
\begin{cases}
2,&m\text{ odd},\\
1,&m\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\tag{8.5}
\]

A mixed parity average predicts

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_p R(p,H)\sim\frac32H.}
\tag{8.6}
\]

The reflection-paired primitive factor is expected to have hyperoctahedral-type fixed-point statistics. In the large-degree model,

\[
r_m-\mathbf1_{m\text{ odd}}
\approx2K_m,
\qquad K_m\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(1/2),
\tag{8.7}
\]

so the primitive contribution has mean \(1\), not \(0.86\).

Under the crude additional fiction that different \(m\)'s are independent, the variance per primitive root count is \(2\), so

\[
\operatorname{sd}\left(\frac{R(p,H)}H\right)
\approx\sqrt{\frac2H}.
\tag{8.8}
\]

At \(H=100\), this is about \(0.141\). The value \(1.36\) is only about one such heuristic standard deviation below \(1.50\). It is entirely compatible with finite-\(H\) fluctuation; it is not evidence for a new constant.

The fact that all five reported values lie below \(1.5\) merits checking, but possible explanations include horizontal correlations, omitted boundary starts, or an implementation that does not count repeat hits. It does not justify promoting \(1.36\) to a law.

### 8.3 Mixing the first few \(m\)'s cannot be diagnosed from one aggregate

Small \(m\) can distort \(H=100\), but the fix is trivial: report dyadic and trimmed means. At minimum give separate rows for

\[
[1,4],\ [5,8],\ [9,16],\ [17,32],\ [33,64],\ [65,100],
\]

and parity-separated means in each block. Also report the same statistics after removing \(m\le8\) and \(m\le16\).

A single aggregate \(R/H\) cannot distinguish a small-\(m\) artifact from a genuine family effect.

### 8.4 The clean experiment must separate vertical and horizontal averages

Chebotarev is a **vertical** statement: fix \(m\), vary \(p\).

The desired theorem is a **horizontal pointwise** statement: fix \(p\), sum all \(m\le\sqrt p\).

These must be measured separately.

For each fixed \(m\le M_0\), average \(r_m(p)\) over thousands of primes and compare with (8.5). Separately, for each prime compute horizontal sums with

\[
H\in\{32,64,128,256,\lfloor p^{1/3}\rfloor,
\lfloor p^{1/2}\rfloor\},
\]

where applicable. Use many primes in each dyadic \(p\)-range, not one or two representatives. Cluster error bars by prime, since the \(m\)-values are strongly dependent.

For every \(p,H\), report:

\[
\begin{array}{l}
R_{\mathrm{alg}}/H,\quad
R_{\mathrm{nw}}/H,\quad
R_{\mathrm{rel}}/H,\\[2mm]
\bigl(R_{\mathrm{rel}}-\lceil H/2\rceil\bigr)/(2H),\\[2mm]
\max_{m\le H}r_m,\quad
\text{dyadic block means},
\quad\text{root-count histogram}.
\end{array}
\]

The current \(p\le1.6\cdot10^4,H=100\) experiment does not probe a stable asymptotic regime: \(H\) is much larger than \(\sqrt p\) near \(p=1009\), but slightly smaller than \(\sqrt p\) near \(p=16001\).

## 9. The proposed intermediate-restart factorization explanation is **FATAL**

A decomposition

\[
m=a+1+b
\]

does **not** produce a factor \(U_a(s)\) of \(U_m(s)\). Equation (4.2) says only

\[
U_m(s)\equiv U_{a+1}(s)U_b(s+a+1)\pmod{U_a(s)}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Therefore an \(s\) with an intermediate hit at time \(a\) and a final hit at time \(m\) is a common root satisfying the additional shifted condition

\[
U_a(s)=0,\qquad U_b(s+a+1)=0.
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is an **intersection/common-root condition across different levels**, not a factorization of the single polynomial \(U_m\).

For \(U_a\mid U_m\), every root of \(U_a\) would have to satisfy the shifted \(U_b\)-condition. Nothing in restart implies that, and the project’s adjacent-resultant identities show strong coprimality rather than automatic divisibility.

Thus there is no composition-tree formula of the form

\[
\#\text{factors}(U_m)=
\sum_{m=a+1+b}\cdots,
\]

and no reason for \(1.36\) to be a geometric series such as \(\sum_j2^{-j}\). Restart governs correlations between root sets at different \(m\); it does not proliferate rational factors of each \(U_m\).

The current machine factorization evidence through \(m\le12\)—only the forced center plus one primitive factor—is direct evidence against the proposed factor-tree interpretation in the tested range.

\ll H L(p),
\qquad L(p)=p^{o(1)}.
\tag{10.1}

## 11. The claimed route from experiment/Chebotarev to the premise is **FATAL**

This is the central break.

### 11.1 Fixed-polynomial Chebotarev is not the needed theorem

For each fixed \(m\), irreducibility and Chebotarev can control the average of \(r_m(p)\) as \(p\) varies. The desired estimate is

\[
\sum_{m\le\sqrt p}r_m(p)
\ll\sqrt p\,p^{o(1)}
\tag{11.1}
\]

for the same prime \(p\), pointwise.

Interchanging these two directions requires precisely the missing family-uniformity/large-sieve statement. A polynomial irreducible over \(\mathbf Q\) may split completely modulo infinitely many primes. The fact that the completely split primes have tiny density for each fixed \(m\) does not prevent a given prime from being exceptional for several \(m\)'s, and does not bound the horizontal sum (11.1).

### 11.2 The factor law is only checked through \(m\le12\)

The current repo state has machine evidence, not an all-\(m\) theorem. Even an all-\(m\) irreducibility theorem would only identify the vertical mean. It would not prove the pointwise horizontal bound.

### 11.3 Bad and ramified primes accumulate with the family

For fixed \(m\), finitely many primes divide the discriminant or endpoint values. For all \(m\le\sqrt p\), the union of bad-prime sets grows with \(p\). A proof must control this union and repeated-root/splitting anomalies uniformly. One cannot discard “finitely many bad primes for each \(m\)” after \(m\) itself grows with \(p\).

### 11.4 An average-over-primes theorem would give only an average/most-prime conclusion

Even a strong dyadic estimate

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}R(p,\sqrt X)
\ll \pi(X)\sqrt X\,X^{o(1)}
\tag{11.2}
\]

would initially yield an average bound for \(Z_p\), and by Markov a bound for most primes with a loss. It would not yield (11.1) for every prime.

The current ledger’s proposed small-gap Chebotarev theorem with \(M(X)\) only of logarithmic size cannot feed the \(p/H\) tail: taking \(H\) polylogarithmic leaves \(p/H\) essentially linear. The ledger itself notes that the tail remains uncontrolled. That is not a minor technicality; it prevents the square-root conclusion.

### 11.5 The fixed \(H=100\) data do not test the required statement

At fixed \(H\), the tail in (3.6) is \(p/100\). No matter how small \(R(p,100)\) is, this does not improve the asymptotic exponent. The experiment becomes directly relevant only when \(H\) scales near \(\sqrt p\) over a substantial range of larger primes.

## 12. Additional hostile findings

### 12.1 **FIXABLE:** distinct roots versus multiplicities

The iterator counts distinct starts \(s\). The polynomial degree bound counts roots with multiplicity and therefore remains an upper bound, but Chebotarev statements about root counts should explicitly mean distinct \(\mathbf F_p\)-roots and should exclude or separately handle discriminant primes.

### 12.2 **FIXABLE:** the experiment’s arbitrary restart family contains many phantom gaps

Most roots \(U_m(s)=0\) do not arise from actual Apéry zero positions; they are potential gaps from an artificially initialized state \(0\). This is harmless for an upper bound but explains why \(R\) can have a deterministic \(H/2\) baseline even when \(Z_p\) is tiny. The experiment is measuring a deliberately loose envelope, not the actual gap process.

### 12.3 **HOLDS:** the center roots are admissible

For odd \(m<p-1\), \(s=(p-m)/2\) avoids \(u=-1\) throughout the window. Thus the \(H/2\) baseline is not a wrap artifact. If such a center root is realized by an actual consecutive zero pair, then

\[
2(z_i+1)+m_i\equiv0\pmod p
\]

becomes

\[
z_i+z_{i+1}=p-1,
\]

which is exactly the reflection pairing.

### 12.4 **FATAL to a shortcut:** bounded total collision energy alone is insufficient

The new projective-orbit reframing may identify \(R\) with short-difference collisions of one orbit. Even if total collision energy over all differences is \(O(p)\), it does not force \(O(H)\) collisions in the first \(H\) differences; all off-diagonal collisions could concentrate at short gaps. A local difference-distribution estimate is still required.

### 12.5 **FIXABLE:** the target statement should be named precisely

The actual missing pointwise theorem is something like:

> For every \(\varepsilon>0\), uniformly for primes \(p>17\) and \(1\le H\le p^{1/2}\),
> \[
> \sum_{m\le H}\#\{1\le s\le p-m-1:U_m(s)=0\}
> \ll_\varepsilon H p^\varepsilon.
> \]

This is not a corollary of the current factorization data. It is the substantive final-strike target.

## Corrected theorem package that is safe to bank now

### Proposition A: exact \(m\)-step denominator

For \(m\ge0\), define \(U_m\) by (2.1). For every prime \(p>17\), every \(m\le p-1\), and every \(s\in\mathbf F_p\) with

\[
\prod_{j=1}^m(s+j)\ne0,
\]

the composite of the \(m\) allowed transfer maps sends \(0\) to \(\infty\) if and only if \(U_m(s)=0\). The polynomial \(U_m\) has degree at most \(3m\) modulo \(p\) and is not identically zero.

### Proposition B: restart-gap inequality

For every \(1\le H\le p-2\),

\[
|Z_p|
\le
1+\sum_{m=1}^H
\#\{1\le s\le p-m-1:U_m(s)=0\}
+\left\lfloor\frac{p-3}{H+1}\right\rfloor.
\]

### Proposition C: reflection baseline

For every \(m<p-1\),

\[
U_m(-s-m)=(-1)^mU_m(s).
\]

On the invariant relevant interval \(1\le s\le p-m-1\), the root count is

\[
r_m=\mathbf1_{m\text{ odd}}+2q_m.
\]

In particular,

\[
R_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,H)\ge\lceil H/2\rceil.
\]

### Conditional corollary

A uniform pointwise bound

\[
R_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,H)\ll H p^{o(1)}
\]

at \(H\asymp\sqrt p\) implies

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{1/2+o(1)}.
\]

This corollary is correct, but its hypothesis is currently unproved.

## Final verdict

**Not safe to bank the chain as a square-root theorem or as compelling evidence that such a theorem is nearly proved.** The restart identity, exact matrix polynomial, and gap-tail inequality are good and should be retained after the notation/domain repair. The reported \(1.36\) should be demoted to an unaudited pilot statistic until its script and raw tables land.

The structural heuristic should also be corrected:

\[
R(p,H)\ 	ext{is predicted to have mean about}\ \frac32H,
\]

with \(H/2\) forced center roots and about \(H/2\) expected primitive root pairs. Intermediate restarts create cross-\(m\) correlations, not a factor-composition series.

The route itself is not killed. But the remaining step is exactly the hard one:

\[
\boxed{
\text{prove a pointwise horizontal family bound for }m\le\sqrt p.}
\]

Neither the fixed-\(H\) experiment, the \(m\le12\) factorization certificates, nor ordinary fixed-polynomial Chebotarev currently supplies that bound.
