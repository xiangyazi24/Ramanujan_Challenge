# Primitive consecutive-return intersections: the exact saturation module, and its height ceiling

## Verdict

There **is** a canonical integral object that counts only primitive consecutive-return roots. It is not another resultant. It is the finite torsion module obtained by localizing the direct three-return intersection away from every intermediate return divisor, and then taking its integral saturation:

\[
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}
=
\left(R_H[x]/I_{a,b,c}\right)[D_{a,b,c}^{-1}]
\cong
R_H[x]/\bigl(I_{a,b,c}:D_{a,b,c}^{\infty}\bigr).
\]

Here `pc` means *primitive consecutive*, \(I_{a,b,c}\) is the fully centrally-deflated direct-return ideal, and \(D_{a,b,c}\) is the product of:

1. all intermediate return polynomials in the three gaps;
2. the cut-edge factors needed by the continuant restart identities; and
3. the three selected central linear factors, so that centered adjacent pairs are removed and handled separately.

Its reduction modulo \(p\) is exactly the local intersection algebra supported on roots having **no** intermediate return. Its Smith radical has the exact valuation formula

\[
v_p\bigl(\mathfrak P_{a,b,c}\bigr)
=
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}
\left(M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf F_p\right).
\]

Thus it counts local intersection length only on the primitive open locus; skipped-return roots contribute zero.

This is the correct replacement for the raw direct pencil content. It is termwise no larger than the full block Fitting divisor, which in turn divides the direct pencil content.

What it does **not** provide automatically is the missing \(H\)-saving. The canonical primitive carrier inherits the existing \(H^{4+o(1)}\) ceiling, and a bound

\[
\sum_{a+b+c\le H}\log \mathfrak P_{a,b,c}
\ll H^{3+o(1)}
\]

is a new arithmetic statement essentially equivalent to the desired prime-first primitive-incidence estimate. Naively clearing the localization denominators is much worse: it introduces the norm \(\operatorname{Res}(F,D)\), whose standard height is \(H^{3+o(1)}\) **per gap triple**, not in total.

So the finite \(H\le 28\) observation is meaningful: it predicts that every residual primary component lies in the \(D\)-power-torsion submodule and therefore vanishes from \(M^{\mathrm{pc}}\). But localization, Fitting formalism, or Möbius inversion alone does not prove that this persists with total height \(H^{3+o(1)}\).

## 1. The exact return-concatenation law

Retain the continuants from `proof.tex`:

\[
N_0=0,\qquad N_1=1,
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(x)
=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x).
\]

Two identities in `proof.tex` are the relevant algebra.

### Addition identity

For \(d,r\ge1\),

\[
N_{d+r}(x)
=
N_d(x)N_{r+1}(x+d-1)
-(x+d)^6N_{d-1}(x)N_r(x+d).
\tag{1.1}
\]

### Saturated Bézout identity

Put

\[
S_d(x)=\prod_{j=2}^{d}(x+j).
\]

For \(2\le d<e\),

\[
N_{e-1}(x+1)N_d(x)
-N_{d-1}(x+1)N_e(x)
=
S_d(x)^6N_{e-d}(x+d).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently, over any field and away from \(S_d(x)=0\),

\[
N_d(x)=N_e(x)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N_d(x)=N_{e-d}(x+d)=0.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The forward implication is (1.2); the reverse implication is (1.1).

This is exactly the restart law for return times. If a column has returns at levels \(d<e\), then after the return at \(d\), the residual length \(e-d\) is a return for the shifted column.

### Unique primitive refinement

Fix a field \(k\), \(x\in k\), and a range \(h\le H\) in which

\[
S_H(x)\ne0.
\]

Call \(h\ge2\) a **primitive return at \(x\)** if

\[
N_h(x)=0,
\qquad
N_j(x)\ne0\quad(2\le j<h).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Repeated use of (1.3) gives a unique ordered decomposition of every return:

\[
h=h_1+\cdots+h_m,
\tag{1.5}
\]

where \(h_1\) is primitive at \(x\), \(h_2\) is primitive at \(x+h_1\), and in general \(h_t\) is primitive at

\[
x+h_1+\cdots+h_{t-1}.
\]

Uniqueness follows by taking the least positive return at each restart.

This proves the exact interpretation of the new audit: a raw common root that “skips an intermediate return” belongs to a longer ordered composition. It is not a primitive gap witness.

## 2. Central factors and the fully-deflated direct equations

The reflection identity is

\[
N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For even \(h\), it forces the central factor

\[
L_h(x)=2x+h+1
\quad\text{to divide}\quad N_h(x)
\quad\text{in }\mathbf Z[x].
\tag{2.2}
\]

For odd \(h\), put \(L_h=1\). Define

\[
N_h^\circ(x)=
\begin{cases}
N_h(x)/L_h(x),&h\text{ even},\\
N_h(x),&h\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For a gap triple \((a,b,c)\), set \(s=a+b+c\) and use the direct adjacent-segment equations

\[
F=N_a^\circ(x),
\qquad
G=N_b^\circ(x+a),
\qquad
K=N_c^\circ(x+a+b).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The selected central divisor is

\[
C_{a,b,c}(x)
=L_a(x)L_b(x+a)L_c(x+a+b).
\tag{2.5}
\]

On the open set \(C_{a,b,c}\ne0\), the deflated equations (2.4) are equivalent to the raw equations

\[
N_a(x)=N_b(x+a)=N_c(x+a+b)=0.
\tag{2.6}
\]

A centered selected gap is therefore excluded exactly by inverting \(C_{a,b,c}\).

For an actual reflection-invariant zero set in \([0,p-1]\), there is at most one adjacent pair whose endpoints sum to \(p-1\). A consecutive zero quadruple can contain that fixed pair in at most three positions. Thus removing all selected centered pairs costs at most three selected quadruples per prime, which is harmless in the level-adaptive counting argument.

## 3. The primitive open divisor

For \(h\ge2\), define the proper-prefix return product

\[
\Pi_h(y)=\prod_{j=2}^{h-1}N_j(y),
\tag{3.1}
\]

with empty product \(1\) when \(h=2\).

For the triple \((a,b,c)\), define

\[
\boxed{
D_{a,b,c}(x)
=
S_s(x)\,C_{a,b,c}(x)\,
\Pi_a(x)\,
\Pi_b(x+a)\,
\Pi_c(x+a+b).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Each factor has a separate role:

- \(S_s(x)\ne0\) makes every use of the restart/Bézout law legitimate;
- the three \(\Pi\)-factors say that each selected gap is the first return after the preceding selected zero;
- \(C_{a,b,c}(x)\ne0\) removes a centered adjacent pair among the three primitive gaps.

If \(p>H\), \(0\le r\), and \(r+s\le p-1\), then \(S_s(r)\ne0\pmod p\). Starting from an actual Apéry zero \(b_r=0\), the gap formula gives, for \(j\le s\),

\[
b_{r+j}
=
\frac{N_j(r)}{\prod_{\nu=2}^{j}(r+\nu)^3}\,b_{r+1}
\pmod p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The denominator is a unit, and \(b_{r+1}\ne0\) by the no-consecutive-zeros lemma. Hence

\[
b_{r+j}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N_j(r)=0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Applying (3.4) after each restart proves:

> A non-centered actual zero chain
> 
> \[
> r,\quad r+a,\quad r+a+b,\quad r+a+b+c
> \]
> 
> consists of four consecutive zeros if and only if it satisfies (2.6) and \(D_{a,b,c}(r)\ne0\).

Only the detection direction is needed below; the polynomial scheme may have additional formal return chains that are not reached by the actual Apéry orbit.

## 4. The exact saturated module

Let

\[
\mathfrak U_H=\prod_{j\le H}j!\,b_j\,V_{j+1}
\]

be the structural carrier already used in `atom_tail_section.tex`, and put

\[
R_H=\mathbf Z[(2\mathfrak U_H)^{-1}].
\tag{4.1}
\]

The factor \(2\) makes every central deflation integral with unit leading coefficient conventions. All relevant leading coefficients are units in \(R_H\).

Let

\[
I_{a,b,c}=(F,G,K)\subset R_H[x]
\tag{4.2}
\]

with \(F,G,K\) from (2.4), and set

\[
Q_{a,b,c}=R_H[x]/I_{a,b,c}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The root-strip First Lemma shows that \(F\) and \(G\) have no common characteristic-zero root: removing a central factor can only remove roots, and the original root strips are disjoint after the shift by \(a\). Therefore

\[
Q_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf Q=0.
\]

Since \(R_H[x]/(F)\) is finite free over \(R_H\), \(Q_{a,b,c}\) is a finite torsion \(R_H\)-module.

### Definition: primitive consecutive module

Define

\[
\boxed{
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}
=Q_{a,b,c}[D_{a,b,c}^{-1}].}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Because \(Q_{a,b,c}\) is finite torsion, this localization is again a finite torsion \(R_H\)-module. It has the following purely integral description.

### Proposition 4.1: saturation formula

One has a canonical isomorphism

\[
\boxed{
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}
\cong
R_H[x]/\bigl(I_{a,b,c}:D_{a,b,c}^{\infty}\bigr),}
\tag{4.5}
\]

where

\[
I:D^\infty=\bigcup_{n\ge0}(I:D^n).
\]

#### Proof

Let \(Q=Q_{a,b,c}\) and let \(D\) denote the image of \(D_{a,b,c}\) in \(Q\). The kernel of

\[
Q\longrightarrow Q[D^{-1}]
\]

is the \(D\)-power-torsion submodule

\[
\Gamma_D(Q)=\{q\in Q:D^nq=0\text{ for some }n\}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus the image of \(Q\) in the localization is

\[
Q/\Gamma_D(Q)
\cong
R_H[x]/(I:D^\infty).
\]

The quotient \(Q/\Gamma_D(Q)\) has finite length over the PID \(R_H\). Multiplication by \(D\) is injective on this quotient, hence surjective, hence an automorphism. Therefore every fraction \(q/D^n\) already comes from \(Q/\Gamma_D(Q)\), proving (4.5). ∎

This is the key point: although an arbitrary open subscheme need not be finite over \(\mathbf Z\), the raw direct intersection here is already a finite torsion scheme. Its primitive open part therefore has a finite integral module and an integer Fitting carrier.

## 5. Exact mod-\(p\) corank and local length

Localization commutes with base change. Hence for every prime

\[
p\nmid 2\mathfrak U_H
\]

there is a canonical isomorphism

\[
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf F_p
\cong
\left(
\mathbf F_p[x]/(\bar F,\bar G,\bar K)
\right)[\bar D^{-1}].
\tag{5.1}
\]

The right side is the common-root algebra restricted to the open set \(ar D\ne0\). Therefore

\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}
(M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf F_p)
=
\sum_{z}
[\kappa(z):\mathbf F_p]\,
\operatorname{length}(\mathcal O_z),
\tag{5.2}
\]

where the sum is over closed points satisfying

\[
\bar F(z)=\bar G(z)=\bar K(z)=0,
\qquad
\bar D(z)\ne0.
\]

Thus no skipped-return point is counted. No separability or simplicity hypothesis is needed.

Let \(t^{\mathrm{pc}}_p(a,b,c)\) be the number of \(\mathbf F_p\)-rational roots satisfying those conditions. Then

\[
t^{\mathrm{pc}}_p(a,b,c)
\le
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}
(M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf F_p).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equality holds when the primitive fiber is reduced and all its points are rational.

### Smith radical carrier

Since \(R_H\) is a PID, write

\[
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}
\cong
\bigoplus_i R_H/(s_i).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Choose integer representatives for the \(s_i\) with all primes dividing \(2\mathfrak U_H\) removed. Define

\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}=\prod_i|s_i|,
\tag{5.5}
\]

and the **primitive Smith radical**

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P_{a,b,c}
=
\prod_i\operatorname{rad}(|s_i|).}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is not merely \(\operatorname{rad}(\Delta^{\mathrm{pc}})\): if \(p\) occurs in \(r\) separate Smith factors, it occurs to exponent \(r\) in \(\mathfrak P\).

For every \(p\nmid2\mathfrak U_H\),

\[
\boxed{
v_p(\mathfrak P_{a,b,c})
=
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}
(M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf F_p).}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Also

\[
v_p(\Delta^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c})
=
\operatorname{length}_{\mathbf Z_p}
(M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf Z_p).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Equation (5.7) is the exact requested integral invariant: its \(p\)-valuation is the mod-\(p\) corank of the primitive open intersection and counts no root lying on an intermediate-return divisor.

## 6. A concrete multiplication-matrix formula

The invariant can be audited without a multivariate Gröbner saturation.

Put

\[
A=R_H[x]/(F).
\]

Let \(M_G,M_K,M_D\) be multiplication by \(G,K,D\) on \(A\). The raw direct-intersection module is the cokernel of

\[
\Phi=[M_G\;M_K]:A^2\longrightarrow A.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For \(p\nmid2\mathfrak U_H\), put

\[
V_p=\operatorname{coker}(\bar\Phi)
\]

and let \(T_p\) be the endomorphism induced by \(ar M_D\) on \(V_p\). By the Fitting decomposition for a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space,

\[
V_p=V_p^{\mathrm{nil}}\oplus V_p^{\mathrm{inv}},
\]

where \(T_p\) is nilpotent on the first summand and invertible on the second. Localization kills exactly \(V_p^{\mathrm{nil}}\). Therefore, for any

\[
n\ge\dim_{\mathbf F_p}V_p
\]

(and in particular for \(n\ge\deg F\)),

\[
\boxed{
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf F_p
\cong
\operatorname{im}(T_p^n),}
\tag{6.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
v_p(\mathfrak P_{a,b,c})
=
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf F_p}(T_p^n).}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is the exact algebraic test suggested by the \(H\le28\) forensics. For every residual prime \(q\) of the raw direct pencil, one should compute the stable image of multiplication by \(D\) on the raw common-root cokernel. If every raw root skips a return, then \(D\) is nilpotent on every \(q\)-primary component and (6.3) is zero.

A support-level root audit is enough to kill higher local multiplicity as well: in an Artinian algebra, if \(D\) vanishes in every residue field of the support, then \(D\) is nilpotent. Thus localization kills the whole local Artin component, not only its reduced point.

## 7. Relation to the raw block Fitting divisor and direct pencil content

Let

\[
\Gamma_{a,b,c}=\Gamma_D(Q_{a,b,c})
\]

be the skipped-return/center/cut-edge torsion. Proposition 4.1 gives an exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\Gamma_{a,b,c}
\longrightarrow Q_{a,b,c}
\longrightarrow M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For finite torsion modules over a PID, orders multiply. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}_{a,b,c}
=
\Delta^{\mathrm{bdry}}_{a,b,c}
\Delta^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}}
\tag{7.2}
\]

up to a unit of \(R_H\). In particular,

\[
\mathfrak P_{a,b,c}\mid
\Delta^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\mid
\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}_{a,b,c}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The raw order \(\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}\) is the zeroth Fitting divisor of the block map (6.1), namely the gcd of all maximal minors of

\[
[M_G\;M_K].
\]

Now form the direct one-parameter pencil

\[
R_{a,b,c}(T)
=
\operatorname{Res}_x(F,G+TK)
=
\det(M_G+TM_K)
\tag{7.4}
\]

up to a unit. Each coefficient of the determinant is an integral signed sum of maximal minors of \([M_G\;M_K]\). Therefore

\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}_{a,b,c}\mid
\operatorname{cont}_T R_{a,b,c}(T).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Combining (7.3) and (7.5), after the same structural saturation,

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P_{a,b,c}\mid
\Delta^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\mid
\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}_{a,b,c}\mid
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{dir},*}_{a,b,c}.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Thus the primitive Smith radical is strictly the weakest and most accurately aligned carrier in this architecture.

The finite observation “every residual root skips an intermediate return” says precisely that, for the observed residual primes,

\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}_{a,b,c}
=
\Delta^{\mathrm{bdry}}_{a,b,c},
\qquad
\Delta^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}=1
\]

on those primary components.

## 8. Why Möbius inversion is not a smaller positive carrier

The return law gives a clean constructible decomposition, but its indexing poset is not the divisor lattice.

Away from \(S_h(x)=0\), let \(\mathcal P_h(x)\) denote the primitive-return condition (1.4). Then

\[
\{N_h(x)=0\}
=
\coprod_{\substack{h=h_1+\cdots+h_m\\h_i\ge2}}
\bigcap_{t=1}^{m}
\mathcal P_{h_t}
\left(x+h_1+\cdots+h_{t-1}\right).
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is a disjoint decomposition into **ordered compositions** of \(h\). The number of such compositions grows exponentially with \(h\) (with the restriction \(h_i\ge2\), it has Fibonacci-type growth).

One may recursively invert (8.1) in the Grothendieck group of constructible sets, or apply ordinary inclusion-exclusion to

\[
D\left(\prod_{2\le j<h}N_j\right).
\]

Neither operation produces a positive integral carrier:

1. the terms have alternating signs;
2. closures of the primitive strata meet in vertical fibers;
3. Fitting ideals and Smith orders are not additive under set-theoretic subtraction;
4. a Čech/Koszul complex records an Euler characteristic, while an upper-bound carrier needs a nonnegative local length.

The positive object obtained after all cancellations is exactly the saturation quotient \(M^{\mathrm{pc}}\). Möbius inversion is therefore an exponentially expanded presentation of the same open restriction, not a height compression.

## 9. Why clearing all localization denominators is worse

The Rabinowitsch presentation is formally exact:

\[
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}
\cong
R_H[x,u]/(F,G,K,uD-1).
\tag{9.1}
\]

But turning (9.1) into a single determinant over \(R_H\) requires clearing the inverse of multiplication by \(D\). On the ambient finite free algebra \(A=R_H[x]/(F)\),

\[
M_D^{-1}
=
\frac{\operatorname{adj}(M_D)}{\det M_D},
\]

and, up to an \(R_H\)-unit,

\[
\det M_D
=
\operatorname{Nm}_{A/R_H}(D)
=
\operatorname{Res}_x(F,D).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Because \(D\) is a product, this norm factors as

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}(F,D)
={}&\operatorname{Res}(F,S_s)
\prod_{2\le i<a}\operatorname{Res}(F,N_i(x))\\
&\cdot
\prod_{2\le j<b}\operatorname{Res}(F,N_j(x+a))\\
&\cdot
\prod_{2\le k<c}\operatorname{Res}(F,N_k(x+a+b))\\
&\cdot
\operatorname{Res}(F,C_{a,b,c}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.3}
\]

again up to units and with the evident deflated convention for \(F\).

The continuant Bézout identity shows what these factors mean: same-column intersections with an earlier return split into a cut-edge factor and a translated separated-return factor. The cut-edge multiplier is \(S_d^6\); after evaluation it produces values such as \(N_a(-a-j)\), and the repo audit already notes that these primes are not all supported on the current structural carrier. Thus (9.3) does not collapse uniformly to the existing \(\mathfrak U_H\).

There is also a direct size obstruction. From (3.1),

\[
\deg\Pi_h
=
\sum_{j=2}^{h-1}3(j-1)
=
3\binom{h-1}{2}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Hence

\[
\deg D_{a,b,c}
=
3\left[
\binom{a-1}{2}+
\binom{b-1}{2}+
\binom{c-1}{2}
\right]
+O(H)
=O(H^2).
\tag{9.5}
\]

The determinant/continuant coefficient estimates used elsewhere in the repo give

\[
\log\|D_{a,b,c}\|=O(H^2\log H).
\]

Since \(\deg F=O(H)\), the standard resultant-height estimate applied to (9.2) gives

\[
\log|\operatorname{Res}(F,D)|
=O(H^3\log H)
\tag{9.6}
\]

for one triple. Summing this denominator carrier over \(O(H^3)\) triples gives an \(O(H^6\log H)\) presentation bound, far worse than the current direct content.

The canonical saturation quotient avoids paying (9.2), because it simply discards the \(D\)-power-torsion part of the already finite module. But then its only unconditional height comparison is (7.6), so it returns to the existing \(H^4\)-versus-\(H^3\) gap.

In short:

- **clear the inverse explicitly:** pay the enormous norm (9.3);
- **use the canonical saturation quotient:** avoid that norm, but obtain no automatic saving beyond the raw Fitting divisor.

## 10. A rigorous no-free-lunch counterexample

No theorem based only on “finite intersection + many nonvanishing conditions + Fitting saturation” can force an \(H^3\) total height.

For each label \(\lambda\), choose a squarefree integer \(M_\lambda\) all of whose prime factors exceed \(H\), and set

\[
F_\lambda(x)=x,
\qquad
G_\lambda(x)=x+M_\lambda,
\qquad
K_\lambda(x)=2x+M_\lambda.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Then

\[
\mathbf Z[x]/(F_\lambda,G_\lambda,K_\lambda)
\cong
\mathbf Z/M_\lambda\mathbf Z.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Introduce as many “intermediate exclusions” as desired by taking

\[
D_\lambda(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{H}(x+j).
\tag{10.3}
\]

On the quotient (10.2), \(x=0\), so \(D_\lambda\) acts as \(H!\). Since every prime factor of \(M_\lambda\) exceeds \(H\), this action is invertible. Therefore

\[
\left(\mathbf Z/M_\lambda\mathbf Z\right)[D_\lambda^{-1}]
=
\mathbf Z/M_\lambda\mathbf Z.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Every vertical root is primitive relative to all the imposed open conditions, and saturation removes nothing.

Take \(\asymp H^3\) labels and let each \(M_\lambda\) contain \(\asymp H\) primes. Then

\[
\sum_\lambda\log M_\lambda
\asymp H^4\log H.
\]

The three equations have degree one. Thus bounded equation degree, generic emptiness, finite Fitting modules, and arbitrarily many primitive open conditions do not imply an \(H^3\) carrier. Any \(H\)-saving must use a genuinely Apéry-specific relation across different gap triples.

## 11. The exact new hypothesis and its consequence

Define the primitive-consecutive mass

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pc}}(H)
=
\sum_{\substack{a,b,c\ge2\\a+b+c\le H}}
\log\mathfrak P_{a,b,c}.
\tag{11.1}
\]

By (5.7), this has the exact prime expansion

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pc}}(H)
=
\sum_{p\nmid2\mathfrak U_H}
(\log p)
\sum_{a+b+c\le H}
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}
(M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf F_p).}
\tag{11.2}
\]

Thus the natural replacement for the current aligned-content hypothesis is

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{PC}^{\mathrm{rad}}:
\qquad
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pc}}(H)
\ll H^{3+o(1)}.}
\tag{11.3}
\]

This is strictly weaker, term by term, than the direct-pencil content bound because of (7.6). It is also exactly aligned with consecutive quadruples.

Let \(Q_p(H)\) be the selected consecutive-zero quadruple count in the block decomposition from `atom_tail_section.tex`. Discarding the at most three quadruples containing the unique centered adjacent pair, every remaining selected quadruple gives one primitive root in the module for its gap triple. Hence, outside the structural exceptional set,

\[
Q_p(H)-3
\le
\sum_{a+b+c\le H}
 v_p(\mathfrak P_{a,b,c}).
\tag{11.4}
\]

The same level-adaptive choice

\[
H=\left\lceil\frac{16X}{T}\right\rceil,
\qquad
T<Z(p)\le2T,
\]

then gives, under (11.3),

\[
T\,|\mathcal P_X(T)\setminus\mathcal E_H|
\ll
\frac{H^{3+o(1)}}{\log X}.
\]

The existing \(O(H^2/\log X)\) structural exceptional-prime estimate is absorbed exactly as in the conditional aligned-content theorem. Summing over dyadic \(T\ge\sqrt X\) yields

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)
\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

Therefore (11.3) is a valid and sharper sufficient condition.

But (11.2) also explains why the saturation formalism does not prove it: \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pc}}(H)\) is literally the weighted prime mass of primitive formal return chains. Bounding it by \(H^{3+o(1)}\) is the new arithmetic incidence theorem one is seeking.

## 12. What the \(H\le28\) audit establishes—and what remains

The audit statement has an exact module-theoretic translation.

Suppose \(q\) is a residual prime of the fully-deflated raw direct pencil. If every common root has an intermediate return, then every support point of the \(q\)-fiber lies on \(D=0\). Consequently \(D\) is nilpotent on the entire \(q\)-fiber, and

\[
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}\otimes\mathbf F_q=0.
\]

If, after applying the unique refinement law, the resulting primitive chain has a centered adjacent pair, then the refined witness lies on one of the selected \(L_h\)-divisors and is also removed by \(C_{a,b,c}\). Thus it does not reappear under a smaller refined gap triple in the non-centered primitive family.

The exact certificate to record for each residual datum is therefore

```text
V_q = coker([M_G M_K] mod q)
T_q = multiplication by D on V_q
primitive_corank(q,a,b,c) = rank(T_q^deg(F))
```

and the observed claim is

```text
primitive_corank(q,a,b,c) = 0
```

for every residual datum through \(H=28\).

That is stronger and cleaner than checking only that a displayed reduced root has an intermediate return: it certifies that no hidden nilpotent local length survives the primitive localization.

What remains open is a uniform proof of (11.3), or at least a theorem showing that all but \(H^{3+o(1)}\) of the raw Smith mass lies in the boundary torsion \(\Gamma_D(Q)\). From (7.2), that required statement is exactly

\[
\sum_{a+b+c\le H}
\left(
\log\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}_{a,b,c}
-\log\Delta^{\mathrm{bdry}}_{a,b,c}
\right)
\ll H^{3+o(1)}.
\]

The continuant identities identify the boundary strata and give unique refinement, but they do not presently supply this global lower bound on the boundary torsion.

## Final answer

The correct primitive object is

\[
\boxed{
M^{\mathrm{pc}}_{a,b,c}
=
R_H[x]/\bigl((F,G,K):D_{a,b,c}^{\infty}\bigr),}
\]

with \(D_{a,b,c}\) given by (3.2). Its Smith radical \(\mathfrak P_{a,b,c}\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{
v_p(\mathfrak P_{a,b,c})
=
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}
\left(
\mathbf F_p[x]/(F,G,K)
\right)[D_{a,b,c}^{-1}],}
\]

so it counts only primitive, non-centered common-root local length. It is computable as the stable image of multiplication by \(D\) on the raw block cokernel and obeys the termwise divisibility

\[
\mathfrak P_{a,b,c}\mid
\Delta^{\mathrm{raw}}_{a,b,c}\mid
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{dir},*}_{a,b,c}.
\]

This exact saturation should replace raw common-root content in the next audit.

However, no \(H^{3+o(1)}\) logarithmic-height theorem follows from localization, Fitting complexes, or Möbius inversion alone. Clearing denominators imports the huge norm \(\operatorname{Res}(F,D)\); avoiding that norm returns to the saturated quotient, whose \(H^3\) mass bound is precisely a new primitive-incidence hypothesis. The \(H\le28\) data strongly supports that hypothesis, but does not prove it.
