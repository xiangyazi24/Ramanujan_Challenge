# 5. Why a mixed ordinary resultant does not exist at this interface

The lower pair gives

\[
\ell\mid N_h(a),
\qquad
\deg N_h=3(h-1),
\tag{5.1}
\]

while the selected upper atom is

\[
q\mid b_t.
\tag{5.2}
\]

These are roots in different residue fields. An ordinary resultant detects a common root of two reductions over the **same** field. It cannot combine an \(\mathbb F_\ell\)-root of one equation with an \(\mathbb F_q\)-root of another.

The logical failure already occurs for linear polynomials. For coprime primes \(\ell,q\), let

\[
F(X)=X-x+\ell,
\qquad
G(X)=X-x+q.
\]

Then

\[
F(x)=0\pmod\ell,
\qquad
G(x)=0\pmod q,
\]

but

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X(F,G)=q-\ell,
\]

which need be divisible by neither prime.

Adding the affine equations for \(q,t\) does not change this. They merely substitute

\[
q=\frac{7\ell-h}{6},\quad t=a+2h-\ell
\]

or

\[
q=\frac{8\ell+h}{7},\quad t=s+\frac{h-6\ell}{7}.
\]

They do not identify the two residue fields.

There is a second obstruction even before height is estimated. In the current hard cell,

\[
h>T^{3/5-\kappa},
\]

so \(N_h\) is not a bounded-degree polynomial family. Fixing \(h\) fixes \(q\) for a fixed \(\ell\), but leaves up to \(Z(\ell)\) starts. Fixing the selected state \((q,t)\), on the other hand, makes \(h\) move with \(\ell\):

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
+:\quad &h(\ell)=7\ell-6q,

-:\quad &h(\ell)=7q-8\ell,
& s(\ell)=t-q+2\ell.

\tag{5.3}

Thus there is no coordinate choice which simultaneously fixes the lower gap-polynomial level and the upper common-value carrier.

A product over moving \(h\)'s can pay every incidence, but its degree and logarithmic height grow with the number and size of those gaps. In the long-gap cell this restores at least the prime-divisibility cost being counted and gives no power saving. This is exactly the product-versus-gcd obstruction already encountered in the PDD/ASALC and DPRM ledgers.
