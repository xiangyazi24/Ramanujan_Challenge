# P32 prime-average sparsification audit

## Conclusion

The requested H^3 logarithmic-height deterministic selector cannot be obtained from the currently available identities alone. The correct conclusion is not that M4-style H^4 counting is necessary, but that the consecutive-four selector still carries a genuine three-parameter obstruction after all obvious compressions.

The useful reduction is:

\[
E_p(H)\leq \sum_{h\le H} A_p(h)
\]

where a selected event is represented by a minimal return chain

\[
(r,r+a,r+a+b,r+a+b+c),\qquad a,b,c\geq1,\ a+b+c\le H,
\]

with the additional conditions that the three gaps are consecutive returns (no intermediate zero). The absence condition can be imposed by inclusion-exclusion, but it does not remove the leading parameter count.

## 1. Möbius/inclusion-exclusion formulation

Let R_h(r) denote the return predicate at gap h. A consecutive return triple with total span ≤H is

\[
R_a(r)R_b(r+a)R_c(r+a+b)\prod_{0<t<a}(1-R_t(r))
\prod_{0<t<b}(1-R_t(r+a))
\prod_{0<t<c}(1-R_t(r+a+b)).
\]

Expanding the products gives a signed sum of ordinary multi-return configurations. Every term is still specified by a collection of offsets contained in an interval of length H. The leading term has three free positive offsets a,b,c with

\[
\#\{(a,b,c):a+b+c\le H\}=\binom{H}{3}=\Theta(H^3).
\]

Thus inclusion-exclusion does not increase the count to H^4, but it also does not produce a lower-dimensional object.

## 2. Gap-record encoding

The canonical gap record

\[
(a,b,c),
\]

is already optimal among local encodings: the endpoint pair determines only the total span s=a+b+c, while the selector needs the internal two break points. Forgetting them loses the information distinguishing consecutive from non-consecutive returns.

A continuant identity gives polynomial relations between endpoints, but the continuant depends on the full composition of the gap. Two different triples with the same sum need not have the same intermediate-return structure. Therefore the determinant identity compresses arithmetic values, not combinatorial choices.

## 3. Reflection does not reduce dimension

The palindrome/reflection law identifies

\[
R_h(r)\leftrightarrow R_h(-r-h),
\]

so it saves at most a factor of two. It does not identify the three independent gap parameters. The quotient space of triples still has size

\[
\Theta(H^3).
\]

## 4. What bound would suffice

The prime-average target follows from a bound of the form

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}E_p(H)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}/\log X.
\]

After the usual prime averaging and determinant/resultant step, the sufficient arithmetic statement is approximately

\[
\sum_{a+b+c\le H}\log \operatorname{Height}(C_{a,b,c})
\ll H^{3+o(1)},
\]

where C is the integer certificate polynomial/resultant attached to a consecutive chain.

This is plausible because the number of triples is already H^3, but it is not obtained by a free reduction from M4. One needs a genuine small-height certificate for each triple.

## 5. Precise obstruction

The obstruction is the following.

A four-subset count uses four positions and hence H^4 after averaging over arbitrary endpoint choices. A consecutive selector removes one degree of freedom because the last three positions are determined by three gaps. However, the no-intermediate-return condition requires retaining all three gaps:

- total span only: H choices;
- unordered endpoint pair: H^2 scale;
- consecutive four-window: H^3 compositions.

No available identity (gcd, reflection, continuant, or palindrome) collapses

\[
(a,b,c)\mapsto \text{one parameter}
\]

without losing the selector property.

Therefore the strongest justified deterministic statement at present is:

\[
\boxed{\#\text{consecutive short chains}=O(H^3)}
\]

and not a proven H^{2+o(1)} or H^{1+o(1)} family.

A future H^3 height theorem would be enough; obtaining it requires a new arithmetic certificate construction rather than a purely combinatorial charging argument.
