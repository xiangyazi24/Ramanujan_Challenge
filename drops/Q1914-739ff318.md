# P3.2 integral continuant descent: target-prime reduction

## Result

I do **not** get a proof of the final nonvanishing from the bare continuant recurrence. I do get two exact identities which isolate the obstruction much more tightly than the unrestricted resultant-support statement.

First, there is an integral seam identity

\[
\boxed{
X_{m+1}=8e_m+(2m+1)G_m
}
\]
with
\[
G_m=
4(34m^2-17m+10)X_m
-(2m-1)^3(4m^2-8m+7)X_{m-1}.
\]
Hence, for the target prime \(p=2m+1\),
\[
\boxed{p\mid e_m\iff p\mid X_{m+1}}.
\]
This part is an identity over \(\mathbf Z\), not scan evidence.

Second, after imposing the target-prime endpoint and the unit hypotheses, the question \(p\nmid Y_m\) is exactly equivalent to nonvanishing of a **division-free first-order Apéry descent scalar** \(F_m\). Explicitly, if \(b_j\) are the ordinary Apéry numbers modulo \(p\), put
\[
B_{-1}=1,\qquad B_j=\prod_{r=0}^j b_r,
\]
\[
a_j=3(4j^2+4j-3),
\]
and define
\[
Z_0=0,
\qquad
Z_{j+1}=b_jZ_j+a_jb_mB_{j-1}
\quad(0\le j\le m-2).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
F_m:=Z_{m-1}-2B_{m-2}
=-2B_{m-2}\frac{Y_m}{P_m}\pmod p.
}
\]
Since \(B_{m-2}\) and \(P_m\) are units, this gives the exact equivalence
\[
\boxed{p\mid Y_m\iff F_m=0\pmod p.}
\]
Moreover the seam equation gives \(b_m=-27b_{m-2}\pmod p\), so the falsifier test for \(F_m\) does not even need \(b_m\) as an independently computed value.

Thus the smaller exact arithmetic premise is:

> For \(p=2m+1\ge5\) prime, if \(b_{m-1}=0\) and \(b_0,\ldots,b_{m-2}\) are units, then the above division-free scalar \(F_m\) is nonzero.

I do not have a proof of that last premise. The Euclidean descent terminates **exactly** at \(F_m\); it does not collapse further to a fixed unit from the known three-term recurrence.

## 1. Denominator ledger

Set
\[
u_0=1,\qquad u_t=\frac{X_t}{d_t}\quad(t\ge1).
\]
Since
\[
d_{t+1}=(2t+1)^3d_t,
\]
the integral recurrence for \(X_t\) is equivalent to
\[
(2t+1)^3u_{t+1}
-4t(68t^2+3)u_t
+(2t-1)^3u_{t-1}=0.
\]
Also
\[
d_m=(2m-1)^3d_{m-1},
\]
so
\[
\frac{e_m}{d_m}=u_{m-1}-5u_m.
\]

For \(p=2m+1\), every factor occurring in every \(d_t\) with \(t\le m\) lies among
\[
3,5,\ldots,2m-1=p-2,
\]
so the stated \(d_t\)-unit hypothesis is actually automatic. The only substantive denominator hypothesis is that
\[
X_2,\ldots,X_m
\]
are nonzero modulo \(p\). Consequently \(u_2,\ldots,u_m\) and
\[
P_m=\prod_{t=2}^mX_t
\]
are units.

Define
\[
R_n:=\frac{Y_n}{P_n},\qquad P_1=1.
\]
Dividing the defining recurrence for \(Y_n\) by \(P_n=P_{n-1}X_n\) gives the exact one-step relation
\[
R_{n-1}=R_n+6(n^2-1)\frac{d_n}{X_n}.
\]
No denominator used here vanishes under the hypotheses.

## 2. Checked integral seam identity

Starting from
\[
X_{m+1}=4m(68m^2+3)X_m-(2m-1)^6X_{m-1}
\]
and subtracting \(8e_m\), the two coefficients factor as
\[
4m(68m^2+3)+40
=(2m+1)\,4(34m^2-17m+10),
\]
while
\[
(2m-1)^6+8(2m-1)^3
=(2m+1)(2m-1)^3(4m^2-8m+7).
\]
Therefore
\[
X_{m+1}-8e_m=(2m+1)G_m
\]
with \(G_m\) as displayed above. This proves
\[
p\mid e_m\iff p\mid X_{m+1}
\]
for \(p=2m+1\), without any unit assumption.

This is useful as a seam checksum, but by itself it does not control \(Y_m\).

## 3. Target-prime reversal gives the ordinary Apéry sequence

Now impose \(p\mid e_m\) and the unit assumptions. Define, in \(\mathbf F_p\),
\[
v_j:=\frac{u_{m-j}}{u_m}\qquad(0\le j\le m).
\]
The endpoint gives
\[
v_0=1,\qquad v_1=5.
\]
For \(1\le j\le m-1\), put \(t=m-j\). Since \(p=2m+1\),
\[
2t+1\equiv-2j,
\qquad
2t-1\equiv-2(j+1),
\]
and
\[
4t(68t^2+3)
\equiv-8(34j^3+51j^2+27j+5)
\pmod p.
\]
Thus the reversed recurrence is
\[
(j+1)^3v_{j+1}
=(34j^3+51j^2+27j+5)v_j-j^3v_{j-1}.
\]
With \(v_0=1,v_1=5\), uniqueness gives
\[
v_j=b_j.
\]
In particular,
\[
b_{m-1}=v_{m-1}=\frac{u_1}{u_m}=0,
\]
and
\[
b_m=v_m=\frac{u_0}{u_m}=u_m^{-1}.
\]
For \(0\le j\le m-2\), \(u_{m-j}\) is a unit, hence every \(b_j\) in that range is a unit.

The final ordinary Apéry recurrence, at \(j=m-1\), now gives
\[
m^3b_m=-(m-1)^3b_{m-2}.
\]
Because \(m\equiv-1/2\pmod p\),
\[
\boxed{b_m=-27b_{m-2}\pmod p.}
\]
This is another denominator-free seam identity; in particular \(b_m\) is automatically a unit once \(b_{m-2}\) is.

## 4. Division-free Euclidean descent for \(Y_m\)

For \(0\le j\le m-2\), the reversal gives
\[
\frac{d_{m-j}}{X_{m-j}}
=\frac{b_m}{b_j}.
\]
Apply the one-step recurrence for \(R_n\) with \(n=m-j\):
\[
R_{m-j-1}
=R_{m-j}+6((m-j)^2-1)\frac{b_m}{b_j}.
\]
Now clear the denominator at every step by defining
\[
B_{-1}=1,\qquad B_j=b_jB_{j-1},
\]
and
\[
T_j:=2R_{m-j}B_{j-1}.
\]
Then
\[
T_{j+1}
=b_jT_j+12((m-j)^2-1)b_mB_{j-1}.
\]
Here the target-prime relation is essential: since \(m\equiv-1/2\pmod p\),
\[
12((m-j)^2-1)
\equiv3(4j^2+4j-3)=a_j.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
T_{j+1}=b_jT_j+a_jb_mB_{j-1}.
}
\]
At the two ends,
\[
T_0=2R_m,
\qquad
T_{m-1}=2R_1B_{m-2}=2B_{m-2},
\]
because \(Y_1=P_1=1\).

Let \(Z_j\) be the same inhomogeneous recurrence with zero initial value:
\[
Z_0=0,
\qquad
Z_{j+1}=b_jZ_j+a_jb_mB_{j-1}.
\]
By induction,
\[
T_j=B_{j-1}T_0+Z_j.
\]
Putting \(j=m-1\) gives
\[
2B_{m-2}
=2B_{m-2}R_m+Z_{m-1}.
\]
Hence the promised checked identity
\[
\boxed{
Z_{m-1}-2B_{m-2}
=-2B_{m-2}R_m
=-2B_{m-2}\frac{Y_m}{P_m}.
}
\]
All equalities in this section are in \(\mathbf F_p\). Every division that occurred before clearing was by a stated unit; the final recurrence for \(Z_j\) itself contains no division at all.

## 5. Exact smaller premise and falsifier search

Using \(b_m=-27b_{m-2}\), a falsifier can be sought without constructing \(X_t,d_t,P_t,Y_t\), and without any unrestricted integer resultant.

```text
for each prime p = 2m+1 >= 5:
    compute b_0,...,b_{m-1} mod p from the Apéry recurrence
    require b_{m-1} = 0
    require b_0,...,b_{m-2} != 0

    beta = -27*b_{m-2} mod p
    B = 1                    # B_{-1}
    Z = 0
    for j = 0,...,m-2:
        Z = b_j*Z + 3*(4*j^2+4*j-3)*beta*B   mod p
        B = B*b_j                              mod p

    F = Z - 2*B mod p
    if F == 0:
        report a target-prime falsifier
```

The only inverses needed to generate the \(b_j\) recurrence are \((j+1)^{-3}\) with \(j+1<p\); the \(Z\)-descent is division-free.

Two small checks are:

* \(p=5,m=2\): \(F_m=1\pmod5\).
* \(p=19,m=9\): the Apéry values modulo 19 begin
  \[
  1,5,16,1,17,10,2,4,0,6,
  \]
  and the descent gives \(B_7=6\), \(Z_8=17\), hence
  \[
  F_9=17-12=5\pmod{19}.
  \]

The exact rational scan supplied in the question through \(m=200\) is consistent with this criterion, but it is only finite evidence. I am not promoting it to a theorem, and the rogue large primes in unrestricted resultants are irrelevant to this target-specialized criterion.

## 6. Precise obstruction to a further bare-continuant Bezout descent

The descent above is triangular and reversible because every \(b_0,\ldots,b_{m-2}\) is a unit. For an arbitrary starting value \(T_0\), it produces exactly one terminal value
\[
T_{m-1}=B_{m-2}T_0+Z_{m-1}.
\]
Thus the known three-term recurrence plus unit divisions cannot create a contradiction at an earlier Euclidean step: all earlier steps are invertible. The **only** remaining scalar obstruction is
\[
F_m=Z_{m-1}-2B_{m-2}.
\]

This also explains the failure recorded in `drops/Q1890-088b864c.md`: before target-prime specialization, the inhomogeneous product derivative carries contributions from every earlier stage, so a Bezout constant supported only on the last denominator/product is too optimistic. The present specialization packages all those contributions into the single first-order sequence \(Z_j\), but it does not make the terminal \(F_m\) a fixed unit.

So the current exact status is:

1. `p | e_m` is exactly the seam condition `p | X_{m+1}`.
2. Under the units, the endpoint reverses exactly to `b_{m-1}=0` with `b_m=-27 b_{m-2}`.
3. `p | Y_m` is exactly equivalent to `F_m=0` for the division-free descent above.
4. No identity presently in the repository forces `F_m != 0`; proving that is the remaining arithmetic lemma.

This is a genuine reduction and a falsifiable target-prime premise, not an unrestricted resultant-support assertion and not an inference from the finite scan.
