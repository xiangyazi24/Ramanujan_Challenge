# Q754 audit: corrected q=1 factorial carrier

## 1. The previous central-binomial carrier is invalid

The statement that every prime $p\in(n/2,n]$ divides $\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ is false. For example

$$
\binom 94=126\not\equiv0\pmod5.
$$

Therefore any argument using the central binomial coefficient as a universal q=1 carrier must be discarded.

The valid q=1 carrier is instead

$$
J=\lfloor (n-1)/3\rfloor,
$$
$$
B_{1,n}=\binom{n}{J+1}\binom{n+J+1}{J+1}.
$$

For $p\in(n/2,n]$ with $r=n-p$, the Apéry divisibility condition $p\mid b_r$ implies

$$
p\mid B_{1,n}.
$$

The reason is that $J+1$ is chosen so that one of the factorial factors in the Apéry boundary decomposition contains the prime $p$ while the complementary factorials avoid it.

## 2. Direct and reflected branches

Write

$$
n=p+r,\qquad 0\le r<n/2.
$$

The p-adic valuation of the carrier is determined by the two binomial factors.

For the first factor,

$$
\binom{p+r}{J+1}=\frac{(p+r)!}{(J+1)!(p+r-J-1)!}.
$$

Because $p>n/2$ there can be at most one multiple of $p$ in each factorial interval. Hence the p-adic valuation is either $0$ or $1$ depending on whether the numerator contains $p$ and the denominator does not.

Similarly,

$$
\binom{p+r+J+1}{J+1}
$$

has a possible p contribution from the interval

$$
[p,p+J+1+r].
$$

Thus the exact branches are determined by whether the denominator endpoint crosses the next multiple of p. In particular

$$
v_p(B_{1,n})\in\{0,1,2\}.
$$

The valuation exceeds one exactly when both binomial factors contribute a p-factor. Therefore division by p alone is not always the correct normalization; the natural residue is

$$
B_{1,n}/p^{v_p(B_{1,n})}\pmod p.
$$

## 3. Explicit residue formula

For a binomial coefficient with $0<a<p$,

$$
\frac1p\binom{p+r}{a}\pmod p
$$

is obtained by isolating the unique factor $p$ in the numerator:

$$
\binom{p+r}{a}=p\cdot\frac{\prod_{i\ne p}(p+r-i)}{a!(p+r-a)!}.
$$

Reducing the remaining factors modulo p gives a product of linear terms in $r$.

The same applies to the reflected branch coming from

$$
p+r+J+1=p+(r+J+1).
$$

The resulting expressions are piecewise polynomial/rational functions of $r$ modulo p, with the branch determined by whether $r+J+1\ge p$ after lifting.

No single formula $B_{1,n}/p$ exists globally because the valuation-two cases saturate it.

## 4. Interaction with $D_{1,n}\equiv b_r\pmod p$

The congruence

$$
D_{1,n}\equiv b_r\pmod p
$$

is valid and gives the target-selective interpretation:

$$
p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow p\mid D_{1,n}.
$$

Combining with the carrier gives two divisibilities:

$$
p\mid D_{1,n},\qquad p\mid B_{1,n}.
$$

However the useful consequence is not automatically obtained by dividing $B_{1,n}$ by p. The saturation factor

$$
p^{v_p(B_{1,n})-1}
$$

must be removed first. The valuation-two branch is the exact obstruction.

## 5. Mod-p^2 Apéry-Lucas jet

A mod-$p^2$ refinement may be used only after proving that the relevant Lucas congruence has a valid lift. The ordinary Lucas theorem gives only

$$
 b_{p+r}\equiv b_r\pmod p,
$$

not automatically modulo $p^2$.

A valid jet would require an additional theorem of the form

$$
b_{p+r}=b_r+pE_p(r)\pmod {p^2}
$$

with controlled $E_p(r)$. Without this input, multiplying the carrier congruence by a guessed mod-$p^2$ correction is invalid.

## 6. Second target-selective congruence: current status

The corrected carrier supplies a first filter, but it does not by itself produce a new small-height integer annihilator. The exact saturation factor is

$$
\boxed{p^{v_p(B_{1,n})-1},\quad v_p(B_{1,n})\le2.}
$$

The remaining possible route is to find another boundary carrier $C_n$ satisfying simultaneously:

1. every q=1 candidate prime divides $C_n$;
2. $v_p(C_n)$ is uniformly exactly one;
3. $C_n/p\pmod p$ has a controlled expression involving $b_r$ or the Apéry jet.

The present $B_{1,n}$ satisfies (1) but not uniformly (2). Therefore it is not yet a complete saturation-free certificate.

## Small-case check

The counterexample above already eliminates the false carrier. For any proposed carrier one must explicitly check:

- whether $p$ appears in numerator factorials;
- whether the denominator also contains $p$;
- whether $v_p$ can be two.

The corrected q=1 argument survives only with these branch distinctions.

## Verdict

- Central binomial carrier: **false**.
- $B_{1,n}=\binom n{J+1}\binom{n+J+1}{J+1}$ carrier: **valid candidate**.
- Universal formula $B_{1,n}/p\pmod p$: **false without valuation branch split**.
- Saturation factor: **exactly the possible extra p-factor from $v_p(B_{1,n})=2$**.
- Combining with a mod-$p^2$ Apéry-Lucas jet: **conditional only; requires an independent mod-$p^2$ theorem**.
- A second target-selective congruence remains the missing ingredient.
