# Q581 — Resultant/Bézout approach to Apéry pair-zero counting

## Executive summary

The resultant idea is useful, but it does not by itself close the pair-zero problem. The correct conclusion is:

* For fixed gap h, the polynomial bound
  \[
  D_p(h)\leq \deg N_h=3(h-1)
  \]
  is sharp as a purely algebraic statement and cannot see the Apéry zero set.
* Resultants can prove that **generic** continuant roots do not systematically coincide with Apéry zeros, but an effective bound requires controlling the arithmetic size of the resultant or its prime divisors.
* The more promising direction is the dual recurrence in h: for fixed r, the sequence N_h(r) is a second-order recurrence over F_p. A spectral argument could potentially give O(1) zeros in h, but this is essentially another finite-field orbit equidistribution problem.
* The integer gcd reformulation is conceptually clean, but estimating gcd(b_r,N_h(r)) requires new information about prime divisors of Apéry numbers.

The missing ingredient is therefore not a better polynomial root bound; it is a **horizontal decorrelation theorem between the two recurrence directions**.

---

# (A) Fixed h: degree bounds and why they saturate

For fixed h,
\[
D_p(h)=\#\{r:b_r\equiv0\pmod p,\ N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]
Since N_h has degree 3(h-1),
\[
D_p(h)\leq 3(h-1).
\]
This is correct but insufficient.

The problem is that summing over h gives
\[
\sum_{h\leq H}D_p(h)\leq \sum_{h\leq H}3h\asymp H^2.
\]
Taking H comparable with p loses completely.

The missing observation is that the degree bound treats Z_p as an arbitrary subset of F_p. It ignores that Z_p itself comes from the same recurrence.

For a random subset of density |Z_p|/p, one would expect
\[
D_p(h)\approx \frac{|Z_p|\deg N_h}{p},
\]
not \(\deg N_h\). Proving this requires information about the intersection of two algebraic conditions.

---

# (B) Can Newton polygons or p-adic methods help?

Usually no, at least not directly.

Newton polygons control valuations of roots in p-adic extensions. Here the issue is different:

\[
N_h(r)=0\quad\text{and}\quad b_r=0
\]
are two conditions inside F_p.

The natural algebraic tool is instead the resultant:
\[
R_h=\operatorname{Res}_r(B_p(r),N_h(r)),
\]
where B_p would be a polynomial whose roots encode the Apéry zeros. The difficulty is that

\[
\{r:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]
is not the zero set of one fixed polynomial B_p of bounded degree. The sequence b_r is a length-p recurrence, not a low-degree polynomial.

Therefore there is no immediate resultant over F_p between "the Apéry zero polynomial" and N_h.

---

# (C) Resultant identity between b_r and N_h

There is a related object that is useful.

If a restart occurs at r, then
\[
(b_r,b_{r+1})=(0,b_{r+1}),
\]
and after h steps:
\[
 b_{r+h}=C_h(r)b_{r+1}.
\]
The numerator of C_h is N_h.

Thus N_h(r)=0 means:

> a zero at r propagates to another zero exactly h steps away.

The pair-zero problem is therefore a recurrence orbit problem.

A genuine resultant statement would involve the transfer matrix:
\[
T_{r+h\leftarrow r}
\]
and the condition that its projective action sends the zero direction back to itself.

Equivalently,
\[
N_h(r)=0
\]
is a projective fixed-point condition for the transfer cocycle.

This connects naturally to monodromy, but the resultant does not automatically become small.

---

# (D) Fix r and vary h: the more promising direction

For fixed r in Z_p, define
\[
u_h=N_h(r).
\]
Then u_h satisfies a second-order recurrence in h.

The question becomes:

\[
\#\{h:u_h=0\}
\]
inside a finite field orbit.

This is analogous to asking how often a linear recurrence over F_p hits zero.

If the associated transfer matrix in the h-direction has generic semisimple eigenvalues λ,μ, then
\[
u_h=A\lambda^h+B\mu^h.
\]
Zeros satisfy
\[
(\lambda/\mu)^h=-B/A.
\]
Hence one expects O(1) zeros unless λ/μ has small multiplicative order.

This is the strongest conceptual route.

The obstruction is exceptional eigenvalue behavior. If
\[
\lambda/\mu
\]
has small order in F_p^*, the sequence can have many zeros.

Therefore one needs a statement such as:

> For Apéry zeros r, the h-transfer matrix has large projective order for almost all p.

This is a finite-field monodromy statement, not a resultant statement.

---

# (E) Double counting over primes

The identity
\[
\sum_pD_p
=
\sum_{h,r}\#\{p:p|b_r,\ p|N_h(r)\}
\]
is correct.

Equivalently:
\[
\#\{p:p|\gcd(b_r,N_h(r))\}
\]
controls the contribution.

However, estimating
\[
\gcd(b_r,N_h(r))
\]
is difficult because N_h(r) is a polynomial expression modulo p, but b_r is an enormous integer with no known factorization structure.

A height argument gives only:
\[
\omega(\gcd(b_r,N_h(r)))
\leq
\frac{\log |N_h(r)|}{\log 2},
\]
which is useless.

The desired estimate would be something like:
\[
\omega(\gcd(b_r,N_h(r)))\ll r^{o(1)},
\]
or on average over r,h.

That would be a new gcd theorem for holonomic sequences.

---

# What would actually prove M2?

The cleanest sufficient statement is not a resultant bound but a recurrence decorrelation theorem:

For every prime p and every Apéry zero r,
\[
\#\{2\le h<p:N_h(r)=0\}\ll p^{o(1)}.
\]

Then
\[
D_p
=\sum_{r\in Z_p}\#\{h:N_h(r)=0\}
\ll |Z_p|p^{o(1)}.
\]
Combined with ZM_2,
\[
\sum_p|Z_p|^2\ll \pi(X),
\]
this gives the pair-zero bound.

A stronger version is:

\[
\Pr_{r\in Z_p}(N_h(r)=0)
\approx \frac{\deg N_h}{p},
\]
which is the expected random model.

# Relation to known theory

## Katz / monodromy

The transfer-matrix interpretation suggests using Katz-style monodromy. The question becomes whether the projective cocycle generated by the Apéry recurrence has large image.

Large monodromy would imply that hitting the same projective point repeatedly is rare.

## Difference Galois

The h-direction recurrence is precisely where difference Galois ideas may help. Showing the h-cocycle has Galois group SL_2 would rule out periodic degeneracies.

But, as with Q563, SL_2 symmetry alone does not automatically give quantitative zero counts.

## Resultants

Resultants are best viewed as a diagnostic:

* compute small-h resultants;
* detect exceptional primes;
* identify whether special algebraic relations exist.

They are unlikely to give the full asymptotic bound.

# Final assessment

| Approach | Likely value |
|---|---|
| Fixed-h degree bound | Too weak |
| Newton polygons | Not the right invariant |
| Direct resultant Res(b_r,N_h) | No bounded-degree b_r polynomial exists |
| Integer gcd(b_r,N_h(r)) | Conceptually nice but hard |
| Fix r, vary h | Most promising |
| h-transfer monodromy | Likely correct framework |

The real missing theorem is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The restart cocycle has no low-order projective returns on Apéry zero fibers.}
}
\]

This is a horizontal equidistribution statement for the same SL_2 dynamics that controls the Apéry recurrence. It is closer to a finite-field monodromy/difference-Galois problem than to a classical resultant estimate.
