# Independent audit of the triple-occupancy/resultant proof

## Executive verdict

The candidate theorem is valid after two points that were only implicit in the sketch are written out:

```text
1. content primes must be excluded before replacing N_h by its primitive part;
2. the Sylvester argument must be stated as a nullity bound, so that
   leading-coefficient degree drops modulo p are harmless.
```

Both repairs are available uniformly from the banked Problem 3.2 inputs.  There is no fatal gap.  In particular, the complex root-strip theorem and the addition law do imply

```text
gcd_Q(N_h,N_k)=1            for every 1 <= h < k.
```

The corrected proof gives, for all sufficiently large `X`,

```text
S(X):=sum_(p<=X) |Z_p|
 << X^(8/5) (log X)^(-4/5).                           (0.1)
```

The proof is genuinely cross-prime: the saving comes from charging triples of nearby zeros to prime divisors of the fixed nonzero integers

```text
Res(N_h^prim,N_k^prim).
```

No numerical regression is needed for the general theorem.  Finite computations can check the identities and small resultants, but cannot replace the all-height root-strip theorem or the all-pairs coprimality argument.

The remainder of this audit gives the proof with every endpoint, content, degree-drop, multiplicity, ceiling, logarithm, and dyadic issue made explicit.

## 1. Inputs and notation

Let

```text
Z_p={1 <= r < p : p divides b_r}.
```

Use the banked facts valid for primes `p>=7` in the nonwrapping range:

```text
(a) no two consecutive b-indices vanish modulo p;

(b) if 1 <= x < x+d < p and
        p | b_x, b_(x+d),
    then
        N_d(x)=0 mod p;

(c) deg N_d=3(d-1);

(d) all complex roots of N_d lie in
        -d < Re z < -1,
    with three roots in every unit strip;

(e) for 1 <= j <= d,

    N_d(-j)
     =(-1)^(j-1) ((j-1)!)^3 ((d-j)!)^3
       b_(j-1)b_(d-j),                                (1.1)

    and in particular these integer values are nonzero;

(f) the addition law

    N_k(X)
     =N_h(X)N_(k-h+1)(X+h-1)
      -(X+h)^6N_(h-1)(X)N_(k-h)(X+h).                (1.2)
```

The proof below uses only these facts, elementary polynomial height estimates, and elementary matrix/occupancy inequalities.

For an integer polynomial `f`, write

```text
cont(f)=gcd of its coefficients,
f^prim=f/cont(f),
```

with positive content.

## 2. Consecutive polynomial coprimality

### Lemma 2.1

For every `h>=2`,

```text
gcd_C(N_h,N_(h-1))=1.                                (2.1)
```

### Proof

Suppose `alpha` is a common root.  The defining recurrence at level `h-1` is

```text
N_h(X)
 =P(X+h-1)N_(h-1)(X)
  -(X+h-1)^6N_(h-2)(X).                              (2.2)
```

At `X=alpha`, either

```text
alpha=-(h-1)
```

or `N_(h-2)(alpha)=0`.  The first possibility is impossible because the exact negative-integer formula gives

```text
N_h(-(h-1)) != 0.
```

Thus `alpha` is a common root of `N_(h-1)` and `N_(h-2)`.  Repeating the argument reaches `N_1=1`, a contradiction.  This proves (2.1).

No squarefreeness assumption is used here.

## 3. All-pairs coprimality over Q

This is the main structural point in the sketch.

### Proposition 3.1

For all integers `1<=h<k`,

```text
gcd_Q(N_h,N_k)=1.                                    (3.1)
```

### Proof

The case `h=1` is immediate from `N_1=1`.  Let `h>=2`, and suppose that `alpha` is a common complex root of `N_h` and `N_k`.

Reduce the addition law (1.2) at `alpha`:

```text
0
 =-(alpha+h)^6 N_(h-1)(alpha)N_(k-h)(alpha+h).       (3.2)
```

The factors preceding the last one are nonzero:

```text
alpha+h != 0,
```

because `N_h(-h)!=0`, and

```text
N_(h-1)(alpha) != 0
```

by Lemma 2.1.  Hence

```text
N_(k-h)(alpha+h)=0.                                   (3.3)
```

If `k-h=1`, this contradicts `N_1=1`.  If `k-h>=2`, the root-strip theorem for `N_h` gives

```text
Re(alpha)>-h,
```

so

```text
Re(alpha+h)>0.                                       (3.4)
```

But every root of `N_(k-h)` has real part strictly less than `-1`, contradicting (3.3).  Thus no common root exists.

The strips for `N_h` and `N_k` need not themselves be disjoint.  The decisive point is that the addition law translates the putative common root by `+h`, moving it into the positive half-plane.

Consequently the primitive resultant

```text
R_(h,k)
 :=Res(N_h^prim,N_k^prim)                             (3.5)
```

is a nonzero integer for every `h<k`.

## 4. Content primes

Passing to primitive parts is not automatically legitimate modulo a prime dividing the content.  In the range used by the proof, such primes do not occur.

### Lemma 4.1

If `p>=7` and `2<=h<p`, then

```text
p does not divide cont(N_h).                          (4.1)
```

### Proof

If `p|cont(N_h)`, then every value of `N_h` is zero modulo `p`.  In particular, using (1.1) at `j=1,2`,

```text
N_h(-1)=((h-1)!)^3 b_(h-1),                          (4.2)

N_h(-2)=-5((h-2)!)^3 b_(h-2).                        (4.3)
```

Because `h<p`, both factorials are units modulo `p`.  Since `p>=7`, (4.2)--(4.3) imply

```text
p | b_(h-1),
p | b_(h-2),
```

contradicting the no-consecutive-zero theorem.  The argument also covers `h=2`, where `b_0=1`.

Therefore, whenever

```text
P<p<=2P,
2<=h<k<H<=P,
```

reduction modulo `p` has the same finite roots for `N_h` and `N_h^prim`, and likewise for `N_k`.

### Scope note

If one did not use the banked no-consecutive-zero theorem, the candidate sketch would indeed have a content-prime omission.  It could also be repaired by separately charging content primes through their coefficient heights, but Lemma 4.1 is cleaner and exact in the repository setting.

## 5. The precise Sylvester/Smith inequality

Let `f,g in Z[X]` have degrees `m,n` over `Q`, and assume

```text
Res(f,g) != 0.
```

Let `S(f,g)` be the `(m+n) x (m+n)` integer Sylvester matrix, so

```text
det S(f,g)=+/-Res(f,g).                               (5.1)
```

For a prime `p`, let

```text
nu_p(f,g)=nullity over F_p of S(f,g) mod p.           (5.2)
```

### Lemma 5.1: Smith bound

```text
nu_p(f,g) <= v_p(Res(f,g)).                           (5.3)
```

### Proof

Write the Smith normal form over `Z` as

```text
diag(d_1,...,d_(m+n)),
```

with every `d_i` nonzero because the rational Sylvester matrix is invertible.  Modulo `p`, the nullity is exactly the number of invariant factors divisible by `p`.  Each such factor contributes at least one to

```text
v_p(det S)=sum_i v_p(d_i).
```

This proves (5.3).

### Lemma 5.2: common roots versus nullity

Assume neither reduction is the zero polynomial.  Let

```text
rho_p(f,g)
 =#{x in F_p : f(x)=g(x)=0}.                          (5.4)
```

Then

```text
rho_p(f,g)
 <= deg gcd_Fp(f mod p,g mod p)
 <= nu_p(f,g).                                        (5.5)
```

The second inequality remains valid when one or both leading coefficients vanish modulo `p`.  One way to see it is to view the Sylvester matrix as the linear map

```text
(A,B) |-> Af+Bg
```

with the original degree bounds.  Every common divisor of degree `q` produces at least a `q`-dimensional kernel.  Degree drops can create additional kernel dimensions (projective roots at infinity); they cannot remove those coming from the finite gcd.

Equivalently, for the distinct-root quantity actually needed here, every common root in an algebraic closure gives a left-null evaluation vector, and distinct roots give independent Vandermonde vectors.

Combining (5.3)--(5.5),

```text
rho_p(f,g) <= v_p(Res(f,g)).                          (5.6)
```

Thus leading-coefficient degree drops are not an exceptional set that needs to be discarded.  They are already absorbed by the Sylvester nullity.  The only dangerous case would be a zero polynomial modulo `p`, which Lemma 4.1 excludes for the present `N_h`.

Multiplicity is also harmless: the triple statistic counts distinct starting indices, while gcd degree and Sylvester nullity count with at least as much multiplicity.

## 6. Uniform coefficient and resultant heights

Use the coefficient `l^1` norm

```text
||sum a_j X^j||_1=sum |a_j|.
```

For the fixed cubic `P`, there is an absolute constant `C` such that

```text
||P(X+n)||_1 <= C(n+1)^3,                             (6.1)

||(X+n)^6||_1=(n+1)^6.                               (6.2)
```

The recurrence and submultiplicativity give

```text
||N_(n+1)||_1
 <= C(n+1)^6 (||N_n||_1+||N_(n-1)||_1).              (6.3)
```

Induction therefore yields

```text
log ||N_h||_1 << h log(2h).                           (6.4)
```

Dividing by the content cannot increase this norm, so the same estimate holds for `N_h^prim`.

Put

```text
d_h=deg N_h^prim=3(h-1).
```

Hadamard's inequality applied row-by-row to the Sylvester determinant gives

```text
log |R_(h,k)|
 <= d_k log ||N_h^prim||_2
    +d_h log ||N_k^prim||_2
 << hk log(2H)                                       (6.5)
```

uniformly for `2<=h<k<H`.

No hidden exponential-in-`hk` coefficient estimate is being assumed: (6.4) is `O(h log h)` in logarithmic height, and multiplication by the opposite degree produces exactly the `O(hk log H)` resultant bound.

Summing over all gap pairs,

```text
sum_(2<=h<k<H) hk << H^4,                             (6.6)
```

because, for example,

```text
sum_(h<k<H) hk
 <= (sum_(n<H)n)^2/2.
```

Hence

```text
sum_(2<=h<k<H) log |R_(h,k)|
 << H^4 log(2H).                                      (6.7)
```

This confirms the candidate's `H^4`, not `H^6`, budget.

## 7. Physical cells and the triple count

Fix a dyadic prime block

```text
P<p<=2P
```

and an integer `H` with

```text
3<=H<=P.                                             (7.1)
```

Partition the physical index interval

```text
{1,2,...,p-1}
```

into consecutive cells, each containing at most `H` integers.  Let

```text
m_p=ceil((p-1)/H),
k_(p,c)=#(Z_p in cell c),
T_p=sum_c binom(k_(p,c),3).                           (7.2)
```

Every triple counted by `T_p` has a unique ordering

```text
x < x+h < x+k.                                       (7.3)
```

Because all three positions lie in one cell,

```text
1<=h<k<H.                                            (7.4)
```

Gap `h=1` cannot occur by no-consecutive zeros, so one may take `h>=2`.  Also

```text
1<=x,
x+k<=p-1,                                           (7.5)
```

which is exactly the physical nonwrapping range needed for the gap implication.  Therefore

```text
N_h(x)=N_k(x)=0 mod p.                               (7.6)
```

For fixed `(h,k)`, distinct triples give distinct starting points `x`.  Consequently, with

```text
rho_p(h,k)
 =#{x in F_p : N_h(x)=N_k(x)=0},                     (7.7)
```

one has

```text
T_p
 <=sum_(2<=h<k<H) rho_p(h,k).                         (7.8)
```

The right side counts all finite-field roots, including nonphysical and cut-edge roots, so (7.8) is safely one-sided.  Endpoint roots such as the known `X=-3 mod 73` coincidence for `N_3,N_4` can only enlarge the upper bound; they do not correspond to a physical triple when `x+k>=p`.

By Lemmas 4.1 and 5.1--5.2,

```text
rho_p(h,k) <= v_p(R_(h,k)).                           (7.9)
```

Thus

```text
sum_(P<p<=2P) T_p
 <=sum_(2<=h<k<H) sum_(P<p<=2P) v_p(R_(h,k)).         (7.10)
```

For every nonzero integer `R`,

```text
sum_(P<p<=2P) v_p(R)
 <= log |R|/log P.                                    (7.11)
```

Using (6.7),

```text
boxed:

sum_(P<p<=2P) T_p
 << H^4 log(2H)/log P.                                (7.12)
```

This is the central cross-prime estimate.

## 8. Occupancy inequality

For every integer `u>=0`,

```text
u <= 2+(6 binom(u,3))^(1/3).                          (8.1)
```

For `u<=2` this is immediate.  For `u>=3`,

```text
6 binom(u,3)=u(u-1)(u-2)>=(u-2)^3.
```

Apply (8.1) in each cell and then Holder with exponents `3` and `3/2`:

```text
|Z_p|
 <=2m_p+(6T_p)^(1/3)m_p^(2/3).                       (8.2)
```

Summing over the prime block gives

```text
Delta S(P)
 :=sum_(P<p<=2P)|Z_p|

 <=2 sum_p m_p
   +6^(1/3)(sum_p T_p)^(1/3)(sum_p m_p)^(2/3).        (8.3)
```

Since `p<=2P`, `H<=P`, and

```text
m_p=ceil((p-1)/H)<=2P/H+1<=3P/H,
```

Chebyshev's prime-counting bound gives

```text
sum_(P<p<=2P)m_p
 << P^2/(H log P).                                    (8.4)
```

Insert (7.12) and (8.4) into (8.3):

```text
Delta S(P)
 << P^2/(H log P)
   +P^(4/3)H^(2/3)(log(2H))^(1/3)/log P.              (8.5)
```

The powers of `H` are

```text
(H^4)^(1/3) (H^-1)^(2/3)=H^(2/3),
```

and the three logarithmic denominators combine to exactly one factor `1/log P`.

Cell ceilings and the final short cell have therefore caused no extra term beyond the first term in (8.5).

## 9. Optimization

Let

```text
L=log P
```

and, for sufficiently large `P`, choose

```text
H=floor(P^(2/5)L^(-1/5)).                              (9.1)
```

Changing `H` by `O(1)` does not affect the estimates.  This choice satisfies

```text
3<=H<=P,
log(2H) asymp log P.                                  (9.2)
```

The two terms in (8.5) are balanced because

```text
H^5 asymp P^2/log P.                                  (9.3)
```

For the first term,

```text
P^2/(H log P)
 << P^(8/5)(log P)^(-4/5).                            (9.4)
```

For the second,

```text
P^(4/3)H^(2/3)(log H)^(1/3)/log P

 << P^(4/3+4/15)
    (log P)^(-2/15+1/3-1)

 = P^(8/5)(log P)^(-4/5).                             (9.5)
```

Therefore

```text
boxed:

sum_(P<p<=2P)|Z_p|
 << P^(8/5)(log P)^(-4/5).                            (9.6)
```

The implied constant is absolute once the banked root-strip and gap-transfer constants are fixed.

## 10. Dyadic summation

Let `X` be large.  Sum (9.6) over dyadic blocks

```text
(X/2^(j+1),X/2^j].                                    (10.1)
```

For blocks with lower endpoint at least `X^(1/2)`, the logarithms are comparable to `log X`, and the powers form a convergent geometric series.  Their total is

```text
<< X^(8/5)(log X)^(-4/5).                             (10.2)
```

For primes `p<=X^(1/2)`, use the elementary bound

```text
|Z_p|<=p-1.
```

Then

```text
sum_(p<=X^(1/2)) |Z_p|
 <=sum_(p<=X^(1/2))p
 << X/log X,                                          (10.3)
```

which is smaller than (10.2).  The finitely many primes below `7` are absorbed into the constant.

Thus

```text
boxed:

S(X)=sum_(p<=X)|Z_p|
 << X^(8/5)(log X)^(-4/5).                            (10.4)
```

## 11. Audit ledger

### Physical endpoints

**Pass.**  A same-cell triple has `x+k<=p-1`; both gap-continuant implications are in the nonwrapping range.  Extending the root count to all of `F_p` is an upper bound, so cut-edge roots do not invalidate the estimate.

### Content primes

**The sketch needed an explicit sentence.**  Lemma 4.1 excludes them for `h<p`, using the exact values at `-1,-2` and no consecutive Apéry zeros.  Thus primitive reduction preserves the relevant roots.

### Leading-coefficient degree drops modulo p

**Pass after correction.**  Do not rely only on the equality

```text
nullity Sylvester = degree gcd,
```

which assumes retained degrees in its simplest form.  Use

```text
degree gcd <= nullity <= v_p(resultant).
```

Degree drops add possible nullity at infinity and therefore only make the upper bound larger.

### Exact Sylvester/Smith inequality

**Pass.**  It follows directly from Smith normal form of the full-rank integer Sylvester matrix.

### Multiplicities and distinct roots

**Pass.**  The statistic counts distinct integer starting points.  Gcd degree and Sylvester nullity count at least those roots, with multiplicity if present, so repeated factors only overcount.

### All-pairs Q-coprimality

**Pass, general theorem.**  It is not a finite regression.  Addition translates a putative common root by `+h`; the root-strip theorem then gives a positive-real-part root of `N_(k-h)`, impossible.

### Cell ceilings

**Pass.**  With `H<=P`,

```text
ceil((p-1)/H)<<P/H.
```

The final short cell has diameter below `H` and causes no endpoint error.

### Resultant-height sum

**Pass.**  Individual logarithmic height is `O(hk log H)`, and

```text
sum_(h<k<H) hk=O(H^4).
```

There is no missing factor `H^2`.

### Logarithmic optimization

**Pass.**  The correct balance is

```text
H^5 asymp P^2/log P,
```

so `H asymp P^(2/5)(log P)^(-1/5)` and both terms have logarithmic exponent `-4/5`.

### Dyadic summation

**Pass.**  The exponent `8/5>0` makes the upper dyadic blocks geometrically dominant; low blocks are disposed of by `|Z_p|<=p`.

## 12. What is machine-checkable and what is not

The following are suitable finite regressions but are not proof-critical once the symbolic lemmas are established:

```text
- construct N_h for small h and verify the addition law;
- verify the negative-integer formula for small h,j;
- compute gcd_Q(N_h,N_k)=1 and nonzero resultants in a finite range;
- compare Sylvester nullity and gcd degrees for sample bad primes;
- enumerate cells/triples for small p and verify (7.8).
```

The following must remain all-height theorems:

```text
- the complex root-strip localization for every N_h;
- consecutive polynomial coprimality;
- the all-pairs coprimality deduction in Proposition 3.1;
- the physical gap implication for every p and h<p;
- no consecutive Apéry zeros modulo p;
- the uniform coefficient-height induction.
```

A finite resultant table cannot substitute for Proposition 3.1, because the optimizing parameter `H` tends to infinity with `P`.
