# Is the Apéry coefficient a single Jacobi sum power?

## Executive summary

The proposed formula

\[
b_r=-\frac{p}{p-1}J(\phi,\omega^{-r})^4
\]

is not the Apéry coefficient. The error is not a small normalization issue: it comes from identifying the Greene \({}_4F_3\) Mellin coefficient with the Apéry sequence term. The Greene character expansion is a trace formula for a hypergeometric sheaf, while the Apéry coefficient \(b_r\) is a Frobenius trace/period coordinate of the Apéry motive. They are related, but not by a single Jacobi sum fourth power.

The correct object is a hypergeometric trace function, which is generally a sum of Jacobi/Gauss products after Fourier transform.

---

# (A) Error in the Greene computation

The algebraic manipulation of the Greene definition is formally correct, but the interpretation is wrong at the last step.

Greene defines

\[
{}_4F_3(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi;\epsilon,\epsilon,\epsilon;t)
\]

as a function of the parameter \(t\in\mathbb F_p\). Expanding in multiplicative characters gives

\[
{}_4F_3(t)=\frac{p}{p-1}\sum_\chi C(\chi)\chi(t).
\]

The coefficient \(C(\chi)\) is a Fourier coefficient of the trace function in the variable \(t\).

However, the Mellin transform of the Apéry sequence is not obtained by simply taking

\[
(p-1)C(\omega^r).
\]

The Apéry sequence is the coefficient sequence of the period solution at a singular fiber. The Mellin transform involves the finite-field period normalization and the Frobenius eigenvalue normalization. These introduce Gauss-sum factors which cancel the apparent powers of \(p\).

The contradiction you found is therefore a diagnostic that the Fourier coefficient was mistaken for the motive trace.

---

# (B) Correct normalization

The finite-field hypergeometric normalization uses Gauss sums rather than raw Jacobi sums.

A typical McCarthy normalization has the form

\[
{}_nF_{n-1}^*(\alpha;\beta;t)
=
\frac1{p-1}\sum_\chi
\left(\prod_i\frac{G(\alpha_i\chi)}{G(\alpha_i)}\right)
\left(\prod_j\frac{G(\overline{\beta_j})}{G(\overline{\beta_j}\chi)}\right)
\chi(t).
\]

The crucial point is that every Gauss sum has complex size \(p^{1/2}\), and the normalization divides by exactly the appropriate powers. The resulting trace value has Weil size compatible with the rank of the motive.

The ordinary Frobenius eigenvalue has slope 0, so its p-adic valuation is 0. That information is encoded only after the Gauss/Jacobi factors are combined into the Frobenius trace; an isolated \(J^4\) does not have this property.

---

# (C) Is Apéry \(b_r\) equal to Greene \({}_4F_3\)?

No, not directly.

The Apéry numbers

\[
b_r=\sum_k {r\choose k}^2 {r+k\choose k}^2
\]

are coefficients of the analytic period

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}b_rx^r.
\]

The Picard-Fuchs equation is the differential equation of this period. A finite-field \({}_4F_3\) gives the Frobenius trace function associated to the reduction of the motive.

The relationship is:

\[
\text{Apéry recurrence}
\longrightarrow
\text{Picard-Fuchs motive}
\longrightarrow
\text{finite-field hypergeometric sheaf}
\longrightarrow
\text{Frobenius trace}.
\]

The last arrow is not the identity map on coefficients.

There is also a subtlety from singular fibers. The Apéry family has bad fibers, and the sheaf must be extended by middle extension. Local monodromy corrections affect the trace formula. They are exactly the kind of terms missing in the naive single Jacobi-sum formula.

# (D) p=7 Jacobi sum test

The proposed test already shows the failure conceptually.

For \(p=7\), take a primitive root \(g=3\). The quadratic character is

\[
\phi=(\cdot/7).
\]

The Jacobi sums

\[
J(\phi,\omega^r)=\sum_x\phi(x)\omega(1-x)^r
\]

live in the cyclotomic field generated by seventh roots of unity. They are algebraic integers of size about \(\sqrt7\), not elements of \(\mathbb F_7\).

Therefore the expression

\[
-7J(\phi,\omega^r)^4/6
\]

cannot be compared directly with

\[
[1,5,3,3,3,5]\pmod7.
\]

A reduction modulo the prime above 7 would have to be chosen, together with the correct Gauss normalization. Different embeddings give different algebraic representatives.

So the mismatch is expected: the proposed expression is not the finite-field Apéry trace.

---

# (E) Correct expression

The correct expression is a hypergeometric trace sum.

Schematically:

\[
T(r)=\sum_{\chi}
\frac{G(\phi\chi)^4}{G(\chi)^3G(\epsilon)}\chi(t_r)
\]

or an equivalent Jacobi/Gauss-sum expression.

After converting Gauss sums into Jacobi sums, one obtains a linear combination of Jacobi products, not a single term:

\[
T(r)=\sum_\chi a_\chi J_\chi^4.
\]

The Mellin variable \(r\) indexes the character twist, but the Frobenius trace sums over all internal characters \(\chi\).

This is why the zero set problem is difficult: \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\) is not asking whether one Jacobi sum vanishes. It is asking when a hypergeometric trace function vanishes.

---

# Consequence for AFP

This actually supports the earlier sheaf approach.

The relevant statement is not:

\[
b_r=J(\phi,\omega^r)^4.
\]

Instead:

\[
b_r=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_r|\mathcal H)
\]

for a hypergeometric/K3 sheaf \(\mathcal H\).

Then AFP becomes a trace-function zero estimate:

\[
\#\{r:b_r=0\}
\]

rather than a Jacobi-sum valuation problem.

The bounded zero multiplicity observed computationally is therefore consistent with Katz hypergeometric monodromy, not with a single Jacobi sum formula.
