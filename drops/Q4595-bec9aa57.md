\tag{3.1}

\tag{3.2}

 \mathscr R_h^+(\lambda;q,q')

\tag{3.3}
\]

\[
 \boxed{p\mid\mathscr R_h^+(\lambda;q,q').}
\tag{3.4}

\[
 B r'+q'h\equiv\lambda\pmod p,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr R_h^-(\lambda;q,q')

\tag{3.5}

\[
 \log\bigl(2+|\mathscr R_h^\pm|\bigr)

\tag{3.6}

Indeed after clearing the denominator \(B\), every scaled matrix entry is a cubic polynomial in \(\lambda\), \(q\), \(q'\), \(B\), and an index at most \(h\); in the present range all have logarithmic height \(O(\log N)\), and the determinant has size \(h-1\).

 \boxed{\ll h.}
\tag{3.7}

However the canonical root-strip theorem says only that every other root of \(N_h\) has real part in \((-h,-1)\); it does **not** prove that there are no other rational values of the two coefficient ratios in (3.3)--(3.5). # 4. A strong positive theorem: the unpruned repeated-prime Gram block

The repeated characteristic has a much better use if one keeps the Fourier rows intact.

For fixed \(p\), define

\[
 G_p(d)
 =\sum_{\substack{q\asymp N\\q\ne p}}
 u_q\widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right)
 F_p(d\bar q)F_q(d\bar p).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The one-shared-prime off-diagonal with common \(p\), before row masks, is

\[
 \sum_d|W(|d|/D)|^2
 \sum_p|u_p|^2
 \sum_{q\ne q'}c_{p,q}(d)\overline{c_{p,q'}(d)},
\]

where the inner coefficients are those in (4.1).  Since

\[
 \sum_{q\ne q'}c_q\bar c_{q'}
 =\left|\sum_qc_q\right|^2-\sum_q|c_q|^2
 \le\left|\sum_qc_q\right|^2,
\tag{4.2}
\]

its real part is bounded by

\[
 \mathfrak H_1
 :=\sum_p|u_p|^2\sum_d|W(|d|/D)|^2|G_p(d)|^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For fixed \(p,d\), Cauchy in the foreign prime gives

\[
 |G_p(d)|^2
 \le
 \left(\sum_q|u_q|^2|F_q(d\bar p)|^2\right)
 \left(\sum_q\left|\widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right)\right|^2
                 |F_p(d\bar q)|^2\right).
\tag{4.4}
\]

The second factor is

\[
 \ll pZ(p).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed \(q\mapsto d\bar q\pmod p\) has bounded multiplicity on fixed ratio boxes, while finite-field Parseval gives

\[
 \sum_{a\bmod p}|F_p(a)|^2=pZ(p).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Now sum the first factor over the literal signed dyadic \(d\)-support.  Since \(D<N\), for fixed \(p,q\) the residues \(d\bar p\pmod q\) have bounded multiplicity, hence again by Parseval

\[
 \sum_d|W(|d|/D)|^2|F_q(d\bar p)|^2
 \ll qZ(q).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \sum_d|W|^2|G_p(d)|^2
 \ll pZ(p)Q_1(N),
\]

where

\[
 Q_1(N)=\sum_{q\asymp N}|u_q|^2qZ(q).
\]

Summing \(p\) gives

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak H_1\ll Q_1(N)^2.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

The first zero moment yields

\[
 Q_1(N)
 \ll \frac{(\log N)^2}{N}
       \sum_{q\asymp N}Z(q)
 \ll N^{3/5}\log N,
\tag{4.9}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak H_1\ll N^{6/5}(\log N)^2.}
\tag{4.10}
\]

This is the genuine repeated-characteristic gain.  It uses the actual \(F_p,F_q\), prime weights, and signed \(d\)-sum.  It does not use the determinant support count (2.4), so both circular branches are retained automatically.

## Insertion-ready proposition

```tex
\begin{proposition}[Unpruned one-shared-prime Gram bound]
\label{prop:ad-one-shared-gram}
Let
\[
 u_p=\frac{\log p}{p}v(p/N),\qquad
 F_p(a)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal Z_p}e_p(ar),
\]
and let $D<N^{1-\delta}$ for a fixed $\delta>0$.  Put
\[
 Q_1(N)=\sum_{p\asymp N}|u_p|^2pZ(p).
\]
For one orientation of the TT* stratum in which the two semiprime
denominators are $pq$ and $pq'$ with $q\ne q'$, define the unpruned
common-$p$ block using the literal dyadic $d$-kernel and the complete
zero-row factors $F_p(d\bar q)F_q(d\bar p)$.  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\mathfrak U_{1\mathrm{sh}}^{(p)}(N,D)
 \ll Q_1(N)^2.
\]
Consequently the sum of all four possible orientations satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\mathfrak U_{1\mathrm{sh}}(N,D)
 \ll Q_1(N)^2
 \ll N^{6/5}(\log N)^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $p$ put
\[
 G_p(d)=\sum_{q\ne p}u_q
 \widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right)
 F_p(d\bar q)F_q(d\bar p).
\]
Removing the equal-denominator terms $q=q'$ from the Gram expansion can
only decrease its real upper bound, since
\[
 \sum_{q\ne q'}c_q\overline{c_{q'}}
 =\left|\sum_qc_q\right|^2-\sum_q|c_q|^2.
\]
Thus it suffices to bound
\[
 \sum_p|u_p|^2\sum_d|W(|d|/D)|^2|G_p(d)|^2.
\]
Cauchy in $q$ gives
\[
 |G_p(d)|^2\le
 \left(\sum_q|u_q|^2|F_q(d\bar p)|^2\right)
 \left(\sum_q\left|\widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right)\right|^2
 |F_p(d\bar q)|^2\right).
\]
On fixed ratio boxes, $q\mapsto d\bar q\pmod p$ has bounded
multiplicity, so the second factor is
\[
 \ll\sum_{a\bmod p}|F_p(a)|^2=pZ(p).
\]
Since $D<N^{1-\delta}$, for fixed $p,q$ the signed dyadic values of
$d\bar p\pmod q$ also have bounded multiplicity.  Hence
\[
 \sum_d|W(|d|/D)|^2|F_q(d\bar p)|^2\ll qZ(q).
\]
It follows that
\[
 \sum_d|W|^2|G_p(d)|^2\ll pZ(p)Q_1(N).
\]
After multiplication by $|u_p|^2$ and summation in $p$ this is
$Q_1(N)^2$.  Finally the unconditional first zero moment gives
\[
 Q_1(N)\ll\frac{(\log N)^2}{N}
 \sum_{p\asymp N}Z(p)\ll N^{3/5}\log N.
\]
\end{proof}
```

# 5. Sparse row masks: how far the raw theorem transfers unconditionally

The reason (4.10) cannot simply be quoted for an arbitrary sharp subset is that deleting complex row terms can destroy cancellation.  There is, however, a strong deterministic stability theorem whenever the deleted rows form a sparse graph/band.

Let \(E_{p,q}\subset\mathcal Z_p\times\mathcal Z_q\) be a deleted row family, and suppose

\[
 \sup_{p,q,r}
 \#\{s\in\mathcal Z_q:(r,s)\in E_{p,q}\}
 \le L.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
 E_{p,q}(d)=
 \sum_{(r,s)\in E_{p,q}}e\!\left(\frac{d\,t_{p,q}(r,s)}{pq}\right).
\]

Then trivially but crucially

\[
 |E_{p,q}(d)|\le LZ(p).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Also, on a fixed prime ratio box,

\[
 \sum_{q\asymp N}|u_q|\ll1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore the common-\(p\) Hilbert-square of the deleted rows is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak H(E)
 &:=\sum_p|u_p|^2\sum_d|W(|d|/D)|^2
 \left|\sum_q u_q\widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right)E_{p,q}(d)\right|^2\\
 &\ll D L^2\sum_{p\asymp N}|u_p|^2Z(p)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Using the canonical pointwise bound \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\) and the first zero moment,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_p|u_p|^2Z(p)^2
 &\ll \frac{(\log N)^2}{N^2}
       N^{2/3}\sum_{p\asymp N}Z(p)\\
 &\ll N^{4/15}\log N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
