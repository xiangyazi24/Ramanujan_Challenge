# P3.2 globalization from the singular unit-companion law

## Verdict

The literal **quadratic slope-8 companion** for the term
\[
p^2c_{2p-1-j}=p^2c_{8p-m-1}
\]
is not the right global object.  Relative to the canonical affine slope-8 cubic row it requires division by the candidate prime \(p\).  Unless one proves a new *global integral divisibility* statement, clearing that division simultaneously over the quotient-six prime window introduces at least one candidate-prime LCM; over the full possible window \(m/7<p\le m/6\) that already has exponential height.  A merely mod-\(p\) slope-8 row also has the expected Bockstein gauge ambiguity and cannot canonically recover the divided coordinate.

There is, however, a smaller denominator-free lift.  Put
\[
j=m-6p,\qquad r=p-1-j=7p-m-1,\qquad s=p+r=2p-1-j=8p-m-1.
\]
Then
\[
s+m+1=8p.
\]
Hence the cubic affine Euler/Newton weight gives the **prime-independent integral slope-8 row**
\[
U_8(m;s):=(s+m+1)^3c_s=(8p)^3c_s=512p^3c_s.
\]
Couple it only to the already needed slope-7 value \(B_7=b_r\), and form the integral mixed row
\[
M:=1024B_7-U_8.
\]
On the singular locus where \(p\mid b_r\), the newly proved unit-companion congruence
\[
p^3c_{p+r}\equiv b_r+pg_r\pmod{p^2}
\]
gives
\[
M\equiv512p\left(\frac{b_r}{p}-g_r\right)
   \equiv512p\eta\pmod{p^2}.
\]
Thus \(M\) is an ordinary prime-independent integral row of the same subexponential-height type as the existing rows, while its **first Bockstein** realizes the desired mixed coordinate:
\[
\beta_p(M):=M/p\pmod p=512\eta.
\]
Because \(p>5\), \(512=2^9\) is a unit modulo every core prime.  Therefore \(512\eta\) is as good as \(\eta\) for all rank/nonvanishing arguments.  This is the smallest surviving lift: **one cubic slope-8 row, one integral row operation with the slope-7 row, and one local first-Bockstein readout.**  No LCM, and in particular no LCM-cubed denominator, is needed.

## 1. Re-index the lower companion on the quotient-six line

For a quotient-six candidate,
\[
j=m-6p,\qquad 0\le j<p.
\]
Define
\[
r:=p-1-j.
\]
Then the companion index in the given formula is
\[
2p-1-j=p+r.
\]
Substituting \(j=m-6p\),
\[
r=7p-m-1,
\]
and
\[
s:=p+r=8p-m-1.
\]
The key affine identity is therefore
\[
\boxed{s+m+1=8p.}
\]
This is precisely the identity needed to globalize the factor \(p^3\) without inserting a prime-dependent denominator.

The local target supplied in the question is
\[
\eta\equiv2\frac{b_r}{p}-p^2c_s\pmod p.
\]
Here the displayed divided term already presupposes the relevant singular branch \(p\mid b_r\).  I use that singularity below; it is not a consequence of \(p\mid b_j\) by itself unless the previously proved slope-7/singular-source relation supplies it.

Now use the unit-companion law at this \(r\):
\[
p^3c_s=p^3c_{p+r}\equiv b_r+pg_r\pmod{p^2}.

Since \(p\mid b_r\), division of this *congruence* by one \(p\) is legitimate and gives
\[
p^2c_s\equiv\frac{b_r}{p}+g_r\pmod p.

Consequently
\[
\boxed{\eta\equiv\frac{b_r}{p}-g_r\pmod p.}
\]
So the missing datum is intrinsically a first divided/Bockstein coordinate.  It is not an ordinary reduction of \(b_r\), \(g_r\), or \(c_s\) separately.

## 2. The cubic affine slope-8 row is integral and prime-independent

Let \(\Theta\) denote the coefficient-index operator on the Newton/Cartier source carrying the coefficients \(c_n\): on the \(n\)-th coefficient, \(\Theta\) acts by multiplication by \(n\).  Then the affine operator
\[
D_m:=\Theta+m+1
\]
acts at \(n=s\) by
\[
D_m|_{n=s}=s+m+1=8p.
\]
Therefore the cubic row
\[
R_8^{[3]}:=D_m^3 C
\]
(where \(C\) denotes the current coefficient source, or equivalently one applies the polynomial weight \(D_m^3\) to the existing Newton/Cartier coefficient row) evaluates at \(s\) to
\[
U_8=[x^s]R_8^{[3]}
=(s+m+1)^3c_s
=512p^3c_s.
\]
The important point is that the row is defined using only the **integral polynomial weight**
\[
(n+m+1)^3,
\]
not using \(p\), \(1/p\), or a product of candidate primes.  The prime enters only after evaluating at the affine slope-8 index \(s=8p-m-1\).

Using the unit-companion law,
\[
\boxed{U_8\equiv512b_r+512pg_r\pmod{p^2}.}
\]
Thus the cubic slope-8 row packages exactly the two pieces that occur in the singular companion law, but at the correct integral \(p^3\) scale.

### Height

Passing from a coefficient row to the row weighted by \((n+m+1)^3\) changes coefficient height by only a polynomial factor.  In the range relevant to the determinant, this is \(m^{O(1)}\).  Hence if the existing slope-4/slope-7 row family has height
\[
\exp(o(m)),
\]
then adjoining \(R_8^{[3]}\) still has
\[
m^{O(1)}\exp(o(m))=\exp(o(m)).
\]
The later row operation uses only the fixed integers \(1024\) and \(1\), so it does not alter that conclusion.

No candidate-prime denominator has appeared.

## 3. Exact mixed row and its Bockstein

Let \(B_7\) denote the existing integral row evaluated at the slope-7 index \(r=7p-m-1\), so that at the candidate under consideration
\[
B_7=b_r.
\]
Define the new integral row by the fixed row operation
\[
\boxed{M:=1024B_7-R_8^{[3]}.}
\]
At \(s=8p-m-1\),
\[
M=1024b_r-512p^3c_s.
\]
Using the local definition of \(\eta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
M/p
 &=1024\frac{b_r}{p}-512p^2c_s\\
 &=512\left(2\frac{b_r}{p}-p^2c_s\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore, on the singular branch \(p\mid b_r\),
\[
\boxed{M\equiv512p\eta\pmod{p^2}}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{\beta_p(M):=M/p\bmod p=512\eta.}
\]
This identity is even more direct than eliminating \(c_s\) using \(g_r\).  Using the unit-companion law gives the equivalent form
\[
\beta_p(M)
=512\left(\frac{b_r}{p}-g_r\right)
=512\eta.
\]

The factor \(512\) should **not** be divided out at the integral-source level.  Keep \(512\eta\) as the determinant coordinate.  For every core prime \(p>5\), \(512\) is invertible modulo \(p\), so
\[
\eta=0\pmod p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta_p(M)=0\pmod p.
\]
That is enough for all rank, nonvanishing, selector, and determinant-exclusion uses.

A compact algebraic specification of the surviving construction is:

```text
j := m - 6 p
r := p - 1 - j        = 7 p - m - 1
s := p + r            = 8 p - m - 1

D_m(n) := n + m + 1
U8     := D_m(s)^3 * c_s
        = 512 * p^3 * c_s

M      := 1024 * b_r - U8

on p | b_r:
    M ≡ 512 * p * eta        (mod p^2)
    beta_p(M) = M/p mod p
              = 512 * eta    (mod p)
```

This is the row that should be coupled to the existing slope-4/slope-7 determinant system.

## 4. Why the literal quadratic slope-8 companion is the wrong global row

One might try to realize \(p^2c_s\) directly by using the quadratic affine Euler weight.  At \(s=8p-m-1\),
\[
D_m(s)^2c_s=(8p)^2c_s=64p^2c_s.
\]
Call this putative row \(Q_8\).  Comparing with the canonical cubic row,
\[
U_8=(8p)^3c_s,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{Q_8=\frac{U_8}{8p}.}
\]
The fixed factor \(1/8\) is harmless.  The factor \(1/p\) is not.

The new unit-companion law proves that \(p^3c_s\) has the required \(p\)-adic integrality and even determines it modulo \(p^2\).  It does **not** prove a prime-independent integral identity saying that the universal cubic row \(U_8\) is divisible, in the global coefficient lattice, by the affine linear factor \(D_m=n+m+1\).  On a selected singular prime we know only the local \(p\)-adic divisibility needed to form the Bockstein.

Thus a literal \(Q_8\) requires one additional ingredient that is currently absent:

\[
\text{global integral divisibility }R_8^{[3]}\in D_m\cdot\mathcal L
\]
for the relevant integral source lattice \(\mathcal L\), or an equivalent canonical divided-Cartier/Frobenius operator whose numerator is *integrally* divisible by \(D_m\).

Without such a theorem, the quadratic row is not an integral prime-independent source.  Its exact deficit is **one candidate prime**.

This is stronger and more precise than saying merely that “denominators get large”: the obstruction is the missing global division
\[
\frac1{p}
\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad
\frac1{s+m+1}
\]
after the fixed factor \(8\) is ignored.

## 5. The LCM obstruction if one insists on literal division

For quotient-six candidates,
\[
0\le m-6p<p
\]
implies
\[
\boxed{m/7<p\le m/6.}
\]
Let \(P_m\) be the actual set of candidate primes surviving the earlier slope-4/slope-7 tests.  If the literal quadratic slope-8 row is produced by clearing the \(1/p\) denominator simultaneously for all \(p\in P_m\), a common scalar must contain
\[
L_m:=\operatorname{lcm}_{p\in P_m}p
     =\prod_{p\in P_m}p,
\]
since the candidates are distinct primes.  Therefore
\[
\log L_m=\sum_{p\in P_m}\log p.
\]
The exact subexponential-height criterion for this strategy is
\[
\sum_{p\in P_m}\log p=o(m).
\]
Nothing in the literal companion construction itself supplies that sparsity.

For the full possible quotient-six prime window, the prime number theorem in Chebyshev form gives
\[
\log\prod_{m/7<p\le m/6}p
 =\vartheta(m/6)-\vartheta(m/7)
 =\left(\frac16-\frac17+o(1)\right)m
 =\left(\frac1{42}+o(1)\right)m.
\]
Hence already one unresolved \(1/p\) costs
\[
\boxed{\exp(m/42+o(m))},
\]
which is not \(\exp(o(m))\).

If one instead starts from the raw \(c_s\) and clears a \(p^3\) denominator candidate-by-candidate, the corresponding common denominator is \(L_m^3\); over the full window its height is
\[
\boxed{\exp(m/14+o(m))}.
\]
The affine cubic row is exactly what removes this LCM-cubed catastrophe: the identity \(s+m+1=8p\) supplies \(p^3\) through a polynomial index weight rather than through scalar denominator clearing.

The remaining quadratic \(1/p\) is still exponentially expensive unless it is left as a **local Bockstein**, which is precisely what \(M\) does.

## 6. Gauge obstruction for a merely mod-p slope-8/Bockstein row

There is a second, independent reason not to seek \(\eta\) as an ordinary mod-\(p\) slope-8 companion.

Suppose a row value \(A\) is known only modulo \(p\).  To define a divided coordinate \(A/p\pmod p\), one must choose a lift modulo \(p^2\).  Replacing a lift by
\[
A' = A+pH
\]
does not change its reduction modulo \(p\), but changes the divided class by
\[
A'/p\equiv A/p+H\pmod p.
\]
Thus the Bockstein is not determined by the mod-\(p\) row: it has a full additive gauge ambiguity.

This is exactly the issue with trying to recover the missing singular coordinate from a first-order slope-8 companion.  A first-order Cartier/Newton row may detect the singular zero, but its reduction does not remember the first divided coefficient.

The newly proved unit-companion law removes the gauge only at the correct level: it specifies \(p^3c_s\) modulo \(p^2\), and the mixed numerator
\[
M=1024b_r-512p^3c_s
\]
is consequently specified modulo \(p^2\).  Since \(M\equiv0\pmod p\) on the singular locus, its class
\[
M/p\pmod p
\]
is canonical there.

So the right conceptual placement is
\[
\boxed{\eta\in\operatorname{gr}^1_p=F^1_p/F^2_p,}
\]
not in the ordinary residue layer \(F^0_p/F^1_p\).  A denominator-free global determinant should retain \(M\) in the integral lattice and read \(\eta\) from its first associated-graded/Bockstein component at each candidate prime.

Trying to avoid that upgrade by putting \(M/p\) itself into the global matrix reintroduces the LCM obstruction above.

This is the smallest structural change that survives the height requirement.

## 9. What would be required to resurrect the literal slope-8 companion

A literal quadratic/Bockstein source would become viable only if one proves one of the following genuinely stronger statements.

1. **Integral operator divisibility:** the cubic slope-8 source factors globally as
   \[
   R_8^{[3]}=D_m R_8^{[2]}
   \]
   inside the integral source lattice, with \(R_8^{[2]}\) still of \(\exp(o(m))\) height.

2. **Canonical divided Cartier/Frobenius:** an integral prime-uniform operation produces the quotient corresponding at \(s=8p-m-1\) to
   \[
   \frac{R_8^{[3]}(s)}{8p}=64p^2c_s
   \]
   without introducing \(1/p\) as a scalar and with a mod-\(p^2\) lift fixing the gauge.

3. **Candidate sparsity strong enough to clear denominators:**
   \[
   \sum_{p\in P_m}\log p=o(m).
   \]
   This would make the first-power candidate LCM subexponential, but it is a separate arithmetic sparsity theorem and does not follow from the companion law.

Absent one of these, the literal quadratic family should be rejected rather than normalized by an LCM.

## 10. Final answer

The affine slope-8 observation is useful, but at **cubic**, not quadratic, order.

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
r=7p-m-1,\qquad s=8p-m-1,\qquad s+m+1=8p,\\
U_8=(s+m+1)^3c_s=512p^3c_s,\\
M=1024b_r-U_8,\\
p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow
M\equiv512p\eta\pmod{p^2},\\
\beta_p(M)=512\eta\pmod p.
\end{gathered}}
\]

So:

- **Literal slope-8 quadratic companion:** obstructed by one unresolved \(1/p\); globally this is at least a candidate-prime LCM, and over the full quotient-six window it has exponential height.  A merely mod-\(p\) version also has Bockstein gauge ambiguity.
- **Naive raw cubic normalization:** would cost \(L_m^3\), exponentially worse.
- **Surviving lift:** a prime-independent integral **cubic affine slope-8 row**, coupled by the fixed integral combination \(1024R_7-R_8^{[3]}\), with \(\eta\) read only in the first \(p\)-adic graded/Bockstein layer.
- **Height:** only a polynomial factor beyond the existing slope-4/7 rows, hence still \(\exp(o(m))\).
- **Smallest next proof obligation:** a filtered/determinantal lemma that consumes the integral relation \(M\equiv512p\eta\pmod{p^2}\) directly, rather than globally dividing \(M\) by \(p\).

That is the denominator-free globalization route supported by the singular unit-companion law.
