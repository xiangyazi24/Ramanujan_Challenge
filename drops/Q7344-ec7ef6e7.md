# Q7344: Apéry gcd carrier analysis

## Verdict

**LIVE REDUCTION.** No proof of `log gcd(U1,U2,U3)=o(n)` is obtained. The strongest exact reduction is a holonomic/differential-system reformulation plus a precise obstruction: existing P-recursive gcd theorems do not apply to this variable-denominator construction.

## A. Holonomic system

Let
\[
L=\theta^3-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\]
Since `LB=0`, for `C_s=B Li_s` we have
\[
L(C_s)= [L,B]Li_s,
\]
so the products are holonomic. The logarithmic polylogarithms satisfy
\[
\theta Li_s=Li_{s-1},\qquad Li_1=-\log(1-t),\qquad \theta Li_0=t/(1-t).
\]
Therefore the closure gives a finite coupled system for
\[
(C_0,C_1,C_2,C_3),\quad C_s=B Li_s,
\]
with forcing terms generated by derivatives of B and the rational function \(t/(1-t)\). Eliminating lower components yields scalar P-recursive equations, but their order increases; there is no evidence that the minimal operators preserve the Apéry order three.

## B. Bezout/telescoping search

An identity of the form
\[
A_1(n)U_1(n)+A_2(n)U_2(n)+A_3(n)U_3(n)=E(n)
\]
with small-height `E` would solve the nuisance problem. The obstruction is denominator support.

Prime-by-prime, for `p<=n`, multiplying by `L_n^s` only guarantees integrality. A cancellation identity must track the exact valuations
\[
v_p(L_n^sR_s(n))
\]
and cannot freely divide by powers of `p`. The apparent large-prime carrier property `p|b_{n mod p}` is compatible with all three U's and does not by itself produce a Bezout relation.

No low-height exact identity among U1,U2,U3 is currently established.

## C. Holonomic gcd literature

Known gcd results for linear recurrences generally assume fixed-order recurrences with constant coefficients or strong arithmetic conditions on characteristic roots. Examples include work on divisibility sequences and recurrence gcd estimates by Bugeaud, Corvaja, Zannier and related results on exponential-polynomial sequences.

The Apéry sequence is instead a diagonal/holonomic sequence with polynomial-coefficient recurrence. The auxiliary sequences U_s additionally contain the moving modulus `L_n`. Thus the hypotheses of constant-coefficient recurrence gcd theorems are not met.

## D. Asymptotic ratio route

The dominant singularity gives
\[
R_s(n)\sim c_s\lambda^n,
\]
where
\[
\lambda=17+12\sqrt2.
\]
The ratio satisfies
\[
\frac{R_2(n)}{R_1(n)}
=\frac{Li_2(\lambda^{-1})}{Li_1(\lambda^{-1})}+O(1/n).
\]
This is only an analytic approximation. A gcd bound would require a lower bound on the linear form measuring the rational approximation created by the common divisor. An irrationality measure for the limiting constant would need to beat the available denominator size (roughly controlled by `L_n`) in a quantified way. The `O(1/n)` error alone gives no subexponential gcd estimate.

## E. Sharp reduction

The remaining exact target is:

1. Construct the minimal coupled differential system for `(C0,C1,C2,C3)`.
2. Compute the induced recurrence matrices modulo primes.
3. Determine whether the common divisor is forced by the Frobenius periodicity `b_{n mod p}` or whether U2/U3 introduce independent congruence constraints.

A successful proof likely needs a new arithmetic input: either a Frobenius-level incompatibility among the three polylog twists, or a quantitative irrationality/transcendence estimate tailored to the Apéry dominant constant.

Current status: **LIVE REDUCTION, not PROOF.**
