## Proposition: content control does not produce a second primitive target coordinate

Let

\[
f_k=\sum_{a=0}^k\binom{k}{a}^3,
\qquad
L_k(n)=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k},
\]

\[
Q_n(t)=\sum_{k=0}^nL_k(n)t^k,
\qquad
K_{n,a}(c)=[y^a]Q_n(c+y),
\]

\[
g_a(c)=\sum_{i=0}^a\binom ai(-c)^{a-i}f_i,
\qquad
T_{n,J}(c)=\sum_{a=0}^J K_{n,a}(c)g_a(c),
\]

and let

\[
\Gamma_{n,J}=\operatorname{cont}_{\mathbf Z[c]}T_{n,J}(c),
\qquad
P_{n,J}(c)=\frac{T_{n,J}(c)}{\Gamma_{n,J}}
\in\mathbf Z[c].
\]

For a shell target prime, write

\[
n=qp+r,\qquad
j=\min(r,p-1-r),\qquad j\le J<p,\qquad p\mid b_j.
\]

The landed shell theorem gives the raw congruence

\[
T_{n,J}(c)\equiv b_jQ_q(c^p)\pmod p
\]

and hence `p | Gamma_{n,J}`. Even if the new theorem supplies a subexponential global bound for `Gamma` and exact all-prime values of `v_p(Gamma)`, this does **not** imply any of the following after full content division:

1. `P_{n,J}` is a polynomial in `c^p` modulo `p`;
2. `P_{n,J}^{(r)}(1)` vanishes modulo `p` for `1 <= r < p`;
3. an adjacent-cutoff primitive Wronskian is divisible by `p`;
4. a primitive two-point divided difference is divisible by `p`.

All four implications fail at the first possible shell target. Moreover, the standard normalized Apéry companion is transverse, rather than target-safe, on an actual fresh three-hit row. Thus the companion/content theorem by itself cannot be turned into the requested second characteristic-zero value or a target-preserving two-row determinant.

## 2. Why the raw shell congruence loses all information after primary division

Suppose

\[
e=v_p(b_j)>0,\qquad v_p(\Gamma_{n,J})=e.
\]

At a target, the congruence

\[
T_{n,J}(c)\equiv b_jQ_q(c^p)\pmod p
\]

is simply `0 = 0`. It does not determine

\[
p^{-e}T_{n,J}(c)\pmod p.
\]

To deduce Frobenius support or derivative vanishing for the primitive quotient, one needs one additional primary digit, for example a congruence modulo `p^{e+1}` of the form

\[
T_{n,J}(c)\equiv b_jQ_q(c^p)\pmod{p^{e+1}}
\]

after matching the unit part of `Gamma`. The example above proves that this proposed lift is false for the uncorrected shell polynomial: for `e=1`, the quotient is `c^2-c^3`, not a constant, modulo `5`.

Exact primary **orders** of the content therefore do not control the first-primary **residue**. That residue is precisely where a second target-safe coordinate would have to live.

### 3.4 Adjoint Green identity

The discrete adjoint/Green identity telescopes a shell sum to endpoint data plus the WZ residual. At a target, its error is annihilated by the same scalar already present in the shell content. There are only two outcomes:

- retain that scalar, obtaining another presentation of the shell gcd or WZ residual;
- divide it out, exposing the first-primary residue, where the exact counterexample is a unit.

No rank-two target lattice is created by telescoping alone.

### 3.5 Two-point divided differences

A divided difference is useful only if both endpoint values remain target-safe after saturation. The exact first quotient gives `76 = 1 mod 5`. More generally, a universal denominator such as `c_1-c_0` is a window-prime unit for fixed small evaluation points and cannot manufacture target depth. If the denominator contains a target prime, division requires an explicit valuation margin; otherwise it removes the desired factor.

### 3.6 Resultants and adjacent Padé identities

The landed projective-resultant scripts already enforce the right rule: a raw resultant is irrelevant until content is stripped and target divisibility is rechecked. The adjacent-degree identity

\[
Q_{a-1}P_a-Q_aP_{a-1}
=\kappa_a\prod_{s=0}^{H}(X-s)
\]

shows that the natural resultant is controlled by a fixed-grid factor. It does not provide an independent target zero. Dividing by the fixed-grid factor leaves the same primitive rank obstruction.

## 5. Why a genuine second coordinate would solve the two-row divisibility problem

Let `S_X(m)` be the target-prime set at row `m`. Suppose one had two integer coordinates

\[
A_X(m),\qquad B_X(m)
\]

such that every `p in S_X(m)` divides both coordinates after all universal factors have been removed. Then for two rows

\[
\Delta_X(m_0,m_1)
=A_X(m_0)B_X(m_1)-A_X(m_1)B_X(m_0)
\]

one has the exact union divisibility

\[
\prod_{p\in S_X(m_0)\cup S_X(m_1)}p
\mid\Delta_X(m_0,m_1).
\]

Indeed, if `p` belongs to either row, both terms in the determinant contain a coordinate from that row. This is the correct mechanism. The present shell/companion lattice fails before the height estimate: after saturation it has only one target direction, while the companion direction is a target-unit.

## 6. Sharply sufficient reopening theorem

The following is the minimal theorem that would reopen this route without hiding the shell gcd.

### Primary-saturated companion cancellation theorem `PSCC(delta)`

There exist explicit integer-valued characteristic-zero coordinates

\[
A_X(m),B_X(m)
\]

constructed from the Franel shell and its companion, and explicit universal divisors

\[
u_A(X,m),u_B(X,m),u_\Delta(X;m_0,m_1),
\]

with the following properties.

**Target margin.** For every window target `p in S_X(m)`,

\[
v_p(A_X(m))\ge v_p(u_A(X,m))+1,
\]

\[
v_p(B_X(m))\ge v_p(u_B(X,m))+1.
\]

Thus

\[
A_X^\sharp(m)=A_X(m)/u_A(X,m),\qquad
B_X^\sharp(m)=B_X(m)/u_B(X,m)
\]

are integers still divisible by `p`. A statement only about the raw values is insufficient.

**Noncollapse.** The two primitive coordinates are not universally proportional, do not both lie in the principal ideal generated by the shell content, and are not both boundary presentations of the same WZ residual. For the selected pair,

\[
\Delta_X^\sharp(m_0,m_1)
e0.
\]

**Determinant margin.** Writing

\[
\Delta_X^{\rm raw}
=A_X(m_0)B_X(m_1)-A_X(m_1)B_X(m_0),
\]

one has, for every target prime in the union,

\[
v_p(\Delta_X^{\rm raw})
\ge v_p(u_\Delta(X;m_0,m_1))+1,
\]

so that

\[
\Delta_X^\sharp
=\Delta_X^{\rm raw}/u_\Delta
\in\mathbf Z\setminus\{0\}
\]

retains every target prime.

**Height saving.** Either pointwise

\[
\log|\Delta_X^\sharp(m_0,m_1)|=o(X),
\]

or on the required family of selected pairs, for some fixed `delta>0`,

\[
\frac1{|\mathcal M_X|}
\sum_{(m_0,m_1)\in\mathcal M_X}
\log^+|\Delta_X^\sharp(m_0,m_1)|
\ll X^{1-\delta+o(1)}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{p\in S_X(m_0)\cup S_X(m_1)}\log p
\le\log|\Delta_X^\sharp(m_0,m_1)|,
\]

with the corresponding averaged estimate. If the selected pair has seven window primes, all seven divide this nonzero primitive determinant.

### Equivalent first-primary formulation

For a direct derivative attack, `PSCC(delta)` can be replaced by a first-primary lift. Let

\[
R_{p,n,J}(c)
=p^{-v_p(\Gamma_{n,J})}T_{n,J}(c)\pmod p
\]

after removing the full unit part of the content. One needs a companion/adjoint functional `L` such that, for every actual Apéry target,

\[
R_{p,n,J}(1)=0,\qquad
\mathcal L(R_{p,n,J},V_{n,J})=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p,
\]

while the two corresponding characteristic-zero values are nonproportional and satisfy the determinant height clause. Ordinary derivatives, the adjacent-cutoff Wronskian, and the two-point divided difference do not satisfy this condition, by the exact obstruction.

This theorem is strictly stronger than subexponential content plus exact primary orders: it controls the first residue **after** content saturation.
