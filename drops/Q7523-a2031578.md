# Verdict

I do **not** find a theorem in the current G-function/Padé/Hankel literature that proves
\[
\operatorname{den}(R_n)=L_n\exp(-o(n))
\]
pointwise for every Apéry index.  The available general denominator theorems go in the opposite direction (upper bounds on denominators), and a theorem based only on “integer D-finite/G-function coefficients convolved with \(-\log(1-z)\)” is impossible by elementary counterexamples.

There is, however, a useful direct denominator reformulation that is stronger than the already-known scalar equivalence.  Define the **lcm-cleared convolution polynomial**
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_n(X):=
 \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{L_n}{k}\,b_{n-k}X^k\in\mathbf Z[X]
}
\tag{1}
\]
and its content
\[
\boxed{
C_n:=\operatorname{cont}(\mathcal P_n)
 =\gcd_{1\le k\le n}\left(\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}\right).
}
\tag{2}
\]
For every prime \(p>\sqrt n\), writing \(n=qp+r\), one has the exact local law
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(C_n)=\mathbf 1_{\{p\mid b_r\}}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
W_n=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}v_p(C_n)\log p,
\qquad
\log C_n=W_n+o(n).
}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(C_n\) is a **characteristic-zero integer carrier with exactly the desired medium-prime support and no companion-prime pollution**.  It also divides the denominator-cancellation factor \(L_n/\operatorname{den}(R_n)\).

This exposes the real obstruction.  Every dangerous medium prime divides **every coefficient** of (1), so it divides every integer linear combination of those coefficients.  But the same is true, for completely non-Apéry reasons, of almost every medium prime if the linear combination uses only a few denominators.  Precisely, if
\[
H_{n,S}=\sum_{k\in S}c_k\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}\ne0,
\qquad c_k\in\mathbf Z,
\qquad |S|=h,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
 \log|H_{n,S}|
 \ge n-h\log n+o(n).
}
\tag{5}
\]
Hence a nonzero certificate of logarithmic height \(o(n)\) must use at least
\[
\boxed{
 h\ge(1-o(1))\frac n{\log n}
}
\tag{6}
\]
distinct convolution positions.  The same obstruction amplifies by the determinant size for any fixed-width Hankel/Padé minor.  This is a rigorous no-go for **all sparse/fixed-width carriers**, independent of singularity estimates.

The Apéry differential equation does give an inhomogeneous differential equation for \(B(z)(-\log(1-z))\) with **integer** right-hand coefficients, but at a target prime its first-order local reduction collapses exactly to the original Apéry recurrence.  Passing to the next \(p\)-adic jet invokes the mod-\(p^2\) horizontal/reflection laws audited in Q7504; those laws have two free first-jet coordinates and do not force a second target-selective condition.  Thus the ODE does not presently manufacture the missing dense cancellation.

A natural mixed \(2\times2\) determinant is target-selective, but asymptotically it has linear logarithmic height with a very large positive constant:
\[
\log|\Delta_n|
=
\bigl(1+2\log(17+12\sqrt2)\bigr)n+o(n),
\tag{7}
\]
so it is also unusable for an \(o(n)\) bound.

The smallest additional Apéry-specific input I can isolate is therefore a **dense adjoint/Bézout identity**, not another fixed-width recurrence:
\[
\boxed{
0\ne H_n=
\sum_{k=1}^n c_{n,k}\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k},
\qquad
\log|H_n|=o(n),
}
\tag{DAB}
\]
where the weights are constructed explicitly from the distinguished Apéry solution/constant-term formula (not from factoring the row or applying abstract Bézout after knowing its gcd).  By (5), any such identity must be genuinely dense, with support \(\ge(1-o(1))n/\log n\).  Proving (DAB), or a growing-dimension determinant analogue with normalized logarithmic height \(o(n)\), would immediately prove \(W_n=o(n)\) and hence the conjecture by the already-established reduction.

So this denominator route is **not solved**, but it yields a sharper target and a rigorous natural-class obstruction: the missing phenomenon is a dense Apéry-specific cancellation across \(\asymp n/\log n\) denominator directions.

# 2. Exact \(p\)-adic denominator arithmetic

Write
\[
e=e_p(n):=v_p(L_n)=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor.
\]
Since every \(k\le n\) divides \(L_n\),
\[
U_n:=L_nR_n
 =\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}
\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(R_n=A_n/D_n\) in lowest terms, then \(D_n\mid L_n\).  Writing \(L_n=D_nM_n\),
\[
U_n=M_nA_n,
\qquad
\gcd(L_n,U_n)=M_n,
\]
because \(\gcd(A_n,D_n)=1\).  Hence the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
K_n:=\frac{L_n}{\operatorname{den}(R_n)}
=\gcd(L_n,U_n).
}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(K_n)
 =\min\left(e,\ v_p(p^eR_n)\right).
}
\tag{13}
\]

For arbitrary \(p\), grouping \(k=p^am\), \(p\nmid m\), gives the exact local expression
\[
\boxed{
 p^eR_n
 =\sum_{a=0}^{e}p^{e-a}
   \sum_{\substack{1\le m\le n/p^a\\p\nmid m}}
   \frac{b_{n-p^am}}m
 \in\mathbf Z_{(p)}.
}
\tag{14}
\]
This is the full prime-power cancellation problem.  No asymptotics have entered.

## 2.1 Medium primes

Now let \(p>\sqrt n\) and write
\[
n=qp+r,
\qquad 1\le q<p,
\qquad0\le r<p.
\]
Then \(e=1\), and (14) becomes
\[
pR_n
=p\!\!\sum_{\substack{1\le k\le n\\p\nmid k}}
 \frac{b_{n-k}}k
+
\sum_{m=1}^{q}\frac{b_{(q-m)p+r}}m.
\]
Modulo \(p\), Lucas gives
\[
\boxed{
 pR_n\equiv b_rR_q\pmod p.
}
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(q<p\), the denominator of \(R_q\) is prime to \(p\).  Therefore
\[
p\mid K_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_r\,\operatorname{num}(R_q).
\tag{16}
\]
This is the exact target/companion split behind the assumed
\(W_n+O(n^{2/3})\) theorem.

More generally, the same computation proves the useful horizontal identity
\[
\boxed{
 pR_{ap+r}\equiv b_rR_a\pmod p
 \qquad(0\le a,r<p).
}
\tag{17}
\]
Together with (10), every first-order quotient-level pair has the form
\[
\bigl(b_{ap+r},\ pR_{ap+r}\bigr)
\equiv
b_r\,(b_a,R_a)\pmod p.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus changing the neighboring quotient \(a\) changes the leading vector but not the target factor \(b_r\).  A determinant made only from several such first-order quotient rows can acquire powers of \(b_r\), but it does not create an independent zero condition on a target digit.

# 3. A new target-only characteristic-zero carrier: the content polynomial

The scalar evaluation \(U_n=\mathcal P_n(1)\) mixes the desired target primes with the companion primes from \(R_q\).  Keeping the **whole coefficient vector** removes that pollution.

## Proposition 3.1 (exact medium-prime content law)

Let \(\mathcal P_n\) and \(C_n\) be defined by (1)--(2).  If \(p>\sqrt n\), \(n=qp+r\), then
\[
\boxed{
v_p(C_n)=
\begin{cases}
1,&p\mid b_r,\\
0,&p\nmid b_r.
\end{cases}}
\tag{19}
\]

### Proof

Put
\[
A_{n,k}:=\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}.
\]
Because \(p>\sqrt n\), \(v_p(L_n)=1\).

Assume first \(p\mid b_r\).  If \(p\nmid k\), then the factor \(L_n/k\) already contains \(p\), so \(p\mid A_{n,k}\).  If \(p\mid k\), then \(p^2>n\) forces
\[
k=ap,\qquad1\le a\le q.
\]
Hence
\[
b_{n-k}=b_{(q-a)p+r}\equiv b_{q-a}b_r\equiv0\pmod p,
\]
again giving \(p\mid A_{n,k}\).  Thus \(p\mid C_n\).

A target cannot have \(r=0\), because \(b_0=1\).  Hence \(p\nmid n\), and the coefficient at \(k=n\) is
\[
A_{n,n}=\frac{L_n}{n}b_0=\frac{L_n}{n},
\]
which has \(p\)-adic valuation exactly one.  Therefore \(v_p(C_n)=1\).

Conversely, suppose \(p\nmid b_r\).  Take
\[
k=qp=n-r.
\]
Since \(q<p\), \(L_n/(qp)\) is a \(p\)-adic unit, and
\[
A_{n,qp}=\frac{L_n}{qp}b_r
\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Thus \(p\nmid C_n\).  This proves (19).  \(\square\)

## Corollary 3.2

The target logarithmic mass is exactly the medium-prime part of one integer:
\[
\boxed{
 W_n
 =\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}v_p(C_n)\log p
 =\log\operatorname{rad}_{(\sqrt n,n]}(C_n).
}
\tag{20}
\]
Moreover the coefficient \(k=n\) shows
\[
\boxed{C_n\mid \frac{L_n}{n}.}
\tag{21}
\]
Therefore the small-prime part has logarithmic mass at most
\[
\sum_{p\le\sqrt n}\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\log p
=O(\sqrt n\log n)=o(n),
\tag{22}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\log C_n=W_n+o(n).
}
\tag{23}
\]
Also \(C_n\mid U_n\), since it divides every summand in (11), and (21) gives \(C_n\mid L_n\); thus
\[
\boxed{C_n\mid K_n=\gcd(L_n,U_n).}
\tag{24}
\]
At medium primes, \(C_n\) retains exactly the target branch and discards the companion branch.

This is the main positive output of the direct-denominator attack.  The denominator problem has been converted into the content problem
\[
\boxed{C_n=e^{o(n)},}
\tag{25}
\]
where the relevant integer vector is explicit and entirely characteristic zero.

## 3.3 Exact local content condition at every prime

There is also a useful all-prime formula.  For \(e=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\),
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(C_n)
 =\min_{1\le k\le n}
 \left(e-v_p(k)+v_p(b_{n-k})\right).
}
\tag{26}
\]
In particular, \(p\mid C_n\) iff the only coefficients not automatically receiving a factor \(p\) from \(L_n/k\) also vanish modulo \(p\).  Since \(p^{e+1}>n\), these exceptional denominators are precisely \(k=mp^e\), so
\[
\boxed{
 p\mid C_n
 \Longleftrightarrow
 p\mid b_{n-mp^e}
 \quad\text{for every }1\le m\le\left\lfloor\frac n{p^e}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{27}
\]
For \(p>\sqrt n\), Lucas collapses all those simultaneous conditions to the single digit condition \(p\mid b_r\), recovering Proposition 3.1.

# 4. An exact derivative-content identity -- and why it is not enough

Differentiating (1) gives
\[
\mathcal P_n'(X)
=L_n\sum_{k=1}^n b_{n-k}X^{k-1}.
\tag{28}
\]
Because the coefficient list contains \(b_0=1\),
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cont}(\mathcal P_n')=L_n.
}
\tag{29}
\]
Write
\[
\mathcal P_n=C_nQ_n,
\qquad Q_n\in\mathbf Z[X]
\quad\text{primitive}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 C_n\,\operatorname{cont}(Q_n')=L_n.
}
\tag{30}
\]
Thus the desired theorem \(C_n=e^{o(n)}\) is equivalent to saying that the derivative of this **specific primitive Apéry polynomial** carries almost all the lcm content.

Equation (30) looks tantalizing, but there is no generic polynomial theorem of the needed strength.  Primitive polynomials can have derivative content assembled from different index primes.  For example
\[
Q(X)=3X^2+2X^3
\]
is primitive while
\[
Q'(X)=6X+6X^2
\]
has content \(6\), larger than the degree.  By distributing prime powers among support indices, derivative content can contain an lcm-scale product.  The degenerate integer sequence
\[
a_0=1,\qquad a_j=0\ (j\ge1)
\]
makes this failure extreme: its convolution is \(R_n=1/n\), whose denominator is only \(n\), not \(L_n e^{-o(n)}\).

So (30) is an exact structural identity, but any proof that \(\operatorname{cont}(Q_n')=L_ne^{-o(n)}\) must use the distinguished Apéry coefficient vector.

# 5. Coupling all target primes into one integer -- and the sparse-support no-go

The content law has an immediate but important consequence.

## Lemma 5.1 (every target prime divides every raw coefficient)

If \(p>\sqrt n\) and \(p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\), then
\[
\boxed{
 p\mid \frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}
 \qquad\text{for every }1\le k\le n.
}
\tag{31}
\]
This was proved coefficient-by-coefficient in Proposition 3.1.

Therefore every target prime divides every integer linear form
\[
H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n c_{n,k}\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k},
\qquad c_{n,k}\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{32}
\]
If one could construct a nonzero such \(H_n\) with \(\log|H_n|=o(n)\), then immediately
\[
W_n\le\log|H_n|=o(n).
\]
This is exactly the “one small-height characteristic-zero carrier” requested in the question.

The difficulty is that sparse linear forms carry many **non-target** primes automatically.

## Theorem 5.2 (universal medium-primorial obstruction)

Let \(S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\), \(|S|=h\), and
\[
0\ne H_{n,S}
=\sum_{k\in S}c_k\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k},
\qquad c_k\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{33}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\log|H_{n,S}|
\ge
\vartheta(n)-\vartheta(\sqrt n)-h\log n,
}
\tag{34}
\]
where \(\vartheta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p\).  Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\log|H_{n,S}|\ge n-h\log n+o(n).
}
\tag{35}
\]

### Proof

Take a prime \(p\in(\sqrt n,n]\) that divides none of the selected indices \(k\in S\).  Then \(v_p(L_n)=1\) and \(p\nmid k\), so
\[
p\mid \frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}
\]
for every selected term, independently of the values of the Apéry numbers.  Hence \(p\mid H_{n,S}\).

A number \(k\le n\) can be divisible by at most one prime \(p>\sqrt n\): two such primes would have product greater than \(n\).  Thus the \(h\) selected indices can remove at most \(h\) primes from the full medium primorial, and the removed logarithmic mass is at most \(h\log n\).  Since \(H_{n,S}\ne0\), the product of all remaining primes divides \(|H_{n,S}|\), proving (34).  The prime number theorem gives (35).  \(\square\)

### Consequences

1. Every fixed-width certificate, and indeed every certificate with
   \[
   h=o(n/\log n),
   \]
   has
   \[
   \log|H_{n,S}|\ge(1-o(1))n.
   \]
2. A successful scalar certificate of height \(o(n)\) must satisfy
   \[
   \boxed{|S|\ge(1-o(1))n/\log n.}
   \]
3. This obstruction does not use the Apéry recurrence, singularities, reflection, or zero-set bounds.  It is the exact lcm geometry of the raw convolution.  Apéry arithmetic is needed to cancel the universal primorial while retaining the target primes.

This is stronger than saying that fixed-width combinations “do not seem to help”: it gives the exact asymptotic support threshold.

## 5.3 Determinant version

The same proof applies to a matrix whose entries are integer linear combinations of raw coefficients using a total union of \(h\) denominator positions.  If \(M_n\) is an \(m\times m\) such matrix and \(\det M_n\ne0\), then every medium prime avoiding those \(h\) indices divides every entry, so its \(m\)-th power divides the determinant.  Hence
\[
\boxed{
 \log|\det M_n|
 \ge
 m\bigl(n-h\log n+o(n)\bigr).
}
\tag{36}
\]
Thus a fixed-size/fixed-window Hankel or Padé determinant cannot possibly give a normalized target bound \(o(n)\).  A determinant escape must also be **dense**.

# 6. Taylor/Baker acceleration has an exact lcm floor

Let
\[
T_m(z)=\sum_{k=1}^m\frac{z^k}{k}.
\]
The coefficient of the Taylor tail is
\[
H_{n,m}
:=L_n[z^n]B(z)\bigl(-\log(1-z)-T_m(z)\bigr)
=L_n\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\frac{b_{n-k}}k.
\tag{37}
\]
For \(m<n\), every summand is positive and the term \(k=n\) equals \(L_n/n\).  Therefore
\[
\boxed{
 H_{n,m}\ge\frac{L_n}{n},
 \qquad
 \log H_{n,m}\ge n-o(n).
}
\tag{38}
\]
For \(m=n\), the coefficient is identically zero.  Thus plain Taylor truncation has an exact dichotomy: zero, or a carrier already of linear logarithmic height.

Signed Padé combinations can create cancellation, but Theorem 5.2 shows that any such cancellation using only \(o(n/\log n)\) raw denominator positions still retains essentially the entire medium primorial.

There is also a characteristic-zero singularity obstruction to **fixed-degree** Padé cancellation.  Put
\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
\qquad
\rho=\alpha^{-1}=17-12\sqrt2,
\qquad
\ell(z)=-\log(1-z).
\]
The dominant Apéry singularity is at \(z=\rho\).  Since
\[
B(z)\ell(z)=C(z),
\]
a rational-polynomial combination has the exact factorization
\[
Q(z)C(z)-P(z)B(z)
=B(z)\bigl(Q(z)\ell(z)-P(z)\bigr).
\tag{39}
\]
If coprime \(P,Q\in\mathbf Q[z]\) exactly cancelled the leading branch at \(\rho\), one would need
\[
P(\rho)=Q(\rho)\ell(\rho).
\]
If \(Q(\rho)\ne0\), this would make \(\ell(\rho)\) algebraic, impossible because a nonzero logarithm of a non-unit algebraic number is transcendental by Lindemann--Weierstrass.  If \(Q(\rho)=0\), then also \(P(\rho)=0\), so both contain the quadratic minimal polynomial of \(\rho\), contradicting coprimality.  Multiplying both by that singular polynomial only raises the local singular exponent; it does not remove the exponential factor \(\rho^{-n}\).

This does not rule out growing-degree Padé systems.  It shows precisely why a successful Padé construction has to grow with \(n\), which is consistent with the density threshold (6).

# 7. A natural target-selective \(2\times2\) determinant is provably too large

Set
\[
U_n=L_nR_n,
\qquad
\lambda_n=\frac{L_{n+1}}{L_n}\in\mathbf Z,
\]
and consider
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_n
:=b_nU_{n+1}-\lambda_nb_{n+1}U_n
=L_{n+1}\bigl(b_nR_{n+1}-b_{n+1}R_n\bigr)
\in\mathbf Z.
}
\tag{40}
\]
If \(p>\sqrt n\) is a target prime at index \(n\), then Lucas gives \(p\mid b_n\), while (15) gives \(p\mid U_n\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{p\mid\Delta_n.}
\tag{41}
\]
So (40) is a legitimate one-integer, target-selective determinant carrier.

Unfortunately its archimedean height is far too large.  McIntosh's complete asymptotic expansion for the Apéry binomial sum gives
\[
b_n=\kappa\alpha^n n^{-3/2}(1+O(n^{-1})),
\qquad \kappa>0.
\tag{42}
\]
At the dominant square-root singularity, multiplication by the analytic function \(\ell(z)\) gives the standard transfer expansion
\[
\frac{R_n}{b_n}
=
\ell(\rho)
+
\frac{3\rho}{2(1-\rho)}\frac1n
+O(n^{-2}).
\tag{43}
\]
The displayed \(1/n\)-coefficient is nonzero.  Therefore
\[
b_nR_{n+1}-b_{n+1}R_n
=-\kappa_1\alpha^{2n}n^{-5}(1+o(1))
\tag{44}
\]
for a constant \(\kappa_1>0\).  Since \(\log L_{n+1}=n+o(n)\),
\[
\boxed{
\log|\Delta_n|
=\bigl(1+2\log\alpha\bigr)n+o(n)
=\bigl(1+2\log(17+12\sqrt2)\bigr)n+o(n).
}
\tag{45}
\]
Numerically the slope is about \(8.051\), not zero.  Thus the most immediate Hankel/Casoratian mixing of \(b_n\) with the logarithmic convolution is not close to the needed height regime.

The same phenomenon persists for any **fixed-size** determinant whose formal leading singular determinant is not identically zero: powers of \(\alpha^n\) survive, and only polynomial factors of \(n\) are cancelled.  Exact all-orders cancellation would itself be a new Apéry-specific identity, not a consequence of generic Padé theory.

The known pure Apéry Hankel theorem of Zhu--Sun is compatible with this conclusion.  They prove, in the present \(\zeta(3)\) normalization,
\[
24^{-m}\det(b_{i+j})_{0\le i,j\le m}\in\mathbf Z.
\]
This is a strong integrality statement, but it neither makes a single target zero divide the determinant in the required way nor supplies an \(o(n)\)-height mixed determinant for the row (1).

# 8. What the Apéry differential equation gives

Let
\[
\theta=z\frac d{dz},
\]
and
\[
\mathcal L
=
\theta^3
-z(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+z^2(\theta+1)^3.
\tag{46}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal LB=0.
\]
Put
\[
C(z)=B(z)\ell(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}R_nz^n,
\qquad \ell(z)=-\log(1-z),
\]
and
\[
u_1=\theta\ell=\frac z{1-z},
\quad
u_2=\theta^2\ell=\frac z{(1-z)^2},
\quad
u_3=\theta^3\ell=\frac{z(1+z)}{(1-z)^3}.
\]
Writing
\[
A(z)=1-34z+z^2,
\]
a direct product-rule calculation gives
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal LC={}&
3A u_1\,\theta^2B\\
&+3\bigl(Au_2+(-34z+2z^2)u_1\bigr)\theta B\\
&+\bigl(Au_3+(-51z+3z^2)u_2+(-27z+3z^2)u_1\bigr)B.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{47}
\]
The coefficients of \(u_1,u_2,u_3\) are respectively \(1,m,m^2\), and \(B,\theta B,\theta^2B\) have integer coefficients.  Hence the right side of (47) has integer Taylor coefficients.  In particular
\[
E_k:=k^3R_k-P(k)R_{k-1}+(k-1)^3R_{k-2}\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{48}
\]
This is a genuine extra integrality statement for the logarithmic convolution.

But its first-order target reduction is not an independent condition.  Let \(k=qp+r\) with a large target prime and \(r\ge2\).  Applying (17) at the three neighboring remainders gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
pE_k
&\equiv
R_q\bigl(
 r^3b_r-P(r)b_{r-1}+(r-1)^3b_{r-2}
\bigr)\\
&\equiv0\pmod p,
\end{aligned}
\tag{49}
\]
exactly by the Apéry recurrence.  No second digit equation appears.

The endpoints do not hide a missing branch: \(r=0\) cannot be a target because \(b_0=1\); \(r=1\) can only be a target at \(p=5\) because \(b_1=5\), so it is a finite small-prime exception in the all-index asymptotic problem; and \(r=p-1\) is excluded by reflection from \(b_0\).

To ask whether \(p\mid E_k\) one must go one \(p\)-adic order deeper.  The connected Q7504 audit does exactly that for the available ingredients: Gessel's exact mod-\(p^2\) horizontal derivative law, mod-\(p^2\) reflection, neighboring quotients, and the Apéry Casoratian.  In the off-center target branch those laws leave two free first-jet coordinates \((b_r/p,b'_r)\); all forced combinations are universal syzygies except the separately classified leading-digit and resonance branches.  Thus **those laws alone** cannot promote (48) to a second target-selective divisibility condition.

This is a scoped no-go, not a statement that every possible consequence of the Apéry differential equation has been exhausted.  A new higher-order identity that cuts the actual Apéry jet set could still succeed.

# 9. Why generic rational-convolution or G-function theorems cannot solve this

The requested conclusion is extremely non-generic.

Take the integer coefficient sequence
\[
a_0=1,
\qquad a_j=0\quad(j\ge1).
\]
Its generating function is the rational function \(A(z)=1\), yet
\[
[z^n]A(z)(-\log(1-z))=\frac1n.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{den}=n,
\qquad
\log\frac{L_n}{n}=n-o(n),
\]
the exact opposite of the desired conclusion.

Even the nonconstant rational function \(A(z)=1-z\) gives, for \(n\ge2\),
\[
[z^n](1-z)(-\log(1-z))
=\frac1n-\frac1{n-1}
=-\frac1{n(n-1)},
\]
again only polynomial denominator.

Thus no theorem whose hypotheses are merely

* integer Taylor coefficients,
* rational/D-finite/G-function generating function,
* a fixed-order polynomial differential equation, and
* convolution with \(-\log(1-z)\)

can imply a near-\(L_n\) denominator at every index.  The proof must use the distinguished Apéry arithmetic (Lucas/constant-term/initial-vector structure) in an essential way.

# 10. Literature audit

I searched specifically for results that could turn the D-finite/G-function or Hankel structure into a **pointwise lower bound** for the denominator of the coefficient of \(B(z)\log(1-z)\).  I did not find one whose hypotheses match.

## 10.1 G-function denominator theorems

S. Fischler and T. Rivoal, *On the denominators of the Taylor coefficients of G-functions*, Kyushu J. Math. 71 (2017), arXiv:1606.00706, prove very precise **upper/divisibility bounds** for common denominators of G-function Taylor coefficients (unconditionally for geometric operators, and under a standard G-operator conjecture in the general form).  Their theorem has the shape that a common denominator divides an lcm power times an exponential factor.  It does not give a lower bound saying that a particular coefficient must retain almost all of an lcm.

This directionality matters: the rational counterexamples in Section 9 satisfy excellent G-function denominator upper bounds while failing the desired lower bound maximally.

## 10.2 Chudnovsky Padé theory

D. V. Chudnovsky and G. V. Chudnovsky, *Padé approximations and diophantine geometry*, Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 82 (1985), 2212--2216, DOI 10.1073/pnas.82.8.2212, prove a converse-to-Eisenstein type bounded-denominator theorem in a broad Padé/diophantine setting.  It is not a theorem forcing a specified coefficient of a logarithmic product to have a denominator asymptotic to \(L_n\).  The sparse-primorial obstruction above also shows why a fixed-width application cannot have the required shape here.

## 10.3 Apéry Hankel determinants

B.-X. Zhu and Z.-W. Sun, *Hankel-type determinants for some combinatorial sequences*, arXiv:1609.06810, prove the integrality statement for the Apéry Hankel determinant quoted in Section 7.  It is valuable arithmetic information about \((b_n)\), but it does not control the content of the lcm-cleared logarithmic convolution vector.

## 10.4 Prime divisors of Apéry numbers

F. Luca and I. E. Shparlinski, *Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers*, J. London Math. Soc. 78 (2008), 545--562, DOI 10.1112/jlms/jdn031, prove density-one **lower bounds** for \(\omega(b_n)\) and the largest prime factor of \(b_n\).  Their quantifiers and direction are different from the all-\(n\) upper bound on the \(n\)-smooth radical needed here.

## 10.5 Asymptotics

R. J. McIntosh, *An Asymptotic Formula for Binomial Sums*, J. Number Theory 58 (1996), gives complete asymptotic expansions for binomial sums including the Apéry numbers.  This supports the height calculation (42)--(45), but asymptotics alone cannot distinguish target primes from the universal lcm content.

**Literature verdict.**  The existing theories explain upper denominator growth, irrationality/Padé approximation, Hankel integrality, or typical prime factors.  None supplies the pointwise lower denominator theorem requested here.  I therefore do not claim that this route is already covered by a known theorem.

# 11. The smallest additional Apéry-specific identity that would suffice

The content formulation and Theorem 5.2 sharply constrain what a successful new identity can look like.

## Dense Apéry Bézout certificate (DAB)

It is sufficient to construct, for every large \(n\), explicit integers \(c_{n,k}\), obtained from the distinguished Apéry recurrence/constant-term data and **not** by factoring the coefficient row or invoking abstract Bézout after computing its gcd, such that
\[
\boxed{
0\ne H_n
=
\sum_{k=1}^n
c_{n,k}\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k},
\qquad
\log|H_n|=o(n).
}
\tag{50}
\]
A natural proof-grade formulation should additionally require, for example,
\[
\log\max_k|c_{n,k}|=o(n)
\tag{51}
\]
and that the weights be generated by a fixed Apéry adjoint/WZ/constant-term rule.  These side conditions prevent (50) from merely restating “the gcd is small” through an uncontrolled extended-Euclidean certificate.

Every target prime divides every summand of (50), so
\[
\prod_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}p
\mid H_n.
\]
Hence
\[
W_n\le\log|H_n|=o(n),
\]
which closes the conjecture by the assumed denominator/radical reduction.

Theorem 5.2 proves that a DAB certificate cannot be fixed-width:
\[
\boxed{
\#\{k:c_{n,k}\ne0\}
\ge(1-o(1))\frac n{\log n}.
}
\tag{52}
\]
So the missing identity must visibly use a **dense range of denominators**.  This is the smallest structural upgrade over the failed Taylor/Padé/recurrence attempts that is compatible with the lcm arithmetic.

### Determinant alternative

A growing determinant can play the same role.  It would suffice to find an explicit \(m_n\times m_n\) integer matrix \(M_n\), whose entries are Apéry-specific dense linear forms in the coefficients (1), such that
\[
\det M_n\ne0,
\qquad m_n\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac1{m_n}\log|\det M_n|=o(n),
\tag{53}
\]
and such that every target prime divides every entry.  Then the target radical to the power \(m_n\) divides \(\det M_n\), yielding
\[
W_n\le\frac1{m_n}\log|\det M_n|=o(n).
\]
But (36) shows that the union of denominator positions in such a determinant must again be dense enough to neutralize essentially all of the universal medium primorial.  A fixed Hankel window cannot do it.

The most concrete next target is therefore not “find another two-by-two determinant.”  It is:

> **Find a dense Apéry adjoint/constant-term telescoper that turns the \(\asymp n/\log n\) necessary denominator directions into a nonzero boundary term of subexponential height.**

That is genuinely stronger than the already-known denominator equivalence, is falsified by generic integer coefficient sequences, and would be sufficient pointwise for every \(n\).

# 12. Bottom line

The direct denominator attack yields one substantial simplification but not a proof.

1. The whole target set is encoded exactly by the content of the explicit polynomial \(\mathcal P_n\); medium target primes occur in that content with exponent exactly one.
2. The content is a divisor of both \(L_n/n\) and the scalar denominator-cancellation factor \(L_n/\operatorname{den}(R_n)\), and \(\log C_n=W_n+o(n)\).
3. Every target prime divides every lcm-cleared convolution coefficient, so one small nonzero integer linear form would close the theorem.
4. However, any nonzero form or determinant using fewer than about \(n/\log n\) denominator positions is forced to carry an essentially full **universal** medium primorial.  This rigorously kills the entire sparse/fixed-width Padé/Hankel class.
5. The inhomogeneous Apéry ODE is integral but gives no second first-order target equation; the available mod-\(p^2\) laws remain locally universal.
6. The simplest target-selective \(2\times2\) determinant has explicit linear logarithmic height, not \(o(n)\).
7. No existing denominator/Hankel/G-function theorem found in the literature supplies the missing dense Apéry-specific cancellation.

Therefore the denominator route remains open.  Its cleanest next theorem is the dense certificate (DAB), or an equivalent growing determinant with normalized sublinear height.  No claim of the all-index Apéry GCD conjecture is made.
