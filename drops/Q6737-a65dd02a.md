On the regular branch the primitive shifted resultant

\bigl(F_{d_1}(X),F_{d_2}(X+d_1)\bigr)
\right|
\tag{4.3}

is a positive nonzero integer. Therefore

\[
\ell _\nu\mid R_3(d_1,d_2)
\qquad(\nu=1,2).
\tag{4.4}

After removal of the fixed exceptional and upper-primary factors,

\[
\boxed{

\tag{4.5}

### 4.1 Exact height

Let

\[
n_i=\deg F_{d_i}=3(d_i-1)-\varepsilon_{d_i}.
\]

The primitive coefficient heights satisfy

\[
h(F_{d_i})\ll d_i\log(d_i+2).

Translation by `d_1` gives

\[
h(F_{d_2}(X+d_1))
\ll d_2\log(2+d_1+d_2).
\]

The Sylvester determinant bound yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log R_3(d_1,d_2)
&\le
n_2h(F_{d_1})
+n_1h(F_{d_2}(X+d_1))\\
&\quad+O((n_1+n_2)\log(n_1+n_2+2))\\
&\ll d_1d_2\log(2+d_1+d_2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is sharper than the coarser ledger form `\ll d_1d_2\log T`.

Since `P\mid\mathcal R_3` and `P\asymp T^2`,

\[
2L+O(1)
\le
\log\mathcal R_3(d_1,d_2)
\ll d_1d_2\log(2+D).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
d_1d_2\gg_J\frac{L}{\log(2+D)}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is a checked short-product exclusion. If `D=T^{\delta+o(1)}` for any fixed `\delta>0`, its right side is only `O(1)`. It supplies no fixed power.

### 4.2 The strongest key-retaining scalar

The fixed quotient-6 roots also give the already-existing primitive prefix carrier

\qquad
G_\Xi
=
\operatorname{Sat}_{\Sigma_0}

\tag{4.8}

in the reflected orientation, equivalently the authorized direct-orientation evaluation. Its height is

\[
\log|G_\Xi|\ll cL.
\tag{4.9}

The strongest scalar obtained by retaining every common primitive evaluation is therefore bounded by

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_\Xi(d_1,d_2;N_0)
=
\gcd\bigl(
G_\Xi,
F_{d_1}(N_0),
F_{d_2}(N_0+d_1),

\tag{4.10}

with

P\mid\mathcal K_\Xi

and

\boxed{

\ll

 d_2L,

\right\}.

\tag{4.11}

This gcd does not create multiplicity. Each lower prime may occur with valuation exactly one in every factor. Products multiply divisor multiplicity and logarithmic height together; radicals and gcds retain only one copy. There is no valuation amplification.

### 4.3 Why no upper colour divides the resultant

For `q_0`, the natural common-root candidate in (4.3) is `X=N_0\bmod q_0=t_0`. To force

\[
q_0\mid F_{d_1}(t_0)

one needs the additional upper zero at `N_1`. To force the second polynomial at the same root one also needs the upper zero at `N_2`. The actual system gives only

\[

Similarly, `q_1` is known only at `N_1`, and `q_2` only at `N_2`; the appropriate no-wrap/borrow representatives at the other two endpoints are not selected atoms.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&q_i\mid b_J,b_{N_i}\quad(i=0,1,2),\\
&P\mid\mathcal R_3(d_1,d_2)
\end{aligned}
\quad\centernot\Longrightarrow\quad
q_i\mid\mathcal R_3(d_1,d_2).

\tag{4.12}

The resultant can of course be accidentally divisible by a `q_i`, possibly through an unrelated algebraic root. Such accidental divisibility is not a forced atom and cannot be charged in a positive majorant.

The endpoint radical statement

\[
q_0q_1q_2P\mid b_J
\tag{4.13}

is genuine, but

\[
\log b_J=\Theta(T).

A fibre of `T^{1/2+o(1)}` shell colours costs only `T^{1/2+o(1)}L=o(T)` in that radical. Hence (4.13) has ample capacity and gives no fixed-power tail bound. It also records each `q_i` only once and cannot imply `q_i^2\mid b_J`.

## 6. Exact comparison with the landed `E_3(D)` theorem

Define the regular one-characteristic three-endpoint incidence

\[

\#\left\{

\begin{array}{l}

For fixed `(d,e)`, every such `\ell` divides `\mathcal R_3(d,e)`. Hence the number of possible shell primes is

\[
\ll

\ll

\tag{6.2}

For each prime, the number of common starting roots is at most

\[
\min(\deg F_d,\deg F_e)\ll\min(d,e).
\tag{6.3}

\begin{aligned}

&\ll
\boxed{D^5\frac{\log D}{L}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}

This recovers the existing theorem exactly.

### 6.1 Restoring the second lower characteristic

shell prime divisors, hence at most `O(M_{d,e}^2)` ordered lower-prime pairs.

\tag{6.5}

This is also obtained by starting from (6.4): for each one-prime event, the companion lower prime is one of at most `O(de\log D/L)` further shell divisors of the same resultant.
