# SL₂ monodromy ⟹ Q(u) large Galois group ⟹ AFP

## Executive summary

The proposed chain contains a real geometric idea, but the implication

\[
\mathrm{SL}_2\text{ monodromy of the Apéry recurrence}
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}(Q_p)=S_{(p-1)/2}
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{AFP}
\]

is not currently a theorem. The obstruction is that the polynomial \(Q_p(u)\) is not a usual specialization of a fixed polynomial family. It is a finite-field object whose degree grows with \(p\), and its construction involves a quotient by the involution \(r\mapsto p-1-r\).

The correct framework is not ordinary Hilbert irreducibility, but a finite-field monodromy problem for the symmetric-power/quotient sheaf.

---

## (A) The recurrence in the variable \(u=r(p-1-r)\)

The reflection law gives

\[
b_r=b_{p-1-r}.
\]

Therefore \(b_r\) descends from the double cover

\[
r\mapsto u=r(p-1-r).
\]

However, this descent is not a second-order recurrence in \(u\).

Indeed,

\[
r=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1-4u}}{2}
\]

and shifting \(r\mapsto r+1\) changes the square-root branch. The original recurrence lives on the two-sheeted cover with coordinate \(r\), not directly on the quotient curve.

The correct object is a rank-2 local system on the hyperelliptic cover

\[
y^2=1-4u.
\]

The involution \(y\mapsto-y\) acts on this local system. The polynomial \(Q_p(u)\) is the invariant section under this involution.

So Step 1 should be replaced by:

\[
\text{Apéry rank-2 sheaf on }\mathbf A^1_r
\quad\rightarrow\quad
\text{invariant pushforward to the }u\text{-line}.
\]

The resulting object is generally not rank 2.

---

## (B) Does Sp₄ monodromy control factorization of \(Q_p\)?

Only indirectly.

The Picard-Fuchs equation has differential Galois group Sp₄, but this controls the four-dimensional geometric motive behind the generating function.

The polynomial

\[
Q_p(u)=b_r
\]

is a Frobenius trace statistic after reduction modulo \(p\). Its roots are conditions on a trace function:

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_r)=0.
\]

A factorization of \(Q_p\) corresponds to a decomposition of the zero locus of this trace condition. It is not the same as a decomposition of the original Sp₄ representation.

Therefore:

\[
\mathrm{Sp}_4\text{ large}
\not\Rightarrow
Q_p\text{ irreducible}.
\]

A stronger theorem would require the monodromy of the **zero divisor family**.

---

## (C) Would large Galois group imply random roots?

If one had

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(Q_p/F_p)=S_d,
\qquad d=(p-1)/2,
\]

then the Frobenius-cycle heuristic would indeed give the random polynomial statistics.

For a random polynomial, the number of roots is approximately Poisson(1). This matches the observed Apéry data.

However, large Galois group is not sufficient by itself for the desired statement over the defining field.

The reason is that AFP concerns the number of fixed points of Frobenius acting on the roots. The relevant statement is a Chebotarev distribution statement for the Frobenius element in the splitting field.

One needs:

\[
\text{Frobenius classes of }Q_p\text{ equidistribute in }S_d.
\]

A single statement \(\operatorname{Gal}=S_d\) does not provide uniformity as \(p\to\infty\).

---

## (D) Hilbert irreducibility does not directly apply

Hilbert irreducibility starts with a fixed polynomial

\[
f(x,t)\in\mathbf Q(t)[x]
\]

and studies specializations \(t=t_0\).

Here the situation is different:

\[
Q_p(u)\in\mathbf F_p[u]
\]

has degree growing with \(p\), and there is no fixed characteristic-zero family.

The appropriate analogue would be:

1. Construct a universal sheaf whose Frobenius at \(p\) gives \(Q_p\).
2. Compute its geometric monodromy group.
3. Apply a function-field Chebotarev theorem.

This is a Katz-style problem, but for a family of trace-zero divisors rather than the original Apéry sheaf.

---

# What is the real missing theorem?

The desired statement is closer to:

**Trace-zero divisor monodromy theorem.**

Let \(F\) be the Apéry sheaf with large monodromy. The divisor

\[
D_p=\{u:Q_p(u)=0\}
\]

should have geometric monodromy close to the full symmetric group on its geometric points.

Then Chebotarev gives

\[
\#D_p(\mathbf F_p)=O(1).
\]

This is exactly the missing bridge.

---

# Relation to the observed Poisson law

The computation

\[
K_p\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(1/2)
\]

strongly suggests that the zero divisor behaves like a random divisor of a high-degree polynomial.

The random model is:

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(Q_p)=S_d
\]

plus Frobenius equidistribution.

The Apéry evidence supports this picture, but current monodromy information only proves the first half:

\[
\text{large Apéry monodromy}
\]

not

\[
\text{large zero-divisor monodromy}.
\]

---

# Final conclusion

The proposed route is conceptually correct but the implication chain has one missing object:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Apéry sheaf monodromy}
\neq
\text{zero-polynomial monodromy}
}
\]

To prove AFP through this route one needs to construct and analyze the monodromy group of the family of trace-zero divisors (or equivalently of \(Q_p\)). If that group is \(S_{(p-1)/2}\) or sufficiently large, then the observed Poisson(1/2) law and AFP follow from Chebotarev.
