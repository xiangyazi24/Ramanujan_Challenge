# P3.2 coefficient algebra audit

## Setup

Let R=F_p[t]/(t^p-t), with even r,k satisfying kr=1. Under the hypothetical bound deg(r)<=p-3,

kr=1+(t^p-t)h,

where deg(h)<=p-4 and h is odd. The parity follows because kr-1 is even while t^p-t is odd.

## Product coefficient equations

Write k=sum k_i t^i and r=sum r_i t^i over even indices. Since deg(k r)<=2p-6, coefficients above degree p-1 must match those of (t^p-t)h.

The missing middle equation is the coefficient of t^(p-1):

sum_{i+j=p-1} k_i r_j = 0.

More generally, for n>=p-1:

[t^n](kr)=[t^n]((t^p-t)h).

Thus the equations determine the h-coefficients from the top half convolutions while forcing the forbidden parity coefficients to vanish.

## H operator equations

The hypothesis

Hk=(t^p-t)(q_3 t^3+q_1 t)

gives

Hk=q_3 t^(p+3)+q_1 t^(p+1)-q_3 t^4-q_1 t^2.

Therefore all coefficients of Hk below degree p except degrees 2 and 4 vanish, and the only top coefficients are

[t^(p+1)]Hk=q_1,

[t^(p+3)]Hk=q_3.

These equations constrain the highest coefficients of k in the even block.

## Elimination

The coefficients of h are not independent: they are given by the high-degree convolutions of k and r. The remaining system is therefore the convolution constraints

sum_{i+j=n} k_i r_j = 0

for all n in the forced zero range, including n=p-1, together with the Hk top and bottom equations.

The evaluations give

r_0=-1,

sum_i r_i=r(1)=1/3,

because r is even.

## Conclusion

The coefficient algebra reduces to the explicit finite convolution/H system above. The t^(p-1) equation is an essential additional constraint. However, these identities alone do not yield a contradiction with the stated evaluations; the surviving smaller system still requires an additional rank or determinant argument.
