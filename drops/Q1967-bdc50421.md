# Q1967 — Exact low-degree reciprocal gate attack

## Status

A complete all-prime contradiction from the degree bound alone is not obtained. The new algebraic reduction is that the contrary hypothesis forces the reciprocal polynomial into a finite-dimensional intersection whose dimension can be tested by a single Sylvester resultant.

Let

\[
r(t)=1/k(t)\in\mathbf F_p[t],\qquad \deg r\le p-3.
\]

Because \(k r=1\) as functions on \(\mathbf F_p\), define

\[
F(t)=k(t)r(t)-1.
\]

The interpolation polynomial satisfies

\[
F(t)=(t^p-t)q(t).
\]

The degree bound on \(r\) gives a sharp degree bound on \(q\). The extra condition \(Hk=0\) implies that applying the adjoint difference operator to the identity \(kr=1\) yields

\[
k\,H^\vee r + [H,k]r=0.
\]

The commutator term is explicit because \(Hk=0\). Hence the reciprocal polynomial must satisfy a second linear recurrence:

\[
H^\vee r=0
\]

away from the two interpolation defects.

Therefore the contrary assumption is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero polynomial in

\[
V_{p-3}=\{f:\deg f\le p-3,\ f(-1-t)=f(t)\}
\]

simultaneously satisfying the reciprocal recurrence and the interpolation constraints.

## Resultant reduction

Write

\[
r(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_i(t^2)^i.
\]

Substitution into the reciprocal recurrence gives a banded linear system

\[
M_p a=0.
\]

The endpoint constraints coming from

\[
k_0=-1,\quad k_{\pm1}=3,
\]

and the gap-one Apéry values give additional rows

\[
B_p a=0.
\]

Hence a necessary condition for \(S_m=0\) is

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}M_p\\B_p\end{pmatrix}=0.
\]

Equivalently, after eliminating the recurrence variables,

\[
\operatorname{Res}(P_p,Q_p)=0,
\]

where \(P_p\) is the reciprocal recurrence polynomial and \(Q_p\) is the endpoint interpolation polynomial.

This is genuinely different from the Green identity and from the cyclic cofactor formula: it uses the degree truncation of \(r\), not merely the identity \(kr=1\).

## Remaining obstruction

The determinant has not yet been proved nonzero for every prime. Finite computations only establish that it is nonzero for checked primes; they do not constitute the requested all-p proof.

The smallest missing lemma is therefore:

> For every endpoint-hit prime \(p=2m+1\), the reciprocal recurrence matrix restricted to the even degree-\(\le p-3\) space has full rank after imposing the two special interpolation rows.

A proof of this lemma would immediately imply

\[
S_m\ne0.
\]

## Reproducible symbolic computation

The following Sage-style computation constructs the candidate obstruction matrix:

```python
R.<x> = PolynomialRing(GF(p))
A = matrix(GF(p), m, m)

# fill A from coefficient reduction of H^vee(sum a_i x^(2i))
for i in range(m):
    f = x^(2*i)
    g = reciprocal_operator(f)
    for j in range(m):
        A[j,i] = g.coefficient(x^(2*j))

# add endpoint constraints
M = A.stack(endpoint_rows())
print(M.rank())
```

The output is a rank test, not a proof. The algebraic advance is the reduction of the original nonlinear reciprocal condition to this canonical finite determinant.

## Conclusion

The low-degree reciprocal gate does not collapse by a tautological Green/cofactor identity. The new obstruction is a Sylvester/resultant condition coming from the simultaneous requirements:

1. \(r=1/k\) as a function;
2. \(\deg r\le p-3\);
3. evenness of \(r\);
4. the adjoint reciprocal recurrence.

A complete solution now reduces to proving nonvanishing of the resulting canonical determinant for all endpoint hits. No unconditional all-p contradiction is claimed here.
