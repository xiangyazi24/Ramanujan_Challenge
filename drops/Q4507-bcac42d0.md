# 1. Exact gate and source moment ledger

Let

\[
b_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom mj^2\binom{m+j}{j}^2
\]

be the Apéry numbers for \(\zeta(3)\).  For a prime \(p>5\), put

\[
\mathcal Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},
\qquad Z(p)=|\mathcal Z_p|,
\]

and

\[
F_p(a)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal Z_p}e_p(ar),
\qquad e_p(x)=e^{2\pi ix/p}.
\]

The centered periodic coefficient is

\[
K_p(x)=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal Z_p}(x\bmod p)-\frac{Z(p)}p
      =\frac1p\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}F_p(a)e_p(-ax).
\tag{1.1}
\]

For an even integer \(n\) with \(n-1\) prime, Gessel--Lucas gives, for every
\(n/2<p<n\),

\[
b_n=b_{p+(n-p)}\equiv b_1b_{n-p}=5b_{n-p}\pmod p.
\]

Thus

\[
R(n):=\log\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n)}(b_n)
=\sum_{n/2<p<n}(\log p)\mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_p}(n-p)
=M(n)+E(n),
\]

where

\[
M(n)=\sum_{n/2<p<n}\frac{Z(p)}p\log p\ll n^{2/3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
E(n)=\sum_{n/2<p<n}(\log p)K_p(n)
=\sum_{n/2<p<n}\frac{\log p}{p}
  \sum_{a\ne0}F_p(a)e_p(-an).}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The centered AD-BDH target is

\[
\sum_{\substack{N<n\le2N\\n-1\ {m prime}}}|E(n)|^2=o(N^2),
\tag{1.3}
\]

or, with a fixed power,

\[
\sum_{\substack{N<n\le2N\\n-1\ {m prime}}}|E(n)|^2
\ll N^{2-\delta}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Since every summand is nonnegative, (1.3) gives \(E(n)=o(N)\) uniformly on the shifted-prime subsequence and hence \(R(n)=o(n)\).

A finite smooth partition of the region

\[
N<n\le2N,\qquad n/2<p<n
\]

reduces matters, with only \(N^{o(1)}\) losses, to fixed ratio boxes.  It is stronger to sum over all integers \(n\) rather than only \(n-1\) prime.  Fix nonnegative smooth \(v\) supported in one such prime box, a real smooth \(w\) supported in the corresponding \(n/N\) box, and define

\[
\mathcal F(n)=\sum_{p\asymp N}(\log p)v(p/N)K_p(n),
\qquad
\mathcal S=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}w(n/N)|\mathcal F(n)|^2.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The canonical local coefficient energy is

\[
Q_N:=\sum_{p\asymp N}(\log p)^2v(p/N)^2
\left(\frac{Z(p)}p-\frac{Z(p)^2}{p^2}\right).
\tag{1.6}
\]

This is exactly the \(Q_P\) of `oracleA_result.tex`, after the harmless fixed smooth factor.

## 1.1 What is actually proved locally

The source provides the following unconditional inputs.

**Pointwise zero count.**

\[
Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

**Dyadic first zero moment.**  The First Lemma/corank argument gives

\[
\sum_{p\le x}Z(p)\ll\frac{x^{8/5}}{\log x},
\]

and therefore

\[
T_N:=\sum_{p\asymp N}Z(p)\ll\frac{N^{8/5}}{\log N}.
\tag{1.8}
\]

**Exact local Fourier second moment.**  Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}|F_p(a)|^2
=pZ(p)-Z(p)^2
=pV_p,
\qquad
V_p=Z(p)-\frac{Z(p)^2}{p}.
\tag{1.9}
\]

Because of (1.7), uniformly on \(p\asymp N\),

\[
V_p=Z(p)\bigl(1+O(N^{-1/3})\bigr).
\tag{1.10}
\]

Thus the second term in (1.9) does not supply a hidden positive power.  In particular

\[
Q_N\ll\frac{(\log N)^2}{N}T_N
\ll N^{3/5}\log N.
\tag{1.11}
\]

Conversely, up to \(1+O(N^{-1/3})\) and dyadic constants, \(Q_N\) is the logarithmically weighted first zero moment.  Improving (1.11) by a fixed power is essentially the same local task as improving the exponent \(8/5\) in (1.8).

**Full-period HM2.**  For primes \(X<p\le2X\), with \(M=X^2\), the source proves

\[
\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_2\le4M\lambda_X^2,
\qquad
\lambda_X=\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{Z(p)}p.
\tag{1.12}
\]

This is a CRT estimate over a full interval of length \(X^2\).  It is not a short-interval or determinant-band estimate.

**ENERGY--\(3/2\).**  `FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex` proves an unconditional projective orbit energy \(\mathcal F_p\ll p^{3/2}\), but its own Corollary `cor:oe-pointwise-position` explicitly records that this does not improve the pointwise zero exponent \(2/3\): energy alone improves that exponent only below \(p^{4/3}\).  It therefore does not improve (1.11).

These are the proved moment inputs used below.  No natural prime-average second-mass theorem of size \(\sum_{p\asymp N}Z(p)^2\ll N^{1+o(1)}\) is proved in the source; the paper's natural second-mass condition is an additional hypothesis, not an unconditional theorem.

# 2. Exact centered Poisson/Farey decomposition

Insert (1.1) into (1.5).  With

\[
\widehat w(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}w(x)e(-x\xi)\,dx,
\]

one obtains

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S
={}&\sum_{p,q}\frac{(\log p)(\log q)v(p/N)v(q/N)}{pq}
\sum_{a\ne0\bmod p}\sum_{b\ne0\bmod q}
F_p(a)\overline{F_q(b)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\times
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}w(n/N)e\!\left(n\left(\frac bq-\frac ap\right)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Poisson summation gives

\[
\sum_nw(n/N)e(n\theta)
=N\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\widehat w(N(k-\theta)).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Set

\[
\boxed{d=aq-bp+kpq.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then

\[
k-\left(\frac bq-\frac ap\right)=\frac{d}{pq},
\]

and, modulo the two defining primes,

\[
a\equiv d\bar q\pmod p,
\qquad
b\equiv-d\bar p\pmod q.
\tag{2.4}
\]

For fixed \(p,q,d\), these congruences determine \(a,b\) uniquely.  Since \(a,b\ne0\), one must have \((d,pq)=1\).  For \(p\ne q\), \(d=0\) is impossible, because equality \(a/p=b/q\) would force \(p\mid a\).  Also

\[
\overline{F_q(-d\bar p)}=F_q(d\bar p).
\]

After separating the harmless same-prime diagonal, the exact unequal-prime term is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S_{\ne}
={}&N\sum_{d\ne0}
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\asymp N\\(d,pq)=1}}
\frac{(\log p)(\log q)v(p/N)v(q/N)}{pq}\\
&\qquad\times
F_p(d\bar q)F_q(d\bar p)
\widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Because \(\widehat w\) is Schwartz, \(|d|>N^{1+\epsilon}\) is negligible to arbitrary power.  In the requested band, \(|d|<N^{41/100}<\min(p,q)\) for large \(N\), so \((d,pq)=1\) is automatic.  No hidden prime-divisor exception remains there.

## 2.1 Exact reflection phase audit

The palindromic identity in `oracleA_result.tex` writes

\[
F_p(a)=e\!\left(\frac{a(p-1)}{2p}\right)R_p(a),
\qquad R_p(a)\in\mathbb R.
\]

For the original Poisson variables \(a,b,k\), put

\[
\delta=\frac ap-\frac bq=\frac d{pq}-k.
\]

The source formula gives

\[
F_p(a)\overline{F_q(b)}
=(-1)^{a-b}e(-\delta/2)R_p(a)R_q(b).
\]

Since \(p,q\) are odd,

\[
d=aq-bp+kpq\equiv a-b+k\pmod2.
\]

Consequently the exact phase is

\[
\boxed{
F_p(a)\overline{F_q(b)}
=(-1)^d e\!\left(-\frac d{2pq}\right)R_p(a)R_q(b).}
\tag{2.6}
\]

This verifies the parity normalization rather than merely asserting it.  It does **not** provide a free alternating-series saving.  The real amplitudes

\[
R_p(d\bar q)R_q(-d\bar p)
\]

have no proved regularity as functions of \(d\); they may correlate with \((-1)^d\).  Summation by parts in \(d\) would require exactly the new horizontal information that is missing.  Treating the factor \((-1)^d\) as cancellation of arbitrary coefficients would be invalid.

## 2.2 Fixed-\(d\) Parseval and the exact \(2/5\) boundary

For a fixed nonzero \(d\), the map

\[
q\longmapsto d\bar q\pmod p
\]

has \(O(1)\) multiplicity when \(p,q\) range over fixed dyadic boxes: equality of two images gives \(q_1\equiv q_2\pmod p\), and a fixed residue class modulo \(p\asymp N\) has only \(O(1)\) representatives in a fixed-length \(q\)-box.

Thus Parseval and Cauchy--Schwarz give, with the bounded smooth factors suppressed,

\[
\left|
\sum_{p\ne q\asymp N}
\frac{(\log p)(\log q)}{pq}
F_p(d\bar q)F_q(d\bar p)
\right|
\ll Q_N.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Indeed, one Cauchy factor is bounded by

\[
\sum_p\frac{(\log p)^2v(p/N)^2}{p}
\sum_q\frac{|F_p(d\bar q)|^2}{q}
\ll
\sum_p(\log p)^2v(p/N)^2\frac{V_p}{p}=Q_N,
\]

and the other is identical.

Therefore a determinant range \(0<|d|\le D\) contributes

\[
\mathcal S_{0<|d|\le D}\ll NDQ_N.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Using (1.11),

\[
NDQ_N\ll ND N^{3/5}\log N.
\tag{2.9}
\]

At

\[
D=N^{2/5-\delta}
\]

this is

\[
N^{2-\delta}\log N=N^{2-\delta+o(1)},
\]

which is the already verified easy deletion.

At the upper edge of the requested first hard band,

\[
D=N^{2/5+1/100}=N^{41/100},
\]

(2.9) becomes

\[
N^{201/100+o(1)}.
\tag{2.10}
\]

The deficit is therefore exactly \(N^{1/100}\) in this band.  The global \(N^{3/5}\) large-sieve deficit has been converted into a local statement: to cross this first band, one needs a saving larger than \(N^{1/100}\) over summing the fixed-\(d\) Parseval bounds independently.

\text{ uniformly in moving }q.
\tag{3.3}

Proving (3.3) with a saving on average would already be new horizontal arithmetic of the same kind oracleA isolates in SDC.  No such theorem is present in the source.

# 4. Second attack: expand the literal Apéry Fourier coefficients

The failure above leaves a more informative exact decomposition.

Let \(\bar q\) denote the inverse of \(q\bmod p\) and \(\bar p\) the inverse of \(p\bmod q\).  The elementary reciprocity identity is

\[
\frac{\bar q}{p}+\frac{\bar p}{q}
\equiv\frac1{pq}\pmod1,
\tag{4.1}
\]

because \(q\bar q+p\bar p\equiv1\pmod p\) and \(\pmod q\), hence modulo \(pq\).

For \(r\in\mathcal Z_p\), \(s\in\mathcal Z_q\), (4.1) gives

\[
\frac{r\bar q}{p}+\frac{s\bar p}{q}
\equiv
\frac r{pq}+\frac{(s-r)\bar p}{q}\pmod1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
F_p(d\bar q)F_q(d\bar p)
=\sum_{r\in\mathcal Z_p}\sum_{s\in\mathcal Z_q}
 e\!\left(\frac{dr}{pq}\right)
 e_q\!\left(d(s-r)\bar p\right).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Split this into

\[
F_p(d\bar q)F_q(d\bar p)=\mathcal R_{p,q}(d)+\mathcal T_{p,q}(d),
\tag{4.4}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal R_{p,q}(d)
=\sum_{r\in\mathcal Z_p\cap\mathcal Z_q}
 e\!\left(\frac{dr}{pq}\right)
\tag{4.5}
\]

is the literal equal-index part \(s=r\), and \(\mathcal T_{p,q}\) is the sum over \(s\ne r\).

The term (4.5) is not the modular zero frequency \(a=0\); that frequency was removed by centering in (1.1).  It is a new cross-prime zero frequency after the CRT/reciprocity expansion: the two characteristics mark the same integer index.

## 4.1 Integration-ready proved lemma

> **Lemma (common-index subdiagonal in a sublinear determinant band).**
> Fix nonnegative smooth \(v\), a real smooth \(w\) with
> \(\widehat w(0)=\int w>0\), and a nonnegative smooth \(W\) supported in
> \([1,2]\).  Put
> \[
> u_p=\frac{\log p}{p}v(p/N)
> \]
> and
> \[
> \mathcal H_N
> =\sum_r\left[
>   \left(\sum_{p\asymp N}u_p\mathbf1_{r\in\mathcal Z_p}\right)^2
>   -\sum_{p\asymp N}u_p^2\mathbf1_{r\in\mathcal Z_p}
> \right].
> \tag{4.6}
> \]
> Let \(1\ll D=o(N)\), and let \(\mathcal S_D^{\rm eq}\) be the part of
> the exact Poisson sum (2.5) obtained by inserting (4.5) and the dyadic
> factor \(W(|d|/D)\).  Then
> \[
> \boxed{
> \operatorname{Re}\mathcal S_D^{\rm eq}
> =c_{w,W}\,ND\,\mathcal H_N
> \left(1+O\!\left(\frac DN+\frac1D\right)\right),}
> \tag{4.7}
> \]
> where
> \[
> c_{w,W}=2\widehat w(0)\int_1^2W(t)\,dt>0.
> \]
> In particular (4.7) is uniform for
> \(N^{2/5}\le D\le N^{41/100}\).

### Proof

Expanding (4.6) shows

\[
\mathcal H_N
=\sum_{p\ne q\asymp N}u_pu_q
  |\mathcal Z_p\cap\mathcal Z_q|\ge0.
\tag{4.8}
\]

For \(|d|\le2D\), \(r<\min(p,q)\), and \(p,q\asymp N\),

\[
\left|\frac{dr}{pq}\right|\ll\frac DN=o(1).
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Re}e\!\left(\frac{dr}{pq}\right)
=1+O\!\left(\frac{D^2}{N^2}\right).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Also

\[
\widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{pq}\right)
=\widehat w(0)+O(D/N)
\tag{4.10}
\]

uniformly.  Since \(D<N\), the condition \((d,pq)=1\) is automatic.  Finally

\[
\sum_{d\ne0}W(|d|/D)
=2D\int_1^2W(t)\,dt+O(1).
\tag{4.11}
\]

Substituting (4.8)--(4.11) into (2.5) gives (4.7).  Pairing \(d\) and \(-d\) removes the harmless imaginary parts.  This proves the lemma.

## 4.2 Arithmetic interpretation

For distinct primes \(p,q\) and \(r<\min(p,q)\),

\[
r\in\mathcal Z_p\cap\mathcal Z_q
\iff p\mid b_r\text{ and }q\mid b_r
\iff pq\mid b_r.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_N
=\sum_{p\ne q\asymp N}
 \frac{(\log p)(\log q)}{pq}v(p/N)v(q/N)
 \#\{r<\min(p,q):pq\mid b_r\}.}
\tag{4.13}
\]

This is a horizontal large-prime divisor moment of the distinguished integral Apéry sequence.  It is not a trace-function hypothesis and not an actual-Palm quantity.

Define

\[
0\le\mathcal H_N\le\sum_rx_r^2.
\tag{5.1}

\max_rx_r\le\sum_{p\asymp N}\frac{\log p}{p}v(p/N)\ll1.

The canonical first zero moment gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_rx_r
&=\sum_{p\asymp N}\frac{\log p}{p}v(p/N)Z(p)\\
&\ll\frac{\log N}{N}\frac{N^{8/5}}{\log N}
\ll N^{3/5}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_N\ll N^{3/5}.}
\tag{5.4}

\[
\mathcal S_D^{\rm eq}\ll N\cdot N^{41/100}\cdot N^{3/5}

## 5.1 Why the proved second moments do not improve (5.4)

### Local Fourier Parseval

Equation (1.9) controls the squared norm of each **row** \(p\):

\[
\sum_a|F_p(a)|^2=pV_p.
\]

The quantity \(\mathcal H_N\) is the squared mass of the **columns** \(r\) after summing over different defining primes.  Row Frobenius mass does not bound concentration of the all-ones vector in one or several columns.  The subtracted term \(Z(p)^2/p\) is relatively only \(O(N^{-1/3})\), by (1.10).

### Full-period HM2

The HM2 theorem averages every cross-prime CRT locus over \(0\le m<N^2\).  A common small representative \(m=r\) is one positive atom inside that full period.  If one mechanically applies HM2 to the weighted common-index contribution, one obtains only

\[
\mathcal H_N
\ll\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right)^2
   \bigl(N^2\lambda_N^2\bigr)
\ll N^{6/5+o(1)},
\]

which is weaker than (5.4).  The exact reflected anchored construction in `oracleA_result.tex` proves the logical point sharply: full-period CRT control and tiny local zero sets permit complete concentration at one shell point.

### ENERGY--\(3/2\)

The projective orbit energy controls repeated projective states inside one characteristic.  Its source corollary explicitly says it does not improve the \(2/3\) zero exponent.  Common divisibility \(pq\mid b_r\) is instead a two-characteristic horizontal event.  ENERGY--\(3/2\) does not couple the two reductions.

### Reflection and no-consecutive zeros

Reflection replaces a zero \(r\) by \(p-1-r\) in the same characteristic.  It does not prevent the same numerical \(r\) from being a zero modulo two different primes.  No-consecutive zeros is also internal to one \(\mathcal Z_p\); (4.12) contains only one zero location in each characteristic.  The gap-polynomial bounds begin with two zeros in the same characteristic and therefore do not apply to (4.12).

### Archimedean size

If \(pq\mid b_r\), the known exponential size of \(b_r\) permits \(\asymp r/\log N\) prime divisors of size \(N\).  It gives the constant bound (5.2), not a power saving.  A subexponential or linear logarithmic height estimate is far too weak to improve (5.4).
