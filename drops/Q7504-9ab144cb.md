# Verdict

The mod-\(p^2\) Gessel law plus exact reflection does **not** escape the squarefree no-go in the generic off-center branch.  It does something very clean, but the clean thing is a coordinate change on the first \(p\)-adic jet, not a second target equation.

Write
\[
s=p-1-r,
\qquad d_r=b'_r,
\]
and, on the target event \(p\mid b_r\) (hence \(p\mid b_s\)), write
\[
x=\frac{b_r}{p},\qquad y=\frac{b_s}{p}.
\]
With the lower member of the reflection pair used as the orientation, exact reflection gives
\[
\boxed{y\equiv x-d_r\pmod p.}\tag{1}
\]
Gessel's horizontal law then becomes
\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_{qp+r}}p
 \equiv b_q\bigl((q+1)x-qy\bigr)\pmod p.
}\tag{2}
\]
Equivalently, before imposing the target,
\[
\boxed{
 b_{qp+r}\equiv
 b_q\bigl((q+1)b_r-qb_s\bigr)\pmod{p^2}.
}\tag{3}
\]
This is the decisive elimination: the derivative disappears, but (3) is a **universal digit identity**.  It holds whether or not \(p\mid b_r\).  Hence the obvious characteristic-zero expression
\[
 b_{qp+r}-b_q\bigl((q+1)b_r-qb_s\bigr)
\]
is divisible by \(p^2\) for tautological Gessel/reflection reasons and does not select dangerous primes.

The reflected horizontal quotient gives, with the same orientation,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_{qp+s}}p
 \equiv b_q\bigl((q+1)y-qx\bigr)\pmod p.
}\tag{4}
\]
For \(p\nmid b_q(2q+1)\), the pair (2),(4) is invertible as a linear map in \((x,y)\).  It therefore recovers the two first-jet coordinates and imposes **no equation on them**.  The two exceptional factors are harmless and completely classifiable:

* if \(p\mid b_q\), then \(p^2\mid b_{qp+r}\) and \(p^2\mid b_{qp+s}\), but this is the separate leading-digit-zero branch; for fixed \(q\), its total prime log-mass is at most \(\log |b_q|=O_q(1)\);
* if \(p\mid 2q+1\), then because \(0\le q<p\) one has \(p=2q+1\), a single possible prime for fixed \(q\).  The only forced relation is equality of the two lifted quotients; it is the two-digit reflection resonance, not a target-selective zero.

Two neighboring horizontal quotients are even more transparent.  Away from \(p\mid b_qb_{q+1}\), after dividing by the nonzero leading digits they are
\[
 u_q=x+qd_r,
 \qquad
 u_{q+1}=x+(q+1)d_r,
\]
whose coefficient matrix has determinant \(1\).  Thus they *parametrize* \((x,d_r)\); they do not constrain it.  If one leading digit vanishes, the corresponding \(p^2\)-lift is automatic, while the no-two-consecutive-zeros theorem prevents both adjacent leading digits from vanishing (in the digit range).  Three quotient levels yield a second-difference congruence, but that congruence is universal for every digit and is therefore again non-selective.

The Apéry Casoratian does not change this conclusion.  Modulo \(p\), at a zero digit it is a **unit determinant** and supplies the missing projective coordinate; the reflected Casoratian is exactly the recurrence-at-the-zero rewritten under reflection.  At the next \(p\)-adic order its free companion lifts can absorb arbitrary \((x,d_r)\).  Thus adjoining the Casoratian does not cut the two-dimensional first-jet fiber.

I therefore get a rigorous scoped no-go:

> **Local-universality theorem.**  In the algebra generated by the exact mod-\(p^2\) Gessel horizontal law, the exact mod-\(p^2\) reflection law, and the Apéry Casoratian/strong mod-\(p\) reflection for the companion solution, the off-center target fiber \(p\mid b_r\) has two free first-order coordinates \((x,d_r)\), equivalently \((x,y)\).  Every forced congruence among reflected or neighboring horizontal quotients is an identity of that two-dimensional linear image (hence tautological/universal), except for the explicitly separated branches \(p\mid b_j\) and \(p=2j+1\).  Consequently these laws alone cannot force a second target-selective characteristic-zero divisibility condition, of sublinear height or of any height.

This is deliberately scoped.  It does **not** say the actual Apéry jets \((b_r/p,b'_r)\) are arbitrary as \((p,r)\) vary.  A new theorem that cuts that actual subset could still win.  What is ruled out is obtaining such a cut by algebraically recombining the stated laws.

No claim of the full Apéry GCD conjecture is made.

# 2. Exact laws, orientation, and endpoints

For \(p\ge5\) prime and digits \(0\le j,r<p\), Q7492 gives
\[
\boxed{
 b_{jp+r}\equiv b_j(b_r+pj b'_r)\pmod{p^2}.
}\tag{H2}
\]
The derivative is the rational number
\[
 b'_r=2\sum_{k=0}^r
 \binom rk^2\binom{r+k}k^2
 (H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}),
\tag{5}
\]
and its denominator is prime to \(p\) for \(r<p\), so every congruence below is legitimate in \(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\).

For reflection, it is cleanest to orient an off-center pair.  Put
\[
 u=\min(r,p-1-r),\qquad v=p-1-u,
\qquad u<v,
\]
and set
\[
 d=b'_u.
\]
The tracked exact reflection audit proves
\[
\boxed{b_v\equiv b_u-pd\pmod{p^2}.}\tag{R2}
\]
The upper-half form is the same statement after swapping the two endpoints; equivalently its derivative satisfies
\[
 b'_v\equiv-d\pmod p.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus no generality is lost by doing all off-center local algebra with \((u,v)\).  If the original remainder is the upper member, swap the names back at the end.

There are three boundary cases to remove before any hard argument.

## 2.1 Remainder endpoints

At \(r=0\), \(b_0=1\), so \(p\nmid b_r\).

At \(r=p-1\), the stronger endpoint congruence recorded in Q7492 gives
\[
 b_{p-1}\equiv b_0=1\pmod{p^3}
 \qquad(p\ge5),
\]
so again there is no target zero.  Hence every target pair has
\[
1\le u\le v\le p-2.
\]

## 2.2 The centered fixed point

If \(u=v\), then
\[
 u=v=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]
Reflection itself gives
\[
 p b'_u\equiv0\pmod{p^2},
 \qquad	ext{so}\qquad b'_u\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{7}
\]
For fixed \(n,q\), the equations
\[
 n=qp+\frac{p-1}{2}
\]
give
\[
\boxed{p=\frac{2n+1}{2q+1},}\tag{8}
\]
so there is at most one centered prime.  It may be peeled off with \(O(\log n)\) log-mass.  In the centered target branch the horizontal quotient is simply
\[
 \frac{b_{jp+u}}p\equiv b_j\frac{b_u}{p}\pmod p,
\]
so there is still no forced second zero unless one assumes either \(p^2\mid b_u\) or \(p\mid b_j\).

Everything genuinely difficult is therefore off-center.

# 3. The two reflected integer quotients

Assume now \(u<v=p-1-u\) and
\[
p\mid b_u.
\]
Ordinary reflection gives \(p\mid b_v\).  Define the actual integers
\[
 x=\frac{b_u}{p},\qquad y=\frac{b_v}{p}.
\tag{9}

Dividing (R2) by \(p\) gives the exact first-jet coupling
\[
\boxed{y\equiv x-d\pmod p,\qquad d=b'_u.}\tag{10}

Equivalently
\[
\boxed{d\equiv x-y\pmod p.}\tag{11}
\]
This is one relation between three written quantities, but only two of them are independent: the reflection quotient has merely replaced the derivative coordinate.

In particular none of the following follows from a zero digit:
\[
 d\equiv0,
 \qquad x\equiv y,
 \qquad x\equiv0,
 \qquad y\equiv0.
\tag{12}
\]
The tracked \(p=5,r=1\) example below already disproves the first three, and the tracked \(p=17,r=3\) example separates the last two.

# 4. Eliminating the derivative: exact universal identities

Apply (H2) to the lower member \(u\):
\[
 b_{jp+u}
 \equiv b_j(b_u+pj d)\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{13}
\]
From (R2),
\[
 pd\equiv b_u-b_v\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{14}
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_{jp+u}
 &\equiv b_j\bigl(b_u+j(b_u-b_v)\bigr)\\
 &\equiv
 b_j\bigl((j+1)b_u-jb_v\bigr)
 \pmod{p^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
 b_{jp+u}\equiv
 b_j\bigl((j+1)b_u-jb_v\bigr)\pmod{p^2}.
}\tag{15}
\]
Using the upper-half derivative relation (6) gives symmetrically
\[
\boxed{
 b_{jp+v}\equiv
 b_j\bigl((j+1)b_v-jb_u\bigr)\pmod{p^2}.
}\tag{16}
\]
These two formulas are the strongest elementary combination of horizontal differentiation and reflection.  Crucially, **the hypothesis \(p\mid b_u\) was never used in deriving (15)--(16)**.

Thus every characteristic-zero expression obtained by moving the right side to the left is a universal mod-\(p^2\) identity, not a dangerous-prime detector.

Adding and subtracting (15),(16) gives two more useful universal forms:
\[
\boxed{
 b_{jp+u}+b_{jp+v}
 \equiv b_j(b_u+b_v)\pmod{p^2},
}\tag{17}
\]
\[
\boxed{
 b_{jp+u}-b_{jp+v}
 \equiv (2j+1)b_j(b_u-b_v)\pmod{p^2}.
}\tag{18}
\]
Any resultant, determinant, or linear combination that merely rediscovers (15)--(18) has not produced a second target equation.

For the original affine relation \(n=qp+r\), replacing \(p\) by \((n-r)/q\) does not change this verdict.  For example reflection yields, after clearing the denominator of \(b'_r\), a characteristic-zero congruence represented schematically by
\[
 q(b_s-b_r)+(n-r)b'_r\equiv0\pmod{p^2};
\tag{19}
\]
but (19) is simply (R2) with \(qp=n-r\).  It is true for every digit in its range, not specifically for a zero digit.

# 5. Target quotient plane and its rank

Now impose the target and divide (15)--(16) by \(p\).  Let
\[
 \beta_j=b_j\bmod p,
\]
and define
\[
 U_j=\frac{b_{jp+u}}p\bmod p,
 \qquad
 V_j=\frac{b_{jp+v}}p\bmod p.
\tag{20}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 U_j=\beta_j\bigl((j+1)x-jy\bigr),
}\tag{21}
\]
\[
\boxed{
 V_j=\beta_j\bigl((j+1)y-jx\bigr).
}\tag{22}
\]
Equivalently, with \(d=x-y\),
\[
\boxed{U_j=\beta_j(x+jd),\qquad
V_j=\beta_j(x-(j+1)d).}\tag{23}
\]
The reflected pair has matrix
\[
\binom{U_j}{V_j}
=
\beta_j
\begin{pmatrix}
 j+1&-j\\
 -j&j+1
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{x}{y}.
\tag{24}
\]
Its determinant is
\[
\boxed{\det=\beta_j^2(2j+1).}\tag{25}
\]
This gives an exact trichotomy.

## 5.1 Generic branch: \(p\nmid b_j(2j+1)\)

The matrix is invertible.  Therefore \((U_j,V_j)\) is equivalent data to \((x,y)\).  Two quotient equations have not overdetermined a target zero; they have simply changed coordinates on its first \(p\)-adic jet.

Explicitly,
\[
\binom{x}{y}
=\frac{\beta_j^{-1}}{2j+1}
\begin{pmatrix}
 j+1&j\\
 j&j+1
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{U_j}{V_j}.
\tag{26}
\]
There is no forced vanishing coordinate.

## 5.2 Leading-digit-zero branch: \(p\mid b_j\)

Here \(b_j=pz\) for some integer \(z\).  On the target,
\[
 b_u+pj d\equiv0\pmod p,
\]
so (H2) gives
\[
\boxed{p^2\mid b_{jp+u}.}\tag{27}
\]
The reflected lift satisfies the same:
\[
\boxed{p^2\mid b_{jp+v}.}\tag{28}
\]
This is a genuine valuation jump, but its cause is an **additional zero digit \(j\)**.  It is not forced by \(p\mid b_u\) alone.

For the quotient \(j=q\) relevant to fixed \(n,q\), every prime in this branch divides the fixed integer \(b_q\), because the dangerous regime has \(q<p\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{p>q\\p\mid b_q}}\log p
 \le \log|b_q|=O_q(1).
}\tag{29}
\]
So this branch is already a sublinear, indeed fixed-\(q\), exceptional support.  It cannot solve the generic branch because it is exactly the branch one may discard cheaply.

## 5.3 Reflection resonance: \(p\mid 2j+1\)

Since \(0\le j<p\),
\[
 p\mid2j+1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2j+1=p.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus for fixed \(j=q\) there is at most one such prime.  Equation (18) gives
\[
\boxed{b_{qp+u}\equiv b_{qp+v}\pmod{p^2}.}\tag{31}
\]
This is not a zero condition.  In fact when \(p=2q+1\),
\[
 p^2-1-(qp+u)=qp+v,
\]
so (31) is exactly the reflected two-digit resonance.  Again the extra relation is universal, not target-selective.

# 6. Two neighboring quotients \(q,q+1\)

Use one remainder, say \(u\).  On the target define
\[
 U_q=\frac{b_{qp+u}}p,
 \qquad
 U_{q+1}=\frac{b_{(q+1)p+u}}p
 \pmod p.
\]
If
\[
 p\nmid b_qb_{q+1},
\]
normalize
\[
 \widetilde U_q=U_q/b_q,
 \qquad
 \widetilde U_{q+1}=U_{q+1}/b_{q+1}
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.
\]
Then (H2) gives
\[
\boxed{
 \widetilde U_q=x+qd,
 \qquad
 \widetilde U_{q+1}=x+(q+1)d.
}\tag{32}
\]
The determinant is exactly \(1\), and
\[
\boxed{
 d=\widetilde U_{q+1}-\widetilde U_q,
}\tag{33}
\]
\[
\boxed{
 x=(q+1)\widetilde U_q-q\widetilde U_{q+1},
}\tag{34}
\]
\[
\boxed{
 y=(q+2)\widetilde U_q-(q+1)\widetilde U_{q+1}.
}\tag{35}
\]
Thus neighboring quotients are literally affine coordinates for the jet.  There is no remaining equation that could force a second divisibility.

If \(p\mid b_q\), then \(U_q=0\) by (27), but, for \(q\le p-2\), the no-two-consecutive-zeros lemma from `proof.tex` gives
\[
 p\nmid b_{q+1}.
\]
So the second quotient still gives one affine coordinate while the first equation has disappeared; the system is **less** restrictive, not more.  The branch \(p\mid b_{q+1}\) is symmetric.  Its fixed-\(q\) aggregate log-mass is at most
\[
\log|b_qb_{q+1}|=O_q(1).
\tag{36}
\]

If \(q=p-1\), the next quotient is no longer a base-\(p\) digit, but then \(p=q+1\) is itself uniquely determined by fixed \(q\).  This is another singleton edge case, not part of an asymptotic carrier problem.

## 6.1 What three neighboring levels produce

One might hope that going from two to three levels creates overdetermination.  It does, but only in the form of a universal second-difference identity.

For \(q+2<p\), (H2) implies
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&b_{q+1}b_{q+2}b_{qp+u}
 -2b_qb_{q+2}b_{(q+1)p+u}\\
&\qquad
 +b_qb_{q+1}b_{(q+2)p+u}
 \equiv0\pmod{p^2}.
\end{aligned}
}\tag{37}
\]
Indeed all three terms have common factor \(b_qb_{q+1}b_{q+2}\) after inserting H2, and the bracket is the second difference of
\[
 b_u+pj d,
\]
which is identically zero.  No target hypothesis was used.  Thus the first apparent “extra equation” from three levels is again non-selective.

With both reflected remainders, the normalized quotients satisfy
\[
 u_j=x+jd,
 \qquad v_j=x-(j+1)d.
\tag{38}
\]
Hence, for example,
\[
 u_{j+1}+v_{j+1}=u_j+v_j,
\tag{39}
\]
and
\[
 (2j+3)(u_j-v_j)=(2j+1)(u_{j+1}-v_{j+1}).
\tag{40}
\]
These are the two linear equations cutting four displayed quotient coordinates down to the same two-dimensional \((x,d)\)-plane.  Clearing the \(b_j\) denominators gives integer mod-\(p^2\) identities, but they are identities of the horizontal/reflection system, not new target conditions.

# 7. Why the Apéry Casoratian does not cut the jet

The connected `proof.tex` uses the companion solution \((a_n)\) with
\[
(a_0,a_1)=(0,6)
\]
and proves
\[
\boxed{
 a_n b_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.
}\tag{41}
\]
It also proves strong mod-\(p\) reflection for every \(p\)-integral solution of the recurrence.

Assume \(1\le u<v\le p-2\) and \(b_u\equiv0\pmod p\).  Reducing (41) at \(u\) gives
\[
\boxed{
 a_u b_{u-1}\equiv\frac6{u^3}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
}\tag{42}
\]
Thus both factors are units.  The Casoratian is not a second zero equation; it says that the companion direction and the neighboring Apéry value form a basis at the zero.

Now inspect the reflected Casoratian.  Strong reflection gives
\[
 a_v\equiv a_u\pmod p,
 \qquad
 b_{v-1}\equiv b_{u+1}\pmod p.
\tag{43}
\]
Since
\[
 v=p-1-u\equiv-(u+1)\pmod p,
\]
the Casoratian at \(v\) reads
\[
 a_u b_{u+1}\equiv-\frac6{(u+1)^3}\pmod p.
\tag{44}
\]
But the Apéry recurrence at the zero \(b_u=0\) gives exactly
\[
 (u+1)^3b_{u+1}\equiv-u^3b_{u-1}\pmod p.
\tag{45}
\]
Multiplying (42) by \(-u^3/(u+1)^3\) yields (44).  Therefore the reflected Casoratian is **exactly the recurrence-at-the-zero**, not an independent condition.

## 7.1 First-order lift argument

The same conclusion persists one \(p\)-adic order higher at the level of the laws presently available.

Write
\[
 b_u=px\pmod{p^2}.
\]
Lift the unit quantities in (42) as
\[
 a_u=A_0+pA_1,
 \qquad b_{u-1}=B_0+pB_1,
\]
with \(A_0B_0=6u^{-3}\) in \(\mathbb F_p\), and write
\[
 a_{u-1}=C_0+pC_1.
\]
Then (41) modulo \(p^2\) imposes one affine equation
\[
 A_0B_1+A_1B_0-C_0x=\text{known constant in }\mathbb F_p.
\tag{46}
\]
Because \(A_0,B_0\ne0\), this equation is solvable for \(A_1\) or \(B_1\) for **every** \(x\in\mathbb F_p\).  Thus the Casoratian does not restrict the quotient coordinate \(x\).

At the reflected endpoint, the mod-\(p\) companion values are already tied by strong reflection, but their \(p\)-corrections are not fixed by any mod-\(p^2\) companion-reflection law in the current hypothesis set.  The second Casoratian again has a unit coefficient and can be solved for one companion lift for every value of \(d\), equivalently every \(y=x-d\).

So adjoining the companion/Casoratian equations leaves the projection onto the \((x,d)\)-plane surjective.  The Casoratian is an invertibility certificate, not a hidden equation between \(b_r/p\) and \(b'_r\).

A genuinely new mod-\(p^2\) theorem for the **distinguished companion together with the Apéry constant-term solution** could change this conclusion.  Such a theorem is not contained in the stated laws and cannot be manufactured from (41).

# 8. Scoped local-universality/no-go theorem

Here is the precise form of the no-go that the preceding calculations prove.

## Theorem

Let \(p\ge5\) be prime, let \(u<v=p-1-u\) be an off-center reflection pair, and let
\[
 k=\mathbb F_p.
\]
Consider only consequences of:

1. Gessel's exact horizontal law (H2);
2. exact mod-\(p^2\) reflection (R2), together with its reflected orientation;
3. the Apéry recurrence modulo \(p\);
4. the companion Casoratian (41) and strong companion reflection modulo \(p\).

Restrict to the target fiber
\[
 b_u\equiv b_v\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Then its first-order Apéry quotient data have coordinate ring
\[
\boxed{k[x,d]\cong k[x,y],\qquad y=x-d.}\tag{47}
\]
More concretely, for arbitrary formal \(x,d\in k\), all horizontal quotient observables are consistently defined by
\[
 U_j=\beta_j(x+jd),
 \qquad
 V_j=\beta_j(x-(j+1)d),
\tag{48}
\]
and the companion/Casoratian equations admit lifts above every such pair \((x,d)\).

Consequently:

* a nonzero linear form in \((x,d)\), or in \((x,y)\), is not forced to vanish by these laws;
* for \(\beta_j(2j+1)\ne0\), \((U_j,V_j)\) is an invertible coordinate system on the same plane;
* for \(\beta_q\beta_{q+1}\ne0\), \((U_q,U_{q+1})\) is an invertible coordinate system on the same plane;
* every polynomial congruence among these observables that is forced **formally by the stated laws** lies in the defining ideal of this two-dimensional linear image.  Such a relation is therefore an identity of the horizontal/reflection system, not a further condition selecting the target zero.

The only rank drops are the explicit factors \(\beta_j=0\) and \(2j+1=0\), handled in Sections 5.2--5.3.

### Proof

Reflection supplies \(y=x-d\), so \((x,d)\) are free formal coordinates unless another stated law supplies an equation between them.  H2 gives (48), which only defines additional coordinates as linear functions of \((x,d)\).  The rank computations (25) and (32)--(35) show that in the generic branches those additional coordinates recover \((x,d)\); hence they cannot cut its dimension.

The mod-\(p\) recurrence determines neighboring zeroth-order values from the zero, but contains no first-order variable \(x=b_u/p\).  The two mod-\(p\) Casoratians reduce to the same recurrence identity by (42)--(45).  Modulo \(p^2\), equation (46) and its reflected analogue are affine equations with unit companion coefficients, hence have a solution for every \((x,d)\).  Therefore adjoining the companion variables does not eliminate any point of the formal \((x,d)\)-plane.

That proves (47)--(48) and the assertions about forced relations. \(\square\)

## Scope

This is intentionally a theorem about **deducibility from the listed local laws**.  It does not assert that for a fixed prime every pair \((x,d)\in\mathbb F_p^2\) occurs for the actual Apéry sequence.  The actual sequence selects one jet at each digit.  A new Apéry-specific theorem about that selected set is precisely what could escape the no-go.

So the missing kind of statement would have to look like a nontrivial relation or distribution theorem for
\[
\left(\frac{b_r}{p},b'_r\right)
\quad\text{conditioned on}\quad p\mid b_r,
\tag{49}
\]
not an algebraic recombination of H2, reflection, and the Casoratian.

# 9. Consequences for characteristic-zero carriers and height

The local-universality theorem gives a stronger obstruction than a mere height lower bound in the generic branch: **there is no second target-forced congruence from these laws to package into a characteristic-zero carrier at all**.

It is still useful to separate the natural carrier attempts.

## 9.1 Reflection-difference carrier

From R2,
\[
 b_v-b_u\equiv-pd\pmod{p^2}.
\]
On the target,
\[
 \frac{b_v-b_u}{p}\equiv-d\pmod p,
\tag{50}
\]
which is generically nonzero.  Thus neither
\[
 p^2\mid b_v-b_u
\]
nor
\[
 x=y
\]
is forced.

Adding the derivative to cancel (50) gives exactly R2 and therefore a universal identity.  Substituting \(p=(n-r)/q\) does not make it target-selective.

## 9.2 Horizontal carrier

The derivative-eliminated expression
\[
 F_{p,j,u}
 =b_{jp+u}-b_j\bigl((j+1)b_u-jb_v\bigr)
\tag{51}
\]
satisfies
\[
 p^2\mid F_{p,j,u}
\]
for every digit pair, target or not.  Its divisibility is useless for thinning the dangerous-prime set.

## 9.3 Determinantal attempts

For example, on the target the natural determinant
\[
 D_j=b_v b_{jp+u}-b_u b_{jp+v}
\]
is automatically divisible by \(p^2\), merely because both factors in each product contain one target \(p\).  Dividing by \(p^2\) and using (21)--(22) gives
\[
\frac{D_j}{p^2}
\equiv
j\beta_j(x^2-y^2)\pmod p,
\tag{52}
\]
which is not forced to vanish.  Hence no third factor of \(p\) appears generically.

Likewise the 2-by-2 determinant made from two neighboring reflected quotient rows leaves, after removing the automatic \(p^2\), a nonzero quadratic in \((x,d)\) in general.  Casoratian-looking determinants do not create a hidden zero.

## 9.4 Raw-value height

Reflection does not magically make the large Apéry values small.  For a fixed quotient \(q\), a dangerous prime satisfies
\[
 \frac{n}{q+1}<p\le\frac nq,
\]
so for its reflection pair
\[
 \max(u,v)\ge\frac{p-1}{2}
 \ge\frac{n}{2(q+1)}-O(1).
\tag{53}
\]
Positivity of the Apéry sum gives the elementary lower bound
\[
 b_m\ge\binom{2m}{m}^2
 \ge\frac{16^m}{(2m+1)^2}.
\tag{54}
\]
Therefore any natural carrier that literally retains the full larger reflected value (for example a product or lcm containing it) has
\[
\boxed{
 \log |C|\ge
 \frac{\log16}{2(q+1)}n-O_q(\log n),
}\tag{55}
\]
so it has linear, not sublinear, log-height.  This statement is intentionally scoped to carriers that retain that full value; it is **not** a lower bound for the gcd carrier of Q7493, where cancellations/divisor extraction can make the integer much smaller.

The new mod-\(p^2\) laws do not improve that gcd carrier: their generic quotient plane supplies no extra divisor with which to intersect it.

# 10. Exact test: \(p=5,r=1\)

This is the right minimal sanity check because it is a target zero but not a derivative-zero digit.

The exact values are
\[
 b_1=5,
 \qquad b_3=1445,
 \qquad b'_1=12,
 \qquad s=5-1-1=3.
\]
Modulo \(25\), reflection says
\[
 b_3\equiv b_1-5b'_1
 =5-60=-55\equiv20\pmod{25},
\]
and indeed
\[
1445\equiv20\pmod{25}.
\]
The target quotient coordinates are
\[
 x=\frac{b_1}{5}=1,
 \qquad
 y=\frac{b_3}{5}=289\equiv4\pmod5,
 \qquad
 d=b'_1\equiv2\pmod5.
\]
Thus
\[
 y\equiv x-d\equiv1-2\equiv4\pmod5,
\]
but
\[
 d\ne0,
 \qquad x\ne y,
 \qquad x\ne0,
 \qquad y\ne0.
\tag{56}
\]
This single example kills all naive claims that reflection upgrades a mod-\(p\) target zero to a mod-\(p^2\) coincidence.

Now test horizontal levels.

### \(q=1\)

Here
\[
 b_q=b_1=5\equiv0\pmod5.
\]
Therefore the leading-digit-zero branch applies and
\[
 25\mid b_{5+1}=b_6.
\]
Indeed \(b_6=21460825\) is divisible by \(25\).  This is **not** evidence for a target-induced second equation: the extra factor comes from the separate zero \(5\mid b_q\).

### \(q=2\)

Here
\[
 b_2=73\equiv3\pmod5,
\]
and
\[
 x+2d\equiv1+4\equiv0\pmod5.
\]
Thus H2 predicts
\[
25\mid b_{11}.
\]
This is an actual affine first-jet zero, but it is an **accidental value of the free jet coordinate**, not something forced by \(5\mid b_1\).

Notice also that \(p=5=2q+1\).  Hence the reflected pair is at the rank-one resonance and the two lifted quotient coordinates agree.  This is precisely the universal two-digit reflection relation described in Section 5.3.

### \(q=4\)

Here
\[
 b_4=33001\equiv1\pmod5,
\]
and
\[
 x+4d\equiv1+8\equiv4\not\equiv0\pmod5.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{b_{21}}5\not\equiv0\pmod5,
\]
so the same target digit has only one factor of \(5\) at this horizontal level.  Thus even for one fixed target zero, the derivative lift can vanish at one quotient and fail at another.

This is exactly the behavior predicted by the two-dimensional jet picture.

# 12. Complete branch ledger for fixed \(n,q\)

For a dangerous prime \(p>\sqrt n\) with
\[
 n=qp+r,
 \qquad 0\le r<p,
\]
one has \(q<p\).  After reflection, the cases are:

1. **Remainder endpoint \(r=0\) or \(p-1\):** impossible target, since the endpoint Apéry digit is \(1\bmod p\).
2. **Centered \(r=s=(p-1)/2\):** at most one prime, given by (8); \(b'_r\equiv0\), but no second zero follows.
3. **Off-center and \(p\mid b_q\):** automatic \(p^2\)-horizontal lift; all such primes lie in the fixed divisor support of \(b_q\), with aggregate log-mass \(\le\log|b_q|\).
4. **Off-center and \(p=2q+1\):** at most one prime; rank-one reflected quotient relation, universal reflection resonance.
5. **Neighbor branch \(p\mid b_{q+1}\):** likewise fixed support of \(b_{q+1}\); cannot coincide with \(p\mid b_q\) in the interior digit range by no consecutive zeros.
6. **Generic off-center branch:**
   \[
   p\nmid b_qb_{q+1}(2q+1).
   \]
   Here reflected quotient equations and neighboring quotient equations are invertible coordinate systems on \((x,d)\).  There is no second target-selective congruence obtainable from the stated laws.

Thus every rank-drop branch is already singleton or fixed-\(q\) support.  The entire asymptotically dangerous mass is exactly the generic branch where the local no-go is strongest.

# 13. Tactical implication

The mod-\(p^2\) direction is still useful, but the useful target must be changed.

What **will not** work is further linear elimination among
\[
 b_r/p,
 \quad b_{p-1-r}/p,
 \quad b_{qp+r}/p,
 \quad b_{qp+p-1-r}/p,
\]
H2 at \(q,q+1\), and the existing Casoratian.  All of those quantities live on the same two-dimensional first-jet plane, and the resulting extra equations are its universal syzygies.

A viable escape must add information not present in that plane.  Concretely, one needs at least one of:

* an Apéry-specific theorem constraining the **actual zero-conditioned jet set**
  \[
  \{(b_r/p,b'_r)\bmod p:p\mid b_r\};
  \]
* a characteristic-zero identity whose reduction is not in the H2/reflection syzygy ideal;
* a genuinely higher-order invariant (mod \(p^3\) or beyond) that, after conditioning only on \(p\mid b_r\), loses one degree of freedom rather than merely measuring valuation lifting;
* a cross-prime theorem for those jets strong enough to give sublinear aggregate mass.

Q7492's mod-\(p^3\) carry decomposition may be a source of candidate higher-order invariants, but the mere existence of its next coefficient is not enough: the squarefree target does not set the first-order affine coordinate to zero, so a second-order coefficient is not automatically constrained.

So the answer to the proposed escape is **negative at the level of the stated exact laws**, with an explicit local-universality theorem and a complete exceptional-branch accounting.  The derivative law remains promising only if one proves a new theorem about the distinguished Apéry first-jet values themselves; reflection, neighboring quotients, and the current Casoratian do not supply that theorem.
