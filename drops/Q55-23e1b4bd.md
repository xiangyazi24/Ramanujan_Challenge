# Sato–Tate does not control Apéry zero positions or pair-witness transversality

## Executive verdict

```text
No: the proved Sato–Tate theorem and the known symmetric-power
L-functions of the Apéry modular form do not give the proposed prime
exponential-sum estimate, and therefore do not close (HM)_3.
```

There are three independent obstructions.

1. **Wrong observable.** Sato–Tate controls the normalized Fourier coefficient of one fixed weight-four newform,
   
   \[
   f(z)=\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4.
   \]
   
   It does not control the positions of the coefficient zeros
   
   \[
   Z_p=\{j\in\mathbf F_p:b_j\equiv0\pmod p\}.
   \]
   
   In particular, the assertion that \(z_p/p\) is equidistributed by Sato–Tate is not a proved statement; it is not even the statistic appearing in the Sato–Tate theorem.

2. **Wrong CRT phase.** The canonical two-prime witness modulo a third prime is not the reduction of the usual CRT idempotent expression. Selecting the representative in \([0,pq)\), and then imposing the cutoff \(m<X^2\), introduces a coupled least-residue/floor term. Consequently the actual pair-witness Fourier sum does not factor into a \(p\)-sum times a \(q\)-sum.

3. **Wrong quantitative range.** Even if one postulated a bounded-complexity formula expressing a chosen zero position in terms of the Sato–Tate angle, the exact Fourier expansion requires frequencies and moduli of size \(r\asymp X\). Effective Sato–Tate only controls fixed or very slowly growing Chebyshev degree. For the Apéry form, the unconditional effective error is logarithmic, not a fixed power saving. Automorphy of all symmetric powers does not give uniform analytic estimates at degree or twist conductor \(\asymp X\).

There is also a logical no-go. One may keep the actual Apéry modular-form coefficients—and hence every Sato–Tate and symmetric-power statement—unchanged while attaching the repository's anchored-star zero sets. The pair-witness correlation then becomes maximal. Thus no theorem whose new input is only Sato–Tate information about the fixed form can imply the desired transversality.

The genuinely missing theorem remains a **mixed-characteristic, pair-witness dispersion estimate**, or equivalently the finite pair-Palm positive-excess bound in `hm3_result.tex`.

## 1. The exact target and a quantifier correction

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},\qquad M=X^2,
\]

\[
\Omega_p=\{m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad f_p(m)=1_{\Omega_p}(m),
\]

and

\[
K(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}f_p(m),
\qquad
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

For a third prime \(r\in\mathcal P_X\), the ordered pair-witness measure is

\[
\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{r\}\\p\ne q}}
\#\{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q:m\equiv u\pmod r\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\nu_r(u)=
\sum_{\substack{m<M\\m\equiv u\pmod r}}
(K(m)-f_r(m))_2.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Put

\[
W_r=\sum_{u\bmod r}\nu_r(u),
\qquad
H_r=\sum_{u\in Z_r}\nu_r(u),
\]

and define the complete-period discrepancy

\[
\Delta_r^{\rm per}
=H_r-rac{|Z_r|}{r}W_r.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Then the exact identity from Q8866 is

\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal P_X}\Delta_r^{\rm per}
=F_3(X)-
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q},
\tag{1.4}
\]

where

\[
F_3(X)=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_3,
\quad
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|,
\quad
\lambda_{p,q}=\sum_{r\notin\{p,q\}}\frac{|Z_r|}{r}.
\]

The proved second factorial-moment bound gives

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}
\ll M\lambda_X^3.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\left(\sum_r\Delta_r^{\rm per}\right)_+
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3
\tag{1.6}
\]

is a valid sufficient condition for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). The proposed absolute estimate

\[
\left|\sum_r\Delta_r\right|=o(X^2\lambda_X^3)
\]

is stronger than necessary.

The exact source-faithful characterization in `hm3_result.tex` is instead the finite pair-Palm positive excess

\[
\mathscr P_3^{\rm fin}(X)
=
\sum_{p\ne q}
\bigl(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}\bigr)_+,
\tag{1.7}
\]

with

\[
(\mathrm{HM})_3
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathscr P_3^{\rm fin}(X)
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\tag{1.8}
\]

A signed aggregate may exploit cancellation between overloaded and underloaded anchors; (1.7) does not. This distinction does not invalidate the proposed signed route, but it must be kept explicit.

There is also a quantifier issue. The third prime is not fixed while \(X\to\infty\). In the target,

\[
X<r\le2X,
\]

so both the modulus and every required uniformity constant move with the dyadic scale. A theorem for each fixed \(r\), with an uncontrolled constant depending on \(r\), would not apply.

## 2. The proposed affine CRT formula is not the witness modulo \(r\)

Let \(x\in Z_p\), \(y\in Z_q\), and let \(\bar p_q\in[0,q)\) be the inverse of \(p\pmod q\). The canonical representative in \([0,pq)\) is

\[
\boxed{
 c_{p,q}(x,y)
 =x+p[(y-x)\bar p_q]_q,
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

where \([\cdot]_q\) is the least nonnegative residue modulo \(q\). The pair contributes only when

\[
c_{p,q}(x,y)<M.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The usual idempotent expression

\[
E_{p,q}(x,y)
=xq\bar q_p+yp\bar p_q
\tag{2.3}
\]

satisfies only

\[
E_{p,q}(x,y)\equiv c_{p,q}(x,y)\pmod{pq}.
\]

In integers,

\[
 c_{p,q}(x,y)
 =E_{p,q}(x,y)
 -pq\left\lfloor\frac{E_{p,q}(x,y)}{pq}\right\rfloor.
\tag{2.4}
\]

If \(r\notin\{p,q\}\), the subtracted multiple of \(pq\) does not vanish modulo \(r\). Hence

\[
 c_{p,q}(x,y)\not\equiv E_{p,q}(x,y)\pmod r
\]

in general.

A small numerical example makes the point. Take

```text
p=5, q=7, x=1, y=2, r=11.
```

Then \(\bar q_p=\bar p_q=3\), so

\[
E=1\cdot7\cdot3+2\cdot5\cdot3=51,
\qquad
c=51-35=16.
\]

But

\[
E\equiv7\pmod{11},
\qquad
c\equiv5\pmod{11}.
\]

Thus the phase cannot be written as an affine combination with coefficients depending separately on \(p\) and \(q\), followed by reduction modulo \(r\).

The exact Fourier transform of the witness measure is

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\nu_r(h)
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q\ne r\\p\ne q}}
\sum_{x\in Z_p}\sum_{y\in Z_q}
1_{\{c_{p,q}(x,y)<M\}}
 e_r\bigl(hc_{p,q}(x,y)\bigr).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Fourier inversion gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_r^{\rm per}
=
\frac1r\sum_{h=1}^{r-1}
\widehat{1_{Z_r}}(-h)\widehat\nu_r(h).
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Both the least-residue map in (2.1) and the cutoff in (2.2) couple \(p,q,x,y\). The one-dimensional sum

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}e_r(a z_p c_p)
\tag{2.7}
\]

is therefore only a toy surrogate. It is not an exact component of (2.5) unless one first proves a new decomposition that removes the \(q\)-dependence, the floor term, the multiplicity of zeros, and the cutoff. No such decomposition is currently available.

## 3. What Sato–Tate for the Apéry form actually controls

Let

\[
f(z)=\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4
=
\sum_{n\ge1}A_f(n)q^n.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is the rational non-CM weight-four newform of level \(8\), LMFDB label `8.4.a.a`. For every odd prime \(p\), write

\[
A_f(p)=2p^{3/2}\cos\theta_p,
\qquad 0\le\theta_p\le\pi.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The Sato–Tate theorem says that the angles \(\theta_p\) are equidistributed with respect to

\[
d\mu_{\rm ST}(\theta)
=\frac2\pi\sin^2\theta\,d\theta.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Equivalently, it controls prime sums of **fixed class functions of the two-dimensional Frobenius conjugacy class**. The irreducible class functions are the Chebyshev polynomials

\[
U_m(\cos\theta_p)
=
\frac{\sin((m+1)\theta_p)}{\sin\theta_p},
\tag{3.4}
\]

which are the prime coefficients of the normalized symmetric-power representations.

Historically, automorphy of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 f\), \(\operatorname{Sym}^3 f\), and \(\operatorname{Sym}^4 f\) supplied only low-degree class functions. Newton and Thorne now prove automorphy of

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^m f
\quad\text{for every }m\ge1
\tag{3.5}
\]

for every non-CM holomorphic eigenform. This establishes all fixed symmetric powers. It does **not** assert estimates uniform when \(m\) grows like \(p\), nor does it turn an unrelated prime statistic into a Frobenius class function.

The known Apéry congruence concerns one distinguished index:

\[
 b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv A_f(p)\pmod p.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus the central membership event

\[
\frac{p-1}{2}\in Z_p
\]

is equivalent to the same-characteristic divisibility event

\[
p\mid A_f(p).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Nothing in (3.6) identifies a general zero \(z\in Z_p\) with \(\theta_p\). In particular:

- \(Z_p\) can be empty or can contain several reflection pairs;
- there is no canonical single number called \(z_p\) in general;
- the full set \(Z_p\) is determined by the row
  \((b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1})\bmod p\), not by the one scalar \(A_f(p)\);
- no theorem expresses \(z/p\), for \(z\in Z_p\), as a fixed algebraic or measurable class function of \(\theta_p\).

The repository gives the exact dual description

\[
 b_j=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-j},
\qquad
A_p(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}b_kt^k.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Therefore \(j\in Z_p\) means that a defining-characteristic Mellin coefficient of the full \(p\)-dependent trace table vanishes. This is not the evaluation condition that one K3 fiber has a prescribed Frobenius trace, and it is not a class function of the fixed newform's Frobenius element.

This is the first decisive answer to the question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sato–Tate does not prove equidistribution of }z_p/p.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

That equidistribution would itself be a new horizontal theorem about Apéry Mellin zeros.

## 4. Sato–Tate does not even resolve the central zero event

The center illustrates the resolution problem without any ambiguity about which zero is chosen. From (3.2) and (3.7),

\[
p\mid A_f(p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2\sqrt p\cos\theta_p\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is a shrinking, same-characteristic lattice condition. Sato–Tate gives the mass of fixed intervals in \([0,\pi]\); it does not count exact membership in a \(p\)-dependent lattice.

Thorner's unconditional effective theorem gives, for a fixed interval \(I\),

\[
\left|
\#\{p\le x:\theta_p\in I\}
-\mu_{\rm ST}(I)\pi(x)
\right|
\ll
\pi(x)\frac{\log(kq\log x)}{\sqrt{\log x}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the fixed Apéry form, \(k=4\) and \(q=8\). The theorem permits intervals whose Sato–Tate mass is roughly no smaller than

\[
\frac{(\log\log x)F(x)}{\sqrt{\log x}},
\qquad F(x)\to\infty.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The lattice in (4.1) has spacing of order \(p^{-1/2}\), and exact divisibility requires still finer localization to individual integral values. Hence even the known central congruence is far below the resolution of effective Sato–Tate.

This is the same-characteristic wall already visible in the repository's Gross–Koblitz analysis: the condition \(p\mid b_j\) is cancellation after reduction at the same prime \(p\), whereas Sato–Tate is an archimedean statement about the normalized complex size of a Frobenius trace.

## 5. What symmetric-power L-functions can sum

The logarithmic derivative of \(L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^m f)\) controls prime sums with weight

\[
U_m(\cos\theta_p).
\tag{5.1}
\]

More generally, a Dirichlet twist controls

\[
\chi(p)U_m(\cos\theta_p).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus the analytic machinery applies to weights in the representation ring of the fixed compatible system, possibly multiplied by a character of the prime \(p\).

To use it for (2.7), one would first need a uniform identity or approximation

\[
e_r(a z_p c_p)
=
\sum_{m\le M}\gamma_{m,r,a}
U_m(\cos\theta_p)
+	ext{small error}
\tag{5.3}
\]

with controlled coefficients and controlled \(M\). No such relation is known, because \(z_p\) is not known to be a function of \(\theta_p\) at all.

Even under the fictional assumption that such a function existed, the quantitative range is wrong. Thorner's effective argument controls the weighted prime number theorem uniformly only for

\[
 m
\ll
\frac{\sqrt{\log x}}
     {\sqrt{\log(kq\log x)}}
\asymp
\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}}
\tag{5.4}
\]

for the fixed Apéry form. The exact pair-witness formula (2.6) contains every frequency

\[
1\le h<r,
\qquad r\asymp X.
\tag{5.5}
\]

A residue-scale test with \(r\) possible phases cannot in general be represented by Chebyshev degree \(O(\sqrt{\log X/\log\log X})\). Its complexity must grow with the residue resolution unless an entirely new algebraic collapse is proved.

There is a second quantitative mismatch. The unconditional error in (4.2) saves only a power of \(\log X\). The proposed estimate

\[
\left|
\sum_{X<p\le2X}e_r(a z_pc_p)
\right|
\ll
\left(\frac X{\log X}\right)^{1-\delta}
\tag{5.6}
\]

asks for a fixed power saving in the number of primes. Current unconditional effective Sato–Tate does not give such a saving even for a general fixed interval statistic, much less for a test whose complexity and modulus grow like \(X\).

Under GRH for all fixed symmetric-power L-functions, stronger effective Sato–Tate errors are known. This still would not prove (5.6), because the missing identity (5.3), the growing degree, and the growing modulus remain. Automorphy or GRH for each fixed \(m\) is not uniform control for \(m\asymp X\).

## 6. Passing to residue classes modulo \(r\) is a different problem

There is a second possible interpretation: perhaps \(z_p\bmod r\) could be recovered from a mod-\(r\) Galois representation and then treated by Chebotarev.

The compatible system of the fixed modular form gives

\[
\det\bigl(1-T\rho_{f,\ell}(\operatorname{Frob}_p)\bigr)
=1-A_f(p)T+p^3T^2
\qquad(p\nmid8\ell).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Reducing at \(\ell=r\) therefore controls

\[
A_f(p)\bmod r
\quad\text{and}\quad
p^3\bmod r.
\tag{6.2}
\]

It does not control the zero positions of the characteristic-\(p\) Apéry row. No representation is known for which

\[
z_p\bmod r
\]

is a class function of \(\operatorname{Frob}_p\). Constructing such a system would be a new **cross-characteristic compatible-system theorem**, not a consequence of Sato–Tate.

Even if such a representation were postulated, the modulus is at the conductor scale:

\[
r\asymp X,
\qquad p\asymp X.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Effective Chebotarev for an extension whose degree and discriminant grow with \(r\) is not uniform at primes of size only \(X\asymp r\). A theorem for fixed \(r\), followed by \(X\to\infty\), has the wrong order of quantifiers.

The additive character can formally be expanded in multiplicative characters. For \(u\ne0\pmod r\),

\[
e_r(u)
=
\frac1{r-1}
\sum_{\chi\bmod r}
\tau(\overline\chi)\chi(u).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Applied to (5.6), this produces prime sums weighted by

\[
\chi(z_pc_p),
\tag{6.5}
\]

not by \(\chi(p)\). Standard twists of the Apéry L-function control the latter. They give no Euler product for the former.

Moreover, (6.4) introduces \(r-1\) characters of conductor \(r\), with Gauss sums of size \(\sqrt r\). Even in a hypothetical reduction to \(\chi(p)U_m(\cos\theta_p)\), one would need estimates simultaneously uniform in

\[
m\lesssim r,
\qquad
\operatorname{cond}(\chi)=r,
\qquad
x\asymp r.
\tag{6.6}
\]

The current symmetric-power and twisted Rankin–Selberg zero-free regions do not provide this conductor-level prime number theorem.

Thus “Sato–Tate in arithmetic progressions” would not repair the route. The required statistic is not an arithmetic progression condition on \(p\), and the modulus is far outside the fixed-modulus regime.

## 7. The fixed newform contains far less information than the zero set

The distinction can be summarized as follows.

### Data visible to the Apéry newform

For each prime \(p\), the fixed two-dimensional compatible system records the conjugacy class with characteristic polynomial

\[
X^2-A_f(p)X+p^3.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Every symmetric-power coefficient is a polynomial in this class.

### Data defining \(Z_p\)

The zero set requires the entire defining-characteristic Mellin spectrum

\[
\left\{
\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-j}
:0\le j<p
\right\}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

This has \(p\)-dependent length and arises from all fibers of the K3 trace function. Compressing (7.2) to (7.1) loses precisely the horizontal information that selects the zero indices.

The Hasse-invariant interpretation does not change this. Evaluation roots of a Hasse section lie in the geometric fiber-parameter line. The set \(Z_p\) lies in the dual Mellin-exponent line. The repository's example

\[
A_7(t)=(t-1)^2(t^2+1)^2\pmod7,
\qquad Z_7=\varnothing,
\tag{7.3}
\]

shows concretely that evaluation roots and coefficient zeros can be completely different.

Therefore the phrase “\(z_p\) is a specific function of the Frobenius class” needs a qualification:

- it may be regarded as a function of the **entire p-dependent Frobenius trace table** of the K3 family;
- it is not known to be a bounded-complexity class function of the Frobenius element of the fixed Apéry newform.

The latter is what symmetric-power L-functions require.

## 8. A rigorous logical no-go from the anchored star

Suppose one is given all of the following information:

- every coefficient \(A_f(p)\);
- every Sato–Tate angle \(\theta_p\);
- automorphy and analytic properties of every symmetric power of \(f\);
- quantitative Sato–Tate for every fixed interval;
- the local facts that each zero set is small and reflection-invariant.

These data still do not force pair-witness transversality.

Indeed, retain the genuine sequence \(A_f(p)\) and hence retain every statement above. Independently attach the anchored-star sets from `prop:hm3-anchored-star`:

\[
Z_p^*=\{m_0\bmod p,\ p-1-(m_0\bmod p)\}
\tag{8.1}
\]

for almost all active shell primes. Then every active prime contains the same row \(m_0\), so

\[
K^*(m_0)\asymp\frac X{\log X}
\]

and

\[
F_3^*(X)\gg\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3},
\tag{8.2}
\]

larger than the independent scale by a factor of order \(X\).

All Sato–Tate data of \(f\) are unchanged. This proves the logical statement

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sato–Tate information about }f
\text{ alone cannot imply pair-witness transversality.}
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

For the actual Apéry sets one must add a theorem linking their positions to the Frobenius data. That link is exactly what is currently missing.

## 9. Why joint Sato–Tate does not handle the pair sum

One might hope to use independent Sato–Tate behavior for the two anchor primes \(p\) and \(q\). This would be useful if the witness phase factorized as

\[
F_p(x)G_q(y).
\]

It does not. Equation (2.1) contains

\[
[(y-x)\bar p_q]_q,
\]

and equation (2.2) clips the resulting integer at \(M\). The variables \(p,q,x,y\) remain coupled.

Joint Sato–Tate theorems concern two fixed, twist-inequivalent modular forms evaluated at the **same prime**. They control weights of the form

\[
U_m(\cos\theta_p^{(1)})
U_n(\cos\theta_p^{(2)}).
\tag{9.1}
\]

The pair-witness problem instead has two varying primes and reciprocal CRT phases. It is a bilinear dispersion problem, closer in analytic shape to sums involving inverses and canonical fractional parts than to joint Sato–Tate.

Even perfect marginal equidistribution of hypothetical variables \(z_p/p\) would not automatically control

\[
[(z_q-z_p)\bar p_q]_q
\]

or the cutoff \(c_{p,q}(z_p,z_q)<M\). Marginal distribution does not imply the needed correlation with modular inverses and the Archimedean landing condition.

## 10. The exact new theorem that would make this route work

A usable Sato–Tate-based proof would first need a theorem far stronger than Sato–Tate itself.

### Required cross-characteristic coding

For every \(r\asymp X\), construct a uniformly controlled arithmetic object whose Frobenius at \(p\ne r\) determines the multiset

\[
Z_p\bmod r
\]

or at least its additive transforms after the CRT weights are inserted.

### Required hybrid cancellation

Prove, uniformly for \(r\asymp X\) and \(h\ne0\pmod r\), cancellation in the actual sum

\[
\sum_{\substack{p,q\ne r\\p\ne q}}
\sum_{x\in Z_p}\sum_{y\in Z_q}
1_{\{c_{p,q}(x,y)<X^2\}}
 e_r\bigl(hc_{p,q}(x,y)\bigr).
\tag{10.1}
\]

This estimate must retain the least-residue operation, pair multiplicities, and the cutoff.

### Required transversality with the third zero set

Finally prove the one-sided aggregate

\[
\left(
\sum_{r\in\mathcal P_X}
\frac1r\sum_{h\ne0}
\widehat{1_{Z_r}}(-h)\widehat\nu_r(h)
\right)_+
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3,
\tag{10.2}
\]

or directly prove the stronger finite pair-Palm estimate (1.7)–(1.8).

Possible frameworks for such a result would be:

- a mixed-characteristic large sieve for the Apéry Mellin-zero positions;
- a product-monodromy theorem for a universal Mellin family, together with uniform control of the incomplete CRT box;
- a Linnik-style bilinear dispersion estimate for the reciprocal/floor phases in (2.1);
- a direct arithmetic incompatibility theorem excluding coherent small-integer alignment across many characteristics.

None of these is supplied by the analytic properties of the fixed Apéry L-function.

## 11. Direct answer to the proposed prime sum

Consider

\[
S_{r,a}(X)=
\sum_{X<p\le2X}e_r(a z_pc_p).
\tag{11.1}
\]

The known symmetric-power L-functions can bound a prime sum only after its weight is represented by a controlled combination of

\[
\chi(p)U_m(\cos\theta_p).
\tag{11.2}
\]

For (11.1), all of the following are missing:

1. a canonical choice of \(z_p\) when \(|Z_p|\ne2\);
2. a formula making \(z_p\) a class function of \(\rho_f(\operatorname{Frob}_p)\);
3. a bounded-degree Chebyshev expansion;
4. a reduction of \(\chi(z_pc_p)\) to a Dirichlet twist \(\chi(p)\);
5. uniformity in the growing modulus \(r\asymp X\);
6. the second anchor prime, canonical quotient, and cutoff appearing in the real witness sum.

Consequently there is currently no known Euler product or automorphic L-function whose prime coefficients are

\[
e_r(a z_pc_p).
\]

The desired estimate

\[
|S_{r,a}(X)|\ll (X/\log X)^{1-\delta}
\]

is therefore not a consequence of Sato–Tate, of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\)–\(\operatorname{Sym}^4\) automorphy, or even of the now-proved automorphy of every fixed symmetric power.

---

## Final conclusion

The new idea correctly identifies that actual Apéry zero locations should prevent the anchored star. The proposed explanation through the Sato–Tate law of the fixed Apéry form, however, uses the wrong data.

Sato–Tate controls

\[
\frac{A_f(p)}{2p^{3/2}}=\cos\theta_p.
\]

Pair-witness transversality depends on

\[
\{j:b_j\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]

the complete defining-characteristic Mellin-zero set, and on its nonlinear interaction with a second characteristic through canonical CRT representatives. No proved theorem maps the second object to the first with bounded complexity.

Accordingly:

```text
Sato–Tate is useful heuristic evidence that the underlying motive is
non-CM and has large monodromy. It is not an analytic estimate for the
Apéry zero positions, and it cannot presently bound the pair-witness
spectrum.

The remaining theorem is a mixed-characteristic CRT/Mellin dispersion
statement, not another symmetric-power theorem for the fixed newform.
```
