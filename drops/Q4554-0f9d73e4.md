 \tag{2.2}

### Proof

Both statements are the primewise uniqueness of integer factorization.

 \operatorname{rad}\bigl(q\prod_{\ell\in S_x(I)}\ell\bigr)

 \log\operatorname{rad}

The \(q\)-term occurs once per state, not once per foreign partner.  If

# 3. Fixed-index determinant attack: exact rank-one collapse

One might hope that the three common primes \(q,p,\ell\) produce a
nonzero determinant from the two fixed values \(b_n,b_B\).  They do not.

Let

\[
 A=b_n/q^{e_q},
 \qquad C=b_B/q^{e_q},
\]

where \(e_q\) is the complete common \(q\)-primary exponent.  For every
lower support prime \(r\), both \(A/r\) and \(C/r\) are integers.  Form
the quotient row

\[
 v_r=\left(\frac Ar,\frac Cr\right).
\]

## Lemma 3.1 — fixed-value quotient rank

For any two lower support primes \(r,s\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 A/r&C/r\\
 A/s&C/s
 \end{pmatrix}=0.}
 \tag{3.1}
\]

The same is true if the selected upper row
\((b_n/q,b_B/q)\) is inserted before complete \(q\)-stripping: all rows
lie on the same rational line.

### Proof

Each row is a scalar multiple of \((A,C)\):

\[
 v_r=r^{-1}(A,C).
\]

Hence the matrix has rank one over \(\mathbb Q\), and every minor of
order at least two vanishes.  \(\square\)

The Smith form says the same thing integrally.  The \(1\times2\) matrix
\([A\ C]\) has one nonzero Smith invariant,

\[
 \gcd(A,C)=G_x^{(q)}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus a fixed-index Smith, Fitting, Bézout, or subresultant construction
has only two outcomes:

```text
zero, because it is a higher minor of a rank-one quotient module; or
a scalar in the ideal (A,C), whose primitive positive generator is G_x^(q).
```

The second outcome is exactly the support-blind fixed-gcd theorem that
(2.7) asks us to prove.  It is not an independent determinant
mechanism.

This is the first exact obstruction to the naive “three primes give a
three-row determinant” proposal.  To obtain a nonzero scalar one must
retain prime-dependent residual or divided-jet data.  The next section
does so without dropping either lower zero row.

# 4. A nonzero two-row exterior minor and its exact failure

The following lemma is new, unconditional, and entirely source-native.
It uses the literal Apéry recurrence, not a random model or finite scan.

## Lemma 4.1 — elementary Apéry ratio bounds

For every \(m\ge0\),

\[
 \boxed{5b_m\le b_{m+1}<34b_m.}
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The left inequality is an equality only at \(m=0\).

### Proof

Positivity follows from the binomial formula for \(b_m\).  The upper
bound is immediate from the recurrence:

\[
 (m+1)^3b_{m+1}
 =P(m)b_m-m^3b_{m-1}
 <P(m)b_m,
\]

and

\[
 34(m+1)^3-P(m)=51m^2+75m+29>0.
\]

For the lower bound, \(b_1=5b_0\).  Assume
\(b_m\ge5b_{m-1}\) with \(m\ge1\).  Then

\[
 (m+1)^3b_{m+1}
 \ge\left(P(m)-\frac{m^3}{5}\right)b_m.
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
 P(m)-\frac{m^3}{5}-5(m+1)^3
 =\frac{144}{5}m^3+36m^2+12m>0.
\]

Thus \(b_{m+1}>5b_m\), completing the induction.  \(\square\)

In particular,

\[
 b_m<34^m
 \qquad(m\ge1).
 \tag{4.2}
\]

## Proposition 4.2 — selected minus exterior minor

Let \(p<\ell\) be two distinct support primes of the same selected minus
state.  Put

\[
 \Delta=\ell-p,
 \qquad d=q-p,
\]

and define the four literal residual indices

\[
 a=d-t-1,
 \qquad r=6d+t,
\]

\[
 a'=a-\Delta,
 \qquad r'=r-6\Delta.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
 p\mid b_a,b_r,
 \qquad
 \ell\mid b_{a'},b_{r'}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Define

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr E_x(p,\ell)
 =b_r b_{a'}-b_a b_{r'}.}
 \tag{4.5}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 p\ell\mid\mathscr E_x(p,\ell),}
 \tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 0<\mathscr E_x(p,\ell)
 <34^{\,7d-\Delta-1}.}
 \tag{4.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 p\ell<34^{\,7(q-p)-(\ell-p)-1}.}
 \tag{4.8}
\]

### Proof

Each term in (4.5) is divisible by \(p\): the first through \(b_r\),
the second through \(b_a\).  Each term is divisible by \(\ell\): the
first through \(b_{a'}\), the second through \(b_{r'}\).  Since
\(p\ne\ell\), (4.6) follows.

For the sign, Lemma 4.1 gives

\[
 \frac{b_r}{b_{r'}}\ge5^{6\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac{b_a}{b_{a'}}<34^\Delta.
\]

Since \(5^6>34\),

\[
 \frac{b_r}{b_{r'}}>\frac{b_a}{b_{a'}},
\]

so \(b_rb_{a'}>b_ab_{r'}\), proving positivity.  Moreover

\[
 0<\mathscr E_x(p,\ell)<b_rb_{a'}.
\]

By (4.2),

\[
 b_rb_{a'}<34^{r+a'}.
\]

Finally,

\[
 r+a'
 =(6d+t)+(d-\Delta-t-1)
 =7d-\Delta-1.
\]

This proves (4.7), and the positive integer divisibility (4.6) gives
(4.8).  \(\square\)

No squarefree or simple-zero hypothesis appears.  The proposition uses
both literal lower rows.  Final actual masks may be retained on one or
both endpoints; deleting masks only enlarges the support to which the
proposition applies.

 \tag{4.17}

then it contains at most one support prime of a fixed selected state.

# 5. Parallel Lucas/reflection determinants

The previous minor is pair-specific.  I next tested whether selected
\(q\)-anchoring supplies several common integer tracks, allowing one
determinant to charge a whole star.

For a lower support prime \(p=q-d\), the two raw zero positions and their
reflections are

\[
 a=d-t-1,
 \qquad r=6d+t,
\]

\[
 \bar a=p-1-a=q-2d+t,
 \qquad
 \bar r=p-1-r=q-7d-t-1.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

All four are Apéry zeros modulo \(p\), with repetitions allowed at a
midpoint.  Fix an integer slope

\[
 L\le-7.
\]

Define four integer tracks

\[
 M_{1,L}(d)=(1-L)q+Ld-t-1,
\]

\[
 M_{6,L}(d)=(6-L)q+Ld+t,
\]

\[
 \overline M_{1,L}(d)=(-1-L)q+Ld+t,
\]

\[
 \overline M_{6,L}(d)=(-6-L)q+Ld-t-1.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

## Lemma 5.1 — four exact parallel selected tracks

For every lower support prime \(p=q-d\),

\[
 p\mid b_{M_{1,L}(d)},\quad
 p\mid b_{M_{6,L}(d)},\quad
 p\mid b_{\overline M_{1,L}(d)},\quad
 p\mid b_{\overline M_{6,L}(d)}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

At \(d=0\), the selected upper prime satisfies

\[
 q\mid b_{M_{1,L}(0)},\quad
 q\mid b_{M_{6,L}(0)},\quad
 q\mid b_{\overline M_{1,L}(0)},\quad
 q\mid b_{\overline M_{6,L}(0)}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

The four tracks have the exact base-\(p\) decompositions

\[
 M_{1,L}(d)=(1-L)p+a,
\]

\[
 M_{6,L}(d)=(6-L)p+r,
\]

\[
 \overline M_{1,L}(d)=(-2-L)p+\bar a,
\]

\[
 \overline M_{6,L}(d)=(-7-L)p+\bar r.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

All quotient digits are nonnegative because \(L\le-7\), and they are
less than \(p\) for all sufficiently large project primes.  Gessel--Lucas
and the four zero positions give (5.3).

At \(d=0\),

\[
 M_{1,L}(0)=(-L)q+n,
 \qquad
 M_{6,L}(0)=(6-L)q+t,
\]

\[
 \overline M_{1,L}(0)=(-1-L)q+t,
 \qquad
 \overline M_{6,L}(0)=(-7-L)q+n.
 \tag{5.6}
\]

The selected upper prime divides both \(b_t\) and \(b_n\), so another
application of Gessel--Lucas proves (5.4).  \(\square\)

Consequently, choose any three of the four tracks and form the matrix
whose rows are evaluated at

\[
 d=0,\quad d=q-p,\quad d=q-\ell.
\]

Every entry in the first row is divisible by \(q\), every entry in the
second by \(p\), and every entry in the third by \(\ell\).  Therefore,
for the resulting determinant \(\mathscr D_{q,p,\ell,L}\),

\[
 \boxed{
 qp\ell\mid\mathscr D_{q,p,\ell,L}.}
 \tag{5.7}
\]

This divisibility is true even when the determinant is zero.  A
height/root-count argument would additionally need nonvanishing; no
nonvanishing theorem for these multi-track determinants is present in
canonical source.

## 5.1 Exact common-gap classification

The pairwise differences between the four parallel tracks are independent
of both \(d\) and \(L\).  Their nonzero absolute values are

\[
 \boxed{
 2q-2t-1,
 \quad
 5q+2t+1,
 \quad
 7q,
 \quad
 12q+2t+1.}
 \tag{5.8}
\]

For example,

\[
 M_{6,L}-M_{1,L}=5q+2t+1,
\]

\[
 M_{1,L}-\overline M_{1,L}=2q-2t-1,
\]

and the remaining differences follow directly from (5.2).

By (1.6), every nonempty raw minus fibre has

\[
 t<\frac{q-7}{8}.
\]

Hence every nonzero parallel gap obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 |H_{\rm parallel}|>\frac74q.}
 \tag{5.9}
\]

This classification closes the hoped-for “equal-slope cancellation gives
a short transfer” mechanism.  Equal slopes do make every determinant term
have the same total index sum, but the fixed separation left after that
cancellation is still \(\asymp T\), never \(O(\Lambda)\).

## 5.2 Bounded track count versus growing height

For one fixed slope \(L\), there are only four source-forced parallel
tracks: the two literal rows and their two reflections.  Thus a same-slope
determinant has dimension at most four.  It can charge at most the upper
prime and three lower primes at once.  Covering a star of \(K\) lower
primes therefore needs \(\gg K\) bounded determinants.

For fixed \(L\), all indices in (5.2) are \(O_{J,L}(T)\).  The elementary
bound \(b_m<34^m\) and Hadamard give, for every nonzero bounded-size
determinant,

\[
 \log|\mathscr D|\ll_{J,L}T.
 \tag{5.10}
\]

A product of \(\gg K\) such determinants has logarithmic height
\(O_J(KT)\), while the \(K\) marked lower primes cost only
\(K\log T+O_J(K)\).  The deficit is \(T/\log T\).

One can create more columns by using distinct slopes \(L_1,L_2,\ldots\).
But there are only four track types for each slope.  An \(m\)-column
matrix therefore requires slopes of magnitude \(\gg m\).  The
corresponding Lucas quotients and Apéry indices are \(\gg mT\).  The
canonical height ledger becomes

\[
 \boxed{
 \log|\det\mathscr M_m|
 \ll_J m^2T+O(m\log m).}
 \tag{5.11}
\]

Whenever the determinant is nonzero, row divisibility supplies only

\[
 \prod_{i=1}^m p_i\mid\det\mathscr M_m,
\]

whose information floor is

\[
 m\log T+O_J(m).
 \tag{5.12}
\]

Thus the standard determinant/Hadamard route misses by at least
\(mT/\log T\).  Natural row normalization cannot be used for free: every
row entry is divisible by the marked row prime, so dividing by the large
row content also divides away the very prime factor that the determinant
was meant to detect.

The exact first missing estimate for this route would be a new primitive
determinant theorem of the form

\[
 \log|\det\mathscr M_m|_{\rm primitive}
 =o(m\log T),
 \tag{5.13}
\]

or a comparably strong support-radical statement.  No such estimate, or
nonvanishing theorem capable of supporting it, follows from canonical
source.  Notice that (5.13) is much stronger than the narrow Palm
cofactor theorem actually needed by P3.2.

# 6. Prescribed-diagonal large sieve: exact diagonal circularity

The exact affine relation can be written in the local variables as

\[
 q-p=a_p+t+1,
 \qquad
 r_p=6a_p+7t+6.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Thus a support incidence is the prescribed diagonal event

\[
 t\in Z_q,
 \qquad
 a_p\in Z_p,
 \qquad
 6a_p+7t+6\in Z_p,
 \qquad
 q=p+a_p+t+1,
 \tag{6.2}
\]

with every mask retained on the actual anchor.  Fourier detection of the
linear relation in (6.1) is exact.  It does not provide the needed
inequality.

For a fixed translated cell, form the binary matrix

\[
 \mathbf M_{\ell,x}=C_x^{2Z}(\ell),
\]

with rows equal to numerical lower primes and columns equal to complete
selected states.  Its column norm is

\[
 \|\mathbf M_{\cdot,x}\|_2^2=s_x.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore every operator/large-sieve bound

\[
 \|\mathbf M\|_{2\to2}^2\le L
\]

necessarily has

\[
 L\ge\max_xs_x.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

A single support-heavy selected column is already an eigenvalue lower
bound; off-diagonal metric codegrees cannot remove it.

More directly, weighting column \(x\) by its actual degree \(k_x\), the
diagonal of the corresponding Gram form is exactly

\[
 \sum_xk_xs_x
 =M_-(T)+\Lambda\mathcal J_-^{2Z}(\Lambda)
\]

after translate averaging and the harmless endpoint convention.  Hence a
finite-measure large-sieve estimate strong enough to bound this diagonal
is precisely the desired anchored incidence estimate.  Replacing the
selected Gram matrix by the determinant-\(7\) ambient mean is the first
false implication; it is a conditional distribution theorem, not a
large-sieve identity.

This is a linear-algebraic circularity statement, not an assertion that
the actual Apéry matrix contains a saturated column.

# 7. Scoped saturation of the proved interfaces

The following is a no-go only for the interfaces proved above.  It is not
an Apéry counterexample.

Let

\[
 J_*=T^{\beta/6},
 \qquad
 R=\left\lfloor c\frac{\Lambda}{\log T}\right\rfloor.
\]

Consider one selected state and one admissible cell with \(R\) numerical
prime labels, all at scale \(T\), and with offsets

\[
 J_*\le q-p_i\le J_*+\Lambda
\]

or, more generally, in any macroscopic admissible interior band.  The
following statements are simultaneously compatible.

1. The upper prime is one common divisor of the two fixed values.
2. Every lower label is a common divisor of the same two values.
3. The q-stripped radical has logarithm
   \[
      \sum_{i=1}^R\log p_i\asymp R\log T\asymp\Lambda=o(T),
   \]
   so Proposition 2.2 and every ordinary operand-height bound allow it
   with a large margin.
4. Every exterior-minor capacity inequality is allowed, because
   \[
      34^{7J_*}\gg T^3.
   \]
5. A pair or matching product of exterior minors has height
   \(O(RJ_*)\), while its marked-prime floor is \(O(R\log T)\); the ratio
   is \(J_*/\log T\to\infty\).
6. Every bounded parallel determinant has macroscopic height \(O(T)\),
   and a growing determinant has the ledger (5.11).
7. There is only one selected state column, so every off-diagonal
   state-codegree estimate is vacuous.
8. The unweighted wrapped horizontal second-moment budget allows the two
   high fixed columns: their contribution \(O(R^2)\) is
   \[
      O\!\left(\frac{\Lambda^2}{(\log T)^2}\right)
      =T^{5\beta/3+o(1)}
      <T^{1/6+o(1)},
   \]
   far below the canonical \(T^{23/10}\) envelope.

Declaring every label actual gives

\[
 M_-(T)=R,
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_-^{2Z}(\Lambda)=R(R-1)
 \asymp M_-(T)\frac{\Lambda}{\log T}.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

Thus, for every fixed \(\eta>0\),

\[
 \frac{\mathcal J_-^{2Z}(\Lambda)}
 {M_-(T)\Lambda T^{-\eta}}
 \asymp\frac{T^\eta}{\log T}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{7.2}
\]

The packet can also be furnished with four reflection-compatible local
zero positions per lower characteristic and the reflected upper pair,
away from the finitely many midpoint and gap-one hyperplanes.  That
keeps it within the pointwise zero-count, reflection, no-consecutive-zero,
raw-window, depth, and degree-capacity interfaces.

**Scope.**  This packet is a simultaneous feasibility assignment for the
named support, height, determinant, and codegree inequalities.  It is not

it does not show that the desired theorem is false.  It proves that the
interfaces established in Sections 2--6 do not imply a fixed positive
\(\eta\).

# 8. Smallest genuinely new sufficient theorem

Put

 Q_\eta=\Lambda T^{-\eta}.

For \(m\ge0\), define the actual-Palm, support-blind dyadic layer

\[
 \mathfrak P_{2Z,m}(Q_\eta)
 =\frac1\Lambda\sum_{\tau,x,j}
 k_{x,j}^\tau
 1_{\{2^mQ_\eta<s_{x,j}^\tau\le2^{m+1}Q_\eta\}}.
 \tag{8.1}
\]

The smallest clean non-collapsing input is:

> **Selected q-stripped two-index Palm high-column tail.**  For some
> fixed \(\eta>0\) and \(\delta>0\), uniformly for every \(m\ge0\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> \mathfrak P_{2Z,m}(Q_\eta)
> \ll_J
> \frac{M_-(T)}{2^m(m+1)^{1+\delta}}.}
> \tag{8.2}
> \]

This is not assumed to be true in any proof above.  The following
propagation is exact.

## Proposition 8.1 — tail-to-M2ZP implication

If (8.2) holds, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal J_-^{2Z}(\Lambda)
 \ll_JM_-(T)\Lambda T^{-\eta}.}
 \tag{8.3}
\]

### Proof

The cells with \(s_{x,j}^\tau\le Q_\eta\) contribute at most

\[
 Q_\eta\frac1\Lambda\sum_{\tau,x,j}k_{x,j}^\tau
 =M_-(T)Q_\eta.
\]

The \(m\)-th dyadic layer contributes at most

\[
 2^{m+1}Q_\eta\mathfrak P_{2Z,m}(Q_\eta)
 \ll_J
 \frac{M_-(T)Q_\eta}{(m+1)^{1+\delta}}.
\]

Summing over \(m\) proves (8.3).  \(\square\)

At

\[
 \eta=\frac\beta3,
\]

we have \(Q_\eta=H\), so (8.2) reaches the literal critical endpoint.
For any fixed \(\eta>0\), it gives a fixed-power final gain through the
existing deterministic scale lift.

The premise is strictly stronger than the actual-pair target and passes
the packaging check: \(s_{x,j}^\tau\) charges every support-blind common
prime of the q-stripped fixed gcd, including primes which fail the final
primitive, residual, depth, or minus-first masks.  One actual anchor and

large left side in (8.2).

 \tag{8.4}

Neither (8.2) nor (8.4) follows from canonical source.  They require a
new global theorem about the foreign large-prime support of the

\begin{lemma}[Elementary growth of the Ap\'ery numbers]
\label{lem:q6-apery-ratio}
For every $m\ge0$,
\[
 5b_m\le b_{m+1}<34b_m.
\]
The left inequality is strict for $m\ge1$.  Consequently $b_m<34^m$
for $m\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Positivity follows from the binomial formula.  The recurrence gives
\[
 (m+1)^3b_{m+1}=P(m)b_m-m^3b_{m-1}<P(m)b_m,

and
$34(m+1)^3-P(m)=51m^2+75m+29>0$.
