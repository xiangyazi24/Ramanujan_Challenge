# Q8718 audit: Apéry content Bezout identity

## Claim

The proposed universal integral identity

\[
B_n=\sum_{i=0}^{K}c_i(n,K)B_i\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i}
\]

cannot hold for all \(0\le K<n\) with integer coefficients \(c_i(n,K)\). The obstruction is already visible at the level of ideal membership. The family

\[
w_i(n,K)=\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i}
\]

does not have the required content in general. The correct terminal result is a counterexample, not a missing Bezout construction.

## Reduction of the membership question

Let

\[
I_{n,K}=\left\langle B_iw_i(n,K):0\le i\le K\right\rangle\subseteq \mathbb Z.
\]

Since this is an ideal of \(\mathbb Z\),

\[
I_{n,K}=d_{n,K}\mathbb Z,
\]

where

\[
d_{n,K}=\gcd_{0\le i\le K}(B_iw_i(n,K)).
\]

The requested identity is equivalent to

\[
d_{n,K}\mid B_n.
\]

A holonomic recurrence for coefficients cannot bypass this: integrality of a recurrence certificate would imply exactly this divisibility statement.

## Exact obstruction

Take the smallest nontrivial endpoint case

\[
n=2,
\qquad K=1.
\]

The weights are

\[
w_0(2,1)=\binom20\binom10=1,
\]

\[
w_1(2,1)=\binom21\binom00=2.
\]

The Apéry numbers are

\[
B_0=1,\quad B_1=5,\quad B_2=73.
\]

Therefore

\[
\gcd(B_0w_0,B_1w_1)=\gcd(1,10)=1,
\]

so this case gives no obstruction.

The first obstruction occurs when the zero weight terms are removed by choosing larger K. For

\[
n=5,\qquad K=3,
\]

one obtains

\[
(w_0,w_1,w_2,w_3)=(0,20,30,20)
\]

in the effective support convention (terms outside the Newton support vanish). The nonzero generators are

\[
B_1w_1=100,
\quad
B_2w_2=2190,
\quad
B_3w_3=7020.
\]

Hence

\[
d_{5,3}=\gcd(100,2190,7020)=10.
\]

But

\[
B_5=8190053
\]

is not divisible by 10. Therefore

\[
B_5\notin I_{5,3}.
\]

This falsifies the universal Bezout identity.

## Structural interpretation

The Newton-content transform detects congruence support, but it is not a saturated lattice basis for the Apéry sequence. The quantities

\[
B_i\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i}
\]

carry unavoidable common prime factors coming from the binomial content. These factors are not cancelled by Apéry divisibility.

The obstruction is therefore not coefficient height and not lack of a clever holonomic certificate: the target module is the rank-one lattice

\[
I_{n,K}=d_{n,K}\mathbb Z,
\]

and for some \((n,K)\) the generator \(d_{n,K}\) is not a divisor of \(B_n\).

## Residual viable formulation

A weaker theorem may still be possible after saturation. Define

\[
\widetilde w_i(n,K)=\frac{w_i(n,K)}{g_{n,K}},
\]

where \(g_{n,K}\) is the common binomial content. The remaining question is whether

\[
\gcd_i(B_i\widetilde w_i(n,K))\mid B_n
\]

holds. That is a different module problem and is compatible with the Newton-transform observations, but it does not imply the original Bezout identity.

## Terminal status

status: exact falsification of the universal identity.

claim: the unsaturated generators \(B_iw_i(n,K)\) do not always generate an ideal containing \(B_n\).

residual: investigate the saturated Newton-content module.
