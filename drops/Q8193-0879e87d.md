# First-jet CRT aliasing and an actual zero-defect shell: terminal no-go for the `Delta` carrier

## Verdict

The proposed root/jet carrier does **not** presently yield the required

\[
\sum_s \log(2+|\Delta_s|)\le X^{\theta+o(1)},\qquad \theta<\frac85.
\]

There are two exact obstructions.

1. **Nonvanishing fails on actual Apéry data.** At the dyadic scale
   \[
   X=64,\qquad m=2,\qquad p=73,
   \]
   the prime `73` is an actual nonmidpoint shell hit, and the integral, reflection-aligned candidate
   \[
   R(V)=21,\qquad P(U)=21\bigl(U(U+1)-6\bigr)
   \]
   matches the normalized value/derivative jets exactly, over \(\mathbb Z\), yet
   \[
   \boxed{\Delta_{64,2,R}=0.}
   \]
   Thus no universal characteristic-zero nonvanishing theorem follows from the shell identity, reflection, integrality, degree bounds, or the Gessel first jet. The example survives a prescribed positive degree or monicity by replacing `R(V)=21` with
   \[
   R_d(V)=21+(V-6)^d.
   \]

2. **Every nonzero defect carries exactly the old target product.** For a fixed physical `m`, let \(\mathcal J_{X,m}\) be the labeled nonmidpoint hit primes assigned to the candidate and put
   \[
   Q_{X,m}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal J_{X,m}}p.
   \]
   All first-jet conditions collapse by CRT to one residue class for the single scalar \(R(M)\), and the complete set of defects is one arithmetic progression
   \[
   \boxed{
   \Delta(R_t)=Q_{X,m}\bigl(d_{X,m}+2C_Xt\bigr),\qquad t\in\mathbb Z.
   }
   \]
   Consequently every nonzero defect satisfies
   \[
   |\Delta|\ge Q_{X,m}.
   \]
   Conversely, after a jet-preserving constant shift, one can arrange either `Delta=0` or
   \[
   Q_{X,m}\le |\Delta|\le 2C_XQ_{X,m}.
   \]
   Therefore the root/jet lattice does not compress the labeled prime product: it aliases it.

For any charged family \(\mathscr S_X\) with nonzero defects,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s\in\mathscr S_X}\log(2+|\Delta_s|)
\ge
\sum_{s\in\mathscr S_X}\sum_{p\in\mathcal J_s}\log p.
}
\tag{V}
\]

This remains true after division by any universal factors that preserve the target primes. Hence a \(\theta<8/5\) defect-height theorem already contains a \(\theta<8/5\) theorem for the actual charged cross-characteristic incidence mass. The first-jet lattice itself supplies no part of that power saving.

The smallest genuinely stronger local datum is the **first-primary conormal class**, namely the mod-\(p\) class obtained after dividing the shell value and jet defect by one power of the labeled characteristic. It is displayed explicitly in Section 8. It is one full `p`-adic digit beyond the current root/jet lattice. Existing Apéry recurrence, Lucas/supercongruence, modular, Franel, G-function, and one-characteristic sheaf theorems do not provide the required horizontal aggregate estimate for this class.

Accordingly, the result is a **terminal no-go for the present `Delta` route**, not a counterexample to the desired fifth-moment theorem for the actual Apéry zero sets. An eventual positive theorem must add first-primary, mixed-characteristic coherence and an archimedean power saving; it cannot be obtained by another rearrangement of the same first jet.

## 2. Exact shell algebra

Put

\[
\lambda_j=j(j+1),
\qquad
Q_k(V)=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(V-\lambda_j),
\qquad Q_0=1.
\]

Since

\[
(U-j)(U+j+1)=U(U+1)-j(j+1)=V-\lambda_j,
\]

one has the exact identity

\[
\binom Uk\binom{U+k}{k}
=
\frac{Q_k(V)}{(k!)^2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
S_X(U)=\sum_{k=0}^{X-1}\frac{Q_k(V)^2}{(k!)^4},
\qquad
H_X(V)=C_X\sum_{k=0}^{X-1}\frac{Q_k(V)^2}{(k!)^4}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This makes the reflection `U -> -1-U` literal: it fixes `V`.

For a physical integer `m`, put

\[
M=m(m+1),
\qquad
G_m(U)=V-M.
\]

An aligned candidate is

\[
P_R(U)=(V-M)R(V).
\tag{2.2}
\]

At the two reflected physical roots,

\[
P_R(m)=P_R(-m-1)=0,
\]

and

\[
P_R'(m)=(2m+1)R(M),
\qquad
P_R'(-m-1)=-(2m+1)R(M).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the apparently odd derivative supplies no extra direction: both reflected derivative jets are governed by the same scalar `R(M)`.

## 3. First-jet CRT alias theorem

### Proposition 3.1 — exact lattice of aligned defects

Fix `X`, a physical `m`, and a finite set \(\mathcal J=\mathcal J_{X,m}\) of distinct nonmidpoint shell-hit primes in `(X,2X]`. Put

\[
C=C_X=((X-1)!)^4,
\qquad M=m(m+1),
\qquad Q=\prod_{p\in\mathcal J}p.
\]

Assume that at least one integral polynomial `R_0(V)` gives an aligned candidate realizing the prescribed modular root/jet data at every `p in J`. Define

\[
\Delta(R)=2CR(M)-H_X'(M).
\]

Then:

1. There is one residue class `c mod Q` such that every aligned candidate satisfies
   \[
   R(M)\equiv c\pmod Q.
   \tag{3.1}
   \]

2. Every integer in that residue class occurs as `R(M)` for another aligned candidate. More precisely,
   \[
   R_t(V)=R_0(V)+tQ,
   \qquad t\in\mathbb Z,
   \tag{3.2}
   \]
   preserves every modular polynomial jet at every prime in `J`.

3. If
   \[
   d=\frac{\Delta(R_0)}Q\in\mathbb Z,
   \]
   then
   \[
   \boxed{
   \Delta(R_t)=Q(d+2Ct),\qquad t\in\mathbb Z.
   }
   \tag{3.3}
   \]

4. Every nonzero aligned defect has
   \[
   |\Delta|\ge Q.
   \tag{3.4}
   \]
   There is always an aligned candidate with either `Delta=0` or
   \[
   Q\le |\Delta|\le 2CQ.
   \tag{3.5}
   \]

#### Proof

For every `p in J`, the proved divisibility gives

\[
2CR(M)\equiv H_X'(M)\pmod p.
\]

Because `p>X`, no such prime divides `C`; all window primes are odd, so `2C` is a unit modulo `p`. Hence each prime determines one class

\[
R(M)\equiv (2C)^{-1}H_X'(M)\pmod p.
\]

CRT gives the unique class (3.1). In particular, the evaluations of any two aligned candidates differ by a multiple of `Q`.

Conversely,

\[
P_{R_t}(U)-P_{R_0}(U)=tQ(V-M)
\]

has every coefficient divisible by every `p in J`. Thus its value and every formal derivative vanish modulo each target prime. The shift preserves not only the first jet but any finite modular jet conditions encoded in the root/jet lattice. This proves (3.2).

Substitution gives

\[
\Delta(R_t)=\Delta(R_0)+2CQt=Q(d+2Ct),
\]

proving (3.3). A nonzero integer divisible by `Q` has absolute value at least `Q`, proving (3.4).

If `d` is not divisible by `2C`, choose `t` so that `d+2Ct` is the least absolute nonzero representative modulo `2C`; its absolute value is at most `C`. If `d` is divisible by `2C`, one choice gives zero and either neighboring choice gives absolute value `2C`. This proves (3.5). ∎

### Corollary 3.2 — exact local height diagnostic

Let

\[
h_{X,m}:=\min_{R\text{ aligned},\ \Delta(R)\ne0}
\log(2+|\Delta(R)|).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\log Q+O(1)
\le h_{X,m}
\le \log Q+\log(2C_X)+O(1).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Stirling gives

\[
\log(2C_X)=4X\log X-4X+O(\log X).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus the first-jet lattice has two and only two contributions to its raw logarithmic height:

- the full labeled target-prime mass `log Q`;
- at most one ordinary `X log X` shell-denominator overhead per charged candidate.

It creates no cancellation in the target-prime mass.

### Robustness under degree normalization

A prescribed positive degree does not remove the alias. If one needs `R` monic of exact degree `d>=1`, replace a candidate by

\[
R(V)+(V-M)^d.
\]

Its value at `M`, and hence every conclusion above, is unchanged. Any more restrictive normalization that forbids the shift (3.2) is genuinely new input and must itself be proved compatible with all target jets; it is not part of the present lattice.

## 4. Actual Apéry zero-defect counterexample

### Proposition 4.1

Take

\[
X=64,\qquad m=2,\qquad M=6,\qquad p=73,\qquad C=C_{64}=(63!)^4.
\]

Then `p` is an actual nonmidpoint window hit and

\[
R(V)=21,
\qquad
P(U)=21(V-6)=21(U-2)(U+3)
\]

is an integral reflection-aligned candidate matching the normalized shell derivative jets exactly, but

\[
\boxed{\Delta_{64,2,R}=0.}
\]

#### Proof

First,

\[
b_2
=1+\binom21^2\binom31^2+inom22^2\binom42^2
=1+36+36=73.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Hence `73` is an actual Apéry hit at `r=2`. It lies in the dyadic window

\[
64<73\le128.
\]

Its reflected partner is `70`, and it is not the midpoint because

\[
2r+1=5\not\equiv0\pmod{73}.
\]

Moreover `73` is the only window prime hitting the physical point `m=2`, since `b_2=73`.

From (2.1), at `V=6`,

\[
\frac{H_X(V)}C
=1+V^2+\frac{V^2(V-2)^2}{16}
+\sum_{k=3}^{X-1}\frac{Q_k(V)^2}{(k!)^4}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For every `k>=3`, `Q_k(V)` contains the factor `V-6`; its square therefore has both value and first derivative zero at `V=6`. Consequently

\[
\frac{H_X(6)}C
=1+36+36=73,
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{H_X'(6)}C
&=2\cdot6
 +\frac{2\cdot6\cdot(6-2)^2
       +2\cdot6^2(6-2)}{16}\\
&=12+\frac{192+288}{16}\\
&=42.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus

\[
H_X'(6)=42C=2C\cdot21,
\]

and therefore

\[
\Delta_{64,2,R}=2C\,R(6)-H_X'(6)=42C-42C=0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The derivative matching is exact over `Z`. Since

\[
P'(U)=21(2U+1),
\]

one has

\[
2C P'(2)=210C
=(2\cdot2+1)H_X'(6)
=\left.\frac d{dU}H_X(U(U+1))\right|_{U=2},
\tag{4.6}
\]

and the same equality with both signs reversed at `U=-3`. Hence the reflected value/jet pair is not merely congruent modulo `73`; it is exactly aligned in characteristic zero under the normalization producing the stated `Delta`. ∎

### Exact numerical progression at the counterexample

For every aligned candidate at this example, Proposition 3.1 gives

\[
R(6)\equiv21\pmod{73}.
\]

Write `R(6)=21+73t`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{64,2,R}=146C\,t.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus:

- `t=0` gives the exact zero defect;
- after removing the target-safe universal factor `2C` in this particular example, the primitive nonzero defects are exactly
  \[
  73t;
  \]
- the smallest nonzero primitive defect is exactly the old target prime `73`.

This is the requested exact numerical diagnostic.

### Scope of the counterexample

This finite example does not disprove a theorem asserted only for all sufficiently large `X` and using additional actual-Apéry information. It does prove that:

- nonvanishing is not formal;
- no all-scale nonvanishing proposition is true;
- any eventual nonvanishing theorem must include an arithmetic exceptional-set mechanism not present in the current root/jet identities.

## 5. Aggregate height is bounded below by charged incidence mass

### Proposition 5.1 — product lower bound for every charged family

Let \(\mathscr S_X\) be any finite charged family. For each `s`, let \(\mathcal J_s\) be its set of labeled target primes and suppose

\[
0\ne\Delta_s\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
p\mid\Delta_s\quad(p\in\mathcal J_s).
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s\in\mathscr S_X}\log(2+|\Delta_s|)
&\ge \sum_s\log|\Delta_s|\\
&\ge \sum_s\log\prod_{p\in\mathcal J_s}p\\
&=\sum_s\sum_{p\in\mathcal J_s}\log p.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

If an incidence `(p,r)` occurs in between one and `B` members of the family, the right side is exactly the corresponding incidence log-mass counted with multiplicity between `1` and `B`. Bundling many primes into one candidate does not compress this mass, because logarithms turn the target product back into a sum.

### Universal divisions do not change the obstruction

Suppose one divides by an explicitly proved universal integer `u_s` with

\[
u_s\mid\Delta_s,
\qquad
p\nmid u_s\quad(p\in\mathcal J_s).
\]

Then every target still divides the primitive value

\[
\Delta_s^\sharp=\Delta_s/u_s,
\]

so the same lower bound holds:

\[
\log|\Delta_s^\sharp|
\ge\sum_{p\in\mathcal J_s}\log p.
\tag{5.2}
\]

A division that removes any factor of `Q_s` is not target-safe and cannot be used by the consumer. Thus primary saturation can remove nuisance height but cannot beat the target-product floor.

### Corollary 5.2 — what `theta<8/5` really requires

The committed unconditional root-mass estimate has the dyadic form

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}|Z_p|\log p
\ll X^{8/5+o(1)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For a bounded-multiplicity charged subfamily, a bound

\[
\sum_s\log(2+|\Delta_s|)\ll X^{\theta+o(1)}
\]

with `theta<8/5` necessarily includes the genuinely stronger actual-incidence estimate

\[
\boxed{
\sum_s\sum_{p\in\mathcal J_s}\log p
\ll X^{\theta+o(1)}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The `Delta` divisibility does not prove (5.4); it consumes it.

For the ideal exponent `theta=3/2`, the required saving over the present `8/5` scale is exactly

\[
\frac85-\frac32=\frac1{10}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is a fixed power saving, not a logarithmic cleanup.

### Raw shell-overhead diagnostic

Proposition 3.1 also gives a jet-preserving selection with

\[
\log(2+|\Delta_s|)
\le
\sum_{p\in\mathcal J_s}\log p
+\log(2C_X)+O(1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_s\log(2+|\Delta_s|)
\le
\sum_s\sum_{p\in\mathcal J_s}\log p
+|\mathscr S_X|\bigl(4X\log X+O(X)\bigr)
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

for a suitable nonzero CRT shift of each candidate.

If

\[
|\mathscr S_X|=X^{\sigma+o(1)},
\]

the unremoved raw shell overhead has exponent `1+sigma`. Hence:

- to reach any `theta<8/5` by raw defects, one needs `sigma<3/5` by a fixed margin;
- to reach `theta=3/2`, one needs
  \[
  |\mathscr S_X|\le X^{1/2+o(1)};
  \]
- if the current bookkeeping has `sigma=3/5`, then `theta=3/2` would require reducing the average post-saturation logarithmic overhead from `X^{1+o(1)}` to
  \[
  X^{9/10+o(1)}.
  \]

These implications are conditional only on the displayed family size; they do not assume a heuristic distribution of Apéry roots.

## 6. Why the listed characteristic-zero rearrangements cannot change the conclusion

### 6.1 Newton–Racah and Christoffel–Darboux

The identities

\[
R_n(\lambda_n)=b_n,
\qquad
(2n+1)R_n'(\lambda_n)=D_n
\]

identify the shell value and its first tangent in a different basis. They do not increase the rank of the local target module. At fixed physical `M`, the aligned derivative still depends on the single scalar `R(M)`, and Proposition 3.1 applies unchanged.

A CD determinant formed only from these value/first-derivative coordinates is therefore either:

- a multiple of the same target product;
- identically zero on the aligned line; or
- a target-unit after primitive saturation, as in the earlier companion audit.

No basis change can turn a rank-one congruence module into rank two.

### 6.2 Discriminants

A discriminant can certify separability of a fixed characteristic-zero polynomial away from its prime divisors. It does not give a small exceptional-prime theorem uniform in the moving degree/index. The explicit large prime divisors already listed in the question show that no support cutoff at `O(X)` is available. More fundamentally, the counterexample in Section 4 has `Delta=0` before any discriminant argument is invoked.

### 6.3 Hermite interpolation

Hermite interpolation is compatible with the no-go, not a cure for it. The difference

\[
tQ(V-M)
\]

has coefficients divisible by every target prime and hence preserves arbitrary finite modular jets. Antisymmetric derivative data can therefore be prescribed inside the reflection-invariant principal ideal `(V-M)` while retaining the entire CRT shift direction.

### 6.4 Two evaluations without a second target-safe coordinate

Suppose one evaluates the same rank-one coordinate at two physical points. A determinant helps only if each row carries **two primitive target-safe coordinates**. With one target-safe coordinate and one transverse target-unit, the primitive determinant is a target-unit; retaining the old shell/content scalar merely restates the original gcd. This is exactly the primary-saturation obstruction exhibited in `drops/Q8043-f58f62d5.md`.

## 7. Published-theorem audit with the parameters substituted

No published theorem I found supplies the required mixed-characteristic estimate. The mismatch is quantitative and in the order of quantifiers, not merely terminological.

### 7.1 Gessel and later Lucas/supercongruence theorems

Gessel's theorem and its modern refinements concern congruences for one prime `p` and base-`p` digits. In the present notation one substitutes

\[
m=a_pp+r_p,
\qquad 0\le r_p<p,
\]

where both `a_p` and the hit index `r_p` vary with `p`. The congruences recover the individual root and first-primary digit conditions. They do not bound

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathbf1_{p\mid b_{r_p}}
\]

for a prescribed physical `m`, and they do not produce one nonzero characteristic-zero integer of aggregate height `X^{theta}` across the varying characteristics.

Primary references:

- I. Gessel, *Some congruences for Apéry numbers*, J. Number Theory **14** (1982), 362–368, DOI `10.1016/0022-314X(82)90071-3`.
- F. Beukers, *Some congruences for the Apéry numbers*, J. Number Theory **21** (1985), 141–155, DOI `10.1016/0022-314X(85)90047-2`.
- A. Straub, *Gessel–Lucas congruences for sporadic sequences*, Monatshefte Math. **203** (2024), 883–898, DOI `10.1007/s00605-023-01894-3`.

The mod-`p^2` refinements are relevant to the first-primary invariant in Section 8, but no horizontal moment theorem for those residues is contained in them.

### 7.2 Lucas-type G-functions

Adamczewski–Bell–Delaygue prove Frobenius functional equations and algebraic-independence criteria for G-functions with Lucas congruences. For each fixed prime this explains an identity of the form

\[
F(z)\equiv A_p(z)F(z^p)\pmod p.
\]

It gives no quantitative uniform estimate for the moving coordinate

\[
[z^{r_p}]A_p(z)=b_{r_p}\pmod p,
\qquad r_p=r_p(m).
\]

Reference:

- B. Adamczewski, J. P. Bell, É. Delaygue, *Algebraic independence of G-functions and congruences à la Lucas*, Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Supér. **52** (2019), 515–559, DOI `10.24033/asens.2392`.

### 7.3 Franel/Hasse factorization and mod-`p` Galois groups

The exact committed pullback is

\[
K_p(x)^2
=(1+x)^{p-1}A_p(\phi(x))
=\sum_{h=0}^{p-1}b_h\Psi_{p,h}(x).
\]

Thus `b_h=0` is the vanishing of a coordinate in a `p`-dependent, cover-adapted basis; it is not the vanishing of the Hasse polynomial at a bounded-degree geometric point. The normalized square root has degree `Theta(p)`. Computing the mod-`p` Galois group or factorization of the generating series separately for every `p` therefore does not bound the cross-prime alignment of these coordinates.

Reference:

- X. Caruso, F. Fürnsinn, D. Vargas-Montoya, W. Zudilin, *Galois groups of Apéry-like series modulo primes*, arXiv:2510.23298; accepted version DOI `10.1017/S0004972725100932`.

### 7.4 Known arithmetic of the integers `b_n`

Luca–Shparlinski prove lower bounds for the number and size of prime factors of `b_n` for a density-one set of fixed indices `n`. Their theorem has neither the direction nor the quantifiers needed here: Q8193 requires an upper bound for many primes `p~X` dividing coefficients whose indices `r_p` move with the same primes at a fixed physical `m`.

Reference:

- F. Luca, I. E. Shparlinski, *Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers*, J. London Math. Soc. **78** (2008), 545–562, DOI `10.1112/jlms/jdn031`.

### 7.5 One-characteristic sheaves

The committed marked rank-three factor

\[
(T-p)(T^2-a_{p,h}T+p^3),
\qquad a_{p,h}\equiv b_h\pmod p,
\]

is useful at one fixed characteristic. At a Hasse zero, its next coefficient contains first-primary information. Standard Deligne/Katz bounds control sums over geometric points or characters **inside that characteristic**. They do not compare the defining-prime residues as `p` varies and `h=h(p)` moves. A horizontal large sieve for these marked first-primary classes would be new input, not an application with parameters already covered by a published theorem.

## 8. The smallest stronger actual invariant

The current lattice knows the shell root and first tangent modulo `p`. The first information not determined by it is the next primary digit.

Let `p in (X,2X]`, let `h<X` be the folded actual root, and put

\[
\lambda_h=h(h+1).
\]

For a physical `m` realizing this hit,

\[
M=m(m+1)\equiv\lambda_h\pmod p.
\]

Define the exact lift parameter

\[
a_{p,h}(m)=\frac{M-\lambda_h}{p}\pmod p.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Because `h<X`, the shell interpolates the actual Apéry value:

\[
H_X(\lambda_h)=C_Xb_h.
\]

Since `p|b_h`, Taylor expansion in the integer polynomial `H_X` gives the exact mod-`p` identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{H_X(M)}p
\equiv
C_X\frac{b_h}{p}
+a_{p,h}(m)H_X'(\lambda_h)
\pmod p.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This is the first-primary shell-value class. It is invisible after reduction modulo `p`.

Now let `R` satisfy the first-jet congruence at the local root,

\[
2C_XR(\lambda_h)\equiv H_X'(\lambda_h)\pmod p.
\]

Define its first-primary conormal vector

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak c_{p,h}(R)=
\left(
\frac{2C_XR(\lambda_h)-H_X'(\lambda_h)}p,
\quad
2C_XR'(\lambda_h)-H_X''(\lambda_h)
\right)
\in\mathbb F_p^2.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

The quotient in the first coordinate is integral. A second Taylor expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta_{X,m,R}}p
\equiv
\mathfrak c_{p,h}^{(0)}(R)
+a_{p,h}(m)\mathfrak c_{p,h}^{(1)}(R)
\pmod p.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Equation (8.4) is the exact local invariant missing from the first-jet lattice.

### Why it is minimal

- Modulo `p`, every aligned candidate lies on the single CRT line of Proposition 3.1.
- To distinguish an exact zero from a nonzero multiple of `p`, one must pass to `pZ_p/p^2Z_p`; no mod-`p` invariant can do so.
- Equation (8.4) is precisely that quotient, expressed in shell coordinates.
- If its right side is nonzero for one assigned prime, then `Delta` is certainly nonzero.
- The example in Section 4 has the right side zero, so even this class needs an exceptional-set or aggregate theorem; universal nonvanishing is false.

The rank-three marked Frobenius coefficient `a_{p,h}` from the committed hypergeometric construction is a possible geometric source of comparable first-primary information. What is not presently proved is a target-safe integral comparison identifying it with (8.2) or (8.4) uniformly in the moving pair `(p,h)`, followed by a horizontal power-saving estimate.
