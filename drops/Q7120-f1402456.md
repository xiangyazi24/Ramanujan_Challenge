## Conclusion

The proposed statement is **not proved by the current identities**, and the available structural information does not justify the stronger claim that every fully-deflated residual common root outside \(U_H\) must contain either an intermediate return or a centered adjacent pair.

The correct unconditional statement supported by the reflection/continuant identities is weaker:

> Every residual common root is a zero-orbit collision whose selected zero times form a symmetric subset of the full reflection orbit. If the subset has two consecutive selected times, the no-consecutive-zero lemma rules it out. If it contains a centered adjacent pair, the residual is explained by the central reflection carrier. Otherwise the obstruction can only be excluded by an additional primitive-return theorem, which is not currently available.

Therefore a nonconsecutive skip-subchain is not eliminated merely by full deflation.

## Audit of the proposed obstruction

Let \(N_H(x)\) denote the gap polynomial and \(U_H\) the known carrier factor. Reflection gives the involution

\[
 j \mapsto -j-H-1
\]

on the root parameters. Hence a common root of two gap polynomials can occur because several zero times belong to one reflection orbit.

The fully-deflated residual content removes the obvious orbit carriers, but it does not encode the order structure of the surviving zero times. In particular:

* removing \(U_H\) does not imply that the remaining orbit points are consecutive;
* a surviving pair can be a skip pair \(m,m+h\) with \(h>1\);
* the gap polynomial recurrence only says such a pair is controlled by the continuant numerator \(N_h(m)\), not that it factors through an intermediate zero.

The missing implication would be:

\[
 b_m\equiv b_{m+h}\equiv0\pmod p
\Longrightarrow
(\exists t,\ m<t<m+h:b_t\equiv0)\ \lor\ \text{centered pair}.
\]

No identity in the current proof establishes this implication.

## Computational search status

A targeted search was organized as follows:

1. enumerate primes \(p\);
2. compute the Apéry zero set
\[
Z_p=\{j\in[0,p-1]:b_j\equiv0\pmod p\};
\]
3. compute all primitive gaps \(h\) with
\[
 j,j+h\in Z_p,
\]
while all intermediate times are nonzero;
4. test whether the pair is in the reflection carrier \(U_H\) or is centered.

No explicit violating primitive skip-chain was found in the tested range used for the audit. However, absence of examples is not evidence for the claimed theorem: the search space is exactly where a hidden hyperelliptic/continuant coincidence could first appear.

The earliest fully-deflated residual contents reported in the project computation at \(H=24\) therefore should be interpreted as unresolved primitive candidates, not as proof of the obstruction.

for p in prime_range(7, 1000):

## Strongest theorem currently justified

The defensible theorem is:

**Primitive residual-orbit theorem (conditional form).**  Assume the primitive-return property:

For every prime \(p\), every primitive zero pair \(m,m+h\) with no intermediate zero is either the centered reflection pair or belongs to the carrier \(U_H\).

Then every fully-deflated residual common root is impossible, and the residual content is trivial.

**Unconditional form.**  Every fully-deflated residual common root corresponds to a primitive zero pair (or higher primitive zero configuration) satisfying a continuant equation \(N_h(m)=0\) modulo \(p\), with reflection symmetry giving the only known forced orbit relation.

This is the strongest statement currently supported by the reflection identity, continuant recurrence, and no-consecutive-zero lemma. A genuine proof of primitive-return requires a new argument controlling skip-subchains of the continuant zero set.
