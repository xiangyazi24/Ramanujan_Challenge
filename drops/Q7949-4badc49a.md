# 1. Exact finite means and the progression Palm measure

The finite interval mean is not \(z_p\).  It is

\[
\rho_p=\frac1M\sum_{m<M}f_p(m)=\frac{A_p}{M}.
\]

For every residue \(a\in\mathcal Z_p\), the progression

\[
\{m<M:m\equiv a\pmod p\}
\]

contains either \(\lfloor M/p\rfloor\) or \(\lceil M/p\rceil\) integers.
Consequently

\[
|A_p-Mz_p|\le Z(p).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Set

\[
\epsilon_p=z_p-\rho_p=\frac{Mz_p-A_p}{M}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Then

\[
|\epsilon_p|\le\frac{Z(p)}M\le\frac{2z_p}{X},
\qquad
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}|\epsilon_p|
\le\frac1M\sum_pZ(p)
\le\frac{2\lambda_X}{X}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Let \(\mathbb E_M\) denote uniform averaging over \([0,M)\), and, for
active \(p\), let

\[
\mathbb E_p H
=\frac1{A_p}\sum_{m\in\Omega_p}H(m)
=\frac1{A_p}
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal Z_p}
 \sum_{\substack{0\le m<M\\m\equiv a\ (p)}}H(m).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Thus \(\mathbb E_p\) is the exact Palm law obtained by conditioning the
finite population on row \(p\) being occupied.

Put

\[
\widetilde g_q=f_q-\rho_q
\tag{1.5}
\]

and define the ordered-pair field away from \(p\) by

\[
Q_{-p}^{\mathrm{fin}}(m)
=
\sum_{\substack{q,\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p\}\\q\ne\ell}}
\widetilde g_q(m)\widetilde g_\ell(m).
\tag{1.6}
\]

The finite Palm discrepancy is

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin}}
=
\mathbb E_p Q_{-p}^{\mathrm{fin}}
-
\mathbb E_M Q_{-p}^{\mathrm{fin}}.
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

This is a conditional ordered-pair covariance excess.  It measures how
much more pair correlation among the other prime rows is seen on the union
of the \(Z(p)\) progressions than in the ambient interval.

Define the completely finite-centered output

\[
\widetilde E_p
=
\sum_{\substack{q,\ell\ne p\\q\ne\ell}}
\sum_{m<M}
\widetilde g_p(m)\widetilde g_q(m)\widetilde g_\ell(m).
\tag{1.8}
\]

Since \(\rho_p=A_p/M\), conditioning gives the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde E_p
&=\sum_{m<M}(f_p(m)-\rho_p)Q_{-p}^{\mathrm{fin}}(m)\\
&=A_p\mathbb E_pQ_{-p}^{\mathrm{fin}}
  -M\rho_p\mathbb E_MQ_{-p}^{\mathrm{fin}}\\
&=\boxed{A_p\mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin}}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.9}
\]

This is the exact conditional-Palm identity requested in the question.

# 3. Raw arithmetic-progression pair-count formula

The statistic in (1.7) can be written without centered variables.

\[
K_{-p}(m)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p\}}f_q(m),
\tag{3.1}

\[
F_{p;2}
=
\sum_{m\in\Omega_p}(K_{-p}(m))_2,
\qquad
F_{2;-p}
=
\sum_{m<M}(K_{-p}(m))_2,
\tag{3.2}
\]

where \((t)_2=t(t-1)\).  Also retain the source's exact finite mean

\[
\mu_{p,q}
=
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}\rho_\ell.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then

\[
F_{p;2}
=
\sum_{a\in\mathcal Z_p}
\sum_{\substack{0\le m<M\\m\equiv a\ (p)}}
(K_{-p}(m))_2,
\tag{3.4}
\]

and direct expansion of (1.7) gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin}}
={}&
\frac{F_{p;2}}{A_p}
-rac{F_{2;-p}}M\\
&-2\sum_{q\ne p}\mu_{p,q}
 \left(\frac{J_{p,q}}{A_p}-\rho_q\right).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the recognizable progression discrepancy requested in the
question.

- The first line is the conditional excess of the ordered-pair count

- The second line removes the change in the one-row conditional marginals
  \(J_{p,q}/A_p\).  Without it, a mere increase in the density of one other
  row would be misreported as a genuine ordered-pair correlation.
- The factor \(2\) is forced by ordered pairs: either member of the pair can
  supply the shifted first marginal.

Equivalently, (AP2-Palm) is the one-sided estimate

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Bigg[&

(K_{-p}(m))_2
-rac1M\sum_{m<M}(K_{-p}(m))_2\\

-2\sum_{q\ne p}\mu_{p,q}
\left(\frac{J_{p,q}}{A_p}-\rho_q\right)
\Bigg]_+
\ll X^{o(1)}\lambda_X^2.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

It is weaker than taking positive parts pair by pair and weaker than an
absolute discrepancy estimate.  Negative conditional pair excess is
irrelevant to the desired upper bound and is deliberately retained.

ordered-pair covariance requires symmetrizing the two extension slots and

# 5. What HM2 and PP3 actually imply

## 5.1 HM2 alone

Since \(F_{p;2}\le F_{2;-p}\le5M\lambda_X^2\), equation (3.5) and
nonnegativity of the raw counts give only a bound of the form

\[
(E_p)_+\ll M\lambda_X^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The desired bound is

\[
M z_p\lambda_X^2.
\]

Thus HM2 misses the output weight \(z_p\); the possible loss is
\(z_p^{-1}\).

## 5.2 The finite PP3 theorem

The source defines

\[
\mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)
=
\sum_{p\ne q}
\bigl(\Pi_{p,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)_+.
\tag{5.2}
\]

It is important to state the logical status correctly:
`hm3_result.tex` proves the sandwich and the equivalence of the **target
estimate** for (5.2) with HM3.  It does not prove that target estimate
unconditionally.

From (4.4), because \(T_{p,q}\ge0\) and \(F_{2;-p}\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{
\bigl(A_p\mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)_+
\le
2\sum_{q\ne p}
\bigl(\Pi_{p,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)_+
+2A_pB_p^{\mathrm{fin}}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since \(B_p^{\mathrm{fin}}\le\mu^2\), summing over \(p\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p
\bigl(A_p\mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)_+
\le
2\mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)+2M\mu^3.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Combining (2.8) with (5.4),

\[
\sum_p(E_p)_+
\ll
\mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)+M\lambda_X^3.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Therefore the PP3 target would imply

\[
\sum_p(E_p)_+
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3,
\tag{5.6}
\]

which is exactly the sum of the desired local scales:

\[
\sum_p Mz_p\lambda_X^2=M\lambda_X^3.
\]

But (5.4) does not allocate this budget among outputs.  For one fixed
\(p\), it gives at best

\[
(E_p)_+
\ll
M\lambda_X^3+A_p\lambda_X^2,
\tag{5.7}
\]

whereas the desired first term is only \(A_p\lambda_X^2\).  The first
exact localization loss is

\[
\frac{M\lambda_X^3}{A_p\lambda_X^2}
\asymp\frac{\lambda_X}{z_p}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Thus PP3 controls the positive outputs only **after summing \(p\)**.
There is no pointwise implication.

A stronger, convenient but nonminimal localized hypothesis would be

\[
\sum_{q\ne p}
\bigl(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}\bigr)_+
\ll A_p\lambda_X^2X^{o(1)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Equation (5.3) shows that (5.9) implies (AP2-Palm).  It is stronger because
it takes positive parts before summing over the second anchor \(q\).
The statistic (3.6) is the weakest natural one-anchor statement: it keeps
all legitimate cancellation between the ordered extension pairs.

# 6. Why the anchored star violates exactly (AP2-Palm)

Use the reflected anchored family from `hm3_result.tex`:

\[
\mathcal Z_p^*
=
\{m_0\bmod p,\ p-1-(m_0\bmod p)\},
\qquad m_0=\lfloor3M/4\rfloor,
\]

for all but \(O(1)\) primes in the block.  Let \(L\) be the number of
active rows.  Then

\[
L\asymp\frac X{\log X},
\qquad
\lambda_X^*\asymp\frac1{\log X},
\qquad
A_p^*\asymp X.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Every active row contains the same integer \(m_0\).  Hence for fixed active
\(p\),

\[
K_{-p}^*(m_0)=L-1.
\]

More directly, for every ordered pair of distinct active
\(q,\ell\ne p\), the centered triple correlation is

\[
\sum_{m<M}g_p^*(m)g_q^*(m)g_\ell^*(m)
=1+O(X^{-1}).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Indeed the common point contributes \(1\), while every centering term is
\(O(X^{-1})\): each \(z_s^*=O(X^{-1})\), each pair intersection has
bounded size, and each column has \(O(X)\) lifts.

Therefore

\[
E_p^*\asymp (L-1)(L-2)\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

By (0.2),

\[
\mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin},*}
\asymp\frac{E_p^*}{A_p^*}
\asymp\frac{X}{(\log X)^2},
\tag{6.4}
\]

whereas

\[
(\lambda_X^*)^2\asymp\frac1{(\log X)^2}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus the anchored star violates (AP2-Palm) by a factor \(X\).  In the raw
formula (3.6), the violation is exactly the anomalously large conditional
ordered-pair count at \(m_0\); the finite first-marginal correction is only
of baseline size and cannot remove it.

# 7. The three-active-row sparse anchor

There is an even smaller diagnostic.  Choose three primes
\(p,q,\ell\in(X,2X]\) and give each a reflected two-point zero set containing
one common integer \(m_0<M\), with all other prime rows empty.  After the
same harmless endpoint/coincidence exclusions,

\[
Z(p)=Z(q)=Z(\ell)=2,
\qquad
A_p,A_q,A_\ell\asymp X,
\qquad
\lambda_X\asymp X^{-1}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For the output \(p\), the two ordered extension pairs are
\((q,\ell)\) and \((\ell,q)\).  Each centered triple correlation equals
\(1+O(X^{-1})\), so

\[
E_p=2+O(X^{-1}).
\tag{7.2}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
M z_p\lambda_X^2\asymp X^{-1}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin}}\asymp X^{-1},
\qquad
\lambda_X^2\asymp X^{-2}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Again the failure factor is \(X\).  This three-prime-row model still
satisfies the second-factorial bound: its pair mass is \(O(1)\), while
\(M\lambda_X^2\asymp1\).  It shows in the smallest possible configuration
that HM2 cannot force the one-output pair-Palm discrepancy.

The model is not a counterexample to an assumed PP3 estimate—its single
triple deliberately violates HM3/PP3 in the very sparse regime.  Its role
is diagnostic: it isolates the exact local progression event which any
per-output theorem must rule out.

# 8. Merge-safe theorem statement

The following is the clean arithmetic interface suggested by the data.

> **Hypothesis (AP2-Palm: one-output ordered-pair progression discrepancy).**
> For every active \(p\in\mathcal P_X\), let
> \(A_p,\rho_p,J_{p,q},\mu_{p,q}\) be the exact finite quantities from
> `hm3_result.tex`, and put
> \[
> \begin{aligned}
> \mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin}}
> ={}&
> \frac1{A_p}
> \sum_{a\in\mathcal Z_p}
> \sum_{\substack{0\le m<M\\m\equiv a\ (p)}}
> (K_{-p}(m))_2
> -\frac1M\sum_{m<M}(K_{-p}(m))_2\\
> &-2\sum_{q\ne p}\mu_{p,q}
> \left(\frac{J_{p,q}}{A_p}-\rho_q\right).
> \end{aligned}
> \]
> Then
> \[
> \bigl(\mathsf D_p^{(2),\mathrm{fin}}\bigr)_+
> \ll X^{o(1)}\lambda_X^2
> \]
> uniformly in active \(p\).

By (0.2)--(0.4), this hypothesis implies

\[
E_p\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^2z_p
\]

for every active \(p\).  Conversely, the latter output bound implies
(AP2-Palm), up to changing the harmless \(X^{o(1)}\) factor.  Thus at the
exponent scale they are equivalent, while (AP2-Palm) exposes the actual
arithmetic content: anti-concentration of ordered pairs of other Apéry-zero
rows on the union of the \(Z(p)\) progressions, after exact finite-marginal
bias removal.

# Final ruling

- The exact identity is (0.2), with the finite Palm statistic (1.7) and the
  exact correction (2.2).
- The raw arithmetic-progression form is (3.6).
- The finite means \(\rho_q=A_q/M\) and \(\mu_{p,q}\), not the complete
  period means \(z_q\) and \(\lambda_{p,q}\), are the correct exact
  centering parameters.
- HM2 gives no \(z_p\)-weighted pointwise result.
- The PP3 theorem, if its missing estimate is assumed, gives the desired
  scale only after summing positive outputs over \(p\); its first pointwise
  loss is \(\lambda_X/z_p\).
- A localized positive pair-Palm estimate (5.9) is sufficient but stronger
  than necessary.
- The weakest recognizable per-output interface is (AP2-Palm).
- Both the full anchored star and the three-active-row sparse anchor violate
  precisely this conditional ordered-pair progression discrepancy by a
  factor \(X\).
