# Q479: Universality of infinite-monodromy Apéry-like sequences

## Executive verdict

The data strongly supports a **universality heuristic**, but not the statement that infinite monodromy alone forces

\[
\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),b_n)=e^{o(n)}.
\]

The correct conjectural principle is weaker:

> A non-isotrivial Picard--Fuchs sequence with sufficiently large geometric monodromy and no persistent congruence obstruction should have sparse Frobenius zero sets: \(|Z_p|=p^{o(1)}\), and conjecturally \(|Z_p|=O(1)\) on average.

Infinite monodromy is a necessary qualitative condition, but not by itself the mechanism producing the bound.

---

## (A) What conjecture is supported?

The strongest reasonable conjecture suggested by the table is not “infinite monodromy implies O(1) zeros” but:

\[
\boxed{\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|\ll \pi(X)(\log X)^C}
\]

for some fixed sequence-dependent constant \(C\), and in the generic case

\[
\boxed{\frac1{\pi(X)}\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|\to \lambda_M}
\]

with finite \(\lambda_M\).

Then the P3.2 consequence follows because

\[
\sum_{p\mid b_n}\log p
\]

is controlled by the average density of congruence zeros.

The finite-monodromy counterexample is exactly the obstruction: for \(\binom{2n}{n}\), the mod-p zero condition is explained by a fixed Kummer character, giving positive-density zeros.

So the real dichotomy is:

- finite monodromy \(\Rightarrow\) algebraic congruence bias can persist;
- large nontrivial monodromy \(\Rightarrow\) expected cancellation unless special Hasse phenomena intervene.

---

## (B) Beyond sporadic Apéry-like sequences

The same heuristic should apply to periods of higher motives, modular-form coefficient sequences, and non-sporadic Picard--Fuchs equations only after adding hypotheses.

Infinite monodromy alone is insufficient. A sequence may have infinite geometric monodromy but still possess:

1. a special congruence family;
2. a hidden CM or endoscopic factor;
3. a factorization producing many automatic zeros;
4. a non-generic Hasse invariant.

The expected generic condition is closer to:

- geometric monodromy is large;
- the relevant representation is absolutely irreducible;
- the zero condition corresponds to a codimension-one condition in a varying Frobenius space;
- no fixed algebraic subvariety contains the Frobenius orbit.

Under those assumptions, Katz-style philosophy predicts probability about \(1/p\) for a zero.

---

## (C) Where the proposed universal proof breaks

The chain

\[
\text{infinite monodromy}
\Rightarrow
\text{Frobenius equidistribution}
\Rightarrow
\Pr(b_r=0)=1/p
\]

breaks at the middle implication.

Katz equidistribution concerns Frobenius conjugacy classes in a fixed \(\ell\)-adic family. The Apéry zero event is different:

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The residue characteristic, the parameter, and the coefficient index all move together.

The missing object is a bounded-conductor sheaf in the coefficient variable \(r\) whose trace realizes the zero predicate. The repository's Q467-type obstruction is precisely that the obvious Mellin/coefficient constructions have complexity growing with \(p\).

Therefore monodromy alone does not bypass the sheaf construction. Monodromy controls the distribution of existing Frobenius objects; it does not automatically create the required horizontal family.

---

## (D) Why Sp4 and SL2 constants differ

The observed constants

\[
\lambda_{Sp_4}\approx1.0,\qquad \lambda_{SL_2}\approx1.4-1.5
\]

are not determined by the density of matrices near the identity.

The zero event is not simply

\[
\operatorname{tr}(g)=0.
\]

It is a Hasse-coordinate vanishing condition after reduction modulo p.

The relevant quantity is a local singularity probability in the chosen representation, not the Haar density of a trace hyperplane.

For a representation \(V\), the heuristic is:

\[
\lambda_V\approx \text{expected number of character parameters where the Hasse coordinate vanishes}.
\]

Thus rank, self-duality, and symmetry matter. The Sp4 Apéry case has extra reflection structure, effectively pairing zeros and reducing independent choices. The SL2 examples lack that exact palindromic constraint.

Hence the ratio \(1.4/1.0\) is an arithmetic representation effect, not a simple ratio of Haar densities.

---

## (E) Can palindromy help HM3?

Yes, but only as a constant-factor improvement.

For Apéry:

\[
Z_p\mapsto p-1-Z_p
\]

is an involution, so off-center zeros occur in pairs.

In CRT moment calculations this changes

\[
|Z_p|^k
\]

into approximately

\[
(2m_p)^k
\]

where \(m_p\) counts independent lower-half zeros.

This is useful because the factorial moments are naturally moments of the lower-half point process.

However, palindromy does not prove HM3. The hard term is not the number of possible zeros but the correlation of zero locations across distinct primes:

\[
\Pr(r\in Z_p,\ s\in Z_q).
\]

Pairing removes duplicate constraints inside one prime, but it does not create independence between primes.

The required theorem remains a two-characteristic dispersion statement.

---

## Final conclusion

The strongest supported universality conjecture is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Generic infinite-monodromy Picard--Fuchs sequences have bounded average Frobenius zero density.}
}
\]

More concretely:

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{\pi(X)}\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|=O(1)
}
\]

should hold for generic large-monodromy motives.

But the proof cannot use monodromy alone. The missing step is a coefficient-variable, same-characteristic Frobenius equidistribution theorem. The Apéry problem is a model case because it appears generic enough to satisfy the conjecture while being structured enough that this missing horizontal theorem can be attacked via gap polynomials, Mellin transforms, and factorial moments.
