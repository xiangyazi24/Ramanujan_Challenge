# Terminal audit: the listed constraints are insufficient

The answer is negative: the stated deterministic constraints do not prove

$$B_p^\circ(K)=O(p^{1+o(1)})$$

for $K\asymp\sqrt p/\sqrt{\log p}$.

The reason is that all listed hypotheses are either local (fixed-gap Apéry caps), involutive (reflection), or quadratic-average (fiber-square energy). None gives long-range decorrelation inside an individual fiber.

## What the hypotheses imply

The raw four-window count satisfies $M_p\le p$. Reflection removal is valid:

$$0\le B_p-B_p^\circ\le 4M_p,$$

because the only removed points are the four automatic reflected returns (with the central point counted once when the orbit meets the center).

However, the fixed-gap estimate

$$\#\{x:x,x+h\in F_q\}\le 3(h-1)$$

only controls one prescribed short displacement. It gives no bound for the number of points at distance $K$ or larger. The energy estimate

$$\sum_q |F_q|^2\le p^{3/2+o(1)}$$

allows exceptional fibers of size $p^{1/2+o(1)}$.

## Countermodel template

Let $H=c\sqrt p$ with small fixed $c$. Put one reflection-symmetric fiber at

$$A\cup(p-1-A),\qquad A=\{0,2,4,\ldots,2(H-1)\},$$

and give every other index a singleton label.

Checks:

* Reflection symmetry holds by construction.
* There are no adjacent repeats.
* For a fixed gap $h$, the arithmetic progression contribution is $O(H)$; choosing the constant $c$ sufficiently small makes every gap obey the cap $3(h-1)$.
* The exceptional fiber contributes $O(H^2)=O(p)$ to square energy, well below the allowed $p^{3/2+o(1)}$.
* Raw chain mass is $O(H)\le p$.

For every four-consecutive chain in the block, after deleting

$$\{x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,p-1-x_3,p-1-x_2,p-1-x_1,p-1-x_0\},$$

there remain many later points in the same fiber. Thus the reflection support does not remove the dense external tail.

The construction gives

$$B_p^\circ(K)\asymp H(H-K),$$

and with $H=c\sqrt p$ and $K=\sqrt p/\sqrt{\log p}$ this is of order $p$; variants with multiple sparse admissible blocks show that the listed axioms alone do not force a stronger sublinear mechanism.

## Central/shared-support cases

If the reflection center is present, it belongs to the support set only once. It changes the removed count by at most one and does not affect the lower bound. Singleton labels outside the constructed fiber create no hidden chains.

## Missing genuinely Apéry-specific input

A successful proof needs a new theorem tying fibers to the actual Apéry recurrence. Examples:

1. A long-interval fiber-density estimate:

$$|F_q\cap I|\le |I|^{1/2+o(1)}.$$

2. A higher-order correlation estimate controlling separated quadruples/triples in one fiber.

3. A projective-orbit rigidity theorem proving that occurrence sets cannot contain artificial dense blocks.

Such a condition must be stronger than restating $B_p^\circ$ itself: it must forbid the mechanism producing far external returns.

## Verdict

Reflection quotienting is correct, and $B_p^\circ$ is the right quantity for canonical separated pairs. But the current deterministic Apéry constraints do not imply the desired bound. The missing ingredient is a long-range orbit-specific decorrelation statement.
