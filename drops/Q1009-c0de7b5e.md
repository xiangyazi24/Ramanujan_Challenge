## 1. Nörlund--Rice representation

Let
\[
f(z)=1/A(z).
\]
For the forward difference identity
\[
\sum_{s=0}^{H}(-1)^s\binom Hs f(s)
=(-1)^H\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_C
f(z)\frac{H!}{z(z-1)\cdots(z-H)}\,dz,
\]
where C is a positively oriented contour enclosing the integers 0,...,H.

Using Gamma functions,
\[
\frac{H!}{z(z-1)\cdots(z-H)}
=(-1)^H\frac{\Gamma(H+1)\Gamma(-z)}{\Gamma(H+1-z)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
U_H=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_C
\frac{\Gamma(H+1)\Gamma(-z)}{\Gamma(H+1-z)}\frac{dz}{A(z)}.
\]
The sign convention follows from the residue of Gamma at z=s:
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=s}\Gamma(-z)=\frac{(-1)^{s+1}}{s!},
\]
and gives the required coefficient \((-1)^s\binom Hs\).

## 2. Contribution of a simple zero

Assume:

1. rho is a simple zero of A;
2. all other zeros satisfy Re(z)<=Re(rho)-epsilon;
3. the shifted contour integrals are controlled by a vertical growth estimate.

Near rho,
\[
\frac1{A(z)}=\frac1{A'(rho)(z-rho)}+O(1).
\]
The residue contribution is
\[
R_H(rho)=
\frac{\Gamma(H+1)\Gamma(-rho)}{\Gamma(H+1-rho)A'(rho)}.
\]
By the Gamma quotient expansion,
\[
\frac{\Gamma(H+1)}{\Gamma(H+1-rho)}
=H^{rho}(1+O(H^{-1})).
\]
Hence
\[
R_H(rho)=C_\rho H^{rho}(1+O(H^{-1})),
\]
with
\[
C_\rho=\frac{\Gamma(-rho)}{A'(rho)}.
\]
The conjugate zero contributes
\[
R_H(\bar rho)=\overline{C_\rho}H^{\bar rho}(1+O(H^{-1})).
\]
Together,
\[
R_H(rho)+R_H(\bar rho)
=2|C_\rho|H^{\sigma}
\cos(\tau\log H+\arg C_\rho)+O(H^{\sigma-1}),
\]
where rho=sigma+i tau.

For the reported numerical zero,
\[
sigma=0.14598677311801086651,
\quad
\tau=0.58242614024485315072.
\]

## 3. Why continuous oscillation is not enough

The expansion above only gives oscillation in the variable
\[
\tau\log H+\phi.
\]
To deduce infinitely many integer sign changes one needs to prove that infinitely many integers H satisfy
\[
|\cos(\tau\log H+\phi)|>c
\]
on both signs.

A sufficient lemma would be logarithmic equidistribution:

For every interval I modulo 2pi with positive length,
\[
\#\{H\le X: \tau\log H+\phi\pmod {2\pi}\in I\}
\sim \int_I \frac{e^t}{X}dt
\]
(or an equivalent discrepancy estimate).

Without such a sampling lemma, continuous sign changes can in principle miss the integer sequence.

## 4. Zero-free alternative

An eventual-sign theorem would require a zero-free region strong enough that the shifted Nörlund--Rice contour can be moved left of the dominant algebraic scale.

A sufficient hypothesis is:

There exists delta>0 such that
\[
A(z)\ne0
\quad\text{for}\quad
\Re z> -\delta,
\]
except for known removable interpolation points, and
\[
|1/A(\sigma+it)|\le C(1+|t|)^M
\]
uniformly in this strip.

Then the contour shift gives a remainder smaller than the main term needed for positivity.

The interpolation symmetry and recurrence are structural facts; the required zero-free strip and vertical reciprocal bounds are additional hypotheses.

## 5. Sparse sign failures and signed Padé minors

If oscillation is real, the codimension-one argument based only on
\[
U_{H-1}U_H>0
\]
cannot be the complete route.

The possible repair is a higher-codimension construction: when a sign failure occurs, replace the degree-one signed-Padé condition by degree-two or degree-three minors. This requires proving:

1. every sufficiently large exceptional H has a bounded codimension witness;
2. the determinant minors do not vanish identically;
3. the primitive normalization retains the needed denominator divisibility.

The current finite computations do not establish this asymptotically.
