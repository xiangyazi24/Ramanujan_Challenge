# Q1930: block-continuant determinant model

## Correct determinant realization

The scalar
\[
Y_m=P_m-\sum_{t=2}^m w_tP_m/X_t
\]
is a product of leading continuants, not the derivative of one tridiagonal determinant. Let \(D_t\) be the \(t\times t\) leading continuant matrix with
\[
\det D_t=X_t.
\]
Define the block diagonal matrix
\[
B=\operatorname{diag}(D_2,D_3,\ldots,D_m).
\]
Then
\[
\det B=\prod_{t=2}^m X_t=P_m.
\]

## Perturbation producing the correction

For each block choose a rank-one perturbation
\[
D_t(\varepsilon)=D_t+\varepsilon w_t u_tv_t^T
\]
with
\[
v_t^T\operatorname{adj}(D_t)u_t=1.
\]
Such vectors exist whenever \(X_t\ne0\). Put
\[
B(\varepsilon)=\operatorname{diag}(D_2(\varepsilon),\ldots,D_m(\varepsilon)).
\]
Jacobi's formula gives
\[
\frac d{d\varepsilon}\det B(0)=\det B(0)\operatorname{tr}(B^{-1}B')
\]
so
\[
\frac d{d\varepsilon}\det B(0)=P_m\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{w_t}{X_t}.
\]
Therefore
\[
Y_m=P_m-\frac d{d\varepsilon}\det B(0).
\]
This is the valid block-continuant analogue.

## Endpoint bordering

The endpoint equation is
\[
e_m=(2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m.
\]
Introduce the bordered matrix
\[
\mathcal B=
\begin{pmatrix}
B&u\\
v^T&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]
where the border couples the last continuant block to the vector representing
\((2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m\). The Schur complement identity is
\[
\det\mathcal B=-\det(B)\,v^TB^{-1}u.
\]
Choosing the border gives
\[
v^TB^{-1}u=e_m/X_m
\]
(up to a fixed unit), hence at \(e_m=0\),
\[
\det\mathcal B=0.
\]

## What Jacobi leaves

Differentiating the bordered identity gives
\[
0=\frac d{d\varepsilon}\det\mathcal B
=-\det(B)\frac d{d\varepsilon}(v^TB^{-1}u)
\]
at the endpoint. The remaining term is the bordered cofactor
\[
\Delta_m=v^T\operatorname{adj}(B)u.
\]
Thus Jacobi does not directly imply \(Y_m\ne0\); it reduces the question to showing that this specific bordered minor cannot vanish modulo \(p\).

## Conclusion

The correct determinant model is block-continuant. The false single-tridiagonal derivative formula is avoided. The endpoint condition annihilates a Schur-complement factor but leaves one precise bordered minor obstruction. A final proof of \(Y_m\not\equiv0\pmod p\) requires an arithmetic evaluation of this remaining minor, not only Jacobi/Desnanot condensation.
