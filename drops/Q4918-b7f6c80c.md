\[
\left\|
\sum_{p,q,d}

\le
C_{BC}\|A\|_{\ell^2_p(H)}\|\beta\|_2\|\nu\|_2.
\tag{2.1}

This is just the scalar operator tensored with \(I_H\).

has the same norm, and (2.2) is its Hilbert pairing.

# 3. A Hilbert--Schmidt operator coefficient also extends for free

There is a slightly more general automatic consequence which is useful for auditing Schatten proposals.

Let

\[
A_p,B_q\in H,
\qquad
C_d\in S_2(H).
\]

Then scalar Bettin--Chandee implies

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{d,p,q}K_{d,p,q}\langle A_p,C_dB_q\rangle
\right|
\ll
C_{BC}
\|A\|_{\ell^2(H)}
\|B\|_{\ell^2(H)}
\left(\sum_d\|C_d\|_{S_2}^2\right)^{1/2}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

To see this, choose an orthonormal basis \(e_i\) of \(H\), write

\[
A_{p,i}=\langle A_p,e_i\rangle,
\quad
B_{q,j}=\langle B_q,e_j\rangle,
\quad
c_{d,ij}=\langle e_i,C_de_j\rangle,
\]

apply scalar Bettin--Chandee to every pair \((i,j)\), and then use Cauchy in \((i,j)\):

\[
\sum_{i,j}
\|A_{\cdot,i}\|_2
\|B_{\cdot,j}\|_2
\|c_{\cdot,ij}\|_2
\le
\|A\|_{\ell^2(H)}
\|B\|_{\ell^2(H)}
\left(\sum_d\|C_d\|_{S_2}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

So an \(S_2\)-valued extension is not speculative; it is an immediate scalar corollary.

What does **not** follow is the stronger estimate with

\[
\left(\sum_d\|C_d\|_{S_\infty}^2\right)^{1/2}
\]

for arbitrary, noncommuting \(C_d\).  That would be a complete-boundedness statement about the trilinear multiplier, and the scalar theorem does not imply it.

This distinction is visible in the actual Bettin--Chandee proof.  Their first Cauchy step squares the scalar combination \(\beta_n\nu_a\); later the off-diagonal estimates contain scalar products such as

\[
|\nu_{a_1}\nu_{a_1'}|
\]

and collapse them to \(\|\nu\|_2^2\).  Replacing \(\nu_a\) by arbitrary operators creates \(C_{a_1}^*C_{a_1'}\), for which there is no scalar absolute-value reduction in operator norm.  Hilbert--Schmidt expansion works because it reduces to scalar matrix entries; \(S_\infty\) does not.

Thus DFI is not blocked by Hilbertization at fixed \(a\); it is blocked by the absence of a theorem synthesizing the moving numerators \(dg\).

# 11. Why an arbitrary \(S_\infty\)-valued Bettin--Chandee theorem is not free

It is tempting to postulate

In the later off-diagonal estimates, scalar absolute values reduce terms containing

The \(S_2\) extension in Section 3 works because matrix entries reduce back to scalar Bettin--Chandee.  The commuting extension in Section 4 works because simultaneous diagonalization reduces back to scalar Bettin--Chandee.  An arbitrary \(S_\infty\) extension has neither mechanism.

# 18. Proof-level audit of Bettin--Chandee itself

The external paper confirms the structural diagnosis.

Theorem 1 bounds

\[
\sum_{a,m,n}
\alpha_m\beta_n\nu_a
 e\!\left(\vartheta a\bar m/n\right)
\]

with three scalar one-variable coefficient sequences.

In the proof outline, their first Cauchy step is applied in the \(m\)-variable and yields a square of

\[
\sum_{a,n}\beta_n\nu_a e(\cdots).
\]

Squaring produces the scalar products

\[
\beta_{n_1}\nu_{a_1}
\overline{\beta_{n_2}\nu_{a_2}}.
\]

The later off-diagonal treatment applies reciprocity and Weil bounds after another Cauchy step, and the \(a\)-coefficients are ultimately reduced through scalar absolute values to \(\|\nu\|_2^2\).

Consequences:

- replacing one scalar coefficient sequence by an \(H\)-valued one is harmless by tensorization;
- replacing the \(p,q\) scalar product by a common Hilbert inner product is harmless by \(T\otimes I_H\);
- replacing the \(a\)-coefficients by Hilbert--Schmidt operators is harmless after matrix-entry expansion;
- replacing them by commuting normal operators is harmless after simultaneous diagonalization;
- replacing the scalar kernel by a \(p,q,a\)-dependent arbitrary operator is **not** covered.

The actual Apéry tensor is in the last class until \(g\) is frozen or \(h=dg\) is introduced.  Free Hilbertization therefore cannot bypass the difference-variable synthesis.
