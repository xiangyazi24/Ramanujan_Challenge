# Apéry three-AP return certificate: midpoint subresultants and the marked obstruction

## Verdict

There is a useful exact reduction, but I do **not** get the requested subquadratic aggregate unconditionally.

The strongest result I can prove from the current transfer algebra is this:

1. the equal-gap pair `N_h(a)=N_h(a+h)=0` has an exact **midpoint parity decomposition** into two integer polynomials of roughly half the degree;
2. for every target prime `p>4(a+2h)`, the two return conditions force a common zero of these two half-degree polynomials at the single scalar
   \[
   T=(2a+2h+1)^2;
   \]
3. this gives a genuine short per-cell Bézout certificate of logarithmic height
   \[
   O\bigl(h\log(a+h+2)\bigr),
   \]
   with only `p`-unit denominators;
4. eliminating `T` gives a one-parameter, `a`-independent **primitive constant ideal** `c_h`; every target prime divides `c_h`, and `c_h` is strictly sharper than the ordinary equal-gap resultant because it removes degree-drop primes automatically;
5. at `h=1,2,3` the primitive Euclidean calculation is completely explicit.  In particular, **`h=1` and `h=2` are impossible for every prime in the requested high-prime range**.  At `h=3` every possible high prime divides one explicit fixed integer;
6. the ordinary Sylvester resultant, and even the canonical midpoint half-resultant, necessarily have quadratic logarithmic height `Omega(h^2 log h)`.  Therefore merely repackaging the unmarked resultant cannot give the desired short carrier;
7. the mark `b_a=0` cannot enter a uniformly short **linear** Bézout identity with a nonzero coefficient in the long-base/short-gap regime: an elementary height inequality forces coefficient height `Omega(a)` when `a >> h log(a+h)`.  This formalizes why the companion/Casoratian mark does not automatically compress the equal-gap resultant.

The remaining exact one-parameter target is therefore the high-prime radical of the primitive midpoint Bézout constant `c_h`.  A bound
\[
\sum_{h\le T}\log\operatorname{rad}(c_h)\ll T^{2-\eta}
\]
for some `eta>0` would solve the requested aggregate.  I do not prove that bound here.

# 2. Midpoint parity decomposition

Put
\[
d=3(h-1),\qquad
\epsilon=(-1)^{h-1},\qquad
c=2h+1,
\tag{2.1}
\]
and define
\[
\boxed{
F_h(Y):=2^dN_h\!\left(\frac{Y-c}{2}\right)\in\mathbb Z[Y].
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
The integrality is immediate term-by-term: multiplying a degree-`d` integer polynomial after the affine substitution by `2^d` clears every power of two.

Write the even/odd decomposition uniquely as
\[
\boxed{
F_h(Y)=E_h(Y^2)+Y O_h(Y^2),
\qquad E_h,O_h\in\mathbb Z[T].
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
For an integer `a>=0`, set
\[
Y=2a+2h+1,
\qquad T=Y^2.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{Y-c}{2}=a,
\qquad
\frac{-Y-c}{2}=-a-2h-1.
\]
Using (1.5) at `X=a+h`,
\[
F_h(-Y)=\epsilon\,2^dN_h(a+h).
\tag{2.5}
\]
Taking even and odd parts gives the exact pair
\[
\boxed{
E_h(T)=2^{d-1}igl(N_h(a)+\epsilon N_h(a+h)\bigr),
}
\tag{2.6}
\]
\[
\boxed{
Y O_h(T)=2^{d-1}igl(N_h(a)-\epsilon N_h(a+h)\bigr).
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

This is the first useful compression.  It is not an arbitrary resultant identity: it is forced by the exact Apéry continuant reflection.

If
\[
p>4(a+2h),
\tag{2.8}
\]
then
\[
0<Y=2a+2h+1<p,
\qquad p>8h,
\tag{2.9}
\]
so both `2` and `Y` are `p`-units.  Consequently
\[
\boxed{
N_h(a)\equiv N_h(a+h)\equiv0\pmod p
\Longrightarrow
E_h(T)\equiv O_h(T)\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{2.10}
\]

Thus every high-prime three-term Apéry zero progression is represented by a common root of **two half-degree polynomials in one scalar `T`**.

# 3. An actual short per-cell Bézout certificate

For the integer `T=Y^2`, put
\[
g_{a,h}=\gcd\bigl(E_h(T),O_h(T)\bigr)>0.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The two integers cannot both vanish in characteristic zero: otherwise (2.6)--(2.7) would give `N_h(a)=N_h(a+h)=0`, impossible for `a>=0` by the positive-definite tridiagonal realization of `N_h(a)`.

Choose ordinary integer extended-gcd coefficients `r_{a,h},s_{a,h}` with
\[
r_{a,h}E_h(T)+s_{a,h}O_h(T)=g_{a,h}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Substituting (2.6)--(2.7) gives the exact requested type of identity:
\[
\boxed{
\left(r_{a,h}+\frac{s_{a,h}}Y\right)N_h(a)
+\epsilon\left(r_{a,h}-\frac{s_{a,h}}Y\right)N_h(a+h)
=\frac{g_{a,h}}{2^{d-1}}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
So in the notation of the question one may take
\[
U_{a,h}=0,
\tag{3.4}
\]
with the two displayed rational coefficients as `V,W` and
\[
H_{a,h}=g_{a,h}/2^{d-1}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Every denominator in (3.3) is a `p`-unit under (2.8).

This certificate really has short **archimedean height**.  The determinant recurrence gives
\[
\log H(N_h)=O(h\log(h+2)),
\tag{3.6}
\]
and evaluating a degree-`O(h)` polynomial at `Y=O(a+h)` gives
\[
\log|E_h(T)|+\log|O_h(T)|
=O\bigl(h\log(a+h+2)\bigr).
\tag{3.7}
\]
Extended-gcd coefficients may be chosen with
\[
|r_{a,h}|\le |O_h(T)|/g_{a,h},
\qquad
|s_{a,h}|\le |E_h(T)|/g_{a,h}.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Hence every coefficient and the right side of (3.3) has logarithmic height
\[
\boxed{O\bigl(h\log(a+h+2)\bigr).}
\tag{3.9}
\]

And an active target prime satisfies
\[
\boxed{p\mid g_{a,h}.}
\tag{3.10}
\]

So a uniform short **per-cell** certificate exists.  The problem is aggregation: summing the trivial height (3.9) over `a,h<=T` is still `O(T^3 log T)`, not subquadratic.

# 4. Removing `a`: the primitive midpoint Bézout ideal

The useful global object is not the ordinary resultant.  Since `E_h,O_h` are coprime over `Q[T]`, consider the constant ideal after inverting only primes that can never be target primes:
\[
\mathcal R_h=\mathbb Z\left[\frac1{(8h)!}\right],
\tag{4.1}
\]
\[
\mathfrak c_h
:=\bigl(E_h,O_h\bigr)\mathcal R_h[T]\cap\mathcal R_h.
\tag{4.2}
\]
This is a nonzero principal ideal.  Choose its positive generator `c_h`, normalized to have no prime factor `<=8h`.  Equivalently, there are
\[
A_h(T),B_h(T)\in\mathcal R_h[T]
\tag{4.3}
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
A_h(T)E_h(T)+B_h(T)O_h(T)=c_h.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

For every prime `p>8h`, all denominators in `R_h` are `p`-units, and therefore
\[
E_h(T)\equiv O_h(T)\equiv0\pmod p
\Longrightarrow
\boxed{p\mid c_h.}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Consequently the original marked three-zero event satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
 p>4(a+2h),\\
 p\mid b_a,\quad p\mid N_h(a),\quad p\mid N_h(a+h)
\end{array}
\Longrightarrow p\mid c_h.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the clean uniform `a`-elimination.  It is strictly sharper than saying `p|Res(E_h,O_h)`: the contracted Bézout ideal removes characteristic degree-drop factors that do not correspond to an affine common root.

The ordinary Sylvester estimate only gives
\[
\log c_h\le O(h^2\log(h+2)),
\tag{4.7}
\]
so (4.6) alone still does not prove a subquadratic aggregate.

# 8. Exact relation to the ordinary equal-gap resultant

The midpoint construction also explains why the ordinary resultant has the wrong height scale.

Let
\[
\lambda_h=\operatorname{lc}(N_h),
\qquad d=3(h-1),
\]
and let
\[
R_h^{\rm mid}:=\operatorname{Res}_T(E_h,O_h).
\tag{8.1}
\]
The reflection shows the roots of `N_h` have average
\[
-\frac{h+1}{2}.
\tag{8.2}
\]
It follows that the coefficient of `Y^(d-1)` in `F_h` is `-\lambda_h d h`, so the expected even/odd degrees are full.  A root-product calculation then gives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_Y(F_h(Y),F_h(-Y))
=2^dF_h(0)\lambda_h\bigl(R_h^{\rm mid}\bigr)^2.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
On the other hand the affine change `Y=2X+2h+1` gives
\[
\left|\operatorname{Res}_Y(F_h(Y),F_h(-Y))\right|
=2^{d^2}|S_{h,h}|.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
2^{d^2}|S_{h,h}|
=2^d|F_h(0)\lambda_h|\,|R_h^{\rm mid}|^2.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]
So the parity reduction is literally a square-root factorization of the raw progression resultant, up to an explicit center factor and powers of two.

It halves the elimination dimension, but that alone cannot change a quadratic-height problem into a linear-height one.

# 9. Scoped height obstruction for the raw transfer eliminant

The root strip gives an explicit lower bound, not merely an upper bound.

Let `alpha_1,...,alpha_d` be the roots of `N_h`, and let
\[
\gamma_j=\alpha_j-h
\]
be the roots of `N_h(X+h)`.  Every real separation satisfies
\[
1<\Re(\alpha_i-\gamma_j)<2h.
\tag{9.1}
\]
For fixed `i`, using the average (8.2),
\[
\frac1d\sum_j\Re(\alpha_i-\gamma_j)
=\Re\alpha_i+\frac{3h+1}{2}
>\frac{h+1}{2}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
If fewer than `d/7` of these `d` numbers were at least `h/4`, their average would be at most
\[
\frac h4+\frac74h\cdot\frac17=\frac h2,
\]
contradicting (9.2).  Thus for every `i`, at least `d/7` root differences have modulus at least `h/4`, while all the others have modulus `>1`.

Therefore, for `h>4`,
\[
\prod_{i,j}|\alpha_i-\gamma_j|
>\left(\frac h4\right)^{d^2/7}.
\tag{9.3}
\]
Since `lambda_h` is a positive integer,
\[
\boxed{
\log|S_{h,h}|
\ge \frac{d^2}{7}\log\frac h4
=\Omega(h^2\log h).
}
\tag{9.4}
\]
Moreover `F_h(0)` is a tridiagonal determinant evaluated at `X=-h-1/2`, so
\[
\log|F_h(0)\lambda_h|=O(h\log h).
\tag{9.5}
\]
Combining (8.5), (9.4), and (9.5) gives
\[
\boxed{
\log|R_h^{\rm mid}|=\Omega(h^2\log h).
}
\tag{9.6}
\]
Thus the **full Sylvester resultant and its canonical midpoint half-resultant are provably too tall** for the hoped-for `O(h polylog h)` constant certificate.  A successful approach must use a *primitive* subresultant/Smith invariant such as `c_h`, allowing structural factors and degree-drop factors to cancel.

This is a scoped obstruction: it does not say the primitive constant ideal `c_h` has quadratic high-prime radical.

# 10. A second obstruction: the mark `b_a` cannot help linearly at short height

The question specifically asks whether the distinguished condition `b_a=0` can shorten the transfer certificate.  In the natural **linear** Bézout ansatz, there is a direct height obstruction.

For a rational number `x=m/n` in lowest terms write
\[
h(x)=\log\max(1,|m|,|n|).
\]
Suppose
\[
U b_a+V N_h(a)+W N_h(a+h)=H
\tag{10.1}
\]
is an exact rational equality with `U!=0` and
\[
h(U),h(V),h(W),h(H)\le L.
\tag{10.2}
\]
Then
\[
|U|\ge e^{-L},
\qquad |V|,|W|,|H|\le e^L,
\]
so
\[
e^{-L}b_a
\le e^L\bigl(1+|N_h(a)|+|N_h(a+h)|\bigr).
\tag{10.3}
\]

The `k=a` summand of the Apéry number gives
\[
b_a\ge\binom{2a}{a}^2
\ge\frac{16^a}{(2a+1)^2}.
\tag{10.4}
\]
For nonnegative arguments, the tridiagonal determinant and
\[
P(n)\le34(n+1)^3
\]
give the safe bound
\[
|N_h(a)|,|N_h(a+h)|
\le\bigl(36(a+2h+1)^3\bigr)^{h-1}.
\tag{10.5}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
L\ge
2a\log2-\log(2a+1)
-\frac{h-1}{2}
 \bigl(\log36+3\log(a+2h+1)\bigr)
-O(1).
}
\tag{10.6}
\]

In particular, whenever
\[
h\log(a+h)=o(a),
\tag{10.7}
\]
a linear identity of uniformly `O(h polylog(a+h))` height cannot have `U!=0`.

So in the long-base/short-gap regime the mark cannot enter a short **linear** Bézout identity.  Any such uniformly short identity must asymptotically have `U=0`, exactly as the midpoint certificate (3.3) does.

This matches the basis-covariant companion audit already recorded in `AVENUES.md`: at a distinguished zero the companion fixes the projective fiber, but subsequent return comparisons yield only the transfer cocycle.  The Green identity (1.9) does not provide an additional independent low-height scalar relation.

The scope matters.  Equation (10.6) does **not** rule out a nonlinear marked identity, a genuinely global Cartier/Hasse input, or a construction in which `h` is comparable to `a`.

# 11. Constant-Bézout lower bound in the natural integral transfer algebra

There is also a clean lower bound on any integral constant relation between the two equal-gap transfer polynomials.

Suppose
\[
A(X)N_h(X)+B(X)N_h(X+h)=C,
\qquad A,B\in\mathbb Z[X],\quad C\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.
\tag{11.1}
\]
Reduce `B` modulo `N_h`, so `deg B<d`.  At every root `alpha_i` of `N_h`,
\[
B(\alpha_i)N_h(\alpha_i+h)=C.
\]
Multiplying over all roots and using resultants gives
\[
|C|^d
=\frac{|\operatorname{Res}(N_h,B)|\,|S_{h,h}|}
       {\lambda_h^{\deg B+d}}.
\tag{11.2}
\]
The first resultant is a nonzero integer, and the root-product form of `S_{h,h}` shows that the remaining leading-coefficient power is nonnegative.  Using (9.3),
\[
\boxed{
\log|C|
\ge\frac d7\log\frac h4
=\Omega(h\log h).
}
\tag{11.3}
\]
for `h>4`.

For a rational Bézout identity, clearing a common denominator `D` yields the same statement for the cleared constant:
\[
\boxed{
\log|D C|\ge\frac d7\log\frac h4.
}
\tag{11.4}
\]
Thus a short rational identity may move the unavoidable factorial-scale height into `p`-unit denominators, but it cannot make that archimedean height disappear.

This is the precise scoped obstruction I can prove.  It is compatible with an `O(h polylog h)` primitive certificate; it only rules out substantially smaller constant height.

# 12. Exact aggregate reduction and the remaining theorem

Let `M_T^{AP}` denote the radical of the primes in the question: primes `p` for which some `a,h<=T` satisfy
\[
p>4(a+2h),
\qquad
p\mid b_a,\ N_h(a),\ N_h(a+h).
\tag{12.1}
\]
By (4.6),
\[
\boxed{
\log M_T^{AP}
\le
\sum_{h\le T}\log\operatorname{rad}(c_h).
}
\tag{12.2}
\]
Here `c_h` is already normalized to contain only primes `>8h`.

Therefore the following one-parameter statement is sufficient:

> **Primitive midpoint radical bound.** There exists `eta>0` such that
> \[
> \boxed{
> \sum_{h\le T}\log\operatorname{rad}(c_h)
> \ll T^{2-\eta}.
> }
> \tag{PMR_eta}
> \]

This is substantially narrower than the original two-parameter marked problem:

- `a` has disappeared;
- the degree is about `3h/2`, not `3h` against another `3h` polynomial;
- small structural primes `<=8h` are inverted from the outset;
- degree-drop primes are removed by the contraction ideal, as the `h=2` calculation demonstrates;
- the distinguished mark `b_a=0` is no longer needed for the **upper** support bound, so any later use of it can only reduce the carrier further.

The current repository does not prove `(PMR_eta)`.  The standard height bound gives only
\[
\sum_{h\le T}O(h^2\log h)=O(T^3\log T),
\tag{12.3}
\]
and (9.6) shows that replacing `c_h` by the full midpoint resultant cannot improve this.

A pointwise statement stronger than needed would be
\[
\log\operatorname{rad}(c_h)\ll h^{1-\eta};
\tag{12.4}
\]
an average saving in `(PMR_eta)` is enough.

---

# Bottom line

The exact Apéry-specific answer is not “take the resultant.”  The equal-gap reflection produces a midpoint parity algebra
\[
F_h(Y)=E_h(Y^2)+YO_h(Y^2),
\]
and the high-prime condition makes `Y=2a+2h+1` invertible.  This yields:

```text
three zero AP
   => N_h(a)=N_h(a+h)=0
   => E_h(Y^2)=O_h(Y^2)=0
   => p divides the primitive constant ideal c_h.
```

For `h=1` the event is impossible.  For `h=2` the primitive Euclidean certificate is `550`, so the event is impossible for every `p>4(a+4)`.  For `h=3` every possible high prime divides the explicit constant `34929539026441`.

The mark `b_a=0` does not admit a uniformly short **linear** insertion when `a` is much larger than `h`: any nonzero coefficient of `b_a` must carry `Omega(a)` logarithmic height.  Thus the natural linear transfer algebra really does reduce to the unmarked midpoint problem in that regime.

The smallest non-tautological statement I would attack next is `(PMR_eta)`: a power-saving bound for the high-prime radical of the primitive midpoint Bézout constants `c_h`.  It is the exact place where subresultant/Smith theory could still beat the quadratic-height Sylvester determinant without reintroducing the exponential Apéry height in `a`.
