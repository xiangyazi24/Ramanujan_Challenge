# Signed fixed-q Padé height attack: audit result

## Verdict

The proposed two-sided reduction

\[
G^+=\gcd_a P_a(n),\qquad G^- =\gcd_a P_a(-n-1)
\]

being radical-exact on the quotient cell does **not** by itself give a sublinear-height carrier. I do not find a valid integer characteristic-zero identity producing

\[
\log \operatorname{rad}_{cell}(G^+G^-)=o_q(n).
\]

The signed reflection gives a useful symmetry, but the attempted cancellation mechanism fails at the level of primitive determinantal divisors.

## Reflection test

The formal identity

```text
binom(-n-1,k)=(-1)^k binom(n+k,k)
```

only transports the negative evaluation into a shifted positive binomial basis. For a determinant/minor built from interpolation data, this produces

```text
M^- = D_shift(n) * M^+(n) * E
```

only if the shifted basis change matrix E is unimodular over Z and preserves the primitive content of every minor. The required Apéry interpolation minors contain additional recurrence coefficients. Those coefficients are not removed by the reflection identity.

Consequently the common binomial factor is not an integer carrier of the radical; it is a normalization factor before primitive reduction.

## Why the bordered-minor cancellation does not close

Let C^+ and C^- denote primitive cross-determinants from the two evaluation families. A valid cancellation would require an identity

```text
T_n^+ C^+(n) + T_n^- C^-(n) = 0
```

with

```text
log |T_n^+ T_n^-| = o_q(n)
```

and with the primitive divisor support of the cell radicals forced into T^+T^-.

The reflection calculation only gives congruence-level relations between the two ledgers. It does not give an equality in Z after taking primitive determinantal parts. Any attempt to divide out the binomial carrier changes the gcd by possible prime powers from the determinant content.

Therefore the missing step is exactly the one that cannot be assumed:

```text
primitive determinant divisor after signed reflection
= reflected primitive determinant divisor.
```

This is false without an additional Apéry-specific unimodularity theorem.

## Concrete obstruction

The first obstruction is normalization, not support.

For a minor with rows indexed by interpolation parameters a and columns indexed by recurrence terms, signed reflection changes the column basis by a triangular matrix whose diagonal entries contain shifted factorial ratios. These ratios have integer prime content growing with n. They are not units in Z. Hence the primitive minors on the two sides can share a large arithmetic carrier even after the visible binomial factor has been removed.

The desired determinant lemma would need to prove:

```text
all diagonal content introduced by reflection is absorbed by a fixed-q unit,
```

or equivalently a bounded-height Smith-normal-form statement for the signed basis-change matrix. No such statement follows from the current Padé family construction.

## Computational check status

I do not claim a numerical theorem from finite experiments. The repository-visible proof inventory currently contains related signed Padé no-go material, but the requested new files/scripts for the complete signed fixed-q family are not present under the expected paths on the checked main tree. Therefore I cannot honestly report new exact cross-determinant counts from those scripts.

A valid computational attack would need to output, for fixed q and increasing H:

```text
(H, A, gcd(P_a(n)), gcd(P_a(-n-1)),
 primitive cross-determinants,
 Smith invariant factors,
 factorization of surviving content)
```

and then identify a stable formula before attempting a proof.

## Remaining possible route

The only plausible salvage is a strictly smaller Apéry-specific theorem:

```text
signed reflection preserves the primitive lattice of the Apéry interpolation module
up to a determinant of height o_q(n).
```

This is stronger than the reflection identity and stronger than the adjacent-minor ledger. It requires an explicit integral lattice calculation. Until such a lattice statement is proved, the two-sided Padé radical reduction does not imply a sublinear height carrier.

## Conclusion

The simultaneous positive/negative evaluations improve the radical reduction, but they do not currently yield

\[
\log \operatorname{rad}_{cell}(G^+G^-)=o_q(n).
\]

The concrete obstruction is the uncontrolled primitive content of the signed-reflection determinant normalization. The next decisive computation is not another CRT/support argument; it is the exact Smith/content ledger of the first signed cross-determinants.
