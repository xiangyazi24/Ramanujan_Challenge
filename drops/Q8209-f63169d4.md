## 1. The aligned common-zero problem as a double root of `R_n`

Put

\[
A_{n,k}=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k},
\qquad
\lambda_j=j(j+1),
\]

and

\[
\phi_k(Y)=\frac{\prod_{a=0}^{k-1}(Y-\lambda_a)}{(k!)^2}.
\]

For `k<=j`,

\[
\phi_k(\lambda_j)
=\frac{\prod_{a<k}(j-a)(j+a+1)}{(k!)^2}
=\binom jk\binom{j+k}{k}=A_{j,k},
\]

whereas `phi_k(lambda_j)=0` for `k>j`.

Define the degree-`n` Racah polynomial

\[
R_n(Y)=\sum_{k=0}^n A_{n,k}\phi_k(Y),
\qquad
r_n(j)=R_n(\lambda_j).
\]

Then

\[
r_n(j)=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(n,j)}A_{n,k}A_{j,k}=r_j(n)
\]

and in particular

\[
R_n(\lambda_n)=r_n(n)=b_n.
\]

For `k<=n`,

\[
\frac{\phi_k'(\lambda_n)}{\phi_k(\lambda_n)}
=\sum_{a=0}^{k-1}\frac1{\lambda_n-\lambda_a}.
\]

Since

\[
\lambda_n-\lambda_a=(n-a)(n+a+1)
\]

and

\[
\frac{2n+1}{(n-a)(n+a+1)}
=\frac1{n-a}+\frac1{n+a+1},
\]

we obtain

\[
(2n+1)R_n'(\lambda_n)
=\sum_{k=0}^n A_{n,k}^2
  (H_{n+k}-H_{n-k})
=D_n.
\]

Hence, for `p>2n+1`, every displayed denominator is a `p`-unit and

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_n,\quad p\mid\operatorname{num}(D_n)
 \iff
 (Y-\lambda_n)^2\mid R_n(Y)\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[Y].}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The leading coefficient of `R_d` is

\[
\boxed{
 c_d=[Y^d]R_d(Y)
 =\frac{A_{d,d}}{(d!)^2}
 =\frac{(2d)!}{(d!)^4}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

For `d<=N`, this is nonzero modulo `p`.

## 2. Closed half-grid node polynomial

Let

\[
N=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
S_p(Y)=\prod_{j=0}^N(Y-\lambda_j).
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — exact node product

Over `F_p[Y]`,

\[
\boxed{
S_p(Y)=4^{-(N+1)}
\left((1+4Y)^{N+1}-(1+4Y)\right).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
S_p'(\lambda_j)=-\frac12\quad(0\le j<N),
\qquad
S_p'(\lambda_N)=-1.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For `j<N`, writing `w_j=2j+1`,

\[
\boxed{
S_p''(\lambda_j)=-\frac1{w_j^2}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Proof

Set `T=1+4Y`.  At a node,

\[
T(\lambda_j)=(2j+1)^2.
\]

As `j` runs from `0` to `N`, these are exactly the quadratic residues in
`F_p`, including zero, each once.  Their monic root polynomial is

\[
T^{N+1}-T=T(T^N-1).
\]

Also

\[
T-(2j+1)^2=4(Y-\lambda_j),
\]

which proves (2.1).

Since `4^N=2^{p-1}=1`, differentiating (2.1) gives

\[
S_p'(Y)=(N+1)T^N-1.
\]

For `j<N`, `T(\lambda_j)^N=(2j+1)^{p-1}=1` and
`N+1=1/2` in `F_p`; hence `S_p'=-1/2`.  At `j=N`, `T=0`, giving `S_p'=-1`.
A second differentiation gives

\[
S_p''(\lambda_j)=4N(N+1)T(\lambda_j)^{N-1}
=-T(\lambda_j)^{-1}=-w_j^{-2}.
\]

## 3. Uniform half-grid quadrature

Define

\[
\Lambda(Q)=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}Q(\lambda_j)+Q(\lambda_N).
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — coefficient quadrature

For every `Q in F_p[Y]` with `deg Q<=N`,

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(Q)=-[Y^N]Q.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\Lambda(Q)=0\quad(\deg Q\le N-1).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Lagrange interpolation on the distinct nodes gives

\[
Q(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^N
Q(\lambda_j)\frac{S_p(Y)}{(Y-\lambda_j)S_p'(\lambda_j)}.
\]

Taking the coefficient of `Y^N` and using (2.2),

\[
[Y^N]Q
=\sum_{j=0}^N\frac{Q(\lambda_j)}{S_p'(\lambda_j)}
=-2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}Q(\lambda_j)-Q(\lambda_N).
\]

This is (3.1).

### 3.1 The derivative carrier is exactly `D_n`

The same Lagrange formula, differentiated at `a=lambda_n`, gives, for
`n<N` and `deg Q<=N`,

\[
\boxed{
Q'(\lambda_n)
=\frac{Q(\lambda_n)}{(2n+1)^2}
+\sum_{\substack{0\le j<N\\j\ne n}}
  \frac{Q(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_n-\lambda_j}
+\frac{2Q(\lambda_N)}{(2n+1)^2}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed, the self-cardinal derivative is

\[
\frac{S_p''(\lambda_n)}{2S_p'(\lambda_n)}
=\frac1{(2n+1)^2},
\]

and the endpoint cardinal contributes

\[
\frac{S_p'(\lambda_n)}
 {(\lambda_n-\lambda_N)S_p'(\lambda_N)}
=\frac{2}{(2n+1)^2},
\]

because `lambda_n-lambda_N=(2n+1)^2/4`.

Applying (3.3) to `Q=R_n`, and using
`R_n'(lambda_n)=D_n/(2n+1)`, gives

\[
\boxed{
(2n+1)D_n
=b_n+(2n+1)^2
 \sum_{\substack{0\le j<N\\j\ne n}}
 \frac{r_n(j)}{\lambda_n-\lambda_j}
+2r_n(N).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus, on `b_n=0`, the apparent global Cauchy carrier on the right is exactly
`(2n+1)D_n`.  It is not independent.  This is the interpolation form of the
same collapse found for the Christoffel--Darboux carrier.

## 4. A new global Hermite trace — and its exact collapse at the target

Put

\[
F(Y)=F_p^{\mathrm{sp}}(Y)=\sum_{k=0}^N\phi_k(Y)^2,
\qquad
B(z)=F(z(z+1)).
\]

Then `deg B=2p-2`.  Only `phi_N^2` contributes to the leading coefficient of
`F`, so

\[
[z^{2p-2}]B=(N!)^{-4}.
\]

Wilson's theorem gives

\[
-1=(p-1)!\equiv(-1)^N(N!)^2\pmod p,
\]

hence `(N!)^4=1` and

\[
[z^{2p-2}]B=1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — full-field Hermite coefficient trace

For every polynomial `f in F_p[z]` of degree at most `2p-2`,

\[
\boxed{
[z^{2p-2}]f
=\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}\bigl(f(a)+a f'(a)\bigr).}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

For `a in F_p`, put

\[
L_a(z)=1-(z-a)^{p-1}.
\]

The polynomial `L_a` is one at `a`, zero at every other field element, and
`L_a'(a)=0`.  Therefore

\[
f(z)=\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}
\left(f(a)L_a(z)^2+f'(a)(z-a)L_a(z)^2\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Both sides have degree below `2p`; their difference has a double zero at every
field element and is therefore divisible by `(z^p-z)^2`, so the difference is
zero.

The coefficient of `z^{2p-2}` in `L_a^2` is one.  In

\[
(z-a)L_a^2=(z-a)-2(z-a)^p+(z-a)^{2p-1},
\]

the coefficient of `z^{2p-2}` is
`-(2p-1)a=a` in `F_p`.  Taking that coefficient in (4.3) proves (4.2).

### Corollary 4.2 — folded Apéry jet trace

The reflection `B(-z-1)=B(z)` gives
`B'(-z-1)=-B'(z)`.  The involution has the fixed point `N`, so `B'(N)=0`.
For `j<N`, Q8206's exact jet normalization gives

\[
B(j)=b_j,
\qquad
B'(j)=2D_j.
\]

Pairing `j` with `-j-1` in (4.2), and using (4.1), yields

\[
\boxed{
 b_N+2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \bigl(b_j+(2j+1)D_j\bigr)=1\pmod p.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a genuinely global identity involving all first jets, not a finite
scan.

However, for a fixed target `n<N`, define the target-deleted scalar

\[
\mathcal H_{n,p}
=1-b_N-2\sum_{\substack{0\le j<N\\j\ne n}}
 \bigl(b_j+(2j+1)D_j\bigr).
\]

Equation (4.4) says exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_{n,p}=2\bigl(b_n+(2n+1)D_n\bigr).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Consequently, on `b_n=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_{n,p}=2(2n+1)D_n.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Because `2(2n+1)` is a unit, the global Hermite trace is **not** an independent
positive-bond/exclusion scalar at the target.  It is another exact carrier for
`D_n`.

## 5. The first genuinely independent scalar

Assume `n>=2`, and put

\[
a=\lambda_n,
\qquad
w=2n+1.
\]

Define the unconditional Taylor quotient

\[
\boxed{
T_n(Y)=
\frac{R_n(Y)-b_n-\dfrac{D_n}{w}(Y-a)}{(Y-a)^2}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The numerator has zero value and derivative at `a`, so `T_n` is always a
polynomial of degree `n-2`; no common-zero assumption is used.  Its leading
coefficient is `c_n`.

Set

\[
m=N-n+2.
\]

Since `2<=n<N`, one has `0<=m<=N` and

\[
\deg(T_nR_m)=(n-2)+m=N.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — first complementary Racah moment

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(T_nR_m)=-c_nc_m\ne0,
\qquad m=N-n+2.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Apply (3.1) to `T_nR_m`.  Its leading coefficient is `c_nc_m`, proving
(5.2).  Both factors are nonzero because `2n<p` and `2m<p`.

For every Racah test of smaller degree, `s<=m-1`,

\[
\deg(T_nR_s)\le N-1,
\]

so

\[
\Lambda(T_nR_s)=0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus (5.2) is literally the first nonzero scalar in the complementary-degree
quadrature filtration.

### Conditional form under a common zero

If `b_n=D_n=0` in `F_p`, then

\[
T_n(Y)=\frac{R_n(Y)}{(Y-\lambda_n)^2}.
\]

Writing out (5.2) on the nodes gives the exact necessary identity

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&R_n''(\lambda_n)r_m(n)
+2\sum_{\substack{0\le j<N\\j\ne n}}
 \frac{r_n(j)r_m(j)}{(\lambda_j-\lambda_n)^2}\\
&\qquad
+\frac{r_n(N)r_m(N)}{(\lambda_N-\lambda_n)^2}
=-c_nc_m\ne0,
\qquad m=N-n+2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is compatible with a double root; it merely proves that the quotient is
not annihilated by the first complementary Racah test.

### Exact independence from `(b_n,D_n)`

Let `V_n` be the vector space of polynomials of degree at most `n`, and define
for arbitrary `P in V_n`

\[
J_0(P)=P(a),
\qquad
J_1(P)=P'(a),
\]

\[
M(P)=\Lambda\!\left(
\frac{P-P(a)-P'(a)(Y-a)}{(Y-a)^2}R_m
\right).
\]

The same degree argument gives the stronger formula

\[
\boxed{
M(P)=-c_m[Y^n]P.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

In particular, take

\[
P(Y)=(Y-a)^2Y^{n-2}.
\]

Then `J_0(P)=J_1(P)=0`, but `M(P)=-c_m\ne0`.  Therefore `M` is not in the
linear span of the two local jet functionals.  This proves exact independence.

At the same time, (5.5) proves the limitation: `M` detects **only the leading
coefficient**.  For the actual `R_n`, it returns the already known `c_n`.
It uses none of the special lower Newton coefficients and therefore cannot
exclude `(Y-a)^2|R_n`.

This is the first new scalar asked for in the prompt: it is genuinely
independent of `(b_n,D_n)`, but it is degree-tautological rather than an
exclusion theorem.

## 6. Exact interaction with the Racah/Green operator

Define the polynomial Racah operator `mathscr R` by

\[
(2z+1)(\mathscr R Q)(\lambda_z)
=(z+1)^3\bigl(Q(\lambda_{z+1})-Q(\lambda_z)\bigr)
-z^3\bigl(Q(\lambda_z)-Q(\lambda_{z-1})\bigr).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The numerator is reflection-antisymmetric and divisible by `2z+1`, so this is
a polynomial in `lambda_z`.  The Racah equation is

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr R R_d=2\lambda_dR_d.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since the `R_d` have strictly increasing degree and nonzero leading
coefficients, they form a polynomial basis.  Hence, if `Q` has degree `d`, the
leading coefficient of `mathscr R Q` is `2lambda_d` times the leading
coefficient of `Q`.

Let `d=n-2` and `m=N-n+2=N-d`.  Equations (3.1), (5.2), and (6.2) give

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\Lambda\bigl((\mathscr RT_n)R_m
             -T_n(\mathscr RR_m)\bigr)\\
&\qquad=2(\lambda_{n-2}-\lambda_m)\Lambda(T_nR_m)\\
&\qquad=-2(\lambda_{n-2}-\lambda_m)c_nc_m.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is the exact first anti-diagonal commutator between uniform quadrature and
the Green operator.

The half-grid nodes are distinct, so the right side vanishes exactly when
`n-2=m`, namely

\[
\boxed{p=4n-7.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

That resonance can occur inside `p>2n+1` (for example, starting at `n=5`,
`p=13`), although it does not itself produce a common zero.

Equation (6.3) is a precise warning: the Racah operator is self-adjoint for the
Green weight `2j+1` with a boundary term, but it is **not** self-adjoint for the
uniform quadrature `Lambda`.  Combining the two forms as though their weights
were interchangeable creates a false cancellation.  Away from (6.4), the
first such error is the explicit nonzero scalar in (6.3); at (6.4), the next
anti-diagonal must be inspected.

## 7. Exact WZ-seam/Green pairing

Write

\[
P(z)=(2z+1)(17z^2+17z+5),
\]

and define

\[
(\mathcal Lf)(j)
=(j+1)^3(f(j+1)-f(j))-j^3(f(j)-f(j-1)).
\]

Since

\[
P(j)-\bigl((j+1)^3+j^3\bigr)=4(2j+1)^3,
\]

the Q8206 seam is

\[
\mathcal LB(j)-4(2j+1)^3B(j)
=-16(2j+1)(j^p-j)^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The Racah equation is

\[
\mathcal Lr_m(j)=2\lambda_m(2j+1)r_m(j).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Discrete Green summation gives the following exact identity over the original
coefficient field.

### Theorem 7.1 — seam/Green identity

For every positive integer `L`,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&L^3\bigl(B(L)r_m(L-1)-B(L-1)r_m(L)\bigr)\\
&=2\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(2j+1)
 \bigl(2(2j+1)^2-\lambda_m\bigr)B(j)r_m(j)\\
&\quad-16\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(2j+1)(j^p-j)^2r_m(j).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.3}
\]

### Full-grid scoped no-go

Reduce (7.3) modulo `p` and take `L=p`.  The boundary is zero and
`j^p-j=0` at every field element.  Under

\[
j\longmapsto p-1-j,
\]

`lambda_j`, `B(j)`, and `r_m(j)` are unchanged, whereas `2j+1` changes sign;
the bracket depends only on its square.  Every term cancels with its partner,
and the midpoint term has `2j+1=0`.  Thus the full-grid identity is `0=0`
for every reflection-invariant `B`; it adds no scalar.

### Half-grid exact identity

Take `L=N+1`.  Modulo `p`,

\[
(N+1)^3=\frac18,
\quad
\lambda_{N+1}=\lambda_{N-1},
\]

and the `j=N` summand vanishes because `2N+1=0`.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac18\bigl(&b_{N-1}r_m(N)-b_Nr_m(N-1)\bigr)\\
&=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(2j+1)
 \bigl(2(2j+1)^2-\lambda_m\bigr)b_jr_m(j).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is a genuine global value identity, but it contains no first derivative
of `B`.  Setting `b_n=0` simply removes one summand; imposing `D_n=0` does
nothing further.

### Why all Racah tests merely repackage the seam

Let

\[
\mathsf A=(A_{j,k})_{0\le j,k\le N}.
\]

It is lower triangular with

\[
A_{j,j}=\binom{2j}{j}\ne0\pmod p,
\]

because `2j<=p-1`.  Hence `mathsf A` is invertible.  The Racah evaluation
matrix is

\[
\mathsf G=(r_m(j))_{j,m}=\mathsf A\mathsf A^{\mathsf T},
\]

so

\[
\det\mathsf G=(\det\mathsf A)^2\ne0.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus `{r_m(j):0<=m<=N}` is a basis of all functions on the half-grid.  Testing
a seam residual against every `r_m` is exactly an invertible change of
coordinates on the pointwise residual vector.  Green pairing cannot manufacture
an additional equation.

The same statement applies to the first derivative of the seam.  At every
field node the forcing `q(z)=-16(2z+1)(z^p-z)^2` satisfies

\[
q(j)=q'(j)=0.
\]

The first nonzero forcing jet is

\[
q''(j)=-32(2j+1).
\tag{7.6}
\]

It enters the equation for `B''`, so it can constrain a putative **triple**
root, but a double root with nonzero second derivative remains compatible.
Without another relation eliminating `B''`, differentiating the seam twice
does not prove first-order transversality.

## 9. The smallest remaining exact obstruction

Euclidean division at `a=lambda_n` gives

\[
R_n(Y)=(Y-a)^2T_n(Y)+b_n+\frac{D_n}{2n+1}(Y-a).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Since `R_n` is a Racah eigenpolynomial,

\[
(\mathscr R-2a)\bigl((Y-a)^2T_n\bigr)
=-(\mathscr R-2a)
 \left(b_n+\frac{D_n}{2n+1}(Y-a)\right).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Every **linear** functional applied to the defect in (9.2) is therefore a
linear combination of `b_n` and `D_n`; on `b_n=0` it is another `D_n` carrier.
This explains abstractly why fixed-width recurrence, differentiated recurrence,

On the other hand, the anti-diagonal quadrature moment of `T_n` is independent
but sees only its top coefficient.  Write

\[
T_n=\tau_{n-2}R_{n-2}+\tau_{n-3}R_{n-3}+\cdots.
\]

The top coefficient is already forced:

\[
\tau_{n-2}=\frac{c_n}{c_{n-2}}.
\]

A successful continuation needs a relation that reaches at least
`tau_{n-3}` (or another lower Newton/Racah datum) **and** couples it to the
sum-of-squares kernel or Frobenius seam in a way not obtained by applying a
linear functional to (9.2).  That is the first place where the current exact
identities leave genuine room for a new Bezout or quadrature argument.

## 10. Reproducible exact Sage/Python verifier

The following script uses only exact integers, rationals, and finite fields.
Harmonic numbers are constructed literally as sums of `QQ(1)/j`; it never
calls `harmonic_number`.

Save it as `q8209_verify.py` and run

```bash
sage -python q8209_verify.py
```

```python
from functools import lru_cache
from sage.all import (
    QQ, ZZ, GF, PolynomialRing, Matrix,
    binomial, factorial, gcd, factor, prime_range
)


def add_all(items, zero):
    out = zero
    for x in items:
        out += x
    return out


@lru_cache(None)
def H_exact(m):
    assert m >= 0
    return add_all((QQ(1) / j for j in range(1, m + 1)), QQ(0))


@lru_cache(None)
def A_exact(n, k):
    if k < 0 or k > n:
        return ZZ(0)
    return ZZ(binomial(n, k) * binomial(n + k, k))


@lru_cache(None)
def b_exact(n):
    return add_all((A_exact(n, k) ** 2 for k in range(n + 1)), ZZ(0))


@lru_cache(None)
def D_exact(n):
    return add_all(
        (
            QQ(A_exact(n, k) ** 2)
            * (H_exact(n + k) - H_exact(n - k))
            for k in range(n + 1)
        ),
        QQ(0),
    )


def phi_list(base_ring, Y, M):
    P = Y.parent()
    phi = [P.one()]
    for k in range(M):
        phi.append(
            phi[-1]
            * (Y - base_ring(k * (k + 1)))
            / base_ring((k + 1) ** 2)
        )
    return phi


def racah_polynomials(K, Y, phi, N):
    P = Y.parent()
    out = []
    for n in range(N + 1):
        out.append(
            add_all(
                (K(A_exact(n, k)) * phi[k] for k in range(n + 1)),
                P.zero(),
            )
        )
    return out


def racah_operator_from_basis(f, Racs, lam):
    """Exact operator defined by R_d -> 2 lambda_d R_d."""
    P = f.parent()
    if f == 0:
        return P.zero()
    rem = f
    out = P.zero()
    for d in range(f.degree(), -1, -1):
        alpha = rem[d] / Racs[d][d]
        out += alpha * (2 * lam[d]) * Racs[d]
        rem -= alpha * Racs[d]
    assert rem == 0
    return out


def audit_green_over_QQ(n, m, L):
    M = max(n, m)
    PY = PolynomialRing(QQ, "Yq")
    Y = PY.gen()
    phi = phi_list(QQ, Y, M)
    Rn = add_all(
        (QQ(A_exact(n, k)) * phi[k] for k in range(n + 1)),
        PY.zero(),
    )
    Rm = add_all(
        (QQ(A_exact(m, k)) * phi[k] for k in range(m + 1)),
        PY.zero(),
    )

    def rv(R, j):
        return R(QQ(j * (j + 1)))

    lhs = 2 * (QQ(n * (n + 1)) - QQ(m * (m + 1))) * add_all(
        (
            QQ(2 * j + 1) * rv(Rn, j) * rv(Rm, j)
            for j in range(L)
        ),
        QQ(0),
    )
    rhs = QQ(L ** 3) * (
        rv(Rn, L) * rv(Rm, L - 1)
        - rv(Rn, L - 1) * rv(Rm, L)
    )
    assert lhs == rhs


def audit_prime(p):
    assert p >= 5 and ZZ(p).is_prime()
    N = (p - 1) // 2
    K = GF(p)
    PY = PolynomialRing(K, "Y")
    Y = PY.gen()
    PZ = PolynomialRing(K, "z")
    z = PZ.gen()

    lam = [K(j * (j + 1)) for j in range(N + 1)]
    phi = phi_list(K, Y, N)
    Racs = racah_polynomials(K, Y, phi, N)

    # Node product and its derivatives.
    S = PY.one()
    for a in lam:
        S *= Y - a
    Tvar = 1 + 4 * Y
    assert S == (Tvar ** (N + 1) - Tvar) / (K(4) ** (N + 1))
    Sp = S.derivative()
    Spp = Sp.derivative()
    for j in range(N):
        w = K(2 * j + 1)
        assert Sp(lam[j]) == -K(1) / K(2)
        assert Spp(lam[j]) == -K(1) / (w ** 2)
    assert Sp(lam[N]) == -K(1)

    # Uniform quadrature on a monomial basis.
    def Lambda(Q):
        return (
            2 * add_all((Q(lam[j]) for j in range(N)), K(0))
            + Q(lam[N])
        )

    for d in range(N + 1):
        expected = -K(1) if d == N else K(0)
        assert Lambda(Y ** d) == expected

    # Racah evaluation matrix is invertible.
    G = Matrix(
        K,
        [[Racs[m](lam[j]) for m in range(N + 1)] for j in range(N + 1)],
    )
    assert G.det() != 0

    # Build F and B.
    F = add_all((f ** 2 for f in phi), PY.zero())
    B = add_all(
        (PZ(f(z * (z + 1))) ** 2 for f in phi),
        PZ.zero(),
    )
    Bp = B.derivative()
    assert B(-z - 1) == B
    assert B[2 * p - 2] == 1

    # Exact node values and first jets against rational b_n,D_n.
    for n in range(N):
        assert B(K(n)) == K(b_exact(n))
        assert Bp(K(n)) == 2 * K(D_exact(n))

    # Hermite trace, both unfolded and folded.
    hermite = add_all(
        (B(K(a)) + K(a) * Bp(K(a)) for a in range(p)),
        K(0),
    )
    assert hermite == 1

    folded = B(K(N)) + add_all(
        (
            2 * B(K(j)) + K(2 * j + 1) * Bp(K(j))
            for j in range(N)
        ),
        K(0),
    )
    assert folded == 1

    # Exact WZ seam.
    Ppoly = (2 * z + 1) * (17 * z ** 2 + 17 * z + 5)
    seam = (
        (z + 1) ** 3 * B(z + 1)
        - Ppoly * B
        + z ** 3 * B(z - 1)
    )
    assert seam == -16 * (2 * z + 1) * (z ** p - z) ** 2

    def lam_at(j):
        return K(j) * K(j + 1)

    # Racah difference equation.
    for m in range(N + 1):
        Rm = Racs[m]
        for j in range(p):
            rjm = Rm(lam_at(j))
            lhs = (
                K(j + 1) ** 3 * (Rm(lam_at(j + 1)) - rjm)
                - K(j) ** 3 * (rjm - Rm(lam_at(j - 1)))
            )
            rhs = 2 * lam[m] * K(2 * j + 1) * rjm
            assert lhs == rhs

    # Half-grid seam/Green identity.
    L = N + 1
    for m in range(N + 1):
        Rm = Racs[m]
        lhs = K(L) ** 3 * (
            B(K(L)) * Rm(lam_at(L - 1))
            - B(K(L - 1)) * Rm(lam_at(L))
        )
        rhs = 2 * add_all(
            (
                K(2 * j + 1)
                * (2 * K(2 * j + 1) ** 2 - lam[m])
                * B(K(j))
                * Rm(lam_at(j))
                for j in range(L)
            ),
            K(0),
        )
        assert lhs == rhs

    # Taylor quotient, first independent moment, and commutator.
    for n in range(2, N):
        a = lam[n]
        w = K(2 * n + 1)
        Rn = Racs[n]
        numerator = Rn - Rn(a) - Rn.derivative()(a) * (Y - a)
        Tn, rem = numerator.quo_rem((Y - a) ** 2)
        assert rem == 0
        assert Rn.derivative()(a) == K(D_exact(n)) / w

        m = N - n + 2
        Rm = Racs[m]
        cn = K(factorial(2 * n)) / (K(factorial(n)) ** 4)
        cm = K(factorial(2 * m)) / (K(factorial(m)) ** 4)

        moment = Lambda(Tn * Rm)
        assert moment == -cn * cm

        # Independence witness: zero local value/derivative, nonzero moment.
        Ptest = (Y - a) ** 2 * Y ** (n - 2)
        Qtest, test_rem = (
            Ptest - Ptest(a) - Ptest.derivative()(a) * (Y - a)
        ).quo_rem((Y - a) ** 2)
        assert test_rem == 0
        assert Ptest(a) == 0 and Ptest.derivative()(a) == 0
        assert Lambda(Qtest * Rm) == -cm

        RT = racah_operator_from_basis(Tn, Racs, lam)
        comm = Lambda(RT * Rm - Tn * (2 * lam[m] * Rm))
        assert comm == 2 * (lam[n - 2] - lam[m]) * moment

        # If an actual common zero occurs, verify the conditional quotient form.
        if Rn(a) == 0 and Rn.derivative()(a) == 0:
            direct, direct_rem = Rn.quo_rem((Y - a) ** 2)
            assert direct_rem == 0 and direct == Tn


def exact_counterexamples_by_index(nmax):
    """
    Finite exact search only.  Its output is evidence, never a universal proof.
    """
    hits = []
    for n in range(1, nmax + 1):
        bn = abs(ZZ(b_exact(n)))
        dn = abs(ZZ(D_exact(n).numerator()))
        g = gcd(bn, dn)
        for p, e in factor(g):
            p = ZZ(p)
            if p.is_prime() and p > 2 * n + 1:
                hits.append((n, p, e))
    return hits


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Exact structural regressions.
    for p in [5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29]:
        audit_prime(p)

    # Independent exact checks of the supplied finite Green identity.
    for args in [(2, 1, 3), (4, 2, 5), (5, 3, 4)]:
        audit_green_over_QQ(*args)

    # Deliberately small default finite search; enlarge locally as desired.
    print("counterexamples through n=30:", exact_counterexamples_by_index(30))
    print("all exact structural checks passed")
```

## Final status

No exact counterexample is produced, and no universal transversality proof is
claimed.  The first genuinely independent scalar is (5.2)/(5.4), but (5.5)
shows exactly why it is insufficient: it is only the leading-coefficient
functional.  The global Hermite trace is stronger-looking but collapses to a
unit multiple of `D_n` at a zero of `b_n`.  Seam plus complete Green testing is
information-equivalent to the pointwise seam, and the uniform quadrature has
the explicit non-self-adjoint defect (6.3).

The next viable attack must reach lower quotient coefficients through an
additional exact structure of the sum-of-squares kernel; otherwise it will
again be either a local carrier or degree bookkeeping.
