1. **Opening reduction, equations (1)–(2): the fixed-\(q\) family really is equivalent to the full master estimate, but two sentences should be corrected.**

   **Defect.** The central quantifier claim is **correct**: no uniformity in \(q\) is needed. For fixed \(Q\),
   \[
   M(n)=\sum_{q\le Q}M_q(n)+
   \sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n/(Q+1)\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}\log p,
   \]
   so finitely many fixed-\(q\) estimates give \(o(n)\) for the first sum, while the second is at most
   \(\vartheta(n/(Q+1))\). Letting \(n\to\infty\) and then \(Q\to\infty\) proves \(M(n)=o(n)\). Conversely \(M_q(n)\le M(n)\), so the reverse implication is immediate.

   There are, however, two textual errors. First, the coefficient \((1+o(1))n/(Q+1)\) is the prime number theorem for \(\vartheta(x)\), not merely “Chebyshev.” Chebyshev alone gives \(O(n/(Q+1))\), which is still enough after taking \(Q\) large, but not with the displayed asymptotic constant \(1\). Second, the phrase “plus \(O_q(1)\) from the finitely many primes dividing \(b_q\)” does not belong to the displayed master sum \(M(n)\), which already contains only the \(b_{n\bmod p}\) channel. It is unnecessary and potentially confusing.

   **Proposed replacement text.**
   ~~~latex
   No uniformity in \(q\) is needed.  Fix \(Q\).  The contribution of
   primes with \(\lfloor n/p\rfloor>Q\) is at most
   \[
     \vartheta\!\left(\frac{n}{Q+1}\right)
     =(1+o(1))\frac{n}{Q+1}
   \]
   by the prime number theorem (the weaker Chebyshev bound
   \(O(n/(Q+1))\) would also suffice).  For each of the finitely many
   \(q\le Q\), the contribution of the cell
   \((n/(q+1),n/q]\) is exactly
   \[
     M_q(n)=(\log n+O_q(1))N_q(n)=o(n).
   \]
   Hence
   \[
     \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{M(n)}n\le\frac1{Q+1}.
   \]
   Letting \(Q\to\infty\) gives \(M(n)=o(n)\).  Conversely
   \(M_q(n)\le M(n)\), so \(M(n)=o(n)\) implies (1) for every fixed \(q\).
   Thus the family of fixed-\(q\) statements is equivalent to the full
   master estimate, with no uniformity in \(q\).
   ~~~

10. **Result 8, the \(f_n^\ast\) remark: the advertised “natural” fixed-modulus extension omits one Lucas factor.**

    **Defect.** Formula (23) shows that after fixing the least prime \(p\) in \(k=pm\), the \(p\)-phase is
    \[
    e_p\!\left(\bar m\,
      b_{\,n\bmod p}\,
      b_{\lfloor n/p\rfloor-m}\right).
    \]
    The definition
    \[
    f_n^\ast(pm)=e_p(\bar m\,b_{Q_p-m})
    \]
    drops the factor \(b_{n\bmod p}\). The subsequent least-prime-factor partition is still an exact partition of the artificially defined \(f_n^\ast\), so there is no combinatorial error in
    \(S_n^\ast=T+\sum C_p\); however, the composite values are no longer the actual fixed-\(p\) CRT--Lucas phase from (23). Thus the remark does not establish what it claims about the natural extension of the Apéry phase.

    The square-root estimate for the \(C_p\) is correctly phrased as conditional (“would give”), and its \(n^{3/4+\varepsilon}\) scale is consistent with summing square-root-length bounds over
    \(n^{1/3}\le p<\sqrt n\).

    **Proposed replacement text.**
    ~~~latex
    For squarefree composite \(k=pm\), with \(p=P^-(k)\) and
    \(Q_p=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\), define instead
    \[
      f_n^\ast(k)=
      e_p\!\left(
        \overline m\,
        b_{\,n\bmod p}\,
        b_{Q_p-m}
      \right),
      \qquad
      f_n^\ast(p)=e_p(b_{n-p}).
    \]
    Then the composite value is exactly the \(p\)-factor furnished by
    (23).  The least-prime-factor partition
    \[
      S_n^\ast(n^{1/3})
        =T(n)+\sum_{n^{1/3}\le p<\sqrt n}C_p(n)
    \]
    is exact.  A square-root estimate for these rough fixed-\(p\) sums
    would imply \(\sum_p|C_p(n)|\ll n^{3/4+\varepsilon}=o(L(n))\);
    this estimate is an additional open hypothesis, not a consequence of
    (23).
    ~~~

11. **Result 9, equations (25)–(26): the Galois covariance is correct. Equation (26a) needs a stronger formulation of hypothesis (1).**

    **Defect.** The reindexing proof of
    \(\sigma_aE_p(r)=E_p(ar)\) is correct.

    The lower bound (26a) is also valid **if** there is one fixed prime
    \(\mathfrak P\) above \(p\) for which the congruence
    \[
    E_p(s)\equiv b_s\pmod{\mathfrak P}
    \]
    holds simultaneously for every residue \(s\) in the Galois orbit under consideration. As hypothesis (1) is currently written, it can be read as asserting the congruence only for the single displayed \(r\). Applying \(\sigma_a\) to a congruence modulo \(\mathfrak P\) normally moves the prime to \(\sigma_a\mathfrak P\); it does **not** by itself give a congruence modulo the same \(\mathfrak P\).

    Consequently the sentence
    \[
    E_p(ar)=\sigma_aE_p(r)\equiv b_{ar}\pmod{\mathfrak P}
    \]
    is justified only under a uniform same-\(\mathfrak P\) version of (1).

    **Proposed replacement text.**
    ~~~latex
    Assume that there is a fixed prime \(\mathfrak P\) of the cyclotomic
    coefficient field above \(p\), with residue field identified with
    \(\mathbb F_p\), such that
    \[
      E_p(s)\equiv b_s\pmod{\mathfrak P}
    \]
    for every \(s\in\mathbb Z/(p-1)\mathbb Z\) occurring in the orbit.
    Then distinct residues \(b_{as}\bmod p\) give distinct conjugates
    \(\sigma_aE_p(s)\), and hence
    \[
      [\mathbb Q(E_p(s)):\mathbb Q]
      \ge
      \#\{b_{as\bmod(p-1)}\bmod p:
           a\in(\mathbb Z/(p-1)\mathbb Z)^\times\}.
    \]
    ~~~
    With this strengthened hypothesis, (26a) is rigorous.

12. **Result 9, equation (27) and the final fixed-field no-go: the exact-degree implication is conditionally correct, but the no-go does not survive the order-\(12\) failure from the text as currently written.**

    **Defect.** Conditional on exact orbit distinctness modulo the involution \(a\sim-a\), equation (27) is correct: the orbit of a character of order
    \(\nu_{p,n}\) has \(\varphi(\nu_{p,n})\) unit actions, and quotienting by \(\pm1\) gives \(\varphi(\nu_{p,n})/2\) for \(\nu>2\).

    The problem is the next logical step. The text explicitly states that hypothesis (2) is false at order \(12\). A failure at one bounded order would indeed be harmless **if one had proved that (2) holds for all sufficiently large orders**, or at least that only finitely many bounded orders are exceptional. But that is not proved. The finite computations do not rule out further collapses at arbitrarily large orders.

    Nor does (26a) alone repair the asymptotic no-go: to exclude a fixed degree \(D\), one would need a theorem that the residue set on the right side of (26a) eventually has cardinality \(>D\) whenever \(\nu_{p,n}\) is sufficiently large. The source supplies only finite numerical ratios, not such a theorem.

    Therefore the sentence
    “Consequently a single fixed bounded-degree coefficient field cannot contain the off-centre lifts for a positive proportion of top-window primes” is **not proved by the text** after hypothesis (2) is known to fail.

    **Proposed replacement text.**
    ~~~latex
    If, in addition, there is a function \(R(D)\) such that
    \[
      \nu_{p,n}>R(D)
      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
      \#\{b_{a(n-p)\bmod(p-1)}\bmod p:
           a\in(\mathbb Z/(p-1)\mathbb Z)^\times\}>D,
    \]
    then (26a) implies that a degree-\(D\) coefficient field can contain
    such lifts only for primes with \(\nu_{p,n}\le R(D)\).
    Since the latter primes are \(n^{o(1)}\) in number, no fixed
    bounded-degree field can cover a positive proportion of the top-window
    primes.

    The stronger orbit-distinctness hypothesis (2), if proved for all
    sufficiently large character orders (allowing finitely many bounded
    exceptions such as order \(12\)), implies this residue-diversity
    condition.  At present neither eventual orbit distinctness nor the
    required residue-diversity estimate is proved; the order-\(12\)
    collapse shows that it cannot simply be asserted for every order.
    ~~~

## Verdict

The section is close to publishable after revision. The opening fixed-\(q\) equivalence is mathematically correct and is a useful improvement over a uniform-in-\(q\) formulation; Result 7's sharpened \(\varepsilon^{-3}(\log X)^2\) estimate is also correct, including the \(-I\) Cauchy--Schwarz step and the advertised absolute constants, conditional only on the two earlier report inputs that should be named explicitly. Results 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 are mathematically sound modulo clearly identifiable standard/earlier lemmas. Result 4 is a valid support-law countermodel once its gap-polynomial identity is imported explicitly. Result 8's CRT--Lucas identity is correct, but the \(f_n^\ast\) extension must restore the missing factor \(b_{n\bmod p}\), and the linear-decomposition no-go should be stated as an “alone” obstruction rather than a universal impossibility theorem. Result 9 needs the largest correction: (26a) is valid only under a uniform same-prime congruence hypothesis, and the fixed-coefficient-field no-go does **not** presently follow after the order-\(12\) collapse unless one adds an eventual orbit-distinctness or residue-diversity hypothesis. The finite \(0.87\)–\(0.94\) evidence cannot substitute for that missing asymptotic statement. With those changes, the section would accurately separate proved implications, conditional barriers, and numerical evidence.
