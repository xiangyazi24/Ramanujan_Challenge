# 1. Precise OR-row convention

Let a true q6 row have primes `p<q`, gap

\[
d=q-p>0,
\]

upper digit `u`, lower digit

\[
r=u+6d,
\]

and shell coordinate

\[
n=6q+u=6p+r.
\]

Assume the usual true-row selectedness

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad q\mid b_u.
\]

A signed secondary witness is either

\[
a_-=L_-=d-u-1
\]

for the minus sign, or

\[
a_+=L_+=u+12d-p
\]

for the plus sign, with the corresponding legality `0<=a_<p` and divisibility

\[
p\mid b_a.
\]

For such a signed witness define

\[
a_0:=\min(a,p-1-a).
\]

For the deduplicated OR theorem the clean definition is:

> `E(U,A)` is the set of underlying true rows for which **at least one active sign** has
> \[
> 1\le u\le U,\qquad 1\le a_0\le A.
> \]
> A row satisfying both signs is counted once.

For each row in `E(U,A)`, choose one active sign witnessing the cutoff `a_0<=A`. If both do, choose either by a fixed deterministic rule. Then choose the orientation

\[
\mathrm O:\ a=a_0,
\qquad
\mathrm R:\ a=p-1-a_0.
\]

If `a_0=(p-1)/2`, both orientation equations coincide; assign it to one of them deterministically. This gives a genuine partition into at most four classes

```text
minus-original
minus-reflected
plus-original
plus-reflected
```

with no double charging.

This convention is the only OR-deduplication point that must be explicit. Simply summing the four signed classes would also give a valid upper bound, because double-sign rows would then be overcounted by only a constant factor, but the above partition matches the “count once” statement exactly.

# 2. Audit of all four parametrizations

Fix `u`, `a0`, and one class.

## 2.1 Minus original

Here

\[
a_-=d-u-1=a_0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{d=u+a_0+1.}
\]

So `d` is fixed. A row is then determined by its lower prime `p`, since

\[
q=p+d,
\qquad
r=u+6d.
\]

Hence distinct rows have distinct `p`; because `d` is fixed they also have distinct `q`.

This parametrization is correct.

## 2.2 Minus reflected

Formally impose

\[
a_-=p-1-a_0.
\]

Since `a_-=d-u-1`,

\[
d-u-1=p-1-a_0,
\]

so

\[
d=p+u-a_0
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{q=p+d=2p+u-a_0.}
\]

Thus `p` determines `q` injectively, and conversely

\[
p=\frac{q-u+a_0}{2}.
\]

So the claimed injectivity is correct.

In fact legal q6 geometry makes this class empty. Since

\[
r=u+6d<p,
\]

we have

\[
6d<p,
\qquad d<p/6.
\]

For a legal minus secondary digit,

\[
0\le a_-=d-u-1<d<p/6<(p-1)/2
\]

for the target primes. Hence always

\[
a_0=a_-.
\]

So a genuine minus row can only be in the original orientation. Keeping the reflected class in the bookkeeping is harmless because its true-row fibre is empty.

## 2.3 Plus original

Here

\[
a_+=u+12d-p=a_0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{p=u+12d-a_0.}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{q=p+d=u+13d-a_0.}
\]

Thus `d` determines both endpoint primes. If two rows had the same `d`, they would have the same `p,q,u`, hence the same `r=u+6d` and the same row. Therefore distinct rows have distinct `d`, distinct `p`, and distinct `q`.

This parametrization is correct.

## 2.4 Plus reflected

Here

\[
a_+=p-1-a_0.
\]

Since `a_+=u+12d-p`,

\[
u+12d-p=p-1-a_0,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{2p=u+12d+1+a_0.}
\]

Consequently

\[
p=\frac{u+12d+1+a_0}{2},
\qquad
q=p+d=\frac{u+14d+1+a_0}{2}.
\]

Again `d` determines both primes injectively. The parity condition merely removes inadmissible `d`; it cannot create collisions.

This parametrization is correct.

# 3. Reflection gives the lower radical certificate

For every true signed row,

\[
p\mid b_a.
\]

If the row is in an original class, `a=a0`, so directly

\[
p\mid b_{a_0}.
\]

If it is in a reflected class,

\[
a=p-1-a_0.
\]

The Apéry reflection congruence gives

\[
b_{p-1-a_0}\equiv b_{a_0}\pmod p
\]

(up to a harmless unit/sign would also suffice for divisibility), hence again

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_{a_0}.}
\]

Thus reflection does exactly what the proposed proof needs. No transfer theorem or finite scan is required here.

At the upper endpoint, true-row selectedness is simply

\[
\boxed{q\mid b_u.}
\]

# 4. Radical multiplicity is exact inside a fixed class

Let `C=C(u,a0,class)` contain `k` rows.

From Section 2, the lower primes of distinct rows in `C` are pairwise distinct. By Section 3, every one divides `b_a0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{R\in C}p_R\mid\operatorname{rad}(b_{a_0}).
}
\]

Likewise the upper primes are pairwise distinct and every one divides `b_u`, so

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{R\in C}q_R\mid\operatorname{rad}(b_u).
}
\]

This is genuine multiplicity control, not merely support: the only reason a radical is enough is that **within the fixed `(u,a0,class)` fibre no lower prime and no upper prime repeats**.

No singleton-prime-pair hypothesis is needed.

# 5. Cross equalities `p_i=q_j` are harmless

Suppose a prime `ell` occurs as the lower endpoint of one row and the upper endpoint of another:

\[
p_i=q_j=\ell.
\]

This does not damage either certificate.

The lower certificate is the independent divisibility

\[
\prod p_R\mid\operatorname{rad}(b_{a_0}),
\]

where `ell` occurs once because the `p_R` are distinct.

The upper certificate is the independent divisibility

\[
\prod q_R\mid\operatorname{rad}(b_u),
\]

where `ell` also occurs once because the `q_R` are distinct.

The proof never asserts that the product of both endpoint products is squarefree or divides a single radical. It takes the **minimum of two separate logarithmic capacities**. Therefore a cross equality causes no loss.

The statement in Q3781 that such equalities are harmless is correct.

# 6. Exact logarithmic bound for one fibre

Assume the usual dyadic q6 shell

\[
T<n\le2T.
\]

Because

\[
n=6p+r<7p,
\]

we have

\[
p>\frac n7>\frac T7.
\]

Similarly

\[
n=6q+u<7q
\]

gives

\[
q>\frac T7.
\]

Therefore, for a class of size `k`,

\[
\left(\frac T7\right)^k
<\prod_{R\in C}p_R
\le\operatorname{rad}(b_{a_0})
\le b_{a_0},
\]

and separately

\[
\left(\frac T7\right)^k
<\prod_{R\in C}q_R
\le\operatorname{rad}(b_u)
\le b_u.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
k\log(T/7)
\le
\min\{\log b_{a_0},\log b_u\}.}
\]

This is the claimed `min`, and it is justified because the same `k` is bounded independently by both radical capacities.

For Apéry numbers, the elementary binomial formula gives the uniform bound already used elsewhere in the project,

\[
b_t\le(t+1)64^t.
\]

For `t>=1`, `t+1<=2^t`, so one may take the completely explicit estimate

\[
\boxed{\log b_t\le t\log128.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
k
\le
\frac{\log128}{\log(T/7)}\min(u,a_0).}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This step is valid uniformly for every fixed `J`; imposing an additional macroscopic-gap restriction `d>T/J` only shrinks the fibres.

# 7. Summing the fibres

After the OR-deduplicated partition, there are at most four classes for each pair `(u,a0)`. Therefore (6.1) gives

\[
E(U,A)
\le
\frac{4\log128}{\log(T/7)}
\sum_{1\le u\le U}
\sum_{1\le a_0\le A}
\min(u,a_0).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The crude bound is already enough:

\[
\sum_{u\le U}\sum_{a_0\le A}\min(u,a_0)
\le UA\min(U,A).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
E(U,A)
\ll
\frac{UA\min(U,A)}{\log T}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For completeness, if `U<=A`, the exact sum is

\[
\sum_{u=1}^{U}\sum_{a=1}^{A}\min(u,a)
=
\frac{A U(U+1)}2
-
\frac{U(U+1)(U-1)}6,
\]

and the symmetric formula applies when `A<=U`. Thus the order `UA min(U,A)` is sharp for a full rectangle; there is no missing logarithm in the combinatorial summation.

Now if

\[
UA\min(U,A)
=o\!\left(\frac{T^2}{\log T}\right),
\]

then, since `log(T/7)~log T`, (7.2) gives

\[
\boxed{
E(U,A)
=o\!\left(\frac{T^2}{\log^2T}\right).
}
\]

The proposed final implication is therefore correct.

# 10. Clean fully prescribed theorem statement

A source-independent formulation is the following.

> **Joint-prefix matched-bistar theorem.**  Fix a dyadic q6 shell `T<n<=2T` and, optionally, any fixed-`J` subfamily of true quotient-six rows. For each underlying true secondary-OR row choose one active sign `s in {+,-}`. Let `a_s` be its legal active lower secondary digit and set
> \[
> a_0(s)=\min\{a_s,p-1-a_s\}.
> \]
> Let `E(U,A)` be the underlying rows admitting at least one active sign with
> \[
> 1\le u\le U,
> \qquad
> 1\le a_0(s)\le A,
> \]
> counted once. Then
> \[
> \boxed{
> E(U,A)
> \le
> \frac{4\log128}{\log(T/7)}
> \sum_{1\le u\le U}
> \sum_{1\le a\le A}\min(u,a)
> }
> \]
> and in particular
> \[
> \boxed{
> E(U,A)
> \ll
> \frac{UA\min(U,A)}{\log T}.
> }
> \]
> Consequently
> \[
> UA\min(U,A)
> =o\!\left(\frac{T^2}{\log T}\right)
> \]

> \[
> \boxed{
> E(U,A)=o\!\left(\frac{T^2}{\log^2T}\right).
> }
> \]

The proof is exactly the four-class argument audited above. Double-sign rows are assigned once to a qualifying sign, central reflection orientations are assigned once, and cross equalities between a lower prime of one row and an upper prime of another are irrelevant because the lower and upper radical capacities are used separately.
