# Q4328 — the determinant-sparse Frobenius detector

## 0. Verdict

The proposed phrase has two materially different formalizations. They must be separated before any construction claim is meaningful.

1. **Exact marked detector.** If a detector is allowed to depend on the two literal lower Apéry-zero tests and is required to be spectrally degenerate exactly at the actual leaves, then the detector exists canonically, but its determinant sparsity is **logically equivalent to the leaf-count bound**. This is the third instance of certificate collapse. A one-dimensional unit-root detector realizes equality: the total multiplicity of its exceptional Frobenius factors is exactly the fibre size \(K\).

2. **Fixed pre-support detector.** There is also a canonical detector defined before factoring or enumerating the lower support: the zero-dimensional arithmetic scheme cut out by the two fixed affine-index Apéry values, after the honest \(q\)-primary and fixed-ledger saturation. Its special fibre at a lower prime \(p\) is nonempty precisely when \(p\) divides the corresponding fixed two-value gcd. Geometric Frobenius on that fibre has the single eigenvalue \(1\). Asking this detector to have at most \(D\) nontrivial local factors in the admissible shell is **strictly stronger** than the actual leaf bound because it includes false-positive common divisors, but it is exactly the already named support-blind restricted-radical theorem. The Frobenius language adds no new mechanism: all arithmetic information lies in which fibres exist, not in their spectra.

There is no third useful interpretation exposed by the current bank.

- A common-\(q\) determinant polynomial is CQRD in determinant notation and is killed by \(q\)-local unit erasure; unrestricted existence is equivalent to the root-count target.
- A determinant container modulo an auxiliary prime is the effective auxiliary-class law, already proved equivalent at the matching scale to root descent.
- An integral determinant or perfect-complex rank-jump detector is a Fitting/scalar carrier and contains the marked-prime information floor.
- Separate detectors at the varying lower characteristics live in orthogonal idempotent components, so their determinant defects add and cannot cancel.
- The ordinary determinant or characteristic polynomial of the natural Apéry \(F\)-crystal is not a detector: Frobenius is invertible, the determinant is row-key blind, and determinants of mixed extensions do not see the extension class.

Thus the **determinant-sparse Frobenius detector is closed as an independent route**. What survives is not a new Frobenius theorem but one of two old counting statements:

```text
exact labelled exceptional support sparse
    = the desired fibre bound itself;

fixed support-blind exceptional support sparse
    = restricted radical / fixed-gcd shell sparsity.
```

No unconditional positive exponent is proved here. The exact reusable deliverable is the collapse theorem and the Frobenius/Fitting trichotomy below.

## 3. Packaging check: a determinant is not automatically a conjunction detector

A first tempting matrix is

\[
\operatorname{diag}(u,v).
\]

Its determinant is \(uv\), which vanishes when **either** coordinate vanishes. The lower leaf requires **both** coordinates to vanish. Thus a naive \(2	imes2\) determinant detects the union, not the conjunction.

There are two honest ways to package the conjunction.

### 3.1 Koszul detector

For a field \(k\) and \(u,v\in k\), take the Koszul complex

\[
0\longrightarrow k
 \xrightarrow{(-v,u)^T} k^2
 \xrightarrow{(u,v)} k
\longrightarrow0.
	ag{3.1}
\]

Because \(k\) is a field, this complex is acyclic whenever \((u,v)
e(0,0)\), and all differentials vanish when \(u=v=0\). Hence

\[
H^ullet K(u,v)
e0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u=v=0.
	ag{3.2}
\]

This is the canonical derived conjunction detector. However, its alternating determinant line has Euler cancellation. One must look at a chosen cohomology group, total cohomology dimension, or an augmented determinant; the bare superdeterminant is not the required statistic.

### 3.2 Norm determinant

Let \(k=\mathbf F_p\), let \(k_2=\mathbf F_{p^2}\), and choose \(	heta\in k_2\setminus k\). Put

\[
w=(1-m)+m(u+	heta v).
	ag{3.3}
\]

If \(m=0\), then \(w=1\). If \(m=1\), linear independence of \(1,	heta\) over \(k\) gives

\[

\]

Multiplication by \(w\) on the two-dimensional \(k\)-space \(k_2\) has determinant

\[

so

\[
\det_k(\mu_w)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad

	ag{3.4}

If \(arphi_p:z\mapsto z^p\) is absolute Frobenius on \(k_2\), then \(arphi_p\) is invertible and

The same construction can incorporate (2.10), but doing so inserts the exact leaf indicator into the mark.

## 4. The exact-detector collapse theorem

### 4.1 Abstract definition

Let \(\Omega\) be a finite candidate set and \(\mathcal L\subseteq\Omega\) the actual leaf set. An **exact local Frobenius detector** is a family

\[
(V_p,F_p)_{p\in\Omega}
\]

of finite-dimensional spaces over suitable local coefficient fields with invertible Frobenius operators, together with a nonnegative integer defect

\[
\delta_p=\dim\ker(1-F_p)
\]

or any other additive spectral multiplicity satisfying

\[
\delta_p\ge1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\in\mathcal L.
	ag{4.1}
\]

Its total determinant defect is

\[
\Delta=\sum_{p\in\Omega}\delta_p.
	ag{4.2}
\]

The support-only version uses

\[
\Delta_{m supp}
=\#\{p:\det(1-F_p)=0\}.
	ag{4.3}
\]

### 4.2 Canonical exact unit-root detector

Let

\[
\ell_p=1_{\mathcal L}(p)\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Take a one-dimensional line \(V_p\) and let Frobenius act by the unit

\[
F_p=2-\ell_p.
	ag{4.4}
\]

Since \(p\ge7\), both \(1\) and \(2\) are units. Then

\[
\det(1-F_p)
=1-(2-\ell_p)
=\ell_p-1.
	ag{4.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
\det(1-F_p)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\ell_p=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\in\mathcal L.
	ag{4.6}
\]

This is a genuine one-dimensional unit-root Frobenius module over a point. It is completely tautological because its eigenvalue was defined from the exact leaf indicator, but that is precisely the collapse test.

The indicator \(\ell_p\) need not be produced by enumerating a precomputed list. Formulae (2.8)--(2.10) construct it canonically from the literal recurrence values and masks. Therefore the phrase “do not enumerate the support” is not enough to exclude the tautology. A non-collapse definition must prohibit using the lower zero tests themselves when defining the exceptional spectrum.

### 4.3 Theorem

#### Theorem 4.1 — exact DSFD collapse

For every finite actual leaf set \(\mathcal L\) and every \(D\ge0\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(\#\mathcal L\le D\).
2. There exists an exact local Frobenius detector with
   \(\Delta_{m supp}\le D\).
3. There exists an exact local Frobenius detector with total multiplicity
   \(\Delta\le D\).

Moreover the canonical detector (4.4) has

\[
\Delta_{m supp}=\Delta=\#\mathcal L.
	ag{4.7}
\]

#### Proof

If an exact detector satisfies (4.1), every leaf contributes at least one exceptional local factor, so

\[
\#\mathcal L\le\Delta_{m supp}\le\Delta.
\]

This proves \(2\Rightarrow1\) and \(3\Rightarrow1\). Conversely, the detector (4.4) is exceptional exactly on \(\mathcal L\), with multiplicity one at each leaf. If \(\#\mathcal L\le D\), it proves both 2 and 3. ∎

### 4.4 One-sided detectors

Suppose only

\[
p\in\mathcal L\Longrightarrow \det(1-F_p)=0,
	ag{4.8}
\]

so false positives are allowed. Then a bound on the exceptional set is strictly stronger than the leaf bound. But unrestricted existence still collapses, because the exact detector (4.4) is an allowed one-sided detector.

To obtain a genuinely stronger theorem, one must fix the detector **before** the lower zero support is known and restrict its construction class. The next section identifies the canonical such object. It is not new: it is the fixed-gcd support scheme.

## 6. Why the natural Apéry Frobenius is not the detector

The repository contains a genuine Apéry differential \(F\)-isocrystal and, when the inhomogeneous companion is retained, a mixed rank-four extension. These are important vertical objects, but neither determinant detects the q6 leaf event.

### 6.1 Invertibility

A Frobenius structure on an overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal is an isomorphism after inverting \(p\). Hence

\[
\det(\operatorname{Frob}_p)
e0.
\]

A vanishing detector cannot be the determinant of Frobenius itself. One must choose a special value such as

\[
\det(1-\operatorname{Frob}_p),
\]

or add a marked evaluation map. Neither is automatic.

### 6.2 Row-key blindness

The natural rank-three Apéry \(F\)-crystal is attached to the Picard-Fuchs equation on the geometric \(t\)-line. Its Frobenius is semilinear over

\[
t\longmapsto t^p.
\]

The q6 leaf instead asks whether two specified coefficients of the Apéry solution, at the \(p\)-dependent recurrence-clock indices (2.1) or (2.3), vanish modulo \(p\). The ordinary Frobenius characteristic polynomial has no slots for

\[
r_\sigma(p),\qquad a_\sigma(p),\qquad
	ext{the raw window},\qquad
	ext{the minus-first assignment}.
\]

To make those coordinates visible one must add marked evaluation maps. Once those maps are inserted, their rank jump is precisely the Koszul/norm detector of Section 3, or the torsion support complex of Section 5.

### 6.3 Translation is not Frobenius

The recurrence transfer from index \(n\) to \(n+h\) is the \(h\)-fold iterate of the nonautonomous difference cocycle over

\[
n\longmapsto n+1.
\]

It is not the \(h\)-th power of crystalline Frobenius. On \(\mathbf F_p\)-points absolute Frobenius fixes the clock coordinate, while recurrence translation advances it. The earlier Dwork audit in

```text
drops/Q6547-6691acda.md
```

proved this category mismatch explicitly. Therefore the two q6 row shifts cannot be reinterpreted as two powers of one Frobenius operator without adding a new hybrid difference/Frobenius structure.

### 6.4 Determinants do not see mixed extension classes

For a Frobenius-stable extension

\[
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow0,
\]

one can choose a block-triangular matrix

\[
F_M=egin{pmatrix}F_A&*\0&F_B\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
\det(1-ZF_M)
=\det(1-ZF_A)\det(1-ZF_B),
	ag{6.1}
\]

independent of the extension entry \(*\). The same is true of \(\det F_M\). Thus the mixed divided-state or normal-function entry is invisible to the ordinary determinant and characteristic polynomial. A determinant that uses the marked extension coordinate must be an augmented minor or mapping-cone determinant; that is again a marked torsion/Fitting object.

This is particularly damaging to the proposed route: a determinant is less sensitive than a trace to the mixed state, not more. The upper selected mark and the two lower zero conditions do not become a three-way spectral conjunction merely because they are placed in one mixed module.

## 7. The characteristic trilemma

Every proposed detector must choose a coefficient characteristic. The three possibilities exhaust the natural options.

### 7.1 At the upper characteristic \(q\): common field, erased support

Every legal lower prime satisfies \(0<p<q\), so its image in \(\mathbf F_q\) is a unit. In the recurrence-algebraic presentation, a lower marked divisibility row has the form

\[
F(p)=pZ.
\]

After base change to \(\mathbf F_q\), this is the split graph equation

\[
Z=p^{-1}F(p),
\]

which imposes no condition on \(p\). With any number of lower rows the Smith pivots are units. Honest saturation leaves the upper elimination ideal unchanged.

The fixed-gcd support scheme says the same thing more tersely. Because the complete \(q\)-primary part was removed,

\[
q
mid G_\sigma(q,t),
\]

and therefore

\[
(\mathbf Z/G_\sigma)\otimes\mathbf F_q=0.
	ag{7.1}
\]

There is no lower support in the \(q\)-fibre for Frobenius to inspect.

Consequences:

- a determinant polynomial in a candidate variable over \(\mathbf F_q\) whose roots contain all partners is exactly CQRD;
- in the natural recurrence-algebraic class the determinant is identically support-blind after unit saturation;
- unrestricted existence of a low-degree determinant polynomial is equivalent to the partner-set bound, as already proved in the CQRD round.

A common-\(q\) determinant therefore does not revive the route.

### 7.2 At each lower characteristic \(p\): exact support, orthogonal components

At characteristic \(p\), the two lower zero tests are meaningful and the exact detectors of Sections 3 and 4 exist. But for distinct primes \(p
e p'\), the state spaces live in different fields.

Over the semilocal product

\[
R_I=\prod_{p\in I}\mathbf F_p,
\]

there are orthogonal primitive idempotents \(e_p\). Every exact detector decomposes as

\[
V=igoplus_{p\in I}e_pV,
\]

and Frobenius is componentwise. In the Grothendieck group or any additive rank/multiplicity ledger, each supporting prime contributes a nonnegative separate component. There is no cross-prime cancellation.

For the canonical detector,

\[
\sum_{p\in I}\dim\ker(1-F_p)=K_{x,I,\sigma}.

A vertical Apéry \(F\)-crystal at \(\lambda\) can detect \(\lambda\)-adic or mod-\(\lambda\) recurrence data. The determinant proposal can be placed in one of four boxes.

### 8.2 A determinant residue container modulo auxiliary \(\lambda\)

### 8.3 An integral determinant or perfect complex

It remembers support and pays the information floor. It supplies no automatic upper bound on the number of primes.

### 8.4 Separate local determinants at each \(p\)

These detect the event exactly, but their exceptional support has size \(K\). A sparse-support theorem is the target; internal spectral shape is irrelevant.

Hence “determinant” does not identify a fifth algebraic category. It selects one of the four already classified routes.

## 9. Why other notions of spectral sparsity do not help

The only useful sparsity is sparsity in the **external prime parameter**. Common internal notions fail as follows.

### 9.1 Few distinct eigenvalues

The identity matrix \(I_K\) has one distinct eigenvalue and a \(K\)-dimensional \(1\)-eigenspace. Thus one eigenvalue can carry arbitrarily many leaves.

### 9.2 Few characteristic-polynomial monomials

A cyclic permutation matrix \(C_K\) has

\[
\det(XI-C_K)=X^K-1,
\]

only two monomials, but dimension \(K\). Sparse coefficients do not bound spectral multiplicity.

In positive characteristic, binomial collapse makes this worse:

\[
(1-Z)^{r^m}=1-Z^{r^m}
\]

in characteristic \(r\), so a root of arbitrarily large multiplicity can be encoded by two terms.

### 9.3 Small determinant set

Symplectic or weight-normalized Frobenius matrices can have a fixed determinant for arbitrarily large rank. A determinant taking one value contains no leaf-count information.

### 9.4 Low rank rather than low corank

A large identity block has rank \(K\) and \(1-F=0\). If one counts the corank of \(1-F\), the result is \(K\), hence the target. If one counts the rank, the direction is wrong. This mirrors the banked partial-isometry no-go for popular-gap bands.

### 9.5 Low local conductor

The schemes \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z/N_S\) from Section 5 have identical rank-one local factors at every supporting prime, with arbitrary support size. Bounded local conductor does not control the number of vertical bad fibres without a global height/support theorem.

### 9.6 Low degree or order of vanishing

The degree of a direct-sum determinant or the order of its root at \(1\) is at least \(K\), and the canonical detector attains \(K\). This is the exact collapse theorem.

Thus every internal sparsity notion is either too weak to count leaves or, once strengthened to count multiplicity/support, is equivalent to the desired bound.

## 10. The finite-presentation/Frobenius dichotomy

The previous sections can be summarized as a structural theorem.

#### Theorem 10.1 — vertical/Fitting dichotomy

Fix an actual selected state \(x\), sign \(\sigma\), and a finite lower-prime candidate set \(I\). Consider a recurrence-algebraic or finite-presentation construction intended to detect the literal lower event.

Exactly one of the following happens.

1. **Flat/lisse Frobenius object.** The construction remains generically nonzero and gives invertible Frobenius at almost all primes. Then the natural determinant is independent of the marked coefficient equations. To detect leaves one needs a new horizontal implication placing the Frobenius conjugacy class in a proper determinantal locus. No such implication is banked.

2. **Marked rank-jump object.** Evaluation maps for the two lower zeros are inserted so that cohomology or a kernel appears at leaves. Then the object is vertical torsion at those primes; its Fitting divisor contains their product. Its support detector is the fixed-gcd/radical object.

3. **Direct \(q\)-base change.** The lower prime is a unit, the marked rows split, and the lower support disappears. The detector is upper-only.

4. **Semilocal lower-characteristic product.** The detector is exact componentwise, but the components are orthogonal and the total defect is additive, equal to or at least the leaf count.

5. **Auxiliary characteristic.** Detection requires an unproved horizontal \(p\)-to-\(\lambda\) transport; an effective determinant container is root descent in disguise.

#### Proof

The q-local and semilocal assertions are the unit-pivot and orthogonal-idempotent calculations of Section 7. A generically acyclic finite-presentation complex whose special fibre has excess cohomology at \(p\) has \(p\) in the support of its determinant/Fitting ideal; multiplying over distinct primes gives (7.3). A flat/lisse object has no such rank-jump support, so the leaf must instead be expressed as a conjugacy-class condition, which the unmarked Apéry Frobenius does not contain. The auxiliary case is the previously proved effective-class-law/root-descent equivalence. ∎

This theorem allows arbitrary growth in the number of rows and matrix dimensions. It does not depend on the bounded-transfer impossibility theorem.

## 11. What a genuinely new horizontal detector would have to add

An actually new object would have to avoid both sides of the dichotomy:

- it cannot insert the exact leaf indicator, or the existence/sparsity statement collapses;
- it cannot be the fixed-gcd torsion scheme, or sparsity is the restricted-radical theorem;
- it cannot live only at \(q\), because of unit erasure;
- it cannot be a separate family of unrelated \(p\)-adic modules, because their defects add;
- it cannot merely give explicit auxiliary residue containers, because that is effective root descent.

The first genuinely new premise would therefore have to be a **support-independent horizontal Frobenius implication** of the following form.

For each actual selected state \(x\), construct before testing the lower zeros:

1. a fixed finite-type arithmetic parameter space carrying the candidate prime/row data;
2. a compatible Frobenius object of controlled rank and global complexity;
3. a proper conjugacy-invariant determinantal locus \(\mathcal D_x\);

   \Longrightarrow
   \operatorname{Frob}_{p,x}\in\mathcal D_x;

The nontrivial datum is (11.1), not the determinant. None of the current vertical theories supplies it. If the construction is made by adding the lower evaluation rows to a perfect complex, it falls back to the Fitting case. If it is made by a polynomial in \(pmod q\), it is CQRD. If it is made modulo auxiliary \(\lambda\), it is the effective auxiliary law.

I do not prove that no entirely new horizontal identity of type (11.1) can exist. Proving such universal nonexistence would itself require a theorem about the actual Apéry family beyond the bank. ## 13. Adversarial audit

### 13.1 Actual-selected normalization

All pointwise and summed inequalities use \(L_\sigma(x;p)\), not a geometric prime shell, an upper-mark-free overcarrier, or a raw two-row surrogate. The upper mark \(q\mid b_t\) is imposed before the fibre is counted and is not charged repeatedly.

### 13.2 Minus-first assignment

Formula (2.10) retains the exact assignment. The support-blind scheme for plus may include a prime that the minus-first rule assigns to minus; this is an allowed false positive and is why the pre-support detector is strictly stronger than the actual count. The exact detector uses (2.10) and does not double-count it.

### 13.3 Conjunction versus disjunction

A single diagonal determinant \(uv\) detects \(u=0\) or \(v=0\), not both. The Koszul and norm constructions repair this. No argument below uses the incorrect determinant.

### 13.4 Genuine Frobenius invertibility

I never claim \(\det\operatorname{Frob}=0\) for an \(F\)-isocrystal. The spectral detector uses \(\det(1-F)\); the raw marked detector uses a singular multiplication map composed with invertible Frobenius.

### 13.5 Same-characteristic versus cross-characteristic transfer

The exact marked detector is local at \(p\). The upper mark is an external Boolean selection. I do not reduce a \(p\)-zero to a \(q\)-zero or an auxiliary-\(\lambda\) zero. The q-local and auxiliary failures are stated explicitly.

### 13.6 Multiplicity

The reduced special fibre of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z/G\) counts a numerical prime once, independently of \(v_p(G)\). This is the correct restricted-radical multiplicity. Typed presentations of one prime are not counted twice.

### 13.7 Fixed-ledger and q-primary normalization

The ideal is saturated away from \(qE_\sigma\). Every admissible lower prime is comparable with \(T\) and lies outside the finite fixed ledger for sufficiently large \(T\), so this normalization preserves relevant shell support.

### 13.8 Fixed-gcd height

The current repository correction is respected. I use only the conditional divisibility floor

\[
\prod_{p\in S}p\mid G_\sigma
\]

when \(S\) is a set of actual/supporting primes. I do not assert a uniform lower bound \(\log G_\sigma\asymp q\). The unconditional general statement remains \(\log G_\sigma=O(q)\), and the actual carrier may be tiny.

### 13.9 Minus M5 height law

No uniform \(\Theta(T)\) minus-edge height claim is used. The M5/M8 distinction is irrelevant to the support/Frobenius collapse theorem. The proof neither multiplies edge permanents nor uses their Archimedean heights.

### 13.10 Finite computation

The empty scans through \(5\cdot10^5\) and \(10^6\), and the fixed-gcd census, are not used as premises. They supply no ratio estimate when the actual mass is zero.

### 13.11 Prime-AP and band rank

No AP-length statement is converted into a count of disjoint pairs, and no large support rank is converted into rarity. The determinant support theorem is pointwise in the actual fibre.

### 13.12 Quantifier check

The useful premise must hold for every actual selected state/sign/cell, or in a weighted exceptional form whose loss is explicitly charged against actual marked mass. A statement that “for most unselected row keys” the detector is sparse does not imply (12.2).

### 13.13 Equivalence check

The exact detector existence statement is equivalent to the target because the local unit-root construction realizes the indicator. The fixed support-scheme statement is not equivalent: it can have false positives. This is the precise boundary between the collapsed and noncollapsed formulations.

### 13.14 No universal impossibility overclaim

The argument closes the recurrence-algebraic, finite-presentation, common-\(q\), effective auxiliary, and separate-local determinant routes. It does not prove that the actual Apéry sequence admits no future horizontal compatible-family theorem of type (11.1).
