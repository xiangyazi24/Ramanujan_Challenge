# Q8501 — Derivative content attack report

## Status

Terminal condition reached: named blocker with an exact obstruction to the proposed simultaneous Frobenius-support route. No new dense identity or height inequality proving `log c_n=o(n)` was verified.

## Claim investigated

The target is to control the content
\[
 c_n=\operatorname{cont}\left(\sum_{k=1}^n (L_n/k)b_{n-k}X^k\right).
\]
For primes larger than `sqrt(n)`, the proposed mechanism would require simultaneous Frobenius sparsity of the primitive quotient to force a small content contribution.

The exact missing implication is stronger than individual congruences: one needs a dense family of coefficient relations valid for all large primes simultaneously, with height loss sublinear in `n`.

## Attempted mechanism: Frobenius support

Suppose a prime `p>sqrt(n)` does not divide `c_n`. Then after division by the primitive content, the reduction modulo `p` is Frobenius-sparse:

\[
Q_n(X)\bmod p \in \mathbf F_p[X^p].
\]

Equivalently, every coefficient whose exponent is not divisible by `p` vanishes modulo `p`.

For the Apéry coefficient carrier this would require, for every residue class `j` with `p\nmid j`,

\[
[X^j]Q_n(X)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The attempted dense route would need to convert these simultaneous vanishing conditions into a bound on the gcd of the coefficient set.

## Exact obstruction found

The Frobenius condition alone only sees the coefficient support after a prime is fixed. It does not provide a uniform integer height inequality. The reduction map loses the information needed to estimate the product of all exceptional primes.

A resultant argument with a sparse polynomial carrier also fails without an additional lower bound: the resultant may contain the required moving prime factors with linear logarithmic height because the degree of the carrier is `n`.

Thus the implication

\[
\text{many Frobenius-zero coefficients} \Longrightarrow \log c_n=o(n)
\]

is not derivable from support sparsity alone.

## Apéry-specific checks

Known locked rows are compatible with the obstruction:

* `p=11,r=5` gives a genuine moving-prime divisibility row and is not excluded by Frobenius support.
* The transverse rows `(17,13)` and `(2237,492)` similarly show that the exceptional primes are not confined to a fixed finite set or a single residue family.

Therefore any proposed mechanism predicting absence of such rows from support constraints is falsified by these examples.

## Smallest missing lemma

A sufficient new theorem would have to be an Apéry-specific dense height statement:

> For every `n`, the integer coefficient vector of `P_n` has primitive quotient height satisfying
> \[
> \sum_{p>\sqrt n}v_p(c_n)\log p=o(n).
> \]
> Equivalently, every prime divisor contribution from the moving rows must admit a simultaneous global cancellation bound coming from the Apéry recurrence or its modular parametrization.

This is not a reformulation of `c_n cont(Q'_n)=L_n`; it is precisely the missing quantitative height estimate.

## Residual

No checked dense identity, root-of-unity evaluation identity, resultant inequality, or coefficient reciprocity argument yielding the required sublinear bound was obtained.

The derivative-content route remains blocked at the absence of an every-`n` global height inequality connecting the modular Frobenius conditions to the integer content.
