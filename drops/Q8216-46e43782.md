# Q8216 — selector-preserving fourth-order Fourier/dispersion audit

## Executive verdict

The stated bound

\[
|U_4|\ll X^{2+o(1)}\Lambda^5
\]

is **not** supplied by any of the cited theorems.  The obstruction occurs
strictly before the usually proposed five-prime Poisson model:

* with the original high-load selector
  \(w=1_{\{1+B\ge L\}}\) retained, its finite Fourier transform has the
  **full product-modulus bandwidth**; there is no legitimate restriction to
  dual frequencies \(|h|\ll P/M\asymp X^3\);
* the exact chart-centred expansion has sixteen Fourier sectors.  The
  zero- and one-oscillation sectors can be bounded now, using only the proved
  second factorial moment and the current bound for \(\Lambda\);
* the **first unresolved exact sector** has two oscillatory prime packets and
  two chart-discrepancy factors.  Its precise missing estimate is
  \((\mathrm{SP2})\) below;
* a sharp singleton-selector construction saturates that sector and violates
  the desired generic bound by \(X/L\).  This construction is not an Apéry
  counterexample; it proves that an argument using only sparse congruence
  selectors, cardinalities, Cauchy, or diagonal packing cannot work.  A
  genuinely Apéry-specific property of the coefficient-zero sets is required;
* after **removing** \(w\), one does obtain the familiar five-prime reciprocal
  phases with \(P\asymp X^5\), \(M=X^2\), and
  \(|h|\ll X^{3+o(1)}\).  In that downstream model, the zero frequency and
  every frequency divisible by at least three of the five packet primes are
  already at the target scale.  The first open imprimitive sector is the
  exact two-divisor inequality \((\mathrm{D2})\), which needs one factor
  \(X\) of cancellation.

Thus this answer does not claim closure.  It does prove two strictly smaller,
noncircular analytic statements with verified exponents:

1. **selector-preserving:** all exact sectors with zero or one oscillatory
   packet contribute \(O(X^{2+o(1)}\Lambda^5)\);
2. **unrestricted five-prime model:** the zero mode and all modes divisible by
   at least three packet primes contribute
   \(O(X^{2+o(1)}\Lambda^5)\).

The theorem audit is decisive.  Deshouillers--Iwaniec/Kuznetsov requires
complete Kloosterman structure and smooth or separated coefficients;
Bettin--Chandee is quantitatively far too weak even after a fictitious
rank-one separation; Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel requires a one-field,
bounded-conductor, nonexceptional trace weight; Bah--Shuddhodan concerns
bounded-complexity perverse sheaves and averages over multiplicative
characters with common tannakian monodromy.  None matches the exact
selector-preserving object.

## 1. Source scope and notation

I use the current repository definitions from
[`atom_tail_section.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/734a5a84c1e4fd8703a811aadaa2b4c7f532b20e/problems/3.2/atom_tail_section.tex)

together with the exact reduction stated in the question.

Put

\[
M=X^2,
\qquad
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},
\]

\[
I_p=\{r\in[0,p):p\mid b_r\},
\qquad
Z(p)=|I_p|,
\qquad
z_p=\frac{Z(p)}p,
\]

and

\[
K(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}1_{\{m\bmod p\in I_p\}},
\qquad
\Lambda=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}z_p.
\]

The current proved inputs used below are

\[
\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_2\le 5M\Lambda^2
\tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
\Lambda\ll \frac{X^{3/5}}{\log X}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Write

\[
L=X^{14/15+\eta};
\]

integer thresholds may be replaced by \(\lceil L\rceil\).  The only
nonvacuous asymptotic regime is \(0<\eta<1/15\): when
\(L>|\mathcal P_X|+1\), the selector is eventually zero.

For a base prime \(p\), a base root \(r\in I_p\), and

\[
m=pa+r<M,
\]

the chart is

\[
\mathcal A_{p,r}=\{0,1,\ldots,N_{p,r}-1\},
\qquad
N_{p,r}=\left\lfloor\frac{M-1-r}{p}\right\rfloor+1.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Uniformly on the dyadic range,

\[
N_{p,r}\asymp X,
\qquad
N_{p,r}<q\quad(q\in\mathcal P_X,\ q\ne p).
\tag{1.4}
\]

For \(q\ne p\), define on this chart

\[
f_q(a)=1_{\{pa+r\bmod q\in I_q\}},
\qquad
\mu_q=\frac1N\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}f_q(a),
\qquad
 g_q=f_q-\mu_q,
\tag{1.5}
\]

where \(N=N_{p,r}\).  Also

\[
B(a)=\sum_{q\ne p}f_q(a),
\qquad
w(a)=1_{\{1+B(a)\ge L\}}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Because the base prime is already selected,

\[
1+B(a)=K(pa+r),
\qquad
w(a)=1_{\{K(pa+r)\ge L\}}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

The exact object supplied in the question is

\[
U_4=24\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\sum_{r\in I_p}
\sum_{a=0}^{N_{p,r}-1}w(a)
 e_4\bigl((g_q(a))_{q\ne p}\bigr).
\tag{1.8}
\]

No step below changes this selector or replaces \(\mu_q\) by \(z_q\).

## 2. Exact chart Fourier expansion

Let

\[
e_q(x)=e^{2\pi i x/q},
\qquad
\bar p_qp\equiv1\pmod q.
\]

For \(c\bmod q\), define the oriented root packet

\[
\mathcal A_{q;p,r}(c)
 =\sum_{\rho\in I_q}
 e_q\!\left(c\bar p_q(r-\rho)\right).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The sign and the factor \(\bar p_q\) are forced by

\[
pa+r\equiv\rho\pmod q.
\]

Orthogonality gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
 f_q(a)=\frac1q\sum_{c\bmod q}
 \mathcal A_{q;p,r}(c)e_q(ca).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Define the normalized chart Dirichlet kernel

\[
d_{N,q}(c)=\frac1N\sum_{b=0}^{N-1}e_q(cb).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Averaging (2.2) over the literal chart gives

\[
\boxed{
\mu_q=\frac1q\sum_{c\bmod q}
\mathcal A_{q;p,r}(c)d_{N,q}(c).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Since

\[
\mathcal A_{q;p,r}(0)=Z(q),
\qquad
d_{N,q}(0)=1,
\]

the zero frequency cancels exactly in \(g_q=f_q-\mu_q\):

\[
\boxed{
 g_q(a)=\frac1q\sum_{\substack{c\bmod q\\c\ne0}}
 \mathcal A_{q;p,r}(c)
 \bigl(e_q(ca)-d_{N,q}(c)\bigr).}
\tag{2.5}
\]

This is the required chart-centred Fourier formula.  In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\mu_q-z_q
=\frac1q\sum_{c\ne0}
\mathcal A_{q;p,r}(c)d_{N,q}(c).}
\tag{2.6}
\]

There is no pointwise justification for replacing \(\mu_q\) by \(z_q\).
For example, a singleton residue class missing the chart has \(\mu_q=0\)
but \(z_q=1/q\).

Two exact Parseval identities will be useful:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{c\bmod q}|\mathcal A_{q;p,r}(c)|^2=qZ(q),}
\tag{2.7}
\]

and, because \(N<q\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{c\bmod q}|d_{N,q}(c)|^2=\frac qN.}
\tag{2.8}
\]

They are identities, not square-root estimates for individual packets.

### 2.1 Global-centred plus chart-discrepancy form

It is convenient to set

\[
h_q(a)=f_q(a)-z_q,
\qquad
\delta_{q;p,r}=\mu_q-z_q.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{g_q(a)=h_q(a)-\delta_{q;p,r},}
\tag{2.10}
\]

and (2.2) gives

\[
\boxed{
 h_q(a)=\frac1q\sum_{c\ne0}
 \mathcal A_{q;p,r}(c)e_q(ca),}
\tag{2.11}
\]

while (2.6) is the corresponding nonoscillatory chart correction.

## 3. Ordered distinct-prime expansion: all sixteen sectors

Since \(24e_4\) is the sum over ordered four-tuples of pairwise distinct
indices,

\[
\boxed{
U_4=
\sum_{p,r}
\sum_{\substack{q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p\}\\
q_i\text{ pairwise distinct}}}
\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}w(a)
\prod_{i=1}^4g_{q_i}(a).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This keeps all five primes \(p,q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4\) distinct.

For a chart, define its selected Fourier transform

\[
W_{p,r}(\theta)=\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}w(a)e(a\theta),
\qquad e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For \(T\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}\), put

\[
\theta_T(\mathbf c)=\sum_{i\in T}\frac{c_i}{q_i}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Expanding every bracket in (2.5) gives

\[
\boxed{U_4=\sum_{T\subseteq[4]}U_T,}
\tag{3.4}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
U_T={}&(-1)^{4-|T|}
\sum_{p,r}
\sum_{\mathbf q}^{\mathrm{ord},\ne,p}
\frac1{q_1q_2q_3q_4}
\sum_{\substack{c_i\bmod q_i\\c_i\ne0}}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^4\mathcal A_{q_i;p,r}(c_i)\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times
\left(\prod_{i\notin T}d_{N,q_i}(c_i)\right)
W_{p,r}\!\left(\theta_T(\mathbf c)\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Here and below
\(\sum^{\mathrm{ord},\ne,p}\) means ordered, pairwise distinct primes, all
different from \(p\).

Equivalently, by (2.9)--(2.11),

\[
\boxed{
U_T=(-1)^{4-|T|}
\sum_{p,r}\sum_{\mathbf q}^{\mathrm{ord},\ne,p}
\left(\prod_{i\notin T}\delta_{q_i;p,r}\right)
\sum_a w(a)\prod_{i\in T}h_{q_i}(a).}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Formula (3.5) is the exact Fourier decomposition requested in the question.
Three cautions are immediate.

1. The sector \(T=\varnothing\) contains \(W_{p,r}(0)=\sum_aw(a)\); it does
   not vanish.
2. The fifteen chart-centering corrections are part of the exact object.
   Keeping only \(T=[4]\) changes \(U_4\).
3. Bounds for the sectors separately are a sufficient route, not a logically
   necessary one: the exact sum may contain cross-sector cancellation.

## 4. A proved selector-preserving theorem: the zero- and one-wave sectors

This section gives the first new noncircular result.

### Lemma 4.1 — chart discrepancy

Uniformly in \((p,r)\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{q\ne p}|\delta_{q;p,r}|\ll\Lambda.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Because \(N<q\), the map

\[
a\longmapsto pa+r\pmod q
\]

is injective on the chart.  Therefore

\[
\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}f_q(a)\le Z(q),
\qquad
0\le\mu_q\le\frac{Z(q)}N.
\]

Since \(N\asymp X\) and \(q\asymp X\),

\[
|\delta_q|\le\mu_q+z_q\ll\frac{Z(q)}X.
\]

Finally,

\[
\sum_{q\in\mathcal P_X}Z(q)\asymp
X\sum_{q\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(q)}q=X\Lambda,
\]

which proves (4.1).  \(\square\)

For \(m=pa+r\), set

\[
D_{p,r}=\sum_{q\ne p}|\delta_{q;p,r}|,
\qquad
H_{p,r}(a)=\sum_{q\ne p}|h_q(a)|.
\]

Then

\[
D_{p,r}\ll\Lambda,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
H_{p,r}(a)\le B(a)+\Lambda=K(m)-1+\Lambda.
\tag{4.3}
\]

On the support of \(w\), (1.2) and \(L=X^{14/15+\eta}\) imply, for large
\(X\),

\[
\Lambda=o(L)\le K(m),
\qquad
H_{p,r}(a)\ll K(m).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Dropping distinctness only increases the absolute majorant in (3.6), so for
\(t=|T|\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|T|=t}|U_T|
\ll
\Lambda^{4-t}
\sum_{m<M}1_{\{K(m)\ge L\}}K(m)^{t+1}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The extra factor \(K(m)\) occurs because every physical \(m\) belongs to
exactly \(K(m)\) selected base charts \((p,r)\).

### Theorem 4.2 — exact low-wave closure

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|T|\le1}|U_T|
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\Lambda^5.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

#### Proof: zero waves

For \(K\ge L\ge2\),

\[
K\le\frac{(K)_2}{L-1}.
\]

Thus (4.5) with \(t=0\), followed by (1.1), gives

\[
|U_\varnothing|
\ll\Lambda^4\frac1{L-1}\sum_m(K(m))_2
\ll\frac{X^2\Lambda^6}{L}.
\]

Since \(\Lambda=o(L)\), this is
\(O(X^2\Lambda^5)\).

#### Proof: one wave

For \(K\ge L\ge2\),

\[
K^2\le2(K)_2.
\]

Therefore (4.5) with \(t=1\) and (1.1) give

\[
\sum_{|T|=1}|U_T|
\ll\Lambda^3\sum_{K(m)\ge L}K(m)^2
\ll\Lambda^3\sum_m(K(m))_2
\ll X^2\Lambda^5.
\]

This proves (4.6).  \(\square\)

This theorem retains the original selector, the literal chart means, every
orientation, and distinct-prime bookkeeping.  It is not a finite scan and it
does not use a conjectural trace-function estimate.

## 5. The first unresolved exact sector: two oscillatory packets

Let

\[
U^{[2]}=\sum_{|T|=2}U_T.
\]

For ordered distinct \(q_1,q_2\ne p\), define the exact complementary
chart-discrepancy coefficient

\[
\Delta^{(2)}_{p,r}(q_1,q_2)=
\sum_{\substack{q_3,q_4\in\mathcal P_X\setminus
\{p,q_1,q_2\}\\q_3\ne q_4}}^{\mathrm{ord}}
\delta_{q_3;p,r}\delta_{q_4;p,r}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

There are \(\binom42=6\) interleavings of the ordered oscillatory and
nonoscillatory pairs.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
U^{[2]}=6\sum_{p,r}
\sum_{\substack{q_1,q_2\ne p\\q_1\ne q_2}}^{\mathrm{ord}}
\Delta^{(2)}_{p,r}(q_1,q_2)
\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}w(a)h_{q_1}(a)h_{q_2}(a).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Using (2.11), this is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
U^{[2]}={}&6\sum_{p,r}
\sum_{q_1\ne q_2}^{\mathrm{ord},\ne,p}
\frac{\Delta^{(2)}_{p,r}(q_1,q_2)}{q_1q_2}
\sum_{\substack{c_1\bmod q_1,\ c_2\bmod q_2\\c_1c_2\ne0}}
\mathcal A_{q_1;p,r}(c_1)
\mathcal A_{q_2;p,r}(c_2)\\
&\hspace{42mm}\times
W_{p,r}\!\left(\frac{c_1}{q_1}+\frac{c_2}{q_2}\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The first exact missing inequality in the sector-by-sector route is therefore

\[
\boxed{
|U^{[2]}|\ll X^{2+o(1)}\Lambda^5.}
\qquad(\mathrm{SP2})
\tag{5.4}
\]

The absolute estimate supplied by (4.5) is only

\[
|U^{[2]}|\ll
\Lambda^2\sum_{K(m)\ge L}K(m)^3.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Inserting the desired third high-load moment into (5.5) would merely replace
this problem by the atom-tail problem at lower order.  That is circular, not
an analytic proof of \((\mathrm{SP2})\).

The sectors with three and four oscillatory packets remain after
\((\mathrm{SP2})\).  Thus \((\mathrm{SP2})\) is the first gate, not by itself a
closure of \(U_4\).

### 5.1 Sharp structural countermodel to a generic \((\mathrm{SP2})\)

The estimate \((\mathrm{SP2})\) is false for arbitrary sparse congruence
selectors, even when their second factorial moment has exactly the allowed
scale.

Choose

\[
R=\lceil L\rceil
\]

primes in \((X,2X]\), one base prime \(p\) near \(X\), and the others in a
fixed subinterval near \(2X\).  Choose one integer \(m_0<M\) and put

\[
I_\ell=\{m_0\bmod\ell\}
\]

for these \(R\) primes, with all other selector sets empty.

Because the product of any two selected primes exceeds \(M=X^2\), two of the
selected congruences have only the common point \(m_0\) inside \([0,M)\).
Consequently

\[
K(m_0)=R,
\qquad
K(m)\le1\quad(m\ne m_0),
\]

so the high-load selector is exactly the singleton \(1_{\{m=m_0\}}\).
Moreover,

\[
\Lambda\asymp\frac RX,
\qquad
\sum_m(K(m))_2=R(R-1)\asymp X^2\Lambda^2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

On the base chart write \(m_0=pa_0+r\).  For every other selected prime
\(q\), injectivity of the chart map gives

\[
f_q(a)=1_{\{a=a_0\}},
\qquad
\mu_q=\frac1N,
\qquad
z_q=\frac1q.
\]

With \(p\) near \(X\) and \(q\) near \(2X\),

\[
\delta_q=\frac1N-\frac1q\asymp\frac1X,
\qquad
h_q(a_0)=1-\frac1q\asymp1.
\tag{5.7}
\]

All relevant terms in (5.2) have the same sign.  The single base-chart
contribution is therefore

\[
|U^{[2]}|\gg
R^2\cdot\frac{R^2}{X^2}\asymp\frac{R^4}{X^2}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

The desired generic right side would be

\[
X^2\Lambda^5\asymp\frac{R^5}{X^3}.
\]

Their ratio is

\[
\frac{X}{R}\asymp\frac XL=X^{1/15-\eta},
\tag{5.9}
\]

which diverges throughout the nonvacuous range \(0<\eta<1/15\).

This is a **structural countermodel**, not a counterexample for the actual
Apéry sets.  Its exact conclusion is important: no theorem that sees only
congruence-set cardinalities, the second factorial moment, distinctness, and
the threshold shape can prove \((\mathrm{SP2})\).  It must use arithmetic
specific to the true coefficient-zero sets \(I_p\).

## 6. Exact dispersion, orientations, and the diagonal no-go

For two chart frequencies,

\[
W(\theta)\overline{W(\theta')}
=\sum_{a,b=0}^{N-1}w(a)w(b)e(a\theta-b\theta').
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus a dispersion square always has both orientations.  If

\[
\theta=\sum_{i\in T}\frac{c_i}{q_i},
\qquad
\theta'=\sum_{j\in T'}\frac{c'_j}{q'_j},
\]

the phase is

\[
\boxed{
\Theta=\sum_{i\in T}\frac{c_i}{q_i}
-
\sum_{j\in T'}\frac{c'_j}{q'_j}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Replacing this by an all-plus phase changes the diagonal and is invalid.

### Lemma 6.1 — exact collision condition

Let \(Q\) be the product of the distinct primes occurring in (6.2).  Then
\(\Theta\in\mathbb Z\) if and only if, for every prime \(\ell\mid Q\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i\in T:q_i=\ell}c_i
-
\sum_{j\in T':q'_j=\ell}c'_j
\equiv0\pmod\ell.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

Multiply (6.2) by \(Q\) and reduce modulo each \(\ell\).  Every term whose
denominator is not \(\ell\) vanishes modulo \(\ell\), while
\(Q/\ell\) is a unit.  \(\square\)

For the full four-wave sector, (6.3) forces equality of the two prime
supports and equality of the matched nonzero frequencies, up to permutation.
For lower-wave sectors, only the oscillatory prime supports must match.  The
nonoscillatory prime labels remain free, so their diagonal is substantially
larger.

This diagonal description is exact, but a count of it is not a bound for the
off-diagonal contribution.

### 6.1 Fixed-tuple additive Parseval gives no prime saving

For a fixed ordered tuple \(q_1,\ldots,q_t\), CRT identifies
\((c_1,\ldots,c_t)\) with one frequency modulo
\(Q_T=\prod_{i\in T}q_i\).  Since \(N<Q_T\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{c_i\bmod q_i}
\left|W\!\left(\sum_i\frac{c_i}{q_i}\right)\right|^2
=Q_T\sum_a w(a).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Together with (2.7), Cauchy gives

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\frac1{Q_T}\sum_{c_i}
\left(\prod_i\mathcal A_{q_i;p,r}(c_i)\right)
W\!\left(\sum_i\frac{c_i}{q_i}\right)
\right|
\le
\left(\prod_iZ(q_i)\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_aw(a)\right)^{1/2}.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

There is no power of any \(q_i\) on the right.  This is exactly where the
generic additive large sieve runs out of strength.

The singleton construction in §5.1 saturates the geometry behind (6.5).  In
that model

\[
\mathcal A_{q;p,r}(c)=e_q(-ca_0),
\qquad
W(\theta)=e(a_0\theta),
\]

and therefore

\[
\prod_i\mathcal A_{q_i;p,r}(c_i)
W\!\left(\sum_i\frac{c_i}{q_i}\right)=1
\tag{6.6}
\]

for every nonzero frequency tuple.  All off-diagonal frequencies are
coherent.  A diagonal packing estimate cannot control this example.

## 7. Why the five-prime Poisson range is not available with \(w\)

Take five distinct primes

\[
\mathbf p=(p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4),
\qquad
P=\prod_{i=0}^4p_i\asymp X^5,
\]

and roots \(\rho_i\in I_{p_i}\).  Let
\(a_{\boldsymbol\rho}\bmod P\) be their CRT solution.  For any weight
\(F:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C\), finite Fourier inversion gives the exact
identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\equiv a_{\boldsymbol\rho}\ (P)}F(m)
=\frac1P\sum_{t\bmod P}
\widehat F_P(t)e_P(-ta_{\boldsymbol\rho}),}
\tag{7.1}
\]

where

\[
\widehat F_P(t)=\sum_mF(m)e_P(tm).
\tag{7.2}
\]

If

\[
F(m)=w(m)V(m/M),
\tag{7.3}
\]

then (7.1) is the exact selector-preserving completion.  There is no general
decay statement for \(\widehat F_P(t)\).

If, and only if, \(w\) is removed, Poisson summation gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_mV(m/M)e_P(tm)
=M\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
\widehat V\!\left(M\left(k-\frac tP\right)\right),}
\tag{7.4}
\]

where

\[
\widehat V(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}V(x)e(-\xi x)\,dx.
\]

Smooth decay then localizes a representative frequency to

\[
|h|\ll\frac PMX^{o(1)}=X^{3+o(1)}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Equation (7.5) is therefore a fact about the **unrestricted smooth count**,
not about the exact \(U_4\).

### 7.1 Full-band countermodel

For the singleton high-load chart of §5.1,

\[
w(a)=1_{\{a=a_0\}}.
\]

Consequently its finite transform is

\[
W(\theta)=e(a_0\theta),
\qquad
|W(\theta)|=1
\tag{7.6}
\]

at every frequency.  In the product-modulus formulation,
\(|\widehat F_P(t)|\) is likewise constant up to the fixed smooth value
\(|V(m_0/M)|\).  The bandwidth is \(P\), not \(P/M\).

Thus any step that keeps the original selector but immediately truncates at
\(|h|\ll X^3\) is false.

### 7.2 The threshold has full multilinear degree

There is also no exact bounded-order factorization of \(w\).  For binary
variables \(x_q=f_q(a)\), put \(B=\sum_qx_q\) and let \(K_0\) be an integer
threshold.  The unique multilinear polynomial representing the threshold is

\[
\boxed{
1_{\{B\ge K_0\}}
=
\sum_{j=K_0}^{R}
(-1)^{j-K_0}\binom{j-1}{K_0-1}
 e_j((x_q)_q),}
\tag{7.7}
\]

where \(R\) is the number of available variables.  Its top coefficient is
nonzero.  Hence its exact degree is \(R\), and its first nonzero homogeneous
part already has degree \(K_0\asymp L\).

Expanding (7.7) therefore replaces the fourth-order problem by an
\(L\)-th-and-higher-order problem.  It is circular.  Removing low
Kummer-order rows while retaining the original selector does not alter this
fact: the retained threshold remains a high-degree Boolean function of the
retained congruence data.

## 8. The clean unrestricted five-prime decomposition

This section records what is true after deleting \(w\).  It is a downstream
model, not an identity for \(U_4\).

For a smooth compactly supported \(V\), Poisson summation in the progression
\(m\equiv a_{\boldsymbol\rho}\pmod P\) gives, after a harmless change
\(h\mapsto-h\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\equiv a_{\boldsymbol\rho}\ (P)}V(m/M)
=
\frac MP\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}
\widehat V\!\left(\frac{hM}{P}\right)
\prod_{i=0}^4
 e_{p_i}\!\left(h\rho_i\overline{P/p_i}\right).}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Define the actual selector packet

\[
\mathcal S_p(t)=\sum_{\rho\in I_p}e_p(t\rho).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Summing (8.1) over the literal roots gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr C_5=
\sum_{\mathbf p}^{\mathrm{ord},\ne}
\frac MP\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}
\widehat V\!\left(\frac{hM}{P}\right)
\prod_{i=0}^4
\mathcal S_{p_i}\!\left(h\overline{P/p_i}\right).}
\tag{8.3}
\]

The only universal packet facts used below are

\[
\mathcal S_p(0)=Z(p),
\tag{8.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{t\bmod p}|\mathcal S_p(t)|^2=pZ(p),}
\tag{8.5}
\]

and, because \(0\notin I_p\) for the Apéry sequence,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{t\bmod p}\mathcal S_p(t)=0.}
\tag{8.6}
\]

No pointwise square-root bound follows from these identities.

## 9. A proved theorem in the unrestricted model: high-imprimitive modes

For a nonzero \(h\) and a prime tuple \(\mathbf p\), let

\[
S(h,\mathbf p)=\{i:p_i\mid h\}.
\]

### Theorem 9.1

The zero mode in (8.3), together with all nonzero modes satisfying

\[
|S(h,\mathbf p)|\ge3,
\]

contributes

\[
\boxed{
\ll_{V,\varepsilon}X^{2+\varepsilon}\Lambda^5.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

This theorem keeps the actual sets \(I_p\) and the distinct-prime condition.
It uses no sheaf model.

#### Proof: zero mode

At \(h=0\),

\[
\left|\mathscr C_{5,0}\right|
\le
M|\widehat V(0)|
\sum_{\mathbf p}^{\mathrm{ord},\ne}
\prod_{i=0}^4\frac{Z(p_i)}{p_i}
\le M|\widehat V(0)|\Lambda^5.
\tag{9.2}
\]

#### Proof: nonzero high-imprimitive modes

Smooth decay permits a truncation

\[
|h|\le H X^\varepsilon,
\qquad
H\asymp\frac PM\asymp X^3,
\tag{9.3}
\]

with an error smaller than any negative power of \(X\).

Fix a subset \(S\subseteq\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) of size \(s\), and put

\[
d_S=\prod_{i\in S}p_i.
\]

The number of nonzero \(h\) in (9.3) divisible by \(d_S\) is

\[
\ll X^\varepsilon\left(\frac H{d_S}+1\right).
\tag{9.4}
\]

Use only

\[
|\mathcal S_p(t)|\le Z(p).
\]

The part coming from \(H/d_S\) is bounded by

\[
X^\varepsilon
\left(\sum_{p}\frac{Z(p)}p\right)^s
\left(\sum_pZ(p)\right)^{5-s}
\ll
X^{5-s+\varepsilon}\Lambda^5.
\tag{9.5}
\]

The `+1` in (9.4) contributes

\[
\ll X^\varepsilon M\Lambda^5
=X^{2+\varepsilon}\Lambda^5.
\tag{9.6}
\]

For \(s\ge3\), (9.5) is also at most
\(X^{2+\varepsilon}\Lambda^5\).  Summing over the finitely many subsets
proves (9.1).  \(\square\)

The corresponding absolute scales are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
|S(h,\mathbf p)|&\text{elementary bound}\hline
0&X^{5+o(1)}\Lambda^5\\
1&X^{4+o(1)}\Lambda^5\\
2&X^{3+o(1)}\Lambda^5\\
\ge3&X^{2+o(1)}\Lambda^5.
\end{array}
\tag{9.7}
\]

The table is an exponent ledger, not a claim that diagonal packing closes the
remaining sectors.

## 10. The first open imprimitive sector in the clean model

Suppose, for definiteness, that \(p_1,p_2\mid h\) and that the other three
packet primes do not divide \(h\).  Write

\[
h=p_1p_2k,
\qquad
P_R=p_3p_4p_5.
\]

For \(j=3,4,5\),

\[
h\overline{P/p_j}
\equiv
k\overline{P_R/p_j}\pmod{p_j}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Thus the exact two-divisor sector is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr C_{2,\{1,2\}}={}&
\sum_{\mathbf p}^{\mathrm{ord},\ne}
\frac{MZ(p_1)Z(p_2)}{p_1p_2P_R}\\
&\times
\sum_{\substack{k\ne0\\(k,P_R)=1}}
\widehat V\!\left(\frac{kM}{P_R}\right)
\prod_{j=3}^5
\mathcal S_{p_j}\!\left(k\overline{P_R/p_j}\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{10.2}
\]

Smooth decay restricts \(|k|\ll X^{1+o(1)}\).  The missing inequality is

\[
\boxed{
|\mathscr C_{2,\{1,2\}}|
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\Lambda^5.}
\qquad(\mathrm{D2})
\tag{10.3}
\]

The trivial bound is

\[
X^{3+o(1)}\Lambda^5,
\tag{10.4}
\]

so \((\mathrm{D2})\) needs one full factor \(X\).

### 10.1 CRT root form

Expanding the three packets gives

\[
\prod_{j=3}^5
\mathcal S_{p_j}\!\left(k\overline{P_R/p_j}\right)
=
\sum_{\rho_j\in I_{p_j}}
 e\!\left(\frac{k\,a_R(\rho_3,\rho_4,\rho_5)}{P_R}\right),
\tag{10.5}
\]

where \(a_R\bmod P_R\) is the CRT integer with

\[
a_R\equiv\rho_j\pmod{p_j}.
\]

The physical dual interval has length \(X\), while its modulus is
\(P_R\asymp X^3\).  Generic oscillation is not forced.

For the structural singleton family

\[
I_p=\{1\},
\]

one has \(a_R=1\), so

\[
e\!\left(\frac{k}{P_R}\right)=1+O(X^{-2})
\qquad(|k|\ll X).
\tag{10.6}
\]

The inner sum in (10.2) is then of order \(X\), with no cancellation.  Notice
that the individual packet

\[
\mathcal S_p(t)=e_p(t)
\]

is itself a bounded-conductor Artin--Schreier trace function and has complete
mean zero.  Therefore even those two one-field properties do not imply the
required cross-prime joint dispersion.

Again, (10.6) is not a claim about actual Apéry roots.  It is a sharp
countermodel to any theorem stated for arbitrary sparse or arbitrary
bounded-conductor packet roots.

## 11. Audit of the proposed primary-source theorems

### 11.1 Deshouillers--Iwaniec and Kuznetsov

Primary source:

* J.-M. Deshouillers and H. Iwaniec,
  [“Kloosterman sums and Fourier coefficients of cusp forms,”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01390728)
  *Inventiones Mathematicae* **70** (1982), 219--288.

The Deshouillers--Iwaniec spectral large sieve controls averages of complete
classical Kloosterman sums obtained through the Kuznetsov formula.  Its most
effective forms require smooth or specially structured weights in the
modulus and Fourier variables.

Neither (5.3) nor the selector-preserving version of (7.1) has this shape:

* \(W_{p,r}\) is a nonsmooth Boolean threshold transform depending on the
  entire prime ensemble;
* the root packets are incomplete Fourier transforms of the literal sets
  \(I_q\), not complete Kloosterman sums;
* five squarefree prime moduli vary simultaneously;
* completion of the root variables would introduce the full complexity of
  the unknown zero selectors and does not manufacture a classical
  Kloosterman amplitude.

There is therefore no Deshouillers--Iwaniec theorem whose hypotheses can be
checked for \((\mathrm{SP2})\), the three-/four-wave sectors, or
\((\mathrm{D2})\).  Calling the reciprocal phases “Kloosterman-like” is not
an application of Kuznetsov.

### 11.2 Bettin--Chandee: quantitative exponent check

Primary source:

* S. Bettin and V. Chandee,
  [“Trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2018.01.026)
  *Advances in Mathematics* **328** (2018), 1234--1262;
  [arXiv:1502.00769](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00769), Theorem 1, equation
  (1.2).

Their theorem bounds a separated trilinear form of the shape

\[
\sum_{a,m,n}\nu_a\alpha_m\beta_n
 e\!\left(\vartheta\frac{a\bar m}{n}\right)
\]

by

\[
\|\nu\|_2\|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2
\left(1+\frac{|\vartheta|A}{MN}\right)^{1/2}
\left[
(AMN)^{7/20+\varepsilon}(M+N)^{1/4}
+(AMN)^{3/8+\varepsilon}(AN+AM)^{1/8}
\right].
\tag{11.1}
\]

Our coefficients and phases do not separate into this form.  Nevertheless,
the theorem is still far too weak even under a fictitious rank-one
separation.

#### Primitive five-prime sector

Group two primes into a variable of length \(M_1=X^2\), three primes into a
variable of length \(N_1=X^3\), and take the dual frequency length
\(A_1=X^3\).  Then

\[
A_1M_1N_1=X^8.
\]

The two brackets in (11.1) have sizes

\[
X^{56/20}X^{3/4}=X^{71/20},
\]

and

\[
X^3X^{6/8}=X^{15/4}.
\]

With unit-density supports, the three \(\ell^2\) norms contribute

\[
X^{3/2}\cdot X\cdot X^{3/2}=X^4.
\]

Thus the bound is

\[
X^{31/4+o(1)}=X^{7.75+o(1)},
\tag{11.2}
\]

against the trivial raw size \(X^8\): only an \(X^{1/4}\) saving.
After the progression factor \(M/P=X^{-3}\), the desired
\(X^2\) scale requires a raw bound \(X^5\), i.e. an \(X^3\) saving.
Bettin--Chandee misses by \(X^{11/4}\).

#### Two-divisor sector \((\mathrm{D2})\)

Under the same fictitious separation, use lengths

\[
A_1=X,
\qquad
M_1=X,
\qquad
N_1=X^2.
\]

Then the dominant bracket is

\[
(AMN)^{7/20}(M+N)^{1/4}
=X^{28/20}X^{1/2}=X^{19/10}.
\]

The product of support norms is \(X^2\), giving

\[
X^{39/10+o(1)}=X^{3.9+o(1)}
\tag{11.3}
\]

against raw size \(X^4\).  This saves only \(X^{1/10}\), whereas
\((\mathrm{D2})\) needs \(X\).  It misses by \(X^{9/10}\).

Therefore Bettin--Chandee is quantitatively insufficient even before the
fatal coefficient-separation issue is restored.

### 11.3 Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel

Primary source:

* É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, and P. Michel,
  [“Algebraic trace functions over the primes,”](https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2690587)
  *Duke Mathematical Journal* **163** (2014), 1683--1736;
  [arXiv:1211.6043v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6043v3), Definition 1.3 and
  Theorem 1.5.

Definition 1.3 requires an isotypic trace weight arising from a geometrically
isotypic, pointwise-pure \(\ell\)-adic Fourier sheaf over one field
\(\mathbb F_p\).  Theorem 1.5 assumes that the sheaf is nonexceptional, and
its implicit constant depends polynomially on the conductor.

No such uniform realization is known for

\[
1_{I_p}(r)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\mathcal S_p(t)=\sum_{r\in I_p}e_p(tr).
\]

The tautological direct-sum realization of \(\mathcal S_p\) has rank
\(Z(p)\), is generally not geometrically isotypic, and has complexity growing
with the zero set.  More importantly, the target is not a prime sum of a
fixed one-field trace weight: it is a short, jointly coupled path through
five varying prime fields, with reciprocal arguments depending on all five
primes, and in the exact problem it is multiplied by \(W_{p,r}\).

The hypotheses of Theorem 1.5 therefore fail before any exponent is
available.  The singleton example (10.6) also proves that bounded conductor
of each individual packet, by itself, would still not imply the needed
cross-prime cancellation.

### 11.4 Bah--Shuddhodan

Primary source:

* A. Bah and K. V. Shuddhodan,
  [“Uniform stratified vanishing and equidistribution on
  \(\mathbb G_m^d\),”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00304v2)
  arXiv:2608.00304v2 (7 August 2026), Theorems 1.1--1.3 and Corollary 1.4.

Theorems 1.1--1.3 are uniform point-count and cohomology-jump results for
complexes/perverse sheaves on \(\mathbb G_m^d\), with constants controlled by
Sawin complexity.  Corollary 1.4 assumes a sequence of bounded-complexity
objects, fixed tannakian dimension, and arithmetic/geometric monodromy groups
conjugate to one fixed reductive group.  It proves horizontal
equidistribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes indexed by the full family of
**multiplicative characters**.

Nothing in the current Apéry setup supplies

* a bounded-Sawin-complexity perverse sheaf whose trace is the literal zero
  selector;
* a common tannakian monodromy group;
* an identification of the additive reciprocal packets in (5.3) or (10.2)
  with the full multiplicative-character family;
* a short-path quantitative bound with the factor \(X\) required in
  \((\mathrm{D2})\);
* any control of the threshold transform \(W_{p,r}\).

Corollary 1.4 is an equidistribution theorem, not a selector-preserving
five-prime dispersion inequality.  It does not apply.

### 11.5 Bilinear trace-function hybrids

Two relevant primary references are

* E. Kowalski, P. Michel, and W. Sawin,
  [“Bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums and applications,”](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2017.186.2.2)
  *Annals of Mathematics* **186** (2017), 413--500;
* É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel, and W. Sawin,
  [“Bilinear forms with trace functions,”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459)
  arXiv:2511.09459 (2025).

These works obtain powerful bilinear estimates after one has a fixed
finite-field trace function satisfying explicit geometric-monodromy
hypotheses.  They do not create that structure for an arbitrary zero
selector, do not identify the five different characteristics with one field,
and do not absorb a Boolean threshold depending on the entire prime
ensemble.

Sequentially applying a one-field theorem is also invalid.  Once four prime
variables and \(w\) are fixed, the coefficient of the fifth field depends on
all of them through reciprocal inverses and through the selector.  Cauchy to
separate that dependence produces precisely the oriented collision problem
of §6 and the imprimitive sectors of §§9--10.  No cited hybrid supplies the
missing exponent.

## 12. Countermodel ledger for invalid analytic steps

The following statements are exact no-go results about proof methods.  None
is asserted for the actual Apéry selectors unless explicitly stated.

### 12.1 Invalid step: discard \(w\), keep the Poisson frequency range

Countermodel: the synchronized singleton selectors of §5.1 give
\(w=1_{\{a=a_0\}}\), whose Fourier transform has constant modulus at every
frequency.  The true bandwidth is the full product modulus.

### 12.2 Invalid step: Cauchy plus additive large sieve must save

Countermodel: for the same selectors,

\[
\mathcal A_q(c)=e_q(-ca_0),
\qquad
W(\theta)=e(a_0\theta),
\]

so every Fourier summand in (6.6) equals one.  Parseval/Cauchy is saturated;
there is no off-diagonal cancellation to recover.

### 12.3 Invalid step: count only the exact diagonal

Countermodel: (6.6) makes every off-diagonal frequency pair coherent.  The
exact collision condition (6.3) remains true, but its diagonal cardinality
has no control over the total sum.

### 12.4 Invalid step: factor the threshold into boundedly many prime packets

Countermodel/theorem: the exact Boolean identity (7.7) has full degree, with
first nonzero degree \(\asymp L\).  No bounded-order exact factorization
exists.

### 12.5 Invalid step: make a rank-one `two primes times three primes` split

Even the elementary reciprocal kernel

\[
K(m,n)=e\!\left(\frac{h}{mn}\right)
\]

is not rank one.  A rank-one matrix must have zero \(2\times2\) determinant,
but generically

\[
\begin{aligned}
&K(m,n)K(m',n')-K(m,n')K(m',n)\\
&\quad\ne0,
\end{aligned}
\]

because the cross-ratio phase is

\[
h\left(\frac1m-\frac1{m'}\right)
 \left(\frac1n-\frac1{n'}\right).
\tag{12.1}
\]

A Schmidt decomposition is possible only at the price of its rank and does
not preserve the Bettin--Chandee coefficient norms for free.

### 12.6 Invalid step: replace actual roots by arbitrary bounded-conductor
sheaf roots

The actual theorem must concern the literal sets \(I_p\).  There is no proved
uniform bounded-conductor/common-monodromy realization.  Conversely, the
singleton family \(I_p=\{1\}\) already gives a bounded-conductor packet but
the coherent short phase (10.6).  Thus the replacement is both unjustified
for Apéry and insufficient as a generic theorem.

## 13. What a genuine theorem would have to say

### 13.1 First exact gate

The first selector-preserving gate is the literal two-wave estimate
\((\mathrm{SP2})\), namely (5.3)--(5.4), with

* the original \(W_{p,r}\);
* the literal packet \(\mathcal A_{q;p,r}\);
* the literal discrepancy coefficient \(\Delta^{(2)}_{p,r}\);
* both reciprocal orientations after dispersion;
* ordered, pairwise distinct primes;
* no substitution of arbitrary sheaf roots.

The structural countermodel proves that such a theorem must state an
Apéry-specific hypothesis or prove an Apéry-specific correlation identity.
A generic sparse-set theorem is false.

### 13.2 Downstream gate after a valid transference theorem

Suppose one first proved a new selector-preserving transference result that
legitimately replaced \(wV\) by a controlled combination of smooth physical
weights **without** losing the required scale.  Only then would the clean
model become relevant.  Its first open imprimitive gate is \((\mathrm{D2})\).

A sufficient downstream theorem would have to save a full factor \(X\) in
(10.2), jointly over

\[
k,\ p_3,p_4,p_5,\ I_{p_3},I_{p_4},I_{p_5},
\]

not merely give square-root cancellation in one fixed field.  The
high-imprimitive theorem of §9 shows that no stronger claim is needed for
modes divisible by three or more packet primes.

### 13.3 Primitive sector

The primitive and one-divisor sectors need still greater savings than
\((\mathrm{D2})\).  Bettin--Chandee's exponent ledger shows that a standard
trilinear Kloosterman-fraction estimate is nowhere near the needed range.
Calling a conjectural joint square-root estimate “large sieve” does not turn
it into a theorem.

## 14. Classification of the conclusions

### Exact theorems proved here

1. The chart Fourier identities (2.2)--(2.6), including literal chart
   centering.
2. The sixteen-sector ordered/distinct decomposition (3.4)--(3.6).
3. The selector-preserving low-wave bound (4.6).
4. The exact two-wave formula (5.2)--(5.3).
5. The oriented collision criterion (6.3) and fixed-tuple Parseval identity
   (6.4).
6. The full-band weighted CRT formula (7.1).
7. The unrestricted five-prime reciprocal decomposition (8.3).
8. The target-scale bound for the zero mode and all modes divisible by at
   least three packet primes, Theorem 9.1.
9. The exact two-divisor reduction (10.2).

### Conditional reductions

* A valid selector-preserving smoothing/transference theorem would reduce a
  portion of the exact problem to the clean five-prime model.
* In that model, \((\mathrm{D2})\) is the first open imprimitive inequality.
* Sector-by-sector estimates for \(|T|=2,3,4\), together with Theorem 4.2,
  would imply the target for \(U_4\).

### Heuristics only

Joint random square-root cancellation in \(h\) and all five prime/root
packets would be more than sufficient.  No cited primary theorem establishes
it for these selectors and parameters.

### Scoped no-go statements

* Poisson truncation at \(X^3\) is invalid while \(w\) is retained.
* Exact threshold factorization has order at least \(L\), not five.
* Cauchy/Parseval and diagonal packing can be saturated.
* Generic sparse selectors and even generic bounded-conductor singleton
  packets do not satisfy the required joint dispersion.
* Bettin--Chandee misses the required power even under a fictitious separated
  model.
* The FKM and Bah--Shuddhodan hypotheses are not verified for the actual
  Apéry selectors.

## Final status

No known Kuznetsov/Deshouillers--Iwaniec, Bettin--Chandee,
Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, Bah--Shuddhodan, or currently available hybrid
trace-function theorem closes

\[
|U_4|\ll X^{2+o(1)}\Lambda^5.
\]

The first exact unresolved sector is the selector-preserving two-wave form
\((\mathrm{SP2})\), not the unrestricted five-prime form.  The latter becomes
legitimate only after deleting \(w\) or proving a new transference theorem.
Within that clean model, the zero and \(\ge3\)-divisor sectors are now closed
at the correct scale, and the first missing inequality is \((\mathrm{D2})\),
which needs one factor \(X\) and is not provided by the cited literature.

The next viable attack must therefore exploit a literal Apéry-zero identity
that defeats the synchronized-singleton geometry—most plausibly a joint
correlation theorem for the actual packets \(\mathcal A_{q;p,r}\) or a
selector-preserving physical-space identity.  A generic large sieve, a
one-field sheaf theorem, or a diagonal count cannot do so.
