# Q1917: inhomogeneous low-degree quotient attack

## 1. Setup and the only immediate identity

Let \(P(t)=t^p-t\). The polynomial \(C\) is the canonical representative of the solution of

\[
HC=-Q\delta_0,
\]

with \(\deg C\le p-3\), evenness, and nonvanishing on \(\mathbb F_p\). The homogeneous kernel satisfies

\[
Hk=0,
\qquad k(0)=-1,\quad k(\pm1)=3.
\]

The rank-one relation gives

\[
C=b_mk-2Q(\delta_1+\delta_{-1}).
\]

Substituting this relation into the inhomogeneous equation is not enough by itself to determine \(S=-\sum_t1/k_t\): the source terms only modify two coordinates of the homogeneous solution.

## 2. Polynomial representative issue

The symbols \(\delta_i\) are not ordinary low-degree monomials. Their canonical polynomial representatives are the Lagrange idempotents

\[
\delta_i(t)=1-\left(\frac{(t-i)^{p-1}-1}{1}\right)
\]

up to the chosen normalization convention. Consequently their ordinary polynomial degree is generally \(p-1\), not bounded by \(p-3\). Therefore one cannot compare coefficients of \(HC=-Q\delta_0\) in the usual polynomial ring while silently replacing the delta functions by degree-zero symbols.

The correct identity is in the quotient algebra

\[
\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^p-t),
\]

and degree reduction must be performed before coefficient comparison.

## 3. What the degree bound really gives

Write

\[
C=b_mk-2Q(\delta_1+\delta_{-1}).
\]

Because \(C\) has degree at most \(p-3\), the top two canonical coefficients of the right side must vanish after reduction modulo \(P\). These are constraints on the high-degree coefficients of the Lagrange representatives of the two delta sources and the degree-\((p-1)\) part of \(k\).

They do not directly involve

\[
\sum_t\frac1{k_t}.
\]

The reciprocal sum is a nonlinear invariant of the full unit-valued function \(k\), whereas the degree condition on \(C\) supplies only linear conditions on the polynomial representative.

## 4. Attempted elimination through the determinant identity

If one introduces

\[
r=1/k,
\]

then

\[
kr=1
\]

as functions on \(\mathbb F_p\). The reciprocal sum is

\[
S=-\sum_t r(t).
\]

The condition \(S=0\) says only that the constant Fourier coefficient of \(r\) vanishes. The equations

\[
HC= -Q\delta_0,
\qquad C=b_mk-2Q(\delta_1+\delta_{-1})
\]

remain linear in \(k\) and \(C\), while \(kr=1\) is nonlinear. No elimination producing a scalar multiple of \(Q\) or \(b_m\) follows from the stated hypotheses alone.

## 5. Smallest remaining algebraic system

After removing the already-known rank-one relation, the remaining unknown is the even polynomial \(k\) of degree \(p-1\):

\[
\begin{cases}
Hk=0,\\
k(0)=-1,\\
k(1)=3,\\
k(-1)=3,\\
\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}1/k(t)=0.
\end{cases}
\]

Equivalently one may use \(r\):

\[
\begin{cases}
Hr^{-1}=0,\\
r(0)=-1,\\
r(\pm1)=1/3,\\
\deg r\le p-3,\\
\sum_t r(t)=0.
\end{cases}
\]

The final equation is precisely the desired contradiction and is not implied by the previous linear constraints.

## Conclusion

The strict degree bound on \(C\) and the one-point source do not currently yield a new explicit identity for \(S\). The first obstruction is exact: the delta source lives naturally in the function algebra \(\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^p-t)\), while the degree restriction is a statement about the canonical reduced representative. After reduction it gives linear coefficient constraints, whereas \(S\) is a nonlinear reciprocal invariant. No complete contradiction or counterexample follows from the listed equations alone; the remaining open system is the reciprocal constraint on the homogeneous kernel above.
