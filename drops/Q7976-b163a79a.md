# Q7976 audit: explicit local and global checks

## 1. The point $(1,1,1)$ at $a=1$

Let $u=1+r$, $v=1+s$, $w=1+t$. Expanding the affine equation
\[
uv(w-1)(1-uvw)-aw(u-1)(v-1)=0
\]
at $a=1$ gives
\[
F=-rs-rt-st-t^2+\text{(terms of degree }\ge 3).
\]
The quadratic part has rank two. The singular tangent direction is
\[
(r,s,t)=(-z,-z,z).
\]
The tangent cone factors as
\[
-(r+t)(s+t).
\]
This is not by itself an $A_2$ proof. One must perform the blow-up.

Use completed local coordinates
\[
 x=r+t,\qquad y=s+t,\qquad z=t.
\]
Then the equation has the form
\[
xy+z^3+z^2(ax+by)+z(cx^2+dxy+ey^2)+\text{terms of order }\ge4.
\]
Because $p\ge5$, the formal Morse lemma with parameters removes the terms involving $z^2x,z^2y$ and the quadratic terms in $x,y$. After a formal coordinate change the completed local ring is
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,(1,1,1)}\cong k[[x,y,z]]/(xy+z^3).
\]
Hence the singularity is exactly $A_2$.

The ordinary blow-up of the maximal ideal resolves it. In the three standard charts:

1. $x$-chart: $y=xY$, $z=xZ$ gives
\[
x^2(Y+Z^3x)=0,
\]
so the strict transform is $Y+xZ^3=0$, smooth.

2. $y$-chart: symmetric, giving $X+ yZ^3=0$.

3. $z$-chart: $x=zX$, $y=zY$ gives
\[
z^2(XY+z)=0,
\]
so the strict transform is $XY+z=0$, smooth.

The exceptional divisor is the chain of two $(-2)$ curves obtained after normalization of the blow-up, giving the standard minimal resolution of $A_2$.

## 2. Anticanonical $(2,2,2)$ hypersurface

Let $Y=(\mathbf P^1)^3$. Its canonical bundle is
\[
\omega_Y=\mathcal O_Y(-2,-2,-2).
\]
For a Cartier hypersurface $X\in|\mathcal O_Y(2,2,2)|$, adjunction gives
\[
\omega_X=(\omega_Y\otimes\mathcal O_Y(X))|_X\cong\mathcal O_X.
\]
This remains valid for isolated rational double points because they are Gorenstein and the resolution is crepant.

For cohomology, use
\[
0\to\mathcal O_Y(-2,-2,-2)\to\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_X\to0.
\]
Kunneth gives
\[
H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=0,
\]
and
\[
H^2(Y,\mathcal O_Y(-2,-2,-2))=0,
\]
so the long exact sequence yields
\[
H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0.
\]
The same calculation gives $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, hence geometric connectedness. A hypersurface in the smooth threefold $Y$ is Cohen--Macaulay; with isolated RDP singularities it is regular in codimension one, hence normal by Serre $S_2+R_1$.

The missing hypothesis is that the displayed section must remain nonzero and define a Cartier divisor over the base trait. Fiberwise multidegree alone does not prove flatness.

## 3. Boundary components at special parameters

The sixteen boundary divisors are obtained from fixed coordinate equations
\[
u,v,w\in\{0,1,\infty\}
\]
and the two additional equations $uvw=1$. Their irreducibility is checked before specialization: each is a linear or $(1,1)$-type divisor on the relevant boundary surface.

At $a=1$ the interior singularity changes from $A_1$ to $A_2$, but the defining equation on every boundary divisor is unchanged because the parameter only appears in the term
\[
-aw(u-1)(v-1).
\]
Therefore no boundary component merges or becomes reducible.

At $D(a)=a^2-34a+1=0$, the extra open singularity occurs at
\[
u=v=(a-5)/12,\qquad w=(29-a)/12.
\]
It is disjoint from the toric boundary, so again the sixteen boundary components remain distinct.

## 4. Remaining missing hypotheses

The rigorous publication version still needs:

1. A relative henselian-trait model proving the chosen equation is a Cartier divisor flat over the trait.
2. An explicit specialization map showing the nineteen divisor classes form a single nondegenerate lattice in the family, not merely in one special fiber.
3. The Picard--Lefschetz argument must be limited to the one-node vanishing-cycle contribution. It gives the required local trace cancellation $e_p(\alpha)=0$, but does not imply full Picard rank or endpoint middle-extension statements.

With these additions, the local $A_2$ computation and the anticanonical K3 argument are complete.
