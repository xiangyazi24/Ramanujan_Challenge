# Cartier shell variation in the top range: exact structure and obstruction

## Summary

Fix \(n\), \(N=n-1\), and write

\[
C_N(d)=\sum_{\mu\in P\cap\mathbf Z^3}c_N(d\mu),
\]

where

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)^N=\sum_{u,v,w}c_N(u,v,w)x^uy^vz^w.
\]

For a top-shell prime \(p=d+1\), Q7317 gives

\[
p\mid b_n\Longrightarrow p\mid C_N(d).
\]

The shell values \(C_N(d)\) do have exact recurrences and symmetries, but the hoped-for conclusion (many nearby prime divisors force a common low-height integer) does not follow from these structures. The strongest exact statement is:

* \(C_N(d)\) is a finite sum of hypergeometric terms in \(d\);
* it satisfies a holonomic recurrence in \(d\) whose order is bounded by the 22-point shell but whose coefficients have height linear in \(N\);
* the natural resultant/common-carrier construction has logarithmic height 
  \[
  \Theta(N\,\#\{d\})
  \]
  and therefore remains linear in the number of top-shell positions.

No hidden constant-height integer carrier appears from the Cartier shell recurrence alone.

---

## 1. Exact coefficient formula

The Laurent polynomial is

\[
\Lambda=\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz}.
\]

The coefficient audit gives

\[
\boxed{
 c_N(u,v,w)=
 \sum_{k=0}^{N}
 \binom Nk
 \binom N{k-u}
 \binom{2N-k}{N-v}
 \binom{2N-k}{N-w}.
}
\]

All dependence on \(d\) therefore occurs through the four lower binomial arguments

\[
 k-du,\qquad N-dv,\qquad N-dw.
\]

Because \(P\cap\mathbf Z^3\) has 22 points, \(C_N(d)\) is explicitly a sum of 22 one-dimensional hypergeometric sequences:

\[
C_N(d)=\sum_{\mu=(a,b,c)}\sum_k T_{N,\mu}(d,k).
\]

For fixed \(N\), this is a proper hypergeometric sum in \(d,k\).

---

## 2. Reflection

The Newton polytope is centrally symmetric only after the involution induced by the toric inversion

\[
(x,y,z)\mapsto(x^{-1},y^{-1},z^{-1}).
\]

The coefficient symmetry is

\[
 c_N(u,v,w)=c_N(-u,-v,-w).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 C_N(d)=C_N(-d).
}
\]

This is the only unconditional reflection in the shell parameter. It does not identify two different positive top-shell values \(d_1,d_2\).

The Apéry reflection

\[
b_{p-1-r}=b_r\pmod p
\]

is stronger because it changes the exponent modulo \(p-1\). It does not lift to an integer identity between \(C_N(d)\) and \(C_N(d')\) for different \(d\).

---

## 3. Exact recurrence in \(d\)

For a fixed lattice point \(\mu=(a,b,c)\), define

\[
F_\mu(d)=c_N(da,db,dc).
\]

The summand ratio in \(k\) is rational:

\[
\frac{T_{N,\mu}(d,k+1)}{T_{N,\mu}(d,k)}
=
\frac{(N-k)(N-k+u)(2N-k-v)(2N-k-w)}
{(k+1)(k+1-u)(N-k+v+1)(N-k+w+1)}.
\]

Therefore Zeilberger creative telescoping gives

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{r}A_i(d,N)F_\mu(d+i)=0
\]

for each of the 22 shell points.

Combining the 22 recurrences gives a recurrence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=0}^{R}A_i(d,N)C_N(d+i)=0
}
\]

with \(R\le22R_0\), where \(R_0\) is the telescoping order of one coefficient orbit.

The important point is the coefficient height. The binomial shifts contain

\[
N-d\mu_i,
\]

so clearing denominators introduces factors

\[
\prod_{m=0}^{O(R)}(N-md).
\]

For \(d\asymp N\), their logarithmic height is

\[
O(RN\log N).
\]

Thus the recurrence exists but is not a bounded-height recurrence independent of \(N\).

---

## 4. Adjacent determinants

Suppose the recurrence has a basis of solutions \(Y_1(d),\dots,Y_R(d)\). The adjacent Casoratian

\[
W(d)=\det(Y_i(d+j))_{0\le i,j<R}
\]

satisfies

\[
W(d+1)=(-1)^RA_0(d,N)A_R(d,N)^{-1}W(d).
\]

Hence

\[
\log|W(d)|
=
\log|W(0)|+
\sum_{m<d}O(\log|A_i(m,N)|).
\]

Since each step contains polynomial factors of degree \(O(N)\),

\[
\boxed{
\log|W(d)|=O(dN\log N).
}
\]

Therefore adjacent determinants do not produce a small common divisor carrier. They grow at the same scale as the naive recurrence height.

---

## 5. Log-concavity attempt

The individual coefficient sequences

\[
d\mapsto c_N(d\mu)
\]

are not globally log-concave.

A certificate is already visible from the binomial factors. For one factor,

\[
\binom N{N-d}
\]

is log-concave in \(d\) only on the interval where the lower index remains in \([0,N]\). Different shell points have incompatible intervals:

* \(d\mu_i\) positive truncates at \(d\le N/\mu_i\);
* \(d\mu_i\) negative truncates in the opposite direction.

The 22-term sum mixes these incompatible supports. Hence no global positivity/log-concavity argument can force sparse prime divisibility.

A finite symbolic check for small \(N\) finds sign changes in

\[
C_N(d)^2-C_N(d-1)C_N(d+1),
\]

so ordinary log-concavity is false.

---

## 6. Can nearby prime divisors force a common low-height integer?

Assume a set of nearby primes

\[
\mathcal P\subset(n/2,n]
\]

satisfies

\[
 p=d+1\mid C_N(d).
\]

A natural carrier is

\[
H_N=\operatorname{Res}_d(C_N(d),\Phi(d))
\]

where \(\Phi(d)\) vanishes at the selected offsets, or simply

\[
H_N=\prod_{d\in D}C_N(d).
\]

The second construction has

\[
\log|H_N|
\le |D|\log(40^N)
=O(|D|N).
\]

For the whole top shell,

\[
|D|\asymp \frac{N}{\log N},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\log|H_N|=O(N^2/\log N).
}
\]

This is not \(o(N)\).

The recurrence resultant gives the same scale. A resultant between two shifts

\[
C_N(d),C_N(d+h)
\]

has degree \(O(N)\) and coefficient height \(O(N\log N)\), hence

\[
\log|\operatorname{Res}|=O(N^2\log N)
\]

in the worst case.

There is no cancellation mechanism from the shell geometry alone.

---

## 7. Strongest rigorous conclusion

The Cartier shell converts the top-shell problem into a family of explicit
hypergeometric values, but the family is not a low-height integer sequence.

The exact conclusions are:

1. **Holonomicity:** \(C_N(d)\) satisfies a linear recurrence in \(d\).
2. **Reflection:** \(C_N(d)=C_N(-d)\).
3. **No log-concavity:** the 22-term signed shell sum is not globally log-concave.
4. **No bounded-height Wronskian:** adjacent determinants inherit \(O(N)\)-scale factors.
5. **No low-height carrier from recurrence/resultants:** natural carriers have logarithmic height at least of order the number of sampled \(d\)'s times \(N\).

Thus the desired implication

\[
\text{many nearby }(d+1)\mid C_N(d)
\Longrightarrow
\text{one small common integer}
\]

is not a consequence of the Cartier shell recurrence.

The missing ingredient would have to be a genuinely new arithmetic relation between different shell parameters \(d\), for example:

* an integer identity linking \(C_N(d)\) for different \(d\);
* a hidden factorization with degree \(o(N)\);
* or a cross-prime congruence stronger than the current Cartier congruence.

The explicit binomial shell formula alone leaves the resultant height linear in the number of shells, so it does not close the all-index radical bound.
