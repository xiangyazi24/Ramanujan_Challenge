# 2. Exactly how much auxiliary class compression is sufficient

## 2.1 One modulus

Let \(S\) be \(K\) integers in an interval of length \(\Lambda\), and let

\[
\nu_m(S)=|S\bmod m|.
\]

Each residue class modulo \(m\) meets the interval in at most

\[
1+\frac{\Lambda}{m}
\]

integers. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K\le \nu_m(S)\left(1+\frac{\Lambda}{m}\right).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

To force

\[
K\ll H Y^{-\varepsilon},
\tag{2.2}
\]

a single modulus must satisfy one of the following genuinely strong conditions:

\[
\frac{\nu_m(S)}m
\ll
\frac{H}{\Lambda}Y^{-\varepsilon}
=Y^{-1/3-\varepsilon}
\qquad(m\le\Lambda),
\tag{2.3}
\]

or

\[
\nu_m(S)\ll H Y^{-\varepsilon}
\qquad(m\ge\Lambda).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus a fixed congruence condition, a fixed quadratic character, or a fixed Atkin--Lehner sign cannot give the required power. They remove at most a fixed proportion of classes.

## 2.2 Exact larger-sieve inequality

The useful family version is elementary enough to prove here.

> **Lemma 2.1 — larger-sieve class inequality.**
> Let \(S\) be \(K\ge2\) distinct integers in an interval of length \(\Lambda\). Let \(\mathcal L\) be a finite set of auxiliary primes and put
> \[
> \nu_\lambda=|S\bmod\lambda|,
> \]
> \[
> A=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal L}
>       \frac{\log\lambda}{\nu_\lambda},
> \qquad
> B=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal L}\log\lambda.
> \]
> If \(A>\log\Lambda\), then
> \[
> \boxed{
> K\le
> \frac{B-\log\Lambda}{A-\log\Lambda}.
> }
> \tag{2.5}
> \]

### Proof

Write the occupancies modulo \(\lambda\) as

\[
n_{\lambda,1},\ldots,n_{\lambda,\nu_\lambda}.
\]

The number of unordered pairs congruent modulo \(\lambda\) is at least

\[
\sum_a\binom{n_{\lambda,a}}2
\ge
\frac12\left(\frac{K^2}{\nu_\lambda}-K\right).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Every such pair contributes a factor \(\lambda\) to its difference. Hence, for

\[
\Delta(S)=\prod_{u<v\in S}|u-v|,
\]

we have

\[
\log\Delta(S)
\ge
\frac12\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal L}
\left(\frac{K^2}{\nu_\lambda}-K\right)\log\lambda.
\tag{2.7}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
\log\Delta(S)\le\binom K2\log\Lambda.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Combining `(2.7)` and `(2.8)` gives

\[
K(A-\log\Lambda)\le B-\log\Lambda,
\]

which is `(2.5)`. ∎

This proof shows what an auxiliary law does: auxiliary primes divide many **differences** of marked primes. No integer is required to be divisible by all marked primes themselves.

# 4. First exact failed implication

For an actual lower leaf, the literal information is

\[
p\mid b_{\rho_x(p)},
\qquad
p\mid b_{\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The desired auxiliary transport would require

\[
\lambda\mid b_{\rho_x(p)\bmod\lambda},
\qquad
\lambda\mid b_{\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p)\bmod\lambda}.

There is no implication from `(4.1)` to `(4.2)`.

```text
p divides b_n

p divides b_n
    does not imply
lambda divides b_n;

p divides b_n
    does not imply
```

The two-slope affine identity only states that the pair of row indices lies on a fixed line. It holds before `(4.1)` and therefore cannot promote `(4.1)` to `(4.2)`.

This is the exact missing implication in the most promising auxiliary construction.

# 5. A strong generic obstruction: divisibility has full auxiliary unit projection

The preceding failure is not merely that a familiar theorem has not been applied cleverly enough. Bare defining-prime divisibility is algebraically invisible to an unrelated modulus.

> **Theorem 5.1 — auxiliary unit-projection obstruction.**
> Let \(m\ge2\), let \(R=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), and let \(u\in R^\times\). For every \(s\ge1\), the map
> \[
> \Phi:R^\times\times R^s\longrightarrow R^\times\times R^s,
> \]
> \[
> \Phi(u,v_1,\ldots,v_s)
> =(u,uv_1,\ldots,uv_s)
> \tag{5.1}
> \]
> is a bijection. Consequently, reducing identities
> \[
> B_i=pU_i\qquad(1\le i\le s)
> \tag{5.2}
> \]
> modulo \(m\), with \(p\nmid m\), imposes no restriction on \(p\bmod m\) unless one has an independent restriction on the divided tuple
> \[
> (U_1,\ldots,U_s)\bmod m.
> \]

### Proof

The inverse of `(5.1)` is

\[
(u,w_1,\ldots,w_s)
\longmapsto
(u,u^{-1}w_1,\ldots,u^{-1}w_s).
\]

Thus every unit class \(u\) is compatible with every tuple of residues \((B_1,\ldots,B_s)\). ∎

Apply the theorem with

\[
B_1=b_{\rho_x(p)},
\qquad
B_2=b_{\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p)}.
\]

Modulo an auxiliary \(m\), the quotient residues are simply

\[
U_i\equiv p^{-1}B_i\pmod m.
\]

The recurrence and Lucas laws may determine \(B_i\bmod m\) from the indices, but they do not independently constrain these divided residues. Hence they do not exclude any unit class of \(p\).

This theorem also explains why knowing a characteristic-zero carrier divisible by \(p\) gives no inverse-sieve input: the quotient by \(p\) is precisely the missing coordinate.

## 5.1 What would evade the obstruction

Any successful auxiliary theorem must add at least one of the following genuinely new data:

1. a congruence law for the divided values
   \[
   \frac{b_{\rho_x(p)}}p,
   \qquad
   \frac{b_{\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p)}}p
   \pmod\lambda;
   \]
2. a fixed auxiliary representation in which the *leaf event itself* is a Frobenius condition and whose cyclotomic/determinant projection is sparse;
3. a direct horizontal covariance or trace estimate which never turns the endpoint conditions into positive divisibility.

The existing divided-jet statements are \(p\)-adic statements in the defining characteristic \(p\). They are not congruences modulo an unrelated \(\lambda\), so they do not supply item 1.

# 6. Exact Galois criterion and the determinant obstruction

A motivic auxiliary law would have to look substantially stronger than “the trace is special.”

## 6.1 Sufficient Frobenius-detection premise

For each auxiliary prime \(\lambda\), suppose one had a fixed-complexity representation

\[
\bar\rho_{\lambda,x}:
G_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow
\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb F_\lambda)
\tag{6.1}
\]

unramified at shell primes, and a conjugacy-stable subset \(C_{\lambda,x}\) such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_\sigma(x;p)=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bar\rho_{\lambda,x}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)
\in C_{\lambda,x}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Assume also

\[
\det\bar\rho_{\lambda,x}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)
=\chi_x(p)p^w
\pmod\lambda,
\tag{6.3}
\]

where \(w\) is fixed and \(\chi_x\) has boundedly many values. If

\[
\boxed{
|\det C_{\lambda,x}|\ll_J\lambda^\theta,
\qquad\theta<1,
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

then `(6.2)`--`(6.4)` imply

\[
|S_{\sigma,x}(I)\bmod\lambda|
\ll_{J,w}\lambda^\theta,
\tag{6.5}
\]

up to the bounded multiplicity of the \(w\)-th-power map. Therefore `(6.4)` is a precise motivic route to `Q6-AUX(theta,eta,epsilon)`.

Call `(6.2)`--`(6.4)` **Frobenius-detectable auxiliary sparsity**, `Q6-FDAS_theta`.

## 6.2 Trace conditions do not give determinant sparsity

> **Lemma 6.1 — full determinant projection of a trace slice.**
> Let \(\lambda\) be prime and let \(A\subset\mathbb F_\lambda\) be nonempty. Then
> \[
> \left\{\det g:
> g\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_\lambda),
> \operatorname{tr}g\in A
> \right\}
> =\mathbb F_\lambda^\times.
> \tag{6.6}
> \]

### Proof

Fix \(a\in A\). For every \(d\in\mathbb F_\lambda^\times\), the companion matrix

\[
g_{a,d}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-d\\
1&a
\end{pmatrix}
\]

has trace \(a\) and determinant \(d\). ∎

Thus even if one could identify an actual leaf with

```text
Frobenius trace = 0;
Frobenius trace in a fixed small set;
ordinary/nonordinary status;
a prescribed unit-root trace congruence;
```

a large \(\mathrm{GL}_2\)-type image would still allow every determinant, hence every cyclotomic class of \(p\bmod\lambda\). Large monodromy is not the missing class compression; for trace-defined events it points in the opposite direction.

A CM image can turn trace-zero into an inert/split condition, but this is a condition modulo a fixed discriminant. It excludes only a fixed proportion of prime classes and does not satisfy `(6.4)` for a growing auxiliary family.

# 7. Audit of the proposed Apéry mechanisms

This section is intentionally narrow. It checks the literal q6 predicate against the actual hypotheses of the relevant mechanisms; it is not a broad literature survey.

## 7.1 Modular parametrization of the Apéry K3 family

The Apéry \(\zeta(3)\) differential equation has a K3/modular interpretation; this is the setting of Beukers--Peters and Stienstra--Beukers. It supplies a common Picard--Fuchs equation, formal Brauer/Frobenius congruences and modular parametrizations of the period.

What it does **not** supply is `(6.2)` for the event

\[
p\mid b_{\rho_x(p)},\ b_{\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p)}.
\]

The q6 event concerns two moving coefficients of the characteristic-\(p\) period/Hasse section. A Frobenius trace or point count concerns a fibre value at a fixed algebraic parameter. Coefficient extraction from the Hasse section is a different operation.

The exact repository result in `gap_kernel_cartier_defect.tex` makes this mismatch concrete: the strict-triangle Cartier defect stores the original Hasse coefficient on the Frobenius boundary, complete coefficient extraction uses a moving frame, and the direct polynomial model has degree \(\Omega(p)\). Thus the natural Cartier compression does not turn a moving coefficient zero into a bounded-complexity Frobenius condition.

**Verdict:** genuine vertical structure, no auxiliary class law for the literal row.

## 7.2 Lucas congruences from modular forms

Beukers--Tsai--Ye prove Lucas congruences for broad modular Apéry-like families. Beukers's modular-supercongruence framework likewise gives

\[
F(t)/F(t^\sigma)\equiv F_p(t)\pmod{p^s}
\]

and specializes the \(p\)-truncation at modular, cusp or CM values.

These results are prime-local. Modulo \(\lambda\), Lucas describes \(b_n\) using the base-\(\lambda\) digits of \(n\). It does not turn the fact that another prime \(p\) divides \(b_n\) into a zero digit modulo \(\lambda\). Supercongruence specializations concern values

\[
F_p(t_0)=\sum_{n<p}b_nt_0^n,
\]

not an arbitrary moving coefficient \(b_{\rho_x(p)}\), much less two such coefficients plus \(q\mid b_t\).

**Verdict:** fails exactly at `(FAIL)`.

## 7.3 Supersingular and CM specializations

At a fixed CM point, modular supercongruences may distinguish primes by split/inert behaviour in a fixed imaginary quadratic field. Such a condition occupies a positive proportion of classes modulo its fixed discriminant. For an auxiliary prime \(\lambda\) coprime to that discriminant, the fixed splitting condition itself excludes no class modulo \(\lambda\); CRT keeps the two coordinates independent.

More importantly, the actual q6 residue pair is not identified with evaluation of the Hasse invariant at a fixed CM point. The fold/order classification fixes affine slopes, not a modular parameter.

Even gifting a CM law on a special midpoint or fixed-rational-index sub-sector would not control the general deep fibre. The affine equality placing one raw index at a prescribed midpoint already determines at most one lower prime \(p\) for a fixed selected state, so that special sector cannot support a rich fibre without any CM theorem.

**Verdict:** potentially useful for isolated special endpoints; incapable of the required growing auxiliary class compression on DPRM.

## 7.4 Galois image modulo an auxiliary prime

The associated motive or modular form may provide a compatible family of Galois representations and Frobenius traces. But no banked theorem identifies

\[
p\mid b_{\rho_x(p)},\ b_{\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p)}
\]

with a conjugacy condition in a fixed auxiliary representation. This is the first missing hypothesis in `(6.2)`.

Even granting a trace identification would not suffice in a large image: Lemma 6.1 shows that trace slices have full determinant projection. The needed statement is the much stronger determinant-sparse condition `(6.4)`.

**Verdict:** a viable abstract template only in the `Q6-FDAS` form; no actual detector is known.

## 7.5 Atkin--Swinnerton--Dyer congruences

ASD congruences relate Fourier coefficients at indices such as \(n\), \(pn\) and \(n/p\), or compare coefficients of specified modular forms in one defining characteristic. The known midpoint congruence highlighted in the ASD literature concerns the \(\zeta(2)\) Apéry sequence, not the present \(\zeta(3)\) two-row q6 predicate.

Here the two indices satisfy

\[
0\le\rho_x(p),\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p)<p
\]

and move affinely with \(p\). They are not a fixed Fourier index and its \(p\)-multiple. The selected condition \(q\mid b_t\) introduces a third varying characteristic.

**Verdict:** hypothesis mismatch; no class law.

## 7.6 Fixed level, Atkin--Lehner signs and Dirichlet characters

The modular family has fixed level. Any restriction arising solely from finitely many fixed-conductor characters removes at most a fixed proportion of primes. By `(2.3)` this is far below the required

\[
Y^{-1/3-\varepsilon}

**Verdict:** no power-saving inverse-sieve input.

## 7.7 Algebraic group or rank-of-apparition mechanism

For an elliptic divisibility sequence, a prime divisor can encode the order of a fixed point modulo \(p\), producing congruence restrictions. No analogous structure is banked for the Apéry sequence. The recurrence is nonautonomous in the index; the q6 zero is a projective return for a transfer product whose factors vary with the index, not the order of one fixed group element.

The universal degree-one two-slope identity is only the affine kinematic line. It contains no marked return information.

**Verdict:** no available rank-of-apparition law.

**Verdict:** no silent full-value/prefix substitution is legal.

# 8. Why an equidistribution obstruction theorem is not available

The question suggests that, if no sparse auxiliary law exists, one might prove that actual q6 lower primes are equidistributed modulo every auxiliary modulus. That conclusion is not supported and is not even the correct uniform target in the present normalization.

1. The bank permits
   \[
   M_\sigma(T)=0.
   \]
   An empty fibre is not equidistributed.
2. `Q6-SLUNI`, which is still open, permits every nonempty fibre to be a singleton. A singleton is maximally class-sparse, not equidistributed, yet it already closes every off-diagonal gate.
3. A relative equidistribution statement would normally divide by the fibre mass or assume it tends to infinity. Both are unavailable here.
4. The interface falsifiers show only that the bank permits class saturation; they are not actual Apéry constructions and do not prove equidistribution.

Therefore I do not claim a no-law theorem for the actual sequence. The rigorous negative result is the **visibility obstruction**: none of the currently proved divisibility, modular, trace or fixed-character interfaces forces an auxiliary class restriction.

# 9. Relation to the marked-prime information floor

The Fitting theorem from the lifting round says that any finite-presentation characteristic-zero object which carries the \(2R_C\) endpoint primes as excess special-fibre kernels has arithmetic degree at least

\[
2R_C\log T+O_J(R_C).
\tag{9.1}
\]

This does not forbid `Q6-AUX`.

A larger-sieve proof uses the implication

\[
\text{few classes modulo }\lambda
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda\mid(p_i-p_j)
\text{ for many pairs }(i,j).
\]

The height paid is the difference product

\[
\prod_{i<j}|p_i-p_j|,
\]

whose factors are at most \(\Lambda\), not a scalar divisible by every \(p_i\). The information is shared across pairs and auxiliary primes. It never resolves each endpoint prime into a positive divisor of one common integer.

Thus a true auxiliary-modulus law is exactly the sort of horizontal information which could get below the marked-prime floor.

The floor does, however, rule out a fake derivation:

```text
construct a common scalar carrying every endpoint prime;
reduce that scalar modulo auxiliary primes;
deduce that endpoint primes lie in few classes.
```

The first step already pays `(9.1)`. A viable auxiliary proof must be pre-support and non-scalar: a determinant-sparse Frobenius detector, a divided-state congruence, or a signed horizontal trace/covariance theorem.
