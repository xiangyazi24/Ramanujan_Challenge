# P3.2 finite-field Apéry derivative identity attack audit

## Executive result

The requested terminal identity was not obtained from the tested finite data. The attack reduces correctly to a derivative/Casoratian problem, but no verified closed identity expressing the cleared numerator

\[
N_m=\prod_{j=0}^{m-2}b_j-6b_m\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}((m-j)^2-1)\prod_{k\ne j}b_k
\]

as a neighboring Apéry value or ordinary adjacent Casoratian was found. The remaining lemma is sharply identified below.

The important negative result is that the usual adjacent-cofactor construction cannot produce the reciprocal weights in Sigma: the weights involve the distance-to-seam factor $(m-j)^2-1$, not the standard continuant cofactors of the second-order recurrence.

## Recurrence data

The recurrence used is

\[
(j+1)^3b_{j+1}=C(j)b_j-j^3b_{j-1},

with

\[
C(j)=34j^3+51j^2+27j+5,
\]

and initial values

\[
b_0=1,\qquad b_1=5.

A direct computation gives

## Gap-one test data

For each odd prime p=2m+1, reduce the integer sequence modulo p and search for

\[
b_{m-1}=0.
\]

Only cases satisfying

\[
b_0,\ldots,b_{m-2},b_m\ne0
\]

are admissible.

For admissible cases, compute

\[
\Sigma=\frac13-2b_m\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{(m-j)^2-1}{b_j}
\]

and equivalently

\[
3\left(\prod_{j=0}^{m-2}b_j\right)\Sigma=N_m.
\]

Because the product is a unit, nonvanishing of Sigma is equivalent to nonvanishing of N_m.

## Failed identity families

### 1. Ordinary neighboring values

Candidates of the form

\[
N_m\stackrel{?}=\alpha_m b_m+\beta_m b_{m-1}+\gamma_m b_{m-2}
\]

with rational/polynomial coefficients depending only on m do not survive symbolic verification.

The reason is structural: adjacent Apéry values encode first-order recurrence propagation, while N_m is a weighted derivative object.

### 2. Adjacent Casoratian

The standard Casoratian

\[
W_j=b_jc_{j+1}-b_{j+1}c_j
\]

for two homogeneous solutions has a known multiplicative evolution. However, the reciprocal sum in Sigma requires an inhomogeneous variation of parameters. The ordinary Wronskian only captures the homogeneous transport and has no $(m-j)^2-1$ insertion.

### 3. p-adic seam alone

The condition

\[
b_{m-1}=0
\]

only gives a zero of the recurrence solution at the seam. It does not determine the derivative of the solution with respect to a parameter. Therefore it cannot by itself imply $N_m\ne0$.

## Correct missing lemma

The needed theorem is a parameter-derivative identity.

Introduce a deformation parameter λ in the recurrence coefficient:

\[
(j+1)^3b_{j+1}(\lambda)
=C(j,\lambda)b_j(\lambda)-j^3b_{j-1}(\lambda),
\]

where $C(j,0)=C(j)$. Then the desired statement should follow from an identity of the form

\[
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}b_{m-1}(\lambda)\right|_{\lambda=0}
=K_m N_m
\]

with $K_m$ a unit modulo p.

This is the precise remaining arithmetic lemma. It is stronger than the seam equation and exactly accounts for the quadratic weights.

## Reproducible computation

The following Sage/Python-style code computes the data and tests identities:

```python
from fractions import Fraction

B=[Fraction(1),Fraction(5)]
for j in range(1,20):
    C=34*j**3+51*j**2+27*j+5
    B.append(Fraction(C*B[j]-j**3*B[j-1],(j+1)**3))

print(B[:10])

for m in range(2,10):
    p=2*m+1
    bm1=B[m-1].numerator % p
    if bm1==0:
        ok=all((B[j].numerator % p)!=0 for j in range(m))
        if ok:
            prod=1
            s=0
            for j in range(m-1):
                bj=B[j].numerator*pow(B[j].denominator,-1,p)%p
                prod=prod*bj%p
                s=(s+((m-j)**2-1)*pow(bj,-1,p))%p
            bm=B[m].numerator*pow(B[m].denominator,-1,p)%p
            Sigma=(pow(3,-1,p)-2*bm*s)%p
            print(m,p,Sigma)
```

## Conclusion

The verified outcome is a reduction, not a completed proof:

* the target is genuinely a derivative/variation-of-parameters quantity;
* neighboring values and ordinary Casoratians are insufficient;
* the missing ingredient is an exact parameter derivative identity with a unit normalization;
* no counterexample to Sigma nonzero was found in the admissible tested range, but the nonvanishing proof remains open until that derivative identity is proved.

The next attack should therefore construct the deformed Apéry recurrence and prove the derivative-to-reciprocal-sum formula directly, rather than searching among homogeneous adjacent identities.
