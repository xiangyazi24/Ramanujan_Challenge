# Cyclic conormal and complementary-moment audit

## Verdict

I do not obtain a proof of pointed simplicity. The new cyclic quotient can be
classified completely: it is a first-conormal system with exactly one global
Fourier constraint. That constraint, the group algebra, the sum-of-squares
form, and the nonzero complementary moment are all compatible with a strict
common node.

There is also a normalization issue. Two distinct functionals have been
called `Lambda`:

\[
\Lambda_{F}(R_aR_b)=\frac{\delta_{ab}}{2a+1}
\]

is the Favard/Green functional, while Q8209's complementary identity uses

\[
\Lambda_{hg}(Q)=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}Q(\lambda_j)+Q(\lambda_N)
              =-[Y^N]Q\quad(\deg Q\le N).
\]

They cannot be the same, since

\[
\Lambda_F(1)=1,\qquad \Lambda_{hg}(1)=2N+1=p=0.
\tag{0.1}
\]

After separating them, the result is a terminal no-go for the cyclic
first-conormal plus complementary-leading-moment route. It is not a
counterexample to the actual Apéry statement.

## 1. Exact cyclic division and node dictionary

Put \(p=2N+1\), \(X=4Y+1\), \(x_j=(2j+1)^2\), and
\(P=X^N-1\). If

\[
\psi_k(X)=\phi_k((X-1)/4),
\qquad
\phi_k(Y)=\frac{\prod_{a<k}(Y-a(a+1))}{(k!)^2},
\]

then the nonzero squares of \(\mathbf F_p\) are exactly
\(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}\), so

\[
\prod_{a=0}^{N-1}(X-x_a)=P(X).
\]

Fermat and Wilson give \(4^N=1\) and \((N!)^4=1\), hence
\(\psi_N^2=P^2\). Every other \(\psi_k^2\) has degree at most
\(2N-2\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\widehat H_p=P^2+A_pP+B_p,
\quad \deg A_p\le N-2,\quad \deg B_p<N.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Differentiation gives, on \(P=0\),

\[
\widehat H_p=B_p,
\qquad
\widehat H_p'=J_p:=B_p'+P'A_p\pmod P.
\tag{1.2}
\]

At a strict node \(x_n\), \(0\le n<N\),

\[
B_p(x_n)=b_n,
\qquad
2(2n+1)J_p(x_n)=D_n.
\tag{1.3}
\]

All displayed multipliers are units. Thus

\[
\gcd(P,B_p,J_p)=1
\]

is exactly strict pointed simplicity. A common root of \(B_p,J_p\) away from
\(P=0\) is irrelevant.

## 2. Complete global-cycle theorem

In

\[
\mathscr R_p=\mathbf F_p[X]/(P)
\simeq\prod_{\xi^N=1}\mathbf F_p,
\]

one has

\[
P'=NX^{N-1}=NX^{-1},
\qquad
\boxed{A=N^{-1}X(J-B')\pmod P.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

For the canonical representative
\(f=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f_kX^k\), Fourier inversion gives

\[
f_k=\frac1N\sum_{\xi^N=1}\xi^{-k}f(\xi).
\]

The condition \(\deg A\le N-2\) is therefore equivalent to

\[
\sum_{\xi^N=1}\xi A(\xi)=0.
\]

Substitution of (2.1), followed by coefficientwise evaluation of \(B'\),
gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\xi^N=1}\xi^2J(\xi)
=(N-1)\sum_{\xi^N=1}\xi B(\xi).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proposition 2.1

For canonical \(B,J\) of degree below \(N\), an \(A\) with
\(\deg A\le N-2\) and

\[
J=B'+P'A\pmod P
\]

exists iff (2.2) holds. It is then unique and is given by (2.1).

Indeed, necessity is the calculation above. Conversely, (2.2) says exactly
that the \(X^{N-1}\) coefficient of the polynomial in (2.1) is zero;
multiplication by the unit \(P'\) recovers \(J-B'\).

Thus the cyclic package is one hyperplane in the full value/jet space.
Imposing \(B(\alpha)=J(\alpha)=0\) at one node leaves dimension at least
\(2N-3\). Semisimplicity of the group algebra supplies no contradiction.

## 3. Exact countermodel to the structural implication

Take

\[
p=11,\qquad N=5,\qquad n=2,\qquad \alpha=x_2=3.
\]

Set

\[
P=X^5-1,\qquad A^*=0,\qquad
B^*=(X-3)^2,\qquad J^*=2(X-3),
\]

and

\[
\widehat H^*=P^2+(X-3)^2.
\]

All degree bounds, the conormal identity, and (2.2) hold, while

\[
\boxed{\gcd(P,B^*,J^*)=X-3.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The shell is monic and is a sum of two squares. It has a double, not triple,
node:

\[
\widehat H^*(3)=(\widehat H^*)'(3)=0,
\]

and, since \(P'(3)=5\cdot3^4=9\),

\[
(\widehat H^*)''(3)=2P'(3)^2+2=10\ne0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence a nonzero second jet proves only exact multiplicity two; it cannot
exclude the pointed double root.

This obstruction survives arbitrary prescribed quotient data. Given any
\(A_0\) of degree at most \(N-2\), put

\[
c=-P'(\alpha)A_0(\alpha),
\qquad
B=(X-\alpha)(c+X-\alpha).
\]

Then \(B(\alpha)=0\) and
\(B'(\alpha)+P'(\alpha)A_0(\alpha)=0\). Thus even the entire coefficient
vector of \(A\) is insufficient by itself; one needs an actual coupling
between lower coefficients of \(A\) and \(B\).

The model is not claimed to equal the actual Apéry shell. It is an exact
counterexample to any inference using only the cyclic division, degree
bounds, first conormal relation, group algebra, and sum-of-squares form.

## 4. Complementary moment remains nonzero

For

\[
T_n(Y)=
\frac{R_n(Y)-b_n-\frac{D_n}{2n+1}(Y-\lambda_n)}
     {(Y-\lambda_n)^2},
\]

one has \(\deg T_n=n-2\), leading coefficient \(c_n\), and, with
\(m=N-n+2\),

\[
\Lambda_{hg}(T_nR_m)=-c_nc_m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is just \(-[Y^N](T_nR_m)\); it reads only the leading coefficient.

The compatibility with a common root is exact in the same \(p=11\) model.
Here

\[
\lambda_2=6,\qquad c_2=7,\qquad c_5=10.
\]

Let

\[
R_2^*(Y)=7(Y-6)^2,\qquad T_2^*=7,\qquad m=5.
\]

Then the value and first jet vanish, but

\[
\boxed{\Lambda_{hg}(T_2^*R_5)=-c_2c_5=7\ne0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

On the other hand \(T_2^*=7R_0\), so

\[
\Lambda_F(T_2^*R_5)=0.
\]

This verifies both the functional distinction and the no-go: the cyclic
common node and the nonzero complementary moment coexist. Since the two
structures are joined here through the same local pair
\((b_2,D_2)=(0,0)\), any proposed proof joining them only through
\((b_n,D_n)\) fails. A successful proof must use an identity between their
actual lower coefficient vectors.

## 5. Resultants and group-algebra traces add no arithmetic

Because \(P\) splits,

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X(P,B+TJ)
=\prod_{\xi^N=1}(B(\xi)+TJ(\xi)).
\]

It is nonzero as a polynomial in \(T\) iff \(P,B,J\) have no common root.
This is exact but only repackages the gcd.

Likewise

\[
e=(1-B^{p-1})(1-J^{p-1})\in\mathscr R_p
\]

is the idempotent indicator of common nodes. Its trace is the number of such
nodes, an integer between \(0\) and \(N<p\). Hence trace zero is equivalent
to pointed simplicity, but supplies no new estimate or identity.

## 6. Midpoint and variable audit

- For \(0\le n<N\), \(x_n\ne0\), \(x_n^N=1\), and every denominator in
  (1.3) is a unit.
- At the midpoint, \(x_N=0\) and \(P(0)=-1\); also \(2N+1=0\). The midpoint
  is outside this theorem.
- Off-node common roots of \(B,J\) are irrelevant unless they also divide
  \(P\).
- The variable \(X=4Y+1\) is the folded coefficient-index/Racah variable,
  not a Franel or Hasse cover variable. No cover-variable root is identified
  with a coefficient-index zero.
- At \(n=0\), \(b_0=1\), so only \(1\le n<N\) is nontrivial.

## 7. Strictly smaller exact gateway

The corrected Q8206 audit defines

\[
E_1=27,
\]

\[
E_n=-3+
\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(102j^2+102j+27)b_j^2
-6\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}(j+1)^2b_jb_{j+1}\quad(n\ge2),
\tag{7.1}
\]

with exact Bézout form

\[
E_n=n^3b_{n-1}(2D_n)
+n^2b_n(3b_{n-1}-n(2D_{n-1})).
\tag{7.2}
\]

If \(p>2n+1\) and \(p\mid b_n\), then \(p\nmid b_{n-1}\); otherwise adjacent
zeros propagate backward to \(b_0=0\). Thus

\[
\boxed{E_n\equiv2n^3b_{n-1}D_n\pmod p,}
\tag{7.3}
\]

with a unit multiplier. Strict pointed simplicity is therefore exactly

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rad}\gcd(b_n,E_n)
\mid\prod_{\substack{\ell\le2n+1\\\ell\ {m prime}}}\ell
\quad\text{for every }n\ge1.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is strictly smaller than the moving degree-\(N\) cyclic gcd: one fixed
pair of characteristic-zero integers per index replaces three polynomials.
The cyclic and complementary structures do not prove (7.4).

The smallest genuine reopening is one additional actual Apéry adjoint
covector. A precise sufficient theorem would construct fixed-order recurrent
integer sequences \(U_n,V_n,S_n\), with explicit initial data, such that

\[
U_nb_n+V_nE_n=S_n,
\tag{7.5}
\]

\[
S_n\ne0,
\qquad
\operatorname{rad}(S_n)
\mid\prod_{\substack{\ell\le2n+1\\\ell\ {m prime}}}\ell.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Then a prime \(p>2n+1\) dividing \(b_n,D_n\) would divide \(E_n\) and hence
\(S_n\), contradicting (7.6). The fixed-order recurrence and explicit support
make this a new structural invariant, not a renamed gcd assertion.

Equivalently, one needs a lower-coefficient functional of \(T_n\) forced to
vanish by the common-root hypothesis but independently proved to be a nonzero
small-prime unit. The complementary moment reads only the already known
leading coefficient \(c_n\).

## Final ruling

The exact chain

\[
\widehat H=P^2+AP+B
\Longrightarrow J=B'+P'A
\Longrightarrow A=N^{-1}X(J-B')
\]

produces only the single Fourier relation (2.2). That relation admits exact
common-node models. The complementary moment remains nonzero because it
sees only a leading coefficient, and a nonzero second derivative merely
certifies multiplicity two.

Therefore the proposed cyclic plus complementary-moment route is terminal
without a new actual Apéry lower-coefficient/adjoint identity. The smallest
exact arithmetic gateway is (7.4), and the smallest structural reopening is
(7.5)-(7.6). The five-prime theorem is outside this answer's scope.
