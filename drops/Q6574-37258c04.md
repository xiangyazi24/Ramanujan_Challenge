## 2. Exact strictness/equivalence map

### 2.1 Actual support and q-stripped shell

For one exact active cell, let

```text
P_C = actual lower Palm/partner support after every mask,
S_C = {p : p divides K_C^(q)},
E_C = S_C minus P_C.
```

Here `K_C^(q)` is Q6548's radical carrier with the entire q-primary part removed. Thus `q notin S_C`. The banked support relation is

```text
P_C subseteq S_C.
```

The inclusion is all that follows from a support-blind carrier. Equality requires the separate support-descent assertions listed in Q6562: an admissible shell prime must produce the actual same-prime lower zero relation and must pass every exact mask.

For a finite set `A` and nonnegative partner weights `lambda_C(p)`, define

```text
L_C(A)   = sum_{p in A} lambda_C(p),
Diag_C(A)= sum_{p in A} lambda_C(p)^2,
SF2_C(A) = sum_{p,r in A; p != r} lambda_C(p) lambda_C(r)
         = L_C(A)^2 - Diag_C(A).
```

At the Q6562 Palm layer, `lambda_C(p)=log p`. Ordered pairs are being used. If Q6544 is written with unordered pairs, divide every off-diagonal expression in this section by 2; no exponent changes.

On the identical exact cell, the two local quantities are

```text
CF2_C = Omega_C u_C SF2_C(P_C),
RAD_C = Omega_C u_C SF2_C(S_C).
```

This is the weight-safe form of the comparison. It also covers a presentation in which some common nonnegative factors are placed inside `lambda_C`, provided precisely the same factors occur on the radical side.

### 2.2 Exact excess identity

Because `S_C = P_C disjoint-union E_C`, direct expansion gives

```text
RAD_C - CF2_C
 = Omega_C u_C
   [ 2 L_C(P_C) L_C(E_C) + SF2_C(E_C) ].              (2.1)
```

This is the exact cellwise strictness formula. It has three immediate consequences.

**Pointwise implication.** `CF2_C <= RAD_C` for every exact cell.

**Exact equality criterion.** If `Omega_C u_C > 0` and `P_C` is nonempty, equality holds if and only if `E_C` is empty, namely `S_C=P_C`. Without the nonempty-anchor condition there is one harmless exceptional equality: `P_C` empty and `S_C` containing at most one positive-weight false positive, since a factorial second moment cannot see a singleton.

**Strictness witness.** If `P_C={p}` and `S_C={p,r}` with `r` false, then

```text
CF2_C = 0,
RAD_C = 2 Omega_C u_C (log p)(log r) > 0.
```

Thus the converse implication fails even on one active cell. This is stronger than a vague statement that the radical quantity is larger: it identifies the exact missing mass.

If Q6548 uses an anchored one-sided carrier rather than the full shell SF2, the corresponding identity is

```text
AnchorShell_C - CF2_C
 = Omega_C u_C L_C(P_C) L_C(E_C),                    (2.2)
```

again with ordered/unordered convention tracked. The same support obstruction remains.

## 3. The q-adic derivative split is a four-cell split

Fix one exact cell and abbreviate

```text
D(X) = D_{h,sigma}^{(q)}(X).
```

Assume only q-unit denominators have been cleared, so `D` is a polynomial over the q-local integers and evaluation at `b_t` is q-integral. The selected upper-zero and border relation give

```text
b_t = 0 mod q,
D(b_t) = 0 mod q.
```

Define the derivative bit and first divided/Witt digit

```text
delta_C = D'(b_t) mod q,
omega_C = (D(b_t)/q) mod q.
```

For every `z mod q`, Taylor expansion gives the exact first-jet identity

```text
D(b_t + q z)/q = omega_C + z delta_C mod q.           (3.1)
```

No higher-order term survives modulo q after division by q.

Therefore the exact inherited cell `C` must be split into four subcells. Each subcell carries exactly the same `Omega_C`, `u_C`, affine constraints, and seven mask factors, multiplied only by the indicated q-jet bits.

| Subcell | Conditions | `v_q(D(b_t))` | Number of `z mod q` with `q^2 | D(b_t+qz)` |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| simple/nonzero-Witt | `delta_C != 0`, `omega_C != 0` | exactly 1 | exactly 1, with nonzero correction `z=-omega_C/delta_C` |
| simple/zero-Witt | `delta_C != 0`, `omega_C = 0` | at least 2 | exactly 1, namely `z=0` |
| degenerate/nonzero-Witt | `delta_C = 0`, `omega_C != 0` | exactly 1 | 0 |
| degenerate/zero-Witt | `delta_C = 0`, `omega_C = 0` | at least 2 | all q first lifts |

Two frequently conflated facts are now separated:

- derivative simplicity controls Hensel lift multiplicity;
- the first Witt digit controls whether the evaluated carrier has exactly one q-factor or at least two.

In particular, a simple derivative does **not** imply `v_q(D(b_t))=1`; the simple/zero-Witt cell has valuation at least 2. Conversely, a derivative-degenerate point may have valuation exactly 1.

If the border determinant has the usual affine bordered form

```text
D(X) = det [ A  u ]
           [ v' X ]
     = X det(A) - v' adj(A) u,
```

then `D'(X)=det(A)` is independent of `X`. The derivative split is then literally a split by a bordered minor.

## 4. Why derivative degeneracy does not count selected indices

### 4.1 Collapse at the selected upper zero

Because every selected upper index satisfies `b_t=0 mod q`, for one fixed polynomial `D` we have

```text
D'(b_t) = D'(0) mod q.                                (4.1)
```

Thus, for fixed `(h,sigma,q)` and fixed border polynomial, the derivative branch is all-or-none across selected digits:

```text
D'(0) != 0 mod q  => no selected t is derivative-degenerate;
D'(0)  = 0 mod q  => every selected t satisfying the border relation is derivative-degenerate.
```

A bound such as `number of common roots of D and D' <= degree gcd(D,D')` counts residue values of `x`; here all selected indices have already collapsed to the single residue `x=0`. It cannot count the different recurrence indices `t` producing that residue.

If the coefficients of `D` themselves depend on `t`, then one has a family `D_t`, not one polynomial. In that case (4.1) need not make the branch all-or-none, but a single-polynomial gcd or discriminant count is unavailable for the opposite reason. One first needs a nonzero polynomial or recurrence detector in the index `t`.

### 4.2 The only unconditional digit bound

Let

```text
Z_deg(q;h,sigma)
 = {t : 0 <= t < q,
        b_t = 0 mod q,
        D(b_t) = 0 mod q,
        D'(b_t) = 0 mod q}.
```

The banked zero-set estimate gives only

```text
|Z_deg(q;h,sigma)| <= |Z(q)| <= q^(2/3+o(1)).         (4.2)
```

This is an absolute fixed-power bound in q, but it is not a derivative saving: in the all-degenerate case equality with the selected set is possible. It therefore does not supply a power saving relative to the actual selected-upper mass, and it does not by itself prove the weighted Q6544 tail.

The same-characteristic gap polynomial gives a useful conditional route: if derivative degeneracy were shown to force a second q-zero at a prescribed nonwrapping gap `g`, then the banked bound

```text
#{t : t,t+g in Z(q)} <= 3(g-1)
```

would count the degenerate digits. No implication of the form

```text
D'(b_t)=0 mod q  =>  b_{t+g}=0 mod q
```

is currently present. The derivative equality alone does not create it.

### 4.3 A conditional aggregate fixed-power theorem from a primitive minor

There is one genuine fixed-power mechanism if the bordered derivative is the reduction of a q-independent nonzero integer minor.

**Conditional minor-divisor lemma.** Suppose `q in [T,2T]`, `1 <= h <= H`, and for every `(h,sigma)` there is an integer `Delta_{h,sigma} != 0` such that

```text
D_{h,sigma}^{(q)'}(0) = Delta_{h,sigma} mod q,
log |Delta_{h,sigma}| <= A h log T.                   (4.3)
```

Then the number of derivative-degenerate primes q for fixed `(h,sigma)` is at most `A h`, and the number of distinct derivative-degenerate triples `(h,q,t)` is

```text
sum_{h<=H} sum_{q~T, q divides Delta_{h,sigma}} |Z(q)|
    <= H^2 T^(2/3+o(1)).                              (4.4)
```

**Proof.** If `m` primes `q in [T,2T]` divide a nonzero `Delta`, then `T^m <= |Delta|`; hence `m log T <= log|Delta| <= A h log T`, so `m<=Ah`. Apply (4.2) for each such q and sum `h`.

At the current scales `H=Y^(1/2)=T^(beta/2)`, (4.4) is

```text
T^(2/3 + beta + o(1)).                                (4.5)
```

For `beta<1/10`, this is a fixed power below T. Log weights are harmless in this distinct-triple count. However, (4.3) has not been established for the live q-normalized determinant, and (4.4) does not automatically control multiplicity over lower roots, translates, or repeated exact cells. Three facts are required before claiming it in Q6544's weighted theorem:

1. a primitive integral lift of the derivative minor independent of q;
2. nonvanishing of that integer minor;
3. the height estimate in (4.3), plus a multiplicity audit from triples `(h,q,t)` back to exact marked cells.

If the minor is identically zero, or if q-normalization builds q into the minor, the divisor argument collapses completely.

## 5. The valid divided-carrier statement, and what it does not prove

### 5.1 Exact q-stripped radical lemma

Let `B_C` be a **nonzero integer** obtained from `D(b_t)` after clearing only denominators whose prime factors are explicitly harmless for both q and the lower primes under consideration. Suppose

```text
q^e exactly divides B_C, with e>=1,
q != ell for every lower marked prime ell.
```

Then

```text
rad^(q)(B_C) <= |B_C|/q^e,                            (5.1)
```

where `rad^(q)` omits q. If every actual lower support prime in `P_C` divides `B_C`, then, because the lower primes are distinct from q,

```text
product_{ell in P_C} ell divides B_C/q^e up to powers,
L_C(P_C) <= log|B_C| - e log q,                       (5.2)
SF2_C(P_C) <= (log|B_C| - e log q)^2.                 (5.3)
```

The same inequalities hold with `P_C` replaced by any q-stripped divisor shell contained in the prime support of `B_C`.

The first Witt digit identifies `e=1` versus `e>=2`:

```text
omega_C != 0  <=> e=1,
omega_C  = 0  => e>=2.
```

This is the honest q-stripped saving. It is a factor `q^e` at the integer-carrier level and an additive `e log q` at the logarithmic SF2 level. Notice that the derivative bit does not occur in (5.1)–(5.3).

### 5.2 When a lower prime survives division by q

If an independent integral identity has already proved

```text
q ell divides B_C,
```

then `q != ell` gives the valid conclusion

```text
ell divides B_C/q.                                    (5.4)
```

This is a legitimate mixed characteristic-zero carrier. It uses one common integer and coprimality. It does not use a resultant, and it does not identify `F_q` with `F_ell`.

But neither lower certificate currently supplies the antecedent of (5.4):

- lower singularity gives statements such as `N_h(a)=0 mod ell` and possibly `N_h'(a)=0 mod ell`;
- the long-gap certificate is a property of the lower zero configuration;
- the upper first Witt digit is an element of `F_q`.

One still needs an integral bridge such as

```text
B_C/q = U_C N_h(a) mod ell, with U_C nonzero mod ell, (5.5)
```

or another explicit identity proving `ell | B_C/q`. Without (5.5), the lower information and the divided upper determinant remain independent.

Even lower singularity does not automatically create an extra ell-adic factor. From

```text
N_h(a)=0 mod ell,
N_h'(a)=0 mod ell
```

one cannot conclude `ell^2 | N_h(a)` as an integer. For example, `N(Y)=Y^2+ell` at `Y=0` has both congruences but `N(0)=ell`, not a multiple of `ell^2`.

### 5.3 Exact failed implication

The implication left implicit by the proposed mixed argument is false from the present hypotheses:

```text
q != ell,
q | D(b_t),
D'(b_t) != 0 mod q,
ell | N_h(a),
[ell | N_h'(a) or lower long-gap]

DOES NOT IMPLY

ell | D(b_t)/q,
or a fixed-power bound for rad^(q)(D(b_t)),
or a fixed-power saving for the marked SF2 mass.       (5.6)
```

A minimal interface counterexample is

```text
D(X)=X+q,       b_t=0,
N(Y)=Y^2+ell,   a=0,
q != ell.
```

Then `q|D(0)`, `D'(0)=1 mod q`, the first Witt digit is `D(0)/q=1 mod q`, and `N(0)=N'(0)=0 mod ell`; nevertheless `D(0)/q=1` has no ell factor. A long-gap flag can be imposed on unrelated lower combinatorial data without changing this arithmetic. This counterexample is not claimed to model the Apéry sequence; it proves that the listed interface hypotheses alone do not imply the desired bridge.

The semilocal reason is simple. At distinct primes,

```text
Z localized/completed at q ell has independent q-adic and ell-adic components.
```

The first q-Witt digit constrains the q-component. Lower singularity constrains the ell-component. A common integer identity can couple them; absent such an identity, they are independent. In particular, `ell` is a unit modulo q, so knowing that a divided integer contains an ell-factor would not force its first q-Witt digit to vanish.

### 5.4 Why the valid q-strip is not yet the required power saving

Suppose a continuant/border height estimate only gives

```text
log|B_C| = O(h log T).
```

Then (5.3) gives

```text
SF2_C <= O((h log T - e log T)^2) = O(h^2 log^2 T).
```

For growing `h` this has the same power scale as the unstripped bound. Removing one or any bounded number of prime factors changes the coefficient by `1-O(1/h)`; it does not multiply the whole SF2 mass by `Y^(-eta)` for fixed `eta>0`.

A true power saving therefore requires at least one of:

1. a subcritical height bound for the divided carrier, substantially smaller than the generic `exp(O(h log T))` height;
2. reuse of the same divided carrier by many exact cells, so segment packing turns one radical budget into many marked incidences;
3. a common-q root detector of degree `O(H Y^(-eta))` that counts actual partners and excludes shell false positives;
4. a direct weighted estimate for the false-positive excess in (2.1).

The first Witt digit by itself supplies none of these.

## 6. Why no resultant is available

The lower equations live in `F_ell`:

```text
N_h(a)=0 mod ell,
possibly N_h'(a)=0 mod ell.
```

The upper equations and q-jet live in `F_q`:

```text
b_t=0 mod q,
D(b_t)=0 mod q,
D'(b_t) or D(b_t)/q considered mod q.
```

Since `q != ell`, a resultant of `N_h` and `D` in a shared variable would have no justified common root and no justified common residue field. The affine relation between the **indices** `a,r,s,t` does not identify the recurrence values `a` and `b_t`, nor does it transport an `F_ell` root to `F_q`.

A valid elimination must first produce one of the following:

- a single integer identity divisible by both q and ell, as in (5.4)–(5.5); or
- a polynomial over one fixed field, normally `F_q`, whose roots are the transported actual lower partners.

Without that transport, a resultant is invalid rather than merely weak.

## 7. Smallest successors

### 7.1 Smallest derivative successor

or an equivalent integral identity. Equation (7.1) is the smallest statement that makes the lower prime a genuine divisor of the q-divided upper carrier. It is strictly weaker than the final moment bound but is the missing arithmetic bridge. A first Witt digit without (7.1) is not such a bridge.
