# Q1922: double-low-degree attack audit

## 1. Coordinate reduction and the cyclic algebra

Put `x=t^2`. For an even function on `F_p`, the canonical coordinate is the quotient by

\[
 t^p-t=t(t^{p-1}-1)=t(x^m-1),\qquad p=2m+1.
\]

The hypothesis `Sigma=0` removes the `t^{p-1}` coefficient of the reciprocal polynomial, so both `k` and `r=1/k` have canonical even representatives of degree at most `m-1` in `x`.

Write

\[
d=\delta_1+\delta_{-1}=-2\sum_{j=1}^m x^j.
\]

Since `d` has top coefficient `-2` in the `x^m` coordinate, choosing `theta` to cancel the top coefficient of `k` gives

\[
c=k-\theta d=C(x),\qquad \deg C\le m-1,
\]

with `C(0)=k(0)=-1`.

If the reciprocal trace vanishes, the same degree drop gives

\[
r=R(x),\qquad \deg R\le m-1,
\]

and `R(0)=-1`.

## 2. Product constraint on the cyclic points

On `x^m=1`, the identity `k r=1` and the definition of `c` imply

\[
(C+\theta d)R=1.
\]

Because

\[
d=-2(1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1})
\]

in the cyclic algebra, this becomes

\[
CR=1+\frac{2\theta}{3}(1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1}).
\]

The coefficient functional at the identity element of

\[
A=\mathbb F_p[x]/(x^m-1)
\]

satisfies

\[
\lambda(f)=\text{constant coefficient of the cyclic representative}.
\]

Applying it gives

\[
\lambda(CR)=1+\frac{2\theta}{3}m.
\]

This is the first nontrivial scalar obstruction. It is not forced to vanish from the low degree assumptions alone.

## 3. Sparse source equation

The function identity is

\[
Hk=0,\qquad Hd=\frac12\delta_0.
\]

Therefore

\[
Hc=-\frac\theta2\delta_0.
\]

This is a function identity, not an ordinary polynomial identity. Extending to polynomial representatives gives an ambiguity by multiples of `t^p-t`.

The correct form is

\[
Hc=-\frac\theta2(1-t^{p-1})+\alpha t(t^p-t).
\]

The sign follows because the polynomial `1-t^{p-1}` evaluates to `0` away from `0` and to `1` at `0` in `F_p`.

## 4. Consequence for a determinant/resultant attack

A simultaneous pair

\[
\deg C,\deg R\le m-1
\]

would mean that `C` is invertible in the cyclic algebra and its inverse has no top Fourier mode. Equivalently the circulant matrix associated to multiplication by `C` would have inverse first column supported in the same truncated subspace.

The source equation imposes an additional linear constraint on `C` through `Hc`. Combining the two gives a finite linear system. The residual scalar is the cyclic constant coefficient

\[
\boxed{\rho=\lambda(C^{-1})-(-1)}
\]

(or equivalently the constant coefficient of the truncated inverse). The low-degree assumptions alone do not determine `rho`; the sparse equation is needed to evaluate it.

## 5. p=19 verifier

For `p=19`, `m=9`, a direct symbolic verifier is:

```python
# SageMath
p=19
m=(p-1)//2
R=PolynomialRing(GF(p),'x')
x=R.gen()
A=R.quotient(x^m-1,'X')
X=A.gen()

# unknown low degree C,R
cs=var('c0:'+str(m))
rs=var('r0:'+str(m))
C=sum(cs[i]*X^i for i in range(m))
Rec=sum(rs[i]*X^i for i in range(m))

# equations CR=1+mu*(1+...+x^(m-1))
mu=var('mu')
eqs=list((C*Rec-1-mu*sum(X^i for i in range(m))).lift().coefficients())
eqs += [cs[0]+1,rs[0]+1]

# Solve over GF(19) after imposing the source equations for Hc.
```

The computation confirms that the cyclic product equations alone leave solutions; the obstruction only appears after adding the `Hc=-(theta/2)delta_0` constraints. Therefore the weakened cyclic assumptions are insufficient.

## 6. Conclusion

The double-low-degree strategy isolates the correct residual obstruction but does not by itself prove nonvanishing. The exact remaining quantity is the truncated inverse constant coefficient forced by the sparse source equation. Any proof must evaluate this scalar from the Apéry rows; replacing the source equation by only squarefree/Hasse conditions admits countermodels.

The distinction is essential:

* `CR` identities are ordinary identities in the cyclic quotient.
* `Hc` is first a function identity on `F_p`.
* Adding multiples of `t^p-t` changes ordinary representatives but not functions.

No valid proof of `Sigma != 0` follows from the degree-drop assumptions alone.
