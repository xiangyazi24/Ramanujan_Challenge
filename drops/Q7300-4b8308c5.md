# A sharp inverse obstruction for high Apéry factorial moments

## Verdict

There is a quantitative inverse statement, but in the stated combinatorial category its sharp conclusion is only a **dyadic forest of stars**.  It cannot force repeated quotients, repeated reflection gaps, a bounded-complexity congruence graph, or a bounded-order additive relation.

More strongly, for every fixed complexity bound one can build a reflection-invariant modular set system in the exact window \(n<X^2\) which

* has \(F_2\) at the independence scale;
* has \(F_k/(X^2\lambda^k)\asymp X^{k-2}\), the largest order allowed by CRT, for every fixed \(k>6\);
* has only one row of degree \(>6\);
* has pair-codegree at most \(6\);
* has distinct quotients \(\lfloor n/p\rfloor\), distinct residues, and distinct signed and unsigned reflection gaps on the high row;
* avoids every prescribed finite list of bounded-completion configurations, including all bounded-coefficient additive relations of any fixed order;
* satisfies the local reflection, interval, gap-polynomial, gap-energy, fiber-energy, and primewise Fourier tests already available in the repository.

Thus no inverse theorem based only on those inputs can conclude more than a star.  The missing statement must be genuinely Apéry-specific and horizontal across characteristics.

The construction below strengthens the anchored-star obstruction in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` and is consistent with the corrected bound \(F_2\le 4X^2\lambda^2\) in `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex`.

## 1. The sharp generic inverse: a dyadic star forest

Put

\[
 M=X^2,\qquad
 \mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},
\]
\[
 K(n)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}{\bf 1}_{\{n\bmod p\in Z_p\}},
 \qquad
 \lambda=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p},
 \qquad
 F_j=\sum_{n<M}(K(n))_j.
\]

### Theorem 1 (dyadic star-forest inverse)

Fix \(k\ge 3\).  Assume

\[
 F_2\le C_2M\lambda^2.
\]

If

\[
 A_k:=\frac{F_k}{M\lambda^k}>0,
\]

then there are a dyadic number \(D\ge k\) and a set of rows

\[
 R_D=\{n<M:D\le K(n)<2D\}
\]

such that, with \(J\le 2+\log_2 X\),

\[
 |R_D|D^k\ge \frac{F_k}{2^kJ},
 \qquad
 |R_D|D^2\le 2F_2\le 2C_2M\lambda^2,
\]

and consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 D\ge
 \lambda\left(\frac{A_k}{2^{k+1}C_2J}\right)^{1/(k-2)}.}
\]

Equivalently, if \(F_k\ge X^\eta M\lambda^k\), then

\[
 D\gg_{k,C_2}
 \lambda\,\frac{X^{\eta/(k-2)}}{(\log X)^{1/(k-2)}}.
\]

#### Proof

Partition all rows with \(K(n)\ge k\) into the \(J=O(\log X)\) bins

\[
 D\le K(n)<2D,
 \qquad D=k,2k,4k,\ldots .
\]

One bin contributes at least \(F_k/J\).  On that bin,

\[
 (K(n))_k\le K(n)^k<(2D)^k,
\]

so

\[
 |R_D|D^k\ge F_k/(2^kJ).
\]

Since \(D\ge k\ge3\),

\[
 (K(n))_2=K(n)(K(n)-1)\ge D(D-1)\ge D^2/2,
\]

and therefore

\[
 |R_D|D^2\le2F_2.
\]

Combining the two inequalities gives

\[
 D^{k-2}\ge
 \frac{F_k}{2^{k+1}JF_2}
 \ge
 \frac{A_k\lambda^{k-2}}{2^{k+1}C_2J},
\]

which is the claimed bound.  ∎

This is more informative than merely selecting one maximum-degree row: it gives the exact tradeoff between the number of popular rows and their degree.  The next theorem shows that this tradeoff is essentially the entire generic inverse theory.  In its example \(R_D\) consists of one row, and both displayed inequalities are sharp up to constants, with no pigeonhole logarithm.

## 2. Bounded-completion configurations

The obstruction will avoid not just repeated quotients and gaps but any fixed family of finite configurations for which the last column is determined up to \(O(1)\) choices.

### Definition 2

Let \(\mathcal C_X\) be a set of candidate primes.  An \(r\)-ary relation \(\mathcal R_X\) on distinct members of \(\mathcal C_X\) has **completion number at most \(C\)** if, after fixing any \(r-1\) entries and the position of the missing entry, at most \(C\) candidates complete an instance of \(\mathcal R_X\).

Examples include the following whenever the coordinate map \(\phi:\mathcal C_X\to\mathbb Z\) has bounded fibers:

\[
 \phi(p_i)=\phi(p_j),
\]

and, for fixed \(s,B\), every relation

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^t c_i\phi(p_i)=0,
 \qquad
 2\le t\le s,
 \qquad
 0<|c_i|\le B.
\]

The same applies to any fixed nonzero polynomial relation of bounded degree in one injective coordinate, provided specialization in the final coordinate is not identically zero.

### Lemma 3 (greedy avoidance)

Fix \(s\ge2\) and a finite family \(\mathfrak C\) of relations of arity at most \(s\), all with completion number at most a fixed \(C\).  If \(N=|\mathcal C_X|\), then \(\mathcal C_X\) contains a subset \(S\) with

\[
 |S|\gg_{s,C,|\mathfrak C|}N^{1/(s-1)}
\]

which contains no instance of any relation in \(\mathfrak C\).

#### Proof

Suppose \(t\) candidates have already been selected.  A relation of arity \(r\le s\) can make a new candidate forbidden only after choosing \(r-1\) old candidates, their order, and the missing position.  There are \(O_s(t^{r-1})\) such choices, and each forbids at most \(C\) new candidates.  Summing over the fixed relation family gives

\[
 O_{s,C,|\mathfrak C|}((t+1)^{s-1})
\]

forbidden candidates.  Greedy selection therefore continues until

\[
 t\asymp_{s,C,|\mathfrak C|}N^{1/(s-1)}.
\]

Relations entirely among old candidates never arise because they were excluded at the earlier stage.  ∎

## 3. A general-position anchored rainbow star

### Theorem 4 (no inverse stronger than a star)

Fix

* a moment order \(k>6\);
* a configuration order \(s\ge2\);
* a coefficient bound \(B\ge1\); and
* any fixed finite family \(\mathfrak C\) of bounded-completion relations on at most \(s\) distinct columns.

For every sufficiently large integral \(X\), there is a family

\[
 (Z_p^*)_{p\in\mathcal P_X},
 \qquad
 Z_p^*\subseteq\{0,\ldots,p-1\},
\]

with the following properties.

1. There is an anchor \(m_0<X^2\) and a set \(S\subset(X,6X/5]\) of active primes, of size

   \[
   L:=|S|\asymp_{s,B,\mathfrak C}
   \left(\frac X{\log X}\right)^{1/(s-1)},
   \]

   such that every active column contains \(m_0\).

2. Writing

   \[
   q_p=\left\lfloor\frac{m_0}{p}\right\rfloor,
   \qquad
   a_p=m_0-q_pp,
   \qquad
   g_p=p-1-2a_p,
   \]

   the four coordinates \(p,q_p,a_p,g_p\), as well as \(|g_p|\), are injective on \(S\).  The selected columns avoid every configuration in \(\mathfrak C\).  In particular, they may be chosen so that for each

   \[
   \phi\in\{p,q,a,g,|g|\}
   \]

   there is no nontrivial relation

   \[
   \sum_{i=1}^t c_i\phi(p_i)=0,
   \qquad
   2\le t\le s,
   \qquad
   0<|c_i|\le B,
   \]

   among distinct active primes.

3. Every nonempty \(Z_p^*\) consists of one reflection orbit,

   \[
   Z_p^*=\{a_p,p-1-a_p\}.
   \]

   It avoids \(0,1,p-2,p-1\), has no consecutive elements, satisfies every interval bound

   \[
   |Z_p^*\cap I|\le 2|I|^{2/3},
   \]

   and has primewise Fourier transform bounded by \(2\).

4. Every pair of distinct rows has codegree at most \(6\):

   \[
   \#\{p:m\bmod p,n\bmod p\in Z_p^*\}\le6.
   \]

   Consequently

   \[
   K^*(n)\le6\qquad(n\ne m_0).
   \]

5. The second factorial moment has the independence scale,

   \[
   F_2^*\asymp L^2\asymp X^2(\lambda^*)^2,
   \qquad
   \lambda^*=\sum_p\frac{|Z_p^*|}{p}\asymp\frac L X.
   \]

   The centered variance also has its natural scale:

   \[
   \sum_{n<X^2}(K^*(n)-\mu^*)^2\ll X^2\lambda^*,
   \qquad
   \mu^*=X^{-2}\sum_{n<X^2}K^*(n).
   \]

6. For every \(j>6\), all of the \(j\)-th factorial moment is carried by the one anchor row:

   \[
   \boxed{F_j^*=(L)_j.}
   \]

   Hence, for every fixed \(j>6\),

   \[
   \boxed{
   \frac{F_j^*}{X^2(\lambda^*)^j}\asymp_j X^{j-2}.}
   \]

This is the maximal possible power of \(X\), since for every modular set system

\[
 F_j\le\left(\sum_p|Z_p|\right)^j
 \le(2X\lambda)^j,
\]

so

\[
 \frac{F_j}{X^2\lambda^j}\le 2^jX^{j-2}.
\]

### Proof

#### 3.1. Candidate primes and the quotient geometry

Let

\[
 M=X^2,
 \qquad
 m_0=\left\lfloor\frac{3M}{4}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 d=2m_0+1.
\]

Delete from the primes in \((X,6X/5]\) those dividing

\[
 E=(m_0-1)m_0(m_0+1)(m_0+2)d.
\]

Only \(O(1)\) primes are deleted: \(|E|\ll X^{10}\), and every deleted prime exceeds \(X\).  The remaining candidate set \(\mathcal C_X\) therefore has

\[
 |\mathcal C_X|\asymp\frac X{\log X}.
\]

For \(p\in\mathcal C_X\), define

\[
 q_p=\lfloor m_0/p\rfloor,
 \qquad
 a_p=m_0-q_pp,
 \qquad
 g_p=p-1-2a_p.
\]

The deleted factors ensure

\[
 a_p\notin\{0,1,p-2,p-1\},
 \qquad
 g_p\ne0.
\]

The quotient map is injective.  If \(p<p'\) are two candidate primes, then \(p'-p\ge2\), and

\[
 \frac{m_0}{p}-\frac{m_0}{p'}
 =\frac{m_0(p'-p)}{pp'}
 \ge
 \frac{2(3X^2/4-1)}{(6X/5)^2}
 =\frac{25}{24}-O(X^{-2})>1.
\]

Thus their floors are different.

The residue map is also injective.  If \(a_p=a_{p'}=a\), then the distinct primes \(p,p'>X\) both divide the positive integer \(m_0-a<X^2\), while \(pp'>X^2\), a contradiction.

Finally,

\[
 d+g_p=(2q_p+1)p.
\]

For a fixed signed value \(g\), every prime in its fiber divides \(d+g\), whose absolute value is \(<2X^2\) for large \(X\).  Three distinct primes \(>X\) cannot divide such an integer.  Hence every signed-gap fiber has size at most \(2\), and every absolute-gap fiber has size at most \(4\).  Retaining one candidate from each absolute-gap fiber leaves a pool

\[
 \mathcal C_X'\subseteq\mathcal C_X,
 \qquad
 |\mathcal C_X'|\gg X/\log X,
\]

on which all five coordinates

\[
 p,\ q_p,\ a_p,\ g_p,\ |g_p|
\]

are injective.

Apply Lemma 3 to \(\mathcal C_X'\), including in \(\mathfrak C\) all desired bounded-coefficient additive relations in those coordinates.  Choose exactly

\[
 L\asymp_{s,B,\mathfrak C}
 (X/\log X)^{1/(s-1)}
\]

surviving primes and call their set \(S\).

#### 3.2. The zero sets and the rainbow spokes

For \(p\in S\), put

\[
 Z_p^*=\{a_p,p-1-a_p\};
\]

for all other primes in \(\mathcal P_X\), put \(Z_p^*=\varnothing\).

Every active prime marks \(m_0\), because \(m_0\bmod p=a_p\).  Its reflected residue marks the companion row

\[
 n_p=m_0+g_p
 =q_pp+(p-1-a_p).
\]

Since \(|g_p|<p\le6X/5\) and \(m_0\) is a distance \(\gg X^2\) from both ends of \([0,M)\), every \(n_p\) also lies in \([0,M)\).  The companion rows are all distinct because \(g_p\) is injective.  Thus the construction is literally an anchored rainbow star with no repeated spoke displacement.

The two residues in an active column are distinct and nonconsecutive.  Indeed their signed difference is \(g_p\), which is a nonzero even integer.  Reflection invariance and endpoint avoidance are built in.  An interval contains at most two marked residues, proving the displayed interval bound.

For every frequency \(t\),

\[
 \left|\sum_{r\in Z_p^*}e_p(tr)\right|\le2.
\]

In particular, for \(K_0\le p/2\),

\[
 \sum_{1\le|t|\le K_0}
 \left|\sum_{r\in Z_p^*}e_p(tr)\right|^2
 \le8K_0,
\]

which is stronger than the available short-window Fourier bound in `atom_tail_section.tex` for a two-point zero set.

There is only one positive integer gap inside \(Z_p^*\).  Hence the usual gap multiplicity is at most one and every standard per-column gap energy is \(O(1)\).

The local continuant certificate is also automatic.  The repository proves the symbolic reflection identity

\[
 N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).
\]

Let

\[
 u_p=\min(a_p,p-1-a_p),
 \qquad
 h_p=|g_p|=p-1-2u_p.
\]

Then \(h_p\) is positive and even, while

\[
 -u_p-h_p-1=u_p-p.
\]

Reducing the reflection identity modulo \(p\) therefore gives

\[
 N_{h_p}(u_p)\equiv -N_{h_p}(u_p)\pmod p,
\]

so

\[
 N_{h_p}(u_p)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Thus even the necessary gap-polynomial certificate for the unique pair is satisfied.

If one also wants a full palindromic value map, partition \(\mathbb F_p\) into the orbits of \(r\mapsto p-1-r\), assign value \(0\) to the selected orbit, and assign distinct nonzero values to all other orbits.  Every fiber then has size at most two and the collision energy is at most \(2p\).

#### 3.3. Uniform row codegrees

Fix distinct rows \(0\le m<n<M\) and put \(h=n-m\).  Suppose an active prime \(p\) marks both rows.  Each residue is either \(a_p\) or \(-1-a_p\pmod p\).  Therefore

\[
 h\equiv0,\quad d,\quad\text{or}\quad-d\pmod p,
\]

because \(2a_p+1\equiv d\pmod p\).  Hence every common active prime divides

\[
 H_h=h(h-d)(h+d).
\]

This integer is nonzero: \(0<h<M<d\).  Moreover

\[
 |H_h|<6M^3=6X^6.
\]

Seven distinct primes \(>X\) have product \(>X^7>6X^6\), so at most six active primes divide \(H_h\).  This proves the codegree bound.

Since every active prime marks \(m_0\), for \(n\ne m_0\) one has

\[
 K^*(n)
 =\#\bigl(\mathcal P^*(n)\cap\mathcal P^*(m_0)\bigr)
 \le6.
\]

#### 3.4. Moments

At the anchor,

\[
 K^*(m_0)=L.
\]

For \(j>6\), every other row contributes zero to the falling factorial, and therefore

\[
 F_j^*=(L)_j.
\]

For the second moment, the anchor gives

\[
 F_2^*\ge(L)_2.
\]

For distinct active primes \(p,q\), each of the four residue pairs has at most one representative below \(M\), because \(pq>M\).  Thus

\[
 F_2^*\le4(L)_2.
\]

Also

\[
 \frac{5L}{3X}
 \le
 \lambda^*=2\sum_{p\in S}\frac1p
 \le
 \frac{2L}{X},
\]

because \(X<p\le6X/5\).  Consequently

\[
 F_2^*\asymp L^2\asymp M(\lambda^*)^2.
\]

The first incidence count is

\[
 \sum_{n<M}K^*(n)=M\lambda^*+O(L)\ll M\lambda^*.
\]

Since \(\lambda^*<1\),

\[
 \sum_n(K^*(n)-\mu^*)^2
 \le\sum_nK^*(n)^2
 =F_2^*+\sum_nK^*(n)
 \ll M\lambda^*.
\]

Finally, for fixed \(j>6\),

\[
 \frac{F_j^*}{M(\lambda^*)^j}
 \asymp
 \frac{L^j}{X^2(L/X)^j}
 =X^{j-2}.
\]

This completes the proof.  ∎

## 4. Exact consequences for proposed inverse conclusions

Theorem 4 defeats each suggested generic conclusion, even when \(F_k\) is as large as CRT permits.

| Proposed inverse conclusion | What the construction does |
|---|---|
| Repeated \(\lfloor m/p\rfloor\) | All \(q_p\) are distinct. |
| Repeated lower residues | All \(a_p\) are distinct. |
| Many shared reflection gaps | Both \(g_p\) and \(|g_p|\) are distinct. |
| Many columns sharing any row gap | Every pair of rows has codegree at most \(6\). |
| A short additive cycle among spoke gaps | Avoided for every prescribed order \(\le s\) and coefficient bound \(B\). |
| A bounded-complexity congruence graph | Any fixed bounded-completion family can be included in \(\mathfrak C\) and avoided. |
| Several high-degree rows | The anchor is the only row of degree \(>6\). |
| Failure of a local continuant certificate | The unique reflection pair satisfies \(N_{h_p}(u_p)\equiv0\pmod p\) automatically. |
| Failure of primewise Fourier control | Every Fourier coefficient has modulus at most \(2\). |

The only unavoidable structure is

\[
 m_0\equiv a_p\pmod p
 \qquad(p\in S),
\]

namely the star itself.  The one-column identities

\[
 m_0=q_pp+a_p,
 \qquad
 d+g_p=(2q_p+1)p
\]

are tautological encodings of that star, not additional multi-column structure.

Therefore a theorem deduced solely from

* \(n<X^2\);
* reflection;
* the \(F_2\) bound;
* bounded pair codegrees;
* interval and gap energy;
* local continuant certificates; and
* primewise Fourier estimates

cannot force any of the stronger configurations listed in the question.  It can only force the dyadic star forest of Theorem 1.

## 5. The minimal Apéry-specific lemma

For the actual Apéry numbers, define for a fixed row \(m<M\)

\[
 q_p(m)=\left\lfloor\frac mp\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 r_p(m)=m-q_p(m)p,
 \qquad
 g_p(m)=p-1-2r_p(m).
\]

If \(p\) is counted by \(K_X(m)\), then

\[
 b_{r_p(m)}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Reflection gives

\[
 b_{p-1-r_p(m)}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Since \(q_p(m)<X<p\), the two-digit Lucas law gives the two genuine integer divisibilities

\[
 p\mid b_m,
 \qquad
 p\mid b_{m+g_p(m)},
\]

because

\[
 m+g_p(m)=q_p(m)p+(p-1-r_p(m)).
\]

Thus an actual high row is a reflected-spoke star in the common integer sequence \(b_n\).  The construction above shows that all spoke displacements may nevertheless be distinct and in bounded-order general position.  That is the configuration the recurrence must exclude.

### Minimal configuration-exclusion interface

A precise falsifiable lemma is the following.

#### \(\mathrm{HRS}_{k}\): horizontal reflected-spoke exclusion

For a fixed \(k>6\), uniformly for \(m<X^2\), every set \(S\subset\mathcal P_X\) satisfying

\[
 b_{r_p(m)}\equiv0\pmod p
 \qquad(p\in S)
\]

and lying in the general-position regime

\[
 q_p(m),\ r_p(m),\ g_p(m),\ |g_p(m)|
\]

with no repeated coordinate and no prescribed bounded-order relation, obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 |S|\ll_k X^{2/k+o(1)}\lambda_X+O_k(1).}
\]

The cleaner, stronger uniform form is

\[
 \tag{HA_k}
 \boxed{
 \max_{m<X^2}K_X(m)
 \ll_k X^{2/k+o(1)}\lambda_X+O_k(1).}
\]

This exponent is forced by the desired moment and is therefore the minimal pointwise scale at which the single-star obstruction can be excluded.  Indeed, if

\[
 F_k\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k,
\]

then for \(K_X(m)\ge2k\),

\[
 (K_X(m))_k\ge(K_X(m)/2)^k,
\]

so

\[
 K_X(m)\ll X^{2/k+o(1)}\lambda_X.
\]

For the two relevant first choices,

\[
 k=7:\quad K_X(m)\ll X^{2/7+o(1)}\lambda_X+O(1),
\]

\[
 k=8:\quad K_X(m)\ll X^{1/4+o(1)}\lambda_X+O(1).
\]

Thus \(\mathrm{HA}_k\) is not an arbitrary strengthening: every proof of \(\mathrm{HM}_k\) must imply it, and it is exactly what rules out a one-row maximal star.

It is important not to overstate this.  \(\mathrm{HA}_k\) by itself does **not** prove \(\mathrm{HM}_k\), because many rows just below the atom threshold may still carry too much mass.  A stronger atom-tail statement such as

\[
 \max_{m<X^2}K_X(m)\ll\lambda_XX^{o(1)}
\]

would suffice, since

\[
 F_k
 \le(\max_mK_X(m))^{k-2}F_2
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k.
\]

For the present question, however, \(\mathrm{HRS}_k\) or \(\mathrm{HA}_k\) is the minimal Apéry-specific lemma needed to kill the configuration produced by Theorem 4.

### A concrete carrier formulation

One possible recurrence-based implementation of \(\mathrm{HRS}_k\) would be:

> For every general-position reflected-spoke family \(S\), construct a nonzero integer \(C(m,S)\) such that
> \[
> \prod_{p\in S}p\mid C(m,S)
> \]
> and
> \[
> \log|C(m,S)|
> \ll
> \bigl(X^{2/k+o(1)}\lambda_X+O_k(1)\bigr)\log X.
> \]

Then \(\prod_{p\in S}p>X^{|S|}\) immediately gives \(\mathrm{HRS}_k\).

This formulation is falsifiable and pinpoints the missing arithmetic.  The currently available common carrier \(C=b_m\) is far too large:

\[
 \log b_m=O(m)=O(X^2),
\]

which gives only \(K_X(m)=O(X^2/\log X)\), weaker than the number of available primes.  The continuant values \(N_{|g_p|}(u_p)\) are not a common carrier: their indices and arguments vary with \(p\), and on a reflection orbit their divisibility is forced by the symbolic reflection identity itself.  Primewise Fourier control is likewise local and survives the counterexample.

Hence a successful proof must supply one of the following genuinely horizontal inputs:

1. a low-height common carrier across varying characteristics;
2. a mixed-characteristic correlation estimate that rules out general-position reflected spokes; or
3. a direct Palm contraction controlling the mass of an entire dyadic star forest.

Repeated-quotient, repeated-gap, and bounded-additive-relation pigeonholes cannot be obtained from the present geometry: Theorem 4 removes all of them while preserving a maximal high moment.

## 6. Project-level conclusion

The pure combinatorial inverse avenue has a precise terminal verdict:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{large }F_k
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{a dyadic star forest, and generically nothing stronger}.}
\]

The loss and exponents are explicit:

\[
 |R_D|D^k\gg_k F_k/\log X,
 \qquad
 |R_D|D^2\ll X^2\lambda_X^2,
 \qquad
 D\gg_k\lambda_X
 \left(\frac{F_k}{X^2\lambda_X^k\log X}\right)^{1/(k-2)}.
\]

This is sharp up to the dyadic logarithm.  Any next step that merely re-pigeonholes quotients, gaps, residues, or local Fourier modes cannot close \(\mathrm{HM}_k\).  The next genuinely new lemma must be an Apéry horizontal orbit-selection statement of the form \(\mathrm{HRS}_k\), or a stronger atom-tail/Palm estimate that implies it.
