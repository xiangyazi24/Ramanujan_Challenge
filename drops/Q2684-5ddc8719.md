# P3.2 targeted M1 route: exact reflection-orbit carrier and the first obstruction

## Verdict

There is a clean characteristic-zero carrier for every pair of **distinct reflection orbits**, and reflection makes its p-adic multiplicity bookkeeping exact. This is stronger and cleaner than using the raw two-gap resultant.

However, the carrier furnished by the banked gap/reflection machinery has ordinary aggregate logarithmic height

\[
\Theta(X^3\log X),
\]

not \(O(X)\) or \(O(X\,\mathrm{polylog}\,X)\). The raw two-gap resultant family is worse: the repository's own Sylvester--Hadamard estimate is

\[
\log |\widehat R_{h,k}|=O(hk\log(h+k)),
\]

which gives an \(O(X^4\log X)\) full-parameter majorant.

The marked/Racah and bordered-determinant statements do **not** provide a lower-height substitute. In fact the exact Q2328 scan shows that on genuine nonmate zero pairs the associated Racah resultant is a **p-unit**, while the marked two-gap determinant has a structural mirror root at exactly the reflection point. Thus neither is an Apéry-selective divisibility carrier after the structural mirror factor is removed.

So M1 is not banked. It reduces to one precise new arithmetic statement:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{h\ge2,\ d\ge2\ {m even}\\2h+d\le2X}}
\log \operatorname{rad}_{(X,2X]} A_{h,d}=O(X),
}
\tag{AH-M1}
\]

for the explicit positive integers

\[
A_{h,d}:=2^{3(h-1)}N_h\!\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(N_h\) is the repository's cleared gap polynomial. `(AH-M1)` implies

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X} Z(p)=O(X/\log X)
\]

with no further conjecture. No banked theorem proves `(AH-M1)`. The first exact obstruction is therefore **large-prime radical compression across the two border parameters \((h,d)\)**, not existence/nonvanishing of a carrier.

An ordinary-height bound \(O(X\,\mathrm{polylog}\,X)\) with a positive power of \(\log X\) is not enough for the stated M1: after division by \(\log X\) it leaves the corresponding polylogarithmic loss.

## 1. Exact reduction of M1 to pairs of reflection orbits

For an odd prime \(p\), write the reflection involution as

\[
\iota_p(r)=p-1-r.
\]

Let \(k_p\) be the number of two-element zero orbits and let
\(\varepsilon_p\in\{0,1\}\) record whether the midpoint \((p-1)/2\) is a zero. Then exactly

\[
Z(p)=2k_p+\varepsilon_p.
\tag{2}
\]

Choose the lower representative from each two-element orbit:

\[
L_p:=\{r\in Z_p:0\le r<(p-1)/2\},
\qquad |L_p|=k_p.
\]

Put

\[
P_2(X):=\sum_{X<p\le2X}\binom{k_p}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

Since for every integer \(k\ge0\)

\[
k\le 1+\binom{k}{2},
\]

we have

\[
Z(p)\le 3+2\binom{k_p}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)
\le 3\bigl(\pi(2X)-\pi(X)\bigr)+2P_2(X).
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the exact sufficient pair target is

\[
\boxed{P_2(X)=O(X/\log X).}
\tag{6}
\]

The midpoint has disappeared at the cost of only \(O(\pi(X))\), exactly as desired.

A canonical orbit pair is simply an unordered pair \(r<s\) in \(L_p\). It represents four raw unordered nonmate zero pairs after choosing independently one point from each two-element orbit. The Q2328 exact scanner sees precisely this factor: every canonical lower pair in its output has `orbit_count = 4`.

## 2. Reflection converts one orbit pair into a fixed half-integer gap value

Take a canonical pair

\[
0\le r<s<(p-1)/2,
\qquad b_r\equiv b_s\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Define

\[
h:=s-r,
\qquad
d:=p-1-2s.
\tag{7}
\]

Then

\[
h\ge1,\qquad d\ge2,\qquad d\equiv0\pmod2,
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
p=2r+2h+d+1.
\tag{9}
\]

The banked gap identity says that a double zero at distance \(h\) forces

\[
N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{10}
\]

The banked reflection identity is

\[
N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).
\tag{11}
\]

Put

\[
x_d:=\frac{d-1}{2}.
\]

Because \(d=p-1-2s\), in \(\mathbf F_p\)

\[
x_d\equiv -s-1,
\]

and therefore

\[
-x_d-h-1\equiv s-h=r.
\]

Combining (10) and (11),

\[
N_h(x_d)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{12}
\]

Since \(\deg N_h=3(h-1)\) and \(N_h\in\mathbf Z[x]\), the denominator-cleared value

\[
\boxed{
A_{h,d}:=2^{3(h-1)}N_h\!\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)\in\mathbf Z
}
\tag{13}
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{p\mid A_{h,d}.}
\tag{14}
\]

This is already the desired characteristic-zero carrier. It uses only one gap polynomial plus reflection; no all-\(h,k\) resultant-coprimality theorem is needed.

### Nonmate condition removes the forced mirror factor

For even \(h\), the repository has the forced factor

\[
2x+h+1\mid N_h(x).
\]

At the actual lower root \(r\),

\[
2r+h+1=r+s+1,
\]

which lies strictly between \(0\) and \(p\), since \(r,s<(p-1)/2\). Hence it is a p-unit. Equivalently, at the reflected border point \(x_d\),

\[
2x_d+h+1=d+h=p-1-r-s,
\]

again a positive integer \(<p\). Therefore the divisibility in (14) is **not** the trivial mate factor. One may divide the even-\(h\) forced factor out of \(A_{h,d}\) without losing the prime \(p\).

Similarly, the banked content restriction says a prime \(p\ge7\) can divide the content of \(N_h\) only when \(h\ge2p\). Here \(h<p/2\), so the dyadic prime is not coming from polynomial content either.

This is the correct way to use border nonvanishing: it shows the characteristic-zero value is nonzero and the p-divisibility is genuine. It does **not** exclude the zero pair.

## 3. The one-sided border value is always nonzero; in fact it is positive

The repository gap continuants use

\[
P(t)=34t^3+51t^2+27t+5
\]

and

\[
N_1(x)=1,\qquad
N_2(x)=P(x+1),
\]

\[
N_{m+1}(x)=P(x+m)N_m(x)-(x+m)^6N_{m-1}(x).
\tag{15}
\]

There is a short all-\(h\) positivity proof on the one-sided border.

For \(x\ge0\), set

\[
R_m(x)=\frac{N_m(x)}{N_{m-1}(x)}.
\]

I claim

\[
R_m(x)>(x+m)^3\qquad(m\ge2).
\tag{16}
\]

For \(m=2\), putting \(t=x+1\ge1\),

\[
P(t)-(t+1)^3
=33t^3+48t^2+24t+4>0.
\]

For the induction step, if \(R_m(x)>(x+m)^3\), put \(t=x+m\). Then

\[
R_{m+1}(x)
=P(t)-\frac{t^6}{R_m(x)}
>P(t)-t^3,
\]

while

\[
P(t)-t^3-(t+1)^3
=32t^3+48t^2+24t+4
=4(2t+1)^3>0.
\]

This proves (16). Consequently

\[
N_h(x)>0\qquad(x\ge0),
\tag{17}
\]

so in particular

\[
\boxed{A_{h,d}>0}
\tag{18}
\]

for every \(h\ge1\), even \(d\ge2\).

Notice what (18) says and does **not** say. It proves that the characteristic-zero border value is a legitimate nonzero integer. A genuine modular zero pair is perfectly compatible with it: the pair says that its characteristic \(p\) divides this positive integer.

For \(h=1\), \(A_{1,d}=1\), so (14) is impossible. This recovers the no-consecutive-zero fact directly.

## 4. Exact size of the border carrier

The same ratio argument gives matching elementary size bounds.

From (16),

\[
N_h(x)>
\prod_{j=2}^{h}(x+j)^3.
\tag{19}
\]

On the other hand the subtraction term in (15) is positive, so

\[
N_h(x)<
\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}P(x+j).
\tag{20}
\]

For \(t\ge1\),

\[
P(t)\le117t^3.
\]

With \(x=(d-1)/2\), equations (19)--(20) become

\[
\prod_{j=2}^{h}(d-1+2j)^3
< A_{h,d}
<117^{h-1}
\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(d-1+2j)^3.
\tag{21}
\]

Hence, uniformly for \(h\ge2,d\ge2\),

\[
\boxed{
\log A_{h,d}\asymp h\log(h+d).
}
\tag{22}
\]

This is the first height number that matters for M1.

For primes \(p\le2X\), relation (9) implies

\[
2h+d\le2X-1.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal D_X
:=\{(h,d):h\ge2,\ d\ge2\text{ even},\ 2h+d\le2X-1\}.
\tag{23}
\]

Then the ordinary aggregate carrier height is

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm raw}(X)
:=\sum_{(h,d)\in\mathcal D_X}\log A_{h,d}.
\]

Using (22),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_{\rm raw}(X)=\Theta(X^3\log X).
}
\tag{24}
\]

The upper bound follows from

\[
\sum_{h<X} h\,#\{d:2h+d\le2X\}
\ll\sum_{h<X}h(X-h)
\asymp X^3.
\]

For the lower bound, restrict for example to
\(X/8\le h\le X/4\) and even \(X/2\le d\le3X/4\). This is a \(\Theta(X^2)\)-sized subset of \(\mathcal D_X\), and each term in it has
\(\log A_{h,d}\gg X\log X\).

Thus the natural one-sided characteristic-zero carrier exists, but its **ordinary total height is provably three powers of \(X\), not one**.

## 5. Exact p-adic multiplicity: reflection solves this part completely

For fixed \(p,h,d\), equations (7) give

\[
s=\frac{p-1-d}{2},
\qquad
r=\frac{p-1-d}{2}-h.
\tag{25}
\]

Hence there is **at most one** canonical orbit pair giving a fixed triple \((p,h,d)\).

Let

\[
m_p(h,d)\in\{0,1\}
\]

be its incidence indicator. Then (14) gives the exact p-adic charge

\[
\boxed{
m_p(h,d)\le v_p(A_{h,d}).
}
\tag{26}
\]

Moreover

\[
\sum_{(h,d)}m_p(h,d)=\binom{k_p}{2}.
\tag{27}
\]

So no higher-order resultant intersection theory is needed: a single unit of p-adic valuation per parameter pair is enough, because reflection makes the parameterization injective at each prime.

Define the dyadic large-prime radical

\[
\operatorname{rad}_{(X,2X]}(m)
:=\prod_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid m}}p.
\]

Then from (26)--(27),

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_2(X)\log X
&\le
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\sum_{h,d}m_p(h,d)\log p\\
&\le
\sum_{(h,d)\in\mathcal D_X}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(X,2X]}(A_{h,d}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]

This is the exact aggregate inequality.

### One-new-lemma implication theorem

If

\[
\sum_{(h,d)\in\mathcal D_X}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(X,2X]}(A_{h,d})
=O(X),
\tag{29}
\]

then (28) gives

\[
P_2(X)=O(X/\log X),
\]

and (5) gives M1:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X} Z(p)=O(X/\log X).
}
\tag{30}
\]

Thus `(AH-M1)` is an exact one-new-aggregate-lemma route to M1.

If instead the right-hand side of (29) is only
\(O(X(\log X)^A)\), then the direct conclusion is only

\[
P_2(X)=O\bigl(X(\log X)^{A-1}\bigr),
\]

so a positive polylogarithmic loss does **not** prove the stated M1.

The ordinary height (24) supplies only the useless bound \(P_2(X)=O(X^3)\). Therefore the missing statement is specifically a **large-prime radical compression theorem**, not an ordinary Archimedean height estimate.

## 6. What the raw two-gap resultants do, exactly

The same orbit pair also gives four zeros

\[
r,\ s,\ p-1-s,\ p-1-r.
\]

Using the anchor \(r\), put

\[
h=s-r,
\qquad
k=(p-1-s)-r=h+d.
\tag{31}
\]

Then

\[
p\mid N_h(r),\qquad p\mid N_k(r).
\tag{32}
\]

Thus, **if** the primitive residual polynomials are coprime over \(\mathbf Q\),

\[
\widehat R_{h,k}:=\operatorname{Res}_x(\widehat N_h,\widehat N_k)\ne0
\]

and

\[
p\mid\widehat R_{h,k}.
\tag{33}
\]

The nonmate condition again removes the forced linear factors. At \(r\),

\[
2r+h+1=r+s+1\not\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

and

\[
2r+k+1=p+r-s\equiv-h\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

So the resultant divisibility, when available, is genuinely in the residual factors.

But the current repository does **not** bank universal all-gap coprimality as an unconditional theorem. Commit `4c02845d7cb76986d13e83c2c9dff986dbbbd9ea` records:

- exact nonzero resultants for all ten pairs \(h_1,h_2\in\{2,\dots,6\}\);
- the odd/even irreducibility pattern verified through \(h=12\);
- the extrapolation to all \(h_1\ne h_2\) explicitly **conditional on that irreducibility pattern continuing**.

More importantly for the present route, the same repository note gives

\[
\deg N_h\le3(h-1),
\qquad
\log H(N_h)=O(h\log h),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\log|\widehat R_{h,k}|=O(hk\log(h+k)).
}
\tag{34}
\]

Summing this generic bound over the reflection-compatible triangle
\(h<k\), \(h\equiv k\pmod2\), \(h+k\ll X\), gives

\[
O(X^4\log X).
\tag{35}
\]

So the raw resultant route has two deficiencies:

1. universal nonvanishing is not presently banked unconditionally;
2. even granting it, the available aggregate height is much too large.

The half-integer border carrier (13) removes deficiency 1 and improves the raw height from quartic to cubic, but it still does not approach M1.

## 7. The marked/bordered determinant has the wrong signature at exactly the reflection point

The repository's theorem `thm:nv-range` (commit `2982edc127c901217e4afffdd3432f91da7510b8`) proves that

\[
\Delta_{h,k}(x)\not\equiv0\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[x]
\]

for \(1\le h<k<p\), \(p\ge7\).

That is a **non-identical-vanishing theorem**, not a zero-free theorem.

The later mirror-root audit (`e962da51045c2457299ff8bb9d427ea8f538d5ef`, independently confirmed in `150dc44ecc8bf5e69ec1bee34f8e6ddfb5289149`) finds the exact structural root

\[
\Psi_{h,k}\!\left(\frac{p-1-h-k}{2}\right)=0
\qquad(h\equiv k\pmod2).
\tag{36}
\]

For our orbit pair, \(k=h+d\) and \(d\) is even, so \(h\equiv k\pmod2\), while (9) gives

\[
r=\frac{p-1-h-k}{2}.
\tag{37}
\]

Therefore the marked determinant vanishes at **exactly our anchor** for a purely reflection-geometric reason, before one asks whether \(b_r\) or \(b_s\) vanishes.

This is the precise signature mismatch:

\[
\boxed{
\text{reflection-orbit pair geometry lands on the forced mirror root of the marked determinant.}
}
\tag{38}
\]

Consequently:

- `Delta != 0 as a polynomial` does not exclude the pair;
- the root at (37) does not certify the Apéry zero pair either;
- after the forced mirror factor/root is removed, the banked theorem supplies no residual p-divisibility attached to a two-orbit zero pair.

This is why the marked-determinant nonvanishing cannot replace `(AH-M1)`.

## 8. The associated Racah resultant is explicitly a p-unit on genuine nonmate pairs

The strongest direct audit is the exact Q2328 scanner on the temporary branch/PR `chatgpt-q2328-scan` (closed draft PR #43, head `60b00584427f4c164a6915471d7a6f60df1791d6`). It constructs, for an actual lower zero pair \(r<s\),

- the monic Racah row \(P_r\);
- the associated block \(C_h=S_{h-1}^{(r)}\), \(h=s-r\);
- the associated resultant
  \(R=\operatorname{Res}(P_r,C_h)\);
- the marked kernel \(K(r,s)\).

For every scanned canonical nonmate zero pair it asserts and verifies

```text
b_r = b_s = 0 mod p,
P_r(s(s+1)) != 0 mod p,
gcd(P_r,C_h) = 1 mod p,
Res(P_r,C_h) != 0 mod p.
```

The workflow run succeeded and found, for primes \(p\le500\),

```text

raw nonmate unordered zero pairs: 40
```

with zero sets including

```text
p=181: {19,47,133,161}
p=193: {30,64,128,162}
p=271: {17,41,229,253}
p=293: {47,83,209,245}
p=379: {85,152,171,207,226,293}
p=499: {67,170,203,295,328,431}.
```

For the concrete pair

\[

the scanner gives

The Q2328 run also has `orbit_count = 4` for every canonical pair, exactly matching the reflection-orbit counting in Section 1.

## 9. What the zero bank contributes

The zero bank is useful as a falsification and calibration device, not as an asymptotic proof of M1.

The repository has banked large finite zero data (for example commit `2ed6e6db22afe0dc5ba4d9aa3c95c0dee0049f06` records 149k zero pairs in the scan used there). It confirms that genuine nonmate orbit pairs occur, and Q2328 shows directly that the Racah/border quantities remain p-units on them.

But a finite zero bank gives no bound on

\[
\sum_{(h,d)\in\mathcal D_X}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(X,2X]}A_{h,d}
\]

as \(X\to\infty\). In particular it supplies neither a product formula nor a large-prime radical estimate for the border values (13).

## 10. Why a short-gap truncation does not secretly repair the height loss

The border carrier does recover, in aggregate form, essentially the already-known \(2/3\)-type barrier.

Order the lower orbit representatives

\[
r_1<\cdots<r_{k_p}.
\]

For a cutoff \(H\), let \(A_p(H)\) count adjacent pairs with
\(r_{i+1}-r_i\le H\). Since the total span is \(<p/2\), the number of adjacent gaps larger than \(H\) is at most \(p/(2H)\). Hence

\[
k_p\le1+A_p(H)+\frac{p}{2H}.
\tag{39}
\]

For a short adjacent pair with \(h\le H\), the carrier (13) applies. From (22), summing the ordinary log heights over all possible \(d\ll X\) gives

\[
\sum_{h\le H}\sum_{d\ll X}\log A_{h,d}
\ll XH^2\log X.
\tag{40}
\]

Therefore the number of such dyadic prime incidences obtained from ordinary height is at best

\[
O(XH^2).
\tag{41}
\]

Summing the long-gap term in (39) over \(X<p\le2X\) gives

\[
O\!\left(\frac{X^2}{H\log X}\right).
\tag{42}
\]

Thus this carrier method yields only

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}k_p
\ll
\frac{X}{\log X}
+XH^2
+\frac{X^2}{H\log X}.
\tag{43}
\]

Balancing the last two terms gives

\[
H\asymp (X/\log X)^{1/3}
\]

and scale

\[
\asymp \frac{X^{5/3}}{(\log X)^{2/3}},
\tag{44}
\]

far above M1. This is the same structural cubic/\(2/3\)-barrier already visible in the repository's gap-degree arguments, now expressed in the reflection-orbit average problem.

So there is no hidden M1 proof obtained merely by choosing nearest orbit pairs.

## 11. Exact first unmatched lemma

Everything before the following line is now elementary/banked:

1. reflection orbit decomposition (2);
2. reduction to canonical orbit pairs (5);
3. gap divisibility (10);
4. reflection identity (11);
5. explicit nonzero characteristic-zero carrier (13)--(18);
6. injective p-adic charging (25)--(28).

The first unmatched arithmetic term is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R(X):=
\sum_{\substack{h\ge2,\ d\ge2\ {m even}\\2h+d\le2X-1}}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(X,2X]}
\left(
2^{3(h-1)}N_h\!\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)
\right).
}
\tag{45}
\]

The exact sufficient theorem is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R(X)=O(X).}
\tag{46}
\]

No current gap-resultant, marked-Racah, bordered-nonvanishing, or zero-bank theorem proves (46).

The ordinary Archimedean counterpart is not merely unproved at the requested scale; for the natural carriers it is false, because (24) gives \(\Theta(X^3\log X)\). Hence any successful version of (46) must use **arithmetic sparsity of large prime divisors** of these positive half-integer gap values, or a genuinely new selective carrier whose large-prime radical is much smaller than its Archimedean size.

That is the exact obstruction.
