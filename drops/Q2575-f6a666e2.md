# P3.2 — the modular parametrization gives an exact Kummer–Frobenius lattice, but not the required last-coordinate saturation

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
B(T)=\sum_{n\ge 0} b_nT^n,
\qquad
\mathscr R(T)=\sum_{n\ge1}R_nT^n,
\qquad
R_n=\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{b_{n-k}}k.
\]

The first exact identity is

\[
\boxed{\mathscr R(T)=-B(T)\log(1-T).}
\tag{E1}
\]

The Apéry modular parametrization and Fricke involution can be written completely explicitly, and they do give a canonical integral **Kummer extension** for the logarithm in (E1). They do **not**, however, force the coefficientwise denominator lower bound

\[
\log\operatorname{den}(R_n)=\log L_n-o(n).
\]

The reason can be isolated exactly.

1. The logarithm in (E1) is not the canonical Picard–Fuchs logarithm
   \(B(T)\log T+h(T)\), and it is not Beukers’s triple Eichler integral producing the Apéry companion. It is the Kummer logarithm attached to the divisor \(1-T=0\), multiplied by the period \(B(T)\).

2. The modular parametrization gives an integral period and the Fricke laws

   \[
   t\!\left(-\frac1{6\tau}\right)=t(\tau),
   \qquad
   E\!\left(-\frac1{6\tau}\right)=-6\tau^2E(\tau),
   \qquad
   E(\tau)=B(t(\tau)).
   \]

   Hence, on a fixed logarithm branch,

   \[
   \mathscr R\!\left(t\!\left(-\frac1{6\tau}\right)\right)
   =-6\tau^2\mathscr R(t(\tau)).
   \]

   Globally this equality is understood modulo the quasiperiod lattice
   \(2\pi i\,E\mathbb Z\). This is a genuine modular Kummer/quasiperiod law, but it is tautological once \(t\) is Fricke-invariant.

3. For every prime \(p\), put

   \[
   \ell(T)=-\log(1-T),
   \qquad
   \lambda_p(T)=\ell(T)-\frac1p\ell(T^p)
   =\sum_{\substack{m\ge1\\p\nmid m}}\frac{T^m}{m}
   \in\mathbb Z_p[[T]].
   \]

   Then

   \[
   p\ell(T)-\ell(T^p)
   =\log\frac{1-T^p}{(1-T)^p}
   =p\lambda_p(T).
   \tag{E2}
   \]

   This is the exact integral Frobenius defect of the Kummer extension. Moreover,

   \[
   \boxed{
   \ell(T)=\sum_{a\ge0}p^{-a}\lambda_p(T^{p^a}).
   }
   \tag{E3}
   \]

4. If \(e=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\), then (E3) gives the exact pointwise denominator ghost

   \[
   \boxed{
   p^eR_n=
   \sum_{a=0}^{e}p^{e-a}
   [T^n]\,B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^a}).
   }
   \tag{E4}
   \]

   The top graded coefficient is

   \[
   \mathcal C_{p,e}(n)
   :=[T^n]B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^e})
   =\sum_{\substack{1\le m\le n/p^e\\p\nmid m}}
   \frac{b_{n-p^em}}m
   \in\mathbb Z_{(p)}.
   \tag{E5}
   \]

   The full exponent \(p^e\) survives in the reduced denominator of \(R_n\) exactly when

   \[
   \mathcal C_{p,e}(n)\not\equiv0\pmod p.
   \tag{E6}
   \]

5. Writing

   \[
   n=qp^e+r,
   \qquad 1\le q<p,
   \qquad 0\le r<p^e,
   \]

   Gessel’s Apéry–Lucas congruence gives the exact first Cartier digit

   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal C_{p,e}(n)\equiv b_rR_q\pmod p.
   }
   \tag{E7}
   \]

   In particular, for \(p>n/2\), one has \(e=q=1\), \(r=n-p\), and

   \[
   \boxed{pR_n\equiv b_{n-p}\pmod p.}
   \tag{E8}
   \]

   Thus \(p\) is retained in \(\operatorname{den}(R_n)\) iff
   \(b_{n-p}\not\equiv0\pmod p\), and is cancelled iff
   \(b_{n-p}\equiv0\pmod p\). This is the orientation forced by the congruence.

6. The ordinary integral lattice statement is completely understood. On the first \(n\) coefficients, multiplication by \(B(T)\) is represented by an integral lower-unitriangular Toeplitz matrix

   \[
   U_n(B)=(b_{i-j})_{1\le j\le i\le n}\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z).
   \]

   Consequently

   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{lcm}_{1\le j\le n}\operatorname{den}(R_j)=L_n.
   }
   \tag{E9}
   \]

   So the modular/integral period loses **no denominator globally on the truncated jet lattice**. The unresolved issue is that the theorem asks about the single final projection \([T^n]\), and a unimodular transformation need not preserve the order of one chosen coordinate.

The precise missing statement is therefore not another generic denominator theorem. It is a **defining-characteristic last-jet saturation theorem** for the tensor product of the Apéry period crystal with the explicit Kummer crystal (E2): the final coefficient functional must be transverse to the Frobenius–Verschiebung extension line represented by \(B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^e})\), including its higher Bockstein carries when (E6) vanishes.

The Fricke law does not imply that transversality. A sharp no-go family proves this. For every \(M\ge2\),

\[
\mathscr R_M(T):=-B(T)\log(1-T^M)
\]

has the same Fricke weight, the same integral logarithmic derivative in the \(q\)-coordinate, and the same kind of integral Kummer Frobenius defect, but

\[
[T^n]\mathscr R_M(T)
=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor n/M\rfloor}\frac{b_{n-Mj}}j
\]

has denominator dividing \(L_{\lfloor n/M\rfloor}\). Hence no theorem using only “modular parametrization + Fricke symmetry + integral \(q\)-lattice + Kummer extension” can yield the desired \(L_n\)-scale coefficientwise lower bound. The special divisor \(1-T\) and the last-jet defining-characteristic matrix coefficient (E5) must enter.

The rest of this answer proves each assertion and gives the exact integral-lattice route that a successful modular proof would have to complete.

# 1. Exact denominator formulation

Because every summand in \(R_n\) has denominator dividing \(L_n\),

\[
L_nR_n\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\operatorname{den}(R_n)\mid L_n.
\]

If \(R_n=a_n/d_n\) in lowest terms and \(d_n\mid L_n\), then

\[
\gcd(L_n,L_nR_n)
=\gcd\!\left(L_n,a_n\frac{L_n}{d_n}\right)
=\frac{L_n}{d_n}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{den}(R_n)\,\gcd(L_n,L_nR_n)=L_n.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\log\operatorname{den}(R_n)=\log L_n-o(n)
\iff
\log\gcd(L_n,L_nR_n)=o(n).
\tag{1.2}
\]

At a fixed prime \(p\), put

\[
e=e_p(n):=v_p(L_n)=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor.
\]

Since \(p^eR_n\in\mathbb Z_p\), the amount by which the available \(p^e\)-denominator is cancelled is

\[
\boxed{
c_p(n)=\min\{e,v_p(p^eR_n)\}.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Thus

\[
v_p(\operatorname{den}(R_n))=e-c_p(n)
\tag{1.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\log\gcd(L_n,L_nR_n)
=\sum_{p\le n}c_p(n)\log p.
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

Equation (1.5) is the exact pointwise exponent ledger. No asymptotic approximation has entered.

# 2. The exact Apéry modular parametrization

Use \(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\) and Dedekind’s eta function. Beukers’s level-six Hauptmodul and weight-two period are

\[
\boxed{
t(\tau)=
\left(
\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau)}
     {\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau)}
\right)^{12}}
\tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
E(\tau)=
\frac{\eta(2\tau)^7\eta(3\tau)^7}
     {\eta(\tau)^5\eta(6\tau)^5}.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Their first expansions are

\[
t=q-12q^2+66q^3-220q^4+495q^5+\cdots,
\tag{2.3}
\]

\[
E=1+5q+13q^2+23q^3+\cdots.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The Apéry period identity is

\[
\boxed{
E(\tau)=B(t(\tau))
=1+5t+73t^2+1445t^3+\cdots.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

In Eisenstein-series form,

\[
\boxed{
24E(\tau)
=-5\bigl(E_2(\tau)-6E_2(6\tau)\bigr)
 +2E_2(2\tau)-3E_2(3\tau).}
\tag{2.6}
\]

The eta transformation formula gives, for the Fricke involution

\[
W_6\tau=-\frac1{6\tau},
\]

\[
\eta\!\left(-\frac1{d\tau}\right)
=\sqrt{-id\tau}\,\eta(d\tau)
\]

with the usual coherent square-root choice. Substitution in (2.1)–(2.2) yields

\[
\boxed{t(W_6\tau)=t(\tau),}
\tag{2.7}
\]

\[
\boxed{E(W_6\tau)=-6\tau^2E(\tau).}
\tag{2.8}
\]

The scalar in (2.8) can be checked directly:

\[
\frac{(3\cdot2)^{7/2}}{6^{5/2}}(-i\tau)^{7-5}
=6(-i\tau)^2=-6\tau^2.
\]

These are precisely the Fricke identities used by Beukers.

## 2.1 The Picard–Fuchs period

The Apéry Picard–Fuchs operator is

\[
\mathcal L
=\theta^3
-T(34\theta^3+51\theta^2+27\theta+5)
+T^2(\theta+1)^3,
\qquad
\theta=T\frac d{dT}.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Its holomorphic solution at \(T=0\) is \(B(T)\). A canonical logarithmic solution has the Frobenius form

\[
\varpi_1(T)
=\frac1{2\pi i}\bigl(B(T)\log T+h_1(T)\bigr),
\qquad h_1(0)=0,
\tag{2.10}
\]

and the mirror coordinate is \(\tau=\varpi_1/B\), with inverse \(T=t(\tau)\).

This logarithm is \(\log T\), attached to maximally unipotent monodromy at \(T=0\). It is not the logarithm \(\log(1-T)\) occurring in (E1). In fact \(T=1\) is not a singular point of (2.9): the finite nonzero Picard–Fuchs singularities are the roots of

\[
1-34T+T^2=0,
\]

namely \((\sqrt2\pm1)^4\), not \(1\).

Thus \(-B(T)\log(1-T)\) is a new Kummer extension coordinate over an ordinary point of the Picard–Fuchs base; it is not one of the three homogeneous K3 periods.

# 3. The exact logarithmic-convolution modular identity

Set

\[
\ell(T):=-\log(1-T)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{T^k}{k}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then coefficient extraction gives

\[
B(T)\ell(T)
=\sum_{n\ge1}
\left(\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{b_{n-k}}k\right)T^n,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\mathscr R(T)=B(T)\ell(T).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The first coefficients are

\[
R_1=1,
\qquad
R_2=5+\frac12=\frac{11}{2},
\qquad
R_3=73+\frac52+\frac13=\frac{455}{6},
\tag{3.3}
\]

hence

\[
\mathscr R(T)
=T+\frac{11}{2}T^2+\frac{455}{6}T^3+\cdots.
\tag{3.4}
\]

After the modular substitution,

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr R(t(\tau))=E(\tau)\ell(t(\tau)).}
\tag{3.5}
\]

## 3.1 Integral logarithmic derivative

Let

\[
\Theta=q\frac d{dq}=\frac1{2\pi i}\frac d{d\tau}.
\]

From the eta product,

\[
\boxed{
\Theta\log t
=\frac12\bigl(
E_2(\tau)+6E_2(6\tau)
-2E_2(2\tau)-3E_2(3\tau)
\bigr).}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
G(\tau):=\Theta\ell(t(\tau))
=\frac{t}{1-t}\,\Theta\log t.}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Because \(t\in q\mathbb Z[[q]]\), both \(\Theta t\) and \((1-t)^{-1}\) have integral \(q\)-coefficients. Consequently

\[
\boxed{G(q)\in q\mathbb Z[[q]].}
\tag{3.8}
\]

The first terms are

\[
G(q)=q-23q^2+163q^3+\cdots,
\tag{3.9}
\]

and termwise integration gives

\[
\ell(t(q))
=q-\frac{23}{2}q^2+\frac{163}{3}q^3+\cdots.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Multiplying by (2.4),

\[
\mathscr R(t(q))
=q-\frac{13}{2}q^2+\frac{59}{6}q^3+\cdots,
\tag{3.11}
\]

which agrees with substituting (2.3) in (3.4).

Equation (3.7) says that \(\ell(t)\) is an Eichler integral of a meromorphic weight-two modular form. The poles of \(G\) lie over the divisor \(t=1\).

## 3.2 Fricke law

Since \(t(W_6\tau)=t(\tau)\), differentiation gives

\[
G(W_6\tau)=6\tau^2G(\tau).
\tag{3.12}
\]

On a simply connected region avoiding \(t=1\), choose one branch of \(\ell\). Then

\[
\ell(t(W_6\tau))=\ell(t(\tau))
\]

and (2.8) gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr R(t(W_6\tau))
=-6\tau^2\mathscr R(t(\tau)).}
\tag{3.13}
\]

After analytic continuation around \(t=1\),

\[
\ell(t)\longmapsto\ell(t)+2\pi i m,
\qquad m\in\mathbb Z,
\]

so globally (3.13) holds modulo

\[
2\pi i\,E(W_6\tau)\mathbb Z.
\tag{3.14}
\]

This is the exact quasiperiod extension attached to (E1).

Crucially, (3.13) introduces no new relation among the Taylor coefficients \(R_n\). The quotient

\[
\frac{\mathscr R(t(\tau))}{E(\tau)}=\ell(t(\tau))
\]

is already a function of the Fricke-invariant coordinate \(t\). The Fricke identity is therefore automatic from (2.7)–(2.8).

# 4. This is not Beukers’s Apéry Eichler integral

The distinction is visible in exact formulas.

Beukers defines a weight-four cusp form \(\Phi_4\) by

\[
40\Phi_4(\tau)
=E_4(\tau)-36E_4(6\tau)
-7\bigl(4E_4(2\tau)-9E_4(3\tau)\bigr),
\tag{4.1}
\]

with

\[
\Phi_4(W_6\tau)=-36\tau^4\Phi_4(\tau).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let \(f\) be its triple Eichler integral, normalized by

\[
\left(\frac d{d\tau}\right)^3f(\tau)
=(2\pi i)^3\Phi_4(\tau),
\qquad
f(i\infty)=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

In Beukers’s normalization, \(L(\Phi_4,3)=\zeta(3)\), and the Fricke law is

\[
\boxed{
6\tau^2\bigl(f(W_6\tau)-\zeta(3)\bigr)
=-\bigl(f(\tau)-\zeta(3)\bigr).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Multiplication by (2.8) yields the Fricke-invariant combination

\[
E(W_6\tau)\bigl(f(W_6\tau)-\zeta(3)\bigr)
=E(\tau)\bigl(f(\tau)-\zeta(3)\bigr).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Its \(t\)-expansion begins

\[
E(t)f(t)=6t+\frac{351}{4}t^2+\cdots,
\tag{4.6}
\]

and Beukers proves that the denominator of its \(t^n\)-coefficient divides \(L_n^3\).

Compare (4.6) with

\[
\mathscr R(t)=t+\frac{11}{2}t^2+\frac{455}{6}t^3+\cdots.
\tag{4.7}
\]

They are different extensions:

| object | logarithm/integral | differential order | denominator scale supplied by construction |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| K3 canonical period | \(B(T)\) | homogeneous order 3 | integral |
| canonical mirror solution | \(B(T)\log T+h_1(T)\) | homogeneous order 3 | mirror-map lattice |
| Beukers companion extension | triple Eichler integral of \(\Phi_4\) | order 3 integral | upper bound \(L_n^3\) |
| present convolution | \(B(T)\ell(T)\), \(\ell=-\log(1-T)\) | rank-one Kummer extension | upper bound \(L_n\) |

The Fricke mechanism in Beukers’s irrationality proof removes an analytic branch obstruction and enlarges a radius of convergence. It never proves that an allowed denominator actually occurs. Applying that argument to (4.7) therefore cannot reverse the denominator divisibility.

# 5. The exact integral coefficient lattice

There is a simple but decisive lattice theorem.

For \(n\ge1\), put

\[
\mathbf h_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\[2pt]1/2\\ \vdots\\1/n
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf R_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
R_1\\R_2\\ \vdots\\R_n
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Let \(U_n(B)\) be the lower-triangular Toeplitz convolution matrix

\[
U_n(B)=
\begin{pmatrix}
 b_0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
 b_1&b_0&0&\cdots&0\\
 b_2&b_1&b_0&\cdots&0\\
 \vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
 b_{n-1}&b_{n-2}&b_{n-3}&\cdots&b_0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since \(b_0=1\),

\[
U_n(B)\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z),
\qquad
\det U_n(B)=1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The convolution identity is exactly

\[
\boxed{\mathbf R_n=U_n(B)\mathbf h_n.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The inverse matrix is integral because it is the truncated convolution matrix for \(B(T)^{-1}\in\mathbb Z[[T]]\).

For a vector \(x\in\mathbb Q^n\), let \(\operatorname{ord}(x+\mathbb Z^n)\) denote its additive order in \((\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)^n\), equivalently the least positive integer \(D\) with \(Dx\in\mathbb Z^n\). Then

\[
\operatorname{ord}(\mathbf h_n+\mathbb Z^n)=L_n.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Because \(U_n(B)\) is a lattice automorphism,

\[
\operatorname{ord}(\mathbf R_n+\mathbb Z^n)=L_n.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{lcm}igl(\operatorname{den}(R_1),\ldots,
\operatorname{den}(R_n)\bigr)=L_n.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is the strongest denominator lower bound supplied by the ordinary integral jet lattice: **the complete truncated coefficient vector has exactly the full lcm order.**

But the target is the order of the final projection

\[
\pi_n:\; (x_1,\ldots,x_n)\longmapsto x_n.
\]

An integral lattice automorphism preserves the order of the whole vector, not the order of one coordinate. It can move the primitive \(p\)-primary component into earlier coordinates. Thus (5.6) does not imply a lower bound for \(\operatorname{den}(R_n)\).

This is already the missing-lattice phenomenon in finite-dimensional exact form.

# 6. The canonical integral Kummer Frobenius extension

The p-adic extension is also explicit; its existence is not the missing theorem.

Fix a prime \(p\), and work over

\[
\mathcal O_p=\mathbb Z_p[[T]][(1-T)^{-1}].
\]

Let

\[
\ell(T)=-\log(1-T).
\]

Define a rank-two connection \(\mathscr K_p\) with basis \(e_0,e_1\) by

\[
\nabla e_0=0,
\qquad
\nabla e_1=e_0\,d\ell(T).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The section

\[
s=e_1-\ell(T)e_0
\tag{6.2}
\]

is horizontal.

Use the Frobenius lift

\[
\sigma(T)=T^p.
\]

Define

\[
\lambda_p(T)
:=\ell(T)-\frac1p\ell(T^p)
=\sum_{\substack{m\ge1\\p\nmid m}}\frac{T^m}{m}
\in\mathbb Z_p[[T]].
\tag{6.3}
\]

The equality follows by separating exponents divisible by \(p\). Equivalently,

\[
p\lambda_p(T)
=p\ell(T)-\ell(T^p)
=\log\frac{1-T^p}{(1-T)^p}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{1-T^p}{(1-T)^p}\equiv1\pmod p,
\]

its logarithm lies in \(p\mathbb Z_p[[T]]\), which is the integral reason for (6.3).

A horizontal Frobenius map \(\Phi:\sigma^*\mathscr K_p\to\mathscr K_p\) is

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(e_0^\sigma)=e_0,
\qquad
\Phi(e_1^\sigma)=p e_1-p\lambda_p(T)e_0.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\nabla\Phi(e_1^\sigma)
=p e_0d\ell(T)-p e_0d\lambda_p(T)
=e_0d\ell(T^p),
\]

and the horizontal section satisfies

\[
\Phi\bigl(e_1^\sigma-\ell(T^p)e_0^\sigma\bigr)
=p\bigl(e_1-\ell(T)e_0\bigr).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus the off-diagonal Frobenius coordinate is exactly the p-depleted logarithm \(-p\lambda_p(T)\).

## 6.1 Frobenius–Verschiebung decomposition

Every positive integer has a unique representation \(m=p^au\) with \(p\nmid u\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell(T)
&=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{T^m}{m}\\
&=\sum_{a\ge0}\frac1{p^a}
  \sum_{\substack{u\ge1\\p\nmid u}}\frac{T^{p^au}}u\\
&=\sum_{a\ge0}p^{-a}\lambda_p(T^{p^a}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.7}
\]

If \(V\) denotes the formal Verschiebung

\[
(Vf)(T)=f(T^p),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\ell=\sum_{a\ge0}p^{-a}V^a\lambda_p.}
\tag{6.8}
\]

This is the exact p-typical decomposition needed for the lcm exponents.

Pulling \(\mathscr K_p\) back along the modular function \(t(\tau)\) gives a Fricke-equivariant Kummer extension on the level-six modular curve. Because \(t(q)\in q\mathbb Z[[q]]\), each \(\lambda_p(t(q))\) is p-integral at the \(q\)-cusp. Thus the integral Kummer lattice itself is completely explicit.

# 7. Exact extraction of every p-adic denominator exponent

Let

\[
e=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor.
\]

Only the terms \(a\le e\) in (6.7) can contribute to \([T^n]\). Multiplying by \(B(T)\) gives

\[
R_n
=\sum_{a=0}^{e}p^{-a}
[T^n]B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^a}).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
C_{p,a}(n)
:=[T^n]B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^a})
=\sum_{\substack{1\le m\le n/p^a\\p\nmid m}}
\frac{b_{n-p^am}}m
\in\mathbb Z_{(p)}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{p,e}(n)
:=\sum_{a=0}^{e}p^{e-a}C_{p,a}(n)
=p^eR_n.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Combining with (1.3),

\[
\boxed{
c_p(n)=\min\{e,v_p(\Gamma_{p,e}(n))\}.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is an exact formula for the cancelled lcm exponent, including all carries. The first digit is particularly simple:

\[
\Gamma_{p,e}(n)\equiv C_{p,e}(n)\pmod p.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\operatorname{den}(R_n))=e
 \iff C_{p,e}(n)\not\equiv0\pmod p.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

If \(C_{p,e}(n)=0\pmod p\), the next denominator digit is not determined by Fricke symmetry; it is the Bockstein/carry

\[
\frac{C_{p,e}(n)}p+C_{p,e-1}(n)\pmod p,
\tag{7.7}
\]

and deeper cancellation involves the successive combinations visible in (7.3).

## 7.1 Finite-jet matrix coefficient

Let

\[
J_n= T\mathbb Z_p[T]/(T^{n+1})
\]

with coefficient functional \(\pi_n=[T^n]\). The top p-denominator graded vector of

\[
(1,1/2,\ldots,1/n)
\]

is

\[
\boxed{
 v_{p,e,n}
 =\sum_{\substack{1\le m\le n/p^e\\p\nmid m}}
 \bar m^{-1}T^{p^em}
 =V^e\bar\lambda_p
 \quad\text{in }J_n\otimes\mathbb F_p.}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Multiplication by \(B(T)\) is the reduction of \(U_n(B)\), so

\[
\boxed{
 C_{p,e}(n)\bmod p
 =\pi_n\bigl(B(T)V^e\bar\lambda_p(T)\bigr).}
\tag{7.9}
\]

The operator \(M_B:f\mapsto Bf\) is invertible on \(J_n\otimes\mathbb F_p\). Therefore

\[
B(T)V^e\bar\lambda_p\ne0.
\]

But invertibility does not imply that its specific last coordinate (7.9) is nonzero. The vector may lie in the hyperplane \(\ker\pi_n\). This is the exact last-jet transversality obstruction.

# 8. The first Cartier digit factors by Apéry–Lucas

Gessel proved the Lucas congruence

\[
\boxed{
 b_{d_0+d_1p+\cdots+d_sp^s}
 \equiv\prod_{i=0}^s b_{d_i}\pmod p.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
B(T)\equiv B_{p-1}(T)B(T^p)\pmod p
\tag{8.2}
\]

in the usual formal sense.

Let

\[
n=qp^e+r,
\qquad
1\le q<p,
\qquad
0\le r<p^e,
\tag{8.3}
\]

where \(e=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\). Since \(m\le q<p\),

\[
n-p^em=(q-m)p^e+r.
\]

Applying (8.1),

\[
b_{n-p^em}
\equiv b_{q-m}b_r\pmod p.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Substituting in (7.2) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{p,e}(n)
&\equiv b_r\sum_{m=1}^{q}\frac{b_{q-m}}m\pmod p\\
&=b_rR_q\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{C_{p,e}(n)\equiv b_rR_q\pmod p.}
\tag{8.5}
\]

This is the exact Cartier block law for the logarithmic extension.

Consequently, the full available exponent \(p^e\) is retained precisely when

\[
\boxed{b_rR_q\not\equiv0\pmod p.}
\tag{8.6}
\]

Since \(q<p\), every denominator in \(R_q\) is a p-unit, so (8.6) is unambiguous.

## 8.1 Top interval

For \(p>n/2\),

\[
e=1,
\qquad q=1,
\qquad r=n-p,
\qquad R_1=1.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{pR_n\equiv b_{n-p}\pmod p.}
\tag{8.7}
\]

There is only one term of \(R_n\) whose denominator is divisible by \(p\), namely \(b_{n-p}/p\). Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ccl}
b_{n-p}\not\equiv0\pmod p
&\Longleftrightarrow& p\mid\operatorname{den}(R_n),\\[2mm]
b_{n-p}\equiv0\pmod p
&\Longleftrightarrow& p\nmid\operatorname{den}(R_n).
\end{array}}
\tag{8.8}
\]

This is the correct cancellation orientation.

## 8.2 General quotient classes

For every \(p>\sqrt n\), one still has \(e=1\), but now

\[
q=\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor
\]

may exceed one. Formula (8.5) becomes

\[
\boxed{
pR_n\equiv b_{n-qp}R_q\pmod p.}
\tag{8.9}
\]

Thus the defining-characteristic last-jet class splits into:

- a low-digit Apéry Hasse factor \(b_{n-qp}\);
- a low-quotient Kummer-extension factor \(R_q\).

This factorization is what a genuinely arithmetic modular/crystalline proof would have to exploit. The complex Fricke law sees neither factor separately.

# 9. Why the Fricke and q-integrality data cannot supply the lower bound

The obstruction is not philosophical; there is an exact counterfamily.

For an integer \(M\ge2\), define

\[
\ell_M(T)=-\log(1-T^M),
\qquad
\mathscr R_M(T)=B(T)\ell_M(T).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Because \(t(W_6\tau)=t(\tau)\), the same calculation gives

\[
\mathscr R_M(t(W_6\tau))
=-6\tau^2\mathscr R_M(t(\tau))
\tag{9.2}
\]

on a fixed branch, with quasiperiods in \(2\pi i E\mathbb Z\).

Its logarithmic derivative is

\[
\Theta\ell_M(t)
=\frac{Mt^M}{1-t^M}\,\Theta\log t
\in q\mathbb Z[[q]].
\tag{9.3}
\]

It also has the integral Kummer Frobenius defect

\[
p\ell_M(T)-\ell_M(T^p)
=p\lambda_p(T^M),
\qquad
\lambda_p(T^M)\in\mathbb Z_p[[T]].
\tag{9.4}
\]

So \(\mathscr R_M\) has all of the following properties possessed by \(\mathscr R\):

- the same Apéry modular period \(B(t)=E\);
- the same Fricke weight and eigenvalue;
- an integral logarithmic derivative in the \(q\)-coordinate;
- a rank-two integral Kummer connection;
- an integral normalized Frobenius off-diagonal;
- a p-typical Frobenius–Verschiebung decomposition.

Yet

\[
\boxed{
[T^n]\mathscr R_M(T)
=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor n/M\rfloor}\frac{b_{n-Mj}}j,}
\tag{9.5}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{den}([T^n]\mathscr R_M)\mid L_{\lfloor n/M\rfloor}.}
\tag{9.6}
\]

In particular, no prime \(p>n/M\) can occur in this denominator at all.

Therefore the formal package

```text
Fricke invariance of t
+ modular weight of E
+ integral q-expansion
+ Eichler/Kummer quasiperiod law
+ integral Frobenius defect
```

cannot force an \(L_n\)-scale lower bound for an individual \(T^n\)-coefficient. Any valid proof for \(M=1\) must use the divisor-specific vector \(V^e\lambda_p\) and its final-coordinate pairing (7.9), not merely the existence of the modular extension.

This also explains why generic G-function denominator theorems point in the wrong direction: they prove that the extension lies inside a prescribed denominator lattice. They do not prove that a chosen coordinate is primitive in that lattice.

# 10. The precise missing integral-lattice statement

The missing object can now be stated without using the phrase “denominator retention.”

## 10.1 The known lattices

There are three explicit ingredients.

### Apéry period jet lattice

For each \(n\), multiplication by \(B(T)\) gives

\[
M_{B,n}:J_n\longrightarrow J_n,
\qquad
M_{B,n}\in\mathrm{GL}(J_n),
\tag{10.1}
\]

integrally over every \(\mathbb Z_p\).

### Kummer Frobenius lattice

The connection and Frobenius (6.1), (6.5) give an integral extension

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbf 1
\longrightarrow\mathscr K_p
\longrightarrow\mathbf 1(-1)
\longrightarrow0
\tag{10.2}
\]

in the chosen normalization, whose normalized extension coordinate is \(\lambda_p(T)\).

### p-typical graded line

At the maximal exponent \(e=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\), the top denominator graded class is the explicit vector

\[
V^e\bar\lambda_p\in J_n\otimes\mathbb F_p.
\tag{10.3}
\]

The full first Cartier matrix coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{p,e,n}
:=\bar\pi_n\circ M_{B,n}igl(V^e\bar\lambda_p\bigr)
=C_{p,e}(n)mod p
=b_rR_qmod p.}
\tag{10.4}
\]

Everything in (10.4) is explicit and already proved.

## 10.2 What is absent

A modular proof of the coefficientwise lower bound needs a theorem asserting that the Frobenius extension line in (10.3), after multiplication by the Apéry period, is not absorbed to high order by the hyperplane

\[
\ker\pi_n\subset J_n.
\]

The first-order form is the nonorthogonality

\[
\bar\pi_nM_{B,n}V^e\bar\lambda_p\ne0.
\tag{10.5}
\]

When (10.5) fails, one needs its integral Bockstein refinement. Namely, the sequence

\[
C_{p,e}(n),
\quad
\frac{C_{p,e}(n)}p+C_{p,e-1}(n),
\quad\ldots
\tag{10.6}
\]

must be interpreted as successive extension classes in the filtered jet lattice, and their common order of vanishing is exactly

\[
c_p(n)=\min\{e,v_p(\Gamma_{p,e}(n))\}.
\tag{10.7}
\]

Thus the missing statement is:

> **Defining-characteristic last-jet saturation.** Construct the integral \(n\)-jet of the tensor product of the Apéry Gauss–Manin lattice with the Kummer extension (6.1), identify its Frobenius–Verschiebung graded extension line with \(M_{B,n}V^e\lambda_p\), and prove a uniform bound on the contact order of this line with \(\ker\pi_n\), including the Bockstein layers (10.6).

This is a concrete Smith/Fitting problem in the finite module \(J_n\), not a generic denominator assertion. One possible certificate would be an explicit primitive minor of the matrix consisting of

\[
M_{B,n}V^a\lambda_p,
\qquad 0\le a\le e,
\tag{10.8}
\]

and the final-coordinate functional. Another would be a Cartier-polarization identity forcing the final matrix coefficient (10.4) to be a unit away from a classified Hasse divisor and controlling the Bockstein multiplicity on that divisor.

The usual modular data supplies neither certificate:

- Fricke acts on the global modular curve and exchanges analytic branches/cusps;
- \(\pi_n\) is a growing-order jet functional at one cusp;
- the reduction prime is the defining characteristic \(p\), varying with \(n\);
- an auxiliary-\(\ell\) monodromy or complex period comparison does not determine (10.4) at \(\ell=p\).

The exact factorization (8.5) shows what such a theorem would have to control at first order: the product of the Apéry Hasse digit \(b_r\) and the Kummer extension digit \(R_q\).

## 10.3 Why this is stronger than the ordinary integral lattice

The unitriangular identity (5.3) proves that

\[
M_{B,n}V^e\bar\lambda_p\ne0.
\]

It does not prove

\[
\pi_nM_{B,n}V^e\bar\lambda_p\ne0.
\]

The distinction is exactly the difference between:

- primitivity of a vector in a lattice;
- primitivity of its image under one prescribed coordinate projection.

Beukers’s modular parametrization establishes the first. P3.2 requires quantitative control of the second.

# 11. A concrete modular/crystalline attack route

The modular route is not empty; it has a sharply defined remaining step.

## Step 1: use the geometric Apéry lattice, not only the scalar series

Take the integral de Rham/crystalline Gauss–Manin module \(\mathscr V_p\) of the Beukers–Peters K3 family. Its holomorphic period at the maximally unipotent cusp is \(B(T)\), and its first Hasse–Witt scalar reduces to the appropriate truncation of the Apéry series.

## Step 2: tensor with the explicit Kummer crystal

Form

\[
\mathscr W_p=\mathscr V_p\otimes\mathscr K_p,
\tag{11.1}
\]

where \(\mathscr K_p\) is (6.1), (6.5). On the holomorphic period line, the extension coordinate is exactly

\[
B(T)\ell(T)=\mathscr R(T).
\]

The normalized Frobenius off-diagonal is exactly

\[
B(T)\lambda_p(T).
\tag{11.2}
\]

No conjectural Eichler object is needed for this construction.

## Step 3: pass to finite jets and Verschiebung layers

On the \(n\)-jet at \(T=0\), apply the layers

\[
V^a\lambda_p=\lambda_p(T^{p^a}),
\qquad 0\le a\le e.
\]

The final coefficient of the top layer is (10.4), and the integral sum of all layers is (7.3).

## Step 4: identify the first Cartier class

Use Apéry–Lucas to obtain

\[
\mu_{p,e,n}=b_rR_q\pmod p.
\]

This is already an exact low-digit reduction of the supposedly high-order coefficient problem.

## Step 5: prove a genuine jet-polarization or primitive-minor theorem

The new theorem must couple:

- the ordinary/Hasse line of \(\mathscr V_p\);
- the Kummer extension line of \(\mathscr K_p\);
- the final jet functional \(\pi_n\).

A valid result would exhibit a nonzero primitive determinant, a perfect pairing, or a bounded-contact divisor for these three pieces. A statement only about the rank of \(\mathscr V_p\), the Fricke eigenvalue, or q-integrality cannot do this, as Section 9 proves.

## Step 6: lift through the Bockstein tower

If \(b_rR_q=0\pmod p\), compute the second and higher extension classes from (7.3). This requires an integral Frobenius comparison, not merely mod-p Hasse–Witt data. The exact objects are the carries in (10.6), so there is no ambiguity about what must be lifted.

This is the shortest honest modular route from the explicit q-series to the pointwise lcm exponent.

# 12. Direct answers to the requested alternatives

## Can the explicit Fricke law prove the denominator lower bound by itself?

No. For the present convolution the Fricke law is

\[
\mathscr R(t(W_6\tau))=-6\tau^2\mathscr R(t(\tau))
\quad\bmod 2\pi iE(W_6\tau)\mathbb Z,
\]

and follows immediately from \(t(W_6\tau)=t(\tau)\). It gives global analytic continuation and quasiperiod information, not the defining-characteristic last coefficient (7.9).

## What exact integrality does modularity give?

It gives

\[
t,q(t),E\in\mathbb Z[[q]]\text{ or }\mathbb Z[[T]],
\qquad
\Theta\ell(t(q))\in q\mathbb Z[[q]],
\]

and the integral Toeplitz lattice automorphism \(U_n(B)\). Consequently

\[
\operatorname{lcm}_{j\le n}\operatorname{den}(R_j)=L_n.
\]

It also gives, after the explicit Kummer construction, the p-integral normalized Frobenius defect \(\lambda_p\).

## What is the exact route to the pointwise lcm exponent?

For each \(p\) and \(e=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\):

\[
p^eR_n
=\sum_{a=0}^ep^{e-a}
[T^n]B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^a}),
\]

so

\[
c_p(n)
=\min\left(e,
 v_p\left(
 \sum_{a=0}^ep^{e-a}[T^n]B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^a})
 \right)
 \right).
\]

The first digit is

\[
[T^n]B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^e})
\equiv b_rR_q\pmod p,
\qquad n=qp^e+r.
\]

This is the exact coefficientwise bridge.

## What precise statement is missing?

A defining-characteristic **last-jet saturation/primitive-minor theorem** for the integral tensor extension \(\mathscr V_p\otimes\mathscr K_p\), controlling the contact of

\[
M_{B,n}V^e\lambda_p
\]

and its Bockstein lifts with \(\ker[T^n]\).

The extension lattice and its Frobenius matrix are explicit; the missing input is the nonorthogonality of one growing-order coefficient functional. The counterfamily \(-B(T)\log(1-T^M)\) shows that this cannot be deduced from modularity, Fricke symmetry, q-integrality, or Kummer integrality alone.

# Bottom line

The modular parametrization does yield a complete and useful structure:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Apéry period}
\;\otimes\;
\text{explicit Kummer }F\text{-crystal}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{p-typical jet formula (7.3)}.}
\]

It also yields the exact first Cartier digit

\[
\boxed{
[T^n]B(T)\lambda_p(T^{p^e})
\equiv b_rR_q\pmod p.}
\]

What it does **not** yield is the required primitivity of that one final jet coordinate. The ordinary integral lattice is globally saturated—indeed the prefix lcm identity (5.6) is exact—but the final projection can annihilate its top p-primary line. Fricke symmetry is blind to that defining-characteristic projection.

Therefore the modular route does not presently prove the coefficientwise denominator lower bound. Its exact remaining target is the last-jet Frobenius–Cartier saturation theorem described in Section 10; anything weaker is an upper-denominator theorem or a global-vector statement, neither of which reaches P3.2.
