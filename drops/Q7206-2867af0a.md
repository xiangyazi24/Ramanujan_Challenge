# R2 correction: far-bridge parameter counts and the exact sufficient average

## 1. Executive correction

Write

$$
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},\qquad
P_X=|\mathcal P_X|\asymp \frac{X}{\log X},
$$

and

$$
H=\lfloor\sqrt{2X}\rfloor\asymp X^{1/2},\qquad
K=\frac{H}{\sqrt{\log X}}.
$$

For a far bridge, $K<G<p$ (with the additional nonwrapping restrictions). Thus the number of possible $G$'s per prime is $O(X)$ and, at the level of formal parameter ranges, is $\asymp X$. The lower cutoff $K$ removes only $O(X^{1/2}/\sqrt{\log X})$ values, so it produces no power or logarithmic saving in the far range.

There are therefore

$$
H\cdot X=X^{3/2+o(1)}
$$

numerical $(s,G)$ labels, or $(s,G)$ cells per fixed prime, but there are

$$
P_XHX\asymp \frac{X^{5/2}}{\log X}
$$

prime-labelled cells $(p,s,G)$ on the whole dyad.

This separates the two quantities that were conflated in Q7203. In particular, if one had the uniform estimate

$$
\sum_{X<p\le2X}A_{s,G}(p)\ll X^{o(1)}
$$

for each numerical pair $(s,G)$, then summing over the $HX$ numerical pairs would give

$$
HX\,X^{o(1)}=X^{3/2+o(1)},
$$

not $X^{5/2+o(1)}$. Such a uniform theorem would be substantially stronger than needed.

There was a second arithmetic error in the old degree-bound line. Since $A_{s,G}(p)\ll s\le H$,

$$
P_X\cdot H\cdot X\cdot H
\asymp
\frac{X}{\log X}\cdot X^{1/2}\cdot X\cdot X^{1/2}
=
\frac{X^3}{\log X},
$$

not $X^{7/2}/\log X$.

Finally, when the common-root count is used directly as a majorant for separated energy, the exact sufficient target is

$$
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X},
$$

not merely $X^{2+o(1)}$. The latter suppresses a logarithm that the variance reduction explicitly needs.

## 2. The three different counting objects

Let $\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s}$ be the admissible far-bridge lengths for the prime $p$ and first span $s$. We only need

$$
|\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s}|\ll X.
$$

When a prime-independent indexing set is convenient, extend $G$ to $1\le G\le 2X$ and declare all inadmissible terms to be zero.

### 2.1 All common-root tuples

Define

$$
A_{s,G}(p)
=
\#\{x\in\mathbf F_p:
N_s(x)=0,\ N_G(x+s)=0\}.
$$

This deliberately ignores wrapping, carrier-clean primitivity, consecutiveness, reflection orientation, separation, and the existence of the second short chain. Put

$$
\mathcal A_X
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
\sum_{1\le s\le H}
\sum_{G\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s}}
A_{s,G}(p).
$$

Thus $\mathcal A_X$ counts the full common-root set

$$
\mathscr A_X
=
\{(p,x,s,G):p\in\mathcal P_X,\ s\le H,\ G\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s},
N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\}.
$$

### 2.2 The canonical separated-pair subset

Let $\mathscr C_X$ be the image of the canonical encoding of ordered, separated, reflection-quotient chain pairs with far bridge. By the established reconstruction and consecutiveness argument, the map to

$$
(p,x,s,G)
$$

is injective. Hence, with the conventions of `near_bridge_energy.tex`,

$$
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}E_{\mathrm{far}}(p)
=
|\mathscr C_X|.
$$

Every canonical tuple satisfies the two displayed continuant equations, so

$$
\mathscr C_X\subseteq\mathscr A_X,
\qquad
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}E_{\mathrm{far}}(p)\le \mathcal A_X.
\tag{2.1}
$$

The right-hand short span $t\le H$ is a property of a canonical tuple, but it is reconstructed from $(p,x,s,G)$. It is not an additional free parameter and must not contribute another factor $H$.

It is useful to retain one intermediate majorant. Let $Z^{\mathrm{ch}}_s(p)$ be the starts of genuine selected primitive off-center short chains whose total span is $s$, and set

$$
\mathcal B_X
=
\sum_{p,s,G}
\sum_{x\in Z^{\mathrm{ch}}_s(p)}
1_{N_G(x+s)=0}.
$$

This drops the terminal-chain and other right-end filters but retains the genuine first-chain support. Therefore

$$
|\mathscr C_X|\le \mathcal B_X\le \mathcal A_X.
\tag{2.2}
$$

A bound for $\mathcal B_X$ is a weaker, and potentially more natural, conditioned incidence statement than a bound for all of $\mathcal A_X$. It is not merely a restatement of $E_{\mathrm{far}}$, because it has forgotten the terminal short-chain condition.

### 2.3 Prime-weighted resultant valuations

For a nonzero integer resultant

$$
R_{s,G}=\operatorname{Res}_x(N_s(x),N_G(x+s)),
$$

the established Smith-normal-form inequality gives, at the good primes under consideration,

$$
A_{s,G}(p)\le v_p(R_{s,G}).
$$

If a center or carrier factor has first been removed, write $R^*_{s,G}$ for the nonzero residual integer and let $\kappa_{s,G}(p)$ denote the explicit common-root contribution discarded by that deflation. The correct inequality then has the form

$$
A_{s,G}(p)\le \kappa_{s,G}(p)+v_p(R^*_{s,G});
\tag{2.3}
$$

one may not silently delete the $\kappa$ term.

Define

$$
\mathcal K_X=\sum_{p,s,G}\kappa_{s,G}(p),
$$

$$
\mathcal V_X
=
\sum_{p,s,G}v_p(R^*_{s,G}),
$$

and the logarithmically prime-weighted sum

$$
\mathcal W_X
=
\sum_{p,s,G}(\log p)\,v_p(R^*_{s,G}).
$$

Because $X<p\le2X$,

$$
(\log X)\mathcal V_X
\le \mathcal W_X
\le (\log(2X))\mathcal V_X.
\tag{2.4}
$$

Combining (2.1)--(2.4),

$$
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}E_{\mathrm{far}}(p)
\le
\mathcal A_X
\le
\mathcal K_X+\mathcal V_X
\le
\mathcal K_X+\frac{\mathcal W_X}{\log X}.
\tag{2.5}
$$

This is the exact conversion between tuple counts and prime-weighted valuations.

## 3. Recomputing the unconditional parameter bounds

The exact degree is

$$
\deg_xN_s=3(s-1).
$$

For $s\le H<p$ and the large primes in the dyad, $N_s$ is a nonzero polynomial modulo $p$. Therefore

$$
A_{s,G}(p)\le \#\{x:N_s(x)=0\}\le 3(s-1).
$$

For one prime,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s\le H}
\sum_{G\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s}}
A_{s,G}(p)
&\le
O(X)\sum_{s\le H}3(s-1)\\
&\ll XH^2\\
&\asymp X^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
$$

Summing over the prime dyad gives

$$
\mathcal A_X
\ll
P_XXH^2
\asymp
\frac{X^3}{\log X}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

This is the sharp parameter-level bound obtained by retaining only the first common-root equation. It is one full power of $X$ above the desired energy target.

For comparison, the number of unrestricted $x$-slots is

$$
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
\sum_{s\le H}
\sum_{G\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s}}
p
\asymp
P_X\cdot H\cdot X\cdot X
\asymp
\frac{X^{7/2}}{\log X}.
\tag{3.3}
$$

The first short-return equation reduces this raw-slot budget from $X^{7/2}/\log X$ to at most $X^3/\log X$, an aggregate factor $X^{-1/2}$. The long common-root equation would then need an additional aggregate factor $X^{-1}$ to reach $X^2/\log X$ under this worst-case marginal normalization.

There is an equivalent chain-pair normalization. By the start-injectivity hypothesis, the total number $M_p$ of oriented primitive windows is at most $p$ (and the reflection-quotient mass is at most $p/2$). Thus the number of all ordered short-chain pairs is at most $p^2$, and

$$
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}M_p^2
\ll
P_XX^2
\asymp
\frac{X^3}{\log X}.
\tag{3.4}
$$

Relative to this explicitly defined all-pairs marginal, the desired separated-energy total again asks for a factor $X^{-1}\asymp p^{-1}$.

## 4. The weakest averaged estimate that is actually sufficient

To retain the subpower allowance while keeping every logarithm visible, write

$$
L_X=X^{o(1)},
\qquad
T_X=\frac{X^2L_X}{\log X}.
$$

The exact target is

$$
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}E_{\mathrm{far}}(p)\ll T_X.
$$

There are three levels of sufficient statements.

1. The formally weakest statement is $|\mathscr C_X|\ll T_X$, but this is just the desired conclusion in canonical coordinates.

2. The weakest immediate non-tautological relaxation obtained by forgetting only the terminal-chain condition is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal B_X\ll T_X.}
\tag{4.1}
$$

3. If one insists on an estimate stated solely through the unfiltered common-root counts $A_{s,G}(p)$, the exact sufficient averaged estimate is

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A_X
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
\sum_{s\le H}
\sum_{G\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s}}
A_{s,G}(p)
\ll
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X}.}
\tag{4.2}
$$

No pointwise or uniform estimate in $p,s,G$ is required. Equation (4.2), rather than $\mathcal A_X\ll X^{2+o(1)}$, is the corrected all-common-root target.

Several exactly equivalent normalizations clarify its strength.

### Average per prime

Since $P_X\asymp X/\log X$, (4.2) asks for

$$
\frac{1}{P_X}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
\sum_{s,G}A_{s,G}(p)
\ll X^{1+o(1)}.
\tag{4.3}
$$

Thus the admissible common-root mass may be as large as $X^{1+o(1)}$ per prime on average.

### Average per prime-labelled $(p,s,G)$ cell

The number of such cells is

$$
N_{\mathrm{cell}}\asymp P_XHX
\asymp \frac{X^{5/2}}{\log X}.
$$

Consequently (4.2) is equivalent to the mean bound

$$
\frac{\mathcal A_X}{N_{\mathrm{cell}}}
\ll X^{-1/2+o(1)}.
\tag{4.4}
$$

Relative to the baseline “one common root per $(p,s,G)$ cell,” the necessary average saving is only $X^{-1/2}=1/H$, not $1/p$.

### Average over primes for each numerical $(s,G)$ label

There are $O(HX)=O(X^{3/2})$ numerical labels. Hence the uniform estimate

$$
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}A_{s,G}(p)
\ll
\frac{X^{1/2+o(1)}}{\log X}
\tag{4.5}
$$

for every $(s,G)$ would suffice. Uniform $X^{o(1)}$, as proposed in Q7203, is far stronger: it gives

$$
\mathcal A_X\ll HX\,X^{o(1)}=X^{3/2+o(1)}.
$$

### Average per $(p,s)$

There are $P_XH\asymp X^{3/2}/\log X$ pairs $(p,s)$. Therefore (4.2) permits

$$
\sum_GA_{s,G}(p)
$$

to have average size $X^{1/2+o(1)}$ over $(p,s)$.

These are all the same target, merely divided by different explicitly counted parameter spaces.

## 5. Precisely when a “gain $1/p$” statement is correct

A naked phrase such as “gain $1/p$ beyond the marginals” is ambiguous. It becomes a correct normalized statement only after the marginal sample space is fixed.

### 5.1 Degree-cap first-return marginal

Let

$$
\mathcal M^{\mathrm{cap}}_1
=
P_X\cdot O(X)\cdot\sum_{s\le H}3(s-1)
\ll \frac{X^3}{\log X}.
$$

This is the worst-case budget for tuples satisfying the first equation $N_s(x)=0$ before the long equation is imposed. Since

$$
\frac{T_X}{\mathcal M^{\mathrm{cap}}_1}
\asymp X^{-1+o(1)},
$$

a theorem of the form

$$
\mathcal A_X
\ll
X^{-1+o(1)}\mathcal M^{\mathrm{cap}}_1
$$

would be sufficient. Because $p\asymp X$, this may accurately be described as a $1/p$ gain relative to the degree-cap first-return marginal.

This is not a logically necessary conditional probability statement: the actual first-return mass may be much smaller than its degree cap. It is a sufficient comparison against a specified worst-case baseline.

### 5.2 All ordered short-chain pairs

The all-pairs budget (3.4) is also $O(X^3/\log X)$. Therefore a $1/p$ same-state or long-alignment density relative to all ordered primitive short-chain pairs would likewise give the target. Again, the denominator must be stated.

### 5.3 One-root-per-cell baseline

If the baseline is instead one root for each prime-labelled $(p,s,G)$ cell, its total mass is $X^{5/2}/\log X$. Relative to that baseline the required saving is

$$
\frac{T_X}{N_{\mathrm{cell}}}=X^{-1/2+o(1)},
$$

not $1/p$.

### 5.4 Raw $x$-slot baseline

Relative to all raw slots (3.3), the required density is

$$
\frac{T_X}{X^{7/2}/\log X}=X^{-3/2+o(1)}.
$$

The degree cap for $N_s$ supplies the first factor $X^{-1/2}$ in aggregate, and a further $X^{-1}$ would reach the target.

Thus the corrected statement is:

> A $1/p$ gain is exactly sufficient relative to either the $X^3/\log X$ degree-cap first-return budget or the $X^3/\log X$ all-short-chain-pairs budget. Relative to the $(p,s,G)$ cell count, the required gain is only $1/\sqrt p$. Without naming the denominator, “gain $1/p$” has no definite exponent content.

Also, $A_{s,G}(p)$ itself has already discarded the second short-chain condition. It counts one short return and one long return. The right-hand short span $t$ belongs to the canonical subset and is not a free $H$-sized marginal in the $A_{s,G}$ sum.

## 6. Prime-weighted valuations and total-height bookkeeping

From (2.5), a sufficient unweighted valuation estimate is

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal K_X+\mathcal V_X
\ll
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X}.}
\tag{6.1}
$$

Equivalently, in logarithmically prime-weighted form it is enough that

$$
\boxed{
(\log X)\mathcal K_X+\mathcal W_X
\ll X^{2+o(1)}.}
\tag{6.2}
$$

There is no additional factor $P_X$ in (6.2): $\mathcal W_X$ already sums over the primes in the dyad.

Using the generic height bound from Q7203,

$$
\log|R^*_{s,G}|\ll sG\log(s+G),
$$

the product formula gives, pair by pair,

$$
\sum_{X<p\le2X}(\log p)v_p(R^*_{s,G})
\le \log|R^*_{s,G}|.
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal W_X
&\ll
\sum_{s\le H}\sum_{G\le2X}sG\log(s+G)\\
&\ll
\log(3X)
\left(\sum_{s\le H}s\right)
\left(\sum_{G\le2X}G\right)\\
&\ll
H^2X^2\log X\\
&\asymp X^3\log X.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
$$

Dividing by $\log X$ yields only

$$
\mathcal V_X\ll X^3.
\tag{6.4}
$$

Compare these with the sufficient targets:

$$
\mathcal W_X\ll X^{2+o(1)},
\qquad
\mathcal V_X\ll \frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
$$

Thus the generic total-height route is one full power of $X$ short at exponent level, and at the displayed-log level its bound exceeds the target by a factor $X\log X$ (up to the allowed $X^{o(1)}$). Q7203's phrase “misses by exactly $X$” was acceptable only after suppressing all logarithms; with the requested bookkeeping, the ratio is $X\log X$.

Notice the different summations:

* The combinatorial cell count $P_XHX$ contains the prime-count factor $P_X$.
* The height sum $\sum_{s,G}\log|R^*_{s,G}|$ does not contain $P_X$, because one integer's logarithmic height already dominates the logarithmically weighted valuations at all primes.
* Passing from $\mathcal W_X$ to $\mathcal V_X$ divides by $\log X$, not by $P_X$ and not by $X$.

These distinctions prevent the previous exponent drift.

## 8. Corrected theorem to use going forward

The clean replacement for the erroneous conclusion in Q7203 is the following.

**Corrected far-bridge sufficiency statement.** Assume the established canonical injection of ordered far separated chain-orbit pairs into $(p,x,s,G)$, with $s\le H\asymp\sqrt X$ and $G\le p\asymp X$. Then

$$
\sum_{X<p\le2X}E_{\mathrm{far}}(p)
\le
\sum_{X<p\le2X}
\sum_{s\le H}
\sum_{G\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s}}
A_{s,G}(p).
$$

Consequently, the weakest estimate stated only in terms of the full common-root counts that is directly sufficient is

$$
\sum_{X<p\le2X}
\sum_{s\le H}
\sum_{G\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{far}}_{p,s}}
A_{s,G}(p)
\ll
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
$$

Equivalently, for a nonzero residual resultant with explicit carrier correction, it is sufficient to prove

$$
(\log X)\mathcal K_X+
\sum_{X<p\le2X}
\sum_{s\le H}
\sum_G
(\log p)v_p(R^*_{s,G})
\ll X^{2+o(1)}.
$$

The deterministic degree marginal gives only $X^3/\log X$; the generic total-height method gives $X^3$ unweighted or $X^3\log X$ weighted. Hence the remaining arithmetic theorem must improve the degree-marginal count by a factor $X^{1-o(1)}$, equivalently improve the generic weighted-height budget by $X\log X/X^{o(1)}$. No additional $H$ factor for the terminal span and no additional prime-count factor in the weighted-height sum are permitted.
