# K3 branch-defect alternative proof: conditional invariant-stalk theorem

## 1. Setup and conclusion

Let $\pi:\mathcal X\to \Delta=\operatorname{Spec}R$ be a proper flat family over a strictly henselian DVR with generic geometric fiber $X_{\bar\eta}$ smooth and special fiber having:

1. persistent rational double points (the fixed boundary ADE singularities), and
2. one additional isolated ordinary double point $y$ in the open torus fiber.

Let $\widetilde X_0$ be the full resolution of the special fiber and assume the resolved special fiber is a K3 surface. The numerical statement

$$\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}|H^2(\widetilde X_0))=20p+\theta(\alpha)$$

and

$$\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}|H^2(X_0^{\rm partial}))=19p+\theta(\alpha)$$

is compatible with the following conditional theorem: the new ODP contributes exactly one algebraic Tate class and the rank-two primitive quotient has invariant trace $\theta(\alpha)$.

The missing point is not the local ODP calculation; it is identifying the rank-19 lattice globally as a Frobenius-stable subspace without using a simultaneous resolution of every persistent ADE component.

## 2. Grothendieck--Lefschetz trace correction

For a smooth projective K3 surface $S/\mathbf F_p$,

$$\#S(\mathbf F_p)=1+p^2+\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}|H^2_{\acute et}(S_{\bar{\mathbf F}_p},\mathbf Q_\ell)).$$

This uses arithmetic Frobenius convention. Since

$$H^0=\mathbf Q_\ell,\qquad H^1=H^3=0,\qquad H^4=\mathbf Q_\ell(-2),$$

only $H^2$ contributes between the two Tate terms.

Under geometric Frobenius, the eigenvalues are inverted, and the equivalent formula is

$$\#S(\mathbf F_p)=1+p^2+\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{\rm geom}|H^2)$$

with the convention that the eigenvalues of geometric Frobenius have weight $2$ after the usual normalization. The expression $1+p^2+p^4-\operatorname{Tr}$ is a threefold formula and is dimensionally wrong for a surface.

If $Y$ is the singular partial resolution and $\widetilde Y$ replaces one split rational $A_1$ point by $E\simeq\mathbf P^1$, then the exceptional class contributes one copy of $\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)$, hence

$$H^2(\widetilde Y)=H^2(Y)\oplus \mathbf Q_\ell(-1),$$

and its Frobenius trace contribution is $p$ under arithmetic Frobenius. Therefore

$$\operatorname{Tr}H^2(\widetilde Y)=\operatorname{Tr}H^2(Y)+p.$$

## 3. Local ODP nearby/vanishing cycle calculation

A split ODP has etale-local equation

$$xy-zw=t$$

after an etale change of coordinates. The Milnor fiber has one vanishing cycle. The nearby-cycle triangle gives

$$H^2(X_{\bar\eta})\to H^2(X_{\bar 0})\to H^0(y,R^2\Phi)\to H^3(X_{\bar\eta}).$$

For a single A1 point,

$$R^2\Phi_y\simeq \mathbf Q_\ell(-1),$$

and the invariant vanishing cycle becomes the exceptional divisor class after resolution. The local contribution is therefore exactly one Tate class.

This proves the ODP contribution provided:

- the singularity is isolated,
- the total space is regular in the transversal direction,
- the vanishing-cycle sheaf is supported only at $y$.

These are local conditions and do not require resolving persistent boundary ADE points.

## 4. Excision from persistent boundary ADE strata

Let $U\subset\mathcal X$ remove disjoint closed neighborhoods of the boundary ADE strata. Assume:

- the boundary singular locus is finite over $R$;
- the boundary neighborhoods admit simultaneous crepant resolutions after an etale base change;
- their exceptional divisor classes extend as relative Cartier divisors.

Then proper base change and excision identify the difference between generic and special cohomology with the sum of local vanishing-cycle terms. Since the boundary terms are constant in the family, they contribute no new rank defect.

Thus the only new primitive defect is the isolated ODP contribution.

## 5. Rank-19 lattice hypothesis

Let $L\subset \operatorname{NS}(X_{\bar\eta})$ be generated by the 19 known boundary divisor closures. The required sufficient hypotheses are:

1. $\mathcal X$ is proper and flat over $R$.
2. The generic fiber is smooth.
3. Each divisor closure $D_i\subset\mathcal X$ is relative effective Cartier.
4. The restrictions $D_i|_{X_t}$ remain Cartier on every smooth fiber.
5. Intersection numbers are constant in the flat family.
6. The Gram matrix of the generic classes has determinant $48\ne0$.
7. Frobenius preserves the set of divisor classes.

Then $L$ is a rank-19 Frobenius-stable subrepresentation with nondegenerate intersection pairing.

The quotient

$$V=L^\perp\subset H^2(X_{\bar\eta})$$

has dimension two. The conifold resolution adds the exceptional Tate class, giving the rank-20 algebraic lattice on the resolved special fiber.

## 6. What is formal versus what must be checked

Formal consequences:

- Grothendieck--Lefschetz trace formula.
- One split A1 exceptional curve contributes one $\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)$.
- A single ODP contributes one vanishing-cycle Tate class.
- A rank-19 nondegenerate Frobenius-stable lattice leaves a rank-two quotient.

Still requiring family-specific verification:

- explicit boundary charts proving simultaneous resolution or Cartier extension;
- disjointness of the open ODP from boundary singular loci;
- flatness of each divisor closure;
- constancy of the intersection matrix under specialization.

The displayed Laurent equation for the toric fiber can verify the first local ODP condition, but it does not by itself prove the global Cartier assertions for boundary divisors.

## 7. Hessian character location

After removing the Tate algebraic part, the rank-two primitive quotient has trace

$$\theta(\alpha)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}|V).$$

The Hessian character is contained in this two-dimensional primitive representation, not in the rank-19 boundary lattice and not in the exceptional $\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)$ factor.

Therefore the correct conditional statement is

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}(H^2_{\rm primitive})=\theta(\alpha)}$$

provided the rank-19 divisor-lattice hypotheses above hold.
