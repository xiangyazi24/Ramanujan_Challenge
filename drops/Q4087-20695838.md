## 2. Correct support ideal: four disjoint edges

Work first in AD1 and abbreviate

\[
X_{ac}=b_{R_a},\quad Y_{ac}=b_{H_c},
\]
\[
X_{bc}=b_{R_c},\quad Y_{bc}=b_{L_b},
\]
\[
X_{bd}=b_{R_b},\quad Y_{bd}=b_{H_d},
\]
\[
X_{ad}=b_{R_d},\quad Y_{ad}=b_{L_a}.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(S\) be a coefficient ring in which the edge primes are not inserted by hand as coefficient factors.  The universal formal condition that a polynomial be forced divisible by \(q_e\) from the support of edge \(e\) is membership in

\[
J_e=(X_e,Y_e).
\]

Hence the exact four-edge support ideal is

\[
I_{\rm AD1}
=(X_{ac},Y_{ac})\cap(X_{bc},Y_{bc})
 \cap(X_{bd},Y_{bd})\cap(X_{ad},Y_{ad}).
\tag{9}
\]

Because these are monomial ideals on four pairwise-disjoint variable pairs,

\[
\boxed{
I_{\rm AD1}
=(X_{ac},Y_{ac})(X_{bc},Y_{bc})
 (X_{bd},Y_{bd})(X_{ad},Y_{ad}).
}
\tag{10}
\]

Exactly the same formula holds for AD2 after replacing the four pairs by (7).

### Proof of (10)

A monomial lies in the intersection (9) iff it contains at least one variable from each of the four edge pairs.  Equivalently it is divisible by one of the monomials

\[
Z_{ac}Z_{bc}Z_{bd}Z_{ad},
\qquad Z_e\in\{X_e,Y_e\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Those are exactly the generators of the product ideal in (10).  Since monomial membership is termwise, this proves equality.  No finite scan and no Apéry asymptotic is involved.

## 7. The equal-index-sum binomial is unique at minimum degree

The cancellation in (15)/(17) is not one of many accidental formal choices.  It is the unique affine index-sum relation between two distinct minimum support generators.

For AD1, let \(\varepsilon_e\in\{-1,0,1\}\) be the difference of the binary endpoint choices of two degree-four generators.  If their total indices agree identically in \(p,a,b,c,d\), then

\[
\sum_e\varepsilon_e\delta_e=0.
\tag{26}
\]

Using (14), the coefficients of \(a,b,c,d\) give

\[
\varepsilon_{bc}=\varepsilon_{ac},\qquad
\varepsilon_{ad}=-\varepsilon_{ac},\qquad
\varepsilon_{bd}=-\varepsilon_{ac}.
\]

The constant and \(p\)-coefficients then vanish automatically.  Thus

\[
(\varepsilon_{ac},\varepsilon_{bc},\varepsilon_{bd},\varepsilon_{ad})
=t(1,1,-1,-1).
\tag{27}
\]

For distinct binary choices, \(t=\pm1\).  Therefore the unordered pair is exactly

\[
(1,1,0,0)\quad\text{versus}\quad(0,0,1,1),
\]

which is (18).  The same coefficient comparison using (16) gives the identical one-dimensional relation for AD2.

Hence, **up to sign and scalar**, (19) is the unique minimum-degree binomial whose two terms have exactly equal affine index sum.

This proves that a genuine cancellation-friendly determinant lane remains, but it starts at quartic degree and has one canonical affine candidate.
