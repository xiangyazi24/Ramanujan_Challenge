# 2. Exact global completion of the second jet

For `1<=j<=n`, put `q=n-j` and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_0(n,j)&=b_jb_q,\\
C_1(n,j)&=b_jg_q-g_jb_q,\\
C_2(n,j)&=b_j\eta_q+\eta_jb_q-g_jg_q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
C_0\in\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\Lambda_nC_1\in\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\Lambda_n^2C_2\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{2.2}
\]

At the pole `Y=-j`, the complete principal part of `G_n` is

\[
\frac{A_3}{(Y+j)^3}+
\frac{A_2}{(Y+j)^2}+
\frac{A_1}{Y+j},
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
A_3(n,j)&=-j^3C_0(n,j),\\
A_2(n,j)&=3j^2C_0(n,j)-j^3C_1(n,j),\\
A_1(n,j)&=-j^3C_2(n,j)+3j^2C_1(n,j)-3jC_0(n,j).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let

\[
\ell_0=0,\qquad \ell_1=1,
\qquad \ell_{a+1}=34\ell_a-\ell_{a-1}.
\]

Since `G_n` has no poles except `-1,...,-n`, all of order at most three,
and its polynomial part is the constant `ell_(n+1)`, the second jets at all
poles determine the **entire rational function**:

\[
\boxed{
G_n(Y)=\ell_{n+1}
+\sum_{j=1}^n\left(
 \frac{A_1(n,j)}{Y+j}
 +\frac{A_2(n,j)}{(Y+j)^2}
 +\frac{A_3(n,j)}{(Y+j)^3}
\right).
}
\tag{PF}
\]

This is the exact repair of the invalid operation “truncate at `Y=0` and
substitute `Y=1/2`.”  No higher remainder remains.

Put

\[
O_n=\prod_{a=1}^n(2a+1)=3\cdot5\cdots(2n+1).
\]

Since

\[
m_{n+1}=O_n^3G_n(1/2),
\]

`(PF)` gives

\[
\boxed{
m_{n+1}=O_n^3\left(\ell_{n+1}+
\sum_{j=1}^n\Phi_{n,j}\right),}
\tag{2.4}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{n,j}={}&
-\frac{2j^3}{2j+1}C_2(n,j)\\
&+\frac{2j^2(4j+3)}{(2j+1)^2}C_1(n,j)\\
&-\frac{2j(4j^2+6j+3)}{(2j+1)^3}C_0(n,j).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Term by term,

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_n^2O_n^3\Phi_{n,j}\in\mathbf Z.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

This is the requested `Lambda^2` scale.

# 3. Put the physical placement into the residue sum

The `m_h` formula above is still `r`-free, so by itself it cannot know which
fixed-gap Dirichlet return is the selected Apéry return.  The necessary step
is to evaluate the **same** residues at

\[
Y=r+1.
\]

Define

\[
D_{r,n}=\prod_{a=1}^n(r+a+1)^3
       =\prod_{u=r+2}^{s}u^3.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Substitution in `(PF)` gives

\[
G_n(r+1)=\ell_{n+1}+\sum_{j=1}^n\Pi_{r,n,j},
\tag{3.2}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_{r,n,j}={}&
-\frac{j^3}{r+j+1}C_2(n,j)\\
&+\frac{j^2(3r+2j+3)}{(r+j+1)^2}C_1(n,j)\\
&-\frac{j\bigl(j^2+3j(r+1)+3(r+1)^2\bigr)}
       {(r+j+1)^3}C_0(n,j).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Define the placement defect

\[
\Theta_{r,n,j}=\Phi_{n,j}-\Pi_{r,n,j}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

A useful factored form is obtained by putting

\[
a=j+\frac12,
\qquad d=r+j+1,
\qquad \delta=r+\frac12=d-a.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_{r,n,j}
=\delta\Bigg[&
-\frac{j^3}{ad}C_2\\
&+\frac{j^2(3ad-j(a+d))}{a^2d^2}C_1\\
&-\frac{j\left(
3a^2d^2-3j(a+d)ad+j^2(a^2+ad+d^2)
\right)}{a^3d^3}C_0
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Now clear the half-step and physical poles simultaneously:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{r,n,j}:=D_{r,n}O_n^3\Theta_{r,n,j}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Because `D_(r,n)` contains `(r+j+1)^3`, it clears every physical placement
pole, while `O_n^3` clears every half-step pole.  The only remaining rational
denominators are those of `C1,C2`.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{den}(\mathcal R_{r,n,j})\mid L^2
\qquad(1\le j\le n).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is termwise, not merely after summation.

Subtract `(3.2)` from `(2.4)/O_n^3`, multiply by `D_(r,n)O_n^3`, and use
`D_(r,n)G_n(r+1)=N_(n+1)(r)=N_h(r)`.  One obtains the exact integer identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^n\mathcal R_{r,n,j}
=D_{r,n}m_h-O_n^3N_h(r).
}
\tag{R-sum}
\]

There is no Taylor remainder and no companion solution here.

# 4. Eliminate the physical Green value with the selected Apéry boundary

The continuant cut gives

\[
\boxed{
 b_{r+1}N_h(r)
 =D_{r,n}b_s+E_{r,n}b_r,
}
\tag{CUT}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
E_{r,n}=(r+1)^3U_{n-1}(r+2)\in\mathbf Z.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Multiplying `(R-sum)` by `b_(r+1)` and substituting `(CUT)` yields

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 b_{r+1}\sum_{j=1}^n\mathcal R_{r,n,j}
={}&D_{r,n}b_{r+1}m_h\\
&-O_n^3D_{r,n}b_s
-O_n^3E_{r,n}b_r.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{PC}
\]

This is the first exact three-generator identity in this attack which both
contains the physical placement and keeps the jet denominators at `Lambda^2`.
Its right side is zero on the ideal `(b_r,b_s,m_h)`, as it should be, but the
left side retains a nontrivial placement-residue sum.

## Prime-local equivalence

Let `p>s+1` be prime and suppose

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad p\mid b_s.
\]

Every factor of `D_(r,n)` and `L` is a `p`-unit.  Reducing `(CUT)` modulo `p`
gives

\[
b_{r+1}N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The proved no-consecutive-zero lemma gives `p∤b_(r+1)`, hence

\[
p\mid N_h(r).
\]

Reduce `(R-sum)` modulo `p`.  Because `D_(r,n)` is a unit,

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid m_h
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p\mid\sum_{j=1}^n\mathcal R_{r,n,j}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r,b_s,m_h
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p\mid b_r,b_s,\sum_j\mathcal R_{r,n,j},
 \qquad p>s+1.
}
\tag{LP-equiv}
\]

No division by `O_n` is used, so this remains valid even when a prime
`p>s+1` divides one of the odd factors of `O_n`.

# 5. The exact remaining WZ target

The original prime-local CL2 statement is now reduced to one finite syzygy.
It is enough to prove rational functions

\[
A^\sharp_{r,n},\quad B^\sharp_{r,n},\quad C^\sharp_{r,n}
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
T
=A^\sharp_{r,n}b_r
+B^\sharp_{r,n}b_s
+C^\sharp_{r,n}\sum_{j=1}^n\mathcal R_{r,n,j},
}
\tag{PR-WZ}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{den}(A^\sharp),
\operatorname{den}(B^\sharp),
\operatorname{den}(C^\sharp)\mid L^2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Indeed, if `p>s+1` divides `b_r,b_s,m_h`, `(LP-equiv)` kills all three terms
on the right and `p∤L`, so `(PR-WZ)` gives

\[
p\mid T=r+s+1.
\]

This is a strictly smaller target than the original global Bezout problem:
all half-step Green values and all higher Taylor coefficients have been
eliminated, and the summand belongs to the fixed bilinear contiguous module

\[
J_j\otimes J_{n-j}
\]

generated by the recurrences `(1.4)--(1.6)`.

A direct global three-generator identity would follow from `(PR-WZ)` after
multiplying by `b_(r+1)` and using `(PC)`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
T b_{r+1}={}&
\bigl(A^\sharp b_{r+1}-C^\sharp O_n^3E_{r,n}\bigr)b_r\\
&+\bigl(B^\sharp b_{r+1}-C^\sharp O_n^3D_{r,n}\bigr)b_s\\
&+C^\sharp D_{r,n}b_{r+1}m_h.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For the **prime-local** theorem this is enough because `b_(r+1)` is a
`p`-unit under `p|b_r`.  It is not yet the requested global CL2 Bezout with a
smooth right-hand unit, because `b_(r+1)` itself can have large prime factors.
That is the exact distinction between the prime-local and global forms.

# 6. First exact obstruction: the residue defect has the wrong universal center

Define, for fixed `n`,

\[
F_n(X)=
\left(\prod_{a=1}^n(X+a+1)^3\right)m_{n+1}
-O_n^3N_{n+1}(X).
\tag{6.1}
\]

At `X=-1/2`, the two terms agree by the definition of `m_(n+1)`, so
`F_n(-1/2)=0`.  Since `F_n` has integer coefficients and `2X+1` is primitive,
Gauss's lemma gives

\[
\boxed{
F_n(X)=(2X+1)K_n(X),
\qquad K_n(X)\in\mathbf Z[X].
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\sum_j\mathcal R_{r,n,j}=(2r+1)K_n(r).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The raw half-step/placement residue defect therefore sees the universal center
`2r+1`, whereas CL2 needs

\[
T=2r+n+2.
\]

For example, at `n=1`,

\[
\boxed{
284(r+2)^3-27P(r+1)
=-(2r+1)(317r^2+1055r+887).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is a precise obstruction to the tempting formula class in which one
throws away the two selected endpoint states and tries to obtain `T` by
factoring/telescoping the residue defect alone.  The missing `n+1` cannot come
from that difference; it must enter through the physical cut and endpoint
boundary.

This is scoped: it does not rule out `(PR-WZ)`, because `(PR-WZ)` explicitly
retains `b_r,b_s`.

# 8. A selected-boundary adjoint/WZ identity -- and why it is still not the unit

There is a natural exact WZ identity which *does* use the selected Apéry
solution and *does* produce `T=r+s+1`.  It is useful because it shows exactly
why a first-order reflected adjoint is still insufficient.

Assume `r>=1` (the case `r=0` is trivial for CL2 because `b_0=1`).  Put

\[
d=r+s,
\qquad T=d+1,
\qquad w_k=b_{d-k}.
\]

Use the self-adjoint operator

\[
\mathcal L_k f
=(k+1)^3f_{k+1}-P(k)f_k+k^3f_{k-1}.
\]

Let `m=d-k=T-1-k`.  Since `P(-1-x)=-P(x)`, adding the Apéry recurrence at
index `m` gives the exact defect

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_k w=T\,R_k,
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
R_k=A_kb_{m-1}-Q_kb_m+C_kb_{m+1},
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
A_k&=T^2-3T(k+1)+3(k+1)^2,\\
Q_k&=34T^2-102kT-51T+102k^2+102k+27,\\
C_k&=T^2-3Tk+3k^2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Indeed these are simply

\[
A_k=\frac{(k+1)^3+m^3}{T},
\quad
Q_k=\frac{P(k)+P(m)}{T},
\quad
C_k=\frac{k^3+(m+1)^3}{T}.
\]

Define the Abel current

\[
J_k=(k+1)^3(b_kw_{k+1}-b_{k+1}w_k).
\]

A direct subtraction gives

\[
J_k-J_{k-1}=b_k\mathcal L_kw=T b_kR_k.
\]

Summing from `r` through `s` therefore gives the exact reflected WZ
certificate

\[
\boxed{
T\sum_{k=r}^s b_kR_k
=\bigl((s+1)^3-r^3\bigr)
 \bigl(b_sb_{r-1}-b_rb_{s+1}\bigr).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
C_{r,s}=(s+1)^3-r^3,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
-C_{r,s}b_{s+1}\,b_r
+C_{r,s}b_{r-1}\,b_s
=T\,S_{r,s},
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

where

\[
S_{r,s}=\sum_{k=r}^s b_kR_k.
\]

This looks tantalizingly close to the desired Bezout identity: it has explicit
`X,Y`, it has exactly `T`, and it uses the selected Apéry solution.
But it is structurally the wrong residual.  On the endpoint ideal
`(b_r,b_s)`, `(8.5)` says

\[
T S_{r,s}=0.
\]

Therefore at every target prime with `p∤T`, the residual `S_(r,s)` vanishes.
It can never be the required prime-local unit.  No amount of factoring trivial
integer gcds from `(8.5)` changes this ideal-theoretic obstruction.

This rules out the most natural first-order reflected-adjoint WZ certificate.
A successful certificate has to use a confluent/second-jet object such as the
placement residue of Sections 2--5, not merely the reflected physical
solution.

# 9. A useful prime-local corollary of the global partial fractions

The exact partial fractions also recover the long-gap singular factorization
without any Taylor argument.

Let `p>s+1` and suppose

\[
p\le2h-1.
\]

Put

\[
c=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad q=h-1-c.
\]

Then `1<=c<=n=h-1`, and the odd product `O_n` contains exactly one factor
`p=2c+1`.  Since `p>n+1`, all jet denominators `Lambda_n` are `p`-units.
In `(2.4)`, every pole term vanishes modulo `p` after multiplication by
`O_n^3` except the cubic part at `j=c`.  Its coefficient reduces to `1` because
`2c=-1 mod p`.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
 m_h\equiv
 \left(\frac{O_n}{p}\right)^3 b_c b_q
 \pmod p.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
p\mid m_h
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_c\ \text{or}\ p\mid b_q
\qquad(p>s+1,\ p\le2h-1).
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is the same central/additive-third-zero obstruction seen in the long
cross-wing analysis of Q2192.  It is an exact structural reduction, but it
still does not prove CL2 because neither the central zero nor the additional
low zero is currently excluded uniformly.  It again shows why selected zero
geometry, not just a two-minor determinant, is the remaining issue.

## The known large-prime mate example
