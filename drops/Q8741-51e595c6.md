The label suggests the algebraic-$Z_p$ window: the relevant point is whether the zero sets $Z_p=\{r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}$ behave like random algebraic objects or have extra structure.

For Apéry $b_n$, there is substantial non-random structure: Lucas factorization $b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p$, reflection symmetry $b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r$, and the associated Hasse-invariant interpretation constrain $Z_p$. Thus a Poisson model can be a useful heuristic for average shell behavior, but it should not be treated as a local independence theorem.

The strongest expected route is algebraic: interpret $b_r\bmod p$ through the Hasse invariant of the Apéry elliptic/K3 family. Then zeros are supersingular-type loci of an algebraic function, and one hopes to control their distribution by geometry (monodromy, Deligne bounds, or equidistribution of Frobenius). This is why the observed $|Z_p|=O(p^{2/3})$ and the gcd bounds are plausible despite non-random correlations.

A realistic conjecture is that for most primes, $|Z_p|/p$ has a stable limiting distribution after normalization, but not necessarily literal Poisson. For the gcd problem, the algebraic structure should dominate the small-prime contribution, while shell primes are governed by average zero-density.

A complete proof would likely require an explicit sheaf-theoretic description of the Hasse invariant family and square-root cancellation for its Mellin transforms, rather than a purely probabilistic argument.
