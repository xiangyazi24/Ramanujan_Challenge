# P3.2 direct computation: the split \(W_m=U_m-2\lambda_rV_m\)

## Verdict

With

\[
\lambda_r=r(r+1),
\qquad
A_m=16(r+m)^3+27(r+m)^2+12(r+m)+3,
\]

the proposed split is exact for every \(m\):

\[
\boxed{W_m=U_m-2r(r+1)V_m.}
\]

Indeed, subtracting twice \(\lambda_r\) times the \(V\)-recurrence from the \(U\)-recurrence gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{m+1}-2\lambda_rV_{m+1}
={}&(r+m+1)^6(U_m-2\lambda_rV_m)\\
&+\Bigl(2A_{m+1}
 -2(2(r+m+1)-1)\lambda_r\Bigr)N_mR_m\\
={}&(r+m+1)^6W_m+\Delta_{m+1}N_mR_m.
\end{aligned}
\]

The initial value is also correct:

\[
U_1-2\lambda_rV_1=1=W_1.
\]

The exact degrees are

\[
\boxed{
\deg U_m=6(m-1)\quad(m\ge1),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\deg V_m=6m-8\quad(m\ge2),
}
\]

while \(V_1=0\), whose degree is undefined (or \(-\infty\) by convention).

Factoring over \(\mathbf Q[r]\), with integer contents displayed separately, gives:

| \(m\) | \(\deg U_m\) | factorization of \(U_m\) | \(\deg V_m\) | factorization of \(V_m\) |
|---:|---:|:---|---:|:---|
| 1 | 0 | \(1\) | \(-\infty\) | \(0\) |
| 2 | 6 | primitive irreducible \(F_6\) | 4 | \((2r+3)^2(3r^2+5r+3)\) |
| 3 | 12 | primitive irreducible \(F_{12}\) | 10 | \(2(2r+3)G_9\), \(G_9\) irreducible |
| 4 | 18 | primitive irreducible \(F_{18}\) | 16 | \(2G_{16}\), \(G_{16}\) irreducible |
| 5 | 24 | primitive irreducible \(F_{24}\) | 22 | \(2G_{22}\), \(G_{22}\) irreducible |

Thus no nonconstant factor occurs in any \(U_m\) for \(2\le m\le5\).  The factor \(2r+3\) occurs in \(V_2\) and \(V_3\), but it does **not** persist into \(V_4\) or \(V_5\).

---

## 1. Degree calculation

Write

\[
a_m=\operatorname{lc}_r(N_m),
\qquad
b_m=\operatorname{lc}_r(R_m).
\]

From the two continuant recurrences,

\[
\deg N_m=3(m-1),\qquad
\deg R_m=3m,
\]

and their leading coefficients satisfy

\[
a_0=0,\quad a_1=1,\quad
\boxed{a_{m+1}=34a_m-a_{m-1}},
\]

\[
b_0=1,\quad b_1=6,\quad
\boxed{b_m=6b_{m-1}-b_{m-2}}.
\]

For the range needed here,

\[
(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)=(1,34,1155,39236),
\]

\[
(b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)=(6,35,204,1189).
\]

Let \(u_m\) and \(v_m\) denote the leading coefficients of \(U_m\) and \(V_m\), respectively. Since \(2A_{m+1}\) has leading coefficient \(32\), and \(2(r+m+1)-1\) has leading coefficient \(2\),

\[
\boxed{u_{m+1}=u_m+32a_mb_m},
\]

\[
\boxed{v_{m+1}=v_m+2a_mb_m}.
\]

All these quantities are positive, so the two top-degree contributions never cancel. Explicitly,

\[
(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4,u_5)
=(1,193,38273,7578113,1500429441),
\]

\[
(v_2,v_3,v_4,v_5)
=(12,2392,473632,93776840).
\]

This proves the claimed degree formulas and independently checks the leading terms below.

# 2. Exact polynomials and factorizations

All irreducibility statements in this section are over \(\mathbf Q[r]\); since the displayed factors are primitive, Gauss's lemma gives the equivalent statement over \(\mathbf Z[r]\).

## \(m=1\)

\[
\boxed{U_1=1,\qquad V_1=0.}
\]

There is no factorization issue for the zero polynomial.

# 3. Structural conclusions from the first five cases

The direct split does not produce a common low-degree factor between \(U_m\) and \(V_m\):

- every \(U_m\), \(2\le m\le5\), is primitive and irreducible;
- \(V_2\) is exceptional, with the square factor \((2r+3)^2\);
- \(V_3\) retains one copy of \(2r+3\);
- the primitive parts of \(V_4\) and \(V_5\) are irreducible and have no linear factor.

In particular, the computations do **not** support a factorization

\[
U_m=C_m(r)\widetilde U_m,\qquad
V_m=C_m(r)\widetilde V_m
\]

with a persistent nonconstant common carrier \(C_m\) visible for \(m=2,3,4,5\). The useful feature of the split is instead that \(W_m\) is affine in the spectral parameter \(\lambda_r\):

\[
\boxed{W_m=U_m-2\lambda_rV_m,}
\]

with degrees differing by exactly two for every \(m\ge2\):

\[
\boxed{\deg U_m-\deg V_m=2.}
\]

The exact symbolic audit also checked, independently from the inductive argument, that

\[
W_j=U_j-2r(r+1)V_j
\qquad(j=1,2,3,4,5).
\]

All five checks returned equality in \(\mathbf Z[r]\).
