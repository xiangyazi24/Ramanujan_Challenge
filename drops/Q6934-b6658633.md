## Verdict

I do **not** obtain a homogeneous recessive row `J_n` from `R_n` by a local rational correction or by a convergent positive tangent tail. I can, however, decisively close the two most plausible proposed routes.

1. The positive tangent tail and each of its first two theta moments have exact nth-root rate **16**, not `mu = 17 - 12 sqrt(2)`. This follows from a uniform coefficientwise inequality

   ```text
   A_(n,m) / A_(n,0) <= 16/(m+2)^4
   ```

   and an exact closed form for `A_(n,0)`. Therefore any nonzero, one-sign, fixed-degree polynomial-in-theta tail has rate 16. Killing finitely many leading tail coefficients does not alter that rate.

2. The singular upper-contiguous iteration is exact on `|z|<1`, but the first iteration that actually hits the zero of `(-n)_(n+1)` has degree `n+1`. Algebraically this is a splitting of the tangent extension over a field containing indefinite harmonic sums; analytically it is a resonant finite-part/logarithmic companion with an exact connection formula. To prove rate `mu` without generic Miller/Levinson, that companion still needs a sign-definite Euler/Laplace representation whose phase has maximum `log(mu)`. The raw positive tail cannot supply this.

I do not claim the requested homogeneous formula has been completed. The exact results below sharply identify the smallest missing input.

This proof requires no Stirling formula.

# 6. Fixed-degree positive theta corrections cannot produce `mu`

The preceding estimate yields a useful no-go theorem.

## Proposition 6.1 — positive local-tail obstruction

Fix a degree `d`. Let

```text
P_n(X)=p_(n,0)+p_(n,1)X+...+p_(n,d)X^d
```

have coefficients rational in `n`, with subexponential numerator and denominator growth. Suppose, for all sufficiently large `n`,

```text
P_n(n+1+m) >= 0   for every m>=0,
```

and `P_n` is not identically zero. Assume the weighted series converges. Then

```text
S_n = Sum_(m>=0) P_n(n+1+m) A_(n,m)
```

has nth-root rate 16, provided the rational coefficients do not have an exponentially small common factor.

### Proof

For each `n`, a nonzero degree-`d` polynomial cannot vanish at all of

```text
n+1, n+2, ..., n+d+1.
```

Thus some `m_n<=d` has `P_n(n+1+m_n)>0`. Passing to a subsequence fixes `m_n=m`. Both

```text
P_n(n+1+m)
```

and

```text
A_(n,m)/A_(n,0)
```

are rational functions of `n` up to a fixed nonzero rational factor, so their nth roots tend to 1. This gives the lower exponential rate 16. The upper rate follows from (3.1), convergence, and a polynomial bound on `P_n(n+1+m)`.

The same conclusion holds if the first finitely many tail terms are explicitly removed: every fixed shift `A_(n,s)` still has nth-root rate 16.

### Consequence

No fixed-degree polynomial-in-theta operation can turn the tangent tail into a **nonzero positive remainder of rate `mu`**. A claimed positive formula must be rejected immediately unless its coefficient support or differential order grows with `n`, or unless it contains an exact signed cancellation of the complete rate-16 boundary contribution.

# 7. Gosper telescoping does not rescue a positive remainder

Suppose a rational certificate gives

```text
P_n(m) A_(n,m)
 = [G_n(m+1)A_(n,m+1)-G_n(m)A_(n,m)] + W_(n,m),     (7.1)
```

where `G_n(m)` has polynomial growth and `W_(n,m)>=0` is summable.
After summing over `m`, the upper certificate boundary vanishes and

```text
Sum P_n(m)A_(n,m)
 = -G_n(0)A_(n,0) + Sum W_(n,m).                    (7.2)
```

There are only three possibilities.

1. `G_n(0)` is nonzero with subexponential size: its boundary term has rate 16.
2. The final displayed proof discards/cancels the boundary and retains a nonzero positive sum `Sum W_(n,m)`: Proposition 6.1 gives rate 16.
3. Two rate-16 quantities cancel exactly. This is no longer a proof from positivity; it is precisely the missing connection-coefficient identity.

Thus a Gosper certificate may reorganize the tangent, but it cannot by itself produce a genuinely positive `mu`-remainder.

# 9. The singular contiguous series diverges at `z=1`

Let `c_(n,k)` denote the coefficient of the shifted series on the right of (8.4). After the eventually fixed sign is removed, its ratio is

```text
c_(n,k+1)/c_(n,k)
 = (k+n+4)^2(k-n-1/2)
   /[(k+7/2)^2(k+4)].                               (9.1)
```

The exact Raabe coefficient is

```text
lim_(k->infinity)
 k(1-c_(n,k+1)/c_(n,k)) = 7/2 - n.                 (9.2)
```

Equivalently, the hypergeometric excess at 1 is

```text
(7/2+7/2+4) - [(n+4)+(n+4)+(-n-1/2)+1]
 = 5/2 - n.
```

Therefore the terms are of order

```text
k^(n-7/2),
```

and the series converges only for `n<=2`. For every `n>=3` it diverges.

This can be made completely elementary without invoking the general `pFq(1)` theorem:

- for `n>=4`, the ratio in (9.1) is eventually greater than 1, so the terms do not even tend to zero;
- for `n=3`, after writing `k=n+1+m`, one checks that for every `m>=65`

  ```text
  c_(3,k+1)/c_(3,k) >= (k+1)/(k+2),
  ```

  because the cleared numerator after `m=65+h` is

  ```text
  7402 + 10825h + 293h^2 + 2h^3 > 0.
  ```

  Hence the terms dominate a positive constant times `1/k`, and the series diverges.

The left side of (8.4) has the same problem. Since `A_(n,m)` is of order `m^(-9/2)`, multiplication by the degree-`n+1` polynomial `P_n(n+1+m)` produces terms of order `m^(n-7/2)`.

Thus (8.4) is valid as a power-series identity for `|z|<1`, but for `n>=3` neither side can be evaluated at `z=1` by termwise summation.

## Consequence

Any use of (8.4) at `z=1` must introduce

```text
FP_(z->1-) 4F3(...,1;...;z),
```

or an equivalent Abel/Hadamard subtraction of its singular expansion. That finite part is not a positive convergent tail. It is exactly the sort of global connection object that was absent from the proposed local argument.

Applying the upper-contiguous relation only once or twice avoids divergence, but it also avoids the singular zero for all sufficiently large `n`: `(-n)_r` is nonzero for fixed `r<=n`. Such fixed iterations merely replace one tangent by another and retain the rate-16 tail.

# 10. Universal rational infinitesimal gauge: exact obstruction

A universal correction

```text
J_n = R_n + B_n Q_n
```

that works for every solution row would require

```text
A_n' + A_n Q_(n+1) = Q_n A_n.                     (10.1)
```

Multiplying by `A_n^(-1)` and taking traces gives

```text
tr Q_(n+1)-tr Q_n
 = -tr(A_n^(-1)A_n')
 = -[4/(2n+7)+1/(n+4)-2/(n+1)].                   (10.2)
```

Rewrite the right side as

```text
-2/(n+7/2) - 1/(n+4) + 2/(n+1).                  (10.3)
```

## Discrete-residue proof of nonsummability in `Q(n)`

For a rational function `q(n)`, the sum of the simple-pole residues of

```text
q(n+1)-q(n)
```

inside any orbit `alpha+Z` is zero. This is immediate from partial fractions: shifting `n` only moves each pole one step inside the orbit, so all internal residues cancel and the finite orbit support has opposite endpoint residues.

For (10.3):

- the half-integer orbit `-7/2+Z` has total residue `-2`;
- the integer orbit has residues `-1` at `-4` and `+2` at `-1`, of total `+1`.

Both violate the zero-orbit-sum condition. Therefore no rational function can equal `tr Q_n`, and no matrix `Q_n in Mat_3(Q(n))` solves (10.1).

This argument uses the exact raw determinant. Replacing it by an arbitrary scalar from a different challenge gauge is invalid.

## What the obstruction does and does not prove

It rules out a **universal rational matrix splitting**. It does not rule out:

- a correction valid only along the particular row `B_n`;
- a matrix whose entries contain harmonic sums;
- an Abel finite part or another global analytic branch.

The trace already identifies the minimum coefficient-field extension. A discrete primitive is

```text
h_n = Sum_(j=0)^(n-1)
      [-4/(2j+7)-1/(j+4)+2/(j+1)],                (10.4)
```

so `h_(n+1)-h_n` is (10.2). This is a rational-valued combination of ordinary and odd harmonic sums. Its asymptotics contain the expected `log n` and `log 2`. Thus the appearance of a logarithmic finite part is structurally forced, not an artifact of the Meijer notation.

3. a correction in a difference field containing harmonic sums;
4. a second resonant Frobenius branch or an Euler transform with a new global integration variable.

All four descriptions are manifestations of the same missing connection object.

# 13. The smallest additional analytic object

The tangent system is the nontrivial extension

```text
[B_(n+1)  R_(n+1)]
 = [B_n  R_n]
   [[A_n, A_n'],
    [  0, A_n ]]
```

(up to the chosen block ordering). A homogeneous `J_n=R_n+B_nQ_n` is a splitting of this extension.

The trace obstruction proves that the extension does not split over `Q(n)`. At minimum one must adjoin the indefinite sum (10.4), or an equivalent odd-harmonic/digamma sequence. This is the **smallest algebraic extra object** forced by the determinant.

That algebraic extension is still insufficient to prove recessiveness. To select the `mu` branch without Miller/Levinson, one needs one additional global analytic object:

> a resonant logarithmic/finite-part companion `L_n(z)` whose theta jet at `z=1` satisfies the homogeneous `A_n(0)` trajectory, together with an exact sign-definite Euler/Laplace representation of its scalar component.

The representation must exhibit a phase `Phi` with

```text
sup Phi = log(mu),
```

and must include the exact subtraction that cancels the rate-16 boundary family. A mere positive sum of the original `A_(n,m)` cannot do this.

A concrete next target, stated without Meijer notation, is:

```text
construct L_n(z) as the Abel finite part of the second resonant
hypergeometric branch at z=1;
prove coefficientwise/contiguously that
    jet(L_(n+1)) = jet(L_n) A_n(0);
reduce jet(L_0) modulo B_0 and verify it is kappa;
derive a positive Euler-type integral for L_n(1) after the singular
counterterms have canceled;
maximize its phase exactly and obtain mu.
```

The first two lines are algebraic/finite-part work. The last two lines are the irreducible analytic connection step. Without such an object, the tangent data do not determine the recessive homogeneous line.

# 14. False implications to avoid

1. **Positive tail implies decay.** False: the exact rate is 16.
2. **Killing the first tail term changes the exponential rate.** False: every fixed shifted term `A_(n,s)` has rate 16.
3. **A polynomial theta weight can expose `mu` while remaining positive.** False under fixed degree, by Proposition 6.1.
4. **The singular contiguous identity can be evaluated at 1 termwise.** False for `n>=3`.
5. **No rational matrix gauge implies no trajectory-specific correction.** False; the trace argument is only a universal-gauge obstruction.
6. **The existence of a harmonic antidifference proves the desired row is recessive.** False; it only removes the first algebraic obstruction.
7. **The positive tail's rate proves the whole `R_n` has rate 16.** Not by itself: the terminating head can cancel it. What is proved is that any final one-sign remainder built locally from this tail has rate 16.
