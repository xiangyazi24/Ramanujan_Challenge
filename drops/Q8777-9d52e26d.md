# Moving-modulus large sieve for Apéry zero hits

## Executive summary

The proposed large-sieve formulation is the correct analytic object, but a classical Bombieri--Vinogradov argument does not immediately close the problem. The obstruction is that the target sets $Z_p$ are not fixed residue classes and are not currently known to arise from bounded-conductor trace functions in the required sense.

The available route is:

1. exploit the sparsity $|Z_p|=O(1)$;
2. obtain average (over $n$) bounds by a large sieve;
3. upgrade to pointwise only with additional arithmetic input on the Apéry zero sets.

At present the missing ingredient is a uniform cross-prime decorrelation theorem.

## 1. Reformulation as a moving-modulus problem

We have

$$S(n)=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}1_{Z_p}(n\bmod p)
=\sum_p\sum_{d\in Z_p}1_{n\equiv d\pmod p}.$$

For fixed $p$, this is a union of $|Z_p|$ arithmetic progressions. Since $|Z_p|$ is typically 0 or 2, the expected contribution of one prime is about

$$\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\approx \frac1p.$$

The sum of expectations over $p>\sqrt n$ is therefore only

$$\sum_{p>\sqrt n}\frac1p\asymp O(1).
$$

This gives the probabilistic heuristic that $S(n)$ should remain bounded.

The difficulty is proving that the exceptional clustering does not occur.

## 2. Why classical Bombieri--Vinogradov does not directly apply

Bombieri--Vinogradov studies

$$\sum_{q\le Q}\max_a\left|\pi(x;q,a)-\frac{\operatorname{li}(x)}{\phi(q)}\right|.$$

The modulus varies, but the residue classes are fixed independently of the modulus.

Here the situation is reversed:

- the integer $n$ is fixed;
- the modulus $p$ varies;
- the admissible residues $Z_p$ are themselves generated by the Apéry recurrence modulo $p$.

Thus the problem is closer to a "sieve with moving targets" than to ordinary BV.

## 3. Large sieve second moment

The dual large sieve heuristic gives

$$
\sum_{n\le N}|S(n)|^2
\ll (N+P^2)\sum_{p\le P}\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p}.
$$

If $|Z_p|\ll1$, then

$$
\sum_{p\le P}\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p}\ll\log\log P,
$$

and hence

$$
\sum_{n\le N}|S(n)|^2\ll (N+P^2)\log\log P.
$$

For $P=\sqrt N$ this gives

$$
\sum_{n\le N}|S(n)|^2\ll N\log\log N.
$$

Therefore for almost all $n$,

$$S(n)\ll (\log N)^{1/2+o(1)}.
$$

This is a genuine intermediate result, but it does not imply the required pointwise bound.

## 4. Why sparsity alone is insufficient

A sparse family $Z_p$ can still be adversarial. For example, if $Z_p$ contained a residue close to $n$ for many primes dividing a structured sequence, the pointwise sum could be large while preserving $|Z_p|=1$.

The missing theorem would be something like:

$$
\#\{p\le X:n\bmod p\in Z_p\}=o(X/\log X)
$$

uniformly in $n$.

This requires understanding how the Apéry recurrence produces $Z_p$.

## 5. Character-sum approach

Writing

$$1_{Z_p}(d)=1_{b_d=0\pmod p},$$

one would like an expansion in additive characters. However the indicator of a zero is nonlinear:

$$1_{b_d=0}=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(ub_d).$$

The crucial estimate would be

$$
\sum_{d<p}e_p(ub_d)\ll p^{1/2}
$$

uniformly for $u\ne0$.

This is not presently known. Although $b_d$ arises from a Picard--Fuchs motive, the variable $d$ is an index of a recurrence, not a geometric parameter on which the usual Deligne theorem directly applies.

The sequence is a trace sequence, but the additive phase $e_p(ub_d)$ is a nonlinear exponential twist of trace values.

## 6. Relation to trace-function technology

FKM-type results require a function

$$t\mapsto K(t)$$

coming from a bounded-conductor lisse sheaf. Then correlation sums such as

$$\sum_x K(x)\overline{K(x+h)}$$

can be bounded.

The Apéry object differs:

$$d\mapsto1_{b_d=0}
$$

is not known to be a trace function of bounded conductor.

A possible future route is to construct a sheaf whose Frobenius trace detects the zero locus, but this is a new geometric problem.

## 7. Pointwise versus average consequences

The large sieve gives:

- density-one control of $S(n)$;
- moment estimates;
- likely Poisson-type behavior.

It does not give:

$$S(n)\le C$$

for every $n$.

To obtain LGTQ-3, one needs a stronger statement controlling correlations of several independent prime hits.

## 8. Relevant literature connections

The closest frameworks are:

- Bombieri--Vinogradov: average distribution over moduli;
- Halberstam--Richert sieve methods: sparse sets of divisibility conditions;
- Hooley-style square sieve arguments;
- Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel: correlations of genuine trace functions.

The Apéry problem sits between these areas: the modulus aspect is classical, but the moving target set is not.

## Conclusion

A moving-modulus large sieve is a plausible intermediate step:

$$
\sum_{n\le N}S(n)^2\ll N\log\log N
$$

is within reach under the empirical sparsity hypothesis.

However, closing LGTQ-3 requires a new input: a theorem that the Apéry zero sets $Z_p$ behave independently across primes. Classical BV, large sieve, and FKM trace-function machinery do not by themselves provide this because $1_{Z_p}$ is not yet a bounded-conductor trace function.

The key missing bridge is an Apéry-specific trace-function or monodromy theorem for the zero indicator.
