# Q7202: Can two short endpoint returns compress the long Apéry return?

## Conclusion

No. The universal 2x2 transfer/Casoratian identities do not compress the long continuant \(N_G\) to bounded data depending only on the two short endpoint returns \(s,t\). The exact algebra reduces the three-return system to an aligned condition, but the remaining bridge is a genuine length-\(G\) matrix word. Any further reduction would require an additional arithmetic symmetry of the specific Apéry recurrence, not a consequence of the transfer algebra.

The precise obstruction is that fixing the two endpoint returns fixes only two scalar conditions on the projective states at the two endpoints. The middle transfer matrix has \(G\)-dependent degrees of freedom, and the Apéry transfer word is non-autonomous.

## 1. Transfer formulation

Write

\[
M_u=\begin{pmatrix}0&(u+1)^6\\-1&P(u)\end{pmatrix}
\]

and

\[
T_G(z)=M_{z+G-1}\cdots M_z.
\]

The established continuant identity gives

\[
T_G(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-(z+G)^6N_{G-2}(z+1)&(z+G)^6N_{G-1}(z)\\
-N_{G-1}(z+1)&N_G(z)
\end{pmatrix}
\]
(up to the fixed index convention used for \(N_h\)).

Therefore

\[
N_G(z)=0
\]
means that the lower-right entry of the bridge transfer vanishes. Equivalently the bridge sends the distinguished restart vector to the opposite projective point.

## 2. What the two endpoint short returns imply

A separated chain gives

\[
N_s(x)=0,
\qquad N_G(x+s)=0,
\qquad N_t(x+s+G)=0.
\]

The first and third equations say that the projective orbit returns after the two short windows:

\[
\pi_x=\pi_{x+s},
\qquad
\pi_{x+s+G}=\pi_{x+s+G+t}.
\]

The addition identity gives the only universal compression:

\[
N_{s+G}(x)=0
\Longleftrightarrow
N_G(x+s)N_{s+1}(x)=0.
\]

The adjacent Casoratian excludes

\[
N_{s+1}(x)=0,
\]

so this becomes exactly

\[
N_{s+G}(x)=0\Longleftrightarrow N_G(x+s)=0.
\]

This removes the first gap triple but leaves the full bridge length \(G\).

Applying the same identity at the right endpoint only rewrites the condition; it does not lower the degree of \(N_G\).

## 3. Why no universal elimination exists

Suppose there were a universal polynomial

\[
F_{s,t}(x)=0
\]

of degree bounded independently of \(G\), equivalent to

\[
N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=N_t(x+s+G)=0.
\]

Then for every \(G\), after imposing the two endpoint constraints, the middle transfer would have bounded algebraic complexity.

But the transfer word

\[
M_{x+s+G-1}\cdots M_{x+s}
\]

contains \(G\) independent shifted coefficients. Its bottom-right entry has

\[
\deg_x N_G=3(G-1).
\]

The endpoint equations have degrees

\[
\deg N_s=3(s-1),\qquad \deg N_t=3(t-1).
\]

Their total algebraic complexity is \(O(s+t)\), while the bridge carries \(3G+O(1)\) degree. Since \(G\) is unrestricted up to \(p\), no identity in the 2x2 transfer algebra can remove these growing degrees.

This is a degrees-of-freedom obstruction: the endpoint data specify two codimension-one conditions in the projective orbit, whereas the bridge word is a separate non-autonomous matrix product with length \(G\).

## 4. Casoratian does not add a hidden relation

The Casoratian is

\[
W_n=b_nc_{n-1}-b_{n-1}c_n=-1/n^3.
\]

It proves invertibility of every regular transfer step and gives adjacent coprimality:

\[
\gcd(N_h,N_{h+1})=1.
\]

It prevents the simultaneous vanishing needed for a fake degree collapse. It does not express \(N_G\) in terms of \(N_s,N_t\).

## 5. Apéry reflection symmetry audit

The recurrence has the reflection identity

\[
U_m(-z-m)=(-1)^mU_m(z),
\]

which yields root pairing and the forced center root for odd \(m\).

This only factors the known reflection component. It does not create periodicity in the index because

\[
P(z+m)\neq P(z).
\]

Hence the bridge transfer is not a power of a fixed matrix and cannot be reduced by Cayley-Hamilton. A constant-matrix recurrence would have a finite-dimensional state algebra; the Apéry recurrence has a growing coefficient field.

## 6. What the second short return can and cannot do

Using both endpoint returns gives the aligned triple system

\[
N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=N_t(x+s+G)=0.
\]

This is valuable for a three-polynomial aligned sieve. It can reduce counting by correlation estimates. However, algebraically it does not replace the bridge polynomial by bounded endpoint data.

The best universal statement is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{two short endpoint returns give alignment, not degree compression.}
}
\]

## 7. Implication for P32 strategy

Any proof of the far-bridge estimate must use arithmetic information beyond transfer identities:

* a conditioned projective mixing theorem;
* a long-return large sieve with degree independent of \(G\);
* an Apéry-specific monodromy/Galois input;
* or an average correlation estimate over primes.

A generic resultant argument necessarily pays degree \(O(G)\). The 2x2 identities alone cannot avoid that cost.

Thus the remaining P32 obstruction is genuinely arithmetic rather than an algebraic identity waiting to be discovered.
