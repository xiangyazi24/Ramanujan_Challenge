# Verdict

**No — not as a nonvacuous algebraic consequence of the identities stated in Q2267.**

The endpoint equations and the one-sided border equation are **local value/jet conditions** for the diagonal Hasse polynomial.  The Racah condition

\[
K_p(\lambda_r,\lambda_s)=0
\]

is a **cross-endpoint collinearity condition** on the two terminal Newton vectors.  The gap-continuant equation selects the scalar Dirichlet return of the Apéry recurrence, but it does not identify the two terminal Newton directions.  The missing datum is exactly the right-boundary/diagonal-square term isolated in Q2257 and retained in the exact Q2260 recoupling identity.

Thus the displayed hypotheses do not yield an identity forcing `K=0`.  For a fixed `(p,h)` the implication could still be vacuously true if the common endpoint/gap/border locus is empty; indeed the current scan saw no border-zero selected pair.  That is different from an algebraic derivation of `K=0`.

There is presently **no concrete prime triple** `(p,r,s)` with

\[
A_p(\lambda_r)=A_p(\lambda_s)=S_h(r)=O_{h-1}(r+1)=0,
\qquad K_p(\lambda_r,\lambda_s)\ne0,
\]

because among the 2,295 scanned nonmate zero pairs for `p <= 200000`, the antecedent `O=0` never occurred.  The exact counterexample requested by the question is therefore the **structural obstruction** below, not an observed Hasse triple.

# 1. The precise Christoffel–Darboux condition

Put

\[
v(T):=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Psi_{M+1}(T)\\
\Psi_M(T)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad p=2M+1.
\]

The Newton/Racah Christoffel–Darboux identity used in Q2259 is

\[
K_p(T,U)
 =c_M\,
 \frac{\det\bigl(v(T),v(U)\bigr)}{T-U},
\qquad c_M\in\mathbb F_p^\times .                         \tag{1.1}
\]

For a nonmate pair, `lambda_r != lambda_s`, so

\[
\boxed{
K_p(\lambda_r,\lambda_s)=0
\iff
C_{r,s}:=\det\bigl(v(\lambda_r),v(\lambda_s)\bigr)=0.}  \tag{1.2}
\]

On the chart where `Psi_M(lambda_r) Psi_M(lambda_s) != 0`, define the terminal ratio

\[
R(T):=\frac{\Psi_{M+1}(T)}{\Psi_M(T)}.
\]

Then (1.1) becomes

\[
K_p(T,U)
=c_M\Psi_M(T)\Psi_M(U)
\frac{R(T)-R(U)}{T-U},                                  \tag{1.3}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
K_p(\lambda_r,\lambda_s)=0
\iff R(\lambda_r)=R(\lambda_s).}                        \tag{1.4}
\]

This is the terminal-ratio equality identified in Q2259/Q2266.

# 2. What the endpoint Hasse zeros actually say

Taking the confluent limit `U -> T` in (1.1) gives

\[
A_p(T)=K_p(T,T)
=c_M\det\bigl(v'(T),v(T)\bigr).                         \tag{2.1}
\]

On the ratio chart this is

\[
A_p(T)=c_M\Psi_M(T)^2R'(T).                             \tag{2.2}
\]

Consequently the two endpoint zeros give only

\[
R'(\lambda_r)=0,
\qquad
R'(\lambda_s)=0,                                        \tag{2.3}
\]

or, without dividing by `Psi_M`,

\[
\det(v'_r,v_r)=0,
\qquad
\det(v'_s,v_s)=0,                                       \tag{2.4}
\]

where `v_n=v(lambda_n)`.

Thus each endpoint terminal vector is tangent to its own projective direction:

\[
v'_r\parallel v_r,
\qquad
v'_s\parallel v_s                                      \tag{2.5}
\]

when the vectors are nonzero.  Nothing in (2.3)–(2.5) says that the **two different directions** are equal.  But equality of those directions is exactly (1.2).

Equivalently:

```text
endpoint Hasse zero at r:       the terminal ratio is stationary at lambda_r
endpoint Hasse zero at s:       the terminal ratio is stationary at lambda_s
Racah-kernel zero at (r,s):     the two stationary values are equal
```

Two critical points need not have the same critical value.

Differentiating (2.1) gives

\[
A_p'(T)=c_M\det\bigl(v''(T),v(T)\bigr),                 \tag{2.6}
\]

and differentiating (2.2) gives

\[
A_p'(T)
=c_M\bigl(2\Psi_M\Psi_M'R'+\Psi_M^2R''\bigr).          \tag{2.7}
\]

At a Hasse zero on the ratio chart this reduces to

\[
A_p'(T)=c_M\Psi_M(T)^2R''(T).                           \tag{2.8}
\]

Therefore first-jet data of `A_p` are still local curvature data for `R`; they do not determine the difference

\[
R(\lambda_r)-R(\lambda_s).                              \tag{2.9}
\]

# 3. What formula (3.5) contributes

After the endpoint value terms in the spectral Hermite convolution vanish, write formula (3.5) exactly in the form

\[
O_{h-1}(r+1)
=\varepsilon_{p,r,h}\,
L_{r,h}(\mathbf d),
\qquad
L_{r,h}(\mathbf d)
:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal P_{r,h}}
\alpha_j(r,h)d_j,                                      \tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
d_j=A_p'(\lambda_j),                                   \tag{3.2}
\]

`epsilon_(p,r,h)` is the explicit admissible unit, and the coefficients `alpha_j` are exactly the Cauchy/Hermite coefficients displayed in (3.5), containing the factors

\[
(j+r+1)^{-1},
\qquad
(j+\tfrac12)^{-1}.                                     \tag{3.3}
\]

Thus border vanishing supplies one homogeneous scalar equation

\[
L_{r,h}(\mathbf d)=0.                                   \tag{3.4}
\]

Using (2.6), it is one relation among the local determinants

\[
\det(v''(\lambda_j),v(\lambda_j)).                      \tag{3.5}
\]

It is not the cross determinant

\[
\det(v(\lambda_r),v(\lambda_s)).                        \tag{3.6}
\]

The exact values of the Cauchy weights do not change this distinction.  To deduce (3.6) from (3.4), one would need an additional, family-specific syzygy proving that the off-diagonal Casoratian lies in the row span generated by the Hermite convolution and the endpoint/gap equations.  No such syzygy is contained in (3.5).

# 4. Why the gap-continuant equation does not close the gap

The equation

\[
S_h(r)=0                                                \tag{4.1}
\]

is the scalar return condition for the length-`h` Apéry transfer.  On a genuine recurrence orbit with `b_r=b_s=0` and a nonzero edge amplitude, it is the condition that the Dirichlet Jacobi block have a kernel.  It constrains the diagonal samples

\[
A_p(\lambda_n)=u_pb_n,                                  \tag{4.2}
\]

hence, through (2.2), the derivative samples of the terminal ratio.  It does not constrain the additive/projective level of `R` at the two endpoints.

This is the same canonicality obstruction recorded in the fraction-free marked-exclusion work: a local Jacobi block has a Dirichlet kernel at every gap root, while identifying that kernel with the global canonical Apéry/Hasse solution is a separate coordinate.

So the three kinds of equations live at different levels:

```text
S_h(r)=0:                         scalar recurrence return / local Dirichlet kernel
A_p(lambda_r)=A_p(lambda_s)=0:   local first-order terminal normality
O_(h-1)(r+1)=0:                  one local second-jet convolution
K_p(lambda_r,lambda_s)=0:        equality of two terminal projective directions
```

The first three do not supply the last equation.

# 5. Exact structural countermodel in the universal jet algebra

This gives a literal algebraic obstruction, not merely a dimension heuristic.

Work in the coordinate ring generated by the terminal vectors and the local jets occurring in (3.1), localized at the admissibility units.  Let `I_jet` be generated by

\[
\det(v'_r,v_r),
\quad
\det(v'_s,v_s),
\quad
S_h(r),
\quad
\sum_j\alpha_j\det(v''_j,v_j).                         \tag{5.1}
\]

Define an evaluation of this coordinate ring by

\[
v_r=\binom10,
\qquad
v_s=\binom01,
\qquad
v'_r=v'_s=0,                                            \tag{5.2}
\]

and, for every node occurring in the Hermite convolution,

\[
v''_j=0,                                                \tag{5.3}
\]

while assigning the scalar gap coordinate `S_h(r)` the value zero.

Every generator in (5.1) then vanishes.  However,

\[
C_{r,s}=\det(v_r,v_s)=1.                                \tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{C_{r,s}\notin\sqrt{I_{\rm jet}}.}               \tag{5.5}
\]

Since `(lambda_r-lambda_s)c_M` is a unit on the nonmate admissible locus, (1.1) gives

\[
K_p(\lambda_r,\lambda_s)\ne0                            \tag{5.6}
\]

in this exact structural model.

The same obstruction on the ratio chart is even simpler.  Let

\[
\rho_r=R(\lambda_r),
\quad
\rho_s=R(\lambda_s).
\]

The hypotheses control derivatives and local curvature variables but not `rho_r-rho_s`.  The specialization

\[
\rho_r=0,
\quad
\rho_s=1,
\quad
R'(\lambda_r)=R'(\lambda_s)=0,
\quad
d_j=0,
\quad
S_h(r)=0                                                 \tag{5.7}
\]

annihilates every stated hypothesis and leaves `rho_r-rho_s=-1`.

This is the smallest structural obstruction: **two independent terminal directions can each satisfy every local Hermite-normal equation.**  It does not claim that (5.2) is a new concrete Hasse triple; it proves that no derivation using only the displayed endpoint, gap, and formula-(3.5) equations can establish the cross determinant.

# 8. The one-variable ideal formulation

To interpret “inside `F_p[T]`” precisely, fix `p,h`, put `s=Z+h`, and substitute the canonical nodes:

\[
\kappa_{p,h}(Z)
:=K_p(\lambda_Z,\lambda_{Z+h})\in\mathbb F_p[Z]         \tag{8.1}
\]

after clearing the admissible denominators.  Let

\[
I_{p,h}:=
\bigl(
A_p(\lambda_Z),
A_p(\lambda_{Z+h}),
S_h(Z),
O_{h-1}(Z+1)
\bigr).                                                  \tag{8.2}
\]

A genuine algebraic forcing theorem over the algebraic closure would require

\[
\kappa_{p,h}\in\sqrt{I_{p,h}}                           \tag{8.3}
\]

in the localized ring.  Equivalently, one would have to exhibit some `N >= 1` and polynomial coefficients `Q_i` such that

\[
\kappa_{p,h}(Z)^N
=Q_1A_p(\lambda_Z)
 +Q_2A_p(\lambda_{Z+h})
 +Q_3S_h(Z)
 +Q_4O_{h-1}(Z+1).                                      \tag{8.4}
\]

For an assertion only about `F_p`-rational `Z`, one adds `Z^p-Z` to the ideal.

Neither formula (3.5), the gap relation, nor the endpoint Hasse identities supplies (8.4).  The universal jet specialization in Section 5 shows why such membership is not a formal consequence of those equations, and Sections 6–7 identify the exact row-level residual that a family-specific proof would still have to eliminate.

# 9. What additional input would make the implication valid

Any one of the following genuinely new statements would close the implication:

1. **Right-boundary vanishing on the marked locus**

   \[
   \langle\mathcal B_R,B\rangle=0
   \quad\text{whenever}\quad
   A_r=A_s=S_h=O=0.                                     \tag{9.1}
   \]

2. **Absorption of the diagonal-square residual** by an explicit row identity such as (7.5).

3. **Direct terminal-ratio rigidity**

   \[
   \Psi_{M+1}(\lambda_r)\Psi_M(\lambda_s)
   -\Psi_M(\lambda_r)\Psi_{M+1}(\lambda_s)=0            \tag{9.2}
   \]

   deduced from an additional global Hasse/square-lift/canonicality theorem.

4. **Explicit ideal membership** of the form (8.4), after all pole, central, and mate factors are saturated.

Without one of these inputs, the exact conclusion from border vanishing is (6.3), not `K=0`.
