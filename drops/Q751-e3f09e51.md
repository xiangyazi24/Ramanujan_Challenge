# Central binomial carrier: exact p-adic quotient and saturation audit

## 1. Setup

Let
\[
n=p+r,\qquad p>n/2,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]
Then \(n\) is in the q=1 range. Put
\[
C_n=\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
\]
The known Kummer fact is
\[
v_p(C_n)=1.
\]
The relevant question is whether the normalized carrier
\[
C_n/p \pmod p
\]
contains a second independent condition when combined with
\[
b_{p+r}=5b_r+10pD_r\pmod {p^2}.
\]

## 2. Explicit central-binomial quotient

### Even case

Let \(n=2m\). Since \(p+r=2m\), one has \(p\) odd and therefore \(r\) odd. Write
\[
r=2s+1,\qquad m=(p+r)/2=(p-1)/2+s+1.
\]
Using
\[
\binom{p+r}{(p+r)/2}=\frac{(p+r)!}{((p+r)/2)!^2}
\]
and separating the unique factor of p from the numerator factorial, Wilson gives
\[
\frac1p\binom{p+r}{(p+r)/2}
\equiv
(-1)^{(p-1)/2-s-1}
\binom{r}{s+1}
\left(1+pH_{r}\right)
\pmod p.
\]
Only the reduction modulo p matters, hence
\[
\boxed{\frac1p\binom{p+r}{(p+r)/2}\equiv
(-1)^{(p-1)/2-(r+1)/2}\binom r{(r+1)/2}\pmod p.}
\]

### Odd case

Let \(n=2m+1\). Then
\[
\lfloor n/2\rfloor=m,
\]
and the same factorial separation gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac1p\binom{p+r}{(p+r-1)/2}
\equiv
(-1)^{(p-1)/2-r/2}\binom r{r/2}\pmod p
}
\]
where now \(r\) is even.

Equivalently, in both parity cases:

\[
\frac{C_n}{p}\equiv \pm \binom r{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}\pmod p.
\]

Thus the central carrier is not an independent Apéry object: after removing the forced p, it collapses to the central binomial coefficient of the Lucas residue r.

## 3. Combine with the mod-p^2 Apéry expansion

At a target prime
\[
p\mid b_r,
\]
we have
\[
b_{p+r}\equiv10pD_r\pmod {p^2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{b_{p+r}}p\equiv10D_r\pmod p.
\]
The natural Bezout/determinant attempt is
\[
\Delta_r=
\begin{vmatrix}
C_{p+r}/p & b_{p+r}/p\\
C_r & 10D_r
\end{vmatrix}.
\]
Modulo p the second column is already determined by \(D_r\), while the first column is
\[
\pm C_r.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Delta_r\equiv
\pm(C_rD_r-C_rD_r)=0\pmod p.
\]
The determinant gives no new selective congruence.

## 4. Saturation mechanism

The precise saturation factor is
\[
\boxed{\binom r{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}.}
\]
The normalized central carrier is congruent to this quantity modulo p. Since the Apéry-Lucas expansion also reduces all information to the same residue parameter r, the two columns share the same Lucas shadow.

Any integer combination of the form
\[
A(r)\frac{C_{p+r}}p+B(r)\frac{b_{p+r}}p
\]
inherits this common factor modulo p unless an additional invariant independent of r is introduced.

## 5. Consequence for the gcd strategy

The central-binomial carrier does prove that q=1 primes divide a controlled integer, but the second-order normalization does not create a new small-height carrier. The obstruction is not factorial height; it is exact residue saturation.

The remaining missing ingredient cannot be obtained from this carrier alone. One needs either:

1. a genuinely independent Apéry derivative congruence involving \(D_r\) not multiplied by the same central binomial residue, or
2. a global cancellation statement over the surviving r values.

Therefore the q=1 second-order central carrier saturates at the common Lucas factor
\[
\binom r{\lfloor r/2\rfloor},
\]
and does not by itself yield a logarithmic-height \(o(n)\) aggregate.
