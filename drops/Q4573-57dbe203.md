# 5. Insertion-ready no-go: determinant-to-continuant elimination

The following is the strongest new rigorously scoped negative result I can prove.  It rules out a natural coefficient-sensitive continuant attack, but deliberately does **not** rule out a genuinely four-field horizontal resultant or trace theorem constructed before reduction.

```tex
\begin{lemma}[Only affine index symmetries of the Ap\'ery singular set]
\label{lem:ad-affine-index-symmetry}
Let $\phi(x)=ax+b$ with $a\ne0$ over a field of characteristic different
from $2$.  If $\phi$ permutes the two singular indices $\{0,-1\}$ of the
Ap\'ery recurrence, then
\[
 \phi(x)=x\qquad\text{or}\qquad\phi(x)=-x-1.
\]
For the middle coefficient
\[
 P(x)=34x^3+51x^2+27x+5
\]
one has
\[
 P(-x-1)=-P(x),
\]
so the second map is exactly the usual reflection symmetry.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\phi$ permutes $\{0,-1\}$, the unordered pair
\[
 \{b,-a+b\}
\]
must equal $\{0,-1\}$.  If $b=0$, then $-a=-1$, hence $a=1$.
If $b=-1$, then $-a-1=0$, hence $a=-1$.  These are the only two
possibilities.  Direct expansion gives $P(-x-1)=-P(x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Four-distinct determinant decoupling]
\label{prop:ad-four-distinct-decoupling}
Let
\[
 P=pq,\qquad P'=p'q'
\]
with $p,q,p',q'$ pairwise distinct primes, and let
\[
 \Delta=tP'-t'P.
\]
For every $\ell\mid P$, reduction of the determinant equation modulo
$\ell$ has coordinate ring
\[
 \mathbb F_\ell[t,t']/(P't-Pt'-\Delta)
 \simeq \mathbb F_\ell[t'],
\]
with
\[
 t=\Delta(P')^{-1}\pmod\ell.
\tag{DC1}
\]
In particular its elimination ideal in the opposite-row variable is zero:
\[
 (P't-Pt'-\Delta)\cap\mathbb F_\ell[t']=(0).
\tag{DC2}
\]
Likewise, for every $\ell\mid P'$ one has
\[
 \mathbb F_\ell[t,t']/(P't-Pt'-\Delta)
 \simeq \mathbb F_\ell[t],
\]
with
\[
 t'=-\Delta P^{-1}\pmod\ell,
\tag{DC3}
\]
and the elimination ideal in $t$ is zero.

Consequently, in the all-distinct stratum the determinant relation by
itself creates exactly one local row label in each defining characteristic.
No procedure consisting only of

\begin{enumerate}
 \item reducing the determinant relation in one defining characteristic,
 \item transporting indices by an affine symmetry of the Ap\'ery recurrence,
       and
 \item applying the single-characteristic gap-polynomial/continuant or
       aligned-resultant identities,
\end{enumerate}

can manufacture a second independent return from the opposite TT* row.
The only affine transport available in step~2 is identity or reflection.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\ell\mid P$.  Since the four primes are distinct,
$P'$ is a unit modulo $\ell$, whereas $P=0$ modulo $\ell$.  Hence the
single determinant equation becomes
\[
 P't-\Delta=0,
\]
which uniquely fixes $t$ and imposes no equation on $t'$.  Solving for
$t$ gives the displayed coordinate-ring isomorphism and (DC2).

If $\ell\mid P'$, then $P$ is a unit and $P'=0$, so the equation becomes
\[
 -Pt'-\Delta=0.
\]
This fixes $t'$ and leaves $t$ free, proving (DC3) and its elimination
statement.

The source continuant certificates are same-characteristic multiple-return
certificates: a nontrivial gap polynomial $N_h(x)$ is produced when two
actual returns $x,x+h$ occur in the same defining field, and the aligned
resultants similarly retain common-root labels inside one characteristic.
The determinant reductions above supply only one independent return in
that field.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:ad-affine-index-symmetry}, an affine
reindexing can only leave that return fixed or replace it by its automatic
reflection.  Neither operation imports the opposite TT* row.  Hence the
three-step mechanism cannot create the missing same-characteristic input.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the no-go]
Proposition~\ref{prop:ad-four-distinct-decoupling} does not say that the
four-characteristic moment is large.  It also does not rule out an integer
elimination performed simultaneously in all four characteristics before
reduction, a compatible trace-function family with a horizontal prime
large sieve, or a genuinely signed four-prime determinant-dispersion
theorem.  It rules out only the natural attempt to turn the determinant
into a source-native single-characteristic continuant problem by local
reduction and affine transport.
\end{remark}
```

# 8. Route-by-route audit

## 8.1 Marginal zero density / random model

Rejected.  The four conditions (7.4) are deterministic reciprocal labels in four different fields.  Replacing them by \(Z(p)/p\), etc., is exactly the independence step Q4545 forbids and is not a theorem of the source.

## 8.2 Ordinary or semiprime Farey spacing

Rejected.  Reduced semiprime fractions can be separated only at scale \(N^{-4}\); the \(d\)-kernel has length \(D=N^{41/100}\).  The circular correction requires both \(\Delta\) branches and does not create the missing factor.

The centered identity (2.5) exposes a prime-denominator shadow, but its coefficient square is the affine zero-set correlation (4.2), for which no canonical power saving is proved.

## 8.3 A second scalar Bettin--Chandee pass

Rejected for the separated sector.  The successful new theorem fixes \((k,\ell)\), so its coefficients factor into a \(p\)-sequence and a \(q\)-sequence.  Once all centered gaps are summed jointly, the coefficient is the incidence matrix of actual zero pairs.  Scalar Bettin--Chandee accepts arbitrary one-variable coefficient sequences, not that joint matrix.  Decomposing it into rank-one pieces charges a nuclear norm not controlled by the periodic fourth moment.

## 8.4 Continuant/resultant with \(\Delta\)

Scoped no-go proved in Proposition `ad-four-distinct-decoupling`.  Modulo each defining prime, \(\Delta\) kills the opposite-row variable rather than coupling it.  The source continuants therefore still see only one independent local return.

## 8.5 Modular-form trace

The canonical theorem is central-index only.  The generic reciprocal labels are not coefficients of one fixed modular form in the source, and the source explicitly warns that the relevant character order grows with \(p\).

## 8.6 Periodic fourth moment

Still insufficient.  It controls coincident/same-row Hilbert--Schmidt mass.  The all-distinct tuples in (7.4) have no repeated defining characteristic across the two TT* rows.  The new centered mask does not change that fact.
