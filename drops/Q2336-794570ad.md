# Q2336: Whipple/Bailey analysis of the Apéry Racah kernel

## 1. Classification of the series

The kernel

\[
K(r,s)={}_4F_3\left(\begin{matrix}-r,r+1,-s,s+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1\right)
\]

is indeed a terminating balanced \({}_4F_3(1)\), because

\[
1+(-r)+(r+1)+(-s)+(s+1)=3=1+1+1.
\]

However, the balanced condition alone is not enough for a Whipple product evaluation. The terminating Whipple summation formulas require an additional very-well-poised or well-poised structure.

The pairings are:

\[
(-r)+(r+1)=1,\qquad (-s)+(s+1)=1,
\]

which is the Racah symmetry, but it is not the Whipple very-well-poised condition

\[
1+a_1=a_2+b_2=a_3+b_3=\cdots .
\]

Taking \(a_1=-r\) would require

\[
1-r=r+2,
\]

which is false except in no nontrivial case. Therefore this is a balanced Racah polynomial, not a Whipple-summable very-well-poised \({}_4F_3\).

## 2. Attempted Whipple substitution

The terminating balanced Whipple transformation is normally written for

\[
{}_4F_3\left(\begin{matrix}a,b,c,-n\\d,e,f\end{matrix};1\right)
\]

with a balancing relation involving the lower parameters. Setting

\[
a=r+1,\quad b=-s,\quad c=s+1,\quad -n=-r,
\]

and

\[
d=e=f=1
\]

does not satisfy the extra well-poised constraint needed to produce a lower-order terminating sum or a gamma-product evaluation.

The formal transformed expression remains another balanced terminating \({}_4F_3\); it does not collapse.

## 3. No product evaluation

A genuine product evaluation would have the form

\[
K(r,s)=\frac{\prod_i \Gamma(\alpha_i r+\beta_i s+\gamma_i)}
{\prod_j \Gamma(\alpha'_j r+\beta'_j s+\gamma'_j)}.
\]

Such evaluations occur for Dougall/Whipple very-well-poised families. They do not occur for the generic Racah polynomial evaluation at a non-endpoint lattice point.

The only product-type simplifications are the known special cases:

* \(r=s\): the norm value from Racah orthogonality;
* endpoint cases where one parameter forces a terminating \({}_3F_2\);
* adjacent values already obtained from the Apéry recurrence.

For generic \((r,s)\), \(K(r,s)\) remains a Racah polynomial value.

## 4. Consequence for P3.2

The Whipple/Bailey route does not produce a hidden factorization of

\[
K(r,s)
\]

and therefore cannot prove the desired nonvanishing of off-diagonal kernel values.

The obstruction is structural: the Apéry kernel has Racah symmetry but not the stronger very-well-poised symmetry required for a classical hypergeometric product theorem.

Hence the answer is:

**No. The Whipple transform does not give a product evaluation or factorization for \(K(r,s)\). The series is balanced Racah, but not Whipple-summable. A new idea must exploit some additional Apéry-specific arithmetic structure rather than a generic \({}_4F_3\) transformation.**
