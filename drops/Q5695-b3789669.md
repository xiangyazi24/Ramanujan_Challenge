# Q5695 — arithmetic group actions on the fixed-moment Apéry shell

## Executive verdict

There is a genuine arithmetic refinement of the fixed-moment shell, but it is not target-selective.

Let

\[
 d=p-1,
 \qquad
 E_a:=E_{a d+r}(d)
 =\sum_{v\in d\mathbf Z^3}[X^v]\Lambda^{ad+r},
 \qquad 1\le r\le p-2.
\]

Then the shell sequence in the quotient parameter `a` satisfies exact prime-power finite-difference congruences

\[
 \boxed{
 v_p(\Delta_a^j E_a)\ge \min\{j,ad+r\}.}
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, for every target residue `r>=2`,

\[
 \boxed{
 E_a\equiv b_r+a p\,U_{p,r}\pmod {p^2}}
\tag{2}
\]

for one explicitly defined first shell jet `U_{p,r}`. Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 E_a-aE_1+(a-1)b_r\equiv0\pmod {p^2}.}
\tag{3}
\]

This is a real `p^2` congruence. It is stronger than the mod-`p` root-of-unity filter, and it has an exact walk/trace interpretation. But it is universal: it holds whether or not `p|b_r`. On a target,

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{E_a}{p}\equiv \frac{b_r}{p}+aU_{p,r}\pmod p.}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus `p|b_r` does **not** force `p^2|E_a`; it leaves the divided scalar on the right free. The shell has reproduced the same first-Witt phenomenon already isolated in the repository: the first lift is a coordinate, not a second target equation.

There is also an exact cyclic-group interpretation. `E_M(d)` is the number of colored length-`M` walks on `(Z/dZ)^3` returning to the origin, or equivalently `d^{-3}` times the trace of the `M`-th power of a Cayley adjacency matrix. The natural translation action is free and has order `d^3=(p-1)^3`, hence prime to `p`; its Burnside strata cannot explain or amplify `p`-divisibility. Rotation of the `M` steps has a `C_p` subgroup only on the thin locus `p|M`, which here is exactly `r=a`. It gives only the usual first Gauss digit and no `p^2` lift.

For two close primes `p` and `ell`, the smallest common walk cover has modulus

\[
 L=\operatorname{lcm}(p-1,\ell-1)
 =\frac{(p-1)(\ell-1)}{\gcd(p-1,\ell-p)}.
\]

When `ell-p=O(log n)`, one has `L \gg p^2/log n`; the common Cayley graph has `L^3` vertices. The two shell sums become sums over two transverse subgroups whose intersection is only the identity. This is an exact common object, but its complexity is much larger than the original block and yields no short resultant or common cyclic-cover equation.

So the route-selection verdict is:

> **The fixed shell possesses Gauss/Mahler congruences in the quotient parameter, but all finite lifts supplied by the natural group actions are universal or leave free Witt coordinates. No known normalization forces a second target-selective congruence, and no bounded-complexity common cyclic cover couples two close residue characteristics.**

The weakest genuinely new theorem that would change the P3.2 situation is not another local `p^k` lift. It is a cross-modulus theorem controlling the simultaneous divisibility of two transverse return-count subgroup sums attached to the same infinite-lattice walk distribution.

## 2. Exact walk, Fourier, and trace interpretations

Write

\[
 \Lambda(X)=\sum_{s\in\mathbf Z^3}m_sX^s,
 \qquad m_s\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{9}
\]

Replace each step `s` by `m_s` colored copies. Then

\[
 c_M(v):=[X^v]\Lambda^M
\tag{10}
\]

is exactly the number of colored words of length `M` whose total displacement in `Z^3` is `v`.

For

\[
 G_d=(\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z)^3,
\tag{11}
\]

one therefore has

\[
 \boxed{
 E_M(d)=\#\{\text{colored length-}M\text{ step words with total }0\text{ in }G_d\}.}
\tag{12}
\]

Let `A_d` be the convolution/Cayley adjacency matrix on `G_d` associated with the colored step multiset. Translation invariance gives

\[
 (A_d^M)_{g,g}=E_M(d)
\quad\text{for every }g\in G_d,
\tag{13}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{tr}(A_d^M)=d^3E_M(d).}
\tag{14}
\]

Diagonalizing the abelian Cayley operator gives the exact root-of-unity filter

\[
 \boxed{
 d^3E_M(d)
 =\sum_{(\zeta_1,\zeta_2,\zeta_3)\in\mu_d^3}
   \Lambda(\zeta_1,\zeta_2,\zeta_3)^M.}
\tag{15}
\]

Formula (15) is valid over the cyclotomic integers for **every** positive `d`; no primality is used.

### 2.1 Burnside interpretation

Let `P_M(d)` be the set of based closed colored walks of length `M` on the Cayley graph. Then

\[
 |P_M(d)|=d^3E_M(d).
\tag{16}
\]

The translation group `G_d` acts freely on `P_M(d)`, and the quotient records only the step word. Therefore

\[
 E_M(d)=|P_M(d)/G_d|.
\tag{17}
\]

Burnside contributes no nontrivial fixed strata: every nonidentity translation fixes no based walk.

For `d=p-1`,

\[
 |G_d|=(p-1)^3
\tag{18}
\]

is a `p`-adic unit. Thus this natural group action has no `p`-orbit mechanism. In particular, the condition

\[
 p\mid E_M(p-1)
\tag{19}
\]

cannot be upgraded to `p^2` by inspecting translation stabilizers: there are none, and the acting group has order prime to `p`.

This is the first sharp group-action obstruction.

## 3. Why the congruence is prime-specific

For `d=p-1`, choose an identification

\[
 \mu_d\longrightarrow\mathbf F_p^\times.
\tag{20}
\]

Then `M≡r (mod d)`, and for every torus point one has

\[
 \Lambda(u)^M=\Lambda(u)^r
\tag{21}
\]

in `F_p`, including zero values because `M,r>0`.

Since every exponent in `Lambda^r` lies in `[-r,r]^3` and `r<d`, only the zero exponent is in `dZ^3`. This proves (8).

For a composite integer `m=d+1`, the exact cyclotomic filter (15) still exists, but the reduction step does not:

- there is no finite field `F_m`;
- `(Z/mZ)^×` need not be cyclic of order `m-1`;
- nonunits and zero divisors intervene;
- the power-sum orthogonality used over `F_p^×` is unavailable;
- even Carmichael-type exponent identities use `lambda(m)`, not `m-1`, and do not reproduce the three-variable coefficient projection.

Thus the observed composite failures are structural, not numerical accidents. The exact shell is general-modulus; the congruence to `b_r` is genuinely prime-specific.

## 4. The correct prime-power lift: Teichmüller roots

Put

\[
 d=p-1,
 \qquad
 E_a=E_{ad+r}(d).
\tag{22}
\]

Embed `Q(mu_d)` into `Q_p` through any prime above `p`. The `d`-th roots of unity become the Teichmüller representatives of `F_p^×`.

For

\[
 \zeta=(\zeta_1,\zeta_2,\zeta_3)\in\mu_d^3,
 \qquad
 t_\zeta=\Lambda(\zeta)\in\mathbf Z_p,
\tag{23}
\]

formula (15) becomes the exact `p`-adic equality

\[
 \boxed{
 d^3E_a=\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_d^3}t_\zeta^{ad+r}.}
\tag{24}
\]

Also, because `r<d`,

\[
 d^3b_r=\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_d^3}t_\zeta^r.
\tag{25}
\]

Subtracting gives

\[
 \boxed{
 d^3(E_a-b_r)
 =\sum_{\zeta}t_\zeta^r
   \bigl((t_\zeta^d)^a-1\bigr).}
\tag{26}
\]

Every term on the right is divisible by `p`:

- if `t_zeta` is a unit, then `t_zeta^d≡1 (mod p)`;
- if `p|t_zeta`, then `r>=1` supplies the factor `p`.

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{E_a=b_r+p\mathcal D_{p,a,r}}
\tag{27}
\]

with the exact `p`-adic integer

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal D_{p,a,r}
 =\frac1{p d^3}
  \sum_{\zeta}t_\zeta^r
   \bigl((t_\zeta^d)^a-1\bigr).}
\tag{28}
\]

On a target `b_r=pq_{p,r}`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{E_a}{p}=q_{p,r}+\mathcal D_{p,a,r}.}
\tag{29}
\]

This formula already answers the main `p^2` question:

\[
 p^2\mid E_a
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 q_{p,r}+\mathcal D_{p,a,r}\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{30}
\]

The target condition gives no relation between these two summands. A `p^2` implication would require a genuinely new identity.

## 5. Exact finite-difference congruences

The strongest unconditional refinement is obtained by taking finite differences in `a`.

Let

\[
 \Delta E_a=E_{a+1}-E_a.
\tag{31}
\]

From (24),

\[
 d^3\Delta^jE_a
 =\sum_{\zeta}
 t_\zeta^{ad+r}(t_\zeta^d-1)^j.
\tag{32}
\]

For an individual summand:

- if `v_p(t_zeta)=0`, then `v_p(t_zeta^d-1)>=1`, hence the valuation is at least `j`;
- if `v_p(t_zeta)>=1`, then `t_zeta^d-1` is a unit and the valuation is at least `ad+r`.

Since `d^3` is a `p`-adic unit, this proves:

### Theorem 5.1 — shell Gauss/Mahler congruence

For every prime `p`, every `1<=r<=p-2`, and all `a,j>=0`,

\[
 \boxed{
 v_p(\Delta^jE_a)\ge\min\{j,ad+r\}.}
\tag{33}
\]

In particular:

1. for every `a>=0`,
   \[
   E_{a+1}\equiv E_a\pmod p;
   \tag{34}
   \]
2. for every `a>=1` and every `j<=p`,
   \[
   \Delta^jE_a\equiv0\pmod {p^j};
   \tag{35}
   \]
3. at `a=0`, the same is valid through grade `j<=r`.

The zero-value terms are exactly why the last statement stops at `j=r`.

### 5.1 The prime-square affine law

For every target residue one has `r>=2` (indeed `b_1=5`). Therefore (33) with `j=2` is valid already at `a=0`, and the sequence is affine modulo `p^2`:

\[
 \boxed{
 E_a\equiv E_0+a(E_1-E_0)\pmod {p^2}.}
\tag{36}
\]

Since `E_0=b_r`, define

\[
 U_{p,r}:=\frac{E_1-b_r}{p}\pmod p.
\tag{37}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 E_a\equiv b_r+apU_{p,r}\pmod {p^2}.}
\tag{38}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 E_a-aE_1+(a-1)b_r\equiv0\pmod {p^2}.}
\tag{39}
\]

This is the cleanest nontrivial prime-power shell congruence.

It is nevertheless universal. On a target,

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{E_a}{p}\equiv
 \frac{b_r}{p}+aU_{p,r}\pmod p.}
\tag{40}
\]

Thus all normalized first shell differences collapse to one scalar:

\[
 \frac{E_a-b_r}{ap}\equiv U_{p,r}\pmod p
 \qquad(a\not\equiv0\pmod p).
\tag{41}
\]

This is the exact first-grade rank-one collapse. It provides a coordinate, not a vanishing equation.

## 6. Explicit first-jet formula and zero-value correction

Split the Teichmüller torus into

\[
 \mathcal U_p=\{\zeta:p\nmid t_\zeta\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal Z_p=\{\zeta:p\mid t_\zeta\}.
\tag{42}
\]

For `zeta in U_p`, define the Fermat quotient

\[
 Q_p(t_\zeta)=\frac{t_\zeta^{p-1}-1}{p}\in\mathbf Z_p.
\tag{43}
\]

From (26), modulo `p`,

\[
 U_{p,r}
 \equiv d^{-3}
 \left(
   \sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal U_p}
     \overline{t_\zeta}^{\,r}\,
     \overline{Q_p(t_\zeta)}
   -\mathbf1_{r=1}
    \sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z_p}
      \overline{t_\zeta/p}
 \right)
 \pmod p.
\tag{44}
\]

The second sum is the exact zero-value correction.

For the specific Apéry polynomial, the zero divisor is

\[
 (1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)=0.
\tag{45}
\]

If a coordinate is `-1`, its Teichmüller lift is literally `-1`; the corresponding factor vanishes exactly in `Z_p`, so `t_zeta=0` and contributes nothing to (44).

The only potentially nonzero values of `t_zeta/p` arise from points where

\[
 (1+y)(1+z)+xyz\equiv0\pmod p
\tag{46}
\]

but the Teichmüller lifts do not satisfy the same additive equation exactly. At such a point,

\[
 \frac{t_\zeta}{p}
 \equiv
 \frac{(1+\zeta_x)(1+\zeta_y)(1+\zeta_z)}
      {\zeta_x\zeta_y\zeta_z}
 \cdot
 \frac{(1+\zeta_y)(1+\zeta_z)+
       \zeta_x\zeta_y\zeta_z}{p}
 \pmod p.
\tag{47}
\]

This term matters only when `r=1` at precision `p^2`. Since `b_1=5`, it never enters a target for `p>=7`.

More generally, a nonunit `t_zeta` contributes first at grade `p^{r}` through the factor `t_zeta^r`. Thus:

> For an interior target with folded residue `r`, zero-value strata are invisible through precision `p^r`; they first become visible one grade later in the divided expansion.

This is the exact boundary/zero-value ledger.

## 7. Higher finite lifts: Mahler coordinates, not target equations

Newton interpolation gives

\[
 E_a=\sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom aj\Delta^jE_0.
\tag{48}
\]

For any fixed `k<=r+1`, Theorem 5.1 yields

\[
 \boxed{
 E_a\equiv
 \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom aj p^jU_{p,r,j}
 \pmod {p^k},}
\tag{49}
\]

where

\[
 U_{p,r,j}:=\frac{\Delta^jE_0}{p^j}\in\mathbf Z_p.
\tag{50}
\]

Beyond the zero-value threshold one can instead expand about `a=1`; for fixed `k<=p`,

\[
 E_a\equiv
 \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
 \binom{a-1}{j}p^jV_{p,r,j}
 \pmod {p^k},
\tag{51}
\]

with

\[
 V_{p,r,j}=\frac{\Delta^jE_1}{p^j}\in\mathbf Z_p.
\tag{52}
\]

Thus each higher grade introduces at most one new Mahler/Witt coordinate. The structure is finite-rank at every fixed precision, but targetness constrains only the zeroth coordinate

\[
 U_{p,r,0}=b_r.
\tag{53}
\]

There is no implication

\[
 p\mid U_{p,r,0}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 p\mid U_{p,r,j}
\tag{54}
\]

for `j>=1` coming from the group action or root filter.

This is the precise sense in which finite lifting collapses:

- modulo `p`, every `E_a` is the same target coordinate `b_r`;
- modulo `p^2`, all normalized differences are the one scalar `U_{p,r}`;
- at higher precision, one obtains further free Witt coordinates, not further target zeros.

The repository's Section 18.13 reaches the same conclusion from the Apéry recurrence. There the first shifted block has

\[
 b_{p+r}\equiv5(b_r+pD_r)\pmod {p^2},
\tag{55}
\]

and on a target the divided coordinates are multiples of

\[
 \xi_{p,r}=b_r/p+D_r.
\tag{56}
\]

The shell jet `U_{p,r}` is another first-Witt coordinate. The corrected Kummer-trace audit in Section 22.1 shows that such marked trace coordinates are related to the existing Witt coordinates by an invertible change, not by an additional vanishing law.

Therefore the shell does not evade the known first-jet obstruction.

## 8. Boundary residues

The interior condition `1<=r<=p-2` is essential.

### 8.1 Residue `r=0`

Here

\[
 M=a(p-1).
\]

Over `F_p`,

\[
 \Lambda(u)^M=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&\Lambda(u)\ne0,\\
 0,&\Lambda(u)=0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{57}
\]

Thus the torus moment counts the nonzero-value locus; it is not the constant term `b_0=1`. The shell `E_M(p-1)` contains many nonzero exponent packets.

### 8.2 Residue `r=p-1`

Again the finite-field power is the nonzero-value indicator, and `Lambda^{p-1}` has exponent vectors equal to nonzero multiples of `p-1`. The root filter therefore does not isolate the constant term.

These are exactly the failures already recorded in Section 48.

### 8.3 Residue `r=1`

The mod-`p` identity is valid, but at precision `p^2` the Teichmüller lifts of residual zeros can contribute through `t_zeta/p`, as in (44). This is the only interior residue with a first-grade zero-stratum term. It is harmless for target analysis because `b_1=5`.

## 9. Cyclic rotation and Dold/Gauss congruences

The step words carry a cyclic rotation action `C_M`. This gives the standard trace/Dold congruences, but only in the exponent variable.

From (14) and the Dold congruence for traces of integer matrices,

\[
 \operatorname{tr}(A_d^{mp^s})
 \equiv
 \operatorname{tr}(A_d^{mp^{s-1}})
 \pmod {p^s}.
\tag{58}
\]

Since `p∤d`, one may divide by `d^3`:

\[
 \boxed{
 E_{mp^s}(d)\equiv E_{mp^{s-1}}(d)\pmod {p^s}.}
\tag{59}
\]

This is a genuine Gauss congruence. It is discussed in the matrix-trace/Dold literature; see, for example:

- Byszewski–Graff–Ward, *Dold sequences, periodic points, and dynamics*, Corollary 4.5: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04031
- Beukers–Houben–Straub, *Gauss congruences for rational functions in several variables*: https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.00423

But our exponent is

\[
 M=a(p-1)+r\equiv r-a\pmod p.
\tag{60}
\]

Thus

\[
 p\mid M
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 r=a
\tag{61}
\]

in the mesoscopic range `a,r<p`.

On this thin locus,

\[
 M=ap,
\tag{62}
\]

and the `C_p` fixed words are `p` repetitions of a word of length `a`. Since multiplication by `p` is invertible on `G_{p-1}`, a repeated word closes exactly when the short word closes. Burnside gives

\[
 E_{ap}(p-1)\equiv E_a(p-1)=b_a=b_r\pmod p.
\tag{63}
\]

If `p|b_a`, then both sides vanish modulo `p`, but

\[
 \frac{E_{ap}(p-1)-E_a(p-1)}p
\tag{64}
\]

counts the free `C_p` orbits and is unconstrained modulo `p`. Hence no `p^2` follows.

Moreover `p^2∤M` because `a<p`. There is no `C_{p^2}` subgroup and no second Burnside layer.

For generic targets `r!=a`, even the first `C_p` action is absent.

## 10. Dwork congruences do not supply a shell square

Mellit–Vlasenko prove Dwork congruences for the **constant terms**

\[
 b_m=[X^0]\Lambda^m
\tag{65}
\]

when the Newton polytope has the required one-interior-point property. Their Theorem 1 and the Samol–van Straten congruence control changes in the base-`p` digits of the exponent; see:

- Mellit–Vlasenko, *Dwork's congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial*, Theorem 1: https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5811
- Samol–van Straten, *Dwork congruences and reflexive polytopes*: https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.0797

These theorems apply to `b_m`, not to the moving projection

\[
 E_M(d)=\sum_{v\in d\mathbf Z^3}c_M(v)
\tag{66}
\]

as `d` varies.

The shell congruences (33) are a separate phenomenon: they come from evaluating the same cyclotomic eigenvalues at powers differing by `p-1`.

Neither family of congruences says

\[
 p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow p^2\mid E_{a(p-1)+r}(p-1).
\tag{67}
\]

The exact formula (40) shows what would have to be proved instead.

## 11. Cyclotomic conjugates and Gauss sums

Because `p≡1 (mod p-1)`, the prime `p` splits completely in `Q(mu_{p-1})`. One might hope that the many primes above `p` produce many congruences.

They do not for the full shell. The root-filter sum in (15) is the rational integer `d^3E_M(d)`. Galois conjugation merely permutes the tuples of roots of unity, so every conjugate equation is identical.

If one refines the sum into individual character packets, then the packets lie in cyclotomic fields and their conjugates are genuinely different. But targetness concerns only the trivial packet average `E_M(d)`. Vanishing of this average modulo `p` does not force any individual nontrivial packet to vanish.

The Kummer/Gauss lift from the previous route-selection audit has the complementary problem: it converts `t^r` into a multiplicative character value, but the character order

\[
 \frac{p-1}{\gcd(r,p-1)}
\tag{68}
\]

and its cyclotomic coefficient field vary with `p`. Divisibility at one prime above `p` does not give a bounded-degree rational norm carrier.

Thus cyclotomic refinement changes coordinates, but it does not create a second rational target equation.

## 12. Why a prime-power unit-group lift does not preserve the fixed moment

A tempting replacement for `F_p^×` is `(Z/p^sZ)^×`, whose order is

\[
 \varphi(p^s)=p^{s-1}(p-1).
\tag{69}
\]

But the fixed exponent satisfies only

\[
 M\equiv r\pmod {p-1},
\tag{70}
\]

not modulo `phi(p^s)`. Therefore the power reduction `Lambda^M=Lambda^r` does not persist at level `p^s`.

One could replace `M` by

\[
 M_s=a\varphi(p^s)+r,
\tag{71}
\]

but then the exponent changes with `s` and the fixed-moment synchronization is lost.

Using all `phi(p^s)`-th roots of unity is also ramified: the averaging denominator has positive `p`-adic valuation, unlike `(p-1)^3`. The unramified Teichmüller lift in Section 4 is therefore the canonical prime-power continuation, and it yields the free-coordinate expansion above.

## 13. Two close primes and the exact common walk cover

Let

\[
 d=p-1,
 \qquad
 e=\ell-1,
 \qquad
 L=\operatorname{lcm}(d,e).
\tag{72}
\]

Let

\[
 P_{M,L}(g)
 =\sum_{v\equiv g\pmod L}c_M(v),
 \qquad g\in G_L.
\tag{73}
\]

Reduction maps `G_L→G_d` and `G_L→G_e` have kernels

\[
 K_d\cong(\mathbf Z/(L/d)\mathbf Z)^3,
 \qquad
 K_e\cong(\mathbf Z/(L/e)\mathbf Z)^3.
\tag{74}
\]

The two shells are exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 E_M(d)=\sum_{g\in K_d}P_{M,L}(g),
 \qquad
 E_M(e)=\sum_{g\in K_e}P_{M,L}(g).}
\tag{75}
\]

Moreover

\[
 K_d\cap K_e=\{0\}.
\tag{76}
\]

If `ell=p+h`, then

\[
 \gcd(d,e)=\gcd(p-1,h),
\tag{77}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 L=\frac{(p-1)(p+h-1)}{\gcd(p-1,h)}.}
\tag{78}
\]

For `h=O(log n)`,

\[
 L\gg \frac{p^2}{\log n}.
\tag{79}
\]

Thus the smallest common Cayley graph has

\[
 |G_L|=L^3\gg \frac{p^6}{(\log n)^3}
\tag{80}
\]

vertices.

This gives an exact common object, but it also proves the complexity obstruction:

- the two relevant subgroup sums are transverse, not nested;
- their intersection contains only the single endpoint `0`;
- the common modulus is essentially the product `(p-1)(ell-1)`;
- any adjacency characteristic polynomial or walk resultant has degree at least `L^3`;
- reducing to the common quotient `gcd(d,e)` discards almost all fiber information and does not determine either target sum.

A common cyclic cover has the same problem. A Kummer cover carrying both character orders must have degree divisible by their least common multiple, generically of size comparable to `L`. A fixed cover

\[
 Y^M=\Lambda(X)
\tag{81}
\]

has common exponent but its point count detects whether `Lambda(X)` is an `M`-th power; it does not compute the field-valued moment `sum Lambda(X)^M`.

An Artin–Schreier cover computing an additive character of `Lambda^M` depends on the residue characteristic and computes the wrong observable.

Therefore no bounded-degree common cyclic-cover construction currently couples the two target equations.

## 14. What the group-action route genuinely proves

The group-action audit produces four exact positive statements.

### A. Return-walk interpretation

`E_M(d)` is an honest orbit/trace count on a finite abelian Cayley graph.

### B. Prime-power finite differences

For fixed `p,r`, the quotient-parameter shell is `p`-adically analytic in the precise Mahler sense (33).

### C. Universal `p^2` relation

For target residues `r>=2`, all first shell lifts satisfy the affine identity (39).

### D. Exact common cover for two moduli

Two close-prime shells are subgroup sums of one endpoint distribution on `G_lcm(p-1,ell-1)`.

None is target-selective:

- A uses a `p`-prime-to-order action.
- B and C leave free divided coordinates.
- D has product-size complexity and transverse kernels.

## 15. Scoped no-go statement

The conclusions can be summarized as follows.

### Theorem 15.1 — natural shell-action no-go

Fix the Apéry Laurent polynomial `Lambda`. Consider constructions generated by:

1. the translation action on the Cayley graph `(Z/(p-1)Z)^3`;
2. cyclic rotation of the `M` steps;
3. root-of-unity Fourier decomposition;
4. Teichmüller lifting to fixed precision `p^k`;
5. passage to a common quotient or common cover for `p-1` and `ell-1`.

Then:

- translation Burnside strata contain no `p`-torsion;
- rotation gives a `p`-orbit congruence only when `r=a`, and then only modulo `p`;
- at precision `p^2`, every normalized shell difference is a multiple of one free scalar `U_{p,r}`;
- at precision `p^k`, the shell is a Mahler polynomial with free Witt coefficients and targetness constrains only its constant coefficient;
- the minimal common close-prime cover has degree at least `lcm(p-1,ell-1)` and group size its cube;
- no target condition forces `p^2` divisibility of a normalized shell expression except for universal identities already valid off the target locus.

This does not prove that accidental `p^2` shell divisibility never occurs at an Apéry target. It proves that the standard arithmetic group actions do not force it and reduce every proposed lift to explicit free coordinates.

## 16. Weakest plausible new theorem

A useful new theorem must be cross-modulus. The natural exact formulation is in terms of the common endpoint distribution (73).

Let `H=n^(1/3)`, fix a quotient branch `a`, put `M=n-a`, and let `I` be a length-`H` prime interval. For `p,ell in I`, define

\[
 L_{p,\ell}=\operatorname{lcm}(p-1,\ell-1),
\tag{82}
\]

and let `P_{M,L}` be the common walk endpoint distribution.

The weakest theorem sufficient for the existing adjacent-pair reduction is:

> **Cross-shell anti-clustering theorem.** For every fixed `A>0`, uniformly in `n`, every fixed quotient branch `a<=H`, and every length-`H` interval `I`,
> \[
> \#\left\{
> \begin{array}{c}
> p<\ell\text{ consecutive target primes in }I,\\
> \ell-p\le A\log n,\\
> p\mid\sum_{g\in K_{p-1}}P_{M,L_{p,\ell}}(g),\\
> \ell\mid\sum_{g\in K_{\ell-1}}P_{M,L_{p,\ell}}(g)
> \end{array}
> \right\}
> =o_A(H/\log n).
> \tag{83}
> \]

This is merely the repository's pure-cross criterion rewritten in the strongest common group-action coordinates. The rewrite identifies the missing input precisely:

> One needs cancellation between two **transverse subgroup sums of one distinguished lattice-walk distribution**, modulo two different nearby primes, with bounds uniform despite the product-size common cover.

A stronger but more structural theorem would assert that the endpoint distributions `P_{M,L}` have no simultaneous subgroup-sum zeros beyond the same-characteristic/degenerate loci, with an error uniform in `M` and `L`. No standard Burnside, Dold, Dwork, cyclic-sieving, or finite-field character theorem has these quantifiers.

## Final conclusion

The fixed-moment shell is not arithmetically empty. It has:

- an exact return-walk model;
- a free translation quotient;
- trace/Dold congruences in the exponent;
- a new family of `p^j` finite-difference congruences in the quotient parameter;
- a universal affine law modulo `p^2`;
- an exact common close-prime cover.

But each positive structure stops at the same point:

\[
 p\mid b_r
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{E_{a(p-1)+r}(p-1)}p
 \equiv
 \frac{b_r}{p}+aU_{p,r}\pmod p,
\]

with `U_{p,r}` free.

The natural group actions therefore do not force `p^2`, do not produce a second independent target congruence, and do not couple close primes at bounded complexity. The shell route can reopen P3.2 only through a genuinely cross-characteristic theorem for the distinguished walk endpoint distribution, not through further finite local lifting.
