# Dwork/Katz unit-root theory and the nonvanishing of K(r,s)

## Short answer

No known Dwork/Katz unit-root theorem directly implies the required statement. The obstruction is structural: the unit root controls the **one-variable Frobenius period** giving the Apéry numbers `b_n`, whereas `K(r,s)` is a **two-index bilinear pairing of truncated hypergeometric solutions**. There is no general theorem identifying this pairing with a product of unit-root evaluations.

A plausible new theorem would have to be a genuinely stronger "two-point unit-root correlation" statement, not an application of existing Dwork theory.

## What Dwork actually controls

The Apéry generating series is a period of the Apéry K3 family. Let `F(t)` denote the holomorphic period. Dwork theory constructs a unit-root function

```
U(t) = F(t) / F(t^p)  (after Frobenius normalization)
```

on the ordinary locus. Katz's theory identifies Frobenius eigenvalues on the unit-root subcrystal and gives congruences such as

```
b_{mp+n} mod p
```
being governed by Frobenius data.

This explains facts such as:

- `b_{p-1} ≡ 1 mod p^3`;
- the relation with the weight-4 modular form at the middle index;
- the CFVZ square structure modulo p.

But the input is still a single period evaluated under Frobenius.

## What K(r,s) is

The kernel is

```
K(r,s)=Σ_k D(r,k)D(s,k)
      = 4F3(-r,r+1,-s,s+1;1,1,1;1).
```

Equivalently, it is the Gram matrix entry of two truncated Racah solutions. It depends on two independent truncation parameters `r` and `s`.

The desired statement is

```
b_r=b_s=0 mod p  ==>  K(r,s) != 0 mod p.
```

This is not a statement about a single Frobenius eigenvalue. It is a statement about a correlation between two vanishing loci.

## Why the hoped-for factorization does not follow

A factorization of the form

```
K(r,s)=U(r)U(s)*unit
```

would solve the problem immediately, because the unit-root factors would be nonzero on the ordinary locus.

However, Dwork theory gives no such factorization. The hypergeometric rank-4 object underlying the `4F3` has nontrivial extension data. The bilinear pairing is a period pairing, not a Frobenius eigenvalue.

The analogue would require a theorem saying that the Frobenius-invariant pairing of two truncated solutions is controlled by the tensor product of their unit-root lines. That is a new statement.

## The precise missing theorem

A useful formulation would be:

Let `H` be the Apéry overconvergent F-isocrystal and let `L` be its unit-root sub-isocrystal on the ordinary locus. One would need a canonical map

```
Trunc_r(H) tensor Trunc_s(H)  --> L_r tensor L_s
```

such that the Racah kernel satisfies

```
K(r,s) = c(r,s) * <u_r,u_s>
```

with `c(r,s)` a p-adic unit and `<u_r,u_s>` a unit-root pairing.

No standard Dwork, Katz, or Crew theorem gives this map for these finite truncations.

## Could a modified route work?

The most realistic arithmetic route is not a direct unit-root evaluation, but a new theorem about Frobenius correlations:

```
If b_r ≡ b_s ≡ 0 mod p and r+s+1 != p,
then the two Frobenius-degenerate directions in H(F_p)
are transverse.
```

The kernel `K(r,s)` would then be the determinant/transversality invariant of those two directions.

This is closer to a Katz monodromy statement than to ordinary Dwork theory.

## Verdict

Among the offered possibilities:

- (a) No known missing elementary identity appears; all attempted algebraic routes reduce to the same kernel problem.
- (b) A CFVZ-style structural theorem for the full K3/Frobenius representation is the most plausible new ingredient.
- (c) A route avoiding `K(r,s)` would require replacing the kernel obstruction by a direct arithmetic independence theorem for Apéry zero sets.
- (d) The honest assessment is that current Dwork/Katz tools are insufficient.

The next genuinely new target is therefore not "apply Dwork to K" but:

**prove a Frobenius-transversality theorem for two Apéry zero indices.**

Such a theorem would imply `K(r,s) != 0`, while existing unit-root theory only explains the individual zeros `b_r ≡ 0`.
