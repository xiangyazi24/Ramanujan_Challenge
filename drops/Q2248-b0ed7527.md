## 2. The opposite-conic jets are exactly `C0,C1,C2`

This is the clean way to organize the local pole algebra.  Put

\[
F_{c,q}(t):=G_c(t)G_q(-t).
\]

Then, directly from the Taylor expansions,

\[
\boxed{
F_{c,q}(t)
=C_0-C_1t+C_2t^2+O(t^3).
}
\tag{OPP}
\]

Hence

\[
-F'_{c,q}(0)=C_1,
\qquad
\frac12F''_{c,q}(0)=C_2.
\]

This is the exact meaning of “differentiate in the opposite parameter”.  It is characteristic zero and contains no `p`-adic division.

The second conic deformation dictated by the endpoint odd coordinates is

\[
R(t)=R+2t,
\qquad
S(t)=S-2t.
\]

It keeps `R(t)+S(t)=R+S` fixed and gives

\[
\boxed{
A(t)=R(t)S(t)=A+4ht-4t^2.
}
\tag{At}
\]

The reciprocal derivatives are

\[
\boxed{
\left(A(t)^{-1}\right)'_{t=0}=-\frac{4h}{A^2},
\qquad
\left(A(t)^{-1}\right)''_{t=0}
=\frac{8(A+4h^2)}{A^3}.
}
\tag{Ainv}
\]

The combination `A+4h^2` in Q2248 is therefore exactly the second reciprocal-conic derivative.  Note also the cubic identity

\[
A+4h^2=R^2-RS+S^2=\frac{R^3+S^3}{R+S},
\]

which is the form naturally produced by the cubic Apéry/Racah Green operator.

## 3. Exact 2-jet packaging of `J2`

Set formally

\[
H_\circ(t):=C_1-C_2t+h\mathcal O_\circ t^2.
\]

Then using `(At)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
A^3[t^2]\frac{H_\circ(t)}{A(t)}
={}&A^3\left(
\frac{h\mathcal O_\circ}{A}
+\frac{4hC_2}{A^2}
+\frac{4(A+4h^2)C_1}{A^3}
\right)\\
={}&hA^2\mathcal O_\circ
+4hAC_2+4(A+4h^2)C_1.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(C2JET)`.

### Why this matters for the WZ attack

Do **not** try to prove the displayed three-term expression term-by-term.  Keep `t` alive and prove a single coefficient identity for

\[
[t^2]\frac{H_\circ(t)}{(R+2t)(S-2t)}.
\]

A successful finite WZ calculation should have the form

\[
[t^2]\frac{H_\circ(t)}{A(t)}
=[t^2]\bigl(
X(t)b_r+Y(t)b_s+Z(t)m_h
\bigr),
\tag{WZ2jet}
\]

with the exact clearing already incorporated into `X,Y,Z`.  Then the characteristic-zero certificates are simply

\[
\boxed{
X_2=A^3[t^2]X(t),
\quad
Y_2=A^3[t^2]Y(t),
\quad
Z_2=A^3[t^2]Z(t).

\tag{cert-extract}

This is smaller than proving a full all-`t` identity: only equality modulo `t^3` is needed.  Conversely, if a proposed deformed border does **not** reproduce the 2-jet `(C2JET)`, its normalization is wrong before any telescoping is attempted.

## 6. Why deleting the central pole creates an interior WZ boundary

For any discrete divergence

\[
D_j=Q_j-Q_{j-1},
\]

full summation gives only endpoint currents:

\[
\sum_{j=a}^bD_j=Q_b-Q_{a-1}.
\]

But deleting an interior index `c` gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{a\le j\le b\\j\ne c}}D_j
=Q_b-Q_{a-1}-(Q_c-Q_{c-1}).
}
\tag{cut}
\]

The last term is an **interior cut current**.  In a triangular/double WZ sum, deleting the ordered pairs `(c,q)` and `(q,c)` creates the analogous cut edges.  Differentiating before or after summation does not make those currents disappear.

Therefore the instruction “delete the central pole before specializing, then sum by parts” has a precise consequence: the proof must either

1. show that the differentiated cut current is itself endpoint-supported, or
2. retain a third generator controlling that cut current.

The checked-in half-step/placement theory identifies `m_h` as exactly the natural third generator.  So `(b_r,b_s,m_h)` is the correct first characteristic-zero target.  Endpoint-only membership requires a further cancellation; it is **not** automatic from WZ.

## 7. The checked-in three-generator certificate and why `Z` is natural

Q2208 proves, with `n=h-1`,

\[
\mathcal R_{r,n,j}=D_{r,n}O_n^3\Theta_{r,n,j},
\]

\[
\sum_{j=1}^n\mathcal R_{r,n,j}
=D_{r,n}m_h-O_n^3N_h(r),
\]

and the exact Green cut

\[
b_{r+1}N_h(r)=D_{r,n}b_s+E_{r,n}b_r,
\]

where

\[
D_{r,n}=\prod_{u=r+2}^{s}u^3,
\qquad
E_{r,n}=(r+1)^3U_{n-1}(r+2).
\]

Combining them gives the characteristic-zero identity

\[
\boxed{
 b_{r+1}\sum_j\mathcal R_{r,n,j}
 =D_{r,n}b_{r+1}m_h
 -O_n^3D_{r,n}b_s
 -O_n^3E_{r,n}b_r.
}
\tag{PC}
\]

This is an explicit three-generator certificate.  If the second-conic WZ reduction produces

\[
\widetilde J_2
=M\sum_j\mathcal R_{r,n,j}+X_0b_r+Y_0b_s,
\tag{bridge}
\]

then `(PC)` immediately gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
b_{r+1}\widetilde J_2
={}&\bigl(b_{r+1}X_0-MO_n^3E_{r,n}\bigr)b_r\\
&+\bigl(b_{r+1}Y_0-MO_n^3D_{r,n}\bigr)b_s\\
&+\bigl(MD_{r,n}b_{r+1}\bigr)m_h.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{XYZ-saturated}

So, for the saturated identity, the certificates are explicitly

\[
\boxed{

Y&=b_{r+1}Y_0-MO_n^3D_{r,n},\\

## 8. What an actual proof of Q2248 now has to show

With `(C2JET)` frozen, the source-specific problem can be stated without any `p`-adic language.

Let the exact local bordered Bailey/Euler or deformed-Racah identity define a rational-in-`t` finite summand `W_j(t)` whose centrally excised second-conic numerator has the 2-jet

\[
H_\circ(t)=C_1-C_2t+h\mathcal O_\circ t^2+O(t^3).
\]

The missing theorem is one of the following two forms.

### Preferred three-generator form

Find finite WZ certificates `Q_j(t)` and boundary coefficients `X(t),Y(t),Z(t)` such that, modulo `t^3`,

\[
\frac{H_\circ(t)}{A(t)}
- X(t)b_r-Y(t)b_s-Z(t)m_h
=\sum_j\bigl(Q_j(t)-Q_{j-1}(t)\bigr),

**with the central cut current included explicitly**.  Taking `[t^2]` and multiplying by `A^3` gives the desired characteristic-zero three-generator certificate via `(cert-extract)`.

### Endpoint-only form

It cannot be inferred from `v_p(m_h)>=2`, and it cannot be inferred from `(PC)` without illegally cancelling `b_{r+1}` or replacing a prime-local unit argument by an integer Bezout identity.

This is the exact place where a finite WZ solver should be aimed.  Solving three unrelated displayed terms in `J2` separately is the wrong normalization.
