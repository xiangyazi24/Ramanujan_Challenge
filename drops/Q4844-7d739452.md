# 8. Audit of the proposed SPG mechanisms

## 8.1 Subinterval zero bounds

The canonical consecutive-subinterval theorem is not invariant under a
nontrivial dilation.  The selected labels are
\[
 r'-np'\pmod{\ell_0},
\]
so consecutive \(n\)'s form an affine progression of step \(-p'\), not a
consecutive interval of residue representatives.  The gap polynomials
control ordinary integer gaps and cannot be applied after multiplying the
zero set by \((p')^{-1}\).

Full finite Parseval gives only
\[
 \#\{n\in I:r'-np'\in\mathcal Z_{\ell_0}\}
 \ll \frac{|I|Z(\ell_0)}{\ell_0}
      +\sqrt{|I|Z(\ell_0)}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
At
\[
 |I|=N^{59/100},
 \qquad Z(\ell_0)\le N^{2/3+o(1)},
\]
the square-root term has exponent \(377/600>59/100\), so (8.1) is
weaker than the trivial length bound already used in (2.5).

## 8.2 The omitted fixed tests

Along (2.1), reduction modulo \(p\) and \(q\) is independent of \(n\),
and reduction modulo \(p'\) is the fixed actual zero \(r'\).  Hence the
actual \(q\)- and \(p'\)-tests do not produce additional \(n\)-averages.
For the two partner primes they are not part of the reciprocal degree and
may not be inserted again without changing the Cauchy factor.

## 8.3 Affine tight frame

For fixed \(\ell_0\), the maps \(n\mapsto r'-p'n\) are affine maps, but
the source does not average over the complete affine group.  The slopes
are shell primes, the translations are actual zeros in varying foreign
characteristics, the tent center depends on the same data, and all masks
remain selected.  Q4655's exact tight frame controls a complete affine
orbit and its mask-robust consequence is an **absolute** coefficient
square.  It supplies no Carleson estimate against the selected denominator
(6.4).

## 8.4 Fixed-column gap/resultant energy

For the dominant term, the selected fixed-\(p\) label is
\[
 r_0=t\pmod p,
\]
whereas the partner certificate starts at a different label
\[
 r_1,\qquad r_1+h.
\]
The fixed-block count is therefore \(Z(p)A_p(h)\).  The exact energy in
`energy_result.tex` controls two different gap levels at the **same**
starting column.  It does not control the product of an arbitrary selected
column \(r_0\) and a one-gap certificate at \(r_1\).

If \(\ell_0\) is a gap endpoint, the columns coincide, but Section 5
shows that only \(O_v(1)\) consecutive gaps are incident to the selected
occurrence.  Those cases are removed before the load-bearing moment is
formed.  Thus the fixed-column \(H^{8/3}\) energy does not touch the
remaining all-distinct selected moment.

Prime-summed resultant bounds likewise give absolute incidence totals.
They do not show that the literal selected Palm measure avoids the primes
or columns carrying those incidences.

## 8.5 Additive large sieve

The fractions belonging to shell moduli have spacing \(\asymp N^{-2}\).
On an \(n\)-interval of length \(N^{59/100}\), the additive large sieve
therefore pays
\[
 N^2+N^{59/100}\asymp N^2.
\]
For every legal \(\alpha\le1/2\), the desired gap scale is at most
\(H^2\le N\).  The generic large-sieve term is therefore at least a full
factor \(N\) too large even before the second partner and the selected

## 8.6 Self-normalized good/bad decomposition

Dyadic conditioning cancels the scalar energy amplitude, but it leaves

the number of bad determinants.  The selected occurrence set on one

## 8.7 Absolute numerator or mixed-first-moment bypass

The corrected absolute bounds (4.2), (V8-tri) and (V8-D) do not contain
\(\mathfrak M_0\).  There is no exact lower normalization for
