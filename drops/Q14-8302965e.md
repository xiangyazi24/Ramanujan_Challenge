# Additive energy of the Apéry top-window atom set

## Executive conclusion

The desired estimate

\[
E(S)=\#\{(p,q,p',q'):p+z_p+p'+z_{p'}=q+z_q+q' + z_{q'}\}=O(N/\log^2 N)
\]

(or equivalently the number of nontrivial collisions of \(S=\{p+z_p\}\) being \(O(N/\log^2 N)\)) is a natural random-model prediction, but current additive-combinatorics technology does not give it from the known constraints on the Apéry zero sets alone.

The main obstruction is that the map

\[
p\mapsto z_p
\]

is a moving-modulus arithmetic object. Sum-product and incidence theorems are strongest when the points lie in a single finite field or when one controls algebraic complexity of the graph. Here the graph is a collection of points in different fields \(\mathbf F_p\), one for each prime.

The right route is probably not a direct Szemerédi--Trotter argument on \(S\), but a character-sum / Frobenius-independence estimate for the collision equation.

---

# 1. Reformulating collisions

A collision is

\[
p+z_p=q+z_q.
\]

Equivalently

\[
z_p-z_q=q-p.
\]

For fixed prime gap \(h=q-p\), one needs to bound

\[
\#\{p,p+h\text{ prime}: z_{p+h}-z_p=h\}.
\]

Thus the additive energy question is a two-prime correlation problem for the Apéry zero locations.

The random model gives probability approximately \(1/N\) for a given pair to collide, hence

\[
\binom{N/\log N}{2}\frac1N\asymp \frac{N}{\log^2N}.
\]

So the conjectured bound is exactly the square-root/birthday scale.

---

# 2. What the known Apéry structure gives

The available constraints are:

* Reflection:
  \[
  z\in Z_p\Longleftrightarrow p-1-z\in Z_p.
  \]

* No consecutive zeros.

* Lucas-type block laws:
  \[
  b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
  \]

* Geometric/K3 interpretation of the zero condition through Hasse invariants and Frobenius traces.

These are strong enough to control individual \(Z_p\), but not obviously enough to control the additive energy across different primes.

In particular, reflection only replaces \(z_p\) by another possible branch

\[
p+z_p\leftrightarrow 2p-1-z_p,
\]

and does not prevent many primes from landing on the same physical integer.

---

# 3. Why standard additive combinatorics does not immediately apply

## Sum-product

Sum-product theorems (Bourgain--Katz--Tao, etc.) say that a subset \(A\subset\mathbf F_p\) cannot have simultaneously small sumset and product set.

Here the relevant object is not one subset of one \(\mathbf F_p\), but

\[
\{p+z_p:p\in(N,2N]\}.
\]

The ambient modulus changes with every element. There is no single field in which additive structure and multiplicative structure interact.

A possible future reduction would be to encode \(z_p\) as the root of a fixed algebraic relation in a common parameter space, but this is not currently available.

## Szemerédi--Trotter

The equation

\[
p+z_p=m
\]

can be viewed as incidences between points and curves. However, the curves depend on Frobenius data of the prime \(p\). Classical incidence bounds require bounded algebraic complexity of the curve family. The Apéry curves have arithmetic complexity growing with \(p\).

Therefore a direct ST bound does not see the needed cancellation.

---

# 4. The plausible arithmetic-combinatorics route

The collision indicator can be expanded using additive characters:

\[
1_{p+z_p=q+z_q}
=\frac1M\sum_{t\bmod M}e(t(p+z_p-q-z_q)/M).
\]

Then

\[
E(S)=\sum_t\left|\sum_p e(t(p+z_p)/M)\right|^2.
\]

Therefore the target estimate reduces to square-root cancellation in the exponential sums

\[
\sum_{p\in(N,2N]}e(t(p+z_p)/M).
\]

This is the key observation: additive energy is equivalent to a Fourier-uniformity statement for the sequence \(p+z_p\).

A sufficient theorem would be:

\[
\sum_{p\in(N,2N]}e(t(p+z_p)/M)
\ll N^{1/2+o(1)}
\]

for all nonzero frequencies \(t\).

Then Parseval gives the desired birthday-scale energy.

---

# 5. Where the Apéry/K3 structure may enter

The strongest possible approach is to express

\[
z_p=r
\]

as a Frobenius condition:

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The K3/Hasse invariant interpretation suggests that this is a trace condition on Frobenius acting on a sheaf.

Then the correlation

\[
z_p-z_q=h
\]

becomes a two-prime correlation of Frobenius conditions.

A Bombieri--Vinogradov style statement would look like:

\[
\sum_{h\le H}
\left|
\#\{p:p,p+h\text{ prime}, b_{z_p}=b_{z_{p+h}}=0\}-\mathrm{main}(h)
\right|
=o(N/\log^2N).
\]

This is much closer to the existing LGTQ-3/cross-prime-independence program than to classical additive combinatorics.

---

# 6. A weaker statement that may already suffice

For the GCD conjecture, one does not need the exact energy asymptotic. A useful target is an upper-tail estimate:

\[
\#\{m:K(m)\ge2\}=O(N^{1-\delta}).
\]

The twin-atom energy estimate is one route, but even a weaker estimate on high multiplicity values would suffice.

A second-moment argument gives

\[
\sum_m K(m)(K(m)-1)
\]

as the relevant quantity. Bounding this by \(N^{1+o(1)}\) already gives strong control of the bad set.

---

# 7. References and analogies

Relevant techniques:

* Bourgain--Katz--Tao, "Sum-product estimates in finite fields" (Gauss sum/sum-product expansion).
* Bourgain--Gamburd, expansion and independence of arithmetic random walks.
* Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem: average distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions.
* Katz, "Gauss sums, Kloosterman sums, and monodromy groups" for the sheaf-theoretic route.
* Deligne's Riemann Hypothesis over finite fields: square-root cancellation once the correct sheaf is identified.

The closest analogy is not a classical additive-energy problem. It is a "moving Frobenius atom" problem: show that zeros of a family of arithmetic sheaves at different primes behave independently enough that their additive coincidences have only random frequency.

---

# Final assessment

The estimate \(E(S)=O(N/\log^2N)\) is a very plausible intermediate conjecture and would essentially be the correct twin-atom bound. However:

1. Reflection, no-consecutive-zero, and the known gap polynomial constraints alone are insufficient.
2. Pure additive combinatorics tools (sum-product, ST) do not directly apply because the ambient field changes with the prime.
3. The promising route is to prove Fourier cancellation for \(p+z_p\) using the K3/Frobenius sheaf structure.
4. A successful proof would likely be a Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem for Apéry zero events rather than a standard energy estimate.

So the right research target is: **prove cross-prime Frobenius independence for the Apéry zero detector; the additive energy bound should then follow by Parseval.**
