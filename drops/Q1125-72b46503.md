# Audit verdict

The proposed all-residue mod-p^2 theorem is not correct as stated. The obstruction is not in the squared carry-valuation argument itself; the failure occurs in the surviving no-carry rectangle: the first-order expansion of the two Apéry binomial factors has an additional dependence on the high block index that is not captured by only the two proposed jets \(D_{p,r}\) and \(J_{p,r}\).

## 1. Carry boundaries

For \(k=ip+v\), the valuation statements are valid in the strict carry cases:

* If \(v>r\), then \(\binom{ap+r}{ip+v}\) has one p-adic carry, hence valuation one, and its square is zero modulo \(p^2\).
* If \(r+v\ge p\), then \(\binom{(a+i)p+r+v}{ip+v}\) has one carry and again its square vanishes modulo \(p^2\).

The boundary \(r+v=p-1\) is not a carry. It is a no-carry case and must remain in the sum. Likewise the midpoint \(r=(p-1)/2\) is included because the two ranges meet exactly; replacing the range by a guessed lower-half symmetry loses a first-order contribution.

The condition \(a+i=p\) is also delicate. The high binomial does not simply disappear by the same argument: its valuation must be computed from the actual base-p digits. The surviving expansion changes at this boundary.

## 2. Failure of the proposed factorization

The surviving terms have the form

\[
W_{a,i,r,v}(1+2p(\alpha_{a,i,v}))
\]

where the correction term contains separate \(a\)- and \(i\)-dependent harmonic pieces. Summing over \(i\) therefore produces more than

\[
a b_aD_{p,r}+M_aJ_{p,r}.
\]

The missing part is a second high-index moment coming from the \(i\)-dependence of the first binomial and the shifted upper factorial in the second binomial. It cannot be absorbed into \(D_{p,r}\) or \(J_{p,r}\) for all \(a\).

A direct exact arithmetic check gives the first failure at the smallest tested boundary where the midpoint interaction appears:

\[
(p,r,a)=(7,3,1).
\]

The claimed right side gives a different residue modulo \(49\) from the exact Apéry sum
\[
b_n=\sum_k\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}k^2,
\]
with \(n=10\).

## 3. Why the numerical tests missed the defect

The false identity can pass many tests because:

1. The carry terms really do vanish modulo \(p^2\).
2. For small residues the missing moment can accidentally be linearly dependent on the displayed jets.
3. The first failing cases require the interaction of the high block and the midpoint/carry boundary.

## 4. Consequence for jet reduction

The proposed simplification

\[
M_a=\sum_i i\binom ai^2\binom{a+i}i^2
\]

is not enough. There is no identity reducing this to a fixed multiple of \(b_a\). It is a genuine weighted moment of the Apéry kernel. Similarly \(J_{p,r}\) is not reducible to \(D_{p,r}\) by symmetry because the two harmonic combinations have different parity under \(v\leftrightarrow r-v\).

The correct mod-\(p^2\) theory therefore requires an enlarged finite jet basis, including at least the missing high-index moment. The claimed two-jet formula (*) is refuted; no recurrence-dimension argument is needed.
