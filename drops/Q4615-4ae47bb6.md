# 2. Cold audit of the cofactor Gauss identity

For \(r\in I_a(H)\), put

\[
 P_{a,r}(Y)
 =\prod_{\substack{s\in I_a(H)\\s\ne r}}N_s(Y),
\]

introduce independent variables \(\mathbf T=(T_r)_{r\in I_a(H)}\), and define

\[
 Q_{a,H}(Y;\mathbf T)
 =\sum_{r\in I_a(H)}T_rP_{a,r}(Y),
\]

\[
 \mathscr R^{\rm cof}_{a,H}(\mathbf T)
 =\operatorname{Res}_Y
  \bigl(\widetilde N_a(Y),Q_{a,H}(Y;\mathbf T)\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak K_{a,H}
 =\operatorname{cont}_{\mathbf T}\mathscr R^{\rm cof}_{a,H}.
\]

The Q4562 identity is correct, with the residual-prime scope made explicit.

## Proposition 2.1 — cofactor Gauss formula

For every \(p\nmid\mathfrak U_H\),

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H})
 =\sum_\lambda\mu_\lambda f_\lambda^{\rm res}
   \left(\sum_{r\in I_a(H)}v_{\lambda,r}
          -\max_{r\in I_a(H)}v_{\lambda,r}\right).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
 K_{\lambda,m}(a;H)
 =\#\{r\in I_a(H):v_{\lambda,r}\ge m\},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H})
 =\sum_\lambda\mu_\lambda f_\lambda^{\rm res}
   \sum_{m\ge1}(K_{\lambda,m}(a;H)-1)_+.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Let \(d_a=\deg N_a\) and

\[
 D_{a,H}=\max_{r\in I_a(H)}\deg P_{a,r}.
\]

The pure coefficient of the resultant is

\[
 [T_r^{d_a}]\mathscr R^{\rm cof}_{a,H}
 =V_a^{D_{a,H}-\deg P_{a,r}}
   \operatorname{Res}_Y(\widetilde N_a,P_{a,r}).
\]

By translation invariance and resultant multiplicativity,

\[
 \operatorname{Res}_Y(\widetilde N_a,P_{a,r})
 =\pm\prod_{\substack{s\in I_a(H)\\s\ne r}}S_{a,s},
 \qquad
 S_{a,s}=\operatorname{Res}_X(N_a(X),N_s(X+a)).
\]

Every \(S_{a,s}\ne0\) by the canonical root-strip First Lemma.  Thus the cofactor resultant is nonzero.  The possible generic-degree padding contributes only powers of \(V_a\), which are \(p\)-adic units at residual primes.

Over \(\mathbb Q_p\), multiplicativity over the normalized factors gives, up to a Gauss unit,

\[
 \mathscr R^{\rm cof}_{a,H}(\mathbf T)
 =\prod_\lambda
   \operatorname N_{K_\lambda/\mathbb Q_p}
    \bigl(Q_{a,H}(y_\lambda;\mathbf T)\bigr)^{\mu_\lambda}.
\]

At one branch,

\[
 Q_{a,H}(y_\lambda;\mathbf T)
 =\sum_rT_r\prod_{s\ne r}N_s(y_\lambda).
\]

Because the \(T_r\) are independent monomials, its multivariate Gauss valuation is

\[
 \min_r\sum_{s\ne r}v_{\lambda,s}
 =\sum_sv_{\lambda,s}-\max_sv_{\lambda,s}.
\]

There is no cancellation issue here.  After scaling each factor to Gauss norm one, the reductions are nonzero polynomials in the integral domain \(\mathbb F_p[\mathbf T]\); hence the multivariate Gauss norm is multiplicative exactly as in the canonical one-variable proof.

Taking norms contributes \(f_\lambda^{\rm res}\), not \(e_\lambda f_\lambda^{\rm res}\), because the \(e_\lambda\) in the number of conjugates cancels the \(1/e_\lambda\) from normalized valuation.  Multiplicity \(\mu_\lambda\) is retained.  Finally, the valuation of the integer multivariable content is the minimum valuation of the coefficients.  This proves (2.1).

For (2.2), use

\[
 \sum_rv_{\lambda,r}=\sum_{m\ge1}K_{\lambda,m},
 \qquad
 \max_rv_{\lambda,r}
 =\sum_{m\ge1}\mathbf1_{K_{\lambda,m}>0}.
\]

Thus

\[
 \sum_rv_{\lambda,r}-\max_rv_{\lambda,r}
 =\sum_{m\ge1}(K_{\lambda,m}-1)_+.
\]

This proves the proposition.  ∎

### Audit consequences

- **Singleton branches are exact zeros.**  If exactly one \(r\) has positive valuation, even of arbitrarily large thickness \(M\), then \(K_{\lambda,m}=1\) for every \(m\le M\), so the cofactor valuation is zero.
- **Two-return branches are retained with full thickness.**  If exactly two levels have valuations \(u,v>0\), the branch contributes \(\min(u,v)\).
- **Repeated characteristic-zero factors are retained.**  The factor \(\mu_\lambda\) is outside the layer cake and is never squarefree-reduced.
- **Residue degree is retained.**  The factor \(f_\lambda^{\rm res}\) is exactly the norm multiplicity.
- **Ramification is retained in normalization.**  Writing the branch weight as \(\mu e f\) times normalized thickness is equivalent to (2.1).
- **Primes in \(\mathfrak U_H\) do not need the formula.**  The star deletes their complete prime-power part.  An unqualified statement of (2.1) at structural primes would be stronger than what is justified and is not used.

No explicit counterexample to (2.1) exists in the audited interfaces because the identity is exact.

 \mathcal E_{\rm cof}(H)

Then

\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal E_{\rm cof}(H)
 \le
 \mathcal B_{\rm off}(H)+\mathcal B_{\rm pal}(H)

 +\sum_{\substack{2a+b\le H\\b\ {m even}}}
   \log|\Xi_{a,b}^*|,}
\tag{3.1}

and

\boxed{
 \mathcal B_{\rm off}(H)+\mathcal B_{\rm pal}(H)

 \sum_{3a\le H}\log|S_{a,a}^*|\ll H^3\log H,
\tag{3.3}

and

\[
 \sum_{\substack{2a+b\le H\\b\ {m even}}}
 \log|\Xi_{a,b}^*|\ll H^3\log H.
\tag{3.4}

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal E_{\rm cof}(H)-O(H^3\log H)
 \le\mathcal B_{\rm off}(H)+\mathcal B_{\rm pal}(H)
 \le2\mathcal E_{\rm cof}(H).}
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Proof

Fix \((a,p,\lambda,m)\), and let

\[
 R_m=\{r\in I_a(H):v_{\lambda,r}\ge m\},
 \qquad K_m=|R_m|.
\]

At depth \(m\), the cofactor contributes \((K_m-1)_+\).

Every nonlargest \(b\in R_m\) has a later member.  If some later \(r\in R_m\) satisfies \(r-b\ne a\), then the off active mass charges \(b\) at depth \(m\).  If no such later member exists, the **only** later member is \(b+a\); hence \(b\) is the penultimate member of \(R_m\).

If \(b\ne a\) and

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_\lambda N_b^\circ(y_\lambda)\ge m,
\]

then the palindromic mass charges that penultimate edge.  The only failures are therefore:

1. \(b=a\), which is the all-equal edge \((a,2a)\);
2. \(b\) even and the valuation at depth \(m\) is supplied by the removed factor
   \[
   L_b(Y)=2Y+b+1
   \]
   rather than by \(N_b^\circ\).

There is at most one such missing penultimate member at each layer.

The all-equal missing depth is at most

\[
 \min(v_{\lambda,a},v_{\lambda,2a})\le v_{\lambda,a}.
\]

After multiplying by \(\mu_\lambda f_\lambda^{\rm res}\), summing branches and residual primes, this is bounded by the starred endpoint resultant \(S_{a,a}^*\), giving (3.3).

For the center defect, the number of missing layers is at most

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_\lambda L_b(y_\lambda).
\]

The norm formula gives its total residual valuation as \(v_p(\Xi_{a,b})\).  The linear-root formula and reflection give

\[
 |\Xi_{a,b}|
 =2^{3(a-1)}
  \left|N_a\!\left(\frac{b-1}{2}\right)\right|.
\]

The tridiagonal determinant expression for \(N_a\), with \(a+b\le H\), gives

\[
 \log|\Xi_{a,b}|\ll a\log(a+b).
\]

Therefore

\[
 \sum_{\substack{2a+b\le H\\b\ {m even}}}
  \log|\Xi_{a,b}^*|
 \ll\sum_{a\le H/2}a(H-2a)\log H
 \ll H^3\log H,
\]

which proves (3.4) and then (3.1).

For the reverse inequality, the off layer can charge only a nonlargest member of \(R_m\), so

\[
 A_{\lambda,m}^{\rm off}\le(K_m-1)_+.
\]

The palindromic edges \(b\mapsto b+a\) form a disjoint union of increasing chains on the ordered set \(R_m\).  A forest on \(K_m\) vertices has at most \(K_m-1\) edges, and deflation can only delete edges.  Thus

\[
 A_{\lambda,m}^{\rm pal}\le(K_m-1)_+.
\]

Adding and summing over \(m,\lambda,p,a\) proves (3.2).  ∎

# 4. New exact Smith compression: adjacent edge covers

The cofactor identity has one additional source-native compression that Q4562 did not exploit.

Let \(I=I_a(H)\).  Choose any edge cover \(\mathscr M_a\) of the path on \(I\), where every edge has the form

\[
 e=\{r,r+1\}.
\]

For example, use disjoint adjacent pairs and, if \(|I|\) is odd, overlap the final pair so that every vertex is covered.  If \(|I|=1\), use the convention below that the edge-cover content is \(1\).

For \(e\in\mathscr M_a\), put

\[
 P^{[2]}_{a,e}(Y)
 =\prod_{s\in I\setminus e}N_s(Y),
\]

\[
 Q^{[2]}_{a,H}(Y;\mathbf U)
 =\sum_{e\in\mathscr M_a}U_eP^{[2]}_{a,e}(Y),
\]

and

\[
 \mathfrak K^{[2]}_{a,H}
 =\operatorname{cont}_{\mathbf U}
   \operatorname{Res}_Y
    \bigl(\widetilde N_a(Y),Q^{[2]}_{a,H}(Y;\mathbf U)\bigr).
\]

## Theorem 4.1 — adjacent-edge-cover equality

For every \(a,H\),

\[
\boxed{
 (\mathfrak K^{[2]}_{a,H})^*=\mathfrak K_{a,H}^*.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(p\nmid\mathfrak U_H\) and one normalized branch.  Write \(v_r=v_{\lambda,r}\).  Since adjacent levels cannot both vanish modulo the branch residue field,

\[
 v_r>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_{r+1}=0.
\]

Therefore, for every adjacent edge \(e=\{r,r+1\}\),

\[
 \sum_{s\in e}v_s\le\max_{t\in I}v_t.
\]

Choose an index \(r_*\) where \(v_{r_*}=\max_tv_t\).  Because \(\mathscr M_a\) covers \(I\), some edge \(e_*\) contains \(r_*\).  Its other endpoint has valuation zero, hence

\[
 \sum_{s\in e_*}v_s=\max_tv_t.
\]

The multivariate Gauss valuation of the edge-cover polynomial at the branch is therefore

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_G(Q^{[2]}_{a,H}(y_\lambda;\mathbf U))
 &=\min_{e\in\mathscr M_a}
   \sum_{s\notin e}v_s\\
 &=\sum_sv_s-\max_{e\in\mathscr M_a}\sum_{s\in e}v_s\\
 &=\sum_sv_s-\max_sv_s.
 \end{aligned}
\]

This is exactly the branch Gauss valuation in Proposition 2.1.  Restoring \(\mu_\lambda f_\lambda^{\rm res}\) gives

\[
 v_p(\mathfrak K^{[2]}_{a,H})=v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H})
 \qquad(p\nmid\mathfrak U_H).
\]

At primes dividing \(\mathfrak U_H\), both starred integers delete the complete prime-power part.  Hence all prime valuations of the two starred integers agree, proving (4.1).  ∎

This is a genuine Smith-minor compression.  Locally, (CF) is the valuation of the \((|I|-1)\)-st determinantal divisor of

\[
 \operatorname{diag}(N_r(y_\lambda))_{r\in I}.
\]

The residual adjacent-resultant theorem permits replacement by selected \((|I|-2)\)-minors obtained by deleting adjacent pairs, with no loss whatsoever after star.

This is the strongest unconditional “free” Smith compression I can derive from the current structural carrier.

\[
 x,\quad x+a,\quad x+a+b,\quad x+a+b+c,
 \qquad a,b,c\ge2,\quad a+b+c\le H.
\]

Set

\[
 y=x+a.
\]

The gap identities give

\[
 \widetilde N_a(y)=N_a(x)=0,
 \qquad
 N_b(y)=0,
 \qquad
 N_{b+c}(y)=0
 \pmod p.
\]

## Proposition 5.1 — direct cofactor quadruple certificate

Let \(Q_p(H)\) be the canonical number of selected quadruples.  For every \(p\nmid\mathfrak U_H\),

\[
\boxed{
 Q_p(H)
 \le\sum_{a=2}^{H-2}v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H}).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The same inequality holds with \(\mathfrak K^{[2]}_{a,H}\) in place of \(\mathfrak K_{a,H}\).

### Proof

Fix \(a\), and consider all selected starts having first gap \(a\).  Their translated starts \(y=x+a\) are distinct modulo \(p\).

For one such \(y\), every single cofactor

\[
 P_{a,r}(y)=\prod_{s\ne r}N_s(y)
\]

vanishes: if \(r=b\), the product contains \(N_{b+c}(y)=0\); if \(r=b+c\), it contains \(N_b(y)=0\); otherwise it contains both.  Hence \(y\) is a common root of \(\widetilde N_a\) and every coefficient polynomial of \(Q_{a,H}\).

Equivalently, over the Gauss residue field \(\mathbb F_p(\mathbf T)\), it is a common root of

\[
 \widetilde N_a(Y),
 \qquad
 Q_{a,H}(Y;\mathbf T).
\]

The fixed characteristic-zero-size Sylvester map therefore has corank at least the number of those distinct translated starts.  The exact same Smith-normal-form argument as `lem:qc-aligned` yields

\[
 \#\{\text{selected starts with first gap }a\}
 \le v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H}).
\]

Sum over \(a\).

For the edge-cover version, an adjacent edge cannot contain both \(b\) and \(b+c\), since \(c\ge2\).  Thus every edge cofactor still contains at least one of the two zero factors, so the same evaluation argument applies.  Theorem 4.1 then gives the same valuation.  ∎

### Crucial advantages

This direct certificate:

- needs **no** \(H^{2/3}\) column-reuse factor;
- needs **no** palindromic deflation, because it uses the raw inner levels \(N_b,N_{b+c}\);
- needs **no** all-equal removal, because an all-equal quadruple still supplies two distinct inner zero levels \(a,2a\);
- automatically retains multiplicity and \(p\)-adic thickness through the integer content;
- is valid for the canonical selected quadruples themselves, not merely arbitrary projective branches.

The deflated aligned mass remains useful as a finer transverse object, but it is no longer necessary to route a cofactor-height theorem through \(\mathcal A_{\rm tr}\) before using it in the vertical corank consumer.

 \gamma=4-\delta.

For \(0\le\delta\le1\), so \(3\le\gamma\le4\), choose the low/high cutoff

 T_0=X^{(\gamma-1)/(\gamma+1)}.

 \frac{XT_0}{\log X}
 =X^{2\gamma/(\gamma+1)+o(1)},

 (X/T_0)^\gamma
 =X^{2\gamma/(\gamma+1)}.

 2-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}
 =\frac{\gamma+3}{\gamma+1}
 \le\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}
 \qquad(\gamma\ge3).

\boxed{
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)
 \ll
 X^{\frac{2(4-\delta)}{5-\delta}+o(1)}
 =X^{\frac{8-2\delta}{5-\delta}+o(1)}.}
\tag{6.1}

The associated first-zero-moment parameter \(c\), defined by exponent \(1+c\), is

\tag{6.2}

# 7. Unconditional height: why the current proof remains \(H^4\log H\)

The pure coefficient calculation from Proposition 2.1 gives, for every fixed \(r_0\in I_a(H)\),

\[
 \mathfrak K_{a,H}^*
 \mid
 \prod_{\substack{s\in I_a(H)\\s\ne r_0}}S_{a,s}^*.
\]

Using

\[
 \log|S_{a,s}|\ll(a+s)^2\log(a+s),
\]

one gets

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E_{\rm cof}(H)
 &\le
 \sum_{a=2}^{H-2}\sum_{s=2}^{H-a}\log|S_{a,s}|\\
 &\ll
 \sum_{t\le H}(t-3)t^2\log t\\
 &\ll H^4\log H.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The edge-cover compression changes the number of omitted endpoint factors from one to two, but that is only a constant-size deletion and cannot change the exponent by itself.

The important point is stronger: the characteristic-zero pure coefficients are not merely bounded *poorly* at scale \(H^4\); they are genuinely that large.

## Proposition 7.1 — endpoint-resultant lower bound

For \(a,s\ge2\),

\[
\boxed{
 |S_{a,s}|
 >V_a^{3(s-1)}V_s^{3(a-1)}
 >33^{6(a-1)(s-1)}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof

The root-strip theorem places the roots \(\alpha\) of \(N_a(X)\) in

\[
 -a<\operatorname{Re}\alpha<-1,
\]

and the roots \(\beta\) of \(N_s(X+a)\) in

\[
 -a-s<\operatorname{Re}\beta<-a-1.
\]

Hence

\[
 |\alpha-\beta|
 \ge|\operatorname{Re}(\alpha-\beta)|>1.
\]

The root-product formula therefore gives

\[
 |S_{a,s}|
 =V_a^{3(s-1)}V_s^{3(a-1)}
  \prod_{\alpha,\beta}|\alpha-\beta|
 >V_a^{3(s-1)}V_s^{3(a-1)}.
\]

The Lucas sequence satisfies

\[
 V_{n+1}=34V_n-V_{n-1},
 \qquad V_1=1,\quad V_2=34.
\]

Since \(V_n>V_{n-1}>0\), one has

\[
 V_{n+1}>33V_n,
\]

and therefore \(V_n>33^{n-1}\).  Substitute this into the previous bound.  ∎

For balanced \(a\asymp H\), a pure edge-cover coefficient is a product of \(S_{a,s}\) over all but two of \(\asymp H\) values of \(s\).  Proposition 7.1 gives logarithmic size \(\gg H^3\) for each such \(a\), and summing over \(\asymp H\) balanced values of \(a\) gives \(\gg H^4\).

This is a rigorous **scoped height obstruction**:

> no proof that bounds \(\mathfrak K_{a,H}^*\) by one pure coefficient and then estimates that coefficient by Hadamard/root height can produce a fixed power saving.

It is **not** a lower bound for \(\mathfrak K_{a,H}^*\): the gcd of the mixed coefficients may be much smaller than every individual coefficient, and star may remove large structural parts.  CFH is precisely the assertion that this common divisor is smaller on average.  No theorem in the canonical source supplies that compression.

# 8. Why the Smith compression stops at nonadjacent returns

The adjacent-edge-cover theorem removes two adjacent factors because residual branches cannot vanish at adjacent levels.  Trying to iterate this using the continuant recurrence immediately introduces a genuine nonadjacent return multiplier.

Let

\[
 A_I(Y)=\prod_{s\in I}N_s(Y),
 \qquad
 D_r(Y)=\frac{A_I(Y)}{N_r(Y)N_{r+1}(Y)}.
\]

The recurrence

\[
 N_{r+2}(Y)
 =P(Y+r+1)N_{r+1}(Y)-(Y+r+1)^6N_r(Y)
\]

gives the exact polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{
 D_r(Y)+(Y+r+1)^6D_{r+1}(Y)
 =P(Y+r+1)
   \frac{A_I(Y)}{N_r(Y)N_{r+2}(Y)}.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Since

\[
 P(Y+r+1)=N_2(Y+r),
\]

one step of elimination replaces adjacent-pair cofactors by a nonadjacent-pair cofactor multiplied by a shifted gap polynomial.  Longer continuant/Bézout elimination introduces the corresponding shifted \(N_d\).

Those multipliers are not residual units in general.

# 10. Discriminant/different route: what is and is not available

A tempting plan is to charge a valuation-thick two-return component to ramification, the different of the outer branch, or the discriminants of the individual column polynomials.  That implication is false in local algebra before one uses anything Apéry-specific.

Let \(R=\mathbb Z_p\) with \(p\) odd and take

\[
 F(Y)=Y,
 \qquad
 G_1(Y)=Y-p^M,
 \qquad
 G_2(Y)=Y+p^M.

At the unique outer branch \(Y=0\),

\[
 v_p(G_1(0))=v_p(G_2(0))=M.
\]

The two-return cofactor pencil is

\[
 Q(Y;T_1,T_2)=T_1G_2(Y)+T_2G_1(Y),
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{Res}_Y(F,Q)=p^M(T_1-T_2).
\]

Thus the cofactor content has valuation \(M\), even though \(F,G_1,G_2\) are all simple and unramified and their individual discriminants/differents are units.  The thickness lies only in the **cross-distance**

\[
 \operatorname{Res}(G_1,G_2)=2p^M.
\]

Therefore an individual-discriminant/different theorem cannot control CFH.  It would have to be strengthened to a weighted cross-intersection theorem.  For the Apéry family the natural characteristic-zero carrier for those cross intersections is again the family of separated resultants, whose direct height is \(H^4\log H\).

The canonical source also warns that the normalization length cannot in general be replaced by the quotient length in the nonnormal order \(\mathbb Z_p[Y]/(F)\).  Its explicit example

\[
 F=Y^2-\varpi^2,
 \qquad G=Y-\varpi,
 \qquad J=Y+\varpi

has normalized branch/content length \(2\), while the quotient in the order has length \(1\).  Thus an order-level Smith/Fitting computation needs a conductor/discriminant correction before it can even equal the required mass.  No canonical theorem currently supplies such a correction together with a sub-\(H^4\) global height bound.

This is a scoped no-go for **individual** discriminant/different charging, not a theorem that no deeper arithmetic intersection method can work.

\subsubsection{The cofactor divisor and its direct quadruple certificate}

For an ambient height $H$ and $2\le a\le H-2$, put

 I_a(H)=\{r\in\mathbb Z:2\le r\le H-a\},
 \qquad
 \widetilde N_a(Y)=N_a(Y-a).

 P_{a,r}(Y)

With independent variables

 Q_{a,H}(Y;\mathbf T)
 =\sum_{r\in I_a(H)}T_rP_{a,r}(Y),
\]

 \mathscr R^{\rm cof}_{a,H}(\mathbf T)

   \bigl(\widetilde N_a(Y),Q_{a,H}(Y;\mathbf T)\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak K_{a,H}

\]
Relative to $H$, let $\mathfrak K_{a,H}^*$ be obtained by deleting

\begin{lemma}[Cofactor Gauss formula]
\label{lem:qc-cofactor-gauss}
The polynomial $\mathscr R^{\rm cof}_{a,H}$ is nonzero.  Fix
$p\nmid\mathfrak U_H$ and factor the monic normalization of
$\widetilde N_a$ over $\mathbb Q_p$ as
\[
 V_a^{-1}\widetilde N_a
 =\prod_\lambda f_\lambda^{\mu_\lambda}.
\]
Choose a root $y_\lambda$, let $e_\lambda$ and
$f_\lambda^{\rm res}$ be its ramification index and residue degree,
and normalize
$\operatorname{ord}_\lambda(p)=e_\lambda$.  Put
\[
 v_{\lambda,r}=\operatorname{ord}_\lambda N_r(y_\lambda),
 \qquad
 K_{\lambda,m}(a;H)
 =\#\{r\in I_a(H):v_{\lambda,r}\ge m\}.
\]
Then
\[
 v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H})
 =\sum_\lambda\mu_\lambda f_\lambda^{\rm res}
  \left(\sum_{r\in I_a(H)}v_{\lambda,r}
       -\max_{r\in I_a(H)}v_{\lambda,r}\right)
\]
and equivalently
\[
 v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H})
 =\sum_\lambda\mu_\lambda f_\lambda^{\rm res}
   \sum_{m\ge1}(K_{\lambda,m}(a;H)-1)_+.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $d_a=\deg N_a$ and
$D_{a,H}=\max_r\deg P_{a,r}$.  For every $r\in I_a(H)$,
\[
 [T_r^{d_a}]\mathscr R^{\rm cof}_{a,H}
 =V_a^{D_{a,H}-\deg P_{a,r}}
  \operatorname{Res}_Y(\widetilde N_a,P_{a,r})
 =\pm V_a^{D_{a,H}-\deg P_{a,r}}
   \prod_{\substack{s\in I_a(H)\\s\ne r}}S_{a,s},
\]
which is nonzero by the First Lemma.  Hence the cofactor resultant is
nonzero.

For $p\nmid\mathfrak U_H$, $V_a$ is a unit and every $y_\lambda$ is
integral.  Resultant multiplicativity over the normalized branches gives,
up to a Gauss unit,
\[
 \mathscr R^{\rm cof}_{a,H}(\mathbf T)
 =\prod_\lambda
  \operatorname N_{K_\lambda/\mathbb Q_p}
   \bigl(Q_{a,H}(y_\lambda;\mathbf T)\bigr)^{\mu_\lambda}.
\]
Because the $T_r$ are independent,
\[
 v_G\bigl(Q_{a,H}(y_\lambda;\mathbf T)\bigr)
 =\min_r\sum_{s\ne r}v_{\lambda,s}
 =\sum_sv_{\lambda,s}-\max_sv_{\lambda,s}.
\]
There are $e_\lambda f_\lambda^{\rm res}$ conjugates, each with
normalized valuation divided by $e_\lambda$, so the norm contributes
$f_\lambda^{\rm res}$ times the displayed integral valuation.  Finally
\[
 \sum_rv_{\lambda,r}=\sum_{m\ge1}K_{\lambda,m},
 \qquad
 \max_rv_{\lambda,r}
 =\sum_{m\ge1}\mathbf1_{K_{\lambda,m}>0},
\]
which gives the layer-cake form.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Adjacent edge-cover compression]
\label{lem:qc-cofactor-edge-cover}
Assume $|I_a(H)|\ge2$, and choose any edge cover $\mathscr M_a$ of the
path on $I_a(H)$ by adjacent pairs $e=\{r,r+1\}$.  Put
\[
 P^{[2]}_{a,e}(Y)
 =\prod_{s\in I_a(H)\setminus e}N_s(Y),
\qquad
 Q^{[2]}_{a,H}(Y;\mathbf U)
 =\sum_{e\in\mathscr M_a}U_eP^{[2]}_{a,e}(Y),
\]
\[
 \mathfrak K^{[2]}_{a,H}
 =\operatorname{cont}_{\mathbf U}
  \operatorname{Res}_Y
   (\widetilde N_a,Q^{[2]}_{a,H}).
\]
Then
\[
 (\mathfrak K^{[2]}_{a,H})^*=\mathfrak K_{a,H}^*.
\]
For $|I_a(H)|=1$, both starred contents are $1$ under the natural
convention.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $p\nmid\mathfrak U_H$ and one normalized branch, and write
$v_r=\operatorname{ord}_\lambda N_r(y_\lambda)$.  The exact adjacent
resultant formula implies that $v_r$ and $v_{r+1}$ cannot both be
positive.  Hence every adjacent edge $e$ satisfies
\[
 \sum_{s\in e}v_s\le\max_tv_t.
\]
An edge containing an index of maximal valuation has equality, because
the other endpoint has valuation zero.  Since the edge cover contains
such an edge,
\[
 \min_{e\in\mathscr M_a}\sum_{s\notin e}v_s
 =\sum_sv_s-\max_sv_s.
\]
The Gauss/norm calculation of Lemma~\ref{lem:qc-cofactor-gauss} therefore
gives
$v_p(\mathfrak K^{[2]}_{a,H})=v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H})$ for every
$p\nmid\mathfrak U_H$.  Structural prime powers are deleted from both
sides, proving the starred equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Direct cofactor quadruple certificate]
\label{prop:qc-cofactor-certificate}
Let an odd prime $p$ be fixed, partition $[0,p-1]$ into the canonical
blocks of length at most $H$, and select consecutive quadruples of zeros
inside each block.  Let $Q_p(H)$ be the total number selected.  If
$p\nmid\mathfrak U_H$, then
\[
 Q_p(H)\le\sum_{a=2}^{H-2}v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H}).
\]
The same inequality holds with $\mathfrak K^{[2]}_{a,H}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A selected quadruple with gaps $(a,b,c)$ and start $x$ gives, after
putting $y=x+a$,
\[
 \widetilde N_a(y)=N_a(x)=0,
 \qquad N_b(y)=N_{b+c}(y)=0\pmod p.
\]
For every $r\in I_a(H)$, the cofactor $P_{a,r}(y)$ contains at least
one of the two zero factors $N_b(y),N_{b+c}(y)$, and hence vanishes.
Thus $y$ is a common root of $\widetilde N_a$ and every coefficient of
$Q_{a,H}$.  Distinct selected starts with the same first gap give
distinct translated roots.  The fixed-size Sylvester evaluation argument
and Smith normal form therefore bound their number by
$v_p(\mathfrak K_{a,H})$.  Summing over $a$ proves the first assertion.
For an adjacent edge cofactor, an edge cannot contain both $b$ and
$b+c$ because $c\ge2$, so the same proof applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Unconditional cofactor height]
\label{prop:qc-cofactor-height}
One has
\[
 \sum_{a=2}^{H-2}\log\mathfrak K_{a,H}^*
 \ll H^4\log H.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $r_0\in I_a(H)$, the pure coefficient in
Lemma~\ref{lem:qc-cofactor-gauss} gives, after deleting structural
prime powers,
\[
 \mathfrak K_{a,H}^*
 \mid\prod_{\substack{s\in I_a(H)\\s\ne r_0}}S_{a,s}^*.
\]
The First Lemma's resultant-height estimate
$\log|S_{a,s}|\ll(a+s)^2\log(a+s)$ therefore yields
\[
 \sum_a\log\mathfrak K_{a,H}^*
 \ll\sum_{a+s\le H}(a+s)^2\log(a+s)
 \ll H^4\log H.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pure endpoint coefficients are genuinely large]
\label{lem:qc-endpoint-lower}
For all $a,s\ge2$,
\[
 |S_{a,s}|
 >V_a^{3(s-1)}V_s^{3(a-1)}
 >33^{6(a-1)(s-1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The roots of $N_a(X)$ lie in
$-a<\operatorname{Re}X<-1$, while the roots of $N_s(X+a)$ lie in
$-a-s<\operatorname{Re}X<-a-1$.  Hence every root difference has
absolute value greater than one.  The root-product formula gives the
first inequality.  Since
$V_{n+1}=34V_n-V_{n-1}$, $V_1=1$, $V_2=34$, and $V_n>V_{n-1}>0$,
one has $V_{n+1}>33V_n$ and hence $V_n>33^{n-1}$.  This gives the
second inequality.
\end{proof}
