## 2. Exact moment inequalities

### 2.1 Two-group ANOVA

The exact variance decomposition is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_t(A_q(t)-\overline m_q)^2
={}&
\sum_{t\in\mathcal Z_q}(A_q(t)-m_1(q))^2\\
&+\sum_{t\in\mathcal Z_q^c}(A_q(t)-m_0(q))^2\\
&+G_q\delta_q^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus

\[
G_q\delta_q^2
\le
\sum_t(A_q(t)-\overline m_q)^2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is exactly the existing UVD/second-moment route.

### 2.2 Fourth between-group term

Jensen in the two groups and `(1.9)` give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_t|A_q(t)-\overline m_q|^4
\ge{}&
Z(q)(1-\rho_q)^4\delta_q^4\\
&+(q-Z(q))\rho_q^4\delta_q^4\\
={}&
G_q\bigl((1-\rho_q)^3+\rho_q^3\bigr)\delta_q^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since

\[
(1-\rho)^3+\rho^3
=1-3\rho(1-\rho)\ge\frac14,
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{
G_q\delta_q^4
\le
4\sum_t|A_q(t)-\overline m_q|^4.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

### 2.3 Stronger one-sided domination

If `Delta_q>0`, then `m_0(q)>=0` implies `Delta_q<=m_1(q)`. For every `r>=1`, Jensen gives

\[
Z(q)m_1(q)^r
\le
\sum_{t\in\mathcal Z_q}A_q(t)^r.
\]

Since `G_q<=Z(q)`,

\[
\boxed{
G_q\Delta_q^r
\le
\sum_{t\in\mathcal Z_q}A_q(t)^r
\le
\sum_{t\bmod q}A_q(t)^r.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

After summing,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_r^+(T,J)
:=\sum_qG_q\Delta_q^r
\le
P_r^{\rm pre}(T,J)
:=\sum_{q,t}A_q(t)^r.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

The last inequality is intentionally crude but noncircular. No factor `rho_q` is inserted.

### 2.4 What spacing can and cannot refine

Let

\[
s_q:=\#\{t\in\mathcal Z_q:A_q(t)>0\}.
\]

Hölder gives

\[
M_q^r
\le
s_q^{r-1}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal Z_q}A_q(t)^r,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
G_q\Delta_q^r
\le
(1-\rho_q)
\left(\frac{s_q}{Z(q)}\right)^{r-1}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal Z_q}A_q(t)^r.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

If the support of `A_q` lay in a bounded number of deterministic intervals, `(0.5)` would control `s_q`. The literal support is instead a union of inverse leaves, and the union of candidate windows can be macroscopic. Also `Z(q)` may equal one. Hence `(2.7)` yields no uniform power or logarithmic saving from current spacing information.

## 6. Entropy route

Assume `A_q^0>0` and define the load probability

\[
P_q(t):=\frac{A_q(t)}{A_q^0},
\qquad
s_q:=P_q(\mathcal Z_q)=\frac{M_q}{A_q^0}.
\]

Under the uniform measure on the fibre, `mathcal Z_q` has mass `rho_q`. Data processing for relative entropy gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr H_q^{\rm load}
:=
\sum_tA_q(t)\log\frac{qA_q(t)}{A_q^0}
\ge
A_q^0d(s_q\Vert\rho_q),
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

where

\[
d(s\Vert\rho)
:=s\log\frac{s}{\rho}
+(1-s)\log\frac{1-s}{1-\rho}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_q^0d(s_q\Vert\rho_q)
=q\bigg[{}
&\rho_qm_1(q)\log\frac{m_1(q)}{\overline m_q}\\
&+(1-\rho_q)m_0(q)\log\frac{m_0(q)}{\overline m_q}
\bigg].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Put

\[
a_q:=(M_q-\rho_qA_q^0)_+,
\qquad
D_q:=\rho_qA_q^0,
\qquad
u_q:=\frac{a_q}{D_q}
\]

when `D_q>0`. Then

\[
\nu_q=\frac{(1-\rho_q)\Delta_q}{\overline m_q}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

For `s>=rho`,

\[
(1-s)\log\frac{1-s}{1-\rho}\ge-(s-\rho).
\]

Therefore, with

\[
h(u):=(1+u)\log(1+u)-u,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr H_q^{\rm load}
\ge
D_qh(\nu_q).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Since `h(u)/u` is increasing, splitting at `nu_q=R` gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{APUC}_A^+
\le
R\mathcal D
+
\frac{R}{h(R)}\mathscr H^{\rm load},
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal D:=\sum_q\rho_qA_q^0,
\qquad
\mathscr H^{\rm load}:=\sum_q\mathscr H_q^{\rm load}.
\]

The density mean is already subcritical:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D
\ll_JT^{-1/3}M_0
=T^{28/15}L^{-3/5}
=:D_0.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

For integer `A>=1`, `A log A <= A(A-1)`. Also

\[
A_q^0\log\frac{q}{A_q^0}
\le A_q^0\log q.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr H^{\rm load}
\le
F_2^{\rm pre}+O_J(L)M_1^{\rm pre}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This is noncircular: its right side is entirely pre-upper.

The largest relative cutoff whose low part is at target scale is

\[
R_{\max}:=\frac{B}{D_0}
=T^{2/15}L^{-7/5}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Take `R=R_max/omega(T)` with `omega->infinity` and `omega=T^{o(1)}`. Then the low term is `B/omega`, while

\[
\frac{R}{h(R)}\asymp\frac1L.
\]

Thus the entropy route closes under

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr H^{\rm load}
=o_J(BL)
=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^2}{L}\right).
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

The current bound from `(6.7)` is

\[
\mathscr H^{\rm load}
\ll_J
T^{13/5}L^{-9/5}+T^{11/5}L^{2/5},
\tag{6.10}
\]

which misses `(6.9)`. The leading term exceeds it by `T^{3/5}L^{-4/5}`.

If one chooses `R=R_max/L`, the low part is `B/L`; the high part is approximately `H_load/L`. Therefore

```text
high part o(B):       requires H_load=o(BL);
whole sum O(B/L):     requires H_load=O(B);
whole sum o(B/L):     requires H_load=o(B).
```

This also shows why entropy can buy at most one logarithm: on a nonempty zero fibre, `1/q <= rho_q`, so the maximal relative alignment has logarithm at most `log q=O(L)`.

The second term is a support-selector alignment problem. Current spacing permits `S_q` itself to be a nonconsecutive reflection-invariant subset of `mathcal Z_q`. Thus support-relative entropy only moves the open theorem into a support-level PRE-CUT statement.
