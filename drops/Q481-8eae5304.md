# Q481: Franel and Domb parity defects from twisted reflection

## A. Franel: exact congruence class for \((-8/p)=-1\)

For odd primes \(p\neq2\),

\[
\left(\frac{-8}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)
=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}(-1)^{(p^2-1)/8}.
\]

The two signs are determined modulo \(8\):

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
p\bmod8&(-1/p)&(2/p)&(-8/p)\\
\hline
1&1&1&1\\
3&-1&-1&1\\
5&1&-1&-1\\
7&-1&1&-1
\end{array}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac{-8}{p}\right)=-1\iff p\equiv5,7\pmod8.}
\]

Equivalently, the forced central zero occurs exactly for primes in the two residue classes

\[
p\equiv5,7\pmod8.
\]

The parity law is therefore

\[
|Z_p|\equiv1\pmod2
\]
for those primes, except for the possibility of additional exceptional behavior at the endpoints (small primes or indexing conventions).

For \(p\equiv1,3\pmod8\), the twist fixes the midpoint and no parity obstruction is forced.

## B. Comparison with the Franel data

Among odd primes, the classes \(5,7\pmod8\) have natural density \(1/2\). The reflection argument predicts:

- for about half the primes, the midpoint is forced to be a zero;
- the remaining zeros occur in reflected pairs.

Hence the generic prediction is

\[
|Z_p|=2k+1
\]
for approximately half of all primes, and even otherwise.

The observed Franel statistic was:

\[
157/212=0.7415
\]
odd among primes with \( |Z_p|>0\).

This does not contradict the prediction. The denominator conditions on the event \(Z_p\neq\varnothing\), which is not independent of the central-zero event.

Let

\[
A=\{p:(-8/p)=-1\}.
\]

Then \(A\) has density \(1/2\) among all primes. But if primes with no zeros are removed, the conditional proportion becomes

\[
\Pr(A\mid Z_p\neq\varnothing)
=\frac{\Pr(Z_p\neq\varnothing\mid A)}{\Pr(Z_p\neq\varnothing)}\cdot\frac12.
\]

The forced-central-zero class has probability \(1\) of being nonempty, while the other class can have a substantial empty probability. Thus the conditional odd proportion can be significantly above \(1/2\). A value near \(0.74\) is exactly the qualitative behavior expected.

The correct comparison is therefore with all primes, not only active primes:

\[
\boxed{\text{Franel odd parity density should approach }1/2\text{ among all primes.}}
\]

## C. Domb: the odd case is an artifact

For Domb the twist is

\[
64^{-r}.
\]

At the fixed point

\[
r=(p-1)/2,
\]

we get the multiplier

\[
64^{-(p-1)/2}.
\]

But

\[
64=2^6,
\]

so

\[
\left(\frac{64}{p}\right)=\left(\frac2p\right)^6=1
\]

for every odd prime \(p\). Hence

\[
64^{(p-1)/2}\equiv1\pmod p
\]

always.

Therefore the midpoint is never forced to vanish. Reflection is an honest involution with no fixed-point contribution, so

\[
\boxed{|Z_p|\text{ should always be even.}}
\]

The observed Domb data

\[
119\text{ even},\qquad1\text{ odd}
\]

cannot be explained by the twist. The single odd case must come from one of:

1. an indexing convention mismatch (using \(0\le r<p\) versus a shifted range);
2. including the endpoint \(r=p-1\) where the reflection formula is not the same normalization;
3. a computational bug in the zero-set extraction;
4. a small-prime exceptional case where denominators in the recurrence normalization vanish.

It is not a genuine geometric phenomenon.

A direct diagnostic is:

- compute whether the odd prime has the midpoint \(r=(p-1)/2\) as a zero;
- check whether the claimed reflection is \(r\mapsto p-1-r\) or a shifted involution;
- remove \(p=2\) and any primes dividing the recurrence denominators.

The corrected theorem should be:

\[
\boxed{
Domb:\quad |Z_p|\equiv0\pmod2\quad(p\text{ odd, after removing normalization exceptions}).
}
\]

---

## Structural interpretation

This is a useful contrast between the three situations:

| sequence | reflection multiplier at midpoint | parity consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Apéry \(\zeta(3)\) | \(1\) | even, except central modular-form zeros |
| Franel | \((-8)^{-(p-1)/2}\) | odd for \(p\equiv5,7\pmod8\) |
| Domb | \(64^{-(p-1)/2}=1\) | always even |

The parity is therefore not controlled by the size of the monodromy group. It is controlled by the character twist appearing in the finite-field reflection formula.
