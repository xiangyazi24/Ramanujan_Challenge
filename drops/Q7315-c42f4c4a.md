# Quotient-six C4 plus branch: cold audit of the two-height theorem

## Verdict

The carrier change of variables and the fixed-`(z,r)` two-height divisor bound are correct.  The dyadic estimate

\[
N(D;Z,R)\ll \frac{ZR\min(Z,R)}{\log T}
\]

is therefore valid, uniformly in the `d`-box and with the original carrier multiplicity retained.

There are two corrections to the claimed localization.

1. The transformed exact domain must retain `0 <= z <= 6d-1`.  In fact the divisibility endpoints force
   \[
   1\le z\le 6d-2,\qquad r\ge1,\qquad h=z+r+1\ge3.
   \]
   Moreover, the displayed size inequality itself strengthens `d<T/18` to
   \[
   d<T/21.
   \]
   Hence the plus carrier is empty for `J<=21`.

2. The direct summations prove that every cutoff
   \[
   H(T)=o\!\left(T^{2/3}(\log T)^{-1/3}\right),\qquad
   M(T)=o\!\left((T/\log T)^{1/2}\right)
   \]
   is negligible.  They do **not** prove that the entire regions below the two critical scales themselves are little-`o`.  A correct heavy localization uses the critical scales divided by an arbitrary function tending to infinity.

Thus there is no counterexample to the two-height bound.  The theorem is accepted after repairing the endpoint domain and the quantifiers in the heavy-region conclusion.

## 1. Expand the original plus carrier before changing variables

For one original plus carrier, set

\[
a=6d-1-z,\qquad v=z+7d.
\]

The inherited index condition is

\[
0\le z\le6d-1,
\]

so that `a` is a nonnegative Apéry index.  Expanding the radical in the original plus sum produces one incidence for each prime `q` for which, with `p=q-d`,

\[
\begin{gathered}
p,q\ \text{prime},\qquad p\in(T/7,T/3],\qquad q>v,\\
T/J<d<T/18,\qquad T<7q-1-v\le2T,\\
p\mid b_z,\qquad p\mid b_a,\qquad q\mid b_v.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Any additional canonical masks may simply be intersected with this set; they can only decrease every count below.

For fixed `(d,z)`, the map `q -> p=q-d` is injective.  Consequently the radical counts each admissible `p` once in that carrier.  The same prime may occur in another outer carrier, and is then counted again, exactly as in the original outer sum.  If `X_+(T,J)` denotes this incidence set, then the weighted plus contribution is exactly

\[
C^+(T,J)=\sum_{\xi\in X_+(T,J)}\log p_\xi.
\tag{1.2}
\]

This is the occurrence multiplicity that must be preserved.

## 2. Exact reflection and the carrier bijection

For every prime `ell` and `0<=m<=ell-1`, the Apéry numbers satisfy

\[
b_{\ell-1-m}\equiv b_m\pmod\ell.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For completeness, termwise one has

\[
\binom{\ell-1-m}{k}\equiv(-1)^k\binom{m+k}{k},\qquad
\binom{\ell-1-m+k}{k}\equiv(-1)^k\binom{m}{k}\pmod\ell,
\]

and squaring the two factors in the defining sum gives (2.1).

Given an incidence (1.1), define

\[
h=p-6d,
\qquad
r=h-1-z.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since `q=p+d` and `v=z+7d`,

\[
q-v=p-6d-z=h-z.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The strict inequality `q>v` is therefore equivalent to

\[
h>z,
\]

and, integrally, to `r>=0`.  Equations (2.2) give

\[
p=6d+h,\qquad q=7d+h,\qquad h=z+r+1.
\tag{2.4}
\]

At the prime `p`,

\[
p-1-a
=(6d+h)-1-(6d-1-z)
=z+h.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus exact reflection gives

\[
p\mid b_a\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid b_{z+h}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

At the prime `q`,

\[
q-1-v
=(7d+h)-1-(z+7d)
=h-1-z=r,
\tag{2.7}
\]

so

\[
q\mid b_v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\mid b_r.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Finally,

\[
\begin{aligned}
7q-1-v
&=7(7d+h)-1-(z+7d)\\
&=42d+7h-1-z\\
&=42d+6z+7r+6.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.9}
\]

Hence (1.1) maps to the transformed incidence

\[
\begin{gathered}
r=h-1-z,\qquad p=6d+h,\qquad q=7d+h,\\
p\mid b_z,\ b_{z+h},\qquad q\mid b_r,\\
T<42d+6z+7r+6\le2T,
\end{gathered}
\tag{2.10}
\]

with the prime, interval, and `d` conditions retained.

Conversely, suppose (2.10) holds, together with

\[
0\le z\le6d-1,
\tag{2.11}
\]

and `r>=0`.  Define `a=6d-1-z` and `v=z+7d`.  Then

\[
q-v=r+1>0,
\]

so the original strict condition `q>v` holds.  Equations (2.5) and (2.7), followed by reflection, recover `p|b_a` and `q|b_v`; (2.9) recovers the original size condition.  Every variable is determined in both directions.  Therefore

\[
(d,z,q)\longleftrightarrow(d,h,z,r,p,q)
\tag{2.12}
\]

is a genuine bijection of incidences, not merely a map of prime pairs.

## 3. No wrap, parity, and endpoint audit

### 3.1 All reflected indices are standard, nonwrapping indices

From `r>=0`, one has `h>=z+1>=1`.  Hence

\[
0\le a=6d-1-z\le6d-1<p=6d+h.
\]

Thus `a` lies in `[0,p-1]`, and (2.5) shows directly that `z+h` also lies in `[0,p-1]`.  Also `z<p` because `z<=6d-1<p`.

Similarly, `v>=0` and `q>v` put `v` in `[0,q-1]`; equation (2.7) puts `r` in `[0,q-1]`.  No periodicity, Lucas reduction, or wrapped representative is being used.

### 3.2 The size condition sharpens the `d` range

Because `z,r>=0`, the upper size inequality gives

\[
42d+6\le42d+6z+7r+6\le2T,
\]

and therefore

\[
d<T/21.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is stronger than the inherited `d<T/18`.  The exact nonempty range is consequently

\[
T/J<d<T/21.
\tag{3.2}
\]

In particular, there are no plus incidences when `J<=21`.

For `T>14`, both primes exceed `2`, so they are odd.  Since `q-p=d`, one obtains

\[
2\mid d.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then `h=p-6d` is odd and

\[
z+r=h-1\equiv0\pmod2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

These parity restrictions may be retained in an exact statement.  Dropping them in an upper bound only enlarges the set.

### 3.3 Zero endpoints are empty

Since `b_0=1`:

- `z=0` is impossible because `p|b_z`;
- `r=0` is impossible because `q|b_r`;
- `z=6d-1` gives `a=0`, and is impossible because the original condition includes `p|b_a`.  Equivalently, the reflected index is then `z+h=p-1`, and (2.1) gives `b_{p-1}=b_0=1 (mod p)`.

Thus every actual incidence satisfies

\[
1\le z\le6d-2,\qquad r\ge1,
\qquad h=z+r+1\ge3.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The positive dyadic decomposition may therefore start at `z,r=1`; no axis box is missing.

### 3.4 Central cases do not alter multiplicity

A reflected index can be fixed by the reflection, for example

\[
a=z+h=(p-1)/2
\]

or

\[
v=r=(q-1)/2.
\]

In such a case (2.6) or (2.8) is the same divisibility condition written twice, but the original carrier is still one carrier and the bijection (2.12) still has one image.  There is neither a factor of two nor a division by two.  The diagonal `z=r` is equally harmless.

The two `p`-indices `z` and `z+h` are always distinct because `h>0`.  Also the original indices `z` and `a` cannot coincide: `z=a` would give `2z=6d-1`, impossible over the integers.

## 4. Fixed-`(z,r)` two-height divisor count

Let

\[
m_{T,J}(z,r)
\]

denote the number of transformed incidences with the specified positive pair `(z,r)`, retaining every admissible `d` and all original masks.  Then `h=z+r+1` is fixed.  Distinct values of `d` give distinct primes

\[
p_d=6d+h,\qquad q_d=7d+h.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Every `p_d` is a prime divisor of both

\[
b_z\quad\text{and}\quad b_{z+h}=b_{2z+r+1},
\]

whereas every `q_d` is a prime divisor of `b_r`.  Therefore

\[
\prod_d p_d\mid
\operatorname{rad}\gcd(b_z,b_{2z+r+1}),
\qquad
\prod_d q_d\mid\operatorname{rad}(b_r).
\tag{4.2}
\]

All relevant `p_d` and `q_d` exceed `T/7`.  Taking logarithms gives the exact radical-height inequality

\[
m_{T,J}(z,r)\log(T/7)
\le
\min\!\left\{
\log\operatorname{rad}\gcd(b_z,b_{2z+r+1}),
\log\operatorname{rad}(b_r)
\right\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
m_{T,J}(z,r)
\le
\frac{\min(\log b_z,\log b_r)}{\log(T/7)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The elementary Apéry height bound is

\[
b_n\le(n+1)64^n.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed, for `0<=k<=n`,

\[
\binom nk\le2^n,
\qquad
\binom{n+k}{k}\le2^{n+k}\le2^{2n},
\]

so each summand is at most `2^(6n)` and there are `n+1` summands.  For `n>=1`, `log(n+1)<=n log 2`, hence

\[
\log b_n\le n\log128.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Combining (4.4) and (4.6),

\[
\boxed{
 m_{T,J}(z,r)
 \le
 \frac{\log128}{\log(T/7)}\min(z,r)
 \ll \frac{\min(z,r)}{L}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This proves the fixed-`(z,r)` count.  It uses no independence and does not multiply two divisor densities.  It takes the minimum of two separately valid height bounds.

The proof is occurrence-preserving for the original plus sum because, after `(z,r)` is fixed, `h` is fixed and each admissible `d` determines exactly one `(p,q)` and exactly one original carrier.  If an upstream object carried extra labels not determined by `(d,z,q)`, those labels would require a separate fibre bound; they are not present in the plus carrier described in Q7315.

## 5. Dyadic box theorem

Use half-open positive dyadic boxes

\[
D\le d<2D,\qquad Z\le z<2Z,\qquad R\le r<2R,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and let `N(D;Z,R)` count incidences in their intersection with the exact carrier domain.  There are at most `ZR` integer pairs `(z,r)` in the last two intervals.  For each such pair, (4.7) gives

\[
m_{T,J}(z,r)\ll\frac{\min(Z,R)}{L}.
\]

The trivial number of possible integers `d` in the first interval is `O(D)`.  Thus the slightly stronger box estimate is

\[
\boxed{
N(D;Z,R)
\ll ZR\min\!\left\{D,\frac{\min(Z,R)}{L}\right\}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
N(D;Z,R)\ll\frac{ZR\min(Z,R)}{L}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The constant is absolute apart from any harmless convention at dyadic endpoints.  Restricting by the size inequality, parity, or canonical masks only decreases `N`.

Because (3.2) confines `d` to an interval whose endpoint ratio is at most `J/21`, the number of `D`-boxes is `O(1+log J)`, hence `O_J(1)`.  There is no `log T` loss from summing in `D` for fixed `J`.

Half-open boxes assign every positive integer to exactly one box.  Partial first or last boxes, the diagonal `z=r`, and points on powers of two are therefore neither lost nor duplicated.

## 6. Direct summations and the exact little-`o` thresholds

For a condition `E`, write

\[
I_+(E)=\#\{\xi\in X_+(T,J):E\},
\qquad
W_+(E)=\sum_{\substack{\xi\in X_+(T,J)\\E}}\log p_\xi.
\]

Since `p` lies in `(T/7,T/3]`,

\[
\log(T/7)I_+(E)
\le W_+(E)
\le\log(T/3)I_+(E).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
W_+(E)=o_J(T^2/L)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
I_+(E)=o_J(T^2/L^2).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The exact domain supplies uniform linear ranges.  From (3.1) and (3.5),

\[
z<6T/21=2T/7,
\]

and the upper size inequality gives `r<2T/7`.  Thus both variables are `O(T)`.

### 6.1 Small `h`

Let `H>=3`.  Since `h=z+r+1`, (4.7) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
I_+(h\le H)
&\ll \frac1L
 \sum_{\substack{z,r\ge1\\z+r+1\le H}}\min(z,r)\\
&\ll\frac{H^3}{L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For example,

\[
\sum_{1\le z,r\le H}\min(z,r)
=\sum_{k=1}^{H}(H-k+1)^2
\ll H^3.
\]

By (6.1),

\[
W_+(h\le H)\ll H^3.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Define

\[
H_{\mathrm c}=T^{2/3}L^{-1/3}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Then the ratio of (6.4) to the weighted target is

\[
\frac{H^3}{T^2/L}
=\left(\frac{H}{H_{\mathrm c}}\right)^3.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
H=o(H_{\mathrm c})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W_+(h\le H)=o_J(T^2/L),
\tag{6.7}
\]

or equivalently `I_+(h<=H)=o_J(T^2/L^2)`.  The logarithmic exponent `-1/3` is correct.

### 6.2 Small `min(z,r)`

Let `M>=1`, and let `X` be any fixed constant multiple of `T` containing both full coordinate ranges, for example `X=2T/7`.  For fixed `z<=M`,

\[
\sum_{1\le r\le X}\min(z,r)
\le Xz.
\]

Hence, using the union bound for `z<=M` or `r<=M`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
I_+(\min(z,r)\le M)
&\ll\frac1L
 \left(
 \sum_{z\le M}\sum_{r\le X}\min(z,r)
 +
 \sum_{r\le M}\sum_{z\le X}\min(z,r)
 \right)\\
&\ll\frac{TM^2}{L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus

\[
W_+(\min(z,r)\le M)\ll TM^2.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Define

\[
M_{\mathrm c}=(T/L)^{1/2}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

The ratio to the weighted target is

\[
\frac{TM^2}{T^2/L}
=\left(\frac{M}{M_{\mathrm c}}\right)^2.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Therefore

\[
M=o(M_{\mathrm c})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W_+(\min(z,r)\le M)=o_J(T^2/L),
\tag{6.12}
\]

or equivalently `I_+(min(z,r)<=M)=o_J(T^2/L^2)`.  The logarithmic exponent `-1/2` is correct.

### 6.3 There are no hidden dyadic logarithms

Starting instead from (5.3) gives the same estimates without any extra logarithm.  In the region `Z<=R`, a box contributes `O(Z^2R/L)`.  Geometric dyadic summation gives

\[
\sum_{R\ll H}R\sum_{Z\le R}Z^2\ll H^3
\]

for the small-`h` region, and

\[
\sum_{R\ll T}R\sum_{Z\ll M}Z^2\ll TM^2
\]

for the small-minimum region.  One must sum dyadic side lengths geometrically, rather than bound every box by the largest box and multiply by the number of boxes.  The latter crude procedure would insert spurious logarithms.

## 7. Correct heavy-region localization

The statements (6.7) and (6.12) quantify over cutoff functions.  They do not license deletion at the critical endpoints themselves.

Let `omega(T)->infinity`, arbitrarily slowly, and set

\[
H_0(T)=\left\lfloor\frac{H_{\mathrm c}}{\omega(T)}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
M_0(T)=\left\lfloor\frac{M_{\mathrm c}}{\omega(T)}\right\rfloor.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For the light region

\[
\mathcal L_\omega
=\{h\le H_0\}
 \cup\{z\le M_0\}
 \cup\{r\le M_0\},
\tag{7.2}
\]

(6.3) and (6.8) give

\[
I_+(\mathcal L_\omega)
\ll_J
\frac{T^2}{L^2}
\left(\omega(T)^{-3}+\omega(T)^{-2}\right)
=o_J(T^2/L^2),
\tag{7.3}
\]

and therefore

\[
W_+(\mathcal L_\omega)
\ll_J
\frac{T^2}{L}
\left(\omega(T)^{-3}+\omega(T)^{-2}\right)
=o_J(T^2/L).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus, modulo an admissible little-`o` error, the exact remaining region is

\[
\boxed{
 h>\frac{T^{2/3}L^{-1/3}}{\omega(T)},
 \qquad
 z>\frac{(T/L)^{1/2}}{\omega(T)},
 \qquad
 r>\frac{(T/L)^{1/2}}{\omega(T)}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Floors and `+1` endpoint changes are harmless and can be retained exactly as in (7.1)-(7.2).

What is **not** proved is the stronger deletion

\[
h\le cH_{\mathrm c}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\min(z,r)\le cM_{\mathrm c}
\]

for a fixed positive `c`.  At those cutoffs, (6.6) and (6.11) give only `O_J(T^2/L)` weighted contribution, not little-`o`.

This endpoint failure is real at the level of the available majorant.  For example, an abstract multiplicity array

\[
\mu(z,r)=\left\lfloor c\frac{\min(z,r)}{L}\right\rfloor
\]

on `z+r+1<=H_c` obeys the fixed-pair bound but has total mass comparable to

\[
H_{\mathrm c}^3/L=T^2/L^2.
\]

This is not asserted to arise from Apéry primes; it shows that the two-height theorem alone cannot turn the critical endpoint from `O` into `o`.

The precise qualitative consequence is: a non-negligible contribution cannot be confined to `h=o(H_c)` or to `min(z,r)=o(M_c)`.  It is not legitimate to replace that statement by fixed-constant lower bounds at `H_c` and `M_c`.

## 8. Commit-ready corrected theorem

### Proposition (plus two-height divisor bound and subcritical localization)

Fix `J` and let `T` tend to infinity.  Let `X_+(T,J)` be the incidence expansion of the quotient-six plus carrier with

\[
a=6d-1-z,\qquad v=z+7d,
\]

and conditions (1.1).  For every incidence put

\[
h=p-6d,\qquad r=h-1-z.
\]

Then:

1. The map
   \[
   (d,z,q)\mapsto(d,h,z,r,p,q)
   \]
   is a bijection onto the tuples satisfying
   \[
   \begin{gathered}
   h=z+r+1,\quad p=6d+h,\quad q=7d+h,\\
   p\mid b_z,b_{z+h},\quad q\mid b_r,\\
   T<42d+6z+7r+6\le2T,
   \end{gathered}
   \]
   together with the original prime/range/mask conditions and `0<=z<=6d-1`, `r>=0`.

2. Every nonempty tuple satisfies
   \[
   1\le z\le6d-2,\qquad r\ge1,\qquad h\ge3,\qquad d<T/21.
   \]
   For sufficiently large `T`, also `2|d`, `h` is odd, and `z` and `r` have the same parity.

3. For fixed positive `(z,r)`, with `h=z+r+1`, the number `m_{T,J}(z,r)` of incidences is bounded by
   \[
   m_{T,J}(z,r)\log(T/7)
   \le
   \min\!\left\{
   \log\operatorname{rad}\gcd(b_z,b_{2z+r+1}),
   \log\operatorname{rad}(b_r)
   \right\},
   \]
   and hence
   \[
   m_{T,J}(z,r)\ll\frac{\min(z,r)}{\log T}.
   \]

4. For positive half-open dyadic boxes,
   \[
   N(D;Z,R)
   \ll ZR\min\!\left\{D,\frac{\min(Z,R)}{\log T}\right\}
   \ll\frac{ZR\min(Z,R)}{\log T}.
   \]

5. Uniformly for cutoffs `H,M>=1`,
   \[
   I_+(h\le H)\ll_J\frac{H^3}{\log T},
   \qquad
   I_+(\min(z,r)\le M)\ll_J\frac{TM^2}{\log T},
   \]
   and, with logarithmic weight,
   \[
   W_+(h\le H)\ll_J H^3,
   \qquad
   W_+(\min(z,r)\le M)\ll_J TM^2.
   \]

6. Consequently, for every `omega(T)->infinity`, the contribution outside
   \[
   h>\frac{T^{2/3}(\log T)^{-1/3}}{\omega(T)},
   \qquad
   z,r>\frac{(T/\log T)^{1/2}}{\omega(T)}
   \]
   is `o_J(T^2/log T)` in logarithmic weight, equivalently `o_J(T^2/log^2 T)` in incidence count.

The same conclusions hold after imposing any of the original canonical masks.

## Final audit conclusion

The claimed fixed-`(z,r)` divisor argument and its dyadic consequence are rigorous.  Reflection is exact, every reflected index is nonwrapping, and the transformation is bijective at the carrier-incidence level.  No occurrence factor is lost, including on central fibres.

The localization should be committed only in the quantified form (7.5).  The phrases `h at least T^(2/3) log^(-1/3) T` and `z,r at least (T/log T)^(1/2)` are too strong if they mean fixed-constant critical lower bounds.  Replace them by the same scales divided by an arbitrary slowly growing `omega(T)`.  Also retain the inherited condition `0<=z<=6d-1`, record the empty axes, and sharpen the exact `d` range to `d<T/21`.
