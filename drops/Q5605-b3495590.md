## 3. Exact CRT programmability

The decisive algebraic fact is elementary.

### Theorem 3.1 -- quotient-residue CRT isomorphism

Let \(R\) be squarefree and \(h\ge1\). The map

\[
 \Phi_h:
 \{B\bmod R^{h+1}:R\mid B\}
 \longrightarrow
 \prod_{p\mid R}\mathbb Z/p^h\mathbb Z,
 \tag{3.1}
\]

\[
 \Phi_h(B)=\left(\frac Bp\bmod p^h\right)_{p\mid R},
\]

is a bijection.

#### Proof

Changing \(B\) by \(R^{h+1}\) changes \(B/p\) by a multiple of \(p^h\), so the map is well defined. If every component is zero, then \(p^{h+1}\mid B\) for every \(p\mid R\), hence \(R^{h+1}\mid B\). Thus the map is injective. Both sides have cardinality \(R^h\), so it is bijective. Equivalently, arbitrary residues \(a_p\bmod p^h\) determine the unique class

\[
 B\equiv pa_p\pmod {p^{h+1}}
 \qquad(p\mid R).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Every representative of this class is divisible by \(R\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 -- exact information content of grade seven

Take \(h=7\) and \(a_p=A_p(n)\). There is a unique \(B_7\in[0,R^8)\) satisfying

\[
 B_7\equiv pA_p(n)\pmod {p^8}
 \qquad(p\mid R).
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Since the actual Apéry number satisfies the same congruences,

\[
 \boxed{B_7=[b_n]_{R^8}.}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Moreover \(R\mid B_7\). Writing \(B_7=RA_7\), one has

\[
 0\le A_7<R^7,
 \qquad
 A_7\equiv R_p^{-1}A_p(n)\pmod {p^7}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Thus \(A_7\) is exactly the CRT representative of the global cofactor \(A=b_n/R\) modulo \(R^7\).

### Corollary 3.3 -- the cross-divisibilities are not extra equations

Choose arbitrary residues \(a_p\in\mathbb Z/p^7\mathbb Z\). Theorem 3.1 produces an integer \(B\), unique modulo \(R^8\), such that

\[
 R\mid B,
 \qquad
 B/p\equiv a_p\pmod {p^7}
 \quad(p\mid R).
 \tag{3.6}
\]

For distinct \(p,q\mid R\), this integer automatically satisfies

\[
 q\mid B/p,
 \qquad
 p\mid B/q.
\]

Therefore the relations \(q\mid x_p\), \(p\mid x_q\) impose no restriction on the residue coordinates \(A_p(n)\). They are exactly the statement that all coordinates come from one integer divisible by \(R\).

This CRT programmability is the requested sharp countermodel for every purely pair-algebraic elimination. It is not a second Apéry sequence; it proves that a successful proof must use an additional global property of the canonical Apéry value.

## 4. Exact two-prime elimination

Fix distinct \(p,q\in T\), and abbreviate

\[
 a_p=A_p(n),
 \qquad
 a_q=A_q(n),
 \qquad
 \rho_p=pa_p,
 \qquad
 \rho_q=qa_q.
\]

Then

\[
 \rho_p=[b_n]_{p^8},
 \qquad
 \rho_q=[b_n]_{q^8}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

### 4.1 Cancelling \(b_n\) loses every target divisor

Put

\[
 E_p=b_n-\rho_p,
 \qquad
 E_q=b_n-\rho_q.
\]

Then

\[
 p^8\mid E_p,
 \qquad
 q^8\mid E_q.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The equal-weight elimination is

\[
 E_p-E_q=\rho_q-ho_p=qa_q-pa_p.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

It has polynomial height:

\[
 |qa_q-pa_p|<2n^8.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

But it has no forced factor \(p\), \(q\), or \(pq\). Theorem 3.1 makes the reason exact: \(a_p,a_q\) are independent CRT coordinates.

The cross-difference may even vanish. In the abstract two-prime CRT model take \(B=pq\). Then

\[
 a_p=q,
 \qquad
 a_q=p,
\]

and (4.3) is zero. Thus nonvanishing is not a formal consequence of the local laws.

### 4.2 A determinant retains \(p^8q^8\), but has full Apéry height

Write

\[
 E_p=p^8u_p,
 \qquad
 E_q=q^8u_q.
\]

Because \(\rho_p,\rho_q\) are canonical remainders,

\[
 u_p=\left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{p^8}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 u_q=\left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{q^8}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

The natural error determinant is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Xi_{p,q}
 &=\det
 \begin{pmatrix}
 E_p&p^8\\
 E_q&q^8
 \end{pmatrix}\\
 &=q^8E_p-p^8E_q\\
 &=p^8q^8(u_p-u_q).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4.6}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{p^8q^8\mid\Xi_{p,q}.}
 \tag{4.7}
\]

For \(p<q\) and \(n\ge32\), this determinant is positive. The single \(j=n\) summand gives

\[
 b_n\ge\binom{2n}{n}^2
 \ge\frac{16^n}{(2n+1)^2}.
 \tag{4.8}
\]

For \(n\ge32\), the right side exceeds \(2n^{16}\). Also, since distinct top-half primes are odd,

\[
 q^8-p^8\ge16(n/2)^7.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \frac{b_n}{p^8}-\frac{b_n}{q^8}>1,
\]

so \(u_p>u_q\) and \(\Xi_{p,q}>0\).

Moreover

\[
 b_n\,n^7/16-O(n^{16})
 \le\Xi_{p,q}<b_n n^8,
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \log\Xi_{p,q}=cn+O(\log n).
 }
 \tag{4.9}
\]

The determinant gains eight powers at each prime but costs one full Apéry exponential.

### 4.3 The optimal pair CRT carrier

Since

\[
 \frac{b_n}{pq}=\frac{x_p}{q}=\frac{x_q}{p},
\]

the local laws determine

\[
 \frac{b_n}{pq}
 \equiv q^{-1}a_p\pmod {p^7},
 \qquad
 \frac{b_n}{pq}
 \equiv p^{-1}a_q\pmod {q^7}.
 \tag{4.10}
\]

Let \(Y_{p,q}\in[0,p^7q^7)\) be the unique CRT solution. Then \(pqY_{p,q}\) is the least nonnegative residue of \(b_n\) modulo \(p^8q^8\), and

\[
 \boxed{
 C_{p,q}=b_n-pqY_{p,q}
 =p^8q^8\left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{p^8q^8}\right\rfloor.
 }
 \tag{4.11}
\]

For \(n\ge32\), (4.8) gives \(b_n>2p^8q^8\). Therefore

\[
 \frac{b_n}{2}<C_{p,q}\le b_n.
 \tag{4.12}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 C_{p,q}>0,
 \quad
 p^8q^8\mid C_{p,q},
 \quad
 \log C_{p,q}=cn+O(\log n).
 }
 \tag{4.13}
\]

This is the strongest clean positive pair theorem furnished by the order-seven law. It is visibly saturated: it is the largest multiple of the reconstructed modulus below \(b_n\).

### 4.4 The exact tradeoff

The two eliminations exhibit the full obstruction.

- Equal weights cancel \(b_n\) and give the small number \(qa_q-pa_p\), but lose divisibility.
- Modulus-intersection weights preserve \(p^8q^8\), but leave a number of size \(b_n\operatorname{poly}(n)\).
- To cancel \(b_n\) and force \(p^8q^8\) universally, the coefficients themselves must contain \(p^8q^8\). The divisor is then inserted explicitly.

This is the pair-level Smith saturation: no small coefficient choice simultaneously cancels the common global value and retains the intersection of the two prime-power moduli.

## 5. Products and sums over pairs

Assume \(K=|T|\ge2\).

### 5.1 Pair product

Define

\[
 {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)
 =\prod_{\{p,q\}\subset T}C_{p,q}.
\]

For \(n\ge32\), all factors are positive. Each prime lies in exactly \(K-1\) pairs, so

\[
 \boxed{
 R^{8(K-1)}\mid {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n).
 }
 \tag{5.1}
\]

More exactly,

\[
 \boxed{
 {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)
 =R^{8(K-1)}
  \prod_{p<q}
  \left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{p^8q^8}\right\rfloor.
 }
 \tag{5.2}
\]

From (4.12),

\[
 \binom K2(\log b_n-\log2)
 \le\log {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)
 \le\binom K2\log b_n.
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \log {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)
 =\binom K2\bigl(cn+O(\log n)\bigr).
 }
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Normalizing by the gained exponent gives

\[
 \frac{\log {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)}{8(K-1)}
 =\frac K{16}\bigl(cn+O(\log n)\bigr),
 \tag{5.4}
\]

not \(o(n)\).

There is a sharp graph-theoretic explanation. A product of pair-supported carriers is a multigraph on \(T\). To give every vertex exponent at least \(8(K-1)\), every vertex must have degree at least \(K-1\); hence at least \(K(K-1)/2\) pair factors are necessary. Since every nonzero factor has height \(cn+O(\log n)\), quadratic total height cannot be avoided by selecting a sparser set of pairs.

### 5.2 Pair sums

An unweighted sum of the \(C_{p,q}\) has no common target-prime divisor: a term supported on \(\{p,q\}\) need not be divisible by a third target prime.

To force every summand to be divisible by \(R^8\), one must insert the missing primes:

\[
 \sum_{p<q}\left(\frac{R}{pq}\right)^8 C_{p,q}
 =R^8\sum_{p<q}
 \left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{p^8q^8}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

This is positive, but the factor \(R^8\) is visible in the definition. Forcing \(R^{8(K-1)}\) requires still larger explicit weights. Such sums are rewritings of the desired modulus, not selective carriers.

CRT programmability excludes a hidden cancellation identity with fixed small coefficients: the local quotient residues can be prescribed independently.

## 8. Boundary values, recurrence, Casoratian, and resultants

### 8.1 Fake value in the recurrence

The exact recurrence is

\[
 (n+1)^3b_{n+1}-P(n)b_n+n^3b_{n-1}=0,
\]

with

\[
 P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]

Replacing only \(b_n\) by an integer \(Y\) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_n(Y)
 &=(n+1)^3b_{n+1}-P(n)Y+n^3b_{n-1}\\
 &=P(n)(b_n-Y).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8.1}
\]

For \(Y=B_7\), this is exactly \(P(n)C_7(n)\). The recurrence changes neither the selective divisor nor the exponential height.

Any finite family of neighboring recurrence defects obtained by changing only the central coordinate belongs to the same principal ideal \((b_n-Y)\). Eliminating those rows cannot produce a transverse factor.

The correct identity is

\[
 a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.
 \tag{8.2}
\]

With a fake value \(Y\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K_n(Y)
 &=a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}Y-\frac6{n^3}\\
 &=a_{n-1}(b_n-Y).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8.3}
\]

After clearing denominators, this remains an exact multiple of \(b_n-Y\). It is not an independent carrier.

The denominator-safe integral Casoratian also contains universal least-common-multiple factors at every candidate prime. Saturating that content leaves exactly the same quotient coordinate, as already proved in the separation audit.

### 8.3 Boundary state \((1,5)\)

The boundary data are already built into every shifted jet. A genuinely new boundary equation would require an independent integral initial state whose solution vanishes at all folded target indices.

The exact simultaneous state lattice proved in the repository is

\[
 \Lambda_T
 =\mathbb Z(1,5)+\mathbb Z(0,R).
 \tag{8.4}
\]

Equivalently, an integral state \((u,v)\) is synchronized at all target zeros exactly when

\[
 R\mid v-5u.
 \tag{8.5}
\]

Therefore every synchronized state independent of \((1,5)\) has determinant

\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\5&v\end{pmatrix}=v-5u
\]

divisible by \(R\), and its norm is at least \(R/\sqrt{26}\). The unknown radical has already entered its height.

The classical companion state has determinant \(6\), so no prime \(p\ge7\) can make it vanish at a target zero. Its block formulas recover the same quotient coordinate rather than add a second synchronized row.

### 8.4 Holonomic resultants

A resultant involving a fake central value and finitely many actual neighboring values eliminates linear recurrence defects whose common factor is \(b_n-Y\). It therefore produces a power of that factor times a target-independent determinant. It cannot make \(C_7(n)\) nonzero when the CRT lift stabilizes to \(b_n\).

This no-go does not cover a genuinely independent arithmetic sequence. For example, the still-open estimate

\[
 \log\gcd\!\left(b_n,\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\right)=o(n)
 \tag{8.6}
\]

would solve the top-half branch. It is a new gcd theorem for a canonical Apéry coefficient and a factorial ratio, not a consequence of the local quotient tower.

## 9. Minimal extra lemma

### Grade-seven small-lift lemma

Construct, from canonical Apéry boundary/recurrence data, an integer \(Y_n\) such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &Y_n\ne b_n,\\
 &Y_n\equiv b_n\pmod {p^8}
   \quad\text{for every }p\in T,\\
 &\log|b_n-Y_n|=o(n).
 \end{aligned}
 }
 \tag{9.1}
\]

Then

\[
 R^8\mid b_n-Y_n,
\]

and immediately \(\log R=o(n)\).

The canonical CRT lift \(B_7\) fails exactly where the proof needs independence:

- it equals \(b_n\) in the large-modulus regime;
- when it differs, its difference has height \(cn+O(\log n)\), not \(o(n)\).

### Uniform-grade version

For every fixed \(h\), construct \(Y_{n,h}\ne b_n\) with

\[
 R^{h+1}\mid b_n-Y_{n,h}
 \tag{9.2}
\]

and

\[
 \log|b_n-Y_{n,h}|\le Cn+o(n),
 \tag{9.3}
\]

where \(C\) is absolute and independent of \(h\). Then

\[
 \log R\le\frac{C}{h+1}n+o(n),
\]

and letting \(h\to\infty\) proves the desired little-oh estimate.

This requires both:

1. fixed local eliminations at arbitrarily high grade;
2. a global non-stabilization/small-lift theorem.

The first input alone only reconstructs more digits of the same diagonal integer.

## 10. Reproducible computation plan

The existing script

```text
problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_order_seven_target_audit.py
```

already computes the shifted jets, \(d_p,z_p\), the small \(\Delta_p^2\) representative, and verifies the fixed law. The following extension reproduces every global assertion.

```python
from math import prod
from sympy.ntheory.modular import crt

EXCEPTIONS = {2, 3, 5, 7, 331, 769}

targets = [
    p for p in primes
    if n // 2 < p <= n and b[n] % p == 0 and p not in EXCEPTIONS
]
R = prod(targets)

alpha = {}      # A_p(n) = b_n/p mod p^7
rho = {}        # b_n mod p^8

for p in targets:
    r = n - p
    s = p - 1 - r
    mod7 = p**7
    mod8 = p**8

    delta = b[p - 1] - 1
    delta_small = (delta // p**3) % p
    eps = p**6 * (delta_small * delta_small % p)

    # direct_residue and reflected_residue are the d_p,z_p
    # already computed by q32_order_seven_target_audit.py.
    fixed = (
        (1680 + 2472 * eps) * direct_residue
        - 25 * reflected_residue
    ) % mod7

    alpha[p] = fixed * pow(1655, -1, mod7) % mod7
    rho[p] = p * alpha[p]

    assert alpha[p] == (b[n] // p) % mod7
    assert rho[p] == b[n] % mod8

# Full CRT representative B_7 = b_n mod R^8.
B7, modulus = crt([p**8 for p in targets], [rho[p] for p in targets])
B7 = int(B7)
modulus = int(modulus)
assert modulus == R**8
assert B7 == b[n] % (R**8)
assert B7 % R == 0

C7 = b[n] - B7
assert C7 == R**8 * (b[n] // R**8)

# Pair cofactor CRT and pair carrier.
pair_carriers = []
for i, p in enumerate(targets):
    for q in targets[i + 1:]:
        yp = alpha[p] * pow(q, -1, p**7) % p**7
        yq = alpha[q] * pow(p, -1, q**7) % q**7
        Ypq, pair_modulus = crt([p**7, q**7], [yp, yq])
        Ypq = int(Ypq)
        pair_modulus = int(pair_modulus)
        assert pair_modulus == p**7 * q**7

        Cpq = b[n] - p * q * Ypq
        assert Cpq == p**8 * q**8 * (b[n] // (p**8 * q**8))
        if n >= 32:
            assert Cpq > 0
        pair_carriers.append(Cpq)

pair_product = prod(pair_carriers)
if len(targets) >= 2:
    assert pair_product % (R**(8 * (len(targets) - 1))) == 0
```

Useful exact diagnostics are:

```text
A_p(n) == 0  <=>  v_p(b_n) >= 8
B_7 == b_n mod R^8
C_7 == 0     <=>  b_n < R^8
log(C_pq)/n  approaches log(17+12sqrt(2))
```

The computation is only a regression check; all divisibility, nonvanishing, and height statements above were proved algebraically.

## 11. Final theorem package

### Proved

```text
1. The fixed law gives a canonical A_p(n) in [0,p^7) with
      A_p(n) == b_n/p mod p^7.

2. Its local height is polynomial:
      log A_p(n) <= 7 log n,
      log |L_p(n)| <= 14 log n + O(1).

3. The quotient-residue map is a CRT isomorphism. All local rows and
   cross-divisibilities encode exactly b_n mod R^8.

4. For every pair p,q there is a canonical positive carrier
      C_pq = p^8 q^8 floor(b_n/(p^8 q^8))
   for n >= 32.

5. The natural pair determinant is also nonzero and satisfies
      p^8 q^8 | Xi_pq,
      log Xi_pq = c n + O(log n).

6. The complete pair product satisfies
      R^(8(K-1)) | product C_pq,
   but has height Theta(K^2 n).

7. All primes together give the optimal global carrier
      b_n-[b_n]_(R^8) = R^8 floor(b_n/R^8).
   It either has full linear Apéry height or vanishes by exact
   reconstruction.

8. Recurrence, Casoratian, and fake-value holonomic resultants factor
   through the same principal difference b_n-Y. An independent
   synchronized boundary state has determinant divisible by R.
```

### Not obtained

```text
- no nonzero carrier with log|C|/e=o(n);
- no polynomial-height pair determinant retaining selective p^e q^e
  divisibility without inserting that modulus in its coefficients;
- no compression of the pair product from Theta(K^2 n) to o(Kn);
- no proof that the full CRT lift differs from b_n in the large-R regime;
- no all-n bound on R_n.
```

### Sharp conclusion

The order-seven result is a genuine positive local theorem. Globally it supplies precisely the residue class

\[
 b_n\pmod {R^8}.
\]

The cross-prime quotient divisibilities do not create a second coordinate. The strongest pair and all-prime carriers are the floor multiples of the reconstructed CRT moduli, so their height is saturated. The missing theorem is not another elimination at grade seven; it is an independent Apéry-specific small lift, non-stabilization principle, or transverse arithmetic gcd law.
