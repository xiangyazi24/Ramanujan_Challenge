## 1. Notation and fixed exceptions

Let

\[
 b_m=\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj^2\binom{m+j}j^2
\]

be the Apéry numbers, and fix \(n\). Put

\[
 T_n=\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n\}.
\]

For \(p\in T_n\), write

\[
 r_p=n-p,
 \qquad
 s_p=p-1-r_p=2p-n-1,
 \qquad
 x_p=\frac{b_n}{p}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
 p\mid b_n
 \iff p\mid b_{r_p},
\]

and reflection gives

\[
 b_{s_p}\equiv b_{r_p}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The fixed order-seven law excludes:

- \(p=7\), because of the proved range of H6;
- \(p=331\), because \(1655=5\cdot331\) is not a unit;
- \(p=769\), because of the reflected endpoint normalization.

Let

\[
 T=T_n\setminus\{7,331,769\},
 \qquad
 R=\prod_{p\in T}p,
 \qquad
 K=|T|.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

The discarded primes contribute only \(O(1)\) to \(\log R_n\), so they are irrelevant to the desired little-oh estimate.

For \(p\in T\), define

\[
 R_p=R/p,
 \qquad
 A=\frac{b_n}{R}.
\]

Then

\[
 x_p=A R_p.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

In particular, for distinct \(p,q\in T\),

\[
 q\mid x_p,
 \qquad
 p\mid x_q.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

The question is whether (1.4), together with the order-seven descriptions of \(x_p\), imposes more than the single common cofactor \(A\).

## 3. The exact CRT theorem: all local and cross-prime data are one coordinate

The following elementary theorem is the decisive algebraic obstruction.

### Theorem 3.1 -- quotient-residue CRT isomorphism

Let

\[
 R=\prod_{p\in T}p
\]

be squarefree and let \(h\ge1\). The map

\[
 \Phi_h:
 \{B\bmod R^{h+1}:R\mid B\}
 \longrightarrow
 \prod_{p\mid R}\mathbb Z/p^h\mathbb Z,
 \tag{3.1}
\]

\[
 \Phi_h(B)=\left(\frac Bp\bmod p^h\right)_{p\mid R},
\]

is a bijection.

#### Proof

If \(B\) is changed by \(R^{h+1}\), then \(B/p\) changes by a multiple of \(p^h\), so the map is well defined.

If every component is zero, then

\[
 p^{h+1}\mid B
\]

for every \(p\mid R\), hence \(R^{h+1}\mid B\). Thus the map is injective. Both sides have cardinality \(R^h\), so it is bijective.

Equivalently, given arbitrary residues \(a_p\bmod p^h\), the inverse is the unique CRT class

\[
 B\equiv pa_p\pmod {p^{h+1}}
 \quad(p\mid R).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Every such \(B\) is automatically divisible by \(R\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 -- exact information content of the order-seven law

Take \(h=7\) and \(a_p=A_p(n)\). There is a unique integer

\[
 0\le B_7<R^8
\]

such that

\[
 B_7\equiv pA_p(n)\pmod {p^8}
 \quad(p\mid R).
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Since the actual Apéry number satisfies the same congruences,

\[
 \boxed{B_7=[b_n]_{R^8}.}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Moreover \(R\mid B_7\). Writing

\[
 B_7=R A_7,
 \qquad
 0\le A_7<R^7,
\]

one has

\[
 A_7\equiv R_p^{-1}A_p(n)\pmod {p^7}
 \quad(p\mid R),
 \tag{3.5}
\]

so \(A_7\) is exactly the CRT representative of the global cofactor

\[
 A=b_n/R
\]

modulo \(R^7\).

The cross-divisibilities

\[
 q\mid x_p,
 \qquad
 p\mid x_q
\]

are not extra equations. They are automatic from \(R\mid B\) in Theorem 3.1.

### Corollary 3.3 -- CRT programmability

Fix any finite set of distinct primes \(T\), and choose **arbitrary** residues

\[
 a_p\in\mathbb Z/p^7\mathbb Z.
\]

There is an integer \(B\), unique modulo \(R^8\), such that

\[
 R\mid B,
 \qquad
 B/p\equiv a_p\pmod {p^7}
 \quad(p\mid R).
 \tag{3.6}
\]

Consequently all relations

\[
 q\mid B/p,
 \qquad
 p\mid B/q,
 \qquad
 p(B/p)=q(B/q)
\]

hold automatically. Therefore no nontrivial algebraic relation among the local residues \(a_p\) can be deduced from the quotient congruences and cross-divisibility alone.

This is a countermodel for the proposed elimination algebra, not a second Apéry sequence. It proves that any successful argument must use an additional global property of the canonical Apéry value, not merely the displayed local equations.

## 4. Exact two-prime elimination

Fix distinct \(p,q\in T\), and abbreviate

\[
 a_p=A_p(n),
 \qquad
 a_q=A_q(n),
 \qquad
 \rho_p=pa_p,
 \qquad
 \rho_q=qa_q.
\]

Then

\[
 \rho_p=[b_n]_{p^8},
 \qquad
 \rho_q=[b_n]_{q^8}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

### 4.1 The small cross-difference loses every target divisor

Put

\[
 E_p=b_n-\rho_p,
 \qquad
 E_q=b_n-\rho_q.
\]

Then

\[
 p^8\mid E_p,
 \qquad
 q^8\mid E_q.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Eliminating \(b_n\) with equal coefficients gives

\[
 E_p-E_q=\rho_q-\rho_p=qa_q-pa_p.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

This has polynomial height:

\[
 |qa_q-pa_p|<p^8+q^8<2n^8.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

But it has no forced factor \(p\), \(q\), or \(pq\). The CRT programmability theorem makes the reason exact: \(a_p\) and \(a_q\) are independent residue coordinates.

For example, in the abstract two-prime model take

\[
 B=pq.
\]

Then

\[
 a_p=q,
 \qquad
 a_q=p,
\]

and the cross-difference (4.3) vanishes identically. Thus its nonvanishing is not a formal consequence of any of the audited local relations.

### 4.2 The natural pair determinant keeps divisibility but has linear height

Write

\[
 E_p=p^8u_p,
 \qquad
 E_q=q^8u_q.
\]

Since \(\rho_p\) and \(\rho_q\) are canonical remainders,

\[
 u_p=\left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{p^8}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 u_q=\left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{q^8}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

The natural \(2\times2\) error determinant is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Xi_{p,q}
 &=\det
 \begin{pmatrix}
 E_p&p^8\\
 E_q&q^8
 \end{pmatrix}\\
 &=q^8E_p-p^8E_q\\
 &=p^8q^8(u_p-u_q).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4.6}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{p^8q^8\mid\Xi_{p,q}.}
 \tag{4.7}
\]

It is also nonzero for all sufficiently large \(n\). In fact, if \(p<q\) and \(n\ge32\), then

\[
 \frac{b_n}{p^8}-\frac{b_n}{q^8}>1,
\]

so \(u_p>u_q\) and \(\Xi_{p,q}>0\).

A completely elementary lower bound suffices. The \(j=n\) summand gives

\[
 b_n\ge\binom{2n}{n}^2
 \ge\frac{16^n}{(2n+1)^2}.
 \tag{4.8}
\]

For \(n\ge32\), the right side is greater than \(2n^{16}\). Since \(p,q\in(n/2,n]\),

\[
 q^8-p^8\ge16(n/2)^7,
\]

and therefore

\[
 \frac{b_n}{p^8}-\frac{b_n}{q^8}
 \ge\frac{b_n}{8n^9}>2.
\]

The height is nevertheless exactly on the forbidden linear scale. Uniformly for \(p<q\), \(n\ge32\),

\[
 \frac{b_n n^7}{2^{20}}
 <\Xi_{p,q}<b_n n^8,
 \tag{4.9}
\]

so

\[
 \boxed{
 \log\Xi_{p,q}=cn+O(\log n).
 }
 \tag{4.10}
\]

Thus the determinant gains eight powers at each of two primes but costs one full Apéry exponential.

### 4.3 The optimal pair CRT carrier

The cross-divisibilities permit a slightly smaller carrier than \(\Xi_{p,q}\).

Since

\[
 \frac{b_n}{pq}=\frac{x_p}{q}=\frac{x_q}{p},
\]

the local laws determine

\[
 \frac{b_n}{pq}
 \equiv q^{-1}a_p\pmod {p^7},
 \qquad
 \frac{b_n}{pq}
 \equiv p^{-1}a_q\pmod {q^7}.
 \tag{4.11}
\]

Let \(Y_{p,q}\) be the unique integer with

\[
 0\le Y_{p,q}<p^7q^7
\]

satisfying (4.11). Then

\[
 b_n-pqY_{p,q}\equiv0\pmod {p^8q^8}.
\]

Because \(pqY_{p,q}\) is the least nonnegative residue of \(b_n\) modulo \(p^8q^8\), one gets the exact formula

\[
 \boxed{
 C_{p,q}=b_n-pqY_{p,q}
 =p^8q^8\left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{p^8q^8}\right\rfloor.
 }
 \tag{4.12}
\]

For \(n\ge32\), (4.8) gives \(b_n>2p^8q^8\), and hence

\[
 \frac{b_n}{2}<C_{p,q}\le b_n.
 \tag{4.13}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 C_{p,q}>0,
 \quad
 p^8q^8\mid C_{p,q},
 \quad
 \log C_{p,q}=cn+O(\log n).
 }
 \tag{4.14}
\]

This is the strongest clean positive two-prime theorem supplied by the order-seven law. It is also visibly saturated: \(C_{p,q}\) is just the largest multiple of the reconstructed modulus not exceeding \(b_n\).

### 4.4 The exact tradeoff

The two canonical eliminations exhibit the whole obstruction:

- Equal weights cancel \(b_n\) and produce the small number
  \[
  E_p-E_q=qa_q-pa_p,
  \]
  but lose every target-prime divisor.
- The modulus-intersection weights \(q^8,-p^8\) produce \(p^8q^8\)-divisibility, but leave a term of size \(b_n\).
- If one both cancels \(b_n\) and insists on universal \(p^8q^8\)-divisibility, the coefficients themselves must contain \(p^8q^8\). For example,
  \[
  p^8q^8(E_p-E_q)=p^8q^8(qa_q-pa_p),
  \]
  whose divisor is inserted explicitly and whose height ledger is tautological.

This is the pair-level Smith saturation: there is no choice of small coefficients that simultaneously cancels the common global value and retains the intersection of the two prime-power moduli.

## 5. Multiplying or summing over all pairs

Let \(K=|T|\ge2\).

### 5.1 The complete pair product

Using the optimal carriers (4.12), define

\[
 {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)
 =\prod_{\{p,q\}\subset T}C_{p,q}.
\]

For \(n\ge32\), every factor is positive. Each prime belongs to exactly \(K-1\) unordered pairs, so

\[
 \boxed{
 R^{8(K-1)}\mid {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n).
 }
 \tag{5.1}
\]

More precisely,

\[
 \boxed{
 {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)
 =R^{8(K-1)}
  \prod_{p<q}
  \left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{p^8q^8}\right\rfloor.
 }
 \tag{5.2}
\]

This is exactly the exponent pattern requested in the question, with \(e=8\).

However, (4.13) gives two-sided height bounds:

\[
 \binom K2(\log b_n-\log2)
 \le\log {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)
 \le\binom K2\log b_n.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \log {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)
 =\binom K2\bigl(cn+O(\log n)\bigr).
 }
 \tag{5.4}
\]

Dividing by the accumulated exponent gives

\[
 \frac{\log {\cal C}_{\rm pair}(n)}{8(K-1)}
 =\frac K{16}\bigl(cn+O(\log n)\bigr).
 \tag{5.5}
\]

This is never \(o(n)\) uniformly in the unknown target size. It grows linearly in \(K\).

There is also a sharp graph-theoretic reason. A product of pair-supported carriers corresponds to a multigraph on \(T\). If every prime is to receive exponent at least \(8(K-1)\), every vertex must have degree at least \(K-1\), so at least

\[
 \frac{K(K-1)}2
\]

pair factors are necessary. Since each nonzero factor has height \(cn+O(\log n)\), quadratic total height cannot be avoided by selecting a sparser family of pairs.

### 5.2 Sums do not retain the pair exponents

An unweighted sum of the \(C_{p,q}\) has no common target-prime divisor: a term supported on \(\{p,q\}\) need not be divisible by a third target prime.

To force every term to be divisible by \(R^8\), one must insert the missing primes explicitly:

\[
 \sum_{p<q}\left(\frac{R}{pq}\right)^8 C_{p,q}
 =R^8\sum_{p<q}
 \left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{p^8q^8}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{5.6}
\]

This is positive and nonzero, but the factor \(R^8\) is now visible in the definition. Its height bound contains \(8\log R\) before any Apéry information is used.

To force \(R^{8(K-1)}\), the coefficients must insert still more powers of the missing primes. Such a sum is a rewritten multiple of the desired modulus, not a selective carrier.

CRT programmability rules out a hidden cancellation theorem for fixed small coefficients: the local residues can be chosen arbitrarily, so no cancellation among different pair rows follows from the local laws themselves.

## 6. The all-prime carrier and the exact reconstruction dichotomy

The pair construction is dominated by the single CRT representative using all primes simultaneously.

Let \(A_7\in[0,R^7)\) be determined by

\[
 A_7\equiv R_p^{-1}A_p(n)\pmod {p^7}
 \quad(p\mid R).
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
 A_7=[A]_{R^7},
 \qquad
 B_7=RA_7=[b_n]_{R^8}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

Define

\[
 C_7(n)=b_n-B_7.
\]

The exact formula is

\[
 \boxed{
 C_7(n)=R^8\left\lfloor\frac{b_n}{R^8}\right\rfloor.
 }
 \tag{6.3}
\]

### Case I: \(R^8\le b_n\)

Then \(C_7(n)>0\), and

\[
 R^8\mid C_7(n),
 \qquad
 0<C_7(n)\le b_n.
\]

Therefore

\[
 8\log R\le cn+O(\log n),
\]

or

\[
 \boxed{
 \log R\le0.440686\ldots n+O(\log n).
 }
 \tag{6.4}
\]

This is exactly the order-seven reconstruction threshold already recorded in Section 11.4 of the separation note.

### Case II: \(R^8>b_n\)

Then

\[
 0\le b_n<R^8,
\]

and since \(B_7\) is the unique representative in this interval,

\[
 \boxed{B_7=b_n,
 \qquad C_7(n)=0.}
 \tag{6.5}
\]

Thus the carrier vanishes precisely when the modulus is large enough to reconstruct the actual integer. Reconstruction is not a contradiction.

This dichotomy is sharp. No manipulation of the same residue class can turn (6.3) into a nonzero carrier in both regimes.

If one deliberately truncates the quotient law at \(p^6\) rather than using the full equation (3.6)/(11.9), every statement above remains true with \(7,8\) replaced by \(6,7\). The obstruction is identical and the numerical threshold becomes \(c/7\) instead of \(c/8\).

## 7. Why reflection and the two target rows add no cross-prime rank

For a single prime, reflection is crucial: the direct and reflected rows remove the endpoint coordinates \(H_p\) and \(W_p\), producing the fixed scalar law (2.8).

After that elimination, however, both rows have collapsed to the same one-dimensional coordinate

\[
 x_p=b_n/p\pmod {p^7}.
\]

For distinct primes \(p,q\), the reflected indices are

\[
 s_p=2p-n-1,
 \qquad
 s_q=2q-n-1.
\]

No single residue characteristic sees both reflected zero conditions. The recurrence continuant can create a selective factor only when one prime sees two zeros; the observed and audited cross-prime pairs are in the complementary pure-cross locus. Thus reflection improves the local precision but does not create a second global row.

Algebraically this is already encoded by Theorem 3.1: replacing the complicated direct/reflected formulas by their output residues loses no global information, and those residues are unrestricted CRT coordinates.

## 8. Boundary values, Casoratians, and holonomic resultants

The requested modifications were checked explicitly.

### 8.1 Substituting a fake value in the Apéry recurrence

Let

\[
 P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]

The exact recurrence is

\[
 (n+1)^3b_{n+1}-P(n)b_n+n^3b_{n-1}=0.
\]

Replace only \(b_n\) by an integer \(Y\). The defect is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_n(Y)
 &=(n+1)^3b_{n+1}-P(n)Y+n^3b_{n-1}\\
 &=P(n)(b_n-Y).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8.1}
\]

Taking \(Y=B_7\) gives

\[
 F_n(B_7)=P(n)C_7(n).
 \tag{8.2}
\]

Thus the recurrence neither changes the divisor nor improves the height. Its fake-value ideal is exactly the principal ideal \((b_n-Y)\).

Any finite elimination using neighboring recurrence equations has the same property: because the recurrence is linear and the actual neighboring values are fixed, every defect lies in \((b_n-Y)\). A holonomic resultant is therefore a power of this principal factor times a determinant independent of the target condition.

### 8.2 The Casoratian

The correct identity is

\[
 a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.
 \tag{8.3}
\]

For a fake value \(Y\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K_n(Y)
 &=a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}Y-\frac6{n^3}\\
 &=a_{n-1}(b_n-Y).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8.4}
\]

After multiplying by any denominator clearing factor, (8.4) remains an exact factorization by \(b_n-Y\). With \(Y=B_7\), it is just a multiple of \(C_7(n)\).

Moreover, the denominator-safe integral Casoratian contains every top-half candidate prime to high universal order through the least-common-multiple denominator. Saturating that universal content leaves no second target factor. This is exactly the Route A obstruction already proved in the separation note.

### 8.3 Inserting the boundary state \((b_0,b_1)=(1,5)\)

The boundary values are already used in constructing every shifted jet and hence every \(A_p(n)\). To get a genuinely new equation one would need an independent integral initial state whose solution is synchronized with all target zeros.

The exact initial-state lattice computed in the repository is

\[
 \Lambda_T
 =\mathbb Z(1,5)+\mathbb Z(0,R).
 \tag{8.5}
\]

Equivalently, an integral state \((u,v)\) vanishes at every folded target zero if and only if

\[
 R\mid v-5u.
 \tag{8.6}
\]

Hence any synchronized state independent of \((1,5)\) has determinant

\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\5&v\end{pmatrix}=v-5u
\]

divisible by \(R\), and its norm is at least \(R/\sqrt{26}\). The unknown radical is already present in the height of the proposed second boundary observable.

The classical companion state has fixed determinant \(6\), so for \(p\ge7\) it cannot vanish at even one target zero. Its block formulas reconstruct the same quotient coordinate rather than adding a synchronized equation.

### 8.4 What remains genuinely outside the no-go

A theorem such as

\[
 \log\gcd\!\left(b_n,\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\right)=o(n)
 \tag{8.7}
\]

would solve the top-half branch and is not refuted by the CRT argument. It is an independent arithmetic gcd theorem for the canonical Apéry value and a factorial ratio. No checked holonomic or moving-target gcd theorem currently proves (8.7).

## 9. The minimal extra lemma

The exact order-seven missing statement can be formulated without ambiguity.

### Small-lift lemma at grade seven

Construct, from the canonical Apéry boundary/recurrence data, an integer \(Y_n\) such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &Y_n\ne b_n,\\
 &Y_n\equiv b_n\pmod {p^8}
   \quad\text{for every }p\in T_n,\\
 &\log|b_n-Y_n|=o(n).
 \end{aligned}
 }
 \tag{9.1}
\]

Then

\[
 R_n^8\mid b_n-Y_n,
\]

and (9.1) immediately gives \(\log R_n=o(n)\).

The canonical CRT lift \(B_7\) fails exactly at the two required points:

- it equals \(b_n\) in the large-modulus regime;
- when it differs, the difference has height \(cn+O(\log n)\), not \(o(n)\).

### Uniform-grade version

A more plausible endpoint-filtration target is: for every fixed \(h\), construct \(Y_{n,h}\ne b_n\) with

\[
 R_n^{h+1}\mid b_n-Y_{n,h}
 \tag{9.2}
\]

and

\[
 \log|b_n-Y_{n,h}|\le Cn+o(n)
 \tag{9.3}
\]

for an absolute constant \(C\) independent of \(h\). Then

\[
 \log R_n\le\frac{C}{h+1}n+o(n),
\]

and letting \(h\to\infty\) proves the desired little-oh estimate.

This requires two ingredients, not one:

1. local fixed laws at arbitrarily high grade;
2. a global **non-stabilization/small-lift theorem** preventing the CRT representative from becoming the diagonal integer \(b_n\).

The order-seven result proves one more local grade but supplies neither ingredient globally.

## 10. Reproducible computation plan

The existing script

```text
problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_order_seven_target_audit.py
```

already computes the shifted jets, \(d_p,z_p\), the small \(\Delta_p^2\) representative, and verifies (2.8). The following additions reproduce every global assertion in this note.

```python
from math import prod
from sympy.ntheory.modular import crt

EXCEPTIONS = {7, 331, 769}

# b is the exact Apéry table; n is fixed.
targets = [
    p for p in primes
    if n // 2 < p <= n and b[n] % p == 0 and p not in EXCEPTIONS
]
R = prod(targets)

alpha = {}      # A_p(n), the quotient residue mod p^7
rho = {}        # b_n residue mod p^8

for p in targets:
    r = n - p
    s = p - 1 - r
    mod7 = p**7
    mod8 = p**8

    delta = b[p - 1] - 1
    delta_small = (delta // p**3) % p
    eps = p**6 * (delta_small * delta_small % p)

    # direct_residue and reflected_residue are exactly the d_p,z_p
    # computed in q32_order_seven_target_audit.py.
    fixed = (
        (1680 + 2472 * eps) * direct_residue
        - 25 * reflected_residue
    ) % mod7

    alpha[p] = fixed * pow(1655, -1, mod7) % mod7
    rho[p] = p * alpha[p]

    assert alpha[p] == (b[n] // p) % mod7
    assert rho[p] == b[n] % mod8

# Full CRT representative B_7 = b_n mod R^8.
B7, modulus = crt([p**8 for p in targets], [rho[p] for p in targets])
B7 = int(B7)
modulus = int(modulus)
assert modulus == R**8
assert B7 == b[n] % (R**8)
assert B7 % R == 0

C7 = b[n] - B7
assert C7 == R**8 * (b[n] // R**8)

# Pair cofactor CRT and pair carrier.
for i, p in enumerate(targets):
    for q in targets[i + 1:]:
        yp = alpha[p] * pow(q, -1, p**7) % p**7
        yq = alpha[q] * pow(p, -1, q**7) % q**7
        Ypq, pair_modulus = crt([p**7, q**7], [yp, yq])
        Ypq = int(Ypq)
        pair_modulus = int(pair_modulus)
        assert pair_modulus == p**7 * q**7

        Cpq = b[n] - p * q * Ypq
        assert Cpq == p**8 * q**8 * (b[n] // (p**8 * q**8))
        if n >= 32:
            assert Cpq > 0

# Complete pair product identity. Use only at modest n: the integers grow fast.
pair_product = 1
for i, p in enumerate(targets):
    for q in targets[i + 1:]:
        pair_product *= p**8 * q**8 * (b[n] // (p**8 * q**8))

assert pair_product % (R**(8 * (len(targets) - 1))) == 0
```

Additional exact checks worth printing are:

```text
A_p(n) == 0  <=>  v_p(b_n) >= 8
B_7 == b_n mod R^8
C_7 == 0     <=>  b_n < R^8
log(C_pq)/n  -> log(17+12sqrt(2))
```

No numerical experiment is used in the proof; these checks only guard implementation and floor/endpoint conventions.

## 11. Final theorem package

### Proved

```text
1. The fixed law gives an exact canonical integer A_p(n) in [0,p^7)
   with A_p(n) == b_n/p mod p^7.

2. The local numerator representatives have polynomial height:
      log A_p(n) <= 7 log n,
      log |L_p(n)| <= 14 log n + O(1).

3. For every pair p,q there is a canonical positive carrier
      C_pq = p^8 q^8 floor(b_n/(p^8 q^8))
   for n >= 32.

4. The natural error determinant is also nonzero and satisfies
      p^8 q^8 | Xi_pq,
      log Xi_pq = c n + O(log n).

5. The complete pair product is one nonzero carrier with
      R^(8(K-1)) | product C_pq,
   but its logarithmic height is Theta(K^2 n).

6. All target primes together determine exactly
      B_7 = b_n mod R^8,
   and the optimal global carrier is
      b_n-B_7 = R^8 floor(b_n/R^8).

7. The quotient residues plus q|x_p and p|x_q are freely programmable
   CRT coordinates. They impose no pair relation beyond the common
   cofactor.

8. Recurrence, Casoratian, and fake-value resultants factor through the
   same principal difference b_n-Y. An independent synchronized boundary
   state has determinant divisible by R.
```

### Not obtained

```text
- no nonzero carrier with log|C|/e = o(n);
- no pair determinant that is both polynomial-height and selectively
  divisible without inserting p^e q^e in its coefficients;
- no compression of the complete pair product from Theta(K^2 n) to
  o(K n);
- no proof that the full CRT lift differs from b_n in the large-R regime;
- no all-n bound on R_n.
```

### Sharp conclusion

The order-seven law is a genuine and useful local theorem. Globally, however, it supplies the exact residue class

\[
 b_n\pmod {R_n^8}.
\]

The cross-prime divisibilities do not create a second coordinate. The best nonzero pair and global carriers are the corresponding floor multiples of their CRT moduli, so their Archimedean height is saturated. The missing theorem is not another pair elimination at the same grade; it is an independent Apéry-specific small lift or non-stabilization principle.
