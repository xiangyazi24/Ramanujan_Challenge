# 3. A source-order local projective bridge

Only the following nonwrapping bridge is needed, and it can be proved before the atom section without any FABLE input.

**Lemma (nonwrapping evaluation-line bridge).**  Let `0<=m<m+q<p`.  Let `u^(0),u^(1)` be the existing two independent `p`-integral solutions of the Apéry recurrence and set
\[
 \Pi_p(j)=[u^{(0)}_j:u^{(1)}_j]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p).
\]
Then
\[
 N_q(m)=0\iff \Pi_p(m)=\Pi_p(m+q).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Moreover `prop:reflection`, applied to both solutions, gives
\[
 \Pi_p(p-1-j)=\Pi_p(j).
\tag{3.2}
\]

**Proof.**  On the nonwrapping interval all recurrence denominators are units modulo `p`.  Equality of the two projective evaluation lines is equivalent to existence of a nonzero solution vanishing at both endpoints.  The no-consecutive-zero lemma makes its next value nonzero; the already-proved restart formula then says its endpoint value is a nonzero unit times `N_q(m)`.  This proves (3.1).  Equation (3.2) is immediate by applying `prop:reflection` to each member of the solution basis.  This proof uses only material already before `atom_tail_section.tex`.  In particular it does not use the later transfer-matrix FABLE section.

This lemma is deliberately **nonwrapping**.  Extending (3.1) across the cut at `0 mod p` is precisely where the short-root argument can go wrong.

# 4. First exact defect: the even centered parity is a boundary factor, not a short projective collision

Assume now
\[
 T\le h=a+b<2T,\qquad h<p,
\tag{4.1}
\]
and, as supplied by the hard product cutoff,
\[
 b>D.
\tag{4.2}
\]
For a short root `d<=D`, (4.2) gives `d<b`.  There are two parity cases.

## 4.1 `b-1-d` even

Write
\[
 r={b-1-d\over2}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad
 s={b-1+d\over2}=r+d.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Then
\[
 0\le r<s\le b-1<p,
 \qquad b=r+s+1,
\tag{4.4}
\]
but the middle starting point is
\[
 t=-r-1.
\tag{4.5}
\]
As a standard residue this middle `b`-step crosses the cut at `0`.  One may **not** invoke the nonwrapping projective bridge.  Instead the already-proved endpoint specialization (1.1), with `q=r+1`, gives the exact identity
\[
 N_b(-r-1)
 =(-1)^r b_r b_s(r!)^3(s!)^3.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Since `r,s<b<p`, the factorials are units modulo `p`.  Therefore
\[
 M_b(d)=0
 \iff p\mid b_r b_s.
\tag{4.7}
\]
This is an **actual endpoint-zero alternative**, not the assertion `N_d(r)=0`.

For fixed `d`, as `b` runs through this parity class, `r` runs injectively through an ordinary interval of length `O(T)` and `s=r+d` runs through its translate.  The earlier subinterval zero theorem therefore gives
\[
 \#\{b:\ b-1-d\ \hbox{even},\ M_b(d)=0\}
 \ll T^{2/3+o(1)}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
No projective-to-actual promotion occurred: (4.7) is a literal consequence of the endpoint formula.

## 4.2 `b-1-d` odd

Because `b>d`, the standard representative of `r` is
\[
 r={p+b-1-d\over2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
If
\[
 b+d<p,
\tag{4.10}
\]
then also
\[
 s={p+b-1+d\over2}<p,
 \qquad
 t={p+d-b-1\over2},
\tag{4.11}
\]
and one checks exactly
\[
 t+b=s<p,\qquad p-1-t=r,\qquad r+d=s<p.
\tag{4.12}
\]
Thus (2.11), the local bridge, and reflection give
\[
 N_b(t)=0
 \Longrightarrow
 \Pi_p(t)=\Pi_p(s)
 \Longrightarrow
 \Pi_p(r)=\Pi_p(s)
 \Longrightarrow
 N_d(r)=0.
\tag{4.13}
\]
Here the last implication is again nonwrapping because `r+d=s<p`.  Since `N_d` is nonzero and has degree `3(d-1)` (with `N_1=1`), there are at most `3(d-1)` possible `r`, hence `O(d)` possible `b`, for fixed `d`.

If instead `b+d>p`, then the nonwrapping argument is not legal, but no theorem is needed: for fixed `d`,
\[
 p-d<b<p,
\]
so there are only `O(d)` possible integer values of `b`.  Equality `b+d=p` cannot occur in this odd parity branch because `b-1-d` odd implies `b` and `d` have the same parity, hence `b+d` is even whereas `p` is odd.

Consequently
\[
 \#\{b:\ b-1-d\ \hbox{odd},\ M_b(d)=0\}
 \ll d.
\tag{4.14}
\]
Combining (4.8) and (4.14), the number of possible middle bases with canonical short size `d<=D` is
\[
 \#\{(b,U):d_p(U)\le D,\ M_b(d_p(U))=0\}
 \ll D\,T^{2/3+o(1)}+D^2.
\tag{4.15}
\]
This is the correct source-order replacement for the unsupported blanket `O(D^2)` projective-collision count.  At the desired `D`, the extra `D T^(2/3)` term is harmless after the outer-column estimate.

# 5. Second exact defect: representative size does not imply cleanliness

The statement “the centered representatives are small or half-period, therefore they are not active polluted columns” is false.  Pollution is an arithmetic condition `p|b_{m-1}`; it does not follow from the size or parity of the representative.

The source-order repair is to define exactly the dirty set needed by `prop:column`.

For the current `T` define
\[
 \mathcal P_{p,T}
 =\{-m\pmod p:
      2\le m<p,\ m\le 2T,\ p\mid b_{m-1}\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
If `2T>=p`, the condition is simply truncated at `m<p`.  Because `T<h<p`, one always has `T<p`; the prefix involved in (5.1) has length `O(T)`.  The earlier subinterval zero theorem therefore gives
\[
 |\mathcal P_{p,T}|\ll T^{2/3+o(1)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
By the already-proved converse pollution characterization, if a column `c` has two consecutive zero levels among the relevant `a<2T`, then `c in mathcal P_{p,T}`.  Hence every
\[
 c\notin\mathcal P_{p,T}
\tag{5.3}
\]
has no consecutive zero levels in any relevant `a`-interval of length at most `T`, and `prop:column` gives
\[
 \#\{a:\ T\le a+b<2T,\ a+b<p,\ N_a(c)=0\}
 \ll T^{2/3+o(1)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Call such a column **locally clean**.  Notice that a globally polluted column whose activation level lies above the present `a`-window need not be deleted; (5.4) is the exact property the consumer requires.

# 6. Source-order local replacement for the late paired-pollution theorem

Classify a formal `U` root by its intrinsic unordered centered pair `{r,s}` from (2.6).  Call it dirty if `r` or `s` belongs to `mathcal P_{p,T}`.

Let
\[
 S_p^{\rm dirty}(T)
 =\sum_{\substack{a,b\ge2\\T\le a+b<2T\\a+b<p}}
   g_{p,\rm dirty}^\square(a,b).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{S_p^{\rm dirty}(T)\ll T^{7/3+o(1)}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

**Proof.**  Put `P=|mathcal P_{p,T}|`.

If both centered columns are dirty, an unordered pair `{r,s}` determines
\[
 b\equiv r+s+1\pmod p,
 \qquad U=(s-r)^2,
\]
and there is at most one physical `b in {1,...,p-1}`.  Thus there are `O(P^2)` such bases.  For each base there are at most `O(T)` possible `a`-levels.  This contributes
\[
 O(TP^2)\ll T^{7/3+o(1)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

If exactly one centered column is dirty, choose which endpoint is dirty (factor at most `2`), choose it in `P` ways, and choose `b` in `O(T)` ways.  The other endpoint is then determined by `r+s=b-1` and is locally clean.  For this base the number of simultaneous outer levels is at most the number of zeros of that clean column, hence `O(T^(2/3+o(1)))` by (5.4).  This contributes
\[
 O(P\,T\,T^{2/3+o(1)})
 \ll T^{7/3+o(1)}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The middle-polynomial and any hard masks only reduce these overcounts.  This proves (6.2).  No `gcdtail_result.tex` statement is used.

This local dirty notion is the smallest safe replacement for the late “paired-polluted” citation.  It is also exactly tailored to the one-column estimate used next.

# 8. Fixed-`a` low-outer-root saturation: multiplicity two, not one

Define the distinct outer root set
\[
 \mathcal R_{a,p}=\{\alpha\in\mathbb F_p:M_a(\alpha)=0\},
 \qquad r_{a,p}=|\mathcal R_{a,p}|.
\tag{8.1}
\]
For a formal `U` root choose its canonical `Y=d_p(U)`.  The two outer roots are
\[
 \alpha=Y-h,
 \qquad
 \beta=Y+h.
\tag{8.2}
\]
They are distinct because `0<h<p` and `p` is odd.  Thus the root gives an unordered pair
\[
 \{\alpha,\beta\}\subset\mathcal R_{a,p}.
\tag{8.3}
\]
For fixed `a`, the unordered pair determines
\[
 Y={\alpha+\beta\over2}
\tag{8.4}
\]
and determines `h` up to the two possibilities corresponding to
\[
 2h=\pm(\beta-\alpha)\pmod p.
\tag{8.5}
\]
For each physical `h in {1,...,p-1}`, `b=h-a` is then unique.  Consequently the map from `(b,U)` to (8.3) has multiplicity at most `2`, and the source-order-legal inequality is
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{b\ge2\\a+b<p}}g_p^\square(a,b)
 \le 2\binom{r_{a,p}}2.}
\tag{8.6}
\]
The factor `2` cannot be dropped from the stated hypotheses: an unordered pair sees `h` and `p-h`.  If an additional range condition such as `h<p/2` is available in the actual AT block, then the factor may collapse to `1`, but it is unnecessary for exponents.

# 10. Exact residual: long, locally clean two-column codegree

The global assertion `pi(r)=pi(s)` should **not** be built into the residual family.  It is true on the regular nonwrapping subfamily, but the parity analysis above exhibits a boundary branch where the middle polynomial has a different exact meaning.  Fortunately the desired two-column reduction does not need that projective-color assertion.

Define `mathscr B_p^{lc}(T,D)` to be the set of pairs `(b,U)` such that

- `U in F_p^*` is a square and `d_p(U)>D`;
- the middle equation `(M_b/Y^{epsilon_b})(Y)=0` holds for the canonical lift `Y=d_p(U)`;
- with `{r,s}` given by (2.6), both `r` and `s` lie outside `mathcal P_{p,T}`;
- `b` is in the possible AT dyadic/hard range (or simply `2<=b<min(2T,p)` as a harmless overdomain).

For a selected atom `x` and such a base put
\[
 K_x(b,U)
 =\sum_a I_x(a,b)
   \mathbf 1_{\{r_{a,p_x}>R\}}
   \mathbf 1_{\{N_a(r)=0\}}
   \mathbf 1_{\{N_a(s)=0\}},
\tag{10.1}
\]
where the `a`-sum is restricted to the actual hard AT domain (`T<=a+b<2T`, `a+b<p`, product/gcd masks, etc.).  Then, after the three saturations above, the remaining gcd first moment is **exactly**
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak G_T^{\rm res}
 =\sum_xw_x
   \sum_{(b,U)\in\mathscr B_{p_x}^{lc}(T,D)}K_x(b,U).}
\tag{10.2}
\]
There is no factor `2` because `mathscr B` is indexed by `U`, not by `Y`.

Equation (10.1) is the desired long-clean two-column codegree: for each middle base `(b,U)`, it asks for common zero levels of the two fixed clean columns `r` and `s`.

The trivial base-size bound is source-order legal:
\[
 |\mathscr B_p^{lc}(T,D)|\ll T^2,
\tag{10.3}
\]
because there are `O(T)` possible `b`, and for each `b` the nonzero split roots of `M_b/Y^{epsilon_b}` are `O(b)=O(T)`.

For one locally clean column, `prop:column` gives only
\[
 K_x(b,U)\ll T^{2/3+o(1)}.
\tag{10.4}
\]
Thus the existing independent-column estimate still gives `T^(8/3+o(1))`.  To obtain the corrected gcd-amplifier target
\[
 \mathfrak G_T\ll T^{76/31-\eta}M_0,
\tag{10.5}
\]
the residual must supply average codegree
\[
 {T^{76/31}\over T^2}=T^{14/31},
\tag{10.6}
\]
which is a gain
\[
 {2\over3}-{14\over31}={20\over93}
