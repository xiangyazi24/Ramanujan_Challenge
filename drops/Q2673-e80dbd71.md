# P3.2 HLOAD-M1: telescoping companion determinant audit

## Verdict

The natural companion determinant does **not** carry a private incidence prime.  In fact it has an exact opposite interpretation: after the local factorial gauge it is precisely the standard gap continuant, so it detects a **repeat of the same prime at a second residual**.

For

```text
P(t)=34*t^3+51*t^2+27*t+5,
```

let `a,b` be the two Apéry solutions

```text
a_0=0, a_1=6,
b_0=1, b_1=5.
```

For integers `0<r<s`, put

```text
T_(r,s) = b_r*a_s - b_s*a_r.
```

Define the factorial-gauged gap continuants

```text
C_0(x)=0,
C_1(x)=1,
C_(h+1)(x)=P(x+h)*C_h(x)-(x+h)^6*C_(h-1)(x).
```

Then, with `h=s-r`,

```text
T_(r,s)
 = 6*C_h(r) / product_(j=r+1..s) j^3.                 (1)
```

Consequently, if `p>=7`, `s<p`, and `p|b_r`, then

```text
p|T_(r,s)  <=>  p|C_h(r)  <=>  p|b_s.               (2)
```

So for a fixed-q incidence column, an adjacent-anchor determinant carries `p` exactly when that same prime occurs again at the adjacent residual.  A private column gives a `p`-unit, not a `p`-divisor.

The most economical Casoratian correction that *does* force the private pin is

```text
r^3*b_(r-1)*T_(r,s) + 6*b_s
 = r^3*b_r*T_(r-1,s).                                (3)
```

After the same local denominator clearing, (3) becomes exactly

```text
Q_(r,s) * (r^3*b_(r-1)*T_(r,s)+6*b_s)
 = 6*b_r*C_(h+1)(r-1),                               (4)

Q_(r,s)=product_(j=r+1..s) j^3.
```

Thus the correction catches a private `p|b_r` only by reinstating the **whole endpoint factor `b_r`**.  The symmetric correction at `s` likewise reinstates `b_s`:

```text
(s+1)^3*b_(s+1)*T_(r,s)+6*b_r
 = (s+1)^3*b_s*T_(r,s+1),                            (5)
```

and hence

```text
Q_(r,s) * ((s+1)^3*b_(s+1)*T_(r,s)+6*b_r)
 = 6*b_s*C_(h+1)(r).                                 (6)
```

This destroys the hoped-for height telescoping.  Since

```text
b_r >= binom(2r,r)^2 >= 16^r/(2r+1)^2,
```

any nonzero corrected integer in (4) that retains its forced `p`-support has

```text
log |6*b_r*C_(h+1)(r-1)|
 >= r*log(16) - O(log r).                            (7)
```

There is no adjacent-gap telescope in this term.  Dividing out `b_r` restores the small gap continuant but simultaneously deletes the only universally forced private-prime factor.

There is a second, independent height obstruction even before the correction.  The statement that `a_r/b_r` approaches zeta(3) makes `T_(r,s)` small in **archimedean value**, but it does not make its arithmetic height `exp(O(h))`.  Formula (1) gives the exact denominator

```text
Q_(r,s)=product_(j=r+1..s) j^3.
```

For an ordered chain `r_1<...<r_L`, these denominators telescope only to a factorial ratio:

```text
product_i Q_(r_i,r_(i+1))
 = (r_L!/r_1!)^3,                                    (8)
```

so

```text
sum_i log Q_(r_i,r_(i+1))
 = 3*sum_(j=r_1+1..r_L) log j.                       (9)
```

When the residual span is `Theta(N)`, this is `Theta(N log N)`, not `o(N)`.  On the numerator side `deg_x C_h=3(h-1)`, and a direct recurrence bound gives

```text
log |C_h(r)| <= (h-1)*(3*log(r+h)+O(1)).             (10)
```

Thus ordered gaps alone give at best `O(N log N)` arithmetic height in the worst allowed span.  The real cancellation in the Apéry approximants is not a sublinear-height certificate after denominator clearing.

For

Therefore the telescoping-companion mechanism cannot prove HLOAD-M1 on the private-incidence sector.  It gives a useful exact carrier for repeated columns, but those are precisely the columns for which one already has two same-characteristic zeros.  On a private column there is a strict dichotomy:


The first construction outside this scoped no-go would have to couple **different characteristics before endpoint-pin saturation** in a way that is not generated by the two-solution transfer determinant/Casoratian algebra.  Merely taking products, gcds, resultants, or adjacent-chain determinants of the `T_(r,s)` does not do this: at a private prime all of the relevant gap determinants are local units.

So the outcome is a concrete terminal no-go for the proposed adjacent-companion telescoping class, not a new HLOAD-M1 contraction.

# 2. Exact determinant identity

Let

```text
A_n=(n!)^3*a_n,
B_n=(n!)^3*b_n.
```

Multiplying the Apéry recurrence by `(n!)^3` gives, for either solution,

```text
X_(n+1)=P(n)*X_n-n^6*X_(n-1).                       (11)
```

For fixed `r`, define `C_h(r)` as above.  By induction in `h`, the coefficient of `X_(r+1)` in `X_(r+h)` is exactly `C_h(r)`.

Now take the determinant of the two factorial-gauged solutions.  The component proportional to `X_r` cancels, so

```text
A_r*B_(r+h)-B_r*A_(r+h)
 = C_h(r)*(A_r*B_(r+1)-B_r*A_(r+1)).                (12)
```

The ordinary Casoratian is

```text
a_(r+1)*b_r-a_r*b_(r+1)=6/(r+1)^3.
```

Therefore

```text
A_r*B_(r+1)-B_r*A_(r+1)
 = 6*(r!)^6.                                         (13)
```

Dividing (12) by `(r!)^3*((r+h)!)^3` gives

```text
T_(r,r+h)
 = 6*C_h(r)*(r!)^3/(r+h)!^3
 = 6*C_h(r)/product_(j=1..h)(r+j)^3.                (14)
```

This proves (1) exactly.

Two points matter for HLOAD-M1.

First, `T_(r,s)` has no remaining dependence on the selected endpoint values `b_r,b_s`: it is a pure connection coefficient of the recurrence.  The zeta(3) cancellation is real, but after using the exact recurrence it has collapsed to the gap continuant.

Second, local denominator clearing is explicit.  If

```text
Q_(r,s)=product_(j=r+1..s) j^3,
```

then

```text
Q_(r,s)*T_(r,s)=6*C_(s-r)(r) in Z.                  (15)
```

If one reduces the fraction, put

```text
g=gcd(6*C_h(r),Q_(r,s)).
```

Then its exact rational height is

```text
H_rat(T_(r,s))
 = max(|6*C_h(r)|/g, Q_(r,s)/g).                    (16)
```

No denominator issue is hidden.

# 3. The determinant is a repeat-incidence detector

Assume

```text
p>=7,
1<=r<s<p,
p|b_r.
```

Because `p>r`, the denominator of `a_r` is a `p`-unit.  Reduce the Casoratian

```text
a_r*b_(r-1)-a_(r-1)*b_r=6/r^3
```

modulo `p`.  Since `p|b_r`,

```text
a_r*b_(r-1)=6/r^3 != 0 mod p.                      (17)
```

Hence both `a_r` and `b_(r-1)` are `p`-units.

Now

```text
T_(r,s)
 = b_r*a_s-b_s*a_r
 = -b_s*a_r mod p.                                  (18)
```

Therefore

```text
p|T_(r,s) <=> p|b_s.                                (19)
```

Since every factor in `Q_(r,s)` is `<p`, (15) gives equivalently

```text
p|C_(s-r)(r) <=> p|b_s.                             (20)
```

This is exactly the standard gap-polynomial/restart law, now read as a statement about the companion determinant.

## Fixed-q adjacent anchors

Suppose the same prime `p` remains in the fixed-q cell at two anchors

```text
n_i < n_j,
r_i=n_i-q*p,
r_j=n_j-q*p.
```

Then

```text
r_j-r_i=n_j-n_i.                                    (21)
```

If `p|b_(r_i)`, (19) says

```text
p | T_(r_i,r_j)
 <=> p | b_(r_j).                                   (22)
```

Thus the adjacent-anchor determinant detects a repeated incidence of that same column.  If the incidence at `n_i` is private, then for every other anchor in the same q-cell

```text
p does not divide T_(r_i,r_j).                       (23)
```

This is an exact obstruction, not a height estimate.

# 4. Cross-prime adjacent residuals do not repair it

Let

```text
p|b_r,
ell|b_s,
p != ell,
max(r,s)<min(p,ell).
```

Then the same argument in the two characteristics gives

```text
p|T_(r,s)   <=> p|b_s,
ell|T_(r,s) <=> ell|b_r.                             (24)
```

Hence

```text
p*ell | T_(r,s)
```

requires the **cross rectangle**

```text
p|b_s and ell|b_r.                                  (25)
```

Two private incidences provide the opposite information.  In particular, a determinant between adjacent residuals belonging to two different private primes carries neither marked prime unless an additional cross-incidence occurs.

If a chosen second residual is at or beyond one of the marked primes, a factorial or lcm denominator clearing can itself contain that prime.  Such a factor is structural clearing content, not selectedness.  Passing to the reduced fraction (16), or equivalently saturating the denominator primes, removes it.  The clean range `max(r,s)<min(p,ell)` is enough for the HLOAD mechanism and is realized by the actual fixture below.

# 5. Smallest Casoratian correction and exact endpoint factorization

The failure in (18) is transparent: at the selected zero, the determinant leaves the unit `a_r` times the neighboring `b_s`.  The smallest available relation eliminating that unit is the adjacent Casoratian.

Starting from

```text
b_(r-1)*a_r = a_(r-1)*b_r + 6/r^3,
```

one obtains

```text
r^3*b_(r-1)*T_(r,s)+6*b_s
 = r^3*b_r*T_(r-1,s).                               (26)
```

Using (14) for the longer gap,

```text
r^3*T_(r-1,s)
 = 6*C_(h+1)(r-1)/Q_(r,s),
```

so

```text
Q_(r,s)*(r^3*b_(r-1)*T_(r,s)+6*b_s)
 = 6*b_r*C_(h+1)(r-1).                              (27)
```

This is the exact locally cleared correction.

At the other endpoint, the Casoratian at `s+1` gives

```text
(s+1)^3*b_(s+1)*T_(r,s)+6*b_r
 = (s+1)^3*b_s*T_(r,s+1),                           (28)
```

hence

```text
Q_(r,s)*((s+1)^3*b_(s+1)*T_(r,s)+6*b_r)
 = 6*b_s*C_(h+1)(r).                                (29)
```

Thus both one-step repairs have the same form:

```text
endpoint Apéry value * local gap continuant.         (30)
```

This is the smallest correction in the literal sense that the single adjacent Casoratian removes the surviving companion unit.  It also exposes why the repair is unusable for HLOAD-M1: the selected pin has returned as an explicit characteristic-zero factor.

# 6. Height ledger: where the telescope really lands

## 6.1 Natural determinant

For `R=r+h`, put

```text
B=120*R^3.
```

Because `P(t)<=117*(t+1)^3` for `t>=0`, the recurrence for `C_h` gives inductively

```text
|C_h(r)| <= B^(h-1).                                (31)
```

Indeed the induction step is

```text
|C_(j+1)|
 <= 117*R^3*B^(j-1) + R^6*B^(j-2)
 < B^j.
```

Therefore

```text
log |6*C_h(r)|
 <= (h-1)*(3*log R+log 120)+O(1).                   (32)
```

This is gap-local, but there is still one `log N` for each unit of gap.

The denominator is exact:

```text
log Q_(r,r+h)=3*sum_(j=r+1..r+h) log j.             (33)
```

For an ordered same-column chain

```text
r_1<r_2<...<r_L,
h_i=r_(i+1)-r_i,
```

one has the exact telescope

```text
product_i Q_(r_i,r_(i+1))
 = (r_L!/r_1!)^3,                                   (34)
```

not cancellation to a bounded denominator.  Thus

```text
sum_i log Q_i
 = 3*log(r_L!/r_1!).                                (35)
```

If the residual span is comparable to `N`, (35) is `Theta(N log N)`.  The sole fact that there are only `O_delta(log N)` anchors does not improve this; it says nothing that forces the total ordered span to be `o(N/log N)`.

So even on repeated columns, where `C_h` really does carry the prime, the raw arithmetic-height ledger obtained solely from ordered gaps is at best `O(N log N)` in the worst legal span.  It is not the desired `o(N)` theorem.

## 6.2 Corrected private carrier

The correction is worse in exactly the relevant way.  The Apéry definition contains the `k=r` summand

```text
binom(2r,r)^2,
```

so

```text
b_r >= binom(2r,r)^2.                               (36)
```

Since the central binomial coefficient is at least the average coefficient of `(1+x)^(2r)`,

```text
binom(2r,r) >= 4^r/(2r+1).                          (37)
```

Hence

```text
log b_r
 >= r*log 16 - 2*log(2r+1).                        (38)
```

The proven root-strip/nonvanishing theorem for the gap continuants implies `C_(h+1)(r-1) != 0` at nonnegative integral `r-1`; in any event a zero correction would be the identically useless carrier `0`.  For every nonzero corrected carrier (27),

```text
log |6*b_r*C_(h+1)(r-1)|
 >= r*log 16 - O(log r).                            (39)
```

For several private residuals the corresponding product ledger has the exact lower bound

```text
sum_private log |J_r|
 >= log(16)*sum_private r - O(M log N).              (40)
```

The right side is controlled by the **absolute residuals**, not by the adjacent gaps.  No telescoping identity removes it.

If the HLOAD residuals happen to lie in a fixed rank-interior subinterval `r>=delta*N`, then even one corrected carrier costs `Omega_delta(N)` and `M` such corrections can cost `Omega_delta(N*M)`.  Without such an interior assumption the precise statement is (40): an `o(N)` conclusion would require the new independent input

```text
sum_private r = o(N),                               (41)
```

which is not a consequence of the ordered-anchor gap telescope.

## 6.3 Why primitive normalization cannot rescue the correction

One may try to divide the right side of (27) by `b_r`.  That produces the small continuant

```text
C_(h+1)(r-1),
```

but then the selected prime is no longer forced.  In the actual fixture below the continuant factor is a marked-prime unit, so the loss is literal, not merely logical.

Taking a gcd with another local gap determinant has the same problem: a private prime is a unit on all the uncorrected gap determinants.  Taking a product of the left- and right-corrected carriers carries two private primes, but it contains `b_r*b_s`; its endpoint height is the sum of the two absolute-index costs.

Finally,

```text
gcd(b_r, n_i-r)
```

would of course recover the incidence prime because `n_i-r=q*p`.  But that is the incidence written back into the carrier; it is tautological and gives no independent height bound.

# 7. Local pin-ideal formulation of the no-go

The previous identities can be packaged as a precise scoped statement.

Fix a regular window with all recurrence denominators inverted, and localize also at `b_(r-1)`.  The Casoratian gives

```text
a_r
 = (a_(r-1)*b_r + 6/r^3)/b_(r-1).                  (42)
```

Every neighboring companion state obtained by a finite transfer segment is linear over this localized coefficient ring in `(a_r,b_r)`.  Substituting (42), every polynomial numerator built from such states becomes a polynomial in the single pin variable `b_r` with coefficients in the localized ring.

Therefore:

> **PRIVATE-PIN SATURATION.** If a local transfer/determinant expression is required to vanish for every specialization with `b_r=0`, using no extra Apéry-specific cross-index equation, then its primitive numerator lies in the principal ideal `(b_r)`.

This is just the factor theorem after (42): the constant term in `b_r` must vanish.

The natural determinant avoids `(b_r)` and hence is a unit at a private pin.  The corrected expression enters `(b_r)` and acquires the full endpoint height.  Products, exterior minors, and resultants inside this same finite transfer algebra do not change the dichotomy.

This statement is deliberately scoped.  It does not exclude a genuinely new mixed-characteristic relation among several different private columns that uses the fixed Apéry initial vector in a way not generated by the local two-solution transfer/Casoratian algebra.

# 10. Consequence for HLOAD-M1

The proposed mechanism hoped to exploit

```text
few ordered anchors
+ small adjacent companion determinants
+ telescoping gaps
```

to compress private incidence primes into `exp(o(N))` total characteristic-zero height.

The exact recurrence algebra shows that the three ingredients cannot coexist:

```text
A. keep the small gap continuant:
   T_(r,s) -> 6*C_h(r)
   => detects only same-prime repeats;

B. force a private incidence prime:
   one Casoratian correction
   => 6*b_r*C_(h+1)(r-1)
   => absolute endpoint height returns;

C. try to use rational-height telescoping:
   product denominators = (r_last!/r_first!)^3
   => generally exp(Theta(N log N)), not exp(o(N)).
```

Therefore this adjacent-companion / connection-coefficient route cannot reach HLOAD-M1 on the private-incidence sector without one genuinely new input beyond the two-solution recurrence algebra.

The smallest possible new input would have to be a **mixed-characteristic private-column identity**: an integer relation involving at least two different incidence primes whose marked divisibility survives after every individual endpoint factor `b_r` is primitively saturated.  No such relation is supplied by the Casoratian, the factorial-gauged transfer continuants, or their adjacent-anchor resultants.
