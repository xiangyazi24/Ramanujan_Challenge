# Two-prime gap polynomials: the optimal CRT construction and the exact obstruction

## Terminal verdict

There are two separate conclusions.

1. **As stated, the requested polynomial exists trivially, and the naive degree \(Z(p)Z(q)\) is not optimal.** Put
   
   \[
   A_p(T)=\prod_{a\in Z_p}(T-a),\qquad
   A_q(T)=\prod_{b\in Z_q}(T-b).
   \]
   
   Then
   
   \[
   \Psi^{\mathrm{exact}}_{p,q}(T)=qA_p(T)+pA_q(T)
   \tag{0.1}
   \]
   
   satisfies
   
   \[
   \Psi^{\mathrm{exact}}_{p,q}(m)\equiv0\pmod{pq}
   \iff
   m\bmod p\in Z_p\ \text{and}\ m\bmod q\in Z_q.
   \]
   
   It is nonzero modulo both \(p\) and \(q\), and
   
   \[
   \deg\Psi^{\mathrm{exact}}_{p,q}=\max\{Z(p),Z(q)\}
   =O(X^{2/3}),
   \]
   
   not \(Z(p)Z(q)=O(X^{4/3})\).

   Under the question's weaker condition “not identically zero modulo \(pq\),” one may even take \(qA_p\) or \(pA_q\), so the optimal degree is
   
   \[
   \min\{Z(p),Z(q)\}.
   \]
   
   This one-sided solution is degenerate for root counting because it is the zero polynomial modulo one of the two primes.

2. **There is no further compression to \(O(h^A)\) coming from the known gap-polynomial structure, where \(h=|p-q|\).** The obstruction is exact: a univariate polynomial congruence modulo the squarefree modulus \(pq\) splits into an independent polynomial congruence modulo \(p\) and one modulo \(q\). Consequently, any polynomial nonzero modulo both primes and vanishing on the full CRT rectangle \(Z_p\times Z_q\) must have degree at least
   
   \[
   \max\{Z(p),Z(q)\}.
   \tag{0.2}
   \]
   
   Thus a degree \(O(h^A)\) construction is **equivalent** to the new vertical estimate
   
   \[
   Z(p),Z(q)\ll h^A,
   \tag{0.3}
   \]
   
   not a route to proving it. For twin primes \(h=2\), this would already give a uniform \(O(1)\) bound for both zero sets on every active twin-prime pair. Nothing in the current gap-polynomial theory proves such a statement.

The conceptual mistake is that the subscript of \(N_h\) is an **index gap inside one characteristic**, not a gap between two characteristics. A twin atom gives one zero modulo \(p\) and one zero modulo \(q\). The certificate \(N_h\) requires two zeros modulo the **same** prime. The equality \(q-p=h\) does not supply the missing second local zero.

There is, however, a correct \(h\)-controlled two-prime polynomial for a different event. If the same two primes hit **both columns** \(m\) and \(m+g\), then

\[
\Theta_g(T):=N_g(T)\prod_{t=1}^{g}(T+t),
\qquad \deg\Theta_g=4g-3,
\tag{0.4}
\]

satisfies \(pq\mid\Theta_g(m)\). Here \(g\) is the column separation. This is exactly the codegree/twin-column mechanism already present in the repository; it does not apply to a single high-load column.

## 2. Exact CRT vanishing-ideal theorem

Assume throughout this section that \(Z_p,Z_q\ne\varnothing\). This is the active-prime case relevant to the question. Put

\[
s_p=|Z_p|,\qquad s_q=|Z_q|,
\]

and define the full periodic twin set

\[
\mathcal T_{p,q}
=
\{x\bmod pq:x\bmod p\in Z_p,\ x\bmod q\in Z_q\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

CRT identifies this set with the Cartesian product \(Z_p\times Z_q\).

### Theorem 2.1 — exact classification

Let

\[
A_p(T)=\prod_{a\in Z_p}(T-a)\in\mathbb F_p[T],
\qquad
A_q(T)=\prod_{b\in Z_q}(T-b)\in\mathbb F_q[T].
\tag{2.2}
\]

Under the coefficientwise CRT isomorphism

\[
(\mathbb Z/pq\mathbb Z)[T]
\simeq
\mathbb F_p[T]\times\mathbb F_q[T],
\tag{2.3}
\]

the ideal of polynomials vanishing on \(\mathcal T_{p,q}\) is

\[
I(\mathcal T_{p,q})=(A_p)\times(A_q).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Equivalently, choose CRT idempotents

\[
e_p\equiv1\pmod p,\quad e_p\equiv0\pmod q,
\qquad
e_q\equiv0\pmod p,\quad e_q\equiv1\pmod q.
\]

Every vanishing polynomial has the form

\[
\Psi(T)\equiv
 e_pA_p(T)U_p(T)+e_qA_q(T)U_q(T)
 \pmod{pq}
\tag{2.5}
\]

for some \(U_p\in\mathbb F_p[T]\), \(U_q\in\mathbb F_q[T]\).

#### Proof

Write \(\Psi_p,\Psi_q\) for the two reductions of \(\Psi\). If \(x\) is the CRT lift of \((a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q\), then

\[
\Psi(x)\equiv0\pmod{pq}
\iff
\Psi_p(a)=0\ \text{and}\ \Psi_q(b)=0.
\]

Fixing any \(b\in Z_q\) and varying \(a\in Z_p\) shows that \(\Psi_p\) vanishes on all of \(Z_p\). Similarly \(\Psi_q\) vanishes on all of \(Z_q\). Over a field, the vanishing ideal of a finite set of distinct points is generated by the product of the corresponding linear factors, giving (2.4). The converse is immediate.

This proof exposes the obstruction: after reduction modulo \(p\), the \(q\)-residue has disappeared completely. No polynomial manipulation can use the \(q\)-condition to lower the number of distinct \(p\)-roots.

### Corollary 2.2 — optimal degrees

Define the weak minimum degree by requiring only

\[
\Psi\not\equiv0\pmod{pq}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\mathrm{weak}}(p,q)=\min\{s_p,s_q\}.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Indeed, at least one of \(\Psi_p,\Psi_q\) is nonzero. If \(\Psi_p\ne0\), it has \(s_p\) distinct roots and hence degree at least \(s_p\); similarly for \(q\). Conversely, if \(s_p\le s_q\), then

\[
\Psi(T)=qA_p(T)
\tag{2.7}
\]

is zero identically modulo \(q\), is nonzero modulo \(p\), vanishes on \(Z_p\), and has degree \(s_p\).

If one imposes the analytically necessary nondegeneracy condition

\[
\Psi\not\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Psi\not\equiv0\pmod q,
\tag{2.8}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\mathrm{bi}}(p,q)=\max\{s_p,s_q\}.
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

The lower bound is the field root bound in both components. Equality is attained by taking integer lifts of \(A_p,A_q\) and setting

\[
\boxed{
\Psi^{\mathrm{exact}}_{p,q}(T)=qA_p(T)+pA_q(T).
}
\tag{2.10}
\]

Modulo \(p\), this is the nonzero scalar \(qA_p\); modulo \(q\), it is the nonzero scalar \(pA_q\). Therefore its roots modulo \(pq\) are **exactly** the CRT lifts of \(Z_p\times Z_q\).

Thus the true optimal degree is local, not multiplicative. The product

\[
\prod_{(a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q}(T-\operatorname{CRT}(a,b))
\]

of degree \(s_ps_q\) is a valid but very inefficient construction.

## 3. The interval-truncated version \(m<X^2\)

The project usually works with

\[
M=X^2<pq
\]

and the truncated fiber

\[
\mathcal T_{p,q}(M)
=
\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p,\ m\bmod q\in Z_q\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let its two local projections be

\[
R_p(M)=\{m\bmod p:m\in\mathcal T_{p,q}(M)\}\subseteq Z_p,
\]

\[
R_q(M)=\{m\bmod q:m\in\mathcal T_{p,q}(M)\}\subseteq Z_q,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and put \(r_p=|R_p(M)|\), \(r_q=|R_q(M)|\).

Exactly the same argument gives:

- under the weak nonzero condition, the minimum degree is
  \(\min(r_p,r_q)\);
- under nonvanishing modulo both primes, the minimum degree is
  \(\max(r_p,r_q)\).

The relevant construction is

\[
q\prod_{a\in R_p(M)}(T-a)
+
 p\prod_{b\in R_q(M)}(T-b).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore truncation does not create a genuinely mixed polynomial either. It merely replaces the full zero sets by the local projections of the actual fiber. A degree \(O(h^A)\) result in the truncated setting is exactly a bound

\[
r_p,r_q\ll h^A,
\tag{3.4}
\]

which is itself the desired pair-projection estimate.

## 4. Why reducing the degree does not improve the CRT root count

A second trap is that the usual field statement “a nonzero polynomial of degree \(d\) has at most \(d\) roots” is false over \(\mathbb Z/pq\mathbb Z\).

For example,

\[
\Psi(T)=qA_p(T)
\]

has degree \(s_p\), is nonzero modulo \(pq\), but has

\[
q\,s_p
\]

roots modulo \(pq\): the \(p\)-coordinate must lie in \(Z_p\), while the \(q\)-coordinate is arbitrary.

If \(\Psi\) is nonzero modulo **both** primes and has degree \(d\), then CRT gives only

\[
\#\{x\bmod pq:\Psi(x)=0\}
=
\#V_p(\Psi_p)\,\#V_q(\Psi_q)
\le d^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This quadratic bound is sharp in form. The exact polynomial (2.10) has degree

\[
d=\max(s_p,s_q)
\]

and exactly \(s_ps_q\) roots. Hence replacing the naive degree \(s_ps_q\) by \(\max(s_p,s_q)\) does not improve the actual CRT fiber estimate: the product reappears in the root count.

For \(M<pq\), each residue class has at most one representative in \([0,M)\), so (4.1) gives \(J_{p,q}\le d^2\). But obtaining \(d\ll h^A\) is, by Section 2 or 3, already equivalent to bounding the relevant local projection sets by \(O(h^A)\).

## 5. Why the prime gap \(q-p\) does not activate \(N_h\)

Assume \(p<q=p+h\). Write a twin column as

\[
m=up+r=vq+s,
\qquad 0\le r<p,\quad0\le s<q.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
r-s=vq-up=vh-(u-v)p.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
r\equiv s+vh\pmod p,
\qquad
s\equiv r-uh\pmod q.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The twin condition says

\[
r\in Z_p,\qquad s\in Z_q.
\tag{5.4}
\]

To invoke \(N_h\) modulo \(p\), one would need

\[
r\in Z_p\quad\text{and}\quad r+h\in Z_p.
\]

To invoke it modulo \(q\), one would need

\[
s\in Z_q\quad\text{and}\quad s+h\in Z_q.
\]

Neither follows from (5.3)–(5.4). The fact that \(s\) is a zero in characteristic \(q\) gives no statement that \(s\), \(s+vh\), or any related index is a zero in characteristic \(p\).

This is not merely a missing clever substitution. The CRT vanishing-ideal theorem proves that any final univariate polynomial modulo \(p\) must annihilate the relevant \(p\)-residues by itself. Information about \(Z_q\) cannot lower that local root requirement.

Reflection also does not repair the problem. A zero \(r\in Z_p\) has the reflected partner

\[
p-1-r\in Z_p,
\]

but the resulting local gap is

\[
g_p=|p-1-2r|,
\]

which depends on \(p\) and \(r\). The corresponding gap for \(q\) is generally different. There is no common gap \(h=q-p\), and the reflected second columns for different primes do not align at one global integer.

This is the repository's certificate-parity obstruction in concrete form:

- \(N_g\) certifies **two local zero incidences at one prime**;
- a high-load atom supplies **one local zero incidence at each of many primes**.

The missing operation is a cross-characteristic transfer from one zero modulo \(q\) to a second zero modulo \(p\). No such identity is present in the recurrence or the compatible-system structure currently available.

## 6. What the gap-polynomial dependencies actually construct

The gap family does impose strong internal dependencies. They lead to two precise polynomial constructions, neither of which has the requested prime-gap consequence.

### 6.1 A local annihilator of degree \(O(p^{2/3})\)

Fix \(2\le H\le p\), partition \([0,p-1]\) into consecutive blocks of length at most \(H\), and let \(F_{p,H}\) be the set consisting of the first element of \(Z_p\) in each nonempty block.

Define

\[
G_{p,H}(T)
=
\prod_{a\in F_{p,H}}(T-a)
\prod_{g=2}^{H-1}N_g(T-g).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then every element of \(Z_p\) is a root of \(G_{p,H}\) modulo \(p\).

Indeed, a first zero in a block is killed by its linear factor. If \(z\) is not first, let \(a\) be the first zero in the same block and put \(g=z-a\). No consecutive zeros gives \(g\ge2\), while the block length gives \(g\le H-1\). Since \(a,a+g=z\in Z_p\),

\[
N_g(a)\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

so \(N_g(z-g)=0\), and the factor \(N_g(T-g)\) kills \(z\).

Moreover \(G_{p,H}\not\equiv0\pmod p\), because each translated \(N_g\) is nonzero modulo \(p\), and

\[
\deg G_{p,H}
\le
\left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil
+
\sum_{g=2}^{H-1}3(g-1)
=
\left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil
+
\frac32(H-1)(H-2).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Optimizing at \(H\asymp(p/3)^{1/3}\) gives

\[
\deg G_{p,H}
\le
\frac{3^{4/3}}2p^{2/3}+O(p^{1/3}).
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is a polynomial form of the existing zero-count proof. It explains exactly how the gap dependencies reduce a naive description to \(O(p^{2/3})\), but also why they stop there: one unpaired “first zero” remains in every block.

Combining the two local annihilators gives

\[
\Psi_{p,q,H}(T)=qG_{p,H}(T)+pG_{q,H}(T),
\tag{6.4}
\]

which is nonzero modulo both primes and has degree \(O(X^{2/3})\). Again, no factor depending only on \(h=q-p\) appears.

If one tries to choose \(H\) as a function of the prime gap \(h\), the degree has the form

\[
\frac pH+O(H^2).
\tag{6.5}
\]

For the typical regime \(h=X^{o(1)}\), no choice of \(H=h^{O(1)}\) removes the \(p/H\) term. That term is the number of local anchor zeros for which no same-prime gap certificate has yet been supplied.

### 6.2 The genuine wrap-completed two-prime gap polynomial

Let \(1\le g<X\) be a **column gap**, and define

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_g(T)=N_g(T)\prod_{t=1}^{g}(T+t).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Then

\[
\deg\Theta_g=3(g-1)+g=4g-3.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Suppose a prime \(p\in(X,2X]\) hits both \(m\) and \(m+g\). Put \(r=m\bmod p\). Since \(g<p\), there are two cases.

- If \(r+g<p\), then \(r,r+g\in Z_p\), so
  
  \[
  p\mid N_g(r)=N_g(m).
  \]

- If \(r+g\ge p\), put \(j=r+g-p\in[0,g-1]\). Then
  
  \[
  p\mid m+g-j,
  \]
  
  and \(g-j\in[1,g]\), so one factor in
  \(\prod_{t=1}^{g}(m+t)\) is divisible by \(p\).

Therefore

\[
p\mid\Theta_g(m)
\tag{6.8}
\]

for every prime hitting both columns, including the wrap case. Since \(N_g(m)>0\) for \(m\ge0\), the integer \(\Theta_g(m)\) is nonzero. Also \(\Theta_g\) is a nonzero polynomial modulo every relevant prime.

Consequently, if two distinct primes \(p,q\) both hit \(m\) and \(m+g\), then

\[
\boxed{pq\mid\Theta_g(m).}
\tag{6.9}
\]

This is the correct “two-prime gap polynomial.” It is universal, has degree \(O(g)\), and its value is common to every prime in the codegree \(W(m,m+g)\). It is essentially the two cases of `lem:atomtail-dichotomy` packaged into one polynomial.

But (6.9) uses a second **column** \(m+g\). A single twin atom at \(m\) does not provide one. Substituting \(g=q-p\) is unjustified.

### 6.3 The closest direct cross-prime certificate from the endpoint identity

The endpoint factorization in `proof.tex` states

\[
N_H(-r)
=
(-1)^{r-1}b_{r-1}b_{H-r}
((r-1)!)^3((H-r)!)^3.
\tag{6.10}
\]

If \(a\in Z_p\) and \(b\in Z_q\), set \(H=a+b+1\) and \(r=a+1\). Then

\[
N_{a+b+1}(-a-1)
=
(-1)^a(a!b!)^3b_ab_b,
\tag{6.11}
\]

so

\[
pq\mid N_{a+b+1}(-a-1).
\tag{6.12}
\]

Thus the current \(N\)-family can indeed combine one \(p\)-zero and one \(q\)-zero into one nonzero integer certificate. The failure is exact:

- its order is \(a+b+1=O(X)\), not \(O(h^A)\);
- both the order and the evaluation point depend on the two CRT residues;
- it is not one polynomial in the global variable \(m\);
- different prime pairs at one high-load column produce different certificates.

Interpolating or eliminating the varying pair \((a,b)\) returns one to the CRT vanishing ideal and its local degree lower bound.

## 7. Exact no-go theorem for the proposed \(O(h^A)\) route

For the full periodic twin condition, Theorem 2.1 gives the following equivalence.

### Theorem 7.1

Let \(D\ge0\). There exists a polynomial \(\Psi\in\mathbb Z[T]\) such that

1. \(\Psi(x)\equiv0\pmod{pq}\) for every \(x\in\mathcal T_{p,q}\);
2. \(\Psi\not\equiv0\pmod p\) and \(\Psi\not\equiv0\pmod q\);
3. \(\deg\Psi\le D\);

if and only if

\[
\max\{Z(p),Z(q)\}\le D.
\tag{7.1}
\]

With only the weak condition \(\Psi\not\equiv0\pmod{pq}\), the corresponding criterion is

\[
\min\{Z(p),Z(q)\}\le D.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For the interval-truncated condition, replace \(Z(p),Z(q)\) by the projection sizes \(r_p,r_q\) from Section 3.

Therefore the hoped-for statement

\[
\deg\Psi_{p,q}\ll |p-q|^A
\tag{7.3}
\]

is not a consequence of the existing gap-polynomial identities. In its useful bi-nonzero form it is precisely the new assertion

\[
Z(p),Z(q)\ll |p-q|^A.
\tag{7.4}
\]

The current theorem \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\) gives the unconditional degree

\[
\deg\Psi_{p,q}=O(X^{2/3}),
\tag{7.5}
\]

and no dependence on the prime gap.

The sharpness discussion already present in `proof.tex` is consistent with this barrier: degree bounds for the family \(N_g\) alone allow abstract zero configurations of size \(p^{2/3}\). Internal pairwise dependencies can prove a zero-count theorem; they cannot make a single nonzero univariate polynomial have fewer degree than its number of distinct roots.

## 8. Why a pair-dependent polynomial does not prove the Fable collapse

Even the optimal polynomial (2.10) is not an anti-concentration certificate for \(K_X(m)\).

Suppose \(m\) is hit by \(K\) primes. For every pair \(p,q\) among them one obtains

\[
pq\mid\Psi_{p,q}(m),
\]

but the polynomial and its value change with \((p,q)\). There is no single nonzero integer whose number of shell prime divisors controls all \(K\) primes. Multiplying all pair-dependent certificates makes the degree and logarithmic height grow like \(\binom K2\), which is circular.

The codegree polynomial \(\Theta_g\) avoids this problem because, for fixed \(m,g\), every prime hitting both columns divides the **same** integer \(\Theta_g(m)\). That is why it gives the existing bound for \(W(m,m+g)\). To turn it into a bound for \(K_X(m)\), one would need a new localization theorem of the form

\[
K_X(m)\ \text{large}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W(m,m+g)\ \text{large for some common }g<X.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Reflection gives each prime its own second zero, but the resulting global gaps depend on the prime and residue; it does not provide the common \(g\) in (8.1).

The genuinely parity-breaking alternative would be a **universal cross-characteristic certificate**: for each prime gap \(h\), a controlled finite family

\[
\Phi_{h,j}(T)\in\mathbb Z[T],
\qquad j\le h^{O(1)},
\tag{8.2}
\]

of degree and logarithmic height \(h^{O(1)}\), independent of the individual pair except through \(h\), such that every twin atom for \(q-p=h\) makes one nonzero value \(\Phi_{h,j}(m)\) divisible by \(pq\). No such transfer identity follows from the present recurrence. It would have to relate reductions of the Apéry sequence in two different characteristics, rather than two indices in one characteristic.

That is the exact remaining mathematical content. Repackaging the local zero sets by CRT does not supply it.

## 9. Final answer to the question

- **Can one construct a nonzero polynomial modulo \(pq\) with the requested vanishing property?** Yes. The optimal exact-root construction is
  
  \[
  \Psi_{p,q}(T)=q\prod_{a\in Z_p}(T-a)
  +p\prod_{b\in Z_q}(T-b),
  \]
  
  of degree \(\max\{Z(p),Z(q)\}=O(X^{2/3})\). Under the weak wording, a one-sided construction has degree \(\min\{Z(p),Z(q)\}\).

- **Does the prime gap \(h=|p-q|\) reduce this degree to \(O(h^A)\)?** Not from the known structure. A useful bi-nonzero polynomial of degree \(D\) exists exactly when both local zero sets, or both truncated projections, have size at most \(D\). Thus \(O(h^A)\) is equivalent to a new zero-count theorem in terms of the prime gap.

- **Can \(N_h\) be used with \(h=|p-q|\)?** No. \(N_h\) detects two zeros separated by \(h\) in one \(Z_p\). A twin atom gives one zero in \(Z_p\) and one in \(Z_q\). The prime gap does not convert one into the other.

- **What is the correct low-degree two-prime polynomial supplied by the current theory?** For two columns separated by \(g\),
  
  \[
  \Theta_g(T)=N_g(T)\prod_{t=1}^{g}(T+t)
  \]
  
  has degree \(4g-3\), is nonzero, and satisfies \(pq\mid\Theta_g(m)\) whenever both \(p\) and \(q\) hit both \(m\) and \(m+g\).

- **Exact obstruction:** the spectrum of \(\mathbb Z/pq\mathbb Z\) is the disjoint union of the \(p\)- and \(q\)-fibers. A univariate congruence modulo \(pq\) contains no intrinsic cross-characteristic coupling. Gap-polynomial dependencies are horizontal within one fiber; cross-prime anti-concentration requires a new vertical transfer or a theorem forcing many primes to share one second column.
