## Verdict

Let

\[
B_m=\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj^2\binom{m+j}{j}^2,

\[

   \[
   \boxed{p\mid\gamma_K\Longrightarrow p\mid B_{4K}.}
   \]
   This includes all collision cases \(p\le K\). The proof uses a canonical \(p\)-unit interpolation node cut immediately above the deepest borrow in the base-\(p\) subtraction of \(K\) from \(4K-1\), followed by Gessel--Lucas.

2. **For a top-quarter prime \(3K<p\le4K\), the statement is exact and squarefree:**
   \[
   \boxed{v_p(\gamma_K)=\mathbf 1_{p\mid B_{4K}}.}
   \]

3. **I did not obtain a proof of the full valuation statement \(\gamma_K\mid B_{4K}\), and I did not find an exact counterexample.** The computation through \(K=128\) is therefore consistent evidence, not a theorem.    B_3=1445=5\cdot17^2,

## 1. Exact Lagrange form of the weights

The signed weights are the Lagrange coefficients for evaluating a degree-\(K\) polynomial at \(n\) from the nodes \(0,1,\ldots,K\):

\[
(-1)^{K-i}w_i
=\prod_{\substack{0\le j\le K\\j\ne i}}
\frac{n-j}{i-j}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
 w_i
 =\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i}
 =\frac{n}{n-i}\binom{n-1}{K}\binom Ki.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The constant-polynomial interpolation identity gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=0}^K(-1)^{K-i}w_i=1.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Thus the bare weights are primitive:

\[
\gcd_{0\le i\le K}w_i=1.
\tag{1.4}
\]

For a fixed prime \(p\), write

\[
U_p=\{0\le i\le K:p\nmid w_i\}.
\]

Equation (1.3) already shows \(U_p\ne\varnothing\), but the radical proof needs a canonical member whose base-\(p\) digits are a suffix of \(n\).

## 2. Complete base-\(p\) characterization of \(U_p\)

Put

\[
L=n-K-1=3K-1.
\]

Because \(w_i\) is a product of two ordinary binomial coefficients, Kummer gives the exact criterion

\[
\boxed{
i\in U_p
\iff
\begin{cases}
\text{the addition }i+(n-i)=n\text{ is carry-free in base }p,\\
\text{the addition }(K-i)+L=n-i-1\text{ is carry-free in base }p.
\end{cases}}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This can be written as a digit automaton. Write

\[
n=\sum_s n_sp^s,\quad K=\sum_s k_sp^s,\quad
i=\sum_s i_sp^s,\quad L=\sum_s\ell_sp^s.
\]

Compute the digits \(d_s\) of \(K-i\) by

\[
\varepsilon_0=0,
\qquad
d_s=k_s-i_s-\varepsilon_s+p\varepsilon_{s+1},
\]

where

\[
\varepsilon_{s+1}=
\mathbf 1_{i_s+\varepsilon_s>k_s},
\qquad 0\le d_s<p.
\]

Since \(i\le K\), the final borrow vanishes. Then

\[
\boxed{
i\in U_p
\iff
i_s\le n_s\text{ for every }s,
\quad d_s+\ell_s\le p-1\text{ for every }s.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This handles \(p\le K\) without pretending that the interpolation nodes remain distinct modulo \(p\).

### Residue-tree formulation

For \(a\ge1\), define

\[
C_a(x)=\#\{j\in\{0,\ldots,K\}:j\equiv x\pmod{p^a}\}.
\]

Taking \(p\)-adic valuations in (1.1) gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
v_p(w_i)=
\sum_{a\ge1}
\left(
C_a(n)-C_a(i)+1-\mathbf 1_{i\equiv n\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p^a)}
\right).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For an interval of consecutive integers, two residue-class counts modulo \(p^a\) differ by at most one. Every summand in (2.3) is therefore nonnegative. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
i\in U_p
\iff
\text{for every }a\text{ at which }i\not\equiv n\pmod{p^a},
\quad C_a(i)=C_a(n)+1.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

In words: after an index leaves the \(p\)-adic residue path of \(n\), it must enter a heavy residue class at every subsequent scale. Formula (2.4) is a collision-safe characterization of the entire unit set.

## 3. The canonical deepest-borrow unit index

The preceding description is exact but larger than needed for the radical implication.

Since

\[
w_0=\binom{n-1}{K}=\binom{4K-1}{K},
\]

if \(p\nmid w_0\), then \(0\in U_p\). Because \(B_0=1\), this immediately gives

\[
p\nmid\gamma_K.
\tag{3.1}
\]

It remains to consider \(p\mid w_0\). Define the base-\(p\) borrow-boundary set

\[
\mathcal J_p(n,K)=
\left\{a\ge1:
K\bmod p^a>(n-1)\bmod p^a
\right\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Kummer says that its cardinality is

\[
|\mathcal J_p(n,K)|=v_p\binom{n-1}{K}>0.
\]

Let

\[
a_*=\max\mathcal J_p(n,K),
\qquad
r=n\bmod p^{a_*}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 — endpoint and unit properties

One has

\[
1\le r\le K
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p\nmid w_r.
\tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

Write

\[
n=Qp^{a_*}+r,\qquad K=Ap^{a_*}+k,\qquad0\le r,k<p^{a_*}.
\]

If \(r=0\), then

\[
(n-1)\bmod p^{a_*}=p^{a_*}-1,
\]

so the strict inequality in (3.2) is impossible. Hence \(r>0\). The borrow inequality becomes

\[
k>r-1,
\]

so \(k\ge r\), and therefore \(r\le K\).

Because \(r\) is exactly the block of the lowest \(a_*\) digits of \(n\), Lucas gives

\[
p\nmid\binom nr.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Moreover,

\[
n-r-1=(Q-1)p^{a_*}+(p^{a_*}-1),
\]

\[
K-r=Ap^{a_*}+(k-r).
\]

The low block of the top is all \(p-1\), so it contributes no carry. If

\[
p\mid\binom{Q-1}{A},
\]

then a borrow in the quotient block would produce a borrow boundary strictly above \(a_*\), contradicting maximality. Hence

\[
p\nmid\binom{Q-1}{A}.
\]

Lucas now gives

\[
\binom{n-r-1}{K-r}
\equiv
\binom{p^{a_*}-1}{k-r}
\binom{Q-1}{A}
ot\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Equations (3.5) and (3.6) prove \(r\in U_p\). ∎

This \(r\) is the canonical unit node required below. It remains valid when \(p\le K\), where many original interpolation nodes collide modulo \(p\).

## 4. Unconditional radical theorem

### Theorem 4.1

For every \(K\ge0\) and every prime \(p\),

\[
\boxed{p\mid\gamma_K\Longrightarrow p\mid B_{4K}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Set \(n=4K\).

If \(p\nmid w_0\), then \(B_0w_0=w_0\) is a \(p\)-adic unit, so \(p\nmid\gamma_K\). Thus a prime divisor of \(\gamma_K\) must satisfy \(p\mid w_0\).

Let \(r=n\bmod p^{a_*}\) be the deepest-borrow index from Lemma 3.1. Since \(p\nmid w_r\) and \(p\mid\gamma_K\),

\[
p\mid B_r.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The base-\(p\) expansion of \(r\) is exactly the block of the lowest \(a_*\) digits of \(n\). Writing \(n=Qp^{a_*}+r\), Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
B_n\equiv B_QB_r\pmod p.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equation (4.2) therefore implies \(p\mid B_n=B_{4K}\). ∎

### What was and was not used

No nonconsecutive-root argument is needed for Theorem 4.1. Nonconsecutive roots are useful for cleaning adjacent coefficient blocks, but the deepest-borrow suffix is stronger: it turns one actual unit coefficient into a literal subset of the digit factors of \(B_{4K}\bmod p\).

## 5. Exact top-quarter shell theorem

Let

\[
3K<p\le4K
\]

be prime and put

\[
z=4K-p.
\]

Then \(0<z<K<p\) and \(4K<2p\).

### Lemma 5.1 — exact shell weight valuation

For \(0\le i\le K\),

\[
\boxed{v_p(w_i)=
\begin{cases}
0,&i=z,\\
1,&i\ne z.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

At \(i=z\),

\[
\binom{p+z}{z}\equiv1\pmod p,
\qquad
\binom{p-1}{K-z}\equiv(-1)^{K-z}\pmod p,
\]

so both factors are units.

If \(i>z\), the numerator interval of \(inom{4K}{i}\) contains \(p=4K-z\) exactly once, while the second binomial is a unit. If \(i<z\), the first binomial is a unit and the numerator interval of

\[
\binom{4K-i-1}{K-i}
\]

contains \(p\) exactly once. Every interval involved has length \(<p\), and \(4K<2p\), so no second multiple of \(p\) occurs. ∎

Because \(B_0=1\) and \(z\ne0\), (5.1) gives

\[
\boxed{v_p(\gamma_K)=\min\{1,v_p(B_z)\}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Also \(4K=p+z\), so Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
B_{4K}\equiv B_1B_z=5B_z\pmod p.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Every prime in this shell is \(>5\), hence \(5\) is a unit. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
p\mid\gamma_K
\iff p\mid B_z
\iff p\mid B_{4K}.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Combining (5.2) and (5.4),

\[
\boxed{
v_p(\gamma_K)=\mathbf1_{p\mid B_{4K}}
\qquad(3K<p\le4K).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the shell-primary component is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{\substack{3K<p\le4K\\p\mid\gamma_K}}p
=
\prod_{\substack{3K<p\le4K\\p\mid B_{4K}}}p.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Prime-power uncertainty is therefore entirely a small-prime issue; every top-quarter contribution to \(\gamma_K\) is already squarefree.

## 6. Why Gessel--Lucas alone cannot prove full valuations

The implication in Theorem 4.1 is intrinsically modulo \(p\). A suffix zero can lose higher valuation when high digits are appended.

The smallest useful actual-Apéry warning is

\[
B_3=1445=5\cdot17^2,
\]

whereas direct use of the defining sum or the recurrence gives

\[
B_{20}=10090942470266994032842836001
\equiv85=5\cdot17\pmod{17^2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Hence

\[
v_{17}(B_3)=2,
\qquad
v_{17}(B_{20})=1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This disproves the false assertion

\[
v_p(B_{n\bmod p^a})\le v_p(B_n)
\]

without a depth condition.

It does **not** disprove the gcd claim. At \(K=5\), \(n=20\), the exact weight valuations are

\[
\bigl(v_{17}(w_0),\ldots,v_{17}(w_5)\bigr)
=(1,1,1,0,1,1).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The unit coordinate is \(i=3\), but the \(i=0\) coordinate already caps the local content exponent at one. Since \(17\mid B_3\),

\[
v_{17}(\gamma_5)=1=v_{17}(B_{20}).
\tag{6.4}
\]

The Newton carry strata protect exactly the valuation that survives. A valid all-power proof must retain those strata and the Dwork quotient multiplier; it cannot simply transfer the valuation of the deepest unit prefix.

## 7. Prime-local formulation of the full divisibility claim

Fix a prime \(p\), and set

\[
V(m)=v_p(B_m),
\qquad
\nu_i=v_p(w_i),
\]

\[
E=E_p(K):=v_p(\gamma_K)
=\min_{0\le i\le K}\bigl(V(i)+\nu_i\bigr).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The full claim \(\gamma_K\mid B_{4K}\) is equivalent to

\[
V(4K)\ge E
\tag{7.2}
\]

for every \(p,K\).

From (1.2), with \(n=4K\), define

\[
h=v_p\binom{n-1}{K},\quad d=v_p(n),\quad
c_i=v_p\binom Ki,\quad a_i=v_p(n-i).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\nu_i=h+d+c_i-a_i.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The \(i=0\) coordinate has \(V(0)=0\) and \(
u_0=h\), so

\[
\boxed{E\le h.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is why a prefix can only be asked to preserve at most the number of available borrow levels.

## 8. Exact projective Dwork quotient-state reduction

The Apéry numbers are constant terms of the integral Laurent polynomial

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz}.
\]

Its Newton polytope has the origin as its unique interior lattice point. The Samol--van Straten/Mellit--Vlasenko Dwork congruence therefore applies. In the form needed here, for \(u,m\ge0\) and \(s\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
B_{u+mp^s}B_{\lfloor u/p\rfloor}
\equiv
B_uB_{\lfloor u/p\rfloor+mp^{s-1}}
\pmod{p^s}.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

A primary reference is Anton Mellit and Masha Vlasenko, *Dwork's congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial*, arXiv:1306.5811.

For \(i<n\), put

\[
I_i=\left\lfloor\frac{i}{p}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
N=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p}\right\rfloor.
\]

If \(a_i=v_p(n-i)\ge1\), (8.1) gives the safe projective congruence

\[
\boxed{
B_nB_{I_i}\equiv B_iB_N\pmod{p^{a_i}}.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

The quotient multiplier \(B_{I_i}\) must not be cancelled unless it is known to be a \(p\)-adic unit.

Define

\[
R_i=(E-\nu_i)_+,
\]

so the content condition implies

\[
V(i)\ge R_i.
\tag{8.3}
\]

For \(a_i\ge1\), put

\[
L_i=\min\{a_i,V(N)+R_i\};
\]

put \(L_i=0\) when \(a_i=0\). From (8.2),

\[
V(n)+V(I_i)\ge L_i.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
V(n)\ge
\Phi_p(n,K;E):=
\max_{0\le i\le K}
\bigl(L_i-V(I_i)\bigr)_+.}
\tag{8.4}
\]

All Apéry indices on the right are strictly below \(n\).

### Named residual: 4K-DQSC

For the present problem define

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{4K\!\!-DQSC}_p(K):
\qquad
\Phi_p(4K,K;E_p(K))\ge E_p(K).}
\tag{8.5}
\]

If 4K-DQSC holds for every prime \(p\), then

\[
\gamma_K\mid B_{4K}.
\]

Conversely, every genuine counterexample must have the positive state defect

\[
\boxed{
\delta_p(K):=E_p(K)-\Phi_p(4K,K;E_p(K))>0.}
\tag{8.6}
\]

Positive defect is necessary, not sufficient: a state with \(\delta_p(K)>0\) could still satisfy \(V(4K)\ge E_p(K)\) for a reason not detected by this one-step functional.

This is strictly narrower than the original divisibility assertion: it uses only the explicit binomial valuations and Apéry valuations at

\[
i\le K,\qquad \lfloor i/p\rfloor<K,\qquad \lfloor4K/p\rfloor<4K.
\]

## 9. Logarithmic-size compression by borrow prefixes

The functional (8.4) has \(K+1\) states. It can be compressed to the actual borrow boundaries.

Let

\[
\mathcal J_p(n,K)=\{j_1<\cdots<j_h\}
\]

as in (3.2), and put

\[
r_t=n\bmod p^{j_t}.
\]

Write

\[
n=Q_tp^{j_t}+r_t,\qquad
K=A_tp^{j_t}+k_t.
\]

Then \(k_t\ge r_t\), and the exact weight valuation is

\[
\boxed{
\omega_t:=v_p(w_{r_t})
=v_p\binom{Q_t-1}{A_t}.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

The quotient-binomial term is essential: it is not always equal to the number of original borrows above \(j_t\), because replacing the low top block by \(p^{j_t}-1\) can reset a persistent borrow.

Define

\[
\rho_t=(E-\omega_t)_+,
\qquad
J_t=\left\lfloor\frac{r_t}{p}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\alpha_t=v_p(n-r_t)\ge j_t.
\]

Then (8.4) gives

\[
\boxed{
V(n)\ge
\Phi_p^{\mathrm{bor}}(n,K;E),}
\tag{9.2}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_p^{\mathrm{bor}}(n,K;E)=
\max_{1\le t\le h}
\left(
\min\{\alpha_t,V(N)+\rho_t\}-V(J_t)
\right)_+.}
\tag{9.3}
\]

At the deepest boundary \(j_h\), maximality gives

\[
\omega_h=0.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Thus the deepest state is the unit node used in the radical proof.

The complete full-valuation problem is now reduced to checking whether one of these quotient-prefix states, or a noncanonical state in the full functional, supplies all \(E\) factors. A counterexample must make every available numerator valuation disappear into the quotient coordinates \(B_{J_t}\).

## 10. Uniform prime-power cases already closed

The projective reduction immediately proves several nontrivial cases.

### 10.1 \(E=0\)

Trivial.

### 10.2 \(E=1\)

This is Theorem 4.1. Hence the full local valuation statement is unconditional whenever

\[
v_p(\gamma_K)\le1.
\]

In particular it covers every top-quarter prime.

### 10.3 Unit deepest quotient

Let \(r_h=n\bmod p^{j_h}\) be the deepest prefix and

\[
J_h=\left\lfloor\frac{r_h}{p}\right\rfloor.
\]

Because \(\omega_h=0\), the content condition gives

\[
V(r_h)\ge E.
\]

Also

\[
v_p(n-r_h)\ge j_h\ge h\ge E.
\]

If

\[
p\nmid B_{J_h},
\]

then (8.2) at \(i=r_h\) gives

\[
B_nB_{J_h}\equiv B_{r_h}B_N\equiv0\pmod{p^E}.
\]

The multiplier is a unit, so

\[
\boxed{p^E\mid B_n.}
\tag{10.1}
\]

The only unresolved states are therefore those in which one or more quotient-prefix Apéry numbers are themselves divisible by \(p\) and can absorb the projective valuation.

## 11. Why the full valuation claim is not yet decided

The exact data above show the remaining obstruction.

From

\[
B_nB_{J_t}\equiv B_{r_t}B_N\pmod{p^{\alpha_t}}
\]

and \(p^E\mid B_{r_t}B_N\), it is invalid to conclude \(p^E\mid B_n\) unless \(B_{J_t}\) is a unit. The only unconditional conclusion is

\[
V(n)+V(J_t)\ge E
\]

at sufficient depth. A nonunit quotient can absorb part of the valuation.

The actual \(17\)-adic calculation (6.1) shows that this is not a formal nuisance: an Apéry prefix can genuinely lose a power under extension. Consequently:

- Kummer and Lucas prove the radical theorem but do not control \(E\ge2\);
- ordinary Gessel--Lucas does not justify cancelling \(B_{J_t}\);
- a scalar congruence
  \[
  B_{pm+r}\equiv B_mB_r
  \pmod{p^{1+v_p(B_m)}}
  \]
  is false in the required generality;
- the safe object is the projective determinant (8.2).

No positive-defect state with

\[
V(4K)<E_p(K)
\]

is presently supplied by the repository or by this audit. Thus it would be incorrect to announce a counterexample. It would be equally incorrect to promote the computation through \(K=128\) to an all-\(K\) theorem.

The narrow residual is exactly:

> **4K-DQSC residual.** Prove \(\Phi_p(4K,K;E_p(K))\ge E_p(K)\) for every \(p,K\), or exhibit the least pair \((K,p)\) with positive defect and then verify directly that \(v_p(B_{4K})<E_p(K)\).

This residual is lower-dimensional, prime-local, and contains every denominator and every interpolation collision explicitly.

## 12. Does \(\gamma_K\) improve the top-quarter radical bound?

At the shell level, no new compression has yet been proved. Equation (5.6) says that the shell part of \(\gamma_K\) is exactly the radical one is trying to bound. Thus \(\gamma_K\) is an exact carrier, but not automatically a smaller one.

The elementary global height bound comes from the \(i=0\) term:

\[
\gamma_K\mid w_0=\binom{4K-1}{K}.
\tag{12.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\log\gamma_K
\le
\log\binom{4K-1}{K}
=4K H(1/4)+O(\log K),
\tag{12.2}
\]

where

\[
H(1/4)=\log4-\frac34\log3\approx0.562335.
\]

Since \(n=4K\), this is only

\[
\log\gamma_K\le0.562335\,n+O(\log n),
\]

not \(o(n)\). It is also above the raw quarter-shell mass \(n/4\). Hence neither the radical theorem nor a future proof of the full divisibility \(\gamma_K\mid B_{4K}\) would by itself settle the desired top-quarter radical estimate.

A non-tautological gain would require an additional theorem such as

\[
\log\gamma_K=o(K)
\]

or a primitive-factor/Bezout cancellation bound. The coefficient-subset entropy audit in Q8710 shows why absolute-size bounds after structural normalization do not provide such a saving.

The set \(U_p\) is given exactly by the two carry-free conditions (2.1), equivalently by the digit automaton (2.2), or by the residue-tree heavy-class condition (2.4).

### Theorem D — exact shell equality

For every prime \(3K<p\le4K\),

\boxed{v_p(\gamma_K)=\mathbf1_{p\mid B_{4K}}.}

### Theorem E — prime-power state bound

For every prime,

\[
\boxed{v_p(B_{4K})\ge
\Phi_p(4K,K;v_p(\gamma_K)),}
\]

with \(\Phi_p\) given explicitly by (8.4), and with the logarithmic canonical subfunctional (9.3).

### Remaining decision

The all-\(K\) divisibility

\[
\gamma_K\mid B_{4K}
\]

is proved for every local exponent \(0\) or \(1\), and at arbitrary exponent whenever the deepest quotient coordinate is a unit. In full generality it remains at the explicit 4K-DQSC gate; no exact counterexample is currently known.
