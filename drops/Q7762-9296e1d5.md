# 1. The claimed root-set equality is false

Let
\[

\]
and let
\[
  B_h=\{-1,-2,\ldots,-h\}\subset\mathbf F_p.
\]
Your identity proves, for \(p>h\), exactly
\[
\boxed{
 \operatorname{Root}_{\mathbf F_p}(N_h)\cap B_h
 =-\bigl((Z_p\cap[0,h-1])+1\bigr)
  \;\cup\;
  \bigl((Z_p\cap[0,h-1])-h\bigr).
}
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, at \(x=-r\),
\[
 N_h(-r)\equiv0\pmod p
 \iff b_{r-1}b_{h-r}\equiv0\pmod p,

because both factorials are \(p\)-units. Nothing in this argument evaluates \(N_h(x)\) for \(x\notin B_h\).

There is also an elementary degree warning: \(N_h\) has degree \(3(h-1)\), while the boundary formula supplies only \(h\) values. Even after removing the forced linear factor for even \(h\), those values cannot determine all roots of a polynomial of degree \(3h-4\).

# 2. What is genuinely vertical, and what is not

For a fixed prime \(p\), the equation
\[
 N_h(x)=0
\]
means that two rows of the same residual Apéry projective orbit coincide. The bridge multiplier, reflection law, degree bound, and resultant constructions all refine this one-prime return geometry. They can strongly constrain \(Z_p\), fibers, gaps, and local multiplicities.

They do **not**, by themselves, couple the choices made at two different primes. This is the real vertical obstruction. The anchored-rainbow-star construction in `hm3_result.tex` makes the logical point sharply: reflection symmetry, no-consecutive-zero constraints, small local fibers, gap certificates, and bounded pair codegrees can all hold prime by prime while the marked residues are adversarially aligned at one common integer across many primes. Therefore no theorem using only that package of local root-support axioms can imply the required cross-prime anti-alignment.

Two qualifications matter.

First, it is too strong to say that \(M_h\) has “no cross-prime content.” For fixed \(h\), \(M_h\in\mathbf Z[X]\), so its factorization and root count modulo varying primes are bona fide Frobenius statistics of one characteristic-zero polynomial. Fixed-\(h\) Chebotarev information is genuinely horizontal.

Second, that fixed-\(h\) horizontal information is not the needed diagonal theorem. In the target application, the gap, row, or bridge label varies with \(p\); degrees and conductors grow; and the observable is evaluated at a \(p\)-dependent point. A fixed-polynomial splitting theorem therefore does not control the cross-prime incidence sum. The missing ingredient is not merely “another root”; it is a compatible, scale-controlled horizontal coordinate.

A useful criterion for a genuinely new invariant is therefore:

* it is not determined by the support set \(Z_p\);
* it is canonically normalized, so independent row rescalings are impossible;
* it is the reduction of a \(p\)-independent rational/integer object, or belongs to a proven compatible system;
* its denominators are \(p\)-units in the high-prime range;
* the joint condition with \(b_r=0\) is codimension two rather than another reformulation of the same root;
* there is a plausible summable height or horizontal-dispersion estimate.

A raw mod-\(p^2\) Lucas quotient supplies the first new \(p\)-adic digit, but remains prime-indexed until one proves a descent law. A two-characteristic trace is much more machinery than is minimally required, and an auxiliary-\(\ell\) trace with \(\ell\ne p\) does not automatically control a defining-characteristic event at \(\ell=p\).

Thus the minimal new information is **one first Hasse/Frobenius jet transverse to the divisor \(b=0\)**.

# 5. Where the mod-\(p^2\) Lucas quotient fits

The minimal \(p\)-adic lift of the Lucas law is
\[
 \mathcal L_p(r)
 :=\frac{b_{p+r}-5b_r}{p}\pmod p.
\tag{8}
\]
It is well-defined because \(b_{p+r}\equiv5b_r\pmod p\). It certainly is not determined by \(Z_p\), but as written it is still indexed by \(p\), so it does not yet provide a characteristic-zero carrier.

The repository’s experimental script `cron_supercong_check.py` tests the stronger descent
\[
 b_{p+r}\equiv5b_r+10pD_r\pmod{p^2},
\tag{9}
\]
where
\[
 D_r=\sum_{k=0}^r
 \binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2
 \bigl(H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}\bigr).
\]
In the tested range this gives \(\mathcal L_p(r)\equiv10D_r\pmod p\). At present this is an experimental identity in the repository, not a banked theorem. Proving (9) uniformly in a clean range and then comparing \(D_r\) with \(J_r\) and \(\kappa_r\) is the shortest route to identifying which first-jet model is canonical.

The exact comparison gates are:

1. prove or refute (9) for all \(p>2r\);
2. on \(b_r=0\), test whether \(\mathcal L_p(r)=0\) is equivalent to \(J_r=0\) or \(K_r=0\) modulo \(p\);
3. test whether the three coordinates \(D_r,J_r,\kappa_r\) satisfy a rational two-dimensional relation over \(\mathbf Q(r)\);
4. only if all first-jet models collapse should one invest in a second Hasse jet or a two-characteristic extension trace.

An auxiliary-characteristic trace may eventually be the proof technology for horizontal equidistribution, but it is not the minimal invariant, and one must still bridge the defining-characteristic diagonal \(\ell=p\). The repository’s affine-codimension audit correctly warns that this bridge cannot be obtained by simply specializing a standard \(\ell\ne p\) trace-function theorem.
