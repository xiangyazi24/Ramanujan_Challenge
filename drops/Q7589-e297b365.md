## 1. Exact reduction of `M_T` to a prime-averaged marked AP3 count

Write

\[
X_p(m):={\bf 1}_{p\mid b_m}.
\]

For `2 <= h <= t <= T`, put

\[
r=t+h.
\]

Then

\[
r,\ r+h,\ r+2h=t+h,t+2h,t+3h\le 4T.
\]

By the support equivalence supplied in the question, for every `p>4T`,

\[
p\mid M_T
\iff
\exists\,2\le h\le t\le T:
X_p(t+h)X_p(t+2h)X_p(t+3h)=1.
\tag{1.1}
\]

It is useful to move the cutoff from `4T` to `10T`.  This costs only

\[
\sum_{p\le 10T}\log p=\vartheta(10T)=O(T).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Define the marked AP3 energy

\[
\mathcal A(T):=
\sum_{p>10T}\log p
\sum_{2\le h\le t\le T}
X_p(t+h)X_p(t+2h)X_p(t+3h).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Because `M_T` is radical, (1.1) gives the **unconditional exact reduction**

\[
\boxed{
\log M_T\le O(T)+\mathcal A(T).
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Equivalently, after interchanging the two finite sums,

\[
\mathcal A(T)=
\sum_{2\le h\le t\le T}
\log \operatorname{rad}_{>10T}
\gcd\bigl(b_{t+h},b_{t+2h},b_{t+3h}\bigr).
\tag{1.5}
\]

Here `rad_{>10T}` means the product of the distinct prime divisors larger
than `10T`.

This already identifies the minimal sufficient theorem.

### Conditional theorem MAP3

If there are absolute constants `eta>0` and `C` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(T)\le C T^{2-\eta}
}
\tag{MAP3_eta}
\]

for all sufficiently large `T`, then

\[
\boxed{
\log M_T=O(T^{2-\eta}).
}
\]

No pointwise assertion that every `Z(p)` is AP3-free is used or needed.

### A collision-energy statement that is slightly stronger but cleaner

Define

\[
\mathcal C(T):=
\sum_{p>10T}\log p
\sum_{2\le h\le t\le T}
X_p(t+h)X_p(t+2h).
\tag{1.6}
\]

Then an active prime in (1.1) contributes at least one term to
`mathcal C(T)`, hence

\[
\boxed{
\log M_T\le O(T)+\mathcal C(T).
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

Also

\[
\mathcal C(T)=
\sum_{2\le h\le t\le T}
\log \operatorname{rad}_{>10T}
\gcd(b_{t+h},b_{t+2h}).
\tag{1.8}
\]

Thus the prime-averaged pair-collision estimate

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C(T)\ll T^{2-\eta}
}
\tag{MCE_eta}
\]

is a sufficient large-sieve target.

An even more symmetric but stronger target is obtained from

\[
Z_p(4T):=\sum_{1\le m\le4T}X_p(m).
\]

Every active prime has at least three distinct marked zeros, so

\[
1_{p\mid M_T}\le {1\over3}{Z_p(4T)\choose2}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\log M_T
\le O(T)+{1\over3}
\sum_{p>10T}{Z_p(4T)\choose2}\log p,
\tag{1.9}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{p>10T}{Z_p(4T)\choose2}\log p
=
\sum_{1\le r<s\le4T}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>10T}\gcd(b_r,b_s).
\tag{1.10}
\]

The existing per-prime projective `ENERGY-3/2` theorem is not (1.9).  It
bounds a full projective-orbit energy **from above for each fixed p**.  An
active marked prime supplies only a constant lower contribution to that full
energy.  Summing the per-prime upper bound therefore does not bound the
number or logarithmic mass of active primes.  What is missing is specifically
an estimate of the form (MAP3_eta), (MCE_eta), or (1.9) **after averaging over
characteristics**.

## 2. Why the known `O(T^2)` is the natural one-coordinate bound

The Apéry numbers have exponential height.  The crude estimate

\[
\binom nk\le2^n,
\qquad
\binom{n+k}{k}\le 2^{n+k}\le4^n
\]

gives

\[
b_n
=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
\le (n+1)64^n,
\tag{2.1}
\]

so

\[
\log b_n=O(n).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Every high prime in `M_T` divides at least one `b_m` with `m<=4T`.  Therefore

\[
\log M_T
\le O(T)+\sum_{m\le4T}\log b_m
=O(T^2).
\tag{2.3}
\]

This derivation is important diagnostically: any power saving has to use the
**simultaneous collision** of two or three marked coordinates.  Repackaging a
single coefficient divisibility condition through Hasse, Cartier, a root
divisor, or a critical-value polynomial cannot by itself improve the exponent
in (2.3).

It also shows why a conventional modulus-aspect large sieve is poorly matched
to the problem.  A prime divisor of `b_m`, `m=O(T)`, can be as large as
`exp(O(T))`; a useful estimate has to be divisor/height sensitive rather than
paying a polynomial cost in the largest modulus.

## 3. Exact Hasse square and the stable low-coordinate prefix

Let

\[
A_p(a):=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m a^m,
\qquad
\Delta(a)=a^2-34a+1,
\]

and

\[
\epsilon_p={1-\left({-6\over p}\right)\over2}\in\{0,1\}.
\]

The exact Hasse factorization in `hasse_franel_descent.tex` is

\[
\boxed{
A_p(a)=\Delta(a)^{\epsilon_p}B_p(a)^2,
\qquad B_p(0)=1.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The degree is

\[
d_p:=\deg B_p={p-1-2\epsilon_p\over2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The two characteristic-zero branches are

\[
C_+(a)=\sum_{r\ge0}c_ra^r,
\qquad
C_-(a)=\sum_{r\ge0}d_ra^r,
\]

with recurrences

\[
4(r+1)^2c_{r+1}
=2(68r^2+34r+5)c_r-(2r-1)^2c_{r-1},
\tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
4(r+1)^2d_{r+1}
=2(68r^2+102r+39)d_r-(2r+1)^2d_{r-1},
\tag{3.4}
\]

starting from `c_{-1}=d_{-1}=0`, `c_0=d_0=1`.  Equivalently these are the
power-series solutions of the fixed second-order operators

\[
L_+=4\theta^2
-2a(68\theta^2+34\theta+5)
+a^2(2\theta+1)^2,
\tag{3.5}
\]

\[
L_-=4\theta^2
-2a(68\theta^2+102\theta+39)
+a^2(2\theta+3)^2,
\tag{3.6}
\]

where `theta=a d/da`.

The exact truncation statement is

\[
B_p(a)=
\begin{cases}
\sum_{r=0}^{(p-1)/2}\bar c_r a^r,
&\left({-6\over p}\right)=1,\\[1ex]
\sum_{r=0}^{(p-3)/2}\bar d_r a^r,
&\left({-6\over p}\right)=-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Now take `p>10T`.  Then, for `T>=2`,

\[
d_p>4T.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Hence throughout the entire marked window,

\[
B_p(a)\equiv
\begin{cases}
C_+(a),&(-6/p)=1,\\
C_-(a),&(-6/p)=-1
\end{cases}
\pmod{(p,a^{4T+1})}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

In particular

\[
\Delta^{\epsilon_p}B_p^2
\equiv \mathcal A(a)
\pmod{(p,a^{4T+1})},
\tag{3.10}
\]

where `mathcal A(a)=sum b_m a^m` is the characteristic-zero Apéry generating
series.

This is the first decisive obstruction to a purely local Hasse attack:
**the marked coefficients are in the stable prefix, before the truncation
edge where the characteristic p geometry becomes new.**

For `r<4T<p`, the factor `4(r+1)^2` in (3.3)--(3.4) is invertible modulo `p`.
Thus the ODE recurrence simply reproduces the reductions of the fixed
characteristic-zero coefficients.  It does not add a second, independent
condition relating which primes kill distinct indices `r,s=O(T)`.

## 4. The cover-adapted basis makes the coefficient/evaluation mismatch exact

Put

\[
\phi(x)={x(1-8x)\over1+x},
\qquad
\Psi_{p,m}(x)=x^m(1-8x)^m(1+x)^{p-1-m}.
\]

The exact finite cover identity is

\[
\boxed{
K_p(x)^2
=(1+x)^{p-1}A_p(\phi(x))
=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m\Psi_{p,m}(x).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The `Psi_{p,m}` are x-adically triangular with diagonal coefficient one, so

\[
\boxed{
[K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,m}}=b_m.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus there is no ambiguity about what the marked condition is: it is an
**exact coordinate zero of the Hasse square in the cover-adapted basis**.

For `1<=m<=p-2`, ordinary multiplicative-character orthogonality gives an
equivalent Mellin formula.  Indeed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(a)a^{-m}
&=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_j
  \sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}a^{j-m}\\
&=-b_m,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

because in the displayed range only `j=m` has exponent divisible by `p-1`,
and `p-1=-1` in `F_p`.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
X_p(m)=1
\iff
\mathscr M_p(m):=
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
\Delta(a)^{\epsilon_p}B_p(a)^2a^{-m}=0.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

For `p>10T`, the desired new theorem is consequently a horizontal statement
about simultaneous zeros of the finite Mellin coefficients

\[
\mathscr M_p(t+h),\quad
\mathscr M_p(t+2h),\quad
\mathscr M_p(t+3h),
\tag{4.5}
\]

averaged over `p,h,t`.

This is not the same problem as locating the roots of `B_p`.

## 5. What quadratic Frobenius orbits actually give, and why it is insufficient

The banked splitting fact

\[
\boxed{B_p(a)\mid a^{p^2}-a}
\tag{5.1}
\]

says that every root of `B_p` lies in `F_{p^2}`; equivalently every
irreducible factor has degree one or two.  Frobenius therefore acts on the
root multiset in orbits of size at most two.  Reciprocity adds the involution

\[
\alpha\longmapsto \alpha^{-1}.
\]

After closing under both operations, a root lies in an orbit of size at most
four.

This is strong **evaluation-side** structure.  It is not coefficient
anti-concentration.  If

\[
B_p(a)=\prod_i(1-\alpha_i^{-1}a),
\]

then its coefficients are elementary symmetric sums in the
`alpha_i^{-1}`, and the coefficients of `Delta^epsilon B_p^2` are convolutions
of those symmetric sums.  Frobenius/reciprocity pair the roots, but a vanishing
coefficient is a cancellation among many orbit contributions.  Nothing in
(5.1) bounds how often those cancellations can occur at prescribed low Mellin
frequencies.

The exact reflection also illustrates the scale mismatch.  Reciprocity of
`A_p` gives

\[
b_m\equiv b_{p-1-m}\pmod p.
\tag{5.2}
\]

When `p>10T` and `m<=4T`, the reflected index lies beyond `6T-1`, outside the
entire selector window.  Reflection therefore gives a second zero, but not a
second zero usable by the same `T`-scale marked selector.

Likewise, the Frobenius map on an evaluation root does not act as a small
translation on the Mellin exponent `m`.  The desired AP3 relation is additive
in the **index/frequency variable**; the available quadratic orbit is in the
**root variable**.  A theorem connecting those two is exactly the missing
input.

The empirical fact, recorded elsewhere in the repository, that actual Apéry
coefficient-zero sets are extremely sparse is therefore evidence for an
Apéry-specific Mellin theorem, not a consequence of (5.1).

## 6. Why fixed-depth Cartier does not repair the gap

For the high-prime range used above,

\[
1\le t+h<t+2h<t+3h\le4T<p.
\]

So every selected index is a **single base-p digit**.  Lucas/Cartier lifting
controls descendants such as `qp+r`, and the exact Lucas zero law says that a
lift is zero when one of its base digits is already a zero atom.  That is
valuable for propagating a known atom, but here all three events in (4.5) are
already at the atom level.

Consequently, a fixed-depth Cartier tower can replicate a marked zero into
higher p-adic digits without forcing a second low atom at a nearby frequency.
The square factorization and the ODE make the atom highly structured, but in
the stable prefix that structure is precisely the fixed characteristic-zero
recurrence (3.3)--(3.4).

A useful coefficient-level theorem would have to look genuinely like a
**two-characteristic Mellin dispersion estimate**.  For example, the
following dyadic form would suffice after summing dyadic prime ranges:

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\log p
\sum_{2\le h\le t\le T}
1_{\mathscr M_p(t+h)=0}
1_{\mathscr M_p(t+2h)=0}
\ll E(T,P),
\tag{6.1}
\]

with

\[
\sum_{P\text{ dyadic},\ P>10T}E(T,P)
\ll T^{2-\eta}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The exact additive-character expansion

\[
1_{\mathscr M_p(m)=0}
={1\over p}\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
 e_p\bigl(u\mathscr M_p(m)\bigr)
\tag{6.3}
\]

shows what such a large sieve must cancel: nontrivial joint phases in two
marked Mellin coefficients while `p` itself varies.  Neither the ODE nor
quadratic splitting currently supplies that cross-prime cancellation.

This is the precise conditional opening.  It is much weaker than universal
AP3-freeness.

## 7. A strict Hasse-polynomial countermodel to the *structural* implication

I now give a model that is substantially stronger than an abstract zero-set
model.  It preserves:

1. the same discriminant polynomial `Delta=a^2-34a+1`;
2. an exact Hasse square `A~=Delta^epsilon B~^2` of degree `p-1`;
3. normalization `B~(0)=1` and the same branch degrees;
4. a fixed p-independent second-order differential equation on each branch;
5. `B~ | a^(p^2)-a` (in fact all roots lie already in `F_p`);
6. reciprocal/anti-reciprocal `B~`, hence exact coefficient reflection of
   `A~`;
7. the exact same cover map `phi`, the same basis `Psi_{p,m}`, and the exact
   marked-coordinate identity; and
8. weighted marked prime mass `Theta(T^2)`.

It does **not** preserve the numerical coefficients of the exact Apéry
operators `L_+`,`L_-`; Section 8 proves that asking for those as well makes an
independent countermodel impossible.

### 7.1 Definition

For every prime `p>=5`, set

\[
\chi_p=\left({-6\over p}\right),
\qquad
\epsilon_p={1-\chi_p\over2},
\qquad
 d_p={p-1-2\epsilon_p\over2}.
\]

If `epsilon_p=0`, define

\[
\widetilde B_p(a)=1-a^{(p-1)/2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

If `epsilon_p=1`, put `n=(p-1)/2`, so `d_p=n-1`, and define

\[
\widetilde B_p(a)
=1+a+\cdots+a^{n-1}
={1-a^n\over1-a}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Finally set

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde A_p(a)
=\Delta(a)^{\epsilon_p}\widetilde B_p(a)^2.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then

\[
\deg\widetilde A_p=2\epsilon_p+2d_p=p-1,
\qquad
\widetilde B_p(0)=1.
\tag{7.4}
\]

### 7.2 Exact quadratic splitting

For `epsilon_p=0`, the roots of `B~_p` are the `d_p`-th roots of unity and
`d_p | p-1`.  For `epsilon_p=1`, the roots are the nontrivial `n`-th roots of
unity and `n | p-1`.  The roots are simple because `d_p,n` are nonzero modulo
`p`.

Thus all roots lie in `F_p^x`, so in particular

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde B_p(a)\mid a^{p^2}-a.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is stronger than factorization into degree-one-or-two Frobenius orbits:
all the Frobenius orbits here have size one.

### 7.3 Reciprocity and reflection

For `epsilon_p=0`,

\[
a^{d_p}\widetilde B_p(1/a)=-\widetilde B_p(a).
\tag{7.6}
\]

For `epsilon_p=1`,

\[
a^{d_p}\widetilde B_p(1/a)=\widetilde B_p(a).
\tag{7.7}
\]

Also

\[
a^2\Delta(1/a)=\Delta(a).
\tag{7.8}
\]

Squaring (7.6)--(7.7) and using (7.8) gives

\[
\boxed{
a^{p-1}\widetilde A_p(1/a)=\widetilde A_p(a).}
\tag{7.9}
\]

Hence, if

\[
\widetilde A_p(a)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}\widetilde b_{p,m}a^m,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde b_{p,m}
=\widetilde b_{p,p-1-m}.
}
\tag{7.10}
\]

So the coefficient reflection is exact, not imposed on an abstract zero set.

### 7.4 Fixed second-order ODEs

Let

\[
\theta=a{d\over da},
\qquad
D:=\theta(2\theta+1).
\]

This is a p-independent differential operator of order two.

If `epsilon_p=0` and `d=(p-1)/2`, then

\[
D\widetilde B_p
=-d(2d+1)a^d
=-dp\,a^d=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[a].
\tag{7.11}
\]

If `epsilon_p=1` and `n=(p-1)/2`, then

\[
(1-a)\widetilde B_p=1-a^n
\]

and therefore

\[
D\bigl((1-a)\widetilde B_p\bigr)
=-n(2n+1)a^n
=-np\,a^n=0.
\tag{7.12}
\]

If one wants the same discriminant divisor to appear explicitly in the
operator, multiply these equations by `Delta(a)`.  Thus the two fixed branch
operators

\[
\widetilde L_0=\Delta(a)D,
\qquad
\widetilde L_1=\Delta(a)D\circ M_{1-a}
\tag{7.13}
\]

are p-independent polynomial-coefficient ODEs of order two annihilating the
corresponding `B~_p`.

This is a genuine differential realization; no zero set was chosen by hand.

### 7.5 Exact cover-adapted marked coefficient interpretation

The same cover map satisfies the exact algebraic identity

\[
\Delta(\phi(x))
={\bigl(1-16x-8x^2\bigr)^2\over(1+x)^2}.
\tag{7.14}
\]

For completeness, with `n=x-8x^2`, the numerator is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&n^2-34n(1+x)+(1+x)^2\\
&=1-32x+240x^2+256x^3+64x^4\\
&=(1-16x-8x^2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Put

\[
R(x)=1-16x-8x^2
\]

and define

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde K_p(x)
=(1+x)^{d_p}R(x)^{\epsilon_p}
\widetilde B_p(\phi(x)).
}
\tag{7.15}
\]

This is a polynomial: each `phi(x)^j` has denominator `(1+x)^j` and
`j<=d_p`.

Using `2d_p+2epsilon_p=p-1`, (7.14) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde K_p(x)^2
&=(1+x)^{p-1}
  \widetilde A_p(\phi(x))\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}
  \widetilde b_{p,m}
  x^m(1-8x)^m(1+x)^{p-1-m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.16}
\]

Therefore, in the **same** cover-adapted basis,

\[
\boxed{
[\widetilde K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,m}}
=\widetilde b_{p,m}.
}
\tag{7.17}
\]

This is the requested marked coefficient interpretation at polynomial level.

### 7.6 `Theta(T^2)` weighted marked prime mass

Take the split branch `chi_p=1`, so `epsilon_p=0`.  Then

\[
\widetilde A_p(a)
=\bigl(1-a^{(p-1)/2}\bigr)^2
=1-2a^{(p-1)/2}+a^{p-1}.
\tag{7.18}
\]

If `p>10T`, then `(p-1)/2>4T` for all sufficiently large `T`, and hence

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde b_{p,m}=0
\qquad(1\le m\le4T).
}
\tag{7.19}
\]

Thus **every** admissible triple

\[
t+h,\quad t+2h,\quad t+3h
\]

is a distinguished marked zero triple for every such split prime.

Now restrict to

\[
\mathcal P_T=
\{p:10T<p\le T^2,\ (-6/p)=1\}.
\]

The prime number theorem for the fixed nontrivial quadratic character
`(-6/.)` gives

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_T}\log p
={1\over2}T^2+o(T^2)-O(T)
=\Theta(T^2).
\tag{7.20}
\]

Therefore the full package

- exact square,
- exact cover coordinate,
- reciprocal/reflection symmetry,
- fixed second-order ODE form,
- and even **linear** rather than merely quadratic Frobenius splitting

allows quadratic weighted marked prime mass.

This proves a genuine structural no-go:

> The listed Hasse/root/ODE/reciprocity mechanisms, interpreted as structural
> axioms rather than the exact numerical Apéry ODE, do not imply any
> `T^(2-eta)` marked-prime bound.

The model is **not** claimed to be an integer Apéry sequence or a
counterexample to the actual `M_T`; it is a countermodel to the attempted
logical implication from the stated local Hasse package to subquadratic
marked mass.  Unlike an abstract zero-set model, it realizes the zeros as
actual coordinates of an exact square polynomial with the full cover and
splitting structure.

## 8. Rigidity theorem: preserving the exact Apéry ODE removes all model freedom

There is an important stronger reading of the countermodel request.  Suppose
one demands not merely a p-independent second-order ODE of the same type, but
**the exact operators (3.5)--(3.6)**, together with `B(0)=1` and the branch
degree (3.2).

Then there is no independent countermodel.

### Proposition (exact-ODE rigidity)

Fix `p>=5` and the sign branch.  Let `\widehat B_p in F_p[a]` have

\[
\widehat B_p(0)=1,
\qquad
\deg\widehat B_p\le d_p,
\]

and suppose it is annihilated by the corresponding exact Apéry operator
`L_+` or `L_-`.  Then

\[
\boxed{\widehat B_p=B_p.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
\widehat B_p=\sum_r\beta_ra^r,
\qquad
\beta_{-1}=0,\quad\beta_0=1.
\]

For the plus branch, coefficient extraction from `L_+ \widehat B_p=0` gives
exactly

\[
4(r+1)^2\beta_{r+1}
=2(68r^2+34r+5)\beta_r
 -(2r-1)^2\beta_{r-1}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For the minus branch one gets

\[
4(r+1)^2\beta_{r+1}
=2(68r^2+102r+39)\beta_r
 -(2r+1)^2\beta_{r-1}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

For every `0<=r<d_p`, one has `r+1<p`, so `4(r+1)^2` is invertible in
`F_p`.  Hence (8.2) or (8.3) determines `beta_{r+1}` uniquely from the two
preceding coefficients.  Starting from `(beta_{-1},beta_0)=(0,1)`, induction
forces the exact truncation (3.7), coefficient by coefficient, through degree
`d_p`.  Therefore `\widehat B_p=B_p`.  QED.

Consequently, a model that preserves

- the exact Apéry `L_+`/`L_-`,
- the normalized regular solution,
- and the prescribed branch degree

is already the actual Hasse polynomial.  Adding the square, splitting,
reflection, and marked cover does not restore any freedom.

So under the exact-operator interpretation the requested fallback
countermodel is logically impossible unless one proves that the **actual**
Apéry system has `Theta(T^2)` marked mass.  This is not a weakness of the
countermodel in Section 7; it identifies precisely where the Apéry-specific
arithmetic resides: in the numerical coefficients of `L_+`,`L_-` and their
single universal characteristic-zero solution, not in the generic fact that
a second-order Hasse equation exists.

## 9. Why the quadratic-root data cannot be combined with ODE rigidity by a formal elimination trick

A tempting plan is:

1. use the ODE to express a few marked coefficient equalities;
2. use `B_p|a^(p^2)-a` to constrain the roots;
3. eliminate the root parameters or a critical value;
4. conclude that three marked zeros force a low-degree condition on `p`.

The stable-prefix observation blocks the generic version of this plan.
For `p>10T`, the ODE has already eliminated all local coefficient freedom:

\[
(b_0,\ldots,b_{4T})\pmod p
\]

is simply the reduction of a fixed integer vector.  The splitting condition
is a global condition on a polynomial of degree about `p/2`, whose unseen
coefficients occupy degrees `>4T`.  Eliminating the root divisor while keeping
only the first `4T` coefficients can therefore return, at best, a congruence
already satisfied by the actual Hasse polynomial for every `p`; to produce a
power saving it must retain a genuinely p-dependent global invariant and
show that **simultaneous low Mellin vanishing makes that invariant exceptional
across primes**.

That missing implication is a horizontal arithmetic theorem, not formal ODE
algebra.

The countermodel in Section 7 makes this failure concrete: the root divisor
is maximally tame (all roots in `F_p`), the differential equation is fixed,
the square and cover are exact, yet the low marked coordinates vanish en
masse.  Thus an elimination argument that uses only those structural
properties cannot prove the desired estimate.

## 10. The precise theorem worth attacking next

The cleanest target is not universal AP3-freeness and not another pointwise
bound for `Z(p)`.

### Minimal target: marked Mellin AP3 dispersion

For some absolute `eta>0`, prove

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p>10T}\log p
\sum_{2\le h\le t\le T}
\prod_{j=1}^3
1_{\mathscr M_p(t+jh)=0}
\ll T^{2-\eta}.
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

By (4.4), this is exactly (MAP3_eta), hence exactly enough for `M_T`.

### Cleaner but stronger target: marked two-collision large sieve

Prove

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p>10T}\log p
\sum_{2\le h\le t\le T}
1_{\mathscr M_p(t+h)=0}
1_{\mathscr M_p(t+2h)=0}
\ll T^{2-\eta}.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

This is (MCE_eta), and (1.7) finishes the theorem immediately.

### What a successful use of the Hasse square would need to add

A viable coefficient-level Hasse/Cartier proof must supply at least one of the
following genuinely new statements.

- **Cross-prime Mellin large sieve:** cancellation in (6.3) after inserting
  `A_p=Delta^epsilon B_p^2`, uniform over the moving exponents `m=O(T)`.
- **Orbit-to-frequency transference:** a theorem converting the degree-1/2
  Frobenius orbit decomposition of the roots of `B_p` into anti-concentration
  of the low Mellin coefficients of `B_p^2`.
- **Apéry-specific ODE determinant:** a nonzero determinant/minor built from
  three low coefficients whose prime divisor mass, after averaging over
  `(t,h)`, has subquadratic logarithmic height.  It must use the exact
  numerical `L_+`,`L_-`, because Section 7 kills any argument depending only
  on order, singularity pattern, reciprocity, and splitting.

A pointwise theorem `Z(p)` is AP3-free would be much stronger than necessary.
A fixed-p square-root or `p^(3/2)` orbit-energy theorem is in the wrong
quantifier order.  The needed gain is specifically across the primes that
simultaneously annihilate two or three **marked low coordinates**.

## 11. Classification of what is actually established here

### THEOREM

The high-prime selector satisfies the exact reductions (1.4)--(1.10).  In
particular, either (MAP3_eta) or (MCE_eta) with any absolute `eta>0` implies

\[
\log M_T=O(T^{2-\eta}).
\]

Also, for `p>10T`, every selected marked coordinate lies in the stable branch
prefix (3.9); the exact ODE recurrence there is just the reduction of the
single characteristic-zero Apéry branch.

### CONDITIONAL

A prime-averaged marked Mellin collision/large-sieve estimate such as
(10.1) or (10.2) is sufficient.  This is the precise form in which a
coefficient-level Hasse/Cartier theorem would close the aggregate without
proving universal AP3-freeness.

### NO-GO

The currently banked facts

\[
A_p=\Delta^{\epsilon_p}B_p^2,
\qquad
B_p\mid a^{p^2}-a,
\]

plus reciprocity/reflection, quadratic Frobenius orbits, and the existence of
a fixed second-order ODE do **not**, as structural statements, imply a
subquadratic marked-prime bound.  The obstruction is the exact
root-variable/Mellin-frequency mismatch, and Section 7 gives a polynomial
countermodel with all those structural features and `Theta(T^2)` marked mass.

If one strengthens “ODE form” to the **exact Apéry operators** `L_+`,`L_-`,
then Section 8 proves rigidity: the only normalized branch is the actual
`B_p`.  In that stronger category there is no separate countermodel to build;
the remaining problem is exactly the missing Apéry-specific horizontal
Mellin dispersion theorem.

## Bottom line

I do **not** see an unconditional proof of `log M_T=O(T^(2-eta))` from the
present Hasse/Cartier package.  The right next target is (10.1), with (10.2)
as a cleaner stronger surrogate.  The exact square, ODE, splitting, and
reflection should be viewed as the algebraic input for such a theorem, not as
already implying it.  Any claimed proof that jumps directly from quadratic
Frobenius roots of `B_p` to AP3 anti-concentration of marked coefficients is
missing the root-to-Mellin transference step exposed above.
