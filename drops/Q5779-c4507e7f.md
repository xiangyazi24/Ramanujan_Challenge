# Q5779: P3.2 tactical proof audit — global shell carriers and the gcd height problem

## 1. What the order-4 construction gives formally

Assume the stated certified identities:

\[
T(b)_m=\sum_{j=0}^4c_j(m)b_{m+j}=0,
\]

and

\[
T(J)_m=\delta b_m.
\]

The crucial gain over the earlier fixed-prime recurrence attempts is that the same polynomial coefficients \(c_j(n)\) define one global integer combination. Therefore

\[
Z_n(d)=\sum_{j=0}^4c_j(n)(C_{n+j}(d-1)+C_{n+j}(d+1))
\]

is a single shell carrier, not a family depending on the prime.

For a target prime \(p\) with \(p\mid b_{n-p}\), the Cartier congruence in the first cell gives

\[
C_{n+j}(p-1)\equiv b_{n+j-p}\pmod p.
\]

Because the two indices differ by \(p\), the shift relation gives the same Apéry residue, and the linear combination becomes

\[
Z_n(p-1)\equiv \sum_{j=0}^4c_j(n)b_{n+j-p}=T(b)_{n-p}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Thus every target prime in the interval divides the same integer pair

\[
A=G_{D-1,N}(C_{n-1}),\qquad B=G_{D-1,N}(Z_n).
\]

Consequently

\[
\prod_{p\in T_n(D,N)}p\mid \gcd(A,B).
\]

This part is a theorem conditional only on the Cartier congruence and the stated exact order-4 identity.

## 2. What remains to prove for the radical bound

The desired statement is

\[
\log\gcd(A,B)=o(n).
\]

The divisibility above only gives the lower bound. To obtain an upper bound one needs an independent arithmetic restriction on the common divisor.

The natural route is a resultant/Bezout identity between the two fixed-moment carriers:

\[
U_n A+V_n B=R_n,
\]

where \(U_n,V_n\) are explicit integers (or controlled rational numbers after clearing denominators), and where

\[
\log |R_n|=o(n).
\]

Then

\[
\gcd(A,B)\mid R_n,
\]

which would immediately prove the radical estimate.

The missing theorem is therefore not another congruence theorem; it is a height theorem for the pair of shell moments.

## 3. The correct CAS target

The smallest useful object to search is not the full gcd, but the Euclidean relation in the holonomic module generated by

\[
C_m(d-1),\qquad C_m(d+1).
\]

A concrete search target is:

Find polynomials \(U(d),V(d),W(d)\), with bounded degree independent of \(n\), such that

\[
U(d)G_{D-1,N}(C_{n-1})+V(d)G_{D-1,N}(Z_n)
=W(d)
\]

where \(W\) has degree/height bounded subexponentially in \(n\).

Equivalently, compute the holonomic resultant of the two annihilating ideals:

\[
I_A=\langle L_A\rangle,\qquad I_B=\langle L_B\rangle .
\]

A successful output would be a scalar operator in the elimination ideal whose evaluation at the Newton projection gives the desired Bezout constant.

## 4. Casoratian route: what it can and cannot do

A Casoratian identity between the two shell solutions can prove a statement of the form

\[
q\mid A(n),\ q\mid B(n)
\Longrightarrow q\mid W(n).
\]

However, this is only useful if the resulting Wronskian is a fixed low-height integer.

The common pitfall is confusing

"all target primes divide both carriers"

with

"the Casoratian captures all target primes."

A Casoratian generally controls simultaneous vanishing of solutions of the same module. Here the two carriers are obtained from different transforms of the shell family. Unless the intertwining map is explicitly inverted over the integer ring, a Casoratian may miss local factors introduced by the projection.

Therefore the next theorem to prove is an integral compatibility statement:

**Integral projection lemma.** The Newton projection of the two shell solutions preserves the saturated lattice of the common holonomic module, up to a fixed constant independent of the interval.

Without this lemma, a rational resultant only gives divisibility after multiplication by denominators, which is insufficient.

## 5. A precise possible obstruction

A countermodel would exist if the shell module contains two independent integral lattices with the same mod-p zeros but arbitrarily large primitive common divisors after projection.

Algebraically, this happens if the projection map

\[
\Phi: \mathcal M_{shell}\rightarrow \mathbb Z^{N+1}
\]

has a cokernel whose elementary divisors grow exponentially.

Then one could have

\[
\gcd(A,B)=E_n
\]

with \(E_n\) large, even though all target primes are forced into it.

Thus the exact obstruction to rule out is:

\[
\operatorname{Smith} (\Phi_n)
\]

having exponential largest invariant factor.

A practical CAS test is therefore:

1. Construct the lattice of coefficient vectors for the two shell carriers.
2. Compute the Smith normal form of the projection matrix for increasing \(n\).
3. Determine whether the largest invariant factor has logarithmic growth \(o(n)\) or linear growth.
