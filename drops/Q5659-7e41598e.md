# Top-half Apéry pair energy: rigorous partial savings, mixed-characteristic obstruction, and the exact missing theorem

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
 b_m=\sum_{k=0}^m\binom{m}{k}^2\binom{m+k}{k}^2,
 \qquad
 T_n=\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n\},
 \qquad K_n=|T_n|,
\]

and

\[
 H_2(N)=\sum_{N<n\le2N}K_n(K_n-1).
\]

I do **not** obtain the desired unconditional estimate

\[
 H_2(N)=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{T}
\]

The live repository and the applicable literature support the following sharper verdict.

1. The strongest unconditional bound for the **full** energy from the current proved vertical input is
   \[
   \boxed{H_2(N)\ll \frac{N^{8/3}}{\log^2N}.}
   \tag{1}
   \]
   This saves a factor \(N^{1/3}\) over the prime-counting bound \(O(N^3/\log^2N)\), but it is still above the target by \(N^{2/3}\).

2. There are genuine power savings on large, rigorously specified subfamilies:
   - for prime gap at most \(H\),
     \[
     \boxed{H_{2,\le H}(N)\ll \frac{H N^{5/3}}{\log^2N};}
     \tag{2}
     \]
     hence \(H\le N^{1/3-\varepsilon}\) gives \(H_{2,\le H}(N)\ll N^{2-\varepsilon}/\log^2N\);
   - same-characteristic, or “degenerate”, short-gap pairs have the much stronger carrier bound
     \[
     \boxed{H_{2,\le H}^{\rm deg}(N)\ll NH^2;}
     \tag{3}
     \]
   - if the selected Kummer character order is at most \(D\), then the both-low sector and the sector with at least one low member satisfy
     \[
     \boxed{H_2^{LL}(N;D)\ll ND^2\log(2D)+D^4,}
     \tag{4}
     \]
     \[
     \boxed{H_2^{L*}(N;D)\ll
       D\,M_N\frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N},
       \qquad M_N=\max_{m\le2N}\tau(m)=N^{o(1)}.}
     \tag{5}
     \]
     Thus, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), both-low pairs are negligible through \(D\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), and all pairs with at least one low member are negligible through \(D\le N^{1/3-\varepsilon}\).

3. After these reductions, the honest residual is:
   - both selected character orders are \(>N^{1/3-o(1)}\);
   - the two target zeros are in **different** characteristics;
   - neither characteristic sees the other endpoint as a zero;
   - the low-height Apéry continuant is forced to be a unit modulo both primes.

   I call these **high-order pure-cross collisions**. They are exactly the sector not touched by same-prime continuants, fixed-field Deligne bounds, ordinary additive large sieve, or the current CFVZ/Hasse--Witt structure.

4. No cited geometric large-sieve theorem applies to this residual. Existing bilinear trace-function theorems work over one finite field with one bounded-conductor sheaf. Existing large sieves for Frobenius work with a fixed algebraic family and auxiliary reductions. Here each term simultaneously involves characteristic \(p\) and characteristic \(\ell\), and the zero mask is a coefficient-vanishing condition whose complexity grows with the characteristic or, equivalently, with the selected Kummer order. A fixed-prime Deligne estimate does not couple \(p\) and \(\ell\).

5. There is a real deterministic low-height carrier for the degenerate sector, but the pure-cross tradeoff is exact:
   - the Casoratian has logarithmic height \(O(h\log N)\) and is a unit at both target primes;
   - forcing both prime factors by cross-weighting leaves an Apéry term of exponential height \(\exp(\Theta(N))\);
   - after universal determinant saturation, a completely contracted polynomial carrier has coefficient content \(p\ell\), while a primitive carrier retains a dominant Apéry slot.

The most precise positive analytic target is therefore an **uncentered mixed-characteristic incidence theorem** for the actual Apéry zero masks, with the complete arc restrictions below. A centered theorem is insufficient by itself unless it simultaneously proves an average zero-fibre theorem, because the formal main term is too large under the currently proved \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\).

## 2. Exact two-characteristic collision formula

For \(n\in(N,2N]\) and \(p\in T_n\), one has \(p>N/2\), hence \(n<2p\). For \(p\ge7\), Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
 p\mid b_n
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p\mid b_{n-p},
\tag{9}
\]

because \(b_1=5\) is a \(p\)-unit.

Take two distinct targets and order them as

\[
 p<\ell.
\]

Put

\[
 h=\ell-p,
 \qquad
 s=n-\ell.
\tag{10}
\]

Then

\[
 n=\ell+s=p+(s+h),
\]

and the two target conditions become

\[
 \boxed{
 \ell\mid b_s,
 \qquad
 p\mid b_{s+h}.}
\tag{11}
\]

The top-half inequalities are exactly

\[
 0\le s<s+h<p<\ell.
\tag{12}
\]

Define

\[
 I_N(p,\ell)=
 \left\{s\in\mathbb Z:
 \max(0,N+1-\ell)
 \le s\le
 \min(2N-\ell,\,2p-\ell-1)
 \right\}.
\tag{13}
\]

The interval is empty unless \(N/2<p<\ell\le2N\) and \(\ell<2p\). Apart from the fixed small-prime exceptions, the exact ordered energy identity is

\[
 \boxed{
 H_2(N)=
 2\!\sum_{\substack{N/2<p<\ell\le2N\\
                     p,\ell\ {m prime}\\
                     \ell<2p}}
 \ \sum_{s\in I_N(p,\ell)}
 1_{\{\ell\mid b_s\}}
 1_{\{p\mid b_{s+\ell-p}\}}.}
\tag{14}
\]

This is the correct object. It is not a same-modulus correlation. One factor lives in \(\mathbb F_\ell\), the other in \(\mathbb F_p\), and the shift is itself \(\ell-p\).

Equivalently, with

\[
 z_q(x)=1_{\{b_x\equiv0\pmod q\}},
 \qquad 0\le x<q,
\tag{15}
\]

formula (14) is

\[
 H_2(N)=
 2\sum_{p<\ell}\sum_{s\in I_N(p,\ell)}
 z_\ell(s)z_p(s+\ell-p).
\tag{16}
\]

Every successful theorem must control this **joint** \((p,\ell)\)-sum. A theorem proved separately for each \(p\) and then summed does not provide the missing coupling.

## 3. Strongest unconditional full-energy bound

Put

\[
 \mathcal I(N)=\sum_{N<n\le2N}K_n.
\tag{17}
\]

Each incidence has a unique representation \(n=p+r\), with \(r\in Z_p\). Therefore

\[
 \mathcal I(N)
 \le
 \sum_{N/2<p\le2N}z_p.
\tag{18}
\]

Using (6) and partial summation,

\[
 \mathcal I(N)\ll\frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}.
\tag{19}
\]

Also, by prime counting,

\[
 \max_{N<n\le2N}K_n
 \le \pi(2N)-\pi(N/2)
 \ll\frac N{\log N}.
\tag{20}
\]

Since \(K_n(K_n-1)\le K_n\max_mK_m\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_2(N)
 &\le
 \left(\max_{N<n\le2N}K_n\right)
 \mathcal I(N)\\
 &\ll
 \frac N{\log N}\cdot
 \frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{H_2(N)\ll\frac{N^{8/3}}{\log^2N}.}
\tag{21}
\]

The same scale follows directly from (14): for a fixed pair \((p,\ell)\), the inner intersection has size at most \(\min(z_p,z_\ell)\ll N^{2/3}\), and there are \(O(N^2/\log^2N)\) admissible prime pairs.

This is a genuine unconditional power saving. It is also the exact saturation of the currently proved one-row information: replacing the actual Apéry masks by reflected masks concentrated on one sparse column preserves all such row-size estimates and can make the pair sum critical.

## 4. A rigorous power saving for bounded prime gaps

Let

\[
 H_{2,\le H}(N)
 =2\sum_{1\le h\le H}
 \sum_{\substack{p,\ p+h\ {m prime}}}
 \#\{s:\text{the constraints in (13) and (11) hold}\}.
\tag{22}
\]

For a fixed even \(h\), the Selberg upper-bound sieve gives, uniformly in the relevant dyadic interval,

\[
 \#\{p\asymp N:p,p+h\text{ prime}\}
 \ll \mathfrak S(h)\frac N{\log^2N},
\tag{23}
\]

where \(\mathfrak S(h)=0\) for odd \(h\) in the large-prime range and

\[
 \sum_{h\le H}\mathfrak S(h)\ll H.
\tag{24}
\]

For every pair \(p,p+h\), the number of allowed \(s\)'s is at most \(O(N^{2/3})\) by (6). Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 H_{2,\le H}(N)
 \ll
 \frac{H N^{5/3}}{\log^2N}.}
\tag{25}
\]

Consequently, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
 H=N^{1/3-\varepsilon}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 H_{2,\le H}(N)
 \ll\frac{N^{2-\varepsilon}}{\log^2N}.
\tag{26}
\]

This is the strongest clean power-saving sector currently available directly from the zero-fibre theorem and the prime-pair sieve.

It does not prove (T). Pairs with gaps larger than \(N^{1/3-\varepsilon}\) still constitute almost all possible prime pairs. Also, a pointwise spike can be detected from **adjacent** small gaps, but \(H_2\) counts every nonadjacent pair; the two problems have different combinatorics.

## 5. Degenerate pairs: a true sublinear-height carrier

Define the cleared Apéry continuants

\[
 N_0(x)=0,
 \qquad N_1(x)=1,
\]

\[
 N_{h+1}(x)
 =P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x),
\tag{27}
\]

where

\[
 P(x)=34x^3+51x^2+27x+5.
\]

For a collision (11), call the pair **degenerate** if

\[
 p\mid b_s
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \ell\mid b_{s+h}.
\tag{28}
\]

Then one characteristic sees zeros at both endpoints. The transfer identity and no-consecutive-zero theorem imply

\[
 p\mid N_h(s)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \ell\mid N_h(s).
\tag{29}
\]

Since \(s=n-h-p\equiv n-h\pmod p\) in the first case and \(s=n-\ell\equiv n\pmod\ell\) in the second, every degenerate pair contributes a top-half prime divisor of

\[
 \boxed{D_{n,h}^{\rm deg}=N_h(n-h)N_h(n).}
\tag{30}
\]

The continuant is a positive tridiagonal determinant of order \(h-1\), and Hadamard gives

\[
 \log D_{n,h}^{\rm deg}\ll h\log n.
\tag{31}
\]

Every relevant prime is \(>n/2\), so the number of such prime divisors is \(O(h)\). Thus, for fixed \(n,h\),

\[
 M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll h.
\tag{32}
\]

Summing gives

\[
 \boxed{
 H_{2,\le H}^{\rm deg}(N)\ll NH^2.}
\tag{33}
\]

In particular,

\[
 H=A\log N
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 H_{2,\le H}^{\rm deg}(N)\ll_A N\log^2N
 =o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{34}
\]

This is a real deterministic carrier at exactly the desired low height. It proves that the hard short-gap sector is not “all pairs”; it is the pure-cross part.

## 6. Pure-cross pairs and the exact continuant obstruction

A collision is **pure cross** when

\[
 p\nmid b_s,
 \qquad
 \ell\nmid b_{s+h},
\tag{35}
\]

while still satisfying

\[
 p\mid b_{s+h},
 \qquad
 \ell\mid b_s.
\tag{36}
\]

Write

\[
 b_{s+h}=pu,
 \qquad
 b_s=\ell v,
 \qquad \ell=p+h.
\tag{37}
\]

The opposite quotients are units:

\[
 u\not\equiv0\pmod\ell,
 \qquad
 v\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{38}
\]

The exact transfer identity is

\[
 Q_h(s)b_{s+h}
 =N_h(s)b_{s+1}
 -(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)b_s,
\tag{39}
\]

where

\[
 Q_h(s)=\prod_{j=2}^h(s+j)^3.
\]

All factors in the interval are units modulo both target primes. A Dodgson/Casoratian identity then gives the stronger fact

\[
 \boxed{
 p\nmid N_h(s),
 \qquad
 \ell\nmid N_h(s).}
\tag{40}
\]

Thus the low-height same-prime detector is forced to be a unit on the residual locus.

Equation (39) becomes

\[
 N_h(s)b_{s+1}
 =pQ_h(s)u
 +\ell(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)v.
\tag{41}
\]

Modulo the two characteristics,

\[
 N_h(s)b_{s+1}
 \equiv h(s+1)^3N_{h-1}(s+1)v\pmod p,
\tag{42}
\]

\[
 N_h(s)b_{s+1}
 \equiv-hQ_h(s)u\pmod\ell.
\tag{43}
\]

The unresolved coordinates are therefore precisely

\[
 \frac{b_s}{\ell}\pmod p,
 \qquad
 \frac{b_{s+h}}p\pmod\ell.
\tag{44}
\]

Same-prime root counting contains no information about these opposite quotients.

Reflection does not change this. It creates another zero in each individual field, but the reflected endpoints agree with the other collision endpoint only in the degenerate central cases already covered by (30).

## 7. Rigorous Kummer-order pruning

The finite-field hypergeometric/CFVZ descriptions attach to the top-half digit

\[
 r=n-p
\]

the selected Teichmüller character \(\omega_p^r\). Since

\[
 r=n-p\equiv n-1\pmod{p-1},
\]

its order is

\[
 d_{p,n}
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-1)}.
\tag{45}
\]

Let \(K_{\le D}(n)\) count top-half candidate primes with \(d_{p,n}\le D\), whether or not they are Apéry targets. Put \(m=n-1\). If \(d=d_{p,n}\), then there is an integer \(j\) such that

\[
 p-1=d\frac mj,
 \qquad
 j\mid m,
 \qquad
 d\le j\le2d,
 \qquad
 \gcd(d,j)=1.
\tag{46}
\]

The interval \(d\le j\le2d\) is exactly the top-half condition. Ignoring primality gives

\[
 K_{\le D}(n)\le D\tau(n-1).
\tag{47}
\]

Let

\[
 M_N=\max_{m\le2N}\tau(m)=N^{o(1)}.
\]

### 7.1 Both selected orders at most \(D\)

For a fixed divisor \(j\le2D\), the number of possible \(d\) in (46) is at most \(j\). Expanding the square and counting integers divisible by \([j,k]\) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_2^{LL}(N;D)
 &\ll
 \sum_{j,k\le2D}jk
 \left(\frac N{[j,k]}+1\right)\\
 &=
 N\sum_{j,k\le2D}(j,k)
 +O(D^4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]

The standard gcd sum satisfies

\[
 \sum_{j,k\le X}(j,k)\ll X^2\log(2X),
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 H_2^{LL}(N;D)
 \ll ND^2\log(2D)+D^4.}
\tag{49}
\]

For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), this is

\[
 o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right)
\]

uniformly for \(D\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\).

### 7.2 At least one selected order at most \(D\)

At one \(n\), the number of ordered pairs with at least one low-order member is at most

\[
 2K_{\le D}(n)K_n.
\]

Using (47), (19), and \(M_N=N^{o(1)}\),

\[
 \boxed{
 H_2^{L*}(N;D)
 \ll
 D M_N\frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}.}
\tag{50}
\]

Therefore, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
 D\le N^{1/3-\varepsilon}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 H_2^{L*}(N;D)
 =o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{51}
\]

This is a genuine Apéry/hypergeometric reduction. The remaining pair energy may be restricted to pairs for which

\[
 d_{p,n},d_{\ell,n}>N^{1/3-o(1)}.
\tag{52}
\]

The low-order norm or divisor mechanism cannot be pushed to nearly primitive order without a new mixed collision theorem. In the high-order sector, the cyclotomic coefficient degree grows with \(d_{p,n}\), so taking rational norms loses exactly the height one hoped to save.

## 8. Constant-term and Hasse--Witt expansion

The repository contains primitive Laurent-polynomial models \(\Lambda\) with

\[
 b_m=\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^m.
\tag{53}
\]

Thus a collision is

\[
 \operatorname{CT}\Lambda^s\equiv0\pmod\ell,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{CT}\Lambda^{s+h}\equiv0\pmod p,
 \qquad h=\ell-p.
\tag{54}
\]

CRT combines these into a statement modulo \(p\ell\), but it does not put the two constant terms in one residue characteristic. The direct positive carrier

\[
 \ell b_{s+h}+p b_s
 =p\ell(u+v)
\tag{55}
\]

is nonzero and divisible by \(p\ell\), but

\[
 \log(\ell b_{s+h}+p b_s)
 =(s+h)\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log N),
\tag{56}
\]

so it has full linear Apéry height.

For a prime \(q\), put

\[
 A_q(t)=\sum_{0\le r<q}b_rt^r\in\mathbb F_q[t].
\tag{57}
\]

For \(1\le r\le q-2\), elementary coefficient extraction gives

\[
 b_r
 \equiv
 -\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_q^\times}A_q(t)t^{-r}\pmod q.
\tag{58}
\]

The pair event therefore becomes the vanishing of two Mellin/power-sum coefficients,

\[
 \sum_{t\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times}A_\ell(t)t^{-s}=0
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_\ell,
\tag{59}
\]

\[
 \sum_{u\in\mathbb F_p^\times}A_p(u)u^{-(s+h)}=0
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.
\tag{60}
\]

This rewriting is exact, but it displays the problem rather than solving it:

- \(A_p\) and \(A_\ell\) are reductions in different characteristics;
- the selected exponents change with the characteristics;
- vanishing is a congruence in the residue field, not a small complex trace;
- the coefficient functional is global in the parameter \(t\).

The CFVZ factorization

\[
 A_q(t)=g_q(t)B_q(t)^2
\tag{61}
\]

controls the algebraic generating series and roots in the variable \(t\). It does not give coefficient anti-concentration for \([t^r]A_q(t)\), and it gives no relation between a coefficient in characteristic \(p\) and a different coefficient in characteristic \(\ell\).

The latest repository congruences strengthen the one-characteristic filtration. For example, a truncated positive hypergeometric sum \(\Theta_n\) satisfies

\[
 b_n\equiv5\Theta_n\pmod{q^2}
\tag{62}
\]

for every top-half candidate prime \(q\), and a later complete-low-block carrier gains a third universal digit on a subband. These are real congruences, but they are **candidate-universal**: the extra powers of every candidate prime divide the difference whether or not \(q\in T_n\). After dividing the universal candidate content, the residual is not target-divisible. Hence these congruences do not multiply a target prime by \(K_n-1\), which is what pair energy needs.

## 9. Fourier form and why ordinary additive large sieve is critical

Extend \(z_q\) periodically and define

\[
 \widehat z_q(a)
 =\sum_{x\bmod q}z_q(x)e_q(-ax).
\tag{63}
\]

Then

\[
 z_q(x)=\frac1q\sum_{a\bmod q}\widehat z_q(a)e_q(ax),
\tag{64}
\]

and Parseval gives

\[
 \sum_{a\bmod q}|\widehat z_q(a)|^2=qz_q.
\tag{65}
\]

Substitution into (16) produces the genuine two-prime term

\[
 \frac1{p\ell}
 \sum_{a\bmod p}
 \sum_{b\bmod\ell}
 \widehat z_p(a)\widehat z_\ell(b)e_p(ah)
 \sum_{s\in I_N(p,\ell)}e_p(as)e_\ell(bs),
 \qquad h=\ell-p.
\tag{66}
\]

The combined phase is a rational frequency of denominator \(p\ell\asymp N^2\), while the interval has length only \(O(N)\). The ordinary large-sieve constant is therefore

\[
 N+Q^2\asymp N^2,
\tag{67}
\]

not \(N\). Using only (65) and \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\) gives a bound at or above the \(N^{8/3}\) scale and is weaker in logarithms than direct counting.

Even a hypothetical \(z_p=O(1)\) would leave the black-box large sieve at the critical \(N^2\)-type scale. This is not just a bad inequality. Reflected two-point masks can have

\[
 |\widehat z_p(a)|\le2
\]

for every nonzero frequency and still be synchronized on one sparse column. Thus one-row Fourier bounds, including square-root bounds stronger than Deligne would normally provide, do not imply mixed-characteristic dispersion.

There is also a main-term issue. Formally separating \(a=b=0\) gives

\[
 \sum_{p<\ell}|I_N(p,\ell)|\frac{z_pz_\ell}{p\ell}.
\tag{68}
\]

With only the proved \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\), this can be bounded only at approximately

\[
 \frac{N^{7/3}}{\log^2N},
\tag{69}
\]

still above the target. Therefore a **centered** cross-prime theorem alone is not enough for full \(H_2\). One needs either:

- the separate average zero-fibre theorem \(\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll\pi(x)\), which would make (68) \(O(N/\log^2N)\); or
- an uncentered theorem that estimates the actual incidence sum (16) directly.

For logarithmic prime gaps, the candidate-pair count is only \(O(HN/\log^2N)\), so the formal main term is already harmless under \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\). This is why the short-gap pointwise dispersion target in the repository is cleaner than the full-energy centered problem.

## 10. Why the available geometric theorems do not apply

### 10.1 Fixed-field bilinear trace-function theorems

The bilinear trace-function results of Kowalski--Michel--Sawin and the 2025 generalization by Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel--Sawin estimate sums over **one** finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) involving the trace function of one bounded-conductor \(\ell\)-adic sheaf. Their geometric input studies tensor products, sum-product transforms, and exceptional loci inside that one field.

Formula (66) is different in every essential quantifier:

- one coefficient is defined over \(\mathbb F_p\);
- the other is defined over \(\mathbb F_\ell\);
- \(p\) and \(\ell\) vary simultaneously;
- the shift is \(\ell-p\);
- the zero masks are not supplied as one bounded-conductor trace function on a common field.

Applying a fixed-field theorem separately at \(p\) and at \(\ell\) cannot create cancellation in the outer \((p,\ell)\)-sum.

### 10.2 Large sieve for Frobenius

Kowalski's large sieve for Frobenius concerns a fixed algebraic family with controlled monodromy, usually over a fixed finite field or an arithmetic base, and sieves Frobenius conjugacy classes through independent auxiliary representations.

Here the prime \(p\) is not an auxiliary sieve prime acting on a fixed object. It is the residue characteristic in which the Hasse--Witt polynomial \(A_p\) is defined, and the selected coefficient index is \(n-p\). As \(p\) changes, both the field and the coefficient functional change. No fixed finite-rank compatible system has been constructed whose Frobenius class at \(p\) encodes

\[
 p\mid b_{n-p}
\tag{70}
\]

uniformly in \(n\).

A geometric large sieve would become relevant only after constructing such a fixed system with:

1. rank and conductor bounded independently of \(p,n\);
2. a conjugacy-stable target subset encoding (70);
3. product-monodromy independence sufficient for two distinct characteristics;
4. a quantitative large-sieve constant below the \(N^2\) Farey-spacing barrier.

None of these four ingredients is currently available.

### 10.3 Hasse--Witt and \(p\)-adic unit-root theorems

Adolphson--Sperber-type results identify unit roots through \(p\)-adic Frobenius matrices built from hypergeometric series. They are powerful one-characteristic statements. They do not estimate a sum in which one factor is a coefficient modulo \(p\) and the other is a different coefficient modulo \(\ell\).

The same limitation applies to a fixed-prime Deligne theorem: it may bound a complete sum in \(\mathbb F_p\), but it has no mechanism to correlate the resulting sign or phase with the corresponding object in \(\mathbb F_\ell\).

### 10.4 Barban--Davenport--Halberstam and Bombieri--Vinogradov

Classical dispersion theorems fix an arithmetic sequence and average its distribution over moduli. In (16), the tested sequence itself is \(z_p\) and changes with the modulus. Moreover, the relevant combined moduli are \(p\ell\asymp N^2\), larger than the interval length \(N\). Each CRT class contributes zero or one integer; there is no long progression over which classical averaging can operate.

### 10.5 Growing Kummer order

A finite-field hypergeometric reformulation may place the selected coefficient in a cyclotomic field attached to the character order \(d_{p,n}\). This is useful when \(d_{p,n}\) is small, and Section 7 exploits exactly that fact. In the surviving sector, \(d_{p,n}>N^{1/3-o(1)}\). Rational norms then have degree and height growing with \(d_{p,n}\), while the two primes generally live in different cyclotomic fields. Deligne bounds on individual conjugates do not compensate for the growing norm degree and do not couple the two fields.

## 11. Deterministic pair carriers

A pair-energy carrier must batch multiplicity, not merely support. Define

\[
 \mathfrak P_N
 =\prod_{N<n\le2N}
   \prod_{p\in T_n}p^{K_n-1}.
\tag{71}
\]

Since every target prime lies between \(N/2\) and \(2N\),

\[
 \log\mathfrak P_N
 \asymp H_2(N)\log N.
\tag{72}
\]

Thus a nonzero integer \(C_N\) satisfying

\[
 \mathfrak P_N\mid C_N,
 \qquad
 \log|C_N|=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log N}\right)
\tag{73}
\]

would prove (T). The exponent \(K_n-1\) is crucial. The familiar carrier \(b_n\), or the central-binomial carrier, contains each target prime only once and therefore cannot bound pair energy.

### 11.1 Linear pure-cross carrier

On the pure-cross locus (37), a linear expression

\[
 cb_s+db_{s+h}
\]

is formally divisible by \(p\ell\) for arbitrary unit quotient residues only if

\[
 p\mid c,
 \qquad
 \ell\mid d.
\tag{74}
\]

Indeed, reducing modulo \(p\) forces \(p\mid c\), because \(b_s=\ell v\) is a \(p\)-unit; reducing modulo \(\ell\) forces \(\ell\mid d\), because \(b_{s+h}=pu\) is an \(\ell\)-unit. Thus the prime content has already been inserted into the coefficient pair. The positive choice (55) is the canonical example, and it has linear height.

### 11.2 Casoratian tradeoff

Let \(a_m\) be the rational Apéry companion. The exact Casoratian is

\[
 \Delta_{s,h}
 =a_{s+h}b_s-a_sb_{s+h}
 =\frac{6N_h(s)}{\prod_{j=1}^h(s+j)^3}.
\tag{75}
\]

It has excellent height,

\[
 h(\Delta_{s,h})\ll h\log N,
\tag{76}
\]

but (40) says its primitive numerator is a unit modulo both pure-cross primes.

The natural crossed expression is

\[
 R_{s,h}
 =p\,a_{s+h}b_s-\ell\,a_sb_{s+h}.
\tag{77}
\]

After clearing companion denominators, which are units at \(p,\ell\), one has

\[
 p\ell\mid R_{s,h}^{\rm int}.
\tag{78}
\]

However,

\[
 R_{s,h}
 =p\Delta_{s,h}-h\,a_sb_{s+h}.
\tag{79}
\]

The first term is the low-gap determinant; the second is the mixed-characteristic defect and has full exponential Apéry height. For fixed \(h\) and bulk \(s\), the standard Poincar\'e asymptotics give

\[
 \log|R_{s,h}^{\rm int}|=\Theta(s).
\tag{80}
\]

Thus the exact tradeoff is:

```text
low height  -> determinant/Casoratian -> unit at both pure-cross primes
both primes -> crossed expression      -> exponential old-orbit term
```

### 11.3 Universal tensor and resultant saturation

The live repository proves a stronger algebraic no-go for universal recurrence constructions. After reducing all nearby states to two endpoint states, pure-cross divisibility is represented by

\[
 \mathcal C_{p,\ell}=(\ell,B)\cap(p,D)
                    =(\ell,B)(p,D).
\tag{81}
\]

Universal dominant cancellation is generated by the rank-one determinant

\[
 \Delta=AD-BC.
\]

For every \(m\ge0\),

\[
 \boxed{(\mathcal C_{p,\ell}:\Delta^m)=\mathcal C_{p,\ell}.}
\tag{82}
\]

Therefore determinant contraction does not remove the pure-cross ideal. A primitive polynomial remaining after contraction retains a dominant endpoint slot; a completely contracted scalar has coefficient content \(p\ell\).

At all degrees, if the divisibility proof uses only the separate moving-zero implications, the intersection of the local target ideals evaluates to exactly the unknown radical, not to a smaller independent integer. Resultants, Smith/Fitting ideals, growing exterior powers, and direct CRT recombination do not change that evaluation ideal.

The scope is important: this does **not** rule out an identity special to the distinguished Apéry initial state, a genuinely new divided target coordinate beyond the current rank-one tower, or an analytic cancellation theorem. It does rule out treating a formal resultant as though it automatically created a second selective equation.

### 11.4 Current higher-precision status

The full transfer frame has local Smith valuations \((0,3)\) at a top target. Through the first three divided grades, all neighboring target coordinates are functions of one scalar

\[
 \xi_r=\frac{b_r}{p}+D_r\pmod p.
\]

The verified lift is

\[
 \frac{b_{p+r}}p\equiv5\xi_r,
 \qquad
 \frac{p^3a_{p+r}}p\equiv6\xi_r\pmod p.
\tag{83}
\]

Thus precision \(p^2\) or \(p^3\) does not supply a second target equation. A second Smith direction becomes visible only at the fourth grade and is represented by companion data. No theorem currently makes that fourth-grade coordinate vanish on targets, and even such a theorem would still need a cross-prime batching mechanism.

## 12. Exact theorem interfaces that would close the problem

### 12.1 Minimal uncentered mixed-characteristic theorem

Define

\[
 \mathcal C(N)=
 \sum_{\substack{N/2<p<\ell\le2N\\
                  p,\ell\ {m prime}\\
                  \ell<2p}}
 \sum_{s\in I_N(p,\ell)}
 z_\ell(s)z_p(s+\ell-p).
\tag{84}
\]

By (16), \(H_2(N)=2\mathcal C(N)+O(1)\). Therefore the exact minimal theorem is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C(N)
 =o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).}
\tag{MAD}
\]

This is not intended as a disguised proof; it fixes every quantifier and identifies the object a geometric or bilinear method must estimate. It couples \(p\) and \(\ell\) because the same summand contains the two actual zero masks, the characteristic-dependent shift \(\ell-p\), and the common integer \(s\).

A meaningful power-saving form would be

\[
 \mathcal C(N)\ll N^{2-\delta}
\tag{85}
\]

for one fixed \(\delta>0\). This is much stronger than needed but is the natural output of a successful bilinear/geometric theorem.

### 12.2 Fourier bilinear theorem

A less tautological analytic interface is to prove cancellation in the nonzero-frequency part of (66), **together with** a bound for the zero-frequency contribution. Concretely, one needs

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{p<\ell}
 \frac1{p\ell}
 \sum_{\substack{a\bmod p,\ b\bmod\ell\\(a,b)\ne(0,0)}}
 \widehat z_p(a)\widehat z_\ell(b)e_p(a(\ell-p))\\
 &\hspace{35mm}\times
 \sum_{s\in I_N(p,\ell)}e_p(as)e_\ell(bs)
 =o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{86}
\]

and either

\[
 \sum_{N/2<p\le2N}z_p\ll\frac N{\log N}
\tag{87}
\]

or an uncentered replacement that absorbs the main term. Equation (86) genuinely couples the two characteristics through the product of the **actual** Fourier coefficients and the combined denominator \(p\ell\). Bounds of the form

\[
 |\widehat z_p(a)|\le Cp^{1/2}
\]

proved independently for each \(p\) do not imply (86).

### 12.3 Short-gap pointwise theorem

For P3.2 itself, a theorem weaker than full \(H_2\) would suffice. Fix \(A>0\). Let \(A_{A\log N}^{\times}(n)\) count adjacent target-prime pairs of even gap at most \(A\log N\) that are pure cross. It is enough to prove

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{N<n\le2N}
 A_{A\log N}^{\times}(n)
 =o_A(N/\log N).}
\tag{88}
\]

Indeed, a spike \(K_n\gg N/\log N\) has \(\gg N/\log N\) adjacent gaps of size \(O(\log N)\); the degenerate ones are \(O(\log^2N)\) by (32). This target avoids the full zero-frequency obstruction because only \(O(N\log N/\log^2N)\) prime pairs are tested.

This is the cleanest current theorem interface if the goal is P3.2 rather than the stronger empirical asymptotic for \(H_2\).

### 12.4 Deterministic batch-carrier theorem

The exact arithmetic alternative is to construct nonzero integers \(C_N\) satisfying (73). A pointwise version would construct \(C_n\) such that

\[
 \prod_{p\in T_n}p^{K_n-1}\mid C_n
\tag{89}
\]

with logarithmic height substantially below \(N^2/\log N\), preferably \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)\). Existing universal carriers contain each target only once; existing pairwise pure-cross carriers are linear-height per pair and do not batch.

Any claimed resultant or determinant solution should therefore be checked against the exponent in (89). Divisibility by \(\prod_{p\in T_n}p\) is only a support carrier and is insufficient for pair energy.

## 13. Final assessment

The rigorous unconditional advances are:

\[
 H_2(N)\ll\frac{N^{8/3}}{\log^2N},
\]

\[
 H_{2,\le H}(N)\ll\frac{HN^{5/3}}{\log^2N},
\]

\[
 H_{2,\le H}^{\rm deg}(N)\ll NH^2,
\]

and the low-order bounds (49)--(50). These isolate the residual to high-order pure-cross collisions.

No currently applicable Deligne, Katz, large-sieve-for-Frobenius, bilinear trace-function, BDH, hypergeometric, constant-term, or resultant theorem controls that residual. The failure is not that the right theorem was applied with a weak constant. The hypotheses do not match:

```text
available geometry: one finite field, bounded conductor, fixed family
needed estimate:    two varying residue characteristics in one summand,
                    moving coefficient indices, and actual zero-mask coupling
```

The deterministic carrier audit reaches the same boundary. Same-characteristic collisions have an \(O(h\log N)\)-height continuant carrier. Pure-cross collisions force that continuant to be a unit. Cross-weighting restores \(p\ell\)-divisibility only by reintroducing a full exponential Apéry slot, and universal contraction saturates at coefficient content \(p\ell\).

Accordingly, the sharp next problem is not another fixed-prime estimate. It is one of the following genuinely new statements:

1. the uncentered mixed-characteristic incidence estimate (MAD);
2. the Fourier coupling theorem (86) plus the average zero-fibre estimate (87);
3. the weaker logarithmic-gap pure-cross dispersion theorem (88);
4. a distinguished-state batch carrier satisfying (89).

Any of these would use information absent from the presently listed inputs. Without such a theorem, the current arithmetic and geometric machinery saturates at (21), with the substantive partial savings recorded above.
