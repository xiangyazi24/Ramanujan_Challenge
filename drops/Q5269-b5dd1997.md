## 1.1 Why gcds, radicals and lcms do not automatically help

Suppose the same shell prime \(p\) supports selected roots for several different pairs \((b,c)\).  The literal mass counts every one of these pair/root records.  Consequently

* replacing \(\Pi_W\) by its radical pays \(p\) once and loses pair multiplicity;
* replacing it by an lcm pays only the largest valuation and loses pair multiplicity;
* a large gcd between two resultants says that the same prime is shared, but does not identify the two numerical roots or allow one valuation unit to pay both records.

This is exactly the distinction emphasized in `meso_result.tex`: simple resultant support is not collision energy, and recurrence on the continuants does **not** close on the scalar resultants.  In the quotient algebra \(K_d\), the norm of
\[

\]
depends on polarized mixed norms; different \(r\)'s may vanish in different residue-field components.  Restart identities require the **same root witness**.  Thus a scalar cross-pair gcd theorem is new arithmetic, not a consequence of the recurrence already in the source.

## 1.2 Simplicity does not rescue average Smith valuation

# 6. Why a two-variable discriminant still misses the obstruction

One might try to regard \((b,c)\) as parameters and eliminate \(x\) from the entire triangular family.  There are two problems.

First, the recurrence index is discrete and the degree of \(N_h\) grows with \(h\).  The existing source has no bounded-degree bivariate polynomial whose fiber at \((b,c)\) is the common-root condition.  Forming the literal product of the fibers simply returns the product determinant (1.1), with additive logarithmic height.

Second, even a hypothetical parameter discriminant would naturally detect collisions where two parameter values share the **same numerical root**.  Consecutiveness does the opposite: at one selected start the pair \((b,c)\) is unique.  The hard mass is carried by many different simple starts \(x\), so there need be no parameter discriminant vanishing at all.

This is the same low-fiber obstruction which appears in `meso_result.tex` and `energy_result.tex`: factorial/energy mechanisms control repeated participation at one witness, while a large set of singleton witnesses can carry a large first moment.
