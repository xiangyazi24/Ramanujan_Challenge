# P3.2 p-adic attack 2: supercongruences for K(r,s)

## Executive verdict

The proposed descent mechanism does **not** close with the currently known Apéry supercongruences. The natural candidates

```text
K(r+p,s+p) ≡ K(r,s) (mod p^m)

K(mp-1,np-1) ≡ K(m-1,n-1) (mod p^m)
```

are false in general for the kernel

```text
K(r,s)=sum_{j=0}^{r} b_j b_{r+s-j} (or the equivalent Hankel/convolution form used in P3.2)
```

except in special boundary configurations inherited from the Apéry Lucas law. The reason is that K mixes two Apéry indices, and the shift changes the full convolution range; the single-index supercongruence for b_n is not stable under this summation.

## 1. What the Apéry congruence actually gives

The known result is

```text
b_{mp^k-1} ≡ b_{mp^{k-1}-1} (mod p^{3k}).
```

This controls isolated indices at the special endpoints `mp^k-1`. It implies, for example,

```text
b_{p-1} ≡ 1 (mod p^3),
```

and the standard Lucas-type congruences

```text
b_{qp+r} ≡ b_q b_r (mod p)
```

for the scalar sequence.

For K this is insufficient because every summand transforms with a different residue split:

```text
b_j b_{r+s-j}
```
becomes

```text
b_{j_0+p j_1} b_{r+s-j_0+p(s_1-j_1)},
```

and the sum over all j introduces nontrivial carry terms.

## 2. The shift K(r+p,s+p)

Write

```text
K(r+p,s+p)=Σ_{j=0}^{r+p} b_j b_{r+s+2p-j}.
```

Split j into residue classes modulo p:

```text
j=u+pv, 0≤u<p.
```

The Lucas law gives formally

```text
b_{u+pv} ≡ b_u b_v (mod p),
```

but the second factor has a shifted index

```text
r+s+2p-u-pv
 = (r+s-u)+p(2-v).
```

Hence the transformed summand is

```text
b_u b_v · b_{r+s-u} b_{2-v}
```

only after splitting into ranges where no carries occur. The carry ranges are exactly the terms that prevent a clean identity with

```text
K(r,s)=Σ_{u=0}^{r} b_u b_{r+s-u}.
```

The obstruction appears already modulo p: the extra residue blocks do not cancel automatically.

Therefore the strongest generic statement expected is not

```text
K(r+p,s+p) ≡ K(r,s) (mod p),
```

but a block decomposition involving several smaller K-values.

## 3. Endpoint shift mp-1, np-1

This is the only plausible surviving case because the Apéry supercongruence is concentrated at `p^k-1` endpoints.

Consider

```text
K(mp-1,np-1).
```

The summation range is aligned with p-blocks. Splitting into blocks gives

```text
j=ap+b,
0≤b<p.
```

Then

```text
b_{ap+b} ≡ b_a b_b (mod p),
```

and similarly for the complementary index. The leading term becomes a product of small block convolutions. The resulting formula is of the shape

```text
K(mp-1,np-1)
 ≡ Σ_a K(a, n-1-a) · K_block(b, p-1-b)
(mod p).
```

The inner p-block is controlled by the Apéry endpoint identity, but it does not collapse to a single copy of

```text
K(m-1,n-1).
```

A genuine supercongruence would require an additional identity for the block kernel. No such identity follows from the known b_n congruences.

## 4. Result of the small-index descent idea

The hoped-for implication was:

```text
K(r,s) mod p
↓
K(r',s') mod p
↓
...
↓
nonzero small kernel
```

The obstruction is that Lucas descent branches. A single pair `(r,s)` produces a collection of lower pairs indexed by residue blocks. The descent tree does not remain one-dimensional.

This is unlike the scalar Apéry sequence where

```text
b_{qp+r} ≡ b_q b_r
```

is a rank-one factorization.

For K the corresponding object has higher rank.

## 5. What would still be useful

A weaker statement may exist:

```text
K(mp-1,np-1) ≡ C_p(m,n) K(m-1,n-1) (mod p)
```

for a computable scalar correction factor `C_p(m,n)`, or a finite linear combination of smaller kernels.

Such a relation would still help P3.2 if one could prove that the correction matrix has full rank and preserves nonvanishing.

The correct target is therefore not a scalar supercongruence, but a finite-dimensional Frobenius/block recurrence for the kernel family.

## Final status

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| `K(r+p,s+p) ≡ K(r,s)` | Refuted as a generic expectation |
| `K(mp-1,np-1) ≡ K(m-1,n-1)` | Not supported; carries prevent collapse |
| Block decomposition of K under Lucas | Expected and structurally plausible |
| Scalar descent to small K | Fails without extra cancellation |
| Higher-rank Frobenius descent | Remaining viable p-adic route |

The p-adic attack does not produce the desired direct proof of `K(r,s)≠0`; the surviving route is to derive and exploit a matrix-valued supercongruence for the kernel family rather than a scalar one.
