## 1. Unified direct/reflected fixed-cell geometry

Let `eps=0` in the direct chart and `eps=1` in the reflected chart, and put

\[
c=a+\varepsilon,\qquad
\tau=2\varepsilon-1,\qquad
M=m+\varepsilon.
\]

Then both prime formulas are

\[
\boxed{p(h)=\frac{M+\tau h}{c}.}
\tag{1}
\]

For

\[
h_S=u+\sum_{j\in S}\delta_j,
\qquad
(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)=(d,e,f),
\]

all eight vertices staying in one quotient/orientation cell implies, by taking numerator differences,

\[
\boxed{c\mid d,e,f.}
\tag{2}
\]

Writing `Delta_j=delta_j/c` and

\[
P=(M+\tau u)/c,
\]

the eight defining primes themselves form the affine Boolean cube

\[
\boxed{
p_S=P+\tau\sum_{j\in S}\Delta_j.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus

\[
|p_T-p_S|=|h_T-h_S|/c\le(d+e+f)/c.
\tag{4}
\]

In the direct chart this is

\[
p_S=P-\sum_{j\in S}\delta_j/a,

and in the reflected chart

p_S=P+\sum_{j\in S}\delta_j/(a+1).

The affine-prime-cube relation is pure quotient geometry; it is present before imposing any Apéry zero.  A determinant that vanishes merely because of (3) is not target-selective.

The `O(1)` reflection exceptions in the reduction must be removed before the argument.  They only cost the stated bounded multiplicity.

## 2. Literal local first-divided equation

The repository has the direct quotient-one supercongruence

\[
\boxed{
b_{p+r}\equiv5b_r+10pD_r\pmod{p^2},}
\tag{5}
\]

where `D_r` is the banked harmonic-binomial correction.  I do **not** promote this formula to general ordinary quotient `a` without a proof.

For any fixed `(a,eps)` where a first-divided lift has actually been proved, write its local equation in the only form needed below:

\[
\boxed{
BV_i\equiv \beta_i z_i+\Gamma_i\pmod{p_i},
}
\tag{6}
\]

where

\[
R=\prod_i p_i,\qquad
B=b_m/R,\qquad
z_i=b_{h_i}/p_i,\qquad
V_i=\prod_{j\ne i}(p_j-p_i),
\]

and `beta_i` is a unit on the legal nonsingular branch.  For (5), `beta_i=5` and `Gamma_i=10D_{h_i}`.

The barycentric coefficient is forced by CRT arithmetic:

\[
\frac Rp_i=\prod_{j\ne i}p_j
\equiv\prod_{j\ne i}(p_j-p_i)=V_i\pmod{p_i}.
\tag{7}
\]

Since the defining primes are distinct, `V_i` is a `p_i`-unit.  If `beta_i` is also a unit, (6) simply gives

\[
\boxed{
z_i\equiv\beta_i^{-1}(BV_i-\Gamma_i)\pmod{p_i}.}
\tag{8}
\]

So the lift solves for the local divided zero.  It does not constrain `B`.

This is why the small height of `V_i` is insufficient: the literal equation also contains `B`, `z_i`, and the correction coordinate.  Dropping them is exactly the invalid elimination the audit is meant to prevent.

## 3. Canonical Vandermonde determinant

Let

\[
F(T)=\prod_{i=1}^8(T-p_i).
\]

Because there are seven other nodes,

\[
F'(p_i)=\prod_{j\ne i}(p_i-p_j)=-V_i.
\tag{9}
\]

For arbitrary nodal data `y_i`, the top coefficient of the unique degree-at-most-seven interpolant is

\[
[T^7]Q=\sum_i\frac{y_i}{F'(p_i)}.
\tag{10}
\]

Put `y_i=BV_i`.  Then

\[
\boxed{[T^7]Q=-8B.}
\tag{11}
\]

Equivalently, for

\[
\Delta(p)=\prod_{i<j}(p_j-p_i),
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
1&p_1&\cdots&p_1^6&BV_1\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&p_8&\cdots&p_8^6&BV_8
\end{pmatrix}
=-8B\Delta(p).
}
\tag{12}
\]

From (4), with `H=d+e+f`,

\[
|V_i|\le(H/c)^7,
\qquad
|\Delta(p)|\le(H/c)^{28}.
\tag{13}
\]

This is the strongest canonical span-controlled determinant I see.  It fails for an exact reason: the quotient `B` survives with coefficient `-8`.

There is no hidden gain from Boolean interpolation either.  The space of multilinear functions on `{0,1}^3` has dimension eight, exactly the number of cube vertices.  Any eight labels interpolate.  A face/cube determinant becomes restrictive only after a **new degree/rank drop theorem**, which the local lift does not supply.

One also cannot treat (6) as eight row equations in one field.  Row `i` is a congruence modulo `p_i`.  If it is lifted to an integer equation,

\[
y_i=BV_i+p_it_i,
\tag{14}
\]

then the eight `t_i` are independent CRT variables.  Substitution into a determinant produces independent cofactor directions and restores full freedom.

## 4. Scoped saturation theorem

### Theorem

Fix one nonexceptional cell `(m,a,eps)` and a nondegenerate eight-vertex cube.  Assume:

1. the eight defining primes `p_i` are distinct;
2. at component `p_i`, `b_{h_i}=0 mod p_i`, while every `b_{h_j}`, `j!=i`, is a unit — the 56 directed cross-unit masks;
3. every short transfer used inside the cube is invertible at the relevant component — the 28 transfer-unit masks;
4. every extra finite-depth lift is Hensel-triangular: after lower digits are fixed, each new scalar equation has a fresh divided local digit with unit coefficient;
5. different primes are coupled only through shared integral boundary variables, `B`, and ordinary algebraic interpolation/determinants.  No extra cross-prime modular/Hecke identity or independent Archimedean height restriction is imported.

After localizing at all those unit coefficients, eliminating the local boundary states, divided digits, finite correction states, lift multipliers, and `B` produces no target equation beyond the quotient/cube geometry (1)-(3).  In the localized formal model, the contraction of the local arithmetic ideal to the geometric parameter ring is exactly the geometric ideal.

### Proof

At one component `p_i`, a finite short transfer is an invertible linear map on the two-dimensional Apéry state.  The condition `b_{h_i}=0 mod p_i` is one nonzero linear functional on that state.  The seven cross values are units on the selected kernel line.

Lift the boundary state from modulo `p_i` to modulo `p_i^2`.  A variation

\[
s_i\mapsto s_i+p_it_i
\]

preserves all mod-`p_i` zero/unit masks.  The target evaluation functional is nonzero, so its value on `t_i` can be prescribed arbitrarily.  Hence

\[
z_i=b_{h_i}/p_i\pmod{p_i}
\]

is a free Hensel coordinate.  A triangular first-divided equation such as (6) determines `z_i` for every prescribed `B mod p_i`.  The same induction works through any fixed number of triangular lift levels.

For all eight primes and any fixed depth `k`, CRT gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf Z/R^k\mathbf Z
\simeq
\prod_i\mathbf Z/p_i^k\mathbf Z.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Therefore one integral boundary vector modulo `R^k` is equivalent to eight arbitrary local boundary vectors; likewise one integer `B mod R^k` is equivalent to eight arbitrary local `B_i`.  The notation “common boundary” or “common B” does not create a congruence coupling between distinct defining characteristics.

Every local component is therefore independently solvable throughout the localized open set.  Existential elimination can recover only the quotient geometry already imposed.  Full-degree interpolation is an isomorphism between the eight nodal values and eight coefficients, so it adds no contraction.  This proves the scoped statement.

### Exact boundary of the theorem

The theorem does **not** say that the true Apéry boundary state at index zero is arbitrary.  It says the finite local package has no access to the global arithmetic condition selecting that state.  If one imports the genuine global boundary, a modular/Hecke identity, a moving-index period theorem, or a nontrivial primitive Fitting minor with independently controlled height, one has left the scope of the saturation theorem.

That caveat is essential: the repository already contains examples where a direct growing transfer word has a different bounded-complexity toric/Kummer realization.  The present theorem is local/CRT, not a universal geometric no-go.

## 5. Why finite transfer/resultants do not repair it

The exact gap polynomial `N_G` has degree `3(G-1)`, and the repository's fixed-pair resultants are useful when one prime divides two endpoint values.  But the literal cube masks say, for each defining prime,

\[
\boxed{
p_i\mid b_{h_i},\qquad p_i\nmid b_{h_j}\ (j\ne i).}
\tag{16}
\]

So no directed pair in the cube supplies the two same-prime zeros needed to force a gap resultant divisor.  The transfer masks are units precisely because all those short words remain on the invertible branch.  They are open conditions, not extra equations.

A new many-row Fitting determinant could still escape if it is formed **before** componentwise localization and if one proves that a primitive nonzero minor has small height after every legitimate saturation.  No such conclusion follows from finite transfer alone; that is a genuinely new theorem.

The banked fixed-rank differential compression of the gap kernel does not change this conclusion: the moving gap remains a coefficient index, and it does not itself couple divisibility events at eight different defining characteristics.

## 6. Weighted aggregate versus pointwise

Write `C_cube(c)` for the actual retained cubes in cell `c`.  The exact reduction gives

\[
\#\{c\}\ll C_X:=X\log^2X/L
\]

and forces

\[
\sum_cC_{\rm cube}(c)\gg F_X,
\qquad
F_X:=L^{15}/(X^{14}\log^{14}X).
\]

A uniform cell bound strong enough to contradict this is

\[
\boxed{
C_{\rm cube}(c)=o(F_X/C_X)
=o\!\left(
\frac{L^{16}}{X^{15}\log^{16}X}
\right).
}
\tag{17}
\]

The weighted route only needs

\[
\sum_cC_{\rm cube}(c)
\le C_X^{1/2}
\left(\sum_cC_{\rm cube}(c)^2\right)^{1/2},
\]

so the sufficient second-moment target is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_cC_{\rm cube}(c)^2
=o(F_X^2/C_X)
=o\!\left(
\frac{L^{31}}{X^{29}\log^{30}X}
\right).
}
\tag{18}
\]

This is a genuine advantage: exceptional cells are allowed, and cross-cell cancellation/rarity can be used.

If the short-span reduction gives `d,e,f<=H_*` (or merely `d+e+f<=H_*`), canonically order the three directions and forget the third.  A fixed square has at most `O(H_*)` choices for that remaining side; inside one fixed quotient cell the count is actually at most `H_*/(a+eps)`.  Thus

\[
\sum_cC_{\rm cube}(c)
\ll H_*\sum_cQ_c(H_*).
\]

Therefore the four-point target

\[
\boxed{
\sum_cQ_c(H_*)
=o\!\left(
\frac{L^{15}}{X^{14}H_*\log^{14}X}
\right)
}
\tag{IC4}
\]

closes the cube gateway.

A pair aggregate with an `H_*^2` completion loss would also be sufficient numerically if made strong enough, but it is not the right arithmetic target: pair resultants are disabled by (16).  A square is the first object that sees a commuting additive face rather than one same-prime bridge.

## 7. Smallest honest escape theorem

I would formulate the remaining arithmetic input as `IC4` above, with all literal masks retained.  Equivalently, an algebraic escape can be stated as:

> **Cross-prime divided-lift rigidity.**  For a positive proportion of retained square faces, the first divided zero/correction coordinates at the four distinct defining primes satisfy a nonzero relation that survives CRT localization and whose primitive integer carrier has logarithmic height controlled by the face span.

That is strictly more than a collection of four local `p^2` laws: the relation has to couple distinct defining characteristics before the local divided digits are eliminated.

A stronger but analytically natural alternative is the cube second moment (18).  Either theorem attacks the aggregate directly and avoids the impossible demand that every cell admit a pointwise small boundary determinant.
