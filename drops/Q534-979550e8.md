# Apéry Kloosterman Dispersion (AKD_3): which structure gives off-diagonal cancellation?

## Summary

The five listed Apéry properties do not contribute equally. The most promising route is a combination of:

1. **Cartier-diagonal representation** (property 5) as the source of algebraic-geometric cancellation;
2. **Sp(4) monodromy** (property 3) as the equidistribution input;
3. **The recurrence** (property 4) as the mechanism identifying the relevant l-adic sheaf.

Palindromicity and the gap polynomial are useful constraints, but alone they are unlikely to prove the required bilinear Kloosterman dispersion.

The expected proof should not try to use only M1. It should prove that the family of Apéry zero sets is generated by a high-monodromy trace function, so that the off-diagonal correlations are controlled by Deligne-type bounds.

---

# 1. Why arbitrary moving targets fail

The Q520 counterexample identifies the key obstruction. A family of sets $Z_p$ can have:

\[
|Z_p|=O(1)
\]

for every prime but still have maximal correlation between different primes. Therefore M1 only controls the diagonal density:

\[
\sum_p |Z_p|,
\]

not the pair correlations:

\[
\sum_{p\neq q}|Z_p\cap Z_q|.
\]

AKD_3 requires genuine randomness of the Apéry residue process.

---

# 2. Property 5: Cartier diagonal is the strongest starting point

The identity

\[
b_r = [x^ry^rz^rw^r]F(x,y,z,w)^{p-1}
\]

places Apéry numbers inside the theory of diagonals of rational functions.

This is important because diagonals over finite fields correspond to Frobenius traces of algebraic varieties. After reduction modulo $p$, one expects:

\[
r\mapsto b_r \pmod p
\]

is not an arbitrary sequence but a trace function

\[
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_r|\mathcal F)
\]

for an underlying l-adic sheaf.

Then the bilinear term becomes a correlation sum of trace functions:

\[
\sum_r T_p(r)\overline{T_q(r)}.
\]

If the geometric monodromy is large and the tensor product sheaf has no trivial component, Deligne's Riemann Hypothesis gives square-root cancellation.

This is exactly the shape needed for AKD_3.

---

# 3. Role of Sp(4) monodromy

The Apéry differential equation has symplectic rank-4 monodromy. This predicts that the associated sheaf has geometric group approximately

\[
\mathrm{Sp}_4.
\]

For the off-diagonal problem, one needs more than the one-point Sato-Tate law. One needs independence of two Frobenius evaluations.

The desired statement is schematically:

\[
\sum_r f_p(r)f_q(r)=O((pq)^{1/2+\epsilon})
\]

unless there is a geometric reason for correlation.

Sp(4) helps because the representation is irreducible and has few invariants. The tensor product representation usually contains no trivial representation, preventing a main term in the off-diagonal correlation.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{large monodromy } + \text{ trace-function realization } \Rightarrow \text{ AKD cancellation}.}
\]

---

# 4. Role of the recurrence

The recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}
\]

is not likely to give cancellation directly by elementary exponential sum estimates.

However, it identifies the rank-4 Picard-Fuchs connection. In a proof it would be used to:

1. construct the l-adic sheaf;
2. prove its rank and singularities;
3. compute geometric monodromy;
4. apply Deligne/Katz estimates.

So recurrence is the bridge from the explicit sequence to algebraic geometry.

---

# 5. Role of palindromicity

The symmetry

\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p
\]

is valuable but insufficient.

It implies that zero sets occur in pairs:

\[
r\in Z_p \Rightarrow p-1-r\in Z_p.
\]

This explains observed symmetry and reduces the effective number of independent zeros.

However, symmetry does not prevent different primes from sharing correlated residues. It cannot replace a dispersion estimate.

---

# 6. Role of the gap polynomial

The bounded-degree polynomial

\[
B(x)=\prod_{r:b_r=0}(x-r)
\]

is useful for counting zeros inside one prime.

It gives rigidity:

\[
|Z_p|\le p^{2/3}.
\]

But this is still a worst-case bound. It does not control correlations between $Z_p$ and $Z_q$.

The gap polynomial may become useful after the trace-function step, for example by converting zero conditions into algebraic subvarieties.

---

# 7. Expected AKD_3 proof architecture

A realistic proof would look like:

## Step 1: Construct the Apéry sheaf

Use the Cartier diagonal representation to associate an l-adic sheaf $\mathcal F$ such that

\[
b_r\equiv \mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_r|\mathcal F)\pmod p.
\]

## Step 2: Prove geometric monodromy

Show

\[
G_{geom}(\mathcal F)=\mathrm{Sp}_4.
\]

The recurrence gives the Picard-Fuchs system; irreducibility excludes smaller groups.

## Step 3: Rewrite the off-diagonal term

The cross term becomes

\[
\sum_r 1_{b_r\equiv0}1_{b_r'\equiv0}
\]

or, using additive characters,

\[
\frac1{p^2}\sum_{u,v}\sum_r e_p(ub_r+vb_r').
\]

The inner sum is now a trace-function correlation.

## Step 4: Apply Deligne/Katz

For nontrivial tensor products:

\[
\left|\sum_r e_p(ub_r+vb_r')\right|
\ll p^{1/2}.
\]

Summing over the auxiliary characters gives the desired dispersion bound.

---

# Final ranking of the five properties

| Property | Expected contribution to AKD_3 |
|---|---|
| Cartier diagonal | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ main route to Deligne bounds |
| Sp(4) monodromy | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ gives irreducibility/randomness |
| Recurrence | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ identifies the sheaf |
| Gap polynomial | ⭐⭐ local zero counting only |
| Palindromicity | ⭐⭐ symmetry, not dispersion |

Therefore the most likely successful route is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Cartier diagonal}
\rightarrow
\text{l-adic sheaf}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{Sp}_4\text{ monodromy}
\rightarrow
\text{Deligne correlation bounds}
\rightarrow
AKD_3.
}
\]

This is the genuinely Apéry-specific ingredient missing from the arbitrary moving-target model.
