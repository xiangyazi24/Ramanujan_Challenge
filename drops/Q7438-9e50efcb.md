# P32 pointwise denominator theorem search

## Conclusion

No known general theorem in the G-function, logarithmic-extension, Hadamard-product, or P-recursive denominator literature implies the required pointwise estimate

\[
\log\gcd(L_n,L_nR_1(n),L_n^2R_2(n),L_n^3R_3(n))=o(n).
\]

The available theorems control either:

1. denominators of a whole sequence through an lcm/common denominator bound;
2. growth of coefficients or solutions outside a thin exceptional set;
3. arithmetic properties of specific sequences after additional structural arguments.

They do not give a uniform lower bound on the reduced denominator of every individual coefficient.

## Relevant denominator theory

### 1. G-functions: global denominator bounds, not pointwise lower bounds

For a G-function \(F(z)=\sum a_nz^n\), the standard denominator theorem is due to the Chudnovskys:

G. V. Chudnovsky and D. V. Chudnovsky, "Applications of Padé approximations to the Grothendieck conjecture on linear differential equations," in *Number Theory*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1135, Springer, 1985.

Their denominator estimates imply that there exists a sequence of integers \(D_n\) with controlled growth such that the first \(n\) coefficients satisfy denominator divisibility conditions. The statement is of the form:

\[
\operatorname{den}(a_0),\ldots,\operatorname{den}(a_n)\mid D_n,
\]

with exponential-type control on \(D_n\).

This is an aggregate upper bound. It says nothing about

\[
\operatorname{den}(a_n)
\]

for a fixed index, and certainly nothing about the numerator gcd after multiplying by a chosen common denominator.

### 2. André's G-function theory

Y. André, *G-functions and Geometry*, Aspects of Mathematics E13, Vieweg, 1989.

André proves structural results for G-functions, including arithmetic restrictions arising from differential equations and geometry. The denominator estimates are again common-denominator statements. They do not imply that infinitely many (or every) coefficient has nearly maximal denominator.

### 3. Bombieri-Dwork and arithmetic differential equations

The Bombieri-Dwork philosophy concerns the geometric origin of G-functions and integrality after suitable rescaling. It does not provide a theorem of the form:

\[
\operatorname{den}(a_n)\ge e^{cn-o(n)}.
\]

No such general pointwise denominator lower bound is known.

## Logarithmic extensions and polylogarithms

The coefficients

\[
R_s(n)=[t^n]B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t)
\]

are logarithmic extensions of the Apéry G-function. Since

\[
\operatorname{Li}_s(t)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{t^m}{m^s},
\]

we have the convolution

\[
R_s(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{n-k}}{k^s}.
\]

The obvious denominator bound is

\[
L_n^sR_s(n)\in\mathbb Z,
\]

coming from the individual factors \(1/k^s\). This gives exactly the upper-triangular/lower-unitriangular aggregate relation used in the P32 argument.

However, logarithmic extension theorems (including results on periods, mixed Tate extensions, and iterated integrals) preserve common denominator control. They do not force cancellation patterns in the reduced fractions.

## Hadamard products

Hadamard-product closure results for G-functions show that operations such as coefficientwise products preserve the G-function class. Relevant references include:

- Y. André, *G-functions and Geometry*, especially the closure properties of G-functions.
- F. Beukers, "G-functions and the hypergeometric function," in *Arithmetic and Geometry around Hypergeometric Functions*, 2007.

These results establish that the resulting sequence remains in a controlled arithmetic class. They do not imply pointwise denominator lower bounds.

## P-recursive rational sequences

The Apéry numbers are P-recursive. General P-recursive theory gives recurrences, asymptotics, and holonomic closure properties. It does not provide denominator-growth lower bounds for individual rational solutions.

A rational P-recursive sequence may have very small reduced denominators even when it satisfies a recurrence whose companion sequences have large common denominators. Additional arithmetic information is required.

## Quantifier mismatch

The target statement is:

\[
\forall n,\quad \log G_n=o(n),
\]

where

\[
G_n=\gcd(L_n,L_nR_1(n),L_n^2R_2(n),L_n^3R_3(n)).
\]

The denominator literature generally proves statements of the following forms:

### Aggregate form

\[
\exists D_N\quad \forall n\le N,\quad D_Na_n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

This controls an lcm over an interval.

### Density-one form

Results in analytic number theory or equidistribution may show that a property holds for almost all indices:

\[
\#\{n\le N:P(n)\}\sim N.
\]

This still allows an exceptional infinite subsequence.

### Required pointwise form

The P32 step requires:

\[
\forall n\text{ sufficiently large},\quad G_n\le e^{o(n)}.
\]

Neither aggregate denominator theory nor density-one results imply this.

## Consequence for P32

The equality of the aggregate denominator

\[
\operatorname{lcm}_{0\le n\le N}\operatorname{den}(R_s(n))=L_N^s
\]

is compatible with very large cancellations at individual indices. The missing theorem is exactly a pointwise anti-cancellation theorem for the simultaneous numerators of the logarithmic Apéry convolutions.

A valid proof would require new input, for example:

1. a direct arithmetic analysis of \(R_1,R_2,R_3\);
2. p-adic lower bounds for infinitely many primes dividing reduced denominators;
3. a structural theorem on the mixed Tate/logarithmic extension attached to the Apéry motive strong enough to control every coefficient.

Existing G-function denominator theory alone does not provide the required estimate.
