# Far-bridge literature audit: no existing theorem is directly applicable

## Verdict

I do **not** find a directly applicable primary-source finite-field large-sieve, trace-function, expander, or time-inhomogeneous transfer-matrix theorem that yields the required prime-averaged bound for the actual common-root mass

\[
\sum_{X<p\le 2X}\ \sum_{2\le s\le \lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor}\ 
\sum_{K<G\le p-1-s}
\#\left\{0\le x\le p-1-s-G:
N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\pmod p\right\}.
\]

The obstruction is not merely that a convenient citation has not been located. Every standard theorem considered below fails at a specific structural hypothesis:

1. **Finite-field large sieves for Frobenius require one fixed bounded-complexity compatible system.** Here the family changes with the ordered word length \(G\), and the natural group-valued realization has \(G-1\) distinct singular points.
2. **Trace-function estimates require bounded or quantitatively controlled conductor and usually a fixed trace function.** Here the relevant polynomial or transfer sheaf has complexity growing at least linearly with \(G\), with \(G\asymp p\), and the zero indicator is not a gallant trace function.
3. **Expansion theorems average random words in a fixed generating set.** The Apéry product is one prescribed, consecutively ordered, position-dependent word.
4. **Time-inhomogeneous random-walk theorems still require genuine random steps with singular values below one.** Point-mass steps have second singular value exactly one and provide no contraction.
5. **Cyclic completion crosses a rank-one step.** The actual nonwrapping interval avoids it, but a full-\(\mathbf F_p\) group-valued completion has \(G-1\) starts whose block contains the singular step; for \(G\asymp p\) this is not an \(O(1)\) error.
6. **The observable is the number of common roots in \(\mathbf F_p\), not the algebraic gcd degree.** A theorem that counts all irreducible common factors can pay for nonsplit factors that never produce an actual index \(x\).

The genuinely new input needed is a **prime-averaged, growing-block, quenched two-return variance theorem for the deterministic Apéry cocycle**, after explicitly projecting away the reflection diagonals. I state a precise sufficient form in Section 8 and prove that it gives the required \(X^{2+o(1)}/\log X\) dyadic bound.

## 1. The exact observable and why gcd degree is not it

Write

\[
I_{p,s,G}=\{0,1,\ldots,p-1-s-G\}
\]

and

\[
C_p(s,G)=
\#\{x\in I_{p,s,G}:N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\pmod p\}.
\]

The exact reduction in `far_bridge_incidence.tex` is

\[
E_p^{\rm far}(K)
\le B_p^{[4]}(K)
\le B_p^\circ(K)
\le B_p(K)
\le A_p^{\rm nw}(K),
\]

with

\[
A_p^{\rm nw}(K)=
\sum_{s=2}^{\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor}
\sum_{G=K+1}^{p-1-s} C_p(s,G).
\]

This is a count of **rational indices \(x\in\mathbf F_p\)** lying in a particular integer interval. If the interval restriction is dropped, the exact full-field count is

\[
\widetilde C_p(s,G)
=\#\{x\in\mathbf F_p:N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\}
\]

and, because \(X^p-X\) is squarefree and is the product of all monic linear polynomials over \(\mathbf F_p\),

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde C_p(s,G)
=\deg\gcd_{\mathbf F_p[X]}
\bigl(N_s(X),N_G(X+s),X^p-X\bigr).
}
\]

By contrast,

\[
\deg\gcd_{\mathbf F_p[X]}(N_s(X),N_G(X+s))
\]

also counts common irreducible factors of degree \(>1\). Those are nonsplit resultant factors and do not yield any \(x\in\mathbf F_p\). Thus gcd degree is an upper bound, not the observable required by the far-bridge injection.

This distinction is essential in a literature audit. A Frobenius or factorization theorem controlling the algebraic common factor need not control the actual common-root mass at the right scale.

## 2. Exact Apéry transfer formulation

The recurrence used in `FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex` is

\[
(n+1)^3y_{n+1}=P(n)y_n-n^3y_{n-1},
\qquad
P(u)=(2u+1)(17u^2+17u+5).
\]

The polynomial is

\[
P(u)=34u^3+51u^2+27u+5.
\]

The factorial-gauge transfer matrix is

\[
M_u=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&(u+1)^6\\
-1&P(u)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det M_u=(u+1)^6.
\]

For \(u\ne-1\) in \(\mathbf F_p\), scalar normalization gives

\[
A_p(u)=(u+1)^{-3}M_u\in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p).
\]

For a nonwrapping gap \(h\ge2\), define the ordered block

\[
T_p(r,h)=A_p(r+h-1)A_p(r+h-2)\cdots A_p(r+1).
\]

Let \(0=[0:1]\) and \(\infty=[1:0]\) in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\). The exact restart dictionary

\[
N_h(r)=U_{h-1}(r+1)
\]

and the transfer-product formula imply

\[
\boxed{
N_h(r)=0
\iff T_p(r,h)\,0=\infty
}
\]

whenever \(0\le r<r+h\le p-1\). Therefore

\[
C_p(s,G)
=
\sum_{x\in I_{p,s,G}}
\mathbf 1_{\{T_p(x,s)0=\infty\}}
\mathbf 1_{\{T_p(x+s,G)0=\infty\}}.
\]

Thus the far problem is a two-hit incidence for two **adjacent deterministic nonautonomous blocks**:

\[
A_p(x+1),\ldots,A_p(x+s-1)
\quad\text{and}\quad
A_p(x+s+1),\ldots,A_p(x+s+G-1).
\]

The gap length \(G\) is itself a parameter and may be of order \(p\). This is not a random walk in a fixed finite set and is not a fixed trace function evaluated at a varying algebraic argument.

### 2.1 The singular step is exact, not cosmetic

Since \(P(-1)=-5\),

\[
M_{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\
-1&-5
\end{pmatrix}
\]

has rank one. It is not an element of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) or \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), and \(A_p(-1)\) is undefined.

The actual nonwrapping range is designed so that neither block reaches that step. Indeed, if \(x\in I_{p,s,G}\), then

\[
x+s+G\le p-1,
\]

so every factor in the second block has index at most \(p-2\).

However, as a rational group-valued function of a completed variable \(x\in\mathbf F_p\), the determinant of the second unnormalized block is

\[
\det\bigl(M_{x+s+G-1}\cdots M_{x+s+1}\bigr)
=\prod_{j=2}^{G}(x+s+j)^6.
\]

Because \(G<p\), this has exactly \(G-1\) distinct zeros in \(\mathbf F_p\). Consequently:

- the natural \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\)-valued block map is defined only after deleting \(G-1\) points;
- the singular locus grows linearly with \(G\);
- completing the nonwrapping interval to all of \(\mathbf F_p\) introduces \(G-1\) starts whose ordered block contains the rank-one step.

For \(G\asymp p\), deleting or trivially estimating those starts costs \(\asymp p\) for a single \(G\). Summed over \(s\le\sqrt p\) and all long \(G\), such an error is vastly beyond the weak-DPLS budget. Any group-expansion or lisse-sheaf argument must therefore treat the triangular nonwrapping interval directly, or prove a separate cancellation theorem for the singular starts. It cannot silently identify the polynomial full-field count with a cyclic product in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\).

### 2.2 Reflection creates genuine trivial constituents

The exact continuant reflection law is

\[
U_m(-r-m)=(-1)^mU_m(r).
\]

Hence, when \(m\) is odd, \(U_m\) has the forced center root \(-m/2\). Since \(N_h(r)=U_{h-1}(r+1)\), an even \(h\) gives the forced linear factor

\[
2r+h+1.
\]

The corresponding endpoints satisfy

\[
r+h=p-1-r,
\]

so this is precisely the reflected return. Any monodromy, irreducibility, or pair-correlation theorem must first split off this known component. The repository does this combinatorially through \(B_p^\circ\), which removes the whole reflection support of the selected four-window.

Adjacent coprimality of \(U_m,U_{m+1}\) does not supply the much stronger statement needed here: it neither proves geometric irreducibility of every deflated \(U_m\), nor linear disjointness of the covers for two independently growing lengths \(s,G\). The explicit \(h=2\) splitting law involving \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-51})\) is an additional warning that fixed thin arithmetic subfamilies must be classified before invoking a generic-monodromy heuristic.

## 3. What the repository already proves, and the exact remaining scale

The near resultant argument is rigorous because for fixed \(s,G\),

\[
R_{s,G}=\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf Z}
\bigl(N_s(X),N_G(X+s)\bigr)\ne0,
\]

using the disjoint complex root strips. If \(N_s,N_G(X+s)\) have a common root in \(\mathbf F_p\), the Sylvester matrix has nonzero nullity modulo \(p\), and the Smith-normal-form argument gives

\[
C_p(s,G)\le v_p(R_{s,G}),
\]

including possible degree drop modulo \(p\). Moreover,

\[
\log|R_{s,G}|\ll sG\log(2H).
\]

Summing this height over \(s\le H\) and \(G\le K\) gives the proved near bound

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}E_p^{\rm near}(K)\ll H^2K^2.
\]

At

\[
H=\lfloor\sqrt{2X}\rfloor,
\qquad K\asymp H/\sqrt{\log X},
\]

this is \(O(X^2/\log X)\).

The same method over the full far range would pay

\[
\sum_{s\le H}\sum_{G\le X}sG
\asymp H^2X^2\asymp X^3,
\]

before logarithms. This is one full power of \(X\) too large. For every *fixed* pair \((s,G)\), the nonzero resultant means that only finitely many primes can contribute; the obstruction is entirely the growing family \(s,G\to\infty\) with \(p\).

The deterministic bounds in `AVENUES.md` are:

- for the three-point majorant,
  \[
  A_p^{\rm nw}(K)\le 6p^{5/3},
  \qquad
  \sum_{X<p\le2X}A_p^{\rm nw}(K)
  \ll X^{8/3}/\log X;
  \]
- retaining both short four-windows,
  \[
  E_p^{\rm sep}\le \frac{15}{2}p\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor,
  \qquad
  \sum_{X<p\le2X}E_p^{\rm sep}
  \ll X^{5/2}/\log X.
  \]

The unconditional full orbit energy

\[
\mathcal F_p\le2\sqrt3\,p^{3/2}
\]

is a one-gap marginal statement. It does not decorrelate a later return after conditioning on a first short return. The project’s exact set-theoretic countermodels show that fixed-gap caps, reflection, and marginal energy cannot force the needed two-block saving.

The missing far estimate is therefore not a standard consequence of the known \(p^{3/2}\) energy theorem.

## 4. Primary-source finite-field large sieve: Kowalski’s Theorem 3.1

### Source and exact hypotheses

The most directly relevant finite-field large sieve I found is Emmanuel Kowalski, [“The large sieve, monodromy and zeta functions of curves,” *J. reine angew. Math.* 601 (2006), 29–69](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503714), Theorem 3.1.

Its setup consists of:

- a fixed smooth affine geometrically connected variety \(U/\mathbf F_q\) of positive dimension \(d\);
- a set \(\Lambda\) of auxiliary primes \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbf F_q\);
- lisse \(\mathbf F_\lambda\)-sheaves \(\widetilde{\mathcal F}_\ell\) of one fixed rank \(r\), with representations
  \[
  \rho_\ell:\pi_1(U)\to G_\ell\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbf F_\lambda);
  \]
- geometric monodromy groups \(G_\ell^g\);
- **linear disjointness**, meaning that for distinct \(\ell,\ell'\),
  \[
  \pi_1(\overline U)\longrightarrow G_\ell^g\times G_{\ell'}^g
  \]
  is surjective;
- a uniform bound \(\kappa\) for the twist stabilizers of the irreducible representations used in the sieve;
- group-growth bounds, denoted here by
  \[
  |G_\ell|\le c_1\ell^{\sigma},
  \qquad
  |G_\ell^\#|\le c_2\ell^{\tau};
  \]
- and one of the following:
  1. \(U\) is a smooth affine curve and the sheaves arise from a compatible system of integral \(\ell\)-adic sheaves, or
  2. \(|G_\ell^g|\) is prime to the characteristic of \(\mathbf F_q\).

The conclusion is the bilinear large-sieve inequality

\[
\sum_{\substack{\ell\in\Lambda\\\ell\le L}}
\sum_{\pi\ne1}^{*}
\left|
\sum_{u\in U(\mathbf F_q)}
\alpha(u)\operatorname{Tr}(\pi\circ\rho_\ell)(\operatorname{Fr}_u)
\right|^2
\le
\left(\kappa q^d+Cq^{d-1/2}L^A\right)
\sum_{u\in U(\mathbf F_q)}|\alpha(u)|^2.
\]

In the compatible-curve case,

\[
A=1+\sigma+\tau/2,
\]

and in the prime-to-characteristic case,

\[
A=1+5\sigma/2+\tau/2.
\]

The constant \(C\) depends on the fixed base and the fixed geometric compatible system; in the curve proof it includes the total Swan-conductor complexity of that system.

### Exact failure for the Apéry far family

This theorem does not apply to the desired sum for four independent reasons.

#### (a) It sieves auxiliary primes at one finite-field characteristic

The theorem fixes \(q\) and varies the auxiliary coefficient primes \(\ell\). The Ramanujan problem varies the **characteristic \(p\)** itself. One may apply the theorem separately at each \(p\), but then all constants must be uniform in \(p,s,G\). The theorem provides such uniformity only for base extension of one fixed compatible system, not for a new sheaf or new rational map for every \(G\).

#### (b) There is no fixed bounded-complexity system as \(G\to p\)

There are two natural encodings, and both grow:

1. A root-cover encoding has degree comparable to
   \[
   \deg N_G=3(G-1),
   \]
   so its permutation rank grows like \(G\).
2. A rank-two transfer encoding keeps matrix rank two, but its group-valued map has the exact singular locus
   \[
   x=-s-2,-s-3,\ldots,-s-G,
   \]
   containing \(G-1\) distinct points. Hence its puncture/Swan/Betti complexity grows at least linearly with \(G\).

Therefore \(U\), the sheaf, or both vary with \(G\), and the constant \(C\) in Kowalski’s theorem is not uniform.

Even granting the most favorable hypothetical root-cover monodromy \(G_\ell^g=\mathrm{SL}_r(\mathbf F_\ell)\) with \(r\asymp G\), Kowalski’s explicit exponents give

\[
\sigma=r^2,
\qquad \tau=r,
\qquad A=1+r^2+r/2.
\]

The useful range \(L^A\ll q^{1/2}\) then forces

\[
L\le q^{O(1/G^2)}.
\]

For \(G\asymp q\), this leaves no nontrivial sieve level.

#### (c) The monodromy hypotheses are not known and reflection supplies actual reducibility

For even positional gap \(h\), \(N_h\) has the forced center factor \(2x+h+1\). Thus the undeformed root cover is reducible. After deleting that factor, the project has no theorem proving, uniformly for all \(h<p\), geometric irreducibility, large monodromy, or pairwise linear disjointness of the \(h\)-families. Adjacent coprimality is far weaker than linear disjointness of geometric monodromy groups.

#### (d) The theorem averages over all \(U(\mathbf F_q)\), not the triangular nonwrapping interval

The actual range \(I_{p,s,G}\) is exactly what avoids the rank-one step. A full-field completion enters \(G-1\) singular starts. The large-sieve theorem has no mechanism that turns their contribution into an acceptable boundary term when \(G\asymp p\).

**Conclusion for Kowalski:** the theorem is not merely quantitatively weak; the fixed-system and linear-disjointness hypotheses are absent, and the natural complexity parameter grows with the long bridge.

## 5. Primary-source trace-function theorems

### 5.1 Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel: trace functions over prime arguments

The primary source is Étienne Fouvry, Emmanuel Kowalski, and Philippe Michel, [“Algebraic trace functions over the primes,” *Duke Math. J.* 163 (2014), 1683–1736](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6043), Theorem 1.5.

Its exact input is:

- a fixed prime modulus \(p\);
- an isotypic trace weight \(K:\mathbf F_p\to\mathbf C\), coming from a geometrically isotypic \(\ell\)-adic Fourier sheaf on \(\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf F_p}\), pointwise pure of weight zero;
- a nonexceptionality hypothesis excluding a Kummer sheaf times a linear Artin–Schreier sheaf;
- a smooth compactly supported weight \(V\) with controlled derivatives.

For any \(\eta<1/24\), it proves

\[
\sum_{q\ {m prime}}K(q)V(q/X)
\ll QX(1+p/X)^{1/6}p^{-\eta},
\]

with a corresponding unsmoothed estimate. The implicit constant is polynomial in the conductor of the sheaf. The theorem is nontrivial in its generic form when the conductor is bounded and \(X\gtrsim p^{3/4+\varepsilon}\).

This theorem fails to address the Apéry sum in the following exact senses.

1. It fixes the modulus \(p\) and sums over **prime arguments \(q\)**. Our outer prime is the modulus itself, while the inner variable is an interval of all residues \(x\).
2. There is no single fixed trace function \(K\). The function changes with \((s,G)\), and \(G\) is a recurrence length rather than an algebraic argument of a fixed \(K\).
3. Additive orthogonality would use
   \[
   \mathbf1_{\{f=g=0\}}
   =\frac1{p^2}\sum_{a,b\in\mathbf F_p}
   e_p(af+bg),
   \]
   with \(f=N_s(x)\), \(g=N_G(x+s)\). For a polynomial \(H\) of degree \(d<p\), Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel record that the Artin–Schreier sheaf \(e_p(H(x))\) has conductor \(d+2\). Here the natural degree is \(O(G)\), and \(G\asymp p\). Thus bounded conductor fails. When the degree reaches or exceeds \(p\), one must additionally reduce the polynomial function modulo \(X^p-X\) and classify possible Artin–Schreier exceptional forms; the theorem gives no uniform result for this growing family.
4. The zero indicator itself is a punctual/generic-rank-zero object, not the nonexceptional geometrically isotypic Fourier sheaf required by Theorem 1.5.

Accordingly, this theorem is about the wrong prime average and has the wrong complexity regime.

### 5.2 Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Sawin: bilinear trace functions

The strongest recent general bilinear result I found is Étienne Fouvry, Emmanuel Kowalski, Philippe Michel, and Will Sawin, [“Bilinear forms with trace functions,” arXiv:2511.09459v3 (11 March 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459), especially Theorems 1.1 and 1.3.

The simplified theorem assumes:

- a fixed prime \(q\);
- a middle-extension \(\ell\)-adic sheaf \(\mathcal F\) on \(\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf F_q}\), pure of weight zero;
- geometric monodromy acting irreducibly on its generic fiber;
- either the identity component of geometric monodromy is a simple algebraic group, or the finite monodromy case has the required quasisimple core;
- a kernel of the fixed monomial form
  \[
  K(m^bn^c),\qquad b,c\ne0;
  \]
- ranges with, in the simplified statement,
  \[
  q^\delta\le M,
  \qquad MN\ge q^{3/4+\delta}.
  \]

The more precise theorem uses a sheaf that is **gallant and light**: its geometric monodromy is irreducible with simple connected core or a quasisimple finite core, and it is mixed of integral weights \(\le0\) and generically pure of weight zero. It gives power saving in bilinear sums, with constants depending on the sheaf complexity. Remark 1.2 explicitly notes that this dependence is not known to be polynomial.

The Apéry application fails each decisive hypothesis:

1. \(\mathbf1_{\{N_G=0\}}\) is punctual and has generic rank zero, hence is not gallant.
2. The additive-character pieces \(e_p(aN_s+bN_G)\) have rank one and abelian geometric monodromy, not a simple or quasisimple core.
3. The kernel \(N_G(x+s)\) is not \(K(m^bn^c)\) for a fixed \(K\); changing \(G\) changes the ordered recurrence word itself.
4. The natural sheaf complexity grows like \(G\). The theorem’s non-polynomial dependence on complexity is therefore unusable even if one could force a formal encoding.
5. Its bilinear variables are algebraic arguments of one trace function. The pair \((x,G)\) here is fundamentally different: \(G\) is the number of noncommuting transfer factors.

So the 2026 theorem is not a hidden solution to the far bridge.

## 6. Primary-source expansion theorems

### 6.1 Bourgain–Gamburd expansion

Jean Bourgain and Alex Gamburd, [“Uniform expansion bounds for Cayley graphs of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\),” *Ann. of Math.* 167 (2008), 625–642](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2008/167-2/p07), prove:

- for a **fixed finite set** \(S\subset\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z)\), the Cayley graphs of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\) with the reductions of \(S\) form an expander family if and only if \(\langle S\rangle\) is non-elementary;
- equivalently in their Theorem 3, a fixed-cardinality symmetric generating set with girth at least \(\tau\log p\) yields a uniform spectral gap.

This controls convolution powers of an averaging measure on a fixed set, or equivalently averages over all random words in those generators. It does **not** control one prescribed word

\[
A_p(x+G-1)\cdots A_p(x+1)
\]

whose letter at time \(j\) is forced to be \(A_p(x+j)\).

The failure is not plausibly repaired by checking that the one-step matrices are too degenerate. For example,

\[
A(0)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A(1)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&8\\-1/8&117/8\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first has trace \(5\), hence infinite order, and

\[
\det\bigl(A(0)A(1)-A(1)A(0)\bigr)=\frac{18767}{64}\ne0,
\]

so the two matrices have no common eigenline. Both traces are nonzero and the matrices do not commute, excluding containment in a torus normalizer. Thus the basic generic non-elementarity diagnostic is present over \(\mathbf Q\).

But expansion of the group generated by possible letters says nothing about a deterministic ordering of those letters. A deterministic sequence may lie on an exceptional subvariety or exhibit exact cancellations even when its ambient Cayley graph is an excellent expander. The theorem requires averaging over choices; the Apéry block contains no such randomness. Moreover, a cyclic word encounters the rank-one \(M_{-1}\), which is not a group element at all.

### 6.2 Time-inhomogeneous random walks do not remove the randomness requirement

Elia Gorokhovsky, [“Time-inhomogeneous random walks on finite groups and cokernels of random integer block matrices,” *Combin. Probab. Comput.* 35 (2026), 329–355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11435), Theorem 1.1, is especially close in vocabulary but still not applicable.

Its hypotheses are:

- a finite group \(G\);
- independent random increments with probability measures \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n\);
- for each subgroup \(H\), the index set
  \[
  I_H=\{i:\langle\operatorname{supp}\mu_i\rangle=H\};
  \]
- a finite set \(\mathcal S\) of normal subgroups whose joint generated subgroup is \(G\);
- nonempty \(I_H\) for every \(H\in\mathcal S\);
- second singular values \(\sigma_i\) of convolution by \(\mu_i\) on the subgroup generated by its support.

Writing \(\nu_n=\mu_1*\cdots*\mu_n\) and \(\pi\) for uniform measure, it proves

\[
\|\nu_n-\pi\|_2
\le
\sum_{H\in\mathcal S}
\prod_{i\in I_H}\sigma_i.
\]

For the prescribed Apéry word, the literal step measure is

\[
\mu_i=\delta_{A_p(x+i)}.
\]

Convolution by a point mass is a permutation operator. Therefore every singular value is one, in particular

\[
\sigma_i=1.
\]

The convolution remains a point mass at the prescribed product and does not approach uniform measure. If instead \(x\) is chosen uniformly once, then all steps \(A_p(x+i)\) are perfectly correlated through that same \(x\); they are not independent increments with laws whose convolution is being studied. Replacing them by independent samples changes the mathematical problem.

Thus the exact quantitative hypothesis that fails is contraction: the theorem’s right-hand side has no factor below one. The singular step again prevents a full-cycle formulation in a finite group.

## 7. Why complete trace sums and square-root cancellation are still insufficient

For the full-field count, additive orthogonality gives the exact identity

\[
\widetilde C_p(s,G)
=\frac1{p^2}
\sum_{a,b\in\mathbf F_p}
\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}
 e_p\bigl(aN_s(x)+bN_G(x+s)\bigr).
\]

Suppose optimistically that every nonzero pair \((a,b)\) gave a nonexceptional polynomial phase of degree \(d<p\). Weil–Deligne would give a termwise bound

\[
\left|
\sum_x e_p(aN_s(x)+bN_G(x+s))
\right|
\ll d\sqrt p.
\]

After summing the \(p^2-1\) nonzero character pairs and dividing by \(p^2\), this yields only

\[
\widetilde C_p(s,G)\ll d\sqrt p\asymp G\sqrt p,
\]

which is much worse than the elementary root bound

\[
\widetilde C_p(s,G)\le\deg N_s=3(s-1)\ll\sqrt p.
\]

Thus even perfect termwise square-root cancellation in \(x\) does not address the problem. One needs cancellation **across the growing block parameters**, or a geometric theorem that treats the simultaneous two-equation incidence before applying triangle inequality over \((a,b)\).

Likewise, the project’s conjectural GAP–2D–SQRT controls one determinant equation jointly in the base point and a short gap \(h\le\sqrt p\). The far term asks for two return equations, with the second gap \(G\) up to \(p\). GAP–2D–SQRT is therefore a valuable one-gap target but not a theorem for this two-block incidence.

## 8. The genuinely new theorem that would suffice

A useful theorem should not treat \(N_G\) as an arbitrary polynomial of degree \(3G\). It must exploit that every \(N_G\) comes from one fixed cubic one-step matrix \(M_u\), while remaining uniform in the prescribed word length \(G\le p\).

### 8.1 Remove the exact reflection diagonals

For a triple

\[
x,\qquad y=x+s,\qquad z=x+s+G,
\]

reflection alone forces the second equality after the first return on two explicit loci:

\[
z=p-1-x
\iff 2x+s+G=p-1,
\]

and

\[
z=p-1-y
\iff 2x+2s+G=p-1.
\]

Define

\[
\mathcal R_{p,s,G}
=
\left\{x\in I_{p,s,G}:
2x+s+G=p-1
\ \text{or}\ 
2x+2s+G=p-1
\right\}.
\]

This set has cardinality at most two. Put

\[
C_p^\circ(s,G)
=
\#\left\{
 x\in I_{p,s,G}\setminus\mathcal R_{p,s,G}:
 N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0
\right\}.
\]

For a \(B_p^\circ\)-hit arising from a selected chain

\[
\gamma=(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3),
\qquad s=x_3-x_0,
\qquad G=z-x_3,
\]

one has \(z\notin\operatorname{Supp}(\bar\gamma)\). In particular,

\[
z\ne p-1-x_0,
\qquad z\ne p-1-x_3.
\]

Therefore \(x_0\notin\mathcal R_{p,s,G}\). The canonical map used in `far_bridge_incidence.tex` remains injective, so

\[
\boxed{
E_p^{\rm far}(K)
\le B_p^\circ(K)
\le
\sum_{s\le\sqrt p}
\sum_{G>K}C_p^\circ(s,G).
}
\]

The reflections of the two internal chain points are also removed by \(B_p^\circ\); retaining them in \(C_p^\circ\) only enlarges this upper bound.

### 8.2 A precise sufficient variance theorem

Let

\[
L_{p,s,G}^\circ
=|I_{p,s,G}\setminus\mathcal R_{p,s,G}|
\]

and take the mirror-random main term

\[
\mu_{p,s,G}
=\frac{L_{p,s,G}^\circ}{(p+1)^2}.
\]

A sufficient new theorem is the following.

> **Growing-block quenched two-return variance theorem.**  
> Let \(H=\lfloor\sqrt{2X}\rfloor\) and let \(K\le H\). Uniformly in \(K\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> \sum_{X<p\le2X}
> \sum_{2\le s\le\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor}
> \sum_{K<G\le p-1-s}
> \left|C_p^\circ(s,G)-\mu_{p,s,G}\right|^2
> \ll
> \frac{X^{3/2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
> }
> \]
> The implicit constant may depend on the fixed one-step polynomial matrix \(M_u\), but not on \(p,s,G\), not on the degree \(3G\), and not on the \(G-1\) punctures of the naively expanded product.

The uniform baseline can be replaced by an explicitly classified arithmetic main term, provided its total mass is \(O(X^{3/2+o(1)}/\log X)\) and the same variance bound holds after subtraction. This allows finite fixed-gap biases such as the \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-51})\) \(h=2\) splitting law without weakening the conclusion.

### 8.3 Proof that this theorem closes the far bridge

Let \(\mathcal T_X(K)\) be the set of triples \((p,s,G)\) in the displayed ranges. Then

\[
|\mathcal T_X(K)|
\ll
\frac{X}{\log X}\cdot\sqrt X\cdot X
=rac{X^{5/2}}{\log X}.
\]

For fixed \(p,s\),

\[
\sum_{G=K+1}^{p-1-s}L_{p,s,G}^\circ
\le
\sum_{G=K+1}^{p-1-s}(p-s-G)
\le \frac{p^2}{2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{(p,s,G)\in\mathcal T_X(K)}\mu_{p,s,G}
\ll
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\sqrt p
\ll
\frac{X^{3/2}}{\log X}.
\]

By Cauchy–Schwarz and the proposed variance theorem,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{(p,s,G)\in\mathcal T_X(K)}C_p^\circ(s,G)
&\le
\sum\mu_{p,s,G}
+|\mathcal T_X(K)|^{1/2}
\left(
\sum|C_p^\circ(s,G)-\mu_{p,s,G}|^2
\right)^{1/2}\\
&\ll
\frac{X^{3/2}}{\log X}
+\left(\frac{X^{5/2}}{\log X}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\frac{X^{3/2+o(1)}}{\log X}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\ll
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Together with the injective far-bridge reduction, this is exactly the weak-DPLS budget.

This is a genuine second-moment equidistribution statement over approximately \(X^{5/2}/\log X\) parameter cells. It is not a restatement of \(B_p^\circ\): it controls all nonreflection actual common-root counts \(C_p^\circ(s,G)\), including starts that do not form selected four-windows, and it specifies the random-scale variance \(X^{3/2}/\log X\).

### 8.4 Equivalent transfer-matrix form of the new input

Let \(\rho_p\) be the permutation representation of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\) on

\[
V_p=\ell^2(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)),
\]

and let

\[
e_a=\delta_a-\frac1{p+1}\mathbf1
\]

be the mean-zero point vector. For a regular block,

\[
\mathbf1_{\{T_p(r,h)0=\infty\}}
=rac1{p+1}
+\left\langle e_\infty,
\rho_p(T_p(r,h))e_0
\right\rangle.
\]

Consequently \(C_p^\circ(s,G)-\mu_{p,s,G}\) is an exact sum over \(x\in I_{p,s,G}\setminus\mathcal R_{p,s,G}\) of:

- two one-block mean-zero matrix coefficients, each with coefficient \(1/(p+1)\); and
- the product of the two mean-zero matrix coefficients for the adjacent ordered blocks.

A structural theorem implying the boxed variance estimate would therefore be a **large sieve for deterministic consecutive products**

\[
x\longmapsto
\bigl(T_p(x,s),T_p(x+s,G)\bigr)
\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)^2,
\]

on the nonwrapping triangular domain, tested against the nontrivial constituents of the projective permutation representation and averaged jointly over \(p,s,G\).

Such a theorem must have all of the following features:

1. **Deterministic ordering:** it must retain the exact word \(A_p(x+j)\), not replace the letters by independent samples.
2. **Growing length:** it must be uniform for \(G\le p\) and must not pay a generic conductor factor proportional to \(G\) at each term.
3. **Prime average:** it must average over the varying characteristic \(p\), not merely over points of one fixed finite field.
4. **Reflection projection:** it must identify and remove the explicit trivial/reflection constituents, including the two endpoint reflection surfaces above.
5. **Singular-step control:** it must work on the incomplete nonwrapping domain or prove a separate acceptable estimate for blocks crossing \(u=-1\).
6. **Rational-root sensitivity:** it must count \(\mathbf F_p\)-points, equivalently include the \(X^p-X\) factor, rather than charge for nonsplit common factors.
7. **Two-block rather than marginal mixing:** it must control the conditional intersection of two return sets. One-gap energy, GPRV, or expansion of the ambient generated group is not sufficient.

No primary-source theorem located in this audit simultaneously has these properties.

## 9. Terminal conclusion

The answer to the literature question is negative in the strong sense:

- Kowalski’s finite-field large sieve fails the fixed-compatible-system, bounded-complexity, and established-monodromy hypotheses.
- Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel fixes the modulus and sums over prime arguments; its bounded-conductor regime is incompatible with \(G\asymp p\).
- Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Sawin requires a fixed gallant/light trace sheaf and a monomial pullback kernel; the Apéry zero indicator and recurrence-length parameter fail those hypotheses, with non-polynomial complexity dependence in any case.
- Bourgain–Gamburd expands random words in a fixed generating set, not one prescribed nonautonomous word.
- Gorokhovsky’s time-inhomogeneous theorem still needs random increments with \(\sigma_i<1\); point-mass Apéry steps have \(\sigma_i=1\).
- Standard complete-sum square-root cancellation, even granted termwise, is weaker than the elementary short-polynomial degree bound and does not save the sum over \(G\).

Therefore the far bridge is not presently covered by an existing off-the-shelf theorem. The exact new result needed is a **word-length-uniform, prime-averaged two-block variance/large-sieve theorem for the Apéry transfer cocycle**, after reflection deflation and with direct control of the rank-one singular step. The boxed theorem in Section 8 is quantitatively sufficient and isolates the missing arithmetic input without replacing the actual common-root observable by nonsplit gcd degree or by a randomized surrogate process.
