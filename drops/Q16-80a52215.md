# Large prime factors of an individual Apéry number: exact literature status and the missing theorem

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}k^2,
\qquad
K_N(m)=\#\{p\text{ prime}:N<p\le 2N,\ p\mid b_m\},
\]

with \(N<m\le 2N\). I audited the current project state at main commit

```text
27f04f2156a76dbc4dd6e5e2422a4948c8334975
```

and the primary literature through August 16, 2026.

The conclusion is definitive:

1. **No known theorem for holonomic sequences, rational diagonals, or K3 periods implies**
   \[
   \max_{N<m\le2N}K_N(m)=N^{o(1)}.
   \]
2. The strongest theorem that applies directly to the Apéry numbers is Luca--Shparlinski's density-one **lower** bound
   \[
   \omega(b_n)>c_0\log\log\log n,
   \qquad
   P(b_n)>c_0(\log n\log\log n)^{1/2},
   \]
   for almost all \(n\). It gives no upper bound for the number of primes in \((N,2N]\), and it permits an arbitrary sparse exceptional set containing every worst-case index.
3. Stewart's large-prime-factor theorem applies to Lucas and Lehmer sequences, hence to constant-coefficient recurrences with a cyclotomic/Binet structure. It does **not** apply to the Apéry recurrence, whose coefficients depend on \(n\).
4. The Apéry supercongruence
   \[
   b_{mp^r}\equiv b_{mp^{r-1}}\pmod{p^{3r}}
   \]
   propagates or stabilizes one prime's valuation along the indices \(m,mp,mp^2,\ldots\). It gives no incompatibility between two distinct primes dividing the same fixed term \(b_m\).
5. There is no Apéry analogue of Siegel--Walfisz presently available. The relevant missing result is not an ordinary prime-number theorem in progressions; it is a **uniform cross-prime anti-concentration theorem for the moving residue sets**
   \[
   Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
   \]

The strongest no-go observation is elementary. The central binomial coefficients \(c_n=\binom{2n}{n}\) are P-recursive, are diagonals of a rational function, and satisfy the Ljunggren supercongruence

\[
\binom{2mp}{mp}\equiv\binom{2m}{m}\pmod{p^3}
\qquad(p\ge5),
\]

but

\[
\omega_{(n,2n]}\!\left(\binom{2n}{n}\right)
=\pi(2n)-\pi(n)
\sim\frac n{\log n}.
\]

Thus P-recursiveness, rational diagonality, and a mod-\(p^3\) supercongruence do not even improve the height bound in this shell. Any successful argument must use a feature specific to the *locations* of Apéry zeros across different characteristics.

## 1. Two normalization corrections

### 1.1 The height constant is \(3.52549\ldots\), not \(7.05\ldots\), per index

The exponential growth constant is

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^4,
\]

and

\[
\log\alpha=4\log(1+\sqrt2)
=3.525494348\ldots.
\]

Hence

\[
\log b_m=m\log\alpha+O(\log m).
\]

If \(N<m\le2N\), then

\[
\log b_m\le 7.050988697\ldots N+O(\log N).
\]

Since the product of the distinct shell primes dividing \(b_m\) is at least \(N^{K_N(m)}\),

\[
\boxed{
K_N(m)\le
\frac{\log b_m}{\log N}
\le(7.050989+o(1))\frac N{\log N}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus the numerical constant in the worst-case height bound is approximately \(7.05\), not \(14\).

### 1.2 The random-integer prediction is much smaller than \(N/\log N\)

For a uniformly random integer, the expected number of prime divisors in \((N,2N]\) is

\[
\sum_{N<p\le2N}\frac1p
=\frac{\log2}{\log N}+O\!\left(\frac1{(\log N)^2}\right).
\tag{1.2}
\]

It tends to zero. The scale \(N/\log N\) is a worst-case packing bound obtained from height; it is not the generic number of shell factors. The Apéry target asks for a uniform statement much closer to this sparse random behavior.

## 2. The literal integer-factor problem is exactly the zero-set incidence problem

The Gessel--Lucas congruence for Apéry numbers states that, for a prime \(p\),

\[
b_{ap+r}\equiv b_a b_r\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Take \(N<m\le2N\) and \(N<p\le2N\). Then \(m<2p\), so writing \(m=ap+r\) gives \(a\in\{0,1\}\). Since

\[
b_0=1,
\qquad
b_1=5,
\]

for every shell prime \(p>5\),

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_m
\iff b_{m\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p
\iff m\bmod p\in Z_p.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

More explicitly:

- if \(m<p\), then \(p\mid b_m\) exactly when \(m\in Z_p\);
- if \(p\le m<2p\), then
  \[
  b_m\equiv 5b_{m-p}\pmod p,
  \]
  so \(p\mid b_m\) exactly when \(m-p\in Z_p\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_N(m)=\sum_{N<p\le2N}1_{Z_p}(m\bmod p).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Treating \(b_m\) as a literal integer does not avoid the cross-prime problem. It is an exactly equivalent formulation of it.

## 3. A sharp no-go theorem for the three proposed formal structures

Consider

\[
c_n=\binom{2n}{n}.
\]

### 3.1 It is holonomic

It satisfies the first-order polynomial recurrence

\[
(n+1)c_{n+1}=2(2n+1)c_n.
\tag{3.1}
\]

In particular it is P-recursive and, if desired, may be viewed as satisfying a degenerate order-two recurrence with polynomial coefficients.

### 3.2 It is a rational diagonal

Indeed,

\[
\frac1{1-x-y}
=\sum_{j\ge0}(x+y)^j,
\]

and therefore

\[
[x^ny^n]\frac1{1-x-y}=\binom{2n}{n}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### 3.3 It has the same kind of mod-\(p^3\) stability

Ljunggren's congruence gives, for every prime \(p\ge5\),

\[
\binom{ap}{bp}\equiv\binom ab\pmod{p^3}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Taking \(a=2m\) and \(b=m\),

\[
\boxed{
c_{mp}\equiv c_m\pmod{p^3}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### 3.4 Nevertheless the shell is maximally populated

For every prime \(n<p\le2n\), Legendre's formula gives

\[
v_p\binom{2n}{n}
=\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{p}\right\rfloor
-2\left\lfloor\frac n p\right\rfloor
=1.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\omega_{(n,2n]}(c_n)
=\pi(2n)-\pi(n)
\sim\frac n{\log n}.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This example proves:

> No theorem depending only on P-recursiveness, rational diagonality, and a two-term mod-\(p^3\) supercongruence can imply a subpolynomial upper bound for the number of shell prime factors.

The Apéry proof must use additional arithmetic that excludes the central-binomial pattern. The relevant extra arithmetic cannot be merely “comes from geometry”; it must control how the zero positions \(Z_p\) correlate as \(p\) varies.

## 4. Part (a): what large-prime-factor theorems actually say

### 4.1 Stewart's theorem: strong, but for the wrong recurrence class

A Lucas sequence has a Binet/cyclotomic form built from fixed algebraic numbers \(\alpha,\beta\):

\[
u_n=\frac{\alpha^n-\beta^n}{\alpha-\beta}.
\]

Its coefficients satisfy a recurrence with **constant** coefficients. The factorization

\[
\alpha^n-\beta^n=\prod_{d\mid n}\Phi_d(\alpha,\beta)
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the fact that primitive divisors of \(\Phi_n(\alpha,\beta)\) lie in congruence classes \(\pm1\pmod n\) are essential.

Stewart proved that, under the standard nondegeneracy hypotheses, there is an effective constant \(C\) such that for \(n>C\),

\[
\boxed{
P\bigl(\Phi_n(\alpha,\beta)\bigr)
>
 n\exp\!\left(\frac{\log n}{104\log\log n}\right).}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently analogous bounds hold for Lucas and Lehmer terms. Bilu--Gun--Hong later gave a uniform explicit version of Stewart's theorem.

The Apéry recurrence is

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}
=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Its transfer matrix changes with \(n\). There is no known factorization analogous to (4.1), no strong-divisibility law \(\gcd(b_m,b_n)=b_{\gcd(m,n)}\), and no rank-of-apparition theory forcing primitive prime divisors into \(\pm1\pmod n\).

Thus Stewart's theorem does not apply. Calling both recurrences “second order” conceals the decisive difference between constant and polynomial coefficients.

The asymptotic

\[
b_n\asymp \alpha^n n^{-3/2}
\]

also rules out an eventual constant-coefficient binary recurrence: a nonzero constant-coefficient recurrence has asymptotics built from terms \(n^j\lambda^n\) with nonnegative integer \(j\), not \(n^{-3/2}\lambda^n\).

### 4.2 Luca's theorem for binary holonomic sequences

Florian Luca proved the following genuinely holonomic result. Let \((u_n)\) be a rational sequence satisfying

\[
f_0(n)u_{n+2}+f_1(n)u_{n+1}+f_2(n)u_n=0,
\qquad f_i\in\mathbb Q[X],
\tag{4.4}
\]

and suppose it is not eventually a binary constant-coefficient recurrence. Then there is a constant \(c>0\), depending on the sequence, such that the product of the numerators and denominators of the nonzero terms \(u_n\), \(n\le N\), has at least \(c\log N\) distinct prime divisors.

For the integer Apéry sequence this yields only

\[
\boxed{
\omega\!\left(\prod_{n\le N}b_n\right)
\ge c\log N.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is a lower bound for the union of prime divisors across the first \(N\) terms. It neither bounds \(\omega(b_m)\) from above nor localizes any prime to \((N,2N]\).

### 4.3 The theorem that applies directly to Apéry numbers

Luca and Shparlinski proved that there is an absolute \(c_0>0\) such that, for a set of positive integers \(n\) of asymptotic density one,

\[
\boxed{
\omega(b_n)>c_0\log\log\log n,}
\tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
P(b_n)>c_0(\log n\log\log n)^{1/2}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Their method uses both the order-two polynomial recurrence and Lucas-type congruences. This is the strongest directly relevant published theorem I located for the classical Apéry sequence.

It points in the opposite direction from Q16:

- (4.6) proves that almost all terms have **many**, not few, prime factors;
- (4.7) proves only a polylogarithmic lower bound for the largest prime factor, much smaller than the shell scale \(N\) when \(n\asymp N\);
- “density one” is insufficient for the project, which needs the maximum over every \(m\in(N,2N]\).

A density-zero exceptional set may contain one bad index in every dyadic interval, or even all indices realizing the maximum.

### 4.4 Even a Stewart-strength largest-factor theorem would not solve the shell problem

Suppose hypothetically that one could prove

\[
P(b_m)>m\exp\!\left(\frac{\log m}{104\log\log m}\right).
\]

This would eventually place one factor above \(2N\). Removing that factor from the height estimate changes (1.1) by only

\[
\frac{\log P(b_m)}{\log N}=1+o(\log N/\log\log N),
\]

whereas the existing bound is of order \(N/\log N\). One large prime does not prevent \(b_m\) from also containing \(N^{0.9}/\log N\), or even \(cN/\log N\), distinct primes near \(N\).

More generally, even a factor of size \(\exp(\delta N)\) would merely replace the constant \(7.05\) in (1.1) by \(7.05-\delta\). To force \(K_N(m)=N^{o(1)}\) by height alone, one would need almost all of \(\log b_m\) to be carried by factors outside the shell, with an error of size only \(N^{o(1)}\log N\). No known largest-prime-factor theorem has this form.

## 5. Part (b): what the Apéry supercongruences do and do not imply

For primes \(p\ge5\), the Apéry numbers satisfy the two-term tower

\[
\boxed{
b_{mp^r}\equiv b_{mp^{r-1}}\pmod{p^{3r}}}
\qquad(m,r\ge1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is proved in the classical work of Gessel and Beukers and has a multivariate rational-diagonal interpretation in Straub's work.

### 5.1 Exact valuation consequence

If integers \(A,B\) satisfy

\[
A\equiv B\pmod{p^L}
\quad\text{and}\quad
v_p(B)<L,
\]

then

\[
v_p(A)=v_p(B).
\]

Applying this to (5.1), once \(3r>v_p(b_{mp^{r-1}})\), the valuation at the next level is unchanged. In particular, if \(e=v_p(b_m)<3\), then

\[
\boxed{v_p(b_{mp})=e.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

More generally, the tower is a p-adic stability statement. It does not say that \(p^3\mid b_{mp}\) whenever \(p\mid b_m\). If \(v_p(b_m)=1\), then typically the congruence preserves valuation one.

### 5.2 It propagates one prime vertically, but does not couple primes horizontally

From (5.1),

\[
p\mid b_m\Longrightarrow p\mid b_{mp^r}.
\]

This concerns the same prime \(p\) at much larger indices. It gives no implication of the form

\[
p,q\mid b_m\Longrightarrow \text{a restrictive relation between }p\text{ and }q.
\]

The desired bound for \(K_N(m)\) is precisely a statement about simultaneous divisibility by many **different** primes at one fixed index. The supercongruence contains no such cross-prime term.

### 5.3 Bounded valuations cannot upper-bound the number of distinct factors

An upper bound such as

\[
v_p(b_m)\le C
\]

would control prime powers but not the number of distinct primes. Indeed, all shell primes in (3.6) divide \(\binom{2n}{n}\) with valuation exactly one. Small valuations are perfectly compatible with \(\asymp n/\log n\) distinct shell factors.

At the height level, knowing \(v_p(b_m)\le C\) can only remove overcounting. It does not improve

\[
N^{K_N(m)}\le b_m.
\]

A lower bound \(v_p(b_m)\ge C\) for every shell divisor would improve the constant by a factor \(C\), but still leave \(O(N/\log N)\), not \(N^{o(1)}\). The actual supercongruence does not supply such a lower bound anyway.

### 5.4 The Lucas congruence, not the supercongruence, is the relevant local input

For the fixed-index shell problem, the useful congruence is (2.1), because it converts \(p\mid b_m\) into membership in \(Z_p\). That reduction is already fully exploited in the current project. The remaining issue is where the zero sets sit relative to one another, not the depth of one p-adic congruence tower.

## 6. What the diagonal and K3 structures currently provide

### 6.1 The diagonal structure is valuable locally

The rational-diagonal realization explains:

- integrality;
- Lucas and Cartier congruences;
- algebraicity of the generating function modulo \(p\);
- the two-term supercongruence tower;
- finite-field descriptions of the first block \((b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1})\).

These are genuine inputs to the repository's local theory of

\[
Z_p=\{r:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

The current project proves the all-prime bound

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

That is a nontrivial consequence of the Apéry recurrence and its gap polynomials.

### 6.2 A local zero-count bound gives only an average over indices

Let \(I_N=(N,2N]\cap\mathbb Z\). Since \(|I_N|=N<p\) for every shell prime, each residue in \(Z_p\) can occur at most once among the integers of \(I_N\). Hence

\[
\sum_{m\in I_N}K_N(m)
\le\sum_{N<p\le2N}|Z_p|.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Using (6.1) and the prime number theorem gives

\[
\sum_{m\in I_N}K_N(m)
\ll\frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N},
\tag{6.3}
\]

so the average is at most

\[
\frac1N\sum_{m\in I_N}K_N(m)
\ll\frac{N^{2/3}}{\log N}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is far from the empirical sparse scale, and it gives no useful maximum bound. One row can support almost all incidences allowed by (6.2). The anchored-star examples in the current project formalize exactly this local-to-global failure.

### 6.3 The K3 interpretation does not produce an integer factorization theorem

The Apéry generating function is a period of a K3 family, and its Picard--Fuchs equation supplies the recurrence. But no known theorem says that coefficients of a motivic period have few prime divisors in a medium interval.

There are two precise obstructions to a direct Chebotarev argument:

1. **The coefficient index is not a fixed geometric parameter.** The map
   \[
   m\longmapsto b_m
   \]
   is a coefficient sequence. It is not currently realized as the trace function of one fixed bounded-conductor sheaf on an \(m\)-line.
2. **The modulus is the residue characteristic itself.** The condition
   \[
   b_m\equiv0\pmod p
   \]
   is a defining-characteristic divisibility condition with \(p\) varying. Standard \(\ell\)-adic Chebotarev fixes an auxiliary \(\ell\ne p\) and controls fixed conjugacy-class conditions. It does not automatically control reduction of a moving integral period modulo its own characteristic.

Thus the K3 structure supplies local identities and plausible monodromy heuristics, but not the required cross-characteristic anti-concentration.

## 7. Part (c): there is no applicable Siegel--Walfisz analogue

### 7.1 Why the literal formulation is not a Siegel--Walfisz problem

For fixed \(m\), the set

\[
\{p:p\mid b_m\}
\]

is finite. Asking how those finitely many primes distribute among arithmetic progressions is not analogous to letting all primes tend to infinity in a fixed progression: once \(x>P(b_m)\), the counting function stops changing.

One must let \(m\) vary together with the prime range. Then the divisibility condition itself changes with \(m\), so there is no fixed Dirichlet \(L\)-function whose zero-free region could yield a Siegel--Walfisz theorem.

### 7.2 What the useful analogue would actually be

For squarefree \(d\) supported on shell primes, define

\[
A_d(I_N)
=
\#\{m\in I_N:m\bmod p\in Z_p\text{ for every }p\mid d\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The independent model predicts

\[
A_d(I_N)
\approx
|I_N|\prod_{p\mid d}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

A sieve-compatible theorem would be

\[
A_d(I_N)
=|I_N|\prod_{p\mid d}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}+R_d
\tag{7.3}
\]

with strong aggregate control of \(R_d\) for products of several shell primes.

This is closer to a Barban--Davenport--Halberstam or Linnik-dispersion theorem for **moving CRT cylinders** than to Siegel--Walfisz. It is also more difficult than classical small-modulus distribution: already for two shell primes,

\[
pq>N^2\gg |I_N|,
\]

so the modulus is much longer than the interval. Each selected CRT class contributes zero or one integer, and the problem is whether those zero-one landing errors cancel across many prime tuples.

### 7.3 Why constant-coefficient recurrence results do not transfer

For a Lucas sequence, one has a rank of apparition \(z(p)\) such that, up to standard exceptional primes,

\[
p\mid u_m\iff z(p)\mid m.
\]

This converts divisibility into group order and splitting conditions in fixed algebraic extensions. Chebotarev and prime-distribution tools can then study various averages over \(p\).

For Apéry numbers, the residue condition is

\[
m\bmod p\in Z_p,
\]

where the set \(Z_p\) itself changes with \(p\). No fixed order \(z(p)\) or divisibility lattice replaces it. The zero condition is not presently a fixed Chebotarev class in a tower with controlled discriminant.

### 7.4 Exact status of the desired theorem

No published theorem I located proves any of the following uniformly for the classical Apéry sequence:

\[
\max_{N<m\le2N}K_N(m)=N^{o(1)},
\tag{7.4}
\]

\[
\#\{m\in I_N:K_N(m)\ge T\}
\ll N^{1+o(1)}e^{-cT\log T},
\tag{7.5}
\]

or squarefree-modulus distribution (7.3) at a level sufficient for a growing number of shell primes.

The repository's current pointwise atom-tail target is therefore not a routine application of a named theorem. It is the genuinely new arithmetic statement.

## 8. Why almost-all and moment theorems do not automatically prove the maximum

Suppose one proves, for each fixed \(r\),

\[
\sum_{m\in I_N}(K_N(m))_r
\ll N^{1+o(1)}\lambda_N^r,
\tag{8.1}
\]

where \(\lambda_N\) is the shell mean. Then

\[
\#\{m:K_N(m)\ge T\}
\le
\frac{N^{1+o(1)}\lambda_N^r}{(T)_r}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For fixed \(r\), this controls density but not necessarily eliminates every exceptional row. To prove the maximum by moments one needs estimates uniform for \(r\) growing with \(N\), or a direct pointwise anti-concentration theorem.

This explains why the direct Luca--Shparlinski density-one result is structurally too weak even before comparing its numerical exponents.

## 9. Direct answers to the three sub-questions

### (a) Are there large-prime-factor results for holonomic sequences?

Yes, but the distinction between recurrence classes is decisive.

- **Stewart:** a powerful bound
  \[
  P(\Phi_n(\alpha,\beta))
  >n\exp(\log n/(104\log\log n))
  \]
  for Lucas/Lehmer sequences. It relies on fixed characteristic roots and cyclotomic factorization and does not apply to Apéry numbers.
- **Luca:** for a genuinely binary holonomic sequence, at least \(c\log N\) distinct primes occur somewhere among the first \(N\) nonzero terms. This applies in spirit to Apéry but is an aggregate lower bound.
- **Luca--Shparlinski:** specifically for Apéry numbers, almost-all lower bounds (4.6)--(4.7). These are the strongest directly applicable published results located.

No theorem in this literature gives a uniform upper bound on \(\omega_{(N,2N]}(b_m)\).

### (b) Can \(b_{mp}\equiv b_m\pmod{p^3}\) bound the number of prime factors?

No. It controls one prime vertically along indices multiplied by that prime. It neither forces high multiplicity nor relates distinct primes. The exact valuation consequence is stability, as in (5.2). The central-binomial example proves that even the same mod-\(p^3\) pattern can coexist with \(\asymp N/\log N\) shell factors.

The Lucas congruence modulo \(p\) is relevant, but it merely converts the question into the current zero-set incidence formulation (2.3).

### (c) Is there a Siegel--Walfisz analogue?

Not one that applies to Apéry factors. For constant-coefficient divisibility sequences, ranks of apparition and Chebotarev provide distribution results for related prime sets. The Apéry condition involves a moving zero set in the defining characteristic and is not encoded by a fixed Dirichlet or Artin \(L\)-function.

The needed replacement is the squarefree-modulus/CRT dispersion theorem (7.3), or directly the pointwise atom-tail bound (7.4). Neither is currently known.

---

## 10. Final mathematical diagnosis

The integer-factor viewpoint is useful because it exposes the exact scale, but it does not create an additional theorem. The product of shell primes gives only

\[
N^{K_N(m)}\mid b_m,
\]

which is exactly the height bound. To improve it, one needs one of two genuinely new kinds of information:

1. a multiplicative decomposition of \(b_m\) showing that almost all of its logarithmic height lies outside \((N,2N]\); or
2. a cross-prime theorem showing that the congruences
   \[
   m\bmod p\in Z_p
   \]
   cannot hold for many shell primes simultaneously.

No cyclotomic decomposition of the first kind is known for Apéry numbers. The current repository is correctly pursuing the second kind.

Therefore the precise answer to Q16 is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The desired shell-factor bound is not a consequence of any known}
\\
\text{large-prime-factor theorem, supercongruence, diagonal theorem, or K3 theorem.}
}
\]

The missing input remains Apéry-specific cross-prime anti-concentration. Proving it would be a new theorem, not an application of Stewart or Siegel--Walfisz.
