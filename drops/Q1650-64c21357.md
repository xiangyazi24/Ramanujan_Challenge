# P3.2 punctured Hermite–WZ attack

## Verdict

There is a clean exact reduction, but it stops one layer before a genuine proper-hypergeometric WZ certificate.  The first unavoidable interior object is a **reciprocal Apéry moment**.  More precisely, after the `ell`-sum and the exact barycentric elimination of `Z'`, the remaining tail contains both

\[
\frac{1}{A_j}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{1}{A_j^2}
\sum_{k=1}^j F_{j,k}^2
\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\frac1{(j-\ell)(j+\ell+1)},
\qquad
A_j:=P(\lambda_j).
\]

Those reciprocals are the first point where the requested `(j,k,ell)` summand leaves the classical WZ/proper-hypergeometric class.  I do **not** find an exact rational WZ certificate reducing the whole quantity to endpoints only.  What I can give is the exact partial telescoper and the residual moment that any successful continuation has to kill.

This conclusion preserves the true shell `deg P = 2m`: nowhere below is `P` replaced by its degree-`<=m` grid interpolant or reduced modulo `G`.  Lagrange interpolation is applied only to `Z`, while `P'(lambda_j)` is the derivative of the actual canonical degree-`2m` polynomial and is inserted using the formula in the question.

## 1. Put the outer Hermite weight in closed form

Write

\[
x_j:=\lambda_j=j(j+1),\qquad
J:=\{r+1,\ldots,m-1\},\qquad
A_j:=P(x_j).
\]

The quadratic-grid difference is

\[
x_j-x_i=(j-i)(j+i+1).
\]

Hence

\[
G'(x_j)=(-1)^{m-j}\frac{(m-j)!(m+j+1)!}{2j+1}.
\]

For `j != r`,

\[
H'(x_j)=
(-1)^{m-j}
\frac{(m-j)!(m+j+1)!}
{(2j+1)(j-r)(j+r+1)}.
\]

For `j in J`,

\[
T'(x_j)=
(-1)^{m-1-j}
\frac{(j-r-1)!(m-1-j)!(j+m)!}
{(j+r+1)!(2j+1)}.
\]

The two signs multiply to `-1`, so the natural positive outer weight is

\[
W_j:=-\frac1{H'(x_j)T'(x_j)}
=
\frac{(2j+1)^2(j-r)(j+r+1)(j+r+1)!}
{(m-j)!(m+j+1)!(j-r-1)!(m-1-j)!(j+m)!}.
\tag{1}
\]

Thus the punctured formula is exactly

\[
\widehat q
=\sum_{j\in J}W_j
\left(Z'(x_j)+\frac{P'(x_j)}{A_j^2}\right).
\tag{2}
\]

No degree reduction of `P` occurs in (1)–(2).

## 2. The `ell` direction does telescope to an explicit harmonic boundary

Set

\[
F_{j,k}=\binom jk\binom{j+k}k,
\qquad
h_{j,k}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}
\frac1{(j-\ell)(j+\ell+1)}.
\]

The elementary partial fraction

\[
\frac1{(j-\ell)(j+\ell+1)}
=\frac1{2j+1}
\left(\frac1{j-\ell}+\frac1{j+\ell+1}\right)
\]

gives the exact finite boundary

\[
h_{j,k}=\frac{\mathsf H_{j+k}-\mathsf H_{j-k}}{2j+1},
\tag{3}
\]

where `mathsf H_n` denotes the ordinary harmonic number.  Therefore, with

\[
D_j:=\sum_{k=1}^jF_{j,k}^2h_{j,k},
\]

the supplied derivative formula is

\[
P'(x_j)=2D_j
=\frac{2}{2j+1}
\sum_{k=1}^jF_{j,k}^2
\bigl(\mathsf H_{j+k}-\mathsf H_{j-k}\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

So `ell` is not the obstruction.

## 3. Exact elimination of `Z'` by the `T` barycentric certificate

This is the useful exact telescoping step.

For `j != r`, define

\[
s_H(j):=\sum_{\substack{0\le i\le m\\i\ne r,j}}
\frac1{x_j-x_i}
=\frac{H''(x_j)}{2H'(x_j)}.
\]

For `j in J`, define

\[
s_T(j):=\sum_{\substack{i\in J\\i\ne j}}
\frac1{x_j-x_i}
=\frac{T''(x_j)}{2T'(x_j)}.
\]

Using only the node values defining `Z`, Lagrange differentiation gives

\[
Z'(x_j)
=\frac{s_H(j)}{A_j}
+H'(x_j)
\sum_{\substack{i\ne r,j}}
\frac{1}{A_iH'(x_i)(x_j-x_i)}.
\tag{5}
\]

The exact `T` partial-fraction certificate is

\[
\frac1{T(Y)}
=\sum_{j\in J}\frac1{T'(x_j)(Y-x_j)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\sum_{j\in J}\frac1{T'(x_j)(x_j-x_i)}
=-\frac1{T(x_i)}
\qquad(i\notin J),
\tag{7}
\]

while at an interior node the regular part is

\[
\sum_{\substack{j\in J\\j\ne i}}
\frac1{T'(x_j)(x_j-x_i)}
=\frac{T''(x_i)}{2T'(x_i)^2}
=\frac{s_T(i)}{T'(x_i)}.
\tag{8}
\]

Substitute (5) into (2), swap the two finite sums, and apply (7)–(8).  The result is the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\widehat q
=
\sum_{i\in\{0,\ldots,r-1,m\}}
\frac1{A_iH'(x_i)T(x_i)}
+
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}W_j
\left(
\frac{s_H(j)+s_T(j)}{A_j}
+\frac{2D_j}{A_j^2}
\right).
}
\tag{9}
\]

This is the smallest exact reduction I get from the punctured formula without invoking the already-known inverse-polynomial route.

For implementation, the two logarithmic-derivative weights can themselves be written explicitly.  For `j in J`,

\[
s_H(j)
=
\frac{\mathsf H_{j+m+1}-\mathsf H_{m-j}}{2j+1}
-\frac1{(2j+1)^2}
-\frac1{(j-r)(j+r+1)},
\tag{10}
\]

and

\[
s_T(j)
=
\frac{
\mathsf H_{j-r-1}-\mathsf H_{m-1-j}
-\mathsf H_{j+r+1}+\mathsf H_{j+m}}
{2j+1}
-\frac1{(2j+1)^2}.
\tag{11}
\]

All denominators in (10)–(11) are units on the stated tail for `p=2m+1`.

## 4. The first unavoidable interior moment

Split the two non-boundary blocks in (9) as

\[
\mathcal L_{m,r}
:=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac1{A_iH'(x_i)T(x_i)},
\tag{12}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M_{m,r}
:=\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}W_j
\left[
\frac{s_H(j)+s_T(j)}{A_j}
+\frac{2}{A_j^2}
\sum_{k=1}^jF_{j,k}^2
\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}
\frac1{(j-\ell)(j+\ell+1)}
\right].
}
\tag{13}
\]

Then the requested corrected scalar has the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\widehat q-\frac{2}{P'(a)T(a)}
=
\mathcal L_{m,r}+\mathcal M_{m,r}
+\frac1{A_mH'(x_m)T(x_m)}
-\frac{2}{P'(a)T(a)}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

The last two terms in (14) are honest explicit endpoint/puncture boundaries.  What remains is `L + M`.  In particular, (13) is the first unavoidable tail moment.

## 5. Why ordinary WZ stops exactly at (13)

Before summing `ell`, the derivative contribution has triply indexed summand

\[
S(j,k,\ell)
:=
2W_j\frac{F_{j,k}^2}{A_j^2}
\frac1{(j-\ell)(j+\ell+1)}.
\tag{15}
\]

Every factor in (15) except `A_j^{-2}` is proper hypergeometric.  Indeed

\[
\frac{S(j+1,k,\ell)}{S(j,k,\ell)}
=R(j,k,\ell;m,r)
\left(\frac{A_j}{A_{j+1}}\right)^2,
\tag{16}
\]

where the completely rational part is

\[
\begin{aligned}
R={}&
\frac{(2j+3)^2}{(2j+1)^2}
\frac{j-r+1}{(j-r)^2}
\frac{(j+r+2)^2}{j+r+1}
\frac{(m-j)(m-j-1)}{(m+j+2)(m+j+1)}\\
&\times
\left(\frac{j+k+1}{j-k+1}\right)^2
\frac{(j-\ell)(j+\ell+1)}
{(j+1-\ell)(j+\ell+2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

Thus the direct Zeilberger/Gosper criterion fails in the `j` direction precisely through

\[
(A_j/A_{j+1})^2.
\]

Opening `A_j` as its defining `k`-sum does not help: the reciprocal of a finite hypergeometric sum is not another fixed-dimensional hypergeometric sum.  Over `F_p` one could formally write `A_j^{-1}=A_j^{p-2}`, but that creates a number of summation factors growing with `p`, so it is not a uniform WZ certificate and is useless for the desired theorem.

The exact elimination of `Z'` above confirms that this is not just a bad choice of representation: it replaces the `Z'` block by additional `A_i^{-1}` moments, while the `D_j/A_j^2` moment remains in (13).  Therefore a successful continuation needs a genuinely new **reciprocal-Apéry telescoper**, not another rational certificate for the raw `(j,k,ell)` kernel.

A precise next target would be a local identity of the schematic form

\[
\Delta_j R_j
=
W_j\left(
\frac{s_H(j)+s_T(j)}{A_j}
+\frac{2D_j}{A_j^2}
\right)
+\text{a compensating first-order reciprocal term},
\tag{18}
\]

where `R_j` is built from a bounded amount of Apéry recurrence data (for example `A_{j-1},A_j,A_{j+1}` and one companion quantity).  Equation (18), if it exists, is the missing lemma.  It is nonlinear in the Apéry solution and lies outside ordinary WZ.

I am **not** claiming a theorem that no broader holonomic/nonlinear telescoper can exist; the exact claim is narrower: there is no direct proper-hypergeometric `(j,k,ell)` WZ continuation past the reciprocal moments exposed in (12)–(13).

## 6. Exact `p=17, r=3` control

Here `m=8`, `a=lambda_3=12`, and

\[
A_3=1445\equiv0\pmod{17}.
\]

The tail values are

\[
(A_4,A_5,A_6,A_7)=(4,13,8,8),
\]

so every denominator in the punctured tail is a unit.

From the supplied derivative formula,

\[
D_3=317,
\qquad
P'(a)=2D_3=634\equiv5\pmod{17}.
\]

Also

\[
T(a)=\prod_{j=4}^7(12-j(j+1))
\equiv 3\pmod{17}.
\]

Hence the puncture correction is

\[
-\frac{2}{P'(a)T(a)}
=-\frac2{5\cdot3}
\equiv1\pmod{17}.
\tag{19}
\]

More importantly, (9) independently reproduces the supplied `qhat=0` control.  The exact reduced data are

```text
p = 17, m = 8, r = 3

tail j          4   5   6   7
A_j             4  13   8   8
P'(lambda_j)    6   7   7   7
W_j             8   4   6  16

                11   5   2   4

qhat = 12 + 5 = 0 mod 17

```

So this example is a sharp death test for a false “all boundaries vanish” certificate: `qhat` itself vanishes, but the corrected target does not.

## 7. `p=7` weighted zero controls

For `p=7`, `m=3`, the canonical half-grid Apéry values are

\[
(A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3)=(1,5,3,3)\pmod7.
\]

Thus **there is no canonical punctured Apéry zero at `p=7`**.  A `p=7` check is therefore a weight/interpolation control only, not an instance of the unique-simple-zero hypothesis.  This distinction matters.

The barycentric signs in (7)–(8) pass the small weighted-zero controls.  For example, take the synthetic deletion `r=0`; the remaining `H` roots are

\[
(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(2,6,5)\pmod7,
\]

with

\[
\left(\frac1{H'(x_1)},\frac1{H'(x_2)},\frac1{H'(x_3)}\right)
=(3,2,2).
\]

Then

\[
3+2+2=0,
\qquad
2\cdot3+6\cdot2+5\cdot2=0
\pmod7,
\]

exactly the degree-0 and degree-1 barycentric zero moments for a cubic.  The corresponding tail polynomial `T=(Y-2)(Y-6)` has weights

\[
\left(\frac1{T'(2)},\frac1{T'(6)}\right)=(5,2),
\]

so

\[
5+2=0\pmod7.
\]

These checks fix the sign in (7); reversing that sign breaks the `p=7` controls immediately.

## Bottom line

The useful exact telescoping packet is:

1. `ell` collapses by (3);
2. `Z'` collapses by the barycentric certificate (6)–(8);
3. the corrected scalar becomes (14);
4. the first genuinely non-boundary object is the reciprocal-Apéry moment (12)–(13).

So I do **not** have a valid pure `(j,k,ell)` WZ certificate to explicit boundaries.  The next theorem must telescope reciprocal Apéry data using the second-order Apéry recurrence (or an equivalent companion/Casoratian mechanism).  Any claimed certificate that stays inside rational WZ certificates while ignoring the factors `A_j^{-1}` and `A_j^{-2}` is structurally incomplete, and the `p=17,r=3` test detects the most dangerous false cancellation.
