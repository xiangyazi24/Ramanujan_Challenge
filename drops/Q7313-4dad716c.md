# Quotient-six C4: centered nonzero-indicator and signed-Fourier audit

## Verdict

The minus-first nondivisibility cannot be turned into a power saving by centering, inclusion-exclusion, or a signed Fourier/large-sieve estimate while retaining the positive marked-incidence consumer.

There are two exact reasons.

1. In the common geometric window, after reflecting the minus secondary zero, the lower characteristic sees the three cyclic positions

   ```text
   x-13d,  x-6d,  x                         (mod p),
   ```

   where `x=a_+=t+12d-p`.  Raw minus is the left edge, raw plus is the right edge, and assigned plus is the right edge with the left endpoint dark:

   ```text
   raw minus:      z_p(x-13d) z_p(x-6d),
   raw plus:       z_p(x-6d)  z_p(x),
   assigned plus:  z_p(x-6d)  z_p(x) [1-z_p(x-13d)].
   ```

   Hence the sign-assigned sum is the Boolean union

   ```text
   z_p(x-6d) [ z_p(x-13d) OR z_p(x) ].
   ```

   Every raw-plus incidence is still counted exactly once: if the third zero is dark it is counted on plus, and if the third zero is bright it is counted on minus.  The negative overlap term in assigned plus is canceled pointwise by the positive overlap term in minus.

2. Let

   ```text
   rho_p = |Z_p|/p,
   f_p   = z_p-rho_p.
   ```

   For the raw-plus coefficient `H_{p,d}(x)>=0`, the exact centered identity is

   ```text
   A_+^com(p,d)
     = (1-rho_p) M_{p,d}
       - sum_x H_{p,d}(x) f_p(x-13d),

   M_{p,d}=sum_x H_{p,d}(x).
   ```

   Since `|Z_p|<<p^(2/3+o(1))`, one has `rho_p<<p^(-1/3+o(1))`.  Thus the zero Fourier mode of the **nonzero** indicator is

   ```text
   1-rho_p = 1-o(1),
   ```

   not a small density.  Even perfect cancellation of every nonzero Fourier mode leaves `(1-o(1))M_{p,d}`.  To make assigned plus power-small one would need a one-sided correlation saying that almost every raw-plus occurrence also has the minus secondary zero.  A large sieve gives an absolute upper bound for a signed correlation, not that one-sided lower bound.

An explicit affine-grid zero-set model below satisfies the exact P/M common-window formulas, primality and range boxes, reflection, no consecutive zeros, the project pointwise and moment envelopes, ordinary prime-density losses, and characteristic-zero radical-height capacity.  It has one Boolean occurrence per `(p,q,t)`, has

```text
p does not divide b_(d-1-t)
```

on every raw-plus row, and carries weighted mass

```text
T^(11/5-o(1)),
```

more precisely `T^(11/5)(log T)^(-A)` for a fixed arbitrarily large `A`.  This remains above `T^2/log T` by a fixed power.  It is an **interface-level zero-set model**, not an actual construction of the Apéry sequence.  It proves that the desired saving is not derivable from the named positive moments, Parseval/large-sieve norms, and sign-assignment algebra.

The smallest useful next input is still a one-sign marked reuse/cross-lower-prime theorem, for example

```text
F_mark(T)
 := sum_(q,t:q prime, q|b_t)
      [K_-(q,t)(K_-(q,t)-1)
       +K_+(q,t)(K_+(q,t)-1)]
 = o_J(T^(12/5)(log T)^(-16/5)).
```

A theorem only asserting that raw plus is usually covered by raw minus would not close the sign-summed gate: it merely moves those rows from plus to minus.

No finite computation, independence assumption about Apéry zeros, or authoritative source edit is used.

## 1. Exact common-window three-point chain

Put

```text
q=p+d,
r=t+6d,
a_-=d-1-t,
a_+=t+12d-p.
```

The common geometric window is

```text
0<=t<=d-1,
t+6d<=p-1,
t+12d-p>=0.
```

Equivalently,

```text
max(0,p-12d) <= t <= min(d-1,p-6d-1).       (1.1)
```

It is nonempty exactly when

```text
6d+1 <= p <= 13d-1.                           (1.2)
```

Let

```text
x:=a_+=t+12d-p.
```

Then `0<=x<p` and

```text
t=x+p-12d,
r=x+p-6d,
a_-^*:=p-1-a_-=x+2p-13d.                    (1.3)
```

Therefore, modulo `p`,

```text
r      == x-6d,
a_-^*  == x-13d.                              (1.4)
```

By reflection,

```text
z_p(a_-)=z_p(a_-^*)=z_p(x-13d mod p).         (1.5)
```

Thus the two sign pairs share their middle zero and form the exact cyclic chain

```text
x-13d  -- gap 7d -->  x-6d  -- gap 6d -->  x  (mod p).
```

This is not a heuristic picture.  It is a change of variables in the literal formulas.

Let `G_{p,d}(x)` be the product of all deterministic masks after the change of variables, including

```text
q=p+d prime,
t=x+p-12d,
q|b_t,
the common window,
the T<6q+t<=2T condition,
and every retained chart/range mask.
```

Define

```text
R_{p,d}(x)=G_{p,d}(x) z_p(x-6d),
U_{p,d}(x)=z_p(x),
V_{p,d}(x)=z_p(x-13d).
```

Then, inside the common window,

```text
M_raw(p,d,x) = R_{p,d}(x)V_{p,d}(x),
P_raw(p,d,x) = R_{p,d}(x)U_{p,d}(x),
P_ass(p,d,x) = R_{p,d}(x)U_{p,d}(x)(1-V_{p,d}(x)).       (1.6)
```

The sign-assigned contribution is therefore

```text
M_raw+P_ass
 =R[V+U(1-V)]
 =R[U+V-UV]
 =R[U OR V].                                             (1.7)
```

Equation `(1.7)` is the complete answer to the positivity question at one state-prime occurrence.  The complement does not create a negative contribution to the consumer.

### 1.1 Exact cancellation of the apparent negative term

Separate minus into overlap and minus-only:

```text
M_raw=RUV+RV(1-U).
```

Then

```text
P_ass+RUV=RU.                                            (1.8)
```

The term `-RUV` produced by expanding assigned plus is canceled pointwise by the same `+RUV` in minus.  Consequently

```text
M_raw+P_ass >= P_raw,
M_raw+P_ass >= M_raw.                                    (1.9)
```

No summation, Cauchy inequality, or Fourier transform is involved.

If some nonzero Boolean mask is sign-specific, the statement becomes the set identity

```text
assigned total = raw minus UNION raw plus,
```

and `(1.9)` remains true on each raw sign support.  A tie-break chooses the owner of an overlap; it does not delete the union element.

## 2. Inclusion-exclusion gives the wrong one-sided information

Fix `(p,d)` and put

```text
H_{p,d}(x):=R_{p,d}(x)U_{p,d}(x),
M_{p,d}:=sum_x H_{p,d}(x),
O_{p,d}:=sum_x H_{p,d}(x)V_{p,d}(x).
```

Thus `M_{p,d}` is raw plus and `O_{p,d}` is the double-sign overlap inside raw plus.  Exactly,

```text
A_+^com(p,d)=M_{p,d}-O_{p,d}.                           (2.1)
```

Every presently available radical, gap-continuant, resultant, or zero-moment estimate is an **upper** bound on a nonnegative zero pattern.  An upper bound

```text
0<=O_{p,d}<=B_{p,d}
```

implies only

```text
M_{p,d}-B_{p,d} <= A_+^com(p,d) <= M_{p,d}.             (2.2)
```

The useful upper bound would require a **lower** estimate

```text
O_{p,d} >= (1-epsilon)M_{p,d}.                          (2.3)
```

No positive carrier theorem supplies `(2.3)`.  Subtracting an upper bound for the overlap from the raw-plus upper bound is an invalid reversal of inequality.

Moreover, even `(2.3)` would not reduce the sign-summed gate.  By `(1.8)`, the covered rows are then counted on minus.  For the total union, overlap size changes only whether a row is labeled plus or minus.

## 3. The unique mean-zero centering has a main coefficient near one

Let

```text
rho_p:=Z(p)/p,
f_p(u):=z_p(u)-rho_p.
```

Then

```text
sum_(u mod p) f_p(u)=0,
||f_p||_2^2=Z(p)(1-rho_p).                              (3.1)
```

The exact centered identities are

```text
O_{p,d}
 =rho_p M_{p,d}
  +E_{p,d},

E_{p,d}:=sum_x H_{p,d}(x)f_p(x-13d),                   (3.2)
```

and

```text
A_+^com(p,d)
 =(1-rho_p)M_{p,d}-E_{p,d}.                            (3.3)
```

The proved pointwise zero-set bound gives

```text
rho_p << p^(-1/3+o(1))=T^(-1/3+o(1)).                  (3.4)
```

Hence the zero mode in `(3.3)` obeys

```text
(1-rho_p)M_{p,d}=(1-o(1))M_{p,d}.                      (3.5)
```

This is the opposite of a density saving.  Nondivisibility is the generic condition because the zero set is sparse.

Suppose, unrealistically strongly, that a large sieve proved

```text
sum_(p,d) (log p) E_{p,d}
 =o(sum_(p,d)(log p)M_{p,d}).                           (3.6)
```

Then `(3.3)` would give

```text
C_+^com=(1-o(1)) C_+^raw,                               (3.7)
```

not a power saving.

To obtain

```text
A_+^com(p,d)<=epsilon M_{p,d}
```

from `(3.3)`, one must prove

```text
E_{p,d}>=(1-rho_p-epsilon)M_{p,d}.                     (3.8)
```

Because `f_p<=1-rho_p`, `(3.8)` says that `H_{p,d}` is almost entirely supported where `z_p(x-13d)=1`.  It is precisely the near-containment statement `(2.3)`, rewritten.  It is not a consequence of a two-sided norm bound.

### 3.1 No alternative center repairs the problem

For any real `lambda`,

```text
A_+^com
 =(1-lambda)M_{p,d}
  -sum_x H_{p,d}(x)(z_p(x-13d)-lambda).                (3.9)
```

If `lambda` is the mean, it is `rho_p=o(1)` and the main term is almost `M`.  If `lambda` is chosen close to one to make the main term small, the centered function is approximately `-1` on almost all of `F_p`; its `L^2` norm is of order `p^(1/2)`, and on a plus-exclusive packet the correlation in `(3.9)` is exactly `-lambda M`.  The two terms again add to `M`.

Choosing `lambda` to be the conditional overlap density

```text
lambda=O_{p,d}/M_{p,d}
```

makes the centered correlation zero by definition, but estimating that `lambda` is near one is exactly the original overlap-coverage problem.  This centering is circular.

## 4. Exact signed Fourier identity and its sharp norm limitation

Use the unnormalized Fourier transform on `F_p`:

```text
hat g(k)=sum_(x mod p) g(x)e_p(-kx),
e_p(y)=exp(2*pi*i*y/p).
```

Since `hat f_p(0)=0`, Fourier inversion in `(3.2)` gives

```text
E_{p,d}
 =1/p sum_(k!=0)
      hat f_p(k) hat H_{p,d}(-k) e_p(-13dk).            (4.1)
```

This is a legitimate same-characteristic identity.  The upper condition `q|b_t` remains inside the scalar coefficient `H_{p,d}`; it is not transported from `F_q` to `F_p`.

Parseval gives the exact norm estimate

```text
|E_{p,d}|
 <= ||H_{p,d}||_2 ||f_p||_2
 = sqrt(M_{p,d} Z(p)(1-rho_p)).                         (4.2)
```

Consequently

```text
O_{p,d}
 >= rho_p M_{p,d}
    -sqrt(M_{p,d}Z(p)(1-rho_p)).                        (4.3)
```

The right side of `(4.3)` is always nonpositive under the trivial facts `M_{p,d}<=p` and `Z(p)<=p`, because

```text
sqrt(MZ) >= (Z/p)M.
```

Thus Parseval recovers only

```text
O_{p,d}>=0.                                             (4.4)
```

A large sieve may improve the **absolute** aggregate size of the nonzero-frequency term.  It still cannot change its required sign.  In fact, as noted in `(3.7)`, making the signed term small leaves almost the full raw-plus mass.

### 4.1 Positivity-preserving Fourier majorants have a large zero mode

Let

```text
g_p(u)=1-z_p(u).
```

Suppose a trigonometric polynomial `Phi_p` is inserted into the positive consumer as a pointwise majorant, so that

```text
Phi_p(u)>=g_p(u) for every u.                           (4.5)
```

Then its zero Fourier coefficient satisfies

```text
hat Phi_p(0)/p
 =average(Phi_p)
 >=average(g_p)
 =1-rho_p.                                              (4.6)
```

Hence every positivity-preserving Fourier upper bound has a zero mode `1-o(1)`.  On a coefficient supported in the complement of `Z_p`, `(4.5)` gives the even stronger pointwise obstruction

```text
sum_x H(x)Phi_p(x-13d) >= sum_x H(x).                  (4.7)
```

If one instead uses a signed approximant that is not a pointwise majorant, it cannot replace `g_p` in a nonnegative consumer without an explicit correlation error.  Taking absolute values of that error returns to `(4.2)` or a large-sieve analogue and loses the hoped-for sign.

This proves a general barrier: **centering and spectral cancellation are useful for a sparse positive indicator, but the complement of a sparse indicator has density almost one.**

## 5. The factorial consumer has the same obstruction

At a fixed selected upper state `(q,t)`, let `H_p` be the raw-plus Boolean coefficient for a lower prime `p` in the common window, and let `V_p` be its minus-secondary zero bit.  Then

```text
K_+(q,t)=sum_p H_p(1-V_p).                              (5.1)
```

The ordered factorial term is exactly

```text
K_+(K_+-1)
 =sum_(p!=p') H_pH_p'(1-V_p)(1-V_p').                  (5.2)
```

Expanding the complements gives

```text
(1-V_p)(1-V_p')
 =1-V_p-V_p'+V_pV_p'.                                  (5.3)
```

Again, an upper bound for any of the positive overlap terms in `(5.3)` cannot be subtracted to obtain an upper bound for `(5.2)`.  The mean-zero expansion has main coefficient

```text
(1-rho_p)(1-rho_p')=1-o(1).                            (5.4)
```

The plus-exclusive model in Section 6 has `V_p=0` for every row, so `(5.2)` equals the raw-plus factorial energy exactly.

For the two sign loads, put

```text
K=K_-+K_+.
```

The tie-break can affect the quadratic energy only by an absolute factor:

```text
K^2/2-K
 <= K_-(K_--1)+K_+(K_+-1)
 <= K(K-1).                                             (5.5)
```

Thus even at the marked factorial level, distributing a union between two signs cannot create a power saving.

## 7. Formal scoped obstruction

### Theorem — `Q6-C4-CENTERED-NONZERO-NO-GAIN`

Consider any class of Boolean marked-incidence configurations satisfying:

```text
1. the exact common-window formulas for r,a_-,a_+;
2. the upper mark q|b_t and all deterministic masks;
3. reflection and the current one-sign zero-set/gap/moment bounds;
4. nonnegative prime-log weights;
5. only two-sided Fourier or large-sieve norm estimates for centered zero indicators;
6. the raw-minus-first set assignment.
```

Assume the class contains the interface packets of Section 6, or more abstractly is closed under deleting the minus-secondary zero while keeping a geometrically separated raw-plus pair.  Then no estimate of the form

```text
C_+^assigned <= T^(-delta+o(1)) C_+^raw               (7.1)
```

with fixed `delta>0`, and no bound

```text
C_-+C_+=o_J(T^2/L)                                    (7.2)
```

can be deduced solely from centering, inclusion-exclusion, or those norm estimates.

### Proof

On the packet, every raw-plus row has `V=0`, so `C_+^assigned=C_+^raw` and `C_-=0`.  Equations `(6.23)`--`(6.24)` contradict `(7.1)` and `(7.2)`.  All centered Fourier identities and norm estimates remain valid, with exact correlation `(6.25)`.  Therefore any proof of `(7.2)` must use an additional arithmetic statement that rules out the packet.  `QED`

This is an interface non-derivability theorem, not a counterexample to an Apéry theorem.

## 8. What new theorem would actually be needed

### 8.1 The one-sided theorem exposed by the complement

For common-window raw plus, define

```text
P_raw^com(T)
 =sum_(p,d,x)(log p)H_{p,d}(x),

O_3(T)
 =sum_(p,d,x)(log p)H_{p,d}(x)z_p(x-13d).
```

Then exactly

```text
C_+^com=P_raw^com-O_3.                                 (8.1)
```

A complement-based saving requires the one-sided three-zero coverage estimate

```text
O_3(T)
 >= P_raw^com(T)-o_J(T^2/L).                           (8.2)
```

Equivalently, the centered correlation must satisfy

```text
sum_(p,d,x)(log p)H_{p,d}(x)f_p(x-13d)
 >= sum_(p,d)(log p)(1-rho_p)M_{p,d}
    -o_J(T^2/L).                                       (8.3)
```

Neither `(8.2)` nor `(8.3)` is a large-sieve norm estimate.  It asserts near-maximal **positive** alignment of a translated zero set with the raw-plus pair support.

But `(8.2)` is not enough for the requested sign-summed gate: the overlap counted by `O_3` is assigned to minus.  By `(1.8)`, it is still present once in `C_-+C_+`.

### 8.2 The useful successor for the total gate

Let `K_sigma(q,t)` count the exact sign-assigned lower primes at one complete selected state, including every mask.  Let

```text
B(T)=# {(q,t):q prime,0<=t<q,q|b_t}
```

in the relevant shell.  Pointwise,

```text
sum_(q,t)[K_-+K_+]
 <=2B(T)+sqrt(2B(T)F_mark(T)),                          (8.4)
```

where

```text
F_mark(T)
 =sum_(q,t:q|b_t)
   [K_-(K_--1)+K_+(K_+-1)].                            (8.5)
```

Using the proved upper-zero first moment

```text
B(T)<<_J T^(8/5)L^(-4/5),                              (8.6)
```

the sufficient new theorem is

```text
F_mark(T)
 =o_J(T^(12/5)L^(-16/5)).                              (8.7)
```

Then `(8.4)` is `o_J(T^2/L^2)`, and multiplication by `log p asymp_J L` gives the desired `o_J(T^2/L)`.

Statement `(8.7)` is strictly smaller than the first-moment gate: a matching with every `K_sigma<=1` has `F_mark=0` while retaining nonzero marked mass.  It also survives the sign-assignment correction: by `(5.5)`, no repartition between two signs can change its required power.

A direct one-sign marked covariance theorem with the same net fifth-of-a-power gain would be equally valid.  What cannot work is another signed rewrite of `1-z_p(a_-)` without new one-sided arithmetic.

## Bottom line

The nondivisibility in minus-first assigned plus is not a rare arithmetic event.  It is the complement of a sparse zero set, so its natural mean is `1-o(1)`.  In the exact common-window coordinates it says that the right edge of a three-point lower chain is present while the far-left endpoint is absent.  Current recurrence and moment inputs allow many such `110` patterns.

Centering produces an exact signed identity, but its zero mode already contains almost the entire raw-plus count.  Parseval and the large sieve control the magnitude of the nonzero modes, whereas a saving would require those modes to have a near-maximal favorable sign.  A pointwise positive majorant cannot avoid the same large zero mode.  Inclusion-exclusion has the same defect: an upper bound for the overlap cannot be subtracted in an upper-bound argument.

Finally, on the sign-summed consumer the apparent cancellation is exactly illusory.  If the minus endpoint is dark, the row is counted on plus; if it is bright, the same raw-plus row is counted on minus.  The tie-break changes ownership, not support.  Therefore the missing fifth of a power must come from marked lower-prime reuse/covariance inside a one-sign slice, not from the nondivisibility complement.
