## 1. Exact row-coordinate recursion

Let

\[
B(n)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&p_n\\-1&1&-q_n\\0&-1&r_n\end{pmatrix}
\]

be the transition in the moving basis. If the row vector of basis coordinates is

\[
z_n=(z_0,z_1,z_2),
\]

then

\[
z_{n+1}=z_nB(n).
\]

Therefore the exact scalar recurrence is

\[
\begin{aligned}
z_0'&=z_0-z_1,\\
z_1'&=z_1-z_2,\\
z_2'&=p_nz_0-q_nz_1+r_nz_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the correct row evolution; treating B as a column map gives the wrong cone conditions.

The natural ratios for a positive third coordinate normalization are

\[
x=\frac{z_1}{z_0},\qquad y=\frac{z_2}{z_1}.
\]

Substitution gives

\[
x'=\frac{z_1'}{z_0'}=\frac{x(1-y)}{1-x},
\]

and

\[
y'=\frac{z_2'}{z_1'}
 =\frac{p_n-q_nx+r_nxy}{x(1-y)}.
\]

This matches the experimentally discovered recursion.

## 2. The fixed asymptotic curve

Using

\[
p_n\sim \frac{96}{n},\qquad q_n\sim32,\qquad r_n\sim33,
\]

seek a formal orbit

\[
x_n=\frac{a}{n+s}+\frac{a_2}{(n+s)^2}+O(n^{-3}),
\]

\[
y_n=\frac{b}{n+s}+\frac{b_2}{(n+s)^2}+O(n^{-3}).
\]

The first equation gives

\[
x'-x=x(y-x)+O(n^{-3}),
\]

while the time shift satisfies

\[
x_{n+1}-x_n=-\frac{a}{(n+s)^2}+O(n^{-3}).
\]

Hence

\[
a(b-a)=-a,
\]

so for the nonzero orbit

\[
b=a-1.
\]

The second equation gives, at leading order,

\[
\frac{b}{n}=\frac{96-32a}{a(n)}+O(n^{-2}),
\]

therefore

\[
a(a-1)=96-32a,
\]

or

\[
a^2+31a-96=0.
\]

The admissible positive root is

\[
a=\frac{-31+\sqrt{1345}}2,
\]

and therefore

\[
b=\frac{-33+\sqrt{1345}}2.
\]

Numerically

\[
a\approx2.836,\qquad b\approx1.836.
\]

Thus the invariant object is not a rectangle but a one-dimensional asymptotic curve.

## 3. Correct correlated cone

The failed rectangle condition was requiring independent bounds on x and y. The correct cone is centered on

\[
\Phi_n(x)=y-\frac{a-1}{a}x+\frac{\kappa}{n}x.
\]

The cone facets are

\[
|\Phi_n(x)|\le \frac{C}{n^2},
\]

with the second facet controlling the transverse error:

\[
0<x<\frac{a+C/n}{n+s}.
\]

Expanding the recursion gives

\[
\Phi_{n+1}(x')
=\left(1-\frac{2}{n}+O(n^{-2})\right)\Phi_n(x)
 +\frac{K}{n^3}+O(n^{-4}),
\]

where K is an explicit rational expression in the coefficients of p,q,r and the chosen shift s.

Choosing

\[
C> |K|/2
\]

makes the strip invariant for all sufficiently large n.

The finite initial segment is checked exactly by clearing denominators.

## 4. Formal certificate for Lean

The usable certificate is not a total positivity slogan. It is the following finite inequality package.

For each n define

\[
L_n(x,y)=y-\alpha_nx,
\]

where

\[
\alpha_n=\frac{b}{a}+\frac{\kappa}{n}.
\]

Then prove:

1. Boundary x-facet:

\[
0\le x\le X_n \implies 0\le x'\le X_{n+1}.
\]

2. Lower L-facet:

\[
L_n=-C/n^2\implies L_{n+1}\ge-C/(n+1)^2.
\]

3. Upper L-facet:

\[
L_n=C/n^2\implies L_{n+1}\le C/(n+1)^2.
\]

After substituting the rational formulas for p_n,q_n,r_n these reduce to polynomial positivity after multiplication by

\[
 n^3(1-x)^2x.
\]

This is the formalization-ready algebraic certificate.

## 5. Why total positivity alone is insufficient

The positive exterior-square minors imply that the projective map is order preserving and variation diminishing for the full cone of sign-regular vectors. They do not imply preservation of a particular two-sided bracket.

The obstruction is that the B(n) skeleton has

\[
p_n-q_n<0,
\]

so the vector (1,1,1) is sent across the boundary of every naive finite-difference cone. Total positivity controls the number of sign changes; it does not locate the sign-change position.

Therefore the missing theorem is not a general TP theorem but a moving-kernel theorem:

> the special orbit must remain inside a time-dependent strip attached to the positive Perron curve.
