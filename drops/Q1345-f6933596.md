# Audit of the zero-block objection

## Verdict

The proposed zero-block correction is valid. The first gap identified in Q1336 disappears: the fixed-prefix partition grouping is licensed by the Mellit--Vlasenko concatenation convention once the convention `ell(0)=1` and `n_r != 0` are applied literally. The remaining boundary lemma is not invalidated by the zero-prefix issue.

## 1. Canonical concatenation and the zero suffix

The relevant definition is

```text
n1 * ... * nr = n1 + n2 p^{ell(n1)} + ...,
```

where every factor is interpreted through its canonical base-p expansion and the last factor is nonzero.

Suppose a fixed low-to-high digit word is

```text
w = w_0 ... w_m 0 ... 0
```

with `z` appended top zero digits. A partition block ending at the top zero position would have a top digit zero. Such a block is not a canonical expansion of a positive integer: its most significant digit would be zero. Therefore every appended top zero must appear as a separate block equal to the integer zero.

After removing these forced singleton blocks, the remaining blocks are exactly the admissible partitions of the canonical expansion of the numerical prefix.

There is no analogous restriction on internal zero digits. A word such as

```text
101001_p
```

already has internal zero digits in its canonical expansion. Those zeros remain inside blocks exactly as permitted by the concatenation definition.

Hence the map

```text
(partition of fixed word)
  -> (partition after deleting forced top zero singleton blocks)
```

is bijective.

## 2. Weight preservation and c(0)

The construction gives

```text
c_n = CT(I_Lambda^n).
```

For `n=0`, the extension is not merely a valuation convention. It follows directly:

```text
S_1^ind = {(0)}
R_0(Lambda^0)=1
I_Lambda^0=1.
```

Therefore

```text
c_0 = CT(1)=1.
```

A forced zero singleton block contributes exactly `c(0)=1`, so deleting or inserting those blocks leaves the partition weight unchanged.

## 3. Boundary lemma re-audit

The grouping by final top-containing block length `l` is therefore legitimate. For each possible `l`:

- the top-containing block contributes its valuation factor `p^(l-1)` through the Mellit--Vlasenko coefficient bound;
- the preceding blocks enumerate precisely the partitions of the truncated canonical prefix;
- their sum is the corresponding Apéry coefficient `b_(T_(l-1))`.

The zero-padding at a fixed digit length does not create extra terms or lose terms.

Thus the implication

```text
p^(e-k)|b_(T_k) for 0<=k<e
    => p^e|b_W
```

survives this audit.

The Q1336 first claimed gap (failure of the fixed-length-prefix grouping because of top zeros) disappears.

## 4. Alternative grouping search

No admissible counterexample word exists under the stated convention. A candidate would need a non-singleton block whose most significant digit is zero, but that violates canonical expansion.

For example, the tempting word

```text
10_p
```

cannot be split as a block representing `0*p+1` with a leading-zero top digit. The only valid treatment of the top zero is a singleton zero block when it is part of a fixed-length padded representation.

Internal-zero examples do not work as counterexamples because they are already part of canonical expansions.

## Final conclusion

The zero-block objection was based on confusing fixed-length digit padding with admissible canonical factors. Mellit--Vlasenko's definition distinguishes these: padding zeros are forced singleton factors with weight one, while internal zeros remain genuine digits. Therefore the proposed bijection is correct, `c(0)=1` follows from the constant-term construction, and Q1336's first gap is closed.
