# 2. Exact reflection-centered character algebra

Let
\[
\mathcal X_p^+:=\{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb F_p^\times}:\chi(-1)=1\}.
\]
Then \(|\mathcal X_p^+|=(p-1)/2\) and its orthogonality relation is
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}\chi(t)
=\frac{p-1}{2}\,\mathbf 1_{t\in\{1,-1\}}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The canonical Apéry reflection
\[
r\longmapsto p-1-r
\]
becomes, after the half-shift,
\[
r+\frac12\longmapsto-(r+\tfrac12).
\]
Hence
\[
X_p=-X_p.
\tag{2.2}
\]
In particular odd characters cancel identically, which is exactly why the residual lies in the even-character sector.

The ratio multiplicity has the symmetries
\[
R_p(\lambda)=R_p(\lambda^{-1})=R_p(-\lambda),
\tag{2.3}
\]
and, because the support is a set in \(\mathbb F_p^\times\),
\[
R_p(1)=z_p,
\qquad
R_p(-1)=z_p.
\tag{2.4}
\]
The first is the diagonal.  The second is the forced reflection pairing.  Thus
\[
E_p^\times
=2z_p^2+E_{p,\mathrm{nt}}^\times,
\tag{2.5}
\]
where `nt` means \(\lambda\notin\{\pm1\}\).

There is no additive-difference interpretation of \(R_p(\lambda)\) with fixed gap.  The exact condition is
\[
\frac{r+1/2}{s+1/2}=\lambda
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r=\lambda s+\frac{\lambda-1}{2}.
\tag{2.6}
\]
Thus \(R_p(\lambda)\) is an affine-**dilate** self-intersection of the actual zero set.  This distinction drives the whole audit.

# 3. Exact second moment

Expanding and using (2.1),
\begin{align*}
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}|A_p(\chi)|^2
&=\sum_{x,y\in X_p}
 \sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}\chi(x/y)\\
&=\frac{p-1}{2}\#\{(x,y)\in X_p^2:x/y=\pm1\}.
\end{align*}
For each \(x\in X_p\), both \(y=x\) and \(y=-x\) are in \(X_p\), and they are distinct because \(p\) is odd and \(x\ne0\).  Hence the cardinality is \(2z_p\), proving
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}|A_p(\chi)|^2=(p-1)z_p.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Multiplying by \(p\) gives (M2).

For the principal character, \(A_p(1)=z_p\), so subtracting its contribution gives (M2\(^\circ\)).

## 3.1 Exact low-dimensional sector estimate

For every even character,
\[
|c_p(\chi)|^2\le pz_p^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Therefore, for any set \(\mathcal C_p\subseteq\mathcal X_p^+\) with \(|\mathcal C_p|\le p^\rho\),
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_p}|c_p(\chi)|^2
\le p^{1+\rho}z_p^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Against the full even second moment,
\[
\frac{\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_p}|c_p(\chi)|^2}
     {\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}|c_p(\chi)|^2}
\le \frac{p^\rho z_p}{p-1}
\ll p^{\rho-1/3+o(1)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
With
\[
\rho<\frac{47}{150},
\]
we have
\[
\frac13-\frac{47}{150}=\frac1{50},
\]
so every such sector carries
\[
\ll p^{-1/50-\varepsilon_\rho+o(1)}
\]
of the full even \(L^2\) mass, where
\[
\varepsilon_\rho:=\frac{47}{150}-\rho>0.
\tag{3.5}
\]
This independently recovers the threshold quoted in Q4889.

Notice the direction: the estimate proves that a small character sector **cannot carry much mass**.  It does not prove that the coefficient vector is concentrated in that sector.

# 4. Exact fourth moment = shifted multiplicative ratio energy

Expand
\[
|A_p(\chi)|^4
=\sum_{a,b,c,d\in X_p}\chi\!\left(\frac{ac}{bd}\right).
\]
Even-character orthogonality gives
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}|A_p(\chi)|^4
=\frac{p-1}{2}
\#\{(a,b,c,d)\in X_p^4:ac=\pm bd\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
For the plus sign,
\[
ac=bd
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a/b=d/c,
\]
so its number of solutions is
\[
\sum_\lambda R_p(\lambda)^2=E_p^\times.
\]
For the minus sign,
\[
ac=-bd
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a/b=-d/c,
\]
whose count is
\[
\sum_\lambda R_p(\lambda)R_p(-\lambda)=E_p^\times
\]
by (2.3).  Hence
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}|A_p(\chi)|^4
=(p-1)E_p^\times.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Multiplying by \(p^2\) gives (M4).  Subtracting the principal term \(p^2z_p^4\) gives (M4\(^\circ\)).

This is the exact fourth-moment identity requested in Q4889.  It is genuinely multiplicative: the collision relation is equality of the ratios
\[
\frac{r_1+1/2}{r_2+1/2}
=
\frac{r_3+1/2}{r_4+1/2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
No additive difference appears.

## 4.1 Support-only bounds and what they do prove

For any finite \(X\subset\mathbb F_p^\times\) of size \(z\),
\[
E^\times(X)\le z^3,
\tag{4.4}
\]
because three of \((a,b,c,d)\) determine the fourth in the equation \(ac=bd\).  For the reflection-symmetric Apéry support, (2.4) also gives
\[
E_p^\times\ge2z_p^2.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}|c_p(\chi)|^4
\le p^2(p-1)z_p^3.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Combining (M2) and (4.6),
\[
\frac{\|c_p\|_4}{\|c_p\|_2}
\le\left(\frac{z_p}{p-1}\right)^{1/4}
\ll p^{-1/12+o(1)}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
This is a real theorem and should not be discarded.

What it does **not** prove is
\[
E_{p,\mathrm{nt}}^\times\ll p^{-\delta}z_p^3+z_p^2
\tag{4.8}
\]
for any \(\delta>0\).  Such a power saving is the arithmetic information needed after the PS14 character dimension has already been exposed.

# 6. Exact bridge from a ratio collision to a new scaled gap-polynomial family

This section isolates the first precise place where the known Apéry gap technology stops.

Take two oriented zero pairs, after the finite nonwrapping/signed circular branch split,
\[
a,a+d\in Z_p,
\qquad
b,b+e\in Z_p,
\qquad 2\le d,e<p.
\tag{6.1}
\]
The canonical gap implication gives
\[
N_d(a)=0,
\qquad
N_e(b)=0
\pmod p.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Suppose their shifted ratios agree:
\[
\frac{a+d+1/2}{a+1/2}
=
\frac{b+e+1/2}{b+1/2}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Put
\[
x=a+\frac12,
\qquad y=b+\frac12.
\]
Then (6.3) is exactly
\[
1+\frac d{x}=1+\frac e{y}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
dy=ex.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Since \(d,e\) are units modulo \(p\), there is a unique \(T\in\mathbb F_p\) with
\[
x=dT,
\qquad y=eT.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Thus
\[
a=dT-\frac12,
\qquad b=eT-\frac12,
\]
and (6.2) becomes
\[
N_d(dT-1/2)=0,
\qquad
N_e(eT-1/2)=0.
\tag{6.6}
\]
This is the exact multiplicative-to-gap bridge.

Define the denominator-cleared scaled polynomial
\[
\mathcal N_h^\times(T)
:=2^{3(h-1)}N_h(hT-1/2)\in\mathbb Z[T].
\tag{6.7}
\]
The integrality is immediate because \(N_h\in\mathbb Z[X]\) has degree \(3(h-1)\).

The reflection identity
\[
N_h(-X-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(X)
\tag{6.8}
\]
shows that for even \(h\), the center \(X=-(h+1)/2\) is a forced root.  Under \(X=hT-1/2\) this is
\[
T=-\frac12.
\]
It is exactly the forced ratio \(-1\).  Therefore one must saturate the scaled polynomial by deleting every power of \(2T+1\):
\[
\mathcal N_h^{\times,\circ}(T)
:=\frac{\mathcal N_h^\times(T)}{(2T+1)^{\nu_h}},
\qquad
\nu_h:=\operatorname{ord}_{T=-1/2}\mathcal N_h^\times(T).
\tag{6.9}
\]
Here \(\nu_h\ge1\) for every even \(h\); taking the full order avoids leaving an automatic reflection factor in the resultant.

Now define the **multiplicative ratio resultant**
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr R_{d,e}^\times
:=\operatorname{Res}_T
\bigl(\mathcal N_d^{\times,\circ}(T),
      \mathcal N_e^{\times,\circ}(T)\bigr).}
\tag{6.10}
\]
A nontrivial ratio collision with \(T\ne-1/2\) forces
\[
p\mid\mathscr R_{d,e}^\times,
\tag{6.11}
\]
up to only the finite branch/denominator exceptions already excluded by \(d,e<p\) and odd \(p\).

Compare this with the banked additive family in `meso_result.tex`:
\[
\mathcal R_{d,e}
=\operatorname{Res}_X\bigl(N_d(X),N_e(X+d)\bigr).
\tag{6.12}
\]
The second arguments are fundamentally different.  If one changes variable \(X=dT-1/2\), the second scaled polynomial becomes
\[
N_e\!\left(\frac ed(X+1/2)-1/2\right),
\tag{6.13}
\]
not \(N_e(X+d)\).  The old family is a translation; the new family has a degree-dependent dilation.

This is the **first exact failed implication**:
\[
\boxed{
\text{prime-summed control of translated gap resultants}
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{control of shifted multiplicative ratio energy}.}
\tag{6.14}
\]
No theorem in the checked source gives a height, gcd, common-factor, or prime-summed estimate for the family (6.10).

## 6.1 Why the fixed-start \(H^{8/3}\) energy does not transfer

`energy_result.tex` defines
\[
k_x(H)=\#\{2\le h\le H:N_h(x)=0\}
\]
and proves, off the finite boundary,
\[
\mathcal E_p^\circ(H)=\sum_x\binom{k_x(H)}2,
\qquad
\mathcal E_p(H)\ll H^{8/3},
\quad H\le\sqrt p.
\tag{6.15}
\]
A ratio collision, however, asks for common zeros of
\[
N_h(hT-1/2)
\]
at one fixed \(T\).  The corresponding start is
\[
x_h=hT-1/2,
\tag{6.16}
\]
which changes with \(h\).  So (6.15) controls vertical columns
\[
(h,x)=(h,x_0),
\]
while ratio energy needs sheared columns
\[
(h,x)=(h,hT-1/2).
\]
There is no inequality from the first family of column factorial moments to the second.  Even a dyadic restriction \(h\asymp H\) leaves the shear intact.

This is why importing \(H^{8/3}\) into (M4) would be a genuine additive/multiplicative category error.

# 7. Finite support-compatible saturation model

The following model proves that the checked support and additive-gap interfaces cannot, by themselves, imply any fixed-power saving in \(E_p^\times/z_p^3\).

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and a sufficiently large odd prime \(p\).  Let \(g\) be a primitive root and put
\[
K:=\lfloor p^{1/4-\varepsilon}\rfloor,
\qquad
Y:=\{\pm g^j:0\le j<K\}\subset\mathbb F_p^\times.
\tag{7.1}
\]
For large \(p\) these \(2K\) elements are distinct.  Set
\[
L:=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor.
\]
We claim there is a scalar \(a\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) such that all distinct points of
\[
X:=aY
\]
have circular additive distance \(>L\), and such that \(\pm1/2\notin X\).

Indeed, for fixed distinct \(y,y'\in Y\) and fixed nonzero circular difference \(d\) with \(|d|_p\le L\), the equation
\[
a(y-y')=d
\]
excludes exactly one scalar \(a\).  The total number of excluded scalars is at most
\[
2L|Y|(|Y|-1)+O(|Y|)
\ll p^{1-2\varepsilon}<p-1
\tag{7.2}
\]
for large \(p\).  Hence a good scalar exists.

Now shift back to labels
\[
S:=\{x-1/2:x\in X\}\subset\mathbb F_p.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Then:

1. **Palindrome.** \(X=-X\), so \(S\) is invariant under \(r\mapsto p-1-r\).
2. **Noncentral and endpoint avoidance.** \(0\notin X\), and the choice of \(a\) avoids \(\pm1/2\), so the reflection center and the two endpoints are absent.
3. **No consecutive zeros.** In fact every two distinct labels have circular distance \(>\sqrt p\).
4. **Canonical subinterval bound.** If an interval has length \(H\le\sqrt p\), it contains at most one point.  If \(H>\sqrt p\), then
   \[
   |S|=2K\ll p^{1/4-\varepsilon}\le H^{2/3}.
   \]
   Thus the model obeys a stronger form of the available \(O(H^{2/3})\) subinterval estimate.
5. **Fixed-start actual short-gap implications are vacuous.** There is no actual pair with gap \(\le\sqrt p\).  Hence every implication “two actual zeros with short gap \(h\) force \(N_h(r)=0\)” places no demand on this support.  In particular the model does not contradict \(\mathcal E_p(H)\ll H^{8/3}\).
6. **Prime-summed translated-resultant bounds are not violated.** Those theorems concern the fixed polynomial family \(\mathcal R_{d,e}\) and only become necessary support conditions when actual short-gap configurations force the relevant divisibilities.  Our support has no such short configurations.  The model is not claiming to alter the Apéry polynomials.

This is deliberately a **support/interface model**, not a fake Apéry sequence.

## 7.1 Its multiplicative energy is cubic

Scaling does not change ratio multiplicities, so compute with \(Y\).  Every ratio is of the form
\[
\pm g^d,
\qquad -(K-1)\le d\le K-1.
\]
For either sign and a fixed \(d\), its multiplicity is
\[
2(K-|d|).
\]
Hence
\begin{align*}
E^\times(X)
&=2\sum_{d=-(K-1)}^{K-1}\bigl(2(K-|d|)\bigr)^2\\
&=8\left(K^2+2\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}j^2\right)\\
&=\frac{16K^3+8K}{3}.
\end{align*}
\tag{7.4}
Since \(z:=|X|=2K\),
\[
\boxed{
E^\times(X)=\frac23z^3+\frac43z.}
\tag{7.5}
\]
The trivial ratios \(\pm1\) contribute \(2z^2\), so
\[
E_{\mathrm{nt}}^\times(X)
=\frac23z^3-2z^2+O(z)
=\left(\frac23+o(1)\right)z^3.
\tag{7.6}
\]
Thus for every fixed \(\delta>0\), the implication
\[
\text{palindrome + no consecutive + subinterval + banked short-gap interfaces}
\Longrightarrow
E_{\mathrm{nt}}^\times\ll p^{-\delta}z^3+z^2
\]
is false at the level of those interfaces.

The construction can be made independently for every prime in any finite dyadic shell.  Its size \(z_p\asymp p^{1/4-\varepsilon}\) is well below the canonical \(p^{2/3+o(1)}\) cap and also below the known shell-summed zero-count ledgers, so the obstruction is not an artifact of violating a global zero budget.

## 7.2 Removing a low-dimensional character sector does not save the model

Let \(\mathcal C_p\) be any set of at most \(p^\rho\) even characters.  By the pointwise bound,
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_p}|c_p(\chi)|^4
\le p^{2+\rho}z^4.
\tag{7.7}
\]
For the model, (M4) and (7.5) give
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X_p^+}|c_p(\chi)|^4
\asymp p^3z^3.
\tag{7.8}
\]
Therefore the fraction of fourth moment removed by \(\mathcal C_p\) is
\[
\ll p^{\rho-1}z
\ll p^{\rho-3/4-\varepsilon}.
\tag{7.9}
\]
For every \(\rho<47/150\), this tends to zero by a large power.  Consequently the high-dimensional complement can retain essentially the entire cubic-order fourth moment.

This is the requested finite saturation mechanism for the high-character residual.

# 8. The smallest new theorem

The exact missing arithmetic statistic is
\[
R_p(\lambda)

 \sum_{\chi(-1)=1}|c_p(\chi)|^2=p(p-1)z_p,

\[
 p((p-1)z_p-z_p^2)

and

\]
Moreover $R_p(1)=R_p(-1)=z_p$ and $E_p^\times\le z_p^3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}

\[
 \sum_{\chi(-1)=1}\chi(t)
 =\frac{p-1}{2}{\bf 1}_{t=\pm1}.

 \frac{p-1}{2}
 \#\{(a,b,c,d)\in X_p^4:ac=\pm bd\}.

The $+$ count is $E_p^\times$.  The $-$ count is
$\sum_\lambda R_p(\lambda)R_p(-\lambda)=E_p^\times$ because
$X_p=-X_p$.  This proves the identities.  The principal character has
$A_p(1)=z_p$.  Finally three variables determine the fourth in a
multiplicative-energy solution, so $E_p^\times\le z_p^3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ratio collisions force scaled gap-resultant divisibility]
Let $p$ be odd and let $2\le d,e<p$.  Suppose

 p\mid
 \operatorname{Res}_T
 (\mathcal N_d^{\times,\circ},
  \mathcal N_e^{\times,\circ}).

\begin{proof}
