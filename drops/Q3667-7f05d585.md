# Cold audit: singleton-edge carrier bound and Smith realization

## Verdict

The proposed reduction has a **correct formal counting lemma** and a **correct Smith-theoretic lower bound**, but the proposed realization is not yet an arithmetic proof of the required edge bound.

The exact non-tautological input still missing is a **uniform finite-field joint-independence (full-spark) theorem for the actual neighbor-induced kernel vectors at the endpoint prime**. Distinct gaps \(d<p/6\) do give distinct residues modulo \(p\), but that only distinguishes the parameters; it does **not** imply that the associated kernel vectors are linearly independent.

There are also two implementation caveats that matter:

1. for a full-column-rank \(m\times n\) matrix, the relevant vectors must be independent **right-kernel** vectors (or one must subtract the structural left-nullity \(m-n\)); and
2. the matrix \(M_I\in\mathbb Z^{m_I\times n_I}\) must be a single, \(p\)-independent integral matrix for the whole macroscopic block \(I\), so that one prescribed integer \(C_I\) really carries all endpoint-prime multiplicities in that block.

Finally, after the modular independence theorem is obtained, a separate **height bound** on the determinantal divisors \(C_I\) is needed for the reduction to give a quantitatively useful P3.2 estimate.

---

## 1. The abstract carrier inequality is valid

Let \(E_J\) be the finite set of singleton prime-pair edges \(e=(p,q)\) with

\[
p<q,\qquad q-p>T/J,\qquad p>T/7.
\]

Suppose each edge is assigned a label \(a(e)\) and an endpoint prime \(r(e)\in\{p,q\}\), and for prescribed nonzero integers \(C_a\) define

\[
\mu(a,r)=\#\{e\in E_J:a(e)=a,\ r(e)=r\}.
\]

Assume

\[
\mu(a,r)\le v_r(C_a)
\]

for every label \(a\) and endpoint prime \(r\). Since \(r(e)\ge p>T/7\), for \(T>7\),

\[
\log r(e)>\log(T/7).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
|E_J|\log(T/7)
&\le \sum_{e\in E_J}\log r(e)\\
&=\sum_a\sum_{r\ \mathrm{prime}}\mu(a,r)\log r\\
&\le\sum_a\sum_{r\ \mathrm{prime}}v_r(C_a)\log r\\
&=\sum_a\log|C_a|.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equality is just unique factorization for the nonzero integer \(C_a\):

\[
\log|C_a|=\sum_{\ell\ \mathrm{prime}}v_\ell(C_a)\log\ell.
\]

So this implication is completely sound. The long-gap condition \(q-p>T/J\) is not used in this bookkeeping inequality; it is only relevant to how the edges are partitioned into blocks or how the carriers are constructed.

Two minor qualifications:

- \(r(e)\) must genuinely be a **prime endpoint**, not merely some residue attached to the edge, because \(v_r(C_a)\) is a prime-adic valuation in the factorization argument.
- If \(|C_a|=1\), then every \(v_r(C_a)=0\), hence no edge can be charged to that label; this is consistent.

Thus the carrier inequality itself is not the bottleneck.

## 2. Exact Smith statement for a full-column-rank integer matrix

Fix a block \(I\), and let

\[
M=M_I\in\mathbb Z^{m\times n},\qquad \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M=n.
\]

Hence \(m\ge n\), at least one \(n\times n\) minor is nonzero, and the positive gcd of all maximal minors

\[
\Delta_n(M):=\gcd\{\,|\det N|:N\text{ is an }n\times n\text{ minor of }M\,\}
\]

is a nonzero integer.

Write the Smith normal form as

\[
UMV=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_n)
\]

(up to zero padding in the extra rows), with

\[
s_1\mid s_2\mid\cdots\mid s_n,\qquad s_i>0.
\]

Then

\[
\Delta_n(M)=\prod_{i=1}^n s_i.
\]

For a prime \(p\), reduction modulo \(p\) gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}(M\bmod p)
 =\#\{i:p\nmid s_i\}.
\]

Consequently the **column corank** is

\[
\operatorname{corank}_p(M)
:=n-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}(M\bmod p)
=\#\{i:p\mid s_i\}.
\]

On the other hand,

\[
v_p(\Delta_n(M))
=\sum_{i=1}^n v_p(s_i)
\ge \#\{i:p\mid s_i\}
=\operatorname{corank}_p(M).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{corank}_p(M)\ge\rho
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_p(\Delta_n(M))\ge\rho.}
\]

This is the exact Smith implication needed by the proposal.

The repository's earlier `agent/q2650-aligned-smith-audit` computation is consistent with this distinction: raw mod-\(p\) rank defect controls the number of Smith invariant factors divisible by \(p\), whereas the determinantal valuation sums their full \(p\)-adic exponents. For example, a diagonal invariant factor \(p^2\) contributes only one dimension of mod-\(p\) corank but contributes valuation \(2\). Thus the inequality above is valid, but equality should not be silently asserted.

## 3. Kernel-side caveat: right kernel versus left kernel

For the full-column-rank shape \(M\in\mathbb Z^{m\times n}\),

\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\ker(M\bmod p)
=n-\operatorname{rank}(M\bmod p)
=\operatorname{corank}_p(M).
\]

Hence if \(ho\) selected neighbors produce \(ho\) linearly independent **right-kernel** vectors

\[
v_1,\ldots,v_\rho\in\ker_{\mathbb F_p}(M\bmod p),
\]

then indeed

\[
\operatorname{corank}_p(M)\ge\rho,
\]

and therefore

\[
v_p(\Delta_n(M))\ge\rho.
\]

But if the natural construction instead produces independent **left-kernel** vectors in

\[
\ker(M^T\bmod p)\subseteq\mathbb F_p^m,
\]

then

\[
\dim\ker(M^T\bmod p)
=m-\operatorname{rank}(M\bmod p)
=(m-n)+\operatorname{corank}_p(M).
\]

The term \(m-n\) is already present in characteristic zero. Thus \(ho\) independent left-null vectors imply only

\[
\operatorname{corank}_p(M)\ge \rho-(m-n),
\]

not \(\operatorname{corank}_p(M)\ge\rho\), unless \(m=n\) or one proves that the \(ho\) vectors contribute \(ho\) new dimensions **beyond** the structural left kernel.

So any concrete Smith realization must state the orientation of the neighbor vectors. If the natural vectors are left-null relations, transpose/reorient the construction or explicitly quotient out the generic \(m-n\) left-nullity.

## 4. How the Smith carrier would instantiate the abstract lemma

For each macroscopic block \(I\), define

\[
C_I:=\Delta_{n_I}(M_I),
\]

where \(M_I\in\mathbb Z^{m_I\times n_I}\) has full column rank over \(\mathbb Q\).

Suppose an endpoint prime \(p\) has \(ho=\mu(I,p)\) singleton edges assigned to block \(I\), with neighboring primes/gaps producing vectors

\[
\kappa_I(e;p)\in\ker_{\mathbb F_p}(M_I\bmod p).
\]

If those \(ho\) vectors are jointly linearly independent, then

\[
\mu(I,p)=\rho
\le\operatorname{corank}_p(M_I)
\le v_p(C_I).
\]

This is exactly the hypothesis needed by the abstract carrier bound, hence

\[
|E_J|\log(T/7)
\le\sum_I\log C_I.
\]

With only \(O_J(1)\) blocks, the number of carriers is harmless.

But this paragraph reveals that essentially all the arithmetic content has been pushed into the phrase **"those vectors are jointly linearly independent."** Smith normal form does not provide that independence; it only converts already-proved modular independence into valuation budget.

## 5. The exact missing non-tautological arithmetic theorem

The missing statement should be formulated as a finite-field full-spark/nonvanishing theorem for the **actual P3.2 neighbor vectors**.

A sufficiently strong version is:

> **Uniform neighbor full-spark lemma.** For every relevant macroscopic block \(I\), every endpoint prime \(p>T/7\), and every family of distinct singleton neighbors \(q_1,\ldots,q_\rho\) assigned to \((I,p)\), let \(d_j=q_j-p\) (or the corresponding canonical residue parameter used by the construction). Then the canonically constructed vectors
> \[
> \kappa_I(d_1;p),\ldots,\kappa_I(d_\rho;p)
> \in\ker_{\mathbb F_p}(M_I\bmod p)
> \]
> are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_p\).

Equivalently, for every such family one must exhibit a \(ho\times\rho\) coordinate minor

\[
D_I(d_1,\ldots,d_\rho)
\]

of the matrix of neighbor vectors and prove

\[
D_I(d_1,\ldots,d_\rho)\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

That modular nonvanishing is the genuinely arithmetic step.

A characteristic-zero statement

\[
D_I(d_1,\ldots,d_\rho)\ne0\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z\text{ or }\mathbb Q
\]

is **not enough**: the nonzero integer determinant can still be divisible by the endpoint prime \(p\). Thus real/complex noncollinearity or a nonzero rational determinant does not automatically supply the finite-field theorem required here.

If the neighbor vectors can be reduced, by a fixed \(p\)-integral invertible transformation and nonzero scalar factors, to a genuine Vandermonde family such as

\[
(1,d,d^2,\ldots,d^{s-1})^T,
\]

then for \(ho\le s\) the relevant determinant is

\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le\rho}(d_j-d_i),
\]

and distinct residues do prove independence. But in the more realistic situation where the determinant factors as

\[
D_I(d_1,\ldots,d_\rho)
=\left(\prod_{i<j}(d_j-d_i)\right)
 U_I(d_1,\ldots,d_\rho),
\]

distinct gaps only show that the Vandermonde factor is a \(p\)-unit. One must still prove

\[
U_I(d_1,\ldots,d_\rho)\not\equiv0\pmod p
\]

for every admissible endpoint prime and neighbor family. That residual factor is exactly where a hidden exceptional-prime or degeneracy problem can remain.

So the proposal is **not** completed merely by noticing that different neighbors have different gap residues.

## 6. Distinct gaps \(d<p/6\): what is true, and what is not

Suppose

\[
0<d_1,d_2<p/6,\qquad d_1\ne d_2.
\]

Then

\[
0<|d_1-d_2|<p/6<p,
\]

so

\[
d_1\not\equiv d_2\pmod p.
\]

If the canonical parameter is \(6d\), then similarly

\[
0<6|d_1-d_2|<p,
\]

hence

\[
6d_1\not\equiv6d_2\pmod p.
\]

Thus the small-gap hypothesis gives a clean injection from integer gaps into residue classes modulo \(p\).

However, distinct parameter residues do **not** imply linear independence of their associated vectors. Two elementary counterexamples make the logical gap clear.

### Constant family

Let

\[
\kappa(d)=v
\]

for a fixed nonzero vector \(v\). Distinct \(d\)'s give distinct residues but all vectors coincide.

### Two-dimensional moment family

Let

\[
\kappa(d)=(1,d)^T\in\mathbb F_p^2.
\]

Distinct \(d\)'s give distinct vectors, and every pair is independent, but any three vectors are automatically dependent because the ambient dimension is \(2\).

Hence even pairwise nonproportionality is insufficient when \(ho\ge3\). The needed statement is **joint** independence for the full family charged to the same \((I,p)\).

## 7. Dimension obstruction that must be checked

If \(M_I\) has \(n_I\) columns, then

\[
\dim\ker(M_I\bmod p)\le n_I.
\]

Therefore a scheme that charges one unit of valuation for each edge by demanding one new independent right-kernel direction can never prove

\[
\mu(I,p)>n_I.
\]

So before using this realization one must verify a uniform multiplicity cap

\[
\mu(I,p)\le n_I
\]

for every block and endpoint, or else modify the construction by increasing the matrix dimension, splitting the block into additional labels, assigning some edges to the other endpoint, or using a stronger \(p\)-adic mechanism than first-order mod-\(p\) corank.

The fact that there are only \(O_J(1)\) macroscopic blocks does not by itself solve this per-endpoint dimensional obstruction.

---

## 8. The carrier must be fixed over the whole block

For the factorization budget to work, \(C_I\) must be a **prescribed integer independent of the endpoint prime** after the block \(I\) (and the global scale parameters such as \(T,J\)) are fixed.

Thus one needs one integral matrix

\[
M_I\in\mathbb Z^{m_I\times n_I}
\]

whose reduction modulo every relevant endpoint prime is being tested.

It is not enough to build a matrix \(M_{I,p}\) after seeing \(p\), choose \(p\)-dependent rows/columns, normalize by \(p\)-dependent units, or clear denominators with \(p\)-dependent factors and then set

\[
C_{I,p}=\Delta(M_{I,p}).
\]

That would create one carrier per prime (or per edge), destroying the intended \(O_J(1)\)-carrier compression. A rational construction is fine only if it admits a **single \(p\)-independent integral model** whose reduction represents the desired relations at all relevant primes; primes dividing any fixed clearing denominator must then be handled explicitly.

This fixed-carrier requirement is conceptually separate from the full-spark theorem, but it is necessary for the stated counting reduction.

## 9. A separate quantitative requirement: height of the Smith carriers

Full column rank proves only

\[
C_I=\Delta_{n_I}(M_I)\ne0.
\]

It gives no useful upper bound on \(\log C_I\).

To turn

\[
|E_J|\log(T/7)\le\sum_I\log C_I
\]

into a strong edge bound, one still needs to prove that the right-hand side has the desired size. Since \(C_I\) divides every nonzero maximal minor, it is enough to exhibit one controlled nonzero maximal minor \(N_I\) and use

\[
C_I\le |\det N_I|,
\]

followed by a Hadamard/entry-height estimate.

If the matrices are fixed-size (for fixed \(J\)) and their entries have controlled polynomial height in \(T\), this may be straightforward. If the entries carry large Apéry numerators/denominators or grow exponentially in the scale, the height bound can itself erase the gain. This is not the same as the missing modular independence theorem, but it is required for the final quantitative payoff.

---

## 10. What singleton combinatorics can and cannot provide

The singleton hypothesis may ensure uniqueness of an edge in a local combinatorial configuration and may make the gap parameter \(d\) well-defined. It can also help prevent two edges from producing the **same** parameter.

But singletonness alone is not a linear-algebra theorem. To obtain

\[
\mu(I,p)\le\dim\ker(M_I\bmod p),
\]

one has to prove that **all** vectors arising from the \(\mu(I,p)\) edges are jointly independent. Showing only

- each vector is nonzero,
- different gaps give different residues,
- different edges give different vectors, or
- every pair of vectors is independent

is insufficient for multiplicities \(\ge3\).

The proof therefore needs an explicit determinant, Wronskian/Casoratian, Vandermonde-type factorization, generalized Vandermonde/Schur determinant, or another finite-field rank certificate whose nonvanishing is uniform over the admissible edge set.

## 12. Sharp formulation of the remaining P3.2 obligation

After stripping away the tautological parts, the reduction needs the following package.

For each of \(O_J(1)\) macroscopic blocks \(I\), construct a fixed full-column-rank integer matrix \(M_I\) of controlled height and a canonical map assigning to every singleton neighbor gap \(d\) at endpoint prime \(p\) a right-kernel vector

\[
\kappa_I(d;p)\in\ker(M_I\bmod p).
\]

Then prove, uniformly in all admissible \(p\) and all admissible distinct gaps \(d_1,\ldots,d_\rho\) charged to \((I,p)\), that

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}
[\kappa_I(d_1;p)\ \cdots\ \kappa_I(d_\rho;p)]
=\rho.
}
\]

Equivalently, prove an explicit selected minor is a \(p\)-unit.

Once that theorem is available, the rest is formal:

\[
\rho
\le\operatorname{corank}_p(M_I)
\le v_p(\Delta_{n_I}(M_I)),
\]

so the determinant divisor supplies exactly the multiplicity budget needed by the carrier lemma.

Without that finite-field full-spark theorem, the Smith proposal merely says:

> *if the edges already give independent modular relations, then Smith theory records their number in an integer valuation.*

That statement is correct but does not establish the independent modular relations. **That is the exact non-tautological arithmetic input still missing.**
