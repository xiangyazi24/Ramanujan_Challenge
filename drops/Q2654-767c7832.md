# Verdict

The linear-height endpoint intersection gives a much sharper quotient sieve than the one-height gate, but it does **not** yield an unconditional `o_q(n)` height bound from binomial-row gcds + Newton--Lucas alone.

There are two separate conclusions.

* First, with the upper endpoint exactly as stated,
  \[
  H_0\le K\le U:=\left\lfloor\frac n{q+1}\right\rfloor,
  \]
  the claimed **exact** candidate-radical preservation is false at one sharp boundary prime.  The already-audited example
  \[
  q=3,\qquad n=289,\qquad p=73
  \]
  is a genuine `q=3` Apéry-zero target, but the height `K=72=p-1` kills it because neither endpoint binomial is divisible by 73.  This is only an `O(log n)` defect in logarithmic height, but it must be repaired in any exact theorem.

* Second, after allowing that one boundary defect, the large-prime support of the stated capped endpoint rectangle can be classified exactly.  The full linear `K`-window collapses every survivor to only two block types:
  \[
  \boxed{\lfloor n/p\rfloor\in\{1,\ldots,q\}}
  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
  \boxed{\lfloor n/p\rfloor=2q+1.}
  \]
  The second possibility is not the whole `2q+1` slice: it is a very thin **reflected low-zero wedge**.  Nevertheless its containing prime interval has positive linear length for fixed `q`, and there is a concrete genuine survivor (`q=1,n=289,p=73`).  Newton--Lucas does not remove it.  Thus proving its logarithmic mass `o(n)` is a new moving Apéry-zero statement, not a consequence of the binomial-row intersection.

The good news is that **all powers and all genuinely certificate-only nuisance are harmless**.  With the current `Lambda_n` cap, the total contribution from `p <= sqrt(2n)` is `O(sqrt(n))`; every larger surviving prime occurs with exponent at most two, the future seam with exponent one, and the only large Newton ghost is a divisor of the fixed integer `8q+7`.

So the exact output of this attack is a clean support theorem plus an exact obstruction, not an unconditional sublinear bound.

# 2. First correction: the stated upper endpoint loses a real target

Put

\[
U=\left\lfloor\frac n{q+1}\right\rfloor.
\]

Take the Q2648 sharp example

\[
q=3,\qquad n=289,\qquad p=73.
\]

Then

\[
289=3\cdot73+70,
\qquad r=70,
\qquad r^*=2,
\qquad b_2=73,
\]
so 73 is a genuine `q=3` lower-digit target by reflection.  Also

\[
H_0=40,
\qquad
U=72=p-1.
\]

At the top endpoint `K=72`, the binomial index is

\[
k=K+1=73=p.
\]

Lucas gives

\[
\binom{289}{73}
\equiv \binom31\binom{70}{0}
\equiv3\pmod{73},
\tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
\binom{289+73}{73}
=\binom{362}{73}
\equiv \binom41\binom{70}{0}
\equiv4\pmod{73}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus neither endpoint gcd at `K=72` contains 73, regardless of `Lambda_n`, `L_K`, or the Newton values.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{73\nmid \widehat E_{289,3}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

So the stated rectangle does not have exact fixed-`q` candidate radical.

This defect is extremely localized.  A `q`-slice prime always satisfies

\[
p\ge U+1.
\]

All targets with `p>U+1` survive the binomial window; only the single possible prime

\[
p_\partial=U+1
\tag{2.4}
\]
can be deleted by the top height.  Hence the difference between the desired target radical and the target part actually retained by the stated rectangle has logarithm at most

\[
\log(U+1)=O(\log n)=o(n).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus this off-by-one is fatal for an **exact radical identity**, but harmless for the desired sublinear asymptotic if it is explicitly separated.

The lowest-churn exact repair is either:

```text
(a) stop the endpoint-carrier rectangle at K=U-1,
```

or

```text
(b) keep K=U but multiply back the explicitly tested boundary factor U+1
    when U+1 is prime and is a genuine target.
```

Repair (a) enlarges the future seam slightly, so for the height attack below it does not solve the real obstruction.

# 3. Power cap: all `p <= sqrt(2n)` are already `o(n)`

Let

\[
E_K^+=\gcd\!\left(\Lambda_n,L_K,B_K(n),\binom n{K+1}\right),
\]

\[
E_K^-=\gcd\!\left(\Lambda_n,L_K,B_K(-n-1),
                         \binom{n+K+1}{K+1}\right),
\]

and

\[
\widehat E_{n,q}=\gcd_{H_0\le K\le U}(E_K^+E_K^-).
\]

Because each sign factor is capped by `Lambda_n`,

\[
v_p(\widehat E_{n,q})
\le 2v_p(\Lambda_n)
\le2\left\lfloor\log_p n\right\rfloor.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Therefore, with `Y=sqrt(2n)`, Chebyshev's bound `pi(Y) << Y/log Y` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{p\le Y}v_p(\widehat E_{n,q})\log p
&\le
2\sum_{p\le Y}\left\lfloor\log_p n\right\rfloor\log p\\
&\le2\pi(Y)\log n\\
&=O(\sqrt n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is already the full prime-power contribution, not merely a radical bound.

Hence it is enough to classify

\[
p>\sqrt{2n}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For such a prime,

\[
p^2>2n>n+K+1,
\]
for every legal `K` once `n` is nontrivial, so there are only two base-`p` digits and no hidden second-digit carry.  Also

\[
v_p(\Lambda_n)=1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus each sign contributes exponent at most one and

\[
\boxed{v_p(\widehat E_{n,q})\le2\qquad(p>\sqrt{2n}).}
\tag{3.5}
\]

# 4. The full `K`-window forces one `p`-block

m=2q+1,

\[
k=K+1

\[
A\le k\le B.

\[
n=tp+r,

k=ap+h,

0\le r,h<p,

\[
r^*=p-1-r,
\qquad
s=\min(r,r^*).
\tag{4.3}

\[
h>r\quad\text{or}\quad h>r^*,

\boxed{A-ap>s.}
\tag{4.6}

This is the exact benefit of taking the gcd over the whole linear height window.

# 5. Exact quotient collapse: only `a=0` or `a=1`

The lower cutoff has a bounded defect.  Write

\[
n-q=mH_0+e,
\qquad 0\le e<m.
\]
Since `A=H_0+1`,

\[
n=mA+\delta,
\qquad
\delta=e-q-1,
\qquad
-q-1\le\delta\le q-1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Assume (4.5).

From `A>ap`,

\[
n=mA+\delta
\ge m(ap+1)-(q+1)
=map+q>map,
\]
so

\[
t=\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor\ge ma.
\tag{5.2}
\]

From `B<(a+1)p` and `B=U+1`,

\[
U\le(a+1)p-2.
\]
Thus

\[
n<(q+1)((a+1)p-1),
\]
and in particular

\[
t\le(q+1)(a+1)-1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Combining (5.2)--(5.3),

\[
(2q+1)a\le(q+1)(a+1)-1.
\]
Equivalently

\[
q(a-1)\le0.
\]
Hence

\[
\boxed{a\le1.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

There are now only two cases.

## Case I: `a=0`

Then

\[
B<p,
\]
so every height in the rectangle is below `p`, and (5.3) gives

\[
0\le t\le q.
\]
Since a prime dividing the cap has `p<=n`, `t=0` is absent.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{1\le t\le q.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The survival inequality is simply

\[
\boxed{s<A.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

## Case II: `a=1`

Then (5.2)--(5.3) become

\[
m\le t\le2(q+1)-1=m.
\]
Therefore

\[
\boxed{t=m=2q+1.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

There are no other large-prime quotient classes.

This is the sharp improvement over the one-height Q2643 gate: a positive-density family of residues modulo `2q+1` has collapsed to the finite set

\[
\boxed{\{1,2,\ldots,q,\ 2q+1\}.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

# 6. `a=0`: no Newton ghost at all

Assume `a=0`, so `k<p` throughout the window.

At the lower endpoint `k=A`, the active sign is the one corresponding to the folded digit `s=min(r,r^*)`, because `A>s`.  On that active gate, Q2643 with `a=0` gives

\[
B_K(n)\equiv b_rB_0(t)=b_r\pmod p
\tag{6.1}
\]
for the direct sign, or

\[
B_K(-n-1)\equiv b_{r^*}B_0(-t-1)=b_{r^*}\pmod p
\tag{6.2}
\]
for the reflected sign.

Since

\[
B_0=1,
\]
there is **no lower-scale Newton multiplier** and therefore no certificate-only ghost.

Moreover the active folded node is already among the interpolation nodes:

\[
s\le A-1=H_0\le K,
\]
so `p|b_s` implies `p|L_K` for every height.  Reflection identifies the two zero conditions.

Thus, for every sufficiently large `p` in Case I,

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid\widehat E_{n,q}
 \iff
 \left\{
 \begin{array}{l}
 1\le t=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\le q,\\
 s=\min(r,p-1-r)<A,\\
 p\mid b_s,
 \end{array}
 \right.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
except for the single top-boundary prime `p=B`, which does not satisfy the strict one-block condition and is killed as in Section 2.

For the desired `q`-slice, Q2648 gives `s<=H_0=A-1` automatically.  Therefore **every q-slice target with `p>B` survives**, and the only desired target possibly lost is `p=B=U+1`.

For `t<q`, (6.3) is genuine earlier-slice contamination, but only when the folded zero lies below the current lower height `H_0`.

The Q2648 example

\[
q=3,\qquad n=75,\qquad p=73
\]
is already such a survivor:

\[
t=1,\qquad s=2,\qquad H_0=10,\qquad b_2=73.
\]

So earlier-slice pollution is not a proof artifact.

# 7. `a=1`: exact future seam and the fixed multiplier `8q+7`

Now assume `a=1`.  Put

\[
h_0=A-p>0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

We know `t=m`.  From (5.1),

\[
mp+r=n=mA+\delta=m(p+h_0)+\delta,
\]
so

\[
r=mh_0+\delta.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Since `h_0>=1` and `delta>=-q-1`,

\[
r-h_0
=(m-1)h_0+\delta
=2qh_0+\delta
\ge q-1\ge0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

But survival requires `h_0>s`.  Hence the folded digit cannot be `r`; necessarily

\[
\boxed{s=r^*=p-1-r<h_0.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

So the seam is always carried by the **reflected** branch at the lowest height.

Because `t=m` and `r=p-1-s`,

\[
n=mp+p-1-s=(m+1)p-1-s.
\tag{7.5}
\]

The upper one-block condition `B<2p` is now exact.  Since `m+1=2(q+1)`,

\[
U
=\left\lfloor\frac{(m+1)p-1-s}{q+1}\right\rfloor
=2p-\left\lceil\frac{s+1}{q+1}\right\rceil.
\]
Hence

\[
B=U+1<2p
\iff
\left\lceil\frac{s+1}{q+1}\right\rceil\ge2
\iff
\boxed{s\ge q+1.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

For the lower condition, (7.5) gives

\[
H_0
=p+\left\lfloor\frac{p-1-s-q}{m}\right\rfloor,
\]
so

\[
h_0=A-p
=\left\lceil\frac{p-s-q}{m}\right\rceil.
\]
Then `h_0>s` is equivalent to

\[
p-s-q>ms,
\]
i.e.

\[
\boxed{(m+1)s<p-q.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Therefore the future seam geometry is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor=2q+1,\\
s=p-1-(n-(2q+1)p),\\
q+1\le s,\\
(2q+2)s<p-q.
\end{gathered}}
\tag{7.8}
\]

On the reflected gate, Q2643 gives

\[
B_K(-n-1)
\equiv b_s B_1(-m-1)\pmod p.
\tag{7.9}
\]
Using (1.1),

\[
B_1(-m-1)
=1+4(-m-1)
=-(4m+3)
=-(8q+7).
\tag{7.10}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
B_K(-n-1)\equiv-(8q+7)b_s\pmod p
}
\tag{7.11}
\]
for **every height** in the seam.

Consequently, outside the fixed prime set dividing `8q+7`, the seam has an exact converse:

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid\widehat E_{n,q}
 \iff
 \left\{
 \begin{array}{l}
 \lfloor n/p\rfloor=2q+1,\\
 q+1\le s,\\
 (2q+2)s<p-q,\\
 p\mid b_s.
 \end{array}
 \right.
}
\tag{7.12}
\]

The only certificate-only seam ghost can come from

\[
p\mid 8q+7.
\tag{7.13}
\]
To survive the full integer gcd it must additionally divide the cap and the relevant `L_K`, but in any event its total logarithmic mass is `O_q(1)`.  For `p>sqrt(2n)` it disappears entirely once `n>(8q+7)^2/2`.

This also explains why a fixed small factor such as 5 can remain in the `q=1` numerical coordinate without representing a large moving obstruction: for `q=1`,

\[
8q+7=15.
\]

# 9. Exact large-prime support theorem

Define

\[
\mathcal A_{n,q}
=\left\{
 p>\sqrt{2n}:
 \begin{array}{l}
 1\le t=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\le q,\\
 s=\min(r,p-1-r)<A,\\
 p\mid b_s,\\
 p\ne U+1
 \end{array}
 \right\},
\tag{9.1}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal W_{n,q}
=\left\{
 p>\sqrt{2n}:
 \begin{array}{l}
 t=2q+1,\\
 s=p-1-r,\\
 q+1\le s,\\
 (2q+2)s<p-q,\\
 p\mid b_s
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Then, for fixed `q` and all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Supp}_{p>\sqrt{2n}}(\widehat E_{n,q})
=\mathcal A_{n,q}\ \dot\cup\ \mathcal W_{n,q}.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

For finite `n`, add only the fixed seam-ghost subset of primes dividing `8q+7`.

There are no other large-prime survivors.

For `p in A_{n,q}`, the exponent is determined at the lowest height:

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\widehat E_{n,q})
 =\mathbf1_{A>r}+\mathbf1_{A>r^*}
 \in\{1,2\}.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

For `p in W_{n,q}`, only the reflected branch is active at the lowest height, hence

\[
\boxed{v_p(\widehat E_{n,q})=1.}
\tag{9.5}
\]

For the desired `q`-slice, Q2648's exact folded-node identities imply that both signs are active at the lowest height only at the midpoint

\[
r=r^*=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]
Equivalently

\[
2n+1=(2q+1)p.
\]
Thus a desired large target has exponent

\[
\boxed{
1+\mathbf1_{\,2n+1=(2q+1)p},
}
\tag{9.6}
\]
apart from the deleted top-boundary prime `p=U+1`.

So powers are completely controlled: small-prime powers have total `O(sqrt(n))`; all moving large primes are radical except for at most the one central q-slice prime, which can be squared.

# 10. Logarithmic decomposition

Let

\[
R_q(n)=
\prod_{\substack{p\text{ prime}\\\lfloor n/p\rfloor=q\\p\mid b_{n-qp}}}p
\]
be the lower-digit fixed-`q` target radical, with reflection understood as in Q2648.

Let `N_{<q}(n)` denote the weighted logarithmic mass of the `t<q` part of `A_{n,q}`, and let

\[
W_q(n)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal W_{n,q}}\log p.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Then the support theorem and the power cap give

\[
\boxed{
\log\widehat E_{n,q}
=\log R_q(n)
 +N_{<q}(n)
 +W_q(n)
 +O_q(\sqrt n+\log n).
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

Here:

* the `O(sqrt(n))` term contains **all** powers from small primes;
* the `O(log n)` term absorbs the possible deleted boundary target `p=U+1` and the possible extra midpoint copy;
* the fixed `8q+7` ghost is `O_q(1)`;
* `N_{<q}` is genuine earlier-slice Apéry-zero mass, not a scalar/certificate artifact;
* `W_q` is genuine future-slice Apéry-zero mass in the exact wedge (9.2).

Equation (10.2) is the sharp reduction supplied by the capped endpoint rectangle.

It does **not** prove the desired `o(n)` statement because neither `N_{<q}` nor `W_q` is currently known unconditionally to be `o(n)` from Lucas/Kummer alone.  For `q=1`, the earlier term vanishes identically, leaving the especially clean obstruction

\[
\boxed{
\log\widehat E_{n,1}
=\log R_1(n)+W_1(n)+O(\sqrt n+\log n).
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

So even the first slice reduces to the `q'=3` low-zero wedge rather than closing.

# 11. Why binomial-row gcds cannot provide the missing `o(n)` bound

This is the decisive no-go for the proposed mechanism.

Consider the pure row product gcd

\[
D_{n,q}
=\gcd_{H_0\le K\le U}
\left(
\binom n{K+1}
\binom{n+K+1}{K+1}
\right).
\tag{11.1}
\]

For every prime in the q-slice with

\[
p>U+1,
\]
Q2648 gives `s<=H_0`, hence for every `k=K+1>=A` at least one of the direct/reflected carries occurs.  Therefore

\[
p\mid D_{n,q}.
\]

Only the single possible boundary prime `p=U+1` is lost.  Consequently

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}D_{n,q}
\ge
\vartheta\!\left(\frac nq\right)
-\vartheta\!\left(\frac n{q+1}\right)
-O(\log n).
\tag{11.2}
\]

By the prime number theorem,

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}D_{n,q}
\ge
\left(\frac1q-\frac1{q+1}+o(1)\right)n
=\left(\frac1{q(q+1)}+o(1)\right)n.
}
\tag{11.3}
\]

So the gcd of the binomial rows itself has **linear** logarithmic height.  There is no hidden sublinear gain in the row intersection.

Newton--Lucas then does exactly one thing on the desired slice: it replaces “all primes in the q-slice” by

\[
p\mid b_s.
\]
But that is precisely the original fixed-slice zero condition.  Since `s<=H_0<=K`, the `L_K` factor is automatic for a true zero at every height.  Hence the `K`-gcd provides no further varying congruence on a q-slice target.

Thus an argument of the form

```text
long K-window -> small gcd of binomial rows -> small Ehat
```

cannot work: the middle assertion is false by (11.3).

Any proof that nevertheless establishes `log(Ehat)=o(n)` must use additional arithmetic about the Apéry zeros themselves; it would not be a consequence of the binomial-row gcd geometry.

---

# 12. The future wedge itself has linear ambient prime mass

The seam is narrow, but not asymptotically negligible for fixed `q` by interval length alone.

In the seam,

\[
s=(m+1)p-1-n.
\tag{12.1}
\]
The lower condition `s>=q+1` gives

\[
p\ge\frac{n+q+2}{m+1}.
\tag{12.2}
\]

The upper condition `(m+1)s<p-q` gives

\[
((m+1)^2-1)p<(m+1)(n+1)-q.
\]
Since

\[
(m+1)^2-1=m(m+2),
\]
we obtain

\[
p<\frac{(m+1)(n+1)-q}{m(m+2)}.
\tag{12.3}
\]

The length of this interval is

\[
\left(
\frac{m+1}{m(m+2)}-\frac1{m+1}
\right)n+O_q(1)
=
\boxed{\frac{n}{m(m+1)(m+2)}+O_q(1).}
\tag{12.4}
\]

For fixed `q`, this is a positive constant times `n`.  PNT therefore gives a positive linear ambient Chebyshev mass.  Hence neither the prime interval geometry nor Chebyshev can prove

\[
W_q(n)=o(n).
\]

One genuinely needs sparsity of the condition

\[
p\mid b_s
\]
in this moving low-zero wedge.

That statement is weaker than the full `2q+1`-slice problem, but it is still an unproved moving-zero statement in the current repository.  The example `(q,n,p,s)=(1,289,73,2)` proves that the wedge is nonempty for genuine Apéry zeros.

# 13. Check against `n=200,q=1`

For the reported example,

\[
q=1,\qquad m=3,
\qquad H_0=66,
\qquad U=100,
\]
so

\[
k\in[67,101].
\]

The large `a=0` survivors are exactly the `t=1` target zeros, apart from a possible top boundary prime.  The reported target factors are

\[
139,\qquad181.
\]

The only possible large future-seam prime lies in the `t=3` wedge.  Here

\[
s=4p-201,
\]
and (7.8) leaves only the tiny lower-edge possibility around `p=53`; in fact the Apéry recurrence gives

\[
b_{11}\equiv43\pmod{53},
\]
so 53 is not a true seam zero.

The remaining factor 5 is small and therefore belongs to the already harmless capped-prime part.  It is also consistent with the fixed seam multiplier

\[
8q+7=15.
\]

Thus

\[
\widehat E_{200,1}=5\cdot139\cdot181
\]
is perfectly consistent with the support theorem.  It is good evidence for the coordinate, but it does not test either the sharp target boundary or a genuine moving future seam.

The pair `n=289,p=73` tests both and shows exactly where the apparent closure breaks.

# 14. What is proved, and what remains

The capped endpoint rectangle does prove the following unconditional statement.

> **Capped endpoint rectangle sieve.**  Fix `q`.  Up to logarithmic mass `O_q(sqrt(n)+log n)`, every prime divisor of `widehat E_(n,q)` is a genuine Apéry zero prime in one of only two classes:
>
> 1. an earlier-or-current quotient `1<=floor(n/p)<=q`, with folded zero `s<H_0+1`; or
> 2. the single future quotient `floor(n/p)=2q+1`, in the reflected wedge
>    \[
>    q+1\le s,\qquad (2q+2)s<p-q.
>    \]
>
> Conversely every such large true-zero prime satisfying the one-block geometry survives all heights, except for the single possible desired boundary prime `p=U+1`.
>
> All large exponents are at most two; the future wedge has exponent one; all small-prime powers have total logarithmic mass `O(sqrt(n))`.

What is **not** proved is the requested conclusion that the non-`q` terms have `o(n)` mass.

The exact remaining quantities are

\[
N_{<q}(n)
\]
from earlier low-zero slices and

\[
W_q(n)
\]
from the `2q+1` low-zero wedge.  The former becomes `o(n)` if one already knows the corresponding earlier fixed-slice bounds; the latter remains even for `q=1` and is not discharged by any current Lucas/Kummer/binomial-row theorem.

Therefore the smallest honest theorem at this coordinate is the decomposition (10.2), not `log widehat E=o_q(n)`.

A genuine closure would require one new input of the form

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{
 p:\ \lfloor n/p\rfloor=2q+1\\
 q+1\le s<(p-q)/(2q+2)\\
 p\mid b_s}}
\log p=o_q(n),
}
\tag{14.1}
\]

together with the corresponding already-smaller-slice induction for `t<q`.

Without (14.1), the capped endpoint rectangle is a very clean **finite-quotient reduction**, but not an unconditional sublinear-height proof.
