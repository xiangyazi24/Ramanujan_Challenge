S_{a,r}=a!(r+2)!.

* **refuted:** any recurrence-only Wronskian/Ore proof from the displayed six coordinates;

# 2. Exact local Smith/rank calculation

Consider an arbitrary seven-term vector

\[
X=(X_{r-4},X_{r-3},X_{r-2},X_{r-1},X_r,X_{r+1},X_{r+2}).
\]

Define

\[
\widetilde\Phi_r(X)
=
\bigl(
X_{r-1},X_r,
(\mathcal LX)_{r-2},(\mathcal LX)_{r-1},(\mathcal LX)_r,
(\mathcal LX)_{r+1},(\mathcal LX)_{r+2}
\bigr).
\]

After a row and column permutation, its matrix is triangular. Starting from \(X_{r-1},X_r\):

* \((\mathcal LX)_r\) solves for \(X_{r-2}\) with pivot \((r-1)^3\);
* \((\mathcal LX)_{r-1}\) solves for \(X_{r-3}\) with pivot \((r-2)^3\);
* \((\mathcal LX)_{r-2}\) solves for \(X_{r-4}\) with pivot \((r-3)^3\);
* \((\mathcal LX)_{r+1}\) solves for \(X_{r+1}\) with pivot \((r+1)^3\);
* \((\mathcal LX)_{r+2}\) solves for \(X_{r+2}\) with pivot \((r+2)^3\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\det\widetilde\Phi_r
=
\pm\bigl((r-3)(r-2)(r-1)(r+1)(r+2)\bigr)^3.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular, \(\widetilde\Phi_r\) is an isomorphism over

\[
R_r=\mathbb Z\left[\frac1{(r+2)!}\right]
\]

and over every \(\mathbb F_p\) with \(p>r+2\).

Remove the first output \(X_{r-1}\), and put

\[
\Phi_r(X)
=
\bigl(
X_r,(\mathcal LX)_{r-2},\ldots,(\mathcal LX)_{r+2}
\bigr).
\]

Then \(\Phi_r\) has rank \(6\) and a one-dimensional kernel. Its canonical generator \(U^{(r)}\) is defined by

\[
U^{(r)}_{r-1}=1,\qquad U^{(r)}_r=0,\qquad
(\mathcal LU^{(r)})_t=0\quad(r-2\le t\le r+2).
\tag{2.2}
\]

For example,

\[
U^{(r)}_{r-2}=\frac{P(r)}{(r-1)^3},
\qquad
U^{(r)}_{r+1}=-\frac{r^3}{(r+1)^3},
\]

and all remaining entries are obtained uniquely by reversible recurrence steps. Every denominator is supported on primes at most \(r+2\).

Thus, at every safe prime,

\[
\boxed{
X_r=(\mathcal LX)_{r-2}=\cdots=(\mathcal LX)_{r+2}=0
\iff
X=\lambda U^{(r)}
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

for a unique \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_p\).

This is the exact local mechanism that survives the endpoint correction. The six displayed coordinates do not force the local sequence to vanish; they force it onto a companion line.

# 3. Exact Wronskian identity and its integration constant

Let \(b\) be the Apéry solution and define, for an arbitrary sequence \(X\),

\[
W_t(X,b)=X_tb_{t-1}-X_{t-1}b_t.
\]

Multiply \((\mathcal LX)_t\) by \(b_{t-1}\), subtract \(X_{t-1}(\mathcal Lb)_t=0\), and collect terms. This gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
t^3W_t(X,b)-(t-1)^3W_{t-1}(X,b)
=(\mathcal LX)_t b_{t-1}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For \(X=T_a\), this is

\[
t^3W_t(T_a,b)-(t-1)^3W_{t-1}(T_a,b)
=E_{a,t}b_{t-1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence five vanishing defects imply only that the weighted Wronskian is locally constant:

\[
(r-3)^3W_{r-3}
=(r-2)^3W_{r-2}
=\cdots
=(r+2)^3W_{r+2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

They do **not** imply that this constant is zero.

More globally, summing (3.1) gives the variation-of-constants formula

\[
\boxed{
v^3W_v
=u^3W_u+
\sum_{t=u+1}^{v}(\mathcal LX)_t b_{t-1}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus \(W_r\) retains a boundary value and all earlier defects. A five-term zero window has no access to that remote source memory.

At the central zero \(X_r=0\),

\[
W_r(X,b)=-X_{r-1}b_r.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For the normalized companion generator (2.2),

\[
\boxed{W_r(U^{(r)},b)=-b_r.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore, if \(p\nmid b_r\), the surviving companion line has nonzero Wronskian. In particular, over the localization where \(b_r\) is a unit, neither \(X_{r-1}\) nor \(W_r(X,b)\) belongs to the row span of the six local functionals in \(\Phi_r\): they evaluate nontrivially on \(U^{(r)}\), while all six generators vanish there.

This proves the promised Ore/Wronskian no-go.

# 4. Precise scope of the no-go

The conclusion is not that no identity involving the actual \(Q_a\) can exist. The conclusion is narrower and exact.

## 4.1 What is impossible

No identity obtained solely from the scalar recurrence operator \(\mathcal L\), its five shifts \(r-2,\ldots,r+2\), and the coordinate \(X_r\) can recover a nonzero multiple of \(X_{r-1}\) or the Wronskian \(W_r(X,b)\) after inverting \((r+2)!\).

Indeed, every such local Ore combination vanishes on \(U^{(r)}\), while

\[
U^{(r)}_{r-1}=1,\qquad
W_r(U^{(r)},b)=-b_r.
\]

At a non-target prime \(p\nmid b_r\), the latter is a unit. This rules out a recurrence-only identity whose right side becomes a unit whenever \(p>r+2\) and \(p\nmid b_r\).

Equivalently, the ideal generated by the six universal linear forms cuts out a line, not the origin. A nonzero constant or a \(b_r\)-unit cannot belong to that universal ideal.

## 4.2 What remains possible

A specialized identity for the single numerator \(Q_a\) can still exist if it imports additional data not implied by \(\mathcal L\), for example:

* a toric numerator relation satisfied by \(Q_a\);
* a second period or boundary functional that detects \(U^{(r)}\);
* a higher-order recurrence for the specific sequence \(T_a\), together with a nonvanishing observability determinant;
* an explicit integer Bézout identity among the six **evaluated integers**, proved by a separate arithmetic construction.

Such an argument would not be a consequence of the local Apéry recurrence alone. It would be exactly the missing Cartier-period rigidity theorem.

# 5. The exact arithmetic Bézout reformulation

Let

\[
G_{a,r}
=\gcd\bigl(T_{a,r},E_{a,r-2},E_{a,r-1},E_{a,r},E_{a,r+1},E_{a,r+2}\bigr),
\]

with \(G_{a,r}\ge0\). Define the \(b_r\)-saturated non-target part by

\[
G_{a,r}^{\mathrm{nt}}
=
\prod_{\ell\nmid b_r}\ell^{v_\ell(G_{a,r})}.
\]

The corrected assertion is

\[
\operatorname{rad}(G_{a,r}^{\mathrm{nt}})
\mid a!(r+2)!.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For \(G_{a,r}>0\), (5.1) is equivalent to

\[
\exists N\ge1:\qquad
G_{a,r}\mid\bigl(a!(r+2)!\,b_r\bigr)^N.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since the six displayed integers generate the principal ideal \((G_{a,r})\), (5.2) is equivalent to an integer identity

\[
\boxed{
\bigl(a!(r+2)!\,b_r\bigr)^N
=U_0T_{a,r}+\sum_{j=-2}^{2}U_jE_{a,r+j}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

One may equivalently write the left side as \(D_{a,r}b_r^e\), where every prime divisor of \(D_{a,r}\) is at most \(\max(a,r+2)\).

Three qualifications matter.

1. This is a **power** identity. Prime support alone gives no first-power valuation bound.
2. The abstract existence of the Bézout coefficients follows only after the desired divisibility has already been proved. It supplies no effective or uniform construction.
3. If all six integers are zero, then \(G_{a,r}=0\), and every prime divides the tuple. Any theorem must separately exclude this all-zero case.

Thus (5.3) is the correct exact target, but writing it down does not solve it.

# 8. The exact counterexample criterion for the actual \(Q_a\)

For a safe prime \(p>\max(a,r+2)\), reduce the seven local values of \(T_a\) modulo \(p\). By (2.3),

\[
p\mid G_{a,r}
\iff
(T_{a,r-4},\ldots,T_{a,r+2})
=\lambda U^{(r)}\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p^7
\tag{8.1}
\]

for some \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_p\).

If also \(p\nmid b_r\), this is a genuine non-target collision with the local companion line. When \(\lambda\ne0\), its Wronskian is

\[
W_r(T_a,b)=-\lambda b_r\ne0.

When \(\lambda=0\), all seven local \(T_a\)-values vanish, an even stronger degeneracy.

Thus the first genuine counterexample mechanism is completely explicit:

\[
\boxed{
\text{actual Cartier period }T_a
\text{ meets the companion line }\mathbb F_pU^{(r)}
\text{ at a prime }p\nmid b_r.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

I did not locate an exact proved instance of (8.2) for \(a\ge3\) in the current repository, and I do not infer its absence from finite scans.

# 9. What a successful \(Q_a\)-specific proof must add

The local Smith calculation identifies the missing coordinate exactly: it is \(T_{a,r-1}\), or equivalently the Wronskian

\[
W_r(T_a,b)=T_{a,r}b_{r-1}-T_{a,r-1}b_r.
\]

A useful proof must do more than show five defects vanish. It must control the integration constant in (3.4). There are several logically sufficient forms.

## 9.1 Direct power-Bézout certificate

Construct explicit integers \(N,U_0,U_j\) and a small-prime-supported \(D_{a,r}\) such that

\[
D_{a,r}b_r^N
=U_0T_{a,r}+\sum_{j=-2}^{2}U_jE_{a,r+j},
\qquad
\operatorname{rad}(D_{a,r})\mid a!(r+2)!.
\tag{9.1}
\]

This proves the corrected statement immediately.

## 9.2 Companion-coordinate observability

Produce a \(Q_a\)-specific functional \(\Xi_{a,r}\) satisfying both:

1. \(\Xi_{a,r}\) is controlled by the six displayed integers with only \(a!(r+2)!\)-denominators;
2. \(\Xi_{a,r}(U^{(r)})\) is a \(b_r\)-unit at every safe non-target prime.

No functional generated solely by the local scalar recurrence can have property 2.

## 9.3 A higher recurrence plus a nonvanishing determinant

If \(T_a\) satisfies a fixed \(Q_a\)-specific recurrence, then the six local equations can be augmented to an observability matrix. One must compute its determinant and prove that, after saturation, its prime support lies in \(a!(r+2)!b_r\). Merely knowing that \(T_a\) is holonomic does not prove the determinant is nonzero or smooth.

The current terminal-packet code confirms that weighted Cartier numerators retain toric boundary coordinates. That is why a naive reduction to the Apéry recurrence does not supply this determinant.

# 10. Consequence for the two-digit square-root barrier

Suppose the corrected statement were proved. In a two-digit decomposition

\[
n=ap+r,\qquad 0\le r<p,\qquad p>\sqrt n,
\]

one automatically has \(a<p\). For the present lemma one additionally needs

\[
a\ge3\text{ odd},\qquad r\le p-3,
\]

because \(p>r+2\). The quotient-one cell \(a=1\), all even quotient cells, and the endpoint residues \(r=p-2,p-1\) remain outside the statement.

Inside its valid range, the lemma would imply:

\[
p\mid G_{a,r},\quad p>\max(a,r+2)
\Longrightarrow p\mid b_r.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Thus every large **non-target** prime would be removed from this carrier. This is valuable as a false-positive sieve.

But (10.1) leaves every target prime \(p\mid b_r\) untouched. Those are exactly the primes that drive the two-digit Apéry zero problem. Therefore the lemma alone does not bound the target radical, does not give a pointwise target count, and does not cross the square-root barrier. A second carrier that remains informative on the fiber \(b_r=0\), or an independent bound on the mass of target primes, is still required.

# Final adjudication

The corrected \(a\ge3\) prime-support statement is not established by the scalar Apéry recurrence, and the desired local Wronskian/Ore proof cannot exist in that recurrence-only form. The exact obstruction is the one-dimensional companion kernel (2.2). The first repaired numerator \(Q_3\) is primitive but already has a genuine toric boundary component, so excluding \(a=1\) does not remove the obstruction.

The remaining arithmetic question is sharply formulated by (5.3) or, equivalently, by exclusion of the actual companion-line collision (8.2). A proof must be \(Q_a\)-specific; a counterexample must exhibit such a collision at a safe prime not dividing \(b_r\). I find neither in the current exact repository state, and I do not promote finite scans to a theorem.
