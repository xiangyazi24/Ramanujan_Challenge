# Function-field model of the Apéry GCD conjecture

## Executive conclusion

The function-field experiment is a very good strategic probe. It is not a literal automatic solution of the number-field problem, but it isolates exactly which parts of the roadmap are genuinely geometric and which parts depend on arithmetic of primes in characteristic zero.

The strongest conclusion is:

* A function-field analogue is natural and should be provable in many formulations.
* Deligne theory does remove much of the Stage C ``randomness'' problem, because all primes are geometric points of one fixed curve.
* However, the proposed model needs correction: the Apéry numbers are constants, not polynomials in a new variable t, unless one introduces a non-isotrivial parameter family. The correct analogue is usually a recurrence over F_q (or over a parameter curve), not simply replacing integers by polynomials.
* Mason-Stothers helps with gcds of polynomial values, but it does not directly prove the Apéry gcd statement.

A publishable theorem is plausible, but the clean statement is likely a geometric variant rather than the literal definition in the question.

## 1. Does the recurrence make sense over F_q(t)?

Yes. The recurrence

\[
(d+1)^3y_{d+1}=(2d+1)(17d^2+17d+5)y_d-d^3y_{d-1}
\]

is defined over any field whose characteristic does not destroy the coefficients.

The two standard solutions are the same formal Apéry solutions:

\[
b_0=1,\quad b_1=5,
\]

and

\[
a_0=0,\quad a_1=6.
\]

Over characteristic zero they are integer/rational sequences. Over F_q they reduce modulo q.

However, they are not naturally elements of F_q[t]. They are elements of F_q. To obtain a genuine F_q(t) family one needs to introduce a parameter into the recurrence or into the underlying Picard-Fuchs family.

Therefore the object

\[
b_d(t)\in F_q[t]
\]

is not canonical.

## 2. Does Deligne eliminate Stage C?

Partially, but not completely.

For a prime polynomial P(t), the condition

\[
P(t)\mid b_d
\]

is equivalent to a Frobenius condition in a finite extension. In a genuine geometric family, such conditions are controlled by Frobenius conjugacy classes.

This is exactly the setting of:

* Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formulas;
* Deligne's Weil II bounds;
* Katz's monodromy methods.

Hence a correlation such as

\[
\sum_{x\in X(F_q)}\chi_1(\operatorname{Frob}_x)\chi_2(\operatorname{Frob}_x)
\]

can often be bounded by O(q^{1/2}).

This is the function-field analogue of the missing cross-prime independence.

But the Apéry Stage C problem involves moving congruence conditions indexed by primes and quotient cells. Deligne handles geometric independence after a sheaf has been constructed; it does not automatically construct the required sheaf.

The bottleneck changes from:

"prove independence of many primes"

to:

"identify the correct l-adic representation whose Frobenius traces encode the Apéry zero conditions."

## 3. Analogue of |Z_P|

For a prime polynomial P of degree m, the analogue is

\[
Z_P=\{d\in F_{q^m}: b_d=0\}.
\]

The relevant variable is now a point of a finite field extension.

If the Apéry recurrence is interpreted as a rank-two l-adic local system, then b_d=0 is an intersection of Frobenius orbits with a hyperplane in a two-dimensional representation.

One expects bounds from monodromy:

\[
|Z_P|\approx q^m/|G|
\]

for large monodromy groups, with square-root error terms.

A uniform boundedness statement analogous to empirical |Z_p|=O(1) is much stronger and is not expected automatically. The function-field setting gives equidistribution, not necessarily bounded zero fibers.

## 4. Mason-Stothers

Mason-Stothers says that for coprime polynomials A,B,C over a function field with

\[
A+B=C,
\]

one has

\[
\max(\deg A,\deg B,\deg C)\leq \deg\operatorname{rad}(ABC)-1.
\]

This is the function-field ABC theorem.

It is useful for proving that polynomial identities cannot have too many repeated factors.

The Apéry gcd problem, however, concerns common factors of two sequences of values:

\[
gcd(d_n^3a_n,d_n^3b_n).
\]

The obstruction is that Mason-Stothers controls additive polynomial relations, while the Apéry gcd is a divisibility problem in a recurrence.

It may help with a derived identity, but it is not a direct proof.

## 5. Function-field Wronskian

The characteristic-zero identity is

\[
a_nb_{n+1}-a_{n+1}b_n=-\frac6{(n+1)^3}.
\]

This comes from the Casoratian of the second-order recurrence.

The same calculation works over any characteristic not dividing the relevant constants:

\[
W_n=a_nb_{n+1}-a_{n+1}b_n
\]

satisfies

\[
W_{n+1}=\frac{n^3}{(n+2)^3}W_n,
\]

so

\[
W_n=-\frac6{(n+1)^3}.
\]

This identity remains a major structural constraint in positive characteristic.

## 6. Is the function-field theorem publishable?

Yes, but the theorem should probably be formulated geometrically.

A realistic theorem would be:

For a non-isotrivial Apéry Picard-Fuchs family over a curve C/F_q, the gcd of numerator and denominator sections has degree o(n), or the bad Frobenius parameters have density zero with explicit bounds.

The proof architecture would be:

1. Construct the l-adic local system attached to the Apéry family.
2. Prove geometric monodromy is large.
3. Apply Deligne/Katz equidistribution.
4. Use local factor bounds to control the gcd.

This would indeed be analogous in spirit to function-field results preceding number-field analogues, such as the philosophy behind Ailon-Rudnick.

## 7. What can go wrong?

### Characteristic problems

The recurrence degenerates when the characteristic divides denominators or the constants appearing in the recurrence. In small characteristics:

* the rank can drop;
* singularities collide;
* monodromy can become smaller.

One must exclude finitely many characteristics.

### Isotriviality

If the family is constant, Frobenius distributions do not provide randomness. The whole advantage comes from a genuinely varying geometric family.

### Wrong model

The literal substitution Z -> F_q(t) is misleading. The primes of F_q[t] do not replace ordinary primes while leaving Apéry numbers unchanged. A successful model needs a parameterized motive.

## Final assessment

The function-field model is a high-value direction. It probably does not prove the original Apéry GCD conjecture, but it can validate the four-stage roadmap.

The likely outcome:

* Stage A (local system): becomes explicit.
* Stage B (monodromy): likely accessible.
* Stage C (cross-prime independence): becomes a Deligne/Katz equidistribution statement.
* Stage D (moments/sieve): becomes a geometric sieve problem.

The main correction is conceptual: the right object is not "Apéry numbers in F_q(t)", but "a non-isotrivial Apéry motive over a function field." A rigorous theorem in that setting would be a credible standalone research result and a strong test of the number-field strategy.
