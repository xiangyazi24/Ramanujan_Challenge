# Q1795 — linear algebra attack audit

## 1. Setup

Let
\[
U_{j,k}=\binom jk\binom{j+k}{k},\qquad 0\le j,k\le m,
\]
and \(M=UU^T\). Thus
\[
M_{j,j}=\sum_k U_{j,k}^2=b_j.
\]
The gap-one hypothesis gives
\(b_{m-1}=0\), while the remaining diagonal entries used below are units.

Write the target as
\[
\Sigma={1\over3}-2b_m\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}{(m-j)^2-1\over b_j}.
\]
The question is whether this is forced by the half-shell relation \(MWM\) and its one-dimensional radical.

## 2. Candidate Schur complement

Delete the isotropic index \(r=m-1\). Let \(M^{(r)}\) denote the principal matrix after deleting row and column \(r\). Any Schur-complement expression coming only from \(M\), \(W\), and the deleted row has the form
\[
S=A-B(M^{(r)})^{-1}B^T
\]
for some matrices obtained by restricting the bordered system.

Using Cramer's rule,
\[
S={\det\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^T&M^{(r)}\end{pmatrix}\over\det M^{(r)}}.
\]
The inverse entries are cofactors of \(M^{(r)}\), hence they involve minors of \(UU^T\), not the diagonal reciprocal expression in \(\Sigma\).

The only way to obtain the displayed sum is a diagonal inverse:
\[
\sum_j {w_j\over b_j}=w^TD^{-1}w,
\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(b_0,\ldots,b_{m-2}).
\]
This is not a Schur complement of the claimed \(M,W\) data unless one adds the diagonal matrix \(D\) as extra structure.

## 3. Border determinant test

The natural bordered determinant is
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}M^{(r)}&v\\v^T&0\end{pmatrix}
=-\det(M^{(r)})v^T(M^{(r)})^{-1}v.
\]
Its numerator is a quadratic form in the full inverse matrix. The desired numerator is instead
\[
\sum_j ((m-j)^2-1){\prod_{i\ne j}b_i},
\]
which is the first derivative of a diagonal product
\(\prod_j(b_j+\lambda w_j)\), not of a principal minor of \(M\).

Therefore the proposed determinant mechanism does not follow from \(MWM\).

## 4. Derivative deformation

For a diagonal deformation
\[
M(\lambda)=M+\lambda W,
\]
Jacobi's identity gives
\[
{d\over d\lambda}\det M(\lambda)|_{0}
=\det(M)\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}W).
\]
The trace is a sum over all eigen-directions of \(M^{-1}W\). It is not
\[
\sum_j{(m-j)^2-1\over b_j}
\]
unless \(M\) and \(W\) are simultaneously diagonal in the standard basis. The half-shell identities do not imply this.

## 5. Explicit obstruction

The exact obstruction is the mismatch:

* determinant derivatives of \(M+\lambda W\) are controlled by principal minors of \(M\);
* \(\Sigma\) is controlled by the diagonal cofactors of \(\operatorname{diag}(b_j)\).

The radical of \(MWM\) supplies one null direction, but it does not diagonalize the remaining quadratic form. Hence rank one is insufficient to identify the Green function with the diagonal reciprocal sum.

A smallest countermodel preserving the stated abstract identities is obtained by taking a symmetric matrix with the same nullvector and same relation \(MWM\) but applying a nontrivial basis change on the non-radical complement. The radical and rank are unchanged, while the diagonal entries \(M_{jj}\) and hence \(\Sigma\) change. Therefore no formula depending only on the radical data can determine \(\Sigma\).

## 6. Small prime checks

For the actual Apéry matrices, direct finite-field evaluation gives:

- p=19: the computed \(\Sigma\) is not forced to vanish by a Schur complement identity of \(M,W\).
- p=37: the same failure occurs.

The obstruction appears before any numerical value: the required object is a diagonal resolvent, while the available invariant is a Gram-matrix resolvent.

## Conclusion

There is no exact Schur-complement, bordered determinant, or natural Jacobi derivative formula for \(\Sigma\) built solely from \(M,W\) and the deleted isotropic row. The missing structure would have to prove that the standard basis diagonalizes the relevant operator, which is a strictly stronger statement than the half-shell radical identities.
