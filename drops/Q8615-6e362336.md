# Q8615: fibre bound audit for regular Apéry shell triples

## 1. Status of the proposed reduction

The reduction to the finite fibres J_X(s,d) is algebraically correct, but the requested all-X estimate
\[
\max_{s,d}J_X(s,d)=X^{o(1)}
\]
is not currently a consequence of the known Apéry congruences. The available Lucas, reflection, and Casoratian identities give strong local restrictions, but they do not by themselves give a divisor bound for simultaneous shell roots.

The natural obstruction is that the condition
\[
p_i\mid B_{u_i}
\]
is a congruence condition on three different Apéry polynomials modulo three unrelated primes. There is no known fixed polynomial whose integer divisors parameterize all solutions. Thus the suggested S-unit route requires an additional arithmetic input.

## 2. Exact parameterization of a fibre

Put
\[
t=z_1,
\qquad N=c_1p_1.
\]
The defect equations imply
\[
c_2p_2=N-d_2,\qquad c_3p_3=N-d_3.
\]
Hence every element of a fibre gives three factorizations
\[
N=c_1p_1,\quad N-d_2=c_2p_2,\quad N-d_3=c_3p_3,
\]
where
\[
X<p_i<2X,
\qquad 1\le c_i<X
\]
(with the negative-sign convention excluding c_i=1).

For fixed N, the shell prime factorization is unique. Therefore multiplicity in J_X comes only from the number of admissible N in the interval
\[
0<N<X(2X)+O(X)
\]
which simultaneously produce three shell factors and whose associated roots satisfy the Apéry conditions.

The residue data are affine in t. For fixed sign choices,
\[
z_i=\epsilon_i t+\alpha_i(d,s),
\]
so the folded roots have the form
\[
u_i=\operatorname{fold}(\epsilon_i t+\alpha_i).
\]
The remaining condition is
\[
B_{u_i}\equiv0\pmod {p_i}.
\]

## 3. What a successful divisor/resultant proof would need

A sufficient statement would be the following explicit finite-index lemma.

**Required lemma.** For every fixed sign pattern and nondegenerate defects, there exists a nonzero integer polynomial R_{s,d}(T) of degree O(1) such that every fibre parameter t satisfies
\[
R_{s,d}(t)=0
\]
up to O(1) exceptional boundary cases.

Then
\[
J_X(s,d)\le \deg R_{s,d}
\]
would be immediate.

A weaker acceptable statement is an S-unit form:

For every fibre element, associate an integer A(t) such that
\[
A(t)\mid C_{s,d}\prod_j L_j(t)^{e_j}
\]
with fixed linear forms L_j and fixed exponents. The standard divisor bound would then give
\[
J_X(s,d)=X^{o(1)}.
\]

The missing step is exactly the construction of such an A(t) from Apéry vanishing.

## 4. Why recurrence-free reflected-star models do not suffice

If the only imposed conditions are reflection
\[
u\leftrightarrow p-1-u
\]
and affine compatibility of residues, then one can choose many independent roots. The reflected-star model therefore can have large fibres.

The Apéry condition adds
\[
p\mid B_u.
\]
This excludes arbitrary stars because the set
\[
Z_p=\{u:B_u\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]
is not an arbitrary symmetric subset. Known restrictions are:

* reflection: if u is a root then p-1-u is a root;
* no consecutive roots;
* root count bounds such as |Z_p|=O(p^{2/3});
* compatibility with the Apéry recurrence.

However these are cardinality restrictions on each prime separately. They do not yet control intersections of three affine root conditions.

## 5. Casoratian route audit

The recurrence gives the primitive Casoratian identity. For the standard Apéry pair,
\[
a_nB_{n+1}-a_{n+1}B_n=-\frac6{(n+1)^3}.
\]
Modulo p, if B_u=0 and u+1 is not exceptional modulo p, then
\[
a_uB_{u+1}\equiv -\frac6{(u+1)^3}\not\equiv0.
\]
Hence roots are simple in the recurrence sense.

This rules out repeated-root amplification but does not connect three different primes. A fixed-index determinant involving three different shell primes would require eliminating the unknown prime moduli, which is precisely the missing resultant step.

## 6. Exceptional cases

The fibre definition already removes the main degeneracies by requiring
\[
0,d_2,d_3
\]
to be distinct.

Remaining exceptional classes:

1. Equal anchors: if d_i=0, then two shell factorizations share the same N and the divisor count changes. These are excluded.

2. Reflected anchors: if two signed residues correspond to the same folded root, then the local reflection symmetry can duplicate a root. Such cases must be separated before any injective parameterization.

3. Boundary coefficients: c_i=0 or c_i=X are impossible from m<X^2; the negative sign convention also removes c_i=1.

4. The midpoint root u=(p-1)/2 is fixed by reflection and must be treated separately. It is not removed by reflection counting alone.

## 7. Consequence for the gcd argument

The exact counting implication
\[
F_3\le(468X^2-756X+288)J_X
\]
is valid. The unresolved statement is only the arithmetic estimate on J_X.

The finite observations J_X=1 through X=4096 demonstrate that no small counterexample is visible, but they do not imply subpolynomial growth.

A complete proof therefore needs one additional theorem of the following kind:

* a uniform resultant identity forcing finitely many t;
* an S-unit/divisor parametrization of the three Apéry-root equations;
* or a descent showing any fibre element creates a strictly smaller fibre.

The currently known Apéry recurrence identities establish simplicity and reflection constraints, but they do not supply that final elimination theorem.
