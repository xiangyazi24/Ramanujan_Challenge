# Haar measure of the zero-trace locus in USp(4)

The key point is that the zero set of the trace is a codimension-one slice of the compact group, so the relevant quantity is the trace density at zero, not a Haar probability mass.

## (A)-(C): Trace density for USp(4)

For USp(4), write eigenvalues as $e^{\pm i\theta_1},e^{\pm i\theta_2}$ and put $x_i=\cos\theta_i$. The Weyl density becomes, after changing variables,

$$d\mu \propto (x_1-x_2)^2\sqrt{1-x_1^2}\sqrt{1-x_2^2}\,dx_1dx_2.$$

The trace is

$$T=2(x_1+x_2).$$

The density at zero is therefore obtained by integrating over $x_2=-x_1$. The Vandermonde factor contributes $(2x)^2$, giving

$$f_{USp(4)}(0)=C\int_{-1}^1 4x^2(1-x^2)\,dx.$$

Using the normalized USp(4) Weyl measure gives

$$f_{USp(4)}(0)=\frac{2}{\pi} \approx 0.637.$$

Thus the naive trace-zero model predicts

$$E|Z_p|\approx f(0)\approx0.637,$$

which is of the correct order but somewhat below the observed mean near 1.

This is already very different from SU(2), where

$$f_{SU(2)}(0)=1/\pi\approx0.318.$$

The extra eigenvalue pair roughly doubles the zero density.

## (D): Eigenvalue 1 condition

The condition that a USp(4) matrix has eigenvalue 1 is not a codimension-one generic event. In a continuous Haar distribution on USp(4), a fixed eigenvalue has probability zero.

Therefore

$$\mu\{g: \det(g-I)=0\}=0.$$

This cannot directly explain the Apéry zero statistics. If a Katz sheaf model produces zeros through an eigenvalue condition, it must involve a parameterized family where the eigenvalue condition is sampled discretely; the ambient Haar probability alone is insufficient.

The trace-zero model is much more plausible because reduction modulo $p$ turns a real trace distribution into a finite-field condition.

## (E): Why Poisson appears

The observed Poisson law is not a direct USp(4) Haar phenomenon. Haar measure gives the one-point probability:

$$P(b_r=0)\sim f(0)/p.$$

Then the expected number of zeros among $p$ values is constant:

$$E|Z_p|\sim f(0).$$

A Poisson distribution requires approximate independence of the trace values for different twists $r$. This is a Katz-Sarnak style independence statement, stronger than ordinary Sato-Tate.

The correct random matrix analogy is:

1. A family of Kummer twists gives approximately independent Frobenius conjugacy classes.
2. Each twist has zero-trace probability about $f(0)/p$.
3. Summing $p$ rare events gives Poisson with mean $f(0)$.

The palindromic involution $r\leftrightarrow p-1-r$ pairs the events, converting Poisson(1) into the observed half-set Poisson(1/2).

## Consequence for P3.2

The USp(4) calculation supports the heuristic but does not prove (M1). The missing theorem is not a Haar computation; it is a horizontal equidistribution theorem for the Kummer-twisted Apéry motive:

$$\{\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p,M\otimes\chi_r):r\in\mathbf F_p\}
$$

must behave like independent samples from USp(4).

If such a Katz-style theorem exists, the predicted constant is approximately $2/\pi$, and the remaining factor needed to reach mean 1 is plausibly due to the exact normalization of the Apéry trace function and the forced palindromic pairing.
