# The one-third truncation is first-order saturated

## Verdict

Put

\[
 A_{n,k}=\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
 \qquad
 J=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{3}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 \Theta_n=\sum_{k=0}^{J}A_{n,k},
\]

and

\[
 U_n=\prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\ge7}}p,
 \qquad
 D_n=b_n-5\Theta_n,
 \qquad
 E_n=\frac{D_n}{U_n^2}\in\mathbf Z.
\]

The supplied theorem gives

\[
 U_n^2\mid D_n                                                     \tag{0.1}
\]

and, for every top-half candidate prime \(p\),

\[
 p\mid b_n\iff p\mid\Theta_n.                                      \tag{0.2}
\]

The decisive conclusion is negative:

> **The congruence \(b_n\equiv5\Theta_n\pmod{p^2}\) is already saturated at first order.**
> After division by \(p^2\), the new residue is not constrained by targetness.  Iterating the division, applying the inhomogeneous recurrence of the truncation, taking finitely many cutoffs, or rewriting the tail by WZ telescoping does not create a second target-selective congruence.  It only introduces new boundary or quotient coordinates.

There are two rigorous parts to this verdict.

1. **Universal saturation theorem.**  In the universal algebra generated by the exact relation \(b-5\Theta=U^2E\), the target fibre at a prime \(p\mid U\) is
   \[
      \mathbf F_p[E].
   \]
   Thus \(E\) is algebraically free after imposing \(p=0\) and \(\Theta=0\).  The ideal of expressions forced to vanish on every target fibre is exactly the target ideal; no power of \(p\) can be extracted from \(E\).  The actual row \((n,p,r)=(20,17,3)\) proves that this freedom is not merely formal.

2. **Height theorem for the natural carriers.**  The three basic quantities have exponential rates
   \[
   \log \Theta_n=n\log16+O(\log n),                                \tag{0.3}
   \]
   \[
   \log b_n=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n),                          \tag{0.4}
   \]
   and
   \[
   \log E_n=
      \bigl(\log(17+12\sqrt2)-1\bigr)n+o(n).                       \tag{0.5}
   \]
   In particular all three natural coordinates have positive exponential height.  A fixed finite collection of truncations and WZ boundary terms also has positive exponential height unless an exact identity makes the proposed carrier identically zero.  Euclidean or Casoratian elimination does not change this balance: every selective factor of a target prime costs a determinant row of linear exponential height.

Consequently this route does **not** yield an integer \(C_n\ne0\), defined independently of the target set, satisfying

\[
 \prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}p\mid C_n,
 \qquad
 \log|C_n|=o(n).                                                    \tag{0.6}
\]

A successful continuation would require an additional Apéry-specific equation constraining the divided residue \(E_n\bmod p\) at target primes.  Neither the truncation identity, its recurrence, WZ telescoping, reflection, nor fixed-\(q\) block analogues provide such an equation.

## 1. The exact information contained in the congruence

Fix a prime

\[
 \frac n2<p\le n,
 \qquad p\ge7.
\]

The theorem in the question says

\[
 b_n=5\Theta_n+p^2e_{n,p}                                         \tag{1.1}
\]

for an integer \(e_{n,p}\).  Since every candidate prime occurs in \(U_n\), the simultaneous form is (0.1).

Reducing (1.1) modulo \(p\) gives

\[
 b_n\equiv5\Theta_n\pmod p.                                      \tag{1.2}
\]

Because \(5\) is a unit, targetness is exactly

\[
 p\mid b_n\iff p\mid\Theta_n.                                    \tag{1.3}
\]

Suppose now that \(p\) is a target.  Write

\[
 b_n=p\beta_{n,p},
 \qquad
 \Theta_n=p\tau_{n,p}.
\]

Dividing (1.1) by \(p\) gives only

\[
 \beta_{n,p}-5\tau_{n,p}=p e_{n,p}.                               \tag{1.4}
\]

Thus

\[
 \beta_{n,p}\equiv5\tau_{n,p}\pmod p,                             \tag{1.5}
\]

but no condition on \(e_{n,p}\bmod p\) follows.  A second division would require

\[
 p\mid e_{n,p},                                                    \tag{1.6}
\]

which is an additional third-order congruence, not a consequence of targetness.

The supplied first counterexample

\[
 (n,p,r)=(20,17,3)                                                 \tag{1.7}
\]

states precisely that the corresponding divided residue is nonzero modulo \(17\).  Hence the implication

\[
 p\mid b_n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p^3\mid b_n-5\Theta_n         \tag{1.8}
\]

is false.  Therefore the most direct iteration stops after the universally forced \(p^2\).

This is not an accidental failure at a small prime.  It is the generic algebraic shape of one first-order congruence: after one divides by the forced power, a new affine coordinate appears.

## 2. Exact saturation theorem

The following universal statement isolates the information content of (1.1).

### Theorem 2.1 — one-cutoff saturation

Let

\[
 \mathcal R=\mathbf Z[U,B,T,E]/(B-5T-U^2E).                        \tag{2.1}
\]

For a prime \(p\mid U\), let

\[
 I_p=(p,T)\subset\mathcal R.                                      \tag{2.2}
\]

Then

\[
 \mathcal R/I_p\cong\mathbf F_p[E].                               \tag{2.3}
\]

In particular:

1. the class of \(E\) is transcendental over \(\mathbf F_p\);
2. \(E\notin I_p\);
3. for a polynomial \(F\in\mathcal R\), vanishing for every specialization satisfying \(p=0\) and \(T=0\) is equivalent to \(F\in I_p\);
4. the \(I_p\)-saturation of the relation introduces no further target equation:
   \[
      (B-5T):I_p^\infty=(U^2E):I_p^\infty
   \]
   has no component forcing \(E=0\).

#### Proof

Modulo \(p\) and \(T\), the defining relation becomes

\[
 B=0,
\]

because \(p\mid U\).  No relation remains on \(E\).  Therefore

\[
 \mathcal R/(p,T)
 =\mathbf F_p[B,E]/(B)
 \cong\mathbf F_p[E].
\]

The four assertions follow immediately.  In particular a polynomial vanishes at all target-fibre points exactly when its image in the polynomial ring \(\mathbf F_p[E]\) is zero, which is exactly membership in \((p,T)\).  ∎

### Interpretation

Map

\[
 B\mapsto b_n,
 \qquad
 T\mapsto\Theta_n,
 \qquad
 U\mapsto U_n,
 \qquad
 E\mapsto E_n.                                                     \tag{2.4}
\]

The relation in \(\mathcal R\) becomes the exact integer identity

\[
 b_n-5\Theta_n=U_n^2E_n.                                          \tag{2.5}
\]

At a target prime, \(T=0\) and hence \(B=0\) modulo \(p\), but \(E\) remains free.  The row \((20,17,3)\) shows that the actual Apéry specialization visits a point with \(E\ne0\) in this fibre.

Thus any proposed proof that uses only

- the exact relation (2.5),
- divisibility \(p\mid\Theta_n\),
- ordinary ring operations,
- Euclidean elimination,
- resultants or determinants formed from these equations,

cannot prove \(p\mid E_n\).  Such a proof would establish the false universal statement \(E\in I_p\).

This is the exact no-go theorem for iterating the divided difference without importing another arithmetic identity.

## 3. Several cutoffs do not repair the rank

Let

\[
 \Theta_{n,J}=\sum_{k=0}^{J}A_{n,k}.                              \tag{3.1}
\]

Choose finitely many cutoffs \(J_1,\ldots,J_s\), independent of the unknown target set.  Their differences are exact boundary blocks:

\[
 \Theta_{n,J_{i+1}}-\Theta_{n,J_i}
 =\sum_{J_i<k\le J_{i+1}}A_{n,k}.                                 \tag{3.2}
\]

Suppose one has congruences of the form

\[
 b_n-c_i\Theta_{n,J_i}=U_{n,i}^{m_i}E_{n,i}.                       \tag{3.3}
\]

For a fixed candidate prime \(p\), reduce all equations modulo \(p\).  Every equation contributes at most one linear relation among

\[
 b_n,\Theta_{n,J_1},\ldots,\Theta_{n,J_s}.                         \tag{3.4}
\]

After dividing by the forced powers of \(p\), each equation introduces a new quotient variable \(E_{n,i}\).  Unless a new theorem relates those quotient variables, they remain independent affine coordinates.

This can again be stated exactly.

### Theorem 3.1 — finite multicutoff saturation

Let

\[
 \mathcal R_s=
 \mathbf Z[U,B,T_1,\ldots,T_s,E_1,\ldots,E_s]/
 (B-c_iT_i-U^{m_i}E_i:1\le i\le s).                               \tag{3.5}
\]

Assume \(p\mid U\), and impose target equations \(T_i=0\) for those cutoffs known to detect the target.  If the constants \(c_i\) are \(p\)-units, then the target quotient contains a polynomial ring in the divided coordinates not eliminated by independent relations.  In the maximally favourable case in which all \(T_i\) vanish,

\[
 \mathcal R_s/(p,T_1,\ldots,T_s)
 \cong
 \mathbf F_p[E_1,\ldots,E_s]/L,                                  \tag{3.6}
\]

where \(L\) consists only of relations explicitly present before specialization.  In particular, if no relation among the \(E_i\) has been proved, none is created by targetness.

#### Proof

Set \(p=0\) and \(T_i=0\).  The defining equations set \(B=0\), because \(U^{m_i}=0\) modulo \(p\), but their terms involving \(E_i\) disappear.  Therefore all relations among the \(E_i\) in the fibre must already come from additional equations.  ∎

### Consequence for nearby cutoffs

Adding \(J-1,J,J+1\) does not give three target equations.  It gives one target equation plus exact boundary identities

\[
 \Theta_{n,J}-\Theta_{n,J-1}=A_{n,J},
 \qquad
 \Theta_{n,J+1}-\Theta_{n,J}=A_{n,J+1}.                            \tag{3.7}
\]

The boundary terms are nonzero hypergeometric integers.  They are new coordinates, not forced zeros.  Eliminating the cutoffs simply replaces divided residues by boundary residues.

This is the same mechanism already encountered in cutoff-transition calculations in the repository: Smith or Euclidean elimination can identify where a prime is lost or regained, but after squarefree saturation the surviving support is exactly the original moving-zero set.

## 4. The inhomogeneous recurrence for \(\Theta_n\)

Because the summand \(A_{n,k}\) is proper hypergeometric, creative telescoping gives an identity

\[
 L_n A_{n,k}=G_{n,k+1}-G_{n,k},                                   \tag{4.1}
\]

where \(L_n\) is the Apéry recurrence operator in \(n\), after a suitable normalization, and \(G_{n,k}\) is a WZ certificate.  Summing only to \(J(n)=\lfloor(n-1)/3\rfloor\) yields

\[
 L_n\Theta_n=H_n,                                                  \tag{4.2}
\]

where \(H_n\) consists of finitely many boundary terms near \(k=J(n)\), together with the correction caused by the changes in the floor function.

The complete Apéry sum has zero right-hand side because its upper boundary vanishes.  The truncation is inhomogeneous precisely because the artificial boundary does not vanish.

### Why recurrence iteration does not help

Write the order-two recurrence schematically as

\[
 a_2(n)\Theta_{n+1}+a_1(n)\Theta_n+a_0(n)\Theta_{n-1}=H_n.         \tag{4.3}
\]

Knowing \(p\mid\Theta_n\) imposes one linear condition on the two-dimensional homogeneous state.  Equation (4.3) introduces \(H_n\), which is an additional boundary coordinate.  It does not force either neighbouring value to vanish.

Modulo \(p\), and away from the fixed singular coefficients, one may solve

\[
 \Theta_{n+1}
 =-\frac{a_1(n)}{a_2(n)}\Theta_n
  -\frac{a_0(n)}{a_2(n)}\Theta_{n-1}
  +\frac{H_n}{a_2(n)}.                                             \tag{4.4}
\]

At a target, the right side still depends freely on

\[
 \Theta_{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad H_n.                            \tag{4.5}
\]

Iterating (4.4) merely expresses all nearby truncations as affine-linear functions of an initial state and a list of boundary forcings.  A Casoratian can eliminate the homogeneous initial state, but then the list of \(H\)-coordinates survives.  A WZ telescoping identity can rewrite the \(H\)-coordinates, but cannot make them vanish.

### Formal recurrence no-go

For any finite interval, an inhomogeneous order-two recurrence defines an affine map

\[
 (\Theta_{m},\Theta_{m+1},H_m,\ldots,H_{m+h-1})
 \longmapsto
 (\Theta_{m+h},\Theta_{m+h+1}).                                   \tag{4.6}
\]

If the \(H_j\) are not constrained independently, then for every prescribed state path one may solve for the forcings.  Therefore elimination from the recurrence alone gives the zero ideal in the forcing variables.  Any scalar equation obtained after eliminating the state is an identity among the boundary terms, not a new target condition.

This is the inhomogeneous analogue of the order-two recurrence obstruction already recorded elsewhere in the project: one zero leaves one free homogeneous coordinate; truncation adds rather than removes boundary freedom.

## 5. WZ boundary terms have exponential height

The obstruction is not only algebraic.  The boundary terms are too large.

Let \(k=\alpha n+O(1)\).  Stirling's formula gives

\[
 \log A_{n,k}
 =n\Phi(\alpha)+O(\log n),                                        \tag{5.1}
\]

where

\[
 \Phi(\alpha)
 =2H(\alpha)+2(1+\alpha)
   H\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\right),                       \tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
 H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x).                                   \tag{5.3}
\]

At the one-third boundary,

\[
 \Phi(1/3)=\log16.                                                 \tag{5.4}
\]

Indeed

\[
 \binom n{n/3}^2\binom{4n/3}{n/3}^2
 =16^n n^{-2+O(1/n)}\cdot C                                      \tag{5.5}
\]

for a positive constant \(C\) along a fixed residue class of \(n\bmod3\).

The summands increase throughout \(0\le k\le n/3+O(1)\), so the endpoint controls the truncated sum up to a polynomial factor.  Hence

\[
 \boxed{\log\Theta_n=n\log16+O(\log n).}                           \tag{5.6}
\]

Every WZ boundary expression is a rational function of \(n,k\) times finitely many nearby terms \(A_{n,k+j}\).  Clearing its polynomial denominators changes the logarithm by \(O(\log n)\).  Therefore any nonzero fixed-width boundary expression at \(k=n/3+O(1)\) has an exponential scale drawn from \(16^n\), unless its leading combination is an exact hypergeometric identity.  In the latter case the expression is identically zero and cannot be a nonzero carrier.

Thus WZ telescoping moves the exponential mass from a partial sum into a finite boundary vector; it does not make it subexponential.

## 6. Exact height of the divided difference

Let

\[
 \lambda=17+12\sqrt2.                                             \tag{6.1}
\]

The standard Apéry asymptotic is

\[
 \log b_n=n\log\lambda+O(\log n).                                 \tag{6.2}
\]

Since

\[
 \lambda>16,                                                      \tag{6.3}
\]

(5.6) implies

\[
 \frac{5\Theta_n}{b_n}
 =\exp\bigl(-n\log(\lambda/16)+O(\log n)\bigr)=o(1).               \tag{6.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
 D_n=b_n-5\Theta_n=b_n(1+o(1)),                                   \tag{6.5}
\]

and

\[
 \log D_n=n\log\lambda+O(\log n).                                \tag{6.6}
\]

The prime number theorem gives

\[
 \log U_n
 =\vartheta(n)-\vartheta(n/2)+O(1)
 =\frac n2+o(n).                                                   \tag{6.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \log E_n
 =\bigl(\log\lambda-1\bigr)n+o(n).
 }                                                                 \tag{6.8}
\]

Numerically,

\[
 \log\lambda-1\approx2.52549>0.                                  \tag{6.9}
\]

Hence the globally divided difference is still exponentially large.  Dividing by the full universal square \(U_n^2\), rather than by one candidate \(p^2\), already extracts every universally available top-prime power and leaves a positive exponential rate.

This is the sharp height obstruction for the most optimistic direct iteration.

## 7. Euclidean and Casoratian combinations

Consider the two integer coordinates

\[
 \Theta_n,
 \qquad
 E_n=\frac{b_n-5\Theta_n}{U_n^2}.                                 \tag{7.1}
\]

At a target prime \(p\), only the first is forced to vanish modulo \(p\).  The second is free by Theorem 2.1.

A Euclidean combination

\[
 C_n=X_n\Theta_n+Y_nE_n                                           \tag{7.2}
\]

with \(p\nmid Y_n\) is therefore not forced to vanish.  To obtain universal target divisibility from the supplied information, one must have

\[
 p\mid Y_n                                                        \tag{7.3}
\]

for every candidate prime.  Thus \(U_n\mid Y_n\), up to the fixed small primes.  Writing \(Y_n=U_nY_n'\), the coefficient alone has height

\[
 \log|Y_n|\ge\frac n2+o(n)                                       \tag{7.4}
\]

unless \(Y_n=0\).  This is already linear.

The same statement holds for a determinant or Casoratian.  Suppose two rows are built from \((\Theta_n,E_n)\):

\[
 R_i=(X_i,Y_i),\qquad i=1,2.                                      \tag{7.5}
\]

Target vanishing of both row evaluations requires both \(Y_i\equiv0\pmod p\), unless a new relation fixes \(E_n\).  Their determinant then contains the universal candidate factor through its \(Y\)-coordinates.  The determinant has not extracted target selectivity; it has inserted the whole candidate support into its coefficients.

Alternatively, if only one row has zero \(E\)-coefficient modulo \(p\), the second row retains the free divided coordinate and no rank-two drop occurs.  Thus there is no Casoratian amplification from one target equation.

### Natural linear-cone no-go

For subexponential integer coefficients \(X_n,Y_n\ge0\), not both zero,

\[
 C_n=X_n\Theta_n+Y_nE_n                                           \tag{7.6}
\]

satisfies

\[
 \log C_n\ge
 \min\{n\log16,(\log\lambda-1)n\}+o(n),                          \tag{7.7}
\]

so it has positive exponential height.  Signed combinations can cancel only by tuning a coefficient ratio to exponential precision.  Encoding that ratio in integers transfers the exponential height into the coefficients; it does not produce a low-height canonical carrier.

## 8. Higher divided digits

Define recursively, when divisibility permits,

\[
 E_n^{(0)}=b_n-5\Theta_n,
 \qquad
 E_n^{(1)}=\frac{E_n^{(0)}}{U_n^2}.                               \tag{8.1}
\]

To define a target-selective second digit one would need, for every target prime,

\[
 p\mid E_n^{(1)}.                                                  \tag{8.2}
\]

The row \((20,17,3)\) disproves this.  If one instead removes only the successful primes by defining

\[
 E_n^{(2)}=
 \frac{E_n^{(1)}}{\prod_{p\in\mathcal T_n,\ p\mid E_n^{(1)}}p},   \tag{8.3}
\]

then the unknown target set has entered the definition.  Such an integer is not a global certificate.

A higher-digit theorem could still exist, but it must have the form

\[
 E_n^{(1)}\equiv F_{n,p}\pmod p                                  \tag{8.4}
\]

with an explicit \(F_{n,p}\), followed by a new proof that targetness forces \(F_{n,p}=0\).  The first-order congruence itself gives no such implication.  In all known Apéry reflection and Lucas refinements, each further division exposes a new normalized derivative or quotient coordinate; it does not close automatically.

## 9. Fixed-\(q\) analogues

For a general block

\[
 n=qp+r,
 \qquad 0\le r<p,                                                  \tag{9.1}
\]

one may seek a \(q\)-dependent truncation \(\Theta_{n,q}\) satisfying

\[
 b_n\equiv c_q\Theta_{n,q}\pmod{p^m}.                             \tag{9.2}
\]

This can be useful locally.  It does not change the saturation mechanism.

Write

\[
 b_n-c_q\Theta_{n,q}=p^mE_{n,p,q}.                                \tag{9.3}
\]

If the lower-channel target condition is

\[
 p\mid\Theta_{n,q},                                                \tag{9.4}
\]

then after division by \(p\) the common first digit is constrained, while \(E_{n,p,q}\bmod p\) remains a new coordinate.  A fixed-\(q\) recurrence or WZ identity may describe it but does not force it to vanish.

Moreover, when \(q\) is fixed and the cutoff remains a positive proportion of \(n\), its boundary summands still have exponential height

\[
 \exp(c_qn+O(\log n))                                              \tag{9.5}
\]

for some \(c_q>0\).  If the cutoff is \(o(n)\), its height may become subexponential, but then it cannot encode all residues in the critical moving lower channel without a separate theorem.  This is the same degree/height tradeoff seen in the repository's Newton, rational-interpolation, and finite-alias constructions.

Thus fixed \(q\) changes constants and support ranges, not the need for a second target-selective equation.

## 10. Exact natural-class no-go theorem

The preceding arguments can be packaged as follows.

### Theorem 10.1 — truncation/WZ saturation

Fix a finite collection of objects obtained from the one-third truncation by the following operations:

1. finitely many cutoffs \(J_i(n)=\alpha_i n+O(1)\) with \(0<\alpha_i<1\);
2. finite sums and differences of the corresponding partial sums;
3. a fixed number of applications of the Apéry recurrence in \(n\);
4. WZ telescoping, with polynomial-denominator clearing;
5. Euclidean combinations, determinants, Casoratians, and resultants of bounded order;
6. division only by prime powers whose divisibility has already been proved uniformly for every candidate prime.

Assume no additional arithmetic equation among the resulting divided or boundary coordinates.

Then:

**(a) Divisibility saturation.**  On a target fibre, every divided coordinate not explicitly constrained by an additional equation remains free.  Hence every universally target-divisible output lies in the ideal generated by the original target cutoff coordinates and universal candidate factors.  In particular one cannot deduce

\[
 p\mid\frac{b_n-5\Theta_n}{p^2}.                                  \tag{10.1}
\]

**(b) Height saturation.**  Every nonzero basic partial sum or WZ boundary coordinate with a linear cutoff has logarithmic height \(c n+O(\log n)\) for some \(c>0\).  A bounded-order determinant has logarithmic height \(O(n)\) per row, while each target prime supplies at most one selective rank condition.  Dividing by all universally forced candidate factors leaves positive exponential height in the one-third construction, specifically (6.8).

**(c) Consequence.**  This class cannot prove (0.6).  Any apparent subexponential output is either identically zero, contains a universal candidate factor inserted by hand, or relies on a new relation outside the stated class.

#### Proof

Part (a) is Theorems 2.1 and 3.1, combined with the affine-forcing form (4.6).  Each allowed operation is elimination inside the same coordinate algebra; it cannot create an equation on a coordinate that is polynomially free in the target quotient.

For part (b), Stirling's formula gives a positive entropy rate for every bulk boundary term, and polynomial-denominator clearing changes the logarithm by only \(O(\log n)\).  Bounded-order algebraic operations add only finitely many such rates.  For the principal divided coordinate, the exact rate is (6.8).  A determinant with \(d\) rows has logarithmic height \(O(dn)\), while the target condition contributes at most one factor of \(p\) per independent rank loss; bounded \(d\) cannot turn a linear rate into little-oh.

Part (c) follows.  ∎

The theorem is deliberately a theorem about a **natural proof class**, not a claim that no unknown identity exists.  Its reopen condition is exact: exhibit an independent Apéry-specific equation that is nontrivial in the free divided coordinate on the target fibre.

## 11. What would genuinely reopen the route

Any successful continuation must supply at least one of the following.

### 11.1 A second target-selective digit

Prove an explicit formula

\[
 \frac{b_n-5\Theta_n}{p^2}\equiv F(n,p)\pmod p                    \tag{11.1}
\]

and then prove

\[
 p\mid b_n\Longrightarrow F(n,p)=0\pmod p.                        \tag{11.2}
\]

The example \((20,17,3)\) rules out \(F=0\) universally, so \(F\) would need an additional correction term invisible in the present truncation.

### 11.2 A cross-prime norm or discriminant

Construct a single characteristic-zero object \(M_n\) such that each target prime is bad for \(M_n\), while

\[
 \log|\operatorname{disc}M_n|=o(n).                               \tag{11.3}
\]

The current divided difference has discriminant/height rate at least (6.8), so it is not such an object.

### 11.3 A low-height signed boundary identity

Find a WZ identity whose boundary combination

- vanishes modulo every target prime,
- is not universally divisible by all candidates,
- and has logarithmic height \(o(n)\).

Ordinary telescoping does not qualify: its boundary terms have rate \(\log16\) at the one-third cutoff.

### 11.4 A same-prime multiplicity theorem

If targetness forced two independent truncation rows to vanish modulo the same \(p\), a determinant could gain \(p^2\).  The saturation theorem shows that the supplied \(p^2\) congruence is not that second row; it is only a first-order thickening of the first.

## 12. Final conclusion

The identity

\[
 U_n^2\mid b_n-5\Theta_n
\]

is a strong exact theorem, but its global arithmetic content is exhausted by one first-order target equation.

- The target prime divides \(\Theta_n\).
- The difference \(b_n-5\Theta_n\) vanishes to order two for every candidate, good or bad.
- After removing this universal square, the quotient is free on the target fibre.
- The first actual target \((20,17,3)\) already realizes a nonzero quotient residue.
- The inhomogeneous recurrence and WZ machinery only expose boundary forcings.
- Those boundary terms have exponential height \(16^n\) at the one-third cutoff.
- The fully divided global difference still has rate
  \[
    \log(17+12\sqrt2)-1>0.
  \]

Therefore the requested subexponential carrier does not arise from iterating this identity, from finitely many nearby cutoffs, from bounded-order Euclidean/Casoratian elimination, from ordinary WZ boundary terms, or from the direct fixed-\(q\) analogue.

The exact missing ingredient is a **new independent equation on the divided residue**.  Without it, the construction is algebraically and exponentially saturated.
