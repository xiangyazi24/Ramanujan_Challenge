# Strategic framing audit: replace the numerical gcd by target-visible shell saturation

## Verdict

The remaining problem is **correctly characteristic-zero**, but I would not frame the final missing theorem as “prove that the evaluated integer gcd \(c_S\) is small.”  That is sufficient, but it is stronger and more value-dependent than necessary.

There is one sharper arithmetic quantity that can imply the required target-radical bound while genuinely evading the dead routes (1)–(5):

> **the radical of the largest Smith invariant (the saturation exponent) of an exact, growing-window, actual-Laurent-shell residual-observability matrix.**

This is not the gcd of the numerical residuals, not a Green endpoint, not a small individual determinant, and not an annihilator of the shift sequence.  It is a characteristic-zero lattice invariant measuring how far the actual shell observation lattice is from being saturated.

The crucial point is that a common prime divisor of all residuals can only come from two places once the residual vector is expressed through an exact shell-specific latent packet:

1. common content already present in that latent packet; or
2. nonsaturation of the observation lattice.

For the actual Laurent-shell origin/common-convolution packet, the first source is the one for which the repository already has the right algebraic mechanism: a polynomial Bézout identity bounds pointwise coefficient content by a fixed \(q\)-dependent integer.  Thus the genuinely new quantity is the second source, the largest Smith factor.

This reframing controls **genuine target primes**, not merely nuisance primes.  If a target prime \(p\) is not paid for by the known boundary factor \(B_S\), then the target-cover theorem forces \(p\mid c_S\), hence \(p\) divides every residual in the window.  The lattice lemma below then forces \(p\) into the shell saturation factor.  Therefore a sublinear bound for the radical of that saturation factor bounds the product of the surviving target primes themselves.

I do **not** claim the required saturation theorem is currently proved.  It is the one candidate lemma I would put at the center of the remaining attack.

## 1. The exact lattice quantity

Fix \(q\).  For a symmetric window put

\[
\mathbf R_{q,n,S}:=(R_{-S},R_{-S+1},\ldots,R_S)^T\in\mathbb Z^{2S+1}.
\]

The proposed shell-specific construction is the following.

Use the exact Laurent coefficient/common-convolution representation of the terminal shell to choose a finite integral latent packet

\[
X_{q,n,S}\in\mathbb Z^{r_{q,n,S}}
\]

and an integral observation matrix

\[
M_{q,n,S}\in M_{(2S+1)\times r_{q,n,S}}(\mathbb Z)
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{\mathbf R_{q,n,S}=M_{q,n,S}X_{q,n,S}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Here “actual-shell” is essential: the columns of \(M_{q,n,S}\) must be the common-convolution Laurent-shell columns before the final origin/operator coefficients are inserted, followed by the same full terminal Newton rows that define the actual \(R_s\).  This is the growing-window version of the structure already exposed in

```text
problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_terminal_directional_cofactor_audit.py
```

where one terminal observability row consists of the common-convolution shell columns together with the \(-Y\) column, and the fixed polynomial origin coefficient/multiplier vector is inserted only afterward.

If several nearby moments are needed for the shifted residuals, stack the corresponding fixed-\(q\) origin packets and then replace the resulting columns by a primitive integral basis of their rational column span.  Thus we may and should require

\[
r:=r_{q,n,S}\le 2S+1,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M_{q,n,S}=r.
\tag{1.2}
\]

For a full-column-rank integer matrix \(M\), let

\[
\delta_j(M)
 :=\gcd\{\,|\det M_{I,J}|:\ |I|=|J|=j\,\},
\qquad \delta_0(M)=1,
\]

and define

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(M):=\frac{\delta_r(M)}{\delta_{r-1}(M)}.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

If

\[
UMV=
\begin{pmatrix}
 d_1&&\\
 &\ddots&\\
 &&d_r\\
 \hline
 &0&
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_r,
\]

is Smith normal form, then

\[
\delta_j=d_1\cdots d_j,
\qquad
\boxed{\sigma(M)=d_r.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Thus \(\sigma(M)\) is the exponent of the torsion in the saturation quotient of the column lattice.  This is much smaller information than the size of a maximal minor.  In particular, all maximal minors may have exponential height while \(\sigma(M)=1\).

That distinction is exactly why this quantity is not the already-dead “find a small growing minor” route.

## 2. The one candidate lemma

### Candidate lemma: target-visible Laurent-shell saturation

Fix \(q\ge1\).  There exist constants

\[
C_q>0,\qquad K_q\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad n_q\ge1
\]

such that for every \(n\ge n_q\), with

\[
S=S(n):=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor,
\]

the actual full-Newton Laurent-shell residual vector admits an **explicit exact integral factorization**

\[
\mathbf R_{q,n,S}=M_{q,n,S}X_{q,n,S}
\tag{LS1}
\]

satisfying

\[
r_{q,n,S}\le 2S+1,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M_{q,n,S}=r_{q,n,S},
\tag{LS2}
\]

\[
\operatorname{cont}(X_{q,n,S})\mid K_q,
\tag{LS3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}\sigma(M_{q,n,S})
 \le C_q S^2\log(n+2).
}
\tag{LS4}
\]

Here

\[
\operatorname{cont}(x)=\gcd(x_1,\ldots,x_r).
\]

Clause (LS3) may be replaced by the slightly weaker quantitative condition

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}\operatorname{cont}(X_{q,n,S})=o(n),
\]

but a fixed \(K_q\) is the natural target in the current origin-operator architecture: the repository already constructs a rational polynomial Bézout identity for the origin coefficients/multiplier and clears one fixed denominator, which is precisely the mechanism that bounds all pointwise common contents by one fixed integer.

The substantive missing assertion is (LS4), together with a completely written proof of the exact all-window factorization (LS1).

The exponent \(2\) on \(S\) is not sacred.  What is actually needed is

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}\sigma(M_{q,n,S(n)})=o(n)
\]

for some \(S(n)=o(\sqrt n)\).  The displayed \(O_q(S^2\log n)\) target is explicit, comfortably sufficient for \(S=n^{1/3}\), and consistent with a local window whose number of shell incidences is quadratic in its width.

## 3. Exact implication to the target-radical theorem

The implication rests on a short Smith-normal-form lemma.

### Lemma 3.1: primitive-vector saturation bound

Let

\[
M\in M_{m\times r}(\mathbb Z),\qquad \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M=r,
\]

and let \(x\in\mathbb Z^r\) be nonzero.  Put

\[
h=\operatorname{cont}(x),
\qquad y=Mx,
\qquad g=\gcd(y_1,\ldots,y_m).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{g\mid h\,\sigma(M).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, for every prime \(p\),

\[
\boxed{v_p(g)\le v_p(h)+v_p(\sigma(M)).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Write \(x=h x_0\) with \(x_0\) primitive.  In Smith coordinates,

\[
UMV=D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r)
\]

(up to zero rows), and \(x'_0=V^{-1}x_0\) is still primitive because \(V\) is unimodular.

Fix \(p\).  Since \(x'_0\) is primitive, some coordinate \(x'_{0,i}\) is a \(p\)-adic unit.  If \(p^e\) divides every coordinate of \(Mx_0\), then it divides every coordinate of

\[
D x'_0.
\]

In particular,

\[
e\le v_p(d_i)\le v_p(d_r)=v_p(\sigma(M)).
\]

Multiplying back by \(h\) gives (3.2), hence (3.1).  No estimate on any determinant is used.

### Apply this to the shell residuals

Let

\[
g_{q,n,S}:=\gcd(R_{-S},\ldots,R_S).
\]

Since

\[
c_S=\gcd(A_0,R_{-S},\ldots,R_S),
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{c_S\mid g_{q,n,S}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

From (LS1)–(LS3) and Lemma 3.1,

\[
g_{q,n,S}\mid K_q\,\sigma(M_{q,n,S}),
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rad}(c_S)\mid
\operatorname{rad}\bigl(K_q\sigma(M_{q,n,S})\bigr).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Now let \(T_q(n)\) denote the fixed-\(q\) target radical.  The assumed exact target cover says

\[
T_q(n)\mid B_S c_S,
\qquad
B_S=\operatorname{rad}\Bigl(\prod_{j\le S+2}b_j\Bigr).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Because \(T_q(n)\) is squarefree, (3.4) and (3.5) imply prime by prime

\[
\boxed{
T_q(n)\mid
B_S\,\operatorname{rad}\bigl(K_q\sigma(M_{q,n,S})\bigr).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is the key target-prime statement.  It is not nuisance cleanup.

Indeed, if \(p\mid T_q(n)\) is a genuine target prime and \(p\nmid B_S\), then (3.5) forces \(p\mid c_S\).  Therefore \(p\) divides **every** residual in the symmetric window; by (LS1) and Lemma 3.1,

\[
\boxed{p\mid K_q\sigma(M_{q,n,S}).}
\tag{3.7}
\]

So every large target prime not already paid by the boundary factor is visibly charged to the characteristic-zero saturation factor.

Using

\[
\log B_S=O_q(S^2)
\]

and (LS4),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log T_q(n)
&\le \log B_S
 +\log\operatorname{rad}K_q
 +\log\operatorname{rad}\sigma(M_{q,n,S})\\
&\le O_q(S^2)+O_q(1)+C_qS^2\log(n+2).
\end{aligned}
\]

With \(S=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\),

\[
\boxed{
\log T_q(n)=O_q(n^{2/3}\log n)=o(n).
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus the candidate lemma is exactly strong enough for the desired all-index target-radical bound.

Notice that no control of \(v_p(c_S)\) beyond first support is needed for this implication, because the final target is a radical.  If a later argument needs the full integer \(c_S\), the same Smith lemma gives the valuation bound (3.2); but for the target radical, (LS4) only needs the radical of the largest Smith factor.

## 4. Why this is not equivalent to the dead scalar recurrence data

Fact (1) says arbitrary finite residual profiles are rationally realizable after denominator clearing.  Therefore the map

\[
(A_{-S-2},\ldots,A_S)\longmapsto(R_{-S},\ldots,R_S)
\]

by itself has no useful arithmetic rigidity.

The proposed matrix \(M_{q,n,S}\) is a different object.  Its columns are not arbitrary sequence coordinates.  They are the **transverse Laurent-shell/common-convolution columns** from which the actual shell residuals are assembled before the fixed origin coefficients are inserted.

In other words, recurrence-only data sees one scalar output sequence.  The saturation matrix sees the multiple Laurent-coefficient directions whose very special common combination produces that sequence.

The countermodel

\[
A_s=M\,b_{n+s}
\]

with all residuals zero is therefore irrelevant to (LS4): it does not come equipped with the same actual Laurent-shell packet \(X_{q,n,S}\), the same common-convolution columns, and the same bounded-content origin vector.  That is exactly the missing rigidity.

So the minimal shell-specific invariant is not “Newton weights are primitive.”  Primitive Newton weights only say an individual stencil row has content one.  The needed invariant is **projective primitivity of the common shell packet together with saturation of its multirow observation lattice**.

## 5. Why this is not a Green/exterior construction

Fact (2) kills the natural universal positive/negative Green tensors because their exterior products carry a large overlap primorial, and the ideal-compatible endpoint space collapses to one dimension.

Nothing in (LS1)–(LS4) takes a positive/negative exterior product.  There is no attempt to telescope the scalar Apéry operator, and no \(A_0^2-R_iR_{-j}\) height estimate.

The Smith factor measures whether a primitive latent shell direction can be mapped into \(p\mathbb Z^{2S+1}\).  This is a **column-lattice saturation question**, not an endpoint-tensor question.

Universal Green theory cannot see the common Laurent-convolution columns, so its one-dimensional endpoint obstruction does not imply that \(\sigma(M_{q,n,S})\) is large.

## 6. Why this does not conflict with the cyclotomic spectral obstruction

Fact (3) says the full-shell shift sequence has

\[
\Omega_q(n^2/\log n)
\]

distinct cyclotomic spectral roots, ruling out low-order constant-coefficient/Ore annihilation and bounded-source telescoping.

The saturation lemma does not postulate any short recurrence in \(s\).  The rows of \(M_{q,n,S}\) are allowed to contain the full shell complexity at each shift.  Only the **common divisibility of those rows after projection onto the actual coefficient packet** is controlled.

A matrix may have entries of exponential or superexponential height and enormous spectral complexity while its column lattice is saturated.  The extreme case is an integral matrix with huge entries but Smith form

\[
\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1).
\]

Therefore a small \(\sigma(M)\) is logically compatible with a huge spectral order.

This is why the right proof, if it exists, must be an exact divisibility/Bézout argument for the Smith quotient, not a small annihilator.

## 7. Why Lucas–Cartier support cleanup is not enough, but can feed this lemma

Fact (4) is important: a theorem saying “all non-target nuisance primes disappear from the shell packet” does not solve the problem because the large genuine target primes still divide \(c_S\).

The saturation lemma has the correct direction.  For a genuine target prime \(p\nmid B_S\), simultaneous residual vanishing forces

\[
p\mid\sigma(M)
\]

(up to the fixed \(K_q\)).  Thus the proof must bound the product of precisely those rank-drop primes, including the target ones.

Two-digit Lucas–Cartier formulas can still be useful only as an **input to proving (LS4)**: for example, by classifying modulo \(p\) when the actual common-convolution rows lose rank and then lifting that classification to a characteristic-zero Bézout identity.  Merely proving that the rank-drop support contains no nuisance primes is insufficient; one needs a characteristic-zero product bound for all rank-drop primes.

## 8. Why this is not the failed growing-minor/LLL route

Fact (5) includes growing-minor and LLL constructions with linear-height obstructions.  The distinction here is algebraically sharp.

A maximal minor measures

\[
\delta_r(M)=d_1d_2\cdots d_r.
\]

The saturation exponent is only

\[
\sigma(M)=d_r=\frac{\delta_r(M)}{\delta_{r-1}(M)}.
\]

Bounding one chosen maximal minor, or even bounding \(\delta_r\), is therefore overkill.  Large universal factors may occupy the earlier Smith invariants \(d_1,\ldots,d_{r-1}\) and disappear completely from the quotient relevant to primitive-vector divisibility.

So one should **not** search for a small determinant.  The candidate lemma requires an exact identity controlling the quotient of consecutive determinantal divisors, equivalently the exponent of the torsion cokernel.

A publication-grade proof could take any of the equivalent forms

\[
\sigma(M)\mid H_{q,n,S},
\qquad
\log\operatorname{rad}H_{q,n,S}=O_q(S^2\log n),
\]

or an explicit Bézout/saturation identity showing

\[
H_{q,n,S}\,\operatorname{Sat}(\operatorname{col}M)
\subseteq \operatorname{col}M.
\]

The coefficients in that Bézout identity may themselves be large; unlike a “small certificate value” argument, their archimedean size is not the target.  Only the integer \(H_{q,n,S}\) matters.

## 9. What the current repository actually supports

The current source has pieces pointing exactly toward this invariant, but not the theorem.

### `q32_terminal_directional_cofactor_audit.py`

This file builds the actual common-convolution observability rows

```text
(shell_column_0, ..., shell_column_4, -Y)
```

for the terminal packet, inserts the polynomial origin coefficient vector afterward, and forms directional cofactors.  Its stated proved/computational relation is a radical divisibility from raw \(2\times2\) content into directional-cofactor content.  Crucially, the stronger short divisibility by an origin-content factor times

\[
(n+2)\cdots(n+7)
\]

is explicitly labelled conjectural.  So this file is evidence for the correct **kind** of saturation invariant, not a proof of (LS4).

### `q32_lower_terminal_observability.sage`

This file computes the lower-packet observability minors after a Freshman reduction, but its own header says the calculation remains diagnostic until the unique-decomposition lemma and the resulting formulas are written down.  Therefore it cannot currently supply the required all-\(n\) rank-drop theorem.

It does, however, contain the exact algebraic mechanism for (LS3): the origin coefficient polynomials have gcd \(1\) over the rational polynomial ring, and clearing the denominators in a polynomial Bézout identity yields one fixed integer containing every pointwise origin content.

### `q32_content_smith.py`

This is a useful model of what a successful proof would look like: in a different fixed-\(q\) Legendre–Euler content problem it identifies an exact integral Smith block, rather than inferring structure from numerical gcds.  The missing theorem here needs the analogous exact Smith statement for the **actual full-Newton Laurent-shell residual observation matrix**.

### `q32_fixed_minor_jet_audit.sage`

This works with first divided digits modulo \(p^2\) and fixed ghost minors.  It is not the requested characteristic-zero growing-window saturation theorem and should not be promoted into one without a genuine global divisibility identity.

## 10. Minimal actual-Laurent-shell invariant that must enter

Even if one does not adopt the matrix notation above, the irreducible invariant is the following.

For the actual shell, take the local latent coefficient packet before it is collapsed to the scalar \(A_s\), and consider the integer lattice generated by its residual-observation columns over the growing symmetric window.  One must control

\[
\boxed{
\text{(projective content of the actual latent packet)}
\times
\text{(saturation exponent of its observation lattice)}.
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

The first factor prevents an arbitrary common scale from being hidden in the latent shell state.  The second prevents a primitive latent state from mapping into \(p\mathbb Z^{2S+1}\) for too many primes.

This invariant is strictly finer than recurrence/Green data:

- recurrence data forgets all transverse Laurent directions and hence permits arbitrary residual profiles;
- Green data is universal for the scalar three-term operator and therefore also forgets those directions;
- Newton-row primitivity is only a one-row statement and cannot control a common scale across many shell columns;
- the saturation exponent is a multirow, multi-column property of the **actual Laurent packet**.

That is the minimum new information a proof must exploit.

## 11. Final strategic statement

The all-index problem is not best described as “find a tiny integer linear combination of \(A_0,R_{-S},\ldots,R_S\).”  All known ways of doing that run into exactly the linear-height/overlap/spectral obstructions already recorded.

The clean remaining formulation is:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Prove that the growing actual-shell residual observation lattice has sublinear radical saturation.}
}
\]

Concretely, the single candidate theorem is (LS1)–(LS4).  Its implication is the exact divisibility

\[
T_q(n)\mid
B_{\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor}
\operatorname{rad}\!\left(
K_q\,\sigma(M_{q,n,\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor})
\right),
\]

followed by

\[
\log B_S=O_q(S^2),
\qquad
\log\operatorname{rad}\sigma(M)=O_q(S^2\log n),
\]

which gives

\[
\log T_q(n)=o(n).
\]

This does not solve the conjecture: the exact growing-window factorization must be written at the full Laurent/Newton level, and the saturation estimate (LS4) is open.  But it is a genuinely different arithmetic target from the already-dead recurrence/Green/annihilator/small-minor routes, and—most importantly—it charges every surviving **genuine target prime** to a characteristic-zero structural divisor rather than merely removing nuisance support.
