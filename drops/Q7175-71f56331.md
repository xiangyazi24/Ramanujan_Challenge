# Audit of the Short-Chain State Uniqueness claim

## Conclusion

The proposed statement

\[ C_p(q)\leq 1 \]

is **not proved by the current identities** in `proof.tex`/`AVENUES.md`. The computation up to p<=5000 is strong evidence for a useful phenomenon, but it does not imply a theorem. The available reflection and continuant identities give uniqueness only in restricted overlap regimes. They do not rule out two primitive non-all-equal off-center chains sharing the same projective state.

No exact counterexample was found from the stated data range. Therefore the correct audit result is:

**PASS as an empirical invariant; FAIL as a claimed unconditional theorem.**

## What sharing a state actually gives

Let two chains have ordered return sets

\[
R=(r_0<r_1<\cdots<r_a),\qquad S=(s_0<s_1<\cdots<s_b)
\]

with the same projective state q. Equality of the state gives equality of the corresponding projective continuants (equivalently the same point in the transfer orbit). The determinant/Wronskian identity gives that two continuant representations cannot be independent when their coefficient ranges overlap too strongly.

A valid consequence is:

* if the two chains share a sufficiently long initial segment of returns;
* or if the union of the two gap words forces a determinant with absolute value < p;

then the determinant must vanish over the integers, forcing the two chains to coincide after removing the reflection ambiguity.

However, this requires an additional small-height hypothesis on the resulting determinant. The hypotheses "span <= sqrt(p)" and "same state" alone do not supply that bound.

## Reflection issue

A chain and its reflected chain must not be counted as two witnesses. The canonical orientation used by the verifier should choose one of:

1. lexicographically smaller gap word;
2. smaller first return coordinate after translating the carrier normalization;
3. a fixed half-orbit convention.

Without this quotient, the reflected copy is an immediate duplicate construction, not a genuine violation of uniqueness.

## Why the observed data does not prove the theorem

The observed maximum

\[
\max_q C_p(q)=1
\]

for p<=5000 only verifies the finite search range. A contradiction argument would need a theorem of the following type:

> If two primitive chains of span H share q, then a nonzero integer continuant determinant D exists with p|D and |D|<p whenever p>H^2.

The current continuant formulas do not establish this. The determinant height can depend on the full pair of gap sequences, not only H^2. Reflection reduces some degrees of freedom but does not automatically make the determinant small enough.

## A provable restricted statement

One can prove a weaker overlap lemma:

If two chains sharing q have overlap length m in their return sequences and the associated continuant determinant satisfies

\[
|D|<p,
\]

then D=0 modulo p, hence the two chains have the same reduced projective word (up to reflection).

This is useful for pruning candidate collisions but is not the full state uniqueness theorem.

## Required next step

To upgrade this to C_p(q)<=1 one needs a new ingredient:

* a canonical minimal-gap tree with bounded determinant height;
* or an arithmetic equidistribution/monodromy statement controlling collisions of short return words;
* or an exact classification of possible short continuant coincidences.

The current verifier data supports the conjecture but does not contain enough structure for a proof.
