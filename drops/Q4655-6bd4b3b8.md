# 2. The exact affine tight frame

Let \(A\subset\mathbb F_q\), \(|A|=Z\), and

\[
f=1_A-\frac Zq.
\]

For \(g=(\lambda,\beta)\in G_q:=\operatorname{Aff}_1(\mathbb F_q)\), let

\[
(\rho_gf)(x)=f(\lambda x+\beta).
\]

Then \(\rho_g\) is unitary on \(\ell^2(\mathbb F_q)\).

## Lemma 2.1 — affine tight-frame identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{g\in G_q}|\rho_gf\rangle\langle\rho_gf|
=Z(q-Z)P_0.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Let \(S\) denote the operator on the left and write its matrix kernel as \(S(x,y)\).

If \(x=y\), then for each \(\lambda\ne0\), translation by \(\beta\) runs \(\lambda x+\beta\) through all of \(\mathbb F_q\).  Thus

\[
S(x,x)
=(q-1)\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_q}f(u)^2
=(q-1)\|f\|_2^2.
\]

If \(x\ne y\), sharp two-transitivity says

\[
(\lambda x+\beta,\lambda y+\beta)
\]

runs once through every ordered pair \((u,v)\) with \(u\ne v\).  Therefore

\[
S(x,y)
=\sum_{u\ne v}f(u)f(v)
=\left(\sum_u f(u)\right)^2-\sum_u f(u)^2
=-\|f\|_2^2.
\]

Since

\[
\|f\|_2^2=Z-\frac{Z^2}q,
\]

the matrix just computed is

\[
q\|f\|_2^2 P_0=Z(q-Z)P_0.
\]

This proves (2.1).  \(\square\)

## Corollary 2.2 — Bessel inequality for any affine subfamily

For arbitrary coefficients \(a_g\),

\[
\boxed{
\left\|\sum_g a_g\rho_gf\right\|_2^2
\le Z(q-Z)\sum_g|a_g|^2.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is exact finite-field harmonic analysis.  No property of \(A\) besides being a literal subset is used.

For \(A=\mathcal K_q\), the inner products are exactly centered affine correlations:

\[
\langle \rho_gf_q,\rho_hf_q\rangle
=\operatorname{AC}_q(hg^{-1})-\frac{Z(q)^2}{q}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the generic affine covariance matrix is positive semidefinite.

# 6. Recurrence / companion / Cartier attack

Write the centered affine relation in uncentered zero positions as

\[
s'\equiv\lambda s+\gamma\pmod q,
\qquad
\gamma=\beta+(1-\lambda)c_q.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The Apéry recurrence is

\[
(u+1)^3y_{u+1}=P(u)y_u-u^3y_{u-1}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Its two singular index classes are \(u=0\) and \(u=-1\).  Consequently any affine reindexing

\[
\psi(u)=\lambda u+\gamma
\]

which carries the same second-order recurrence to itself up to multiplication by nowhere-vanishing scalar/gauge factors must permute these two singular classes.  Therefore

\[
\{\psi(0),\psi(-1)\}
=\{0,-1\},
\]

that is

\[
\{\gamma,\gamma-\lambda\}=\{0,-1\}.
\]

The only solutions are

\[
\boxed{(\lambda,\gamma)=(1,0),\quad(-1,-1).}
\tag{6.3}
\]

# 7. Gap-polynomial / resultant attack and exact saturation

Fix a generic affine map \(g(\ell)=\lambda\ell+\beta\), \(\lambda\ne1\), and suppose

\[
\ell\in\mathcal K_q,
\qquad
\ell'=g(\ell)\in\mathcal K_q.
\]

Let

\[
s=c_q+\ell,
\qquad
s'=c_q+\ell'
\]

be the corresponding representatives in \([0,q-1]\).

A generic affine map has at most one fixed point.  For every other incidence \(s\ne s'\), put

\[
h=|s-s'|.
\]

No-consecutive-zeros gives \(h\ne1\).  Hence \(h\ge2\), and the canonical gap-polynomial theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
q\mid N_h(\min(s,s')).}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Canonical positivity gives

\[
N_h(\min(s,s'))\ge1.
\]

The tridiagonal determinant defining \(N_h\) has entries of size \(q^{O(1)}\), so

\[
\boxed{
\log N_h(\min(s,s'))\ll h\log q.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus \(M\) affine incidences give a nonzero carrier product \(R\) with

\[
q^{M-1}\mid R,
\qquad
\log|R|\ll\left(\sum_i h_i\right)\log q.
\]

The resulting capacity inequality is only

\[
M\ll1+\sum_i h_i.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since every nontrivial gap is already at least two, (7.3) is tautological at the scale needed here.  In the high-centered generic sector there is no reason for \(\sum h_i\) to be sublinear in \(M\).

Trying to pair two incidences by a resultant does not improve the height.  The source First Lemma proves nonvanishing for the special shifted pair

\[
N_d(x),\quad N_r(x+d),
\]

because their root strips are disjoint.  A generic affine pair would require a resultant of the shape

\[
\operatorname{Res}_x
\bigl(N_h(x),N_{h'}(\lambda x+\gamma)\bigr).
\]

The canonical root-strip theorem does not make these transformed strips uniformly disjoint, and even when such a resultant is nonzero its degree/height grows with both moving gaps.  Multiplying over the affine star again makes certificate height grow at the same or a faster rate than the number of marked incidences.

This is the first exact saturation of the direct elimination route.

\begin{lemma}[Affine tight frame for a centered zero set]
\label{lem:ad-affine-tight-frame}
Let $q$ be prime, let $A\subset\mathbb F_q$, $|A|=Z$, and put
\[
 f=1_A-\frac Zq.
\]
For $(\lambda,\beta)\in\mathbb F_q^\times\ltimes\mathbb F_q$ set
\[
 (\rho_{\lambda,\beta}f)(x)=f(\lambda x+\beta).
\]
If $P_0$ denotes orthogonal projection onto the mean-zero subspace of
$\ell^2(\mathbb F_q)$, then
\[
 \sum_{\lambda\ne0}\sum_{\beta\bmod q}
 |\rho_{\lambda,\beta}f\rangle
 \langle\rho_{\lambda,\beta}f|
 =Z(q-Z)P_0.
\]
Consequently, for every scalar family $(a_{\lambda,\beta})$,
\[
 \left\|\sum_{\lambda,\beta}
 a_{\lambda,\beta}\rho_{\lambda,\beta}f\right\|_2^2
 \le Z(q-Z)\sum_{\lambda,\beta}|a_{\lambda,\beta}|^2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $S$ be the operator on the left.  If $x=y$, then for each
$\lambda\ne0$ the variable $\lambda x+\beta$ runs through all of
$\mathbb F_q$, so
\[
 S(x,x)=(q-1)\|f\|_2^2.
\]
If $x\ne y$, sharp two-transitivity of
$\operatorname{Aff}_1(\mathbb F_q)$ gives
\[
 S(x,y)=\sum_{u\ne v}f(u)f(v)=-\|f\|_2^2,
\]
because $\sum_u f(u)=0$.  Hence
\[
 S=q\|f\|_2^2P_0.
\]
Since $\|f\|_2^2=Z-Z^2/q$, this is $Z(q-Z)P_0$.
The Bessel inequality follows by taking the operator norm.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Prime-averaged affine coefficient square]
\label{prop:ad-affine-prime-averaged-square}
Fix a smooth prime-box weight $v$, put
\[
 u_p=\frac{\log p}{p}v(p/N),
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_p=\left\{r-\frac{p-1}{2}:r\in\mathcal Z_p\right\},
\]
and let $M_{p,k;q,J}\in\{0,1\}$ and
$|\theta_{p,k;q,J}|\le1$ be arbitrary deletion masks and phases.
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{q\asymp N}|u_q|^2\sum_{J\bmod q}
\left|
 \sum_{\substack{p\asymp N,\ p\ne q\\k\in\mathcal K_p}}
 u_p\theta_{p,k;q,J}M_{p,k;q,J}
 1_{\mathcal K_q}(k+pJ)
\right|^2\\
&\hspace{30mm}\ll_v N^{7/15+o(1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If the coefficients are independent of $J$ and the exact affine mean is
subtracted, then the stronger centered estimate holds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{q\asymp N}|u_q|^2\sum_{J\bmod q}
\left|
 \sum_{\substack{p\asymp N,\ p\ne q\\k\in\mathcal K_p}}
 c_{p,k}
 \left(1_{\mathcal K_q}(k+pJ)-\frac{Z(q)}q\right)
\right|^2\\
&\hspace{30mm}\ll_v N^{1/5+o(1)},
\qquad |c_{p,k}|\le|u_p|.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 L_N=\sum_p|u_p|Z(p),\qquad
 E_N=\sum_p|u_p|^2Z(p).
\]
The canonical first zero moment and $Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}$ give
\[
 L_N\ll N^{3/5+o(1)},\qquad
 E_N\ll N^{-2/5+o(1)}.
\]
For fixed $q$, the map $(p,k)\mapsto(J\mapsto k+pJ)$ has bounded
multiplicity on fixed ratio boxes.  Group equal affine maps.  If the
grouped positive coefficients are $a_g$, then
\[
 \sum_g a_g^2\ll E_N,\qquad \sum_g a_g\le L_N.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:ad-affine-tight-frame} to
$f_q=1_{\mathcal K_q}-Z(q)/q$.  Orthogonality to constants gives
\[
 \sum_J\left(\sum_ga_g1_{\mathcal K_q}(gJ)\right)^2
 \ll \frac{Z(q)^2}{q}L_N^2+qZ(q)E_N.
\]

is
\[
 \ll N E_N^2\ll N^{1/5+o(1)}.
\]
For the first term use

assertion follows directly from the tight-frame Bessel inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Centered affine covariance is a Schur contraction]
\label{lem:ad-affine-schur}
Let $v_i$ be vectors in a Hilbert space and put
$G_{ij}=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle$.  Then for every scalar matrix $T$,
\[
 \|G\circ T\|_{2\to2}
 \le\left(\max_i\|v_i\|^2\right)\|T\|_{2\to2}.
\]
In particular, for
\[
 v_{p,k}(J)=m_{p,k}(J)
 \left(1_{\mathcal K_q}(k+pJ)-\frac{Z(q)}q\right),
 \qquad |m_{p,k}(J)|\le1,
\]
the associated masked centered affine covariance is a Schur multiplier
of operator norm at most $Z(q)-Z(q)^2/q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define $V e_i=e_i\otimes v_i$.  Then
\[
 G\circ T=V^*(T\otimes I)V,
\]
and
\[
 \|V\|^2=\max_i\|v_i\|^2.
\]
The stated estimate follows.
\end{proof}
