# Q5790: corrected full-margin saturation audit

## Verdict

The correction is essential. The previous target-persistence argument incorrectly reduced the normalized determinant modulo `p` after dividing by the binomial normalizer `C=binom(d+L+1,L)`. At an interior target node `p-1=d+j`, one has `v_p(C)=1`, so this division is not a legal operation in `F_p`.

The correct argument is valuation-theoretic:

* Each carrier satisfies `G_{d,L}(Y) ≡ Y_{p-1} (mod p)` because all stencil weights except the distinguished node vanish modulo `p`.
* At a target, `p | Y_{p-1}` and similarly for the second sequence, hence every entry in the 2x2 determinant is divisible by `p`.
* Therefore the unnormalised determinant has valuation at least two.
* The primitive normalization removes exactly the forced factor `p` coming from `v_p(C)=1`, leaving `p | E_{d,L}`.

For a non-target node, only the universal proportionality layer exists; it gives one factor of `p`, not a target divisor after normalization.

## Integral identities

Write

`E_{d,L}=det(G_{d,L}(Y),G_{d,L}(Z);G_{d+1,L}(Y),G_{d+1,L}(Z))/C_{d,L}`.

The Newton carrier identities are

`G_{d,L}-G_{d+1,L}=(-1)^{L+1} binom(d+L+1,L) Delta^{L+1}Y_d`.

Hence the normalized determinant is exactly

`E_{d,L}=(-1)^{L+1}(Delta^{L+1}Y_d B_d-Delta^{L+1}Z_d A_d)`.

This identity is integral and is the correct starting point for horizontal/vertical propagation.

## Horizontal relation

The first horizontal relation comes from applying the adjacent carrier identity to both columns:

`A_d-A_{d+1}=C_{d,L}(-1)^{L+1} Delta^{L+1}Y_d`,

`B_d-B_{d+1}=C_{d,L}(-1)^{L+1} Delta^{L+1}Z_d`.

Expanding the difference of adjacent minors gives

`C_{d,L} E_{d,L}=det(A_d,B_d;A_{d+1},B_{d+1})`.

The next horizontal difference is therefore controlled by the pair

`(Delta^{L+1}Y_d, Delta^{L+1}Z_d)`

rather than by a new independent target scalar. After saturation, the only guaranteed content is the gcd of the adjacent normalizers and the common finite-difference factors.

## Vertical relation

Changing `L` gives the analogous identity from the Pascal relation in the second binomial coefficient:

`G_{d,L+1}-G_{d,L}`

is an integral linear combination of `G_{d,L}` and a single `(L+1)`st difference term. Consequently

`E_{d,L+1}` is an integral combination of `E_{d,L}` and determinants involving one higher finite difference.

The coefficient denominators are precisely the danger: they cannot be cancelled modulo a target prime. Any iteration must be performed in the saturated integer lattice generated by the primitive minors.

## Candidate full-margin theorem

The strongest realistic statement is not that the minors themselves have a simple recurrence, but:

For a full first-cell rectangle of primitive minors, the determinantal divisor of the saturated lattice divides

`K * prod(boundary factors)`

where `K` is an absolute content constant and the boundary factors arise from endpoint finite-difference evaluations at the two cell boundaries.

The proof strategy is:

1. Form the integer matrix whose rows are translated primitive minors.
2. Apply Smith reduction over `Z`, not modulo target primes.
3. Use horizontal/vertical Pascal relations to replace interior rows by boundary rows.
4. Identify the final determinantal divisor with endpoint evaluations.

What is proved: the finite-difference/Pascal reduction exists integrally.

What remains conjectural: the final endpoint divisor has only polynomial height in `n`.

## Target persistence

The corrected target statement is:

`p | E_{d,L}` whenever the common stencil contains `p-1` and `p | Y_{p-1}` (and the corresponding second component condition needed for the determinant).

It is not obtained by cancelling `C` modulo `p`; it follows from valuations before normalization.

For the Apéry specialization the candidate congruence is

`E_{d,L}=unit * b_{n-p} * Delta^{L+1}W_d (mod p)`,

with `W=Z-M(n)Y`, but this does not by itself imply a second independent relation.

## Conclusion

Full-margin saturation remains a plausible route because gcd aggregation permits long Euclidean chains without multiplying all coefficients. The correct object is a saturated determinantal divisor. The main unresolved theorem is an endpoint reduction theorem proving that the saturated divisor has only boundary linear factors and bounded content.
