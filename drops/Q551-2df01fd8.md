# Equidistribution of the Apéry transfer matrix orbit

## Summary

The right framework is **algebraic-group orbit equidistribution / trace-function theory**, not elementary random matrix theory. The heuristic that a transfer matrix product lands in a codimension-one subset with probability about \(1/p\) is exactly the type of statement proved by Deligne-Katz style theorems, but the object is not an i.i.d. random walk in \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\). It is a deterministic orbit generated by the rational map \(n\mapsto M(n)\).

The rigorous target is to prove that the family

\[
T_{r,r+h}=M(r+h-1)\cdots M(r)
\]

has sufficiently large geometric monodromy, and then apply equidistribution of Frobenius.

---

# 1. Why random matrix theory is not the correct model

A naive model says:

\[
M(n)\sim \text{random element of }SL_2(\mathbf F_p),
\]

so

\[
\Pr((T_{r,s})_{12}=0)\approx\frac1p.
\]

This predicts the observed result:

\[
\#\{(r,s):T_{12}=0\}\approx p.
\]

However, the matrices are not independent. They come from the single rational recurrence

\[
M(n)=
\begin{pmatrix}
A(n)&-B(n)\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore the correct question is not whether a random walk mixes, but whether the algebraic family generated by this cocycle has full monodromy.

---

# 2. The algebraic-group formulation

Consider the difference equation

\[
\binom{x_{n+1}}{x_n}=M(n)\binom{x_n}{x_{n-1}}.
\]

The transfer matrix is a fundamental solution matrix of a rank-2 difference connection.

The relevant object is the difference Galois group / geometric monodromy group

\[
G\subseteq GL_2.
\]

The desired genericity statement is:

\[
G=SL_2 \quad\text{(or a large subgroup)}.
\]

Once this holds, the entries of transfer matrices behave like generic matrix coefficients of \(SL_2\).

---

# 3. The zero condition as an algebraic subset

The condition

\[
T_{12}=0
\]

inside \(SL_2\) defines a hypersurface.

For a matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
\]

this is simply

\[
b=0.
\]

The subset

\[
\{g\in SL_2:g_{12}=0\}
\]

has codimension one.

For a uniformly distributed element of \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\), the Lang-Weil estimate gives approximately

\[
\frac{|\{g:g_{12}=0\}|}{|SL_2(\mathbf F_p)|}
\sim \frac1p.
\]

This is exactly the observed density.

---

# 4. What theorem is needed?

A possible rigorous route is:

1. Construct the l-adic sheaf corresponding to the transfer cocycle.

2. Prove its geometric monodromy group is large:

\[
G_{geom}=SL_2
\]

(or the appropriate symplectic subgroup inherited from the Apéry motive).

3. Apply Deligne's equidistribution theorem:

Frobenius conjugacy classes become equidistributed in the compact form of \(G\).

Then for a regular function \(f(g)=g_{12}\),

\[
\#\{r:f(T_r)=0\}
\]

is controlled by the geometric size of the zero locus.

---

# 5. Connection with the Apéry Sp(4) motive

The original Apéry object has rank 4 monodromy

\[
Sp_4.
\]

The transfer matrix is a rank-2 shadow obtained from the recurrence.

The correct relationship is likely:

\[
\text{Apéry }Sp_4\text{ monodromy}
\Rightarrow
\text{large rank-2 transfer monodromy}.
\]

This is stronger than saying the recurrence coefficients look random.

The empirical facts:

- 38363 tests,
- zero exceptions,
- density matching \(1/p\),

are exactly what one expects from a large monodromy theorem.

---

# 6. Why Schwartz-Zippel loses a factor \(p\)

For fixed gap \(h\),

\[
T_{12}(r,r+h)
\]

has degree \(O(h)\), hence

\[
N_h\le O(h).
\]

But this is a worst-case algebraic bound.

A generic degree-\(h\) polynomial over \(\mathbf F_p\) has expected zero count about one.

The monodromy theorem replaces:

\[
N_h\le h
\]

with

\[
N_h=1+O(p^{-1/2})
\]

on average.

Then

\[
\sum_hN_h=O(p).
\]

---

# 7. Concrete proof program for P3.2

A realistic rigorous program is:

### Step 1
Define the transfer sheaf

\[
\mathcal T_h(r)=M(r+h-1)\cdots M(r).
\]

### Step 2
Show geometric irreducibility and compute its monodromy group.

### Step 3
Apply Deligne's Riemann hypothesis for trace functions.

### Step 4
Estimate the zero fiber

\[
\#\{r:\operatorname{Tr}_{12}(\mathcal T_h(r))=0\}.
\]

### Step 5
Average over \(h\).

This is the algebraic-geometric analogue of the random matrix heuristic.

---

# Conclusion

The \(1/p\) density is not expected to come from Chevalley-Warning or degree counting. The correct framework is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{l-adic sheaf attached to the Apéry recurrence}
+
\text{large geometric monodromy}
+
\text{Deligne/Katz equidistribution}
}
\]

Random matrix theory gives the correct heuristic, but algebraic group orbit equidistribution is the rigorous replacement.

The key missing theorem for P3.2 is therefore a monodromy computation: prove that the Apéry transfer cocycle has sufficiently large geometric Galois group. Once that is established, the observed \(p/C\) pair-zero count should follow from standard trace-function technology.
