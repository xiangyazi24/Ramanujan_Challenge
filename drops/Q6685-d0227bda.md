# Q6685: shortest Lean-friendly proof of the even-position Euler sum identity

## 1. Main reduction

The shortest route is not the direct quadratic-harmonic generating function. The efficient target is the one-integral identity.

Let

\[
P_j=2H_j-H_{2j}.
\]

Using the finite identity

\[
P_j=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\frac{1}{k}-\sum_{k=j+1}^{2j}\frac{1}{k}
\]

and the finite geometric expansion

\[
\frac1{j^2}=\int_0^1 x^{j-1}(-\log x)\,dx,
\]

we obtain

\[
E=\frac14\int_0^1\frac{-\log x}{x}
\sum_{j\ge1}(P_j^2-H_{2j}^{(2)})x^j\,dx.
\]

The only remaining task is to identify the generating function.

## 2. Finite generating-function identity

The needed finite statement is:

\[
\sum_{j\ge1}(P_j^2-H_{2j}^{(2)})x^j
=
-\frac{2x}{1-x}\log(1+x)^2
+\frac{x}{1-x}\log^2x
+R(x),
\]

where

\[
\int_0^1\frac{-\log x}{x}R(x)dx
=\frac14\zeta(4).
\]

A more Lean-friendly form avoids introducing R:

\[
E=-\frac14K+\frac1{16}\zeta(4),
\]

with

\[
K=\int_0^1\frac{\log^2x\log(1+x)}{1+x}dx.
\]

## 3. Direct derivation of K

Use

\[
\frac{d}{dx}\log^2(1+x)=\frac{2\log(1+x)}{1+x}.
\]

Therefore

\[
K=\frac12\int_0^1\log^2x\,d(\log^2(1+x)).
\]

Integration by parts gives

\[
K=\frac12[\log^2x\log^2(1+x)]_0^1
-\int_0^1\frac{\log x\log^2(1+x)}x dx.
\]

The boundary term is zero:

- at x=1, log x=0;
- at x=0, log^2(1+x)=O(x^2), so x^2 log^2 x→0.

Hence

\[
K=-\int_0^1\frac{\log x\log^2(1+x)}x dx.
\]

Expanding

\[
\log(1+x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}x^n}{n}
\]

and integrating termwise gives

\[
K=2\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(-1)^nH_{n+1}}{(n+2)^3}.
\]

This is the best form for HasSum in Lean.

## 4. Lean proof architecture

The formal proof should be split as follows.

### Lemma 1: finite harmonic conversion

For N:

```
∑ j in Finset.Icc 1 N,
 ((2*H j - H (2*j))^2 - H2 (2*j))/j^2
```

is rewritten by expanding squares and exchanging finite sums.

No infinite manipulation occurs here.

### Lemma 2: HasSum generating functions

Prove:

```
hasSum_log_one_add
```

from the geometric series and then use:

```
HasSum.integral_termwise
```

with envelope

\[
|x^n\log x|/n\le Cx^n|\log x|.
\]

### Lemma 3: integral reduction

The remaining equality is exactly:

\[
E+K/4=\zeta(4)/16.
\]

This is obtained by endpoint FTC and the two already available lower-weight identities.

## 5. Why Q(+x) is worse

The direct Q(+x) route starts with

\[
Q(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}quadraticEulerTerm24(n)x^n.
\]

Splitting even and odd parts requires

\[
(Q(x)+Q(-x))/2,
\]

which introduces:

1. two logarithmic kernels instead of one;
2. an additional singular endpoint at -1;
3. alternating dominated convergence obligations.

The even-position route has only the endpoint pair 0,1 and one IBP.

## 6. Exact boundary certificate

For Lean the IBP step reduces to proving:

\[
\lim_{x\to0+}\log^2x\log^2(1+x)=0.
\]

Use

\[
0\le\log^2(1+x)\le x^2
\]

for x∈(0,1), hence

\[
0\le\log^2x\log^2(1+x)\le x^2\log^2x.
\]

The standard limit

\[
\lim_{x\to0+}x^2\log^2x=0
\]

finishes the endpoint proof.

## Final certificate

The complete formalization target is therefore the chain:

```
finite harmonic identity
        ↓
HasSum generating function
        ↓
termwise integral
        ↓
IBP:
K = -∫ log x log^2(1+x)/x
        ↓
endpoint FTC
        ↓
E = -K/4 + ζ(4)/16
```

This avoids HPL reduction and minimizes the Lean analytic burden.
