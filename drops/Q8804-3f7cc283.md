# Frobenius compression for the Apéry digital large sieve

## Executive verdict

The synthesis is the right conceptual direction, but the decisive compression step does **not** currently exist. The obstruction is very precise:

\[
 b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p
\]

is not a bounded-complexity Frobenius condition attached only to the prime p. It is a **moving-index orbit hitting problem** in a family of local systems. The dependence on the residue r=n mod p forces the complexity to grow with p.

The promising replacement is not a single Frobenius condition, but a two-variable trace-function problem in the pair (p,r). A successful theorem would resemble a Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem for a family of Apéry orbit trace functions.

---

# 1. Why the naive Frobenius compression fails

The recurrence defines a rank-two local system:

\[
v_{d+1}=M(d)v_d,
\]

where

\[
v_d=(b_d,b_{d-1})^T.
\]

The condition

\[
b_r=0\pmod p
\]

is equivalent to

\[
M(r-1)\cdots M(0)v_0\in \{[0:1]\}.
\]

This is a legitimate geometric condition, but it is not a fixed Frobenius conjugacy condition.

A Frobenius condition normally has the form:

\[
\mathrm{Frob}_p\in C\subset G
\]

for a fixed algebraic group G and fixed conjugacy subset C.

Here instead we have:

\[
\mathrm{Frob}_{p,r}:
M(r-1)\cdots M(0),
\]

where the object itself changes with r.

Since

\[
0\le r<p,
\]

there are p possible transfer products at prime p. Therefore the conductor/complexity of the family grows with p.

This is the fundamental obstruction.

---

# 2. Does tensor power help?

Suppose we try to encode

\[
b_r=0
\]

as a matrix coefficient.

Let

\[
T_r=M(r-1)\cdots M(0).
\]

The condition is

\[
e_1^TT_re_2=0.
\]

Matrix coefficients can be converted into traces:

\[
e_1^TT_re_2=\mathrm{Tr}(T_rE)
\]

for a rank-one matrix E.

So formally this is a trace condition.

However T_r is not a fixed representation of Frobenius. It is an r-step monodromy product.

A tensor-power representation does not solve this:

\[
T_r^{\otimes k}
\]

has rank 2^k, but k must grow if one wants to encode an arbitrary length-r product as bounded data.

Hence the required rank is effectively unbounded.

Answer to Q1: **no bounded-rank tensor construction is known or expected from the current local system.**

---

# 3. The real obstruction is the moving target

The large sieve wants a family like:

\[
\{p: \mathrm{Frob}_p\in C_n\}.
\]

Here we have:

\[
\{p:b_{n-p}=0\pmod p\}.
\]

The target changes with p.

For each prime:

\[
r_p=n-p.
\]

Therefore the tested orbit length is approximately p/2.

The family is not:

\[
p\mapsto \mathrm{Frob}_p(X)
\]

but:

\[
p\mapsto \mathrm{Frob}_p(X_{r_p}).
\]

This is analogous to asking Chebotarev to control Frobenius at a varying point on a varying fiber.

That is outside the standard Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel framework.

---

# 4. Is the orbit equidistribution route better?

Possibly, but it is a different theorem.

The desired statement would be:

For most primes p,

\[
\{T_r v_0:0\le r<p\}
\]

is equidistributed in P^1(F_p).

Then one expects

\[
|Z_p|\approx1.
\]

This explains the observed Poisson law.

However LGTQ-3 needs a quenched statement:

fixed n, varying p:

\[
\sum_{p>\sqrt n}1_{b_{n\bmod p}=0}=o(n/\log n).
\]

Orbit equidistribution gives fixed p, varying r.

These are dual but not equivalent.

A second layer of large sieve across primes is still required.

---

# 5. Could Bombieri--Vinogradov solve it?

The analogy is useful.

Bombieri--Vinogradov controls

\[
\sum_{q<Q}\max_a\left|\pi(x;q,a)-\frac{\mathrm{Li}(x)}{\phi(q)}\right|.
\]

The Apéry analogue would require controlling

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}1_{b_{n\bmod p}=0}-\sum_{p\sim X}\frac{|F_p|}{p}.
\]

The expected main term is small:

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}\frac{|F_p|}{p}\approx O(1).
\]

The difficulty is obtaining cancellation uniformly in n.

This is a moving-modulus digital large sieve problem.

Known Bombieri--Vinogradov does not apply because the residue condition is nonlinear:

\[
n\bmod p\in F_p.
\]

The sets F_p are not fixed residue classes.

---

# 6. Could the modular form make F_p computable?

This is the most promising sub-question.

If one had an identity

\[
b_{n-p}\pmod p
=
\Phi_n(a_p(f),p)
\]

for a fixed weight-four modular form f, then the problem would become Frobenius nonvanishing.

One could then use:

- Sato--Tate type distributions,
- Chebotarev,
- large sieve for Frobenius.

But currently known Lucas congruence only gives:

\[
b_{mp+r}\equiv b_m b_r\pmod p.
\]

For the top window this gives no simplification because

\[
n=p+(n-p)
\]

has quotient 1:

\[
b_n\equiv b_1b_{n-p}=5b_{n-p}\pmod p.
\]

It reduces the problem to the same moving residue.

No known formula expresses b_{n-p} only through Frobenius data of p.

---

# 7. Digital automata viewpoint

The automaton statement is correct:

\[
b_d=0
\]

is recognized by the digit automaton with forbidden digits F_p.

But automata theory does not give the needed maximum bound.

Cobham's theorem concerns one fixed set recognized in two fixed multiplicatively independent bases.

Here the automaton itself changes with p:

\[
\mathcal A_p.
\]

There is no common base-presentation object.

The intersection

\[
\bigcap_p\{n:n\bmod p\notin F_p\}
\]

is not a Cobham situation.

The correct analogy is a family of p-automatic sequences with growing alphabet and growing automaton.

---

# 8. What would actually close the proof?

A sufficient theorem would be:

## Apéry digital large sieve conjecture

For every N,

\[
\max_{n\le N}
\sum_{p\sim X}
1_{b_{n\bmod p}=0}
\ll X^{1-\delta}
\]

for some δ>0.

The expected value is O(1), so this has enormous room.

A weaker logarithmic bound would already solve the LGTQ-3 obstruction.

The needed ingredients are likely:

1. two-variable exponential sum estimates
\[
\sum_{p,r}e_p(ub_r)W(r/p),
\]

2. p-adic orbit mixing for the Apéry recurrence,

3. a genuine cross-prime independence theorem.

---

# Final answers

## Q1

No. The zero condition is not currently a bounded-complexity Frobenius condition. The obstruction is the growing transfer product length.

## Q2

Tensor powers do not fix the problem. The required rank grows with the orbit length.

## Q3

The best hope is a trace-function interpretation in two variables (p,r), not a single sheaf over Spec(Z).

## Q4

Orbit equidistribution would explain |Z_p|≈1 but does not automatically give the all-n bound.

## Q5

Bombieri--Vinogradov is the right analogy, but the missing theorem is a new digital/Frobenius large sieve.

## Overall verdict

The Frobenius compression idea identifies the correct missing bridge, but the bridge is not compression of F_p to one Frobenius conjugacy class. The true target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a two-variable large sieve for Apéry Frobenius orbit traces.}
}
\]

This is substantially more plausible than controlling |Z_p| pointwise, and it directly attacks LGTQ-3, but it is currently an open arithmetic geometry problem rather than a consequence of standard automata or Chebotarev theory.
