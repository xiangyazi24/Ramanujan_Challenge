# Q567 — Kloosterman structure of the palindromic cosine sum

## Summary

The AKD₃ phase is indeed a bilinear inverse-exponential sum, but it is important to distinguish it from a classical one-dimensional Kloosterman sum. The object is closer to a **bilinear Kloosterman sum with prime-supported variables**. The modulus factorization `M = pp'` helps conceptually, but by itself does not provide the missing factor `X` needed for the AKD₃ bound.

The near-injection argument from Q554 is therefore not merely a weak version of a Kloosterman estimate: it supplies a different geometric mechanism. To upgrade it from RMS/phase-average to fixed Apéry phases, one needs additional information on the Apéry phase coefficients, not just standard Weil bounds.

## (A) Is this a standard Kloosterman object?

The sum

\[
\sum_{q,r\sim X\atop q,r\ \mathrm{prime}} e_M(c(qr)^{-1})
\]

is not a classical Kloosterman sum in the usual sense.

A classical Kloosterman sum is

\[
S(a,b;M)=\sum_{x\bmod M}^{*}e_M(ax+b x^{-1}),
\]

with one complete variable. Here the variable is the product `qr`, restricted to the sparse set of semiprimes. The relevant family is instead:

\[
\sum_{q\sim X}\sum_{r\sim X} \alpha_q\beta_r e_M(c(qr)^{-1}),
\]

with prime-supported coefficients.

This belongs to the general class of bilinear forms with Kloosterman phases studied in work of Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel and related papers, but the exact Apéry sum is not a standard object with a ready-made theorem giving the required strength.

## (B) Does CRT factorization help?

Yes, CRT gives

\[
e_{pp'}(c(qr)^{-1})
 =e_p(c_1(qr)^{-1})e_{p'}(c_2(qr)^{-1}).
\]

However, this does not turn the sum into a product of two independent sums:

\[
\sum_{q,r}A_q(r)B_q(r)\neq
\left(\sum_{q,r}A_q(r)\right)
\left(\sum_{q,r}B_q(r)\right).
\]

The same primes `q,r` occur in both phases, so the two local oscillations are correlated.

The factorization is useful for interpreting the phase as a tensor product of two finite-field oscillations, but one still needs cancellation in the joint distribution.

## (C) Why the naive square-root bound is insufficient

The complete modulus-‘M’ square-root heuristic gives

\[
|C|\lesssim \sqrt{N M},
\]

where

\[
N\approx P^2,\qquad M\approx X^2.
\]

Hence

\[
|C|\lesssim PX.
\]

AKD₃ requires approximately

\[
|C|\lesssim P.
\]

Therefore a generic Weil/Deligne square-root estimate is missing exactly the additional cancellation coming from the fact that the summation set is a very thin semiprime set.

Possible extra sources are:

1. prime restrictions;
2. bilinear structure;
3. the near-injectivity of `(q,r) -> (qr)^{-1}`;
4. special symmetry of the Apéry zero phases.

But none follows automatically from the standard Kloosterman bound.

## (D) Can Vaughan/Vinogradov methods solve it?

A Vaughan identity decomposition could reduce prime weights to bilinear Type I/II sums. Schematically one would obtain sums like

\[
\sum_{m,n} a_m b_n e_M(c(mn)^{-1}).
\]

These are exactly the kind of objects where bilinear Kloosterman technology applies.

However, there is a serious parameter issue:

- modulus: `M ~ X^2`;
- variables: each of size roughly `X`;
- modulus is comparable to the square of the summation length.

Many classical Type II estimates gain cancellation when the modulus is not too large relative to the bilinear ranges. Here we are at the boundary case.

A successful proof would probably need a result exploiting the special semiprime modulus and not just a generic Vaughan argument.

## Relation to Q554 near-injection

Q554's key lemma says the map

\[
(q,r)\mapsto(qr)^{-1}\pmod{pp'}
\]

has bounded multiplicity.

This immediately gives an RMS estimate because Parseval sees only collisions. It does not give a pointwise estimate because fixed coefficients

\[
\widehat Z_p,\widehat Z_{p'}
\]

can align with the image set.

The missing ingredient is therefore a form of **Fourier decay of the Apéry zero measure**:

\[
\sum_{(q,r)}e_M(c(qr)^{-1})
\]

must be small for these specific frequencies `c` generated by zero locations.

## Final conclusion for P3.2

1. The AKD₃ phase is a bilinear Kloosterman-type sum, not a classical Kloosterman sum.

2. CRT factorization explains the local structure but does not provide independence.

3. Generic square-root cancellation gives `PX`, one factor too large.

4. Vaughan/Vinogradov decomposition is a plausible route, but current technology does not immediately cover the modulus range `M~X^2` with prime variables of length `X`.

5. The Q554 near-injection proves the correct RMS statement. To obtain fixed Apéry phases, the minimal missing input is a nontrivial Fourier-decay statement for the Apéry zero sets (or an equivalent bilinear Kloosterman estimate specialized to these phases).

Thus the geometric Q554 mechanism is likely the correct foundation, but a new ingredient beyond generic Kloosterman theory is required for the final AKD₃ bound.
