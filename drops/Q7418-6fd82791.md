# Problem 3.2 q6 C4 — quantifier audit of the near/far cutoff

## Verdict

There is **no asymptotic gain** available from choosing the near/far parameter inside the bounded ranges in which the plus branch is empty or reflection-compressed.

There are two distinct statements that must not be conflated.

1. From the proved fixed-`J` estimates one may extract a sufficiently slow, generally non-effective, step function
   \[
   J(T)\longrightarrow\infty
   \]
   for which the near and far estimates hold simultaneously. This is a standard diagonal extraction from the iterated limit. It is only a repackaging of the existing proof, and it eventually has `J(T)>42`; hence it cannot exploit the bounded-`J` sectors asymptotically.

2. One may **not** insert an arbitrary prescribed function such as `J(T)=log log T` into a statement of the form `o_J(·)`. The fixed-`J` little-`o` has no uniformity in `J`. Such a substitution needs an additional uniform estimate, which the present proof does not provide.

The exact geometric obstruction is the positive plus slab
\[
\mathcal R_+(T):=\{\text{legal plus occurrences with }0<d\le T/42\}.
\tag{R}
\]
For every cutoff:

- if `J<=42`, all of \(\mathcal R_+(T)\) is placed in the near remainder;
- if `J>42`, the part
  \[
  T/J<d\le T/42
  \]
  is placed in the far remainder, but it is outside the bounded-`J` full-compression sector.

Thus the same slab is never removed. It only changes owner. If one enlarges “near” to keep it there, the near cutoff is a fixed positive fraction of `T` and the original near consumer no longer tends to zero. If one shrinks “near” as required by the original consumer, the slab reappears as uncompressed far mass.

The strongest exact conclusion is therefore:

> Any reorganization that uses only the existing near theorem and the bounded-`J` empty/compression facts is equivalent to proving a new estimate for \(\mathcal R_+(T)\). Without that new arithmetic estimate, every maneuver merely transfers the unresolved positive mass between the near and far ledgers.

This remains true with sign-dependent cutoffs and with Boolean union/minus-first ownership. Overlap can delete duplicate occurrences, but it cannot create cancellation; exploiting it would itself require a new theorem showing that the unresolved plus slab is almost always already owned by minus.

## 1. The exact order of limits

Let
\[
N(T,J)=M(\mathcal N_-(T,J))+M(\mathcal N_+(T,J)),
\]
\[
F(T,J)=M(\mathcal F_-(T,J))+M(\mathcal F_+(T,J)).
\tag{1.1}
\]
The current proof architecture has the following quantifiers.

### Near input

There is a function \(\eta(J)\to0\) such that
\[
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{N(T,J)}{B(T)}\le \eta(J)
\qquad\text{for every fixed }J.
\tag{1.2}
\]
The literal formula for \(\eta\) is not needed for the quantifier audit. What matters is that its smallness is obtained only by sending `J` to infinity after the `T` limit.

### Far input

For every fixed `J`,
\[
F(T,J)=o_J(B(T)),
\tag{1.3}
\]
meaning precisely

```text
for every fixed J and every epsilon>0,
there is T0=T0(J,epsilon) such that
T>=T0 implies F(T,J)<=epsilon B(T).
```

There is no assertion that `T0(J,epsilon)` is uniform for growing `J`.

### Consumer

From the exact partition,
\[
M^-(T)+M^+(T)=N(T,J)+F(T,J).
\tag{1.4}
\]
For every fixed `J`, (1.2)–(1.3) give
\[
\limsup_{T\to\infty}
\frac{M^-(T)+M^+(T)}{B(T)}
\le \eta(J).
\tag{1.5}
\]
Only now is `J` sent to infinity:
\[
\lim_{J\to\infty}
\limsup_{T\to\infty}
\frac{M^-(T)+M^+(T)}{B(T)}=0.
\tag{1.6}
\]
Since the left side of (1.5) is independent of `J`, (1.6) proves the desired full limit.

This is an iterated limit. It does **not** assert either
\[
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\sup_J\frac{F(T,J)}{B(T)}=0
\tag{1.7}
\]
or convergence along every diagonal `J=J(T)`.

A monotone toy example already shows the issue:
\[
r(T,J)=\mathbf 1_{\{J\ge T\}}.
\tag{1.8}
\]
For each fixed `J`, \(r(T,J)\to0\), but along `J(T)=T` it is identically one. Thus fixed-`J` little-`o` cannot be evaluated at a growing parameter without a separate uniform theorem.

## 2. Exact plus geometry at 21 and 42

All legal plus occurrences satisfy
\[
0<d<T/21.
\tag{2.1}
\]
This immediately gives the first bounded sector.

### 2.1 `J<=21`: far plus is empty

If `J<=21`, then
\[
T/J\ge T/21.
\]
The far gate requires \(d>T/J\), while plus legality requires \(d<T/21\). Hence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal F_+(T,J)=\varnothing\qquad(J\le21).}
\tag{2.2}
\]
But the exact complement is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_+(T)=\mathcal N_+(T,J)\qquad(J\le21).}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Therefore emptiness of far plus does not delete one plus occurrence. It assigns **all** plus occurrences to near.

### 2.2 `21<J<=42`: only the upper proportional slab is far

In this range,
\[
T/42\le T/J<T/21.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Thus every far plus occurrence lies in
\[
T/42<d<T/21,
\tag{2.5}
\]
with the lower endpoint replaced by `T/J` when `J<42`. This is exactly the range in which the full far-plus reflection compression is available.

The benefit is support compression/reindexing, not deletion of the underlying marked incidences. Even if it halves an upper-state occupancy, the state-adaptive threshold
\[
F_+=o\!\left(\frac{B(T)^2}{U_+}\right)
\tag{2.6}
\]
is relaxed only by an absolute factor. No power of `T`, no power of `log T`, and no new outer small parameter is created.

### 2.3 `J>42`: the omitted lower far slab appears

Now
\[
T/J<T/42.
\]
The far plus set splits exactly as
\[
\mathcal F_+(T,J)
=\{T/J<d\le T/42\}
 \sqcup
 \{T/42<d<T/21\},
\tag{2.7}
\]
subject, of course, to all the other literal q6 gates and integer endpoints. The second set is the old upper slab. The first is a new far slab that was near whenever `J<=42`.

Even granting that the reflection argument can be localized and reused on the second set, it says nothing about the first. If the compression theorem really requires the global hypothesis `J<=42` rather than merely `d>T/42`, the conclusion is only stronger: then neither part is available after `J>42`.

## 3. The invariant critical remainder

Define the exact integer boundary
\[
h_{42}(T)=\left\lfloor\frac{T}{42}\right\rfloor
\tag{3.1}
\]
and the plus critical remainder
\[
\mathcal R_+(T)
:=\{e\in\mathcal E_+(T):d(e)\le h_{42}(T)\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Let \(R_+(T)\) denote its nonnegative marked mass.

### Lemma 3.1 — owner dichotomy

For every `J`:

1. if `J<=42`,
   \[
   \mathcal R_+(T)\subseteq\mathcal N_+(T,J);
   \tag{3.3}
   \]
2. if `J>42`,
   \[
   \mathcal R_+(T)
   =\{d\le h_J(T)\}
    \sqcup
    \{h_J(T)<d\le h_{42}(T)\},
   \tag{3.4}
   \]
   where the first set is near and the second is far but below the full-compression scale.

#### Proof

If `J<=42`, then \(h_J(T)\ge h_{42}(T)\), so every occurrence in (3.2) satisfies the near inequality. If `J>42`, split the integer interval \([1,h_{42}(T)]\) at \(h_J(T)\). By (0.8)–(0.9), the two pieces have the stated owners. ∎

This is the exact no-go. The bounded-`J` reflection theorem never controls \(\mathcal R_+(T)\): it is near while that theorem is globally applicable and becomes partly uncompressed far as soon as the near cutoff is shrunk below `T/42`.

Define also the upper plus slab
\[
\mathcal H_+(T)
:=\{e\in\mathcal E_+(T):h_{42}(T)<d(e)<T/21\}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Then, with exact endpoint assignment,
\[
\mathcal E_+(T)=\mathcal R_+(T)\sqcup\mathcal H_+(T),
\qquad
M^+(T)=R_+(T)+H_+(T).
\tag{3.6}
\]
Consequently, if some argument—including reflection compression plus an arithmetic estimate—proves
\[
H_+(T)=o(B(T)),
\tag{3.7}
\]
then the full plus target is **exactly equivalent** to
\[
\boxed{R_+(T)=o(B(T)).}
\tag{3.8}
\]
Indeed, (3.8) and (3.7) imply the full target, while the full target implies (3.8) by nonnegativity.

Thus bounded-sector manipulation does not remove the missing theorem; at best it identifies (3.8) as the irreducible remainder.

## 4. Why bounded `J` cannot be used in the outer limit

### 4.1 Holding `J<=21`

Equation (2.3) gives
\[
M^+(T)=M(\mathcal N_+(T,J)).
\tag{4.1}
\]
The current near theorem gives at fixed `J` only a bound at scale \(\eta(J)B(T)\), where \(\eta(J)\) is made arbitrarily small by taking `J` large. With `J<=21`, \(\eta(J)\) is a fixed number. Even a bound such as
\[
M^+(T)\ll B(T)/J
\tag{4.2}
\]
would be merely `O(B(T))` at `J=21`, not `o(B(T))`.

To turn (4.1) into `o(B)` one would need a new fixed-macroscopic-cutoff theorem for the entire plus branch. That is already the original plus gate, not a quantifier saving.

### 4.2 Holding `21<J<=42`

The far part may be compressed, but \(\mathcal R_+(T)\) remains near by Lemma 3.1. The interval of admissible `J` values is finite and bounded, so it cannot support the outer operation `J->infinity` in (1.6).

### 4.3 Oscillating back into the bounded sector

Suppose a proposed diagonal has infinitely many `T` with `J(T)<=42`. Along that subsequence, \(\mathcal R_+(T)\) is near. The fixed-`J` near theorem supplies no vanishing bound uniformly over that subsequence. Because the target is a full limit/limsup and all contributions are nonnegative, making those times sparse does not allow them to be discarded.

A proof could still succeed if it separately established \(R_+(T)=o(B(T))\). But that is precisely the new estimate isolated in (3.8).

## 5. What diagonalization is actually legal

The fixed-`J` proof does permit one diagonal extraction, but it gives no bounded-sector advantage.

### Proposition 5.1 — sufficiently slow diagonal

Assume (1.2)–(1.3). Then there is a nondecreasing integer-valued step function
\[
J(T)\to\infty
\tag{5.1}
\]
such that
\[
\frac{N(T,J(T))+F(T,J(T))}{B(T)}\to0.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Moreover, the diagonal may be chosen with `J(T)>42` for all sufficiently large `T`.

#### Proof

Choose integers
\[
42<J_1<J_2<\cdots\to\infty
\]
so that
\[
\eta(J_k)<2^{-k-2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
By the definition of limsup and by the fixed-`J_k` far estimate, choose increasing thresholds \(T_k\) such that for every \(T\ge T_k\),
\[
N(T,J_k)<2^{-k}B(T),
\qquad
F(T,J_k)<2^{-k}B(T).
\tag{5.4}
\]
Define
\[
J(T)=J_k
\quad\text{for }T_k\le T<T_{k+1}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Then `J(T)->infinity`, and (5.2) follows from (5.4). ∎

This diagonal is selected **after** the fixed-`J` convergence thresholds are known. It need not have any explicit formula, and it is not a uniform-in-`J` theorem.

### Exact logical table

The following implications are valid:

```text
fixed-J near/far package
    ==> existence of some sufficiently slow diagonal;

one successful diagonal for the exact partition
    ==> the original total target, because near+far=total.
```

The following implications are invalid without new uniformity:

```text
fixed-J o_J estimate
    =/=> validity for a prescribed growing J(T);

iterated limit
    =/=> validity for every diagonal;

far-plus empty/compressed for bounded J
    =/=> a statement surviving J->infinity.
```

Thus the legal diagonal is equivalent to the iterated proof only as a way of packaging the final total estimate. It does not strengthen the arithmetic input and necessarily exits the interval `J<=42`.

An explicit diagonal would require a statement such as
\[
\sup_{1\le J\le J_{\max}(T)}
\frac{F(T,J)}{B(T)}\to0
\tag{5.6}
\]
for some \(J_{\max}(T)\to\infty\), together with a compatible uniform near bound. No such level of uniformity is contained in `o_J`.

## 6. Arbitrary cutoffs: exact transfer identity

The same obstruction is independent of the notation `T/J`.

Let \(H(T)\ge0\) be any integer cutoff and define
\[
N_\sigma(T;H)=M_\sigma(0<d\le H),
\qquad
F_\sigma(T;H)=M_\sigma(d>H).
\tag{6.1}
\]
For two cutoffs \(H_1\le H_2\), let
\[
S_\sigma(T;H_1,H_2)
:=M_\sigma(H_1<d\le H_2).
\tag{6.2}
\]
Then the following identities are exact:
\[
\boxed{
N_\sigma(T;H_2)
=N_\sigma(T;H_1)+S_\sigma(T;H_1,H_2),}
\tag{6.3}
\]
\[
\boxed{
F_\sigma(T;H_1)
=F_\sigma(T;H_2)+S_\sigma(T;H_1,H_2).}
\tag{6.4}
\]
The same positive slab is added to near exactly when it is removed from far. There is no cancellation term.

### 6.1 Emptying far plus

To make far plus empty using only \(d<T/21\), one must take
\[
H(T)\ge T/21+O(1).
\tag{6.5}
\]
Then
\[
N_+(T;H)=M^+(T).
\tag{6.6}
\]
Any proof that this near term is `o(B)` is a direct proof of the full plus target.

### 6.2 Keeping all far plus in the compression slab

To ensure that every far plus occurrence has \(d>T/42\), one must take
\[
H(T)\ge T/42+O(1).
\tag{6.7}
\]
Then the near cutoff is a fixed positive fraction of `T`. It is not admissible for the original near mechanism, whose small parameter is
\[
\frac{H(T)}{T}=\frac1{J_{\rm eff}(T)}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6.8}
\]

### 6.3 Preserving the original near consumer

If a new cutoff is to use the same near theorem rather than a new theorem, it must satisfy
\[
H(T)=o(T),
\qquad
J_{\rm eff}(T):=T/H(T)\to\infty.
\tag{6.9}
\]
Then, eventually, \(H(T)<T/42\), and the far ledger contains the entire intermediate slab
\[
H(T)<d\le T/42.
\tag{6.10}
\]
That is exactly the mass removed from near by shrinking the cutoff. It is outside the bounded full-compression sector.

The incompatibility is therefore literal:
\[
\boxed{
\text{same near theorem: }H(T)/T\to0,
\qquad
\text{full bounded-sector compression: }H(T)/T\ge1/42+o(1).}
\tag{6.11}
\]
No function \(H(T)\) satisfies both.

## 7. Three-block formulation of the no-go

Every cutoff \(H(T)\le T/42\) gives the exact plus partition
\[
\{0<d<T/21\}
=\{0<d\le H(T)\}
 \sqcup
 \{H(T)<d\le T/42\}
 \sqcup
 \{T/42<d<T/21\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The three blocks have the following roles:

1. \(0<d\le H(T)\): near;
2. \(H(T)<d\le T/42\): far but outside the bounded full-compression sector;
3. \(T/42<d<T/21\): the only block accessible to the bounded-`J` compression.

There are only two possibilities.

- If \(H(T)=o(T)\), block 1 is eligible for the original near theorem, but block 2 remains.
- If \(H(T)\ge T/42+O(1)\), block 2 disappears, but block 1 is macroscopic and the original near smallness is lost.

This proves, without an interchange of limits, that a different threshold decomposition cannot exploit the bounded sectors to close the consumer.

A non-threshold partition or a smooth partition of unity changes nothing. Each occurrence in \(\mathcal R_+(T)\) must still be assigned nonnegative weight summing to one across the owners. A signed decomposition would not preserve the positive consumer: after taking absolute values to obtain an upper bound, the transferred mass reappears.

## 8. Sign-dependent cutoffs do not help

One may certainly choose separate cutoffs \(H_-(T)\) and \(H_+(T)\). This is compatible with the branch-separated consumer
\[
M^-+M^+
\le U_-+U_++\sqrt{U_-F_-}+\sqrt{U_+F_+}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
But the plus dichotomy is unchanged.

- Taking \(H_+(T)\ge T/21\) makes far plus empty and turns plus-near into the full plus gate.
- Taking \(H_+(T)\ge T/42\) keeps far plus in the compressed upper slab but leaves a macroscopic plus-near term.
- Taking \(H_+(T)=o(T)\) preserves the old near theorem and recreates the uncompressed slab (6.10).

The minus branch cannot pay for this plus remainder: both contributions are nonnegative and the target is their sum.

## 9. Boolean union and overlap

There are two conventions in the q6 C4 records.

### Raw signed sum

For the literal positive sum \(C^-+C^+\), a prime satisfying both signs is present in both nonnegative terms. The preceding argument applies sign by sign with no modification.

### Boolean union/minus-first assignment

For a Boolean consumer, define at every upper state
\[
P_\vee=P_-\cup P_+
=P_-\sqcup(P_+\setminus P_-).
\tag{9.1}
\]
Use `minus` as the first owner and call \(P_+^{\rm only}=P_+\setminus P_-\) the assigned plus set. This is an exact disjoint representation of the union. Intersecting both owners with a d-range preserves all the transfer identities (6.3)–(6.4).

The critical assigned remainder is
\[
\mathcal R_+^{\rm only}(T)
=\mathcal R_+(T)\cap(P_+\setminus P_-).
\tag{9.2}
\]
Overlap can make this smaller, but there is no automatic cancellation and no quantifier principle showing that it is negligible. To exploit overlap one would need the substantive arithmetic estimate
\[
M(\mathcal R_+^{\rm only}(T))=o(B(T)),
\tag{9.3}
\]
or an equivalent theorem asserting that almost all critical plus incidences are already minus-owned.

That is new input, not a choice of `J`. Pooling the signs before a factorial estimate is actually less favorable: it introduces cross-sign pairs between distinct sign-exclusive primes, as recorded in the earlier union-overlap audit.

## 10. Commit-ready theorem

### Theorem — bounded-`J` sector no-go for q6 C4

Let \(M(T)\) be the nonnegative q6 C4 marked-incidence mass, normalized by \(B(T)=T^2/(\log T)^2\). For fixed `J`, split each occurrence by
\[
d\le\lfloor T/J\rfloor
\quad\text{or}\quad
d\ge\lfloor T/J\rfloor+1.
\]
Assume:

1. the split is occurrence-preserving;
2. for every fixed `J`, the far mass is `o_J(B(T))`;
3. the near mass satisfies
   \[
   \lim_{J\to\infty}\limsup_{T\to\infty}
   \frac{N(T,J)}{B(T)}=0;
   \]
4. every legal plus occurrence has \(d<T/21\);
5. the full far-plus reflection compression is available only for \(21<J\le42\).

Then:

1. the iterated limit proves \(M(T)=o(B(T))\);
2. the fixed-`J` hypotheses imply existence of some sufficiently slow diagonal \(J(T)\to\infty\), but do not imply the result for an arbitrary prescribed diagonal;
3. every such successful diagonal is eventually outside `J<=42` and gains no asymptotic advantage from the empty/compressed bounded sectors;
4. for every cutoff, the critical plus mass with \(d\le T/42\) is either near or is split between near and an uncompressed far remainder;
5. replacing `T/J` by any cutoff that still uses the original near theorem cannot remove this critical mass: enlarging the cutoff transfers it to near, while shrinking the cutoff transfers it to far;
6. the same statements hold after exact Boolean-union/minus-first ownership.

Therefore any claimed improvement based solely on `J<=21` emptiness or `21<J<=42` reflection compression must contain, explicitly or implicitly, a new estimate for the critical plus remainder. Without such an estimate it is only a relabeling of the unresolved mass.

#### Proof

Parts 1–2 are Sections 1 and 5. Part 3 follows because every function tending to infinity eventually exceeds 42. Part 4 is Lemma 3.1. Part 5 is the exact transfer identity (6.3)–(6.4) and incompatibility (6.11). Part 6 follows from the disjoint assignment (9.1). ∎

## 11. Claim boundary

This audit does **not** prove that the critical slab is large, nor that the q6 C4 target is false. It proves the narrower and exact logical statement requested:

- bounded-`J` emptiness/compression is not an outer-limit saving;
- `o_J` cannot be evaluated on an arbitrary growing diagonal;
- a legal slow diagonal exists but is only the iterated proof in another form;
- every alternative cutoff preserving the original near consumer leaves the same positive critical plus mass in one ledger or the other.

A genuine advance would be one of the following:

1. a uniform far theorem valid for a growing range of `J`, together with a new estimate on the low far slab;
2. a macroscopic-near theorem proving the plus mass with \(d\le T/42\) is `o(B)`;
3. a direct Boolean-overlap theorem proving the critical plus-only remainder is negligible.

Each is new arithmetic information. None follows from changing the order or labels of the existing near/far split.
