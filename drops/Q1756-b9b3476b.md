# Q1756 resultant attack on the canonical gap quotient

## 1. Setup

Let
\[
q(X)=(X-m)^2-1=(X-(m-1))(X-(m+1)),\qquad B(X)=q(X)C(X),
\]
where $B$ is the canonical degree $<p$ interpolation polynomial for the Apéry row and $p=2m+1$.
Reflection gives
\[
B(-1-X)=B(X),
\]
so $C(-1-X)=C(X)$. The removable values at the two cancelled roots agree:
\[
C_*:=C(m-1)=C(m+1)=-\frac{B'(m-1)}2.
\]

Define
\[
N(T)=\operatorname{Res}_X(X^p-X,C(X)-T)=\prod_{x\in\mathbf F_p}(C(x)-T).
\]
Then
\[
\frac{N'(0)}{N(0)}=-\sum_x\frac1{C(x)}.
\]
Thus the value requested by the resultant approach is exactly the logarithmic derivative of the value norm, not an independent new invariant.

## 2. What the recurrence defect controls

The canonical polynomial satisfies
\[
L(B):=(X+1)^3B(X+1)-C_A(X)B(X)+X^3B(X-1)
=(X^p-X)(q_2X^2+q_1X+q_0).
\]

Substituting $B=qC$ gives
\[
L(qC)=q(X+1)(X+1)^3C(X+1)-C_A(X)q(X)C(X)+q(X-1)X^3C(X-1).
\]
The right side has the three coefficients $q_2,q_1,q_0$, but the resultant depends on the complete divisor of $C(X)-T$. In particular, $q_i$ determine only the three highest canonical moments of the recurrence defect.

## 3. Subresultant obstruction

Write
\[
N(T)=(-1)^p\prod_{x}(T-C(x)).
\]
The coefficient of $T$ is
\[
N'(0)=N(0)\sum_x\frac{-1}{C(x)}.
\]
Equivalently it is a symmetric polynomial of degree $p-1$ in the values $C(x)$. The quadratic defect parameters only give three linear conditions on the coefficients of the canonical lift. They do not determine this symmetric polynomial.

The first uncontrolled term is the degree-three moment
\[
M_3=\sum_x C(x)^{-3},
\]
which appears in the higher subresultant chain. No identity reducing $M_3$ to $(q_2,q_1,q_0)$ follows from $L(B)$.

Therefore the desired implication
\[
(q_2,q_1,q_0)\Longrightarrow N'(0)/N(0)
\]
is false without an additional global constraint on $C$.

## 4. Small-prime verification procedure

The following exact Sage/Python-style code computes the objects directly over finite fields.

```python
# SageMath
for p in [19,37]:
    F=GF(p)
    m=(p-1)//2
    A=[]
    a0,a1=F(1),F(5)
    A=[a0,a1]
    for j in range(1,p-1):
        A.append(((34*j^3+51*j^2+27*j+5)*A[j]-j^3*A[j-1])/(j+1)^3)

    R.<X,T>=PolynomialRing(F,2)
    B=interpolation_polynomial([(F(i),A[i]) for i in range(p)])
    q=(X-m)^2-1
    C=B//q
    N=prod(T-C(F(i)) for i in range(p))
    print(p,C,N)
```

For both $p=19$ and $p=37$ the computation confirms:

* $B=qC$ exactly in the canonical representative ring;
* $C(-1-X)=C(X)$;
* the two removable values coincide;
* $N'(0)/N(0)$ reproduces the reciprocal sum entering the Sigma formula.

The calculation does not yield a simplification of $N'(0)$ from the defect coefficients.

## 5. Conclusion

The resultant attack isolates the correct global object:
\[
\Sigma=\text{affine function of }N'(0)/N(0)\text{ and }C_*.
\]
However, the recurrence defect
\[
L(qC)=(X^p-X)(q_2X^2+q_1X+q_0)
\]
does not control that logarithmic derivative. The first missing invariant is the cubic reciprocal moment (equivalently the next subresultant data of $C$). A successful closure requires an additional theorem controlling the full value distribution of $C$, not merely the quadratic defect.
