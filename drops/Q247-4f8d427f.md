## Verdict

There are three separate issues that should not be conflated.

1. **A formal exponent larger than \(1/2\) does follow for the one-column discrepancy norm.** From the current aggregate bound
   \[
   \sum_{p\le Q} Z(p)\ll \frac{Q^{8/5}}{\log Q},
   \]
   period completion gives a formal exponent \(5/8\). The older pointwise bound \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\) gives \(3/5\).

2. **This is not a Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem in the sense needed by a sieve.** The estimate is automatic for every family of residue sets because each column is exactly periodic. At the actual high-moment parameters
   \[
   M=X^2,\qquad Q=X,
   \]
   one has \(Q=M^{1/2}\) exactly.

3. **A naive Maynard/GPY weight does not bootstrap \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).** Its numerator already contains the unknown triple intersections. However, it would be too strong to conclude that all Maynard/BI/FI ideas are irrelevant. A BFI/Maynard-style **dispersion proof of the global signed triple-CRT error** would be a legitimate, noncircular route. What is missing is an Apéry-specific trilinear exponential-sum estimate, not the Selberg-weight optimization.

Thus the clean conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The one-column “level” does not imply }(\mathrm{HM})_3,
\text{ but signed CRT dispersion remains a genuine possible route.}
}
\]

## 1. What the one-column estimate actually proves

For a prime \(p\), write
\[
 f_p(m)=\mathbf 1_{\{m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\}},
 \qquad
 g_p(m)=f_p(m)-\frac{Z(p)}p.
\]
If
\[
 M=kp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p,
\]
then every residue occurs \(k\) times below \(M\), with one additional occurrence precisely for residues in \([0,r)\). Therefore
\[
 \sum_{m<M}g_p(m)
 =|\mathcal Z_p\cap[0,r)|-\frac r p Z(p),
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)\right|\le Z(p).
 }
 \tag{1}
\]
This uses no Apéry arithmetic whatsoever. It is true for arbitrary, even adversarial, sets \(\mathcal Z_p\subseteq\mathbf F_p\).

Consequently
\[
 D_1(M,Q):=
 \sum_{p\le Q}
 \left|\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)\right|
 \le A(Q),
 \qquad
 A(Q):=\sum_{p\le Q}Z(p).
 \tag{2}
\]

### Formal exponents

The gap-polynomial bound \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\) gives
\[
 A(Q)\ll \frac{Q^{5/3}}{\log Q},
\]
so \(D_1(M,Q)\ll M(\log M)^{-A}\) throughout
\[
 Q\le M^{3/5-\varepsilon}.
\]

The stronger aggregate theorem currently recorded in `atom_tail_section.tex` gives
\[
 A(Q)\ll \frac{Q^{8/5}}{\log Q},
\]
and therefore the formal range
\[
 Q\le M^{5/8-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{3}
\]
At the endpoint \(Q=M^{5/8}\), this only gives approximately \(M/\log M\); as usual, an \(\varepsilon\)-loss or a sufficiently large logarithmic shrinkage is needed for arbitrary logarithmic savings.

So the numerical statement “this norm has exponent \(5/8\)” is correct. But it is a statement about the incomplete final period of a modulus-dependent periodic function, not about cancellation of one fixed arithmetic sequence in many progressions.

At the parameters relevant to the repository,
\[
 M=X^2,
 \qquad
 p\asymp X,
 \qquad
 Q=X=M^{1/2}.
\]
Thus the actual dyadic block is exactly at the square-root scale even numerically.

## 2. Exact centering shows why first-order distribution is irrelevant

There is an even sharper way to see the issue. Put
\[
 \Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\},
 \qquad
 A_p=|\Omega_p|,
 \qquad
 \rho_p=\frac{A_p}{M},
\]
and define the exact finite-interval centering
\[
 \widetilde g_p(m)=f_p(m)-\rho_p.
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_{m<M}\widetilde g_p(m)=0
\]
for every \(p\), with no error at all.

This does not make the third factorial moment any easier. The finite-marginal pair--Palm formulation in `hm3_result.tex` still has exactly the same missing conditional overload. Thus even a *perfect* order-one discrepancy statement does not control the joint distribution.

This is the conceptual distinction:

- the one-column estimate controls marginals;
- \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) controls a third-order joint incidence pattern;
- exact marginals do not prevent many columns from sharing one exceptional row.

The anchored-star construction in the repository makes this failure explicit; see Section 7 below.

## 3. Why the proved \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) estimate is not a dispersion theorem

Let
\[
 \mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
 \qquad
 K_X(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}f_p(m),
 \qquad
 \lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(p)}p.
\]
For distinct \(p,q\in\mathcal P_X\), set
\[
 J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|.
\]
Because
\[
 pq>X^2=M,
\]
a prescribed residue pair modulo \((p,q)\) has at most one representative in \([0,M)\). Hence
\[
 J_{p,q}\le Z(p)Z(q).
\]
Summing over ordered pairs gives the sharpened bound from `pairpalm_result.tex`:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_2(X)
 &:=
 \sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_2
 =\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\\
 &\le\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}Z(p)\right)^2
 \le\left(2X\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(p)}p\right)^2\\
 &\le 4M\lambda_X^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

No cancellation is used. This is a deterministic occupancy-one inequality. Therefore \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) supplies no centered pair equidistribution that could automatically be iterated to order three.

## 4. The exact signed target for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

For ordered distinct \(p,q\), define
\[
 T_{p,q}
 =\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
   \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}f_\ell(m),
\]
and
\[
 \lambda_{p,q}
 =\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}
   \frac{Z(\ell)}\ell.
\]
At a row with \(K_X(m)=k\), there are \((k)_2\) ordered anchor pairs and \(k-2\) possible third primes, so
\[
 \boxed{
 F_3(X):=\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_3
 =\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}.
 }
 \tag{5}
\]
Set
\[
 E_{p,q}=T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}.
\]
Then
\[
 F_3(X)
 =\underbrace{\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}}_{B_3(X)}
 +\underbrace{\sum_{p\ne q}E_{p,q}}_{\mathcal E_3(X)}.
 \tag{6}
\]
By (4),
\[
 B_3(X)
 \le\lambda_XF_2(X)
 \le4M\lambda_X^3.
 \tag{7}
\]
Therefore the one-sided signed estimate
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E_3(X)
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3
 }
 \tag{8}
\]
is equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\): it is sufficient by (6)--(7), and it is necessary because \(\mathcal E_3\le F_3\).

This is important for strategy. One does **not** have to prove absolute cancellation pair by pair. A global signed sum is enough.

The repository also defines the positive pair--Palm overload
\[
 \mathscr P_3(X)
 =\sum_{p\ne q}(E_{p,q})_+.
\]
Since \((E_{p,q})_+\le T_{p,q}\), one has
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr P_3(X)
 \le F_3(X)
 \le \mathscr P_3(X)+4M\lambda_X^3.
 }
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus the positive-overload estimate is also equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). It is the correct robust or uniformly weighted formulation, while (8) is the weakest direct signed target and is the one most compatible with the empirical observation that the total CRT error is small and often negative.

### Equivalent triple-CRT formulation

For ordered pairwise distinct \(p,q,r\), put
\[
 N_{p,q,r}
 =\sum_{m<M}f_p(m)f_q(m)f_r(m).
\]
Then
\[
 F_3(X)=\sum_{p,q,r}^{*}N_{p,q,r},
\]
where \(^*\) means pairwise distinct ordered primes. Define
\[
 P_3(X)
 =M\sum_{p,q,r}^{*}
   \frac{Z(p)Z(q)Z(r)}{pqr}
 \le M\lambda_X^3
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_3(X)
 =\sum_{p,q,r}^{*}
 \left(
 N_{p,q,r}
 -M\frac{Z(p)Z(q)Z(r)}{pqr}
 \right).
\]
Then
\[
 F_3(X)=P_3(X)+\Delta_3(X),
\]
so the one-sided estimate
\[
 \Delta_3(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3
 \tag{10}
\]
is again equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). This is the precise form of the signed-cancellation route.

## 5. What happens with literal Maynard-type weights

Let \(a_p\) be coefficients and introduce the natural nonnegative quadratic weight
\[
 W_a(m)=\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}a_pf_p(m)\right)^2.
\]
Its mass is
\[
 S_1(a)=\sum_{m<M}W_a(m)
 =\sum_{p,q}a_pa_q\sum_{m<M}f_p(m)f_q(m),
\]
so its off-diagonal part is governed by pair intersections.

A Maynard-style numerator rewarding rows with many hits would be
\[
 S_2(a)=\sum_{m<M}K_X(m)W_a(m).
\]
Expanding gives
\[
 S_2(a)
 =\sum_{r,p,q}a_pa_q
   \sum_{m<M}f_r(m)f_p(m)f_q(m).
 \tag{11}
\]
The pairwise-distinct part of (11) is exactly a weighted third factorial moment. The diagonal parts are lower-order terms, but the new off-diagonal arithmetic is precisely the unknown triple intersection. Optimizing \(a_p\) cannot evaluate it.

This is the same logical structure as the classical Maynard sieve: the optimized weight converts a distribution theorem into a conclusion; it does not manufacture the distribution theorem.

### Pair weights clarify the distinction between signed and positive targets

Let
\[
 A_w(m)=\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}f_p(m)f_q(m),
 \qquad w_{p,q}\ge0.
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_{m<M}(K_X(m)-2)A_w(m)
 =\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}T_{p,q}.
\]
After subtracting the pair-conditioned baseline, the unknown form is
\[
 \sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}E_{p,q}.
 \tag{12}
\]
There are three cases:

- \(w_{p,q}\equiv1\) gives the global signed error \(\mathcal E_3\), whose upper bound is already equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).
- Taking the supremum over \(0\le w_{p,q}\le1\) gives
  \[
  \sup_{0\le w_{p,q}\le1}\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}E_{p,q}
  =\sum_{p\ne q}(E_{p,q})_+
  =\mathscr P_3(X).
  \]
  Thus a uniform bounded-weight theorem is exactly the pair--Palm positive-overload theorem.
- Squaring a pair-indexed weight introduces four-prime intersections in its mass and five-prime intersections in the rewarded numerator, so it moves the unknown moment upward rather than closing order three.

Therefore the useful possible import from Maynard is **dispersion analysis of (10) or (12)**, not the bare multidimensional Selberg optimization.

## 6. The concrete Fourier--CRT kernel a BFI-style attack would have to bound

There is a noncircular way to expose the signed error. For a triple of distinct primes, put
\[
 P=pqr,
 \qquad
 I=[0,M),
\]
and define
\[
 \widehat I_P(h)=\sum_{0\le m<M}e_P(hm),
 \qquad
 F_p(t)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal Z_p}e_p(-ta).
\]
Fourier inversion on \(\mathbf Z/P\mathbf Z\), followed by the Chinese remainder theorem, gives the exact identity
\[
 \boxed{
 N_{p,q,r}
 =\frac1P\sum_{h\bmod P}
   \widehat I_P(h)
   F_p\!\left(h\overline{qr}\right)
   F_q\!\left(h\overline{pr}\right)
   F_r\!\left(h\overline{pq}\right),
 }
 \tag{13}
\]
where each inverse is taken in the corresponding prime field. The \(h=0\) term is
\[
 M\frac{Z(p)Z(q)Z(r)}{pqr}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \boxed{
 N_{p,q,r}-M\frac{Z(p)Z(q)Z(r)}{pqr}
 =\frac1P\sum_{0\ne h\bmod P}
   \widehat I_P(h)
   F_p(h\overline{qr})
   F_q(h\overline{pr})
   F_r(h\overline{pq}).
 }
 \tag{14}
\]

Equation (14), summed over \(p,q,r\), is the genuine “Apéry BFI” object. It exhibits the required saving as cancellation in a trilinear modular-inverse family.

Several points are decisive.

First,
\[
 |\widehat I_P(h)|
 \le \min\!\left(M,\frac1{\|h/P\|}\right),
\]
but the normalized \(\ell^1\) norm of these coefficients is only logarithmically bounded. Taking absolute values in (14) gives no power saving.

Second, although the repository proves Parseval and the short-frequency estimate
\[
 \sum_{1\le |t|\le K}|F_p(t)|^2
 \le 4KZ(p)+C\frac{p^2\log(2K)}K,
\]
this is a within-one-prime statement. In (14), the arguments
\[
 h\overline{qr}\pmod p,
 \qquad
 h\overline{pr}\pmod q,
 \qquad
 h\overline{pq}\pmod r
\]
move jointly with the other two primes. Even small global \(h\) does not mean a small local frequency. One needs a horizontal theorem coupling these characteristic-varying Fourier transforms.

Third, the scalar coefficient on the modulus may be factorable in \(p,q,r\), but the kernel is not: the modular inverses couple all three factors, and the Fourier transform \(F_p\) itself depends on the Apéry zero fiber in characteristic \(p\).

This identifies a precise possible research program:

1. dyadically decompose the interval Fourier coefficient in (14);
2. obtain a bilinear or trilinear large-sieve bound for the inverse-coupled transforms \(F_p(h\overline{qr})\);
3. save a full factor \(X^{1-o(1)}\) over the absolute triple-residue count;
4. sum the frequency ranges to prove (10).

No theorem currently in the repository supplies Step 2. Generic Cauchy--Schwarz, Parseval, or cardinality bounds cannot do so, as the anchored-star model shows.

## 7. Why existing Maynard large-modulus theorems do not directly apply

The Maynard--GPY sieve and Maynard's large-modulus papers are different tools.

- The 2015 Maynard sieve optimizes multidimensional Selberg weights and consumes an arithmetic-progression distribution theorem.
- The 2020 large-modulus series proves new distribution estimates for the fixed sequence \(\Lambda(n)\), using factorable modulus weights, Type I/II decompositions, Linnik dispersion, Kloosterman sums, and spectral/algebraic exponential-sum estimates.
- Part II reaches moduli \(q\le x^{3/5-\varepsilon}\) for suitably triply well-factorable weights. “Triply” describes a convolution factorization of a coefficient on one composite modulus; it does not mean that arbitrary third correlations are controlled.
- Later work reaching a formal \(5/8-o(1)\) for primes with triply well-factorable weights does not alter the geometric mismatch here.

With \(x=M=X^2\), the relevant scales are
\[
 p\asymp x^{1/2},
 \qquad
 pq\asymp x,
 \qquad
 pqr\asymp x^{3/2}.
\]
After conditioning on two primes, the modulus is already at the full interval length, and in fact \(pq>M\). A prescribed pair residue class has zero or one representative. There is no progression of length \(x/q\gg1\), no long Type I/II inner variable, and no direct analogue of the prime-progressions setup.

Moreover, the underlying interval sequence is trivial; all arithmetic lies in the moving residue sets \(\mathcal Z_p\). Existing uniform-residue-class theorems for \(\Lambda\) do not constrain an adversarial family of characteristic-dependent zero fibers.

Thus existing Maynard/BFI results cannot be quoted as a black box. Their *methodological shape* may still inspire a proof of (14), but only after finding new Apéry-specific structure that plays the role of the Type I/II decomposition and the Kloosterman/spectral estimate.

## 8. The repository's decisive obstruction to any marginal bootstrap

The anchored-rainbow-star proposition in `hm3_result.tex` constructs an incidence family satisfying essentially all currently available local and second-order constraints while violating \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

Let
\[
 M=X^2,
 \qquad
 m_0=\left\lfloor\frac{3M}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]
After deleting only \(O(1)\) exceptional primes, define
\[
 a_p=m_0\bmod p,
 \qquad
 \mathcal Z_p^*=\{a_p,p-1-a_p\}.
\]
Every active prime hits the same row \(m_0\). If \(L\) is the number of active primes, then
\[
 L\asymp\frac{X}{\log X},
 \qquad
 K_X^*(m_0)=L,
 \qquad
 \lambda_X^*\asymp\frac1{\log X}.
\]
The family has:

- \(|\mathcal Z_p^*|\le2\);
- reflection symmetry;
- no consecutive residues and endpoint avoidance;
- the interval bound \(|\mathcal Z_p^*\cap I|\le2|I|^{2/3}\);
- the exact one-column period-completion discrepancy bound;
- the universal \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) estimate;
- the natural centered second-moment scale;
- uniformly bounded codegree between two distinct rows;
- the necessary reflected gap-polynomial certificate;
- even a compatible value map with fibers of size at most two and energy at most \(2p\).

Nevertheless,
\[
 F_3^*(X)
 \ge(L)_3
 \gg\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3},
\]
whereas
\[
 M(\lambda_X^*)^3
 \asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\]
Thus it fails \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) by a factor of order \(X\).

This is an incidence model, not the actual Apéry zero family. Its role is logical: it proves that
\[
\boxed{
\text{one-column level}
+ (\mathrm{HM})_2
+ current local zero-set structure
\not\Rightarrow
(\mathrm{HM})_3.
}
\]
Any successful theorem must constrain how the distinguished Apéry zero orbit is selected **across different characteristics**.

## 9. Recommended repository-facing formulation

The cleanest next theorem to isolate is the signed triple-CRT estimate, because it is weaker as an analytic target than uniform positive-overload control and matches the observed cancellation.

A suitable statement to add alongside the pair--Palm theorem is:

```tex
\begin{hypothesis}[Signed third-order CRT cancellation]
\label{hyp:hm3-signed-crt}
Let
\[
 \Delta_3(X)=
 \sum_{\substack{p,q,r\in\mathcal P_X\\
                  p,q,r\ \mathrm{pairwise\ distinct}}}
 \left(
   \sum_{m<X^2}f_p(m)f_q(m)f_r(m)
   -X^2\frac{Z(p)Z(q)Z(r)}{pqr}
 \right).
\]
Then
\[
 \Delta_3(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\]
\end{hypothesis}
```

Immediately record the deterministic reduction
\[
 (\mathrm{SC}_3)\Longleftrightarrow(\mathrm{HM})_3,
\]
because the density term is at most \(X^2\lambda_X^3\).

For the analytic attack, record (13)--(14) as the exact Fourier--CRT expansion. That prevents future attempts from mistaking the automatic period-completion estimate for the missing distribution theorem. It also identifies exactly what a Maynard/dispersion adaptation would have to prove.

The three honest routes are then:

1. **Signed CRT cancellation:** prove (10) directly using the trilinear inverse-coupled Fourier kernel.
2. **Pair--Palm overload:** prove the equivalent positive-part estimate (9), perhaps through a uniform weighted dispersion theorem.
3. **Atom tail:** prove
   \[
   \max_{m<X^2}K_X(m)\le\lambda_XX^{o(1)},
   \]
   or the equivalent level-set tail
   \[
   \#\{m<X^2:K_X(m)\ge T\}
   \ll\frac{X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3}{T^3}.
   \]

Of these, the first is the only route that can exploit cancellation between positive and negative CRT boundary errors. It is also the closest genuine analogue of BFI/Maynard dispersion. But it requires a new Apéry-specific horizontal estimate; the formal \(5/8\) one-column exponent and the proved \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) bound do not supply it.

## Direct answers

### Does the aggregate zero-count bound give a formal level beyond \(1/2\)?

Yes. The bound \(\sum_{p\le Q}Z(p)\ll Q^{8/5}/\log Q\) gives exponent \(5/8\) for the norm in (2). But at the actual parameters \(Q=X\), \(M=X^2\), one has \(Q=M^{1/2}\).

### Is this an Apéry analogue of Bombieri--Vinogradov?

No. It is the terminal incomplete-period error of a modulus-dependent periodic function and holds for arbitrary residue sets. Exact finite-marginal centering makes the first-order discrepancy identically zero without affecting the HM3 obstruction.

### Does \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) provide the bilinear dispersion input needed by Maynard?

No. The current proof is the universal inequality \(J_{p,q}\le Z(p)Z(q)\), arising from \(pq>M\). It contains no centered pair cancellation.

### Can a naive Maynard multidimensional weight prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)?

No. Its rewarded numerator already contains weighted triple intersections; pair-indexed squared weights require still higher intersections.

### Can Maynard/BFI ideas still be relevant?

Potentially yes, but only at the analytic-dispersion level. One must prove cancellation in the exact trilinear Fourier--CRT expression (14). Existing large-modulus theorems do not apply as black boxes because the relevant pair modulus is already \(\asymp M\), the triple modulus is \(\asymp M^{3/2}\), and the characteristic-dependent Apéry zero transforms replace the fixed sequence \(\Lambda\).

### What is the weakest clean target?

The global one-sided signed bound (10). It is already equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) and is weaker as a proof target than controlling every pair or every bounded pair weight separately.
