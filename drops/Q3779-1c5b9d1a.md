\ll_J

\tag{39}

The determinant of the two forms is \(\pm h\), so only primes dividing a fixed constant times \(h\) inflate the local factor.

\sum_{h\le H}\mathfrak S_-(h)\ll H,
\qquad
\sum_{h\le H}h\,\mathfrak S_-(h)\ll H^2.
\tag{40}

Indeed, after expanding the squarefree Euler product, the mean involves

# 8. Why the whole matched diagonal still does not close

The exact bistar cap (35) and the prime-pair sieve do improve the raw full-gap estimate, but not by a power sufficient to close the theorem.

The plus analogue has the same order.  Requiring \(q=p+d\) to be prime supplies, at this level, essentially one sieve logarithm.  After weighting by \(\log p\) and summing over lower primes \(p\ll_JT\), the resulting bound remains of size
\[
\boxed{O_J(T^{8/3}/\log T),}
\tag{53}

up to harmless fixed-\(J\) constants (and standard averaged singular-series factors).  This is still larger than

\]
by the power \(T^{2/3}\).

The upper zero condition has already been used in (35): it is exactly the \(w^{2/3}\) side of the minimum.  Applying it a second time would be circular double counting.

The restart/column bound does not repair (53).  In the range \(h\le H\), the pushed rectangular estimate is

\min(H^2,pH^{2/3}).
\]
For \(H<p^{3/4}\), the row-degree total \(H^2\) is already the better term; this is precisely the regime used in the new minus strip.  For larger \(H\), the column term is still too large after summing over \(p\) and over the neighboring prime characteristic.

Dodgson and the separated-block identity are also one-characteristic statements.  They can relate several \(N_h(x)\) conditions modulo the same \(p\), but they do not turn

into a congruence modulo \(p\).  Thus no conclusion of the form “the remaining intersection is small” follows merely by inserting `rem:dodgson`, `lem:restart`, or `cor:sep-block` into (23)/(32).

This is a scoped failure of the **named marginal one-characteristic bounds**, not a no-go theorem for a deeper use of the Apéry recurrence.

Taking a minimum of (59) is exactly (35), and its aggregate is still supercritical.  What is missing is a theorem that the **actual p-orbit return set and the reflected q-zero set do not align at full marginal capacity across the neighboring characteristics**.

That is strictly more precise than asking for generic smoothness, a generic resultant, or a global \(Z(p)\) improvement.

# 11. Scope of the negative conclusion

I have not proved that (MB) is inaccessible.  The negative statement is only this:

- `prop:zp-bound` and `cor:subinterval`, used separately, give (35) but no cross-characteristic intersection saving;
- `lem:restart`, `prop:column`, and `rem:incidence` improve one-characteristic root incidence but retain a supercritical aggregate outside the new small-\(h\) bands;
- reflection is essential for producing the short gaps \(|p-7d|\) and \(p-6d\), and **does** yield the deletions (43)/(48), but does not by itself correlate the neighboring prime \(q\);
- Dodgson and separated-block/Bezout identities stay in characteristic \(p\); no currently pushed identity obtained from them transfers the condition \(q\mid b_u\) into the same field;
- the two-linear-form sieve uses the primality of \(p,q\) and gives a logarithmic improvement, but it does not see Apéry zero correlation, so it cannot remove the remaining \(T^{2/3}\) power excess.

No finite computation, observed smoothness, or heuristic independence is used in any theorem above.
