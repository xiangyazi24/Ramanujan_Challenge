# Q406 — Can a square sieve or large sieve prove the average Apéry zero bound M1?

## Executive verdict

**No currently available square-sieve, classical large-sieve, Selberg-sieve, or Chebotarev-sieve theorem proves**

\[
\mathrm{M1}(X):=\sum_{p\le X}z_p\ll \pi(X)
\]

from the Apéry inputs presently established in the repository.

This is not merely because the polynomial in the coefficient index has degree about \(p\). The more structural obstruction is that the condition

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad 0\le r<p,
\]

is a **self-characteristic, exact-value condition**:

- the modulus being detected is the same prime \(p\) that indexes the finite-field problem;
- its expected density among the \(p\) possible values of \(r\) is \(1/p\), so the expected count is of order one;
- no presently known construction turns \(p\mid b_r\) into congruence conditions modulo many auxiliary primes \(\ell\ne p\);
- no bounded-conductor compatible sheaf in the **coefficient variable** \(r\) has been constructed whose trace is \(b_r\bmod p\).

Those are exactly the ingredients on which the Murty--Murty--Saradha method, Heath-Brown's square sieve, and the large sieve for Frobenius depend.

The closest existing framework is **Kowalski's large sieve for Frobenius in compatible systems**, together with later trace-function variants. But applying it here would first require a new geometric theorem: a coherent, bounded-conductor, large-monodromy system in the variable \(r\), with a cross-characteristic realization of the Apéry vanishing predicate. Even in standard examples, the published Frobenius-sieve results usually give a power-saving zero-density bound, not the local-limit estimate \(O(1)\) per field that M1 requires.

Thus the honest answer is:

> A sieve may be the final analytic wrapper, but it cannot currently supply the missing arithmetic input. The missing theorem is a uniform or averaged **local-limit/cross-characteristic rigidity statement** for the Apéry coefficient zeros.

There is also an important scale distinction. M1 is stronger than the average statement actually needed for P3.2:

\[
\sum_{p\le X}z_p=o(X).
\tag{AVG-ZERO}
\]

Any logarithmic saving below \(X\), for example

\[
\sum_{p\le X}z_p\ll \frac{X}{(\log X)^\delta}
\qquad(\delta>0),
\]

already proves AVG-ZERO. One need not reach the conjectural sharp order \(\pi(X)\) in the first breakthrough.

## 1. The exact scale of the problem

For a dyadic prime block define

\[
\mathcal M(P)
:=\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p
=\#\{(p,r):P<p\le2P,\ 0\le r<p,\ p\mid b_r\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

There are about

\[
\frac{P}{\log P}\cdot P
=\frac{P^2}{\log P}
\]

ambient pairs \((p,r)\). The random-value heuristic assigns probability \(1/p\asymp1/P\) to \(p\mid b_r\), predicting

\[
\mathcal M(P)\asymp \frac{P}{\log P}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus M1 requires a saving by a full factor of \(P\) from the ambient pair count.

This explains why a square-root estimate at each fixed prime does not suffice. Put

\[
S_p(h)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}e_p(hb_r),
\qquad e_p(x)=e^{2\pi i x/p}.
\]

Additive orthogonality gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
 z_p
 =\frac1p\sum_{h\bmod p}S_p(h)
 =1+\frac1p\sum_{h\ne0}S_p(h).}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Even the very strong-looking individual estimate

\[
|S_p(h)|\ll \sqrt p
\qquad(h\ne0)
\tag{1.4}
\]

would yield only

\[
z_p=1+O(\sqrt p),
\tag{1.5}
\]

and therefore

\[
\sum_{p\le X}z_p\ll \frac{X^{3/2}}{\log X},
\tag{1.6}
\]

still a factor \(X^{1/2}\) above M1.

The required statement is not merely Deligne cancellation for each \((p,h)\). It is horizontal cancellation in the complete two-aspect sum

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\frac1p
 \sum_{h\ne0}S_p(h)
\ll \frac{P}{\log P}.}
\tag{HLL}
\]

By (1.3), (HLL) is essentially M1 itself. A useful proof must obtain it from a deeper representation of \(S_p(h)\), not from orthogonality alone.

This is the **local-limit barrier**: a standard square-root error is larger than the expected main term \(1\) at each prime.

## 2. The Murty--Murty--Saradha method is not the needed square sieve

Two related but distinct methods are often grouped together here.

### 2.1 Murty--Murty--Saradha: fixed Frobenius traces via auxiliary primes

Murty, Murty, and Saradha's paper [“Modular Forms and the Chebotarev Density Theorem”](https://doi.org/10.2307/2374502) studies fixed Fourier-coefficient/Frobenius conditions through the mod-\(\ell\) Galois representations of a fixed modular form.

For a fixed integer \(a\), the condition

\[
a_q(f)=a
\]

implies, for every auxiliary prime \(\ell\ne q\), that the Frobenius class at \(q\) has trace \(a\bmod\ell\). Thus the exceptional prime \(q\) is forced into a sparse conjugacy subset of a finite Galois group for many independent \(\ell\). Effective Chebotarev and a sieve over \(\ell\) can then be used.

The Apéry incidence

\[
p\mid b_r
\tag{2.1}
\]

has no such consequence. For \(\ell\ne p\), (2.1) imposes no known condition on

\[
b_r\pmod\ell.
\]

In particular, it does **not** force a Frobenius class attached to \((p,r)\) into a sparse subset modulo every auxiliary \(\ell\). The only visible modulus is the same prime \(p\) that defines the finite field.

This can be summarized as an auxiliary-prime obstruction:

> To use an MMS-style sieve, one first needs objects \(\rho_\ell(p,r)\) and sparse sets \(\Omega_\ell\) such that
> \[
> p\mid b_r
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> \rho_\ell(p,r)\in\Omega_\ell
> \]
> for many \(\ell\ne p\), with \(|\Omega_\ell|/|G_\ell|\ll1/\ell\) and effective independence in \(\ell\). No such cross-characteristic certificate is currently known.

Constructing that certificate would be the new arithmetic theorem; Chebotarev would only be the final counting mechanism.

### 2.2 Why the midpoint modular form still does not solve M1

The form \(f_8\) is indeed the rational non-CM newform 8.4.a.a; see the [LMFDB record](https://www.lmfdb.org/ModularForm/GL2/Q/holomorphic/8/4/a/a/). The midpoint congruence has the schematic form

\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

But:

1. this controls only the fixed point of reflection, already bounded by one per prime;
2. the condition is \(p\mid a_p(f_8)\), not \(a_p(f_8)=a\) for a fixed integer \(a\);
3. for weight \(4\), Deligne gives \(|a_p|\le2p^{3/2}\), so \(p\mid a_p\) permits
   \[
   a_p=mp,\qquad |m|\le2\sqrt p,
   \]
   a union of about \(\sqrt p\) moving trace values.

An MMS bound for one fixed trace cannot simply be summed over those \(\sqrt p\) possibilities without losing the very square-root factor one needs to save. More fundamentally, the modulus is again the varying prime \(p\), not an auxiliary modulus.

For M1 this issue is secondary anyway: the midpoint contribution is at most \(\pi(X)\) with no theorem about \(f_8\) at all.

## 3. Heath-Brown's square sieve also lacks a square certificate

Heath-Brown's [square sieve](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01475576) detects members of an integer multiset that are squares by comparing quadratic characters modulo many auxiliary primes. In the elliptic-curve setting, Cojocaru--Fouvry--Murty's [“The Square Sieve and the Lang--Trotter Conjecture”](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-2005-045-7) can use it because a fixed Frobenius-field condition yields a genuine discriminant identity of the form

\[
a_q(E)^2-4q=D_Km^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus the desired event forces a naturally defined integer to be a square up to a fixed factor. Its Legendre symbols modulo auxiliary primes become character sums that can be estimated through Chebotarev.

No analogous identity is presently available for an Apéry zero. The implication

\[
p\mid b_r
\]

does not force a naturally attached integer \(D_{p,r}\) to be a square, an \(m\)-th power, or the value of a fixed bounded-degree polynomial.

The obvious attempts are tautological:

- \(b_r^2\) is a square for every \((p,r)\), so it does not detect zeros;
- \((b_r/p)^2\) is defined integrally only after assuming the target event and contains no independent information;
- reflection pairs \(r\) with \(p-1-r\), but a pairing of roots is not a quadratic-residue certificate;
- passing to the symmetric coordinate \(r(p-1-r)\) merely halves the degree of the zero polynomial; it does not make that degree bounded.

A power sieve or polynomial-image sieve has the same issue: it requires a bounded-complexity algebraic predicate before the sieve starts.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{The square sieve cannot currently distinguish Apéry zero pairs from nonzero pairs.}}
\]

It could become relevant only after one proves a new identity analogous to (3.1).

## 4. Why \(L_3=\operatorname{Sym}^2(L_2)\) does not provide that certificate

This point is crucial. The factorization

\[
L_3=\operatorname{Sym}^2(L_2)
\]

is a statement about the Picard--Fuchs local system in the **evaluation/generating-function variable**. The values \(b_r\) are Taylor or Mellin coefficients at a fixed expansion point. It does not follow that there is an elliptic curve \(E_r/\mathbb F_p\), varying algebraically with \(r\), for which

\[
b_r\equiv
\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{Sym}^2\operatorname{Frob}_{E_r})
\pmod p
\tag{4.1}
\]

with uniformly bounded geometric complexity.

The repository's exact Jacobi-sum analysis makes the distinction concrete. In `lemmaA_result.tex`, coefficient extraction produces a Mellin expansion with \(p-1\) character terms. The unit terms reduce to

\[
\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2
\]

and reconstruct the original Apéry sum. The number of surviving terms grows with \(r\) and \(p\); cancellation among them is precisely the original vanishing problem.

Thus the fixed-rank geometry of the differential equation has not yet descended to a fixed-rank, bounded-conductor trace sheaf in the coefficient index. A naive sheaf attached to a degree-\(p\) coefficient polynomial inherits complexity growing with \(p\), so the conductor/Betti-number terms in Deligne or large-sieve bounds become just as bad as the degree bound.

If one could prove a bounded-complexity descent

\[
b_r\equiv F_p(r)\pmod p,
\qquad
F_p\text{ of uniformly bounded algebraic complexity},
\tag{4.2}
\]

then M1 might become much easier: a uniformly zero-dimensional fiber could be bounded directly by Bézout, often without any sieve. But (4.2), not the analytic sieve, is the missing result.

## 5. The classical additive large sieve and BDH have the wrong conductor and the wrong coefficients

The classical large sieve has the schematic form

\[
\sum_{q\le Q}\sum_{a\bmod q}^{*}
\left|\sum_{n\le M}c_ne(an/q)\right|^2
\ll (M+Q^2)\sum_{n\le M}|c_n|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

In the relevant range \(M\asymp Q\asymp P\), the conductor term is \(Q^2\asymp P^2\), whereas M1 lives on the scale \(P/\log P\).

There is an additional mismatch: the phase in (1.3) is

\[
e_p(hb_r),
\]

not the Fourier transform of one fixed coefficient sequence \((c_r)\) against the linear phase \(hr/p\). Both the modulus and the nonlinear map \(r\mapsto b_r\bmod p\) vary with \(p\).

A Barban--Davenport--Halberstam theorem likewise starts with a fixed arithmetic sequence and averages its discrepancies among ordinary residue classes. Here the sought local density

\[
\frac{z_p}{p}
\]

is itself unknown, and the target class is an exact value of a modulus-dependent dynamical sequence.

Even an \(L^2\) estimate for the individual sums \(S_p(h)\) does not automatically recover (HLL). Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{h\bmod p}|S_p(h)|^2
=p\,\#\{(r,s):b_r\equiv b_s\pmod p\},
\tag{5.2}
\]

which measures collisions of the Apéry orbit. It does not isolate the zero fiber, and Cauchy--Schwarz loses the same square-root factor.

So a successful “large sieve” here would have to be a genuinely new theorem, closer to

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{h\ne0}S_p(h)
\]

with simultaneous cancellation in \(p\), \(h\), and the Apéry coefficient variable. No standard additive large sieve provides this.

## 6. Selberg sieve is circular at the local-density step

Suppose one tries to sieve the integer sequence \((b_r)\). For a prime \(p\), its local root count is exactly

\[
\rho(p)=\#\{r\bmod p:p\mid b_r\}=z_p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The Selberg sieve requires these local densities, and usually distribution information for

\[
\rho(d)=\#\{r\bmod d:d\mid b_r\},
\]

as input. Its main sieve product contains factors such as

\[
1-\frac{z_p}{p}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore using a Selberg sieve to prove an average bound for the \(z_p\) is circular unless one first supplies independent information about those same local densities.

The exact cross-prime reformulation from the repository is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le X}z_p
=\sum_{r<X}\#\{p:r<p\le X,\ p\mid b_r\}.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This says that M1 is an average upper bound for large prime divisors of the integers \(b_r\). Generic size arguments give only

\[
\#\{p>r:p\mid b_r\}
\le \frac{\log |b_r|}{\log r}
\ll \frac r{\log r},
\]

and hence

\[
\sum_{p\le X}z_p\ll \frac{X^2}{\log X},
\tag{6.4}
\]

which is even weaker than (0.6). A Selberg sieve cannot improve (6.4) without a level-of-distribution or average-large-prime-factor theorem specific to the Apéry sequence.

That latter theorem would be a legitimate route, but it would be new arithmetic input rather than a formal consequence of the sieve.

## 7. Gallagher's larger sieve also has no global set to sieve

The [larger sieve](https://eudml.org/doc/205009) is effective when one fixed set of integers occupies few residue classes modulo many auxiliary primes. It converts those simultaneous congruence restrictions into a size bound by comparing products of pairwise differences.

Here there is no fixed set \(A\subset[1,N]\) whose reductions are known to be sparse. Instead, for every prime \(p\) there is a different set

\[
Z_p\subset\mathbb F_p,
\]

and bounding its size on average is exactly the problem. Taking the numbers of occupied classes to be \(z_p\) simply inserts the unknown quantity as a hypothesis.

Nor does

\[
p\mid b_r
\]

force the integer \(r\) into a small set modulo auxiliary primes \(\ell\). A larger-sieve proof would again require a prior cross-characteristic rigidity theorem.

## 8. The large sieve for Frobenius is the closest framework—but still does not close M1

Kowalski's [large sieve for Frobenius](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503714) and Perret-Gentil's [trace-function variant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06965) are designed for situations where:

1. a parameter \(u\in U(\mathbb F_q)\) indexes a fixed algebraic family;
2. there is a coherent compatible system \((\mathcal F_\lambda)_\lambda\);
3. rank and conductor are uniformly bounded;
4. the integral monodromy groups are known and independent across auxiliary \(\lambda\);
5. the exceptional trace values reduce into sparse algebraic subsets of those monodromy groups.

Under such hypotheses, one can prove zero-density estimates for parameters whose Frobenius data lie in exceptional algebraic sets.

For Apéry coefficients, items 1--4 have not been established in the variable \(r\). The Picard--Fuchs operator gives fixed geometry in the generating variable, while coefficient extraction introduces a Mellin character whose order grows with \(p\). The exact repository computation confirms that the obvious character expansion has length \(p-1\), not bounded complexity.

There is also a predicate mismatch. Frobenius sieves naturally detect exact algebraic conditions such as

\[
T(u)=0
\]

by reducing the same algebraic integer modulo many auxiliary primes. The Apéry condition is only

\[
T_p(r)\equiv0\pmod p,
\tag{8.1}
\]

where the characteristic \(p\) varies. It need not imply that the characteristic-zero trace is zero, and it supplies no restrictions modulo \(\ell\ne p\).

Finally, standard Frobenius-sieve bounds are usually power-saving **zero-density** estimates of the form

\[
\#\{u\in U(\mathbb F_q):u\text{ exceptional}\}
\ll q^{1-\delta},
\tag{8.2}
\]

under favorable monodromy hypotheses. M1 asks for \(O(1)\) exceptional values when \(q=p\), or at least an average \(O(1)\) over the prime fields. That is a much finer local-limit scale. The square-root term in Deligne's theorem is larger than the expected main term.

Thus the Frobenius large sieve identifies the correct *kind* of missing structure, but it does not presently prove M1.

## 9. A precise no-go criterion for auxiliary-prime sieves

The discussion can be distilled into a useful decision gate.

An auxiliary-prime sieve has a plausible route to M1 only after constructing, for many \(\ell\ne p\):

- a finite group or algebraic variety \(G_\ell\);
- a map \(\rho_\ell(p,r)\in G_\ell\);
- a sparse subset \(\Omega_\ell\subset G_\ell\), with relative density about \(1/\ell\);
- an implication
  \[
  p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow \rho_\ell(p,r)\in\Omega_\ell;
  \tag{9.1}
  \]
- square-root cancellation and independence for the simultaneous conditions at distinct \(\ell\).

At present, no candidate satisfying (9.1) is known. Without (9.1), the sieve denominator has no accumulation: every auxiliary modulus sees essentially the full ambient set.

This is why merely saying “the monodromy is \(\mathrm{SO}_3\) because of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\)” is insufficient. One must first connect the coefficient-zero event to conjugacy data in that monodromy group uniformly across auxiliary characteristics.

## 10. Weaker sufficient statements that are more realistic than pointwise \(z_p=O(1)\)

Several averaged statements would imply M1 without proving a uniform bound for every prime.

### 10.1 A dyadic second moment

It suffices to prove

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p^2\ll \frac{P}{\log P}.}
\tag{SM1}
\]

Indeed, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\left(\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p\right)^2
\le \pi(2P)-\pi(P)
   \sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p^2
\ll \left(\frac{P}{\log P}\right)^2.
\]

Equivalently, a centered form

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(z_p-1)^2
\ll \frac{P}{\log P}
\tag{SM1'}
\]

also suffices. This matches the observed bounded-moment/Poisson behavior.

The arithmetic content of (SM1) is

\[
\sum_{r,s<2P}
\#\{P<p\le2P:\max(r,s)<p,\ p\mid b_r,\ p\mid b_s\}
\ll \frac{P}{\log P},
\tag{10.1}
\]

so it is an averaged large-prime divisor theorem for pairwise gcds of Apéry numbers. This is hard, but it is a clean target and is closer to existing gap-polynomial work than a square predicate is.

### 10.2 A factorial-moment bound

Since \(z\le1+z(z-1)\) for every nonnegative integer \(z\), it also suffices to prove

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p(z_p-1)
\ll \frac{P}{\log P}.}
\tag{FM1}
\]

This directly counts ordered pairs of distinct zero positions at one prime. Reflection makes such pairs especially natural.

### 10.3 A horizontal Fourier local-limit theorem

The signed estimate (HLL) is exactly tailored to the zero fiber. A stronger mean-square version such as

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}
\left|rac1p\sum_{h\ne0}S_p(h)\right|^2
\ll \frac{P}{\log P}
\tag{HF2}
\]

would imply M1. Proving it would require cancellation simultaneously across frequencies and characteristics.

### 10.4 The weaker P3.2 target

For AVG-ZERO it is enough to prove, on dyadic blocks,

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p
\ll \frac{P}{(\log P)^\delta}
\qquad\text{for any fixed }\delta>0.
\tag{10.2}
\]

This is much weaker than the conjectural \(P/\log P\). A first attack should exploit this logarithmic slack rather than insist immediately on the sharp constant-order mean.

## 11. What genuinely could work

The viable routes are not generic sieves by themselves; each requires a new Apéry-specific theorem.

### Route A: bounded-complexity coefficient geometry

Construct a fixed-rank algebraic or \(\ell\)-adic object in the coefficient/Mellin parameter whose zero fiber equals \(Z_p\), with conductor independent of \(p\). If the zero fiber is uniformly zero-dimensional, direct geometry may give \(z_p=O(1)\).

This is the cleanest possible route, but the current Jacobi skeleton shows that the obvious Mellin representation has growing length.

### Route B: a cross-characteristic auxiliary certificate

Find an invariant \(D_{p,r}\) such that

\[
p\mid b_r
\]

forces \(D_{p,r}\) into a sparse algebraic subset modulo every auxiliary \(\ell\). Then an MMS/Frobenius/square sieve might become applicable.

The decisive milestone is the implication, not the subsequent sieve estimate.

### Route C: horizontal dispersion in \((p,h)\)

Prove (HLL) or (HF2) by expressing \(S_p(h)\) as a trace function with enough uniformity in both \(p\) and \(h\), then use a bilinear or spectral large sieve. This would be a new two-aspect trace-function theorem.

### Route D: average large-prime divisors of Apéry numbers

Use (6.3) directly and prove

\[
\sum_{r<X}\#\{r<p\le X:p\mid b_r\}
\ll \frac{X}{\log X}
\]

or merely \(o(X)\). This is an arithmetic-statistics theorem for a holonomic/hypergeometric sequence. Standard integer-size and primitive-divisor methods do not give the required upper bound, but the formulation avoids the high-degree finite-field polynomial.

### Route E: averaged pair-zero/gap rigidity

Target (FM1) or (SM1), perhaps using the existing gap-polynomial/determinant identities and averaging the prime divisors of the resulting integer resultants. This is more plausible than an individual Weil estimate because the averaging is over the exact object M1 needs.

## 12. Recommended research decision

I would **not invest further in a square-sieve implementation yet**. Before any such implementation can have content, one should demand one of the following concrete certificates:

```text
(AUX)  p | b_r  =>  rho_l(p,r) lies in a subset of relative size O(1/l)
       for every auxiliary prime l in a long range;

or

(SHEAF) r -> b_r mod p is the trace/reduction of a coherent compatible
        system of uniformly bounded rank and conductor;

or

(SQUARE) p | b_r forces a naturally attached integer D_{p,r}
         to equal a fixed factor times a square.
```

At present none of `AUX`, `SHEAF`, or `SQUARE` is established. Without one of them, the named sieve methods either become circular or return only the fixed-prime square-root scale.

The most promising near-term target is therefore not “apply the square sieve,” but one of:

1. prove the dyadic factorial moment (FM1);
2. construct a bounded-conductor Mellin/coefficient sheaf and verify its integral monodromy;
3. prove a logarithmically saving version of the large-prime-divisor identity (6.3).

No known off-the-shelf sieve handles the present \(1/p\)-density,
self-modulus Apéry zero problem. Murty--Murty--Saradha requires
auxiliary-prime Frobenius constraints; the square sieve requires a
square/discriminant certificate; the classical large sieve loses at
the \(Q^2\) conductor; and the Selberg sieve takes \(z_p/p\) as input.
The large sieve for Frobenius is the closest framework, but applying it
requires a new bounded-conductor coefficient-family construction and,
even then, an additional local-limit improvement beyond ordinary
square-root equidistribution.
\end{minipage}}
\]

The empirical law \(z_p\) of mean essentially \(1\) is extremely compelling. The gap is not the probabilistic prediction; it is the absence of a mechanism that transfers fixed-rank Picard--Fuchs geometry to the coefficient index uniformly across characteristics.
