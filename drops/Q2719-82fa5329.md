# P3.2 fixed-q singleton first-moment attack

## Verdict

For a fixed quotient `q`, the requested pointwise bound

\[
\#\Bigl\{p:\frac{n}{q+1}<p\le \frac nq,\ p\mid b_n\Bigr\}=o_q\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)
\tag{1}
\]

is **not currently derivable unconditionally** from any of the three proposed standard routes (prime divisors of one G-function coefficient, large sieve/polynomial sieve, or Turan--Rankin/size), nor from a quotient-by-quotient use of the Apéry recurrence alone.

There is, however, a clean fixed-`q` reduction and a substantially stronger unconditional *averaged* input than the old pointwise `Z(p) \ll p^{2/3}` shell estimate. The current root-strip/resultant + corank chain proves

\[
\sum_{p\le X} Z(p)\ll \frac{X^{8/5}}{\log X},
\tag{2}
\]

where

\[
Z(p)=\#\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\}.
\]

From (2), for every fixed `q`, one gets the rigorous dyadic first moment

\[
\sum_{N<n\le 2N}
\#\Bigl\{p:\frac{n}{q+1}<p\le \frac nq,\ p\mid b_n\Bigr\}
\ll_q \frac{N^{8/5}}{\log N}.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus the average fixed-`q` slice size is

\[
\ll_q \frac{N^{3/5}}{\log N}
=o_q\!\left(\frac N{\log N}\right),
\tag{4}
\]

and, more sharply, for every fixed `q` and every fixed `\eta>0`,

\[
\#\left\{N<n\le2N:
\#\Bigl\{p:\frac{n}{q+1}<p\le\frac nq,\ p\mid b_n\Bigr\}
\ge \eta\frac N{\log N}
\right\}
\ll_{q,\eta}N^{3/5}.
\tag{5}
\]

So **a positive-density subset of the available primes is already ruled out for all but a power-saving exceptional set of `n`**. What remains is exactly the singleton concentration problem: exclude even one prescribed `n` carrying a rainbow pile-up of fresh primes.

That is the smallest honest conclusion I can get. It is average-in-`n`, not pointwise-in-`n`; (5) must not be promoted to (1).

## 1. Exact fixed-q reduction

Let

\[
I_q(n)=\left(\frac n{q+1},\frac nq\right].
\]

For `p in I_q(n)`, write uniquely

\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

The Apéry Lucas law gives

\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv b_q b_r\pmod p.
\tag{6}
\]

Because `q` is fixed, `b_q` is one fixed nonzero integer. Hence the set of primes dividing `b_q` is finite. But every prime in `I_q(n)` tends to infinity with `n`. Therefore, for all sufficiently large `n` (depending only on `q`), no prime in `I_q(n)` divides `b_q`, and (6) becomes the exact equivalence

\[
p\mid b_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_{n-qp}.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the hard quantity is precisely

\[
S_q(n):=
\#\left\{p\in I_q(n): p\mid b_{n-qp}\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]

There is one moving residue `r=n-qp` for each prime `p`. There is no second free variable.

The number of available primes is, by the prime number theorem,

\[
\pi(n/q)-\pi(n/(q+1))
\sim \frac{1}{q(q+1)}\frac n{\log n}.
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently the requested `o_q(n/log n)` bound is exactly a zero-relative-density statement inside this fixed quotient shell.

This also shows why the quotient factor itself is not the issue. For fixed `q`, the `p\mid b_q` channel is eventually empty. The entire fixed-`q` problem is the singleton lower-digit channel (8).

## 2. The strongest unconditional input: a fixed-q first moment

The current First Lemma/root-strip + corank argument gives (2). Double-count (8) over `n in (N,2N]`.

If `p` contributes to some `S_q(n)`, then

\[
\frac{N}{q+1}<p\le \frac{2N}{q},
\]

and `n=qp+r` for a unique `r` with `0<=r<p`. For a fixed `p`, the number of possible contributing `r` is at most `Z(p)`. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{N<n\le2N}S_q(n)
&\le
\sum_{N/(q+1)<p\le2N/q} Z(p)\\
&\le \sum_{p\le2N/q}Z(p)\\
&\ll_q \frac{N^{8/5}}{\log N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

This proves (3)--(4).

Now fix `eta>0`. If `S_q(n)>=eta N/log N`, Markov applied to (10) gives

\[
\#\{N<n\le2N:S_q(n)\ge\eta N/\log N\}
\ll_{q,\eta}
\frac{N^{8/5}/\log N}{N/\log N}
=O_{q,\eta}(N^{3/5}).
\tag{11}
\]

Using (9), changing `eta` by a fixed `q`-dependent constant turns (11) into the statement that an `eta`-fraction of the available primes can occur only on `O_{q,eta}(N^{3/5})` integers in a dyadic block.

This is the useful unconditional first-moment theorem for this question.

### Comparison with the older shell estimate

The earlier Franel/shell bookkeeping used the pointwise bound `Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}`. The same double count gives only the coarse scale

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}S_q(n)
\ll_q \frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}
\quad\text{(or }O_q(N^{5/3})\text{ if logs are discarded),}
\]

hence average `N^{2/3}/log N`. The current first moment (2) improves `2/3` to `3/5` on the average slice size and improves the exceptional exponent from `2/3` to `3/5`.

But neither statement controls `max_{N<n<=2N} S_q(n)`.

## 3. Exact weaker premise than Q32-SLICE-COUNT-FAMILY

The full family statement carries uniformity in a growing quotient range. None of that is needed here. The exact strictly weaker premise is:

> **[Q32-FIXED-q-SINGLETON]**. For each fixed integer `q>=1`,
> \[
> \sup_{N<n\le2N}
> \#\left\{p:\frac n{q+1}<p\le\frac nq,\ p\mid b_{n-qp}\right\}
> =o_q\!\left(\frac N{\log N}\right).
> \tag{FQ}
> \]

There is no uniformity in `q`, no summation over quotient families, and no statement about the other denominator channels. By (7), `(FQ)` is exactly sufficient for the fixed-`q` target in the question.

The actually proved input that nearly reaches `(FQ)` is its `L^1` shadow:

> **[Q32-FIXED-q-SINGLETON-L1] (proved).** For each fixed `q`,
> \[
> \sum_{N<n\le2N} S_q(n)
> \ll_q \frac{N^{8/5}}{\log N}.
> \tag{FQ-L1}
> \]

Equivalently,

\[
\frac1N\sum_{N<n\le2N}S_q(n)
\ll_q\frac{N^{3/5}}{\log N}.
\]

So the remaining gap is not a logarithm and not a local zero-count exponent. It is the passage

\[
L^1\text{ in }n\quad\longrightarrow\quad L^\infty\text{ in }n
\]

for a moving one-point-per-prime incidence system. The present codegree/resultant machinery can make the exceptional set tiny, but it does not rule out one exceptional vertex.

This is strictly less than `Q32-SLICE-COUNT-FAMILY` and is, in my view, the right replacement premise to put on the fixed-`q` board.

## 4. Why a theorem about prime divisors of one G-function coefficient does not give (FQ)

All target primes divide the single integer `b_n`, so the first temptation is to forget the Lucas slice and ask for a theorem saying that a G-function coefficient cannot have too many prime divisors in a proportional interval.

There are two obstructions.

### 4.1 Size gives only the wrong order

For the zeta(3) Apéry numbers,

\[
\log b_n = n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n),
\qquad
\log(17+12\sqrt2)\approx3.52549.
\tag{12}
\]

If `M` distinct target primes lie in `I_q(n)`, then

\[
\left(\frac n{q+1}\right)^M\le b_n,
\]

so

\[
M\le
(\log(17+12\sqrt2)+o(1))\frac n{\log n}.
\tag{13}
\]

This is only `O(n/log n)`, exactly the scale we must beat.

In fact the magnitude obstruction is even clearer in logarithmic mass. The product of *all* primes in the shell has logarithm

\[
\vartheta(n/q)-\vartheta(n/(q+1))
\sim \frac{n}{q(q+1)},
\tag{14}
\]

which is at most `n/2`, whereas `log b_n ~ 3.52549 n`. Thus the size of `b_n` is compatible with containing every prime in the shell. No Turan/Rankin argument whose only arithmetic input is the size of `b_n` can even rule out full shell occupancy.

### 4.2 There cannot be a generic G-function/diagonal theorem of the desired form

The central binomial coefficients are a decisive control example. The sequence

\[
c_m=\binom{2m}{m}
\]

is a diagonal/G-function coefficient sequence, and every prime `m<p<=2m` divides `c_m`: `p` occurs once in the numerator `(m+1)\cdots(2m)` and not in `m!`.

Hence a perfectly standard G-function coefficient can contain a positive-density (indeed the whole) proportional prime shell. Any theorem proving (1) must use Apéry-specific structure, not merely holonomicity, diagonality, integrality, or G-function growth.

This matches the repository's literature audit: general holonomic/G-function prime-factor technology is structurally incapable of the requested upper bound; the known Stewart/Luca--Shparlinski type inputs go in the opposite direction (large prime factors / lower bounds), not toward excluding many large prime divisors of one coefficient.

## 5. Why the classical large sieve does not upgrade (FQ-L1) to (FQ)

For `n` in a dyadic block, the condition is

\[
n\equiv qp+r\pmod p,\qquad r\in Z(p),
\]

or simply `n-qp in Z(p)` with one candidate residue chosen by `n` for each `p`.

For fixed `q`, the moduli satisfy `p \asymp_q N`. Thus a classical large-sieve inequality has large-sieve constant

\[
N+Q^2\asymp_q N^2,
\]

because `Q\asymp_q N`. This is the occupancy-one range: the modulus is as large as the ambient interval. There are not `N/p` many samples per residue class; there are `O(1)`.

This is exactly why the first moment double count is easy while a fixed `n` is hard. Averaging over `n` lets us exchange the sums and use `\sum Z(p)`. For one `n`, each prime contributes one unrelated membership query

\[
n-qp\stackrel{?}{\in}Z(p).
\]

A large sieve can help only after an additional cross-prime cancellation/dispersion theorem for the **exact zero indicator**. Supplying that theorem is essentially supplying the missing arithmetic.

The repository already identified this as the occupancy-one / microscopic moving-target barrier, distinct from the Bombieri--Vinogradov or Zhang level-of-distribution barrier: those methods still have many integers per progression or a factorable modulus. Here `p` is simultaneously the varying prime and the modulus, with one point sampled from each `Z(p)`.

So a standard large sieve does not turn (10) into a maximum bound.

## 6. What the shell-Dirichlet weak-edge calculation does and does not buy

The recovered rc4 weak-edge calculation gives an exact boundary-corrected shell Dirichlet identity of the form

\[
\Delta_p=
\frac{1}{MZ(p)}
\sum_{a\ne0}F_p(a)\,\mathcal Q_p(a),
\tag{15}
\]

with the ordered semiprime packet, the `1/(q\ell)` weights, and the interval kernel included in `\mathcal Q_p`.

The useful local input is a fourth-moment estimate of the schematic strength

\[
\sum_a |F_p(a)|^4
\ll p^{1+o(1)}Z(p)^2.
\tag{16}
\]

But (16) does **not** close the global shell discrepancy. After Holder/duality the missing statement is a cross-prime `L^{4/3}` dispersion estimate for the semiprime packet (the `OSPD_{4/3}` edge isolated in that audit). That is not furnished by the classical large sieve.

For the present fixed-`q` singleton problem, freezing `q` does not magically create the missing averaging. It removes one quotient variable and leaves precisely the one-point sample `r=n-qp` from each prime zero set. The local fourth moment in `a` controls the shape of one prime row; it does not prevent many different prime rows from all hitting the one prescribed `n`.

Thus the weak-edge calculation is consistent with (FQ-L1), but it does not supply the missing `L^infty` anti-alignment.

## 7. Why quotient-by-quotient recurrence elimination stops at codegree

The recurrence gives a very strong statement when two or more indices are linked. The gap-polynomial machinery turns two zero events at the same prime into divisibility by a fixed integer/polynomial resultant. This is the source of the codegree theorem and the small exceptional-set results.

But after the Lucas reduction (7), a singleton event is merely

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad r=n-qp.
\]

The three-term recurrence at `r` says that if one knows neighboring values then the next value is constrained, but one zero by itself does not generate a second zero. The Wronskian in fact prevents some neighboring simultaneous zeros; it does not force one.

Trying to eliminate from `b_r` back to the initial conditions produces a transfer product of length `r`. Here `r` is typically of order `p`, hence of order `n` for fixed `q`. The resulting certificate is not a bounded-degree polynomial in `p` with subexponential height. This is exactly the distinction recorded in the doctrine:

- **pair of indices / two hits:** fixed-gap polynomial and resultant certificate; bounded codegree;
- **one prescribed index / one hit per prime:** no fixed-height certificate; degree/range grows with the prime.

Bounded codegree controls how *many vertices* can have large degree. It never excludes one high-degree vertex assembled from fresh, non-overlapping primes. That one vertex is the fixed-`n` problem.

Higher fixed moments suffer the same quantifier defect. The project already has a fixed-index moment death certificate: every fixed moment order loses a power from the `H^{k-1}` tuple count, and even ideal pair-flatness does not produce the required pointwise singleton bound. A growing-moment or genuinely cross-prime input would be new arithmetic, not a corollary of the recurrence.

## 8. Turan/Rankin cannot exploit a positive-density contradiction

Suppose, toward the desired contradiction, that for infinitely many `n` the target contains a fixed positive fraction `delta` of the primes in `I_q(n)`. Then the number of targets is

\[
\sim \frac{\delta}{q(q+1)}\frac n{\log n}
\]

and their logarithmic product is

\[
\sim \frac{\delta}{q(q+1)}n.
\tag{17}
\]

But (12) allows logarithmic mass `3.52549... n`. There is therefore no contradiction even at `delta=1`.

Rankin's trick only helps if one has an additional moment/valuation inequality specific to the prime factors of `b_n`. The target primes may all occur to exponent one, so high valuations cannot be forced. The known denominator/supercongruence information does not give a positive lower valuation per target beyond one. Hence the multiplicative budget remains (17) versus (12), which has ample room.

So this route cannot produce `o_q(n/log n)` without a genuinely new Apéry-specific prime-factor distribution theorem.

## 9. What would actually close fixed q

The right target is not a stronger bound on `Z(p)` by itself. Even replacing the current average theorem by `Z(p)=O(1)` pointwise would still leave the possibility that the one selected residue `n-qp` lands in `Z(p)` for a positive proportion of the primes in this particular shell.

What is needed is **cross-prime anti-alignment of the selected residues**.

The minimal statement is `(FQ)` above. An analytically more informative equivalent formulation is: for every fixed `q` and `eta>0`, there exists `N_0(q,eta)` such that no `n>N_0` satisfies

\[
S_q(n)\ge
\eta\bigl(\pi(n/q)-\pi(n/(q+1))\bigr).
\tag{18}
\]

The proved statement (11) says that, in `(N,2N]`, the number of violations of (18) is `O_{q,eta}(N^{3/5})`. The missing theorem is to replace

\[
O_{q,\eta}(N^{3/5})
\]

by

\[
0\qquad\text{for all sufficiently large }N.
\]

That is a very sharp way to see the remaining logical gap.

A theorem of the shell-Dirichlet type could close it only if it supplied a maximal/cross-prime estimate for the exact zero indicator, not merely a local trace-function moment. A recurrence certificate could close it only if it converted a single hit into a bounded-height arithmetic obstruction. Neither input is presently proved.

## 10. Bottom line for the P3.2 board

1. **Fixed `q` quotient factor is harmless.** For large `n`, primes in the shell cannot divide the fixed integer `b_q`, so Lucas reduces the target exactly to `p | b_{n-qp}`.

2. **The desired pointwise count remains open.** There is no unconditional theorem currently giving `S_q(n)=o_q(n/log n)` for every `n`.

3. **Generic one-coefficient G-function theorems cannot do it.** Central binomial coefficients give an elementary counterexample to any such generic proportional-shell sparsity statement.

4. **Turan/Rankin/height cannot do it.** Even the product of every prime in the shell has logarithmic size below the exponential growth budget of `b_n`.

5. **Classical large sieve is at the wrong scale.** With `p asymp_q N`, the large-sieve constant is `N+Q^2 asymp N^2`; the problem is occupancy-one / microscopic moving-target, not ordinary distribution in progressions.

6. **The recurrence gives codegree, not degree.** Two hits yield gap-polynomial/resultant certificates; one hit does not. This is why exceptional-set theorems can be strong while one prescribed `n` remains uncontrolled.

7. **Strongest current unconditional near-input:**
   \[
   \sum_{p\le X}Z(p)\ll X^{8/5}/\log X
   \]
   implies
   \[
   \sum_{N<n\le2N}S_q(n)\ll_q N^{8/5}/\log N,
   \]
   average `O_q(N^{3/5}/log N)`, and only `O_{q,eta}(N^{3/5})` positive-density exceptions in a dyadic block.

8. **Recommended exact replacement premise:** `[Q32-FIXED-q-SINGLETON]` `(FQ)`, not the full `Q32-SLICE-COUNT-FAMILY`. It asks only for one fixed quotient and is exactly sufficient for this subproblem. Its proved `L^1` shadow `(FQ-L1)` is already power-saving; the unresolved step is solely the `L^1 -> L^infty` anti-concentration across primes.

So the first-moment attack **does make genuine unconditional progress**, but it stops at a power-saving exceptional set. I do not see a legitimate standard theorem that removes the last exceptional `n`; claiming otherwise would conflate an average-in-`n` theorem with the required pointwise statement.
