# Route selection: a Cartier–Hurwitz primitive for the Apéry Hasse square root

## Verdict

I choose one route: **use the Apéry-specific Hasse square root to turn the exact moving coefficient condition \(p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\) into a tame rational cover, make the Kummer order \(d\) a quantitative indecomposability parameter for that cover, and then attack the remaining large-\(d\) primes by a defining-characteristic moving-character large sieve.**

This already yields new unconditional arithmetic.  In particular, the normalized Apéry Hasse square root \(B_p\), whose squarefreeness/coprimality with the conifold is only recorded computationally in the current repository, is in fact squarefree and coprime to the conifold for every \(p\ge5\).  At a bad residue \(r\), the coefficient obstruction integrates to a degree-\(p-1\) tame map whose two pole indices are \(r\) and \(p-1-r\).  If
\[
d=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(r,p-1)},
\]
then every nontrivial imprimitive outer degree divides \((p-1)/d\); for \(d>2\) it is necessarily odd, and decomposition forces a common-critical-value packet of at least \(d\) distinct roots of \(B_p\).

There is also a necessary falsification: the apparent \(\asymp p\) many critical-point equations are rank one as a scalar congruence certificate—their raw resultant is only a unit times \(b_r^{\deg B_p}\).  So the route does **not** revive the ordinary-resultant strategy killed by established fact (4).  The remaining irreducible statement is an operator-norm horizontal large sieve stated in §5; combined with the already proved small-Kummer-order estimate, it implies \(W_n=o(n)\) pointwise for every \(n\).

# 1. Apéry-specific input and a new squarefreeness theorem

For a prime \(p\ge5\), put
\[
N=p-1,\qquad
A_p(T)=\sum_{m=0}^{N}b_mT^m\in\mathbf F_p[T],\qquad
\Delta(T)=T^2-34T+1.
\]
The project records the exact factorization
\[
\boxed{A_p(T)=\Delta(T)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(T)^2,}
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
\varepsilon_p=\frac{1-\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)}2\in\{0,1\},\qquad
B_p(0)=1,\qquad
M_p:=\deg B_p=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon_p}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
The same file constructs characteristic-zero square roots
\[
\mathcal A(T)=C_+(T)^2=\Delta(T)C_-(T)^2
\tag{3}
\]
and identifies \(B_p\) with the appropriate truncation of \(C_+\) or \(C_-\).  With \(\vartheta=T\,d/dT\), they satisfy
\[
L_+C_+=0,\qquad L_-C_-=0,
\]
where
\[
L_+=4\vartheta^2-2T(68\vartheta^2+34\vartheta+5)+T^2(2\vartheta+1)^2,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
L_-=4\vartheta^2-2T(68\vartheta^2+102\vartheta+39)+T^2(2\vartheta+3)^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{L_+=4\Delta\vartheta^2+4T(T-17)\vartheta+T(T-10),}
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\boxed{L_-=4\Delta\vartheta^2+12T(T-17)\vartheta+3T(3T-26).}
\tag{7}
\]

## Theorem 1 — exact mod-\(p\) square-root ODE and reduced Hasse divisor

For every prime \(p\ge5\):

\[
\varepsilon_p=0\Longrightarrow L_+B_p=0,
\qquad
\varepsilon_p=1\Longrightarrow L_-B_p=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[T].
\tag{8}
\]
Moreover
\[
\boxed{\gcd(B_p,T\Delta)=1,\qquad B_p\text{ is squarefree over }\overline{\mathbf F}_p.}
\tag{9}
\]
Hence \(B_p\) has exactly \(M_p\) distinct geometric roots, none at \(0\) or at a conifold point.

### Proof

Write
\[
C_+(T)=\sum_{m\ge0}c_mT^m,\qquad C_-(T)=\sum_{m\ge0}d_mT^m.
\]
The coefficient recurrences are
\[
4(m+1)^2c_{m+1}
=2(68m^2+34m+5)c_m-(2m-1)^2c_{m-1},
\tag{10}
\]
\[
4(m+1)^2d_{m+1}
=2(68m^2+102m+39)d_m-(2m+1)^2d_{m-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

If \(arepsilon_p=0\), then \(M_p=(p-1)/2\) and
\[
B_p=\sum_{m=0}^{M_p}\bar c_mT^m.
\]
Compare the coefficient of \(T^{M_p+1}\) in \(\mathcal A=C_+^2\) with the same coefficient in the exact identity \(A_p=B_p^2\).  Since \(M_p+1=(p+1)/2<p\), both represent the same Apéry coefficient modulo \(p\).  All convolution terms agree except the two endpoint terms containing \(c_{M_p+1}\), so
\[
2\bar c_{M_p+1}=0,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\bar c_{M_p+1}=0.
\tag{12}
\]
The recurrence (10) then kills every coefficient of \(L_+B_p\) through degree \(M_p+1\).  The only possible term in degree \(M_p+2\) is
\[
(2M_p+1)^2\bar c_{M_p}T^{M_p+2}=p^2\bar c_{M_p}T^{M_p+2}=0.
\]
Thus \(L_+B_p=0\) exactly.

If \(arepsilon_p=1\), then \(M_p=(p-3)/2\) and
\[
B_p=\sum_{m=0}^{M_p}\bar d_mT^m.
\]
Because \(\Delta(0)=1\), division by \(\Delta\) is valid in \(\mathbf F_p[[T]]\).  The identities \(\mathcal A/\Delta=C_-^2\) and \(A_p/\Delta=B_p^2\) agree through degree \(p-1\).  Comparing degree \(M_p+1=(p-1)/2\) gives
\[
\bar d_{M_p+1}=0.
\tag{13}
\]
Now (11) kills all coefficients of \(L_-B_p\) through degree \(M_p+1\), and the last possible coefficient is
\[
(2M_p+3)^2\bar d_{M_p}=p^2\bar d_{M_p}=0.
\]
Hence \(L_-B_p=0\).

It remains to locate multiple roots.  Let
\[
B_p(T)=(T-\alpha)^m u(T),\qquad u(\alpha)=\nu\ne0.
\]
If \(\alpha\ne0\), \(\Delta(\alpha)\ne0\), and \(m\ge2\), then the coefficient of \((T-\alpha)^{m-2}\) in \(4\Delta\vartheta^2B_p\) is
\[
4\Delta(\alpha)\alpha^2m(m-1)\nu\ne0,
\]
because \(2\le m<p\).  Every other term in (6) or (7) has order at least \(m-1\), contradicting \(L_\pm B_p=0\).  Thus every root away from \(T\Delta=0\) is simple.  Also \(B_p(0)=1\).

Finally let \(\Delta(\alpha)=0\).  Its discriminant is \(1152\), so for \(p\ge5\) its roots are distinct; \(\alpha\ne0\), and \(\alpha\ne17\) because \(\Delta(17)=-288\).  Since
\[
\Delta'(\alpha)=2(\alpha-17),
\]
the lowest-order term in (6), if \(arepsilon_p=0\), is
\[
4\alpha^2(\alpha-17)m(2m-1)u(\alpha)(T-\alpha)^{m-1}.
\tag{14}
\]
Here \(1\le m\le(p-1)/2\), so \(1\le2m-1\le p-2\); (14) is nonzero.  In the defect branch the corresponding coefficient from (7) is
\[
4\alpha^2(\alpha-17)m(2m+1)u(\alpha),
\tag{15}
\]
and \(1\le m\le(p-3)/2\) gives \(3\le2m+1\le p-2\).  This is again nonzero.  Therefore \(B_p\) has no root on \(\Delta=0\).  Combining the cases proves (9). \(\square\)

This closes an explicit repository caveat and is already a non-generic Apéry theorem.

# 2. The marked coefficient is a Cartier exactness obstruction

Fix \(1\le r\le p-2\) and define
\[
\boxed{
Q_{p,r}(T)=
\sum_{\substack{0\le m\le p-1\\m\ne r}}
\frac{b_m}{m-r}T^m\in\mathbf F_p[T].
}
\tag{16}
\]
Then coefficientwise
\[
\boxed{(\vartheta-r)Q_{p,r}=A_p-b_rT^r.}
\tag{17}
\]
For the all-index target \(r=n\bmod p\), \(artheta-r=artheta-n\) over \(\mathbf F_p\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_r
\iff
(\vartheta-n)Q_{p,r}=A_p=\Delta^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^2.
}
\tag{18}
\]

Equivalently, for the Laurent differential
\[
\omega_{p,r}=T^{-r-1}A_p(T)\,dT,
\tag{19}
\]
the only exponent congruent to \(-1\pmod p\) in its exponent window is the \(m=r\) term.  Thus its Cartier obstruction is exactly the marked coefficient \(b_r\); (16) explicitly constructs an antiderivative when that obstruction vanishes.  This is why I call the route Cartier–Hurwitz rather than merely “integrate the Hasse polynomial.”

Put
\[
\boxed{H_{p,r}(T)=T^{-r}Q_{p,r}(T).}
\tag{20}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
H_{p,r}'(T)=T^{-r-1}\bigl(A_p(T)-b_rT^r\bigr).
}
\tag{21}
\]
At a bad residue,
\[
\boxed{
H_{p,r}'(T)=T^{-r-1}\Delta(T)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(T)^2.
}
\tag{22}
\]
For primes counted by \(W_n\), the bad residue is never \(0\) or \(p-1\), since \(b_0=1\) and \(b_{p-1}\equiv b_0=1\pmod p\).

## Theorem 2 — complete tame passport

Assume \(p\ge5\), \(1\le r\le p-2\), and \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\).  Then
\[
H_{p,r}:\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1
\]
has degree \(p-1\), is tame, and has exactly the following ramification:

- \(0\) and \(\infty\) are the only poles, of indices
  \[
  r,\qquad p-1-r;
  \tag{23}
  \]
- every root of \(B_p\) is a distinct finite ramification point of index \(3\);
- if \(arepsilon_p=1\), the two roots of \(\Delta\) are distinct index-\(2\) points; if \(arepsilon_p=0\), there are no finite index-\(2\) points;
- there is no other ramification.

### Proof

The constant coefficient of \(Q_{p,r}\) is \(-1/r\ne0\).  By reflection \(b_{p-1}=1\) in \(\mathbf F_p\), so its top coefficient is \(1/(p-1-r)\ne0\).  Therefore \(H_{p,r}\) has exact pole orders \(r\) and \(p-1-r\), hence degree \(p-1\); both indices are \(<p\).

Theorem 1 and (22) show that each root of \(B_p\) is a double zero of \(H'\), hence has ramification index \(3\), and each conifold root in the defect branch is a simple zero of \(H'\), hence has index \(2\).

These points exhaust Riemann–Hurwitz.  The poles contribute
\[
(r-1)+(p-2-r)=p-3;
\]
the \(M_p\) roots of \(B_p\) contribute
\[
2M_p=p-1-2\varepsilon_p;
\]
and the conifold contributes \(2\varepsilon_p\).  The total is
\[
2p-4=2\deg(H_{p,r})-2.
\]
So there is no hidden ramification. \(\square\)

# 3. Large Kummer order becomes a decomposition obstruction

Set
\[
g=\gcd(r,p-1),\qquad d=\frac{p-1}{g}.
\tag{24}
\]
This is exactly the Kummer order in established fact (5).

## Theorem 3 — outer-degree divisibility and cubic packet amplification

Assume \(p\ge7\), \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\), and over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\) suppose
\[
H_{p,r}=F\circ G,
\qquad
a=\deg F>1,\quad b=\deg G>1,\quad ab=p-1.
\tag{25}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{a\mid g=\frac{p-1}{d},\qquad b\ge d.}
\tag{26}
\]
If \(d>2\), then
\[
\boxed{a\text{ is odd}.}
\tag{27}
\]
Moreover some finite ramification point \(y\) of \(F\) has \(b\ge d\) distinct preimages under \(G\); those preimages are roots of \(B_p\), all have ramification index \(3\) for \(H_{p,r}\), and all share the same critical value \(F(y)\).

Consequently, if \(d>2\) and \(g\) is a power of \(2\), then \(H_{p,r}\) is indecomposable.  In particular maximal Kummer order \(d=p-1\) forces indecomposability.

### Proof

The pole set of \(H\) is exactly \(\{0,\infty\}\).  Every pole of \(F\) has a nonempty \(G\)-preimage contained in this set, so \(F\) has one or two poles.

Suppose \(F\) has two poles.  Each must have exactly one preimage under the degree-\(b\) map \(G\), so \(G\) is totally ramified at \(0\) and \(\infty\).  After a Möbius change in the intermediate coordinate,
\[
G(T)=cT^{\pm b},
\]
and hence
\[
H(\zeta T)=H(T)\qquad(\zeta^b=1).
\tag{28}
\]
But \(H=T^{-r}Q_{p,r}\) is a Laurent polynomial.  Since \(p\ge7\) and \(b_r=0\), \(r\ne1\) because \(b_1=5\not\equiv0\pmod p\).  Therefore the \(m=0\) and \(m=1\) terms of \(Q_{p,r}\) are both present and nonzero, giving nonzero exponents \(-r\) and \(1-r\).  Invariance (28) forces every nonzero Laurent exponent to be divisible by \(b\); two such exponents cannot differ by \(1\) when \(b>1\).  Contradiction.  Thus \(F\) has one pole.

A degree-\(a\) rational function with one pole has pole order \(a\) there.  Its preimage under \(G\) is \(\{0,\infty\}\), so
\[
r=a\,e_G(0),\qquad p-1-r=a\,e_G(\infty).
\]
Hence \(a\mid r\) and \(a\mid p-1-r\), proving \(a\mid g\), and
\[
b=\frac{p-1}{a}\ge\frac{p-1}{g}=d.
\]

Now assume \(d>2\), so \(b>2\).  After changing the intermediate coordinate, \(F\) is a polynomial of degree \(a\).  Because \(1<a<p\), it has a finite ramification point \(y\).  For each \(x\in G^{-1}(y)\),
\[
e_H(x)=e_F(y)e_G(x).
\tag{29}
\]
Theorem 2 says every finite ramification index of \(H\) is \(2\) or \(3\).  Thus \(e_F(y)\in\{2,3\}\) and \(e_G(x)=1\) throughout that fiber.  If \(e_F(y)=2\), then \(G^{-1}(y)\) would give \(b>2\) distinct index-\(2\) points of \(H\), but Theorem 2 allows at most the two conifold points.  Therefore every finite ramification point of \(F\) has index \(3\).  Its fiber under \(G\) consists of \(b\ge d\) distinct index-\(3\) points of \(H\), necessarily roots of \(B_p\), all with value \(F(y)\).

Finally infinity contributes \(a-1\) to Riemann–Hurwitz for the polynomial \(F\), so the total finite contribution is also \(a-1\).  Every finite critical point contributes \(3-1=2\); hence \(a-1\) is even and \(a\) is odd.  If \(g\) is a power of \(2\), no odd \(a>1\) divides it, proving the final claim. \(\square\)

Thus the already-controlled small-\(d\) range and the unresolved large-\(d\) range are geometrically complementary: when \(d>D\), every imprimitive outer degree is \(<(p-1)/D\), and a nontrivial decomposition forces a packet of \(>D\) cubic critical points.

# 4. Falsification of the naive critical-point resultant

The preceding packet cannot be treated as \(M_p\asymp p\) independent scalar congruences.

For arbitrary \(r\), (21) gives at a root \(\alpha\) of \(B_p\)
\[
H_{p,r}'(\alpha)=-b_r\alpha^{-1}.
\tag{30}
\]
Also
\[
A_p-b_rT^r\equiv-b_rT^r\pmod{B_p}.
\tag{31}
\]
Since \(B_p(0)=1\), \(T\) is a unit modulo \(B_p\), so
\[
\gcd(B_p,A_p-b_rT^r)=
\begin{cases}
B_p,&b_r=0,\\
1,&b_r\ne0.
\end{cases}
\tag{32}
\]
Moreover the leading coefficient of \(B_p\) has square \(1\), because \(A_p\) and \(\Delta^{\varepsilon_p}\) are monic.  Therefore the root-product formula for the resultant gives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_T(B_p,A_p-b_rT^r)=u_{p,r}\,b_r^{M_p},
\qquad u_{p,r}\in\mathbf F_p^\times.
}
\tag{33}
\]
Indeed the extra factors come only from \(B_p(0)^r=1\), the leading coefficient of \(B_p\), and signs, all units.

So the \(M_p\) critical-point incidences have scalar rank one.  This rigorously kills the ordinary-resultant interpretation of the route and agrees with established obstruction (4).  What survives is the **global tame cover structure**—pole gcd, decomposition, critical-value packets—and the defining-characteristic rank-two Frobenius trace.

# 5. The next irreducible statement: a Cartier–Hurwitz operator large sieve

The project already proves in `lemmaBprime_result.tex` an effective factor
\[
P_{p,r}(X)=(X-p)\bigl(X^2-\mathfrak a_{p,r}X+p^3\bigr),
\tag{34}
\]
with
\[
\mathfrak a_{p,r}\equiv b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\tag{35}
\]
Thus a bad coefficient is exactly defining-characteristic loss of the unit root in a fixed-rank hypergeometric factor.  Generic fixed-conductor monodromy does not control this; the repository has already isolated that obstruction.  Theorems 1--3 add an Apéry-specific large-order geometric constraint.

The precise next theorem I would attack is the following.

## CHLS — Cartier–Hurwitz large sieve

There are absolute constants \(0<\eta<1\) and \(C>0\) such that, uniformly for \(P\ge2\), \(D\ge2\), and every interval \(I\subset\mathbf Z\) of \(\lceil P\rceil\) consecutive integers, the matrix
\[
\mathsf M_{P,D,I}(p,m)=
\mathbf1_{\{d_p(m)>D\}}
\mathbf1_{\{b_{r_p(m)}\equiv0\pmod p\}},
\tag{36}
\]
where
\[
r_p(m)=m\bmod p,
\qquad
d_p(m)=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(r_p(m),p-1)},
\]
and \(p\) runs over primes \(P<p\le2P\), satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\|\mathsf M_{P,D,I}\|_{\ell^2(I)\to\ell^2(\{p:P<p\le2P\})}^{\,2}
\le
C\left(
\frac{P}{D^{\eta}\log P}+P^{1-\eta}
\right).
}
\tag{CHLS}
\]

This is deliberately stronger than the desired estimate for one moving target: it controls every linear combination of targets in an interval and therefore excludes the anchored-star pathology.  It is not merely a rewrite of \(W_n=o(n)\).

## Pointwise consequence

Fix \(n\).  In a dyadic block \(P<p\le2P\) intersecting \((\sqrt n,n]\), choose an interval \(I\) of length \(\lceil P\rceil\) containing \(n\), and apply (CHLS) to the delta vector \(e_n\).  Then
\[
\#\{P<p\le2P:d_p(n)>D,\ p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}
\le\|\mathsf M_{P,D,I}\|^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\d_p(n)>D\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}\log p
\ll
\frac{P}{D^\eta}+P^{1-\eta}\log P.
\tag{37}
\]
Summing dyadically,
\[
W_n^{(d>D)}
\ll
\frac{n}{D^\eta}+n^{1-\eta}\log n.
\tag{38}
\]
Established fact (5) supplies
\[
W_n^{(d\le D)}\ll D^2\log(nD).
\tag{39}
\]
Choosing
\[
D=(\log n)^{2/\eta}
\]
gives
\[
D^2\log(nD)=n^{o(1)}=o(n),
\qquad
nD^{-\eta}=\frac n{(\log n)^2}=o(n),
\qquad
n^{1-\eta}\log n=o(n).
\]
Therefore (CHLS) implies
\[
\boxed{W_n=o(n),}
\]
and hence, by established facts (1)--(2), the all-index Apéry GCD conjecture.

Theorem 3 is relevant to proving (CHLS), not cosmetic: on the large-\(d\) support, every imprimitive outer quotient has degree at most \(P/D\), while any decomposition creates a \(>D\)-point cubic critical packet.  Theorem 1 ensures those points are genuinely distinct.  Equation (33) simultaneously warns that the sieve must act on the full cover/rank-two trace rather than pretending the individual critical points are independent congruences.

# 6. Audit against the established obstructions

**Facts (1)--(2).**  The exact target \(p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\) is never replaced by a zero-count statement.  The residue \(r=n\bmod p\) remains in the nonhomogeneous operator \(artheta-n\), and (CHLS) has a direct pointwise consequence.

**Fact (3).**  Nothing here assumes a height bound for the top-half carrier \(C_n\).  The construction is prime-by-prime on the exact residue event, so the presently controlled contamination of \(C_n\) is neither used nor confused with total control.

**Fact (4).**  The positive input is specifically Apéry: the CFVZ Hasse square, the two universal second-order square-root ODEs, and the fixed toric/hypergeometric marked trace.  Equation (33) explicitly shows why ordinary resultant height gives no gain.  No fixed-width recurrence, fixed-depth CRT jet, or generic monodromy theorem is being invoked to conclude anti-concentration; the missing theorem is explicitly defining-characteristic and moving-character.

**Fact (5).**  The route is designed around the proved small-\(d\) estimate.  In the complementary range, the same \(d\) has the exact geometric interpretation
\[
\frac{p-1}{d}=\gcd(\text{the two pole indices of }H_{p,r}),
\]
which yields Theorem 3.  Thus increasing \(d\) genuinely strengthens the cover rather than simply relabeling the hard set.  The Poisson numerics are not used.

# 7. Central-binomial counterexample

This mechanism is not generic for diagonals.  For \(c_m=\binom{2m}{m}\) and odd \(p\), Kummer carry gives \(c_m\equiv0\pmod p\) for \(m>(p-1)/2\), while for \(0\le m\le(p-1)/2\),
\[
\binom{2m}{m}
\equiv(-4)^m\binom{(p-1)/2}{m}\pmod p.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}\binom{2m}{m}T^m
=(1-4T)^{(p-1)/2}
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[T].
}
\tag{40}
\]
Its entire Hasse critical divisor is concentrated at one point of multiplicity \((p-1)/2\).  It has no analogue of Theorem 1's \(\asymp p\) distinct square-root roots and therefore no analogue of the tame cubic passport of Theorem 2.  The route is consequently genuinely Apéry-specific and does not contradict the central-binomial obstruction to generic diagonal arguments.

Conversely, (33) prevents overclaiming on the Apéry side: distinct critical points alone still do not prove independence.  Their correlation has to be controlled through the full Cartier–Hurwitz/rank-two object.

The live route is
\[
\boxed{
\text{Apéry Hasse square root}
\Longrightarrow
\text{target-preserving Cartier primitive}
\Longrightarrow
\text{large-}d\text{ Hurwitz rigidity}
\Longrightarrow

The first three arrows now contain rigorous new information:

- \(B_p\) is squarefree and coprime to \(\Delta\) for every \(p\ge5\);

- the raw critical-point resultant collapses to a unit times \(b_r^{M_p}\), so the ordinary resultant route is rigorously rejected.

The next irreducible statement is (CHLS).  It is strictly stronger than the pointwise target, has the correct moving-character quantifiers, and together with the established small-\(d\) mass estimate gives \(W_n=o(n)\) by (37)--(39).  This is the single route I would pursue next.
