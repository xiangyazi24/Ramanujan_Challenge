# 1. New theorem: the Apéry Hasse square root is reduced

For a prime \(p\ge5\), set
\[
N=p-1,\qquad
A_p(T)=\sum_{m=0}^{N}b_mT^m\in\mathbf F_p[T],\qquad
\Delta(T)=T^2-34T+1.
\]
The project records
\[
\boxed{A_p(T)=\Delta(T)^{\epsilon_p}B_p(T)^2,}
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
\epsilon_p=\frac{1-\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)}2\in\{0,1\},\qquad
B_p(0)=1,\qquad
M_p:=\deg B_p=\frac{p-1-2\epsilon_p}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
It also constructs characteristic-zero square roots
\[
\mathcal A(T)=C_+(T)^2=\Delta(T)C_-(T)^2
\tag{3}
\]
and identifies \(B_p\) with the appropriate truncation.  Put
\[
\theta=T\frac d{dT}.
\]
The two square roots satisfy
\[
L_+C_+=0,\qquad L_-C_-=0,
\]
where
\[
L_+=4\theta^2-2T(68\theta^2+34\theta+5)+T^2(2\theta+1)^2,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
L_-=4\theta^2-2T(68\theta^2+102\theta+39)+T^2(2\theta+3)^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{L_+=4\Delta\theta^2+4T(T-17)\theta+T(T-10),}
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\boxed{L_-=4\Delta\theta^2+12T(T-17)\theta+3T(3T-26).}
\tag{7}
\]

## Theorem 1

For every prime \(p\ge5\),
\[
\epsilon_p=0\Longrightarrow L_+B_p=0,
\qquad
\epsilon_p=1\Longrightarrow L_-B_p=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[T].
\tag{8}
\]
Moreover
\[
\boxed{\gcd(B_p,T\Delta)=1,\qquad B_p\text{ is squarefree over }\overline{\mathbf F}_p.}
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(B_p\) has exactly \(M_p\) distinct geometric roots, none at \(0\) or at a conifold point.

### Proof

Write
\[
C_+(T)=\sum_{m\ge0}c_mT^m,\qquad C_-(T)=\sum_{m\ge0}d_mT^m.
\]
Their coefficient recurrences are
\[
4(m+1)^2c_{m+1}=2(68m^2+34m+5)c_m-(2m-1)^2c_{m-1},
\tag{10}
\]
\[
4(m+1)^2d_{m+1}=2(68m^2+102m+39)d_m-(2m+1)^2d_{m-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

In the square branch, \(M_p=(p-1)/2\) and
\[
B_p=\sum_{m=0}^{M_p}\bar c_mT^m.
\]
Compare degree \(M_p+1=(p+1)/2<p\) in \(\mathcal A=C_+^2\) and in the exact identity \(A_p=B_p^2\).  All convolution terms agree except the two endpoint terms containing \(c_{M_p+1}\), hence
\[
2\bar c_{M_p+1}=0,
\qquad\bar c_{M_p+1}=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Recurrence (10) kills every coefficient of \(L_+B_p\) through degree \(M_p+1\).  The only possible degree-\(M_p+2\) coefficient is
\[
(2M_p+1)^2\bar c_{M_p}=p^2\bar c_{M_p}=0.
\]
So \(L_+B_p=0\) exactly.

In the defect branch, \(M_p=(p-3)/2\) and
\[
B_p=\sum_{m=0}^{M_p}\bar d_mT^m.
\]
Since \(\Delta(0)=1\), division by \(\Delta\) is valid in \(\mathbf F_p[[T]]\).  Comparing degree \(M_p+1=(p-1)/2\) in \(\mathcal A/\Delta=C_-^2\) and \(A_p/\Delta=B_p^2\) gives
\[
\bar d_{M_p+1}=0.
\tag{13}
\]
Recurrence (11) kills all lower coefficients of \(L_-B_p\), while the final possible one is
\[
(2M_p+3)^2\bar d_{M_p}=p^2\bar d_{M_p}=0.
\]
Thus \(L_-B_p=0\).

Now suppose
\[
B_p(T)=(T-\alpha)^m u(T),\qquad u(\alpha)=\nu\ne0.
\]
If \(\alpha\ne0\), \(\Delta(\alpha)\ne0\), and \(m\ge2\), the coefficient of \((T-\alpha)^{m-2}\) in \(4\Delta\theta^2B_p\) is
\[
4\Delta(\alpha)\alpha^2m(m-1)\nu\ne0,
\]
because \(2\le m<p\).  Every other term in (6) or (7) has order at least \(m-1\), contradiction.  Hence every root away from \(T\Delta=0\) is simple; \(B_p(0)=1\) excludes \(0\).

Finally let \(\Delta(\alpha)=0\).  Its discriminant is \(1152\), so its roots are distinct for \(p\ge5\); also \(\alpha\ne0,17\), since \(\Delta(0)=1\) and \(\Delta(17)=-288\).  As
\[
\Delta'(\alpha)=2(\alpha-17),
\]
the lowest term in (6) in the square branch is
\[
4\alpha^2(\alpha-17)m(2m-1)u(\alpha)(T-\alpha)^{m-1}.
\tag{14}
\]
Here \(1\le m\le(p-1)/2\), so \(2m-1\not\equiv0\pmod p\).  In the defect branch the corresponding coefficient from (7) is
\[
4\alpha^2(\alpha-17)m(2m+1)u(\alpha),
\tag{15}
\]
and \(1\le m\le(p-3)/2\) gives \(2m+1\not\equiv0\pmod p\).  Both are nonzero, contradiction.  Therefore \(B_p\) has no conifold root and is squarefree. \(\square\)

This closes an explicit caveat in `hasse_franel_descent.tex`.

# 2. The marked coefficient is a Cartier exactness obstruction

Fix \(1\le r\le p-2\) and define
\[
\boxed{
Q_{p,r}(T)=\sum_{\substack{0\le m\le p-1\\m\ne r}}
\frac{b_m}{m-r}T^m\in\mathbf F_p[T].
}
\tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{(\theta-r)Q_{p,r}=A_p-b_rT^r.}
\tag{17}
\]
For \(r=n\bmod p\), the operators \(	heta-r\) and \(	heta-n\) agree over \(\mathbf F_p\), so
\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_r
\iff
(\theta-n)Q_{p,r}=A_p=\Delta^{\epsilon_p}B_p^2.
}
\tag{18}
\]

For the Laurent differential
\[
\omega_{p,r}=T^{-r-1}A_p(T)\,dT,
\tag{19}
\]
the only exponent congruent to \(-1\pmod p\) in its exponent window is the \(m=r\) term.  Thus its Cartier obstruction is exactly \(b_r\); (16) explicitly gives an antiderivative when the obstruction vanishes.

Put
\[
\boxed{H_{p,r}(T)=T^{-r}Q_{p,r}(T).}
\tag{20}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{H_{p,r}'(T)=T^{-r-1}\bigl(A_p(T)-b_rT^r\bigr).}
\tag{21}
\]
At a bad residue,
\[
\boxed{H_{p,r}'(T)=T^{-r-1}\Delta(T)^{\epsilon_p}B_p(T)^2.}
\tag{22}
\]
Bad endpoints do not occur: \(b_0=1\) and \(b_{p-1}\equiv b_0=1\pmod p\).

## Theorem 2 — complete tame passport

Assume \(p\ge5\), \(1\le r\le p-2\), and \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\).  The map
\[
H_{p,r}:\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1
\]
has degree \(p-1\), is tame, and has exactly this ramification:

- \(0\) and \(\infty\) are its only poles, with indices \(r\) and \(p-1-r\);
- every root of \(B_p\) is a distinct finite ramification point of index \(3\);
- if \(\epsilon_p=1\), the two conifold roots have index \(2\); if \(\epsilon_p=0\), there are no finite index-\(2\) points;
- there is no other ramification.

### Proof

The constant coefficient of \(Q_{p,r}\) is \(-1/r\ne0\), while its top coefficient is \(b_{p-1}/(p-1-r)=1/(p-1-r)\ne0\).  Thus the exact pole orders are \(r\) and \(p-1-r\), and the degree is \(p-1\).  Both are less than \(p\).

By Theorem 1 and (22), a root of \(B_p\) is a double zero of \(H'\), hence has index \(3\); a conifold root in the defect branch is a simple zero of \(H'\), hence has index \(2\).

Riemann–Hurwitz is already saturated.  The poles contribute
\[
(r-1)+(p-2-r)=p-3,
\]
the roots of \(B_p\) contribute
\[
2M_p=p-1-2\epsilon_p,
\]
and the conifold contributes \(2\epsilon_p\).  The total is
\[
2p-4=2\deg(H_{p,r})-2.
\]
No other ramification exists. \(\square\)

# 3. Large Kummer order is an exact decomposition obstruction

Set
\[
g=\gcd(r,p-1),\qquad d=\frac{p-1}{g}.
\tag{23}
\]
This is exactly the Kummer order in established fact (5).

## Theorem 3 — outer-degree divisibility and cubic packet amplification

Assume \(p\ge7\), \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\), and over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\)
\[
H_{p,r}=F\circ G,
\qquad
a=\deg F>1,\quad b=\deg G>1,\quad ab=p-1.
\tag{24}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{a\mid g=\frac{p-1}{d},\qquad b\ge d.}
\tag{25}
\]
If \(d>2\), then \(a\) is odd.  Moreover some finite ramification point \(y\) of \(F\) has \(b\ge d\) distinct preimages under \(G\); those preimages are roots of \(B_p\), all have index \(3\) for \(H_{p,r}\), and all have the same critical value \(F(y)\).

Consequently, if \(d>2\) and \(g\) is a power of \(2\), then \(H_{p,r}\) is indecomposable.  In particular \(d=p-1\) forces indecomposability.

### Proof

The pole set of \(H\) is exactly \(\{0,\infty\}\).  Every pole of \(F\) has a nonempty \(G\)-preimage contained there, so \(F\) has one or two poles.

If \(F\) had two poles, each would have exactly one preimage under the degree-\(b\) map \(G\).  Thus \(G\) is totally ramified at \(0\) and \(\infty\), and after changing the intermediate coordinate
\[
G(T)=cT^{\pm b}.
\]
Hence
\[
H(\zeta T)=H(T)\qquad(\zeta^b=1).
\tag{26}
\]
Since \(p\ge7\) and \(b_r=0\), we have \(r\ne1\), because \(b_1=5\not\equiv0\pmod p\).  The \(m=0\) and \(m=1\) terms in \(Q_{p,r}\) are therefore both nonzero, so the Laurent polynomial \(H=T^{-r}Q_{p,r}\) contains exponents \(-r\) and \(1-r\).  Invariance (26) forces every nonzero exponent to be divisible by \(b\), impossible for two exponents differing by \(1\) when \(b>1\).  Thus \(F\) has one pole.

A degree-\(a\) one-pole map has pole order \(a\).  Its preimage under \(G\) is \(\{0,\infty\}\), hence
\[
r=a\,e_G(0),\qquad p-1-r=a\,e_G(\infty).
\]
So \(a\mid\gcd(r,p-1)=g\), and
\[
b=\frac{p-1}{a}\ge\frac{p-1}{g}=d.
\]

Assume now \(d>2\), so \(b>2\).  After changing the intermediate coordinate, \(F\) is a polynomial of degree \(1<a<p\), hence has a finite ramification point \(y\).  For every \(x\in G^{-1}(y)\),
\[
e_H(x)=e_F(y)e_G(x).
\tag{27}
\]
Theorem 2 allows finite indices only \(2\) and \(3\).  Therefore \(e_F(y)\in\{2,3\}\) and \(G\) is unramified over \(y\).  If \(e_F(y)=2\), its \(b>2\) preimages would give more than two index-\(2\) points of \(H\), impossible.  Thus every finite ramification point of \(F\) has index \(3\); its fiber gives \(b\ge d\) distinct roots of \(B_p\) with one common critical value.

For the polynomial \(F\), infinity contributes \(a-1\) to Riemann–Hurwitz, so the finite contribution is also \(a-1\).  Every finite ramification point contributes \(2\), hence \(a-1\) is even and \(a\) is odd.  If \(g\) is a power of \(2\), no odd \(a>1\) divides it. \(\square\)

Thus the unresolved large-\(d\) range has an actual rigidity parameter: if \(d>D\), every imprimitive outer degree is \(<(p-1)/D\), and a nontrivial decomposition forces a packet of more than \(D\) cubic critical points.

# 4. Necessary falsification: the raw resultant has rank one

For arbitrary \(r\), (21) gives at a root \(\alpha\) of \(B_p\)
\[
H_{p,r}'(\alpha)=-b_r\alpha^{-1}.
\tag{28}
\]
Also
\[
A_p-b_rT^r\equiv-b_rT^r\pmod{B_p}.
\tag{29}
\]
Since \(B_p(0)=1\), \(T\) is a unit modulo \(B_p\), and therefore
\[
\gcd(B_p,A_p-b_rT^r)=
\begin{cases}
B_p,&b_r=0,\\
1,&b_r\ne0.
\end{cases}
\tag{30}
\]
The leading coefficient of \(B_p\) has square \(1\), because \(A_p\) and \(\Delta^{\epsilon_p}\) are monic.  The root-product formula therefore gives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_T(B_p,A_p-b_rT^r)=u_{p,r}b_r^{M_p},
\qquad u_{p,r}\in\mathbf F_p^\times.
}
\tag{31}
\]
The remaining factors are only \(B_p(0)^r=1\), signs, and powers of the leading coefficient, hence units.

So the \(M_p\asymp p\) critical-point incidences are not independent arithmetic conditions.  Ordinary resultant height cannot finish the argument.  What survives is the global cover structure and the defining-characteristic rank-two trace.

# 5. The next irreducible statement: a Cartier–Hurwitz operator large sieve

The project proves in `lemmaBprime_result.tex` an effective factor
\[
P_{p,r}(X)=(X-p)\bigl(X^2-\mathfrak a_{p,r}X+p^3\bigr),
\tag{32}
\]
with
\[
\mathfrak a_{p,r}\equiv b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus a bad coefficient is exactly defining-characteristic loss of the unit root in a fixed-rank hypergeometric factor.  Generic fixed-conductor monodromy does not control this; Theorems 1–3 add the Apéry-specific large-order geometry.

The precise next theorem I would attack is:

## CHLS

There are absolute constants \(0<\eta<1\) and \(C>0\) such that, uniformly for \(P\ge2\), \(D\ge2\), and every interval \(I\subset\mathbf Z\) of \(\lceil P\rceil\) consecutive integers, the matrix
\[
\mathsf M_{P,D,I}(p,m)=
\mathbf1_{\{d_p(m)>D\}}
\mathbf1_{\{b_{r_p(m)}\equiv0\pmod p\}},
\tag{34}
\]
where
\[
r_p(m)=m\bmod p,\qquad
d_p(m)=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(r_p(m),p-1)},
\]
and \(p\) runs over primes \(P<p\le2P\), satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\|\mathsf M_{P,D,I}\|_{\ell^2(I)\to\ell^2(\{p:P<p\le2P\})}^{\,2}
\le
C\left(\frac{P}{D^\eta\log P}+P^{1-\eta}\right).
}
\tag{CHLS}
\]

This is strictly stronger than the desired estimate for one moving target: it controls every linear combination of targets in an interval and therefore excludes the anchored-star pathology.  It is not an equivalent rewrite of \(W_n=o(n)\).

## Pointwise consequence

Fix \(n\).  In a dyadic block \(P<p\le2P\) intersecting \((\sqrt n,n]\), choose an interval \(I\) of length \(\lceil P\rceil\) containing \(n\), and apply (CHLS) to the delta vector \(e_n\).  Then
\[
\#\{P<p\le2P:d_p(n)>D,\ p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}
\le\|\mathsf M_{P,D,I}\|^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\d_p(n)>D\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}\log p
\ll
\frac{P}{D^\eta}+P^{1-\eta}\log P.
\tag{35}
\]
Summing dyadically,
\[
W_n^{(d>D)}\ll\frac n{D^\eta}+n^{1-\eta}\log n.
\tag{36}
\]
Established fact (5) gives
\[
W_n^{(d\le D)}\ll D^2\log(nD).
\tag{37}
\]
Take \(D=(\log n)^{2/\eta}\).  Then the right sides of (36)–(37) are \(o(n)\), so
\[
\boxed{W_n=o(n).}
\]
By established facts (1)–(2), this proves the all-index Apéry GCD conjecture.

Theorem 3 is relevant to a proof of (CHLS), rather than cosmetic: on the large-\(d\) support, every imprimitive outer quotient has degree at most \(P/D\), while decomposition creates a \(>D\)-point cubic packet.  Theorem 1 makes those points genuinely distinct.  Equation (31) simultaneously says the sieve must act on the full cover/rank-two trace, not on the roots as independent congruences.

# 6. Audit against the five established facts

**Facts (1)–(2).**  The exact event \(p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\) is retained throughout.  The target residue remains in the nonhomogeneous operator \(	heta-n\), and (CHLS) has the explicit pointwise consequence (35)–(37).

**Fact (3).**  Nothing assumes a total-height bound for the top-half carrier \(C_n\).  The construction is prime-by-prime on the exact residue event, so the presently controlled contamination of \(C_n\) is not mistaken for total control.

**Fact (4).**  The input is Apéry-specific: the Hasse-square factorization, the two universal second-order square-root ODEs, and the toric/hypergeometric marked trace.  Equation (31) proves that the naive ordinary resultant gives no gain.  No fixed-width recurrence, fixed-depth CRT jet, or generic monodromy theorem is being used to infer anti-concentration; the missing theorem is explicitly defining-characteristic and moving-character.

**Fact (5).**  The already proved small-\(d\) estimate is kept exactly.  In the complementary range the same \(d\) satisfies
\[
\frac{p-1}{d}=\gcd(\text{the two pole indices of }H_{p,r}),
\]
which yields Theorem 3.  Increasing \(d\) therefore strengthens the geometry rather than merely relabeling the hard set.  The Poisson-looking numerics are unused.

# 7. Central-binomial counterexample audit

For \(c_m=\binom{2m}{m}\) and odd \(p\), Kummer carry gives \(c_m\equiv0\pmod p\) for \(m>(p-1)/2\), while for \(0\le m\le(p-1)/2\),
\[
\binom{2m}{m}\equiv(-4)^m\binom{(p-1)/2}{m}\pmod p.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}\binom{2m}{m}T^m=(1-4T)^{(p-1)/2}
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[T].
}
\tag{38}
\]
Its entire Hasse divisor is concentrated at one point of multiplicity \((p-1)/2\).  It has no analogue of Theorem 1's \(\asymp p\) distinct square-root roots and therefore no analogue of Theorem 2's tame cubic passport.  The mechanism is genuinely Apéry-specific and does not contradict the central-binomial obstruction to generic diagonal arguments.

Conversely, (31) prevents overclaiming on the Apéry side: distinct critical points alone do not imply arithmetic independence; their correlation has to be controlled through the full Cartier–Hurwitz/rank-two object.
