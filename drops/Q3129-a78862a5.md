# P3.2 analytic-index recurrence / NCT audit

## Verdict

The natural analytic hypergeometric interpolation does **not** satisfy the homogeneous Apéry recurrence off the integers.  The exact continuous-index equation has a nonzero WZ boundary term at infinity:

\[
\boxed{
(x+1)^3B(x+1)-P(x)B(x)+x^3B(x-1)
=\Phi(x):=\frac{8(2x+1)\sin^2(\pi x)}{\pi^2},
}
\]
with
\[
P(x)=34x^3+51x^2+27x+5=(2x+1)(17x^2+17x+5).
\]

This is the exact analytic-index failure.  It is benign at first order at every integer:
\[
\Phi(s)=\Phi'(s)=0,
\qquad
\Phi''(s)=16(2s+1)\ne0.
\]
Thus the ordinary recurrence for \(b_s=B(s)\) and the **differentiated** recurrence for \(J_s=B'(s)\) are nevertheless exact.

Writing \(c_s\) for the project-normalized canonical companion
\[
c_0=0,\qquad c_1=6,
\]
(the sequence called \(a_s\) in `problems/3.2/proof.tex`), the differentiated recurrence gives an exact variation-of-parameters/Casoratian identity
\[
\boxed{J_s=\alpha_{s-1}b_s+\beta_{s-1}c_s}\qquad(s\ge1),
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
\beta_r=\frac{(r+1)^3}{6}
       \bigl(b_rJ_{r+1}-b_{r+1}J_r\bigr)
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\beta_r-eta_{r-1}=\frac{b_rF_r}{6},\qquad
\beta_0=2,
}
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
F_r=\frac{3}{r+1}
\bigl((17r^2+16r+4)b_r-r^2b_{r-1}\bigr).
}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\beta_r
=2+\sum_{m=1}^r
\frac{b_m\bigl((17m^2+16m+4)b_m-m^2b_{m-1}\bigr)}{2(m+1)}.
}
\]

For every prime \(p>2s+1\) with \(p\mid b_s\), the usual Apéry Casoratian makes \(c_s\) a \(p\)-unit, and all denominators in the displayed identity are \(p\)-units.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_s,\ p>2s+1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(p\mid \operatorname{num}J_s\bigr)
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \bigl(p\mid \operatorname{num}\beta_{s-1}\bigr).
}
\]
So the analytic-index attack reduces NCT exactly to a new gcd problem
\(\gcd(b_s,\operatorname{num}\beta_{s-1})\); it does not itself eliminate the large prime.

Moreover, the **canonical Casoratian coefficient is not smooth** in the requested sense already at \(s=2\):
\[
\beta_1=232=2^3\cdot29,
\qquad 29>2\cdot2+1=5.
\]
Indeed
\[
J_2=-276\,b_2+232\,c_2,
\qquad
b_2=73,\quad c_2=\frac{351}{4},\quad J_2=210.
\]
Thus no scaling of this canonical two-index Casoratian decomposition by a number whose prime support is \(\le 2s+1\) can make its companion coefficient \(C_s\) have only such prime support.

There is an important nonuniqueness caveat: a **single-index** identity
\(D_sJ_s=A_sb_s+C_sc_s\) is not canonical.  For the same \(s=2\), for example,
\[
\boxed{J_2=75b_2-60c_2,}
\]
and \(-60\) is 5-smooth.  This is obtained from the canonical coefficient by
\(232-4b_2=-60\) and \(-276+4c_2=75\).  Hence the mere existence of some one-index decomposition with smooth \(C_s\) cannot be disproved from \(\beta_1\); without an additional canonicalization/integrality condition it is also too nonrigid to prove NCT.  What fails is the hoped-for **canonical Casoratian** smooth-coefficient mechanism.

I therefore do **not** claim the target theorem
\[
p>2s+1,\ p\mid b_s\Longrightarrow p\nmid J_s
\]
here.  The continuous-index recurrence gives an exact reduction, but no new reason that a common large prime cannot divide \(b_s\) and \(\beta_{s-1}\).

## 2. The analytic interpolation and its integer derivative

Set
\[
t_k(x):=
\frac{(-x)_k^2(x+1)_k^2}{(k!)^4}
\]
and take the natural analytic continuation
\[
\boxed{
B(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_k(x)
={}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}-x,-x,x+1,x+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1\right).
}
\]
For \(s\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}\), \((-s)_k=0\) for \(k>s\), hence
\[
B(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{s}
\binom{s}{k}^2\binom{s+k}{k}^2=b_s.
\]

### Analyticity

Using
\[
\frac{(-x)_k}{k!}
=\frac{\Gamma(k-x)}{\Gamma(-x)\Gamma(k+1)},
\qquad
\frac{(x+1)_k}{k!}
=\frac{\Gamma(k+x+1)}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(k+1)},
\]
the uniform gamma-ratio asymptotics on compact \(x\)-sets give
\[
\frac{\Gamma(k-x)}{\Gamma(k+1)}=k^{-x-1}(1+O_K(k^{-1})),
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma(k+x+1)}{\Gamma(k+1)}=k^x(1+O_K(k^{-1})).
\]
Therefore
\[
t_k(x)
=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-x)^2\Gamma(x+1)^2}\frac1{k^2}
  (1+O_K(k^{-1}))
=\frac{\sin^2(\pi x)}{\pi^2k^2}
  (1+O_K(k^{-1})).
\]
The differentiated terms have an extra \(O_K(\log k)\), still summable against \(k^{-2}\).  Hence \(B\) is entire and termwise differentiation is valid.

For \(k>s\), \((-x)_k^2\) has a zero of order at least two at \(x=s\), so those nonterminating analytic-tail terms contribute neither to \(B(s)\) nor to \(B'(s)\).  For \(0\le k\le s\), logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
\frac{t_k'(s)}{t_k(s)}
=2\left(
  \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac1{s-j}
 +\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac1{s+1+j}
\right)
=2(H_{s+k}-H_{s-k}).
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
J_s:=B'(s)
=2\sum_{k=0}^{s}
\binom{s}{k}^2\binom{s+k}{k}^2
\bigl(H_{s+k}-H_{s-k}\bigr).
}
\]
In particular every prime occurring in the denominator of \(J_s\) is at most \(2s\).  Hence reduction of \(J_s\) modulo a prime \(p>2s+1\) is equivalent to reduction of its reduced numerator.

The first values are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
s&0&1&2&3&4\\ \hline
b_s&1&5&73&1445&33001\\
J_s&0&12&210&4438&104825.
\end{array}
\]

## 3. Continuous-index WZ equation: the exact boundary defect

The repository WZ certificate is, in the shifted \((x,x+1,x+2)\) convention,
\[
a_0(x)=-(x+1)^3,
\]
\[
a_1(x)=(2x+3)(17x^2+51x+39),
\]
\[
a_2(x)=-(x+2)^3,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
R(x,k)=
\frac{4k^4(2x+3)
(4x^2+12x-2k^2+3k+8)}
{(x-k+1)^2(x-k+2)^2}.
}
\]
Put
\[
G_k(x)=R(x,k)t_k(x).
\]
The apparent poles of \(R\) are cancelled by the corresponding squared zeros of \(t_k\), so \(G_k\) has the required removable continuation.  The WZ identity is
\[
\boxed{
-(x+1)^3t_k(x)
+(2x+3)(17x^2+51x+39)t_k(x+1)
-(x+2)^3t_k(x+2)
=G_{k+1}(x)-G_k(x).
}
\]
It is a rational identity in \((x,k)\), hence the polynomial identity already verified in the repository for the binomial summand extends to generic complex \(x\), and then by removable continuation to all \(x\).

The crucial point is the upper WZ boundary.  As \(k\to\infty\),
\[
R(x,k)=-8(2x+3)k^2+O_K(k),
\]
while
\[
t_k(x)=\frac{\sin^2(\pi x)}{\pi^2k^2}+O_K(k^{-3}).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{k\to\infty}G_k(x)
=-\frac{8(2x+3)\sin^2(\pi x)}{\pi^2}.
}
\]
Since \(G_0(x)=0\), summing the WZ identity over \(k\ge0\) gives
\[
\boxed{
-(x+1)^3B(x)
+(2x+3)(17x^2+51x+39)B(x+1)
-(x+2)^3B(x+2)
=-\frac{8(2x+3)\sin^2(\pi x)}{\pi^2}.
}
\]
Replacing \(x\) by \(x-1\) and multiplying by \(-1\) yields the standard form
\[
\boxed{
(x+1)^3B(x+1)-P(x)B(x)+x^3B(x-1)
=\frac{8(2x+1)\sin^2(\pi x)}{\pi^2}.
}
\]

This isolates the precise mistake in a naive continuous-index proof: for integer \(s\) the hypergeometric sum terminates and the telescoping boundary vanishes, but after analytic continuation the infinite tail is nonzero and its WZ antiderivative tends to a nonzero constant.  One cannot simply reuse the terminating boundary argument for noninteger \(x\).

A one-line falsification of the homogeneous continuous equation is \(x=1/2\):
\[
\Phi(1/2)=\frac{16}{\pi^2}\ne0.
\]
More structurally,
\[
\Phi(s)=0,\qquad \Phi'(s)=0,
\qquad \boxed{\Phi''(s)=16(2s+1)}.
\]
So the interpolation agrees with the homogeneous recurrence to first order at every integer, but not to second order.

## 4. Differentiated integer recurrence

Differentiate
\[
(x+1)^3B(x+1)-P(x)B(x)+x^3B(x-1)=\Phi(x).
\]
Since \(\Phi'(s)=0\), evaluation at an integer \(s\ge1\) gives
\[
\boxed{
(s+1)^3J_{s+1}-P(s)J_s+s^3J_{s-1}
+3(s+1)^2b_{s+1}-P'(s)b_s+3s^2b_{s-1}=0,
}
\]
where
\[
P'(s)=102s^2+102s+27.
\]
Define
\[
L_su:=(s+1)^3u_{s+1}-P(s)u_s+s^3u_{s-1}.
\]
Then
\[
L_s b=0,
\qquad
\boxed{L_sJ=F_s},
\]
with
\[
F_s=-3(s+1)^2b_{s+1}+P'(s)b_s-3s^2b_{s-1}.
\]
Eliminating \(b_{s+1}\) by the Apéry recurrence gives the useful two-term form
\[
\boxed{
F_s=\frac{3}{s+1}
\left((17s^2+16s+4)b_s-s^2b_{s-1}\right).
}
\]
For example
\[
F_1=276,
\qquad
8J_2-117J_1+J_0=1680-1404=276,
\]
and
\[
F_2=7572.
\]
These are exact arithmetic checks of the differentiated formula.

## 5. Canonical companion and ordinary Casoratian

\[
c_0=0,\qquad c_1=6,\qquad L_sc=0.
\]
The existing project Casoratian is
\[
\boxed{
c_sb_{s-1}-c_{s-1}b_s=\frac6{s^3}}\qquad(s\ge1).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
b_sc_{s+1}-b_{s+1}c_s=\frac6{(s+1)^3}}.

The first companion values are
\[

\qquad
c_2=\frac{351}{4},

\quad\text{and}\quad

\]
So the canonical companion is exactly the right second basis vector for testing \(J_s\) at a target prime.

## 6. Exact mixed Casoratian / variation of parameters

Define the mixed determinant
\[
\Delta_s:=b_sJ_{s+1}-b_{s+1}J_s.
\]
Subtracting \(J_s\) times the homogeneous recurrence for \(b\) from \(b_s\) times the inhomogeneous recurrence for \(J\) gives
\[
\boxed{
(s+1)^3\Delta_s-s^3\Delta_{s-1}=b_sF_s.
}
\]
Since
\[
\Delta_0=b_0J_1-b_1J_0=12,
\]
put
\[
\boxed{
\beta_s:=\frac{(s+1)^3}{6}\Delta_s.
}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\beta_0=2,
\qquad
\beta_s-\beta_{s-1}=\frac{b_sF_s}{6}.
}
\]
This gives the closed finite sum quoted in the verdict:
\[
\boxed{
\beta_s
=2+\sum_{r=1}^s
\frac{b_r\bigl((17r^2+16r+4)b_r-r^2b_{r-1}\bigr)}{2(r+1)}.
}
\]

Likewise define
\[
\boxed{
\alpha_s:=\frac{(s+1)^3}{6}
\bigl(J_sc_{s+1}-J_{s+1}c_s\bigr).
}
\]
The same determinant subtraction, now against \(c\), gives
\[
\boxed{
\alpha_0=0,
\qquad
\alpha_s-\alpha_{s-1}=-\frac{c_sF_s}{6}.
}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\alpha_s=-\sum_{r=1}^s\frac{c_rF_r}{6}.
}
\]

The determinant of the fundamental matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
b_s&c_s\\
b_{s+1}&c_{s+1}
\end{pmatrix}
\]
is \(6/(s+1)^3\).  Cramer's rule therefore gives, **simultaneously at the two adjacent indices**,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
J_s&=\alpha_s b_s+\beta_s c_s,\\
J_{s+1}&=\alpha_s b_{s+1}+\beta_s c_{s+1}.
\end{aligned}}
\]
Shifting the pair by one gives the desired one-index form
\[
\boxed{
J_s=\alpha_{s-1}b_s+\beta_{s-1}c_s.
}
\]
This is the canonical Casoratian/Bezout identity attached to the adjacent fundamental pair \((s-1,s)\).

### Denominator support

From the harmonic formula for \(J_r\), the project denominator bound for \(c_r\), and the explicit factor \(r+1\) in \(F_r\), every denominator of \(\alpha_{s-1}\) and \(\beta_{s-1}\) has prime support at most \(s\) (an overkill common clearing factor is, for example, \(6\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,s)^4\)).  Thus for every \(p>2s+1\) the coefficients \(\alpha_{s-1},\beta_{s-1}\) are \(p\)-integral.

Consequently, at a target prime \(p\mid b_s\),
\[
J_s\equiv\beta_{s-1}c_s\pmod p.
\]
Since \(c_s\) is a \(p\)-unit,
\[
\boxed{
 p>2s+1,\ p\mid b_s
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 p\mid\operatorname{num}J_s
 \iff
 p\mid\operatorname{num}\beta_{s-1}.
}
\]
This is, in my view, the strongest clean exact output of the analytic-index recurrence attack.

## 7. Why the hoped-for smooth canonical coefficient fails

At \(s=1\),
\[
F_1=276,
\]
so
\[
\beta_1=\beta_0+\frac{b_1F_1}{6}
=2+\frac{5\cdot276}{6}
=232.
\]
Also
\[
\alpha_1=-\frac{c_1F_1}{6}=-276.
\]
Therefore the adjacent-pair identity used at index \(s=2\) is
\[
\boxed{J_2=-276b_2+232c_2.}
\]
Numerically,
\[
-276\cdot73+232\cdot\frac{351}{4}
=-20148+20358=210=J_2.
\]
But
\[
\boxed{232=2^3\cdot29},
\qquad 29>5=2s+1.
\]
Thus the natural/canonical coefficient \(\beta_{s-1}\) already has a forbidden large prime at \(s=2\).

If one writes the canonical identity after clearing denominators as
\[
D_sJ_s=A_sb_s+C_sc_s
\]
with \(D_s\) having only primes \(\le2s+1\), then in the canonical adjacent-pair normalization \(C_s=D_s\beta_{s-1}\).  Multiplication by such a \(D_s\) cannot remove the numerator factor 29 from \(\beta_1\).  Hence the requested support property for \(D_sC_s\) is **false for the canonical Casoratian coefficients**.

This does **not** refute NCT: 29 does not divide \(b_2=73\).  It only shows that a stronger global statement “the coefficient of \(c_s\) has no large prime factors” is not the mechanism.

## 8. Nonuniqueness of a one-index Bezout identity

One must distinguish the canonical two-index Cramer decomposition from an arbitrary identity at one fixed index.  Since an identity
\[
J_s=A_sb_s+C_sc_s
\]
uses only one scalar equation for two coefficients, \(C_s\) can be shifted by a multiple of \(b_s\) whenever the corresponding change in \(A_s\) is allowed.

The \(s=2\) example makes this explicit:
\[
\beta_1-4b_2=232-4\cdot73=-60,
\]
and
\[
\alpha_1+4c_2=-276+4\cdot\frac{351}{4}=75.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{J_2=75b_2-60c_2.}
\]
Here \(-60\) has prime support \(\{2,3,5\}\), all \(\le5\).

So an assertion merely of the form

> there exist \(D_s,A_s,C_s\) with \(D_sC_s\) supported on primes \(\le2s+1\)

is not sufficiently canonical to be forced or ruled out by the Casoratian.  If arbitrary rational \(A_s\) is allowed it is nearly vacuous: one can prescribe \(C_s\) and solve for \(A_s\).  Even requiring \(A_s\in\mathbf Z\) does not restore uniqueness, as the displayed \(s=2\) identity shows.

For an NCT proof one needs a **uniform explicit choice** whose coefficients are known to be \(p\)-integral for every \(p>2s+1\), and whose coefficient of the \(p\)-unit \(c_s\) is itself a \(p\)-unit.  The adjacent-Casoratian choice is canonical and \(p\)-integral, but its companion coefficient is \(\beta_{s-1}\), whose numerator has uncontrolled large primes.

Thus the recurrence has moved, not removed, the obstruction.

### 9.1 WZ rational identity

Let
\[
q_k(x)=\frac{t_{k+1}(x)}{t_k(x)}
=\left(\frac{(k-x)(k+x+1)}{(k+1)^2}\right)^2,

\[
r_1(x,k)=\frac{t_k(x+1)}{t_k(x)}
=\left(\frac{x+k+1}{x-k+1}\right)^2,

and
\[
r_2(x,k)=\frac{t_k(x+2)}{t_k(x)}

Then verify identically in \(\mathbf Q(x,k)\):
\[
\boxed{

+(2x+3)(17x^2+51x+39)r_1(x,k)
-(x+2)^3r_2(x,k)
=R(x,k+1)q_k(x)-R(x,k).

A direct Sage-style checker is:

```python
R.<x,k> = PolynomialRing(QQ)
K = Frac(R)

Psh = (2*x+3)*(17*x^2+51*x+39)
Rcert = lambda kk: (
    4*kk^4*(2*x+3)*(4*x^2+12*x-2*kk^2+3*kk+8)
    / ((x-kk+1)^2*(x-kk+2)^2)
)

r1 = ((x+k+1)/(x-k+1))^2
r2 = (((x+k+1)*(x+k+2))/((x-k+1)*(x-k+2)))^2

lhs = -(x+1)^3 + Psh*r1 - (x+2)^3*r2
rhs = Rcert(k+1)*q - Rcert(k)
assert (lhs-rhs) == 0
```

This is exactly the continuous rational form of the repository's already-verified cleared polynomial certificate.

## 10. Consequence for the proposed NCT route

The hoped-for route was:

1. analytically interpolate \(b_s\) by \(B(x)\);
2. differentiate a homogeneous continuous recurrence;
3. solve the resulting inhomogeneous recurrence against the canonical companion;
4. obtain \(D_sJ_s=A_sb_s+C_sc_s\) with \(D_sC_s\) supported only on primes \(\le2s+1\);
5. for \(p>2s+1\), \(p\mid b_s\), use the Casoratian to make \(c_s\) a unit and conclude \(J_s\ne0\pmod p\).

Steps 1--3 can be made exact after inserting the WZ boundary defect.  Step 4 is where the route breaks:

* the homogeneous continuous recurrence is false, with exact defect \(\Phi(x)\);
* the defect has a double integer zero, so it does **not** damage the first differentiated recurrence;
* the resulting canonical companion coefficient is the explicit \(\beta_{s-1}\) above;
* \(\beta_{s-1}\) has no global small-prime-support property (already \(\beta_1=232\) contains 29);
* modulo a target prime, the original problem is exactly transferred to whether that prime divides \(\beta_{s-1}\).

So the analytic-index/Casoratian attack does not currently prove
\[
\boxed{
\text{every prime divisor of }\gcd(b_s,\operatorname{num}J_s)
\text{ is }\le2s+1.
}
\]
The exact reusable output for a next attack is instead the reduction
\[
\boxed{
