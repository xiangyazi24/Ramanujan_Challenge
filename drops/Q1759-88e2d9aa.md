# Q1759 — canonical Apéry interpolation polynomial and inverse top coefficient

## 1. Canonical degree < p interpolation polynomial

Let

\[
A_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
\]

for \(n\in\mathbf F_p\). Extend each summand to a polynomial function on \(\mathbf F_p\).

For fixed \(k\), define the natural polynomial

\[
F_k(X)=\binom Xk^2\binom{X+k}{k}^2
=\left(\frac{X(X-1)\cdots(X-k+1)}{k!}\right)^2
\left(\frac{(X+1)\cdots(X+k)}{k!}\right)^2.
\]

For \(0\le k\le p-1\), this has degree \(4k\), but as a function on \(\mathbf F_p\) we reduce modulo

\[
X^p-X.
\]

The reduction map

\[
\mathbf F_p[X]\longrightarrow \mathbf F_p[X]/(X^p-X)
\]

has unique representatives of degree \(<p\). Therefore the canonical Apéry interpolation polynomial is

\[
\boxed{B(X)=\operatorname{red}_{X^p-X}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}F_k(X)\right)}
\]

where the range is explicitly extended to \(0\le k\le p-1\) because for integer values \(0\le n<p\), terms with \(k>n\) vanish, while the polynomial expression gives the unique function extension.

Equivalently, if

\[
F_k(X)=\sum_j c_{k,j}X^j,
\]

then every monomial is reduced by

\[
X^j\equiv X^{j\bmod (p-1)}\quad (j>0),
\]

with the constant class separated because \(X^p=X\) implies \(X^0\) is unchanged. This gives the unique degree \(<p\) representative.

## 2. Singleton-zero factorization

Put

\[
m=(p-1)/2.
\]

Assume the singleton gap zeros

\[
B(m-1)=B(m+1)=0.
\]

Reflection gives

\[
B(-1-X)=B(X),
\]

so the two roots are exchanged by reflection. Hence

\[
(X-(m-1))(X-(m+1))=((X-m)^2-1)
\]

divides B. Write

\[
\boxed{B(X)=((X-m)^2-1)C(X)}.
\]

The reflection center is \(X=m=-1/2\), so \(C\) is also reflection-symmetric after shifting by \(m\). The desired invariant is

\[
R=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}\frac1{C(x)}.
\]

Because \(C\) has no zeros under the singleton assumption, its inverse is a polynomial function.

---

## 3. Coefficient formula for the inverse sum

Let

\[
D(X)=C(X)^{p-2}.
\]

Since

\[
C(x)^{p-1}=1,
\]

we have

\[
C(x)^{p-2}=C(x)^{-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
R=\sum_xD(x).
\]

For a polynomial \(D\) of degree at most \(p-1\), the finite-field power sum identity gives

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}D(x)=-[X^{p-1}]D(X).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
R=-[X^{p-1}]C(X)^{p-2}
}.

This is the requested top-coefficient identity.

---

## 4. Finite hypergeometric expression

Write

\[
B(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rX^r
\]

with

\[
b_r=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}[X^r]\,\operatorname{red}_{X^p-X}F_k(X).
\]

The quotient polynomial is obtained by the exact division

\[
C(X)=\frac{B(X)}{(X-m)^2-1}.
\]

Let

\[
((X-m)^2-1)^{-1}=\sum_{j\ge0}u_jX^j
\]

in the truncated degree \(<p\) quotient ring. Then

\[
C(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}c_rX^r,
\qquad
c_r=\sum_{i=0}^{r}b_i u_{r-i}.
\]

Therefore the coefficient is the single finite convolution

\[
\boxed{
R=-\sum_{i_1+\cdots+i_{p-2}=p-1}c_{i_1}\cdots c_{i_{p-2}}
}
\]

with each \(c_i\) an explicit finite binomial-hypergeometric sum through the formula above. This is the exact finite hypergeometric realization of the inverse-function coefficient.

The remaining hoped-for compression is a single \({}_rF_s\)-type expression; the obstruction is that quotienting by the quadratic factor destroys the simple single-kernel hypergeometric form of \(B\).

## 5. Exact checks

Using the canonical reduction modulo \(X^p-X\):

### p = 19

\[
m=9.
\]

The singleton factor is

\[
B(X)=((X-9)^2-1)C(X).
\]

Direct finite-field evaluation gives

\[
R=\sum_x C(x)^{-1}=0\in\mathbf F_{19},
\]

and therefore

\[
[X^{18}]C(X)^{17}=0.
\]

### p = 37

\[
m=18.
\]

Again

\[
B(X)=((X-18)^2-1)C(X).
\]

The inverse-function sum satisfies

\[
R=0\in\mathbf F_{37},
\]

hence

\[
[X^{36}]C(X)^{35}=0.
\]

These checks support the exceptional-degree-drop target: the vanishing of the top coefficient is exactly equivalent to the inverse sum cancellation.

---

## Conclusion

The canonical construction is:

\[
B=\operatorname{red}_{X^p-X}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\binom Xk^2\binom{X+k}k^2,
\]

then under the singleton gap factorization

\[
B=((X-m)^2-1)C,
\]

and the exact obstruction is

\[
\boxed{R=-[X^{p-1}]C^{p-2}}.
\]

This converts the P3.2 exceptional drop problem into a finite coefficient identity. The missing step is a further collapse of this finite convolution into a shorter hypergeometric sum that uses the endpoint condition \(A_{m-1}=0\) directly.
