## 1. Fully-deflated common-root/content lemma

### Verdict

The lemma as currently phrased is not fully general enough for the objects
\(F^{fd}_{a,b,c},G^{fd}_{a,b,c},J^{fd}_{a,b,c}\). The issue is not nonvanishing or denominators; it is that the usual sentence

```
 t distinct common roots imply v_p(Resultant(f,g)) >= t
```

requires an explicit hypothesis that the Sylvester map is the fixed characteristic-zero map after padding to the ambient degrees. In particular, the proof must not argue from the reduced degrees after leading coefficients vanish modulo p.

### Minimal patch

Replace the local lemma by:

```latex
\begin{lemma}[Deflated common-root valuation]
Let $f,g,j\in\mathbb Z[x]$ be nonzero. Fix ambient degrees
$A\ge\deg f$, $B\ge\deg g$, $C\ge\deg j$, and let
$M(f,g,j)$ be any fixed integer Macaulay/Sylvester-type matrix whose
maximal minors generate the corresponding common-root content ideal in
these ambient degrees. If modulo $p$ the reductions of $f,g,j$ are all
nonzero and have $t$ distinct common roots in $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$,
then
\[
 v_p(\operatorname{cont} M(f,g,j))\ge t.
\]
The statement remains true if the reductions lose leading degree.
\end{lemma}
```

Proof:

```latex
\begin{proof}
For each common root $\alpha$ the evaluation vector at $\alpha$
annihilates the rows of the reduced Macaulay map. Distinct roots give
linearly independent evaluation functionals after extension to
$\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. Hence the reduction of the fixed ambient
matrix has corank at least $t$. Smith normal form of the integer
matrix then implies that at least $t$ invariant factors are divisible
by $p$, so the product of the invariant factors, namely the content of
the maximal-minor ideal, has valuation at least $t$.
No argument involving the reduced leading coefficient is used.
\end{proof}
```

Applied to the paper: after Definition qc-fd, add that the ambient
degrees are frozen before reduction. Then Lemma qc-fd applies to the
three fully-deflated polynomials. Multiple roots are irrelevant because
only distinct common roots are counted. Multiplicity would require a
Hermite/Macaulay refinement and is not used.

## 2. Block lower bound and level constant

The selected family gives, for a block decomposition into intervals of
length at most $H$,

\[
Q_p(H)\ge Z(p)-3\lceil p/H\rceil .
\]

The loss of 3 is global because each occupied block contributes the
number of selected sliding quadruples minus at most three endpoint zeros.

For the level

\[
T<Z(p)\le2T,
\qquad H=\left\lceil\frac{16X}{T}\right\rceil,
\]

we have $p\le2X$, hence

\[
\frac pH\le \frac{2X}{16X/T}=\frac T8.
\]
Therefore

\[
\left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil
\le \frac T8+1,
\]
and consequently

\[
3\left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil
\le\frac{3T}{8}+3.
\]

Thus

\[
Q_p(H)
\ge Z(p)-\frac{3T}{8}-3.
\]
Using $Z(p)>T$ gives

\[
Q_p(H)>\frac{5T}{8}-3.
\]

The smallest dyadic level in the proof is $T\ge\sqrt X$. Since the
exceptional small levels were already separated, for sufficiently large
$X$ one has $T\to\infty$, and in particular

\[
\frac{5T}{8}-3\ge \frac T2.
\]

Therefore the displayed level certificate can safely be strengthened
from the previous informal $T/3$ to

```latex
\begin{equation}
\frac T2\le
\sum_{\substack{a,b,c\ge2,\ a+b+c\le H\\\neg(a=b=c)}}
 v_p(\mathcal C_{a,b,c})
+\sum_{3a\le H}v_p(S_{a,a}).
\end{equation}
```

No change to the final exponent is needed.

## Final verdict

1. The fully-deflated certificate argument is valid after replacing the
local valuation lemma by the ambient-degree Macaulay/Sylvester version
above. The gap is a missing hypothesis/proof detail, not a mathematical
failure.

2. The block bookkeeping is valid, but the constant in the level lower
bound should be patched. The exact arithmetic gives $5T/8-3$, hence
$T/2$ for the actual dyadic range. The previous $T/3$ is weaker but also
correct for sufficiently large $X$; the patch improves clarity and makes
the ceiling loss explicit.
