## 1. Exact primitive and the finite second-order ODE

Fix a prime `p >= 5`.  Work in `F_p`.  Put

\[
A=A_p(T)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_mT^m
       =W(T)B(T)^2,
\qquad
W(T)=\Delta(T)^\varepsilon,
\qquad
\Delta(T)=T^2-34T+1,
\]

where

\[
\varepsilon=\frac{1-\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)}2\in\{0,1\},
\qquad B=B_p,
\]

and `B(0)=1`.  I use the squarefreeness of `B` as in the question.

Let `1 <= r <= p-2` and assume `b_r=0`.  Since `m-r` is nonzero in `F_p` whenever `0 <= m <= p-1` and `m != r`, the polynomial

\[
Q=Q_{p,r}(T)=\sum_{m\ne r}\frac{b_m}{m-r}T^m

is well-defined.  With `theta=T d/dT`, coefficient comparison gives the exact identity

\[
(\theta-r)Q=A. \tag{1.1}

\[
H=T^{-r}Q,

then

\[
H'=T^{-r-1}A=T^{-r-1}WB^2. \tag{1.2}

The condition `b_r=0` is exactly what removes the missing `T^r` coefficient in (1.1).  Also the endpoints are harmless: `b_0=1`, and the Apéry reflection gives `b_{p-1}=b_0=1 (mod p)`, so an Apéry target zero is automatically interior.

### Proposition 1 (the truncation `B_p` satisfies the actual ODE)

#### Proof

\mathcal A=C_+^2=\Delta C_-^2,
\qquad L_+C_+=0,

If `epsilon=0`, then `A_p=B^2`, hence

\[
C_+=BC_+^p. \tag{1.4}

Therefore the coefficients of `C_+` in the interval

\[

If `epsilon=1`, divide the Lucas identity by `Delta` and use `C_-^2=\mathcal A/\Delta`:

## 2. The degree-`<=2` factorization is unconditional

### Theorem 4 (quadratic Frobenius descent of every `B_p` root)

Let `p>=5`.  Every root `alpha` of `B_p` in `\overline{F}_p` belongs to `F_{p^2}`.  Consequently

\[
\boxed{
  B_p \text{ is a product over }F_p\text{ of irreducibles of degree }1\text{ or }2.
} \tag{2.1}
\]

No target condition `b_r=0` is required.

#### Proof, Step 1: the Franel polynomial is the Hasse invariant of the exact elliptic family

Use the elliptic curve from `toric_fiber_sym2.tex`

\[
E_x:\quad Y^2+(1-2x)XY+x^2Y=X^3. \tag{2.2}
\]

Put `e=(p-1)/2`.  Completing the square gives

\[
(2Y+(1-2x)X+x^2)^2
=4X^3+(1-2x)^2X^2+2(1-2x)x^2X+x^4. \tag{2.3}
\]

For an odd prime, the normalized Hasse invariant is the coefficient of `X^{p-1}` in the `e`th power of the cubic on the right.  After `X=xZ`, the cubic factors as

\[
4X^3+(1-2x)^2X^2+2(1-2x)x^2X+x^4
=x^2(Z+x)\bigl(Z+x(2Z-1)^2\bigr). \tag{2.4}
\]

Therefore the coefficient of `x^n` in the Hasse polynomial is

\[
(-1)^n2^n
\sum_{a+b=n}\binom ea\binom eb\binom{2b}{n}. \tag{2.5}
\]

For `0<=j<=e`,

\[
\binom ej\equiv (-1)^j4^{-j}\binom{2j}{j}\pmod p.
\]

Substitution into (2.5), followed by the terminating Franel--Vandermonde convolution, gives

\[
[x^n]\operatorname{Ha}_p(E_x)
=2^{-n}\sum_{b=0}^n
 \binom{2(n-b)}{n-b}\binom{2b}{b}\binom{2b}{n}
=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^3=f_n. \tag{2.6}
\]

Hence the Hasse invariant is exactly

\[
\operatorname{Ha}_p(E_x)=K_p(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}f_nx^n. \tag{2.7}
\]

At a smooth parameter `x`, `K_p(x)=0` is therefore equivalent to `E_x` being supersingular.

#### Step 2: `x` is the coarse `X_0(6)` Hauptmodul

The same checked file gives the exact point

of order six, with

## 3. Frobenius-orbit factorization of the critical-value resultant

Factor

\[
B=\prod_{g\in\mathcal G_p}g
\]

into monic irreducibles over `F_p`.  By Theorem 4,

\[
\deg g\in\{1,2\}. \tag{3.1}
\]

Because `B(0)=1`, every `g(0)` is nonzero, so `T` is a unit modulo every `g`.

For `e_g=deg g`, define the **Cartier orbit-value polynomial**

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{g,r}(Y)
:=\frac{(-1)^{e_g}\operatorname{Res}_T
 (g(T),Q_{p,r}(T)-YT^r)}
 {\operatorname{Res}_T(g(T),T^r)}.
} \tag{3.2}
\]

The denominator is a nonzero element of `F_p`.  If the roots of `g` are `alpha`, then

\[
\Phi_{g,r}(Y)
=\prod_{g(\alpha)=0}(Y-H(\alpha)), \tag{3.3}
\]

so `Phi_{g,r}` is monic of degree `e_g<=2`.

If `d=deg B`, multiplicativity of the resultant gives the exact global factorization

\[
\boxed{
\frac{(-1)^d\operatorname{Res}_T(B,Q-YT^r)}
 {\operatorname{Res}_T(B,T^r)}
=\prod_{g\mid B}\Phi_{g,r}(Y).
} \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the packet polynomial is a product of linear and quadratic Frobenius-orbit factors.

### Explicit quadratic formula

Let

\[
g(T)=T^2-sT+n
\]

be irreducible quadratic.  Reduce `H` in the two-dimensional algebra `F_p[T]/(g)`:

\[
H(T)=T^{-r}Q(T)\equiv u_gT+v_g\pmod g. \tag{3.5}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{g,r}(Y)
=Y^2-(u_gs+2v_g)Y
 +(u_g^2n+u_gv_gs+v_g^2).
} \tag{3.6}
\]

Moreover

\[
\operatorname{disc}_Y\Phi_{g,r}
=u_g^2(s^2-4n). \tag{3.7}
\]

Since `g` is irreducible, `s^2-4n` is a nonsquare.

Therefore exactly one of the following occurs:

* `u_g=0`: both conjugate roots of `g` have the same value `v_g in F_p`, and
  \[
  \Phi_{g,r}=(Y-v_g)^2;
  \]
* `u_g!=0`: `Phi_{g,r}` is irreducible quadratic, and the two roots of `g` have two distinct conjugate values `y,y^p in F_{p^2}\setminus F_p`.

For a linear factor `g=T-a`,

\[
\Phi_{g,r}=Y-H(a),\qquad H(a)\in F_p. \tag{3.8}
\]

This gives a complete orbit-by-orbit classification of possible packet contributions.

## 4. Exact multiplicity-four criterion

Because every `H(alpha)` lies in `F_{p^2}`, there are only two cases.

### 4.1 Non-rational packet values

Suppose

\[
y\in F_{p^2}\setminus F_p.
\]

A linear factor cannot contribute.  A quadratic collapsed factor (`u_g=0`) cannot contribute either.  Each contributing irreducible quadratic contributes exactly one root to `y` and its conjugate root to `y^p`.

For two noncollapsed quadratic factors `g,h`, define the **Cartier orbit-collision resultant**

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C^{(r)}_{g,h}
:=\operatorname{Res}_Y(\Phi_{g,r}(Y),\Phi_{h,r}(Y)).
} \tag{4.1}
\]

Since both polynomials are monic irreducible quadratics,

\[
\mathcal C^{(r)}_{g,h}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi_{g,r}=\Phi_{h,r}. \tag{4.2}
\]

Writing

\[
\Phi_g=Y^2-A_gY+B_g,
\qquad
\Phi_h=Y^2-A_hY+B_h,
\]

one may evaluate the resultant without any large determinant:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C^{(r)}_{g,h}
=(B_h-B_g)^2
 +A_g(A_g-A_h)(B_h-B_g)
 +B_g(A_g-A_h)^2.
} \tag{4.3}
\]

This is the promised fixed low-degree obstruction.

### Theorem 5 (non-rational four-packet criterion)

There is a critical value

\[
y\in F_{p^2}\setminus F_p
\]

with at least four roots of `B` above it if and only if there exist four distinct irreducible quadratic factors

\[
g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4\mid B
\]

with `u_{g_i}!=0` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C^{(r)}_{g_1,g_i}=0,
\qquad i=2,3,4.
} \tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

If four roots `alpha_i` have value `y`, each lies in a different quadratic Frobenius orbit, since its conjugate has value `y^p != y`.  For the corresponding factors `g_i`, every `Phi_{g_i}` is the minimal polynomial `(Y-y)(Y-y^p)`, so (4.4) holds.

Conversely, (4.4) says all four monic irreducible quadratics `Phi_{g_i}` are equal.  Choose either root `y` of this common polynomial.  Each `g_i` has exactly one root mapping to `y`, giving four distinct roots of `B` over `y`.  ∎

So an actual non-rational multiplicity-`4` counterexample is equivalent to a **four-clique in the graph whose edges are the zeroes of the explicit quadratic resultant (4.3)**.  Proving the cap `<=3` on this side is exactly the statement that this graph has no `K_4` for the actual Apéry data.

### 4.2 Rational packet values

Now let `y in F_p`.  A contributing linear orbit has weight `1`.  A contributing irreducible quadratic must be collapsed (`u_g=0`) and then has weight `2`.  Hence any rational packet of size at least four contains a subcollection `J` of orbit factors of total degree exactly four.

Put

\[
F_J(Y)=\prod_{g\in J}\Phi_{g,r}(Y)
=Y^4+c_3Y^3+c_2Y^2+c_1Y+c_0. \tag{4.5}
\]

### Theorem 6 (rational four-packet criterion)

A rational critical value has multiplicity at least four if and only if there is a total-degree-four subcollection `J` for which

\[
F_J(Y)=(Y-y)^4
\quad\text{for some }y\in F_p. \tag{4.6}
\]

For `p>=5`, this is equivalent to the three fixed quartic covariants

\[
\boxed{8c_2-3c_3^2=0,} \tag{4.7}
\]

\[
\boxed{16c_1-c_3^3=0,} \tag{4.8}
\]

\[
\boxed{256c_0-c_3^4=0.} \tag{4.9}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\deg\gcd(F_J,F_J')=3.} \tag{4.10}
\]

Thus the first three principal subresultants

\[
\operatorname{SRes}_0(F_J,F_J'),\quad
\operatorname{SRes}_1(F_J,F_J'),\quad
\operatorname{SRes}_2(F_J,F_J')
\]

all vanish.  I will call this the **Cartier quartic packet subresultant condition**.

#### Proof

If a rational packet has size at least four, its contributing orbit weights are `1` and `2`, so one can select total weight exactly four.  Every selected orbit-value polynomial is then a power of `Y-y`, and their degree-four product is `(Y-y)^4`.

Conversely (4.6) forces every factor of `F_J` to have the unique root `y`, so all roots represented by `J` lie over `y`.

Expanding `(Y-y)^4` and eliminating `y=-c_3/4` gives (4.7)--(4.9).  Since `p>=5`, differentiation of a quartic is nonzero and `deg gcd(F_J,F_J')=3` is equivalent to `F_J` having exactly one distinct root, hence to (4.6).  ∎

Theorems 5 and 6 are completely independent of Hurwitz/passport arguments.  They use the special Apéry fact from Theorem 4 that all Frobenius orbits have degree at most two.

## 5. An additional four-packet obstruction from the actual Apéry ODE

The orbit resultants are the smallest `F_p`-level test.  The second-order ODE adds a stronger necessary condition on any candidate packet itself.

Let `k` be `F_p` for a rational packet and `F_{p^2}` for a non-rational packet.  Suppose four distinct roots

\[
\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_4
\]

of `B` have a common value `y in k`, and set

\[
S(T)=\prod_{i=1}^4(T-\alpha_i)\in k[T],
\qquad
U=B/S,
\qquad
C_y=Q-yT^r. \tag{5.1}
\]

For non-rational `y`, Theorem 4 ensures all `alpha_i in F_{p^2}`, so indeed `S in F_{p^2}[T]`.

By Corollary 3, every selected root is an exact triple root of `C_y`, hence

\[
\boxed{S^3\mid C_y.} \tag{5.2}
\]

Write

\[
C_y=S^3V. \tag{5.3}
\]

Exact cubic contact also gives `gcd(S,V)=1`.  Dividing (1.1) by `S^2` yields the polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{
3TS'V+S(TV'-rV)=WU^2.
} \tag{5.4}
\]

Reducing the exact second-order ODE (1.3) for `B=SU` modulo `S` gives

\[
\boxed{
2a_2S'U'+(a_2S''+a_{1,\varepsilon}S')U\equiv0\pmod S.
} \tag{5.5}
\]

By Proposition 2 and squarefreeness, `T`, `Delta`, `S'`, `U`, and `V` are all units modulo `S`.  Hence

\[
\frac{U'}U
\equiv-rac12\left(\frac{S''}{S'}+
\frac{a_{1,\varepsilon}}{a_2}\right)\pmod S. \tag{5.6}
\]

Differentiate (5.4), reduce modulo `S`, and use (5.4) and (5.6).  One obtains the explicit logarithmic four-jet congruence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{V'}V
\equiv
\frac34\left(
 \frac{W'}W-\frac{a_{1,\varepsilon}}{a_2}
 -2\frac{S''}{S'}-\frac{3-r}{3T}
\right)\pmod S.
} \tag{5.7}
\]

All denominators are legitimate for `p>=5` by Proposition 2.

After clearing them, define the **Cartier--Apéry four-jet obstruction**

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak J_{\varepsilon,r}(S,V):={}&
4a_2WT S'V'
-3a_2T S'W'V
+3a_{1,\varepsilon}WT S'V\\
&+6a_2WT S''V
+(3-r)a_2W S'V.
\end{aligned} \tag{5.8}
\]

Then every four-packet necessarily satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rem}_S\mathfrak J_{\varepsilon,r}(S,V)=0.
} \tag{5.9}
\]

The remainder has degree `<4`.  Thus, once Theorem 5 or 6 produces a candidate degree-four packet divisor `S`, the full order-`p` problem collapses to a degree-four polynomial congruence with the **actual Apéry coefficients** `a_2,a_1` displayed in Proposition 1.

This is stronger than merely checking that four values of `H` coincide: (5.2) records the exact cubic Cartier contact, while (5.5)--(5.9) force that contact to be compatible with the second-order Apéry square-root equation.

A_i=u_is_i+2v_i,
\qquad
B_i=u_i^2n_i+u_iv_is_i+v_i^2.
\]

Then a non-rational four-packet exists **iff** four indices with `u_i != 0` satisfy

\[
(A_1,B_1)=(A_2,B_2)=(A_3,B_3)=(A_4,B_4), \tag{6.1}
\]

or equivalently the three scalar resultants (4.3) vanish.  There is no growing-degree polynomial left in `Y`.

A proof of `packet <=3` on this side is therefore exactly a no-fourfold-repetition theorem for the trace/norm pair `(A_i,B_i)` attached to the supersingular Frobenius orbits.

### Rational target

For every linear factor use its single value `H(a)`; for every irreducible quadratic use it only when `u_g=0`, in which case its value is `v_g` with weight two.  A four-packet exists **iff** some total weight-four choice satisfies the quartic subresultant equations (4.7)--(4.9).  Again the test has fixed degree, independent of `p`.

For either case, a candidate degree-four `S` must then satisfy the ODE obstruction (5.9).  This is the point at which I would attack nonvanishing next: it is no longer a global Hurwitz problem or a degree-`~p` resultant, but a compatibility problem between a quartic divisor and the explicit rational connection

\[
\frac{B''}{B'}=-\frac{a_{1,\varepsilon}}{a_2}
\quad\text{at roots of }B, \tag{6.2}
\]

together with the cubic primitive jet (5.7).

## 7. Characteristic and endpoint audit

All divisions used above are accounted for.

## 8. Final status

What is now proved unconditionally is stronger than the finite factorization observation:

\[
\boxed{
\text{every }B_p\text{-root is }F_{p^2}\text{-rational for every }p>=5.
}
\]

Together with the exact ODE this gives

\[
\boxed{
\text{every critical contact is ordinary and exactly cubic.}
}
\]

It follows that multiplicity `>=4` is **not** a question about arbitrary ramification.  It is exactly a repeated-value problem among degree-one/two supersingular Frobenius orbits.  The sharpest fixed-degree reduction I can prove is:

* non-rational multiplicity `>=4` iff three explicit **quadratic orbit-collision resultants** (4.3) vanish for four distinct quadratic factors;
* rational multiplicity `>=4` iff a degree-four **Cartier quartic packet subresultant** vanishes to order three, equivalently (4.7)--(4.9);
* in either case the resulting quartic packet divisor must also satisfy the explicit **Cartier--Apéry four-jet ODE obstruction** (5.9).

I therefore do **not** claim `packet <=3` as a theorem.  The single missing theorem is now very concrete: rule out a degree-four `S` satisfying the relevant orbit-value condition and (5.2), (5.5), equivalently the cleared quartic congruence (5.9), for the two explicit coefficient triples `(a_2,a_{1,epsilon},a_{0,epsilon})` above.  The supplied scan through `p<=2000`, including genuine triples but no quadruple, is finite evidence that this nonvanishing statement may be true; it is not used in the proof.
