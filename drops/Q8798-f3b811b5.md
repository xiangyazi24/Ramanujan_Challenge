# Constructing a non-isotrivial Apéry motive over a function field

## Executive conclusion

The literal replacement of the Apéry sequence by a sequence over F_q(t) is not the correct construction. The correct function-field analogue is a non-isotrivial family of Apéry motives whose Frobenius variation is controlled by a parameter variety. The best model is not the one-parameter pencil alone, but a parameterized family of Picard-Fuchs systems where the geometric parameter plays the role of the varying prime.

## 1. The K3 pencil construction

The family X_a defined by

Λ(x,y,z)=a

is a genuine non-isotrivial K3 family over the a-line away from singular fibers. Its transcendental cohomology gives a l-adic local system. The Taylor coefficients of the Picard-Fuchs period at a chosen regular point recover the Apéry recurrence.

This is the correct geometric object. However, the coefficients b_d themselves are constants after reduction modulo a finite field. They are not elements of F_q(t), so they should not be interpreted as a recurrence over the function field itself.

The function-field analogue should instead study Frobenius variation of the family.

## 2. Better parameter family

The proposed s-parameter deformation

(d+1)^3 y_{d+1}=(2d+1)(s d^2+s d+5)y_d-d^3y_{d-1}

is closer to a function-field model because s becomes a geometric parameter. For each s, one obtains a fiber motive and a zero set varying with s.

The advantage is that values of s are points of a curve over F_q. Deligne equidistribution applies to Frobenius conjugacy classes as s varies.

The remaining requirement is that this deformation must come from an actual algebraic family of motives, not only a formal recurrence. A Picard-Fuchs realization is needed.

## 3. Correct function-field GCD analogue

A workable statement is:

Let U/F_q be the parameter curve of a non-isotrivial Apéry motive and let B_n(u) denote the nth period coefficient in the fiber over u. Define

G_N(u)=gcd(D_N A_N(u),D_N B_N(u)).

Then prove that for every epsilon>0,

#{u in U(F_q): log G_N(u)>epsilon N}=o(q)

as q tends to infinity.

A stronger geometric statement is a moment estimate:

sum_{u in U(F_q)} |sum_{P} 1_{Z_P(u)}|^k \ll q

for fixed k.

This is provable in principle from Deligne bounds once geometric monodromy is sufficiently large.

## 4. Monodromy

The expected transcendental rank-two piece has symplectic determinant pairing, so the generic geometric group is expected to be

Sp_2 = SL_2.

For the Apéry K3 family this is consistent with known Picard-Fuchs monodromy calculations and the fact that the family is non-isotrivial. A rigorous proof requires identifying the actual transcendental sheaf and verifying irreducibility plus absence of extra endomorphisms.

## 5. What Deligne gives

For a non-isotrivial l-adic local system with SL_2 monodromy, Frobenius elements at parameter points equidistribute in SL_2. Consequently, incidence conditions such as

Frob_u v_0 in a fixed line

have square-root error:

#{u:Frob_u in V}=mu(V)q+O(q^{1/2}).

This is exactly the cross-parameter independence missing in the number-field problem.

## 6. Singular fibers

The singular fibers occur at the discriminant locus of the Laurent polynomial pencil. They must be computed from the discriminant of Λ-a. The precise values depend on normalization of the parameter a; they are not obtained merely from the recurrence constant 17.

For q=7, one can compute the reduced recurrence coefficients directly:

b_0=1,

b_1=5,

b_2=73=3 mod 7,

b_3=1445=4 mod 7,

b_4=33001=5 mod 7,

b_5=819005=2 mod 7,

b_6=21460825=6 mod 7.

These values verify the reduction of the classical sequence, but they are not themselves the function-field construction.

## Final verdict

The publishable function-field theorem should not be stated as an Apéry recurrence over F_q(t). The correct object is:

1. Construct an algebraic non-isotrivial Apéry motive over a parameter curve U/F_q.
2. Prove geometric monodromy SL_2 (or the appropriate large subgroup).
3. Translate gcd divisibility into Frobenius incidence conditions.
4. Apply Deligne/Katz equidistribution to obtain the analogue of LGTQ without the number-field moving-target obstruction.

The two-variable deformation is the most promising explicit route because it turns the missing cross-prime independence into ordinary geometric independence of Frobenius across parameter values.
