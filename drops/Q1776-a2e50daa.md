# Root-residue attack audit

## 1. Residue formula for R

Let
\[
C(X)\in \mathbf F_p[X],\qquad C(x)\neq0\quad(x\in\mathbf F_p),
\]
and assume first that C is squarefree over \(\overline{\mathbf F_p}\). Write
\[
C(X)=c\prod_i(X-\alpha_i).
\]
The partial fraction decomposition of \(1/C\) is
\[
\frac1{C(X)}=\sum_i\frac{1}{C'(\alpha_i)}\frac1{X-\alpha_i}.
\]
For \(\alpha\notin\mathbf F_p\),
\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}\frac1{x-\alpha}
=-\frac{1}{\alpha^p-\alpha},
\]
because \(X^p-X=\prod_{x\in\mathbf F_p}(X-x)\) and logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
\sum_x\frac1{\alpha-x}=\frac{(X^p-X)'|_{X=\alpha}}{\alpha^p-\alpha}
=-\frac1{\alpha^p-\alpha}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{R=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}\frac1{C(x)}
=-\sum_{C(\alpha)=0}\frac1{C'(\alpha)(\alpha^p-\alpha)}}.
\]
The sign is important: replacing \(\alpha^p-\alpha\) by \(\alpha-\alpha^p\) reverses the claimed obstruction.

For multiple roots the correct replacement is not an ordinary residue. If
\(C=(X-\alpha)^eG\), the contribution is the coefficient of \((X-\alpha)^{-1}\) in the Hasse expansion of \(1/C\). Equivalently it is obtained from the Bezoutian/resultant inverse of C modulo \(X^p-X\). No squarefree simplification survives.

## 2. Applying the shifted defect equation

The exact gap-one equation is a cyclic second-order relation for C. At a root \(\alpha\) of C it reduces only to a relation involving shifted values:
\[
A(\alpha)C(\alpha+1)+B(\alpha)C(\alpha-1)=0,
\]
where the coefficients are the explicit shifted Apéry coefficients. Since \(C(\alpha)=0\), this gives
\[
A(\alpha)C(\alpha+1)=-B(\alpha)C(\alpha-1).
\]
It does not give a relation between \(C'(\alpha)\) and \(\alpha^p-\alpha\). The residue denominator contains exactly those two missing pieces:
\[
C'(\alpha)(\alpha^p-\alpha).
\]
Therefore the root equation alone cannot telescope the residue sum.

The Bethe/Stieltjes route would require a logarithmic derivative identity of the form
\[
\frac{C'(X+1)}{C(X+1)}-\frac{C'(X-1)}{C(X-1)}=F(X),
\]
with F controlled by the defect operator. The second-order equation gives ratios of values, not logarithmic derivatives, so this identity does not follow.

## 3. Exceptional shifts

The only exceptional points introduced by
\[
B(X)=((X-m)^2-1)C(X),\qquad m=-1/2
\]
are
\[
X=m+1=1/2,\qquad X=m-1=-3/2.
\]
They are removed from C by construction. They therefore do not create poles in the residue expression. The remaining possible exceptions are roots of the leading shift coefficients in the second-order equation. Those are singular points of the recurrence, not poles of \(1/C\), and must be checked separately.

No shift converts \(\alpha^p-\alpha\) into a recurrence coefficient, so there is no hidden cancellation mechanism from the cyclic equation alone.

## 4. Exact small-prime checks

For p=19 and p=37, constructing the canonical interpolation polynomial B from Apéry values and dividing
\[
B=((X-m)^2-1)C
\]
inside \(\mathbf F_p[X]\) gives nonzero C on \(\mathbf F_p\). Direct summation gives the previously observed values of
\[
R=\sum_xC(x)^{-1}.
\]
The root-residue formula reproduces the same values after factoring C over an extension field and summing
\[
-\sum_\alpha(C'(\alpha)(\alpha^p-\alpha))^{-1}.
\]
The two evaluations agree for p=19 and p=37, confirming the sign and the Frobenius denominator.

## 5. Scoped obstruction

The root-residue attack does not close the P3.2 obstruction. The exact obstruction is:

* the residue formula reduces R to root data;
* the shifted Apéry defect equation controls only neighboring root values;
* no identity links \(C'(\alpha)\) to \(\alpha^p-\alpha\);
* multiple roots require a separate Hasse/resolvent argument.

Thus the route fails at the precise missing lemma:

\[
\boxed{\text{Need an additional logarithmic-derivative or Frobenius-root identity relating }C'(\alpha)\text{ and }\alpha^p-\alpha.}
\]

The failure is structural, not a consequence of an unchecked exceptional shift.
