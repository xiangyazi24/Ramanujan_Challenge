# P3.2 — Ostafe–Shparlinski orbit sums for the Apéry recurrence

## Executive verdict

There is **no direct Ostafe–Shparlinski black box** that gives a power saving for

\[
S(a,p)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}e_p(a b_j),\qquad a\ne0.
\]

The right diagnosis is more precise than simply “their results are autonomous and the Apéry system is non-autonomous”:

1. Some Ostafe–Shparlinski work **does** permit a different polynomial map at each step, but only inside a very special, uniformly triangular family with controlled leading monomials; the strongest varying-map conclusions are also naturally averaged over initial states.
2. The Apéry recurrence is a rational \(\mathrm{GL}_2\)-**cocycle over translation** \(n\mapsto n+1\), not powers of one fixed matrix or one fixed algebraic-group endomorphism.
3. Adjoining the clock makes the system formally autonomous, and a factorial rescaling even gives a polynomial autonomous lift. This removes the superficial “non-autonomous” objection, but it does **not** create the stationary group structure used in matrix-power/orbit theorems.
4. The product of all Möbius steps only records an endpoint. The sum \(S(a,p)\) depends on every prefix product and on the affine scale that projectivization discards.
5. The degree-three one-step recurrence does not make \(n\mapsto b_n\) a bounded-degree rational function. The useful complexity statement is instead that an \(h\)-step transfer has degree \(O(h)\); the repository makes this exact through continuants of degree \(3h\). This is useful for **short-shift correlations**, but not for a one-shot Weil bound over all \(p\) indices.

The most realistic orbit-based route is therefore:

> prove square-root cancellation, with conductor growing only polynomially in the shift \(h\), for the correlations
> \[
> C_h(a;p)=\sum_n e_p\!\bigl(a(b_{n+h}-b_n)\bigr),
> \]
> and then use van der Corput differencing.

A second, more Ostafe–Shparlinski-like formulation is to prove additive-energy and directional Fourier-decay estimates for the set of **dual prefix products**. Both are genuinely new Apéry-specific inputs; neither follows from the published fixed-map theorems.

For the weaker goal of bounding \(|Z_p|=\#\{j:b_j=0\}\), the repository’s existing projective-energy program (E1/GPRV/ATR in `problems/3.2/FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex`) is more directly aligned than uniform ACSOP. The repository already has the unconditional pointwise bound \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\), so an ACSOP estimate \(p^{1-\delta}\) improves the current exponent only if \(\delta>1/3\), although any \(\delta>0\) would still be a genuine Fourier theorem.

## 0. Indexing correction and the exact cocycle

Write

\[
P(n)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)
     =34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]

For \(n\ge1\), put

\[
w_n=\binom{b_{n-1}}{b_n},\qquad
A(n)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\[2mm]
-\dfrac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}&\dfrac{P(n)}{(n+1)^3}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
w_{n+1}=A(n)w_n,\qquad
\det A(n)=\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^3.
\]

Thus the natural initial state is

\[
w_1=(b_0,b_1)^T=(1,5)^T,
\]

and, for \(j\ge2\),

\[
w_j=A(j-1)\cdots A(1)w_1.
\]

So applying \(F_0\) to \((1,5)\) is not the right indexing: it gives \((5,25)\), whereas \((b_1,b_2)=(5,73)\). The transition from \((b_0,b_1)\) to \((b_1,b_2)\) is \(F_1\).

The clock-augmented map

\[
\Phi(n,u,v)=\left(n+1,v,\frac{P(n)v-n^3u}{(n+1)^3}\right)
\]

can nevertheless be started at \((0,1,1)\). The first coordinate \(u=1\) is merely a dummy at time zero because the coefficient \(n^3\) vanishes there. One has

\[
\Phi^j(0,1,1)=(j,b_{j-1},b_j)\qquad (j\ge1),
\]

while the third coordinate at \(j=0\) is already \(b_0=1\). No value of \(b_{-1}\) is being defined.

The map is regular along the required segment \(0\le n\le p-2\); applying it once more at \(n=p-1=-1\) would encounter its pole. The target sum stops before that update.

### A polynomial autonomous lift

The denominator is not the fundamental obstruction. Define

\[
d_n=(n!)^3b_n,\qquad q_n=(n!)^3.
\]

Then

\[
d_{n+1}=P(n)d_n-n^6d_{n-1},\qquad
q_{n+1}=(n+1)^3q_n,\qquad b_n=d_n/q_n
\]

for \(0\le n<p\). Hence the four-dimensional map

\[
\Psi(n,q,u,v)=igl(n+1,(n+1)^3q,v,P(n)v-n^6u\bigr)
\]

is a fixed polynomial map, and along the orbit the observable is the rational function \(v/q\). At time \(p\), \(q_p=0\), but \(q_n\ne0\) for every term \(0\le n<p\) in the sum.

This lift is useful conceptually: the real issue is not the presence of denominators or the word “non-autonomous.” The issue is the nonconstant companion block and the fixed exceptional initial orbit.

## 1. What extends from Ostafe–Shparlinski, and what fails?

### 1.1 Bibliographic clarification

I could not match the title/journal combination quoted in the question (“Exponential sums over orbits of explicit endomorphisms on algebraic groups,” *Compositio* 2022) to an Ostafe–Shparlinski paper.

The closest 2022 publication relevant to the stated mechanism is Ostafe–Shparlinski–Voloch,

> **Equations and Character Sums with Matrix Powers, Kloosterman Sums over Small Subgroups, and Quantum Ergodicity**, *International Mathematics Research Notices* 2023, no. 16, 14196–14238, published online 18 August 2022.

That paper studies \(A^x\) for **one fixed matrix** \(A\) of finite order. Its additive-energy equations have the form

\[
A^{x_1}+\cdots+A^{x_\nu}
 =A^{x_{\nu+1}}+\cdots+A^{x_{2\nu}}.
\]

There is also an earlier and directly relevant Ostafe–Shparlinski paper,

> **Pseudorandom Numbers and Hash Functions from Iterations of Multivariate Polynomials**, arXiv:0908.4519,

which explicitly allows different maps at different steps. However, all maps must belong to a fixed special triangular class

\[
F_i=X_iG_i(X_{i+1},\ldots,X_m)+H_i(X_{i+1},\ldots,X_m),
\]

with a common upper-triangular exponent matrix and unique leading monomials. Its varying-map permutation results yield discrepancy bounds for almost all initial states. Thus “Ostafe–Shparlinski only treats autonomous maps” is too broad, but the available non-autonomous theorem still does not cover the Apéry cocycle.

### 1.2 The stationary identity that is lost

For one fixed map \(f\),

\[
f^{n+h}=f^h\circ f^n.
\]

After one Weyl/van der Corput differencing step, a phase \(g(f^n(P))\) produces

\[
g(f^{n+h}(P))-g(f^n(P))
   =\bigl(g\circ f^h-g\bigr)(f^n(P)).
\]

For each fixed \(h\), this is one fixed algebraic observable evaluated on the same orbit. Degree growth, exceptional invariant subvarieties, character-lattice actions, eigenvalue orders, and additive relations can all be studied through the single iterate \(f^h\).

For a genuinely varying sequence \(F_0,F_1,\ldots\), the lag-\(h\) map is

\[
F_{n+h-1}\circ\cdots\circ F_n,
\]

which depends on \(n\). There is no common semigroup power, no fixed eigenvalue/order, and no fixed character action. In a general system, both the degree and the bad locus of the lag map can vary uncontrollably with \(n\) and \(h\).

Adjoining the clock restores the formal identity

\[
\Phi^{n+h}=\Phi^h\circ\Phi^n,
\]

but it does not restore the missing **group structure**. The degree and polar divisor of \(\Phi^h\) still grow with \(h\), and the fiber action is a cocycle rather than a power of one element.

### 1.3 The fixed-seed obstruction in an exact formula

For the Apéry cocycle, define

\[
T_1=I,\qquad
T_j=A(j-1)\cdots A(1)\quad (2\le j\le p-1),
\]

and the row vectors

\[
\lambda_j=e_2^TT_j\in\mathbb F_p^2.
\]

Then

\[
b_j=\lambda_j\cdot(1,5)^T.
\]

For a general seed \(w\in\mathbb F_p^2\), set

\[
S_w(a)=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}e_p\bigl(a\lambda_j\cdot w\bigr).
\]

Orthogonality gives the exact identity

\[
\sum_{w\in\mathbb F_p^2}|S_w(a)|^2
 =p^2\#\{(j,k):\lambda_j=\lambda_k\}.
\]

More generally,

\[
\sum_w|S_w(a)|^{2\nu}
 =p^2\#\left\{j_1,\ldots,j_{2\nu}:
 \sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\lambda_{j_i}
 =\sum_{i=\nu+1}^{2\nu}\lambda_{j_i}\right\}.
\]

This explains both the relevance and the limitation of the published methods:

* averaged over seeds, square-root behavior follows from low collision/additive energy of the dual prefix set \(\Lambda_p=\{\lambda_j\}\);
* the project needs the single seed \(w=(1,5)\);
* in the fixed-matrix setting, \(\lambda_j=\lambda A^j\), so the displayed energy equations are matrix-power equations of exactly the type treated by Ostafe–Shparlinski–Voloch;
* here \(\lambda_j=e_2^TA(j-1)\cdots A(1)\), a time-ordered nonstationary product with no cyclic subgroup behind it.

This is the key obstruction in its cleanest form. A theorem saying “almost every seed is good” does not exclude \((1,5)\) from the exceptional set for infinitely many primes.

This row-prefix construction should not be confused with the false propagation rule warned about in `problems/3.2/UNDERSTANDING.md`: it is a row of the **full transfer matrix from time 1 to time \(j\)**, not a claim that the two-solution row \((b_j,c_j)\) is propagated by the one-solution companion matrix.

### Answer to Question 1

So the answer is:

* **Abstract extension:** yes, any clocked system is autonomous, and some Ostafe–Shparlinski arguments already allow varying maps in special triangular families.
* **Black-box extension to the Apéry recurrence:** no.
* **Key obstruction:** absence of a stationary power/cyclic-group structure together with the need to control one fixed seed. Equivalently, one must control additive relations among the nonstationary prefix products \(A(j-1)\cdots A(1)\), not among powers \(A^j\) of one matrix.

## 2. Does the autonomous embedding fit an algebraic group?

### 2.1 What structure it actually has

The correct object is the skew product

\[
(n,w)\longmapsto(n+1,A(n)w),
\]

or, in difference-algebra language, the rank-two system

\[
\sigma(w)=A(n)w,\qquad \sigma(n)=n+1.
\]

It is a rational \(\mathrm{GL}_2\)-cocycle over the additive translation. The base translation by itself is harmless: it can be represented as powers of a fixed unipotent element. The obstruction is the fiber cocycle \(A(n)\).

The map \(n\mapsto n+1\) is a translation of \(\mathbb G_a\), not a group endomorphism of \(\mathbb G_a\) because it does not preserve the identity. More importantly, the full map is not a homomorphism for the direct-product group law, and the polynomial lift \(\Psi\) is still not a group homomorphism.

The literal three-dimensional \(\Phi\) is not a regular endomorphism of all of \(\mathbb A^3\) because of the divisor \(n=-1\). The polynomial lift shows that this pole can be moved into the rational observable and is therefore not the decisive issue. The decisive issue is still that the companion matrices vary with \(n\).

### 2.2 The precise “untwisting” test

The Apéry cocycle would reduce to fixed-matrix dynamics if one could find a rational gauge \(G(n)\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p(n))\), of degree bounded independently of \(p\), and a constant matrix \(A_0\) such that

\[
G(n+1)^{-1}A(n)G(n)=A_0.
\]

Then, with \(w_n=G(n)z_n\), one would have

\[
z_{n+1}=A_0z_n,
\]

and matrix-power technology would become relevant.

There is no known gauge of this kind. Over a single finite orbit one can define \(G(n)\) recursively, but interpolating those \(p\) values generally produces degree \(\asymp p\); that is a tautological finite-field encoding, not a bounded-complexity conjugacy.

The determinant is unusually simple:

\[
\det A(n)=\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^3,\qquad
\prod_{k=1}^m\det A(k)=\frac1{(m+1)^3}.
\]

Thus the determinant part is a multiplicative coboundary and can be normalized away. This does **not** make the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-part constant.

### 2.3 Relation to the repository’s projective transfer matrices

The repository uses the scaled Riccati state

\[
x_n=[n^3b_{n-1}:b_n]
\]

and the polynomial projective matrices

\[
M_u=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&(u+1)^6\\
-1&P(u)
\end{pmatrix},\qquad x_{n+1}=M_nx_n.
\]

In `problems/3.2/FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex`, the product

\[
G_m(s)=M_{s+m-1}\cdots M_s
\]

is written exactly in terms of continuants \(U_m(s)\), with

\[
\deg U_m=3m.
\]

This is real and useful algebraic structure, but it is structure of a **nonconstant cocycle**. It does not identify \(G_m(s)\) with \(M^m\) for one fixed \(M\), and it does not make the orbit an algebraic-group endomorphism orbit.

### Answer to Question 2

The Apéry recurrence does not presently fit the cited algebraic-group/matrix-power hypotheses. A bounded-degree gauge to a constant cocycle would change that assessment, but finding such a gauge would itself be a major new theorem and is not suggested by the known Apéry structure. The appropriate language is a rational difference module or skew-linear dynamical system, not an endomorphism of a fixed algebraic group.

## 3. Why the single Möbius product does not permit a Weil bound

For \(n\ge1\), whenever an affine ratio is defined, let

\[
r_n=\frac{b_n}{b_{n-1}}.
\]

Then

\[
r_{n+1}
 =\frac{P(n)r_n-n^3}{(n+1)^3r_n}
 =\frac{P(n)}{(n+1)^3}
  -\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}\frac1{r_n}.
\]

Homogeneously, this is the \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) transformation represented by

\[
R_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(n)&-n^3\\
(n+1)^3&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]

which is invertible for \(1\le n\le p-2\). The \(n=0\) step is singular, and the \(n=p-1\) update has a zero denominator, so there is no product of \(p-1\) invertible Möbius transformations taking a naturally defined \(r_0\) to \(r_p\). Starting with \(r_1=5\), the regular products determine \(r_2,\ldots,r_{p-1}\).

There are three fatal losses in replacing all prefix products by the total product.

### 3.1 Endpoint compression is not path compression

Let

\[
Q_j=R_{j-1}\cdots R_1.
\]

Then \(r_j=Q_j(r_1)\), while the total product \(Q_{p-1}\) gives only the last ratio. The sum contains all \(p\) intermediate values. Knowledge of the endpoint places essentially no constraint on the oscillation of the path of prefixes \(Q_j\).

This is exactly analogous to a word in a group whose total product is the identity: the intermediate prefix products can still trace a highly nontrivial path.

### 3.2 Projectivization loses the affine scale

Even complete knowledge of every \(r_j\) does not express \(b_j\) as a bounded-degree rational function of \(j\). Away from zeros,

\[
b_j=b_0\prod_{k=1}^j r_k,
\]

so the affine value contains an accumulated multiplicative scale. Homogeneous projective dynamics remembers the line \([b_{j-1}:b_j]\), not the normalization needed by \(e_p(ab_j)\).

At a zero, the affine ratio chart also breaks; the homogeneous \(\mathbb P^1\) dynamics remains valid, but the product formula must be restarted. This restart structure is very useful for zero gaps and is exploited in the repository, but it still does not recover an additive-character phase in \(b_j\).

### 3.3 Weil needs one bounded-complexity function

The classical Weil/Bombieri setup is a sum such as

\[
\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}e_p(f(t))
\]

for one rational function \(f\) whose degree or sheaf conductor is controlled independently of \(p\), excluding Artin–Schreier degeneracy. Here the \(j\)-th summand is obtained from a different prefix product \(Q_j\). The total matrix \(Q_{p-1}\) does not turn the family \(\{Q_j\}\) into one bounded-degree rational function of \(j\).

### Answer to Question 3

No: a Weil bound cannot be applied merely because the total composition is one Möbius transformation. To use the projective structure, one needs a theorem controlling the **whole prefix-product path**—for example additive energy, expansion, or a bounded-conductor sheaf for the family of prefixes. The repository’s continuant/return-polynomial machinery is such a path-sensitive tool for projective collisions, but it does not directly estimate \(S(a,p)\).

## 4. What is the effective algebraic complexity of \(n\mapsto b_n\)?

### 4.1 Local degree is not global degree

The recurrence has coefficients of degree three in \(n\), but it is a relation among three consecutive values. It does not provide a bounded-bidegree equation

\[
Q(n,b_n)=0
\]

for one algebraic function \(b(n)\).

A simple warning example is

\[
x_{n+1}=(n+1)x_n,\qquad x_0=1,
\]

whose solution is \(x_n=n!\). The update has degree one, but the finite-field table \(n\mapsto n!\) is not thereby a bounded-degree algebraic function, and factorial exponential sums are a genuinely difficult subject. Low-degree recurrence coefficients and low-degree interpolation are different notions.

The Apéry sequence is holonomic/P-recursive; that is not the same as being algebraic as a function of the discrete index.

### 4.2 The useful complexity is the shift complexity

For the companion matrices,

\[
w_{n+h}=A_h(n)w_n,\qquad
A_h(n)=A(n+h-1)\cdots A(n).
\]

A common denominator for \(A_h(n)\) is

\[
D_h(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{h}(n+i)^3,
\]

of degree \(3h\), and the numerator entries have degree \(O(h)\). In the repository’s scaled projective form, the exact continuant has degree \(3h\), not merely an upper bound.

Thus:

* for fixed \(h\), the lag relation has bounded complexity and one can hope for a Bombieri/Deligne estimate;
* for \(h\asymp p\), the complexity is \(\asymp p\), so the geometric constant can overwhelm the square-root saving;
* to obtain a power saving by differencing, one needs useful estimates uniformly for \(h\le p^\eta\), with conductor polynomial rather than exponential in \(h\).

This linear degree growth is the main positive feature of the orbit approach.

### 4.3 Interpolation gives only \(O(p)\) complexity

Every function \(\mathbb F_p\to\mathbb F_p\) has a unique interpolation polynomial \(\mathcal B_p(X)\) of degree at most \(p-1\) satisfying

\[
\mathcal B_p(n)=b_n\qquad(0\le n<p).
\]

This statement alone is tautological. Applying the polynomial Weil bound with degree \(p-1\) gives at best \(O(p^{3/2})\), worse than the trivial bound \(p\).

The Apéry reflection law does give a genuine compression. Since

\[
b_{p-1-n}=b_n,
\]

one has as polynomials

\[
\mathcal B_p(-1-X)=\mathcal B_p(X).
\]

For odd \(p\), the invariants of \(X\mapsto-1-X\) are generated by \(X(X+1)\), so

\[
\mathcal B_p(X)=Q_p(X(X+1)),\qquad \deg Q_p\le\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

This halves the tautological parameter count but leaves complexity linear in \(p\); it does not make a Weil bound nontrivial.

### 4.4 Encoding the whole recurrence as a variety does not fix the problem

One can introduce variables \(y_0,\ldots,y_{p-1}\) and impose the \(p-2\) cubic-coefficient recurrence equations. Each equation has low degree, but the dimension of the ambient space, the number of equations, and the relevant Betti/conductor complexity all grow with \(p\). Deligne’s theorem is uniform for a fixed-complexity family, not for an encoding whose complexity grows linearly with the field size.

Likewise, a bounded-conductor sheaf whose trace is some geometric quantity related to \(b_n\) is not automatically enough: the desired phase is

\[
e_p(a b_n),
\]

which is a nonlinear additive character of the residue value. One needs an actual Artin–Schreier/sheaf realization of this phase or of its shifted correlations, together with a proof of geometric nontriviality.

### Relation to the repository’s existing orbit result

`FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex` already extracts what fixed-degree algebraic geometry can presently give from the projective recurrence:

* the gap/return polynomial for a displacement \(h\) has degree \(3(h-1)\);
* fixed-gap root counting is therefore \(O(h)\);
* optimizing a block decomposition yields the unconditional projective orbit-energy exponent \(3/2\);
* obtaining linear energy E1 requires a **family-compatible** estimate across all gaps, not just a fixed-\(h\) degree bound.

This is the same quantifier obstruction facing ACSOP: bounded complexity for each fixed shift is not enough unless the constants remain controlled over a growing range of shifts.

### Answer to Question 4

There is no known bounded-degree algebraic function \(n\mapsto b_n\) to which a one-variable Weil bound applies. The unconditional global complexity is \(O(p)\); the meaningful local complexity is \(O(h)\) for an \(h\)-step transfer. Pure algebraic geometry can help only after one constructs a fixed-dimensional, polynomial-conductor model for short-shift phases or correlations.

## 5. The most realistic route to a power saving

### 5.1 Normalize the target correctly

The literal inequality

\[
|S(a,p)|\le p^{1-\delta}
\]

for every prime with constant one fails already at \(p=2\): \(b_0=b_1=1\pmod2\), so \(|S(1,2)|=2\). The natural theorem is

\[
|S(a,p)|\le C p^{1-\delta}
\]

for an absolute \(C\), all \(a\ne0\), and all primes, or the constant-one version for all sufficiently large primes.

The zero-indicator identity is

\[
|Z_p|
 =\frac1p\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}S(a,p)
 =1+\frac1p\sum_{a\ne0}S(a,p).
\]

Hence a uniform ACSOP bound gives

\[
|Z_p|\le1+C p^{1-\delta}.
\]

In the current repository this improves the already proved \(p^{2/3}\) pointwise exponent only when \(\delta>1/3\).

### 5.2 A concrete conditional van der Corput theorem

Define the truncated shifted correlations

\[
C_h(a;p)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1-h}
 e_p\bigl(a(b_{n+h}-b_n)\bigr).
\]

Suppose there is a constant \(A\ge0\) such that, uniformly for \(a\ne0\) and \(1\le h\le H\),

\[
|C_h(a;p)|\ll p^{1/2}h^A.
\tag{SC(A)}
\]

The van der Corput inequality gives, for \(N=p\),

\[
|S(a,p)|^2
 \le \frac{N+H-1}{H^2}
 \left(HN+2\sum_{h=1}^{H-1}(H-h)|C_h(a;p)|\right).
\]

Under \(\mathrm{SC}(A)\),

\[
|S(a,p)|^2
 \ll \frac{p^2}{H}+p^{3/2}H^A.
\]

Choosing

\[
H=p^{1/(2(A+1))}
\]

balances the two terms and yields

\[
|S(a,p)|
 \ll p^{\,1-1/(4(A+1))}.
\]

Thus any square-root correlation theorem whose conductor grows polynomially in \(h\) gives an explicit power saving

\[
\delta=\frac1{4(A+1)}.
\]

This calculation identifies the exact missing input. A theorem only for each fixed \(h\), with an uncontrolled constant, is insufficient. Exponential conductor growth in \(h\) permits only \(H\asymp\log p\) and gives at best a logarithmic saving.

### 5.3 What geometry would be required?

The repository’s continuants show that the \(h\)-step cocycle itself has degree \(O(h)\). To convert this into \(\mathrm{SC}(A)\), one still needs to place the moving state \(w_n\) inside a geometric model. A viable proof would have to provide:

1. a fixed-dimensional variety or sheaf whose trace is the phase
   \(e_p(a(b_{n+h}-b_n))\), or a transform reducing the correlation to such trace sums;
2. conductor \(O(h^A)\) for some fixed \(A\);
3. geometric irreducibility/nontriviality, excluding Artin–Schreier phases and classifying every exceptional pair \((a,h)\);
4. uniformity for \(h\le p^\eta\) and every \(a\ne0\).

The most plausible source of this additional structure is not the recurrence alone but the Apéry-specific binomial/diagonal, Picard–Fuchs, modular, and Mellin descriptions already developed elsewhere in the project. A crucial warning is that “\(b_n\) is a trace/Mellin coefficient” does not by itself make \(e_p(ab_n)\) a bounded-conductor trace function; exponentiating a trace value is nonlinear. One needs a new transform identity at the level of the additive phase or its correlations.

### 5.4 The closest Ostafe–Shparlinski-style alternative

The dual-prefix formulation suggests a separate program. Let

\[
\Lambda_p=\{\lambda_j=e_2^TA(j-1)\cdots A(1):1\le j\le p-1\}
 \subset\mathbb F_p^2.
\]

Then, up to the single \(b_0\) term,

\[
S(a,p)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_p}^{\text{with multiplicity}}
 e_p\bigl(\lambda\cdot a(1,5)^T\bigr).
\]

A power saving would follow from sufficiently strong **directional Fourier decay** of this prefix set along the line \(\{a(1,5):a\in\mathbb F_p^\times\}\). The natural intermediate targets are:

* low additive energies of \(\Lambda_p\);
* nonconcentration of \(\Lambda_p\) on affine lines and proper algebraic subsets;
* higher-energy bounds strong enough to turn average Fourier control into a maximum along the distinguished line.

This is the true analogue of the matrix-power method. The difference is that \(\Lambda_p\) is a deterministic nonstationary prefix-product path, not a cyclic matrix orbit. Merely showing that the individual matrices generate a large subgroup is insufficient: expansion of a generating set does not imply that one prescribed ordered word has equidistributed prefixes.

This route is conceivable, but it requires a new product-growth theorem for polynomial matrix cocycles. At present it is less developed than the project’s continuant/projective-energy machinery.

### 5.5 For zero counting alone, uniform ACSOP is stronger than necessary

Let

\[
m_t=\#\{0\le n<p:b_n=t\},\qquad
E_{\rm val}(p)=\sum_t m_t^2.
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}|S(a,p)|^2=pE_{\rm val}(p),
\]

and

\[
|Z_p|^2=m_0^2\le E_{\rm val}(p).
\]

Thus a power-saving **value-collision** bound can improve \(|Z_p|\) without controlling the maximum Fourier coefficient for every \(a\).

Even more directly, the repository’s projective energy

\[
\mathcal F_p=\sum_{v\in\mathbb P^1}m_p(v)^2,
\qquad \pi(n)=[b_n:c_n],
\]

contains \(|Z_p|^2\) as the contribution of the fiber \([0:1]\). The open E1 target \(\mathcal F_p\ll p\) would immediately imply

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{1/2}.
\]

This projective energy is not the same as \(E_{\rm val}(p)\), and neither is equivalent to uniform ACSOP, but both are weaker and more directly targeted ways to control zeros.

### Answer to Question 5

The ranking is:

1. **Best orbit-based ACSOP route:** short-shift correlation sheaves plus van der Corput, exploiting the proved \(O(h)\) transfer complexity.
2. **Most faithful Ostafe–Shparlinski analogue:** additive-energy/directional Fourier decay for the dual prefix-product set \(\Lambda_p\); this requires a new theorem for nonstationary matrix cocycles.
3. **Best route if only \(|Z_p|\) matters:** continue the repository’s E1/GPRV/ATR projective-energy program or prove a value-collision bound; uniform ACSOP is stronger than necessary.
4. **Low-priority route:** search for a bounded-degree gauge reducing \(A(n)\) to a constant matrix. This would be decisive, but there is currently no evidence that such an untwisting exists.

## Condensed answers to the five questions

1. **Non-autonomous extension?** Special extensions exist, so non-autonomy alone is not fatal. They require uniform triangular/degree structure and often give almost-all-seed results. The Apéry obstruction is the nonstationary prefix-product cocycle and the fixed seed \((1,5)\).

2. **Algebraic-group structure?** No known one. The clocked map is a rational skew product; after factorial rescaling it is polynomial, but still not a group endomorphism. A bounded-degree gauge to a constant cocycle would be sufficient and is presently unavailable.

3. **Single total Möbius product plus Weil?** No. The total product gives one endpoint, while \(S(a,p)\) depends on every prefix and on affine scale. Projectivization is effective for zero gaps and collision energy, not directly for the additive phase \(e_p(ab_j)\).

4. **Pure Bombieri/Deligne from the degree-three recurrence?** Not directly. The global interpolation complexity is \(O(p)\), so the standard Weil bound is trivial. The useful fact is linear \(O(h)\) complexity for an \(h\)-step transfer; this can support a short-shift correlation argument if one constructs a polynomial-conductor sheaf.

5. **Most realistic power-saving path?** Prove \(|C_h(a;p)|\ll p^{1/2}h^A\) uniformly for a power-sized range of \(h\), then apply van der Corput. For zero counting alone, the current projective-energy route is more economical and would give \(p^{1/2}\) under E1.
