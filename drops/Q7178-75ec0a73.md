# P32 SWR R3 correction: fail-closed order lemma

## 1. Retraction of the previous inequality

The inequality claimed in Q7170

\[
d\geq \frac{p-1-s}{2}
\]

for the distance of an off-center consecutive four-zero window from the reflection center

\[
m=(p-1)/2
\]

is false and is explicitly retracted.

The counterexample already cited is

\[
99<868<1011<1294\subset \mathbf F_{3727},
\]

with

\[
s=1294-99=1195.
\]

The center is

\[
m=1863,
\]

and the distance of the window from the center is

\[
d=1863-1294=569.
\]

But

\[
(p-1-s)/2=(3726-1195)/2=1265.5,
\]

so the proposed lower bound fails by a large margin.

The error was treating the reflected interval as if it had to be separated from the original interval without allowing the centered adjacent pair to lie in the gap between the two reflected blocks.

## 2. Correct reflected order constraints

Let the consecutive zeros be

\[
z_0<z_1<z_2<z_3,
\]

with span

\[
s=z_3-z_0.
\]

Reflection gives another consecutive block

\[
z'_0=p-1-z_3<z'_1=p-1-z_2<z'_2=p-1-z_1<z'_3=p-1-z_0.
\]

The reflected block is ordered after the original block exactly when

\[
z_3< p-1-z_3,
\]

that is

\[
z_3<m.
\]

However, there is no requirement that the midpoint gap between these blocks is zero-free. The reflected gap contains the possible centered adjacent pair

\[
(m-1,m+1)
\]
(or the corresponding pair under integer indexing), and this pair may occur between the two blocks.

Therefore the only universal inequalities are

\[
z_0<z_1<z_2<z_3<m<p-1-z_3<\cdots
\]

in the case where the block lies entirely on one side of the center, but this does not imply a lower bound on \(m-z_3\).

## 3. Strongest deterministic inequality from only p and s

For an off-center window, after translating by the center, write

\[
a_i=z_i-m.
\]

Reflection gives the set

\[
\{a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3,-a_3,-a_2,-a_1,-a_0\}.
\]

The only consequence depending only on the span is that the two extreme points satisfy

\[
\max_i |a_i|-\min_i |a_i|\le s.
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
D=\max_i |z_i-m|,
\]

then

\[
D\ge s/2.
\]

This is just the trivial diameter bound. There is no positive lower bound of order \(p-s\) for the nearest distance to the center.

Indeed, the window can approach the center while maintaining arbitrary span constraints, because the reflected copy then appears on the opposite side and the centered adjacency may occur in between.

The sharp fail-closed statement is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{From reflection, consecutiveness, and span }s\text{ alone, no nontrivial lower bound on }\operatorname{dist}(I,m)\text{ follows.}
}
\]

## 4. Test against the p=3727 example

The reflected block is

\[
2432<2715<2858<3627.
\]

The midpoint is

\[
m=1863.
\]

The central gap is

\[
1294<1863<2432.
\]

Its length is

\[
2432-1294=1138.
\]

The window can therefore be far from its reflection while still having the center gap occupied by the global zero structure. Nothing in reflection or consecutiveness excludes a centered adjacent pair inside this interval.

The example is exactly a witness that the proposed separation argument is invalid.

## 5. Abstract pair-cap extremizer

The same obstruction appears in `pair_cap_extremal.tex`: palindrome symmetry and pair caps control the number of equal-gap pairs, but they do not force reflected blocks to leave a zero-free middle interval.

The pair-cap conditions bound multiplicities of differences

\[
A_Z(h)=\#\{z:z,z+h\in Z\},
\]

but do not forbid a special central adjacent pair. Hence they cannot recover the missing inequality.

## 6. What extra Apéry input is required

Any successful SWR proof needs information beyond:

1. reflection symmetry;
2. global consecutiveness;
3. degree bounds for \(N_h\);
4. pair caps.

The missing input must couple the actual Apéry orbit to the center. Examples of possible sufficient inputs are:

- a theorem that every short off-center actual four-zero window forces a centered adjacent return;
- a distinguished-orbit reflection law stronger than formal projective reflection;
- an arithmetic estimate ruling out the central-gap configurations.

The local continuant identities alone cannot supply this because they only see the existence of returns, not the global ordering of all actual zeros.

## Final corrected lemma

**Fail-closed SWR lemma.** Let \(Z\subset\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\) be reflection-palindromic. Let \(z_0<z_1<z_2<z_3\) be globally consecutive zeros with span \(s\). Then the reflected block is

\[
p-1-z_3<p-1-z_2<p-1-z_1<p-1-z_0.
\]

Beyond these order constraints, and the trivial diameter relations implied by span \(s\), there is no deterministic inequality depending only on \(p,s\) that lower-bounds the distance of the original block from the center.

The Q7170 inequality is false and withdrawn.
