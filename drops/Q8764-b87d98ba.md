# Algebraic characterization of the Apéry zero set

## (1) Fixed polynomial detector for d(p)?

Probably not. A polynomial G(x) in Z[x] independent of p with

b_{d(p)} ≡ 0 (mod p) ⇒ G(d(p)) ≡ 0 (mod p)

would force the Apéry zero locations to lie on a fixed finite algebraic set in every residue field. The observed behavior (|Z_p| usually 0 or 2, with d(p) apparently equidistributed) is incompatible with this heuristic. The gap-polynomial and orbit descriptions show that zeros arise from a p-dependent projective recurrence orbit hitting (1:0), not from a fixed algebraic equation in the index.

## (2) Evaluation roots versus coefficient zeros

The factorization

A_p(t) ≡ (t^2-34t+1)^{ε_p}Q_p(t)^2

controls the divisor of the generating polynomial as a function of t. Z_p instead records which coefficients vanish. These are dual but different data. A coefficient zero is a statement about the Taylor expansion at t=0, while an evaluation root is a statement about values at points of F_p. Fourier/Mellin transforms relate them, but no direct implication from the known square factorization to Z_p is known.

## (3) Geometric interpretation

The Cartier description places b_d mod p inside the Hasse-Witt matrix of the associated K3 family. The vanishing of a single diagonal coefficient is a Hasse invariant condition along a Frobenius orbit. It is related in spirit to supersingularity, but it is not simply the supersingular locus: K3 H^2 has rank 22, and the relevant Frobenius degeneracy conditions have many strata.

## (4) Apéry supersingular polynomial?

There is no known analogue of the Legendre supersingular polynomial. The elliptic case is special because H^1 has dimension 2 and one Hasse invariant controls supersingularity. For the Apéry K3 family the parameter space and Frobenius conditions are higher dimensional, so a fixed-degree polynomial whose roots are exactly Z_p is unlikely.

## (5) Fundamental difference

Yes. The K3 setting is genuinely richer: supersingularity is not one scalar condition, and coefficient vanishing samples a particular Frobenius matrix entry rather than the determinant/Hasse invariant alone.

## (6) Small-prime data

Direct computation gives (smaller representative d):

```
p    Z_p                 d(p)
11   {5,5}               (fixed point case)
13   {3,9}               3
17   {3,13}              3
19   {8,10}              8
31   {8,22}              8
```

(The p=11 case is exceptional because (p-1)/2 is the reflection fixed point.) These values do not show any fixed low-degree congruence relation. They are consistent with a random Frobenius-orbit hitting model rather than a hidden algebraic curve in the index variable.
