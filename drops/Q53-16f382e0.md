# Verification of the gap-polynomial decomposition argument

The calculation is correct. The proposed decomposition does **not** by itself give a useful upper bound for the isolated zero contribution, and the reason is exactly the direction-of-control issue identified in the question.

## 1. What the gap polynomial actually controls

For a fixed prime p and h >= 1, a root of N_h(X) modulo p detects a pair

    r in Z_p,  r+h in Z_p.

Since deg N_h = 3(h-1), the number of such ordered pairs at distance h is at most 3(h-1) (up to the usual exceptional cases where the polynomial degenerates modulo p, which are handled separately).

Therefore the number of zeros participating in a short gap is bounded by

    |C_H(p)| <= sum_{h=2}^H 6(h-1) = O(H^2).

This is a bound on the clustered part only.

## 2. The direction is opposite to what HM_3 needs

The decomposition is

    Z_p = C_H(p) union I_H(p),

with

    |I_H(p)| = |Z_p| - |C_H(p)|.

An upper bound on |C_H(p)| gives a lower bound on |I_H(p)|, not an upper bound:

    |I_H(p)| >= |Z_p| - O(H^2).

The only general upper bound remains

    |I_H(p)| <= |Z_p|.

Hence the gap polynomial does not make the isolated population sparse.

## 3. Typical primes show the obstruction sharply

For the empirical typical case |Z_p|=2, the two zeros are related by reflection:

    Z_p = {r, p-1-r}.

Their separation is

    p-1-2r.

For a generic r this is of size comparable to p, and in particular much larger than H=X^{1/3} when p is in (X,2X]. Therefore the two zeros are not clustered at all.

So for most primes with |Z_p|=2 one expects

    C_H(p)=0,
    I_H(p)=Z_p.

The gap polynomial is then proving that there are few short-distance coincidences, but the mass of zeros is sitting in isolated points.

## 4. Consequence for (HM)_3

The hoped-for strategy would need something like

    sum_p |I_H(p)|^3 / p^3

being substantially smaller than the naive contribution. But the gap-polynomial estimate only removes the rare short-gap configurations. It does not reduce the one-zero-per-prime background.

Therefore the H=X^{1/3} gap-polynomial decomposition is not a proof of (HM)_3. It may still be useful for controlling the exceptional clustered configurations in a higher-moment argument, but a separate mechanism is needed for isolated triples: for example CRT anti-correlation, Frobenius/orbit constraints, character cancellation, or a large-sieve type estimate.

The claimed breakthrough calculation fails at exactly the identified point: the gap polynomial controls clusters, whereas the dominant zeros are isolated.
