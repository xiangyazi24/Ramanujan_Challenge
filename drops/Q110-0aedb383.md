# Q110: Gap-polynomial + birthday argument analysis

## Executive conclusion

The proposed route identifies the correct obstruction: the top-window hit count

\[
K(n)=\#\{p\in(n/2,n]:p\mid b_n\}
\]

is a moving-modulus collision problem. However, the naive birthday argument cannot by itself give
\(K(n)\le n^{2/3+\epsilon}\). The gap-polynomial machinery controls **same-prime collisions** (one prime dividing two nearby Apéry values), while the top-window hits are naturally **different-prime events**.

The missing theorem is therefore a large-prime collision theorem, not a direct gap bound.

---

## 1. Reformulating the hit set

For each hit

\[
p_i\mid b_n,\qquad p_i>n/2,
\]

Lucas gives

\[
p_i\mid b_{n-p_i}.
\]

Put

\[
r_i=n-p_i.
\]

Then

\[
0\le r_i<n/2,
\]

and

\[
p_i=n-r_i\mid b_{r_i}.
\]

The set of hits is therefore a set of distinct integers \(r_i\) such that the large prime \(n-r_i\) divides the corresponding Apéry number.

This is a strong arithmetic condition, but it is not a polynomial vanishing condition in the variable \(r_i\).

---

## 2. Why the polynomial annihilator idea fails

The polynomial

\[
P(x)=\prod_i(x-r_i)
\]

has roots at the hit locations. The hoped-for step would be to show that \(P\) divides some bounded-degree Apéry polynomial.

This fails because there is no fixed polynomial \(F(x)\) with

\[
F(r_i)\equiv0\pmod {n-r_i}
\]

for all hits.

The modulus changes with the root:

\[
\text{modulus}=n-r_i.
\]

This is exactly the moving-modulus difficulty.

---

## 3. What the gap polynomial actually proves

Suppose a single prime \(\ell\) divides two Apéry values:

\[
\ell\mid b_r,\qquad \ell\mid b_{r+h}.
\]

Then the recurrence-gap identity gives

\[
\ell\mid N_h(r),
\]

where

\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1).
\]

For \(\ell>n/2\), the number of possible such primes is bounded by the number of large prime factors of \(N_h(r)\):

\[
\omega_{>n/2}(N_h(r))\ll h.
\]

This is excellent control of collisions of the form:

\[
\ell\mid b_{r_i},b_{r_j}.
\]

But the top-window hit condition only gives:

\[
p_i\mid b_{r_i},\qquad p_j\mid b_{r_j}.
\]

There is no reason for

\[
p_i\mid b_{r_j}.
\]

Hence the gap polynomial does not directly constrain the hit set.

---

## 4. The birthday heuristic and its limitation

The heuristic picture is:

- each prime \(p\) has a zero set \(Z_p\) of size about 1;
- a hit chooses a residue slot;
- collisions among chosen slots should become unlikely below square-root scale.

However, the slots are not sampled from one common universe. They are pairs:

\[
(p,r),\qquad r\in Z_p.
\]

Different primes have different residue spaces.

A birthday argument requires a mechanism forcing two hits to interact. The only known interaction is when the same prime appears twice.

Thus the birthday analogy suggests the right theorem, but does not prove it.

---

## 5. The correct intermediate theorem

The needed statement is a large-prime collision estimate:

For \(R<n/2\), define

\[
C(n)=\#\{(p,q):p\ne q,\ p,q>n/2,\ p\mid b_{n-p},\ q\mid b_{n-q},\text{ with correlated residues}\}.
\]

One needs a theorem showing that excessive simultaneous hits force many same-prime collisions after lifting through the Apéry recurrence.

A plausible target is:

\[
K(n)^2\ll n^{1+o(1)}+C(n).
\]

Then a bound

\[
C(n)\ll n^{1+o(1)}
\]

would imply

\[
K(n)\ll n^{1/2+o(1)}.
\]

Even the weaker

\[
K(n)\ll n^{2/3+\epsilon}
\]

would suffice for the GCD conjecture.

---

## 6. A possible bridge: use the recurrence transfer matrix

The recurrence viewpoint may be the missing link.

For each prime \(p\), the condition

\[
p\mid b_r
\]

means that the transfer matrix product lands on a special line in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\).

The question becomes:

Can many different primes force the same integer orbit segment to hit many independent exceptional lines?

This is closer to a sieve problem than to a polynomial divisibility problem.

The possible tools are:

1. large sieve for moving finite-field conditions;
2. monodromy/spectral gap of the Apéry transfer matrices;
3. gap-polynomial control of repeated hits after CRT lifting.

---

## 7. Final verdict

The Q109 skeleton is close to the right conceptual path, but one correction is essential:

**The gap polynomial does not bound the set of top-window hits directly. It bounds repeated divisibility by the same prime.**

The missing theorem is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{large-prime cross-hit independence / collision theorem}}
\]

not merely a stronger zero-set estimate.

If such a theorem gives

\[
K(n)\le n^{2/3+\epsilon},
\]

then the top-window contribution satisfies

\[
B(n)\ll K(n)\log n=o(n),
\]

which would indeed prove the Apéry GCD conjecture after handling the lower prime ranges.

So the birthday + gap idea is not a proof yet, but it has isolated the exact missing bridge: convert same-prime algebraic rigidity into cross-prime anti-concentration.
