# R2 countermodel audit: a full reflection-symmetric labeled system

## Verdict

The dense-block example in Q7201 was not a valid all-constraints countermodel: as stated, it did not verify the fixed-gap cap or the energy of a complete reflection-symmetric labeling. I withdraw that particular example.

However, the conclusion needed for the proof strategy survives. The packet construction already proved in `problems/3.2/pair_cap_extremal.tex` at commit `d924bb6` can be completed to a labeling of **all** indices. The completed system satisfies, simultaneously:

1. pointwise reflection symmetry;
2. no adjacent equal labels;
3. the **global** Apéry fixed-gap cap
   \[
   \#\{n:\pi(n)=\pi(n+h)\}\le 3(h-1)
   \qquad(1\le h<p),
   \]
   which is stronger than imposing the cap separately on each fiber;
4. the exact filtered-four-consecutive-occurrence convention of `projective_variance_reduction.tex`;
5. raw selected mass at most `p`;
6. full occurrence energy
   \[
   \mathcal F_p:=\sum_q|\pi^{-1}(q)|^2
   =p^{4/3+o(1)}\le p^{3/2+o(1)};
   \]
7. for
   \[
   K_p=\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt p}{\sqrt{\log p}}\right\rfloor,
   \]
   canonical far-bridge ordered quotient energy
   \[
   E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_p)
   =(10^{-8}+o(1))p^{4/3}.
   \]

In fact,

\[
\frac{E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_p)}{\sum_q\overline C_p(q)^2}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\frac{E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_p)}{\mathcal F_p}\longrightarrow\frac14,
\]

while `K_p/sqrt(p) -> 0`. Thus the listed deterministic constraints do not imply the weak-DPLS far-energy target and do not imply any fixed positive power of `K/H` as a **relative** saving against the actual chain-square energy or the actual full occurrence energy.

A qualification is necessary: this does not disprove every weak inequality normalized by the coarse ceiling `p^(3/2+o(1))`. For example, the present model is compatible with the numerically much larger bound `(K/H)p^(3/2)`. What it disproves is the target-level conclusion and any universal relative decay of the form `(K/H)^eta` times the energy actually present.

## 1. Exact packet from `pair_cap_extremal.tex`

Fix a sufficiently large odd prime `p` and put

\[
m=100\left\lfloor\frac{p^{1/3}}{1000}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
R=\frac m{100},
\qquad
N=mR=\frac{m^2}{100},
\qquad D=1.
\]

For `0 <= i < N`, define

\[
g_i=3m+(i\bmod m),
\qquad
P_0=0,
\qquad
P_j=\sum_{0\le i<j}g_i.
\]

One period has sum

\[
S=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(3m+i)=\frac{m(7m-1)}2,
\]

and the packet endpoint is

\[
L=P_N=RS=\frac{m^2(7m-1)}{200}.
\]

Let `rho(n)=p-1-n` and set

\[
\mathcal P=\{D+P_0,\ldots,D+P_N\},
\qquad
\mathcal Z=\mathcal P\cup\rho(\mathcal P).
\]

Since `m <= p^(1/3)/10`, for all sufficiently large `p`,

\[
4(D+L)<4+\frac{7m^3}{50}<p-1.
\tag{1}
\]

Hence the two packets are disjoint and lie on opposite sides of the reflection center. Their central gap is

\[
C_0=p-1-2(D+L)>\frac{p-1}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]

The exact cardinalities are

\[
|\mathcal Z|=2(N+1),
\qquad
m=\left(\frac1{10}+o(1)\right)p^{1/3},
\qquad
N=\left(10^{-4}+o(1)\right)p^{2/3}.
\tag{3}
\]

The proof in `pair_cap_extremal.tex` gives, for

\[
A_{\mathcal Z}(h)=\#\{z:z,z+h\in\mathcal Z\},
\]

the following stronger piecewise estimates:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
A_{\mathcal Z}(1)=0, & \\
A_{\mathcal Z}(h)\le h/75,
  &2\le h<m^2,\\
A_{\mathcal Z}(h)\le m^2/50\le h,
  &m^2\le h\le L,\\
A_{\mathcal Z}(h)\le h-1,
  &h\text{ is a cross-packet difference},\\
A_{\mathcal Z}(h)=0,
  &\text{otherwise}.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]

In particular `A_Z(h) <= 3(h-1)` for every `1 <= h < p`.

## 2. Completing the packet to a full projective-state labeling

Let

\[
c=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

Choose distinct labels in `P^1(F_p)` as follows:

- one special label `q_*`;
- one center label `q_c`;
- for every reflection pair `{n,rho(n)}` contained in the complement of `Z`, one private label `q_n`.

Define

\[
\pi_p(u)=
\begin{cases}
q_*,&u\in\mathcal Z,\\
q_c,&u=c,\\
q_{\min(u,\rho(u))},&u\notin\mathcal Z\cup\{c\}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]

This uses

\[
2+\frac{p-|\mathcal Z|-1}{2}
=2+\frac{p-2N-3}{2}
=\frac{p-2N+1}{2}<p+1
\]

labels, so they can indeed be chosen in `P^1(F_p)`. Equation (5) has the exact pointwise reflection law

\[
\pi_p(p-1-u)=\pi_p(u).
\tag{6}
\]

Every private fiber has two points and the center fiber has one point. The special fiber is exactly `Z`.

### No adjacent equal labels

The special-fiber gaps are at least `3m`. A private reflection pair has gap

\[
p-1-2n,
\]

which is a positive even integer and hence at least `2`. The center label is a singleton. Therefore

\[
\#\{u:\pi_p(u)=\pi_p(u+1)\}=0.
\tag{7}
\]

### The full global fixed-gap cap

Define

\[
B_p(h)=\#\{0\le u<p-h:\pi_p(u)=\pi_p(u+h)\}.
\]

Different private reflection pairs have different gaps `p-1-2n`. Thus private fibers contribute at most one additional pair at each fixed `h`. Consequently

\[
B_p(h)=A_{\mathcal Z}(h)+\varepsilon_h,
\qquad \varepsilon_h\in\{0,1\},
\qquad \varepsilon_1=0.
\tag{8}
\]

Using (4):

- for `h=1`, `B_p(1)=0`;
- for `2 <= h < m^2`,
  \[
  B_p(h)\le h/75+1\le3(h-1);
  \]
- for `m^2 <= h <= L`,
  \[
  B_p(h)\le h+1\le3(h-1);
  \]
- for a cross-packet difference,
  \[
  B_p(h)\le h\le3(h-1);
  \]
- in every remaining range, `B_p(h) <= 1 <= 3(h-1)`.

Hence the completed labeling satisfies the exact **global** cap

\[
\boxed{B_p(h)\le3(h-1)\quad(1\le h<p).}
\tag{9}
\]

This is stronger than the fiberwise formulation challenged in the question.

## 3. Selected chains and every deterministic filter

For `0 <= i <= N-3`, let

\[
W_i=(D+P_i,D+P_{i+1},D+P_{i+2},D+P_{i+3}).
\]

Define the selected collection by

\[
\Gamma_p(q_*)=\{W_i,R(W_i):0\le i\le N-3\},
\qquad
\Gamma_p(q)=\varnothing\quad(q\ne q_*),
\tag{10}
\]

where `R` is the quadruple reflection used in `projective_variance_reduction.tex`.

Put

\[
k=N-2=\frac{m^2}{100}-2.
\tag{11}
\]

There are `k` selected windows in each packet, hence `2k` raw chains and exactly `k` reflection orbits.

Each selected quadruple is a window of four consecutive occurrences of its label. Its three gaps are successive terms of

\[
3m,3m+1,\ldots,4m-1
\]

with periodic wrap. Thus:

- every gap is at least `3m >= 2`;
- no gap triple is constant, including the two wrap patterns;
- the span is at most
  \[
  (4m-3)+(4m-2)+(4m-1)=12m-6<\sqrt p;
  \tag{12}
  \]
- every adjacent pair in a left-packet window has sum below `p-1`, and every adjacent pair in a right-packet window has sum above `p-1`, by (1).

The three sliding windows in the full `q_*` occurrence list that cross between the packets have span at least the central gap `C_0>(p-1)/2`, and hence fail the `sqrt(p)` span filter. They are not members of (10).

This last point resolves a possible ambiguity. The exact hypothesis in `projective_variance_reduction.tex` is that **no selected member** has a reflection-centered adjacent pair. The special fiber necessarily has a centered middle pair, but every four-window containing it crosses the central gap and is filtered out. A stronger global axiom saying that no fiber may contain any centered adjacent pair is not one of the hypotheses at commit `d924bb6`.

The raw selected mass and quotient mass are

\[
M_p=2k=\frac{m^2}{50}-4<p,
\qquad
\overline M_p=k.
\tag{13}
\]

The first index uniquely determines a selected raw chain. Also, the total occurrence mass is of course exactly

\[
\sum_q|\pi_p^{-1}(q)|=p.
\]

No arithmetic carrier predicate is asserted here. As in `pair_cap_extremal.tex`, this is a logical model of the deterministic occurrence-list axioms; carrier support and the Apéry transfer recurrence are additional arithmetic structure.

## 4. Full projective-orbit energy

There is one fiber of size `2N+2`,

\[
\frac{p-2N-3}{2}
\]

private fibers of size `2`, and one singleton center fiber. Therefore the full square energy is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_p
&=(2N+2)^2
 +4\left(\frac{p-2N-3}{2}\right)+1\\
&=4N^2+4N+2p-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

Using (3),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_p
=\left(4\cdot10^{-8}+o(1)\right)p^{4/3}
=p^{4/3+o(1)}
\le p^{3/2+o(1)}.}
\tag{15}
\]

The off-diagonal occurrence energy is `F_p-p` and has the same `p^(4/3)` leading term. The quotient selected-chain square energy is even simpler:

\[
\sum_q\overline C_p(q)^2=k^2,
\tag{16}
\]

because all selected quotient chains have label `q_*`.

## 5. Exact canonical bridges

Index the quotient chain orbits by their left representatives `W_0,...,W_(k-1)`. Let

\[
I_i=[D+P_i,D+P_{i+3}].
\]

For `i<j`, the reflection-invariant supports of the two orbits are disjoint exactly when

\[
r:=j-i\ge4.
\tag{17}
\]

Indeed, closed intervals with index distance `1`, `2`, or `3` overlap, with distance `3` sharing an endpoint.

Assume `r>=4`. For the ordered pair `(i,j)`, the same-packet representatives have bridge

\[
G_{i,j}=P_j-P_{i+3}
       =\sum_{u=i+3}^{j-1}g_u.
\tag{18}
\]

For the reverse ordered pair `(j,i)`, the two reflected representatives have the same bridge. Any cross-packet choice has bridge at least `C_0`, whereas (18) is at most `L`. By (1)--(2), `C_0>L`; hence the deterministic minimization in `near_bridge_energy.tex` selects exactly the same-packet bridge (18). Thus alignment and canonical orientation have not been relaxed.

Since `3m <= g_u <= 4m-1`,

\[
3m(r-3)\le G_{i,j}\le(4m-1)(r-3).
\tag{19}
\]

## 6. Far energy at `K ~ sqrt(p)/sqrt(log p)`

Let

\[
H_p=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor,
\qquad
K_p=\left\lfloor\frac{H_p}{\sqrt{\log p}}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
r_0=4+\left\lfloor\frac{K_p}{3m}\right\rfloor.
\tag{20}
\]

If `j-i=r>=r_0`, then (19) gives

\[
G_{i,j}\ge3m(r-3)>K_p.
\]

There are `k-r` unordered index pairs at distance `r`, and both orderings are counted. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_p)
&\ge2\sum_{r=r_0}^{k-1}(k-r)\\
&=(k-r_0)(k-r_0+1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

The scales are

\[
r_0
=\left(\frac{10}{3}+o(1)\right)
  \frac{p^{1/6}}{\sqrt{\log p}},
\qquad
k=\left(10^{-4}+o(1)\right)p^{2/3},
\tag{22}
\]

so

\[
\frac{r_0}{k}
=O\left(\frac{p^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\log p}}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The total ordered distinct quotient-pair count is at most `k(k-1)`. Combining this upper bound with (21) gives the precise asymptotic

\[
\boxed{
E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_p)
=(1+o(1))k^2
=\left(10^{-8}+o(1)\right)p^{4/3}.}
\tag{23}
\]

For an entirely explicit coarse constant, for all sufficiently large `p` one has

\[
m\ge\frac{p^{1/3}}{20},
\qquad
k\ge\frac{p^{2/3}}{80000},
\qquad
r_0\le\frac k2,
\]

and therefore

\[
E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_p)
\ge\frac{k^2}{4}
\ge\frac{p^{4/3}}{25{,}600{,}000{,}000}.
\tag{24}
\]

Equations (15), (16), and (23) yield

\[
\frac{E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_p)}{\sum_q\overline C_p(q)^2}\to1,
\qquad
\frac{E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_p)}{\mathcal F_p}\to\frac14.
\tag{25}
\]

Yet

\[
\frac{K_p}{H_p}=\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{\log p}}\to0.
\tag{26}
\]

Thus, for every fixed `eta>0`, the deterministic axioms cannot imply

\[
E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K)
\ll
\left(\frac KH\right)^\eta
\sum_q\overline C_p(q)^2,
\]

nor the analogous estimate with `F_p` on the right and an absolute constant independent of `p`.

## 7. Dyadic exponent and constant

One may use the uniform dyadic threshold

\[
K_X=\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{2X}}{\sqrt{\log X}}\right\rfloor.
\]

For every `X<p<=2X`, it is comparable to `sqrt(p)/sqrt(log p)`, and the analogue of `r_0/k -> 0` is uniform. Applying the construction independently at every prime gives

\[
E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_X)
=\left(10^{-8}+o(1)\right)p^{4/3}
\]

uniformly on the dyadic interval. Hence the prime number theorem and partial summation give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{X<p\le2X}E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K_X)
&\sim
10^{-8}\sum_{X<p\le2X}p^{4/3}\\
&\sim
10^{-8}\frac{3}{7}\left(2^{7/3}-1\right)
\frac{X^{7/3}}{\log X}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

This exceeds the required weak-DPLS scale

\[
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X}
\]

by the power `X^(1/3-o(1))`.
