# Q544: Apéry transfer matrix zero pairs and the missing M1 saving

## Summary

The computation for all primes p < 2000 is strong evidence that non-reflection zero pairs satisfy

\[
T_{12}(r\to s)=0.
\]

The transfer-matrix reformulation is therefore the right object. However, a direct Schwartz-Zippel argument in the gap variable h does not immediately prove M1, because it gives an O(p^2) bound rather than O(p). The missing saving must come from additional structure: recurrence continuants, arithmetic dependence on h, and the palindromic/reflection involution.

---

## (1) Explicit expansion of T_{12}

Let

\[
T_{r\to s}=M(s-1)\cdots M(r),
\]

with

\[
M(n)=\begin{pmatrix}A(n)&-B(n)\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Write the gap as

\[
h=s-r.
\]

The (1,2) entry is a continuant polynomial.

For h=1:

\[
T_{12}(r,r+1)=-B(r).
\]

For h=2:

\[
T_{12}(r,r+2)=-A(r+1)B(r).
\]

For h=3:

\[
T_{12}(r,r+3)=-(A(r+2)A(r+1)-B(r+1))B(r).
\]

In general,

\[
T_{12}(r,r+h)
=-B(r)K_{h-1}(r+1),
\]

where K_m is the continuant satisfying

\[
K_0=1,
\]

\[
K_1=A(r+1),
\]

and

\[
K_m=A(r+m)K_{m-1}-B(r+m)K_{m-2}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
K_m=
\sum_{S}\n(-1)^{|S|}\prod_{i\notin S}A(r+i)\prod_{j\in S}B(r+j),
\]

where the sum is over subsets S of {1,...,m} with no adjacent elements. This is the standard continuant expansion.

Thus the vanishing condition is a polynomial relation among the recurrence coefficients.

---

## (2) Degree in r and why Schwartz-Zippel is insufficient

For the Apéry recurrence,

\[
A(n)=\frac{(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)}{(n+1)^3},
\]

and

\[
B(n)=\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}.
\]

Over \(\mathbf F_p\), after clearing denominators, these are rational functions of degree O(1).

For fixed gap h, the cleared polynomial

\[
P_h(r)=0
\]

coming from

\[
T_{12}(r,r+h)=0
\]

has degree growing at most linearly in h:

\[
\deg P_h=O(h).
\]

Therefore Schwartz-Zippel gives

\[
\#\{r:P_h(r)=0\}=O(h).
\]

Summing over all h gives only

\[
\sum_{h\le p/2}O(h)=O(p^2).
\]

This is not the desired M1 scale.

The issue is that Schwartz-Zippel treats every gap h independently. It ignores that all h are generated by the same second-order recurrence.

The missing saving must exploit global recurrence rigidity.

---

## Where the extra saving can come from

There are several possible sources.

### 1. A zero at one gap propagates constraints

The equations

\[
T_{12}(r,r+h)=0
\]

are not independent for different h. They are values of a single solution of the same second-order recurrence.

A sequence satisfying

\[
X_{n+1}=A(n)X_n-B(n)X_{n-1}
\]

cannot have O(h) zeros for every h independently unless the coefficients satisfy exceptional algebraic relations.

Thus one expects a much stronger bound:

\[
\sum_h \#\{r:T_{12}(r,r+h)=0\}=O(p).
\]

This is analogous to a low-complexity zero set of a linear recurrence sequence.

---

### 2. Monodromy rigidity

The transfer matrices lie in the Apéry monodromy group. Since the group is Zariski dense in Sp_4, accidental annihilation of a matrix coefficient should have codimension one.

Hence the expected probability is roughly

\[
\Pr(T_{12}=0)\approx\frac1p.
\]

There are p possible starting positions and p possible gaps, so a naive count gives p^2/p=p.

The Schwartz-Zippel estimate loses exactly this probability interpretation by replacing average codimension-one behavior with a worst-case degree bound.

---

## (3) Palindromic symmetry

Yes, the reflection symmetry is likely the missing reduction.

The Apéry congruence gives

\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv(-1)^?b_r \pmod p
\]

(up to the known twist), so zeros pair under

\[
r\mapsto p-1-r.
\]

For a pair (r,s), reflection gives

\[
(r,s)\mapsto(p-1-s,p-1-r).
\]

The gap changes as

\[
h=s-r
\]

into

\[
h'=(p-1-r)-(p-1-s)=h.
\]

So the reflection does not reduce the number of gaps directly; it identifies pairs inside each gap class.

However, it removes half of the apparent pair count because every non-exceptional pair belongs to a reflection orbit of size two.

More importantly, the reflection relation implies that the transfer polynomial satisfies a functional equation relating

\[
T_{12}(r,r+h)
\]

and

\[
T_{12}(p-1-r-h,p-1-r).
\]

Therefore roots occur in symmetric packets. A generic root count O(h) is too pessimistic because roots must satisfy this involutive constraint.

---

## Conclusion

1. The exact formula is a continuant:

\[
T_{12}(r,r+h)=-B(r)K_{h-1}(r+1),
\]

with the usual non-adjacent subset expansion.

2. For fixed h, the degree in r is O(h), but Schwartz-Zippel alone gives O(p^2) total pairs. It misses recurrence dependence across h.

3. The extra saving toward the M1 target O(p) should come from a combination of:

- second-order recurrence rigidity,
- Sp_4 monodromy/codimension-one trace-zero behavior,
- palindromic involution reducing independent configurations.

The computational fact (116 pairs, 0 exceptions for p<2000) strongly suggests that the true theorem is not a generic polynomial zero bound, but a global algebraic statement: the family of transfer coefficients has only O(p) accidental vanishing points because the Apéry recurrence has rigid symplectic monodromy.
