# Cold strategic proof attack: a pruned high-load fifth moment is enough

## Executive verdict

I do not obtain an unconditional proof of the full all-index Apéry GCD conjecture. I do obtain a strictly smaller closing target than full R3-CRT, a root-birthday theorem, or \((\mathrm{HM})_7\).

The new target is a **one-sided, high-load fifth factorial moment only for the roots that survive every currently proved short-return carrier**. It asks for no lower bound on \(\lambda_X\), divides by no Palm denominator, and ignores all low-load outputs.

Let
\[
 \mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},\qquad M=X^2,
\]
\[
 Z_p=\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\},\qquad
 K_X(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}{\bf1}_{Z_p}(m\bmod p),
\]
\[
 \lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]
Fold a root by
\[
 \sigma_p(r)=\min(r,p-1-r),\qquad
 Y_p=\sigma_p(Z_p)\subseteq[0,(p-1)/2].
\]
For a scale \(H\), call \(s\in Y_p\) **\(H\)-isolated** if
\[
 |s-t|>H\qquad(t\in Y_p,\ t\ne s).
\]
Let \(I_p(H)\subseteq Z_p\) be the roots whose fold is \(H\)-isolated, and put
\[
 K_X^{\mathrm{iso}}(m;H)
 =\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}{\bf1}_{I_p(H)}(m\bmod p),
\qquad
 \Lambda_X(H)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|I_p(H)|}{p}.
\]
Take
\[
 \boxed{
 H_X=\left\lfloor X^{7/15}(\log X)^{-1/3}\right\rfloor
 }
\tag{0.1}
\]
with the harmless convention \(H_X\ge2\).

The weakest clean new proposition I find is the following tail statement.

> **Isolated five-star high-load estimate \((\mathrm{I5})_\eta\).**  For one fixed
> \[
> 0<\eta<\frac1{15},
> \]
> uniformly in dyadic \(X\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> \sum_{\substack{0\le m<X^2\\
> K_X^{\mathrm{iso}}(m;H_X)\ge X^{14/15+\eta}}}
> \bigl(K_X^{\mathrm{iso}}(m;H_X)\bigr)_5
> \ll
> X^{o(1)}\left(1+X^2\Lambda_X(H_X)^5\right).
> }
> \tag{I5}
> \]

The additive \(1\) is mandatory if the statement is to be uniform when \(\Lambda_X(H_X)\) is tiny. No lower-mass hypothesis is used.

I prove below that \((\mathrm{I5})_\eta\), together with already proved one-prime recurrence facts, implies
\[
 \max_{m<X^2}K_X(m)\ll X^{14/15+\eta+o(1)},
\tag{0.2}
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \log G_n\ll n^{14/15+\eta+o(1)}=o(n).
 }
\tag{0.3}
\]
For example, \(\eta=1/30\) gives \(\log G_n\ll n^{29/30+o(1)}\).

The proof uses an actual Apéry carrier for all nonisolated roots and a packing estimate for the isolated roots. The exponent \(5\) is not arbitrary: with the presently proved carrier height \(O(H^2\log X)\), a rank-three or rank-four moment cannot close the all-index problem. The general optimized exponent is
\[
 \theta_t=\frac23+\frac{4}{3t};
\tag{0.4}
\]
thus \(\theta_3=10/9\), \(\theta_4=1\), and \(\theta_5=14/15\). Fifth order is the first order that gives a genuine power saving in this hybrid architecture.

I do not prove \((\mathrm{I5})_\eta\) from the present rank-three K3/Franel structure. The smallest remaining arithmetic theorem is exactly the one-sided isolated five-star estimate above. A full fifth moment on all roots, full \((\mathrm{HM})_7\), a complete CRT restriction theorem, and a pointwise root-birthday theorem all contain substantially more information than is needed.

# 2. Exact all-index reduction

The committed high-moment hypothesis is
\[
 \sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_k
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k.
\tag{2.1}
\]
If
\[
 M_X=\max_{m<X^2}K_X(m),
\]
then, for \(M_X\ge2k\),
\[
 (M_X/2)^k

\]
Consequently
\[
 M_X\ll X^{2/k+o(1)}\lambda_X+1.
\tag{2.2}
\]
The proved vertical estimate gives
\[
 \lambda_X\ll \frac{X^{2/3}}{\log X},
\tag{2.3}

so \((\mathrm{HM})_k\) yields
\[
 M_X\ll \frac{X^{2/3+2/k+o(1)}}{\log X}.

# 3. Why HM3 or PP3 cannot close the all-index problem

The committed `hm3_result.tex` gives the exact ordered pair-Palm identity. If
\[
 J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|,
\]
and \(T_{p,q}\) counts third-prime extensions of that pair locus, then
\[
 \sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}=\sum_m(K_X(m))_2,
\qquad
 \sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}=\sum_m(K_X(m))_3.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The positive pair-Palm excess is sandwiched between the third factorial moment and that moment minus \(O(X^2\lambda_X^3)\). Hence the periodic and finite PP3 statements are equivalent, up to the explicit lattice correction, to HM3.

Even the full lattice-corrected HM3 bound gives only
\[
 M_X\ll X^{2/3+o(1)}\lambda_X+X^{o(1)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Using (2.3) gives the useless formal exponent \(4/3\); the trivial prime-count cap then takes over. The problem is not a lost Palm denominator. It is that \(\lambda_X\) may be much larger than its expected logarithmic size.

The anchored reflected-star constructions in `hm3_result.tex` show that reflection, no consecutive roots, row-size bounds, pairwise CRT injectivity, and bounded codegree do not repair this. An actual all-index proof must use a cross-prime property that attacks high loads themselves.

# 4. An actual Apéry carrier for every nonisolated hit

This section is the deterministic half of the new argument.

## 4.1 Gap polynomials

Let
\[
 P(x)=34x^3+51x^2+27x+5.
\]
The canonical gap polynomials are
\[
 N_1(x)=1,
\]
\[
 N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x).
\tag{4.1}
\]
They satisfy
\[
 \deg N_h=3(h-1),
\tag{4.2}
\]
and the exact reflection law
\[
 N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).
\tag{4.3}
\]
If \(0\le a<a+h<p\) and both \(a,a+h\in Z_p\), then
\[
 p\mid N_h(a).
\tag{4.4}
\]
For nonnegative integer arguments the tridiagonal determinant formula gives
\[
 N_h(n)>0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

## 4.2 Horizontal transfer of a nonreflection return

Fix \(0\le m<X^2\), \(p\in\mathcal P_X\), and assume
\[
 r=m\bmod p\in Z_p.
\]
Set
\[
 s=\sigma_p(r)=\min(r,p-1-r)\in Y_p.
\]
Suppose there is another \(t\in Y_p\), \(t\ne s\), and put
\[
 h=|t-s|.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 p\mid N_h(m)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 m\ge h\ \text{and}\ p\mid N_h(m-h).
 }
\tag{4.6}
\]

Here is the complete orientation check.

### Direct branch

If \(r=s\), then \(s\equiv m\pmod p\).

- If \(t=s+h\), (4.4) gives \(p\mid N_h(s)\), hence \(p\mid N_h(m)\).
- If \(t=s-h\), then \(s\ge h\), so \(m\ge h\), and (4.4) gives
  \(p\mid N_h(s-h)\), hence \(p\mid N_h(m-h)\).

### Reflected branch

If \(r=p-1-s\), then
\[
 s\equiv-m-1\pmod p.
\]

- If \(t=s+h\), (4.4) gives \(p\mid N_h(s)\), hence
  \(p\mid N_h(-m-1)\). Applying (4.3) with \(x=m-h\) gives
  \[
   N_h(-m-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(m-h).
  \]
  Also \(m\ge h\), because \(s+h\le(p-1)/2\) implies
  \(h\le(p-1)/2-s\le p-1-s\le m\).
- If \(t=s-h\), then
  \(s-h\equiv-m-h-1\pmod p\), and (4.3) gives
  \[
   N_h(-m-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(m).
  \]

This proves (4.6). Notice that the second zero is a **nonreflection return in the folded half**. The forced partner \(p-1-s\) alone gives no such condition; its gap polynomial is divisible by \(p\) through the universal centered factor.

## 4.3 One carrier for all short-return primes

For \(H\ge2\), define
\[
 \mathcal C_{m,H}
 =\prod_{2\le h\le H}N_h(m)
  \prod_{2\le h\le\min(H,m)}N_h(m-h).
\tag{4.7}
\]
No consecutive-zero theorem ensures that a distinct folded return has \(h\ge2\). By (4.6), every hit whose folded root has another folded root within distance \(H\) divides \(\mathcal C_{m,H}\).

A convenient explicit height bound follows directly from (4.1). For \(n\ge0\), put \(R=n+h+1\). Since
\[
 P(n+j)\le117R^3,
 \qquad
 (n+j)^6\le R^6,
\]
induction gives
\[
 0<N_h(n)\le118^{h-1}R^{3(h-1)}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
For \(m<X^2\) and \(h<X\), this yields
\[
 \log N_h(m),\ \log N_h(m-h)\ll h\log X.
\]
Summing over \(h\le H\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \log\mathcal C_{m,H}\ll H^2\log X.
 }
\tag{4.9}
\]
If \(R_{m,H}\) distinct primes in \((X,2X]\) divide this nonzero integer, then
\[
 R_{m,H}\log X
 \le\log\mathcal C_{m,H}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 K_X^{\mathrm{near}}(m;H)\ll H^2
 }
\tag{4.10}
\]
uniformly in \(m<X^2\), where `near` denotes the complement of the isolated bank.

This is a genuine pointwise characteristic-zero carrier. It uses the actual Apéry recurrence and the actual normalized Apéry zero condition. It is not an abstract CRT estimate.

# 6. Complete implication of the isolated five-star estimate

Assume \((\mathrm{I5})_\eta\). Suppose, for contradiction, that for some \(m<X^2\),
\[
 K_X^{\mathrm{iso}}(m;H_X)
 \ge X^{14/15+\eta}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
For large \(X\),
\[
 (K_X^{\mathrm{iso}}(m;H_X))_5
 \ge 2^{-5}X^{14/3+5\eta}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
On the other hand, (5.6) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 1+X^2\Lambda_X(H_X)^5
 &\ll
 1+X^2
   \left(X^{8/15}(\log X)^{-2/3+o(1)}\right)^5\\
 &\ll X^{14/3+o(1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]
The one term in (6.2) already exceeds the whole right side of \((\mathrm{I5})_\eta\) by \(X^{5\eta-o(1)}\), a contradiction. Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \max_{m<X^2}K_X^{\mathrm{iso}}(m;H_X)
 <X^{14/15+\eta}
 }
\tag{6.4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(X\).

Combining (4.10), (5.5), and (6.4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_X
 &\le
 \max_mK_X^{\mathrm{near}}(m;H_X)
 +\max_mK_X^{\mathrm{iso}}(m;H_X)\\
 &\ll X^{14/15}(\log X)^{-2/3+o(1)}
       +X^{14/15+\eta}\\
 &\ll X^{14/15+\eta+o(1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.5}
\]
Insert this into (2.5):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \log G_n
 &\ll \sqrt n+

 X^{14/15+\eta+o(1)}\log X\\
 &\ll n^{14/15+\eta+o(1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.6}
\]
The dyadic sum is geometric and is dominated by its largest block. Since \(\eta<1/15\), the exponent is strictly below one. This proves the full all-index conjecture.

There is no use of
\[
 \Lambda_X(H_X)\gg1,

 X^2\Lambda_X(H_X)^5\gg1,
\]
or of a nonempty pair-Palm support. The lattice correction \(+1\) in \((\mathrm{I5})_\eta\) handles every sparse case.

# 7. Why this is strictly weaker than HM7

The new input controls only:

1. roots with no other folded root inside distance \(H_X\);
2. outputs whose isolated load exceeds \(X^{14/15+\eta}\);
3. the fifth falling factorial;
4. an aggregate one-sided tail.

It places no condition on:

- the near-root bank, already controlled by the carrier;
- isolated outputs below the threshold;
- the full fifth moment;
- the sixth or seventh moment;
- any individual five-prime CRT tuple.

The following exact residue-bank model separates the statements.

Choose
\[
 R=\lfloor cH_X^2\rfloor
\]
distinct primes in \((X,2X]\), and for each active prime define
\[
 Z_p^\star=\{2,4,p-5,p-3\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Then:

- \(Z_p^\star\) is reflection invariant;
- it has no consecutive residues;
- the folded set is \(Y_p^\star=\{2,4\}\);
- at \(m_0=2\), every active row hits;
- both folded roots are nonisolated for every \(H_X\ge2\), so the isolated bank is empty;
- the near load is exactly \(R\le H_X^2\), consistent with the deterministic carrier conclusion.

Thus \((\mathrm{I5})_\eta\) holds vacuously. Also
\[
 \lambda_X^\star
 =\sum_{p\ \mathrm{active}}\frac4p
 \asymp\frac RX.
\tag{7.2}
\]
The second factorial moment at the anchor is \((R)_2\), while
\[
 X^2(\lambda_X^\star)^2\asymp R^2,
\]
so the existing HM2 scale is respected.

But the seventh factorial moment contains
\[
 (R)_7\asymp R^7,
\]
whereas the HM7 target is
\[
 X^2(\lambda_X^\star)^7
 \asymp X^2\left(\frac RX\right)^7
 =\frac{R^7}{X^5}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Hence HM7 fails by a factor \(\asymp X^5\).

This is an incidence-model separation, not a claim that (7.1) is an actual Apéry zero bank. Its logical role is exact: the new cross-prime proposition plus the already proved near-load cap does not recover HM7. It is therefore genuinely weaker rather than a disguised normalization of HM7.

The same comparison shows why a full R3-CRT theorem or a pointwise root-birthday theorem is more than is needed. Such a theorem would control all relevant tuples or force a collision witness at every sufficiently large star. \((\mathrm{I5})_\eta\) permits arbitrary behavior below its high-load threshold and permits arbitrary near-root tuple counts.

 \ll X^{o(1)}\left(1+X^{2/t}\Lambda_X(H)\right).
\tag{8.2}

Using (5.3) and the near-load carrier,

 M_X
 \ll

\tag{8.3}

Ignoring only logarithmic factors, the two terms balance when
\[
 H^3=X^{1+2/t},
\qquad

\tag{8.4}

\tag{8.5}

Thus
\[
 \theta_3=\frac{10}{9},
 \qquad

 \qquad

\tag{8.6}

At \(t=4\), keeping the logarithms gives the optimum
\[

\[
 M_X\ll X(\log X)^{-2/3+o(1)}.
\tag{8.7}

But (2.5) weights the block by \(\log X\), producing
\[
 X(\log X)^{1/3+o(1)},

not \(o(X)\). No choice of \(H\) repairs this: making \(H\) larger reduces the isolated term but makes the carrier term exceed the required scale, and making \(H\) smaller does the reverse.

Therefore:

> **Architecture-minimality theorem.** With the presently proved
> \(O(H^2)\) short-return carrier and only the packing estimate
> \(\Lambda_X(H)\ll X/(H\log X)\), no independence-scale moment of order

This is an exponent obstruction, not merely a failure of one application of Cauchy--Schwarz.

A matching abstract model can saturate it. Put \(\asymp X/H\) isolated folded roots in each active row, spaced by more than \(H\), and align one selected root from every row at a common output. Then
\[
 \Lambda_X(H)\asymp\frac{X}{H\log X},
\]
while the common load may be \(\asymp X/\log X\). For \(t=3\) or \(4\), the independence-scale right side is large enough to permit that star whenever \(H=o(X^{1/2})\), exactly the range required to keep the carrier term sublinear. The fifth moment is the first one whose scale rules it out.

# 9. Attempted proof from actual Apéry structure

## 9.1 The universal fixed-index carrier is too tall

If \(p>X\), \(m<X^2\), and
\[
 m=qp+r,\qquad0\le q,r<p,
\]
then Apéry--Lucas gives
\[
 b_m\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Thus every hit prime divides the same characteristic-zero integer:
\[
 r\in Z_p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid b_m.
\tag{9.2}
\]
This is exact and genuinely cross-prime, but
\[
 \log b_m=\Theta(m),
\]
and \(m\) may be of order \(X^2\). Height alone therefore permits \(O(X^2/\log X)\) window primes. It gives no useful all-index bound.

The short-return carrier in Section 4 is precisely the improvement available when the selected root has a second nonreflection return. On an isolated root, the carrier collapses back to the tall \(b_m\) scale.

## 9.2 Truncating the output index does not close the large part

Equation (9.2) gives
\[
 K_X(m)\log X\ll m.
\tag{9.3}
\]
Hence very small \(m\) are harmless. But a putative high load
\(K_X(m)\ge X^{14/15}\) only forces
\[
 m\gg X^{14/15}\log X,
\]
leaving nearly the whole interval \([0,X^2)\). The truncation is useful bookkeeping but creates no cross-prime saving on the surviving outputs.

## 9.3 Exceptional-output decomposition plus HM3 is insufficient

Suppose one grants the lattice-corrected third moment for the isolated bank. Then
\[
 \#\{m:K_X^{\mathrm{iso}}(m;H)\ge L\}
 \ll
 \frac{1+X^2\Lambda_X(H)^3}{L^3}X^{o(1)}.
\tag{9.4}
\]
This may make the exceptional set sparse, but it cannot exclude one exceptional output. Applying the tall carrier \(b_m\) separately to each exceptional output still permits \(O(X^2/\log X)\) primes at that output. Multiplying the carriers over the exceptional set only worsens the height.

A successful exceptional-output proof would need a new relation **between the exceptional outputs themselves**, or a small carrier attached to each high-load isolated output. Neither is currently available.

## 9.4 Entropy of a high load identifies exactly the fifth-order gap

At an output with isolated load \(L\), there are
\[
 (L)_3
\]
ordered triples and
\[
 (L)_5=(L)_3(L-3)(L-4)
\tag{9.5}
\]
ordered five-stars. A rank-three estimate controls only the unweighted triple count. The high-load tail needs the additional quadratic weight
\[
 (L-3)(L-4).
\]
Any attempt to insert that weight by Cauchy--Schwarz, Hölder, or a Palm reweighting recreates a fifth moment or requires a pointwise cap that is already the desired conclusion.

Thus the proposed gate is naturally described as a **quadratically amplified rank-three tail**, but this description does not prove it. The amplification is precisely the new arithmetic content.

## 9.5 The Franel/Hasse shell remains vertical

The committed Franel pullback is
\[
 K_p(x)^2
 =(1+x)^{p-1}A_p\left(\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x}\right)
 =\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r\Psi_{p,r}(x).
\tag{9.6}
\]
The basis \(\{\Psi_{p,r}\}\) is triangular, so
\[
 [K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,r}}=b_r.
\tag{9.7}
\]
The normalized square-root factorization and the rank-three K3/elliptic description are strong same-characteristic structures. They do not presently couple the five defining characteristics
\[
 p_1,\ldots,p_5

selected by one output \(m\). The basis in (9.7), the Hasse polynomial, and the coefficient field all vary with \(p\).
