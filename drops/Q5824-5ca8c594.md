For

\[

\[
N_{h+1}(X)=P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X),

F_h(X)=N_h(X),
\qquad
G_h(X)=1+(X+1)^6N_{h-1}(X+1).

\[
F_h(x)=G_h(x)=0

\[
\boxed{
N_h(x)=0,
\qquad
N_{h-1}(x)+Q_h(x)=0,
}
\qquad
Q_h(X):=\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(X+j)^6.
\tag{V}
\]

The equivalence follows from an exact Dodgson identity and the polynomial combination

\[
\boxed{
N_{h-1}(X)G_h(X)
 -(X+1)^6N_{h-2}(X+1)F_h(X)
 =N_{h-1}(X)+Q_h(X).
}
\tag{E}
\]

This is the main positive result of the present audit. It removes the awkward shift from the second equation without taking a determinant of a transfer minus the identity and without losing the fixed Apéry initial vector in the surrounding collision problem.

\[
\deg F_h^\circ=3(h-1)-\mathbf 1_{2\mid h},

\[
\rho_h(p,r)
 :=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p\,\text{admitted}}
   \min\{r,\operatorname{ord}_xF_h^\circ,
             \operatorname{ord}_xG_h\}

\tag{B}
\]

Thus the total possible multiplicity over \(h\le H\) is still

\[
\sum_{2\le h\le H}\rho_h(p,r)

For the Q5767 fixed-reference codegree insertion, this is the saturated exponent \(\kappa=2\), hence the same \(H^{3+o(1)}\) affine-energy line and the same \(N^{12/5+o(1)}\) lower-endpoint Q5605 contribution. ## 1. Exact transfer normalization and the two equations

### 1.1 Rational companion transfer

The canonical Apéry recurrence is

\[
(n+1)^3u_{n+1}=P(n)u_n-n^3u_{n-1}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For \(z_n=(u_n,u_{n+1})^t\), applying (1.1) at \(n+1\) gives

\[
z_{n+1}=M_nz_n,
\qquad
M_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-\dfrac{(n+1)^3}{(n+2)^3}&
 \dfrac{P(n+1)}{(n+2)^3}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The sign in the lower-left entry is negative and the shift in the middle coefficient is \(P(n+1)\). Also

\[
\det M_n=\frac{(n+1)^3}{(n+2)^3},
\]

which is the rational-transfer form of the Casoratian telescoping in `proof.tex` and `Wronskian.lean`.

On a nonwrapping interval \(0\le x<x+h<p\), every displayed denominator is a unit in characteristic \(p\). No equation below is obtained by clearing a factor that can vanish at the counted local root.

### 1.2 Polynomial-cleared recurrence

Put

\[
c_n=(n!)^3u_n.
\]

Then (1.1) becomes the denominator-free recurrence

\[
\boxed{c_{n+1}=P(n)c_n-n^6c_{n-1}.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Let

\[
\mathbf r_n=(D_n,C_n)
\]

be a normalized fundamental row for (1.3), so that an initial column \(w=(u_0,u_1)^t\) produces the corresponding cleared solution by \(\mathbf r_nw\). The distinguished Apéry column is retained literally as

\[
w_0=(1,5)^t.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The consecutive rows are a basis on the regular locus. Indeed their determinant obeys the cleared Casoratian recurrence

\[
\det(\mathbf r_n,\mathbf r_{n+1})
 =n^6\det(\mathbf r_{n-1},\mathbf r_n),
\]

so it is a fixed nonzero normalization constant times \((n!)^6\). For \(p\ge7\) and \(n<p\), the Q5767 structural and pole-unit masks make this determinant a unit, including in the local Artin ring used for jets.

### 1.3 Continuant bridge

Define \(N_0=0,N_1=1\) and the moving-start recurrence above. Induction on \(h\) in (1.3) gives the exact bridge

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf r_{x+h}
 =N_h(x)\mathbf r_{x+1}
  -(x+1)^6N_{h-1}(x+1)\mathbf r_x.
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The base case \(h=1\) is immediate. In the induction step, the coefficient of \(\mathbf r_{x+1}\) becomes

\[
P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x)=N_{h+1}(x),
\]

while the coefficient of \(\mathbf r_x\) becomes

\[
-(x+1)^6
 \left(P(x+h)N_{h-1}(x+1)
       -(x+h)^6N_{h-2}(x+1)\right)
=-(x+1)^6N_h(x+1).
\]

This verifies simultaneously the minus sign, the \(x+1\) shift, and the sixth power.

Subtracting \(\mathbf r_x\) from (1.5) gives

\[
\mathbf r_{x+h}-\mathbf r_x
 =F_h(x)\mathbf r_{x+1}-G_h(x)\mathbf r_x.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Because \(\mathbf r_x,\mathbf r_{x+1}\) are a basis, the exact unprojected row return

\[
\mathbf r_{x+h}=\mathbf r_x
\]

is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
F_h(x)=0,
\qquad
G_h(x)=0.
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

This is Q5767 (7.6)--(7.7). It is stronger than equality after applying \(w_0\), and much stronger than projective proportionality.

### 1.4 Local jets

Let \(R=\mathbf F_p[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^r)\), \(\epsilon=X-x\). The consecutive-row determinant remains a unit in \(R\), so (1.6) gives

\[
\mathbf r_{X+h}=\mathbf r_X\quad\text{in }R^2
\iff
F_h,G_h\in(\epsilon^r).
\tag{1.8}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\partial_X^{[j]}F_h(x)=
\partial_X^{[j]}G_h(x)=0,
\qquad 0\le j<r,
\tag{1.9}
\]

for Hasse derivatives. Thus the gcd multiplicity, not merely the number of distinct roots, is the correct local-level quantity.

Nothing here replaces the distinguished collision equation

\[
(P_\omega T_\omega-P_{\omega'}T_{\omega'})w_0=0
\]

by \(\det(T-I)=0\). The latter only says that some vector is fixed and is not used.

## 2. Tridiagonal/Dodgson identities

For \(h\ge2\), \(N_h(X)\) is the determinant of the \((h-1)\times(h-1)\) symmetric tridiagonal matrix with diagonal

\[
P(X+1),P(X+2),\ldots,P(X+h-1)
\]

and off-diagonal

\[
(X+2)^3,(X+3)^3,\ldots,(X+h-1)^3.
\]

Desnanot--Jacobi condensation applied to the next tridiagonal matrix gives, for every \(h\ge2\),

\[
\boxed{
N_{h+1}(X)N_{h-1}(X+1)
 -N_h(X)N_h(X+1)
 =-\prod_{j=2}^{h}(X+j)^6.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The sign can be checked already at \(h=2\):

\[
N_3(X)-N_2(X)N_2(X+1)=-(X+2)^6.
\]

Using (2.1) with \(h-1\) gives

\[
N_h(X)N_{h-2}(X+1)
 -N_{h-1}(X)N_{h-1}(X+1)
 =-\prod_{j=2}^{h-1}(X+j)^6.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Multiplying (2.2) by \((X+1)^6\) and inserting the definition of \(G_h\) yields the exact combination (E):

\[
N_{h-1}G_h
 -(X+1)^6N_{h-2}(X+1)F_h
 =N_{h-1}+Q_h.
\tag{2.3}
\]

There is also the forward companion identity

\[
\boxed{
N_{h+1}G_h
 -(X+1)^6N_h(X+1)F_h
 =N_{h+1}-Q_{h+1}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently, at every common root on the unit locus,

\[
N_{h-1}(x)=-Q_h(x),
\qquad
N_{h+1}(x)=Q_{h+1}(x).
\tag{2.5}
\]

These identities are exact, but their right sides still have degree \(6(h-1)\) and \(6h\). They do not produce a bounded-degree eliminant.

### 2.1 Scheme-theoretic same-point reduction

In a local ring in which \(Q_h\) is a unit, (2.3) proves

\[
(F_h,G_h)=(F_h,E_h),
\qquad
E_h:=N_{h-1}+Q_h,
\tag{2.6}
\]

in the sense relevant to the local quotient. Indeed \(E_h\in(F_h,G_h)\). Conversely, modulo \((F_h,E_h)\),

\[
N_{h-1}\equiv-Q_h
\]

is a unit, and (2.3) then gives \(G_h=0\). This argument works in \(\mathbf F_p[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^r)\), so no jet information is lost.

This is stronger and cleaner than merely saying that the two systems have the same set of reduced roots.

## 4. Exact gcd and resultant reduction

Let

\[
R_h^\circ:=\operatorname{Res}_X(F_h^\circ,G_h),
\qquad
S_h^\circ:=\operatorname{Res}_X(F_h^\circ,E_h),
\]

and

\[
A_h^\circ:=\operatorname{Res}_X(F_h^\circ,N_{h-1}).
\]

Because (2.3) says \(E_h\equiv N_{h-1}G_h\pmod{F_h^\circ}\), and because

\[
\deg E_h=\deg N_{h-1}+\deg G_h=6h-6,
\]

the product formula for resultants gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
S_h^\circ=A_h^\circ R_h^\circ.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

There is no unspecified sign or leading-coefficient correction in (4.1): it is simply multiplicativity of the values \(E_h(\alpha)=N_{h-1}(\alpha)G_h(\alpha)\) over the roots \(\alpha\) of \(F_h^\circ\), with matching total degrees.

The adjacent factor \(A_h^\circ\) is structural. On the canonical admitted locus it is a unit; its possible prime support is among the factorial/Apéry/leading-coefficient carrier split in `atom_tail_section.tex`. With

\[
V_{n+1}=34V_n-V_{n-1},
\qquad V_0=0,\quad V_1=1,
\]

the source carrier is

\[
\mathfrak U_H=\prod_{j\le H}j!\,b_j\,V_{j+1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore, for \(h\le H\) and \(p\nmid\mathfrak U_H\),

\[
v_p(S_h^\circ)=v_p(R_h^\circ).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently, after removing only the source-authorized structural prime powers, the shifted pair and the same-point pair have the same reduced resultant.

For even \(h\), the full resultant factors exactly as

\[
\operatorname{Res}(N_h,G_h)
 =\operatorname{Res}(2X+h+1,G_h)R_h^\circ,
\tag{4.4}
\]

where

\[
\operatorname{Res}(2X+h+1,G_h)
 =2^{3h}G_h\!\left(-\frac{h+1}{2}\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is the complete central removal. It is not permissible to delete any further factor merely because it appears repeatedly in finite data.

### 4.1 What is and is not determined uniformly

Let

\[
D_h^{(0)}=\gcd_{\mathbf Q[X]}(F_h^\circ,G_h).
\]

The exact reductions above determine the relevant ideal and the structural factorization of the resultant, but they do **not** prove

\[
D_h^{(0)}=1
\qquad\text{for every }h.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Exact symbolic computation gives \(D_h^{(0)}=1\) for \(1\le h\le7\). That is not an all-\(h\) proof. If (4.6) fails at some \(h\), then \(R_h^\circ=0\), and a resultant-prime argument cannot even begin. If (4.6) holds, modular common factors are apparitions supported on primes dividing the nonzero integer \(R_h^\circ\), after leading and structural factors are made units.

Thus the first all-\(h\) arithmetic gap is characteristic-zero coprimality; the next, quantitatively decisive gap is a uniform bound on the modular gcd degree.

## 6. Subresultants: the exact missing theorem

Let

\[
\operatorname{psc}_{h,j}
\]

be the \(j\)-th principal subresultant coefficient of \(F_h^\circ\) and \(E_h\), computed over \(\mathbf Z\) after primitive normalization. At a prime where the leading coefficients and structural factors are units,

\[
\deg\gcd_{\mathbf F_p[X]}(F_h^\circ,E_h)\ge t
\]

if and only if

\[
\operatorname{psc}_{h,0}\equiv\cdots\equiv
\operatorname{psc}_{h,t-1}\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus a concrete bounded-root target is

\[
\boxed{
\text{for }p>h,\ p\nmid\mathfrak U_H,
\quad
\gcd\bigl(\operatorname{psc}_{h,0},
          \operatorname{psc}_{h,1},
          \operatorname{psc}_{h,2}\bigr)
ot\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{SUB2}
\]

`SUB2` would imply

\[
\deg\gcd(F_h^\circ,G_h)\le2
\]

including multiplicity and therefore all retained jet levels. Small computations are consistent with this, but no proof of `SUB2` is present in the landed chain and none follows from the identities above.

The weakest statement giving any fixed saving is

\[
\boxed{
\deg\gcd_{\mathbf F_p[X]}(F_h^\circ,G_h)
 \ll h^{1-\delta}
}
\tag{SUB\(_\delta\)}
\]

for some fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly under the masks. This is the first quantitatively decisive arithmetic statement.

There are therefore two logically ordered missing assertions:

```text
AR0:     gcd_Q(F_h^circ,G_h)=1 for every h.
SUB_delta: modular gcd degree is O(h^(1-delta)) outside U_H.
```

`AR0` makes the reduced resultant nonzero. `SUB_delta` supplies the saving. A mere height bound for the nonzero resultant does not.

## 7. Why Dodgson does not close a scalar recurrence in \(h\)

The same-point replacement suggests the resultant sequence

\[
\mathcal S_h=\operatorname{Res}
  \left(N_h,N_{h-1}+Q_h\right).
\]

However,

\[
E_{h+1}=N_h+Q_{h+1},
\]

and modulo \(E_{h+1}\), so that \(N_h=-Q_{h+1}=-(X+h)^6Q_h\), the recurrence gives

\[
N_{h+1}
\equiv-(X+h)^6
 \left(N_{h-1}+P(X+h)Q_h\right)
 \pmod{E_{h+1}}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The next resultant therefore introduces the new mixed boundary polynomial

\[
N_{h-1}+P(X+h)Q_h,
\]

not \(E_h=N_{h-1}+Q_h\). Repeating the step creates an expanding family of boundary coefficients. The scalar sequence \(\mathcal S_h\) does not close under the one Dodgson relation.

The exact Euclidean combination (2.3) is likewise not low degree: its remainder \(E_h\) has degree \(6h-6\), and ordinary division by \(F_h^\circ\) leaves degree up to \(\deg F_h^\circ-1=3h-4+O(1)\). The computed subresultant chains for \(2\le h\le7\) descend one degree at a time rather than exposing a fixed-degree terminal polynomial.

This does not prove that no deeper recurrence exists. It proves that neither the landed Dodgson identity nor the first Euclidean step supplies one. No all-\(h\) low-order recurrence for \(R_h^\circ\), \(\mathcal S_h\), or the first three principal subresultants was found or is present in the canonical source.

## 8. Quantitative effect on Q5767 and Q5605

Let

\[
K^{\rm row}_{\nu,H}(\omega)
\]

count only those separated partners of a fixed fully masked occurrence \(\omega\) for which the source-certified cancellation really leaves one contiguous unprojected row return of length \(h\). Bounded orientation/source/chart labels are included in the occurrence, as in Q5767.

If each such partner maps with bounded multiplicity to an admitted pair \((h,x)\), then

\[
K^{\rm row}_{\nu,H}(\omega)
 \ll H^{o(1)}\sum_{h\le H}\rho_h(p,r).
\tag{8.1}
\]

### 8.1 Current theorem

From (B),

\[
K^{\rm row}_{\nu,H}(\omega)\ll H^{2+o(1)}.
\]

Thus \(\kappa=2\) in Q5767's fibre ledger. Its inequality

\[
\sum_\nu w_\nu\sqrt{E_\nu}
 \ll H^{2+\kappa/2+o(1)}
\]

gives

\[
H^{3+o(1)}.
