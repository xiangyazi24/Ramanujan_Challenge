# Q933: cross fourth-order companion/Hecke contact audit

## Verdict

The proposed cross pairing does not currently produce a new forced scalar support law. The strongest conclusion available from the two banked fourth-order structures is a sharply scoped saturation result: after the required division by p^3, the first Laurent defect coordinate and the companion marked residue share the same grade-three Apéry residue, but their contact determinant has no structural reason to remove that residue. A nonzero Wronskian would require an additional independence theorem between the canonical companion tail and the divided Hecke defect.

Thus the outcome is option (3): a scoped transition theorem. The surviving range is precisely the cases where the divided Laurent defect has only one independent first-order coordinate.

## 1. Correct cross coordinates

Write the companion coordinate as

\[
X_{p,Q,r}=a_Q/b_Q \pmod p
\]

and define the divided Hecke defect expansion

\[
R_{p,Q,r}=R_0+R_1 q+R_2q^2+\cdots .
\]

The ordinary Hecke relation before division is rank one and cannot be used. After division by p^3 the possible information begins at

\[
(R_0,R_1,\ldots)\pmod p.
\]

The companion congruence gives

\[
p^3a_{Qp+r}=C_{p,Q}b_{Qp+r}+p^3a_r/b_Q\pmod {p^4}.
\]

The surviving target coordinate is therefore the normalized residue

\[
\rho_r=b_r/p\quad (\text{in the grade-three sense}).
\]

Both constructions see the same residue through different lifts.

## 2. Q=1 contact calculation

For Q=1 the companion coordinate reduces to

\[
X_{p,1,r}=a_1/b_1.
\]

The first divided defect coordinate has the form

\[
R_0=\alpha_1 X_{p,1,r}+\beta_1\rho_r,
\]

with coefficients determined by the exact level-six parametrization.

The candidate determinant is

\[
\Delta_1=\det\begin{pmatrix}
X_{p,1,r}&R_0\\
X'_{p,1,r}&R_1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The reflection constraint forces both rows to transform through the same involution on the residue class. Consequently the antisymmetric part cancels the shared grade-three coordinate. What remains is a regulator term involving the next canonical tail coefficient, not a universal multiple of b_r.

Therefore no identity

\[
\Delta_1=0 \iff p\mid b_r
\]

or

\[
\Delta_1\ne0 \text{ for every target }p\mid b_r
\]

follows from the existing laws.

## 3. General Q

For general Q the companion factor changes to

\[
X_{p,Q,r}=a_Q/b_Q,
\]

while the Hecke grid remains

\[
U_p\Omega_{Qp+r}=c_r(Qp+r)\Omega_Q\pmod {p^3}.
\]

After division, the defect coordinates have the shape

\[
R_i=\alpha_i(Q)X_{p,Q,r}+\beta_i(Q)\rho_r+T_i(Q,r),
\]

where T_i is the independent canonical tail contribution.

A two-coordinate determinant removes X only if

\[
\alpha_0\beta_1-\alpha_1\beta_0
\]

is a unit modulo p and simultaneously the T terms vanish or are controlled. The banked congruences do not imply this. In particular they only determine the rank-one quotient, not the transverse tail.

## 4. Saturation statement

For any fixed number k of divided Laurent coefficients, the currently proved information has the form

\[
(R_0,\ldots,R_{k-1})
 = M_Q(X_{p,Q,r},\rho_r)+T_{k}(Q,r),
\]

where M_Q has rank at most two over the residue field and T_k is unconstrained by the two stated fourth-order laws.

Hence fixed k cannot yield a universal support law unless one proves a new independence statement eliminating T_k.

This is not an all-coordinate saturation theorem: it says only fixed finite coordinate systems are saturated by the available identities.

## 5. What additional input would close the gap

A sufficient new Apéry/modular input would be one of:

1. A nonvanishing transverse regulator theorem:

\[
\det(X_{p,Q,r},R_0,R_1)\not\equiv0\pmod p
\]

on every target prime.

2. A common-divisor theorem proving that the residual tail term T_k is divisible only by a sequence coprime to the grade-three residue.

3. A direct symbolic formula identifying the first two divided defect coefficients with independent canonical sequences.

Any of these would convert the cross structure into a stronger support carrier.

## Final answer

The companion/Hecke cross determinant is not yet a valid new carrier. The rank-one obstruction disappears after division by p^3, but the remaining contact matrix still contains an uncontrolled canonical tail. The exact transition theorem is:

**Fixed finite collections of divided Laurent coefficients plus the companion marked residue do not imply a stronger support law from the currently banked congruences alone. The remaining open regime is the first coordinate where an independently controlled transverse tail appears.**

No counterexample to the actual modular pairing is established; the failure is logical saturation of the available identities, not an abstract dimension argument.
