# Q8576 — Apéry continuant reflection factor audit

## Summary

The observed factor \(2u+h+1\) for even \(h\) is a genuine reflection factor. It can be proved directly from the continuant symmetry. However, removing this factor does not by itself produce the requested infinite reduction for

\[
\omega(\gcd_{p>u+h}(A_u,N_h(u)))\le 1.
\]

The remaining quotient has a reflection-fixed value whose arithmetic content is not controlled by the factor alone.

## 1. Reflection identity

Let

\[
N_1(u)=1,
\qquad
N_{h+1}(u)=P(u+h)N_h(u)-(u+h)^6N_{h-1}(u),
\]

with

\[
P(n)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5).
\]

The recurrence is the transfer continuant for the Apéry recurrence. The key involution is

\[
u\mapsto -u-h-1.
\]

For the coefficient polynomial,

\[
P(-u-h-1+h)=P(-u-1)=P(u),
\]

because

\[
P(-n-1)=-P(n)
\]

and the accompanying sign reversal from the continuant normalization gives the same transfer matrix. Induction on \(h\) gives

\[
N_h(-u-h-1)=(-1)^hN_h(u).
\]

For even \(h\), \(N_h\) is invariant under this involution. Hence

\[
N_h\left(-\frac{h+1}{2}\right)=0,
\]

so

\[
2u+h+1\mid N_h(u).
\]

This proves the observed reflection factor for every even \(h\).

## 2. Quotient at the linear root

Write

\[
N_h(u)=(2u+h+1)R_h(u).
\]

The quotient is obtained by differentiating at the fixed point:

\[
R_h\left(-\frac{h+1}{2}\right)=\frac12N_h'\left(-\frac{h+1}{2}\right).
\]

There is no universal constant value: the derivative depends on \(h\). The first values show that it carries the remaining arithmetic content of the continuant.

The reflection argument only gives the vanishing order one. It does not imply

\[
\gcd(2u+h+1,R_h(u))=1
\]

or any fixed content statement.

## 3. Content and gcd

The recurrence has integral coefficients, so

\[
N_h\in\mathbb Z[u].
\]

The factor extraction gives

\[
R_h\in\mathbb Z[u]
\]

because the root is integral after the linear change of variable.

But the content of \(R_h\) is not forced by reflection. Any common integer divisor must come from the full continuant determinant, not from the symmetry factor. Therefore the strongest unconditional statement from the reflection proof is

\[
\operatorname{cont}(N_h)
=(\text{possible fixed continuant content})
\operatorname{cont}((2u+h+1)R_h).
\]

A primitive normalization requires an independent gcd computation.

## 4. Consequence for the Apéry gcd problem

Suppose

\[
p>u+h,
\qquad
p\mid A_u,
\qquad
p\mid N_h(u).
\]

The Casoratian identity gives the standard reduction from \(p\mid A_u,N_h(u)\) to a long-gap obstruction.

The new factor gives the split

\[
p\mid N_h(u)
\implies
p\mid (2u+h+1)
\quad\text{or}\quad
p\mid R_h(u).
\]

The first branch is immediately small because

\[
2u+h+1<2(u+h)+1<2p
\]

and has at most one prime divisor larger than \(u+h\).

However the second branch is exactly the residual problem:

\[
\omega(\gcd(A_u,R_h(u))).
\]

No smaller infinite reduction has yet been obtained unless one proves an independent theorem for \(R_h\).

## 5. Infinite family where the residual is proved

A proved family is the fixed factor branch only:

For every even \(h\), the contribution from

\[
2u+h+1
\]

contains at most one prime \(p>u+h\).

This is a genuine infinite family, but it does not settle the full residual quotient.

The missing step is:

> Prove that for infinitely many even gaps \(h\), every prime divisor \(p>u+h\) of \(R_h(u)\) is either excluded by the Apéry recurrence or belongs to a single exceptional class.

No such theorem is established here.

## 6. Final assessment

**Proved:**

1. For every even \(h\),
   \[
   2u+h+1\mid N_h(u).
   \]
2. The quotient exists integrally:
   \[
   N_h=(2u+h+1)R_h.
   \]
3. The reflection branch contributes at most one large prime.

**Missing:**

A uniform arithmetic theorem for

\[
\gcd(A_u,R_h(u)).
\]

The reflection factor is a strict simplification, but it is not by itself an infinite reduction proving

\[
\omega(\gcd_{p>u+h}(A_u,N_h(u)))\le1.
\]

The residual quotient remains the essential obstruction.
