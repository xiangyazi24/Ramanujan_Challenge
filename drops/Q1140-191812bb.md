# Primary-source audit of higher Apéry congruences

## Normalization

I use the classical Apéry numbers

\[
 b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n {n\choose k}^2 {n+k\choose k}^2 .
\]

Some papers use the Franel-like normalization of the Apéry differential equation or the sequence \(A(n)\) with the same coefficients after a change of basis. The conversion relevant here is that the sequence with initial values

\[
1,5,73,1445,33001,819005,\ldots
\]

is the one denoted \(b_n\) here.

## 1. The classical r=0 congruence

### Gessel (1982)

Primary source:

M. Gessel, "Some congruences for Apéry numbers", *Journal of Number Theory* 14 (1982), 362--368.

DOI: 10.1016/0022-314X(82)90028-0.

The basic result is the prime congruence

\[
 b_{pn}\equiv b_n\pmod {p^3}
\]

for primes \(p\ge5\).

This is the strongest classical single-step Apéry supercongruence and is the result usually called the Gessel congruence.

It is an r=0 statement. It does not give a formula for \(b_{pN+r}\) with \(r\ne0\).

## 2. Beukers' formulation

Primary source:

F. Beukers, "Some congruences for the Apéry numbers", *Journal of Number Theory* 21 (1985), 141--155.

DOI: 10.1016/0022-314X(85)90012-4.

Beukers gives another proof and interpretation through the Apéry recurrence and associated modular objects. The congruence obtained is again the p-multiple case:

\[
 b_{pn}\equiv b_n\pmod {p^3}.
\]

It does not contain the shifted residue term needed for a complete \(pN+r\) expansion.

## 3. Higher powers p^m

A Dwork-style formulation appears in:

Z.-W. Sun, "On congruences for Apéry numbers", *Electronic Research Announcements in Mathematical Sciences* 16 (2009), 1--10.

and related papers by Sun on Apéry-like sequences.

The typical strengthened form is a tower congruence of the shape

\[
 b_{p^m n}\equiv b_{p^{m-1}n}\pmod {p^{3m}}
\]

under the hypotheses stated in the individual theorem (prime \(p\ge5\), integer \(m\ge1\), and with the usual Apéry sequence).

These results strengthen the r=0 tower only. They do not provide residue-class corrections.

## 4. Dwork/formal-group generalizations

The formal-group viewpoint treats the Apéry recurrence as a unit-root congruence. Examples include:

- C. Krattenthaler and T. Rivoal, works on Apéry-like supercongruences;
- L. Long, "Hypergeometric evaluation identities and supercongruences" and related papers on Calabi--Yau cases.

These generalize the mechanism behind

\[
A(pn)/A(n)
\]

being p-adically controlled. However, the precise theorem statements depend on the particular hypergeometric family and normalization. They are not blanket statements for every residue class \(r\).

## 5. General b_(pN+r) modulo p^2 and p^3

The literature has many residue-class congruences for special residues or special Apéry-like families, but there is no standard published theorem giving the full arbitrary-residue identity

\[
 b_{pN+r}\pmod {p^3}
\]

with all carry corrections for arbitrary \(N,r\).

The known mod-p^2 formulas for arbitrary residues are closer in spirit to Lucas-type congruences:

\[
 b_{pN+r}\equiv b_N b_r\pmod {p^2}

after including the known first harmonic correction terms.
\]

These do not automatically lift to p^3.

## 6. Does the literature control the one-carry rows?

No cited classical theorem above controls the one-carry rows required for a complete

\[
 b_{pN+r}\pmod {p^3}
\]

expansion.

Reason:

- Gessel and Beukers control the residue-zero slice \(r=0\).
- Dwork/formal-group results control p-adic unit-root behavior and often imply towers of the form \(p^mN\).
- The one-carry rows arise from terms where one of the binomial factors has valuation one, so that after squaring it contributes at order \(p^2\). These terms are invisible in a theorem stated only as \(b_{pN}\) versus \(b_N\).

Therefore the known theorems cannot be used as a replacement for an explicit Kummer carry decomposition of the \(r\ne0\), mod-p^3 problem.

## 7. Summary table

| Result | Modulus | Scope | Controls one-carry rows? |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Gessel congruence | p^3 | b_(pN) vs b_N | No |
| Beukers congruence | p^3 | b_(pN) vs b_N | No |
| Dwork/formal-group towers | higher p powers in r=0 tower | b_(p^mN) | No |
| residue-class Lucas-type congruences | usually p^2 | b_(pN+r) | Only first-order residue corrections |
| full p^3 shifted expansion | p^3 | arbitrary r | No published general theorem identified |

## Conclusion

The strongest securely identified published congruences are the Gessel/Beukers p^3 congruence for the zero-residue case and later p-adic tower generalizations. They do not solve the missing one-carry contribution problem for a full arbitrary-residue \(b_{pN+r}\) modulo p^3 formula. A new proof must explicitly analyze the valuation-one binomial rows rather than invoke the classical supercongruence.
