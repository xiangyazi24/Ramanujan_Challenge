# Verdict

The determinant family is a useful necessary obstruction, but it does not currently prove the desired bound
\[
J_X=o(X/\log^3 X).
\]
More precisely, the three determinants can force strong restrictions when they are nonzero, but the available Apéry identities do not couple the three characteristics strongly enough to turn those restrictions into a logarithmic saving. The cases $D_i=0$ are the decisive obstruction: they collapse the determinant divisibility argument into an identity between two Apéry values and can retain one free translation parameter.

The correct audit is therefore:

* nonzero $D_i$ gives a genuine divisor-packing condition;
* the three conditions $p_ip_i'\mid D_i$ cannot presently be combined into a bounded-multiplicity theorem;
* $D_i=0$ must be separated and treated by recurrence/Casoratian identities;
* no theorem currently rules out an $O(X)$-sized family from this determinant mechanism alone.

# 1. Determinant divisibility

Fix a full-defect label with defects $d_i$ and signs. A fibre point gives
\[
N-d_i=c_ip_i,
\qquad
p_i\mid B_{u_i(t)},
\]
with $X<p_i\le2X$.

For two fibre points $(t,N)$ and $(t',N')$, define
\[
D_i=(N'-d_i)B_{u_i(t)}-(N-d_i)B_{u_i(t')}.
\]
Because
\[
p_i\mid N-d_i,\quad p_i\mid B_{u_i(t)},
\]
and similarly for the primed point,
\[
p_ip_i'\mid D_i.
\]
Thus every collision consumes a pair of shell primes dividing an explicit integer.

The naive hope is that three such integers force the collision multiplicity to be bounded. This requires an upper bound on the gcd structure of the three $D_i$, not merely their separate divisibility.

# 2. Nonzero determinant case

Assume $D_i\ne0$. Since
\[
|N|,|N'|\ll X^2,
\]
and Apéry growth satisfies
\[
\log B_n=O(n),
\]
one has
\[
\log |D_i|\ll X^2.
\]
Therefore the number of shell prime pairs dividing $D_i$ is bounded only by
\[
\omega_{(X,2X]}(D_i)\ll \frac{X^2}{\log X}.
\]
This is far too weak. The required saving would need a bound comparable to $O(\log^C X)$ choices per fibre point.

The fact that there are three determinants does not immediately improve this. The prime pairs in $D_1,D_2,D_3$ are attached to different shifted Apéry indices
\[
u_i(t),\nu_i(t'),
\]
so one needs a theorem saying that the same two translations cannot simultaneously make three unrelated determinant integers highly divisible.

The standard recurrence identities do not provide this.

# 3. Casoratian audit

The Apéry recurrence gives the exact Casoratian relation
\[
a_nb_{n+1}-a_{n+1}b_n=-\frac6{(n+1)^3}.
\]
Equivalently, transfer matrices control linear dependence of adjacent states.

This is useful when two indices are close. However $u_i(t)$ and $u_i(t')$ are folded residues modulo different shell primes. Their differences are not a single fixed shift:
\[
u_i(t')-\nu_i(t)
\]
depends on $p_i$ and the folding sign. Hence the Casoratian gives no uniform divisor bound for
\[
B_{u_i(t)}B_{u_i(t')}
\]
with the required cross-prime information.

The same issue occurs for rank-two transfer matrices. A based $p_i$-step matrix lives over $\mathbf F_{p_i}$; the three primes give three independent characteristics. CRT combines them as a product and creates no new relation among the three local states.

# 4. The singular case $D_i=0$

This case cannot be ignored.

The equality
\[
(N'-d_i)B_{u_i(t)}=(N-d_i)B_{u_i(t')}
\]
means the two rational ratios agree:
\[
\frac{N'-d_i}{N-d_i}
=rac{B_{u_i(t')}}{B_{u_i(t)}}.
\]

There are two subcases.

## 4.1 Equal Apéry values

If
\[
B_{u_i(t')}=B_{u_i(t)},
\]
then also
\[
N'=N
\]
unless the common value is zero in the relevant integer relation. This is the rigid subcase. It can be attacked using monotonicity of positive Apéry numbers, but only after lifting the congruence information to an integer equality.

## 4.2 Genuine ratio equality

If the two Apéry values differ, then $D_i=0$ says that the translation ratio exactly matches an Apéry ratio. The recurrence does not forbid this: Apéry values form a rapidly growing but highly structured sequence, and no known theorem gives the required rational independence.

Therefore $D_i=0$ is not eliminated by the usual recurrence package.

# 5. Can divisor packing give the required logarithmic saving?

A divisor-packing argument would need something like:

For every fixed label and every fibre point $(t,N)$, the number of other points satisfying all three determinant conditions is
\[
O(\log^{-4}X).
\]

The determinant family only proves that every collision produces divisors of three integers. Since each $D_i$ can have $\gg X/\log X$ shell divisors in principle, the divisor bound alone gives no improvement over linear multiplicity.

To obtain the desired result one would need an additional theorem of one of the following forms:

1. **Cross-determinant gcd bound:**
\[
\gcd(D_1,D_2,D_3)\ll X^{o(1)}.
\]

2. **Apéry ratio rigidity:** a theorem bounding solutions of
\[
\frac{B_a}{B_b}=\frac{N-d_i}{N'-d_i}
\]
for folded indices.

3. **Prime-consumption theorem:** every additional fibre point consumes a new shell prime from a set of size $o(X/\log^3X)$.

None follows from the known recurrence, reflection, Lucas congruence, or Casoratian identities.

# 6. Negative conclusion about this determinant route

The precise obstruction is not that determinants are useless. They provide a correct collision certificate:
\[
(p_1p_1',p_2p_2',p_3p_3')\mid(D_1,D_2,D_3).
\]
The obstruction is that the map
\[
(t,N,t',N')\longmapsto(D_1,D_2,D_3)
\]
does not have a known height-to-divisor conversion strong enough to beat $O(X)$.

In particular:

* the nonzero determinant case loses because $|D_i|$ has exponential-in-$X^2$ height;
* the zero determinant case loses because it becomes an Apéry ratio equation;
* Casoratian identities are local in the index difference and do not couple the three shell characteristics;
* CRT does not create extra dependence between the three prime conditions.

Therefore the requested $o(X/\log^3X)$ bound is not presently derivable from the determinant family alone. A successful proof needs a genuinely new ingredient: a global constraint on simultaneous Apéry ratios or a stronger prime-consumption principle.
