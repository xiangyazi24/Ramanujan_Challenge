# Triple tensor monodromy check for the Apéry K3 argument

## Short answer

The representation-theoretic decomposition is correct, but the conclusion is not yet enough to prove (HM)_3. The statement

\[
V^{\otimes 3}=\operatorname{Sym}^3 V\oplus V\oplus V
\]

for the standard representation of \(SL_2\) is correct. In particular there is no copy of the trivial representation. However, applying Deligne's Riemann hypothesis requires more than checking the abstract fiber representation: one must check the actual geometric monodromy representation of the relevant three-variable sheaf, including possible diagonal identifications, twists, duals, and constant pieces arising from the Fourier transform construction.

If all those checks succeed, then square-root cancellation for the triple correlation is plausible, but the claimed bound must be summed carefully over primes and translated into the factorial moment estimate.

## 1. The SL(2) tensor calculation

Let \(V\) be the two-dimensional standard representation. Clebsch-Gordan gives

\[
V\otimes V=\operatorname{Sym}^2V\oplus\wedge^2V.
\]

For \(SL_2\), \(\wedge^2V\cong \mathbf 1\). Hence

\[
V^{\otimes3}=V\otimes(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\oplus\mathbf1).
\]

Again by Clebsch-Gordan,

\[
V\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2V=\operatorname{Sym}^3V\oplus V.
\]

Therefore

\[
V^{\otimes3}=\operatorname{Sym}^3V\oplus V\oplus V.
\]

The dimensions are \(4+2+2=8\), and every irreducible summand has positive highest weight. Thus

\[
(V^{\otimes3})^{SL_2}=0.
\]

So the purely representation-theoretic assertion is correct.

## 2. Why this does not immediately prove (HM)_3

The correlation is not literally an integral of three copies of the same fixed representation:

\[
\sum_m t_p(m)t_q(m)t_r(m).
\]

The sheaves live over different finite fields and arise after a Mellin/Fourier transform. The relevant object is a tensor product of pullbacks of local systems. One must prove that its geometric monodromy group is the product expected by independence:

\[
G_p\times G_q\times G_r,
\]

and that no accidental invariant appears after restriction to the common parameter space.

The dangerous cases are:

1. hidden rank-one Tate pieces;
2. self-duality twists causing \(V\otimes V\) pairings;
3. exceptional geometric isomorphisms between the three local systems;
4. diagonal monodromy if the three sheaves are not genuinely independent.

The absence of invariants in \(V^{\otimes3}\) for one copy of \(SL_2\) does not by itself exclude these phenomena.

## 3. If the sheaf-theoretic hypotheses hold

Assume one proves:

* each Apéry trace sheaf is geometrically irreducible of rank 2;
* geometric monodromy is exactly \(SL_2\);
* the three pullbacks are geometrically independent;
* the triple tensor has no constant subsheaf.

Then Deligne's theorem gives a square-root estimate of the form

\[
|C(p,q,r)|\ll (pqr)^{1/2}X
\]

(up to conductor factors).

This is a strong estimate for an individual triple.

## 4. The remaining summation obstruction

The third factorial moment is

\[
\sum_m(K(m))_3=\sum_{p,q,r}\sum_m \delta_p(m)\delta_q(m)\delta_r(m).
\]

After subtracting the main term, the error becomes

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}C(p,q,r).
\]

With \(p,q,r\sim X\), the individual bound gives roughly

\[
|C(p,q,r)|\ll X^{5/2}.
\]

There are about

\[
(X/\log X)^3
\]

triples, so absolute summation gives an error around

\[
X^{11/2}/(\log X)^3,
\]

which is far too large compared with the desired \(X^2\lambda^3\).

Therefore Deligne square-root cancellation for each triple is not enough. One needs cancellation in the prime-triple sum as well, or a much sharper bound after averaging over primes.

## 5. What would actually prove (HM)_3

The missing theorem is closer to an averaged trace estimate:

\[
\sum_{p,q,r\in(X,2X]} C(p,q,r)
\ll X^2\lambda^3.
\]

This requires a second layer of cancellation beyond Deligne. Possible mechanisms:

* large sieve for trace functions;
* independence of Frobenius conjugacy classes across primes;
* Katz-style equidistribution of the monodromy group;
* a Bombieri-Vinogradov type theorem for the family of Apéry trace functions.

## Conclusion

The tensor-product calculation is correct:

\[
V^{\otimes3}=\operatorname{Sym}^3V\oplus V^{\oplus2},
\]

so there is no invariant vector for a single \(SL_2\) monodromy group. This removes one major obstruction.

However, it does not by itself prove (HM)_3. The true missing step is not the absence of a trivial representation inside \(V^{\otimes3}\); it is an averaged three-prime trace estimate controlling the sum over \((p,q,r)\). The Apéry K3 monodromy result is therefore a necessary ingredient, but not the complete proof of the triple moment bound.
