## 6. Why gcds and resultants do not beat (4.4+)/(5.4-)

### 6.1 The strongest immediate mixed gcd

For either sign, write the three actual Apery values on the triangle as

```text
A = b_u,
B = b_(x_i),
C = b_(x_j).
```

Then selectedness gives

```text
q   | gcd(B,C),
p_i | gcd(A,B),
p_j | gcd(A,C).
```

Hence

```text
q*p_i*p_j | gcd(A*B, A*C, B*C).                   (6.1)
```

This is a genuine mixed gcd divisor.

But for arbitrary integers `A,B,C`, prime by prime,

```text
gcd(A*B,A*C,B*C)
  | gcd(A,B)*gcd(A,C)*gcd(B,C).                   (6.2)
```

Indeed, if the three valuations are `a,b,c`, the left valuation is

```text
min(a+b,a+c,b+c),
```

while the right valuation is

```text
min(a,b)+min(a,c)+min(b,c),
```

and the latter is at least the former after ordering `a,b,c`.

Thus the mixed gcd is controlled by the product of the three pairwise gcd channels. In the present triangle those are exactly

```text
one q-return channel,
two companion reflection channels.
```

The q-channel is the banked short continuant. Each companion channel is the forced midpoint factor from Section 3. No fourth channel appears.

### 6.2 Resultants

A resultant can help only if, after forced factors are removed, two polynomial conditions still have a common marked prime/root constraint.

For a companion return,

```text
N_t(X) = (2*X+t+1)*R_t(X).
```

At the physical base the first factor equals `p_sigma`. Therefore:

```text
resultant using N_t:
  companion factor is tautological;

resultant using R_t:
  selectedness supplies no p_sigma divisibility.          (6.3)
```

A resultant between a q-return polynomial and a reduced companion quotient also has no same-prime common-root hypothesis: the marked primes are `q` and `p_sigma`, which are distinct.

So the resultant route has exactly the same dichotomy as the determinant route: keep the geometric midpoint factor and pay it, or remove it and lose the forced companion divisor.

### 6.3 Determinants

For three recurrence positions, (2.1) is the unique recurrence-universal linear dependence. Equations (4.4+) and (5.4-) are that dependence after every marked prime supplied by selectedness has been divided out.

Consequently any three-point determinant produced only from the banked second-order transfer algebra reduces to the same factorization, up to boundary units and normalization factors. It cannot supply a second independent reduced companion equation.

uniformly for `N<q<=2N`.



So neither the actual companion height nor the q-carrier height contradicts a star at the critical multiplicity scale.

# COUNTEREXAMPLE

## 9. Short-opposite-pair deletion gives no same-sign spacing theorem

Let `R_+(q),R_-(q)` be the surviving root sets after every opposite-sign pair of root gap at most

```text
D = floor(sqrt(N))
```

has been deleted.

The strongest direct consequence of the deletion rule is a cross-sign statement:

```text
r_+ in R_+(q), r_- in R_-(q)
  => |r_+-r_-| > D,                                (9.1)
```

assuming, as in the question, that all such opposite pairs have been removed.

There is no analogous conclusion for two roots in `R_+(q)` or two roots in `R_-(q)`.

The only unconditional within-sign lower bound supplied by the physical definition is parity:

```text
r_i == u (mod 2)
  => c_i = r_(i+1)-r_i >= 2 and c_i even.          (9.2)
```

In particular, deletion does **not** imply

```text
c_i > D,
c_i >> D,
span(R_sigma) >> D*|R_sigma|,
```

or any other `D`-scale within-sign separation.

### Exact physical counterfixture to the deletion inference

This is a counterfixture to a **geometric/deletion implication**, not an assertion of actual Apery selectedness.

Take only plus arms. Then there are no opposite-sign pairs, so the deletion operation removes nothing, regardless of how tightly the plus roots are packed.

The prime geometry itself permits a dense one-sign star. For a fixed large prime `q`, every prime

```text
(q+1)/2 < p < q
```

defines a legitimate plus-arm root

```text
r = p - (q+1)/2,
```

because then

```text
p = u+1+r = p_+(q,r),
0 < r < u,
r == u (mod 2)
```

(the last parity relation is automatic because `q,p` are odd, so `q-p` is even).

By the ordinary PNT there are `Theta(q/log q)` such geometric companion primes. Partition `(q/2,q)` into intervals of length `D`. At least one interval contains

```text
>> D/log q
```

of them. The corresponding plus roots lie in a root interval of length at most `D`. Hence the physical prime geometry plus the deletion rule allow a one-sign star with


## 10. Saturation theorem

Here is the precise scope in which the mechanism is exhausted.

Consider arguments generated by the following inputs only:

```text
(A) the actual Apery recurrence at a marked prime;
(B) the banked principal endpoint-return continuant at that prime;
(C) Apery reflection and continuant reflection;
(D) the one-digit Cartier/Lucas law in the physical range;
(E) Casoratian/transfer/addition identities among those continuants;
(F) integer gcd/resultant/determinantal elimination of those identities;
(G) the banked q-carrier height bound;
(H) product_i p_i | b_u;
(I) deletion of opposite-sign pairs at gap <=D.
```

The prompt states, and Q2125 audited, that `(A)-(E)` are principal at one marked prime.

### Theorem 10.1 -- actual-star prime-local saturation

For the actual star geometry above, `(A)-(I)` have the following properties.

1. At the center prime `q`, two selected roots give exactly the one principal q-return condition, represented by `U_c` (or the committed `N_c` before normalization).

2. At a companion prime `p_sigma`, the two incident zero positions are `u` and `x_sigma` with

```text
u+x_sigma = p_sigma-1.
```

The corresponding return continuant has the forced factor

```text
2*X+t+1,
```

which evaluates to `p_sigma`. After that factor is removed, `(A)-(E)` provide no second companion divisibility condition.

3. For two same-sign arms, the unique three-point transfer relation is (4.2) or (5.2). After all marked prime factors are extracted, it is exactly (4.4+) or (5.4-):

```text
reduced mixed determinant
  = boundary
    * (reduced q-carrier/q)
    * (b_u/(p_i*p_j)).                            (10.1)
```

4. Consequently, any further companion prime seen by this determinant is present because it already divides `b_u/(p_i*p_j)`. A resultant that retains the full companion return sees only the forced midpoint factor; a resultant after midpoint reduction has no forced companion root. Gcd compression is bounded by the product of the three pairwise channels as in (6.2).

5. Therefore `(A)-(I)` do not produce a nonzero mixed integer carrier whose forced marked-prime divisor contains the q-return multiplicity and the companion support but whose archimedean height is uniformly smaller, by an unbounded factor, than the sum of the banked q-carrier height and the one-copy `b_u` companion height.

**Proof.** Items 1-2 are the banked principal-return theorem plus the exact midpoint factorization (3.2)-(3.4). Item 3 is the exact characteristic-zero transfer identity with the marked primes divided out. Item 4 follows literally from the right side of (10.1), together with the gcd valuation inequality and the resultant dichotomy (6.3). Item 5 follows because every candidate in the stated generated class either keeps one of these prime-local generators, multiplies them, or eliminates them through the unique transfer relation; (10.1) shows that this elimination factorizes through the old two quotients instead of creating a new reduced prime-support equation. QED.

This is a no-go for the **specified mechanism**, not for all possible arithmetic facts about the actual Apery sequence.

The first object outside its scope is exactly a cross-modulus prime-support correlation: how often two distinct affine companion primes simultaneously divide the same actual `b_u` while the associated two roots simultaneously lie in the q-zero set.

# Smallest stronger arithmetic input

## 11. `[STAR-SAME2]`: an actual-selected same-sign pair estimate

Define the raw, pre-deletion actual selected counts

```text
a_sigma(q)
  = # { r :
        0 <= r < u,
        r == u (mod 2),
        p_sigma(q,r) is prime,
        q | b_r,
        p_sigma(q,r) | b_u },

u = (q-1)/2,
sigma in {+,-}.                                    (11.1)
```

This is not a reformulation of `Xi_N`: it is a raw second-order statistic before deletion.

Define

```text
S_same2(N)
  = sum_{N<q<=2N, q prime}
      ( binom(a_+(q),2) + binom(a_-(q),2) ).        (11.2)
```

The first genuinely stronger arithmetic estimate is

```text
[STAR-SAME2]

for every eps>0 there exists N_0(eps) such that
for all N>=N_0(eps),

S_same2(N) <= eps*N/loglog N.                       (11.3)
```

Equivalently,

```text
S_same2(N) = o(N/loglog N).                         (11.4)
```

### Why this closes the gate

Let `w_+(q),w_-(q)` be the post-deletion counts. Then

```text
w_sigma(q) <= a_sigma(q).                           (11.5)
```

For every integer `a>=0`,

```text
(a-1)_+ <= binom(a,2).                              (11.6)
```

Using (8.1), (11.5), and (11.6),

```text
(w(q)-1)_+
 <= binom(a_+(q),2)
    + binom(a_-(q),2)
    + 1.                                            (11.7)
```

The final `+1` is needed only when both surviving signs are nonempty. Summing and using the prime count gives

```text
Xi_N
 <= S_same2(N) + O(N/log N).                        (11.8)
```

Therefore `[STAR-SAME2]` implies

```text
Xi_N = o(N/loglog N).                               (11.9)
```

### Why it is genuinely stronger, not equivalent

The desired gate controls a first excess. `[STAR-SAME2]` controls a quadratic same-sign multiplicity.

Combinatorially the implication cannot be reversed. For example, a profile with one center having

```text
w_+(q) ~ sqrt(N/loglog N)
```

and all other centers empty contributes

```text
Xi_N ~ sqrt(N/loglog N)
     = o(N/loglog N),
```

but contributes

```text
binom(w_+(q),2) ~ N/(2*loglog N),
```

which is not little-oh at the `[STAR-SAME2]` scale.

So (11.3) is a strictly stronger arithmetic statement.

### What new arithmetic it asks for

Expanding one summand of (11.2), `[STAR-SAME2]` asks for cancellation/sparsity in tuples

```text
(q, r, s, p_sigma(q,r), p_sigma(q,s))
```

with

```text
r != s,
r == s == u (mod 2),
q | gcd(b_r,b_s),
p_sigma(q,r)*p_sigma(q,s) | b_u,
```

and both affine companions prime.

The q-side is seen by the short continuant. The companion side is seen by `b_u`. What is missing is the **joint frequency** of those two actual prime-support events. The exact mixed determinant of Sections 4-5 does not control that frequency because it factorizes through their product.

This is the smallest new arithmetic layer I can justify after the saturation theorem: a same-sign two-arm correlation, not another one-arm carrier identity and not the original first-excess gate in different notation.
