# 7. Why the direct deep attacks stop

This section is deliberately restricted to the actual residual `(RES)`.

## 7.1 The fixed two-value gcd has the wrong local height

Every plus leaf satisfies

\[
p\mid b_{n_q},b_{N_q},
\]

and every minus leaf satisfies

\[
p\mid b_{n_q},b_{s_q},
\]

after the fixed Lucas coefficient primes are removed. Thus all leaves of one state divide one fixed two-value gcd.

But

\[
\log b_m=\gamma m+O(\log(m+2)),
\qquad
\gamma=\log(17+12\sqrt2),
\]

so its elementary height is \(\Theta_J(T)\). This permits \(\gg Y/\log T\) prime divisors in one \(Y\)-cell. The fact that the same upper prime is singleton removes no foreign divisor from this estimate.

## 7.2 Q4179's incomplete theorem does not apply to actual deep leaves

For one row and defining prime \(p\), the prefix sequence reaches its folded plateau at \(j_p(M)\). Every actual leaf in the cell is captured only after the fixed cutoff has reached that plateau:

\[
J_k(q,I)\ge j_p(M_k).
\]

On `(RES)`, both fixed cutoffs are deep and every actual leaf is saturated in both rows.

The Q4179 degree-\(4(h-1)\), leading-coefficient-\(h\) continuant theorem applies to two **strictly incomplete** zero cutoffs. It therefore controls phantom/incomplete divisor configurations, not these actual saturated leaves.

The first scalar prefix difference is

\[
\mathscr K_R(M)-\mathscr K_{R-1}(M)
=
\binom MR^2\binom{M+R}{R}^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Once \(R\) has crossed the folded remainder, the defining prime divides this increment for the universal Lucas-carry reason, whether or not the Apéry plateau value is zero. Saturating that carry factor removes exactly the divisibility one hoped to exploit. Hence finite differencing the plateau does not produce a primitive local radical.

## 7.3 The primitive pair carrier is nonzero but too large

For a same-state collision \(p'=p+h\), put

\[
r=r_6(p),\qquad a=r_\sigma(p),
\]

\[
r'=r-6h,
\qquad
a'=a-c_\sigma h,
\qquad c_+=13,\ c_-=1.
\]

The positive bilinear carrier from Q4185 is

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr B_{c_\sigma,h}(q,p)
:=b_r b_{a'}+b_a b_{r'}>0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Both primes divide it:

\[
pp'\mid\mathscr B_{c_\sigma,h}(q,p).
\tag{7.3}
\]

After every restart and denominator factor is saturated, the primitive content is still `(7.2)`. In the deep region,

\[
\log\mathscr B_{13,h}
=\gamma(r+a-6h)+O_J(\log T)=\Theta_J(T),
\]

\[
\log\mathscr B_{1,h}
=\gamma(r+a-h)+O_J(\log T)=\Theta_J(T).
\]

The small condition \(h<Y=o(T)\) changes only the lower-order term. Moreover the carrier depends on the pair, so multiplying it over all pairs in a rich cell pays the same quadratic multiplicity on both sides and gives no occupancy saving.

## 7.4 Continuant resultants have the wrong characteristic quantifier

Within one lower characteristic, two zeros \(x<x+d<p\) give

\[
p\mid N_d(x).
\]

For the second lower prime one obtains a different congruence in \(\mathbb F_{p'}\). A resultant between the two continuants would be a carrier only if both polynomials had a common root in one field. They do not: the two return conditions live in different CRT components

\[
\mathbb Z/(pp')\mathbb Z
\simeq
\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_{p'}.
\]

The long common recurrence block does not change this. Adjacent transfer minors cancel only to the Desnanot restart factor

\[
N_d(X)N_d(X+1)
-N_{d+1}(X)N_{d-1}(X+1)
=S_d(X)^6,
\]

which is a shell-prime unit after the prescribed saturation.

Thus the same-characteristic root-count argument behind Q4130 cannot be transplanted by replacing its one fixed modulus with the varying primes of a singleton cell.

## 7.5 There is no inverse-sieve input yet

An inverse sieve needs the set of candidate lower primes to occupy few residue classes modulo many auxiliary small primes. The condition

\[
p\mid\gcd(b_{n_q},b_{m_{q,\sigma}})
\]

places no residue-class restriction on \(p\bmod\ell\) for an unrelated small \(\ell\). An arbitrary subset of primes in a short interval can be the divisor set of some integer.

The two quotient-row equations merely reconstruct \((q,t_q)\); their degree-one eliminant

\[
c_\sigma r_6-6r_\sigma
=c_\sigma n_q-6m_{q,\sigma}
\]

holds for every geometrically legal point before any Apéry zero is imposed. It is a longitudinal kinematic factor and cannot seed an inverse sieve.

An Apéry-specific inverse sieve would therefore have to prove a new auxiliary-modulus law for the **primitive saturated radical**, not just invoke generic inverse-sieve machinery.

## 7.6 Fixed-shift and vertical moments do not self-normalize

For each fixed separation \(h\), the banked same-state injection gives an absolute bound at the lower \(S_2(T)\) scale. Summing over \(h<Y\) gives an absolute estimate involving \(Y S_2(T)\).

There is no proved lower bound

\[
M_{1,\sigma}(T)\gg T^\delta S_2(T)
\]

or any substitute. Dividing an absolute \(S_2\)-bound by the actual singleton mass would therefore use exactly the forbidden normalization. The singleton condition on \(q\) does not repair this quantifier.
