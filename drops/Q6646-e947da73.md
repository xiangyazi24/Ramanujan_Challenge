## 4. Exact second-factorial decomposition: `R_col` plus `R_end`

\tag{4.1}

Define

\[
 E_{\rm end}

\tag{4.2}

and

\[
 E_{\rm col}
 :=\sum_\kappa\sum_{J\ne J'}d_\kappa(J)d_\kappa(J').
\tag{4.3}

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_\kappa(F_\kappa)_2
 =E_{\rm end}+E_{\rm col}.

\tag{4.4}

- `P` is unordered, but the literal orientation/source labels remain in the atoms counted by `d_κ(J)`;

    +\sum_{J<J'}d_\kappa(J)d_\kappa(J').

Thus `(FM2)` is a legitimate way to avoid **separate theorem statements** for `R_col` and `R_end`. But it does not evade their content: it proves their nonnegative sum at once.

### 8.3 Consequence

Any proposed proof based only on projected diagonal moments must reverse `(4.7)`. The two packets show that no bounded constant can make that reversal true:

\[
\boxed{
 \text{projected column/endpoint moments}
 \not\Longrightarrow
 \text{full numerical-key high-fibre tail}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

To exclude both packets, a theorem must either:

1. prove separate column and endpoint controls; or
2. count full mixed literal tuples over `κ`, as `(HF2)` or `(FM2)` does.

There is no third interpolation furnished by the current interfaces.

## 9. The smallest high-fibre theorem

There are three useful formulations, ordered from logically weakest to more conventional.

### 9.1 Literal weakest consumer

Let

\[
 H_*=\left\lceil T^{2/5-2\eta}L^{-3/2}\right\rceil.
\]

The exact smallest direct statement is

> **`M21-WORDCV-NUMKEY-HF_eta`.**
> \[
> \boxed{
>  \sum_{\kappa=(m,P)}(F_\kappa-H_*)_+
>  \ll_J T^{27/10-2\eta+o(1)}L^{-3}.
> }
> \tag{9.1}
> \]

If the six residuals are stored separately, replace `F_κ` by their disjoint master sum. By `(2.5)` and `(2.6)`, `(9.1)` closes all six slots. This is the literal remaining consumer and is therefore equivalent to the strongest tail target, not a new explanatory theorem.

### 9.2 Smallest clean non-equivalent moment theorem

The narrowest standard stronger statement is the centered-square theorem

> **`M21-WORDCV-NUMKEY-HF2_eta`.**
> \[
> \boxed{
>  \sum_{\kappa=(m,P)}
>   (F_\kappa-H_*)_+^2
>  \ll_J T^{31/10-4\eta+o(1)}L^{-9/2}.
> }
> \tag{9.2}
> \]

Indeed, by Cauchy and the support theorem,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_\kappa(F_\kappa-H_*)_+
 &\le
 |\operatorname{supp}F|^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_\kappa(F_\kappa-H_*)_+^2\right)^{1/2}\\
 &\ll_J
 \left(T^{23/10}L^{-3/2}\right)^{1/2}
 \left(T^{31/10-4\eta}L^{-9/2}\right)^{1/2}\\
 &=T^{27/10-2\eta}L^{-3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Statement `(9.2)` is strictly stronger than `(9.1)`: a single extremely high fibre may satisfy the `L^1` budget but violate the centered `L^2` budget. It is strictly weaker than a global second moment because it ignores every fibre below `H_*`.

### 9.3 Conventional full factorial theorem

A simpler but stronger sufficient premise is

> **`M21-WORDCV-NUMKEY-FM2_eta`.**
> \[
> \boxed{
>  \sum_{\kappa=(m,P)}F_\kappa(F_\kappa-1)
>  \ll_J T^{31/10-4\eta+o(1)}L^{-9/2}.
> }
> \tag{9.4}
> \]

Equation `(4.4)` shows exactly what `(9.4)` contains:

```text
same-endpoint/source factorial energy
+
distinct orientation-marked endpoint-column energy.
```

Thus `(9.4)` is the clean single theorem that replaces separate `R_col` and `R_end` theorems. It is not derivable from the banked primitive, pair-column, or horizontal moments.

A fourth full-key factorial theorem would also suffice at the scale

\[
 \sum_\kappa(F_\kappa)_4
 \ll_J T^{39/10-8\eta+o(1)}L^{-15/2},
\tag{9.5}
\]

but it is strictly more than is needed. A fourth **horizontal** moment is not `(9.5)`.
