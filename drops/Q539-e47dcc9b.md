# Affine transfer proof strategy for the Apéry non-reflection pair-zero estimate

## Summary

The affine transfer approach is the right framework for turning the heuristic codimension-two picture into an average bound. The key point is that a pair of independent zeros is not merely two unrelated trace-zero events: it is a simultaneous vanishing condition on a two-point Frobenius/Kummer orbit. The relevant transfer object should therefore live on a two-copy affine state space.

The expected result is:

\[
\sum_{p\le N}D_p=O(\pi(N)),
\]

where

\[
D_p=\binom{u_p}{2}
\]

counts pairs of zero orbits not related by the reflection symmetry.

---

## (1) The affine transfer element

For a single index $r$, the Apéry congruence can be represented through the affine Frobenius transfer operator attached to the Cartier-diagonal realization. Schematically,

\[
T_r\in \mathrm{Aff}(V_p)
\]

encodes the action of Frobenius on the relevant period vector, with the zero condition

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\]

becoming membership in a distinguished affine hyperplane:

\[
T_r(v_0)\in H.
\]

For two indices $r,s$, introduce the product transfer

\[
T_{r,s}=T_r\times T_s
\]

acting on

\[
V_p\times V_p.
\]

The simultaneous zero event becomes

\[
(T_r(v_0),T_s(v_0))\in H\times H.
\]

The reflection pair $(r,p-1-r)$ is excluded because it is forced by the functional symmetry

\[
b_{p-1-r}=b_r.
\]

Thus the remaining event is genuinely a codimension-two intersection.

The affine transfer element is therefore the two-point Frobenius orbit element whose fixed affine conditions are exactly

\[
b_r=b_s=0,
\]

with the diagonal/reflection component removed.

---

## (2) Expected density and required equidistribution

The ambient codimension-two density predicted by the affine model is

\[
\frac1{p(p+1)}.
\]

The number of independent pairs is approximately

\[
\frac{p^2}{8},
\]

because:

* there are $p/2$ independent reflection orbits;
* choosing two gives about $p^2/8$ pairs.

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E[D_p]
\approx
\frac{p^2/8}{p(p+1)}
\approx \frac18.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{p\le N}D_p
\approx \frac18\pi(N),
\]

which is exactly the required scale.

To make this rigorous one needs an equidistribution theorem of the following form:

For the family of two-point affine Frobenius transfers

\[
\{T_{r,s}: r,s\in\mathbf F_p\},
\]

the proportion landing in the codimension-two affine subvariety

\[
H\times H
\]

must equal the Haar/geometric density plus an error whose total contribution over primes is $O(\pi(N))$.

A sufficient estimate would look like

\[
\sum_{r\neq s,\,s\neq p-1-r}
1_{b_r=b_s=0}
=
\frac{p^2}{8p(p+1)}+O(p^{1-\delta}).
\]

Any fixed $\delta>0$ would be enough after summing over primes in the P3.2 range.

---

## (3) Compact-group orbit sampling or defining-characteristic residual?

Pure compact-group Sato--Tate equidistribution is not enough.

Weyl integration and ergodic theory describe the distribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes after fixing the prime and varying the geometric parameter. They can predict the continuous USp(4) density, but they do not directly control the finite-field arithmetic event

\[
b_r\equiv b_s\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The missing step is the defining-characteristic residual problem:

* the parameter space is over $\mathbf F_p$;
* the Frobenius orbit is sampled in characteristic $p$;
* the zero condition is an algebraic condition on the actual mod-$p$ Apéry sequence.

Therefore the proof needs a finite-field equidistribution theorem, not only a compact Lie-group theorem.

The likely route is:

1. Use the Cartier diagonal to construct the algebraic family controlling $b_r$.
2. Construct the two-copy affine variety for $(r,s)$.
3. Remove the reflection diagonal component.
4. Apply Deligne-type bounds for the relevant sheaf/cohomology.
5. Obtain square-root cancellation for the nontrivial Frobenius traces.
6. Sum over primes.

The Sp(4) monodromy is important because it prevents hidden decompositions and supplies geometric irreducibility. The recurrence alone gives the local dynamics but not the required cancellation. The Cartier diagonal is the best entry point because it realizes the sequence as an algebraic family where the needed cohomological bounds are available.

---

## Final assessment

Among the known properties:

1. **Palindromic symmetry** removes the forced codimension-one component but does not prove cancellation.
2. **Gap polynomial bounds** help after the fact but do not naturally give pair dispersion.
3. **Sp(4) monodromy** supplies the genericity/irreducibility needed for a trace estimate.
4. **The recurrence** provides a concrete dynamical model but is difficult to use directly in two variables.
5. **Cartier diagonal representation** is the strongest route because it converts the problem into counting points on an algebraic variety.

The expected proof architecture is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Cartier diagonal} + \text{two-copy affine transfer}
+\text{Sp(4) monodromy}
+\text{Deligne bounds}
\Longrightarrow
\sum_pD_p=O(\pi(N)).
}
\]

This is a genuinely Apéry-specific argument; M1 alone cannot provide it for arbitrary moving targets.
