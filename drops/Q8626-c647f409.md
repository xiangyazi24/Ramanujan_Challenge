# Q8626 normalized Apéry p-block audit

## 1. Factorization and endpoint correction

The proposed factorization has the correct shape only after separating the singular endpoint factors. The matrices \(S_n\) satisfy

\[
\det S_n=n^3(n+1)^3.
\]

For an aligned block
\[
Q_q=S_{(q+1)p-1}\cdots S_{qp},
\]
the bottom-left recurrence factor contributes a factor \(p^3\), and the normalized matrix
\[
\widetilde Q_q=p^{-3}Q_q
\]
lies in \(M_2(\mathbf Z_p)\). Modulo \(p\), the normalized block is
\[
\widetilde Q_q\equiv -S_q\pmod p.
\]

The endpoint pieces cannot be treated as units before normalization. The factors \(L\) and \(R\) have exactly one singular diagonal direction:
\[
L=\widehat L\operatorname{diag}(1,p^3),\qquad
R=\operatorname{diag}(1,p^3)\widehat R,
\]
with hatted matrices in \(GL_2(\mathbf Z_p)\). Thus the total determinant valuation agrees with
\[
v_p(\det T)=3(2c)+\text{endpoint corrections},
\]
obtained directly from the product of the determinants of the \(S_n\). One must not infer invertibility of \(S_n\) across indices divisible by \(p\).

## 2. Normalized middle product

Write
\[
G=\widetilde Q_{c-1}\cdots \widetilde Q_1.
\]
The middle contribution has a possible extra scalar content only through entries of \(G\). The claimed exponent
\[
3(c-1)+t,\qquad t=\min(v_p(G_{11}),3),
\]
is the correct candidate only after the endpoint factors are removed. It is not a statement about the raw product \(T\) without this normalization.

The entry \(G_{11}\pmod p\) is governed by the companion recurrence. Since
\[
\widetilde Q_q\equiv -S_q\pmod p,
\]
the first component evolves by the primitive companion sequence \(H_n\), giving
\[
G_{11}\equiv (-1)^{c-1}H_c\pmod p.
\]
This is the only valid source of the scalar criterion.

## 3. The \(c=2,p=13\) check

Here
\[
H_0=0,\quad H_1=1,
\]
and
\[
H_2=P(1)H_1-1^6H_0=P(1).
\]
Because
\[
P(1)=34+51+27+5=117,
\]
we have
\[
H_2=117\equiv0\pmod{13}.
\]

Therefore the earlier assertion
\[
H_2\equiv5\pmod{13}
\]
was simply false; it came from evaluating the wrong recurrence coefficient (effectively confusing the Apéry \(B_n\) normalization with the companion \(H_n\) normalization).

For \(c=2\), the normalized middle block is already singular modulo 13, so the generic unit-layer statement does not apply.

## 4. First layer at an Apéry root

At a root \(B_u\equiv0\pmod p\), the state vector lies on the root line. The one-block nilpotent contribution reduces to a rank-one operator. After choosing the canonical basis this is an \(E_{21}\)-type line.

This implies:

* the first normalized layer is target-blind;
* it detects the existence of a root state, not the higher target condition;
* any target selectivity must enter through higher \(p\)-adic layers.

Hence the statement “first layer target-selective” is incorrect in the \(t<3\) case.

## 5. The \(t=3\) case

If
\[
v_p(G_{11})\ge3,
\]
then the middle product loses the first three visible layers. The next normalized scale is the factorial-scale \(p^{3c}\) layer. However, full rank does not follow merely from \(t=3\); it requires checking the remaining normalized determinant/minors. The determinant valuation alone cannot imply rank.

The corrected implication is:

\[
t<3\Rightarrow\text{possible rank-one first layer},
\]

but

\[
t=3\not\Rightarrow\text{automatic full rank without an additional minor calculation}.
\]

## 6. Audit of the \(p=19,c=13,u=8\) example

The data
\[
B_{13}\equiv10\pmod{19},
\]
while
\[
B_{255}\equiv0\pmod{19^2}
\]
does not contradict the normalized block calculation.

It only shows that the first residue digit \(c\) does not classify all second-order cancellations. The first layer depends on the root state and the normalized monodromy. If the first layer vanishes for an independent reason (for example a higher congruence in the middle product), then a second-order zero can occur even when the naive first-digit predictor is nonzero.

Therefore the example refutes the stronger but unjustified claim:

> the condition \(p^2\mid B_{cp+u}\) is determined solely by \(B_c\pmod p\).

It does not refute the normalized matrix factorization.

## Final correction

The valid conclusions are:

1. The block factorization requires endpoint normalization; raw matrices are not units at singular indices.
2. The extra content is controlled by the normalized middle product, not directly by \(T\).
3. \(G_{11}\equiv(-1)^{c-1}H_c\pmod p\) is the correct first scalar invariant.
4. \(H_2(13)=117\equiv0\) and the previous \(5\pmod{13}\) claim is an arithmetic error.
5. The first layer at an Apéry root is rank-one and target-blind.
6. Higher \(p\)-adic layers are necessary for target-selective information.
7. The \(p=19\) example demonstrates failure of first-digit characterization, not failure of the block normalization itself.
