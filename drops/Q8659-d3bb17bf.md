# Q8659 rc5 audit: short-CRT coefficient window

## 1. Fixed-key reduction

For a fixed key, translation t, and branch choices define
\[
a_i(p)=1_{p\in(X,2X],\ p\mid B_{u_i(t)}}.
\]
A candidate ordered triple is counted by
\[
T=\sum_{p_1,p_2,p_3}a_1(p_1)a_2(p_2)a_3(p_3)\,1_{\exists c_1\in[1,X-1]:
 c_1p_1\equiv d_2\pmod {p_2},\ c_1p_1\equiv d_3\pmod {p_3}}.
\]
Because
\[
p_2p_3>X^2
\]
the CRT class for c_1 modulo p_2p_3 has at most one representative in the coefficient window. Thus the problem is a discrepancy problem for the fractional CRT coordinate
\[
 c_1\equiv p_1^{-1}d_2\pmod {p_2},\qquad
 c_1\equiv p_1^{-1}d_3\pmod {p_3}.
\]

## 2. Available bilinear estimate

The natural completion introduces additive characters modulo p_2p_3. The resulting sums are bilinear Kloosterman-fraction sums of the shape
\[
\sum_{p_1,p_2,p_3}a_1(p_1)a_2(p_2)a_3(p_3)
 e\left(\frac{\alpha \bar p_1}{p_2}+\frac{\beta\bar p_1}{p_3}\right).
\]
For arbitrary 0-1 weights there is no Apéry-specific cancellation. The relevant input is therefore a uniform large-sieve/Kloosterman estimate, not a root-distribution theorem.

A standard reference point is the Deshouillers-Iwaniec spectral large sieve for Kloosterman sums: after dyadic decomposition one obtains square-root cancellation in the modulus aspect but pays the size of the coefficient support. In the present three-prime setting this gives schematically
\[
\mathcal E\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}(\|a_1\|_2\|a_2\|_2\|a_3\|_2),
\]
with the exact form depending on the chosen completion.

The main term from the interval condition has size
\[
\frac{X}{p_2p_3}\,|P_1||P_2||P_3|,
\]
where \(P_i=\{p:a_i(p)=1\}\). Since the coefficient interval has length X and modulus p_2p_3\asymp X^2, the density is about \(1/X\).

## 3. Exponent audit

For unrestricted weights \(|P_i|\le X/\log X\), the trivial count is
\[
T\ll \frac{|P_1||P_2||P_3|}{X}
\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^3X}.
\]
This is already too large by a factor X for a fixed fibre target
\[
o(X/\log^3X).
\]

The spectral error is no better in this regime. With \(\|a_i\|_2\asymp |P_i|^{1/2}\), the square-root estimate gives an error of size roughly
\[
X^{3/2+o(1)}(X/\log X)^{3/2}=X^{3+o(1)}/\log^{3/2}X,
\]
which is much larger than the required scale.

Even optimistic completion to square-root cancellation in the complete three-variable sum leaves an error around \(X^{3/2+o(1)}\), still larger than \(X/\log^3X\).

## 4. Summing over translations

The accepted anchor energy bounds control quantities such as
\[
\sum_t |P_i(t)|^2
\]
and HM2-type averages. They can reduce an average over t, but they do not improve the maximum fixed fibre for arbitrary weights.

Using Cauchy over t converts a fixed-fibre estimate into an averaged estimate. The loss is precisely that the desired statement requires
\[
\max_t T(t)=o(X/\log^3X),
\]
whereas energy gives only bounds on
\[
\sum_tT(t)^2
\]
or related moments.

## 5. Exact obstruction

The short-CRT window is a valid compression, but standard bilinear Kloosterman/spectral technology does not reach the required exponent.

The missing saving would have to be at least one extra factor X beyond square-root cancellation, or an additional structural estimate on the Apéry prime weights. Such an estimate cannot be replaced by lambda^3 or root independence because the weights are arbitrary in this formulation.

Therefore the present method proves only a discrepancy bound of size substantially larger than
\[
o(X/\log^3X).
\]

**No-go statement.** Uniform spectral large-sieve estimates for arbitrary 0-1 prime weights do not prove the required fixed all-distinct fibre bound. The precise exponent loss is the coefficient-window density: the CRT condition supplies probability about 1/X, leaving a natural count of order X^2/log^3X, while available spectral errors are at least X^{3/2+o(1)} and generally larger. A successful proof needs additional Apéry-specific correlation beyond the stated anchor energy/HM2 input.
