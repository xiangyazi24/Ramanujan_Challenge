# Audit of the proposed GM-large degree bound

The proposed argument identifies the right obstruction, but the last step is not justified from the currently stated Sturm information. The claimed bound

\[
D_\ell(h_2)=\#\{h_1\le H:\ell\mid S_{h_1,h_2}\}\le 3(h_2-1)
\]

(or even the weakened bound \(O(h_2)\)) does not follow merely from the root count of \(N_{h_2}\).

## What is correct

If

\[
\ell\mid \operatorname{Res}(N_{h_1}(x),N_{h_2}(x+h_1)),
\]

then there exists \(\beta\in\overline{\mathbf F}_\ell\) such that

\[
N_{h_2}(\beta)=0,
\qquad
N_{h_1}(\beta-h_1)=0.
\]

Since \(\deg N_{h_2}=3(h_2-1)\), the first equation gives at most

\[
3(h_2-1)
\]

possible values of \(\beta\) (counting multiplicity).

The missing point is controlling, for a fixed root \(\beta\) of \(N_{h_2}\), the number of indices \(h_1\) satisfying

\[
N_{h_1}(\beta-h_1)=0.
\]

## Why the naive root count fails

For fixed \(\beta\), the polynomials involved are different:

\[
N_1(\beta-1),\ N_2(\beta-2),\ N_3(\beta-3),\ldots
\]

There is no degree argument preventing many of these values from vanishing modulo \(\ell\). The fact that the roots \(\beta-h_1\) are different points does not create a contradiction, because they belong to different gap polynomials.

Therefore one cannot replace the count by

\[
\#\{h_1:N_{h_1}(\beta-h_1)=0\}\le 2
\]

without an additional theorem.

## The Sturm step is the missing theorem

The proposed sequence

\[
v_h=N_h(\beta-h)
\]

would indeed be the correct object. If one had a theorem saying that for every admissible parameter \(\beta\), the recurrence sequence \(v_h\) has at most two zeros (over the relevant characteristic-zero lift), then the desired estimate would follow:

\[
D_\ell(h_2)
\le 3(h_2-1)\cdot 2
=6(h_2-1).
\]

However, the existing real Sturm/oscillation statement is not automatically a statement about zeros modulo \(\ell\) in \(\overline{\mathbf F}_\ell\). The reduction map destroys the sign information that Sturm theory uses.

In particular:

* a characteristic-zero sequence with few real zeros can have many zeros after reduction modulo a prime;
* Teichmüller lifting does not preserve an ordering or a sign variation principle;
* the half-plane nonvanishing strips are complex-analytic statements, not finite-field zero-count statements.

## What would make the argument rigorous

A valid replacement would be a finite-field recurrence lemma of the form:

> For every algebraic parameter \(\beta\) outside a controlled exceptional set, the sequence \(h\mapsto N_h(\beta-h)\) has at most \(C\) zeros in \(\mathbf F_\ell\) (or in \(\overline{\mathbf F}_\ell\)).

Then the argument gives

\[
D_\ell(h_2)\ll C h_2.
\]

But this is a new arithmetic statement; it is not a consequence of the present root-strip/Sturm analysis.

## Consequence for GM-large

The good news is that the measured gcd bound from the reduced resultants is still enough for the present computation:

\[
\log\gcd(S^*_{h_1,h_2},S^*_{h_2,h_3})
\]

is empirically tiny after saturation. Thus the GM-large contribution may indeed be negligible.

But the proposed proof of the universal divisor bound

\[
D_\ell(h_2)\le 3(h_2-1)
\]

is not closed. The root-count part is valid; the missing bridge is an arithmetic anti-zero theorem for the shifted gap recurrence modulo \(\ell\).
