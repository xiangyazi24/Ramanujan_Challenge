The definitive conclusions are:

1. With the ordering in the question, the sign is positive:
   
   ```math
   G_nG_{n+1}\,\delta_n=\Omega_n,
   ```
   
   not `-Omega_n`. The absolute-value identity is unchanged.

   ```math
   h_n=\gcd(A_n,b_n),
   ```

   ```math
   (x_n,y_n)=\left(\frac{E_nb_n}{h_n},\frac{A_n}{h_n}\right),
   \qquad
   \delta_n=\frac{6E_nE_{n+1}}
   {(n+1)^3h_nh_{n+1}}.
   ```

3. Writing `D_n=lcm(1,...,n)^3` and `R_n=D_n/E_n`, one has the exact factorization
   
   ```math
   G_n=R_nh_n.
   ```
   
   Therefore the full conjecture is equivalent to the conjunction
   
   ```math
   \log R_n=o(n),
   \qquad
   \log h_n=o(n).
   ```
   
   Proving `h_n=1` would be a valuable theorem, but it would leave the exact-denominator problem `log R_n=o(n)`, which is already essentially the original Stage-C obstruction.

4. Numerically, `delta_n` becomes enormous very quickly:
   
   ```text
   3, 32, 972, 497664, 800000, 19200000,
   35294700000, 48189030400000, ...
   ```
   
   But absolute size is misleading. The meaningful normalized statistic is
   
   ```math
   \frac{\delta_n}{\Omega_n}=\frac1{G_nG_{n+1}}.
   ```
   
   Thus every numerical observation about `delta_n/Omega_n` is exactly the same observation about the original gcds.

5. The premise that all Apéry fractions `a_n/b_n` form a subsequence of the **regular** continued-fraction convergents of `zeta(3)` is false. The first two nonzero Apéry fractions happen to be regular convergents, but the third is neither a regular convergent nor an intermediate convergent.

6. The correct continued-fraction statement is stronger and cleaner: after the factorial gauge, the Apéry pair gives the convergents of Apéry's **generalized polynomial continued fraction**. Its unreduced adjacent determinant is exactly
   
   ```math
   6(n!)^6.
   ```
   
   The primitive determinant is obtained by dividing this known determinant by the two column contents. Controlling those contents is precisely the gcd/denominator problem.

7. Padé theory explains the recurrence, convergence, and nonzero raw Casoratian. Standard Padé theory works over a field and does not control the integral contents removed during primitive reduction. A Padé proof that `delta_n` is exponentially large would require a new **integral saturation theorem** and would essentially prove the conjecture.

8. Height theory gives an exact interpretation:
   `delta_n` is the lattice index of the two primitive adjacent vectors and the unnormalized wedge measuring their angle. General height theory gives the upper bound `delta_n <= H_nH_{n+1}` and the trivial lower bound `delta_n >= 1`; it gives no lower bound remotely close to `exp(6n)`. Arbitrarily high primitive vectors can have determinant one.

9. The Mahler measure
   
   ```math
   \log(17+12\sqrt2)=4\log(1+\sqrt2)
   ```
   
   controls the archimedean growth and projective contraction of the Apéry recurrence. It cancels out of the determinant exponent. The target exponent `6` comes from two copies of the cubic least-common-multiple denominator, not from the Mahler measure.

The best use of this angle is therefore not to assert that “continued fractions make the determinant large,” but to formulate and attack an **adjacent integral-saturation theorem** or the two sharper residuals `R_n` and `h_n` separately.

# 1. Notation and the sign correction

The repository defines

```math
D_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)^3.
```

The question writes `d_n^3`; to avoid accidentally taking a ninth power, I will use

```math
L_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),
\qquad D_n=L_n^3.
```

Set

```math
U_n=D_nb_n,\qquad V_n=D_na_n,
\qquad G_n=\gcd(U_n,V_n),
```

and define the primitive column

```math
v_n=(x_n,y_n)=\left(\frac{U_n}{G_n},\frac{V_n}{G_n}\right).
```

The repository's Casoratian identity is

```math
 a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.
```

Replacing `n` by `n+1` gives

```math
 a_{n+1}b_n-a_nb_{n+1}=\frac6{(n+1)^3}.
```

Therefore, with the determinant ordering used in the question,

```math
\begin{aligned}
G_nG_{n+1}\delta_n
&=D_nD_{n+1}
  \bigl(b_na_{n+1}-b_{n+1}a_n\bigr)\\
&=\frac{6D_nD_{n+1}}{(n+1)^3}
=: \Omega_n.
\end{aligned}
```

Thus

```math
\boxed{G_nG_{n+1}\delta_n=\Omega_n>0.}
```

If one reverses the two rows or reverses the determinant order, the sign becomes negative. All magnitude statements are unaffected.

Since `G_n,G_{n+1} >= 1`,

```math
0\le \log\Omega_n-\log\delta_n
=\log G_n+\log G_{n+1}.
```

The prime number theorem in the form

```math
\log L_n=\psi(n)=n+o(n)
```

implies

```math
\log\Omega_n
=3\psi(n)+3\psi(n+1)-3\log(n+1)+O(1)
=6n+o(n).
```

Consequently the clean exact equivalence is

```math
\boxed{
\log G_n=o(n)\text{ for all }n
\iff
\log\Omega_n-\log\delta_n=o(n)\text{ for all }n.
}
```

Because the difference is nonnegative, this is also equivalent to the informal statement

```math
\log\delta_n=6n-o(n).
```

The exact deficit `log(Omega_n/delta_n)` is preferable: it is literally `log G_n+log G_{n+1}` and avoids hiding the `o(n)` term in `log Omega_n`.

# 2. Exact reduced-fraction anatomy

Write the rational Apéry numerator in lowest terms as

```math
 a_n=\frac{A_n}{E_n},
 \qquad \gcd(A_n,E_n)=1,
 \qquad E_n>0.
```

Apéry's denominator theorem gives `E_n | D_n`. Put

```math
 R_n=\frac{D_n}{E_n},
 \qquad h_n=\gcd(A_n,b_n).
```

Then

```math
U_n=R_nE_nb_n,
\qquad
V_n=R_nA_n.
```

Since `gcd(A_n,E_n)=1`,

```math
\begin{aligned}
G_n
&=R_n\gcd(E_nb_n,A_n)\\
&=R_n\gcd(b_n,A_n)\\
&=R_nh_n.
\end{aligned}
```

Hence

```math
\boxed{G_n=\frac{D_n}{E_n}\,h_n.}
```

After dividing by `G_n`, the primitive vector is

```math
\boxed{
(x_n,y_n)=
\left(\frac{E_nb_n}{h_n},\frac{A_n}{h_n}\right).
}
```

In particular,

```math
\frac{y_n}{x_n}=\frac{a_n}{b_n}
```

in lowest terms. Thus the primitive vector is not an auxiliary construction: it is exactly the reduced numerator-denominator vector of the rational approximant `a_n/b_n`.

Substitution into the determinant gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
\delta_n
&=\frac{E_nE_{n+1}}
        {h_nh_{n+1}}
 \bigl(b_na_{n+1}-b_{n+1}a_n\bigr)\\
&=\boxed{
\frac{6E_nE_{n+1}}
     {(n+1)^3h_nh_{n+1}}}.
\end{aligned}
```

This formula is one of the most useful outputs of the primitive-determinant viewpoint.

## 2.1 Exact splitting of the conjecture

The two factors in

```math
G_n=R_nh_n
```

are positive integers. Therefore

```math
\log G_n
=\log R_n+\log h_n
```

is a sum of two nonnegative quantities. It follows that

```math
\boxed{
G_n=e^{o(n)}
\iff
R_n=e^{o(n)}\text{ and }h_n=e^{o(n)}.
}
```

Equivalently, the conjecture splits into:

1. **Exact-denominator saturation**
   
   ```math
   \log E_n=\log D_n-o(n)=3n-o(n).
   ```

2. **Intrinsic coprimality at exponential scale**
   
   ```math
   \log\gcd(A_n,b_n)=o(n).
   ```

The empirical assertion `h_n=1` would completely settle the second item, but not the first.

# 4. The regular continued-fraction premise is false

The regular continued fraction of `zeta(3)` begins

```math
\zeta(3)=[1;4,1,18,1,1,1,4,1,9,9,2,\ldots].
```

See [OEIS A013631](https://oeis.org/A013631). Its initial convergents are

```text
1/1,
5/4,
6/5,
113/94,
119/99,
232/193,
351/292,
1636/1361,
1987/1653,
19519/16238,
177658/147795,
...
```

The first two nonzero Apéry approximants,

```math
\frac65,\qquad \frac{351}{292},
```

are indeed regular convergents, at indices 2 and 6. The next Apéry approximant is

```math
\frac{62531}{52020}.
```

It is not a regular convergent: the relevant regular-convergent denominators jump from

```text
16,238 to 147,795.
```

It is not even an intermediate convergent in that jump. Since the preceding denominator is `1,653` and the next partial quotient is `9`, the possible intermediate denominators are

```text
1,653 + k*16,238
= 17,891, 34,129, 50,367, 66,605,
  82,843, 99,081, 115,319, 131,557
```

for `1 <= k <= 8`. The value `52,020` is absent.

Thus there is no general theorem that the Apéry approximants form a subsequence of the simple continued-fraction convergents or semiconvergents of `zeta(3)`.

## 4.1 What `delta_n` would mean in the regular-CF setting

For two reduced rational numbers `p/q` and `p'/q'`,

```math
|pq'-p'q|
```

is their Farey determinant. It equals one exactly when the two primitive vectors form a unimodular basis of `Z^2`—equivalently, when the fractions are Farey neighbors at a suitable level.

For two nonconsecutive regular convergents, the cross determinant is a continuant built from the intervening partial quotients. Therefore, **if** every Apéry approximant were known to be a regular convergent, `delta_n` would encode the block of simple-CF digits skipped between two Apéry approximants.

That hypothesis fails from the third nonzero Apéry approximant onward, so the interpretation cannot be used as a proof mechanism.

## 4.2 Why the simple-CF criterion is exactly critical

Let

```math
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^4.
```

The Apéry approximants have archimedean error of exponential order

```math
\left|\zeta(3)-\frac{a_n}{b_n}\right|
=\alpha^{-2n+o(n)}.
```

Their reduced denominator is

```math
x_n=\frac{D_nb_n}{G_n}.
```

If along a subsequence

```math
\log G_n\sim\gamma n,
```

then

```math
\log x_n=(3+\log\alpha-\gamma)n+o(n).
```

The Legendre threshold for forcing a rational approximation to be a regular convergent is essentially `error < 1/(2x_n^2)`. On the exponential scale this would require

```math
2\log\alpha
>2(3+\log\alpha-\gamma),
```

or

```math
\gamma>3.
```

But the natural maximal scale for `log G_n/n` is `3`. Thus the simple-CF theorem has **no exponential slack**: it activates only at the extremal boundary where the gcd nearly consumes the entire least-common-multiple factor. It cannot rule out a gcd of size `e^{cn}` for a fixed `0<c<3`.

This explains why the regular continued fraction is not a viable route to the full conjecture even apart from the false subsequence premise.

# 5. The correct generalized continued fraction

The Apéry approximants do arise from a continued fraction, but it is a generalized—or polynomial—continued fraction.

Define the factorial-gauged integer solutions

```math
Q_n=(n!)^3b_n,
\qquad
P_n=\frac{(n!)^3a_n}{6}.
```

The integrality of `P_n` follows from the recurrence and the initial data `P_0=0`, `P_1=1`. Both sequences satisfy

```math
Z_{n+1}=C(n)Z_n-n^6Z_{n-1},
```

where

```math
C(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
```

The ratios `Q_n/P_n` are the convergents to Apéry's generalized continued fraction

```math
\frac6{\zeta(3)}
=5-\cfrac{1^6}{117-
     \cfrac{2^6}{535-
     \cfrac{3^6}{1463-\ddots}}}.
```

Here

```text
5=C(0), 117=C(1), 535=C(2), 1463=C(3), ...
```

A modern detailed discussion is Henri Cohen's [*Apéry Acceleration of Continued Fractions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17720). See also Marc Chamberland and Armin Straub, [*Apéry limits: Experiments and proofs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03400).

## 5.1 Exact generalized-CF determinant

The recurrence gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
Q_nP_{n+1}-Q_{n+1}P_n
&=n^6\bigl(Q_{n-1}P_n-Q_nP_{n-1}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
```

Since the initial determinant is one,

```math
\boxed{
Q_nP_{n+1}-Q_{n+1}P_n=(n!)^6.
}
```

Equivalently, for the factorial-gauged vectors representing `a_n/b_n`,

```math
\boxed{
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
(n!)^3b_n & ((n+1)!)^3b_{n+1}\\
(n!)^3a_n & ((n+1)!)^3a_{n+1}
\end{pmatrix}
=6(n!)^6.
}
```

This is the exact analogue of the determinant `+/-1` for adjacent regular-CF convergents. The difference is that the generalized fraction has partial numerators `-n^6`, so the determinant is their product rather than one.

## 5.2 Primitive reduction is exactly the hard part

Let

```math
c_n=\gcd\bigl((n!)^3b_n,(n!)^3a_n\bigr).
```

Because `D_n | (n!)^3`, put

```math
F_n=\frac{(n!)^3}{D_n}.
```

The factorial-gauged vector is `F_n(U_n,V_n)`, hence

```math
c_n=F_nG_n.
```

Dividing the two adjacent factorial-gauged columns by their contents gives the primitive determinant:

```math
\boxed{
\delta_n=
\frac{6(n!)^6}{c_nc_{n+1}}
=
\frac{6(n!)^6}{F_nF_{n+1}G_nG_{n+1}}.
}
```

Since

```math
\frac{6(n!)^6}{F_nF_{n+1}}=\Omega_n,
```

this is exactly the original identity.

This is the decisive continued-fraction diagnosis:

> Generalized continued-fraction theory computes the **unreduced** adjacent determinant exactly. The open arithmetic problem is how much of that determinant is removed by the contents of the two columns.

The determinant identity is therefore not a new source of lower bounds. It is a transparent encoding of the saturation problem.

# 6. Padé and Hermite–Padé interpretation

There are several Padé/Hermite–Padé constructions recovering Apéry's sequences. For example:

- Marc Prévost, [*A new proof of the irrationality of zeta(2) and zeta(3) using Padé approximants*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0377-0427(95)00019-4), recovers the Apéry sequences from Padé approximation to error-term expansions.
- Anier Soria-Lorente and Stefan Berres, [*A single parameter Hermite–Padé series representation for Apéry's constant*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.05988), derive the same Diophantine approximations from a Hermite–Padé problem.

These constructions explain:

1. why two independent solutions satisfy one recurrence;
2. why the approximation error is very small;
3. why an adjacent Casoratian is nonzero;
4. why a generalized continued fraction appears.

They do **not** automatically give a lower bound for the primitive determinant.

## 6.1 Field-level normality versus integral saturation

Padé theory is normally formulated over a field. Multiplying a numerator-denominator pair by any nonzero scalar does not change the rational approximant. Consequently, field-level Padé normality detects whether a determinant is zero, but is insensitive to the integer content of the coefficient vector.

In the present problem:

- Padé/recurrence theory gives the raw determinant `6(n!)^6`.
- Apéry integrality supplies a large forced common factor `F_n=(n!)^3/D_n` in the `n`th column.
- The extra common factor is `G_n`.
- Primitive reduction divides the determinant by `F_nG_nF_{n+1}G_{n+1}`.

A generic Padé theorem has no way to distinguish `F_n` from `F_nG_n`: both are scalar contents invisible over `Q`.

Thus the needed Padé statement would have to be an **integral Padé saturation theorem** of the form

```math
\log c_n
=\log F_n+o(n).
```

Since `c_n=F_nG_n`, this is exactly `log G_n=o(n)`.

## 6.2 Does Padé theory give any one-sided evidence?

It gives two useful but insufficient facts:

- `delta_n` is nonzero, hence `delta_n >= 1`.
- The archimedean approximation rate and the heights determine the expected exponential scale of `delta_n` once denominator saturation is assumed.

It does not rule out very large column contents. In fact, rescaling Padé pairs shows why no purely analytic normality statement can do so.

Therefore there is no known Padé-theoretic reason, independent of the arithmetic denominator problem, forcing

```math
\delta_n=e^{6n-o(n)}.
```

# 7. Lattice, height, and Arakelov interpretation

Let

```math
v_n=(x_n,y_n)\in\mathbf Z^2
```

be primitive. Since the two vectors are independent,

```math
\boxed{
\delta_n
=[\mathbf Z^2:\mathbf Zv_n+\mathbf Zv_{n+1}].
}
```

Because each column is primitive, the Smith normal form of the adjacent primitive matrix is

```math
\operatorname{diag}(1,\delta_n).
```

Thus `delta_n` is exactly the covolume—or saturation index—of the lattice generated by the two consecutive approximation vectors.

## 7.1 It is the unnormalized angle, not the angle itself

For the Euclidean norm,

```math
\sin\theta_n
=\frac{\delta_n}{\|v_n\|\,\|v_{n+1}\|}.
```

The actual rational difference is exactly

```math
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}-\frac{a_n}{b_n}
&=\frac{\delta_n}{x_nx_{n+1}}\\
&=\frac6{(n+1)^3b_nb_{n+1}}.
\end{aligned}
```

Hence the adjacent projective angle is extremely small even while `delta_n` is extremely large. Large determinant and small angle coexist because the vector heights are much larger still.

## 7.2 Exact height identity

Let

```math
H_n=\max(|x_n|,|y_n|).
```

Up to a bounded factor—because `y_n/x_n -> zeta(3)`—one has `H_n asymp x_n`. Therefore

```math
-\log d_\infty(P_n,P_{n+1})
=\log H_n+\log H_{n+1}-\log\delta_n+O(1),
```

where `P_n=[y_n:x_n]` and `d_infty` is the archimedean projective/chordal distance.

This is the natural Arakelov identity. It is exact bookkeeping, but it points in the wrong direction for a general theorem:

- General height theory gives
  
  ```math
  \log\delta_n\le \log H_n+\log H_{n+1}+O(1).
  ```

- Integrality gives only
  
  ```math
  \log\delta_n\ge0.
  ```

There is no general lower bound proportional to the heights. Primitive vectors of arbitrarily large height can have determinant one; consecutive ordinary-CF convergents are the canonical examples.

The standard reference for the height formalism is Enrico Bombieri and Walter Gubler, [*Heights in Diophantine Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511542879).

## 7.3 Why Roth/irrationality-measure theory does not close it

Roth's theorem applies to algebraic irrational targets; `zeta(3)` is not known to be algebraic. Known upper bounds for the irrationality exponent of `zeta(3)` are also far too weak to control the present denominator contents.

More structurally, the Apéry error has exponent approximately

```math
2\log\alpha=8\log(1+\sqrt2),
```

whereas the conjectural reduced-denominator exponent is

```math
3+\log\alpha.
```

The ratio is only

```math
\frac{2\log\alpha}{3+\log\alpha}
\approx1.08053.
```

Thus the approximants are much better than a generic `1/q` approximation but nowhere near a regime in which existing irrationality-measure bounds force detailed continued-fraction structure.

## 7.4 Adelic interpretation

Since `delta_n` is a positive integer,

```math
\log\delta_n
=\sum_p v_p(\delta_n)\log p.
```

For primitive projective points, `v_p(delta_n)` is the local intersection multiplicity of the two lines modulo powers of `p` whenever the usual unit-coordinate normalization is used.

The identity

```math
v_p(\delta_n)
=v_p(\Omega_n)-v_p(G_n)-v_p(G_{n+1})
```

says that primitive reduction removes local intersection mass from the raw adjacent solution lattice. The conjecture asks that the total removed logarithmic mass be `o(n)`.

This is a legitimate Arakelov reformulation, but no general positivity theorem supplies the required lower bound. The special Apéry arithmetic still has to enter locally.

# 8. Can one prove `gcd(A_n,b_n)=1`?

The current repository checks

```math

```

computationally in its exact-rational scripts. The initial OEIS data also satisfy

```math
\operatorname{denom}(a_n/b_n)=E_nb_n,
```

which is equivalent to `h_n=1`.

I found no proof in the audited repository or in the cited Apéry/Padé literature that `h_n=1` for every `n`. This is a statement about what I could verify, not a claim that no such result exists anywhere.

## 8.1 A new exact necessary condition: the adjacent-pole lemma

There is, however, a sharp Wronskian consequence that may be useful.

Let `p >= 5`, suppose

```math
p\mid A_n,
\qquad
p\mid b_n,
```

and put

```math
t=v_p(b_n)\ge1.
```

Because `a_n=A_n/E_n` is reduced, `p` does not divide `E_n`, so

```math
v_p(a_n)\ge1.
```

Use the two Wronskians

```math
 a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3},
```

and

```math
 a_{n+1}b_n-a_nb_{n+1}=\frac6{(n+1)^3}.
```

In the first identity, the term `a_n b_{n-1}` has valuation at least one, while the right side has valuation `-3v_p(n) <= 0`. Therefore the other term must have exactly the right-side valuation. The second identity is identical. Hence

```math
\boxed{
 v_p(a_{n-1})=-3v_p(n)-t,
 \qquad
 v_p(a_{n+1})=-3v_p(n+1)-t.
}
```

Equivalently, the exact neighboring denominators satisfy

```math
\boxed{
 v_p(E_{n-1})=3v_p(n)+t,
 \qquad
 v_p(E_{n+1})=3v_p(n+1)+t.
}
```

Thus a common prime factor of `A_n` and `b_n` forces a highly rigid local pattern:

> a simultaneous numerator zero at index `n` is flanked by two exact denominator poles, with depths prescribed by `v_p(b_n)` and the boundary factors `n^3,(n+1)^3`.

Immediate consequences include:

- every prime `p >= 5` dividing `h_n` must satisfy `p <= n-1`;
- its multiplicity is limited by both neighboring lcm envelopes;
- any proof of `h_n=1` may be reduced to excluding this zero–pole–zero pattern in the integral local system.

This is a genuine narrower local target. It does not address `R_n`.

## 8.2 What `h_n=1` would buy

If one proves `h_n=1` for every `n`, then

```math
G_n=R_n=\frac{D_n}{E_n}
```

and

```math
\delta_n=\frac{6E_nE_{n+1}}{(n+1)^3}.
```

The full conjecture becomes

```math
\log E_n=3n-o(n).
```

This is a significant conceptual simplification: all gcd growth would be caused by the classical lcm envelope over-clearing the exact denominator of `a_n`.

It is not a proof of the conjecture. In the top-prime window the current repository's valuation results identify denominator defects with the same conditions `p | b_n` that drive the existing Stage-C problem. Thus the cross-prime incidence bottleneck reappears inside `E_n`.

A more proportionate first target is

```math
h_n=e^{o(n)},
```

or a theorem that `h_n` is supported on a controlled set of small primes. Either would remove the intrinsic-gcd term without demanding exact coprimality.

# 9. Mahler measure, Lyapunov exponents, and entropy

The original recurrence can be written as a nonautonomous transfer system. Its limiting transfer matrix is

```math
T_\infty=
\begin{pmatrix}
34&-1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
```

The characteristic polynomial is

```math
z^2-34z+1,
```

with roots

```math
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^4,
\qquad
\alpha^{-1}=17-12\sqrt2.
```

The logarithmic Mahler measure of this reciprocal polynomial is

```math
m=\log\alpha=4\log(1+\sqrt2).
```

Poincaré–Perron theory gives, at the exponential scale,

```math
b_n=\alpha^{n+o(n)},
```

while the recessive solution has rate `alpha^{-n}`. Consequently

```math
\frac{a_n}{b_n}-\zeta(3)
=\alpha^{-2n+o(n)}.
```

Cohen's continued-fraction analysis states the same rate as

```math
(1+\sqrt2)^{-8n}=\alpha^{-2n}.
```

## 9.1 Why the Mahler exponent cancels from `delta_n`

The reduced denominator is

```math
x_n=\frac{E_nb_n}{h_n}.
```

Under the target estimates

```math
\log E_n=3n+o(n),
\qquad
\log h_n=o(n),
```

one has

```math
\log x_n=(3+\log\alpha)n+o(n).
```

On the other hand,

```math
\log\left(
\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}-
\frac{a_n}{b_n}
\right)
=-2n\log\alpha+o(n).
```

Using

```math
\delta_n
=x_nx_{n+1}
\left(
\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}-
\frac{a_n}{b_n}
\right),
```

we obtain

```math
\begin{aligned}
\log\delta_n
&=2(3+\log\alpha)n
 -2n\log\alpha+o(n)\\
&=6n+o(n).
\end{aligned}
```

The two Mahler/entropy contributions cancel exactly.

This gives the conceptual answer to Question 6:

> The Mahler measure controls the archimedean growth of the approximation vectors and the contraction of their projective slopes. It does not control the primitive integral content. The exponent `6` is the residual of two cubic denominator-clearance factors after the archimedean entropy cancels.

## 9.2 What an entropy approach would actually need

A standard Lyapunov-exponent theorem controls norms of products of transfer matrices. Here those norms are already understood through `alpha`. The missing quantity is the gcd/content of an integral vector under a deterministic nonautonomous cocycle.

A successful entropy formulation would have to be **adelic**: it would compare the archimedean Lyapunov growth with the total `p`-adic content growth and prove that the extra content beyond the forced factorial/lcm factor is subexponential.

No standard Mahler-measure or dynamical-entropy theorem supplies that arithmetic saturation estimate.

One should also distinguish the Mahler measure of `z^2-34z+1` from possible Mahler-measure identities for Laurent polynomials defining the Apéry K3 family. The latter may encode special `L`-values, but they do not presently control `G_n` or `delta_n`.

# 10. What this angle genuinely adds

The identity is algebraically equivalent to the original conjecture, so it cannot be advertised as an independent closure. It adds three useful pieces of structure.

## 10.1 A canonical lattice invariant

The primitive determinant is the cyclic index

```math
\mathbf Z^2/(\mathbf Zv_n+\mathbf Zv_{n+1})
\cong \mathbf Z/\delta_n\mathbf Z.
```

This makes “primitive reduction” and “saturation” precise and may invite local Smith-normal-form arguments.

## 10.2 A two-residual decomposition

The exact formula

```math
G_n=\frac{D_n}{E_n}\gcd(A_n,b_n)
```

separates:

- over-clearance of the known denominator envelope, and
- a genuine numerator common divisor.

Because both logarithms are nonnegative, they can be attacked independently without cancellation.

## 10.3 A narrower local obstruction for intrinsic coprimality

The adjacent-pole lemma shows exactly what a prime dividing `h_n` must do to the neighboring exact denominators. This gives a concrete local-system problem rather than a vague coprimality conjecture.

# 11. Recommended next theorems

The following targets are ordered by how much new information they would provide without simply renaming the original conjecture.

## Target A: intrinsic subexponential gcd

Prove

```math
\log\gcd(A_n,b_n)=o(n).
```

A stronger and computationally supported version is `gcd(A_n,b_n)=1`. The adjacent-pole lemma is a usable first reduction.

## Target B: exact-denominator saturation

Prove

```math
\log E_n=\log D_n-o(n).
```

This is exactly the denominator-defect part. It is likely as difficult as the current prime-incidence problem, but it may admit different tools from those used on `b_n` directly.

## Target C: local adjacent saturation

For each prime `p`, study the two-column integral solution matrix and prove a pointwise or averaged upper bound on

```math
v_p(G_n)+v_p(G_{n+1}).
```

The determinant gives the exact budget

```math
v_p(G_n)+v_p(G_{n+1})
=v_p(\Omega_n)-v_p(\delta_n).
```

To be noncircular, the input must come from the recurrence/local system, not from an assumed lower bound on `delta_n`.

## Target D: exclusion of bounded primitive determinants

A weaker theorem such as

```math
\delta_n\to\infty
```

or

```math
\delta_n\ge e^{c n}
```

for some explicit `c>0` would be new, but only `c=6-o(1)` closes the conjecture. Regular-CF arguments might conceivably exclude the extremal case `delta_n=O(1)`, but they have no evident path to the full exponent.

# 12. Direct answers to the six questions

## (1) Numerical behavior

`delta_n` is positive and grows very rapidly in the initial data:

```text
3, 32, 972, 497664, 800000, 19200000,
35294700000, 48189030400000, ...
```

There is no observed small-determinant behavior. However, the normalized ratio is exactly `1/(G_nG_{n+1})`, so this is not independent evidence. No all-`n` growing lower bound is currently proved.

## (2) Continued fractions

The Apéry fractions are **not** a subsequence of the regular convergents of `zeta(3)`. The first two nonzero coincidences stop at the third fraction. They are exactly convergents of Apéry's generalized polynomial continued fraction, and the generalized adjacent determinant is `(n!)^6`—or `6(n!)^6` for the `a_n/b_n` vectors—before primitive reduction.

## (3) Padé theory

Padé/Hermite–Padé theory explains the recurrence, approximation, and nonzero raw determinant. It does not bound the primitive determinant because scalar contents are invisible over a field. A theorem that did so would be an integral saturation theorem equivalent in strength to the gcd conjecture.

## (4) Heights

`delta_n` is the lattice index and unnormalized wedge. The normalized angle is `delta_n/(||v_n||||v_{n+1}||)`. General height theory supplies no useful exponential lower bound for `delta_n`; determinant one is compatible with arbitrarily large heights.

## (5) `gcd(A_n,b_n)=1`

It is strongly supported computationally, and the repository explicitly checks it, but I found no all-`n` proof. A common prime forces exact poles in both neighboring `a`-terms, as proved above. Proving coprimality would reduce `G_n` to the denominator defect `D_n/E_n`; it would simplify, but not close, the full conjecture.

## (6) Mahler measure/entropy

The Mahler measure of `z^2-34z+1` is `log(17+12sqrt(2))` and controls the archimedean growth and convergence rate. It cancels from the determinant exponent. The desired `6n` comes from the two cubic lcm factors. No known entropy theorem controls the missing integral content.

---

# Final assessment

The primitive adjacent determinant is a **good structural lens** and a useful new section for the project. Its strongest contribution is the exact identity

```math
\delta_n=
\frac{6E_nE_{n+1}}
{(n+1)^3\gcd(A_n,b_n)\gcd(A_{n+1},b_{n+1})},
```

and the resulting nonnegative split

```math
\log G_n
=\log\frac{D_n}{E_n}
 +\log\gcd(A_n,b_n).
```

It does not make Stage C disappear. The generalized continued fraction and Padé constructions compute the raw Casoratian exactly, while the conjecture is about how much survives **primitive integral saturation**. Height and Mahler-measure theory explain the archimedean side but do not constrain the arithmetic content.

The most promising genuinely narrower problem exposed by this angle is the intrinsic coprimality question, together with the adjacent-pole lemma. The exact-denominator residual remains the load-bearing obstruction and is another form of the same cross-prime incidence problem already isolated in the repository.
