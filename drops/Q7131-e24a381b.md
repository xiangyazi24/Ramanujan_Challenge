# Size barrier audit for off-center primitive four-return configurations

## Verdict

The statement

\[
\boxed{p\le s^2}
\]

for every fully-deflated off-center primitive four-return configuration outside \(\mathfrak U_s\) is **not proved by the current continuant, restart, reflection, or endpoint identities**.  The Q7119 example with

\[
(p,r,a,b,c,s)=(28933,7602,3074,1166,253,4493)
\]

is irrelevant to this barrier because \(s^2\gg p\), but it also exposes the missing mechanism: the identities certify a finite zero pattern; they do not bound the characteristic prime by the span.

No exact counterexample with \(p>s^2\) is established by the current verified data.  Therefore the correct publication status is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The universal formal statement remains open; the missing input is a prime-size lemma.}
}
\]

For actual Apéry-zero starts the available census evidence is stronger, but still does not constitute a proof.

---

## 1. What the verified identities actually imply

Let

\[
z_0=r,\quad z_1=r+a,\quad z_2=r+a+b,\quad z_3=r+a+b+c,
\]

with

\[
s=a+b+c.
\]

The restart identity gives

\[
N_a(z_0)=0,\qquad
N_b(z_1)=0,\qquad
N_c(z_2)=0
\]

from four consecutive Apéry returns.  Conversely, for a formal configuration, these are the local continuant conditions.

The reflection identity gives

\[
N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).
\]

For even \(h\), removing

\[
2x+h+1
\]

removes only the forced reflection-center root.  It does not constrain the remaining roots.

Outside

\[
\mathfrak U_s=\prod_{j\le s}j!b_jV_{j+1},
\]

endpoint evaluations imply that the reduced gap polynomials retain their generic degree and leading coefficients.  This yields the degree bound

\[
\#\{x:N_h(x)=0\}\le3(h-1)
\]

for each fixed gap.

Nothing in these facts relates the size of the prime \(p\) to the total span \(s\).

---

## 2. Why a naive degree argument cannot prove \(p\le s^2\)

For fixed \((a,b,c)\), the equations are three polynomial equations over \(\mathbf F_p\):

\[
N_a(x)=0,
\]

\[
N_b(x+a)=0,
\]

\[
N_c(x+a+b)=0.
\]

The degrees are

\[
3(a-1),\quad3(b-1),\quad3(c-1).
\]

A resultant argument can produce an integer carrier divisible by primes admitting such a configuration.  However, the archimedean size of that resultant is exponential in the polynomial degrees.  The generic bound is of size roughly

\[
\exp(O(s^2\log s)),
\]

not \(s^2\).

Therefore the implication

\[
p\mid R_{a,b,c}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\le s^2
\]

would require a new small-resultant theorem after removing \(\mathfrak U_s\).  The existing reduced-content computations do not prove this.

---

## 3. Why reduced content values do not give a counterexample

The values seen in the small computation include

\[
24649=157^2.
\]

This is not a prime witness.  The relevant statement is about prime divisors:

\[
p\mid \mathcal C^*_{a,b,c}.
\]

A square residual content can arise from repeated intersection multiplicity or from a non-squarefree resultant.  It does not mean a new characteristic prime larger than \(s^2\) exists.

The small residual primes

\[
131,157,431,653
\]

are all below the corresponding \(s^2\) thresholds and are therefore consistent with the conjectured barrier.

## 4. Actual Apéry-zero starts versus formal configurations

The actual statement is stronger:

> If \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\) and the four consecutive zeros
> \(r,r+a,r+a+b,r+s\) form an off-center primitive chain, with
> \(p\nmid\mathfrak U_s\), then \(p\le s^2\).

The current census through \(p<2\cdot10^6\) supports this, but the missing step is converting an actual orbit return into a short arithmetic relation.

The known bridge is only:

\[
 b_r=b_{r+s}=0
\Longrightarrow
N_s(r)=0.
\]

This gives a polynomial root modulo \(p\), not an integer root.  To obtain \(p\le s^2\), one needs a lemma of the following form.

---

## 5. Missing lemma required for the mesoscopic block argument

A sufficient statement would be:

### Small-characteristic four-return lemma

For every \(s\ge1\), every prime \(p\nmid\mathfrak U_s\), and every off-center primitive triple \((a,b,c)\) with

\[
a+b+c=s,
\]

if

\[
N_a(r)=N_b(r+a)=N_c(r+a+b)=0\pmod p,
\]

then

\[
 p\le s^2.
\]

Equivalently, after saturation by \(\mathfrak U_s\), every transverse resultant carrier must have all prime divisors bounded by the quadratic span.

This is substantially stronger than:

1. nonvanishing of reduced resultants;
2. bounded degree of \(N_h\);
3. reflection symmetry;
4. endpoint formulas.

It is an arithmetic size theorem for the residual transverse content.

---

## 6. Consequence for the mesoscopic block proof

The desired block argument needs exactly the contrapositive:

\[
 p>s^2
\Longrightarrow
\text{no off-center primitive four-return block of span }s.
\]

The Q7119 counterexample does not affect this regime because it lies in the opposite range

\[
s^2/p\approx697.
\]

The barrier remains untested precisely where the proof needs it:

\[
s^2\le p.
\]

In that range, a proof cannot be obtained by recycling Q7119's reflection-window analysis.
