# Q545: Trace-zero fibers in Apéry-type Sp(4) families

## Executive summary

The short answer is:

- There is **no known generic theorem** saying that for an arbitrary rank-4 l-adic representation with big monodromy (for example geometric monodromy Sp(4)) one has

\[
\#\{t\in\mathbf F_p:a_p(t)=0\}=O(1).
\]

- The Weil conjectures and Newton polygon/Hasse invariant methods control the size and geometry of Frobenius eigenvalues, but they generally do **not** control the number of parameters where the trace is exactly zero modulo p.

- Katz-Sarnak equidistribution predicts the probability of trace zero is about \(1/p\), giving an expected O(1) number of zeros in a one-parameter family, but proving this requires a much stronger finite-field trace-distribution theorem than currently available in this generality.

- The Achter-Kedlaya/Newton polygon literature is related to variation of p-adic cohomology, but Newton polygon strata do not directly encode the trace-zero fiber.

Therefore the Apéry M1 problem remains a stronger trace-fiber problem than standard monodromy or Newton polygon results.

---

# (1) Fixed variety with Sp(4) monodromy: do Weil or Hasse invariants control trace-zero points?

Suppose we have a fixed smooth variety \(X/\mathbf F_p\) whose middle cohomology gives a rank-4 representation

\[
\rho: G_{\mathbf F_p}\to Sp_4.
\]

For a single Frobenius element,

\[
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p))
\]

is bounded by Deligne:

\[
|a_p|\leq 4p^{3/2}
\]

(for weight 3 normalization).

This only gives the size of the trace. It does not say how often the trace vanishes in a family of specializations.

The zero condition is an equation:

\[
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_t)=0.
\]

Geometrically, this is a pullback of the trace-zero divisor in the algebraic group \(Sp_4\).

If Frobenius elements were perfectly equidistributed in \(Sp_4(\mathbf F_p)\), then the probability would be roughly

\[
\Pr(\mathrm{Tr}=0)\asymp \frac1p,
\]

because trace is one scalar condition.

But proving this requires a quantitative equidistribution statement.

---

## Newton polygons and Hasse invariants

Newton polygons measure valuations of Frobenius eigenvalues:

\[
\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_4.
\]

They detect the p-adic slopes

\[
v_p(\alpha_i).
\]

The Hasse invariant detects special loci where the Newton polygon changes.

However:

\[
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob})=\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_4
\]

is an additive relation among eigenvalues, while Newton polygons only record valuations.

Different Frobenius conjugacy classes can have the same Newton polygon but different traces, including trace zero and nonzero cases.

Therefore:

\[
\text{Newton polygon information}\not\Rightarrow\text{trace-zero count}.
\]

Hasse invariants are useful for ordinary/supersingular stratifications, but not directly for the Apéry zero set.

# (2) Katz-Sarnak statistics for one-parameter families

Let

\[
\Delta(p)=\#\{t\in\mathbf F_p:a_p(t)=0\}.
\]

For a family with connected geometric monodromy group \(G\), Katz's philosophy says Frobenius classes become equidistributed in the compact form \(G^\mathrm{cpt}\).

For the Apéry situation:

\[
G^\mathrm{cpt}=USp(4).
\]

The limiting distribution of normalized traces is the USp(4) trace distribution.

However the condition relevant here is stronger:

\[
a_p(t)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

This is not the same as a real trace lying near zero. It is an arithmetic congruence condition in \(\mathbf F_p\).

The heuristic prediction is:

\[
\Pr(a_p(t)=0)\approx \frac1p,
\]

hence

\[
\Delta(p)\approx1.
\]

This matches the observed Apéry statistic:

\[
|Z_p|\sim \mathrm{Poisson}(1).
\]

But Katz-Sarnak equidistribution alone is qualitative. It does not automatically provide the uniform finite-field estimate needed for

\[
\Delta(p)=O(1).
\]

# What theorem would actually solve M1?

For Apéry we need something like:

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\#\{r\in\mathbf F_p:b_r=0\}
=O(\pi(X)).
\]

A sufficient local statement would be:

\[
\Delta(p)=O(1)
\]

for almost all p.

A stronger but natural sheaf-theoretic statement would be:

For the trace function

\[
t\mapsto \mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_t|\mathcal V),
\]

one has square-root cancellation for the additive Fourier transform:

\[
\sum_t e_p(u a_p(t))\ll \sqrt p.
\]

Then one would still need a careful treatment of the zero-frequency term and uniformity in the family.

# (3) Achter, Kedlaya, and Newton polygon distribution results

There is important related work:

- Achter studied monodromy and Newton polygon variation in families of abelian varieties.
- Kedlaya studied p-adic cohomology, overconvergent F-isocrystals, and variation of Newton polygons.
- Katz developed the theory of rigid local systems and exponential sums.

These results establish strong information about:

- geometric monodromy groups,
- generic Newton polygons,
- ordinary loci,
- slope stratifications.

They do not generally give:

\[
\#\{t:a_p(t)=0\}.
\]

The obstruction is that trace-zero is a codimension-one condition inside the Frobenius conjugacy class space, whereas Newton polygons describe a different invariant.

---

# Relevance to the Apéry M1 problem

The Apéry situation is unusually favorable:

1. The monodromy is expected to be large:

\[
G=Sp_4.
\]

2. The observed distribution is exactly what random trace heuristics predict:

\[
|Z_p|\approx\mathrm{Poisson}(1).
\]

3. The transfer-matrix computation gives an additional algebraic constraint:

\[
b_r=b_s=0
\Rightarrow
T_{12}(r\to s)=0.
\]

This extra structure is not present in a generic Sp(4) family.

Hence the route for P3.2 is probably not a generic Newton polygon theorem. The missing ingredient is a special trace-fiber estimate exploiting the Apéry recurrence, transfer matrices, and Sp(4) monodromy simultaneously.

---

# Final answer

(1) **No**, Weil bounds and Hasse/Newton polygon methods do not control the number of trace-zero specializations. They control eigenvalue sizes and p-adic slopes, not additive trace equations.

(2) **Katz-Sarnak predicts**

\[
\Delta(p)=O(1)
\]

because trace zero has probability approximately \(1/p\), but a published theorem proving this for generic rank-4 Sp(4) l-adic families is not known in this generality.

(3) **Achter/Kedlaya and related work is adjacent but insufficient**: Newton polygon distribution is weaker than trace-fiber distribution.

For Apéry, the promising direction is a new theorem combining:

- Sp(4) monodromy,
- finite-field trace-function estimates,
- the transfer-matrix vanishing identity,
- and the special hypergeometric structure of the Apéry recurrence.
