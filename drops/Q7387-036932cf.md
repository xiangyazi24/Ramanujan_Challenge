# Q32 literature search: simultaneous gcd of holonomic sequences

## Conclusion

No published theorem currently gives the required estimate
\[
\log\gcd(U_1(n),U_2(n),U_3(n))=o(n)
\]
for these Apéry--LCM moment sequences. The obstruction is not merely a missing citation: the available subspace-theorem and holonomic-gcd results require hypotheses that fail for the present construction.

The nearest relevant results concern either:

1. constant-coefficient linear recurrences / exponential polynomials;
2. gcd of polynomial values or fixed linear recurrence sequences;
3. denominator growth and arithmetic properties of individual G-functions.

None controls simultaneous gcd cancellation among coefficients of
\(B(z)\operatorname{Li}_s(z)\) after the LCM normalization.

## Why Bugeaud--Corvaja--Zannier does not apply

The Bugeaud--Corvaja--Zannier theory (for example, Bugeaud, Corvaja, Zannier, *An upper bound for the GCD of \(a^n-1\) and \(b^n-1\)*, and related Subspace-Theorem applications) concerns sequences represented by exponential polynomials or values of algebraic dynamical objects. The essential input is a representation
\[
G(n)=\sum_i P_i(n)\alpha_i^n
\]
(or an equivalent S-unit structure).

Here
\[
U_s(n)=\sum_{k=1}^n b_{n-k}(L_n/k)^s.
\]
The factors are not fixed exponentials:

* \(b_n\) is P-recursive, not constant-coefficient recurrent;
* \(L_n\) changes with n and introduces new prime factors;
* the summation kernel depends on n.

Thus there is no fixed finite set of algebraic numbers \(\alpha_i\) producing \(U_s(n)\).

## G-functions and holonomic results

The Apéry generating function
\[
B(z)=\sum b_nz^n
\]
is a G-function. The products
\[
B(z)\operatorname{Li}_s(z)
\]
are again expected to be holonomic/G-functions. However, known G-function theorems concern:

* analytic continuation;
* coefficient denominators;
* differential equations;
* growth of individual coefficients.

They do not imply that the gcd of several unrelated coefficient transforms is small.

The failed hypothesis is that one needs arithmetic independence of the three coefficient sequences after reduction modulo primes. Holonomicity alone gives a finite differential equation, not a nonvanishing statement for common residue classes.

## Closest usable arithmetic theorem type

A closest family is the theory of gcd bounds for values of non-degenerate recurrence sequences and polynomial/exponential sequences using the Subspace Theorem. Typical hypotheses include:

* a fixed recurrence operator;
* fixed characteristic roots;
* non-degeneracy (no quotient of roots is a root of unity);
* finitely generated multiplicative groups.

The present \(U_s(n)\) fails at every structural point:

| Required hypothesis | Present sequence |
|---|---|
| fixed recurrence | absent |
| fixed characteristic roots | absent |
| fixed S-unit support | absent because of \(L_n\) |
| finite-dimensional exponential representation | absent |

## Coefficient-product viewpoint

Writing formally
\[
U_s(n)=L_n^s[z^n]B(z)\operatorname{Li}_s(z),
\]
the coefficient sequence is holonomic before the LCM multiplier. Even if one proves that the three coefficient sequences are independent as solutions of a differential system, this does not imply
\[
\gcd(U_1,U_2,U_3)=e^{o(n)}.
\]
The missing ingredient would be a theorem converting linear independence of holonomic functions into quantitative lower bounds for simultaneous reductions modulo primes. No such theorem is known.

## Simultaneous denominator cancellation

Results on denominators of G-functions (Chudnovsky/André type denominator bounds) give estimates of the form
\[
D^n a_n\in\mathbb Z
\]
for suitable constants D. They do not estimate the gcd after multiplying by the rapidly growing sequence \(L_n^s\).

Here the relevant denominator cancellation problem is stronger:
\[
\gcd\left(L_n[z^n]B(z)\operatorname{Li}_1(z),
L_n^2[z^n]B(z)\operatorname{Li}_2(z),
L_n^3[z^n]B(z)\operatorname{Li}_3(z)\right).
\]
No denominator theorem gives this simultaneous cancellation.

## What would be needed for Q32

A theorem sufficient for the conjecture would need a new statement of the following type:

For three explicitly independent holonomic coefficient sequences \(A_s(n)\), after multiplying by controlled common denominators, the common prime divisor set satisfies
\[
\sum_{p\mid A_1(n),A_2(n),A_3(n)}\log p=o(n).
\]

Existing G-function theory does not provide this.

## Final assessment

There is no verified published theorem that settles the required Apéry GCD estimate. The nearest literature gives only individual coefficient denominator/growth control or gcd estimates for fixed exponential recurrences. The precise failed hypothesis is the absence of a fixed linear recurrence/exponential-polynomial representation and the moving LCM prime support.

Therefore the Q32 route remains a new arithmetic problem rather than an application of an existing holonomic gcd theorem.
