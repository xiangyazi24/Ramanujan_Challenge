# Modular/automorphic structure and cross-prime information for the Apéry sequence

## Verdict

There is real automorphic and Galois structure here, but **no existing theorem turns it into the cross-prime estimate required by the Apéry GCD problem**. The obstruction is sharper than “we do not yet know a sufficiently strong Rankin--Selberg bound.” It is a mismatch of quantifiers and of cohomology theories:

```text
standard automorphic/Galois input:
    fix an automorphic object and a residual prime ell,
    then vary unramified Frobenius primes r != ell;

the Apéry event:
    at each varying prime p, reduce at that same prime p,
    while the Mellin character/index also varies with p.
```

Thus standard compatible-system results are **off-diagonal** in the two prime variables `(Frobenius prime, residual characteristic)`, whereas the event \(p\mid b_j\) lies on the **diagonal**.

The strongest precise positive statement is the central-row congruence

\[
 b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod {p^2},
 \qquad
 f_8(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
          \in S_4(\Gamma_0(8)),
\]

for odd good primes \(p\). Hence the central Apéry zero is exactly the event that \(p\) is nonordinary for the fixed non-CM form \(f_8\). But even the distribution of these central nonordinary primes is a difficult open problem, and it controls only one index out of the \(p\) possible indices. For generic \(j\), the relevant object is not the Hecke eigenvalue of a fixed modular form but a **defining-characteristic Mellin coefficient of the K3 trace function**.

The correct missing input is therefore not another fixed symmetric-power \(L\)-function. It is a new theorem of the following kind:

```text
DIAGONAL CRYSTALLINE MELLIN LARGE SIEVE

Uniformly as p varies, control reduction modulo p of the Mellin trace
attached to one prescribed, p-dependent character chi_{p,j(p)}, and prove
a product/dispersion estimate for two distinct defining characteristics
p and q.
```

No current Rankin--Selberg, symmetric-power, Serre-modularity, Fontaine, companion, or Mellin-equidistribution theorem supplies this statement.

## 1. First correct the modular object

### 1.1 The level-\(6\) and level-\(8\) appearances must not be conflated

The eta quotient displayed in the question simplifies to

\[
 f_6(\tau)
 =\eta(\tau)^2\eta(2\tau)^2\eta(3\tau)^2\eta(6\tau)^2,
\]

a weight-\(4\), level-\(6\) newform. Level \(6\) is genuinely present in the Apéry story: the Picard--Fuchs equation has a level-\(6\) modular uniformization, and the repository's K3 model admits a quadratic level-\(6\) elliptic cover with a point of order \(6\).

However, the fixed form whose \(p\)-th Hecke coefficient gives the **central Apéry congruence** is instead

\[
\boxed{
 f_8(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
 =\sum_{n\ge1}a_n(f_8)q^n
 \in S_4(\Gamma_0(8)).
}
\]

This is the non-CM newform with LMFDB label `8.4.a.a`. The project uses

\[
\boxed{
 b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod p,
}
\]

and the theorem of Ahlgren--Ono gives the stronger congruence modulo \(p^2\) in the usual normalization. Thus the level-\(6\) modular parametrization of the **family** and the level-\(8\) modular form attached to the **quadratic central Mellin slice** are two different modular appearances.

In particular, the approximate relation in the question involving \(1+p^3\) and a correction term should not be used. The exact central congruence above is the relevant statement.

### 1.2 Rankin--Selberg must be unitary-normalized

For a weight-\(4\) newform with trivial character,

\[
 L_p(f,s)^{-1}=1-a_p p^{-s}+p^{3-2s},
 \qquad |a_p|\le 2p^{3/2}.
\]

Put

\[
 \lambda_p=\frac{a_p}{p^{3/2}}\in[-2,2].
\]

The Rankin--Selberg pole at \(s=1\) is a statement in unitary normalization; its prime term is governed by

\[
 \sum_p \frac{|\lambda_p|^2}{p^s}.
\]

Without this normalization, \(\sum_p |a_p|^2p^{-s}\) has its natural singular scale at \(s=4\), not at \(s=1\).

### 1.3 Symmetric powers are no longer known only through degree \(4\)

The premise “​\(\operatorname{Sym}^n f\) is automorphic for \(n\le4\)” is now obsolete. Newton--Thorne proved symmetric-power functoriality for **all** symmetric powers of holomorphic Hecke eigenforms. Therefore the issue is not a shortage of fixed symmetric-power lifts.

Even all symmetric powers do not solve the present problem, because they provide all **fixed-order archimedean moments** of \(\lambda_p\), whereas \(p\mid a_p\) is a congruence modulo a modulus that grows with, and equals, the Frobenius prime.

## 2. What Rankin--Selberg and symmetric powers actually control

Rankin--Selberg and symmetric-power \(L\)-functions control expressions such as

\[
 \sum_{p\le X}P(\lambda_p)\log p
\]

for a fixed polynomial \(P\), or equivalently fixed moments of the Sato--Tate distribution. With suitable twists they can also impose fixed congruence conditions on \(p\), and with effective analytic input they give quantitative equidistribution at a fixed macroscopic scale.

They do **not** detect the condition

\[
 p\mid a_p.
\]

Indeed, writing \(a_p=p m_p\), Deligne's bound only gives

\[
 |m_p|\le 2\sqrt p.
\]

Thus nonordinary coefficients may occur throughout the full Sato--Tate interval:

\[
 \lambda_p=\frac{m_p}{\sqrt p},
 \qquad |m_p|\le 2\sqrt p.
\]

This is not a shrinking interval around \(0\). It is a \(p\)-dependent arithmetic sublattice of the possible integral coefficients. Detecting it requires residue-class information at resolution \(1/p\), not a fixed continuous test function of \(\lambda_p\).

This distinction is especially clear by comparison with weight \(2\). For an elliptic curve,

\[
 |a_p|\le 2\sqrt p,
\]

so \(p\mid a_p\) forces \(a_p=0\) for \(p>4\). There the nonordinary condition is a trace-zero condition. In weight \(4\), \(p\mid a_p\) allows approximately \(4\sqrt p\) different nonzero values, so it is genuinely a congruence condition.

### A useful no-go lemma for moment methods

Suppose a set of primes \(\mathcal S\) has density \(0\). Altering \(\lambda_p\) arbitrarily for \(p\in\mathcal S\), while keeping \(|\lambda_p|\le2\), does not change any limiting fixed moment or the Sato--Tate measure. The expected nonordinary set is much thinner still: the random-residue heuristic predicts

\[
 \Pr(p\mid a_p)\asymp\frac1p,
 \qquad
 \#\{p\le X:p\mid a_p\}\asymp C_f\log\log X.
\]

A set of this size is invisible to every fixed-moment limit. Even knowing all fixed symmetric powers therefore does not imply the desired divisibility estimate. One would need uniformity in the symmetric-power degree growing with \(p\), together with integral congruence information; existing automorphy gives neither such uniform analytic control nor the required residual observable.

The same objection applies to fourth, sixth, or higher Rankin--Selberg moments. They remain one-prime marginal moments of a complex-normalized trace, not a local limit theorem modulo \(p\).

## 3. The exact modular information: one central row

For the Apéry numbers

\[
 b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\]

Ahlgren--Ono, completing a conjecture of Beukers, prove for odd primes the supercongruence

\[
\boxed{
 b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod {p^2}.
}
\]

Consequently

\[
 p\mid b_{(p-1)/2}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p\mid a_p(f_8).
\]

For a weight-\(4\) form, the right side says that \(f_8\) is **nonordinary at \(p\)**.

This has three genuine consequences for the project.

1. It gives an exact interpretation of the only reflection-fixed index.
2. Since \(b_{p-1-j}\equiv b_j\pmod p\), all other roots occur in reflected pairs, so
   \[
   |Z_p|\equiv \mathbf 1_{p\mid a_p(f_8)}\pmod2.
   \]
3. It explains the contrast with the ζ(2) Apéry sequence. There the relevant form is CM and inert primes can force central vanishing with positive density. The form \(f_8\) is non-CM, so there is no analogous inert-prime mechanism.

But this is only one row. It gives no bound for

\[
 Z_p=\{0\le j<p:p\mid b_j\}
\]

beyond its parity. Even a complete theorem proving the conjectural \(O(\log\log X)\) count of nonordinary primes for \(f_8\) would not control the remaining \(p-1\) coefficient positions.

Moreover, the distribution of nonordinary primes for a fixed non-CM weight-\(4\) form is itself open. The literature through August 18, 2026 does not provide a density theorem for the set

\[
 \{p:p\mid a_p(f_8)\}.
\]

The paper of Jin--Ma--Ono studies nonordinary primes and shows, among other things, that one can construct forms nonordinary at any prescribed finite set; it does not give the needed horizontal distribution for this fixed form. The repository therefore correctly notes that even the central slice contains a recognized open problem.

## 4. Genuine cross-prime information from compatible systems: the off-diagonal theorem

Let \(f\) be a fixed newform and fix an auxiliary prime \(\ell\). Deligne attaches

\[
 \rho_{f,\ell}:G_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell),
\]

with an integral lattice and residual representation

\[
 \bar\rho_{f,\ell}:G_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell).
\]

For a prime \(r\nmid N\ell\), the representation is unramified at \(r\), and

\[
 \operatorname{tr}\bar\rho_{f,\ell}(\operatorname{Frob}_r)
 \equiv a_r(f)\pmod\ell,
 \qquad
 \det\bar\rho_{f,\ell}(\operatorname{Frob}_r)
 \equiv r^3\pmod\ell.
\]

This is exactly the setting of Chebotarev. If the residual image is large, then for fixed \(\ell\)

\[
 \#\{r\le X:a_r(f)\equiv0\pmod\ell\}
\]

has the density predicted by the fraction of trace-zero matrices in the residual image, approximately \(1/\ell\). For two fixed auxiliary primes \(\ell_1,\ell_2\), the product representation and Goursat/open-image arguments often yield the corresponding product density, approximately \(1/(\ell_1\ell_2)\). This is genuine cross-prime/residual independence.

But its quantifiers are

\[
 \boxed{
 \ell\text{ fixed},\quad r\text{ varying},\quad r\ne\ell.
 }
\]

The desired event has

\[
 \boxed{
 \ell=r=p.
 }
\]

At \(r=p=\ell\), the residual representation \(ar\rho_{f,p}\) is ramified at \(p\). There is no unramified conjugacy class

\[
 \bar\rho_{f,p}(\operatorname{Frob}_p)
\]

to which Chebotarev can be applied. The coefficient \(a_pmod p\) is instead encoded by the local crystalline representation at \(p\).

This is the **diagonal obstruction**. It persists even if the adelic image of the compatible system is maximal. Adelic/open-image independence tells us about reductions modulo \(\ell_1,\ell_2\) of Frobenius at a third prime \(r\nmid \ell_1\ell_2N\); it does not count local reduction types at the primes \(\ell_1\) and \(\ell_2\) themselves.

A useful matrix picture is

\[
 (a_r\bmod\ell)_{r,\ell}.
\]

Compatible-system Chebotarev controls fixed columns \(\ell\) away from the diagonal. The Apéry central event asks for the diagonal entries \(a_p\bmod p\).

This explains why product residual monodromy, although valuable, does not by itself imply independence of

\[
 p\mid a_p(f),\qquad q\mid a_q(f).
\]

## 5. What Fontaine and integral \(p\)-adic Hodge theory do give

For \(p\nmid N\), the \(p\)-adic representation \(V_p(f)\) is crystalline. For the weight-\(4\) form \(f_8\), its Hodge--Tate weights are

\[
 \{0,3\},
\]

and crystalline Frobenius has characteristic polynomial

\[
\boxed{
 X^2-a_p(f_8)X+p^3.
}
\]

Thus

\[
 p\nmid a_p(f_8)
\]

is exactly the ordinary case: the filtered φ-module has a slope-\(0\) unit-root line. When \(p\mid a_p(f_8)\), there is no slope-\(0\) eigenvalue. Fontaine--Laffaille theory for \(p>3\) also describes the integral reduction and the local shape of \(ar\rho_{f_8,p}|_{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}\).

This is a precise and useful **local interpretation** of the central Apéry zero. But it does not count the primes for which that local interpretation occurs. Fontaine's functors are applied one prime at a time:

\[
 V_p \longleftrightarrow D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V_p).
\]

They do not provide a probability law or a large sieve coupling the strongly divisible lattices for different \(p\). Hida, Coleman, eigenvariety, and \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function methods likewise fix a prime \(p\) and vary weights or characters \(p\)-adically; they do not average the diagonal condition over the rational primes \(p\).

Crystalline companions are relevant but do not remove the obstruction. At each fixed finite-field characteristic \(p\), a crystalline companion can reproduce the Frobenius characteristic polynomials of an ℓ-adic sheaf. It does not create independent residual quotients as \(p\) varies, and it does not turn reduction modulo the base characteristic into an auxiliary-prime Chebotarev problem.

So Fontaine theory **translates** the defining-characteristic condition into slopes/Hasse invariants; it does not provide the missing horizontal anti-concentration theorem.

## 6. The generic Apéry coefficient is a K3 Mellin coefficient, not a Hecke coefficient of one fixed form

The repository's toric/K3 calculation gives a fixed rank-\(3\) primitive trace function \(	heta_p(a)\), coming from the transcendental part of the K3 fiber, such that for \(1\le j\le p-2\), up to the harmless sign/exponent convention,

\[
\boxed{
 b_j\equiv-​\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
       \theta_p(a)a^j\pmod p.
}
\]

Equivalently, if \(\omega_p\) is the Teichmüller character and

\[
 \chi_{p,j}=\omega_p^j,
\]

then \(b_jmod p\) is the reduction of a multiplicative Mellin trace

\[
 \mathscr M_{p,j}
 =\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
   \widetilde\theta_p(a)\chi_{p,j}(a)
\]

at a prime above \(p\). Thus

\[
 p\mid b_j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathfrak p_p\mid\mathscr M_{p,j}.
\]

This formula is the right bridge to cohomology, but it also exposes two independent obstacles.

### 6.1 The character moves

At \(j=(p-1)/2\), the character has order \(2\). This fixed quadratic Kummer twist is why a fixed modular form \(f_8\) emerges.

For generic \(j\), the order

\[
 \frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,j)}
\]

tends to infinity. There is no fixed modular form whose \(p\)-th Hecke coefficient equals every such Mellin trace. Even if one proved automorphy separately for each fixed-order twist, that would produce only finitely many special “beacon” indices such as \(j=a(p-1)/d\) for fixed \(d\). It would not control a growing set of \(j\)'s or the full zero count \(Z_p\).

### 6.2 The required observable is residual, not complex

Deligne bounds and automorphic \(L\)-functions control complex absolute values and Frobenius conjugacy classes. The event here is not

\[
 \mathscr M_{p,j}=0
\]

as an algebraic integer. It is

\[
 \mathscr M_{p,j}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.
\]

A nonzero algebraic integer of generic complex size may vanish modulo its defining prime. Consequently, square-root bounds, purity, bounded conductor, and complex equidistribution do not imply residual anti-concentration.

The repository's explicit K3 theorem makes this distinction concrete. It improves the pointwise fiber trace to \(O(p)\) with fixed cohomological rank, but it expressly does not bound the number of zero Mellin coefficients. Exact cancellation modulo \(p\) can still occur.

### Coefficient zeros are not supersingular fibers

Let

\[
 A_p(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jT^j\in\mathbb F_p[T]
\]

be the Hasse polynomial in the project. The modular/K3 geometry naturally studies **evaluation zeros** \(A_p(t)=0\), which correspond to nonordinary or supersingular fibers. The set \(Z_p\) instead consists of **coefficient zeros** \(b_j=0\). These are dual Mellin questions. A theorem about the number of supersingular fibers does not bound coefficient sparsity.

This is exactly why the rich K3 geometry does not immediately imply \(|Z_p|=O(1)\).

## 7. The newest Mellin equidistribution result gets closer, but still misses twice

Forey--Fresán--Kowalski developed the convolution-Tannakian framework for Mellin transforms of sheaves. A very recent result of Bah--Shuddhodan, arXiv:2608.00304 (July/August 2026), proves uniform stratified generic vanishing and equidistribution for bounded-complexity perverse sheaves on tori over finite fields of **varying characteristic**, under a common Tannakian-group hypothesis.

This is important: one should no longer say simply that “horizontal Mellin equidistribution with varying characteristic does not exist.” It now does in an all-character, complex-Frobenius sense.

It still does not solve the Apéry problem for two separate reasons.

1. **All characters versus one selected character.** The theorem averages over essentially all multiplicative characters in each field. The project asks for one deterministic section
   \[
   \chi_p=\omega_p^{j(p)}.
   \]
   One character has mass \(1/(p-1)\) in the all-character empirical measure and may be exceptional for every \(p\) without affecting equidistribution.

2. **Complex conjugacy class versus defining-prime divisibility.** The theorem controls unitary Frobenius conjugacy classes/complex trace distributions. It does not test whether the corresponding algebraic integer is divisible by the prime over the base characteristic \(p\).

Thus even granting all geometric and Tannakian hypotheses for the Apéry K3 Mellin sheaf, the selected-character and same-prime reductions remain outside the theorem.

The compatible-system large sieve has the same problem in another form. It reduces an ℓ-adic sheaf modulo auxiliary primes \(\ell\ne p\) while the base field has characteristic \(p\). Setting \(\ell=p\) is not an admissible specialization. The diagonal cannot be obtained as a limiting case of the off-diagonal sieve.

## 8. The modular parametrization is not a CM-value formula for \(b_j\)

The classical modular parametrization has the form

\[
 F(t(\tau))=\sum_{n\ge0}b_n\,t(\tau)^n,
\]

where \(t\) is a modular function/Hauptmodul and \(F\) is a modular period. This says that \(b_n\) is a **Taylor coefficient in the modular local parameter \(t\)**. It does not say that \(b_n\) is

- the \(n\)-th Hecke eigenvalue of a fixed cusp form,
- the value of a modular form at a CM point, or
- the Frobenius trace of a fixed motive indexed by \(n\).

A Fourier coefficient and a value at a point are different types of data. Cauchy's coefficient formula can express \(b_n\) as a contour integral of a modular function, but that does not turn it into a CM value with class-field-theoretic prime factorization.

The central congruence is not produced by evaluating at a CM point. It is produced by the fact that the finite-field Mellin character at \(j=(p-1)/2\) is quadratic, giving a fixed Kummer twist and hence the fixed form \(f_8\).

CM theory can be decisive when a fixed CM motive is actually present: inert primes force trace zero, and Chebotarev gives a positive-density law. That explains the ζ(2) analogue. For the ζ(3) sequence, \(f_8\) is non-CM, and no family of CM values representing all \(b_j\) is known or suggested by the modular parametrization.

At most, modular transformations and fixed-order Kummer twists can produce finitely many special-position beacons for each fixed denominator \(d\). A bounded number of beacons per \(p\) cannot control \(Z_p\) or the moving indices \(j=m-p\) occurring in the top-window GCD problem.

## 9. Why Serre modularity does not add a cross-prime constraint

For each \(p\), the residual representation

\[
 \bar\rho_{f_8,p}:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)
\]

is already known to arise from \(f_8\). Serre's conjecture, now a theorem, classifies odd irreducible residual representations by modular forms; applying it to a representation already constructed from \(f_8\) is therefore logically tautological.

The theorem can identify the Serre weight and conductor and can constrain the local representation at \(p\). It does not say how often, as \(p\) varies, the local crystalline representation is ordinary or nonordinary.

Large-image theorems for \(ar\rho_{f_8,p}\) also do not help with \(p\mid a_p\). They describe the global image and allow Chebotarev at primes \(r\ne p\). The desired datum is local at the ramified prime \(p\). Product-image independence for \(ar\rho_{f,p}\times\bar\rho_{f,q}\) controls Frobenius at a third prime \(r\nmid pqN\); it does not make the local conditions at \(p\) and \(q\) independent.

This is analogous to the fact that Serre open image for a non-CM elliptic curve does not by itself solve the distribution of supersingular primes. The diagonal local condition is an additional arithmetic problem.

## 10. Why the proposed cross-prime chain does not close

Suppose first that we restrict to the central row. The desired pair event is

\[
 p\mid a_p(f_8),
 \qquad
 q\mid a_q(f_8).
\]

No standard Rankin--Selberg \(L\)-function has coefficients equal to this pair of indicators. Multiplicativity gives \(a_{pq}=a_pa_q\), but the size bound on \(a_{pq}\) leaves ample room for both divisibilities and yields no sparsity. Symmetric powers give moments of \(a_p/p^{3/2}\) and \(a_q/q^{3/2}\), not residues modulo \(p\) and \(q\).

For generic Apéry rows the mismatch is larger. The pair event is

\[
 \mathfrak p_p\mid\mathscr M_{p,j_p},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak p_q\mid\mathscr M_{q,j_q},
\]

where both the finite field and the character vary. There is no fixed global automorphic representation whose two Euler factors encode these two events. Product monodromy of the underlying K3 local systems is necessary for an eventual independence theorem, but it controls rational/ℓ-adic Frobenius distributions, not reduction of each Mellin trace at its own characteristic.

In the top-window formulation \(j_p=m-p\), the residue is also tied arithmetically to the same prime that supplies the modulus. The fixed-quotient calculation in `chatgpt_q32_fixed_exponent_mellin_attack.md` shows that the geometric condition reduces exactly to

\[
 p\mid b_{m-p}
\]

along two affine prime-divisor rays after reflection. This is useful, but it confirms that the residual decision is a prime-divisor correlation for the fixed characteristic-zero Apéry sequence, not a standard automorphic Frobenius condition.

Therefore the chain

\[
 \text{symmetric-power automorphy}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{high moments}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{cross-prime divisibility independence}
\]

has a genuine missing implication in its final arrow.

## 11. What automorphy can still contribute

The direction is not empty. It contributes four concrete pieces.

### 11.1 It identifies exact special slices

The quadratic character gives the central congruence with \(f_8\). Other fixed-order characters may yield additional fixed-motive or automorphic beacons. These can settle finitely many rows and reveal forced symmetries.

### 11.2 It proves nondegeneracy and bounded cohomological complexity

The K3/elliptic description supplies a fixed-rank local system, purity, explicit singularities, and large geometric monodromy. These are precisely the geometric hypotheses one would need before attempting a new crystalline Mellin sieve.

### 11.3 It gives rigorous off-diagonal models of independence

For fixed auxiliary ℓ, Chebotarev and product residual images show the kind of \(1/\ell\) and \(1/(\ell_1\ell_2)\) laws one expects. They provide a correct heuristic template, although not the diagonal theorem.

### 11.4 It gives the right probabilistic prediction

Non-CM Sato--Tate plus an additional unproved local-uniformity assumption predicts

\[
 \Pr(p\mid a_p(f_8))\sim\frac1p,
\]

and the same \(1/p\)-scale is natural for a generic residual Mellin coefficient. This supports the repository's sparse Poisson model. The \(1/p\) factor comes from the extra local-uniformity assumption, not from Sato--Tate alone.

## 12. The precise new theorem one would need

There are two levels.

### 12.1 Central diagonal nonordinary theorem

Even the following would be new and significant:

\[
 N_{f_8}(X)
 :=\#\{p\le X:p\mid a_p(f_8)\}
 =o(\pi(X)),
\]

with the conjectural strengthening

\[
 N_{f_8}(X)\sim C_{f_8}\log\log X.
\]

A pair-correlation form would assert that the local nonordinary conditions at two varying rational primes behave independently. This would clarify the central parity bit of \(Z_p\), but it would still not bound \(|Z_p|\).

### 12.2 Diagonal crystalline Mellin anti-concentration

Let \(\mathscr M_{p,j}\) be an integral Mellin trace lifting \(-b_jmod p\). A one-prime theorem strong enough for bounded Apéry zeros would be

\[
\boxed{
 \#\{j\in\mathbb Z/(p-1):
       \mathfrak p_p\mid\mathscr M_{p,j}\}=O(1)
}
\]

uniformly in \(p\), or at least \(O(p^{1/2-δ})\).

For the cross-prime GCD problem, the more relevant statement is a **selected-section dispersion theorem**. Schematically, for every controlled moving section \(j_p\) arising from a quotient cell,

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}
 \left(
  \mathbf1_{\mathfrak p_p\mid\mathscr M_{p,j_p}}
  -\mu_{p,j_p}
 \right)
 =o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right),
}
\]

with \(\mu_{p,j_p}\) at the expected \(1/p\) scale, together with the bilinear/product version

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}
 \left(\mathbf1_{p\mid b_{j_p}}-\mu_p\right)
 \left(\mathbf1_{q\mid b_{j_q}}-\mu_q\right)
 =o(\text{diagonal mass})
}
\]

for the weights and moving indices required by the project.

A theorem of this form must simultaneously handle:

1. varying finite-field characteristic;
2. one deterministic character per field, not an average over all characters;
3. reduction at the defining prime, not complex trace equidistribution;
4. product independence for two different crystals/character sections.

That is why it is reasonable to call it a **diagonal crystalline Mellin large sieve**. It is not presently a corollary of any standard automorphic theorem.

## 13. Direct answers to the six questions

### 1. Does Rankin--Selberg give useful cross-prime information?

It gives fixed normalized moments and, after fixed twists, averages over Frobenius primes in fixed arithmetic conditions. It does **not** control \(p\mid b_j\), \(p\mid a_p\), or pairwise independence of such diagonal events. The pole concerns \(|a_p|^2/p^3\), not divisibility of \(a_p\) by \(p\).

### 2. Do higher symmetric powers solve the problem?

No. In fact all symmetric powers are now automorphic, not only those through degree \(4\). They yield all fixed Sato--Tate moments, but a \(p\)-dependent congruence class is invisible to fixed moments. Detecting it would require uniform, growing-degree and integral information absent from the theory.

### 3. Is \(p\mid b_n\) connected to Hecke eigenvalues?

Exactly at the central index:

\[
 p\mid b_{(p-1)/2}
 \iff p\mid a_p(f_8).
\]

For generic \(n=j\), the correct connection is to a K3 Mellin trace \(\mathscr M_{p,j}\), not to the \(p\)-th coefficient of one fixed modular form. Fixed-order character slices may provide finitely many additional modular beacons, but no growing family is known.

### 4. Can CM theory determine the prime divisors of the relevant coefficients?

Not for the ζ(3) Apéry sequence. The modular parametrization expresses \(b_n\) as Taylor coefficients in a modular parameter, not as CM values. The central form \(f_8\) is non-CM. CM explains systematic vanishing in the ζ(2) analogue, but no comparable mechanism exists here.

### 5. Can the integral \(p\)-adic representation and Fontaine theory relate the defining-characteristic Frobenius?

Yes locally: \(p\mid a_p(f_8)\) is exactly the absence of a unit-root line in the crystalline realization, and the relative K3 crystal/Hasse--Witt matrix encodes the mod-\(p\) Apéry data. But Fontaine theory gives a dictionary at each fixed \(p\), not a horizontal distribution theorem or cross-prime sieve. It reformulates the obstruction rather than removing it.

### 6. Does Serre modularity constrain cross-prime behavior?

No new constraint of the required kind. The residual representations are already modular because they come from \(f_8\). Serre modularity classifies them but does not count primes at which the local crystalline representation is nonordinary. Big residual image applies to Frobenius away from the residual characteristic, not at the diagonal prime itself.

---

## 14. Final project-level assessment

The automorphic/Galois direction is valuable as a **structural reduction**, not as a completed proof route.

It establishes:

- a fixed non-CM modular form controlling the central root;
- a fixed-rank K3/elliptic local system controlling the first Mellin layer;
- large monodromy and off-diagonal residual independence;
- a compelling \(1/p\), sparse-Poisson heuristic.

It does not establish:

- a bound for generic coefficient zeros \(b_j\bmod p\);
- a selected-character theorem as \(p\) varies;
- defining-prime residual anti-concentration;
- the two-prime dispersion needed for all-\(n\) Apéry GCD.

So the deepest honest verdict is:

```text
There is a powerful automorphic skeleton, but the missing muscle is a new
same-characteristic theorem.  The target is not another symmetric-power
L-function; it is diagonal crystalline Mellin anti-concentration, with a
product version across p and q.
```

Proving such a theorem would indeed be a major and potentially complete route to the one-prime and cross-prime problems. Existing automorphic and Galois machinery supplies many of its geometric hypotheses, but not the decisive residual estimate.
