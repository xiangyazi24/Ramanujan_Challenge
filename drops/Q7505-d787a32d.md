# High-order Kummer characters: exact Mellin reduction, barriers, and the missing cross-prime estimate

## Verdict

Taking the Q7495 bounded-order estimate in the form stated in the question,

\[
L_D(n)\ :=\!\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt n\;\text{direct target}\\
\operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^{\,n\bmod p})\le D}}\log p
\ \ll\ D^2\log(nD),
\]

I do **not** get a sublinear bound for the complementary high-order part from the exact formulas in `hasse_franel_descent.tex` and `toric_mellin_square.tex`, even after allowing the strongest natural complex/\(\ell\)-adic square-root cancellation one could hope to extract from the K3/elliptic trace family.

There is, however, a sharp deterministic explanation of the failure.

For a high-order target, the toric formula does **not** say that a complex or \(\ell\)-adic trace is zero. It says that a cyclotomic Mellin algebraic integer is divisible by **one chosen degree-one prime above \(p\)**. Since the character order \(d\) divides \(p-1\), the rational prime \(p\) splits completely in \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_d)\). Therefore a single mod-\(p\) zero gives only one prime-ideal divisibility among \(\varphi(d)\) conjugate places. A norm/product-formula argument then pays Archimedean height proportional to \(\varphi(d)\) while gaining only one factor \(p\). High character order makes this imbalance worse.

Likewise:

* complex character orthogonality controls \(|\mathcal M_p(\chi)|\), not divisibility of \(\mathcal M_p(\chi)\) at a chosen prime above \(p\);
* ordinary uncertainty principles allow prescribed high-order Mellin coordinates to vanish;
* the Franel/CFVZ square is a coordinate identity in a triangular basis, not a positive norm;
* a classical large sieve has no common coefficient sequence across the varying primes, and the exact zero detector is a defining-characteristic, nonlinear condition;
* Katz/Deligne-type equidistribution or trace bounds, even if optimal on the complex side, do not bridge this defining-characteristic divisibility gap and do not address the every-\(n\) cross-prime alignment quantifier.

The clean missing input is a **defining-characteristic moving-character large sieve**, not an ordinary complex trace large sieve. In its weakest useful \(L^2\) form it only has to prove, for some growing cutoff \(D=D(N)\) with

\[
D^2\log(ND)=o(N),
\]

that a centered high-order Hasse-zero sum has dyadic variance \(o(N^2)\). That already rules out a linear spike at one single index and therefore combines with Q7495 to prove the all-\(n\) theorem.

A quantitative version with a power saving in \(D\),

\[
V_D(N)\ll N^2D^{-\eta}(\log N)^A,
\qquad \eta>0,
\]

would give the explicit optimized cutoff

\[
D=N^{2/(4+\eta)}(\log N)^{(A-2)/(4+\eta)}
\]

and hence

\[
\log\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),b_n)
\ll
n^{2/3}
+n^{4/(4+\eta)}
 (\log n)^{(2A+\eta)/(4+\eta)},
\]

up to the already-bankable discrepancy terms. Any \(\eta>0\) is enough for \(o(n)\); if \(\eta\ge2\), the existing \(n^{2/3}\) discrepancy becomes the dominant term.

No claim below proves the Apéry GCD conjecture.

# 2. Exact high-order Mellin reformulation

Let

\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p,
\]

with \(p>\sqrt n\). Put

\[
d=d_{p,n}:=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathbf F_p^\times}(a\mapsto a^r)
=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,r)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Because \(p\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\),

\[
r=n-qp\equiv n-q\pmod{p-1},
\]

so equivalently

\[
\boxed{
 d_{p,n}=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-q)}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For \(d>1\), necessarily \(1\le r\le p-2\).

The toric fiber identity in `toric_mellin_square.tex` gives

\[
\sum_{a\ne0}\mu_p(a)a^r=-b_r\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p,
\tag{2.3}
\]

where \(\mu_p(a)\) is the integer point count of the nonzero fiber \(\Lambda=a\). The symmetric-square calculation in `toric_fiber_sym2.tex` gives the exact integer decomposition

\[
\mu_p(a)=p^2-6p+12+\theta_p(a),
\tag{2.4}
\]

with \(\theta_p(a)\) an explicit symmetric-square or inert Asai elliptic trace. Since \(r\not\equiv0\pmod{p-1}\),

\[
\sum_{a\ne0}a^r=0\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p,
\]

so the constant term in (2.4) drops out and

\[
\boxed{
 b_r=-\sum_{a\ne0}\theta_p(a)a^r\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

This is the exact place where the Kummer character enters.

## 2.1 Cyclotomic lift

Let \(K_d=\mathbf Q(\zeta_d)\). Since \(d\mid p-1\), \(p\nmid d\) and \(p\equiv1\pmod d\), so \(p\) splits completely in \(K_d\).

Choose a prime \(\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_{p,r}\mid p\) and a multiplicative character

\[
\widetilde\chi_{p,r}:\mathbf F_p^\times\longrightarrow\mu_d\subset K_d
\]

whose reduction modulo \(\mathfrak p\) is \(a\mapsto a^r\). Define the cyclotomic Mellin value

\[
\mathcal M_{p,r}
:=\sum_{a\ne0}\theta_p(a)\widetilde\chi_{p,r}(a)
\in\mathcal O_{K_d}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Reduction of (2.6) modulo \(\mathfrak p\), followed by (2.5), gives the exact equivalence

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathfrak p_{p,r}\mid\mathcal M_{p,r}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus the high-order problem is a **one-prime-ideal divisibility problem for a moving cyclotomic Mellin value**.

This is categorically different from

\[
\mathcal M_{p,r}=0\quad\text{in }\mathbf C
\]

or from the vanishing of an \(\ell\)-adic trace.

# 3. What the exact K3 formula gives on the complex side

The symmetric-square formulas imply a uniform elementary Hasse bound on each fiber. In the split and branch cases,

\[
\theta_p(a)=a_p(E)^2-p-\{0,p\},
\]

and \(|a_p(E)|\le2\sqrt p\). In the inert case,

\[
\theta_p(a)=\chi_p(-3)a_{p^2}(E)-p-\{0,p\},
\]

with \(|a_{p^2}(E)|\le2p\). Hence in every case

\[
\boxed{|\theta_p(a)|\le6p.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The trivial Archimedean estimate from (2.6) is therefore

\[
|\sigma\mathcal M_{p,r}|\ll p^2
\tag{3.2}
\]

for every complex embedding \(\sigma:K_d\hookrightarrow\mathbf C\).

A geometrically nontrivial fixed-conductor Mellin sheaf would suggest square-root cancellation of the shape

\[
|\sigma\mathcal M_{p,r}|\ll p^{3/2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

I do **not** need to assume or prove (3.3). The no-go below is stronger: **even granting (3.3) uniformly for every high-order character does not control (2.7).**

This is the first key distinction requested in the question. Complex/\(\ell\)-adic square-root cancellation bounds the Archimedean size of an algebraic integer. The target condition is divisibility by a selected prime of its coefficient field.

# 4. Exact product-formula barrier

The obstruction can be stated as a small lemma independent of the Apéry geometry.

## Lemma 4.1 — one split prime is invisible to Archimedean height

Let \(d\mid p-1\), choose \(t\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}\) of exact order \(d\) modulo \(p\), and put

\[
K_d=\mathbf Q(\zeta_d),
\qquad
\mathfrak p=(p,\zeta_d-t).
\]

Then

\[
\alpha=t-\zeta_d\in\mathfrak p,
\tag{4.1}
\]

but for every complex embedding \(\sigma\),

\[
|\sigma(\alpha)|\le p,
\tag{4.2}
\]

while

\[
N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(\alpha)=\pm\Phi_d(t)
\]

is divisible by \(p\).

### Proof

Since \(t\) has exact order \(d\) modulo \(p\), \(\Phi_d(t)\equiv0\pmod p\), so \((p,\zeta_d-t)\) is a degree-one prime above \(p\) and (4.1) is immediate. Every conjugate is \(t-\zeta_d^u\), whose absolute value is at most \(t+1\le p\). The norm identity is the defining product for \(\Phi_d(t)\). \(\square\)

This example satisfies an Archimedean bound **stronger** than the hypothetical K3 bound (3.3), yet it is divisible by the selected prime above \(p\) and is not zero.

Therefore no argument of the form

> “Deligne/Katz gives \(|\mathcal M|\ll p^{3/2}\), hence \(\mathfrak p\mid\mathcal M\) is rare”

is valid without additional p-adic information.

## 4.2 Norm bookkeeping gets worse with high order

From an optimistic uniform bound (3.3), one only obtains

\[
\log|N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(\mathcal M_{p,r})|
\le
\varphi(d)\left(\frac32\log p+O(1)\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The target condition (2.7) gives merely

\[
v_p\!\left(N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(\mathcal M_{p,r})\right)\ge1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The important point is that (4.4) has **no factor \(\varphi(d)\)**. Because \(p\) splits completely, the chosen congruence is at one of the \(\varphi(d)\) different primes above \(p\); it need not propagate to any of the other primes.

Thus the local efficiency is

\[
\frac{\text{forced finite-place log mass}}
     {\text{available Archimedean log height}}
\asymp\frac1{\varphi(d)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

High order is therefore the *worst* regime for a cyclotomic norm argument.

Even a hypothetical Galois-orbit amplification

\[
v_p(N\mathcal M)\ge c\varphi(d)
\tag{4.6}
\]

would merely compare \(c\varphi(d)\log p\) with the \((3/2)\varphi(d)\log p\) Archimedean budget. A purely local norm contradiction would require, in this weight-three normalization, an average p-adic multiplicity strictly larger than \(3/2\) per conjugate. The actual target supplies only one valuation at one split prime.

There is no common global norm over different \(p\)'s whose height is independently bounded: both \(d\) and the cyclotomic field vary with \(p\). Multiplying the individual norms simply adds the same excessive local heights.

# 5. Character orthogonality does not see the target condition

For a fixed prime \(p\), let \(G=\mathbf F_p^\times\) and let \(\widehat G\) be its complex character group. Put

\[
\mathcal M_p(\chi)=\sum_{a\in G}\theta_p(a)\chi(a).
\]

Ordinary multiplicative Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}|\mathcal M_p(\chi)|^2
=(p-1)\sum_{a\in G}|\theta_p(a)|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Using (3.1), the right side is \(O(p^4)\). This is a perfectly valid complex second moment. It gives no upper bound for

\[
\#\{r:\mathfrak p_{p,r}\mid\mathcal M_{p,r}\},
\]

because p-adic divisibility does not imply small complex absolute value.

Even replacing (5.1) by arbitrary higher complex moments has the same logical defect unless one proves a quantitative bridge from the chosen \(\mathfrak p\)-adic valuation to all complex conjugates.

## 5.1 Why high character order does not repair Parseval

The order restriction \(d>D\) only chooses a subset of the Fourier coordinates. It does not change the identity (5.1), and there is no positive lower bound for a nonzero complex Mellin coefficient that becomes stronger with \(d\).

At the algebraic level, \(p\equiv1\pmod d\) makes the residue-degree issue maximally unfavorable: all character values already live in degree-one residue fields over \(\mathbf F_p\).

# 6. Ordinary uncertainty principles are also insufficient

The obstacle is visible already in finite Fourier linear algebra.

Let \(G\) be any finite cyclic group and choose any pair of high-order characters \(\chi,\chi^{-1}\). Prescribe a complex Fourier transform by

\[
\widehat f(\psi)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\psi=\chi,\chi^{-1},\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The inverse Fourier transform exists uniquely. It has full or essentially full support and bounded size after the standard normalization, while its two prescribed high-order Mellin coordinates vanish exactly.

Thus support-product, Donoho--Stark-type, or rank-only uncertainty statements cannot exclude even **exact complex** zeros at selected high-order characters. The actual Apéry condition (2.7), which asks only for a reduction modulo one prime ideal to vanish, is weaker still.

An uncertainty theorem could become useful only if it used an extra property special to the actual K3 trace function \(a\mapsto\theta_p(a)\) and produced a quantitative statement about its **defining-characteristic reductions**, not merely its complex Fourier support.

# 7. Why the Franel/CFVZ square does not give positivity

The exact descent in `hasse_franel_descent.tex` says

\[
K_p(x)^2=(1+x)^{p-1}A_p(\phi(x))
=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m\Psi_{p,m}(x),
\tag{7.1}
\]

where the \(\Psi_{p,m}\) form a triangular basis with diagonal one. Hence

\[
b_m=[K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,m}}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

It also gives

\[
A_p(a)=\Delta(a)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(a)^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

These are strong Apéry-specific identities, but they do not make the coordinate in (7.2) a positive norm. A square polynomial over \(\mathbf F_p\) can have zero coordinates in a non-orthogonal triangular basis. The repository's toric formula makes this even more explicit:

\[
b_j=-\sum_{t\ne0}t^j(1-t)^{p-1-2j}A_j(t)^2
\tag{7.4}
\]

for \(j\le(p-1)/2\). This is an **indefinite finite-field pairing**, not a positive sum of squares.

So neither (7.1), (7.3), nor (7.4) turns \(b_j=0\) into a complex norm-zero statement.

The square structure is extremely useful for identifying the geometry; it does not supply the missing cross-prime anti-alignment.

# 8. Why a standard large sieve does not close the every-\(n\) problem

There are two different large-sieve ideas one might try.

## 8.1 A multiplicative-character large sieve

Classical multiplicative large sieves control expressions of the type

\[
\sum_{\chi\bmod p}\left|\sum_m c_m\chi(m)\right|^2
\]

for a **common coefficient sequence** \((c_m)\), or variants with uniformly controlled families.

Here the object being sampled is instead

\[
\mathcal M_{p,r}
=\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\theta_p(a)\widetilde\chi_{p,r}(a),
\]

and the coefficient function \(\theta_p(a)\) itself changes with \(p\). More importantly, the event is not a bound on \(|\mathcal M_{p,r}|\); it is

\[
v_{\mathfrak p_{p,r}}(\mathcal M_{p,r})\ge1.
\]

Thus ordinary complex large-sieve inequalities are not inequalities for the quantity we need.

## 8.2 Detecting the zero by additive characters

One can write formally

\[
\mathbf 1_{x=0\;(\bmod p)}
=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(ux).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Substituting \(x=b_r\) makes the inner phase

\[
e_p\!\left(u\sum_a\theta_p(a)a^r\right).
\]

This is the additive character of an **already summed Mellin coefficient**. It does not factor into a bounded-conductor one-variable trace sum in \(a\). Using instead Fermat's detector

\[
\mathbf 1_{x=0}=1-x^{p-1}
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p
\]

introduces tensor/exponent complexity growing with \(p\). Either way, zero detection destroys the fixed-complexity setting in which a routine Deligne large sieve would operate.

This is the second exact categorical gap: square-root cancellation for the trace function is not square-root cancellation for the **zero set of its defining-characteristic Mellin transform**.

# 9. Why Katz equidistribution is not the missing step

Even an ideal equidistribution theorem for the normalized complex quantities

\[
p^{-3/2}\mathcal M_p(\chi)
\]

as \(\chi\) varies would have two unresolved quantifier changes.

1. **Complex trace versus mod-\(p\) coefficient.** The target is \(\mathfrak p\mid\mathcal M_p(\chi)\), not \(\mathcal M_p(\chi)=0\) in \(\mathbf C\). Lemma 4.1 shows these can be completely different events even under much stronger Archimedean bounds.
2. **Within one characteristic versus one moving character in every characteristic.** The all-\(n\) problem chooses one character \(\chi_{p,n}\) for each different prime \(p\). A theorem saying that each row has few exceptional characters does not prevent all chosen characters from landing in those exceptional positions for one adversarial \(n\).

Thus I do not use Katz equidistribution as a solution, and no such implication is hidden in the checked-in formulas.

# 10. Product over rational primes returns the linear-height carrier

The direct lower-digit condition already implies, by the Apéry Lucas congruence,

\[
p\mid b_r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid b_n
\]

on the direct channel. Hence the whole direct target radical divides \(b_n\). This gives only

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{p>\sqrt n}(b_n)
\le\log|b_n|=\Theta(n),
\tag{10.1}
\]

which is the original linear wall.

Replacing \(b_n\) by the product of the local cyclotomic norms (4.3) is not a compression: the field degree \(\varphi(d)\) appears separately at each prime, so the total Archimedean height is at least as badly scaled as the direct carrier and generally much worse in the high-order regime.

There is therefore no useful deterministic product-over-primes bound produced by the current Mellin/K3 identities.

# 11. A scoped countermodel to “high order should decorrelate”

The order condition itself is not horizontally selective.

Fix a quotient \(q\) and an index \(n\). The order formula (2.2) shows that whenever

\[
\gcd(p-1,n-q)=1,
\]

the selected character has maximal order \(d=p-1\). It is therefore logically possible for an entire fixed-\(q\) prime arc to consist of maximal-order selected characters.

More strongly, at the level of the second-order Apéry recurrence alone, local target lines in the two-dimensional initial-state space can be prescribed independently modulo different primes and then assembled by CRT into one integral initial state. Hence recurrence-universal transfer/continuant identities cannot prove that maximal-order selected characters avoid a fixed column.

This does **not** construct an alternative K3 pencil and does not refute an Apéry-specific geometric theorem. Its scope is precise: any successful high-order argument must use information about the distinguished Apéry/K3 initial object which is absent from the recurrence, character order, reflection, and generic Fourier linear algebra.

# 12. No unconditional \(D\)-saving emerges from the exact formulas

Let

\[
H_D(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\
p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\\
d_{p,n}>D}}
\log p.
\tag{12.1}
\]

The exact formulas above give no estimate of the shape

\[
H_D(n)\ll nD^{-\eta}
\]

or even \(o(n)\), uniformly in every \(n\). The only deterministic bound available from prime counting alone is still

\[
H_D(n)\le\vartheta(n)=O(n),
\tag{12.2}
\]

with no decrease as \(D\) grows.

Consequently the Q7495 splitting currently yields only

\[
\log\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),b_n)
\le
O(n^{2/3})+O(D^2\log(nD))+O(n).
\tag{12.3}
\]

One may take, for example,

\[
D=\frac{\sqrt n}{(\log n)^2},
\]

for which the low-order term is

\[
O\!\left(\frac{n}{(\log n)^3}\right),
\]

but (12.2) leaves the high-order contribution linear. This is a useful localization of the obstruction, not a new bound for the theorem.

In general the low-order side permits any

\[
D=o\!\left(\sqrt{\frac n{\log n}}\right).
\tag{12.4}
\]

So a future high-order theorem is allowed to begin very deep in the character spectrum; bounded and moderately growing orders are already disposable.

# 13. The minimal large-sieve estimate that would finish

There is a clean formulation which is weaker than an every-\(n\) high-order theorem and is exactly adapted to the desired “large-sieve” route.

For \(0\le r<p\), define

\[
\delta_{p,D}(r)
=
\mathbf 1_{\{p\mid b_r\}}
\mathbf 1_{\{(p-1)/\gcd(p-1,r)>D\}},
\tag{13.1}
\]

and its row density

\[
\rho_{p,D}=\frac1p\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}\delta_{p,D}(r).
\tag{13.2}
\]

By (2.7), (13.1) may equivalently be written as the indicator that the appropriate high-order cyclotomic Mellin value is divisible by its selected prime \(\mathfrak p_{p,r}\).

For \(N<n\le2N\), put

\[
X_D(n)=
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}
(\log p)\bigl(\delta_{p,D}(n\bmod p)-\rho_{p,D}\bigr)
\tag{13.3}
\]

and

\[
V_D(N)=\sum_{N<n\le2N}|X_D(n)|^2.
\tag{13.4}
\]

## Proposition 13.1 — exact threshold for this \(L^2\) route

Suppose there is a function \(D=D(N)\to\infty\) such that

\[
D(N)^2\log(ND(N))=o(N)
\tag{13.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{V_{D(N)}(N)=o(N^2).}
\tag{13.6}
\]

Then the direct high-order target mass is \(o(n)\) for every \(n\), and Q7495 plus the existing \(O(n^{2/3})\) discrepancy proves

\[
\log\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),b_n)=o(n).
\]

### Proof

First, by (1.1),

\[
\rho_{p,D}\le\frac{Z(p)}p\ll p^{-1/3}.
\]

For \(N<n\le2N\), partial summation with \(\vartheta(x)\ll x\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}(\log p)\rho_{p,D}
&\ll\sum_{p\le2N}(\log p)p^{-1/3}\\
&\ll N^{2/3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13.7}
\]

By definition,

\[
H_D(n)
=
X_D(n)
+
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}(\log p)\rho_{p,D}.
\]

Therefore

\[
H_D(n)\ll N^{2/3}+|X_D(n)|.
\tag{13.8}
\]

For every single \(n\in(N,2N]\),

\[
|X_D(n)|^2\le V_D(N)=o(N^2),
\]

so \(|X_D(n)|=o(N)\) uniformly over the dyadic block. Equation (13.8) yields \(H_D(n)=o(N)\). Add the Q7495 low-order term using (13.5), and add the already-controlled discrepancy. \(\square\)

### Why (13.6) is the right threshold

A single forbidden spike

\[
|X_D(n_0)|\ge cN
\]

already forces

\[
V_D(N)\ge c^2N^2.
\]

Thus \(o(N^2)\) is essentially the sharp variance threshold for excluding a linear spike by this method. It is far weaker than proving a good pointwise estimate for every row or every character.

This is the minimal genuinely new **cross-prime** statement I would target.

# 14. Quantitative crystalline/moving-character large sieve and optimized \(D\)

A power-saving version of the missing estimate would be

\[
\boxed{
V_D(N)\ll N^2D^{-\eta}(\log N)^A
}
\qquad(\eta>0),
\tag{14.1}
\]

uniformly over the range of \(D\) used below.

Then (13.8) gives

\[
H_D(n)
\ll
N^{2/3}
+ND^{-\eta/2}(\log N)^{A/2}.
\tag{14.2}
\]

Combining with Q7495 gives

\[
\log\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),b_n)
\ll
N^{2/3}
+D^2\log(ND)
+ND^{-\eta/2}(\log N)^{A/2}.
\tag{14.3}
\]

Balancing the last two terms, with \(\log(ND)\asymp\log N\), gives

\[
D^{2+\eta/2}
=N(\log N)^{A/2-1},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
D=N^{2/(4+\eta)}
  (\log N)^{(A-2)/(4+\eta)}.
}
\tag{14.4}
\]

Substitution yields

\[
\boxed{
\log\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),b_n)
\ll
N^{2/3}
+
N^{4/(4+\eta)}
(\log N)^{(2A+\eta)/(4+\eta)}.
}
\tag{14.5}
\]

For every \(\eta>0\), the exponent \(4/(4+\eta)\) is strictly below one. Thus **any genuine power of \(D\) in the defining-characteristic cross-prime variance closes the all-index theorem.**

If \(\eta\ge2\), then \(4/(4+\eta)\le2/3\), so the existing \(N^{2/3}\) discrepancy is the dominant scale.

# 15. What the missing estimate must know geometrically

Estimate (14.1) cannot be a statement only about complex traces. Written using (2.7), its basic atom is

\[
\mathbf1_{\{v_{\mathfrak p_{p,r}}(\mathcal M_{p,r})\ge1\}},
\tag{15.1}
\]

where

* the characteristic \(p\) varies;
* the cyclotomic field \(K_d\) varies;
* the character \(\chi_{p,r}\) varies and has order \(>D\);
* the prime \(\mathfrak p_{p,r}\mid p\) varies with the chosen reduction of the character;
* the selected exponent is the moving section \(r=n\bmod p\).

So the genuinely relevant object is a **Hasse-Witt/crystalline divisibility process on the Mellin family**, sampled along one moving character in each characteristic.

A useful theorem would have to correlate those defining-characteristic divisibilities across distinct primes. For example, either of the following would suffice:

1. the variance estimate (13.6)/(14.1);
2. a stronger pointwise estimate
   \[
   H_D(n)\ll nD^{-\eta}(\log n)^A,
   \]
   which is more than needed;
3. a crystalline Galois-orbit theorem strong enough to turn one selected-prime divisibility into a positive proportion of conjugate p-adic divisibilities **together with** an independent global height carrier. Orbit amplification without a common height carrier is not enough, by Section 4.

The first is the weakest and most directly matched to a large-sieve strategy.

---

# 16. Why a single-prime “few bad high-order characters” theorem is still not enough

Suppose one proved, optimistically, that for every \(p\) there are only \(O(1)\) high-order characters \(\chi\) for which the Hasse/Mellin reduction vanishes. That is still a **row bound**.

The every-\(n\) problem chooses one prescribed character in each row. A family of rows with one bad character each can align those bad characters along a single column. This is the same row-versus-column quantifier obstruction already present in the Apéry zero formulation.

Therefore the missing theorem must be either:

* cross-prime, as in (13.6); or
* a much stronger arithmetic classification of the bad characters which makes their positions incompatible with the congruence \(r=n\bmod p\).

Plain single-prime equidistribution does neither.

# 17. Final adjudication

The exact Mellin/K3 identities successfully identify the high-order target as

\[
\mathfrak p_{p,r}\mid\mathcal M_{p,r}
\]

for a cyclotomic Mellin value of an explicit symmetric-square/Asai trace family. They do **not** convert the target into complex trace zero.

The decisive barriers are:

1. **split-prime product formula:** \(p\equiv1\pmod d\) means one target imposes only one degree-one prime-ideal divisibility in a field of degree \(\varphi(d)\); high order worsens norm efficiency;
2. **orthogonality mismatch:** Parseval and complex large sieves measure Archimedean magnitudes, not selected-prime divisibility;
3. **uncertainty mismatch:** Fourier support inequalities permit prescribed high-order zeros and contain no cross-prime alignment information;
4. **square-not-norm:** the Franel/CFVZ and toric square formulas are finite-field coordinate identities with indefinite pairing;
5. **quantifier mismatch:** even excellent within-\(p\) distribution does not control one moving character in every different characteristic.

So I find no rigorous high-order saving from the present formulas alone. The precise reopen condition is the defining-characteristic moving-character variance estimate (13.6), or quantitatively (14.1). Combined with Q7495, any power saving in \(D\) in that variance suffices, with the optimized cutoff and final exponent given in (14.4)--(14.5).
