# P3.2 saturated Bezout construction: homogeneous transfer descent

## Result

Let

\[
S_m:=3d_mP_m,
\qquad
R_m:=\mathbf Z[S_m^{-1}],
\]

with

\[
a_t=4t(68t^2+3),\qquad c_t=(2t-1)^3,
\]

\[
X_0=1,\quad X_1=0,\quad
X_{t+1}=a_tX_t-c_t^2X_{t-1},
\]

\[
d_t=\prod_{1\le s<t}(2s+1)^3,
\qquad h_t=6(t^2-1)d_t,
\]

\[
P_m=\prod_{t=2}^mX_t,
\qquad e_m=c_mX_{m-1}-5X_m,
\]

and

\[
Y_1=1,\qquad
Y_n=X_nY_{n-1}-h_nP_{n-1}.
\]

I get a completely constructive Euclidean descent in the localization, with every denominator explicit. It does **not** by itself prove the saturation statement; rather, it reduces it exactly to one terminal integer gcd. This is sharper than a generic resultant statement and identifies the precise arithmetic lemma still missing.

The descent has four useful features.

1. The moving endpoint ratio is homogenized, so **no moving boundary coefficient is ever inverted**.
2. Every inverse used in the proof lies in the multiplicative system generated by the factors of `d_m` and `P_m`.
3. The descent can be composed into one exact **integer** elimination identity
   \[
   \boxed{P_mH_m=\sigma_2Y_m+W_me_m.}
   \tag{I}
   \]
4. Consequently
   \[
   \boxed{(e_m,Y_m)R_m=(e_m,H_m)R_m.}
   \tag{II}
   \]
   Thus Q1921's saturation claim is equivalent to the explicit terminal assertion
   \[
   \boxed{\operatorname{Supp}\gcd(e_m,H_m)
   \subseteq \operatorname{Supp}(3d_mP_m).}
   \tag{T}
   \]

If `(T)` is proved, then ordinary extended Euclid plus `(I)` gives explicit integers
\(\mathcal A_m,\mathcal B_m,N_m\) satisfying

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_me_m+\mathcal B_mY_m=S_m^{N_m}.}
\]

I give the formulas below. What I do **not** obtain from the known continuant recurrence is a proof of `(T)` itself. The descent terminates there, for a structural reason at the singular initial pivot `X_1=0`.

## 1. Denominator ledger in the localization

The relevant facts are elementary but need to be kept explicit.

Since

\[
d_m=\prod_{j=2}^m c_j,
\]

every

\[
c_2,\ldots,c_m
\]

is a unit of `R_m`. Since

\[
P_m=\prod_{j=2}^mX_j,
\]

every

\[
X_2,\ldots,X_m
\]

is also a unit of `R_m`. In particular `X_2=-1`.

Thus the only inverses allowed below are products of

```text
c_2,...,c_m,  X_2,...,X_m,  and 3.
```

In fact the construction below never needs to invert `3` separately. More importantly, it never divides by a moving endpoint numerator, by an `a_t`, or by a new Euclidean remainder.

That last point is essential.

## 2. Why the naive affine endpoint descent is invalid

For a scalar boundary parameter `q`, put

\[
E_n(q):=c_nX_{n-1}-qX_n.
\]

Using

\[
X_n=a_{n-1}X_{n-1}-c_{n-1}^2X_{n-2},
\]

one obtains, whenever one is allowed to divide by `q`,

\[
E_n(q)
=q\,c_{n-1}E_{n-1}(q'),
\]

where

\[
q'=rac{qa_{n-1}-c_n}{q\,c_{n-1}}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is the tempting `m -> m-1` ratio descent, but it is not a valid descent in `R_m`: after the first step, `q'` need not be a unit.

The smallest concrete warning is `m=3`. Here

\[
a_2=2200,\qquad c_2=27,\qquad c_3=125,
\]

so from `q=5`,

\[
q_2=rac{5a_2-c_3}{5c_2}
=rac{10875}{135}
=rac{725}{9}
=rac{5^2\cdot29}{3^2}.
\]

The prime `29` is not in the localization support

\[
3d_3P_3,
\]

and in fact it is a primitive factor of `e_3=10875`. Any induction which next divides by the numerator of `q_2` has silently inverted exactly the kind of prime that the proof is supposed to exclude.

So the affine/Riccati form is the wrong representation for a saturated Bezout proof.

## 3. Homogeneous endpoint transfer: no forbidden divisions

Fix `m`. Define integer pairs `(lambda_n,mu_n)` downward by

\[
\lambda_m=5,\qquad \mu_m=1,
\]

and, for `n=m,m-1,...,3`,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{n-1}&=a_{n-1}\lambda_n-c_n\mu_n,\\
\mu_{n-1}&=c_{n-1}\lambda_n.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\binom{\lambda_{n-1}}{\mu_{n-1}}
=
T_n\binom{\lambda_n}{\mu_n},
\qquad
T_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
a_{n-1}&-c_n\\
c_{n-1}&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Define the homogeneous endpoint residual

\[
\mathcal E_n
:=\mu_nc_nX_{n-1}-\lambda_nX_n.
\]

A direct substitution of the continuant recurrence gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_n
&=(\mu_nc_n-a_{n-1}\lambda_n)X_{n-1}
  +\lambda_nc_{n-1}^2X_{n-2}\\
&=\mu_{n-1}c_{n-1}X_{n-2}
  -\lambda_{n-1}X_{n-1}\\
&=\mathcal E_{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_n=e_m\quad(2\le n\le m).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

At the bottom, since `X_1=0` and `X_2=-1`,

\[
\boxed{\lambda_2=e_m.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is the denominator-free replacement for the unsafe moving ratios `(2.1)`.

### Unimodularity of the moving homogeneous pair

The transfer determinant is

\[
\det T_n=c_nc_{n-1},
\]

which is a unit of `R_m`. The starting pair `(5,1)` is unimodular. Hence every pair `(lambda_n,mu_n)` is unimodular in `R_m`.

This can be made completely explicit. Put

\[
r_m=0,\qquad s_m=1,
\]

and recurse by

\[
(r_{n-1},s_{n-1})=(r_n,s_n)T_n^{-1}.
\]

Since

\[
T_n^{-1}
=rac1{c_nc_{n-1}}
\begin{pmatrix}
0&c_n\\
-c_{n-1}&a_{n-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\]

this is

\[
\boxed{
r_{n-1}=-\frac{s_n}{c_n},
\qquad
s_{n-1}=\frac{r_nc_n+s_na_{n-1}}{c_nc_{n-1}}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus

\[
r_n\lambda_n+s_n\mu_n=1
\tag{3.5}
\]

for every `n`, and the only denominators in `r_n,s_n` are products of the `c_j`, hence factors of powers of `d_m`.

Even more specifically, combining `(3.5)` with

\[
e_m=\mu_nc_nX_{n-1}-\lambda_nX_n
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
-\frac{r_n}{X_n}e_m
+
\left(
 s_n+r_n\frac{c_nX_{n-1}}{X_n}
\right)\mu_n
=1.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

So `mu_n` is invertible modulo the ideal `(e_m)` in `R_m`, with an explicit inverse. The only additional denominator in `(3.6)` is `X_n`, already inverted through `P_m`.

No `lambda_n` or `mu_n` has been inverted as an integer.

## 4. Homogeneous descent for the reciprocal-trace term

The endpoint transfer alone does not carry `Y_n`. Introduce a second integer pair `(rho_n,sigma_n)` by

\[
\rho_m=0,\qquad \sigma_m=1,
\]

and, again for `n=m,m-1,...,3`, define

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{n-1}
&=\lambda_n(\rho_n+h_n\sigma_n),\\
\sigma_{n-1}
&=c_n\mu_n\sigma_n.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4.1}
\]

All four sequences `lambda,mu,rho,sigma` are integral. There is no division in `(3.1)` or `(4.1)`.

Now put

\[
G_n:=\sigma_nY_n-\rho_nP_{n-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

At the top,

\[
G_m=Y_m.
\]

For `n>=3`, using

\[
Y_n=X_nY_{n-1}-h_nP_{n-1},
\qquad
P_{n-1}=X_{n-1}P_{n-2},
\]

and the endpoint identity `(3.2)`, a direct calculation gives the exact **integer** one-step elimination

\[
\boxed{
X_nG_{n-1}
=c_n\mu_nG_n
 +(\rho_n+h_n\sigma_n)P_{n-2}e_m.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently, in `R_m`,

\[
G_{n-1}
=\frac{c_n\mu_n}{X_n}G_n
 +\frac{(\rho_n+h_n\sigma_n)P_{n-2}}{X_n}e_m.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Every denominator in `(4.4)` is exactly `X_n`.

### The one-step ideal equality

Equation `(4.4)` gives

\[
(e_m,G_{n-1})R_m\subseteq(e_m,G_n)R_m.
\]

For the reverse inclusion, use the explicit inverse of `mu_n` modulo `e_m` from `(3.6)`. Write

\[
\alpha_n=-\frac{r_n}{X_n},
\qquad
\beta_n=s_n+r_n\frac{c_nX_{n-1}}{X_n},
\]

so that

\[
\alpha_ne_m+\beta_n\mu_n=1.
\]

Multiplying `(4.4)` by `beta_n X_n/c_n` gives the explicit reverse elimination

\[
\boxed{
G_n
=\beta_n\frac{X_n}{c_n}G_{n-1}
+
\left(
 \alpha_nG_n
 -\beta_n\frac{(\rho_n+h_n\sigma_n)P_{n-2}}{c_n}
\right)e_m.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
(e_m,G_n)R_m=(e_m,G_{n-1})R_m
\qquad(3\le n\le m).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The denominators in `(4.5)` are products of `c_j` and `X_n`, and therefore lie entirely in the prescribed localization.

Iterating `(4.6)` gives

\[
(e_m,Y_m)R_m=(e_m,G_2)R_m.
\tag{4.7}
\]

## 5. The exact terminal scalar

The descent stops at `n=2`. Here

\[
h_2=486,\qquad X_2=-1,\qquad Y_2=-487,\qquad P_1=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
G_2
=\sigma_2Y_2-\rho_2
=-487\sigma_2-\rho_2.
\]

Define the integer

\[
\boxed{H_m:=\rho_2+487\sigma_2.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
G_2=-H_m,
\]

and `(4.7)` becomes

\[
\boxed{
(e_m,Y_m)R_m=(e_m,H_m)R_m.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the promised constructive reduction.

### Why there is no legitimate `2 -> 1` continuation

The formula `(4.3)` used

\[
P_{n-1}=X_{n-1}P_{n-2}.
\]

For `n=2` this would require

\[
P_1=X_1P_0.
\]

But `P_1=1` while `X_1=0`, so no choice of finite `P_0` can satisfy it. This is the singular initial pivot of the continuant.

Thus one cannot append a fake final Euclidean step and use `Y_1=1` to manufacture a unit. Any argument which does so has divided through the singular `X_1=0` seam.

The genuine terminal datum is exactly the constant `487` in `(5.1)`.

## 6. Compose the descent into one exact integer Bezout elimination

The localization proof above can be compressed into a useful denominator-free identity.

For `n>=2`, set

\[
\Delta_2=1,
\qquad
\Delta_n=\prod_{j=3}^nX_j,
\]

and

\[
M_2=1,
\qquad
M_n=c_n\mu_nM_{n-1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Also set

\[
W_2=0,
\]

and for `3<=n<=m`, define

\[
\boxed{
W_n
=X_nW_{n-1}
 +M_{n-1}(\rho_n+h_n\sigma_n)P_{n-2}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Multiplying `(4.3)` successively gives, by induction,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_nG_2=M_nG_n+W_ne_m.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

At `n=m`,

\[
\Delta_m=\prod_{j=3}^mX_j=-P_m
\]

because `X_2=-1`, while

\[
M_m=\prod_{j=3}^m c_j\mu_j=\sigma_2.
\]

Together with `G_2=-H_m` and `G_m=Y_m`, equation `(6.3)` becomes

\[
\boxed{
P_mH_m=\sigma_2Y_m+W_me_m.
}
\tag{I}
\]

This is an identity in `Z`; it contains **no denominators at all**.

It is the cleanest Euclidean elimination I obtain from the transfer recurrence.

## 7. Exact equivalence with the saturated ideal statement

Identity `(I)` immediately shows, after inverting `P_m`, that

\[
H_m\in(e_m,Y_m)R_m.
\]

Conversely, each `mu_n` is invertible modulo `e_m` by `(3.6)`, and each `c_n` is already a unit. Therefore

\[
\sigma_2=\prod_{n=3}^m c_n\mu_n
\]

is invertible modulo `e_m` in `R_m`.

For complete constructivity, multiply the identities `(3.6)` for `n=3,...,m`, inserting the units `c_n^{-1}`. This produces explicitly some `eta_m,epsilon_m in R_m` satisfying

\[
\eta_m\sigma_2+\epsilon_me_m=1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

No new denominator appears: the denominators are products of `c_j` and `X_j` only.

From `(I)` and `(7.1)`,

\[
Y_m
=\eta_mP_mH_m
 +e_m(\epsilon_mY_m-\eta_mW_m),
\]

so

\[
Y_m\in(e_m,H_m)R_m.
\]

This proves `(II)`:

\[
\boxed{(e_m,Y_m)R_m=(e_m,H_m)R_m.}
\]

Now let

\[
g_m:=\gcd(e_m,H_m)>0.
\]

Since `e_m,H_m` are ordinary integers,

\[
(e_m,H_m)R_m=R_m
\]

if and only if `g_m` is a unit in `R_m`, i.e. if and only if every prime divisor of `g_m` divides `S_m`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(e_m,Y_m):(3d_mP_m)^\infty=\mathbf Z
\iff
\operatorname{Supp}(g_m)
\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(3d_mP_m).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is not merely a sufficient reduction: it is an exact equivalence.

So the remaining arithmetic statement is the explicit terminal-prime lemma `(T)`, with `H_m` generated by the finite recurrences `(3.1)`, `(4.1)`, `(5.1)`.

## 8. Explicit `A_m,B_m,N_m` once the terminal support lemma is known

This part is completely constructive and uses only the ordinary integer extended Euclidean algorithm.

Compute integers `u_m,v_m` with

\[
\boxed{u_me_m+v_mH_m=g_m.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Substitute the integer identity `(I)` into `(8.1)` after multiplying by `P_m`:

\[
P_mu_me_m+v_mP_mH_m=P_mg_m,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
(P_mu_m+v_mW_m)e_m
 +(v_m\sigma_2)Y_m
 =P_mg_m.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This is already an explicit integer Bezout identity. Its only potentially unwanted prime factors on the right are those in `g_m`.

Assume the terminal support lemma `(T)`. Then every prime of `P_mg_m` divides `S_m`. Choose

\[
\boxed{
N_m=
\max_{\ell\mid P_mg_m}
\left\lceil
\frac{v_\ell(|P_mg_m|)}{v_\ell(|S_m|)}
\right\rceil,
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

with the empty maximum interpreted as `0`. Then

\[
Q_m:=\frac{S_m^{N_m}}{P_mg_m}\in\mathbf Z.
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_m
&:=Q_m(P_mu_m+v_mW_m),\\
\mathcal B_m
&:=Q_mv_m\sigma_2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Equations `(8.2)`--`(8.4)` give exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_me_m+\mathcal B_mY_m=S_m^{N_m}
=(3d_mP_m)^{N_m}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus, once `(T)` is supplied, the requested Bezout coefficients and exponent are not existential: they are obtained by the displayed finite recurrences plus one integer `xgcd`.

No resultant is needed.

\boxed{

 +162000\cdot10875.

## 10. Relation to the Q1914/Q1921 obstruction

Q1914's target-prime descent showed that, after imposing the endpoint relation modulo `p=2m+1`, all reversible steps collapse to one terminal division-free scalar `F_m`. Q1921 showed that the natural determinant/Desnanot--Jacobi geometry does not force a contradiction and isolated the saturated ideal as the correct target.

The present calculation explains both phenomena at the integral level:

- `(lambda_n,mu_n)` is the denominator-safe homogeneous endpoint transfer;
- `(rho_n,sigma_n)` carries the accumulated reciprocal-trace source;
- `H_m=rho_2+487 sigma_2` is the integral terminal obstruction;
- the exact integer identity `(I)` is the Euclidean elimination hidden behind the localization;
- the singular `X_1=0` seam is why the descent stops rather than reaching the unit `Y_1=1`.

Modulo any prime `ell` not dividing `S_m`, if `ell|e_m`, then every `mu_n` and `sigma_2` is nonzero by the unimodularity argument. Reducing `(I)` modulo `ell` therefore gives

\[
\ell\mid Y_m
\iff
\ell\mid H_m.
\]

So a common prime outside the prescribed support exists **exactly when** it divides the terminal pair `(e_m,H_m)`.

## 11. What remains unproved

The transfer recurrence and Euclidean elimination do **not** force

\[
\gcd(e_m,H_m)
\]

to have support in `3d_mP_m`. They reduce the original statement to that claim without losing or adding any off-support prime.

In particular, the following would be an unjustified last step:

```text
(lambda_2,H_m) is a terminal integer pair,
therefore its gcd is supported on the transfer determinants.
```

That conclusion does not follow from unimodularity over the localization; it is precisely what has to be proved. The transfer matrices control which divisions are legal, but they do not by themselves compute the final integer gcd.

Therefore the current exact status is:

1. **Constructive descent proved:** equations `(3.1)`, `(4.1)`, `(4.3)` give an exact `n -> n-1` Euclidean induction with every denominator accounted for.
2. **Exact integer elimination proved:** `P_mH_m=sigma_2Y_m+W_me_m` with `H_m,W_m` given by explicit integer recurrences.
3. **Saturation equivalence proved:** the Q1921 ideal is the unit ideal exactly when `gcd(e_m,H_m)` has no prime outside `3d_mP_m`.
4. **Explicit certificate algorithm proved conditionally on that terminal support:** `(8.1)`--`(8.5)` construct the requested integers `A_m,B_m,N_m` using only extended Euclid and integer arithmetic.
5. **Missing lemma:** prove `(T)` for the terminal pair. No generic resultant statement can replace this, and no additional division by a moving transfer coefficient is legitimate.

So this route does give the requested constructive localization/Euclidean reduction, but it does **not** yet close the all-`m` saturated Bezout theorem. The remaining problem has been compressed to one explicit terminal integer gcd with no hidden denominators.
