# 8. Why subresultants and Bezout do not amplify the local capacity

Let

\[
f=N_h(X),
\qquad
g=N_k(X+h).
\]

A marked common root \(x\pmod p\) supplies one evaluation functional on the Sylvester cokernel. The exact lower bound is one null direction:

\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\ker \overline{\operatorname{Sylv}}(f,g)
\ge1.
\]

To force a second \(p\)-adic unit one would need an additional theorem such as

- two distinct common roots;
- a multiple common root/nontransverse intersection;
- or divisibility of the first nonzero subresultant as well as the resultant.

None follows from one marked three-zero configuration.

If the reduced gcd is exactly linear, the degree-one subresultant is nonzero and simply recovers the marked factor \(X-x\). It is a **root locator**, not a second vanishing invariant.

The reflection presentation is integrally equivalent to the first Sylvester presentation, and (5.1) puts the combined-gap polynomial in the same ideal. Therefore all Bezout constants produced solely from these three equations lie in the same elimination package. Evaluating several such identities at the same root repeatedly proves divisibility by the same prime; it does not prove a larger Smith corank or a higher valuation.

The elementary model

\[
f=X,
\qquad
g=X+p
\]

has one common root modulo \(p\), Sylvester corank one, and resultant exactly \(p\). This is not an Apéry counterexample; it certifies the precise logical scope: **one common root plus ordinary Bezout/Sylvester algebra does not formally imply \(p^2\)**. An Apéry-specific transversality or multiplicity theorem would be genuinely new input.

Finally, the two upper primes live in characteristics \(q_1,q_2\), whereas all these Sylvester minors live in characteristic \(p\). There is no implication

or \(q_i\)-divisibility of a \(p\)-subresultant. The only currently forced bridge is the common integer \(b_n\), which is exactly what (7.2) uses.

Thus the new coupled carrier is useful locally but not globally on the large-product region.

## 9.2 Common-row/capacity envelope

The common-row carrier has \(O_J(T)\) possible row indices, each of logarithmic height \(O_J(T)\). With capacity \(R\), its aggregate height is \(O_J(RT^2)\), giving

\[
\#\mathcal M_{\epsilon\eta}^{\mathrm{live}}
\ll_J
\frac{RT^2}{\log T}+D_{\epsilon\eta,R}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Even if the deficiency vanishes,

\[
\frac{RT^2/\log T}{RT^2/(\log T)^2}=\log T.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Neither \(z u_1u_2>\Xi\) nor \(hk>\Psi\) reduces the number of possible common row indices or the height \(O(T)\) of one \(b_n\).

## 9.3 Unconditional vertical-moment envelope

For one prime \(p\), one zero triple supports at most

\[
3\quad(++),
\qquad
6\quad(+-),
\qquad
3\quad(--)
\]

post-OR configurations. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\#\mathcal M_{++}^{\mathrm{live}}
&\le3\sum_{p\asymp_JT}\binom{Z(p)}3,\\
\#\mathcal M_{+-}^{\mathrm{live}}
&\le6\sum_{p\asymp_JT}\binom{Z(p)}3,\\
\#\mathcal M_{--}^{\mathrm{live}}
&\le3\sum_{p\asymp_JT}\binom{Z(p)}3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.6}
\]

Using

\[
Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}
\]

and (0.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}\binom{Z(p)}3
&\le
\frac{\max Z(p)}6
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}Z(p)(Z(p)-1)\\
&\ll_J
T^{2/3}
T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}\\
&=
\boxed{O_J\!\left(T^{43/15}L^{-3/5}\right).}
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.7}
\]

The live product filters only shrink the left side, but the named inputs supply no extra factor depending on \(\Xi\) or \(\Psi\).

For

\[
R\asymp\frac{T^{2/5}}{L^{6/5}\Omega},
\]

the desired unweighted scale is

\[
A_R(T)
\asymp
\frac{T^{12/5}}{L^{16/5}\Omega}.
\tag{9.8}
\]

The ratio of (9.7) to (9.8) is

\[
\boxed{
T^{7/15}L^{13/5}\Omega,
}
\tag{9.9}
\]

so the vertical second moment is far from sufficient.

The logarithmically weighted versions have the same relative failure: multiply both the event envelope and target by one factor of \(\log T\).

# 10. Exact horizontal reformulation

For a row \(n\), define the quotient-six hit set

\[
\mathcal H_6(n)
:=
\left\{
\ell\text{ prime}:
0\le n-6\ell<\ell,
\ \ell\mid b_{n-6\ell}
\right\},
\tag{10.1}
\]

and

\[
K_6(n):=|\mathcal H_6(n)|.
\]

Every live lower-primary marked pair gives three distinct quotient-six hits

\[
\{p,q_1,q_2\}\subset\mathcal H_6(n).
\tag{10.2}
\]

The map

\[
e\longmapsto(n,\{p,q_1,q_2\})
\tag{10.3}
\]

is injective:

- \(p\) is the smallest prime in the triple;
- \(z=7p-1-n\);
- each row \((p,q_i,n)\) is recovered;
- the candidate secondary is
  \[
  a_i^+=6(q_i-p)-z-1
  \]
  on plus and
  \[
  a_i^-=7(q_i-p)+z-p
  \]
  on minus;
- the deterministic OR rule recovers the retained sign;
- sorting \(\{z,a_1,a_2\}\) recovers \(h,k\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\#\mathcal M^{\mathrm{live}}
\le
\sum_{T<n\le2T}\binom{K_6(n)}3.
}
\tag{10.4}
\]

More exactly, the live tail is a specified subset of the triples on the right satisfying the sign, \(z u_1u_2>\Xi\), and \(hk>\Psi\) conditions.

This identifies the first honest aggregate input which would close the reduction:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{T<n\le2T}
\#\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\{p,q_1,q_2\}\subset\mathcal H_6(n):\\
\text{the recovered post-OR pair is legal},\\
z u_1u_2>\Xi,\ hk>\Psi
\end{array}
\right\}
=o_J\!\left(A_R(T)\right).
}
\tag{10.5}
\]

The broader sufficient statement

\[
\sum_{T<n\le2T}\binom{K_6(n)}3
=o_J(A_R(T))
\tag{10.6}
\]

is stronger than necessary.

The result proved in `Q32_AVERAGE_SECOND_FACTORIAL_RESULTANT_BOUND.md` is a **vertical** second factorial moment in \(p\). It does not control the restricted horizontal third factorial moment (10.5). That change of orientation and moment order is the genuine remaining arithmetic gap.

# 11. Corrected theorem

## Theorem — sign-resolved coupled carrier and scoped obstruction

Fix \(J\). Let \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{live}}_{\epsilon\eta}(T;\Xi,\Psi)\) be the post-OR unordered marked pairs described at the start of this audit.

For every pair, sort its three lower \(p\)-zeros as

\[
x<x+h<x+h+k.
\]

Then:

1. \(h,k\ge2\), \(h+k<p\), and
   \[
   p\mid N_h(x),\qquad p\mid N_k(x+h).
   \]

2. The exact sign formulas are
   \[
   d_+=(z+a+1)/6,
   \quad q_+=p+d_+,
   \quad u_+=p-a-2z-2,
   \]
   and
   \[
   d_-=(p-z+a)/7,
   \quad q_-=p+d_-,
   \quad u_-=(p-z-6a-7)/7.
   \]

3. The configuration fibres over \((p,x,h,k)\) are at most \(3,6,3\) in sectors \(++,+-,--\).

4. With the standard resultant convention,
   \[
   S_{h,k}=S_{k,h}>0.
   \]
   Reflection gives an integrally equivalent Sylvester system and no second independent determinantal condition.

5. The combined-gap equation is in the ideal generated by the two adjacent-gap equations, and its resultant factors as (5.3). Thus it also supplies no independent forced prime divisor.

6. Every live pair satisfies
   \[
   p q_1q_2\mid b_{7p-1-z}.
   \]

7. For each fixed sign/configuration type \(\tau\) and \((h,k)\), the prescribed carrier (7.3) satisfies
   \[
   \prod_{e\in\mathcal M_{\tau;h,k}^{\mathrm{live}}}
   p_eq_{e,1}q_{e,2}\mid\mathfrak G_{\tau;h,k},
   \]
   and
   \[
   \log\mathfrak G_{\tau;h,k}
   \ll_J hk\log(h+k).
   \]
   Hence
   \[
   \#\mathcal M_{\tau;h,k}^{\mathrm{live}}
   \ll_J hk\log(h+k)/\log T.
   \]

8. Summing this bound over \(hk>\Psi\) at the current \(\Psi=o(T^2)\) does not yield \(o_J(A_R(T))\). The strongest current global statements remain (V7), with its one-log loss, and (V8), with its power loss.

Therefore the local coupled carrier theorem is unconditional, but the full live marked double-extension estimate remains open at exactly the restricted horizontal third-factorial scale (10.5).

# Bottom line

There is a real coupled theorem: the common marked root selects the lower prime through \(S_{h,k}\), and the common row \(b_{7p-1-z}\) selects the two upper primes; the factorwise gcd product (7.3) combines them with exact occurrence multiplicity and logarithmic height \(O_J(hk\log(h+k))\).

But every natural attempt to obtain a **second** same-characteristic constraint from reflection, the combined gap, subresultants, or Bezout collapses back to the same Sylvester system. The large-product restrictions point in the wrong direction for the current height estimates: they remove the cheap ranges and leave the expensive ones.

Accordingly, the live tail is not closed. The smallest exact remaining theorem is the restricted horizontal quotient-six third-factorial estimate (10.5), or an equivalently strong new cross-gap compression whose total logarithmic carrier height is

\[
o_J\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{\log T}\right).
\]

Neither follows from the present vertical second factorial resultant theorem.
