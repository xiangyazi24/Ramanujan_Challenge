# Q3218 — raw-resultant contamination and the exact saturation obstruction

## Scoped verdict

There is a clean all-
\(r\) obstruction to the **raw** and **primitive-content-normalized** unpointed resultants.
It is stronger than a Hadamard estimate: the forbidden endpoint \(X=r\) gives an
exact factor of the raw resultant and an exact divisor of the primitive resultant.
Writing

\[
R_r^{\rm raw}=\operatorname{Res}_X(G_r^-,G_r^+),\qquad
c_r^\pm=\operatorname{cont}(G_r^\pm),\qquad
P_r^\pm=G_r^\pm/c_r^\pm,
\]

and \(R_r^{\rm prim}=\operatorname{Res}_X(P_r^-,P_r^+)\), one has the following
dichotomy for every \(r\geq1\):

\[
R_r^{\rm prim}=0,
\quad\text{or}\quad
\log |R_r^{\rm prim}|
 \ge 2\log(r!)+\log\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac r2\right\rfloor!\right).
\]

Thus a nonzero primitive resultant already has height
\((5/2)r\log r+O(r)\), entirely before any admissible-root information is
used.  The zero alternative cannot be counted as a small-height success:
it means that the unpointed resultant is not a finite prime carrier at all.

This does **not**, without an additional statement about the candidate set and
nonvanishing, prove a numerical superlinear lower bound for every conceivable
fixed-
\(M\) sum.  It gives the exact criterion.  For candidate remainders
\(\mathcal R(M)\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{r\in\mathcal R(M)\\R_r^{\rm prim}\ne0}}
 \log|R_r^{\rm prim}|
 \gg
\sum_{\substack{r\in\mathcal R(M)\\R_r^{\rm prim}\ne0}}
 r\log(r+1).
\]

Consequently the raw envelope is superlinear whenever the right-hand weighted
sum is \(\omega(M)\).  In particular, if all \(1\le r\le M\) occur and the
resultants are nonzero, then

\[
\sum_{r\le M}\log|R_r^{\rm prim}|
 \ge \frac54M^2\log M-O(M^2),
\]

and the corresponding raw lower bound has leading coefficient \(7/4\).
Even one nonzero candidate with \(r\asymp M\) costs \(\gg M\log M\).

After genuine boundary saturation, however, the endpoint factor disappears.
There is a legitimate single **geometric support integer**, constructed as a
Fitting/elimination ideal, provided that no characteristic-zero common
component remains outside the boundary.  It detects common roots over
\(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\), not necessarily roots in \(\mathbf F_p\).  For the
actual candidate-remainder condition \(x\in\mathbf F_p\), no ordinary
prime-independent resultant or saturated elimination ideal is exact; one must
perform a residue-degree/Frobenius test prime by prime.  The distinction is
proved below, rather than asserted.

No all-
\(r\) recurrence, coprimality theorem, or smoothness theorem giving \(o(M)\)
residual mass follows from the displayed sums.  There is one useful exact
one-step reduction, but it does not close in \(r\).  The missing lemma is a
localized Bézout/Fitting-radical bound, stated precisely in Section 5.

## 1. Degree, leading coefficients, and the endpoint value

Put

\[
q=\left\lfloor\frac r2\right\rfloor,
\qquad
n_r=\left\lfloor\frac{5r}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Since

\[
\deg Z_r=\left\lceil\frac r2\right\rceil,
\]

it is enough to determine the degree-
\(3r\) terms of \(A_r^\pm\).

For \(A_r^-\), the summand with index \(j\) has degree \(r+2j\).  Hence the
unique top-degree term is \(j=r\), and

\[
\operatorname{lc}(A_r^-)
 =\frac1{r!}\left(\frac{2^r}{r!}\right)^2
 =\frac{4^r}{(r!)^3}.
\]

For \(A_r^+\), every summand has degree \(3r\), and its leading coefficient is

\[
\frac1{j!(r-j)!(r!)^2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{lc}(A_r^+)
 =\frac1{(r!)^2}\sum_{j=0}^r\frac1{j!(r-j)!}
 =\frac{2^r}{(r!)^3}.
\]

Because \(Z_r\) is monic,

\[
\boxed{
\deg G_r^-=\deg G_r^+=n_r,
\quad
\operatorname{lc}(G_r^-)=4^r r!,
\quad
\operatorname{lc}(G_r^+)=2^r r!.
}
\]

At \(X=r\), the minus sum is exactly

\[
A_r^-(r)
 =\sum_{j=0}^r\binom rj^2\binom{r+j}{j}^2=b_r.
\]

For the plus sum, replacing \(j\) by \(r-j\) gives the same expression, so
\(A_r^+(r)=b_r\).  Also

\[
Z_r(r)=\frac{r!}{q!}.
\]

Thus the two integral polynomials have the common endpoint value

\[
\boxed{
E_r:=G_r^-(r)=G_r^+(r)=(r!)^3q!\,b_r.
}
\]

This is a forbidden boundary value, not an admissible common root in
characteristic zero: \(E_r>0\).

The \(j=r\) term in \(b_r\) gives

\[
b_r\ge \binom{2r}{r}^2\ge4^r,
\]

where the last inequality follows from
\(\binom{2r}{r}=\prod_{k=1}^r(r+k)/k\ge2^r\).

## 2. Exact raw and primitive endpoint factors

### 2.1 Raw resultant

The equality at \(X=r\) gives an integral quotient

\[
G_r^-(X)-G_r^+(X)=(X-r)Q_r(X),\qquad Q_r\in\mathbf Z[X].
\]

The leading coefficients above show
\(\deg Q_r=n_r-1\).  Resultants are unchanged by subtracting the second
polynomial from the first, and are multiplicative in a product.  Hence, up to
the harmless convention-dependent sign,

\[
\boxed{
R_r^{\rm raw}
 =E_r\,\operatorname{Res}_X(Q_r,G_r^+).
}
\]

So the endpoint is an exact factor, not merely a divisor obtained from a
loose evaluation estimate.  If \(R_r^{\rm raw}\ne0\),

\[
\log|R_r^{\rm raw}|
 \ge 3\log(r!)+\log(q!)+\log b_r
 \ge 3\log(r!)+\log(q!)+r\log4.
\]

This is

\[
\frac72r\log r+O(r)
\]

per nonzero remainder.

### 2.2 Passage to primitive cores

Let \(c_r^\pm>0\) be the contents.  Since content divides every coefficient,
in particular it divides the leading coefficient:

\[
c_r^-\mid4^r r!,\qquad c_r^+\mid2^r r!.
\]

It also divides the endpoint value \(E_r\).  Therefore

\[
P_r^-(r)=\frac{E_r}{c_r^-},
\qquad
P_r^+(r)=\frac{E_r}{c_r^+},
\]

and

\[
\gcd\bigl(P_r^-(r),P_r^+(r)\bigr)
 =\frac{E_r}{\operatorname{lcm}(c_r^-,c_r^+)}.
\]

For any two integer polynomials \(F,H\), evaluation of an integral Bézout
identity for the resultant gives

\[
\gcd(F(a),H(a))\mid\operatorname{Res}(F,H).
\]

Here this divisor can also be extracted by an exact unimodular change of the
pair.  If

\[
u=P_r^-(r),\quad v=P_r^+(r),\quad g=\gcd(u,v),
\]

choose an 
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z)\) matrix whose first row is
\((v/g,-u/g)\).  The first transformed polynomial vanishes at \(r\), while a
suitable second row makes the second transformed value equal to \(g\).
The first transformed leading coefficient is

\[
\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(c_r^-,c_r^+)}{c_r^-c_r^+}
 (4^r-2^r)r!\ne0,
\]

so it is genuinely \((X-r)\) times a degree-
\(n_r-1\) polynomial.  Resultant invariance under this unimodular change then
factors \(R_r^{\rm prim}\) by \(g\) exactly.

Now

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(c_r^-,c_r^+)\mid4^r r!,
\]

because \(2^r r!\mid4^r r!\).  Consequently, whenever the primitive
resultant is nonzero,

\[
\begin{aligned}
|R_r^{\rm prim}|
&\ge \frac{E_r}{\operatorname{lcm}(c_r^-,c_r^+)}\\
&\ge \frac{(r!)^3q!\,b_r}{4^r r!}\\
&=(r!)^2q!\frac{b_r}{4^r}\\
&\ge (r!)^2q!.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
R_r^{\rm prim}=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
\log|R_r^{\rm prim}|
 \ge2\log(r!)+\log(q!).
}
\]

Stirling gives

\[
2\log(r!)+\log(q!)
 =\frac52r\log r-\frac12r\log2-\frac52r+O(\log r).
\]

Content normalization therefore removes the large scalar powers

\[
R_r^{\rm raw}
 =(c_r^-)^{n_r}(c_r^+)^{n_r}R_r^{\rm prim},
\]

but it cannot remove the factorial-size forbidden endpoint divisor.

### 2.3 The zero-resultant caveat is essential

A conventional projective height may assign height zero to the integer zero.
That convention is unusable here: \(R_r=0\) means that the two polynomials
have a common factor over \(\mathbf Q\), so the resultant is not a finite
prime envelope.  In the prime-divisor sense, zero is divisible by every
prime.

Accordingly the rigorous statement is the **nonzero-or-invalid dichotomy**
above.  An unconditional claim that the numerical sum of
\(\log|R_r|\) is superlinear still requires either:

1. nonvanishing for the candidate remainders; or
2. a convention that records a zero resultant as failure rather than as
   height zero.

No nonvanishing theorem is being inferred from the computations for
\(r\le8\).

## 3. Other forbidden contributions and why numerical division is not saturation

### 3.1 Degree drop at infinity

For the raw pair the leading coefficients have the same prime support:

\[
\operatorname{rad}\bigl(\operatorname{lc}(G_r^-)
                         \operatorname{lc}(G_r^+)\bigr)
 =\operatorname{rad}(2\,r!).
\]

Thus every prime in that support makes both raw degree-
\(n_r\) homogenizations vanish at the point at infinity; if the resultant is
nonzero, that prime divides it.  This is an exact projective contamination,
not a height estimate.  Its radical height is \(\asymp r\), already enough to
produce a quadratic dense-
\(r\) sum, but the endpoint factor is much stronger.

After primitive normalization, the relevant leading coefficients are

\[
\lambda_r^-=rac{4^r r!}{c_r^-},
\qquad
\lambda_r^+=\frac{2^r r!}{c_r^+}.
\]

Hence one must define degree-drop support **after** taking primitive parts.
If every degree drop is excluded, use
\(\operatorname{rad}(\lambda_r^-\lambda_r^+)\).  If only a simultaneous
projective common root at infinity is excluded, use
\(\operatorname{rad}(\gcd(\lambda_r^-,\lambda_r^+))\).  These notions need not
agree after content removal.

### 3.2 Boundary values do not multiply automatically

For each boundary integer \(m\in\{0,\ldots,r\}\), put

\[
d_{r,m}=\gcd(P_r^-(m),P_r^+(m)).
\]

Each \(d_{r,m}\) divides a nonzero primitive resultant, and therefore

\[
\operatorname{lcm}_{0\le m\le r}d_{r,m}\mid R_r^{\rm prim}.
\]

The product of the \(d_{r,m}\) need not divide it: the same prime can occur at
several boundary points, boundary residues can collide when \(p\le r\), and
\(v_p(R_r)\) records local intersection length rather than merely a list of
values.  At a good prime where degrees do not drop, one has the precise lower
bound

\[
v_p(R_r^{\rm prim})
 \ge \deg\gcd(\overline{P_r^-},\overline{P_r^+}),
\]

but this still does not canonically split the valuation into boundary and
nonboundary parts.

Most importantly, admissible and boundary roots may coexist at the same
prime.  For example, take

\[
F=X(X-1),\qquad H=(X+3)(X+2),\qquad B=X.
\]

Modulo \(3\), both \(0\) and \(1\) are common roots.  The root \(0\) is removed
by \(B\), while \(1\) must remain.  Therefore the admissible prime set is not
obtained by taking the prime divisors of the raw resultant and subtracting all
primes seen at a boundary value.  Removing the prime \(3\) globally would
erase the admissible component as well.

This is the exact reason that dividing by endpoint values, boundary gcds, or
their radicals is not a legitimate saturation.  Saturation must be performed
on the intersection algebra before taking its support.

## 4. What a legitimate global saturated integer can and cannot detect

Let

\[
F=P_r^-,\qquad H=P_r^+,
\qquad B_r(X)=\prod_{m=0}^r(X-m).
\]

Let \(\Lambda_r\) be the chosen primitive degree-drop carrier, and work over

\[
R_r=\mathbf Z[1/\Lambda_r].
\]

The correct globally saturated intersection algebra is

\[
\mathcal A_r
 =R_r[X,T]/(F,H,T B_r-1).
\]

The equation \(TB_r=1\) removes every boundary point simultaneously.  It is
equivalent to localizing the common-zero algebra at \(B_r\).

### 4.1 Geometric roots: a canonical support integer exists

Assume the residual characteristic-zero coprimality condition

\[
\boxed{
\gcd_{\mathbf Q[X,B_r^{-1}]}(F,H)=1.
}
\tag{RC_r}
\]

Equivalently, the generic fiber of \(\mathcal A_r\) is zero.  Because a leading
coefficient is a unit in \(R_r\), \(R_r[X]/(F,H)\) is finite over \(R_r\).
Condition \((RC_r)\) supplies a nonzero \(d\in R_r\) annihilating the localized
algebra, so \(\mathcal A_r\) is a finite torsion \(R_r\)-module.

Its zeroth Fitting ideal is therefore principal.  Let
\(S_r^{\rm geom}\) be the unique positive squarefree integer, coprime to
\(\Lambda_r\), representing the radical of that Fitting ideal.  Then for every
prime \(p\nmid\Lambda_r\),

\[
\boxed{
p\mid S_r^{\rm geom}
\iff
\exists\alpha\in\overline{\mathbf F}_p:
F(\alpha)=H(\alpha)=0,
\quad B_r(\alpha)\ne0.
}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\mathcal A_r\otimes\overline{\mathbf F}_p
 \simeq
\overline{\mathbf F}_p[X,T]/(\bar F,\bar H,T\bar B_r-1),
\]

and this fiber is nonzero exactly when such a geometric point exists.  Thus

\[
\log S_r^{\rm geom}
 =\sum_{\substack{p\nmid\Lambda_r\\
      \text{geometric common root outside }0,\ldots,r}}
   \log p.
\]

This is a legitimate single **support eliminant**.  It is canonical up to the
units introduced by inverting \(\Lambda_r\), and taking the positive
squarefree representative removes that ambiguity.  It is not, in general, a
quotient of the raw Sylvester resultant by a product of boundary values.

The same object may be described by the radical of

\[
(F,H,T B_r-1)\cap R_r,
\]

or by the radical of the annihilator of \(\mathcal A_r\); the Fitting
formulation makes finiteness and support transparent.

### 4.2 Exact obstruction when residual coprimality fails

If \((RC_r)\) fails, choose a nonconstant common factor
\(D\in\mathbf Z[X]\) whose roots are not all boundary roots.  After removing
finitely many primes dividing leading coefficients, contents, or
\(\operatorname{Res}(D,B_r)\), the reduction of \(D\) is nonconstant and
coprime to \(B_r\).  It therefore has a root over
\(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\) outside the boundary for every remaining prime.

Hence the geometric admissible support is cofinite.  No nonzero integer has
that set of prime divisors; the elimination/Fitting ideal is zero.  This is
the precise algebraic obstruction behind a zero residual resultant.

Notice that a raw zero resultant could conceivably come only from a
characteristic-zero boundary factor and become harmless after saturation.
The condition needed for a finite saturated integer is \((RC_r)\), not merely
raw nonvanishing.

### 4.3 Rational roots: ordinary elimination is not exact

The fixed-
\(M\) application supplies an actual residue \(x=M\bmod p\), hence a root in
\(\mathbf F_p\), not merely in its algebraic closure.  The Fitting integer
above detects a common irreducible factor of any positive degree.

This distinction is real even for a nonzero, monic, degree-stable resultant.
Take

\[
F=X^2+1,\qquad H=X^2+4.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{Res}(F,H)=9.
\]

Modulo \(3\), the two polynomials coincide with \(X^2+1\), which is irreducible
over \(\mathbf F_3\).  Thus \(3\) is present in the geometric saturated
support, but there is no common root in \(\mathbf F_3\).

The rational-root condition is

\[
\gcd(\bar F,\bar H,X^p-X)\ne1,
\]

and the polynomial \(X^p-X\) depends on \(p\).  Therefore no ordinary
prime-independent resultant, annihilator, contraction, or Fitting support of
the saturated ideal can distinguish degree-one common factors from
higher-degree common factors.  Exact rational-root support requires modular
factorization or an equivalent Frobenius test prime by prime.

A squarefree integer

\[
S_r^{\rm lin}
 =\prod_{\substack{p\nmid\Lambda_r\\
     \exists x\in\mathbf F_p:\,F(x)=H(x)=0,\ B_r(x)\ne0}}p
\]

exists set-theoretically whenever the residual resultant support is finite,
but defining it by this product is merely recording the outcome of the
primewise test.  It is not an independent saturated-resultant construction
from which a height estimate follows.

For a *specified* \(M\), there is of course an exact pointed integer: take the
radical of \(\gcd(F(M),H(M))\) after deleting primes dividing
\(\Lambda_r B_r(M)\).  That object is \(M\)-dependent and is precisely what the
unpointed resultant was intended to avoid.

The three notions should therefore not be conflated:

| criterion | exact object | limitation |
|---|---|---|
| fixed point \(x=M\bmod p\) | saturated pointed gcd | depends on \(M\) |
| some root over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\) | \(S_r^{\rm geom}\) from the saturated Fitting ideal | may count residue degree \(>1\) |
| some root over \(\mathbf F_p\) | \(S_r^{\rm lin}\) | requires a primewise Frobenius/factorization test |

## 5. The best elementary all-
\(r\) reduction, and where it stops

There are two exact cancellations available for every \(r\):

\[
Q_r(X)=\frac{G_r^-(X)-G_r^+(X)}{X-r}\in\mathbf Z[X],
\]

and

\[
K_r(X)=G_r^-(X)-2^rG_r^+(X)\in\mathbf Z[X].
\]

The first uses the endpoint equality; the second uses the ratio of leading
coefficients.  Both have degree at most \(n_r-1\), and \(Q_r\) has degree
exactly \(n_r-1\).  They satisfy

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
(X-r)Q_r\\ K_r
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1\\ 1&-2^r
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
G_r^-\\G_r^+
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det=-(2^r-1).
\]

Consequently, over any field of characteristic not dividing \(2^r-1\), and
at any point \(x\ne r\),

\[
G_r^-(x)=G_r^+(x)=0
\iff
Q_r(x)=K_r(x)=0.
\]

After also excluding contents and primitive degree-drop primes, this is a
valid one-degree descent of the residual common-root problem.  It is the
strongest simple all-
\(r\) identity I can derive directly from the definitions.

It does **not** close the argument:

* the exceptional determinant introduces the uncontrolled support
  \(p\mid2^r-1\);
* neither \(Q_r\) nor \(K_r\) has been identified with a unit multiple or a
  controlled low-degree combination of \(G_{r-1}^-,G_{r-1}^+\);
* the degree comparison merely leaves room for such a relation:
  \(n_r-1=n_{r-1}+2\) for even \(r\), and
  \(n_r-1=n_{r-1}+1\) for odd \(r\);
* a holonomic recurrence for either hypergeometric sum would not by itself
  control the common-zero ideal or the radical of its resultant.

The Apéry recurrence for the endpoint numbers \(b_r\) likewise controls only
the boundary specialization that saturation removes.  It supplies no bridge
to common roots of the two polynomials away from \(0,\ldots,r\).

The exact first missing theorem is residual characteristic-zero coprimality
\((RC_r)\).  Even that only makes the saturated support finite.  The mass
statement actually needed for a fixed-
\(M\) envelope is the following stronger radical theorem.

```text
SAT-RAD(M):
  For the fixed-M candidate set R(M), let S_r^lin be the squarefree product
  of good primes for which P_r^- and P_r^+ have a common F_p-root outside
  {0,...,r}.  Prove

      sum_{r in R(M)} log S_r^lin = o(M).

  A sufficient stronger version replaces S_r^lin by the canonical geometric
  support integer S_r^geom from the saturated Fitting ideal.
```

An algebraically checkable route to `SAT-RAD` would be a localized Bézout
certificate

\[
U_rF+V_rH+W_r(TB_r-1)=D_r,
\qquad D_r\in\mathbf Z\setminus\{0\},
\]

with the radical of \(D_r\) outside \(\Lambda_r\) satisfying the required
aggregate \(o(M)\) bound.  The minimal possible prime support of such a
certificate is exactly the support of \(\mathcal A_r\).  Alternatively, a
unimodular transfer in \(r\) whose determinant is supported only on excluded
primes would give the needed coprimality/smoothness control.  Neither identity
has been proved here or in the checked formulas.

Coprimality over \(\mathbf Q\) alone is insufficient: it says only that there
are finitely many modular common-root primes for each \(r\), not that their
radical has small height.  Smoothness alone is also insufficient unless its
prime range is below the admissible range or its aggregate radical mass is
quantified.  The observations for \(r\le8\) therefore remain fixtures, not an
all-
\(r\) theorem.

## Final scoped conclusion

1. **Raw/primitive height obstruction.**  The endpoint \(X=r\) gives an exact
   raw resultant factor \(E_r=(r!)^3\lfloor r/2\rfloor!b_r\).  After primitive
   content removal, the surviving endpoint divisor is
   \(E_r/\operatorname{lcm}(c_r^-,c_r^+)\), at least
   \((r!)^2\lfloor r/2\rfloor!\).  Thus every nonzero primitive resultant has
   height \(\Omega(r\log r)\).  A zero resultant is not a small carrier but a
   separate common-factor failure.  This rigorously obstructs the naive raw
   height envelope whenever its candidate set has weighted size
   \(\sum r\log r=\omega(M)\), but does not manufacture a numerical sum bound
   without nonvanishing/candidate information.

2. **Saturated integer.**  Boundary and degree-drop removal cannot be done by
   deleting prime divisors of endpoint values from the raw resultant, because
   the same prime may support both forbidden and admissible roots.  After
   localizing degree-drop primes and saturating by
   \(B_r=\prod_{m=0}^r(X-m)\), a canonical squarefree Fitting integer exists
   exactly when the residual generic fiber is empty.  It retains precisely
   the **geometric** admissible primes.  Exact \(\mathbf F_p\)-root support is
   not a usual saturated resultant; it requires a primewise Frobenius test.

3. **No proved \(o(M)\) recurrence.**  The exact pair
   \((Q_r,K_r)\) lowers the degree by one outside primes dividing
   \(2^r-1\), but no all-
   \(r\) transfer to the \(r-1\) pair, localized Bézout identity, or aggregate
   radical bound has been established.  The precise missing lemma is
   `SAT-RAD(M)` (with residual coprimality as its preliminary nonzero
   condition), not a finite-
   \(r\) pattern extrapolation.
