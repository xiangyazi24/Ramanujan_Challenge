# Problem 3.2: the removed-target slope after the full Hensel lift

## Verdict

There is a real extra calculation here, but it does **not** give a second free contradiction from the degree drop.

Let

\[
V=U+GC,\qquad AU-1=GE,\qquad M=GT,
\]

with the canonical Hensel correction

\[
C\equiv-UE\pmod T,
\qquad \deg C<\deg T.
\]

Then the sign is

\[
\boxed{E+AC=TS},
\]

not \(E-AC=TS\), and therefore

\[
\boxed{S(y)=\frac{E(y)+A(y)C(y)}{T(y)}}. \tag{1}
\]

At the removed target \(y=\lambda_r\), where \(P_m=(Y-y)A\), this is also the exact normalized terminal slope

\[
\boxed{
S(y)=\frac{(AV-1)'(y)}{M'(y)}
     =\frac{(AV-1)'(y)}{G'(y)T(y)}.
} \tag{2}
\]

Since \(G'(\lambda_r)=-1/2\) for \(r<m\),

\[
\boxed{S(y)=-\frac{2}{T(y)}(AV-1)'(y).} \tag{3}
\]

Thus \(S(y)\) is not "free": it is the first nonzero Hensel slope of the full inverse at the simple factor \(Y-y\) of \(M\).

If trace failure is the vanishing of the leading coefficient of the canonical residue \(C\), then the quotient really drops one degree. In the usual nondegenerate shell \(\deg P_m=m\), writing

\[
d:=\deg T=m-r-1,
\]

we have generically \(\deg S\le m-2\), while trace failure gives

\[
\boxed{\deg S\le m-3.} \tag{4}
\]

But this kills **exactly one coefficient at infinity**. It does not force \(C(y)=0\), and hence does not force \(S(y)=0\). The precise new relation is a barycentric/Hensel moment relation, given below.

The useful terminal theorem is stronger and more specific:

* trace failure kills the \(t_j\)-weighted first Hensel-jet moment;
* \(C(y)\) is minus \(T(y)\) times the corresponding zeroth Hensel-jet moment;
* if the jet error satisfies the forced Apéry linearized recurrence, Green summation converts trace failure into an **exact formula for the terminal slope** \(\eta_{m-1}\), and then gives \(C(y)\) explicitly after eliminating that slope.

In the homogeneous case this would contradict any nonzero jet. In the actual Problem 3.2 situation the differentiated/Riccati equation has interior forcing, so the forcing term survives. Therefore the ordinary Apéry WZ recurrence by itself does **not** contradict trace failure. What remains is one concrete scalar forcing evaluation, not an unspecified freedom in \(S(y)\).

# 1. Exact full-lift identity and all signs

Start from

\[
AU-1=GE,
\qquad
V=U+GC.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
AV-1
&=A(U+GC)-1\\
&=(AU-1)+AGC\\
&=GE+GAC\\
&=G(E+AC).
\end{aligned}
\]

The congruence defining \(C\) has the sign

\[
C\equiv-UE\pmod T.
\]

At a root of \(T\), \(AU\equiv1\), so

\[
AC\equiv-AUE\equiv-E\pmod T.
\]

Hence \(E+AC\equiv0\pmod T\). There is a unique polynomial \(S\) such that

\[
\boxed{E+AC=TS.} \tag{5}
\]

Multiplying by \(G\) gives

\[
\boxed{AV-1=GTS=MS.} \tag{6}
\]

This checks both signs simultaneously. In particular, a formula with \(E-AC\) would be inconsistent with the defining congruence \(C\equiv-UE\pmod T\).

At \(Y=y\), \(T(y)\ne0\) because the tail starts at \(r+1\). Thus (5) gives immediately

\[
\boxed{S(y)=T(y)^{-1}\bigl(E(y)+A(y)C(y)\bigr).} \tag{7}
\]

If

\[
E(Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-2}e_iY^i,
\qquad
C(Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}c_iY^i,
\]

then, literally,

\[
\boxed{
S(y)=\frac{
\displaystyle \sum_i e_i y^i
+A(y)\displaystyle\sum_i c_i y^i
}{T(y)}.
} \tag{8}
\]

Under trace failure \(c_{d-1}=0\), the second sum terminates at \(d-2\).

# 2. The removed target is a genuine slope, not a value constraint

Because \(y=\lambda_r\) is a simple root of \(G\) and is not a root of \(T\), \(y\) is a simple root of \(M=GT\). Equation (6) therefore gives

\[
(AV-1)'(y)=M'(y)S(y)=G'(y)T(y)S(y).
\]

This proves (2). Expanding \(V'=U'+G'C+GC'\) at \(y\), where \(G(y)=0\), gives

\[
(AV-1)'(y)
=A'(y)U(y)+A(y)U'(y)+A(y)G'(y)C(y).
\]

On the other hand, differentiating \(AU-1=GE\) at \(y\) gives

\[
A'(y)U(y)+A(y)U'(y)=G'(y)E(y).
\]

Therefore

\[
(AV-1)'(y)=G'(y)\bigl(E(y)+A(y)C(y)\bigr),
\]

again reproducing (7). This is a useful sign audit: the correction contributes **plus** \(A(y)C(y)\) to the normalized target slope.

# 3. Degree bookkeeping: exactly one coefficient dies

Let

\[
d=\deg T=m-r-1.
\]

For the canonical complete-grid interpolation one has

\[
\deg P_m\le m,
\qquad
A=P_m/(Y-y),
\qquad
\deg A\le m-1,
\]

and the canonical inverse modulo the degree-\(m+1\) polynomial \(G\) may be taken with \(\deg U\le m\). Hence

\[
\deg E\le m-2
\]

from \(AU-1=GE\). Also the canonical residue representative satisfies

\[
\deg C<d.
\]

In the nondegenerate case \(\deg A=m-1\), write

\[
A(Y)=a_{m-1}Y^{m-1}+a_{m-2}Y^{m-2}+\cdots,
\]

\[
C(Y)=c_{d-1}Y^{d-1}+c_{d-2}Y^{d-2}+\cdots,
\]

and, since \(T\) is monic,

\[
S(Y)=s_{m-2}Y^{m-2}+s_{m-3}Y^{m-3}+\cdots.
\]

Comparing the top coefficient in

\[
E+AC=TS
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{s_{m-2}=a_{m-1}c_{d-1}.} \tag{9}
\]

The term \(E\) is too small to enter that coefficient. Thus, provided \(a_{m-1}\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{c_{d-1}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_{m-2}=0.} \tag{10}
\]

So trace failure really is a one-degree drop of the full quotient:

\[
\boxed{\deg S\le m-3.} \tag{11}
\]

For \(d\ge2\), the next coefficient is

\[
\boxed{s_{m-3}=a_{m-1}c_{d-2}}
\qquad\text{when }c_{d-1}=0, \tag{12}
\]

apart from the obvious very-short-tail edge where the lower-degree \(E\) term reaches the same coefficient. The important point is structural: there is one lost coefficient, not a second hidden evaluation equation at \(y\).

If \(P_m\) already has lower degree, all of these bounds only improve; the conclusion that the vanishing top coefficient of \(C\) kills one top coefficient of \(S\) remains valid with the effective degree of \(A\).

# 4. Radical form: \(P_m-q=GqE\)

Introduce the rational radical reconstruction

\[
q(Y):=\frac{Y-y}{U(Y)}.
\]

Since

\[
A=\frac{P_m}{Y-y},
\qquad
U=\frac{Y-y}{q},
\]

we have

\[
AU=\frac{P_m}{q}.
\]

Thus \(AU-1=GE\) is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{P_m-q=GqE.} \tag{13}
\]

The sign in (13) is important.

At a complete-grid node \(\lambda_j\), both \(P_m\) and \(q\) take the Apéry value \(b_j\). Put

\[
t_j:=\lambda_j-y,
\qquad
\delta_j:=q'(\lambda_j)-P_m'(\lambda_j),
\]

where the derivative here is with respect to the spectral variable \(Y\). Differentiating (13) at a grid node gives

\[
P_m'(\lambda_j)-q'(\lambda_j)
=G'(\lambda_j)b_jE(\lambda_j).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_j: = E(\lambda_j)
=-\frac{\delta_j}{G'_j b_j}.
} \tag{14}
\]

On the tail, \(U_j=t_j/b_j\). Since \(C\equiv-UE\pmod T\),

\[
C(\lambda_j)=-U_jE_j
=\frac{t_j\delta_j}{b_j^2G'_j}.
\]

Hence the exact tail Hensel values are

\[
\boxed{
C_j:=C(\lambda_j)
=\frac{t_j\delta_j}{b_j^2G'_j}.
} \tag{15}
\]

Again the sign is **plus** in (15): the minus in \(C=-UE\) cancels the minus in (14).

# 5. Trace failure is a first moment; \(C(y)\) is the zeroth moment

Let

\[
T(Y)=\prod_{j=r+1}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_j),
\qquad d=m-r-1.
\]

For every polynomial \(C\) of degree \(<d\), Lagrange interpolation on the tail gives

\[
C(Y)=\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
C_j\frac{T(Y)}{(Y-\lambda_j)T'_j}.
\]

Since each Lagrange basis polynomial is monic up to the factor \(1/T'_j\), the leading coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
[c_{d-1}]C
=\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}\frac{C_j}{T'_j}.
} \tag{16}
\]

Substituting (15),

\[
\boxed{
c_{d-1}
=\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{t_j\delta_j}{b_j^2G'_jT'_j}.
} \tag{17}
\]

Thus the assumed trace failure is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{t_j\delta_j}{b_j^2G'_jT'_j}=0.
} \tag{18}
\]

Now evaluate the same interpolation formula at the removed target \(y\). Because

\[
y-\lambda_j=-t_j,
\]

(15) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
C(y)
&=T(y)\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{C_j}{(y-\lambda_j)T'_j}\\
&=-T(y)\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{\delta_j}{b_j^2G'_jT'_j}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{C(y)}{T(y)}
=-\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{\delta_j}{b_j^2G'_jT'_j}.
} \tag{19}
\]

This is the exact relation on \(C(y)\) that the radical Hensel data supplies.

The distinction is now transparent:

\[
\text{trace failure:}
\quad
\sum t_j w_j\delta_j=0,
\]

whereas

\[
\text{target value:}
\quad
\frac{C(y)}{T(y)}=-\sum w_j\delta_j,
\]

with

\[
w_j:=\frac1{b_j^2G'_jT'_j}.
\]

So the trace condition kills the **first \(t\)-moment** of the jet defect; \(C(y)\) is the **zeroth moment**. There is no algebraic reason for the zeroth moment to vanish when the first moment vanishes.

This is the precise replacement for the vague statement that "\(S(y)\) is free."

# 6. Completely explicit factorial form

For \(r+1\le j\le m-1\), the already-derived finite-field formulas are

\[
G'(\lambda_j)=-\frac12
\]

and

\[
T'(\lambda_j)
=-2\frac{(j-r-1)!}{(j+r+1)!(2j+1)^2}.
\]

Therefore the two negatives cancel:

\[
\boxed{
G'_jT'_j
=\frac{(j-r-1)!}{(j+r+1)!(2j+1)^2}.
} \tag{20}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{G'_jT'_j}
=\frac{(j+r+1)!(2j+1)^2}{(j-r-1)!}.
} \tag{21}
\]

The trace condition becomes

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{t_j\delta_j}{b_j^2}
\frac{(j+r+1)!(2j+1)^2}{(j-r-1)!}
=0.
} \tag{22}
\]

The removed-target value is

\[
\boxed{
C(y)
=-T(y)
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{\delta_j}{b_j^2}
\frac{(j+r+1)!(2j+1)^2}{(j-r-1)!}.
} \tag{23}
\]

Also

\[
t_j=\lambda_j-\lambda_r=(j-r)(j+r+1),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
T(y)=(-1)^d d!\frac{(m+r)!}{(2r+1)!},
\qquad d=m-r-1.
} \tag{24}
\]

Every factorial in (21)--(24) is a unit in the nondegenerate tail range.

# 7. What the degree drop really forces at the terminal tail node

There is one concrete value relation coming from \(\deg C\le d-2\). Equation (16) with \(c_{d-1}=0\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}\frac{C_j}{T'_j}=0.
} \tag{25}
\]

Thus the last tail value is redundant:

\[
\boxed{
C_{m-1}
=-T'_{m-1}
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-2}\frac{C_j}{T'_j}.
} \tag{26}
\]

In Hensel variables,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U_{m-1}E_{m-1}}{T'_{m-1}}
=-\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-2}
\frac{U_jE_j}{T'_j}.
} \tag{27}
\]

There is also a removed-target interpolation relation. Put

\[
T_-(Y):=\prod_{j=r+1}^{m-2}(Y-\lambda_j).
\]

Since \(\deg C\le d-2=\deg T_- -1\), the first \(d-1\) tail values already determine \(C\), and

\[
\boxed{
C(y)=T_-(y)
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-2}
\frac{C_j}{(y-\lambda_j)T_-'(\lambda_j)}.
} \tag{28}
\]

Equations (26) and (28) are the exact finite-dimensional consequences of the degree drop.

They do **not** say \(C(y)=0\). For \(d\ge2\), the two linear functionals on tail data

\[
(C_j)_j\mapsto \sum_j\frac{C_j}{T'_j}
\]

and

\[
(C_j)_j\mapsto \sum_j\frac{C_j}{(y-\lambda_j)T'_j}
\]

are not proportional, because \((y-\lambda_j)^{-1}\) is not constant as \(j\) varies. Hence the hyperplane defined by trace failure is not contained in the hyperplane \(C(y)=0\).

This proves rigorously that the one-degree drop cannot, by polynomial algebra alone, force a target zero.

### Short-tail edge case

If \(d=1\), then \(C\) is constant. Its leading coefficient is the constant itself, so trace failure forces \(C=0\), hence \(C(y)=0\). This exceptional one-node tail does not generalize to the actual long-tail obstruction.

# 8. Second jet at the removed target

The radical identity (13) contains more local information at \(y\).

At \(Y=y\), both \(G(y)=0\) and \(q(y)=0\). Therefore the first derivative of the right side of

\[
P_m-q=GqE
\]

vanishes, and

\[
\boxed{P_m'(y)=q'(y).} \tag{29}
\]

This is also immediate from \(A(y)U(y)=1\): since \(q=(Y-y)/U\),

\[
q'(y)=\frac1{U(y)}=A(y)=P_m'(y).
\]

Differentiating (13) twice gives

\[
P_m''(y)-q''(y)
=2G'(y)q'(y)E(y).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
E(y)=\frac{P_m''(y)-q''(y)}{2G'(y)P_m'(y)}.
} \tag{30}
\]

Using \(G'(y)=-1/2\),

\[
\boxed{
E(y)=\frac{q''(y)-P_m''(y)}{P_m'(y)}.
} \tag{31}
\]

Substituting (19) into (7), and using \(A(y)=P_m'(y)\), gives the fully explicit target slope

\[
\boxed{
S(y)
=\frac{q''(y)-P_m''(y)}{P_m'(y)T(y)}
-P_m'(y)
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{\delta_j}{b_j^2G'_jT'_j}.
} \tag{32}
\]

Equation (32) is an exact terminal Hensel-slope theorem before using trace failure. It includes both the local second-jet defect at \(y\) and the global zeroth tail moment.

# 9. Why the ordinary Apéry WZ recurrence does not yet contradict (25)

The repository's WZ theorem is the usual Apéry recurrence

\[
\boxed{
(j+1)^3b_{j+1}
-(2j+1)(17j(j+1)+5)b_j
+j^3b_{j-1}=0.
} \tag{33}
\]

The formalized Wronskian has the compatible sign

\[
a_jb_{j-1}-a_{j-1}b_j=\frac6{j^3}.
\]

At the half-shell endpoint \(p=2m+1\), the middle coefficient in (33) vanishes at \(j=m\). Since

\[
(m+1)^3=-m^3\pmod p,
\]

(33) gives

\[
\boxed{b_{m+1}=b_{m-1}.} \tag{34}
\]

That is the exact WZ terminal **value** relation.

But (18), (19), and (25) involve the **first spectral jets** \(\delta_j=q'(\lambda_j)-P_m'(\lambda_j)\), equivalently the Hensel values \(E_j\). Equation (33) is a recurrence for the values \(b_j\). By itself it gives no linear relation between the zeroth and first Hensel-jet moments

\[
\sum_jw_j\delta_j,
\qquad
\sum_jt_jw_j\delta_j.
\]

Therefore the implication

\[
c_{d-1}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(y)=0
\]

cannot be extracted from the ordinary WZ value recurrence.

To get a genuine slope contradiction one must use the **differentiated/linearized** recurrence for the jet defect, including its forcing. This is exactly where the earlier Riccati/second-jet calculation enters.

# 10. Exact Green terminal-slope theorem

Here is the clean terminal theorem that packages the remaining obstruction.

Let

\[
(Lx)_j=(j+1)^3x_{j+1}
-(2j+1)(17j(j+1)+5)x_j
+j^3x_{j-1}.
\]

Let

\[
\chi_j:=a_j/6.
\]

The project Wronskian identity gives

\[
\boxed{
b_{j-1}\chi_j-\chi_{j-1}b_j=\frac1{j^3}.} \tag{35}
\]

Define the two weights

\[
w_j^{(0)}:=\frac1{b_j^2G'_jT'_j},
\qquad
w_j^{(1)}:=t_jw_j^{(0)}.
\]

For \(s=0,1\), define the left-anchored Green test sequence

\[
\rho_r^{(s)}=\rho_{r+1}^{(s)}=0,
\]

and, for \(j\ge r+2\),

\[
\boxed{
\rho_j^{(s)}
=\sum_{k=r+1}^{j-1}
\bigl(\chi_jb_k-b_j\chi_k\bigr)w_k^{(s)}.
} \tag{36}
\]

The sign in (35) implies

\[
\boxed{L\rho^{(s)}=w^{(s)}} \tag{37}
\]

on the open tail. At the first source point,

\[
\rho_{r+2}^{(s)}
=\frac{w_{r+1}^{(s)}}{(r+2)^3},
\]

so \((L\rho^{(s)})_{r+1}=w_{r+1}^{(s)}\), confirming the sign.

Now let \(\eta_j\) be the correctly normalized jet defect and suppose the differentiated WZ/Riccati equation has the forced form

\[
\boxed{L\eta=f} \tag{38}
\]

on \(r+1,\ldots,m-1\), with the shell endpoint conditions

\[
\eta_r=\eta_m=0.
\]

Use the oriented Casoratian

\[
\mathcal K_j(\rho,\eta)
=j^3(\rho_{j-1}\eta_j-\rho_j\eta_{j-1}).
\]

Then

\[
\rho_j(L\eta)_j-\eta_j(L\rho)_j
=\mathcal K_{j+1}-\mathcal K_j.
\]

Summation from \(r+1\) to \(m-1\), with the left anchor and \(\eta_m=0\), yields

\[
\boxed{
Z_s:=\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}w_j^{(s)}\eta_j
=\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}\rho_j^{(s)}f_j
+m^3\rho_m^{(s)}\eta_{m-1}.
} \tag{39}
\]

The **plus** sign on the terminal term is important. Indeed

\[
\mathcal K_m
=m^3(\rho_{m-1}\eta_m-\rho_m\eta_{m-1})
=-m^3\rho_m\eta_{m-1},
\]

and the summation formula contains \(-\mathcal K_m\).

If the jet normalization is exactly \(\eta_j=\delta_j\), then

\[
Z_1=c_{d-1},
\qquad
Z_0=-\frac{C(y)}{T(y)}.
\]

If the earlier \(\eta\) is an index derivative rather than a \(Y\)-derivative, one must first insert the chain-rule factor; on the open tail \(2j+1\ne0\), but one must **not** divide by \(2m+1\) at the ramified endpoint.

# 11. Trace failure gives an exact terminal slope

Assume now trace failure:

\[
Z_1=0.
\]

If

\[
\rho_m^{(1)}\ne0,
\]

then (39) gives the exact terminal slope

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{m-1}
=-\frac{
\displaystyle\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}\rho_j^{(1)}f_j
}{m^3\rho_m^{(1)}}.
} \tag{40}
\]

This is the desired terminal-slope theorem. It is not a statement that the endpoint is free: trace failure fixes the terminal jet completely from the interior forcing.

Now apply (39) with \(s=0\):

\[
Z_0
=\sum_j\rho_j^{(0)}f_j
+m^3\rho_m^{(0)}\eta_{m-1}.
\]

Eliminating \(\eta_{m-1}\) with (40) gives

\[
\boxed{
Z_0
=\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\left(
\rho_j^{(0)}
-\frac{\rho_m^{(0)}}{\rho_m^{(1)}}\rho_j^{(1)}
\right)f_j.
} \tag{41}
\]

Since \(C(y)/T(y)=-Z_0\),

\[
\boxed{
C(y)
=-T(y)
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\left(
\rho_j^{(0)}
-\frac{\rho_m^{(0)}}{\rho_m^{(1)}}\rho_j^{(1)}
\right)f_j.
} \tag{42}
\]

Finally, substitute into (7):

\[
\boxed{
S(y)
=\frac{E(y)}{T(y)}
-A(y)
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\left(
\rho_j^{(0)}
-\frac{\rho_m^{(0)}}{\rho_m^{(1)}}\rho_j^{(1)}
\right)f_j.
} \tag{43}
\]

Equation (43) is the exact full-inverse terminal slope under trace failure. It expresses \(S(y)\) solely in terms of the local radical datum \(E(y)\), the Apéry Green kernel, and the interior jet forcing.

Using (31), it can equally be written

\[
\boxed{
S(y)
=\frac{q''(y)-P_m''(y)}{P_m'(y)T(y)}
-P_m'(y)
\sum_j
\left(
\rho_j^{(0)}
-\frac{\rho_m^{(0)}}{\rho_m^{(1)}}\rho_j^{(1)}
\right)f_j.
} \tag{44}
\]

This is the strongest clean terminal formula available from the present algebra.

### Degenerate Green denominator

If \(\rho_m^{(1)}=0\), trace failure does not determine \(\eta_{m-1}\) by division. Instead (39) says that trace failure requires the additional scalar condition

\[
\boxed{
\sum_j\rho_j^{(1)}f_j=0.
} \tag{45}
\]

That case must be split off rather than hidden by dividing by \(\rho_m^{(1)}\).

# 12. Does this contradict the Apéry WZ recurrence?

## Homogeneous jet: yes

If the differentiated jet equation were homogeneous,

\[
f_j=0,
\]

and \(\rho_m^{(1)}\ne0\), then trace failure would give from (40)

\[
\eta_{m-1}=0.
\]

Together with \(\eta_m=0\), the second-order recurrence \(L\eta=0\) propagates zero backward across the unit range, so

\[
\eta_j=0
\]

throughout the tail. Therefore any independently known nonzero jet would contradict trace failure.

In the homogeneous situation (42) would also give

\[
C(y)=0.
\]

So the hoped-for contradiction is real **only** in the homogeneous jet model.

## Actual radical Hensel jet: not automatically

The actual Problem 3.2 differentiated/Riccati shell has a nonzero interior forcing. Then (40) becomes

\[
\eta_{m-1}
=-\frac{\sum\rho^{(1)}f}{m^3\rho_m^{(1)}},
\]

not \(0\), and (42) gives a generally nonzero \(C(y)\).

Thus the degree drop does **not** contradict the ordinary Apéry WZ recurrence by itself. The missing theorem is no longer vague: one must evaluate the WZ-derived forcing in (40), or prove that its resulting terminal slope disagrees with the independently derived second-jet endpoint slope.

A convenient exact obstruction scalar is

\[
\boxed{
\Theta
:=m^3\rho_m^{(1)}\eta_{m-1}
+\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}\rho_j^{(1)}f_j.
} \tag{46}
\]

By (39),

\[
\Theta=Z_1=c_{d-1}.
\]

Hence the desired no-trace-failure theorem is exactly

\[
\boxed{\Theta\ne0.} \tag{47}
\]
