# Q4149 — exact q6 positive-part truncation

## Verdict

There is a clean unconditional pair-amplification step, and it is stronger than the naive statement “large \(\kappa\) gives many arbitrary lower-prime pairs”: after a canonical tie-break, every unit of overflow can be charged to **ordered pairs lying in one of the two individual gcd components**. Thus no mixed `plus/reverse` pair theorem is needed.

What I do **not** find is an unconditional closure of the whole target from the currently available deletion mechanisms. The strongest safe truncation is:

\[
\sum_{q,t:q\mid b_t}(\kappa(q,t)-2R)_+
\le o_J\!\left(\frac{T^2}{(\log T)^2}\right)
 +\frac{\mathcal R_{\rm pair}(T)}{4\lfloor R\rfloor-2},
\]

where \(\mathcal R_{\rm pair}(T)\) is an **actual ordered-pair occurrence count** of overloaded same-component lower-prime pairs left outside the proved narrow-width / bounded-aspect pair-deletion region. Hence the exact residual gate is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_{\rm pair}(T)
=o_J\!\left(R\frac{T^2}{(\log T)^2}\right).}
\]

The existing reverse-shift and factorial-correlation identities do not, by themselves, prove this remaining second-factorial occurrence estimate. In particular, a height/divisibility carrier is not interchangeable with an occurrence-preserving pair count.

## 1. Split the q-free lcm into two prime sets

For an upper state \(u=(q,t)\), write

\[
G_-(u)=\bigl(\gcd(b_{6q+t}/q,b_{q-1-t}/q)\bigr)^{(q)},
\qquad
G_+(u)=\bigl(\gcd(b_{6q+t}/q,b_{12q+t}/q)\bigr)^{(q)},
\]

where the superscript means q-free part, and restrict prime divisors throughout to the **exact lower q6 candidate window**. Put

\[
P_-(u)=\{p:p\mid G_-(u)\},\qquad
P_+(u)=\{p:p\mid G_+(u)\}.
\]

Then

\[
\kappa(u)=|P_-(u)\cup P_+(u)|.
\]

Overlap between the two gcds is inconvenient only if one insists on counting both copies. Remove it canonically. For example set

\[
A_-(u)=P_-(u),\qquad
A_+(u)=P_+(u)\setminus P_-(u),
\]

and denote \(a_\pm(u)=|A_\pm(u)|\). Then

\[
a_-(u)+a_+(u)=\kappa(u),
\]

while every pair of primes in \(A_-\) is a legitimate reverse/shifted-gap pair and every pair in \(A_+\) is a legitimate plus common-target pair. The tie-break can of course be reversed; nothing below depends on that choice.

This is important: the lcm/OR structure causes **no mixed-pair obstruction** at the amplification stage.

## 2. Exact ordered-pair amplification

Let

\[
r=\lfloor R\rfloor.
\]

Since \(R\to\infty\), we may assume \(r\ge1\). For every integer \(m\ge0\),

\[
(m-r)_+\le \frac{m(m-1)}{4r-2}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For \(m\le r\) this is trivial. For \(m>r\), the claimed inequality is equivalent to

\[
m(m-1)-(4r-2)(m-r)\ge0,
\]

and the left side factors as

\[
(m-(2r-1))(m-2r).
\]

For integral \(m\), this product is nonnegative: there is no integer strictly between \(2r-1\) and \(2r\).

Now

\[
(\kappa-2R)_+
\le (\kappa-2r)_+
\le (a_--r)_+ +(a_+-r)_+.
\]

Applying (2.1) gives the pointwise bound

\[
\boxed{
(\kappa(u)-2R)_+
\le
\frac{a_-(u)(a_-(u)-1)+a_+(u)(a_+(u)-1)}{4r-2}.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

We may insert the indicators \(1_{a_\sigma>r}\) on the right, because the corresponding term is needed only on an overloaded side. Thus define

\[
E_\sigma(u)=1_{a_\sigma(u)>r}\,a_\sigma(u)(a_\sigma(u)-1),
\]

which is exactly the number of ordered distinct prime pairs in the canonically assigned \(\sigma\)-component of an overloaded upper state. Summing,

\[
\boxed{
S_R:=\sum_{q,t:q\mid b_t}(\kappa(q,t)-2R)_+
\le \frac{1}{4r-2}
\sum_{u}\bigl(E_-(u)+E_+(u)\bigr).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

So the right scale for a pair theorem is not merely \(T^2/L^2\), but

\[
A_R:=R\frac{T^2}{L^2},\qquad L=\log T.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Any collection of these actual ordered pairs whose total cardinality is \(o_J(A_R)\) contributes \(o_J(T^2/L^2)\) to the original positive-part sum.

This answers the first structural question positively: **high \(\kappa\) necessarily produces enough same-carrier ordered lower-prime pairs.** The remaining issue is whether those pairs are forced into a pair family for which an \(o(A_R)\) occurrence estimate is already proved.

## 3. Brun–Titchmarsh gives a genuine zero-contribution narrow window

Let \(I(u)\) be the exact lower-prime candidate interval for the state \(u\), and let \(W(u)=|I(u)|\) be its length. Since all prime divisors counted by \(\kappa(u)\) lie in that interval,

\[
\kappa(u)\le \#\{p\text{ prime}:p\in I(u)\}.
\]

Using the ordinary Brun–Titchmarsh interval bound

\[
\#\{p\in[x,x+W]\}\le \frac{2W}{\log W}
\]

in its admissible range, define the exact implicit threshold

\[
W_{\rm BT}(r)
:=\sup\left\{W:\frac{2W}{\log W}\le 2r\right\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then every state with \(W(u)\le W_{\rm BT}(r)\) has

\[
\kappa(u)\le2r\le2R,
\]

and therefore contributes exactly zero. Asymptotically

\[
W_{\rm BT}(r)
=r\bigl(\log r+\log\log r+O(1)\bigr).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the positive-part problem may unconditionally be restricted to the broad-window states

\[
W(u)>W_{\rm BT}(r).
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is stronger and cleaner than trying to extract a tiny average saving from Brun–Titchmarsh: below (3.1) the summand vanishes identically.

## 4. What the bounded-aspect resultant deletion really buys

The relevant established dyadic ledger distinguishes a base/root count \(B\) from the **unweighted actual occurrence count** \(C\). In a dyadic pair-width box \((X_0,Y_0)\), \(X_0\le Y_0\), the safe occurrence estimate is of the form

\[
C(X_0,Y_0)
\ll_J X_0^2Y_0^2\log(2Y_0),
\]

and after hyperbolic/aspect summation the occurrence version is

\[
C_{\rm asp}(P,K)
\ll_J P^2 L\log(2K),
\tag{4.1}
\]

for

\[
XY\le P,\qquad Y/X\le K.
\]

The corresponding base/root ledger has one fewer factor of \(L\),

\[
B_{\rm asp}(P,K)\ll_JP^2\log(2K),
\tag{4.2}
\]

but (4.2) cannot simply replace (4.1) in the present problem: \(S_R\) is an unweighted sum of actual upper-state/lower-pair occurrences. This is exactly where a hidden multiplicity factor would be fatal.

Consequently the unconditional occurrence-safe cutoff is obtained from (4.1), not (4.2). Let \(\eta(T)\to\infty\) arbitrarily slowly and put

\[
P_{\rm occ}(K)
:=\left\lfloor
\sqrt{\frac{A_R}{\eta(T)L\log(2K)}}
\right\rfloor
=\left\lfloor
\frac{T\sqrt R}{L^{3/2}\sqrt{\eta(T)\log(2K)}}
\right\rfloor.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
C_{\rm asp}(P_{\rm occ}(K),K)
\ll_J \frac{A_R}{\eta(T)}
=o_J(A_R).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence, by (2.3), all overloaded same-component ordered pairs satisfying

\[
XY\le P_{\rm occ}(K),\qquad Y/X\le K
\tag{4.5}
\]

make \(o_J(T^2/L^2)\) total contribution.

The standard convenient choice from the current ledger is

\[
K=L^{1/8},
\]

in which case

\[
P_{\rm occ}
\asymp
\frac{T\sqrt R}{L^{3/2}\sqrt{\eta(T)\log L}}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

There is a formally larger cutoff

\[
P_{\rm base}(K)
\asymp
\frac{T\sqrt R}{L\sqrt{\eta(T)\log(2K)}}
\tag{4.7}
\]

if one is allowed to use the base/root estimate (4.2). The fixed-upper occupancy lemma in the current P3.2 line does provide the saving

\[
\nu_\sigma(X)\ll \frac{X}{\log(2+X)}
\]

on the polynomial-height residual, so on a subregion where its hypotheses are already established one can recover the missing \(1/L\) and use a larger product range. But it is **not valid to replace \(\log(2+X)\) by \(L\) uniformly over all small widths**. Therefore (4.7) is not a globally unconditional replacement for (4.3) unless that exact fixed-upper composition has itself been proved on the whole residual under consideration.

For the requested cold audit, (4.3) is the safe global cutoff.

## 5. Reverse-shift and factorial-correlation routes do not delete the full complement

The reverse-shift algebra is useful because a same-minus pair gives an exact shifted-gap/common-target carrier rather than two unrelated divisibilities. Likewise factorial normalization converts certain repeated-zero configurations into divisibility by explicit continuant/determinant-type integers. These are real structural reductions.

But the target after (2.3) is an **actual second-factorial occurrence sum**. The currently established reverse-window estimates are local energy bounds of \(H^{5/2}\)-type (for example the forward and backward window square/pair bounds), and earlier aggregate audits found that their available width cutoffs do not by themselves make the full q6 pair mass \(o(A_R)\). Likewise the factorial-normalized determinant/carrier bounds control divisibility/height, but the all-plus endpoint route still saturates at the target scale without a genuine cross-characteristic occurrence correlation.

So these facts may delete additional named subfamilies of the remainder, but they do **not** justify the inference

\[
\text{“every same-component ordered pair is already summable.”}
\]

That inference is the missing step.

A related trap is to use a simultaneous `plus AND reverse` estimate. The present \(C^\circ\) is an lcm, hence an `OR`. An estimate for primes or pairs satisfying both component conditions does not control the one-sided masses. The canonical assignment in Section 1 makes this distinction explicit.

## 6. Exact remaining term

Let \(\mathscr E_\sigma\) be the set of tuples

\[
(u,p_1,p_2),\qquad u=(q,t),\quad p_1\ne p_2,
\]

such that

1. \(q\mid b_t\) and \(u\) is an actual q6 upper oriented state;
2. \(p_1,p_2\) are in the exact lower q6 candidate window;
3. \(p_1,p_2\in A_\sigma(u)\);
4. \(a_\sigma(u)>r\).

Attach to the pair the exact two positive width variables \((X,Y)\), \(X\le Y\), used by the corresponding current common-target/shifted-gap dyadic carrier ledger. Define

\[
\mathscr E_{\sigma}^{\rm good}(P,K)
=\{(u,p_1,p_2)\in\mathscr E_\sigma:
XY\le P,\;Y/X\le K\}.
\]

After the Brun–Titchmarsh zero region is removed, set

\[
\mathscr E_{\sigma}^{\rm rem}(P,K)
=\left\{
(u,p_1,p_2)\in\mathscr E_\sigma:
W(u)>W_{\rm BT}(r),\;
\bigl(XY>P\;\text{ or }\;Y/X>K\bigr)
\right\},
\]

and

\[
\mathcal R_{\rm pair}(T;P,K)
:=|\mathscr E_-^{\rm rem}(P,K)|
 +|\mathscr E_+^{\rm rem}(P,K)|.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Any separately proved fixed-upper, reverse-shift, factorial-correlation, or exceptional-root deletion can simply be subtracted from these sets; doing so only makes \(\mathcal R_{\rm pair}\) smaller. With \(P=P_{\rm occ}(K)\), (2.3) and (4.4) give the rigorous truncation

\[
\boxed{
S_R
\le
o_J\!\left(\frac{T^2}{L^2}\right)
+
\frac{\mathcal R_{\rm pair}(T;P_{\rm occ}(K),K)}{4r-2}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

For \(K=L^{1/8}\), this says the only pair mass still needing a new global estimate is the broad-window region satisfying

\[
XY>
\frac{T\sqrt R}{L^{3/2}\sqrt{\eta(T)\log L}}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac YX>L^{1/8},
\tag{6.3}
\]

up to floors, constants, and the additional already-deleted special subfamilies.

Thus the exact remaining sufficient estimate is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{\rm pair}(T;P_{\rm occ},L^{1/8})
=o_J\!\left(R\frac{T^2}{L^2}\right).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

## 7. Why high kappa does not automatically put the pairs in the deleted region

The pair amplification (2.2) is unconditional, but it does not control pair geometry. In particular, \(a_\sigma>R\) is compatible, purely combinatorially, with all selected widths lying above \(\sqrt{P_{\rm occ}}\); then every same-side pair has \(XY>P_{\rm occ}\). Likewise a large family can spread over widely separated width scales, producing a substantial wide-aspect portion.

The fact that there are only \(O(L/\log K)\) multiplicative width bands does force some bounded-aspect pairs when \(R\gg L/\log K\), but the naive convexity lower bound loses precisely the number of bands. It does not force the \(\gg R(\kappa-2R)\) **summable** pair mass needed at the target without paying an extra logarithmic factor, and it says nothing about the product condition \(XY\le P_{\rm occ}\). Taking \(K=T^\theta\) makes the number of aspect bands constant, but then \(\log K\asymp L\) in (4.1), which gives back the lost logarithm. This is the same bounded-aspect tradeoff rather than a closure.

Therefore the pair-forcing observation is valuable—it converts the positive part to a concrete second-factorial pair gate—but it is not by itself the missing analytic estimate.

## Final conclusion

1. **Yes:** high \(\kappa(q,t)\) forces many ordered lower-prime pairs, and they can be chosen entirely within the two existing carrier types. The exact inequality is (2.2), with denominator \(4\lfloor R\rfloor-2\).
2. **Yes:** Brun–Titchmarsh removes the genuinely narrow candidate windows with zero contribution, via (3.1).
3. **Yes:** the occurrence-preserving bounded-aspect/hyperbolic ledger deletes the region (4.5); the safe product cutoff for this unweighted positive-part problem is (4.3). One must not use the larger base/root cutoff without an already-proved fixed-upper multiplicity transfer.
4. **No:** the current reverse-shift and factorial-correlation carriers do not provide a global \(o(A_R)\) second-factorial occurrence bound for the complementary wide-product/wide-aspect pair region. Algebraic divisibility/height alone does not supply it.
5. Hence the strongest unconditional truncation is (6.2), and the precise remaining analytic gate is (6.4). A theorem proving (6.4)—or an occurrence-preserving extension of the common-target/shifted-gap pair deletion to that complementary geometry—would finish the stated q6 positive-part target.
