# 3. Exact two-root and m-root Smith theorem

## Theorem 3.1 — independent physical roots give independent Smith directions

Suppose

\[
x_1,\ldots,x_m\in\mathbf F_p
\]

are distinct common roots of `F` and `G`. Then

\[
\boxed{m\le\rho_p(w).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

In particular this holds for the retained physical distinguished starts in `X_{p,gamma}(w)`.

### Proof

For a common root `x`, evaluation

\[
\operatorname{ev}_x:\mathbf F_p[X]_{<n}\to\mathbf F_p
\]

annihilates the image of `Phi_w mod p`, because

\[
(AF+BG)(x)=0.
\]

Thus `ev_x` is a left-null functional of the reduced Sylvester matrix. For distinct roots, the functionals are linearly independent. Indeed, their restriction to `1,X,...,X^{m-1}` has Vandermonde determinant

\[
\det(x_i^{j-1})_{1\le i,j\le m}
=\prod_{1\le i<j\le m}(x_j-x_i)\ne0\pmod p.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence the reduced Sylvester matrix has corank at least `m`, proving (3.1). ∎

The determinant in (3.2) is the first nonzero determinant in the root-independence argument. For two roots it is simply

\[
x_2-x_1.
\]

It has root-variable degree one and `p`-adic valuation zero. It proves that the roots are distinct directions, but it is not a fixed coefficient carrier: it depends on the physical roots and may have size on the `p` scale.

## Corollary 3.2 — determinant and next-to-last determinant valuations

Let

\[
\Delta_k(S_w)
=\gcd\{\text{all }k\times k\text{ minors of }S_w\},
\qquad \Delta_0=1.
\]

Then

\[
\Delta_k(S_w)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}d_{w,i}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

If `m` distinct common roots occur, then

\[
\boxed{v_p(R_w)\ge m,}
\tag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{v_p(\Delta_{n-1}(S_w))\ge m-1.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

More generally, for `0<=j<=m`,

\[
v_p(\Delta_{n-j}(S_w))\ge m-j.
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Proof

There are at least `m` Smith factors divisible by `p`. Their contribution to `Delta_n=R_w` gives (3.4). Removing the largest `j` factors leaves at least `m-j` divisible factors among the first `n-j`, proving (3.6). ∎

For two roots, every cofactor of `S_w` vanishes modulo `p`. The gcd of all cofactors, `Delta_{n-1}`, is therefore the first nonzero fixed coefficient integer forced to acquire a new `p`.

If the reduced common-root scheme has exactly `m` split simple roots, every local Bockstein is nonzero, and there is no other `p`-primary direction, then the `p`-primary Smith module is exactly

\[
(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^m.
\]

In that transverse exact-rank case,

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\Delta_{n-j})=\max(m-j,0).}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus (3.5) is sharp.

# 5. What divided values and Omega add

=s_F(x)W_G(x)-s_G(x)W_F(x),
\tag{5.1}

and

\[
\Xi_w(x)
=\frac{\Omega_w(x)}{s_F(x)s_G(x)}.
\tag{5.2}

Let `alpha_F,alpha_G` be the two Hensel lifts of the reduced root for `F` and `G`. Then modulo `p^2`,

\[
\alpha_F=x-p\frac{W_F(x)}{s_F(x)},
\qquad
\alpha_G=x-p\frac{W_G(x)}{s_G(x)},

so

\[
\frac{\alpha_F-\alpha_G}{p}
\equiv \Xi_w(x)\pmod p.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Because both derivatives are units, the local factor of the resultant is a unit times `alpha_F-alpha_G`. Hence:

\iff \ell_x(F,G)=1,}

\[

\tag{5.5}

where `ell_x` is the local `p`-adic intersection length/resultant contribution at that split simple residue root.

Thus the selected roots contribute

\tag{5.6}

\text{in a tagged fixed-left packet}
\end{array}
\Longrightarrow
\begin{array}{c}
\text{m-1 fixed-word Smith units}\\
\text{of total tagged height }D R_\theta
\end{array}}
\]

The first line implies the `m-1` units, but not the asserted total tagged height. The same coefficient unit may be reused by up to `O(H)` fixed-left directions. Neither `q0`, the Casoratian, nor first-Witt transport distinguishes those anchor tags.

The `p=5683` fixture must not be used to identify roots across companion columns. It shows exactly that a shared first gap can enter two word resultants through different roots. The present theorem keeps each fixed word and each physical root separate, so it is compatible with that fixture.

The `p=1049` fixture is also exact:

```text
w=(10,4),
t_1049^0(w)=1,
rho_1049(w)=1,
v_1049(R_w)=1,
v_1049(Q_w^rep)=0.
```

Thus the carrier does not assign a false second unit to a singleton.

# 9. Smallest strictly narrower non-equivalent residual

The local-thick part has an exact coefficient carrier (5.7), but preserving all fixed-left tags again incurs the factor `H`. After removing every local source of extra valuation, the genuinely hard core is the split, length-one, actual-root reuse.

Fix `alpha,w`, one side, and one terminal. Define

\[
X_{p,\gamma}^{\mathrm{et}}(w)
\subseteq X_{p,\gamma}(w)
\]

by retaining only starts `x` such that

```text
Omega_w(x) != 0;
the local Sylvester component at x has length one;
there is no degree drop, center factor, or structural prime;
x is a physical q0 start satisfying every inherited hard mask.
```

To remove coefficient phantoms from the residual itself, also require at the counted prime that the selected split roots exhaust the relevant `p`-Smith directions of `w` and that

\[
v_p(R_w)=\rho_p(w).
\tag{9.1}
\]

This leaves the squarefree split case in which the inequalities of Sections 3–5 are equalities and no local `p^2` phenomenon remains.

## `Q0-ANCH-ET2_theta(alpha,w)`

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal A_p(\alpha)}
\bigl(|X_{p,\gamma}^{\mathrm{et}}(w)|-1\bigr)_+
\ll D R_\theta H^{o(1)}.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is strictly narrower than `REP-RICH_theta`:

```text
one exact companion word w only;
one fixed side and terminal;
no singleton;
no first hit;
no Omega=0 root;
no higher local resultant length;
no nonsplit or phantom Smith direction;
no degree-drop or structural component;
all physical q0 and final masks retained.
```

It is non-equivalent to the full row. A packet can be `REP-RICH` through many different words or through locally thick directions while (9.2) is zero. Conversely, the unmarked coefficient carrier (8.2) may be large through roots in the wrong projective state while the physical quantity in (9.2) vanishes.

The current proof gives only

\[
\sum_{p,\gamma}(\log p)
(|X_{p,\gamma}^{\mathrm{et}}(w)|-1)_+
\ll H D H^{o(1)}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus the exact missing gain is

\[
H^{-1/2-2\theta+o(1)}.
\]

At the RR closing value `theta=5 sigma/2`, (9.2) asks for

\[
H^{5/2-5\sigma+o(1)},
\]

whereas (9.3) is `H^{3+o(1)}`. The remaining gap is

\[
\boxed{H^{1/2+5\sigma+o(1)}.}
\tag{9.4}
\]

A coefficient-only sufficient successor is the shell depletion estimate

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\rho_p(\alpha)(\rho_p(w)-1)_+
\ll D R_\theta H^{o(1)}.
\tag{9.5}
\]

It is stronger than (9.2) and deliberately non-equivalent: it counts every projective state and every phantom direction. Equation (9.5), rather than another pointwise `p^2` assertion, is the exact anchored second-Smith statement needed by this lane.

The following script rechecks the exact bank and prints the Bockstein data for any repeated word found in an extended bank. It uses no random sampling.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Exact Q6430 repeated physical q0 word scan.

Run from the repository root:

    python3 q6430_repeated_word_scan.py \
      problems/3.2/data_zp_pairs.bin

The canonical bank is expected to print zero repeated words.  If the data
file is extended, every same-prime repeated word is printed together with
raw N-values mod p^2, derivatives, and Omega at each physical start.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from collections import defaultdict
from hashlib import sha256
from pathlib import Path
import struct
import sys

EXPECTED_SHA256 = (
    "8746d0b400c1b669b001eae955c602908a10c9ee4cb3cac62c6676ea2ddd874d"
)


def P(t: int) -> int:
    return 34 * t**3 + 51 * t**2 + 27 * t + 5


def dP(t: int) -> int:
    return 102 * t**2 + 102 * t + 27


def gap_value_derivative(h: int, x: int, modulus: int) -> tuple[int, int]:
    """Return (N_h(x), N_h'(x)) modulo modulus."""
    if h < 0:
        raise ValueError("negative gap")
    n_prev, n_cur = 0, 1
    d_prev, d_cur = 0, 0
    if h == 0:
        return n_prev, d_prev
    if h == 1:
        return n_cur, d_cur
    for k in range(1, h):
        t = x + k
        n_next = (P(t) * n_cur - t**6 * n_prev) % modulus
        d_next = (
            dP(t) * n_cur
            + P(t) * d_cur
            - 6 * t**5 * n_prev
            - t**6 * d_prev
        ) % modulus
        n_prev, n_cur = n_cur, n_next
        d_prev, d_cur = d_cur, d_next
    return n_cur, d_cur


def omega_at(prime: int, a: int, b: int, x: int) -> dict[str, int]:
    mod = prime * prime
    na, dna = gap_value_derivative(a, x, mod)
    nb, dnb = gap_value_derivative(b, x + a, mod)
    assert na % prime == 0
    assert nb % prime == 0
    wa = (na // prime) % prime
    wb = (nb // prime) % prime
    sa = dna % prime
    sb = dnb % prime
    simple = (sa * sb) % prime != 0
    omega = (sa * wb - sb * wa) % prime
    xi = None
    if simple:
        xi = omega * pow((sa * sb) % prime, -1, prime) % prime
    return {
        "x": x,
        "Wa": wa,
        "Wb": wb,
        "sa": sa,
        "sb": sb,
        "Omega": omega,
        "Xi": -1 if xi is None else xi,
        "simple": int(simple),
    }


def load_zero_bank(path: Path) -> dict[int, tuple[int, ...]]:
    raw = path.read_bytes()
    digest = sha256(raw).hexdigest()
    print(f"sha256={digest}")
    if digest != EXPECTED_SHA256:
        print("warning: noncanonical or extended bank")
    if len(raw) % 8:
        raise ValueError("bank length is not divisible by 8")
    grouped: dict[int, list[int]] = defaultdict(list)
    previous = (-1, -1)
    for p, r in struct.iter_unpack("<II", raw):
        if (p, r) <= previous:
            raise ValueError("bank is not strictly sorted")
        if not (0 <= r < p):
            raise ValueError("bad residue")
        grouped[p].append(r)
        previous = (p, r)
    return {p: tuple(rs) for p, rs in grouped.items()}


def main() -> None:
    path = Path(sys.argv[1]) if len(sys.argv) > 1 else Path(
        "problems/3.2/data_zp_pairs.bin"
    )
    zero_sets = load_zero_bank(path)

    global_words: dict[tuple[int, int], list[tuple[int, int]]] = defaultdict(list)
    repeated: list[tuple[int, tuple[int, int], tuple[int, ...]]] = []
    triples = 0

    for p, zeros in zero_sets.items():
        local: dict[tuple[int, int], list[int]] = defaultdict(list)
        for i in range(len(zeros) - 2):
            x, y, z = zeros[i : i + 3]
            word = (y - x, z - y)
            local[word].append(x)
            global_words[word].append((p, x))
            triples += 1
        for word, starts in local.items():
            if len(starts) >= 2:
                repeated.append((p, word, tuple(starts)))

    print(f"zero_incidences={sum(map(len, zero_sets.values()))}")
    print(f"represented_primes={len(zero_sets)}")
    print(f"maximum_prime={max(zero_sets)}")
    print(f"consecutive_triples={triples}")
    print(f"distinct_oriented_words={len(global_words)}")
    print(f"same_prime_repeated_words={len(repeated)}")

    if sha256(path.read_bytes()).hexdigest() == EXPECTED_SHA256:
        assert sum(map(len, zero_sets.values())) == 149112
        assert len(zero_sets) == 58555
        assert max(zero_sets) == 1999993
        assert triples == 32004
        assert len(global_words) == 32004
        assert not repeated

    for p, (a, b), starts in repeated:
        print(f"candidate p={p} word=({a},{b}) starts={starts}")
        for x in starts:
            print("  ", omega_at(p, a, b, x))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

For any candidate printed by an extended bank, the following Sage fragment computes the exact primitive, center-deflated Sylvester Smith factors and verifies the valuation ledger. It is intentionally run only after a physical candidate is found.

```python
# SageMath
from sage.all import ZZ, PolynomialRing, matrix, gcd

R = PolynomialRing(ZZ, "X")
X = R.gen()


def Ppoly(T):
    return 34*T**3 + 51*T**2 + 27*T + 5


def gap_polys(H):
    N = [R(0), R(1)]
    for h in range(1, H):
        N.append(Ppoly(X+h)*N[h] - (X+h)**6*N[h-1])
    return N


def primitive_center_deflate(f, h):
    if h % 2 == 0:
        q, rem = f.quo_rem(2*X+h+1)
        assert rem == 0
        f = q
    cont = gcd([ZZ(c) for c in f.list()])
    f = f // cont
    return -f if f.leading_coefficient() < 0 else f


def sylvester_map_matrix(f, g):
    r, s = f.degree(), g.degree()
    n = r+s
    cols = []
    for j in range(s):
        col = [ZZ(0)]*n
        for k, c in enumerate(f.list()):
            col[j+k] = c
        cols.append(col)
    for j in range(r):
        col = [ZZ(0)]*n
        for k, c in enumerate(g.list()):
            col[j+k] = c
        cols.append(col)
    return matrix(ZZ, n, n, lambda i, j: cols[j][i])


def check_candidate(p, a, b, starts):
    N = gap_polys(max(a, b))
    f = primitive_center_deflate(N[a], a)
    g = primitive_center_deflate(N[b], b)(X+a)
    S = sylvester_map_matrix(f, g)
    ds = [abs(ZZ(d)) for d in S.elementary_divisors()]
    rho = sum(d % p == 0 for d in ds)
    vdet = abs(S.det()).valuation(p)
    vnext = sum(d.valuation(p) for d in ds[:-1])
    print({"rho": rho, "vdet": vdet, "vnext": vnext, "starts": starts})
    assert rho >= len(starts)
    assert vdet >= len(starts)
    assert vnext >= len(starts)-1
```

# Conclusion

The two-root idea succeeds exactly once: it produces the next Smith direction. The sharp fixed coefficient carrier is the squarefree-by-direction next-to-last Smith volume `Q_w^rep`, and its primewise valuation is at least `m-1`. Divided values, derivatives, first-Witt data, and Casoratians classify the thickness of those directions but do not generate an anchor-dependent new one.

The obstruction is no longer local. It is tagged reuse. Once the same prime, physical fixed-left orbit, side, terminal, and masks are preserved, the same fixed-word second direction may be reused across `O(H)` left directions. The best current bound is therefore `H D`, while the required budget is `D R_theta`. The exact remaining gain is `H/R_theta=H^{1/2+2theta+o(1)}`. At the RR closing value it is `H^{1/2+5sigma+o(1)}`.

Accordingly, the next theorem should target anchored split-root anti-reuse, not another singleton `p^2` claim and not another renewal identity.
