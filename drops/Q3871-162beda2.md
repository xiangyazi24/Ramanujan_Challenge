# 1. Full \(q\)-primary stripping is exact and harmless for lower support

Because \(q\mid b_u\), Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
b_{6q+u}\equiv b_6b_u\equiv0\pmod q,
\qquad
b_{12q+u}\equiv b_{12}b_u\equiv0\pmod q.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Hence

\[
\nu_\sigma=v_q(g_\sigma)\ge1,
\]

and (V2) is an integer with

\[
q\nmid A_\sigma.
\tag{1.2}
\]

For every prime \(\ell\), the exact valuation formula is

\[
\boxed{
v_\ell(A_\sigma)
=
\min\{v_\ell(b_{6q+u}),v_\ell(b_{12q+u})\}
-
\mathbf1_{\ell=q}\,\nu_\sigma.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Every genuine lower prime satisfies \(p<q\), so \(p\ne q\), and therefore

\[
\boxed{
v_p(A_\sigma)
=
\min\{v_p(b_{6q+u}),v_p(b_{12q+u})\}.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Thus full stripping removes no lower-support unit and no lower \(p\)-adic multiplicity. It merely corrects the caveat in Q3751, where division by one copy of \(q\) could leave further \(q\)-power.

This remains true globally. If the same prime \(p\) is a lower endpoint in several upper states, then

\[
\#\{\sigma:p\mid A_\sigma\}
\le
v_p\!\left(\prod_\sigma A_\sigma\right).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The fact that the same integer \(p\) might be the **upper** prime of another state causes no loss. Full \(p\)-primary stripping in that other factor only makes that factor have valuation zero at \(p\); it does not subtract the positive valuations contributed by states in which \(p\) is lower. Valuations of a product add and never cancel.

Finally, \(\kappa_\sigma\) is squarefree support. A prime with \(v_p(A_\sigma)>1\) is counted once. This is correct because, at fixed \((q,u)\), one lower prime determines at most one physical plus edge.

# 9. Why multiplying pair carriers is circular

Fix one state and write its distinct lower support as

\[
\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\},
\qquad
P_\sigma:=\prod_{i=1}^k p_i.
\]

The q-free gcd gives only

\[
P_\sigma\mid A_\sigma.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Every unordered pair is supported by \(p_ip_j\), but multiplying all pair supports gives

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{1\le i<j\le k}p_ip_j
=P_\sigma^{k-1}.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Thus the only automatic pair carrier obtained from the first-degree gcd support is \(A_\sigma^{k-1}\). Its logarithmic height is

\[
(k-1)\log A_\sigma,
\]

which already contains the unknown degree \(k\). At shell scale this is of order \(k^2L\), exactly the factorial quantity one is trying to bound. No saving is produced.

The same failure occurs if one multiplies the individual continuant or resultant certificates attached to all pairs. Every endpoint is repeated \(k-1\) times, so the aggregate certificate height grows by the same degree factor as the desired pair count.

This is the precise multiplicity obstruction: the two-value gcd supplies one lower-prime valuation unit per endpoint, not one unit per unordered pair of endpoints.

## 11.3 Wronskian scope

The classical Apéry Casoratian controls the pair of solutions \((a_m,b_m)\) at adjacent indices. It gives strong information about \(\gcd(d_ma_m,d_mb_m)\), but it is not a small closed formula for

A transfer-matrix expression between \(n\) and \(N\) crosses multiples of the lower primes. After the correct Lucas reductions, it becomes exactly the digit-12/13 continuant split in Q3752. No additional low-height cross-index gcd identity is currently proved.

Thus the WZ and p-adic directions are not logically impossible; they simply require a new cross-characteristic support theorem, not another divisibility lift.

\log R_\sigma^{\rm low}
\le\log A_\sigma
\le\min\{\log b_n,\log b_N\}
\ll_JT.
\tag{12.1}
\]

This gives one logarithmic unit for each distinct lower factor:

\[
\kappa_\sigma L\ll_JT.
\]

It cannot distinguish whether those factors occur in one high-support state or are spread across many states. Summing over all selected states yields only (4.5).

Full q-primary stripping changes (12.1) to

\[
\log A_\sigma
=
\log g_\sigma-\nu_\sigma\log q.
\tag{12.2}
\]

The known lower bound \(\nu_\sigma\ge1\) saves only \(O(L)\) per state. Aggregated over \(B_1\) states, this is at most

\[
O(B_1L)=O(T^{8/5}L^{1/5}),
\]

far below the \(T^2/L\) carrier-height scale relevant to the target. A useful value-gcd attack must prove an average radical saving specifically on high-\(\kappa\) states; this is exactly `(UGCD-rad)`.

# 13. A support model showing that the current inequalities do not imply the target

This section is a logical non-implication model only. It is **not** asserted to arise from actual Apéry numbers.

Let

\[
N_*\asymp B_1
\asymp T^{8/5}L^{-4/5}
\]

states be active, and give every active state the same support degree

\[
K_*\asymp
\frac{T^{11/5}L^{-8/5}}{B_1}
=
T^{3/5}L^{-4/5}.
\tag{13.1}
\]

Then

\[
N_*K_*\asymp T^{11/5}L^{-8/5},
\tag{13.2}
\]

so the current common-value first-moment bound is saturated.

The pointwise height constraint is respected because

\[
K_*\ll T/L
\]

and in fact

\[
K_*L=T^{3/5}L^{1/5}=o(T).
\tag{13.3}
\]

Thus an integer of logarithmic height \(O(T)\) can support that many shell primes.

On the other hand,

\[
\frac{K_*}{R}
\asymp
\eta(T)T^{1/5}L^{2/5}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{13.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{\sigma}(K_*-R)_+
\sim N_*K_*
\asymp T^{11/5}L^{-8/5},
\tag{13.5}
\]

which exceeds \(Q_0\) by the factor in (V7).

This model simultaneously respects:

- the number-of-states bound \(B_1\);
- the pointwise value-height support ceiling;
- the strongest current first-incidence bound;
- the fact that the vertical second-factorial theorem is only being used to count endpoints once.

Hence those numerical inputs alone cannot prove the truncated excess theorem. A genuinely Apéry-specific concentration statement is necessary.

# 14. The smallest new arithmetic theorem

Let

\[
H_R^{\rm supp}(T)
:=
\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathcal U(T)\\
                 \kappa_\sigma>R}}
\kappa_\sigma.
\tag{14.1}
\]

From (7.1),

\[
H_R^{\rm supp}(T)
=E_R^+(T)+R|\mathcal U_{>R}|.
\tag{14.2}
\]

Since \(|\mathcal U_{>R}|\le B_1\), (V4) gives

\[
0\le H_R^{\rm supp}(T)-E_R^+(T)
\le RB_1
=o(Q_0).
\tag{14.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
E_R^+(T)=o_J(Q_0)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H_R^{\rm supp}(T)=o_J(Q_0).
}
\tag{14.4}
\]

This proves that `(UGCD-tail)` is the smallest clean high-value support theorem: it differs from the exact residual only by the layer already paid in (V4).

Because every counted prime is in a fixed macroscopic interval,

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{\mathcal P_L(T)}(A_\sigma)
\asymp_J \kappa_\sigma L.
\tag{14.5}
\]

Therefore `(UGCD-tail)` is equivalent to `(UGCD-rad)`. The radical form is often the more useful arithmetic target:

> **Depth-restricted plus upper-state gcd-radical theorem.**  On upper states with more than \(R\) legal lower shell factors, the aggregate lower-shell radical of the full-q-free gcd
> \[
> \frac{\gcd(b_{6q+u},b_{12q+u})}
>      {q^{v_q(\gcd(b_{6q+u},b_{12q+u}))}}
> \]
> has logarithmic height \(o_J(T^2/L)\).

It retains every exact Apéry condition, every actual prime, and the full q-primary normalization. It assumes no statistical independence.

If a theorem with a genuine moment surplus is desired, `(KAPPA-2)` is the first natural one. It is strictly stronger than (14.4), but it is the form that a new cross-characteristic determinant or averaged gcd theorem would most naturally attack.

# 15. Exact amount of missing saving

There are two useful ways to state the deficit.

## 15.1 First-degree/high-radical route

The current common-value estimate gives

\[
L\sum_\sigma\kappa_\sigma
\ll_J
T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}+T^2.
\tag{15.1}
\]

The required high-state radical height is

\[
o_J(T^2/L).
\tag{15.2}
\]

The leading ratio is

\[
\frac{T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}}{T^2L^{-1}}
=
\boxed{T^{1/5}L^{2/5}}.
\tag{15.3}
\]

Thus a new theorem must save this complete factor, and then an additional function tending to infinity to obtain little-\(o\).

Relative to the already negligible first-\(R\) budget,

\[
RB_1\asymp Q_0/\eta,
\]

while the current first incidence scale is \(M(T)\). Their ratio is

\[
\frac{M(T)}{RB_1}
\asymp
\eta(T)T^{1/5}L^{2/5}.
\tag{15.4}
\]

This is the exact gap between the average support permitted by current inputs and the truncation threshold.

## 15.2 Second-factorial route

The required centered second-factorial scale is (8.3), whereas the best current generic bound is (8.4). The leading ratio is

\[
\boxed{\eta(T)T^{4/5}L^{3/5}.}
\tag{15.5}
\]

This larger deficit explains why forcing a second-moment proof from the present first-degree carrier is less efficient than first proving a high-radical tail theorem.

# Bottom line

1. Full \(q\)-primary stripping is completely safe: it removes all upper-prime contamination and preserves every lower factor and every repeated lower occurrence across stars.
2. At fixed \((q,u)\), a legal lower prime determines one and only one plus endpoint. If \(\kappa\) is defined on the coarse shell rather than the exact plus fibre, it is an overcount because of the digit-12 branch.
3. The shifted-resultant theorem injects each endpoint into one pair of lower zeros and proves only a first moment of \(\kappa\).
4. The common-value \(p,q\)-mate theorem gives the strongest current estimate
   \[
   E_R^+(T)\ll_J T^{11/5}L^{-8/5}+T^2/L,
   \]
   which is supercritical by \(T^{1/5}L^{2/5}\).
5. Pairing lower primes within one upper state crosses characteristics. Ordinary same-characteristic resultants, WZ lifts, and q-adic stripping do not supply the missing factorial multiplicity.
6. The minimal high-value arithmetic statement is `(UGCD-tail)`, equivalently `(UGCD-rad)`. The first natural stronger moment statement is `(KAPPA-2)` at scale
   \[
   o_J\!\left(T^{12/5}/(\eta L^{16/5})\right).
   \]

Therefore an unconditional \(o(T^2/\log^2T)\) theorem is **not** obtainable from the current pushed inputs. The first genuinely new edge is an averaged high-support radical theorem for the full-q-free two-value Apéry gcd, or a cross-characteristic second-factorial theorem for \(\kappa\) with the exact scale above.
