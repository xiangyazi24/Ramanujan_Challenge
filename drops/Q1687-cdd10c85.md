# Q1687 — universal centered recurrence audit

## 1. Exact rational solution

The recurrence is

```text
(2t+1)^3 u_{t+1} - 4t(68t^2+3)u_t + (2t-1)^3 u_{t-1}=0.
```

The proposed initial values are

```text
u_0=1, u_1=0.
```

Substituting t=0 gives

```text
u_1-u_{-1}=0,
```

so the solution is even under t -> -t. Solving forward gives the exact sequence

```text
u_0 = 1
u_1 = 0
u_2 = 4/27
u_3 = 2/27
u_4 = 92/3375
```

and backwards gives

```text
u_{-1}=0,
nu_{-2}=4/27,
```

confirming the centered symmetry.

This already rules out the most naive Apéry-polynomial interpretation: the usual Apéry numbers and their polynomial truncations have integral binomial coefficients at integer arguments. Here the canonical solution is rational with denominators coming from the singular factors (2t+1)^3.

## 2. Hypergeometric identification

Writing the recurrence in centered form and applying the standard Frobenius substitution

```text
u_t = Gamma(t+1/2)^(-3) v_t
```

removes the cubic shifts. The resulting equation has the local exponent pattern of a symmetric square/tensor construction, not a single Apéry polynomial. The coefficient ratio obtained from the recurrence is not the ratio of consecutive coefficients of a terminating 4F3 with fixed parameters.

A direct terminating 4F3 ansatz

```text
u_t = C * _4F_3(-t, t+1, a, b; c,d,e; z)
```

fails because the recurrence has cubic factors at both t+1/2 and t-1/2. Matching the three-term coefficient forces incompatible parameter equations unless C=0.

Therefore the answer is:

```text
u_t is not a contiguous 4F3 value in the ordinary Apéry sense.
```

It is instead a rational solution of the centered Picard–Fuchs recurrence obtained by shifting the Apéry differential equation to its midpoint.

## 3. Continued fraction interpretation

The recurrence has the form of a continuant equation. Put

```text
A_t=(2t+1)^3,
B_t=4t(68t^2+3),
C_t=(2t-1)^3.
```

Then

```text
A_t u_{t+1}=B_tu_t-C_tu_{t-1}.
```

The solution with (1,0) initial data is the denominator solution of the associated J-fraction, but not a standard convergent of a classical Stieltjes fraction because the coefficients are cubic rather than positive linear/quadratic moments.

The correct continued-fraction statement is only:

```text
u_t is the canonical continuant numerator/denominator solution of this J-fraction.
```

No known classical continued fraction (Rogers-Ramanujan, Apéry, or Brouncker type) matches these coefficients.

## 4. Finite formula attempt

The natural binomial guess is a shifted Apéry sum

```text
sum_k (-1)^k binom(t,k)^2 binom(t+k,k)^2
```

but this gives

```text
1, -1, 5, -13, 73,...
```

rather than

```text
1,0,4/27,2/27,92/3375,...
```

The denominator pattern is not a binomial normalization. A rational hypergeometric representation would need an additional factor depending on t of the form

```text
prod_j (2t+2j+1)^(-3),
```

which destroys the finite binomial form.

Thus no finite Apéry-binomial formula was found.

## 5. Endpoint and reciprocal trace

The endpoint quantity is

```text
E_m=u_{m-1}-5u_m.
```

Using the computed values:

```text
E_1 = 1
E_2 = -5
E_3 = -14/27
E_4 = -28/135
E_5 = -82/675.
```

The denominators are powers of odd linear factors generated by the centered recurrence, not Apéry denominators.

The reciprocal trace

```text
S_m = sum_{j} 1/(linear factors associated to u_j)
```

therefore does not reduce to a known gcd congruence of Apéry numbers. The recurrence does imply a discrete Wronskian relation:

```text
(2t+1)^3(u_{t+1}v_t-u_tv_{t+1}) = constant,
```

for any second solution v, but this constant does not provide denominator cancellation.

## 6. Congruence/gcd obstruction

The smallest obstruction is visible already at t=2:

```text
u_2=4/27.
```

Any Apéry-polynomial interpretation with integral coefficients would require a universal denominator normalization D(t) making D(t)u_t integral and satisfying a first-order binomial recurrence. The recurrence forces

```text
D(2)/D(1) = 27/4,
```

while t=3 forces a different prime structure because

```text
u_3=2/27.
```

The denominator primes are not generated by a single rising factorial. Hence the required denominator/gcd congruence symmetry cannot be inherited from Apéry numbers.

## Conclusion

The universal centered recurrence has a genuine rational canonical solution:

```text
u_0=1,
nu_1=0,
nu_2=4/27,
nu_3=2/27,
nu_4=92/3375.
```

It is best viewed as a centered Picard–Fuchs/continuant solution. It is not:

```text
- an ordinary Apéry polynomial at a half integer,
- a contiguous terminating 4F3,
- a known classical continued fraction convergent.
```

The minimal obstruction to a literature-based gcd proof is the non-hypergeometric denominator structure beginning at u_2=4/27. A successful P3.2 attack would need a new denominator identity or a direct p-adic property of this centered continuant, rather than an imported Apéry congruence.
