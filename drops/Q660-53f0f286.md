# Q669: Hidden conservation law / coboundary for \(1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}\)

## Verdict

There are two important corrections to the proposed conclusion.

1. **A uniformly bounded exact coboundary is much stronger than AFP, not equivalent to it.** In the present `proof.tex`, AFP/\(\bar Z\) is the Cesàro statement
   \[
   \sum_{p\le X} Z_p\ll \pi(X).
   \]
   An identity
   \[
   1_{b_r=0}-\frac1{p+1}=F_p(r+1)-F_p(r),
   \qquad \sup_{p,r}|F_p(r)|<\infty,
   \]
   would give \(Z_p=1+O(1)\) **for every prime**. The same `proof.tex` records the Poisson model and predicts
   \(\max_{p\le X}Z_p\asymp 2\log\log X\), hence likely unbounded. Thus a uniformly bounded exact telescope would contradict the project’s current probabilistic prediction, though of course that prediction is not a theorem.

2. **On one finite orbit, an exact \(F\) always exists tautologically.** Namely,
   \[
   F_p(R):=\sum_{r<R}\left(1_{b_r=0}-\frac1{p+1}\right).
   \]
   Therefore the meaningful question is not existence, but whether \(F_p\) has a formula of uniformly bounded algebraic/representation-theoretic complexity and whether its endpoint has a controllable average over \(p\).

The recurrence and its second solution do give a very useful exact structure, but it is a **local scattering law**, not a global zero-count conservation law. The best genuine candidate is a p-adic local-time/Busemann construction for the projective ratio \(b_r/c_r\), necessarily with a residual “crossing” term. A pure first-difference identity for the raw zero indicator is obstructed both by periodic cycles and by algebraic degree.

Up to the harmless endpoint conventions in the displayed Steinberg sum, write
\[
A_p(x)=1_{x=w}-\frac1{p+1}
\]
on \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\). The correct target is more plausibly
\[
A_p(x_r)=H_{p,r+1}(x_{r+1})-H_{p,r}(x_r)+E_{p,r},
\tag{1}
\]
where \(H\) is controlled and the accumulated residual \(\sum_rE_{p,r}\) has bounded first or second moment over primes. This is a deterministic analogue of a martingale–coboundary or local-time decomposition. Setting \(E=0\) is too rigid.

## 1. The exact second solution is \(c_r=a_r/6\)

Let
\[
P(r)=(2r+1)(17r^2+17r+5)
     =34r^3+51r^2+27r+5.
\]
Both Apéry solutions satisfy
\[
(r+1)^3x_{r+1}=P(r)x_r-r^3x_{r-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
The project normalizes
\[
(a_0,a_1)=(0,6),\qquad (b_0,b_1)=(1,5).
\]
Consequently, the solution requested in part (E),
\[
c_0=0,\qquad c_1=1,
\]
is exactly
\[
\boxed{c_r=a_r/6.}
\tag{3}
\]
For \(r<p\), its rational denominators are \(p\)-adic units, so reduction modulo \(p\) is unambiguous.

Dividing the project’s Casoratian identity
\[
a_rb_{r-1}-a_{r-1}b_r=\frac6{r^3}
\]
by \(6\) gives
\[
\boxed{c_rb_{r-1}-c_{r-1}b_r=\frac1{r^3}.}
\tag{4}
\]
Hence, whenever \(1\le r\le p-1\) and \(b_r=0\pmod p\),
\[
\boxed{c_rb_{r-1}=r^{-3}\ne0.}
\tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
c_r\ne0,\qquad b_{r-1}\ne0,
\]
and the requested quotient is always the completely rigid value
\[
\boxed{b_r/c_r=0.}
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(b_r/c_r\) at a zero cannot reveal a subtler numerical pattern. The useful information is (5), together with the adjacent-value law
\[
\boxed{\frac{b_{r+1}}{b_{r-1}}
       =-\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^3}
\qquad (b_r=0).
\tag{7}
\]

## 2. What the recurrence really supplies: a projective scattering law

Put
\[
s_r=\frac{b_r}{b_{r-1}}\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p).
\]
Then (2) is the non-autonomous Riccati recurrence
\[
s_{r+1}
 =\frac{P(r)}{(r+1)^3}
  -\frac{r^3}{(r+1)^3}\frac1{s_r}.
\tag{8}
\]
A zero of \(b_r\) is the event \(s_r=0\). In projective terms it forces
\[
0\longmapsto\infty
\]
at the next step, and (7) determines the two-step passage from \(b_{r-1}\) to \(b_{r+1}\). This explains the no-consecutive-zero phenomenon.

It does **not** limit the number of later returns to \(0\). A fixed-order recurrence with bounded-degree coefficients, by itself, has no bounded-zero theorem over finite fields. Even the constant-coefficient order-two recurrence
\[
x_{r+2}+x_r=0,\qquad x_0=0,\ x_1=1,
\]
has a zero at every even index. Thus the desired conclusion cannot follow from “order \(2\), degree \(3\)” alone. It must use an additional Apéry-specific invariant, monotonicity, sheaf-theoretic cancellation, or arithmetic of the ordered transfer product.

### 2.1 Why the logarithmic-ratio potential does not count zeros

Let \(V_r=v_p(b_r)\). The natural p-adic logarithmic slope is
\[
\phi_r=v_p(b_r/b_{r-1})=V_r-V_{r-1}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is already a first difference. At an isolated zero modulo \(p\), \(V_r>0\) while its two neighbors are normally \(0\); therefore \(\phi_r\) jumps up on entry and \(\phi_{r+1}\) jumps down on exit. Summing \(\phi_r\) records **net displacement**, not the number of visits. The positive and negative jumps cancel.

The ordinary p-adic logarithm is even less suitable: it is defined on the unit part and does not detect the valuation. The truncated valuation
\[
\min(1,V_r)=1_{p\mid b_r}
\]
detects the zero, but that simply restates the observable to be explained.

The real logarithm \(\log|b_r/b_{r-1}|\) sees archimedean growth of the integer sequence and has no direct sensitivity to divisibility by the varying prime \(p\).

This is the standard distinction between a **Busemann/displacement cocycle** and **local time**. A first difference measures signed crossings; occupation count requires an additional local-time term.

## 3. The best recurrence-side coordinate is \(q_r=b_r/c_r\)

Where the denominators are nonzero, (4) gives two exact discrete logarithmic-derivative identities:
\[
\frac{b_r}{c_r}-\frac{b_{r-1}}{c_{r-1}}
 =-\frac1{r^3c_rc_{r-1}},
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\frac{c_r}{b_r}-\frac{c_{r-1}}{b_{r-1}}
 =\frac1{r^3b_rb_{r-1}}.
\tag{11}
\]
The first ratio
\[
q_r:=b_r/c_r
\]
is especially natural because every zero of \(b_r\) is an honest zero of \(q_r\), never a \(0/0\), by (5).

Over the reals, an analogue of \(q_r\) can be converted into a Prüfer phase, and monotonicity of that phase leads to Sturm oscillation theorems. Over \(\mathbf F_p\), there is no ordered circle and no notion of monotone phase. Equation (10) permits repeated returns to \(0\) without contradiction.

A serious p-adic candidate is therefore:

> Lift \(q_r\) canonically to \(\mathbf Q_p\), view its reductions as vertices/ends in the Bruhat–Tits tree, and construct a truncated Busemann or local-time functional for the residue ball \(q\equiv0\pmod p\).

But a Busemann function alone only telescopes net motion. To recover visits one needs a p-adic analogue of a Tanaka/local-time formula, hence an error or crossing term as in (1). Proving that the accumulated crossing term has bounded average would be a genuine new Apéry theorem.

### 3.1 An intrinsic \(SL_2\)-frame

The second solution completes the single projective orbit to a full fundamental matrix. Define
\[
Y_r=
\begin{pmatrix}
 b_r&c_r\\
 b_{r-1}&c_{r-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
By (4),
\[
\det Y_r=-r^{-3}.
\]
Thus
\[
G_r:=Y_r
\begin{pmatrix}-r^3&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}
-r^3b_r&c_r\\
-r^3b_{r-1}&c_{r-1}
\end{pmatrix}
\in SL_2(\mathbf F_p).
\tag{12}
\]
The event \(b_r=0\) is the Schubert/Borel-coset condition
\[
(G_r)_{11}=0.
\tag{13}
\]
At such a time, (5) says that the remaining off-diagonal entries are forced to have product \(-1\), so no rank degeneration occurs.

This suggests a geometric potential measuring p-adic distance from the tube above the divisor \(g_{11}=0\). It is the most concrete candidate generated by the recurrence. However:

* \(v_p(g_{11})\) detects visits but is not a group cocycle;
* a genuine Busemann cocycle is additive under products but records displacement rather than visits;
* converting one into the other requires a no-backtracking or bounded-crossing theorem for the lifted Apéry path.

That no-backtracking/local-time estimate is a meaningful formulation of the missing “hidden law.” It is not supplied by the Casoratian itself.

## 4. The transfer-matrix identity is true but does not telescope the occupation count

Let
\[
x_{r+1}=T_r x_r,\qquad x_r=M_rv,\qquad
M_r=T_{r-1}\cdots T_0,
\]
and let
\[
A(x)=1_{x=w}-\frac1{p+1}.
\]
The proposed identity
\[
1_{x_{r+1}=w}-1_{x_r=w}
 =1_{x_r=T_r^{-1}w}-1_{x_r=w}
\tag{14}
\]
is correct. Its left side telescopes, but its right side compares the fixed target \(w\) with a **different moving target** \(T_r^{-1}w\) at every time. There is no invariant notion that two distinct points of \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\) are “close”; in the permutation/Steinberg norm, all distinct point masses are equally separated.

The exact cohomological equation on the time-dependent skew product is
\[
H_{r+1}(T_rx)-H_r(x)=A(x).
\tag{15}
\]
It always has a formal solution. For example, with \(M_0=1\),
\[
H_r(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}A(M_jM_r^{-1}y)
\tag{16}
\]
satisfies (15). Evaluating at \(y=x_r=M_rv\) gives
\[
H_r(x_r)=\sum_{j<r}A(x_j).
\]
Thus the formal transfer function is exactly the occupation discrepancy in disguise. Bounding it is equivalent to the original problem.

### 4.1 Steinberg-module interpretation

The mean-zero functions on \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\) form the Steinberg summand of the permutation representation, and
\[
A=\delta_w-\frac1{p+1}\mathbf 1
\]
lies in that summand. Each \(T_r\) acts unitarily by permutation. Therefore the deterministic time-shift operator has no contraction merely because the static Cayley graph generated by the set \(\{T_r\}\) is an expander. The random-walk averaging operator may have a spectral gap; the prescribed ordered product is a product of unitary permutations and need not inherit it.

One can average the steps and solve a Poisson equation such as
\[
(I-\mathcal P_p)H=A,
\qquad
\mathcal P_pf=\frac1{p-2}\sum_r f\circ T_r,
\tag{17}
\]
but transferring this averaged solution back to the deterministic order requires precisely the missing discrepancy theorem. So (17) is a useful organizational device, not a proof.

### 4.2 The endpoint involution gives a cycle obstruction, not a bound

Suppose one identifies the time variable after \(L\) steps and the total product is the involution \(S=M_L\). Iterating (15) gives
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}A(M_rx)
 =H_0(Sx)-H_0(x)
\tag{18}
\]
for a time-periodic transfer function. If \(S^2=1\), the left side must be \(S\)-anti-invariant:
\[
\mathcal S_A(Sx)=-\mathcal S_A(x),
\]
and it must vanish at every fixed point of \(S\). This can explain reflection relations, but it gives no control of the magnitude on a two-cycle.

More generally, an exact global coboundary on a finite skew product must have zero sum on every closed cycle. Here that would require
\[
\#\{\text{hits of }w\text{ on the cycle}\}
   =\frac{\text{cycle length}}{p+1}.
\tag{19}
\]
This generally fails even by integrality. Hence the raw centered point indicator cannot be an exact globally defined periodic coboundary. This is the finite-state version of the usual periodic-orbit obstruction in Livšic theory.

### 4.3 A bounded-degree algebraic potential is impossible

There is also a simple algebraic obstruction. Suppose \(H_r(x)\) were a rational function of \(x\) of degree at most \(D\), uniformly in \(p\), with no poles on \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\). Since \(T_r\) is Möbius, the left side of (15) would have degree \(O(D)\). But it would equal one constant at all \(p\) points \(x\ne w\) and a different value at \(w\).

After subtracting the common constant and clearing denominators, a nonzero polynomial of degree \(O(D)\) would have at least \(p\) roots. For \(p\gg D\), this is impossible. Equivalently, the affine delta function has polynomial representative
\[
1_{x=0}=1-x^{p-1},
\]
whose degree necessarily grows with \(p\).

Therefore an exact regular potential of uniformly bounded rational degree cannot exist. Any useful construction must be non-algebraic/discontinuous (valuation, local time), have complexity growing with \(p\), or include a residual term.

## 5. The asserted unit-root factorization through \(c_r\) is false

The relation proposed in part (C),
\[
b_r\equiv \alpha_p^r c_r\pmod p,
\tag{20}
\]
with \(c_0=0,c_1=1\), cannot hold:

* at \(r=0\), it says \(1=0\);
* at \(r=1\), it would force \(\alpha_p\equiv5\pmod p\) for every ordinary prime.

Conceptually, it mixes two different parameters. The unit root \(\alpha_p\) belongs to Frobenius on the cohomology of a fixed K3 fiber (or to the Picard–Fuchs system in the fiber variable). The index \(r\) is the recurrence/Mellin-character parameter. A unit-root formula in the fiber variable does not imply exponential dependence \(\alpha_p^r\) in the character index.

The complementary recurrence solution is exactly \(a_r/6\); it is not the non-unit part of a Frobenius eigenvector decomposition.

If a character-side Gross–Koblitz normalization supplies units \(u_{p,r}\) and one defines
\[
m_r=u_{p,r}b_r,
\]
then the zeros are unchanged, and the recurrence merely undergoes the gauge transformation
\[
(r+1)^3m_{r+1}
=P(r)\frac{u_{p,r+1}}{u_{p,r}}m_r
-r^3\frac{u_{p,r+1}}{u_{p,r-1}}m_{r-1}.
\tag{21}
\]
Such a gauge is useful only if the two unit ratios in (21) have an additional simple structure that yields monotonicity, a p-adic geodesic property, or correlation cancellation. Multiplication by units alone cannot create a zero-return bound.

The p-adic route should therefore be stated as follows:

> Construct the character-side Frobenius-normalized projective orbit and prove a bounded-crossing/local-time theorem for it.

That is materially stronger than merely identifying the unit root.

## 6. What modularity gives for all \(r\)

The central congruence
\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p\bigl(\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4\bigr)\pmod p
\]
is special because \(r=(p-1)/2\) corresponds to the quadratic character, whose order is fixed. Extra descent and symmetry can then isolate a fixed modular motive.

For general \(r\), the project already has the more appropriate all-character statement: in its K3/Mellin normalization,
\[
b_r\equiv-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\theta_p(t)t^r\pmod p,
\tag{22}
\]
or equivalently a Mellin transform against the Teichmüller character \(\omega^r\). Thus there is an “automorphic/sheaf quantity” for every \(r\), but it is a **varying character twist**, not the coefficient or value of one fixed classical modular form.

As \(r\) varies, the order of \(\omega^r\) and the associated descent data vary with \(p\). Consequently, (22) turns all-\(r\) zero counting into a character-aspect equidistribution/large-sieve problem. It does not turn it into a fixed modular-form zero problem or a lattice-point count.

This is why modularity is more naturally aligned with AFP through averaged Mellin cancellation than with an exact telescope. The central modular congruence controls one distinguished character; AFP requires simultaneous control of roughly \(p/2\) characters.

## 8. Candidate functions \(F\), ranked by mathematical plausibility

### Candidate 1: p-adic local time of \(q_r=b_r/c_r\) — the strongest recurrence-side candidate

Define a canonical p-adic lift \(\widetilde q_r\) and a residue-ball occupation functional
\[
L_w(\widetilde q_r)=1_{v_p(\widetilde q_r)>0}.
\]
Seek a non-Archimedean Tanaka formula
\[
L_w(\widetilde q_r)-\frac1{p+1}
=B_{r+1}-B_r+E_r,
\tag{23}
\]
where \(B_r\) is a Busemann/Prüfer-type potential and \(E_r\) is a crossing/local-time compensator. The Casoratian formula (10) is concrete input for controlling crossings. A theorem that \(\sum_rE_r\) has bounded average over primes would prove AFP without forcing \(Z_p\) to be uniformly bounded.

### Candidate 2: a time-dependent Steinberg Poisson solution — useful if accompanied by an averaged norm bound

Solve (15) after projecting away the cycle-invariant obstruction, or solve the averaged equation (17), and then prove that the deterministic order has small residual. The needed theorem is an \(L^1\) or \(L^2\) estimate such as
\[
\sum_{p\le X}
 \left|\sum_r E_{p,r}\right|
 \ll \pi(X),
\quad\text{or}\quad
\sum_{p\le X}
 \left|\sum_r E_{p,r}\right|^2
 \ll \pi(X).
\tag{24}
\]
This formulation is compatible with Poisson fluctuations.

### Candidate 3: a p-adic Schubert-height potential on the normalized frame \(G_r\)

Lift (12) to \(SL_2(\mathbf Q_p)\), and measure distance to the tube above \(g_{11}=0\). This is equivalent in spirit to Candidate 1 but retains the full two-dimensional state and may interact better with Frobenius and the Bruhat–Tits building. The missing input is again bounded backtracking/crossing, not the existence of the height.

### Candidates that do not work by themselves

* \(\log|b_r/b_{r-1}|\) or \(v_p(b_r/b_{r-1})\): signed displacement, not occupation.
* The second solution \(c_r\) as a factor \(b_r=\alpha_p^rc_r\): false.
* A uniformly bounded-degree rational \(H(r,x)\): ruled out by the delta-function degree obstruction.
* The endpoint involution alone: supplies reflection/cycle symmetry but no magnitude bound.
* A fixed classical modular form for all \(r\): the general values form a varying-character Mellin family instead.

## 9. Recommended reformulation of the missing theorem

The exact-coboundary statement should be replaced by one of the following two precise targets.

### Apéry local-time theorem

For a canonical p-adic lift of the normalized fundamental frame \(G_r\), prove a decomposition
\[
1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}-\frac1{p+1}
=\mathcal B_p(G_{r+1})-\mathcal B_p(G_r)+\mathcal E_{p,r},
\tag{25}
\]
with
\[
\frac1{\pi(X)}
\sum_{p\le X}
\left|\sum_{r=1}^{p-2}\mathcal E_{p,r}\right|=O(1).
\tag{26}
\]
A stronger \(o(1)\) centered form would yield the asymptotic mean.

### Steinberg approximate-coboundary theorem

Construct \(H_{p,r}\) in the Steinberg representation such that
\[
A_p=H_{p,r+1}\circ T_r-H_{p,r}+E_{p,r}
\tag{27}
\]
and prove the prime-averaged endpoint/residual estimate (24). The residual should encode the genuinely random/Poisson part; forcing it to vanish would be incompatible with the expected unbounded tail.

These targets preserve the useful “hidden law” intuition while matching the actual statistical conjecture.

---

## Bottom line

* The Apéry recurrence gives a precise zero scattering rule and, together with the second solution, an exact \(SL_2\) frame.
* The second solution is \(c=a/6\), and at every zero of \(b_r\), \(c_r\ne0\) and \(c_rb_{r-1}=r^{-3}\).
* The proposed relation \(b_r=\alpha_p^rc_r\) is false; fiberwise unit-root Frobenius and the character index \(r\) must not be conflated.
* A pure bounded coboundary for the centered indicator is both far stronger than AFP and structurally obstructed.
* The most promising recurrence-specific object is the p-adic local time of \(q_r=b_r/c_r\), or equivalently the Schubert-tube local time of the normalized frame \(G_r\), with a nonzero crossing residual.
* Modularity for the central quadratic character does not extend to one fixed modular form for all \(r\); the correct all-\(r\) object is the varying-character Mellin transform already present in the project.

So the convergent conclusion should be revised to:

> **The missing input for AFP is not an exact Apéry coboundary for the Steinberg observable. It is an Apéry-specific approximate-coboundary/local-time theorem whose residual has bounded prime average.**
