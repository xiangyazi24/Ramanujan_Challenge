# Signed cancellation for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\): what the variance heuristic really says

## Verdict

The random-cancellation heuristic is mathematically coherent and predicts a discrepancy much smaller than the \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) allowance. But the proposed deduction from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) does **not** work.

There are four separate points.

1. The quoted quantities \(T_3-X^2\lambda_X^3\) contain a built-in negative **diagonal bias**: \(X^2\lambda_X^3\) includes repeated prime labels, whereas \(T_3=\sum_m(K_X(m))_3\) uses three distinct primes. Thus the observed negative signs are not purely cancellation among distinct-triple CRT errors.
2. The scalar errors for the actual interval \([0,X^2)\) are deterministic. A variance appears only after introducing a probability space. The two natural probability spaces lead to different conclusions:
   - random shifts give an averaged/typical-shift theorem, not a bound at the actual shift;
   - random \(m<X^2\) leaves disjoint triples coupled through a six-prime short-prefix correlation.
3. \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) alone cannot imply the desired variance or \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). The anchored-rainbow-star construction already present in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` satisfies the natural second-moment bound, reflection, spacing, interval bounds, and bounded row codegrees, but violates \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) by a factor of order \(X\).
4. A fourth-factorial-moment interpolation does not rescue the argument. The exact overlap identity contains an unavoidable \(F_3\) self-overlap term, and rows with exactly three hits contribute to \(F_3\) while contributing zero to \(F_4\).

So the correct conclusion is:

> **Signed cancellation remains a plausible Apéry-specific route, but it must come from a new mixed-prime short-prefix equidistribution theorem (or an equivalent pair-Palm extension theorem). It cannot be obtained from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) by an abstract variance or fourth-moment argument.**

The repository already contains the right exact formulation: the finite-marginal pair-Palm positive excess in `hm3_result.tex`.

## 1. First correct the main term: \(X^2\lambda^3\) is not the distinct-triple expectation

Put
\[
 M=X^2,\qquad z_p=|\mathcal Z_p|,\qquad a_p=\frac{z_p}{p},
 \qquad \lambda=\sum_{X<p\le 2X}a_p.
\]
Let
\[
 A_2=\sum_pa_p^2,\qquad A_3=\sum_pa_p^3.
\]
With ordered, pairwise-distinct prime labels,
\[
 F_3:=T_3=\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_3
      =\sum_{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}J_{pqr},
\]
where
\[
 J_{pqr}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q\cap\Omega_r|,
 \qquad
 \Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\}.
\]

The correct complete-period Bernoulli baseline for this factorial moment is
\[
 B_3^{\circ}
 =M\sum_{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}a_pa_qa_r
 =6M e_3((a_p)_p).
\]
Newton's identity gives
\[
 \boxed{
 B_3^{\circ}
 =M\bigl(\lambda^3-3\lambda A_2+2A_3\bigr).}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 E_{pqr}=J_{pqr}-Ma_pa_qa_r,
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 F_3-M\lambda^3
 =\sum_{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}E_{pqr}
  -M\bigl(3\lambda A_2-2A_3\bigr).}
\]
The second term is always nonpositive in this display. Indeed,
\[
 3\lambda A_2-2A_3
 =\sum_pa_p^2(3\lambda-2a_p)
 \ge \sum_pa_p^3>0
\]
unless all zero sets are empty.

Therefore the empirical values
\[
 -4,\ -72,\ -59
\]
for \(F_3-M\lambda^3\) are **not** the signed sum of the distinct-triple CRT errors alone. They include an automatic negative correction caused by the repeated-prime monomials in \(\lambda^3\).

This exactly matches the generated data in `problems/3.2/hm3_exploration.md`. For example,
\[
 X=256:\quad F_3=60,\qquad M\lambda^3=131.989926\ldots,
\]
so the displayed difference is \(-71.989926\ldots\). At \(X=512\),
\[
 F_3=150,\qquad M\lambda^3=209.308570\ldots,
\]
so the difference is \(-59.308570\ldots\).

A cleaner diagnostic is therefore
\[
 D_3^{\circ}:=F_3-B_3^{\circ},
\]
not \(F_3-M\lambda^3\).

There is also an exact finite-marginal version. Set
\[
 \rho_p=\frac{|\Omega_p|}{M},\qquad \mu=\sum_p\rho_p.
\]
Then the independent finite-column baseline is
\[
 B_{3,\mathrm{fin}}^{\circ}
 =M\sum_{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}\rho_p\rho_q\rho_r
 =M\left(
   \mu^3-3\mu\sum_p\rho_p^2+2\sum_p\rho_p^3
 \right).
\]
This removes every one-column endpoint discrepancy before testing genuine cross-prime dependence.

Under the usual heuristic \(z_p=O(1)\), the diagonal deficit has scale about
\[
 M\lambda A_2\asymp \frac{X}{(\log X)^2},
\]
whereas the ideal random standard deviation below has scale
\[
 \frac{X}{(\log X)^{3/2}}.
\]
Thus the diagonal term should be asymptotically smaller than the random fluctuation, but it can visibly bias the small and medium numerical blocks. In any event, it must be removed before interpreting the sign.

Also, \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) concerns the one-sided upper bound
\[
 F_3\ll M\lambda^3X^{o(1)},
\]
not the two-sided approximation \(|F_3-M\lambda^3|\ll M\lambda^3\). At \(X=256\), the actual ratio is
\[
 \frac{F_3}{M\lambda^3}=0.45458\ldots,
\]
so the \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) inequality itself is not “barely” true there. The stronger two-sided Poisson approximation is what is being tested by the signed defect.

## 2. What the ideal random model predicts

After the distinct-label correction, the heuristic is strong.

For a fixed ordered prime triple \((p,q,r)\) and a fixed zero-residue tuple
\[
 (u,v,w)\in\mathcal Z_p\times\mathcal Z_q\times\mathcal Z_r,
\]
let \(x_{pqr}(u,v,w)\in[0,pqr)\) be its CRT representative. Since
\[
 pqr\asymp X^3\gg M=X^2,
\]
an ideal random-phase model treats
\[
 \mathbf 1_{x_{pqr}(u,v,w)<M}
\]
as a Bernoulli variable of mean
\[
 \frac{M}{pqr}\asymp\frac1X.
\]
Hence the variance contributed by that residue tuple is also \(\asymp1/X\). Summing over all distinct prime and zero-residue tuples gives the diagonal variance
\[
 \sum_{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}
 \frac{Mz_pz_qz_r}{pqr}
 \asymp B_3^{\circ}
 \asymp M\lambda^3.
\]
Thus the ideal prediction is
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{sd}(D_3^{\circ})
 \asymp \sqrt{M\lambda^3}
 =X\lambda^{3/2}.}
\]
When \(\lambda\asymp1/\log X\), this is
\[
 \frac{X}{(\log X)^{3/2}},
\]
which is far below
\[
 M\lambda^3\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\]
So **yes**: ideal random cancellation would prove much more than \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

The corrected heuristic is not “random signs of size \(O(z_pz_qz_r)\).” It is a sparse Bernoulli occupancy model: most residue tuples miss the short prefix, and the variance is their small landing probability \(M/(pqr)\).

The problem is not the heuristic scale. The problem is converting that random-phase calculation into a theorem for the one distinguished, arithmetic phase supplied by the Apéry zero sets.

## 3. The word “variance” hides three different objects

This is the central logical issue.

### 3.1 The actual prefix errors are deterministic

For the actual zero sets and the actual interval \([0,M)\),
\[
 E_{pqr}=J_{pqr}-Ma_pa_qa_r
\]
is a fixed real number. Therefore
\[
 \sum_{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}E_{pqr}
\]
is also fixed. It has no variance until an auxiliary randomization is introduced.

Accordingly, an inequality of the form
\[
 \left(\sum E_{pqr}\right)^2\le \operatorname{Var}\left(\sum E_{pqr}\right)
\]
is not a deterministic principle. For a random variable \(Y\), variance controls
\[
 \mathbb E|Y-\mathbb EY|^2,
\]
not the value of one prescribed realization.

### 3.2 Random shifts: disjoint triples do become independent, but the result is only typical-shift

A natural randomization is to shift the window. Let
\[
 Q=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_X}p
\]
and choose \(U\) uniformly modulo \(Q\). Define
\[
 K_U(m)=\sum_p
 \mathbf 1_{\mathcal Z_p}(m+U\bmod p),
\]
\[
 F_3(U)=\sum_{0\le m<M}(K_U(m))_3.
\]
CRT gives the exact shift average
\[
 \mathbb E_U F_3(U)=B_3^{\circ}.
\]
Moreover, a triple-window count supported on primes \(p,q,r\) depends only on \(U\bmod pqr\). Thus counts attached to disjoint prime triples are indeed independent under uniform \(U\).

Suppose one proves the ideal averaged estimate
\[
 \mathbb E_U|F_3(U)-B_3^{\circ}|^2
 \ll M\lambda^3X^{o(1)}.
\]
Chebyshev then says that almost all shifts satisfy
\[
 |F_3(U)-B_3^{\circ}|
 \ll \sqrt{M\lambda^3}\,X^{o(1)}.
\]
But the Apéry problem asks for \(U=0\). Nothing in the variance estimate forces that one shift to be typical.

The missing implication would be a derandomization statement such as
\[
 \boxed{
 |F_3(0)-B_3^{\circ}|^2
 \le X^{o(1)}
 \mathbb E_U|F_3(U)-B_3^{\circ}|^2.}
\]
No such inequality follows from CRT or \((\mathrm{HM})_2\). The anchored-star construction below is precisely a family for which the distinguished phase is catastrophically exceptional even though random shifts destroy the anchor.

Thus a random-shift variance theorem could be true and still fail to prove the desired result.

### 3.3 Random \(m<X^2\): the actual phase is retained, but disjoint triples are not independent

Alternatively choose \(m\) uniformly from the actual prefix and put, for a triple support \(A=\{p,q,r\}\),
\[
 Y_A(m)=\prod_{p\in A}\mathbf 1_{\Omega_p}(m).
\]
For two disjoint supports \(A,B\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{m<M}(Y_A,Y_B)
 =\frac{J_{A\cup B}}M
  -\frac{J_AJ_B}{M^2}.
\]
This is a genuine six-prime short-prefix correlation. It is generally not zero.

CRT gives zero covariance only when averaging over a complete period of all six primes,
\[
 P_{A\cup B}=\prod_{p\in A\cup B}p\asymp X^6.
\]
But the interval used in the problem has length
\[
 M=X^2,
\]
only an \(X^{-4}\)-fraction of that period. Complete-period independence gives no short-prefix discrepancy estimate.

Therefore:

- under random shifts, disjoint triples factor but the actual shift is uncontrolled;
- under the actual prefix, the desired value is retained but disjoint triples require six-prime information.

One cannot use the favorable part of each probability space simultaneously without proving a new arithmetic theorem.

## 4. The covariance bookkeeping is larger than stated

Let
\[
 N=\#\{p\in(X,2X]:z_p>0\},
\]
heuristically \(N\asymp X/\log X\).

There are \(\asymp N^3\) triple supports. Ordered pairs of triple supports have the following counts:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{number of shared primes}&\text{union size}&\text{number of pairs}\\
\hline
0&6&\asymp N^6\\
1&5&\asymp N^5\\
2&4&\asymp N^4\\
3&3&\asymp N^3.
\end{array}
\]

Thus the overlapping covariance terms are not merely \(O(N^3)\). There are \(O(N^5)\) pairs sharing one prime and \(O(N^4)\) pairs sharing two primes.

In an ideal independent-Bernoulli row model with \(a_p\asymp1/X\), their covariance sizes shrink correspondingly:

- same triple: size \(\asymp X^{-3}\), total \(\asymp\lambda^3\);
- two shared primes: size \(\asymp X^{-4}\), total \(\asymp\lambda^4\);
- one shared prime: size \(\asymp X^{-5}\), total \(\asymp\lambda^5\);
- disjoint triples: covariance zero.

After multiplying by \(M\), the diagonal \(M\lambda^3\) dominates. This recovers the random prediction.

But proving the \(X^{-4}\) and \(X^{-5}\) covariance sizes for the actual arithmetic family is exactly proving four- and five-prime product mixing. \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) supplies only an aggregate two-prime mass bound,
\[
 F_2=\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\ll M\lambda^2.
\]
It says nothing about whether those pair incidences are spatially dispersed or all concentrated on a small collection of rows, nor whether conditioning on a pair hit greatly increases the probability of additional prime hits.

So even in the random-shift model, where disjoint supports factor, the overlapping terms require new four- and five-prime information. In the actual-prefix model, the disjoint terms additionally require six-prime information.

This is not necessarily “circular because it is literally \((\mathrm{HM})_6\).” A specially structured mixed exponential-sum theorem might bound the covariance without proving the full positive sixth factorial moment. But it is unquestionably information beyond \((\mathrm{HM})_2\).

## 5. A theorem from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) alone is impossible: the anchored star

The decisive obstruction is already proved in

`problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex`, Proposition “An anchored rainbow star with bounded codegrees,”

and checked computationally in

`problems/3.2/pairpalm_verify.py`.

For almost every prime \(p\in(X,2X]\), choose a common integer
\[
 m_0=\left\lfloor\frac{3X^2}{4}\right\rfloor
\]
and set
\[
 \mathcal Z_p^*
 =\{m_0\bmod p,\ p-1-(m_0\bmod p)\}.
\]
After deleting only \(O(1)\) exceptional primes, these sets have all the following properties:

- size two;
- reflection invariance;
- no consecutive residues;
- avoidance of the endpoint residues used in the Apéry structural arguments;
- the required interval-count bound;
- a natural centered second moment;
- a uniform \(O(1)\) codegree bound between distinct rows.

Let \(L\asymp X/\log X\) be the number of active columns. Every active column contains the same row \(m_0\), so
\[
 K_X^*(m_0)=L.
\]
Consequently
\[
 F_2^*(X)\ge (L)_2\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2}
          \asymp M(\lambda_X^*)^2,
\]
and the construction also satisfies the matching natural upper bound. Thus it obeys \((\mathrm{HM})_2\).

But
\[
 F_3^*(X)\ge (L)_3
 \asymp\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3},
\]
whereas
\[
 M(\lambda_X^*)^3
 \asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac{F_3^*(X)}{M(\lambda_X^*)^3}\asymp X.
\]

This rules out every general implication of the form
\[
 (\mathrm{HM})_2
 \Longrightarrow
 |F_3-B_3^{\circ}|^2\ll M\lambda^3X^{o(1)},
\]
or even
\[
 (\mathrm{HM})_2\Longrightarrow(\mathrm{HM})_3,
\]
using only the primewise, reflection, spacing, interval, and pair-codegree information presently available.

The obstruction is conceptually simple:

> \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) controls the total number of pair incidences, but permits essentially all of that allowed pair mass to sit on one row. Adding a third choice at that row multiplies the mass by \(K_X(m_0)\asymp X/\log X\).

That is exactly the coherent alignment which the random model excludes and the actual proof must rule out by Apéry-specific arithmetic.

## 6. A fourth-moment interpolation does not fix the problem

The exact overlap audit in `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex` proves, for every \(K\),
\[
 (K)_k^2
 =(K)_2\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}
 \binom{k-2}{i}^2i!\,(K)_{2k-2-i}.
\]
For \(k=3\), this specializes to
\[
 \boxed{
 (K)_3^2=(K)_2\bigl((K)_4+(K)_3\bigr).}
\]
After Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \boxed{
 F_3^2\le F_2(F_4+F_3).}
\]
The \(F_3\) term is not an artifact. It represents the case in which the two ordered third-prime extensions are the same prime.

In particular, a row with \(K=3\) contributes
\[
 (K)_3=6,\qquad (K)_4=0.
\]
So no inequality
\[
 F_3^2\le F_2F_4
\]
can hold.

The actual Apéry computations make this obstruction concrete. `pairpalm_result.tex` records
\[
 X=128:\qquad F_3=60,\qquad F_4=0,
\]
and
\[
 X=1024:\qquad F_3=486,\qquad F_4=24.
\]
At the first scale, a fourth factorial moment sees none of the third-moment mass at all.

Even assuming an independence-scale bound for \(F_4\), the valid inequality gives
\[
 F_3^2\ll M\lambda^2(F_4+F_3),
\]
and the self-overlap term only yields an \(M\lambda^2\)-scale alternative, not the needed \(M\lambda^3\) scale when \(\lambda\to0\).

An ordinary fourth moment \(\sum K^4\) is no better as a deduction from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\): \(L^2\) information does not control spikes in \(L^4\), and the anchored star realizes the extremal spike. Moreover, for a sparse Poisson variable the ordinary centered moments are dominated by the one-hit diagonal scale \(\lambda\), while the factorial third moment is of scale \(\lambda^3\). Recovering the latter requires cancellation of lower-order diagonals, not merely absolute bounds for ordinary moments.

Thus the proposed “fourth-moment bound on \(T_3\) from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\)” is impossible in this generality.

## 7. What mechanism is actually suggested by the data?

After removing the repeated-prime bias, the plausible mechanism is:

> **The normalized CRT representatives associated with Apéry zero triples are approximately equidistributed in the short initial interval, with no coherent cross-prime phase alignment.**

This can be written exactly as a mixed Fourier problem.

For \(P=pqr\), define the centered short-interval function on \(\mathbb Z/P\mathbb Z\),
\[
 \Delta_{M,P}(x)=\mathbf1_{[0,M)}(x)-\frac MP.
\]
Then
\[
 E_{pqr}
 =\sum_{u\in\mathcal Z_p}
   \sum_{v\in\mathcal Z_q}
   \sum_{w\in\mathcal Z_r}
   \Delta_{M,pqr}(x_{pqr}(u,v,w)).
\]
Expanding \(\Delta_{M,P}\) in finite Fourier series gives schematically
\[
 E_{pqr}
 =\sum_{h\ne0\bmod P}
   \widehat\Delta_{M,P}(h)
   S_p(h\overline{qr})
   S_q(h\overline{pr})
   S_r(h\overline{pq}),
\]
where
\[
 S_p(t)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal Z_p}e^{2\pi itu/p}
\]
and the bars denote the appropriate CRT inverses.

The cancellation must therefore occur in a **mixed** sum over \(h,p,q,r\), with the inverse of the other two primes entering each local phase. Bounding each \(S_p(t)\) separately is insufficient. The anchored star has \(|S_p(t)|\le2\) for every active \(p\), yet its phases are coherently arranged so that all columns hit \(m_0\).

This agrees with the warning already recorded in `pairpalm_result.tex`: primewise Fourier control does not eliminate the star; one needs mixed cross-prime phase control.

The numerical evidence is consistent with this product-mixing picture:

- `hm3_exploration.md` has
  \[
  \frac{F_3}{M\lambda^3}
  =0.4546,0.7166,0.8331,0.9335,0.9487
  \]
  at \(X=256,512,1024,2048,4096\);
- at \(X=4096\), the observed conditional third-prime extension rate is
  \[
  0.073422,
  \]
  close to
  \[
  \lambda_X=0.076828;
  \]
- the contributing triples are spread across many rows rather than concentrated at one anchor.

These are exactly the diagnostics expected under approximate product mixing. They do not identify a theorem that follows from CRT alone.

A successful proof could plausibly come from a mixed large-sieve estimate, a trace-function/unit-root tensor estimate, or an Apéry transfer-matrix theorem that prevents simultaneous phase alignment. Such a theorem would be genuinely three-prime or higher arithmetic input.

## 8. The exact noncircular target is pair-Palm extension, not an abstract variance

The sharp formulation already proved in `hm3_result.tex` is the right one.

For ordered \(p\ne q\), define
\[
 J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|,
\]
\[
 T_{p,q}
 =\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}(K_X(m)-2),
\]
and with finite marginals
\[
 \mu_{p,q}=\sum_{\ell\notin\{p,q\}}\rho_\ell.
\]
Then the exact anchor identity is
\[
 \boxed{
 F_3=\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}.}
\]
Define the finite pair-Palm positive overload
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)
 =\sum_{p\ne q}
  \bigl(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}\bigr)_+.}
\]
The repository proves the sandwich
\[
 \mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)
 \le F_3
 \le \mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)+O(M\lambda^3).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 (\mathrm{HM})_3
 \iff
 \mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)
 \ll M\lambda^3X^{o(1)}.}
\]

This formulation says precisely what random cancellation must accomplish:

> When one conditions on an actual two-prime CRT hit, the aggregate number of additional prime hits must not exceed the global finite-marginal rate by more than \(O(M\lambda^3X^{o(1)})\).

It uses the already-controlled pair loci as anchors and asks only for the conditional **first** extension moment. It does not square the cubic statistic and therefore does not automatically manufacture a six-prime positive correlation sum.

Of course this is an exact characterization, not a proof. But it identifies the new arithmetic input without the false claim that it follows from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\).

A variance strategy can still be made into a legitimate research program, but it needs two separate lemmas:

1. an averaged random-shift variance estimate at the scale \(M\lambda^3X^{o(1)}\);
2. an Apéry-specific phase-nonconcentration/derandomization theorem showing that the distinguished shift \(U=0\) is not exceptional.

The second lemma is indispensable. The anchored star proves that it is false for general reflection-invariant sparse zero sets satisfying all current pairwise constraints.

## 9. Recommended next diagnostics

The current experiment should distinguish the genuine triple discrepancy from the Poisson diagonal bias. For each \(X\), record:

\[
 B_3^{\circ}
 =M(\lambda^3-3\lambda A_2+2A_3),
\]
\[
 D_3^{\circ}=F_3-B_3^{\circ},
\]
\[
 \text{diagonal bias}
 =B_3^{\circ}-M\lambda^3
 =-M(3\lambda A_2-2A_3),
\]
and the finite-marginal analogue using \(\rho_p\).

Then test the mechanism directly by recording:

- the distribution of
  \[
  \frac{T_{p,q}}{J_{p,q}}-\mu_{p,q}
  \]
  weighted by \(J_{p,q}\);
- how much of \(F_3\) comes from the largest few rows;
- the contribution by \(K_X(m)=3,4,5,\ldots\);
- the percentile rank of the actual phase among many random window shifts;
- covariance grouped by whether two triple supports share zero, one, two, or three primes.

The random-shift experiment would be useful evidence: if \(U=0\) looks typical at growing scales, it supports the mixed-equidistribution conjecture. But it should be labeled explicitly as a phase-typicality diagnostic, not as a proof derived from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\).

## Final answer to the concrete questions

### Is the small signed sum produced by random cancellation?

The data are consistent with random-like cancellation, but part of the observed negative sign is the deterministic repeated-prime deficit in \(M\lambda^3\). After correcting to the distinct-prime baseline, the remaining plausible mechanism is mixed short-prefix equidistribution of the CRT representatives.

### Does the ideal variance have the right scale?

Yes. The ideal model predicts
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(D_3^{\circ})\asymp M\lambda^3,
 \qquad
 |D_3^{\circ}|_{\mathrm{rms}}\asymp\sqrt{M\lambda^3},
\]
which is far smaller than the \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) allowance.

### Can that variance bound be proved from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\)?

No. A deterministic implication is refuted by the anchored-star construction. A random-shift variance controls almost every shift but not the actual shift. A variance over the actual prefix has six-prime covariance even for disjoint triples.

### Do shared-prime covariances reduce to \((\mathrm{HM})_2\)?

No. There are \(\asymp N^5\) pairs of triples sharing one prime and \(\asymp N^4\) sharing two. Their ideal smallness is a four-/five-prime product-mixing assertion, not a consequence of the aggregate pair count.

### Can a fourth-moment bound on \(T_3\) be obtained from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\)?

No. The exact identity is
\[
 F_3^2\le F_2(F_4+F_3),
\]
not \(F_3^2\le F_2F_4\). Rows with exactly three hits make \(F_3>0\) and \(F_4=0\), and the anchored star gives the asymptotic obstruction.

### What is the strongest viable formulation of the “last hope”?

Prove an Apéry-specific mixed-prime phase theorem strong enough to establish
\[
 \mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\]
Equivalently, prove that actual two-prime CRT loci do not have an aggregate positive overload of third-prime Apéry zeros. This is genuinely new arithmetic input, but it is a three-prime conditional target rather than a circular demand for the full sixth moment.
