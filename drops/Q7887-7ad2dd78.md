# Adelic strong approximation for the Apéry shift: what is true, what is false, and the theorem actually needed for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

## Executive verdict

There are three different statements here, and they must not be conflated.

1. **Adelic generation.** A fixed finitely generated subgroup attached to the Apéry transfer matrices has full reduction
   \[
   \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z/q\mathbf Z)
   \simeq \prod_{p\mid q}\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)
   \]
   for every squarefree \(q\) outside a finite exceptional set. This is a genuine Matthews--Vaserstein--Weisfeiler/Weisfeiler strong-approximation theorem, and one can prove its Zariski-density hypothesis directly from the first two Apéry transfer matrices.

2. **Equidistribution of the prescribed prefix word in the product group.** This is not a consequence of strong approximation, Nori, or expansion. In the literal group-valued formulation it is actually impossible: a prefix of length \(q\) has at most \(q\) points, whereas
   \[
   \left|\prod_{p\mid q}\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\right|\asymp q^3.
   \]
   Its total-variation distance from Haar measure tends to \(1\), not \(0\).

3. **The statement relevant to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).** One needs short-prefix discrepancy for the synchronized **projective** orbit, or equivalently an incomplete-CRT discrepancy estimate for the zero sets, on an interval of length
   \[
   M=X^2\asymp (p_1p_2p_3)^{2/3}.
   \]
   Over a complete period modulo \(p_1p_2p_3\), independence is already an exact consequence of the Chinese remainder theorem; no monodromy theorem is needed. The open problem is the location of those CRT representatives inside the initial segment \([0,X^2)\).

The logical situation is therefore

```text
G_diff = SL_2
        |
        | supplies irreducibility/non-solvability and useful monodromy input
        v
Zariski-dense fixed arithmetic subgroup
        |
        | MVW / Weisfeiler / Nori / super-approximation
        v
full congruence quotients + expansion for RANDOM generator choices
        |
        |  DOES NOT control one prescribed non-autonomous prefix word
        X
short synchronized projective-orbit discrepancy at length Q^(2/3)
        |
        v
(HM)_3 and Poisson-scale triple-prime rarity
```

Thus the answer to the main question is:

> **Yes**, there is an honest adelic strong-approximation theorem for a subgroup generated by Apéry transfer matrices. **No**, it does not imply that the Apéry prefix orbit is equidistributed, does not by itself imply \(Z(p)=O(1)\), and does not prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). The missing theorem is a deterministic-word/incomplete-CRT discrepancy estimate, not a congruence-image theorem.

The rest of this note makes each assertion precise.

## 1. A determinant-one Apéry transfer system

Write
\[
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5
\]
and
\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\]
Define
\[
v_n=\binom{b_n}{n^3b_{n-1}}.
\]
Then
\[
v_{n+1}=A_n v_n,
\qquad
A_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{P(n)}{(n+1)^3}&-\dfrac1{(n+1)^3}\\[5pt]
(n+1)^3&0
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{1.1}
\]
and
\[
\det A_n=1.
\]
This is the natural determinant-coboundary gauge corresponding to the given \(G_{\mathrm{diff}}=\mathrm{SL}_2\) result.

For a prime \(p\), the matrices \(A_n\bmod p\) are invertible for
\[
0\le n\le p-2.
\]
Starting from \(v_0=(1,0)^t\), the local projective orbit is
\[
\pi_p(r)=[v_r]\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p),
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]
The zero event is the hit of the fixed target
\[
p\mid b_r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\pi_p(r)=[0:1].
\tag{1.2}
\]
The vector cannot vanish because every intervening transfer matrix is invertible.

Two initial matrices are
\[
A_0=
\begin{pmatrix}5&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_1=
\begin{pmatrix}117/8&-1/8\\8&0\end{pmatrix}
\in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z[1/2]).
\tag{1.3}
\]
These two matrices already suffice for a genuine strong-approximation theorem.

## 2. A concrete strong-approximation theorem that really does hold

### Theorem 2.1 (adelic generation by two Apéry transfer matrices)

Let
\[
\Gamma=\langle A_0,A_1\rangle
\subset \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z[1/2]),
\]
with \(A_0,A_1\) as in (1.3). Then \(\Gamma\) is Zariski dense in \(\mathrm{SL}_2\). Consequently there is an integer \(q_0\ge1\), divisible by \(2\), such that for every squarefree \(q\) with \((q,q_0)=1\),
\[
\pi_q(\Gamma)=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z/q\mathbf Z)
\simeq\prod_{p\mid q}\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Moreover, for the fixed symmetric generating set
\[
\Omega=\{A_0^{\pm1},A_1^{\pm1}\},
\]
the Cayley graphs
\[
\operatorname{Cay}\bigl(\pi_q(\Gamma),\pi_q(\Omega)\bigr)
\]
form an expander family as \(q\) ranges over squarefree integers prime to a possibly enlarged \(q_0\).

The theorem does not identify the finite exceptional set effectively; doing that would require a separate finite computation or an effective strong-approximation argument. The existence statement is enough to settle the abstract adelic-image question.

### Proof of Zariski density

The matrix \(A_0\) has trace \(5\), hence infinite order. Thus \(\Gamma\) is not finite.

Use the affine projective coordinate \(z=x/y\). The fixed lines of \(A_0\) and \(A_1\) satisfy respectively
\[
z^2-5z+1=0,
\tag{2.2}
\]
\[
64z^2-117z+1=0.
\tag{2.3}
\]
A common root would satisfy, after subtracting \(64\) times (2.2) from (2.3),
\[
203z-63=0,
\qquad z=\frac{63}{203}.
\]
But substitution in (2.2) gives numerator
\[
63^2-5\cdot63\cdot203+203^2=-18767\ne0.
\]
Hence \(A_0\) and \(A_1\) have no common eigenline over \(\overline{\mathbf Q}\).

Every proper algebraic subgroup of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) over an algebraically closed field is finite, contained in a Borel, or contained in the normalizer of a torus. The first case is excluded by the infinite order of \(A_0\). A subgroup contained in a Borel has a common invariant line, excluded above. Finally, an element of \(N(T)\setminus T\) has trace zero. Both \(A_0\) and \(A_1\) have nonzero trace, so if they lay in a common torus normalizer they would both lie in the torus and would share its eigenlines, again a contradiction. Therefore
\[
\overline\Gamma^{\,\mathrm{Zar}}=\mathrm{SL}_2.
\]

Since \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) is connected, simply connected, and absolutely almost simple, the strong-approximation theorems of Matthews--Vaserstein--Weisfeiler and Weisfeiler give (2.1) after deleting finitely many primes. Salehi Golsefidy--Varjú then gives squarefree-modulus expansion because the connected Zariski closure is perfect.

### What this theorem says about all Apéry letters

For every good prime \(p\), the subgroup generated by all defined Apéry letters \(A_n\bmod p\) contains the subgroup generated by \(A_0,A_1\), and hence is all of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\). Likewise at a good squarefree modulus, wherever the letters are simultaneously defined, the generated subgroup contains the full product quotient in (2.1).

This is a strong and useful structural theorem. It is nevertheless a theorem about **words one is allowed to choose**, not about the one chronological word dictated by the recurrence.

## 3. Why \(G_{\mathrm{diff}}=\mathrm{SL}_2\) does not by itself imply (2.1)

The proved difference Galois group is important, but it is a different object from an arithmetic subgroup of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Q)\).

The difference Galois group describes algebraic relations among solutions over the difference field \(\mathbf Q(n)\), and is invariant under rational gauge transformations
\[
A(n)\longmapsto B(n+1)A(n)B(n)^{-1}.
\]
By contrast, a strong-approximation theorem starts with one fixed, finitely generated subgroup
\[
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Q)
\]
and studies its reductions. Integer specializations of a rational cocycle are not gauge invariant in this sense, and a Zariski-dense difference Galois group does not automatically say that any prescribed finite set of specialized matrices is Zariski dense.

Thus the implication
\[
G_{\mathrm{diff}}=\mathrm{SL}_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{strong approximation for a specialized subgroup}
\]
is not a formal theorem. In the Apéry case, however, the direct calculation with \(A_0,A_1\) supplies the missing arithmetic subgroup and proves its Zariski density. The new \(G_{\mathrm{diff}}=\mathrm{SL}_2\) theorem is best viewed as a powerful global non-degeneracy result, while Theorem 2.1 is the separate arithmetic strong-approximation input.

In particular, Nori's theorem can be used to recognize large subgroups of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf F_p)\) after suitable unipotent-generation hypotheses are verified, but it does not convert a difference Galois group directly into a statement about one orbit or one ordered word.

## 4. Answer to (A): what Nori/MVW/Weisfeiler do and do not apply to

### They do apply to the generated subgroup

The standard strong-approximation input has the form
\[
\Gamma=\langle S\rangle\subset G(\mathbf Q),
\qquad
\overline\Gamma^{\mathrm{Zar}}=G,
\]
where \(S\) is a fixed finite set. For simply connected semisimple \(G\), the closure of \(\Gamma\) in the prime-to-\(q_0\) adelic group is open, and after enlarging \(q_0\) the reductions modulo squarefree \(q\) are the expected products.

This applies to \(\Gamma=\langle A_0,A_1\rangle\). It proves that the Apéry system has no congruence-image obstruction outside finitely many primes.

### They do not apply to a non-autonomous prefix as an equidistribution theorem

Put
\[
g_r=A_{r-1}A_{r-2}\cdots A_0.
\]
The chronological orbit is the deterministic list
\[
g_0,g_1,\ldots,g_R.
\]
Strong approximation says that arbitrary words in \(A_0^{\pm1},A_1^{\pm1}\) fill the congruence quotient. It says nothing about how frequently the very special prefixes \(g_r\) visit subsets.

Super-approximation similarly concerns the averaging operator
\[
T_\Omega=\frac1{|\Omega|}\sum_{s\in\Omega}\rho(s)
\]
on nontrivial representations. Its spectral gap describes the random walk that chooses a generator from \(\Omega\) at each step. The non-autonomous Apéry evolution instead uses the singleton measures
\[
\delta_{A_0},\delta_{A_1},\delta_{A_2},\ldots,
\]
whose convolution is still a point mass:
\[
\delta_{A_{r-1}}*\cdots*\delta_{A_0}=\delta_{g_r}.
\]
Each individual action \(\rho(A_n)\) is unitary (or a permutation matrix on a finite orbit), with operator norm \(1\). There is no averaging and therefore no spectral contraction to inherit from an expander theorem.

A trivial counterexample illustrates the logical gap: in any expander Cayley graph, the deterministic word
\[
s,s^{-1},s,s^{-1},\ldots
\]
visits at most two vertices, even though the generators span the whole group and the associated random walk mixes rapidly.

The additional theorem one would need is a deterministic matrix-coefficient estimate such as
\[
\left\|
\frac1R\sum_{r<R}\rho(g_r)
\right\|\ll q^{-\delta}
\tag{4.1}
\]
for the relevant nontrivial representations \(\rho\), uniformly in squarefree \(q\) and in the required range of \(R\). Neither strong approximation nor a Cayley spectral gap gives (4.1).

For the zero problem it would be enough to control the spherical/permutation representations arising from the action on products of projective lines, rather than all irreducible representations of the full group. But even this restricted estimate is a new deterministic-word theorem.

## 5. Answer to (B): the Apéry prefix is not presently an “essentially random walk”

There are three separate obstructions.

### 5.1 A cardinality obstruction in the full group

For squarefree \(q\),
\[
G_q=\prod_{p\mid q}\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)
\]
has size
\[
|G_q|
=\prod_{p\mid q}p(p^2-1)
=q^3\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-2})
\asymp q^3.
\tag{5.1}
\]
The empirical measure of the first \(q\) prefixes,
\[
\mu_q=\frac1q\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}\delta_{g_r},
\]
has support of size at most \(q\). If \(u_q\) is uniform measure on \(G_q\), then
\[
\|\mu_q-u_q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\ge 1-\frac{q}{|G_q|}
=1-O(q^{-2}).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Therefore the prefix image cannot be close to the product group in total variation, in relative cardinality, or against arbitrary subsets.

The correct finite homogeneous space is instead
\[
X_q=\prod_{p\mid q}\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p),
\qquad
|X_q|=\prod_{p\mid q}(p+1)\asymp q.
\tag{5.3}
\]
A length-\(q\) orbit could in principle equidistribute on \(X_q\). Strong approximation guarantees transitivity of the product group action, but not equidistribution of the prescribed orbit.

### 5.2 The transfer cocycle has singular times modulo every prime

In the determinant-one gauge (1.1), \(A_n\bmod p\) is undefined when
\[
n\equiv-1\pmod p.
\]
The local orbit from \(0\) through \(p-1\) is well-defined because it uses only \(A_0,\ldots,A_{p-2}\). But a literal word of length \(q\) modulo a composite squarefree \(q\) crosses a singular time for every prime divisor of \(q\). In the ungauged integral recurrence the same place appears as a noninvertible step rather than a pole.

Thus the expression
\[
A(1)A(2)\cdots A(r)\pmod q,
\qquad 1\le r\le q,
\]
is not, without an additional desingularization or wrap map, an everywhere-defined orbit in \(\prod_{p\mid q}\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\). The scalar sequence \(b_r\bmod p\) remains defined, and the local maps \(r\mapsto\pi_p(r)\) remain defined on one residue period, but these should be synchronized by CRT rather than represented as one globally invertible matrix cocycle through all singular times.

### 5.3 Determinism is the entire difficulty

The matrices vary rationally with time and the word is fixed. A difference-Galois obstruction to a Riccati solution rules out an algebraic invariant line over \(\mathbf Q(n)\), but it does not provide quantitative cancellation modulo \(p\) along the first \(p\) times. Likewise, unequal exponential roots \(\alpha,\beta\) rule out several solvable or isotrivial scenarios but do not imply finite-field discrepancy.

A useful deterministic starting point is van der Corput on the prefix sequence. Its correlations involve
\[
g_{r+h}g_r^{-1}
=A_{r+h-1}\cdots A_r.
\tag{5.4}
\]
The gap-polynomial identities and low-gap nonvanishing machinery already developed in the repository are precisely concrete information about such block correlations. To get mixing, however, one would need cancellation for a growing range of \(h\), not merely algebraic nonvanishing for each fixed short block.

### Random-walk counterfactual

If the letters really were independently selected from a fixed lazy expander generating set, then after \(O(\log q)\) steps the state on \(X_q\) would be close to uniform. A fixed projective target at one prime has stationary probability \(1/(p+1)\), so a run of length \(p\) would make about one hit. At three primes \(p_i\asymp X\), a run of length \(M=X^2\) would make about
\[
\frac{M}{(p_1+1)(p_2+1)(p_3+1)}\asymp\frac1X
\]
triple hits for each ordered prime triple. Summing over about \((X/\log X)^3\) ordered triples gives the desired scale
\[
\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\]
This validates the Poisson heuristic. It is not a proof that the deterministic Apéry word behaves like that random walk.

## 6. Full-period adelic independence is already exact CRT

For every prime \(p\), let
\[
\mathcal Z_p=\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\},
\qquad Z(p)=|\mathcal Z_p|.
\]
For squarefree
\[
Q=\prod_{i=1}^k p_i,
\]
the Chinese remainder theorem gives the exact identity
\[
\#\{m\bmod Q:m\bmod p_i\in\mathcal Z_{p_i}\ \forall i\}
=\prod_{i=1}^k Z(p_i).
\tag{6.1}
\]
No monodromy, strong approximation, or randomness enters (6.1).

Equivalently, define the synchronized local projective orbit
\[
\Pi_Q(m)
=\bigl(\pi_{p_i}(m\bmod p_i)\bigr)_{i=1}^k
\in\prod_i\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_{p_i}).
\tag{6.2}
\]
Over a complete period \(m\bmod Q\), the coordinates in (6.2) are exactly independent with respect to their **local empirical distributions**. If each local projective orbit were itself close to uniform, this would also be close to uniform on the product projective space.

Therefore an “adelic equidistribution over a complete modulus” is not the missing bridge. The issue is an incomplete interval.

For \((\mathrm{HM})_3\), take distinct primes
\[
p_1,p_2,p_3\in(X,2X]
\]
and
\[
Q=p_1p_2p_3\asymp X^3,
\qquad M=X^2=Q^{2/3+o(1)}.
\]
The required count is
\[
N_{p_1p_2p_3}(M)
=\#\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p_i\in\mathcal Z_{p_i}\ \forall i\}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Each triple of local zero residues has exactly one CRT representative modulo \(Q\); the question is whether that representative lies in the first proportion
\[
\frac{M}{Q}\asymp\frac1X
\]
of the period. The anchored-rainbow construction in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` shows that reflection symmetry, interval sparsity, natural second moments, bounded row codegrees, and the existing local fiber bounds do not prevent many residue triples from sharing an early representative. This is exactly why a genuine cross-prime discrepancy theorem is needed.

## 7. Fourier form of the missing incomplete-CRT estimate

Let
\[
Q=\prod_{i=1}^k p_i,
\qquad Q_i=Q/p_i,
\qquad \overline Q_iQ_i\equiv1\pmod{p_i}.
\]
For a local zero set define
\[
F_{p_i}(a)=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{p_i}}e_{p_i}(-az),
\]
and define the interval transform
\[
S_M(h;Q)=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}e_Q(hm).
\]
Fourier expansion and CRT give the exact identity
\[
N_Q(M)
=\frac1Q\sum_{h\bmod Q}
S_M(h;Q)
\prod_{i=1}^k
F_{p_i}(h\overline Q_i).
\tag{7.1}
\]
The zero frequency is
\[
\frac{M}{Q}\prod_i Z(p_i).
\tag{7.2}
\]
Everything difficult is the nonzero-frequency contribution
\[
\frac1Q\sum_{0\ne h\bmod Q}
S_M(h;Q)
\prod_iF_{p_i}(h\overline Q_i).
\tag{7.3}
\]

For \(k=3\), \(p_i\asymp X\), and conjecturally \(Z(p_i)=O(1)\), the main term (7.2) is only \(O(1/X)\) per prime triple. Since \(N_Q(M)\) is an integer, a uniform relative asymptotic for every triple is impossible and is much stronger than needed. The correct target must aggregate the positive excess over many prime triples. This matches the pair--Palm characterization already proved in `hm3_result.tex`.

## 8. The correct “adelic” theorem for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

Put
\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p:X<p\le2X\},
\qquad M=X^2,
\]
\[
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(p)}p,
\qquad
K_X(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
\mathbf 1_{m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p}.
\]
For ordered distinct \(p,q,r\in\mathcal P_X\), let \(N_{pqr}(M)\) be (6.3), and define the positive incomplete-CRT excess
\[
\mathcal E_3(X)
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q,r\in\mathcal P_X\\
                  p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}}
\left(
N_{pqr}(M)-\frac{MZ(p)Z(q)Z(r)}{pqr}
\right)_+.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Proposition 8.1 (thin-CRT discrepancy implies \((\mathrm{HM})_3\))

If
\[
\mathcal E_3(X)
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3,
\tag{8.2}
\]
then
\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_3
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\tag{8.3}
\]
Thus (8.2) is a sufficient adelic discrepancy theorem for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). It is equivalent in scale and content to controlling the positive pair--Palm overload isolated in `hm3_result.tex`.

### Proof

Expanding the ordered factorial moment gives
\[
\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_3
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q,r\in\mathcal P_X\\
                  p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}}
N_{pqr}(M).
\]
For each triple, \(A\le B+(A-B)_+\), so
\[
\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_3
\le
M\sum_{p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}
\frac{Z(p)Z(q)Z(r)}{pqr}
+\mathcal E_3(X).
\]
The first term is at most \(M\lambda_X^3\). Equation (8.2) proves (8.3).

### Why this is the right replacement for “orbit fills the product group”

The target (8.2) asks only for the statistic needed by the proof:

* the synchronized projective orbit, not the entire matrix group;
* the interval \([0,X^2)\), not a complete period of length \(pqr\);
* an aggregate one-sided excess, not an impossible relative asymptotic for every prime triple;
* the actual local marginals \(Z(p)/p\), so local reflection and other deterministic biases are retained rather than incorrectly erased.

This is the theorem that a product-monodromy/sheaf argument would have to prove.

## 9. Answer to (C): consequences for \(Z(p)\) and \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

### 9.1 Strong approximation alone gives no nontrivial \(Z(p)\) bound

Theorem 2.1 gives reachability by arbitrary words in a fixed generating set. It does not bound the number of times the chronological orbit hits \([0:1]\). Even on a full expander, a prescribed deterministic word can repeatedly avoid a target or repeatedly revisit it.

Therefore the implication
\[
\text{full congruence image}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Z(p)=O(1)
\]
is false as a general group-theoretic principle. For the Apéry sequence, strong approximation by itself yields no improvement over bounds proved by separate recurrence/gap-polynomial arguments, and it yields no new \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) estimate.

### 9.2 What a local projective-discrepancy theorem would imply

Define
\[
D_p=\max_{x\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)}
\left|
\#\{0\le r<p:\pi_p(r)=x\}
-\frac{p}{p+1}
\right|.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Then for the zero target,
\[
Z(p)=\frac{p}{p+1}+O(D_p),
\qquad
Z(p)\le1+D_p.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Consequently:

* \(D_p=O(1)\) would prove \(Z(p)=O(1)\), the expected rare-zero scale.
* A mere square-root discrepancy \(D_p\ll p^{1/2}\) gives only \(Z(p)\ll p^{1/2}\), much too weak for the Poisson prediction.
* An upper-mixing statement
  \[
  \#\{r<p:\pi_p(r)=x\}\le C\frac p{p+1}
  \]
  uniformly in \(x\) is already enough to give \(Z(p)\le C\); a two-sided asymptotic is not necessary.

The reflection identity for the Apéry orbit means that local hits need not look like independent Bernoulli trials in the time variable; they can occur in reflected pairs. This does not prevent a Poisson law for the number of **different primes** hitting a fixed \(m\), but it means one should preserve \(\lambda_X\) rather than force a universal local constant prematurely.

### 9.3 Quantitative factorial-moment consequences

Suppose generally that
\[
Z(p)\ll p^\theta
\qquad (X<p\le2X).
\]
Then
\[
\lambda_X
\ll \frac{X^\theta}{\log X}.
\tag{9.3}
\]
Combining (9.3) with \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) gives
\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_3
\ll
\frac{X^{2+3\theta+o(1)}}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{9.4}
\]
In particular, the expected local bound \(Z(p)=O(1)\) gives
\[
\lambda_X\ll\frac1{\log X},
\tag{9.5}
\]
\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_3
\ll
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{9.6}
\]
Since \((K)_3\ge6\) whenever \(K\ge3\), this also gives
\[
\#\{m<X^2:K_X(m)\ge3\}
\ll
\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{9.7}
\]

In an ideal uniform model with one effective zero state per prime,
\[
\lambda_X
\sim\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac1p
\sim\frac{\log2}{\log X}.
\tag{9.8}
\]
Poisson behavior would therefore predict the sharper asymptotic
\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_3
\sim
X^2\lambda_X^3
\sim
\frac{(\log2)^3X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{9.9}
\]
The repository only needs an upper bound of the scale (9.6), not the full asymptotic (9.9).

### 9.4 Two independent hypotheses are needed

It is useful to state the logical decomposition explicitly:

1. **Local anti-concentration:** \(Z(p)=O(1)\), or a suitable averaged substitute.
2. **Cross-prime thin-CRT discrepancy:** (8.2), or the equivalent pair--Palm positive-overload bound.

The first controls \(\lambda_X\); the second controls the third factorial moment relative to \(\lambda_X^3\). Neither follows from subgroup surjectivity alone.

## 10. What the K3, \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\), and Mellin input really contributes

The new inputs are substantial, but four monodromy notions must be kept separate.

1. The difference Galois group \(G_{\mathrm{diff}}=\mathrm{SL}_2\) of the recurrence in \(r\).
2. The geometric monodromy of the rank-two elliptic system \(E\), and the induced \(\mathrm{SO}_3\)-type monodromy of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)\simeq V^{\mathrm{prim}}\).
3. The finite-field Mellin cohomology \(C_{p,r}\), of open-locus dimension \(6\) or middle-extension dimension \(2\), with trace congruent to \(-b_r\bmod p\).
4. A fixed arithmetic subgroup of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Q)\) and its congruence quotients.

Large monodromy in any one category does not automatically give deterministic prefix discrepancy in another.

### What the geometric input does give

The files `toric_fiber_k3.tex`, `toric_fiber_sym2.tex`, and `toric_mellin_square.tex` provide exactly the ingredients one would want at the start of a sheaf-theoretic attack:

* fixed rank and bounded geometric complexity;
* purity and Deligne-type complete-sum bounds;
* a rank-two source with connected \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) monodromy;
* a primitive K3 system identified as a symmetric square/Asai object;
* a plausible route to product-monodromy independence through Goursat--Kolchin--Ribet arguments.

The difference-Galois theorem additionally rules out a rational Riccati reduction and other solvable explanations for concentration.

### What it does not yet give

The zero indicator is not the trace itself. Over \(\mathbf F_p\),
\[
\mathbf 1_{b_r=0}
=\frac1p\sum_{s\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(sb_r).
\tag{10.1}
\]
To exploit (10.1), one needs uniform control of the two-parameter family
\[
(r,s)\longmapsto e_p(sb_r),
\]
or an equivalent Fourier transform of \(\mathcal Z_p\). The statement that \(b_r\) is the trace of a bounded-rank cohomology group does not by itself make the nonlinear exponential \(e_p(sb_r)\) a bounded-conductor trace function in \(r\).

There is also a defining-prime problem. Standard compatible-system large sieves fix a finite-field characteristic \(p\) and reduce coefficient systems at auxiliary primes \(\ell\ne p\). The event here is divisibility at the same rational prime that is the base characteristic. Setting the auxiliary prime equal to \(p\) is not an allowed specialization of those étale large-sieve theorems.

The current literature audit gives the following verdicts as of 12 August 2026:

* Forey--Fresán--Kowalski give a convolution-Tannakian and equidistribution framework for arithmetic Fourier transforms.
* Bah--Shuddhodan now give varying-characteristic equidistribution for bounded-complexity perverse sheaves with common Tannakian group. This is an important advance, but it averages over essentially all multiplicative characters in each field. A single deterministically selected character has mass \(1/(p-1)\) and can remain exceptional for every \(p\) without affecting that theorem.
* Perret-Gentil's compatible-system large sieve uses auxiliary coefficient primes different from the base characteristic and therefore does not detect the same-prime congruence \(p\mid b_r\).
* Kedlaya's crystalline companions provide a same-characteristic coefficient object at each fixed \(p\), but do not couple these objects across different rational primes or produce the incomplete-CRT estimate (8.2).
* Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel cancellation for sums of products becomes highly relevant only after the local zero/Fourier functions are realized as bounded-conductor trace functions and the required shift/product independence has been proved.

This agrees with the warning already present in `toric_fiber_sym2.tex`: the exact \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\) formula does not by itself prove Mellin anti-concentration in the parameter.

## 11. A realistic theorem-level research program

The shortest plausible route from the new \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) results to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) is not “invoke strong approximation.” It is the following DAG.

### Step 1: use the synchronized local orbit, not a fictitious global \(q\)-periodic matrix cocycle

Work with
\[
\Pi_Q(m)=\bigl(\pi_p(m\bmod p)\bigr)_{p\mid Q}.
\]
This automatically handles the singular time \(n=-1\bmod p\) separately at each prime and preserves the exact CRT structure.

### Step 2: prove a local upper-mixing theorem

A sufficient target is
\[
\max_{x\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)}
\#\{r<p:\pi_p(r)=x\}=O(1),
\tag{11.1}
\]
or an averaged version strong enough to give
\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{Z(p)}p\ll\frac{X^{o(1)}}{\log X}.
\]
The difference-Galois \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) theorem is a necessary non-degeneracy input, but (11.1) still needs finite-field cancellation.

### Step 3: construct the relevant Fourier sheaf or prove direct exponential-sum bounds

One needs control of
\[
F_p(a)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal Z_p}e_p(-ar),
\tag{11.2}
\]
not only of \(b_r\) itself. A sheaf realization should have conductor bounded independently of \(p\), with all geometrically trivial, additive, Kummer, and reflected diagonal constituents classified explicitly.

The \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)\) description and the rank-six/rank-two Mellin cohomology are the right inputs for this classification, but the passage from trace to zero indicator is an additional construction.

### Step 4: prove product independence for two and three prime factors

For the complete sums arising from (7.3), establish Goursat--Kolchin--Ribet independence for the relevant external tensor products. The known reflection
\[
r\longmapsto p-1-r
\]
and any geometric self-twists must be factored out rather than incorrectly treated as generic independence.

Here the connected \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) monodromy of \(E\), the \(\mathrm{SO}_3\) symmetric square, and the no-Riccati difference-Galois result can do real work: they should rule out many diagonal identifications. They still do not supply the incomplete interval estimate automatically.

### Step 5: pass from complete sums to the \(Q^{2/3}\)-length interval

Insert the complete-sum bounds into (7.1), use completion/van der Corput/bilinear forms or an appropriate large sieve, and sum over prime triples. Because the expected main term per triple is below \(1\), optimize for the aggregate positive excess (8.1), not for a pointwise asymptotic.

A natural terminal theorem is precisely
\[
\mathcal E_3(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\]
The pair--Palm formulation in `hm3_result.tex` may be more efficient than attacking all triple Fourier sums symmetrically: condition on the unique two-prime CRT point and bound the positive overload of third primes there.

### Step 6: a purely group-theoretic alternative would still need a new deterministic theorem

If one insists on the group language, the target is a bound like
\[
\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal R_q}
\left\|
\sum_{m<M}\rho(\Pi_Q(m))
\right\|
\ll Mq^{-\delta},
\tag{11.3}
\]
for the nontrivial spherical representations \(\mathcal R_q\) controlling the target point in \(X_q\). Proving (11.3) would amount to a deterministic-word analogue of expander mixing for this rational non-autonomous cocycle. It is not contained in Nori, MVW, Bourgain--Gamburd, or Salehi Golsefidy--Varjú.

The repository's gap-polynomial and separated-chain estimates can be interpreted as initial low-order correlation bounds toward (11.3). The missing leap is uniform cancellation over a sufficiently long range of shifts and across three varying primes.

## 12. Direct answers to the three questions

### (A) Does strong approximation for \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) apply to the non-autonomous orbit?

**To the subgroup generated by suitable Apéry transfer matrices: yes.** The explicit subgroup \(\Gamma=\langle A_0,A_1\rangle\) is Zariski dense, so its good squarefree reductions are the full product groups, and its fixed-generator Cayley graphs are expanders.

**To the chronological non-autonomous orbit as an equidistribution theorem: no.** Strong approximation is a generation/surjectivity theorem. Nori is a finite-subgroup structure theorem. Super-approximation is an averaging/random-walk theorem. None estimates one prescribed prefix sequence.

### (B) Is \(A(1)\cdots A(r)\bmod q\) essentially a random walk?

Not on the basis of current results. In the full group, length \(q\) is far too short even to support a measure close to uniform. The literal product also crosses prime-dependent singular times. After passing to the correct synchronized projective orbit, random-walk behavior is a compelling heuristic and predicts exactly the desired scale, but proving it requires a new deterministic discrepancy theorem such as (4.1), (8.2), or (11.3).

### (C) If strong approximation holds, what are the implied \(Z(p)\) and \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) bounds?

**From strong approximation alone:** no nontrivial \(Z(p)\) bound and no \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) bound.

**With local projective upper mixing:** \(Z(p)=O(1)\), hence \(\lambda_X\ll1/\log X\).

**With local upper mixing plus the thin-CRT positive-excess estimate (8.2):**
\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_3
\ll\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{(\log X)^3},
\]
which is \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) at the Poisson scale. Under a full Poisson asymptotic one expects the leading scale
\[
X^2\lambda_X^3
\sim\frac{(\log2)^3X^2}{(\log X)^3}
\]
when the effective local mass is one zero state per prime.

## Final assessment

The newly proved \(G_{\mathrm{diff}}=\mathrm{SL}_2\) result removes a major algebraic-degeneracy concern. Together with the K3 \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)\) description, it gives the correct connected monodromy and bounded-rank geometric platform for a product-independence argument. It also allows a clean, unconditional adelic-generation theorem through the concrete subgroup \(\langle A_0,A_1\rangle\).

But this does **not** close the cross-prime moment problem. The exact obstruction is now sharper:

> The remaining theorem is a \(Q^{2/3}\)-length incomplete-CRT/deterministic-projective-word discrepancy estimate, preferably in the aggregate positive-excess form (8.2). Full congruence images and random-walk expansion are structural inputs, not substitutes for that estimate.

Accordingly, I would record Theorem 2.1 as a real new lemma in the project, but I would not advertise it as progress on \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) until it is coupled to a theorem controlling the actual chronological prefixes or the Fourier transforms of the zero sets.
