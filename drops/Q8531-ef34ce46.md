# Q8531 — FDAC residue degree audit

## Status

Terminal status: **named blocker**. No unconditional large-prime residue-degree-one theorem for the fully deflated FDAC pencil content was proved from the supplied definitions, and no exact proper-residue-field counterexample was verified. The acceptance gate is therefore not met.

## Attempted algebraic reduction

For a non-progression gap triple `(a,b,c)` with span `s=a+b+c`, the intended route is:

1. Remove the structural saturation factors (central factors, degree-drop factors, and the explicitly identified primitive-return factors).
2. Form the fully deflated polynomial pair and its pencil resultant.
3. Show that a large prime `p>C*s^2` dividing the remaining content forces a common zero of the deflated pair in `F_p` rather than only in an extension field.

The missing implication is exactly the residue-degree-one assertion. A resultant divisibility statement alone only gives a common zero over an algebraic closure of `F_p`; it does not force an `F_p`-rational zero. The finite degree of the pencil does not by itself eliminate extension-field roots.

## Why normalization length is insufficient

The normalization-length estimates in `atom_tail_section.tex` control the size of the possible content contribution after the deflation step, but they do not prove that every surviving valuation corresponds to a primitive return quadruple. A valuation bound requires the stronger statement that every unsaturated prime divisor has a controlled geometric multiplicity and residue degree.

No proof was obtained of:

`p | content(deflated pencil) and p not in saturation => exists x in F_p with P_a(x)=P_b(x)=0`

for all `p>C*s^2`.

## Remaining required lemma

A sufficient missing theorem would be:

> There exists an absolute `C` such that for every non-progression triple of span `s`, every prime `p>C*s^2` outside the saturation set, every irreducible factor of the fully deflated pencil over `F_p` having a common root has degree one. Moreover the sum of valuations of degree-one factors over all triples is bounded by the FDAC normalization mass.

This is stronger than the desired FDAC conclusion and is the exact bottleneck separating resultant divisibility from primitive-return counting.

## Checks against known routes

- Reusing FDAC as an assumption is invalid.
- The normalization formula alone does not imply residue degree one.
- Finite computations through bounded span cannot establish the uniform large-prime statement.
- No proper residue extension counterexample was produced, so the proposed theorem remains unrefuted but unproved.

## Residual

The surviving target is an explicit residue-degree/multiplicity theorem for the saturated Apéry gap-polynomial pencil. Once proved, it can be combined with the existing normalization-length estimate to attempt the `c=1/2` zero-count consequence.
