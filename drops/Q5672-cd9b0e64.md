## 1. Exact preliminaries

### 1.1 Casoratian and cocycle

The recurrence gives

\[
\boxed{a_mb_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m=\frac6{m^3}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Indeed, for

\[
W_m=a_mb_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m,
\]

substitution of `(0.1)` yields

\[
W_{m+1}=\frac{m^3}{(m+1)^3}W_m,
\]

and \(W_1=6\). Dividing `(1.1)` by \(b_mb_{m-1}\) and telescoping gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{a_N}{b_N}
=\sum_{m=1}^N\frac6{m^3b_mb_{m-1}}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

This identity is exact in \(\mathbf Q\). If an intermediate \(b_m\) vanishes modulo \(q\), its summands may have negative valuation and cannot be reduced separately.

### 1.2 First Frobenius digit

The proved laws are

\[
\boxed{q^3a_{kq+j}\equiv a_kb_j\pmod q,}
\tag{1.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{b_{kq+j}\equiv b_kb_j\pmod q.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The second is Gessel's Lucas congruence. The first is obtained from the denominator-free companion sum after complete block cancellation. Neither may be divided by \(b_k\) or \(b_j\) on a zero fiber.

### 1.3 Beukers endpoint supercongruences

For \(q\ge5\) prime and \(m,r\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{b_{mq^r}\equiv b_{mq^{r-1}}\pmod{q^{3r}},}
\tag{1.5}
\]

\[
\boxed{b_{mq^r-1}\equiv b_{mq^{r-1}-1}\pmod{q^{3r}}.}
\tag{1.6}
\]

In particular,

\[
b_{kq}\equiv b_k\pmod{q^3},\qquad
b_{kq-1}\equiv b_{k-1}\pmod{q^3}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

If \(k=uq^s\), \(q\nmid u\), the precise version needed after division by \(k^3\) is

\[
\boxed{
 b_{kq}\equiv b_k\pmod{q^{3(s+1)}},\qquad
 b_{kq-1}\equiv b_{k-1}\pmod{q^{3(s+1)}}.}
\tag{1.8}
\]

Primary sources are F. Beukers, *Some congruences for the Apéry numbers*, J. Number Theory **21** (1985), 141–155, and *Another congruence for the Apéry numbers*, J. Number Theory **25** (1987), 201–210.

## 2. Exact within-block transfer

Fix \(x=kq\). Define \(F_j(x),G_j(x)\) by

\[
F_0(x)=1,\qquad
F_1(x)=\frac{P(x)}{(x+1)^3},
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
G_0(x)=0,\qquad
G_1(x)=-\frac{x^3}{(x+1)^3},
\tag{2.2}
\]

and, for \(Z=F,G\),

\[
(x+j+1)^3Z_{j+1}(x)
=P(x+j)Z_j(x)-(x+j)^3Z_{j-1}(x).
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 — exact transfer

For every solution \(y_m\) of `(0.1)`,

\[
\boxed{y_{x+j}=F_j(x)y_x+G_j(x)y_{x-1}.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

**Proof.** The right side has the correct values at \(j=0,1\) and satisfies the same second-order recurrence in \(j\). ∎

For \(0\le j<q\), every denominator in \(F_j(kq),G_j(kq)\) is a product of

\[
(kq+r)^3,\qquad1\le r\le j,
\]

and is a \(q\)-unit.

### Lemma 2.2 — cubic memory factor

There are rational functions \(R_j(x)\), regular at \(x=0\), such that

\[
\boxed{G_j(x)=x^3R_j(x),}
\tag{2.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{R_j(0)=-\frac{a_j}{6}.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

**Proof.** The initial values `(2.2)` contain \(x^3\), and `(2.3)` preserves it. After division by \(x^3\) and specialization \(x=0\), \(R_j(0)\) satisfies the Apéry recurrence with values \(0,-1\). Since \(a_j/6\) has values \(0,1\), `(2.6)` follows. ∎

Define

\[
\Delta_j=q^3a_{x+j}b_k-a_kb_{x+j},
\tag{2.7}
\]

\[
E_{q,k}=q^3a_{x-1}b_k-a_kb_{x-1}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Applying `(2.4)` to \(a,b\) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_j=F_j(kq)\Delta_0+G_j(kq)E_{q,k}.}
\tag{2.9}
\]

By `(2.5)`,

\[
G_j(kq)\in q^3\mathbf Z_{(q)}.
\tag{2.10}
\]

When \(0\le k<q\), \(q^3a_{kq-1}\) is \(q\)-integral and \(a_k\) is \(q\)-integral, so \(E_{q,k}\in\mathbf Z_{(q)}\). Therefore:

### Corollary 2.3 — endpoint equivalence in the two-digit range

For \(0\le k<q\),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{q,k,j}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}
\text{ for every }j<q
\Longleftrightarrow
\Delta_{q,k,0}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}.}
\tag{2.11}
\]

For arbitrary \(k\), `(2.9)` is still exact, but \(E_{q,k}\) can have negative valuation. Its cancellation is part of the inherited-zero problem and must not be assumed.

## 3. The digit-ordinary theorem

Call \(q\) **digit-ordinary** when

\[
q\nmid b_r\qquad(0\le r<q).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Lucas then implies that every \(b_m\) is a \(q\)-unit.

Put

\[
S_N=\frac{a_N}{b_N}.
\]

Multiplying `(1.2)` at \(N=kq\) by \(q^3\),

\[
q^3S_{kq}
=\sum_{m=1}^{kq}\frac{6q^3}{m^3b_mb_{m-1}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

If \(q\nmid m\), the summand lies in \(q^3\mathbf Z_q\). If \(m=tq\), write \(t=uq^s\), \(q\nmid u\). Equation `(1.8)` and unit denominators give

\[
\frac6{t^3b_{tq}b_{tq-1}}
\equiv
\frac6{t^3b_tb_{t-1}}\pmod{q^3}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{q^3S_{kq}\equiv S_k\pmod{q^3}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

For \(0<j<q\), the indices \(kq+1,\dots,kq+j\) are \(q\)-units, and all their Apéry values are units. Therefore

\[
q^3(S_{kq+j}-S_{kq})
=q^3\sum_{r=1}^j
\frac6{(kq+r)^3b_{kq+r}b_{kq+r-1}}
\in q^3\mathbf Z_q.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Combining `(3.4)` and `(3.5)`,

\[
q^3S_{kq+j}\equiv S_k\pmod{q^3}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Finally, exactly—not merely modulo \(q\)—

\[
\Delta_{q,k,j}
=b_kb_{kq+j}(q^3S_{kq+j}-S_k).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Both factors outside the parentheses are \(q\)-units. This proves:

### Theorem 3.1 — ordinary projective Frobenius

For every digit-ordinary prime \(q\ge5\), every \(k\ge0\), and \(0\le j<q\),

\[
\boxed{
q^3a_{kq+j}b_k-a_kb_{kq+j}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}.}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Every division in this proof is justified by digit-ordinarity plus Lucas. No integrality of \(E_{q,k}\) for large \(k\) is assumed.

## 4. Exact nonadjacent Casoratian identity

The ordinary proof fails when a digit \(b_r\) vanishes because individual cocycle terms can have poles. The following identity cancels them before division.

Put

\[
\Delta_{q,k}=q^3a_{kq}b_k-a_kb_{kq},
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\Delta^-_{q,k}
=q^3a_{kq-1}b_{k-1}-a_{k-1}b_{kq-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proposition 4.1 — exact projective edge identity

For \(k\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}\Delta_{q,k}
-b_kb_{kq}\Delta^-_{q,k}\\
&\qquad=\frac6{k^3}
\left(b_kb_{k-1}-b_{kq}b_{kq-1}\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4.3}
\]

**Proof.** Expanding the left side and grouping gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&q^3b_kb_{k-1}
(a_{kq}b_{kq-1}-a_{kq-1}b_{kq})\\
&\quad+b_{kq}b_{kq-1}
(a_{k-1}b_k-a_kb_{k-1}).
\end{aligned}
\]

The two parentheses are \(6/(kq)^3\) and \(-6/k^3\) by `(1.1)`. ∎

No Apéry value is inverted.

## 5. Corner regularization and the exact missing invariant

Write

\[
k=uq^s,\qquad q\nmid u.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By `(1.8)`,

\[
C^+_{q,k}=
\frac{b_{kq}-b_k}{q^{3(s+1)}},\qquad
C^-_{q,k}=
\frac{b_{kq-1}-b_{k-1}}{q^{3(s+1)}}
\tag{5.2}
\]

are integers. Set

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_{q,k}
=b_kC^-_{q,k}+b_{k-1}C^+_{q,k}
+q^{3(s+1)}C^+_{q,k}C^-_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
b_{kq}b_{kq-1}-b_kb_{k-1}
=q^{3(s+1)}\mathcal B_{q,k}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Substitution in `(4.3)`, using \(k^3=u^3q^{3s}\), yields

\[
\boxed{
 b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}\Delta_{q,k}
=b_kb_{kq}\Delta^-_{q,k}
-\frac{6q^3}{u^3}\mathcal B_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The sole denominator \(u^3\) is a \(q\)-unit.

Assume the preceding projective edge is known:

\[
\Lambda^-_{q,k}=rac{\Delta^-_{q,k}}{q^3}\in\mathbf Z_q.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Dividing `(5.5)` by \(q^3\),

\[
\boxed{
 b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}\frac{\Delta_{q,k}}{q^3}
=b_kb_{kq}\Lambda^-_{q,k}
-\frac6{u^3}\mathcal B_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Define

\[
c_{q,k}=v_q(b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}),
\tag{5.8}
\]

\[
\beta_{q,k}=q^{-c_{q,k}}b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}\in\mathbf Z_q^\times,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{q,k}
=b_kb_{kq}\Lambda^-_{q,k}
-\frac6{u^3}\mathcal B_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{q,k}
\frac{\Delta_{q,k}}{q^{3-c_{q,k}}}
=\mathcal E_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
v_q(\Delta_{q,k})
=3-c_{q,k}+v_q(\mathcal E_{q,k}),}
\tag{5.11}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal E_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^{c_{q,k}}}.}
\tag{5.12}
\]

This is an exact necessary-and-sufficient lower invariant. Every division has been justified: \(u,eta_{q,k}\) are units, \(C^\pm\) are integers by the endpoint supercongruences, and \(\Lambda^-_{q,k}\) is integral by the explicitly stated preceding-edge hypothesis.

### 5.1 Immediate unit case

If \(q\nmid b_{k-1}\), then \(q\nmid b_{kq-1}\) by `(1.8)`, so \(c_{q,k}=0\). Equation `(5.7)` proves \(\Delta_{q,k}/q^3\in\mathbf Z_q\). Thus every endpoint whose preceding coarse value is a unit closes once the preceding edge is known.

### 5.2 Inherited zero blocks

Write

\[
k=Aq+r,\qquad0\le r<q.
\]

For \(r>0\), Lucas gives

\[
b_k\equiv b_Ab_r,\qquad
b_{k-1}\equiv b_Ab_{r-1}\pmod q.
\tag{5.13}
\]

The bottom-digit recurrence has unit pivots, so \(b_{r-1},b_r\) cannot both vanish. Hence

\[
q\mid b_{k-1},b_k
\Longrightarrow q\mid b_A.
\tag{5.14}
\]

For \(r=0\),

\[
b_k\equiv b_A,\qquad
b_{k-1}\equiv b_{A-1}b_{q-1}\equiv b_{A-1}\pmod q,
\tag{5.15}
\]

so a consecutive pair descends to \(b_{A-1},b_A\). Repeated descent strictly lowers the index.

The singular obstruction is therefore an inherited zero block. If \(v_q(b_{k-1})=v_q(b_{kq-1})=1\), then \(c_{q,k}=2\), and `(5.12)` requires \(\mathcal E_{q,k}\) modulo \(q^2\). The first Frobenius digit determines only the preceding quotient modulo \(q\). One new extension digit remains.

A complete proof of `(P3)` must supply a WZ/Dwork certificate establishing

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^{c_{q,k}}}}
\tag{5.16}
\]

on every inherited block. A failure of `(5.16)` would be an exact counterexample. Neither is available from the stated inputs.

## 6. Why the explicit companion sum stops at the same coordinate

The rational companion has the denominator-free finite representation

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
a_N={}&b_NH_N^{(3)}\\
&+\frac12\sum_{m=0}^N
\binom Nm^2\binom{N+m}{m}^2
\sum_{\ell=1}^m
\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}
{\ell^3\binom N\ell\binom{N+\ell}{\ell}}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.1}
\]

After reversing the finite sums, all potentially singular terms can be grouped before reduction. This is why `(1.3)` remains true even when some \(b_j\) vanishes.

Lifting `(6.1)` from the first digit to projective precision \(q^3\) requires two more quotient layers of every no-carry, one-carry, and suffix block. On digit-ordinary primes those layers cancel through Section 3. On inherited zero blocks the uncancelled boundary is exactly \(\mathcal E_{q,k}\).

Thus the phrase “Dwork Frobenius preserves the projective line” is not a certificate. The missing assertion is the explicit divisibility `(5.16)`. A complete WZ proof must exhibit a telescoper whose boundary is \(\mathcal E_{q,k}\) and prove its \(q^{c_{q,k}}\)-divisibility. No such certificate occurs at commit `590c2a2`.

## 7. First defect divided by \(q^3\)

Assume \(0\le k<q\) and assume endpoint `(E)` for \((q,k)\). Define

\[
\kappa_{q,k}=rac{\Delta_{q,k,0}}{q^3}\pmod q,\qquad
K_{q,k,j}=rac{\Delta_{q,k,j}}{q^3}\pmod q.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For \(k=0\), \(\Delta_{q,0,j}=q^3a_j\), so the result below is immediate. Let \(1\le k<q\).

From `(2.9)`, after division by \(q^3\),

\[
F_j(kq)\equiv F_j(0)=b_j\pmod q.
\tag{7.2}
\]

By `(2.5)`–`(2.6)`,

\[
\frac{G_j(kq)}{q^3}
\equiv k^3R_j(0)
=-\frac{k^3a_j}{6}\pmod q.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The first-digit laws at \(kq-1=(k-1)q+(q-1)\) give

\[
q^3a_{kq-1}\equiv a_{k-1},\qquad
b_{kq-1}\equiv b_{k-1}\pmod q,
\]

because \(b_{q-1}\equiv1\pmod q\). Since \(k\) is a \(q\)-unit,

\[
E_{q,k}
\equiv a_{k-1}b_k-a_kb_{k-1}
=-\frac6{k^3}\pmod q.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Substitution proves:

### Theorem 7.1 — exact defect propagation

Conditional only on endpoint divisibility, for \(0\le k<q\),

\[
\boxed{
K_{q,k,j}=\kappa_{q,k}b_j+a_j\pmod q.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The \(a_j\) term is the cubic previous-block memory and has coefficient exactly one.

### 7.1 Target locus

Let \(1\le j<q\) and suppose \(q\mid b_j\). Consecutive bottom-digit zeros are impossible, hence \(q\nmid b_{j-1}\). From `(1.1)`,

\[
a_jb_{j-1}\equiv\frac6{j^3}\not\equiv0\pmod q,
\]

so \(q\nmid a_j\). Reducing `(7.5)`,

\[
\boxed{K_{q,k,j}=a_j\in\mathbf F_q^\times.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Therefore, whenever `(P3)` holds,

\[
\boxed{v_q(\Delta_{q,k,j})=3}
\tag{7.7}
\]

on the target locus. The divided defect is a transverse unit, not another target-divisible scalar.

For arbitrary \(k\), extending `(7.5)` through inherited zero blocks requires the saturated crossing digit in `(5.10)`; it cannot be used circularly to prove `(P3)`.

## 8. Exact \(q^4\) counterexample

Take

\[
(q,k,j)=(5,1,1),\qquad kq+j=6.
\]

Work modulo \(5^4=625\). The recurrence gives

\[
a_2=\frac{351}{4}\equiv244,\qquad
 a_3\equiv296,\qquad a_4\equiv140\pmod{625}.
\]

Set

\[
A_m=125a_m\qquad(m\ge5).
\]

At \(m=4\), since \(P(4)=3105\equiv-20\pmod{625}\),

\[
A_5=P(4)a_4-64a_3\equiv131\pmod{625}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

At \(m=5\), multiply the recurrence by \(125\):

\[
216A_6=P(5)A_5-125^2a_4.
\]

Since \(P(5)=5665\equiv40\pmod{625}\),

\[
216A_6\equiv40\cdot131\equiv240\pmod{625}.
\]

As \(216\cdot140\equiv240\pmod{625}\),

\[
A_6\equiv140\pmod{625}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Also

\[
b_6=21460825\equiv200\pmod{625}.
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{5,1,1}
&=125a_6b_1-a_1b_6\\
&=5A_6-6b_6\\
&\equiv5\cdot140-6\cdot200\\
&\equiv125\pmod{625}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{v_5(\Delta_{5,1,1})=3.}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This is an exact recurrence certificate. It proves that \(q^4\) is false and that \(q^3\), if proved globally, is the maximal uniform modulus. Moreover

\[
\frac{\Delta_{5,1,1}}{5^3}\equiv1\equiv a_1\pmod5,
\]

exactly as predicted by `(7.6)`.

## 9. Exact theorem frontier

The proved statements are:

1. **First digit:**
   \[
   q^3a_{kq+j}\equiv a_kb_j\pmod q,\qquad
   b_{kq+j}\equiv b_kb_j\pmod q
   \]
   in the range where the companion clearing is defined; the cross-multiplied form is the safe statement on singular fibers.

2. **Projective \(q^3\), digit-ordinary primes:** Theorem 3.1 holds for all \(k,j\).

3. **Exact singular reduction:** The endpoint on an inherited block is equivalent to
   \[
   \mathcal E_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^{c_{q,k}}}.
   \]

4. **Conditional defect law:** In the two-digit range,
   \[
   \frac{q^3a_{kq+j}b_k-a_kb_{kq+j}}{q^3}
   \equiv\kappa_{q,k}b_j+a_j\pmod q.
   \]

5. **Sharp ceiling:** \(q^4\) fails at \((5,1,1)\) with exact valuation \(3\).

What remains is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^{c_{q,k}}}
\text{ on every inherited zero block}.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

A proof of `(9.1)` would establish the user's option 1. An exact failure would establish option 2. Neither follows from the current repository state or the stated classical inputs.

---

## 10. Consequence for P3.2

Even a future proof of the full projective \(q^3\) theorem would not provide a second target-selective vanishing condition. At a lower-digit target,

\[
q\mid b_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\Delta_{q,k,j}}{q^3}\equiv a_j\ne0\pmod q.
\]

The row certifies exact depth three but is transverse to the target divisor. To turn it into a characteristic-zero carrier one must still insert a prime-local detector for \(q\mid b_j\), paying the same unknown target radical.

Thus this projective conjecture is valuable as a local denominator-depth theorem, but it does not by itself supply the horizontal compression needed for P3.2.
