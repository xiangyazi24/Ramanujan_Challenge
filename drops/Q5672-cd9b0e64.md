## 1. Exact preliminaries

### 1.1 Casoratian and cocycle

The recurrence gives

\[
a_mb_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m=\frac6{m^3}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Indeed, if

\[
W_m=a_mb_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m,
\]

then substitution of `(0.1)` yields

\[
W_{m+1}=\frac{m^3}{(m+1)^3}W_m,
\]

and \(W_1=6\). Dividing `(1.1)` by \(b_mb_{m-1}\) and telescoping gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{a_N}{b_N}
 =\sum_{m=1}^N\frac6{m^3b_mb_{m-1}}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Formula `(1.2)` is exact in \(\mathbf Q\). It may not be reduced termwise modulo \(q\) when an intermediate \(b_m\) vanishes modulo \(q\); the sum must first be regularized or grouped.

### 1.2 First digit

The proved block laws are

\[
\boxed{
q^3a_{kq+j}\equiv a_kb_j\pmod q,}
\tag{1.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
b_{kq+j}\equiv b_kb_j\pmod q.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The second is Gessel's Lucas congruence. The first follows from the denominator-free companion sum after full block cancellation. Neither law may be divided by \(b_k\) or \(b_j\) on a zero fiber.

### 1.3 Beukers endpoint supercongruences

For every prime \(q\ge5\), every \(m\ge1\), and every \(r\ge1\), the Apéry numbers satisfy

\[
\boxed{
b_{mq^r}\equiv b_{mq^{r-1}}\pmod{q^{3r}},}
\tag{1.5}
\]

\[
\boxed{
b_{mq^r-1}\equiv b_{mq^{r-1}-1}\pmod{q^{3r}}.}
\tag{1.6}
\]

The special cases used below are

\[
b_{kq}\equiv b_k\pmod{q^3},
\qquad
b_{kq-1}\equiv b_{k-1}\pmod{q^3}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

If \(k=uq^s\), \(q\nmid u\), the stronger form is

\[
b_{kq}\equiv b_k\pmod{q^{3(s+1)}},
\qquad
b_{kq-1}\equiv b_{k-1}\pmod{q^{3(s+1)}}.
\tag{1.8}
\]

Primary references are F. Beukers, *Some congruences for the Apéry numbers*, J. Number Theory **21** (1985), 141–155, and *Another congruence for the Apéry numbers*, J. Number Theory **25** (1987), 201–210.

## 2. Exact within-block transfer

Fix \(x=kq\). Define \(F_j(x),G_j(x)\) by

\[
F_0(x)=1,\qquad
F_1(x)=\frac{P(x)}{(x+1)^3},
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
G_0(x)=0,\qquad
G_1(x)=-\frac{x^3}{(x+1)^3},
\tag{2.2}
\]

and, for either \(Z=F\) or \(Z=G\),

\[
(x+j+1)^3Z_{j+1}(x)
=P(x+j)Z_j(x)-(x+j)^3Z_{j-1}(x).
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 — transfer formula

For every solution \(y_m\) of `(0.1)`,

\[
\boxed{
y_{x+j}=F_j(x)y_x+G_j(x)y_{x-1}.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

**Proof.** The right side agrees with \(y_x,y_{x+1}\) at \(j=0,1\), and it satisfies the same second-order recurrence in \(j\). Uniqueness proves `(2.4)`. ∎

For \(0\le j<q\), every denominator introduced while computing \(F_j(kq),G_j(kq)\) is a product of

\[
(kq+r)^3,\qquad 1\le r\le j,
\]

and is therefore a \(q\)-unit.

### Lemma 2.2 — cubic memory factor

There are rational functions \(R_j(x)\), regular at \(x=0\), such that

\[
\boxed{G_j(x)=x^3R_j(x).}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{R_j(0)=-\frac{a_j}{6}.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

**Proof.** The initial values `(2.2)` have the factor \(x^3\), and the recurrence `(2.3)` preserves it because all its denominators are regular at \(x=0\) for the stated range. Dividing by \(x^3\) and setting \(x=0\), the values \(R_j(0)\) satisfy the Apéry recurrence with

\[
R_0(0)=0,\qquad R_1(0)=-1.
\]

Since \(a_0=0,a_1=6\), uniqueness gives `(2.6)`. ∎

Define

\[
\Delta_j:=q^3a_{x+j}b_k-a_kb_{x+j},
\tag{2.7}
\]

\[
E_{q,k}:=q^3a_{x-1}b_k-a_kb_{x-1}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Applying `(2.4)` to \(a\) and \(b\) and subtracting gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_j=F_j(kq)\Delta_0+G_j(kq)E_{q,k}.}
\tag{2.9}
\]

Because of `(2.5)`,

\[
G_j(kq)\in q^3\mathbf Z_{(q)}.
\tag{2.10}
\]

In the range \(0\le k<q\), \(q^3a_{kq-1}\) is \(q\)-integral, hence \(E_{q,k}\in\mathbf Z_{(q)}\). Therefore:

### Corollary 2.3 — endpoint equivalence in the two-digit range

For \(0\le k<q\),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{q,k,j}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}
\text{ for all }0\le j<q
\Longleftrightarrow
\Delta_{q,k,0}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}.}
\tag{2.11}
\]

For arbitrary \(k\), identity `(2.9)` remains exact, but \(E_{q,k}\) can carry negative \(q\)-adic valuation. The required integrality is then part of the inherited-zero problem rather than a formal consequence of the recurrence.

## 3. The digit-ordinary theorem

Call \(q\) **digit-ordinary** if

\[
q\nmid b_r\qquad(0\le r<q).
\tag{3.1}
\]

By Lucas, every \(b_m\) is then a \(q\)-unit.

Let

\[
S_N=\frac{a_N}{b_N}.
\]

Multiply `(1.2)` at \(N=kq\) by \(q^3\):

\[
q^3S_{kq}
=\sum_{m=1}^{kq}\frac{6q^3}{m^3b_mb_{m-1}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

If \(q\nmid m\), the summand lies in \(q^3\mathbf Z_q\) and is zero modulo \(q^3\).

If \(m=tq\), write \(t=uq^s\), \(q\nmid u\). Then `(1.8)` gives

\[
b_{tq}b_{tq-1}
\equiv b_tb_{t-1}\pmod{q^{3(s+1)}}.
\]

All four factors are units. Division by \(t^3=u^3q^{3s}\) therefore gives

\[
\frac6{t^3b_{tq}b_{tq-1}}
\equiv
\frac6{t^3b_tb_{t-1}}\pmod{q^3}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Summing over \(t=1,\dots,k\),

\[
\boxed{q^3S_{kq}\equiv S_k\pmod{q^3}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Since \(b_{kq},b_k\) are units and \(b_{kq}\equiv b_k\pmod{q^3}\), `(3.4)` is equivalent to

\[
q^3a_{kq}b_k-a_kb_{kq}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Equation `(2.9)` then propagates the endpoint through the block.

### Theorem 3.1 — ordinary projective Frobenius

For every digit-ordinary prime \(q\ge5\), every \(k\ge0\), and every \(0\le j<q\),

\[
\boxed{
q^3a_{kq+j}b_k-a_kb_{kq+j}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This proof is completely insensitive to the size of \(k\). Its use of division is legal precisely because digit-ordinarity and Lucas make every Apéry denominator in `(3.2)` a unit.

## 4. Exact nonadjacent Casoratian identity

The ordinary proof fails as soon as a digit \(b_r\) vanishes, because individual terms of `(3.2)` can have poles. The following identity performs the required cancellation before any division.

Put

\[
\Delta_{q,k}:=q^3a_{kq}b_k-a_kb_{kq},
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\Delta^-_{q,k}
:=q^3a_{kq-1}b_{k-1}-a_{k-1}b_{kq-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proposition 4.1 — exact projective edge identity

For every \(k\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}\Delta_{q,k}
-b_kb_{kq}\Delta^-_{q,k}\\
&\qquad=\frac6{k^3}
\bigl(b_kb_{k-1}-b_{kq}b_{kq-1}\bigr).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4.3}
\]

**Proof.** Expanding the left side and grouping terms gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
& q^3b_kb_{k-1}
 (a_{kq}b_{kq-1}-a_{kq-1}b_{kq})\\
&\quad+b_{kq}b_{kq-1}
 (a_{k-1}b_k-a_kb_{k-1}).
\end{aligned}
\]

The two parentheses are \(6/(kq)^3\) and \(-6/k^3\), respectively, by `(1.1)`. This is exactly `(4.3)`. ∎

No Apéry value has been inverted.

## 5. Corner regularization and the exact missing invariant

Write

\[
k=uq^s,\qquad q\nmid u.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By `(1.8)`, the integers

\[
C^+_{q,k}
 :=\frac{b_{kq}-b_k}{q^{3(s+1)}},
\qquad
C^-_{q,k}
 :=\frac{b_{kq-1}-b_{k-1}}{q^{3(s+1)}}
\tag{5.2}
\]

are well-defined. Set

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_{q,k}
=b_kC^-_{q,k}+b_{k-1}C^+_{q,k}
+q^{3(s+1)}C^+_{q,k}C^-_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
b_{kq}b_{kq-1}-b_kb_{k-1}
=q^{3(s+1)}\mathcal B_{q,k}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Substitution into `(4.3)`, using \(k^3=u^3q^{3s}\), gives

\[
\boxed{
 b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}\Delta_{q,k}
=b_kb_{kq}\Delta^-_{q,k}
-\frac{6q^3}{u^3}\mathcal B_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The only denominator is \(u^3\), a \(q\)-unit.

Assume the preceding projective edge has been proved, so that

\[
\Lambda^-_{q,k}:=\frac{\Delta^-_{q,k}}{q^3}\in\mathbf Z_q.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Dividing `(5.5)` by \(q^3\) gives

\[
\boxed{
 b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}\frac{\Delta_{q,k}}{q^3}
=b_kb_{kq}\Lambda^-_{q,k}
-\frac6{u^3}\mathcal B_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Define

\[
c_{q,k}:=v_q(b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}),
\tag{5.8}
\]

\[
\beta_{q,k}
:=q^{-c_{q,k}}b_{k-1}b_{kq-1}\in\mathbf Z_q^\times,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{q,k}
:=b_kb_{kq}\Lambda^-_{q,k}
-\frac6{u^3}\mathcal B_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Then `(5.7)` becomes the primitive saturated identity

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{q,k}
\frac{\Delta_{q,k}}{q^{3-c_{q,k}}}
=\mathcal E_{q,k}.}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Since \(eta_{q,k}\) is a unit,

\[
\boxed{
v_q(\Delta_{q,k})
=3-c_{q,k}+v_q(\mathcal E_{q,k}).}
\tag{5.11}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal E_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^{c_{q,k}}}.}
\tag{5.12}
\]

This is the promised exact lower invariant. It is finite, division-free apart from the proven unit \(u^{-3}\), and can be checked symbolically from the recurrence and the two Beukers corner defects.

### 5.1 What closes immediately

If \(q\nmid b_{k-1}\), then `(1.8)` implies \(q\nmid b_{kq-1}\), so \(c_{q,k}=0\). Equation `(5.7)` then directly gives

\[
\Delta_{q,k}/q^3\in\mathbf Z_q.
\]

Thus every endpoint whose preceding coarse Apéry value is a unit closes once the preceding projective edge is known.

### 5.2 The inherited-zero locus

Write

\[
k=Aq+r,\qquad0\le r<q.
\]

Lucas gives, for \(r>0\),

\[
b_k\equiv b_Ab_r,
\qquad
b_{k-1}\equiv b_Ab_{r-1}\pmod q.
\tag{5.13}
\]

The bottom-digit recurrence has unit pivots for \(0<r<q\), so \(b_{r-1},b_r\) cannot both vanish modulo \(q\). Hence

\[
q\mid b_{k-1},b_k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q\mid b_A.
\tag{5.14}
\]

If \(r=0\), then

\[
b_k\equiv b_A,
\qquad
b_{k-1}\equiv b_{A-1}b_{q-1}\equiv b_{A-1}\pmod q,
\tag{5.15}
\]

so a consecutive zero pair descends to the lower pair \(b_{A-1},b_A\). Repeated descent strictly lowers the index.

Thus the singular obstruction is not an arbitrary pole: it is an **inherited zero block**. At a first inherited simple zero one typically has

\[
c_{q,k}=2.
\]

The first-digit theorem determines \(\Lambda^-_{q,k}\) only modulo \(q\), while `(5.12)` requires the residue of \(\mathcal E_{q,k}\) modulo \(q^2\). This is one genuinely new Frobenius-extension digit.

Neither `(1.3)`, `(1.4)`, the exact cocycle `(1.2)`, nor the Beukers corner congruences determine that digit. A proof of `(P3)` must therefore add a new WZ/Dwork identity proving

\[
\mathcal E_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^{c_{q,k}}}
\tag{5.16}
\]

on every inherited zero block. Conversely, `(5.16)` would complete the induction.

This is the precise proof-level stopping point; it is not a finite-data objection.

## 6. Why the existing companion sum proves only the first digit

A useful denominator-free representation is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
a_N={}&b_NH_N^{(3)}\\
&+\frac12\sum_{m=0}^N
\binom Nm^2\binom{N+m}{m}^2
\sum_{\ell=1}^m
\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}
{\ell^3\binom N\ell\binom{N+\ell}{\ell}}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.1}
\]

After reversing the finite sums, all cancellation can be grouped into rational block coefficients before reduction. This proves

\[
q^3a_{kq+j}\equiv a_kb_j\pmod q
\]

without ever inverting an Apéry number.

To lift `(6.1)` from the first digit to the projective modulus \(q^3\), one must compute two additional quotient layers of every nonmultiple and carry block. On digit-ordinary primes these layers cancel through the cocycle proof in Section 3. On inherited zero blocks, the uncancelled residue is exactly the coordinate \(\mathcal E_{q,k}\) in `(5.9)`.

Thus a statement such as “Dwork Frobenius preserves the projective line” is not a proof: the missing assertion is precisely the extension-class congruence `(5.16)`. Any complete WZ proof must provide a term \(R(N,m,\ell)\) whose telescoping boundary evaluates to \(\mathcal E_{q,k}\) and then proves the required \(q^{c_{q,k}}\)-divisibility. No such certificate is present at commit `590c2a2`.

## 7. The first divided defect

This section records what the conjecture would give and proves that it supplies no second target-zero equation.

Assume \(0\le k<q\) and assume the endpoint `(E)` for this pair. Define

\[
\kappa_{q,k}
:=\frac{\Delta_{q,k,0}}{q^3}\pmod q,
\qquad
K_{q,k,j}
:=\frac{\Delta_{q,k,j}}{q^3}\pmod q.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For \(k=0\), \(\Delta_{q,0,j}=q^3a_j\), so

\[
K_{q,0,j}=a_j,\qquad \kappa_{q,0}=0.
\]

Now suppose \(1\le k<q\). Divide `(2.9)` by \(q^3\). First,

\[
F_j(kq)\equiv F_j(0)=b_j\pmod q.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Second, by `(2.5)`–`(2.6)`,

\[
\frac{G_j(kq)}{q^3}
\equiv k^3R_j(0)
=-\frac{k^3a_j}{6}\pmod q.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Finally, the first-digit laws at the previous endpoint give

\[
q^3a_{kq-1}\equiv a_{k-1}b_{q-1}\equiv a_{k-1}\pmod q,
\]

\[
b_{kq-1}\equiv b_{k-1}b_{q-1}\equiv b_{k-1}\pmod q.
\]

Since \(k\) is a \(q\)-unit,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{q,k}
&\equiv a_{k-1}b_k-a_kb_{k-1}\\
&=-\frac6{k^3}\pmod q
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.4}
\]

by the Casoratian. Substitution of `(7.2)`–`(7.4)` into `(2.9)` proves:

### Theorem 7.1 — defect propagation

For \(0\le k<q\), conditional only on endpoint divisibility,

\[
\boxed{
K_{q,k,j}\equiv\kappa_{q,k}b_j+a_j\pmod q.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This identity is symbolic. The \(a_j\) term is exactly the cubic previous-block memory; its coefficient is one.

### 7.1 Target locus

Let \(1\le j<q\) and suppose \(q\mid b_j\). Consecutive bottom-digit zeros are impossible, so

\[
q\nmid b_{j-1}.
\]

The Casoratian gives

\[
a_jb_{j-1}\equiv\frac6{j^3}\not\equiv0\pmod q,
\]

hence

\[
q\nmid a_j.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Reducing `(7.5)` on the target locus yields

\[
\boxed{
K_{q,k,j}\equiv a_j\in\mathbf F_q^\times.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{v_q(\Delta_{q,k,j})=3}
\tag{7.8}
\]

whenever `(P3)` holds and \(q\mid b_j\).

This is maximally nonselective in the required sense. The first defect does not vanish at a target; it is forced to be a unit. It cannot be used as a second target-divisible scalar or as a new codimension-one condition.

For arbitrary \(k\), the same formal law requires the saturated crossing residue hidden in `(5.10)`. Proving that law in inherited zero blocks is equivalent to computing the same missing extension digit, so it must not be quoted as an independent proof of `(P3)`.

\[
A_5=125a_5
=P(4)a_4-64a_3
\equiv131\pmod{625}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

At \(m=5\), multiply the recurrence by \(125\):

\[
216A_6=P(5)A_5-125^2a_4.
\]

Since \(P(5)=5665\equiv40\pmod{625}\),

\[
216A_6\equiv40\cdot131\equiv240\pmod{625}.
\]

As \(216\cdot140\equiv240\pmod{625}\),

\[
A_6\equiv140\pmod{625}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The Apéry number is

\[
b_6=21460825\equiv200\pmod{625}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{5,1,1}
&=125a_6b_1-a_1b_6\\
&=5A_6-6b_6\\
&\equiv5\cdot140-6\cdot200\\
&\equiv-500\\
&\equiv125\pmod{625}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{v_5(\Delta_{5,1,1})=3.}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This proves two facts at once:

- the conjectural modulus \(q^3\) is attained exactly;
- every proposed uniform strengthening to \(q^4\) is false.

The divided defect is

\[
\frac{\Delta_{5,1,1}}{5^3}\equiv1\pmod5,
\]

consistent with `(7.7)`, since \(5\mid b_1\) and \(a_1=6\equiv1\pmod5\).

## 9. Correct statement of the present theorem frontier

The following are proved:

### Theorem A — first digit, all legal blocks

\[
q^3a_{kq+j}\equiv a_kb_j\pmod q,
\qquad
b_{kq+j}\equiv b_kb_j\pmod q,
\]

in the range in which the stated companion clearing is defined; the cross-multiplied formulation remains the safe one on singular fibers.

### Theorem B — projective \(q^3\), digit-ordinary primes

For every digit-ordinary \(q\ge5\), every \(k\ge0\), and \(0\le j<q\),

\[
q^3a_{kq+j}b_k-a_kb_{kq+j}\equiv0\pmod{q^3}.
\]

### Theorem C — exact singular reduction

On an inherited zero block, the endpoint is equivalent to

\[
\mathcal E_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^{c_{q,k}}},
\]

with \(\mathcal E_{q,k}\) and \(c_{q,k}\) explicitly defined by `(5.8)`–`(5.10)`.

### Theorem D — conditional first defect

Once the endpoint is granted, in the two-digit range

\[
\frac{q^3a_{kq+j}b_k-a_kb_{kq+j}}{q^3}
\equiv\kappa_{q,k}b_j+a_j\pmod q.
\]

At \(q\mid b_j\), this is the nonzero unit \(a_j\).

### Theorem E — sharp ceiling

The modulus \(q^4\) fails at \((5,1,1)\), with defect valuation exactly \(3\).

What remains unproved is exactly:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{q,k}\equiv0\pmod{q^{c_{q,k}}}
\text{ for every inherited zero block}.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

A complete proof of `(9.1)` would settle the user's option 1. An exact failure of `(9.1)` would provide the user's option 2. Neither is currently available from the repository or the cited classical supercongruences.

## 10. Strategic consequence for P3.2

Even a future proof of the full projective \(q^3\) theorem would not create a second target-selective equation. On the actual lower-digit target locus,

\[
q\mid b_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\Delta_{q,k,j}}{q^3}\equiv a_j\ne0\pmod q.
\]

Thus the projective row is transverse to the Hasse-zero divisor. It certifies exact depth three but does not force a new zero. Any attempt to multiply these prime-local units into one characteristic-zero carrier must still insert one detector for each prime satisfying \(q\mid b_j\), and therefore pays the unknown target radical.

The projective conjecture is consequently valuable as a local exactness theorem and as a denominator-depth statement, but it does not by itself provide the missing horizontal compression for P3.2.
