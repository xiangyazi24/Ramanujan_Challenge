# 1. Exact Newton--Kummer selector theorem

Put

\[
B_{D,N}=\binom{D+N}{N},
\]

and, using the Section 54 convention with left endpoint \(d=D-1\), define

\[
G_{D-1,N}(Y)
 =\sum_{i=0}^{N}\omega_iY_{D-1+i},
\]

where

\[
\omega_i=(-1)^i
 \binom{D-1+i}{i}
 \binom{D+N}{N-i}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The weights have the exact factorial form

\[
\boxed{
\omega_i=(-1)^i B_{D,N}\frac{D}{D+i}\binom Ni.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\frac{\binom{D-1+i}{i}\binom{D+N}{N-i}}
     {\binom{D+N}{N}}
 =\frac{D}{D+i}\binom Ni.
\]

Assume throughout the mesoscopic-block condition

\[
D>N.
\tag{1.3}
\]

This holds with a large margin in the first-cell and low-label blocks under discussion.

## Proposition 1.1: selector at every large prime divisor of the binomial

Let \(p>N\) be prime and suppose \(p\mid B_{D,N}\).  Since \(p\nmid N!\) and the numerator interval

\[
[D+1,D+N]
\]

has length less than \(p\), it contains a unique multiple

\[
m_p=k_pp.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
G_{D-1,N}(Y)\equiv Y_{m_p-1}\pmod p.
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

### Proof

Write \(m_p=D+i_p\), with \(1\le i_p\le N\).  Modulo \(p\), the interpolation nodes

\[
D-1,D,\ldots,D+N-1
\]

are pairwise distinct because their differences have absolute value at most \(N<p\).  The matched node satisfies

\[
D-1+i_p=m_p-1\equiv-1\pmod p.
\]

The Newton weight \(\omega_i\) is the Lagrange basis coefficient for evaluation at \(-1\).  Therefore

\[
\omega_{i_p}\equiv1\pmod p,
\qquad
\omega_j\equiv0\pmod p\quad(j\ne i_p),
\]

which proves (1.5).

One may also verify the matched coefficient directly:

\[
\binom{k_pp-1}{i_p}\equiv(-1)^{i_p}\pmod p,
\]

and, since \(N-i_p<p\),

\[
\binom{D+N}{N-i_p}
 =\binom{k_pp+N-i_p}{N-i_p}\equiv1\pmod p.
\]

The two signs cancel.  ∎

## Corollary 1.2: complete large-prime decomposition

Taking radicals, Proposition 1.1 gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rad}_{>N}\gcd(G_{D-1,N}(Y),B_{D,N})
 =
 \prod_{\substack{p>N,\ k_pp\in[D+1,D+N]\\
                    p\mid Y_{k_pp-1}}}p.
}
\tag{1.6}
\]

The multiplier \(k_p=1\) is the desired interval-prime sector.  The multipliers \(k_p\ge2\) are the complementary large-prime ghost sectors.

Notice that for a ghost prime one can have \(v_p(B_{D,N})>1\) if the unique numerator multiple is divisible by \(p^2\).  Formula (1.6) is a radical statement and does not require valuation one.  For an interval prime \(D<p\le D+N\), however, the unique multiple is exactly \(p\), and therefore

\[
v_p(B_{D,N})=1.
\tag{1.7}
\]

# 2. The exact CRT normal form of the target radical

Let

\[
\mathcal P_{D,N}=\{p\text{ prime}:D<p\le D+N\},
\qquad
Q_{D,N}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_{D,N}}p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Every \(p\in\mathcal P_{D,N}\) satisfies \(p>N\), and (1.7) shows

\[
Q_{D,N}\mid B_{D,N},
\qquad
\gcd\left(Q_{D,N},\frac{B_{D,N}}{Q_{D,N}}\right)=1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For \(p\in\mathcal P_{D,N}\), put

\[
e_p\equiv\omega_{p-D}\pmod {Q_{D,N}}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The selector proof gives

\[
e_p\equiv1\pmod p,
\qquad
e_p\equiv0\pmod q\quad(q\in\mathcal P_{D,N},\ q\ne p).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus the Newton weights are already CRT idempotents on the interval primes.  All weights whose node is not \(p-1\) for an interval prime vanish modulo every factor of \(Q_{D,N}\).  Hence

\[
\boxed{
G_{D-1,N}(Y)
 \equiv
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{D,N}}e_pY_{p-1}
 \pmod {Q_{D,N}}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(G_{D-1,N}(Y),Q_{D,N})
 =\prod_{\substack{D<p\le D+N\\p\mid Y_{p-1}}}p.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Since \(Q_{D,N}\) is precisely the squarefree interval-prime part of the binomial, (2.6) is the stated block identity

\[
R_{D,N}(Y)
 =\operatorname{rad}_{(D,D+N]}
   \gcd\left(G_{D-1,N}(Y),\binom{D+N}{N}\right).
\tag{2.7}
\]

The important audit conclusion is that (2.5) is not merely a convenient proof of (2.7).  It is the **complete p-adic normal form** of the interval part.  There is no remaining Kummer multiplicity to exploit: every target prime occurs once in the binomial and reads exactly one independent shell coordinate.

# 3. Specialization to the Apéry fixed-moment shell

In one fixed quotient block, Section 48 gives, for the interior nodes,

\[
C_M(p-1)\equiv b_{r_p}\pmod p,
\qquad
r_p=M-a(p-1),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where \(a\) is the fixed quotient label.  Therefore the block radical has the exact form

\[
\boxed{
R_{D,N}(C_M)
 =
 \prod_{\substack{D<p\le D+N\\
                    p\mid b_{M-a(p-1)}}}p.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equivalently, reindexing by the affine residue \(r=M-a(p-1)\),

\[
R_{D,N}(C_M)
 =
 \prod_{r\in\mathcal A_{M,D,N,a}}
 \gcd\left(\frac{M+a-r}{a},b_r\right),
\tag{3.3}
\]

where the product is restricted to those \(r\) for which \((M+a-r)/a\) is prime in the block.  Every factor in (3.3) is either \(1\) or that prime, and the prime factors are pairwise distinct.

Formula (3.2) makes the limit of finite-field periodicity explicit.  The torus moment identity reduces the exponent \(M\) modulo \(p-1\) and produces \(b_{r_p}\).  That is the entire available period reduction.  As \(p\) varies, both the field and the reduced exponent vary.  The product ring

\[
\mathbb Z/Q_{D,N}\mathbb Z
 \simeq
 \prod_{p\in\mathcal P_{D,N}}\mathbb F_p
\]

turns the moment family into exactly the independent coordinate vector in (2.5); it does not produce an additional norm or trace relation among those coordinates.

Sections 60 and 62 show that the scalar shell state has bounded order at most \(39\) after adjoining the origin coordinate.  That is valuable for constructing identities, but it does not alter the CRT description (2.5).  To use bounded order here one must prove a new arithmetic restriction on the **distinguished Apéry initial state across different characteristics**.

# 4. Why the complementary factorial factors cannot control the target sector

Write

\[
A_{D,N}=\frac{B_{D,N}}{Q_{D,N}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

By (2.2), \(A_{D,N}\) is coprime to the entire target modulus \(Q_{D,N}\).

Its prime factors have two types.

## 4.1 Large complementary primes are ghost-node selectors

If \(p>N\) divides \(A_{D,N}\), the unique numerator multiple in the interval is

\[
k_pp\in[D+1,D+N]
\]

with \(k_p\ge2\).  Proposition 1.1 gives

\[
\boxed{
G_{D-1,N}(Y)\equiv Y_{k_pp-1}\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus a reduction modulo the large-prime part of the complement does not evaluate a derivative, quotient, or second Witt digit at the target node \(p-1\).  It evaluates a **different shell node** \(k_pp-1\).

## 4.2 Small complementary primes have collided interpolation nodes

For \(p\le N\), the denominator \(N!\) has nontrivial \(p\)-valuation and several nodes in the stencil can coincide modulo \(p\).  Kummer's carry formula describes the valuation of the binomial, but the clean one-coordinate selector is lost.  These primes do not create a second target equation either; they are the ordinary small-prime/overlap content already separated in the previous ledger.

## 4.3 Exact coprime-component obstruction

The following elementary proposition rules out any proof that tries to infer a target bound from the complementary residue alone.

### Proposition 4.1

Fix \(D,N\) with \(D>N\).  Fix any residue class

\[
c\pmod {A_{D,N}}
\]

and any subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal P_{D,N}\).  There exists an integer \(g\) such that

\[
g\equiv c\pmod {A_{D,N}},
\]

\[
g\equiv0\pmod p\quad(p\in S),
\qquad
g\equiv1\pmod p\quad(p\in\mathcal P_{D,N}\setminus S).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\gcd(g,Q_{D,N})=\prod_{p\in S}p,
\tag{4.4}
\]

while the complementary residue is fixed.

### Proof

The moduli \(A_{D,N}\) and the distinct interval primes are pairwise coprime by (2.2).  Apply the Chinese remainder theorem.  ∎

This obstruction remains inside the Newton formalism.  Take the constant node sequence

\[
Y_j=g.
\]

The Newton weights sum to one, so

\[
G_{D-1,N}(Y)=g.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus the binomial valuation profile, every universal Newton identity, and the full complementary residue can be held fixed while the interval radical is an arbitrary subproduct of \(Q_{D,N}\).

This is **not** an Apéry counterexample: the constant sequence is not the distinguished shell.  It proves exactly what extra information a successful proof must use.  It must show that the actual Apéry CRT vector is not free in the manner of (4.3).  No theorem about the binomial factors themselves can do that.

Even a generic diagonal or constant-term hypothesis is insufficient.  A constant Laurent polynomial gives a constant shell sequence, and multiplying a Laurent polynomial by an integer scalar preserves its Newton support and scalar telescopers while inserting arbitrary common prime content.  The actual proof must use the primitive, normalized coefficients of the specific Apéry polynomial \(\Lambda\), not only the existence of a diagonal representation.

# 5. An actual Apéry ghost factor in the complementary binomial part

The complement is not merely formally independent; it carries genuine zeros of the distinguished Apéry shell.

Take

\[
M=146,\qquad p=73,\qquad M=2p+0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The Apéry value

\[
b_2=73
\tag{5.2}
\]

is divisible by \(p\).  In Section 61, choose

\[
a=2,\quad s=0,\quad r=1,\quad d=M-r=145.
\]

The Cartier zero-segment hypotheses hold:

\[
2s=0<1=r<p,\qquad d>M/2,\qquad p\mid b_ab_s=b_2b_0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
73\mid C_{146}(145).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Now take the Newton block

\[
D=141,\qquad N=5.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Its binomial is

\[
B_{141,5}=\binom{146}{5}.
\]

The numerator interval is \([142,146]\), whose unique multiple of \(73\) is

\[
146=2\cdot73.
\]

Thus \(73\) is a complementary ghost prime, not an interval prime.  Proposition 1.1 and (5.3) give

\[
\boxed{
G_{140,5}(C_{146})
 \equiv C_{146}(145)
 \equiv0\pmod {73}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Also

\[
v_{73}\binom{146}{5}=1.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This exact example disproves each of the following possible shortcuts:

- the complementary large-prime part is always a unit on the actual shell;
- the full gcd \(\gcd(G_{D-1,N}(C_M),B_{D,N})\) differs from the target radical only by small primes;
- a ghost factor must be a recurrence singularity rather than actual state content;
- reducing modulo the complement supplies a second condition on the interval target coordinates.

The example is the one-step Cartier state factor from Section 61 seen through the p-adic selector (1.5).

# 6. Why primitive-divisor and product-formula attempts stop here

## 6.1 Kummer valuation at an interval target is tautological

For every candidate prime \(D<p\le D+N\),

\[
v_p\binom{D+N}{N}=1.
\]

The prime occurs as the literal numerator factor \(p\), and no denominator factor is divisible by \(p\).  This valuation is identical for target and non-target primes.  Kummer carries therefore distinguish neither sector.

A higher-valuation argument would have to use \(G/p\bmod p\), not the binomial.  That requires a new Apéry-specific \(p^2\) identity whose divided residue is still forced to vanish on targets.  The existing fixed-order and Cartier identities do not provide such a zero.

## 6.2 The complementary factor gives aliases, not a product relation

For each large complementary prime, (4.2) reads a different node \(k_pp-1\).  The full large-prime gcd decomposes exactly into the products

\[
\prod_{k\ge1}
\prod_{\substack{p>N,\ kp\in[D+1,D+N]\\p\mid Y_{kp-1}}}p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The target radical is only the \(k=1\) factor.  Section 61 and the witness (5.5) show that the \(k\ge2\) factors can contain genuine Apéry state divisors.  Bounding the full product is therefore a strictly harder and contaminated problem, not a route to the \(k=1\) factor.

## 6.3 Finite-field period has already been spent

For \(Y=C_M\), the torus period modulo \(p\) gives exactly

\[
C_M(p-1)\equiv b_{M-a(p-1)}\pmod p.
\]

There is no remaining common exponent after this reduction.  Replacing the family of fields by the product ring modulo \(Q_{D,N}\) yields the CRT vector (2.5), not a scalar period or norm of smaller height.

## 6.4 No primitive-divisor exclusion follows from the size of the node

At a target, \(p\mid b_{r_p}\) with \(r_p<p\) in the relevant cells.  In the binomial sum for \(b_{r_p}\), the divisibility is cancellation among terms after the ordinary Kummer truncation.  The fact that \(p\) is a large or primitive divisor of the external binomial \(B_{D,N}\) imposes no primitive-divisor condition on \(b_{r_p}\).

# 7. The exact missing Apéry-specific theorem

After Sections 63 and 64, the surviving sector consists of unique low-label folded nodes.  On one mesoscopic fixed-label block, all known reductions leave the exact object

\[
\prod_{\substack{D<p\le D+N\\p\mid b_{M-a(p-1)}}}p.
\tag{7.1}
\]

A successful proof must add information of one of the following genuinely new forms.

## 7.1 A low-height Bézout or subresultant certificate

Construct explicit integers \(U,V,H\), depending target-blindly on \((M,D,N)\), such that

\[
U\,G_{D-1,N}(C_M)+V\,Q_{D,N}=H\ne0,
\tag{7.2}
\]

with

\[
\log|H|=o(N).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then \(R_{D,N}(C_M)\mid H\).  The load-bearing point is that \(H\) must use a distinguished Apéry identity; universal Newton transformations cannot produce it because of Proposition 4.1.

## 7.2 A cross-characteristic primitive-content restriction

Prove that the actual CRT vector

\[
\left(b_{M-a(p-1)}\bmod p\right)_{D<p\le D+N}
\tag{7.4}
\]

cannot vanish on a set of primes of logarithmic weight \(\gg N\).  This could come from a global resultant, a norm identity coupling different residue characteristics, or a new arithmetic theorem for the distinguished diagonal.  It cannot be a one-prime period or recurrence statement.

## 7.3 A target-forced divided digit

Find an integer observation \(H_p\), assembled globally over the block, for which

\[
p\mid C_M(p-1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p^2\mid H_p,
\tag{7.5}
\]

while the coefficient-forced binomial content contributes only one copy of \(p\).  After division, a genuinely new zero would remain.  The current prime-power shell jet has a free first Witt coordinate, so (7.5) is not available from existing identities.

These are not three cosmetic reformulations.  Each would couple the independently selected coordinates in (2.5), which is exactly the information absent from p-adic binomial valuation theory.

# 8. Dependency-free verification script

The following standard-library script checks:

- the exact weight factorization (1.2);
- the large-prime selector (1.5), including ghost multiples;
- the interval CRT identity (2.6);
- the coprime interval/complement split (2.2);
- the constant-state CRT countermodel;
- the Apéry top-cell shell congruence on small primes;
- the actual ghost factor \((M,p,D,N)=(146,73,141,5)\).

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
from functools import lru_cache
from math import comb, gcd, prod


@lru_cache(maxsize=None)
def C(n, k):
    if n < 0 or k < 0 or k > n:
        return 0
    return comb(n, k)


def primes_up_to(limit):
    sieve = bytearray(b"\x01") * (limit + 1)
    if limit >= 0:
        sieve[0] = 0
    if limit >= 1:
        sieve[1] = 0
    for p in range(2, int(limit**0.5) + 1):
        if sieve[p]:
            sieve[p * p : limit + 1 : p] = b"\x00" * (
                (limit - p * p) // p + 1
            )
    return [p for p in range(2, limit + 1) if sieve[p]]


def vp(value, prime):
    value = abs(value)
    exponent = 0
    while value and value % prime == 0:
        value //= prime
        exponent += 1
    return exponent


def weight(D, N, i):
    # G_{D-1,N}: node D-1+i.
    return (-1) ** i * C(D - 1 + i, i) * C(D + N, N - i)


def carrier(D, N, values):
    return sum(weight(D, N, i) * values[D - 1 + i]
               for i in range(N + 1))


def crt(congruences):
    modulus = prod(m for _, m in congruences)
    value = 0
    for residue, m in congruences:
        partial = modulus // m
        value += residue * partial * pow(partial, -1, m)
    return value % modulus


def audit_selector():
    for D in range(8, 36):
        for N in range(1, min(8, D - 1) + 1):
            B = C(D + N, N)
            values = {
                j: (j + 2) ** 5 + 3 ** j + 17 * j + 11
                for j in range(D - 1, D + N)
            }
            G = carrier(D, N, values)

            # Exact factorial form and primitive constant interpolation.
            for i in range(N + 1):
                assert weight(D, N, i) * (D + i) == (
                    (-1) ** i * B * D * C(N, i)
                )
            assert sum(weight(D, N, i) for i in range(N + 1)) == 1

            interval_primes = [
                p for p in primes_up_to(D + N) if D < p <= D + N
            ]
            Q = prod(interval_primes)
            assert B % Q == 0
            assert gcd(Q, B // Q) == 1

            expected = prod(
                p for p in interval_primes if values[p - 1] % p == 0
            )
            assert gcd(abs(G), Q) == expected

            # The matched Newton coefficient is the CRT idempotent.
            for p in interval_primes:
                matched = p - D
                for i in range(N + 1):
                    assert weight(D, N, i) % p == (1 if i == matched else 0)

            # General large-prime selector, including k >= 2 ghosts.
            for p in primes_up_to(D + N):
                if p <= N or B % p:
                    continue
                multiples = [m for m in range(D + 1, D + N + 1)
                             if m % p == 0]
                assert len(multiples) == 1
                m = multiples[0]
                assert G % p == values[m - 1] % p


def audit_coprime_component_countermodel():
    D, N = 100, 20
    B = C(D + N, N)
    interval_primes = [
        p for p in primes_up_to(D + N) if D < p <= D + N
    ]
    Q = prod(interval_primes)
    A = B // Q
    assert gcd(A, Q) == 1

    selected = set(interval_primes[::2])
    congruences = [(17 % A, A)] if A > 1 else []
    congruences += [
        (0 if p in selected else 1, p) for p in interval_primes
    ]
    g = crt(congruences)
    values = {j: g for j in range(D - 1, D + N)}

    assert carrier(D, N, values) == g
    assert gcd(g, Q) == prod(selected)
    if A > 1:
        assert g % A == 17 % A


def apery(n):
    return sum(C(n, k) ** 2 * C(n + k, k) ** 2 for k in range(n + 1))


def coefficient(M, u, v, w):
    # Formula (49.1) for [x^u y^v z^w] Lambda^M.
    return sum(
        C(M, t)
        * C(M, t - u)
        * C(2 * M - t, M - v)
        * C(2 * M - t, M - w)
        for t in range(M + 1)
    )


def shell(M, d):
    bound = M // d
    return sum(
        coefficient(M, d * x, d * y, d * z)
        for x in range(-bound, bound + 1)
        for y in range(-bound, bound + 1)
        for z in range(-bound, bound + 1)
    )


def audit_apery_shell():
    # Fixed-moment top-cell congruence C_{p+s}(p-1) = b_{s+1} mod p.
    for p in (5, 7, 11, 13):
        for s in range(min(4, p - 1)):
            assert shell(p + s, p - 1) % p == apery(s + 1) % p

    # Actual complementary ghost factor from the Cartier zero segment.
    M, p, D, N = 146, 73, 141, 5
    B = C(D + N, N)
    assert apery(2) == 73
    assert vp(B, p) == 1
    assert [m for m in range(D + 1, D + N + 1) if m % p == 0] == [146]
    assert not (D < p <= D + N)  # p is not an interval target prime.

    values = {d: shell(M, d) for d in range(D - 1, D + N)}
    assert values[145] % p == 0
    assert carrier(D, N, values) % p == 0


def main():
    audit_selector()
    audit_coprime_component_countermodel()
    audit_apery_shell()
    print("PASS: Newton--Kummer selector, CRT split, and Apéry ghost factor")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

The script deliberately audits the general selector on arbitrary non-polynomial data, so the identities are not interpolation accidents tied to a low-degree test sequence.

# 9. Final cut line

The direct p-adic attack reaches a complete structural answer but not the desired little-oh estimate.

What is proved:

\[
R_{D,N}(C_M)
 =\gcd\left(G_{D-1,N}(C_M),Q_{D,N}\right)
 =\prod_{D<p\le D+N,\ p\mid C_M(p-1)}p,
\]

and the Newton carrier modulo \(Q_{D,N}\) is exactly the CRT assembly of those shell residues.

What is ruled out:

- extracting a saving from the valuation \(v_p\binom{D+N}{N}\) at target primes;
- obtaining a second target equation from the complementary factorial factors;
- replacing the interval radical by the full binomial gcd;
- using finite-field period alone to couple different target primes;
- deducing a primitive-divisor exclusion from the fact that the target prime is a primitive numerator factor of the binomial.

What remains genuinely open:

> Prove a global arithmetic restriction on the distinguished Apéry CRT vector (7.4), or produce a nonzero target-divisible characteristic-zero certificate of logarithmic height \(o(N)\).

That is the exact extra Apéry-specific input missing after Sections 54, 56, and 60--64.
