## 9. Why the distinguished initial line gives no local corank

Let \(c\) be the companion solution with

\[
(c_0,c_1)=(0,1).
\]

Every solution with initial vector \((u_0,u_1)\) is

\[
u_n=u_0b_n+(u_1-5u_0)c_n.
\]

Hence evaluation at index \(x\) is the row functional

\[
R_x=(b_x-5c_x,c_x).
\tag{9.1}
\]

If \(N_h(x)=0\), the exact transfer identity gives

\[
R_{x+h}=\mu_h(x)R_x
\tag{9.2}
\]

for a nonzero universal multiplier \(\mu_h(x)\).  Therefore a two-gap common root makes

\[
R_x,\quad R_{x+d},\quad R_{x+d+e}
\]

all proportional.  This is the rank-one statement detected by the continuants.

The distinguished Apéry solution has initial vector \((1,5)\), and

\[
\det\!\begin{pmatrix}
 b_x-5c_x&c_x\\
 -5&1
\end{pmatrix}
=b_x.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
 b_x=0
 \iff
 R_x\text{ has the fixed direction }[-5:1].}
\tag{9.4}
\]

Equation (9.4) selects the direction of the already rank-one row.  It does not lower the rank again.

### 9.1 Projective-fiber equivariance

For every \(q\in\mathbb F_p\), define

\[
u^{(q)}=b-qc.
\]

The sequence \(u^{(q)}\) satisfies exactly the same recurrence.  If

\[
q=\frac{b_x}{c_x}
\]

(with \(q=\infty\) when \(c_x=0\)), then

\[
u^{(q)}_x=0,
\]

and every continuant return at \(x\) becomes a zero return of \(u^{(q)}\).  Therefore the common-root scheme carries a natural label map

\[
\lambda_p(x)=[b_x:c_x]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p),
\tag{9.5}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal A_p(H)
=\#\{(x,d,e)\in\mathscr X_{p,H}:
 \lambda_p(x)=[0:1]\}.
\tag{9.6}
\]

All local continuant equations are independent of \(\lambda_p(x)\).  The missing theorem is therefore a **horizontal anti-concentration theorem for the label map on the common-root incidence scheme**.

This explains the exact first failed implication:

\[
\boxed{
\text{common-root/subresultant information}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{anti-concentration of the label }[0:1].}
\tag{9.7}
\]

The repository's `distinguished_label_verify.py` proves the same point at the multiplier level: the companion-coordinate transport law is universal on actual and phantom projective chains, and multiplying along a chain reproduces only the direct transfer identity.

## 10. Reflection, Hasse, Green-kernel, and K3 routes

### 10.1 Reflection

Pointwise reflection gives

\[
\lambda_p(p-1-x)=\lambda_p(x).
\]

It preserves the distinguished label but does not randomize it.  After the reflection-degenerate deletion of §6, triples occur in free reflected pairs.  The gain is an absolute factor two, not a power.

Full-cycle multiplier reciprocity similarly produces a prescribed unit.  Crossing the singular index is rank one and cannot be inverted to manufacture a new zero condition.  The exact source theorem therefore rules out a second independent bridge equation from reflection.

### 10.2 Hasse-square factorization

The defining-characteristic Hasse section satisfies

\[
A_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2.
\]

However, the actual zeros \(b_x=0\) are coefficient conditions, not roots of \(A_p\).  `hasse_square_no_go.tex` proves triangular freedom: reciprocity and square factorization alone permit arbitrary early coefficient-zero patterns and their reflections.  Hence this factorization supplies no two-gap anti-concentration.

### 10.3 Green kernel and Cartier defect

The rank-four moving-gap Green kernel is a genuine fixed-order differential compression.  On the strict triangle its Frobenius defect is

\[
\mathscr D_aF_{p,a}^{\triangle}
=a^3-a^3b_{a-1}z^{p-a}.
\]

On the distinguished fiber \(b_{a-1}=0\), the defect vanishes.  But the source proves that this is tautological:

\[
V_m(a)=\frac{b_{a+m}}{b_a}
\]

on that fiber.  The truncated Green column is simply the normalized future Apéry row.  Direct polynomial models have degree \(\Omega(p)\), while exact Mellin/Cartier extraction moves through all coefficient indices.  No bounded-conductor label detector follows.

### 10.4 Toric/K3 collapse

The toric fiber count collapses to a K3 trace with a pointwise \(O(p)\) remainder, but

\[
b_j=-\sum_{a\ne0}\mu_p(a)a^j
\]

is its defining-characteristic Mellin coefficient.  A pointwise trace bound does not prevent exact cancellation of a Mellin coefficient modulo \(p\).  The source explicitly records this as an exact reformulation, not an anti-concentration theorem.

Thus every available distinguished construction ends at the same place: it identifies the label \(b_x=0\) exactly but gives no power-saving distribution theorem for that label on the two-gap common-root scheme.

## 11. Prime averaging and the height barrier

Let \(I\) be any interval of primes with \(p\asymp T\), and take

\[
H\le\sqrt T.
\]

For each fixed \((d,e)\), Smith normal form gives

\[
\sum_{p\in I}m_{d,e}(p)\log p
\le\log|\mathcal R_{d,e}|.
\tag{11.1}
\]

The source's Hadamard estimate is

\[
\log|\mathcal R_{d,e}|
\ll de\log(2H).
\tag{11.2}
\]

Since

\[
\sum_{\substack{d,e\ge2\\d+e\le H}}de
\asymp H^4,
\tag{11.3}
\]

one obtains only

\[
\sum_{p\in I}\mathcal A_p(H)
\le\sum_{p\in I}\mathcal E_p^{\rm proj}(H)
\ll H^4.
\tag{11.4}
\]

If a prime is critical when

\[
\mathcal A_p(H)\ge cH^{8/5},
\]

then (11.4) gives

\[
\#\{p\in I:\mathcal A_p(H)\ge cH^{8/5}\}
\ll H^{12/5}.
\tag{11.5}
\]

For Q5921,

\[
T=H^2,
\qquad
|I|\asymp T^{2/5}=H^{4/5},
\]

and the intended prime block has

\[
K\asymp\frac{H^{4/5}}{\log T}.
\tag{11.6}
\]

Thus the resultant average exceeds the desired count by the factor

\[
\boxed{H^{8/5}\log T.}
\tag{11.7}
\]

This is a power failure, not a removable logarithmic loss.

### 11.1 Why deflation does not change the exponent

Removing the exact projective-infinity lattice is mandatory, but it only removes false support.  Removing all forced central factors is justified after §6, but the center-deflated resultants still have logarithmic height of order \(de\log H\) in the balanced bulk.

On the diagonal, the square law

\[
\mathcal R_{d,d}=\ell_d|D_d|Q_d^2
\]

makes many supported primes occur with valuation at least two.  In prime averaging this divides the bound by two.  The source proves explicitly that this is a constant improvement, not an \(H\)-exponent saving.

### 11.2 The Q5921 aligned-chain height

The translated rectangle has natural internal gap scale

\[
D_*\asymp T^{2/5}=H^{4/5}.
\]

For a fixed positive anchor \(A\asymp T\),

\[
\log|N_d(A)|\ll d\log T.
\tag{11.8}
\]

A block of

\[
K\asymp D_*/\log T
\]

primes has radical logarithm

\[
K\log T\asymp D_*.
\tag{11.9}
\]

The one-segment height (11.8) is

\[
O(D_*\log T),
\]

so it misses the needed restricted-radical saving by one full factor \(\log T\).  Pair resultants have height \(O(D_*^2\log D_*)\) and are worse.  Multiplying all segment equations raises the required prime radical and the available height in parallel and produces no saving.

This is the exact logarithmic slack left after the Q5955 all-gap compatibility correction.

## 12. The bounded-codegree route

Let \(G_p(H)\) be the simple bipartite graph whose left and right vertices are gap lengths and whose edge \((d,e)\) is present when

\[
m_{d,e}(p)>0.

Give that edge weight \(m_{d,e}(p)\).

If the following two statements held uniformly in the hard core,

\[
m_{d,e}(p)\ll H^\mu,
\tag{12.1}

and every pair of left vertices had at most \(H^\kappa\) common right neighbors, then the Kővári--Sós--Turán bound would give

\[
\#E(G_p(H))
\ll H^{3/2+\kappa/2}+H.
\tag{12.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\mathcal E_p^{\rm proj}(H)
\ll H^{3/2+\mu+\kappa/2}.
\tag{12.3}

This beats \(H^{8/5}\) whenever

\[
\boxed{\mu+\frac\kappa2<\frac1{10}.}

## 13. The weakest non-equivalent block lemma

The actual two-gap theorem is pointwise in \(p\).  `(SBATR)` is weaker: it allows exceptional primes but forbids a critical number of them in a short interval.  The exact Q5921 rectangle is more special still: the same primes support two fixed translated chains.

This leads to a strictly weaker statement.

### 13.1 Translated double-chain rarity `(TDCR)`

Let

\[
W=T^{2/5},
\qquad
Z_*=T^{3/5},
\qquad
K_*=\frac{W}{\log T}.
\tag{13.1}
\]

Fix \(p_0\asymp T\), an interval

\[
I\subset[p_0-CW,p_0+CW],
\]

and two fixed integer sets

\[
\mathsf A=\{A_1<\cdots<A_m\},
\qquad
\mathsf R=\{R_1<\cdots<R_n\},
\tag{13.2}
\]

with

\[
m,n\ge cZ_*.
\]

For a prime \(p\in I\), define the translated digits

\[
a_i(p)=A_i-13p,
\qquad
r_j(p)=R_j-6p.
\tag{13.3}
\]

Assume the deterministic central margins make all these digits legal:

\[
0\le a_i(p),r_j(p)<p.
\]

The translated double-chain rarity statement is

\[
\boxed{
\#\left\{
 p\in I\cap\mathbb P:
 a_i(p)\in\mathcal Z_p\ \forall i,
 \ r_j(p)\in\mathcal Z_p\ \forall j
\right\}
=o\!\left(\frac{T^{2/5}}{\log T}\right).}
\tag{TDCR}
\]

This statement contains no upper prime \(q\), no upper zero \(t\), no selected-state mask, and no C4 threshold.  It concerns only recurrence compatibility of the lower translated block.

### 13.2 Exact all-gap carrier

Put

\[
d_i=A_{i+1}-A_i,
\qquad
s_j=R_{j+1}-R_j.
\]

Define the fixed integers

\[
\mathfrak C_A
=\gcd\bigl(
 b_{A_1},
 N_{d_1}(A_1),
 N_{d_2}(A_2),
 \ldots,
 N_{d_{m-1}}(A_{m-1})
 \bigr),
\tag{13.4}
\]

\[
\mathfrak C_R
=\gcd\bigl(
 b_{R_1},
 N_{s_1}(R_1),
 N_{s_2}(R_2),
 \ldots,
 N_{s_{n-1}}(R_{n-1})
 \bigr).
\tag{13.5}
\]

For all sufficiently large legal primes excluding the fixed divisors of \(b_{13}b_6\), Lucas gives

\[
b_{A_1}\equiv b_{13}b_{a_1(p)}\pmod p,
\qquad
b_{R_1}\equiv b_6b_{r_1(p)}\pmod p.
\tag{13.6}
\]

Also

\[
N_{d_i}(A_i)\equiv N_{d_i}(a_i(p))\pmod p,
\]

and similarly for \(R\).  The exact restart criterion therefore proves

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&a_i(p)\in\mathcal Z_p\quad\forall i
\\
&\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
p\mid\mathfrak C_A,
\end{aligned}}
\tag{13.7}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&r_j(p)\in\mathcal Z_p\quad\forall j
\\
&\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
p\mid\mathfrak C_R.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{13.8}
\]

Thus `(TDCR)` is exactly the restricted-radical statement

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{Rad}_{I}
 \gcd(\mathfrak C_A,\mathfrak C_R)
=o(T^{2/5}).}
\tag{13.9}
\]

Indeed, every prime in \(I\) has logarithm \(\asymp\log T\), and

\[
K_*\log T\asymp T^{2/5}.
\]

Equation (13.9) is a genuine arithmetic theorem about the common restricted radical of a long family of anchored continuant values.  Ordinary height proves only

\[
\log\operatorname{Rad}_{I}
 \gcd(\mathfrak C_A,\mathfrak C_R)
\ll T^{2/5}\log T,
\tag{13.10}
\]

because one of the \(m-1\) gaps is \(O(T/m)=O(T^{2/5})\).  Thus current height misses (13.9) by the exact logarithmic factor already identified in §11.2.

### 13.3 Why `(TDCR)` is strictly weaker than `(SBATR)`

Every prime counted in `(TDCR)` has at least

\[
cT^{3/5}
\]

actual zeros.  By the deterministic threshold calculation of §2,

\[
\mathcal A_p(\sqrt p)\gg T^{4/5}.
\]

Therefore `(SBATR)` implies `(TDCR)`.

The converse does not hold.  `(TDCR)` tests only one fixed pair of slope-13 and slope-6 chains shared by many primes.  A family may have \(K_*\) heavy primes whose actual zero sets are all unrelated, so that no fixed translated double chain is reused by more than one prime.  Such a profile violates `(SBATR)` while satisfying `(TDCR)`.  This is a logical separation of the quantified statements; it is not asserted to be a genuine Apéry construction.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{pointwise two-gap theorem}
\Longrightarrow
\text{SBATR}
\Longrightarrow
\text{TDCR},}
\tag{13.11}
\]

and neither reverse implication is formal.

### 13.4 Why `(TDCR)` is not C4 in disguise

`(TDCR)` deliberately omits:

```text
the upper prime q;
the selected upper digit t;
the condition q | b_t;
the plus/minus state inversion;
the C4 excess threshold;
the upper-state multiplicity ledger.
```

It counts lower translated blocks that may never be selected by any upper state.  Hence C4 may hold while `(TDCR)` fails on entirely unselected blocks.  Conversely, `(TDCR)` only kills the Q5921 shared-chain extremizer; it does not by itself control every possible C4 configuration.

This is the weakest clean non-equivalent arithmetic lemma isolated by the present attack.

## 14. A source-natural average lemma, if one wants to continue the energy route

For completeness, the first genuinely distinguished input on the direct energy route can be stated as a short-interval mean rather than a pointwise theorem.

Let \(I\) have length \(\asymp T^{2/5}\), and let

\[
H_p=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor.
\]

For some fixed \(\rho>0\), require

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in I\cap\mathbb P}
 \mathcal A_p^{\rm hard}(H_p;\xi)
\ll
\frac{T^{2/5}}{\log T}
 T^{4/5-\rho}.}
\tag{HSAEM}_\rho
\]

This is a **hard-core short-interval actual-energy mean**.  It is strictly weaker than a uniform pointwise \(H^{8/5-\eta}\) theorem because it allows sparse exceptional primes of arbitrarily large energy.  Markov immediately gives `(SBATR)`.

The arithmetic mechanism one would need is anti-concentration of the label map

\[
\lambda_p:\mathscr X_{p,H}^{\rm hard}	o\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)
\]

at the fixed label \([0:1]\).  No current projective-energy, Hasse, Green-kernel, K3, resultant, or reflection theorem supplies this.  Stating the mean directly is logically clean; claiming that the label is uniformly distributed would be a much stronger and presently unjustified premise.

`(HSAEM)_\rho` is stronger than `(SBATR)` but weaker than the pointwise theorem.  `(TDCR)` is weaker still and is tailored only to the translated block.
