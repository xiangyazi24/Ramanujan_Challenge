# 3. Why fixed Manin-symbol/congruence-module theory does not give BFH

The obstruction can be stated algebraically without appealing to dimensions alone.

## 3.1 The natural block matrix loses singleton labels

Let

\[
L_R=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2R)^3.
\]

By (1.6), \(L_R\kappa_r\in\mathbf Z\) for every \(r\in I_R\). Restrict the fixed pair of functionals to

\[
V_R:=\bigoplus_{r=R+1}^{2R}\mathbf Z\Omega_r
\simeq W_{2R}/W_R.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The integral evaluation map is

\[
\Phi_R:V_R\longrightarrow\mathbf Z^2,
\qquad
\Omega_r\longmapsto
\binom{b_r}{L_R\kappa_r}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For \(p>2R\), \(L_R\) is a \(p\)-unit. A fresh transverse hit at row \(r\) is exactly the statement that **the \(r\)-th column of \(\Phi_R\) is zero modulo \(p\)**.

But a zero column does not force a rank drop of the full \(2\times R\) matrix. If two other columns span \(\mathbf F_p^2\), then

\[
\operatorname{coker}(\Phi_R)\otimes\mathbf F_p=0
\]

although one labelled column is zero. In particular the ordinary determinantal/Fitting ideals of the global map forget the BFH singleton.

This is the exact analogue, in the modular principal-part setting, of the label-loss phenomenon in the earlier scalar-resultant audits.

To retain the label formally one can instead take the direct sum of one-column maps. Its torsion order is

\[
\prod_{r\in I_R}\gcd(b_r,L_R\kappa_r),
\]

whose \(p>2R\) part is exactly (V0). That construction is label-faithful but gives no compression: it is the BFH content itself.

So the problem is not “find a Smith normal form”. There are two choices:

- global SNF: small enough to be interesting, but loses singleton labels;
- direct-sum/labelled SNF: sees exactly the correct primes, but is the original rowwise content.

A successful congruence module must provide a **third** object: one global module in which a single zero column creates controlled torsion because of a new cross-label level-6 identity.

## 3.2 Fixed holomorphic spaces are too small in the wrong sense

The new exact Sage audit gives, at level \(6\),

The point is narrower: the obvious fixed holomorphic Eisenstein/cuspidal lattice is tiny and has only small-prime index. It does not see either observed fresh prime \(17\) or \(2237\).

This is exactly what the structural formula predicts. BFH is not asking for congruence of two fixed holomorphic eigenforms. It asks whether one fixed Eichler class vanishes simultaneously with the period functional on one of \(R\) **different principal parts** \(t^{-r}\).

## 3.3 Why a fixed Hecke algebra theorem does not automatically repair this

The usual finite-rank Hecke/congruence module can produce unbounded arithmetic through varying Hecke operators, so finite dimension alone is not a logical obstruction. What is missing here is more specific:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No proved identity identifies the pole-order label }r
\text{ with a Hecke operator }T_r
\text{ on one fixed integral cohomology lattice.}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The label comes from multiplication by the modular unit \(t^{-r}\), i.e. increasing cusp pole order. Once one enlarges the cohomology/principal-part lattice enough to contain all \(\Omega_{R+1},\ldots,\Omega_{2R}\), its rank is already \(\Theta(R)\).

Thus the standard fixed-level finite-dimensional congruence module does not supply an \(R\)-block determinant for (BFH). An \(R\)-dependent relative/weakly-holomorphic congruence module is allowed in principle, but then a new covolume/saturation theorem is required.

# 4. Modular-unit and Eichler denominator theorems remove only small primes

The modular unit and q-integrality are extremely useful for normalization but do not control the fresh numerator overlap.

From Q7623,

\[

\qquad

\[
\boxed{
\ell_1(W_M)
\subset \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,M)^{-3}\mathbf Z.

For \(f=\Omega_r\), this recovers the denominator theorem

\[
\operatorname{den}(\kappa_r)\mid\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,r)^3.

In the BFH range \(p>2R\), all these denominator factors are units. So modular-unit/Eichler denominator theorems give the exact clean localization one needs, but they have no mechanism for bounding

# 5. The exact Hadamard/diagonal law for \(\Xi\)

The fixed-source structure does give a clean generating function, but it is Hadamard rather than an ordinary modular product.

Let

\[
X(z)=\sum_{r\ge0}\Xi_rz^r.
\]

From

\[
\Xi_r-\Xi_{r-1}=-5g_rb_{r-1}
\]

and

\[
C(z)=\sum g_rz^r,
\qquad zF(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}b_{r-1}z^r,
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{
(1-z)X(z)=-1-5(C\odot zF)(z).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The Hadamard product has the diagonal/constant-term form

\[
\boxed{
(C\odot zF)(z)
=\operatorname{CT}_w
\left[
C(w)\frac zwF(z/w)
\right].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently, from the Casoratian formula,

\[
\boxed{
X(z)+1
=\theta_z^3\left((zF)\odot K-F\odot(zK)\right).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

These identities are exact.

They also explain why the most tempting Beukers/Picard-Fuchs compression fails. Although

\[
C=\frac1{F^2\sqrt\Delta}
\]

is built from the same period, (5.1) multiplies **equal-index coefficients**. It is not the coefficient series of an ordinary product in \(\mathbf Q(t,F,\theta F)\). Passing to the diagonal introduces a second variable. No fixed low-degree rational relation in the level-6 function field follows from (5.1).

C_{\rm CM}=14\zeta(3)-\frac{20\pi^3}{9\sqrt6}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Also

\[
b_n\sim B\rho^{-n}n^{-3/2},
\qquad
\rho=17-12\sqrt2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

This immediately makes the two-row determinant much smaller *relative to* \(b_mb_n\):

\[
D_{m,n}=b_m\kappa_n-b_n\kappa_m

cancels \(C_{\rm CM}\). Q7669 proves uniformly for distinct \(R\le m<n\le2R\)

\tag{6.4}

So CM asymptotics explain and quantify the quadratic barrier; they do not remove it.

# 7. Exact determinant carriers and the quadratic product no-go

For completeness, here is the exact arithmetic result that now closes the natural sparse determinant route.

Set

\[
D_{m,n}=b_m\kappa_n-b_n\kappa_m.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For fixed \(m\),

\[
\boxed{
n^3D_{m,n}-P(n-1)D_{m,n-1}+(n-1)^3D_{m,n-2}
=-5b_mg_n.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The adjacent determinant is

\[
\boxed{D_{n-1,n}=\frac{\Xi_n}{n^3}.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Let \(\widetilde D_{m,n}\) be the numerator after minimal rational denominator clearing. If \(p>\max(m,n)\) and an endpoint \(r\in\{m,n\}\) is a transverse target, then \(p\mid\widetilde D_{m,n}\), with the expected minimum valuation. If both endpoints are targets for the same prime, the valuations add.

Therefore a graph of determinant edges covering every row is a genuine singleton-sensitive product carrier.

However Q7669 proves from (6.4): if \(E_R\) is any \(O(R)\)-edge family on \(I_R\) with every vertex incident to an edge, then

\[
\sum_{\{m,n\}\in E_R}(m+n)
=\sum_{r\in I_R}r\deg_{E_R}(r)
\ge\sum_{r=R+1}^{2R}r
=\frac{3R^2+R}{2},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\log\prod_{\{m,n\}\in E_R}|\widetilde D_{m,n}|
\ge
\frac32\log(17+12\sqrt2)R^2-O(R\log R).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This rules out every sparse endpoint-cover **product** as an \(e^{o(R^2)}\) BFH carrier.

A gcd can discard the exponential bulk, but the canonical two-anchor gcd is exact:

\[
\widetilde D_{0,r}=u_r,
\]

\[
\widetilde D_{1,r}
=\frac{5u_r+36d_rb_r}{\gcd(d_r,5)},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(\widetilde D_{0,r},\widetilde D_{1,r})
=\gcd(u_r,36b_r).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus, away from \(2,3\), the determinant gcd is precisely the original row content. This is a useful normalization theorem, but using (7.5) as the “solution” would be tautological; I do not do that.

# 9. What a genuine modular-symbol solution would have to prove

The preceding analysis suggests a precise target that is stronger than BFH but not a tautological renaming of the row gcd product.

For

\[
V_R=W_{2R}/W_R
=\bigoplus_{r=R+1}^{2R}\mathbf Z\bar\Omega_r,
\tag{9.1}
\]

retain the fixed level-6 Eisenstein Eichler class \([U]\) and the fixed period functional \(\ell_0\). A successful modular-symbol/relative-cohomology construction should produce a **single** finite Hecke/Fricke-stable quotient

\[
\mathcal Q_R
\tag{9.2}
\]

functorially from

\[
(V_R,\ell_0,[U],t,W_6),
\]

without inserting \(\gcd(b_r,u_r)\) row by row.

The needed theorem is:

> **Eisenstein–Eichler jet saturation (EEJS).** There exists such a functorial \(\mathcal Q_R\) for which, for every prime \(p>2R\),
> \[
> \sum_{r=R+1}^{2R}
> \min\{v_p(b_r),v_p(u_r)\}
> \le
> \operatorname{length}_{\mathbf Z_p}
> (\mathcal Q_R\otimes\mathbf Z_p),
> \tag{9.3}
> \]
> and globally
> \[
> \log|\mathcal Q_R|=o(R^2).
> \tag{9.4}
> \]

Then (1.9) immediately yields BFH.

Why this is not tautological:

- the direct sum \(\bigoplus_r\mathbf Z/\gcd(b_r,u_r)\) is **not allowed** as the definition of \(\mathcal Q_R\);
- \(\mathcal Q_R\) must be constructed from the fixed Eichler class and the principal-part/Hecke/Fricke lattice before the common divisors are known;
- (9.3) asks for a new theorem that a labelled zero column forces torsion in this one cross-label module;
- (9.4) asks for a new global saturation/covolume estimate;
- the sparse determinant product no-go (7.4) proves that the most obvious endpoint graph cannot furnish such a module.

A particularly strong and useful version would give

\[
\log|\mathcal Q_R|=O(R\log^A R)
\]

for some fixed \(A\), but any \(o(R^2)\) bound suffices.

In classical language, the missing ingredient is therefore not merely a bound on a fixed congruence number. It is a **relative-cohomology congruence theorem uniform in cusp pole order**, coupling the \(R\) principal parts through the one fixed level-6 Eisenstein Eichler class.

# 10. Why the standard alternatives do not already imply EEJS

## 10.1 Manin symbols

Manin symbols model the fixed holomorphic/parabolic cohomology of \(\Gamma_0(6)\) efficiently. They can encode the cocycle of \(U\), but the vectors \(\Omega_r\) have pole order growing with \(r\). One must pass to relative/weakly-holomorphic cohomology with a cuspidal thickening of order \(2R\). The resulting integral principal-part piece has rank \(\Theta(R)\). No standard Manin-symbol denominator theorem supplies (9.3) for individual zero columns.

## 10.2 Hecke algebra congruence modules

A fixed Eisenstein-versus-cuspidal congruence ideal measures congruences among fixed Hecke eigensystems. BFH instead measures simultaneous vanishing of two period/Eichler pairings on \(t^{-r}\Psi\). No landed identity converts multiplication by \(t^{-r}\) into \(T_r\) on a fixed Hecke module. Thus a fixed congruence ideal has the wrong label mechanism.

## 10.3 Modular-unit denominators

They prove integrality of \(\Omega_r\) and the \(\operatorname{lcm}^3\) bound for \(\kappa_r\). For \(p>2R\) those denominators are units, so the theorem has already done all it can: it validates reduction modulo \(p\). It does not constrain the two numerator pairings to avoid simultaneous zero.

## 10.4 Fitting/block determinants

The global \(2\times R\) Fitting ideal loses singleton columns; the labelled direct sum is BFH itself. Sparse determinant edge covers retain singleton labels but Q7669 proves they have quadratic height. Hence an EEJS module must use a genuinely new cross-label cohomological relation rather than these standard presentations.
