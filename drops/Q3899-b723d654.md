\[

u=\frac{p-z-6a-7}{7}.

\tag{1.2}

Here the symbol in (1.2) is the ordinary upper digit \(u\): explicitly,

\[
\boxed{
u:=\frac{p-z-6a-7}{7}.}
\tag{1.3}

\[
\boxed{v=q-1-u=p+a.}

# 2. Exact two-test convolution

\[
(q,u),
\qquad
q\asymp_JT,
\qquad
0\le u<q,

q\mid b_u.

All filters only delete occurrences, so

\[
\boxed{
|\mathscr E_U(T)|

\ll_JT^{8/5}L^{-4/5}.

\tag{2.1}

For \(t>0\), define

\[
C(q,t):=
\#\left\{

 (q,u)\in\mathscr E_U(T),\\
 q+t\ \text{is a legal upper prime},\\

\tag{2.2}

This is the exact joint test

\[
u\in Z_q,

# 3. Brun--Titchmarsh and the exact height ledger

Fix a left occurrence \((q,u)\). The right prime lies in

\[
q+D<q_2\le q+2D.

\#\{q_2\ \text{prime}:q+D<q_2\le q+2D\}

\[

\ll_J
M_1(T)

# 5. Normalized quotients do not create an extra factor

At fixed \(n\), the upper tests are equivalent to

\[
q_1q_2\mid b_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Set

\[
B_{12}:=\frac{b_n}{q_1q_2},
\qquad
C_1:=\frac{b_{v_1}}{q_1}.

\[
\log B_{12}=O_J(T),

\log C_1=O_J(T).

Dividing by the two upper primes saves only \(2L+O_J(1)\) from an \(O(T)\) height. The only short component in (0.1) is \(N_h(v_1)\), and that yields exactly the already-used \(O((q_2-q_1)L)\) bound.

Dividing by all known upper divisors of \(b_n\) also does not pay unordered-pair multiplicity: one occurrence of a divisor of \(b_n\) cannot be reused as an independent height unit for every pair containing it.

# 6. Large-sieve barrier

Let \(I\) be a row interval of length \(N\asymp_JT\), and define

\[
r(n):=
\sum_{q\asymp_JT}\mathbf 1_{Z_q}(n\bmod q).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Dropping quotient-six and deletion filters only enlarges this count. Fourier inversion and Parseval give

\[
\mathbf 1_{Z_q}(n)
=
\frac{Z(q)}q
+
\frac1q\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}
\widehat{1_{Z_q}}(a)e^{2\pi ian/q},
\tag{6.2}
\]

\[
\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}|\widehat{1_{Z_q}}(a)|^2=qZ(q).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The ordinary large sieve therefore yields

\[
\sum_{n\in I}
\left|r(n)-\sum_q\frac{Z(q)}q\right|^2
\ll_J
(N+T^2)\sum_q\frac{Z(q)}q
\ll_JTM_1(T).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Including the mean,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in I}r(n)^2
\ll_J
TM_1(T)+\frac{M_1(T)^2}{T}
\ll_JT^{13/5}L^{-4/5}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The \(T^2\) term is the usual \(Q^2\) barrier for moduli of size \(T\). Localizing by prime gap and applying Brun--Titchmarsh improves logarithms to (3.2), but not the large-gap power. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to

\[
C(q,t)=|Z_q\cap(Z_{q+t}+6t)|
\]

returns the same \(O(D/L)\) maximum degree of the prime-gap graph.

# 7. Sharp upper-support obstruction

This subsection is intentionally scoped to the upper interfaces; it is not asserted to be an actual Apéry zero family.

Choose a short macroscopic prime interval

\[
I_Q=[\alpha T,(\alpha+\varepsilon)T]
\]

such that \(\bigcap_{q\in I_Q}[6q,7q)\) has length \(\asymp T\). Let \(\mathcal Q\) be the primes in \(I_Q\), so \(|\mathcal Q|\asymp T/L\). Put

\[
m:=\lfloor T^{3/5}L^{1/5}\rfloor.
\]

Choose \(m\) row indices \(n_j\) in an interior common interval, spaced by

\[
T/m=T^{2/5}L^{-1/5}.
\]

For each \(q\in\mathcal Q\), define

\[
\widetilde Z_q
:=
\{n_j-6q\}_j
\cup
\{q-1-(n_j-6q)\}_j.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The halves may be separated, so the support is reflection-closed and has no consecutive points. For every digit interval of length \(W\),

\[
|\widetilde Z_q\cap J|
\ll
1+\frac{W}{T^{2/5}L^{-1/5}}
\ll W^{2/3}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Also

\[
\max_q|\widetilde Z_q|\asymp m=o(T^{2/3}),
\]

\[
\sum_q|\widetilde Z_q|\asymp T^{8/5}L^{-4/5},
\]

and

\[
\sum_q|\widetilde Z_q|^2\asymp T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Every selected row \(n_j\) is hit by every \(q\in\mathcal Q\). Splitting \(I_Q\) into two separated macroscopic subintervals gives, for \(D\asymp T\),

\[
|\mathscr U(D)|
\asymp
m\left(\frac{T}{L}\right)^2
=T^{13/5}L^{-9/5},
\tag{7.4}
\]

which equals the scale \(M_1(T)D/L\) in (3.2). Thus the Brun--Titchmarsh convolution is saturated, including its logarithmic scale, by an affine-compatible upper support model satisfying every banked upper marginal and interval condition.

Therefore no argument using only reflection, no-consecutive-zeroes, upper zero moments, interval caps, prime spacing, and \(6q+u=n\) can sharpen (3.2). A gain must couple the upper tests to the lower \(p\)-arithmetic.

# 8. Exact Green-identity obstruction

The integral transfer over a gap \(g\) has the form

\[
D_g(x)b_{x+g}
=
N_g(x)b_{x+1}+E_g(x)b_x,
\tag{8.1}
\]

up to the fixed sign/normalization convention. With one common prime \(\ell\) dividing both endpoint values, the endpoint terms vanish in one residue field and the usual unit conditions force \(\ell\mid N_g(x)\). This is the valid lower mechanism.

For the upper pair set

\[
q:=q_1,
\qquad
q_2=q+t,
\qquad
x:=u_2,
\qquad
g:=u_1-u_2=6t.
\]

Write

\[
b_{u_1}=qB_1,
\qquad
b_{u_2}=(q+t)B_2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since \(0\le u_2<u_1<q<q+t\), this low-digit transfer is nonwrapping. Substitution gives

\[
D_g(u_2)qB_1
=
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+E_g(u_2)(q+t)B_2.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Modulo \(q\),

\[
\boxed{
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+tE_g(u_2)B_2
\equiv0\pmod q.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Modulo \(q+t\),

\[
\boxed{
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+tD_g(u_2)B_1
\equiv0\pmod{q+t},
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

up to a harmless sign. Neither prime is forced to divide \(N_g(u_2)\): the other term contains an uncontrolled normalized quotient. No-consecutive-zeroes does not make \(B_1\) or \(B_2\) vanish.

For the lower prime \(p\), both lower endpoint values vanish modulo the same prime, so the continuant condition is valid. This is the exact same-characteristic versus cross-characteristic distinction.

Generic elimination of \(B_1,B_2\) loses the short height because \(\log|B_i|=O_J(T)\). No bounded-height Bezout cancellation of these quotient terms is present in the current recurrence bank.

# 9. Moving-prime resultant is not bounded complexity

At fixed \(n\), the sampled functions

\[
F_n(q):=b_{n-6q},
\qquad
G_n(q):=b_{7q-n-1}
\]

are not bounded-degree polynomials in \(q\). In an interior range, the standard recurrence asymptotic

\[
b_m=c(17+12\sqrt2)^m m^{-3/2}(1+O(m^{-1}))
\]

gives, for every fixed \(r\ge1\),

\[
\frac{\Delta_q^rF_n(q)}{F_n(q)}
\longrightarrow
\left((17+12\sqrt2)^{-6}-1\right)^r\ne0,
\]

and

\[
\frac{\Delta_q^rG_n(q)}{G_n(q)}
\longrightarrow
\left((17+12\sqrt2)^7-1\right)^r\ne0.
\]

A polynomial of degree less than \(r\) has zero \(r\)-th forward difference. Thus no degree bound independent of \(n\) exists, and interpolation over the legal range has growing degree and macroscopic height. This rejects the ordinary resultant-in-the-moving-prime candidate, not every possible cross-characteristic identity.
