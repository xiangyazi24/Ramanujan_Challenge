\[

\[

\iff

\tag{7.1}

Reflection adds no independent condition:

\[

\iff

\tag{7.2}

\boxed{

is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{

\qquad
q_i\in\mathcal Q_6(n).

\tag{7.3}
\]

Therefore one cannot multiply two putative probabilities for the upper tests.  They are two selected prime divisors of one common Apéry value.

# 8. Ordinary large sieve and its exact barrier

The large-sieve formulation makes the obstruction quantitative.  Let \(I\) be an interval of row indices of length \(N\asymp_JT\), and define

\[
r(n):=
\sum_{q\asymp_JT}
\mathbf 1_{Z_q}(n\bmod q).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Dropping the quotient-six and deletion filters only enlarges this count.  Write

\[
\widehat{1_{Z_q}}(a)
:=
\sum_{z\in Z_q}e^{-2\pi iaz/q}.
\]

Fourier inversion gives

\[
\mathbf 1_{Z_q}(n)
=
\frac{Z(q)}q
+
\frac1q
\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}
\widehat{1_{Z_q}}(a)e^{2\pi ian/q}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}
|\widehat{1_{Z_q}}(a)|^2
=qZ(q).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Applying the ordinary large sieve to the nonzero frequencies in (8.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n\in I}
\left|
 r(n)-\sum_q\frac{Z(q)}q
\right|^2
&\ll_J
(N+T^2)
\sum_q\frac{Z(q)}q\\
&\ll_J
T^2\frac{M_1(T)}T\\
&\ll_J
T M_1(T).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.4}
\]

The mean term contributes

\[
N\left(\frac{M_1(T)}T\right)^2
\ll
\frac{M_1(T)^2}{T}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in I}r(n)^2
\ll_J
T M_1(T)+\frac{M_1(T)^2}{T}
\ll_J
T^{13/5}L^{-4/5}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_n\binom{r(n)}2
\ll_JT^{13/5}L^{-4/5}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

The \(T^2\) term in the large-sieve constant is the usual \(Q^2\) barrier for moduli \(q\asymp T\).  Equation (8.5) is not strong enough even before the lower-prime fibre \(\omega(H_{12})\) is inserted.  Localizing by the prime gap and using Brun--Titchmarsh gives the stronger logarithmic estimate (3.2), but no new power on large gaps.

The upper second-factorial marginal does not remove this barrier.  Parseval in (8.3) naturally produces \(\sum Z(q)/q\), and a Cauchy--Schwarz treatment of

\[
C(q,t)=|Z_q\cap(Z_{q+t}+6t)|
\]

leads back to the maximum degree of the prime-gap graph, which is \(O(D/L)\), and hence again to (3.2).

\frac{T}{m}
=T^{2/5}L^{-1/5},
\tag{9.2}
\]

and with all low digits bounded away from \(0\), the midpoint, and \(q-1\).  For each \(q\in\mathcal Q\), define the synthetic reflection-closed support

\[
\widetilde Z_q
:=
\{n_j-6q:1\le j\le m\}
\cup
\{q-1-(n_j-6q):1\le j\le m\}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The two halves lie in separated digit intervals.  The spacing in (9.2) gives no consecutive points.  For every interval of digit length \(W\),

\[
|\widetilde Z_q\cap J|
\ll
1+\frac{W}{T^{2/5}L^{-1/5}}
\ll W^{2/3}
\qquad(1\le W\le T).
\tag{9.4}
\]

Thus the arbitrary-interval zero cap is satisfied.  Moreover,

\[
\max_q|\widetilde Z_q|
\asymp m
=o(T^{2/3}),
\tag{9.5}
\]

\[
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}|\widetilde Z_q|
\asymp
\frac{T}{L}m
=
T^{8/5}L^{-4/5},
\tag{9.6}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}
|\widetilde Z_q|^2
\asymp
\frac{T}{L}m^2
=
T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}.
\tag{9.7}
\]

Hence the pointwise, first-moment, second-factorial, reflection, no-consecutive, and interval-support inputs all hold at their banked scales.

For every selected row \(n_j\), every prime \(q\in\mathcal Q\) is an upper hit, because

\[
n_j-6q\in\widetilde Z_q.
\]

Split \(I_Q\) into two disjoint macroscopic subintervals.  Pairs with one prime in each subinterval have

\[
q_2-q_1\asymp T,
\]

and their number is

\[
\asymp
m\left(\frac{T}{L}\right)^2
=
T^{13/5}L^{-9/5}.
\tag{9.8}
\]

On a macroscopic gap block, the right side of (3.2) is

\[
M_1(T)\frac{D}{L}
\asymp
T^{8/5}L^{-4/5}\frac{T}{L}
=
T^{13/5}L^{-9/5}.
\tag{9.9}
\]

Thus (3.2) is saturated, including its logarithmic scale, by an affine-compatible upper support model satisfying every listed upper marginal and interval condition.  The model may be placed in the interior digit region, so the upper-side close-boundary and small-digit deletions do not disturb it.

Therefore no argument which uses the upper tests only through

```text
reflection closure;
no consecutive zeroes;
pointwise and arbitrary-interval zero caps;
first and second upper zero moments;
prime spacing / Brun--Titchmarsh;
and the affine equality 6q+u=n
```

can improve (3.2).  Any improvement must use additional arithmetic coupling to the lower \(p\)-conditions; it cannot come from an upper marginal convolution alone.

# 10. Exact failure of the ordinary Green/continuant transfer at the upper primes

The recurrence transfer over a gap \(g\) has an integral form

\[
D_g(x)b_{x+g}
=
N_g(x)b_{x+1}+E_g(x)b_x,
\tag{10.1}
\]

where \(D_g\) clears the recurrence denominators and \(N_g\) is the usual continuant numerator, up to the fixed normalization convention.  When one prime \(\ell\) divides both \(b_x\) and \(b_{x+g}\), and the intervening denominator and \(b_{x+1}\) are units modulo \(\ell\), (10.1) forces

\[
\ell\mid N_g(x).
\]

That is the one-characteristic mechanism used by the lower carrier.

For the two upper tests, set

\[
q:=q_1,
\qquad
q_2=q+t,
\qquad
x:=u_2,
\qquad
g:=u_1-u_2=6t.
\]

Write the exact normalized quotients

\[
b_{u_1}=qB_1,
\qquad
b_{u_2}=(q+t)B_2.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Since

\[
0\le u_2<u_1<q<q+t,
\]

the transfer from \(u_2\) to \(u_1\) is in the ordinary nonwrapping range for both upper primes.  Substituting (10.2) into (10.1) gives

\[
D_g(u_2)qB_1
=
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+E_g(u_2)(q+t)B_2.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Reducing modulo \(q\),

\[
\boxed{
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+tE_g(u_2)B_2
\equiv0\pmod q.
}
\tag{10.4}
\]

Reducing modulo \(q+t\),

\[
\boxed{
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+tD_g(u_2)B_1
\equiv0\pmod{q+t},
}
\tag{10.5}
\]

up to the harmless sign convention for \(E_g\).

Equations (10.4)--(10.5) are the exact obstruction.  Neither upper prime is forced to divide \(N_g(u_2)\): the other term contains an uncontrolled normalized quotient.  The facts

\[
q\nmid t,
\qquad
q+t\nmid t
\]

make the modulus shift visible rather than eliminating it.  No-consecutive-zeroes controls \(b_{u_2+1}\) modulo \(q+t\), but it does not make the quotient terms vanish.

For the lower prime \(p\), both secondary lower zeroes \(a_1,a_2=a_1+7t\) vanish modulo the **same** prime.  The analogue of both quotient terms therefore vanishes in one residue field, and the continuant condition follows.  This is precisely the distinction between the valid lower \(N_h\)-carrier and the invalid proposed upper one.

Eliminating \(B_1,B_2\) from (10.4)--(10.5) by a generic resultant does not preserve short height.  The quotients satisfy only

\[
\log|B_i|=O_J(T),
\]

so a naive elimination reintroduces the full macroscopic Apéry-value height.  No bounded-height Bezout identity cancelling these quotient terms is present in the banked recurrence theory.

## 10.1 No bounded-degree resultant in the moving upper prime

At fixed \(n\), the tempting sampled functions are

\[
F_n(q):=b_{n-6q},
\qquad
G_n(q):=b_{7q-n-1}.
\tag{10.6}
\]

They are not bounded-degree polynomials in \(q\).  In an interior range where both indices tend to infinity, the standard Poincaré asymptotic for the Apéry recurrence,

\[
b_m=c\,(17+12\sqrt2)^m m^{-3/2}(1+O(m^{-1})),
\]

gives, for every fixed \(r\ge1\),

\[
\frac{\Delta_q^rF_n(q)}{F_n(q)}
\longrightarrow
\left((17+12\sqrt2)^{-6}-1\right)^r\ne0,
\tag{10.7}
\]

and

\[
\frac{\Delta_q^rG_n(q)}{G_n(q)}
\longrightarrow
\left((17+12\sqrt2)^7-1\right)^r\ne0.
\tag{10.8}
\]

A polynomial of degree less than \(r\) has zero \(r\)-th forward difference.  Hence no degree bound independent of \(n\) is possible.  Interpolation on the \(\asymp T\)-long legal integer range has growing degree and full macroscopic coefficient height; it is not a short-transfer resultant.

This rejects exactly the candidate method “take a resultant of the two upper tests as functions of the moving prime.”  It does not rule out a different, genuinely cross-characteristic Apéry identity.

# 11. What has and has not been proved

The positive unconditional result is (V1)--(V5): the exact two-upper convolution, together with the exact \(H_{12}\)-height, kills all dyadic gaps below

\[
T^{2/5}L^{-7/10}\Omega(T)^{-1/2}
\]

up to an arbitrary divergent auxiliary factor.  This is a full-multiplicity logarithmic estimate.

After the existing close-gap deletion, the same method gives only the blockwise envelope

\[
\mathcal S_-(D)
\ll_J
\min\left\{
T^{8/5}D^2L^{-4/5},
T^{43/15}L^{2/5}
\right\},
\]

which is supercritical on every remaining polynomial gap block beginning at \(D\ge T^{2/5+o(1)}\).

The exact scoped obstruction is:

1. at fixed \(n\), the two upper tests collapse to \(q_1q_2\mid b_n\);
2. Brun--Titchmarsh gives (3.2), and the upper support model in Section 9 saturates it while satisfying all current upper marginals;
3. the ordinary large sieve stops at its \(Q^2\) barrier (8.5);
4. forgetting the lower prime has an unavoidable \(O(D)\) fibre paid by \(\log H_{12}\);
5. the Green identity at the two upper primes contains the uncontrolled quotient terms in (10.4)--(10.5), because the moduli are \(q\) and \(q+t\), not one common characteristic.

Accordingly, the requested full little-\(o\) estimate is **not obtained** by the reindexing/BT/large-sieve/normalized-quotient/ordinary-continuant route.  The first arithmetic step not supplied by that route would have to cancel the quotient terms in (10.4)--(10.5) while simultaneously retaining the lower \(p\)-multiplicity encoded by \(H_{12}\).  This statement identifies the failed algebraic mechanism; it is not a renamed assumption and is not used as a premise anywhere above.
