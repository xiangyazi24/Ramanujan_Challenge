# 6. Ordinary large sieve and the \(Q^2\) barrier

\[
\mathbf 1_{Z_q}(n)

+

\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}
\widehat{1_{Z_q}}(a)e^{2\pi ian/q},
\tag{6.2}

with Parseval

\[
\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}
|\widehat{1_{Z_q}}(a)|^2
=qZ(q).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The ordinary large sieve gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n\in I}
\left|
r(n)-\sum_q\frac{Z(q)}q
\right|^2
&\ll_J
(N+T^2)
\sum_q\frac{Z(q)}q\\
&\ll_J
T M_1(T).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}

The mean contributes \(O(M_1(T)^2/T)\), so

\[
\boxed{

# 8. Exact Green-identity obstruction

The recurrence transfer over a gap \(g\) has an integral form

\[
D_g(x)b_{x+g}
=
N_g(x)b_{x+1}+E_g(x)b_x,
\tag{8.1}
\]

where \(D_g\) clears the recurrence denominators and \(N_g\) is the continuant numerator, up to the fixed normalization convention.

When one prime \(\ell\) divides both \(b_x\) and \(b_{x+g}\), the endpoint terms vanish in one residue field and the usual unit conditions force \(\ell\mid N_g(x)\). This is the valid same-characteristic mechanism used by the lower carrier.

For the upper pair put

\[
q:=q_1,
\qquad
q_2=q+t,
\qquad
x:=u_2,
\qquad
g:=u_1-u_2=6t.
\]

Write

\[
b_{u_1}=qB_1,
\qquad
b_{u_2}=(q+t)B_2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since

\[
0\le u_2<u_1<q<q+t,
\]

the low-digit transfer is nonwrapping for both upper primes. Substitution into (8.1) gives

\[
D_g(u_2)qB_1
=
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+E_g(u_2)(q+t)B_2.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Modulo \(q\),

\[
\boxed{
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+tE_g(u_2)B_2
\equiv0\pmod q.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Modulo \(q+t\),

\[
\boxed{
N_g(u_2)b_{u_2+1}
+tD_g(u_2)B_1
\equiv0\pmod{q+t},
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

up to the harmless sign convention for \(E_g\).

Neither upper prime is forced to divide \(N_g(u_2)\). The other term contains an uncontrolled normalized quotient. No-consecutive-zeroes controls the adjacent sequence value in the relevant characteristic, but it does not make \(B_1\) or \(B_2\) vanish.

For the lower prime \(p\), both secondary lower zeroes vanish modulo the same prime, so both endpoint terms disappear in one field and the continuant condition follows. This is the precise difference between the valid lower \(N_h\)-carrier and the invalid proposed upper one.

A generic elimination of \(B_1,B_2\) loses the short-transfer height because

\[
\log|B_i|=O_J(T).
\]

No bounded-height Bezout identity cancelling these quotient terms is present in the current recurrence bank.

# 9. No bounded-degree resultant in the moving upper prime

At fixed \(n\), the tempting sampled functions are

\[
F_n(q):=b_{n-6q},
\qquad
G_n(q):=b_{7q-n-1}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

They are not bounded-degree polynomials in \(q\). In an interior range, the standard Poincare asymptotic for the Apéry recurrence,

\[
b_m=c(17+12\sqrt2)^m m^{-3/2}(1+O(m^{-1})),
\]

gives, for every fixed \(r\ge1\),

\[
\frac{\Delta_q^rF_n(q)}{F_n(q)}
\longrightarrow
\left((17+12\sqrt2)^{-6}-1\right)^r\ne0,
\tag{9.2}
\]

and

\[
\frac{\Delta_q^rG_n(q)}{G_n(q)}
\longrightarrow
\left((17+12\sqrt2)^7-1\right)^r\ne0.
\tag{9.3}
\]

A polynomial of degree less than \(r\) has zero \(r\)-th forward difference. Hence no degree bound independent of \(n\) is possible. Interpolation over the legal range has growing degree and macroscopic coefficient height, so it is not a short-transfer resultant.

This rejects exactly the candidate method of treating the two moving upper tests as bounded-complexity polynomials in \(q\). It does not exclude a different cross-characteristic identity.
