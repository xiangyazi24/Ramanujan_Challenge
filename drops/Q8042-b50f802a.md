# P3.2 shell polynomial: arithmetic-determinant attack and scoped no-go

## Decisive verdict

The common shell polynomial is a genuine structural gain, but the package stated in Q8042 is not enough to prove seven-prime nonalignment.

Let $Q_X$ be the primitive integer shell polynomial of degree $X$, and suppose that for window primes it has the exact support law

$$
p divides Q_X(m)  iff  m mod p lies in Z_X(p),   for X < p ≤ 2X.
$$

All generic routes tested below stop at the same numerical barrier:

$$
q = p_1 p_2 ... p_7 ≍ X^7,     M = X^2,     q/M ≍ X^5.
$$

For one seven-prime modulus, knowing all modular roots is not enough. One must show that their CRT lifts land in the initial interval $[0,M)$ with density $M/q = X^{-5}$. Degree, content, height, reflection, squarefree good reduction, and exact prime support do not force this horizontal placement.

This is proved by an explicit no-go family. There are reflected, monic, primitive polynomials $Q_X^*$ with all of the following properties:

- degree exactly $X$;
- coefficient height exp$(O(X))$;
- content $1$;
- full-degree squarefree reduction at every retained window prime;
- exactly two reflected roots at every retained prime;
- exact evaluated-gcd support;
- one common integer $m_0 < X^2$ hit by every retained window prime.

For this family the seventh factorial moment exceeds the independence target by $X^{5-o(1)}$. A three-term polynomial in the reflected coordinate has the same obstruction, although its degree drops modulo the target primes. Thus abstract rank-three or sparse-carrier structure also does not suffice.

The exact surviving target is the seven-prime short-CRT discrepancy in Section 10. A concrete additional Apéry-specific property that would close the route is a bounded-degree horizontal eliminant in the target prime: after primitive normalization, every hit $(m,p)$ would have to satisfy one nonzero exact integer equation of degree at most six in $p$. Such an eliminant preserves the common $m$ and the actual target primes. No such relation follows from the generic shell-polynomial package.

## 1. Exact seven-prime target and the 20/21 exponent

Set

$$
P_X = {p prime : X < p ≤ 2X},     M = X^2,
$$

$$
z_p = Z_X(p)/p,     λ_X = Σ_{p in P_X} z_p,
$$

and

$$
K_X(m) = #{p in P_X : p divides Q_X(m)}.
$$

For ordered distinct primes, define

$$
N_X(p_1,...,p_7)
 = #{0 ≤ m < M : p_i divides Q_X(m) for every i}.
$$

Then the seventh factorial moment is exactly

$$
S_7(X)
 := Σ_{m<M} (K_X(m))_7
 = Σ_{ordered distinct p_1,...,p_7} N_X(p_1,...,p_7).
$$

The required averaged nonalignment statement is

$$
S_7(X) ≤ X^{2+o(1)} λ_X^7.                     [STAR7]
$$

Suppose the available one-prime estimate is

$$
Z_X(p) ≤ p^{2/3+o(1)}.
$$

Then $λ_X ≤ X^{2/3+o(1)}$. If $R=K_X(m)$, then $(R)_7 ≤ S_7(X)$, and STAR7 gives

$$
R ≤ S_7(X)^{1/7}+6
  ≤ X^{2/7+o(1)} λ_X
  ≤ X^{20/21+o(1)}.
$$

The exponent is exactly

$$
2/7 + 2/3 = 20/21.
$$

### Exact pointwise carrier gap

Write $d = deg Q_X = X$ and $H_X = max_j |[T^j]Q_X|$. For $0 ≤ m < M$ with $Q_X(m) ≠ 0$,

$$
|Q_X(m)| ≤ (d+1) H_X (M+1)^d.
$$

Under the height normalization used by the carrier argument, namely

$$
log H_X = o(X log X),
$$

we obtain

$$
log |Q_X(m)| ≤ (2+o(1)) X log X.
$$

Every supported window prime contributes at least $log X$, hence

$$
K_X(m) log X
 ≤ Σ_{p in P_X, p divides Q_X(m)} log p
 ≤ log |Q_X(m)|,
$$

so

$$
K_X(m) ≤ (2+o(1))X.
$$

Thus the quoted $O(X log X)$ carrier is the logarithm of the window radical; in prime-count form it is $O(X)$. Its exponent misses $20/21$ by exactly

$$
1 - 20/21 = 1/21.
$$

Raising the value or a resultant to a bounded power cannot alter this ratio: both the divisibility lower bound and the archimedean upper bound are multiplied by that power.

## 2. The short-CRT scale that must be saved

Fix seven distinct window primes and put

$$
q = p_1 p_2 ... p_7 ≍ X^7.
$$

Let

$$
Z_X(q) = {a mod q : Q_X(a) ≡ 0 mod q}.
$$

CRT gives

$$
ρ_X(q) := |Z_X(q)| = Π_{i=1}^7 Z_X(p_i).
$$

Since $q>M$, each residue modulo $q$ has at most one representative in $[0,M)$. Therefore

$$
N_X(p_1,...,p_7) = # ( Z_X(q) intersect [0,M) ).
$$

The independence main term is

$$
M ρ_X(q)/q.
$$

Keeping only the modular root count gives $ρ_X(q)$. The ratio is

$$
q/M ≍ X^5.
$$

This is the exact saving every proposed method must produce. The common polynomial compresses seven congruences into one congruence modulo $q$, but it does not locate the CRT roots in a segment of relative length $X^{-5}$.

There is an exact Fourier identity. Define

$$
D_M(h/q) = Σ_{m<M} exp(-2π i hm/q),
$$

$$
F_q(h) = Σ_{a in Z_X(q)} exp(2π i ha/q).
$$

Then

$$
N_X(q) = q^{-1} Σ_{h mod q} D_M(h/q) F_q(h).
$$

The zero frequency is $Mρ_X(q)/q$. Since $M<q$, Parseval gives

$$
Σ_h |D_M(h/q)|^2 = qM,
$$

$$
Σ_h |F_q(h)|^2 = qρ_X(q).
$$

Bare Cauchy therefore gives only

$$
|N_X(q)-Mρ_X(q)/q| ≤ sqrt(Mρ_X(q)).
$$

The missing theorem is cancellation of nonzero frequencies jointly over seven changing characteristics, not another use of Parseval.

## 3. Bombieri-Pila and Heath-Brown with the actual parameters

For a fixed seven-prime product $q$, divisibility is represented by the irreducible affine graph

$$
C_q : qY = Q_X(T).
$$

It is irreducible because it is linear in $Y$. Its degree is

$$
d = X.
$$

The horizontal range is $B_T=M=X^2$. The vertical range satisfies

$$
B_Y ≤ (X+1)H_X(M+1)^X/q
    = exp((2+o(1))X log X)/X^7.
$$

Bombieri-Pila gives, for fixed degree and fixed positive epsilon, an affine curve bound of the shape

$$
N_C(B) ≤ constant(d,epsilon) B^{1/d+epsilon}.
$$

Even after ignoring the serious nonuniformity caused by $d=X$, the formal main exponent is

$$
B_Y^{1/X}
 = exp((log B_Y)/X)
 = X^{2+o(1)} q^{-1/X}
 = X^{2+o(1)},
$$

because

$$
q^{-1/X} = X^{-7/X} = 1-o(1).
$$

Thus the theorem returns the entire interval scale $M$, with no power saving. Taking epsilon equal to $1/X$ is not a uniform application: the constant is not controlled in that regime. The exponent calculation already fails before constants are considered.

Heath-Brown's projective curve scale formally contains $B^{2/d+epsilon}$. Homogenizing the graph and inserting the same vertical height gives

$$
B_Y^{2/X}=X^{4+o(1)},
$$

which is worse. An anisotropic determinant construction can recover the natural graph scale $X^2$, but the divisor $q=X^7$ still enters only through $q^{-1/X}$.

Writing

$$
Q_X(m)=y p_1...p_7
$$

and treating the primes as variables creates one equation in nine variables, with degree $X$ in $m$. The extra dimension destroys rather than improves the count; fixing the primes returns the graph above.

**Determinant-method verdict:** a divisor with logarithm $7 log X$ is negligible after division by the growing curve degree $X$, while the value height has logarithm about $2X log X$. No $X^{-5}$ factor emerges.

## 4. Larger sieve, polynomial large sieve, and additive duality

### 4.1 Gallagher larger sieve

Gallagher's theorem states that if $A subset [1,N]$ occupies at most $g(p)$ residue classes modulo each $p$ in a set $S$, then, when the denominator is positive,

$$
|A| ≤
( Σ_{p in S} log p - log N )
/
( Σ_{p in S} (log p)/g(p) - log N ).
$$

For one seven-prime intersection, substitute

$$
N=X^2,     |S|=7,     g(p)=Z_X(p).
$$

At $Z_X(p) ≤ X^{2/3+o(1)}$,

$$
Σ_{p in S} (log p)/g(p) - log N
≤ 7X^{-2/3+o(1)} log(2X) - 2 log X < 0.
$$

The theorem does not apply.

Even in the strongest two-root case $g(p)=2$, it gives only an $O(1)$ bound for each seven-prime tuple. One common anchor is fully compatible with that conclusion. Summing $O(1)$ over about $(X/log X)^7$ ordered tuples loses $X^5$ relative to $Mλ^7$ when $λ ≍ 1/log X$.

Modern larger-sieve refinements still bound the cardinality of a set ill-distributed modulo many primes. They cannot exclude a singleton lying in the designated classes for every prime, while a seventh factorial moment is extremely sensitive to exactly such a singleton.

### 4.2 Additive large sieve

For one modulus $q ≍ X^7$, distinct fractions $a/q$ have spacing $1/q$. The dual additive large sieve has constant

$$
M+q ≍ X^7,
$$

which is a factor $q/M=X^5$ above the interval scale.

Combining all seven-prime products directly is worse: fractions with denominators $q,q' ≍ X^7$ may be separated only by $1/(qq') ≍ X^{-14}$, leading to a generic all-moduli constant of size $X^{14}$. Tensor and CRT structure are necessary to avoid this $X^{12}$ loss, but generic tensor orthogonality merely returns to the single-modulus $X^5$ barrier.

### 4.3 Polynomial large sieve and inverse sieve

Polynomial value-set large sieves normally hold a fixed-degree polynomial fixed while the prime varies. Here

$$
deg Q_X=X,
$$

and the polynomial changes with $X$. More importantly, one-prime root counts do not address where the product root set lands inside a segment of relative length $X^{-5}$. The counterfamily below has only two roots per prime and still has maximal alignment.

Inverse-sieve results that force an ill-distributed set onto low-degree algebraic structure are circular here: the set is already described by one polynomial, and the obstruction may consist of one point.

## 5. Generic gcd-of-values theorems do not preserve the target

Gcd-of-values theorems gain from two independent algebraic objects, such as coprime fixed polynomials or multiplicatively independent recurrence sequences. Their algebraic input is a nonzero resultant or a comparable nondegeneracy condition.

Here the proposed common object is the same polynomial at the same integer:

$$
gcd(Q_X(m),Q_X(m))=|Q_X(m)|,
$$

and

$$
Res(Q_X,Q_X)=0.
$$

There are only three obvious repairs.

1. Replace one factor by $Q_X(T+h)$. For $h ≠ 0$, a target prime dividing $Q_X(m)$ need not divide $Q_X(m+h)$, so the common $m$ is lost. For $h=0$, the resultant is zero.
2. Use a second shell polynomial. That would be genuinely new Apéry-specific input; the one-polynomial package gives no coprimality or uniformly small nonzero resultant.
3. Use different evaluations $m_i$. This controls primes distributed among different integers, not seven primes dividing one value at a common integer.

Primitive normalization does not repair the problem. The strong counterfamily is monic and has content $1$, so no coefficient gcd is responsible for its alignment.

## 6. Divided differences and evaluation determinants

A Vandermonde or evaluation determinant extracts information from distinct points. Seven characteristics hitting one common integer give seven copies of the same evaluation vector

$$
(1,m,m^2,...,m^X).
$$

The determinant is identically zero.

The confluent replacement uses derivatives, but

$$
p divides Q_X(m)
$$

does not imply divisibility of $Q_X'(m),Q_X''(m)$, or higher derivatives. In the strong counterfamily every retained reduction is squarefree and both modular roots are simple. At its roots the derivative is nonzero modulo $p$, so the derivative channel is specifically unavailable.

Choosing distinct lifts $m+p_i t_i$ makes a Vandermonde nonzero, but it changes one common integer into seven different integers. Reimposing the congruence that ties those lifts to one $m$ recreates the modulus $q=p_1...p_7$ and the $q/M=X^5$ short-CRT loss.

Also, one entry in row $i$ being divisible by $p_i$ does not make the whole determinant divisible by $p_i$. A product divisibility requires a whole-row rank defect or an equivalent modular relation. The scalar equation $Q_X(m) ≡ 0 mod p_i$ supplies the same scalar relation seven times, not seven independent rows.

## 7. Multi-evaluation resultants and subresultants

For an integer $m$,

$$
Res_T(Q_X(T),T-m)=Q_X(m).
$$

Repeating the evaluation seven times gives

$$
Res_T(Q_X(T),(T-m)^7)=Q_X(m)^7.
$$

This multiplies the seven-prime divisibility lower bound by seven and also multiplies the archimedean upper bound by seven. The exponent gap is unchanged.

For distinct integers $m_1,...,m_7$,

$$
Res_T(Q_X(T), Π_{j=1}^7(T-m_j)) = Π_{j=1}^7 Q_X(m_j),
$$

which no longer concerns one common $m$.

Principal subresultants of $Q_X$ and $Q_X'$ detect repeated roots and discriminant divisibility. Simple roots supply no such divisibility. The strong no-go family has squarefree reductions, so a discriminant route cannot classify it as bad reduction.

A multi-evaluation subresultant is therefore either:

- a bounded power of the original carrier;
- an object involving different integers;
- or an object requiring derivative divisibility not present in the target.

## 8. Scoped no-go: a reflected monic good-reduction family

### Theorem 8.1

For every sufficiently large even $X$, there exist an integer polynomial $Q_X^*(T)$, an integer $m_0$ with $0≤m_0<X^2$, and a set

$$
P_X^circ subset {p prime : X<p≤2X}
$$

obtained by deleting only $O(1)$ primes, such that:

1. $Q_X^*$ is monic of degree $X$, hence primitive with content $1$.
2. $Q_X^*(-T-1)=Q_X^*(T)$.
3. $log H(Q_X^*)=O(X)$.
4. For every retained prime, the reduction has degree $X$, is squarefree, and has exactly the roots $m_0$ and $-m_0-1$ modulo $p$.
5. With $P=Π_{p in P_X^circ}p$, every integer $m$ satisfies

$$
gcd(Q_X^*(m),P)
 = Π_{p in P_X^circ, m ≡ m_0 or -m_0-1 mod p} p.
$$

6. $Q_X^*(m_0)≠0$, and every retained prime divides this one value.

Consequently, with

$$
K_X^*(m)=#{p in P_X^circ : p divides Q_X^*(m)},
$$

$$
λ_X^*=2 Σ_{p in P_X^circ} 1/p,
$$

we have

$$
Σ_{m<X^2}(K_X^*(m))_7
 ≥ (|P_X^circ|)_7,
$$

and

$$
[Σ_{m<X^2}(K_X^*(m))_7] / [X^2(λ_X^*)^7]
 ≥ X^{5-o(1)}.
$$

### Construction

Put

$$
M=X^2,     m_0=3M/4,     U=T(T+1),
$$

$$
U_0=m_0(m_0+1),     D=X/2.
$$

Delete window primes dividing $2m_0+1$. If endpoint avoidance and nonadjacency are desired, also delete primes dividing

$$
(m_0-1)m_0(m_0+1)(m_0+2).
$$

Only $O(1)$ primes are removed: the product has size $X^{O(1)}$, while every removed prime exceeds $X$.

Let

$$
P=Π_{p in P_X^circ}p.
$$

For every retained prime choose a monic squarefree polynomial

$$
h_p(U) in F_p[U]
$$

of degree $D-1$ with no root in $F_p$. Such a polynomial can be built from distinct irreducible quadratics and, when needed, one irreducible cubic.

Use coefficientwise CRT on the lower coefficients to construct a monic integer polynomial $H_X(U)$ of degree $D-1$ such that

$$
H_X(U) ≡ h_p(U) mod p
$$

for every retained prime, with each nonleading coefficient represented in $[-P/2,P/2]$.

Define

$$
Q_X^*(T) = (U-U_0+P) H_X(U),     U=T(T+1).             [19]
$$

### Verification of the properties

The degree in $U$ is $D$ and both factors are monic. Hence $Q_X^*$ is monic of degree $2D=X$ in $T$. Reflection follows from

$$
(-T-1)(-T)=T(T+1).
$$

For the height, every prime in $(X,2X]$ divides the central binomial coefficient $C(2X,X)$, so

$$
P ≤ C(2X,X) < 4^X.
$$

Thus $log P=O(X)$. The $U$-basis height after multiplying by $U-U_0+P$ is $O(P^2+U_0P)$. Expanding

$$
U^j=T^j(T+1)^j
$$

costs at most $2^j$ with $j≤D$. Therefore

$$
log H(Q_X^*)=O(log P+D+log U_0)=O(X).
$$

Modulo a retained prime,

$$
Q_X^*(T) ≡ (U-U_0) h_p(U) mod p.                       [20]
$$

The cofactor has no $F_p$ root, so the roots are exactly the solutions of

$$
T(T+1)=m_0(m_0+1),
$$

namely $T=m_0$ and $T=-m_0-1$. They are distinct because $p$ does not divide $2m_0+1$.

The first factor is simple. The composed factor $h_p(U(T))$ is squarefree: $h_p$ is squarefree, and the only critical value of $U(T)$ is $-1/4$, an element of $F_p$ and therefore not a root of root-free $h_p$. It is coprime to $U-U_0$ because $h_p(U_0)≠0$. This proves full-degree squarefree good reduction.

Equation [20] gives exact root support and hence the exact gcd identity. At the anchor,

$$
Q_X^*(m_0)=P H_X(U_0).
$$

For each retained prime,

$$
H_X(U_0) ≡ h_p(U_0) ≠ 0 mod p.
$$

Thus the value is nonzero and its gcd with $P$ is exactly $P$.

Finally, the prime number theorem and Mertens' theorem give

$$
|P_X^circ| ≍ X/log X,
$$

$$
λ_X^* ≍ 1/log X.
$$

The anchor contributes $(|P_X^circ|)_7 ≍ X^7/(log X)^7$, while $X^2(λ_X^*)^7 ≍ X^2/(log X)^7$. Their ratio is $X^{5+o(1)}$.

### Scope

This construction is not asserted to be the actual Apéry polynomial. It proves the following implication is invalid:

> degree $X$ + primitive content + height exp$(O(X))$ + reflection + full-degree squarefree good reduction + exact small root sets + exact evaluated-gcd support  ⇒  STAR7.

A successful proof must use some additional property of the actual Apéry coefficients that excludes [19].

## 9. A three-term reflected carrier already fails

With the same $U,U_0,P,D$, define

$$
Q_X^{(3)}(T)=P U^D + U-U_0+P.                          [21]
$$

It has exactly three nonzero coefficients in the $U$ basis. It has degree $X$, content $1$, reflection, and height exp$(O(X))$. Modulo every retained prime,

$$
Q_X^{(3)}(T) ≡ U-U_0 mod p,

so it has the same exact two-root support and common anchor. Also

$$
Q_X^{(3)}(m_0)=P(U_0^D+1)≠0.

Its only weaker feature is degree drop modulo each target prime. The monic construction [19] proves that full-degree squarefree reduction does not save the generic package either.

## 10. Exact actual-structure reduction

For a squarefree seven-prime product $q$, put

$$
ρ_X(q)=Π_{p divides q} Z_X(p),
$$

$$
N_X(q)=#{0≤m<M : Q_X(m)≡0 mod q}.
$$

The exact missing estimate is the one-sided short-CRT discrepancy

$$
Σ_{ordered distinct p_1,...,p_7}
max(0, N_X(p_1...p_7) - Mρ_X(p_1...p_7)/(p_1...p_7))
≤ M X^{o(1)} λ_X^7.                                    [S7-CRT]
$$

The ordered convention counts a squarefree product with the same $7!$ multiplicity as the factorial moment. The total main term is at most

$$
M λ_X^7.
$$

Therefore S7-CRT implies STAR7.

Equivalently, for each tuple of modular roots, let $a_X(r_1,...,r_7;p_1,...,p_7)$ be its least nonnegative CRT lift modulo $q$. The theorem must control the positive excess of

$$
1_{a_X<M}
$$

above its expected value $M/q$, averaged over the actual seven-prime root tuples.

This reduction preserves both items that fixed-gap and shifted-value arguments tend to destroy:

- one common integer $m$;
- the actual seven target characteristics.

It is a mixed-characteristic distribution theorem for CRT lifts of the actual Apéry roots.

## 11. One additional Apéry-specific coefficient property that would close the route

A concrete sufficient property is the following.

### Horizontal eliminant HORIZ-6

After primitive normalization, suppose the actual coefficient/carrier algebra produces integer functions $A_{0,X}(m),...,A_{6,X}(m)$ such that

$$
H_X(m,P)
 = A_{0,X}(m)+A_{1,X}(m)P+...+A_{6,X}(m)P^6
$$

has these properties:

1. for every $0≤m<X^2$ and every target prime $p$,

$$
p divides Q_X(m)  implies  H_X(m,p)=0
$$

as an exact integer equality, not merely modulo $p$;

2. for every $m$, $H_X(m,P)$ is not the zero polynomial;
3. any exceptional $m$ for which all seven coefficients vanish is absent or controlled separately at the STAR7 scale.

Then a fixed $m$ admits at most six target primes, because a nonzero degree-six polynomial has at most six integer roots. Hence

$$
(K_X(m))_7=0
$$

outside the controlled exceptional set.

This is the precise kind of coefficient property that would turn a rank-three carrier into a determinant or elimination proof. Rank three alone is insufficient by [21]. What is needed is a nonzero bounded-prime-degree horizontal minor after all contents and denominators have been removed.

A weaker fixed-total-degree bivariate equation could be attacked by fixed-degree determinant methods with constants independent of $X$, but if its degree in $p$ exceeds six an averaged point estimate would still be needed. Degree six is exactly adapted to a seven-star.

An analytic alternative would be a compatible bounded-conductor horizontal trace family whose sevenfold tensor has no constant constituent, together with a cross-characteristic large sieve at length $X^2$. That is stronger and less concrete than HORIZ-6.

## 12. Why a shell cofactor average is not enough

Let

$$
P_X=Π_{p in P_X}p,
$$

$$
G_X(m)=gcd(Q_X(m),P_X).
$$

Exact support gives the exact first-moment identity

$$
Σ_{m<M} log G_X(m)
 = Σ_{p in P_X} log p · #{m<M : p divides Q_X(m)}.
$$

For one prime,

$$
#{m<M : p divides Q_X(m)}
 = MZ_X(p)/p + O(Z_X(p)).
$$

This controls an average logarithmic carrier, not the seventh factorial moment.

The counterfamily makes the distinction sharp. Its one anchor contributes only

$$
log P = O(X)
$$

to the first moment, negligible on an interval of length $X^2$, but it contributes

$$
(|P_X^circ|)_7 ≍ X^7/(log X)^7
$$

to the seventh moment. No first-moment cofactor average can exclude such concentration.

A useful cofactor theorem would have to be a high-moment anti-concentration theorem at the same $m$, essentially S7-CRT rather than an average size estimate for $G_X(m)$.

## 14. Merge-safe lemmas

### Lemma A: exponent ledger

Under $Z_X(p)≤p^{2/3+o(1)}$, STAR7 implies

$$
max_{m<X^2}K_X(m)≤X^{20/21+o(1)}.
$$

The degree-height carrier yields only $K_X(m)=O(X)$, leaving exactly $1/21$ in the exponent.

### Lemma B: short-CRT barrier

For seven window primes, $q≍X^7$ and $M=X^2$. Passing from modular root count to the independent interval count requires a factor $M/q=X^{-5}$.

### Lemma C: determinant ceiling

For $qY=Q_X(T)$, degree $d=X$ and value height exp$((2+o(1))X log X)$, the formal Bombieri-Pila scale is

$$
B_Y^{1/d}=X^{2+o(1)}.
$$

The divisor $q=X^7$ changes this only by $q^{-1/X}=1-o(1)$.

### Lemma D: scoped no-go

Family [19] satisfies degree $X$, monicity/content $1$, height exp$(O(X))$, reflection, full-degree squarefree good reduction, exact two-root support, and exact evaluated-gcd support, yet violates STAR7 by $X^{5-o(1)}$.

### Lemma E: exact missing estimate

The actual shell-polynomial route closes if S7-CRT holds. S7-CRT is exactly the positive excess of seven-prime CRT roots in the initial interval $[0,X^2)$, retaining the common $m$ and every target prime.

---

## Final ruling

The common polynomial $Q_X$ compresses the seven congruences but does not solve the $X^5$ CRT-placement problem. At degree $X$, none of the tested standard methods recovers that factor. The reflected monic family proves that the stated generic hypotheses cannot recover it.

The next valid arithmetic attack must establish an Apéry-specific horizontal property absent from [19]. The cleanest finite target is HORIZ-6, derived from the actual rank-three carrier by a nonvanishing bounded-degree eliminant in the target prime. Without that, the honest analytic target is S7-CRT. Calling S7-CRT a polynomial large sieve, a determinant estimate, or a cofactor average does not reduce its arithmetic content.
