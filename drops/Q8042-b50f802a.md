# P3.2 shell polynomial: arithmetic-determinant attack and scoped no-go

## Decisive verdict

The common shell polynomial is a real structural gain, but the package stated in the question is not enough to prove seven-prime nonalignment.

Let \(Q_X\in\mathbf Z[T]\) be the shell polynomial, of degree \(X\), and suppose its primitive normalization has controlled height and the exact support law

\[
 p\mid Q_X(m)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad mmod p\in Z_X(p)
 \qquad(X<p\le 2X).
\]

The determinant method, Bombieri--Pila/Heath--Brown, the ordinary and larger sieves, generic gcd-of-values theorems, divided differences, and multi-evaluation resultants all stop at the same barrier:

\[
 q=p_1\cdots p_7\asymp X^7,\qquad M=X^2,\qquad rac qM\asymp X^5.
\]

For a fixed seven-prime modulus, the modular root tuples must be shown to land in the initial interval \([0,M)\) with density \(M/q=X^{-5}\). Degree, coefficient content, coefficient height, reflection, and exact prime support do not force that horizontal placement.

This is not just a failure to find the right inequality. There is an explicit reflected, monic, primitive family \(Q_X^*\) with

- degree exactly \(X\);
- coefficient height \(\exp(O(X))\);
- content \(1\);
- full-degree squarefree reduction at every retained window prime;
- exactly two reflected roots at every such prime;
- exact evaluated-gcd support;
- one common integer \(m_0<X^2\) hit by every retained window prime.

For that family the seven-star exceeds the independence target by \(X^{5+o(1)}\). A still sparser three-term carrier has the same obstruction, though its degree drops modulo the target primes. Thus even abstract rank-three/sparse-carrier structure does not repair the argument.

The exact surviving arithmetic target is the seven-prime short-CRT discrepancy stated in Section 10 below. A concrete extra Apéry-specific property that would close it is a **bounded-degree horizontal eliminant in the target prime**: after primitive normalization, every hit \((m,p)\) must satisfy one nonzero exact integer equation of degree at most six in \(p\). That would preserve the common \(m\) and the actual target primes and would make elementary root counting, hence the seven-star bound, immediate. No such horizontal eliminant follows from the stated shell-polynomial package.

Recent refinements of the larger sieve still bound the cardinality of a set that is ill-distributed modulo many primes. They cannot rule out a singleton that lies in the designated classes for every prime. A seventh factorial moment is sensitive to exactly such a singleton.

### 4.2 Additive large sieve

For one modulus \(q\asymp X^7\), distinct fractions \(a/q\) have spacing \(1/q\). The dual additive large sieve therefore has constant

\[
 M+q\asymp X^7,
\]

which is a factor

\[
 rac qM=X^5
\]

above the interval scale. This is not a bookkeeping accident: it is the same ratio as (7).

Combining all seven-prime products directly is worse. Fractions with distinct denominators \(q,q'\asymp X^7\) may be only \(1/(qq')\asymp X^{-14}\) apart, giving the generic all-moduli constant \(M+X^{14}\). One must use the tensor/CRT structure to avoid that \(X^{12}\) loss, but the tensor structure alone only returns to the single-\(q\) \(X^5\) barrier.

### 4.3 Polynomial large sieve/inverse sieve

Polynomial large-sieve theorems for value sets are useful when a fixed-degree polynomial is held fixed while the prime varies. Here

\[
 \deg Q_X=X
\]

and the polynomial itself changes with \(X\). More importantly, the desired statistic is not the number of roots modulo one prime; it is the location of the CRT product of seven root sets inside a segment of relative length \(X^{-5}\). The reflected counterfamily in Section 8 has the smallest possible nontrivial root sets and still has maximal alignment. Therefore no polynomial-sieve theorem whose hypotheses are only degree, height, content, and one-prime root counts can imply STAR7.

Inverse-sieve conclusions that an ill-distributed set lies on low-degree algebraic structure are also circular here: the set is already cut out by one polynomial, and the obstruction can be a single point.

## 5. Gcd of polynomial values does not preserve the target

Known gcd-of-values results gain from two independent algebraic objects, for example coprime fixed polynomials \(F,G\), or multiplicatively independent recurrence sequences. Their algebraic input is a nonzero resultant or a Subspace-Theorem nondegeneracy condition.

Here the proposed common object is the same polynomial at the same integer:

\[
 \gcd(Q_X(m),Q_X(m))=|Q_X(m)|,
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{Res}(Q_X,Q_X)=0.
\]

There are only three possible repairs, and each loses the problem.

1. **Use \(Q_X(T+h)\).** For \(h
e0\), a target prime dividing \(Q_X(m)\) need not divide \(Q_X(m+h)\); the common \(m\) is lost. For \(h=0\), the resultant is zero.
2. **Use a second shell polynomial.** This would be a genuinely new Apéry-specific input. Nothing in the one-polynomial package supplies coprimality or a nonzero uniformly small resultant.
3. **Use different evaluations \(m_i\).** Then one bounds primes distributed among different integers, not seven primes dividing the value at one common integer.

Primitive normalization does not help. The counterfamily below is monic and has coefficient content \(1\), so no fixed coefficient divisor is responsible for the alignment.

## 6. Divided differences and evaluation determinants

A Vandermonde/evaluation determinant extracts information from distinct points. Seven characteristics hitting one common integer give seven copies of the same evaluation vector

\[
 (1,m,m^2,\ldots,m^X).
\]

The corresponding determinant is identically zero.

The confluent replacement introduces derivatives. But

\[
 p\mid Q_X(m)
\]

implies no divisibility of \(Q_X'(m),Q_X''(m),\ldots\). In the strong counterfamily below every retained reduction is squarefree and the two modular roots are simple, so in fact

\[
 p
mid Q_X'(m)\pmod p
\]

at a root. The derivative channel is deliberately absent.

Choosing distinct lifts \(m+p_it_i\) makes a Vandermonde nonzero, but it turns one common integer into seven different integers. Restoring the congruence that ties those lifts to one \(m\) recreates a modulus \(q=p_1\cdots p_7\) and hence the \(q/M=X^5\) short-CRT loss.

A determinant in which only one entry of row \(i\) is divisible by \(p_i\) is not automatically divisible by \(p_i\). To obtain the product \(p_1\cdots p_7\), an entire row/column or an appropriate rank defect must occur modulo each \(p_i\). The scalar congruence \(Q_X(m)\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\) supplies only the same scalar relation seven times.

## 11. One additional Apéry-specific coefficient property that would close the route

A concrete sufficient property is the following.

## 12. Why the shell cofactor average is not enough

Let

\[
 P_X=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_X}p,\qquad
 G_X(m)=\gcd(Q_X(m),P_X).
\]

The exact support law gives the exact first-moment identity

\[
 \sum_{m<M}\log G_X(m)
 =\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\log p\;\#\{m<M:p\mid Q_X(m)\}.
 	ag{24}
\]

For one prime,

\[
 \#\{m<M:p\mid Q_X(m)\}
 =rac{M}{p}Z_X(p)+O(Z_X(p)).
\]

Thus (24) controls an average logarithmic carrier. It does not control

\[
 \sum_m(K_X(m))_7.
\]

The counterfamily makes the distinction sharp. One anchor contributes only

\[
 \log P_X^\circ=O(X)
\]

to (24), negligible compared with an \(X^2\)-length first moment, but contributes

\[
 (|\mathcal P_X^\circ|)_7=X^{7+o(1)}
\]

to the seventh factorial moment. No first-moment cofactor average can exclude this concentration.

A useful cofactor theorem would therefore have to be a high-moment or anti-concentration statement at a common \(m\), essentially S7-CRT itself, rather than an average size estimate for \(G_X(m)\).

## 14. Merge-safe lemmas and final ruling

### Lemma A: exact exponent ledger

Under \(Z_X(p)\ll p^{2/3+o(1)}\), STAR7 implies

\[
 \max_{m<X^2}K_X(m)\ll X^{20/21+o(1)}.
\]

The degree-height carrier gives only \(K_X(m)=O(X)\), so the missing exponent is \(1/21\).

### Lemma B: short-CRT barrier

For seven window primes, \(q\asymp X^7\) and \(M=X^2\). Passing from the CRT root count to the independent interval count requires a factor

\[
 M/q=X^{-5}.
\]

Generic additive duality has constant \(M+q\), and generic root counting has no mechanism for this factor.

### Lemma C: determinant-method ceiling

For \(qY=Q_X(T)\), degree \(d=X\) and value height \(\exp((2+o(1))X\log X)\), the formal Bombieri--Pila scale is

\[
 B_Y^{1/d}=X^{2+o(1)};
\]

the divisor \(q=X^7\) changes this only by \(q^{-1/X}=1-o(1)\). Hence no power saving follows.

### Lemma D: scoped no-go

The family (19) satisfies degree \(X\), monicity/content \(1\), height \(\exp(O(X))\), reflection, full-degree squarefree good reduction, exact two-root support, and exact evaluated-gcd support, but violates STAR7 by \(X^{5-o(1)}\).

### Lemma E: precise missing estimate

The actual shell-polynomial route closes if S7-CRT holds. This estimate is exactly the positive excess of seven-prime CRT roots in the initial interval \([0,X^2)\), with the common \(m\) and all target primes preserved.

### Final ruling

The common polynomial \(Q_X\) is not, by itself, the missing seven-prime theorem. It compresses the congruences but leaves an \(X^5\) CRT-placement problem. None of the tested standard methods recovers that factor at degree \(X\), and the reflected monic counterfamily proves that the stated generic hypotheses cannot do so.

The next valid arithmetic attack must prove an Apéry-specific horizontal property not shared by (19). The cleanest finite target is HORIZ-6, derived from the actual rank-three carrier by a nonvanishing bounded-degree eliminant in the target prime. Failing that, the honest analytic target is S7-CRT; renaming it as a polynomial large sieve or determinant estimate does not reduce its content.
