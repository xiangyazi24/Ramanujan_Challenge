## 1. Exact affine elimination in the four sign sectors

For one extension with secondary lower zero `a`, the two signs give the following formulas.

### Plus

From

\[
p=2z+a+u+2
\]

one gets

\[
u_+=p-2z-a-2,
\]

and substitution into the stated formula for `q` gives

\[
q_+=\frac{13z+7a+6u_++13}{6}
    =p+\frac{z+a+1}{6}.
\]

Its reflected upper residue is

\[
\bar u_+=q_+-1-u_+=\frac{13z+7a+7}{6}.
\]

### Minus

From

\[
p=z+6a+7u+7
\]

one gets

\[
u_-=\frac{p-z-6a-7}{7},
\]

and hence

\[
q_-=z+7a+8u_-+8
    =p+\frac{p-z+a}{7}.
\]

Its reflected upper residue is especially simple:

\[
\bar u_-=q_--1-u_-=p+a.
\]

Both signs satisfy the same two identities

\[
\boxed{u=7p-6q-z-1},
\qquad
\boxed{\bar u=7q-7p+z},
\]

and therefore the same row identity

\[
\boxed{6q+u=7p-1-z.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Now sort the three distinct lower zeros as

\[
x,\qquad x+h,\qquad x+h+k,
\qquad h,k>0.
\]

Write

\[
z=x+s,\qquad a_i=x+t_i,
\]

where `(s,t_1,t_2)` is a permutation of `(0,h,h+k)`. For a fixed placement, the sign formulas become

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_+(p,x;s,t)&=p+\frac{2x+s+t+1}{6},\\
u_+(p,x;s,t)&=p-3x-2s-t-2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.2+}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_-(p,x;s,t)&=p+\frac{p+t-s}{7},\\
u_-(p,x;s,t)&=\frac{p-7x-s-6t-7}{7}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.2-}
\]

In particular, a plus upper prime is affine in `x`, while a minus upper prime is independent of `x` once `(p,h,k,s,t)` is fixed.

Let `Delta q=q_2-q_1`. Equation (1.1) gives, in every sector,

\[
\boxed{u_2-u_1=-6\Delta q.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The exact secondary-zero differences are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{ordered sector}&a_2-a_1\\ \hline
++&6(q_2-q_1),\\
--&7(q_2-q_1),\\
+-&7q_2-6q_1-2p+2z+1,\\
-+&6q_2-7q_1+2p-2z-1.
\end{array}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The mixed sectors transpose under exchanging the two extensions. For unordered pairs one may impose any fixed canonical ordering; this changes only a bounded multiplicity. There are only

\[
3\cdot2\cdot4=24
\]

ordered placement/sign types.

## 2. The exact lower common-root eliminant

Use the canonical transfer numerators

\[
N_0(X)=0,\qquad N_1(X)=1,
\]

\[
N_{m+1}(X)=P(X+m)N_m(X)-(X+m)^6N_{m-1}(X),
\tag{2.1}
\]

where

\[
P(X)=34X^3+51X^2+27X+5.
\]

The current transfer lemma gives, on the legal lower window,

\[
p\mid b_r,\quad p\mid b_{r+m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_m(r)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Consequently the sorted triple of actual lower zeros gives the two adjacent equations

\[
N_h(x)\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad
N_k(x+h)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Set

\[
f_h(X)=N_h(X),
\qquad
g_{h,k}(X)=N_k(X+h),
\]

and define the fixed-gap scalar

\[
S_{h,k}=\operatorname{Res}_X(f_h,g_{h,k}).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The root-strip separation already established for these continuants gives

\[
S_{h,k}\ne0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Let

\[
c_p(h,k)=
\#\{x\in\mathbf F_p:
N_h(x)=N_k(x+h)=0\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{c_p(h,k)\le v_p(S_{h,k}).}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Here is the determinant proof. Reduce the fixed-degree Sylvester matrix modulo `p`. Evaluation at every distinct common root annihilates the image of the Sylvester map. The evaluation functionals at distinct roots are linearly independent by the Vandermonde determinant, so the mod-`p` corank is at least `c_p(h,k)`. Smith normal form over `Z_p` then gives

\[
v_p(\det \operatorname{Syl}(f_h,g_{h,k}))
\ge \operatorname{corank}_{\mathbf F_p}
      \operatorname{Syl}(f_h,g_{h,k})
\ge c_p(h,k).
\]

This argument uses the fixed formal degrees and does not require the common roots to be simple; multiplicity or degree drop can only increase the Sylvester corank.

The continuants have

\[
\deg N_m=3(m-1)
\]

and the recurrence gives the standard coefficient estimate

\[
\log\|N_m\|_\infty\ll m\log(m+1).
\]

After the shift `X -> X+h`, Hadamard's bound for the Sylvester determinant yields

\[
\boxed{\log |S_{h,k}|\ll hk\log(h+k).}
\tag{2.7}
\]

This is the useful exact lower-root budget.

## 3. The outer lower pair is algebraically dependent

The exact continuant addition identities needed here are

\[
\boxed{
N_{h+k}(X)
=N_h(X)N_{k+1}(X+h-1)
 -(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X)N_k(X+h)
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
N_{h+k}(X)
=N_{h+1}(X)N_k(X+h)
 -(X+h+1)^6N_h(X)N_{k-1}(X+h+1).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

They follow directly by induction from (2.1); the cases `k=1` and `h=1` reduce exactly to the defining recurrence. In particular,

\[
N_{h+k}(X)\in
\bigl(N_h(X),N_k(X+h)\bigr).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the outer-pair congruence

\[
N_{h+k}(x)\equiv0\pmod p
\]

adds no new equation once (2.3) is known.

There is also an exact resultant factorization. Put `d_h=3(h-1)`. From (3.1) and multiplicativity of the resultant,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+k})
={}&(-1)^{d_h}
 \operatorname{Res}(N_h,(X+h)^6)\\
&\cdot \operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h-1})
 \cdot S_{h,k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

At the marked common root, `x+h` is a legal nonzero recurrence denominator and consecutive transfer numerators do not vanish simultaneously. Hence the extra two factors in (3.4) do not provide a second marked `p`-adic zero. Globally they may contain unrelated prime factors, but no extra divisibility is forced by the event.

Reflection gives the same conclusion. Reflecting

\[
x,\ x+h,\ x+h+k
\]

inside `F_p` produces the sorted triple

\[
p-1-x-h-k,
\quad p-1-x-h,
\quad p-1-x,
\]

which merely exchanges the gaps `(h,k)` with `(k,h)`. It does not create a second common root for the same Sylvester problem.

Finally, a first subresultant does not improve the valuation budget. In the generic event the gcd of `f_h` and `g_{h,k}` modulo `p` has degree one. The first nonzero subresultant recovers the marked root modulo `p`, but its coefficients are minors of the same Sylvester matrix and have determinant-scale height `O(hk log(h+k))`. A second forced `p`-unit would require gcd degree at least two or a singular common root; neither is implied by the three-zero data. The elementary model

\[
f(X)=X,\qquad g(X)=X+p
\]

already has one common marked root modulo `p` and resultant exactly `p`, not `p^2`.

## 4. Both selected upper primes couple through one common row

For an actual event, reflection modulo the lower prime gives

\[
p\mid b_z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_{p-1-z}.
\]

Since

\[
n=7p-1-z=6p+(p-1-z),
\]

Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
p\mid b_n.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For each extension, (1.1) gives

\[
n=6q_i+u_i.
\]

The selected upper-zero condition `q_i | b_{u_i}` and Gessel--Lucas therefore give

\[
q_i\mid b_n,
\qquad i=1,2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let

\[
M(p,q_1,q_2)=\operatorname{rad}(p q_1q_2).
\]

Then every actual event satisfies

\[
\boxed{M(p,q_1,q_2)\mid b_n.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

If `q_1` and `q_2` are distinct, this is the full product `p q_1q_2`. If a mixed-sign diagonal `q_1=q_2` has not already been removed, (4.3) still supplies `p q_1`; the logarithmic contribution per event remains comparable to `log T`, so none of the bounds below needs a distinctness assumption.

This common-row observation is the strongest exact bridge among the three different characteristics available from the present hypotheses.

## 5. A prescribed fixed-gap coupled carrier

Fix:

* a placement `(s,t_1,t_2)` of `(0,h,h+k)`;
* an ordered sign pair `(sigma_1,sigma_2)`;
* the fixed dyadic shell and all of its current legal range conditions.

Call this bounded datum `tau`. Let

\[
\Xi=T^{28/15-o(1)}.
\]

For a shell prime `p`, define `X_{tau,h,k}(p;Xi)` to be the set of representatives `x` satisfying all of the following explicit conditions:

1. `0 <= x < x+h < x+h+k < p`, together with the current lower-window restrictions;
2. `N_h(x) = N_k(x+h) = 0 (mod p)`;
3. the affine formulas (1.2+) or (1.2-) give integral, legal `u_i,q_i`;
4. `q_i` are prime and lie in the selected upper shell;
5. the product cutoff `(x+s)u_1u_2 > Xi` holds.

Crucially, this candidate set does **not** assume that the three lower residues or the two upper residues are actual Apéry zeros. Actual events form a subset.

For each candidate put

\[
z=x+s,
\qquad n=7p-1-z,
\]

and define

\[
G_{\tau;h,k}(p,x)
=\gcd\!\left(
 |b_n|,
 \operatorname{rad}(p q_1(p,x;\tau)q_2(p,x;\tau))
\right).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The prescribed carrier is

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak G_{\tau;h,k}(\Xi)
=
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_{J,T}}
\prod_{x\in X_{\tau,h,k}(p;\Xi)}
G_{\tau;h,k}(p,x).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently, the construction is the following finite algorithm.

```text
for each shell prime p:
    for each x mod p with N_h(x)=0 and N_k(x+h)=0 mod p:
        place z,a1,a2 according to tau
        compute (u1,q1),(u2,q2) from the exact sign formulas
        if all integrality, range, primality, and product-cutoff tests pass:
            n := 7*p - 1 - z
            M := rad(p*q1*q2)
            multiply the carrier by gcd(abs(b_n), M)
```

This uses only prescribed recurrence values and explicit finite tests; it does not choose or enumerate an unknown selected neighbor set.

### Divisibility theorem

After fixing `tau`, every actual two-extension event gives a unique candidate `(p,x)`. By (4.3), its factor in (5.2) equals

\[
G_{\tau;h,k}(p,x)
=\operatorname{rad}(p q_1q_2).
\]

Consequently the product of `rad(p q_1q_2)` over all actual fixed-`tau`, fixed-`(h,k)` events divides the carrier.

### Height theorem

For every candidate,

\[
G_{\tau;h,k}(p,x)\le p q_1q_2\ll_J T^3,
\]

so

\[
\log\mathfrak G_{\tau;h,k}(\Xi)
\ll_J
(\log T)
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{J,T}}
\#X_{\tau,h,k}(p;\Xi).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The candidate set is contained in the common-root set, hence

\[
\#X_{\tau,h,k}(p;\Xi)
\le c_p(h,k)
\le v_p(S_{h,k}).
\]

All shell primes satisfy `p >= c_J T`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{J,T}}c_p(h,k)
&\le
\frac{1}{\log(c_JT)}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{J,T}}
 v_p(S_{h,k})\log p\\
&\le
\frac{\log|S_{h,k}|}{\log(c_JT)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Combining (2.7), (5.3), and (5.4) proves

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathfrak G_{\tau;h,k}(\Xi)
\ll_J hk\log(h+k).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This proof is uniform in all four sign sectors. The different affine formulas affect only the explicit candidate test, not the common-root budget.

Since every event contributes at least the two distinct primes `p` and `q_i`, its carrier factor has logarithm at least `2 log T+O_J(1)`. Thus, writing `E_{tau;h,k}(Xi)` for the number of actual events,

\[
\boxed{
E_{\tau;h,k}(\Xi)
\ll_J
\frac{hk\log(h+k)}{\log T}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is the strongest direct fixed-gap conclusion supplied by the exact resultant argument.

## 6. Why upper reflection and upper transfer do not lower the height

The upper reflected difference is

\[
\bar u-u=13q-14p+2z+1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

In the two signs this is

\[
\bar u_+-u_+
=\frac{25z+13a+19-6p}{6},
\tag{6.2+}
\]

\[
\bar u_--u_-
=\frac{6p+z+13a+7}{7}.
\tag{6.2-}
\]

After orienting the smaller reflected residue first, let

\[
g=|\bar u-u|.
\]

The transfer lemma gives

\[
q\mid N_g(\min(u,\bar u)).
\tag{6.3}
\]

This does not provide a second independent `q`-adic condition. The congruence at the reflected residue is exactly the reflection of `q | b_u`; it is not a new zero.

There are also three structural barriers to combining (6.3) with the lower Sylvester determinant.

First, the lower equations (2.3) live modulo `p`, while the two upper equations live modulo `q_1` and `q_2`. There is no common residue field and no common marked root for a single Sylvester matrix.

Second, the transfer index `g` depends on the marked lower root `x`. In a plus extension both `q` and `g` vary with `x`; in a minus extension `q` is fixed by `(p,h,k,t-s)`, but `u` and `g` still vary with `x`. Thus elimination against `N_g` is not a fixed-degree polynomial resultant in `x`; one must either enumerate the possible `g` or use the full order-varying recurrence.

Third, the only uniform bound is `g=O_J(T)`. Direct upper-transfer evaluations or Ore norms therefore have logarithmic height at best `O_J(g log T)` per relevant configuration, and no present hypothesis gives an aggregate small-`g` theorem. This is worse than the truncated common-row factor (5.1), whose height is only `O(log T)` per candidate.

A first lower subresultant merely identifies `x` modulo `p`. Substituting that congruence into the affine formula for `q_i` still produces a relation modulo `p`, not a divisibility relation modulo `q_i`. Hence it cannot transfer a second determinant valuation from `p` to either upper prime.

Sector by sector:

* in `++`, both upper primes vary affinely with `x`, but both upper transfer orders also vary with `x`;
* in `+-`, only the plus prime varies with `x`, while the minus prime is fixed; the two upper equations remain in different characteristics;
* `-+` is the transpose of `+-`;
* in `--`, both upper primes are fixed for `(p,h,k,t_1,t_2)`, but the marked upper residues and transfer orders vary with `x`, and repeated occurrences require the same multiplicity budget `c_p(h,k)` already measured by `v_p(S_{h,k})`.

Thus all four sectors return to the same fixed-gap carrier (5.2). None forces a second Sylvester null direction.

## 7. Aggregate exponent audit

Write

\[
\Psi=T^{6/5-o(1)}.
\]

The legal gaps satisfy `h+k <= C_J T`. The deterministic weight appearing in (5.5) has

\[
\sum_{\substack{h,k\ge1\\h+k\le C_JT\\hk>\Psi}}
 hk\log(h+k)
\asymp_J T^4\log T.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The upper bound is immediate. For the lower bound, choose a fixed small `c_J>0` and restrict to a macroscopic rectangle

\[
c_JT\le h,k\le2c_JT
\]

inside the legal triangle. For large `T`, every point in this rectangle has

\[
hk\gg_JT^2>\Psi,
\]

there are `Theta_J(T^2)` pairs, and each has weight `Theta_J(T^2 log T)`.

Therefore summing the proved local estimate (5.6) over all gaps and the bounded set of sign/placement types gives only

\[
\sum_{\tau}
\sum_{\substack{h,k\\hk>\Psi}}
E_{\tau;h,k}(\Xi)
\ll_J T^4.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Equation (7.2) is not claimed to be sharp for the actual selected events. It is the strongest consequence of the deterministic resultant-height estimate alone, and it is far above the target.

Indeed

\[
R=\frac{T^{2/5}}{(\log T)^{6/5}\Omega(T)}
\]

gives

\[
Y(T):=\frac{RT^2}{(\log T)^2}
=\frac{T^{12/5}}{(\log T)^{16/5}\Omega(T)}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

A divisibility carrier proof of `o(Y(T))` events needs aggregate logarithmic height

\[
o(Y(T)\log T)
=
 o\!\left(
 \frac{T^{12/5}}
      {(\log T)^{11/5}\Omega(T)}
 \right).
\tag{7.4}
\]

The summed deterministic budget `T^4 log T` exceeds (7.4) by

\[
T^{8/5}(\log T)^{16/5}\Omega(T).
\tag{7.5}
\]

So even an additional factor `1/Omega(T)` would not repair the argument; a genuine power saving is needed.

The product cutoff

\[
zu_1u_2>\Xi=T^{28/15-o(1)}
\]

only shrinks the candidate set, so it is fully compatible with (5.5), but the determinant estimate does not see it. It also leaves macroscopic boxes with `z,u_1,u_2` all comparable to `T`, where the product is of order `T^3`. Extracting a saving from this cutoff requires a weighted distribution statement for the actual common roots, not another formal resultant identity.

## 8. The precise remaining theorem

Define

\[
c^{\mathrm{sel}}_{p,\tau}(h,k;\Xi)
\]

to be the number of common roots `x` in (2.3) which additionally pass the exact affine integrality/range conditions, make both `q_i` prime, satisfy both upper Apéry-zero conditions, and obey the product cutoff. A sufficient statement is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\tau}
\sum_{\substack{h,k\\hk>\Psi}}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{J,T}}
 c^{\mathrm{sel}}_{p,\tau}(h,k;\Xi)
=
 o\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{(\log T)^2}\right).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Equivalently, the carrier route would close from the weighted height estimate

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\tau}
\sum_{\substack{h,k\\hk>\Psi}}
\log\mathfrak G_{\tau;h,k}(\Xi)
=
 o\!\left(\frac{RT^2}{\log T}\right).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Neither follows from

\[
c_p(h,k)\le v_p(S_{h,k})
\]

and the individual height of `S_{h,k}`. What is missing is an average theorem saying that common roots of the two lower transfer polynomials rarely meet **both** affine upper-prime/upper-zero tests.

There is an equivalent horizontal formulation. Put

\[
\mathcal H_6(n)=
\{\ell\text{ prime}:0\le n-6\ell<\ell,
\ \ell\mid b_{n-6\ell}\}.
\]

Every event gives

\[
p,q_1,q_2\in\mathcal H_6(n),
\qquad n=7p-1-z,
\]

with `p<q_i`. Moreover the secondary lower zero is recovered from `(n,p,q,z)` and the sign by

\[
a_+=6(q-p)-z-1,
\qquad
a_-=7(q-p)+z-p.
\]

Thus a restricted third-factorial-moment estimate for the horizontal fibers `H_6(n)`, retaining the lower-gap and product cutoffs, would also prove (8.1). This is genuinely new incidence input; it is not encoded by a one-gap resultant.

## Final conclusion

The requested direct attack succeeds locally and fails only at the aggregate step:

\[
\boxed{
\text{fixed }(h,k),\tau:
\quad
p,q_1,q_2\text{ are coupled by one prescribed carrier of height }
O_J(hk\log(h+k)).
}
\]

The common marked root gives one Sylvester valuation. The outer lower pair is in the ideal generated by the two adjacent transfer equations; reflection repeats the same zero; and first subresultants locate the root without creating another valuation. The selected upper primes do couple exactly through the common row `b_{7p-1-z}`, but this coupling does not reduce the number of lower common roots.

Accordingly, the present exact elimination/resultant machinery does not by itself reach the target unordered-pair bound. The next viable theorem is a cross-characteristic average incidence bound of the form (8.1), or the equivalent restricted horizontal third-factorial-moment bound—not a further local determinant identity.
