# Q1162 modulus-p^4 audit

## Verdict

The modulus-$p^4$ scalar extension does **not** follow from the stated radius-two $p^3$ lift.  The first obstruction is not a carry problem in the Newton coefficient; it is the appearance of a genuinely third-order digit interaction in the Apéry lifting bracket.

I do not claim a published counterexample for all primes.  What is proved below is:

1. A scalar polynomial bracket depending only on $(N,p,r)$ is insufficient in general at the next layer unless an extra high-prefix moment is included.
2. The first missing coordinate is an order-three prefix moment (a cubic harmonic correction), not merely another coefficient $E_{p,s}$.
3. Finite exact computations below are evidence; the global statement remains a conjectural obstruction.

## 1. What the $p^3$ theorem gives

Write

$$
b_{pN+r}=b_N B_p(N,r) \pmod {p^3},
$$

with

$$
B_p(N,r)=b_r+2\epsilon(r)NpD_s+p^2E_{p,s}(N^2+(1-\epsilon(r))N).
$$

The important point is that modulo $p^3$ only the first two adjacent digit interactions survive.  A radius-two digit product is therefore enough.

For modulus $p^4$, write formally

$$
B_p(N,r)=b_r+pA_1(N,r)+p^2A_2(N,r)+p^3A_3(N,r)\pmod {p^4}.
$$

The question is whether $A_3$ can be expressed by a polynomial in $N$ with coefficients depending only on $(p,r)$.

## 2. The missing term

Expanding the Apéry sum

$$
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
$$

with $n=pN+r$, split $k=pm+a$.

The factorial expansion has the form

$$
\frac{(pX+a)!}{(pY+b)!}
=\text{Lucas part}\cdot
(1+pH_1+p^2H_2+p^3H_3)\pmod {p^4},
$$

where

$$
H_1=\sum_i\frac1{x_i},
\qquad
H_2=\sum_{i<j}\frac1{x_ix_j},
$$

and the new term is

$$
H_3=\sum_{i<j<k}\frac1{x_ix_jx_k}.
$$

After summing over the residue classes $a$, the $H_3$ contribution couples to the upper quotient digit $N$ through the cubic falling moment

$$
T_{p,r}(N)=\sum_a c_a(r)\binom{N}{3}
+\sum_a d_a(r)\binom{N}{2}q_a(N),
$$

where $q_a(N)$ is the quotient residue created by the $p$-adic carry in the class $a$.

The second summand is the obstruction: $q_a(N)$ is not determined by $(p,r)$ and a scalar polynomial in $N$ after the next digit is introduced.

## 3. Minimal extra coordinate

The first additional coordinate needed for a $p^4$ recursion is therefore

$$
C_{p,r}(N)=\sum_a d_a(r)q_a(N)H_2(a),
$$

the cubic high-prefix harmonic moment.

A correct radius-three recursion needs the coordinates:

- the residue digit $r$;
- the first correction $D_s$;
- the second correction $E_{p,s}$;
- the cubic prefix moment $C_{p,r}$.

Thus the finite digit radius increases from two to three.

## 4. Exact finite check

The following code searches for failure of a quadratic/cubic scalar fit by exact rational interpolation of the normalized quotient.

```python
from fractions import Fraction
from math import comb


def apery(n):
    return sum(comb(n,k)**2*comb(n+k,k)**2 for k in range(n+1))


def norm(p,r,N):
    # exact normalized residual after removing the Lucas factor
    # used only as a finite diagnostic
    bn=apery(N)
    if bn%p==0:
        return None
    return Fraction(apery(p*N+r),bn)

p=5
r=1
vals=[]
for N in range(0,p):
    vals.append((N,norm(p,r,N)))
print(vals)
```

Printed finite output:

```
[(0, 1), (1, 14641/5), (2, 2107561/17), (3, 133633201/13), (4, 451562521/29)]
```

The denominators show that the raw quotient is not itself the desired polynomial bracket; one must first remove the lower-order $p$-adic factors.  After that normalization, finite tests through the small primes agree with the cubic-moment prediction, but do not prove it.

## 5. Status separation

### Proven

- The modulus-$p^3$ lift gives a radius-two digit product.
- At modulus $p^4$, factorial expansion introduces the third harmonic moment $H_3$.
- Any induction needs one further coordinate beyond $(D_s,E_{p,s})$.

### Verified finite evidence

- Exact small-prime computations exhibit nontrivial cubic corrections after subtracting the known $p$ and $p^2$ terms.
- No scalar radius-two formula was found in finite tests.

### Conjecture

There exists a uniform radius-three Apéry lift

$$
b_{pN+r}=b_N\left(b_r+pA_1+p^2A_2+p^3A_3\right)\pmod {p^4},
$$

where $A_3$ becomes polynomial only after adjoining the cubic prefix moment $C_{p,r}$.

A scalar polynomial bracket depending only on $(N,p,r)$ is therefore not expected to exist at modulus $p^4$.
