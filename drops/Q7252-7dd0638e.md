# Finite-field Legendre norm audit for the Apéry zeros

## Verdict

The proposed identity is correct, including the endpoint cases, but the weighted Legendre norm viewpoint does not currently yield a stronger zero restriction than the existing gap-polynomial machinery.

The reason is structural: the weight depends on the same index `j` as the polynomial degree. The resulting bilinear form is not one fixed orthogonality measure with respect to which the Legendre family is orthogonal. Christoffel-Darboux and Gram determinant identities therefore reduce to tautological restatements of the Apéry recurrence rather than producing a new nonvanishing criterion.

## 1. Polynomial identity

Define

\[
A_j(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom jk^2t^k.
\]

Using

\[
A_j(t)=(1-t)^jP_j\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right),
\]

where `P_j` is the ordinary Legendre polynomial, the standard parity identity

\[
P_j(-x)=(-1)^jP_j(x)
\]

implies the finite-field reflection formula.

Indeed

\[
A_{p-1-j}(t)=(1-t)^{p-1-j}P_{p-1-j}\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right).
\]

Over `F_p`, the binomial definition gives

\[
P_{p-1-j}(x)=(-1)^jP_j(x)
\]

in the relevant range `1 <= j <= (p-1)/2`. Therefore

\[
A_{p-1-j}(t)=(1-t)^{p-1-2j}A_j(t).
\]

Equivalently, directly from coefficients,

\[
\binom{p-1-j}{k}\equiv(-1)^k\binom{j+k}{j}\pmod p,
\]

and substitution gives the same identity.

## 2. Boundary cases

### Case t=1

For `j < (p-1)/2`, the exponent satisfies

\[
p-1-2j>0.
\]

Hence

\[
A_{p-1-j}(1)=0.
\]

The formula gives

\[
(1-1)^{p-1-2j}A_j(1)=0.
\]

No ambiguity occurs.

For

\[
j=(p-1)/2,
\]

we have exponent zero. The identity becomes

\[
A_{(p-1)/2}(t)=A_{(p-1)/2}(t).
\]

At `t=1`, both sides equal

\[
\sum_k\binom{(p-1)/2}{k}^2.
\]

Vandermonde gives

\[
A_{(p-1)/2}(1)=\binom{p-1}{(p-1)/2}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}.
\]

Thus the endpoint is consistent.

## 3. Apéry zero as weighted norm

Using the toric/finite-field representation of `b_j`, one obtains

\[
b_j=-\sum_{t\in F_p^\times}t^jA_j(t)A_{p-1-j}(t).
\]

Substituting the reflection identity gives

\[
\boxed{
 b_j=-\sum_{t\ne0}t^j(1-t)^{p-1-2j}A_j(t)^2.
}
\]

Since

\[
(1-t)^{p-1}=1
\]

for `t != 1`, this is equivalently

\[
b_j=-\sum_{t\ne0,1}\left(\frac{t}{(1-t)^2}\right)^jA_j(t)^2
\]

with the `t=1` contribution treated separately. For the allowed range below the midpoint the latter contribution vanishes.

This is a genuine quadratic form, but the measure is

\[
w_j(t)=t^j(1-t)^{p-1-2j},
\]

which varies with `j`.

## 4. Why standard Legendre orthogonality does not apply

Classical discrete orthogonality requires a fixed weight:

\[
\sum_t w(t)P_i(t)P_j(t)=0
\]

for varying degrees.

Here the expression is

\[
\sum_t w_j(t)P_j(t)^2.
\]

The measure changes exactly when the polynomial degree changes. Therefore the Gram matrix is not a single Hankel moment matrix.

The formal Gram determinant

\[
\det(\sum_t w_j(t)P_r(t)P_s(t))
\]

has no adjacent-degree meaning because replacing `j` changes every matrix entry.

This prevents a direct Christoffel-Darboux argument.

## 5. Adjacent-j identities

The Legendre recurrence gives

\[
(j+1)P_{j+1}(x)=(2j+1)xP_j(x)-jP_{j-1}(x).
\]

After transforming back to `A_j`, this gives a three-term recurrence

\[
A_{j+1}=R_j(t)A_j+S_j(t)A_{j-1}.
\]

Squaring inside the norm gives

\[
\langle A_{j+1},A_{j+1}\rangle_{w_{j+1}}
\]

but the previous norm is with `w_j`, not `w_{j+1}`. The cross terms therefore contain

\[
\sum_t(w_{j+1}-w_j)A_j^2,
\]

which is another weighted Apéry sum.

Thus the recurrence closes only after introducing the whole moving family

\[
N_j=\sum_t w_j(t)A_j(t)^2.
\]

No sign or positivity statement follows over finite fields.

## 6. Gauss/Jacobi sums

Expanding

\[
A_j(t)^2
\]

reduces the norm to sums of the form

\[
\sum_t t^e(1-t)^f.
\]

These are Jacobi sums:

\[
J(\chi^e,\chi^f).
\]

However the coefficients of `A_j^2` have size `O(j)` and vary with `j`.

The result is a linear combination

\[
 b_j=\sum_{r,s}c_{r,s}(j)J(r,s),
\]

not a single Jacobi sum with known valuation.

Hence Gauss-sum evaluation does not isolate a forced nonzero factor.

## 7. Relation to known gap information

A useful improvement would need to imply something such as:

\[
 b_j=0 \Longrightarrow b_{j+r}\ne0
\]

for a new range of `r`, or an improved lower bound on zero gaps.

The weighted norm currently gives no such statement.

The existing no-consecutive-zero theorem comes from the companion Casoratian:

\[
b_nc_{n-1}-b_{n-1}c_n=-\frac1{n^3},
\]

which directly forbids two adjacent zeros. The Legendre norm does not reproduce a stronger determinant identity.

## 8. Scoped no-go statement

The obstruction is not that Legendre polynomials lack structure. The obstruction is the moving measure:

\[
\boxed{w_j(t)=t^j(1-t)^{p-1-2j}.}
\]

For fixed `w`, Christoffel-Darboux would control neighboring norms. Here changing `j` changes both the vector and the inner product.

Therefore:

- the reflection identity is exact;
- the weighted norm formula is exact;
- endpoint cases are harmless;
- Gauss/Jacobi expansion is possible;
- but no fixed orthogonal-polynomial structure survives.

The remaining missing theorem would have to be a genuinely arithmetic statement about the moving family, for example a nonconcentration theorem for

\[
\sum_t w_j(t)A_j(t)^2
\]

as `(p,j)` vary.

D-finite/Legendre closure alone does not provide this: it gives neither bounded conductor nor finite-field zero nonconcentration.
