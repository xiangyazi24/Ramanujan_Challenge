from the currently available Apéry recurrence/continuant/Hasse machinery or from a known gcd/Subspace/arithmetic-dynamics theorem.

There is, however, a sharper exact reduction than the existing \(O(T^2)\) divisibility ledger.  It preserves the Apéry mark and turns the high-prime problem into a one-index, pointwise **equal-return row-gcd problem**.

For
\[
D_{a,h}=\gcd\bigl(b_a,N_h(a),N_h(a+h)\bigr),
\]
put
\[
M_T=\operatorname{rad}\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le h\le t\le T}D_{t+h,h}.
\]
Then primes \(p\le4T\) cost only \(O(T)\) in logarithmic radical.  For every prime \(p>4T\), with \(a=t+h\), one has the exact equivalence
\[
\boxed{
 p\mid D_{a,h}
 \iff
 p\mid b_a,\quad p\mid b_{a+h},\quad p\mid b_{a+2h}.
}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the difficult part is not an unmarked continuant resultant: it is a prime divisor of the **actual** Apéry value \(b_a\) whose zero returns twice with the same spacing \(h\).

Define the horizon-free marked row radical
\[
\mathfrak R(a):=
\prod_{\substack{p>2a,\ p\mid b_a\\
\exists\,1\le h\le\lfloor a/2\rfloor:\
 p\mid N_h(a),\ p\mid N_h(a+h)}}p.
\tag{2}
\]
Equivalently, by (1),
\[
\mathfrak R(a)=
\prod_{\substack{p>2a,\ p\mid b_a\\
\exists\,1\le h\le\lfloor a/2\rfloor:\
 p\mid b_{a+h},\ p\mid b_{a+2h}}}p.
\tag{3}
\]
Every \(p>4T\) occurring in \(M_T\) belongs to \(\mathfrak R(a)\) for some \(a\le2T\), hence
\[
\boxed{
M_T^{(p>4T)}\mid\prod_{a\le2T}\mathfrak R(a).
}
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently the following is a sufficient pointwise theorem.

> **Marked equal-return row-gcd lemma (missing).**  There exist constants \(C>0\) and \(\eta>0\) such that, uniformly for every \(a\ge2\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> \log\mathfrak R(a)\le C a^{1-\eta}.
> }
> \tag{5}
> \]

Indeed, (4) gives
\[
\log M_T^{(p>4T)}
\le C\sum_{a\le2T}a^{1-\eta}
=O(T^{2-\eta}),
\]
while
\[
\log M_T^{(p\le4T)}
\le\sum_{p\le4T}\log p
=O(T).
\]
So (5) gives the requested \(O(T^{2-\eta})\) for all of \(M_T\).

I regard (5), or its equivalent three-Apéry-value form (3), as the smallest useful exact row-gcd theorem left after saturation.  It has one moving integer \(a\), no global \(T\), no CRT surrogate, no unmarked resultant, and the prime is required to divide the actual \(b_a\).

The rest of this note proves (1)--(4), shows why the natural restart/continuant descent is genuinely terminal at this row problem, and audits the strongest relevant gcd theorems against (5).

# 1. Exact recurrence and return notation

Write
\[
P(u)=(2u+1)(17u^2+17u+5)
=34u^3+51u^2+27u+5.
\]
The Apéry numbers satisfy
\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1},
\qquad b_0=1,\ b_1=5.
\tag{6}
\]
The gap numerators in `proof.tex` are
\[
N_1(r)=1,\qquad N_2(r)=P(r+1),
\]
\[
N_{h+1}(r)
=P(r+h)N_h(r)-(r+h)^6N_{h-1}(r),
\qquad \deg_rN_h=3(h-1).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(p\ge7\), \(b_m\equiv0\pmod p\), and \(m+h\le p-1\), the gap lemma gives
\[
b_{m+h}\equiv C_h(m)b_{m+1}\pmod p,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(C_h(m)\) has numerator \(N_h(m)\) and denominator a product of powers of \(m+2,\ldots,m+h\).  On a nonwrapping regular interval all those denominator factors are units modulo \(p\).

The no-consecutive-zero lemma gives
\[
b_m\equiv0\pmod p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_{m+1}\not\equiv0\pmod p
\tag{9}
\]
for the same regular range.  Hence (8) immediately upgrades to
\[
\boxed{
 b_m\equiv0\pmod p,\quad m+h<p
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 N_h(m)\equiv0\pmod p
 \iff b_{m+h}\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{10}
\]
This equivalence, not just one-way divisibility, is the key high-prime fact.

There is also an exact endpoint identity.  The manuscript evaluates
\[
N_h(-1)=((h-1)!)^3b_{h-1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus
\[
N_{a+1}(-1)=(a!)^3b_a.
\tag{12}
\]
The restart identity in `proof.tex` says that if \(N_r(x_0)=0\) and \(N_{r+1}(x_0)\ne0\), then
\[
N_{r+d}(x_0)
=N_{r+1}(x_0)N_d(x_0+r).
\tag{13}
\]
So the mark \(p\mid b_a\), followed by \(p\mid N_h(a)\), followed by \(p\mid N_h(a+h)\), is literally a marked zero followed by two equal return lengths.

# 2. High primes are exactly actual three-term AP zeros

## Proposition 2.1 — marked return \(=\) three Apéry zeros

Let \(a\ge1\), \(1\le h\le a/2\), and let \(p>2a\) be prime.  Then
\[
\boxed{
 p\mid D_{a,h}
 \iff
 p\mid\gcd(b_a,b_{a+h},b_{a+2h}).
}
\tag{14}
\]

### Proof

Since \(h\le a/2\),
\[
a+2h\le2a<p.
\]
Thus both intervals \([a,a+h]\) and \([a+h,a+2h]\) are nonwrapping and every recurrence denominator on them is nonzero modulo \(p\).

Assume first that \(p\mid D_{a,h}\).  Then \(p\mid b_a\) and \(p\mid N_h(a)\).  By (10),
\[
p\mid b_{a+h}.
\]
Now apply (10) again at the starting point \(a+h\).  Since \(p\mid N_h(a+h)\),
\[
p\mid b_{a+2h}.
\]
This proves the forward implication.

Conversely, suppose
\[
p\mid b_a,\qquad p\mid b_{a+h},\qquad p\mid b_{a+2h}.
\]
Apply (10) to the first two zeros to obtain
\[
p\mid N_h(a),
\]
and to the last two zeros to obtain
\[
p\mid N_h(a+h).
\]
Hence \(p\mid D_{a,h}\).  ∎

This is the exact marked content of the saturation certificate.  In particular, for \(p>2a\) it is not merely a statement that \(p\) divides some continuant determinant: it says the actual Apéry solution has the zero pattern
\[
a,\quad a+h,\quad a+2h.
\tag{15}
\]

## Corollary 2.2 — reduction of \(M_T\)

For \(h\le t\le T\), put \(a=t+h\).  Then
\[
2h\le a\le2T,
\qquad h\le a/2.
\tag{16}
\]
If \(p>4T\), then \(p>2a\), so Proposition 2.1 applies and
\[
p\mid D_{t+h,h}
\iff
p\mid b_{t+h},\ b_{t+2h},\ b_{t+3h}.
\tag{17}
\]
This proves (1).

Now every prime \(p>4T\) in \(M_T\) is counted by the enlarged horizon-free row set (2) for \(a=t+h\le2T\).  Therefore (4) follows.

The primes \(p\le4T\) are harmless for a power-saving problem because
\[
\log\operatorname{rad}\prod_{p\le4T}p
=\vartheta(4T)=O(T).
\tag{18}
\]
Thus all of Q7600 may be concentrated on the range \(p>4T\), where the exact triple-zero interpretation is valid.

# 3. Why the restart/continuant descent stops exactly here

The mark can be inserted into the same continuant language by (12).  If \(p>2a\) and \(p\mid D_{a,h}\), then the actual Apéry zeros give
\[
N_{a+1}(-1)\equiv0,
\quad
N_{a+h+1}(-1)\equiv0,
\quad
N_{a+2h+1}(-1)\equiv0
\pmod p.
\tag{19}
\]
The three zero levels are
\[
r_0=a+1,\qquad r_1=a+h+1,\qquad r_2=a+2h+1,
\]
with successive differences
\[
r_1-r_0=h,\qquad r_2-r_1=h,\qquad r_2-r_0=2h.
\tag{20}
\]
Applying the restart identity (13) to \(r_0,r_1\) gives \(N_h(a)=0\); applying it to \(r_1,r_2\) gives \(N_h(a+h)=0\).  Applying it to \(r_0,r_2\) gives only the length \(2h\) return, which is already implied by the two length-\(h\) returns.

This gives a precise obstruction for the **restart/difference/Plücker descent class**:

> Starting from the marked zero levels \(r_0,r_1,r_2\), every gap length produced solely by taking differences of existing zero levels lies in \(h\mathbb Z\).  The smallest positive such gap is \(h\).  Hence the restart identity cannot force a new continuant \(N_d\) with \(0<d<h\).

Equivalently, the Euclidean descent has reached the equal-gap terminal configuration \(h,h\).  This is a rigorous no-go only for constructions whose new certificates are generated by repeated restart/difference identities.  It is **not** a general impossibility statement for other arithmetic identities.

This explains why adding the mark \(b_a\) is genuinely useful—it converts the old unmarked collision into an actual orbit hit—but also why the natural continuant descent does not by itself create a smaller-height certificate after the second equal return.

# 4. Why the existing Apéry estimates do not give a power saving

## 4.1 Degree/nonvanishing bounds count residues, not logarithmic prime mass

The manuscript proves
\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1)
\]
and \(N_h\not\equiv0\pmod p\) in the relevant range.  Consequently, for fixed \(p,h\), there are at most \(3(h-1)\) starting residues for a gap-\(h\) return.  The restart machinery also gives fixed-column zero bounds, and the global zero-set theorem gives
\[
Z(p)=O(p^{2/3}).
\]

None of these estimates controls the quantity in (5).  The reason is weighted and pointwise: \(\mathfrak R(a)\) is supported on prime divisors of one integer \(b_a\).  Even if one could prove that only \(O(1)\) primes \(p>2a\) satisfy the equal-return condition for each \(a\), a single such prime may have
\[
\log p\asymp a
\]
without contradicting the elementary height \(\log b_a=O(a)\).  Summing \(O(a)\) over \(a\le2T\) is still \(O(T^2)\).

Therefore a cardinality theorem for zero residues, an AP-count theorem for \(Z_p\), or a bounded-codegree theorem is not enough.  A power saving requires a **logarithmically weighted common-divisor estimate** such as (5).

## 4.2 The existing common-bad-prime codegree lemma is not weighted strongly enough

`proof.tex` proves that for two integers \(m,n\in(N,2N]\), \(h=n-m\), the number of primes \(p>P_0\) that are simultaneously lower-digit bad is
\[
O\!\left(\frac{h\log N}{\log P_0}\right),
\tag{21}
\]
and hence \(O(h)\) for \(P_0\ge N^\delta\).

For the present problem, \(p>4T\) and (17) gives three actual zeros, so one can feed any pair of them into this codegree estimate.  But (21) controls the **number** of common primes, not the sum of their logarithms.  Replacing each \(\log p\) by the only general pointwise upper bound \(O(T)\) loses exactly the power one needs.  Thus (21) does not imply (5).

## 4.3 Hasse/modular structure is also vertical

The Hasse--Witt interpretation packages \(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1}\) as coefficients of the Apéry Hasse polynomial.  The present condition is that three **coefficients** in a moving arithmetic progression vanish.  Existing Hasse/finite-field estimates in the manuscript control the size or energy of the zero/fiber set for each fixed \(p\); they do not bound the logarithmic product, over moving \(p\), of prime divisors of a fixed characteristic-zero coefficient \(b_a\) that happen to generate an equal-return pattern.

This is the same private-prime obstruction in geometric language.  A single large prime with one marked AP3 is invisible to vertical equidistribution estimates.

# 5. The exact row-gcd lemma that would suffice

There are two useful forms.

## 5.1 Pointwise form

For \(a\ge2\), let
\[
\mathfrak P(a)=
\left\{
 p>2a:
 p\mid b_a,
 \ \exists\,1\le h\le a/2\text{ with }
 p\mid b_{a+h},\ p\mid b_{a+2h}
\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Then \(\mathfrak R(a)=\prod_{p\in\mathfrak P(a)}p\).  The missing theorem is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in\mathfrak P(a)}\log p
\ll a^{1-\eta}
}
\tag{23}
\]
uniformly in \(a\), for some fixed \(\eta>0\).

This is the cleanest formulation: one actual Apéry number \(b_a\), and only those of its large prime divisors for which the same solution returns to zero at \(a+h\) and \(a+2h\).

## 5.2 Weaker summatory form

For the requested \(M_T\) estimate it would already suffice to prove, for some \(\eta>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{a\le X}
\sum_{p\in\mathfrak P(a)}\log p
\ll X^{2-\eta}.
}
\tag{24}
\]
This is weaker than (23), but (23) is the genuinely pointwise row-gcd statement and is the natural target for a gcd theorem.

Neither (23) nor (24) is presently proved in the checked repository material.

# 6. Audit of known gcd/Subspace theorems

I checked the following primary results against (23).  None currently applies.

## 6.1 Bugeaud--Corvaja--Zannier (2003)

Bugeaud, Corvaja and Zannier prove, for fixed multiplicatively independent integers \(A,B\ge2\), that for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\log\gcd(A^n-1,B^n-1)\le\varepsilon n+O_{A,B,\varepsilon}(1).
\tag{25}
\]
The proof is a Schmidt Subspace Theorem argument on a fixed finite-rank multiplicative group.

**Failure here.**  The Apéry sequence is not a constant-coefficient linear recurrence and has no finite Binet/S-unit representation.  Its recurrence coefficients vary polynomially with \(n\).  Moreover, even (25), as stated, is an \(o(n)\)-type estimate with a constant depending on fixed \(\varepsilon\); it does not by itself supply a uniform fixed power \(n^{1-\eta}\).

Primary source: Y. Bugeaud, P. Corvaja, U. Zannier, *An upper bound for the G.C.D. of \(a^n-1\) and \(b^n-1\)*, Math. Z. 243 (2003), 79--84, DOI `10.1007/s00209-002-0449-z`.

## 6.2 Levin (2019)

Levin's algebraic-torus gcd theorem treats coprime fixed polynomials evaluated on a finitely generated subgroup of \(\mathbb G_m^r\), and its recurrence applications concern simple constant-coefficient algebraic linear recurrences.

**Failure here.**  There is no fixed finite-rank torus point whose coordinates generate \(b_a,N_h(a),N_h(a+h)\).  The factorial/polynomial coefficients in the Apéry transfer introduce new rational prime factors as the index moves, and \(h\) changes the continuant itself.

Primary source: A. Levin, *Greatest common divisors and Vojta's conjecture for blowups of algebraic tori*, Invent. Math. 215 (2019), 493--533, DOI `10.1007/s00222-018-0831-z`.

## 6.3 Grieve--Wang moving targets (2019)

Grieve--Wang prove a moving-target gcd inequality for moving coprime polynomials evaluated at moving \(S\)-unit points.  Their Theorem 1.2 requires, among other things, that the polynomial degrees are positive **constants independent of the moving index**, and that the coefficient heights are negligible relative to the heights of the moving \(S\)-unit arguments.

**Failure here.**  The relevant continuant has
\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1),
\]
so the degree tends to infinity with the moving return length \(h\).  More fundamentally, the actual mark \(b_a\) is not an \(S\)-unit evaluation on a fixed finite-rank torus.  Freezing \(h\) does not help: the constants may then depend arbitrarily on \(h\), whereas (23) needs uniformity for \(h\le a/2\).

Primary source: N. Grieve, J. T.-Y. Wang, *Greatest common divisors with moving targets and consequences for linear recurrence sequences*, arXiv:1902.09109.

## 6.4 Xiao (2021/2023)

Xiao extends gcd results for polynomials in almost units and obtains applications to nonsimple algebraic linear recurrence sequences, still using Schmidt's Subspace Theorem.

**Failure here.**  The recurrence class is again constant-coefficient/exponential-polynomial.  The Apéry \(b_n\) is P-recursive with polynomially varying coefficients, and \(N_h(a+h)\) is a moving diagonal continuant rather than a term of a fixed algebraic linear recurrence.

Primary source: Z. Xiao, *Greatest common divisors for polynomials in almost units and applications to linear recurrence sequences*, arXiv:2110.01751.

## 6.5 Ailon--Rudnick

Ailon--Rudnick prove a strong bounded-gcd statement in the **polynomial/function-field** setting for fixed multiplicatively independent polynomials, using torsion points on curves.  The analogous integer problem is much harder and their integer statement is conjectural.

**Failure here.**  We are in the integer setting, the moving objects are not fixed powers, and the return length \(h\) changes the continuant.  The function-field boundedness theorem has no direct specialization giving (23).

Primary source: N. Ailon, Z. Rudnick, *Torsion points on curves and common divisors of \(a^k-1\) and \(b^k-1\)*, Acta Arith. 113 (2004), 31--38; arXiv:math/0202102.

## 6.6 Silverman/Vojta generalized gcd

Silverman's blowup formalism interprets logarithmic gcd as a height relative to a codimension-\(\ge2\) subscheme and derives strong consequences conditional on Vojta-type conjectures.

**Failure here.**  This does not give an unconditional estimate, and the natural marked-return subscheme itself moves with \(h\).  A fixed-\(h\) application would again have constants depending on \(h\), not the uniform \(1\le h\le a/2\) pointwise estimate needed in (23).

Primary source: J. H. Silverman, *Generalized Greatest Common Divisors, Divisibility Sequences, and Vojta's Conjecture for Blowups*, Monatsh. Math. 145 (2005), 333--350; arXiv:math/0407415.

# 7. Arithmetic dynamics: the natural autonomization has dynamical degree 1

There is a tempting way to package the nonautonomous Apéry recurrence as a fixed rational map, but the known dynamical gcd theorems still miss it for a precise reason.

Set
\[
x_n:=\frac{n^3b_{n-1}}{b_n}.
\]
From (6),
\[
\boxed{
x_{n+1}=\frac{(n+1)^6}{P(n)-x_n}.}
\tag{26}
\]
Thus the pair \((n,x_n)\) is an orbit of the fixed rational skew map
\[
F(s,x)=\left(s+1,\frac{(s+1)^6}{P(s)-x}\right)
\tag{27}
\]
on \(\mathbb P^1_s\times\mathbb P^1_x\).

This does **not** put us in the standard expanding arithmetic-dynamics gcd setting.  On divisor classes \(H_s,H_x\),
\[
F^*H_s=H_s,
\qquad
F^*H_x=6H_s+H_x.
\tag{28}
\]
Hence the pullback matrix is unipotent and
\[
d_1(F)=1.
\tag{29}
\]
The map is birational: from \(s'=s+1\) and \(x'=(s+1)^6/(P(s)-x)\) one recovers \(s=s'-1\) and \(x=P(s)-(s+1)^6/x'\), so the topological dynamical degree is also \(1\).

Along the Apéry orbit,
\[
h(s_n,x_n)=O(n)
\]
because \(\log b_n=O(n)\).  Therefore the arithmetic degree is
\[
\alpha_F=1.
\tag{30}
\]

This rules out the two most relevant dynamical gcd templates:

* Keping Huang's orbit gcd theorem assumes fixed degree-\(d\ge2\) polynomial/rational iteration and Vojta's conjecture.
* Matsuzawa's 2025 generalized-gcd height theorem requires the strict gap
  \[
  d_c(F)^{1/c}<\alpha_F(x).
  \]
  For the Apéry skew map both sides are \(1\), so the hypothesis fails before one reaches the marked-return subscheme.

Primary sources:

* K. Huang, *Generalized Greatest Common Divisors for the Orbits under Rational Functions*, arXiv:1702.03881.
* Y. Matsuzawa, *Growth of generalized greatest common divisors along orbits of self-rational maps on projective varieties*, arXiv:2507.05027.

This is a useful exact audit: autonomizing the time-dependent Apéry transfer does not create hidden exponential dynamics to which existing orbit-gcd estimates can be applied.

# 8. What a successful theorem must add

The high-prime problem has now been stripped to the following arithmetic statement:

> For a prime divisor \(p>2a\) of the single integer \(b_a\), how large can the logarithmic product of those \(p\) be for which the same Apéry solution has two further zeros at equal spacing before \(2a\)?

The recurrence, Wronskian, and continuant identities tell us **exactly when** this happens; they do not presently show that the corresponding prime factor of \(b_a\) is small.

A viable new input must therefore be one of the following in substance:

1. a weighted common-divisor theorem proving (23) directly;
2. a prime-size theorem plus a sufficiently sparse support theorem strong enough together to imply (23);
3. an Apéry-specific arithmetic-dynamics theorem for the parabolic/unipotent skew map (27), where standard dynamical-degree expansion is absent;
4. a Hasse/coefficient theorem that is horizontal in the characteristic and bounds the logarithmic prime mass of marked AP3 coefficient zeros, not merely the number of zeros for each fixed prime.

Merely proving fewer return residues for each \(p\), another continuant degree bound, or a new unmarked resultant does not address the private-large-prime obstruction.

The strongest exact new reduction I can certify is:
\[
\boxed{

\[
\boxed{

\[

\[
\log M_T=O(T^{2-\eta}).

It is also the smallest surviving arithmetic gcd theorem I can isolate that is not an unmarked carrier: it retains \(p\mid b_a\) and asks for the logarithmic mass of the large prime factors of that actual Apéry value that support two equal returns.

The natural restart/Plücker descent is rigorously terminal at the equal-gap \(h,h\) configuration, and the checked BCZ/Levin/Grieve--Wang/Xiao/Silverman/arithmetic-dynamics theorems fail their hypotheses before reaching this moving \(a,h\) problem.  In particular, current vertical Hasse/zero-set estimates and common-prime cardinality bounds do not control the weighted private-prime mass required by (23).
