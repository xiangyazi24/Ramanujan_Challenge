# Parabolic dyadic convolution-content support: exact CRT pair energy and the remaining CHLS operator wall

## Verdict

There **is** an unconditional parabolic pair-energy power saving once the convolution contents are viewed as one incidence matrix across the whole `m`-range rather than as unrelated integers row by row.

Let

\[
\mathcal P_P:=\{p\text{ prime}:P<p\le 2P\},
\qquad
Z_p:=\{r\bmod p:p\mid b_r\},
\qquad z_p:=|Z_p|.
\]

The only property of the exact convolution content needed below is the support identity stated in the question:

\[
p\mid \mathfrak c_m
\iff
m\bmod p\in Z_p
\qquad(p\in\mathcal P_P).
\tag{0.1}
\]

Thus, with

\[
X_p(m):=\mathbf 1_{Z_p}(m\bmod p),
\]

one has the **exact whole-matrix identity**

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_P(m)
=\omega_{(P,2P]}(\mathfrak c_m)
=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_P}X_p(m).
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

The current manuscript already has the unconditional single-prime bound

\[
\boxed{z_p=Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}.}
\tag{0.3}
\]

Using only (0.2), (0.3), and CRT injectivity before the first product modulus, one gets:

> **Parabolic CRT energy theorem.** For every set \(\mathcal M\subset\mathbb Z\) of diameter at most \(P^2\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> \sum_{m\in\mathcal M}(\Lambda_P(m))_2
> \le
> \left(\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p\right)^2
> \ll \frac{P^{10/3}}{\log^2 P}.
> }
> \tag{A}
> \]
> Here \((x)_2=x(x-1)\). In particular the theorem holds on the entire parabolic block \(0\le m\le P^2\), and on every CHLS subregion contained in such a block.

If the number-axis scale is denoted by

\[
T=P^2,
\]

then (A) is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{0\le m\le T}
(\Lambda_{\sqrt T}(m))_2
\ll \frac{T^{5/3}}{\log^2 T}.
}
\tag{B}
\]

Thus this is a genuine aggregate bound of the requested shape

\[
T^{2-\eta},\qquad \boxed{\eta=1/3},
\]

up to logarithms. It is not a row-height estimate: it is a theorem about simultaneous intersections of different prime columns of the actual incidence matrix.

There is also an exact unrestricted-interval version:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in I}(\Lambda_P(m))_2
\le
|I|\lambda_P^2+S_P^2,
\qquad
\lambda_P:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_P}\frac{z_p}{p},
\quad
S_P:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_P}z_p.
}
\tag{C}

This formula is the correct way to respect the CRT obstruction. On intervals longer than the product scale, the genuine CRT density term \(|I|\lambda_P^2\) takes over; no length-independent pair saving survives. The parabolic gain comes precisely from being **before the first repetition modulo \(pq\)** for every distinct \(p,q\in(P,2P]\).

However, (A) does **not** prove the full CHLS operator statement described in the earlier same-project audit. For arbitrary prime-column coefficients, the exact centered matrix still has the classical Farey conductor \(P^2\):

\alpha_p\left(X_p(m)-\frac{z_p}{p}\right)

\tag{D}
\]

At \(|I|\asymp P^2\), (D) has operator constant \(\asymp P^2\), with no fixed power saving. The exact off-diagonal entries reduce to the horizontal HCF correlations

\[
F_p(k\bar q)F_q(k\bar p),

at determinants \(|k|\lesssim pq/|I|\). For \(|I|\asymp P^2\), only fixed \(k=O(1)\) are dangerous; for \(|I|\asymp P^{5/3}\), the dangerous band is \(|k|\ll P^{1/3}\), exactly the previously isolated parabolic near band.

# 2. Turn the exact contents into one incidence matrix

For each \(p\in\mathcal P_P\), define a periodic column

\[
X_p:\mathbb Z\to\{0,1\},
\qquad
X_p(m)=\mathbf1_{Z_p}(m\bmod p).
\tag{2.1}
\]

By exactness of the convolution content,

\[
X_p(m)=1
\iff p\mid\mathfrak c_m.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since \(\omega_{(P,2P]}\) counts distinct prime divisors,

\[
\Lambda_P(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_P}X_p(m).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the relevant object is the binary matrix

\[
\mathbf X_P=(X_p(m))_{
 m\in\mathcal M,\ p\in\mathcal P_P}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The rowwise integer size of \(\mathfrak c_m\) has disappeared. What matters for pair energy is the Gram matrix of the prime columns.

For later notation put

\[
S_P:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_P}z_p,
\qquad
\rho_p:=\frac{z_p}{p},
\qquad
\lambda_P:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_P}\rho_p.
\tag{2.5}
\]

# 3. Exact CRT pair formula

Expand the falling factorial:

\[
(\Lambda_P(m))_2
=\sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathcal P_P\\p\ne q}}
X_p(m)X_q(m).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Therefore for any finite \(\mathcal M\),

\[
\mathcal E_P(\mathcal M)
:=\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}(\Lambda_P(m))_2
=\sum_{p\ne q}C_{p,q}(\mathcal M),
\tag{3.2}
\]

where

\[
C_{p,q}(\mathcal M)
=\#\{m\in\mathcal M:
 m\bmod p\in Z_p,
 m\bmod q\in Z_q\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For every ordered pair \((r,s)\in Z_p\times Z_q\), CRT gives exactly one class

\[
\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(r,s)\pmod{pq}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Hence, if \(I\) is an interval of \(L\) consecutive integers,

\[
C_{p,q}(I)
\le
z_pz_q\left(\frac{L}{pq}+1\right).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Summing gives the general interval theorem

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_P(I)
&\le
L\sum_{p\ne q}\frac{z_pz_q}{pq}
 +\sum_{p\ne q}z_pz_q\\
&\le L\lambda_P^2+S_P^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This proves (C).

## 3.1 The genuinely parabolic improvement

Assume now that \(\mathcal M\) has diameter at most \(P^2\). Since distinct \(p,q\in(P,2P]\) satisfy

\[
pq>P^2,
\tag{3.7}
\]

two different integers in \(\mathcal M\) cannot lie in the same CRT class modulo \(pq\). Consequently every residue pair \((r,s)\) contributes **at most one** \(m\), and

\[
C_{p,q}(\mathcal M)\le z_pz_q.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_P(\mathcal M)
\le
\sum_{p\ne q}z_pz_q
=S_P^2-\sum_pz_p^2
<S_P^2.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

This is the structural parabolic theorem. It is valid for arbitrary subsets of the block, not just full intervals.

The proof is exactly one use of the **whole cross-row matrix**: the map

\[
m\longmapsto(m\bmod p,m\bmod q)
\tag{3.10}
\]

is injective on a region of diameter at most \(P^2\) for every distinct dyadic pair \(p,q\).

# 6. Why this does not contradict the rowwise countermodel

A row-cardinality or row-content countermodel can force many prime rows to hit one selected value \(m_0\). Nothing in (3.9) forbids that.

If \(R\) prime columns all hit one \(m_0\), that single row contributes

\[
R(R-1)
\tag{6.1}
\]

to the falling-factorial energy. The global budget (3.9) permits isolated spikes as long as their total ordered pair mass stays below \(S_P^2\).

Thus the theorem does not perform the invalid implication

```text
small row support  =>  small every column.
```

Instead it proves the genuinely two-row statement

```text
before pq, one ordered residue pair can be realized at most once.
```

That is why the exact rowwise countermodel and theorem (A) coexist without tension.

# 7. Why the parabolic hypothesis is essential: the CRT obstruction

For a general interval \(I\), each CRT class repeats every \(pq\). Equation (3.5) therefore has the genuine density term

\[
\frac{|I|}{pq}z_pz_q.
\tag{7.1}
\]

In fact, over a complete residue system modulo \(pq\), one has the exact identity

\[
\#\{m\bmod pq:X_p(m)=X_q(m)=1\}
=z_pz_q.
\tag{7.2}
\]

So on long intervals the natural pair main term is

\[
|I|\sum_{p\ne q}\frac{z_pz_q}{pq}
\asymp |I|\lambda_P^2
\tag{7.3}
\]

(up to the diagonal correction).

This is the precise unrestricted-interval obstruction. The parabolic saving is not mysterious cancellation; it is the injective pre-period regime

\[
\operatorname{diam}\mathcal M<Pq
\quad\text{for every }p\ne q.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Once the interval is much longer than \(P^2\), the first term in (C) dominates and there is no analogous length-independent power saving from CRT alone.

# 8. The full centered coefficient matrix and the classical CHLS wall

The previous theorem controls the **all-ones incidence vector** and its falling-factorial pair energy. CHLS, in the stronger operator formulation recorded by the same-project audit, asks for arbitrary horizontal coefficient vectors.

Center the columns:

\[
Y_p(m):=X_p(m)-\rho_p,
\qquad \rho_p=\frac{z_p}{p}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Use

\[
e(x)=e^{2\pi i x},
\qquad
F_p(a):=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e(-ar/p).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Fourier inversion gives

\[
\boxed{
Y_p(m)
=\frac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}F_p(a)e(am/p).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Let \(I\) be any interval of length \(L\), and let \((\alpha_p)\) be arbitrary complex coefficients. Then

\[
\sum_{m\in I}
\left|\sum_p\alpha_pY_p(m)\right|^2
\tag{8.4}
\]

is an ordinary additive large-sieve form on the reduced fractions \(a/p\).

For distinct pairs \((p,a)\ne(q,b)\), their circular separation satisfies

\[
\left\|\frac ap-\frac bq\right\|
\ge\frac1{pq}
\ge\frac1{4P^2}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

The classical large sieve therefore gives

\[
\sum_{m\in I}
\left|\sum_p\alpha_pY_p(m)\right|^2
\le
(L+4P^2)
\sum_p\frac{|\alpha_p|^2}{p^2}
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}|F_p(a)|^2.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Parseval is exact:

\[
\sum_{a\bmod p}|F_p(a)|^2=pz_p,
\qquad F_p(0)=z_p,
\tag{8.7}
\]

hence

\[
\frac1{p^2}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}|F_p(a)|^2
=rac{z_p}{p}-\frac{z_p^2}{p^2}
=\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
\tag{8.8}
\]

Substitution proves the announced whole-matrix inequality

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in I}
\left|\sum_p\alpha_pY_p(m)\right|^2
\le
(L+4P^2)
\sum_p|\alpha_p|^2\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
}
\tag{8.9}
\]

For \(L\le P^2\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in I}
\left|\sum_p\alpha_pY_p(m)\right|^2
\le
5P^2\sum_p|\alpha_p|^2\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
}
\tag{8.10}
\]

This theorem uses the **entire coefficient matrix across \(m\)**. But its normalized operator constant is still \(P^2\). Therefore it does not supply a CHLS improvement of the form \(P^{2-\delta}\) for arbitrary \(\alpha\).

I do not claim that the bound (8.10) is attained by the actual Apéry columns. The point is sharper: all presently proved single-prime information feeds only the column norms in (8.8); obtaining a smaller operator norm requires a new theorem about the actual off-diagonal columns.

# 9. Exact off-diagonal Gram entry: the actual remaining arithmetic object

Let

\[
D_I(\theta):=\sum_{m\in I}e(m\theta).
\tag{9.1}
\]

For distinct \(p,q\), the centered Gram entry is exactly

\[
\boxed{
G_{p,q}(I)
:=\sum_{m\in I}Y_p(m)Y_q(m)
=
\frac1{pq}
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\sum_{b=1}^{q-1}
F_p(a)\overline{F_q(b)}
D_I\!\left(\frac ap-\frac bq\right).
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Put

\[
k=aq-bp\pmod{pq}
\]

and take its least signed representative. Because \(p,q\) are distinct primes, every admissible nonzero \(k\) determines uniquely

\[
a\equiv kq^{-1}\pmod p,
\qquad
b\equiv-kp^{-1}\pmod q.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Since the zero-set indicator is real,

\[
\overline{F_q(-u)}=F_q(u).
\tag{9.4}
\]

Thus (9.2) becomes the exact horizontal-correlation formula

\[
\boxed{
G_{p,q}(I)
=
\frac1{pq}
\sum_{\substack{k\bmod pq\\p\nmid k,\ q\nmid k}}
F_p(k\bar q)
F_q(k\bar p)
D_I(k/(pq)).
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

This is the actual two-characteristic matrix entry; no resultant or row-height surrogate remains.

The Dirichlet-kernel bound is

\[
|D_I(k/(pq))|
\ll
\min\left(L,\frac{pq}{|k|}\right).
\tag{9.6}
\]

Hence the genuinely dangerous determinant band is

\[
\boxed{|k|\lesssim \frac{pq}{L}\asymp\frac{P^2}{L}.}
\tag{9.7}
\]

Two important scales are therefore:

- **full parabolic block** \(L\asymp P^2\): only \(|k|=O(1)\) is dangerous;
- **shorter parabolic block** \(L\asymp P^{5/3}\): \(|k|\ll P^{1/3}\), exactly the previously isolated near-diagonal HCF band.

Thus a genuine CHLS operator saving must prove horizontal cancellation in

\[
\boxed{
F_p(k\bar q)F_q(k\bar p)
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

as \(p,q\) vary, uniformly for the small determinant range (9.7). Vertical Parseval, one-prime gap bounds, Hasse factorization, and separate content heights do not imply that cancellation.

This is the precise **actual-matrix obstruction** left after the positive pair-energy theorem.

# 10. Exact cross-row formulation of any improvement beyond (A)

On a parabolic region \(\mathcal M\subset[0,P^2]\), CRT injectivity gives the identity

\[
\mathcal E_P(\mathcal M)
=
\sum_{p\ne q}
\#\left\{
(r,s)\in Z_p\times Z_q:
\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(r,s)\in\mathcal M
\right\}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Therefore improving the exponent \(5/3\) in the \(T=P^2\) scale is **exactly** the problem of proving an Apéry-specific cross-row scarcity estimate such as

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}
\#\left\{
(r,s)\in Z_p\times Z_q:
\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(r,s)\in\mathcal M
\right\}
\ll
P^{10/3-\delta}

\tag{10.2}

for some fixed \(\delta>0\), or the corresponding signed/centered operator version (9.2).

No separate bound on \(\log|\mathfrak c_m|\), no global Smith content of the row family, and no per-prime cardinality theorem can imply (10.2) by itself. This is a statement about the **locations of actual marked residues in two different characteristics**.

The rowwise countermodel shows why an extra horizontal theorem is genuinely needed: local sets can be small while their CRT representatives align in selected columns. The current theorem (A) extracts everything that follows solely from cardinality plus the parabolic nonrepetition geometry.

# 11. What this buys for CHLS, and what it does not

There are two possible CHLS interfaces, and they must not be conflated.

## Interface A: unweighted pair/falling-factorial energy

If the needed input is only

\[
\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}(\Lambda_P(m))_2
\]

on \(\mathcal M\subset[0,P^2]\), then (4.6) is already an unconditional fixed-power theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}(\Lambda_P(m))_2
\ll T^{2-1/3}/\log^2T,
\qquad T=P^2.
}
\tag{11.1}
\]

That is the strongest positive conclusion of this audit.

## Interface B: arbitrary-coefficient horizontal operator norm

If CHLS needs the stronger statement

\[
\sup_{\alpha\ne0}
\frac{
\sum_{m\in I}|\sum_p\alpha_pY_p(m)|^2
}{
\sum_p|\alpha_p|^2\rho_p(1-\rho_p)
}
\ll P^{2-\delta},
\tag{11.2}
\]

then current inputs do **not** prove it. The unconditional bound is only

\[

\tag{11.3}

and the missing saving is exactly the off-diagonal correlation (9.5).

This agrees with the earlier same-project CHLS audit: a local cap or diagonal energy theorem is not the arbitrary-coefficient, pair-of-characteristics statement.

Accordingly, I do **not** promote (11.1) to full CHLS without checking which of these two interfaces the intended CHLS step actually consumes.

# 12. Reproducible exact audit of the parabolic theorem

No numerical scan is needed. The following pseudocode is only a direct checker for small data if one wants to verify the identities mechanically:

```text
input P, Apéry zero sets Z_p for primes P < p <= 2P
for m in 0..P^2:
    Lambda[m] = sum_p 1[(m mod p) in Z_p]

E_direct = sum_m Lambda[m] * (Lambda[m]-1)

E_crt = 0
for ordered p != q:
    for r in Z_p:
        for s in Z_q:
            x = CRT(r mod p, s mod q) in [0,p*q)
            if x <= P^2:
                E_crt += 1

assert E_direct == E_crt
assert E_crt <= (sum_p |Z_p|)^2
```

The equality is exact because \(pq>P^2\): each pair \((r,s)\) has at most one representative in the parabolic block.

A Fourier audit of (9.5) can likewise compare each centered Gram entry with its determinant-parameterized Fourier sum; that check would test normalization/signs but is not needed in the proof.

# 13. Smallest remaining gap

The current parabolic content problem is therefore much narrower than a row-height problem.

The first new theorem that would go beyond the result proved here is one of the following equivalent-strength horizontal statements:

1. **Actual CRT scarcity beyond cardinality:** prove (10.2) with some \(\delta>0\).
2. **Small-determinant HCF cancellation:** prove power cancellation in (9.5) after summing over \(p\ne q\), uniformly for \(|k|\lesssim P^2/L\).
3. **CHLS operator saving:** prove (11.2) for the actual centered Apéry incidence columns.

At the full parabolic scale \(L\asymp P^2\), the new arithmetic is already concentrated at a **fixed number of determinants \(k\)**. At the \(P^{5/3}\) scale it is the previously identified \(P^{1/3}\)-wide determinant band.

That is the correct place for an Apéry-specific cross-characteristic identity, reciprocity argument, dispersion estimate, or product-monodromy theorem. The convolution content itself has already done its job: it converts the target divisibility into the exact binary matrix (2.4). Further manipulation of individual contents cannot improve the horizontal exponent.

=\frac{T^{5/3}}{\log^2T},\quad T=P^2.
}
\]

This is an unconditional \(\eta=1/3\) pair-energy saving in the number-axis parabolic scale. The stronger CHLS arbitrary-coefficient saving is not proved; its exact residual obstruction is the small-determinant cross-characteristic Fourier matrix (9.5).
