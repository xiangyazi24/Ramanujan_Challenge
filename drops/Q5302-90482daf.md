# 3. Raw moment ladder: line-by-line audit

Define, exactly as in Q5285,

\[
\mathfrak M_r(D)
=
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\sum_{J\in S_p} a_p(J)m_D(p,J)^r,
\]

\[
\mathfrak I_s
=
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\sum_{J\in S_p} a_p(J)^{-s}.
\]

Fix one prime `p` and source `J`, and write

\[
\mathcal K_{p,J}=\{K\in S_p:D<K-J\le2D\}.
\]

## 3.1 Target interval packing

For `K_1<K_2` in `\mathcal K_{p,J}`, both are actual `p`-zeros.  Let `a_p(K_1)` be the first positive return gap from `K_1`.  Since `K_2` is one available later zero,

\[
a_p(K_1)\le K_2-K_1.
\]

Therefore the half-open intervals

\[
[K,K+a_p(K))
\]

are pairwise interior-disjoint.  Because `K in (J+D,J+2D]` and `a_p(K)<H`, every such interval lies in an interval of length at most `D+H`.  If `D>H`,

\[
\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{p,J}}a_p(K)\le D+H\le2D.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is the first place where the **target** first-return label is essential.  Replacing it by the source label `a_p(J)` would not be justified without an extra pointwise spacing theorem.

## 3.2 Hölder for arbitrary fixed real `r`

For `r>1`, write

\[
1=a_p(K)^{(r-1)/r}a_p(K)^{-(r-1)/r}.
\]

Hölder with conjugate exponents `r/(r-1)` and `r` gives

\[
m_D(p,J)^r
\le
\left(\sum_K a_p(K)\right)^{r-1}
\sum_K a_p(K)^{1-r}.
\]

Using (3.1),

\[
m_D(p,J)^r
\le
(2D)^{r-1}
\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{p,J}}a_p(K)^{1-r}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For `r=1`, (3.2) is simply the identity/inequality

\[
m_D(p,J)\le\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{p,J}}1,
\]

so the endpoint should be stated separately rather than invoking a formal conjugate exponent `infinity` without comment.

No integrality of `r` is used.  Nothing changes at `r=2`, `r=3`, or any noninteger `r>2`.

## 3.3 Target reindexing and source interval packing

Multiply (3.2) by `a_p(J)` and sum over source starts `J`:

\[
\sum_Ja_p(J)m_D(p,J)^r
\le
(2D)^{r-1}
\sum_Ka_p(K)^{1-r}
\sum_{\substack{J\in S_p\\D<K-J\le2D}}a_p(J).
\tag{3.3}
\]

For fixed target `K`, the source starts lie in `[K-2D,K-D)`.  Their intervals

\[
[J,J+a_p(J))
\]

are again pairwise interior-disjoint, and because `a_p(J)<H`, they fit into a container of length at most `D+H<=2D`.  Hence

\[
\sum_{\substack{J\in S_p\\D<K-J\le2D}}a_p(J)\le2D.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Substituting (3.4) into (3.3),

\[
\sum_Ja_p(J)m_D(p,J)^r
\le
2^rD^r\sum_Ka_p(K)^{1-r}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Now multiply by exactly one `log p` and sum `p`:

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak M_r(D)
\le
2^rD^r\mathfrak I_{r-1}.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus Q5285's `(M_r)` is correct and can actually be sharpened: for fixed `r`, the moment lemma itself needs no `P^{o(1)}` loss.

The constant `2^r` is exactly why the safe theorem wording is “for every **fixed** real `r>=1`.”  If `r=r(P)` is allowed to grow, `2^r` is not automatically `P^{o(1)}`, and the later use of `A=P^{77/200-o(1)}` also introduces `(r-1)o(1)`.  Q5285's detailed Proposition 1 already says “fixed real `r`”; that is the correct quantifier.  Its shorter verdict sentence should be read in that sense.

# 4. Fractional inverse-label moment

Let

\[
Z_{J,s}=\sum_a\frac{R(J,a)}{a^s},
\qquad 0\le s\le1,
\]

and suppose the literal canonical packet cap is uniformly

\[
R(J,a)\le aP^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For any `T>=1`, split at `a=T`.  The low labels satisfy

\[
\sum_{a\le T}\frac{R(J,a)}{a^s}
\ll
P^{o(1)}\sum_{a\le T}a^{1-s}
\ll
P^{o(1)}T^{2-s},
\tag{4.2}
\]

uniformly for `0<=s<=1`.  Positivity gives

\[
\sum_{a>T}\frac{R(J,a)}{a^s}
\le C_JT^{-s}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If `C_J=0`, there is nothing to prove.  Otherwise take `T=C_J^{1/2}`.  Then both terms are

\[
\ll C_J^{1-s/2}P^{o(1)}.
\]

If `T` falls below the allowed label interval, the low sum is empty and (4.3) alone gives the result.  If `T` lies above the allowed interval, the high sum is empty and `H<T` implies the low estimate is no larger than the same target.  Thus

\[
\boxed{Z_{J,s}\ll C_J^{1-s/2}P^{o(1)}}
\tag{4.4}
\]

is correct.

This estimate is genuinely sharp under the packet cap.  A finite model with `L` labels all of size `a\asymp L` and packet mass `R_a\asymp L` has

\[
C\asymp L^2,
\qquad
Z_s\asymp L^{2-s}=C^{1-s/2}.
\]

So there is no missing Hölder gain hidden here.

# 5. Consumption through the literal weighted simple graph

Write

\[
Q_{r,D}(J,a)
=
\sum_{\substack{p:\ J\in S_p\\a_p(J)=a}}
(\log p)m_D(p,J)^r,
\]

\[
R_D(J,a)
=
\sum_{\substack{p:\ J\in S_p\\a_p(J)=a}}
(\log p)m_D(p,J),
\]

and

\[
Y_{r,J}=\sum_a aQ_{r,D}(J,a).
\]

Then

\[
\sum_JY_{r,J}=\mathfrak M_r(D).
\]

Let `deg_{J,D}(p)` denote the foreign logarithmic neighbor mass in the exact next-common-prefix graph and

\[
E_{J,a}
=
\sum_{\substack{p:\ J\in S_p\\a_p(J)=a}}
(\log p)\deg_{J,D}(p).
\]

## 5.1 Simple-graph cap

If the consumer marks one next common selected prefix, then for fixed source `J` the partner vertices form a simple graph.  Hence

\[
\deg_{J,D}(p)\le C_J
\]

and

\[
E_{J,a}\le C_JR(J,a).
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is the source-sensitive step most likely to fail if canonical `CC2q40`--`CC2q44` changed the consumer from “next prefix” to an all-future-prefix multigraph.  Under Q5285's stated graph it is correct.

## 5.2 Exact-offset radical cap and log weights

Fix an owner `p` and one exact endpoint offset `d` occurring among its graph neighbors.  Every foreign partner `q` at that offset has literal actual selected endpoints at `J` and `J+d`.  Under the nonwrapping restart theorem,

\[
q\mid N_d(J).
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{\substack{q\text{ partner at }d}}\log q
\le
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(P,2P]}N_d(J)
\ll DP^{o(1)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Every used offset `d` forces `J+d\in S_p`, and distinct offsets give distinct later selected `p`-starts.  Thus there are at most `m_D(p,J)` used offsets, so

\[
\deg_{J,D}(p)\ll Dm_D(p,J)P^{o(1)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

After multiplying by the owner weight `log p`,

\[
E_{J,a}\ll DR_D(J,a)P^{o(1)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The weight bookkeeping is exact: (5.2) contributes one `log q`; the outer sum defining `E_{J,a}` contributes one `log p`.  The multiplicity `m_D` is an unweighted count.  There is no hidden `(log p)^2` and no second independent prime density.

## 5.3 Owner Hölder

For fixed `(J,a)`, use the positive measure

\[
d\mu(p)=(\log p).
\]

For `r>1`, Hölder gives

\[
R_D(J,a)
=\int m_D\,d\mu
\le
\mu(1)^{1-1/r}
\left(\int m_D^r\,d\mu\right)^{1/r},
\]

that is

\[
R_D(J,a)
\le
R(J,a)^{1-1/r}Q_{r,D}(J,a)^{1/r}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For `r=1`, this is equality.

Taking the geometric mean of (5.1) and (5.4), then using (5.5), gives

\[
E_{J,a}
\ll
D^{1/2}C_J^{1/2}
R(J,a)^{1-1/(2r)}
Q_{r,D}(J,a)^{1/(2r)}P^{o(1)}.
\tag{5.6}

\qquad

\[

In particular there is no break at `r=2`; for `r>2`, `s` merely moves farther into `(0,1)`.

The key identity is

\[
R^{1-x}Q^x

(aQ)^x\left(\frac{R}{a^s}\right)^{1-x},

because

\[
x-s(1-x)=0.

Hölder with exponents `1/x=2r` and `1/(1-x)=2r/(2r-1)` gives

\sum_aR^{1-x}Q^x

Y_{r,J}^xZ_{J,s}^{1-x}.
\tag{5.7}

Using (4.4), and absorbing the harmless factor two between oriented degree mass and the undirected edge mass,

\[

\ll

The exponent on `C_J` is correct:

Therefore
