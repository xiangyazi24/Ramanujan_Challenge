# P32 moving-gcd literature audit

## Executive conclusion

The requested estimate

\[
\sum_{X<p\le 2X}\log p\sum_{r<p,\ p\mid b_r}
\binom{\#\{1\le h\le H:r+h<p,\ p\mid b_{r+h}\}}{3}
\ll X^{1+\epsilon}H
\]

(or even the consecutive-window analogue) is **not supplied by any known theorem** on Apéry numbers, holonomic sequences, G-functions, arithmetic dynamics, or moving gcd estimates. The obstruction is the simultaneous averaging over a varying prime p and a moving residue/index pair r with shifts h up to H. Existing results generally treat one of:

* a fixed prime and varying n;
* a fixed shift and varying n;
* fixed integer sequences with large prime divisors;
* algebraic recurrence sequences over characteristic zero.

None gives the required two-parameter finite-field zero correlation estimate.

## 1. What the desired statement means

Let

\[
Z(p)=\#\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

The target is a fourth-order short-range energy estimate for the zero set of b modulo p. Writing

\[
R_{p,r}(H)=\#\{1\le h\le H:b_{r+h}\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]

the marked sum counts triples of later returns from every zero r.

The desired bound implies that the average number of clustered zero patterns is small enough to force

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)} /\log X.
\]

The needed input is therefore a moving finite-field correlation theorem, not merely a divisibility theorem.

## 2. Apéry-specific literature

### Beukers supercongruences

Known Apéry congruences give strong information along p-power towers, for example congruences of the form

\[
A_{mp^r}\equiv A_{mp^{r-1}}\pmod {p^{3r}}
\]

for appropriate Apéry sequences.

Parameter substitution:

* index n = mp^r;
* p fixed;
* no averaging over primes p in an interval;
* no control of pairs (r,r+h).

Therefore this does not imply a bound for

\[
\{(p,r,h):p\mid b_r,\ p\mid b_{r+h}\}.
\]

The missing step is converting congruences on sparse p-adic towers into uniform finite-field zero correlation. No such theorem is known.

## 3. Hypergeometric/binomial formula

The representation

\[
b_r=\sum_{k=0}^r {r\choose k}^2{r+k\choose k}^2
\]

modulo p gives Lucas-type decompositions when r is split into base-p digits.

However the required range is

\[
0\le r<p,
\]

so Lucas decomposition has no nontrivial digit splitting. It gives identities between special residue classes, but not an average estimate over moving primes.

A formula such as

\[
b_r\pmod p
\]

being a truncated hypergeometric value does not currently yield bounds of the form

\[
\sum_p\sum_r 1_{b_r=0}1_{b_{r+h}=0}.
\]

This would require cancellation/equidistribution for finite-field hypergeometric functions with the parameter itself varying with p.

## 4. Holonomic and G-function results

General results on G-functions and recurrence sequences establish:

* growth control;
* arithmetic denominators;
* p-adic integrality;
* zero estimates in characteristic zero.

They do not control reductions modulo varying primes.

The relevant missing statement would be something like:

For a nondegenerate holonomic sequence u_n,

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}\sum_{r<p}
1_{u_r\equiv0(p)}1_{u_{r+h}\equiv0(p)}
\]

has square-root cancellation uniformly for h up to X^{1/2}.

No such general theorem exists.

## 5. Arithmetic dynamics / large sieve candidates

### Large sieve for finite fields

Classical large sieve estimates can bound sums over primes or moduli when the objects are characters or fixed algebraic varieties.

Here the variety depends on the recurrence orbit:

\[
(b_r,b_{r+h})=(0,0).
\]

For a fixed h this can sometimes be interpreted algebraically, but h grows with X and the degree/height of the defining equations grows with h. Therefore the constants are not uniform.

The missing uniformity is exactly the P32 difficulty.

### Subspace theorem / moving gcd estimates

Results on moving gcds of linear recurrence sequences usually concern expressions such as

\[
gcd(u_n,u_{n+k})
\]

with fixed k.

Substitution:

* k=h;
* h\le X^{1/2}.

The constants depend exponentially or effectively on h. Hence they do not produce a uniform bound after summing h.

A typical fixed-shift estimate gives something morally like

\[
\#\{n:gcd(u_n,u_{n+h})>1\}\ll C(h)X^{\theta},
\]

where C(h) is uncontrolled. Since here h grows, this is unusable.

## 6. Closest available type of theorem and shortfall

The closest results are fixed-shift divisibility estimates for recurrence sequences.

They can at best give, schematically,

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}\sum_{r<p}1_{p|b_r}1_{p|b_{r+h}}
\ll C(h)X^{1+o(1)}.
\]

For h fixed this is meaningful. For

\[
1\le h\le H=X^{1/2},
\]

no uniform estimate on C(h) is available.

If C(h) is exponential in h, the loss is catastrophic:

\[
C(H)=e^{X^{1/2+o(1)}}.
\]

Even a polynomial loss

\[
C(h)\sim h^A
\]

would introduce

\[
H^A=X^{A/2}
\]

and destroy the desired exponent unless A is very small.

## 7. Consecutive-window version

Replacing all triples by consecutive returns helps combinatorially, because one only needs chains

\[
r,r+h_1,r+h_1+h_2,r+h_1+h_2+h_3.
\]

But it does not solve the arithmetic issue. The condition

\[
b_r=b_{r+h_1}=b_{r+h_1+h_2}=b_{r+h_1+h_2+h_3}=0\pmod p
\]

still requires a four-point orbit collision over varying p.

No known theorem supplies this.

## 8. Status of the proposed M4R hypothesis

The M4R hypothesis is stronger than necessary because it counts every four-subset inside a dense zero block. The exact reduction only needs either:

1. consecutive zero quadruples; or
2. excess multiplicity beyond three zeros per H-block.

However weakening the combinatorics does not remove the arithmetic gap. The required new theorem remains a prime-averaged moving-correlation estimate.

A sparse deterministic selector of O(k) quadruples inside a block can reduce the combinatorial count, but it still requires a uniform bound for the selected algebraic collisions.

## 9. Best unconditional conclusion

Current unconditional results give only trivial bounds.

For every p:

\[
Z(p)\le p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)
\ll \sum_{X<p\le2X}p
\ll \frac{X^2}{\log X}.
\]

The desired result is

\[
\frac{X^{3/2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
\]

Hence the exact unconditional shortfall is

\[
X^{1/2-o(1)}.
\]

For the M4R energy itself the trivial bound is

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}\log p\sum_{r<p}\binom{R_{p,r}(H)}3
\ll X^2H^3,
\]

because R\le H. The conjectural bound is

\[
X^{1+\epsilon}H.
\]

At H=X^{1/2}, the gap is

\[
X^{5/2+o(1)} / X^{3/2+o(1)}=X.
\]

## Final assessment

No currently known theorem proves M4R, nor its consecutive-window weakening, for actual Apéry numbers. The explicit hypergeometric formula and Lucas congruences provide structural identities but no varying-prime moving-zero average. The missing ingredient is a new uniform finite-field orbit-correlation theorem for the Apéry recurrence, with both the residue index r and shift h growing.
