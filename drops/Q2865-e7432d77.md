# Corrected affine/projective Smith-radical lemma

Let
\[
F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}f_i x^i,\qquad
G(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}g_j x^j
\]
be nonzero elements of \(\mathbf Z[x]\), with their displayed degrees
\(m,n\ge 1\). Assume

1. \(\gcd_{\mathbf Q[x]}(F,G)=1\) (equivalently \(\operatorname{Res}(F,G)\ne0\));
2. the pair is **common-content saturated** in the only sense needed below:
   no rational prime divides every coefficient of both polynomials, i.e.
   \[
   \gcd(\operatorname{cont}F,\operatorname{cont}G)=1.
   \]
   Equivalently, for every prime \(p\), \((\bar F,\bar G)\ne(0,0)\) in
   \(\mathbf F_p[x]^2\).

Put
\[
A=\mathbf Z[x]/(F,G).
\]
Then \(A\) is a finite abelian group. Write its nontrivial invariant factors
as
\[
A\simeq\bigoplus_i \mathbf Z/s_i\mathbf Z,
\qquad s_i>1,
\]
and define the **affine Smith radical**
\[
\Sigma_{\rm aff}:=\prod_i \operatorname{rad}(s_i).
\]
For a prime \(p\), let
\[
d_p:=\gcd_{\mathbf F_p[x]}(\bar F,\bar G)
\]
be the monic gcd after trimming both reductions to their true degrees; if one
reduction is zero, use \(\gcd(0,H)=H/\operatorname{lc}(H)\). Then
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\Sigma_{\rm aff})=\deg d_p.
}
\tag{1}
\]
This is valid with repeated factors: \(\deg d_p\) counts their full gcd
multiplicity, not merely the number of distinct common roots.

Now let \(S(F,G)\) be the nominal \((m+n)\times(m+n)\) Sylvester matrix and
write its finite cokernel as
\[
\operatorname{coker}S(F,G)
 \simeq\bigoplus_j\mathbf Z/t_j\mathbf Z.
\]
Define the **projective Sylvester Smith radical**
\[
\Sigma_{\rm proj}:=\prod_j\operatorname{rad}(t_j).
\]
For each prime \(p\), define the degree drops
\[
\alpha_p=
\begin{cases}
 m-\deg\bar F,&\bar F\ne0,\\
 +\infty,&\bar F=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\beta_p=
\begin{cases}
 n-\deg\bar G,&\bar G\ne0,\\
 +\infty,&\bar G=0.
\end{cases}
\]
and, since common-content saturation excludes \(\alpha_p=\beta_p=+\infty\), set
\[
\delta_p:=\min(\alpha_p,\beta_p)<\infty.
\]
Equivalently, \(\delta_p\) is the multiplicity of the common factor \(Y\)
at infinity in the gcd of the two **nominal homogenized reductions**.
Then
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\Sigma_{\rm proj})=\deg d_p+\delta_p.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, with
\[
I_\infty(F,G):=\prod_p p^{\delta_p},
\]
one has the exact global identity
\[
\boxed{
 \Sigma_{\rm aff}=\frac{\Sigma_{\rm proj}}{I_\infty(F,G)}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
The quotient is an integer. This is the corrected form of the proposed
"projective Smith radical divided by infinity" statement.

There is an explicit coefficient-only formula for the infinity factor. Extend
\(f_i,g_j\) by zero outside their displayed index ranges and, for
\(1\le r\le\max(m,n)\), put
\[
c_r=\gcd\bigl(
 f_m,f_{m-1},\ldots,f_{m-r+1},
 g_n,g_{n-1},\ldots,g_{n-r+1}
\bigr),
\]
with positive gcd. Then
\[
\boxed{
 I_\infty(F,G)
 =\prod_{r=1}^{\max(m,n)}\operatorname{rad}(c_r).
}
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, for each \(p\), the number of factors on the right divisible by
\(p\) is exactly the number of \(r\) for which the top \(r\) coefficients
of both nominal polynomials vanish mod \(p\), namely \(\delta_p\).

## Proof

Because \(F,G\) are coprime over \(\mathbf Q[x]\), there is a nonzero
integer \(c\in(F,G)\cap\mathbf Z\). Hence \(cA=0\). Put
\(R=\mathbf Z/c\mathbf Z\) and let \(J=\operatorname{rad}(R)\); \(J\) is
nilpotent. For every \(p\mid c\), common-content saturation says that
\(\bar F,\bar G\) are not both zero, and since \(\mathbf F_p[x]\) is a PID,
\[
A/pA
 \simeq \mathbf F_p[x]/(\bar F,\bar G)
 \simeq \mathbf F_p[x]/(d_p),
\]
which has dimension \(\deg d_p<\infty\). Thus \(A/JA\) is a finite
\(R/J\)-module. Lift finitely many generators to an \(R\)-submodule
\(M\subset A\). Then \(A=M+JA\). Iterating
\[
A=M+JA=M+J^2A=\cdots=M+J^NA=M
\]
for \(J^N=0\) proves that \(A\) is finite over \(R\), hence finite as an
abelian group.

From the invariant-factor decomposition,
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}(A/pA)
 =\#\{i:p\mid s_i\}
 =v_p\!\left(\prod_i\operatorname{rad}(s_i)\right).
\]
Together with
\(\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\mathbf F_p[x]/(d_p)=\deg d_p\), this proves (1).
No squarefreeness is used, so repeated common factors create no exception.

For (2), reduce the Sylvester map modulo \(p\):
\[
\Phi_p:
 \mathbf F_p[x]_{<n}\oplus\mathbf F_p[x]_{<m}
 \longrightarrow \mathbf F_p[x]_{<m+n},
 \qquad (U,V)\mapsto U\bar F+V\bar G.
\]
Because the matrix is square,
\[
v_p(\Sigma_{\rm proj})
 =\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\operatorname{coker}\Phi_p
 =\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\ker\Phi_p.
\]
Suppose first that both reductions are nonzero. Write
\[
\bar F=d_pF_1,\qquad \bar G=d_pG_1,
\qquad \gcd(F_1,G_1)=1,
\]
and put \(r=\deg d_p\), \(a=\deg\bar F=m-\alpha_p\),
\(b=\deg\bar G=n-\beta_p\). Every syzygy is uniquely
\[
(U,V)=(C G_1,-C F_1).
\]
The nominal Sylvester degree restrictions give
\[
\deg C< n-(b-r)=r+\beta_p,
\qquad
\deg C< m-(a-r)=r+\alpha_p.
\]
Therefore
\[
\dim\ker\Phi_p=r+\min(\alpha_p,\beta_p)
=\deg d_p+\delta_p.
\]
If \(\bar F=0\) and \(\bar G\ne0\), then \(V\bar G=0\) forces \(V=0\),
while \(U\) is arbitrary, so the kernel has dimension \(n\). Here
\[
\deg d_p=\deg\bar G=n-\beta_p,
\qquad \delta_p=\beta_p,
\]
and again the sum is \(n\). The case \(\bar G=0\) is symmetric. This
proves (2), hence (3). Formula (4) is exactly the prime-by-prime definition
of \(\delta_p\).

## Localization/contraction clause

The identity above is an identity for the **actual post-saturation affine
ideal**. Two operations must not be confused with it.

- If an original pair has a common scalar content, first pass to a pair (or
  contracted ideal) for which no prime makes both reductions zero. If one
  defines saturation by localization and contraction, the theorem applies to
  that contracted ideal; one may not silently replace the contraction by a
  different two-generator ideal.
- **Do not localize at common leading coefficients to remove infinity.**
  Localization at a prime removes the entire \(p\)-primary affine fiber, not
  only its infinity summand. Equation (3) instead subtracts exactly
  \(\delta_p\) Smith-radical dimensions and retains the affine
  \(\deg d_p\) dimensions. The Apéry \(p=2\) case below is an explicit
  counterexample to leading-coefficient localization.

Thus common leading coefficients create no exception to (1)--(3), but they
force the exponent-weighted factor \(I_\infty\); replacing it by
\(\operatorname{rad}(\gcd(f_m,g_n))\) is generally wrong.

# Apéry test: \((h,k)=(2,2)\)

Take
\[
F(x)=N_2(x)=P(x+1),\qquad
G(x)=N_2(x+2)=P(x+3),
\]
and put \(y=x+1\), so the pair is \(P(y),P(y+2)\). The exact resultant is
\[
\operatorname{Res}(F,G)
 =2^5 5^4 11^2 17^3 71.
\]
The four requested primes give:

```text
p    affine gcd over F_p[y]          deg d_p   drops (alpha,beta)  delta_p   v_p Sigma_proj   v_p Sigma_aff
2    y^2+y+1                          2         (1,1)               1         3                2
11   y^2+3y+4=(y-3)(y-5)             2         (0,0)               0         2                2
17   1                                0         (2,2)               2         2                0
71   2y+3                              1         (0,0)               0         1                1
```

The reductions are directly checkable:

- \(p=2\):
  \[
  P(y)\equiv y^2+y+1,
  \qquad P(y+2)\equiv P(y),
  \]
  so the affine gcd has degree \(2\). Both nominal cubics drop by one,
  giving one additional projective infinity dimension. Hence
  \(3=2+1\). In particular, localizing at the common leading coefficient
  \(34\) would incorrectly delete this genuine affine \(2^2\) contribution.

- \(p=11\): the difference \(P(y+2)-P(y)\) has degree two and its two roots
  \(y=3,5\) also annihilate \(P(y)\), giving exactly
  \[
  d_{11}=y^2+3y+4.
  \]
  There is no degree drop, so all two Sylvester-corank dimensions are affine.

- \(p=17\):
  \[
  P(y)\equiv5(2y+1),
  \qquad P(y+2)\equiv5(2y+5),
  \]
  with distinct roots. Thus \(d_{17}=1\). Both cubics drop from degree three
  to degree one, so \(\delta_{17}=2\): the entire two-dimensional Sylvester
  Smith-radical contribution is at infinity. This explains why
  \(17^3\mid\operatorname{Res}(F,G)\) but \(17\nmid\Sigma_{\rm aff}\).

- \(p=71\):
  \[
  P(y+2)-P(y)
  =2(102y^2+306y+265)
  \equiv 62(y-34)^2\pmod{71}.
  \]
  Also \(P(34)\equiv0\), while
  \(P'(34)\equiv68\ne0\pmod{71}\). Hence the common gcd is exactly the
  simple factor \(y-34\), equivalently \(2y+3\), of degree one. There is no
  infinity term.

So the proposed statement is **true after the correction above**:
\(\Sigma_{\rm aff}\) measures the full affine gcd degree prime-by-prime,
and the projective Sylvester Smith radical differs from it by exactly the
multiplicity-weighted common degree-drop factor at infinity. There are no
extra exceptions from repeated factors or common leading coefficients; the
only excluded case is an unsaturated prime at which both reduced
polynomials are identically zero.
