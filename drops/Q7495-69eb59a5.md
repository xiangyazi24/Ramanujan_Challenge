# Verdict

There is an exact modular/Lagrange transform for the Apéry generating series, and there is an exact fixed-Hecke-form transform on the central half-index.  They do **not** extend to the whole family of lower-digit congruences.

The strongest statement I can justify is:

1. The characteristic-zero modular parametrization is
   \[
   t(\tau)=\left(\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau)}{\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau)}\right)^{12},
   \qquad
   Z(\tau)=\frac{\eta(2\tau)^7\eta(3\tau)^7}{\eta(\tau)^5\eta(6\tau)^5},
   \]
   and, near \(i\infty\),
   \[
   \boxed{Z(\tau)=\sum_{n\ge0} b_n\,t(\tau)^n.}
   \]
   Lagrange inversion gives an exact formula for each \(b_n\), but the extractor depends on \(n\) and has growing pole order.  It does not lie in one fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module.

2. There is a genuine one-dimensional fixed Hecke module
   \[
   g_4(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
            =\sum_{m\ge1}\gamma_mq^m\in S_4(\Gamma_0(8)),
   \]
   for which
   \[
   \boxed{b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv\gamma_p\pmod {p^2}}
   \]
   for every odd prime \(p\) (Beukers modulo \(p\), Ahlgren--Ono modulo \(p^2\)).  Beukers' higher half-index congruence is governed by the same Hecke polynomial
   \[
   1-\gamma_pT+p^3T^2.
   \]
   This is a real fixed-form phenomenon, not a heuristic.

3. The reason it stops there is exact.  In the half-index theorem the Hecke-multiplicative variable is
   \[
   N=2j+1.
   \]
   For \(0\le j<p\),
   \[
   p\mid N\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad j=(p-1)/2.
   \]
   Thus this fixed Hecke channel sees exactly one lower-digit position per prime.  More generally, a fixed finite collection of bounded-degree reindexing channels can select only \(O(1)\) residue positions modulo large \(p\), not all \(p\) defining-characteristic coefficient positions.

4. On the toric/K3 side, the exact Mellin coordinate attached to the lower digit \(j\) carries the Kummer character \(\omega_p^j\), of order
   \[
   d_{p,j}=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(j,p-1)}.
   \]
   For generic \(j\) this order is unbounded (already \(j=1\) gives \(d_{p,1}=p-1\)).  A fixed finite collection of characteristic-zero modular eigensystems / lisse sheaves has bounded tame local-monodromy orders, so it cannot realize this family of Kummer twists.  The central index is precisely the exceptional bounded-order case \(d_{p,(p-1)/2}=2\).

5. The special bounded-order range is automatically negligible for the all-index radical problem.  If \(j=n\bmod p\) has Kummer order at most \(D\), then
   \[
   j=\frac{a}{d}(p-1),\qquad d\le D,\quad (a,d)=1,
   \]
   and, writing \(n=qp+j\),
   \[
   \boxed{p\mid dn+a.}
   \]
   Hence all such primes have total logarithmic radical mass
   \[
   O\!\left(D^2\log(nD)\right).
   \]
   For fixed \(D\) this is \(O_D(\log n)\); more generally it is \(o(n)\) whenever \(D^2\log(nD)=o(n)\).  In particular the truly modular central channel contributes only \(O(\log n)\) for \(p>\sqrt n\).  Together with the trivial \(O(\sqrt n)\) radical mass for \(p\le\sqrt n\), this is \(o(n)\).

So the modular parametrization does not solve the moving lower-digit problem.  The unresolved mass lives in the **high-order Kummer-character regime**, exactly where no fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module can have the required local type.  A successful modular approach would need an infinite/variable-character object with uniform arithmetic control across residue characteristics, not one fixed form.

No claim of the main conjecture is made below.

# 2. The exact characteristic-zero modular parametrization

Let
\[
F_\alpha(T)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nT^n,
\qquad
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]

Chan--Zudilin give the convenient exact eta-quotient form of Beukers' parametrization:
\[
\boxed{
 t(\tau)=X_{6,6}(\tau)
 =\left(\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau)}
              {\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau)}\right)^{12}}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
 Z(\tau)=Z_{6,6}(\tau)
 =\frac{(\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau))^7}
        {(\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau))^5}}
\]
with
\[
\boxed{Z(\tau)=F_\alpha(t(\tau)).}
\]
Here \(t\) is a Hauptmodul for the relevant genus-zero index-two extension of \(\Gamma_0(6)\), and \(Z\) is weight 2.  Beukers' original normalization has
\[
 t=q-12q^2+66q^3-220q^4+495q^5-\cdots
\]
and
\[
 Z=1+5q+13q^2+\cdots,
\]
so after inversion \(q=q(t)\),
\[
Z=1+5t+73t^2+1445t^3+\cdots,
\]
which is exactly the Apéry sequence.

This is the correct starting point for Lagrange inversion.

# 3. What Lagrange inversion actually gives

Because \(t(q)=q+O(q^2)\), its local inverse \(q(t)\) exists.  For \(n\ge1\), Lagrange--Bürmann gives
\[
\boxed{
 b_n
 =\frac1n[q^{n-1}]\frac{dZ}{dq}(q)
       \left(\frac q{t(q)}\right)^n.}
\]
Equivalently, with \(\Theta=q\,d/dq\),
\[
\boxed{
 b_n
 =\frac1n[q^n]\Theta Z(q)
       \left(\frac q{t(q)}\right)^n.}
\]
The residue form is even cleaner:
\[
 b_n
 =\operatorname*{Res}_{T=0}F_\alpha(T)T^{-n-1}\,dT
 =\operatorname*{Res}_{q=0}
   Z(q)t(q)^{-n-1}t'(q)\,dq.
\]

This is an exact transformation, but it immediately exposes the obstruction: the factor carrying the coefficient index is \(t^{-n}\), or equivalently \((q/t)^n\).  The modular parametrization has **not** turned the sequence into the ordinary \(q\)-coefficients of a fixed form; it has turned the \(n\)-th coefficient into a residue of an \(n\)-dependent weakly holomorphic object.

A particularly transparent family is
\[
 H_n(\tau):=Z(\tau)t(\tau)^{-n}.
\]
In the local \(t\)-coordinate,
\[
 H_n=\sum_{m\ge0}b_m t^{m-n},
\]
so the \(t^0\)-coefficient is exactly \(b_n\).  Since \(t=q+O(q^2)\) and \(Z=1+O(q)\), \(H_n\) has exact pole order \(n\) at the cusp \(i\infty\).

## Proposition: growing-pole obstruction

Let \(W\) be any fixed finite-dimensional space of meromorphic modular forms of fixed weight and fixed group.  Then the pole orders at a fixed cusp of elements of \(W\) are uniformly bounded.

Indeed, choose a basis \(w_1,\ldots,w_D\); the pole order of every linear combination is at most the maximum pole order of the basis.  But \(H_n\) has exact pole order \(n\).  Therefore
\[
\boxed{H_n\notin W\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.}
\]

In particular, the exact Lagrange coefficient extractors cannot lie in one fixed finite-dimensional Hecke-stable module.  A fixed holomorphic Hecke module is even more restrictive: Hecke operators preserve holomorphy at the cusps, while \(H_n\) acquires unbounded poles.

This is a **narrow** no-go, deliberately stated at the correct strength.  It rules out the direct Beukers-parametrization/Lagrange-extractor route inside a fixed finite-dimensional module.  It does not logically forbid a completely unrelated miraculous fixed form \(g\) together with an artificial indexing function whose coefficients happen to encode the same divisibility set.

For completeness, even the naive equality \(b_n=a_n(g)\) is impossible for a fixed holomorphic modular form for a much simpler reason: \(b_n\) grows exponentially like \((17+12\sqrt2)^n\) up to a power of \(n\), whereas Fourier coefficients of a fixed holomorphic modular form have polynomial growth.  But that size observation says nothing by itself about divisibility patterns, which is why it is not the main obstruction here.

# 4. What fixed Hecke operators *can* do

Verrill's theorem precisely describes the useful situation.  In one standard form, suppose
\[
 f=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nt^n
\]
is a weight-\(k\) modular form expanded in a weight-zero modular function \(t\), and suppose the logarithmic derivative is controlled by a fixed weight-\(k+2\) Hecke eigenform
\[
 \ell=\sum_{m\ge1}\gamma_mq^m
\]
through an identity of the form
\[
 f\frac q t\frac{dt}{dq}=\sum_i a_i\ell(d_i\tau).
\]
Then, away from the fixed bad levels, the \(t\)-coefficients satisfy the Hecke-type congruence
\[
\boxed{
 c_{mp^r}-\gamma_pc_{mp^{r-1}}
 +\varepsilon_p p^{k+1}c_{mp^{r-2}}
 \equiv0\pmod {p^r}.}
\]

The key point for the present problem is not the exact constants; it is the **index geometry**:
\[
 m,\ mp,\ mp^2,\ldots.
\]
Fixed Hecke operators control multiplicative \(p\)-rays of Fourier/power-series indices.  They do not give a projector onto an arbitrary defining-characteristic residue \(j=n\bmod p\).

The classical Apéry half-index theorem is exactly of this type after an affine change of index.

# 5. The strongest genuine fixed-form case: the half-index / quadratic channel

Put
\[
\boxed{
 g_4(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
 =\sum_{m\ge1}\gamma_mq^m.}
\]
This is the normalized weight-4 cusp form of level 8 used by Beukers and by Ahlgren--Ono.

Beukers proved, for every odd prime \(p\),
\[
 b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv\gamma_p\pmod p.
\]
Ahlgren--Ono, Theorem 5, prove the supercongruence
\[
\boxed{
 b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv\gamma_p\pmod {p^2}.}
\]

Moreover Beukers' higher congruence, for odd \(m\) and \(r\ge2\), is
\[
\boxed{
 b_{(mp^r-1)/2}
 -\gamma_p b_{(mp^{r-1}-1)/2}
 +p^3 b_{(mp^{r-2}-1)/2}
 \equiv0\pmod {p^r}.}
\]
The base \(r=1\) congruence is also immediate from Apéry--Lucas plus the central congruence:
\[
 b_{(mp-1)/2}
 \equiv \gamma_p\,b_{(m-1)/2}\pmod p.
\]
Thus the local polynomial is exactly the weight-4 Hecke polynomial
\[
1-\gamma_pT+p^3T^2.
\]

This is the strongest fixed-form transform I know for the actual \(\zeta(3)\) Apéry sequence: it is not merely the one point \((p-1)/2\), but the full \(p\)-adic half-index ray governed by one fixed weight-4 eigenform.

## Why this ray selects only the central lower digit

Set
\[
N=2j+1.
\]
Beukers' ray is exactly \(N=mp^r\).  At the first \(p\)-step a Hecke relation needs \(p\mid N\).  But for a lower digit \(0\le j<p\),
\[
 p\mid(2j+1)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 2j+1=p
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{j=(p-1)/2.}
\]
There is no second residue class hidden here.

This gives a very concrete coefficient-index obstruction: the fixed form \(g_4\) cannot be used, via its ordinary \(T_p\) recurrence, to interrogate arbitrary \(j\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\).  The arithmetic reason the central index is modular is exactly that the affine shifted index \(2j+1\) lands on the Hecke prime \(p\).

A useful generalization is immediate.  Suppose a proposed fixed finite collection of Hecke channels can only be activated when
\[
p\mid P_i(j),\qquad i=1,\ldots,L,
\]
for fixed nonzero polynomials \(P_i\in\mathbf Z[X]\) of bounded degrees.  For all sufficiently large \(p\), the number of residues selected is at most
\[
\sum_{i=1}^L\deg P_i=O(1).
\]
Therefore no fixed finite set of bounded-degree reindexing channels can cover the whole set of \(p\) lower-digit positions.  To cover all positions, either the number of channels or their degrees must grow with \(p\).  This is the precise bounded-degree/index version of the no-go.

# 6. The stronger natural obstruction: Kummer character order is unbounded

The preceding index argument is specific to Hecke reindexing.  The toric/K3 description gives a more invariant obstruction.

The repository proves
\[
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\mu_p(a)a^j=-b_j,
\]
where \(\mu_p(a)\) is the fiber count of the fixed Laurent polynomial \(\Lambda\).  Let \(\omega_p:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\mu_{p-1}\) be the Teichmüller character.  Since
\[
\omega_p(a)^j\equiv a^j\pmod p,
\]
the mod-\(p\) moment defining \(b_j\) is the reduction of the multiplicative Mellin component with Kummer character \(\omega_p^j\).

Its exact order is
\[
\boxed{
 d_{p,j}=\operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^j)
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(j,p-1)}.}
\]

At the central index,
\[
 j=(p-1)/2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_{p,j}=2,
\]
so one has a fixed quadratic local type.  This is precisely the regime in which the fixed weight-4 modular form occurs.

For a generic index the situation is completely different.  For example,
\[
 j=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{p,1}=p-1\to\infty.
\]
More generally, for typical \(j\), \((p-1)/\gcd(j,p-1)\) is large.

## Proposition: fixed-local-type no-go

Consider any construction in the natural K3/Mellin class which realizes the \(j\)-th Mellin component by tensoring/pushing forward a fixed characteristic-zero geometric object with Kummer local systems, and suppose the output is required to belong to one fixed finite collection of characteristic-zero lisse sheaves / modular eigensystems.

Then it cannot realize all pairs \((p,j)\).

Reason: the Kummer sheaf \(\mathcal L_{\omega_p^j}\) has tame local monodromy of exact order \(d_{p,j}\) at \(0\) (and inverse character at \(\infty\)).  A fixed finite collection of characteristic-zero lisse sheaves has only finitely many local monodromy eigenvalues, hence a uniform bound on the orders of their finite semisimple parts.  Tensoring with a fixed sheaf cannot erase arbitrarily large new tame character orders for all \(p\).  Since \(d_{p,1}=p-1\), the required local types are unbounded.

Equivalently in classical modular-form language: a fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module has fixed level(s), fixed nebentypus data and finitely many eigensystems.  It cannot contain a family whose finite-field Mellin twist has character order tending to infinity with \(p\).  Allowing those twists forces the conductor/local type, hence the ambient modular or automorphic space, to vary.

This is the obstruction I would use in the paper.  It is stronger than saying “\(b_n\) is not a Fourier coefficient,” and it exactly matches the defining-characteristic issue already visible in `proof.tex`.

Again, the scope is important: this is a no-go for the natural modular/K3/Kummer realization with a **fixed finite collection of local types**.  It is not a theorem against every logically conceivable encoding of an integer sequence into sparse coefficients of some unrelated automorphic object.

# 7. Quantitative payoff: every bounded-character-order range is already negligible

The character-order obstruction has a useful deterministic consequence for the all-index problem.

Fix \(n\), let \(p>\sqrt n\), and put
\[
j=n\bmod p,\qquad n=qp+j,\quad0\le j<p.
\]
Assume
\[
d_{p,j}=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(j,p-1)}=d.
\]
If \(j\ne0\), there is a unique reduced \(a\), \(1\le a<d\), \((a,d)=1\), with
\[
\boxed{j=\frac{a}{d}(p-1).}
\]
Multiply \(n=qp+j\) by \(d\):
\[
 dn=dqp+a(p-1)=(dq+a)p-a.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{p\mid dn+a.}
\]

Now let \(\mathcal P_{\le D}(n)\) be any set of target primes for which \(d_{p,n\bmod p}\le D\).  Every such prime divides one of the integers
\[
 dn+a,
 \qquad
 1\le d\le D,\quad1\le a<d,\quad(a,d)=1.
\]
Consequently
\[
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_{\le D}(n)}p
\mid
\operatorname{rad}
\prod_{d\le D}\prod_{\substack{1\le a<d\\(a,d)=1}}(dn+a),
\]
and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\le D}(n)}\log p
&\le
 \sum_{d\le D}\sum_{\substack{1\le a<d\\(a,d)=1}}
 \log(dn+a)\\
&\ll \left(\sum_{d\le D}\varphi(d)\right)\log(nD)\\
&=O\!\left(D^2\log(nD)\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
D^2\log(nD)=o(n)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{all targets of Kummer order }\le D\text{ have }o(n)\text{ radical mass}.}
\]

For a fixed finite-dimensional modular package, \(D=O(1)\), giving merely
\[
O(\log n).
\]
Even a hypothetical collection covering all character orders up to, say, \(D=n^{1/3}\) would contribute only
\[
O(n^{2/3}\log n)=o(n).
\]
So the main difficulty is forced into genuinely high-order characters.

This is, in my view, the most useful quantitative conclusion of the modular-form audit.

# 8. Central channel: exact all-index radical bookkeeping

The actual fixed modular form corresponds to \(d=2,a=1\), i.e.
\[
j=(p-1)/2.
\]
For fixed \(n\), if \(n=qp+(p-1)/2\), then
\[
\boxed{2n+1=(2q+1)p.}
\]
Therefore every prime whose lower digit is central divides the single characteristic-zero integer \(2n+1\).  Hence, without using any information about \(\gamma_p\),
\[
\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt n\\n\bmod p=(p-1)/2\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}\log p
\le \log\operatorname{rad}(2n+1)
\le\log(2n+1)
=O(\log n).
\]

The stronger Beukers half-index ray has the same property.  If an index \(N\) is of the form
\[
N=\frac{mp^r-1}{2},
\]
then
\[
p\mid 2N+1.
\]
Thus, at any fixed integer index, **all primes accessible through this one fixed Hecke ray already lie among the prime divisors of \(2N+1\)**.  Their distinct-prime logarithmic mass is at most \(O(\log N)\).

For the remaining small primes one needs no modularity at all:
\[
\sum_{p\le\sqrt n,\ p\mid b_n}\log p
\le\sum_{p\le\sqrt n}\log p
=O(\sqrt n)
=o(n)
\]
by Chebyshev.  Hence the known fixed-form special range plus all small primes contributes only
\[
O(\sqrt n)+O(\log n)=o(n).
\]

This does **not** prove the full smooth-radical conjecture, because it leaves the generic high-order lower-digit primes untouched.

# 9. Why Hasse/Sym\(^2\) and Hecke do not combine to evade the obstruction

One might hope to use the characteristic-zero square
\[
\mathcal A(a)=C_+(a)^2
\]
from the Hasse/Franel descent, modularize \(C_+\), and then apply Hecke operators to its coefficients.  The repository's exact identity shows why this does not remove the problem.

Modulo \(p\), the Hasse polynomial has \(p\) independent coordinates
\[
[K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,j}}=b_j.
\]
A fixed Hecke trace controls evaluations / Frobenius traces in a fixed local system.  The target is instead the vanishing of one of these \(p\) moving coordinates.  To isolate coordinate \(j\), the Mellin transform introduces precisely the Kummer character \(\omega_p^j\).  Thus the apparent attempt to pass

```text
Hasse square -> modular form -> Hecke coefficient
```

returns, at the coordinate-extraction step, to the variable-character family.  The central coordinate is exceptional because its variable character degenerates to the fixed quadratic character.

This is also why ordinary monodromy or Deligne bounds on the fixed K3/Sym\(^2\) fibers do not solve the problem: they control the underlying fixed sheaf before the high-order Kummer projection that defines the generic coefficient.

# 10. What extra structure would be required

A modular strategy capable of handling the full moving lower-digit family would need at least one ingredient outside a fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module:

- **A universal character-space object:** a geometric/automorphic Mellin transform varying over the character torus, with estimates uniform as the character order grows to \(p-1\), together with compatibility strong enough to compare different residue characteristics.

- **Or an infinite weakly-holomorphic Hecke tower:** the growing-pole family \(Zt^{-n}\) (or an equivalent family) would have to carry a uniform arithmetic structure whose common content has sublinear height.  Merely knowing that every member is modular is not enough; the dimension and pole order grow with \(n\).

- **And, for the every-\(n\) theorem, cross-prime control:** even perfect single-prime descriptions of each character do not by themselves prevent the moving residues \(n\bmod p\) from aligning at one fixed \(n\).  Any final argument still needs a characteristic-zero content/divisibility mechanism or a genuine cross-prime dispersion theorem.

A single fixed modular form or fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module supplies none of these variable-character degrees of freedom.

# Bottom line

The exact transformation is:
\[
\boxed{
 b_n=\frac1n[q^{n-1}]Z'(q)\left(\frac q{t(q)}\right)^n,}
\]
not \(b_n=[q^n]g\) for one fixed \(g\).  The associated modular extractor has pole order \(n\), so the Lagrange family is intrinsically infinite-dimensional.

The one genuine fixed-form collapse is the quadratic/half-index channel
\[
\boxed{
 b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv[q^p]\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4\pmod{p^2},}
\]
with the full Beukers \((mp^r-1)/2\) Hecke ray.  For a fixed all-index integer \(n\), every prime in that channel divides \(2n+1\), so its radical mass is only \(O(\log n)\).

More generally all lower digits of Kummer order \(\le D\) have radical mass
\[
O(D^2\log(nD)),
\]
so the unresolved problem is forced into high-order Kummer characters.  Those local types grow with \(p\), which is the rigorous natural obstruction to replacing the whole family by one fixed modular form or one fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module.

This narrows the modular route; it does not prove the all-index Apéry gcd conjecture.
