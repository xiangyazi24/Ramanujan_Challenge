## 2. One stencil is an exact prime-node selector

Let \(F\) be the degree-at-most-\(L\) polynomial satisfying

\[
F(d+i)=Y_{d+i},\qquad0\le i\le L.
\]

Then \(G_{d,L}=F(-1)\). If

\[
q=d+j+1
\]

is prime, \(0\le j\le L\), and \(q>L\), then modulo \(q\) the node \(d+j\) equals \(-1\), while all other stencil nodes remain distinct. Therefore

\[
\boxed{G_{d,L}\equiv Y_{q-1}\pmod q.}
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently every target node contained in a stencil divides its carrier.

The coefficient row is already primitive:

\[
\sum_i w_i^{d,L}=1,
\qquad
\gcd_i w_i^{d,L}=1.
\tag{8}
\]

Thus its large coefficients cannot be removed by scalar content normalization.

## 3. Exact adjacent Smith form

Let \(r_{d,L}\) and \(r_{d+1,L}\) be the two carrier coefficient rows on their union of \(L+2\) columns. Put

\[
\delta_L=\left((-1)^{L+1-i}\binom{L+1}{i}\right)_{0\le i\le L+1}.
\]

Equation (1) is the row identity

\[
r_{d,L}-r_{d+1,L}
=(-1)^{L+1}B_{d,L}\delta_L,
\quad B_{d,L}=\binom{d+L+1}{L}.
\tag{9}
\]

Every two-by-two minor is divisible by \(B_{d,L}\). After dividing the difference row by \(B_{d,L}\), suppose a prime \(p\) divided every two-by-two minor of the primitive matrix with rows \(r_{d,L},\delta_L\). The rows would be proportional modulo \(p\). The final coordinate of \(r_{d,L}\) is zero and that of \(\delta_L\) is one, so proportionality forces \(r_{d,L}=0\pmod p\), contradicting the coefficient sum one. Hence the primitive maximal-minor gcd is one, proving (2).

The common interior moduli are

\[
d+2,d+3,\ldots,d+L+1.
\]

Every prime \(q\) among them with \(q>L\) occurs exactly once in

\[
B_{d,L}=\frac{(d+2)\cdots(d+L+1)}{L!}.
\tag{10}
\]

Thus the common-prime rank drop is already contained in the universal Smith factor.

## 4. Several translated stencils

Take rows

\[
r_t=r_{d+t,L},\qquad0\le t\le m-1,
\]

and put

\[
B_t=\binom{d+t+L+1}{L}.
\]

Successive row differences transform the stack into

\[
r_0,\ B_0\delta_{L,0},\ldots,B_{m-2}\delta_{L,m-2},
\]

where \(\delta_{L,t}\) is the shift of \(\delta_L\) by \(t\). The shifted primitive differences are the coefficient rows of

\[
T^t(T-1)^{L+1}.
\]

They are independent over every field because their highest nonzero columns are distinct. They all vanish under evaluation at \(T=1\), while the carrier row has value one there. Therefore the primitive stack has full rank modulo every prime. This proves the exact maximal-minor content (3).

If a prime node \(q\) lies in every stencil, every carrier row reduces modulo \(q\) to the same functional \(Y_{q-1}\). The row rank is one. Each \(B_t\) has exactly one factor \(q\), so the total valuation of the maximal-minor content is \(m-1\). Hence the local Smith valuations are exactly (4).

This proves the central no-go: overlap multiplicity produces \(m-1\) universal coefficient factors and only one target-selective evaluation factor.

## 6. Varying the scale does not help

Let

\[
t=\gcd(d+1,L),\quad u=(d+1)/t,\quad v=L/t.
\]

Dividing (6) by \(t\) gives the primitive relation

\[
\boxed{vG_{d,L}+uG_{d+1,L-1}=(u+v)G_{d,L-1}.}
\tag{11}

The corresponding Pascal transition presentation has nonzero Smith invariants

\[
1,\quad
\gcd\left(\binom{d+L}{L},\binom{d+L}{L-1}\right),\quad
\binom{d+L+1}{L}.
\tag{12}

Indeed, with

\[
A=\binom{d+L}{L},\quad C=\binom{d+L}{L-1},

\gcd(A,C)=A/u=C/v.

Thus the four carriers at lengths \(L,L-1\) have rank three and add another universal Pascal invariant, not a new target coordinate. Dyadic scale families merely repeat this relation and increase total span by a factor \(\log B\).

## 7. Sharp interval-cover lower bound

Let the block positions be \(0,1,\ldots,N\), and fix \(B\le N\). Suppose every pair

\[
0\le x<y\le N,\qquad y-x\le B
\]

is contained in at least \(m\) intervals. An interval of edge length \(L\) contains at most \(B(L+1)\) such pairs. The block has

\[
B(N+1)-\frac{B(B+1)}2
\]

short pairs. Double counting gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_I(L_I+1)
\ge m\left(N+1-\frac{B+1}{2}\right).}
\tag{13}
\]

For strict common interiors, replace \(L_I+1\) by \(L_I-1\), with only an \(O(mB)\) boundary change.

The order and leading constant are sharp: \(m\) near-full intervals have total span \(mN+O(m)\). Hence dyadic or multiscale covers cannot beat \(\Theta(mN)\) total span.

## 8. Efficient families

### Global optimum

For a block \(I=[D,D+N-1]\), take

\[
G^-_I=G_{D-1,N},\qquad G^+_I=G_{D,N}.
\]

The common nodes are exactly the block nodes. If \(R_I\) is the target radical, then

\[
\boxed{R_I\mid\gcd(G^-_I,G^+_I).}
\tag{14}
\]

The total span is \(2N\), which is optimal.

### Local LAC family

Set

\[
B=\lceil A\log n\rceil.
\]

Use two shifted tilings by cores of \(2B\) consecutive nodes. Every pair of gap at most \(B\) lies in one core. For

\[
K=[u,u+2B-1]

define

\[
G_K^-=G_{u-1,2B},\qquad
G_K^+=G_{u,2B},\qquad
D_K=\gcd(G_K^-,G_K^+).
\tag{15}
\]

There are \(N/B+O(1)\) cores and total stencil span \(4N+O(B)\). This uses a target-blind family of \(O(N/B)\) gcds, not one certificate per pair.

## 9. Height ledger

In a mesoscopic quotient block,

\[
D\gg N^2,\qquad N=H=n^{1/3}.
\]

Before saturation,

\[
\log\binom{d+L+1}{L}
\ge L\log(d/L).
\tag{16}
\]

Combining (13) with \(L\le N\) gives total raw Smith-content height

\[
\boxed{\Omega(N\log(d/N))=\Omega(N\log N).}
\tag{17}
\]

This content is not target-safe to divide: every common-interior prime node divides it.

After full primitive saturation, the remaining finite-difference row has height

\[
\log\binom{L+1}{\lfloor(L+1)/2\rfloor}
=(L+1)\log2+O(\log L).
\tag{18}
\]

The covering lower bound forces total primitive height \(\Omega(N)\). A single global adjacent pair has one order-\(N+1\) finite-difference row and attains \((\log2+o(1))N\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{no interval/Newton presentation has total height }o(H).}
\tag{19}
\]

This remains true after perfect Smith saturation.

## 10. Exact LAC-level reopening theorem

For a local core \(K\), let

\[
\Pi_K=\prod_{\substack{q\text{ prime}\\q-1\in K}}q.
\]

For every prime node in the core, (7) gives

\[
\boxed{q\mid D_K\iff q\mid Y_{q-1}.}
\tag{20}
\]

Hence

\[
\gcd(\operatorname{rad}D_K,\Pi_K)
\]

is exactly the target radical in the core.

Assign every short adjacent target pair to one core containing it. If a core has \(t_K\) target nodes, it receives at most \((t_K-1)_+\) adjacent edges. Remove one prime factor from each core gcd. A target-blind sufficient theorem is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_K
\log\frac{\operatorname{rad}|D_K|}{P^+(|D_K|)}
=o_A(N),}
\tag{21}
\]

where \(P^+(1)=1\). Removing the largest prime factor removes at most one block target; every additional target still divides the quotient. Since block primes have logarithm comparable to \(\log n\), (21) implies

\[
C_A(n,I)=o_A(N/\log n),
\]

which is the required logarithmic-adjacency criterion.

Equation (21) is the precise distinguished-array gcd saving that would globalize the observed phenomenon without using a separate certificate per pair.

## 11. Stronger global reopening theorem

For the optimal two-stencil family, set

\[
D_I=\gcd(G^-_I,G^+_I).

Then

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\operatorname{rad}|D_I|}{P^+(|D_I|)}=o(N)}
\tag{22}

would prove the full block estimate \(\log R_I=o(N)\), because at most one target is deleted and one target costs only \(O(\log n)=o(N)\).

The adjacent law rewrites the gcd as

\[
D_I=\gcd\left(
G_{D-1,N},
\binom{D+N}{N}\Delta^{N+1}Y_{D-1}
\right).
\tag{23}
\]

The binomial factor contains every common block prime node. Therefore one cannot simply divide it and retain targetness. A proof of (22) must use the actual shell values, not only the high-difference identity.

## 12. Exact formal countermodels

Two countermodels show why no matrix identity can prove (21) or (22).

1. **Constant array.** If \(Y_d=K\), then every carrier equals \(K\), because its coefficient sum is one. All finite differences vanish and every overlapping gcd is \(|K|\), with arbitrary height.
2. **Free primitive differences.** On a finite block, the Newton transform between values and finite differences is invertible over \(\mathbf Q\). Once the exact integer indices (2)--(3) are removed, the primitive finite-difference coordinates are independent. The primitive Fitting module contains no hidden small-gcd equation.

Thus any small gcd is arithmetic of the distinguished array

\[
Y_d=C_M(d),
\]

not a consequence of overlap.
