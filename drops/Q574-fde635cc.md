# Q574 — Overconvergent Frobenius, unit roots, and the Apéry first-moment problem

## Executive verdict

Dwork theory gives a **valuable reformulation** of AFP, but it does not presently prove it.

The strongest clean statement available from the slope formalism is this:

> After the correct Gauss/Gross–Koblitz normalization, if the Kummer-twisted Apéry Frobenius block has a **unique minimal-slope eigenvalue**, then its normalized trace is a \(p\)-adic unit, and therefore the corresponding Apéry coefficient \(b_r\) is nonzero modulo \(p\).

Thus one may hope to dominate

\[
 Z_p=\{r:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]

by an exceptional set of nonordinary or first-slope-degenerate Kummer twists. If one could prove

\[
 \sum_{p\le X}\#\{	ext{exceptional twists at }p\}\ll \pi(X),
\]

then AFP would follow.

What Dwork theory **does not** provide is precisely this horizontal exceptional-twist bound. It gives the Frobenius complexes, trace formula, Hodge lower polygon, meromorphy of unit-root \(L\)-functions, and in favorable cases explicit unit-root formulas. It does not imply that only \(O(1)\) of the \(p-1\) tame character blocks are nonordinary, nor that only \(O(1)\) normalized block traces vanish modulo \(p\).

There are four decisive reasons.

1. **The basic operator/rank in the question is misidentified.** The Apéry \(\zeta(3)\) recurrence gives a third-order Picard–Fuchs operator, not the displayed fourth-order one. The generic K3 transcendental crystal has rank \(3\), with ordinary slopes \(0,1,2\), not \(0,1,2,3\).
2. **\(b_r\) is a Mellin coefficient, not a unit-root evaluation.** The variable \(r\) is a Kummer-character exponent, dual to the K3 fiber parameter. The statement \(b_r=u(\omega^r)\) is not correct.
3. **Slopes control eigenvalue valuations, not arbitrary matrix coefficients.** They imply nonvanishing only after one proves a unique-minimal-slope statement for the particular normalized twisted trace.
4. **Overconvergence and bounded cohomological complexity do not imply Mellin anti-sparsity.** A fixed entire function can have almost all of its finite Mellin coefficients equal to zero. The project’s Jacobi-sum counterexample is stronger: a rank-one fixed-conductor sheaf can have \(\gg p\) tame Mellin transforms divisible by \(p\).

There is no known “Dwork–Katz theorem” saying that a full-monodromy hypergeometric motive has only \(O(1)\), or bounded-average, defining-characteristic zero Mellin coefficients. Katz-type theorems give \(\ell\)-adic/complex equidistribution of normalized Mellin transforms. AFP asks for **same-characteristic divisibility at the rare \(1/p\) scale**, which is not detected by those theorems.

The precise surviving target is therefore:

\[
oxed{
	ext{horizontal tame ordinarity / crystalline Mellin anti-concentration}
}
\]

for the \(p-1\) Kummer twists of the Apéry crystal.

From

# 1. The correct Dwork object

Let \(U\) be the smooth locus of the Apéry K3 pencil in the fiber parameter \(a\), and let \(\mathcal V_p\) denote the relevant overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal after removing the explicit Tate part. For a tame character

\[
\chi_r=\omega^{-r},\qquad 0\le r\le p-2,
\]

let \(\mathcal K_{\chi_r}\) be the Kummer \(F\)-isocrystal. The natural twisted object is

\[
\mathcal C_{p,r}
=R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c}
 igl(U,\mathcal V_p\otimes\mathcal K_{\chi_r}igr),
\]

with Frobenius \(\Phi_{p,r}\).

After controlling the finitely many boundary terms, the crystalline analogue of the Grothendieck trace formula identifies the relevant Mellin sum with a Frobenius trace on this twisted complex. One expects an equality of the form

\[
\mathscr M_{p,r}
=\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_{p,r}\mid\mathcal C_{p,r}),
\]

where \(\mathscr M_{p,r}\) is an algebraic lift of the finite Mellin coefficient. To compare with \(b_rmod p\), one must additionally choose an integral lattice and remove the canonical Gauss/Jacobi factor forced by the tame twist.

Write

\[
a_r=rac{r}{p-1}.
\]

In the toric Dwork model, the raw Hodge polygon begins at slope \(a_r\), not at slope \(0\). Accordingly, the useful operator is a **Gauss-normalized Frobenius block**

\[
\widetilde\Phi_{p,r}=G_{p,r}^{-1}\Phi_{p,r},
\]

where \(G_{p,r}\) has \(p\)-adic valuation \(a_r\) and is chosen using the Gross–Koblitz/Stickelberger normalization. The desired integral comparison is then

\[
oxed{
 b_r\equiv u_{p,r}\,\operatorname{Tr}\widetilde\Phi_{p,r}\pmod p,
 \qquad u_{p,r}\in\mathbf F_p^	imes,
}
\]

possibly after deleting \(O(1)\) boundary characters.

This comparison is the correct starting point for a slope argument. It is stronger and more precise than saying vaguely that all \(b_r\) are matrix coefficients of one Frobenius operator.

Indeed, the \(p-1\) coefficients do **not** sit as \(p-1\) entries of one fixed rank-three or rank-four matrix. They are either:

1. traces of \(p-1\) different twisted complexes \(\mathcal C_{p,r}\); or
2. traces of \(p-1\) isotypic blocks inside one direct sum whose total dimension grows like \(p\).

This growth is exactly where bounded-complexity intuition can fail.

# 2. (A) What slopes can and cannot control

## 2.1 Slopes do not control arbitrary matrix coefficients

A Newton polygon records valuations of Frobenius eigenvalues. It is invariant under change of basis. Individual matrix entries are not.

For example,

\[
F=\operatorname{diag}(1,p,p^2)
\]

has slopes \(0,1,2\). In its eigenbasis, six of its nine matrix entries are zero. After conjugating by a generic matrix in \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbf Z_p)\), all nine entries can be nonzero, while the slopes are unchanged.

Therefore no theorem of the form

\[
	ext{“few slope breaks”}\Longrightarrow
	ext{“few zero matrix coefficients”}
\]

can hold without additional structure tying the chosen coefficients to the slope filtration.

The twisted traces are basis-invariant, so there is one useful exception.

## 2.2 The unique-minimal-slope lemma

Let the normalized Frobenius eigenvalues of one twisted block be

\[
\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m.
\]

Suppose

\[
v_p(\alpha_1)=0,\qquad v_p(\alpha_i)>0\quad(i\ge2).
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\widetilde\Phi_{p,r}
=\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_m
\equiv\alpha_1
ot\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Hence:

\[
oxed{
	ext{unique normalized slope }0
\Longrightarrow b_r
ot\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\]

More generally, before normalization, a unique smallest slope \(a_r\), separated from the next slope by at least \(1\), gives the same conclusion after division by \(G_{p,r}\).

This is the exact positive content of the slope route.

Define

\[
E_p^{\mathrm{sl}}
=\left\{r:	ext{the normalized twisted block does not have a unique slope-zero line}ight\}.
\]

Once the integral trace comparison is established,

\[
oxed{|Z_p|\le |E_p^{\mathrm{sl}}|+O(1).}
\]

Thus the following statement would prove AFP:

\[
oxed{
\sum_{p\le X}|E_p^{\mathrm{sl}}|\ll\pi(X).
}
	ag{HTO}
\]

Call this **horizontal tame ordinarity**.

## 2.3 What the Hodge polygon gives

Adolphson–Sperber theory gives a combinatorial Hodge polygon and proves that the Frobenius Newton polygon lies on or above it. In the toric model recorded in `proof.tex`, the minimal Hodge slope \(a_r\) has multiplicity one.

Therefore, if the twist is ordinary at the first break—equivalently, if the Newton polygon meets the Hodge polygon at the first vertex—then the minimal Frobenius slope is unique, and \(b_r\) is forced to be nonzero after the correct normalization.

This turns AFP into a first-break ordinarity problem.

What the general theory does **not** prove is that the first break is ordinary for all but \(O(1)\) values of \(r\), or even for all but \(O(1)\) values on average over \(p\).

Generic ordinarity theorems usually vary the coefficients of a Laurent polynomial in a positive-dimensional parameter space. Here the Laurent polynomial/motive is fixed and the parameter runs through all \(p-1\) tame characters. Those are different problems.

## 2.4 Overconvergence radius does not bound Mellin zeros

There are two elementary counterexamples to the radius heuristic.

### Evaluation zeros

The polynomial

\[
f_p(T)=T^{p-1}-1
\]

is entire, hence overconvergent on every fixed strict neighborhood, yet it vanishes at all \(p-1\) Teichmüller units.

Thus an overconvergence radius alone does not bound the number of Teichmüller evaluation zeros. One would need a uniform bound on the Weierstrass degree, not merely a radius of convergence.

### Mellin-coefficient zeros

The fixed polynomial

\[
f(T)=1+T
\]

has bounded degree and infinite radius of convergence. Nevertheless, its finite Mellin transform satisfies

\[
\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^	imes}(1+t)t^{-r}=0
\]

for every \(r
ot\equiv0,1\pmod{p-1}\). Thus it has \(p-3\) vanishing nontrivial Mellin coefficients.

So even **uniformly bounded analytic complexity can force almost complete Mellin sparsity**. Overconvergence is a regularity statement in the physical variable; AFP is an anti-sparsity statement in the Fourier-dual variable. There is no implication between them in the desired direction.

## 2.5 The unit-root subobject need not be overconvergent

Even the premise that the slope-zero line itself is represented by an overconvergent function is not automatic. The ambient geometric \(F\)-isocrystal may be overconvergent while its slope filtration is only convergent. Etesse gives explicit elliptic-family examples and shows that the unit-root sub-\(F\)-isocrystal of the ordinary Legendre family is not overconvergent in Berthelot’s sense.

This phenomenon is one reason Dwork’s unit-root \(L\)-function conjecture was nontrivial: the ordinary ambient object is overconvergent, but the extracted unit-root part generally is not.

Even if the Apéry unit-root function has a stronger overconvergence property, Sections 2.3–2.4 show that the required Mellin zero bound still does not follow.

### Answer to (A)

Dwork slopes can yield the implication

\[
	ext{unique first slope}\Longrightarrow b_r
e0\pmod p,
\]

but they do not control zero matrix coefficients in general, and overconvergence radius does not control zero Mellin coefficients. The missing input is the average horizontal tame-ordinarity estimate (HTO).

# 3. (B) What the Dwork trace formula gives across twists

## 3.1 What “the same Frobenius, different twists” really means

Dwork’s trace formula can package the Kummer twists uniformly. There are two equivalent descriptions.

1. One constructs a family of Frobenius matrices whose entries depend on the character exponent \(r\).
2. One passes to a Kummer cover and decomposes a larger cohomology group into the \(p-1\) character isotypic components.

In the second description there is literally one Frobenius operator, but it acts on a space of total dimension \(\asymp p\). Uniform bounded rank of each block does not make the total family bounded-dimensional.

Moreover, the different blocks are not all conjugate. The twist changes the exponent lattice, Gauss factors, Hodge weights, and integral structure. The phrase “same Frobenius” is therefore useful organizationally but does not create probabilistic independence or nonvanishing.

## 3.2 What the trace formula can prove

For this family it can reasonably provide:

- finite-dimensional rigid cohomology after reduction from the nuclear Dwork complex;
- a Frobenius determinant expression for each twisted \(L\)-function;
- rationality for the ordinary twisted \(L\)-functions and \(p\)-adic meromorphy for unit-root \(L\)-functions;
- uniform bounds on rank, local monodromy, and cohomological degree;
- a combinatorial Hodge lower polygon;
- functional equations and dualities in favorable hypergeometric/toric cases;
- explicit formulas for the first Frobenius block through Dwork splitting functions and \(p\)-adic gamma values;
- congruences between the first normalized trace and the finite hypergeometric/Mellin coefficient.

These are substantial outputs. None is a bound on the number of zero normalized traces.

## 3.3 Trace cancellation is not a slope defect unless the first slope is simple

If a twisted block has two or more unit eigenvalues, their sum may vanish modulo \(p\). Thus even complete knowledge of the multiset of slopes does not determine the trace valuation.

For example, a normalized block with eigenvalues \(1,-1,peta\) has slopes \(0,0,\ge1\) but trace divisible by \(p\). Replacing \(-1\) by \(2\) preserves the slopes and makes the trace a unit.

Therefore one needs either:

- a unique minimal slope; or
- extra information on the reduction of the unit-root Frobenius matrix preventing cancellation.

The Newton polygon alone supplies neither in the degenerate case.

## 3.4 “Generic vanishing” is a different statement

Katz and Forey–Fresán–Kowalski prove generic/stratified vanishing theorems for the **cohomology groups** of character twists and use them to construct Mellin/Fourier equidistribution categories.

Here “generic vanishing” means that, for generic characters, cohomology is concentrated in the expected degree. It does not mean that the numerical Frobenius trace is nonzero, and still less that it is nonzero modulo the residue characteristic.

Thus those theorems justify the bounded-rank twisted trace package. They do not prove AFP.

## 3.5 The zero detector creates a second Fourier layer

Even if the normalized traces

\[
m_{p,r}=\operatorname{Tr}\widetilde\Phi_{p,r}mod p
\]

are trace functions in \(r\), the exact zero detector is

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{m_{p,r}=0\}}
=rac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(um_{p,r}).
\]

Hence

\[
|Z_p|
=rac{p-1}{p}
+rac1p\sum_{u
e0}\sum_r e_p(um_{p,r}).
\]

The inner sum is an additive Fourier transform of the **Mellin-transform values**. This is the “second Fourier layer” already isolated in `proof.tex`. Ordinary Dwork/Katz monodromy controls the first layer. It does not automatically construct a bounded-conductor object for the nonlinear function \(r\mapsto e_p(um_{p,r})\).

A square-root estimate for every fixed \(u\) would still give only

\[
|Z_p|=1+O(\sqrt p),
\]

because there are \(p-1\) values of \(u\). AFP requires cancellation stronger than applying Weil independently to all zero-detector frequencies.

## 3.6 The project’s Jacobi counterexample is a decisive no-go theorem

`proof.tex` contains the following fixed-complexity example. Fix \(d\ge2\), and for primes \(p\equiv1\pmod d\) let

\[
\psi=\omega^{-(p-1)/d}.
\]

For the rank-one Kummer sheaf on
\(\mathbf P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}\), the Mellin transforms are Jacobi sums

\[
M_j=J(\omega^{-j},\psi).
\]

Gross–Koblitz/Stickelberger gives

\[
v_p(M_j)=1
\]

for a terminal interval of about \((p-1)/d\) character exponents. Thus

\[
\#\{j:p\mid M_j\}\gg p
\]

although the sheaf has rank \(1\), fixed conductor, an explicit Dwork trace formula, and completely understood slopes.

This does not rule out an Apéry-specific theorem using the symmetric-square/K3 structure. It does rule out every inference based only on:

- bounded rank;
- bounded conductor;
- overconvergence;
- existence of a uniform trace formula;
- a bounded number of Hodge slopes.

## 3.7 The exact missing theorem after the trace formula

Let \(H_{p,r}\) be the first-break Hasse scalar obtained by reducing the Gauss-normalized minimal-slope Frobenius block. In the rank-one first Hodge piece,

\[
H_{p,r}
e0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
	ext{unique minimal slope}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
b_r
e0\pmod p.
\]

Dwork theory can in principle construct \(H_{p,r}\) explicitly. AFP would follow from

\[
oxed{
\sum_{p\le X}\#\{r:H_{p,r}=0\}\ll\pi(X).
}
	ag{FH}
\]

But no general theorem bounds the number of zero Hasse scalars across all tame characters.

There is also a serious possibility that, after the correct comparison, \(H_{p,r}\) is simply a unit multiple of \(b_r\). In that case (FH) is exactly AFP written in Dwork language. The slope reformulation would be correct but tautological.

### Answer to (B)

The uniform trace formula packages the problem and identifies a strong sufficient first-slope condition. It does not provide the horizontal nonordinary-twist count. The gap is not cohomological construction; it is mod-\(p\) anti-concentration of the first normalized Frobenius block as the tame character varies.

# 4. (C) Why the unit-root \(L\)-function does not count \(Z_p\)

## 4.1 The \(T\)-variable and the \(r\)-variable are different

For a unit-root family over a base \(U/\mathbf F_p\), the global unit-root \(L\)-function has the form

\[
L_{\mathrm{unit}}(T)
=\prod_{x\in|U|}
 \left(1-u(x)T^{\deg x}ight)^{-1}
\]

or equivalently

\[
L_{\mathrm{unit}}(T)
=\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}rac{T^n}{n}\,S_night),
\]

where \(S_n\) is a sum of powers of local unit roots over \(\mathbf F_{p^n}\).

Zeros and poles in \(T\) describe the spectrum of a global Frobenius/Dwork operator as the extension degree \(n\) varies.

AFP varies the tame Kummer exponent

\[
r=0,\ldots,p-2.
\]

That is an entirely different direction.

For each character one may form a twisted \(L\)-function

\[
L_r(T)=L(\mathcal V_p\otimes\mathcal K_{\chi_r},T).
\]

The normalized \(b_r\) is related to the first logarithmic coefficient of \(L_r(T)\), not to a zero or pole of one fixed untwisted \(L\)-function.

Knowing the zeros and poles of the product

\[
\prod_r L_r(T)
\]

does not control how often the first coefficient of an individual factor is divisible by \(p\). The product loses the character labels and allows cancellation within every factor.

## 4.2 What Wan’s theorem says

Wan proved Dwork’s conjecture: unit-root \(L\)-functions coming from ordinary geometric families are \(p\)-adically meromorphic. This is an analytic continuation theorem. It is not a zero-density theorem for the first coefficients of \(p-1\) different tame twists.

Haessig–Sperber prove much more in certain toric deformation families: the relevant unit-root \(L\)-function has a unique \(p\)-adic unit zero and admits an explicit hypergeometric-ratio formula. They also emphasize that very little is known about zeros and poles of unit-root \(L\)-functions in general.

Even their unique-unit-zero theorem concerns the global \(T\)-spectrum of one unit-root \(L\)-function. It does not identify the \(p-1\) Apéry coefficients with those zeros.

## 4.3 Iwasawa variation is vertical, AFP is horizontal

The \(p\)-adic weight space of continuous characters of \(\mathbf Z_p^	imes\) decomposes as

\[
\mathcal W_p
=igsqcup_{a\in\mathbf Z/(p-1)\mathbf Z}\mathcal W_{p,a},
\]

where each \(\mathcal W_{p,a}\) is one open \(p\)-adic disc corresponding to a fixed tame Teichmüller character \(\omega^a\).

Iwasawa/Weierstrass theory controls zeros as the wild parameter moves **inside one disc**:

\[
\omega^a\langle\cdotangle^s,\qquad s\in\mathbf Z_p.
\]

The Apéry family

\[
\omega^0,\omega^{-1},\ldots,\omega^{-(p-2)}
\]

samples one point from each of the \(p-1\) distinct discs. A bounded \(\lambda\)-invariant on every disc gives no bound on the number of discs whose distinguished point is a zero.

This is exactly the branch obstruction from Q564.

Likewise, Liu–Wan \(T\)-adic theory interpolates \(p\)-power-order characters. It gives deep control in the wild tower, but the Apéry exponents run through the prime-to-\(p\) tame characters.

## 4.4 A simple coefficient/root mismatch

Let

\[
L_r(T)=\prod_i(1-\alpha_{r,i}T)^{-1}.
\]

Then

\[
[T]\log L_r(T)=\sum_i\alpha_{r,i}.
\]

The location of the reciprocal roots \(\alpha_{r,i}^{-1}\) gives their valuations. It does not determine whether their sum vanishes modulo \(p\) unless one root has strictly smaller valuation than all the others.

Thus the unit-root \(L\)-function route reduces to the same unique-first-slope criterion as Section 2; it does not bypass it.

### Answer to (C)

Zeros and poles of \(L_{\mathrm{unit}}(T)\) control extension-degree Frobenius spectra. They do not control the horizontal tame-character zero set \(Z_p\). Iwasawa theory acts within a fixed tame branch, while AFP samples all branches once. No known zero/pole theorem supplies the missing cross-branch bound.

# 5. (D) Is there a Dwork–Katz AFP theorem?

## 5.1 No theorem of the requested form is currently known

As of August 2026, there is no standard theorem asserting that a rank-three or rank-four hypergeometric motive with full monodromy has

\[
\#\{r:	ext{its Kummer Mellin coefficient vanishes mod }p\}=O(1),
\]

nor the averaged version

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\#\{r:\cdots\}\ll\pi(X).
\]

The closest theorem families stop at different boundaries.

| Theory | What it gives | Why it does not give AFP |
|---|---|---|
| Adolphson–Sperber Dwork cohomology | Rationality, degree, Hodge polygon, Newton-above-Hodge, explicit twisted complexes | No bound on the number of tame twists where the first Hodge break fails to be ordinary |
| Wan’s proof of Dwork’s conjecture | \(p\)-adic meromorphy of unit-root \(L\)-functions | Meromorphy in \(T\) does not bound first trace coefficients across \(p-1\) tame branches |
| Dwork/Adolphson–Sperber unit-root formulas | Explicit unit root as a ratio of \(p\)-adic hypergeometric functions in suitable toric families | Physical-parameter formula, not horizontal mod-\(p\) Mellin anti-concentration |
| Katz finite-field Mellin equidistribution | Distribution of normalized \(\ell\)-adic Mellin transforms as characters vary | Controls complex/\(\ell\)-adic conjugacy classes, not divisibility by the residue characteristic |
| Forey–Fresán–Kowalski | Generic cohomological vanishing and character equidistribution; a horizontal framework | Their “vanishing” is cohomology vanishing, and their horizontal quantitative theory does not address same-characteristic trace divisibility |
| Gross–Koblitz/Stickelberger | Exact \(p\)-adic valuations of Gauss/Jacobi sums | Shows that fixed-complexity families can have \(\gg p\) bad tame characters |
| Iwasawa and \(T\)-adic variation | Analytic variation in wild \(p\)-power character directions | AFP moves across the \(p-1\) tame components |

## 5.2 Why full monodromy is not enough

Full geometric monodromy is an \(\ell\)-adic or characteristic-zero algebraic statement. It controls invariant tensors and the limiting distribution of normalized Frobenius conjugacy classes.

AFP is sensitive to:

- the integral crystalline lattice;
- the residue prime \(p\);
- the first Newton slope;
- cancellation of unit eigenvalues modulo \(p\);
- Gauss-sum normalizations depending on \(r/(p-1)\).

These data are not determined by the Zariski closure of the \(\ell\)-adic monodromy representation.

The modular-form slice in the project is a useful warning. The central coefficient satisfies

\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_4)\pmod p
\]

for the non-CM weight-four form \(f_4=\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4\). Its compatible Galois representations have large image, but the distribution of nonordinary primes \(p\mid a_p(f_4)\) is a difficult same-characteristic problem. Large monodromy does not settle it.

This slice contributes at most one zero per prime, so it does not obstruct AFP numerically. It demonstrates the logical point: full monodromy does not imply mod-\(p\) nonvanishing.

## 5.3 The rare-event scale is below ordinary equidistribution error

If \(m_{p,r}\) behaved uniformly in \(\mathbf F_p\), one would expect about one zero among \(p-1\) values. Detecting one residue class therefore requires accuracy at scale \(1/p\).

Deligne/Katz equidistribution typically gives square-root discrepancy for fixed test functions. Even an \(O(p^{-1/2})\) error in normalized proportions is much larger than the \(1/p\) main term for a single residue class.

Moreover, the indicator of “trace \(=0\) modulo \(p\)” is not a fixed continuous class function on a compact monodromy group; it changes with \(p\) and depends on the crystalline integral structure.

Thus Katz equidistribution is compatible with the Poisson heuristic but cannot certify AFP at the required residue-class resolution.

### Answer to (D)

There is no direct Dwork–Katz AFP theorem. The nearest available results build the correct twisted cohomology and predict randomness, but the defining-characteristic zero detector lies beyond their resolution.

# 6. The precise gap, stated as two conjectures

## 6.1 Direct crystalline Mellin anti-concentration

Let \(\widetilde\Phi_{p,r}\) be the correctly normalized integral Frobenius block and assume the comparison

\[
b_r\equiv u_{p,r}\operatorname{Tr}\widetilde\Phi_{p,r}\pmod p,
\qquad u_{p,r}\in\mathbf F_p^	imes.
\]

Then AFP is exactly

\[
oxed{
\sum_{p\le X}\#\left\{0\le r\le p-2:
 v_p\!\left(\operatorname{Tr}\widetilde\Phi_{p,r}ight)>0
ight\}\ll\pi(X).
}
	ag{CMA}
\]

This is the cleanest formulation: **crystalline Mellin anti-concentration**.

## 6.2 Stronger first-slope sufficient condition

Let \(E_p^{\mathrm{sl}}\) be the set of twists for which the normalized minimal slope has multiplicity other than one. Then

\[
Z_p\subseteq E_p^{\mathrm{sl}}+O(1),
\]

and the stronger condition

\[
oxed{
\sum_{p\le X}|E_p^{\mathrm{sl}}|\ll\pi(X)
}
	ag{HTO}
\]

implies AFP.

This is a genuinely slope-theoretic target. It is also stronger than necessary, since a block with several unit roots may still have unit trace.

## 6.3 First Hasse scalar formulation

When the first Hodge piece has rank one, let \(H_{p,r}\) be the reduction of the first normalized Frobenius block. Then

\[
H_{p,r}
e0\Longrightarrow r
otin Z_p.
\]

The desired theorem is

\[
oxed{
\sum_{p\le X}\#\{r:H_{p,r}=0\}\ll\pi(X).
}
	ag{FH}
\]

This formulation identifies exactly where a new argument must enter. Dwork constructs \(H_{p,r}\); it does not bound its horizontal zero set.

# 7. Why ordinary algebraic root bounds are unlikely to close (FH)

A natural hope is that \(r\mapsto H_{p,r}\) is a bounded-degree Laurent polynomial on the character group. The current project already gives two warnings against this.

1. The direct character interpolation of the unnormalized Mellin coefficients has Laurent degree at least
   
   \[
   D\gerac{p-7}{4}.
   \]
   
   Thus the raw tame Hasse function has linear complexity.
2. The Mahler/Strassmann audit in Q564 found an essentially flat Newton polygon and Strassmann index \(p-2\) or \(p-3\) for every prime \(11\le p\le101\).

Consequently a successful Dwork route needs a **nontrivial normalization or factorization**, not just a different interpolation basis.

The genuinely promising possibility is an explicit formula

\[
H_{p,r}
=U_{p,r}\,Q_p(\omega^r),
\qquad U_{p,r}\in\mathbf F_p^	imes,
\]

where \(Q_p\) has bounded degree only after removing canonical Gauss/Jacobi factors and symmetric-square carry terms. No such formula is currently known for the Apéry family.

The Jacobi example shows that the normalization is essential: Stickelberger carries can themselves produce a linear interval of positive valuations.

# 8. A concrete research program for this route

The Dwork approach is not useless; it gives a sharply testable program. The following sequence avoids repeating the closed Strassmann and monodromy routes.

## Step 1. Fix the exact crystal and normalization

Use the toric Laurent polynomial and K3 collapse already proved in:

- [`toric_mellin_square.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/toric_mellin_square.tex),
- [`toric_fiber_k3.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/toric_fiber_k3.tex),
- [`toric_fiber_sym2.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/toric_fiber_sym2.tex).

Construct the twisted rigid complex and state exactly which scalar Gauss factor makes its first Frobenius trace integral.

The output should be a theorem, not a heuristic:

\[
b_r\equiv u_{p,r}\operatorname{Tr}\widetilde\Phi_{p,r}\pmod p.
\]

## Step 2. Compute the first Hasse block symbolically

Use Dwork’s splitting function and the Kummer exponent \(a_r=r/(p-1)\) to compute the reduction of the minimal Hodge block.

The key diagnostic is:

\[
H_{p,r}\stackrel{?}{=}u_{p,r}b_r.
\]

If yes, the slope route is a tautological repackaging and should be closed immediately.

If no, determine the exact relation between \(H_{p,r}\) and \(b_r\), including all boundary and Gauss factors.

## Step 3. Separate slope failure from unit-root cancellation

For each twist distinguish:

1. no normalized slope-zero eigenvalue;
2. exactly one normalized slope-zero eigenvalue;
3. at least two normalized slope-zero eigenvalues;
4. unique slope-zero eigenvalue but a comparison/boundary anomaly.

Only case 2 gives automatic nonvanishing. This audit will tell whether the observed Apéry zeros are primarily failures of first-break ordinarity or cancellations among multiple unit roots.

That distinction is mathematically decisive. If most zeros occur in case 3, Newton polygons alone can never prove AFP.

## Step 4. Search for a Gross–Koblitz carry law

Express \(H_{p,r}\) through \(p\)-adic gamma values and isolate the Stickelberger carry terms. The best possible outcome is that all positive valuations arise from a bounded list of congruence/carry configurations specific to the Apéry hypergeometric parameters.

A theorem of the form

\[
\#\{r:	ext{Apéry carry defect at }r\}\ll1
\]

on average over \(p\) would prove AFP.

This is more plausible than an overconvergence-radius argument because it attacks the defining-characteristic valuation directly.

## Step 5. Prove horizontal, not vertical, variation

Any Iwasawa family used must vary the **tame branch index**. Variation only in

\[
r,\ r+(p-1),\ r+(p-1)p,\ldots
\]

is vertical and does not address AFP.

The needed object is a finite-character or cyclotomic family that retains all \(p-1\) branches simultaneously and gives arithmetic control of the first Hasse scalar across them.

## Step 6. Aim first for AFP, not pointwise \(O(1)\)

The data predict that \(|Z_p|\) is unbounded with a \(2\cdot\mathrm{Poisson}(1/2)\) law. Therefore a pointwise theorem \(|E_p^{\mathrm{sl}}|\le C\) may itself be false.

The natural target is the prime average

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|E_p^{\mathrm{sl}}|\ll\pi(X),
\]

or directly (CMA), not a uniform bound for every prime.

# 9. Deliverables (A)–(D), condensed

## (A) Slopes and overconvergence

- Slopes do **not** control arbitrary matrix coefficients.
- A unique normalized minimal slope **does** force the normalized twisted trace, hence \(b_r\), to be a unit.
- The Apéry K3 ordinary transcendental slopes are \(0,1,2\), not \(0,1,2,3\).
- \(b_r\) is a Mellin coefficient, not an evaluation \(u(\omega^r)\).
- Overconvergence radius does not bound Mellin zeros; even \(1+T\) has \(p-3\) zero nontrivial finite Mellin coefficients.
- The missing theorem is horizontal tame ordinarity across the \(p-1\) twists.

## (B) Uniform Dwork trace formula

- It uniformly constructs the twisted Frobenius blocks and their \(L\)-functions.
- Packaging all twists uses a space of total dimension \(\asymp p\).
- Uniform rank/conductor and Newton-above-Hodge do not imply trace nonvanishing.
- The Jacobi-sum example proves that fixed Dwork complexity can coexist with \(\gg p\) bad tame characters.
- The gap is an average zero bound for the first normalized Hasse block \(H_{p,r}\).

## (C) Unit-root \(L\)-functions

- Zeros/poles in \(T\) concern extension-degree Frobenius spectra, not the Kummer exponent \(r\).
- \(b_r\) is a first coefficient of a **different twisted \(L_r(T)\)** for each \(r\), not a zero or pole of one global \(L\)-function.
- Wan’s theorem gives meromorphy, not a horizontal tame-character zero-density estimate.
- Iwasawa theory controls one tame branch at a time; AFP samples all \(p-1\) branches once.

## (D) Dwork–Katz theorem

- No theorem currently gives M1/AFP for full-monodromy hypergeometric motives.
- Katz/Forey–Fresán–Kowalski give \(\ell\)-adic equidistribution of normalized Mellin transforms, not mod-\(p\) divisibility.
- Adolphson–Sperber give Hodge/Newton bounds, not an \(O(1)\) exceptional-twist theorem.
- Full monodromy does not determine crystalline slopes or integral trace cancellation.

# 10. Recommended statement for `proof.tex`

The following paragraph accurately records the status.

> The Apéry Mellin coefficients admit a uniform Dwork realization through the Kummer twists of the primitive K3 \(F\)-isocrystal. After a character-dependent Gauss normalization, a twist with a unique minimal Frobenius slope has unit normalized trace and therefore cannot contribute to \(Z(p)\). Hence Hypothesis \(ar Z\) would follow from a bounded-average theorem for the twists at which the first Newton break fails to be ordinary. Existing overconvergent-cohomology theory supplies the twisted complexes, Hodge lower polygons, trace formulas, and unit-root \(L\)-function meromorphy, but no theorem bounds this horizontal exceptional set. Overconvergence in the fiber parameter does not imply anti-sparsity of its finite Mellin transform; fixed entire functions may have \(\gg p\) zero Mellin coefficients, and Stickelberger’s formula gives fixed-conductor Jacobi families with a positive proportion of \(p\)-divisible twists. Thus the remaining single-prime problem is a genuinely Apéry-specific crystalline Mellin anti-concentration theorem, not a formal consequence of Dwork meromorphy or Katz monodromy.

## Bottom line

Dwork theory reaches the correct doorstep:

\[
oxed{
	ext{Apéry zero}\Rightarrow
	ext{failure of unique normalized first slope}
}
\]

provided the integral Gauss-normalized trace comparison is made explicit.

The door that remains closed is

\[
oxed{
\sum_{p\le X}\#\{	ext{first-slope-exceptional tame twists}\}\ll\pi(X).
}
\]

Neither overconvergence radius, unit-root \(L\)-function meromorphy, full monodromy, nor existing Katz equidistribution proves that estimate. A successful route must extract an Apéry-specific formula for the first normalized Frobenius block—most likely through Gross–Koblitz carry arithmetic and the symmetric-square/cover-adapted structure—and then prove its horizontal nonvanishing on average.
