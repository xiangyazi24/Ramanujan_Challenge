## A.3 The three precise mismatches with existing large sieves

### 1. The selected-character mismatch

Bah–Shuddhodan prove varying-characteristic Mellin equidistribution for bounded-complexity perverse sheaves with a common tannakian group. This is the strongest nearby theorem and it is directly relevant to the new fixed-\(\mathcal W\) realization.

But it averages over essentially all multiplicative characters of each finite field. Our datum is one deterministic section

\[
\chi_p=\omega_p^{\,j_p},\qquad j_p=mmod(p-1)
\]

(or its quotient-cell normalization). One character has relative mass \(1/(p-1)\). An all-character equidistribution theorem permits that selected character to be exceptional for every \(p\) without changing the empirical measure.

There is also an observable mismatch: the theorem controls complex/unitary Frobenius conjugacy classes. The condition \(\mathfrak p_p\mid T_{j,p}\) is a microscopic \(p\)-adic integrality condition. Complex equidistribution does not detect it.

### 2. The defining-characteristic diagonal

\[

Our condition is exactly the forbidden diagonal

\[

\]

Letting the base prime vary does not repair this. At each new base prime, the forbidden auxiliary prime changes to that same prime. There is no legitimate specialization of an auxiliary-\(\ell\) Frobenius sieve to \(\ell=p\).

Kedlaya’s crystalline companion theorem supplies a \(p\)-adic companion for each fixed characteristic \(p\), with matching Frobenius polynomials. It does not couple the crystalline companions for different rational primes and does not provide a product-sieve mechanism across those primes.

## C.3 Why degree-\(\le2\) factors do not create cross-prime independence

The fact that every root of \(B_p\) lies in \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) means

\[
B_p(a)\mid a^{p^2}-a
\]

and identifies the root divisor with a supersingular divisor of the level-6 family. This is excellent input for modular geometry and for proving the packet theorem.

It is not a bounded-dimensional factorization of the coefficient event:

- \(B_p\) still has \(M_p\asymp p/2\) roots;
- its coefficients are elementary symmetric functions of all those roots;
- \(b_r\) is a quadratic combination of those coefficients;
- the number of degree-one or degree-two factors is still \(\asymp p\);
- the polynomial and its splitting field data change with \(p\).

Chebotarev can exploit a bounded number of Frobenius orbits in a fixed number field. Here there are linearly many orbits in a finite-field divisor that itself varies with \(p\). “Every orbit has size at most two” is therefore vertical rationality, not cross-prime independence.

Equidistribution of supersingular points against fixed low-degree test functions would also be insufficient: the coefficient \(b_r\) for \(r\asymp p\) is a high-degree elementary-symmetric observable, not a fixed test function.

## C.4 The two legitimate ways to combine the structures

### Route 1: symmetric-square/Asai convolution

The factorization and the direct elliptic realization explain why the rank-three K3 trace descends from a rank-two elliptic object and why the Mellin \(H^1\) has dimension two. This is the correct representation-theoretic meaning of the square.

But trace zero modulo \(p\) is cancellation in that two-dimensional motive, equivalently loss of the unit root. It is not the intersection of two independent root events. The convolution structure may help identify monodromy and integral lattices, but it does not by itself supply a \(2+\delta\) moment.

### Route 2: exact differential and critical-value geometry

The coefficient condition \(b_r=0\) is exactly what makes

\[
a^{-r-1}A_p(a)\,da
\]

an exact differential. The square factorization then makes its primitive a cover with triple ramification at the roots of \(B_p\). This is the strongest genuine bridge between coefficient space and root geometry.

The packet theorem belongs here. If the critical values descend through a fixed degree-three modular coordinate, then the root geometry has been compressed to bounded degree. The remaining question is whether the **zero index \(r\)** or a packet label also descends to a fixed arithmetic object across primes. Only that extra descent could feed a Chebotarev/large-sieve argument.

## C.5 What would be enough for \((\mathrm{HM})_{2+\delta}\)

One of the following genuinely new statements would be sufficient.

### Bounded-degree arithmetic resolvent

Construct a fixed finite-type arithmetic scheme \(\mathscr R/\mathbf Z[1/6]\), a bounded-degree map to the character/index coordinate, and a conjugacy condition \(C\) such that

\[
r\in Z_p\iff \operatorname{Frob}_{p,r}\in C
\]

for the relevant points, with product monodromy for distinct primes. Then ordinary large-sieve technology could plausibly enter—provided the condition is imposed at an auxiliary prime, or the construction somehow removes the defining-characteristic diagonal.

At present the marked Hurwitz cover has degree \(p-1\) and \(\gg p\) branch values, so such a bounded-degree resolvent is not yet present.

### Horizontal Fourier law for supersingular coefficient zeros

Prove directly the mixed-prime estimate (A.6) for

\[
F_p(u)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(ur).
\]

This would be a theorem about positions of nonordinary Kummer Mellin characters across varying characteristics. It is exactly the missing input, but no current supersingular equidistribution theorem has this strength or these quantifiers.

### A global packet label

Show that every zero \(r\in Z_p\) determines a bounded-height algebraic packet invariant \(\eta_{p,r}\) which is the reduction of a characteristic-zero object depending on the quotient cell, and that many simultaneous prime hits force many primes to divide one nonzero integer of controlled height. This would convert packet rigidity into the atom-tail/divisor mechanism needed by the GCD problem.

Without one of these additions, degree-\(\le2\) factorization of \(B_p\) is not enough.

# Recommended frontier program

The highest-value next steps are now quite concrete.

## 1. Run the degree-three packet-label test first

For every stored packet through \(p\le3000\):

1. lift each \(B_p\)-root \(a\) to the two \(x\)-points satisfying \(\phi(x)=a\);
2. compute the unordered pair \(\mathcal Y(a)=\{y(x),y(\sigma x)\}\);
3. test implication (B.11);
4. record whether packet size \(1,2,3\) is exactly explained by a fiber of \(y\), by the deck involution, or by another fixed level-6 correspondence.

This experiment cleanly distinguishes “degree-three modular descent” from a vague ODE explanation.

## 2. Compute the symmetric-square Hermite reduction

Using the exact second-order operator for \(B_p\), reduce the primitive \(H_{p,r}\) to the basis

\[
B_p^2,\quad B_pB_p',\quad (B_p')^2.
\]

The target is (B.4), followed by the root-value formula (B.5). This is the algebraic interface needed to prove any modular packet identity.

## 3. Prove packet \(\le3\), but state its logical output honestly

The theorem should be packaged as:

- an Apéry-specific critical-value theorem;
- a monodromy/indecomposability corollary if available;
- an explicit statement that it gives \(\gg p\) branch values and therefore does not yield bounded conductor;
- no claim of \((\mathrm{HM})_{2+\delta}\) without a separate cross-prime bridge.

## 4. Formulate the missing horizontal theorem in the exact CRT/Fourier form

Do not formulate it merely as “Frobenius traces at different primes are independent.” Use (A.6) or \((\mathrm{DM})_k\). This prevents an all-character or auxiliary-\(\ell\) theorem from being mistaken for the needed result.

## 5. Search for a global packet invariant only after step 1

If equal critical values are exactly degree-three modular fibers, inspect the corresponding \(y\)-value, modular polynomial, or isogeny class for a characteristic-zero lift with controlled height. That is the one scenario in which the packet theorem could plausibly connect to an atom-tail argument across primes.

```text
Most likely outcome tree

packet = degree-3 modular fiber
    -> prove with ODE + modular pullback
    -> search for a bounded-height/global packet label
        -> if found: possible atom-tail / moving-prime sieve route
        -> if not found: strong vertical theorem, no HM implication

packet not explained by degree-3 fiber
    -> classify finite level-6 correspondences
    -> if correspondence degree is still bounded, same next step
    -> otherwise packet bound remains isolated vertical rigidity
```

# Bottom line

The new direct Mellin realization and the packet computation are both substantial advances, but they address different layers.

- The fixed sheaf solves the **geometric uniformity** problem.
- The Hasse–Franel square root solves the **rank reduction and exact-differential** problem.
- The packet bound appears to expose a **degree-three modular rigidity**.
- The remaining GCD barrier is still the **horizontal defining-characteristic CRT dispersion** of the zero positions.

The best immediate theorem to attack is the modular packet identity (B.12). It is concrete, falsifiable with the existing data, and has a plausible explanation for the exact constant \(3\). The best formulation of the remaining all-\(n\) barrier is \((\mathrm{DM})_{2+\delta}\), not ordinary Chebotarev independence.
