### (A) Fixed sheaf and moving-prime independence

The fact that `W` is one fixed sheaf over `Z[1/6]` is genuinely valuable. It gives uniform rank, purity, tame local monodromy, conductor, a common geometric monodromy problem, and an integral compatible-system framework away from the defining characteristic.

It does **not**, however, place the required events inside an existing Chebotarev or Frobenius-large-sieve theorem. There are three simultaneous diagonal obstructions:

1. one deterministic Kummer character is selected in each finite field, instead of averaging over all characters;
2. divisibility is tested at the defining prime `𝔭_p | p`, not at an auxiliary residual prime `λ | ℓ` with `ℓ ≠ p`;
3. `(HM)_(2+δ)` is an incomplete-CRT discrepancy statement over `m < X²`, not merely a one-prime trace-distribution statement.

The closest current result, Bah–Shuddhodan’s varying-characteristic Mellin equidistribution, solves a major geometric-uniformity issue but not any of these three diagonal issues. I do not find an off-the-shelf moving-modulus estimate that gives `(HM)_(2+δ)`. The genuinely missing result is a new **diagonal crystalline Mellin large sieve**.

### (B) Why the critical-value packet size appears to be at most three

The ODE should provide an algebraic reduction of each critical value to a quadratic expression in `B_p` and `B_p'`. The modular degree-three map should provide the global collision bound. The exact number `3` is too well matched to this degree-three descent to ignore. There is a decisive quotient-ring test below.

A proof of packet `≤ 3` would be an important Apéry-specific theorem and, with mild extra hypotheses, could give very large geometric monodromy for the marked covers. It does **not by itself** imply `(HM)_(2+δ)`, because it is a single-prime theorem and does not control how the zero indices `r ∈ Z_p` align across different primes. It also implies that the direct branch locus has linearly many distinct values, confirming that the closed bounded-conductor route cannot be revived.

### (C) Hasse–Franel factorization versus coefficient zeros

The premise that the zero indicator factors through evaluations of `B_p` is false. The identity

```text
A_p(a) = Δ(a)^ε_p B_p(a)²
```

is an identity in the **evaluation variable** `a`, whereas

```text
r ∈ Z_p  ⇔  b_r = coefficient of a^r in A_p(a) = 0
```

is a **coefficient/Mellin** condition. The roots of `B_p` and the zero positions of the coefficient vector live in dual spaces.

The repository already contains a decisive counterexample:

```text

The fact that every irreducible factor of `B_p` has degree at most two is strong vertical modular information, but it does not create cross-prime independence.

# (A) What the fixed sheaf really gives—and why standard Chebotarev still misses the target

## A.1 The first quantifier correction: higher moments are incomplete CRT

Let

```text
P_X = { primes p : X < p ≤ 2X },
M   = X²,
K_X(m) = sum over p in P_X of 1_(Z_p)(m mod p).
```

For an integer `k ≥ 2`, expansion of the falling factorial gives the exact identity

```text
sum_(m<M) (K_X(m))_k
  = sum_(distinct p_1,...,p_k in P_X)
      #{ m<M : m mod p_i lies in Z_(p_i) for every i }.
                                             (A.1)
```

For one ordered prime tuple, put

```text
P = p_1 p_2 ... p_k.
```

By CRT, each tuple

```text
(r_1,...,r_k) in Z_(p_1) × ... × Z_(p_k)
```

determines one residue in `[0,P)`. The inner count in (A.1) is therefore

```text
sum over zero tuples r of
  1_{ the CRT lift of r lies in [0,M) }.
                                             (A.2)
```

Over a complete period, independence is exact. The difficulty is the ratio

```text
M/P is of order X^(2-k).
```

For `k = 2`, the interval length and the CRT modulus have the same scale. This is exactly why the second moment is accessible. For `k = 3`, the interval sees only about a `1/X` fraction of one CRT period. Higher moments ask whether the actual Apéry zero tuples land in that short initial interval with the expected frequency.

So the hard object is not simply the probability, for a fixed integer `m`, that `b_(m mod p)` vanishes modulo `p`. It is the distribution of CRT lifts of zero atoms from several different primes.

Two more quantifier points matter.

First, if `m` were literally fixed independently of `X`, then for all sufficiently large `p` one has `m mod p = m`, and only the finitely many prime divisors of the fixed integer `b_m` could occur. The hard regime is the triangular range `m < X²`, where `m` grows with `X`.

Second, the geometric Mellin exponent is naturally modulo `p-1`, while `K_X` uses a residue modulo `p`. In a quotient cell

```text
m = q p + r,
```

the repository’s fixed-exponent normalization is

```text
m - q = q(p - 1) + r,
```

so `m-q` and `r` agree modulo `p-1`. Thus the fixed-character interpretation is cellwise in `q`, not one global character across all primes and all `m`.

For a fractional moment, the same issue appears through the tail. Up to harmless lower-order terms, `(HM)_(2+δ)` asks for a bound of the form

```text
sum_(m<M) (K_X(m))_2 (K_X(m)+1)^δ
  ≪ M λ_X^(2+δ) X^o(1),
                                             (A.3)
```

or an equivalent estimate for the rare `m` that receive many simultaneous prime hits. This remains an incomplete-CRT problem.

## A.2 What the fixed sheaf buys

The new direct realization is a major improvement over a `p`-dependent ad hoc sheaf. It supplies all of the following.

### Uniform geometric complexity

Rank, tame singular set, weights, and local monodromy come from one integral geometric object.

### A common monodromy problem

It is now meaningful to seek one geometric or tannakian group rather than a different group for every prime.

### Uniform Mellin cohomology

The Kummer twists have two-dimensional `H¹`, pure of weight `3`, with the Deligne bound

```text
|T_(j,p)| ≤ 2 p^(3/2)
```

under every complex embedding.

### Auxiliary-ℓ compatibility

For `ℓ ≠ p`, compatible-system and product-monodromy methods become plausible and natural.

### A precise p-adic interpretation

The repository’s marked-coordinate calculation gives, after removing the Tate line, a quadratic Frobenius factor of the shape

```text
X² - T_(j,p) X + p³,
                                             (A.4)
```

up to a harmless finite determinant twist, with

```text
T_(j,p) ≡ b_j  mod 𝔭_p.
```

The Newton slopes are then

```text
(0,3)       if v_(𝔭_p)(T_(j,p)) = 0,
(1,2)       if v_(𝔭_p)(T_(j,p)) = 1,
(3/2,3/2)   if v_(𝔭_p)(T_(j,p)) ≥ 2.
```

Consequently,

```text
b_j ≡ 0 mod p
  ⇔ the Mellin motive has no slope-zero Frobenius root.
                                             (A.5)
```

This is a horizontal **nonordinary-locus** problem.

That is valuable conceptual compression. It also explains why ordinary Chebotarev is the wrong mechanism. Modulo the defining prime, the determinant `p³` vanishes. The target event is a jump of a Newton polygon or `F`-crystal, not membership in a conjugacy-stable subset of a fixed invertible residual group.

## A.3 The three precise mismatches with existing large sieves

### 1. Selected-character mismatch

Bah–Shuddhodan prove varying-characteristic Mellin equidistribution for bounded-complexity perverse sheaves with a common tannakian group. This is the strongest nearby theorem and is directly relevant to the fixed `W` realization.

But it averages over essentially all multiplicative characters of each finite field. Our datum is one deterministic section

```text
χ_p = ω_p^(j_p),
j_p = m mod (p-1),
```

or the quotient-cell-normalized version. One character has relative mass about `1/p`. An all-character equidistribution theorem permits that selected character to be exceptional for every prime without changing the limiting empirical measure.

There is also an observable mismatch. The theorem controls complex or unitary Frobenius conjugacy classes. The condition

```text
𝔭_p divides T_(j,p)
```

is a microscopic p-adic integrality condition. Complex equidistribution does not detect it.

### 2. Defining-characteristic diagonal

Compatible-system large sieves, including the framework used by Perret-Gentil, reduce an ℓ-adic sheaf modulo auxiliary valuations over primes `ℓ ≠ p`, where `p` is the characteristic of the base finite field. This exclusion is structural: the étale representation is unramified at the Frobenius point only away from its coefficient characteristic.

Our condition lies exactly on the forbidden diagonal

```text
ℓ = p.
```

Letting the base prime vary does not repair this. At each new base prime, the forbidden auxiliary prime changes to that same prime. There is no legitimate specialization of an auxiliary-ℓ Frobenius sieve to `ℓ = p`.

Kedlaya’s crystalline companion theorem supplies a p-adic companion for each fixed characteristic, with matching Frobenius polynomials. It does not couple the crystalline companions for different rational primes and does not provide a product-sieve mechanism across those primes.

### 3. Incomplete-CRT diagonal

Even a hypothetical theorem giving the correct one-prime frequency

```text
#{ j : T_(j,p) ≡ 0 mod 𝔭_p } is of order 1
```

would not prove `(HM)_(2+δ)`. Abstract singleton sets

```text
Z_p = {r_p}
```

can align adversarially so that one integer `m` hits many primes. The repository already records this logical obstruction. Higher moments require the **positions** of the atoms to disperse through CRT, not only the correct cardinality at each prime.

## A.4 The exact new theorem one would need

For a prime `p`, define the additive Fourier transform of the zero positions by

```text
F_p(u) = sum_(r in Z_p) exp(2πi u r/p).
```

For a prime tuple `p_1,...,p_k`, let

```text
P   = p_1 ... p_k,
P_i = P/p_i,
Pbar_i = inverse of P_i modulo p_i.
```

Fourier expansion of the interval in (A.2) gives an exact discrepancy formula:

```text
sum_(r_1,...,r_k) 1_{ CRT(r_1,...,r_k) < M }
  - (M/P) product_i |Z_(p_i)|

= (1/P) sum_(h mod P, h≠0)
      Fourier_[0,M)(-h)
      product_i F_(p_i)(h Pbar_i mod p_i).
                                             (A.6)
```

Thus the correct moving-modulus theorem is a mixed horizontal estimate for the Fourier transforms of the **Hasse-zero positions**, at frequencies coupled by the other primes in the tuple.

A clean integer-moment target is the following.

```text
(DM)_k:

sum_(distinct p_1,...,p_k in P_X)
  | sum_(r_i in Z_(p_i))
      [ 1_{CRT(r_1,...,r_k)<X²}
        - X²/(p_1...p_k) ] |

  ≪ X^(2+o(1)) λ_X^k.
```

A weighted `k=2` tail version would give `(HM)_(2+δ)`. The case `(DM)_3` gives the full third factorial moment.

The role of the fixed sheaf would be to prove a new horizontal theorem for

```text
F_p(u)
  = sum_j 1_{ T_(j,p) ≡ 0 mod 𝔭_p } exp(2πi u j/p),
```

not merely for the traces `T_(j,p)` themselves.

```text
Existing machinery
    fixed sheaf + all-character Mellin equidistribution
    fixed sheaf + auxiliary-ℓ compatible-system sieve

Missing theorem
    selected character + defining characteristic + incomplete CRT

Suggested name
    diagonal crystalline Mellin large sieve
```

### Conclusion for (A)

- **Yes:** fixed `W` is the right geometric foundation.
- **No:** I do not find a current Chebotarev or large-sieve theorem that supplies the required `2+δ` estimate.
- The missing result is not a routine sharpening of an error term. It is a new diagonal theorem with different quantifiers.

# (B) What can force critical-value packets of size at most three?

## B.1 The exact marked cover

Write

```text
A_p(a) = sum_(j=0)^(p-1) b_j a^j.
```

For `r ∈ Z_p`, define the Laurent polynomial

```text
H_(p,r)(a)
  = sum_(0≤j<p, j≠r) [ b_j/(j-r) ] a^(j-r).
                                             (B.1)
```

All denominators are nonzero in `F_p`, and the missing logarithmic term is absent exactly because `b_r = 0`. Therefore

```text
H_(p,r)'(a)
  = a^(-r-1) A_p(a)
  = a^(-r-1) Δ(a)^ε_p B_p(a)².
                                             (B.2)
```

This explains the passport exactly.

- The poles at `0` and `∞` have orders `r` and `p-1-r`.
- A simple root of `B_p` is a double zero of `H'`, hence a ramification point of index `3`.
- In the defect branch, the two roots of `Δ` give the fixed additional simple critical points.

The packet problem is the fiber problem on the finite scheme

```text
B_p(u) = 0,
B_p(v) = 0,
H_(p,r)(u) = H_(p,r)(v).
                                             (B.3)
```

The passport controls multiplicities of `H'`, but not the global values of a primitive. That is why it permits packets of order `p`.

## B.2 Why the ODE alone is not the likely explanation

The fixed second-order ODE is much stronger than a generic passport, but a packet equality is a global integral relation. The ODE directly controls the jets

```text
B_p(ξ), B_p'(ξ), B_p''(ξ)
```

at a root `ξ`; it does not by itself identify the primitive value `H_(p,r)(ξ)`.

The useful way to exploit the ODE is a symmetric-square Hermite reduction. One should seek rational functions `U_(p,r)`, `V_(p,r)`, and `W_(p,r)` such that, modulo an additive constant,

```text
H_(p,r)
  = U_(p,r) B_p²
    + V_(p,r) B_p B_p'
    + W_(p,r) (B_p')².
                                             (B.4)
```

The condition `b_r = 0` is exactly the kind of condition that can kill the residual cohomology term in such a reduction. If (B.4) holds, then at a root `ξ` of `B_p`,

```text
H_(p,r)(ξ)
  = W_(p,r)(ξ) B_p'(ξ)² + constant.
                                             (B.5)
```

This converts a primitive-value problem into a modular derivative-value problem. But (B.5) alone still does not force a uniform packet bound; a low-degree modular descent must enter next.

## B.3 The strongest structural candidate: the degree-three Franel modular map

The repository proves the exact Hasse–Franel pullback

```text
K_p(x)² = (1+x)^(p-1) A_p(φ(x)),
φ(x) = x(1-8x)/(1+x).
                                             (B.6)
```

The characteristic-zero Franel solution has the hypergeometric parametrization

```text
F(x)
  = 1/(1-2x)
    × 2F1(1/3, 2/3; 1; 27x²/(1-2x)³).
                                             (B.7)
```

Set

```text
y(x) = 27x²/(1-2x)³.
                                             (B.8)
```

This is a rational map of degree `3`. The same `x`-line carries the level-6 elliptic family

```text
E_x : Y² + (1-2x)XY + x²Y = X³,
```

and `φ` is the degree-two quotient used in the Asai realization. Thus the structural tower is

```text
hypergeometric / level-3 coordinate y
            ← x-line carrying E_x
            → a-line through φ.
                                             (B.9)
```

This gives a compelling explanation for the observed constant `3`: a critical-value packet should be a fiber of the degree-three modular coordinate after accounting for the deck involution of `φ`.

This is presently a diagnosis, not a theorem. It has a decisive computational and algebraic test.

For a root `a` of `B_p`, let `x` and `σ(x)` be its two lifts under `φ`, and retain the unordered modular label

```text
Ylabel(a) = { y(x), y(σ(x)) }.
                                             (B.10)
```

Test every computed packet for the implication

```text
H_(p,r)(a_1) = H_(p,r)(a_2)
  ⇒ Ylabel(a_1) and Ylabel(a_2) have a common element.
                                             (B.11)
```

If this holds throughout the existing data, the theorem to prove is a quotient-ring identity of the following kind. After clearing the fixed denominators, seek

```text
H_(p,r)(φ(x)) - H_(p,r)(φ(z))

  = [y(x)-y(z)] U_(p,r)(x,z)
    + K_p(x) V_(p,r)(x,z)
    + K_p(z) W_(p,r)(x,z),
                                             (B.12)
```

with `U_(p,r)` nonzero on the off-diagonal supersingular locus.

Then equal critical values force `y(x)=y(z)`. A fiber of `y` contains at most three `x`-points, and passing to `a=φ(x)` cannot increase that number. Hence packet size is at most three.

The most efficient proof strategy for (B.12) is likely:

1. use the second-order ODE to obtain the Hermite reduction (B.4);
2. pull the root-value formula (B.5) to the `x`-line;
3. use the hypergeometric equation and level-6 modular identities to show that the root value factors through `y(x)`;
4. isolate the fixed exceptional points from `Δ`, `x=-1`, and the branch points of `φ`.

If test (B.11) fails, the next candidate is still modular rather than a bare ODE: the coincidence scheme (B.3) may be a union of graphs of a finite collection of level-6 normalizer, Atkin–Lehner, or low-degree isogeny correspondences whose total fiber degree is three.

A useful falsification hierarchy is:

```text
passport only
    packets can be about p/4                         [known]

passport + all roots in F_(p²)
    test this null model

passport + same singularity pattern of the ODE
    test random accessory data

exact fixed Apéry ODE
    test

exact level-6 / degree-2 / degree-3 pullback
    leading candidate
```

If the second null model already permits large packets, then “all factors have degree at most two” is eliminated as the cause. If the exact ODE without its modular normalization permits large packets, the modular arithmetic is decisively responsible.

## B.4 What packet `≤ 3` would actually imply

Let

```text
M_p = degree of B_p, approximately p/2.
```

Packet `≤ 3` gives at least

```text
ceil(M_p/3), hence a positive constant times p,
                                             (B.13)
```

distinct branch values coming from the triple points.

### 1. It confirms growing complexity

Any direct sheaf, discriminant, or pushforward construction that records these branch values has complexity at least linear in `p`. Thus the packet theorem does not rescue the closed bounded-conductor `ψ ∘ Tr` route; it gives a structural reason that the conductor grows.

### 2. It is promising for geometric monodromy

If one can also prove that `H_(p,r)` is indecomposable and that at least one packet is a singleton, the local monodromy contains an actual `3`-cycle. Standard Jordan-type group theory then makes `A_(p-1)` or `S_(p-1)` a realistic conclusion. Even without a singleton, packet `≤ 3` restricts local cycle types to products of at most three disjoint `3`-cycles.

This would justify the empirical generic-cover and sub-Poisson picture at one prime and would be a strong independent theorem.

### 3. It does not by itself control the GCD

The all-`n` GCD conjecture is cross-prime. A vertical theorem about every individual marked cover does not prevent the zero indices `r_p` from lining up through CRT. Even the stronger statement `|Z_p| ≤ 1` for every prime would not suffice without positional dispersion.

To feed the GCD argument, packet rigidity needs one additional bridge of one of the following forms:

- a **global arithmetic packet label** whose equality across primes is controlled by divisibility of a characteristic-zero integer of controlled height;
- a **bounded-degree resolvent** in which `r ∈ Z_p` becomes a Frobenius condition for one fixed arithmetic family;
- a direct mixed-prime estimate for packet labels strong enough to imply `(DM)_(2+δ)` or `(DM)_3`.

Without such a bridge, packet `≤ 3` is a major vertical theorem but not a proof of `(HM)_(2+δ)`.

# (C) The correct interaction between Hasse–Franel and Mellin

## C.1 Coefficient zeros are not evaluation zeros

The definitions are

```text
A_p(a) = sum_(r=0)^(p-1) b_r a^r,
A_p(a) = Δ(a)^ε_p B_p(a)².
```

Therefore

```text
r ∈ Z_p
  ⇔ coefficient of a^r in Δ(a)^ε_p B_p(a)² is zero,
                                             (C.1)
```

whereas `B_p(ξ)=0` is an evaluation statement. These are different operations.

The `p=7` example is definitive:

```text
A_7(a)
 = 1 + 5a + 3a² + 3a³ + 3a⁴ + 5a⁵ + a⁶
 = (a-1)²(a²+1)².
```

The evaluation `a=1` is a root, but none of the seven coefficients is zero modulo `7`.

Thus the proposed equivalence

```text
b_r = 0
  ⇔ B_p(r)=0 or Δ(r)=0
```

must not be used.

## C.2 What the square factorization says about coefficients

Write

```text
B_p(a) = sum_i c_i a^i.
```

In the square branch,

```text
b_r = sum_(i+j=r) c_i c_j.
                                             (C.2)
```

In the defect branch, since `Δ(a)=1-34a+a²`,

```text
b_r
 = sum_(i+j=r)   c_i c_j
   - 34 sum_(i+j=r-1) c_i c_j
   +    sum_(i+j=r-2) c_i c_j.
                                             (C.3)
```

So the zero event is cancellation in a quadratic convolution. For central indices, this involves on the order of `p` terms. The factorization lowers the differential order—from the rank-three Apéry equation to a rank-two square root—but it does not lower the coefficient complexity to a bounded number of terms.

The same point appears in the exact Mellin identity. For `1 ≤ r ≤ p-2`,

```text
b_r
 = - sum_(a in F_p^×)
       Δ(a)^ε_p B_p(a)² a^(-r).
                                             (C.4)
```

This is a Fourier coefficient of the value vector `B_p(a)²`, not an evaluation of `B_p` at `r`.

If `Bhat(u)` denotes the multiplicative Fourier transform of `B_p(a)`, then, up to normalization,

```text
Fourier(B_p²)(r)
  = sum_u Bhat(u) Bhat(r-u).
                                             (C.5)
```

Multiplication by `Δ` only adds the three fixed shifts `r`, `r-1`, and `r-2`. Again, one obtains a long quadratic convolution, not a product of two independent scalar events.

The Franel pullback gives an equally exact but basis-sensitive version:

```text
K_p(x)² = sum_(m=0)^(p-1) b_m Ψ_(p,m)(x),

Ψ_(p,m)(x)
  = x^m (1-8x)^m (1+x)^(p-1-m).
                                             (C.6)
```

Thus `Z_p` is the set of zero coordinates of the square `K_p²` in the cover-adapted basis `{Ψ_(p,m)}`. It is not the root set of `K_p`, nor the set of zero monomial coefficients of `K_p²`.

## C.3 Why degree-at-most-two factors do not create cross-prime independence

The fact that every root of `B_p` lies in `F_(p²)` means

```text
B_p(a) divides a^(p²)-a
```

and identifies the root divisor with a supersingular divisor of the level-6 family. This is excellent input for modular geometry and for proving the packet theorem.

It is not a bounded-dimensional factorization of the coefficient event:

- `B_p` still has about `p/2` roots;
- its coefficients are elementary symmetric functions of all those roots;
- `b_r` is a quadratic combination of those coefficients;
- the number of linear or quadratic factors is still proportional to `p`;
- the polynomial and its splitting data change with `p`.

Chebotarev can exploit a bounded number of Frobenius orbits in a fixed number field. Here there are linearly many orbits in a finite-field divisor that itself varies with the prime. “Every orbit has size at most two” is therefore vertical rationality, not cross-prime independence.

Equidistribution of supersingular points against fixed low-degree test functions would also be insufficient: a coefficient `b_r` with `r` proportional to `p` is a high-degree elementary-symmetric observable, not a fixed test function.

## C.4 The two legitimate ways to combine the structures

### Route 1: symmetric-square or Asai convolution

The factorization and direct elliptic realization explain why the rank-three K3 trace descends from a rank-two elliptic object and why the Mellin `H¹` has dimension two. This is the correct representation-theoretic meaning of the square.

But trace zero modulo `p` is cancellation in that two-dimensional motive, equivalently loss of the unit root. It is not the intersection of two independent root events. The convolution structure may help identify monodromy and integral lattices, but it does not by itself supply a `2+δ` moment.

### Route 2: exact differential and critical-value geometry

The coefficient condition `b_r=0` is exactly what makes

```text
a^(-r-1) A_p(a) da
```

an exact differential. The square factorization then makes its primitive a cover with triple ramification at the roots of `B_p`. This is the strongest genuine bridge between coefficient space and root geometry.

The packet theorem belongs here. If the critical values descend through a fixed degree-three modular coordinate, then the root geometry has been compressed to bounded degree. The remaining question is whether the **zero index `r`**, or a packet label attached to it, also descends to a fixed arithmetic object across primes. Only that extra descent could feed a Chebotarev or atom-tail argument.

## C.5 What would be enough for `(HM)_(2+δ)`

One of the following genuinely new statements would be sufficient.

### Bounded-degree arithmetic resolvent

Construct a fixed finite-type arithmetic scheme over `Z[1/6]`, a bounded-degree map to the character or index coordinate, and a conjugacy condition `C` such that

```text
r ∈ Z_p
  ⇔ the relevant Frobenius datum lies in C,
```

with product monodromy for distinct primes. Then ordinary large-sieve technology could plausibly enter—provided the construction also removes the defining-characteristic diagonal.

At present, the marked Hurwitz cover has degree `p-1` and linearly many branch values, so such a bounded-degree resolvent is not yet present.

### Horizontal Fourier law for supersingular coefficient zeros

Prove directly the mixed-prime estimate (A.6) for

```text
F_p(u) = sum_(r in Z_p) exp(2πiur/p).
```

This would be a theorem about positions of nonordinary Kummer Mellin characters across varying characteristics. It is exactly the missing input, but no current supersingular-equidistribution theorem has this strength or these quantifiers.

### A global packet label

Show that every zero `r ∈ Z_p` determines a bounded-height algebraic packet invariant `η_(p,r)` that is the reduction of a characteristic-zero object depending on the quotient cell, and that many simultaneous prime hits force many primes to divide one nonzero integer of controlled height. This would convert packet rigidity into the atom-tail or divisor mechanism needed by the GCD problem.

Without one of these additions, degree-at-most-two factorization of `B_p` is not enough.

# Recommended frontier program

## 1. Run the degree-three packet-label test first

For every stored packet through `p ≤ 3000`:

1. lift each `B_p` root `a` to the two `x`-points satisfying `φ(x)=a`;
2. compute the unordered pair `Ylabel(a)={y(x),y(σ(x))}`;
3. test implication (B.11);
4. record whether packet sizes `1`, `2`, and `3` are exactly explained by a fiber of `y`, by the deck involution, or by another fixed level-6 correspondence.

This experiment cleanly distinguishes a degree-three modular descent from a vague ODE explanation.

## 2. Compute the symmetric-square Hermite reduction

Using the exact second-order operator for `B_p`, reduce the primitive `H_(p,r)` to the basis

```text
B_p²,
B_p B_p',
(B_p')².
```

The target is (B.4), followed by the root-value formula (B.5). This is the algebraic interface needed to prove any modular packet identity.

## 3. Prove packet `≤ 3`, but state its logical output precisely

Package the theorem as:

- an Apéry-specific critical-value theorem;
- a monodromy or indecomposability corollary if available;
- an explicit statement that it gives linearly many branch values and therefore does not yield bounded conductor;
- no claim of `(HM)_(2+δ)` without a separate cross-prime bridge.

## 4. Formulate the missing horizontal theorem in the exact CRT/Fourier form

Do not formulate it merely as “Frobenius traces at different primes are independent.” Use (A.6) or `(DM)_k`. This prevents an all-character or auxiliary-ℓ theorem from being mistaken for the required result.

## 5. Search for a global packet invariant only after step 1

If equal critical values are exactly degree-three modular fibers, inspect the corresponding `y`-value, modular polynomial, or isogeny class for a characteristic-zero lift of controlled height. This is the one scenario in which the packet theorem could plausibly connect to an atom-tail argument across primes.

```text
Most likely outcome tree

packet = degree-three modular fiber
    → prove with ODE + modular pullback
    → search for a bounded-height global packet label
        → if found: possible atom-tail / moving-prime sieve route
        → if absent: strong vertical theorem, no HM implication

packet not explained by the degree-three fiber
    → classify finite level-6 correspondences
    → if correspondence degree is still bounded, use the same next step
    → otherwise packet rigidity remains an isolated vertical theorem
```

# Bottom line

The new direct Mellin realization and the packet computation are both substantial advances, but they address different layers.

- The fixed sheaf solves the **geometric uniformity** problem.
- The Hasse–Franel square root solves the **rank reduction and exact-differential** problem.
- The packet bound appears to expose a **degree-three modular rigidity**.
- The remaining GCD barrier is the **horizontal defining-characteristic CRT dispersion** of the zero positions.

The best immediate theorem to attack is the modular packet identity (B.12). It is concrete, falsifiable with the existing data, and gives a plausible explanation for the exact constant `3`. The best formulation of the remaining all-`n` barrier is `(DM)_(2+δ)`, not ordinary Chebotarev independence.
