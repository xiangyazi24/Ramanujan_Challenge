# Cold audit: the comparison is valid, but it is an equivalent carrier, not a new main bound

## Verdict

The proposed comparison survives a cold audit, after making the quantifiers explicit. For \(n\ge 1\) (with the lcm of an empty family interpreted as \(1\)), one can prove the stronger parameterized estimate

\[
\boxed{
\log C_n\le \log R_n+O\!\left(\sqrt n+K^2+\frac nK\right)
}
\qquad(K\ge 3),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\log C_n\ge \log R_n-O(\log(2n)).
}
\]

Taking \(K=\lceil n^{1/3}\rceil\) (and absorbing finitely many small \(n\)) gives exactly

\[
\log R_n-O(\log n)\le \log C_n
\le \log R_n+O(n^{2/3}).
\]

There are two important qualifications.

1. **Neither exact divisibility direction is true.** There are genuine non-target primes in \(C_n\), and the omitted center can genuinely remove a target prime from \(C_n\). I give explicit examples below: \(n=20,p=5\) for the former and \(n=16,p=11\) for the latter.
2. **This is not a proof of the main \(R_n=e^{o(n)}\) estimate.** It proves that \(C_n\) is a target-preserving equivalent carrier up to \(e^{O(n^{2/3})}\):
   \[
   \log C_n=o(n)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \log R_n=o(n).
   \]
   An independent sublinear estimate for \(C_n\) is still needed. The quotient triangle by itself does not supply one.

This matches the current repository state. `SUBMIT/3.2/FINAL_AUDIT.md`, §1.3, identifies the still-open exact master mass as

\[
M(n)=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}\log p\;\mathbf 1_{p\mid b_{n\bmod p}},
\]

which is exactly \(\log R_n\). Thus the present lemma is a legitimate new reformulation of that live target, but it does not change the repository's terminal status that the every-\(n\) theorem remains open. I used the current `main` audit as the mathematical source of truth; the legacy `DOCTRINE.md` still present on `chatgpt-drop` predates the current consolidated `UNDERSTANDING.md`/`SUBMIT/3.2/FINAL_AUDIT.md` state.

# 1. Basic geometry of the top-half carrier

For

\[
\frac n2<m\le n,
\qquad r=n-m,
\qquad s=2m-n-1,
\]

we have

\[
r\ge0,\qquad s\ge0,\qquad r+s=m-1.
\]

Indeed, \(m\le n\) gives \(r\ge0\), while the strict top-half condition on an integer \(m\) gives \(2m\ge n+1\), hence \(s\ge0\).

Suppose now that a prime \(p\) satisfies

\[
p\mid C_n,\qquad p^2>n.
\]

By the definition of an lcm, there is at least one admissible witness \(m\) with

\[
p\mid \gcd(m,b_r,b_s).
\]

Since \(p\mid m\), write

\[
m=kp,\qquad n=tp+a,\qquad 0\le a<p.
\]

Because \(m\le n<p^2\),

\[
1\le k<p.
\]

Also \(p\le m\le n\), so \(t\ge1\), and \(n<p^2\) gives

\[
t=\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor<p.
\]

From \(kp=m\le n=tp+a<(t+1)p\), integrality gives

\[
k\le t.
\]

The strict top-half inequality gives

\[
2kp>tp+a,
\]

hence

\[
(2k-t)p>a.
\]

Since \(0\le a<p\), this forces

\[
2k-t\ge1.
\]

Define

\[
u=t-k,\qquad v=2k-t-1.
\]

Then

\[
u\ge0,\qquad v\ge0,\qquad u+v=k-1.
\]

Moreover

\[
u\le k-1
\]

because \(t\le2k-1\), and

\[
v\le k-1
\]

because \(k\le t\). Thus

\[
0\le u,v\le k-1<p.
\]

This verifies every digit-range assertion needed for a one-step Apéry-Lucas reduction; there is no hidden carry.

# 2. The exact Lucas/reflection dichotomy

The two physical indices have the base-\(p\) expansions

\[
\begin{aligned}
r&=n-m=(t-k)p+a=up+a,\\
s&=2m-n-1=(2k-t)p-a-1\\
 &= (2k-t-1)p+(p-1-a)=vp+(p-1-a).
\end{aligned}
\]

All four digits \(u,v,a,p-1-a\) lie in \([0,p-1]\). Therefore ordinary Apéry-Lucas gives

\[
b_r\equiv b_u b_a\pmod p,
\]

and

\[
b_s\equiv b_v b_{p-1-a}\pmod p.
\]

Using the available reflection identity

\[
b_{p-1-a}\equiv b_a\pmod p,
\]

we get

\[
b_r\equiv b_u b_a,
\qquad
b_s\equiv b_v b_a
\pmod p.
\]

Hence, over the field \(\mathbf F_p\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
p\mid b_r\text{ and }p\mid b_s
&\iff b_a b_u\equiv0\text{ and }b_a b_v\equiv0\pmod p\\
&\iff \boxed{\,p\mid b_a\quad\text{or}\quad(p\mid b_u\text{ and }p\mid b_v)\,}.
\end{aligned}
\]

So step (1) of the sketch is correct.

There is also a useful strengthening in the non-target branch. If

\[
p\nmid b_a,
\]

then \(p\mid b_u,b_v\). Since \(b_0=1\), neither \(u\) nor \(v\) can be \(0\). Therefore

\[
u,v\ge1,
\qquad k=u+v+1\ge3.
\]

Eliminating \(k\) also gives the sharper quotient formula

\[
\boxed{t=2u+v+1\ge4.}
\]

Thus every large non-target extra prime automatically has

\[
p\le n/4.
\]

This is a real structural fact, although by itself it is only a linear-mass saving and is weaker than the \(n^{2/3}\) split below.

Endpoint cases cause no failure:

- \(u=0\) or \(v=0\) is allowed in the raw digit algebra, but then the second branch is impossible because \(b_0=1\).
- \(a=0\) and \(a=p-1\) are harmless; reflection still applies.
- \(m=n\) is harmless.
- \(p=2,3\) can simply be absorbed into the small-prime term; nothing in the asymptotic proof depends on treating them as large primes.

# 3. Upper bound: target primes plus shallow/deep contamination

Separate the prime-power factorization of \(C_n\) into primes \(p\le\sqrt n\) and primes \(p>\sqrt n\).

## 3.1 Small primes and prime powers really cost only \(O(\sqrt n)\)

For every prime \(p\), an exponent occurring in a witness gcd is at most \(v_p(m)\). Therefore

\[
v_p(C_n)\le \max_{m\le n}v_p(m)=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{p\le\sqrt n}v_p(C_n)\log p
\le
\sum_{p\le\sqrt n}\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\log p.
\]

Writing \(artheta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p\), the right side is

\[
\vartheta(\sqrt n)+\sum_{e\ge2}\vartheta(n^{1/e})
=2\vartheta(\sqrt n)+\vartheta(n^{1/3})+\cdots.
\]

Chebyshev's bound \(artheta(x)=O(x)\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le\sqrt n}v_p(C_n)\log p=O(\sqrt n).
}
\]

For \(p>\sqrt n\), one has \(p^2>n\ge m\), so \(v_p(m)\le1\). Thus the large-prime portion of \(C_n\) is automatically squarefree. There is no unaccounted large-prime-power issue.

## 3.2 Large target primes are exactly supported on \(R_n\)

Let \(p>\sqrt n\) divide \(C_n\), and use a witness as above. If the first branch holds,

\[
p\mid b_a,\qquad a=n\bmod p.
\]

We already know \(p\le n\), and \(p^2>n\), so \(p\) is one of the defining primes of \(R_n\). Thus every large prime of \(C_n\) not already charged to \(R_n\) must be in the second branch

\[
p\nmid b_a,\qquad p\mid b_u,\qquad p\mid b_v.
\]

Call these primes **extras**.

## 3.3 The shallow-union \(K^2\) charge is valid

Fix \(K\ge3\). Call an extra prime **shallow** if it has at least one witness with

\[
k\le K.
\]

This existential quantifier is important: a prime with both shallow and deep witnesses is classified as shallow. A **deep** extra is one for which every witness has \(k>K\).

For a shallow witness the second branch gives \(u,v\ge1\), and

\[
u,v<k\le K.
\]

Therefore the prime divides at least one member of

\[
b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_{\lceil K\rceil-1}.
\]

In fact, using \(u+v=k-1\), it divides \(b_j\) for

\[
j=\min(u,v)\le\frac{k-1}{2}<\frac K2,
\]

so one may charge to an even shorter product. The coarse version already suffices:

\[
\prod_{p\in E_{\rm sh}(K)}p
\mid
\prod_{1\le j<K}b_j.
\]

This divisibility is legitimate because the left side is squarefree: if every distinct prime in the shallow union divides the product on the right, their product divides it.

From the combinatorial formula for the Apéry numbers,

\[
b_j=\sum_{q=0}^j\binom jq^2\binom{j+q}q^2,
\]

one has, very crudely,

\[
b_j\le (j+1)64^j,
\]

so

\[
\log b_j=O(j).
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in E_{\rm sh}(K)}\log p
\le\sum_{j<K}\log b_j
=O(K^2).
}
\]

Thus the proposed **union charge is correct**. It really does avoid the unnecessary \(O(K^3)\) obtained by charging a fresh product separately for each \(k\).

A small refinement is available: charging each prime to \(\min(u,v)<K/2\) replaces the product through \(K\) by a product through \(K/2\). This improves only the constant, not the exponent.

## 3.4 Deep extras cost \(O(n/K)\)

If an extra is deep, choose any witness. Then \(k>K\) and

\[
p=\frac mk\le\frac nk<\frac nK.
\]

Since we are summing distinct primes,

\[
\sum_{p\in E_{\rm deep}(K)}\log p
\le \vartheta(n/K)
=O(n/K).
\]

Combining the target, shallow, deep, and small-prime pieces gives

\[
\boxed{
\log C_n
\le
\log R_n+O\!\left(\sqrt n+K^2+\frac nK\right).
}
\]

Balancing \(K^2\) against \(n/K\) gives \(K\asymp n^{1/3}\), hence

\[
\boxed{
\log C_n\le\log R_n+O(n^{2/3}).
}
\]

So the upper comparison is rigorous.

# 4. Lower bound and the omitted center

Let \(p\) be a target prime of \(R_n\). Write

\[
n=tp+a,\qquad 0\le a<p.
\]

Since \(p\le n\), \(t\ge1\). Take the canonical multiple

\[
m=tp=n-a.
\]

It lies in the required top half because

\[
m>n/2
\iff tp>a,
\]

and \(tp\ge p>a\). Of course \(m\le n\).

For this canonical witness,

\[
r=n-m=a,
\]

while

\[
s=2m-n-1=tp-a-1=(t-1)p+(p-1-a).
\]

The target assumption gives \(p\mid b_a=b_r\). Lucas plus reflection gives

\[
b_s\equiv b_{t-1}b_{p-1-a}
\equiv b_{t-1}b_a
\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Also \(p\mid m\). Therefore \(p\mid C_n\) unless this one witness is removed by the condition \(r=s\).

## 4.1 The center is unique

The equation \(r=s\) is

\[
n-m=2m-n-1,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{3m=2n+1.}
\]

Thus there is at most one physical center,

\[
m_0=\frac{2n+1}{3},
\]

and it exists only when \(n\equiv1\pmod3\).

The sketch's statement that every lost target prime divides this unique \(m_0\) is correct. Hence the product of all lost target primes divides \(\operatorname{rad}(m_0)\le m_0\le n\), already giving a loss of at most \(\log n\).

But in this particular canonical situation one can say more.

If the canonical target witness is central, then

\[
r=s
\iff a=tp-a-1
\iff tp=2a+1.
\]

Because \(a\le p-1\),

\[
tp=2a+1\le2p-1,
\]

so \(t=1\). Hence

\[
p=m_0=\frac{2n+1}{3},
\qquad
 a=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
 n=\frac{3p-1}{2}.
\]

Since \(t=1\), \(n<2p\), so \(p\) is the **only** positive multiple of \(p\) in the top-half range. Therefore a central target prime is not merely missed by the canonical witness; it has no alternate witness at all.

Thus the lost-target set is actually either empty or a singleton. Precisely, the only possible lost target is

\[
p_0=\frac{2n+1}{3}
\]

when this is prime and

\[
p_0\mid b_{(p_0-1)/2}.
\]

The conditions \(p_0\le n\) and \(p_0^2>n\) are then automatic for the relevant prime \(p_0>1\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log C_n\ge\log R_n-O(\log(2n)).
}
\]

and the lower error is in fact the logarithm of at most one prime.

## 4.2 If distinct supports are not essential, the center omission can be removed

For the comparison lemma alone, the condition \(r_m\ne s_m\) is unnecessary. Define

\[
C_n^+
=\operatorname{lcm}_{n/2<m\le n}\gcd(m,b_{r_m},b_{s_m}).
\]

The upper proof above is unchanged, including at \(r=s\): in quotient variables one simply has \(u=v\), and the second branch becomes \(p\mid b_u\). The canonical target witness is never discarded, so

\[
\boxed{
\log R_n\le\log C_n^+
\le\log R_n+O(n^{2/3}).
}
\]

If the purpose of \(r\ne s\) is to insist on genuinely distinct physical supports for a later nonlinear argument, keep the original \(C_n\). If the purpose is only target equivalence, \(C_n^+\) is slightly cleaner.

# 5. Explicit stress tests: both defects are real

## 5.1 A genuine non-target extra: \(n=20,p=5\)

Take

\[
n=20,\qquad p=5,\qquad m=15=3p.
\]

Then

\[
r=20-15=5,
\qquad
s=30-20-1=9,
\]

so \(r\ne s\). Also \(p^2=25>20\).

Here

\[
t=4,\quad a=0,\quad k=3,\quad u=t-k=1,\quad v=2k-t-1=1.
\]

The target branch is false because

\[
b_a=b_0=1.
\]

But \(b_1=5\), so the second branch holds. Directly by Lucas,

\[
b_5\equiv b_1b_0\equiv0\pmod5,
\]

and

\[
b_9\equiv b_1b_4\equiv0\pmod5.
\]

Thus

\[
5\mid\gcd(15,b_5,b_9),
\]

so

\[
5\mid C_{20},\qquad 5\nmid R_{20}.
\]

This is also the simplest warning against interpreting the quotient pair as two independent zero constraints: here

\[
u=v=1
\]

even though the physical indices \(r=5\) and \(s=9\) are distinct.

## 5.2 A genuine center-lost target: \(n=16,p=11\)

The Apéry value

\[
b_5=819005=11\cdot74455
\]

is divisible by \(11\). For

\[
n=16,\qquad p=11,
\]

we have \(p^2=121>16\), \(n\bmod p=5\), so

\[
11\mid R_{16}.
\]

The canonical witness is

\[
m=11=\frac{2\cdot16+1}{3},
\]

and

\[
r=s=5.
\]

It is therefore excluded from \(C_{16}\). Since \(16<2\cdot11\), there is no other multiple of \(11\) in the top half. Hence

\[
11\nmid C_{16}.
\]

So the center-loss term is not a fictitious edge case; exact target inclusion genuinely fails for the stated \(C_n\).

These examples show that in general neither \(C_n\mid R_n\) nor \(R_n\mid C_n\) holds.

# 6. What quotient-triangularity really gives

The quotient variables contain more structure than just

\[
u+v=k-1.
\]

Since \(u=t-k\), one has

\[
\boxed{
k=u+v+1,\qquad t=2u+v+1.}
\]

Thus for a fixed quotient

\[
t=\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor,
\]

a non-target extra can only occur when there is an integer

\[
1\le u\le\frac{t-2}{2},
\qquad
v=t-2u-1\ge1,
\]

with

\[
p\mid b_u,\qquad p\mid b_v.
\]

This is a genuine triangular organization of the contamination. It is useful bookkeeping, but with the currently available arithmetic it does **not** improve the \(2/3\) exponent.

## 6.1 Why the formal triangle does not improve \(K^2\)

Every non-target extra has \(u,v\ge1\). Put

\[
j=\min(u,v).
\]

Then

\[
p\mid b_j,\qquad j\le\frac{k-1}{2},\qquad p\le\frac nk.
\]

This yields the same generic two-regime bound:

- \(j\le J\): charge all distinct primes to \(\prod_{j\le J}b_j\), whose log is \(O(J^2)\);
- \(j>J\): then \(k\ge2j+1>2J\), so \(p\ll n/J\), and Chebyshev gives \(O(n/J)\).

Balancing again gives exponent \(2/3\). The triangle changes constants, not powers.

Moreover the diagonal

\[
u=v
\]

is genuinely present. On the diagonal

\[
k=2u+1,\qquad t=3u+1,
\]

and the condition \(p\mid b_u,b_v\) collapses to the single condition

\[
p\mid b_u.
\]

The \(n=20,p=5\) example above is exactly such a diagonal extra. Therefore an attempted improvement based merely on “a prime must divide two different small Apéry numbers” is false.

For the off-diagonal \(u\ne v\), the existing gap-polynomial machinery in the repository can impose an additional congruence on a common-zero pair. But the current `SUBMIT/3.2/FINAL_AUDIT.md` already records the relevant obstruction: no uniform cross-prime aggregate saving strong enough for the every-\(n\) target is proved. Summing fixed-pair/resultant heights naively is not better than the union charge, and the diagonal channel escapes such a two-distinct-zero argument entirely.

A clean way to state what would be needed is this. Suppose one could improve the shallow-prime estimate to

\[
\sum_{p\in E_{\rm sh}(J)}\log p=O(J^\alpha)
\]

for some \(\alpha<2\). Balancing with the unavoidable Chebyshev tail \(O(n/J)\) would give

\[
O\!\left(n^{\alpha/(\alpha+1)}\right),
\]

which is strictly better than \(n^{2/3}\). Thus an exponent improvement requires **new arithmetic information**, for example a subquadratic aggregate bound on the relevant large-prime radicals/common-zero pairs. It does not follow from quotient-triangularity plus \(\log b_j=O(j)\).

# 7. There is a formal descent, but it does not close a pointwise recursion

There are two useful descent observations. They are worth recording because they show exactly where a hoped-for recursion fails.

## 7.1 The quotient geometry reproduces a smaller carrier geometry — but loses the modulus factor

For a non-target witness,

\[
t=2u+v+1,\qquad k=u+v+1.
\]

Now look formally at the carrier geometry at the smaller index \(t\), with the putative witness \(m'=k\). Then

\[
t-k=u,
\]

and

\[
2k-t-1=v.
\]

So the pair \((u,v)\) is **exactly** the pair of support indices generated by the same top-half formulas at index \(t\).

However, a true \(C_t\) witness also requires the prime to divide its first argument \(m'=k\). Here

\[
k<p,
\]

so

\[
p\nmid k.
\]

Therefore the prime is not a \(C_t\) prime. This is the precise reason the attractive quotient self-similarity does not produce a recursion \(C_n\to C_t\) or \(R_n\to R_t\).

The geometry recurses; the arithmetic modulus condition does not.

## 7.2 Any small Apéry zero can be reinterpreted as a quotient-one target

If \(j<p\) and \(p\mid b_j\), define

\[
N=p+j.
\]

Then

\[
N\bmod p=j,\qquad p>N/2,
\]

and, for the primes under discussion, \(p^2>N\). Hence

\[
p\mid R_N
\]

and in fact \(p\) is in the quotient-\(1\) part of \(R_N\).

For a non-target extra, choose \(j=\min(u,v)\). Since \(t\ge4\), \(p\le n/4\), and \(j<p\),

\[
N=p+j<2p\le n/2.
\]

Thus every extra prime can be reinterpreted as a quotient-one target at some smaller index \(N<n/2\).

This is a valid support descent, but it is **weaker** than the already proved \(O(n^{2/3})\) contamination estimate. The union of quotient-one target supports over all \(N\le x\) can have Chebyshev mass \(O(x)\); pointwise bounds on individual \(R_N\)'s do not control that union.

## 7.3 The same descent for actual target primes leaves quotient \(1\) untouched

Let \(p\in R_n\), with \(n=tp+a\). Put again

\[
N=p+a.
\]

Then \(p\) is a quotient-one target prime of \(R_N\). If \(t=1\), then

\[
N=n,
\]

so there is **no descent at all**. If \(t\ge2\), then

\[
N<\frac{2n}{3}.
\]

Indeed, for \(t=2\), \(n<3p\), hence \(p>n/3\) and \(N=n-p<2n/3\); for \(t\ge3\), \(N<2p\le2n/t\le2n/3\).

If \(T_m\) denotes the quotient-one part

\[
T_m:=\prod_{m/2<p\le m\atop p\mid b_{m-p}}p,
\]

and

\[
U(x):=\operatorname{rad}\prod_{m\le x}T_m,
\]

then one gets the genuine support relation

\[
\boxed{
R_n\mid T_n\,U(2n/3).
}
\]

(up to the harmless integer-floor interpretation of \(2n/3\)).

But this does not close the theorem. The only unconditional bound from prime support is

\[
\log U(x)\le\vartheta(x)=O(x),
\]

and the hard quotient-\(1\) factor \(T_n\) remains at the original index. Replacing \(U\) by a sum of pointwise \(\log R_m\)'s is even worse. Thus this is a support recursion, not a contraction proving \(\log R_n=o(n)\).

This also explains why the current repository audit warns that the top window is only quotient \(1\), not the full theorem: lower quotient classes can be *represented* by earlier quotient-one events, but controlling the union of those historical events is itself a linear-mass problem.

# 8. Why \(C_n\) itself has no automatic sublinear bound

One might hope that inserting the factor \(m\) and taking a top-half lcm makes \(C_n\) small for elementary reasons. It does not.

Certainly

\[
C_n\mid \operatorname{lcm}_{n/2<m\le n}m.
\]

But every integer \(d\le n\) divides some integer in the top half: if \(d<n/2\), take \(m=\lfloor n/d\rfloor d>n-d>n/2\); if \(d=n/2\), take \(m=n\); and if \(d>n/2\), take \(m=d\). Hence

\[
\operatorname{lcm}_{n/2<m\le n}m
=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,n).
\]

Its logarithm is \(\Theta(n)\), not \(o(n)\). Therefore the elementary lcm structure of \(C_n\) supplies no main saving. Any proof that \(\log C_n=o(n)\) must use the simultaneous Apéry divisibility in a genuinely arithmetic way.

# 9. Final classification

The proposed reduction should be kept, but labeled correctly.

### What is proved

For the stated carrier,

\[
\boxed{
\log R_n-O(\log n)\le\log C_n
\le\log R_n+O(n^{2/3}).
}
\]

More precisely,

\[
\log C_n\le\log R_n+O(\sqrt n+K^2+n/K)
\]

for every \(K\ge3\), and the lower loss is caused by at most the single center prime \(p=(2n+1)/3\).

The \(K^2\) union charge is sound. The center argument is sound and can be sharpened to a singleton characterization. Prime powers/small primes are genuinely \(O(\sqrt n)\).

### What is not proved

This does **not** give a new sublinear bound on \(\log R_n\). It gives

\[
\log C_n=o(n)\iff\log R_n=o(n),
\]

so \(C_n\) is an **equivalent target-preserving nonlinear carrier**. The main theorem remains exactly as hard until one proves an independent \(o(n)\) estimate for \(C_n\) or a new arithmetic estimate on its shallow common-zero primes.

Quotient-triangularity yields useful exact formulas and a formal support descent, but no currently justified pointwise recursion closes. The obstruction is concrete:

- the smaller quotient geometry omits the crucial divisibility \(p\mid k\);
- the diagonal \(u=v\) reduces the apparent two-zero condition to one zero;
- the hardest quotient-\(1\) target branch does not descend at all;
- unioning lower-index target supports costs \(O(n)\), not \(o(n)\).

### Best next arithmetic target suggested by this carrier

If this construction is pursued, the next genuinely load-bearing statement is not another formal rearrangement. It is a subquadratic shallow-mass theorem, for example a bound of the shape

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\text{ extra}\nexists\text{ witness }k\le K}}\log p
=O(K^{2-\delta})
\qquad(\delta>0),
\]

or a stronger moving-interval/common-zero estimate that implies it. Such a theorem would immediately improve the \(2/3\) contamination exponent to

\[
\frac{2-\delta}{3-\delta}<\frac23.
\]

But even that would still only sharpen the equivalence \(C_n\leftrightarrow R_n\); to solve the challenge one ultimately needs a direct sublinear estimate for the target-bearing part of \(C_n\), especially the quotient-\(1\) branch.
