# Q390 — Equidistribution of Apéry CRT residues: the direct attack

## Executive verdict

The proposed CRT route identifies the correct **suffix/suffix pair-dispersion problem**, but several premises must first be corrected.

1. The statement “\(|Z_p|\approx1\), so write \(a_p\) for the single zero” is misleading. Reflection
   \[
   r\longmapsto p-1-r
   \]
   has the unique fixed point \((p-1)/2\). Hence a singleton zero set is necessarily
   \[
   Z_p=\left\{\frac{p-1}{2}\right\}.
   \]
   In particular its CRT residue is completely rigid:
   \[
   \operatorname{CRT}\!\left(\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{q-1}{2}\right)
   =\frac{pq-1}{2}.
   \]
   It is not equidistributed. Empirically the mean \(|Z_p|\approx1\) comes mainly from a mixture of empty sets and reflected pairs, not from singleton sets.

2. For a reflected pair
   \[
   Z_p=\{a,p-1-a\},
   \]
   reflection pairs the CRT representatives by
   \[
   x\longleftrightarrow pq-1-x.
   \]
   This annihilates only the part of a test function that is odd about the midpoint. The cutoff \([0,N]\) has a substantial even part, so reflection does not force the desired cancellation.

3. The \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\)-root factorization of the Apéry truncation polynomial concerns **evaluation roots in the variable \(T\)**. The set \(Z_p\) consists of **zero coefficients indexed by \(r\)**. These are different spaces. The repository already records the exact Mellin identity separating them. Galois pairing of the evaluation roots gives no direct CRT equidistribution of coefficient-zero indices.

4. The modular-form congruence
   \[
   b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod p,
   \qquad f_8=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4,
   \]
   detects only the central fixed point. It gives no location information for a noncentral reflected pair. Sato–Tate is an archimedean distribution theorem for normalized Hecke traces; the condition \(p\mid a_p(f_8)\) is a same-prime \(p\)-adic divisibility condition and is not implied by Sato–Tate.

5. The simplest sufficient input is not ordinary one-prime equidistribution of \(a_p/p\). It is a **horizontal two-prime discrepancy theorem**, equivalently square-root cancellation for the inverse-twisted Fourier sums
   \[
   F_p(h\bar q)F_q(h\bar p),
   \qquad
   F_p(k):=\sum_{a\in Z_p}e_p(ka).
   \]
   This is the exact place where an Apéry-specific Mellin/crystalline or bilinear-large-sieve theorem would enter.

Thus the direct answer is:

> **Reflection, the \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) factorization, Sato–Tate, and a supersingular interpretation do not by themselves prove CRT equidistribution. The minimal viable theorem is a cross-characteristic, inverse-twisted bilinear discrepancy estimate for the coefficient-zero sets \(Z_p\).**

## 0. Scope corrections

### 0.1 The second-moment theorem is sufficient, not equivalent

Let

\[
B_N(n)=\#\{p:\sqrt N<p\le N,\ p\mid b_n\}.
\]

A bound

\[
\sum_{n\le N}B_N(n)^2=O(N)
\tag{0.1}
\]

implies

\[
\max_{n\le N}B_N(n)\le O(\sqrt N)
=o(N/\log N),
\]

and a dyadic application is sufficient for the all-\(n\) GCD conclusion. It is not logically equivalent to the pointwise assertion \(B(n)=o(n/\log n)\); (0.1) is much stronger.

Likewise, for an \(O(N)\) second-moment bound, the centered CRT error need only be \(O(N)\). An \(o(N)\) error is needed only for a Poisson-scale asymptotic with a specified leading constant.

### 0.2 The displayed CRT identity is only the suffix/suffix channel

For

\[
B_N^S(n):=\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le N}
 \mathbf1_{\{n\bmod p\in Z_p\}},
\]

the pair term is indeed a CRT incidence count. But for the actual event \(p\mid b_n\), Lucas gives, since \(p>\sqrt N\),

\[
n=u_pp+r_p,\qquad 0\le u_p,r_p<p,
\]

and

\[
p\mid b_n
\iff u_p\in Z_p\quad\text{or}\quad r_p\in Z_p.
\]

Thus the full fixed window has four pair channels: suffix/suffix, suffix/leading, leading/suffix, and leading/leading. This was isolated in `drops/Q359-4a66b8d3.md`. The CRT problem in this question is exact for the suffix model and especially for the top-half shell, where the leading digit is \(1\) and \(b_1=5\not\equiv0\pmod p\).

### 0.3 “Mean one” does not mean “usually a singleton”

The repository’s scan through \(p\le2{,}000{,}000\) gives mean \(|Z_p|\approx1.0012\), but the distribution is dominated by

\[
|Z_p|=0,2,4,6,\ldots.
\]

Only the rare central cases have odd cardinality. Therefore the natural random variable is

\[
K_p=\frac{|Z_p|-\varepsilon_p}{2},
\qquad
\varepsilon_p=\mathbf1_{\{(p-1)/2\in Z_p\}},
\]

not a universally defined single zero \(a_p\).

## 1. Exact CRT formulation for arbitrary zero sets

Put

\[
\mathcal P_N:=\{p\text{ prime}:\sqrt N<p\le N\},
\qquad L:=N+1.
\]

For distinct \(p,q\in\mathcal P_N\), and \(a\in Z_p\), \(b\in Z_q\), let

\[
c_{p,q}(a,b)\in\{0,1,\ldots,pq-1\}
\]

be the CRT representative satisfying

\[
c_{p,q}(a,b)\equiv a\pmod p,
\qquad
c_{p,q}(a,b)\equiv b\pmod q.
\]

Since \(pq>N\), each residue class modulo \(pq\) has at most one representative in \([0,N]\). Therefore

\[
C_{p,q}(N)
:=\#\{0\le n\le N:n\bmod p\in Z_p,\ n\bmod q\in Z_q\}
\]

has the exact form

\[
C_{p,q}(N)
=\sum_{a\in Z_p}\sum_{b\in Z_q}
 \mathbf1_{\{c_{p,q}(a,b)<L\}}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Its random-CRT main term is

\[
\frac{L|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}.
\]

Define

\[
E_{p,q}(N)
:=C_{p,q}(N)-\frac{L|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{n=0}^{N}(B_N^S(n))_2
=2\sum_{p<q}C_{p,q}(N),
\tag{1.3}
\]

and hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n=0}^{N}(B_N^S(n))^2
={}&\sum_p|E_p^S|
+2L\sum_{p<q}\frac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}
+2\sum_{p<q}E_{p,q}(N),
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.4}
\]

where \(E_p^S=\{0\le n\le N:n\bmod p\in Z_p\}\).

If

\[
\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le N}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}=O(1),
\tag{1.5}
\]

then the main pair term in (1.4) is already \(O(N)\). Thus the exact suffix/suffix target is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\sqrt N<p<q\le N}E_{p,q}(N)=O(N),
}
\tag{CRT2}
\]

or \(o(N)\) if one wants the random leading constant.

The trivial pointwise estimate

\[
|E_{p,q}(N)|\le |Z_p||Z_q|
\]

sums to roughly \(N^2/\log^2N\), so cancellation across the prime pair is indispensable.

## 2. What reflection symmetry actually gives

The reflection theorem is

\[
Z_p=-1-Z_p\pmod p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### 2.1 Singleton sets are forced to be central

If \(Z_p=\{a_p\}\), then (2.1) gives

\[
a_p\equiv-1-a_p\pmod p.
\]

Since \(0\le a_p<p\),

\[
\boxed{a_p=(p-1)/2.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Now let

\[
x=\operatorname{CRT}\left((p-1)/2,(q-1)/2\right),
\qquad 0\le x<pq.
\]

Then

\[
2x+1\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad
2x+1\equiv0\pmod q.
\]

Thus \(pq\mid2x+1\). Since

\[
1\le2x+1<2pq,
\]

one must have

\[
\boxed{x=(pq-1)/2.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Therefore the singleton contribution is

\[
\mathbf1_{\{pq\le2N+1\}}-\frac{N+1}{pq},
\tag{2.4}
\]

not a random Bernoulli error. Reflection creates rigidity, not equidistribution.

This does not by itself destroy an \(O(N)\) second-moment estimate; it only shows that the Poisson main constant must not be justified by treating singleton central roots as independent uniform residues.

### 2.2 A reflected pair gives complementary CRT representatives

Suppose

\[
Z_p=\{a,-1-a\},
\qquad
Z_q=\{b,-1-b\},
\]

with residues understood modulo their primes. If

\[
x=c_{p,q}(a,b),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,q}(-1-a,-1-b)=pq-1-x.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Indeed \(pq-1-x\equiv-1-a\pmod p\) and \(\equiv-1-b\pmod q\). The two mixed choices are also paired:

\[
c_{p,q}(-1-a,b)
+c_{p,q}(a,-1-b)=pq-1.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Hence the four CRT points split into two complementary pairs.

For one complementary pair \(\{x,pq-1-x\}\), the contribution to the low interval is

\[
\mathbf1_{\{x\le N\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{pq-1-x\le N\}}
-\frac{2(N+1)}{pq}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

There is no deterministic cancellation in (2.7). At the exact midpoint \(2N+1=pq\), exactly one point of a nonfixed complementary pair lies in \([0,N]\), so the centered contribution vanishes. Away from this special surface it can be positive or negative.

Thus reflection can make the range \(pq\approx2N\) especially regular, but it does not control the full prime-pair sum.

### 2.3 Fourier meaning of reflection

Set

\[
F_p(k):=\sum_{a\in Z_p}e_p(ka).
\]

From (2.1),

\[
F_p(k)=e_p(-k)F_p(-k)
=e_p(-k)\overline{F_p(k)}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Define the centered transform

\[
G_p(k):=e_p(k/2)F_p(k),
\]

where \(1/2\) denotes the inverse of \(2\) modulo \(p\). Then

\[
\boxed{G_p(k)\in\mathbf R,\qquad G_p(-k)=G_p(k).}
\tag{2.9}
\]

So reflection removes the odd component about the midpoint and lets one rewrite the CRT Fourier expansion with cosine-type coefficients. It does **not** bound \(|G_p(k)|\), and hence supplies no power saving by itself.

This agrees with the repository’s extremal warning: reflection, no adjacency, and individual gap-count caps can all hold while a horizontally aligned family still has large energy.

## 3. Why the \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) root structure does not control \(Z_p\)

Let

\[
A_p(T):=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jT^j\in\mathbf F_p[T].
\]

The project uses the factorization

\[
A_p(T)=\Delta(T)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(T)^2,
\qquad
\Delta(T)=T^2-34T+1,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and the roots/factors of \(B_p\) have strong \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) structure.

But

\[
Z_p=\{j:[T^j]A_p(T)=0\}
\]

is a set of **coefficient indices**. It is not

\[
\{T:A_p(T)=0\}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\{T:B_p(T)=0\}.
\]

The exact relation is finite Mellin duality. For \(1\le j\le p-2\),

\[
\boxed{
b_j=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-j}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus \(j\in Z_p\) means that a Mellin coefficient of the function \(A_p(t)\) vanishes. Galois conjugation of the evaluation roots of \(B_p\) constrains the factorization of the function in \(t\)-space, but it does not directly constrain which Mellin frequencies \(j\) vanish.

The repository gives the concrete separation at \(p=7\):

\[
A_7(T)=(T-1)^2(T^2+1)^2,
\]

so the evaluation polynomial has roots, while none of its coefficients vanishes and \(Z_7=\varnothing\).

Therefore the statement “the roots come in Frobenius-conjugate pairs in \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\)” cannot be inserted into the CRT problem as a theorem about the positions of \(Z_p\).

A geometric route remains possible only after constructing a sheaf/crystalline object whose **marked Mellin coordinate** vanishes exactly when \(b_j=0\), and then proving cross-characteristic cancellation for those marked coordinates. That is the corrected object already isolated in `oracleB_result.tex`.

## 4. Hecke eigenvalues and the central zero

The available modular congruence is

\[
\boxed{
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod p,}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
f_8=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
\]

is the weight-four level-eight newform.

### 4.1 What (4.1) does give

It detects the reflection-fixed zero:

\[
\frac{p-1}{2}\in Z_p
\iff p\mid a_p(f_8).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently, if \(|Z_p|=1\), then \(p\) is nonordinary for \(f_8\) and the zero is central.

### 4.2 What it does not give

It says nothing about the location \(a\) of a noncentral reflected pair

\[
\{a,p-1-a\}\subset Z_p.
\]

There is no known formula expressing this index \(a\) as an ordinary Hecke eigenvalue of one fixed modular form.

### 4.3 Why Sato–Tate does not solve the divisibility event

Sato–Tate concerns the archimedean distribution of

\[
\frac{a_p(f_8)}{2p^{3/2}}
\]

in fixed subintervals of \([-1,1]\). The event in (4.2) is

\[
p\mid a_p(f_8),
\]

a same-characteristic \(p\)-adic condition. If \(a_p(f_8)=pm_p\), then Deligne’s bound permits

\[
|m_p|\le2\sqrt p.
\]

Thus the normalized values allowed by divisibility form a grid of spacing about \(p^{-1/2}\) spread throughout the Sato–Tate interval. They are not contained in a fixed or shrinking interval whose Sato–Tate mass tends to zero.

Accordingly, the repository correctly treats density zero for these nonordinary primes of the non-CM weight-four form as open. Even a density theorem for the central primes would control only the exceptional fixed point, not the noncentral CRT distribution.

## 5. Why the supersingular/Sato–Tate analogy is a category error

There are three different objects that must not be identified.

1. A Hecke trace \(a_p(E)\) of a fixed elliptic curve as \(p\) varies.
2. A supersingular parameter or \(j\)-invariant in the moduli space over \(\mathbf F_p\).
3. A coefficient index \(r\in Z_p\), defined by \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\).

Sato–Tate addresses the first object. Equidistribution results for supersingular points or Hecke orbits address the second, with their own measures and quantifiers. The Apéry CRT problem concerns the third.

In particular, a supersingular \(j\)-invariant is not a Frobenius trace, and there is no general theorem asserting that a canonically selected supersingular coordinate divided by \(p\) is uniform in \([0,1]\) as \(p\) varies. More importantly, the repository’s Mellin calculation shows that the coefficient-zero set \(Z_p\) is not literally the evaluation supersingular locus of the truncation polynomial.

The K3/supersingular geometry may eventually produce the right marked Mellin object, but an additional transform and a horizontal independence theorem are indispensable. Sato–Tate alone does not perform either step.

## 6. Exact Fourier expansion of the CRT error

This is the cleanest form of the direct attack.

Let

\[
M=pq,
\qquad
I_L(x)=\mathbf1_{\{0\le x<L\}}
\quad (x\in\mathbf Z/M\mathbf Z),
\]

and define

\[
D_{L,M}(h):=\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}e_M(-hm).
\]

Fourier inversion gives

\[
I_L(x)=\frac1M\sum_{h\bmod M}D_{L,M}(h)e_M(hx).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let \(\bar q\) denote the inverse of \(q\) modulo \(p\), and \(\bar p\) the inverse of \(p\) modulo \(q\). A CRT representative satisfies

\[
c_{p,q}(a,b)\equiv aq\bar q+bp\bar p\pmod{pq},
\]

so

\[
e_{pq}\bigl(hc_{p,q}(a,b)\bigr)
=e_p(h\bar q a)e_q(h\bar p b).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Summing (6.1) over \(a\in Z_p,b\in Z_q\) yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
E_{p,q}(N)
=\frac1{pq}
\sum_{\substack{h\bmod pq\\h\ne0}}
D_{L,pq}(h)
F_p(h\bar q)F_q(h\bar p).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This formula identifies the actual missing structure. The frequencies are not merely \(F_p(h)F_q(h)\); they are twisted by the modular inverses

\[
h\bar q\pmod p,
\qquad
h\bar p\pmod q.
\]

Thus one needs cancellation jointly in

- the prime pair \((p,q)\),
- the interval frequency \(h\),
- the zero-set Fourier coefficients,
- and the reciprocal twists \(q^{-1}\bmod p\), \(p^{-1}\bmod q\).

The repository proves the useful one-prime estimate

\[
\sum_{1\le|k|\le K}|F_p(k)|^2
\ll K|Z_p|+\frac{p^2}{K}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is a genuine Fourier non-concentration theorem. But (6.4) alone does not control (6.3): the frequencies sampled at \(p\) depend on the other prime \(q\), and taking absolute values or applying Cauchy–Schwarz independently prime by prime loses the required cross-prime cancellation.

## 7. The simplest sufficient Apéry-specific input

There are three equivalent levels at which to state the missing theorem.

### 7.1 Minimal signed CRT discrepancy

The literal minimal input for the suffix channel is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\sqrt N<p<q\le N}
\sum_{a\in Z_p}\sum_{b\in Z_q}
\left(
 \mathbf1_{\{c_{p,q}(a,b)<N+1\}}
 -\frac{N+1}{pq}
\right)
=O(N).
}
\tag{AP-CRT2}
\]

Replacing \(O(N)\) by \(o(N)\) gives the random main constant. This is exactly the signed pair-dispersion theorem; no one-prime marginal statement is equivalent to it.

### 7.2 A square-root dyadic theorem

A more structural and stronger target is the following. For dyadic \(P,Q\) with

\[
\sqrt N\le P\le Q\le N,
\qquad PQ>N,
\]

put

\[
\mathcal D_N(P,Q)
:=\sum_{P<p\le2P}
  \sum_{Q<q\le2Q}
  \sum_{a\in Z_p,b\in Z_q}
\left(
 \mathbf1_{\{c_{p,q}(a,b)<N+1\}}
 -\frac{N+1}{pq}
\right),
\]

with the diagonal/order restrictions inserted when the blocks overlap.

If

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal D_N(P,Q)|
\ll
\left(
 \sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|
 \sum_{Q<q\le2Q}|Z_q|
\right)^{1/2}
}
\tag{AP-SQRT}
\]

uniformly in the dyadic blocks, then, using the empirically expected and separately desired bound

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|\ll P/\log P,
\]

the sum over all dyadic blocks is

\[
\ll
\left(\sum_P\sqrt{P/\log P}\right)^2
\ll \frac{N}{\log N}
=o(N).
\]

Even a logarithmic loss smaller than one full factor of \(\log N\) would still suffice. This is the natural “centered Bernoulli square-root cancellation” formulation.

### 7.3 A Kloosterman-twisted Fourier theorem

Using (6.3), a genuinely Apéry-specific analytic input is a bilinear estimate of the form

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{Q<q\le2Q}
\frac1{pq}
\sum_{h\ne0}
D_{N+1,pq}(h)
F_p(h\bar q)F_q(h\bar p)
\ll \frac{\sqrt{PQ}}{(\log N)^{1+\eta}}
}
\tag{AP-KL2}
\]

for some \(\eta>0\), uniformly in the relevant blocks. Variants with an \(L^2\)-average over \(h\), Vaaler truncation, or smooth interval weights are equally suitable.

This is the cleanest place to combine

- the one-prime Fourier estimate (6.4),
- the Mellin/sheaf interpretation of \(b_r\),
- and bilinear cancellation for modular inverses.

It is also exactly what reflection cannot provide: reflection makes the centered \(F_p\) real, but AP-KL2 needs a saving in the magnitude of the horizontal bilinear sum.

## 8. Why marginal equidistribution of \(a_p/p\) would not suffice

Suppose one could prove that a chosen first-half zero \(a_p/p\) is equidistributed in \([0,1/2]\). This is still only a one-prime marginal theorem. The low CRT event depends on the joint reciprocal expression

\[
c_{p,q}(a_p,a_q)
=a_p+p\left((a_q-a_p)p^{-1}\bmod q\right).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus it depends on how \(a_p\) and \(a_q\) correlate with the inverse of one prime modulo the other.

A scale-dependent anchored family illustrates the gap. Fix \(m_N\le N\) and choose

\[
a_p\equiv m_N\pmod p.
\]

Then for every pair

\[
\operatorname{CRT}(a_p,a_q)=m_N,
\]

so every CRT event hits, even though the individual normalized residues may wander with \(p\). Reflection can be imposed by adjoining \(-1-a_p\) without removing the common anchor.

Therefore any successful theorem must be horizontal and joint in \((p,q)\). Sato–Tate, one-prime discrepancy, or a bound on each \(|F_p(k)|\) separately cannot rule out the anchored-star mechanism.

## 9. Recommended direct program

The direct CRT route is still worthwhile, but it should be formulated as follows.

1. **Work with all zero pairs, not singleton zeros.** Separate the central fixed point and choose one representative from each noncentral reflected pair only for bookkeeping; restore all four CRT sign choices in the incidence count.

2. **Subtract the forced reflection component.** In centered coordinates, use the real-even transforms \(G_p(k)\). This removes odd test functions and isolates the genuinely off-center discrepancy, but do not count it as a power saving.

3. **Use smooth dyadic blocks.** Smooth the cutoff \([0,N]\), split \(p\sim P,q\sim Q\), and apply the exact factorization (6.2). The target is AP-KL2.

4. **Measure the correct bilinear phases computationally.** The repository already measures low-frequency Fourier mass and multiplicative collisions within each \(Z_p\). The next statistic should be
   \[
   \sum_{p\sim P,q\sim Q}
   F_p(h\bar q)F_q(h\bar p)
   \]
   for smooth frequency ranges, with central/reflection terms removed.

5. **Do not use the roots of \(B_p(T)\) as substitutes for \(Z_p\).** A geometric proof must pass through the marked Mellin coordinate whose vanishing is equivalent to \(b_r=0\).

6. **Remember the full-window four-channel issue.** AP-CRT2 would prove the suffix/suffix energy theorem. To close the full \((\sqrt N,N]\) model one must also control the two mixed channels and the leading/leading interval-overlap channel, or restrict first to the top-half laboratory described in `drops/Q388-ec3b50d9.md`.

## 10. Direct answers

### 1. Does reflection force cancellation in \(\sum\varepsilon_{p,q}\)?

Only partially and algebraically. A singleton is forced to the midpoint and has CRT value \((pq-1)/2\). A noncentral pair produces complementary CRT values \(x,pq-1-x\). This kills odd centered observables and gives exact cancellation when \(pq=2N+1\), but it does not cancel the general cutoff error. Reflection supplies symmetry, not equidistribution.

### 2. Does the \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) Galois structure of the truncation roots constrain CRT distribution?

Not directly. Those are evaluation roots in \(T\)-space. The indices in \(Z_p\) are zero Mellin coefficients. The exact identity (3.2) shows the required transform. A new theorem on the marked Mellin coordinate is needed before the Galois structure can enter the CRT problem.

### 3. Can Hecke eigenvalues and Sato–Tate locate the Apéry zeros?

Only the central coefficient is presently linked to one Hecke eigenvalue. Sato–Tate does not control the divisibility \(p\mid a_p(f_8)\), and it gives no location for noncentral zero pairs. Hence it does not imply CRT equidistribution.

### 4. Are the zero indices normalized supersingular \(j\)-invariants, equidistributed by Sato–Tate?

No. A supersingular parameter, a Frobenius trace, and a coefficient index are different objects. Sato–Tate concerns traces, not positions of supersingular parameters. The current Apéry zero set is Mellin-dual to the geometric family rather than literally its evaluation supersingular locus.

### 5. What is the simplest Apéry-specific input that proves the needed estimate?

The exact minimum is AP-CRT2. The simplest structurally useful strengthening is AP-SQRT: square-root cancellation of the centered CRT incidences in every dyadic prime block. In Fourier language, the needed arithmetic statement is AP-KL2, a bilinear cancellation theorem for

\[
F_p(hq^{-1})F_q(hp^{-1}).
\]

That is the direct target. It is weaker and more precise than a full Sato–Tate-type geometric theory, but stronger than every currently proved one-prime property of \(Z_p\).

```text
form is square-root cancellation in every dyadic (p,q)-block.  Fourier
inversion turns the error into inverse-twisted bilinear sums

  F_p(h q^{-1}) F_q(h p^{-1}),

so the simplest genuinely Apéry-specific input is a horizontal bilinear
large-sieve/Mellin theorem for these phases.  Reflection makes the centered
Fourier coefficients real and even, but provides no magnitude saving.
```
