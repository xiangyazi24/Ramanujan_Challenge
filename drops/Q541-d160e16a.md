# Transfer-matrix interpretation of the Apéry pair-zero condition

The recurrence route is a promising way to isolate the genuinely Apéry-specific part of the pair-zero problem. The key point is that the two-zero condition is not a random pair of equations: it is a vanishing condition for a matrix coefficient of the nonautonomous cocycle.

## (1) When is $L_1\cap T^{-1}(L_2)$ empty or a point?

Let
\[
T=T_{r\to s}=M(s-1)\cdots M(r)
=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}&T_{12}\\T_{21}&T_{22}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Take
\[
L_1=\{(0,b):b\in\mathbf F_p\},
\qquad
L_2=\{(0,c):c\in\mathbf F_p\}.
\]

A vector in $L_1$ has the form $(0,b)$. Applying $T$ gives
\[
T(0,b)^T=(T_{12}b,T_{22}b)^T.
\]

Therefore
\[
T(L_1)\cap L_2\neq\varnothing
\]
for a nonzero vector exactly when
\[
T_{12}=0.
\]

Equivalently,
\[
L_1\cap T^{-1}(L_2)
\]
contains a nonzero line exactly when the appropriate off-diagonal transfer coefficient vanishes.

The precise entry depends on the convention for whether the state vector is $(b_r,b_{r-1})$ or $(b_{r-1},b_r)$ and whether the transfer is applied left-to-right or right-to-left. Under the convention in the question, the condition is written as $T_{11}=0$; after changing basis to put the zero line in the first coordinate, it becomes the corresponding off-diagonal matrix coefficient. The invariant statement is:

\[
\boxed{\text{two independent zeros occur exactly when a specific transfer-matrix coefficient vanishes.}}
\]

Thus the pair locus is not generically a point. Generically the intersection is empty; the exceptional locus where the relevant coefficient is zero gives a one-dimensional intersection.

This is important: the recurrence does not predict a $1/p^2$ density for every pair $(r,s)$. Rather, the transfer coefficient itself has to vanish, and that is an additional arithmetic condition.

## (2) Density heuristic for the transfer coefficient zero locus

For a random element of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)$, a fixed matrix coefficient vanishes with probability approximately
\[
\frac{1}{p}.
\]

Therefore for fixed $(r,s)$ one expects
\[
\Pr(\text{pair transfer degeneracy})\sim\frac1p.
\]

Conditional on this degeneracy, the first zero condition contributes another factor $1/p$, giving
\[
\Pr(b_r=b_s=0)\sim\frac1{p^2}.
\]

There are about $p^2/8$ unordered non-reflection pairs, hence
\[
\mathbb E D_p
\approx \frac{p^2/8}{p^2}
=\frac18.
\]

This agrees with the pair-zero heuristic from Q522.

The required theorem would therefore have two layers:

1. **Cocycle equidistribution:** the transfer matrices
\[
T_{r\to s}
\]
for the Apéry recurrence must become equidistributed in the relevant finite-field symplectic/linear group.

2. **Matrix coefficient equidistribution:** the particular coefficient controlling the zero line intersection must have the expected $1/p$ vanishing probability.

A suitable statement would be something like:

For most primes $p$,
\[
\#\{(r,s):r<s,\ r\neq p-1-s,\ T_{r\to s}^{*}=0\}
=\left(c+o(1)\right)p,
\]
where $T^{*}$ denotes the relevant matrix coefficient.

Then summing over the initial zero condition gives
\[
\sum_p D_p=O(\pi(N)).
\]

## (3) Compact-group orbit sampling or defining-characteristic residual?

The easy analogy is compact-group orbit sampling, but it is not sufficient by itself.

The Sato--Tate/USp(4) picture controls Frobenius conjugacy classes of the geometric monodromy representation. It gives distributions of global invariants such as traces. However, the quantity here is much more local:

\[
T_{r\to s}\quad\text{for varying positions }r,s\text{ inside one finite-field recurrence orbit}.
\]

This is a finite-field dynamical equidistribution problem for a nonautonomous cocycle.

Therefore one needs a stronger "defining-characteristic residual" input:

* reduction of the Apéry differential equation modulo $p$;
* control of the finite-field monodromy representation;
* irreducibility / large image of the associated $\ell$-adic sheaf;
* Deligne-type bounds for the relevant trace functions.

The correct analogy is not simply Weyl integration on $USp(4)$, but rather a Katz-style theorem for a family of lisse sheaves. The transfer coefficient is a function on the geometric monodromy group, and its average requires cancellation of the associated character sum.

## Conclusion for P3.2

The recurrence route gives a plausible bridge:

\[
\text{two zeros}
\Longrightarrow
\text{vanishing of a transfer matrix coefficient}
\Longrightarrow
\text{codimension-one condition in monodromy}
\Longrightarrow
D_p=O(1).
\]

The strongest Apéry-specific ingredient is therefore not merely the Sp(4) Sato--Tate distribution, but the **large finite-field monodromy of the recurrence cocycle**. The desired theorem is essentially:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Apéry cocycle matrix coefficients satisfy Deligne/Katz square-root cancellation.}
}
\]

Such a result would give the non-reflection pair-zero estimate and hence the M1 bound.
