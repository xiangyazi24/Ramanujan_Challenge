# Q442: Joint independence of Apéry alpha parameters across primes

## Executive verdict

The Dwork/crystalline description gives an exact **local marker** for an Apéry zero, but the currently established structure does **not** imply the cross-prime decorrelation needed for P3.2.

There are two important corrections to the proposed route.

1. The displayed rectangle law for all pairs \(p<q<X\) is not a genuinely new two-prime independence theorem. After restricting to primes for which \(\alpha_p\) is defined, it follows automatically from marginal equidistribution of the single sequence \((\alpha_p)\).
2. P3.2 does not average over all prime pairs with equal weight. It samples a scale-dependent, frequency-dependent set of **near-Farey pairs**. Marginal equidistribution, and even the unweighted all-pairs rectangle law, can coexist with perfect correlation on that sparse weighted graph.

The correct conclusion is therefore

```text
marginal alpha equidistribution
        => unweighted all-pairs rectangle law
        != near-Farey / growing-frequency / two-characteristic dispersion
```

The precise missing input is already isolated in the repository:

- analytically, the one-sided cross-prime short-determinant estimate `(SDC)` in `oracleA_result.tex`;
- arithmetically, the marked two-characteristic crystalline Mellin dispersion estimate in `oracleB_result.tex`.

Product monodromy or independence of compatible realizations would be a useful algebraic stage of such a proof, but it does not by itself give the required \(1/p\)-scale anti-concentration or the weighted horizontal large sieve.

## 0. Two scope corrections

### 0.1 \(\alpha_p\) is not defined for all primes

Most primes in the computation have \(Z_p=\varnothing\). Thus one must first specify an active set, for example

\[
\mathcal P_*:=\{p:Z_p\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

If the argument uses the exact two-cosine formula, the relevant set is narrower:

\[
\mathcal P_2:=\left\{p:Z_p=\{r_p,p-1-r_p\},\quad
0\le r_p<\frac{p-1}{2}\right\}.
\]

On \(\mathcal P_2\), define

\[
\alpha_p=\frac{2r_p+1}{p}\in(0,1).
\]

The normalization in the question should then use

\[
\binom{\pi_2^*(X)}2,
\qquad
\pi_2^*(X):=\#\{p<X:p\in\mathcal P_2\},
\]

not the number of all prime pairs. If one insists on normalizing by all primes and the active primes have relative density \(\delta\), then the limiting mass in a rectangle in \((0,1)^2\) contains an extra factor \(\delta^2\).

There is also a possible central zero at \(r=(p-1)/2\), for which the displayed \(\alpha_p\) equals \(1\), not a point of \((0,1)\).

### 0.2 A single \(\alpha_p\) determines \(F_p\) only on the doublet sector

For

\[
F_p(a):=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(ar),
\qquad e_p(x)=e^{2\pi i x/p},
\]

and a doublet \(Z_p=\{r_p,p-1-r_p\}\), put

\[
h_p=p-1-2r_p=p(1-\alpha_p).
\]

Then the exact finite-field identity is

\[
F_p(a)
=2e_p\!\left(\frac{a(p-1)}2\right)
  \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi a h_p}{p}\right).
\tag{0.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
|F_p(a)|=2|\cos(\pi a\alpha_p)|.
\tag{0.2}
\]

The absolute-value identity loses the moving phase in (0.1), and that phase is part of the signed bilinear form. Moreover, if \(z_p\ge4\), then \(F_p(a)\) is a sum of several reflected cosine terms; the smallest zero alone does not determine it. The repository gives the concrete example

\[
Z_{181}=\{19,47,133,161\}.
\]

Thus an \(\alpha_p\)-only proof must either:

1. prove that non-doublet and central sectors contribute negligibly; or
2. replace \(\alpha_p\) by the full zero set, equivalently the full vector \(F_p(a)\).

Finally, the continuous Fourier sum for the actual shell \((N,2N]\) has a clipping issue. The two displayed lifts of a reflected pair do not always both lie in the shell, and additional quotient lifts may occur. The exact shell argument must use the lift sets in `oracleA_result.tex`, not an unrestricted two-term cosine formula.

## 1. The proposed all-pairs joint law follows from marginal equidistribution

This is the most useful conceptual correction.

Enumerate the active primes in increasing order,

\[
p_1<p_2<\cdots,
\qquad x_n:=\alpha_{p_n}.
\]

Assume that \((x_n)\) is uniformly distributed in \((0,1)\). For intervals \(I,J\subset(0,1)\), put

\[
A_I(m)=\#\{n\le m:x_n\in I\}.
\]

Define the prefix discrepancy

\[
\Delta(n):=\sup_{m\le n}\sup_I
\left|A_I(m)-|I|m\right|.
\]

Uniform distribution implies \(\Delta(n)=o(n)\).

### Proposition: marginal equidistribution implies the unweighted pair law

For fixed intervals \(I,J\),

\[
\#\{1\le i<j\le n:x_i\in I,\ x_j\in J\}
=|I||J|\binom n2+O(n\Delta(n)+n).
\tag{1.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\frac{1}{\binom n2}
\#\{i<j:x_i\in I,\ x_j\in J\}
\longrightarrow |I||J|.
\tag{1.2}
\]

#### Proof

Write \(a=|I|\), \(b=|J|\), and \(1_J(j)=\mathbf1_{x_j\in J}\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{I,J}(n)
&=\sum_{j=1}^n1_J(j)A_I(j-1)\\
&=a\sum_{j=1}^n(j-1)1_J(j)+O(n\Delta(n)).
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(B_J(m)=\sum_{j\le m}1_J(j)=bm+O(\Delta(n))\) for \(m\le n\). Summation by parts gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^n(j-1)1_J(j)
=(n-1)B_J(n)-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}B_J(m)
=b\binom n2+O(n\Delta(n)).
\]

Substitution proves (1.1).

### Consequence

The rectangle law in the question is too weak to certify stochastic independence. It is simply a consequence of the one-point empirical distribution when all \(\asymp n^2\) ordered-by-size pairs are averaged.

A simple counterexample is

\[
x_{2m-1}=x_{2m}=u_m,
\]

where \((u_m)\) is uniformly distributed. Then \((x_n)\) is uniformly distributed and satisfies (1.2), but every adjacent pair is perfectly correlated.

This is exactly the logical shape of the P3.2 issue: the relevant kernel selects a structured family of pairs, just as “adjacent pairs” did in the example. The all-pairs average washes out any dependence supported on a sparse graph.

The repository's reflected anchored construction in `oracleA_result.tex` is a much stronger Apéry-shaped version of this counterexample. It gives palindromic doublets of uniformly bounded local conductor that still concentrate on one common shell point and violate the desired short-arc estimate by a power of \(N\).

## 2. What Dwork theory does and does not make independent

### 2.1 The local crystalline statement is exact

The marked Kummer-twisted object developed in `lemmaBprime_result.tex` has effective Frobenius factor

\[
P_{p,j}(X)
=(X-p)\bigl(X^2-a_{p,j}X+p^3\bigr),
\qquad
 a_{p,j}\equiv b_j\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Its unit-root count satisfies

\[
m_0(p,j)=
\begin{cases}
1,&b_j\not\equiv0\pmod p,\\
0,&b_j\equiv0\pmod p.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus Dwork theory really does identify \(Z_p\) as the set of moving Kummer twists for which the marked unit root disappears.

But (2.2) is a local identification, not a distribution theorem. The first zero is the nonlinear order statistic

\[
r_p=\min\{j:m_0(p,j)=0\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

To understand \(r_p\), one needs the joint behavior of the entire row

\[
\bigl(m_0(p,1),\ldots,m_0(p,p-2)\bigr),
\]

not merely one Frobenius conjugacy class.

### 2.2 Different completions are not independent random objects

The \(p\)-adic and \(q\)-adic realizations are different local realizations, but they arise from the same global recurrence/motive. They are linked by compatibility, integral structures, functional equations, and common algebraic correspondences. “Different coefficient fields” does not mean “independent random variables.”

There are several notions of independence here, and they must not be conflated.

1. **Linear disjointness or product monodromy.** Serre's “almost independence” of \(\ell\)-adic representations, or a Goursat argument giving a product monodromy group, means that the images have no unexpected common quotient after a suitable finite extension.
2. **Chebotarev independence for fixed class functions.** Given a fixed compatible system and fixed conjugacy-invariant tests, product monodromy may yield product distributions of Frobenius classes.
3. **The P3.2 event.** Here the characteristic \(p\) is simultaneously the prime being averaged, the coefficient field, and the modulus in the exact event \(a_{p,j}\equiv0\pmod p\), while \(j\) itself ranges up to \(p\) and is selected by a minimum.

The third statement does not follow formally from the first two.

In an ordinary Chebotarev argument one fixes an auxiliary \(\ell\) and studies \(\operatorname{Frob}_p\) for \(p\ne\ell\). The Apéry zero event is instead **self-characteristic**: it uses the crystalline realization at the same place \(p\). There is presently no fixed regular function on one fixed adelic monodromy group whose value at \(\operatorname{Frob}_p\) is the first zero \(r_p\).

Even granting the product-monodromy results already developed in the project, their correct interpretation is:

> there is no evident algebraic coupling forcing the two marked systems to move together.

That is excellent heuristic evidence and an important algebraic prerequisite. It is not yet an effective horizontal mixing theorem for the minima.

### 2.3 Product monodromy is still above the wrong scale

Suppose one constructs a fixed-rank sheaf and proves full product monodromy. Deligne-type equidistribution typically gives square-root errors when averaging over a finite-field parameter. Here the expected number of exact Hasse zeros among \(p\) twists is of order \(1\). A generic \(O(\sqrt p)\) error is much larger than the main term.

The missing step is therefore a local-limit or anti-concentration result at probability scale \(1/p\), uniform in all moving tame characters. This is substantially finer than ordinary Sato-Tate or geometric equidistribution.

## 3. Effective joint equidistribution: what is available and what would be relevant

### 3.1 Effective all-pairs equidistribution is no harder than the marginal problem

If one proves an effective marginal discrepancy

\[
\Delta(n)\ll E(n),
\]

then (1.1) immediately gives

\[
C_{I,J}(n)
=|I||J|\binom n2+O(nE(n)).
\tag{3.1}
\]

So the all-pairs rectangle theorem in the question requires no new Dwork independence input. The current repository, however, records finite KS and permutation tests, not a proof of a marginal discrepancy estimate. In `oracleB_result.tex`, the finite audit through \(p\le20000\) gives a small KS distance on the doublet sector; this remains numerical evidence.

### 3.2 Fixed-frequency Weyl sums are also insufficient

Marginal equidistribution would imply, for each fixed integer \(h\ne0\),

\[
\sum_{p<X\atop p\in\mathcal P_2}
\cos(\pi h\alpha_p)=o(\pi_2^*(X)).
\tag{3.2}
\]

But the P3.2 bilinear form uses frequencies \(a,b\) that grow with \(p,q\), often up to order \(P\), and the admissible pairs are selected by

\[
\left\|\frac ap-\frac bq\right\|<\frac1M.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus one would need uniformity in microscopic intervals or growing Fourier modes, together with the moving Farey weight. Fixed-mode Weyl equidistribution does not provide it.

### 3.3 Why the standard analytic frameworks do not apply as black boxes

- **Bombieri-Friedlander-Iwaniec / dispersion methods.** These exploit specific convolution, character, or Kloosterman structures. The deterministic selector \(p\mapsto r_p\) is not presently known to have such a representation.
- **Selberg moments of L-functions and Katz-Sarnak.** These concern distributions of normalized Frobenius eigenvalues or zeros in a fixed family. The quantity \(r_p\) is the first vanishing coefficient among \(p\)-dependent Kummer twists, not a fixed trace or low-degree class function of the K3 local factor.
- **Large sieve for Frobenius.** Kowalski's framework and Perret-Gentil's trace-function variant require a compatible system with known monodromy and a sparse algebraic exceptional subset. The repository does not yet have a bounded-complexity cross-characteristic object in the coefficient index whose exceptional set is exactly \(b_j\equiv0\pmod p\).
- **Trace functions over primes.** Fouvry-Kowalski-Michel treat sums of a fixed finite-field trace function over prime arguments. The modulus/sheaf is fixed while the integer argument varies. Here both characteristics \(p,q\), both marked twists, and both slopes move.
- **Recent bilinear trace-function bounds.** The 2026 revision of Fouvry-Kowalski-Michel-Sawin gives broad bilinear bounds under structural monodromy hypotheses for a trace function over one fixed finite field. It is potentially relevant after a common-modulus encoding has been constructed, but it does not itself create that encoding for two varying self-characteristics.

Accordingly, these theories describe plausible ingredients, not an existing theorem that can be cited to close P3.2.

## 4. The minimal distribution statement actually needed

The exact Fourier reduction in `oracleA_result.tex` is preferable to an informal statement about pairwise correlations.

For a dyadic block \(P<p\le2P\), define

\[
c_{p,a}=\frac{(\log p)F_p(a)}p,
\qquad
Q_P=\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}|c_{p,a}|^2,
\]

and

\[
K_M(t)=\left(\frac1M-\|t\|\right)_+.
\]

The unequal-prime term is

\[
R_{\ne}(P,M)=
\sum_{\substack{P<p,q\le2P\\p\ne q}}
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\sum_{b=1}^{q-1}
K_M\!\left(\frac ap-\frac bq\right)
 c_{p,a}\overline{c_{q,b}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The quantifier-correct missing analytic lemma is the one-sided Rayleigh estimate

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}R_{\ne}(P,M)
\ll M^{-1+o(1)}Q_P
}
\tag{SDC}
\]

uniformly in the range needed by the Gallagher dilation argument, namely \(\sqrt M<P\le M\).

This is weaker than full joint equidistribution in many directions and stronger in the one direction that matters. It asks only for cancellation of the actual Apéry vector against the actual near-Farey kernel.

The reciprocal-fraction parametrization makes the moving nature explicit. One obtains

\[
R_{\ne}(P,M)=\frac1M\mathcal B_{P,M},
\]

where

\[
\mathcal B_{P,M}
=\sum_{p\ne q}
 \frac{(\log p)(\log q)}{pq}
 \sum_{\substack{0<|k|<pq/M\\(k,pq)=1}}
 \left(1-\frac{M|k|}{pq}\right)
 F_p(k\bar q)F_q(k\bar p).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Both arguments \(k\bar q\pmod p\) and \(k\bar p\pmod q\) move jointly with the two primes. This is not reduced by knowing the macroscopic distribution of \((\alpha_p,\alpha_q)\).

### A constant correlation gap is not enough

Suppose the sum of absolute weights is \(A_N\asymp N^2/\log^2N\). A bound of the form

\[
|\text{signed sum}|\le(1-\varepsilon)A_N
\]

with fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) is still of order \(N^2/\log^2N\); it does not give little-oh. One needs the normalized weighted correlation to tend to zero, or a one-sided estimate at the much smaller diagonal-energy scale in `(SDC)`.

This also explains why the signed/absolute ratio \(0.009\) is good evidence but not the theorem. The proof needs the complete weighted sum, uniformly over dyadic scales and frequency ranges, with control of rare concentrated configurations—not a random sample of pairs at one scale.

### Equivalent arithmetic target

The repository's `oracleB_result.tex` gives a second, incidence-space formulation. For \(N<p\le2N\), \(m\in I_N=(N,2N]\), set

\[
X_p(m)=\mathbf1_{\{p\le m\}}
       \mathbf1_{\{b_{m-p}\equiv0\pmod p\}},
\]

and

\[
T_N=\sum_{N<p\le2N}\sum_{m\in I_N}X_p(m).
\]

The desired marked two-characteristic estimate is

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{\substack{N<p,q\le2N\\p\ne q}}
\sum_{m\in I_N}X_p(m)X_q(m)
-\frac{T_N^2}{N}
\right|
\ll N^{o(1)}T_N.
}
\tag{MH2}
\]

This is enough because, with \(L_N(m)=\sum_pX_p(m)\),

\[
\sum_{m\in I_N}
\left(L_N(m)-\frac{T_N}{N}\right)^2
=T_N+F_N-\frac{T_N^2}{N},
\]

where \(F_N\) is the ordered off-diagonal collision count. Thus `(MH2)` is exactly a centered second-factorial-moment theorem.

For P3.2, `(SDC)` or `(MH2)` is the correct minimal target. Full two-dimensional rectangle equidistribution of \(\alpha_p\) is neither necessary nor sufficient.

## 5. What CRT and the gcd structure actually give

### 5.1 CRT gives exact independence over a complete period

For fixed primes \(p\ne q\), define

\[
Y_p(m)=\mathbf1_{\{m\bmod p\in Z_p\}}.
\]

Over a complete period modulo \(pq\), CRT gives the exact identity

\[
\sum_{m\bmod pq}Y_p(m)Y_q(m)=z_pz_q,
\tag{5.1}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\sum_{m\bmod pq}
\left(Y_p(m)-\frac{z_p}{p}\right)
\left(Y_q(m)-\frac{z_q}{q}\right)=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is a useful exact algebraic fact, but it averages the **common integer variable** \(m\). It says nothing about the distribution of the row statistic \(r_p=\min Z_p\) as \(p\) varies.

### 5.2 The P3.2 interval is shorter than the CRT period

For \(p,q>\sqrt N\), one has \(pq>N\). On an interval of length \(N\), each CRT class occurs at most once. The complete-period main term is

\[
\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq},
\]

which is typically much smaller than \(1\), while the elementary boundary discrepancy is \(O(1)\) per pair. Summed over many pairs, that \(O(1)\) error is precisely the familiar CRT barrier.

Thus CRT identifies the expected main term but does not provide the cancellation of incomplete-period errors.

There is also a Lucas qualification. The first-digit event

\[
m\bmod p\in Z_p
\]

implies \(p\mid b_m\) by the Apéry-Lucas product. The full divisibility predicate \(p\mid b_m\), however, depends on all base-\(p\) digits, not only the first one.

### 5.3 Gcd and size arguments control common anchors, but at the wrong strength

If many primes hit the same integer \(m\), then their product divides \(b_m\) in the relevant Lucas channel. Since

\[
\log b_m\asymp m,
\]

size alone permits as many as \(O(m/\log m)\) prime divisors of size \(\asymp m\). That is still the size of a full dyadic prime block and therefore does not rule out the anchored obstruction.

The existing gcd/Casoratian and gap-polynomial machinery is valuable in other directions:

- no consecutive zeros and gap polynomials constrain multiple zeros for one fixed prime;
- resultants control repeated large-prime divisors at fixed gaps;
- fixed integer anchors involve only finitely many prime divisors.

But \(r_p\) and \(r_q\) are minima in different rows and usually correspond to different integer indices. There is no direct gcd whose smallness forces the pair \((r_p,r_q)\) to be independent.

A gcd route could still succeed if it proves a genuinely new theorem such as a uniform large-prime collision bound or a many-prime rank-drop forcing theorem. That would be new cross-prime arithmetic input—not a formal consequence of CRT.

## 6. What a rigorous Dwork-based proof would have to contain

A realistic proof architecture has two logically distinct layers.

### Layer A: algebraic independence

Construct one integral marked family whose specialization at \((p,j)\) gives the Kummer-twisted crystalline object

\[
\mathcal M_{p,j}
=R\Gamma_{c,\mathrm{rig}}
\bigl(U_p,\mathcal V_{\mathrm{tr},p}
      \otimes\mathcal K_{\omega_p^{-j}}\bigr),
\]

with a coordinate \(c_{p,j}\) satisfying

\[
c_{p,j}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b_j\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then prove nonisomorphism and product monodromy for distinct moving twists. The product-monodromy work already present in the project is evidence that this stage may be feasible.

### Layer B: microscopic horizontal anti-concentration

Prove that the marked coordinates in (6.1) satisfy a two-characteristic large-sieve estimate uniformly for

\[
j\asymp p,\qquad k\asymp q,\qquad p,q\asymp P,
\]

at exact-zero probability scale \(1/p\) and with the Farey weights in (4.1) or the common-anchor weights in `(MH2)`.

This is the genuinely missing theorem. Product monodromy alone generally supplies square-root cancellation for ordinary trace averages; it does not automatically produce a local-limit theorem for a residue being exactly zero in its own characteristic.

### Layer C: analytic transfer

Insert the Layer B estimate into the exact Fejér/triangular-kernel identity of `oracleA_result.tex`. This step is already rigorous in the repository: `(SDC)` implies the weighted dispersion bound needed for all-index P3.2.

So the shortest honest description of the missing package is:

```text
marked integral crystal
+ product monodromy for moving Kummer twists
+ self-characteristic 1/p anti-concentration
+ horizontal two-prime large sieve
=> SDC / MH2
=> P3.2
```

The first two lines are geometric. The third and fourth are a new arithmetic-statistical theorem and cannot be replaced by marginal equidistribution of \(\alpha_p\).

## 7. Direct answers to the four questions

### Question 1

The \(p\)-adic and \(q\)-adic crystals are distinct local realizations, and product monodromy can show absence of an algebraic common quotient. They are not independent random objects merely because the completions differ. Both come from the same global Apéry structure, and the first-zero statistic is a nonlinear, self-characteristic function of an entire moving family of twists.

**Answer:** algebraic independence is plausible and partly supported by the project, but statistical independence of \(r_p,r_q\) does not follow from Dwork theory alone.

### Question 2

The all-pairs rectangle law, correctly normalized on the active/doublet primes, follows from marginal equidistribution by Proposition (1.1). No extra two-prime theorem is needed for that statement.

No existing large-sieve, Katz-Sarnak, or trace-function theorem currently yields the relevant near-Farey weighted, growing-frequency, two-characteristic estimate for the Apéry minima.

**Answer:** effective unweighted joint equidistribution reduces to an effective marginal discrepancy; effective P3.2 decorrelation remains open and is substantially stronger.

### Question 3

The minimal useful statement is not a fixed correlation gap \(1-\varepsilon\). It is the one-sided scalar bound `(SDC)`, or equivalently the marked centered second-moment estimate `(MH2)`, uniformly over the required dyadic scales.

**Answer:** the normalized signed correlation on the actual weighted Farey graph must tend to zero or be bounded at the diagonal-energy scale. A constant-factor saving does not imply the required little-oh estimate.

### Question 4

CRT gives exact product counts over a complete period \(pq\), but P3.2 works on intervals shorter than \(pq\), where the \(O(1)\)-per-pair discrepancy dominates. It also concerns row minima, not a common residue variable. Gcd and size structure can control fixed anchors or repeated divisors, but current bounds do not exclude a moving anchored star.

**Answer:** CRT/gcd structure is a useful component of a future proof, but it does not turn marginal randomness of \(r_p\) into rigorous cross-prime decorrelation.

---

## Final conclusion

The proposed heuristic is sound at the level of intuition: there is no evident motivic reason for the first Apéry zero in characteristic \(p\) to coordinate with the first zero in characteristic \(q\). But the rigorous inference

\[
\text{different local crystals}
+\text{marginal equidistribution}
\Longrightarrow
\text{P3.2 decorrelation}
\]

is false as a formal implication.

The unweighted pair law is already contained in the marginal theorem and is too coarse. The load-bearing target is a **scale-sensitive, weighted, growing-frequency, two-characteristic anti-concentration theorem** for the marked Dwork coordinates. In the repository's current language, prove `(SDC)` or `(MH2)`; product monodromy should be viewed as one algebraic input toward that theorem, not as its substitute.
