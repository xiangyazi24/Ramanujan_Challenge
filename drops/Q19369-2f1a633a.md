# 1. Reconstructing exactly where §4.5 loses a logarithm

For \(n\in(N,2N]\), write
\[
\mathcal P(n)
=
\{p>\sqrt n:\ n\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad
t(n)=|\mathcal P(n)|.
\]
From FINAL_AUDIT.md (4.14), there is an absolute \(c_0>0\) such that,
for every fixed \(0<\varepsilon\le1\) and all sufficiently large
\(N\) (depending on \(\varepsilon\)),
\[
\log G_n>\varepsilon n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t(n)\ge
T:=c_0\varepsilon\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

All primes in these sets lie in \((\sqrt N,2N]\).  Let
\[
L_N:=\#\{p:\sqrt N<p\le2N\}.
\]
Chebyshev/PNT gives, with an absolute \(C_L\),
\[
L_N\le C_L\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

For distinct \(m<n\) in the block, put \(h=n-m\).  The repaired codegree
lemma gives
\[
|\mathcal P(m)\cap\mathcal P(n)|
\le C_{\rm cd}\,h
\tag{1.3}
\]
with an absolute \(C_{\rm cd}\), by taking \(P_0=\sqrt N\).

Of course one should always read (1.3) as
\[
|\mathcal P(m)\cap\mathcal P(n)|
\le
\min\!\left(C_{\rm cd}h,\ L_N\right).
\tag{1.4}
\]
Thus the theorem is genuinely nontrivial only while
\[
h\lesssim \frac{N}{\log N}.
\]

For an exceptional integer, the Cauchy lower bound on pair incidence is of
scale
\[
\frac{T^2}{L_N}
\asymp
\varepsilon^2\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{1.5}
\]
Therefore the natural critical distance is exactly
\[
\boxed{
Y_2\asymp
\varepsilon^2\frac{N}{\log N}.
}
\tag{1.6}
\]

This confirms the coordinator's bottleneck: the pair proof has no saving on
the scale of the whole dyadic block.  The only correction is that
\(h\asymp N\) is not the only missing range; everything between \(Y_2\) and
\(N\) is missing.

# 2. What an FP theorem would actually have to say

A convenient sufficient form is the following.

> **FP-plus(\(\varepsilon\)).**  There is
> \(\kappa(\varepsilon)=o(\varepsilon^2)\) such that, for all sufficiently
> large \(N\) and all distinct \(m,n\in(N,2N]\) with
> \[
> n-m\ge c\varepsilon^2N/\log N,
> \]
> one has
> \[
> |\mathcal P(m)\cap\mathcal P(n)|
> \le
> \kappa(\varepsilon)\frac{N}{\log N}.
> \tag{2.1}
> \]

The current \(O(h)\) theorem handles the complementary short range after
choosing \(c\) small.  Together they give, for **all distinct exceptional
pairs in the block**,
\[
|\mathcal P(m)\cap\mathcal P(n)|
\le
c_1\varepsilon^2\frac{N}{\log N}
\tag{2.2}
\]
with \(c_1\) smaller than the constant supplied by the Cauchy lower bound.

If the block contains \(M\) exceptional integers and
\[
I=\sum_p d_p\ge TM,
\]
then
\[
\sum_p\binom{d_p}{2}
\ge
\frac12\left(\frac{I^2}{L_N}-I\right)
\asymp
\varepsilon^2\frac{N}{\log N}M^2
\tag{2.3}
\]
once \(M\gg1/\varepsilon\).

On the other hand, (2.2) gives
\[
\sum_p\binom{d_p}{2}
\le
c_1\varepsilon^2\frac{N}{\log N}\binom M2.
\tag{2.4}
\]
For \(c_1\) sufficiently small, (2.3) and (2.4) force
\[
M=O(1/\varepsilon).
\tag{2.5}
\]
Hence FP-plus would give
\[
\#\{n\in(N,2N]:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}
=O_\varepsilon(1),
\]
and summing dyadic blocks gives
\[
O_\varepsilon(\log X).
\]

## 2.1 Why “h asymptotic to N only” is insufficient

Suppose a theorem is available only when
\[
cN<h<CN.
\]
All exceptional integers could lie in an interval of length \(N/100\).
Then no pair activates the theorem.  The existing local argument still
permits \(O_\varepsilon(\log N)\) points inside that smaller interval.

So an \(h\asymp N\) estimate is evidence for the right phenomenon, but the
actual input needed by this proof is a medium/far statement beginning at
the critical scale (1.6), or an averaged statement strong enough to play
the same role.

## 2.2 Is FP necessary?

Not as a theorem of logic.

One could replace it by:

* a sufficiently strong average pair-codegree estimate;
* a \(k\)-wise intersection theorem and a higher-moment argument;
* a direct pointwise theorem that prevents exceptional \(n\);
* or a structural theorem forcing empty characteristics.

Thus FP is **one natural sufficient bridge** from
\(O_\varepsilon((\log X)^2)\) to \(O_\varepsilon(\log X)\), not a necessary
condition for the conclusion.

Within the present second-moment incidence proof, however, something with
the content of (2.1) is unavoidable: once \(h\gg N/\log N\), the banked
pair estimate is indistinguishable from the trivial universe bound.

# 3. FP, W, and the pointwise q=1 target are different statements

For q=1 let
\[
B_1(n)=
\{p\in(n/2,n]:p\mid b_{n-p}\},
\qquad
L_1(n)=\pi(n)-\pi(n/2).
\]

W says
\[
|B_1(n)|\le(1-\delta)L_1(n)
\tag{3.1}
\]
for every large \(n\).

The full q=1 pointwise target says
\[
|B_1(n)|=o(L_1(n)).
\tag{3.2}
\]

A q=1 pair analogue would be
\[
|B_1(m)\cap B_1(n)|
=o\!\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)
\tag{3.3}
\]
for suitable \(m,n\asymp N\).

The implications are:

\[
\boxed{(3.2)\Longrightarrow(3.3)}
\]
because the intersection is contained in either marginal.

But
\[
\boxed{\text{W does not imply (3.3).}}
\]
At the level of set information, all \(B_1(n)\) could equal the same
\((1-\delta)L\)-element set.  Every marginal would satisfy W while every
pair intersection would still have size \((1-\delta)L\).

Conversely,
\[
\boxed{\text{pair-FP does not imply W or (3.2).}}
\]
One isolated \(B_1(n)\) may have size \(L\) while all other sets are disjoint
from it.  Pair intersections are tiny, but W fails at that one \(n\).

This is the same singleton barrier already identified in Q19347.

There is a second distinction relevant to §4.5.  The exceptional-set theorem
uses the **master lower-digit set**
\[
\mathcal P(n)=
\{p>\sqrt n:p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\},
\]
not just q=1.  Therefore a q=1 FP theorem alone does not improve (4.13):
an exceptional \(n\) could receive its load from quotient cells \(q\ge2\).
For the GCD exceptional-set argument one needs a master FP, or sufficiently
uniform fixed-q analogues that reassemble to one.

Finally, even master FP-plus would only give
\(O_\varepsilon(\log X)\) exceptions.  It still allows one exceptional
integer per dyadic scale, so it is far weaker than the full GCD target,
which is equivalent to **finitely many** exceptions for every
\(\varepsilon>0\).

# 4. New unconditional theorem: the local bound is O(1/epsilon), not O(1/epsilon^2)

This is a small but real improvement to the strongest theorem.

## Theorem 4.1 — epsilon-refined critical-window bound

There are absolute constants \(c,C>0\) such that, for every fixed
\(0<\varepsilon\le1\), for all sufficiently large \(N\), every interval
\[
J\subset(N,2N]
\]
of length
\[
Y=c\varepsilon^2\frac{N}{\log N}
\tag{4.1}
\]
contains at most
\[
\boxed{\frac{C}{\varepsilon}}
\tag{4.2}
\]
integers \(n\) with \(\log G_n>\varepsilon n\).

### Proof

Let the exceptional integers in \(J\) be \(n_1,\ldots,n_M\).  For each
eligible prime \(p\), let
\[
d_p=\#\{j:p\in\mathcal P(n_j)\},
\qquad
I=\sum_p d_p.
\]
By (1.1),
\[
I\ge TM,
\qquad
T=c_0\varepsilon\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let \(L=L_N\), so by (1.2)
\[
L\le C_L\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Cauchy gives
\[
\sum_p\binom{d_p}{2}
=
\frac12\left(\sum_pd_p^2-I\right)
\ge
\frac12\left(\frac{I^2}{L}-I\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The function
\[
f(x)=x^2/L-x
\]
is increasing for \(x\ge L/2\).

Assume for contradiction that
\[
M>\frac{2L}{T}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Then \(TM>2L\), hence \(I\ge TM>L/2\), and (4.5) may be evaluated at
the lower endpoint \(TM\):
\[
\sum_p\binom{d_p}{2}
\ge
\frac12\left(
\frac{T^2M^2}{L}-TM
\right).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The codegree theorem gives
\[
\sum_p\binom{d_p}{2}
\le
C_{\rm cd}
\sum_{i<j}|n_i-n_j|
\le
C_{\rm cd}YM^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Choose \(c\) in (4.1) so small that
\[
C_{\rm cd}Y
\le
\frac18\,\frac{T^2}{L}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
This is possible with an absolute \(c\), because
\[
\frac{T^2}{L}
\ge
\frac{c_0^2}{C_L}
\varepsilon^2\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Combining (4.7)--(4.9),
\[
\frac12\frac{T^2M^2}{L}-\frac12TM
\le
\frac18\frac{T^2M^2}{L}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac38\frac{T^2M^2}{L}
\le
\frac12TM,
\]
and hence
\[
M\le\frac{4L}{3T}.
\tag{4.11}
\]
This contradicts (4.6).  Thus
\[
M\le\frac{2L}{T}
\le
\frac{2C_L}{c_0}\,\varepsilon^{-1}.
\]
This proves (4.2). \(\square\)

## Corollary 4.2 — explicit leading epsilon dependence

Partition \((N,2N]\) into intervals of length \(Y\) from (4.1).  Their
number is
\[
O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\varepsilon^2}\right).
\]
Each contains \(O(\varepsilon^{-1})\) exceptions by Theorem 4.1.  Hence
\[
\boxed{
\#\{n\in(N,2N]:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}
\ll
\varepsilon^{-3}\log N.
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

Summing over dyadic blocks gives
\[
\boxed{
\#\{n\le X:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}
\ll
\varepsilon^{-3}(\log X)^2+O_\varepsilon(1).
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Equivalently, the leading asymptotic constant in the existing
\(O_\varepsilon((\log X)^2)\) theorem can be taken
\(O(\varepsilon^{-3})\), after the same large-\(N\) threshold required for
(4.14) of FINAL_AUDIT.md.

### Where the old epsilon^-4 came from

The older proof bounded the negative term in
\[
\frac12(I^2/L-I)
\]
using the crude upper bound \(I\le LM\), leading to the threshold
\(M=O(\varepsilon^{-2})\).

The monotonicity of \(I^2/L-I\) once \(I>L/2\) lets one substitute the
**lower** bound \(I\ge TM\) instead.  The true threshold for forcing repeated
prime incidences is
\[
M\asymp\frac{L}{T}\asymp\frac1\varepsilon.
\]

This is also combinatorially sharp for second moments: for
\(M<c/\varepsilon\), one can abstractly give each exceptional point a
disjoint set of \(\asymp\varepsilon L\) primes.  Then all pair intersections
are zero.  No argument using only marginal loads and pair intersections can
force a smaller local threshold.

# 5. The subinterval Z_p bound: corrected calculation

The question proposes the following argument.

If each prime has load
\[
d_p\ll Y^{2/3},
\tag{5.1}
\]
then
\[
I=\sum_pd_p
\ll L_NY^{2/3}.
\]
Together with \(I\gg\varepsilon NM/\log N\), this gives
\[
M
\ll
\frac{L_NY^{2/3}\log N}{\varepsilon N}
\ll
\frac{Y^{2/3}}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Algebraically, (5.2) is correct.  But (5.1) needs a qualification.

## 5.1 Why d_p <= C Y^(2/3) is false when p <= Y

As \(n\) varies in an interval \(J\) of length \(Y\), the condition for one
fixed prime is
\[
n\bmod p\in Z_p.
\]
If \(Y>p\), one zero \(r\in Z_p\) can be hit by
\[
r,\ r+p,\ r+2p,\ldots
\]
inside the same \(J\).

Thus \(d_p\) need not count \(d_p\) distinct zero positions.  The
subinterval theorem cannot be applied directly.

## 5.2 Correct split at p=Y

For \(p>Y\), the map \(n\mapsto n\bmod p\) is injective on \(J\).
The residues form either one interval of length \(Y\), or two intervals
created by a single wrap, with total length \(Y\).  By the banked
subinterval theorem and concavity,
\[
d_p\ll Y^{2/3}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Primes \(p\le Y\) contribute at most \(\pi(Y)\) incidences to any one
exceptional integer.

If
\[
Y\le c\varepsilon N
\tag{5.4}
\]
with \(c\) sufficiently small, then for large \(N\)
\[
\pi(Y)\le T/2.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Hence at least \(TM/2\) incidences come from \(p>Y\).

Using (5.3),
\[
\frac{TM}{2}
\ll
L_NY^{2/3},
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
M\ll\varepsilon^{-1}Y^{2/3}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

So the proposed inequality survives after this correction.

## 5.3 Why it does not improve the theorem

At the current optimal pair scale
\[
Y=Y_2\asymp
\varepsilon^2N/\log N,
\]
(5.6) says only
\[
M
\ll
\varepsilon^{1/3}
\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)^{2/3}.
\tag{5.7}
\]
The pair argument already gives
\[
M\ll\varepsilon^{-1}.
\tag{5.8}
\]
Thus the row bound is completely inactive.

If instead one covers a dyadic block by intervals of a longer length \(Y\)
and uses only (5.6), the total number of exceptions is bounded by
\[
\frac{N}{Y}\cdot
\frac{Y^{2/3}}{\varepsilon}
=
\boxed{
\frac{N}{\varepsilon Y^{1/3}}.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]
This improves as \(Y\) grows.  But (5.5) permits at most
\(Y\asymp_\varepsilon N\); even there one gets only
\[
\ll
\varepsilon^{-4/3}N^{2/3},
\tag{5.10}
\]
which is enormously worse than \(\varepsilon^{-3}\log N\).

Therefore the optimal combination is still:

* use pair codegree at \(Y\asymp\varepsilon^2N/\log N\);
* ignore the \(Y^{2/3}\) row cap for the exceptional-set count.

There is no intermediate \(Y\) where the row cap beats the pair proof
asymptotically for fixed \(\varepsilon\).

## 5.4 Combining the cap with Cauchy gives the same weak bound

From \(d_p\le D_{\max}\) one also has
\[
\sum_p\binom{d_p}{2}
\le
\frac{D_{\max}-1}{2}\sum_pd_p.
\]
Combining with the Cauchy lower bound gives roughly
\[
I/L_N\ll D_{\max}.
\]
Since \(I\ge TM\) and \(D_{\max}\ll Y^{2/3}\), this again gives
\[
M\ll \varepsilon^{-1}Y^{2/3}.
\]
So there is no hidden gain from inserting the cap inside the second moment.

# 6. Triple intersections and shifted resultants

This route is mathematically real, but its scale is too short.

The relevant banked result is in
problems/3.2/atom_tail_section.tex.

For gaps \(h_1,h_2\ge1\), define
\[
S_{h_1,h_2}
=
\operatorname{Res}_x
\bigl(N_{h_1}(x),N_{h_2}(x+h_1)\bigr).
\tag{6.1}
\]
The root-strip theorem proves
\[
\boxed{S_{h_1,h_2}\ne0}
\tag{6.2}
\]
for all \(h_1,h_2\), and the height estimate is
\[
\boxed{
\log|S_{h_1,h_2}|
\ll
(h_1+h_2)^2\log(h_1+h_2).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If one prime \(p\) hits three indices
\[
m,\quad m+h_1,\quad m+h_1+h_2,
\]
the per-prime separation theorem says
\[
p\mid S_{h_1,h_2}
\quad\text{or}\quad
p\mid b_j\ \text{for some }j<h_1+h_2.
\tag{6.4}
\]
The second alternative is the wrap correction.

Consequently, if \(H=h_1+h_2\), the number of primes \(p>\sqrt N\)
that can hit one fixed triple is bounded, in the range \(H<\sqrt N\), by
\[
K_3(H)
\ll
\frac{H^2\log H}{\log N}.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Indeed, the resultant contributes (6.3), and
\[
\log\prod_{j<H}b_j\ll H^2
\]
handles the wrap alternative.

## 6.1 Constant triple codegree: exactly the sqrt(log N) scale

To make the right side of (6.5) \(<1\), one needs
\[
H^2\log H\ll\log N.
\]
Thus
\[
H
\ll
\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}}.
\tag{6.6}
\]
This is precisely the quantitative scale already stated by the repository's
Per-prime separation theorem.

So the answer to the question's “only O(sqrt(log N))?” is:

* for **zero/constant triple codegree**, yes, essentially
  \(H\asymp\sqrt{\log N/\log\log N}\);
* for a **third-moment constant-fraction saving**, one may go farther, but
  still nowhere near the second-moment critical scale.

## 6.2 What a third moment actually needs

Suppose an interval of length \(H\) contains \(M\) exceptional integers.
The incidence mass is \(I\ge TM\).

For \(M\gg1/\varepsilon\), convexity gives a third-factorial-moment lower
bound of the scale
\[
\sum_p\binom{d_p}{3}
\gg
\varepsilon^3L_NM^3.
\tag{6.7}
\]
If every triple of exceptional integers has at most \(K_3(H)\) common
primes, then
\[
\sum_p\binom{d_p}{3}
\le
K_3(H)\binom M3.
\tag{6.8}
\]
To contradict (6.7), it is enough that
\[
K_3(H)
\ll
\varepsilon^3L_N
\asymp
\varepsilon^3\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Using (6.5), this requires
\[
H^2\log H
\ll
\varepsilon^3N.
\tag{6.10}
\]
For polynomial-size \(H\), this is roughly
\[
\boxed{
H
\lesssim
\varepsilon^{3/2}
\sqrt{\frac{N}{\log N}}.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

Compare this with the pair critical scale
\[
Y_2
\asymp
\varepsilon^2\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{6.12}
\]
For fixed \(\varepsilon\),
\[
\frac{H}{Y_2}
\ll
\varepsilon^{-1/2}
\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{N}}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Thus the triple resultant is useful on a **strictly shorter** scale than the
existing pair codegree.  Covering a dyadic block by intervals of length
(6.11) would require \(\gg\sqrt{N\log N}\) intervals, catastrophically worse
than the current \(O(\log N)\).

Therefore the third moment gives no improvement to the exceptional-set
exponent.

# 8. Wrap/no-wrap carriers do not hide an extra logarithm

For two indices at distance \(h\), §4.5 uses:

* no wrap:
  \[
  p\mid N_h(m),
  \qquad
  \log|N_h(m)|=O(h\log N);
  \]
* wrap:
  \[
  p\mid\prod_{j=1}^h(m+j),
  \qquad
  \log\prod_{j=1}^h(m+j)=O(h\log N).
  \]

For \(p>\sqrt N\), each eligible prime contributes
\(\gg\log N\) to the logarithm.  Both carriers therefore give only
\[
O(h)
\]
possible primes.

This is sharp at the level of height bookkeeping.  There is no missing
\(\log N\) factor to recover by distinguishing wrap from no-wrap.

For \(h\gg N/\log N\), even \(O(h)\) exceeds the total prime universe
\(L_N\), so the pair theorem becomes trivial.  This is exactly the barrier
identified in (1.5)--(1.6).

# 9. Why the row bound cannot improve the log exponent after the current theorem is known

There is also a simple a posteriori reason.

The current theorem already says that a dyadic block contains only
\(O_\varepsilon(\log N)\) exceptional integers.

A polynomial row cap such as
\[
d_p\ll N^{2/3}
\]
is therefore vacuous on that set: automatically
\[
d_p\le M=O_\varepsilon(\log N)\ll N^{2/3}.
\]

Even the local cap
\[
d_p\ll Y_2^{2/3}
\asymp
\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)^{2/3}
\]
is enormously larger than the entire exceptional population in one
critical interval, which Theorem 4.1 bounds by \(O(1/\varepsilon)\).

So a \(D^{2/3}\) marginal row estimate cannot refine the already-polylogarithmic
exceptional count.  To lower the exponent of \(\log X\), one needs a
statement that is itself at the **polylogarithmic incidence scale**:
far-pair decorrelation, transverse higher intersections with unexpectedly
small content, or a pointwise exclusion theorem.

# 10. A useful sharpness model for the epsilon refinement

The \(1/\varepsilon\) local threshold is not an artifact of the proof.

Let the prime universe have \(L\) abstract elements.  Take
\[
M=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\right\rfloor
\]
abstract exceptional points and give each point a disjoint bad-prime set of
size \(\lfloor\varepsilon L\rfloor\).

Then:

* every point has the required marginal load \(\asymp\varepsilon L\);
* every pair codegree is zero;
* every prime has load \(d_p\le1\), much stronger than any
  \(Y^{2/3}\) cap.

Thus no argument using only marginal load, pair intersections, and row-load
caps can force \(M=o(1/\varepsilon)\).

Likewise, the factor
\[
\varepsilon^{-2}
\]
in the number of critical intervals comes from the intrinsic scale
\[
Y_2\asymp\varepsilon^2N/\log N.
\]
Without improving the pair codegree beyond \(O(h)\), that scale cannot be
enlarged.

This explains why \(\varepsilon^{-3}\) is the natural endpoint of the present
second-moment architecture.

~~~python
from math import log, sqrt, ceil

def audit(N, eps, cT=0.25, CL=4.0, Ccd=1.0, Cz=4.0):
    """
    cT:  T >= cT * eps*N/log N
    CL:  L <= CL * N/log N
    Ccd: pair codegree <= Ccd*h
    Cz:  d_p <= Cz*Y^(2/3) for p>Y
    """
    ln = log(N)
    T = cT * eps * N / ln
    L = CL * N / ln

    # Choose safely inside Ccd*Y <= (1/8)*T^2/L.
    Y2 = T*T / (8.0*Ccd*L)

    # New local threshold.
    M0 = ceil(2.0*L/T)

    def cs_lower(M):
        I0 = T*M
        assert I0 > L/2.0
        return 0.5*(I0*I0/L - I0)

    def pair_upper(M):
        return Ccd*Y2*M*M

    # Every integer M beyond the claimed threshold must contradict
    # the pair upper bound. Test a finite range above the threshold.
    for M in range(M0 + 1, M0 + 1000):
        assert cs_lower(M) > pair_upper(M), (N, eps, M)

    # The corrected row-cap consequence:
    # M <= const * Y^(2/3)/eps.
    row_local = (2.0*CL*Cz/cT) * (Y2**(2.0/3.0)) / eps

    # Covering a full dyadic block with critical intervals.
    pair_block = (N/Y2 + 1.0) * M0

    # Best row-only scale allowed by pi(Y) << T is Y ~ eps*N
    # (constant factors suppressed).
    Yrow = eps*N/100.0
    row_block = (N/Yrow + 1.0) * (
        (2.0*CL*Cz/cT) * (Yrow**(2.0/3.0)) / eps
    )

    # Triple resultant: H^2 log H ~ eps^3 N.
    # A conservative asymptotic proxy.
    H3 = eps**1.5 * sqrt(N/max(ln, 1.0))

    # Zero/constant triple-codegree scale.
    Hzero = sqrt(ln/max(log(ln), 1.0))

    print(f"N={N:g} eps={eps:g}")
    print("T =", T, "L =", L)
    print("critical pair Y2 =", Y2)
    print("new local M0 =", M0)
    print("row cap at Y2 =", row_local)
    print("pair dyadic-block bound proxy =", pair_block)
    print("best row-only block proxy =", row_block)
    print("third-moment span H3 =", H3)
    print("zero-triple span Hzero =", Hzero)
    print("H3/Y2 =", H3/Y2)
    print()

for N in (10**8, 10**12, 10**18):
~~~

# 13. Recommended next target

The best next unconditional target is not another marginal zero bound.

To improve
\[
O_\varepsilon((\log X)^2)
\]
to
\[
O_\varepsilon(\log X),
\]
the shortest new input is:

> **Medium/far master codegree saving.**  
> For each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), uniformly for
> \(m<n\in(N,2N]\) with
> \[
> n-m\ge c\varepsilon^2N/\log N,
> \]
> prove
> \[
> \#\{p>\sqrt N:
> p\mid b_{m\bmod p},\
> p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}
> \le
> \kappa(\varepsilon)\frac{N}{\log N},
> \qquad
> \kappa(\varepsilon)=o(\varepsilon^2).
> \]

Pointwise control for every pair is stronger than necessary; an average
version over the exceptional pairs would suffice.

The triple/shifted-resultant machinery is already too short-range to furnish
this.  The row \(D^{2/3}\) bound is far too coarse once the exceptional set
is polylogarithmic.  Therefore a genuine improvement of the logarithmic
exception exponent still requires a new **cross-characteristic far-correlation
input**, exactly as the earlier W audits suggest.
