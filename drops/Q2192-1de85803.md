# 2. Exact reflection of the gap continuant

Write the continuant as the determinant of the `(h-1)x(h-1)` tridiagonal matrix

\[
K_h(X)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(X+1)&1\\
(X+2)^6&P(X+2)&1\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&& (X+h-1)^6&P(X+h-1)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
N_h(X)=\det K_h(X).
\tag{7}
\]

The cubic has the exact antisymmetry

\[
P(-1-t)=-P(t),
\tag{8}
\]

because

\[
P(t)=(2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5).
\]

Apply `X -> -h-1-X` and reverse the order of rows and columns.  The diagonal entries are the reversed original diagonal entries with a minus sign, while the adjacent products are unchanged because the off-diagonal weight is a sixth power.  Every monomial in the tridiagonal determinant uses `h-1-2k` diagonal entries, so every term acquires the same sign `(-1)^(h-1)`.  Hence

\[
\boxed{N_h(-h-1-X)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(X).}
\tag{9}
\]

For even `h`, the involution has fixed point

\[
X=-\frac{h+1}{2},
\]

and (9) gives

\[
N_h\!\left(-\frac{h+1}{2}\right)=0.
\]

Since `N_h` is integral,

\[
\boxed{2X+h+1\mid N_h(X)\qquad(h\text{ even}).}
\tag{10}
\]

Modulo an odd prime `ell`, if `r` is a zero of `N_h`, then so is

\[
r^*\equiv-h-1-r\equiv\ell-1-r-h\pmod\ell.
\tag{11}
\]

Under the physical inequalities

\[
0\le r<r+h\le\ell-2,
\]

`r^*=r` is exactly

\[
\boxed{2r+h+1=\ell.}
\tag{12}
\]

This is precisely the reflection-mate equation in the question.

The factor `2X+21` itself is nonzero there (`2*20+21=61=20 mod 41`), so the endpoint collision is an additional root of the cofactor, not a tautological consequence of the mate factor.  The numeric checker below asserts

with `D_0=0,D_1=1`.  In particular every `D_h` is an integer.

# 4. The exact same-wing coordinate reduction

Let

\[
\ell=2m+1.
\]

The clean physical same-wing hypotheses are:

### Lower wing

\[
\boxed{
0\le r<r+h\le m-1=\frac{\ell-3}{2}.}
\tag{18L}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
2(r+h)\le\ell-3.
\]

### Upper wing

\[
\boxed{
m+1=\frac{\ell+1}{2}\le r<r+h\le\ell-2.}
\tag{18U}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
2r\ge\ell+1.
\]

The upper case reflects to the lower case, so it is enough to study (18L).

For a lower-wing pair put

\[
i=m-r,
\qquad
j=m-(r+h)=i-h.
\]

Then

\[
1\le j<i\le m,
\qquad
h=i-j.
\tag{19}
\]

The centered Apéry sequence

\[
y_k=b_{m-k}
\]

satisfies the exact recurrence from `scripts/e3_center_param.py`,

\[
(2k+1)^3y_{k+1}
=4k(68k^2+3)y_k-(2k-1)^3y_{k-1}.
\tag{20}
\]

Thus `y_j=y_i=0` implies

\[
\boxed{\ell\mid C(j,i).}
\tag{21}
\]

For fixed gap `h`, it is convenient to use the sum coordinate

\[
S=i+j.
\]

Define

\[
C_h(S):=C\!\left(\frac{S-h}{2},\frac{S+h}{2}\right).
\tag{22}
\]

The center reflection gives

\[
\boxed{C_h(-S)=(-1)^{h-1}C_h(S),}
\tag{23}
\]

and the endpoint is

\[
\boxed{C_h(h)=C(0,h)=D_h.}
\tag{24}
\]

For the physical lower-wing pair,

\[
S=i+j=2m-2r-h,
\qquad
S+h=2i\le2m=\ell-1,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\ell>S+h.}
\tag{25}
\]

Therefore the desired same-wing theorem can be isolated very cleanly:

> **Missing same-wing lemma.**  Let `ell` be an odd prime and let `0<h<S` have the same parity, with `ell>S+h`.  If the distinguished centered Apéry solution has zeros at `(S-h)/2` and `(S+h)/2`, then
> \[
> C_h(h)\not\equiv0\pmod\ell.
> \]

A stronger, solution-free statement would be

\[
\boxed{
C_h(S)\equiv0\pmod\ell,\quad \ell>S+h
\Longrightarrow
C_h(h)\not\equiv0\pmod\ell,}
\tag{26}
\]

but I have **not** proved (26), and it is stronger than needed because `C_h(S)=0` only says that *some* Dirichlet solution transfers from the first endpoint to the second; the actual-zero hypothesis also fixes the distinguished Apéry projective state.

This is the exact place where a resultant or a finite-field Sturm/Green argument would have to enter.

## Why the banked Pluecker/Dodgson identity does not already prove it

The center script verifies exactly

\[
W(i,j)W(i+1,j-1)-W(i,j-1)W(i+1,j)
=-\frac1{(2i+1)^3(2j-1)^3}.
\tag{27}
\]

When the endpoint weights are units modulo `ell`, (27) forbids two **adjacent** Dirichlet minors from vanishing together.  The rank-two Pluecker identity likewise relates four endpoint pairs.

But our two minors are

\[
C(0,h)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
C(j,j+h),
\]

which have equal length and a translated coefficient window.  Neither is adjacent to the other in the Dodgson lattice.  Substituting both zeros into the Pluecker relation leaves a product equality between the two cross minors; it does not force a unit to vanish.  Thus using (27) alone as a “Sturm proof” would be a gap.

The repository's exact symbolic scan also shows that the fixed-gap polynomial `C_h(S)` does not separate into a pure difference/sum product; the tested EDS-style ansatz fails.  So there is no banked translation law that turns `C_h(S)` into a scalar multiple of `C_h(h)`.

This identifies a precise missing lemma rather than making a broad impossibility claim.

# 5. Exact cross-wing decomposition

This is the part that the bare non-mate condition does not remove.

Let `a<b<m` be two lower-wing Apéry zeros.  By reflection, `ell-1-a` and `ell-1-b` are upper-wing zeros.  Besides the same-wing pair `(a,b)`, there is the non-mate cross-wing pair

\[
r=a,
\qquad
r+h=\ell-1-b.
\]

Its gap is

\[
\boxed{h=\ell-1-a-b=2m-a-b.}
\tag{28}
\]

The reflection mate would have used the **same** lower zero on both sides, `a=b`; hence `a<b` makes (28) genuinely non-mate.

There are two subranges:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 a+b\le m-1 & h\ge m+1 \\ 
 a+b\ge m   & h\le m.
\end{array}
\tag{29}
\]

The first (“long cross-wing”) range has an exact singular-site factorization.

# 6. Long cross-wing factorization theorem

Recall

\[
B_n=(n!)^3b_n=N_{n+1}(-1).
\tag{30}
\]

## Theorem

For `ell=2m+1` prime and every

\[
m+1\le h\le\ell-2,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
N_h(m)\equiv(-1)^m B_mB_{h-m-1}\pmod\ell.}
\tag{31}
\]

### Proof for `h=m+1`

The reflection identity (9) gives

\[
N_{m+1}(m)
=(-1)^mN_{m+1}(-2m-2).
\]

Modulo `ell=2m+1`, `-2m-2=-1`, so

\[
N_{m+1}(m)\equiv(-1)^mN_{m+1}(-1)=(-1)^mB_m.
\]

This is (31) because `B_0=1`.

### Proof for `h>=m+2`

Use the determinant (7) at `X=m`.  Its offsets are `k=1,...,h-1`.  At

\[
k=m+1
\]

we hit

\[
X+k=m+(m+1)=2m+1=\ell.
\]

The subdiagonal entry at that cut is

\[
(X+k)^6=\ell^6\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

Therefore the tridiagonal matrix becomes block upper triangular modulo `ell`.

The left block has determinant

\[
N_{m+1}(m)\equiv(-1)^mB_m.
\]

For the right block, reduce its indices modulo `ell`: its diagonal sequence is

\[
P(\ell),P(\ell+1),\ldots
\equiv
P(0),P(1),\ldots,
\]

and its subdiagonal sixth powers are

\[
1^6,2^6,\ldots.
\]

This is exactly the determinant `N_{h-m}(-1)=B_{h-m-1}`.  Multiplying the two block determinants proves (31).  QED.

Because `2` is a unit, (17) and (31) give

\[
\boxed{
\ell\mid D_h
\iff
B_mB_{h-m-1}=0\pmod\ell.}
\tag{32}
\]

All factorials through `m` are units modulo `ell`, so this is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\ell\mid D_h
\iff
b_m=0\pmod\ell
\quad\text{or}\quad
b_{h-m-1}=0\pmod\ell.}
\tag{33}
\]

## Apply it to a non-mate cross-wing actual pair

For (28), if `a+b<=m-1`, put

\[
c=h-m-1=m-1-a-b\ge0.
\tag{34}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\ell\mid D_h
\iff
b_m=0\pmod\ell
\quad\text{or}\quad
b_c=0\pmod\ell.}
\tag{35}
\]

Thus if the center is nonzero, an endpoint collision forces three lower-wing zero indices satisfying

\[
\boxed{a+b+c=m-1.}
\tag{36}
\]

This is the strongest exact structural reduction I found for the cross-wing branch.

It also explains why “non-mate” is not the same as “same-wing”: `a<b` excludes the reflection mate but leaves (35) as a possible mechanism.  To prove the full non-mate theorem one still needs to rule out the central-zero alternative and the additive triple (36), and one must separately treat the short cross-wing range `a+b>=m`.

No such all-prime theorem is present in the inspected repository state.

# 8. Precise theorem status

It is useful to label the statements so a later proof does not accidentally use a scan as a lemma.

### Proved here

**R (reflection).**
\[
N_h(-h-1-X)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(X).
\]

**E (endpoint recurrence).**
\[
D_h=2^{3(h-1)}N_h(-1/2)=C(0,h)
\]
with recurrence (13).

**M (mate geometry).**
A physical bridge root is fixed by reflection iff
\[
2r+h+1=\ell.
\]

**X-long (long cross-wing factorization).**
For `h>=m+1`, equation (31) holds; for a lower-zero pair `a<b` crossing to `ell-1-b`, endpoint collision is exactly (35).

### Finite computation, not a theorem

**F50k.**  The reported `653` canonical same-wing pairs below `50000` have no endpoint collision.  The checker below is designed to verify this exact claim.

### Still open in this attack

**SW.**  Under the same-wing inequalities (18L)/(18U), prove `ell not| D_h` for an actual zero pair.

**X-short.**  Treat non-mate cross-wing pairs with `a+b>=m` (equivalently `h<=m`).

**X-add.**  Rule out the central/additive obstruction (35)-(36) for all actual Apéry zero sets.

A proof of `SW + X-short + X-add` would prove the full non-mate statement.  The current Pluecker/Dodgson identities do not by themselves supply any of those three assertions.

This is a narrow statement of the gap, not an impossibility claim.

# 9. Exact scanner: actual zero pairs, first counterexample, `653` check, and long-cross factorization

The following is standalone Python 3 and uses only the standard library.  Every modular division is by a number proved to be in `1,...,ell-1`.  It computes the **actual** Apéry row, checks reflection directly, checks the `ell=41` mate collision, scans every physical zero pair, and reports the first non-mate endpoint collision in increasing prime order.

It also counts **canonical lower-wing pairs** `0<=a<b<m`.  Reflection gives one upper-wing copy of each, so this is the natural count to compare with the reported `653`.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
from itertools import combinations
from math import isqrt


def P(n: int) -> int:
    return 34*n**3 + 51*n**2 + 27*n + 5


def primes_upto(limit: int):
    sieve = bytearray(b"\x01") * (limit + 1)
    if limit >= 0:
        sieve[0] = 0
    if limit >= 1:
        sieve[1] = 0
    for p in range(2, isqrt(limit) + 1):
        if sieve[p]:
            start = p*p
            sieve[start:limit+1:p] = b"\x00" * (((limit-start)//p)+1)
    return [p for p in range(2, limit + 1) if sieve[p]]


def apery_row_mod_p(p: int):
    """b_0,...,b_{p-1} mod p, by the exact Apéry recurrence."""
    assert p >= 5 and p % 2 == 1
    b = [0] * p
    b[0] = 1
    b[1] = 5 % p
    for n in range(1, p - 1):
        # (n+1)^3 b_{n+1} = P(n)b_n - n^3 b_{n-1}
        den = pow(n + 1, 3, p)
        assert den != 0                  # n+1 <= p-1
        num = (P(n)*b[n] - n**3*b[n-1]) % p
        b[n+1] = num * pow(den, -1, p) % p
    return b


def N_mod(p: int, h: int, x: int) -> int:
    """N_h(x) mod p from N_0=0,N_1=1."""
    assert h >= 0
    if h == 0:
        return 0
    prev, cur = 0, 1
    for k in range(1, h):
        t = (x + k) % p
        prev, cur = cur, (P(t)*cur - pow(t, 6, p)*prev) % p
    return cur


def endpoint_table(p: int, max_h: int):
    """D_h=2^(3(h-1))*N_h(-1/2) mod p for 0<=h<=max_h."""
    D = [0] * (max_h + 1)
    if max_h >= 1:
        D[1] = 1
    for h in range(1, max_h):
        D[h+1] = (
            4*h*(68*h*h + 3)*D[h]
            - pow(2*h - 1, 6, p)*D[h-1]
        ) % p
    return D


def factorial_table_mod_p(p: int, n: int):
    f = [1] * (n + 1)
    for k in range(1, n + 1):
        f[k] = f[k-1] * k % p
    return f


def audit_41():
    p = 41
    m = 20
    r = 10
    h = 20
    b = apery_row_mod_p(p)
    D = endpoint_table(p, h)
    assert b[10] == 0
    assert b[30] == 0
    assert N_mod(p, h, r) == 0
    assert 2*r + h + 1 == p
    assert D[h] == 0
    # D_h really is the scaled half-integer evaluation.
    lhs = D[h]
    rhs = pow(2, 3*(h-1), p) * N_mod(p, h, m) % p
    assert lhs == rhs == 0
    print("PASS p=41 mate endpoint collision")


def audit_long_cross_factorization(p: int, b, D, low):
    """Check (31) on every long cross gap arising from lower zero pairs."""
    m = (p - 1)//2
    if len(low) < 2:
        return 0
    fact = factorial_table_mod_p(p, m)
    Bm = pow(fact[m], 3, p) * b[m] % p
    checks = 0

    for a, bb in combinations(low, 2):
        if a + bb > m - 1:
            continue
        h = 2*m - a - bb
        c = h - m - 1
        assert c == m - 1 - a - bb
        assert 0 <= c < m
        assert h >= m + 1

        # Recover N_h(m) from the scaled endpoint D_h.
        scale = pow(2, 3*(h-1), p)
        Nh_m = D[h] * pow(scale, -1, p) % p
        Bc = pow(fact[c], 3, p) * b[c] % p
        rhs = Bm * Bc % p
        if m & 1:
            rhs = (-rhs) % p
        assert Nh_m == rhs, (p, a, bb, h, c, Nh_m, rhs)

        # Since factorials are units, endpoint zero iff center or c is zero.
        assert (D[h] == 0) == (b[m] == 0 or b[c] == 0)
        checks += 1
    return checks


def classify_same_wing(r: int, s: int, m: int) -> bool:
    return (s < m) or (r > m)


def scan(limit: int, expect_same_wing_pairs: int | None = None):
    same_canonical = 0
    same_all = 0
    nonmate_all = 0
    long_cross_checks = 0
    endpoint_collisions = []
    first_nonmate = None

    for p in primes_upto(limit):
        if p < 5:
            continue
        m = (p - 1)//2
        b = apery_row_mod_p(p)

        # Exact Apéry reflection audit, not assumed by the pair scan.
        assert all(b[n] == b[p-1-n] for n in range(p)), p

        # Physical indices are 0,...,p-2.  b_{p-1}=b_0=1 anyway.
        zeros = [n for n in range(p - 1) if b[n] == 0]
        low = [n for n in zeros if n < m]

        same_canonical += sum(1 for _ in combinations(low, 2))

        pairs = []
        gaps = set()
        for r, s in combinations(zeros, 2):
            h = s - r
            assert 0 <= r < s <= p - 2
            mate = (r + s + 1 == p)       # 2r+h+1=p
            same = classify_same_wing(r, s, m)
            if same:
                same_all += 1
            if not mate:
                nonmate_all += 1
                gaps.add(h)
                pairs.append((r, s, h, same))

        # Long-cross theorem checks need cross gaps even when we do not
        # encounter an endpoint collision.
        for a, bb in combinations(low, 2):
            if a + bb <= m - 1:
                gaps.add(2*m - a - bb)

        if not gaps:
            continue
        D = endpoint_table(p, max(gaps))

        long_cross_checks += audit_long_cross_factorization(p, b, D, low)

        for r, s, h, same in pairs:
            if D[h] == 0:
                rec = {
                    "p": p,
                    "r": r,
                    "s": s,
                    "h": h,
                    "same_wing": same,
                    "center": m,
                    "zeros": zeros,
                }
                endpoint_collisions.append(rec)
                if first_nonmate is None:
                    first_nonmate = rec

    if expect_same_wing_pairs is not None:
        assert same_canonical == expect_same_wing_pairs, (
            same_canonical,
            expect_same_wing_pairs,
        )

    print(f"limit={limit}")
    print(f"canonical lower same-wing pairs = {same_canonical}")
    print(f"all reflected same-wing pairs   = {same_all}")
    print(f"all physical non-mate pairs     = {nonmate_all}")
    print(f"long-cross factor checks        = {long_cross_checks}")
    print(f"non-mate endpoint collisions    = {len(endpoint_collisions)}")
    print(f"first non-mate collision        = {first_nonmate}")

    if endpoint_collisions:
        print("COLLISIONS:")
        for rec in endpoint_collisions:
            print(rec)

    return first_nonmate


def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument("--limit", type=int, default=50_000)
    ap.add_argument("--expect-same-wing-pairs", type=int, default=None)
    args = ap.parse_args()

    audit_41()
    scan(args.limit, args.expect_same_wing_pairs)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

# 10. Exact symbolic verifier for all identities used above

This second script uses SymPy only for **exact** polynomial/rational arithmetic.  It verifies:

- `P(-1-X)=-P(X)`;
- the gap recurrence;
- the determinant representation (for the requested small symbolic range);
- `N_h(-h-1-X)=(-1)^(h-1)N_h(X)`;
- the forced factor `2X+h+1` for even `h`;
- the scaled endpoint recurrence;
- `D_h=C_h(h)`;
- `C_h(-S)=(-1)^(h-1)C_h(S)`;
- the center-basis Casoratian;
- the exact Dodgson identity;
- the rank-two Pluecker identity.

The mathematical derivations above are uniform in `h`; `--max-h` is only how far the regression checker expands the exact polynomials.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
from fractions import Fraction

from sympy import Matrix, Poly, QQ, Rational, expand, symbols

X, S = symbols("X S")


def P(t):
    return 34*t**3 + 51*t**2 + 27*t + 5


def N_poly(h: int):
    if h == 0:
        return 0
    Nm1 = 0
    N = 1
    for k in range(1, h):
        Nm1, N = N, expand(P(X+k)*N - (X+k)**6*Nm1)
    return expand(N)


def N_det(h: int):
    if h == 0:
        return 0
    if h == 1:
        return 1
    n = h - 1
    M = [[0 for _ in range(n)] for _ in range(n)]
    for row in range(n):
        k = row + 1
        M[row][row] = P(X+k)
        if row + 1 < n:
            M[row][row+1] = 1
        if row > 0:
            M[row][row-1] = (X+k)**6
    return expand(Matrix(M).det())


def q(k):
    return 4*k*(68*k*k + 3)


def pw(k):
    return (2*k - 1)**3


def C_h(h: int):
    """C_h(S)=C((S-h)/2,(S+h)/2)."""
    if h == 0:
        return 0
    left = (S - h) / 2
    prev2, prev1 = 0, 1
    for off in range(1, h):
        k = left + off
        prev2, prev1 = prev1, expand(q(k)*prev1 - pw(k)**2*prev2)
    return expand(prev1)


def endpoint_integer(h: int):
    if h == 0:
        return 0
    value = expand(N_poly(h).subs(X, Rational(-1, 2)))
    value *= 2**(3*(h-1))
    value = expand(value)
    assert value.is_Integer
    return int(value)


def center_basis(max_i: int):
    A = [Fraction(1), Fraction(0)]
    B = [Fraction(0), Fraction(1)]
    if max_i == 0:
        return A[:1], B[:1]
    for i in range(1, max_i):
        den = (2*i + 1)**3
        mid = 4*i*(68*i*i + 3)
        back = (2*i - 1)**3
        A.append((mid*A[i] - back*A[i-1]) / den)
        B.append((mid*B[i] - back*B[i-1]) / den)
    return A, B


def W(A, B, i, j):
    return A[i]*B[j] - A[j]*B[i]


def symbolic_audit(max_h: int):
    assert expand(P(-1-X) + P(X)) == 0
    assert expand(P(X) - (2*X+1)*(17*X**2+17*X+5)) == 0

    D = [0] * (max_h + 2)
    D[0] = 0
    D[1] = 1

    for h in range(1, max_h + 1):
        Nh = N_poly(h)

        # Exact reflection polynomial identity.
        reflected = expand(Nh.subs(X, -h-1-X))
        assert expand(reflected - (-1)**(h-1)*Nh) == 0

        # Exact determinant identity.  Large symbolic determinants are slow;
        # the recurrence proof above is uniform, so six is ample regression.
        if h <= min(max_h, 6):
            assert expand(N_det(h) - Nh) == 0

        if h % 2 == 0:
            poly = Poly(Nh, X, domain=QQ)
            divisor = Poly(2*X+h+1, X, domain=QQ)
            quo, rem = divmod(poly, divisor)
            assert rem.is_zero
            assert quo * divisor == poly

        D[h] = endpoint_integer(h)
        Ch = Poly(C_h(h), S, domain=QQ)

        # Center reflection/parity.
        parity = expand(Ch.as_expr().subs(S, -S) - (-1)**(h-1)*Ch.as_expr())
        assert parity == 0

        # Endpoint C_h(h)=D_h.
        assert expand(Ch.as_expr().subs(S, h) - D[h]) == 0

    # Scaled endpoint recurrence.
    for h in range(1, max_h):
        expected = 4*h*(68*h*h+3)*D[h] - (2*h-1)**6*D[h-1]
        assert D[h+1] == expected

    # Center basis, Dodgson, and Pluecker.
    max_i = max(8, max_h + 2)
    A, B = center_basis(max_i)

    for i in range(0, max_i-2):
        for j in range(i+2, max_i):
            lhs = W(A,B,i,j)*W(A,B,i+1,j-1) \
                - W(A,B,i,j-1)*W(A,B,i+1,j)
            rhs = Fraction(-1, (2*i+1)**3 * (2*j-1)**3)
            assert lhs == rhs, (i, j, lhs, rhs)

    for a in range(0, max_i-3):
        for b in range(a+1, max_i-2):
            for c in range(b+1, max_i-1):
                for d in range(c+1, max_i):
                    pl = (
                        W(A,B,a,b)*W(A,B,c,d)
                        - W(A,B,a,c)*W(A,B,b,d)
                        + W(A,B,a,d)*W(A,B,b,c)
                    )
                    assert pl == 0, (a,b,c,d,pl)

    print(f"PASS exact symbolic audit for h<= {max_h}")


def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument("--max-h", type=int, default=8)
    args = ap.parse_args()
    symbolic_audit(args.max_h)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

# 11. A compact exact resultant/gcd target for the next attack

The same-wing reduction suggests a very specific next lemma rather than a generic “finite-field Sturm” slogan.

For fixed `h` and shift `j>=1`, put

\[
S=2j+h.
\]

A same-wing endpoint collision forces

\[
\ell\mid C_h(h),
\qquad
\ell\mid C_h(2j+h),
\qquad
\ell>2j+2h.
\tag{37}
\]

Therefore every such prime divides the explicit integer

\[
\boxed{
G_{h,j}:=\gcd\bigl(C_h(h),C_h(2j+h)\bigr).}
\tag{38}
\]

A solution-free theorem stronger than needed would follow from

\[
\boxed{
q\mid G_{h,j}\Longrightarrow q\le2j+2h
\quad\text{for every prime }q.}
\tag{39}
\]

I have **not** proved (39); it should be tested before being adopted as the next conjecture.  It is nevertheless a useful exact target because it removes `ell` from the polynomial recurrence entirely.  If (39) is false, the offending `(h,j,q)` are abstract equal-length endpoint collisions; one can then impose the distinguished Apéry zero condition and see which survive.

An exact Sage/SymPy diagnostic is only a few lines once `C_h` is defined:

```python
from math import gcd

Dh = int(C_h(h).subs(S, h))
shifted = int(C_h(h).subs(S, 2*j+h))
G = gcd(abs(Dh), abs(shifted))
# factor G exactly with Sage's factor(G), then inspect prime q > 2*j+2*h.
```

This is the clean resultant/Smith direction I would pursue next: first determine whether the stronger solution-free bound (39) is true; if it fails, add the actual centered projective condition

\[
A_j b_m+B_j b_{m-1}=0
\tag{40}
\]

from `scripts/e3_center_param.py` and eliminate the center slope together with the two continuant conditions.  That is an exact three-time/resultant formulation of the distinguished problem.

# 12. Bottom line for P3.2

The strongest statement justified by the current attack is not yet the desired universal endpoint theorem, but it materially narrows it:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{mate branch: exactly understood by reflection;}\\[2mm]
\text{long cross-wing non-mate branch: endpoint collision}\
\qquad\iff\text{central zero or additive third zero }a+b+c=m-1;\\[2mm]
\text{same-wing branch: reduces to the explicit shifted-minor problem (37);}\
\text{short cross-wing branch: still requires a separate argument.}
\end{array}}
\]

Thus the exact stronger **physical inequalities** that isolate the empirical same-wing phenomenon are (18L)/(18U), not merely `2r+h+1!=ell`.  But I am not claiming those inequalities alone are already proved sufficient: the missing same-wing lemma is (SW)/(37), and the current finite `653` scan is evidence for it, not its proof.

Conversely, I cannot honestly give a “smallest non-mate counterexample” because none is established in the connector-visible state.  The supplied exact scanner is arranged to produce that smallest counterexample immediately if one exists below a chosen search limit, while also certifying the reported `653` count and the exact long-cross factorization on every encountered case.
