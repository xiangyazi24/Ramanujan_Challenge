# 3. The two midpoint laws are inequivalent

Put

\[
 m_p=\frac{p-1}{2},
 \qquad
 H(p)=1_{\{p\mid b_{m_p}\}}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

For a sign \(\sigma\in\{+,-\}\), let \(A_\sigma(x,p)\) be the literal actual selected leaf indicator. Define the positional-rigidity indicator

\[
 R_\sigma(x,p)
 =1_{\{r_\sigma(x,p)=m_p\ \text{or}\ a_\sigma(x,p)=m_p\}}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

On an actual leaf,

\[
 R_\sigma(x,p)\le H(p),
 \tag{3.3}
\]

because a required digit equal to the midpoint is already a required zero. The reverse implication is not formal, not banked, and not a consequence of allowing extra zero orbits.

The literal law in the dispatch is

\[
 A_\sigma(x,p)=1\Longrightarrow H(p)=1.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The law that forces the affine prime formulas is

\[
 A_\sigma(x,p)=1\Longrightarrow R_\sigma(x,p)=1.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

The difference is decisive: `(3.4)` is a congruence of an Apéry coefficient, whereas `(3.5)` is an equality of integer labels. A proof of \(p\mid b_{m_p}\) supplies no equation equating \(m_p\) to either \(r_\sigma\) or \(a_\sigma\).

## 3.1 Exact rigid candidates

The positional law gives at most one numerical prime in each selected state/sign fibre.

### Plus

If \(r_+=m_p\), then

\[
 p=\frac{12q+2t+1}{13}.
 \tag{3.6+}
\]

But this is strictly larger than the raw upper endpoint \((12q+t)/13\), so it is impossible. If \(a_+=m_p\), then

\[
 \boxed{p=p_+(q,t):=\frac{24q+2t+1}{27}.}
 \tag{3.7+}
\]

Thus a positional plus leaf, if it exists at all, has this one value.

### Minus

If \(a_-=m_p\), then

\[
 p=\frac{2q-2t-1}{3}.
 \tag{3.6-}
\]

But

\[
 \frac{2q-2t-1}{3}<\frac{6q+t}{7},
 \]

since the difference after clearing denominators is \(4q+17t+7>0\). Hence this branch violates the raw lower endpoint. If \(r_-=m_p\), then

\[
 \boxed{p=p_-(q,t):=\frac{12q+2t+1}{13}.}
 \tag{3.7-}
\]

Again there is at most one numerical prime.

Therefore `(RPOS)` implies selected lower-fibre uniqueness exactly. Literal `(MID0)` does not yield any of `(3.6+)`--`(3.7-)`.

 \tag{7.5}
\]

Indeed, if \(R=0\), then \(K=N\) and \(K(K-1)\le B_\Lambda N\). If \(R=1\), then \(K=N+1\) and

\[
 K(K-1)=N(N+1)=NK\le B_\Lambda N.
\]

Define the literal shorter-scale ordered pair count

\[
 D_\sigma(\Lambda)
 =\frac1\Lambda
   \sum_{\tau=0}^{\Lambda-1}\sum_{x,j}
   K_{x,j}^{\tau}(K_{x,j}^{\tau}-1)
 \tag{7.6}
\]

and the actual non-positional mass

\[
 M^{\mathrm{nr}}_\sigma(T)
 =\sum_{x,p}A^{\mathrm{nr}}_\sigma(x,p).
 \tag{7.7}
\]

Each actual incidence belongs to exactly one cell for each translate. Summing `(7.5)` and dividing by \(\Lambda\) gives the unconditional theorem

\[
 \boxed{
 D_\sigma(\Lambda)
 \le B_\Lambda(T)M^{\mathrm{nr}}_\sigma(T)
 \ll_J\frac{\Lambda}{\log T}M^{\mathrm{nr}}_\sigma(T).
 }
 \tag{7.8}
\]

No lower bound on \(M_\sigma(T)\) is used. If the actual mass is zero, every quantity is zero.

Now use

\[
 \Lambda=Y^{5/6},
 \qquad
 H=Y^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \frac H\Lambda=Y^{-1/3}=T^{-\beta/3}.
 \tag{7.9}
\]

The critical pair gate follows from

\[
 \boxed{
 M^{\mathrm{nr}}_\sigma(T)
 \ll_J M_\sigma(T)T^{-\beta/3}\log T.
 }
 \tag{7.10}
\]

More generally `(NRSP_eta)` gives

\[
 D_\sigma(\Lambda)
 \ll_J M_\sigma(T)H T^{-\eta}.
 \tag{7.11}
\]

This is a genuine non-collapsing sufficient premise. The reverse implication fails: every selected state could contain exactly one non-positional leaf, giving \(D_\sigma(\Lambda)=0\) identically while

\[
 M^{\mathrm{nr}}_\sigma(T)=M_\sigma(T).
\]

Thus `(NRSP_eta)` is not the pair target rewritten in first-moment language.

## 7.1 What literal `(MID0)` buys in this ledger

By `(3.10)` and `(7.8)`,

\[
 \boxed{
 D_\sigma(\Lambda)
 \ll_J\frac{\Lambda}{\log T}
 \left(
   M^{\mathrm{ord}}_\sigma(T)
  +M^{\mathrm{free\text{-}mid}}_\sigma(T)
 \right).
 }
 \tag{7.12}
\]

A proof of literal `(MID0)` would set the first term to zero, leaving

\[
 D_\sigma(\Lambda)
 \ll_J\frac{\Lambda}{\log T}
 M^{\mathrm{free\text{-}mid}}_\sigma(T).
 \tag{7.13}
\]

No source theorem bounds this residual by \(M_\sigma T^{-\beta/3}\log T\), or by any fixed-power saving. This is the exact first new estimate still required if the campaign insists on attacking the coefficient law first.

 \mathcal C_{-,\mathrm{Palm}}^{2Z}(\Lambda)

# 8. What a successful theorem must add

There are three logically distinct targets.

## 8.1 Literal coefficient midpoint

\[
 \mathrm{Q6\text{-}MID0}:\qquad
 A_\sigma(x,p)=1\Longrightarrow p\mid a_p(f).
 \tag{8.1}
\]

This is the exact selected support inclusion `(4.7)`. It needs a mixed correlation between the horizontal prime support of \(G_\sigma(q,t)\) and defining-characteristic nonordinarity of \(f\). Nothing banked supplies it.

## 8.2 Positional required midpoint

\[
 \mathrm{Q6\text{-}RPOS}:\qquad
 A_\sigma(x,p)=1\Longrightarrow
 r_\sigma=m_p\ \text{or}\ a_\sigma=m_p.
 \tag{8.2}
\]

This is the law that gives exact lower-fibre uniqueness. It is strictly weaker than a global bound \(Z(p)\le3\), because arbitrary unrelated zeros are permitted, but it is strictly stronger than `(8.1)`.

## 8.3 Quantitative non-positional sparsity

The weakest clean fixed-power substitute isolated here is `(NRSP_eta)`. It allows isolated non-positional leaves, allows exceptional selected states, allows arbitrary extra zero orbits, and asks only that their **actual selected marked mass** be small enough for the pair consumer.

If `(MID0)` is proved first, the still-smaller named residual is

\[
 \boxed{
 M^{\mathrm{free\text{-}mid}}_\sigma(T)
 \ll_JM_\sigma(T)T^{-\beta/3-\eta}\log T.
 }
 \tag{8.3}
\]

This is the first genuinely new estimate which would repair the literal midpoint route without reverting to \(Z(p)\le3\).

A support-blind stronger variant would bound, under actual-anchor weighting, all admissible prime divisors of \(G_\sigma(q,t)\) outside the singleton candidate `(3.7+)` or `(3.7-)`. That would connect directly to Q4527's cofactor covariance, but no such estimate is proved.

\begin{lemma}[The two q6 midpoint laws]
\label{lem:q6-two-midpoint-laws}
Let $x=(q,t,\ldots)$ be a complete selected q6 state, let $p<q$ be
an odd lower prime, put $d=q-p$ and $m_p=(p-1)/2$, and retain the
complete raw sign windows and all actual masks.  On the plus sign put
\[
 r_+=6d+t,\qquad a_+=13d+t-q,
\]
and on the minus sign put
\[
 r_-=6d+t,\qquad a_-=d-t-1.
\]
Then $r_\sigma-a_\sigma$ is odd.  If one of the two required digits is
$m_p$, then the only raw-window-compatible possibilities are
\[
 \sigma=+:\quad p=\frac{24q+2t+1}{27},
 \qquad
 \sigma=-:\quad p=\frac{12q+2t+1}{13}.
\]
Consequently, for every fixed selected state and sign, at most one
actual lower prime has a required digit equal to $m_p$.

The coefficient condition $p\mid b_{m_p}$ alone does not imply either
of the displayed index equalities.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $p,q$ are odd, $d$ is even.  The identities
\[
 r_+-a_+=q-7d,
 \qquad
 r_--a_-=5d+2t+1
\]
prove the parity assertion.  Solving $r_+=m_p$ gives
$p=(12q+2t+1)/13$, which is larger than the plus upper endpoint
$(12q+t)/13$.  Solving $a_+=m_p$ gives
$p=(24q+2t+1)/27$.  Solving $a_-=m_p$ gives
$p=(2q-2t-1)/3$, which is smaller than the minus lower endpoint
$(6q+t)/7$, while solving $r_-=m_p$ gives
$p=(12q+2t+1)/13$.  The final sentence records the distinction between
a coefficient congruence and an equality of integer labels.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Non-positional q6 mass controls the pair moment]
\label{prop:q6-nonpositional-mass}
Let $A_\sigma(x,p)$ be the literal actual q6 leaf indicator and put
\[
 R_\sigma(x,p)=
 1_{\{r_\sigma(x,p)=m_p\ \mathrm{or}\ a_\sigma(x,p)=m_p\}},
 \qquad
 A_\sigma^{\rm nr}(x,p)=A_\sigma(x,p)(1-R_\sigma(x,p)).
\]
Let
\[
 M_\sigma^{\rm nr}(T)=\sum_{x,p}A_\sigma^{\rm nr}(x,p).
\]
For translated cells $I_j^\tau$ of length $\Lambda$, let
$D_\sigma(\Lambda)$ be the ordered actual second factorial moment and
let
\[
 B_\Lambda(T)=
 \sup_{\tau,j}\#(I_j^\tau\cap\mathbb P\cap\text{lower shell}).
\]
Then
\[
 D_\sigma(\Lambda)
 \le B_\Lambda(T)M_\sigma^{\rm nr}(T)
 \ll_J \frac{\Lambda}{\log T}M_\sigma^{\rm nr}(T).
\]
In particular, if
\[
 M_\sigma^{\rm nr}(T)
 \ll_JM_\sigma(T)T^{-\beta/3-\eta}\log T,
\]
then, for $\Lambda=Y^{5/6}$ and $H=Y^{1/2}$,
\[
 D_\sigma(\Lambda)\ll_JM_\sigma(T)H T^{-\eta}.
\]
No lower bound on $M_\sigma(T)$ is required.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a state, translate and cell.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:q6-two-midpoint-laws}, the cell contains at most one
positional leaf.  Write its total actual occupancy as $K=N+R$, where
$N$ is the non-positional occupancy and $R\in\{0,1\}$.  Since
$K\le B_\Lambda(T)$,
\[
 K(K-1)\le B_\Lambda(T)N:
\]
for $R=0$ this is $N(N-1)\le B_\Lambda N$, and for $R=1$ it is
$N(N+1)=NK\le B_\Lambda N$.  Sum over states and cells and average over
translates.  Each non-positional incidence occurs once for every
translate, so the factor $1/\Lambda$ leaves exactly
$M_\sigma^{\rm nr}(T)$.  Brun--Titchmarsh gives the displayed prime cap.
Finally, $\Lambda T^{-\beta/3}=Y^{5/6}Y^{-1/3}=H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Residual left by a midpoint-coefficient theorem]
\label{cor:q6-midpoint-coefficient-residual}
Put
\[
 H(p)=1_{\{p\mid b_{(p-1)/2}\}}.
\]
On actual leaves $R_\sigma(x,p)\le H(p)$, and hence
\[
 M_\sigma^{\rm nr}(T)
 =\sum_{x,p}A_\sigma(x,p)(1-H(p))
 +\sum_{x,p}A_\sigma(x,p)H(p)(1-R_\sigma(x,p)).
\]
Thus the theorem
\[
 A_\sigma(x,p)=1\Longrightarrow p\mid b_{(p-1)/2}
\]
removes only the first term.  To reach the critical q6 pair gate one
still needs
\[
 \sum_{x,p}A_\sigma(x,p)H(p)(1-R_\sigma(x,p))
 \ll_JM_\sigma(T)T^{-\beta/3}\log T,
\]
or a stronger positional theorem.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Local cross-characteristic unit erasure]
\label{prop:q6-local-unit-erasure}
For every legal raw q6 lower prime, $d=q-p$ is a unit in both
$\mathbb F_p$ and $\mathbb F_q$.  In quotient-witness form the upper
mark $b_t=qU$ becomes the graph equation $U=d^{-1}b_t$ after reduction
modulo $p$, while the lower marks $b_r=pV$, $b_a=pW$ become the graph
equations $V=-d^{-1}b_r$, $W=-d^{-1}b_a$ after reduction modulo $q$.
Consequently direct functorial elimination from the integral recurrence,
Lucas, reflection, divided rows and legal-window localizations creates no
cross-characteristic equation forcing $p\mid b_{(p-1)/2}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The raw lower bound gives $p>6q/7$, hence
$0<d=q-p<q/7<p/6$.  Thus $d$ is nonzero in both residue fields.  The
stated quotient equations follow from $q\equiv d\pmod p$ and
$p\equiv-d\pmod q$.  Each has a unit pivot, so eliminating its quotient
variable imposes no further equation in the opposite-characteristic
state.
\end{proof}
