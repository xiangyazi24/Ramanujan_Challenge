# Level of distribution for Apéry zeros, and whether a Maynard sieve can prove \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

## Verdict

There are two important corrections and one decisive obstruction.

1. With the notation in the question,
   \[
   M=X^2,\qquad Q=X,
   \]
   one has
   \[
   Q=M^{1/2},
   \]
   **exactly**, not beyond the square-root level.

2. More importantly, the estimate
   \[
   \sum_{p\le Q}\left|\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)\right|
   \le \sum_{p\le Q} Z(p)
   \]
   is not a Bombieri--Vinogradov phenomenon. It is only the boundary error obtained by completing periods of an exactly periodic function. It holds for **every** family of residue sets \(Z_p\), including adversarial families with catastrophic higher correlations.

3. Consequently this first-order ``level of distribution'' gives no route to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). The current repository contains an explicit anchored-star family with:

   - \(|Z_p|\le 2\);
   - reflection symmetry;
   - no consecutive residues;
   - optimal single-prime interval discrepancy;
   - the proved \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) bound;
   - even uniformly bounded two-row codegrees;

   but for which the third factorial moment is too large by a factor of order \(X\).

There is therefore no purely Maynard/GPY-style bootstrap from the proposed one-dimensional distribution estimate and \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

A Maynard-like weighted formulation can certainly be written down. However, its required analytic estimate is exactly the **pair--Palm positive-overload estimate** already proved in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` to be equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). The optimization does not remove the missing theorem; it merely gives a dual formulation of it.

The honest conclusion is:

> A Maynard-style architecture could be useful only after one proves a new Apéry-specific distribution theorem on the sparse CRT loci cut out by two already-hit primes. Existing BFI/Maynard large-modulus technology does not supply that theorem.

## 1. What the one-prime estimate actually says

Let

\[
f_p(m)=\mathbf 1_{\{m\bmod p\in Z_p\}},
\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{Z(p)}p,
\qquad
g_p(m)=f_p(m)-\rho_p.
\]

Write

\[
M=kp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

Each residue in \(Z_p\) occurs either \(k\) or \(k+1\) times in
\(0\le m<M\). Hence

\[
\sum_{m<M}f_p(m)=kZ(p)+e_p(M),
\qquad 0\le e_p(M)\le Z(p),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\left|\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)\right|
\le Z(p).
}
\tag{1}
\]

Nothing about the arithmetic location of the elements of \(Z_p\) was used. This is simply exact periodicity plus one incomplete terminal period.

Define the first-order discrepancy

\[
D_1(M,Q)
=
\sum_{p\le Q}
\left|\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)\right|.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
D_1(M,Q)\le A(Q),
\qquad
A(Q):=\sum_{p\le Q}Z(p).
}
\tag{2}
\]

### 1.1 What exponent does the gap bound formally give?

The pointwise gap-polynomial estimate

\[
Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}
\]

gives

\[
A(Q)\ll \frac{Q^{5/3}}{\log Q}.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus, if one insists on traditional level-of-distribution notation, (2) is smaller than
\(M/(\log M)^A\) throughout

\[
Q\le M^{3/5-\varepsilon}.
\]

The **current repository state is slightly stronger**. The root-strip/First-Lemma and corank argument recorded in
`problems/3.2/atom_tail_section.tex` gives the aggregate estimate

\[
\boxed{
A(Q)\ll \frac{Q^{8/5}}{\log Q}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Formally, this permits

\[
Q\le M^{5/8-\varepsilon}.
\tag{5}
\]

So there is indeed a numerical exponent larger than \(1/2\) available for this **one-column period-completion norm**.

But at the parameters in the question,

\[
M=X^2,\qquad Q=X=M^{1/2},
\]

we are exactly at the square-root exponent.

### 1.2 Why even the formal \(5/8\) exponent is not analogous to BV

Bombieri--Vinogradov studies one fixed arithmetic sequence, essentially \(\Lambda(n)\), projected into many residue classes. The discrepancy inside each modulus is genuinely arithmetic; it is not removed by completing periods.

Here the test function itself changes with the modulus:

\[
p\longmapsto g_p.
\]

Moreover, every \(g_p\) is exactly \(p\)-periodic and has mean zero over one full period. Therefore (1) is automatic for any sets \(Z_p\).

For example, if every \(Z_p\) were the entire residue field, then every discrepancy in (1) would be exactly zero, although all columns would be perfectly correlated. At the opposite extreme, the anchored-star example below has \(|Z_p|=2\) and excellent one-dimensional discrepancy, but fails \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) by a power of \(X\).

Thus (2) measures **boundary completion**, not randomness or equidistribution between different primes.

Calling (5) an Apéry Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem obscures the essential issue.

## 2. Where the real difficulty begins: pair moduli have occupancy one

For the high-moment problem, the relevant dyadic block is

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},
\qquad M=X^2.
\]

Put

\[
\Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad
K_X(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}f_p(m),
\]

and

\[
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(p)}p.
\]

For one prime \(p\asymp X\), the interval \([0,M)\) contains about

\[
\frac Mp\asymp X
\]

complete periods. That is why the one-prime discrepancy is trivial.

For two distinct primes \(p,q\in\mathcal P_X\), however,

\[
pq>X^2=M.
\]

Consequently every prescribed residue pair modulo \((p,q)\) has **at most one** representative below \(M\).

This is the occupancy-one barrier isolated earlier in the repository. It is not the usual BFI square-root barrier. At the pair level there is no long arithmetic progression left to average.

### 2.1 Why \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) is universal rather than dispersive

Let

\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|.
\]

Because every residue pair contributes at most one integer below \(M\),

\[
J_{p,q}\le Z(p)Z(q).
\]

Therefore, with ordered pairs,

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_2(X)
&:=\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_2\\
&=\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\\
&\le \left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}Z(p)\right)^2\\
&\le \left(2X\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(p)}p\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
F_2(X)\le 4X^2\lambda_X^2=4M\lambda_X^2.
}
\tag{6}
\]

This is the sharpened constant recorded in `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex`.

No cancellation, Fourier transform, Kloosterman bound, or spectral estimate appears in (6). The estimate is a bare CRT occupancy bound.

This distinction matters: \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) does not say that the pair intersections are distributed randomly. It only says that there cannot be more intersections than there are residue pairs.

## 3. The exact object needed for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

The third factorial moment is

\[
F_3(X)=\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_3.
\]

For ordered distinct \(p,q\), define

\[
T_{p,q}
=
\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}f_\ell(m),
\]

and

\[
\lambda_{p,q}
=
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}
\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell.
\]

At a row hit by \(k\) primes, there are \((k)_2\) ordered anchor pairs and \(k-2\) choices of a third prime. Hence

\[
\boxed{
F_3(X)=\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}.
}
\tag{7}
\]

The independent-density prediction on a pair locus is

\[
T_{p,q}\approx J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}.
\]

Define the total positive pair--Palm overload

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_3(X)
=
\sum_{p\ne q}
\bigl(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}\bigr)_+.
}
\tag{8}
\]

The exact pair--Palm theorem in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex`, combined with the constant-4 form of (6), gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_3(X)
\le F_3(X)
\le \mathscr P_3(X)+4M\lambda_X^3.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(\mathrm{HM})_3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathscr P_3(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
}
\tag{10}
\]

This is not merely a sufficient condition. It is an exact characterization up to the already acceptable main term.

### 3.1 Why the one-prime level says nothing about (8)

The pair excess can be written as

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}
=
\sum_{\ell\notin\{p,q\}}
\left(
\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}f_\ell(m)
-
J_{p,q}\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The one-prime estimate (1) controls \(g_\ell\) on an **interval containing about \(M/\ell\asymp X\) full periods**.

Equation (11) tests \(g_\ell\) on the sparse, pair-dependent set

\[
\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q.
\]

Because \(pq>M\), this set contains at most one point for each residue pair and typically only \(O(Z(p)Z(q))\) points in total. There is no period completion.

Thus the missing statement is a conditional local-limit theorem on isolated CRT representatives, not an extension of (1).

A useful name for it is the one already suggested in the repository:

> **Microscopic moving-target equidistribution for Apéry zero residues.**

The target scale is \(1/\ell\), and the test points themselves move with \((p,q)\).

## 4. A decisive counterexample: perfect first-order distribution and failure of \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

The anchored-rainbow-star proposition in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` rules out any implication from the currently proposed inputs.

Let

\[
M=X^2,
\qquad
m_0=\left\lfloor\frac{3M}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]

After deleting only \(O(1)\) exceptional primes from \(\mathcal P_X\), define

\[
a_p=m_0\bmod p,
\qquad
Z_p^*=\{a_p,p-1-a_p\}.
\tag{12}
\]

Then every active prime hits the same row \(m_0\):

\[
m_0\bmod p=a_p\in Z_p^*.
\]

Let \(L\) be the number of active primes. Then

\[
L\asymp\frac{X}{\log X},
\qquad
K_X^*(m_0)=L.
\]

The family has all of the following properties:

- \(|Z_p^*|=2\) or \(0\);
- reflection invariance under \(r\mapsto p-1-r\);
- no consecutive residues;
- the local interval bound \(|Z_p^*\cap I|\le 2|I|^{2/3}\);
- the single-prime discrepancy
  \[
  \left|\sum_{m<M}g_p^*(m)\right|\le2;
  \]
- the universal \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) estimate;
- the natural centered second-moment scale;
- a uniform \(O(1)\) codegree bound between two distinct rows;
- compatibility with the necessary reflected gap certificate.

Nevertheless,

\[
F_3^*(X)\ge (L)_3
\gg \frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{13}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
\lambda_X^*\asymp\frac1{\log X},
\]

so the \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) target is

\[
M(\lambda_X^*)^3
\asymp
\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{14}
\]

Comparing (13) and (14), the star violates \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) by a factor of order \(X\).

This counterexample is stronger than necessary. It shows:

\[
\boxed{
\text{first-order level}
+ (\mathrm{HM})_2
+ local zero-set structure
+ bounded codegrees
\not\Rightarrow
(\mathrm{HM})_3.
}
\tag{15}
\]

In particular, even the conjectural first-moment estimate

\[
\sum_{p\le Q}Z(p)\ll\frac Q{\log Q},
\]

which would give a formal one-dimensional level almost as large as \(M\), still would not imply \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

The obstruction is not lack of marginal distribution. It is the possibility of a high-degree **atom** shared by many columns.

## 5. The Maynard 2020 large-modulus work is not a generic GPY-to-third-moment machine

There are two distinct bodies of work that are easy to conflate.

### 5.1 The Maynard--GPY multidimensional sieve

Maynard's 2015 paper *Small gaps between primes* refines the GPY/Selberg sieve by introducing multidimensional divisor weights. The weights are optimized to make a ratio of two already-evaluable quadratic forms large.

The sieve does not create the distribution theorem used to evaluate those forms. It consumes Bombieri--Vinogradov, Elliott--Halberstam, or related arithmetic-progression estimates as an input.

### 5.2 The 2020 large-moduli trilogy

The papers

- *Primes in arithmetic progressions to large moduli I: Fixed residue classes*,
- *II: Well-factorable estimates*, and
- *III: Uniform residue classes*

extend BFI/Fouvry/Zhang-style distribution estimates beyond \(x^{1/2}\) in restricted settings.

Part II, in particular, proves estimates up to \(x^{3/5-\varepsilon}\) with **triply well-factorable weights**. But “triply” means that a weight on one composite modulus \(q\) can be decomposed as a convolution of three sequences supported on factors

\[
q=q_1q_2q_3.
\]

It does **not** mean that a third correlation of three unrelated modulus-indexed events is controlled.

Maynard's proof uses:

- Heath-Brown's identity to decompose \(\Lambda\);
- bilinear convolution estimates;
- repeated Cauchy--Schwarz and Linnik dispersion;
- flexible factorization of the modulus weights;
- Kloosterman-sum estimates from Kuznetsov/spectral theory and Weil--Deligne input.

The additional third factor gives room to rebalance diagonal and off-diagonal terms. It is not a general trilinear sieve theorem.

### 5.3 Why the needed Apéry geometry has the wrong shape

In the prime-progressions problem, a modulus

\[
q\asymp x^{3/5}
\]

still leaves

\[
\frac{x}{q}\asymp x^{2/5}
\]

candidate integers in each progression. Factorization of \(q\) produces long bilinear boxes on which dispersion and spectral estimates can act.

In the Apéry high-moment problem,

\[
p,q\asymp X,
\qquad
M=X^2,
\qquad
pq>M.
\]

After fixing \((p,q)\) and a residue pair, there are zero or one candidates below \(M\). There is no long inner variable to complete, no balanced box, and no Kloosterman family supplied by factorization.

Treating \(pq\) as a conveniently factorable modulus does not help: it is already at the full interval length. Treating \(pqr\) as a modulus is worse:

\[
pqr\asymp X^3\gg M.
\]

A triple residue tuple is simply an isolated CRT point, if it occurs at all.

Moreover, the residue sets \(Z_p,Z_q,Z_r\) arise in three different defining characteristics. They are not the roots modulo factors of one fixed bounded-degree congruence whose Chinese-remainder product gives a fixed algebraic kernel.

This is precisely the occupancy-one distinction recorded in repository commit
`59b96a9214b23634bd0efdd558077cb3182a58a8`.

## 6. What happens if one literally introduces Maynard-type weights?

The circularity is visible by expanding the most natural weight.

Let \((a_p)\) be coefficients and put

\[
W_a(m)
=
\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}a_pf_p(m)\right)^2.
\tag{16}
\]

This is the direct analogue of a nonnegative quadratic Selberg weight.

Its total mass is

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_1(a)
&=\sum_{m<M}W_a(m)\\
&=\sum_{p,q}a_pa_q
  \sum_{m<M}f_p(m)f_q(m).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The off-diagonal part of (17) is a weighted pair-intersection sum, so \((\mathrm{HM})_2\)-type information can control it for suitably bounded coefficients.

To reward rows hit by many primes, the Maynard numerator would be

\[
S_2(a)
=
\sum_{m<M}K_X(m)W_a(m).
\tag{18}
\]

Expanding gives

\[
\boxed{
S_2(a)
=
\sum_{r,p,q}a_pa_q
\sum_{m<M}f_r(m)f_p(m)f_q(m).
}
\tag{19}
\]

The terms with distinct \(r,p,q\) are precisely weighted triple intersections. Thus evaluating the numerator already requires the missing \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) arithmetic.

The optimization of \(a_p\) cannot estimate (19). It only optimizes the result after (19) has been evaluated or bounded.

### 6.1 Pair-indexed weights make the moment order worse

Suppose instead one attaches coefficients to pairs:

\[
A_w(m)=\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}f_p(m)f_q(m).
\]

If one squares this to obtain a nonnegative Selberg weight,

\[
W_w(m)=A_w(m)^2,
\]

then

- \(\sum_mW_w(m)\) involves intersections of as many as four primes;
- \(\sum_mK_X(m)W_w(m)\) involves intersections of as many as five primes.

So the literal multidimensional weight raises the required moment rather than proving the third one.

If one does **not** square and assumes \(w_{p,q}\ge0\), then

\[
\sum_m (K_X(m)-2)A_w(m)
=
\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}T_{p,q}.
\tag{20}
\]

The natural centered expression is

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}
\bigl(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}\bigr).
\tag{21}
\]

But the optimal pair-weight problem is already solved formally:

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_3(X)
=
\sup_{0\le w_{p,q}\le1}
\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}
\bigl(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}\bigr).
}
\tag{22}
\]

Indeed, the optimizing weight is \(1\) exactly on the pairs with positive excess.

Combining (10) and (22), a uniform Maynard-type estimate for all bounded nonnegative pair weights is **equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)**.

It is not a route around the theorem.

### 6.2 Restricting to well-factorable weights does not currently close the gap

One could ask only for product or low-rank pair weights, such as

\[
w_{p,q}=u_pv_q.
\]

That would be a genuine bilinear-form problem. But two further steps would still be needed:

1. prove an Apéry-specific bilinear estimate for the matrix
   \[
   E_{p,q}=T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q};
   \]
2. show that controlling a structured family of weights controls the full positive selector in (22).

Neither step is supplied by the current local bounds.

The anchored star is especially instructive: on its active primes, the overload matrix is essentially a positive rank-one block. Even the simplest product weight \(u_p=v_p=1\) detects the large excess. Factorability does not cancel it; an arithmetic theorem excluding the star is still required.

## 7. The correct hierarchy of “levels of distribution”

The one-column estimate is an order-one marginal statement:

\[
\mathcal D_1:
\qquad
\sum_{p}\left|\sum_{m\in I}g_p(m)\right|
\text{ is small for intervals }I.
\]

What \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) requires is a conditional order-three statement:

\[
\mathcal D_{1\mid2}:
\qquad
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(
\sum_{\ell\ne p,q}
\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}g_\ell(m)
\right)_+
\text{ is small}.
\tag{23}
\]

Equation (23) is exactly (8), up to the displayed notation.

There is no implication

\[
\mathcal D_1\Longrightarrow\mathcal D_{1\mid2}
\]

for arbitrary sparse periodic systems. The star gives a counterexample even after imposing the currently proved Apéry zero-set constraints.

This is analogous to the distinction in additive combinatorics between:

- uniform distribution in single residue classes; and
- a higher-complexity linear-forms condition.

First-order Fourier information does not automatically control a three-linear pattern.

## 8. What new input would make a Maynard-style strategy viable?

A weighted sieve could become useful if one first established one of the following genuinely new Apéry-specific statements.

### 8.1 The exact pair--Palm distribution theorem

Prove

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(
T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}
\right)_+
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
}
\tag{24}
\]

This is exactly \((\mathrm{HM})_3\), but it identifies the right analytic object: third-prime overload conditioned on an actual two-prime CRT locus.

A Maynard-style amplifier might organize an attack on (24), but the amplifier would need a new trace/Kloosterman-like estimate for the Apéry kernel. No such estimate is currently available.

### 8.2 An atom-tail theorem

The repository's moment-collapse inequality gives

\[
F_3(X)\le
\left(\max_{m<M}K_X(m)\right)F_2(X).
\]

Using (6), it is enough to prove

\[
\boxed{
\max_{m<M}K_X(m)
\ll X^{o(1)}\lambda_X.
}
\tag{25}
\]

This is the strong anti-tail hypothesis \((\mathrm{AT}'')\) from
`problems/3.2/atom_tail_section.tex`.

The anchored star violates (25) maximally. Thus (25) is exactly the kind of arithmetic input that a first-order level cannot provide.

### 8.3 A spectral or low-rank theorem for the pair-overload matrix

One could seek a bound for

\[
\sum_{p,q}u_pv_qE_{p,q}
\]

uniformly in bounded coefficients, together with a decomposition theorem converting the positive selector in (22) into a controlled combination of such low-rank tests.

This would resemble amplification more closely. But it would still be a new horizontal theorem coupling the defining-characteristic zero sets for many primes. The present Mellin/K3 description does not construct a bounded-conductor cross-prime kernel for it.

## 9. Direct answers to the questions

### Does the Apéry family have a super Bombieri--Vinogradov level?

Not in the sense relevant to BV.

- At the parameters stated, \(Q=X=\sqrt M\), so there is no numerical inequality beyond the square root.
- The pointwise \(p^{2/3}\) bound formally gives exponent \(3/5\).
- The current aggregate \(x^{8/5}/\log x\) bound formally gives exponent \(5/8\).
- These exponents arise from summing boundary errors of periodic functions, not from cancellation of an arithmetic sequence among progressions.

They should not be compared to GRH, BFI, or Elliott--Halberstam as if they measured the same phenomenon.

### Can this one-prime level be used for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)?

No.

The pair modulus \(pq\) is already larger than the sample length \(M\), so conditioning on two hits leaves isolated CRT points. The one-prime interval discrepancy contains no information about the behavior of a third zero set on those points.

The anchored-star proposition gives an explicit theorem-level counterexample to any such implication.

### Is \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) a BFI-style bilinear dispersion estimate?

No. It is a universal CRT occupancy bound:

\[
J_{p,q}\le Z(p)Z(q).
\]

It does not control centered pair errors or conditional third-prime behavior.

### Can Maynard's 2020 large-modulus method be adapted directly?

No direct adaptation is currently available.

Maynard's large-modulus method relies on flexible factorization of composite modulus weights and on long bilinear sums ultimately controlled by Kloosterman/spectral estimates. Here the moduli are prime, the pair product has occupancy at most one, and the zero sets vary in the defining characteristic.

Also, the 2020 large-modulus trilogy should not be identified with the 2015 Maynard--GPY multidimensional sieve. The former is an extension of BFI/Fouvry/Zhang distribution technology; the latter is an optimized Selberg sieve consuming distribution estimates.

### Is there a Maynard-type pair-weight formulation?

Yes, but its optimal form is exactly (22), and bounding it is equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

Thus the formulation is useful diagnostically, not as a proof from current inputs.

## Final conclusion

The proposed single-prime estimate is real but essentially tautological:

\[
\sum_{p\le Q}
\left|\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)\right|
\le
\sum_{p\le Q}Z(p).
\]

It measures only incomplete periods. It does not measure cross-prime randomness.

The true transition occurs when two primes have already been fixed. Since

\[
pq>M,
\]

the pair locus consists of isolated CRT representatives. Proving that third primes hit these representatives with their expected density is precisely the pair--Palm theorem (24), equivalently \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

Maynard-style weights cannot manufacture this conditional equidistribution. A quadratic weight already has \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) in its numerator, and pair-indexed squared weights require still higher moments. The optimal unsquared pair-weight problem is exactly the positive-overload norm \(\mathscr P_3\).

Therefore the clean answer is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The apparent super-level does not help with }(\mathrm{HM})_3.
}
\]

A genuinely new ingredient must exclude anchored high-degree rows. The most precise available formulations are:

- the pair--Palm positive-overload estimate;
- the atom-tail bound \(\max K_X\ll\lambda_XX^{o(1)}\); or
- an Apéry-specific spectral/local-limit theorem for the pair-conditioned defining-characteristic kernel.

That new arithmetic input could then be combined with sieve-style optimization, but it is not supplied by existing BFI or Maynard theory.
