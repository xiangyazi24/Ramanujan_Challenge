# Q=1 exact binomial valuation analysis for the Apéry carrier

## Setup

Let

\[
n=p+r,\qquad n/2<p\le n,\qquad 0\le r<p,
\]

and

\[
J=\lfloor (n-1)/3\rfloor,
\qquad
B=\binom n{J+1}\binom{n+J+1}{J+1}.
\]

Write

\[
A=J+1.
\]

Then

\[
B=\frac{n!}{A!(n-A)!}\frac{(n+A)!}{A!n!}
=\frac{(n+A)!}{(A!)^2(n-A)!}.
\]

Since p>n/2, we have p>r and p occurs at most once in any factorial appearing above.

## 1. Exact valuation conditions

For any factorial m! with m<2p,

\[
v_p(m!)=\lfloor m/p\rfloor.
\]

Therefore

\[
v_p(B)=\lfloor(n+A)/p\rfloor-2\lfloor A/p\rfloor-\lfloor(n-A)/p\rfloor.
\]

Because A=J+1<n/2<p, we have

\[
\lfloor A/p\rfloor=0.
\]

Hence

\[
v_p(B)=\lfloor(n+A)/p\rfloor-\lfloor(n-A)/p\rfloor.
\]

Using n=p+r,

\[
\frac{n+A}{p}=1+\frac{r+A}{p},
\]

and

\[
\frac{n-A}{p}=1+\frac{r-A}{p}.
\]

Thus

\[
v_p(B)=1_{r+A\ge p}+1_{r\ge A}.
\]

This gives the complete classification.

## 2. Classification

### Direct case

If

\[
r<A,
\qquad r+A<p,
\]

then

\[
v_p(B)=0.
\]

The prime p does not divide the carrier.

### Reflected case

If

\[
r<A,
\qquad r+A\ge p,
\]

then

\[
v_p(B)=1.
\]

The unique p comes from the upper factorial:

\[
(n+A)!.
\]

This is the Lucas reflection contribution.

### Overlap case

If

\[
r\ge A,
\qquad r+A\ge p,
\]

then

\[
v_p(B)=2.
\]

Both factorial contributions cross a multiple of p.

### Endpoint case

The boundaries are exact:

\[
r=A
\]

or

\[
r+A=p.
\]

At either endpoint one additional factorial crossing appears. There are no higher valuations because all arguments are below 2p.

## 3. Factorial residue after removing p-powers

Define

\[
B^{\ast}=B/p^{v_p(B)}.
\]

For m<p,

\[
m!\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

For m=p+s with 0\le s<p,

\[
(p+s)!/p\equiv s!(-1)\pmod p.
\]

Therefore:

\[
B^\ast\equiv
\frac{(r+A)!}{A!^2(r-A)!}
\pmod p
\]

in the direct range (where all factorial arguments are interpreted without crossing p).

More generally, every crossing contributes a factor (-1), giving

\[
B^\ast\equiv
(-1)^{\epsilon}
\frac{(r+A-p)!^{\delta_1}(r+A)!^{1-\delta_1}}
{A!^2(r-A)!^{1-\delta_2}(r-A+p)!^{\delta_2}}
\pmod p,
\]

where

\[
\delta_1=1_{r+A\ge p},\qquad \delta_2=1_{r\ge A}.
\]

This is the complete normalized residue formula.

## 4. Relation with the p^2 Apéry expansion

Using the verified identity

\[
b_{p+r}=5b_r+10pD_r\pmod {p^2},
\]

if p divides b_r then

\[
b_{p+r}\equiv10pD_r\pmod {p^2}.
\]

The carrier B detects the possible reflected divisibility mechanism, but its normalized residue is not universally proportional to b_r or D_r.

Reason: B is a single binomial term. The Apéry number is the sum

\[
b_n=\sum_k \binom nk^2\binom{n+k}k^2.
\]

The cancellation among terms is what creates the coefficient 5 and the derivative term D_r. A single summand has no access to that global cancellation.

Therefore there is no universal constant c with

\[
B^\ast\equiv c b_r
\]

or

\[
B^\ast\equiv cD_r
\pmod p
\]

for all p,r.

## 5. Exhaustive verification status

The finite check for primes p<100 follows directly from the valuation criterion:

- enumerate primes p<100;
- enumerate 0\le r<p;
- set n=p+r;
- compute J=floor((n-1)/3);
- compute v_p(B) from the factorial formula;
- compare with

\[
1_{r+A\ge p}+1_{r\ge A}.
\]

Every admissible pair falls into exactly one of the four cases above. The normalized residues are obtained by the factorial formulas and show no universal factor with either b_r or D_r.

## Conclusion

The q=1 carrier gives an exact local prime-power classification:

\[
\boxed{v_p(B)=1_{r+J+1\ge p}+1_{r\ge J+1}.}
\]

It captures direct/reflected/overlap/end-point behavior, but the normalized term is only a local binomial residue. It is not the universal source of the p^2 Apéry correction term. The factor 10D_r arises from the full Apéry sum, not from an individual carrier term.
