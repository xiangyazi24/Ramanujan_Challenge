# R1b — the first pointwise theorem: audit of W

## Verdict

I do **not** have an unconditional proof of W. I also do not find a weaker new worst-case theorem that improves the trivial top-window count at a prescribed integer.

What I can prove is a fairly sharp negative statement about the three proposed routes, together with a clean repairing premise.

1. **Short-interval zero bounds and the existing two-index codegree theorem cannot by themselves give W.** At a fixed \(n\), each characteristic \(p\in(n/2,n]\) is sampled at only one index \(r=n-p\). The \(O(D^{2/3})\) bound for zeros in an interval of length \(D\) is a statement *inside one characteristic*; it never sees two of the sampled indices in the same row. The nearby-index codegree theorem constrains the intersection of two high-load integers \(m,n\), but one isolated high-load integer need not create a second one.

2. **Reflection does not supply the missing second congruence.** This is more decisive than I expected. If \(p\mid b_r\), reflection supplies the second zero \(s=p-1-r\). Let
   \[
   x=\min(r,s),\qquad h=|s-r|.
   \]
   Then \(h\) is even and \(2x+h+1=p\). The proved even-gap factorization in the repository is
   \[
   N_h(x)=(2x+h+1)M_h(x).
   \]
   Hence the return congruence \(p\mid N_h(x)\) forced by the pair \(\{r,s\}\) is automatic from the **structural linear factor \(p\)**. It gives no primitive second condition at all.

3. **Gallagher's larger sieve is exactly saturated by a singleton.** If a candidate set \(A\subset[1,X]\) occupies at most \(\nu(p)\) residue classes modulo each \(p\), the larger sieve bounds \(|A|\). But for \(A=\{n\}\), the actual occupied class count is \(\nu_A(p)=1\) for every relevant prime. In Gallagher's formula numerator and denominator are then equal, so the conclusion is exactly \(|A|\le1\). Replacing \(1\) by the allowed-class bound \(|Z_p|\ge1\) only weakens the inequality. The fact that the product of the moduli is much larger than \(X\) gives uniqueness of a CRT representative in \([1,X]\), not nonexistence. Thus idea (3) really is an averaging/counting machine here, not a pointwise exclusion principle.

4. There is a very simple **W-specific planted diagonal model** which satisfies the support-level one-characteristic constraints, including the recurrence return law for the reflected pair, while making essentially every top-window prime bad at one prescribed \(n\). This identifies the exact first failed implication.

5. The cleanest non-tautological repairing premise is a positive dyadic density of **zero-free primes**
   \[
   Z_p=\varnothing.
   \]
   If for some \(\delta_0>0\)
   \[
   \#\{p\in(n/2,n]:Z_p=\varnothing\}
   \ge \delta_0\bigl(\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)\bigr)
   \]
   for all sufficiently large \(n\), then W follows immediately with \(\delta=\delta_0\). By Lucas, \(Z_p=\varnothing\) is equivalent to saying that \(p\) divides no Apéry number at all. Malik--Straub conjecture that these primes have natural density
   \[
   e^{-1/2}=0.6065306597\ldots.
   \]
   That conjecture would prove a much stronger form of W (indeed for every fixed quotient window), but even infinitude of such primes is still an open problem in the literature I found.

So my R1b conclusion is:

> **The first missing pointwise input is not a better marginal zero bound or a stronger ordinary large sieve. It is either (a) a positive supply of completely empty characteristics \(Z_p=\varnothing\), or (b) a recurrence-specific theorem about the distinguished Apéry fibre that prevents the moving diagonal \(r=n-p\) from aligning across characteristics. The ordinary two-zero return continuant does not qualify, because for the reflected pair it is exactly the structural center factor.**

That is compatible with the Aug. 31 C4/interface verdict, but is sharper for W: even the first recurrence-visible support law from Q7577 does not repair the top-window model.

## 2. Rewrite W in the most useful form

For \(p\in(n/2,n]\), put

\[
r_p=n-p.
\]

Then \(0\le r_p<p\), and Apéry--Lucas gives

\[
p\mid b_n
\iff p\mid b_{n-p}
\iff r_p\in Z_p
\]

for all large relevant primes. Thus

\[
N_1(n)=\sum_{n/2<p\le n}{\bf1}_{r_p\in Z_p}.
\]

W asks for an absolute \(\delta>0\) such that

\[
N_1(n)\le(1-\delta)L(n),
\qquad
L(n):=\pi(n)-\pi(n/2),
\]

for every sufficiently large \(n\).

Notice how weak this is compared with the main theorem: it asks only that a fixed positive fraction of the prime rows miss their *one prescribed point*. It does not ask \(N_1(n)=o(L(n))\).

That weakness makes the following obstruction particularly informative: even a two-point zero row is already enough to saturate the target if its location may be chosen adversarially.

## 3. Exact failure of idea (1): a short \(p\)-interval does not create a single-row interval problem

Take a short prime interval

\[
I=(P,P+D]\subset(n/2,n].
\]

As \(p\) runs through \(I\), the sampled index

\[
r_p=n-p
\]

runs through an interval \(J\) of length \(D\).

It is tempting to invoke the proved statement

\[
\#(Z_p\cap J')\ll |J'|^{2/3}
\]

for every interval \(J'\) of a fixed row. But the sampled sum is

\[
\sum_{p\in I}{\bf1}_{\,n-p\in Z_p}.
\]

Each summand lives in a **different** zero set \(Z_p\). The interval estimate says, for each fixed \(p\),

\[
\#(Z_p\cap J)\ll D^{2/3},
\]

while the diagonal uses only the single point \(n-p\in J\). Therefore it gives only

\[
{\bf1}_{n-p\in Z_p}\le1,
\]

and after summing,

\[
\sum_{p\in I}{\bf1}_{n-p\in Z_p}
\le \#(I\cap\mathbb P),
\]

which is the original trivial bound.

The same quantifier mismatch explains why shrinking \(D\) does not help. The local zero estimate becomes stronger as a *row statement*, but the diagonal still asks one yes/no question per row.

### Why the nearby-index codegree theorem does not rescue this

Let

\[
B(n):=\{p\in(n/2,n]:p\mid b_{n-p}\}.
\]

The repaired exceptional-set theorem uses the fact that for two nearby integers \(m<n\), the set of primes simultaneously loaded by both rows has size

\[
|B(m)\cap B(n)|\ll n-m
\]

(after the relevant window bookkeeping).

This is powerful for ruling out **many nearby exceptional integers**. It has no implication for one prescribed exceptional integer. A set system can have one member of size \(L\) and all neighboring members empty while every pairwise intersection is zero.

The exact first failed implication in idea (1) is therefore:

\[
|B(n)|\ \text{large}
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists m\ne n\ \text{near }n:
|B(m)\cap B(n)|\ \text{large}.
\]

A premise of that form would indeed repair the argument: if large load at \(n\) forced a shift \(1\le h\ll n/\log n\) for which \(B(n)\cap B(n-h)\gg n/\log n\), the codegree bound \(O(h)\) could contradict it after choosing the constants. But no such “shift amplification” theorem is present, and nothing in the current one-row data implies it. Proving it would itself be a new cross-characteristic recurrence theorem.

## 4. Reflection looks like a second equation, but for W it is exactly the structural factor

This is the cleanest concrete calculation in this audit.

Assume \(p\ge7\), \(0<r<p\), and \(p\mid b_r\). Reflection gives

\[
p\mid b_s,\qquad s=p-1-r.
\]

If \(r\ne s\), let

\[
x=\min(r,s),\qquad h=|s-r|.
\]

Then:

- \(x+h=\max(r,s)\);
- \(h\) is even, because \(p-1\) and \(2r\) are both even;
- and
  \[
  2x+h+1=r+s+1=p.
  \]

The return-continuant law says that two zeros at \(x,x+h\) force

\[
p\mid N_h(x).
\]

But for even \(h\) the repository proves

\[
N_h(X)=(2X+h+1)M_h(X).
\]

Substituting \(x\) gives

\[
N_h(x)=p\,M_h(x).
\]

Thus

\[
p\mid N_h(x)
\]

contains **zero extra information**: it is the universal center factor.

This also explains why trying to use the reflection partner to manufacture a first \(p\)-adic jet or a second independent resultant keeps failing. At the support level, the reflected return is not merely dependent; it is carried by an explicit linear factor.

If \(r=s=(p-1)/2\), there is only one zero and no second return at all.

So any successful “two-zero” repair for W must involve a **non-reflected** zero or some non-support coordinate of the distinguished Apéry solution. But a row can perfectly well have only one reflected pair, both empirically and in the random model. Consequently a theorem that starts “every bad row has another non-reflected zero” is not a credible repair.

## 5. A W-specific planted diagonal obstruction

Fix a large integer \(n\). For every prime

\[
p\in(n/2,n]
\]

except the \(O(1)\) endpoint cases described below, define

\[
r_p=n-p,\qquad
s_p=p-1-r_p=2p-n-1,
\]

and plant the abstract zero row

\[
Z_p^\star=\{r_p,s_p\}.
\]

If \(r_p=s_p\), take the singleton instead.

To respect the genuine fixed values \(b_0=1,b_1=5\), simply omit primes for which one of \(r_p,s_p\) is \(0\) or \(1\). These can occur only at

\[
p=n,\quad n-1,\quad (n+1)/2,\quad (n+2)/2,
\]

so at most four primes are lost.

For every retained \(p\):

1. \(0<r_p,s_p<p\).
2. Reflection is exact:
   \[
   r_p+s_p=p-1.
   \]
3. There are no consecutive zeros. If the points are distinct, their gap is a positive even integer, hence at least \(2\).
4. \(Z^\star(p)\le2\), much stronger than \(O(p^{2/3})\).
5. Every subinterval contains at most two zeros, much stronger than the banked \(O(D^{2/3})\) estimate.
6. The support-visible recurrence return condition for the pair is satisfied automatically. If \(x=\min(r_p,s_p)\), \(h=|r_p-s_p|\), then
   \[
   h\ \text{is even},\qquad
   2x+h+1=p,
   \]
   so the proved factorization gives
   \[
   p\mid N_h(x).
   \]
7. Yet
   \[
   r_p=n-p\in Z_p^\star
   \]
   for every retained \(p\). Therefore
   \[
   N_1^\star(n)
   \ge L(n)-4
   =(1-o(1))L(n).
   \]

Hence for every fixed \(\delta>0\), this interface model violates W for all sufficiently large \(n\).

### Scope of this obstruction

This is **not** an actual Apéry sequence modulo all these primes. It does not impose the distinguished initial state \((b_0,b_1)=(1,5)\) through the full recurrence, nor the entire nonzero-value fibre structure. It therefore does not prove W false.

What it proves is the exact no-go needed for the proposed arguments:

> reflection + no adjacency + the pointwise/subinterval zero bounds + the ordinary two-zero return-continuant law do not imply even a fixed positive saving in W.

This is narrower than the Aug. 31 all-interface countermodel in the brief, but it is tailored to the top window and shows why the new recurrence support law from Q7577 is still insufficient here.

The missing information is exactly: **why the distinguished Apéry fibre cannot choose the centered zero \(r=n-p\) in almost every characteristic simultaneously.**

## 6. Idea (2): small residues, parity, and \(p\)-adic local structure

### 6.1 Small-modulus automaticity does not couple to the working characteristic

Knowing \(b_r\) modulo a fixed small prime \(\ell\), or modulo \(\ell^k\), places restrictions on the integer \(b_r\). But the event under study is

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad p\asymp n,\qquad r=n-p.
\]

Without a reciprocity law or a common algebraic carrier involving both \(\ell\) and \(p\), a congruence modulo \(\ell^k\) does not prohibit divisibility by the unrelated large prime \(p\).

In particular, the map \(p\mapsto n-p\pmod m\) merely partitions the top-window primes into arithmetic progressions. To turn this into W one would need a theorem such as “for primes \(p\) in a positive-density set of progressions, the distinguished zero \(n-p\) is impossible modulo \(p\).” I find no such theorem in the repository or the literature checked.

### 6.2 There is no zero-free relative-index region available

A statement of the form

\[
Z_p\cap[0,cp)=\varnothing
\]

for a fixed \(c>0\) would immediately help the top window. But it is incompatible with familiar examples: for instance \(73\mid b_2\) and \(17\mid b_3\). More generally, the zero distribution in the repo looks roughly uniform after reflection, not repelled from an endpoint.

### 6.3 Reflection parity is already spent

The parity law for \(Z(p)\) is a consequence of reflection pairing, with the midpoint as the only possible fixed point. For W, the planted model above uses exactly one reflected pair per bad characteristic. Thus parity and reflection are fully compatible with saturation of the moving diagonal.

### 6.4 A useful local premise does exist: zero-free characteristics

There is, however, one genuinely local obstruction that *does* kill a singleton diagonal hit: an empty allowed set

\[
Z_p=\varnothing.
\]

This leads to the repair in Section 8.

## 7. Idea (3): the large/larger sieve cannot exclude one integer

This can be made completely explicit.

A standard form of Gallagher's larger sieve is as follows. Let \(A\subset[1,X]\). Suppose that modulo each \(q\) in a set \(\mathcal S\) of prime powers, \(A\) occupies at most \(\nu(q)\) residue classes. Then, if the denominator is positive,

\[
|A|
\le
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\mathcal S}\Lambda(q)-\log X}
     {\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\mathcal S}\frac{\Lambda(q)}{\nu(q)}-\log X}.
\tag{LS}
\]

Take \(A=\{n\}\). For every modulus \(p\),

\[
\nu_A(p)=1.
\]

If we use the **actual occupied-class count** in (LS), then numerator and denominator are identical:

\[
|A|\le1.
\]

That is sharp and says nothing about whether the singleton exists.

If instead we only know that an admissible \(n\) must lie in one of the \(|Z_p|\) classes modulo \(p\), then

\[
\nu(p)\le |Z_p|.
\]

For every nonempty row, \(|Z_p|\ge1\), so

\[
\sum\frac{\log p}{|Z_p|}
\le
\sum\log p.
\]

The larger-sieve upper bound is therefore no better than the singleton-saturated case; often the denominator is smaller or nonpositive.

### 7.1 Choosing only the bad primes does not help

Suppose W fails at \(n\), and let

\[
S=B(n)=\{p\in(n/2,n]:n-p\in Z_p\}.
\]

One might apply the sieve using the many moduli in \(S\). But this set is selected **after** the candidate \(n\) is known. For \(A=\{n\}\), every one of those moduli still sees one occupied residue. The sieve again returns exactly one candidate.

### 7.2 “The modulus product exceeds \(X\)” gives uniqueness, not impossibility

If we choose one allowed residue \(a_p\in Z_p\) for each of several distinct primes, CRT produces one residue class modulo

\[
Q=\prod p.
\]

When \(Q>X\), at most one representative of that class lies in \([1,X]\). That is precisely the pointwise barrier: a violating integer would be that one representative. Neither CRT nor the large sieve says that the representative cannot occur in the interval.

The second factorial moment in FINAL_AUDIT has the same logic. Pairwise CRT controls how **many** integers can realize two prescribed local conditions over a long interval. It does not exclude a single specially aligned integer.

### 7.3 The exact local-sieve repair

If some row has

\[
Z_p=\varnothing,
\]

then there is no allowed class modulo \(p\), and a candidate required to be bad at that \(p\) is impossible. Therefore, among purely local row-cardinality inputs, empty rows are qualitatively different from all bounds of the form

\[
1\le |Z_p|\le p^\theta.
\]

As long as the allowed set is nonempty, an adversary may plant its one allowed residue at the candidate \(n\bmod p\), and CRT/cardinality sieve does not object.

This is why the tiny heuristic quantity \(\sum |Z_p|/p\) controls averages beautifully but does not by itself imply W.

## 8. The smallest clean repairing premise: positive density of zero-free primes

Define

\[
\mathcal E:=\{p:Z_p=\varnothing\}.
\]

By Apéry--Lucas, for \(p\ge7\),

\[
Z_p=\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\nmid b_m\ \text{for every }m\ge0.
\]

Indeed, if every digit value \(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1}\) is nonzero modulo \(p\), then the Lucas product for the base-\(p\) digits of \(m\) is nonzero; the converse is immediate.

Consider the premise

\[
\boxed{
\#\bigl(\mathcal E\cap(n/2,n]\bigr)
\ge \delta_0\bigl(\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)\bigr)
}
\tag{ZF_\delta}
\]

for some absolute \(\delta_0>0\) and every sufficiently large \(n\).

Then no \(p\in\mathcal E\) can contribute to \(N_1(n)\), so

\[
N_1(n)
\le L(n)-\#(\mathcal E\cap(n/2,n])
\le(1-\delta_0)L(n).
\]

Thus \((ZF_\delta)\) proves W with no further work.

This is stronger than necessary, but I think it is the **smallest transparent repair of the local-sieve idea**: it inserts a local impossibility, rather than merely making an allowed set sparse.

### 8.1 Malik--Straub predicts much more

Malik and Straub, *Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers*, Research in Number Theory 2 (2016), article 5, Conjecture 6.3, predict that

\[
\frac{\#\{p\le x:Z_p=\varnothing\}}{\pi(x)}
\longrightarrow e^{-1/2}.
\]

Their heuristic is exactly the reflected random-zero model: among the roughly \((p+1)/2\) independent representatives, each vanishes with probability about \(1/p\), so the zero-free probability tends to \(e^{-1/2}\).

If this natural-density conjecture holds, the prime number theorem implies

\[
\frac{\#\{p\in(n/2,n]:Z_p=\varnothing\}}
{\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)}
\longrightarrow e^{-1/2}.
\]

Consequently W would hold with any fixed

\[
\delta<e^{-1/2}\approx0.60653.
\]

This would in fact give the same fixed positive saving in every fixed quotient interval

\[
\left(\frac{n}{q+1},\frac nq\right].
\]

However this is not presently a theorem. Malik--Straub explicitly note that even infinitude of primes dividing no Apéry number was open. I searched for subsequent work through the current date and did not find a theorem proving infinitude or positive lower density; current references/OEIS still present it as open. So \((ZF_\delta)\) is a clean repair, not an unconditional solution.

## 9. Why the ordinary return-continuant law is not the needed “new recurrence input”

Q7577 correctly identifies the first recurrence-native support condition missing from a generic planted pair: if \(p\mid b_x,b_{x+h}\), then

\[
p\mid K_h(x)
\]

for the cleared return continuant.

For the q6 countermodel this can be genuinely restrictive because the planted lower-zero gaps are odd and not the forced reflection center.

For W, however, the adversarial row only needs the one prescribed zero \(r=n-p\), plus its mandatory reflection \(p-1-r\). Their gap is even, and Section 4 shows

\[
K_h(x)\ \text{(equivalently the repo's }N_h(x)\text{)}
\]

vanishes modulo \(p\) for the structural reason

\[
2x+h+1=p.
\]

Therefore the first support-visible recurrence law is **not** the repairing premise for W.

This pinpoints what “recurrence-specific” must mean in the top window:

- not merely a relation among the two reflected zero locations;
- but information that the **distinguished initial vector** \((1,5)\) cannot land on the zero line at \(r=n-p\) for almost all varying characteristics;
- equivalently, a horizontal/cross-characteristic statement about the locations of the distinguished zero fibre.

This is consistent with the direct \(n=321\) experiment already in the repo: trying to join two bad characteristics by the same continuant that works within one characteristic fails.

## 10. A precise formulation of the failed pointwise implication

Let

\[
\mathscr I_p
\]

denote all currently banked **single-characteristic support information** used by the proposed routes: reflection, no adjacency, the global/subinterval zero bounds, and the pair-return condition.

The tempting implication is

\[
\forall p,\ \mathscr I_p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sup_n\frac{\#\{p\in(n/2,n]:n-p\in Z_p\}}
{\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)}
\le1-\delta.
\tag{F}
\]

The planted family \(Z_p^\star\) in Section 5 shows that (F) is false even if the one-row bounds are strengthened all the way to

\[
|Z_p|\le2.
\]

The **first missing bit** is the distinguished-fibre compatibility across characteristics. Put differently: the support information tells us what happens *if* the Apéry solution vanishes at a point; it does not control, across varying \(p\), at which centered return the fixed initial vector actually vanishes.

There are then two logically clean ways forward.

### Route A: prove many rows are empty

Prove \((ZF_\delta)\), or merely a positive lower dyadic density of \(Z_p=\varnothing\). This is enough for W and bypasses diagonal location entirely.

### Route B: prove cross-characteristic distinguished-fibre nonalignment

Keep rows nonempty, but prove a new theorem that couples the actual distinguished zero locations for different characteristics. This must use more than the centered return factor. It could be a horizontal trace/Mellin estimate, a new common-value resultant identity, or the two-row/three-characteristic mechanism already isolated by the Aug. 31 interface no-go.

What will **not** be enough is any further improvement

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^\theta,\quad \theta<1,
\]

or even a uniform constant bound \(|Z_p|\le C\), unless it comes with information on the *locations* across characteristics.

The planted model already has \(C=2\).

## 11. q=2 and other fixed quotients

The zero-free repair immediately extends to every fixed \(q\).

For

\[
p\in\left(\frac{n}{q+1},\frac nq\right],
\qquad
n=qp+r,\quad0\le r<p,
\]

a zero-free prime has \(b_r\not\equiv0\pmod p\), so it cannot contribute through the \(b_r\) channel. Thus a positive dyadic/short-ratio density of zero-free primes in that interval gives the same fixed positive saving for \(N_q(n)\).

The planted-reflection obstruction is most transparent for \(q=1\). For \(q\ge2\), the geometry of the prescribed remainder is less symmetric, and the Aug. 31 q6 work shows that genuinely noncentered odd return constraints can become useful. I have **not** checked a q=2 analogue far enough to claim an unconditional pointwise saving there.

## 12. Reproducible check for the W-specific planted model

Per the delivery instruction I did not run a new Python/code-interpreter job. The following standard-library script checks the arithmetic identities used in Section 5 for any chosen \(n\). It deliberately checks only the interface model, not that it is an actual Apéry row.

~~~python
from math import isqrt

def primes_upto(N):
    s = bytearray(b"\x01") * (N + 1)
    if N >= 0:
        s[0] = 0
    if N >= 1:
        s[1] = 0
    for d in range(2, isqrt(N) + 1):
        if s[d]:
            s[d*d:N+1:d] = b"\x00" * (((N - d*d) // d) + 1)
    return [i for i in range(2, N + 1) if s[i]]

def planted_top_rows(n):
    P = [p for p in primes_upto(n) if 2*p > n]
    kept = []
    omitted = []

    for p in P:
        r = n - p
        s = p - 1 - r

        # Keep the genuine fixed nonzero endpoints b_0=1, b_1=5 out
        # of the abstract planted zero sets.
        if r in (0, 1) or s in (0, 1):
            omitted.append((p, r, s))
            continue

        assert 0 < r < p
        assert 0 < s < p
        assert r + s == p - 1

        x = min(r, s)
        h = abs(r - s)

        if h:
            assert h % 2 == 0
            assert h >= 2

            # For even h the proved factorization is
            # N_h(x) = (2*x+h+1) M_h(x).
            # The reflected pair sits exactly on the structural factor.
            assert 2*x + h + 1 == p

        kept.append((p, r, s, h))

    assert len(omitted) <= 4
    return P, kept, omitted

for n in (100, 321, 1000, 6000, 10000):
    P, kept, omitted = planted_top_rows(n)
    print(
        n,
        "window_primes", len(P),
        "planted_hits", len(kept),
        "omitted_endpoints", omitted,
        "fraction", len(kept) / len(P) if P else 0.0,
    )
~~~

Since at most four primes are omitted,

\[
\frac{\#\text{planted hits}}{L(n)}
\ge1-\frac4{L(n)}
\to1.
\]

So the model defeats every fixed \(\delta>0\).

## 13. What I attacked in my own argument

1. **The countermodel is not an Apéry solution.** This is intentional. Its conclusion is only an interface no-go. It cannot be cited as evidence that W is false.

2. **I checked that the first recurrence-native pair law does not kill it.** This is the main added value over a generic arbitrary-set counterexample: the reflected gap is even and lands on the exact factor \(2x+h+1=p\).

3. **I did not prove that every conceivable small-modulus or \(p\)-adic reciprocity route is impossible.** I checked the existing automaticity/reflection machinery and found no cross-characteristic prohibition. A genuinely new reciprocity identity could change the picture.

4. **The zero-free premise is much stronger than W.** It is attractive because it is clean and local, not because it is known. The literature status makes clear that it is itself difficult.

## 14. Recommendation for R2

If the next round wants the most economical genuinely new target, I would not spend it on another large-sieve variant. I would choose between:

1. **Zero-free-prime route:** ask whether the Apéry Hasse/Cartier description can prove even a positive lower density of primes with \(Z_p=\varnothing\). This is already enough for W, but the literature warning is severe: even infinitude is open.

2. **Distinguished-fibre horizontal route:** exploit the mod-\(p\) diagonal/Mellin/K3 representation specifically at the moving exponent \(r=n-p\), and seek a theorem saying that a fixed positive proportion of characteristics cannot have the distinguished coefficient vanish. Here a constant saving, not a power saving, is enough for W. This relaxes the earlier full-theorem target substantially.

The second option is probably the more informative research direction, because it attacks exactly what the planted reflected-pair model leaves unconstrained: **which centered return is selected by the fixed Apéry initial vector in each characteristic.**

A useful sanity gate for any proposed R2 identity is immediate:

> Test it on a row with exactly the reflected pair \(\{r,p-1-r\}\). If the alleged “second condition” reduces to the even-gap center factor \(2r+h+1=p\), it cannot prove W.
