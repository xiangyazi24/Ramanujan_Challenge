# Q861: OracleA SDC doublet-sector audit

## Conclusion

The requested SDC estimate does not follow from a generic large-sieve argument. In the doublet sector the remaining object is a genuinely structured bilinear form in moving rational slopes, and the standard inequalities leave the forbidden generic loss. A proof of SDC therefore requires an additional Apéry-specific horizontal correlation statement.

## Doublet expansion

Write

\[
F_p(a)=e_p(ar_p)+e_p(-a(1+r_p)).
\]

Then the pair contribution in \(B_{P,M}\) is a sum of four phase interactions. For example the first sign choice contributes

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{0<|k|<pq/M}
 e_p(kq^{-1}r_p)e_q(kp^{-1}r_q),
\]

and the other three terms replace \(r_p,r_q\) independently by \(-1-r_p,-1-r_q\). Additive reciprocity gives

\[
e_p(kq^{-1}x)=e_{pq}(k\bar q x),\qquad
 e_q(kp^{-1}y)=e_{pq}(k\bar p y),
\]

so each term has phase

\[
e_{pq}(k(\bar q r_p+\bar p r_q))
\]

(up to the corresponding affine shifts from the other doublet choices).

Thus the obstruction is not a single-variable incomplete Kloosterman sum; it is a bilinear family with moduli \(pq\) and moving slopes.

## Why standard inequalities fail

1. Hilbert inequality.

The moduli \(pq\) are not ordered frequencies with a fixed separation. After reciprocity the spacing depends on the moving quantities

\[
\bar q r_p+\bar p r_q.
\]

No uniform separation bound is available. Applying Hilbert inequality therefore reproduces the generic diagonal/off-diagonal loss.

2. Additive large sieve.

The available large sieve scale is essentially

\[
(P^2+K)\sum |a|^2,
\]

with effective length \(K\asymp P^2/M\). After normalizing the SDC quantity this leaves the generic \(P^2/M\) loss. It does not see cancellation in the special sequence \(r_p\).

3. Dispersion.

Dispersion helps only after one has a second-moment input for shifted correlations of the coefficients. Here that input is exactly the missing statement.

4. One-sided completion.

Completion turns the problem into complete sums involving the same moving slopes. Without a bound on those completed sums, no saving beyond the generic barrier appears.

## Sharp barrier model

Take an anchored model with

\[
r_p\equiv r_0 \pmod p.
\]

Then

\[
\bar q r_p+\bar p r_q
\]

has no random variation from the slope variables. The double sum becomes a coherent family of near-resonant phases. The large sieve upper bound is then essentially attained, showing that no theorem based only on bounded coefficients and modulus sizes can imply

\[
\operatorname{Re}B_{P,M}\le M^{o(1)}Q_P.
\]

The missing ingredient must exploit the actual Apéry recurrence.

## Minimal additional Apéry-specific lemma

A sufficient reduced statement is the following bilinear correlation estimate.

For every dyadic \(P\), every \(M\) in the SDC range, and every choice of signs \(\epsilon_p,\epsilon_q\in\{0,1\}\), prove

\[
\sum_{P<p,q\le2P\atop p\ne q}
\sum_{0<|k|<pq/M}
 w_{p,q,k}
 e_{pq}\!\left(k(\bar q s_p^{\epsilon_p}+\bar p s_q^{\epsilon_q})\right)
\ll P^{o(1)}Q_P,
\]

where

\[
s_p^0=r_p,\qquad s_p^1=-1-r_p,
\]

and \(w_{p,q,k}\) are the SDC weights from OracleA with the same support and normalization.

This is the exact horizontal input needed; all other steps are standard completion and bookkeeping.

## Comparison with finite data

The finite scans are consistent with strong cancellation in the Apéry slopes, but they cannot distinguish the required theorem from a weaker average phenomenon. In particular, observing values substantially below the large-sieve scale is evidence for structure, not a proof of uniform cancellation over all dyadic ranges and all admissible weights.

## Final assessment

The OracleA SDC target is reduced to a precise moving-slope bilinear lemma. No generic inequality currently closes the gap. The weakest missing statement is not a stronger vertical estimate for individual \(F_p\), but a horizontal correlation bound for the reciprocal-slope phases generated by the Apéry residues \(r_p\).
