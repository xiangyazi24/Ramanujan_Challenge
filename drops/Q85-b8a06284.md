# Product monodromy is the right direction, but the naive statement is false

The proposed theorem is close to the correct bridge, but there is a subtle obstruction: the two reductions modulo p and q are not reductions of one geometric representation over a common finite field. Therefore one cannot simply invoke Katz's single-characteristic monodromy theorem and conclude that the product monodromy is SL(2) x SL(2).

The correct replacement is a **two-prime arithmetic monodromy theorem** for the Apéry recurrence.

## 1. The object that should replace the single orbit

Let

\[
M(t)\in GL_2(\mathbb Z[t,1/D])
\]

be the Apéry transfer matrix. For two primes p and q define the combined cocycle

\[
\rho_{p,q}:\mathcal G\to GL_2(\mathbb F_p)\times GL_2(\mathbb F_q).
\]

The desired statement is not merely that each projection has image SL(2). It is:

\[
\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{p,q})\supset SL_2(\mathbb F_p)\times SL_2(\mathbb F_q).
\]

This is a genuine independence statement about reductions of the same integral cocycle.

## 2. Why different characteristics should help

For a fixed prime, Katz gives geometric monodromy

\[
G_p=SL_2(\mathbb F_p).
\]

The possible obstruction to the product being full is a hidden correspondence between the two factors. This is analogous to Goursat's lemma: a subgroup of a product with surjective projections can still be smaller if the two representations share a common quotient.

For two finite simple groups SL(2,F_p) and SL(2,F_q), with p different from q and outside small exceptional cases, there is no common nontrivial simple quotient.

Therefore the only possible obstruction is not group-theoretic; it is arithmetic. One must rule out a hidden congruence relation between the two reductions of the cocycle.

## 3. Proposed new theorem: arithmetic product monodromy

Conjecture:

Let M(t) be an integral polynomial cocycle with generic geometric monodromy SL(2). For all sufficiently large distinct primes p,q,

\[
G_{p,q}=SL_2(\mathbb F_p)\times SL_2(\mathbb F_q).
\]

Moreover the orbit of any non-exceptional vector satisfies

\[
\#\{n<N:(v_n^{(p)},v_n^{(q)})\in A\}
=
\frac{|A|}{|\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)||\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)|}N
+O((pq)^C N^{1-\delta}).
\]

This is the missing theorem needed for the Goldbach reformulation.

## 4. Why the existing Katz framework is insufficient

Katz controls the sheaf over one finite field. It gives cancellation of sums such as

\[
\sum_{r\in\mathbb F_p} \psi(T_p(r)).
\]

The required estimate is instead a mixed-characteristic correlation:

\[
\sum_r f_p(r)f_q(r).
\]

There is no single l-adic sheaf whose trace function simultaneously lives over F_p and F_q. Hence this is outside the direct scope of Deligne-Katz.

## 5. The Apéry-specific ingredient

The crucial extra feature is the integral second-order recurrence.

The binomial sequence does not have this. Its first-order cocycle cannot produce a genuinely large product monodromy action.

For Apéry, the recurrence produces a non-abelian cocycle:

\[
v_{n+1}=M(n)v_n.
\]

The monodromy group being SL(2) means that long products do not preserve any algebraic invariant. The expected consequence is that the reductions modulo distinct primes lose correlation.

## 6. Concrete proof strategy

A realistic route is:

1. Construct the global algebraic group generated by the generic matrices M(t).
2. Reduce this group modulo pq.
3. Prove the reduction map

\[
G(\mathbb Z[1/D])\to G(\mathbb F_p)\times G(\mathbb F_q)
\]

is surjective.

4. Apply expansion for the resulting finite group action.

The key new computation is therefore not another zero count. It is:

\[
\operatorname{Mon}(M) \pmod{pq}
=?
\]

and proving it equals the full product.

## 7. First computation to test

For small primes p,q:

- generate the subgroup of

\[
SL_2(\mathbb F_p)\times SL_2(\mathbb F_q)
\]

from the matrices M(0),...,M(pq-1);
- compute its index;
- compare with the index of the two individual monodromy groups.

Prediction:

\[
[SL_2(F_p)\times SL_2(F_q):G_{p,q}]=1
\]

for all tested pairs except small bad primes.

## Conclusion

The Q85 idea is not the final theorem as stated, because single-prime Katz monodromy does not automatically tensor across characteristics. But it identifies the correct wall-breaking object:

\[
\boxed{\text{the arithmetic product monodromy of the Apéry recurrence cocycle}.}
\]

If this product-monodromy theorem is proved, the cross-prime independence estimate follows by expansion/mixing. The distinctive Apéry feature is exactly the one absent for central binomial coefficients: a rank-two non-abelian cocycle with large monodromy.
