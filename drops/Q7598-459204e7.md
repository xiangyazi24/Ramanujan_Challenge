# 2. New lemma: the truncation seam actually closes `L_\pm B_p=0`

The pushed text identifies `B_p` as a truncation of the normalized formal solution.  A priori that proves the recurrence through its displayed truncation range.  Reciprocity closes the two top coefficients and gives the stronger polynomial identity below.

## Lemma 2.1 — exact polynomial-solution closure

For every prime `p>10T` (indeed for every relevant odd prime away from the obvious tiny denominators),
\[
\boxed{L_+B_p=0\quad\text{or}\quad L_-B_p=0\ \text{in }\mathbf F_p[x],}
\tag{2.1}
\]
according to the CFVZ branch.

### Proof

The Apéry reflection law makes `A_p` reciprocal of degree `p-1`, and `Delta` is reciprocal of degree two.  Hence from (1.1)
\[
\bigl(x^dB_p(1/x)\bigr)^2=B_p(x)^2.
\]
Since `F_p[x]` is a domain and `p` is odd,
\[
\boxed{x^dB_p(1/x)=\sigma B_p(x),\qquad \sigma\in\{\pm1\}.}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Writing
\[
B_p(x)=\sum_{j=0}^d u_jx^j,
\]
we get
\[
u_d=\sigma,\qquad u_{d-1}=\sigma u_1.
\tag{2.3}
\]

All coefficients of `L_\pm B_p` through degree `d` vanish by the formal recurrence.  Because `L_\pm` raises degree by at most two, only degrees `d+1,d+2` remain.

In the plus branch, `d=(p-1)/2=-1/2` in `F_p`.  The `x^(d+2)` coefficient is
\[
(2d+1)^2u_d=p^2u_d=0.
\tag{2.4+}
\]
The `x^(d+1)` coefficient is
\[
-2(68d^2+34d+5)u_d+(2d-1)^2u_{d-1}.
\]
At `d=-1/2`, the middle quadratic equals `5` and `(2d-1)^2=4`; using `u_1=5/2` and (2.3), this is
\[
-10\sigma+4\sigma\frac52=0.
\tag{2.5+}
\]

In the minus branch, `d=(p-3)/2=-3/2`.  The top coefficient is
\[
(2d+3)^2u_d=p^2u_d=0,
\tag{2.4-}
\]
and the next one is
\[
-2(68d^2+102d+39)u_d+(2d+1)^2u_{d-1}.
\]
At `d=-3/2`, the middle quadratic is `39` and `(2d+1)^2=4`; using `u_1=39/2`, this is
\[
-78\sigma+4\sigma\frac{39}{2}=0.
\tag{2.5-}
\]
Thus no seam residual survives.  QED.

### Audit

This is stronger than merely saying that the first `4T` coefficients agree with the characteristic-zero solution.  It is also very specific to the numerical Apéry operators: the cancellations `5,5/2` and `39,39/2` are exactly what closes the top seam.

It still does **not** by itself create a new low-mark equation: the closure happens at degree about `p/2`, far above the three marked positions.

\[
\frac{B''(\alpha)}{B'(\alpha)}
=2\sum_{\substack{\beta\in R_p\\\beta\ne\alpha}}

\tag{4.1}

This is a rigid Apéry-specific Bethe system for a set of about `p/2` points in `F_{p^2}^*`.

# 7. A split-specific mixed Frobenius identity

The one-variable identity
\[
S_{pm}=S_m
\tag{7.1}
\]
is **not** special to quadratic splitting: it holds for the roots of every polynomial in `F_p[x]`, since `S_m in F_p`.

Quadratic splitting gives a stronger two-index identity.  Because every root satisfies `alpha^(p^2)=alpha`, for all integers `a,b`,
\[
\boxed{
S_{a+pb}=S_{pa+b}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
Indeed,
\[
S_{a+pb}^p
=\sum_{\alpha\in R_p}\alpha^{pa+p^2b}
=\sum_{\alpha\in R_p}\alpha^{pa+b},
\]
and `S_{a+pb} in F_p`.

Together with reciprocity,
\[
S_{-m}=S_m.
\tag{7.3}
\]

This is the first clean symmetric identity here that actually uses the **orbit length at most two**, rather than merely the fact that `B_p` has coefficients in `F_p`.

But it also exposes the seam.  If `|a|,|b|=O(T)` and `p>10T`, then:

- `a=0` or `b=0` reduces to the generic Frobenius identity (7.1);
- `a=b` is tautological;
- `a=-b` becomes tautological after reciprocity (7.3);
- otherwise at least one of `a+pb`, `pa+b` has size comparable to `p`.

So the genuinely split-specific relation does not live inside the low prefix containing the three marks.

# 8. Hostile audit: why this still does not give `eta>0`

## 8.1 The exact seam closure is high, not low

Lemma 2.1 is genuinely stronger than the unitriangular truncation statement, but its two new cancellations occur at degrees `d+1,d+2 ~ p/2`.  It supplies no extra equation among `b_m` or `S_m` for `m<=4T`.

## 8.2 Newton does not manufacture rank

Equation (6.8) is an exact root-to-coefficient bridge, but it is triangular.  In the stable range the moment recurrence (6.4) already fixes `S_1,S_2,...` from the normalized branch.  Thus simply rewriting the three zeros by (6.9) does not create three new independent root variables; it recodes the same three Apéry divisibilities.

A proof must combine those marks with genuinely global information such as (5.6) or the nontrivial cases of (7.2).

## 8.3 Do not replace quadratic splitting by a norm-one assertion

From
\[
B_p\mid x^{p^2}-x
\]
we know only that Frobenius has cycles of length one or two on the roots.  Reciprocity gives another involution `alpha -> alpha^{-1}`.  The group generated by them can have four-element orbits.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{\alpha^p=\alpha^{-1}}
\]
is **not** a consequence of the supplied inputs.  That stronger assertion would put every quadratic root on the norm-one torus and would collapse (7.2) dramatically, but it is not banked and must not be smuggled in.

## 8.4 The complement identity is rootwise but its direct degree is huge

The quotient `C_p` in (5.2) has degree
\[
p^2-1-d = p^2+O(p).
\]
So a direct resultant/elimination with (5.6), without a further compression theorem, pays quadratic-in-`p` degree.  That is much larger than the `T` scale and cannot itself produce the required global height saving.

This is not a generic-ODE countermodel; it is the actual degree of the exact complement forced by the supplied splitting theorem.

## 8.5 Critical values do not directly mark roots

Equation (3.3) proves the Hasse roots avoid `Delta=0`.  Thus an argument based on the critical values `17 +/- 12 sqrt(2)` needs a nonlocal connection/monodromy identity.  Merely evaluating the root equation at a critical value is invalid.

## 8.6 What remains unused

The only genuinely new arithmetic datum in the supplied root package that is not already visible in the stable coefficient recurrence is the **rootwise Frobenius orbit structure**.  The symmetric shadow (7.2) first appears at long indices.  I do not have a valid descent taking a nontrivial long identity in (7.2) back to the three low marks with subquadratic aggregate height.

That is the precise block.
