# Separate-base-field independence: why it does not solve CRT independence

## Short answer

The proposed argument identifies a real structural fact, but it proves a weaker statement than the one needed. Different primes do give different geometric objects, but the desired event is not an ordinary Frobenius-distribution event. It is a defining-characteristic congruence event.

The factorization

\[
\Pr(m\bmod p\in Z_p,\ m\bmod q\in Z_q)
=\Pr(m\bmod p\in Z_p)\Pr(m\bmod q\in Z_q)+O(1/N)
\]

is actually a finite CRT identity only after the sets \(Z_p,Z_q\) are fixed. The hard part is proving useful information about the densities \(|Z_p|/p\) and correlations when summing over many primes.

## 1. What separate base fields really give

For fixed distinct primes p and q, the two congruence conditions are functions

\[
f_p(m)=1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p},\qquad f_q(m)=1_{m\bmod q\in Z_q}.
\]

Because p and q are coprime, the pair

\[
(m\bmod p,m\bmod q)
\]

runs uniformly over \(\mathbf F_p\times\mathbf F_q\) as m runs over a complete interval of length pq.

Therefore:

\[
\frac1{pq}\sum_{m<pq}f_p(m)f_q(m)
=\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\frac{|Z_q|}{q}.
\]

This is exact CRT independence. It does not require Katz, monodromy, or sheaves.

For arbitrary N:

\[
\sum_{m<N}f_p(m)f_q(m)
=N\frac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}+O(1).
\]

So the proposed statement with O(1/N) normalized error is true for fixed p,q.

## 2. Why this does not solve the GCD problem

The GCD problem requires controlling

\[
B(n)=\#\{p: p\mid b_n\}.
\]

The relevant random variable is

\[
S_X(m)=\sum_{X<p\le2X}(1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p}-|Z_p|/p).
\]

The second and third moments require sums over many primes:

\[
\sum_{p_1,\dots,p_k}
\sum_m\prod_i(1_{m\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i}}-|Z_{p_i}|/p_i).
\]

CRT gives independence for fixed distinct primes. The missing theorem is a uniform estimate over growing prime tuples, especially k=3.

The obstruction is not coupling between the finite fields. It is that the sets \(Z_p\) themselves are defined-characteristic zero sets.

## 3. The Mellin/Katz gap

Katz equidistribution gives information about

\[
T_p(\chi)/p^{3/2}
\]

as an l-adic trace value. It controls complex Frobenius distributions.

The zero condition is

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

or equivalently a crystalline/unit-root condition. It asks about reduction modulo the same prime as the characteristic of the base field.

There is no implication:

\[
\text{SU(2) distribution of }T_p(\chi)
\Rightarrow
\text{independence of }(T_p(\chi)\bmod p).
\]

The two primes being different does not repair this missing bridge.

## 4. What product monodromy DOES prove

A genuine consequence is a product geometric statement. For distinct primes p,q, the geometric Galois data of the two l-adic sheaves factors:

```
G_pq = G_p x G_q
```

under the usual independence hypotheses.

This supports an l-adic pair equidistribution theorem:

\[
(T_p(\chi_1),T_q(\chi_2))
\]

has product Sato--Tate distribution.

But the Apéry zero problem needs the reduction map

\[
\text{Frobenius trace}\rightarrow b_r\pmod p.
\]

That map is not controlled by the l-adic theorem.

## 5. The exact missing theorem

A sufficient statement would be a defining-characteristic analogue:

For fixed k and distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\),

\[
\#\{(r_1,\ldots,r_k):b_{r_i}\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\}
\]

has the expected product density with power-saving error after the CRT identification.

Equivalently, one needs a crystalline Mellin equidistribution theorem controlling unit-root traces modulo p.

For k=3 this would imply (HM)_3.

## 6. Final conclusion

The separate-base-field observation is correct but is not the missing argument. It proves:

1. fixed-prime CRT independence exactly;
2. l-adic product independence of different Mellin sheaves;
3. product Sato--Tate for complex traces.

It does not prove:

\[
\Pr(b_{m\bmod p}=0\ \&\ b_{m\bmod q}=0)
\approx
\frac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}
\]

in the sense needed for uniform prime averages, because the obstacle is not cross-prime geometry. The obstacle is the defining-characteristic distribution of each individual zero set.

The proposed route therefore removes a possible source of dependence, but it does not remove the central crystalline gap.
