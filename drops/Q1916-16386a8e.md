# Q1916: polynomial quotient compatibility audit

## 1. The two quotient identities

Let \(P=t^p-t\). The assumed inverse relation is

\[
kr=1+Ph,
\]

with \(k\) of degree \(p-1\), \(r\) even of degree at most \(p-3\), and \(h\) odd of degree at most \(p-4\). The kernel equation gives

\[
Hk=a k(t+1)+b k(t)+c k(t-1)=P(q_3t^3+q_1t).
\]

Because \(P(t+1)=P(t-1)=P(t)\), multiplying the kernel equation by shifted copies of \(r\) is legitimate in the polynomial ring, not only in the quotient ring of polynomial functions.

For example:

\[
 k(t+1)r(t+1)=1+P(t)h(t+1),
\]

and similarly for \(t-1\).

Therefore

\[
Hk\,r(t) =a(1+Ph(t+1))r(t)+b(1+Ph(t))r(t)+c(1+Ph(t-1))r(t)
\]

is an exact polynomial identity. This is the elimination seam: the kernel equation and inverse equation interact through the same factor \(P\).

## 2. Leading coefficient calculation

Write

\[
k=A t^{p-1}+\cdots .
\]

The given leading quotient identity gives

\[
q_3=-32A.
\]

The Riccati residual

\[
R=a r_-r+b r_-r_++c rr_+
\]

has leading term

\[
[t^{2p-2}]R=-32A_r^2,
\]

where \(A_r\) is the top coefficient of \(r\). Hence the leading term does not cancel. Since \(R=P Q_R\), its quotient has leading coefficient

\[
[t^{p-2}]Q_R=-32A_r^2.
\]

Thus divisibility by \(P\) alone does not force \(A_r=0\); it only determines the top quotient coefficient.

## 3. Trailing coefficients

The endpoint values give

\[
k(0)=-1,
\qquad k(1)=k(-1)=3,
\]

and

\[
r(0)=-1,
\qquad r(1)=r(-1)=1/3.
\]

Substitution into \(kr=1+Ph\) gives

\[
h(0)=h(1)=h(-1)=0,
\]

because \(P(0)=P(\pm1)=0\). Therefore \(h\) has the forced factor

\[
h=t(t^2-1)\tilde h.
\]

This is the first concrete restriction coming from the endpoint seam.

## 4. What can be eliminated

The equations imply

\[
H(kr)=H(1+Ph).
\]

Using \(Hk=P(q_3t^3+q_1t)\), the left side becomes

\[
P(q_3t^3+q_1t)r + k\,H(r),
\]

whereas the right side is

\[
H(1)+P H(h).
\]

Since \(H(1)\) is explicit, this gives a polynomial identity after moving all terms to one side and dividing by \(P\). However, the remaining unknowns are still the coefficients of \(k,r,h,q_1\).

The endpoint constraints only fix four evaluations and do not determine these coefficient systems uniquely.

## 5. Smallest remaining exact residual system

After eliminating \(k=(1+Ph)/r\), the open system is:

1. \(r\) even, degree \(\le p-3\).
2. \(h\) odd, degree \(\le p-4\), with \(h=t(t^2-1)\tilde h\).
3. The polynomial identity

\[
H\left(\frac{1+Ph}{r}\right)=P(q_3t^3+q_1t).
\]

4. The endpoint equations

\[
r(0)=-1,\qquad r(1)=1/3.
\]

5. The requirement that \(r\) is a unit in the polynomial-function algebra and corresponds to the canonical inverse.

No contradiction follows from leading/trailing coefficients alone. In particular, the nonzero coefficient \(-32A_r^2\) is a constraint on the Riccati quotient, not an impossibility.

## 6. Conclusion

The compatibility attack reduces the contradiction problem but does not close it. The exact obstruction is that the two quotient equations determine compatible quotient coefficients rather than forcing a coefficient to vanish. The smallest unresolved object is the above finite coefficient system for \(r,h,q_1\) together with the endpoint interpolation constraints.

A proof must introduce an additional invariant (for example a genuinely new reciprocity, Wronskian, or arithmetic constraint). The polynomial quotient identities by themselves do not yet contradict the assumed zero scalar.
