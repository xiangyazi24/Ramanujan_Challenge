# Audit of the characteristic-zero gcd attack

## Verdict

The stronger gcd

```text
gcd(b_n,B_n),   B_n = binom(n-1,floor(n/2)-1)
```

is not currently reduced to a proved subexponential estimate by the supplied ingredients. The all-exponent Gamma theorem reduces the original target to the same odd-quotient obstruction, but replacing `Gamma_n` by `gcd(b_n,B_n)` does not remove that obstruction automatically.

The first exact missing lemma is a classification of large prime divisibility of `b_n` that is independent of the Apéry divisibility already encoded in Gamma.

## Large primes p > sqrt(n)

Write

```text
n = qp+r,
```

with `p^2>n`, so `q<p`.

Lucas gives

```text
B_n = binom(n-1,M-1) mod p
```

as a finite digit condition. Since `M=floor(n/2)`, there is a carry-dependent condition on the base-p digits of `n-1` and `M-1`. Thus the large-prime support of `B_n` is not simply the support of `b_n`.

The Apéry sequence has the Mellit--Vlasenko digit factorization

```text
b_n = b_q b_r mod p.
```

Hence for `p>sqrt(n)`:

```text
p | b_n  iff  p | b_q or p | b_r.
```

The nuisance support of `gcd(b_n,B_n)` consists of primes satisfying simultaneously:

```text
p | b_q b_r
and
p | binom(n-1,M-1).
```

The second condition is a Kummer carry condition. The current Gamma argument only controls the smaller family where the odd quotient condition forces the same residue factor into the half-cutoff coefficients. It does not prove that every such simultaneous prime is already in Gamma.

## Why the Casoratian does not close the gap

The Apéry recurrence and companion/Casoratian provide linear independence and growth control of solutions, but they do not yield a characteristic-zero divisibility implication

```text
p | b_n and p | B_n => p is in a known small family.
```

A resultant argument would need an explicit polynomial relation whose height is subcritical and whose reduction detects the common prime. No such relation follows merely from the recurrence.

## Height calculation

The binomial term satisfies

```text
log B_n = log binom(n-1,floor(n/2)-1)
        = n log 2 + O(log n).
```

Therefore `B_n` has full exponential height. A direct estimate on `gcd(b_n,B_n)` cannot come from the size of `B_n`; it requires cancellation of its prime support.

Likewise

```text
log b_n = Theta(n)
```

so a subexponential gcd needs a structural support theorem, not a magnitude argument.

## Possible smaller missing lemma

A sufficient replacement would be:

> For every prime `p>sqrt(n)`, if `p | b_n` and `p | B_n`, then either `p` divides the Gamma support family (`q` odd and `p|b_r`) or `p` belongs to an explicitly bounded exceptional set whose total logarithmic contribution is `o(n)`.

This is strictly weaker than proving `log gcd(b_n,B_n)=o(n)` directly.

## Subcritical second integer question

A second integer could remove nuisance support only if it has:

1. characteristic-zero divisibility by all Gamma primes;
2. height `o(n)` or controlled enough that its gcd contribution is negligible;
3. a resultant relation excluding the extra Kummer-carry primes.

The current `B_n` fails the third requirement: it is a half-binomial cutoff with its own carry geometry.

## Final classification

The stronger gcd attack does not fail because of generic Dwork limitations. The exact obstruction is:

```text
large prime p:
 p | b_q b_r       (Apéry block law)
 p | B_n            (independent Kummer carry law)
```

with no proved implication reducing this intersection to the Gamma family.

Thus the remaining missing lemma is a simultaneous Apéry-block/Kummer-carry exclusion theorem, not another valuation estimate.
