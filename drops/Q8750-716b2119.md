# Cross-characteristic Frobenius correlations for LGTQ-3

## Summary

The K3/Frobenius viewpoint is useful conceptually, but it does not by itself supply the missing anti-concentration. The key obstruction is that the three congruences are not three samples of one fixed geometric object: they are evaluations of the same integral holonomic sequence in three different residue characteristics. The natural framework is therefore not a single K3 surface over one finite field, but a compatible system over arithmetic schemes together with the recurrence dynamics.

The correct conclusion is:

* cross-characteristic independence is plausible and is the right heuristic;
* existing Weil/Deligne/Sato--Tate technology does not directly prove the needed logarithmic-gap anti-correlation;
* the exploitable structure is likely a hybrid of the Frobenius interpretation with recurrence geometry and sieve estimates.

## 1. Joint geometric framework

For a fixed prime p, the condition

\[
b_d\equiv0\pmod p
\]

can be viewed as a Frobenius trace constraint on the fibre X_d over F_p. However, a transverse triple involves

\[
(X_{u_0}\bmod p_0,\ X_{u_1}\bmod p_1,\ X_{u_2}\bmod p_2).
\]

There is no ordinary fibre product over a common finite field because the characteristics differ. The closest object is an arithmetic moduli problem over Spec(Z):

\[
\mathcal X\to \mathbb A^1_{\mathbb Z},
\]

with three independent pullbacks along Spec(F_{p_i}). The triple condition is a codimension-three condition in a mixed-characteristic arithmetic scheme.

This is closer in spirit to Ekedahl sieve, large sieve for Frobenius, or compatible-system arguments than to a direct Weil bound.

## 2. Do close parameters and close primes correlate?

The recurrence relation gives a strong relation inside one characteristic:

\[
b_{u+h}=N_h(u)b_{u+1}-C_h(u)b_u.
\]

Thus if p divides b_u,

\[
b_{u+h}\equiv N_h(u)b_{u+1}\pmod p.
\]

This says that after conditioning on a zero at u modulo p, nearby values are controlled by one remaining Frobenius coordinate.

But replacing p by a nearby prime p-h/q does not preserve this information. Congruence modulo p and modulo p' are unrelated unless one has a lifting statement modulo pp'. The size of |p-p'|=O(log n) is too small to create algebraic dependence; it only creates a smooth variation of the coefficients.

Therefore the proximity gives a structured family of tests, not an automatic correlation.

## 3. Why Weil bounds are insufficient

A Weil bound controls the trace distribution for a fixed parameter as the prime varies. It gives estimates of the form

\[
|\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)-\text{mean}|\ll p^{3/2}.
\]

The LGTQ-3 problem asks something different:

\[
\#\{p: p\sim P,\ b_{u(p)}\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]

where both p and the parameter u(p) move together.

The required estimate is a moving-parameter Frobenius sieve estimate. Deligne's theorem controls individual fibres but does not control correlations among nearby fibres in varying characteristics.

## 4. Eigenvalue independence heuristic

The Frobenius eigenvalues should behave independently once both p and d vary, except for algebraic constraints coming from the compatible system. The recurrence relation gives the main known constraint.

A useful heuristic model is:

\[
\Pr(p|b_d)\approx \frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

For three transverse primes this predicts

\[
\prod_i\frac{|Z_{p_i}|}{p_i}.
\]

This is exactly the desired anti-concentration scale.

However, the heuristic is not a theorem because conditioning on the first zero changes the recurrence state. The sequence is not a random sequence of integers; it is a rank-two dynamical system.

## 5. Varying-parameter Sato--Tate

There are partial analogues:

* Sato--Tate for fixed fibres and varying p;
* Deligne equidistribution for families over finite fields;
* Katz-style monodromy results for parameter families.

The missing regime is the diagonal regime:

\[
p\to\infty,\qquad d=d(p)\to\infty.
\]

Here the parameter is not sampled independently; it is coupled to p through

\[
u=n-qp.
\]

A future proof would likely need a quantitative Katz large sieve statement for this diagonal family.

## 6. What breaks naive independence?

The naive estimate is too optimistic because the event is not random among all residues. There are three sources of dependence:

### (a) Orbit structure

Zeros are hits of a projective recurrence orbit. The set Z_p is not a random subset of size |Z_p|.

### (b) Nearby-gap conditioning

The triple is selected because

\[
u_1-\nu_0,
u_2-\nu_1=O(\log n).
\]

This is already a rare event and biases the sample.

### (c) Same integer n

The residues are generated by the same n. Thus one is not sampling three independent pairs (p,u), but one arithmetic trajectory through many moduli.

The correct target is therefore not independence, but a power-saving correlation estimate after conditioning on the recurrence orbit.

## 7. The logarithmic-gap family

Writing

\[
p_1=p_0-h_1/q+O(1/p_0),
\]

turns the problem into a moving-modulus question:

\[
b_u\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

\[
b_{u+h_1}\equiv0\pmod{p-h_1/q},
\]

and similarly for h_1+h_2.

The right object is a correlation sum

\[
\sum_p 1_{p|b_u}1_{p-h/q|b_{u+h}}1_{p-(h+h')/q|b_{u+h+h'}}.
\]

The recurrence alone cannot reduce this sum (as already shown by the saturation theorem). The missing ingredient must exploit that the three congruences occur in different residue fields.

A promising route is:

1. use the recurrence to encode the local zero condition as a Frobenius/projective-orbit condition;
2. use compatible-system monodromy to show different characteristics have low correlation;
3. combine with the existing gap-polynomial/resultant machinery to handle exceptional algebraic coincidences.

## Final assessment for LGTQ-3

The K3 connection identifies the correct source of randomness but does not yet give the required theorem. The strongest realistic statement currently is:

> Transverse triples should satisfy a cross-characteristic large-sieve estimate, but proving it requires a theorem about moving-parameter Frobenius correlations for the Apéry K3 compatible system.

The arithmetic analogue of independence exists conceptually (large sieve for Frobenius), but the Apéry problem lies in the difficult diagonal regime where both the Frobenius prime and the parameter move together. This is exactly the remaining gap after recurrence saturation.
