# Q8625 — literature audit for a bounded-conductor Apéry index trace function

## Verdict

I audited the public repository state at `main` commit
`4783c5345b8ff4dc32ff51b204646994e0c00513`, including
`problems/3.2/proof.tex`, `hasse_franel_descent.tex`,
`UNDERSTANDING_P32.md`, `AVENUES.md`, the Christol/diagonal question files,
and the prior moving-index conductor audit `drops/Q7231-dbbed013.md`.
I then checked the primary literature listed below.

The result is two-part.

1. **There is a published all-index finite-field hypergeometric formula.**
   Kalita–Chetry, Theorem 1.2, can be specialized for every
   `1 <= u <= p-2` to express the Apéry number `A_u mod p` as a Greene
   `{}_4F_3` value.  However, the hypergeometric argument is fixed at `1`;
   the index `u` occurs in the **multiplicative-character parameters**.
   Thus, as `u` varies, one samples one point from a changing family of
   hypergeometric sheaves.  Katz's bounded-conductor theorem applies to each
   fixed character tuple as a sheaf in the field argument, not to the map
   `u |-> A_u` on the additive index line.

2. **I found no published theorem representing the whole function**

   ```text
   u in F_p  |-->  A_u mod p
   ```

   **or its zero indicator**

   ```text
   u |--> 1[A_u = 0 mod p]
   ```

   as the trace of one middle-extension sheaf on the `u`-line with conductor
   bounded independently of `p`.  In particular, none of the diagonal,
   p-Lucas, Hasse-invariant, finite-field hypergeometric, or modular-form
   theorems found supplies the three-prime affine-shift correlation required
   by the project.

There is an additional warning: even a bounded-conductor trace realization of
`A_u` itself would not automatically realize the nonlinear predicate
`A_u=0`.  To invoke Deligne directly for zero correlations one would need a
bounded-conductor realization of the **centered zero indicator**, or another
uniform algebraic description of the zero fiber.  No such result was found.

## 1. What would count as the desired theorem

For a prime `p`, put

\[
Z_p=\{u\in\mathbf F_p:A_u=0\pmod p\},\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{|Z_p|}{p},
\]

and

\[
g_p(u)=1_{Z_p}(u)-\rho_p.
\]

The ideal input would be a family of middle-extension sheaves
`mathcal Z_p` on `A^1/F_p` such that

\[
t_{\mathcal Z_p}(u)=g_p(u)
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal Z_p)\le C
\]

for one absolute `C`, together with geometric independence of the affine
pullbacks that occur in the selector problem.  Standard
Grothendieck–Lefschetz plus Deligne would then give, for distinct affine forms
`L_j(u)=a_j u+b_j` and no geometrically trivial tensor constituent,

\[
\left|\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
 \prod_{j=1}^k g_p(L_j(u))\right|
 \ll_{C,k} p^{1/2}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

A weaker statement

\[
A_u=t_{\mathcal F_p}(u),\qquad
\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal F_p)=O(1),
\]

would control complete sums of the **values** `A_u`, but not (1.1).  In
general

\[
1_{A_u=0}=\frac1p\sum_{h\in\mathbf F_p}\psi(hA_u)
\tag{1.2}
\]

is useful only when `A_u` is a bounded-degree algebraic function of `u`, so
that the summands in (1.2) are controlled Artin–Schreier pullbacks.  If `A_u`
is merely the numerical trace of another sheaf, applying an additive
character to that trace is not a bounded-conductor sheaf operation.  Even in
the algebraic-function case, summing the `p` detectors in (1.2) loses a factor
unless a second cancellation theorem is available.

No theorem found reaches even the value-trace statement uniformly in the
additive index variable, and none reaches the stronger zero-indicator
statement.

## 3. The strongest positive result: an all-index hypergeometric formula, but in the wrong variable

Write the generalized Apéry sum used by Kalita and Chetry as

\[
\mathcal A(f_1,f_2,m,l,\lambda)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{f_2}
   \binom{f_1+j}{j}^{m}\binom{f_2}{j}^{l}\lambda^j.
\]

Our Apéry number is

\[
A_u=\mathcal A(u,u,2,2,1).
\]

Kalita–Chetry, *Congruences for generalized Apéry numbers and Gaussian
hypergeometric series*, Theorem 1.2, states that if
`p congruent 1 mod lcm(r,s)`, `0<a<r`, `0<b<s`, and `w=m+l`, then

\[
\mathcal A\!\left(\frac{a(p-1)}r,\frac{b(p-1)}s,m,l,\lambda\right)
\equiv
(-p)^{w-1}
{}_wF_{w-1}\!\left(
\begin{matrix}
\psi^a,\ldots,\psi^a,
\bar\xi^b,\ldots,\bar\xi^b\\
\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon
\end{matrix}
\middle|(-1)^l\lambda
\right)_p
\pmod p,
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `psi` has order `r`, `xi` has order `s`, the first character is repeated
`m` times, and the second is repeated `l` times.

Now take

\[
r=s=p-1,\qquad a=b=u,\qquad m=l=2,\qquad \lambda=1,
\]

and let `T` generate the multiplicative-character group of `F_p^*`.  For
`1<=u<=p-2`, (3.1) becomes, in the authors' Greene normalization,

\[
\boxed{
A_u\equiv
(-p)^3
{}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}
T^u,T^u,T^{-u},T^{-u}\\
\epsilon,\epsilon,\epsilon
\end{matrix}
\middle|1
\right)_p
\pmod p.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The omitted endpoints are harmless as identities: `A_0=1`, and the standard
reflection/Lucas laws determine the opposite endpoint.  Formula (3.2) is the
strongest genuine all-index finite-field hypergeometric formula I found.

It nevertheless has the wrong geometry for this problem.

* The hypergeometric **argument** is the constant `1`.
* The index `u` changes four multiplicative-character parameters.
* Katz's hypergeometric sheaf for a fixed tuple
  `(T^u,T^u,T^{-u},T^{-u}; epsilon,epsilon,epsilon)` has bounded rank and a
  bounded set of singularities, but it is a different sheaf when `u` changes.
* The exponent `u` naturally belongs to the dual cyclic group
  `Z/(p-1)Z`, not to the additive algebraic line `A^1(F_p)` on which the
  selector shifts `u=t+d` live.
* Replacing a character by `T^u(x)` amounts, after choosing a primitive
  element, to inserting a discrete logarithm.  This is not an algebraic
  coordinate change of bounded degree on the additive `u`-line.

Katz, *Exponential Sums and Differential Equations*, Section 8.4 and
Theorem 8.4.2, therefore gives square-root estimates when the **field
argument** varies for a fixed character tuple.  It does not give

\[
\sum_u 1_{A_{u+d_1}=0}\,1_{A_{u+d_2}=0}\,1_{A_{u+d_3}=0}
\]

when the character tuple itself varies with `u`.

For three different primes the mismatch is even sharper: (3.2) places the
same integer translation parameter simultaneously in character groups of
orders `p_1-1`, `p_2-1`, and `p_3-1`.  No single finite field or published
bounded-conductor sheaf family identified in the search couples those three
Mellin parameters.

The elementary classical identity

\[
A_u={}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}-u,-u,u+1,u+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1\right)
\tag{3.3}
\]

has exactly the same defect: `u` is a hypergeometric parameter, not the
argument.

## 4. Diagonal and constant-term geometry: genuine, but coefficient-indexed

Straub, *Multivariate Apéry numbers and supercongruences of rational
functions*, Theorem 1.1, proves that `A_n` is the diagonal coefficient of

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1}{(1-x_1-x_2)(1-x_3-x_4)-x_1x_2x_3x_4}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This places the generating series in the geometric class of diagonals and
implies algebraicity after reduction modulo every prime.  Straub also records
a constant-term-of-powers realization and the resulting Dwork/Lucas
congruences.

Neither formulation evaluates a fixed sheaf at the point `u`.

* In (4.1), `u` tells which multivariate coefficient to extract.
* In a constant-term formula `A_u=CT(Lambda^u)`, `u` is an exponent.  For a
  fixed `u`, Kummer/toric methods may express the moment geometrically; as `u`
  changes, the Kummer character or tensor power changes.
* A bounded cohomological dimension for each separate moment is not a
  bounded-conductor trace function in the moment index.

This is the exact distinction already encountered in (3.2): exponent/Mellin
parameters are not automatically algebraic point variables.

Adamczewski–Bell, *Diagonalization and rationalization of algebraic Laurent
series*, gives a useful quantitative warning.  For a fixed diagonal over the
integers, its reduction modulo `p` is algebraic of degree at most `p^C` and
height at most `C^2 p^{C+1}`, where `C` depends on the original rational or
algebraic presentation.  This is effective algebraicity, but not an
`O(1)`-degree or `O(1)`-conductor statement.

Rowland–Yassawi, *Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational functions*,
Theorem 2.1, constructs a finite automaton for the diagonal coefficients
modulo `p^alpha`.  Their theorem is prime-by-prime; it does not assert a
number of states bounded uniformly in `p`.  Indeed, their paper treats the
structure for fixed primes, and the later quantitative algebraicity results
allow polynomial or worse growth with `p`, not an absolute bound.

## 5. The exact p-Lucas algebraic equation is tautological on the first block

Gessel, *Some congruences for Apéry numbers*, Theorem 1, proves

\[
A_{np+r}\equiv A_nA_r\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
F_p(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}\bar A_nt^n,
\qquad
H_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}\bar A_rt^r.
\]

Equation (5.1) gives the exact functional equation

\[
F_p(t)=H_p(t)F_p(t^p)=H_p(t)F_p(t)^p.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `F_p(0)=1`,

\[
\boxed{F_p(t)^{p-1}=H_p(t)^{-1}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the algebraic equation contains the whole unknown polynomial
`H_p(t)`—whose coefficients are precisely the values whose zeros are being
counted.  For `0<=u<p`, the base-`p` expansion has one digit, so (5.1) imposes
no relation among the entries of this first block.

This is not merely a philosophical objection.  Given arbitrary digit weights

\[
h_0=1,h_1,\ldots,h_{p-1}\in\mathbf F_p,
\qquad H(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}h_rt^r,
\]

define

\[
F(t)=\prod_{j\ge0}H(t^{p^j}).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Then

\[
F(t)=H(t)F(t)^p,
\]

and if `n=sum n_jp^j`, its coefficient is

\[
a_n=\prod_j h_{n_j}.
\]

The first-block zero set

\[
\{1\le r<p:h_r=0\}
\]

can therefore be an arbitrary subset while the sequence has exactly the same
p-Lucas/Mahler algebraicity pattern.  This does not say that the Apéry seed is
arbitrary; it proves that **p-Lucas algebraicity by itself contains no theorem
that bounds it**.

Adamczewski–Bell make the limitation definitive.  In *On vanishing
coefficients of algebraic power series over fields of positive
characteristic*, Theorem 1.4, they prove that the coefficient-zero set of an
algebraic power series is p-automatic.  Immediately after the theorem they
record the converse equivalence:

\[
E\text{ is p-automatic}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
E=Z(f)\text{ for some algebraic power series in characteristic }p.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Hence no density, sparsity, or affine-correlation estimate follows from the
word “algebraic” alone.  Even rational series of degree one range from having
no zero coefficients to having almost every coefficient zero.

The recent Apéry-specific theorem shows that the complexity growth is real,
not just an artifact of general bounds.  Caruso–Fürnsinn–Vargas-Montoya–
Zudilin, *Galois groups of Apéry-like series modulo primes*, prove:

* **Theorem 1.1.**  If `p congruent 1,5,7,11 mod 24`, then
  `Gal(F_p(t,F_p)/F_p(t))` is the subgroup of squares in `F_p^*`, of order
  `(p-1)/2`; if `p congruent 13,17,19,23 mod 24`, it is all of `F_p^*`, of
  order `p-1`.
* **Theorem 1.2.**  There is `B_p(t) in F_p[t]` such that

  \[
  H_p(t)=B_p(t)^2
  \]

  in the first four residue classes, while

  \[
  H_p(t)=(t^2-34t+1)B_p(t)^2
  \]

  in the second four residue classes.

Because the extensions are Galois, Theorem 1.1 gives exact algebraic degree
`(p-1)/2` or `p-1`.  The mod-`p` Apéry generating series is therefore not a
uniformly bounded-degree algebraic function.

## 6. Hasse invariants and truncated periods: roots in the geometric parameter are not zero coefficients

The Stienstra–Beukers K3/formal-Brauer-group framework and its descendants
relate the Apéry Picard–Fuchs solution to arithmetic invariants of a family.
The relevant finite-characteristic object is a truncation such as

\[
H_p(t)=\sum_{u=0}^{p-1}A_ut^u,
\tag{6.1}
\]

viewed as a polynomial in the **geometric family parameter** `t` (or after a
rational pullback).  Calling (6.1), or a normalized version of it, a Hasse
invariant does not exchange its two roles:

* a sheaf or variety over the `t`-line controls evaluations `H_p(t_0)`;
* the set `Z_p` counts indices for which the coordinate `[t^u]H_p` is zero.

There is no general operation turning coefficient coordinates of a degree
`p-1` Hasse polynomial into values of an `O(1)`-conductor sheaf on an
`u`-line.

The current repository's exact Franel pullback makes this issue especially
transparent.  It derives

\[
K_p(x)^2=(1+x)^{p-1}H_p\!\left(\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x}\right)
       =\sum_{u=0}^{p-1}A_u\,
        x^u(1-8x)^u(1+x)^{p-1-u}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The basis on the right has `p` elements and depends on `p`; `A_u` is its
`u`-th coordinate.  Equation (6.2) is an exact Apéry-specific algebraic
reduction, but it is not a trace-function evaluation in `u`.  General
Deligne, Dwork, or monodromy estimates for the left side do not imply
anti-concentration of its coordinates in this moving basis.

Caruso et al. Theorem 1.2 is likewise a factorization of the polynomial in
`t`; it is not a theorem about which coefficients vanish.

## 7. Special modular-form congruences do not move the index

Ahlgren–Ono, *A Gaussian hypergeometric series evaluation and Apéry number
congruences*, Theorem 5, prove the Beukers central-index supercongruence

\[
A_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a(p)\pmod{p^2},
\tag{7.1}
\]

where `a(p)` is the `p`-th coefficient of the relevant weight-four
eta-product.  Their paper also evaluates a Gaussian `{}_4F_3(1)_p` and uses
modularity of a Calabi–Yau threefold.

This is a deep theorem, but (7.1) concerns one index.  It does not supply a
formula for arbitrary `u`, much less a bounded-conductor sheaf in `u` or
three-prime affine-shift correlations.  The same warning applies to other
congruences at rational fractions of `p-1`: finitely many special indices do
not control the full set `Z_p`.

## 8. What a genuine bounded-conductor zero sheaf would actually give

It is useful to record the strongest standard conclusion, because even it
would not by itself close the project's sparse three-prime gate.

Assume hypothetically that the centered zero indicators `g_p` are trace
functions of conductor at most `C`, with the needed affine geometric
independence.  For distinct primes `p_1,p_2,p_3`, put

\[
Q=p_1p_2p_3,
\]

let `I` be an integer interval of length `M<=Q`, and let `L_i(m)` be affine
forms modulo `p_i`.  Expanding

\[
1_{Z_{p_i}}=\rho_i+g_i
\]

and completing each periodic term gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{m\in I}
 \prod_{i=1}^3 1_{Z_{p_i}}(L_i(m))\\
&\quad=M\rho_1\rho_2\rho_3
 +O_C\!\left(
  \log Q
  \sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}}
  \left(\prod_{i\in S}p_i\right)^{1/2}
  \prod_{j\notin S}\rho_j
 \right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The term `S={1,2,3}` is

\[
O_C(Q^{1/2}\log Q).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Formula (8.1) follows from the square-root bound for every nonzero additive
Fourier coefficient and the `L^1` norm `O(q log q)` of an interval's Fourier
transform modulo a period `q`.

In the shell `p_i asymp X`, (8.2) is

\[
O_C(X^{3/2}\log X)
\]

for one prime triple.  For the physical interval `M=X^2`, the sparse main
term is typically of size

\[
X^2\rho_1\rho_2\rho_3,
\]

which is around `X^{-1}` when each `|Z_{p_i}|` is `O(1)`.  Thus the generic
Deligne error overwhelms the sparse main term even for a fixed prime triple.
After summing over about `(X/log X)^3` prime triples, the raw complete-sum
error is of order

\[
\frac{X^{9/2}}{\log^2 X},
\]

far above the desired `X^2 lambda_X^3` scale.

Therefore the missing theorem is stronger than “bounded conductor.”  One
would need both:

1. an index-variable zero detector of controlled complexity; and
2. a density-sensitive mixed-prime family estimate or large sieve that
   retains all three powers of `lambda_X`.

No published theorem found supplies item 1, and the standard trace-function
formalism alone would not supply item 2.

## 9. Exact logical status for the project

The following statements are supported by published theorems.

* `A_u` is a diagonal/constant-term coefficient of fixed geometric data.
* For each fixed `p`, the mod-`p` coefficient sequence is algebraic and
  p-automatic.
* The first truncation `H_p(t)` has the exact square/discriminant-times-square
  factorization of Caruso et al.
* Each `A_u`, for `1<=u<=p-2`, has the moving-character finite-field
  hypergeometric representation (3.2).
* Special indices such as `(p-1)/2` satisfy modular-form supercongruences.

None of these statements implies

\[
\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
 g_p(u+d_1)g_p(u+d_2)g_p(u+d_3)\ll p^{1/2}
\]

uniformly in the shifts, and none implies a three-different-prime analogue.
The obstruction is not that hypergeometric sheaves lack bounded conductor;
it is that the available bounded-conductor sheaf has the wrong variable.
The coefficient index appears as a coefficient label, an exponent, or a
moving character parameter.

Accordingly, the literature hunt does **not** close DSEL3 or its
full-defect translation form.  A claim that “Apéry numbers are
hypergeometric/diagonal/Hasse, hence Deligne applies in `u`” omits the central
quantifier change and is invalid.

The smallest honest new theorem would be something like:

> **Uniform Apéry index-zero family.**  Construct, for every sufficiently
> large prime `p`, a bounded- or `p^{o(1)}`-conductor object on the additive
> index line whose trace is the centered predicate `1[A_u=0]-|Z_p|/p`, prove
> affine-pullback independence, and then prove a mixed-prime sparse large
> sieve retaining the product of the three zero densities.

No such theorem was located in the published literature searched here.
