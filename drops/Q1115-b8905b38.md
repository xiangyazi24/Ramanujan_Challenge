# Q1115 audit: all-quotient shifted carrier and height

## 1. Verdict on the aggregate theorem

The large-prime support statement is essentially correct under the stated hypotheses, but the characteristic-zero height conclusion needs a correction. The identity

\[
 C_n\mid \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)
\]

is true only after inserting the extra factor \(H-h+1\) in the definition of \(A_{n,h}\); it is not a consequence of the shifted Newton coefficients alone.

For
\[
A_{n,h}=\gcd(\Gamma_{n,h},H-h+1),
\]
we have immediately
\[
A_{n,h}\mid H-h+1\mid H\le n.
\]
Therefore every shifted contribution with \(h>0\) is automatically an lcm divisor of \(1,\ldots,n\). The remaining term is
\[
\Gamma_{n,0}=\gcd_j\left(\binom n j\binom{n-j-1}{M-1-j}b_j\right).
\]

The claimed divisibility
\[
\Gamma_{n,0}\mid \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)
\]
requires the classical Apéry divisibility input
\[
\gcd_{0\le j<M}L_j(n)b_j\mid \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),
\]
not merely the binomial factors. The binomial part alone is not enough: a common prime power can come from the gcd with the Apéry values. Thus the theorem is valid only if the one-sided carrier divisibility lemma is imported/proved.

## 2. Large-prime radical

For \(p^2>n\), write
\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]
The Kummer analysis gives the correct support mechanism.

For the special shift satisfying
\[
p\mid(H-h+1),
\]
the Newton stencil has unit nodes exactly where the lower base-p digit is fixed to \(r\):

\[
j\equiv r\pmod p.
\]

Because \(q<p\), there are no higher carries. Hence all p-unit nodes are
\[
 j=ap+r
\]
inside the allowed interval.

Lucas then gives
\[
b_{ap+r}\equiv b_a b_r\pmod p.
\]
The gcd over unit nodes therefore vanishes modulo p exactly when all these multipliers vanish. The adjacent non-vanishing property
\[
(b_a,b_{a+1})\ne(0,0)
\]
prevents this except when
\[
p\mid b_r.
\]

Hence the support theorem for the selected shifts is sound:

\[
p\mid A_{n,h}\iff p\mid b_r.
\]

The parity split is also correct:

* \(h=0\) catches odd quotient \(q\).
* \(h=\lceil r/2\rceil+1\) catches even quotient \(q\).

Therefore
\[
\operatorname{rad}_{p>\sqrt n}(C_n)
=
\operatorname{rad}_{p>\sqrt n}(\{p:p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\}).
\]

## 3. Comparison with existing carriers

### One-sided carrier

The old carrier
\[
D_n=\operatorname{lcm}_{n/2<m\le n}\gcd(m,b_{n-m})
\]
uses the decomposition
\[
m=ap+r.
\]
It directly stores the quotient-one geometry.

The shifted carrier improves the organization:

* \(D_n\) has one factor per terminal index.
* \(C_n\) has one factor per Newton shift.

There is no general divisibility
\[
C_n\mid D_n
\]
or
\[
D_n\mid C_n.
\]
The reason is structural: \(D_n\) is a gcd of scalar Apéry values with integers, while \(C_n\) is a gcd of convolution coefficients.

A Smith-normal-form relation between the shifted Newton matrix and the diagonal matrix of Apéry values would be needed to compare them. No such relation follows from Lucas alone.

---

## 4. Comparison with the smooth Apéry carrier

The carrier
\[
S_n=\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),b_n)
\]
contains only the endpoint value \(b_n\).

It cannot dominate the shifted carrier because the shifted carrier deliberately extracts lower digits:
\[
b_{ap+r}\equiv b_a b_r\pmod p.
\]
The information is distributed over many residues. There are possible primes dividing a lower residue component but not appearing in \(b_n\).

Thus no exact inclusion
\[
C_n\mid S_n
\]
is expected.

---

## 5. Parity-split convolution carriers

A natural improvement is to separate
\[
q=2Q\quad\text{and}\quad q=2Q+1.
\]
The current construction already does the first useful parity split: the special shift moves the Newton window so that the missing even quotient becomes a terminal lcm factor.

Further splitting does not obviously reduce height because every additional split introduces another lcm component. Without a rank reduction in the Newton module, the height remains controlled by the ambient exponential lcm.

---

## 6. Can the height be improved below \(\exp(O(n))\)?

The proven bound is
\[
C_n\mid \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),
\]
so
\[
\log C_n\le \psi(n)=n+o(n).
\]

The desired statement
\[
\log C_n=o(n)
\]
would require proving that most prime powers in the ambient lcm disappear.

The current shifted Newton construction proves support elimination for large primes, but not exponent elimination. The obstruction is prime powers:

* Lucas controls whether \(p\) divides.
* Kummer controls the Newton coefficients modulo \(p\).
* Neither controls the exact valuation
  \[
  v_p(C_n).
  \]

A possible route would be a valuation-level Smith form:
\[
\nu_p(C_n)=O(\log n)
\]
for every prime p, together with a density estimate on surviving primes. No such identity is presently forced by the carrier construction.

---

## 7. First concrete obstruction

The missing step is not large-prime support; that part is resolved. The obstruction is the following valuation gap:

\[
\gcd(W_jb_j)\quad\text{can retain prime powers from several rows even when every large-prime occurrence is explained.}
\]

The adjacent Apéry non-vanishing lemma only gives
\[
\min(v_p(b_a),v_p(b_{a+1}))=0
\]
in the zero/nonzero sense. It does not imply
\[
\min(v_p(b_a),v_p(b_{a+1}))=0
\]
for higher valuations after convolution with Newton coefficients.

Therefore the final theorem is:

1. The all-quotient large-prime radical theorem is valid.
2. The lcm divisibility is valid only with the independent one-sided carrier divisibility lemma for \(\Gamma_{n,0}\).
3. No strict divisibility relation with the old carriers follows from current identities.
4. The remaining challenge is a valuation/Smith problem, not a support problem.

The shifted carrier resolves the nuisance-support obstruction but does not by itself prove the little-oh height bound.
