ANSWER Q7492 c575fb2c

# Horizontal two-digit Apéry expansion: exact mod p² law, explicit mod p³ carry decomposition, and a squarefree-radical no-go

## Verdict

There **is** a sharp, completely proved horizontal congruence modulo \(p^2\), and it is stronger and cleaner than ordinary Lucas:

\[
\boxed{
 b_{qp+r}\equiv b_q\bigl(b_r+p q\,b'_r\bigr)\pmod{p^2},
 \qquad 0\le q,r<p,
}\tag{H2}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
 b'_r
 =2\sum_{s=0}^{r}
 \binom rs^2\binom{r+s}s^2
 \bigl(H_{r+s}-H_{r-s}\bigr).
}\tag{D}
\]

Here \(b'_r\in\mathbb Q\); for a base-\(p\) digit \(r<p\), its denominator is prime to \(p\), so its reduction modulo \(p\) is well-defined. This is Gessel's 1982 Theorem 4, restated and audited in Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler (2021, Theorem 8), and generalized to all sporadic Apéry-like sequences by Straub (2024, Theorem 1.3).

The important point for the present GCD problem is what happens on the target event \(p\mid b_r\). Writing

\[
 c_{p,r}:=b_r/p\in\mathbb Z,
\]

(H2) becomes

\[
\boxed{
 \frac{b_{qp+r}}p
 \equiv b_q\bigl(c_{p,r}+q b'_r\bigr)\pmod p.
}\tag{T2}
\]

Thus, when \(p\nmid b_q\),

\[
 p^2\mid b_{qp+r}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 c_{p,r}+q b'_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{LIFT}
\]

This is a genuine second-order horizontal invariant, but it is **not a second target-selective congruence for the squarefree problem**. The squarefree target requires only \(p\mid b_{qp+r}\), and once \(p\mid b_r\), that condition is already automatic modulo \(p\); it imposes no vanishing condition on the affine quotient \(c_{p,r}+q b'_r\). Moreover \(c_{p,r}=b_r/p\) itself depends on the target prime. Therefore (H2), by itself, produces no new characteristic-zero integer/rational carrier \(F(q,r)\) with

\[
 p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow p\mid F(q,r)
\]

that is independent of the original divisor condition.

I also derive below an implementation-ready **full mod-\(p^3\) decomposition** directly from the binomial formula. It has a clean no-carry second-order term plus three explicit one-carry boundary sums. That refinement does not change the squarefree verdict: on \(p\mid b_r\), the extra \(p^2\)-coefficient controls lifting to \(p^3\), not membership of \(p\) in the radical, and its carry pieces are explicitly \(p\)-dependent.

So the tactical outcome is a **scoped no-go** for this route: horizontal expansion gives excellent information about *valuation lifting at one prime*, but no second vanishing law forced by the squarefree target.

# 2. Exact carry audit for a summand

Fix

\[
 n=qp+r,\qquad k=ap+s,\qquad 0\le q,r,a,s<p.
\]

The two binomial factors in \(T_n(k)\) are

\[
 B_1=\binom{qp+r}{ap+s},
 \qquad
 B_2=\binom{(q+a)p+r+s}{ap+s}.
\]

Since each is squared, Kummer's theorem immediately makes the carry geometry unusually favorable.

## 2.1 First binomial

For \(k\le n\),

\[
 v_p(B_1)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&s\le r,\\
 1,&s>r.
 \end{cases}
\tag{C1}
\]

In the second case necessarily \(a<q\).

## 2.2 Second binomial

For \(B_2=\binom{n+k}{k}\), Kummer counts carries in the addition of \(n\) and \(k\). Put

\[
 c_0=\mathbf1_{r+s\ge p},
 \qquad
 c_1=\mathbf1_{q+a+c_0\ge p}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{v_p(B_2)=c_0+c_1.}\tag{C2}
\]

Therefore

\[
 v_p(T_n(k))=2v_p(B_1)+2v_p(B_2).
\]

### Consequence modulo \(p^2\)

Every summand with **any carry at all** is already divisible by \(p^2\). Hence modulo \(p^2\) only the simultaneous no-carry rectangle survives:

\[
 s\le r,\qquad r+s<p,\qquad a\le q,\qquad q+a<p.
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
 \alpha=\min(q,p-1-q),
 \qquad
 \rho=\min(r,p-1-r),
\]

we only need

\[
 0\le a\le\alpha,\qquad0\le s\le\rho.
\tag{R}
\]

### Consequence modulo \(p^3\)

A carry summand contributes modulo \(p^3\) **only if the total carry count among the two binomials is exactly one**. It then contributes \(p^2\) times the square of an explicit carry quotient modulo \(p\). Two or more carries give a multiple of \(p^4\) and disappear modulo \(p^3\).

This leaves exactly three one-carry strata, listed in Section 5.

# 3. The no-carry binomial expansion through p² in the bracket

The following elementary expansion is the basic brick.

## Lemma 3.1

Let \(p\ge5\), \(0\le u\le v<p\), and in the applications below let \(0\le B\le A<p\). Then

\[
\binom{Ap+v}{Bp+u}
\equiv
\binom AB\binom vu
\left[1+pL+\frac{p^2}{2}(L^2-Q)\right]
\pmod{p^3},
\tag{B3}
\]

where

\[
 L=A H_v-BH_u-(A-B)H_{v-u},
\tag{L}
\]

and

\[
 Q=A^2H_v^{(2)}-B^2H_u^{(2)}-(A-B)^2H_{v-u}^{(2)}.
\tag{Q}
\]

### Direct derivation

Exactly,

\[
\frac{\binom{Ap+v}{Bp+u}}{\binom{Ap}{Bp}\binom vu}
=
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^v(1+pA/i)}
 {\prod_{i=1}^u(1+pB/i)
  \prod_{i=1}^{v-u}(1+p(A-B)/i)}.
\]

All denominators here are prime to \(p\). Expanding the logarithm gives

\[
 \log R=pL-\frac{p^2}{2}Q\pmod{p^3},
\]

hence

\[
 R=1+pL+\frac{p^2}{2}(L^2-Q)\pmod{p^3}.
\]

Finally use (J). No Dwork or crystalline statement enters.

# 4. The surviving no-carry Apéry summand

On the rectangle (R), apply Lemma 3.1 to

\[
 B_1=\binom{qp+r}{ap+s}
\]

with \((A,B,v,u)=(q,a,r,s)\), and to

\[
 B_2=\binom{(q+a)p+r+s}{ap+s}
\]

with \((A,B,v,u)=(q+a,a,r+s,s)\).

Define

\[
\boxed{
E_{q,r}(a,s)
=q\bigl(H_{r+s}-H_{r-s}\bigr)
+a\bigl(H_{r+s}+H_{r-s}-2H_s\bigr).
}\tag{E}
\]

The second-harmonic combination is

\[
\boxed{
G_{q,r}(a,s)
=(q+a)^2H_{r+s}^{(2)}
-2a^2H_s^{(2)}
-(q-a)^2H_{r-s}^{(2)}.
}\tag{G}
\]

The \(H_r\) and \(H_r^{(2)}\) terms cancel between the two binomials. Squaring the product gives the compact summand formula

\[
\boxed{
T_{qp+r}(ap+s)
\equiv
T_q(a)T_r(s)
\left[1+2pE_{q,r}(a,s)
+p^2\bigl(2E_{q,r}(a,s)^2-G_{q,r}(a,s)\bigr)\right]
\pmod{p^3}.
}\tag{S3}
\]

This is the useful local horizontal Taylor expansion. All harmonic denominators in (E)--(G) are prime to \(p\), because on (R)

\[
0\le r-s\le r+s\le p-1.
\]

Modulo \(p^2\), simply drop the final \(p^2\)-term.

# 5. The three explicit one-carry p³ boundary strata

We need one elementary carry quotient.

## Lemma 5.1 (one low borrow/carry quotient)

Let \(0\le v<u<p\) and \(A>B\ge0\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac1p\binom{Ap+v}{Bp+u}
\equiv
(-1)^{u-v-1}\binom AB
\frac{A-B}{u\binom{u-1}{v}}
\pmod p.
}\tag{CQ}
\]

Indeed, start at lower index \(Bp+v\) and raise it from \(v\) to \(u\):

\[
\binom{Ap+v}{Bp+u}
=
\binom{Ap+v}{Bp+v}
\prod_{j=1}^{u-v}
\frac{(A-B)p-j+1}{Bp+v+j}.
\]

The \(j=1\) numerator supplies the unique factor \(p(A-B)\); reducing all remaining factors modulo \(p\) gives (CQ).

Now define the following sets. Empty ranges contribute zero.

## 5.2 Carry in B1 only

Conditions:

\[
\mathcal C_1:
\quad
0\le a\le\min(q-1,p-1-q),
\qquad
r+1\le s\le p-1-r.
\]

Here \(B_1\) has its one carry, while \(B_2\) has none. Put

\[
\boxed{
J_1(q,r;a,s)=
(-1)^{s-r-1}\binom qa
\frac{q-a}{s\binom{s-1}{r}}
\binom{q+a}{a}\binom{r+s}{s}.
}\tag{J1}
\]

Then

\[
 T_{qp+r}(ap+s)\equiv p^2J_1(q,r;a,s)^2\pmod{p^3}.
\tag{J1T}
\]

## 5.3 Low carry in B2 only

Conditions:

\[
\mathcal C_L:
\quad
0\le a\le\min(q,p-q-2),
\qquad
p-r\le s\le r.
\]

This range exists only for large \(r\). The standard low digit of \(n+k\) is

\[
 v=r+s-p<s,
\]

and the high digit is \(q+a+1<p\), so there is exactly one carry. Put

\[
\boxed{
J_L(q,r;a,s)=
\binom qa\binom rs
(-1)^r\binom{q+a+1}{a}
\frac{q+1}{s\binom{s-1}{r+s-p}}.
}\tag{JL}
\]

Then

\[
 T_{qp+r}(ap+s)\equiv p^2J_L(q,r;a,s)^2\pmod{p^3}.
\tag{JLT}
\]

The sign is \((-1)^{p-r-1}=(-1)^r\) for odd \(p\).

## 5.4 High carry in B2 only

Conditions:

\[
\mathcal C_H:
\quad
p-q\le a\le q,
\qquad
0\le s\le\rho.
\]

This exists only when \(q\ge(p+1)/2\). There is no low carry, but \(q+a\ge p\). The needed high-digit quotient is

\[
\boxed{
\frac1p\binom{q+a}{a}
\equiv
\frac{(-1)^q}{a\binom{a-1}{p-q-1}}
\pmod p.
}\tag{HQ}
\]

It is just (CQ) applied to \(\binom{p+(q+a-p)}{a}\). Hence set

\[
\boxed{
J_H(q,r;a,s)=
\binom qa\binom rs\binom{r+s}s
\frac{(-1)^q}{a\binom{a-1}{p-q-1}}.
}\tag{JH}
\]

Then

\[
T_{qp+r}(ap+s)\equiv p^2J_H(q,r;a,s)^2\pmod{p^3}.
\tag{JHT}
\]

## 5.5 Full implementation-ready mod-p³ formula

Combining the no-carry rectangle and the three one-carry strata gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
b_{qp+r}\equiv{}&
\sum_{a=0}^{\alpha}\sum_{s=0}^{\rho}
T_q(a)T_r(s)
\Bigl[1+2pE_{q,r}(a,s)
+p^2\bigl(2E_{q,r}(a,s)^2-G_{q,r}(a,s)\bigr)\Bigr]\\
&+p^2\sum_{(a,s)\in\mathcal C_1}J_1(q,r;a,s)^2
+p^2\sum_{(a,s)\in\mathcal C_L}J_L(q,r;a,s)^2\\
&+p^2\sum_{(a,s)\in\mathcal C_H}J_H(q,r;a,s)^2
\pmod{p^3}.
\end{aligned}
}\tag{FULL3}
\]

There are no omitted carry types: if \(B_1\) carries and \(B_2\) also carries, or if \(B_2\) has both its low and high carry, then \(T_n(k)\) is divisible by at least \(p^4\).

I have not found in the checked primary sources a published uniform two-digit mod-\(p^3\) factorization as compact as (H2). Formula (FULL3) is instead a direct binomial/Jacobsthal/Kummer expansion; it is deliberately written in a form that can be independently implemented term by term.

# 6. Collapse modulo p²: Gessel's exact horizontal derivative law

Reducing (S3) modulo \(p^2\) and summing gives

\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv
\sum_{a=0}^{\alpha}\sum_{s=0}^{\rho}
T_q(a)T_r(s)\bigl(1+2pE_{q,r}(a,s)\bigr)
\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{SUM2}
\]

Write

\[
U_r(s)=H_{r+s}-H_{r-s},
\]

\[
V_r(s)=H_{r+s}+H_{r-s}-2H_s.
\]

Then \(E=qU_r(s)+aV_r(s)\). The exact finite harmonic cancellation underlying Gessel's summation is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s=0}^{r}T_r(s)V_r(s)=0.
}\tag{HV}
\]

Also

\[
\boxed{
 b'_r=2\sum_{s=0}^rT_r(s)U_r(s).
}\tag{DU}
\]

The second identity is immediate by differentiating the natural polynomial/gamma summand

\[
\left[\binom zs\binom{z+s}s\right]^2
\]

with respect to \(z\) and then setting \(z=r\). Terms \(s>r\) have a double zero, hence zero first derivative. Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler explicitly identify this with the derivative of the entire Apéry interpolation.

For the modular truncation, terms with \(r+s\ge p\) have \(T_r(s)\) divisible by \(p^2\); even though \(H_{r+s}\) then contains \(1/p\), their contribution to the first-order harmonic sum is still divisible by \(p\). Thus the active range \(s\le\rho\) suffices modulo \(p\). The same observation applies to the truncated \(a\)-sum for \(b_q\).

Using (HV)--(DU) in (SUM2) gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
 b_{qp+r}\equiv b_q\bigl(b_r+p q b'_r\bigr)\pmod{p^2}.
}\tag{H2 again}
\]

This is not a conjectural Dwork relation. It is Gessel's proved Theorem 4. Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler, Theorem 8, state it for every prime, every digit \(0\le r<p\), and all integer quotients; Straub's Theorem 1.3 gives the corresponding Gessel--Lucas law for all 15 sporadic Apéry-like sequences.

A useful conceptual reading from Straub is that ordinary Lucas modulo \(p\) is a one-state digit law, while (H2) is an explicit **two-state** modulo-\(p^2\) law, the new state being the formal derivative \(b'_r\).

# 7. Exact target specialization p | b_r

Assume now

\[
1\le r\le p-2,
\qquad p\mid b_r.
\]

(The endpoints are disposed of in Section 8.) Put

\[
b_r=p c_{p,r}.
\]

Then (H2) is

\[
b_{qp+r}
\equiv
p b_q\bigl(c_{p,r}+q b'_r\bigr)
\pmod{p^2},
\]

which yields (T2).

There are two cases.

### If p does not divide b_q

\[
 v_p(b_{qp+r})\ge2
\iff
c_{p,r}+q b'_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

So the affine expression is a sharp **lifting criterion**.

### If p divides b_q

Both base-\(p\) digits are zero digits for the Lucas system, and the right side is already divisible by \(p^2\). The squarefree target was already satisfied before the derivative term entered.

## A concrete sanity check that the derivative is not target-forced

Take \(p=5,r=1\). Then

\[
b_1=5,
\qquad b'_1=12.
\]

Thus

\[
5\mid b_1,
\qquad 5\nmid b'_1.
\]

So even at the most elementary target digit, target membership does **not** force the derivative state to vanish.

# 8. Endpoint and carry audits

## 8.1 r = 0

Here \(b_0=1\) and \(b'_0=0\). Thus (H2) becomes

\[
b_{pq}\equiv b_q\pmod{p^2}.
\]

For \(p\ge5\), Gessel's stronger endpoint supercongruence is

\[
\boxed{b_{pq}\equiv b_q\pmod{p^3}.}\tag{EP0}
\]

In (FULL3), the active rectangle has only \(s=0\), while the \(\mathcal C_1\) and, for large \(q\), \(\mathcal C_H\) boundary sums remain. Their total cancellation against the no-carry \(p^2\)-coefficient is an excellent implementation checksum for (FULL3).

For the radical problem \(r=0\) is never dangerous because \(p\nmid b_0=1\).

## 8.2 r = p - 1

Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler prove for \(p\ge5\)

\[
\boxed{
 b_{pq+p-1}\equiv b_q\pmod{p^3}.
}\tag{EPM1}
\]

In particular

\[
 b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^3},
\]

so \(r=p-1\) is also never a dangerous lower digit.

In (FULL3), \(\rho=0\), so the active range has only \(s=0\); the low-carry stratum \(\mathcal C_L\) supplies the terms \(1\le s\le p-1\), and \(\mathcal C_H\) may also occur. Again the published endpoint theorem gives a strong independent checksum on the entire carry bookkeeping.

## 8.3 Interior carry boundary s > r

This is precisely the one-carry \(B_1\) stratum \(\mathcal C_1\). It is invisible modulo \(p^2\) because \(B_1^2\) supplies \(p^2\), but it is essential modulo \(p^3\).

## 8.4 Interior carry boundary r + s >= p

If the high digit does not also carry, this is \(\mathcal C_L\) and contributes \(p^2J_L^2\). If \(q+a+1\ge p\), there are two carries in \(B_2\), so the squared term is \(0\pmod{p^3}\).

## 8.5 High carry q + a >= p with no low carry

This is \(\mathcal C_H\). It is easy to miss if one expands only the low digits. The quotient (HQ) makes its contribution explicit.

# 9. Why the p² law does not create a second squarefree target carrier

This is the main tactical question.

Suppose the radical target prime \(p\) is present because

\[
p\mid b_r,
\qquad r=n\bmod p.
\]

The mod-\(p^2\) law reads

\[
b_n=p b_q\bigl(c_{p,r}+q b'_r\bigr)+p^2R
\]

for some \(p\)-integral \(R\).

The squarefree condition is only

\[
p\mid b_n.
\]

After substituting \(p\mid b_r\), that condition has become an identity: the displayed right side visibly contains one factor \(p\), regardless of the value of

\[
c_{p,r}+q b'_r\pmod p.
\]

Therefore no elimination step can legitimately conclude

\[
p\mid b'_r,
\qquad
p\mid(c_{p,r}+q b'_r),
\]

or any other new vanishing merely from squarefree target membership. The affine factor becomes relevant **only if one additionally asks for \(p^2\mid b_n\)**.

There is a second obstruction to using \(c_{p,r}+q b'_r\) as a fixed characteristic-zero carrier:

\[
c_{p,r}=b_r/p
\]

is defined by dividing by the very prime one is trying to compress. It is not a function of \((q,r)\) alone. Thus even the exact lift quotient is prime-specific.

The one genuinely characteristic-zero new state, \(b'_r\), is not forced to vanish by \(p\mid b_r\), as the \((p,r)=(5,1)\) example already demonstrates.

This is a logical no-go for deriving a second target carrier **from (H2) alone**. It does not say that some further Apéry identity coupling \(b_r\) and \(b'_r\) can never exist.

---

# 10. Why going to p³ does not repair the squarefree mismatch

The full expansion (FULL3) can schematically be collapsed on a target digit into

\[
 b_{qp+r}
 =p b_q\bigl(c_{p,r}+q b'_r\bigr)
 +p^2\Xi_p(q,r)
 \pmod{p^3},
\tag{SC3}
\]

where \(\Xi_p(q,r)\) is explicitly obtained from

- the no-carry second-order term \(2E^2-G\), and
- the three carry sums \(J_1^2,J_L^2,J_H^2\).

Again, squarefree membership only asks for the first factor \(p\). It imposes **no condition at all** on \(\Xi_p(q,r)\). The new coefficient governs whether the already-present prime lifts another level.

Moreover, unlike \(b'_r\), the carry part of \(\Xi_p\) visibly depends on the characteristic through thresholds and denominators such as

\[
p-r,\qquad p-q,\qquad
\binom{s-1}{r+s-p},\qquad
\binom{a-1}{p-q-1}.
\]

So the mod-\(p^3\) refinement is even less naturally a single characteristic-zero carrier shared by many target primes.

This is the scoped no-go:

> **Finite horizontal expansion to higher p-adic order refines the valuation of an already selected prime. The all-index radical asks which distinct primes are selected at all. Unless an additional theorem forces one of the higher-order coefficients to vanish on every zero digit, higher p-adic precision does not shrink the squarefree support.**

That distinction is exactly analogous to the difference between controlling \(v_p(b_n)\) and controlling the support set \(\{p:p\mid b_n\}\).

# 11. Relation to the proved p² Lucas-support theorem

Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler define the digits \(d\) for which the ordinary product law itself lifts to \(p^2\):

\[
 b_{pn+d}\equiv b_db_n\pmod{p^2}
 \quad\text{for all }n.
\]

By (H2), this is equivalent to

\[
 p b'_d\equiv0\pmod{p^2},
\]

i.e. \(b'_d\equiv0\pmod p\), and their Theorem 9 characterizes the same digit set by the reflection congruence

\[
 b_d\equiv b_{p-1-d}\pmod{p^2}.
\]

This is an informative local structure theorem, but it does not save the radical route. A dangerous zero digit need not belong to this special derivative-zero set. In particular, \(d=1\) for \(p=5\) is a zero digit but \(b'_1\not\equiv0\pmod5\).

So “mod-\(p^2\) Lucas digit” and “mod-\(p\) zero digit” are genuinely different local conditions.

# 12. Independent implementation recipe

For a direct checker, no large exact harmonic rationals are required.

## Mod p² checker

For \(p\ge5\), \(0\le q,r<p\):

```text
input p,q,r
compute bq = b_q mod p^2
compute br = b_r mod p^2
rho = min(r,p-1-r)

# derivative only needed mod p
U = 0 mod p
for s = 0..rho:
    Tr = C(r,s)^2 * C(r+s,s)^2 mod p
    h = H[r+s] - H[r-s] mod p
    U += Tr*h
bprime = 2*U mod p

rhs = bq * (br + p*q*bprime) mod p^2
check b_{p*q+r} mod p^2 == rhs
```

Here `H[j] mod p` is built from inverses of \(1,\dots,p-1\). The truncation at \(\rho\) is safe modulo \(p\) for the derivative for the valuation reason explained above.

On a target \(br\equiv0\pmod p\), compute

```text
c = (br mod p^2) / p   mod p
lift = c + q*bprime    mod p
```

If \(b_q\not\equiv0\pmod p\), then `lift == 0` iff \(p^2\mid b_{pq+r}\).

## Mod p³ checker

Precompute

```text
H1[j] = sum_{i=1}^j 1/i mod p^2
H2[j] = sum_{i=1}^j 1/i^2 mod p
```

for \(0\le j<p\). In the active rectangle, compute `E mod p^2` and `G mod p`, use (S3), then add each carry contribution `p^2 * J^2` with `J mod p` from (J1), (JL), (JH). Every denominator displayed in a `J` is a nonzero residue modulo \(p\) on its stated range.

Two mandatory regression checks are

```text
b_{p*q}       == b_q mod p^3
b_{p*q+p-1}   == b_q mod p^3
```

for every tested \(q\), \(p\ge5\). These test precisely the carry boundaries that a naive no-carry expansion misses.

# 14. Tactical conclusion for the all-index radical

The strongest clean horizontal fact presently available at two digits is not merely Lucas; it is the exact Gessel--Lucas derivative law (H2). It deserves to be in the project's toolbox because it gives a precise local tangent state at every digit.

But for the stated squarefree target, its logical content is the wrong kind:

\[
\text{target selection }(p\mid b_r)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{one factor }p\text{ in }b_{qp+r},
\]

while the derivative and second-order carry data answer only

\[
\text{does that selected }p\text{ lift to }p^2,p^3,\ldots ?
\]

They do not force a second characteristic-zero divisibility condition shared by the target primes. In particular:

- \(b'_r\) is characteristic-zero but not target-forced;
- \(b_r/p\) is target-forced data but is prime-dependent;
- the mod-\(p^3\) carry correction is explicitly characteristic-dependent;
- endpoint supercongruences concern \(r=0,p-1\), which are not dangerous zero digits anyway.

Therefore this horizontal expansion does **not** by itself compress

\[
\operatorname{rad}\gcd\!\left(b_n,\prod_{p\le n}p\right).
\]

A productive next theorem would have to add genuinely new information, for example a proved implication on actual zero digits of the form

\[
p\mid b_r
\Longrightarrow
p\mid F(r)
\]

for a characteristic-zero \(F(r)\) of sufficiently small height and not divisible for tautological reasons by \(b_r\), or a mixed-prime correlation theorem for the pairs \((b_r,b'_r)\). The binomial horizontal expansion itself supplies no such implication.

**No claim of the all-index Apéry GCD conjecture is made.**
