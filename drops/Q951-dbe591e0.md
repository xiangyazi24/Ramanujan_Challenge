# Q951 — exact gap decomposition of the affine-folded AAT carrier

## Verdict

The proposed split into singleton and repeated-index prime mass is **not currently a valid reduction to a strictly weaker singleton lemma**. The collision classification is exact at the congruence level, but the repeated-index contribution is not controlled by the stated divisor bounds alone. The obstruction is that one shell prime may hit many folded indices \(s<X\), and the affine congruence only controls pairwise gaps after choosing two such indices; it does not give a uniform bound on the number of zeros of \(A_s\pmod p\).

The correct reduction is therefore a classification plus a remaining many-hit obstruction.

## 1. One-prime collision equations

Fix \(m\) with

\[
X^2<m\le 2X^2,
\]

and a shell prime \(p\in(X,2X]\). A folded index \(s<X\) contributes when

\[
p\mid A_s,
\qquad
p\mid (m-s)(m+s+1).
\]

Write the two affine branches as

\[
D_s: m\equiv s\pmod p,
\]

and

\[
R_s: m\equiv -s-1\pmod p.
\]

For two distinct indices \(r,s\) using the same prime \(p\), there are four cases.

## 2. Direct/direct collisions

Assume

\[
m\equiv r\pmod p,
\qquad
m\equiv s\pmod p.
\]

Subtracting gives

\[
r-s\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Hence

\[
p\mid r-s.
\]

Because \(0<|r-s|<X<p\), this forces

\[
r=s,
\]

contradicting distinctness.

Therefore there are no nontrivial direct/direct collisions in a shell.

## 3. Reflected/reflected collisions

Assume

\[
m\equiv-r-1\pmod p,
\qquad
m\equiv-s-1\pmod p.
\]

Again subtraction gives

\[
r-s\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The same size argument gives

\[
r=s.
\]

Therefore reflected/reflected collisions are also impossible for distinct folded indices.

## 4. Direct/reflected collisions

Assume

\[
m\equiv r\pmod p,
\qquad
m\equiv-s-1\pmod p.
\]

Then

\[
r+s+1\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Hence

\[
p\mid r+s+1.
\]

Since

\[
1\le r+s+1\le 2X-1<2p,
\]

there is at most one possible multiple of \(p\). Thus the collision condition is exactly

\[
r+s+1=p.
\]

The reflected/direct case is identical.

## 5. Central factor \(2m+1\)

The two affine factors coincide exactly when

\[
s\equiv -s-1\pmod p,
\]

or

\[
2s+1\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

This is the central folded index.

For the fixed \(m\)-equation the corresponding affine branches satisfy

\[
m-s\equiv m+s+1\pmod p
\]

iff

\[
2m+1\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Therefore the exceptional central prime set is

\[
p\mid 2m+1.
\]

These primes must be separated from ordinary direct/reflected collisions.

## 6. Exact decomposition of prime mass

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X(m)=\{p\in(X,2X]:\exists s<X,\ p\mid A_s,\ p\mid(m-s)(m+s+1)\}.
\]

Split

\[
\mathcal P_X(m)=\mathcal P_{\rm sing}\sqcup\mathcal P_{\rm rep}\sqcup\mathcal P_{\rm cen},
\]

where:

* \(\mathcal P_{\rm sing}\): exactly one folded index \(s\) contributes;
* \(\mathcal P_{\rm rep}\): at least two distinct folded indices contribute;
* \(\mathcal P_{\rm cen}\): the central branch \(p\mid2m+1\).

The logarithm becomes

\[
\log A_X(m)=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm sing}}\log p
+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm rep}}\log p
+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm cen}}\log p
+\text{(multiplicity terms from higher powers)}.
\]

The classification gives:

* direct/direct: impossible;
* reflected/reflected: impossible;
* direct/reflected: requires \(p\mid r+s+1\);
* central: requires \(p\mid2m+1\).

## 7. Why the repeated part does not close

A tempting estimate is to count pairs \((r,s)\) satisfying

\[
p\mid r+s+1.
\]

The number of possible pairs for one prime is only \(O(X)\), and the divisor condition on \(r+s+1\) is useful.

However this does **not** bound

\[
\#\{s<X:p\mid A_s,\ p\mid(m-s)(m+s+1)\}
\]

for a fixed \(p\).

The missing implication would be a uniform bound of the form

\[
\#\{s<X:p\mid A_s\}\ll X^\theta
\]

for every shell prime \(p\), or an equivalent Apéry-specific zero-distribution statement. Such a statement is not available from the banked common-zero/gap carrier alone.

The near-return carrier controls pairs after they are known to share a prime:

\[
p\mid A_r,\ p\mid A_s
\Longrightarrow
p\mid C(r,s),
\]

where the carrier height is controlled by the gap. But this controls the arithmetic size of a chosen gap; it does not prevent many indices from sharing the same prime.

## 8. Central contribution

The central part is harmless. Since

\[
p\mid2m+1,
\qquad p>X,
\]

and

\[
2m+1\le4X^2+1,
\]

there are only

\[
O(1)
\]

shell prime divisors. Therefore

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm cen}}\log p=O(\log X).
\]

## 9. Verifier

The finite verifier for the proposed split should check only the exact affine collision identities:

```text
for p in (X,2X]:
  collect s<X with
       p | A_s
       p | (m-s)(m+s+1)

  for every pair r != s:
       if both direct:
           verify p | r-s  (impossible since |r-s|<p)
       if both reflected:
           verify p | r-s  (impossible since |r-s|<p)
       if mixed:
           verify p | r+s+1

  separately check p | 2m+1 central branch
```

This verifies the classification but does not verify the desired bound on repeated mass.

## Final obstruction

The requested conclusion

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\rm rep}}\log p
=O(X^{2/3+\epsilon})
\]

is not a consequence of divisor bounds plus the banked near-return carriers. The missing ingredient is a theorem controlling the number of Apéry zeros modulo one large prime. Without such a theorem, the repeated-index mass cannot be discarded, and the singleton lemma is not yet strictly weaker.

Therefore the exact reduction is valid, but the claimed repeated-mass elimination is the first obstruction.
