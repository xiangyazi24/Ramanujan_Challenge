# Q1932: cyclic cofactor target

## 1. Circulant criterion for the existence of R

Let
\[
A=\mathbf F_p[x]/(x^m-1),\qquad C=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_jx^j.
\]
Multiplication by C is represented in the standard basis by the circulant matrix \(M_C\). The equation
\[
CR=1+\mu(1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1})
\]
is the linear system
\[
M_C r=b_\mu,
\]
where
\[
b_\mu=(1+\mu,\mu,\ldots,\mu)^T.
\]
Therefore existence is equivalent to
\[
b_\mu\in \operatorname{im}M_C.
\]
If \(\det M_C\ne0\), this is equivalent to ordinary invertibility and
\[
r=M_C^{-1}b_\mu.
\]
The entries are cofactors:
\[
r_i=\frac{\det M_C^{(i)}}{\det M_C},
\]
where \(M_C^{(i)}\) is obtained by replacing column i by \(b_\mu\).

The coefficient \(x^{m-1}\) of R is therefore a Cramer cofactor divided by \(\det M_C\), and the constant term condition \(R(0)=-1\) is
\[
\det M_C^{(0)}+\det M_C=0.
\]

## 2. Splitting off the augmentation ideal

The element
\[
N=1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1}
\]
is the projector direction for evaluation at x=1. The algebra decomposes as
\[
A=\mathbf F_p\cdot N\oplus I,
\]
where \(I=(x-1)\) is the augmentation ideal.

On the first factor C acts by multiplication by
\[
C(1)=3-\theta.
\]
On I the relevant matrix is the restriction
\[
M_C|_I.
\]
Hence for \(\theta\ne3\), existence is equivalent to
\[
(3-\theta)\det(M_C|_I)\ne0.
\]

For \(\theta=3\), the scalar factor vanishes. The right side becomes
\[
1+\mu N,
\]
with \(\mu=2\). Its augmentation component must vanish because C has zero eigenvalue on the N-direction. Thus one needs the separate compatibility condition
\[
1+2m=0\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p,
\]
or the equation has no solution. Therefore \(\theta=3\) cannot be treated by the inverse formula.

## 3. Cofactors on the augmentation restriction

Choose a basis \(v_1,\ldots,v_{m-1}\) of I. Let
\[
\widetilde M_C
\]
be the matrix of multiplication by C in this basis. Then
\[
\det \widetilde M_C=\prod_{\zeta^m=1,\zeta\ne1}C(\zeta).
\]
The coefficients of R modulo the augmentation component are ratios of minors of \(\widetilde M_C\). In particular both the leading coefficient and the normalized constant coefficient are specified cofactors of this matrix.

## 4. Combining with the sparse source equation

The equation
\[
HC=-\frac\theta2\delta_0
\]
becomes another circulant linear system
\[
M_C h=-\frac\theta2 e_0.
\]
Writing the unknown coefficient vector of H as h gives a block system
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
M_C& e_0\\
\ell^T&0
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}h\\s\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}-\frac\theta2e_0\\0\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(\ell\) imposes the normalization coming from the trace-zero condition.

The Schur complement is
\[
-s\,\ell^TM_C^{-1}e_0.
\]
Consequently the final obstruction is a single bordered determinant:
\[
\Delta_C=
\det\begin{pmatrix}
M_C&e_0\\
\ell^T&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Nonvanishing of \(\Delta_C\) gives the required contradiction. It is not automatic from circulant structure; it requires proving that this particular minor does not vanish.

## Conclusion

The exact determinant condition is:

- for \(\theta\ne3\):
\[
(3-\theta)\det(M_C|_I)\ne0
\]
is the invertibility condition for the required R;

- for \(\theta=3\): the augmentation component gives a separate compatibility test and Cramer inversion is invalid;

- the target arithmetic statement reduces to the nonvanishing of the explicit bordered determinant \(\Delta_C\) coupling the sparse-source equation with the normalization cofactor.

No automatic invertibility claim is valid: the remaining task is exactly the proof that this explicit determinant is nonzero.
