# The family mechanism: deterministic Apery cocycle mixing beyond sqrt(p)

## Executive verdict

No checked theorem in the finite-field dynamics, expander, or trace-function literature gives the required COINC estimate for the Apery schedule.

The obstruction is sharper than "Bourgain-Gamburd assumes random generators." On every regular nonwrapping interval, the untwisted non-autonomous cocycle is a coboundary. After a time-dependent change of coordinates, its transfer operator is exactly the ordinary shift in the clock variable. Therefore the natural operator on

```text
clock x P^1(F_p)
```

has no state-space mixing to exploit. Its low clock frequencies have eigenvalues arbitrarily close to 1 when H=o(p). A constant expander gap is impossible for this operator, independently of the special form of P(u).

There is a second correction. The projective orbit

```text
pi(n)=[b_n:c_n]
```

is not itself a first-order PGL_2 walk. The PGL_2 matrices M_u evolve the Riccati state of one scalar solution. Exact Casoratian values require the fundamental frame, or an anchored two-point extension. Thus an operator only on P^1(F_p) x clock does not contain enough information to recover Delta_{r,h}.

The exact useful operator is the twisted upper-triangular kernel on the index set

```text
(L_{t,H} f)(r)
  = H^(-1/2) sum_{1<=h<=H, r+h<=M}
      e_p(t Delta_{r,h}) f(r+h).
```

With the unnormalized l2 inner product,

```text
B_t(H)=sqrt(H) <1,L_{t,H}1>.
```

Strong COINC is implied by the Ramanujan-size matrix-coefficient bound

```text
|<1,L_{t,H}1>| << sqrt(p)
```

for every t!=0. A usual constant spectral gap would only give O(p), which is useless. At the minimal p^(4/3) scale H about p^(2/3), the weaker coefficient size p^(2/3+epsilon) in all-value l2 average is enough.

The proposed short-block gluing does have a rigorous local part: for a fixed number of blocks of length L, complete Weil-Deligne estimates give square-root cancellation, with constants growing like the total rational complexity O(kL). It fails when the number of blocks grows. Consecutive blocks all depend on the same base variable r, so their joint law has only p samples, not p^k independent samples. The cocycle identity factors the same long product; it does not create fresh variables or entropy. After k blocks, the conductor has grown to O(kL). At kL>sqrt(p), the standard complete-sum estimate is again trivial.

The exact first decisive lemma worth attacking is a prefix-graph restriction theorem: bounded-complexity short-block observables should remain square-root orthogonal after restriction to the single deterministic graph of long Apery prefixes, uniformly in prefix length m<=p^(2/3). The full-state version is within standard orthogonality/Deligne technology and resembles Ostafe-Shparlinski's average-over-all-initial-states theorem. The graph-restricted version is new. Its named obstruction is:

```text
conductor growth under deterministic prefix-graph pullback.
```

Pulling a bounded-complexity short-block observable back along the actual prefix graph produces a one-variable rational function of degree O(m). Standard Weil then gives O(m sqrt(p)), which is useless precisely when m>sqrt(p).

So the verdict is:

```text

NO untwisted cocycle spectral gap.
YES to a precise new target: deterministic prefix-graph sampling,
or equivalently a twisted block large sieve for the Casoratian labels.
```

## 1. Exact scale and what COINC must provide

Put

```text
S_H={(r,h):1<=h<=H, 1<=r<=M-h},
A_H=#S_H=H M-H(H+1)/2.
```

Write

```text
m_H(a)=#{(r,h) in S_H:Delta_{r,h}=a},
N_coinc(H)=sum_{a in F_p} m_H(a)^2,
E_H=N_coinc(H)-A_H^2/p.
```

Parseval gives exactly

```text
sum_{t!=0}|B_t(H)|^2=p E_H.                         (1.1)
```

Strong COINC is

```text
E_H<=K A_H.                                          (1.2)
```

It implies

```text
Z_H:=sum_{h<=H} C_h
 <= A_H/p+sqrt(K A_H)
 << H+sqrt(pH).                                       (1.3)
```

The already-proved block argument gives

```text
F_p <= ceil(M/H) (M+2 Z_H).                          (1.4)
```

If H=p^alpha and strong COINC holds at that one scale, then

```text
F_p << p^(2-alpha)+p^(3/2-alpha/2).                  (1.5)
```

For alpha<1, the first term is larger. Therefore:

```text
alpha>1/2 gives a new exponent below 3/2;
alpha=2/3 gives F_p<<p^(4/3);
alpha=1 gives the exponent-level E1 bound p^(1+epsilon).
```

The absolute-K statement is stronger than needed for p^(4/3). At H about p^(2/3), it is enough to prove

```text
E_H <<_epsilon p^(2+epsilon),                        (1.6)
```

or equivalently

```text
sum_{t!=0}|B_t(H)|^2 <<_epsilon p^(3+epsilon).       (1.7)
```

Strong COINC would instead give E_H<<pH=p^(5/3), so it has about p^(1/3) more saving than the minimal one-scale target.

This distinction matters when specifying the spectral estimate: the first theorem need not reach an absolute K.

## 2. What Bourgain-Gamburd expansion actually uses

### 2.1 Primary source

Jean Bourgain and Alex Gamburd,
"Uniform expansion bounds for Cayley graphs of SL_2(F_p),"
Annals of Mathematics 167 (2008), 625-642.

Primary article and PDF:

```text
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2008/167-2/p07
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v167-n2-p07.pdf
https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2008.167.625
```

Theorem 1, printed pp. 626-627, concerns the Cayley graphs obtained by reducing one fixed finite set S in SL_2(Z). The hypothesis is that the subgroup generated by S is non-elementary.

Theorem 2, printed p. 627, concerns generators chosen independently and uniformly at random.

Theorem 3, printed pp. 627-628, assumes a fixed symmetric generating set with logarithmic girth.

The analytic mechanism is visible in Section 2. The probability measure

```text
mu_S=|S|^(-1) sum_{g in S} delta_g
```

is repeatedly convolved. Proposition 2, printed p. 631, is an l2-flattening statement for a symmetric probability measure that is already spread out and does not concentrate on proper subgroups.

### 2.2 Why the Apery schedule does not enter

On a regular index u, normalize

```text
S_u=(u+1)^(-3) M_u in SL_2(F_p).
```

This scalar normalization does not change the projective action. A deterministic schedule applies

```text
S_r,S_{r+1},...,S_{r+h-1}
```

in exactly one order. At one step, the relevant measure is the Dirac mass delta_{S_u}. Convolution of Dirac masses remains a Dirac mass:

```text
delta_{S_{r+h-1}}*...*delta_{S_r}
 = delta_{S_{r+h-1}...S_r}.
```

There is no flattening. In particular, Bourgain-Gamburd Proposition 2 cannot even begin: the l-infinity norm is 1.

One can form the empirical block measure

```text
mu_L=(1/p) sum_r delta_{S_{r+L-1}...S_r}.            (2.1)
```

It is conceivable that mu_L is spread out and generates SL_2(F_p). If independent samples were drawn from mu_L, Bourgain-Gamburd technology might become relevant. But the actual q-block product uses

```text
G_L(r),G_L(r+L),...,G_L(r+(q-1)L),                  (2.2)
```

with one common r. Its law is not mu_L^{*q}. Proving that (2.2) behaves like independent sampling is already the missing family theorem.

Thus the phrase "Bourgain-Gamburd needs i.i.d. generators" is directionally correct but incomplete. The exact failure is absence of convolution entropy.

## 3. Polynomial dynamics over F_p: the closest primary theorems

### 3.1 Autonomous triangular systems

Alina Ostafe and Igor Shparlinski,
"On the Degree Growth in Some Polynomial Dynamical Systems and Nonlinear Pseudorandom Number Generators,"
Mathematics of Computation 79 (2010), 501-511.

Primary source:

```text
https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3884
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.3884
https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-09-02271-6
```

Their systems are fixed autonomous triangular polynomial maps in m+1 affine variables. Theorem 4, printed p. 6, bounds exponential sums along one purely periodic orbit by

```text
p^(alpha_{m,nu}) N^(1-beta_{m,nu}).                  (3.1)
```

Optimizing the fixed moment parameter nu shows that a nontrivial estimate requires, in the limiting exponent, roughly

```text
N >= p^(m+1/2+epsilon).                              (3.2)
```

This is a deduction from their displayed alpha and beta. For m=1 it begins around p^(3/2), far beyond the length-p regime here. More basically, their iteration is one fixed triangular polynomial system, not a PGL_2 cocycle with a changing clock.

### 3.2 Distinct polynomial systems: a genuine non-autonomous theorem

Alina Ostafe and Igor Shparlinski,
"Pseudorandom Numbers and Hash Functions from Iterations of Multivariate Polynomials,"
Cryptography and Communications 2 (2010), 49-67.

Primary source:

```text
https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.4519
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0908.4519
https://doi.org/10.1007/s12095-009-0016-0
```

This paper really does permit a different polynomial system at every step. Equation (5) and Lemma 1, printed pp. 4-5, define and control the composition of a sequence F_k of special triangular systems.

The closest result is Theorem 8, printed p. 15. For permutation polynomial systems F_k, it estimates the square of the exponential sum after summing over every initial vector w in F_p^(m+1). Its bound is

```text
A(N,p)=N p^(m+1)                         if N<=p^(1/(m+1)),
A(N,p)=N^2 p^(m(m+2)/(m+1))              otherwise.    (3.3)
```

Corollary 9, also printed p. 15, obtains discrepancy for all but O(epsilon p^(m+1)) initial values.

Theorem 10, printed pp. 15-17, treats full vectors under an additional common-last-multiplier hypothesis, again through an average over every initial vector.

This is the most relevant positive precedent found in the audit. It confirms that a deterministic schedule can be handled when one is allowed to average over the whole initial-state space and exploit exact finite-field orthogonality.

It does not give the needed result here for three separate reasons:

1. Its maps belong to a special triangular affine class, not the Apery rational/frame cocycle.
2. The theorem averages over all p^(m+1) initial states.
3. The Apery problem concerns one distinguished initial frame, a set of density about p^(-2) or smaller in the natural frame torsor.

Specializing an average-over-all-initial-states theorem to that one frame is exactly the missing graph-restriction problem.

### 3.3 Autonomous orbit-intersection results

The broader Ostafe-Shparlinski orbit literature and related work of Chang and others controls fixed autonomous polynomial or rational maps, often by algebraic independence and degree growth of iterates. Those arguments require iterates f^k of one map. They do not supply an estimate uniform in the ordered product

```text
M_{r+h-1}...M_r
```

when h grows with p.

I found no checked primary theorem whose hypotheses are a deterministic consecutive polynomial schedule in PGL_2(F_p), one distinguished initial state, and length H>sqrt(p).

This is a bounded literature verdict, not a claim that no such statement could exist under another terminology.

## 4. Fixed-h trace-function estimates and their honest range

### 4.1 The direct bound

For fixed h, D1 gives

```text
Delta_{r,h}=N_h(r)/D_h(r),
D_h(r)=product_{j=1}^h(r+j)^3.                       (4.1)
```

After cancellation, this is a rational function with degree and number of poles O(h). If the additive phase is not of Artin-Schreier form, the complete Weil-Deligne bound has the shape

```text
sum_{r in F_p, regular} e_p(t Delta_{r,h})
 << h sqrt(p).                                        (4.2)
```

The literal nonwrapping interval 1<=r<=p-2-h omits only O(h) residues from a complete regular sum. Therefore no completion logarithm is needed here:

```text
sum_{r=1}^{p-2-h} e_p(t Delta_{r,h})
 << h sqrt(p)+h.                                      (4.3)
```

For an arbitrary short subinterval in r, ordinary completion would introduce a log(p) factor. That is a different problem.

Summing (4.3) by the triangle inequality gives

```text
B_t(H)<<H^2 sqrt(p)+H^2.                             (4.4)
```

Since A_H is about pH, (4.4) is nontrivial only for

```text
H<<sqrt(p).                                           (4.5)
```

This is the best directly applicable one-variable theorem. It exactly reproduces the campaign wall.

The non-Artin-Schreier condition in (4.2) is not automatic uniformly in h modulo p. A useful proof must also control cancellations of the denominator poles after reduction. This is a project-specific algebraic gate, not a consequence of the general theorem.

### 4.2 Short sums of fixed trace functions

Fouvry, Kowalski, Michel, Raju, Rivat, and Soundararajan,
"On short sums of trace functions,"
arXiv:1508.00512.

Primary source:

```text
https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00512
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.00512
```

Theorem 1.1, printed p. 2, gives a nontrivial estimate just above sqrt(m) for a periodic function whose sup norm and normalized Fourier-transform sup norm are bounded.

Corollary 1.3, printed p. 4, applies this to fixed rational and fixed trace functions over F_p on intervals of length p^(1/2) beta(p), with beta(p)->infinity.

The critical word is fixed. In the Apery family, the conductor of the Artin-Schreier sheaf attached to Delta_{r,h} grows at least linearly with h. Thus the constant in the trace-function estimate is not uniform when h is p^(2/3).

### 4.3 Recent bilinear trace-function theorems

Etienne Fouvry, Emmanuel Kowalski, Philippe Michel, and Will Sawin,
"Bilinear forms with trace functions,"
arXiv:2511.09459, current arXiv version dated March 22, 2026.

Primary source:

```text
https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09459
```

Theorem 1.3 in Section 1.2 gives type I and type II estimates for kernels

```text
K(m^b n^c)
```

where K is the trace function of one gallant and light l-adic sheaf. The constants depend on the sheaf complexity. Remark 1.2 explicitly notes that the dependence produced by their quantitative sheaf machinery is not polynomial in general.

These results are powerful below the Polya-Vinogradov range, but they do not fit the current phase:

```text
K_h(r)=e_p(t Delta_{r,h})
```

because the sheaf changes with h, its complexity grows with h, and the dependence on (r,h) is not a fixed monomial pullback K(r^b h^c).

Thus recent Sawin-related trace-function technology does not supply the desired growing-family estimate.

## 5. The untwisted cocycle transfer operator is gauge-trivial

This is the main structural audit.

### 5.1 Prefix gauge

Work on a regular interval and use the determinant-one matrices S_u defined above. Fix a left endpoint r_0 and set

```text
Q_{r_0}=1,
Q_r=S_{r-1}...S_{r_0}                                (r>r_0).
```

Then

```text
S_{r+h-1}...S_r=Q_{r+h} Q_r^(-1).                   (5.1)
```

Consider the h-averaging operator on functions on clock x P^1(F_p):

```text
(T_H F)(r,x)
 = (1/H) sum_{1<=h<=H} F(r+h,G_h(r)x),               (5.2)
```

with zero boundary convention if r+h leaves the regular interval.

Make the time-dependent change of variables

```text
F_tilde(r,y)=F(r,Q_r y).                              (5.3)
```

Using (5.1), equation (5.2) becomes exactly

```text
(T_H_tilde F_tilde)(r,y)
 = (1/H) sum_{1<=h<=H} F_tilde(r+h,y).                (5.4)
```

The state y is frozen. All matrix dynamics has disappeared.

This is not special to Apery. Any invertible deterministic cocycle over a line is a coboundary after choosing prefix coordinates.

### 5.2 Spectrum

On a cyclic clock model, the clock Fourier mode e_p(kr) has eigenvalue

```text
lambda_k(H)=(1/H) sum_{h=1}^H e_p(kh).                (5.5)
```

Each lambda_k has multiplicity at least |P^1(F_p)| because y is arbitrary. For k=1 and H=o(p),

```text
1-|lambda_1(H)|=O((H/p)^2).                           (5.6)
```

At H=p^(2/3), the gap is only O(p^(-2/3)). There is no p-independent spectral gap. On the non-cyclic interval, truncated low-frequency clock modes give the same approximate obstruction.

The singular clock value u=-1 prevents a global invertible cycle, but it does not help: every nonwrapping window used in S_H is gauge-trivial.

### 5.3 Consequence

A theorem saying that the matrices S_u generate a large subgroup, or that the set {S_u} defines an expander when random generator choices are allowed, cannot imply mixing of the deterministic schedule.

The arithmetic content is entirely in the special observable and in the special prefix graph, not in the untwisted cocycle action.

## 6. P^1 is not the full state for Delta

For one scalar solution y, the Riccati point

```text
x_n=[n^3 y_{n-1}:y_n]
```

evolves projectively under M_n. But

```text
pi(n)=[b_n:c_n]
```

is a row of a fundamental solution matrix. It does not evolve from pi(n) alone by M_n.

Define the fundamental frame

```text
Y_n = [[b_{n-1},c_{n-1}],
       [b_n,    c_n    ]].                            (6.1)
```

Then

```text
Y_{n+1}=A_n Y_n,
A_n=[[0,1],[-n^3/(n+1)^3,P(n)/(n+1)^3]],             (6.2)
```

and the Casoratian identity gives

```text
det Y_n=1/n^3.                                       (6.3)
```

For

```text
A_{r,h}=A_{r+h-1}...A_r,
```

define on the determinant-fixed frame torsor

```text
phi_h(r,Y)
 = det(row_2(Y),row_2(A_{r,h}Y)).                    (6.4)
```

Then

```text
Delta_{r,h}=phi_h(r,Y_r).                            (6.5)
```

Therefore an exact transfer formalism for the value Delta must live on one of the following:

```text
a determinant-fixed GL_2 frame torsor;
an anchored pair (v_r,Y_{r+h});
a two-point extension carrying both the anchor and current state.
```

An operator only on P^1 x clock forgets the scalar normalization needed by the additive character e_p(t Delta).

## 7. The exact twisted operator and the required gap

Let R={1,...,M}, extend functions by zero outside R, and use the unnormalized inner product

```text
<f,g>=sum_{r=1}^M f(r) conjugate(g(r)).
```

For t!=0 define

```text
(L_{t,H} f)(r)
 = H^(-1/2) sum_{1<=h<=H, r+h<=M}
     e_p(t Delta_{r,h}) f(r+h).                       (7.1)
```

Then

```text
B_t(H)=sqrt(H) <1,L_{t,H}1>.                         (7.2)
```

### 7.1 Strong COINC in operator language

From (1.1) and (7.2), strong COINC is equivalent to

```text
sum_{t!=0}|<1,L_{t,H}1>|^2
 <= K p A_H/H
 << K p^2.                                            (7.3)
```

A sufficient pointwise estimate is

```text
|<1,L_{t,H}1>|<<sqrt(p)                              (t!=0).    (7.4)
```

Since ||1||_2^2 about p, (7.4) is relative cancellation p^(-1/2). If one insists on deriving it from a full operator-norm bound, one would need

```text
||L_{t,H}||_{2->2}<<p^(-1/2),                        (7.5)
```

which is much stronger than an expander gap of the form 1-c. The matrix coefficient (7.4), not the full norm (7.5), is the sensible target.

### 7.2 Minimal p^(4/3) target

At H=p^(2/3), the minimal condition (1.7) becomes

```text
sum_{t!=0}|<1,L_{t,H}1>|^2
 <<_epsilon p^(7/3+epsilon).                          (7.6)
```

A sufficient uniform estimate is

```text
|<1,L_{t,H}1>|<<_epsilon p^(2/3+epsilon).             (7.7)
```

Thus the first new energy theorem needs a power-decaying twisted matrix coefficient, but not necessarily the full Ramanujan size sqrt(p).

### 7.3 Powers of L_t do not encode longer gaps

There is another trap. The kernel of L_{t,L}^q contains phases

```text
Delta_{r_0,h_1}+Delta_{r_1,h_2}+...+Delta_{r_{q-1},h_q}.
```

The desired long-gap phase is

```text
Delta_{r,h_1+...+h_q}.
```

These are not equal. The determinant sequence satisfies a second-order linear recurrence, not an additive cocycle law. Therefore a spectral gap for powers of the short-block twisted operator does not automatically control the long-gap phase.

One can augment the state by the two consecutive determinant values so that block evolution is linear. That repairs the semigroup bookkeeping, but the block matrices are still evaluated on one deterministic clock orbit, so the prefix-graph obstruction remains.

## 8. Reflection and the genuine trivial modes

Define the involution on S_H

```text
J(r,h)=(p-1-r-h,h).                                  (8.1)
```

The proved reflection theorem gives

```text
v_{p-1-n}=v_n,
```

hence

```text
Delta_{J(r,h)}=-Delta_{r,h}.                          (8.2)
```

The consequences are exact:

```text
m_H(a)=m_H(-a),
B_t(H)=B_{-t}(H),
B_t(H) is real.                                       (8.3)
```

The fixed points of J satisfy

```text
2r+h=p-1.                                             (8.4)
```

They occur exactly for even h and force Delta=0, giving the known kappa_h=1 collision.

These are the only automatic symmetry modes relevant here:

1. t=0 is the constant Fourier mode and is removed in COINC.
2. The reflection-odd sine sector cancels exactly.
3. The even-h fixed points contribute about H/2 deterministic zeros.

The forced zeros contribute O(H^2) to N_coinc. This lies within the strong allowance pH for H<=p. They are not an eigenvalue obstruction comparable to the clock modes in (5.5).

Thus it would be misleading to describe kappa_h as a trivial eigenvalue of the cocycle transfer operator. It is a fixed-point atom of the value map.

## 9. A block large-sieve formulation of COINC

Let H=qL and partition the h-range into consecutive blocks

```text
J_j={jL+1,...,(j+1)L},       0<=j<q.                 (9.1)
```

Define

```text
B_{t,j}=
 sum_{h in J_j}
 sum_{1<=r<=M-h} e_p(t Delta_{r,h}).                  (9.2)
```

Then B_t(H)=sum_j B_{t,j}.

The exact spectral statement that implies strong COINC is the block large sieve

```text
sum_{t!=0} |sum_{j<q} alpha_j B_{t,j}|^2
 <= C p^2 L sum_{j<q}|alpha_j|^2                     (9.3)
```

for all complex coefficients alpha_j.

Taking alpha_j=1 gives

```text
sum_{t!=0}|B_t(H)|^2<=C p^2 L q=C p^2 H,             (9.4)
```

which is exactly p times the K A_H budget.

Equivalently, if

```text
T_L(alpha)(t)=(p sqrt(L))^(-1) sum_j alpha_j B_{t,j},
```

then (9.3) says

```text
||T_L||_{2->2}<=sqrt(C).                              (9.5)
```

This is the correct "spectral gap" formulation. It is a large-sieve bound for the value labels, not expansion of the untwisted PGL_2 cocycle.

By additive orthogonality, the Gram matrix is

```text
sum_{t!=0}B_{t,j} conjugate(B_{t,k})
 = p N_{j,k}-A_j A_k,                                (9.6)
```

where N_{j,k} counts equal Delta values between the two h-blocks. Thus (9.3) is exactly a family-averaged cancellation statement for the difference curves, compressed into an operator norm.

It is not a Fourier-diagonal alias of the original fiber energy F_p; it is the precise all-value second moment in the gap family.

## 10. What short-block Weil estimates really prove

Choose, as suggested,

```text
L=p^(1/6),
H=p^(2/3),
q=H/L=p^(1/2).                                       (10.1)
```

For a fixed number k of consecutive L-block observables, their product is a trace function of one variable r whose conductor is O(kL), assuming the usual nondegeneracy and no pole cancellation. Complete Weil-Deligne then gives

```text
correlation << kL sqrt(p).                            (10.2)
```

This is genuine finite-order decorrelation. For each fixed k, (10.2) is nontrivial.

It cannot be iterated to k=q. At k=q, the complexity is

```text
kL=H=p^(2/3),                                         (10.3)
```

so (10.2) becomes p^(7/6), larger than the trivial length p.

The composition identity

```text
G_{m+g}(s)=G_g(s+m)G_m(s)                            (10.4)
```

does not reduce the total conductor. It is the factorization of the same rational function of degree O(m+g).

### 10.1 The independence fantasy

If the block matrices in (2.2) were independent samples, one could hope to apply convolution flattening repeatedly. They are not.

The joint empirical measure

```text
r -> (G_L(r),G_L(r+L),...,G_L(r+(q-1)L))             (10.5)
```

has support at most p. The independent product measure has up to p^q sample paths. No entropy is created by writing one deterministic product as q factors.

Moreover every block shares the same base variable r. Conditioning on all previous blocks generally determines a high-degree algebraic restriction on r; the next block is then not sampled from its unconditional empirical distribution.

### 10.2 Randomizing the decomposition does not help automatically

One might write the same h in many ways

```text
h=h_1+...+h_k
```

and average over decompositions. But the cocycle law collapses every decomposition to the same matrix G_h(r). This creates multiplicity, not independent words.

To gain anything, one would need a theorem saying that different decompositions produce analytically independent intermediate states after inserting a nontrivial observable. No standard finite-field theorem supplies this.

### 10.3 Van der Corput does not create fresh variables

Applying van der Corput in the block index replaces a one-block phase by differences of two shifted block phases. For a fixed number of differencing steps, Weil applies. Each step increases the rational complexity, and all phases still depend on the same r. After a growing number of steps, the same O(H) conductor wall reappears.

Thus block gluing is not refuted as a possible new method, but its missing input is exactly quantitative conditional independence of deterministic consecutive blocks.

## 11. A bankable local lemma and the decisive new lemma

There are two logically different targets.

### 11.1 Local fixed-order block decorrelation

The first gate is a theorem of the following form.

```text
SHORT-BLOCK(k,L).
Fix k. Let 1<=g_i<=L and let a_i be disjoint consecutive shifts.
For coefficients t_i in F_p, not all zero, put

F(r)=sum_{i=1}^k t_i Delta_{r+a_i,g_i}.

After removing the explicit reflection/fixed-zero degeneracies,
F is not of Artin-Schreier form R(r)^p-R(r)+c, and

|sum_{r in the complete regular set} e_p(F(r))|
 <= C_k L sqrt(p).                                    (11.1)
```

A proof has two parts:

1. Use the recurrence, pole locations, and adjacent coprimality to prove that at least one pole survives and has order not divisible by p.
2. Apply the standard Weil bound for an Artin-Schreier sheaf.

Only the first part is project-specific. If it succeeds, (11.1) certifies finite-order independence of consecutive short blocks.

This lemma is worth proving and machine-testing, but it does not imply COINC because C_k is not uniform for k about p^(1/2).

### 11.2 The decisive prefix-graph restriction lemma

Let

```text
Gamma_m={ (r,v_r,Y_{r+m}) : 1<=r<=M-m }              (11.2)
```

be the actual graph of length-m prefixes in the anchored frame bundle. A short-block observable of length g<=L is

```text
Psi_{g,t}(r,a,Y)
 = e_p(t det(a,row_2(A_{r+m,g}Y))).                   (11.3)
```

On Gamma_m this is exactly

```text
Psi_{g,t}(r,v_r,Y_{r+m})
 = e_p(t Delta_{r,m+g}).                              (11.4)
```

The decisive first new statement is:

```text
PREFIX-GRAPH-SAMPLING(L,H).
For L<=p^(1/6), every m<=H<=p^(2/3), and every
bounded-complexity short-block observable Psi with zero mean on the
ambient determinant-fixed frame fiber,

|sum_r Psi(r,v_r,Y_{r+m})|
 <= C_epsilon L^C p^(1/2+epsilon),                    (11.5)

with C independent of m and p.                       
```

A sequential strengthening, stable after multiplication by observables from earlier blocks, would imply the block large sieve (9.3) by a standard martingale/Gram-matrix argument. That strengthening is the exact deterministic analogue of fresh randomness at each block.

### 11.3 Proof sketch and exact stopping point

The ambient version of (11.5) is approachable:

1. Sum over all frames Y of the prescribed determinant and all anchors a.
2. Center Psi by its fiber average.
3. Use finite-field orthogonality and the geometric monodromy/nondegeneracy of the short block.
4. Apply Deligne to obtain square-root cancellation with complexity depending only on L.

This is the same structural advantage exploited by Ostafe-Shparlinski Theorem 8: average over all initial states first.

The actual theorem restricts the ambient sum to Gamma_m, one point per r. Pulling (11.3) back to Gamma_m produces

```text
r -> e_p(t Delta_{r,m+g}),                            (11.6)
```

whose rational complexity is O(m+g). Standard Deligne gives only

```text
O((m+g)sqrt(p)).                                      (11.7)
```

At m>sqrt(p), (11.7) is trivial.

This is the named obstruction:

```text
PREFIX-GRAPH RESTRICTION / CONDUCTOR GROWTH UNDER DETERMINISTIC PULLBACK.
```

No standard result remains after this point. Proving (11.5) requires a new reason that the high-degree prefix graph samples every bounded-complexity short-block local system as though it were random, with constants independent of the prefix degree.

---

## 12. Why this is the correct first research target

The prefix-graph lemma is narrower than COINC in three useful ways:

1. It asks only about bounded-length local observables.
2. It isolates the one place where the dynamics in h could matter.
3. Its full-state analogue is standard enough to serve as a calibration theorem.

It is also falsifiable. For each prime and L, compute the singular values of the empirical restriction operator

```text
ambient short-block observables -> values on Gamma_m
```

as m ranges up to p^(2/3). If the norm grows like a positive power of m, block gluing is dead in this form. If it stays near p^(1/2) after centering and removing reflection modes, there is a real theorem to seek.

A practical first symbolic test is SHORT-BLOCK(2,L): classify every linear combination of two consecutive block phases for which the outermost poles cancel. The only acceptable exceptions should be those forced by reflection or exact recurrence identities. Unexpected cancellation would be a written death certificate for naive block independence.

## 13. Final verdict by requested task

### Task 1: literature

Bourgain-Gamburd expansion does not apply to the deterministic schedule. Its l2-flattening acts on convolution powers of a spread-out symmetric measure. The Apery schedule gives one Dirac word.

Ostafe-Shparlinski provides the closest non-autonomous precedent, Theorem 8 of arXiv:0908.4519, but only after averaging over every initial state in a special triangular permutation system. It does not control one distinguished Apery frame.

Fixed-h Weil/Deligne gives O(h sqrt(p)) complete sums and hence B_t(H)<<H^2 sqrt(p), useful only below sqrt(p).

Modern short-sum and bilinear trace-function theorems require one bounded-complexity sheaf or a fixed monomial pullback. The Apery conductor grows with h, so those theorems do not reach H=p^(2/3).

No checked primary theorem controls ordered products of the polynomially varying matrices M_{r+j} on one deterministic length-p orbit in the required range.

### Task 2: spectral operator and block gluing

The untwisted operator on clock x P^1 is exactly conjugate to a clock moving average and has no useful spectral gap.

The correct exact spectral object is either the twisted index operator L_{t,H} in (7.1) or the block large-sieve operator T_L in (9.5).

Strong COINC asks for Ramanujan-size p^(-1/2) cancellation in the constant matrix coefficient, or an O(1) block large-sieve norm after normalization.

Reflection makes the t and -t sectors identical and contributes one forced zero for each even h. These modes fit inside the target budget and should be projected out before numerical spectral tests.

Short-block Weil estimates glue only for a fixed number of blocks. They fail for q growing because every block shares r, the joint support has size p, the total conductor becomes O(H), and powers of the twisted operator do not reproduce the long-gap determinant phase.

### Task 3: first lemma

First prove SHORT-BLOCK(2,L) with a complete exceptional-case classification. That proof should end with the standard Artin-Schreier Weil bound.

The first decisive theorem is PREFIX-GRAPH-SAMPLING(L,H), equation (11.5), followed by its sequential version. The exact nonstandard step is restriction from the full frame torsor to the single high-degree Apery prefix graph with a constant independent of prefix length.

If that restriction theorem cannot be proved, then the block-gluing mechanism has a precise death certificate. If it can be proved, the block large sieve (9.3) is the clean route to COINC and hence to a new energy exponent.
