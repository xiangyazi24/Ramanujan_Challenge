# Exact toric-boundary reduction and the surviving exponential resultant

## Verdict

There is an exact Griffiths--Dwork divergence operator for the zero-surplus kernel, but the hoped-for rank-one reduction of the insertion does **not** occur. Two explicit toric faces of the actual Laurent polynomial give incompatible scalar reductions. Their elimination resultant is

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_M
=u_0(M+1)\,K_MK_{M+1}(K_{M+1}-K_M),
\qquad
K_M=\binom{2M}{M}+2,
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

where `u_0(n)=integer_coefficients[0]` is the constant coefficient of the **actual** Ore--CRT polynomial

\[
U_n(T)=\sum_{j=0}^4u_j(n)T^j
\]

constructed in `q32_doubled_period_gauge_audit.sage`. The repository certifies

\[
\deg u_0=32,
\qquad
\deg M_{\rm poly}=30,
\]

so `u_0` is not the zero polynomial. Except at its finitely many integral roots,

\[
\boxed{
\log|\Omega_M|=M\log 64+O(\log M).
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Thus the exact toric reduction terminates in a packet of exponential, not polynomial or `exp(o(M))`, height. The multiplier `Mpoly(n)` cancels from the resultant, and every coefficient `u_j`, `j>=1`, disappears already on the first toric boundary grade. This is a concrete algebraic obstruction, not a dimension count.

The result rules out a low-height certificate obtained solely from toric divergence reduction of the displayed zero-surplus kernel. It does not rule out a new identity that mixes different toric grades or different moments before taking the terminal specialization.

## 1. Exact zero-surplus toric divergence

Put

\[
q=X^\kappa=x^{\kappa_1}y^{\kappa_2}z^{\kappa_3},
\qquad
H_\kappa=\Lambda^M q^{-M}(1-q)^L,
\qquad d+L=M,
\]

and let

\[
\theta_i=X_i\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]

For every Laurent polynomial `S`, define

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_{i,\kappa}^{M,L}(S)
={}&(1-q)\Lambda\,\theta_iS
 +(M+1)(1-q)(\theta_i\Lambda)S\\
&-\kappa_i\{M(1-q)+(L+1)q\}\Lambda S.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Then the identity is literally

\[
\boxed{
\theta_i\!\left(
 H_\kappa(1-q)\Lambda S
\right)
=H_\kappa\mathcal D_{i,\kappa}^{M,L}(S).
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Taking constant terms gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{CT}\left[
 H_\kappa\mathcal D_{i,\kappa}^{M,L}(S)
\right]=0.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Proof

The logarithmic derivative of the kernel is

\[
\frac{\theta_iH_\kappa}{H_\kappa}
=M\frac{\theta_i\Lambda}{\Lambda}
-M\kappa_i
-L\kappa_i\frac q{1-q}.
\]

Also

\[
\theta_i\{(1-q)\Lambda S\}
=-\kappa_iq\Lambda S
 +(1-q)(\theta_i\Lambda)S
 +(1-q)\Lambda\theta_iS.
\]

Adding the two product-rule contributions gives (1.1)--(1.2). Equation (1.3) is the Laurent identity `CT(theta_i F)=0`; there is no analytic boundary assumption.

Consequently the exact zero-surplus Griffiths--Dwork module is the quotient by the images of the three operators (1.1). The question is whether the insertion

\[
\Psi_{n,\kappa}
=\Lambda(q+q^{-1})U_n(\Lambda)-M_{\rm poly}(n)
\tag{1.4}
\]

becomes a single scalar multiple of `1`, or has an exterior class with a small terminal representative. The following two face residues answer this negatively.

## 2. Two explicit toric faces of the actual `Lambda`

Write

\[
R(t)=t^{-1}+2+t=\frac{(1+t)^2}{t}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The Laurent polynomial can be written as

\[
\Lambda
=(x^{-1}+1)R(y)R(z)+(1+x)(1+y)(1+z).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Two faces are especially simple.

### Edge face `E`: `y=z=1`

For the weight `(0,1,1)`, the maximal face is

\[
E=\{(-1,1,1),(0,1,1),(1,1,1)\},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\Lambda_E=yzR(x).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Facet face `F`: `x=-1`

For the weight `(-1,0,0)`, the maximal face is

\[
F=\{(-1,b,c):-1\le b,c\le1\},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\Lambda_F=x^{-1}R(y)R(z).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Both sets are subsets of `P cap Z^3`; in fact they are complete lattice faces of the 22-point Newton polytope.

The toric face filtration is compatible with (1.1). On the first nonzero face grade:

1. only `kappa` lying on the selected face occurs;
2. only the constant term of `(1-q)^L` occurs;
3. in `Lambda(q+q^{-1})U_n(Lambda)`, only the `q^{-1}u_0(n)` term occurs;
4. the face of every divergence (1.1) is again a face divergence, whose constant term is zero.

The third point is exact exponent bookkeeping, not an asymptotic claim. On `E`, every term other than `u_0 Lambda q^{-1}` has a strictly positive `yz` exponent. On `F`, every other term has a strictly negative `x` exponent. The powers of `q` in `(1-q)^L` move in the same forbidden direction. Hence none can contribute a face constant term.

Thus the two face constant-term maps descend to honest linear functionals on the toric divergence quotient.

## 3. Exact face values of the `Y` and `W` terminal classes

Let

\[
C_M=\binom{2M}{M},
\qquad
K_M=C_M+2,
\qquad n=M+1,
\]

and abbreviate

\[
u_0=u_0(n),
\qquad
\mu=M_{\rm poly}(n).
\]

The elementary identity

\[
\operatorname{CT}_t R(t)^M=\binom{2M}{M}=C_M
\tag{3.1}
\]

will be used repeatedly.

### 3.1 Edge face

Write `kappa=(a,1,1)`, with `a=-1,0,1`. Since

\[
\Lambda_E^M X^{-M\kappa}
=R(x)^M x^{-aM},
\]

one obtains

\[
\operatorname{CT}_x R(x)^M x^{-aM}
=\begin{cases}
1,&a=-1,\\
C_M,&a=0,\\
1,&a=1.
\end{cases}
\]

Summing over the three lattice points of `E` gives

\[
\boxed{
\rho_E(Y)=K_M.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Only `u_0 Lambda_E q^{-1}-mu` survives from the insertion, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\rho_E(W)=u_0K_{M+1}-\mu K_M.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

These formulas are independent of `L`; all nonconstant terms of `(1-q)^L` have zero face constant term.

### 3.2 Facet face

For `kappa=(-1,b,c)`,

\[
\Lambda_F^M X^{-M\kappa}
=R(y)^M y^{-bM}R(z)^M z^{-cM}.
\]

The sum over `b,c=-1,0,1` factorizes, so

\[
\boxed{
\rho_F(Y)=K_M^2,
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

and similarly

\[
\boxed{
\rho_F(W)=u_0K_{M+1}^2-\mu K_M^2.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Equations (3.2)--(3.5) use the actual coefficient `u_0(n)` of the certified `U_n`; no generic replacement for `U` has been made.

## 4. Exact wedge and elimination resultant

Apply the two face functionals to the exterior class `Y wedge W`. The determinant is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_M
&=\det
\begin{pmatrix}
K_M&u_0K_{M+1}-\mu K_M\\
K_M^2&u_0K_{M+1}^2-\mu K_M^2
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=u_0K_MK_{M+1}(K_{M+1}-K_M).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The entire `Mpoly` contribution cancels. This is the exact terminal toric packet.

There is an equivalent resultant formulation. Introduce a possible common scalar `T` and put

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_E(T)
&=K_MT-\{u_0K_{M+1}-\mu K_M\},\\
P_F(T)
&=K_M^2T-\{u_0K_{M+1}^2-\mu K_M^2\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_T(P_E,P_F)
=-u_0K_MK_{M+1}(K_{M+1}-K_M)
=-\Omega_M.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore, whenever `u_0(M+1) != 0`, the insertion class is not a common scalar multiple of the shell class modulo toric divergences. More generally, every toric-divergence formula for the exterior class must retain a packet whose two-face image is `Omega_M`.

This is a concrete algebraic obstruction: two explicit linear equations have a nonzero exact resultant.

## 5. Height of the terminal packet

The central binomial ratio is exact:

\[
\frac{C_{M+1}}{C_M}
=\frac{2(2M+1)}{M+1}
=4-\frac2{M+1},
\]

so

\[
C_{M+1}-C_M
=\frac{3M+1}{M+1}C_M.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Also

\[
C_M=\frac{4^M}{\sqrt{\pi M}}\left(1+O(M^{-1})\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The repository proves `deg u_0=32`. Hence, away from its finite root set,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log|\Omega_M|
&=\log|u_0(M+1)|
 +\log K_M+\log K_{M+1}
 +\log(K_{M+1}-K_M)\\
&=3M\log4+O(\log M)\\
&=M\log64+O(\log M).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus

\[
|\Omega_M|=64^{M+o(M)}.
\]

This is not `exp(o(M))`. Dividing by this packet would also be unusable for the target ledger: its central-binomial factors are ambient factorial content and can contain candidate primes without target selectivity.

The obstruction is stronger than merely observing that individual terminal shell values are large. It identifies an exact nonzero exterior component, proves its factorization, and computes its exponential rate.

## 6. Exact scope of the no-go

What is proved:

1. the toric divergence identity (1.1)--(1.3);
2. two explicit quotient functionals `rho_E,rho_F`;
3. the exact actual-`U` face values (3.2)--(3.5);
4. the resultant (4.3);
5. the height law (5.3).

Consequently no reduction of the form

\[
[\Psi_{n,\kappa}]
=c(n)[1]+\text{toric divergences}
\]

can hold simultaneously on all toric boundary components, and no exterior reduction using only these divergences can terminate in a packet of polynomial or `exp(o(M))` height.

What is not ruled out:

- an identity coupling different moments before setting `d+L=M`;
- a cancellation using a new non-toric relation between different face periods;
- a nonlinear identity specific to the complete sum that does not respect the toric face filtration.

Such an identity would be genuinely new input. It is not supplied by the certified Ore relation alone.

```sage
#!/usr/bin/env sage
"""Exact toric-boundary obstruction for the zero-surplus Y/W packet."""

from pathlib import Path

HERE = Path(__file__).resolve().parent
load(str(HERE / "q32_doubled_period_gauge_audit.sage"))

# These are the actual certified objects constructed by the repository.
U = tuple(R(value) for value in integer_coefficients)
Mpoly = R(multiplier)
assert len(U) == 5
assert [value.degree() for value in U] == [32, 32, 32, 32, 32]
assert Mpoly.degree() == 30
assert U[0] != 0

LP.<x,y,z> = LaurentPolynomialRing(QQ)
variables = (x, y, z)

Lambda = (
    (1+x)*(1+y)*(1+z)*((1+y)*(1+z)+x*y*z)/(x*y*z)
)
R1 = x^(-1) + 2 + x


def monomial(exponent):
    return prod(
        variables[index]^ZZ(exponent[index])
        for index in range(3)
    )


def ct(value):
    for exponent, coefficient in value.dict().items():
        if tuple(ZZ(entry) for entry in exponent) == (0, 0, 0):
            return QQ(coefficient)
    return QQ(0)


def theta(value, index):
    variable = variables[index]
    return variable * value.derivative(variable)


def toric_D(kappa, MM, LL, insertion, index):
    q = monomial(kappa)
    return (
        (1-q)*Lambda*theta(insertion, index)
        + (MM+1)*(1-q)*theta(Lambda, index)*insertion
        - kappa[index]
          * (MM*(1-q)+(LL+1)*q)
          * Lambda*insertion
    )


# Exact multiplication-back checks of (1.2).
divergence_checks = 0
for kappa in ((0,1,1), (-1,0,0), (1,1,1)):
    q = monomial(kappa)
    for MM, LL in ((2,0), (3,1), (5,3)):
        H = Lambda^MM * q^(-MM) * (1-q)^LL
        for insertion in (LP.one(), x+y+z, x^(-1)*y^2-z):
            for index in range(3):
                left = theta(
                    H*(1-q)*Lambda*insertion,
                    index,
                )
                right = H*toric_D(
                    kappa, MM, LL, insertion, index
                )
                assert left == right
                assert ct(left) == 0
                divergence_checks += 1


# Verify the two actual faces of Lambda.
def initial_face(value, weight):
    data = value.dict()
    maximum = max(
        sum(ZZ(weight[i])*ZZ(exponent[i]) for i in range(3))
        for exponent in data
    )
    return sum(
        coefficient * monomial(exponent)
        for exponent, coefficient in data.items()
        if sum(
            ZZ(weight[i])*ZZ(exponent[i]) for i in range(3)
        ) == maximum
    )


edge_face = y*z*(x^(-1)+2+x)
facet_face = x^(-1)*(y^(-1)+2+y)*(z^(-1)+2+z)
assert initial_face(Lambda, (0,1,1)) == edge_face
assert initial_face(Lambda, (-1,0,0)) == facet_face

EDGE = ((-1,1,1), (0,1,1), (1,1,1))
FACET = tuple(
    (-1, b, c)
    for b in (-1,0,1)
    for c in (-1,0,1)
)


def U_at(index, value):
    return sum(ZZ(U[j](index))*value^j for j in range(5))


def face_pair(face_polynomial, kappas, MM, LL):
    index = MM + 1
    upoly = U_at(index, face_polynomial)
    mu = ZZ(Mpoly(index))
    y_value = ZZ(0)
    w_value = ZZ(0)
    for kappa in kappas:
        q = monomial(kappa)
        kernel = (
            face_polynomial^MM
            * monomial(tuple(-MM*entry for entry in kappa))
            * (1-q)^LL
        )
        psi = face_polynomial*(q+q^(-1))*upoly-mu
        y_value += ZZ(ct(kernel))
        w_value += ZZ(ct(kernel*psi))
    return y_value, w_value


face_checks = 0
for MM in range(1, 8):
    index = MM + 1
    c0 = ZZ(binomial(2*MM, MM))
    c1 = ZZ(binomial(2*MM+2, MM+1))
    A = c0 + 2
    B = c1 + 2
    u0 = ZZ(U[0](index))
    mu = ZZ(Mpoly(index))

    for LL in range(MM+1):
        edge = face_pair(edge_face, EDGE, MM, LL)
        facet = face_pair(facet_face, FACET, MM, LL)

        assert edge == (A, u0*B-mu*A)
        assert facet == (A^2, u0*B^2-mu*A^2)

        determinant = edge[0]*facet[1]-facet[0]*edge[1]
        expected = u0*A*B*(B-A)
        assert determinant == expected
        face_checks += 1


# Formal elimination resultant.
S.<AA,BB,UU,MU,T> = PolynomialRing(QQ, order="lex")
PE = AA*T-(UU*BB-MU*AA)
PF = AA^2*T-(UU*BB^2-MU*AA^2)
resultant = PE.resultant(PF, T)
assert resultant == UU*AA*BB*(AA-BB)

# Exact campaign diagnostic: finite exceptional roots of this obstruction.
campaign_zeros = tuple(
    index for index in range(60, 501)
    if U[0](index) == 0
)

print("Q32_TORIC_BOUNDARY_OBSTRUCTION=PASS")
print("DIVERGENCE_MULTIPLICATION_BACK_CHECKS", divergence_checks)
print("FACE_CONSTANT_TERM_CHECKS", face_checks)
print("U_DEGREES", tuple(value.degree() for value in U))
print("MPOLY_DEGREE", Mpoly.degree())
print("ACTUAL_U0", U[0])
print("ACTUAL_U0_FACTORIZATION", factor(U[0]))
print("U0_ZEROS_60_500", campaign_zeros)
print("EDGE_FACE", edge_face)
print("FACET_FACE", facet_face)
print("FORMAL_RESULTANT", factor(resultant))
print(
    "TERMINAL_WEDGE",
    "u0(M+1)*(binomial(2M,M)+2)"
    "*(binomial(2M+2,M+1)+2)"
    "*(binomial(2M+2,M+1)-binomial(2M,M))",
)
print("LOG_HEIGHT", "M*log(64)+O(log(M))")
```

## Final conclusion

The toric-boundary route does give a clean exact answer, but it is a no-go for the desired height scale. The first two nontrivial face reductions already produce the nonzero resultant

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_M
=u_0(M+1)
\left(\binom{2M}{M}+2\right)
\left(\binom{2M+2}{M+1}+2\right)
\left(\binom{2M+2}{M+1}-\binom{2M}{M}\right).
}
\]

Its logarithmic height is `M log 64 + O(log M)`. Therefore an exact Griffiths--Dwork reduction of the actual insertion cannot turn the `Y/W` terminal wedge into a polynomial-height or `exp(o(M))` integer. The precise surviving obstruction is the incompatible edge/facet scalar pair, equivalently the resultant (4.3).
