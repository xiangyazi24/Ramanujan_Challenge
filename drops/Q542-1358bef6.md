# Artin–Schreier zero detectors for the Apéry sheaf

## (1) Is the zero-indicator sheaf the Fourier transform at zero?

The identity

\[
1_{f(r)=0}=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}\psi(uf(r))
\]

is the finite-field additive Fourier inversion formula. For a single value of r this is correct, but one must distinguish two notions:

* the Fourier transform of the function f itself;
* the Fourier transform of the trace function of a sheaf.

If \(F\) is a l-adic sheaf with trace function

\[
t_F(r)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_r|F),
\]

then Deligne–Laumon Fourier transform gives a new sheaf \(FT_\psi(F)\) whose trace at u is essentially

\[
\sum_r t_F(r)\psi(ur).
\]

For the Apéry problem, however, the zero detector is built from the Artin–Schreier twists

\[
\mathcal L_\psi(uf),\qquad u\in\mathbf F_p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac1p\sum_u \mathcal L_\psi(uf)
\]

is better viewed as a projector onto the zero fiber of f. It is related to Fourier transform, but it is not literally \(FT(f)(0)\). The latter is a global sum over r; the former is a sheaf-valued object whose trace at r is the indicator.

The correct relation is:

\[
\sum_r1_{f(r)=0}
=\frac1p\left(p+\sum_{u\neq0}\sum_r\psi(uf(r))\right).
\]

Thus the zero count is controlled by Fourier cancellation of the nontrivial Artin–Schreier twists.

## (2) What happens to the conductor of the sum?

A naive bound would say:

\[
\operatorname{cond}\left(\bigoplus_{u\neq0}\mathcal L_\psi(uf)\right)
\le (p-1)\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal L_\psi(f)),
\]

but this is not the correct geometric picture.

The different twists \(u f\) are not unrelated sheaves. They form the Fourier family of a single object. The conductor is controlled by the Fourier transform formalism, not by summing conductors term-by-term.

For a fixed rational function f, Laumon’s Fourier transform gives bounds of the form

\[
\operatorname{cond}(FT(F))\ll \operatorname{cond}(F)^2,
\]

rather than multiplying by p.

However, the Apéry situation is harder because

\[
f(r)=b_r\pmod p
\]

is not a fixed low-degree polynomial. It is a trace function arising from a rank-2/Sp(4)-type l-adic object. The main issue is proving that the family of twists

\[
\mathcal L_\psi(ub_r)
\]

has bounded geometric complexity uniformly in p.

If that uniformity holds, there is substantial cancellation among the u-summands; there is no p-fold conductor explosion.

## (3) Two independent trace functions and convolution

For independent functions \(f_p,f_q\), the product detector is

\[
I_{p,q}(r,s)=1_{f_p(r)=0}1_{f_q(s)=0}.
\]

At the Fourier level,

\[
\widehat{I_p}(u)=\frac1p\sum_x\psi(uf_p(x)),
\]

and similarly for q.

If the variables are independent, the Fourier transform of the product satisfies the expected tensor-product rule:

\[
\widehat{I_p\otimes I_q}
=\widehat{I_p}\otimes\widehat{I_q}.
\]

For convolution on a single additive group, the usual theorem gives

\[
\widehat{g*h}=\widehat g\,\widehat h,
\]

while products transform to convolutions:

\[
\widehat{gh}=\widehat g*\widehat h.
\]

The relevant Apéry pair-zero problem is closer to a tensor product of two independent Fourier sheaves than a convolution.

## Consequence for P3.2

The Artin–Schreier viewpoint identifies the zero detector with a Fourier projector. The desired M1 bound reduces to proving cancellation for

\[
\sum_{u\neq0}\sum_r\psi(u b_r).
\]

A successful proof would require:

1. a uniform conductor bound for the Fourier family attached to the Apéry trace function;
2. geometric irreducibility/nondegeneracy of the twists \(\mathcal L_\psi(ub)\);
3. Deligne square-root cancellation for each nontrivial Fourier coefficient.

The main obstacle is not the formal Artin–Schreier construction; it is establishing that the Apéry mod-p sequence really behaves as a bounded-complexity l-adic trace function uniformly over p. This is exactly the arithmetic-geometric step needed beyond compact-group Sato–Tate heuristics.
