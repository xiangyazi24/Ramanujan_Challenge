For the actual event

```text

t=n+2h,
```

```text
b_(q-1-j) = b_j - q e_j                 (mod q^2)
```

whenever `q>2j` is an odd prime.  Consequently

```text
R_p   = e_(n+1)                           (mod p),
R_ell = e_(n+1)                           (mod ell).
```

```text
H=b_n/(p*ell)
```

```text

b_t/ell = p*H   - e_n                     (mod ell).
```

```text
D = lcm(1,...,2n+2),
E = D e_(n+1) in Z,
X = D R_p,
Y = D R_ell.
```

```text
X-E = p s_p,
Y-E = ell s_ell                              (1)
```

for integers `s_p,s_ell`.  The smallest nonfactorized cross-combination is

```text
J_1
```


contains only one guaranteed copy of each mark.  There is no forced `p` or `ell` left after primitive division.

Thus the first reflection normalization still loses a factor of order `N/log N` in a dyadic range.  Products over event pairs simply multiply both the required multiplicity and this exponential height; they repay the missing second-moment multiplicity exactly.

# 2. Exact mod-q^2 reflection defect

Write

```text
A(j,k)=binom(j,k)^2 binom(j+k,k)^2,
Harm(r)=sum_(1<=s<=r) 1/s,
Harm(0)=0.
```

Define the rational sequence

```text
e_j
 = 2 sum_(k=0..j)
       A(j,k) [Harm(j+k)-Harm(j-k)].           (4)
```

The `k=0` summand is zero.  Every denominator occurring in (4) divides

```text
D_j=lcm(1,...,2j).                             (5)
```

Hence

```text
E_j:=D_j e_j in Z.                             (6)
```

Let `q` be an odd prime with

```text
0<=j<=(q-1)/2.
```

For `0<=k<=j`, expand the two binomial factors modulo `q^2`:

```text
binom(q-1-j,k)
 = (-1)^k binom(j+k,k)
   [1-q(Harm(j+k)-Harm(j))]                   (mod q^2),

binom(q-1-j+k,k)
 = (-1)^k binom(j,k)
   [1-q(Harm(j)-Harm(j-k))]                   (mod q^2).
```

After squaring and multiplying, the first-order correction is

```text
1-2q[Harm(j+k)-Harm(j-k)].
```

For `k>j`, the summand of `b_(q-1-j)` contains a factor `q^2` after the square, so it vanishes modulo `q^2`.  Summing gives the exact reflection lift

```text
b_(q-1-j)
 = b_j-q e_j                                  (mod q^2).   (7)
```

Because `q>2j` in every use below, `q` is coprime to `D_j`; thus (7) may be read either in `Z_(q)` or after multiplying by `D_j` as an ordinary integer congruence.

This formula is stronger than ordinary reflection

```text
b_(q-1-j)=b_j mod q.
```

It also shows why a zero does **not** automatically reflect modulo `q^2`: the first divided defect is `e_j`, which need not vanish.

# 3. Exact recurrence for the universal divided defect e_j

Let

```text
P'(j)=102j^2+102j+27.
```

The sequence (4) has

```text
e_0=0,
e_1=12,
```

and satisfies the exact rational recurrence

```text
(j+1)^3 e_(j+1)
 = P'(j)b_j + P(j)e_j
   -3j^2 b_(j-1) - j^3 e_(j-1)
   -3(j+1)^2 b_(j+1).                         (8)
```

One way to derive (8) without any analytic interpolation is to define, for a prime `q`,

```text
C_q(j):=(b_j-b_(q-1-j))/q.                    (9)
```

This is integral in the reflection range.  Substituting

```text
b_(q-1-j)=b_j-q C_q(j)
```

into the Apéry recurrence at the reflected index `q-1-j` gives the exact q-dependent recurrence

```text
(q-j)^3 [b_(j-1)-q C_q(j-1)]
-P(q-1-j)[b_j-q C_q(j)]
+(q-j-1)^3[b_(j+1)-q C_q(j+1)]
=0.                                             (10)
```

Subtract the `q=0` reflected copy of the ordinary recurrence, divide by `q`, and reduce modulo `q`.  The result is

```text
(j+1)^3 C_q(j+1)-P(j)C_q(j)+j^3 C_q(j-1)
 = P'(j)b_j
   -3j^2b_(j-1)-3(j+1)^2b_(j+1)               (mod q).   (11)
```

By (7),

```text
C_q(j)=e_j mod q.
```

For infinitely many primes larger than the fixed denominators, (11) gives the same rational identity, hence (8) follows over `Q`.

Equation (10) is the exact recurrence for the actual prime-dependent reflection defect; equation (8) is its universal first Bockstein reduction.

# 4. Apply the lift to the actual p,ell event

We have

```text
p-1=n+u,
ell-1=n+t.
```

Therefore

```text
u=p-1-n,
t=ell-1-n,
u-1=p-1-(n+1),
t-1=ell-1-(n+1).
```

Since `h` is a positive even integer, `h>=2`, and

```text
p>=2n+3>2n+2,
ell>p.
```

Thus the denominators of `e_n,e_(n+1)` are units at both primes.

## 4.1 The reflected zero coordinates

Apply (7) at `j=n`:

```text
b_u = b_n-p e_n                               (mod p^2),
b_t = b_n-ell e_n                             (mod ell^2).
```

Write

```text
U_p=b_u/p,
U_ell=b_t/ell.
```

Both are integers, because ordinary reflection gives `p|b_u` and `ell|b_t`.  Since

```text
b_n=p*ell*H,
```

division by the corresponding prime gives

```text
U_p   = ell H-e_n                             (mod p),
U_ell = p H-e_n                               (mod ell).  (12)
```

This is the first place `H` appears.  It appears separately, with the opposite prime as a unit coefficient.

## 4.2 The predecessor reflection defects

Apply (7) at `j=n+1`:

```text
b_(u-1)=b_(n+1)-p e_(n+1)                    (mod p^2),

b_(t-1)=b_(n+1)-ell e_(n+1)                  (mod ell^2).
```

By definition,

```text
R_p=(b_(n+1)-b_(u-1))/p,
R_ell=(b_(n+1)-b_(t-1))/ell.
```

Hence

```text
R_p   = e_(n+1)                               (mod p),
R_ell = e_(n+1)                               (mod ell).  (13)
```

These are the exact first divided congruences requested in the question.

## 4.3 The recurrence at the common zero

The original recurrence at `n` is exactly

```text
(n+1)^3 b_(n+1)+n^3 b_(n-1)
 = P(n) p ell H.                              (14)
```

The divided-reflection recurrence (8) at `n` is exactly

```text
(n+1)^3 e_(n+1)
 = P'(n)p ell H
   +P(n)e_n
   -3n^2 b_(n-1)-n^3 e_(n-1)
   -3(n+1)^2 b_(n+1).                         (15)
```

Put

```text
Phi_n
 = P(n)e_n
   -3n^2 b_(n-1)-n^3 e_(n-1)
   -3(n+1)^2 b_(n+1).
```

The `H` term in (15) contains the full product `p*ell`.  Therefore it disappears modulo either mark, and (13) gives

```text
(n+1)^3 R_p   = Phi_n                         (mod p),
(n+1)^3 R_ell = Phi_n                         (mod ell). (16)
```

This is the decisive saturation feature:

```text
H does not enter the first-Bockstein recurrence modulo p or ell.
```

The first divided data at the two primes are simply two reductions of the same universal rational number `e_(n+1)`.

# 5. Endpoint and valuation exceptions

## 5.1 n=0 is impossible

`b_0=1`, so `p*ell|b_0` cannot occur.  Hence every actual event has `n>=1`.

## 5.2 h=2 is a genuine geometric fixed-point exception

If `h=2`, then

```text
u=n+2,
u-1=n+1,
```

so

```text
R_p=0                                             (17)
```

as an exact integer identity.  Also

```text
n+1=(p-1)/2,
```

so this is exactly the fixed point of the `p`-reflection at the differentiated index.  Equation (13) reduces to

```text
e_(n+1)=0 mod p.
```

This zero is geometric; it supplies no additional `ell`-divisibility.  In this branch `p,ell=p+2` are twin primes, but the event still requires the independent actual condition `ell|b_n`.

## 5.3 Higher marked valuation does not help automatically

The hypothesis only says

```text
p*ell | b_n.
```

It does not say `v_p(b_n)=v_ell(b_n)=1`.  Thus `H` is not a forced `p`- or `ell`-unit.  Any argument that divides by `H` must first isolate a simple-valuation sector.

The Casoratian gives

```text
a_(n+1)b_n-a_n b_(n+1)=6/(n+1)^3.
```

Since


and both divide `b_n`, reduction modulo either mark gives

```text
-a_n b_(n+1)=6/(n+1)^3 !=0.
```

Hence

```text
gcd(b_(n+1),p*ell)=1,
```

and `a_n` is also a simultaneous unit.  This is a useful normalization, but it does not force `R_p` or `R_ell` to be units.

# 6. The minimal first-order joint carrier

Set

```text
D=D_(n+1)=lcm(1,...,2n+2),
E=E_(n+1)=D e_(n+1).
```

Because

```text

```

we have

```text
gcd(D,p*ell)=1.
```

Equation (13) is equivalent to the ordinary integer divisibilities

```text
p   | D R_p-E,
ell | D R_ell-E.                              (18)
```

Define the second digits

```text
s_p   := (D R_p-E)/p,
s_ell := (D R_ell-E)/ell.                     (19)
```

They are integers.

## 6.1 Product carrier

The obvious product gives

```text
p*ell | (D R_p-E)(D R_ell-E),
```

but exactly

```text
(D R_p-E)(D R_ell-E)
 = p*ell*s_p*s_ell.                            (20)
```

There is no extra marked factor after division.

## 6.2 Smallest nonfactorized linear carrier

A linear combination

```text
A(D R_p-E)+B(D R_ell-E)
```

is divisible by `p*ell` for arbitrary first-order lift digits only if, locally,

```text
ell | A,
p   | B.
```

Thus the primitive coefficient choice is `A=ell`, `B=-p`.  It gives

```text
J_1
 = ell(D R_p-E)-p(D R_ell-E)
 = p*ell(s_p-s_ell).                           (21)
```

Since

```text
ell-p=h,
```

this may also be written

```text
J_1
 = D(ell R_p-p R_ell)-hE.                     (22)
```

Equation (22) is the strongest actual cancellation available at this level: the shared universal `E` is reduced to the small coefficient `h` instead of coefficients of size `p` and `ell`.

But (21) is the hostile audit.  The entire guaranteed marked content is exactly the already-known `p*ell`.  The primitive quotient

```text
J_1/(p*ell)=s_p-s_ell                         (23)
```

has no forced marked divisor.

This is a genuinely cross-prime **formula**, but not a genuinely new marked layer.

# 7. Scoped first-Bockstein saturation theorem

The first reflection lift can be written universally as

```text
D R_p   = E+p s_p,
D R_ell = E+ell s_ell.                         (24)
```

At this level the two second digits are independent local lift coordinates.  The actual recurrence (15) does not couple them: its only `H` term is already multiplied by `p*ell` and vanishes in the first reduction.

More formally, work locally at `p`.  Since `ell` and `D` are units modulo `p`, fixing

```text
D R_p=E mod p
```

leaves the residue of `R_ell`, `H`, and the next `p`-adic digit `s_p` unconstrained by the first reflection equation.  The symmetric statement holds locally at `ell`.  CRT then keeps the two next digits independent until a genuinely second-order actual-orbit identity is supplied.

Therefore:

```text
The first divided reflection package forces one p-layer at the p vertex
and one ell-layer at the ell vertex, but no second marked layer.
```

Cross-combinations such as (21) are allowed, but after dividing the paid `p*ell` their quotient is an arbitrary polynomial/linear combination of the second digits.  Squaring or multiplying such carriers creates higher marked powers only by paying the same carrier height again.

This is the precise sense in which the normalization saturates.  It is not a claim that the true Apéry sequence has no hidden relation among `s_p,s_ell,H`; it says such a relation is **not present in the first reflection Bockstein, the recurrence modulo the marks, or the Casoratian**.

# 8. Height audit

The harmonic formula (4) gives the elementary bound

```text
0 <= e_j <= 2 Harm(2j) b_j.                   (25)
```

Indeed every summand is nonnegative and the harmonic difference is at most `Harm(2j)`.

The standard Apéry growth is

```text
log b_j = j log alpha + O(log j),
alpha=17+12 sqrt(2).
```

Also, by the elementary Chebyshev bound for the lcm,

```text
log D_j=O(j).                                  (26)
```

Thus

```text
log |E_j| <= j log alpha + O(j).               (27)
```

For `h>2`, using positivity and monotonicity only for an upper bound,

```text
|R_p|
 <= (b_(n+1)+b_(u-1))/p
 <= 2 b_(u-1),

|R_ell|
 <= (b_(n+1)+b_(t-1))/ell
 <= 2 b_(t-1).
```

Therefore

```text
log |R_p|   <= (u-1)log alpha+O(log(n+h)),
log |R_ell| <= (t-1)log alpha+O(log(n+h)).      (28)
```

For the product carrier (20), the available bound is roughly the sum of the two outer heights:

```text
log |(D R_p-E)(D R_ell-E)|
 <= (u+t-2)log alpha+O(n+h)
 = (2n+3h-2)log alpha+O(n+h).                 (29)
```

The cross-linear carrier (22) is better but still has the outer `t-1` state:

```text
log |J_1|
 <= (t-1)log alpha+O(n+h).                     (30)
```

No term in `H` or `R_p` contains `b_(t-1)`, so the `R_ell` outer state cannot be cancelled by a universal linear identity in `(H,R_p,R_ell)`.

In a dyadic top-prime regime,

```text
p,ell=Theta(N),
n,h=O(N),
```

and hence

```text
log(p*ell)=Theta(log N),
log|J_1|=O(N)
```

with no known improvement to `O(log N)`.  The first-order normalization therefore loses an `N/log N` factor before any second-moment summation.

This is why taking a product of one such carrier for every ordered event pair cannot help: the product height is additive in the number of pairs, exactly as the desired marked multiplicity is additive.  The missing factorial multiplicity is repaid in height.

# 9. Companion solution and discrete Wronskian audit

Let `a_j` be the companion Apéry solution.  For an odd prime `q` and indices below `q`, ordinary reflection sends the solution space to itself modulo `q`.

The distinguished solution satisfies

```text
b_(q-1-j)=b_j mod q.
```

For the companion there is therefore a scalar `kappa_q` such that

```text
a_(q-1-j)=A_q a_j+kappa_q b_j mod q.
```

The Casoratian determines `A_q=1`.  Indeed

```text
W_j(a,b)=a_jb_(j-1)-a_(j-1)b_j=6/j^3,
```

while the reflected Casoratian is

```text
-W_(q-j)(a,b)
 = -6/(q-j)^3
 =  6/j^3 mod q.
```

Hence

```text
a_(q-1-j)=a_j+kappa_q b_j mod q.              (31)
```

At the common zero `j=n`, (31) gives

```text
a_u=a_n mod p,
a_t=a_n mod ell.
```

At `j=n+1`, the companion defects are

```text
a_(u-1)-a_(n+1)=kappa_p b_(n+1) mod p,

a_(t-1)-a_(n+1)=kappa_ell b_(n+1) mod ell.
```

Since `b_(n+1)` is a simultaneous unit, the two `kappa` values are simply two independent local reflection parameters.  They do not identify across characteristics.

Likewise, divide (13) by the common unit `b_(n+1)`:

```text
R_p/b_(n+1)
 = e_(n+1)/b_(n+1) mod p,

R_ell/b_(n+1)
 = e_(n+1)/b_(n+1) mod ell.
```

The Wronskian therefore repackages the same first Bockstein but supplies neither a forced zero nor an extra marked divisor.

# 10. Why eliminating the shared actual state after division still gives no new layer

There are two natural first-order state rows:

```text
V_p = ( C_p(n), C_p(n+1) )
    = ( ell H-U_p, R_p ),

V_ell = ( C_ell(n), C_ell(n+1) )
      = ( p H-U_ell, R_ell ).
```

Modulo their own characteristics,

```text
V_p   = (e_n,e_(n+1)) mod p,
V_ell = (e_n,e_(n+1)) mod ell.                (32)
```

Thus each row reduces to the same universal actual-orbit derivative state, but in a different field.

A determinant formed from the two integer rows does **not** inherit both marks: modulo `p` only the first row is controlled, and modulo `ell` only the second row is controlled.  A scalar resultant has the same problem and can mix different roots/lift digits.

If one instead first subtracts the universal state and clears denominators, the two rows become respectively `p` times a second-defect row and `ell` times a second-defect row.  Every determinant then gains exactly one `p*ell`, but after dividing it away the determinant is simply a determinant of the second-defect rows.  No further mark is forced.

So “eliminate the shared state after division” is the correct route to the **next coordinate**, but it does not by itself prove a theorem about that coordinate.

# 11. The next nonlinear coordinate

For every valid reflection index define

```text
C_q(j)=(b_j-b_(q-1-j))/q,
S_q(j)=(D_j C_q(j)-E_j)/q.                    (33)
```

Equation (7) proves that `S_q(j)` is integral.

At the common zero and its successor,

```text
C_p(n)   = ell H-U_p,
C_p(n+1) = R_p,

C_ell(n)   = p H-U_ell,
C_ell(n+1) = R_ell.
```

Thus

```text
S_p^0
 = [D_n(ell H-U_p)-E_n]/p,

S_p^+
 = [D_(n+1)R_p-E_(n+1)]/p,

S_ell^0
 = [D_n(p H-U_ell)-E_n]/ell,

S_ell^+
 = [D_(n+1)R_ell-E_(n+1)]/ell.               (34)
```

These four integers are the first coordinates not determined modulo the relevant prime by the universal reflection lift.

The most economical genuinely cross state is

```text
Xi_(n,h)
 = det [ S_p^0    S_p^+   ]
       [ S_ell^0  S_ell^+ ]

 = S_p^0 S_ell^+ - S_ell^0 S_p^+.            (35)
```

Nothing proved in the present repository forces

```text
p|Xi,
ell|Xi,
Xi=0,
```

or a unit condition.  Those would be new second-order arithmetic statements.

For a robust carrier one should not rely on a single square determinant.  Extend (33) over a legal common reflection window `J` and form the wide two-row second-Bockstein matrix

```text
S^(2) = ( S_q(j) )
        q in {p,ell}, j in J.
```

The invariant relevant to a simultaneous row carrier is the maximal determinantal divisor

```text
delta_2(S^(2))
 = gcd_(j1<j2 in J)
     det [ S_p(j1)    S_p(j2)   ]
         [ S_ell(j1)  S_ell(j2) ].            (36)
```

One chosen `Xi` can vanish accidentally; only (36) detects a genuine wide rank defect.
