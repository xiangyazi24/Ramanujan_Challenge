# Q7110: residual-prime rank amplification audit

## Executive conclusion

The four examples

\[
(107,27),(131,33),(157,79),(653,327)
\]

are not evidence for a theorem of the form
\(\rho(p)\mid p+1\) with small quotient forced by aligned four-zero
collisions.  They are compatible with the genuine reflection symmetry of the
Apéry recurrence, but the quotient pattern is a small-data phenomenon.  A
nonstructural divisor of the aligned content can be charged to a rank of
apparition only after proving an additional orbit-size lemma; the current
pencil identity does not provide such a lemma.

The correct invariant is the Frobenius orbit of the two-dimensional recurrence
state, not merely \(\rho(p)\).

## 1. The actual reflection law

Modulo a prime \(p\ge 5\), away from the singular indices, the recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3u_{n+1}=P(n)u_n-n^3u_{n-1}
\]

has transfer matrices

\[
V_{n+1}=M(n)V_n,
\qquad
V_n=(u_n,u_{n-1})^T .
\]

The key identity is the involution

\[
P(-1-n)=-P(n).
\]

Together with the determinant relation of the transfer matrices this gives the
reflection involution on the projective state line.  Consequently a zero at
an index \(r\) is paired with a reflected zero at

\[
 p-1-r
\]

whenever the recurrence is transported through the complete residue interval.

Thus if

\[
b_\rho\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

then one obtains the reflected zero

\[
b_{p-1-\rho}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

This proves only a two-point symmetry.  It does not imply
\(\rho\mid p+1\).

## 2. What four separated zeros imply

The aligned pencil gives

\[
 b_a\equiv b_b\equiv b_{b+c}\equiv b_{a+b+c}\equiv0\pmod p
\]

only after the relevant gap polynomial/root certificate vanishes.

The four-zero event gives a collision among transfer states:

\[
[M(a)]V_0=[M(b)]V_0=[M(b+c)]V_0=\cdots
\]

in projective space.

This constrains the orbit in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\), but it does not
force the orbit length to divide \(p+1\).  The possible projective orders are
controlled by the splitting type of the characteristic polynomial of the
monodromy element:

* split semisimple: order divides \(p-1\);
* nonsplit semisimple: order divides \(p+1\);
* unipotent: order divides \(p\).

The observed cases are all consistent with the nonsplit case, but the pencil
contains no argument excluding split or unipotent cases.

## 3. Fast falsification test

The proposed charging principle would predict:

\[
 p\mid C_{a,b,c}^* \implies \rho(p)\mid p+1
\]

or at least a bounded quotient

\[
(p+1)/\rho(p)=O(1).
\]

The decisive computation is therefore not a large census of values of
\(\rho\), but:

1. enumerate residual primes from reduced pencils;
2. compute \(\rho(p)\);
3. compute the projective order of the transfer product producing the
   collision;
4. compare the two orders.

A single prime with

\[
 p\mid C_{a,b,c}^*,\qquad \rho(p)\nmid(p+1)
\]

kills the proposed invariant.

The corrected object is the pair

\[
(\rho(p),\mathcal O_p),
\]

where \(\mathcal O_p\) is the projective Frobenius orbit of the recurrence
state.

## 4. Why a rank charge does not save one H factor yet

Suppose optimistically that every residual prime had

\[
\rho(p)=\frac{p+1}{k}.
\]

Then the number of possible zero locations below \(H\) would be bounded by

\[
O(H/\rho(p)).
\]

A fixed \((p,\rho,k)\) could support at most

\[
O((H/\rho)^4)
\]

ordered quadruples by the crude zero-location count.  This is insufficient by
itself: the content sum is weighted by valuations and the number of candidate
triples \((a,b,c)\) is already \(O(H^3)\).

To gain an H factor one needs an additional theorem such as

\[
\sum_{p\sim X} v_p(C^*)
\#\{(a,b,c):p\mid C^*_{a,b,c}\}
\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

A rank formula alone does not imply this.

## 5. Correct replacement invariant

The useful invariant is a transfer-matrix codegree:

\[
E_p(H)=\#\{(i,j):0\le i,j<H,
\ [M(i)]\xi=[M(j)]\xi\}.
\]

The residual content problem is controlled by projective state collisions,
not just zero recurrence.

A viable theorem would be:

\[
E_p(H)\ll H^{1+o(1)}+H^2/\rho(p),
\]

uniformly for residual primes.  Combined with the aligned pencil this would
convert rank information into the missing mass saving.

## Final verdict

* The four examples are a real reflection/monodromy signal but not a theorem.
* Reflection gives zero pairing \(r\leftrightarrow p-1-r\), not
  \(\rho\mid p+1\).
* Four-zero aligned collisions constrain projective monodromy, but do not
  force the nonsplit torus case.
* The first falsification target is a residual prime with split/unipotent
  monodromy or \(\rho\nmid p+1\).
* The smallest missing lemma is a uniform PGL2 transfer codegree bound, not a
  rank-of-apparition divisibility statement.
