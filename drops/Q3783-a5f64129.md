# 4. Section 5: two-linear-form sieve for minus

Set
\[
h=|p-7d|,
\qquad
p=7d+\varepsilon h,
\qquad
q=8d+\varepsilon h,
\quad \varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
The determinant of the two affine forms is exactly
\[
\Delta_-=7(\varepsilon h)-8(\varepsilon h)=-\varepsilon h.
\]
Thus, away from fixed coefficient primes, the only primes at which their two roots modulo \(\ell\) coalesce are \(\ell\mid h\).

For actual large prime pairs there is an additional automatic support condition:
\[
(h,14)=1.
\]
Indeed, if \(2\mid h\), then \(q=8d\pm h\) is even; if \(7\mid h\), then \(p=7d\pm h\) is divisible by \(7\). In the fixed-\(J\) macroscopic range neither can equal the small exceptional prime.

For \(\ell\nmid 14h\), the two forms have two distinct roots modulo \(\ell\), so \(\nu(\ell)=2\). For \(\ell\mid h\), \(\ell>7\), the roots coincide and \(\nu(\ell)=1\). Hence the standard dimension-two singular series satisfies
\[
\mathfrak S_-(h)
\ll
C_-
\prod_{\substack{\ell\mid h\\\ell>7}}
\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}
\ll
C_-
\prod_{\substack{\ell\mid h\\\ell>7}}
\left(1+\frac{2}{\ell}\right).
\]
The fixed local factors at \(2,7\) are absorbed into \(C_-\).

The needed Selberg statement is the standard uniform upper-bound-sieve theorem for two primitive affine linear forms with fixed slopes: if the variable \(d\) is restricted to an interval of length \(O_J(T)\), both forms are \(\asymp_JT\), and \(|h|=o(T)\), then
\[
\#\{d:7d+\varepsilon h,\ 8d+\varepsilon h\text{ prime}\}
\ll_J
\frac{T}{(\log T)^2}\mathfrak S_-(h),
\]
uniformly in such \(h\). Q3779 did not state this theorem as a named canonical lemma; that is a source-completeness issue, not an arithmetic error. In the final deletion range \(H=T^{1/2}/\omega=o(T)\), the required uniformity is safely inside this regime.

The singular-series averages asserted in Q3779 are correct. Write
\[
F(h):=
\prod_{\substack{\ell\mid h\\\ell>7}}
\left(1+\frac{2}{\ell}\right)
=
\sum_{r\mid h}\frac{a(r)}r,
\]
where \(a(r)\) is supported on squarefree \(r\), with \(a(r)\le2^{\omega(r)}\). Then
\[
\sum_{h\le H}h^\alpha F(h)
\ll
H^{\alpha+1}
\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{2^{\omega(r)}}{r^2}
\ll H^{\alpha+1}
\]
for every fixed \(\alpha\ge0\), because
\[
\prod_\ell\left(1+\frac2{\ell^2}\right)<\infty.
\]
In particular
\[
\sum_{h\le H}\mathfrak S_-(h)\ll H,
\qquad
\sum_{h\le H}h\mathfrak S_-(h)\ll H^2.
\]

For a surviving prime pair at fixed \(h\), Section 4 gives
\[
|X^{-,\mathrm{act}}_{p,d}(T)|\ll h.
\]
The q6 fixed-\(J\) range has \(p,q\asymp_JT\), so every row contributes \(\log p\asymp_J\log T\). Therefore, summing over the two signs,
\[
\begin{aligned}
W^-_{\le H}(T)
&\ll_J
\sum_{2\le h\le H}
\frac{T}{(\log T)^2}\mathfrak S_-(h)\,h\,\log T\\
&\ll_J
\boxed{\frac{TH^2}{\log T}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This derivation is correct for the range actually used, e.g. \(H=o(T)\).

With
\[
H_-(T)=\frac{T^{1/2}}{\omega(T)},
\qquad \omega(T)\to\infty,
\]
we get
\[
W^-_{\le H_-}(T)
\ll_J
\frac{T^2}{\log T\,\omega(T)^2}
=o_J(T^2/\log T).
\]
So the minus deletion exponent \(1/2\) is **VALID**.

One wording correction: if one wants to describe this as a band of width \(T^{1/2-o(1)}\), choose \(\omega(T)=T^{o(1)}\) with \(\omega\to\infty\) (for example \(\log\log T\)). For arbitrary \(\omega\to\infty\), the little-o bound remains true, but the geometric width need not be \(T^{1/2-o(1)}\).
