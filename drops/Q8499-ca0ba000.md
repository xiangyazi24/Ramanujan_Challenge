## 1. Sequence and available modular structure

The sequence is

\[
b_n=\sum_k {n\choose k}^2 {n+k\choose k}^2.
\]

The available exact congruence structure is:

* Apéry/Lucas congruence: for primes p,
  \(b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p\).
* Reflection: \(b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p\).
* Zero set control: the residues \(r<p\) with \(p\mid b_r\) have no consecutive elements and size \(O(p^{2/3})\).

These statements classify divisibility of a fixed prime p across residue classes, but they do not bound the number of distinct primes p dividing one fixed term b_n.

## 2. Literature theorem check

No standard theorem checked has the required form.

### Excluded theorem classes

1. **Density-one recurrence results.** Results controlling primitive divisors or prime factors for almost all indices do not imply a statement for every n.

2. **Fixed finite S-unit results.** They assume the allowed prime support is fixed before n. Here the required set is
\(\{p:p\le n, p\mid b_n\}\), which varies with n.

3. **Constant-coefficient recurrence results.** The Apéry sequence satisfies a polynomial-coefficient recurrence, not a fixed coefficient linear recurrence. Moreover, even suitable fixed recurrence results would need an additional uniform height-to-radical estimate.

4. **One-large-prime results.** Existence of a large prime divisor gives a lower bound on radical size and is logically opposite to the desired smooth-radical upper bound.

Therefore no imported theorem supplies the required implication.

## 3. Minimal new lemma giving the target

A sufficient new lemma is:

> **Every-n Apéry smooth-support lemma.** For every \(\epsilon>0\), there exists \(N_\epsilon\) such that for all \(n>N_\epsilon\),
> \[
> \sum_{\substack{p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}\log p\le \epsilon n.
> \]

Equivalently, since all relevant primes are at most n,
\[
\log\operatorname{rad}\{p\le n:p\mid b_n\}=o(n).
\]

A potentially more structural version, compatible with the modular parametrization, would be:

> For every n, the set of primes p≤n for which the Frobenius/Cartier residue class of n lies in the Apéry zero locus has total logarithmic weight o(n).

This is exactly the missing bridge from prime-by-prime Lucas congruences to an every-index radical estimate.

## 4. Why current modular parametrization does not prove it

The modular/K3/elliptic descriptions give strong information for a fixed prime p:

\[
p\mid b_n \Longleftrightarrow b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p
\]

after Lucas reduction. The obstruction is that n determines many different residues modulo many different primes. A proof would require controlling the simultaneous intersection

\[
\{p\le n:b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]

with a bound on the logarithmic mass of all such p. Existing single-prime estimates do not sum uniformly over this moving family.
