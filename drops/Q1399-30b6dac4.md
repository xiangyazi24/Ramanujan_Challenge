# General one-wrap primitive-Smith theorem audit

## Conclusion

The statement

> if `d >= max Z_p` and `a = |Z_p|`, then the primitive numerator/evaluation is automatically a p-unit

is false without an additional invariant-factor transversality hypothesis.

The reason is that the condition

\[
\pi_P\ker(C_{K,a}\bmod p)=N_{d,a}(p)\cap V(\mathbf Z_p,a)
\]

controls only the projected modular kernel.  It does not control the p-adic valuations of the primitive maximal minors after saturation.  Denominator-only kernel directions can survive with positive p-content.

Therefore the correct theorem has an extra Smith condition.

---

## Primitive block Smith form

Let the one-wrap normalized block be written over \(\mathbf Z_p\) as

\[
B_{d,a}= [\,T_{d,a}\mid U_{d,a}\,],
\]

where the columns corresponding to the folded zero labels have been separated from the regular directions.

After integral row/column operations there is a Smith decomposition

\[
P B_{d,a} Q
=
\operatorname{diag}(p^{e_1},\ldots,p^{e_s},0,\ldots,0).
\]

The primitive numerator is p-unit exactly when

\[
e_1=\cdots=e_s=0.
\]

The modular kernel dimension only detects the number of zero diagonal entries modulo p.  It does not determine the exponents \(e_i\).

Hence

\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\ker(B_{d,a}\bmod p)
\]

is insufficient.

---

## Counterexample pattern

Take

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}
p&0\\0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Modulo p,

\[
B\bmod p=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\0&1
\end{pmatrix}
\]

has a one-dimensional kernel projection.

However every nonzero maximal minor contains a factor p:

\[
\det(B)=p.
\]

After removing the modular kernel direction, the primitive determinant is still not a p-unit.

This is exactly the failure mode possible for denominator-only directions in the triangular Padé block: a direction may disappear in the F_p projection but retain positive p-adic valuation in the determinantal divisor.

---

## Weakest additional hypothesis

The minimal required condition is:

**Primitive transversality condition.**

Let \(L_{d,a}\) be the reduced block obtained after quotienting the folded nullspace

\[
N_{d,a}(p)\cap V(\mathbf Z_p,a).
\]

Then require

\[
\operatorname{Fitt}_0(L_{d,a})=\mathbf Z_p.
\]

Equivalently, the first nonzero determinantal divisor satisfies

\[
v_p(\Delta_s(L_{d,a}))=0.
\]

This condition is both necessary and sufficient for a p-unit primitive numerator.

It is strictly stronger than projection dimension.

---

## Computation size

The invariant can be computed from the reduced block only.

The required matrix is

\[
L_{d,a}: \mathbf Z_p^{O(d+|Z_p|)}\to \mathbf Z_p^{O(d+|Z_p|)}.
\]

No matrix of size K is needed.

Algorithm:

1. Construct the folded zero-label basis of size |Z_p|.
2. Quotient the known modular nullspace.
3. Compute Smith pivots of the reduced block.
4. Check whether the smallest nonzero invariant factor is a p-unit.

The unresolved invariant is therefore

\[
\boxed{v_p(\Delta_s(L_{d,a}))}.
\]

## p=379 defect

For

\[
Z_{379}=\{85,152,171\},
\]

and d=171, a=3, the fact that the local projection becomes one-dimensional does not prove saturation.

The full modular kernel dimension seven means that four additional directions may be denominator-only.  The missing datum is precisely the Smith valuation of the reduced seven-dimensional kernel complement.

---

## Heights up to ceil(3p/2)

Increasing the height to

\[
K=\lceil 3p/2\rceil
\]

crosses the one-wrap threshold and can remove regular non-target primes only if the primitive transversality condition holds for every non-target prime.

It cannot be concluded from d>=max Z_p alone.

For target primes, retaining the target factor requires that the same Smith invariant not be killed by the purification block.  Thus purification and target preservation are simultaneous invariant-factor requirements, not consequences of projection rank.

---

## Final precise statement

**Proved lemma.** If the reduced one-wrap block satisfies

\[
v_p(\Delta_s(L_{d,a}))=0,
\]

then the primitive maximal-minor numerator is a p-unit.

**Counterexample.** Modular kernel dimension and projected nullspace information alone do not imply this, as shown by

\[
\operatorname{diag}(p,1).
\]

**Open Smith invariant.** For the Ramanujan Challenge block, the remaining unresolved quantity is

\[
\boxed{v_p(\Delta_s(L_{d,a}))}
\]

for the reduced matrix of size O(d+|Z_p|).  Establishing that it vanishes for one-wrap non-target primes is the exact missing theorem.
