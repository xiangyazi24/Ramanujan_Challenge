# Corrected singularity--Padé audit

## 1. Local singular structure

The corrected indicial polynomial at \(\rho=17-12\sqrt2\) is
\[
(576-408\sqrt2)r(r-1)(r-1/2).
\]
Hence the local exponent set is \(0,1/2,1\). The Apéry generating function selects the non-analytic square-root branch. In a slit neighborhood of \(\rho\),
\[
B(t)=A_0(t)+A_{1/2}(t)(1-t/\rho)^{1/2},
\]
with analytic \(A_0,A_{1/2}\) and \(A_{1/2}(\rho)\ne0\). Therefore transfer gives
\[
b_n=[t^n]B(t)=\rho^{-n}\left(Cn^{-3/2}+O(n^{-5/2})\right).
\]
The earlier logarithmic expansion is not applicable.

Since \(\operatorname{Li}_s(t)\) is analytic at \(\rho\), multiplication only changes the analytic amplitude:
\[
B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t)=A_{0,s}(t)+A_{1/2,s}(t)(1-t/\rho)^{1/2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
R_s(n)=\rho^{-n}n^{-3/2}\left(C_s+\frac{C_{s,1}}n+\cdots+O(n^{-m-1})\right)
\]
for fixed truncation order \(m\), with \(C_s\) proportional to \(\operatorname{Li}_s(\rho)\).

## 2. Growing Taylor subtraction

If the analytic multiplier is expanded to order \(m\), the formal remainder near the singular point gives an asymptotic saving of order \(n^{-m-1}\) (times the same exponential factor \(\rho^{-n}n^{-3/2}\)). This is an archimedean estimate only.

A Padé/Hermite--Padé construction would replace the expansion by a rational relation among
\[
1,\operatorname{Li}_1(\rho),\operatorname{Li}_2(\rho),\operatorname{Li}_3(\rho).
\]
No known theorem gives the required pointwise lower bound for such a four-dimensional linear form with the needed dependence on the coefficient height.

## 3. Arithmetic height obstruction

The denominator information is carried by
\[
U_s(n)=L_n^sR_s(n).
\]
A hypothetical large gcd \(g_n\) gives integer relations after dividing by \(g_n\). To contradict a large gcd one would need a lower bound of the shape
\[
|a_0+a_1\operatorname{Li}_1(\rho)+a_2\operatorname{Li}_2(\rho)+a_3\operatorname{Li}_3(\rho)|
>H^{-\kappa-o(1)}
\]
for the produced coefficients.

The coefficients are not bounded independently of \(n\): clearing \(L_n\) introduces at least
\[
\log L_n=\psi(n)=n+o(n),
\]
and Hermite--Padé denominators/coefficient sizes grow exponentially in the construction order. Thus the useful comparison would require a lower bound uniform in a family of coefficient heights of size at least \(e^{\Theta(n)}\) (and potentially larger if the Padé order grows).

A small analytic remainder does not imply a small arithmetic height. The missing inequality is exactly:
\[
\text{(Padé remainder)} < \text{(available lower bound for the 4-term linear form)}.
\]
No such lower bound is known.

## 4. Applicable theorems

Baker theory applies to logarithms of algebraic numbers, not to the simultaneous values
\[
\operatorname{Li}_1(\rho),\operatorname{Li}_2(\rho),\operatorname{Li}_3(\rho).
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Li}_1(\rho)=-\log(1-\rho)\), but the higher polylogarithms are not covered by standard Baker linear-form estimates.

G-function denominator theorems (e.g. Chudnovsky/André-type denominator-growth results) control arithmetic nature of coefficients of G-functions; they do not provide a pointwise lower bound for the simultaneous values above.

Classical Padé theory gives constructions and upper bounds for remainders, but not the required irrationality measure for this vector of constants.

## 5. Conclusion

The corrected square-root singularity removes the false logarithmic premise, but it does not rescue the Padé route. The strongest rigorous conclusion is an obstruction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{singularity analysis gives exponentially small relative remainders, but no known lower bound compares against the arithmetic coefficient heights.}
}
\]

Therefore this method does not currently prove
\[
\log\gcd(L_n,U_1(n),U_2(n),U_3(n))=o(n).
\]
The precise failed step is the absence of a quantitative irrationality measure for the four-dimensional linear form involving \(1,\operatorname{Li}_1(\rho),\operatorname{Li}_2(\rho),\operatorname{Li}_3(\rho)\) at the coefficient heights generated by the denominator-clearing construction.
