# Verdict

The capacitated Hall criterion is **correct**. More generally, if every cycle has at least \(r\) available labels, then
\[
\delta:=\max_{A\subseteq\mathcal P}\bigl(|A|-h|N(A)|\bigr)
\le \frac1r\sum_s (d_s-rh)_+,
\qquad d_s:=|\{C\in\mathcal P:s\in N(C)\}|.
\]
Thus the proposed \(1/2\) bound follows from \(r=2\), and the directed-cycle \(1/4\) bound follows from \(r=4\), provided that a directed \(C_4\) really contributes four distinct signed tail-star labels.

Both constants are graph-theoretically sharp in the intended sense, with two qualifications:

1. In the \(K_{2,n}\) example one must take an **edge-disjoint packing** of \(C_4\)s, not the family of all \(C_4\)s of \(K_{2,n}\).
2. In the directed \(K_{n,n}\) example, sharpness assumes the label convention identifies a fixed signed tail-star consistently across the cycles containing that physical tail. Directedness alone only gives the \(r=4\) conclusion if distinct tail vertices necessarily give distinct signed tail-star labels.

The maximal-packing statements are also correct (assuming \(
u_4\) denotes the maximum size of an edge-disjoint \(C_4\) packing): every inclusion-maximal packing \(M\) has an edge union meeting every \(C_4\), and
\[
\nu_4\le 4|M|.
\]
The factor \(4\) is sharp even for actual \(C_4\)-hypergraphs arising from ordinary graphs.

Finally, standard bounded cardinality local-exchange optimality does **not** imply a uniform linear bound on pairwise label-collision energy such as
\(
\sum_s \binom{d_s}{2}
\)
or on the number of pairs of packed cycles sharing a label. The dense examples remain globally maximum packings and nevertheless have quadratic or \(3/2\)-power collision growth. But the phrase “any bounded local-exchange condition” is too broad literally: a local condition that explicitly bounds star degrees or label overlaps could of course imply a linear collision bound. What is ruled out is deriving such a bound from edge-disjoint packing local optimality alone.

# 1. Capacitated Hall is exact

Let the left side of a bipartite incidence graph be the cycles \(C\in\mathcal P\), and let the right side be the labels \(s\). Join \(C\) to every \(s\in N(C)\).

Replace each label \(s\) by \(h\) clones
\[
s^{(1)},\dots,s^{(h)},
\]
and join \(C\) to all \(h\) clones whenever \(s\in N(C)\). A matching covering every cycle is exactly a choice of one label for each cycle in which no original label is used by more than \(h\) cycles.

For \(A\subseteq\mathcal P\), its neighborhood in the cloned graph has cardinality
\[
h|N(A)|.
\]
Therefore Hall's theorem gives exactly
\[
\boxed{\text{a capacity-}h\text{ selection exists}
\iff |A|\le h|N(A)|\ \text{for every }A\subseteq\mathcal P.}
\]

If the empty set is allowed in the maximum, then \(\delta\ge0\). Failure of Hall is equivalent to \(\delta>0\). Standard bipartite deficiency theory also says that \(\delta\) is exactly the minimum number of cycles that must be left unmatched/deleted before a capacity-\(h\) selection becomes possible.

# 2. General overload-deficiency inequality

Assume
\[
|N(C)|\ge r\qquad(C\in\mathcal P).
\]
For \(A\subseteq\mathcal P\), set
\[
d_s(A):=|\{C\in A:s\in N(C)\}|.
\]
Double-counting incidences gives
\[
\sum_{s\in N(A)} d_s(A)
=\sum_{C\in A}|N(C)|
\ge r|A|.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
r\bigl(|A|-h|N(A)|\bigr)
&=r|A|-rh|N(A)|\\
&\le \sum_{s\in N(A)}\bigl(d_s(A)-rh\bigr)\\
&\le \sum_{s\in N(A)}\bigl(d_s(A)-rh\bigr)_+\\
&\le \sum_s (d_s-rh)_+,
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(d_s(A)\le d_s\) and \(x\mapsto(x-rh)_+\) is monotone.

Taking the maximum over \(A\) yields
\[
\boxed{\delta\le \frac1r\sum_s(d_s-rh)_+.}
\]

For arbitrary oriented \(C_4\)s, the hypothesis supplied in the question is \(r=2\), so
\[
\boxed{\delta\le \frac12\sum_s(d_s-2h)_+.}
\]

For directed-cycle-only families, if the four different tails of the directed \(4\)-cycle necessarily determine four different signed tail-star labels, then \(r=4\), giving
\[
\boxed{\delta\le \frac14\sum_s(d_s-4h)_+.}
\]

There is no extra graph theory hidden here: this is purely an incidence-set inequality.

# 3. Sharpness of the \(1/2\) bound: \(K_{2,n}\)

Take \(n=2m\) and the graph
\[
K_{2,2m}
\]
with left vertices \(u,v\) and right vertices
\[
x_1,\dots,x_{2m}.
\]
Orient every edge from the two-vertex side toward the \(2m\)-vertex side.

For \(i=1,\dots,m\), take
\[
C_i=u-x_{2i-1}-v-x_{2i}-u.
\]
These \(m\) cycles are edge-disjoint; in fact they partition all \(4m\) edges of \(K_{2,2m}\).

Under the intended tail-star labeling, let all outgoing edges at \(u\) carry one signed tail-star label \(s_u\), and all outgoing edges at \(v\) carry one label \(s_v\). Then
\[
N(C_i)=\{s_u,s_v\}
\]
for every \(i\), and therefore
\[
d_{s_u}=d_{s_v}=m.
\]
For every nonempty \(A\subseteq\mathcal P\),
\[
N(A)=\{s_u,s_v\},
\]
so
\[
\delta=(m-2h)_+.
\]
The proposed upper bound is
\[
\frac12\bigl((m-2h)_++(m-2h)_+\bigr)=(m-2h)_+.
\]
Thus equality holds.

So the factor \(1/2\) and threshold \(2h\) are sharp at the graph-incidence level.

**Qualification.** The family of *all* \(C_4\)s in \(K_{2,2m}\) is not edge-disjoint; there are
\[
\binom{2m}{2}
\]
of them. Sharpness uses the displayed packing of \(m\) edge-disjoint cycles.

# 4. Sharpness of the directed \(1/4\) bound

Let \(n=2k\). Write the two sides of \(K_{2k,2k}\) as
\[
L=A\sqcup B,\qquad R=C\sqcup D,
\]
where each of \(A,B,C,D\) has \(k\) vertices:
\[
A=\{a_1,\dots,a_k\},\ B=\{b_1,\dots,b_k\},\ C=\{c_1,\dots,c_k\},\ D=\{d_1,\dots,d_k\}.
\]
Orient the four complete bipartite edge classes as
\[
A\to C,\qquad C\to B,\qquad B\to D,\qquad D\to A.
\]
For each \((i,j)\in[k]^2\), take the directed cycle
\[
C_{ij}: a_i\to c_j\to b_i\to d_j\to a_i.
\]
These \(k^2\) directed \(C_4\)s are edge-disjoint and partition all \(4k^2=(2k)^2\) edges of \(K_{2k,2k}\).

Assume the signed tail-star label at each of the \(4k\) vertices is consistent across its outgoing edges. Then each \(C_{ij}\) has exactly four labels,
\[
N(C_{ij})=\{s_{a_i},s_{c_j},s_{b_i},s_{d_j}\},
\]
and every label occurs in exactly \(k\) packed cycles:
\[
d_s=k\qquad\text{for all }4k\text{ labels }s.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac14\sum_s(d_s-4h)_+
=\frac14(4k)(k-4h)_+
=k(k-4h)_+.
\]
Taking \(A=\mathcal P\),
\[
|\mathcal P|=k^2,
\qquad |N(\mathcal P)|=4k,
\]
so
\[
|\mathcal P|-h|N(\mathcal P)|=k^2-4hk=k(k-4h).
\]
When \(k>4h\) (equivalently \(n>8h\)), this lower bound on \(\delta\) equals the general upper bound, hence
\[
\boxed{\delta=k(k-4h)
=\frac14\sum_s(d_s-4h)_+.}
\]
So the \(1/4\) constant is sharp.

This construction is exactly an edge-disjoint \(K_{2,2}\)-block decomposition: pair \(a_i\) with \(b_i\), pair \(c_j\) with \(d_j\), and use the unique \(K_{2,2}\) on those two pairs.

**Qualification.** If “signed tail-star label” contains extra arithmetic data that can split one physical tail into several different labels across its incident edges, then the above graph orientation does not by itself prove arithmetic sharpness. The abstract \(1/4\) inequality only requires \(|N(C)|\ge4\); the sharpness realization additionally requires the stated consistent label incidence.

# 5. Inclusion-maximal edge-disjoint \(C_4\) packing

Let \(M\) be inclusion-maximal among edge-disjoint simple \(C_4\) packings, and write
\[
E(M):=\bigcup_{C\in M}E(C).
\]

## 5.1 The edge union hits every \(C_4\)

If some \(C_4\), say \(Q\), were disjoint from \(E(M)\), then \(Q\) would be edge-disjoint from every cycle of \(M\), so
\[
M\cup\{Q\}
\]
would be a strictly larger edge-disjoint packing. This contradicts inclusion-maximality. Therefore
\[
\boxed{E(M)\text{ is an edge transversal for all }C_4\text{s}.}
\]

## 5.2 The \(4\)-approximation bound

Let \(M^*\) be a maximum edge-disjoint \(C_4\) packing, with
\[
|M^*|=\nu_4.
\]
Every \(Q\in M^*\) meets \(E(M)\). Choose one edge
\[
e_Q\in E(Q)\cap E(M).
\]
Because the cycles in \(M^*\) are edge-disjoint, two different \(Q,Q'\in M^*\) cannot choose the same edge. Thus
\[
Q\mapsto e_Q
\]
is injective into \(E(M)\). Since every member of \(M\) has four edges and the members of \(M\) are edge-disjoint,
\[
|E(M)|=4|M|.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\nu_4\le4|M|.}
\]

If \(
u_4\) were intended to mean the total number of \(C_4\) subgraphs rather than the maximum edge-disjoint packing number, this statement would be false. With the standard packing-number meaning, it is correct.

## 5.3 The factor \(4\) is itself sharp

Start with one central \(C_4\) with edges \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\). For each central edge \(e_i=u_iv_i\), add two fresh vertices \(a_i,b_i\) and the three-edge path
\[
u_i-a_i-b_i-v_i.
\]
Together with \(e_i\), this gives an outer \(C_4\). The four outer \(C_4\)s are pairwise edge-disjoint, while the singleton packing consisting only of the central \(C_4\) is inclusion-maximal because every outer \(C_4\) shares its corresponding \(e_i\) with the central one. Thus
\[
|M|=1,\qquad \nu_4=4.
\]

# 6. What local exchange can and cannot control

There are several different quantities that could be called “collision.” They behave very differently.

## 6.1 First-moment incidence is always linear

Because a \(C_4\) has only four edges,
\[
|N(C)|\le4,
\]
so automatically
\[
\sum_s d_s
=\sum_{C\in\mathcal P}|N(C)|
\le4|\mathcal P|.
\]
Thus any claim only about total label incidence already has a uniform linear bound, with no local-exchange hypothesis needed.

The Hall deficiency is also trivially at most \(|\mathcal P|\), and the overload bounds above are first-moment bounds.

## 6.2 Pairwise label-collision energy can be superlinear

A natural collision energy is
\[
\mathcal E(\mathcal P):=\sum_s\binom{d_s}{2}.
\]
This counts cycle-pair/label collision incidences.

In the \(K_{2,2m}\) packing above,
\[
|\mathcal P|=m,
\qquad d_{s_u}=d_{s_v}=m,
\]
so
\[
\mathcal E(\mathcal P)=2\binom m2=\Theta(m^2)=\Theta(|\mathcal P|^2).
\]
Moreover every pair of packed cycles shares both labels, so even the number of unordered pairs of cycles sharing at least one label is
\[
\binom m2=\Theta(|\mathcal P|^2).
\]
But this packing is globally maximum: it partitions all \(4m\) edges, so no edge-disjoint packing can contain more than \(m\) four-edge cycles. Therefore it satisfies every cardinality-based \(t\)-local optimality condition, for every fixed \(t\), indeed every global exchange condition forbidding an increase in packing size.

So no such local-optimality hypothesis can imply
\[
\mathcal E(\mathcal P)=O(|\mathcal P|).
\]

For the directed \(K_{2k,2k}\) construction,
\[
|\mathcal P|=k^2,
\qquad 4k\text{ labels each have }d_s=k,
\]
and hence
\[
\mathcal E(\mathcal P)
=4k\binom k2
=2k^2(k-1)
=\Theta(k^3)
=\Theta(|\mathcal P|^{3/2}).
\]
Again the packing partitions every edge, so it is globally maximum. Thus even restricting the packed family to directed \(C_4\)s does not give a linear pair-collision bound from local exchange alone.

## 6.3 Even the number of alternative \(C_4\)s hitting a maximum packing can be superlinear

In \(K_{2,2m}\), the maximum packing has size \(m\), but the graph contains
\[
\binom{2m}{2}=\Theta(m^2)
\]
simple \(C_4\)s. Because the packed cycles partition all edges, every one of those \(C_4\)s meets the packing's edge union.

There is an analogous directed version in the oriented \(K_{2k,2k}\) above. Every choice
\[
a_i\in A,\ c_j\in C,\ b_\ell\in B,\ d_t\in D
\]
gives a directed cycle
\[
a_i\to c_j\to b_\ell\to d_t\to a_i,
\]
so there are
\[
k^4=|\mathcal P|^2
\]
directed \(C_4\)s in the support, while the displayed maximum edge-disjoint directed packing has only \(k^2\) members.

Thus a statement of the form “bounded local exchange implies only \(O(|M|)\) competing \(C_4\)s/collisions around \(M\)” is also false in arbitrary support graphs.

## 6.4 Precise scope of the negative conclusion

The dense examples rule out conclusions based only on facts such as:

- the selected \(C_4\)s are edge-disjoint;
- the packing is inclusion-maximal;
- the packing is \(t\)-locally maximum for any fixed \(t\);
- indeed, the packing is globally maximum;
- each cycle has at least two labels, or in the directed model exactly four tail labels.

They do **not** rule out a collision theorem whose hypotheses explicitly impose additional local structure, for example bounded label degree, bounded codegree, forbidden dense bipartite patterns, geometric separation, or arithmetic congruence restrictions.

So the literal phrase “any bounded local-exchange condition” should be narrowed. A condition can be designed to include collision control by definition. What the examples prove is:

\[
\boxed{\text{cardinality-based local optimality of an edge-disjoint }C_4\text{ packing alone gives no uniform linear pair-collision bound.}}
\]

# 7. Arbitrary support graphs versus the Ramanujan arithmetic support

The conclusions separate cleanly.

### Universal / incidence-theoretic facts

The following require no arithmetic input and therefore remain valid for any arithmetic instance once its cycle-label incidence sets \(N(C)\) are defined:

1. the capacity-\(h\) Hall criterion;
2. the general inequality
   \[
   \delta\le\frac1r\sum_s(d_s-rh)_+;
   \]
3. its \(r=2\) and \(r=4\) specializations when the corresponding lower bound on \(|N(C)|\) is valid;
4. maximal packing implies the packing-edge union hits every \(C_4\);
5. \(
u_4\le4|M|\).

### Only graph-theoretic sharpness/counterexamples

The \(K_{2,2m}\) and oriented \(K_{2k,2k}\) constructions show that no stronger collision conclusion follows from those universal hypotheses alone. They do **not** show that the Ramanujan/Apéry arithmetic support actually contains these configurations, nor that its signed tail-star labels can realize the same incidence multiplicities.

An arithmetic proof may still obtain substantially better bounds by using facts absent from the abstract graph model: congruence restrictions, prime/divisibility constraints, bounded star multiplicity, actual physical-row injectivity, transversality, forbidden support patterns, or other number-theoretic structure.

Conversely, if a proposed arithmetic collision bound is justified only by “maximum/maximal edge-disjoint \(C_4\) packing + bounded local exchanges + each cycle has \(r\) labels,” then these dense support graphs are genuine countermodels to that **graph-theoretic inference**.

# Bottom line

- **PASS:** capacitated Hall iff condition.
- **PASS:** \(\displaystyle \delta\le\frac12\sum_s(d_s-2h)_+\).
- **PASS, graph-theoretically sharp:** \(K_{2,2m}\) edge-disjoint pairing construction; not the family of all \(C_4\)s.
- **PASS under the four-distinct-label premise:** \(\displaystyle \delta\le\frac14\sum_s(d_s-4h)_+\).
- **PASS, graph-theoretically sharp:** oriented \(K_{2k,2k}\) \(K_{2,2}\)-block decomposition, with consistent signed tail-star labels.
- **PASS:** inclusion-maximal \(M\) has \(E(M)\) hitting every \(C_4\), and \(
u_4\le4|M|\) for packing number \(
u_4\).
- **FAIL as a generic inference:** cardinality-based bounded local exchange does not force a uniform linear pair-collision/second-moment bound; the dense examples are already global optima.
- **No arithmetic contradiction follows:** those dense examples are arbitrary support graphs, not demonstrated Ramanujan arithmetic instances.
