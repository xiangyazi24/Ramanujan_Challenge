# 2. The general reflection-cocycle lemma

The four-slope computation is an instance of a general two-line identity.

## Lemma 2.1 — one-row affine reflection transport

Fix integers `B,c`. For a running integer `p`, put

\[
u_c(p)=B-cp,
\qquad
u_c^*(p)=p-1-u_c(p).
\]

For every integer `Delta`,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u_c(p+\Delta)&=u_c(p)-c\Delta,\\
u_c^*(p+\Delta)&=u_c^*(p)+(c+1)\Delta.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

The first identity is immediate:

\[
B-c(p+\Delta)=B-cp-c\Delta.
\]

For the starred coordinate,

\[
\begin{aligned}
u_c^*(p+\Delta)
  &=(p+\Delta)-1-u_c(p+\Delta)\\
  &=p-1-u_c(p)+\Delta+c\Delta\\
  &=u_c^*(p)+(c+1)\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\]

QED.

## 2.2 Dependence of the starred equation

Suppose only that

\[
p_2-p_1=\Delta,
\qquad
u_{2}=u_{1}-c\Delta,
\]

and that reflection holds at both endpoints:

\[
u_i+u_i^*=p_i-1.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
u_2^*-u_1^*
  &=(p_2-1-u_2)-(p_1-1-u_1)\\
  &=(p_2-p_1)-(u_2-u_1)\\
  &=\Delta+c\Delta\\
  &=(c+1)\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus the starred slope equation is logically equivalent to the unstarred equation once the modulus shift and reflection are known. It contributes no new rank.

## 2.3 Flatness

The maps

\[
T_{c,\Delta}(p,u,u^*)
=
(p+\Delta,u-c\Delta,u^*+(c+1)\Delta)
\]

satisfy

\[
T_{c,\Delta_2}\circ T_{c,\Delta_1}
=T_{c,\Delta_1+\Delta_2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

So the normal form is a flat additive action of the prime displacement. There is no curvature or compatibility defect waiting to be detected by traversing two paths: all such rectangles commute identically.

This is the structural reason the four slopes look coordinated. They are coordinated because they are generated by one affine action.

# 6. Which Apéry conditions are actually independent?

For an exact-four leaf at one prime `p`, the local conditions are

\[
p\mid b_r,
\qquad
p\mid b_a.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The banked reflection theorem gives

\[
p\mid b_{r^*},
\qquad
p\mid b_{a^*}
\tag{6.2}
\]

for free. Therefore the four points do not impose four independent vanishing conditions. There are two.

Exact cardinality four additionally asserts

\[
p\nmid b_u
\qquad
\text{for every }u\notin\{r,r^*,a,a^*\}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is a large collection of local nonvanishing predicates. It can make exact-four primes arithmetically sparse, but it does not create a relation with another defining prime.

For two primes, the primary conditions are

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
p_1\mid b_{r_1},&p_1\mid b_{a_1},\\
p_2\mid b_{r_2},&p_2\mid b_{a_2}.
\end{array}
\tag{6.4}
\]

They live in

\[
\mathbb F_{p_1}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathbb F_{p_2}.
\]

There is no common coefficient field in which `(6.4)` becomes four equations on one variable. Counting them as “four constraints versus one `Delta`” is therefore invalid.

## 6.1 Direct and cross continuant returns are not extra marks

At one exact-four prime, the direct pair `(r,a)` and the cross-reflected pair `(r,a^*)` both consist of zeros. But the second pair follows from the first pair plus reflection. The associated `7/20` or `5/8` return orders are two coordinate presentations of the same two-orbit event, not two additional independent Apéry marks.

At two different primes, their continuants live in different characteristics. A resultant or common-root argument would require packaging them into one field or one scalar object, which is exactly the route excluded by the standing packaging warning. No such step is used here.

## 6.2 The only automatic joint integer consequence

For a fixed selected state and sign, both actual lower primes divide the same q-stripped two-value Apéry gcd:

\[
p_1p_2\mid A_\sigma(q,t).
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is genuine horizontal serialization, but its known height is compatible with many shell primes. Exact-four does not force a second copy of either lower prime. Thus `(6.5)` supplies no contradiction.
