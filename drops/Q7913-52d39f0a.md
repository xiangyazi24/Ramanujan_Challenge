# Source audit of the Q7904/Q7911 invariant-cycle claim

## Conclusion

The proposed identification must be weakened. The standard local invariant cycle theorem does give a Frobenius-equivariant **surjection**

\[
H^2(Y_{\bar s},\mathbf Q_\ell)\twoheadrightarrow H^2(X_{\bar\eta},\mathbf Q_\ell)^I
\]

for a proper degeneration. It is not, by itself, the statement that the two spaces are isomorphic. The missing input is the vanishing-cycle kernel. In the one-node K3 case the kernel is precisely the part that must be checked.

Therefore the claimed cancellation is not a formal consequence of SGA 7 alone. The missing lemma is: the specialization map has zero kernel after passing to the chosen primitive quotient (equivalently, the invariant vanishing-cycle contribution is absent).

## Frobenius convention

Let arithmetic Frobenius act on \(H^i\). With this convention

\[
H^2(\mathbf P^1_{\bar{\mathbf F}_q},\mathbf Q_\ell)=\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)
\]

has eigenvalue \(q\). Geometric Frobenius has the inverse eigenvalue. All Tate-class traces below use the point-count convention, hence eigenvalue \(q\).

## The relevant exact sequences

Let \(Y=X_s\) be the singular special fiber and \(\widetilde X\to Y\) its minimal resolution. For one split A1 singularity there is an additional exceptional divisor class:

\[
0\to H^2(Y_{\bar s},\mathbf Q_\ell)\to
H^2(\widetilde X_{\bar s},\mathbf Q_\ell)
\to \mathbf Q_\ell(-1)\to0.
\]

The last term is generated by the exceptional \(\mathbf P^1\). If the resolution is defined over \(\mathbf F_q\), this sequence is Frobenius equivariant and the exceptional contribution has trace \(q\).

For nearby cycles one has the SGA 7 local invariant cycle sequence

\[
H^2(Y_{\bar s},\mathbf Q_\ell)\longrightarrow
H^2(X_{\bar\eta},\mathbf Q_\ell)^I
\longrightarrow
H^1(Y_{\bar s},R^1\Phi\mathbf Q_\ell),
\]

where \(\Phi\) is the vanishing-cycle complex. The first arrow is not automatically an isomorphism.

For a single ordinary double point the geometric vanishing space is rank one. Whether it contributes to the invariant quotient depends on the Frobenius action on that vanishing cycle, not merely on the existence of the exceptional curve.

## References

The applicable result is the local invariant cycle theorem in SGA 7 II, Exp. XIV/XV: for a proper morphism over a henselian trait, the image of specialization equals the inertia invariants. The theorem gives image/surjectivity, not injectivity without an additional vanishing-cycle argument.

The Rapoport--Zink weight spectral sequence gives the same obstruction: extra terms are contributed by the monodromy/vanishing part of the special fiber. Regularity of the total space does not eliminate these terms automatically.

## Application to the toric K3 family

Let \(L\) denote the rank-19 divisor lattice coming from global boundary curves and let \(E\) be the extra exceptional A1 class. Then

\[
H^2(\widetilde X)=L\oplus \langle E\rangle\oplus V_{\rm prim,res}
\]

with \(\dim V_{\rm prim,res}=2\).

The generic invariant space has the form

\[
H^2(X_{\bar\eta})^I=L_{\eta}\oplus V^I,
\]

but identifying \(V_{\rm prim,res}\) with \(V^I\) requires proving that the primitive vanishing cycle does not survive in the kernel/cokernel of specialization.

The local Morse calculation only computes the rank-one vanishing character. It does not prove that this character appears in the middle extension stalk after quotienting by \(L\) and \(E\).

Hence Q7909's Legendre-symbol term cannot be inserted into

\[
e_p=\theta_p-t_p
\]

without this additional identification. The correct statement is conditional:

- if the primitive specialization map is an isomorphism after removing \(L\) and \(E\), then \(e_p=0\);
- otherwise the defect is the Frobenius trace of the surviving vanishing-cycle quotient.

## Missing lemma required for publication

One must prove the Frobenius-equivariant exactness

\[
0\to L\oplus E\to H^2(\widetilde X_{\bar s})
\xrightarrow{\sim} H^2(X_{\bar\eta})^I/(L_\eta)	o0
\]

on the primitive quotient.

Neither SGA 7 local invariant cycles nor regularity alone supplies this. A proof must explicitly compute the vanishing-cycle Frobenius action and show its image is exactly the exceptional divisor contribution (or zero after removing it). Until that calculation is supplied, the branch defect is not established to vanish.
