## 5.1 Why upper trace powers do not give (5.4)

For \(k\ge2\), an actual Schatten trace power is
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(A^*A)^k
 =\sum_j\sigma_j^{2k}.
 \tag{5.6}
\]
Upper bounds for (5.6) emphasize the largest singular values and do not bound the sum of a large population of small singular values.  The exact flat-spectrum certificate is:
\[
 r=N^2,
 \qquad
 \sigma_j=N^{-7/5}\quad(1\le j\le r).
 \tag{5.7}
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_j\sigma_j^2=N^{-4/5},
 \qquad
 \sum_j\sigma_j=N^{3/5},
 \tag{5.8}
\]
while for every fixed \(k\ge2\),
\[
 \sum_j\sigma_j^{2k}=N^{2-14k/5},
 \tag{5.9}
\]
which is extremely small.  Thus even genuine strong upper estimates for all fixed \(S_{2k}\)-traces are compatible with saturation of (5.2).

The canonical periodic fourth moment is not, in any case, the trace
\(\operatorname{Tr}(A^*A)^2\): it is a fourth moment of integer-row/column degrees before the surviving joint matrix is formed.  The later PAAC quantity is also not that trace.  PAAC counts two actual representations meeting in one prime-shadow cell \((q,J)\); a Schatten fourth trace is an alternating closed four-cycle in one fixed row/column flattening.  These identifications are different.

# 6. High-centered determinant repulsion

The exact centered determinant is (2.5), and both branches are (2.6).  There are two regimes.

### 6.1 Critical annulus

On (2.9), the determinant support is automatic by (2.10).  Therefore no argument based only on

```text
large |a| + coprime denominators + nonzero determinant
```

can give a power saving there.  In particular, multiplying pairwise recurrence carriers or appealing to primitive survival would be both unsupported and numerically irrelevant.

### 6.2 Ultra-high rows

For \(|a|>N^{159/100+o(1)}\), the two branches become genuine ratio constraints:
\[
 \frac{h_x}{P_x}\approx\frac{h_y}{P_y}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \frac{h_x}{P_x}\approx\frac{h_y}{P_y}\pm2.
 \tag{6.1}
\]
What is missing is a weighted codegree theorem for actual Apéry CRT rows along these moving rational slopes.  The canonical moments control columns, equal/reflected gaps, and same-characteristic returns; they do not control the moving four-characteristic slopes in (6.1).

Bettin--Chandee Corollary 1 is not directly applicable.  Its determinant equation has separated weights of the form
\[
 f(m_1)g(m_2)\alpha_{n_1}\beta_{n_2}
\]
with smooth weights in the archimedean variables.  Here the condition that \(t\) is the CRT lift of two actual Apéry zeros, together with the final masks, is a joint condition on \((t,p,q)\).  Decomposing it into rank-one pieces returns exactly the nuclear norm already audited in Q4596.

A positive unsigned estimate for all pairs satisfying (2.6) would be at least as hard as the TT-star target and would throw away the signed kernel (1.8); it is not an acceptable “smaller” theorem.  This is why the determinant route ranks below the affine-transfer route despite its attractive literal geometry.

# 7. The q6 fixed-gap four-zero route

Use the Q4631 notation.  For a complete selected state \(x\) and one translated cell \(I\), let
\[
 A_x(p)\in\{0,1\}
\]
be the **actual occurrence indicator**, including every selected-state and final mask.  Define
\[
 R^{\rm act}_{x,I}(\Delta)
 =\sum_{p,p+\Delta\in I}A_x(p)A_x(p+\Delta).
 \tag{7.1}
\]
If an actual anchor has more than \(H\) far actual partners, the mask-preserving combinatorial theorem gives one even gap
\[
 H<\Delta<\Lambda
\]
with
\[
 \boxed{
 R^{\rm act}_{x,I}(\Delta)\gg \frac{H^2}{\Lambda}.}
 \tag{7.2}
\]
At the critical scales
\[
 H=T^{\beta/2},
 \qquad
 \Lambda=T^{5\beta/6},
 \tag{7.3}
\]
this is
\[
 \frac{H^2}{\Lambda}=T^{\beta/6}.
 \tag{7.4}
\]

For a pair \(p,p+\Delta\), the four literal zero tests are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 p\mid b_{n-p}, & p\mid b_{B-6p},\\
 p+\Delta\mid b_{n-p-\Delta},
 &p+\Delta\mid b_{B-6p-6\Delta}.
\end{array}
\tag{7.5}
\]
They are mixed-characteristic tests.  The within-characteristic gaps differ by
\[
 h_{p+\Delta}=h_p-5\Delta,
\]
so the canonical same-characteristic twin theorem does not apply.

Let \(\mathcal C^{4Z}_{x,I}(\Delta)\) be the raw carrier count imposing the prime window and all four conditions (7.5), but not necessarily every final selected-state mask.  Then
\[
 R^{\rm act}_{x,I}(\Delta)
 \le \mathcal C^{4Z}_{x,I}(\Delta).
 \tag{7.6}
\]
The desired local theorem is
\[
 \sup_{x,I}\sup_{H<\Delta<\Lambda}
 \mathcal C^{4Z}_{x,I}(\Delta)
 =o\!\left(\frac{H^2}{\Lambda}\right),
 \tag{7.7}
\]
or the fixed-power version
\[
 \mathcal C^{4Z}_{x,I}(\Delta)
 \ll T^{\beta/6-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{FG4Z}
\]
Either would make every far-rich anchor impossible.

## 7.1 Direct Selberg-sieve improvement

Since the carrier is a subset of fixed-gap prime pairs,
\[
 \mathcal C^{4Z}_{x,I}(\Delta)
 \le \#\{p\in I:p\text{ and }p+\Delta\text{ are prime}\}.
 \tag{7.8}
\]
For completeness, the dimension-two sieve hypotheses are literal.  For squarefree \(d\), the number of \(m\in I\) satisfying
\[
 d\mid m(m+\Delta)
\]
is
\[
 \frac{|I|\rho_\Delta(d)}d+O(\rho_\Delta(d)),
\]
where \(\rho_\Delta\) is multiplicative and, for odd primes,
\[
 \rho_\Delta(\ell)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&\ell\mid\Delta,\\
 2,&\ell\nmid\Delta.
 \end{cases}
\]
Applying the standard Selberg upper-bound sieve at a fixed small power of \(|I|=\Lambda\) gives
\[
 \#\{p\in I:p,p+\Delta\text{ prime}\}
 \ll \mathfrak S(\Delta)\frac{\Lambda}{(\log\Lambda)^2}.
 \tag{7.9}
\]
Because \(\log\Lambda\asymp\log T\) and
\[
 \mathfrak S(\Delta)
 \ll \prod_{\substack{\ell\mid\Delta\\\ell>2}}
 \frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}
 =T^{o(1)},
\]
we obtain
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C^{4Z}_{x,I}(\Delta)
 \ll \frac{\Lambda}{(\log T)^{2-o(1)}}.}
 \tag{7.10}
\]
This is a valid unconditional improvement over the cap \(\Lambda/\log T\).  It is still far from (7.7): dividing (7.10) by (7.4) gives
\[
 \frac{\Lambda^2}{H^2(\log T)^{2-o(1)}}
 =T^{2\beta/3+o(1)}.
 \tag{7.11}
\]
No power is saved.

## 7.2 Why FG4Z is genuinely local and not the Palm target

FG4Z is pointwise in one state, one cell, and one fixed gap.  It can fail because of many disjoint fixed-gap pairs even when every degree is at most \(H\), while the Palm target only controls the mass of rare rich anchors after averaging.  Conversely, the Palm target permits exceptional cells whose total actual mass is small.  Thus FG4Z is strictly stronger locally and is not a renamed form of the final q6 consumer.

# 9. Strongest route: actual affine collision plus fractional reciprocal transfer

## 9.1 The proved arithmetic square

For each prime \(p\asymp N\), put
\[
 \mathcal K_p
 =\left\{r-\frac{p-1}{2}:r\in\mathcal Z_p\right\}.
\]
The canonical first zero moment gives
\[
 L_N:=\sum_{p\asymp N}|u_p|Z(p)
 \ll N^{3/5+o(1)},
 \qquad
 E_N:=\sum_{p\asymp N}|u_p|^2Z(p)
 \ll N^{-2/5+o(1)}.
 \tag{9.1}
\]
For \(g=(\lambda,\beta)\in\operatorname{Aff}_1(\mathbb F_q)\), define
\[
 \operatorname{AC}_q(g)
 =\#\{\ell\in\mathcal K_q:
       \lambda\ell+\beta\in\mathcal K_q\}.
 \tag{9.2}
\]
Define the raw positive multiplicity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W_q(\lambda,\beta)
 ={}&\sum_{\substack{p,p'\asymp N\\p,p'\ne q}}
 \sum_{\substack{k\in\mathcal K_p\\k'\in\mathcal K_{p'}}}
 |u_pu_{p'}|\\
 &\quad\times
 \mathbf 1_{\{\lambda\equiv p'p^{-1}\ (q)\}}
 \mathbf 1_{\{\beta\equiv k'-\lambda k\ (q)\}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.3}
\]
The literal mask-specific multiplicity \(W_q^\sharp\) inserts the all-distinct, high-centered, row, state, and cell masks before the pair is counted.  Hence
\[
 0\le W_q^\sharp(g)\le W_q(g).
\]
Put
\[
 \mathfrak A_N^\sharp
 =\sum_{q\asymp N}|u_q|^2
   \sum_gW_q^\sharp(g)\operatorname{AC}_q(g),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak A_N
 =\sum_{q\asymp N}|u_q|^2
   \sum_gW_q(g)\operatorname{AC}_q(g).
 \tag{9.4}
\]
The naive estimate \(\operatorname{AC}_q(g)\le Z(q)\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak A_N\ll L_N^2E_N
 \ll N^{4/5+o(1)}.
 \tag{9.5}
\]
The connected PAAC theorem proves instead
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak A_N^\sharp\le\mathfrak A_N
 \ll N^{1/2+o(1)}.}
 \tag{9.6}
\]
This is not a random-independence assertion.  The mean term \(Z(q)^2/q\) is the exact affine-group orbit mean, and the centered covariance is retained.  The gain in the coefficient square is
\[
 N^{-3/10},
 \tag{9.7}
\]
or \(N^{-3/20}\) after a square root.
