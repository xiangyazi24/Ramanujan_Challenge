# Q8863: Does (HM)_2 imply (HM)_3?

## Short answer

No.  The implication

\[(HM)_2 \Longrightarrow (HM)_3\]

is false from the currently available Apéry structural input.  The pair bound controls the second factorial moment, but the third factorial moment contains a genuinely new three-way correlation.  The missing input is not another pair CRT estimate; it is a bound on a conditional second moment (equivalently a 3D discrepancy / triple correlation statement).

The induction picture in the question is essentially correct, but the base case is exactly the hard part: (HM)_3 does not descend from (HM)_2.  It must be supplied by an additional Apéry-specific theorem.

---

## 1. Why (HM)_2 is insufficient

Write

\[
K(m)=\sum_p I_p(m),
\]

where \(I_p(m)=1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p}\).  Then

\[
F_r=\sum_m {K(m)\choose r}
\]

is the r-th factorial moment, while

\[
B_r=\sum_{p_1<\cdots<p_r} M\rho_{p_1}\cdots\rho_{p_r}
\]

is the independent model.

The pair statement gives

\[
\sum_{p<q}\left(J_{pq}-M\rho_p\rho_q\right)=O(\sqrt{B_2}),
\]

where

\[
J_{pq}=\#\{m:m\equiv z_p\pmod p,\ m\equiv z_q\pmod q\}.
\]

But the triple moment is

\[
F_3-B_3=\sum_{p<q<r}(J_{pqr}-M\rho_p\rho_q\rho_r).
\]

The pair sums contain no information about the sign pattern of the triple discrepancies.  A family can have every pair marginal correct while having a large third-order correlation.

This is exactly the probability-theoretic obstruction: pairwise independence does not imply three-wise independence.

---

## 2. Why the anchored-star obstruction is the right counterexample

The anchored-star construction demonstrates the missing degree of freedom.

Choose a common witness \(m_0\), and define each zero set to contain \(m_0\bmod p\).  Then every pair has a CRT solution through \(m_0\), and one can arrange the pair statistics to look acceptable, but the triple intersections all share the same point:

\[
K(m_0)\asymp \#\{p\in(X,2X]\}.
\]

Hence

\[
F_3\gg K(m_0)^3,
\]

which destroys the expected size.

The construction obeys many local Apéry constraints:

- bounded \(|Z_p|\),
- reflection symmetry,
- no adjacent zeros,
- CRT compatibility.

Therefore these properties alone cannot provide the missing implication.

The additional property must be global: it must prevent concentration of many zero conditions on the same integer.

---

## 3. CRT gives triple independence, but not enough cancellation

For fixed triples \((p,q,r)\), CRT gives

\[
J_{pqr}=\frac{M}{pqr}+O(1).
\]

This is stronger than pairwise independence.  The problem is summing the errors.

The deterministic bound is

\[
\sum_{p<q<r}O(1)=O(L^3),
\]

where

\[
L=\#\{p\in(X,2X]\}\asymp X/\log X.
\]

Thus the CRT error can be

\[
O\left(\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3}\right),
\]

while

\[
B_3\asymp \frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\]

The missing saving is a square-root cancellation statement:

\[
\sum_{p<q<r}
\left(J_{pqr}-\frac{M}{pqr}\right)
=O(\sqrt{B_3}).
\]

This is a genuine three-dimensional discrepancy theorem.  Ordinary CRT alone cannot give it.

---

## 4. The needed Apéry-specific property X

A valid implication would have the form

\[
(HM)_2+X\Longrightarrow(HM)_3.
\]

The required property X is something like:

**Conditional anti-concentration of Apéry zero sets.**

For every pair anchor \((p,q,z_p,z_q)\), condition on the CRT class occupied by the pair.  The remaining primes must still have the expected second moment:

\[
\mathbb E\left[{K_{pq}(m)\choose2}\mid I_p(m)=I_q(m)=1\right]
=
\mathbb E\left[{K(m)\choose2}\right]+O(\sqrt{B_2}).
\]

Equivalently, after conditioning on two zero conditions, the remaining zero conditions must behave like a fresh Apéry distribution.

This is much stronger than any known single-prime statement about \(Z_p\).

---

## 5. The pair-anchored decomposition is correct, but it exposes the missing theorem

The decomposition

\[
\binom r2(F_r-B_r)
=
\sum_{p<q}\left(T^{(r-2)}_{pq}-J_{pq}b^{(r-2)}_{pq}\right)
+
\sum_{p<q}(J_{pq}-M\rho_p\rho_q)b^{(r-2)}_{pq}
\]

is the right framework.

The second term is controlled by pair independence.

For example, for \(r=3\), the weight is essentially \(b^{(1)}_{pq}\), and the PP term becomes

\[
O(\lambda\sqrt{B_2})=O(\sqrt{B_3}).
\]

The first term is the obstruction:

\[
T^{(r-2)}_{pq}-J_{pq}b^{(r-2)}_{pq}.
\]

For \(r=3\), this is a conditional first moment.  It is not implied by the unconditional pair estimate.

So the decomposition does not prove induction; it identifies exactly what conditional estimate is missing.

---

## 6. Is the induction on parity correct?

Yes, structurally:

\[
(HM)_2\Rightarrow(HM)_4\Rightarrow(HM)_6\Rightarrow\cdots
\]

and

\[
(HM)_3\Rightarrow(HM)_5\Rightarrow(HM)_7\Rightarrow\cdots
\]

is the correct induction pattern if one has a conditional version of the hypothesis.

However, the induction hypothesis must be strengthened.

Ordinary \((HM)_r\) is not enough.  One needs:

**Conditional (HM)_r:** after fixing any admissible set of anchor primes, the remaining primes satisfy the same moment bound in the conditioned CRT fiber.

Then the induction closes.

Without this conditional strengthening, the induction step fails at the first step:

\[
(HM)_2\not\Rightarrow(HM)_3.
\]

---

## 7. Does conditioning on a pair preserve the Apéry distribution?

This is the central open point.

For random independent zero sets, yes: conditioning on two occupied primes only removes two Bernoulli variables.

For Apéry zeros, conditioning fixes an arithmetic fiber:

\[
m\equiv z_p\pmod p,\qquad m\equiv z_q\pmod q.
\]

The remaining events involve the sequence

\[
m\bmod r
\]

as this CRT progression runs modulo r.

The question becomes whether Apéry zero sets equidistribute along these special CRT progressions.

That is not a consequence of:

- Lucas congruences,
- reflection symmetry,
- bounded zero count,
- pair CRT independence.

It is essentially a new mixing/equidistribution theorem for the Apéry recurrence modulo varying primes.

---

# Final conclusion

1. **No:** (HM)_2 alone does not imply (HM)_3.

2. The missing property X is a **conditional anti-concentration / fiber equidistribution theorem** for Apéry zero sets.

3. CRT already gives fixed-triple independence up to ±1, but summing the deterministic discrepancy is too large by a factor of about X.

4. The pair-anchored decomposition is the correct induction mechanism, but it requires a conditional moment hypothesis.

5. The true reduction is therefore not:

\[
(HM)_2\Rightarrow(HM)_3\Rightarrow(HM)_5\cdots
\]

but rather:

\[
\boxed{\text{Conditional }(HM)_3\text{ is the first genuinely new theorem.}}
\]

Once conditional \((HM)_3\) is proved, the higher odd moments should follow by the anchored decomposition; even moments follow from conditional \((HM)_2\).
