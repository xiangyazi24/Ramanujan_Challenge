# Q8220 — complete first-order p-adic convolution defect and pointed-jet audit

## Executive verdict

There is a useful exact result, but it does **not** prove pointed simplicity.

1. The statement of the question has one normalization inconsistency. The sequence
   `c_r = binom(2r,r)^2` is not the square-root branch satisfying the displayed
   recurrence and does not convolve to the Apéry numbers. The recurrence-defined
   branch is
   \[
   c_0=1,\qquad c_1=\frac52,\qquad c_2=\frac{267}{8},\ldots,
   \]
   and its generating series satisfies `B(t)=C(t)^2`. Everything below uses this
   recurrence-defined `c`; this is the only normalization compatible with the rest
   of the question.

2. On the **square branch** (equivalently, in the current P3.2 notation,
   \(\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)=+1\)), the omitted upper-half convolution tail is
   completely controlled by one scalar
   \[
   \tau_p:=\frac{c_{N+1}}p\pmod p,\qquad N=\frac{p-1}{2}.
   \]
   If
   \[
   t_s:=\frac{c_{N+s}}p\pmod p\qquad(1\le s\le N),
   \]
   then \(t_s=\alpha_s\tau_p\), where \(\alpha_s\) is a universal second-order
   recurrence given explicitly below.

3. For every \(0\le n\le N\), put
   \[
   Q_n=\sum_{a=0}^n c_aq_{n-a},\qquad
   T_n=\sum_{s=1}^{N-n}t_sc_{n+s}.
   \]
   The **complete** first-order convolution defect is
   \[
   \boxed{
   b_{p-1-n}\equiv b_n+2p\ell\,(Q_n+T_n)\pmod{p^2}.}
   \]
   Combining this with the already banked
   \(b_{p-1-n}\equiv b_n-2pD_n\pmod{p^2}\) gives
   \[
   \boxed{D_n\equiv-\ell(Q_n+T_n)\pmod p.}
   \]
   Thus the tempting central-only calculation is valid **iff** its tail correction
   vanishes; dropping it without proof is invalid.

4. There is an exact transverse **stencil**, obtained without the square-root branch.
   If
   \[
   \mathcal A_r(y)=(r+1)^3y_{r+1}-A(r)y_r+r^3y_{r-1},\qquad
   A(r)=34r^3+51r^2+27r+5,
   \]
   then
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal A_r(D)=
   -\frac12\left(
      3(r+1)^2b_{r+1}-(102r^2+102r+27)b_r+3r^2b_{r-1}
   \right).}
   \]
   Hence at any pointed zero \(1\le n<N\),
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal A_n(D)=
   -\frac{3n^2}{2(n+1)}b_{n-1}\ne0\pmod p.}
   \]
   This is rigorous transversality of the **three-point derivative stencil**, but it
   does **not** imply \(D_n\ne0\): the left side also contains
   \(D_{n-1},D_{n+1}\).

5. In fact the exact Racah polynomial in the current repository identifies
   \(F_p'(n)=2D_n\). Therefore pointed simplicity is exactly the assertion that a
   zero field-node of the finite Racah polynomial is a simple root. The repository's
   exact order-two Racah seam is a universal double seam at *every* field node, so
   the nonzero stencil above is the derivative of a universal identity rather than
   a new target-selective coordinate. This agrees with the current failure record:
   ordinary Racah/discriminant/CD arguments do not prove the needed node
   squarefreeness.

6. There is a clean exact reduction of the remaining open point. Let \(v\) be the
   independent homogeneous Apéry solution with \(v_0=0,v_1=1\), and define
   \[
   U_r=r^3(b_{r-1}D_r-b_rD_{r-1}).
   \]
   Then
   \[
   U_{r+1}-U_r=b_r\,\mathcal A_r(D).
   \]
   At a zero \(b_n=0\), the homogeneous Casoratian gives
   \[
   \boxed{D_n=v_nU_{n+1},\qquad v_n\ne0.}
   \]
   Thus pointed simplicity is equivalent to a **global accumulated Casoratian
   nonvanishing** \(U_{n+1}\ne0\), not to the local nonzero forcing at row \(n\).
   This is the precise theorem-level obstruction left by the p-adic reflection
   attack.

7. There is a striking but **finite-only** bonus: direct exact computation gives
   \(\tau_p=0\) for the first 100 square-branch primes, through \(p=1297\). If the
   endpoint supercongruence
   \[
   c_{(p+1)/2}\equiv0\pmod{p^2}
   \tag{ES}
   \]
   could be proved on every square-branch prime, then the tail recurrence would give
   \(t_s=0\) for every \(s\), so the full first-order convolution defect really would
   collapse to the central term. I do **not** promote (ES) from this finite pattern.
   Even (ES), by itself, still does not prove \(D_n\ne0\).

The strict endpoint in the pointed statement is essential: at the midpoint
\(n=N\), antisymmetry forces \(D_N=0\) identically. For example
\(p=11,n=5\) has \(b_5=819005\equiv0\pmod{11}\), so the implication fails at
\(p=2n+1\).

## 1. Exact upper-half tail on the square branch

Let \(p\ge5\) be a square-branch prime, put
\[
N=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
and assume the banked square-branch reversal
\[
c_{N-j}\equiv\ell c_j\pmod p,
\qquad \ell=c_N\pmod p\in\{+1,-1\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]
Define
\[
q_j:=\frac{c_{N-j}-\ell c_j}{p}\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le j\le N.
\tag{1.2}
\]
Then directly from the definition
\[
q_{N-j}=-\ell q_j.
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Theorem 1 — every upper-half coefficient is divisible by `p`

For \(1\le s\le N\),
\[
c_{N+s}\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{1.4}
\]

**Proof.** At the midpoint \(r=N\), reducing (C) modulo \(p\) gives
\[
c_{N+1}-10c_N+4c_{N-1}=0.
\]
Using \(c_N=\ell\) and
\(c_{N-1}=\ell c_1=5\ell/2\), the right side is zero, so
\(c_{N+1}=0\).

Now take \(r=N+s-1\), \(s\ge2\). Modulo \(p\), (C) becomes
\[
(2s-1)^2c_{N+s}
-2(68s^2-170s+107)c_{N+s-1}
+4(s-2)^2c_{N+s-2}=0.
\tag{1.5}
\]
For \(s=2\), the last coefficient is zero modulo \(p\) (before reduction it is
actually \(p^2\)); for \(s>2\), both previous upper-half coefficients vanish by
induction. Finally \(1\le2s-1\le p-2\) for \(s\le N\), so the leading coefficient
is a unit. This proves (1.4). ∎

The last unit check is important: the recurrence stops exactly where it should.
At \(s=N\), \(2s-1=p-2\) is still a unit. At \(s=N+1\), the analogous leading
factor becomes \(p^2\), so no division by it is permitted.

Define the tail lift
\[
t_s:=\frac{c_{N+s}}p\pmod p,
\qquad 1\le s\le N,
\tag{1.6}
\]
and its first value
\[
\tau_p:=t_1=\frac{c_{N+1}}p\pmod p.
\tag{1.7}
\]

### Theorem 2 — exact midpoint initial value

One has
\[
\boxed{\tau_p=10q_0-4q_1-24\ell.}
\tag{1.8}
\]

**Proof.** At \(r=N\), the exact coefficient expansions modulo \(p^2\) are
\[
4(N+1)^2=(p+1)^2,
\]
\[
2(68N^2+34N+5)=34p^2-34p+10,
\]
\[
(2N-1)^2=(p-2)^2.
\]
Insert
\[
c_{N+1}=p\tau_p,
\quad c_N=\ell+pq_0,
\quad c_{N-1}=\frac52\ell+pq_1
\pmod{p^2}
\]
into (C), divide by \(p\), and reduce modulo \(p\). The result is
\[
\tau_p-10q_0+4q_1+24\ell=0.
\]
∎

Equivalently, extending (1.2) to negative indices using (0.3),
\[
q_{-s}:=\frac{c_{N+s}-\ell c_{-s}}p=t_s,
\]
so \(q_{-1}=\tau_p\). Formula (1.8) is exactly the missing boundary value in the
inhomogeneous reversal equation.

### Theorem 3 — tail recurrence

For \(2\le s\le N\),
\[
\boxed{
(2s-1)^2t_s
-2(68s^2-170s+107)t_{s-1}
+4(s-2)^2t_{s-2}=0\pmod p,}
\tag{1.9}
\]
where at \(s=2\) the last term is zero, so no `t_0` is needed.

**Proof.** Substitute \(r=N+s-1\) in (C), use (1.4), divide the equation by
\(p\), and reduce modulo \(p\). At \(s=2\), the coefficient of \(c_N\) before
this division is \(p^2\), hence contributes zero after division and reduction. ∎

Thus the whole tail is rank one. Define universal rational numbers
\[
\alpha_1=1,\qquad \alpha_2=\frac{26}{3},
\tag{1.10}
\]
and for \(s\ge3\),
\[
(2s-1)^2\alpha_s
=2(68s^2-170s+107)\alpha_{s-1}
-4(s-2)^2\alpha_{s-2}.
\tag{1.11}
\]
For \(s\le N\), every denominator introduced by this recursion is a `p`-unit, so
\[
\boxed{t_s=\alpha_s\tau_p\pmod p.}
\tag{1.12}
\]
This is the requested closed recurrence for \(c_{N+s}/p\).

## 2. Complete first-order convolution defect

It is enough to treat \(0\le n\le N\); the upper half follows from
\(D_{p-1-n}=-D_n\).

Set \(p-1=2N\). The convolution coefficient at the reflected index is
\[
b_{2N-n}=\sum_{j=0}^{2N-n}c_jc_{2N-n-j}.
\]
There are exactly two types of terms:

- a central-central block, where both indices are at most \(N\);
- two symmetric central-tail blocks.

There is **no** tail-tail block, since two indices strictly greater than \(N\) would
sum to at least \(2N+2>2N-n\). Therefore the exact split is
\[
\boxed{
 b_{2N-n}
 =\sum_{a=0}^n c_{N-a}c_{N-(n-a)}
 +2\sum_{s=1}^{N-n}c_{N+s}c_{N-n-s}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]
This is the piece that a central-only reflection calculation misses.

Use
\[
c_{N-a}=\ell c_a+pq_a\pmod{p^2}
\]
in the first sum. It becomes
\[
b_n+2p\ell\sum_{a=0}^nc_aq_{n-a}\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
For the tail, (1.4) and reversal give
\[
c_{N+s}=pt_s,
\qquad
c_{N-n-s}\equiv\ell c_{n+s}\pmod p,
\]
so the second sum is
\[
2p\ell\sum_{s=1}^{N-n}t_sc_{n+s}\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Define
\[
Q_n:=\sum_{a=0}^nc_aq_{n-a},
\qquad
T_n:=\sum_{s=1}^{N-n}t_sc_{n+s}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Combining (2.1)--(2.3) proves:

### Theorem 4 — complete defect

For \(0\le n\le N\),
\[
\boxed{
 b_{p-1-n}\equiv b_n+2p\ell(Q_n+T_n)\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{2.5}
\]
Hence, with the banked definition of \(D_n\) through
\[
b_{p-1-n}\equiv b_n-2pD_n\pmod{p^2},
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{D_n=-\ell(Q_n+T_n)\pmod p.}
\tag{2.6}
\]
Using (1.12), the whole omitted tail is one scalar:
\[
\boxed{
D_n=-\ell Q_n-\ell\tau_pR_n,
\qquad
R_n:=\sum_{s=1}^{N-n}\alpha_sc_{n+s}.}
\tag{2.7}
\]

A compact Laurent form is obtained by extending \(q_{-s}=t_s\):
\[
D_n=-\ell\sum_{j=n-N}^{n}c_{n-j}q_j.
\tag{2.8}
\]
The negative-index part is exactly the physical upper-half tail.

### Near-midpoint checks

These are useful sanity tests because this is where an omitted tail is easiest to
hide.

At \(n=N\), the tail is empty. Moreover
\[
Q_N=0.
\]
Indeed pair the terms \(a\leftrightarrow N-a\) and use
\(c_{N-a}=\ell c_a\), \(q_{N-a}=-\ell q_a\). If a fixed term occurs,
then either \(q_a=0\) (when \(\ell=1\)) or \(c_a=0\) (when \(\ell=-1\)).
Thus
\[
D_N=0,
\tag{2.9}
\]
in agreement with antisymmetry.

At \(n=N-1\), there is exactly one tail term:
\[
T_{N-1}=t_1c_N=\tau_p\ell,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{D_{N-1}=-\ell Q_{N-1}-\tau_p.}
\tag{2.10}
\]
Thus one row below the midpoint, dropping the tail changes the putative jet by the
**entire** endpoint scalar \(-\tau_p\).

At \(n=N-2\),
\[
T_{N-2}=\tau_pc_{N-1}+\frac{26}{3}\tau_pc_N.
\tag{2.11}
\]
The denominator `3` is the only new one visible here; `p=3` is excluded from the
square-branch discussion, so it is a unit.

## 4. Reflection of the Apéry recurrence: an exact nonzero stencil

This part does not use the square-root `c` sequence.

Let
\[
A(r)=34r^3+51r^2+27r+5,
\]
so
\[
\mathcal A_r(b):=(r+1)^3b_{r+1}-A(r)b_r+r^3b_{r-1}=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Put
\[
w_r=b_{p-1-r}.
\]
The recurrence at the reflected index \(p-1-r\) is
\[
(p-r)^3w_{r-1}-A(p-1-r)w_r+(p-1-r)^3w_{r+1}=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Use the exact symmetry
\[
A(-1-r)=-A(r)
\]
and the modulo-\(p^2\) expansions
\[
(p-r)^3=-r^3+3pr^2,
\]
\[
(p-1-r)^3=-(r+1)^3+3p(r+1)^2,
\]
\[
A(p-1-r)=-A(r)+pA'(r),
\qquad A'(r)=102r^2+102r+27.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Since
\[
w_r=b_r-2pD_r\pmod{p^2},
\]
substitution in (4.2), division by \(p\), and reduction modulo \(p\) give:

### Theorem 5 — inhomogeneous recurrence for the reflection jet

For \(1\le r\le p-2\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_r(D)=\Phi_r,}
\tag{4.4}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_r=
-\frac12\left(
3(r+1)^2b_{r+1}
-(102r^2+102r+27)b_r
+3r^2b_{r-1}
\right).}
\tag{4.5}
\]
All divisions are by `2`, a unit for odd \(p\).

Now suppose \(1\le n<N\) and \(b_n=0\). The Apéry recurrence gives
\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}+n^3b_{n-1}=0.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Two consecutive Apéry values cannot both vanish modulo \(p\): otherwise the
second-order recurrence propagates the zero pair back to \(b_0=1\). Thus
\(b_{n-1}\ne0\), and (4.5) simplifies to
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_n=
-\frac{3n^2}{2(n+1)}b_{n-1}\ne0\pmod p.}
\tag{4.7}
\]
For the pointed range \(p>2n+1\), the factors `2`, `n`, and `n+1` are all units.

This is a theorem, not a finite observation.

### What it does and does not prove

It proves that the derivative/reflection sequence cannot satisfy the homogeneous
Apéry recurrence at a zero. In particular, if one additionally had \(D_n=0\), then
\[
(n+1)^3D_{n+1}+n^3D_{n-1}
=-\frac{3n^2}{2(n+1)}b_{n-1}\ne0,
\tag{4.8}
\]
so at least one adjacent derivative value is nonzero.

But (4.7) does **not** imply \(D_n\ne0\). It is a nonzero three-point stencil, not a
pointwise nonvanishing theorem.

## 5. Exact identification with the Racah coefficient-index derivative

The current repository defines the finite Racah polynomial
\[
F_p(Z)=\sum_{m=0}^{N}R_m(Z),
\qquad
R_m(Z)=
\left(\frac{(-Z)_m(Z+1)_m}{(m!)^2}\right)^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
For an integer \(0\le n\le N\), all terms with \(m>n\) vanish, and
\[
F_p(n)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom nm^2\binom{n+m}m^2=b_n.
\tag{5.2}
\]
For \(m\le n\), logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
R_m'(n)=2R_m(n)(H_{n+m}-H_{n-m}).
\tag{5.3}
\]
For \(m>n\), \((-Z)_m^2\) has a double zero at \(Z=n\), so the first derivative
also vanishes. Therefore, with the `D` used by the current
`cron_supercong_check.py`,
\[
\boxed{F_p'(n)=2D_n.}
\tag{5.4}
\]
This is an exact characteristic-zero identity before reduction modulo \(p\).

Consequently the pointed-simplicity statement
\[
p>2n+1,\quad p\mid b_n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\nmid D_n
\tag{PS}
\]
is exactly the statement that the field node \(Z=n\) is not a repeated root of
\(F_p(Z)\bmod p\).

This puts the earlier Racah route in the correct place: it is not a different jet.
It is precisely the same coefficient-index derivative.

The repository's exact seam (commit
`590c2a2fcbcfab073da6a3a7f4cb10e1b982d5f0`) is
\[
\boxed{
(Z+1)^3F_p(Z+1)-A(Z)F_p(Z)+Z^3F_p(Z-1)
=-16(2Z+1)(Z^p-Z)^2.}
\tag{5.5}
\]
At every \(Z\in\mathbb F_p\), the right side and its first derivative vanish.
Differentiating (5.5) at a field node and using (5.4) reproduces exactly
(4.4)--(4.5). Thus the nonzero forcing (4.7) is the derivative of a **universal
field-node seam**. It does not add a target-selective equation capable of ruling out
\(F_p(n)=F_p'(n)=0\).

This is a theorem-level explanation of why the Racah/CD rewrite does not, by itself,
prove (PS). The repository's later higher-jet experiments also have false positives;
those remain finite no-go evidence, not an all-prime impossibility theorem.

## 6. Exact Casoratian reduction of pointed simplicity

There is a sharper way to expose the obstruction left by (4.7).

Let \(v\) be the second homogeneous Apéry solution over \(\mathbb F_p\), normalized
by
\[
v_0=0,\qquad v_1=1.
\]
Define the homogeneous Casoratian
\[
W_r=r^3(b_{r-1}v_r-b_rv_{r-1}).
\tag{6.1}
\]
The recurrence gives \(W_{r+1}=W_r\), and
\[
W_1=b_0v_1-b_1v_0=1.
\]
Hence
\[
W_r=1\qquad(1\le r<p).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Now define the inhomogeneous Casoratian
\[
U_r=r^3(b_{r-1}D_r-b_rD_{r-1}).
\tag{6.3}
\]
A direct discrete Lagrange identity using (4.1) and (4.4) gives
\[
\boxed{U_{r+1}-U_r=b_r\Phi_r.}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Because the harmonic formula gives \(D_0=0,D_1=6\), one may write this explicitly as
\[
\boxed{
U_{n+1}=6+\sum_{r=1}^{n}b_r\Phi_r.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

At a zero \(b_n=0\),
\[
U_{n+1}=-(n+1)^3b_{n+1}D_n,
\tag{6.6}
\]
while (6.2) gives
\[
1=-(n+1)^3b_{n+1}v_n.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{D_n=v_nU_{n+1},\qquad v_n\ne0.}
\tag{6.8}
\]

So (PS) is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{U_{n+1}\ne0\pmod p\quad\text{whenever }p>2n+1\text{ and }p\mid b_n.}
\tag{6.9}
\]
This is strictly more informative than saying “a transverse jet is missing.” It
locates the missing arithmetic in one accumulated Wronskian value.

There is an important local/global mismatch: although \(\Phi_n\ne0\) at a zero,
the jump in (6.4) at that row is
\[
b_n\Phi_n=0.
\]
Thus the local nonzero forcing **does not change the accumulated Casoratian at the
zero itself**. A proof of (PS) must control the preceding global sum (6.5), or an
equivalent endpoint quantity. This is the cleanest obstruction I can derive from the
current first-order p-adic machinery.

## 7. Endpoint and denominator audit

### 7.1 Midpoint

Antisymmetry gives
\[
D_N=-D_N,
\]
so \(D_N=0\) for every odd prime. Hence no theorem can replace the strict pointed
hypothesis by \(p\ge2n+1\).

There is an actual zero at the boundary:
\[
b_5=819005=11\cdot74455.
\]
Thus \((p,n)=(11,5)\) has
\[
p=2n+1,\quad p\mid b_n,\quad D_n=0\pmod p.
\]
This is an explicit obstruction, not a numerical pattern.

### 7.2 Pointed range

If \(p>2n+1\), then
\[
1\le n<n+1\le2n+1<p,
\]
so every denominator in (4.7) is a unit. In the harmonic formula for \(D_n\), all
harmonic denominators are at most \(2n<p\), so that formula is also `p`-integral.

### 7.3 Tail range

For \(1\le s\le N\), the divisor `(2s-1)^2` in the tail recurrence is a unit.
The first singular next step is \(s=N+1\), exactly beyond the tail needed by the
reflected convolution for \(0\le n\le N\).

### 7.4 Small primes

The square-branch statements above are stated for \(p\ge5\). Prime `2` destroys the
`1/2` normalization; prime `3` is excluded by the square-branch discriminant and
also occurs in the first visible rational tail coefficient \(26/3\).

## 8. The finite-only endpoint supercongruence clue

The exact tail recurrence shows that proving a single endpoint congruence would
remove the entire first-order tail.

### Conditional implication

If
\[
(\mathrm{ES})_p:\qquad c_{N+1}\equiv0\pmod{p^2},
\]
then \(\tau_p=0\), and Theorem 3 inductively gives
\[
c_{N+s}\equiv0\pmod{p^2}
\qquad(1\le s\le N).
\tag{8.1}
\]
Consequently
\[
T_n=0
\]
for every lower-half index and
\[
\boxed{D_n=-\ell Q_n.}
\tag{8.2}
\]
So the original “central-only” idea can be repaired by proving **one** endpoint
supercongruence, rather than separately controlling every tail coefficient.

### Finite fact, not theorem

Using exact arithmetic modulo \(p^2\), I checked the first 100 square-branch primes,
through \(p=1297\), and found
\[
\tau_p=0
\]
in every case. I did not find a proof of (ES) in the current repository, and the
question explicitly forbids finite-pattern extrapolation, so I record this only as a
candidate next lemma.

It is worth separating this from the known Apéry supercongruence currently checked
in `cron_supercong_check.py`; that file concerns
\[
b_{p+r}\equiv5b_r+10pD_r\pmod{p^2},
\]
not the pre-Apéry endpoint \(c_{(p+1)/2}\pmod{p^2}\).

Even if (ES) is proved, the Casoratian obstruction in §6 remains: the central formula
still requires a new theorem showing the corresponding pointwise derivative does
not vanish at a zero.

## 9. Independent exact verifier

I used an independent Wolfram Language kernel in this audit; no Python/code-interpreter
or sandbox computation was used. The verifier has two deliberately separate paths:

- the `c/q/t` path checks the complete convolution defect;
- the `b`-only path defines `D` from the reflected Apéry recurrence modulo \(p^2\),
  with no use of the square-root branch, and searches pointed zeros.

The harmonic formula is also checked independently against the reflection-defined
`D`.

```wl
ClearAll[bP2, cP2, dRef, harmD, squarePrimeQ, fullSquareAudit,
  dStencilAudit, pointedAudit, tauAudit];

squarePrimeQ[p_Integer] := JacobiSymbol[-6, p] == 1;

(* Apéry b_0,...,b_(p-1) modulo p^2, recurrence only. *)
bP2[p_Integer] := Module[{m = p^2, b = ConstantArray[0, p], r},
  b[[1]] = 1;
  b[[2]] = 5;
  For[r = 1, r <= p - 2, r++,
    b[[r + 2]] = Mod[
      ((34 r^3 + 51 r^2 + 27 r + 5) b[[r + 1]] - r^3 b[[r]]) *
        PowerMod[(r + 1)^3, -1, m], m]
  ];
  b
];

(* Recurrence-defined pre-Apéry square root c modulo p^2. *)
cP2[p_Integer] := Module[{m = p^2, c = ConstantArray[0, p], r},
  c[[1]] = 1;
  c[[2]] = Mod[5 PowerMod[2, -1, m], m];
  For[r = 1, r <= p - 2, r++,
    c[[r + 2]] = Mod[
      (2 (68 r^2 + 34 r + 5) c[[r + 1]] - (2 r - 1)^2 c[[r]]) *
        PowerMod[4 (r + 1)^2, -1, m], m]
  ];
  c
];

(* D_n = (b_n-b_(p-1-n))/(2p) mod p, from p^2 reflection. *)
dRef[p_Integer, b_List, n_Integer] := Module[{m = p^2},
  Mod[
    Quotient[Mod[b[[n + 1]] - b[[p - n]], m], p] *
      PowerMod[2, -1, p], p]
];

(* Independent harmonic/binomial definition of D_n.  Requires 2n<p. *)
harmD[p_Integer, n_Integer] := Module[{H},
  H = Prepend[
    Accumulate[Table[PowerMod[j, -1, p], {j, 1, 2 n}]], 0];
  Mod[Sum[
    Binomial[n, k]^2 Binomial[n + k, k]^2 *
      (H[[n + k + 1]] - H[[n - k + 1]]),
    {k, 0, n}], p]
];

fullSquareAudit[p_Integer] := Module[
  {m = p^2, N = (p - 1)/2, c, b, ell, q, t,
   n, s, a, Q, T, E, D, conv, bad = {}},

  If[! squarePrimeQ[p], Return[{p, "not-square-branch"}]];
  c = cP2[p];
  b = bP2[p];

  ell = Which[
    Mod[c[[N + 1]], p] == 1, 1,
    Mod[c[[N + 1]], p] == p - 1, -1,
    True, 999
  ];
  If[ell == 999, Return[{p, {"bad-ell"}}]];

  q = Table[
    Mod[Quotient[
      Mod[c[[N - j + 1]] - ell c[[j + 1]], m], p], p],
    {j, 0, N}];

  If[! And @@ Table[q[[N - j + 1]] == Mod[-ell q[[j + 1]], p],
                    {j, 0, N}],
    AppendTo[bad, "q-antisymmetry"]];

  If[! And @@ Table[Mod[c[[N + s + 1]], p] == 0, {s, 1, N}],
    AppendTo[bad, "tail-not-divisible-by-p"]];

  t = Table[Mod[Quotient[c[[N + s + 1]], p], p], {s, 1, N}];

  If[t[[1]] != Mod[10 q[[1]] - 4 q[[2]] - 24 ell, p],
    AppendTo[bad, "bad-t1"]];

  If[N >= 2 && ! And @@ Table[
      Mod[(2 s - 1)^2 t[[s]]
        - 2 (68 s^2 - 170 s + 107) t[[s - 1]]
        + If[s == 2, 0, 4 (s - 2)^2 t[[s - 2]]], p] == 0,
      {s, 2, N}],
    AppendTo[bad, "bad-tail-recurrence"]];

  (* Directly check B=C^2 for the coefficients used below. *)
  For[n = 0, n <= N, n++,
    conv = Mod[Sum[c[[a + 1]] c[[n - a + 1]], {a, 0, n}], m];
    If[conv != b[[n + 1]], AppendTo[bad, {"bad-convolution", n}]; Break[]]
  ];

  (* Complete defect, including all physical tail terms. *)
  For[n = 0, n <= N, n++,
    Q = Mod[Sum[Mod[c[[a + 1]], p] q[[n - a + 1]], {a, 0, n}], p];
    T = Mod[Sum[t[[s]] Mod[c[[n + s + 1]], p], {s, 1, N - n}], p];
    E = Mod[Q + T, p];
    D = dRef[p, b, n];
    If[D != Mod[-ell E, p],
      AppendTo[bad, {"bad-complete-defect", n, D, Mod[-ell E, p]}];
      Break[]]
  ];

  {p, bad}
];

(* Check the inhomogeneous D recurrence using b only. *)
dStencilAudit[p_Integer] := Module[
  {b = bP2[p], D, r, Aprime, lhs, rhs, bad = {}},
  D = Table[dRef[p, b, n], {n, 0, p - 1}];
  For[r = 1, r <= p - 2, r++,
    Aprime = 102 r^2 + 102 r + 27;
    lhs = Mod[(r + 1)^3 D[[r + 2]]
      - (34 r^3 + 51 r^2 + 27 r + 5) D[[r + 1]]
      + r^3 D[[r]], p];
    rhs = Mod[-PowerMod[2, -1, p] *
      (3 (r + 1)^2 Mod[b[[r + 2]], p]
       - Aprime Mod[b[[r + 1]], p]
       + 3 r^2 Mod[b[[r]], p]), p];
    If[lhs != rhs, AppendTo[bad, r]; Break[]]
  ];
  {p, bad}
];

(* Pointed-simplicity search: this path never constructs c,q,t. *)
pointedAudit[p_Integer] := Module[
  {b = bP2[p], nmax = (p - 3)/2, n, D, zeros = 0, bad = {}},
  For[n = 1, n <= nmax, n++,
    If[Mod[b[[n + 1]], p] == 0,
      zeros++;
      D = dRef[p, b, n];
      If[D == 0, AppendTo[bad, n]]
    ]
  ];
  {p, zeros, bad}
];

(* Finite-only test of the candidate endpoint supercongruence. *)
tauAudit[p_Integer] := Module[{c = cP2[p], N = (p - 1)/2},
  If[! squarePrimeQ[p], Return[Nothing]];
  If[Mod[c[[N + 2]], p] != 0, Return[{p, "not-even-p-divisible"}]];
  {p, Mod[Quotient[c[[N + 2]], p], p]}
];
```

## 10. Status ledger

### THEOREM / exact algebra

- The recurrence/convolution normalization requires the pre-Apéry branch
  `c_0=1,c_1=5/2`; the literal central-binomial-square definition is incompatible.
- On square-branch primes, `p | c_(N+s)` for every `1<=s<=N`.
- `t_1 = 10 q_0 - 4 q_1 - 24 ell`.
- The full tail obeys (1.9), hence `t_s = alpha_s tau_p`.
- The complete defect is
  \[
  D_n=-\ell\left(\sum_{a=0}^nc_aq_{n-a}
  +\sum_{s=1}^{N-n}t_sc_{n+s}\right).
  \]
- The reflection derivative obeys the exact inhomogeneous recurrence (4.4).
- At a pointed zero, the forcing stencil (4.7) is nonzero.
- `F_p'(n)=2D_n` for the finite Racah interpolation polynomial.
- Pointed simplicity is equivalent to nonvanishing of the accumulated Casoratian
  `U_(n+1)` in (6.9).
- At the midpoint `D_N=0`; `(p,n)=(11,5)` is an explicit boundary counterexample.

### FINITE FACT ONLY

- Complete first-order defect verified on 60 square-branch primes through 683.
- Inhomogeneous `D` recurrence verified on 301 primes through 1997.
- Reflection-defined `D` agrees with the harmonic/binomial `D` on 101 primes
  through 557 in the pointed range.
- 275 pointed zero incidences among 601 primes through 4423; no `D_n=0` found.
- `tau_p=0` on the first 100 square-branch primes through 1297.

### CONDITIONAL

If the endpoint supercongruence
\[
c_{(p+1)/2}\equiv0\pmod{p^2}
\]
holds for every square-branch prime, then **all** upper-half tail lifts vanish
modulo `p`, and the full first-order convolution defect reduces to the central
convolution. This does not, without a further argument, imply pointed simplicity.

### FAILURE / obstruction

- Dropping the physical upper-half tail is invalid without an endpoint theorem.
- The nonzero local stencil \(\mathcal A_n(D)\) does not force \(D_n\ne0\).
- The Racah/Newton/CD presentation is exactly the same coefficient-index derivative;
  the exact repository seam makes its first-order recurrence compatibility universal.
- Ordinary discriminant squarefreeness is not available: the current project already
  records large discriminant primes and higher-jet false positives.
- The remaining exact point is global: prove
  \(U_{n+1}\ne0\pmod p\) at a pointed zero, or prove an equivalent endpoint/global
  Casoratian statement. The first-order local p-adic reflection machinery alone does
  not supply that nonvanishing.

## Recommended next theorem target

The highest-value next lemma is therefore **not** another local reflection expansion.
It is one of the following, in order:

1. prove the square-branch endpoint supercongruence (ES), which removes the entire
   tail and validates the central first-order formula; then
2. attack the exact Casoratian target
   \[
   6+\sum_{r=1}^{n}b_r\Phi_r\not\equiv0\pmod p
   \quad\text{whenever }p>2n+1,\ p\mid b_n;
   \]
   equivalently, prove the corresponding finite Racah field-node is simple.

The first is a sharply isolated p-adic endpoint problem. The second is the actual
pointed-simplicity problem after every local/reversal/tail ambiguity has been removed.
