# The Diophantine approximation angle: what it proves and what it misses

## Summary

The irrationality-measure viewpoint is genuinely connected to the Apéry GCD problem, but only through a very coarse invariant: the size of the reduced denominator of an approximation to ζ(3). It cannot see the arithmetic reason that the observed gcd is tiny.

The key point is:

- irrationality measure controls **how much cancellation can occur in a denominator**;
- the Apéry GCD conjecture needs control of **which primes occur in the cancellation**.

These are different levels of information.

A proof must use the hypergeometric/recurrence structure of the Apéry construction, not only the approximation quality.

---

# 1. What the irrationality argument actually gives

Let

\[
R_k=\frac{a_k}{b_k}.
\]

The Apéry estimate gives

\[
|\zeta(3)-R_k| \ll b_k^{-2}\alpha^{-k},
\]

where

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
|b_k\zeta(3)-a_k|\ll b_k^{-1}\alpha^{-k}.
\]

After clearing denominators by \(d_k\),

\[
\left|d_kb_k\zeta(3)-d_ka_k\right|
\ll d_kb_k^{-1}\alpha^{-k}.
\]

The crucial point is that the denominator after reduction is

\[
q_k=\frac{d_kb_k}{G_k}.
\]

If \(G_k\) is large, then \(q_k\) is small.

Therefore irrationality theory gives a lower bound on \(q_k\).

Using

\[
|\zeta(3)-p/q|\gg q^{-\mu-\epsilon},
\]

we get only

\[
q_k\gg \exp(c k)
\]

for some constant \(c>0\).

Hence

\[
G_k\ll \exp(Ck).
\]

This improves the trivial exponential constant but does not approach

\[
\log G_k=o(k).
\]

---

# 2. Why even the optimal irrationality measure is insufficient

Suppose the dream happened:

\[
\mu(\zeta(3))=2,
\]

which is essentially the best possible.

The reduced denominator satisfies roughly

\[
q_k^{-2}\lesssim e^{-0.526k},
\]

so

\[
q_k\gtrsim e^{0.263k}.
\]

But

\[
d_kb_k\approx e^{(3+3.526)k}=e^{6.526k}.
\]

Therefore

\[
G_k\lesssim e^{6.263k}.
\]

Still exponential.

The reason is structural:

irrationality theory only knows the total denominator size

\[
d_kb_k,
\]

not the distribution of its prime factors.

---

# 3. What the conjecture is really asking

The conjecture is not saying the approximation denominator is small.

The denominator is enormous:

\[
b_k\sim (17+12\sqrt2)^k.
\]

It asks that the common factor

\[
G_k=\gcd(d_ka_k,d_kb_k)
\]

is small.

Equivalently, only a small portion of the denominator-clearing factor survives simultaneously in both sequences.

This is a local statement:

\[
v_p(G_k)
\]

must be small for almost every prime.

Irrationality measures are global and archimedean. They cannot distinguish

\[
p=101
\]

from

\[
p=10^{100}.
\]

But the GCD conjecture is entirely about such distinctions.

---

# 4. Why the observed small gcd is not explained by approximation quality

The numerical fact that

\[
G_k\leq 4590
\]

for small k is much stronger than any irrationality estimate.

This indicates a hidden algebraic mechanism.

The candidates are exactly the known Apéry structures:

## (a) Casoratian rigidity

The identity

\[
a_{k-1}b_k-a_kb_{k-1}=-\frac6{k^3}
\]

forces

\[
\gcd(a_k,b_k)
\]

to be supported only on primes dividing \(6k\).

Large common primes between the two solutions are impossible.

## (b) p-adic denominator matching

The missing statement is roughly:

\[
p\mid b_k
\Longrightarrow
p\text{ is absorbed by the denominator of }a_k.
\]

This is exactly what the lcm factor \(d_k\) is designed to measure.

## (c) Hypergeometric origin

The Apéry sequences are not arbitrary approximations. They come from a rigid Picard-Fuchs equation and a second-order recurrence.

The prime factors are constrained by:

- Lucas congruences;
- p-adic supercongruences;
- Frobenius traces;
- the Apéry motive.

None of these appear in a generic irrationality estimate.

---

# 5. Could Padé theory give the missing statement?

Possibly, but not through the approximation error alone.

The Padé construction gives much more than

\[
|\zeta(3)-a_k/b_k|.
\]

It gives explicit linear forms

\[
L_k=b_k\zeta(3)-a_k
\]

whose coefficients have hypergeometric descriptions.

A successful route would need to study the divisibility of these coefficients.

For example, one would like a theorem of the form:

\[
p>k/2,\ p\mid b_k
\implies
p\nmid \frac{d_ka_k}{d_kb_k/p}
\]

or equivalently a local cancellation theorem.

That is much closer to a p-adic theorem than an irrationality theorem.

---

# 6. The right reformulation

The Diophantine approximation perspective suggests replacing the GCD conjecture by:

> Large prime divisors of the Apéry denominator cannot survive as common divisors after denominator clearing.

For primes \(p>k/2\), this becomes especially sharp:

\[
p\mid b_k
\]

must imply a forced denominator contribution in \(a_k\).

This matches the top-window zero-set viewpoint:

\[
p\mid b_k
\iff
k-p\in Z_p.
\]

The problem is therefore not to prove ζ(3) is irrational enough. It is to prove that the finite-field zero sets \(Z_p\) have insufficient simultaneous alignment.

---

# Final conclusion

The Diophantine approximation route gives a useful sanity check:

- it proves that a huge GCD would contradict irrationality;
- it gives a nontrivial exponential saving;
- it explains why \(G_k\) cannot be as large as the full denominator.

But it cannot prove

\[
G_k=e^{o(k)}.
\]

Even an optimal irrationality measure leaves an exponential-scale bound.

The real theorem needed is arithmetic, not analytic:

\[
\boxed{
\text{large primes dividing }b_k\text{ must be killed by the p-adic denominator structure of }a_k.
}
\]

The Apéry recurrence, Casoratian, Lucas congruences, and Frobenius geometry contain precisely the extra information absent from irrationality theory.
