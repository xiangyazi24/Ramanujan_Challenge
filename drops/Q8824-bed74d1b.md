# Linear Palm extension ratios: what the new data actually show, and the first provable target

## Executive verdict

The numerical discovery is valuable, but its interpretation needs one decisive normalization correction.

Write

\[
M=X^2,
\qquad
F_r=\sum_{0\le m<M}(K_X(m))_r,
\qquad
R_r=\frac{F_{r+1}}{(r+1)F_r}.
\]

Under the ordered \(r\)-fold Palm measure, the expected number of **additional** hit primes is

\[
\boxed{
H_r:=\mathbb E_r[K_X-r]
=\frac{F_{r+1}}{F_r}
=(r+1)R_r.}
\tag{0.1}
\]

Thus \(R_r\) itself is not the Palm extension mean; it is that mean divided by \(r+1\).

This changes the diagnosis. For a Poisson random variable of mean \(\mu\),

\[
F_r=M\mu^r,
\qquad
R_r=\frac{\mu}{r+1},
\qquad
H_r=\mu.
\tag{0.2}
\]

So a decreasing sequence \(R_0>R_1>R_2>\cdots\) is already the exact prediction of **independent Poisson hits**. It does not, by itself, show that Palm conditioning suppresses later hits.

For the reported data, the true Palm means are:

| \(X\) | \(H_0=R_0\) | \(H_1=2R_1\) | \(H_2=3R_2\) | \(H_1/H_0\) | \(H_2/H_0\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 200 | 0.126 | 0.112 | 0.033 | 0.889 | 0.262 |
| 500 | 0.095 | 0.092 | 0.075 | 0.968 | 0.789 |
| 1000 | 0.082 | 0.080 | 0.072 | 0.976 | 0.878 |

At \(X=1000\), a Poisson law with mean \(0.082\) predicts

\[
(R_0,R_1,R_2)=(0.082,0.041,0.0273\ldots),
\]

while the observed values are

\[
(0.082,0.040,0.024).
\]

That is strikingly close to Poisson. The same is true at \(X=500\). Moreover, the displayed \(R_2\) values are not decreasing in \(X\): they are \(0.011,0.025,0.024\). The severe depletion at \(X=200\) weakens as \(X\) grows. Exhaustive computation removes sampling error, but it does not remove finite-size and integer-count effects in the very small third-factorial tail.

The correct conclusion is therefore:

> **The data strongly reject anchored-star/Cox-type clustering and support a Poisson or slightly sub-Poisson law. They do not yet reveal a new height-depletion mechanism.**

There is no known arithmetic theorem that explains the small downward correction. Height is definitively not the cause: even the product of **all** primes in \((X,2X]\) has logarithm \(\asymp X\), whereas for \(m\asymp X^2\),

\[
\log b_m=\Theta(X^2).
\]

All shell primes together consume only an \(O(1/X)\) fraction of the available logarithmic size.

The exact first missing theorem is already visible in the repository. For ordered \(p\ne q\), put

\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|,
\qquad
T_{p,q}^{[1]}
=\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}(K_X(m)-2).
\]

Then

\[
F_2=\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q},
\qquad
F_3=\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}^{[1]},
\tag{0.3}
\]

and hence

\[
H_2=\frac{F_3}{F_2}
\]

is exactly the pair-Palm weighted mean number of third-prime extensions. HM\(_2\), the CRT pair estimate, and the row-codegree theorem control the denominator \(F_2\); they do not control the numerator. The anchored-star construction proves that no argument using only the currently established local and pairwise facts can do so.

The most actionable new target is not the raw inequality with a fixed \(A\), but the **Poisson-scale Palm contraction**

\[
\boxed{
F_{r+1}\ll \lambda_X F_r}
\tag{PSC\(_r\)}
\]

for successive fixed \(r\), beginning with \(r=2\). The data say the implied constant is close to one. The first case \(\mathrm{PSC}_2\) is a precise third-order moving-modulus dispersion theorem. Proving it would be a real advance; it is not contained in the current pair-codegree machinery.

## 1. Exact Palm meaning of the ratios

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
\]

and define

\[
\Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad
f_p(m)=\mathbf1_{\Omega_p}(m).
\]

Then

\[
K(m)=K_X(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}f_p(m).
\]

For \(r\ge1\), define the ordered \(r\)-Palm sample space

\[
\mathscr A_r=
\left\{
(m,p_1,\ldots,p_r):
0\le m<M,
\ p_i\text{ pairwise distinct},
\ f_{p_i}(m)=1
\right\}.
\]

Its cardinality is exactly

\[
|\mathscr A_r|=F_r.
\]

Give every element of \(\mathscr A_r\) equal mass. At a sampled anchor
\((m,p_1,\ldots,p_r)\), the number of unanchored hit primes is \(K(m)-r\). Summing this quantity over the Palm space gives

\[
\sum_{(m,\mathbf p)\in\mathscr A_r}(K(m)-r)
=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_r(K(m)-r)
=F_{r+1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_r[K-r]=\frac{F_{r+1}}{F_r}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The ratio used in the question is

\[
R_r=\frac{1}{r+1}\mathbb E_r[K-r].
\tag{1.2}
\]

This factor is essential when interpreting monotonicity.

### 1.1 The correct numerical diagnostic

Define

\[
D_r:=\frac{H_r}{H_0}
=\frac{F_{r+1}}{F_r}\frac{F_0}{F_1}
=\frac{(r+1)R_r}{R_0}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

For an exact Poisson law, \(D_r=1\) for every \(r\). Values below one measure genuine Palm depletion relative to the observed mean.

The reported data give

\[
D_1=(0.889,0.968,0.976),
\qquad
D_2=(0.262,0.789,0.878)
\]

for \(X=200,500,1000\). The normalized ratios move **toward** one as \(X\) grows. This is evidence for a Poisson limit with finite-size underdispersion, not evidence for increasing repulsion with \(X\).

### 1.2 “Generic \(K=1\) rows dominate” is not the right formulation

When the mean is small, the ordinary row count is indeed dominated by \(K=0\) and \(K=1\). But factorial moments deliberately remove those rows:

\[
F_2\text{ receives contributions only from }K\ge2,
\qquad
F_3\text{ only from }K\ge3.
\]

Thus the decline of the Palm ratios is a statement about the relative abundance of the successive high-degree tails. It cannot be explained directly by saying that the total population contains many \(K=1\) rows.

## 2. The LPE inequality and the exact MGF consequence

Put

\[
a_r:=\frac{F_r}{M r!}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{R_r=\frac{a_{r+1}}{a_r}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

If

\[
R_r\le A
\qquad\text{for all }r,
\tag{2.2}
\]

then induction gives

\[
F_r\le M A^r r!.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The factorial generating function is

\[
Q_X(s)
:=\frac1M\sum_{m<M}(1+s)^{K(m)}
=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{F_r}{M r!}s^r.
\tag{2.4}
\]

For \(s\ge0\) with \(As<1\), (2.3) gives

\[
Q_X(s)\le\sum_{r\ge0}(As)^r
=\frac1{1-As}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Setting \(s=e^t-1\) yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac1M\sum_{m<M}e^{tK(m)}
\le\frac1{1-A(e^t-1)}}
\tag{2.6}
\]

whenever

\[
t<\log(1+1/A).
\]

For \(A=0.13\), the endpoint is approximately \(2.16\), consistent with the stated \(2.2\).

A uniform bound in (2.6) immediately gives a pointwise row-degree bound. Since one term in the average is \(e^{tK(m)}\),

\[
e^{tK(m)}\le M\,Q_X(e^t-1),
\]

and hence

\[
K_X(m)\le\frac{2\log X+O_t(1)}{t}
=O_t(\log X)
\qquad(m<X^2).
\tag{2.7}
\]

After multiplying by \(\log p\) and summing the dyadic shells, this is far more than enough for \(\log G_n=o(n)\).

### 2.1 Constant LPE is a geometric envelope

There is an exact probability model behind equality in (2.2). A geometric random variable on \(\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\) with mean \(A\) has

\[
\mathbb P(K=k)
=\frac1{1+A}
\left(\frac{A}{1+A}\right)^k
\]

and factorial moments

\[
\mathbb E(K)_r=r!A^r.
\]

Thus

\[
R_r=A
\]

for every \(r\). LPE says that the binomial-moment generating function of the Apéry row degree is coefficientwise dominated by this geometric envelope.

The observed data are much closer to a Poisson law than to the geometric extremizer.

## 3. What decreasing \(R_r\) means probabilistically

### 3.1 Exact log-concavity formulation

Because \(R_r=a_{r+1}/a_r\), the inequalities

\[
R_{r+1}\le R_r
\]

are exactly log-concavity of the binomial-moment sequence \((a_r)\):

\[
a_{r+1}^2\ge a_ra_{r+2}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

In terms of the original factorial moments,

\[
\boxed{
(r+2)F_{r+1}^2
\ge(r+1)F_rF_{r+2}.}
\tag{BMLC\(_r\)}
\]

Thus the strongest clean conjecture suggested by the data is not merely LPE but **binomial-moment log-concavity** for the row-degree distribution.

If BMLC holds for all \(r\), then

\[
R_r\le R_0=\frac{F_1}{M}=:\mu_X.
\]

Consequently a uniform bound \(\mu_X=O(1)\) would imply all-r LPE immediately.

This exposes another genuine missing input. Let

\[
A_p=|\Omega_p|,
\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{A_p}{M},
\qquad
\mu_X=\sum_p\rho_p.
\]

For every \(p\),

\[
|A_p-M|Z_p|/p|\le |Z_p|,
\]

so the repository proves

\[
\left|\mu_X-\lambda_X\right|
\le\frac1M\sum_p|Z_p|
\le\frac{2\lambda_X}{X}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore the first LPE ratio is already

\[
R_0=\mu_X=(1+o(1))\lambda_X.
\]

A uniform constant \(A\) in LPE requires at least

\[
\lambda_X=O(1),
\tag{3.3}
\]

or equivalently \(\sum_{X<p\le2X}|Z_p|=O(X)\). This is much weaker than the expected mean-one law, but it is still not implied by the unconditional bound \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\).

So LPE is not “free at level zero”: even its first ratio contains an open average zero-count theorem.

### 3.2 Poisson model

For \(K\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)\),

\[
\mathbb E(K)_r=\mu^r,
\qquad
R_r=\frac{\mu}{r+1},
\qquad
H_r=\mu.
\]

This is the principal explanation of the observed decline.

### 3.3 Independent Bernoulli / Poisson-binomial model

Let independent Bernoulli variables \(Y_p\) have hit probabilities \(\rho_p\), and let

\[
K_{\rm ind}=\sum_pY_p.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb E(1+s)^{K_{\rm ind}}
=\prod_p(1+\rho_ps),
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E(K_{\rm ind})_r
=r!\,e_r((\rho_p)_p).
\]

Thus

\[
R_r^{\rm ind}
=\frac{e_{r+1}(\rho)}{e_r(\rho)},
\qquad
H_r^{\rm ind}
=(r+1)\frac{e_{r+1}(\rho)}{e_r(\rho)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Newton’s inequalities imply that \(R_r^{\rm ind}\) decreases. They also imply that \(H_r^{\rm ind}\) decreases for a finite collection of Bernoulli slots. When the individual probabilities are tiny and their sum is fixed, this finite-population depletion disappears and \(H_r^{\rm ind}\to\mu\), the Poisson value.

For example,

\[
H_1^{\rm ind}
=\mu-rac{\sum_p\rho_p^2}{\mu}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Therefore a small downward displacement of \(H_1\) from \(\mu\) is already predicted by independent Bernoulli hits with nonzero individual probabilities.

### 3.4 The exact baseline that should be computed

The proper comparison is not merely

\[
R_r\stackrel{?}{\le}\frac{\mu}{r+1}.
\]

Using the exact finite marginals \(\rho_p=A_p/M\), compute

\[
R_r^{\rm PB}=rac{e_{r+1}((\rho_p))}{e_r((\rho_p))}
\]

and the normalized arithmetic excess

\[
\Gamma_r
:=\frac{F_{r+1}/F_r}
{(r+1)e_{r+1}((\rho_p))/e_r((\rho_p))}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Then:

* \(\Gamma_r\approx1\) means that all observed decline is explained by the exact independent Poisson-binomial model;
* \(\Gamma_r<1\) stably and significantly means genuine arithmetic negative dependence;
* \(\Gamma_r>1\) detects Palm clustering.

This is the first numerical computation that should be performed on the exhaustive data. The presently quoted ratios do not separate these effects.

### 3.5 Mixed Poisson models diagnose clustering

Suppose instead that the rows have a random intensity \(\Lambda\), and conditional on \(\Lambda\), \(K\) is Poisson. Then

\[
\mathbb E(K)_r=\mathbb E\Lambda^r,
\qquad
H_r=\frac{\mathbb E\Lambda^{r+1}}{\mathbb E\Lambda^r}.
\]

The moment sequence of \(\Lambda\) is log-convex, so \(H_r\) is nondecreasing. Palm conditioning size-biases toward rows of larger intensity.

The anchored star is an extreme version of this phenomenon: once the Palm measure lands on the anchor row, the extension mean is of order \(X/\log X\).

The observed decreasing \(H_r\) therefore gives genuine evidence **against** a Cox/mixed-Poisson cluster model. This is a stronger and more accurate statement than merely observing that \(R_r\) decreases.

### 3.6 “Strict decrease” does not automatically mean super-exponential decay

From

\[
F_r=M r!\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}R_j,
\tag{3.7}
\]

strict decrease of \(R_j\) gives no quantitative rate by itself.

For a Poisson law,

\[
R_j=\frac\mu{j+1},
\]

but

\[
F_r=M\mu^r,
\]

which is ordinary exponential behavior in \(r\), not a mysterious super-exponential suppression. A genuinely super-exponential improvement requires a quantified decay stronger than the natural \(1/(r+1)\) factor.

## 4. The arithmetic mechanism is not height depletion

Assume \(m\asymp X^2\). The Apéry asymptotic gives

\[
\log b_m=\Theta(m)=\Theta(X^2).
\]

If \(r\) distinct shell primes divide \(b_m\), they use logarithmic radical mass about

\[
r\log X.
\]

Even at the maximum possible shell degree

\[
r\asymp\frac X{\log X},
\]

the total cost is only \(O(X)\). Equivalently,

\[
\log\prod_{X<p\le2X}p
=\vartheta(2X)-\vartheta(X)
\asymp X.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since \(X\ll X^2\), the full shell fits comfortably inside the height of \(b_m\). This remains true even if every shell prime occurs only once and \(\operatorname{rad}(b_m)\) is nearly as large as \(b_m\).

Therefore none of the following explains the observed Palm depletion:

* the size bound \(b_m=\exp(O(m))\);
* squarefreeness of most prime factors;
* an \(abc\)-quality radical estimate;
* a primitive-divisor theorem;
* the fact that \(\prod p_i\mid b_m\).

The repository’s `pairpalm_result.tex` records the exact same obstruction: the common integer sequence only gives

\[
K_X(m)\log X\le\log b_m=O(m),
\]

which is weaker, at \(m\asymp X^2\), than the trivial shell size \(K_X(m)\ll X/\log X\).

### What mechanism could be real?

The only plausible mechanism consistent with the data is **cross-characteristic nonalignment**:

> after an occupied tuple of shell congruences has selected a row \(m\), the moving target \(m\bmod q\) does not preferentially land in the Apéry zero set \(Z_q\) for the remaining primes \(q\).

This is an arithmetic equidistribution statement, not a height statement. It is exactly the missing content of HM\(_3\), HM\(_8\), LGTQ-MTD, and the pair-Palm residuals.

No current theorem derives this nonalignment from the local Möbius orbit. The local recurrence varies \(d\) at one fixed characteristic; Palm extension fixes \(m\) and varies the characteristic. The quantifiers are transverse.

## 5. Formalizing “generic positioning after \(r\) hits”

For an ordered tuple of pairwise distinct shell primes

\[
\mathbf p=(p_1,\ldots,p_r),
\]

put

\[
J_{\mathbf p}
:=\sum_{m<M}\prod_{i=1}^rf_{p_i}(m)
=\left|\bigcap_{i=1}^r\Omega_{p_i}\right|,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and define its total one-prime extension count

\[
T_{\mathbf p}
:=\sum_{m<M}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^rf_{p_i}(m)\right)
\sum_{q\notin\{p_1,\ldots,p_r\}}f_q(m).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Counting by rows gives the exact identities

\[
\boxed{
F_r=\sum_{\mathbf p}J_{\mathbf p},
\qquad
F_{r+1}=\sum_{\mathbf p}T_{\mathbf p}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Let

\[
\rho_q=\frac{|\Omega_q|}{M},
\qquad
\mu_{\mathbf p}=\sum_{q\notin\mathbf p}\rho_q,
\qquad
\mu=\sum_q\rho_q=F_1/M.
\]

The independent finite-marginal prediction is

\[
T_{\mathbf p}\approx J_{\mathbf p}\mu_{\mathbf p}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Define the positive Palm extension excess

\[
\mathscr E_r^+
:=\sum_{\mathbf p}
\left(T_{\mathbf p}-J_{\mathbf p}\mu_{\mathbf p}\right)_+.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Since \(\mu_{\mathbf p}\le\mu\),

\[
F_{r+1}
\le \mu F_r+\mathscr E_r^+.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore the Poisson-scale contraction

\[
F_{r+1}\le C\mu F_r
\]

would follow from

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_r^+
\le(C-1)\mu F_r.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Likewise, LPE with a fixed \(A\) follows if

\[
\mathscr E_r^+
\le\bigl(A(r+1)-\mu\bigr)F_r.
\tag{5.8}
\]

These are exact formulations of “the remaining primes are generically positioned.” The phrase has no content until an estimate such as (5.7) or (5.8) is proved.

### 5.1 What CRT does and does not give

When \(r\ge2\),

\[
p_1\cdots p_r>X^r\ge X^2=M.
\]

Thus any fixed zero-residue tuple

\[
(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in Z_{p_1}\times\cdots\times Z_{p_r}
\]

has at most one CRT representative in \([0,M)\). This proves injectivity.

It does **not** prove that the unique representative is uniformly distributed in \([0,p_1\cdots p_r)\), and it says nothing about whether that representative happens to satisfy many additional Apéry conditions. The anchored star is precisely a family in which the CRT representatives are coherently aligned at one row.

Therefore “a specific residue class modulo \(p_1\cdots p_r\)” is not the conclusion; the relevant intersection is a union of \(\prod_i|Z_{p_i}|\) such classes, and the short-interval landing of their representatives is the entire problem.

## 6. Why HM\(_2\) and pair codegree do not prove the next LPE step

For ordered \(p\ne q\), the repository defines

\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|
\]

and

\[
T_{p,q}^{[1]}
=\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}(K(m)-2).
\]

Then

\[
F_2=\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q},
\qquad
F_3=\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}^{[1]}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_3}{F_2}
=\frac{\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}^{[1]}}
{\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}}}
\tag{6.2}
\]

is exactly the pair-Palm extension mean.

The proved second moment gives

\[
F_2\le4M\lambda_X^2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Together with \(F_1=M\mu\sim M\lambda\), it already implies

\[
H_1=\frac{F_2}{F_1}\ll\lambda_X.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the first Palm extension, after one hit, is controlled at the correct scale up to a constant. The first genuinely new step is

\[
H_2=\frac{F_3}{F_2}.
\]

### 6.1 The exact pair-Palm obstruction

With

\[
\lambda_{p,q}
=\sum_{\ell\notin\{p,q\}}\frac{|Z_\ell|}{\ell},
\]

define

\[
\mathscr P_3(X)
=\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(T_{p,q}^{[1]}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}\right)_+.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The sharp theorem in `hm3_result.tex` gives

\[
\mathscr P_3(X)
\le F_3(X)
\le \mathscr P_3(X)+O(M\lambda_X^3).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus the missing information is exactly the positive conditional overload of third-prime hits on actual two-prime CRT loci.

To prove the Poisson-scale extension

\[
F_3\ll\lambda_XF_2,
\tag{6.7}
\]

one needs, for example,

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_3(X)\ll\lambda_XF_2(X).}
\tag{6.8}
\]

This is a genuine third-order theorem. It is not an algebraic rearrangement of HM\(_2\).

### 6.2 Why the row-codegree theorem has the wrong orientation

The codegree theorem controls, for two **distinct rows** \(m\ne n\),

\[
|\mathcal P(m)\cap\mathcal P(n)|,
\qquad
\mathcal P(m)=\{p:f_p(m)=1\}.
\]

It exploits the nonzero difference \(n-m\) and gap-polynomial certificates.

But \(F_3\) measures three columns passing through one row. A high-degree isolated row produces no pair of distinct rows and therefore no nonzero difference to which the codegree argument can attach.

Pointwise one has only

\[
(K(m))_3=(K(m))_2(K(m)-2)
\le(L_X-2)(K(m))_2,
\]

where

\[
L_X=\#\{p\in\mathcal P_X:Z_p\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

Consequently

\[
F_3\le(L_X-2)F_2,
\tag{6.9}
\]

and the current inputs give no replacement of \(L_X\) by a constant under the pair-Palm measure.

### 6.3 Anchored-star no-go

The repository’s anchored-star family has all of the following:

* \(|Z_p|\le2\);
* reflection symmetry;
* no adjacent zeros;
* interval sparsity;
* the correct HM\(_2\) scale;
* uniformly bounded row codegrees between distinct rows;
* the established one-prime fiber and energy bounds.

Nevertheless one row is hit by

\[
L\asymp X/\log X
\]

primes. Therefore

\[
\frac{F_3}{F_2}\asymp L,
\]

which diverges. This is a rigorous impossibility theorem for deriving LPE from the present primewise and pairwise package.

## 7. What exact random-variable model corresponds to decreasing LPE ratios?

### 7.1 Constant \(R_r\): geometric tail

As shown above, \(R_r\equiv A\) corresponds to a geometric law of mean \(A\). This is the extremal distribution behind the MGF bound.

### 7.2 \(R_r=\mu/(r+1)\): Poisson

This is the observed leading model.

### 7.3 Binomial law

For \(K\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(L,\theta)\),

\[
\mathbb E(K)_r=(L)_r\theta^r,
\]

so

\[
R_r=\frac{(L-r)\theta}{r+1},
\qquad
H_r=(L-r)\theta.
\]

Both ratios decrease because each Palm conditioning consumes one of finitely many available Bernoulli slots.

### 7.4 Poisson-binomial and strongly Rayleigh laws

For independent nonidentical Bernoulli hits, the polynomial

\[
\prod_p(1+\rho_ps)
\]

has only negative real roots. Newton’s inequalities force log-concavity of its coefficients and hence decreasing \(R_r\).

More generally, let the uniform row distribution induce the random hit set

\[
H(m)=\{p\in\mathcal P_X:f_p(m)=1\}
\]

and define the multivariate generating polynomial

\[
G_X((z_p)_p)
=\frac1M\sum_{m<M}\prod_{p\in H(m)}z_p.
\tag{7.1}
\]

If \(G_X\) were real stable, the row measure would be **strongly Rayleigh**. Its rank generating polynomial

\[
P_X(z)=G_X(z,z,\ldots,z)
=\frac1M\sum_{m<M}z^{K(m)}
\tag{7.2}
\]

would have only real nonpositive zeros. Newton’s inequalities would then imply BMLC and all the observed ratio monotonicity, together with much stronger negative-dependence conclusions.

This is a mathematically clean possible mechanism. It is also far beyond what is currently known. The Apéry hit-set family is not known to be determinantal, matroidal, stable, Lorentzian, or even pairwise negatively associated prime by prime.

The immediate numerical test is:

1. form the exact rank polynomial \(P_X(z)=M^{-1}\sum_mz^{K(m)}\);
2. compute all its zeros;
3. test whether they are real and nonpositive;
4. independently test the multivariate Rayleigh inequalities at positive specializations for small shells.

If the univariate polynomial already has nonreal roots, the real-rootedness route is closed. If it remains real-rooted over substantial ranges, that is a much stronger discovery than the first three ratio inequalities.

### 7.5 Factorial sub-Poisson is the sharper conjecture

A natural strong condition is

\[
H_r=\frac{F_{r+1}}{F_r}\le\mu_X
\qquad(r\ge0).
\tag{7.3}
\]

It implies

\[
F_r\le M\mu_X^r.
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is the factorial-moment definition of a sub-Poisson tail. It is much stronger than decreasing \(R_r\), because decreasing \(R_r\) alone gives only

\[
F_r\le M r!\mu_X^r.
\]

The normalized data \(D_r=H_r/H_0\) are the direct test of (7.3), and they are below one in all displayed cases.

## 8. Why a naive large sieve still becomes circular

The first analytic target can be written as a pair-anchored discrepancy. Let

\[
\mathscr A_2
=\{(m,p,q):p\ne q,\ m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q\}.
\]

Then

\[
F_3-
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}
=
\sum_{(m,p,q)\in\mathscr A_2}
\sum_{\ell\notin\{p,q\}}
\bigl(f_\ell(m)-\rho_\ell\bigr).
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is a concrete moving-modulus dispersion sum.

For one prime \(\ell\), Fourier expansion gives schematically

\[
f_\ell(m)-\frac{|Z_\ell|}{\ell}
=\frac1\ell
\sum_{h\ne0}
\widehat{\mathbf1_{Z_\ell}}(h)e_\ell(hm),
\tag{8.2}
\]

up to replacing the complete-period mean by the exact finite marginal.

A classical large sieve can average the exponentials in (8.2), but after Cauchy–Schwarz it introduces the square energy of the pair-anchor weight

\[
w_2(m)=(K(m))_2.
\]

That energy contains fourth and higher factorial moments. A star row has enormous \(w_2(m)^2\), and the large-sieve inequality faithfully pays for it. Thus the argument becomes circular: to prove the third-moment contraction, it asks for control of an even higher moment or of the same high-degree atom one is trying to exclude.

This is the same obstruction diagnosed by the exact double-Cauchy–Schwarz audit in `pairpalm_result.tex`.

A successful analytic theorem must exploit additional arithmetic cancellation of the Apéry targets; generic large-sieve energy is not enough.

## 9. A specific proof program for LPE

The following program is concrete and ordered by increasing difficulty.

### Stage 1: separate Poisson-binomial depletion from Apéry depletion

For every computed shell, record:

\[
\rho_p=|\Omega_p|/M,
\qquad
\mu=\sum_p\rho_p,
\]

and compute the exact independent baselines

\[
R_r^{\rm PB}=e_{r+1}(\rho)/e_r(\rho),
\qquad
H_r^{\rm PB}=(r+1)R_r^{\rm PB}.
\]

Then report

\[
\Gamma_r=H_r/H_r^{\rm PB}.
\]

Also compute:

* factorial cumulants through the highest supported order;
* the roots of the rank polynomial \(P_X(z)\);
* BMLC inequalities for every available \(r\), not only \(r=0,1,2\).

This determines whether the new phenomenon is merely Poisson-binomial or genuinely arithmetic.

### Stage 2: target the first missing inequality, not all-r LPE

The sharp first theorem is

\[
\tag{Palm-3}
F_3(X)\ll\lambda_XF_2(X).
\]

An even more explicit sufficient form is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(
T_{p,q}^{[1]}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}
\right)_+
\ll\lambda_XF_2(X).}
\tag{9.1}
\]

This is the exact pair-Palm third-extension statement. It is the first point at which current technology fails.

A signed version,

\[
F_3-\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}
\ll\lambda_XF_2,
\tag{9.2}
\]

would already suffice and is weaker than (9.1). It allows cancellation between overloaded and underloaded anchor pairs.

### Stage 3: formulate the pair-CRT moving-target large sieve

For each ordered pair \((p,q)\) and zero residues

\[
a\in Z_p,
\qquad b\in Z_q,
\]

let \(m_{p,q}(a,b)\) denote the unique CRT representative, when it lies below \(M\). The desired theorem is an aggregate estimate of the shape

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\\p,q\in\mathcal P_X}}
\sum_{\substack{a\in Z_p,\ b\in Z_q\\m_{p,q}(a,b)<M}}
\sum_{\ell\notin\{p,q\}}
\left(
\mathbf1_{m_{p,q}(a,b)\bmod\ell\in Z_\ell}
-\frac{|Z_\ell|}{\ell}
\right)
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The left side is exactly the signed pair-Palm discrepancy, up to harmless finite-marginal corrections. Equation (9.3) is a precise “Apéry Palm Bombieri–Vinogradov” theorem.

This is the best analytic target suggested by the LPE data.

### Stage 4: seek one of three genuinely new inputs

#### 4A. Cross-characteristic Kummer–Mellin dispersion

The fixed toric Laurent polynomial gives

\[
b_j\equiv-\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbb F_p^\times)^3}
\Lambda(x,y,z)^j\pmod p.
\]

This realizes the marked scalar with uniform geometric complexity before reduction. To prove (9.3), one would need a large-sieve theorem for the exact divisibility event of these marked Mellin supertraces as the residue characteristic varies and the exponent is the pair-CRT moving target.

No existing Deligne/Katz theorem gives this. The event is not a bounded-conductor complex trace condition; it is the reduction modulo the same varying prime of a cyclotomic Frobenius supertrace. Nevertheless, this is the most plausible source of arithmetic structure not present in the anchored-star model.

#### 4B. Stable/strongly-Rayleigh incidence polynomial

Prove that the multivariate polynomial \(G_X\) in (7.1), or at least its rank specialization \(P_X\), has a stability or real-rootedness property. One theorem would then give all ratio monotonicity at once.

This route is concrete and falsifiable by the root computations proposed in Stage 1. At present there is no structural reason to expect it, so it should be tested before serious proof investment.

#### 4C. A compressed global certificate for extensions

If an occupied anchor tuple forced every extension prime \(\ell\) to divide one nonzero integer

\[
\Delta(m,p_1,\ldots,p_r)
\]

with

\[
\log|\Delta|
\ll r\log^{O(1)}X,
\]

then the number of extensions would be \(O(r\log^{O(1)}X/\log X)\). This would give an LPE-type bound.

The obvious certificate \(b_m\) has logarithmic height \(\Theta(X^2)\) and is useless. The resultant and gap-polynomial carriers currently in the repository also retain too much height or lose a free gap parameter. A successful certificate would have to be genuinely new and much smaller than \(b_m\).

### Stage 5: only then iterate the Palm hierarchy

If Palm-3 is established with a mechanism stable under Palm weighting, attempt

\[
F_{r+1}\ll\lambda_XF_r
\qquad(2\le r\le7).
\tag{9.4}
\]

The finite chain through \(r=7\) implies HM\(_8\) immediately:

\[
F_8\ll X^2\lambda_X^8.
\]

An all-r version gives the stronger MGF theorem.

## 10. Is LPE more accessible than HM\(_8\)?

There is no simple logical ordering.

### Constant-A all-r LPE

The statement

\[
F_{r+1}\le A(r+1)F_r
\qquad\text{for every }r
\]

allows, at the eighth moment,

\[
F_8\le M\,8!\,A^8.
\]

If \(\lambda_X\to0\), this is much weaker than HM\(_8\), which asks for

\[
F_8\ll M\lambda_X^8.
\]

But all-r LPE controls the complete tail and gives an MGF, whereas HM\(_8\) controls only one finite moment. The two statements are not comparable.

### Poisson-scale truncated LPE

The finite collection

\[
F_{r+1}\ll\lambda_XF_r
\qquad(0\le r\le7)
\]

is stronger and more structured than HM\(_8\), and it implies HM\(_8\) by iteration.

### Current accessibility

LPE is **not presently more accessible from the established machinery**, for three exact reasons:

1. its base ratio \(R_0\) requires the unproved average bound \(\lambda_X=O(1)\) if the constant \(A\) is to be uniform;
2. its first new conditional step \(F_3/F_2\) is exactly the open pair-Palm third-extension problem;
3. all-r LPE requires an infinite hierarchy unless one finds a single structural certificate such as real stability.

Its advantage is conceptual and experimental: it converts the large eighth-order target into a one-step conditional-contraction question and suggests two sharp signatures—Poisson-binomial normalization and binomial-moment log-concavity. That makes it an excellent research interface, but not yet a proof shortcut.

The most reasonable verdict is:

> **LPE is more actionable as a diagnostic and as a search for a single negative-dependence structure. The narrowest provable next theorem remains Palm-3. Until Palm-3 is solved, the data do not make LPE technically easier than HM\(_8\).**

## 11. Direct answers to questions (a)–(f)

### (a) What arithmetic mechanism could make the ratios decrease?

The decline of \(R_r\) needs no special arithmetic mechanism: it is exactly what a Poisson law predicts because of the factor \(r+1\) in the definition. The true Palm extension means \(H_r=(r+1)R_r\) are nearly constant and approach the Poisson value as \(X\) grows. Any residual decline of \(H_r\) is consistent with finite Poisson-binomial depletion or genuine negative dependence. Height depletion is ruled out quantitatively.

### (b) Can generic positioning after an occupied tuple be formalized?

Yes. Equations (5.1)–(5.7) give the exact formulation. After an occupied \(r\)-tuple, the independent prediction is

\[
T_{\mathbf p}\approx J_{\mathbf p}\mu_{\mathbf p}.
\]

The positive extension excess \(\mathscr E_r^+\) measures the failure. CRT injectivity does not bound it; proving such a bound is the missing theorem.

### (c) Can current pair-Palm or codegree methods prove \(F_3\le3AF_2\)?

No. They control \(F_2\), not the pair-Palm extension numerator \(F_3\). The exact obstruction is \(\mathscr P_3(X)\) in (6.5). The row-codegree theorem compares two distinct rows and is blind to one isolated high-degree row. The anchored-star construction is a formal counterexample to any deduction from the current package.

### (d) What model has decreasing LPE ratios?

Constant ratios correspond to a geometric law. Poisson gives \(R_r=\mu/(r+1)\). Binomial and Poisson-binomial laws give decreasing \(R_r\) and decreasing true Palm means. Strongly Rayleigh measures provide a broad structural class with real-rooted rank polynomials and ultra-log-concavity. The exact algebraic condition suggested by the data is BMLC:

\[
(r+2)F_{r+1}^2\ge(r+1)F_rF_{r+2}.
\]

Strict decrease alone does not imply a super-exponential improvement without a quantified rate.

### (e) What mechanism replaces the failed height argument?

Only cross-characteristic anti-alignment can do so: the CRT-selected row must fail to land unusually often in the moving zero sets \(Z_q\). This is a distribution theorem for Apéry zero incidences, not a radical budget theorem. No current theorem supplies it.

### (f) What is the specific proof strategy, and is it easier than HM\(_8\)?

First compute the exact Poisson-binomial baseline and the normalized ratios \(\Gamma_r\). Then attack Palm-3, preferably through the explicit pair-CRT discrepancy (9.3). The plausible new inputs are a cross-characteristic Kummer–Mellin large sieve, a stable incidence polynomial, or a compressed divisibility certificate. Current pair-codegree and classical large-sieve estimates do not close Palm-3.

LPE is not presently easier than HM\(_8\), but it gives the clearest sequential target. A successful mechanism for Palm-3 that survives Palm reweighting could turn LPE into the most efficient route.
