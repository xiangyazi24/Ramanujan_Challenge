\[

\[

\[
\mathbf 1_{b_r=0}=1-\operatorname{Tr}\Pi^{(0)}_{p,r}.

To obtain the last identity one would need a genuinely new theorem asserting, for example, that the transformed slope-zero piece has rank at most one and that its Frobenius trace is congruent to \(b_r\) with no other slope-zero contributions. # 4. Two distinct Cartier operators that should not be conflated

The project currently uses two related but different operations.

## 4.1 Coefficient Cartier

For a power series

\[
f(t)=\sum_na_nt^n,
\]

coefficient Cartier is

\[
\mathcal C_p(f)=\sum_na_{pn}t^n.
\]

After a moving monomial shift, it extracts a selected coefficient, as in (0.3) and (3.1).

## 4.2 Geometric Hasse-Witt Cartier

For a smooth K3 fiber, Cartier/inverse Cartier acts on the Hodge pieces of \(H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}\). In a chosen trivialization of the one-dimensional Hodge line, its scalar is the Hasse-Witt value \(H_p(t)\).

The toric residue formalism explains why coefficients of a \((p-1)\)-st power appear in the geometric Hasse-Witt matrix. But an entry of a Hasse-Witt matrix and the coefficient-extraction operator are not automatically one and the same endomorphism on a fixed vector space.

The Mellin construction connects them as follows:

\[
	ext{fiberwise Hasse scalar }H_p(t)
\quad\xrightarrow{	ext{Kummer Mellin}}\quad
	ext{coefficient }b_r,
\]

whereas the direct diagonal construction gives

\[
\mathscr F^{p-1}
\quad\xrightarrow{	ext{monomial shift + Cartier}}\quad
b_r.
\]

They compute the same scalar, but they live in different geometries. This is exactly why the coefficient index cannot simply be relabeled as a K3 fiber parameter.

# 5. Literature precedent

The proposed construction is not speculative; each of its ingredients is standard. What is not standard is the uniform Apéry-specific estimate one ultimately wants from it.

## 5.1 Dwork's \(p\)-adic hypergeometric analysis

Dwork constructed Frobenius structures on generalized hypergeometric differential equations and expressed Frobenius matrices through \(p\)-adic analytic hypergeometric functions. The basic precedent is:

- Bernard Dwork, *On \(p\)-adic differential equations IV: generalized hypergeometric functions as \(p\)-adic analytic functions in one variable*, Ann. Sci. ÉNS 6 (1973), 295-316, DOI 10.24033/asens.1249.

In Dwork's framework, the geometric argument of a hypergeometric function and the exponent parameters are genuinely different variables. The Apéry index \(r\) belongs on the exponent/Kummer side, not on the geometric-fiber side.

## 5.2 Twisted toric exponential sums

Adolphson and Sperber developed Dwork cohomology for toric exponential sums and then for multiplicatively twisted sums. This is the direct precedent for (2.1)-(2.3):

- Alan Adolphson and Steven Sperber, *Exponential sums and Newton polyhedra: cohomology and estimates*, Ann. of Math. 130 (1989), 367-406, DOI 10.2307/1971424.
- Alan Adolphson and Steven Sperber, *Exponential sums on \((G_m)^n\)*, Invent. Math. 101 (1990), 63-80.
- Alan Adolphson and Steven Sperber, *On twisted exponential sums*, Math. Ann. 290 (1991), 713-726.

Their theory supplies the Newton-polyhedron nondegeneracy test, middle-degree concentration, rank/degree formulas, and \(p\)-adic Frobenius estimates for exactly the kind of Kummer-twisted sum in (2.1).

## 5.3 Rigid-cohomological trace formula

The cohomological interpretation of \(L\)-functions of overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystals is standard:

- Jean-Yves Étesse and Bernard Le Stum, *Fonctions \(L\) associées aux \(F\)-isocristaux surconvergents I: interprétation cohomologique*, Math. Ann. 296 (1993), 557-576.

This supplies the formal justification for (1.6).

## 5.4 Katz, multiplicative convolution, and Kummer sheaves

On the \(\ell\)-adic side, Katz's hypergeometric sheaves are built by multiplicative convolution of Kummer and Artin-Schreier sheaves. Their Mellin transforms are controlled by Gauss sums. This is the sheaf-theoretic prototype of turning a coefficient/exponent parameter into a character-isotypic cohomology group.

The relevant standard reference is Nicholas Katz, *Exponential Sums and Differential Equations*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 124, Princeton University Press, 1990.

## 5.5 Modern \(p\)-adic hypergeometric/GKZ complexes

Modern work makes the same construction directly in \(p\)-adic cohomology:

- Kazuaki Miyatani, *\(p\)-adic generalized hypergeometric equations from the viewpoint of arithmetic \(\mathscr D\)-modules*, arXiv:1607.04852. The construction uses multiplicative convolution and produces overconvergent hypergeometric \(F\)-isocrystals.
- Lei Fu, Peigen Li, Daqing Wan, and Hao Zhang, *The \(p\)-adic Gelfand-Kapranov-Zelevinsky hypergeometric complex*, arXiv:1804.05297. Their object is a twisted relative de Rham complex with Frobenius; its Teichmüller-fiber traces are finite-field hypergeometric functions.
- Kiran Kedlaya, *Frobenius structures on hypergeometric equations*, arXiv:1912.13073. This gives explicit Dwork/GKZ Frobenius formulas and \(p\)-adic gamma factors.
- Frits Beukers and Masha Vlasenko, *Dwork Crystals I*, IMRN 2021, 8807-8844, DOI 10.1093/imrn/rnaa119. This develops explicit Hasse-Witt matrices and \(p\)-adic limit formulas for toric hypersurfaces.

These are genuine precedents for the coefficient-versus-fiber issue. None turns the vanishing of a Mellin trace into the absence of a unit-root constituent without an additional special theorem.

## 5.6 The remaining cross-prime wall

For one fixed rational exponent \(\lambda\), hypergeometric \(F\)-crystals can often be organized uniformly over primes satisfying denominator conditions. Here

\[
\lambda_{p,r}=-rac{r}{p-1}
\]

moves with both \(p\) and \(r\). The associated tame character has order

\[
rac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,r)},
\]

which may be as large as \(p-1\). Thus the construction is finite and controlled at each prime, but it is not automatically a single fixed compatible system across all defining characteristics.

This is the precise version of the “same-characteristic wall” already encountered elsewhere in the project.

## Gate E: decisive projector test

At every \(r\) with \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\), test whether the slope-zero part in (6.2) vanishes.

Independently compute, on the ordinary K3 base,

# 7. What theorem would actually move the cross-prime problem

The construction above supplies an exact coefficient-indexed Frobenius object. To become useful for the Ramanujan Challenge, one needs a theorem stronger than its existence.

A realistic first target is:

> **Uniform Mellin-cohomology theorem.** Outside a fixed explicitly classified set of resonant characters, the complexes \(\mathcal C_{p,r}\) are concentrated in a fixed degree, have uniformly bounded rank and tame conductor, and their connected tensor square has no rank-one Kummer constituent after the fixed boundary/Tate pieces are removed.

Even this theorem is still single-prime. The cross-prime step would then require a defining-characteristic dispersion estimate for the traces

\[
J_{p,r}\equiv b_r\pmod p
\]

as both \(p\) and \(r\) move. Standard Deligne/Katz theory with an auxiliary coefficient prime does not automatically specialize to this diagonal defining-characteristic situation.

The advantage over Q7799 is that the object now type-checks:

\[
oxed{
	ext{fiber parameter }t
\quad	ext{is replaced by}\quad
	ext{Kummer character }\omega^{-r}
	ext{ through a genuine cohomological transform}.}
\]

The disadvantage is equally clear: the result is no longer rank three, and \(p\mid b_r\) remains a trace-zero condition.

# Direct answers

## (1) Is there a known cohomological construction realizing \(b_rmod p\)?

**Yes, as a Frobenius supertrace on Kummer-twisted rigid/Dwork cohomology.** The exact object is (V1), and the proof is (1.3)-(1.6). Under an additional middle-degree concentration theorem, it becomes a trace on a single primitive \(H^3\) group of the Straub threefold with Kummer coefficients.

It is not the de Rham cohomology of a K3 fiber “at \(r\).” The index \(r\) enters through a Kummer logarithmic exponent or a character-isotypic projector.

## (2) Can one build \(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{p,r}\) by Cartier followed by Mellin projection?

One can build:

1. the exact coefficient Cartier extractor (3.1);
2. the Kummer idempotent \(e_r^{\mathrm{Kum}}\) in (1.10);
3. the derived Mellin Frobenius complex (3.2);
4. a \(p\)-adic Mellin lift \(\widetilde b_{p,r}\) from the unit-root Frobenius eigenvalues, as in (3.5).

One **cannot formally build a zero-detecting unit-root projector**. Mellin-transforming the bare projector loses the Hasse eigenvalue, while Mellin-transforming \(\Phi\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}\) gives a trace that can cancel. Any claimed indicator identity requires a new rank-one/no-cancellation theorem.

## (3) Is there precedent in Dwork's hypergeometric analysis?

**Yes.** Kummer exponent parameters, twisted de Rham complexes, Dwork Frobenius, multiplicative convolution, Gauss sums, and GKZ hypergeometric complexes are exactly the standard machinery for this coefficient-versus-fiber distinction. The references in Section 5 are the relevant chain.

The missing Apéry theorem is not the existence of the transform. It is uniform structural and analytic control of the transformed Frobenius traces as the defining prime and the character index vary together.
