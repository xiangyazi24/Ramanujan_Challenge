# 1. Why a genuinely quenched statement is necessary

Put

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
\qquad
I_X=(X^2,2X^2]\cap\mathbf Z,
\]

\[
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\},
\qquad z_p=|Z_p|,
\]

and

\[
K_X(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}1_{Z_p}(m\bmod p),
\qquad
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{z_p}{p}.
\]

The proved pointwise bound \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\) gives

\[
\lambda_X\ll \frac{X^{2/3}}{\log X}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The desired statement is therefore the atom bound

\[
\max_{m\in I_X}K_X(m)\le \lambda_X X^{o(1)}+X^{o(1)},
\tag{1.2}
\]

which implies the target in the question.

The current `atom_tail_section.tex` already records that global large-sieve dispersion, Linnik dispersion with completed CRT sums, variance layer-cake, and codegree-weighted pair dispersion either require the atom bound at their diagonal or collapse back to the proved second moment.  The anchored reflection-star model explains why: one may have excellent prime-by-prime spacing, reflection, no consecutive zeros, and bounded codegrees, yet deliberately place one permitted residue at the same integer \(m_0\) for \(\asymp X/\log X\) primes.

Therefore any proposed “first lemma” that is only a theorem about one \(Z_p\) at a time is logically incapable of closing (1.2).  This includes:

- a better bound for \(z_p\) alone;
- square-root Fourier decay for each fixed \(Z_p\) alone;
- simplicity of almost every Hasse zero alone;
- rarity of \(p\mid\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)\) alone.

The last point is easy to misuse.  The observed rarity of transverse common zeros says that \(\Xi_r\) is usually a **nonzero color/separator on the target divisor**.  It does not say that ordinary targets \(p\mid b_r\) are rare.  A successful use of \(\Xi\) would have to exploit its full values in a cross-prime mixing theorem, not replace \(Z_p\) by the much smaller common-zero set.

# 2. The one concrete lemma to test

For distinct \(p,q\in\mathcal P_X\), define the exact pair-CRT fibre

\[
A_{p,q}
=\{a\in I_X:a\bmod p\in Z_p,\ a\bmod q\in Z_q\},
\qquad J_{p,q}=|A_{p,q}|.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Because \(pq>X^2=|I_X|\), each residue pair \((r,u)\in Z_p\times Z_q\) has at most one representative in \(I_X\).  Hence

\[
J_{p,q}\le z_pz_q\ll X^{4/3}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For \(a\in A_{p,q}\), let

\[
L_{p,q}(a)
=\#\{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}:
       a\bmod\ell\in Z_\ell\},
\]

and put

\[
\lambda_{p,q}
=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}
  \frac{z_\ell}{\ell}.
\]

The proposed first lemma is the following.

## Quenched pair-fibre Poisson moments (QPF)

There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that, uniformly for distinct
\(p,q\in\mathcal P_X\) and

\[
2\le k\le k_X:=\left\lfloor
c\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}
\right\rfloor,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{a\in A_{p,q}}
   (L_{p,q}(a))_k
\ \ll\ X^{o(1)}
\left(J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}^{\,k}+k!\right).
}
\tag{QPF}
\]

Here \((y)_k=y(y-1)\cdots(y-k+1)\).  The \(k!\) term is only an integral sparse-fibre correction; it may be replaced by any \(X^{o(1)}k!\)-scale term.

This is the first lemma I would test on the actual \(Z_p\).

## Why this is not a disguised restatement

`(QPF)` is stronger than a fixed global factorial moment but has a specific geometric shape:

- the base set \(A_{p,q}\) is explicitly parametrized by two zero sets and one truncated CRT inverse;
- the estimate is **quenched**—uniform in the chosen pair, so one bad pair cannot hide in a global average;
- only logarithmically many moment levels are requested;
- the anchored two-point reflection star violates it violently: for an active pair, \(J_{p,q}\le4\), while the common anchor has \(L_{p,q}(m_0)\asymp X/\log X\).

It is thus a falsifiable structural claim about the actual Apéry fibres, not a consequence of the existing vertical axioms.

# 3. `(QPF)` gives the required moving-prime bound

Let \(m\in I_X\) and write \(K=K_X(m)\).  If \(K<2k_X+4\), then
\(K=X^{o(1)}\), which is harmless.  Otherwise choose any two hit primes
\(p,q\).  Then

\[
m\in A_{p,q},
\qquad
L_{p,q}(m)=K-2.
\]

For \(K\ge2k+4\), every factor in \((K-2)_k\) is at least \(K/2\), so `(QPF)` yields

\[
\left(\frac K2\right)^k
\le X^{o(1)}
   \left(J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}^{\,k}+k!\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Take \(k=k_X\).  From (2.2),

\[
J_{p,q}^{1/k}
\le
\exp\!\left(
\frac{(4/3+o(1))\log X}{c\log X/\log\log X}
\right)
=(\log X)^{4/(3c)+o(1)}=X^{o(1)}.
\]

Also \((k!)^{1/k}\ll k=X^{o(1)}\).  Taking \(k\)-th roots in (3.1) therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
K_X(m)\ll \lambda_X X^{o(1)}+X^{o(1)}
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

uniformly in \(m\in I_X\).  Combining (3.2) with (1.1),

\[
\boxed{
\max_{X^2<m\le2X^2}K_X(m)
\ll \frac{X^{2/3+o(1)}}{\log X}.
}
\]

So `(QPF)` is not merely diagnostic: its logarithmic-moment form proves the surviving sharp target.

# 4. Why the three named mechanisms rank as stated

## 4.1 Affine sieve: presently the wrong state space

Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak's affine sieve starts with a fixed orbit of a finitely generated subgroup of \(GL_n(\mathbf Z)\), a fixed integral polynomial, and expansion of congruence graphs.  The Apéry transfer relation after factorial clearing is

\[
\binom{B_{r+1}}{B_r}
=
\begin{pmatrix}P(r)&-r^6\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\binom{B_r}{B_{r-1}},
\qquad B_r=(r!)^3b_r.
\]

The matrix changes with \(r\); the word is one deterministic ordered cocycle rather than a random word in a fixed generating set; and the event is

\[
p\mid b_{m\bmod p},
\]

not divisibility of a fixed polynomial evaluated on an averaged orbit.  Normalizing the determinant or adjoining the index does not fix the quantifier mismatch.  Affine sieve should be reconsidered only after an actual finite-generator orbit model for the distinguished Apéry zero fibre has been proved.

Primary comparison: Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak, *Affine linear sieve, expanders, and sum-product*, Invent. Math. 179 (2010), DOI `10.1007/s00222-009-0225-3`.

## 4.2 \(q\)-van der Corput: the right phase, not yet the right amplitude theorem

For \(r\in Z_p\), \(u\in Z_q\), the least pair-CRT representative is

\[
a_0(r,u;p,q)
=r+p\left(((u-r)p^{-1})\bmod q\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The representative in \(I_X\), when it exists, differs from \(a_0\) by at most one multiple of \(pq\).  Fourier expansion of the further test
\(1_{Z_\ell}(a\bmod\ell)\) therefore produces exactly the kind of modular-inverse, sharply truncated phase for which \(q\)-van der Corput and arithmetic exponent-pair methods are designed.

But the coefficients are the actual zero-set Fourier transforms

\[
\widehat{1_{Z_p}}(h)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(-hr),
\]

not bounded smooth weights.  Generic Cauchy/large-sieve treatment loses the diagonal and returns to the already exhausted second-moment barrier.  Hence \(q\)-van der Corput is ranked second: it is the likely engine for `(QPF)`, not a complete mechanism by itself.

Primary comparison: Wu–Xi, *Arithmetic exponent pairs for algebraic trace functions and applications*, arXiv:`1603.07060`.

## 4.3 Dwork/trace functions: potentially the missing amplitude input

The Apéry family is geometric, has exact Cartier/Dwork congruences, and admits level-6/K3 descriptions.  This makes a trace-function input much less fanciful than for a generic recurrence.  If one could realize a canonically normalized function on the target fibre—possibly using the first transverse jet \(\kappa_r\), \(\Xi_r\), or the boundary derivative discussed in Q7762—as a bounded-complexity sheaf trace uniformly in \(p\), then the inverse-phase sums from §4.2 would become accessible to Deligne/Katz technology.

The missing bridge is substantial:

- exact zero detection uses a Fermat-type operation whose degree grows with \(p\);
- \(1_{b_r=0}\) is not currently a bounded-conductor trace function;
- the same prime is both the defining characteristic and the modulus of the coefficient event;
- fixed-\(p\) equidistribution over characters does not control one deterministic moving index in each varying characteristic.

Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel prove strong prime sums for bona fide bounded-complexity trace functions; Bah–Shuddhodan's 2026 varying-characteristic theorem allows bounded complexity and common monodromy.  Neither presently applies to the raw Apéry zero indicator.  This is why Dwork ranks below the CRT mechanism but remains its best prospective arithmetic input.

Primary comparisons: Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel, *Algebraic trace functions over the primes*, arXiv:`1211.6043`; Bah–Shuddhodan, *Uniform stratified vanishing and equidistribution on \(\mathbb G_m^d\)*, arXiv:`2608.00304`.

# 5. Exact Sage experiment for `(QPF)`

The following code reconstructs the actual \(Z_p\) from the Apéry recurrence, verifies reflection and no consecutive zeros, builds every pair-CRT fibre in \(I_X\), and reports the worst normalized `(QPF)` ratio for the tested moment range.

```python
from sage.all import *


def P(n):
    n = ZZ(n)
    return 34*n^3 + 51*n^2 + 27*n + 5


def apery_zero_set(p):
    """Return Z_p={0<=r<p : b_r=0 mod p}."""
    p = ZZ(p)
    vals = [ZZ(1) % p, ZZ(5) % p]
    # n=1,...,p-2 computes b_2,...,b_{p-1}; all pivots are units.
    for n in range(1, p-1):
        den = power_mod(n + 1, 3, p)
        nxt = (
            (P(n) % p) * vals[n]
            - (ZZ(n)^3 % p) * vals[n-1]
        ) * inverse_mod(den, p) % p
        vals.append(ZZ(nxt))

    assert len(vals) == p
    Z = {ZZ(r) for r in range(p) if vals[r] == 0}

    # Locked structural regressions.
    assert vals[0] == 1 and vals[p-1] == 1
    assert all((p - 1 - r) in Z for r in Z)
    assert all(not (r in Z and r + 1 in Z) for r in range(p-1))
    return Z


def shell_crt_representative(r, u, p, q, lo, hi):
    """Unique a in (lo,hi] with a=r mod p, a=u mod q, if it exists."""
    r, u, p, q = map(ZZ, (r, u, p, q))
    modulus = p*q
    t = ((u-r) * inverse_mod(p, q)) % q
    a0 = r + p*t                    # 0 <= a0 < pq
    shift = (ZZ(lo)-a0)//modulus + 1
    a = a0 + shift*modulus
    return a if lo < a <= hi else None


def falling(y, k):
    y, k = ZZ(y), ZZ(k)
    if y < k:
        return ZZ(0)
    out = ZZ(1)
    for j in range(k):
        out *= y-j
    return out


def audit_qpf(X, kmax=8):
    X = ZZ(X)
    lo, hi = X^2, 2*X^2
    primes = [ZZ(p) for p in prime_range(X+1, 2*X+1)]
    Z = {p: apery_zero_set(p) for p in primes}
    lam = sum(QQ(len(Z[p]), p) for p in primes)

    pair_fibres = {}
    candidates = set()
    for i, p in enumerate(primes):
        for q in primes[i+1:]:
            A = []
            for r in Z[p]:
                for u in Z[q]:
                    a = shell_crt_representative(r, u, p, q, lo, hi)
                    if a is not None:
                        A.append(a)
                        candidates.add(a)
            assert len(A) == len(set(A))
            pair_fibres[(p, q)] = A

    # Every point with K_X(a)>=2 occurs in at least one pair fibre.
    def K(a):
        return sum(1 for ell in primes if a % ell in Z[ell])

    max_K = max([K(a) for a in candidates] + [0])
    RR = RealField(100)
    worst = None

    for (p, q), A in pair_fibres.items():
        if not A:
            continue
        lam_pq = lam - QQ(len(Z[p]), p) - QQ(len(Z[q]), q)
        Lvals = [
            sum(
                1 for ell in primes
                if ell != p and ell != q and a % ell in Z[ell]
            )
            for a in A
        ]
        J = ZZ(len(A))

        for k in range(2, kmax+1):
            lhs = sum(falling(L, k) for L in Lvals)
            rhs = QQ(J)*lam_pq^k + factorial(k)
            ratio = RR(lhs/rhs)
            rec = (ratio, p, q, k, J, max(Lvals), lhs, rhs)
            if worst is None or ratio > worst[0]:
                worst = rec

    if worst is None:
        worst = (RR(0), None, None, None, 0, 0, 0, 1)

    exponent = (
        log(worst[0])/log(RR(X))
        if worst[0] > 1 else RR(0)
    )
    print("X=", X)
    print("prime_count=", len(primes), "lambda=", RR(lam))
    print("actual_max_K_on_pair_candidates=", max_K)
    print("QPF_worst_ratio_record=", worst)
    print("log(worst_ratio)/log(X)=", exponent)
    return worst


for X in [64, 128, 256]:
    audit_qpf(X, kmax=8)
```

The decisive diagnostic is

\[
R_X=\max_{p<q}\max_{2\le k\le k_{\rm test}}
\frac{\sum_{a\in A_{p,q}}(L_{p,q}(a))_k}
     {J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}^k+k!}.
\]

The first empirical question is not whether \(R_X<1\), but whether

\[
\frac{\log R_X}{\log X}\longrightarrow0
\]

and whether the worst pair/fibre is structurally stable under increasing \(X\).  A persistent \(R_X\asymp X^\delta\) would be a clean terminal verdict against this program.  Polylogarithmic growth, together with Poisson-like behaviour up to increasing \(k\), would justify investing in the analytic proof.

# 6. What a proof attempt should do next

The proof architecture for `(QPF)` should be attacked in this order:

1. **Expand one quenched factorial moment exactly.**  Fix \(p,q\), expand the \(k\) further-prime indicators, and insert additive Fourier inversion only after conditioning on \(A_{p,q}\).  Do not average away the bad pair first.
2. **Expose the modular-inverse phase.**  Use (4.1) and the one-shell truncation to obtain a bilinear/incomplete Kloosterman-fraction sum in \(p,q\) and the further primes.
3. **Prove an inverse theorem for failure.**  A power-sized violation should force either a low-complexity additive phase common to many actual \(Z_\ell\), or concentration of a canonical transverse color.
4. **Use Apéry geometry only at the inverse-theorem endpoint.**  Dwork/Hecke/first-jet information should rule out the structured alternative.  Applying fixed-\(p\) geometry before the cross-prime inverse step merely returns to the vertical no-go.

This division of labor is important.  The analytic method should discover the only possible obstruction; the Apéry-specific arithmetic should then exclude it.  Asking Dwork theory directly for an \(L^\infty\) bound on \(K_X(m)\), or asking \(q\)-van der Corput to handle arbitrary zero-set amplitudes, is too large a first step.

# Final recommendation

\textbf{Top choice: quenched, logarithmic-moment expansion of the actual pair-CRT fibres.}

Treat \(q\)-van der Corput as the phase engine and Dwork/trace geometry as the amplitude/inverse-theorem input.  Do not start with a classical affine sieve.  The single gateway lemma to compute and then prove is `(QPF)` above.
