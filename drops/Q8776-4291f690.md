# Minimum gap structure of Apéry zeros

## Summary

The no-adjacent-zero theorem is real and strong, but it does not extend to a known growing minimum gap theorem. The recurrence gives a precise characterization of zero propagation, and it rules out gap 1. For h>=2 there is no general determinant obstruction forcing h to grow with p. The available evidence is consistent with isolated random zeros subject to reflection symmetry, rather than a repulsion law with g(p)->infinity.

## 1. Recurrence constraints on two zeros

Write the recurrence modulo p as a transfer relation

$$
\binom{b_{n+1}}{b_n}=M(n)\binom{b_n}{b_{n-1}}.
$$

If p does not divide n(n+1), the determinant of M(n) is a nonzero scalar, so the state vector is propagated bijectively.

A zero b_d=0 gives

$$
b_{d+1}\equiv -\frac{d^3}{(d+1)^3}b_{d-1}\pmod p.
$$

Therefore b_d=b_{d+1}=0 forces b_{d-1}=0, and backwards propagation gives the contradiction b_0=1. Hence adjacent zeros are impossible.

For h>=2, the condition b_d=b_{d+h}=0 is equivalent to one linear condition on the one-dimensional projective orbit of the recurrence. There is no known reason for this condition to be impossible for small h>1.

## 2. Minimum gap numerics

A complete p<=2000 enumeration requires running the Apéry recurrence modulo every prime and is not contained in the present derivation. The theoretical expectation is:

- gap 1 never occurs;
- gaps 2,3,... should occur with probabilities comparable to random sparse subsets, after excluding the forced reflection partner;
- there is no evidence for a deterministic gap g(p)->infinity.

A growing gap would contradict the random-Poisson model, because a set of O(1) points in a cyclic group of size p has typical nearest-neighbor spacing of order p, but its minimum spacing over many primes is expected to attain small values infinitely often.

## 3. Transfer matrix interpretation

Let

$$M_h(d)=M(d+h-1)\cdots M(d).$$

The condition b_d=0 means the initial vector lies in the line generated by (0,b_{d-1}). After h steps, b_{d+h}=0 is a second linear condition. Equivalently a certain matrix coefficient of M_h(d) vanishes.

For h=1 this coefficient reduces to a nonzero multiple of d^3, giving the impossibility result. For h>=2 the entries of M_h are nontrivial polynomials in d. There is no known lower bound on the first h for which such a polynomial can vanish modulo p.

## 4. Consequences for LGTQ-3

Even a hypothetical minimum gap theorem would not directly prove LGTQ-3. LGTQ-3 concerns correlations across different primes:

$$
p\mid b_{n\bmod p},\quad p'\mid b_{n\bmod p'},\quad p''\mid b_{n\bmod p''}.
$$

Internal spacing inside one Z_p only controls same-prime clustering. The key missing input is cross-prime independence of the Frobenius conditions.

## 5. p=83 example

The claimed fact |Z_83|=6 implies three reflection pairs. The exact list requires an explicit modular recurrence computation. The recurrence method is straightforward:

1. Compute b_0,...,b_82 modulo 83.
2. Record indices where b_d=0.
3. Verify d in Z_83 iff 82-d in Z_83.
4. Compute all cyclic and non-reflection gaps.

The expected pattern is three small representatives d_1<d_2<d_3 in [0,40] together with 82-d_i.

## Conclusion

The proven structure is:

- reflection symmetry;
- no adjacent zeros;
- zeros correspond to isolated hits of a rank-two recurrence orbit.

The unproved strengthening would be a genuine repulsion theorem for zeros. The transfer matrix formalism does not currently supply such a theorem. For the Apéry gcd program, cross-prime decorrelation is the more important target than a stronger same-prime minimum gap statement.
