# Logarithmic-order moments: the exact Apéry-only input

## Verdict

There is not yet a proved mechanism in the repository that yields (6.1).  The known Apéry properties—reflection, the bound `Z(p)=O(p^(2/3))`, gap-polynomial degree bounds, no consecutive zeros, bounded row codegrees, the K3/Sym² description at one characteristic, and the ordinary large sieve—do not imply it.  The anchored-star construction was designed precisely to survive all of those inputs.

The correct additional property is a **horizontal tensor-primitivity statement for the actual Apéry zero indicators**:

> after centering, connected correlations of the functions
> `1_{p | b_{(y-1)/2}}`, with distinct defining characteristics `p`, remain at Poisson/Bernstein scale through tensor order `O(log X)`; equivalently, the additive Fourier transforms of the actual `Omega_p` have no persistent common rank-one translation phase, and no nontrivial connected tensor constituent, across those characteristics.

The star fails this property in the strongest possible way: all of its Fourier transforms contain the same phase `e_p(h y_0)`, so every active prime is synchronized at one integer `y_0`.

A single precise lemma capturing the required Apéry arithmetic is stated below.  It implies (6.1) by a standard cumulant-to-moment argument.  It also has an exact Fourier expansion and an exact Selberg/CRT formulation, so it is a checkable target rather than a slogan.

The important honesty point is that proving this lemma requires a genuinely new **selected-character, defining-prime horizontal tensor large sieve**.  Existing all-character Mellin equidistribution and compatible-system large sieves do not supply it: they either average over all characters or reduce at auxiliary primes `ell != p`, whereas here one selected character is tested modulo the same prime that is the base characteristic.

## 1. Normalization on the actual odd interval

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
\qquad
\mathcal Y_X=\{1,3,5,\ldots,4X^2-1\}.
\]

Thus

\[
N_X:=|\mathcal Y_X|=2X^2.
\]

For `p in P_X`, put

\[
A_p(y)=\mathbf 1_{\Omega_p}(y\bmod p),
\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{|\Omega_p|}{p}=\frac{Z(p)}p,
\]

and

\[
K_X(y)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}A_p(y),
\qquad
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\rho_p.
\]

Let `Y_X` be uniformly distributed on `mathcal Y_X`, and define the exact finite-interval mean and centered variable

\[
\mu_X=\mathbb E K_X(Y_X),
\qquad
S_X=K_X(Y_X)-\mu_X.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Because multiplication by two is invertible modulo every odd `p`, each residue class occurs among the `N_X` odd integers either `floor(N_X/p)` or `ceil(N_X/p)` times.  Hence

\[
\left|\mathbb E A_p(Y_X)-\rho_p\right|
\le\frac{Z(p)}{N_X}.
\]

Summing and using `p <= 2X` gives

\[
\boxed{
|\mu_X-\lambda_X|
\le\frac1{N_X}\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}Z(p)
\le\frac{\lambda_X}{X}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The known pointwise zero-set estimate gives, after enlarging the harmless `X^{o(1)}` envelope if necessary,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_X\le B_X,
\qquad
B_X=\frac{X^{2/3+o(1)}}{\log X}.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Only two consequences of this choice will be used:

\[
\lambda_X\le B_X,
\qquad
\frac{B_X}{\log X}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{1.4}
\]

## 2. The one new lemma

For a real random variable `T`, write `kappa_j(T)` for its `j`-th cumulant:

\[
\log\mathbb E e^{zT}
=\sum_{j\ge1}\kappa_j(T)\frac{z^j}{j!}
\]

as a formal power series at `z=0`.  Since `S_X` has finite support, all cumulants are well-defined algebraically.  Also `kappa_1(S_X)=0`.

### Apéry logarithmic connected-tensor lemma (`ALCT_log`)

There exist absolute constants `A>0` and `c_0>0` such that, for every sufficiently large `X` and every integer

\[
2\le j\le2c_0\log X,
\]

the actual Apéry zero sets satisfy

\[
\boxed{
|\kappa_j(S_X)|
\le (Aj)^j\,(1+\lambda_X).}
\tag{ALCT}
\]

Everything in this statement is explicit:

* `S_X` is formed from the actual integer Apéry sequence `b_r`;
* the probability space is exactly the `2X^2` odd integers below `4X^2`;
* the range of cumulant orders is logarithmic;
* the right side is the Poisson/Bernstein scale, with only the standard factorial growth in the order.

This is the single new lemma I recommend attacking.  It is strictly stronger and structurally cleaner than a fixed eighth moment, but much weaker than independence of all residue columns.  It asks only that **connected** correlations have the expected sparse scale through the order needed for the moment-to-maximum argument.

The factor `(Aj)^j` is harmless.  Since `j=O(log X)` while `B_X` dominates every fixed power of `log X`, all such tensor-complexity factors are absorbed by the `B_X^t` allowance in (6.1).

## 3. `ALCT_log` implies the full logarithmic moment bound (6.1)

A standard finite-order cumulant-to-moment estimate gives the following consequence of (ALCT): for every integer `s <= c_0 log X`,

\[
\boxed{
\|S_X\|_{2s}
\le C_A\left(\sqrt{s(1+\lambda_X)}+s\right),}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where the norm is with respect to uniform `Y_X` and `C_A` depends only on `A`.

For completeness, this does not require an infinite moment-generating-function argument.  The moment-cumulant formula is

\[
\mathbb E S_X^{2s}
=\sum_{\substack{\pi\text{ a partition of }\{1,\ldots,2s\}\\
                  \text{no singleton blocks}}}
  \prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_{|B|}(S_X).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Insert (ALCT), group partitions according to their number of blocks, and use Stirling's bound `j^j <= e^j j!`.  This is the usual Bernstein/Rosenthal partition estimate and yields (3.1).  A much cruder direct partition count would also suffice here, because powers of `s=O(log X)` are negligible compared with `B_X`.

Now fix

\[
1\le t\le c\log X
\]

with `c>0` chosen small enough in terms of `c_0`, and put

\[
s=\lceil t/2\rceil.
\]

Then `2s <= 2c_0 log X` for large `X`.  By monotonicity of normalized `L^q` norms, (1.2), and (3.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|K_X\|_t
&\le \mu_X+\|S_X\|_t\\
&\le (1+X^{-1})\lambda_X
 +C_A\left(\sqrt{s(1+\lambda_X)}+s\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since `lambda_X <= B_X`, `s=O(log X)`, and `log X=o(B_X)`, one has for all sufficiently large `X`

\[
\sqrt{s(1+\lambda_X)}\le 2B_X,
\qquad
s\le B_X.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\|K_X\|_t\le C_1 B_X}
\tag{3.4}
\]

with an absolute `C_1`.

Finally `(K)_t <= K^t` for every nonnegative integer `K`, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_t(X)
&=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_X}(K_X(y))_t\\
&\le\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y_X}K_X(y)^t\\
&=N_X\|K_X\|_t^t\\
&\le2X^2(C_1B_X)^t\\
&\le4X^2(CB_X)^t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is exactly (6.1), after increasing the absolute constant `C`.

Thus the whole all-index theorem is reduced to the single connected-cumulant estimate (ALCT).

## 4. Why this property excludes the anchored star

In the anchored-star model, after deleting `O(1)` primes, one has

\[
\Omega_p^*=\{y_0,-y_0\}\pmod p
\]

for one fixed odd integer `y_0`.  Its nonzero additive Fourier coefficients are

\[
\widehat{\Omega_p^*}(h)
=e_p(-hy_0)+e_p(hy_0)
=2\cos\frac{2\pi h y_0}{p}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is a coherent rank-one translation phase across every prime.  In particular,

\[
K_X^*(y_0)=\pi(2X)-\pi(X)-O(1)
\asymp\frac X{\log X}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Take `s` to be a sufficiently small positive multiple of `log X`.  Since the probability of the single point `y_0` is `1/N_X`,

\[
\|K_X^*-\mu_X^*\|_{2s}
\ge N_X^{-1/(2s)}
    \left(K_X^*(y_0)-\mu_X^*\right)
\asymp\frac X{\log X}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Here `N_X^{-1/(2s)}` is bounded below by a positive constant.  On the other hand, the right side of (3.1) for the star is only `O(log X)`, because `lambda_X^* asymp 1/log X`.

Hence the star violates (ALCT) at some order at most `2s`.  This is not merely a cardinality distinction: it identifies the exact forbidden structure.  The star has a common additive character constituent that remains connected at every tensor order.

For the actual Apéry sets, the required arithmetic assertion is that no such common rank-one phase—and no more complicated connected tensor constituent of comparable strength—survives through logarithmic order.

## 5. Exact Fourier form of the missing input

Define the unnormalized Fourier transform

\[
\widehat\Omega_p(h)
=\sum_{a\in\Omega_p}e_p(-ha)
=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}
  \mathbf1_{p\mid b_r}\,e_p(-h(2r+1)),
\tag{5.1}
\]

and, for `h != 0 mod p`,

\[
c_{p,h}=\frac{\widehat\Omega_p(h)}p.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Complete-period Fourier inversion gives

\[
A_p(y)-\rho_p
=\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}c_{p,h}e_p(hy).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Parseval gives the exact one-prime energy

\[
\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}|c_{p,h}|^2
=\rho_p-\rho_p^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
V_X:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
     \sum_{h=1}^{p-1}|c_{p,h}|^2
=\sum_p(\rho_p-\rho_p^2)
\le\lambda_X.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equation (5.5), together with the ordinary additive large sieve, is exactly the already-known second-moment input.  It says nothing about one anchored spike.

For the higher-order expansion, put

\[
D_{N_X}^{\rm odd}(\theta)
=\frac1{N_X}\sum_{u=0}^{N_X-1}
 e\bigl((2u+1)\theta\bigr).
\tag{5.6}
\]

For a block `B` of indices and primes/frequencies `(p_i,h_i)`, one has the exact identity

\[
\mathbb E
\prod_{i\in B}(A_{p_i}(Y_X)-\rho_{p_i})
=
\sum_{\substack{1\le h_i<p_i\\i\in B}}
\left(\prod_{i\in B}c_{p_i,h_i}\right)
D_{N_X}^{\rm odd}
\left(\sum_{i\in B}\frac{h_i}{p_i}\right).
\tag{5.7}
\]

The joint cumulant is obtained by the connected-part inclusion-exclusion

\[
\operatorname{cum}(f_1,\ldots,f_j)
=
\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_j}
 (|\pi|-1)!(-1)^{|\pi|-1}
 \prod_{B\in\pi}\mathbb E\prod_{i\in B}f_i.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Since cumulants of order at least two are unchanged by adding constants,

\[
\kappa_j(S_X)
=
\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_j\in\mathcal P_X}
\operatorname{cum}
\bigl(A_{p_1}(Y_X),\ldots,A_{p_j}(Y_X)\bigr).
\tag{5.9}
\]

Equations (5.7)--(5.9) are the exact Fourier large-sieve target.  The partition alternating sum removes all products of lower-order CRT statistics.  What remains is the **connected, non-Wick tensor contribution**.  The desired estimate is precisely

\[
\left|
\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_j}
\operatorname{cum}
\bigl(A_{p_1},\ldots,A_{p_j}\bigr)
\right|
\le(Aj)^j(1+\lambda_X)
\tag{5.10}
\]

through `j=O(log X)`.

This is substantially more informative than saying “apply a higher large sieve.”  A proof must control the signed connected sum in (5.10).  Bounding each Fourier tuple absolutely is much too strong and unnecessary; the cancellation is tensorial and aggregate.

## 6. The Apéry-specific arithmetic behind a possible proof

The repository gives two compatible descriptions of the actual coefficients:

1. `b_r` is a diagonal/constant-term coefficient of one fixed Laurent polynomial over the integers;
2. modulo `p`, `b_r` is the selected Mellin coefficient of the fixed rank-three K3/Sym² Apéry system, with
   \[
   M_p(\omega_p^r)\equiv-b_r
   \]
   at the selected prime above `p`.

These are exactly the structures absent from the anchored star.  The star inserts a new translation `y_0 mod p` independently into every characteristic; it is not generated by the same fixed integral coefficient object.

However, fixed-object provenance by itself is not enough.  The quantitative property that has to be proved is the following.

### Horizontal tensor primitivity

For every signed tensor word of length at most `2c_0 log X` formed from the Apéry residual Mellin coefficients and the additive/Kummer twists appearing in (5.7), every rank-one constituent must be one of the finite structural invariants forced by the monodromy category—duality contractions and, for the `SO_3`-type rank-three system, the determinant tensor.  After those bounded-size blocks are removed, the tensor word has no trivial or translation-character constituent.

In additive-Fourier language this says that a common phase

\[
e_p(hy_0)
\]

cannot persist across a positive proportion of defining primes unless it comes from one of the explicitly classified structural contractions.  That is exactly what excludes the anchored star.

### Quantitative horizontal trace estimate

Tensor primitivity must then be combined with a trace estimate whose constants grow at most

\[
\exp(O(j\log j))
\]

at tensor order `j`.  Such growth is acceptable: for `j=O(log X)`, every fixed power of `j` is swallowed by `B_X`.

The required trace estimate is **horizontal and defining-characteristic**.  It must bound the aggregate connected sum (5.10) while the base characteristic, the selected character, and the residual prime all vary together.

This is the exact new input.  It is not currently supplied by:

* all-character Mellin equidistribution, which may allow one selected character per field to be exceptional;
* a compatible-system large sieve with auxiliary `ell != p`;
* a complex Deligne bound, which does not detect divisibility at the selected prime over `p`;
* the natural Artin--Schreier sheaf of the interpolating function `r -> b_r mod p`, whose conductor grows like `p`;
* fixed-depth Cartier or local gap-polynomial identities, which the anchored star survives.

Thus the proposed proof mechanism is concrete but genuinely new:

1. construct an integral residual Mellin/tensor object before specializing the characteristic;
2. classify its invariant tensors uniformly through logarithmic order;
3. prove a selected-character, defining-prime horizontal trace/large-sieve estimate for every nonstructural connected tensor;
4. sum the structural tensor partitions using (5.4), obtaining (ALCT);
5. invoke Section 3 to obtain (6.1) and the all-index theorem.

A proof of Steps 1--3 would be the actual breakthrough.  Merely citing big monodromy of the fixed-`p` K3 family does not prove them.

## 7. Exact Selberg/CRT face of the same lemma

For a squarefree integer `d` supported on `mathcal P_X`, define

\[
N_X(d)=
\#\{y\in\mathcal Y_X:
      y\bmod p\in\Omega_p\text{ for every }p\mid d\},
\tag{7.1}
\]

and

\[
\rho(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}\rho_p.
\tag{7.2}
\]

There is an exact identity

\[
\boxed{
F_t(X)
=t!\sum_{\substack{d\text{ squarefree}\\
                    p\mid d\Rightarrow p\in\mathcal P_X\\
                    \omega(d)=t}}
N_X(d).}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Indeed, an integer with `K_X(y)=k` is counted once for every unordered choice of `t` bad primes, and multiplication by `t!` turns `binom(k,t)` into `(k)_t`.

The independent-density main term already has the correct size:

\[
\begin{aligned}
t!\sum_{\omega(d)=t}N_X\rho(d)
&\le N_X\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\rho_p\right)^t\\
&=N_X\lambda_X^t\\
&\le N_XB_X^t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus a Selberg-sieve proof of (6.1) would amount to the positive connected-discrepancy estimate

\[
\boxed{
 t!\sum_{\omega(d)=t}
 \bigl(N_X(d)-N_X\rho(d)\bigr)_+
 \le N_X(CB_X)^t,
 \qquad t\le c\log X.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The cumulant lemma (ALCT) is the connected Fourier form of this assertion: Möbius inversion on set partitions removes products of lower-order discrepancies before they are summed.

The exact fixed-exponent reduction in

```text
problems/3.2/ORACLE_COMM/chatgpt_q32_fixed_exponent_mellin_attack.md
```

shows what (7.5) means arithmetically.  In each quotient cell, reflection folds a hit to one of the two affine rays

\[
p=\frac{m-s}{q},
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
p=\frac{m+s+1}{q+1},
\]

with

\[
p\mid b_s.
\]

Therefore (7.5) is a logarithmic-order upper-bound sieve for simultaneous large-prime divisibility along these two affine rays of the **fixed characteristic-zero Apéry sequence**.

Classical Selberg sieve does not prove (7.5).  For `t>=3`, the product modulus is already much larger than the `X^2` interval, and arbitrary sparse residue sets can place one CRT representative at an anchor.  The new ingredient must be an Apéry-specific bound for the connected simultaneous-divisibility excess, which is exactly (ALCT)/(5.10).

## 10. What would and would not count as a proof

A proof of (6.1) along this route must contain all three of the following items.

1. **Connected tensor classification.**  Identify every invariant tensor that can contribute without cancellation, including the `SO_3` determinant invariant as well as duality pairings.  A claim that “only Wick pairings occur” is too strong for an orthogonal rank-three system unless the determinant blocks are explicitly handled.
2. **Uniform logarithmic complexity.**  Bound ranks, conductors, and exceptional loci by `exp(O(j log j))` or better for `j<=c log X`.
3. **Defining-prime horizontal estimate.**  Control the selected residual coefficient while `p` is simultaneously the base and residual characteristic.  An auxiliary-`ell` estimate or an average over all characters does not type-check as a substitute.

After these are proved, the remaining steps are formal Fourier/cumulant bookkeeping and are given above.

What would **not** count:

* proving a stronger pointwise bound for `Z(p)` but no cross-prime correlation;
* proving fixed-`p` monodromy or complex equidistribution;
* proving (5.4) again;
* proving bounded row codegrees;
* obtaining finitely many moments while their order remains fixed as `X` grows;
* replacing the defining-prime condition by characteristic-zero vanishing.
