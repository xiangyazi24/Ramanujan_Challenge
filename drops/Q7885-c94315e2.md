# The Apéry unit-root/Mellin attack: what can be constructed, what it proves, and where cross-prime decorrelation still fails

## Executive verdict

There is a real unit-root theorem here, but it does **not** have the form assumed in the question.

1. **Fiberwise and over the ordinary locus, the rank-two elliptic system has a canonical rank-one slope-zero factor.** At an ordinary fiber its Frobenius eigenvalue is the unique \(p\)-adic unit root \(\alpha_x\) of
   
   \[
   X^2-a_p(E_x)X+p=0.
   \]
   
   Katz's slope filtration constructs this factor in the category of **convergent** \(F\)-isocrystals. In the present modular family it is not, in general, a Berthelot-overconvergent sub-\(F\)-isocrystal. The assertion in the question that Katz automatically supplies an overconvergent line is false.

2. **The slope-zero line of the rank-three K3 system is exactly the symmetric square of the elliptic unit-root line on the split quadratic cover.** Its Frobenius eigenvalue is \(\alpha_x^2\); after descent it is the canonical slope-zero line of the symmetric-square/Asai K3 system. The elementary slope calculation \((0,1)\mapsto(0,1,2)\) is correct.

3. **Modulo \(p\), the Apéry Mellin coefficient can be rewritten using only this unit-root value.** If \(u_p(a)\) denotes the slope-zero Frobenius eigenvalue of the primitive K3 fiber when it exists, and \(u_p(a)=0\) on the nonordinary locus, then for every interior character index \(1\le r\le p-2\), with the repository's fixed Teichmüller/exponent convention,
   
   \[
   oxed{
   \sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^	imes}u_p(a)\,\omega_p(a)^r
   \equiv -b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.}
   	ag{URM}
   \]
   
   On a split ordinary fiber, \(u_p(a)=\alpha_x^2\). This is a genuine theorem and is the strongest positive outcome of the unit-root proposal.

4. **However, the Mellin transform of a rank-one fiber object is not a rank-one object in \(r\).** The relevant transform is compactly supported cohomology, or in the non-overconvergent unit-root setting a Fredholm/nuclear trace construction. Rank is not preserved by Mellin transform. Worse, extracting the unit-root line forces removal of the supersingular divisor, whose degree grows linearly with \(p\); the fixed-complexity advantage of the original rank-three middle extension is lost.

5. **There is no overconvergent modular form in the Kummer index \(r\) produced by this construction.** The variable \(r\) labels the \(p-1\) tame characters of \(\mathbf F_p^	imes\). It is a discrete component label, not a \(p\)-adic weight variable. The tame character group algebra splits into \(p-1\) independent idempotents, and \(r\mapsto\omega_p^r\) has no continuous interpolation on \(\mathbf Z_p\).

6. **Dwork theory applies directly and powerfully in the geometric fiber parameter.** It constructs the Hasse-Witt scalar, the ordinary unit-root crystal, and a \(p\)-adic limit formula for its Frobenius. In fact, modulo \(p\), the unit-root Frobenius function is the Apéry Hasse polynomial
   
   \[
   A_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m t^m.
   \]
   
   Taking its finite multiplicative Mellin transform merely extracts the coefficient \(b_r\). Thus, at the residual level, the proposed unit-root attack is an exact re-expression of the existing Hasse/Mellin coefficient problem, not a reduction in complexity.

7. **Applying Dwork/Gross-Koblitz directly to the terminating hypergeometric expression for \(b_r\) does not produce a unique unit root.** The repository has already proved that every interior index has at least two equal-slope unit contributions, and a zero is exactly cancellation among them. This is a terminal obstruction to a valuation-only or unique-unit-root proof in the index variable.

8. **The rank-one unit-root pieces cannot form a fixed cross-prime compatible subsystem.** Such a subsystem would give an invariant line in the characteristic-zero rank-three \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathrm{SL}_2)\) local system, contradicting irreducibility. The slope-zero line is intrinsically \(p\)-dependent.

The corrected research conclusion is therefore:

> The unit-root object already exists and gives the exact congruence (URM). The frontier is not its construction. The frontier is a new residual, same-characteristic, cross-prime decorrelation theorem for the finite Mellin coefficients of a \(p\)-dependent, generally non-overconvergent unit-root system—or, more plausibly, a theorem using higher \(p\)-adic unit-root digits as extra marked data inside the full fixed-complexity Mellin-difference crystal.

# 2. Part A: construction of the elliptic unit-root line

## 2.2 Family construction by the slope filtration

Let \(C_p^{\mathrm{ord}}\) be the ordinary locus of the elliptic family (1.2). Katz's slope-filtration theorem applies because the Newton polygon is constant with slopes \(0,1\) on this locus. In the isogeny category of convergent \(F\)-crystals there is a canonical filtration

\[

\[

The invariant datum is the rank-one \(F\)-isocrystal, not a globally preferred scalar function.

## 2.4 The crucial correction: this line is not automatically overconvergent

The words **convergent** and **overconvergent** cannot be interchanged here.

The full relative cohomology \(\mathcal H_p\) comes from a smooth proper family and has an overconvergent realization on the fixed smooth base. Katz's slope filtration is a filtration in the convergent category. The slope-zero factor need not inherit Berthelot overconvergence.

Etesse gives an explicit theorem-level counterexample: the unit-root sub-\(F\)-isocrystal of the ordinary Legendre family is not overconvergent in Berthelot's sense. This is not an exotic pathology; it is the standard behavior of ordinary elliptic families.

For unit-root \(F\)-isocrystals on a curve, Crew--Tsuzuki theory identifies overconvergence with finite local monodromy at the missing boundary points. The ordinary elliptic unit-root line corresponds to the étale part of the \(p\)-divisible group, hence to the character defining the Igusa tower. Around a supersingular point, its local monodromy is infinite. Therefore it is not overconvergent across the supersingular boundary.

This applies to the present family for a concrete reason. The curve (1.2), with its rational point of order \(6\), is a \(\Gamma_1(6)\)-type universal elliptic family. After passing to a common finite modular cover with the Legendre family, the two unit-root representations are pullbacks of the same universal ordinary elliptic representation. A finite pullback cannot turn an infinite local inertia image into a finite one. Hence:

\[
oxed{
\mathcal U_{E,p}	ext{ is a rank-one convergent unit-root }F	ext{-isocrystal,
not a generally overconvergent one.}}
	ag{2.12}
\]

One may retain an overconvergent Frobenius module after forgetting part of the connection, and Wan's nuclear \(\sigma\)-module methods can handle the associated unit-root \(L\)-function. That is weaker than a finite-rank overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal with ordinary rigid cohomology.

## 2.5 The ordinary locus has growing complexity

There is a second obstruction even before one Mellin-transforms.

# 3. Can the unit-root line be Mellin-transformed to a rank-one object in \(r\)?

No. There is a scalar Mellin transform, but no rank-preserving functor of the asserted kind.

## 3.1 The scalar transform exists

Let \(O_p\) denote the ordinary \(a\)-locus, and let \(\mathcal U_{K3,p}\) be the descended rank-one slope-zero factor of the primitive K3 system. For a Kummer character \(\chi_r=\omega_p^r\), define

\[
S_p(r)=\sum_{a\in O_p(\mathbf F_p)}
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_a\mid\mathcal U_{K3,p,a})\chi_r(a).
	ag{3.1}
\]

This is a perfectly meaningful \(p\)-adic number. It is also the coefficient of \(T\) in the logarithm of the twisted unit-root \(L\)-function:

\[
S_p(r)=[T]\log L(O_p,\mathcal U_{K3,p}\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_r},T).
	ag{3.2}
\]

Wan's theory supplies a Fredholm/nuclear description and \(p\)-adic meromorphy for unit-root \(L\)-functions in this general setting.

## 3.2 Rank is not preserved by Mellin transform

If the coefficient object were overconvergent, the sheaf-theoretic Mellin transform at \(\chi_r\) would be

\[
R\Gamma_c(O_p,\mathcal U_{K3,p}\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_r}).
	ag{3.3}
\]

Even though the coefficient sheaf has rank one, the dimension of (3.3) is controlled by the Euler characteristic and local conductors, not by \(1\). A rank-one sheaf on a many-punctured curve can have arbitrarily large \(H_c^1\). In the present case the number of ordinary-boundary punctures grows with \(p\).

Moreover, because \(\mathcal U_{K3,p}\) is generally only convergent, (3.3) is not an ordinary fixed-dimensional rigid-cohomology carrier. The correct object is typically an infinite-dimensional nuclear operator whose Fredholm determinant gives the unit-root \(L\)-function.

Thus the implication

\[
	ext{rank-one fiber object}\Longrightarrow	ext{rank-one Mellin object}
\]

is false.

## 3.3 Slope projection does not commute with compactly supported cohomology

There are two different operations:

\[
\mathcal V_p\longmapsto R\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal V_p\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_r})
	ag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal V_p|_{O_p}\longmapsto \mathcal U_{K3,p}\longmapsto
R\Gamma_c(O_p,\mathcal U_{K3,p}\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_r}).
	ag{3.5}
\]

There is no theorem identifying the second with the slope-zero part of the first. Derived compact support, middle extension, and slope filtration do not commute in this manner. This is why the dimension-two middle-extension Mellin module from Q7871/Q7880 is not a hidden rank-one transform of the fiberwise unit-root line.

## 3.4 The tame Kummer index has no \(p\)-adic analytic interpolation

For fixed \(p\), the characters \(\chi_r\) form the character group of \(\mathbf F_p^	imes\), a cyclic group of order \(p-1\). After adjoining the tame roots of unity, its group algebra is finite étale and splits:

\[
\mathbf Z_p[\mathbf F_p^	imes]\otimes\mathbf Q_p^{\mathrm{nr}}
\simeq\prod_{r=0}^{p-2}\mathbf Q_p^{\mathrm{nr}}.
	ag{3.6}
\]

A module of rank one over this algebra can hide \(p-1\) unrelated rank-one factors. It does not couple adjacent exponents.

There is also a direct continuity obstruction. Let \(\zeta
e1\) be a tame \((p-1)\)-st root of unity. In \(\mathbf Z_p\),

\[
p^n\longrightarrow0,
\]

but

\[
\zeta^{p^n}=\zeta
\]

because \(p^n\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\), whereas \(\zeta^0=1\). Thus

\[
r\longmapsto\zeta^r
\]

is not a continuous function on \(\mathbf Z_p\). There is no analytic \(r\)-line interpolating these tame characters.

Weight space decomposes as

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbf Z_p^	imes,\mathbf C_p^	imes)
=\coprod_{\eta:\mu_{p-1}	o\mathbf C_p^	imes}\mathcal W_\eta.
	ag{3.7}
\]

The index \(r\) labels the different tame components \(\eta=\omega^r\); it does not move analytically within one component. Across primes, even the number of components changes.

This rules out the proposed “rank-one object indexed analytically by \(r\)” in the standard \(p\)-adic Mellin or eigenvariety sense.

# 4. Part B: the symmetric-square unit-root projector

## 4.1 Exact slope calculation

At an ordinary elliptic fiber, write the two eigenvalues of Frobenius as

\[
\alpha,\qquadeta=rac p\alpha,
\]

with slopes \(0\) and \(1\). The symmetric square has eigenvalues

\[

	ag{4.1}
\]

Hence its slopes are

\[

	ag{4.2}
\]

The canonical slope-zero line is

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal U_{E,p},
	ag{4.3}
\]

and Frobenius acts by \(\alpha^2\). This proves the proposed slope-zero projector statement on the split cover.

Because the slope filtration is canonical, the line is stable under the deck/twist descent. On the \(a\)-line it becomes the slope-zero factor of the rank-three symmetric-square/Asai K3 system. In an inert fiber it is better to call its eigenvalue \(u_p(a)\); it is the descended Asai unit root and need not be written as the square of a scalar defined over \(\mathbf F_p\).

## 4.2 Reduction of the full primitive trace to the unit root

In the split case, the repository formula (1.4) and

\[

	heta_p(a)\equiv\alpha^2\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.

Multiply (4.7) by \(\omega_p(a)^r\), sum over \(a\in\mathbf F_p^	imes\), and use (1.8). This proves

\[
oxed{

	ag{4.8}

Thus the unit-root line really does recover the residual Apéry coefficient.

This result should be added to the project as a theorem because it cleanly separates a true unit-root statement from the false overconvergence and rank-preservation claims.

## 4.4 Why (4.8) is an exact reformulation, not yet a saving

The scalar Hasse-Witt matrix for the Apéry principal period is the polynomial

\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m t^m.
	ag{4.9}
\]

Dwork's unit-root Frobenius matrix satisfies, after the fixed normalization,

\[
\Lambda_p(t)\equiv A_p(t)\pmod p.
	ag{4.10}
\]

For an interior index \(r\), multiplicative orthogonality gives

\[
egin{aligned}

&=-\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m\sum_{t

\end{aligned}
	ag{4.11}
\]

There is exactly one surviving exponent \(m=r\) for \(1\le r\le p-2\). The endpoints have the familiar trivial-character corrections.

Equation (4.11) is the finite-field shadow of (4.8). It shows precisely what has happened:

\[
oxed{
	ext{unit-root Mellin transform modulo }p
=	ext{coefficient extraction from the Apéry Hasse polynomial}.}
	ag{4.12}
\]

The roots of \(A_p(t)\) describe nonordinary geometric fibers. The zeros \(p\mid b_r\) describe zero **coefficients** of \(A_p(t)\). Dwork's unit-root construction controls the evaluation variable \(t\), whereas the conjecture needs anti-concentration of the coefficient vector. The distinction is exactly the repository's established “Hasse-root indicator is the wrong observable” warning.

## 4.5 Is this an overconvergent modular form in the Kummer variable?

No.

On the \(x\)-modular curve, after choosing an Igusa trivialization, the Frobenius ratio \(\lambda_{E,p}(x)\) is a \(p\)-adic analytic modular **function of the geometric modular parameter**. The unit-root splitting is one of the structures underlying Katz's \(p\)-adic modular forms.

But the finite Mellin coefficient

\[
S_p(r)=\sum_x\lambda_{E,p}(x)^2\chi_r(\phi(x))
	ag{4.13}
\]

is not a modular form in \(r\). It is a finite character sum. The variable \(r\) is neither the modular parameter nor a \(p\)-adic weight variable. It jumps among the tame components (3.7), and there is no canonical analytic continuation from one component to another.

At best, for a fixed \(p\), one obtains a collection of \(p-1\) twisted unit-root Fredholm determinants. Calling this collection an overconvergent modular form in \(r\) would conflate:

- overconvergence on the modular curve;
- variation in \(p\)-adic modular weight;
- finite-field Mellin/Fourier transform in the geometric parameter.

They are different operations.

## 5.1 The exact hypergeometric identity

The Apéry number is a terminating hypergeometric value:

\[

	ag{5.1}

\[

=inom nk^2inom{n+k}k^2.
	ag{5.2}
\]

However, the parameters in (5.1) move with \(n\). The generating series

\[

	ag{5.3}
\]

is the principal period of the Apéry Laurent/K3 family and satisfies the third-order Apéry Picard--Fuchs equation. It is not simply one fixed-parameter classical \({}_4F_3(t)\). On the quadratic modular cover, the third-order system is a symmetric square of a second-order Heun/modular system.

Thus there are two distinct Dwork applications:

1. Dwork's hypergeometric parameter theory applied to the terminating values (5.1);
2. Dwork's toric/crystalline theory applied to the principal period \(B(t)\) as a function of the geometric parameter.

Only the second produces the geometric unit root discussed above.

## 5.2 Dwork's period-ratio unit root applies directly

For the toric model, let \(eta_{p^s}(t)\) be the scalar higher Hasse-Witt matrices. Dwork congruences imply

\[

eta_{p^s}(t)eta_{p^{s-1}}(t^p)^{-1}\pmod{p^s},
	ag{5.4}
\]

Equivalently, using suitable truncations \(B_{<p^s}(t)\) of the principal period, one obtains the familiar Dwork ratio

## 5.4 What Delaygue--Rivoal work supplies—and what it does not

The relevant literature must be separated accurately.

- Éric Delaygue's *Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers* proves lower bounds for \(p\)-adic valuations of factorial-ratio multisums, Beukers-type valuation conjectures for Apéry numbers, and criteria for the \(p\)-Lucas property.
- Delaygue--Rivoal--Roques' memoir *On Dwork's \(p\)-Adic Formal Congruences Theorem and Hypergeometric Mirror Maps* proves broad formal congruence theorems and integrality results for hypergeometric mirror maps.
- The recent Delaygue--Rivoal papers on Abel's problem, Gauss congruences, and Cartier congruences give arithmetic criteria for algebraicity of first-order differential equations and applications in hypergeometric settings.

These results support:

- Lucas and Dwork congruences;
- valuation lower bounds;
- integrality and algebraicity criteria;
- \(p\)-adic analytic continuation of period ratios.

They do **not** give:

- anti-concentration of the coefficient vector \((b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1})\);
- a bound for \(Z(p)=\#\{r:p\mid b_r\}\);
- independence of the defining-prime congruences at two different rational primes;
- the pair-Palm or higher cross-prime extension estimate needed by the repository.

There does not appear to be a recent Delaygue--Rivoal theorem that supplies the missing unit-root decorrelation statement. The most directly Apéry-like paper among those cited above is Delaygue's 2018 paper, not a joint unit-root paper.

# 6. Why the unit-root line cannot itself provide cross-prime compatibility

## 6.1 Irreducible monodromy forbids a global rank-one submotive

The characteristic-zero rank-two elliptic local system has connected geometric monodromy of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) type. Its symmetric square is the irreducible three-dimensional representation

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathrm{Std}).
	ag{6.1}
\]

Suppose that the \(p\)-adic unit-root lines for infinitely many \(p\) came from one rank-one compatible motivic/local-system subobject of the rank-three K3 system. After passage to an auxiliary \(\ell\)-adic realization, this would give a one-dimensional invariant subspace of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathrm{Std})\) under geometric \(\mathrm{SL}_2\). No such invariant line exists. Therefore:

\[
oxed{
	ext{the unit-root line is intrinsically \(p\)-dependent and is not a fixed compatible submotive.}}
	ag{6.2}
\]

This is not a technical gap. It is forced by the same large monodromy that makes the K3 system attractive.

## 6.2 The ordinary divisor changes with \(p\)

At prime \(p\), the line exists only on the complement of the \(p\)-dependent Hasse divisor. At a different prime \(q\), both the ordinary locus and the local unit-root representation are different. There is no common geometric open curve carrying both lines.

The full rank-three compatible system does exist on a fixed open curve, but its slope-zero factor does not commute with change of characteristic. Cross-prime tensor independence must therefore be formulated for the full compatible system or for an adelic difference object, not for the separate unit-root lines.

## 6.3 Rank one does not imply decorrelation

Even in the ideal overconvergent setting, rank one is not a randomness theorem. Two elementary examples show the logical gap.

- If \(u(t)=1\), then its finite Mellin transform is supported only at the trivial character.
- If \(u(t)=t^m\), then its Mellin transform is a delta mass at one character.

Both are rank-one trace functions and both have maximal Fourier concentration. A rank-one object can be more correlated, not less, than a large-monodromy object.

What controls Mellin anti-concentration is geometric monodromy and conductor of the **transform**, not rank of the original coefficient line.

## 6.4 The same-prime diagonal remains

The event is

\[
\mathfrak p_p\mid M_p(r),
\]

where \(p\) is simultaneously:

- the characteristic of the fiber family;
- the coefficient prime at which the trace is reduced;
- the modulus defining the character group;
- the prime whose divisibility is being tested.

Ordinary compatible-system large sieves use auxiliary primes \(\ell
e p\). Unit-root theory does not remove this diagonal. It only changes the expression for the same residual scalar.

For two rational primes \(p
e q\), the lines \(\mathcal U_{K3,p}\) and \(\mathcal U_{K3,q}\) live in different rigid categories over different ordinary loci. There is no tensor product in one fixed Tannakian category to which a standard independence theorem applies.

# 7. The relation with the dimension-six and dimension-two Mellin complexes

The unit-root proposal must be reconciled with the earlier AMDC-1 audit.

Let

\[
U=\mathbf G_m-\{\alpha,eta\}
\]

be the fixed smooth K3 parameter locus, and let \(\mathcal V_p\) be the rank-three primitive system.

The open-locus complex

\[
C_{\mathrm{open}}(p,r)=R\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal V_p[1]\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_r})
\]

has dimension \(6\) in perverse degree \(0\). The middle-extension complex

\[
C_{\mathrm{IC}}(p,r)=R\Gamma_c(\mathbf G_m,j_{!*}\mathcal V_p[1]\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_r})
\]

has dimension \(2\) in perverse degree \(0\). They differ by the two rank-two invariant stalks at the conifold points.

Neither is the Mellin transform of the fiberwise unit-root line. They use the **full** fixed-rank K3 system, whose geometric singularities are fixed. Their Frobenius traces are weight-three algebraic integers of Weil size \(O(p^{3/2})\), and reduction of the exact normalized trace gives \(-b_r\).

The unit-root reduction (4.8) says only that the same trace is congruent modulo \(p\) to a slope-zero pointwise sum. It does not identify the two-dimensional Frobenius module with a rank-one unit-root cohomology group.

This distinction can be summarized as

\[
egin{aligned}

M_p(r)&\equiv S_p(r)\pmod{\mathfrak p_p},\

\end{aligned}
	ag{7.1}
\]

Only the middle line is a congruence; it is not an isomorphism of Frobenius modules.

# 8. A precise corrected theorem package for the repository

The following statements are merge-safe targets.

## Theorem UR-1: elliptic ordinary slope factor

For every good \(p\), on the ordinary locus of (1.2), the convergent rank-two \(F\)-isocrystal \(\mathcal H_p\) has a canonical rank-one slope-zero factor \(\mathcal U_{E,p}\). At an ordinary closed point \(x\), Frobenius acts on it by the unique unit root \(\alpha_x\) of (2.1).

Do **not** call this factor Berthelot-overconvergent without a separate local-monodromy proof; for the modular family, the standard Igusa-monodromy obstruction shows that it is generally not.

## Theorem UR-2: K3 symmetric-square unit root

On the split quadratic cover,

\[
\mathcal U_{K3,p}\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal U_{E,p},

With \(u_p(a)\) defined by (4.6), for every \(1\le r\le p-2\),

## Theorem UR-4: Dwork realization

On the Hasse-invertible \(p\)-adic formal ordinary locus, the scalar higher Hasse-Witt matrices define a free rank-one Dwork unit-root quotient with Frobenius matrix (2.9), congruent modulo \(p\) to the Apéry Hasse polynomial.

# 9. What a viable unit-root research program would have to add

The present idea is not useless. It identifies a concrete source of extra \(p\)-adic data. But the useful information starts **above the first residue digit**.

## 9.1 Step 1: compute the exact Apéry Dwork unit root

Fix the repository's toric differential form and Frobenius lift. Prove an exact formula

\[
\Lambda_p(a)=\lim_{s	o\infty}rac{eta_{p^s}(a)}{eta_{p^{s-1}}(a^p)}
	ag{9.1}
\]

and identify its pullback under \(\phi\) with the square of the elliptic period-ratio unit root, including the \(-3\) twist and gauge factor. Verify:

\[
\Lambda_p(a)\equiv A_p(a)\pmod p.
	ag{9.2}
\]

This is finite and realistic; it should be done.

## 9.2 Step 2: extract the second \(p\)-adic digit

Write on the ordinary locus

\[
\Lambda_p(a)=A_p(a)+pR_p(a)\pmod{p^2}.
	ag{9.3}
\]

Define the Mellin coefficients

\[
\widehat\Lambda_p(r)=\sum_a\Lambda_p(a)\omega_p(a)^r.
	ag{9.4}
\]

Then

\[
\widehat\Lambda_p(r)\equiv-b_r\pmod p.
\]

At a residual zero, the normalized second digit

\[
J_{p,r}:=rac{\widehat\Lambda_p(r)}p\pmod p
	ag{9.5}
\]

is a genuine extra label. The project should determine whether \(J_{p,r}\) is the already-seen companion/Eichler coordinate, a linear combination of the Dwork depth-tower coordinates, or a new invariant.

The first residue digit is tautological. The second digit is the earliest point where unit-root theory can add information beyond \(b_rmod p\).

## 9.3 Step 3: use a nuclear Mellin operator, not a fictitious rank-one transform

For a Kummer character \(\chi\), form the twisted unit-root \(L\)-function

\[
L_\chi(T)=L(O_p,\mathcal U_{K3,p}\otimes\mathcal L_\chi,T).
	ag{9.6}
\]

Wan's method represents \(L_\chi\) by a quotient of Fredholm determinants of nuclear operators. A concrete Apéry implementation should construct the operator explicitly from the Dwork Cartier operator and the Kummer multiplier.

The desired finite sum is

\[
[T]\log L_{\chi_r}(T).
	ag{9.7}
\]

The challenge is then to prove uniform correlation estimates among these first Fredholm coefficients as \(r\) and \(p\) vary. Meromorphy of \(L_\chi\) alone gives no such estimate.

## 9.4 Step 4: quantify the supersingular local monodromy

Determine the local representation of \(\mathcal U_{E,p}\) around every zero of \(K_p(x)\). The correct output is likely a logarithmic-decay or Igusa-tower character, not finite monodromy. One needs:

- ramification breaks;
- radii of overconvergence/log-decay;
- nuclear norms of the resulting Dwork operator;
- dependence on \(p\) and on the Hasse zero.

This is required before any uniform trace formula can be credible.

## 9.5 Step 5: restore an exponent-shift operator

Packaging the \(p-1\) tame characters in their split group algebra does not couple them. The unit-root construction would need the same missing ingredient identified in Q7841:

\[
(\mathscr M,\Phi,\Sigma,\Lambda),
\]

where \(\Phi\) is Frobenius, \(\Sigma\) implements \(r\mapsto r+1\), and \(\Lambda\) is an integral lattice. The Apéry recurrence should be the scalar shadow of \(\Sigma\), not merely a relation among the already-separated character traces.

Without \(\Sigma\), the “universal Mellin object” is just a product of unrelated fibers.

## 9.6 Step 6: formulate the actual cross-prime theorem

For distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), the repository ultimately needs a squarefree-modulus estimate for

\[
\#\left\{m<M:\ p_i\mid b_{mmod p_i}\ 	ext{for all }iight\},
	ag{9.8}
\]

or the equivalent higher pair-Palm conditional extension statistic.

A unit-root reformulation replaces each condition in (9.8) by a residual Mellin equation, but it does not place the equations in one common \(p\)-adic category. A successful theorem must use either:

- an adelic characteristic-zero Mellin-difference object with strong approximation and residual mixing; or
- arithmetic independence of the higher marked digits \(J_{p,r}\) derived from Dwork Frobenius; or
- a new product trace formula that works simultaneously in different defining characteristics.

No existing Katz, Dwork, Wan, or Delaygue--Rivoal theorem supplies this.

# 10. Decision on the three proposed routes

## (A) Elliptic unit-root line and Mellin transform

**Construction of the line:** yes, theorem-level. It is the canonical slope-zero factor of the convergent ordinary elliptic \(F\)-crystal; Dwork/Katz gives an explicit period-ratio realization.

**Overconvergent subcrystal:** no, not as stated. For this non-isotrivial modular family, supersingular/Igusa local monodromy is the standard obstruction. The line is generally not Berthelot-overconvergent.

**Rank-one object indexed by \(r\):** no. The scalar Mellin sums exist, but their cohomological/Fredholm carrier is not rank one, and the tame index has no analytic \(p\)-adic interpolation.

## (B) Symmetric-square projector and modularity

**Slopes \((0,1,2)\) and eigenvalue \(\alpha^2\):** correct. On the split cover this is an immediate symmetric-square calculation; canonically it descends to the K3 unit-root line.

**Residual Apéry coefficient:** correct via (4.8).

**Overconvergent modular form in the Kummer variable:** false/underdefined. The unit-root ratio is a \(p\)-adic modular function of the geometric modular parameter on the ordinary Igusa tower. Its finite-field Mellin coefficients are not modular forms in \(r\).

## (C) Dwork hypergeometric theory

**Application to the Apéry period/family:** yes, directly and strongly. It gives Hasse-Witt matrices, Dwork congruences, analytic continuation, and the unit-root Frobenius ratio.

**Application to the terminating index formula as a unique unit root:** no. The exact Gross--Koblitz/Morita decomposition has at least two unit terms at every interior index; zero is unit cancellation.

**Cross-prime decorrelation:** not supplied. Delaygue and Delaygue--Rivoal(-Roques) results establish congruence, valuation, integrality, and algebraicity phenomena, not the required microscopic residual independence.

# 11. Final assessment

The unit-root attack produces an important exact identity but not the hoped-for reduction.

The positive theorem is

\[
-b_r\equiv\operatorname{Mellin}_r(u_p)\pmod p,
\]

where \(u_p\) is the slope-zero K3 Frobenius function, equal to \(\alpha_x^2\) on split ordinary fibers.

The obstruction is that \(u_p\) is not a fixed low-complexity trace function as \(p\) varies:

- it is defined only on a \(p\)-dependent ordinary locus;
- its unit-root line is generally non-overconvergent;
- its supersingular boundary has linear complexity;
- its Mellin transform does not preserve rank;
- its tame character index is discrete;
- its mod-\(p\) reduction is exactly the existing Hasse polynomial \(A_p\).

Thus the first-digit unit-root projector does not explain the observed Poisson, Palm, Sidon, or ARD data. It repackages the same residual coefficient vector.

The best new direction is narrower and more concrete:

> Construct the exact Dwork unit-root Frobenius in the repository's normalization, compute its second and higher \(p\)-adic Mellin digits, and test whether those digits give a bounded-dimensional marked state whose conditional distribution is transverse across primes. Keep the full fixed-complexity K3 Mellin complex as the global carrier; use the unit-root expansion as additional marked arithmetic data, not as a replacement sheaf.

That is a plausible research program. A claim that the rank-one unit-root line itself yields cross-prime decorrelation is not presently justified.
