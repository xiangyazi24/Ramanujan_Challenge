# The Apéry unit-root and Mellin attack

## Executive verdict

There is a genuine unit-root theorem here, but it does not have the form assumed in the question.

1. **The rank-two elliptic system has a canonical rank-one slope-zero factor on its ordinary locus.** At an ordinary fiber, Frobenius has a unique p-adic unit eigenvalue α. Katz's slope filtration constructs the corresponding line in the category of convergent F-isocrystals.

2. **That line is not automatically Berthelot-overconvergent.** For non-isotrivial ordinary elliptic families, the unit-root factor is the standard place where overconvergence fails. Etesse proves this explicitly for the Legendre family. The present Γ1(6)-type family has the same Igusa-tower local-monodromy obstruction after passage to a common modular cover.

3. **The symmetric-square slope calculation is correct.** If the elliptic slopes are 0 and 1, the symmetric-square slopes are 0, 1, and 2. On a split ordinary fiber, the K3 slope-zero eigenvalue is α².

4. **Modulo p, the Apéry Mellin coefficient can be written using only the K3 unit-root value.** If u_p(a) is the slope-zero eigenvalue on an ordinary K3 fiber and is defined to be 0 on a nonordinary fiber, then for every interior exponent 1 ≤ r ≤ p − 2, in the repository's fixed Teichmüller convention,

```text
sum over a in F_p^× of u_p(a) omega_p(a)^r  =  -b_r   mod p.
```

This is a theorem and is the strongest positive output of the proposal.

5. **A rank-one fiber object does not Mellin-transform to a rank-one object in r.** The transform is compactly supported cohomology when an overconvergent coefficient exists, and a Fredholm or nuclear trace construction in the actual non-overconvergent unit-root setting. Mellin transform does not preserve rank.

6. **Extracting the unit-root line loses the fixed-complexity advantage.** The full primitive K3 system has fixed rank and a fixed singular divisor. The unit-root line exists only after deleting the p-dependent supersingular divisor, whose degree grows linearly with p.

7. **There is no overconvergent modular form in the Kummer index r produced by this construction.** The index r labels the p − 1 tame characters of F_p^×. It labels disconnected tame components, not a p-adic analytic weight coordinate.

8. **Dwork theory applies directly to the geometric fiber parameter.** It constructs the Hasse-Witt scalar, the unit-root crystal, and a p-adic limit formula for Frobenius. Modulo p, that Frobenius function is the Apéry Hasse polynomial. Its finite multiplicative Mellin transform merely extracts the coefficient b_r.

9. **Dwork and Gross-Koblitz applied directly to the terminating hypergeometric expression for b_r do not isolate one unit root.** The repository proves that every interior r has at least two minimum-slope unit summands. A zero is cancellation among those units.

10. **The rank-one unit-root lines cannot form one cross-prime compatible subsystem.** A compatible rank-one subobject would give an invariant line in the characteristic-zero Sym²(SL2) local system, contradicting irreducibility. The slope-zero line is intrinsically p-dependent.

The corrected research conclusion is:

> The unit-root object already exists and gives an exact residual Mellin identity. The frontier is not its construction. The frontier is a new same-characteristic residual decorrelation theorem for the Mellin coefficients of a p-dependent, generally non-overconvergent unit-root system, or a theorem using higher p-adic unit-root digits as extra marked data inside the full fixed-complexity Mellin-difference carrier.

The repository supplies an explicit quadratic cover and elliptic family, not merely a comparison of differential equations.

For the K3 parameter a, put


On the quadratic cover


the elliptic curve is

```text
E_x : Y² + (1 − 2x)XY + x²Y = X³.
```

The deck involution and covering map are

```text
sigma(x) = (1 − 8x) / (8(1 + x)),
phi(x)   = x(1 − 8x) / (1 + x) = a.
```

The point Q = (x, −x) has exact order 6. Thus this is the genus-zero Γ1(6)-type elliptic family used by the repository. The two elliptic factors on the cover are 6-isogenous up to the constant quadratic twist −3.

Let H_p be the rank-two relative crystalline or rigid H¹ of this elliptic family. Let V_p be the rank-three primitive K3 system on the a-line. The repository proves that V_p is the symmetric-square or Asai descent of H_p, with one Tate line removed in the Kummer description.

### Inert quadratic cover

```text
theta_p(a) = a_p(E_x)² − p − chi_p(−6) p.
```

# 2. Part A: construction of the elliptic unit-root line

## 2.1 Pointwise construction

Let x be an ordinary closed fiber of degree d. Its crystalline Frobenius polynomial on H¹ is

```text
P_x(T) = T² − a_{p^d}(E_x) T + p^d.
```

Ordinarity means that the Newton slopes are 0 and 1. Equivalently, the polynomial has roots α_x and β_x such that

```text
alpha_x beta_x = p^d,
v_p(alpha_x) = 0,
v_p(beta_x)  = d.
```

Hensel factorization makes α_x the unique unit root. Therefore the slope-zero graded factor of H¹ is one-dimensional and Frobenius acts on it by α_x.

This is the precise fiberwise unit-root line.

## 2.2 Family construction by Katz's slope filtration

Let C_p^ord be the ordinary locus of the elliptic family. The Newton polygon is constant there, with slopes 0 and 1. Katz's slope-filtration theorem yields a canonical filtration in the isogeny category of convergent F-crystals whose graded factors have those two slopes.

Write U_E,p for the rank-one slope-zero factor. Depending on the covariant or contravariant Dieudonné convention, it is represented as a subobject or as a quotient. The convention-free statement is:

```text
rank(U_E,p) = 1,
Frobenius on the fiber at x acts by alpha_x.
```

Polarization identifies the slope-one factor with the dual of U_E,p up to the usual Tate twist.

### Canonical line versus scalar function

The line is canonical. A scalar function alpha(x) is not canonical until a local basis and a Frobenius lift are chosen.

If e is a local basis and Frobenius is written as

```text
Phi(sigma^* e) = lambda_p(x) e,
```

then replacing e by g(x)e changes the scalar by

```text
lambda_p(x)  ->  g(x) lambda_p(x) / g(x^p).
```

The invariant object is the rank-one F-isocrystal, not one globally preferred scalar representative.

## 2.3 Explicit Dwork construction

The toric Apéry model gives a second, explicit construction.

Let beta_m be the higher Hasse-Witt matrices of the defining Laurent polynomial. In the principal-period part of the Apéry model there is one relevant interior lattice point, so the interior Hasse-Witt matrix is scalar.

Let beta_p(t) be this scalar and work over the p-adically completed Hasse localization

```text
R_p = p-adic completion of
      Z_p[t, beta_p(t)^{-1}, fixed-singular-factors^{-1}].
```

Dwork Crystals I constructs a free rank-one unit-root quotient Q_f,p over R_p. For the Frobenius lift sigma(t) = t^p, its Frobenius matrix is

```text
Lambda_p(t)
  = limit as s -> infinity of
    beta_{p^s}(t) / beta_{p^{s-1}}(t^p).
```

The Dwork congruences imply

```text
Lambda_p(t) = beta_p(t) mod p.
```

The corresponding limiting logarithmic derivatives recover the Gauss-Manin connection. This is an explicit Dwork/Katz unit-root crystal, not merely an existence theorem.

On the elliptic cover, after choosing a compatible Frobenius lift and period normalization, the familiar local form is

```text
lambda_E,p(x) = fixed_unit * F(x) / F(x^p),
```

where F is a normalized elliptic period. The K3 unit root is the symmetric-square or descended version of this ratio.

## 2.4 Crucial correction: the line is not automatically overconvergent

The words convergent and overconvergent cannot be interchanged.

The full relative cohomology H_p comes from a smooth proper family and has an overconvergent realization on the fixed smooth base. Katz's slope filtration is a filtration in the convergent category. A slope factor need not inherit Berthelot overconvergence.

Etesse proves an explicit theorem-level counterexample: the unit-root sub-F-isocrystal of the ordinary Legendre family is not overconvergent in Berthelot's sense.

For a unit-root F-isocrystal on a curve, Crew-Tsuzuki theory identifies overconvergence with finite local monodromy at every missing boundary point. The ordinary elliptic unit-root line corresponds to the etale part of the p-divisible group, hence to an Igusa-tower character. Around a supersingular point its local inertia image is infinite.

The present E_x family is modular of Γ1(6)-type. After passing to a common finite modular cover with the Legendre family, both unit-root representations are pullbacks of the same universal ordinary elliptic representation. A finite-index pullback cannot turn an infinite local inertia image into a finite one.

Therefore the source-correct statement is

```text
U_E,p is a rank-one convergent unit-root F-isocrystal.
It is not, in general, a Berthelot-overconvergent sub-F-isocrystal.
```

One can sometimes retain an overconvergent Frobenius module after forgetting the overconvergence of the connection. Wan's nuclear sigma-module theory is designed for that weaker setting. It does not produce an ordinary finite-rank overconvergent coefficient object with fixed-dimensional rigid cohomology.

## 2.5 The ordinary locus has growing complexity

Passing to the unit-root factor changes the base.

The ordinary locus is obtained by inverting the Hasse invariant. On a modular curve, the Hasse invariant has weight p − 1 and its zero divisor is the supersingular divisor. Its degree is linear in p.

The same growth is visible in the repository's exact formulas:

```text
A_p(a) = sum from m=0 to p−1 of b_m a^m
       = Delta(a)^{epsilon_p} B_p(a)^2,
```

with deg B_p proportional to p, and on the elliptic cover

```text
K_p(x)^2 = (1 + x)^{p−1} A_p(phi(x)).
```

Thus the full primitive K3 system has fixed rank and a fixed singular divisor, whereas its unit-root line exists on a p-dependent ordinary locus with a linearly growing supersingular boundary.

If a rank-one lisse coefficient on a curve with s punctures were overconvergent, nontrivial at the punctures, and had H_c^0 = H_c^2 = 0, the Euler characteristic formula would give a first cohomology dimension of the shape

```text
dim H_c^1 = s − 2 + total Swan conductor.
```

Here s grows with p. In the actual unit-root situation, local monodromy is infinite, so one does not even have a bounded-conductor overconvergent object to which that finite-dimensional formula applies.

The complexity trade is therefore:

```text
full primitive K3 system:
  geometric rank 3,
  fixed singular complexity;

unit-root factor:
  geometric rank 1,
  singular complexity growing linearly with p.
```

Rank one is not a net prime-aspect complexity reduction.

# 3. Can the unit-root line be Mellin-transformed to rank one in r?

No. A scalar Mellin transform exists, but there is no rank-preserving construction of the asserted kind.

## 3.1 Scalar Mellin transform

Let O_p be the ordinary a-locus and let U_K3,p be the descended rank-one slope-zero factor of the K3 system. For a Kummer character chi_r, define

```text
S_p(r)
  = sum over a in O_p(F_p) of
    Trace(Frob_a on U_K3,p,a) chi_r(a).
```

This is a well-defined p-adic number.

It is also the linear coefficient of the logarithm of the twisted unit-root L-function:

```text
S_p(r)
  = coefficient of T in
    log L(O_p, U_K3,p tensor L_chi_r, T).
```

Wan's theory supplies a Fredholm or nuclear description and p-adic meromorphy for such unit-root L-functions.

## 3.2 Mellin transform does not preserve rank

If the coefficient line were overconvergent, the sheaf-theoretic specialization of its Mellin transform would be

```text
R Gamma_c(O_p, U_K3,p tensor L_chi_r).
```

Its dimension is governed by Euler characteristic and local conductors, not by the coefficient rank 1. A rank-one sheaf on a many-punctured curve can have arbitrarily large H_c^1. In this problem the number of punctures grows with p.

Because the actual unit-root factor is generally only convergent, the correct trace carrier is typically an infinite-dimensional nuclear operator whose Fredholm determinant gives the L-function.

Therefore

```text
rank-one fiber object
  does not imply
rank-one Mellin object.
```

## 3.3 Slope projection does not commute with compact support

Compare two operations:

```text
V_p
  -> R Gamma_c(fixed base, V_p tensor L_chi_r),
```

and

```text
V_p restricted to O_p
  -> U_K3,p
  -> R Gamma_c(O_p, U_K3,p tensor L_chi_r).
```

There is no theorem identifying the second with the slope-zero part of the first. Middle extension, compact support, and slope filtration do not commute in this way.

This is why the dimension-two middle-extension Mellin module from the AMDC-1 audit is not secretly a rank-one transform of the fiberwise unit-root line.

## 3.4 No p-adic analytic interpolation of the tame index

For fixed p, the characters chi_r form the character group of F_p^×, which has order p − 1. After adjoining the tame roots of unity, the group algebra splits as a product of p − 1 fields.

```text
Q_p^nr[F_p^×]  is isomorphic to
product over r=0,...,p−2 of Q_p^nr.
```

A module of rank one over this split algebra can hide p − 1 unrelated factors. It does not couple neighboring exponents.

There is also a direct continuity obstruction. Let zeta be a nontrivial tame root of unity. In Z_p,

```text
p^n -> 0,
```

but

```text
zeta^(p^n) = zeta,
```

because p^n = 1 mod p − 1, while zeta^0 = 1. Hence r -> zeta^r is not continuous on Z_p.

The p-adic weight space decomposes into p − 1 tame components. The exponent r labels those components; it does not vary analytically inside one component. Across primes, even the number of components changes.

This rules out the proposed analytic rank-one object in r in the standard p-adic Mellin, Hida, or eigenvariety sense.

# 4. Part B: the symmetric-square unit-root projector

## 4.1 Exact slope calculation

At an ordinary elliptic fiber, write the Frobenius eigenvalues as

```text
alpha,
beta = p / alpha,
```

with slopes 0 and 1. The symmetric square has eigenvalues

```text
alpha²,
alpha beta = p,
beta² = p² / alpha².
```

Therefore its slopes are

```text
0, 1, 2.
```

The slope-zero line is Sym²(U_E,p), and Frobenius acts on it by alpha². Because the slope filtration is canonical, the line is stable under the deck and twist descent. On an inert fiber it is safest to denote the descended Asai unit eigenvalue by u_p(a), rather than force a square-root expression over F_p.

## 4.2 Reduction of the primitive trace to the unit root

In the split case, write

```text
a_p(E_x) = alpha + beta,
alpha beta = p.
```

The repository formula gives

```text
theta_p(a)
  = (alpha + beta)² − p − 1_{a=1}p
  = alpha² + p + beta² − 1_{a=1}p.
```

Thus

```text
theta_p(a) = alpha² mod p.
```

At a branch point, the extra term −chi_p(−6)p also vanishes modulo p. In the inert case, the same conclusion follows from the canonical slope decomposition of the Asai crystal: all positive-slope eigenvalues lie in the maximal ideal, so the trace reduces to the slope-zero eigenvalue.

At a nonordinary fiber there is no slope-zero eigenvalue. Define

```text
u_p(a) = slope-zero K3 Frobenius eigenvalue, if ordinary;
u_p(a) = 0,                               if nonordinary.
```

Then at every nonzero fiber, including the explicit Tate and branch corrections,

```text
theta_p(a) = u_p(a) mod p.
```

## 4.3 Exact unit-root Mellin identity

Multiply the pointwise congruence by omega_p(a)^r and sum over a. The exact repository Mellin comparison gives

```text
sum over a in F_p^× of u_p(a) omega_p(a)^r
  = −b_r mod p,
1 ≤ r ≤ p − 2.
```

On a split ordinary fiber, u_p(a) = alpha_x².

This is a clean theorem and should be recorded separately from any overconvergence claim.

## 4.4 Why this is an exact reformulation, not a saving

The scalar Hasse-Witt polynomial for the Apéry principal period is

```text
A_p(t) = sum from m=0 to p−1 of b_m t^m.
```

The Dwork unit-root Frobenius matrix satisfies, after the fixed normalization,

```text
Lambda_p(t) = A_p(t) mod p.
```

For an interior exponent r, multiplicative orthogonality gives

```text
− sum over t in F_p^× of A_p(t) t^(−r)
  = b_r mod p.
```

Indeed, after expanding A_p, the inner sum over t vanishes unless m = r, and equals −1 when m = r.

Therefore

```text
unit-root Mellin transform mod p
  = coefficient extraction from the Apéry Hasse polynomial.
```

The roots of A_p describe nonordinary geometric fibers. The zeros p | b_r describe zero coefficients of A_p. Dwork's unit-root construction controls the evaluation variable t; the conjecture needs anti-concentration of the coefficient vector.

This is exactly the category distinction already isolated in the repository's Hasse-root no-go.

## 4.5 Is this an overconvergent modular form in r?

No.

On the x-modular curve, after an Igusa trivialization, the Frobenius ratio lambda_E,p(x) is a p-adic analytic modular function of the geometric modular parameter. The unit-root splitting is part of Katz's theory of p-adic modular forms.

But a finite sum such as

```text
sum over x of lambda_E,p(x)² chi_r(phi(x))
```

is a finite character sum. It is not a modular form in r. The index r is neither the modular parameter nor a p-adic weight coordinate.

At best, fixed p gives a collection of p − 1 twisted unit-root Fredholm determinants. Calling that collection an overconvergent modular form in r conflates three different notions:

- overconvergence on the modular curve;
- variation in p-adic modular weight;
- finite-field Mellin transform in the geometric parameter.

# 5. Part C: what Dwork theory does for Apéry

## 5.1 Exact terminating hypergeometric identity

The Apéry number is

```text
b_n = 4F3(−n, −n, n+1, n+1 ; 1, 1, 1 ; 1).
```

The k-th term is exactly

```text
binom(n,k)² binom(n+k,k)².
```

However, the hypergeometric parameters move with n. The generating series

```text
B(t) = sum from n≥0 of b_n t^n
```

is the principal period of the Apéry Laurent and K3 family and satisfies the third-order Apéry Picard-Fuchs equation. It is not one fixed-parameter classical 4F3 in t. On the quadratic modular cover, the rank-three equation is the symmetric square of a rank-two Heun or modular equation.

Thus two Dwork applications must be distinguished:

1. parameter and congruence theory for the terminating value at index n;
2. toric and crystalline unit-root theory for the principal period as a function of the geometric parameter t.

Only the second produces the geometric unit-root line.

## 5.2 Dwork period-ratio theory applies directly

For the toric model, let beta_{p^s}(t) be the scalar higher Hasse-Witt matrices. Dwork congruences imply that the ratios

```text
beta_{p^s}(t) / beta_{p^{s−1}}(t^p)
```

form a p-adic Cauchy sequence on the Hasse-invertible domain. Their limit is the Frobenius matrix of the unit-root crystal.

Equivalently, with a source-correct choice of truncations of the principal period, one obtains the familiar ratio

```text
unit root  =  limit of
truncation at p^s divided by Frobenius-twisted truncation at p^{s−1},
```

up to the fixed sign, gauge, and differential-form normalization. The higher Hasse-Witt matrix formula is the invariant version.

This is precisely the domain of:

- Dwork's p-adic cycles and period matrices;
- Dwork's p-adic hypergeometric analytic continuation;
- Samol and van Straten;
- Mellit and Vlasenko;
- higher Hasse-Witt matrices;
- Beukers and Vlasenko's Dwork crystals.

The repository's Franel-tail Dwork proof is already a genuine use of this mechanism. It proves an integrality theorem by a coefficient-ring Dwork congruence. It does not prove coefficient-zero decorrelation.

## 5.3 Direct Gross-Koblitz analysis of b_r has many unit terms

The strongest exact obstruction is already proved in the repository.

For 0 ≤ k ≤ r ≤ p − 1, define the Morita-unit factor

```text
U_{p,r,k}
  = Gamma_p(r+k+1)
    / (Gamma_p(k+1)² Gamma_p(r−k+1)).
```

It is a p-adic unit, and

```text
binom(r,k) binom(r+k,k)
  = p^{1_{r+k≥p}} U_{p,r,k}.
```

Therefore

```text
b_r
  = sum from k=0 to r of
    p^{2 1_{r+k≥p}} U_{p,r,k}².
```

Put

```text
rho = min(r, p−1−r).
```

Exactly the terms k = 0, 1, ..., rho have minimum valuation zero. Hence the minimum-slope packet has size

```text
rho + 1 = 1 + min(r, p−1−r).
```

For every interior 1 ≤ r ≤ p − 2, this number is at least 2.

Reducing the exact expression gives

```text
p divides b_r
if and only if
sum from k=0 to rho of reduction(U_{p,r,k})² = 0 in F_p.
```

Thus there is no unique lowest-slope summand whose nonvanishing excludes p | b_r. The zero event is cancellation among a packet of p-adic units, and the packet can have linear size.

The unique unit root belongs to the ordinary geometric fiber in t. It is not one distinguished summand of the terminating index formula in r.

## 5.4 Scope of Delaygue and Delaygue-Rivoal work

The relevant results should be stated accurately.

- Delaygue's 2018 paper proves lower bounds for p-adic valuations of factorial-ratio multisums, Beukers-type valuation conjectures for Apéry numbers, and criteria for the p-Lucas property.
- Delaygue, Rivoal, and Roques prove a broad form of Dwork's formal congruence theorem and integrality results for hypergeometric mirror maps.
- The recent Delaygue-Rivoal papers on Abel's problem, Gauss congruences, and Cartier congruences provide arithmetic criteria for algebraicity and applications in hypergeometric settings.

These results supply congruences, valuation bounds, integrality, and p-adic analytic continuation. They do not supply:

- anti-concentration of the coefficient vector (b_0, ..., b_{p−1});
- a bound for Z(p);
- independence at two defining primes;
- the pair-Palm or higher extension estimate needed by the repository.

There is no recent Delaygue-Rivoal theorem that closes the proposed cross-prime unit-root step. The most directly Apéry-like paper in this list is Delaygue's solo 2018 paper.

# 6. Why the unit-root line cannot itself give cross-prime compatibility

## 6.1 Irreducible monodromy forbids a common rank-one submotive

The characteristic-zero rank-two elliptic local system has connected geometric monodromy of SL2 type. Its symmetric square is the irreducible three-dimensional representation Sym²(Standard).

Suppose the p-adic unit-root lines for infinitely many p came from one rank-one compatible motivic or local-system subobject of the rank-three K3 system. An auxiliary l-adic realization would then give a one-dimensional invariant subspace of Sym²(Standard) under geometric SL2.

No such invariant line exists.

Therefore

```text
the unit-root line is intrinsically p-dependent;
it is not a fixed compatible rank-one submotive.
```

This is forced by the same large monodromy that makes the K3 system useful.

## 6.2 The ordinary divisor changes with p

At prime p, the line exists on the complement of the p-dependent Hasse divisor. At prime q, the ordinary locus and local unit-root representation are different. There is no common geometric open curve carrying both lines.

The full rank-three compatible system exists on a fixed open curve, but its slope-zero factor does not commute with change of characteristic. Cross-prime tensor independence must therefore be formulated for the full compatible system or for an adelic difference object, not for the separate unit-root lines.

## 6.3 Rank one is not a randomness theorem

Two elementary rank-one examples show the gap.

```text
u(t) = 1
```

has Mellin transform supported only at the trivial character.

```text
u(t) = t^m
```

has Mellin transform supported at one character.

Both are rank-one trace functions and both have maximal Fourier concentration. Rank one can increase correlation rather than destroy it.

What controls Mellin anti-concentration is the geometry and conductor of the transform, not the rank of the original coefficient line.

## 6.4 The defining-prime diagonal remains

The event is

```text
the chosen prime above p divides M_p(r),
```

where p is simultaneously:

- the characteristic of the geometric family;
- the coefficient prime at which the trace is reduced;
- the modulus defining the tame character group;
- the prime whose divisibility is tested.

Compatible-system large sieves normally use auxiliary primes l different from p. Unit-root theory does not remove this diagonal. It only rewrites the same residual scalar.

For p ≠ q, the lines U_K3,p and U_K3,q live in different rigid categories over different ordinary loci. There is no tensor product in one fixed Tannakian category to which a standard independence theorem applies.

# 7. Relation to the dimension-six and dimension-two Mellin complexes

Let U be the fixed smooth K3 parameter locus obtained by removing the two finite Picard-Fuchs singularities, and let V_p be the rank-three primitive system.

The open-locus complex

```text
C_open(p,r)
  = R Gamma_c(U, V_p[1] tensor L_chi_r)
```

has dimension 6 in perverse degree 0.

The middle-extension complex

```text
C_IC(p,r)
  = R Gamma_c(G_m, j_!* V_p[1] tensor L_chi_r)
```

has dimension 2 in perverse degree 0. The difference is the two rank-two invariant stalks at the conifold points.

Neither is the Mellin transform of the fiberwise unit-root line. They use the full fixed-rank K3 system and a fixed singular divisor. Their traces are weight-three algebraic integers of size O(p^(3/2)), and the exact normalized trace reduces to −b_r.

The unit-root statement says only

```text
exact full Mellin trace
  = unit-root point sum mod p.
```

It does not identify their Frobenius modules.

A source-correct summary is

```text
STr(Frob on C_exact(p,r)) = M_p(r) as an algebraic integer;
M_p(r) = S_p(r) mod p;
S_p(r) is the first logarithmic coefficient of a twisted unit-root Fredholm determinant.
```

Only the middle statement is a congruence.

# 8. Merge-safe theorem package

## UR-1: elliptic ordinary slope factor

For every good p, on the ordinary locus of E_x, the convergent rank-two F-isocrystal H_p has a canonical rank-one slope-zero factor U_E,p. At an ordinary closed point x, Frobenius acts by the unique unit root alpha_x.

Do not call this factor Berthelot-overconvergent without a separate finite-local-monodromy theorem. For the modular family, Igusa monodromy gives the opposite conclusion in general.

## UR-2: K3 symmetric-square unit root

On the split quadratic cover,

```text
U_K3,p = Sym²(U_E,p),
```

and Frobenius acts by alpha_x². The line descends canonically to the ordinary locus of the symmetric-square or Asai K3 system.

## UR-3: residual unit-root Mellin identity

For u_p(a) defined as the K3 slope-zero eigenvalue on ordinary fibers and 0 otherwise,

```text
sum over a in F_p^× of u_p(a) omega_p(a)^r
  = −b_r mod p,
1 ≤ r ≤ p − 2.
```

## UR-4: Dwork realization

On the Hasse-invertible p-adic formal ordinary locus, the scalar higher Hasse-Witt matrices define a free rank-one Dwork unit-root quotient with Frobenius

```text
Lambda_p(t)
  = limit beta_{p^s}(t) / beta_{p^{s−1}}(t^p),
```

and Lambda_p(t) = A_p(t) mod p in the Apéry normalization.

The category must be named correctly: a Dwork crystal over a completed Hasse localization or a convergent F-isocrystal, not automatically a Berthelot-overconvergent subcrystal.

## UR-NO-GO: no bounded-rank Mellin consequence

UR-1 through UR-4 do not imply a bounded-rank Mellin carrier in r because:

1. the unit-root line is generally non-overconvergent;
2. its ordinary base has a p-dependent divisor of linear degree;
3. compactly supported Mellin cohomology does not preserve rank;
4. the tame character parameter has no Z_p-analytic interpolation;
5. modulo p, the transform is exactly coefficient extraction from A_p.

# 9. Viable research program

The unit-root idea can still add information above the first residual digit.

## Step 1: compute the exact Apéry Dwork unit root

Fix the repository's toric differential form and Frobenius lift. Prove the exact scalar higher-Hasse-Witt limit and identify its pullback under phi with the square of the elliptic unit-root ratio, including the −3 twist and gauge factor.

The first gate is

```text
Lambda_p(a) = A_p(a) mod p.
```

This is finite and realistic.

## Step 2: extract the second p-adic digit

Write on the ordinary locus

```text
Lambda_p(a) = A_p(a) + p R_p(a) mod p².
```

Define its finite Mellin coefficient

```text
hat_Lambda_p(r)
  = sum over a of Lambda_p(a) omega_p(a)^r.
```

Then

```text
hat_Lambda_p(r) = −b_r mod p.
```

At a residual zero, the normalized second digit

```text
J_{p,r} = hat_Lambda_p(r) / p mod p
```

is genuine extra information. The project should determine whether J_{p,r} is the existing companion or Eichler coordinate, a Dwork depth-tower coordinate, or a new marked invariant.

The first digit is tautological. The second digit is the earliest place where unit-root theory can add information beyond b_r mod p.

## Step 3: construct the nuclear Mellin operator

For a character chi, form the twisted unit-root L-function

```text
L_chi(T) = L(O_p, U_K3,p tensor L_chi, T).
```

Wan's method represents this by Fredholm determinants of nuclear operators. An Apéry implementation should construct those operators explicitly from the Dwork Cartier operator and the Kummer multiplier.

The desired finite Mellin sum is the coefficient of T in log L_chi(T). Meromorphy alone gives no anti-concentration, so the next theorem would have to be a uniform correlation estimate among these first Fredholm coefficients.

## Step 4: quantify supersingular local monodromy

Determine the local representation of U_E,p around every zero of K_p(x). Needed outputs include:

- ramification breaks;
- logarithmic decay or radii of convergence;
- nuclear norms of the Dwork operator;
- uniform dependence on p.

The expected object is an Igusa-type log-decay representation, not an overconvergent line with finite local monodromy.

## Step 5: restore an exponent-shift operator

The split tame group algebra merely packages p − 1 independent fibers. A useful universal object still needs the missing structure identified in Q7841:

```text
M        fixed-rank or controlled nuclear carrier,
Phi      Frobenius,
Sigma    exponent shift r -> r+1,
Lattice  Frobenius- and shift-stable integral structure.
```

The Apéry recurrence should be the scalar shadow of Sigma. Without Sigma, the universal tame-character package has no coupling.

## Step 6: target the actual cross-prime statistic

For distinct primes p_1, ..., p_k, the repository ultimately needs control of

```text
number of m < M such that
p_i divides b_{m mod p_i} for every i,
```

or the equivalent higher pair-Palm conditional extension statistic.

The unit-root identity replaces each divisibility event by a residual Mellin equation, but it does not put different defining characteristics in one p-adic category.

A successful theorem must instead use one of:

- an adelic characteristic-zero Mellin-difference object with strong approximation and residual mixing;
- independence of higher marked digits such as J_{p,r};
- a genuinely new product trace formula across defining characteristics.

No existing Katz, Dwork, Wan, Delaygue, or Rivoal theorem supplies this step.

# 10. Direct answers

## A. Rank-two elliptic unit-root line

**Yes:** the ordinary elliptic F-crystal has a canonical rank-one slope-zero factor, and Dwork gives an explicit period-ratio realization.

**No:** Katz's theorem does not automatically make this line Berthelot-overconvergent. For this modular family, supersingular Igusa monodromy is the standard obstruction.

**No:** its finite Mellin transform is not a rank-one object indexed analytically by r. It is a scalar coefficient of a twisted unit-root L-function, carried in general by a nuclear Fredholm operator.

## B. Symmetric-square projector

**Yes:** slopes 0 and 1 become 0, 1, and 2, and the slope-zero eigenvalue is alpha² on the split cover.

**Yes:** its residual Mellin sum equals −b_r.

**No:** this does not produce an overconvergent modular form in the Kummer variable. The p-adic modular dependence is in the geometric elliptic parameter; r is a discrete tame-character label.

## C. Dwork hypergeometric theory

**Yes:** Dwork theory applies directly to the Apéry principal period, Hasse-Witt scalar, and unit-root Frobenius ratio.

**No:** it does not isolate one unit term in the terminating index formula. The exact Gross-Koblitz decomposition has at least two minimum-slope units for every interior r.

**No known theorem:** the cited Delaygue and Delaygue-Rivoal work gives congruences, valuations, integrality, and algebraicity criteria, not cross-prime residual decorrelation.

# 11. Final assessment

The positive theorem is

```text
−b_r = Mellin_r(u_p) mod p,
```

where u_p is the K3 slope-zero Frobenius function and equals alpha_x² on split ordinary fibers.

The obstruction is that u_p is not a fixed low-complexity trace function as p varies:

- it is defined on a p-dependent ordinary locus;
- its line is generally non-overconvergent;
- its supersingular boundary has linear complexity;
- Mellin transform does not preserve rank;
- the tame exponent is discrete;
- modulo p, u_p is exactly the existing Hasse-Witt polynomial A_p.

Thus the first-digit unit-root projector does not explain the observed Poisson cumulants, sub-Poisson Palm behavior, Sidon structure, or tiny ARD. It repackages the same residual coefficient vector.

The best new direction is narrower:

> Construct the exact Dwork unit-root Frobenius in the repository's normalization, compute its second and higher p-adic Mellin digits, and test whether those digits provide a bounded-dimensional marked state with cross-prime transversality. Keep the full fixed-complexity K3 Mellin complex as the global carrier; use the unit-root expansion as extra arithmetic data, not as a replacement sheaf.
