# Executive verdict

There is no general discrepancy theorem for an arbitrary prescribed word

```text
A(1), A(2), ..., A(N)
```

in a finite linear group. Expansion of the ambient Cayley graph controls a probability distribution obtained by averaging over words; it does not control one path. A prescribed path in an expander can deliberately avoid a target for a long time. Thus Bourgain--Gamburd, strong approximation, and product monodromy are structural inputs, not a deterministic-word discrepancy theorem.

For the Apéry word, however, there is a substantially closer existing framework than random-walk expansion:

> After a factorial gauge and adjoining the clock, the Apéry recurrence becomes one fixed algebraic skew-product over the integers. Its affine ratio coordinate is a triangular rational dynamical system of the type studied by Ostafe--Shparlinski, and its homogeneous form is a fixed three-variable polynomial system of the type treated in the finite-field pseudorandom-dynamics literature.

Concretely, put

$$
B_n=(n!)^3b_n.
$$

Then

$$
B_{n+1}=P(n)B_n-n^6B_{n-1},
\qquad
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5,
$$

and the non-autonomous recurrence is the orbit of the single map

$$
\Phi(s,[X:Y])=
\bigl(s+1,[P(s)X-s^6Y:X]\bigr)
$$

on $\mathbf A^1\times\mathbf P^1$. In the affine ratio $z=X/Y$ this is

$$
(s,z)\longmapsto
\left(s+1,P(s)-\frac{s^6}{z}\right).
$$

This is the most useful consequence of the universal polynomial schedule. It turns the question from “one mysterious varying matrix word” into “one explicit algebraic dynamical system with a clock.”

But the published triangular-rational theorems are not a black-box solution here. The natural Apéry map lies exactly on a boundary of their degree hypotheses, their standard convention $0^{-1}=0$ gives the wrong dynamics at an Apéry zero, and ordinary box discrepancy is too coarse to resolve a target of density $1/p$. The repository's continuant identities supply much of the missing algebra, so a specialized adaptation is realistic, but it still requires new exponential-sum work.

The answers to the three questions are therefore:

1. **Specific deterministic words:** yes, discrepancy and exponential-sum theorems exist when the word is generated by a fixed low-complexity algebraic dynamical system, especially autonomous polynomial/rational maps and certain triangular non-autonomous systems after a clock lift. The Ostafe--Shparlinski line is the closest match. There is no corresponding theorem for an arbitrary prescribed word in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)$.
2. **Universality across primes:** it helps greatly by producing a single integral skew-product, fixed block-continuant polynomials, and CRT-compatible reductions. It does **not** make a fixed step $A(r_0)$ a rational function of $p$, and ordinary Chebotarev only controls fixed block lengths or averages over starting parameters. The needed block length grows with $p$, which is exactly the repository's family-compatibility obstruction.
3. **Zaremba/continued fractions:** there is an exact algebraic connection. The Apéry ratio is a generalized continued fraction, and the gap polynomials are continuants/Jacobi determinants. But Zaremba technology averages exponentially many words from a finite bounded alphabet; Apéry supplies one word with polynomially varying partial denominators and non-unit partial numerators. Thus the connection is structurally useful but presently gives no discrepancy theorem.

My recommendation is to prioritize a **projective Ostafe--Shparlinski specialization** and a **homogeneous polynomial-system exponential-sum audit** before attempting Bourgain--Gamburd expansion or classical Zaremba methods.

# 1. The exact autonomous lift of the Apéry schedule

## 1.1 Factorial gauge

The Apéry recurrence is

$$
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
$$

For

$$
B_n=(n!)^3b_n
$$

one obtains the division-free recurrence

$$
\boxed{B_{n+1}=P(n)B_n-n^6B_{n-1}.}
\tag{1.1}
$$

For $0\le n<p$, the scalar $(n!)^3$ is a unit modulo $p$, so

$$
B_n\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b_n\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{1.2}
$$

Set

$$
T(s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(s)&-s^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Then

$$
\binom{B_{n+1}}{B_n}=T(n)\binom{B_n}{B_{n-1}},
\qquad
\det T(n)=n^6.
\tag{1.3}
$$

For $1\le n\le p-1$, $T(n)$ is invertible modulo $p$ and acts on $\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)$.

## 1.2 The clock lift

Introduce the clock as a state coordinate and define

$$
\boxed{
\Phi(s,[X:Y])=
\bigl(s+1,[P(s)X-s^6Y:X]\bigr).
}
\tag{1.4}
$$

Then

$$
\Phi\bigl(n,[B_n:B_{n-1}]\bigr)
=igl(n+1,[B_{n+1}:B_n]\bigr).
$$

Thus the apparently non-autonomous word is the orbit of one map defined over $\mathbf Z$. The clock component also makes the first $p$ states distinct as points of the full skew-product space: before the clock wraps, two different times have different first coordinates.

There is a polynomial homogeneous model

$$
\boxed{
\Psi(s,X,Y)=igl(s+1,P(s)X-s^6Y,X\bigr),
}
\tag{1.5}
$$

and an affine ratio model, whenever $Y\ne0$,

$$
\boxed{
F(s,z)=\left(s+1,P(s)-\frac{s^6}{z}\right),
\qquad z=\frac XY.
}
\tag{1.6}
$$

The zero target is $z=0$. Consecutive-zero exclusion guarantees that at a zero $B_n=0$, the denominator $B_{n-1}$ is nonzero, so the target is represented honestly by $z_n=0$.

## 1.3 Exact iterates and continuants

Define the continuants

```text
U_{-1}(s)=0,
U_0(s)=1,
U_1(s)=P(s),
U_{m+1}(s)=P(s+m)U_m(s)-(s+m)^6U_{m-1}(s).
```

A direct induction gives

$$
T(s+m-1)\cdots T(s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
U_m(s)&-s^6U_{m-1}(s+1)\\
U_{m-1}(s)&-s^6U_{m-2}(s+1)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1.7}
$$

Consequently

$$
F^m(s,z)=
\left(
s+m,
\frac{U_m(s)z-s^6U_{m-1}(s+1)}
     {U_{m-1}(s)z-s^6U_{m-2}(s+1)}
\right).
\tag{1.8}
$$

The repository proves

$$
\deg_s U_m=3m
$$

and the positional-gap dictionary

$$
N_h(r)=U_{h-1}(r+1).
\tag{1.9}
$$

Thus the iterate complexity grows linearly, not exponentially. This exact slow-growth theorem is the main reason the finite-field pseudorandom-dynamics literature is relevant.

# 2. What deterministic-word discrepancy theorems actually exist?

## 2.1 An impossibility boundary

No theorem can control an arbitrary prescribed path merely from expansion of the ambient group. Let $S$ generate an expander Cayley graph of a finite group $G$. A random sequence of generators mixes rapidly, but a deterministic sequence may alternate $g,g^{-1}$, remain in a short cycle, or be chosen to postpone a specified vertex. Spectral expansion is a statement about the averaging operator

$$
\mu\longmapsto \mu*\nu,
$$

not about one atom in the support of $\nu^{*N}$.

The same distinction persists if every prefix product is distinct: expansion does not force every self-avoiding path to have small discrepancy. Therefore any useful deterministic theorem must exploit the algebraic form of the schedule, not only the group generated by its values.

## 2.2 The main existing frameworks

| Framework | What is averaged | Typical output | Fit to Apéry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bourgain--Gamburd / Bourgain--Varjú / Salehi Golsefidy--Varjú | Random words or convolution powers from a fixed finite generating set | Spectral gap and mixing on congruence quotients | Does not control the single Apéry schedule |
| Full subgroup-orbit exponential sums | All points $Hv$ for a subgroup $H<\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbf F_p)$ | Fourier decay if the orbit is large and not concentrated in subspaces | Prefixes of one word need not sample $Hv$ |
| Autonomous rational-map orbits | One orbit of one fixed rational function | Exponential sums, interval expansion, discrepancy for sufficiently long orbits | Relevant after adjoining the clock |
| Triangular polynomial/rational systems | One orbit of a fixed low-degree multivariate system; some work permits varying maps with shared structure | Power-saving exponential sums and discrepancy from slow degree growth and shifted-iterate independence | Closest existing architecture |
| Explicit derandomizations such as small-bias sets or universal traversal sequences | A specially designed deterministic list | Guaranteed Fourier bias or graph coverage | Shows deterministic lists can work, but does not analyze this list |

Two distinctions are especially important.

First, Sarah Peluse's theorem on exponential sums over $H$-orbits in $\mathbf F_p^d$ averages over the entire subgroup orbit. It becomes useful only after proving that the Apéry prefixes sample that orbit. That sampling statement is the missing theorem, not a consequence of Peluse's result.

Second, the finite-field polynomial-dynamics papers do study **one deterministic orbit**. Their cancellation comes from algebraic independence of shifted iterates and Weil-type complete-sum estimates, not from random choice of generators. This is much closer to what is needed.

# 3. The closest match: triangular rational and polynomial pseudorandom dynamics

## 3.1 Natural match with the Ostafe--Shparlinski class

Ostafe--Shparlinski study triangular rational systems of the form

$$
F_1(X_1,X_2)=X_1^{e_1}G_1(X_2)+H_1(X_2),
\qquad
F_2(X_2)=gX_2^{e_2}+h,
$$

with $e_i\in\{-1,1\}$. The Apéry ratio map is exactly of this shape:

$$
X_1=z,\quad X_2=s,\quad e_1=-1,\quad
G_1(s)=-s^6,\quad H_1(s)=P(s),\quad F_2(s)=s+1.
\tag{3.1}
$$

This is not just a vague analogy. Their iterate formula for an $e_1=-1$ coordinate is a quotient of two continuant-like recurrences, precisely as in (1.8).

Their method needs two algebraic inputs:

1. polynomially bounded degree growth of $F^m$;
2. nonconstancy of differences of shifted iterates, so the resulting complete character sums are nontrivial.

The repository supplies strong Apéry-specific substitutes for both: exact degree $3m$, adjacent coprimality, renewal, and explicit transfer products.

## 3.2 Why the published rational theorem is not literally applicable

There are two exact mismatches.

### Boundary-degree resonance

For an inverse coordinate, the natural Ostafe--Shparlinski hypothesis takes $H_1$ to have leading degree $d$ and requires

$$
\deg G_1<2d.
$$

For Apéry,

$$
\deg H_1=\deg P=3,
\qquad
\deg G_1=6=2\deg H_1.
$$

So the Apéry map lies exactly on the excluded boundary, not in the interior of their stated hypothesis. The repository's exact continuant calculation shows that slow degree growth nevertheless survives, but their theorem cannot be quoted without replacing the general leading-term argument by an Apéry-specific one.

### Wrong pole convention

The rational-dynamics paper uses the standard pseudorandom-generator convention

$$
0^{-1}=0.
$$

That convention changes the Apéry dynamics at the event being studied. In the genuine projective recurrence,

$$
z_n=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z_{n+1}=\infty,
$$

and then the following step returns from infinity. Under $0^{-1}=0$, the first of these steps is instead assigned the finite value $P(n)$. Thus one must redo the argument on $\mathbf P^1$, or work with the homogeneous polynomial system (1.5). Treating the convention as harmless would erase the zero event.

## 3.3 The homogeneous polynomial route

The map

$$
\Psi(s,X,Y)=(s+1,P(s)X-s^6Y,X)
$$

is a fixed polynomial system over $\mathbf Z$. Ostafe--Pelican--Shparlinski prove exponential-sum and discrepancy bounds for essentially arbitrary multivariate polynomial systems when the orbit is long enough, and Ostafe--Shparlinski's earlier work permits structured sequences involving distinct polynomial maps.

This makes the homogeneous model a serious candidate. It avoids the pole and preserves the exact zero equation $X=0$.

However, a source-level audit is still needed before claiming any numerical exponent:

- the published theorem's orbit-length and periodicity hypotheses must be checked for the distinguished Apéry state;
- the map is skew-linear in $(X,Y)$ rather than triangular in all three variables;
- the general theorem's error may be nontrivial for ordinary box discrepancy yet too large at the one-point scale;
- the clock wraps at $s=0$, where the determinant of the two-dimensional block degenerates;
- reflection creates forced correlations that must be removed from multi-point statements.

The clock is favorable in one respect: on $1\le s\le p-1$, different times give different full states because their clock coordinates differ. Thus there is no early collision in the lifted three-dimensional orbit.

## 3.4 The exact Fourier target

Ordinary star discrepancy is not the right endpoint. To count zeros, use additive orthogonality directly. For $1\le N\le p-2$, define

$$
S_p(t;N)=\sum_{n=1}^N e_p(tB_n),
\qquad t\in\mathbf F_p.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\#\{1\le n\le N:b_n\equiv0\pmod p\}
=\frac Np+rac1p\sum_{t\ne0}S_p(t;N).
}
\tag{3.2}
$$

Hence a uniform estimate

$$
\max_{t\ne0}|S_p(t;p-2)|\le E(p)
$$

implies

$$
Z(p)\le1+E(p).
\tag{3.3}
$$

To improve the repository's pointwise $p^{2/3}$ scale, it is enough to prove

$$
E(p)\ll p^{2/3-\delta}
$$

for some fixed $\delta>0$. A square-root estimate would give $Z(p)\ll p^{1/2+o(1)}$, but even that would not force a hit for every prime, because the expected main term is only about one.

This is a clean test for the polynomial-dynamics literature: not “does it prove some discrepancy?” but “does its specialized exponent make (3.3) beat $p^{2/3}$?”

## 3.5 Why shifted-iterate methods align with the repository

The standard deterministic-orbit argument averages a sum over short time shifts, applies Hölder or van der Corput, and reduces to complete sums involving differences of iterates

$$
F^k-F^\ell.
$$

For Apéry those iterates are controlled by the continuants $U_m(s)$. The repository's renewal identity and adjacent coprimality are precisely the algebraic information needed to prove that the shifted phases are not constant or Artin--Schreier-degenerate.

This suggests a realistic theorem-development sequence:

1. prove a projective iterate formula with all pole divisors explicit;
2. classify when a linear combination of two shifted Apéry coordinate functions can be constant or of Artin--Schreier form;
3. bound the conductor/degree of the resulting complete sums polynomially in the shift length;
4. run the Ostafe--Shparlinski shift amplifier and optimize the shift length;
5. compare the resulting exponent with $2/3$ and with the repository's `GAP--2D--SQRT` target.

This route uses the deterministic schedule itself. It does not require pretending that the word is random.

# 4. What the universal $r$-dependence does and does not buy

## 4.1 A fixed step is not a rational function of $p$

For fixed $r_0$, the matrix $A(r_0)$ is one fixed rational matrix. The prime $p$ merely selects its reduction. Likewise, the finite prefix

$$
A(r_0-1)\cdots A(1)
$$

is a fixed rational matrix, and its marked coordinate is the fixed integer or rational number $b_{r_0}$ after clearing denominators.

Therefore

$$
p\mid b_{r_0}
$$

means that $p$ belongs to the finite set of prime divisors of one fixed nonzero integer. Chebotarev is unnecessary and does not reveal a density law for the orbit hit.

Chebotarev can describe such fixed-matrix questions as whether the characteristic polynomial of $A(r_0)$ splits modulo $p$, or whether a fixed algebraic eigenvalue has a specified Frobenius class. But the target condition depends on a distinguished initial vector and covector, not merely the conjugacy class of $A(r_0)$.

## 4.2 The useful family is a fixed block with variable starting point

For a fixed block length $h$, the product

$$
T(s+h-1)\cdots T(s)
$$

has entries given by fixed polynomials $U_h(s)$ and their shifts. This is a genuine algebraic family over the $s$-line.

For fixed $h$, one may use:

- Weil/Deligne bounds as $s$ varies in $\mathbf F_p$;
- function-field monodromy and Chebotarev for a fixed cover;
- number-field Chebotarev as $p$ varies for the splitting of a fixed polynomial $U_h$.

This is exactly what the repository's fixed-$H$ Chebotarev/Burnside proposition does. It also explains the explicit $h=2$ splitting law

$$
R_2^{\rm alg}(p)=1+2\mathbf 1_{\left(\frac{-51}{p}\right)=1}.
$$

The universal schedule therefore gives excellent **annealed** information over starting points or over primes for each fixed block.

## 4.3 The quantifier wall is the growing block length

The desired quenched estimates require $h$ to grow with $p$. But

$$
\deg U_h=3h,
$$

and the discriminant, splitting field, conductor, genus, and exceptional-prime set all vary with $h$. Standard Chebotarev fixes the extension before letting $p$ tend to infinity. It does not provide uniformity for the diagonal family $h=h(p)$ at the required scale.

This is precisely the warning already recorded in [`FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex`](../problems/3.2/FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex): fixed-$H$ prime averages do not control gaps of size $\sqrt p$ or $p$ at one prime.

Thus universality helps by exposing the moving family; it does not eliminate its complexity.

## 4.4 Why strong approximation is not yet available off the shelf

The factorial-gauge matrix is integral but has

$$
\det T(n)=n^6.
$$

To normalize it to determinant one, one divides by $n^3$, introducing denominators supported on the prime factors of $n$. As $n$ varies, there is no fixed finite set $S$ for which every normalized step lies in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z[1/S])$.

Moreover, the schedule uses infinitely many different matrices. The standard super-strong-approximation theorems start with a **fixed finite symmetric generating set** in a fixed $S$-arithmetic group. Therefore one cannot simply cite Bourgain--Varjú or Salehi Golsefidy--Varjú for the family $\{T(n)\}$.

A strong-approximation route first needs one of the following new reductions:

1. **clock compression:** express all relevant block matrices as bounded or logarithmic-length words in a fixed finite $S$-integral generating set;
2. **finite block generators:** identify finitely many block specializations whose action reproduces the distinguished schedule with controlled error;
3. **semigroup/correspondence expansion:** develop expansion directly for the algebraic family $T(s)$ rather than a finitely generated group;
4. **bypass groups:** prove the needed character-sum estimates directly from the skew-product geometry.

At present, option 4 is the most concrete.

## 4.5 The shared clock can help or hurt

At the same absolute index $n<\min(p,q)$,

$$
p\mid b_n\ \text{and}\ q\mid b_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
pq\mid b_n.
$$

This is maximal arithmetic coupling, not independence.

In the repository's CRT-row problem, a common integer $m$ uses different local indices

$$
r_p=m\bmod p,
\qquad
r_q=m\bmod q.
$$

The base clocks are independent over a complete period modulo $pq$ by CRT. But the projective states at $r_p$ and $r_q$ are accumulated products of all previous local matrices. Independence of the clock coordinates does not imply independence of those accumulated states.

The correct two-prime Fourier object is of the form

$$
\sum_{m<M}
 e_p\bigl(t\widetilde B_p(m\bmod p)\bigr)
 e_q\bigl(u\widetilde B_q(m\bmod q)\bigr),
\tag{4.1}
$$

where

$$
\widetilde B_p(r)=(r!)^3b_r\pmod p.
$$

Expanding two zero indicators by additive orthogonality reduces pair independence to cancellation in (4.1). Higher pair--Palm moments require the analogous fixed-$k$ mixed sums, not just $k=2$. The exact repository target is recorded in [`problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex).

# 5. The continued-fraction connection is exact

## 5.1 Generalized continued fraction

Let

$$
z_n=\frac{B_n}{B_{n-1}}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{z_{n+1}=P(n)-\frac{n^6}{z_n}.}
\tag{5.1}
$$

Since $z_1=5$, iteration gives

$$
z_n=P(n-1)-\cfrac{(n-1)^6}
 {P(n-2)-\cfrac{(n-2)^6}
  {\ddots-\cfrac{1^6}{5}}}.
\tag{5.2}
$$

This is a finite generalized continued fraction, or $J$-fraction. Its transfer step factors as

$$
\begin{pmatrix}P(n)&-n^6\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&P(n)\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}0&-n^6\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.3}
$$

The second factor is projectively a conjugate of the standard inversion matrix by $\operatorname{diag}(n^3,1)$. Thus each step is “inversion, scale by $n^6$, then translate by $P(n)$.”

The polynomials $U_m(s)$ are exactly the associated continuants; equivalently, they are determinants of tridiagonal Jacobi matrices. The repository's reflection, renewal, adjacent-coprimality, and root-strip results are continued-fraction/continuant phenomena in this precise sense.

## 5.2 Why classical Zaremba technology does not transfer

Classical Zaremba asks for finite regular continued fractions with partial quotients from a fixed bounded alphabet. Bourgain--Kontorovich form a thin semigroup generated by finitely many matrices and average over exponentially many words. The proof obtains its cancellation from that large word ensemble and a bilinear/circle-method decomposition.

Apéry differs in every relevant feature:

- there is one prescribed word rather than exponentially many words;
- the translations $P(n)$ vary cubically and are not drawn from a finite alphabet;
- the generalized partial numerators are $-n^6$, not $1$;
- the integral matrices have varying determinant $n^6$ rather than determinant $\pm1$;
- the target is an exact zero modulo the defining prime, not representation of denominators by an ensemble.

Therefore Zaremba's expansion technology cannot be imported merely because both problems use $2\times2$ continuant matrices.

There is a possible **methodological** analogy: if one could enlarge the single Apéry word into a controlled ensemble of shifted blocks and prove that the distinguished word is typical within it, then bilinear decompositions and local-global analysis might become relevant. But constructing that ensemble and proving typicality is again the deterministic-word problem.

## 5.3 Function-field continued fractions

Polynomial analogues of Zaremba study ordinary continued fractions in $\mathbf F_q(T)$ with bounded degrees of partial quotients. In the Apéry generalized fraction, each local denominator $P(s+j)$ has degree $3$ in $s$ and each local numerator $(s+j)^6$ has degree $6$, so there is a superficial bounded-degree resemblance.

But standard function-field continued fractions have unit partial numerators after normalization. Converting the Apéry $J$-fraction to that form requires successive equivalence scalings that accumulate products of $(s+j)^3$; the resulting partial quotients no longer belong to a fixed bounded alphabet in the usual sense. The known polynomial Zaremba and McMullen theorems concern existence and bounded-degree expansions, not discrepancy of one prescribed generalized fraction modulo varying primes.

Thus function-field continued fractions are useful for algebraic identities and perhaps for Galois questions about the continuants, but they do not currently provide the needed hitting estimate.

# 6. A concrete Apéry-specific discrepancy program

## 6.1 First target: a specialized shifted-iterate theorem

The smallest meaningful new theorem is not full product expansion. It is:

> **Apéry shifted-iterate nondegeneracy.** For shifts $0\le k<\ell\le K$, classify every linear combination of coordinate functions of $\Psi^k$ and $\Psi^\ell$ that is constant or Artin--Schreier-degenerate over $\mathbf F_p$, with degree/conductor bounded polynomially in $K$.

The exact transfer formula (1.7), adjacent coprimality, and renewal identity make this a finite algebraic problem. Reflection and the forced midpoint strata must be listed explicitly rather than hidden in an exceptional $O(1)$.

## 6.2 Second target: optimize a deterministic shift amplifier

Apply a high-moment van der Corput/Ostafe--Shparlinski argument to

$$
S_p(t;N)=\sum_{n\le N}e_p(tB_n).
$$

The immediate benchmark is:

$$
\max_{t\ne0}|S_p(t;p-2)|\ll p^{2/3-\delta}.
\tag{D1}
$$

Any fixed $\delta>0$ in (D1) improves the current pointwise zero exponent through (3.2). The aspirational form is square-root cancellation.

A more repository-aligned first theorem may be the two-parameter phase already isolated as `GAP--2D--SQRT`:

$$
\sum_{1\le h\le H}
\sum_r e_p\bigl(t\det(v_r,v_{r+h})\bigr).
$$

The shift amplifier naturally produces block-transfer continuants, so the two-parameter gap statistic may be easier than the direct scalar sequence $B_n$.

## 6.3 Third target: an adelic large sieve, not pointwise pair mixing

For distinct primes $p_1,\ldots,p_k$, expand each zero indicator by additive characters. The desired $k$-prime count becomes a sum of mixed modes over a common row interval. The target should be an aggregate large-sieve estimate over the primes and nonzero frequency vectors, uniform for fixed $k$.

For the present paper architecture, stopping at pairwise independence is insufficient. [`pairpalm_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex) shows that $(\mathrm{HM})_7$ is equivalent to controlling the fifth factorial extension above an actual two-prime CRT locus. Thus the eventual deterministic-word theorem must be stable under conditioning and fixed products of up to seven prime factors, or prove the exact pair--Palm positive-excess estimate directly.

## 6.4 What to compute first

A bounded experimental/theoretical audit should do the following.

1. Express every coordinate of $\Psi^k$ in terms of $U_k$ and its shifts.
2. Verify the precise hypotheses of the Ostafe--Pelican--Shparlinski arbitrary-polynomial-system theorem for $\Psi$.
3. Compute the exponent that theorem actually yields at $N=p+O(1)$ in dimension three.
4. If it does not beat $2/3$, redo the proof using the linear degree growth and the special two-dimensional linear block rather than the generic theorem.
5. Prove a projective version that treats $0$ and $\infty$ correctly.
6. Test whether the same correlation classification survives over products $\mathbf F_p\times\mathbf F_q$ and under pair--Palm conditioning.

This is a well-defined project with a decisive pass/fail criterion. It is much more concrete than “apply expansion to the non-autonomous word.”

# 7. Final rulings on the proposed avenues

## Question (1): one prescribed word

**Ruling:** no general finite-linear-group discrepancy theorem controls one arbitrary prescribed word. The closest applicable results are deterministic algebraic-orbit exponential-sum theorems. The Apéry clock lift places the problem near the Ostafe--Shparlinski triangular rational and multivariate polynomial frameworks. A specialized adaptation is genuinely promising, but the natural map is a boundary case and must be projectivized.

## Question (2): universal dependence on $r$

**Ruling:** universality helps through the single integral skew-product, fixed block polynomials, and CRT compatibility. It does not turn $A(r_0)$ into a function of $p$. Fixed-$r_0$ divisibility is merely divisibility of one fixed Apéry integer. Chebotarev is useful only after introducing a genuine varying algebraic parameter, such as the starting point $s$ for a fixed block length. The required growing block length defeats ordinary fixed-cover Chebotarev.

## Question (3): Zaremba and rational-function continued fractions

**Ruling:** the Apéry transfer is exactly a generalized continued fraction and its gap polynomials are continuants. This explains many existing algebraic identities and may help with irreducibility, root geometry, and block factorization. Classical and function-field Zaremba theorems do not give discrepancy for this one polynomially varying generalized word because they depend on bounded alphabets and large word ensembles.

# Bottom line

The most promising corrected attack is

```text
Apéry clock lift
+ exact continuant degree and nondegeneracy
+ deterministic shifted-iterate exponential sums
+ projective treatment of 0 and infinity
+ squarefree-modulus large sieve / pair-Palm extension.
```
