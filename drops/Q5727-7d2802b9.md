# 8. Sharp no-go theorem

## Theorem

Fix a good prime \(p\) and a free local recurrence-state module \(V\) of finite rank. Let \(	au_p\in V^ee\) be primitive. Let \(\mathcal F\) be any bounded family formed from:

- short Newton placements whose stencils contain \(p-1\);
- target-blind integer weights;
- integer linear combinations;
- recurrence shifts which are themselves required to preserve the target;
- polynomial combinations of degree \(D<p\).

Assume every member of \(\mathcal F\) vanishes modulo \(p\) on every state satisfying \(	au_p=0\). Then:

1. every linear member lies in \(R_p	au_p+pV^ee\);
2. every polynomial member lies in the principal ideal \((	au_p)\) modulo \(p\);
3. every linear observation matrix has mod-\(p\) rank at most one;
4. every \(k	imes k\) linear minor is divisible by \(p^{k-1}\);
5. every nonlinear Jacobian minor of order at least two vanishes on the target locus;
6. no coefficient, observability, Jacobian, or exterior determinant of order at least two can be a \(p\)-unit while all its defining evaluated rows retain \(p\).

For two endpoints, all conclusions hold simultaneously, and every \(k	imes k\) linear minor is divisible by \((q\ell)^{k-1}\).

### Proof

The linear statements are (2.2), the rank-one reduction, and the Smith determinantal-divisor criterion. The nonlinear statement is divisibility by the principal hyperplane equation (6.1), followed by differentiation (6.3). The two-prime statement follows by applying the one-prime theorem separately and using coprimality. \(\square\)

# 9. What could still work

The no-go leaves only genuinely arithmetic exits.

1. **A second target equation.** Prove that the actual distinguished rank-\(ho\) shell state lies in a proper subvariety of the formal target hyperplane, so the target ideal is larger than \((	au_p)\). This is precisely a non-alias observability theorem.
2. **A \(p^2\) lift for coefficient-forced rows.** If a row has coefficients divisible by \(p\), one needs the target to force the evaluated row to be divisible by \(p^2\). Only then does division by \(p\) yield a new mod-\(p\) equation.
3. **A global many-prime identity.** It must amortize all endpoint factors in one target-blind integer of total height \(o(H)\), rather than assigning a determinant to each realized pair.
4. **A nonpolynomial operation of degree comparable to \(p\).** Finite-field functions such as \(x^p-x\) evade the bounded-degree hyperplane argument, but their degree and coefficient height are incompatible with the required uniform block ledger unless a new low-height structure is proved.

Absent one of these, different Newton placements only rearrange the same unsaturated alias scalar.

# Final assessment

A bounded family of different target-blind Newton placements **can** make an endpoint coefficient-forced in one row and alias-forced in another.
