# Q7230 rc4: bridge cutoff optimization audit

## Conclusion

The currently proved inequalities do not reach
\[
\sum_{X<p\le 2X}E_p^{\rm sep}\ll X^2/\log X.
\]
Allowing a nonuniform cutoff \(K=K(s,p)\), dyadic decomposition in \(s,G\), and interpolation between the existing bounds does not improve the exponent. The optimization problem has a hard barrier: the best combination of the available estimates remains at least \(X^{5/2-o(1)}/\log X\) in the far regime.

The near-bridge estimate is already at the target scale only for bridges of length \(K\asymp \sqrt{X}/\sqrt{\log X}\); it cannot be extended to the full separated energy with the existing inputs.

## Available inequalities

The relevant inputs are:

1. Resultant-height bound. Summing all possible gap pairs pays the algebraic height:
\[
\sum_{p,s,G}\#\{x:N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\}
\ll X^3/\log X.
\]
This corresponds to average mass about \(X^{1/2}\) per labelled \((p,s,G)\) cell.

2. Fiber-cap estimate. For a fixed state and gap,
\[
\#\{x:\pi_x=\pi_{x+h}\}\le 3(h-1).
\]
After summing over \(s,G\), this gives the same cubic-scale obstruction because the number of gap labels is too large.

3. Full fiber-square energy:
\[
\sum_a |\pi^{-1}(a)|^2\ll p^{3/2}.
\]
This controls collisions after forgetting the bridge labels, but does not distinguish the additional long-gap condition.

4. Short-chain/near energy:
\[
\sum_p E_p^{\rm near}(K)\ll H^2K^2,
\]
with \(H\asymp\sqrt X\). Choosing
\[
K=H/\sqrt{\log X}
\]
gives \(X^2/\log X\).

## Dyadic optimization

Let
\[
s\asymp S,\qquad G\asymp L.
\]
The number of cells is
\[
\#(p,S,L)\asymp \frac{X}{\log X}\,S\,L.
\]
The target would require total conditional mass
\[
O(X^2/\log X),
\]
so the average contribution per cell must be
\[
O((SL)^{-1}).
\]

The cap estimate supplies at best a factor comparable to \(S\), while the energy estimate supplies a square-root saving after summing states. Neither supplies the missing factor \(L^{-1}\) for the long bridge range \(L>K\).

Interpolating the two bounds gives a family of the form
\[
M(S,L)\ll S^\theta (X^{1/2}S^{1/2})^{1-\theta},
\]
for \(0\le\theta\le1\). Summing over \(L\) leaves
\[
\sum_L M(S,L)\gg X^{1/2+o(1)}S^{1+o(1)}
\]
in the relevant range. Summing over \(S\le X^{1/2}\) gives
\[
\gg X^{5/2-o(1)},
\]
not the desired \(X^2/\log X\).

## Nonuniform cutoff

Take a variable cutoff \(K(S,p)\). Splitting at \(G\le K(S,p)\) gives

- short bridges: bounded by the near-chain energy;
- long bridges: bounded by resultant/fiber estimates.

The short contribution is minimized by making \(K\) small, but then the long contribution contains nearly all bridges. Making \(K\) large reduces the long contribution only linearly while increasing the near-energy term quadratically. The balance equation is
\[
K^2\approx H^2/\log X,
\]
which recovers
\[
K\asymp H/\sqrt{\log X}.
\]
Therefore a nonuniform choice cannot beat the existing square-root logarithmic cutoff unless a new estimate couples \(G\) with the Apéry orbit structure.

## Linear-program obstruction

Treat each dyadic box as a variable. The constraints are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{input}&\text{saving}\
\hline
\text{resultant height}&X^3/\log X\\
\text{cap}&\text{removes only fixed-gap multiplicity}\\
\text{energy}&p^{3/2}\text{ after forgetting }G\\
\text{short-chain}&X^2/\log X\text{ only for }G\le K
\end{array}
\]

The dual obstruction is the family of boxes
\[
S\asymp X^{1/2},\qquad G\asymp X,
\]
where the long-gap parameter survives. Every current inequality either:

* pays the number of possible long bridges, or
* discards the bridge coordinate and pays full fiber energy.

Neither controls both simultaneously.

## Relation to AVENUES.md

The negative conclusion matches the current project direction: arithmetic intersection-height compression and generic large-sieve/product-formula approaches were already found insufficient without an Apéry-specific cross-gap identity. The missing ingredient is not a better interpolation of current estimates but a new theorem producing cancellation in the actual \(\mathbf F_p\)-root events.

## Final answer

No rigorous improved decomposition exists from the present inequalities. The optimal exponent forced by the available estimates is above the target:
\[
\boxed{\sum_{X<p\le2X}E_p^{\rm sep}\not\ll X^2/\log X\text{ from current inputs alone}.}
\]
A successful proof needs genuinely new transverse information: for example a cross-prime large sieve for the Apéry transfer orbit, a Frobenius-block theorem controlling long returns, or an arithmetic identity linking \(N_s(x)\) and \(N_G(x+s)\) beyond degree/resultant data.
