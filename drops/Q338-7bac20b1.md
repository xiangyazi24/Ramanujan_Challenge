# Q338 — Elementary approaches to the Sparse Bridge Theorem for Apéry zero-pairs

## Executive verdict

There is no deduction of the Sparse Bridge Theorem from the presently proved Lucas, gap-polynomial, endpoint, and sieve inputs alone. The five proposed directions have sharply different status.

| Route | Verdict | Exact obstruction or missing input |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Lucas digits | **No saving in the relevant window.** | Every index \(r<p\) has one base-\(p\) digit, so Lucas is tautological there. Multi-digit amplification creates zero cylinders but does not distinguish one zero digit from two zero digits without a uniform \(p^2\)-level valuation law. |
| (b) Gap-polynomial coprimality | **Useful for heavy tails, but not Poisson scale.** | It converts three zeros into a common root of two coprime gap polynomials and hence into resultant valuation. The aggregate height budget is still \(H^4\), reproducing the \(3/5\) wall rather than a bounded second factorial moment. |
| (c) Endpoint swap | **No independent equation.** | It is the matrix form of reflection. The mirrored gap root and the central mirror return are algebraically forced by \(N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x)\). |
| (d) Horizontal averaging | **The best genuinely elementary candidate.** | After switching order, the theorem becomes a large-prime gcd/Titchmarsh-divisor problem for \(\gcd(b_r,N_h(r))\). Standard Bombieri–Vinogradov does not apply; a new two-parameter dispersion theorem is needed. |
| (e) Sieve theory | **A framework, not the missing theorem.** | A Selberg/Gallagher argument would require precisely the joint local density and level of distribution that are currently unknown. The larger sieve has no auxiliary-prime residue restriction to exploit. |

The most concrete recommendation is therefore:

> **Do not spend further effort trying to extract an independent constraint from Lucas or the endpoint swap.** Develop the horizontal large-prime gcd formulation below. In parallel, the gap-resultant machinery can continue to control the high-\(K_p\) tail, but it does not by itself prove the Sparse Bridge Theorem.

There is also one correction to the premise. In the current project normalization,

\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1),
\]

not approximately \((2/3)h\). The exponent \(2/3\) in the uniform estimate \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\) comes from optimizing

\[
\frac pH+\sum_{h\le H}\deg N_h\ll \frac pH+H^2,
\]

not from a degree-\((2/3)h\) gap polynomial and not from pairwise coprimality. Pairwise coprimality becomes relevant when two different return gaps occur simultaneously.

## 1. Exact setup and the fundamental divisor switch

For a prime \(p\ge 7\), write

\[
\mathcal H_p:=\left\{r:1\le r<\frac{p-1}{2},\ b_r\equiv0\pmod p\right\},
\qquad K_p:=|\mathcal H_p|.
\]

The desired dyadic form of the Sparse Bridge Theorem is

\[
\mathscr S(P):=\sum_{P<p\le2P}(K_p)_2\ll \frac P{\log P}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Dyadic summation is equivalent to the stated global bound

\[
\sum_{p\le x}(K_p)_2\ll\pi(x).
\]

Use the integral gap-polynomial normalization

\[
N_1(X)=1,\qquad N_2(X)=P(X+1),
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(X)=P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X),
\]

where \(P(t)=(2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5)\). Put

\[
D_h(X):=\prod_{j=2}^{h}(X+j)^3.
\]

The exact zero-return congruence proved in the project is:

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D_h(r)b_{r+h}\equiv N_h(r)b_{r+1}\pmod p.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The no-consecutive-zeros lemma says that \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\) implies \(b_{r+1}\not\equiv0\pmod p\) whenever \(0\le r\le p-2\).

### Proposition 1.1 — exact pair/gap equivalence

If

\[
0<r<r+h<\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r,\ b_{r+h}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p\mid b_r,\ N_h(r).
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

#### Proof

Every factor \(r+j\), \(2\le j\le h\), is strictly between \(0\) and \(p\), so \(D_h(r)\) is a unit modulo \(p\). If \(p\mid b_r\), then \(b_{r+1}\) is also a unit modulo \(p\). The equivalence follows immediately from (1.2). ∎

This gives an exact horizontal order switch. Since \((K_p)_2\) counts ordered pairs,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr S(P)
&=2\sum_{P<p\le2P}
  \sum_{1\le r<s<(p-1)/2}
  \mathbf 1_{p\mid b_r}\mathbf 1_{p\mid b_s}\\
&=2\sum_{\substack{r\ge1,\ h\ge2\\r+h<P}}
 \#\left\{
 p\in(P,2P]:
 2(r+h)<p-1,\ p\mid b_r,\ p\mid N_h(r)
 \right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Define, for a set of primes \(\mathcal P\),

\[
\operatorname{rad}_{\mathcal P}(m)
 :=\prod_{\substack{q\in\mathcal P\\q\mid m}}q.
\]

The logarithmically weighted form is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(K_p)_2\log p
=2\sum_{\substack{r\ge1,\ h\ge2\\r+h<P}}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{\mathcal P(P;r,h)}
  \bigl(\gcd(b_r,N_h(r))\bigr),
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1.5}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal P(P;r,h)
=\{p\in(P,2P]:2(r+h)<p-1\}.
\]

Because \(\log p\asymp\log P\) on the dyadic interval, (1.1) is equivalent to the assertion that the right side of (1.5) is \(O(P)\).

This identity is the cleanest elementary reformulation of the Sparse Bridge Theorem. It will be used repeatedly below.

A pure size estimate is nowhere near sufficient. The project has

\[
\log b_r\asymp r
\]

and, from \(\deg N_h=3(h-1)\) and \(\log\|N_h\|_\infty\ll h\),

\[
\log|N_h(r)|\ll h\log(r+h).
\]

Thus

\[
\log\gcd(b_r,N_h(r))
\le \min\{O(r),O(h\log(r+h))\},
\]

and summing this trivial bound over the \(O(P^2)\) pairs \((r,h)\) gives a quantity of order at most \(P^3\), whereas (1.5) requires order \(P\). The missing saving is genuinely two-dimensional.

## 2. Route (a): Lucas digits

### 2.1 Why Lucas is tautological for the Sparse Bridge window

The Apéry Lucas congruence is

\[
b_n\equiv\prod_i b_{n_i}\pmod p,
\qquad n=\sum_i n_ip^i.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For every \(r<(p-1)/2<p\), the base-\(p\) expansion of \(r\) consists of the single digit \(r\). Equation (2.1) then reads

\[
b_r\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\]

Consequently Lucas imposes **no relation at all** between two distinct first-half indices \(r,s<p\). The statement “both \(r\) and \(s\) have a zero digit” is merely a restatement that the one digit \(r\), respectively \(s\), belongs to the unknown zero set.

### 2.2 Multi-digit amplification is exact but non-coercive

Let

\[
Z_p^{\rm dig}:=\{0\le a<p:p\mid b_a\},
\qquad z_p^{\rm dig}:=|Z_p^{\rm dig}|.
\]

For \(0\le n<p^k\), Lucas gives the exact count

\[
\#\{n<p^k:p\mid b_n\}
=p^k-(p-z_p^{\rm dig})^k.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus one zero digit propagates to an entire coordinate cylinder. This is amplification, not repulsion.

Restrict both digits to an alphabet \(A\subset[0,p)\) of size \(m\), and suppose exactly \(K\) digits in \(A\) are Apéry zeros. Then

\[
\#\{(u,v)\in A^2:p\mid b_{u+pv}\}
=m^2-(m-K)^2=2mK-K^2.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The term \(K^2\) is present, but modulo \(p\) there is no way to distinguish:

- a number with exactly one zero digit;
- a number with two zero digits.

Both simply satisfy \(p\mid b_n\). The count (2.3) is dominated by the linear cylinder term \(2mK\), so it does not give an upper bound for \(K^2\) without already controlling \(K\) at the desired scale.

### 2.3 What a Lucas route would actually require

One would need a uniform valuation statement of the form

\[
v_p(b_{u+pv})
\ge \mathbf 1_{p\mid b_u}+\mathbf 1_{p\mid b_v},
\tag{2.4}
\]

or at least a criterion that detects two zero digits by divisibility modulo \(p^2\). The known Lucas congruence modulo \(p^2\) for Apéry numbers is restricted to a special set of digits; it is not a uniform two-digit valuation-additivity theorem for all zero digits.

Even the hypothetical statement (2.4) would not by itself close the bridge. Charging all two-zero-digit pairs to \(p^2\mid b_{u+pv}\) and then using only the size of \(b_n\) loses several powers of \(P\), because the new indices are of size \(P^2\) and \(\log b_n\asymp n\).

### Verdict on (a)

Lucas is useful for propagating a zero set after it is known, and for block laws in the original GCD problem. It supplies no anti-correlation within the one-digit first half. A viable Lucas attack would first need a new uniform \(p^2\)-valuation theorem and then a second, nontrivial global incidence estimate. It is not presently an elementary route to the Sparse Bridge Theorem.

## 3. Route (b): sharpening gap-polynomial coprimality

### 3.1 What ordinary degree counting proves

For the projective collision count

\[
C_p(h):=\#\{r:N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]

in the regular window, nonvanishing modulo \(p\) and degree give

\[
C_p(h)\le3(h-1).
\tag{3.1}
\]

For successive zero gaps, the usual short/long decomposition gives

\[
z_p\ll \frac pH+H^2,
\]

and \(H=p^{1/3}\) gives \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\). Pairwise coprimality is not needed for this first bound.

### 3.2 The genuine joint condition is a zero triple

Suppose

\[
0<r<r+h<r+k<\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad h<k,
\]

and all three Apéry values vanish modulo \(p\). Proposition 1.1 gives

\[
N_h(r)\equiv N_k(r)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define

\[
M_p(h,k):=\#\{x\in\mathbf F_p:N_h(x)=N_k(x)=0\}.
\]

Since \(\gcd_{\mathbf Q[X]}(N_h,N_k)=1\) for \(h\ne k\), the integer resultant

\[
R_{h,k}:=\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_k)
\]

is nonzero. The Sylvester-matrix corank/valuation lemma used in Q326 gives

\[
\boxed{M_p(h,k)\le v_p(R_{h,k}).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus coprimality is naturally a **triple-return tool**.

This is relevant because, for every integer \(K\ge0\),

\[
(K)_2\le 2+(K)_3.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Indeed the only exceptional case is \(K=2\), for which \((K)_2=2\); for \(K\ge3\), \((K)_3=(K)_2(K-2)\ge(K)_2\). Therefore the Sparse Bridge Theorem would follow from the stronger zero-triple estimate

\[
\sum_{p\le x}(K_p)_3\ll\pi(x).
\tag{3.5}
\]

The gap-resultant framework is exactly adapted to (3.5).

### 3.3 Why the present resultant budget still stops at \(3/5\)

The height bound has the form

\[
\log|R_{h,k}|\ll hk\log(2hk),
\tag{3.6}
\]

with an even sharper linear-in-\(hk\) main term available in the current project. Therefore, for \(H<P\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{P<p\le2P}
 \sum_{2\le h<k\le H}M_p(h,k)
&\le \frac1{\log P}
 \sum_{2\le h<k\le H}\log|R_{h,k}|\\
&\ll \frac{H^4\log(2H)}{\log P}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
\]

The exponent \(H^4\) is decisive.

To see the resulting barrier, partition the first-half interval into

\[
J\asymp P/H
\]

blocks of length \(H\). If a prime has \(K_p=T\) first-half zeros and \(T\gg J\), convexity gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\binom{k_j}{3}
\gg \frac{T^3}{J^2}
\gg \frac{T^3H^2}{P^2},
\tag{3.8}
\]

where \(k_j\) is the number of zeros in block \(j\). Every triple inside one block yields a common root (3.2) with \(h,k\le H\).

Choose the smallest useful scale \(H\asymp P/T\). Then a prime at level \(T\) creates \(\gg T\) short triple certificates, whereas the total horizontal resultant budget is

\[
H^4\asymp \frac{P^4}{T^4}.
\]

Consequently the level-set estimate furnished by this mechanism is only

\[
\#\{p\in(P,2P]:K_p\asymp T\}
\ll \frac{P^4}{T^5},
\tag{3.9}
\]

up to logarithmic factors. This first beats the trivial prime count only when

\[
T\gtrsim P^{3/5}.
\]

This is the same \(3/5\) wall that appears in the adjacent-gap shifted-resultant argument of Q326. Using all anchored triples rather than only consecutive triples changes constants and presentation, not the fundamental exponent.

For the desired second moment, the level-\(T\) contribution allowed by (3.9) is

\[
T^2\cdot\frac{P^4}{T^5}
=\frac{P^4}{T^3},
\]

which is far larger than \(P/\log P\) throughout the moderate-\(T\) range.

### 3.4 Why coprimality alone cannot do more

Coprimality over \(\mathbf Q[X]\) does not say that the reductions of \(N_h\) and \(N_k\) have no common root modulo \(p\). It says that every such common root consumes a prime divisor, and more precisely a unit of \(p\)-adic valuation, of \(R_{h,k}\). A single prime may divide many different resultants, and the aggregate logarithmic height of the growing family is the limiting resource.

Moreover, \(N_h(r)=0\) is a universal projective return. A zero-pair needs the additional distinguished-fibre condition \(b_r=0\). Pure gap algebra sees only one of the two random codimensions. Any sharpening that continues to ignore the anchor condition \(b_r=0\) necessarily counts many returns in other projective fibres.

The project already contains a useful negative test: the reflected extremal configurations described in `AVENUES.md`/`pair_cap_extremal.tex` obey reflection, no adjacency, and all individual bounds \(C_h\le3(h-1)\), yet have large off-center energy. Thus reflection plus the separate degree caps cannot imply the Sparse Bridge Theorem as a set-theoretic statement.

### What would be enough

A genuinely stronger algebraic theorem would have to compress the whole two-gap family, for example a distinguished-fibre estimate of the shape

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}
\sum_{h<k\le H}
\#\{r:p\mid b_r,N_h(r),N_k(r)\}
\ll \frac P{\log P}+H^{2+o(1)},
\tag{3.10}
\]

or another estimate strong enough to sum the zero-triple factorial moment. Pairwise coprimality and separate resultant heights do not supply such compression.

### Verdict on (b)

This is a valid tail-control mechanism and remains useful. It can be sharpened from adjacent triples to all anchored triples, and the inequality (3.4) explains why a Poisson-scale zero-triple theorem would suffice. But the present \(H^4\) resultant budget stops at \(P^{3/5}\); reaching the Sparse Bridge Theorem requires a new aggregate identity, a distinguished-fibre input, or cancellation across the gap parameters.

## 4. Route (c): the endpoint swap matrix

Work in any of the projectively equivalent companion-matrix normalizations. The claimed full product identity is the matrix form of the already-proved reflection symmetry

\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The gap polynomials themselves satisfy the exact reflection identity

\[
\boxed{N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### 4.1 The mirrored pair is the same polynomial root

If \(r,r+h\) is a zero-pair, its reflected pair begins at

\[
r^*:=p-1-r-h.
\]

Modulo \(p\),

\[
r^*\equiv-r-h-1.
\]

Thus (4.2) gives

\[
N_h(r)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N_h(r^*)=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The endpoint identity has not produced a second polynomial equation; it has merely supplied the involutive partner of the original root.

### 4.2 The central mirror gap is a forced root

Take a zero \(s<(p-1)/2\). Reflection supplies the zero \(p-1-s\), whose gap from \(s\) is

\[
g:=p-1-2s.
\]

This \(g\) is even. Applying (4.2) at \(x=s\),

\[
-s-g-1=s-p\equiv s\pmod p,
\]

while \((-1)^{g-1}=-1\). Hence

\[
N_g(s)=-N_g(s)\pmod p,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{N_g(s)=0\pmod p}
\tag{4.4}
\]

for **every** \(s\), independently of whether \(b_s=0\). This is the forced center root of every even positional gap, the \(\kappa_h\) term already isolated in the project’s GPRV formulation.

Consequently, a first-half pair \(r<s\) creates four reflected zeros

\[
r<s<p-1-s<p-1-r,
\]

but the apparent extra gap equations are algebraically dependent:

- the outer reflected gap is the root paired by (4.2);
- each central mirror return is the forced center root (4.4).

No new codimension is gained.

### 4.3 Abstract group-theoretic reason

Knowing the total word in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\) and knowing that one subword lies in the stabilizer of \(L\) do not bound how often the orbit visits \(L\). Given arbitrary prescribed return times, one may choose prefix matrices realizing those returns and choose the final step so that the total product equals the required endpoint involution. The special polynomial dependence of the actual Apéry steps is additional information, but that is precisely route (d), not a consequence of the endpoint value alone.

### Verdict on (c)

The endpoint swap is indispensable for reflection bookkeeping and for subtracting forced center collisions. It does not restrict off-center first-half zero-pairs beyond the conditions already encoded by \(N_h\). It should not be counted as a second independent equation in any resultant or codimension argument.

## 5. Route (d): horizontal structure and a Bombieri–Vinogradov analogue

This is the strongest elementary-looking route, because the divisor switch (1.4) removes the growing finite-field orbit from the statement and replaces it by common large prime divisors of two fixed integers.

### 5.1 The exact horizontal gcd theorem

The dyadic Sparse Bridge Theorem is equivalent to the following weighted assertion.

```text
HORIZONTAL GCD BRIDGE (HGB)

For every dyadic P,

  sum_{r>=1, h>=2, r+h<P}
    log rad_{p in (P,2P], 2(r+h)<p-1}
      gcd(b_r, N_h(r))

  << P.
```

This is simply (1.5), so it is not being asserted as an already easier theorem. Its value is that it identifies the exact missing arithmetic object:

\[
G_{r,h}:=\gcd(b_r,N_h(r)).
\]

Equivalently one may use \(\gcd(b_r,b_{r+h})\), but \(N_h(r)\) has controlled degree and height and is therefore the better second entry.

The problem is now an Apéry analogue of a Titchmarsh divisor problem: average the large prime divisors of \(G_{r,h}\) over a two-dimensional triangular family.

### 5.2 A precise BV-scale box target

Let \(r\asymp R\) and \(h\asymp H\) denote dyadic ranges with \(R+H<P\), and put

\[
\mathcal C(P;R,H)
:=\sum_{\substack{r\asymp R\\h\asymp H}}
\sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\2(r+h)<p-1}}
\mathbf1_{p\mid b_r}\mathbf1_{p\mid N_h(r)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The random two-codimension scale is

\[
\frac{RH}{P\log P},
\]

because there are \(RH\) parameter pairs, \(P/\log P\) primes, and heuristic probability \(p^{-2}\) for the two congruences.

A uniform estimate of the following kind, for some fixed \(A>4\), would prove the Sparse Bridge Theorem after summing \(O((\log P)^2)\) dyadic boxes:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C(P;R,H)
\ll
\frac{RH}{P\log P}
+rac{P}{(\log P)^A}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the most precise formulation of what “Bombieri–Vinogradov for Apéry zero-pairs” should mean in the present problem.

### 5.3 Why standard Bombieri–Vinogradov does not apply

The fact that each local companion matrix has fixed polynomial entries is not enough. Four distinct obstructions remain.

#### (i) The prime is a divisor, not a modulus of an external progression

Classical Bombieri–Vinogradov controls primes in residue classes \(a\bmod q\), averaged over an independent modulus \(q\). Here \(p\) is simultaneously:

- the prime being summed;
- the modulus of the recurrence;
- a prime divisor of the fixed integers \(b_r\) and \(N_h(r)\).

The condition \(p\mid N_h(r)\) is not a congruence condition \(p\equiv a\bmod q\) to which the classical dispersion method can be applied directly.

#### (ii) The global word has growing algebraic complexity

For fixed \(h\), the relevant transfer entry is a polynomial of degree \(3(h-1)\), with coefficient height exponential in \(h\). Thus the local degree-three step does not create a bounded-complexity family when \(h\) ranges up to \(p\). Effective Chebotarev or Frobenius large-sieve estimates for a fixed \(h\) do not give uniform control for \(h\asymp p\).

#### (iii) Fixed-gap Chebotarev sees only the universal return

For fixed \(h\), Chebotarev may control the mean number of roots of \(N_h\bmod p\). It does not impose the distinguished-fibre condition \(b_r=0\). The expected extra factor \(1/p\), which is exactly what turns \(O(p)\) possible universal returns into \(O(1)\) zero-pairs, is the missing correlation theorem.

#### (iv) The ordinary additive large sieve has the wrong ambient length

Expanding

\[
\mathbf1_{p\mid b_r}
=\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}e_p(ab_r)
\]

leads to phases indexed by the exponentially growing integers \(b_r\). Applying the standard large sieve to the set of frequencies \(b_r\) pays for an ambient interval of length comparable to \(b_R\), which is exponential in \(R\), and is useless at \(R\asymp P\). One needs recurrence-specific cancellation, not the black-box large sieve.

### 5.4 What an elementary horizontal proof would need

There are three plausible forms.

1. **Large-prime gcd energy.** Prove HGB directly by controlling
   
   \[
   \sum_{r+h<P}
   \log\operatorname{rad}_{(P,2P]}\gcd(b_r,N_h(r)).
   \]

2. **Two-parameter dispersion.** Prove the box estimate (5.2), probably through bilinear decomposition in \(r\) and \(h\), together with a recurrence-specific large sieve for the pair \((b_r,N_h(r))\bmod p\).

3. **A large-prime collision theorem for the integer sequence.** Show that primes \(p\) larger than the indices cannot divide many pairs among \(b_1,\ldots,b_R\) on average. In weighted form, a sufficient statement is
   
   \[
   \sum_{1\le r<s\le R}
   \log\operatorname{rad}_{\{p>2s\}}\gcd(b_r,b_s)
   \ll R.
   \tag{5.3}
   \]
   
   This is stronger than needed in some ranges but is completely elementary in formulation.

The present recurrence, Casoratian, and pairwise gap-coprimality results are natural inputs to these statements, but none currently gives the required \(P^2\)-scale saving over the trivial gcd-height budget.

### Verdict on (d)

Yes: varying \(p\) horizontally is the best elementary direction. But the appropriate theorem is not a routine Bombieri–Vinogradov corollary. It is a new Apéry Titchmarsh/dispersion estimate such as HGB or (5.2). The fixed polynomial nature of the one-step matrices explains why such a theorem may exist; it does not prove it.

## 6. Route (e): sieve theory

### 6.1 Why “\(r\) sifted by \(p\)” is not yet a sieve sequence

A classical sieve begins with one fixed set of integers and, for every auxiliary prime \(\ell\), knows which residue classes modulo \(\ell\) are removed. Here the set

\[
\mathcal H_p=\{r<p/2:p\mid b_r\}
\]

depends on the same prime \(p\) that defines the divisibility condition and the length of the interval.

To apply Gallagher’s larger sieve to \(\mathcal H_p\), one would need to show that \(\mathcal H_p\bmod\ell\) occupies few classes for many auxiliary primes \(\ell\). Lucas modulo \(\ell\) describes the condition \(\ell\mid b_r\), not the unrelated condition \(p\mid b_r\). It gives no restriction on the reductions modulo \(\ell\) of the \(p\)-zero set. Such a restriction would itself be a cross-prime independence theorem.

### 6.2 What Selberg sieve would require

A Selberg or combinatorial sieve for (5.1) would need:

1. a multiplicative local density for the simultaneous congruences
   
   \[
   b_r\equiv N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod q
   \]
   
   for squarefree \(q\);
2. a level of distribution uniform in the growing gap \(h\);
3. a bounded average singular series after summing over \(r,h\).

None is presently available. In particular, \(b_r\bmod q\) is not a fixed low-degree polynomial in \(r\), and Lucas makes its dependence on \(r\) digit-automatic rather than periodic in a single residue class over the ranges needed here.

Once these three properties were proved, the final Selberg-sieve step would likely be routine. But those properties contain essentially all of the missing mathematics.

### 6.3 The Gallagher analogy is conditional, not a proof mechanism

Gallagher’s Poisson law for primes in short intervals is obtained from uniform Hardy–Littlewood prime-tuple asymptotics together with an average of singular series. The analogue here would start from a uniform asymptotic for simultaneous Apéry zero events. At second order, that input is already the Sparse Bridge phenomenon.

Thus the correct analogy is:

- **Hardy–Littlewood input for primes** ↔ a uniform joint Apéry-zero distribution theorem;
- **Gallagher’s averaging argument** ↔ the final moment conversion after that theorem is known.

Gallagher’s method does not create the joint distribution from reflection, Lucas, or the recurrence alone.

### 6.4 Sieve parity is not the main obstruction

The desired result is an upper bound, so the classical parity problem is not the first barrier. The more basic obstacle is the absence of a usable local-density/level-of-distribution statement. Even an upper-bound sieve has nothing to operate on until that arithmetic input is supplied.

### Verdict on (e)

Sieve theory is a plausible final wrapper around a future horizontal distribution theorem. It is not currently an independent route. The meaningful sieve subproblem is to prove the box estimate (5.2) or a comparable level-of-distribution statement, not to choose different Selberg weights.

## 7. One additional Apéry-specific possibility: the square truncation

The project also has the finite-field factorization

\[
A_p(T):=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rT^r
\equiv \Delta(T)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(T)^2\pmod p.
\tag{7.1}
\]

A zero coefficient \(b_r=0\) is therefore a vanished quadratic convolution coefficient of \(B_p\), and a zero-pair gives two such quadratic equations. This is superficially elementary.

However, arbitrary squares over \(\mathbf F_p[T]\) can have many zero coefficients; for example sparse choices of \(B\) produce sparse \(B^2\). Thus (7.1) alone gives no anti-concentration theorem for the coefficient sequence. One would need strong additional structure on the coefficients or roots of the specific \(B_p\), such as a uniform recurrence, a Brandt-module interpretation with coefficient-level control, or a spectral gap. That route is more modular than elementary and is not presently shorter than the horizontal gcd problem.

## 8. Recommended research targets, in order

### Target 1 — Horizontal dyadic dispersion

Prove (5.2), or directly prove HGB. This target uses all genuinely relevant elementary structure:

- \(b_r\) is a fixed integer sequence independent of \(p\);
- \(N_h(r)\) is an explicit continuant value with controlled degree and height;
- pairwise coprimality can control repeated large-prime divisibility across different \(h\);
- the no-consecutive-zero lemma makes the divisor switch exact;
- \(p>2(r+h)\) places every pair prime above all recurrence singularities.

This is the route most likely to admit divisor switching, bilinear decomposition, or an arithmetic large sieve without growing sheaf conductor.

### Target 2 — Zero-triple resultant compression

Use

\[
(K_p)_2\le2+(K_p)_3
\]

and seek a direct \(O(P/\log P)\) bound for the zero-triple sum. The current separate-resultant budget is too large, so the target must exploit one of:

- a common aggregate determinant of height much smaller than the product of all pairwise resultants;
- cancellation in the gap variables;
- the distinguished anchor equation \(b_r=0\);
- a structural classification of primes dividing many gap resultants.

Without one of these, the calculation in §3.3 proves that the method remains at the \(3/5\) tail threshold.

### Target 3 — A genuine \(p^2\)-digit valuation theorem

Investigate whether zero digits lie in a uniform modulo-\(p^2\) Lucas class and whether two zero digits force two units of valuation. This is a clearly testable algebraic question, but even a positive answer needs a second incidence argument and therefore ranks below the horizontal gcd route.

### Targets to retire as standalone approaches

- Lucas modulo \(p\) in the first-half window;
- the endpoint swap without additional polynomial-word input;
- a black-box larger sieve;
- fixed-\(h\) Chebotarev summed naively up to \(h\asymp p\).

## 9. Bottom line by question

### (a) Lucas

No. For \(r,s<p\), Lucas is one-digit tautology. Multi-digit propagation gives cylinders, not pair repulsion. A uniform \(p^2\)-valuation law would be new and still not sufficient by size alone.

### (b) Gap-polynomial coprimality

Partially. It cleanly controls zero triples through

\[
N_h(r)=N_k(r)=0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
M_p(h,k)\le v_p(\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_k)).
\]

Since \((K)_2\le2+(K)_3\), this is conceptually relevant. But the total \(H^4\) height budget yields the same \(3/5\) barrier as Q326. A new aggregate compression theorem is required.

### (c) Endpoint swap

No independent saving. It is exactly reflection. The mirrored root is forced by (4.2), and the central reflected gap is the forced center root for even gaps. It supplies bookkeeping, not a new codimension.

### (d) Horizontal structure

Yes, as the best formulation. The exact bridge is the large-prime radical/gcd estimate (1.5). A BV-type box bound such as (5.2) would prove the theorem. No existing black-box BV theorem covers this moving-prime, growing-word, distinguished-fibre problem.

### (e) Sieve

Only after a new local-density and level-of-distribution theorem is proved. Gallagher’s Poisson mechanism is an analogy for the last step, not a source of the missing pair correlation.
