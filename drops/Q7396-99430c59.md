## 3. Exact switch, including all floor effects

Set

\[
s:=q-14t,
\qquad n:=q-t.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The two unclipped integer p-upper endpoints are

\[
m_-:=q-t-1=n-1,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_+
&:=\left\lfloor\frac{12q+t}{13}\right\rfloor\\
&=\left\lfloor q-t-\frac{q-14t}{13}\right\rfloor\\
&=n-\left\lceil\frac{s}{13}\right\rceil.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
m_- - m_+
 =\left\lceil\frac{q-14t}{13}\right\rceil-1.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

It follows that

\[
q>14t\Longrightarrow m_-\ge m_+,
\tag{3.5}
\]

whereas

\[
q\le14t\Longrightarrow m_-<m_+.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The weak inequality in (3.5) is important. When

\[
1\le q-14t\le13,

the real minus endpoint is larger, but the two **integer** upper endpoints are equal. At the exact boundary \(q=14t\), the inclusive plus endpoint is one integer larger.

Let

\[
C_0(q):=\min\{B,q-h-1\}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Then

\[
U_-^J=\min\{C_0,m_-\},
\qquad
U_+^J=\min\{C_0,m_+\}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Because the map \(x\mapsto\min(C_0,x)\) is monotone, (3.5)--(3.6) survive the shell and long-gap clipping. The common lower endpoint then proves (V4).

For actual prime \(q>13\), it is convenient to state the switch directly in the integer t-variable:

\[
q>14t
\iff
0\le t\le\left\lfloor\frac{q-1}{14}\right\rfloor,
\tag{3.9}
\]

and

\[
q<14t
\iff
t\ge\left\lceil\frac q{14}\right\rceil.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Since a prime \(q>13\) is not divisible by 14, these two ranges are adjacent and exhaustive.

### 3.1 Union and intersection endpoints in one formula

Define

m_\vee:=\max\{m_-,m_+\},
\qquad
m_\cap:=\min\{m_-,m_+\}.
\tag{3.11}

 =\min\{C_0,m_\vee\},
\qquad
U_\cap^J
 =\min\{C_0,m_\cap\},

\mathcal P_-^J\cup\mathcal P_+^J

\mathcal P_-^J\cap\mathcal P_+^J
 =\mathbb P\cap[L,U_\cap^J]_{\mathbb Z}.
\tag{3.14}

N_\vee^J=\max\{N_-^J,N_+^J\},
\qquad
N_\cap^J=\min\{N_-^J,N_+^J\}.}
\tag{4.3}

\tag{4.4}

## 5. The `tau_p` inclusion-exclusion collapses pointwise

Let

\[
\chi_-(p;q,t)=1_{\mathcal P_-^J(q,t)}(p),
\qquad
\chi_+(p;q,t)=1_{\mathcal P_+^J(q,t)}(p).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
\chi_-(p;q,t)\chi_+(p;q,t)
 =1_{\mathcal P_-^J\cap\mathcal P_+^J}(p).
\tag{5.2}
\]

If \(q>14t\), nesting gives \(\chi_+\le\chi_-\), so

\[
\chi_-+\chi_+-\chi_-\chi_+
 =\chi_-.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If \(q\le14t\), then \(\chi_-\le\chi_+\), so

\[
\chi_-+\chi_+-\chi_-\chi_+
 =\chi_+.
\tag{5.4}
\]

This proves (V8). Thus the inclusion-exclusion should be simplified **before** any absolute-value, Cauchy, or variance estimate. Expanding the three terms and estimating them separately merely loses constants and hides the exact Boolean union.

The support of the product coefficient is

\[
\operatorname{supp}_p(\chi_-\chi_+)
 =\mathcal P_\cap^J,
\]

which is the smaller complete interval. It is not supported only near \(q=14t\), and it is not the symmetric-difference tail. Equations (4.11)--(4.14) show that it may be as large as the full union.

## 6. Consequence for the lower-density term sum R

Suppose the common lower-density coefficient is \(\rho_p\ge0\), and define

\[
R_-(q,t)=\sum_p\chi_-(p;q,t)\rho_p,
\qquad
R_+(q,t)=\sum_p\chi_+(p;q,t)\rho_p,
\tag{6.1}
\]

\[
R_\cap(q,t)=\sum_p\chi_-(p;q,t)\chi_+(p;q,t)\rho_p,
\tag{6.2}
\]

and

\[
R(q,t)=R_-+R_+-R_\cap
       =\sum_p\tau_p(q,t)\rho_p.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Nesting gives the exact identities

\[
\boxed{
q>14t:\quad R_\cap=R_+,\quad R=R_-,}
\tag{6.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
q\le14t:\quad R_\cap=R_-,\quad R=R_+.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
R(q,t)=\max\{R_-(q,t),R_+(q,t)\}
\tag{6.6}
\]

for nonnegative common weights. A sign-separated sum satisfies only

\[
R\le R_-+R_+\le2R.
\tag{6.7}
\]

The inclusion-exclusion improves this by an absolute factor, not an exponent.

In the current density decomposition one has schematically

\[
\rho_p=\frac{Z(p)^2}{p^2}\ll T^{-2/3}
\]

on the lower shell. A macroscopic candidate interval contains \(O(T/\log T)\) primes, so both the union and the overlap obey the same statewise scale

\[
R_\vee(q,t),R_\cap(q,t)
 \ll_J \frac{T^{1/3}}{\log T}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The overlap bound has the same powers because its support is still \(\asymp_JT\). Thus the existing selected-state estimate

\[
\sum_{\text{selected }(q,t)}R(q,t)
 \ll_J
 T^{8/5}(\log T)^{-4/5}
 \cdot T^{1/3}(\log T)^{-1}
 =T^{29/15}(\log T)^{-9/5}
\tag{6.9}
\]

is unchanged except for an absolute constant. Relative to

\[
B=T^2(\log T)^{-2},
\]

its ratio is

\[
T^{-1/15}(\log T)^{1/5}\to0.
\]

So `tau_p` cleans up the Boolean coefficient, but it does not improve the already-subcritical exponent of \(\sum R\).

## 7. Consequence for variance

### 7.1 Variance of the common `tau_p`-weighted coefficient

Let

\[
R_q(t)=\sum_p\tau_p(q,t)\rho_p,
\qquad
\overline R_q=q^{-1}\sum_{t\bmod q}R_q(t).
\]

Then the centered energy is simply

\[
V_R
 =\sum_q\sum_{t\bmod q}|R_q(t)-\overline R_q|^2,
\tag{7.1}
\]

where \(R_q(t)\) is the single switched larger-window coefficient in (6.4)--(6.5). There is no separate negative “intersection variance” to discard. If one expands

\[
R_-+R_+-R_\cap
\]

inside the square, all covariance terms are required to reconstruct the pointwise identity \(R=R_\vee\). Estimating the three pieces independently can only lose constants.

Moreover,

\[
\tau_p^2=\tau_p,
\]

so diagonal support in the squared coefficient is the union support, while the off-diagonal support is \((\mathcal P_\vee^J)^2\). The shorter intersection support does not replace either of these sets. Since \(N_\cap^J\) can equal \(N_\vee^J\), no uniform power or logarithmic gain in (7.1) follows from the overlap geometry.

The formal switch row \(q=14t\) contributes no actual prime-q state. Even if arbitrary integer q were allowed, it would change at most one p-endpoint and one t-row per q. That codimension-one endpoint fact is negligible, but it is unrelated to the support of the intersection coefficient, which exists throughout a macroscopic region.

### 7.2 If the arithmetic summands are sign-specific

The preceding pointwise collapse applies when the three terms carry the same base coefficient \(\rho_p\). If the local `tau_p` formula instead has the form

\[
\alpha_p\chi_-+\beta_p\chi_+
 -\gamma_p\chi_-\chi_+,
\tag{7.2}
\]

with different arithmetic coefficients, nesting proves only

\[
\operatorname{supp}(\chi_-\chi_+)=\mathcal P_\cap^J.
\]

It does not identify \(\gamma_p\) with the coefficient on the smaller sign.

This distinction is essential for the lower-pair defect. If

\[
Y_p^-=\chi_-X_p^-,
\qquad
Y_p^+=\chi_+X_p^+,
\]

then the Boolean union is

\[
Y_p^-+Y_p^+-Y_p^-Y_p^+.
\tag{7.3}
\]

On the overlap, the last term imposes both sign-specific lower-zero conditions. Window nesting does not turn that four-zero or cross-sign arithmetic event into either two-zero event. Its p-support is still macroscopic by Section 4. Therefore any extra smallness from (7.3) must come from a new simultaneous-zero estimate, a smaller arithmetic coefficient such as a genuinely justified \(\rho_p^2\), or cancellation in a centered defect. It cannot be claimed from support length alone.

This also answers the variance question in its strongest form: interval geometry supplies no new variance theorem for the intersection term.

## 8. Copy-paste lemma for the proof file

```text
Lemma (capped quotient-six window nesting).
Fix J and put D=T/J, A=floor(T/7)+1, B=floor(T/3), and
h=floor(D). For integral q,t define

  L=max(A,floor((6q+t)/7)+1),
  C=min(B,q-h-1),
  Uminus=min(C,q-t-1),
  Uplus=min(C,floor((12q+t)/13)).

Let Pminus and Pplus be the prime subsets of [L,Uminus]_Z and
[L,Uplus]_Z, respectively. Then

  q>14t  =>  Pplus subseteq Pminus,
  q<=14t =>  Pminus subseteq Pplus.

Consequently

  Pminus union Pplus = Pminus,
  Pminus inter Pplus = Pplus

when q>14t, and the two roles are reversed when q<=14t.
At q=14t the only possible integer in Pplus\Pminus is p=13t;
it occurs iff L<=13t<=C and 13t is prime. If q>13 is prime,
q=14t never occurs.

Proof. Set mminus=q-t-1 and
mplus=floor((12q+t)/13). Since

  mminus-mplus=ceil((q-14t)/13)-1,

the stated ordering holds. Applying the common monotone clipping
x |-> min(C,x) preserves it, and both windows have the same lower
endpoint L. The equality-row assertion follows from
mminus=13t-1 and mplus=13t. QED.
```

  tau_p=chi_minus+chi_plus-chi_minus*chi_plus

is exactly chi_minus for q>14t and exactly chi_plus for q<=14t.
Thus the overlap term removes Boolean double counting but has no

the union, and for every fixed J>21 it has length asymptotic to a

Therefore it changes neither the T/log-T exponent of sum R nor the

## Final conclusion

The exact capped geometry is a one-interval geometry, not a genuine two-window union:

```text
q>14t:  union = minus, intersection = plus;
q<14t:  union = plus,  intersection = minus.
```

The formal equality row places one inclusive endpoint in plus, but it is absent for prime \(q>13\). Shell clipping and the strict long-gap condition enter through common integer endpoints and preserve nesting. They may make the two windows equal; they never produce an additional crossing.

The overlap coefficient therefore has **no asymptotically useful support-length saving**. Its only unconditional effects are exact de-duplication, an absolute factor improvement over adding signs, and boundary/empty-sector deletions. It does not alter the exponent of \(\sum R\) and does not lower the variance scale. Any stronger gain must be arithmetic rather than geometric.
