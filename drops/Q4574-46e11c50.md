# 1. What the fixed-prime collision energy literally consumes

The canonical `energy_result.tex` defines, for \(p\ge7\) and \(H\le\sqrt p\),

\[
 \mathscr D_H=\{(d,r)\in\mathbf Z_{\ge2}^2:d+r\le H\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_p(H)=\sum_{(d,r)\in\mathscr D_H}m_{d,r}(p).
\]

For

\[
 k_x(H)=\#\{2\le h\le H:N_h(x)=0\}
\]

and \(\partial_H=\{-2,\ldots,-H\}\), Proposition `prop:energy-column-exact` gives the exact off-boundary identity

\[
 \mathcal E_p^\circ(H)
 =\sum_{x\notin\partial_H}\binom{k_x(H)}2,
\]

with only a one-sided boundary comparison.  The current pointwise theorem is

\[
 \mathcal E_p(H)\ll H^{8/3}.
\]

The **only load-bearing use of a hypothetical pointwise improvement** in that file is Proposition `prop:energy-beta-optimization`.  Partition \([0,p-1]\) into \(B_p\ll p/H\) nonwrapping blocks and write \(z_{p,j}\) for their Apéry zero counts.  The proof gives

\[
 \sum_j\binom{z_{p,j}}3\le \mathcal E_p(H),
 \tag{1.1}
\]

and the elementary inequality

\[
 z\le 2+\bigl(6\tbinom z3\bigr)^{1/3}
\]

followed by Hölder gives

\[
 \boxed{
 Z(p)\ll B_p+B_p^{2/3}\mathcal E_p(H)^{1/3}.}
 \tag{1.2}
\]

The published pointwise consequence

\[
 \mathcal E_p(H)\ll H^\beta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 Z(p)\ll \frac pH+p^{2/3}H^{(\beta-2)/3}
\]

is obtained only after applying (1.2) to one fixed prime.

**Nothing in (1.1)--(1.2) requires us to do that.**  The prime sum may be taken first.

# 2. First refactor: sum the literal consumer before Hölder in the prime variable

Let

\[
 \mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
 \qquad
 A_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}Z(p),
\]

and take one common \(H\le\sqrt X\).  Summing (1.2) gives

\[
 A_X
 \ll \pi_X\frac XH
   +\left(\frac XH\right)^{2/3}
     \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\mathcal E_p(H)^{1/3}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

This is already a legal prime-averaged consumer.  There is no pointwise conclusion anywhere.  If one wants a first moment rather than the cube-root moment, Hölder *across primes* gives

\[
 \sum_p\mathcal E_p(H)^{1/3}
 \le \pi_X^{2/3}
      \left(\sum_p\mathcal E_p(H)\right)^{1/3},
\]

hence

\[
 \boxed{
 A_X
 \ll
 \frac{X^2}{H\log X}
 +\frac{X^{4/3}}{H^{2/3}(\log X)^{2/3}}
  \left(\sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(H)\right)^{1/3}.}
 \tag{2.2}
\]

At \(H=\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor\),

\[
 A_X
 \ll \frac{X^{3/2}}{\log X}
  +\frac{X}{(\log X)^{2/3}}
   \left(\sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(\sqrt X)\right)^{1/3}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

This proves immediately that a prime average can replace the fixed-prime theorem in this consumer.  It also shows the **weakest moment actually requested by (1.2)**:

\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(H)^{1/3},
\]

not \(\max_p\mathcal E_p(H)\).

There is a further weakening.  Define the actual block statistic

\[
 \mathcal T_p(H)
 :=\sum_j\binom{z_{p,j}}3.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Then (1.1) says only

\[
 \mathcal T_p(H)\le\mathcal E_p(H).
\]

The literal proof of (1.2) is valid with \(\mathcal T_p(H)\) in place of \(\mathcal E_p(H)\).  Thus the consumer does **not** need phantom gap-polynomial roots, nonsplit finite-extension roots, or the full ambient energy.  It needs only actual Apéry zero triples in the short blocks.

For example, at \(H=\sqrt X\), a theorem

\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal T_p(H)^{1/3}
 \ll X^{7/6+o(1)}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

would give \(A_X\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}\).  To beat the present \(X^{8/5+o(1)}\) by this fixed-\(H\) version, it would already suffice to have

\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal T_p(H)^{1/3}
 \ll X^{19/15-\varepsilon}
\]

for some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\).

This is a valid refactor, but it is not the optimal use of the dyadic zero-count parameter.  The next section gives the sharper consumer that exactly matches the canonical \(8/5\) and \(3/2\) exponents.

# 3. Exact dyadic summation order: \(H\asymp X/T\)

Put

\[
 \mathcal P_X(T)
 =\{p\in\mathcal P_X:T<Z(p)\le2T\}.
\]

For \(T\ge C\sqrt X\), choose

\[
 H=\left\lceil\frac{16X}{T}\right\rceil.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

After increasing the harmless absolute constant \(C\), this satisfies \(H\le\sqrt p\) for every \(p\in\mathcal P_X\), so the collision-energy identities are available.  Partition \([0,p-1]\) into blocks of length at most \(H\).  Their number satisfies

\[
 B_p\le \frac{T}{8}+O(1).
\]

Since

\[
 \binom z3\ge z-2\qquad(z\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}),
\]

we get, uniformly for \(p\in\mathcal P_X(T)\),

\[
 \mathcal T_p(H)
 =\sum_j\binom{z_{p,j}}3
 \ge Z(p)-2B_p
 \gg T.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
 T\,|\mathcal P_X(T)|
 \ll \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X(T)}\mathcal T_p(H)
 \le \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(H).
 \tag{3.3}
\]

This is the exact point where the summation order changes.  The prime average is now on the **same side** of the inequality as the zero-level count.  No estimate for any individual \(p\) is inferred.

Suppose, for some \(\gamma\ge3\), that in the required range

\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(H)
 \ll H^{\gamma}X^{o(1)}.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Then (3.1)--(3.3) give the dyadic level estimate

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X(T)}Z(p)
 \ll \left(\frac XT\right)^{\gamma}X^{o(1)}.}
 \tag{3.5}
\]

For low levels \(Z(p)\le T_0\), prime counting gives

\[
 \sum_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\ Z(p)\le T_0}}Z(p)
 \ll \frac{XT_0}{\log X}.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

The high dyadic levels are dominated by the first one in (3.5).  Balancing

\[
 XT_0\asymp \frac{X^\gamma}{T_0^\gamma}
\]

gives

\[
 T_0=X^{(\gamma-1)/(\gamma+1)}.
\]

For \(\gamma\ge3\) this is at least the \(\sqrt X\) transition, so all quantifiers line up.  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)
 \ll X^{\,1+c(\gamma)+o(1)},
 \qquad
 c(\gamma)=\max\left\{\frac12,\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}\right\}.}
 \tag{3.7}
\]

The important special cases are

\[
 \gamma=4
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 c=\frac35,
 \qquad
 \sum Z(p)\ll X^{8/5+o(1)},
 \tag{3.8}
\]

and

\[
 \gamma=3
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 c=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \sum Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.
 \tag{3.9}
\]

More generally, if \(\gamma=4-\delta\), \(0<\delta\le1\), then

\[
 c(4-\delta)=\frac{3-\delta}{5-\delta}
 =\frac35-\frac{2\delta}{5(5-\delta)}.
 \tag{3.10}
\]

So **any fixed saving below the prime-summed \(H^4\) ledger improves the current \(8/5\) first zero moment.**  The critical \(H^3\) ledger reaches the average \(p^{1/2}\) scale.

This is the correct averaged analogue of `prop:energy-beta-optimization`.  Notice that its exponent is not obtained by averaging the pointwise conclusion; it is obtained by changing the order of summation before the optimization.

# 4. Existing Apéry machinery already proves the \(H^4\) prime-summed baseline

This is the most useful positive result of the refactor.

For every \((d,r)\in\mathscr D_H\), `meso_result.tex` proves the integer resultant

\[
 \mathcal R_{d,r}
 =\operatorname{Res}_x\bigl(N_d(x),N_r(x+d)\bigr)
\]

is nonzero.  A split common root modulo \(p\) contributes to the nullity of the reduced Sylvester matrix, so

\[
 m_{d,r}(p)\le v_p(\mathcal R_{d,r})
\]

in the present range.  The same norm estimate used explicitly in `near_bridge_energy.tex` gives

\[
 \log|\mathcal R_{d,r}|\ll dr\log(2H).
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(H)
 &\le
 \sum_{d+r\le H}\sum_{X<p\le2X}v_p(\mathcal R_{d,r})\\
 &\le \frac1{\log X}
       \sum_{d+r\le H}\log|\mathcal R_{d,r}|\\
 &\ll \frac{\log(2H)}{\log X}
       \sum_{d+r\le H}dr\\
 &\ll H^4\frac{\log(2H)}{\log X}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the repository already proves the prime-summed energy exponent

\[
 \boxed{\gamma=4+o(1).}
\]

Putting (4.2) into Section 3 reproduces \(c=3/5\), i.e. the existing \(8/5\) zero-mass exponent.  This is not a new exponent, but it proves that the average-energy refactor is arithmetically real rather than merely formal.

It also explains the exact threshold: the obstacle is no longer “prove \(\mathcal E_p(H)=o(H^2)\) for every prime.”  For the averaged zero-mass consumer, the first useful new theorem is simply a fixed power saving below **total prime-summed height \(H^4\)**.

A still weaker consumer-shaped statement is enough.  Define \(\mathcal T_p(H)\) by (2.4) using the actual zero positions and the chosen short-block partition.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X(T)}\mathcal T_p(H)
 \ll H^{4-\delta}X^{o(1)},
 \qquad H=\left\lceil\frac{16X}{T}\right\rceil
 }
 \tag{ATPE}_{\delta}
\]

is sufficient for the same improvement.  This is strictly weaker than \((\mathrm{PE})_\delta\): it retains actual Apéry incidences only and ignores every ambient collision not used by the zero-count consumer.

# 5. Second refactor: the canonical quadruple consumer deletes isolated two-return energy entirely

The preceding refactor still packages a zero triple into a two-return energy witness.  The canonical aligned-content section goes one step further.

For a block containing \(m\) ordered Apéry zeros, select the \(m-3\) sliding consecutive quadruples.  If \(Q_p(H)\) denotes the total number of selected quadruples over the short-block partition, `atom_tail_section.tex` proves the exact lower bound

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_p(H)\ge Z(p)-3\left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil.}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

This is the cleanest answer to the “isolated two-return” question.  A block containing only two or three zero positions contributes **nothing** to \(Q_p(H)\); its entire mass is absorbed into the deterministic `3 per block` term.  Only the fourth and subsequent return in the same block is charged to arithmetic.

Thus, on a high-zero level with \(H\asymp X/T\), one has

\[
 Q_p(H)\gg T,
\]

and an averaged certificate theorem for these quadruples gives the same dyadic level bound as Section 3 without ever bounding the isolated two-return layer \(I_2(H)\).

The coefficient-sensitive aligned-content mechanism is designed exactly for this.  For a non-all-equal gap triple \((a,b,c)\), the source defines

\[
 \mathcal R_{a,b,c}(U)
 =\operatorname{Res}_x(F_{a,b,c},G_{a,b,c}+U J_{a,b,c}),
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_{a,b,c}=\operatorname{cont}_U\mathcal R_{a,b,c},
\]

and proves unconditionally (`lem:qc-aligned`) that if the three reduced polynomials have \(t\) distinct common \(\mathbf F_p\)-roots, then

\[
 t\le v_p(\mathcal C_{a,b,c}).
 \tag{5.2}
\]

This retains the **same root component** and does not replace an aligned common root by unrelated scalar-resultant divisibility.

What is not proved is the global mass hypothesis

\[
 \mathrm{AC}^{\mathrm{tr}}:
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{tr}}(H)
 =\sum_{a+b+c\le H}\log\mathcal C^*_{a,b,c}
 \ll H^{3+o(1)}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Under (5.3), `thm:qc-main` applies the same dyadic choice

\[
 \mathcal P_X(T)=\{p:T<Z(p)\le2T\},
 \qquad
 H=\left\lceil\frac{16X}{T}\right\rceil,
\]

and proves

\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

So the strongest answer is:

> A fixed-prime isolated-two-return theorem is **strictly overqualified** for the prime-averaged vertical route.  The canonical quadruple consumer proves that the prime average can be organized so isolated two-return columns are never charged at all.  What is missing is an averaged aligned-certificate mass theorem, not a pointwise two-return theorem.

But (5.3) remains conditional.  No existing unconditional theorem in `main` supplies it.

# 6. Why the other existing moment tools do not supply the missing fixed saving

## 6.1 Near-bridge energy

`near_bridge_energy.tex` is already prime-averaged:

\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}E_p^{\mathrm{near}}(K)
 \ll H^2K^2,
 \qquad H=\lfloor\sqrt{2X}\rfloor.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

At \(K=H/\sqrt{\log X}\) this is \(O(X^2/\log X)\).

But its objects are **ordered separated pairs of quotient orbits, each orbit built from four consecutive occurrences of one projective state**.  Its injective certificate is \((p,x,s,G)\), with the common-root equations

\[
 N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0.
\]

An isolated two-return column has no second four-return orbit and can contribute zero to (6.1).  There is no inequality

\[
 I_{2,p}(H)\le E_p^{\mathrm{near}}(K),
\]

nor any reverse comparison with the total collision energy.  Promoting (6.1) to a bound on isolated return energy would therefore be a set-theoretic error before any exponent calculation.

The theorem is useful for a high-multiplicity separated sector, but it does not touch the q=1 singleton in the Q4531 return-tail dictionary.

## 6.2 Q4531 aligned first/second return tails

For an off-boundary column with zero levels

\[
 h_1<\cdots<h_k,
\]

Q4531 rederived from the restart identity that

\[
 q_{h_i,x}(H)=k-i.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \sum_iq_{h_i,x}=\binom k2,
 \qquad
 \sum_i\binom{q_{h_i,x}}2=\binom k3,
\]

while

\[
 \sum_i1_{\{q_{h_i,x}=1\}}=1_{\{k\ge2\}}.
\]

The last identity is crucial: `q=1` is the **penultimate** return component.  It is not the same as “the column has exactly two zero levels” unless one also imposes the minimal-anchor nonvanishing condition.  Isolation is an open localization, not a third common-root equation.

Thus aligned three-polynomial gcd/content controls the \(\binom k3\) layer and misses exactly the singleton first-return mass.  This is why a proof that keeps the energy consumer has an isolated-layer problem, whereas the quadruple zero-count consumer can simply pay those low columns in its deterministic baseline.

## 6.3 Common-index/twin-atom high columns

The repository-visible Q4530 theorem uses

\[
 P_N(r)=\{p:r<p,\ p\mid b_r\},
 \qquad m_N(r)=|P_N(r)|,
\]

with the canonical twin-atom codegree estimate to prove

\[
 \sum_rm_N(r)^2
 \ll \frac{N^{23/10}}{(\log N)^{3/2}}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

For the weighted Farey diagonal,

\[
 u_p=\frac{\log p}{p}v(p/N),
\]

and

\[
 \mathcal H_N(v)
 =\sum_{p\ne q}u_pu_q|\mathcal Z_p\cap\mathcal Z_q|
 \ll N^{3/10}(\log N)^{1/2}.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

This is a **horizontal cross-prime same-index** moment.  Collision energy is a **same-prime multiple-return** moment.  They are transposes of the incidence graph, not the same statistic.

The high-column lemma itself has the tail

\[
 |S_t|\ll \frac{NM_N^2}{t^3}+\frac{M_N}{t}.
\]

The second term deliberately allows an isolated saturated column.  Therefore the theorem cannot be repurposed to eliminate the isolated-return layer by dropping a term or applying Cauchy again.

## 6.4 Canonical first zero moment

The unconditional

is entirely compatible with a sparse set of primes carrying large isolated-return mass.  It is the output we are trying to improve, not a relative bound on the conditional return distribution.  Feeding it back into (3.3) gives no new fixed saving.

 \tag{7.3}

# 9. Why Cauchy/Hölder does not repair the selected normalization

There are two Cauchy/Hölder operations in play and they have opposite statuses.

### Legal: prime Hölder in the unselected vertical sum

Equation (2.1) has a genuine sum over primes with no denominator depending on the selected data.  Therefore

\[
 \sum_p\mathcal E_p^{1/3}
 \le \pi_X^{2/3}(\sum_p\mathcal E_p)^{1/3}

is legal and gives (2.2).

### Illegal as a closure: Cauchy after conditioning on selected anchors

 \sum k_{x,j}^\tau s_{x,j}^\tau,
\]

and the normalizing first mass is

\[
 \sum k_{x,j}^\tau=M_-(T).
\]

An unweighted second moment of the ambient \(s\)-columns does not control this diagonal at independence scale unless one also controls how the actual weights \(k\) concentrate on high \(s\).  Applying Cauchy merely replaces the missing Palm statement by a weighted second moment such as

which is not supplied by the ordinary prime average.  Q4554's packet obstruction makes exactly this diagonal issue explicit.

So the failure is not “Hölder loses a small power.”  It is a measure mismatch: the available average is with respect to counting primes/ordinary indices, while the consumer is with respect to the selected Palm measure.

# 11. Insertion-ready LaTeX: proved prime-average refactor

The following statements are direct consequences of the canonical objects and can be inserted without assuming a new pointwise theorem.

```latex
\begin{proposition}[Prime-averaged collision-energy consumer]
\label{prop:energy-prime-average-consumer}
Let $X$ be large, let
$\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\}$, and let
$2\le H\le\sqrt X$.  Then
\[
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}Z(p)
 \ll
 \frac{X^2}{H\log X}
 +\frac{X^{4/3}}{H^{2/3}(\log X)^{2/3}}
  \left(
   \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\mathcal E_p(H)
  \right)^{1/3}.
\]
More precisely, if $\mathcal T_p(H)$ denotes the sum of
$\binom{z_I(p)}3$ over a partition of $[0,p-1]$ into intervals of
length at most $H$, then the same assertion holds with
$\mathcal E_p(H)$ replaced by $\mathcal T_p(H)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For one prime, the block-triple argument gives
\[
 Z(p)\ll B_p+B_p^{2/3}\mathcal T_p(H)^{1/3},
 \qquad B_p\ll X/H,
\]
and $\mathcal T_p(H)\le\mathcal E_p(H)$.  Sum over $p$ and use
H\"older in the form
\[
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\mathcal E_p(H)^{1/3}
 \le |\mathcal P_X|^{2/3}
      \left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\mathcal E_p(H)\right)^{1/3},
\]
together with $|\mathcal P_X|\ll X/\log X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Prime-summed resultant baseline]
\label{prop:energy-prime-average-resultant}
Uniformly for $H\le\sqrt X$,
\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(H)
 \ll H^4\frac{\log(2H)}{\log X}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $(d,r)\in\mathscr D_H$, every split common root counted by
$m_{d,r}(p)$ contributes to the nullity of the reduced Sylvester
matrix, so
\[
 m_{d,r}(p)\le v_p(\mathcal R_{d,r}).
\]
The separated root-strip theorem gives
$\mathcal R_{d,r}\ne0$, and the standard resultant-height estimate gives
\[
 \log|\mathcal R_{d,r}|\ll dr\log(2H).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(H)
 \le\frac1{\log X}
    \sum_{d+r\le H}\log|\mathcal R_{d,r}|
 \ll H^4\frac{\log(2H)}{\log X}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dyadic consequence of a prime-summed energy exponent]
\label{cor:energy-prime-average-exponent}
Suppose, uniformly in the relevant range $H\le\sqrt X$, that
\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}\mathcal E_p(H)
 \ll H^{\gamma}X^{o(1)}
\]
for some fixed $\gamma\ge3$.  Then
\[
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)
 \ll X^{1+c(\gamma)+o(1)},
 \qquad
 c(\gamma)=\max\left\{\frac12,
                       \frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}\right\}.
\]
In particular $\gamma=4$ gives the exponent $8/5$, every
$\gamma=4-\delta$ with $\delta>0$ gives a fixed improvement, and
$\gamma=3$ gives $X^{3/2+o(1)}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For dyadic $T\gg\sqrt X$, put
\[
 \mathcal P_X(T)=\{p:T<Z(p)\le2T\},
 \qquad H=\left\lceil\frac{16X}{T}\right\rceil.
\]
Partition $[0,p-1]$ into intervals of length at most $H$.  Their number
is at most $T/8+O(1)$, so
\[
 \sum_I\binom{z_I(p)}3
 \ge Z(p)-2\#\{I\}\gg T.
\]
Hence
\[
 T|\mathcal P_X(T)|
 \ll \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X(T)}\mathcal E_p(H)
 \ll (X/T)^{\gamma}X^{o(1)}.
\]
The levels below a cutoff $T_0$ contribute
$O(XT_0/\log X)$, while the higher dyadic levels contribute
$O(X^{\gamma}T_0^{-\gamma+o(1)})$.  Taking
$T_0=X^{(\gamma-1)/(\gamma+1)}$ gives the claim; when this cutoff would
fall below $\sqrt X$, use the $\sqrt X$ transition instead.
\end{proof}
```
