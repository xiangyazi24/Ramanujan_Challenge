## 7. What genuinely discriminates PRE-CUT from sparsity

There are four logically different finite outcomes.

### 7.3 First positive q fibre

A witness `a_q>0` proves that reflection, no-consecutive spacing, and exact clipped geometry do not force pointwise nonpositivity of the pre-upper projection. It is the first nondegenerate PRE-CUT datum. It does **not** contradict an asymptotic statement

\[

### 7.4 Populated marked mass but depleted positive excess

This is the observation that specifically tests PRE-CUT rather than sparsity. In several predeclared complete new shells, require:

1. `E_hit` and `E_+` are both nontrivial;
2. actual marked mass is not in the extreme lower tail of the conditional null;
3. actual positive-fibre count and `PRE-CUT+` are in a strong lower tail;
4. the theorem-normalized ratio
   \[
   K_{\rm pre}(T,J)
   :=\frac{\operatorname{PRE\!\mbox{-}CUT}^+(T,J)}
           {T^2/(\log T)^2}
   \tag{28}
   \]
   decreases rather than remaining flat or growing.

A useful pair of conditional ratios is

\[
R_{\rm mass}
=\frac{M_{\rm mark}}{\mathbf E_0M_{\rm mark}},
\qquad
R_{\rm cut}
=\frac{\operatorname{PRE\!\mbox{-}CUT}^+}
       {\mathbf E_0\operatorname{PRE\!\mbox{-}CUT}^+}.
\tag{29}
\]

The discriminating pattern is

```text
R_mass bounded away from zero,
R_cut strongly below one,
exact conditional cut-tail probability small,
K_pre decreasing across complete shells.
```

If both `R_mass` and `R_cut` are tiny, the data show avoidance/sparsity but do not distinguish a centered cut theorem. If both are near one, the p100k zero was a finite sparse accident. If `R_cut` is critical or growing after exposure is large, PRE-CUT is strategically disfavored.
