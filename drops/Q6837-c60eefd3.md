# A target-specific Catalan certificate: parity-locked Lima–Guillera tails inside one positive rank-seven kernel

## Executive verdict

The observed coefficient-space error ratio `-1/8` identifies the correct Catalan input much more sharply than the ordinary Euler partial sums do. The right insertion is the rapidly convergent Lima–Guillera central-binomial expansion

\[
G=\sum_{m\ge 0} c_m,
\qquad
c_m:=\frac{(-1)^m(3m+2)8^m}
 {2(2m+1)^3\binom{2m}{m}^3}.
\tag{1}
\]

Its term quotient is exactly

\[
\frac{c_{m+1}}{c_m}
=-\frac{(m+1)^3(3m+5)}{(2m+3)^3(3m+2)}
\longrightarrow -\frac18.
\tag{2}
\]

Even better, its tail has a three-beta-integral representation with a pointwise fixed sign. This removes the precise obstruction that killed the obvious affine Catalan remainder.

The shortest plausible representation of the Delannoy coefficients is therefore not
`g_k=f_k` times an ordinary Catalan partial sum. It is one terminating positive hypergeometric sum whose numerator inserts **parity-locked accelerated partial sums** of (1):

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
f_k&=\rho_k\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}W(k,j),\\
g_k&=\rho_k\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}W(k,j)A_{k+2j+3},
\end{aligned}}
\qquad
A_M:=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}c_m.
\tag{3}
\]

Here `rho_k > 0` is hypergeometric in `k`, and `W(k,j)>0` is a terminating proper-hypergeometric kernel of minimal natural type `7F6`. The shift `k+2j+3` is load-bearing:

- every inserted tail has the same parity, so the whole error has one pointwise sign;
- every inserted index is at least `k+3`, giving an immediate `O(k 8^{-k})` ratio bound;
- the shift `3` is forced as the smallest positive two-point seed compatible with the exact initial ratio;
- a rank-seven bulk plus one indefinite Catalan insertion predicts order `8` annihilators with an order `7` right GCRD.

I do **not** claim below that the rational parameters of `W` have already been solved. I give a finite exact inverse-creative-telescoping problem which determines them without numerical recognition. Once that polynomial system has a solution, (3) and the displayed WZ certificates are a complete analytic proof of the Catalan center.

## 1. The exact accelerated Catalan block

Write

\[
A_M=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}c_m,
\qquad A_0=0.
\]

Using

\[
\binom{2m}{m}=4^m\frac{(1/2)_m}{(1)_m},
\qquad
2m+1=\frac{(3/2)_m}{(1/2)_m},
\]

the summand is

\[
c_m=\left(1+\frac32m\right)
\left(-\frac18\right)^m
\frac{(1)_m^3}{(3/2)_m^3}.
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, if `theta = z d/dz`,

\[
G=\left.
\left(1+\frac32\theta\right)
{}_4F_3\!\left(\begin{matrix}1,1,1,1\\3/2,3/2,3/2\end{matrix};z\right)
\right|_{z=-1/8}.
\tag{5}
\]

Formula (1) is Theorem 1 of F. M. S. Lima, *A rapidly converging Ramanujan-type series for Catalan's constant*, arXiv:1207.3139. Lima derives it from a parameterized Guillera hypergeometric identity. Thus the appearance of `-1/8` is exact analytic structure, not a fit to the computed errors.

### 1.1 A positive beta-cube representation

Put

\[
U=x(1-x)y(1-y)z(1-z),
\qquad (x,y,z)\in[0,1]^3.
\]

The beta integral gives

\[
\int_0^1 x^m(1-x)^m\,dx
=\frac1{(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac1{(2m+1)^3\binom{2m}{m}^3}
=\int_{[0,1]^3}U^m\,dx\,dy\,dz.
\tag{6}
\]

Since `0 <= U <= 1/64`, the series below is uniformly absolutely convergent, and summing the elementary geometric derivative gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
G
&=\int_{[0,1]^3}
 \sum_{m\ge0}\frac{3m+2}{2}(-8U)^m\,dx\,dy\,dz\\
&=\int_{[0,1]^3}
 \frac{1-4U}{(1+8U)^2}\,dx\,dy\,dz.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The integrand in (7) is strictly positive, because `U <= 1/64`.

### 1.2 Exact signed tail

For every integer `M >= 0`, finite geometric summation gives

\[
\boxed{
G-A_M=(-1)^M\int_{[0,1]^3}
\frac{(8U)^M\bigl(3M+2+(24M-8)U\bigr)}
 {2(1+8U)^2}\,dx\,dy\,dz.}
\tag{8}
\]

The factor after `(-1)^M` is pointwise positive. For `M=0`, this follows from `2-8U>0`; for `M>=1`, every displayed coefficient is nonnegative and the constant term is positive. Hence

\[
(-1)^M(G-A_M)>0.
\tag{9}
\]

For `M>=1`, the same formula and `U<=1/64` give the explicit bound

\[
0<(-1)^M(G-A_M)
\le \frac{27M+15}{16\,8^M}.
\tag{10}
\]

This is the desired sign-bearing Catalan remainder. It is substantially better suited to the present recurrence than

\[
\int_0^1\frac{-\log t}{1+t^2}R_N(t^2)\,dt,
\]

whose polynomial factor has no pointwise sign.

## 2. The proposed common positive kernel

The order-eight/order-seven operator pattern suggests a common rank-seven bulk and one extra indefinite-summation direction. The smallest standard one-fold proper-hypergeometric class with a generic rank-seven contiguous module is a terminating `7F6` kernel.

Use the ansatz

\[
\boxed{
W(k,j)=
\frac{(-1)^j(-k-1)_j(k+\sigma)_j
      \prod_{r=1}^{5}(a_r)_j}
     {j!\prod_{r=1}^{6}(b_r)_j}
\frac{P(k,j)}{Q(k,j)},
\qquad 0\le j\le k+1.}
\tag{11}
\]

The unknowns satisfy

- `sigma, a_r, b_r` are rational;
- all `a_r,b_r,k+sigma` are positive on the summation range;
- `P,Q` are low-degree rational polynomials, initially set to `1`;
- `P(k,j)/Q(k,j)>0` for `k>=0`, `0<=j<=k+1`;
- `rho_{k+1}/rho_k` is rational in `k` and `rho_k>0`.

Because

\[
(-1)^j(-k-1)_j=\frac{(k+1)!}{(k+1-j)!}>0,
\]

all terms in (11) are positive under these conditions.

The concrete target is (3), repeated here:

```text
f_k = rho_k * Sum[ W(k,j),                    j=0..k+1 ]
g_k = rho_k * Sum[ W(k,j) * A_(k+2j+3),       j=0..k+1 ]
```

No transcendental constant occurs in either right-hand side: every `A_M` is rational. Catalan enters only when one subtracts the infinite sum and applies (8).

### 2.1 Why the index is `k+2j+3`

The factor `2j` parity-locks all tails. If

\[
m=k+2j+3,
\]

then `(-1)^m=(-1)^{k+3}` for every summand. Therefore (8) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
Gf_k-g_k
={}&(-1)^{k+3}\rho_k
\int_{[0,1]^3}\frac{1}{2(1+8U)^2}\\
&\quad\times
\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}W(k,j)(8U)^{k+2j+3}
\bigl(3m+2+(24m-8)U\bigr)
\,dx\,dy\,dz.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

Everything after `(-1)^{k+3}` is strictly positive. Thus the desired alternating behavior is not asymptotic or numerical:

\[
\boxed{(-1)^k\left(\frac{g_k}{f_k}-G\right)>0.}
\tag{13}
\]

Moreover, since `k+3 <= m <= 3k+5`, (10) and positivity of the weights imply the uniform bound

\[
\boxed{
0<(-1)^k\left(\frac{g_k}{f_k}-G\right)
\le
\frac{81k+150}{16\,8^{k+3}}.}
\tag{14}
\]

So any exact identity of the form (3) immediately proves the Catalan limit, with a much stronger quantitative estimate than is needed.

The limiting quotient `-1/8` then follows once the found kernel passes the elementary edge-dominance check: the normalized `j=0` mass must not be exponentially small, and the rational `k`-shift quotient of the edge must have limit `1`. Terms with `j>=1` carry an additional factor `8^{-2j}=64^{-j}` in (12), so this check is normally immediate from the factored term ratios of `W`.

### 2.2 The shift `3` is forced by the exact initial data

Let

\[
r_0=\frac{g_0}{f_0}=\frac{30921}{33750}.
\]

The smallest odd shift for which a positive two-point parity-locked average can equal `r_0` is `3`. Indeed, exact rational arithmetic gives

\[
A_5<r_0<A_3,
\]

whereas the pair `A_1,A_3` lies entirely above `r_0`. More sharply,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{30921}{33750}
=\frac{7832 A_3+27783 A_5}{35615}.}
\tag{15}
\]

Therefore a normalization consistent with (3) is

\[
W(0,0):W(0,1)=7832:27783,
\qquad
\rho_0=\frac{33750}{35615}.
\tag{16}
\]

Then (3) gives `f_0=33750` and `g_0=30921` exactly. This is an exact target-specific seed, not a decimal fit.

Equation (16), the terminating endpoint `j=k+1`, and the first-order boundary factors extracted from the Ore operators should determine the rational parameters in (11) very tightly.

## 3. Why the order-eight/order-seven pattern supports this ansatz

Let the exact annihilators be `L_f,L_g` and let their right GCRD be `D`, of order seven. Normalize in the shift Ore ring so that

\[
L_f=A_fD,
\qquad
L_g=A_gD,
\tag{17}
\]

with `A_f,A_g` first-order operators.

For a WZ representation, one should not expect `D` itself to annihilate the definite sum. Instead, the natural identity is

\[
D_k W(k,j)=\Delta_j\bigl(C_f(k,j)W(k,j)\bigr).
\tag{18}
\]

Summing (18) over `0<=j<=k+1` leaves a hypergeometric boundary term `B_f(k)`. The first-order factor `A_f` annihilates this boundary, giving

\[
A_fD f=0.
\]

For the numerator, put `m=k+2j+3`. The inserted sequence has the two first-order relations

\[
A_{m+1}=A_m+c_m,
\qquad
c_{m+1}=q(m)c_m,
\tag{19}
\]

where

\[
q(m)=-\frac{(m+1)^3(3m+5)}{(2m+3)^3(3m+2)}.
\tag{20}
\]

Thus `W(k,j)A_m` lives in a rank-two extension of the same hypergeometric summand module. Creative telescoping produces the same order-seven bulk `D` and a different hypergeometric boundary `B_g(k)`, killed by `A_g`.

This is exactly the observed algebraic architecture:

```text
common rank-seven summand module
        + one Catalan indefinite-sum direction
        + one boundary factor
     -> two order-eight operators with right GCRD of order seven.
```

By contrast, a bare identity `g_k=f_k A_{k+s}` has no room for an order-seven common bulk, and ordinary Euler partial sums have the wrong tail quotient.

## 4. Exact discovery by inverse creative telescoping

The search should be driven by the exact Ore operators, not by decimal values of `g_k/f_k`.

### Step 1: normalize and factor the known operators

Work over `QQ(k)<S_k>`.

1. Primitive-normalize `L_f,L_g`.
2. Compute their right GCRD `D` and exact left quotients `A_f,A_g`.
3. Apply `D` to the exact sequences `f,g`; the results must satisfy first-order recurrences.
4. Factor the rational quotients
   
   \[
   \frac{(Df)_{k+1}}{(Df)_k},
   \qquad
   \frac{(Dg)_{k+1}}{(Dg)_k}.
   \]
   
   Their affine zeros and poles identify the endpoint Pochhammer factors and the external gauge `rho_k`.

This is the highest-leverage computation. An order-seven GCRD with first-order quotients contains much more structural information than hundreds of raw terms.

### Step 2: solve the inverse WZ equation for `W`

For (11), the `j`-quotient is

\[
\frac{W(k,j+1)}{W(k,j)}
=
\frac{(k+1-j)(k+\sigma+j)\prod_{r=1}^{5}(j+a_r)}
 {(j+1)\prod_{r=1}^{6}(j+b_r)}
\frac{P(k,j+1)Q(k,j)}{P(k,j)Q(k,j+1)}.
\tag{21}
\]

Its `k`-quotient is also rational. Insert these quotients into (18), take

\[
C_f(k,j)=\frac{N_f(k,j)}{D_f(k,j)}
\]

with denominator supported on the visible shift factors, clear denominators, and compare polynomial coefficients in `k,j`.

Search in the following order:

1. `P=Q=1`;
2. a linear rational prefactor;
3. a quadratic rational prefactor.

Do not enlarge to a double sum until these three exact systems are inconsistent. The initial constraints (16) should be imposed from the outset.

The output must be an identity in `QQ[k,j]` after denominator clearing. That identity, not a guessed recurrence, is the certificate.

### Step 3: telescope the Catalan-inserted summand

Use the module basis

```text
e0(k,j) = W(k,j)
e1(k,j) = W(k,j) * A_(k+2j+3)
e2(k,j) = W(k,j) * c_(k+2j+3)
```

and reduce every `k`- or `j`-shift with (19)-(21). Chyzak-style creative telescoping on this finite module seeks

\[
D_k e_1=\Delta_j(\text{certificate})+B_g(k,j),
\tag{22}
\]

with the summed boundary annihilated by `A_g`.

One should require, exactly:

- the bulk operator in (22) is the already computed `D` up to a unit;
- the first-order boundary annihilator is `A_g`;
- the denominator identity similarly gives `A_f`;
- the endpoint terms at `j=0` and `j=k+2` are evaluated explicitly.

### Step 4: identify the sequences without numerical recognition

Once the two WZ recurrences are proved, equality with the repository sequences follows from exact initial values. Since the operators have order eight, check `k=0,...,7` by rational arithmetic. No decimal value of Catalan and no floating root comparison enters.

## 6. Positivity and the final Catalan proof

After the WZ identities are found, the proof has four short parts.

### 6.1 Positivity

Factor every term quotient and prove

\[
\rho_k>0,
\qquad
W(k,j)>0
\]

on `k>=0`, `0<=j<=k+1`. Then (3) gives `f_k>0`. The accelerated partial sums `A_M` are also positive: this follows from the alternating decreasing series beginning with `c_0=1`, or directly from (8) and the elementary bound `0<G<1`.

### 6.2 Exact center and sign

Substitute (8) into (3) and obtain (12). The integrand is pointwise positive, proving (13). This is the analytic information the rational Ore module alone cannot supply.

### 6.3 Convergence

The positive-weight average and (10) give (14), hence

\[
\frac{g_k}{f_k}\longrightarrow G.
\]

No Poincare connection constant has to be identified.

### 6.4 The observed `-1/8`

Factor the exact edge ratios of the discovered kernel. Because every `j>=1` term in the error has the additional factor `64^{-j}`, the `j=0` edge determines the exponential quotient unless its mass is exponentially suppressed. The required check is rational and local; it should yield

\[
\frac{g_{k+1}/f_{k+1}-G}{g_k/f_k-G}\longrightarrow-\frac18.
\]

Thus the machine-observed quotient becomes a consequence of the certificate rather than an input used to identify a constant.

## 8. Why this route survives the previous no-go results

1. **It does not ask a rational projective cone to identify a transcendental center.** Catalan enters through the independently proved identity (1).

2. **It is not the failed ordinary Euler rectangle.** The inserted partial sums have exact tail quotient `-1/8`, matching the observed mode.

3. **It is not a bare Delannoy/Wilson embedding.** The Delannoy transform supplies the positive coefficient sequences; the Lima–Guillera tail supplies the missing marked solution.

4. **It repairs the sign failure of the affine remainder integral.** Formula (8) has a pointwise positive integrand after extracting the known parity.

5. **It explains the operator algebra.** A rank-seven hypergeometric bulk plus one partial-sum extension naturally gives order eight, while the shared bulk appears as the order-seven right GCRD.

6. **It avoids forbidden numerical identification.** The parameters are solved from exact Ore/WZ polynomial identities, and the final sequence match is by exact initial values.
