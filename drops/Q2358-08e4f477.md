# P3.2 critical obstacle: fixed-gap resultants are intrinsically nonuniform

## Verdict

The obstacle is **real**.  The gap-by-gap resultant argument proves a theorem with the quantifiers

\[
\forall h\quad \exists\text{ finite }E(h)\quad
\forall p\notin E(h),
\]

whereas the fixed-\(q\) application needs something like

\[
\exists p_0\quad \forall p>p_0\quad
\forall h\le \frac p2.
\]

Those quantifiers cannot be interchanged merely because every individual exceptional set is finite.  If the relevant saturated resultant at gap \(h\) has logarithmic height of order \(h^3\), then the elementary threshold obtained from its prime factors is exponentially larger than \(h\); at \(h\asymp p\), the inequality needed to put \(p\) beyond the exceptional set is in the wrong direction by an enormous margin.

There is one important nuance.  The fact that the largest prime factor of the moving resultant is much larger than \(p\) does **not** prove that the diagonal prime \(p\) actually divides that resultant.  It proves that a size argument cannot exclude it.  Thus this is a genuine obstruction to the **method**, not evidence that the desired nonvanishing theorem is false.

The three proposed escapes have the following status.

| escape | status | precise verdict |
|---|---|---|
| (a) force \(h=O(\log p)\) from physical zeros | **not viable as stated** | Literally false because reflection produces actual mate pairs with \(h\asymp p\).  After excluding mates, rarity of zeros gives no upper bound on their separation and heuristically predicts linear, not logarithmic, gaps. |
| (b) control resultant primes by Apéry quantities | **naive form false; strengthened form is the best live route** | The first nontrivial associated resultant already has phantom primes not supported by small Apéry values.  What could work is an endpoint-conditioned Bézout/Casoratian identity whose right side is a known unit, not a factorization of the same large integer resultant. |
| (c) sum gap-resultant heights directly into \(\log G_n\) | **not viable with present heights** | Even under the optimistic assumption of at most one prime per gap, \(\sum_{h\le H}O(h^3)=O(H^4)\).  With \(H\asymp n\) this is vastly larger than \(o(n)\).  The argument is useful only for a short-gap truncation unless a new cross-gap compression theorem is proved. |

So the most promising way around the obstacle is a sharpened version of **(b)**:

> Replace the scalar resultant by a uniform, localized two-recurrence Wronskian or Bézout certificate that becomes a unit on the physical endpoint-zero locus.

That would remove the dependence on the size of the gap entirely.  No such all-gap certificate is currently proved.

## 1. Exact fixed-gap reduction and the quantifier failure

Write \(s=r+h\).  The Racah difference equation from Q2337 gives, when \(b_r=0\),

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)
=-\frac{r^3b_{r-1}}{6(r+1)_h^3}\,
\mathcal R_{h-1}(r),
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

where \(\mathcal R_m(r)\) is the Racah gap continuant

\[
\mathcal R_{-1}=0,
\qquad
\mathcal R_0=1,
\]

\[
\mathcal R_m(r)
=Q_m(r)\mathcal R_{m-1}(r)
 -(r+m)^6\mathcal R_{m-2}(r),
\tag{1.2}
\]

with

\[
Q_m(r)
=(2r+2m+1)
 \bigl(3r^2+2rm+m^2+3r+m+1\bigr).
\tag{1.3}
\]

In the nonwrapping range \(0<r<r+h<p\), the factors in (1.1) are units once \(b_r=0\): no two consecutive Apéry values vanish, so \(b_{r-1}\ne0\).  Hence

\[
K(r,r+h)=0
\iff
\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)=0
\pmod p.
\tag{1.4}
\]

On the other hand, the Green-kernel/Casoratian identity in `gap_kernel_green.tex` gives

\[
\frac{N_h(r)}{\prod_{j=1}^{h}(r+j)^3}
=b_rc_{r+h}-c_rb_{r+h}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Thus a physical zero pair satisfies

\[
b_r=b_{r+h}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_h(r)=0\pmod p.
\tag{1.6}
\]

A nonmate counterexample to the desired kernel theorem therefore forces

\[
\boxed{
N_h(r)=0,
\qquad
\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)=0
\pmod p.
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

After removing the known reflection-mate factor when present and saturating the obvious contents/leading coefficients, let

\[
\mathfrak R_h
=\operatorname{Res}_X
 \bigl(N_h^{\mathrm{sat}}(X),
       \mathcal R_{h-1}^{\mathrm{sat}}(X)\bigr)
\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.
\tag{1.8}
\]

For a fixed \(h\), (1.7) implies

\[
p\mid \mathfrak R_h
\]

apart from the explicitly saturated small-characteristic factors.  Therefore fixed \(h\) leaves only finitely many primes.  This is fully legitimate and already closes \(h=1,2,3\).

The problem is that the statement is not uniform in \(h\).  The fixed-gap proof has the logical form

\[
\forall h\ \forall p>P(h):\quad
\text{no counterexample at gap }h,
\tag{1.9}
\]

with a threshold such as

\[
P(h)=P^+(\mathfrak R_h),
\]

where \(P^+\) is the largest prime factor.  If

\[
\log P(h)\asymp h^3,
\]

then at \(h\asymp p/2\), the condition \(p>P(h)\) is impossible to verify by size.  What is actually needed is the diagonal nondivisibility theorem

\[
\boxed{
p\nmid\mathfrak R_h
\quad\text{for every relevant pair }(p,h),\ h\le p/2,}
\tag{1.10}
\]

and (1.10) is arithmetic information that the height of \(\mathfrak R_h\) does not provide.

Q2355 is a good illustration of both the strength and the limitation.  At gap \(3\), a Q2355 Euclidean reduction gives

\[
N_3-\mathcal R_2
=4(2r+3)(2r+5)(r+2)(r+3)
 (70r^2+219r+173),
\tag{1.11}
\]

so in the first-half range the linear factors are units and the common-root problem drops to a quadratic obstruction.  This produces the finite exceptional set \(\{2,5,7,61,73\}\) and closes gap \(3\).  It does not yet give a bounded-complexity remainder chain for arbitrary \(h\).  Without such a uniform Euclidean collapse, the same quantifier problem returns.

## 2. Escape (a): physical zero pairs do not have logarithmic gaps

### 2.1 The literal statement is false because of reflection

The Apéry reflection law is

\[
b_{p-1-r}=b_r\pmod p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Hence every noncentral zero automatically gives the actual zero pair

\[
r,\quad p-1-r
\]

with gap

\[
h=p-1-2r.
\tag{2.2}
\]

This gap can be a positive proportion of \(p\).  The exact example already recorded in Q2195 is

\[
p=41,\qquad r=10,\qquad s=30,\qquad h=20,
\]

with \(b_{10}=b_{30}=0\) and \(r+s+1=p\).  Thus no theorem of the form

\[
\text{every actual zero pair has }h=O(\log p)
\]

can be true without first quotienting out reflection mates.

### 2.2 Rarity controls cardinality, not diameter

Suppose one keeps only lower-half representatives of reflection orbits.  Even then, a bound

\[
|Z_p|=O(1)
\]

would not imply that two representatives are close.  A set of two points in an interval of length \(p/2\) can have separation \(\asymp p\).  In fact the random sparse-set heuristic points in the opposite direction: conditional on two independent lower-half zero orbits, their typical separation is linear in \(p\), not logarithmic.

The million-prime computation in the repository strongly supports small average cardinality, but this is evidence for **few** zeros, not for a short interval containing all zeros.  No existing recurrence, reflection, or no-consecutive-zero argument converts small cardinality into a bound on the largest gap.

The active actual-zero statements in `AVENUES.md` are correspondingly short-window statements of scale \(\sqrt p\) for selected quadruples.  Even those require genuinely Apéry-specific input.  They do not imply that every pair of zero orbits is separated by \(O(\log p)\).

### 2.3 The only potentially useful reformulation is essentially the target itself

One could ask for the weaker-looking assertion

\[
b_r=b_{r+h}=K(r,r+h)=0,\quad
2r+h+1\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h\le H(p).
\tag{2.3}
\]

This restricts only **dangerous triple collisions**, not all zero pairs.  If one could prove (2.3) with a sufficiently small \(H(p)\), then fixed-gap resultants could handle the remaining range.

But (2.3) is already a strong collision-rigidity theorem.  The computational observation that the only known collision is the mate \((41,10,30)\) suggests the sharper desired statement

\[
b_r=b_s=K(r,s)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r+s+1=p,
\tag{2.4}
\]

not a logarithmic gap bound.  Proving (2.4) is essentially the all-gap theorem one is trying to obtain; it is not a free preliminary reduction.

Therefore escape (a) does not currently shorten the proof.  Its literal form is false, and its nonmate form is at least as deep as the target.

## 3. Escape (b): prime-support control is false, but a relative unit certificate could work

### 3.1 A concrete obstruction at gap \(3\)

Q2355 computes

\[
\operatorname{Res}(N_3,\mathcal R_2)
=-2^{18}5^7\,7\,61^2\,73^3.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The prime \(61\) is a genuine common-root prime for the two continuants: the common roots are \(r=8,49\pmod{61}\).  But they are **phantom** from the physical endpoint perspective:

\[
(b_8,b_{11})\equiv(12,7)\pmod{61},
\]

and the reflected pair gives the reversed residues.  Moreover

\[
b_1=5,\qquad b_2=73,\qquad b_3=1445,
\]

so \(61\) divides none of the first three positive Apéry numbers.  Thus even the simple claim

\[
p\mid\mathfrak R_h
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid\prod_{j\le h}b_j
\tag{3.2}
\]

is already false at \(h=3\).

The broader separated-continuant census in `meso_resultants.md` points in the same direction.  Among the prime factors at most \(5000\), most are in neither the tested Apéry support nor the tested Pell support; stripping all such known supports explains only about \(17.5\%\) of the aggregate logarithmic height.  Large residual cofactors remain.

The full Racah-polynomial resultants have the same contamination phenomenon.  Q2310 records, for example, large primes \(199\) and \(359\) arising from common roots unrelated to the selected Hasse nodes.  A scalar resultant sees **every** common root over the algebraic closure, not just the physical root selected by \(b_r=b_s=0\).

### 3.2 Even perfect support containment would not by itself give incompatibility

Suppose optimistically that one proved

\[
p\mid\mathfrak R_h
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid B_h
\]

for some explicit product of Apéry values \(B_h\).  This still would not contradict

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad p\mid b_{r+h}.
\]

A prime may divide several Apéry values.  To finish, one needs an actual **unit relation** showing that the extra divisor is incompatible with the endpoint-zero locus.  Prime-support containment alone is too weak.

### 3.3 The viable strengthened form of (b)

The right all-gap target is a relative Bézout/Casoratian identity of the schematic form

\[
\boxed{
A_h(r)N_h(r)
+B_h(r)\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)
+C_h(r)b_r
+D_h(r)b_{r+h}
=U_h(r),
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

where every denominator in the coefficients and the right-hand side is built only from quantities known to be units in the nonwrapping nonmate range, for example

\[
2,\quad r,r+1,\ldots,r+h,\quad b_{r-1},\quad c_r,\quad 2r+h+1.
\]

At a physical zero pair with \(K(r,r+h)=0\), the left side of (3.3) would vanish, while \(U_h(r)\) would be a unit.  This would give an immediate contradiction **uniformly in \(h\) and \(p\)**.

The ordinary scalar resultant is the same kind of identity with right side equal to the integer \(\mathfrak R_h\).  Its defect is precisely that \(\mathfrak R_h\) need not be a unit modulo the moving prime.  Thus the required improvement is qualitative, not merely a sharper estimate of the same resultant.

### 3.4 Exact two-recurrence Wronskian residual

There is a concrete all-gap algebraic object to attack.  Put

\[
A_m(r):=N_{m+1}(r),
\qquad
B_m(r):=\mathcal R_m(r),
\]

with

\[
A_{-1}=B_{-1}=0,\qquad A_0=B_0=1.
\]

They satisfy recurrences with the same off-diagonal coefficient:

\[
A_m=P(r+m)A_{m-1}-(r+m)^6A_{m-2},
\]

\[
B_m=Q_m(r)B_{m-1}-(r+m)^6B_{m-2}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Define the mixed discrete Wronskian

\[
W_m=A_mB_{m-1}-A_{m-1}B_m.
\tag{3.5}
\]

A direct subtraction gives

\[
\boxed{
W_m
=\delta_m(r)A_{m-1}B_{m-1}
 +(r+m)^6W_{m-1},
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\delta_m(r)
&=P(r+m)-Q_m(r)\\
&=2(2r+2m+1)
 \bigl(7r^2+16rm+8m^2+7r+8m+2\bigr).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Unrolling (3.6),

\[
\boxed{
W_m
=\sum_{j=1}^{m}
 \delta_j(r)A_{j-1}(r)B_{j-1}(r)
 \prod_{t=j+1}^{m}(r+t)^6.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

A terminal common zero at gap \(h\) has

\[
A_{h-1}=N_h=0,\qquad
B_{h-1}=\mathcal R_{h-1}=0,
\]

so \(W_{h-1}=0\), and therefore the right side of (3.8) vanishes.  This is an exact all-gap obstruction, but it is not yet a contradiction: the summands can cancel in \(\mathbf F_p\), and the quadratic factor in \(\delta_j\) can itself vanish.

Q2355's gap-3 factorization (1.11) is a small instance in which a Euclidean/Wronskian difference collapses, after dividing known units, to a low-degree obstruction.  A viable all-gap theorem would show that an iterated saturated remainder chain of this type either

1. reaches a known unit on the physical endpoint locus, or
2. forces descent to a shorter common zero, ultimately to the already-closed adjacent case.

That is the precise residual for escape (b).  It is a genuinely new theorem, but unlike a raw height estimate it would solve the scaling problem at its source.

## 4. Escape (c): direct summation of resultant heights is far too expensive

Let \(\mathcal B_n\) be the set of bad primes contributing to the large-prime part of \(G_n\), and suppose that to each \(p\in\mathcal B_n\) one can assign a dangerous gap \(h(p)\le H_n\) such that

\[
p\mid\mathfrak R_{h(p)}.
\]

Then, grouping by gap,

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal B_n}\log p
\le
\sum_{h\le H_n}
 \log\operatorname{rad}|\mathfrak R_h|,
\tag{4.1}
\]

provided one chooses one witnessing gap for each bad prime.  This is the correct general height inequality.

The proposed replacement

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}|\mathfrak R_h|
\quad\text{by}\quad
\log P^+(\mathfrak R_h)
\]

is valid only if at most one relevant prime is charged to each gap.  If several bad primes divide the same resultant, the largest prime factor does not bound their total logarithmic contribution.  Even granting the optimistic one-prime-per-gap assumption does not save the method.

Assume optimistically that

\[
\log P^+(\mathfrak R_h)\ll h^3
\]

or even the stronger radical-height bound

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}|\mathfrak R_h|
\ll h^3(\log h)^A.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{h\le H_n}
 \log\operatorname{rad}|\mathfrak R_h|
\ll
H_n^4(\log H_n)^A.
\tag{4.3}
\]

In the fixed-\(q\) slice, \(p\asymp n/q\) and the possible gap is \(H_n\asymp p\asymp n\) for fixed \(q\).  Formula (4.3) is then of order \(n^4\), not \(o(n)\).  Even one long gap \(h\asymp n\) is represented by an integer certificate with logarithmic height \(\asymp n^3\), whereas the actual prime contributes only \(O(\log n)\) to \(\log G_n\).  The resultant carrier loses far too much information.

This calculation also identifies the limited regime where the method can help.  Under (4.2):

- if all gaps up to \(H_n\) are included, the total resultant height is \(o(n)\) only when roughly
  \[
  H_n=o\bigl(n^{1/4}(\log n)^{-A/4}\bigr);
  \]
- if a separate argument leaves only one selected gap, a single resultant has height \(o(n)\) only when roughly
  \[
  H_n=o\bigl(n^{1/3}(\log n)^{-A/3}\bigr).
  \]

Thus fixed-gap resultants can legitimately bank a bounded or slowly growing **short-gap range**.  They cannot cover \(h\asymp p\) through height alone.

One could hope that the radicals of many \(\mathfrak R_h\) overlap so strongly that

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}
 \prod_{h\le H}\mathfrak R_h
=o(H)
\tag{4.4}
\]

despite the individual heights.  That would indeed make a direct all-index strategy viable.  But (4.4) is a new cross-gap compression theorem, not a consequence of fixed-gap finiteness.  The exact mesoscopic computations show many residual primes after structural saturation, and `AVENUES.md` records that generic product-formula, Fitting, Smith, large-sieve, and block-determinant compression do not remove a full gap parameter without an Apéry-specific cross-gap identity.

The prime-first reduction in `UNDERSTANDING.md` makes the strategic mismatch clear.  The banked large-prime target is a weighted count of primes whose residue lies in the actual zero set.  The observed rarity of zeros is potentially useful there because it controls the number of actual prime events.  Replacing each event by divisibility of an enormous moving resultant discards that sparsity and replaces an \(O(\log p)\) event by an \(O(h^3)\) height certificate.

Therefore escape (c) fails in its proposed raw form.  It can only be one half of a hybrid short-gap/long-gap argument.

## 5. What is actually viable

### 5.1 Bank the fixed-gap theorem, but do not overstate it

The resultant argument is a real theorem schema:

- gaps \(1,2,3\) are closed;
- every fixed finite collection of gaps can be closed after finitely many exact exceptional-prime checks;
- with explicit uniform height estimates, one may allow a slowly growing short-gap cutoff.

This is useful local progress.  It does not imply any statement for all \(h\le p/2\).

### 5.2 Best algebraic route: an endpoint-relative all-gap certificate

The strongest viable target is (3.3), or an equivalent descent theorem for the Wronskian (3.6).  A sufficient statement would be:

> In the localization where \(2\), all \(r+j\), \(b_{r-1}\), \(c_r\), and the nonmate factor \(2r+h+1\) are inverted, the ideal generated by the physical endpoint equations, \(N_h(r)\), and \(\mathcal R_{h-1}(r)\) is the unit ideal, uniformly in \(h\).

This formulation uses the physical zero conditions rather than every algebraic common root.  It automatically ignores the phantom roots responsible for primes such as \(61\) at gap \(3\), and it has no growing integer exceptional set.

### 5.3 Best long-gap analytic/geometric route: keep the Green coefficient index visible

`gap_kernel_green.tex` realizes the long-gap continuant as a coefficient of a fixed-order inhomogeneous differential system.  Its concluding “jet barrier” is exactly the present scaling issue: the differential operator has fixed rank, but the bridge length remains the coefficient index.  A successful long-gap theorem would require either

- a fixed-dimensional arithmetic realization of those two-index coefficients, or
- prime-averaged/pointwise nonconcentration for the Green coefficients.

That is a more faithful long-gap problem than bounding the height of a degree-\(O(h)\) resultant.

### 5.4 Natural hybrid split

A realistic architecture is:

1. choose a short-gap cutoff \(H(n)\) small enough that the summed saturated resultant height is \(o(n)\);
2. eliminate all dangerous pairs with \(h\le H(n)\) by exact resultants;
3. handle \(h>H(n)\) by a prime-first actual-zero dispersion theorem, a Green-kernel coefficient theorem, or the uniform relative Wronskian certificate.

Without step 3, changing the cutoff only moves the unresolved mass.

## 6. Final assessment of the three escapes

### (a) Physical gaps are logarithmic

**No.**  Reflection makes the literal statement false, and after quotienting by reflection, zero rarity does not control separation.  A theorem restricting only nonmate triple collisions would be useful, but it is essentially the desired collision-rigidity theorem.

### (b) Resultant primes are Apéry-controlled

**Not in the simple prime-support sense.**  Gap \(3\) already supplies the phantom prime \(61\), and larger continuant-resultant data contain extensive residual support.  The viable replacement is much stronger and more local: a Bézout/Casoratian identity whose right side is a guaranteed unit on the endpoint-zero locus.  This is the best of the three directions.

### (c) Sum resultant heights directly into \(\log G_n\)

**No with the current growth.**  The correct carrier is the radical of the product, not merely the largest prime factor, and even the optimistic one-prime-per-gap cost is \(H^4\) when \(\log P^+(\mathfrak R_h)\asymp h^3\).  It works only for a short-gap truncation unless a new cross-gap radical-compression theorem is proved.

Hence the scaling obstacle is not cosmetic.  It marks the exact boundary between a valid fixed-gap theorem and the missing all-gap theorem.  The next useful attack should target the shared-off-diagonal two-recurrence structure—especially the discrete Wronskian (3.6) and Q2355-style saturated Euclidean descent—rather than trying to make the moving scalar resultants small enough by height estimates.
