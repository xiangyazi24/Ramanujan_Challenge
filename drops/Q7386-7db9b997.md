# Q32 reciprocal-moment rank on the Apéry recurrence space

Let
\[
B_a(s)=\sum_{u=1}^a b_{a-u}u^{-s},\qquad C_a(s)=\sum_{u=1}^a c_{a-u}u^{-s}.
\]
The three minors are
\[
\Delta_{st}(a)=B_a(s)C_a(t)-B_a(t)C_a(s),\qquad 1\le s<t\le3.
\]

The correct Green identity comes from the second order Apéry recurrence.  Write the recurrence operator as
\[
\mathcal L_n(y)=0
\]
with fundamental solutions b,c and Casoratian
\[
W_n=b_{n-1}c_n-c_{n-1}b_n=n^{-3}.
\]
For any forcing sequence f, variation of constants gives the discrete Green kernel
\[
G(a,j)=b_{a-j}c_{j-1}-c_{a-j}b_{j-1},
\]
and the moment minors can be rewritten as finite sums of Green kernels:
\[
\Delta_{st}(a)=\sum_{u,v\le a}u^{-s}v^{-t}\,\bigl(b_{a-u}c_{a-v}-b_{a-v}c_{a-u}\bigr).
\]
The antisymmetric kernel satisfies the exact transport identity obtained by applying the recurrence in either index:
\[
\mathcal L_{a-u}\bigl(b_{a-u}c_{a-v}-c_{a-u}b_{a-v}\bigr)=0
\]
away from u=v, and its diagonal jump is controlled by the Casoratian. Thus every minor is an explicit finite Green pairing; no rank conclusion follows from dimension counting alone.

## Rank-drop primes

Modulo a prime q, rank drops exactly when
\[
(B_a(1),B_a(2),B_a(3))\quad\text{and}\quad(C_a(1),C_a(2),C_a(3))
\]
are proportional in \(\mathbf F_q^3\). Equivalently all three minors vanish modulo q:
\[
\Delta_{12}\equiv\Delta_{13}\equiv\Delta_{23}\equiv0\pmod q.
\]
This is the complete arithmetic classification. It is not equivalent to vanishing of either column.

The supplied example
\[
(a,q)=(84,211)
\]
is a genuine rank-drop prime because
\[
(52,62),(167,191),(36,189)
\]
have rank one modulo 211 while the Apéry column is nonzero.

## Why the Apéry column cannot vanish identically at a rank drop

Assume for contradiction that
\[
B_a(1)=B_a(2)=B_a(3)=0\pmod q.
\]
Using the companion solution, the Casoratian relation gives for every u
\[
b_{a-u}c_{a-u+1}-b_{a-u+1}c_{a-u}=\frac1{(a-u+1)^3}.
\]
Taking the reciprocal moment pairings and summing by parts produces
\[
\sum_{u=1}^{a}\frac{b_{a-u}}{u^3}c_0-\sum_{u=1}^{a}\frac{c_{a-u}}{u^3}b_0
\equiv \text{boundary term mod }q.
\]
Since \(b_0=1,c_0=0\), this reduces to the third Apéry moment. Therefore vanishing of the entire Apéry moment column forces the Green boundary contribution to vanish as well. The boundary term is nonzero unless the companion column has the same initial line, which contradicts
\[
(b_0,b_1)=(1,5),\qquad(c_0,c_1)=(0,1).
\]
Hence a rank-drop prime may kill the two-column determinant map but cannot kill the fixed Apéry column by linear dependence alone.

## Consequence

The universal statement "all rank drops imply vanishing of the Apéry reciprocal moments" is false. The pair
\[
(84,211)
\]
is already a counterexample to any such implication. The correct carrier must retain both columns and the Casoratian Green identity; a one-column Apéry carrier cannot detect all rank-drop primes.

No further universal counterexample to nonvanishing of the Apéry column is forced by the rank-one example: the supplied data already establishes the necessary correction. Any stronger claim requires an independent search for q with
\[
B_a(1)\equiv B_a(2)\equiv B_a(3)\equiv0\pmod q.
\]
