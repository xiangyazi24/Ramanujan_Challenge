# Q7208: Correct far-range resultant formulation and applicability audit

## 1. Correct far separated-energy target

Let $H=\lfloor\sqrt{2X}\rfloor$. The canonical separated pair reduction gives a tuple
\[
(p,x,s,G),\qquad s\le H,
\]
with
\[
N_s(x)=0\pmod p,\qquad N_G(x+s)=0\pmod p,
\]
and
\[
0\le x<x+s+G<p.
\]
For the far range we take
\[
G>G_0:=H/\sqrt{\log X},
\]
with no upper bound smaller than $p$; in particular $G\le 2X$ in a dyadic prime interval.

The resultant certificate is
\[
R_{s,G}=\operatorname{Res}_T(N_s(T),N_G(T+s)).
\]
Since the two polynomials have no common characteristic-zero root (by the separated root-strip argument),
\[
R_{s,G}\ne0.
\]
Every canonical tuple for a prime $p$ contributes
\[
p\mid R_{s,G}.
\]
Therefore the exact implication needed is
\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}E_{\rm far}(p)\log X
\le
\sum_{s\le H}\sum_{G_0<G\le2X}\log |R_{s,G}|.
\]
This is the correct two-parameter height inequality.

However, the trivial height estimate gives
\[
\log |R_{s,G}|
\ll sG\log(s+G),
\]
so summing gives
\[
\sum_{s\le H}\sum_{G\le2X}sG\log X
\ll XH^2X^{o(1)}
= X^2X^{o(1)}?\n
\]
More explicitly,
\[
\sum_{s\le H}s\sum_{G\le2X}G
\asymp H^2X^2,
\]
so with $H^2\asymp X$ this is
\[
X^3X^{o(1)},
\]
which misses the desired
\[
X^{2+o(1)}
\]
raw height by a factor $X$.

Hence the exact sufficient arithmetic statement is:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s\le H}\sum_{G_0<G\le2X}
\log |R_{s,G}|_{\rm effective}
\ll X^{2+o(1)}.
}
\]

Equivalently, after the $p$-adic valuation step,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X}E_{\rm far}(p)
\ll \frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
}
\]

## 2. What large-prime-factor information would have to prove

The needed result is not a statement about the largest prime divisor of one resultant. It is a dyadic weighted support estimate:

For the set
\[
\mathcal R_X=\{R_{s,G}:s\le H,\ G_0<G\le2X\},
\]
we need
\[
\sum_{R\in\mathcal R_X}\sum_{X<p\le2X}v_p(R)\log p
\ll X^{2+o(1)}.
\]

A theorem controlling only
\[
P^+(R),
\]
the largest prime factor, or only the smooth part
\[
R_{\le X^\alpha},
\]
does not imply this. The relevant primes are exactly those in the dyadic interval $(X,2X]$, and multiplicity matters.

Known large-prime-factor/smooth-number results do not directly apply because:

1. The family is two-parameter with $G$ growing to order $X$.
2. The degree of $N_G$ is $3(G-1)$, hence the resultant family has conductor/degree growing with $G$.
3. Standard polynomial large sieve statements require fixed degree or explicitly controlled conductor; those hypotheses fail here.
4. A bound on the distribution of prime divisors of individual integers is weaker than a simultaneous bound over $O(HX)$ resultants.

Therefore no standard large-prime-factor theorem can currently be inserted without a new uniform theorem for this Apéry resultant family.

## 3. Why carrier-deflated height lower bounds are irrelevant

Let $R^*_{s,G}$ denote a carrier/center-deflated integer. Removing factors gives
\[
R_{s,G}=D_{s,G}R^*_{s,G},
\]
where $D_{s,G}$ contains known forced factors.

A lower bound such as
\[
\log |R^*_{s,G}|\ge c sG
\]
or even an asymptotic equality only proves that the remaining integer is large.

The energy argument needs an upper bound:
\[
\sum\log |R^*_{s,G}|.
\]
A lower bound cannot improve this inequality. It also cannot obstruct it, because a large integer may have very few prime factors in $(X,2X]$.

For example, an integer of size $e^{sG}$ supported entirely on primes below $X$ contributes zero to the dyadic valuation sum, while a much smaller integer may contain a large dyadic prime. Size and relevant prime support are different quantities.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{height lower bounds neither prove nor disprove the far-bridge estimate.}
}
\]

## 4. Exact conclusion

The far-range problem is not a missing carrier-deflation argument. The correct remaining lemma is an arithmetic distribution statement:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s\le\sqrt{2X}}
\sum_{\sqrt{2X}/\sqrt{\log X}<G\le2X}
\sum_{X<p\le2X}
 v_p(R_{s,G})\log p
\ll X^{2+o(1)}.
}
\]

No currently standard resultant-height, smooth-number, or large-prime-factor theorem applies because the family has parameter-dependent degree $\asymp G$.

A successful proof must exploit additional Apéry arithmetic: cancellation, projective mixing, aligned three-return information, or a genuinely uniform theorem for the long-return resultant family. Generic height theory stops exactly one factor $X$ short.
