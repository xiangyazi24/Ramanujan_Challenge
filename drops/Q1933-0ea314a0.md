# P3.2 exact determinant factorization

I read the current default-branch state at commit `d5e0aab63fb456b53f04b8608a57fcab99a7e91d` (`Add Q1779 answer`). In particular, `drops/Q1691-19ba7455.md` records the normalized half-shift Apéry recurrence and endpoint functional, while `drops/Q1779-cc11a9ab.md` uses the endpoint functional \(u_{m-1}-5u_m\) and explicitly distinguishes that endpoint determinant from the separate reciprocal-sum obstruction. The argument below is exact over \(\mathbf F_p\); no finite-prime examples are used.

## Verdict

With the standard domain basis

\[
(1,t^2,t^4,\ldots,t^{2m-2})
\]

and the standard target basis of \(\mathbf F_p^m\), put

\[
\Delta_m:=\prod_{1\le i<j\le m}(j^2-i^2).
\]

Then \(\Delta_m\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), and the exact determinant is

\[
\boxed{
\det T_m
=
\frac{(-1)^m}{4}\,\Delta_m
\left(\prod_{t=2}^m\frac{t^2-1}{8t}\right)X_{m+1}.
}
\]

For the project continuant normalization

\[
X_0=1,\qquad X_1=0,
\]
\[
X_{t+1}=4t(68t^2+3)X_t-(2t-1)^6X_{t-1},
\]

and

\[
e_m=(2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m,
\]

one has, in \(\mathbf F_p\) with \(p=2m+1\),

\[
\boxed{X_{m+1}=8e_m.}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\det T_m
=(-1)^m\Delta_m\,
\frac{(m+1)(m-1)!}{8^{m-1}}\,e_m.
}
\]

If the capital normalization is

\[
E_m:=u_{m-1}-5u_m,
\qquad
u_t:=\frac{X_t}{d_t},
\qquad
d_1=1,\quad d_t=d_{t-1}(2t-1)^3,
\]

then

\[
e_m=d_mE_m,
\]

so equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\det T_m
=(-1)^m\Delta_m\,
\frac{(m+1)(m-1)!d_m}{8^{m-1}}\,E_m.
}
\]

All displayed prefactors are units in \(\mathbf F_p\). Thus the determinant is unit-equivalent to **both** endpoint normalizations; the raw tridiagonal continuant is \(X_{m+1}\), and over \(\mathbf F_p\)

```text
X_{m+1} = 8 e_m = 8 d_m E_m.
```

There is no additional determinant factor coming from the \(\delta_0\)-source scalar or from the reciprocal-sum quantity.

If \(e_m=0\) (equivalently \(E_m=0\)), then \(\ker T_m\) is one-dimensional. With the normalization \(C_*(0)=-1\), write the normalized recurrence solution as \(u_t=X_t/d_t\). Then

\[
C_*(\pm t)=\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}\qquad(2\le t\le m),
\]

and

\[
C_*(\pm1)
=
\frac12-\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}.
\]

It satisfies

\[
HC_*=-Q_{\rm center}\,\delta_0,
\]

with the exact source coefficient

\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm center}
=
\frac54+\frac12\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}.
}
\]

Equivalently, if \(k\) is the even cyclic kernel vector

\[
k_0=-1,\qquad k_{\pm1}=3,\qquad
k_{\pm t}=\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}\quad(2\le t\le m),
\]

then

\[
q:=\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}k_a
=5+2\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1},
\qquad
Q_{\rm center}=\frac q4,
\]

and

\[
C_*=k-\frac q2(\delta_1+\delta_{-1}).
\]

This is the precise distinction between the cyclic even kernel and the kernel of \(T_m\): the latter is the unique mean-zero even correction of \(k\). It coincides with \(k\) exactly when \(q=0\), in which case the \(\delta_0\) source also vanishes.

## 1. Evaluation coordinates and the Vandermonde unit

Let

\[
V=\{C(t)\in\mathbf F_p[t]: C(-t)=C(t),\ \deg C\le p-3=2m-2\}.
\]

The evaluation map

\[
\operatorname{ev}:V\longrightarrow\mathbf F_p^m,
\qquad
C\longmapsto (C(1),\ldots,C(m))
\]

has matrix

\[
W=(i^{2j})_{1\le i\le m,\ 0\le j\le m-1}
\]

in the monomial basis \(1,t^2,\ldots,t^{2m-2}\). Hence

\[
\det W=\Delta_m
=\prod_{1\le i<j\le m}(j^2-i^2).
\]

This is a unit: if \(j^2=i^2\pmod p\), then \(p\mid(j-i)(j+i)\), but

\[
0<j-i<p,\qquad 0<j+i\le 2m-1=p-2,
\]

which is impossible.

There is also one useful exact relation among the values. For every \(C\in V\),

\[
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}C(a)=0.
\]

Indeed each monomial \(t^{2r}\) occurring in \(C\) has exponent \(0\le2r\le p-3\), and its sum over \(\mathbf F_p\) is zero. Therefore, writing

\[
y_j:=C(j)\qquad(1\le j\le m),
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{C(0)=-2\sum_{j=1}^m y_j.}
\]

Thus positive-point values are legitimate coordinates on \(V\).

Let \(M\) be the matrix of \(T_m\) in these value coordinates, so the matrix in the monomial basis is \(MW\), and

\[
\det T_m=(\det M)\Delta_m.
\]

---

## 2. The first column separates exactly

Write

\[
a_t=(t+\tfrac12)^3(t+2),
\qquad
b_t=(34t^2+\tfrac32)(t^2-1),
\qquad
c_t=(t-\tfrac12)^3(t-2),
\]

so

\[
(HC)(t)=a_tC(t+1)-b_tC(t)+c_tC(t-1).
\]

At \(t=1\),

\[
a_1=\frac{81}{8},\qquad b_1=0,\qquad c_1=-\frac18.
\]

Using \(C(0)=-2\sum_jy_j\),

\[
(HC)(1)
=\frac{81}{8}y_2-\frac18C(0)
=\frac14y_1+\frac{83}{8}y_2
 +\frac14\sum_{j=3}^m y_j.
\]

For every row \(t\ge2\), the coefficient of \(y_1\) is zero. At \(t=2\) this is the exact zero

\[
c_2=(\tfrac32)^3(2-2)=0,
\]

and for \(t\ge3\) the three-point stencil never reaches \(1\).

Hence \(M\) has the block form

\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac14 & *\\
0&B
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where \(B\) is indexed by rows and columns \(2,\ldots,m\). Consequently

\[
\boxed{\det M=\frac14\det B.}
\]

This is the key exact column separation; the dense contribution caused by \(C(0)=-2\sum y_j\) affects only the starred part and therefore does not enter the determinant.

At the right endpoint, \(m+\tfrac12=p/2=0\) in \(\mathbf F_p\), so \(a_m=0\). Thus \(B\) is the ordinary tridiagonal matrix

\[
B_{t,t}=-b_t,
\qquad
B_{t,t+1}=a_t\quad(2\le t<m),
\qquad
B_{t,t-1}=c_t\quad(3\le t\le m).
\]

No cyclic corner remains in this even positive-half block.

## 3. Exact column and row rescaling to the continuant

For \(2\le t\le m\), put

\[
z_t=(t^2-1)y_t.
\]

All \(t^2-1\) here are units: \(t\not\equiv\pm1\pmod p\) for \(2\le t\le m\). Let

\[
S=\operatorname{diag}(t^2-1)_{t=2}^m,
\qquad
R=\operatorname{diag}(8t)_{t=2}^m.
\]

Then

\[
L:=RBS^{-1}
\]

has entries

\[
L_{t,t}=-4t(68t^2+3),
\]

\[
L_{t,t+1}=(2t+1)^3,
\qquad
L_{t,t-1}=(2t-1)^3.
\]

For example, the upper coefficient is obtained algebraically as

\[
8t\frac{(t+\frac12)^3(t+2)}{(t+1)^2-1}
=
8t\frac{(2t+1)^3(t+2)/8}{t(t+2)}
=(2t+1)^3,
\]

and the lower coefficient is identical after replacing \(t+1\) by \(t-1\).

Therefore

\[
\det B
=\left(\prod_{t=2}^m\frac{t^2-1}{8t}\right)\det L.
\]

Let \(D_t\) denote the determinant of the leading tridiagonal block with indices \(2,\ldots,t\), with the empty convention \(D_1=1\). Then

\[
D_t
=-4t(68t^2+3)D_{t-1}-(2t-1)^6D_{t-2}.
\]

Since the project sequence satisfies

\[
X_2=-1,
\]

we have

\[
D_1=1=(-1)^1X_2,
\]

and

\[
D_2=-4\cdot2(68\cdot2^2+3)=X_3=(-1)^2X_3.
\]

Induction using the two recurrences gives

\[
\boxed{D_t=(-1)^tX_{t+1}.}
\]

In particular,

\[
\det L=(-1)^mX_{m+1},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\det T_m
=
\frac{(-1)^m}{4}\Delta_m
\left(\prod_{t=2}^m\frac{t^2-1}{8t}\right)X_{m+1}.
}
\]

This is the raw determinant factorization before choosing the endpoint normalization.

## 4. Exact comparison of \(X_{m+1}\), \(e_m\), and \(E_m\)

The identity here is purely a continuant identity. It does **not** assert an extra interior \(H\)-recurrence step at \(t=m\).

By definition,

\[
X_{m+1}
=4m(68m^2+3)X_m-(2m-1)^6X_{m-1}.
\]

Because \(2m+1=0\) in \(\mathbf F_p\),

\[
m=-\frac12,
\qquad
4m(68m^2+3)=-40,
\qquad
(2m-1)^6=64.
\]

Therefore

\[
X_{m+1}
=-40X_m-64X_{m-1}.
\]

On the other hand,

\[
e_m=(2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m
=-8X_{m-1}-5X_m,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{X_{m+1}=8e_m.}
\]

Now

\[
\prod_{t=2}^m\frac{t^2-1}{8t}
=
\frac{(m-1)!(m+1)!/2}{8^{m-1}m!}
=
\frac{(m+1)(m-1)!}{2\cdot8^{m-1}}.
\]

Substitution yields

\[
\boxed{
\det T_m
=(-1)^m\Delta_m
\frac{(m+1)(m-1)!}{8^{m-1}}e_m.
}
\]

For the normalized recurrence \(u_t=X_t/d_t\),

\[
d_m=(2m-1)^3d_{m-1},
\]

and therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
e_m
&=(2m-1)^3d_{m-1}u_{m-1}-5d_mu_m\\
&=d_m(u_{m-1}-5u_m).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus with \(E_m=u_{m-1}-5u_m\),

\[
\boxed{e_m=d_mE_m.}
\]

Since every odd factor \(3,5,\ldots,2m-1\) is nonzero modulo \(p\), \(d_m\) is a unit. Hence

\[
\det T_m=0
\iff X_{m+1}=0
\iff e_m=0
\iff E_m=0.
\]

So there is no genuine `e_m versus E_m` disagreement in the determinant: they are two unit-related normalizations of exactly the same seam.

---

## 5. Direct endpoint row: why the seam is \(u_{m-1}=5u_m\)

It is useful to see the endpoint without invoking \(X_{m+1}\).

At \(t=m=-1/2\) in \(\mathbf F_p\),

\[
a_m=0,
\qquad
-b_m=\frac{15}{2},
\qquad
c_m=\frac52.
\]

Hence the final equation \(HC(m)=0\) is

\[
\frac52y_{m-1}+\frac{15}{2}y_m=0,
\]

or

\[
y_{m-1}+3y_m=0.
\]

Since \(z_t=(t^2-1)y_t\), and

\[
(m-1)^2-1=\frac54,
\qquad
m^2-1=-\frac34,
\]

this becomes

\[
\frac45z_{m-1}-4z_m=0,
\]

that is

\[
\boxed{z_{m-1}=5z_m.}
\]

For the normalized null solution below, \(z_t=u_t\), so the endpoint is exactly \(E_m=u_{m-1}-5u_m=0\).

## 6. Singular case: exact kernel of \(T_m\)

Assume \(e_m=0\), equivalently \(E_m=0\).

The tridiagonal block \(L\) has unit superdiagonal entries

\[
(2t+1)^3\ne0
\qquad(2\le t<m),
\]

because \(2t+1\le2m-1=p-2\). Therefore a null vector is determined by its first coordinate, and a null vector with first coordinate zero is identically zero. Since \(\det L=0\),

\[
\dim\ker L=1,
\qquad
\dim\ker B=1,
\qquad
\dim\ker T_m=1.
\]

Normalize the kernel polynomial by

\[
C_*(0)=-1.
\]

The \(t=1\) equation \(HC_*(1)=0\) is

\[
\frac{81}{8}C_*(2)-\frac18C_*(0)=0,
\]

so

\[
C_*(2)=-\frac1{81}.
\]

Thus

\[
z_2=(2^2-1)C_*(2)=-\frac1{27}=u_2.
\]

The rows \(t=2,\ldots,m-1\) are exactly the normalized recurrence, so uniqueness gives

\[
z_t=u_t\qquad(2\le t\le m).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
C_*(\pm t)=\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}\quad(2\le t\le m).
}
\]

The value at \(\pm1\) is then forced by the mean-zero condition defining \(V\):

\[
-1+2C_*(1)+2\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
C_*(\pm1)
=\frac12-\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}.
}
\]

These values determine the unique polynomial \(C_*\in V\), since positive-point evaluation is an isomorphism.

## 7. Cyclic even kernel and the \(\delta_0\) source

Under the same endpoint condition \(E_m=0\), define an even function \(k\) on \(\mathbf F_p\) by

\[
k_0=-1,
\qquad
k_{\pm1}=3,
\qquad
k_{\pm t}=\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}\quad(2\le t\le m).
\]

Then \(Hk=0\) exactly:

- at \(t=0\),
  \[
  (Hk)(0)=\frac12k_1+\frac32k_0=\frac32-\frac32=0;
  \]
- at \(t=1\), the values \(k_0=-1\), \(k_2=-1/81\) give cancellation;
- at \(2\le t<m\), the equation is precisely the \(u_t\) recurrence after multiplying by \(8t\);
- at \(t=m\), the endpoint equation is \(u_{m-1}=5u_m\);
- negative \(t\) follows by evenness of \(k\) and parity preservation of \(H\).

So \(k\) is the cyclic even kernel vector. However, \(k\) need not lie in \(V\), because \(V\) is the mean-zero even subspace.

Put

\[
q:=\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}k_a
=5+2\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}.
\]

Let

\[
e_+:=\delta_1+\delta_{-1}.
\]

A direct application of the stencil gives

\[
\boxed{He_+=\frac12\delta_0.}
\]

Indeed the only possible off-zero contributions are at \(t=\pm1,\pm2\); the \(t=\pm1\) diagonal factor \(t^2-1\) and the \(t=\pm2\) edge factor \(t\mp2\) kill them, while the two contributions at \(0\) sum to \(1/2\).

Now define

\[
C_*:=k-\frac q2e_+.
\]

It is even and has total sum

\[
q-\frac q2\cdot2=0.
\]

For an even function on \(\mathbf F_p\), mean zero is exactly the condition that its degree-\(\le p-1\) interpolation polynomial have no \(t^{p-1}\) term; hence its interpolation degree is at most \(p-3\). Thus this function is precisely the element of \(V\) found above.

Finally,

\[
HC_*
=Hk-\frac q2He_+
=-\frac q4\delta_0.
\]

Therefore, in the project sign convention

\[
HC_*=-Q_{\rm center}\delta_0,
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm center}=\frac q4
=\frac54+\frac12\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}.
}
\]

This also agrees with the direct \(t=0\) calculation

\[
(HC_*)(0)
=\frac12C_*(1)+\frac32C_*(0)
=-\frac54-\frac12\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}.
\]

Thus the source scalar is a second quantity after the determinant seam. Singular \(T_m\) alone does not force \(Q_{\rm center}=0\).

---

## 8. Final bounded conclusion

The exact row/column reduction proves:

1. The standard-basis determinant is
   \[
   \det T_m
   =(-1)^m\Delta_m\frac{(m+1)(m-1)!}{8^{m-1}}e_m.
   \]
2. With \(E_m=u_{m-1}-5u_m\), one has \(e_m=d_mE_m\); hence the determinant is also a unit times \(E_m\).
3. The raw tridiagonal continuant appearing before endpoint normalization is \(X_{m+1}\), and \(X_{m+1}=8e_m=8d_mE_m\) in \(\mathbf F_p\).
4. Therefore
   \[
   T_m\text{ singular}\iff e_m=0\iff E_m=0.
   \]
5. In the singular case \(\ker T_m\) is exactly one-dimensional, generated by the mean-zero even correction \(C_*\) described above.
6. With \(C_*(0)=-1\), its exact source is
   \[
   HC_*=-Q_{\rm center}\delta_0,
   \qquad
   Q_{\rm center}=\frac14\sum_a k_a
   =\frac54+\frac12\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}.
   \]

So the determinant question is completely separated from the later source/reciprocal-sum question: the determinant detects exactly the endpoint continuant seam and nothing more.
