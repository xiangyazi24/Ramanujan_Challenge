# Verdict

For the reflected q6 row multigraph at a fixed lower prime `p`, two graph-theoretically independent cycles do **not** universally give two algebraically independent Apéry constraints. The exact obstruction is parallel-row multiplicity: distinct q6 rows can induce the same reflected lower-p edge, hence the same lower involution `z -> (a+b)-z`; a cycle made from parallel copies is algebraically tautological at characteristic `p`.

After collapsing that multiplicity, a genuine bicyclic **support** does force a nontrivial even closed-walk relation among the exact plus/minus centers

```text
plus:   sigma_e = 6 d_e - 1,
minus:  sigma_e = 7 d_e - 1,
q_e = p + d_e,
```

and hence a bounded-coefficient affine relation among `p` and the upper primes `q_e`. It also forces an extra same-characteristic p-zero/continuant equation at a branching vertex. Neither consequence is forbidden by the banked inputs, and neither by itself produces a uniformly low-height fixed integer carrier.

However, for the **aggregate cycle-excess term**, pseudoforestness is unnecessary. Each unordered pair of lower p-zeros supports at most two oriented plus rows and at most one minus row, so

```text
|E(Gamma_p)| <= 3 * binom(Z(p),2) = (3/2) Z(p)(Z(p)-1).
```

Thus for either the ordinary cyclomatic number or the excess beyond pseudoforest components,

```text
delta_p <= |E(Gamma_p)| <= (3/2) Z(p)(Z(p)-1).
```

Using the already-banked unconditional second factorial moment from the q6 campaign,

```text
sum_{p<=X} Z(p)(Z(p)-1)
  = O(X^(8/5) (log X)^(2/5)),
```

one gets on a dyadic macroscopic block `p ~ T`

```text
sum_{p~T} delta_p
  = O(T^(8/5) (log T)^(2/5))
  = o(T^2/(log T)^2),
```

and, with one lower-prime logarithmic weight,

```text
sum_{p~T} delta_p log p
  = O(T^(8/5) (log T)^(7/5))
  = o(T^2/log T).
```

So if `Q32_Q6_REFLECTED_ZERO_GRAPH_CYCLE_EXCESS_REDUCTION.md` needs only an aggregate `delta` budget at those scales, that reduction closes unconditionally from the named factorial-moment input. What does **not** follow is pointwise pseudoforestness.

# 1. Exact row dictionary

Write

```text
q = p + d,
n = 6p + r = 6q + u,
u = r - 6d.
```

For an actual q6 row, the lower digit `r` is an actual p-zero and the upper digit `u` is an actual q-zero. Put

```text
s   = p - 1 - r,
L_+ = r + 6d - p,
L_- = 7d - r - 1.
```

Pointwise reflection gives `s in Z_p`, and q-reflection of the original upper zero gives

```text
q - 1 - u = p + L_- in Z_q.
```

The two reflected lower edges are exactly the following.

## Plus row

The lower endpoints are

```text
L_+ , s in Z_p,
L_+ + s = 6d - 1.
```

The genuine nonwrapping lower p-zero pair before reflecting one endpoint is

```text
L_+ , r = L_+ + (p-6d),
```

so the underlying continuant gap is

```text
h_+ = p - 6d > 0.
```

The enlarged residual condition from the q6 reduction selects the q-zero

```text
y_+ = p - 6d - 1 - L_+.
```

There is a particularly useful symmetric rewriting. Set

```text
t_+ = p - 12d.
```

Since `s = 6d-1-L_+`, one has

```text
u   = r - 6d = t_+ + L_+,
y_+ = t_+ + s.
```

Hence a plus edge does not merely give a formal root: it transports the entire reflected lower pair by one translation into the upper zero set:

```text
{L_+, s} subset Z_p,
{L_+ + t_+, s + t_+} subset Z_q.
```

Equivalently, reflecting `y_+` in characteristic `q` gives the selected high digit

```text
v_+ = q - 1 - y_+ = L_+ + 7d = r + 13d - p.
```

Thus the two upper q-zeros `u,y_+` have exactly the same difference as the reflected lower endpoints:

```text
y_+ - u = s - L_+.
```

So if

```text
g = |s-L_+|,
m = min(s,L_+),
```

then, in addition to the lower equation,

```text
p | N_g(m),
q | N_g(m+t_+).
```

This is a genuine cross-characteristic pair statement, not a statement about arbitrary formal roots of `N_g`.

## Minus row

The lower endpoints are

```text
L_- , r in Z_p,
L_- + r = 7d - 1.
```

The genuine lower pair before the reflection is `L_-` against `s=p-1-r`, with gap

```text
h_- = |p-7d|.
```

The residual window forces the canonical inequality

```text
0 <= L_- < d,
```

and therefore the original upper digit is

```text
u = r - 6d = d - 1 - L_- >= 0.
```

Its q-reflection is

```text
q - 1 - u = p + L_- < p+d=q.
```

Thus the selected upper digits on a minus row are just the original q-zero and its forced q-reflection:

```text
u = d-1-L_-,
p+L_- = q-1-u.
```

Unlike the plus row, there is no second translated q-zero pair built into this gate.

# 2. The reflected graph is a graph of affine involutions

Let `Gamma_p` have the actual touched lower zeros as vertices and one row-edge for every actual reflected q6 row retained by the reduction. Forgetting row orientation for a moment, an edge `e={a,b}` has

```text
sigma_e := a+b = kappa_e d_e - 1,
```

where

```text
kappa_e = 6  for a plus row,
kappa_e = 7  for a minus row.
```

The lower reflected edge therefore acts by the affine involution

```text
R_e(z) = sigma_e - z.
```

This is the correct object to compose around a cycle.

For a path

```text
P = (e_1,...,e_m)
```

from `z_0` to `z_m`, repeated substitution gives the exact formula

```text
z_m = (-1)^m z_0 + A(P),
A(P) = sum_{j=1}^m (-1)^(m-j) sigma_{e_j}.
```

For a closed cycle based at `z_0`:

- if `m` is even,

```text
sum_{j=1}^m (-1)^(m-j) sigma_{e_j} = 0,
```

hence, because the alternating signs sum to zero,

```text
sum_{j=1}^m (-1)^(m-j) kappa_{e_j} d_{e_j} = 0;
```

- if `m` is odd,

```text
2 z_0 + 1
  = sum_{j=1}^m (-1)^(m-j) kappa_{e_j} d_{e_j}.
```

A useful immediate special case is that an all-plus odd cycle is impossible: its right side is divisible by `6`, while `2z_0+1` is odd. Thus the all-plus support is bipartite (indeed every plus edge has odd endpoint sum).

For a general odd mixed cycle the displayed equation merely fixes the absolute phase `z_0`; it is not a contradiction.

# 3. What a genuine bicyclic support forces

Let `H` be the simple support obtained from a connected component of `Gamma_p` by collapsing parallel row-edges. Suppose its cycle rank is at least two.

Choose a base vertex and two independent closed support walks `C_1,C_2`.

If one is even, the even-cycle identity above already gives a pure relation among the edge labels. If both are odd, each gives a formula for the same `2z_0+1`; subtracting them again gives a pure relation. Equivalently, concatenate one odd closed walk with the reverse of the other to obtain an even closed walk.

Thus there is an even closed walk `W`, with traversal coefficients `c_e` in `{0, +/-1, +/-2}` after repeated edges are collected, such that

```text
sum_e c_e = 0,
sum_e c_e (kappa_e d_e - 1) = 0,
```

and hence

```text
boxed:  sum_e c_e kappa_e d_e = 0.                 (3.1)
```

Substitute `d_e=q_e-p`:

```text
sum_e c_e kappa_e q_e
  = p * sum_e c_e kappa_e.
```

Since `kappa_e=6+1_{e minus}` and `sum c_e=0`, this becomes

```text
boxed:
sum_e c_e kappa_e q_e = mu_W p,
mu_W := sum_{e minus} c_e.                          (3.2)
```

This is the strongest universal affine relation coming only from the two independent **support** cycles.

For a monochromatic even cycle it reduces to ordinary alternating prime energy:

```text
q_1 + q_3 + ... = q_2 + q_4 + ... .
```

For a mixed cycle, the only extra term is the small integer multiple `mu_W p`.

This relation is exact but is not forbidden by the two-linear-form sieve or by primality alone. It is an additive relation among several prime variables; the number of variables grows with the cycle length.

## Linear-algebra sharpness

Let `B_H` be the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix of the support graph, so the edge equations are

```text
B_H^T z = (sigma_e)_e.
```

For a connected graph over characteristic zero,

```text
rank B_H = |V|-1  if H is bipartite,
rank B_H = |V|    if H is non-bipartite.
```

Therefore a bicyclic support (`|E|-|V|+1=2`) has

```text
2 compatibility relations  if bipartite,
1 compatibility relation   if non-bipartite.
```

So one should not expect two independent label identities merely because the graph cycle space has dimension two: an odd cycle consumes one degree of freedom by fixing the absolute vertex phase.

# 4. Extra same-characteristic equation at a genuine branching vertex

Every simple bicyclic core with at least three vertices has a vertex `v` of support degree at least three. Choose two incident support edges with distinct other endpoints `w_1,w_2`.

Write

```text
sigma_i = v+w_i = kappa_i d_i - 1.
```

Then

```text
w_1-w_2 = sigma_1-sigma_2
          = kappa_1 d_1 - kappa_2 d_2.
```

Since `w_1,w_2` are **actual** p-zeros, not merely formal roots, with

```text
H_12 = |kappa_1 d_1-kappa_2 d_2| > 0,
m_12 = min(w_1,w_2),
```

the exact nonwrapping restart identity gives

```text
boxed: p | N_{H_12}(m_12).                           (4.1)
```

This is an additional same-characteristic continuant equation attached to the branching forced by the support bicycle.

It is real information, but it is not a low-height closure:

```text
H_12 can be Theta(p),
```

and `m_12` still depends on the actual p-zero vertex. Eliminating `m_12` by a resultant returns to a degree/height of macroscopic size. The banked root-strip/First-Lemma machinery proves many such resultants nonzero, but their logarithmic heights at `H_12 ~ T` are far above the desired per-event budget.

Also note that equation (4.1) is conceptually a triple-zero consequence: two row-neighbors of the same p-zero vertex are themselves p-zeros, so their difference is another actual zero gap. It does not supply an independent Bernoulli factor.

# 5. Parallel cycles are the exact lower-characteristic obstruction

The multigraph matters. Distinct q6 rows can map to the **same** unordered reflected lower pair `{a,b}`.

Put

```text
S = a+b+1.
```

For that fixed pair:

- a plus row, if legal, must have

```text
d_+ = S/6,
q_+ = p+d_+;
```

there are at most two row orientations, according to which endpoint is the distinguished `L_+`;

- a minus row, if legal, must have

```text
d_- = S/7,
q_- = p+d_-;
```

and the inequality `L_-<d_-` makes the minus orientation unique.

Therefore

```text
multiplicity({a,b}) <= 3.                              (5.1)
```

Crucially, all parallel copies have the same lower involution

```text
z -> (a+b)-z.
```

Hence a 2-cycle formed by two parallel rows contributes **zero new lower-p equation**: composing the two identical reflections is the identity tautologically.

This is why graph-theoretic cycle independence is not the same as algebraic independence.

## The minimal bicyclic multigraph obstruction

A component on two vertices can be bicyclic only if it carries three parallel row-edges. Formula (5.1) shows this is the extremal possibility.

For two plus orientations and one minus row on the same pair, necessarily

```text
6 d_+ = 7 d_- = S.
```

Thus `S` is divisible by `42`; write

```text
S = 42 t,
d_+ = 7t,
d_- = 6t,
q_+ = p+7t,
q_- = p+6t.
```

The relation `6d_+=7d_-` is not a new cycle theorem: it is just the two definitions of the same endpoint sum.

Even more importantly, the two plus orientations do not produce two independent upper constraints. For the plus edge, with

```text
t_+ = p-12d_+ = p-84t,
```

the upper q-plus zero pair is simply

```text
{a+t_+, b+t_+} subset Z_{q_+}.
```

Swapping the plus orientation only swaps these two upper digits. Thus the parallel plus 2-cycle is algebraically duplicated at both the lower and upper characteristics.

The minus row adds

```text
{6t-1-L_-, p+L_-} subset Z_{q_-},
```

but there is no contradiction with the plus pair from the currently banked identities.

So a two-vertex triple-parallel bicycle is the sharply scoped obstruction to any proof of pseudoforestness based only on “a second cycle must create another lower zero/continuant equation.” It need not create another lower vertex at all.

This is an algebraic obstruction statement, not a claim that such a configuration is realized by actual Apéry zeros.

# 6. A stronger cross-characteristic certificate in the all-minus support case

There is one useful branch-specific strengthening.

On a minus edge the distinguished endpoint `L_-` is the smaller endpoint, because

```text
L_- < d,
r = 7d-1-L_- > 6d-1.
```

Orient every minus edge from `L_-` to `r`. On any all-minus support cycle, choose a local minimum vertex `ell`. The two cycle edges incident to `ell` are both oriented outward, with distinct upper primes

```text
q_i = p+d_i,   i=1,2.
```

For each edge, q-reflection gives

```text
q_i | b_{p+ell}.
```

Also `p|b_ell`; by the Apéry-Lucas congruence,

```text
b_{p+ell} = b_1 b_ell = 5 b_ell (mod p),
```

so `p|b_{p+ell}` for `p>=7`. Therefore

```text
boxed: p q_1 q_2 | b_{p+ell}.                         (6.1)
```

This is a genuine single-integer cross-characteristic product divisor forced already by an all-minus cycle.

It is much lower-height than a macroscopic resultant:

```text
log b_{p+ell} = O(p) = O(T).
```

But it still does not prove pseudoforestness. An Apéry number of index `Theta(T)` can have more than two prime divisors of size `Theta(T)`, and the existing height bound only gives `O(T/log T)` such large prime factors in the worst case. Summing (6.1) over all possible lower zero anchors loses too much.

Thus (6.1) is the strongest clean low-height certificate I find in a substantial subcase, but it is not universal: plus/mixed bicycles and the parallel obstruction above escape it.

# 7. No universal fixed integer carrier from the two cycles

The preceding analysis isolates why the natural carrier attempts stop.

1. **Lower support elimination.** Two genuine support cycles eliminate the vertices only to the exact equality (3.1)/(3.2). The resulting integer is zero, so there is no nonzero integer whose prime divisors can be charged.

2. **Branching continuant.** Equation (4.1) is nontrivial, but its evaluation point remains an actual moving p-zero and its gap can be macroscopic. Eliminating the point by a resultant creates a height of macroscopic resultant scale.

3. **Upper conditions.** Each edge lives at its own upper characteristic `q_e=p+d_e`. Plus edges give a translated pair of q-zeros; minus edges give the original q-zero and its reflection. The cycle relation aligns the integer indices but does not put these upper zero equations into one common finite field.

4. **Parallel excess.** A graph cycle can be created by duplicate row constraints with the same lower involution; no lower resultant sees that extra multiplicity at all.

Therefore no universal nonzero low-height certificate follows from “cycle rank at least two” alone.

# 8. Aggregate cycle-excess bound

Let

```text
V_p = number of vertices of Gamma_p,
E_p = number of row-edges, counted with multiplicity,
c_p = number of connected components.
```

The ordinary cyclomatic number is

```text
beta_p = E_p - V_p + c_p.
```

If the reduction instead uses excess beyond pseudoforest components, write

```text
delta_p = sum_C max(beta(C)-1,0).
```

In either convention,

```text
0 <= delta_p <= beta_p <= E_p.                        (8.1)
```

By (5.1), each unordered pair of distinct actual p-zero vertices supports at most three row-edges. Reflection-center loops have already been deleted. Hence

```text
E_p <= 3 binom(Z(p),2)
    = (3/2) Z(p)(Z(p)-1).                              (8.2)
```

Combining (8.1)-(8.2),

```text
boxed:
delta_p <= (3/2) Z(p)(Z(p)-1).                        (8.3)
```

Now use the banked unconditional second factorial moment from the current q6 campaign,

```text
sum_{p<=X} Z(p)(Z(p)-1)
  = O(X^(8/5) (log X)^(2/5)).                          (8.4)
```

For `p` in any fixed macroscopic dyadic range `cT<=p<=CT`,

```text
sum delta_p
  = O(T^(8/5) (log T)^(2/5)).                          (8.5)
```

The target comparison is

```text
T^(8/5) (log T)^(2/5) / (T^2/(log T)^2)
 = T^(-2/5) (log T)^(12/5) -> 0.
```

Therefore

```text
boxed:
sum_{p~T} delta_p = o(T^2/(log T)^2).                  (8.6)
```

With one logarithmic prime weight,

```text
sum_{p~T} delta_p log p
  = O(T^(8/5) (log T)^(7/5)),
```

and

```text
T^(8/5) (log T)^(7/5) / (T^2/log T)
 = T^(-2/5) (log T)^(12/5) -> 0,
```

so

```text
boxed:
sum_{p~T} delta_p log p = o(T^2/log T).                (8.7)
```

This preserves row multiplicity: no row is identified with another in (8.2); the constant `3` is exactly the worst reflected-pair multiplicity.

If the particular reduction has already canonically collapsed the two plus orientations, replace `3` by `2`. No exponent changes.

# 9. What can and cannot be concluded

## Proven unconditionally

- Exact edge equations:

```text
plus:  a+b=6d-1,
minus: a+b=7d-1,
q=p+d.
```

- Exact selected upper-digit formulas:

```text
plus:
  {L_+,s} subset Z_p,
  {p-12d+L_+, p-12d+s} subset Z_q;

minus:
  {L_-,r} subset Z_p,
  {d-1-L_-, p+L_-} subset Z_q.
```

- A genuine bicyclic simple support forces at least one pure affine closed-walk relation (3.1), equivalently the prime relation (3.2).

- A genuine branching vertex forces the extra actual p-zero continuant equation (4.1).

- An all-minus cycle forces the single-integer triple-prime divisibility (6.1).

- Regardless of whether individual bicycles occur, the aggregate cycle excess satisfies (8.5)-(8.7), hence is subcritical at the requested q6 scale.

## Not proved

- Pointwise pseudoforestness of `Gamma_p`.
- A universal forbidden affine relation: (3.2) is an exact relation, but it is not arithmetically impossible.
- A universal nonzero low-height resultant/Fitting carrier for every bicycle.
- Any extra saving obtained by multiplying independent probabilities across the distinct upper characteristics. No such independence has been established.

# 10. Smallest missing estimate if one insists on pseudoforestness / first-moment-only control

The exact obstruction has two pieces and should not be broadened beyond them.

### (A) Parallel-row excess

One must control multiple legal q6 rows on the same reflected lower pair. The minimal bad support is precisely the triple-parallel configuration above. Same-p continuants cannot distinguish the parallel copies; the missing information is entirely in the distinct upper-prime zero conditions.

### (B) Genuine support branching

After parallel copies are collapsed, every bicyclic core has a vertex of support degree at least three. Define the actual q6 support degree

```text
deg_p(v)
 = # {legal plus/minus support edges incident to v}.
```

A sufficient sharply scoped replacement for pointwise pseudoforestness is an aggregate excess-degree estimate

```text
sum_{p~T} sum_{v in Z_p} (deg_p(v)-2)_+
  = o(T^2/(log T)^2),                                  (10.1)
```

together with the analogous parallel-multiplicity excess. Equivalently one may bound the actual triple-star count

```text
sum_{p~T} sum_{v in Z_p} binom(deg_p(v),3).
```

Arithmetic expansion of one such triple star is exactly: one common actual lower p-zero `v`, three additional actual lower p-zeros obtained from

```text
w_i = kappa_i d_i - 1 - v,
```

three upper primes

```text
q_i=p+d_i,
```

and their corresponding selected upper q-zero digits from Section 1.

That mixed-prime triple-star estimate is the smallest genuinely new arithmetic statement needed to turn the cycle-excess reduction into a first-moment-level structural theorem. It is strictly more specific than a broad cross-prime decorrelation conjecture.

For the aggregate theorem actually requested here, however, (10.1) is unnecessary because the banked second factorial moment already gives (8.6)-(8.7).
