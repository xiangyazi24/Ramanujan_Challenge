# 1. Exact Jacobi determinant and the corner minor

Set

\[
X_m=(m!)^3b_m.
\]

Multiplying the Apéry recurrence by `(m!)^3` gives

\[
X_{m+1}=P(m)X_m-m^6X_{m-1},
\qquad X_0=1,\ X_1=5.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The determinant of the leading `m x m` principal Jacobi block obeys exactly the same continuant recurrence and initial conditions. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\det J_s=X_s=(s!)^3b_s.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Now delete row `s-1` and column `0` (equivalently read the `(0,s-1)` adjugate entry). Because `J_s` is tridiagonal, there is only one permutation contributing to that minor: the unique path through all off-diagonal entries. The cofactor signs cancel the signs of the negative off-diagonals. Hence

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{adj}J_s)_{0,s-1}
=1^3 2^3\cdots(s-1)^3
=((s-1)!)^3.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The same identity also drops out of the full adjugate formula. In the current notation,

\[
(\operatorname{adj}J_s)_{0,s-1}
=\frac{(s!)^3}{6}
\left(a_sb_{s-1}-b_sa_{s-1}\right),
\]

and the Apéry Casoratian

\[
a_sb_{s-1}-a_{s-1}b_s=\frac6{s^3}
\]

gives (1.3) immediately.

Thus (1.3) is doubly certified: directly by the tridiagonal path and by the recurrence/Casoratian formula.

# 2. The product constraint across **all** Smith invariants

Write the Smith form as

\[
U J_s V=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_s),
\qquad
\sigma_1\mid\cdots\mid\sigma_s,
\]

with `U,V` unimodular and every `sigma_i>0`.

Let `Delta_k` be the `k`-th determinantal divisor. Standard Smith theory gives

\[
\Delta_k=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `k=s-1`, `Delta_(s-1)` is the gcd of all cofactors. Therefore it divides every one of them, in particular the corner cofactor (1.3):

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{s-1}
=\Delta_{s-1}\mid((s-1)!)^3.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

On the other hand

\[
\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_s
=|\det J_s|
=(s!)^3b_s.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Divide (2.3) by (2.2). Since `Delta_(s-1)` actually divides `((s-1)!)^3`, not merely being numerically at most it, we obtain the **exact integer factorization**

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_s
=s^3b_s\,\eta_s,
\qquad
\eta_s:=\frac{((s-1)!)^3}{\Delta_{s-1}}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is the key new fact for the height attack.

It is stronger than the lower bound `sigma_s >= s^3 b_s`: the entire factor `s^3 b_s` divides the terminal Smith invariant.

## Interpretation

The determinant has two qualitatively different sources of size:

```text
factorial transfer content:  (s!)^3,
Apéry growth:                 b_s.
```

One might hope the lower Smith invariants absorb most of both. Equation (2.2) says they cannot absorb any of the Apéry factor at the Archimedean level: their entire product is a divisor of `((s-1)!)^3`. Therefore the factor `b_s`, together with `s^3`, is forced into `sigma_s`.

This is an exact global Smith obstruction, not a local rank heuristic.

# 3. Refutation of the `L_M^3` normalized height target

Now put

\[
s=n-Q,
\qquad
M=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{Q+1}\right\rfloor,
\qquad Q\ge1\text{ fixed}.
\]

Assume, exactly as allowed in the question, that

\[
L_M^3\mid d_s,
\]

and identify `d_s=\sigma_s`.

From (2.4),

\[
\frac{d_s}{L_M^3}
\ge\frac{s^3b_s}{L_M^3}
\]

as positive real numbers. Therefore

\[
\log\frac{d_s}{L_M^3}
\ge
\log b_s+3\log s-3\log L_M.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The Apéry number is

\[
b_s=\sum_{k=0}^s
\binom{s}{k}^2\binom{s+k}{k}^2.
\]

Taking only the final summand `k=s` gives

\[
b_s\ge\binom{2s}{s}^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Since the central binomial coefficient is the largest term in

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\binom{2s}{j}=4^s,
\]

we have the elementary lower bound

\[
\binom{2s}{s}\ge\frac{4^s}{2s+1}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\log b_s
\ge s\log16-2\log(2s+1).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

For the lcm,

\[
\log L_M=\psi(M)=M+o(M).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Substituting

\[
s=n-Q,
\qquad
M=\frac{n}{Q+1}+O(1)
\]

into (3.1), using (3.3)--(3.4), gives

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{d_{n-Q}}{L_M^3}
\ge
\left(\log16-\frac3{Q+1}\right)n+o_Q(n).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

For `Q>=1`,

\[
\log16-\frac3{Q+1}
\ge\log16-\frac32>0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac1n\log\frac{d_{n-Q}}{L_M^3}
\ge
\log16-\frac3{Q+1}>0.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

This disproves the requested little-`o` estimate.

### Sharper optional constant

Using the standard Apéry asymptotic

\[
\log b_s
=s\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log s)
\]

would improve the lower constant to

\[
\log(17+12\sqrt2)-\frac3{Q+1},
\]

but no asymptotic for `b_s` is needed. The elementary final-summand bound already gives a positive constant for every fixed `Q>=1`.

### If `L_M^3 | d_s` is not yet proved

Then the proposed integer quotient is not yet licensed. But this does not save the route: as a positive real ratio `d_s/L_M^3` it still satisfies (3.5). Thus either

```text
L_M^3 does not always divide d_s:
    the proposed integer normalization is invalid;

or L_M^3 always divides d_s:
    the integer quotient has logarithmic height Omega_Q(n).
```

There is no third outcome in which this particular normalization has `o_Q(n)` height.

# 4. Why the other suggested Smith mechanisms cannot rescue it

## 4.1 Product constraints

The strongest possible product constraint is already (2.2):

\[
\prod_{i<s}\sigma_i\mid((s-1)!)^3.
\]

This works **against** the desired estimate. To make the terminal invariant small, one would need the lower invariants to absorb a factor of size comparable to `b_s`; they are arithmetically forbidden from doing so beyond the corner factorial.

## 4.2 Determinantal divisors

The exact formula

\[
d_s=\frac{(s!)^3b_s}{\Delta_{s-1}}
\]

shows that proving a large `Delta_(s-1)` is the only Smith-theoretic way to shrink `d_s`. But

\[
\Delta_{s-1}\mid((s-1)!)^3
\]

puts an absolute ceiling on that strategy. Even the **maximum possible** value `Delta_(s-1)=((s-1)!)^3` leaves

\[
d_s=s^3b_s.
\]

So the desired sublinear height is impossible even in the most favorable conceivable Smith distribution.

## 4.3 Jacobi eigenvalue interlacing

Real Jacobi interlacing controls eigenvalue order and simplicity of roots of consecutive principal determinants. It does not enlarge the integer gcd of the cofactors. Equation (1.3) is an exact integer minor, so no spectral interlacing statement can make `Delta_(s-1)` exceed it.

Thus interlacing is orthogonal to the height obstruction.

## 4.4 Desnanot--Jacobi / Dodgson

The current marked-Jacobi adjugate identity is

\[
\operatorname{adj}(J_s)
\equiv C_{0,s}\,\mathbf b\mathbf b^T\pmod{b_s}.
\]

This is useful for exact local marked support, but it reinforces the rank-one nature of the cofactors. Dodgson identities among these entries are already rank-one identities modulo `b_s`; they do not manufacture an extra common divisor of every cofactor.

And regardless of any such relation, `Delta_(s-1)` must still divide the corner entry `((s-1)!)^3`.

## 4.5 Recurrence bounds

The recurrence gives both ingredients of the obstruction exactly:

\[
\det J_s=(s!)^3b_s,
\]

and

\[
(\operatorname{adj}J_s)_{0,s-1}=((s-1)!)^3.
\]

Archimedean bounds on other cofactors cannot help. The determinantal divisor is a gcd, and one explicit small cofactor already caps it.

# 5. The canonical forced-factor-free quotient

Equation (2.4) suggests the only honest Smith normalization which removes the proved obstruction:

\[
\boxed{
\eta_s
:=\frac{d_s}{s^3b_s}
=\frac{((s-1)!)^3}{\Delta_{s-1}}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This scalar is canonical, integral, and depends only on the Smith lattice.

It has a clean interpretation:

\[
\eta_s
=\frac{\text{corner cofactor}}{\gcd(\text{all cofactors})}.
\]

So `eta_s` measures precisely how far the full cofactor ideal is from being generated by the corner path cofactor.

## What can be proved about its height?

Only the trivial bound follows unconditionally from current data:

\[
1\le\eta_s\le((s-1)!)^3,
\]

hence

\[
\log\eta_s=O(s\log s).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The marked rank-one adjugate theorem does not give a lower bound on `Delta_(s-1)` strong enough to improve (5.2) to `o(s)`.

## Why `eta_s` is not a support-preserving replacement

Dividing by `s^3b_s` can delete a prime `p>M` which divides both the terminal Smith invariant and `b_s`. Nothing in the currently banked Jacobi identities proves that all fixed-`Q` selected primes are coprime to `b_s`.

For `Q=1`, no-consecutive-zero geometry sometimes makes such a stripping safer on a branch, but that is not a uniform fixed-`Q` theorem, and it does not solve the height of `eta_s` anyway.

Therefore I do **not** claim

\[
\operatorname{supp}_{>M}(\eta_s)
=\operatorname{supp}_{>M}(d_s/L_M^3).
\]

That equality is precisely the kind of new target-safe factor-removal theorem one would have to prove before using `eta_s` as a selector.

# 7. What the full-Jacobi selector is still good for

The negative height theorem does **not** invalidate a local support theorem proved in the unseen `Q32_FULL_JACOBI_FIXEDQ_SMITH_SELECTOR.md`.

If that note proves, after the `L_M^3` baseline is removed, an exact statement of the form

```text
for p > M:
    p divides d_(n-Q)/L_M^3
    iff p is in the desired fixed-Q target support
    (up to explicitly listed fixed/endpoint exceptions),
```

then that support theorem can still be correct.

What fails is the next intended step:

```text
exact support
+ hope that the normalized terminal Smith invariant is small
=> target mass bound.
```

Equation (3.7) says the normalized integer itself is necessarily exponentially large. A support-exact integer can carry enormous powers of small primes and/or unrelated multiplicity while still having the right large-prime radical; its Archimedean size is therefore the wrong invariant to estimate here.

This sharply separates two questions:

1. **Support theorem:** potentially valuable and reusable.
2. **Height of the whole normalized Smith invariant:** provably the wrong target.

The next attack should therefore target the **squarefree above-`M` part** or another target-safe primitive projection, not the absolute size of `d_(n-Q)/L_M^3`.

# 8. Smallest genuinely new theorem that would reopen the route

There are two viable forms.

## A. Target-safe removal of the forced Apéry factor

Prove that for every safe fixed-`Q` selected prime `p>M`,

\[
p\nmid s^3b_s,
\qquad s=n-Q,
\]

or more generally prove an exact factorization

\[
d_s=s^3b_s\,\eta_s

in which every target prime is forced into `eta_s`, not into the removed factor.

Then `eta_s` would preserve target support. One would still need

\[
\log\eta_s=o_Q(n),
\]

which is currently unproved.

For general fixed `Q`, the first coprimality statement is not supplied by current no-consecutive-zero/adjugate theory, so this is a genuinely new arithmetic theorem, not a formal Smith consequence.

## B. Direct squarefree/cofactor-ideal estimate

Avoid the full invariant and prove directly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{p>M\\p\mid d_s}}\log p=o_Q(n).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

using the local Smith selector to translate `p|d_s` into the moving Apéry target condition. This is exactly the desired support mass, but now it is not contaminated by the mandatory exponential factor `b_s` at the Archimedean level.

A new cross-prime arithmetic input is required for (8.1); determinant/interlacing identities alone cannot provide it.

# 9. Theorem-ready negative statement

## Theorem — terminal full-Jacobi Smith factor has unavoidable Apéry exponential height

Let `J_s` be the `s x s` integral symmetric Apéry Jacobi matrix

\[
(J_s)_{ii}=P(i),\qquad
(J_s)_{i-1,i}=(J_s)_{i,i-1}=-i^3,
\]

and let

\[
\sigma_1\mid\cdots\mid\sigma_s
\]

be its Smith invariant factors. Then

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{i=1}^{s-1}\sigma_i\mid((s-1)!)^3
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
s^3b_s\mid\sigma_s.}
\]

Consequently, for fixed `Q>=1`, `s=n-Q`, and

\[
M=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{Q+1}\right\rfloor,
\]

if `L_M^3 | sigma_s`, then

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\sigma_s}{L_M^3}
\ge
\left(\log16-\frac3{Q+1}\right)n+o_Q(n).
}
\]

In particular

\[
\log(\sigma_s/L_M^3)\ne o_Q(n).
\]

### Proof

The determinant identity is

\[
|\det J_s|=(s!)^3b_s.
\]

The `(0,s-1)` adjugate entry is the unique path product

\[
((s-1)!)^3.
\]

Hence the `(s-1)`st determinantal divisor divides that integer. Smith theory then gives

\[
\sigma_s
=\frac{(s!)^3b_s}{\Delta_{s-1}}
=s^3b_s\frac{((s-1)!)^3}{\Delta_{s-1}}.
\]

Finally use

\[
b_s\ge\binom{2s}{s}^2\ge16^s/(2s+1)^2
\]

and

\[
\log L_M=M+o(M).
\]

This proves the claim. `square`
