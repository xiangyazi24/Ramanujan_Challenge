# 1. The first center is a genuine fixed-motive trace

Write the Apéry numbers as

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]

For

\[
N=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

we have in \(\mathbf F_p\)

\[
N\equiv-\frac12.
\]

Using

\[
\binom{N+k}{k}=(-1)^k\binom{-N-1}{k},
\]

both binomial factors at the center reduce to the same half-parameter.  Thus

\[
 b_N\equiv
 \sum_{k=0}^{N}\binom{-1/2}{k}^4
 =\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{\binom{2k}{k}^4}{256^k}\pmod p.
 \tag{6}
\]

The Ahlgren--Ono supercongruence strengthens this to

\[
\boxed{b_N\equiv a_p\pmod{p^2}},
\tag{7}
\]

where

\[
f_8(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
=q-4q^3-2q^5+24q^7-44q^{11}+22q^{13}+\cdots
\]

is the unique normalized weight-4 newform on \(\Gamma_0(8)\).

This is not merely a numerical modularity coincidence.  The compatible system attached to \(f_8\) has rank two, weight three, trivial nebentypus, and at every odd good prime its characteristic polynomial is

\[
\boxed{X^2-a_pX+p^3.}
\tag{8}
\]

Therefore the first central coordinate is a conjugacy-invariant Frobenius datum of one fixed pure motive.

There is also a useful local consistency check from the Apéry recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}
=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\]

At \(n=N\), \(2N+1=p\), so modulo \(p\)

\[
P(N)=0,
\qquad
N+1\equiv-N.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{b_{N+1}\equiv b_{N-1}\pmod p.}
\tag{9}
\]

This explains the local two-sided symmetry, but (9) by itself says nothing about a global compatible system.  That distinction is essential below.

# 2. The adjacent center is a contiguous half-parameter truncation

Now set \(n=N-1=(p-3)/2\).  Modulo \(p\),

\[
n\equiv-\frac32,
\qquad
-n-1\equiv\frac12.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_{N-1}
&=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{k}^2
                    \binom{N-1+k}{k}^2\\
&\equiv
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
\binom{-3/2}{k}^2\binom{1/2}{k}^2
\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The missing \(k=N\) term may be inserted: \(\binom{-3/2}{N}\) contains the factor \(p/2\) in its numerator and is therefore zero modulo \(p\).  Thus

\[
\boxed{
 b_{N-1}\equiv
 \sum_{k=0}^{N}
 \binom{-3/2}{k}^2\binom{1/2}{k}^2
 \pmod p.
}
\tag{11}
\]

This already shows what changes at the second coordinate.  The first center is the symmetric \((-1/2,-1/2)\) truncation; the adjacent center is the contiguous \((-3/2,+1/2)\) truncation.

One can also express it directly in terms of the central summands.  Put

\[
c_k:=\binom{-1/2}{k}^4
=\frac{\binom{2k}{k}^4}{256^k}.
\]

For \(k\ge1\),

\[
\binom{1/2}{k}
=-\frac{1}{2k-1}\binom{-1/2}{k}
\]

up to the irrelevant squared sign.  Consequently the extra object naturally carries the denominator \((2k-1)^2\); it is not another copy of the central trace sum.

# 3. Exact Golyshev--Zagier reduction: proof of the second-coordinate formula

Golyshev--Zagier prove the following finite telescoping identity for every integer \(K\ge0\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=0}^{K}\Bigg(&
 \binom{-1/2}{k}^4
 +\binom{1/2}{k}^2\binom{-3/2}{k}^2\\
&\qquad
 -24k^2\binom{-1/2}{k}^2\binom{1/2}{k}^2
\Bigg)
=2(2K+1)^2\binom{-1/2}{K}^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

The exact right-hand normalization is not important for the reduction; the load-bearing fact is its factor \((2K+1)^2\).

Take

\[
K=N=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

Then \(2K+1=p\), while \(\binom{-1/2}{N}\) has denominator a power of \(2\), hence is \(p\)-integral.  The right-hand side of (12) is therefore \(0\pmod p\).  By (6) and (11), the first two sums on the left are respectively \(b_N\) and \(b_{N-1}\) modulo \(p\).  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 b_N+b_{N-1}
 \equiv
 24\sum_{k=0}^{N}k^2
 \binom{-1/2}{k}^2\binom{1/2}{k}^2
 \pmod p.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Define the finite quasiperiod truncation

\[
Q_p:=\sum_{k=0}^{N}k^2
 \binom{-1/2}{k}^2\binom{1/2}{k}^2.
\tag{14}
\]

Since, for \(k\ge1\),

\[
\binom{-1/2}{k}^2\binom{1/2}{k}^2
=\frac{1}{(2k-1)^2}inom{-1/2}{k}^4
=\frac{1}{(2k-1)^2}rac{\binom{2k}{k}^4}{256^k},
\tag{15}
\]

we obtain (2).  Combining (13) with Ahlgren--Ono (7) gives the promised exact formula

\[
\boxed{
 b_{(p-3)/2}
 \equiv
 -a_p
 +24\sum_{k=1}^{(p-1)/2}
 \frac{k^2}{(2k-1)^2}
 \frac{\binom{2k}{k}^4}{256^k}
 \pmod p.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Every denominator in (16) is a \(p\)-unit: for \(1\le k\le(p-1)/2\), one has \(1\le2k-1\le p-2\).

This is the strongest concrete second-coordinate formula I obtain from the exact half-shift geometry.

# 4. Why \(Q_p\) is the right quasiperiod-type object

The finite identity is not an isolated binomial trick.  It is the finite counterpart of the characteristic-zero half-index theory in Golyshev--Zagier.

They interpolate the Apéry numbers to a function \(\mathcal A(x)\) with summands

\[
\alpha_k(x)=
 \binom{x-1/2}{k}^2
 \binom{x+k-1/2}{k}^2.
\]

At the symmetry point one gets the \((-1/2)\)-hypergeometric period.  Their Theorem 2 identifies it with the central \(L\)-value of the same level-8 newform:

\[
A_{-1/2}=\frac{16}{\pi^2}L(f_8,2).
\tag{17}
\]

The next half-index is more subtle.  Their Theorem 3 says that \(A_{1/2}\) is a rational linear combination of

\[
A_{-1/2},\qquad \pi^{-2},\qquad
\text{a real quasiperiod of }f_8.
\tag{18}
\]

The binomial sum occurring in (14) is precisely the finite truncation of the second-kind/quasiperiod sum used in the exact relation between the two half-shifts.  Thus (16) is geometrically consistent with the characteristic-zero picture:

```text
first center      = pure modular period / Frobenius trace;
second center     = - first center + quasiperiod-type correction.
```

This is much stronger information than “the Apéry recurrence has local rank two.”  It identifies exactly what additional datum is needed.

# 5. The first compatibility obstruction: quasiperiods have no canonical splitting

The same Golyshev--Zagier paper gives the decisive warning against immediately naming \(Q_p\) a second Frobenius coordinate.

For a weight-\(k\) eigenform they describe the enlarged de Rham/parabolic-cohomology object containing a meromorphic cusp form of the second kind.  It fits a canonical filtration, but **there is no canonical splitting**.  In the level-8 case the meromorphic eigenform representing the quasiperiod is only determined up to

- a rational scaling,
- addition of a rational multiple of the original cusp form \(f_8\), and
- addition of an exact \((k-1)\)-st derivative.

Correspondingly, the quasiperiod itself is defined only up to rational scaling and addition of rational multiples of the ordinary periods.

That is exactly the obstruction one meets if one tries to write

\[
(b_N,b_{N-1})
\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{two entries of }\mathrm{Frob}_p.
\]

A Frobenius **trace** is conjugacy invariant.  A matrix entry is not.  To make a second entry arithmetic across all primes one must give a global compatible framing, or at least a canonical extension/flag together with an integral splitting normalization.  The existing period/quasiperiod geometry does not provide such a splitting.

Therefore the honest status is:

- **fixed pure rank-two motive:** yes for \(b_N\), no for the pair as its two characteristic-polynomial invariants;
- **same characteristic-zero motivic/Hodge package:** yes, period plus quasiperiod;
- **one fixed canonically framed mixed compatible system whose local Frobenius coordinate is exactly \(Q_p\):** not established by these results.

This is a scoped no-go, not a theorem that no conceivable mixed motive can ever realize \(Q_p\).  A new theorem constructing such a framed extension would change the conclusion.

# 6. CFVZ: the exact \(p\bmod24\) signs, and why they are not a second trace

Let

\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}b_nt^n.
\]

The Lucas property gives the usual characteristic-\(p\) algebraic relation for the full generating series.  Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin compute its Galois group and, equivalently, prove an exact factorization of the Hasse polynomial.  Put

\[
\Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1.
\]

Then

\[
A_p(t)=
\begin{cases}
 B_p(t)^2,
 &p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},\\[2mm]
 \Delta(t)B_p(t)^2,
 &p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]

The split can be written uniformly with the quadratic character \(\chi_{-6}(p)=\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)\):

\[
\boxed{
A_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{(1-\chi_{-6}(p))/2}B_p(t)^2.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Indeed, quadratic reciprocity gives

\[
\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)
=\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)
 \left(\frac{2}{p}\right)
 \left(\frac{3}{p}\right),
\]

and the eight odd classes prime to \(6\) give

| \(p\bmod24\) | \(\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)\) | \(\left(\frac2p\right)\) | \(\left(\frac3p\right)\) | \(\chi_{-6}(p)\) | CFVZ factor |
|---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---|
| 1  | + | + | + | + | \(B_p^2\) |
| 5  | + | - | - | + | \(B_p^2\) |
| 7  | - | + | - | + | \(B_p^2\) |
| 11 | - | - | + | + | \(B_p^2\) |
| 13 | + | - | + | - | \(\Delta B_p^2\) |
| 17 | + | + | - | - | \(\Delta B_p^2\) |
| 19 | - | - | - | - | \(\Delta B_p^2\) |
| 23 | - | + | + | - | \(\Delta B_p^2\) |

This table is the requested exact \(p\bmod24\) sign law.

There is no hidden extra \(p\bmod24\) sign in formula (16).  The mod-24 character controls the **quadratic descent of the whole Hasse polynomial**.  It does not turn the neighboring coefficient into \(\pm a_p\).

CFVZ also exposes why their theorem cannot itself be interpreted as a fixed bounded-rank motive.  They prove

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf F_p(t,f_\alpha)/\mathbf F_p(t))
=
\begin{cases}
(\mathbf F_p^\times)^2,&p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},\\
\mathbf F_p^\times,&p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.
\end{cases}
\tag{21}
\]

Thus the extension degree is respectively

\[
\frac{p-1}{2}\quad\text{or}\quad p-1.
\tag{22}
\]

It grows with \(p\).  The CFVZ object is a characteristic-\(p\) Kummer/Lucas Galois extension attached to the generating function, not a fixed-rank \(\ell\)-adic compatible system as \(p\) varies.  The order-two recurrence and the order-\(p\) Kummer extension are different notions of rank and must not be conflated.

# 8. What can and cannot be meant by a “Frobenius vector”

There are three mathematically different proposals here.

## 8.1 Characteristic-polynomial vector

For a rank-two pure compatible system the canonical Frobenius data are

\[
(\operatorname{tr}\mathrm{Frob}_p,
 \det\mathrm{Frob}_p)=(a_p,p^3).
\]

The Apéry pair is not this vector, by the preceding fixture.

## 8.2 Two traces from fixed pure constituents

One could ask for some other fixed bounded-rank pure motive \(M'\) with trace \(c_p\) satisfying

\[
c_p\equiv b_{N-1}\pmod p.
\]

Nothing in the exact Hasse/CFVZ/Golyshev--Zagier geometry constructs such an \(M'\).  Formula (16) instead identifies an extension/quasiperiod correction.  The simple possibility that \(M'\) is a finite-order twist of \(M_{f_8}\) is already refuted by \(p=11\).  (The level-8 newform also has no nontrivial inner twist in the standard newform data.)

A universal theorem excluding every imaginable fixed higher-rank pure motive from having these residues would require much more than the present data, so I do **not** claim one.

## 8.3 A framed mixed-motive/F-crystal matrix entry

This is the geometrically plausible version.  Period plus quasiperiod naturally suggests an extension object.  But a matrix entry of Frobenius is basis dependent.  To make it into a prime-varying arithmetic scalar one needs a canonical flag and a canonical splitting/integral framing.  Golyshev--Zagier explicitly identify the absence of a canonical splitting on the quasiperiod side.

Thus the first precise missing theorem is:

> **Framed crystalline quasiperiod theorem.** Construct one fixed mixed motive/compatible filtered object over \(\mathbf Q\), with bounded rank and fixed bad set, together with a canonical integral framing at every good \(p\), such that a specified crystalline Frobenius entry reduces to \(Q_p\) in (14).

Without this, “the pair is a Frobenius vector” is not a well-defined compatible-system assertion.

# 10. Why ordinary Chebotarev does not bound (23)

There are two independent obstructions.

## 10.1 A matrix entry is not a conjugacy-class function

Chebotarev assigns a prime an unramified Frobenius **conjugacy class**.  Traces, determinants, and characteristic polynomials are therefore natural Chebotarev observables.

If \(Q_p\) is only an off-diagonal or framed matrix entry, the condition

\[
(U_m-V_m)a_p+24V_mQ_p=0
\]

is not conjugacy invariant unless the framing is itself part of a globally defined Galois-stable structure.  The noncanonical splitting in the quasiperiod construction is exactly the missing datum.

So before any sieve estimate one must first make the slope event a well-defined conjugacy-stable Galois condition.

## 10.2 The modulus is the Frobenius prime itself

Even after granting a canonical mixed system, the target is

\[
p\mid L_{m,p},
\qquad
L_{m,p}:=(U_m-V_m)a_p+24V_mQ_p.
\tag{24}
\]

For a fixed auxiliary prime \(\ell\), a compatible system gives an unramified representation at primes \(p\ne\ell\), and Chebotarev/large sieve can study conditions such as

\[
L_{m,p}\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

But (24) asks for reduction modulo \(p\), not modulo a fixed \(\ell\).  If one sets \(\ell=p\), one is looking at the \(p\)-adic representation at its coefficient prime.  The relevant object is crystalline/semistable local Frobenius, not the unramified global Frobenius class used by ordinary Chebotarev.

This is the first unavoidable “diagonal” obstruction.

It is already visible when \(V_m=0\).  Then (24) becomes

\[
p\mid a_p,
\]

i.e. nonordinarity of the weight-4 modular form at \(p\).  That is a local \(p\)-adic property at the same prime, not a fixed-\(\ell\) Chebotarev hyperplane condition.

Therefore a big-image theorem for the usual \(\ell\)-adic representation, by itself, does not produce the desired bound.

# 11. Why allowing \(m\) to grow makes the standard large-sieve route even less applicable

For a fixed auxiliary \(\ell\), one can sometimes combine large monodromy with a large sieve to count primes for which Frobenius lies in one of many conjugacy-stable subsets.  If \(m\) grows, the usual bookkeeping would additionally require uniform control of

- the number of distinct slope conditions modulo \(\ell\),
- their densities in the finite monodromy groups,
- the conductors/discriminants of the fields used by the sieve, and
- the dependence of all effective Chebotarev constants on \(m\) and \(\ell\).

But this is secondary here.  Even a perfect uniform theorem of that type would count

\[
L_{m,p}\equiv0\pmod\ell
\]

for fixed auxiliary \(\ell\), not the diagonal divisibility \(p\mid L_{m,p}\).

Trying instead to let \(\ell=p\) means the field/representation used by the sieve changes with the prime being counted.  There is no single finite Galois extension to which effective Chebotarev can be applied.  In the CFVZ incarnation the failure is especially concrete: the relevant Kummer Galois group itself has order \((p-1)/2\) or \(p-1\), so the degree grows linearly with \(p\).

Thus I do not see a Chebotarev/large-sieve estimate uniform enough for growing \(m\) arising from the currently identified objects.

The needed input would instead have to be genuinely **vertical \(p\)-adic/crystalline**: for example, a theorem controlling the reductions of a canonically normalized unit-root/quasiperiod extension coordinate as \(p\) varies.

At every good prime the determinant is \(p^3\), hence zero modulo \(p\).  Again \(p=11\) falsifies the identification immediately.

p        5   7   11  13  17  19

A Sturm-bound computation can prove an equality between two **fixed modular forms** once an actual fixed modular form is proposed.  It cannot, by itself, turn the varying congruence

\[
c_p\equiv b_{(p-3)/2}\pmod p
\]

into an identity of modular forms; that varying modulus must first be explained.

## Test D: quasiperiod normalization

For each test prime compute

R_p:=b_N+b_{N-1}-24Q_p\pmod p.

The exact theorem predicts

\]

for every odd \(p\ge5\).  This is the correct regression test for implementations of (16).

Any proposed crystalline extension should first reproduce the same \(Q_p\) values under its claimed normalization.

# 13. Scoped theorem / no-go statement

The conclusions can be packaged as follows.

### Theorem (second-center identity)

Let \(p\ge5\) be prime and \(N=(p-1)/2\).  Let \(a_p\) be the \(p\)-th coefficient of

\[
f_8=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4.
\]

Then

\[
b_N\equiv a_p\pmod{p^2},
\]

and

\[
b_{N-1}\equiv-a_p+24Q_p\pmod p,
\]

with \(Q_p\) given by (2)--(3).

**Proof.**  The first congruence is Ahlgren--Ono.  The second follows from the hypergeometric reductions (6), (11) and the exact finite Golyshev--Zagier identity (12) with \(K=N\), whose right side contains \((2N+1)^2=p^2\).  Equation (15) gives the explicit central-binomial expression.  \(\square\)

### Corollary (no mod-24 trace twist)

There is no sign function \(\varepsilon(p)\), in particular no quadratic Dirichlet character modulo 24, for which

\[
b_{(p-3)/2}\equiv\varepsilon(p)a_p\pmod p
\]

for every good prime.  The prime \(p=11\) is a counterexample.

### CFVZ theorem in the present notation

For

\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}b_nt^n,
\]

one has

\[
A_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{(1-(-6/p))/2}B_p(t)^2,
\qquad
\Delta=t^2-34t+1.
\]

The corresponding characteristic-\(p\) Galois extension has degree \((p-1)/2\) or \(p-1\), hence is not a fixed bounded-rank compatible system.

### Scoped no-go (current compatible-system route)

The exact known geometry identifies \(b_N\) with a pure Frobenius trace and \(b_{N-1}\) with that trace plus a finite quasiperiod correction.  It does **not** canonically identify \(Q_p\) with a conjugacy-invariant Frobenius datum of one fixed bounded-rank compatible system.  Golyshev--Zagier's quasiperiod construction has a canonical filtration but no canonical splitting, which is the first exact compatibility obstruction.

Even after postulating such a framed mixed system, ordinary Chebotarev/large-sieve methods do not directly bound

\[
U_mb_N+V_mb_{N-1}\equiv0\pmod p
\]

because the modulus is the Frobenius prime itself.  Fixed-\(\ell\) Chebotarev sees \(p\ne\ell\), while \(\ell=p\) is the crystalline/local place.  This obstruction already occurs for \(p\mid a_p\).

Therefore the route is not closed by “rank two + Chebotarev.”  The missing arithmetic theorem would have to be a vertical crystalline/quasiperiod distribution result, not merely a big-monodromy theorem for a fixed auxiliary \(\ell\).

## Bottom line for P3.2

The second central coordinate is **not another modular trace hiding behind a mod-24 sign**.  The exact formula is

\[
\boxed{
(b_N,b_{N-1})
\equiv
\left(
 a_p,
 -a_p+24Q_p
\right)
\pmod p.
}
\]

The first entry is pure Frobenius.  The second contains a finite quasiperiod shadow.  The CFVZ \(p\bmod24\) law describes square/\(\Delta\)-square descent of the full Hasse polynomial and has unbounded Kummer degree; it does not turn that quasiperiod into a fixed-rank trace.

For the growing selected slopes, the immediate target is therefore **not** a Chebotarev theorem.  It is first a canonical crystalline realization of \(Q_p\), and then a genuinely vertical \(p\)-adic distribution theorem for

\[
(U_m-V_m)a_p+24V_mQ_p\pmod p.
\]

Without those two new inputs, the second-center compatible-system attack stops here.
