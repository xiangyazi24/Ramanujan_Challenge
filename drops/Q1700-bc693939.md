# Coefficient-level attack on the gap-one norm criticality

## 1. Reduction of the finite-field sum to a coefficient

Let

\[
f(z)=Q(z(z+1)),\qquad Q(Y)=\frac{P_m(Y)}{Y-y}.
\]

For the gap-one case, assuming the repository definition of the shell polynomial \(P_m\), the target is

\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbf F_p}\frac1{f(z)}.
\]

Since \(f(z)\ne0\) on the summation set, the finite-field identity

\[
\frac1{f(z)}=f(z)^{p-2}
\]

holds. Therefore, for a polynomial representative reduced modulo \(z^p-z\),

\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbf F_p} f(z)^{p-2}
\]

is determined by the coefficient of monomials whose exponents are positive multiples of \(p-1\). More explicitly, if

\[
f(z)^{p-2}=\sum_a c_a z^a,
\]

then

\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbf F_p}z^a=
\begin{cases}
0,&a=0,\\
-1,&(p-1)\mid a,\ a>0,
\end{cases}
\]

so

\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbf F_p}\frac1{f(z)}=-\sum_{j\ge1}c_{j(p-1)}.
\]

This is the exact coefficient identity. It is not a single top coefficient unless one proves all other multiples vanish.

## 2. Newton basis expansion

Write the shell polynomial in the factorial/Newton basis

\[
P_m(Y)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\phi_k(Y)^2.
\]

After dividing by \(Y-y\),

\[
Q(Y)=\sum_k q_k\phi_k(Y)
\]

for explicitly computable Newton coefficients \(q_k\). Substitution \(Y=z(z+1)\) gives

\[
f(z)=\sum_k q_k\phi_k(z(z+1)).
\]

Consequently

\[
f(z)^{p-2}
=
\sum_{k_1,\ldots,k_{p-2}}
q_{k_1}\cdots q_{k_{p-2}}
\prod_i\phi_{k_i}(z(z+1)).
\]

The surviving coefficient is therefore a sum over all multinomial choices satisfying the congruence condition

\[
\deg_z\left(\prod_i\phi_{k_i}(z(z+1))\right)\equiv0\pmod{p-1}.
\]

## 3. Failure of the unique surviving term mechanism

The hoped-for argument was that the all-one shell coefficients would leave only the maximal term. This is false already at the first tested primes.

For \(p=19\) and \(p=37\), expansion modulo \(z^p-z\) shows that multiple Newton-basis products contribute to the coefficient sum. In particular the contribution is not concentrated in the maximal degree term of \(f^{p-2}\); lower Newton degrees also produce exponents divisible by \(p-1\) after the substitution \(Y=z(z+1)\).

The obstruction is structural: the map

\[
Y\mapsto z(z+1)
\]

folds degrees. A term of degree \(r\) in \(Y\) becomes degree \(2r\) in \(z\), and products of different Newton levels collide modulo \(p-1\). Therefore no unique multinomial survivor follows from the positivity/all-one property of \(P_m\).

## 4. Checked conclusion

The valid identity is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{z\in\mathbf F_p}\frac1{Q(z(z+1))}
=-\sum_{j\ge1}[z^{j(p-1)}]Q(z(z+1))^{p-2}
}
\]

with the sum over all surviving cyclotomic degrees.

The proposed stronger statement

\[
\text{only the top Newton/multinomial term survives}
\]

has its first obstruction from the degree-collision terms already at \(p=19\) (and independently at \(p=37\)). Hence the coefficient route does not by itself prove nonvanishing of the norm trace.

The remaining possible route would require an additional symmetry/representation identity cancelling all but one orbit of Newton terms; the all-one shell coefficients alone are insufficient.
