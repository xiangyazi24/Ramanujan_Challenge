# Corrected audit of Calkin Proposition 3 and the Apéry top shell

## Executive correction

Q7315 conflated three different parts of Calkin's paper and consequently reached two false Apéry conclusions.

1. Calkin's **Proposition 3** is not the central-binomial alternating-sum theorem.  It is a prime-interval divisibility theorem for the **nonalternating even-power sums**
   \[
   f_{n,a}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk^a.
   \]
2. For every prime \(p\) and \(0\le r<p\), the Apéry reflection is
   \[
   b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p.
   \]
3. If \(p=n-j>n/2\), then \(0\le j<p\), and Gessel's Lucas congruence gives
   \[
   b_n=b_{p+j}\equiv b_1b_j=5b_j\pmod p.
   \]
   Hence
   \[
   p\mid b_j\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid b_n.
   \]
   For \(p\ne5\) this is an equivalence.
4. The **Lucas-propagation half** of Calkin's proof therefore transfers exactly (with \(f_1=2\) replaced by \(b_1=5\)).  The **interval-forced local-zero half** does not transfer: its Apéry replacement is reflection/folding, not automatic vanishing.

The full proof and the complete errata table follow.

## 1. Calkin Proposition 3: exact statement and exact location

Calkin defines, on printed page 17,
\[
f_{n,a}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk^a.
\]

The following is the exact first sentence of Proposition 3 (printed page 18):

> “For every integer \(m\ge1\), if \(p\) is a prime in the interval
> \[
> \frac nm<p<\frac{2a(n+1)-1}{2ma-1}
> =\frac{n+1}{m}+\frac{n+1-m}{m(2ma-1)},
> \]
> then \(p\mid f_{n,2a}\).”

The paper immediately gives the \(m=1\) specialization
\[
n<p<\frac{2a(n+1)-1}{2a-1}
=n+1+\frac{n}{2a-1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid f_{n,2a}.
\]

### Page/equation locator

* Statement: **printed page 18, Proposition 3, first unnumbered display**.
* Lucas factorization: **Lemma 4, printed page 18**, also an unnumbered display.
* Propagation: **Corollary 5, printed page 19**.
* Local prime-above-index case: **Lemma 6, printed pages 19–20**.
* Completion of Proposition 3: **printed page 20**.

There is **no equation number** attached to Proposition 3 or its interval.  Thus an honest equation citation is “Proposition 3, p. 18, unnumbered display”; assigning it a numbered equation would be fabrication.

The standing variables are positive integers \(n,a\); Proposition 3 additionally quantifies \(m\ge1\), and \(p\) is prime.  Both interval inequalities are strict.

### What Q7315 confused with Proposition 3

Calkin's central-binomial statement is **Theorem 1**, printed page 17:
\[
\binom{2n}{n}\mid
\sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^k\binom{2n}{k}^{a}.
\]
The q-binomial result is **Theorem 2**, not Proposition 3.  Calkin's paper does not label Proposition 3, or any displayed formula used in its proof, as a “star identity.”  The useful structured formulas for Proposition 3 are instead the rising-factorial expansion and summation displayed below.

## 2. Proof of Calkin Proposition 3

I reproduce the argument because it identifies exactly which half transfers to Apéry numbers.

### 2.1 Lucas factorization (Calkin Lemma 4)

Write the base-\(p\) expansion
\[
n=n_0+n_1p+\cdots+n_sp^s.
\]
Lucas's theorem, followed by separation of the digit sums, gives
\[
f_{n,a}\equiv\prod_{i=0}^{s}f_{n_i,a}\pmod p. \tag{C1}
\]
Consequently, if \(0\le \ell<p\) and \(p\mid f_{\ell,a}\), then for every integer \(q\ge0\),
\[
p\mid f_{\ell+qp,a}. \tag{C2}
\]
This is Calkin's Corollary 5.  It is explicitly a relation between different outer indices.

### 2.2 The local forcing lemma for \(n<p\) (Calkin Lemma 6)

Assume
\[
n<p<\frac{2a(n+1)-1}{2a-1},
\]
and put \(p=n+r\), so \(1\le r<p\).  Write rising factorials as
\[
x^{\overline d}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+d-1),\qquad x^{\overline0}=1.
\]
Modulo \(p\),
\[
\binom{p-r}{k}^{2a}
\equiv
\left(\frac{(k+1)^{\overline{r-1}}}{(r-1)!}\right)^{2a}.
\]
Because the rising factorials form a monic integral basis of the polynomial ring by degree, there are integers \(c_j\) such that, with
\[
D=(r-1)(2a-1),
\]
one has the exact polynomial identity
\[
\left((k+1)^{\overline{r-1}}\right)^{2a-1}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{D}c_j(k+r)^{\overline j}. \tag{C3}
\]
The key product identity is
\[
(k+1)^{\overline{r-1}}(k+r)^{\overline j}
=(k+1)^{\overline{r+j-1}}. \tag{C4}
\]
The rising-factorial hockey-stick identity is
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{p-r}(k+1)^{\overline{r+j-1}}
 =\frac{(p-r+1)^{\overline{r+j}}}{r+j}. \tag{C5}
\]
Substituting (C3)–(C5) gives Calkin's actual structured congruence
\[
f_{n,2a}
\equiv
\frac{1}{((r-1)!)^{2a}}
\sum_{j=0}^{D}c_j
\frac{(p-r+1)^{\overline{r+j}}}{r+j}
\pmod p. \tag{C6}
\]
If
\[
r+D=r+(r-1)(2a-1)<p, \tag{C7}
\]
then \(1\le r+j<p\), so every denominator \(r+j\) is invertible modulo \(p\), while every numerator \((p-r+1)^{\overline{r+j}}\) contains the factor \(p\).  Also \((r-1)!\) is invertible modulo \(p\).  Thus every summand in (C6) is zero modulo \(p\).

Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
r+(r-1)(2a-1)<p
&\iff 2ar-2a+1<p\\
&\iff (2a-1)p<2a(n+1)-1\\
&\iff p<\frac{2a(n+1)-1}{2a-1},
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(r=p-n\).  This proves Lemma 6.

### 2.3 General \(m\)

Suppose
\[
\frac nm<p<\frac{2a(n+1)-1}{2ma-1}.
\]
Put
\[
\ell=n-(m-1)p.
\]
The lower inequality gives \(\ell<p\).  The upper inequality is equivalent to
\[
(2a-1)p<2a(\ell+1)-1,
\]
or
\[
p<\frac{2a(\ell+1)-1}{2a-1}.
\]
It also forces \(\ell>0\).  Lemma 6 therefore gives \(p\mid f_{\ell,2a}\), and (C2), with \(n=\ell+(m-1)p\), gives
\[
p\mid f_{n,2a}.
\]
That is Proposition 3.

### 2.4 Its strict-top-shell form

For \(p>n/2\) with \(p\le n\), the relevant Calkin value is \(m=2\).  Write
\[
n=p+j,\qquad 0\le j<p.
\]
Then the upper interval condition
\[
p<\frac{2a(n+1)-1}{4a-1}
\]
is equivalent to
\[
p<\frac{2a(j+1)-1}{2a-1}. \tag{C8}
\]
Calkin's proof has two logically separate steps:

1. **local forcing:** (C8) implies \(p\mid f_{j,2a}\);
2. **Lucas propagation:**
   \[
   f_{p+j,2a}\equiv f_{1,2a}f_{j,2a}=2f_{j,2a}\pmod p.
   \]

This separation is exactly what must be used in the Apéry comparison.

## 3. The correct Apéry congruences

Let
\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\qquad b_0=1,\quad b_1=5.
\]

### 3.1 Gessel–Lucas congruence

For every prime \(p\), every \(q\ge0\), and every \(0\le r<p\),
\[
b_{pq+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p. \tag{A1}
\]
Equivalently, if \(n=n_0+n_1p+\cdots+n_sp^s\), then
\[
b_n\equiv\prod_{i=0}^{s}b_{n_i}\pmod p. \tag{A2}
\]
This is Gessel's theorem, recorded as equation (1.2) in Malik–Straub.

For completeness, (A1) can also be checked directly from the binomial sum.  Write \(k=ps+t\), \(0\le t<p\), and apply Lucas to both binomial factors.  If a low-digit carry makes \(r+t\ge p\), the low digit in \(\binom{pq+r+ps+t}{ps+t}\) is smaller than \(t\), so that term is zero modulo \(p\); this agrees with \(p\mid\binom{r+t}{t}\).  The remaining terms factor into the \(s\)-summand of \(b_q\) times the \(t\)-summand of \(b_r\), and the double sum separates.

### 3.2 Strict top shell

Let \(p=n-j>n/2\).  Then
\[
n=p+j,\qquad 0\le j<p.
\]
By (A1),
\[
b_n=b_{p+j}\equiv b_1b_j=5b_j\pmod p. \tag{A3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\ p\mid b_j\Longrightarrow p\mid b_n\ }. \tag{A4}
\]
For \(p\ne5\), multiplication by \(5\) is invertible, so
\[
\boxed{\ p\mid b_j\Longleftrightarrow p\mid b_n\quad(p\ne5)\ }. \tag{A5}
\]
For \(p=5\), the forward implication still holds, but the reverse implication need not: (A3) makes every \(b_{5+j}\), \(0\le j<5\), vanish modulo \(5\).

No recurrence argument is needed.

### 3.3 Reflection

For every prime \(p\) and \(0\le j<p\),
\[
\boxed{\ b_{p-1-j}\equiv b_j\pmod p\ }. \tag{A6}
\]
Here is a direct proof.  Put \(j=p-r\), with \(1\le r\le p\).  For \(k\ge r\),
\[
\binom{p-r+k}{k}
\]
is divisible by \(p\), so those terms in \(b_{p-r}\) vanish modulo \(p\).  For \(0\le k<r\),
\[
\binom{p-r}{k}\equiv(-1)^k\binom{r+k-1}{k}\pmod p,
\]
and
\[
\binom{p-r+k}{k}\equiv(-1)^k\binom{r-1}{k}\pmod p.
\]
After squaring, the signs disappear, and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_{p-r}
&\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}
 \binom{r+k-1}{k}^2\binom{r-1}{k}^2\\
&=b_{r-1}\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(r-1=p-1-j\), this is (A6).

Combining (A3) and (A6) gives the exact folded top-shell formula
\[
\boxed{\ b_{p+j}\equiv5b_j\equiv5b_{p-1-j}\pmod p\ }. \tag{A7}
\]
This is the congruence used by the repository's folded-index and q=1 carrier computations.

### 3.4 Why the old \((n,p)=(10,5)\) “counterexample” was invalid

For \(n=10\), \(p=5\), one has \(p=n/2\), not \(p>n/2\).  Equivalently, \(j=n-p=5\) is **not** a base-\(5\) digit \(<p\).  The base-\(5\) expansion is \(10=(20)_5\), so
\[
b_{10}\equiv b_2b_0=73\equiv3\pmod5,
\]
whereas
\[
b_5\equiv b_1b_0=5\equiv0\pmod5.
\]
Thus the example only shows that the strict inequality is essential; it says nothing against (A4).

## 4. Does the exact Calkin proposition transfer?

The answer has to be split into its two proof components.

### 4.1 Lucas propagation transfers exactly

Calkin has
\[
f_{p+j,2a}\equiv2f_{j,2a}\pmod p.
\]
Apéry has
\[
b_{p+j}\equiv5b_j\pmod p.
\]
Thus, once a local zero \(p\mid b_j\) is known, the passage from \(j\) to the strict top-shell index \(n=p+j\) is completely rigorous and unconditional.  This is not merely analogous to Calkin's Corollary 5; it is the same base-\(p\) digit mechanism.

### 4.2 Calkin's interval-forced local zero does not transfer

Calkin's special ingredient is Lemma 6: a short prime interval above the index forces
\[
p\mid f_{j,2a}.
\]
For Apéry numbers, the exact computation above instead gives, when \(j=p-r\),
\[
b_j=b_{p-r}\equiv b_{r-1}=b_{p-1-j}\pmod p. \tag{A8}
\]
This is a fold to another Apéry value, not a forced zero.  Therefore an interval condition alone cannot replace the missing hypothesis \(p\mid b_j\).

Two strict-top-shell counterexamples cover both natural exponent identifications.

| Interpretation of Calkin's \(2a\) | \((n,p,j)\) | Exact interval check | Apéry residue |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Match the square on each binomial factor: \(a=1\) | \((11,7,4)\) | \(11/2<7<(2\cdot12-1)/3=23/3\) | \(b_4=33001\equiv3\pmod7\), hence \(b_{11}\equiv5\cdot3\equiv1\pmod7\) |
| Match total binomial degree four: \(a=2\) | \((12,7,5)\) | \(12/2<7<(4\cdot13-1)/7=51/7\) | \(b_5=819005\equiv5\pmod7\), hence \(b_{12}\equiv5\cdot5\equiv4\pmod7\) |

Both have \(p>n/2\).  Hence the literal proposition obtained by replacing \(f_{n,2a}\) with \(b_n\) is false, and the natural fixed choice \(a=2\) is false as well.

### 4.3 Strehl–Franel representation

The correct identity is
\[
\boxed{
 b_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}F_kL_k(n),\qquad
 F_k=\sum_{h=0}^{k}\binom kh^3,\qquad
 L_k(n)=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}.
} \tag{S1}
\]
There is no factor \((-1)^k\).  For example, at \(n=1\), (S1) gives
\[
1+2\cdot2=5,
\]
whereas the signed expression in Q7315 gives \(1-4=-3\).

Calkin Proposition 3 does not apply directly to (S1):

* the Franel number is \(F_k=f_{k,3}\), a **nonalternating odd-power** sum, while Proposition 3 requires \(f_{k,2a}\);
* Calkin explicitly observes on printed page 20 that no analogous regular prime-interval result holds for nonalternating odd powers (apart from the trivial exponent-one case);
* the outer sum in (S1) is weighted by the varying kernel \(L_k(n)\), so it is not another \(f_{n,2a}\).

This does **not** mean that the Strehl identity is useless.  The current repository uses its unsigned truncations to construct exact q=1 carriers.  It means only that those carriers are not a specialization of Calkin Proposition 3.

```python
from math import comb


def apery(n: int) -> int:
    return sum(comb(n, k) ** 2 * comb(n + k, k) ** 2
               for k in range(n + 1))


def primes_below(limit: int) -> list[int]:
    out = []
    for p in range(2, limit):
        if all(p % q for q in range(2, int(p**0.5) + 1)):
            out.append(p)
    return out


P = primes_below(100)
B = [apery(n) for n in range(2 * max(P))]

# Universal reflection.
for p in P:
    for r in range(p):
        assert (B[p - 1 - r] - B[r]) % p == 0

# Strict top shell only: n=p+j with 0 <= j < p.
for p in P:
    for n in range(p, 2 * p):
        j = n - p
        assert 2 * p > n
        assert (B[n] - 5 * B[j]) % p == 0
        if B[j] % p == 0:
            assert B[n] % p == 0
        if p != 5:
            assert (B[n] % p == 0) == (B[j] % p == 0)

# The two relevant strict-top-shell failures of the literal Calkin transfer.
for a, n, p in [(1, 11, 7), (2, 12, 7)]:
    assert 2 * p > n
    assert (4 * a - 1) * p < 2 * a * (n + 1) - 1
    assert B[n] % p != 0
```

The prior Q7315 loop omitted `2*p > n`; that omission is exactly why it admitted the invalid boundary row \((10,5)\).

## 7. Rigorous verdict

### What transfers

For every prime \(p\) and every strict-top-shell decomposition
\[
n=p+j,\qquad 0\le j<p,
\]
the exact Calkin-style propagation mechanism transfers:
\[
\boxed{b_n\equiv5b_j\pmod p}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{p\mid b_j\Longrightarrow p\mid b_n},
\]
and for \(p\ne5\) the implication is an equivalence.  The exact reflection
\[
\boxed{b_{p-1-j}\equiv b_j\pmod p}
\]
is also valid and gives the repository's canonical folding.

### What does not transfer

Calkin's full Proposition 3 contains an additional local theorem: a particular prime interval forces \(p\mid f_{j,2a}\).  For Apéry numbers the corresponding exact reduction is
\[
b_j\equiv b_{p-1-j}\pmod p,
\]
not \(0\).  The strict-top-shell counterexamples \((n,p)=(11,7)\) for \(a=1\) and \((12,7)\) for the natural total-degree choice \(a=2\) disprove the literal interval transfer.

### Final answer

**The exact proposition does not transfer as an automatic prime-interval divisibility theorem for Apéry numbers.  Its Lucas-propagation half does transfer exactly and already proves the required top-shell implication.**

The Strehl–Franel representation does not turn Proposition 3 into an Apéry theorem: its Franel coefficient is a nonalternating cube and its outer sum is weighted.  It remains independently useful for the q=1 carrier constructions already present in the repository, but that usefulness is not a consequence of Calkin Proposition 3.
