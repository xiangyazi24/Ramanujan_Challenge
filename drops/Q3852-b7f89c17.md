# Verdict

**PASS.** The multiplication is valid. It is a deterministic base-times-fibre count, not an independence assumption between the conditions `p | b_a` and “`q` is prime.”

There is no multiplicity leak that changes

\[
S_L(\Delta)\ll \frac{\Delta^3}{(\log T)^2}.
\]

The only literal bookkeeping point is this: if plus and minus rows are pooled without a deterministic sign assignment, one must tag the sign or insert an absolute factor at most `2`. A fixed untagged triple `(p,a,q)` can support at most one plus row and at most one minus row. That factor is harmless in `\ll` notation.

# 1. The first count is a count of base fibres

Put

\[
\mathcal D_\Delta
:=
\left\{
(p,a):
\begin{array}{l}
1\le a\le \lfloor\Delta\rfloor,\\
p>T/7\text{ prime},\\
p\mid b_a
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

Then the quantity in the question is exactly

\[
A=|\mathcal D_\Delta|.
\]

For fixed `a`, the primes in the corresponding fibre are distinct, so their squarefree product divides the positive integer `b_a`. Therefore

\[
\#\{p>T/7:p\mid b_a\}\,\log(T/7)
\le
\sum_{\substack{p>T/7\\p\mid b_a}}\log p
\le \log b_a.
\]

Summing in `a` gives

\[
A\log(T/7)
\le
\sum_{a\le\Delta}\log b_a
\ll \sum_{a\le\Delta}a
\ll \Delta^2,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
A\ll \frac{\Delta^2}{\log T}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

This count does **not** attempt to pay all rows directly. It pays the number of possible base labels `(p,a)`. Reuse of one pair `(p,a)` by many rows is handled entirely by the second, fibre-size estimate.

No valuation statement is being used. A prime `p` is counted once for a fixed `a`, even if `v_p(b_a)>1`; all rows using that same `(p,a)` remain in the fibre over `(p,a)`.

# 2. Exact injection inside one sign

Let a physical quotient-six lower row have lower prime `p`, upper prime `q`, upper digit `u`, and the oriented lower secondary coordinate `a` belonging to its assigned sign.

For plus, the displayed affine identity is

\[
q=\frac{13p+a-u}{12},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{u=13p+a-12q.}
\tag{2.1+}
\]

For minus,

\[
q=p+a+u+1,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{u=q-p-a-1.}
\tag{2.1-}
\]

Consequently, after fixing the sign, `(p,a,q)` determines `u` uniquely. It then determines

\[
d=q-p,
\qquad
n=6q+u,
\]

and all remaining quotient-six row coordinates. Thus the maps

\[
\Phi_+:e\longmapsto(p_e,a_e,q_e),
\qquad
\Phi_-:e\longmapsto(p_e,a_e,q_e)
\]

are injective on the respective physical sign classes.

Equivalently:

```text
fixed sign + fixed (p,a,q)
    -> unique u
    -> unique d=q-p
    -> unique n=6q+u
    -> at most one physical row.
```

There is no hidden plus-orientation multiplicity here. The coordinate `a` is the **oriented secondary endpoint**, not an unordered two-zero pair. Swapping the two plus endpoints changes which endpoint is called `a` and reconstructs a different oriented cell. It cannot produce two plus rows with the same `(p,a,q)`.

The minus secondary role is likewise oriented, so its small/large endpoint distinction does not create a second row with the same triple.

# 3. The fibre over fixed `(p,a)`

Fix a realized pair `(p,a)\in\mathcal D_\Delta`.

## 3.1 Plus

From `0\le u\le\Delta`,

\[
\frac{13p+a-\Delta}{12}
\le q\le
\frac{13p+a}{12}.
\]

Thus every legal plus `q` lies in the ordinary interval

\[
I_+(p,a)
:=
\left[
\frac{13p+a-\Delta}{12},
\frac{13p+a}{12}
\right],
\]

whose real length is `\Delta/12`. The integrality condition merely requires

\[
u\equiv13p+a\pmod {12};
\]

it deletes candidates and cannot enlarge the prime count. The condition `q>p` and all other row-legality conditions also only shrink the interval candidate set.

Distinct legal `u` give distinct `q`, and conversely a fixed `q` gives the unique `u` in (2.1+).

## 3.2 Minus

Here

\[
p+a+1\le q\le p+a+\Delta+1,
\]

so every legal minus `q` lies in

\[
I_-(p,a)
:=[p+a+1,p+a+\Delta+1],
\]

of real length `\Delta`. Again, `q` determines the unique `u` through (2.1-).

Hence, for either fixed sign, the number of physical rows above `(p,a)` is bounded by the number of primes in an interval of length

\[
W\le C\Delta
\]

for an absolute constant `C`.

# 4. Uniform prime count in the fibre

Write

\[
L:=\log T,
\qquad
\Delta=\frac{T^{2/3}}{\omega(T)},
\qquad
\log\omega(T)=o(L).
\]

The subpower assumption implies

\[
\Delta=T^{2/3-o(1)},
\qquad
\frac{\Delta}{L}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\Delta=o(T).
\]

Consider an interval of candidate `q` values of length `W\le C\Delta`.

If

\[
W\le\frac{\Delta}{L},
\]

then the trivial integer count gives

\[
\#\{q\text{ prime in the interval}\}
\le W+1
=O\!\left(\frac{\Delta}{L}\right),
\]

because `\Delta/L\to\infty`.

If instead

\[
W>\frac{\Delta}{L},
\]

then

\[
\log W
\ge
\log\Delta-\log L+O(1)
=
\frac23L-\log\omega(T)-\log L+O(1)
\asymp L.
\]

Also every legal upper prime satisfies `q>p>T/7`, while `W=o(T)`. Thus the interval is in the ordinary Brun--Titchmarsh range for all sufficiently large `T`. Brun--Titchmarsh gives

\[
\#\{q\text{ prime in the interval}\}
\ll \frac{W}{\log W}
\ll \frac{\Delta}{L}.
\]

Therefore, uniformly in the realized pair `(p,a)` and uniformly in the sign,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\text{physical rows of a fixed sign above }(p,a)\}
\ll \frac{\Delta}{\log T}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The exact denominator in the large-interval branch is `\log W`; the subpower hypothesis is precisely what permits the comparison `\log W\asymp\log T`. The small-interval branch prevents any problem when `W` is too short for that comparison.

# 5. Multiplication is a fibre sum, not an independence step

Let `\mathcal R_+` and `\mathcal R_-` be the sign-tagged narrow row sets. From the injection and (4.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal R_\varepsilon|
&\le
\sum_{(p,a)\in\mathcal D_\Delta}
\#\{e\in\mathcal R_\varepsilon:(p_e,a_e)=(p,a)\}\\
&\ll
|\mathcal D_\Delta|\frac{\Delta}{\log T}\\
&=A\frac{\Delta}{\log T},
\qquad \varepsilon\in\{+,-\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus

\[
|\mathcal R_+|+|\mathcal R_-|
\ll
2A\frac{\Delta}{\log T}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

If the physical rows have already been assigned deterministically to one sign, their total is the disjoint sum of these two classes. If they have not, a double-sign physical row may appear twice in the sign-tagged sum, but

\[
S_L(\Delta)
=|\mathcal R_+\cup\mathcal R_-|
\le |\mathcal R_+|+|\mathcal R_-|,
\]

so the same upper bound remains valid.

Combining (1.1) and (5.2),

\[
\boxed{
S_L(\Delta)
\ll
\frac{\Delta^2}{\log T}
\frac{\Delta}{\log T}
=
\frac{\Delta^3}{(\log T)^2}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

No statistical or arithmetic independence between `p | b_a` and the primality of `q` is asserted or needed. The legal `q` fibre may depend arbitrarily on `(p,a)`; (4.1) is a uniform pointwise upper bound for every such fibre.

# 6. Explicit double-counting audit

The possible multiplicities are all harmless:

1. **One prime `p` divides several `b_a`.**  
   It is counted once for each distinct pair `(p,a)`. A sign-specific physical row has one canonical secondary `a`, so it belongs to one such base fibre.

2. **One pair `(p,a)` supports many rows.**  
   This is exactly the multiplicity paid by the `O(\Delta/\log T)` prime-`q` fibre bound.

3. **One `q` occurs for several different pairs `(p,a)`.**  
   These correspond to different lower labels or different secondary coordinates. Summing the fibres is supposed to count those distinct rows separately.

4. **Two rows of the same sign have the same `(p,a,q)`.**  
   Impossible: the inverse affine formula gives the same `u`, then `n=6q+u` gives the same row.

5. **A plus row and a minus row have the same untagged `(p,a,q)`.**  
   There can be at most one of each sign, because each sign has its own unique inverse for `u`. Hence this causes at most a factor `2`, not a growing multiplicity.

6. **A physical row satisfies both sign conditions.**  
   A deterministic sign convention counts it once. Without such a convention, the sign-tagged union bound counts it at most twice, which is still safe.

7. **High `p`-adic valuation of `b_a`.**  
   Irrelevant. The first stage counts distinct base primes; the second stage pays all row reuse above that prime.

8. **Plus integrality modulo `12` or additional legality filters.**  
   These only remove candidate `u` or `q` values and cannot increase a fibre.

# Final conclusion

There is **no invalid logical dependence and no unbounded multiplicity leak**. The proof should phrase the argument as

\[
\text{number of base pairs }(p,a)
\times
\sup_{p,a,\varepsilon}
\text{number of rows in that fibre},
\]

with the sign either included in the indexing key or paid by an absolute factor `2`. With that bookkeeping made explicit, the claimed estimate

\[
\boxed{S_L(\Delta)\ll \Delta^3/(\log T)^2}
\]

is correct.
