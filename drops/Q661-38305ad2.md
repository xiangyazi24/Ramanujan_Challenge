# Constructing the Apéry zero-divisor family: the exact universal object

## Executive verdict

There **is** a clean universal construction, but it is not a fixed polynomial
family on a \(u\)-line.

The universal input is the single integral period series

\[
F(Z)=\sum_{n\ge 0} b_n Z^n\in \mathbf Z[[Z]].
\]

For each prime \(p\), the Apéry--Lucas/Dwork congruence extracts from it the
first Frobenius block

\[
H_p(Z):=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}\bar b_r Z^r
       =\frac{\bar F(Z)}{\bar F(Z^p)}
       \in \mathbf F_p[Z],
\tag{0.1}
\]

where bars denote reduction modulo \(p\).  The polynomial \(Q_p\) is then
obtained from the **coefficient vector** of \(H_p\), not by evaluating \(H_p\)
or \(F\):

\[
\boxed{
F
\xrightarrow{\text{Frobenius quotient mod }p}
H_p
\xrightarrow{\text{interpolate coefficient labels }r\in\mathbf F_p}
B_p(X)
\xrightarrow{X\mapsto-1-X\text{ quotient}}
Q_p(U).
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Here

\[
B_p(r)=\bar b_r,
\qquad
B_p(X)=Q_p(-X(X+1)).
\tag{0.3}
\]

This is the correct explicit universal object: a fixed integral period,
followed by a characteristic-dependent Frobenius/Cartier operation and a
characteristic-dependent interpolation transform.

It has four immediate consequences.

1. There is no fixed \(Q(U,z)\in\mathbf Z[U,z]\) whose specialization at one
   \(z\)-value reduces to all the \(Q_p\).  In fact, the degrees of the \(Q_p\)
   are provably unbounded.
2. The proposal to evaluate \(F\) at \(Z=1\) in \(\mathbf Z_p\) is false:
   \(F(1)\) generally does not converge, and \(n\mapsto b_n\) has no continuous
   extension to all of \(\mathbf Z_p\).
3. Dwork congruence is very useful: it produces (0.1).  It does **not** remove
   the \(p\)-dependence, nor does it turn the coefficient index into a
   geometric coordinate.
4. There is no ordinary finite-type “trace-zero divisor family” over an open
   subscheme of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z\) whose fiber is
   \(D_p=V(Q_p)\).  The degrees grow with \(p\).  Consequently there is no
   horizontal \(S_d\)-monodromy group of such a family to compute.

The natural geometric monodromy remains the monodromy of the fixed Apéry
period/K3 family and its finite-field Mellin transform.  The missing theorem
for AFP is therefore a **horizontal defining-characteristic Mellin
anti-concentration theorem**, not an ordinary polynomial-divisor monodromy
theorem.

There are also two corrections to the premise that matter logically:

- the minimal scalar Picard--Fuchs equation for the standard Apéry generating
  function is order \(3\), not order \(4\);
- roots of \(Q_p\) outside the image of \(r\mapsto-r(r+1)\) are “ghost roots,”
  so the number of all \(\mathbf F_p\)-roots of \(Q_p\) is not exactly the
  Apéry zero count.

## 1. First separate the three variables

Three different variables are being mixed in the proposed constructions.

### 1.1 The period variable \(Z\)

The series

\[
F(Z)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nZ^n
\]

is a period in the geometric parameter \(Z\).  The Apéry numbers are its
**Taylor coefficients at \(Z=0\)**.  They are not obtained by setting
\(Z=1/16\).

For the normalization used in this repository, \(F\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\left[
\theta^3
-Z(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+Z^2(\theta+1)^3
\right]F=0,
\qquad \theta=Z\frac d{dZ}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The finite singularities of this minimal scalar equation occur at \(Z=0\)
and at the two roots of

\[
1-34Z+Z^2=0,
\]

namely \(17\pm12\sqrt2\), together with infinity.  Rank-four cohomological
presentations can occur before removing a Tate factor, but the primitive
Apéry system governing \(F\) is the familiar rank-three symmetric-square
system.

The value \(Z=1/16\) can be considered after analytic continuation in some
realizations, but it gives one period value.  It cannot encode the entire
sequence \(b_0,b_1,\ldots\) by ordinary evaluation.

### 1.2 The coefficient index \(n\)

The integer \(n\) labels Taylor coefficients.  It is dual to the period
variable.  In the toric realization it is produced by constant-term or
diagonal extraction; in the finite-field realization used in this repository
it is produced by a multiplicative Mellin projection.

In particular, the repository's exact bridge has the form

\[
M_p(m)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
   \theta_p(a)\,\omega_p(a)^m,
\qquad
M_p(m)\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}
\quad(m\equiv r\bmod p-1),
\tag{1.2}
\]

where \(a\) is the geometric fiber parameter, while \(r\) is a **character
exponent**.  Thus \(r\) is not another point on the original geometric
parameter line.

### 1.3 The interpolation coordinate \(X\), and its quotient \(U\)

Only after reduction modulo \(p\) do we regard the labels

\[
0,1,\ldots,p-1
\]

as the points of \(\mathbf F_p\), interpolate the values \(b_r\), and then
quotient the label involution

\[
X\longmapsto-1-X.
\]

The quotient coordinate is

\[
U=-X(X+1).
\tag{1.3}
\]

This \(U\)-line is therefore an **index-interpolation line**, not the original
Picard--Fuchs parameter line.  That distinction is the main structural
answer to the question.

## 2. The exact Frobenius block supplied by Dwork/Lucas

Let

\[
\bar F(Z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\bar b_n Z^n\in\mathbf F_p[[Z]]
\]

and define

\[
H_p(Z)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}\bar b_r Z^r.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The full Apéry--Lucas congruence says

\[
b_{mp+r}\equiv b_m b_r\pmod p,
\qquad m\ge0,\quad 0\le r<p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Splitting a coefficient index into its first base-\(p\) digit gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar F(Z)
 &=\sum_{m\ge0}\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}
   \bar b_{mp+r}Z^{mp+r}\\
 &=\sum_{m\ge0}\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}
   \bar b_m\bar b_r Z^{mp+r}\\
 &=H_p(Z)\bar F(Z^p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since \(\bar F(0)=1\), division is legitimate in the formal power-series
ring, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
H_p(Z)=\frac{\bar F(Z)}{\bar F(Z^p)}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

In characteristic \(p\), \(\bar F(Z^p)=\bar F(Z)^p\), so this can also be
written formally as

\[
H_p(Z)=\bar F(Z)^{\,1-p}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The nontrivial content is that the quotient, a priori a power series, is the
polynomial (2.1) of degree at most \(p-1\).

Iterating (2.3) yields the digit factorization

\[
\bar F(Z)=\prod_{j\ge0}H_p\!\left(Z^{p^j}\right),
\tag{2.6}
\]

where every fixed coefficient sees only finitely many factors.  This is the
precise universal multiplicative structure behind the first \(p\)-block.

One can also form the formal \(p\)-adic ratio

\[
\Phi_p(Z)=\frac{F(Z)}{F(Z^p)}\in\mathbf Z_p[[Z]],
\tag{2.7}
\]

whose reduction is \(H_p\).  Stronger Dwork congruences compare ratios of
successive truncations and, on an ordinary domain, lead to a unit-root
function.  But the mod-\(p\) object relevant to \(Q_p\) is already exactly
\(H_p\).

Thus the answer to part (D) is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Dwork congruence defines the universal first-Frobenius block }H_p;
\text{ it does not define a fixed }Q(U).
}
\]

## 3. A genuinely universal interpolation object over \(\mathbf Z\)

There is an explicit characteristic-zero object that simultaneously stores
all integer values \(b_n\).  It is not an ordinary power series in the index;
it is a Newton/binomial series.

Let

\[
c_k=\Delta^k b_0
    =\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}b_j\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Newton inversion gives, for every nonnegative integer \(n\),

\[
\boxed{
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n c_k\binom nk.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence define the formal Newton object

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr B(X)=\sum_{k\ge0}c_k\binom Xk.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This lives naturally in the binomial/Integer-valued-polynomial completion

\[
\prod_{k\ge0}\mathbf Z\binom Xk,
\]

not in \(\mathbf Z[X]\) and not automatically in a \(p\)-adic Tate algebra.
Its evaluation at \(X=n\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}\) is nevertheless finite and equals
\(b_n\).

For a prime \(p\), truncate before the first noninvertible factorial:

\[
\mathscr B_{<p}(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}c_k\binom Xk.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Because \(k!\) is invertible modulo \(p\) for \(k<p\), this reduces to a
polynomial in \(\mathbf F_p[X]\) of degree at most \(p-1\).  For
\(r\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\), all terms with \(k>r\) vanish, so

\[
\mathscr B_{<p}(r)\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Therefore the unique interpolation polynomial \(B_p\) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
B_p(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\bar c_k\binom Xk
\in\mathbf F_p[X].
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Equivalently, using the finite-field delta functions,

\[
\boxed{
B_p(X)=\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}
\bar b_r\left(1-(X-r)^{p-1}\right).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Formula (3.6) is probably the cleanest answer to part (B): it separates the
universal integer data from the \(p\)-dependent truncation as far as is
possible.

It also corrects a small misconception in the question.  There is no single
statement that “\(b_r\) is a polynomial in \(r\) of degree \(2r\).”  For fixed
\(k\),

\[
\binom Xk^2\binom{X+k}{k}^2
\]

is a polynomial in \(X\) of degree \(4k\), but the upper summation limit in the
Apéry formula changes with \(r\).  The universal object for a sequence with a
moving upper limit is the Newton series (3.3), not a polynomial of a fixed
degree.

## 4. Reflection descent and an explicit formula for \(Q_p\)

For odd \(p\), the reflection congruence is

\[
\bar b_r=\bar b_{p-1-r}=\bar b_{-1-r}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently \(B_p(-1-X)\) and \(B_p(X)\) agree at every point of
\(\mathbf F_p\).  Both have degree at most \(p-1\), so

\[
B_p(-1-X)=B_p(X)
\quad\text{as polynomials.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The invariant ring is

\[
\mathbf F_p[X]^{\langle X\mapsto-1-X\rangle}
 =\mathbf F_p[X(X+1)]
 =\mathbf F_p[-X(X+1)].
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus there is a unique polynomial \(Q_p(U)\) satisfying

\[
\boxed{
B_p(X)=Q_p(-X(X+1)),
\qquad
\deg Q_p\le\frac{p-1}{2}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Let

\[
d=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
u_r=-r(r+1),\quad 0\le r\le d.
\]

These \(d+1\) values are distinct in \(\mathbf F_p\).  Therefore an entirely
explicit formula is

\[
\boxed{
Q_p(U)=\sum_{r=0}^{d}\bar b_r
\prod_{\substack{0\le s\le d\\s\ne r}}
\frac{U-\nu_s}{\nu_r-\nu_s}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Combining (2.1), (3.7), and (4.5) gives a completely explicit universal
construction.  The point is that the Lagrange kernel and the quotient map are
rebuilt in each characteristic.

A useful compact description is

\[
\boxed{
Q_p
 =\operatorname{Desc}_{X\mapsto-1-X}
  \circ\operatorname{Interp}_{\mathbf F_p}
  \circ\operatorname{Coeff}_{<p}
  \left(\frac{\bar F(Z)}{\bar F(Z^p)}\right).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the correct replacement for a hypothetical \(Q(U,Z)\).

## 5. The exact relation between roots of \(Q_p\) and Apéry zeros

The quotient map satisfies

\[
1-4U=(2X+1)^2
\quad\text{when }U=-X(X+1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Define

\[
\mathcal H_p
 =\{u\in\mathbf F_p:\chi_p(1-4u)=1\},
\tag{5.2}
\]

where \(\chi_p\) is the quadratic character.  Every point of
\(\mathcal H_p\) has two preimages under \(X\mapsto-X(X+1)\).  The branch
value \(u=1/4\) has the single preimage \(X=(p-1)/2\).

Set

\[
P_p=\#\{u\in\mathcal H_p:Q_p(u)=0\},
\qquad
\varepsilon_p=\mathbf 1_{Q_p(1/4)=0}.
\]

Then the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
Z(p):=\#\{r\in\mathbf F_p:b_r=0\}
     =2P_p+\varepsilon_p.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

A root \(u\) with \(\chi_p(1-4u)=-1\) is a genuine root of \(Q_p\) but has no
index \(r\in\mathbf F_p\) above it.  It is a ghost root for the Apéry zero
problem.

Therefore

\[
\#\{u\in\mathbf F_p:Q_p(u)=0\}=O(1)
\]

is sufficient for a pointwise \(Z(p)=O(1)\) statement, but it is not literally
equivalent.  The exact object is the weighted root count (5.3) on the
quadratic-image subset.

There is a second caution.  The observed Poisson\((1/2)\) law predicts bounded
moments and bounded mean, but it predicts an unbounded maximum over primes.
Thus if “AFP” is interpreted as a uniform pointwise \(Z(p)=O(1)\), the random
model is evidence against that formulation.  The prime-averaged factorial
moment statements used elsewhere in the project are the formulation aligned
with the computation.

## 6. Why no fixed polynomial or finite-type divisor family can exist

The growing degree is not merely an empirical observation; it follows from
the growth of the Apéry sequence.

### Proposition 6.1

For every fixed \(D\), only finitely many primes \(p\) can satisfy

\[
\deg Q_p\le D.
\tag{6.1}
\]

In particular, the degrees \(\deg Q_p\) are unbounded along the primes, even
though degree drops below \((p-1)/2\) may occur.

#### Proof

Assume that an infinite set of primes \(\mathcal P\) satisfies (6.1).  Let
\(q_D(U)\in\mathbf Q[U]\) be the unique polynomial of degree at most \(D\)
with

\[
q_D(-i(i+1))=b_i,
\qquad 0\le i\le D.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For every sufficiently large \(p\in\mathcal P\), avoiding the finitely many
primes in the denominators of \(q_D\), the points
\(-i(i+1)\), \(0\le i\le D\), remain distinct modulo \(p\).  Both \(Q_p\) and
the reduction of \(q_D\) have degree at most \(D\) and agree at these \(D+1\)
points.  Hence

\[
Q_p\equiv q_D\pmod p.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Fix any \(n\ge0\).  For all sufficiently large \(p\in\mathcal P\) with
\(p>n\), interpolation gives

\[
q_D(-n(n+1))\equiv Q_p(-n(n+1))\equiv b_n\pmod p.
\tag{6.4}
\]

After multiplying by a fixed denominator, the rational number
\(q_D(-n(n+1))-b_n\) is divisible by infinitely many primes, so it is zero.
Thus

\[
b_n=q_D(-n(n+1))
\quad\text{for every }n\ge0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The right side grows polynomially in \(n\), while

\[
b_n\ge \binom{2n}{n}^2
\]

from the \(k=n\) summand in the Apéry formula, and hence grows exponentially.
This is a contradiction. \(\square\)

### Consequences

1. A fixed \(Q(U,Z)\in\mathbf Z[U,Z]\), specialized at any fixed rational
   \(Z=Z_0\), has uniformly bounded \(U\)-degree.  It cannot reduce to all
   \(Q_p\).
2. More generally, a finite locally free family over a nonempty open subset of
   \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z\) has constant finite rank.  It cannot have
   \(D_p=V(Q_p)\) as fibers of unbounded degree.
3. A quasi-finite finite-type morphism likewise has bounded fiber degree after
   a finite stratification.  Allowing a general finite-type family does not
   fix the problem.
4. The disjoint union
   \(\coprod_p\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf F_p[U]/(Q_p)\) exists tautologically,
   but its base is a disconnected collection of characteristics.  It has no
   horizontal paths and no useful horizontal monodromy.

This disposes of the literal universal-polynomial and universal finite-cover
interpretations.

## 7. Why the proposed \(p\)-adic evaluation construction is not correct

Part (C) proposes:

1. evaluate \(F\) at \(Z=1\) in \(\mathbf Z_p\);
2. restrict to \(Z^p=Z\);
3. quotient by reflection.

All three steps mix the period variable with the coefficient index.

### 7.1 \(F(1)\) generally diverges

A necessary condition for

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}b_n
\]

to converge \(p\)-adically is \(b_n\to0\) in \(\mathbf Z_p\).  But the Lucas
congruence gives, for every \(s\ge1\),

\[
b_{p^s}\equiv b_1=5\pmod p.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For every prime \(p\ge7\) (and also for \(p=3\)), these are \(p\)-adic units.
Thus the terms do not tend to zero, and \(F(1)\) does not converge.

Dwork theory constructs analytic continuations or unit-root **ratios** on
appropriate ordinary domains.  It does not assert that the original Taylor
series can be summed term-by-term at every \(p\)-adic unit.

### 7.2 There is no continuous interpolation \(n\mapsto b_n\) on all of
\(\mathbf Z_p\)

Since \(p^s\to0\) in \(\mathbf Z_p\), a continuous function
\(f:\mathbf Z_p\to\mathbf Z_p\) satisfying \(f(n)=b_n\) on nonnegative
integers would have

\[
b_{p^s}\longrightarrow f(0)=b_0=1.
\]

But (7.1) gives \(b_{p^s}\equiv5\pmod p\), which is incompatible with
convergence to \(1\) for \(p\ne2\).  Thus the Newton series (3.3) is a formal
pointwise interpolation on nonnegative integers, not a globally continuous
Mahler function on \(\mathbf Z_p\).

### 7.3 The equation \(Z^p=Z\) concerns the wrong variable

The Teichmüller points lie in the period/fiber variable.  The reflection

\[
r\longmapsto p-1-r
\]

acts on coefficient or character-exponent labels.  There is no operation
“restrict \(F(Z)\) to \(Z^p=Z\), then quotient” that turns period values into
Taylor coefficients.

Coefficient extraction is a transform: constant term, diagonal, Cauchy
coefficient, or finite-field Mellin projection.  It is not evaluation.

Hence part (C), as written, is not the right framework.

## 8. What a \(p\)-adic power-series lift of \(Q_p\) would mean

For a **fixed** prime \(p\), lifting \(Q_p\) is trivial.  Choose any lifts of
its coefficients and obtain

\[
\widetilde Q_p(U)\in\mathbf Z_p[U]\subset\mathbf Z_p\langle U\rangle.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Because it is a polynomial, it converges on all of \(\mathbf Z_p\).  This
construction carries no extra geometry and has no canonical monodromy.  A
coefficientwise Teichmüller lift is one possible normalization, but it is
still just a lift of an already known finite-field polynomial.

A single global formal series

\[
Q(U)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kU^k
\]

whose reduction is \(Q_p\) for every \(p\) would require strong, presently
unknown cross-prime compatibility of every low coefficient
\([U^k]Q_p\).  The natural construction above does not provide that
compatibility: the Lagrange denominators, the truncation length, and the
identification of exponents with \(\mathbf F_p\) all change with \(p\).

One can always package all coefficient systems tautologically in
\(\widehat{\mathbf Z}=\prod_p\mathbf Z_p\), but that merely restates the list
of \(Q_p\)'s.  It gives no finite-type geometry, no comparison between
characteristics, and no useful monodromy theorem.

The meaningful universal formal object is instead (3.3), the Newton series in
the **index variable**.  Its reduction must still be truncated at \(p\) and
then descended by the mod-\(p\) reflection involution.  It is not an
overconvergent modular form on a universal \(U\)-line.

## 9. Where the crystal actually lives

The Apéry crystal/F-isocrystal lives over the geometric parameter of the
fixed toric or Picard--Fuchs family.  In the notation already used in the
repository, one has a fixed Laurent-polynomial/K3 family with fiber parameter
\(a\), a rank-three primitive trace \(\theta_p(a)\), and then the finite-field
Mellin transform (1.2).

The architecture is therefore

```text
fixed integral toric/K3 family in the geometric parameter a
        |
        | Frobenius trace and finite-field Mellin transform against omega^r
        v
Apéry coefficient b_r modulo p
        |
        | identify exponent labels with r in F_p,
        | interpolate, and quotient r <-> -1-r
        v
Q_p(U).
```

The first arrow is geometric and Tannakian.  The second arrow is a
\(p\)-dependent finite linear-algebra operation.

There is a further structural obstruction: the exponent \(r\) naturally
belongs to

\[
\widehat{\mathbf F_p^\times}\cong\mathbf Z/(p-1)\mathbf Z,
\]

whereas interpolation treats its chosen integer representative as a point of
\(\mathbf F_p\).  These are different group structures, and the identification
depends on a choice of generator.  As \(p\) varies, there is no fixed
algebraic curve carrying all these exponent sets.

For a fixed \(p\), \(p\)-adic weight space

\[
\mathcal W_p=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}
(\mathbf Z_p^\times,\mathbf G_m)
\]

is a natural analytic parameter space for \(p\)-adic characters.  It can host
\(p\)-adic Mellin families.  But it is prime-specific, and the map
\(r\mapsto-r(r+1)\bmod p\) is not a universal coordinate on weight space.
Thus even the weight-space formulation does not produce a single crystalline
object “restricted to the \(u\)-line.”

## 10. What monodromy is available, and what is not

There are several distinct monodromy notions here.

| Object | Parameter | Natural monodromy | What it controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apéry Picard--Fuchs/K3 system | geometric fiber \(a\) or \(Z\) | fixed-rank differential, \(\ell\)-adic, or crystalline group; primitive system is \(SO_3/PSL_2\)-type | periods and Frobenius traces of fibers |
| Finite-field Mellin transform | multiplicative character \(\chi\) | Katz/Tannakian Mellin monodromy | distribution and correlations of Mellin trace values as characters vary |
| \(D_p=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf F_p[U]/(Q_p)\) | no varying parameter | arithmetic Frobenius, hence a cyclic finite-field Galois group | the one factorization of \(Q_p\) |
| \(Q_p(U)-T=0\) | additive shift \(T\) | a permutation group in \(S_{\deg Q_p}\), often testable by Morse/branch criteria | factorization of \(Q_p(U)-t\) as \(t\) varies for one fixed \(p\) |
| hypothetical family across primes | none presently constructed | none | would be needed for horizontal divisor monodromy |

Two points are decisive.

### 10.1 A single zero divisor has no \(S_d\) monodromy

The splitting field of a polynomial over \(\mathbf F_p\) is a finite field, so
its Galois group is cyclic, generated by Frobenius.  To obtain an ambient
\(S_d\)-question one must add a parameter and study

\[
Q_p(U)-T
\quad\text{over }\overline{\mathbf F}_p(T).
\tag{10.1}
\]

Even if this cover has geometric monodromy \(S_d\), Chebotarev describes
shifts \(T=t\) as \(t\) varies.  It does not force the distinguished fiber
\(t=0\) to behave generically as \(p\) varies.

### 10.2 Q659's proposed “local system on \(y^2=1-4u\)” is not yet a geometric
construction

The curve

\[
y^2=1-4u
\]

correctly records the two-to-one quotient of the finite label set
\(r\mapsto-r(r+1)\).  But the recurrence index \(r\) is a difference variable,
not automatically a geometric parameter carrying the original Apéry
\(\ell\)-adic local system.  One cannot pull the K3 sheaf to this curve merely
by renaming the coefficient index.

Thus the “trace-zero divisor monodromy theorem” proposed in Q659 presupposes
the very family whose existence is in question.  The present construction
shows that no ordinary finite-type version of that family exists.

This answer therefore sharpens and partially corrects Q659:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the missing bridge is not an uncomputed monodromy group of an already
existing }u\text{-family};
\text{ the }u\text{-family itself is only a }p\text{-dependent interpolation.}
}
\]

## 11. The correct replacement theorem for AFP

The defining-characteristic zero event is, through (1.2),

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\iff
M_p(r)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}
\tag{11.1}
\]

with the normalization and endpoint qualifications already recorded in the
repository.

The natural theorem is therefore horizontal anti-concentration for these
Mellin values.  Let \(P_p\) be the number of noncentral zero pairs from (5.3).
A useful hierarchy is:

### Bounded first moment

\[
\sum_{p\le X}P_p\ll\pi(X).
\tag{11.2}
\]

### Bounded fixed factorial moments

For every fixed \(k\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le X}(P_p)_k\ll_k\pi(X),
}
\tag{11.3}
\]

where \((x)_k=x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)\).

### Poisson-strength asymptotics

The random symmetric-interpolation model predicts

\[
\frac1{\pi(X)}\sum_{p\le X}(P_p)_k
\longrightarrow\left(\frac12\right)^k.
\tag{11.4}
\]

Equivalently, one seeks correlation estimates for distinct character
exponents \(r_1,\ldots,r_k\):

\[
\sum_{p\le X}
\#\left\{
(r_1,\ldots,r_k):
M_p(r_i)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}
\text{ for all }i
\right\}
\ll_k\pi(X),
\tag{11.5}
\]

with the admissible reflection quotient imposed.

This is a defining-characteristic, horizontal, shrinking-target statement.
Ordinary complex/\(\ell\)-adic Sato--Tate equidistribution does not see a
single residue class of relative size \(1/p\), and generic \(S_d\) monodromy
of \(Q_p(U)-T\) does not control \(T=0\).  The repository's Gross--Koblitz
analysis further shows that the zero event is cancellation among many
minimum-slope units, not a simple Newton-polygon condition.

So the corrected missing theorem is:

> **Horizontal Mellin trace-zero theorem.**  The defining-characteristic
> reductions of the Apéry Mellin coefficients have bounded fixed factorial
> moments (and conjecturally Poisson\((1/2)\) moments) across primes.

That theorem is precisely aligned with the current P3.2 moment route.

## 12. Direct answers to (A)--(E)

### (A) Is there \(Q(U,Z)\in\mathbf Z[U,Z]\) with
\(Q(U,1/16)\bmod p=Q_p(U)\)?

**No.**  A fixed polynomial has bounded \(U\)-degree, while Proposition 6.1
shows \(\deg Q_p\) is unbounded.  More conceptually, \(Z\) is the period
variable and \(r\) is a coefficient index.  Specializing \(Z\) cannot perform
coefficient extraction and finite-field interpolation.

The standard Apéry numbers are the coefficients of the order-three period
(1.1), not values obtained at \(Z=1/16\).

### (B) Can the \(p\)-dependence be separated from the integer values?

**Yes, exactly, but in the Newton basis rather than by a fixed ordinary
polynomial.**  The universal coefficients are

\[
c_k=\Delta^k b_0\in\mathbf Z,
\]

and

\[
B_p(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\bar c_k\binom Xk.
\]

Then \(Q_p\) is the reflection descent of \(B_p\).  The truncation at \(p\),
the reduction of factorial denominators, and the quotient interpolation
remain intrinsically \(p\)-dependent.

### (C) Is \(F(1)\), Teichmüller restriction, and reflection the right
framework?

**No.**  \(F(1)\) generally diverges; \(n\mapsto b_n\) is not continuous on
all of \(\mathbf Z_p\); Teichmüller restriction acts on the period variable,
whereas reflection acts on coefficient labels.  The correct p-adic object is
the Frobenius ratio \(F(Z)/F(Z^p)\) or the corresponding unit-root period,
followed by coefficient/Mellin extraction—not evaluation at \(Z=1\).

### (D) Does Dwork congruence define the universal family?

It defines the most important universal intermediate object:

\[
H_p(Z)=\bar F(Z)/\bar F(Z^p)
      =\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}\bar b_rZ^r.
\]

It also gives the base-\(p\) digit product (2.6).  But \(Q_p\) is obtained from
the **coefficients** of \(H_p\) by a \(p\)-dependent interpolation and
reflection quotient.  Dwork congruence does not turn these \(Q_p\)'s into the
fibers of one finite-type polynomial family.

### (E) Is there a universal \(p\)-adic formal series in \(U\)?

For each fixed \(p\), a lift \(\widetilde Q_p\in\mathbf Z_p[U]\) exists
trivially and is already overconvergent because it is a polynomial.  This
contains no new information.

A single cross-prime \(U\)-series would require unproved coefficient
compatibilities and is not supplied by the crystal.  The actual universal
formal object is the Newton series \(\mathscr B(X)\), while the actual
crystalline object lives over the geometric fiber parameter and reaches
\(b_r\) through a Mellin transform.

## 13. Concrete next steps

The construction above suggests three sharply separated computational
programs.

### 13.1 Verify the universal pipeline

For each prime, compute independently

\[
H_p(Z)=\frac{\bar F(Z)}{\bar F(Z^p)},
\qquad
B_p(X)=\sum_{k<p}\bar c_k\binom Xk,
\qquad
B_p(X)=Q_p(-X(X+1)).
\]

This provides exact consistency checks on the Dwork, interpolation, and
reflection stages.

### 13.2 Test whether any low \(U\)-coefficient compatibility exists

For fixed \(j\), record \([U^j]Q_p\) across primes.  A global \(U\)-series
would force these residues to be reductions of one rational/integral
coefficient after excluding finitely many primes.  The expected behavior is
that no such compatibility exists.  This is a direct falsification test for
the global-\(U\)-series idea.

### 13.3 Compute the statistics that actually enter AFP

Record

\[
(P_p)_k,
\qquad k=1,2,3,4,
\]

and their prime averages, with roots restricted to the admissible quadratic
image.  These quantities directly test (11.3)--(11.4).  They are more
relevant to P3.2 than the factorization type of \(Q_p\) on its ghost-root half.

The per-prime Morse/discriminant test for \(Q_p(U)-T\) remains an interesting
independent experiment, but it should be labeled correctly: it studies shift
monodromy in \(T\), not the horizontal zero fiber.

## Bottom line

The universal object is not a polynomial \(Q(U,Z)\), not the value \(F(1)\),
and not a crystal on the interpolation \(u\)-line.  It is the fixed Apéry
period together with its Frobenius/Cartier extraction:

\[
\boxed{
H_p(Z)=\frac{\bar F(Z)}{\bar F(Z^p)}
      =\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}\bar b_rZ^r,
}
\]

followed by the universal Newton interpolation

\[
\boxed{
B_p(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\overline{\Delta^k b_0}\binom Xk
}
\]

and the characteristic-\(p\) reflection descent

\[
\boxed{
B_p(X)=Q_p(-X(X+1)).
}
\]

This is an explicit, exact, and genuinely universal construction, but it is a
**Frobenius-transform construction**, not a finite-type family of divisors.
Accordingly, “zero-divisor monodromy” is not presently a well-defined bridge
from Apéry monodromy to AFP.  The correct bridge is horizontal
anti-concentration of the defining-characteristic Mellin coefficients, most
naturally expressed through bounded prime-averaged factorial moments.
