# Dwork analyticity does not compress the Apéry Hasse DFT

## Executive verdict

The Dwork-crystal viewpoint is valuable, but the proposed implication

```text
p-adic overconvergence in the fiber parameter
    => bounded-degree Hasse function
    => few zero Mellin coefficients
```

fails at both arrows.

The most important correction is elementary and decisive:

```text
If H_p(t) is a polynomial of degree O(1) on F_p^×,
then its multiplicative Fourier transform has only O(1) nonzero modes.
Hence almost all b_r vanish.
```

So the dream conclusion is exactly backwards.  Low monomial degree in the geometric parameter means **spectral concentration**, whereas AFP asks for **spectral anti-concentration**.

There are four further corrections.

1. In the notation of `proof.tex`,
   \[
   A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r
   \]
   is the scalar Hasse--Witt polynomial.  Its evaluation roots are nonordinary fibers; `Z(p)` counts zero **coefficients**, equivalently zero finite Mellin modes.  These are dual loci.
2. A Dwork unit-root formula is defined on the ordinary locus, or on a strict rigid neighborhood of it.  At a Hasse zero the unit-root line disappears.  Thus the slogan
   \[
   A_p(t)=\alpha_p(t)\pmod p
   \]
   is at best a basis-dependent ordinary-locus shorthand, not a global equality of functions across the Hasse locus.
3. “Radius of convergence greater than one” by itself gives no degree bound.  A polynomial of arbitrarily large degree is entire.  To force bounded reduction degree one needs a **uniform Gauss-norm estimate** as well as a radius uniformly separated from one.
4. AFP in the current project is the average statement
   \[
   \sum_{p\le X}Z(p)\ll\pi(X).
   \]
   The pointwise assertion `Z(p)=O(1)` is the stronger Hypothesis `Z`, which the Poisson model in `proof.tex` predicts is false: the support should be unbounded, although extremely slowly growing.

The viable Dwork program is therefore not to make the fiberwise Hasse polynomial low-degree.  It is to construct the **character-side normalized Mellin crystal** and prove that its first-Hasse failure locus has bounded average mass, or bounded second factorial moment.  That missing input is precisely the “crystalline Mellin anti-concentration” theorem already isolated in `proof.tex`.

## 1. Exact Fourier algebra: the low-degree dream has the wrong sign

Let
\[
G=\mathbf F_p^\times,
\qquad N=p-1,
\]
and work in the group algebra
\[
\mathbf F_p[T,T^{-1}]/(T^N-1).
\]
Every function `f:G→F_p` has a unique expansion
\[
f(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}c_kT^k.
\]
For `0≤r<N`, define its multiplicative Fourier coefficient
\[
\mathcal M_r(f)=\sum_{t\in G}f(t)t^{-r}.
\]
Orthogonality gives
\[
\sum_{t\in G}t^{k-r}
=
\begin{cases}
N=-1 & k=r,\\
0 & k\ne r
\end{cases}
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\mathcal M_r(f)=-c_r.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

For the Apéry Hasse polynomial
\[
A_p(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}b_kT^k,
\]
the endpoint term `T^(p-1)` merges with the constant term on `G`, while for every interior index `1≤r≤p-2` equation (1.1) gives exactly
\[
\boxed{
b_r=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-r}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]
Thus the coefficient vector `(b_r)` is not merely related to the Mellin spectrum: it **is** the Mellin spectrum.

### Consequence 1: bounded polynomial degree is catastrophic for AFP

Suppose a function on `G` is represented by a polynomial
\[
P(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{D}c_kT^k,
\qquad D<p-1.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal M_r(P)=0
\qquad(D<r<p-1).
\]
Hence
\[
\#\{r:\mathcal M_r(P)=0\}\ge p-2-D.
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\deg P=O(1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{all but }O(1)\text{ Mellin coefficients vanish}.}
\tag{1.3}
\]
This is the opposite of AFP.

The same statement holds for a Laurent polynomial of bandwidth `D`: it has at most `2D+1` nonzero Mellin modes.  A constant function and a monomial are the extreme examples; each is entire and of minimal algebraic complexity, yet its nontrivial Mellin transform is supported on zero or one character.

### Consequence 2: the actual Apéry function has linear spectral bandwidth

The no-two-consecutive-zeros theorem in `proof.tex` already implies that a positive proportion of the `b_r` are nonzero.  Hence the canonical group-algebra representative of `A_p|_G` has `≫p` nonzero monomials and any centered Laurent representative has bandwidth `≫p`.

The current manuscript records a sharper direct obstruction: its canonical character interpolation cannot be represented by a Laurent polynomial of degree below
\[
\frac{p-7}{4}.
\]
So the direct unnormalized horizontal complexity is provably linear in `p`; it is not an artifact of a poor interpolation.

This also explains why the square factorization
\[
A_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
\qquad \Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1,
\]
does not solve the problem.  `hasse_square_no_go.tex` proves that reciprocity and squareness alone permit arbitrary initial coefficient patterns.  They constrain evaluation roots and global factorization, not the Fourier-coordinate zero pattern.

## 2. What Dwork theory actually supplies

Dwork's construction begins with a period series and its `p^s`-truncations.  In the scalar ordinary case one obtains congruences of the schematic form
\[
F_{p^{s+1}}(t)\,F_{p^s}(t^p)^{-1}
\equiv
F_{p^s}(t)\,F_{p^{s-1}}(t^p)^{-1}
\pmod{p^s},
\]
and a limit Frobenius/unit-root function on the locus where the first Hasse truncation is invertible.

This is exactly the mechanism reviewed in Beukers--Vlasenko, *Dwork Crystals I*: for the Legendre family, the unit root is a limit of ratios of truncated hypergeometric periods, provided the first truncation is a `p`-adic unit.  Adolphson--Sperber similarly express toric unit roots using the `p`-adic analytic continuation of a ratio of `A`-hypergeometric functions.  Yu's Dwork-family result explicitly states the analytic formula **away from the Hasse locus**.

These results give:

- a finite-rank Frobenius module;
- congruences between successive truncation levels;
- an analytic Frobenius matrix or unit-root eigenvalue on an ordinary affinoid;
- quantitative control in the vertical `p`-power direction.

They do **not** give:

- a bounded-degree reduction in the parameter `t`;
- a bounded number of nonzero monomial coefficients of the Hasse polynomial;
- a bound for zero Fourier coordinates;
- coupling among the `p-1` distinct tame Kummer twists.

### 2.1 The unit root is not a global analytic function across supersingular points

At an ordinary point, the first Frobenius/Hasse scalar is a unit and a rank-one slope-zero subcrystal exists.  At a Hasse zero, that slope-zero line disappears.  One should therefore distinguish:

1. the mod-`p` Hasse scalar `A_p(t)`;
2. a Frobenius matrix entry in a chosen integral basis;
3. the unit-root eigenvalue on the ordinary locus;
4. the Dwork ratio used to compute that eigenvalue.

They are related, but they are not globally identical objects.  In particular, analytic continuation of the unit-root ratio on the ordinary tube cannot be used as though it were one integral power series whose reduction on every fiber is `A_p(t)`.

### 2.2 The classical Legendre family is a complete counterexample to the generic inference

Let
\[
m=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
D_p(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}^2t^k
\]
be the Deuring Hasse polynomial of the Legendre family.  This is the basic rank-two hypergeometric family in Dwork's unit-root theory.  Nevertheless, for `1≤r≤p-2`,
\[
\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}D_p(t)t^{-r}
=
\begin{cases}
-\binom mr^2,&1\le r\le m,\\
0,&m<r\le p-2.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus exactly
\[
\frac{p-3}{2}
\]
nontrivial Mellin coefficients vanish.

So even the package

```text
rank 2 + hypergeometric rigidity + unit-root formula + p-adic analyticity
```

does not imply Mellin anti-concentration.  Any successful theorem must use an additional Apéry-specific property.

## 3. What a radius estimate would have to look like

There is a precise conditional statement relating analytic radius to reduction degree, but it requires much more than `R>1`.

### Lemma: radius plus Gauss norm implies a truncation bound

Let
\[
f_p(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_{p,n}t^n\in\mathcal O_p[[t]]
\]
be analytic on the closed disk `|t|_p≤R_p`, with
\[
R_p=p^{\delta_p}>1,
\qquad
\|f_p\|_{R_p}\le p^{C_p}.
\]
The nonarchimedean Cauchy estimate gives
\[
|a_{p,n}|_pR_p^n\le p^{C_p},
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{v_p(a_{p,n})\ge \delta_p n-C_p.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Therefore the reduction of `f_p` modulo `p` has degree at most
\[
\boxed{
D_p\le \left\lfloor\frac{C_p+1}{\delta_p}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{3.2}
\]
An analogous estimate on an annulus, with decay in both directions, bounds Laurent bandwidth.

To deduce `D_p=O(1)`, one would need both
\[
\inf_p\delta_p>0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sup_p C_p<\infty,
\tag{3.3}
\]
for one integral expansion covering the relevant points.

### Why Dwork overconvergence does not provide (3.3) here

1. **Radius alone is insufficient.**  A polynomial of degree `p` is entire, but its Gauss norm on a disk of radius `R>1` grows like `R^p`.  The parameter `C_p` in (3.2) then grows with `p`, and the bound is tautological.
2. **The often-quoted radius `>1` belongs to the Dwork splitting function or to a strict ordinary affinoid.**  It is not automatically a single parameter disk centered at zero containing every Teichmüller point of `F_p^×`.
3. **The singularities lie at unit distance.**  In the standard coordinate of `proof.tex`, the conifold discriminant is
   \[
   \Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1,
   \]
   so at good primes its two roots are `p`-adic units.  A disk centered at zero of radius `>1` contains them and cannot be a uniform nonsingular parameter disk for the Picard--Fuchs object.  If one uses a pullback coordinate with a singularity at `1/16`, that point is also a `p`-adic unit for every odd prime, so the same obstruction remains.
4. **Ordinary tubes are disconnected modulo `p`.**  Analyticity separately on residue tubes does not couple the values on all `p-1` Teichmüller points.  A locally analytic function can have unrelated reductions on different residue disks unless a global norm/continuation theorem supplies additional relations.
5. **The actual Hasse polynomial disproves bounded reduction degree.**  It is explicitly
   \[
   A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r,
   \]
   and has `≫p` nonzero coefficients.  Hence no integral lift reducing to this polynomial can satisfy uniform bounds of the form (3.3) in the monomial coordinate.

Finally, even if (3.3) miraculously held, equation (1.3) shows that the result would be the wrong one: bounded degree would produce almost all-zero Mellin spectrum.

## 4. Which notions of “bounded complexity” help, and which do not

The word `complexity` hides several inequivalent notions.

### 4.1 Polynomial degree or monomial sparsity

This controls Fourier **support** directly:

```text
D monomials  =>  at most D nonzero Mellin coefficients.
```

It is adverse to AFP.

### 4.2 Number of p-adic interpolation disks

A bound inside one disk controls zeros or valuations inside that disk.  It says nothing across a number of disks growing with `p`.  The same obstruction appears on weight space: the tame characters `ω^r` occupy `p-1` distinct tame components, one sampled point per component.  Strassmann on each component permits `O(p)` total exceptional points.

### 4.3 Bounded rank and conductor of a sheaf

This gives Deligne-type square-root bounds for individual character sums and bounded dimensions of Mellin cohomology.  It does not bound the number of characters at which the trace is zero or divisible by the defining prime.

The current `proof.tex` already records the decisive Jacobi counterexample: bounded rank, conductor, and overconvergence are compatible with a positive proportion of defining-characteristic Mellin vanishings.  The Legendre calculation above is the rank-two Hasse analogue.

### 4.4 Few evaluation roots of `H_p`

This concerns the support of `H_p` as a function, not the support of its Fourier transform.  The uncertainty principle only gives a weak product lower bound for support sizes.  If `H_p(t)` is nonzero at almost every `t`, that merely forces at least one nonzero Fourier coefficient; it does not force almost every Fourier coefficient to be nonzero.

### 4.5 Bounded rational-function complexity

This is also insufficient without a nondegeneracy hypothesis.  The constant rational function has all nontrivial Mellin coefficients zero.  Special rational or trace functions can have all Mellin coefficients nonzero because of an exact Gauss-product identity, as for Kloosterman sums, but the nonvanishing comes from that product formula, not from bounded degree alone.

The correct principle is therefore:

> Geometric or analytic boundedness controls the size and cohomological realization of Mellin values.  AFP needs arithmetic nonvanishing of their reductions, which is an additional same-characteristic statement.

## 5. What a successful Dwork approach would actually need

Dwork theory can still contribute decisively, but the low-complexity object must live on the **character side after normalization**, not on the original `t`-line.

Let
\[
\mathcal M_{p,r}
=H^1_{\mathrm{rig},c}
  \bigl(U,\mathcal V\otimes\mathcal K_{\omega^{-r}}\bigr)
\]
be the Kummer-twisted Mellin crystal, and let `h_p(r)` be the expected first Hodge valuation, including the Gauss factor.  One wants an integral normalized first trace
\[
\widetilde M_{p,r}
=\pi^{-h_p(r)}
 \operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_{p,r}\mid\mathcal M_{p,r})
\in\mathcal O_p
\]
with an exact comparison
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde M_{p,r}\bmod\pi
=u_{p,r}b_r,
\qquad u_{p,r}\in\mathbf F_p^\times.}
\tag{5.1}
\]
If the first Hodge slope has multiplicity one, then a unique minimal-slope Frobenius root makes `\widetilde M_{p,r}` a unit.  Thus (5.1) converts an Apéry zero into failure at the first Newton--Hodge breakpoint.

The remaining task is to count those exceptional twists.  Any one of the following would be sufficient.

### 5.1 Bounded-degree tame Hasse polynomial

Prove that for some absolute `D`, some units `u_{p,r}`, and a nonzero Laurent polynomial `Q_p` of degree at most `D`,
\[
\widetilde M_{p,r}\equiv u_{p,r}Q_p(\omega^r)\pmod\pi.
\]
Then
\[
Z(p)\le 2D+O(1).
\]
This is a genuine character-side bounded-divisor theorem.  The current proof shows that it cannot hold for the direct unnormalized interpolation; a new Gauss/unit-root normalization would have to collapse linear horizontal complexity.

### 5.2 Average exceptional-twist theorem

For AFP it is enough to prove
\[
\sum_{p\le X}
\#\{r:\lambda_1(p,r)>h_1(p,r)\}
\ll\pi(X).
\]
This avoids the probably false pointwise bound.  A bounded average second factorial moment would be even better and interfaces with the ZM2/TCMA2 route.

### 5.3 Fixed-complexity difference equation in the tame exponent

Derive from Gross--Koblitz/Dwork formulas a fixed-order recurrence or `p`-adic difference equation for the normalized residues
\[
r\longmapsto \widetilde M_{p,r}\bmod\pi,
\]
with coefficients of uniformly bounded degree and with all Kummer/descent symmetries classified.  One would then need a genuine zero-return theorem for that recurrence.  This would be the character-side analogue of the existing Apéry recurrence/gap-polynomial method.

### 5.4 Frobenius sieve in a universal tame-parameter object

Package all tame twists into a single integral object over a character torus or group algebra, prove uniform monodromy and conductor control, and show that the first-Hasse divisor has average bounded intersection with the `p-1` tame points.  Ordinary one-disk overconvergence is not enough; the theorem must couple the tame branches.

### 5.5 Exact factorization of normalized Mellin values

The Kloosterman model succeeds because, after the correct character-dependent Hodge normalization, the Mellin value is a single gamma-unit product and cannot cancel.  An Apéry analogue would require a factorization or determinant formula that replaces the current sum of several minimal-valuation units by an explicitly nonzero object.  No such factorization is presently known.

## 6. Direct answers to (A), (B), and (C)

### (A) Does overconvergence make `H_p(t)` low-complexity on `F_p^×`?

**No, not in the sense needed.**  Dwork analyticity is an ordinary-locus, vertical Frobenius statement.  It neither bounds the monomial degree of the mod-`p` Hasse polynomial nor reduces the number of residue tubes.  The actual Apéry Hasse polynomial has linearly many nonzero coefficients, and the Legendre family gives a classical Dwork counterexample with linearly many zero Mellin modes.

### (B) Does bounded p-adic complexity imply DFT anti-concentration?

**No.**  Polynomial low degree implies the reverse.  Bounded conductor or analytic rank controls individual Mellin sums but not how often their reductions vanish.  A theorem would need an added spectral nondegeneracy condition, such as a bounded-degree **character-side** Hasse divisor, a Gauss-product formula, or a residual Frobenius sieve.

### (C) Can the overconvergence radius bound effective degree?

Only under the strong quantitative hypotheses (3.3): a uniform radius `p^δ` and a uniform Gauss norm for one integral expansion.  A bare statement `R_p>1` gives no uniform bound, and the unit-distance singularities prevent the proposed single-disk interpretation in the standard Apéry coordinate.  Moreover, bounded effective degree in `t` would imply almost all Mellin coefficients vanish, so it would not prove AFP even if available.

## 7. Recommended concrete Dwork computation

The next computation should not estimate the radius of `A_p(t)` itself.  It should compute the first normalized Frobenius minor for every tame Kummer twist.

A useful deliverable would have the following form.

1. Choose a genuine Apéry constant-term model and an integral Dwork basis.
2. Compute the twisted Frobenius matrix for `ω^{-r}`.
3. Remove the exact Gross--Koblitz/Gauss valuation predicted by the twisted Hodge polygon.
4. Reduce the first normalized minor modulo the Dwork uniformizer and verify the identity (5.1).
5. Express the resulting residue as an explicit gamma-unit sum or determinant in `r`.
6. Search for a fixed-order recurrence, a bounded-degree resultant, or a two-twist correlation identity.
7. Target the average bounds
   \[
   \sum_{p\le X}Z(p)\ll\pi(X)
   \quad\text{or}\quad
   \sum_{p\le X}\binom{Z(p)/2}{2}\ll\pi(X),
   \]
   rather than a uniform pointwise cap.

This is the point where Dwork theory could add genuinely new information: not by making the Hasse polynomial smooth, but by exposing rigid arithmetic relations among the **normalized character-side first-Hasse residues**.
