# Cross-prime large sieve for Apéry zeros

## Short answer

A large sieve inequality adapted to the Apéry zero sets can improve the generic additive large sieve only if one proves genuinely stronger cancellation for the Fourier transforms of the zero indicators. Katz equidistribution by itself does **not** give the desired bound

\[
\max_a |\widehat{1_{Z_p}}(a)| \ll p^{-1}.
\]

The obstruction is geometric: \(Z_p\) is the zero locus of a Hasse invariant, not a random subset of \(\mathbf F_p\). The relevant trace function is a nonlinear function of the Hasse--Witt trace, and controlling its additive Fourier transform requires a new geometric monodromy statement.

## 1. Large sieve formulation

Write
\[
g_p(n)=1_{n\bmod p\in Z_p}-|Z_p|/p.
\]
Then
\[
f(n)=\sum_{X<p\le 2X}g_p(n).
\]
The additive Fourier expansion is
\[
g_p(n)=\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^*}\widehat g_p(a)e_p(an),
\]
with
\[
\widehat g_p(a)=\frac1p\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(-ar).
\]
The generic large sieve treats these coefficients as arbitrary and gives a bound essentially controlled by
\[
\sum_p\sum_a|\widehat g_p(a)|^2.
\]
Since Parseval gives
\[
\sum_a|\widehat g_p(a)|^2=\frac{|Z_p|}{p}-\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p^2},
\]
there is no gain at second moment level. Any improvement must use higher moments or cross-prime structure.

## 2. What Katz equidistribution can give

The Apéry K3 family gives a rank-2 weight-3 l-adic sheaf whose Frobenius trace controls the Hasse-Witt polynomial. Existing Katz-type results imply square-root cancellation for trace sums of the form
\[
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}t(a)\chi(a),
\]
when \(t\) is a geometrically irreducible trace function of bounded conductor.

However,
\[
1_{Z_p}(r)=1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}
\]
is not itself the trace function. It is an indicator of the vanishing locus of the Hasse invariant. Passing from trace cancellation to zero-set Fourier cancellation requires understanding the sheaf obtained from the zero divisor of the Hasse section.

The desired estimate would be
\[
\sum_{r:b_r=0}e_p(ar)\ll 1
\]
uniformly for \(a\ne0\). Equivalently,
\[
|\widehat g_p(a)|\ll p^{-1}.
\]
This is much stronger than Deligne square-root cancellation.

## 3. Expected geometric scale

If the zero set behaved like a generic divisor of bounded degree, then one expects
\[
\left|\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(ar)\right|\ll C,
\]
so
\[
|\widehat g_p(a)|\ll C/p.
\]
This is the proposed improvement.

But the known bound
\[
|Z_p|=O(p^{2/3})
\]
only gives the trivial estimate
\[
|\widehat g_p(a)|\le |Z_p|/p=O(p^{-1/3}).
\]
The gap is exactly the missing zero-divisor equidistribution theorem.

## 4. Higher Fourier moments

A possible weaker route is to prove
\[
\sum_a|\widehat g_p(a)|^{2k}\ll p^{1-k+o(1)}
\]
for some fixed \(k>1\).

Expanding gives
\[
\sum_a|\widehat g_p(a)|^{2k}
=p^{-2k}\#\{(r_1,\ldots,r_k,s_1,\ldots,s_k)\in Z_p^{2k}:\sum r_i=\sum s_i\}.
\]
Thus the needed input is an additive-energy bound
\[
E_k(Z_p)=\#\{\sum r_i=\sum s_i\}\ll p^{k+o(1)}.
\]
A Katz-style theorem would have to prove that the Apéry zero sets have near-random additive energy.

This is a more realistic target than the pointwise Fourier bound.

## 5. Exact missing geometric input

The required theorem is not ordinary Katz equidistribution. One needs:

1. A l-adic sheaf controlling the divisor
\[
\mathcal Z_p=\{t:H_p(t)=0\}
\]
of the Apéry Hasse invariant.

2. Geometric irreducibility and large monodromy for the induced zero-locus sheaf.

3. A bound for additive twists of the zero-cycle trace:
\[
\sum_{H_p(t)=0}\psi(at)\ll p^{o(1)}.
\]

4. Alternatively, bounded conductor estimates for the convolution sheaves controlling
\[
E_k(Z_p).
\]

The rank-2 K3 sheaf gives the Frobenius trace input, but the zero indicator is a nonlinear operation (taking the vanishing locus), and that operation is not covered automatically by Katz's general equidistribution theorem.

## 6. Consequence for the Apéry GCD program

If the strong estimate
\[
\max_{a\ne0}|\widehat g_p(a)|\ll p^{-1+o(1)}
\]
were proved, then the dyadic large sieve would gain roughly a factor of the typical zero multiplicity \(|Z_p|\), changing the shell-prime variance from the current generic scale to a near-independent scale. This would directly strengthen the high-moment bounds needed for controlling
\[
K_X(m)=\#\{p\in(X,2X]:m\bmod p\in Z_p\}.
\]

At present the realistic geometric milestone is weaker: prove bounded-conductor additive-energy estimates for \(Z_p\). That would already give nontrivial high-moment bounds and could feed the LGTQ-3 / cross-prime anti-concentration route.

## Conclusion

A K3/Katz approach is promising, but the required statement is a new theorem about the **zero divisor of the Apéry Hasse invariant**, not a direct application of Katz equidistribution. The exact missing input is a geometric monodromy and Deligne bound for the zero-locus sheaf (or an equivalent additive-energy estimate for Apéry zero sets).
