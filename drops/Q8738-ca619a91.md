1. A plausible conjecture is indeed log G_n = Θ(√n), but it is not currently a theorem. The Poisson model for shell zeros predicts only a small contribution from primes p≈n: expected shell multiplicity is bounded, giving at most O(log n). The √n scale naturally comes from small primes, where many p-adic valuations can accumulate because the recurrence has structured congruence blocks. A sharper model would need the joint distribution of v_p(a_n), v_p(b_n) over p≲√n.

2. I am not aware of published tables for this exact gcd quantity at n=10^4 or 10^5. Such computations are possible with modular recurrence plus rational reconstruction, but the full denominator-cleared gcd is much more expensive than computing Apéry numbers alone. The n≤150 data are therefore useful evidence but not yet asymptotic evidence.

3. For the ζ(2) Apéry analogue, denominator and gcd phenomena are better controlled because the associated recurrence and irrationality construction are simpler. However, I do not know of a theorem proving a universal C√n law. A similar √n heuristic from small primes is reasonable.

4. A bound log G_n=O(√n log log n) already rules out Liouville behavior of ζ(3), since the Apéry approximants would not have the required denominator growth. It does not by itself determine the irrationality exponent μ(ζ(3)); that depends on the balance between the approximation error (roughly (17+12√2)^−2n) and the denominator size. A √n gcd correction is far too small to change the known finite irrationality-exponent bounds, but a precise asymptotic could sharpen constants.
