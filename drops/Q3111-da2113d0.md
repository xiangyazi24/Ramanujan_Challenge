# P3.2 targeted literature / theorem-transfer audit

## Executive verdict

I found **no published theorem in the targeted primary literature whose stated quantifiers imply, for every row integer `N`, a sublinear bound on the logarithmic mass of primes in a fixed quotient cell that divide the Apéry number `b_N`**.

For fixed `q>=1`, the needed statement is

\[
W_q(N):=\sum_{\substack{N/(q+1)<p\le N/q\\ p\ \mathrm{prime}\\p\mid b_N}}\log p=o_q(N)
\qquad\text{for every sufficiently large }N.
\tag{T}
\]

After the exact Lucas reduction, this is a **varying-prime diagonal problem**:

\[
W_q(N)=\sum_{\substack{p\in I_q(N)\\N-qp\in Z_p}}\log p,
\qquad
I_q(N):=\left(\frac N{q+1},\frac Nq\right]\cap\mathbb P,
\tag{D}
\]

where

\[
Z_p:=\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\}.
\]

The published pointwise congruence theory describes each fixed `p` extremely well, but it does not bound the correlation of the affine clock `r=N-qp` with the **different set `Z_p` for each varying prime**.

The sharpest directly transferable published pointwise theorem for the **support event** `p|b_N` remains Gessel's Lucas congruence. The later `p^2` congruences, valuation theorems, automatic/diagonal theorems, modular-form supercongruence, and the 2026 Galois-group theorem are arithmetically stronger in other senses, but none shrinks the support set in `(D)`.

The one special published pointwise modular-form theorem of Ahlgren--Ono does give an `O(log N)` fixed-`q` bound, but only for the single midpoint slice `N-qp=(p-1)/2`; at fixed `(N,q)` there is at most one such prime.

The direct prime-factor theorem of Luca--Shparlinski is not transferable: it is density-one in `N`, and moreover gives **lower** bounds on the number/largest size of prime factors rather than an upper bound on their mass in a prescribed proportional interval.

No unpublished strengthening is used below.

# 1. Exact fixed-`q` target before consulting literature

The repository state used for this audit is `main` at


Relevant project sources are:

- [`problems/3.2/ORACLE_COMM/chatgpt_q32_horizontal_mellin_sieve_corrected.md`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/47fa0e653f52c4a71e9a8c26b31ca9f66f6bbe86/problems/3.2/ORACLE_COMM/chatgpt_q32_horizontal_mellin_sieve_corrected.md), which fixes the quotient-cell quantifiers.

Write

\[
N=qp+r,\qquad q=\left\lfloor\frac Np\right\rfloor,\qquad0\le r<p.
\]

For a fixed `q>=1`, the prime cell is

\[
I_q(N)=\left(\frac N{q+1},\frac Nq\right]\cap\mathbb P.
\]

The ambient logarithmic prime mass is

\[
\Theta_q(N):=\sum_{p\in I_q(N)}\log p
=\vartheta(N/q)-\vartheta(N/(q+1))
=\left(\frac1{q(q+1)}+o_q(1)\right)N.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus any theorem that merely gives

\[
W_q(N)\le C_qN
\]

with fixed positive `C_q` does not reach the required `o(N)` threshold.

## 1.1 Clean Lucas threshold and an explicit finite-exception constant

Let

\[
P_q^+:=P^+(b_q)
\]

be the largest prime divisor of the fixed nonzero integer `b_q` (put `P_q^+=1` if needed), and define

\[
M_q:=\max\{q,P_q^+\}.
\]

If

\[
N>(q+1)M_q,
\tag{1.2}
\]

then every `p in I_q(N)` satisfies both `p>q` and `p\nmid b_q`. Since `N=qp+r` has the two base-`p` digits `(q,r)`, Gessel gives

\[
b_N\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p,
\]

and therefore, in the clean range,

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_N\iff p\mid b_r,\qquad r=N-qp.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Without imposing `(1.2)`, the total logarithmic mass of the fixed dirty set

\[
\{p:p\le q\text{ or }p\mid b_q\}
\]

is at most

\[
E_q:=\vartheta(q)+\log\operatorname{rad}(b_q)
\le\vartheta(q)+\log b_q,
\tag{1.4}
\]

a constant depending only on `q`. Hence all asymptotic fixed-`q` questions reduce exactly to `(D)` up to `O_q(1)`.

This is the transfer target against which each published theorem below is tested.

# 2. Direct prime-factor theorem: Luca--Shparlinski (2008)

**Primary source.** Florian Luca and Igor E. Shparlinski, *Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers*, Journal of the London Mathematical Society 78 (2008), 545--562.

- Publisher / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jdn031
- Publisher page: https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1112/jlms/jdn031

## Published theorem: exact quantifier shape

They prove that there exists an **absolute constant** `c_0>0` such that both

\[
\omega(b_N)>c_0\log\log\log N
\]

and

\[
P^+(b_N)>c_0(\log N\log\log N)^{1/2}
\]

hold for a set of positive integers `N` of asymptotic density `1`.

Equivalently, if `G` is the set where both inequalities hold, then

\[
\frac{\#(G\cap[1,X])}{X}\longrightarrow1.
\]

The paper does **not** assert these inequalities for every `N`.

### Classification

- Quantifier in the row variable: **density-one / average**, not pointwise.
- Direction of information: **lower bound** on number/largest size of prime factors.
- Location of those factors: unrestricted; no prescribed proportional cell.

## Fixed-`q` transfer

There is no nontrivial upper bound on `W_q(N)` obtainable from this theorem.

Even for `N in G`, the conclusions only say that `b_N` has sufficiently many distinct prime factors and one factor exceeding a polylogarithmic threshold. They do not bound how many factors lie in

\[
(N/(q+1),N/q]
\]

or their logarithmic mass. In particular, they are compatible with the worst ambient fixed-cell estimate

\[
W_q(N)\le\Theta_q(N)
=\left(\frac1{q(q+1)}+o_q(1)\right)N.
\tag{2.1}
\]

On the density-zero exceptional set the theorem says nothing at all, whereas `(T)` is required for **every** `N`.

### Verdict

**Does not apply.** There are two independent mismatches:

1. `density one in N` versus `for every N`;
2. lower bounds on prime factors versus the required upper bound on logarithmic mass in a fixed interval.

So the known Luca-style result cannot even be converted into a weaker pointwise fixed-`q` estimate by changing constants.

# 3. Gessel's Lucas theorem: strongest published pointwise support theorem

**Primary source.** Ira Gessel, *Some congruences for Apéry numbers*, Journal of Number Theory 14 (1982), 362--368.

- ScienceDirect: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022314X82900713
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(82)90071-3

A modern primary-source restatement with the corrected digit quantifier appears in Armin Straub's theorem quoted in Section 4 below.

## Published theorem: exact quantifiers

For **every prime `p`**, every integer `m>=0`, and every digit `r` with

\[
0\le r<p,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{b_{mp+r}\equiv b_mb_r\pmod p.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
N=n_0+n_1p+\cdots+n_sp^s,
\qquad0\le n_i<p,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{b_N\equiv\prod_{i=0}^s b_{n_i}\pmod p.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus

\[
p\mid b_N
\iff
\text{at least one base-`p` digit }n_i\text{ belongs to }Z_p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Classification

This is completely **pointwise** in both `p` and `N`: no exceptional primes and no exceptional row integers occur.

## Exact fixed-`q` consequence, with constants

Using Section 1.1, for every fixed `q` and every

\[
N>(q+1)M_q,
\]

we get the exact equality

\[
\boxed{
W_q(N)
=\sum_{\substack{p\in I_q(N)\\N-qp\in Z_p}}\log p.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

For arbitrary `N`, the difference between the two sides is supported on the fixed dirty set and has logarithmic mass at most `E_q` from `(1.4)`.

This is a very strong structural reduction. But quantitatively the theorem alone gives only

\[
\boxed{
W_q(N)\le\Theta_q(N)+E_q
=\left(\frac1{q(q+1)}+o_q(1)\right)N+E_q.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

There is no saving in the leading constant, let alone `o(N)`.

## Precise quantifier mismatch

Gessel gives

\[
\forall p\quad\forall N:\quad
p\mid b_N\ \text{is determined by the fixed-`p` digit-zero set }Z_p.
\]

What `(T)` needs is

\[
\forall N\quad
\sum_{\substack{p\asymp_q N\\N-qp\in Z_p}}\log p=o_q(N),
\]

where **`p` varies and so the set `Z_p` varies with it**.

No bound on the individual cardinalities `|Z_p|`, and no cross-prime anti-correlation between `Z_p` and the affine residue `N-qp`, is contained in the Lucas theorem.

This is the central quantifier reversal:

```text
published pointwise theorem: fix p, then describe all N;
needed theorem:             fix N, then control many varying p simultaneously.
```

### Verdict

**Strongest genuinely relevant published pointwise support theorem, but insufficient.** It identifies the fixed-`q` bad event exactly; it does not count it across primes.

# 4. Stronger p-adic pointwise results do not shrink the support

Two primary sources make the point especially sharply.

## 4.1 Delaygue (2018): digit-zero multiplicity gives a valuation lower bound

**Primary source.** Éric Delaygue, *Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers*, Compositio Mathematica 154 (2018), 249--274.

- Primary preprint/full text: https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4131
- DOI / publisher: https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X17007552

Delaygue defines, for a fixed prime `p`,

\[
\alpha_p(b,N)
:=\#\{\text{base-`p` digits }n_i\text{ of }N: p\mid b_{n_i}\}.
\]

For the Apéry sequence `b=A_1`, the hypotheses of his main theorem are verified: the relevant factorial-ratio multisum is covered and its differential operator is of type I for every prime. The resulting specialized conclusion is pointwise:

\[
\boxed{
\forall p\ \forall N\ge0:\qquad
b_N\in p^{\alpha_p(b,N)}\mathbb Z.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

In particular it proves Beukers' digit-count valuation conjectures.

### Fixed-`q` transfer

In the clean fixed-`q` range, the two base-`p` digits are `(q,r)` and `p\nmid b_q`. Therefore

\[
\alpha_p(b,N)=\mathbf1_{\{p\mid b_r\}}.
\]

So `(4.1)` says precisely that a target digit supplies at least one factor of `p`; it does not remove any prime from the support `(3.4)` and gives no upper bound on the number of target primes.

Hence the quantitative consequence remains `(3.5)`.

### Verdict

**Pointwise and stronger in valuation, but wrong direction for support counting.** It is a lower bound on `v_p(b_N)` conditional on digit hits, not an upper bound on how often digit hits occur as `p` varies.

## 4.2 Straub (2024): exact Gessel--Lucas congruence modulo `p^2`

**Primary source.** Armin Straub, *Gessel--Lucas congruences for sporadic sequences*, Monatshefte für Mathematik 203 (2024), 883--898.

- Author's primary page (including corrigendum): https://arminstraub.com/pub/gessel-lucas
- Author PDF: https://arminstraub.com/downloads/pub/gessel-lucas.pdf
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-023-01894-3

The author records an important corrigendum: in Theorems 1.2, 1.3 and 5.1 the correct digit range is

\[
0\le k<p.
\]

Theorem 1.3 states: for each of the 15 sporadic Apéry-like sequences `A`, for **all primes `p>=3`** and **all integers `m,k` with `0<=k<p`**,

\[
\boxed{
A(pm+k)\equiv A(k)A(m)+pmA'(k)A(m)\pmod{p^2}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the ordinary Apéry numbers this extends Gessel's original `p^2` result.

### Fixed-`q` transfer

Put `m=q`, `k=r=N-qp`. Then

\[
b_N\equiv b_q\bigl(b_r+pq\,b'(r)\bigr)\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Reducing `(4.3)` modulo `p` gives exactly Gessel's support condition

\[
p\mid b_N\iff p\mid b_r
\]

in the clean range. The `p^2` term contains first-lift information, but Straub's theorem does **not** assert a uniform nonvanishing statement for the transverse expression at all target pairs `(p,r)`, nor a bound on how many `r` satisfy `p\mid b_r`.

Therefore `(4.2)` supplies no stronger fixed-`q` support bound than `(3.5)`.

### Verdict

**Pointwise and substantially stronger p-adically, but support-neutral.** Any use of the first-lift term to exclude most target primes would require an additional theorem not stated in this source; I do not infer such a strengthening.

# 5. Modular-form transfer: Ahlgren--Ono is powerful but only at the midpoint

**Primary source.** Scott Ahlgren and Ken Ono, *A Gaussian hypergeometric series evaluation and Apéry number congruences*, Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik 518 (2000), 187--212.

- Author-hosted primary PDF: https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/ken-ono/files/049.pdf
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.2000.004

They write

\[
\eta^4(2z)\eta^4(4z)=\sum_{m\ge1}a(m)q^m,
\]

a normalized weight-4 Hecke eigenform on `Gamma_0(8)`.

## Published theorem: exact quantifier and index

Their Theorem 5 states that for **every odd prime `p`**,

\[
\boxed{
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a(p)\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The paper explicitly notes that primes with

\[
p\mid b_{(p-1)/2}
\]

do occur, so `(5.1)` is not a pointwise nonvanishing theorem.

### Classification

- Pointwise in every odd `p`.
- Very strong (`mod p^2`).
- But it concerns only the **single Apéry index `(p-1)/2`** attached to `p`.

## Exact fixed-`q` consequence

Suppose a fixed-`q` row has the midpoint residue

\[
r=N-qp=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

Then

\[
2N+1=(2q+1)p,
\qquad
\boxed{p=\frac{2N+1}{2q+1}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus for fixed `(N,q)` there is **at most one** candidate prime on the Ahlgren--Ono slice.

In the clean Lucas range, `(5.1)` gives

\[
p\mid b_N
\iff p\mid b_{(p-1)/2}
\iff p\mid a(p).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore the total logarithmic mass of all fixed-`q` targets covered by this modular-form theorem is bounded pointwise by

\[
\boxed{
W_q^{\mathrm{mid}}(N)
\le \log\frac{2N+1}{2q+1}
\le\log(2N+1).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is `O(log N)` with leading constant `1` in front of `log(2N+1)`.

That bound is genuinely sufficient for this special slice.

## Why it does not transfer to the full fixed-`q` row

A generic target has

\[
r=N-qp
\]

with arbitrary `1<=r<=p-2`. The theorem `(5.1)` provides no relation between `b_r` and the modular coefficient `a(p)` unless

\[
r=(p-1)/2.
\]

I found no primary-source theorem in this modular-form line asserting a corresponding identity for arbitrary `r`. Such an extension must not be inferred from `(5.1)`.

### Verdict

**Applies pointwise to a one-prime midpoint subfamily and gives `O(log N)` there; does not cover the generic fixed-`q` residues.**

# 6. Diagonal / automatic-sequence theorem: Rowland--Yassawi (2015)

**Primary source.** Eric Rowland and Reem Yassawi, *Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational functions*, Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux 27 (2015), 245--288.

- Open primary article: https://numdam.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.901/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.901
- Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.8635

## Published theorem: exact hypotheses and quantifiers

Their Theorem 2.1 is the following fixed-prime statement. Let

\[
R,Q\in\mathbb Z_p[x_1,\dots,x_k],
\qquad Q(0,\dots,0)\not\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad \alpha\ge1.
\]

Then the coefficient sequence of

\[
\operatorname{Diag}(R/Q)\pmod{p^\alpha}
\]

is `p`-automatic.

In their Apéry section they use a rational diagonal representation of the generating series and conclude explicitly:

\[
\boxed{(b_N\bmod p^\alpha)_{N\ge0}\text{ is }p\text{-automatic for every prime power }p^\alpha.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

They also restate Gessel's exact Lucas product and observe that for modulus `p` it gives the structure more directly.

### Classification

- Pointwise/effective after **fixing `p` and `alpha`**.
- The automaton is allowed to depend on `p`.
- No theorem in this result bounds, uniformly in `p`, the number of one-digit inputs `0<=r<p` whose output is zero.

Their general state bound itself grows with `p`; it is not a bounded-conductor family across primes.

## Fixed-`q` transfer

For each fixed `p`, automaticity gives an algorithmic description of

\[
Z_p=\{r<p:p\mid b_r\}.
\]

But `(D)` asks for

\[
N-qp\in Z_p
\]

simultaneously over varying `p`. The automaton changes when `p` changes. The theorem supplies no estimate of the form

\[
|Z_p|=o(p),
\quad |Z_p|=O(1),
\quad\text{or}\quad
\sum_{p\in I_q(N)}\mathbf1_{N-qp\in Z_p}=o(N/\log N).
\]

So the only unconditional mass bound obtained from `(6.1)` is again the ambient `(3.5)`.

A useful indication of the quantifier gap is that the same paper asks whether infinitely many primes divide **no** Apéry number at all. Thus even the prime-by-prime zero/nonzero landscape is not settled there by automaticity.

### Verdict

**Pointwise at each fixed prime, but not uniform across primes.** Automaticity is descriptive, not a coefficient-zero density theorem, and cannot be promoted to the needed cross-prime diagonal estimate without a new uniform input.

# 7. Latest associated G-function theorem: Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin (2026)

This is the strongest recent primary result I found that is explicitly about the reduction modulo `p` of the **Apéry generating G-function**.

**Primary source.** Xavier Caruso, Florian Fürnsinn, Daniel Vargas-Montoya and Wadim Zudilin, *Galois Groups of Apéry-like Series Modulo Primes*, Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society 114 (2026), 65--78.

- Cambridge open-access article: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-australian-mathematical-society/article/galois-groups-of-aperylike-series-modulo-primes/F2153B80BE545DAC0C4426A268F05541
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972725100932
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23298

Let

\[
f_\alpha(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n,
\qquad
A_p(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}b_nt^n\in\mathbb F_p[t].
\]

The starting p-Lucas identity is

\[
f_\alpha\equiv A_p f_\alpha^p\pmod p.
\]

## Published Theorems 1.1--1.2: exact prime hypotheses

Let `S` denote the subgroup of squares in `F_p^*`. For every odd prime `p` they prove:

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_p(t,f_\alpha)/\mathbb F_p(t))
=
\begin{cases}
S,&p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},\\
\mathbb F_p^*,&p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equivalently at the truncation level, there exists `B_p in F_p[t]` such that

\[
\boxed{
A_p(t)=
\begin{cases}
B_p(t)^2,&p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},\\
(t^2-34t+1)B_p(t)^2,&p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Classification

This is a striking **pointwise theorem for every odd prime**, with exact residue-class alternatives. It is genuinely more global than merely saying that `f_alpha mod p` is algebraic.

## Fixed-`q` transfer

The fixed-`q` target, however, asks whether the **single coefficient**

\[
[t^{N-qp}]A_p(t)=b_{N-qp}
\]

vanishes in `F_p`.

The factorization `(7.2)` is a factorization in the polynomial ring. It creates convolution identities among coefficients, but the published theorem does **not** state any bound on

\[
\#\{0\le r<p:[t^r]A_p=0\}=|Z_p|,
\]

nor does it state a coefficientwise nonvanishing theorem on either residue class of `p mod 24`.

Consequently `(7.2)` does not imply a bound on

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\in I_q(N)\\[t^{N-qp}]A_p(t)=0}}\log p.
\]

In particular, it cannot presently improve the ambient constant `1/(q(q+1))` in `(3.5)` by theorem transfer alone.

### Precise mismatch

The theorem controls the **field extension / polynomial factorization for each entire truncation `A_p`**. The desired estimate is about **coefficient zeros at one `p`-dependent index of each different truncation**. A square-factor theorem does not, by itself, furnish coefficient-zero sparsity.

Any assertion that `(7.2)` forces `|Z_p|=O(1)` or forces cancellation along `r=N-qp` would be an additional, unpublished theorem. I do not make that inference.

### Verdict

**Highly relevant and fully pointwise, but not a coefficient-zero theorem.** It does not close `(T)`.

# 8. G-function Lucas machinery does not change the quantifier

For completeness, the general G-function/Lucas framework also reaches exactly the same fixed-prime boundary.

**Primary source.** Boris Adamczewski, Jason P. Bell and Éric Delaygue, *Algebraic independence of G-functions and congruences “à la Lucas”*, Annales Scientifiques de l'École Normale Supérieure 52 (2019), 515--559.

- Open primary article: https://numdam.org/articles/10.24033/asens.2392/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.2392

Their Apéry specialization gives another proof that the Apéry sequence has the `p`-Lucas property for **all primes `p`**. Thus at the coefficient level it returns `(3.1)`--`(3.3)`.

This is important conceptually: passing from the binomial formula to the associated G-function/Frobenius language does not by itself produce a cross-prime distribution theorem. The fixed-`q` transfer remains `(3.4)`, and the same quantifier reversal remains.

I therefore do not count this as an independent route to a stronger fixed-`q` bound; it is a different proof architecture for the same support law.

# 10. Strongest published pointwise result and exact missing quantifier

There are two reasonable notions of “strongest.”

## 10.1 Strongest for the actual support event `p|b_N`

For the question posed here, Gessel's theorem is strongest because it gives an **if and only if** description of the support:

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_N\iff N-qp\in Z_p}
\]

for all sufficiently large fixed-`q` rows, with only the explicit `O_q(1)` dirty mass `(1.4)` outside that clean range.

The later p-adic results refine valuations after a support hit but do not reduce the set of possible support hits.

## 10.2 Strongest structural pointwise theorem on the generating function

The 2026 Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin theorem gives the exact Galois group and square/discriminant-square factorization of `A_p(t)` for every odd `p`. It is stronger structural information about the whole reduction, but the theorem is not coefficientwise and therefore does not currently improve the support count.

## 10.3 The precise missing statement

What is missing is not another fixed-prime congruence. It is a theorem with the quantifiers in the opposite order, for example

\[
\boxed{
\forall q\ge1\text{ fixed},\quad
\forall N\ge N_0(q):\quad
\sum_{\substack{p\in I_q(N)\\N-qp\in Z_p}}\log p
\le \varepsilon_q(N)N,
\qquad \varepsilon_q(N)\to0.
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

A stronger count form would also suffice. For instance, if a published theorem gave constants `C_q,eta_q>0` with

\[
\#\{p\in I_q(N):N-qp\in Z_p\}
\le C_q\frac{N}{(\log N)^{1+\eta_q}}
\tag{10.2}
\]

for **every** sufficiently large `N`, then, since `log p<=log N`,

\[
W_q(N)
\le C_q\frac{N}{(\log N)^{\eta_q}}+E_q
=o_q(N).
\tag{10.3}
\]

None of the primary theorems above has the quantifier pattern `(10.1)` or `(10.2)`.

The mismatch can be stated in one line:

```text
Known:  for each p, describe/control the sequence (b_N mod p)_N.
Needed: for each N, control a correlated set of many different p.
```

That is a cross-prime, quenched/diagonal anti-concentration problem, not a missing local congruence identity.

# 11. Final theorem-transfer verdict

No targeted published theorem found here proves the desired every-`N` fixed-proportional-interval mass estimate.

The rigorous published transfers are:

1. **Gessel** gives the exact pointwise reduction
   \[
   W_q(N)=\sum_{p\in I_q(N),\,N-qp\in Z_p}\log p
   \]
   after the explicit fixed threshold `(1.2)`, with at most
   \[
   E_q\le\vartheta(q)+\log b_q
   \]
   exceptional log mass before cleaning.

2. **Luca--Shparlinski** cannot upgrade this because their theorem is density-one in `N` and is in the lower-bound direction.

3. **Delaygue / Straub** strengthen the p-adic information pointwise but do not shrink the support in `p`.

4. **Ahlgren--Ono** gives a genuine `O(log N)` pointwise fixed-`q` bound on the midpoint slice, because `(p-1)/2=N-qp` determines at most the single prime
   \[
   p=(2N+1)/(2q+1).
   \]
   It does not address arbitrary residues.

5. **Rowland--Yassawi** makes the reduction automatic for each fixed prime power, but the automaton and zero set depend on `p`; no cross-prime zero-frequency theorem follows.

6. **Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin (2026)** gives exact, prime-uniform Galois/factorization structure for the Apéry G-function, but no stated coefficient-zero sparsity. It therefore does not control the varying coefficient `[t^{N-qp}]A_p(t)`.

Accordingly, the strongest genuinely relevant published **pointwise support** theorem remains Gessel's Lucas law, and its precise shortfall is not pointwise-versus-average: it is **fixed-prime pointwise versus fixed-row cross-prime**. The density-one theorem has an additional row-quantifier failure, while the modular-form theorem has an index-scope failure.

So there is no literature theorem here that can be inserted as a black box to prove `(T)`. Any such step would require a new cross-prime coefficient-zero theorem, and I have not inferred one from modularity, automaticity, Galois-group structure, or p-adic supercongruences.
