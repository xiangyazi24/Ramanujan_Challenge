# 1. Exact affine identities

Fix an actual selected upper state

\[
q\text{ prime},\qquad 0\le t<q,\qquad q\mid b_t,
\]

and a foreign lower prime `p<q`.  Put

\[
d:=q-p.
\]

The three lower digits are

\[
\boxed{
 r=t+6d,
 \qquad a_+=t+13d-q,
 \qquad a_-=d-t-1.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The common values are

\[
\boxed{
 n=6q+t=6p+r,
 \qquad N=12q+t=13p+a_+,
 \qquad s=q-1-t=p+a_-.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Every identity in `(1.2)` follows by direct substitution.  The advertised three-row relation is also exact:

\[
\begin{aligned}
7N-13n-6
&=7(12q+t)-13(6q+t)-6\\
&=6q-6t-6\\
&=6(q-1-t)\\
&=\boxed{6s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.3}
\]

This relation says that the minus common value is determined by the two plus common values at one upper state.  It does not say that the lower characteristic supporting the plus pair must also support the minus pair.

## 1.1 Plus endpoint map

For fixed `p`, write the map from `(q,t)` to the unreflected plus endpoint pair `(r,a_+)`:

\[
\binom r{a_+}
=
\begin{pmatrix}6&1\\12&1\end{pmatrix}
\binom qt
-
\binom{6p}{13p}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Its determinant is

\[
6\cdot1-12\cdot1=-6.
\]

Thus the absolute determinant is exactly `6`.  The inverse is

\[
\boxed{
 q=\frac{7p+a_+-r}{6},
 \qquad
 t=2r-p-a_+,
 \qquad
 d=\frac{p+a_+-r}{6}.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The full integer endpoint lattice is therefore

\[
\Lambda_+(p)
:=\{(r,a)\in\mathbb Z^2:p+a-r\equiv0\pmod6\}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Because both shell primes are odd, the physical shift `d=q-p` is even.  The physical prime lattice has the stronger congruence

\[
\boxed{p+a-r\equiv0\pmod{12}.}
\tag{1.7}
\]

This parity refinement does not change the determinant calculation, but it must be retained in an exact occurrence bijection.

## 1.2 Minus endpoint map

For fixed `p`,

\[
\binom r{a_-}
=
\begin{pmatrix}6&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}
\binom qt
-
\binom{6p}{p+1}.
\tag{1.8}
\]

The determinant is

\[
6(-1)-1=-7.
\]

The inverse is

\[
\boxed{
 d=\frac{r+a_-+1}{7},
 \qquad
 q=p+\frac{r+a_-+1}{7},
 \qquad
 t=\frac{r-6a_--6}{7}.}
\tag{1.9}
\]

Hence the integer lattice is

\[
\Lambda_-(p)
:=\{(r,a)\in\mathbb Z^2:r+a+1\equiv0\pmod7\},
\tag{1.10}
\]

and the physical odd-prime lattice is

\[
\boxed{r+a+1\equiv0\pmod{14}.}
\tag{1.11}
\]

## 1.3 Exact cross-sign shear

Let the plus occurrence use `p`, and let a minus occurrence at the same upper state use

\[
p'=p+\Delta,
\qquad \Delta>0.
\]

Write `(r,a)` for the plus endpoints at `p`, and `(R,A)` for the minus endpoints at `p'`.  Since the new shift is `d'=d-\Delta`,

\[
\boxed{R=r-6\Delta.}
\tag{1.12}
\]

Using `(1.5)` and `t=2r-p-a`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
A
&=d-\Delta-t-1\\
&=\frac{p+a-r}{6}-\Delta-(2r-p-a)-1\\
&=\boxed{
\frac{7p+7a-13r-6}{6}-\Delta.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.13}
\]

Conversely,

\[
\boxed{
 r=R+6\Delta,
 \qquad
 a=\frac{6A+13R+84\Delta-7p+6}{7}.}
\tag{1.14}
\]

The linear part of `(1.12)--(1.13)` is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\-13/6&7/6
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det=7/6.
\tag{1.15}
\]

This is exactly the covolume ratio between the index-`6` plus lattice and the index-`7` minus lattice.  Indeed,

\[
R+A+1
=7\left(\frac{p+a-r}{6}-\Delta\right),
\tag{1.16}
\]

so `(1.12)--(1.13)` maps `\Lambda_+(p)` into `\Lambda_-(p')`; `(1.14)` gives the inverse.  If `p,p'` are odd, then `\Delta` is even, and `(1.16)` also maps the physical mod-`12` lattice bijectively to the physical mod-`14` lattice.

Thus Q5961 is correct that the cross-sign algebra is an affine bijection/shear after the exact congruence lattices are imposed.  It is not an overdetermined pair of independent equations.

The mandatory caveat is that this is a bijection of the **affine index lattices**, not of their entire legal domains.  The legal inequalities in Section 2 still cut out a proper wedge.

# 4. Disjoint sign supports and the direct three-value carrier

A common lower prime in both signs would satisfy

\[
p\mid b_r,b_{a_+},b_{a_-},

Both legal candidate intervals can be macroscopic when `t` is deep below `q/8`.  Their actual support subsets may therefore be chosen disjoint in a formal incidence model.  The corrected model in Section 7 uses disjoint macroscopic prime boxes for the two signs.

Consequently assertion 3 is valid as a **non-forcing statement**:

> Current affine geometry does not imply a same-characteristic triple-zero mass.

It is not a theorem that the genuine Apéry plus and minus supports are usually disjoint.

## 4.2 Valuation ledger

Put

\[
X:=\frac{b_n}{q},
\qquad Z:=\frac{b_N}{q},
\qquad Y:=\frac{b_s}{q}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

These are integers for an actual selected upper state.  Let `G_+` and `G_-` be the squarefree products of the distinct foreign lower primes in the two exact sign supports, and put

\[
G_\cap:=\gcd(G_+,G_-).
\tag{4.2}

Then

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(G_+,G_-)\mid X,
\qquad G_+\mid Z,
\qquad G_-\mid Y.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Multiplying gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{G_+^2G_-^2}{G_\cap}\mid XYZ.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The local exponents are exact for the information used:

```text
plus only:   exponent 2 = one from X, one from Z;
minus only:  exponent 2 = one from X, one from Y;
both signs:  exponent 3 = one from each of X,Y,Z.
```

Thus division by only one copy of `G_cap` is correct.  Dividing by two copies would discard one available common-sign valuation; omitting the division would demand a fourth valuation not forced by the three common values.

The word “strongest” must be scoped.  Formula `(4.4)` is the strongest squarefree **direct monomial carrier obtained only by multiplying the three support divisibilities in `(4.3)`**.  It is not a theorem that no deeper Apéry recurrence identity can do better.

# 5. Threshold splitting

Let `S_+,S_-\ge0` satisfy

\[
S_++S_-=2R.
\]

For every `x,y\ge0`,

\[
\boxed{
(x+y-2R)_+
\le (x-S_+)_+ +(y-S_-)_+.}
\tag{5.1}

This follows by considering whether each variable exceeds its threshold.  It is pointwise sharp for every split: for any integer `m\ge1`, take

\[
x=S_++m,
\qquad y=S_-.

\[
\min(S_+,S_-)\le R,

with equality only at `S_+=S_-=R`, the symmetric split maximizes the minimum threshold.  Equivalently, in a pair-amplification estimate with coefficients proportional to `1/S_\sigma`, it minimizes

Since the total in `(7.2)` is `\asymp\varphi(M)\mu` and no class has more than `C\mu` primes by `(7.3)`, a positive proportion of the reduced classes must have at least `c\mu` primes.  Explicitly, if fewer than `g` classes had `P_a\ge\mu/2`, then

This uses no prime number theorem in each progression and no independence heuristic: only the global prime number theorem plus a uniform Brun--Titchmarsh upper bound.

# 9. Independent executable checker

The following Python 3 script uses only the standard library.  It checks:

```text
- the two determinants;
- the exact plus/minus inverse maps and physical congruences;
- the legal-window equivalences, including even-shift parity;
- the cross-sign shear on every small legal prime example;
- the concrete q=101 example;
- a finite 2x2 simultaneous plus/minus block;
- the difference-set fibre bound;
- the threshold inequality and symmetric minimax claim.
```

It is a finite identity certificate, not an asymptotic proof.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
from __future__ import annotations

from collections import Counter
from itertools import product
from math import isqrt


def primes_upto(n: int) -> list[int]:
    sieve = bytearray(b"\x01") * (n + 1)
    if n >= 0:
        sieve[0] = 0
    if n >= 1:
        sieve[1] = 0
    for p in range(2, isqrt(n) + 1):
        if sieve[p]:
            sieve[p * p:n + 1:p] = b"\x00" * (((n - p * p) // p) + 1)
    return [i for i, v in enumerate(sieve) if v]


PRIMES = set(primes_upto(5000))


def is_prime(n: int) -> bool:
    if n < 2:
        return False
    for p in range(2, isqrt(n) + 1):
        if n % p == 0:
            return False
    return True


def det2(a: int, b: int, c: int, d: int) -> int:
    return a * d - b * c


def plus_endpoints(q: int, t: int, p: int) -> tuple[int, int]:
    d = q - p
    return t + 6 * d, t + 13 * d - q


def minus_endpoints(q: int, t: int, p: int) -> tuple[int, int]:
    d = q - p
    return t + 6 * d, d - t - 1


def plus_legal(q: int, t: int, p: int) -> bool:
    if not (p < q and is_prime(p) and is_prime(q) and 0 <= t < q):
        return False
    r, a = plus_endpoints(q, t, p)
    return 0 <= r < p and 0 <= a < p


def minus_legal(q: int, t: int, p: int) -> bool:
    if not (p < q and is_prime(p) and is_prime(q) and 0 <= t < q):
        return False
    r, a = minus_endpoints(q, t, p)
    return 0 <= r < p and 0 <= a < p


def ceil_div(a: int, b: int) -> int:
    return -((-a) // b)


def plus_window(q: int, t: int, p: int) -> bool:
    d = q - p
    return (
        p < q
        and d % 2 == 0
        and ceil_div(q - t, 13) <= d <= (q - t - 1) // 7
    )


def minus_window(q: int, t: int, p: int) -> bool:
    d = q - p
    return (
        p < q
        and d % 2 == 0
        and t + 1 <= d <= (q - t - 1) // 7
    )


def invert_plus(p: int, r: int, a: int) -> tuple[int, int, int]:
    num = p + a - r
    assert num % 6 == 0
    d = num // 6
    q = p + d
    t = 2 * r - p - a
    return q, t, d


def invert_minus(p: int, r: int, a: int) -> tuple[int, int, int]:
    num = r + a + 1
    assert num % 7 == 0
    d = num // 7
    q = p + d
    t = (r - 6 * a - 6) // 7
    assert r - 6 * a - 6 == 7 * t
    return q, t, d


def plus_to_minus_shear(
    p: int, delta: int, r: int, a: int
) -> tuple[int, int]:
    R = r - 6 * delta
    num = 7 * p + 7 * a - 13 * r - 6
    assert num % 6 == 0
    A = num // 6 - delta
    return R, A


def minus_to_plus_shear(
    p: int, delta: int, R: int, A: int
) -> tuple[int, int]:
    r = R + 6 * delta
    num = 6 * A + 13 * R + 84 * delta - 7 * p + 6
    assert num % 7 == 0
    a = num // 7
    return r, a


def check_core_identities() -> None:
    assert det2(6, 1, 12, 1) == -6
    assert det2(6, 1, 1, -1) == -7

    # Concrete cross-sign example.
    q, t, p, pp = 101, 2, 89, 97
    delta = pp - p
    assert plus_legal(q, t, p)
    assert minus_legal(q, t, pp)

    r, ap = plus_endpoints(q, t, p)
    R, am = minus_endpoints(q, t, pp)
    assert (r, ap) == (74, 57)
    assert (R, am) == (26, 1)
    assert plus_to_minus_shear(p, delta, r, ap) == (R, am)
    assert minus_to_plus_shear(p, delta, R, am) == (r, ap)

    # Physical parity lattices.
    assert (p + ap - r) % 12 == 0
    assert (R + am + 1) % 14 == 0

    n = 6 * q + t
    N = 12 * q + t
    s = q - 1 - t
    assert (n, N, s) == (608, 1214, 98)
    assert n == 6 * p + r
    assert N == 13 * p + ap
    assert s == pp + am
    assert 6 * s == 7 * N - 13 * n - 6


def check_all_small_shears(limit: int = 220) -> None:
    ps = [p for p in primes_upto(limit) if p > 13]
    for q in ps:
        for t in range(q):
            plus = [p for p in ps if plus_legal(q, t, p)]
            minus = [p for p in ps if minus_legal(q, t, p)]

            for p in ps:
                if p >= q:
                    continue
                assert plus_legal(q, t, p) == plus_window(q, t, p)
                assert minus_legal(q, t, p) == minus_window(q, t, p)

                if plus_legal(q, t, p):
                    r, a = plus_endpoints(q, t, p)
                    qq, tt, d = invert_plus(p, r, a)
                    assert (qq, tt, d) == (q, t, q - p)
                    assert (p + a - r) % 12 == 0

                if minus_legal(q, t, p):
                    r, a = minus_endpoints(q, t, p)
                    qq, tt, d = invert_minus(p, r, a)
                    assert (qq, tt, d) == (q, t, q - p)
                    assert (r + a + 1) % 14 == 0

            for p in plus:
                r, a = plus_endpoints(q, t, p)
                for pp in minus:
                    if pp <= p:
                        continue
                    delta = pp - p
                    assert delta % 2 == 0
                    R, A = minus_endpoints(q, t, pp)
                    assert plus_to_minus_shear(p, delta, r, a) == (R, A)
                    assert minus_to_plus_shear(p, delta, R, A) == (r, a)


def check_minus_H_margin(limit: int = 250) -> None:
    for q in [p for p in primes_upto(limit) if p > 13]:
        for t in range(q):
            ds = [
                d for d in range(2, q, 2)
                if t + 1 <= d <= (q - t - 1) // 7
            ]
            for H in range(1, len(ds) + 1):
                if t % 2 == 0:
                    assert q >= 8 * t + 14 * H + 1
                else:
                    assert q >= 8 * t + 14 * H - 6


def check_difference_set(max_z: int = 80) -> None:
    for Z in range(2, max_z + 1):
        counts = Counter(7 * j - 13 * i for i in range(Z) for j in range(Z))
        assert len(counts) <= 20 * Z - 19
        assert max(counts.values()) <= 1 + (Z - 1) // 13
        assert len(counts) * max(counts.values()) >= Z * Z


def check_threshold_extremum(max_R: int = 16) -> None:
    for R in range(1, max_R + 1):
        split_scores = []
        for Splus in range(0, 2 * R + 1):
            Sminus = 2 * R - Splus
            split_scores.append(min(Splus, Sminus))

            for x, y in product(range(0, 4 * R + 1), repeat=2):
                lhs = max(x + y - 2 * R, 0)
                rhs = max(x - Splus, 0) + max(y - Sminus, 0)
                assert lhs <= rhs

            # Pointwise sharp example for this particular split.
            x, y = Splus + 1, Sminus
            assert max(x + y - 2 * R, 0) == 1
            assert max(x - Splus, 0) + max(y - Sminus, 0) == 1

        assert max(split_scores) == R
        assert [s for s in range(2 * R + 1) if min(s, 2 * R - s) == R] == [R]


def check_finite_joint_block() -> None:
    # n_i=n0+6*M*i, N_j=N0+6*M*j with M=10.
    M, Q0, t0 = 10, 1009, 61
    n0 = 6 * Q0 + t0
    N0 = 12 * Q0 + t0
    s0 = Q0 - 1 - t0

    selected = [(0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1)]
    plus_pool = [907, 919, 929]
    minus_pool = [883, 887]

    states = []
    for i, j in selected:
        n = n0 + 6 * M * i
        N = N0 + 6 * M * j
        q = (N - n) // 6
        t = 2 * n - N
        s = q - 1 - t
        assert is_prime(q)
        assert 0 <= t < q
        assert 6 * s == 7 * N - 13 * n - 6
        assert s == s0 + M * (7 * j - 13 * i)
        states.append((q, t, n, N, s))

    assert states == [
        (1009, 61, 6115, 12169, 947),
        (1019, 1, 6115, 12229, 1017),
        (1009, 121, 6175, 12229, 887),
    ]

    for q, t, n, N, s in states:
        for p in plus_pool:
            assert plus_legal(q, t, p)
            r, a = plus_endpoints(q, t, p)
            assert n == 6 * p + r
            assert N == 13 * p + a

        for p in minus_pool:
            assert minus_legal(q, t, p)
            r, a = minus_endpoints(q, t, p)
            assert n == 6 * p + r
            assert s == p + a

    assert set(plus_pool).isdisjoint(minus_pool)
    assert {7 * j - 13 * i for i, j in selected} == {0, 7, -6}


def main() -> None:
    check_core_identities()
    check_all_small_shears()
    check_minus_H_margin()
    check_difference_set()
    check_threshold_extremum()
    check_finite_joint_block()
    print("Q5989 affine/window/threshold certificate: OK")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```
