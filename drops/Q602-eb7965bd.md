# Katz hypergeometric sheaves and bounded Apéry Mellin zeros

## Executive verdict

Grant the comparison developed in Q594/Q599/Q600: after the correct Gauss/Tate normalization, the Apéry coefficient \(b_rmod p\) is the reduction of the first-slope Frobenius trace of the \(r\)-th Kummer twist, and

\[
 b_r\equiv0\pmod p
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H_{p,r}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{failure of ordinarity at the first Hodge break}.
\]

Then Katz's Mellin-transform theory is **highly relevant structural input**, but it does **not** prove AFP. The crucial distinction is between three different exceptional sets:

| set | condition on the Kummer twist | what Katz controls |
|---|---|---|
| cohomologically bad characters | the forget-supports map fails; rank/purity changes | bounded, often by \(2\operatorname{rk}\) |
| exact trace-zero characters | the characteristic-zero Frobenius trace is exactly \(0\) | equidistribution may give density \(0\), not \(O(1)\) |
| first-break bad characters | the normalized trace/Hasse scalar is \(0mod p\) | **not controlled by ordinary \(\ell\)-adic Katz theory** |

AFP concerns the third row, not the first.

The sharp conclusion is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{Katz gives a bounded structural exceptional set, but not a bounded mod-}p
\text{ trace-zero set.}
}
\]

There is a precise multiplicative analogue of Fourier-transform theory, but the proposed implication

\[
\text{large monodromy or rigidity}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\#\{r:M_p(r)\equiv0\pmod p\}=O(1)
\]

is false in that generality and is not a theorem in *ESDE*, *Rigid Local Systems*, or Katz's book on finite-field Mellin transforms.

## 1. The correct cohomological Mellin transform

Let \(U\subset\mathbf G_m/\mathbf F_p\) be the lisse locus of a sheaf \(\mathcal F_p\), let \(j:U\hookrightarrow\mathbf G_m\), and form the normalized perverse middle extension

\[
N_p=j_{!*}\mathcal F_p[1](w/2).
\]

For a multiplicative character \(\chi:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times\), let \(\mathcal L_\chi\) be its Kummer sheaf. The Mellin coefficient is given by the trace formula:

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_p(\chi)
&=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
 \chi(t)\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_t\mid N_{p,t}) \\
&=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
 H_c^0(\mathbf G_{m,\overline{\mathbf F}_p},N_p\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
 \right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Without the perverse shift, the same statement is usually written as a signed trace on \(H_c^1\).

Katz calls \(\chi\) **good** for \(N_p\) when the canonical forget-supports map

\[
Rj_!(N_p\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
 \longrightarrow
Rj_*(N_p\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
\]

is an isomorphism. For good characters, the relevant cohomology has the generic dimension and the expected purity. The bad characters are determined by tame characters occurring in the local monodromy at \(0\) and \(\infty\); in Katz's one-dimensional torus setup there are at most

\[
2\operatorname{gen.rk}(N_p)
\]

such Kummer characters, up to the standard norm-pullback identification.

This is the genuine bounded-exceptional-set theorem. But it says only that the **vector space and its weights behave generically**. A nonzero vector space of fixed rank can have Frobenius trace zero. For example, a two-dimensional Frobenius operator with eigenvalues \(1\) and \(-1\) has perfectly good rank and purity but zero trace.

Thus

\[
\#\operatorname{Bad}_{\mathrm{Katz}}(N_p)=O(1)
\]

does not imply

\[
\#\{\chi:M_p(\chi)=0\}=O(1),
\]

and still less does it imply a statement about \(M_p(\chi)\bmod p\).

A second important distinction is that Katz's Mellin equidistribution group \(G_{\mathrm{geom},N_p}\) is the **Tannakian group for convolution of the perverse object \(N_p\)**. It is not automatically the usual geometric monodromy group of the original lisse Picard--Fuchs sheaf. Proving that the differential equation has large usual monodromy does not by itself identify the Mellin Tannakian group.

## (A) The Apéry datum must first be identified correctly

The datum written in the question needs correction before Katz's hypergeometric theorems can be applied.

The ordinary series

\[
{}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2\\1,1,1\end{matrix};x
\right)
\]

has coefficient

\[
\frac{(1/2)_n^4}{(n!)^4}
=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}^4}{256^n},
\]

not the Apéry number

\[
b_n=\sum_k\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]

In Katz's equal-length character notation, the displayed \({}_4F_3\) corresponds to four quadratic numerator characters and four trivial denominator characters. These lists are disjoint, so the cancellation theorem does **not** reduce the rank from \(4\) to \(3\). A disjoint type-\((4,4)\) Katz hypergeometric sheaf has generic rank \(4\).

By contrast, the Apéry generating function satisfies the third-order operator

\[
\theta^3
-x(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+x^2(\theta+1)^3.
\]

The project has constructed a rank-\(3\) primitive K3 trace \(\theta_p(a)\) by splitting off the algebraic classes of the toric K3 fibers. That rank-\(3\) object is the correct starting point. To replace it by a Katz hypergeometric sheaf, one still needs an explicit comparison showing that it is a rational pullback, symmetric square, subquotient, or other explicitly normalized constituent of a particular hypergeometric local system.

If the rank-\(3\) object is a symmetric square of a rank-\(2\) system with usual monodromy \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), then its rank-\(3\) monodromy is the image

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathrm{SL}_2)\simeq\mathrm{SO}_3\simeq\mathrm{PGL}_2,
\]

not an \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) action obtained by simply splitting off a rank-one summand. These descriptions should not be interchanged.

Likewise, the Apéry equation has the additional finite singularities coming from

\[
1-34x+x^2=0.
\]

It may be a pullback of a rigid three-point system, but rigidity on the actual four-punctured base must be proved rather than inferred from the word “hypergeometric.”

So the first required theorem is an **identification theorem**:

\[
\boxed{
N_p^{\mathrm{Apery,prim}}
 \cong
 \text{an explicitly specified pullback/constituent of a Katz hypergeometric object},
}
\]

including every Tate twist, Gauss factor, exceptional fiber, and rank-one correction.

## (B) What happens for a literal Katz hypergeometric sheaf

For a genuine Katz hypergeometric object

\[
\operatorname{Hyp}(!,\psi;\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_n;
 \rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m),
\]

Katz computes its multiplicative Fourier transform exactly. For every multiplicative character \(\Lambda\), up to the displayed sign conventions in the definition,

\[
\sum_{t\in E^\times}
 \Lambda(t)\operatorname{Hyp}(\psi;\boldsymbol\chi;\boldsymbol\rho)(E,t)
=
\prod_{i=1}^n g(\psi_E,\Lambda\chi_{i,E})
\prod_{j=1}^m g(\bar\psi_E,\bar\Lambda\bar\rho_{j,E}).
\]

The corresponding compactly supported cohomology is one-dimensional. Every Gauss sum in this formula is nonzero, including the trivial-character Gauss sum. Consequently:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the exact characteristic-zero Mellin transform of a literal Katz hypergeometric sheaf never vanishes.}
}
\]

This does **not** prove the Apéry assertion. It exposes the normalization issue. A nonzero Gauss product can have positive valuation at the prime above \(p\). After dividing by the Hodge-predicted Gauss/Tate factor and reducing modulo that prime, its first-break scalar may vanish. This is a defining-characteristic statement invisible to nonvanishing in \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\).

Katz's use of Stickelberger in *ESDE*, §8.16, computes \(p\)-adic valuations of these Gauss products. But valuation information alone is not enough in the Apéry situation, because the project has already proved in `apery_mellin_carry_polygon.tex` that the relevant coefficient is a sum of multiple minimal-valuation units. Explicitly,

\[
b_j=\sum_{k=0}^j p^{2\epsilon_{j,k}}U_{p,j,k}^2,
\]

and for every interior index \(1\le j\le p-2\), at least two summands have minimal valuation \(0\). Reduction gives

\[
b_j\equiv
\Theta_{p,j}:=
\sum_{k=0}^{\min(j,p-1-j)}\overline U_{p,j,k}^{\,2}\pmod p.
\]

Thus the zero event is cancellation among unit residues. The complete termwise Stickelberger polygon is identical at \((p,j)=(11,5)\) and \((13,5)\), yet \(11\mid b_5\) and \(13\nmid b_5\). This is an exact project-level obstruction to proving AFP from slopes or Gauss valuations alone.

## (C) No universal \(O(\operatorname{rank})\) trace-zero theorem

The proposed assertion

\[
\#\{\chi:M(\mathcal F,\chi)=0\}
 \le C(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal F,
       G_{\mathrm{geom}})
\]

is false even for geometrically irreducible sheaves with large connected monodromy and no rank-one direct summand.

Here is a clean obstruction. Fix \(d>1\), take primes \(p\equiv1\pmod d\), and let

\[
[d]:\mathbf G_m\longrightarrow\mathbf G_m,\qquad t\longmapsto t^d.
\]

Choose a geometrically irreducible sheaf \(\mathcal G\) with large connected geometric monodromy such that the pullback remains irreducible, and put

\[
\mathcal F=[d]^*\mathcal G.
\]

Its trace function has the form

\[
K_{\mathcal F}(t)=K_{\mathcal G}(t^d),
\]

so it is invariant under \(t\mapsto\zeta t\) for every \(\zeta\in\mu_d\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
M(\mathcal F,\chi)
&=\sum_t K_{\mathcal G}(t^d)\chi(t)\\
&=\chi(\zeta)^{-1}M(\mathcal F,\chi).
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(\chi|_{\mu_d}\ne1\), the Mellin transform is zero. Only \((p-1)/d\) characters are trivial on \(\mu_d\), so

\[
\#\{\chi:M(\mathcal F,\chi)=0\}
\ge \left(1-\frac1d\right)(p-1).
\]

Pullback along the finite étale map \([d]\) restricts the fundamental-group representation to a finite-index subgroup, so the connected Zariski closure of the monodromy can remain the same. The example can therefore retain large connected monodromy and irreducibility.

This shows that “no rank-one summand” is not enough. At minimum one must impose **multiplicative primitivity**:

- no descent through a nontrivial power map \([d]\);
- no nontrivial multiplicative-translation symmetry;
- no Kummer-induced structure;
- no twist/translate relation that forces character cosets to vanish.

Katz repeatedly imposes precisely such hypotheses when proving large Mellin Tannakian monodromy. Even after all of them are verified, however, the conclusion is equidistribution, not a universal constant trace-zero bound.

## (D) The multiplicative Fourier analogue exists, but its exceptional set is different

There is a full multiplicative analogue of additive Fourier-transform theory:

- Gabber--Loeser develop Mellin transforms and generic vanishing for perverse sheaves on tori;
- Katz packages Kummer twists through convolution and a Tannakian fiber functor;
- local monodromy determines finitely many bad or resonant Kummer characters;
- for good characters, the Mellin cohomology has constant rank and purity.

What does **not** transfer is the inference

\[
\text{nonzero/generic stalk}
 \Longrightarrow
\text{nonzero Frobenius trace}.
\]

Even for the additive Fourier transform, a lisse stalk of constant rank can have trace zero. The bounded singular or rank-drop locus of the Fourier-transform sheaf is not the zero set of its trace function.

The multiplicative character variable also differs from an ordinary affine dual coordinate. The characters of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) are torsion points in the tame character group. Katz's “bad character” theorem concerns cohomological resonance at those torsion points, not the zeros of a scalar function on them.

Thus the precise analogue is

\[
\boxed{
\text{bounded Mellin rank/purity exceptional set},
}
\]

not

\[
\boxed{
\text{bounded Mellin trace-zero set}.
}
\]

## (E) The precise Katz results and what rigidity contributes

The relevant results are the following.

### 1. Bounded bad characters

In Katz's *Convolution and Equidistribution*, a character is good when the forget-supports map is an isomorphism. For a pure perverse object \(N\) on \(\mathbf G_m\), only the inverses of finitely many tame characters occurring at \(0\) and \(\infty\) can be bad, and Katz records the bound

\[
\#\operatorname{Bad}(N)\le 2\operatorname{gen.rk}(N).
\]

For a correctly constructed rank-\(3\) Apéry perverse object, this suggests at most six **cohomologically bad** Kummer characters. It does not suggest at most six Apéry zeros.

### 2. Mellin Sato--Tate equidistribution

If the convolution Tannakian groups satisfy

\[
G_{\mathrm{geom},N}=G_{\mathrm{arith},N}=G,
\]

Katz's Theorem 7.3 and Corollary 7.4 give equidistribution of the normalized Frobenius conjugacy classes, and hence their traces, as the extension field grows. Theorem 28.1 allows a sequence of varying finite fields, including prime fields, provided the group, Tannakian dimension, generic rank, and number of bad characters are uniformly controlled.

Suppose additionally that \(G\) is connected and the trace of its defining representation is not identically zero on a connected component. Then the trace-zero subset of a maximal compact form of \(G\) has Haar measure zero. Equidistribution can consequently imply

\[
\#\{\chi:\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p,\chi})=0
      \text{ exactly in characteristic }0\}=o(p).
\]

This is useful, but it is much weaker than \(O(1)\), and it concerns exact algebraic equality. Equidistribution permits, for example, \(p^{1/2}\), \(p^{0.9}\), or any other \(o(p)\) number of exact zeros.

More importantly, it says nothing about

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p,\chi})\equiv0
 \pmod{\mathfrak p},
\]

where \(\mathfrak p\mid p\). Complex or \(\ell\)-adic Sato--Tate controls archimedean size and conjugacy distribution. Divisibility at the varying prime \(p\) is a different valuation-theoretic statistic.

### 3. Rigidity

Rigidity can make the following tasks feasible:

- identify the sheaf from its local monodromy;
- compute conductor and local invariants;
- prove irreducibility;
- exclude Kummer pullbacks and multiplicative translates;
- determine usual and convolution Tannakian monodromy groups.

Rigidity does **not** imply that the trace, as a function of the character, has bounded algebraic degree. Nor does it control the reduction of the trace modulo the residue characteristic. *Rigid Local Systems* contains no theorem turning rigidity into an \(O(1)\) mod-\(p\) trace-zero count.

So the answer to the bibliographic question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Katz has a bounded theorem for bad Mellin cohomology, not for vanishing Mellin traces.}
}
\]

## (F) What Katz predicts in the actual Apéry case

The exact project identity is more informative than the generic hypergeometric slogan. In `toric_mellin_square.tex`, for \(1\le j\le p-2\),

\[
b_j\equiv-\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\mu_p(a)a^j\pmod p.
\]

In `toric_fiber_k3.tex`,

\[
\mu_p(a)=p^2-6p+12+\theta_p(a),
\]

where \(\theta_p(a)\) is the rank-\(3\) weight-\(2\) primitive K3 Frobenius trace, away from the explicitly described rank-drop fibers. Since the Mellin transform of the constant term vanishes for nontrivial \(j\),

\[
\boxed{
 b_j\equiv
 -\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\theta_p(a)a^j
 \pmod p.
}
\]

This gives the correct defining-characteristic Mellin problem.

Katz's theory supports three different predictions:

1. **Structural bad characters:** after the correct perverse object is constructed, their number should be \(O(1)\), and a rank-\(3\) application may give a crude bound such as \(6\).

2. **Exact \(\ell\)-adic trace zeros:** if the object is literally hypergeometric, the Gauss-product formula gives none. For a more general primitive K3 Mellin object with connected large Tannakian group, equidistribution predicts density zero, not a constant bound.

3. **Apéry zeros modulo \(p\):** a random first Hasse scalar in \(\mathbf F_p\) has probability about \(1/p\) of vanishing, so one heuristically expects \(O(1)\) exceptional characters, modified by the reflection/self-duality symmetry. This is the correct heuristic behind AFP, but it is **not** a consequence of Katz's \(\ell\)-adic equidistribution theorem.

The palindromic pairing of Apéry zeros is entirely compatible with this: it changes the limiting constant and dependence pattern, not the order of magnitude.

## 2. Why large monodromy cannot see first-break ordinarity by itself

The first-break event is a reduction statement. If \(A_{p,r}\) is the normalized algebraic Frobenius trace, then

\[
r\in Z_p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v_{\mathfrak p}(A_{p,r})>0
\]

for the selected prime \(\mathfrak p\mid p\). Large \(\ell\)-adic monodromy for \(\ell\ne p\) controls the conjugacy class of \(A_{p,r}\) under embeddings into \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\) or \(\mathbf C\). It does not control its valuation at \(\mathfrak p\).

Gauss sums are the simplest illustration: every Gauss sum is a nonzero algebraic number with completely controlled complex absolute value, while Stickelberger assigns nontrivial and character-dependent \(p\)-adic valuations to it. Thus even the strongest possible exact nonvanishing theorem over \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\) is logically compatible with many first-break failures.

Katz's own ordinarity discussions emphasize this separation: large \(\ell\)-adic monodromy does not automatically imply nonemptiness, let alone quantitative largeness, of an ordinary locus. Ordinarity requires an additional \(F\)-crystal/Hasse-invariant argument.

## 3. The theorem that would actually close AFP

After the comparison \(Z_p=E_p^{\mathrm{sl},1}\) is made rigorous, the missing theorem is a defining-characteristic Mellin anti-concentration statement:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le X}
\#\left\{r\in\mathbf Z/(p-1):H_{p,r}=0\right\}
\ll\pi(X).
}
\tag{PHMA}
\]

Katz supplies several prerequisites for (PHMA), but not (PHMA) itself. A realistic division of labor is:

### Katz/\(\ell\)-adic layer

1. Construct the exact primitive K3 perverse object \(N_p\).
2. Prove compatibility in \(p\), including the exceptional fibers.
3. Compute local monodromy and all Katz-bad characters.
4. Prove multiplicative primitivity: no \([d]\)-descent, translate symmetry, or Kummer induction.
5. Determine the convolution Tannakian group, likely an orthogonal rank-\(3\) group if the system is a symmetric square.
6. Obtain Mellin Sato--Tate and moment estimates for characteristic-zero normalized traces.

### New \(p\)-adic layer

7. Identify the first Hasse scalar with an explicit normalized residue, for example the project quantity \(\Theta_{p,r}\).
8. Prove anti-concentration of that residue as \(r\) varies.
9. Establish the horizontal average over \(p\).

One especially strong route would be to show that \(H_{p,r}\), as a function of the tame character, is the reduction of a nonzero universal function of uniformly bounded Weierstrass degree. Then Strassmann/Bézout would give \(O(1)\) zeros. But no such bounded-degree character-space function follows from Katz's rigidity, and the current formula for \(\Theta_{p,r}\) has a number of unit terms growing with \(r\) and \(p\). Proving bounded effective degree would therefore be a genuinely new \(p\)-adic theorem, not a reformulation of the existing \(\ell\)-adic theory.

A second possible route is a residual large sieve for the Hasse scalar:

\[
\sum_{r}
 \psi\bigl(uH_{p,r}\bigr)
\]

with cancellation uniform in \(u\ne0\), followed by averaging over primes. Again, this is a statement about the reductions \(H_{p,r}\in\mathbf F_p\), rather than about the complex Sato--Tate distribution of \(M_p(\chi_r)\).

## Final answer to (A)--(F)

**(A)** Katz can be applied only after an exact sheaf comparison. The raw \({}_4F_3(1/2^4;1^3)\) datum is rank \(4\), is not literally the Apéry series, and does not become rank \(3\) by cancellation. The project's primitive rank-\(3\) K3 trace is the correct object; its relation to a rigid hypergeometric system must be proved.

**(B)** Large Mellin Tannakian monodromy gives equidistribution of normalized characteristic-zero Mellin traces for good characters. It does not give nonvanishing modulo \(p\). For a literal Katz hypergeometric sheaf, the exact Mellin transform is a nonzero product of Gauss sums, but its normalized reduction can still lose its unit root.

**(C)** No. There is no general bound \(C(\operatorname{rank},G)\) for trace-zero characters. Power-map pullbacks give irreducible large-monodromy examples with a positive proportion of zero Mellin coefficients. Even after primitivity hypotheses, Katz gives equidistribution rather than \(O(1)\) exact zeros, and gives no mod-\(p\) zero theorem.

**(D)** Yes, there is a multiplicative Fourier/Mellin theory. Its bounded exceptional locus is the rank/purity/forget-supports exceptional locus, not the Frobenius trace-zero locus.

**(E)** The precise bounded result is Katz's bound on bad Kummer characters, together with Mellin Sato--Tate equidistribution for good characters. There is no theorem in *ESDE* or *Rigid Local Systems* bounding the number of characters whose trace is \(0mod p\). Rigidity helps compute the sheaf and its monodromy but does not control first-break Hasse residues.

**(F)** For Apéry, Katz predicts bounded structural exceptions and supports a random-matrix model for characteristic-zero Mellin traces. The observed \(O(1)\) mod-\(p\) zero count is instead a random-\(\mathbf F_p\) prediction for the first Hasse scalar. Turning that prediction into AFP requires a new \(p\)-adic anti-concentration or horizontal ordinarity theorem.

The decisive conceptual correction is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Katz-bad characters}
\ne
\text{trace-zero characters}
\ne
\text{first-break nonordinary characters}.
}
\]

Only the last set is \(Z_p\).
