# P3.2 gap-by-gap resultant audit for \(h=3,4,5,6\)

## Verdict

The proposed fixed-gap strategy needs two corrections before the arithmetic is interpreted.

1. **The Q2337 Racah gap continuant does not have degree \(h-1\) in \(r\).**  With Q2337's actual definition,
   
   \[
   \mathcal R_0=1,\qquad \mathcal R_1=Q_1,\qquad
   \mathcal R_m=Q_m\mathcal R_{m-1}-(r+m)^6\mathcal R_{m-2},
   \]
   
   where
   
   \[
   Q_m(r)=(2r+2m+1)
   \bigl(3r^2+2rm+m^2+3r+m+1\bigr),
   \]
   
   one has
   
   \[
   \boxed{
   \deg_r N_h=\deg_r\mathcal R_{h-1}=3(h-1).
   }
   \]

2. **For even \(h\), the raw resultant is zero.**  Direct exact division gives
   
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   h=4:&\qquad 2r+5\mid N_4,\quad 2r+5\mid\mathcal R_3,\\
   h=6:&\qquad 2r+7\mid N_6,\quad 2r+7\mid\mathcal R_5.
   \end{aligned}
   \]
   
   This is the algebraic mate factor
   
   \[
   L_h(r)=2r+h+1=r+(r+h)+1.
   \]
   
   Consequently
   
   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{Res}(N_4,\mathcal R_3)
   =\operatorname{Res}(N_6,\mathcal R_5)=0.
   }
   \]
   
   A positive-range Gershgorin argument does not alter this: a resultant sees common roots anywhere over the algebraic closure, and the mate root lies outside the physical nonnegative range.  The meaningful nonmate object is obtained only after saturating \(L_h\).

After making these corrections, the exact computation is:

- gap \(3\): largest exceptional prime \(73\);
- gap \(4\), after mate saturation: largest exceptional prime \(4{,}453{,}913\);
- gap \(5\): largest exceptional prime \(45{,}845{,}668{,}061{,}051\);
- gap \(6\), after mate saturation: largest exceptional prime
  
  \[
  426{,}071{,}304{,}778{,}147{,}835{,}921{,}390{,}329{,}046{,}147{,}579.
  \]

Thus the computation does **not** reveal a polynomially controlled exceptional-prime threshold.  It reveals exponential-size resultant integers whose logarithmic heights grow rapidly with \(h\).  Every fixed \(h\) still reduces to finitely many primes, but this cannot be combined with an unbounded gap cutoff merely by exchanging quantifiers.

\tag{1.1}

\[
\mathcal R_0(r)=1,\qquad \mathcal R_1(r)=Q_1(r),

\mathcal R_j(r)=Q_j(r)\mathcal R_{j-1}(r)

\tag{1.2}

For even \(h\), define the mate-divided polynomials

\[

\(\mathcal R_{h-1}^{\#}=\mathcal R_{h-1}\).

\[
\boxed{

\operatorname{Res}_r
\bigl(\operatorname{pp}(N_h^{\#}),

}
\tag{1.4}
\]

This is the integer whose prime support is relevant to a **nonmate** common root.

## 7. Does the exceptional-prime threshold grow polynomially?

### 7.1 What is rigorously determined by these four calculations

If the threshold is defined by

\[
C(h)=P^+(\mathfrak D_h),
\]

then the exact values are

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
h&C(h)\\ \hline
3&73\\
4&4{,}453{,}913\\
5&45{,}845{,}668{,}061{,}051\\
6&426{,}071{,}304{,}778{,}147{,}835{,}921{,}390{,}329{,}046{,}147{,}579.
\end{array}
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The primitive/saturated resultant sizes have respectively about

\[
21,\quad49,\quad123,\quad190
\]

decimal digits.

This is emphatically **not evidence for a useful polynomial threshold**.  It is the behavior expected from a determinant whose degree and coefficient height both grow with the gap.

There is a logical distinction:

- four exact samples do not by themselves prove an asymptotic theorem saying that \(P^+(\mathfrak D_h)\) must be exponential;
- but the resultant method supplies only exponential-size bounds for \(|\mathfrak D_h|\) (equivalently, polynomial bounds for its logarithmic height), and the actual largest factors already grow explosively;
- no uniform theorem of the form \(P^+(\mathfrak D_h)\le h^A\) is present in Q2337 or in the current continuant algebra.

Therefore the correct classification is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{fixed-gap finiteness: yes; polynomially controlled all-gap threshold: no.}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### 7.2 Counting prime factors is not the relevant notion

The numbers of distinct prime factors in the unsaturated exact calculations are only

\[
5,\quad8,\quad12,\quad13.
\]

That modest count is irrelevant.  To eliminate a prime \(p\) by size one needs control of the **largest prime factor** or of the whole radical, and those quantities are already enormous.

## 8. Why fixed-gap induction does not close the full problem

For each fixed \(h\), after mate/content saturation one obtains the valid statement

\[
\forall h\quad
\exists E_h\text{ finite}\quad
\forall p\notin E_h:
N_h\text{ and }\mathcal R_{h-1}
\text{ have no nonmate common root}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

This does not imply a uniform statement for gaps growing with \(p\):

\[
\exists p_0\quad
\forall p>p_0\quad
\forall h\le H(p):
p\notin E_h.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The quantifiers cannot be interchanged.

Suppose optimistically that one only had an exponential-size threshold

\[
C(h)\le \exp(O(h^3)).
\]

To infer \(p>C(h)\) by size would require roughly

\[
h=O((\log p)^{1/3}),
\]

which is vastly stronger than

\[
H(p)=o(p/\log p).
\]

Even a hypothetical polynomial estimate

\[
C(h)=O(h^A)
\]

would require

\[
H(p)^A=o(p).
\]

The statement \(H(p)=o(p/\log p)\) does not imply this for an arbitrary degree \(A>1\).

Hence the proposed final step is invalid:

\[
\boxed{
\text{“every fixed gap has finitely many exceptions”}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{“all relevant growing gaps are eventually exception-free.”}
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

The resultant calculation remains useful for closing particular small gaps, but it cannot by itself complete P3.2.  A complete proof still needs one of the following genuinely uniform inputs:

1. a diagonal nondivisibility theorem
   
   \[
   p\nmid\mathfrak D_h
   \quad\text{for every physically relevant }(p,h);
   \]

2. a uniform Bézout/Casoratian certificate whose right side is a known \(p\)-unit on the endpoint-zero locus;

3. a much stronger gap cutoff, on the scale of a power of \(\log p\), together with a quantified resultant-height theorem.

## 9. Exact reproducibility

The computation used only integer polynomial recurrence, Euclidean division, `polresultant`, and exact integer factorization.  This PARI/GP script is valid as a file (all control-statement bodies are kept on one physical line where GP requires it):

```gp
P(t)=34*t^3+51*t^2+27*t+5;
Q(m,t)=(2*t+2*m+1)*(3*t^2+2*t*m+m^2+3*t+m+1);

N(H,T)={
  my(a=1,b,c);
  if(H==1,return(a));
  b=P(T+1);
  if(H==2,return(b));
  for(k=2,H-1,c=P(T+k)*b-(T+k)^6*a;a=b;b=c);
  b
};

RR(M,T)={
  my(a=1,b,c);
  if(M==0,return(a));
  b=Q(1,T);
  if(M==1,return(b));
  for(k=2,M,c=Q(k,T)*b-(T+k)^6*a;a=b;b=c);
  b
};

audit(H)={
  my(A=N(H,X),B=RR(H-1,X),L,res);
  if(H%2==0,L=2*X+H+1;A=divrem(A,L)[1];B=divrem(B,L)[1]);
  print(H," exact quotient resultant = ",polresultant(A,B,X));
  A=A/content(A);B=B/content(B);
  res=polresultant(A,B,X);
  print(H," primitive factorization = ",factor(abs(res)))
};

audit(3);
audit(4);
audit(5);
audit(6);
```

The \(h=3\) output independently matches Q2355, providing a normalization check on the entire audit.

## Final conclusion

The literal answers to the three requested parts are:

### (a)

- \(h=3\): nonzero resultant (3.1).
- \(h=4\): raw resultant \(0\); the correct mate-saturated resultants are (4.3) and (4.4).
- \(h=5\): nonzero resultant (5.1).
- \(h=6\): raw resultant \(0\); the correct mate-saturated resultants are (6.3) and (6.4).

### (b)

The exact data are not polynomially controlled in any useful sense.  The resultants are exponential-size integers, and the largest observed exceptional prime grows from \(73\) at \(h=3\) to a \(36\)-digit prime at \(h=6\).  Four values do not prove an asymptotic law for the largest prime factor, but they decisively rule out treating the current resultants as a small polynomial threshold.

### (c)

For every **fixed** \(h\), the saturated resultant does reduce the proof to finitely many primes.  This is a valid fixed-gap theorem.  It does **not** combine with \(H=o(n/\log n)\) to close the full problem, because \(h\) remains unbounded and the exceptional-prime threshold is nonuniform and explosively large.  The missing ingredient is a uniform arithmetic certificate, not more fixed-gap resultant factorizations.
