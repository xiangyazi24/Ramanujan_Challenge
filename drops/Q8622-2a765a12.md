# Q8622 — cold audit of the density-free full-defect reduction

## Verdict

The proposed implication is **valid under the stated structural premises**, with two quantifier/convention checks that must be made explicit:

1. Because \((R)_3\) counts **ordered** triples while \(J_X\) counts **unordered** triples in one full-defect fibre, the factor \(6\) must already be included in \(P_X\). The displayed polynomial is consistent with exactly that convention:
   \[
   P_X=468X^2-756X+288
      =6\bigl(78X^2-126X+48\bigr).
   \]
   If instead \(P_X\) were the number of unordered labels before ordering, the correct bound would be \(F_3\le 6P_XJ_X\). This changes only an absolute constant, not the conclusion.

2. The banked theorem for the exceptional/upper-digit channel must be a **global weighted pointwise theorem** for the entire set \(A_X\) appearing in the disjoint decomposition, not a nonuniform shellwise little-\(o\) statement and not a theorem for only one proper subset of \(A_X\).

With those conventions, no factor \(\lambda_X^3\) is needed. The absolute full-defect estimate gives a pointwise shell bound directly:
\[
\max_{0\le m<X^2}R_X^\dagger(m)
 \le 2+(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}.
\]
Consequently the weakest pointwise condition on the dyadic sequence \(J_X\) furnished by this argument is
\[
\boxed{J_X=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log^3(2X)}\right)}
\qquad (X\to\infty,\ X\text{ dyadic}).
\]
Equivalently, without replacing \(P_X\) by its asymptotic,
\[
\boxed{P_XJ_X\log^3(2X)=o(X^3).}
\]
The proposed hypothesis \(J_X=X^{o(1)}\) is much stronger and therefore suffices.

This is a conditional reduction, not a proof of the new arithmetic hypothesis. The nearby repository audits correctly leave the subpower fibre estimate as the unresolved recurrence-aware input.

## 1. Exact hypotheses and quantifiers

Let
\[
\Lambda_n:=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),
\qquad
H_n:=\gcd(\Lambda_n,B_n).
\]
For a dyadic integer \(X=2^k\), let
\[
\mathcal P_X=(X,2X]\cap\{\text{primes}\}.
\]
For every integer \(0\le m<X^2\), let
\[
\mathcal R_X^\dagger(m)\subseteq\mathcal P_X
\]
be the quotient-regular, target-filtered lower-root support, and put
\[
R_X^\dagger(m):=|\mathcal R_X^\dagger(m)|.
\]
The word “target-filtered” is load-bearing: every prime in this set must satisfy \(p\le m\). In particular, the artificial \(q=0\), \(p>m\) lower-root incidences are absent.

Assume the following three statements.

### H1. Exact ordered third-moment count

For every sufficiently large dyadic \(X\),
\[
F_3(X)
:=\sum_{0\le m<X^2}\bigl(R_X^\dagger(m)\bigr)_3
\le P_XJ_X,
\tag{H1}
\]
where
\[
(y)_3=y(y-1)(y-2),
\qquad
P_X=468X^2-756X+288.
\]
Every ordered triple counted on the left must be one of the nondegenerate full-defect triples covered by the label census. If quotient-regularity leaves any unlabelled degenerate triple, it needs a separate term; H1 would then not be the complete bound.

### H2. Density-free fibre hypothesis

The claimed hypothesis means, exactly,
\[
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists X_\varepsilon\ \forall\text{ dyadic }X\ge X_\varepsilon:
\qquad J_X\le X^\varepsilon.
\tag{H2}
\]
There is no exceptional set of dyadic scales and no averaging over labels, rows, or primes.

### H3. Exact disjoint support decomposition and banked companion bound

At the set level,
\[
\mathcal T_X(m)
=
\mathcal R_X^\dagger(m)\ \dot\cup\ \mathcal A_X(m),
\tag{H3a}
\]
where \(\mathcal T_X(m)\) is the actual target support in the shell and \(\mathcal A_X(m)\) is the **entire** complementary upper-digit/companion channel, including the stated \(E\)-sector and companion-only hits.

The theorem needed from the bank is the global weighted assertion
\[
M_A(n):=
\sum_{\substack{X\text{ dyadic}\\X>\sqrt n,\ X<n}}
\ \sum_{p\in\mathcal A_X(n)}\log p
=o(n)
\qquad(n\to\infty),
\tag{H3b}
\]
with the final shell understood as target-truncated by \(p\le n\).

The repository's repaired companion-height ledger is stronger for its named companion channel, giving an \(O(n^{2/3})\) global logarithmic bound. For the present reduction one must verify that the banked theorem's set is exactly the full \(\mathcal A_X\) in (H3a), or explicitly add the omitted exceptional subsets.

## 2. Ordered versus unordered triples

For a fixed row \(m\), \((R_X^\dagger(m))_3\) counts ordered triples of distinct primes. Each unordered triple has exactly \(3!=6\) orderings.

If
\[
L_X=78X^2-126X+48
\]
is the number of unordered full-defect labels and each label contains at most \(J_X\) unordered actual triples, then
\[
\#\{\text{unordered actual triples over all rows}\}
\le L_XJ_X.
\]
Therefore
\[
F_3(X)
=6\#\{\text{unordered actual triples}\}
\le6L_XJ_X
=P_XJ_X.
\]
Thus the displayed \(P_X\) has already absorbed the ordering factor. This is the only convention under which “unordered \(J_X\)” and the stated H1 agree without another \(6\).

Even if a convention error required replacing \(P_X\) by \(6P_X\), all exponents and all little-\(o\) thresholds below would remain unchanged.

## 3. Falling-third moment to a pointwise maximum

Fix a dyadic \(X\) and one row \(m<X^2\). Since every summand in \(F_3(X)\) is nonnegative,
\[
\bigl(R_X^\dagger(m)\bigr)_3
\le F_3(X)
\le P_XJ_X.
\tag{3.1}
\]
This is a direct pointwise inequality. One must **not** divide by the number \(X^2\) of rows; an average gives no upper bound for the maximum.

For every integer \(r\ge3\),
\[
(r-2)^3\le r(r-1)(r-2)=(r)_3.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Hence (3.1) gives
\[
R_X^\dagger(m)-2\le(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}
\qquad\text{when }R_X^\dagger(m)\ge3.
\]
For \(R_X^\dagger(m)\in\{0,1,2\}\), the same final inequality is automatic. Thus, uniformly for every row,
\[
\boxed{
R_X^\dagger(m)
\le2+(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Since \(P_X\le468X^2\) for \(X\ge1\),
\[
R_X^\dagger(m)
\le2+468^{1/3}X^{2/3}J_X^{1/3}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Under H2 this is
\[
R_X^\dagger(m)=X^{2/3+o(1)}
\tag{3.5}
\]
uniformly in \(m<X^2\).

## 4. Prime count to logarithmic shell mass

Define the regular weighted shell mass
\[
M_R(X,m)
:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal R_X^\dagger(m)}\log p.
\]
Every such prime satisfies \(p\le2X\), so
\[
M_R(X,m)
\le R_X^\dagger(m)\log(2X).
\]
Using (3.3),
\[
\boxed{
M_R(X,m)
\le
\left(2+(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}\right)\log(2X).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
M_R(X,m)
\ll
\log(2X)+X^{2/3}J_X^{1/3}\log(2X).
\tag{4.2}
\]
No prime-density normalization occurs. In particular, there is no \(\lambda_X^3\): H1 already counts actual triples absolutely.

For \(p>\sqrt n\), one has
\[
v_p(\Lambda_n)=1.
\]
Thus, on every shell used below, a target prime contributes exactly one copy of \(\log p\) to \(\log H_n\), not a higher prime-power weight.

## 5. Loads \(0,1,2\)

The third falling factorial does not see rows of load at most two. Formula (3.3) retains them through the additive constant \(2\).

For one shell their total possible logarithmic cost is at most
\[
2\log(2X).
\]
There are \(O(\log n)\) relevant dyadic shells, and every one has \(X\le n\). Therefore the aggregate cost of the invisible small loads is
\[
\sum_X2\log(2X)=O((\log n)^2)=o(n).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Hence the loads \(0,1,2\) create no gap in the reduction.

---

## 6. Correct local-to-global choice of dyadic shells

The local estimate is only stated for \(m<X^2\). For a global row \(m=n\), it is invalid to use a shell whose lower endpoint satisfies \(X\le\sqrt n\).

Let \(Y=Y(n)\) be the smallest dyadic integer strictly larger than \(\sqrt n\):
\[
Y=2^{\lfloor\log_2\sqrt n\rfloor+1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then
\[
\sqrt n<Y\le2\sqrt n,
\qquad
n<Y^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]
The strict inequality matters when \(n\) is a dyadic square.

Use the shells
\[
(X,2X],
\qquad
X=Y,2Y,4Y,\ldots,
\qquad X<n.
\tag{6.3}
\]
For every such shell,
\[
n<Y^2\le X^2,
\]
so the local theorem applies at the row \(m=n\).

The intervals are left-open and right-closed, so adjacent shells are disjoint: a prime \(p=2X\) belongs to \((X,2X]\) and not to \((2X,4X]\). The final interval is automatically truncated by the target condition \(p\le n\); applying the bound for the full shell only enlarges the admissible set.

A shifted choice such as \(X=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor+1\) would also satisfy the range condition, but it is not legitimate when H1/H2 are available only for dyadic \(X\). The choice (6.1) preserves the dyadic hypothesis.

## 7. Small primes and the transition interval

All primes \(p\le Y\) are handled without the shell theorem.

For \(p\le\sqrt n\),
\[
v_p(H_n)\log p
\le v_p(\Lambda_n)\log p
=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\log p
\le\log n.
\]
The elementary Chebyshev bound \(\pi(x)\ll x/\log x\) gives
\[
\sum_{p\le\sqrt n}v_p(H_n)\log p
\ll\pi(\sqrt n)\log n
\ll\sqrt n.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For the transition interval \(\sqrt n<p\le Y\le2\sqrt n\), the lcm exponent is one, and Chebyshev's \(\vartheta(x)\ll x\) gives
\[
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le Y}v_p(H_n)\log p
\le\sum_{p\le2\sqrt n}\log p
\ll\sqrt n.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Thus every prime not covered by a valid local shell costs only
\[
O(\sqrt n)=o(n).
\tag{7.3}
\]

## 8. Summing the regular dyadic shells

Let
\[
\mathscr D(n)=\{X:Y(n)\le X<n,\ X/Y(n)\text{ is a power of }2\}.
\]
By (4.1),
\[
M_R(n)
:=\sum_{X\in\mathscr D(n)}M_R(X,n)
\le
\sum_{X\in\mathscr D(n)}
\left(2+(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}\right)\log(2X).
\tag{8.1}
\]
The constant-two part is \(O((\log n)^2)\). The main part is
\[
S_J(n)
:=\sum_{X\in\mathscr D(n)}(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}\log(2X).
\tag{8.2}
\]

### 8.1 Direct consequence of \(J_X=X^{o(1)}\)

Fix any \(0<\delta<1\). H2 gives, for every sufficiently large dyadic \(X\),
\[
J_X\le X^\delta.
\]
Then
\[
(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}\log(2X)
\ll X^{2/3+\delta/3}\log(2X).
\tag{8.3}
\]
Because the \(X\)'s are dyadic and the exponent is positive, the sum is geometrically dominated by its largest scale:
\[
S_J(n)
\ll n^{2/3+\delta/3}\log n.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Taking, for example, \(\delta=1/2\) gives
\[
S_J(n)\ll n^{5/6}\log n=o(n).
\tag{8.5}
\]
More invariantly,
\[
M_R(n)=n^{2/3+o(1)}=o(n).
\tag{8.6}
\]
There is no extra factor \(O(\log n)\) multiplying the largest shell term: the shell sizes form a geometric progression, so one must sum them geometrically rather than bound every shell by the top scale.

### 8.2 The sharp quantitative condition supplied by this argument

Define
\[
\epsilon_X
:=
\frac{(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}\log(2X)}{X}
=
\left(\frac{P_XJ_X\log^3(2X)}{X^3}\right)^{1/3}.
\tag{8.7}
\]
Then
\[
S_J(n)=\sum_{X\in\mathscr D(n)}X\epsilon_X.
\]
Since the dyadic sum satisfies
\[
\sum_{X\in\mathscr D(n)}X<2n,
\]
we obtain
\[
S_J(n)
\le2n\sup_{X\ge Y(n)}\epsilon_X.
\tag{8.8}
\]
Therefore
\[
\epsilon_X\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
S_J(n)=o(n).
\tag{8.9}
\]
Conversely, a uniform dyadic majorant of this form cannot give \(o(n)\) if the single-scale term is not \(o(X)\): take a global row near the scale \(n\asymp X\), for which that shell is present. Thus the sharp pointwise threshold for this method is
\[
(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}\log(2X)=o(X),
\tag{8.10}
\]
or equivalently
\[
P_XJ_X\log^3(2X)=o(X^3).
\tag{8.11}
\]
Since
\[
P_X\sim468X^2,
\]
this is exactly
\[
\boxed{
J_X=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log^3(2X)}\right).
}
\tag{8.12}
\]
The corresponding summatory formulation is
\[
\sum_{\substack{X\le N\\X\text{ dyadic}}}
(P_XJ_X)^{1/3}\log(2X)=o(N).
\tag{8.13}
\]
For a nonnegative dyadic sequence, (8.13) is equivalent to (8.10): the summatory condition bounds its final summand, while (8.10) plus geometric summation gives (8.13).

The distinction between little-\(o\) and big-\(O\) at the threshold is real. Merely
\[
J_X=O(X/\log^3X)
\]
would yield only an \(O(X)\) shell bound and an \(O(n)\) global bound, not \(o(n)\).

## 9. Reinserting the full \(A\)-channel

By the exact disjoint decomposition (H3a), the weighted target mass on every valid shell is
\[
M_T(X,n)=M_R(X,n)+M_A(X,n).
\tag{9.1}
\]
Summing over the dyadic shells,
\[
M_T(n)=M_R(n)+M_A(n).
\tag{9.2}
\]
Sections 5–8 give
\[
M_R(n)=o(n).
\]
The banked global pointwise theorem (H3b) gives
\[
M_A(n)=o(n).
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{M_T(n)=o(n).}
\tag{9.3}
\]

The following weaker-looking statements would **not** be enough without further uniformity:

- an average over rows \(m<X^2\);
- an almost-all-\(n\) theorem;
- for each fixed shell, an \(o(n)\) estimate whose threshold depends nonuniformly on the shell;
- a theorem covering companion-only primes but omitting the \(E\)-sector included in the definition of \(A_X\).

The repository's repaired companion theorem is pointwise and globally weighted, so it has the correct quantifier shape for its covered channel.

## 10. Completion of the gcd estimate

Split \(\log H_n\) at \(Y(n)\). By Section 7, the contribution from \(p\le Y(n)\) is \(O(\sqrt n)\).

For \(p>Y(n)>\sqrt n\), one has \(v_p(\Lambda_n)=1\), so the contribution of all remaining primes is exactly bounded by the target logarithmic mass \(M_T(n)\). Therefore
\[
\log H_n
\le O(\sqrt n)+M_T(n).
\tag{10.1}
\]
Using (9.3),
\[
\boxed{
\log\gcd\bigl(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),B_n\bigr)=o(n).
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

Thus \(J_X=X^{o(1)}\) is sufficient as the only **new** arithmetic input after the exact shell count and the banked full \(A\)-channel theorem. It is not literally sufficient in isolation from those structural premises.

## Final conclusion

The reduction is sound. A subpower bound on the maximum actual full-defect fibre yields
\[
F_3(X)\le X^{2+o(1)},
\qquad
\max_mR_X^\dagger(m)\le X^{2/3+o(1)},
\]
and hence regular logarithmic shell mass \(X^{2/3+o(1)}\). Correctly chosen dyadic shells sum to \(n^{2/3+o(1)}\), while small loads cost \(O((\log n)^2)\), small/transition primes cost \(O(\sqrt n)\), and the stipulated complete \(A\)-channel costs \(o(n)\).

Accordingly, the claimed density-free implication is **verified**, and \(\lambda_X^3\) is unnecessary. The arithmetic bottleneck is now exactly the all-dyadic fibre estimate; the weakest pointwise version needed by this route is \(J_X=o(X/\log^3X)\), while \(J_X=X^{o(1)}\) is a stronger clean hypothesis.
