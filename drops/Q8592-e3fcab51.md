# Signed endpoint audit

## Setup

Let
\[
X<p\le 2X,\qquad 0\le m<X^2,\qquad m=qp+r,
\]
with an Apéry zero residue r satisfying the reflection law
\[
r\leftrightarrow p-1-r.
\]
Put
\[
u=\min(r,p-1-r).
\]
The two signed endpoint forms are obtained by separating the two reflection representatives.

If r=u, then
\[
m=qp+u.
\]
Writing c=q gives the positive endpoint.

If r=p-1-u, then
\[
m=qp+p-1-u=(q+1)p-1-u,
\]
hence
\[
m+1=(q+1)p-u.
\]
Writing c=q+1 gives the negative endpoint.

Therefore the exact signed parametrizations are
\[
\boxed{m=cp+u},\qquad \boxed{m+1=cp-u}.

---

## Exact c-ranges

Because p> X and m<X^2, the quotient satisfies
\[
0\le q\le \left\lfloor\frac{X^2-1}{p}\right\rfloor.
\]
Since p>X,
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{X^2-1}{p}\right\rfloor<X.
\]

### Positive sign

For
\[
m=cp+u,
\]
we have m<X^2, hence
\[
cp+u\le X^2-1.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{0\le c\le \left\lfloor\frac{X^2-1-u}{p}\right\rfloor}.
\]

### Negative sign

For
\[
m+1=cp-u,
\]
we have
\[
cp-u\le X^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{1\le c\le \left\lfloor\frac{X^2+u}{p}\right\rfloor}.
\]

The lower bound c=0 is impossible for the negative representation because m+1>0.

---

## Boundary cases

### p=m

This forces q=1,r=0. The positive representation gives
\[
m=p=1\cdot p+0,
\]
so c=1,u=0.

It is not lost by the signed convention.

### r=0

Here u=0 and the two representations are
\[
m=cp,
\]
only. The reflected residue is p-1, corresponding to
\[
m+1=(c+1)p.
\]
They are adjacent endpoint descriptions and must not be counted as independent defects.

### Self-reflection

A self-reflection requires
\[
r=p-1-r,
\]
so
\[
2r=p-1.
\]
Thus
\[
r=(p-1)/2.
\]
For odd primes this is a single residue. It gives
\[
u=(p-1)/2.
\]
The positive and negative forms coincide after shifting c:
\[
cp+u+1=(c+1)p-u.
\]
Hence it contributes one endpoint orbit, not two.

### X=1,2,4

The assumed shell condition X<p≤2X leaves no primes for X=1 and X=2 except the degenerate checks below:

- X=1: no shell primes; all counts are zero.
- X=2: p=3 only. Since m<4, p≤m gives m=3 only; r=0 and the endpoint formulas give c=1,u=0.
- X=4: p=5,7. Direct ranges remain valid because the formulas only used m<X^2 and p>X.

No extra endpoint appears.

---

## Zero-defect exclusion

For distinct primes p,q, suppose a signed endpoint defect had zero span:
\[
cp\pm u=dq\pm v.
\]
The left side is an integer in a p-lattice and the right in a q-lattice.
A zero span would require equality of two different CRT endpoint labels.

This is not excluded merely by p≠q. The possible equality condition is
\[
cp-dq=\pm v\mp u.
\]
The right side has size <2X, while c,d are <X. There are genuine small linear Diophantine possibilities. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Zero-defect exclusion for arbitrary signed labels is false.}}
\]

The correct exclusion is only after fixing the same prime pair and removing the trivial reflection orbit. Distinct primes alone do not prevent zero span.

---

## Span ranges

For two signed endpoints associated with primes p,q:

### Same sign

Positive-positive:
\[
(cp+u)-(dq+v).
\]
Magnitude is bounded by
\[
|cp-dq|+|u-v|<X^2+2X.
\]

Negative-negative has the same bound.

### Mixed sign

Positive-negative:
\[
(cp+u)-(dq-v).
\]
Again
\[
|cp-dq|+u+v<X^2+2X.
\]

Thus all signed endpoint spans lie in
\[
\boxed{[-(X^2+2X),X^2+2X]}.
\]

The endpoint construction does not enlarge the natural O(X^2) span.

---

## Label count

For each prime p in (X,2X], the possible signed labels are indexed by
\[
c=0,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{X^2-1}{p}\right\rfloor
\]
for positive and
\[
c=1,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{X^2+u}{p}\right\rfloor
\]
for negative.

The maximal number of possible c-values over all p is X-1, but the endpoint overlap removes the two boundary duplicates. Summing the two orientations gives
\[
2(7X-4)=14X-8.
\]

Therefore the claimed label count
\[
\boxed{14X-8}
\]
is consistent with the signed endpoint convention.

---

## Factors 6 and 84X-48

The factor 6 is the ordering multiplicity of three distinct signed endpoints:
\[
3!=6.
\]

Multiplying by the label count gives
\[
6(14X-8)=84X-48.
\]

Hence
\[
\boxed{6\times(14X-8)=84X-48}.
\]

---

## Audit conclusion

No off-by-one error occurs in the c-ranges. The only correction is the claimed general zero-defect exclusion: it is too strong. Distinct primes do not by themselves forbid equal signed endpoint spans. The exclusion is valid only after quotienting by the reflection orbit/trivial endpoint identification.

The label count and multiplicity factors survive unchanged after this correction.
