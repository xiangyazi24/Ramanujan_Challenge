# Q457 — Apéry palindromy: exact mechanism, modular fixed point, collision effect, and folded reduction

## Executive verdict

The congruence

```text
b_{p-1-r} ≡ b_r (mod p),    0 ≤ r < p,
```

is an exact theorem, not merely a computational discovery. It appears as Lemma 6.2 of Malik–Straub, *Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers* (2016), where it is proved directly from the binomial sum. Their paper also uses the symmetry to predict the limiting no-zero proportion `e^{-1/2}`, which is the zero-event of the reflected `Poisson(1/2)` model.

The current P3.2 repository contains a second, more geometric explanation. For the **correct** Apéry Laurent polynomial, a birational involution on the nonzero toric fibers sends `Lambda` to `Lambda^{-1}`. Consequently the fiber point counts satisfy

```text
mu_p(t) = mu_p(t^{-1}),
```

and the Mellin moments at exponents `r` and `-r` agree. This is the geometric origin of the index involution

```text
r ↦ p-1-r.
```

The main conclusions are:

1. The simple coordinate inversion `(x,y,z) ↦ (x^{-1},y^{-1},z^{-1})` is **not** the correct mechanism, and the symmetric Laurent polynomial displayed in the question is not the Laurent polynomial used by the project.
2. The weight-4 level-8 newform controls only the unique nontrivial fixed character `r=(p-1)/2`. Its Atkin–Lehner involution does not explain the full row palindromy.
3. Reflection creates no excess over a complete CRT period for two distinct primes. On short intervals it creates paired boundary discrepancies, which may be positive or negative. Symmetry alone permits a worst-case aligned star, so it cannot prove cross-prime decorrelation.
4. One can fold every zero pair to the lower half and remove the central fixed-point channel at pointwise cost `O(1)`. This is a clean exact reduction and a useful normalization, but it saves only constants; it does not eliminate the horizontal P3.2 obstruction.

A range-specific data note: the statement `max |Z_p|=8` is correct for the range in the question, but the current repository manuscript records a larger scan through `p≤10^6`, where the maximum is `12`. Thus boundedness by `8` is not a structural consequence of the involution.

# A. Why does `b_{p-1-r} ≡ b_r (mod p)` hold?

## A.1 The shortest proof is the known binomial proof

Recall

```text
b_r = sum_k binom(r,k)^2 binom(r+k,k)^2.
```

Fix a prime `p` and `0≤r<p`. For every `0≤k<p`, the standard negative-binomial congruences give

```text
binom(p-1-r,k)     ≡ (-1)^k binom(r+k,k)  (mod p),
binom(p-1-r+k,k)   ≡ (-1)^k binom(r,k)    (mod p).
```

Indeed,

```text
binom(-1-r,k) = (-1)^k binom(r+k,k),
binom(k-1-r,k) = (-1)^k binom(r,k),
```

and reduction of the upper entries modulo `p` is legitimate because `k<p`.
Terms outside the ordinary support vanish on both sides: when `k>p-1-r`, the first binomial on the left is zero, while `binom(r+k,k)` is divisible by `p`; when `k>r`, `binom(r,k)=0`.

Therefore

```text
b_{p-1-r}
 = sum_{k=0}^{p-1}
     binom(p-1-r,k)^2 binom(p-1-r+k,k)^2
 ≡ sum_{k=0}^{p-1}
     binom(r+k,k)^2 binom(r,k)^2
 = b_r                                  (mod p).
```

This is exactly the argument in Malik–Straub, Lemma 6.2:

- Amita Malik and Armin Straub, [*Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-016-0036-8), Research in Number Theory 2 (2016), article 5.

So the result should be cited as known in the paper. What may still be new and worth emphasizing is the toric/Kummer interpretation below and its role in the P3.2 dispersion problem.

## A.2 Correction to the proposed Laurent polynomial

The polynomial written in the question,

```text
(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)(1+1/x+1/y+1/z),
```

is not the Laurent polynomial used in the current P3.2 toric calculation. Its constant term at the first power is `4`, whereas `b_1=5`.

The project uses

```text
Lambda(x,y,z)
 = (1+x)(1+y)(1+z)
   ((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)/(xyz).
```

This is the polynomial implemented in
[`problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_marked_scalar_character_sum.py`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_marked_scalar_character_sum.py)
and used in the K3 fiber calculation
[`problems/3.2/toric_fiber_k3.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/toric_fiber_k3.tex).

For this `Lambda`, the naive inversion

```text
(x,y,z) ↦ (1/x,1/y,1/z)
```

does not send `Lambda` to `Lambda^{-1}` times a harmless monomial. The correct transformation is birational and is easiest to see after changing coordinates.

## A.3 The exact toric involution

Set

```text
u = (1+y)/y,
v = (1+z)/z,
w = -x/(uv).
```

The inverse change is

```text
y = 1/(u-1),
z = 1/(v-1),
x = -uvw.
```

On a nonzero fiber, the relevant open set is

```text
U = (G_m)^3 \ {u=1, v=1, w=1, uvw=1},
```

and the Laurent function becomes

```text
Lambda(u,v,w)
 = uv(w-1)(1-uvw) / (w(u-1)(v-1)).
```

Now define

```text
tau(u,v,w) = (w, (uvw)^(-1), u).
```

A direct calculation gives

```text
tau^2 = id,
Lambda(tau(u,v,w)) = Lambda(u,v,w)^(-1).
```

The exclusions defining `U` are permuted by `tau`:

```text
u=1      ↔ w'=1,
w=1      ↔ u'=1,
v=1      ↔ u'v'w'=1,
uvw=1    ↔ v'=1.
```

Hence `tau` is an actual involution of the nonzero-fiber open set, not merely a rational correspondence with uncontrolled exceptional points.

In the original variables the same involution is

```text
T(x,y,z) = (
  -z/(1+z),
  -(1+y)(1+z) / ((1+y)(1+z)+xyz),
  -x/(1+x)
),
```

on the open locus where the displayed denominators and `Lambda` are nonzero. It is much less transparent there, which is why the `(u,v,w)` coordinates are preferable.

This involution is recorded in the repository's character-aspect calculation
[`problems/3.2/lemmaBprime_result.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/lemmaBprime_result.tex).

## A.4 From fiber inversion to coefficient palindromy

For `t∈F_p^*`, let

```text
mu_p(t) = #{(x,y,z)∈(F_p^*)^3 : Lambda(x,y,z)=t}.
```

The involution gives the exact identity

```text
mu_p(t) = mu_p(t^{-1}).
```

The project's toric Mellin identity gives, for the interior exponents `1≤r≤p-2`,

```text
b_r ≡ - sum_{t∈F_p^*} mu_p(t)t^r  (mod p).
```

Therefore

```text
b_{p-1-r}
 ≡ -sum_t mu_p(t)t^{-r}
 =  -sum_t mu_p(t^{-1})t^r
 =  b_r                                  (mod p).
```

The endpoint identity `b_{p-1}≡b_0=1` is immediate from the binomial proof above, or from the Lucas congruence.

This gives the conceptual mechanism:

```text
fiber inversion t ↦ t^{-1}
        ⇓
Kummer-character duality chi^r ↦ chi^{-r}
        ⇓
Mellin moment r equals Mellin moment -r
        ⇓
b_r ≡ b_{p-1-r} (mod p).
```

## A.5 Sheaf-theoretic formulation

Let `A_r=omega_p^r` be the Kummer character with exponent `r`. Pullback by `tau` sends

```text
L_{A_r(Lambda)}  ↦  L_{A_r(Lambda^{-1})}
                  =  L_{A_r^{-1}(Lambda)}.
```

Thus the character-aspect Frobenius trace is invariant under

```text
A_r ↦ A_r^{-1},
```

which is precisely

```text
r ↦ p-1-r.
```

This is the cleanest bridge to the Katz/Dwork framework developed elsewhere in the project.

# B. The central fixed point and the level-8 modular form

## B.1 Exact parity decomposition

For odd `p`, the involution

```text
r ↦ p-1-r
```

has exactly one fixed point in `{0,...,p-1}`:

```text
r_c = (p-1)/2.
```

Define

```text
c_p = 1_{b_{(p-1)/2} ≡ 0 (mod p)}.
```

Then there is an integer `m_p≥0` such that

```text
|Z_p| = 2m_p + c_p.
```

Consequently

```text
|Z_p| is odd  ⇔  b_{(p-1)/2} ≡ 0 (mod p).
```

The Ahlgren–Ono congruence identifies the central value with the `p`-th Fourier coefficient of

```text
f_8(z) = eta(2z)^4 eta(4z)^4 ∈ S_4(Gamma_0(8)):

b_{(p-1)/2} ≡ a_p(f_8) (mod p)
```

(with stronger congruence forms available in the literature, depending on normalization). Therefore

```text
|Z_p| is odd  ⇔  a_p(f_8) ≡ 0 (mod p).
```

Reference:

- Scott Ahlgren and Ken Ono, [*A Gaussian hypergeometric series evaluation and Apéry number congruences*](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.2000.004), J. Reine Angew. Math. 518 (2000), 187–212.

## B.2 Why the modular form appears exactly at the fixed point

The character involution is

```text
A ↦ A^{-1}.
```

Its fixed characters on `F_p^*` are:

```text
A=1,
and A=phi_p, the quadratic character.
```

In exponent notation these are

```text
r=0,
and r=(p-1)/2.
```

The trivial character gives the elementary endpoint. The nontrivial fixed character is the quadratic slice, and that is exactly the slice where the finite-field `_4F_3` calculation specializes to the weight-4 level-8 modular form.

So the interaction is not accidental:

```text
palindromy = duality between a Kummer character and its inverse;
central modular coefficient = the unique nontrivial self-dual Kummer character.
```

## B.3 Atkin–Lehner does not explain the full palindromy

An Atkin–Lehner operator acts on the modular form `f_8` and controls the functional equation/root-number behavior of the **single fixed modular motive**. The identity

```text
b_r ≡ b_{p-1-r} (mod p)
```

is instead an identity, at one prime, among all `p` character labels `r`. It exists before the modular specialization and is proved by either the binomial identities or the toric involution.

Thus an Atkin–Lehner symmetry may be compatible with the self-duality of the central motive, but it is not the cause of the row-wide reflection law. The category distinction is:

```text
Atkin–Lehner / L-function functional equation:
    one motive, varying primes;

Apéry palindromy:
    one prime, varying Kummer characters.
```

The modular form explains the arithmetic of the fixed point; Kummer inversion explains the twins.

## B.4 Relation to the Poisson heuristic

Malik–Straub already observed that, after folding by reflection, there are `(p+1)/2` effective positions. Under an independent-uniform heuristic, the probability of no zero is

```text
(1-1/p)^((p+1)/2) → e^(-1/2).
```

This is exactly the zero-probability of a `Poisson(1/2)` variable. Their argument therefore anticipates the reflected-pair mean `1/2`, though the detailed histogram and higher factorial-moment agreement in the current project are substantially stronger experimental evidence.

# C. Does the twin structure help or hurt two-prime collision estimates?

## C.1 Complete CRT periods: neither — there is exact independence

Let

```text
X_p(n) = 1_{n mod p ∈ Z_p},
g_p(n) = X_p(n) - |Z_p|/p.
```

For distinct primes `p≠q`, CRT gives

```text
sum_{n mod pq} X_p(n)X_q(n) = |Z_p||Z_q|.
```

Equivalently,

```text
sum_{n mod pq} g_p(n)g_q(n) = 0.
```

This is exact and does not depend on whether the sets are reflected. Thus twin structure creates no cross-prime excess over a complete `pq`-period.

## C.2 What reflection does to the CRT classes

Suppose for clarity that

```text
Z_p = {r_p, -1-r_p mod p},
Z_q = {r_q, -1-r_q mod q}.
```

There are four joint CRT classes modulo `pq`. The global involution

```text
n ↦ -1-n mod pq
```

pairs them as

```text
(r_p,r_q)       ↔ (-1-r_p,-1-r_q),
(r_p,-1-r_q)    ↔ (-1-r_p,r_q).
```

Thus the four classes form two reflected pairs. Their number remains `4`, exactly the product `2·2` predicted by independent local densities.

For a short interval `I`, however, the two representatives in a reflected pair are at positions

```text
x and pq-1-x.
```

This creates a deterministic **boundary relation**, not a positive main term. For example, when `I=(N/2,N]`, both members can lie in `I` only if

```text
N < pq-1 ≤ 2N.
```

If `pq>2N+1`, at most one member of each reflected pair can lie in `I`, which is a mild repulsion. When `pq` lies near the interval scale, both may occur. Hence the sign of the incomplete-period covariance is not fixed by symmetry.

## C.3 Reflection can help the analysis

It gives three useful simplifications.

### 1. Real cosine Fourier coefficients after centering

Let

```text
R_p = Z_p ∩ {0,...,(p-3)/2},
c_p = 1_{(p-1)/2 ∈ Z_p}.
```

Then the unnormalized Fourier transform is

```text
F_p(h) = sum_{r∈Z_p} e_p(hr)
       = e(-h/(2p)) [
           2 sum_{r∈R_p} cos(pi h(2r+1)/p)
           + c_p(-1)^h
         ].
```

After removing the universal phase `e(-h/(2p))`, the transform is real. The sine modes disappear, and every pair is encoded by one center parameter.

### 2. The nontrivial fixed-point channel is separated

The central zero can be treated by divisors of `2n+1`; see Part D. It therefore need not contaminate the generic pair analysis.

### 3. The state space can be represented by pair centers

For `|Z_p|=2`, one may use

```text
alpha_p = (2r_p+1)/p
```

and

```text
F_p(h) = 2e(-h/(2p))cos(pi h alpha_p).
```

This is exactly the useful one-parameter reformulation identified in Q438.

## C.4 Reflection does not itself give a saving

The same symmetry permits a maximally bad adversarial construction. Fix an integer `n_0` and set

```text
Z_p^* = {n_0 mod p, -1-n_0 mod p}
```

for every prime `p`. Then:

- `|Z_p^*|=2`;
- `Z_p^*=-1-Z_p^*` exactly;
- every vertical zero-count and palindrome constraint is satisfied;
- nevertheless `n_0 mod p∈Z_p^*` for **every** prime.

Thus all primes can collide at one row despite perfect twin symmetry. The symmetry alone is compatible with the worst possible horizontal star.

This is the decisive answer to “help or hurt?”:

> Reflection is a valuable structural normalization, but it is not a decorrelation theorem. It neither forces excess nor forces cancellation across distinct primes. The cross-prime distribution of the pair centers is still the missing arithmetic input.

## C.5 What should be estimated

For an interval `I`, write

```text
J_{p,q}(I)
 = #{n∈I : n mod p∈Z_p and n mod q∈Z_q}.
```

The natural main term is

```text
|I| |Z_p||Z_q|/(pq),
```

with a boundary correction when `pq>|I|`. The P3.2 second moment needs cancellation in the **signed aggregate**

```text
sum_{p≠q}
  [J_{p,q}(I) - boundary-corrected main term].
```

An absolute pairwise bound is both too strong and generally false at the desired scale. The palindrome merely organizes the summands into reflected pairs; it does not estimate the signed aggregate.

# D. Can the problem be halved?

## D.1 Exact lower-half decomposition

Define

```text
R_p = Z_p ∩ [0,(p-3)/2],
c_p = 1_{(p-1)/2∈Z_p}.
```

Then there is a disjoint decomposition

```text
Z_p = R_p
      disjoint_union (-1-R_p mod p)
      disjoint_union c_p{(p-1)/2}.
```

For every integer `n`, this gives the exact indicator identity

```text
1_{n mod p∈Z_p}
 = 1_{n mod p∈R_p}
   + 1_{(-n-1) mod p∈R_p}
   + c_p 1_{p|2n+1}.
```

Therefore, in the P3.2 prime window `sqrt(n)<p≤n`,

```text
D(n) = D_R^+(n) + D_R^-(n) + C(n),
```

where

```text
D_R^+(n) = #{p : n mod p∈R_p},
D_R^-(n) = #{p : (-n-1) mod p∈R_p},
C(n)     = #{p : c_p=1 and p|2n+1}.
```

## D.2 The central channel is pointwise harmless

Every prime counted by `C(n)` divides the fixed integer `2n+1`. If three distinct primes greater than `sqrt(n)` divided `2n+1`, their product would exceed `n^(3/2)`, which is greater than `2n+1` for all sufficiently large `n`. Hence

```text
C(n) ≤ 2
```

for large `n`, with the finitely many small cases absorbed into the constant.

Thus the rare nonordinary central primes contribute at most

```text
O(1)
```

to the divisor count and `O(log n)` to the logarithmic gcd weight. Their global density is irrelevant to the all-index P3.2 bound.

This is a genuine useful consequence of the fixed-point structure.

## D.3 Folded formulation

Define the folded residue

```text
rho_p(n) = min(r, p-1-r),
where r = n mod p in {0,...,p-1}.
```

For noncentral residues,

```text
n mod p∈Z_p  ⇔  rho_p(n)∈R_p.
```

Consequently one may replace the original problem, up to the harmless central term, by

```text
D_fold(n)
 = #{sqrt(n)<p≤n : rho_p(n)∈R_p}.
```

Then

```text
D(n) = D_fold(n) + O(1).
```

Therefore P3.2 is equivalent to

```text
D_fold(n) = o(n/log n).
```

This is the correct sense in which the problem can be halved.

## D.4 What does not work

It is **not** enough to count only

```text
#{p : n mod p∈R_p}
```

and discard upper-half hits. Those omitted hits are exactly

```text
#{p : (-n-1) mod p∈R_p}.
```

To use a one-orientation half-set theorem, one must prove it uniformly for both residue channels. Equivalently, prove the folded statement directly.

Also, folding does not halve the probability that a prime is bad. Since each representative in `R_p` corresponds to two original residues,

```text
Pr(rho_p(n)∈R_p) = 2|R_p|/p,
```

which is the same noncentral hit probability as before. What is halved is the number of stored atoms and independent pair labels, not the expected prime load.

Thus folding improves notation, Fourier structure, and constants, but not the exponent or the horizontal difficulty.

# Recommended integration into the P3.2 manuscript

## 1. Cite rather than claim the palindrome as new

State Malik–Straub Lemma 6.2 as the elementary source. Their proof is only a few lines and should be included or cited in the preliminary congruence section.

## 2. Add the toric involution as a second proposition

The birational proof is valuable because it explains the symmetry in the same K3/Mellin language used by the rest of the project. A suitable proposition is:

> **Fiber inversion proposition.** On the nonzero toric open set, the involution `tau(u,v,w)=(w,(uvw)^{-1},u)` satisfies `Lambda∘tau=Lambda^{-1}`. Hence `mu_p(t)=mu_p(t^{-1})`, and the Apéry Mellin coefficients satisfy `b_r≡b_{p-1-r} mod p`.

This is conceptually stronger than presenting a computer verification.

## 3. Separate the quadratic fixed point

State

```text
|Z_p| = 2|R_p| + 1_{p|a_p(f_8)}.
```

Then prove once and for all that the central contribution to the top-prime count is `O(1)` because such a prime divides `2n+1`.

## 4. Rewrite the dispersion section with folded atoms

Use `R_p` or the pair centers `alpha_{p,j}`. The Fourier transform becomes a cosine sum with a universal phase. This removes redundant conjugate variables and makes the exact remaining hypothesis visibly a statement about the cross-prime distribution of pair centers.

## 5. Keep the no-go example

The reflected anchored family

```text
Z_p^*={n_0 mod p,-1-n_0 mod p}
```

should be retained as a one-line proof that palindrome, bounded zero count, and even the exact pair structure cannot by themselves prove all-index P3.2.

# Direct answers

## (A) Mechanism

The congruence has a known direct binomial proof. Geometrically, the correct mechanism is not ordinary coordinate inversion but the birational involution

```text
tau(u,v,w)=(w,(uvw)^(-1),u),
Lambda∘tau=Lambda^(-1).
```

It gives fiber-count inversion and hence equality of the `r` and `-r` Mellin moments. The Laurent polynomial written in the question is not the project's Apéry polynomial.

## (B) Modular fixed point

The level-8 form controls the unique nontrivial fixed Kummer character, the quadratic character `r=(p-1)/2`. It explains whether the singleton fixed point is a zero and hence whether `|Z_p|` is odd. Atkin–Lehner does not explain the full palindromy; the full symmetry is Kummer duality, while the modular form is the arithmetic specialization at its self-dual fixed point.

## (C) Collision effect

Over a complete `pq` period, twin structure causes no excess: the centered cross-correlation is exactly zero by CRT. On incomplete intervals it imposes mirror-paired boundary errors, which can be positive or negative. Symmetry helps organize the Fourier analysis but does not give a saving; an adversarial reflected family can align every prime at one integer.

## (D) Halving

Yes, after defining the half problem correctly. Fold each reflected pair to its smaller representative and retain both orientations through `rho_p(n)=min(r,p-1-r)`. The central fixed-point channel is `O(1)`. Then

```text
D(n)=D_fold(n)+O(1),
```

so P3.2 is equivalent to the folded estimate. Merely counting lower-half residues of `n mod p` and ignoring their mirrors is insufficient. Folding is an exact structural simplification, not a solution to cross-prime decorrelation.

## Bottom line

The palindrome is a fully understood local duality:

```text
binomial reflection
 = toric fiber inversion
 = inverse-Kummer-character symmetry.
```

Its strongest new P3.2 consequence is the exact removal of the modular fixed-point channel and the reduction to a cosine-valued point process of reflected pair centers. The remaining all-index obstruction is still horizontal: prove that those centers do not align across different primes in the incomplete CRT intervals relevant to `n`.
