# Boundary-value analysis of the K recurrence

## Short verdict

The proposed induction is a useful reformulation, but by itself it cannot prove the nonvanishing theorem. The two endpoint seeds do not determine the sign/nonvanishing of an interior second-order recurrence over F_p. The missing ingredient is a genuine invariant (a Wronskian, positivity, monotonicity, or divisibility constraint) connecting the two boundary conditions.

The correct formalization is a discrete Dirichlet problem, and its solution shows exactly where the obstruction lies.

## Recurrence form

Fix r and write

\[
Y_j=K(r,r+j).
\]

The Racah recurrence gives

\[
(r+j+1)^3Y_{j+1}=c_jY_j-(r+j)^3Y_{j-1},
\]

where

\[
c_j=c(r,r+j)
\]

is the spectral coefficient. For p larger than all relevant indices, this is a well-defined second-order recurrence.

The left boundary condition is

\[
Y_0=K(r,r)=0,
\]

and the adjacent computation gives

\[
Y_1\neq0.
\]

Therefore every Y_j is a scalar multiple of Y_1:

\[
Y_j=D_j(r)Y_1,
\]

where D_0=0, D_1=1 and

\[
(r+j+1)^3D_{j+1}=c_jD_j-(r+j)^3D_{j-1}.
\]

Thus the desired statement is equivalent to proving

\[
D_h(r)\neq0.
\]

## Why the second endpoint condition is insufficient

The right endpoint condition gives

\[
K(r+h,r+h-1)=\frac{b_{r+h-1}}6\neq0.
\]

By symmetry of K this is information about the neighboring kernel row at the other endpoint. It is not an equation forcing D_h(r) to be nonzero.

A generic second-order recurrence can satisfy

\[
Y_0=0,\quad Y_1\neq0,\quad Y_h=0,
\]

while the reversed solution from the right endpoint is also nonzero. Hence the two endpoint seeds do not contradict an interior zero.

The Q2291 phenomenon is exactly this generic behavior when the target index is not constrained by a second Apéry zero.

## What extra structure would make induction work

One needs a new identity of the following type:

\[
D_h(r)=\frac{F(r,h)}{G(r,h)}b_{r+h}+E(r,h)b_r,
\]

or more generally a conserved quantity

\[
W_j(Y,Z)=\text{constant in }j
\]

where Z is a second explicitly controlled solution. Then b_r=b_{r+h}=0 would force the constant to be nonzero and imply D_h(r)≠0.

The previous cross-Casoratian attempt failed because the natural companion solution does not give a constant Wronskian. Therefore the required object would have to be a different bilinear invariant, likely involving the Apéry sequence b_n itself rather than only K.

## Relation to h=2

The h=2 proof succeeds because the recurrence degenerates to a one-step computation:

\[
Y_2=\frac{c_1}{(r+2)^3}Y_1,
\]

because Y_0=0. No cancellation is possible.

For h>=3, the second term

\[
-\frac{(r+j)^3}{(r+j+1)^3}Y_{j-1}
\]

enters and creates the possible cancellation.

## Final assessment

The boundary-value formulation is correct, but solving it does not finish P3.2. The new theorem needed is not ordinary induction; it is an additional Apéry/Racah identity giving a conserved quantity or a factorization of the transfer determinant.

The most promising direction is therefore a variant of option (a): discover an explicit determinant identity for the two-sided boundary problem. Without such an identity, the current recurrence data are insufficient to rule out higher-gap zeros.
