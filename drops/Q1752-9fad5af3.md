# Q1752 audit: canonical remainder equation and the gap-one seam

## 1. Fixed canonical setup

Let \(B\in \mathbf F_p[X]\) be the unique representative of degree \(<p\) satisfying \(B(x)=A_x\) for all \(x\in\mathbf F_p\). The recurrence operator is

\[
L B=(X+1)^3B(X+1)-(34X^3+51X^2+27X+5)B(X)+X^3B(X-1).
\]

Since the Apéry row satisfies the recurrence at every field point where the shifts are interpreted in \(\mathbf F_p\),

\[
(LB)(x)=0\qquad(x\in\mathbf F_p).
\]

Therefore \(LB\) is divisible by \(X^p-X\). Because \(\deg B<p\), the degree estimate gives

\[
\deg LB\le p+2,
\]

hence

\[
LB=(X^p-X)(\alpha X^2+\beta X+\gamma).
\]

The earlier examples are therefore consistent with a quadratic quotient \(Q_2(X)=\alpha X^2+\beta X+\gamma\).

## 2. Extracting \(Q_2\) from the top coefficients

Write

\[
B(X)=b_{p-1}X^{p-1}+b_{p-2}X^{p-2}+b_{p-3}X^{p-3}+\cdots .
\]

Expanding only the top three degrees of \(LB\) determines \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). No lower coefficient enters because shifts preserve degree and the cubic multipliers only raise degree by three.

Thus the correct algebraic route is not to differentiate an arbitrary lift: it is to compute the three-dimensional defect space of the recurrence operator on the canonical quotient

\[
\mathbf F_p[X]_{<p}/(X^p-X).
\]

The resulting equations are a linear system in

\[
(b_{p-1},b_{p-2},b_{p-3})
\]

and give \(Q_2\) exactly.

## 3. Evaluation at the gap zeros

Assume

\[
B(a)=B(a+2)=0,
\]

with \(a=m-1\), \(p=2m+1\). Evaluating \(LB=(X^p-X)Q_2\) at \(a\) gives

\[
(a+1)^3B(a+1)+a^3B(a-1)=(a^p-a)Q_2(a).
\]

Differentiating the exact polynomial identity gives

\[
L B'(a)= (p a^{p-1}-1)Q_2(a)+(a^p-a)Q_2'(a).
\]

The derivative equation is legitimate because \(B\) is the fixed canonical representative; no change of representative by a multiple of \(X^p-X\) is allowed.

At the second zero \(a+2\), the same equations give a second linear relation. Together they constrain

\[
B'(a),\quad B'(a+2),\quad Q_2(a),\quad Q_2(a+2).
\]

Reflection gives

\[
B'(a+2)=-B'(a),
\]

so the removable value is

\[
C^*=-\frac{B'(a)}2.
\]

## 4. Obstruction to the requested closure

The remainder identity reduces the problem, but it does not by itself force

\[
\Sigma=0
\]

or exclude it. The remaining unknown is the quadratic defect \(Q_2\), equivalently the top canonical coefficients of \(B\). The recurrence controls the defect only through this three-dimensional quotient.

A sufficient next lemma would be:

\[
Q_2\text{ is determined by the endpoint data }B(a),B(a+1),B(a+2),
\]

or any prime-support restriction showing that the resulting expression

\[
-\frac23-A_mR+\frac{2A_m}{C^*}
\]

cannot vanish.

The current calculation does not establish such a law.

## 5. Conclusion

The corrected global remainder equation is a real structural improvement over the failed derivative argument. It proves that the canonical derivative information is coupled to a quadratic recurrence defect. However, the final exclusion step is still open: \(Q_2\) has not been eliminated, and the equations above alone do not prove that endpoint and trace cannot vanish simultaneously.

Therefore the status is:

**Conditional reduction proved; no complete nonvanishing theorem obtained.**

The missing certificate is an explicit formula or support theorem for the quadratic defect \(Q_2\).
