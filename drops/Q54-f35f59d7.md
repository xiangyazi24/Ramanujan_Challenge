# Pair-witness spectral transversality for Apéry zeros

## Executive conclusion

The pair-witness measure can be written explicitly, but there is no known identity reducing the CRT point of a pair of Apéry zeros to a third Apéry zero condition. The desired statement is therefore genuinely a triple-correlation theorem for the Apéry compatible system.

The key point is:

\[
\operatorname{CRT}(z_p,z_q)\pmod r
\]

is an elementary finite-field expression involving the residues and inverses of p and q modulo r. The Apéry recurrence gives strong relations inside one characteristic, but it does not presently give a congruence transporting a zero modulo p and a zero modulo q into a zero modulo r.

Thus spectral transversality is the correct formulation of the missing independence theorem.

---

## 1. Explicit formula for the pair-witness measure

For fixed distinct primes p,q and zeros

\[
z_p\in Z_p,\qquad z_q\in Z_q,
\]

write

\[
m_{p,q}=z_pq(q^{-1}\bmod p)+z_pp(p^{-1}\bmod q)\pmod {pq}.
\]

For the physical interval [0,X^2), define

\[
W_{p,q}(z_p,z_q)=1_{m_{p,q}<X^2}.
\]

Then

\[
\nu_r(u)=\sum_{p\ne q}
\sum_{z_p\in Z_p,z_q\in Z_q}
\frac{W_{p,q}(z_p,z_q)}{pq}
1_{m_{p,q}\equiv u\pmod r}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\widehat{\nu_r}(a)=
\sum_{p\ne q}\frac1{pq}
\sum_{z_p,z_q}
W_{p,q}(z_p,z_q)
 e_r(a m_{p,q}).
\]

This is an explicit finite exponential sum.

The difficulty is not computation but cancellation.

---

## 2. Reduction of CRT modulo the third prime

Reducing the CRT formula modulo r gives

\[
m_{p,q}\equiv
z_pq(q^{-1}\bmod p)+z_pp(p^{-1}\bmod q)\pmod r.
\]

However, the coefficients

\[
q(q^{-1}\bmod p),\qquad p(p^{-1}\bmod q)
\]

are integers naturally defined modulo pq, not modulo r. To reduce modulo r one must choose the representative in [0,pq), so the expression is not a bilinear form over \(\mathbf F_r\).

This is the first obstruction: CRT is global arithmetic, while the Apéry zero condition modulo r is local arithmetic in characteristic r.

---

## 3. Is there an Apéry identity linking the CRT point to b_n mod r?

The desired identity would be something like

\[
r\mid b_{m_{p,q}}
\iff
F_r(z_p,z_q)=0
\]

for a simple expression F_r.

No such identity is known, and the existing Apéry congruences do not produce one.

Known identities include:

\[
b_{ap+b}\equiv b_a b_b\pmod p
\]

and the block law

\[
b_{qp+s}\equiv b_qb_s\pmod p.
\]

These relate indices separated by multiples of the SAME prime p. They do not relate

\[
Z_p\times Z_q
\]

to zeros modulo a third prime r.

The CRT index has no distinguished relation to r, so the block law cannot be applied.

---

## 4. Fourier formulation

The transversality quantity is

\[
\Delta_r
=\frac1r\sum_{a\ne0}
\widehat{1_{Z_r}}(a)
\widehat{\nu_r}(-a).
\]

The anchored-star obstruction corresponds to the extreme case

\[
\nu_r\approx c\,\delta_{m_0\bmod r},
\]

so

\[
\widehat{\nu_r}(a)=ce_r(am_0),
\]

which aligns with \(\widehat{1_{Z_r}}\).

For genuine Apéry zeros, one expects

\[
\widehat{\nu_r}(a)\ll X^{-\eta}
\]

on average, giving cancellation against the Fourier coefficients of Z_r.

But this is precisely the missing theorem.

---

## 5. The triple-correlation interpretation

The desired statement is equivalent to

\[
\Pr(r\mid b_{\operatorname{CRT}(z_p,z_q)}\mid z_p\in Z_p,z_q\in Z_q)
\approx \frac{|Z_r|}{r}.
\]

This is the cleanest formulation:

> Given two independent Apéry zeros in two characteristics, the CRT-produced index should behave randomly with respect to the third characteristic.

This is a three-characteristic independence statement.

---

## 6. What automorphic object controls this?

The relevant object is not the classical Rankin-Selberg L-function of the modular form.

It is the correlation of three reductions of the Apéry motive:

\[
\mathcal M_p\boxtimes\mathcal M_q\boxtimes\mathcal M_r.
\]

A successful theorem would require a large-sieve/Deligne estimate for the associated trace functions:

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}
\sum_{z_p,z_q,z_r}
1_{z_p\in Z_p,z_q\in Z_q,z_r\in Z_r}
F(p,q,r,z_p,z_q,z_r)
\]

with cancellation after removing the independent density term.

This is a varying-characteristic analogue of a triple Rankin convolution.

---

## 7. Why the recurrence helps but does not finish the proof

The Apéry recurrence provides:

- reflection symmetry;
- no consecutive zeros;
- gap polynomial constraints;
- bounded zero-set size;
- monodromy information.

These control the geometry of each individual Z_p.

They do not currently control the relative position of

\[
Z_p,Z_q,Z_r
\]

inside three different finite fields.

The anchored-star countermodel demonstrates that all one-prime constraints are insufficient.

Therefore the remaining theorem must be genuinely cross-prime.

---

## Final statement

The pair-witness measure is explicitly computable:

\[
\widehat{\nu_r}(a)=
\sum_{p\ne q}\frac1{pq}
\sum_{z_p,z_q}
W_{p,q}(z_p,z_q)e_r(am_{p,q}).
\]

But there is no known Apéry identity converting

\[
m_{p,q}\pmod r
\]

into an Apéry recurrence quantity modulo r.

The spectral transversality conjecture is therefore equivalent to a new theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The three-characteristic Apéry Hasse--Witt system has square-root cross-prime correlation.}
}
\]

Proving this would establish the missing \((HM)_3\) input and defeat the anchored-star obstruction using genuinely Apéry-specific arithmetic.
