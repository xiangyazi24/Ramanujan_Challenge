# Apéry full-Newton shell Bézout audit

## 0. Executive conclusion

I do **not** currently have a publication-grade nonzero Bézout certificate

\[
B_{n,q}=\lambda_0A_0^2+\sum_{1\le j\le5}\lambda_j\Delta_j\ne0,
\qquad \log|B_{n,q}|=o(n),
\]

for the actual full-Newton shell, where the \(\Delta_j\) are the five nonzero \(2\times2\) minors of the two \(S=2\) rows. The repository contains several pieces that come close, but each has a precise obstruction:

* the full Newton weights themselves are primitive, but every adjacent-stencil elimination introduces a Pascal factor whose logarithm is \(\Theta_q(n)\) for fixed \(q\); this factor must be divided out before any height claim;
* the exact doubled-period/Ore construction produces a low-degree polynomial source, but its first Newton image is **universally aliased** modulo every candidate prime, target or not;
* the first divided Newton digit can recover rank two, but the exact Ore wedge identity contains additional wedge terms, so it has not been reduced to the original six-generator ideal;
* homogeneous toric integration-by-parts cannot kill the terminal shell packet: restriction to the singular curve \(y=z=-1\) gives an explicit nonzero Laurent polynomial with a unit coefficient \(\pm1\) or \(\pm9\);
* exact first-cell ray telescopers exist, of total order at most 38, but the 14 ray modules have pairwise unit GCRD. Thus a scalar shared-solution argument is impossible; one needs a gauge/intertwiner or a distinguished nonlinear/inhomogeneous relation.

The cleanest remaining target is a **two-sided common-observable identity**. It has an automatic determinantal wrapper that is already an explicit integer linear combination of the five minors. The missing actual-shell lemma is to find two common polynomial-coefficient observables whose shell evaluations have a nonzero polynomial/sublinear determinant after all Pascal content is removed.

I give the exact algebra below, including the ideal-membership distinction from mere divisibility by \(c_+c_-\), the primitive Newton identity, the rigorous homogeneous no-go, and the smallest plausible escape.

# 1. Put the six generators in their exact normal form

Write

\[
a=A_0,
\qquad r_0=R_0,
\qquad r_1=R_1,
\qquad r_2=R_2,
\qquad \bar r_1=R_{-1},
\qquad \bar r_2=R_{-2}.
\]

The two rows are

\[
u=(a,r_0,r_1,r_2),
\qquad
v=(a,r_0,\bar r_1,\bar r_2).
\]

There are six column-pair minors, but the first is identically zero. Put

\[
x=\bar r_1-r_1,
\qquad y=\bar r_2-r_2,
\qquad z=r_1\bar r_2-r_2\bar r_1.
\]

Then the five nonzero minors are exactly

\[
\Delta_{02}=ax,
\qquad
\Delta_{03}=ay,
\qquad
\Delta_{12}=r_0x,
\qquad
\Delta_{13}=r_0y,
\qquad
\Delta_{23}=z.
\]

Therefore the integer ideal whose gcd is \(J\) is

\[
\boxed{
I=(a^2,ax,ay,r_0x,r_0y,z)\subset\mathbf Z.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Since

\[
z=r_1y-r_2x,
\tag{1.2}
\]

the final generator is the compatibility relation between the two defect differences.

This normal form is useful because it separates the genuinely new shell problem from generic determinantal algebra. Nothing about the recurrence alone prevents arbitrary common factors in (1.1).

## 1.1 Exact relation to \(c_+c_-\)

Let

\[
c_+=\gcd(a,r_0,r_1,r_2),
\qquad
c_-=\gcd(a,r_0,\bar r_1,\bar r_2).
\]

Every \(2\times2\) determinant of \(u,v\) is a difference of two products, each containing one factor divisible by \(c_+\) and one divisible by \(c_-\). Also \(a^2\) is divisible by \(c_+c_-\). Hence

\[
\boxed{c_+c_-\mid J.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

This is only divisibility. It does **not** say that \(J/(c_+c_-)\) is itself generated by some visible quotient of the six generators.

However, if one constructs any explicit integer combination

\[
B=\lambda_0a^2+\lambda_1ax+\lambda_2ay+\lambda_3r_0x+\lambda_4r_0y+\lambda_5z
\ne0,
\tag{1.4}
\]

then

\[
J\mid B
\quad\text{and}\quad
c_+c_-\mid B.
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{J}{c_+c_-}\mid \frac{B}{c_+c_-},
\qquad
\left|\frac{J}{c_+c_-}\right|\le |B|.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Thus a single nonzero \(B\in I\) with \(\log|B|=o(n)\) is sufficient. No equality \(J=B\), and no factorization of \(J\), is required.

Conversely, a statement such as

\[
\operatorname{rad}(J)\mid D
\]

or a finite observation that every prime divisor of \(J\) divides a short product is **not** a Bézout certificate. It must be upgraded to actual integral ideal membership (1.4), or at least to a valuation inequality strong enough to bound \(J/(c_+c_-)\).

# 2. Universal determinantal wrapper for any common observable

There is one general construction that should be used to organize every serious attempt.

For coefficient vectors

\[
\alpha=(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_3),
\qquad
\beta=(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_3)
\]

with integer coefficients depending on \((n,q)\), define

\[
L_\alpha^+=\alpha\cdot u,
\qquad
L_\alpha^-=\alpha\cdot v,
\]

and similarly for \(\beta\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_{\alpha,\beta}
&=L_\alpha^+L_\beta^- -L_\beta^+L_\alpha^-\\
&=\sum_{0\le i<j\le3}
(\alpha_i\beta_j-\alpha_j\beta_i)
(u_iv_j-u_jv_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Since the \((0,1)\) minor vanishes, this is automatically an explicit integral combination of the five nontrivial minors:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\alpha,\beta}\in I.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

More generally,

\[
B=\lambda a^2+\mathcal M_{\alpha,\beta}
\tag{2.3}
\]

is already a publication-ready six-generator Bézout expression once \(\lambda,\alpha,\beta\) are explicitly specified.

This reduces the actual-shell problem to a sharply stated task:

> Find two **common** two-sided observables \(\alpha,\beta\) for which the actual Laurent/Newton shell allows \(\mathcal M_{\alpha,\beta}\) (possibly after adding \(\lambda a^2\)) to be evaluated as a nonzero subexponential integer.

The word “common” is essential. If different coefficient vectors are applied on the positive and negative sides, the determinant is no longer automatically in the minor ideal.

# 3. Exact Newton identity and the universal Pascal content

Put

\[
L=D-d
\]

and, for an arbitrary integer sequence \(Y_j\),

\[
G_{d,L}(Y)
=\sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^i
\binom{d+i}{i}
\binom{d+L+1}{L-i}Y_{d+i}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The weights are primitive: their gcd is 1. This is the same primitive Newton/Lagrange functional used throughout the repository.

The exact adjacent-stencil identity is

\[
\boxed{
G_{d,L}(Y)-G_{d+1,L-1}(Y)
=(-1)^L\binom{d+L+1}{L}\,\Delta^L Y_d,
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

where

\[
\Delta^L Y_d
=\sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^{L-i}\binom LiY_{d+i}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

This follows directly from Pascal’s identity and is implemented, in the terminal specialization, in `q32_terminal_family_audit.py`.

For the present full stencil, \(d+L=D\), so the unavoidable adjacent-translation factor is

\[
\Pi_{n,q}=\binom{D+1}{L}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

For fixed \(q\),

\[
D=\frac nq+O(1),
\qquad
L=\frac{n}{q(q+1)}+O(1).
\]

Hence Stirling’s formula gives

\[
\boxed{
\log \Pi_{n,q}
=\frac nq H\!\left(\frac1{q+1}\right)+O_q(\log n)
=\Theta_q(n),
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

with \(H(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t)>0\).

Therefore **any proposed sublinear certificate that still contains the raw Pascal factor (3.4) has already failed**. The factor is exponentially large for fixed \(q\). One must divide all exact universal Newton/Pascal content before measuring primitive height.

This is precisely why the repository’s later cross-minor code divides the raw determinant by its exact binomial prefactor before inspecting the remaining minor.

At a safe candidate prime \(p\), the Pascal factor often contains exactly one copy of \(p\) regardless of targetness. Thus one cannot count that copy as arithmetic selectivity either.

# 4. What the exact Ore machinery really gives

The strongest exact low-degree object in the current repository is in `q32_doubled_period_gauge_audit.sage`.

There is an order-four integral Ore operator

\[
U=\sum_{j=0}^{4}u_j(n)S^j
\]

with

\[
\deg u_j=32,
\]

and a degree-30 polynomial \(M(n)\), all primitive after denominator/content removal, such that for the distinguished doubled-period sequence \(J_n\),

\[
\boxed{U(J)_n=M(n)b_n,}
\tag{4.1}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{U(b)_n=0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover \(U\) is exactly right-divisible by the Apéry recurrence operator \(\mathcal A\):

\[
\boxed{U=V\mathcal A}
\tag{4.3}
\]

for an order-two Ore quotient \(V\) over \(\mathbf Q(n)\). After denominator clearing, applying \(U\) to any sequence is therefore an explicit polynomial linear combination of three consecutive Apéry defects.

This is directly relevant to \(S=2\): after the correct index shift, one copy of (4.3) uses only

\[
R_0,R_1,R_2,
\]

and the reflected copy uses only

\[
R_0,R_{-1},R_{-2}.
\]

So the Ore construction is not “too wide” in the time direction. It is compatible with the given \(S=2\) data.

The follow-up `q32_operator_syzygy_audit.sage` proves an exact second syzygy. If \(\mathcal A_M\) is the Apéry operator twisted to annihilate \(M(n)b_n\), then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_M U=Q\,\operatorname{lclm}(P,\mathcal A),}
\tag{4.4}
\]

where the primitive quotient \(Q\) has order one. This is a genuine Ore identity, not a guessed recurrence.

## 4.1 Why this still does not give the desired \(B\)

When the corresponding transformed shell is pushed through the Newton selector, the repository proves the local congruence

\[
Z\equiv M(n)Y\pmod p
\tag{4.5}
\]

for **targets and non-targets alike** in the safe candidate range.

At the full-block level, the exact difference

\[
G(Z)-M(n)G(Y)
\tag{4.6}
\]

is divisible by the entire candidate-prime product. Thus the first transformed observable is universally aliased to the old one modulo candidate primes. It supplies no extra rank before one divides the universal candidate factor.

This is the same obstruction seen at first divided precision in `q32_fixed_minor_jet_audit.sage`: after dividing the Newton carrier by \(p\), one can obtain a nonzero \(2\times2\) determinant, but the exact Ore elimination identity has the form

\[
q_1\,\det(O_0,G_1)
=
\alpha_1\det(O_0,O_1)
+\alpha_2\det(O_0,O_2)
+\det(O_0,D)
-q_0\det(O_0,G_0),
\tag{4.7}
\]

with four other wedge terms. Thus the interesting divided determinant is not isolated by the original \(S=2\) minor ideal.

Finite nonvanishing of the left-hand determinant is therefore **not** a Bézout certificate for \(J\).

# 5. Exact homogeneous no-go: the summed terminal packet survives every toric IBP quotient

The cleanest rigorous negative result comes from `q32_terminal_ct_packet_audit.sage`.

In the first cell, define the terminal packet

\[
\Phi_{Q,L}(X)
=
\sum_{\kappa\in P\cap\mathbf Z^3}
X^{-Q\kappa}(1-X^\kappa)^L.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Its constant-term pairing with \(\Lambda^N\) is the terminal finite difference of the shell.

Restrict to the torus curve

\[
y=z=-1,
\qquad x=t.
\]

On this curve

\[
\Lambda=0
\]

and every logarithmic derivative \(\theta_i\Lambda\) also vanishes. Therefore restriction descends through the usual toric integration-by-parts/Jacobian quotient: any identity produced solely by adding multiples of \(\Lambda\) and its logarithmic derivatives must still hold after this restriction.

The exact restricted packet is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\Phi_{Q,L})={}&4(-1)^Q2^L\\
&+t^{Q-L}\bigl(5(t-1)^L+4(-1)^Q(t+1)^L\bigr)\\
&+2t^{-Q}\bigl((1-t)^L+(-1)^Q(1+t)^L\bigr).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The coefficient of the lowest positive power \(t^{Q-L}\) is

\[
\boxed{5(-1)^L+4(-1)^Q\in\{\pm1,\pm9\}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

It is never zero.

Hence

\[
\boxed{\Phi_{Q,L}\ne0}
\]

in every toric IBP quotient detected by this singular curve.

### Scoped no-go

This proves the following, and no more:

> A homogeneous fixed-width terminal-shell strategy that tries to annihilate the entire summed boundary packet using only toric integration-by-parts/logarithmic-derivative relations cannot work. The packet has an explicit surviving mode with unit coefficient.

This is a symbolic theorem, not finite evidence.

It does **not** rule out:

* an inhomogeneous identity with a source term;
* a rational gauge between ray modules;
* a bilinear/quadratic identity using the distinguished state \(\Lambda^M\);
* a two-sided determinant in which the surviving boundary mode cancels between observables.

Those are exactly the remaining escape classes.

# 6. Exact ray decomposition: why a literal common scalar telescoper is also blocked

`q32_first_cell_ray_telescopers.sage` decomposes the 21 nonzero first-cell lattice rays into 14 \(y/z\)-symmetry classes. Each ray coefficient

\[
c_M((M-r)\kappa)
\]

is one proper hypergeometric sum in the summation variable and has an exact creative-telescoping operator in \(r\) of order at most 3.

The total order bound obtained by summing the 14 orders is

\[
\boxed{38.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The same script proves that for every pair of distinct ray operators,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gcrd}(L_i,L_j)=1.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore no two ray classes share a literal nonzero scalar solution of a common right factor.

This rules out the simplest version of “all boundary rays secretly satisfy the same short recurrence.” It does **not** rule out a rational gauge or an intertwiner; indeed the doubled-period module elsewhere in the repository demonstrates that gauge equivalence can exist even when naive polynomial factor tests fail.

A particularly important surviving ray is \(\kappa=(0,0,1)\). At terminal residue zero its value is

\[
\sum_{k=0}^M\binom Mk^2\binom{M+k}{k},
\tag{6.3}
\]

the classical \(\zeta(2)\)-Apéry sequence. Thus the singular-curve obstruction is not just an abstract nuisance coordinate: it contains a distinguished holonomic boundary sequence with its own low-order arithmetic structure.

# 7. Directional cofactors: useful radical information, but not yet \(J\)-membership

`q32_terminal_directional_cofactor_audit.py` forms the actual common-convolution observability matrix from one shell coordinate \(Y\) and the transformed coordinate \(W\). For each choice of five rows of a six-column observability matrix it takes the six signed maximal cofactors and then forms directional combinations

\[
u_i c_j-u_j c_i.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The code verifies, for the audited rows,

\[
\operatorname{rad}(g_{\rm raw})\mid\operatorname{rad}(D_{\rm dir}).
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is exactly the right *shape*: if the observed \((Y,W)\) vectors have rank one, the fixed Ore coefficient vector must align with the cofactor kernel of the observability matrix, unless the matrix rank drops further.

But there are two gaps before this can answer the present question.

First, (7.2) is radical divisibility, not an explicit integral combination of the original six \(S=2\) generators.

Second, the terminal \((Y,W)\) raw minors are an enlarged shell ideal. One still needs an exact reduction of the directional determinants to a determinant of **common functionals of the two rows** \(u,v\), as in (2.1).

The tempting short bound in that script,

\[
D_{\rm dir}\mid C\prod_{j=2}^{7}(n+j)
\]

(or its raw-minor analogue), is explicitly labelled conjectural/regression-only in the current code. It must not be promoted to a theorem.

The related `q32_lower_terminal_observability.sage` has an exact polynomial Bézout constant for the pointwise gcd of the degree-32 origin coefficients and the degree-30 multiplier, because those polynomials have gcd 1 over \(\mathbf Q[n]\). But the assertion that all remaining observability content is supported only on the short boundary product is again checked only over a finite residue range. This is useful evidence for what the final theorem should look like, not the theorem itself.

# 8. Turán/Hankel minors do not yet escape the universal first divided digit

The terminal scalar family has exact normalized Turán determinants

\[
E_j=
\frac{F_{j-1}F_{j+1}-F_j^2}
{\gcd(P_j,P_{j-1})},
\tag{8.1}
\]

where \(P_j\) denotes the appropriate Pascal multiplier. `q32_terminal_turan_hankel_audit.py` verifies the exact adjacent elimination identity

\[
(a_1-a_2)D_0-(a_0-a_1)D_1
=a_1(a_0a_2-a_1^2),
\tag{8.2}

and the Desnanot--Jacobi relation for the first three Hankel levels.

More importantly, it derives the once-divided rank-one alias law at every safe candidate prime, including non-targets. In other words, after removing the universal single \(p\)-factor, the first local increments remain rank one for purely Newton/Pascal reasons.

Therefore ordinary adjacent Turán/Hankel formation cannot be advertised as “new target rank.” One must either pass to a genuinely new divided digit or insert an actual-shell source/intertwiner.

Again, finite gcd values in that script are diagnostics only.

# 9. A rigorous no-go for retaining raw full-stencil Pascal content

There is one height no-go that is unconditional and worth stating separately.

Suppose a proposed \(B_{n,q}\) is obtained from a bounded number of adjacent translations of the full Newton stencil, and after expansion every term still has a common factor containing a fixed positive power of

\[
\binom{D+1}{L}.
\]

Then for fixed \(q\), by (3.5),

\[
\log|B_{n,q}|\ge c_qn-O_q(\log n)
\]

unless the remaining factor is exactly zero and cancels the Pascal factor algebraically before evaluation.

Thus the raw translated-stencil class cannot yield a sublinear-height nonzero certificate. All such constructions must first be **primitive-saturated** with respect to the exact Pascal/Newton content.

I do **not** currently have a rigorous lower bound proving that every primitive fixed-width shell determinant still has exponential height. The repository has strong numerical evidence that many do, but that is not enough for publication. The scoped theorem is only the raw-Pascal statement above plus the symbolic homogeneous packet obstruction of Section 5.

# 10. The smallest exact escape I would pursue

The minimal plausible escape is not “take a larger resultant.” It is:

## 10.1 Build two common two-sided Ore observables

Use the exact quotient

\[
U=V\mathcal A
\]

and its reflected version to manufacture coefficient vectors

\[
\alpha(n,q),\beta(n,q)\in\mathbf Z[n,q]^4
\]

such that

\[
L_\alpha^+=\alpha\cdot(A_0,R_0,R_1,R_2),
\qquad
L_\alpha^-=\alpha\cdot(A_0,R_0,R_{-1},R_{-2}),
\tag{10.1}
\]

and similarly for \(\beta\).

Then the determinant

\[
\boxed{
B_{\alpha,\beta}
=L_\alpha^+L_\beta^-
-L_\beta^+L_\alpha^-
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

is automatically an explicit integer linear combination of the five nontrivial minors, with coefficients given by the \(2\times2\) minors of \((\alpha;\beta)\).

No ideal-membership argument remains: (10.2) is the Bézout combination.

## 10.2 Force an inhomogeneous boundary evaluation

The shell identity then has to evaluate the four observables in (10.2). A homogeneous evaluation is insufficient by Section 5. One needs an inhomogeneous source term which survives the singular-curve quotient but has controlled arithmetic.

The smallest natural source is a **single distinguished first-cell ray**, preferably the \((0,0,1)\) ray (6.3), because:

1. it is already present in the exact terminal packet;
2. it is P-recursive of fixed small order by creative telescoping;
3. it is not killed by the homogeneous toric quotient;
4. its recurrence is different from the \(\zeta(3)\)-Apéry recurrence, so a genuine two-module resultant/intertwiner is possible;
5. the relevant elimination can be done symbolically in \(\mathbf Z[n,q]\) before any exponentially large shell value is evaluated.

The target should be an identity of the schematic form

\[
L_\alpha^\pm
=p_\alpha(n,q)H_m+p_\alpha^{\partial}(n,q)H_{m+1},
\]

\[
L_\beta^\pm
=p_\beta(n,q)H_m+p_\beta^{\partial}(n,q)H_{m+1}
+s_\beta(n,q),
\tag{10.3}
\]

where \(H\) is one fixed boundary-ray solution and \(s_\beta\) is a polynomial (or bounded-height) inhomogeneous source. Then the determinant in (10.2) collapses to a fixed-order Casoratian/resultant expression. Only **after** primitive content is removed should its height be measured.

A scalar resultant is acceptable here because its degree would be bounded in terms of the fixed ray/Ore orders and fixed \(q\), rather than in terms of the full Newton length \(L=\Theta(n)\). If the coefficient polynomials in \(n\) have bounded degree and polynomial coefficient height, then the resultant has

\[
\log|B_{n,q}|=O_q(\log n),
\]

which would be more than enough.

This is the exact place where a resultant is potentially honest. A resultant of the raw full-shell values is not.

# 11. A second possible escape: nonlinear distinguished-state convolution

Outside the first cell, for fixed carrier quotient \(q\), the shell is a finite packet over

\[
qP\cap\mathbf Z^3,
\]

whose size depends only on \(q\). Linear packet identities alone still leave hidden-state freedom. The genuinely special fact is that the packet is not arbitrary: it is the coefficient packet of the **power** \(\Lambda^M\).

Thus the next-smallest nonlinear identity class is to use

\[
\Lambda^{M+1}=\Lambda\Lambda^M
\tag{11.1}
\]

at the packet level, together with the two-sided determinant wrapper (2.1). This is a convolution identity among the distinguished coefficients, not a homogeneous recurrence valid for arbitrary packet data.

A publication-grade implementation would:

1. choose the smallest finite packet closed under the needed \(\pm1\) node shifts and \(s=-2,\ldots,2\) moment shifts;
2. write the exact convolution matrices induced by multiplication by \(\Lambda\);
3. quotient by all known linear toric/PDE relations;
4. compute the saturation of the two-sided rank-one ideal by the primitive Pascal factors;
5. eliminate the packet variables symbolically over \(\mathbf Z[n,q]\).

If the elimination ideal meets \(\mathbf Z[n,q]\) nontrivially, its primitive generator is precisely the desired \(B_{n,q}\). If it does not, that is an exact no-go for that finite nonlinear packet class.

This is preferable to another finite gcd scan because it produces either a symbolic Bézout certificate or a symbolic positive-dimensional obstruction.

# 12. What can already be stated as theorems

The following statements are publication-safe now.

### Theorem A: six-generator normal form

For the stated \(u,v\),

\[
J=\gcd(a^2,ax,ay,r_0x,r_0y,r_1y-r_2x).
\]

Moreover \(c_+c_-\mid J\), and any nonzero integer \(B\) in this six-generator ideal bounds \(J/(c_+c_-)\) by \(|B|\).

### Theorem B: common-observable determinant

For all \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf Z^4\),

\[
(\alpha\cdot u)(\beta\cdot v)-(\beta\cdot u)(\alpha\cdot v)
\]

is the explicit linear combination (2.1) of the five nonzero minors.

### Theorem C: adjacent Newton identity and exponential Pascal content

Equation (3.2) holds for every integer sequence. For fixed \(q\), the Pascal factor has logarithm \(\Theta_q(n)\). Hence raw adjacent-stencil certificates retaining that factor cannot have sublinear logarithmic height.

### Theorem D: exact Ore source and exact Ore syzygy

The current repository certifies (4.1)--(4.4) with primitive polynomial data of bounded degree.

### Theorem E: homogeneous terminal-packet obstruction

The restriction (5.2) is exact and has nonzero coefficient (5.3). Therefore no identity generated solely by homogeneous toric IBP/logarithmic-derivative reductions can annihilate the summed terminal packet.

### Theorem F: first-cell ray holonomy

The 14 symmetry classes have exact scalar telescopers of order at most 3, total order bound 38, and pairwise unit GCRD.

None of Theorems A--F by itself supplies the desired \(B_{n,q}\).

# 13. The exact missing lemma

The shortest formulation of what remains is this.

> **Two-sided primitive observability lemma.** For each fixed \(q\), there exist two explicitly given vectors
> \[
> \alpha,\beta\in\mathbf Z[n]^4
> \]
> of degree bounded in terms of \(q\), and an explicitly given nonzero
> \[
> B_{n,q}\in\mathbf Z,
> \qquad \log|B_{n,q}|=O_q(\log n),
> \]
> such that for the actual Apéry Laurent/Newton shell
> \[
> (\alpha\cdot u)(\beta\cdot v)

# 15. Bottom line

The current actual-shell machinery does **not** yet justify a claim that

\[
\log\frac{J}{c_+c_-}=o(n)
\]

through an explicit six-generator Bézout certificate.

What is rigorously known is narrower but useful:

* the exact six-generator ideal has the simple normal form (1.1);
* common two-sided observables give automatic, explicit minor-ideal membership via (2.1);
* raw full-Newton adjacent elimination has exponential Pascal height and must be primitive-saturated;
* the bounded-degree Ore source exists and fits inside \(S=2\), but its first Newton image is universally aliased;
* homogeneous toric/IBP elimination of the boundary shell is impossible by the singular-curve certificate;
* the terminal boundary decomposes into finitely many exact low-order ray modules, so a **bounded-order inhomogeneous/gauge elimination** is still genuinely available.

The smallest credible escape is therefore a common two-sided Ore observable coupled to one distinguished boundary ray (or, failing that, one bilinear convolution identity for the distinguished state \(\Lambda^M\)). That is the next place where a polynomial-height \(B_{n,q}\) could honestly appear.
