# Q7209: resultant lower bounds from Apéry continuant root geometry

## Conclusion

The proposed Jacobi/interlacing route does not currently yield a rigorous lower bound of the form

\[
\exp(c sG\log(s+G))
\]

for the carrier-deflated resultant

\[
R_{s,G}=\operatorname{Res}_x(N_s(x),N_G(x+s)).
\]

The obstruction is not a missing estimate on individual root distances. The obstruction is that the relevant resultant is a determinant of two shifted continuant families whose common near-boundary geometry is not controlled by the real-root/interlacing information available from the tridiagonal representation. In particular, even if one had perfect information on the roots of each individual N_h, the shift x -> x+s couples two different Jacobi systems and allows near collisions at exactly the strip boundary where the lower bound would need uniform separation.

Therefore a root-strip argument can at most give a conditional analytic lower bound with an additional uniform non-collision hypothesis. It does not prove the far-bridge theorem and says nothing about which primes divide R_{s,G}.

## 1. Jacobi representation and what it controls

The continuant recurrence is

```
N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x).
```

After the standard normalization, this is a tridiagonal determinant. A Jacobi interpretation gives information of the following type:

* zeros of a fixed N_h are simple under the usual positivity hypotheses;
* zeros interlace between consecutive h-level continuants;
* roots lie in a controlled strip after the affine normalization.

These statements concern the polynomial N_h as a function of x.

The resultant requires instead comparison of

\[
N_s(x)
\quad\text{and}\quad
N_G(x+s).
\]

The second polynomial has its own root set shifted by -s. Interlacing gives no lower bound for

\[
|\alpha_i-(\beta_j-s)|
\]

where α_i is a root of N_s and β_j is a root of N_G.

## 2. Formal resultant factorization

If α_i are roots of N_s and β_j roots of N_G, then schematically

\[
\operatorname{Res}(N_s(x),N_G(x+s))
 =a_s^{G}a_G^{s}\prod_{i,j}(\alpha_i+s-\beta_j).
\]

A bound

\[
\log |R_{s,G}|\gg sG\log(s+G)
\]

requires a positive lower bound on the average logarithmic separation

\[
\sum_{i,j}\log|\alpha_i+s-\beta_j|.
\]

Interlacing only controls products inside one family, for example

\[
\prod_{i<j}|\alpha_i-\alpha_j|.
\]

It does not control the mixed Vandermonde product above.

## 3. Near-boundary obstruction

The precise obstruction is a possible aligned boundary root.

Suppose the normalized root locations have a limiting strip boundary ρ. It is compatible with interlacing that

\[
\alpha_i=\rho+\epsilon_s,
\qquad
\beta_j=\rho+s+\epsilon_G
\]

with ε_s and ε_G exponentially small or algebraically small in the continuant parameters. Then

\[
\alpha_i+s-eta_j=\epsilon_s-\epsilon_G
\]

can be tiny while each polynomial separately has perfectly separated simple roots.

Thus the missing statement is not root separation but shifted-family non-alignment.

A sufficient additional hypothesis would be:

```
There exists c>0 such that for every admissible s,G and every roots α of N_s,
β of N_G,

|α+s-β| >= exp(-O(log(s+G))).
```

or equivalently a mixed discriminant lower bound. No such theorem follows from the current Jacobi facts.

## 4. Leading coefficients and carrier removal

The integer resultant contains predictable factors from leading coefficients and normalization denominators. Removing these carriers gives a residual integer.

However, dividing by known content cannot improve the analytic root product. If the mixed product is small before carrier removal, the residual resultant may also be small.

Conversely, a large complex absolute value does not imply large support on primes p approximately X.

The implication

```
large bit length => many large prime divisors
```

is false. A number of size exp(H) may be composed entirely of primes below any chosen threshold.

## 5. Explicit method audit

A successful proof would require a new theorem of one of the following forms:

### Mixed-root separation theorem

For all admissible s,G,

\[
\prod_{i,j}|\alpha_i+s-\beta_j|\ge 
\exp(c sG\log(s+G)).
\]

This is essentially the desired resultant theorem and is not implied by interlacing.

### Arithmetic nonconcentration theorem

After carrier removal,

\[
\sum_{p\in(X,2X]}v_p(R_{s,G})
\]

would need direct control. This is independent of complex root geometry.

## 6. Prime-range warning

Even a hypothetical lower bound

\[
|R_{s,G}^{\mathrm{def}}|\ge e^{csG\log(s+G)}
\]

would only provide total height. It does not show whether the prime divisors lie below X, near X, or above X.

Therefore this route cannot replace the missing far-bridge argument.

## Final theorem-level statement

The available Jacobi/tridiagonal structure proves individual continuant regularity but does not prove a lower bound for the carrier-deflated shifted resultant. The exact missing ingredient is a uniform shifted-family non-concentration estimate preventing roots of N_s and the shifted N_G from approaching each other near the common strip boundary. Without that new theorem, the resultant method is only a height audit and cannot resolve the P32 prime-averaged far-bridge contribution.
