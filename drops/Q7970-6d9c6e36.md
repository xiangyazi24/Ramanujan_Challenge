# Cold audit of the finite-Palm expansion

## 1. Algebraic identities

Write
\[
\widetilde g_s=g_s+\varepsilon_s,
\qquad g_s=f_s-z_s,
\qquad \varepsilon_s=z_s-\widehat z_s.
\]
Hence
\[
g_p=\widetilde g_p-\varepsilon_p.
\]
For fixed p, with ordered pairs q,ell distinct and both different from p,
\[
E_p=\sum_m g_p\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}g_qg_\ell.
\]
Substitute:
\[
\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}g_qg_\ell
=\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}\widetilde g_q\widetilde g_\ell
-\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}(\varepsilon_q\widetilde g_\ell+\varepsilon_\ell\widetilde g_q)
+\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}\varepsilon_q\varepsilon_\ell.
\]
Because the pair sum is ordered, the two linear terms are equal after swapping q and ell. Therefore
\[
=-2\sum_{\ell\ne p}\widetilde g_\ell\sum_{q\ne p,\ell}\varepsilon_q
+\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}\varepsilon_q\varepsilon_\ell.
\]
Also
\[
\sum_m g_pF(m)=\sum_m(\widetilde g_p-\varepsilon_p)F(m).
\]
The first term is exactly the finite-Palm expression:
\[
\sum_m\widetilde g_pH_{-p}^{\sim}=A_pD_p^{fin}.
\]
The remaining terms are
\[
-\varepsilon_p\sum_mH_{-p}^{\sim}
-2\sum_{\ell\ne p}\left(\sum_m\widetilde g_p\widetilde g_\ell\right)\sum_{q\ne p,\ell}\varepsilon_q
+\sum_m\varepsilon_p\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}\varepsilon_q\varepsilon_\ell.
\]
Since the constants are independent of m, the last term is
\[
-M\varepsilon_p\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}\varepsilon_q\varepsilon_\ell.
\]
With \(C^{\sim}_{ab}=\sum_m\widetilde g_a\widetilde g_b\), this gives exactly
\[
R_p=-\varepsilon_p\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}C^{\sim}_{q\ell}
-2\sum_{\ell\ne p}C^{\sim}_{p\ell}\sum_{q\ne p,\ell}\varepsilon_q
-M\varepsilon_p\sum_{q\ne\ell\ne p}\varepsilon_q\varepsilon_\ell.
\]
The signs and ordered multiplicity are therefore correct. No factor 1/2 occurs. The excluded-index conditions are preserved in every sum.

## 2. Bound for R_p

We have
\[
|\varepsilon_s|=|A_s/M-z_s|=|A_s-Mz_s|/M.
\]
Using \(|A_s-Mz_s|\le Z(s)\),
\[
|\varepsilon_s|\le Z(s)/M.
\]
The first term satisfies
\[
|\varepsilon_p|\sum_{q\ne\ell}|C^{\sim}_{q\ell}|.
\]
The required HM2 input controls the total pair correlation by
\[
\sum_{q\ne\ell}|C^{\sim}_{q\ell}|\ll M\lambda^2
\]
(up to the same ordered convention). Hence
\[
\ll Z(p)M\lambda^2/M=Z(p)\lambda^2.
\]
Because \(Z(p)=pz_p\), this is
\[
\ll pz_p\lambda^2\asymp Xz_p\lambda^2.
\]
This is smaller than the claimed
\[
M z_p\lambda^2/X=Xz_p\lambda^2
\]
by the same scale.

For the second term, use
\[
|C^{\sim}_{p\ell}|\le J_{p\ell}\le Z(p)Z(\ell).
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{\ell\ne p}|C^{\sim}_{p\ell}|\sum_q|\varepsilon_q|
\le Z(p)\sum_\ell Z(\ell)\sum_q Z(q)/M.
\]
Now
\[
\sum_s Z(s)=X\lambda+o(X\lambda)
\]
with the prime weights suppressed only at this coarse stage, giving
\[
\ll Z(p)(X\lambda)^2/M
\ll Xz_p\lambda^2.
\]
The third term gives
\[
M|\varepsilon_p|\left(\sum_q|\varepsilon_q|\right)^2
\ll Z(p)\left((X\lambda)/M\right)^2M
\ll Xz_p\lambda^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
|R_p|\ll Xz_p\lambda^2
=\frac{M}{X}z_p\lambda^2.
\]
The claimed remainder bound is algebraically consistent.

## 3. Normalized Delta identity

Divide by \(Mz_p\):
\[
\Delta_p=\frac{E_p}{Mz_p}
=\frac{A_p}{Mz_p}D_p^{fin}+O(\lambda^2/X).
\]
Now
\[
A_p=Mz_p+O(Z(p))
=Mz_p+O(Xz_p),
\]
so
\[
\frac{A_p}{Mz_p}=1+O(X/M)=1+O(1/X).
\]

## 4. Missing dependence audit

The algebraic expansion has no hidden max-z dependence. The bound uses only:

1. \(|A_p-Mz_p|\le Z(p)\);
2. the HM2 ordered pair estimate;
3. \(J_{p\ell}\le Z(p)Z(\ell)\).

However, the conversion of sums of Z(p) into \(\lambda\) requires the weighted identity
\[
\sum_p Z(p)/p=\lambda,
\]
not merely \(\sum_pZ(p)\ll X\lambda\). Without the prime weights, an additional factor depending on \(\max_p Z(p)/p=\max z_p\) can appear.

Thus the exact expansion is correct, and the normalized statement follows under the stated HM2 normalization. The only possible missing dependence is in replacing unweighted row-size sums by \(\lambda\); that step requires explicit use of \(p\asymp X\) and does not introduce a small-\(\lambda\) loss.
