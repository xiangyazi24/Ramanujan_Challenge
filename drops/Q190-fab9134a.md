# Bounded Critical Points — The Key Computation

## Summary

The bounded-critical-point observation is very interesting, but the proposed Bezout argument does not work for the Apéry recurrence. The main obstruction is that the recurrence orbit modulo p is not an algebraic curve of fixed degree in (n,b_n,b_{n-1}); it is a length-p orbit of a non-autonomous polynomial cocycle. Its elimination degree grows with p. Therefore a fixed Bezout bound cannot be obtained this way.

A proof of O(1) critical points would require a new rigidity theorem for this cocycle, most plausibly via the underlying elliptic/Hasse invariant interpretation rather than elementary recurrence algebra.

## 1. Critical points and the projective orbit

The Apéry recurrence is

(n+1)^3 b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1},

with

P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.

The condition

b_{n+1}=b_n

is equivalent to

((n+1)^3-P(n))b_n=-n^3b_{n-1}.

Since

P(n)-(n+1)^3=33n^3+48n^2+24n+4,

we obtain

(33n^3+48n^2+24n+4)b_n=n^3b_{n-1}.

Equivalently the projective ratio satisfies

b_n/b_{n-1}=n^3/(33n^3+48n^2+24n+4).

Thus a critical point is a hitting condition for the projective cocycle.

## 2. Can the cocycle hitting [1:1] happen only O(1) times?

For a fixed prime p, define

v_n=[b_n:b_{n-1}] in P^1(F_p).

The transfer is

v_{n+1}=T_n(v_n),

where T_n depends polynomially on n.

The problem is that this is not iteration of one fixed map. For a fixed rational map T, one can sometimes use dynamical arguments. Here we have a sequence

T_0,T_1,...,T_{p-1}.

The orbit can revisit special points many times unless one proves a strong non-concentration theorem.

The statement that the diagonal is hit only 1,3,5 times is therefore a genuine arithmetic cancellation phenomenon, not a formal consequence of degree.

## 3. The ratio argument

The heuristic is correct:

b_n/b_{n-1}

looks pseudorandom in F_p, while the target value

R(n)=n^3/(33n^3+48n^2+24n+4)

is a deterministic rational function.

A random map from F_p to F_p would intersect R(n) about once. However, proving this requires showing that the projective cocycle has sufficient mixing/equidistribution.

Possible tools:

- monodromy of the associated rank-2 sheaf;
- Deligne bounds for trace functions;
- expansion of the transfer matrices in SL_2(F_p).

A recurrence-only proof is not currently available.

## 4. Why the Bezout idea fails

The proposed algebraic curve argument would require an algebraic variety C of bounded degree such that

(n,b_n,b_{n-1}) in C

for all n.

For a constant-coefficient recurrence this exists. For example

u_n=c_1 alpha_1^n+c_2 alpha_2^n

lies on a fixed algebraic torus orbit.

The Apéry recurrence is different. The matrices are

T_n = [[P(n)/(n+1)^3,-n^3/(n+1)^3],[1,0]].

The product

T_{n-1}...T_0

has entries whose algebraic degree grows with n. Eliminating n from the equations defining the orbit produces degrees depending on p, not a fixed degree.

Hence there is no fixed Bezout number.

## 5. What would a successful Bezout replacement look like?

One would need to identify an algebraic surface of bounded complexity carrying the reduction modulo p of the Apéry cocycle. The natural candidate is not the recurrence orbit itself but the geometric object behind it:

- the Beukers-Peters K3 family;
- the associated elliptic/Kummer surface;
- the Hasse invariant polynomial.

Then the critical condition could become an intersection condition between algebraic cycles of bounded degree.

This is much closer to a sheaf-theoretic stationary phase argument.

## 6. Consequence for stationary phase

If one proves

# {j : b(j+1)-b(j)=0} <= C

uniformly in p, then the stationary phase formula for the exponential sum

S_c=sum_j psi(c b_j)

would indeed give

|S_c| <= C' sqrt(p).

Combined with the Mellin representation this would imply strong bounds on the zero set Z_p.

However, the missing theorem is precisely the bounded critical-point theorem.

## Conclusion

1. The critical equation is correctly a codimension-one condition on the projective ratio.

2. The random-ratio heuristic predicts O(1) solutions.

3. The Apéry recurrence alone does not provide a fixed algebraic curve, so a Bezout-degree argument does not work.

4. The likely route is geometric: interpret the projective cocycle through the underlying elliptic/K3 family and prove bounded ramification/critical behavior of the corresponding trace function.

5. A proof of bounded critical points would be a major breakthrough: it would upgrade the current |Z_p|=O(p^{2/3}) type estimates to |Z_p|=O(1), directly attacking the cross-prime independence problem.
