# Q7694 — scale-sensitive cross-row identity for transverse Apéry content

## Result

I found an exact Apéry-specific cross-row identity that genuinely fixes the row scale:

\[
\boxed{\Xi_r=r^3\bigl(b_{r-1}\kappa_r-b_r\kappa_{r-1}\bigr).}
\tag{1}
\]

It is obtained by inverting the **unit-Casoratian normalized** Apéry frame, so it is not another projective reformulation of the pair minors.  It gives an exact target-preserving integer carrier at every prime \(p>r\): if \(\kappa_r=K_r/d_r\) is reduced, then on the actual transverse condition \(p\mid b_r\),

\[
\boxed{v_p\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)=v_p\gcd(b_r,K_r).}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, for \(r\in(R,2R]\),

\[
\boxed{
[C_R]_{p>2R}
=\prod_{R<r\le2R}[\gcd(b_r,K_r)]_{p>2R}.}
\tag{3}
\]

This closes the *independent row-scaling* objection.  It does **not** close the height theorem.  The natural primitive-numerator carrier \(K_r\) has provably quadratic unsaturated block height, and after maximal legitimate removal of every prime \(q\le2R\), the exact finite audit still shows essentially the same quadratic height.  At \(R=256\),

\[
H_K/R^2=9.703487841,\qquad
H_K^{\rm sat}/R^2=9.702098228.
\]

Thus I do not claim a height saving.  The smallest surviving identity class is the adjacent unit-Casoratian identity together with the banded source recurrence; any further algebraic compression must couple many rows **before** multiplying one exponential integer per row, e.g. through a genuinely multirow Fitting/resultant determinant.  A modular/Hecke route also remains logically distinct.

## 1. Unit-Casoratian normalization

Write

\[
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]

Define the canonical second homogeneous Apéry solution by

\[
u_0=0,\qquad u_1=1,
\]

\[
(n+1)^3u_{n+1}=P(n)u_n-n^3u_{n-1}.
\tag{4}
\]

Let

\[
W_r=b_{r-1}u_r-b_ru_{r-1}.
\]

Using the two homogeneous recurrences at index \(r-1\),

\[
r^3W_r=(r-1)^3W_{r-1}.
\]

Since \(W_1=b_0u_1-b_1u_0=1\),

\[
\boxed{r^3(b_{r-1}u_r-b_ru_{r-1})=1.}
\tag{5}
\]

This is the scale-sensitive ingredient: the second solution is normalized by \(u_0=0,u_1=1\), and the Casoratian is literally the unit \(1/r^3\).  There is no independent row rescaling left.

## 2. Invert the exact \(\kappa\)-frame across adjacent rows

Define

\[
\Phi_0=0,\qquad
\Phi_r=5\sum_{m=1}^r g_m u_{m-1},
\tag{6}
\]

and use the exact frame

\[
\kappa_r=\Xi_ru_r+\Phi_rb_r.
\tag{7}
\]

The prefix definitions imply

\[
\Xi_r-\Xi_{r-1}=-5g_rb_{r-1},
\qquad
\Phi_r-\Phi_{r-1}=5g_ru_{r-1}.
\tag{8}
\]

The increments cancel when the new coordinates are evaluated on the previous homogeneous row:

\[
\Xi_ru_{r-1}+\Phi_rb_{r-1}
=\Xi_{r-1}u_{r-1}+\Phi_{r-1}b_{r-1}
=\kappa_{r-1}.
\tag{9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\binom{\kappa_r}{\kappa_{r-1}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
u_r&b_r\\
u_{r-1}&b_{r-1}
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{\Xi_r}{\Phi_r}.
\tag{10}
\]

The determinant of the matrix is \(1/r^3\) by (5).  Exact inversion gives

\[
\boxed{\Xi_r=r^3(b_{r-1}\kappa_r-b_r\kappa_{r-1}),}
\tag{11}
\]

and simultaneously

\[
\boxed{\Phi_r=r^3(u_r\kappa_{r-1}-u_{r-1}\kappa_r).}
\tag{12}
\]

Equation (11) is the new cross-row identity requested in the question.

## 3. The exact source recurrence and its real boundary defect

Subtract (11) at consecutive rows.  For \(r\ge2\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Xi_r-\Xi_{r-1}
={}&r^3(b_{r-1}\kappa_r-b_r\kappa_{r-1})\\
&-(r-1)^3(b_{r-2}\kappa_{r-1}-b_{r-1}\kappa_{r-2}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Using

\[
r^3b_r+(r-1)^3b_{r-2}=P(r-1)b_{r-1},
\]

we obtain

\[
\Xi_r-\Xi_{r-1}
=b_{r-1}\left(
 r^3\kappa_r-P(r-1)\kappa_{r-1}+(r-1)^3\kappa_{r-2}
\right).
\]

Since \(\Xi_r-\Xi_{r-1}=-5g_rb_{r-1}\),

\[
\boxed{
A_r\kappa:=r^3\kappa_r-P(r-1)\kappa_{r-1}+(r-1)^3\kappa_{r-2}
=-5g_r,\quad r\ge2.}
\tag{13}
\]

At \(r=1\), however,

\[
A_1\kappa=\kappa_1-P(0)\kappa_0=-36,
\]

while \(-5g_1=-35\).  The correct uniform form is

\[
\boxed{A_r\kappa=-5g_r-\mathbf1_{r=1}.}
\tag{14}
\]

The verifier explicitly checks this defect and rejects the false boundary equation \(A_1\kappa=-5g_1\).

## 4. Summation by parts / Green identity

For \(2\le a\le s\), multiply (13) by \(b_{m-1}\) and sum:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m=a}^s b_{m-1}A_m\kappa
=-5\sum_{m=a}^s g_mb_{m-1}
=\Xi_s-\Xi_{a-1}.}
\tag{15}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Xi_s-\Xi_{a-1}
={}&s^3(b_{s-1}\kappa_s-b_s\kappa_{s-1})\\
&-(a-1)^3(b_{a-2}\kappa_{a-1}-b_{a-1}\kappa_{a-2}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

This is a genuine exact interval identity, and the verifier checks it over many intervals.  It is **not** a second height improvement: (15)-(16) are the telescoped form of the same adjacent determinant identity plus the Apéry recurrence.  I therefore do not count them twice.

## 5. Denominator saturation and exact target preservation

Write \(\kappa_r=K_r/d_r\) in lowest terms, \(d_r>0\).  From (13), starting at \(\kappa_0=0,\kappa_1=-36\), induction gives:

> Every prime divisor of \(d_r\) is at most \(r\).

The only new denominator introduced at row \(r\) comes from division by \(r^3\); all previous denominator primes are \(\le r-1\).  The verifier checks reduced denominator support exactly through row 520.

Now take a prime \(p>r\) and impose the actual target condition \(p\mid b_r\).  Reducing (5) in \(\mathbf Z_{(p)}\) gives

\[
r^3b_{r-1}u_r\equiv1\pmod p,
\]

so \(r^3b_{r-1}\) is a \(p\)-adic unit.  Rewriting (11),

\[
\Xi_r=(r^3b_{r-1})\kappa_r-b_r(r^3\kappa_{r-1}).
\tag{17}
\]

All denominators are \(p\)-units.  Hence

\[
\min(v_p(b_r),v_p(\Xi_r))
=
\min(v_p(b_r),v_p(\kappa_r))
=
\min(v_p(b_r),v_p(K_r)).
\tag{18}
\]

This proves (2)-(3), including prime powers.

This argument is deliberately conditioned on \(p\mid b_r\).  It does **not** turn BFH into a theorem about every prime divisor of \(b_r\), nor does it turn BFH's row-aggregate information into a pointwise statement.

## 6. Maximal small-prime saturation before any height claim

Since only primes \(p>2R\) matter, define

\[
\operatorname{sat}_{>2R}(n)
=\frac{|n|}{\prod_{q\le2R}q^{v_q(n)}}.
\tag{19}
\]

This removes the complete \(q\)-primary content for **every** prime \(q\le2R\), not a selected list of convenient factors.  Because \(K_r/d_r\) is reduced and all denominator primes are \(\le r\le2R\), this saturation leaves the right side of (3) unchanged.

## 7. Rigorous no-go for the unsaturated primitive-numerator carrier

Let

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2,\qquad \rho=\alpha^{-1}=17-12\sqrt2.
\]

The standard Apéry Poincare asymptotic gives

\[
b_r=c_b\alpha^r r^{-3/2}(1+O(r^{-1})),\qquad c_b>0.
\tag{20}
\]

Thus \(F(\rho)\) is finite and positive.  Since

\[
D(t)=(1-\alpha t)(1-\alpha^{-1}t)
\]

has a simple zero at \(t=\rho\), the leading singularity of

\[
g(t)=\frac1{F(t)^2\sqrt{D(t)}}
\]

is square-root inverse, and standard singularity transfer yields

\[
g_r=c_g\alpha^r r^{-1/2}(1+o(1)),\qquad c_g>0.
\tag{21}
\]

From (13), for large \(r\),

\[
5g_r
\le
\bigl(r^3+P(r-1)+(r-1)^3\bigr)
\max(|\kappa_r|,|\kappa_{r-1}|,|\kappa_{r-2}|).
\]

The coefficient is \(O(r^3)\), so

\[
\max(|\kappa_r|,|\kappa_{r-1}|,|\kappa_{r-2}|)
\ge c\alpha^r r^{-7/2}.
\tag{22}
\]

Take \(r=R+3,R+6,\ldots\le2R\).  These three-row windows are disjoint and lie inside \((R,2R]\).  Each contributes one index \(j\) with

\[
\log\max(1,|\kappa_j|)
\ge r\log\alpha-O(\log R).
\]

Since \(K_j=d_j\kappa_j\) and \(d_j\ge1\), \(|K_j|\ge|\kappa_j|\).  Summing over \(\asymp R\) disjoint windows gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{R<j\le2R}\log\max(1,|K_j|)=\Omega(R^2).}
\tag{23}
\]

So the **raw primitive numerator carrier cannot have subquadratic block height**.  This is the precise no-go theorem that survives.

It does not imply the same lower bound after removal of all primes \(\le2R\), because a large integer may be very smooth.  That is why the saturated table was computed separately and why I do not call the finite saturation behavior a theorem.

## 8. What remains viable

The useful exact identity class is now reduced to:

\[
\Xi_r=r^3(b_{r-1}\kappa_r-b_r\kappa_{r-1}),
\]

\[
A_r\kappa=-5g_r\quad(r\ge2),
\]

and their interval Green identity (15)-(16).

These identities genuinely remove independent row scaling and give the exact high-prime transverse carrier.  What they do **not** do is make a product of one primitive integer per row cheap.

Therefore any further algebraic attack has to compress many rows before paying their height: a banded resultant/Fitting determinant is the natural surviving class.  The test for such an object is strict: after primitive denominator clearing and complete small-prime saturation, its block height must actually be \(o(R^2)\), while preserving every singleton target prime.  Merely rewriting (11), (13), or (15) in matrix form is not a saving.

A modular/eta/Hecke identity also remains outside this no-go, because it could couple the normalized \(K_r\) residues globally in \(r\) rather than multiply their Archimedean sizes rowwise.

## 9. BFH scope

No part of the derivation uses BFH to control all primes with \(p\mid b_r\).  The exact equivalence is only

\[
p\mid b_r,\Xi_r
\Longleftrightarrow
p\mid b_r,K_r
\qquad(p>r),
\]

with equality of gcd valuations.  Any later BFH input must still be applied only to the simultaneous transverse target and only in its row-aggregate form.  No pointwise BFH statement is inferred here.

## Bottom line

Equation (11) is the actual Apéry-specific scale-sensitive cross-row identity.  It fixes the projective scaling defect and gives an exact high-prime integer carrier.  After the denominator and content audit, however, that carrier does not yield a subquadratic rowwise height bound: raw quadratic height is proved, and maximal small-prime saturation remains essentially quadratic in the exact tested blocks.  The next step must therefore be genuinely multirow (Fitting/resultant) or genuinely modular/Hecke; multiplying the normalized \(K_r\) row by row is not an improvement.
