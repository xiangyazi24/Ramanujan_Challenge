# Exact hypergeometric reflection-orbit benchmark

## 1. Correct random model

For an odd prime p, let the admissible reflection orbits be
\[
\mathcal O_p=\{\{r,p-1-r\}\},
\]
with the fixed point removed. Thus |\mathcal O_p|=(p-1)/2 (or the corresponding reduced size if endpoint exclusions are imposed). Let k_p be the observed number of chosen orbits, and choose a subset S_p of \mathcal O_p uniformly among all k_p-subsets.

For m modulo p put
\[
f_p(m)=1_{m\bmod p\in S_p},\qquad \pi_p=\frac{k_p}{|\mathcal O_p|}\frac{2}{p}\,|\mathcal O_p|?\]

More simply, because each selected orbit contributes two residues,
\[
\pi_p=\frac{2k_p}{p-1}.
\]
The centered variable is
\[
g_p(m)=f_p(m)-\pi_p.
\]
This differs from the earlier s_p/p centering by O(1/p), and that difference matters at the exact variance level.

## 2. Hypergeometric covariance

Let N_p=|\mathcal O_p| and let A_r be the orbit indicator. For distinct orbits i,j,
\[
E(A_iA_j)=\frac{k_p(k_p-1)}{N_p(N_p-1)},
\]
while
\[
E(A_i)=\frac{k_p}{N_p}.
\]
Hence the orbit covariance is
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_i,A_j)= -\frac{k_p(N_p-k_p)}{N_p^2(N_p-1)}
\]
for i\ne j, and
\[
\operatorname{Var}(A_i)=\frac{k_p(N_p-k_p)}{N_p^2}.
\]

If m and m' lie in the same reflection orbit modulo p, the covariance is the diagonal value. If they lie in different orbits, it is the negative hypergeometric covariance above. Therefore
\[
\Gamma_p(m,m'):=E(g_p(m)g_p(m'))
\]
is completely determined by whether
\[
m'\equiv m\pmod p\quad\text{or}\quad m'\equiv -1-m\pmod p
\]
and otherwise.

Explicitly, away from the endpoint exceptions,
\[
\Gamma_p(m,m')=
\begin{cases}
\pi_p(1-\pi_p),&m'\equiv m\text{ or }-1-m,\\[3pt]
-\pi_p(1-\pi_p)/(N_p-1),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

## 3. Which triple covariances survive

Write
\[
C=\sum_{(p,q,l)}\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)g_q(m)g_l(m),\qquad M=X^2.
\]
Prime choices are independent. For two ordered triples
\((p,q,l)\) and \((p',q',l')\),
\[
E(T_{pql}T_{p'q'l'})
\]
vanishes unless every prime occurring in one triple occurs in the other with the same multiplicity. Since each triple has three distinct primes, the only surviving cases are permutations of the same unordered triple.

There are no "one-prime overlap" or "two-prime overlap" terms: the remaining single prime factor has mean zero and kills the covariance.

Thus the variance is
\[
\operatorname{Var}(C)=6\sum_{p<q<l}V_{pql},
\]
where
\[
V_{pql}=\sum_{m,m'<M}\Gamma_p(m,m')\Gamma_q(m,m')\Gamma_l(m,m').
\]

## 4. Evaluation of one unordered triple

The diagonal/reflection conditions are congruence conditions modulo p,q,l. By CRT, for M much larger than pql the number of simultaneous solutions factors. Here M=X^2 while pql\asymp X^3, so the incomplete CRT regime is essential.

The unrestricted complete-box estimate gives
\[
\sum_{m,m'<M}\prod_{r\in\{p,q,l\}}\Gamma_r(m,m')
\asymp M^2\prod_r\frac{\pi_r(1-\pi_r)}{r}
\]
up to lower-order terms from the negative covariance background.

Because M<X^3, only O(M^2/(pql)+M) CRT classes occur. The boundary contribution is bounded by
\[
O\left(M\prod_r\pi_r(1-\pi_r)\right)
\]
and is not negligible pointwise, but it is still smaller after summing over triples.

For \pi_p\asymp \lambda, the main diagonal scale is
\[
V_{pql}\ll X^4\lambda^3/X^3=X\lambda^3.
\]
Summing over O(X^3) unordered triples gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(C)\ll X^4\lambda^3,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{sd}(C)\ll X^2\lambda^{3/2}.}
\]

The normalized random scales are therefore
\[
\frac{C}{X^2\lambda^3}=O_{prob}(\lambda^{-3/2}),
\]
which is not a bounded constant prediction, and
\[
\frac{C}{X^3\lambda^3}=O_{prob}(X^{-1}\lambda^{-3/2}).
\]
For \lambda\sim1/\log X this gives respectively
\[
(\log X)^{3/2},\qquad \frac{(\log X)^{3/2}}X.
\]

## 5. Comparison with observed HM3 ratios

The observed values
\[
0.176154,-0.252615,-0.196256,-0.084574
\]
on the X^2\lambda^3 normalization are not inconsistent with this benchmark: the benchmark standard deviation grows slowly like (log X)^{3/2}, so constants of size 0.1 are expected.

They are far larger than the X^3\lambda^3 normalization, whose predicted random size tends to zero. Therefore the observed normalization is the only plausible random scale.

The interval truncation M=X^2 does not by itself create an extra factor X: it changes the CRT density and produces boundary terms, but the exact variance calculation still has size X^4\lambda^3, not X^6\lambda^6.

## 6. Consequence

The earlier variance claim giving standard deviation X\lambda^{3/2} was based on an incorrect normalization of the CRT diagonal count. The exact hypergeometric model gives
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{sd}(C)\asymp X^2\lambda^{3/2}}
\]
(up to logarithmic and endpoint corrections).

This benchmark does not prove HM3. It only shows that the observed centered cubic correlation is of the natural random magnitude once the reflection-orbit sampling and incomplete CRT boundary are treated exactly.
