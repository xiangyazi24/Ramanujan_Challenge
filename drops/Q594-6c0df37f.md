# Q594: First-break Hasse scalar computation

## Executive summary

The Hasse-scalar reformulation is the right geometric direction, but the identification

$$H_{p,r}=0 \iff b_r\equiv 0\pmod p$$

is not automatic. The Mellin coefficient $b_r$ is a trace/Fourier coefficient, while a first-break Hasse scalar is a determinant/minor of a Frobenius operator on a filtered crystal. They agree only after proving that the chosen Mellin piece is rank one at the first break and that the Frobenius trace is exactly its unit-root contribution.

The likely outcome is:

$$Z_p\subseteq E_p^{sl}$$

but equality requires removing possible cancellation from higher-slope pieces.

---

## (A) Explicit formula for $H_{p,r}$

For the Apéry motive, the correct object is the Kummer-twisted crystalline cohomology

$$
H^2_{cris}(X/\mathbf Z_p)\otimes \mathcal L_r.
$$

After decomposition into tame characters, the Frobenius matrix has the shape

$$
\Phi_{p,r}\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
 p^{\lambda_0}u_0 & * & *\\
0&p^{\lambda_1}u_1&*\\
0&0&p^{\lambda_2}u_2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The first-break Hasse scalar is the reduction of the leading Frobenius coefficient:

$$
H_{p,r}=\overline{u_0}
$$

when the first Newton slope is equal to the first Hodge slope (ordinary case). Thus

$$
H_{p,r}=0
$$
means that the expected unit-root contribution disappears and the Newton polygon jumps upward.

However, $b_r$ is a Mellin trace:

$$
b_r=-\sum_t \theta_p(t)t^r.
$$

Therefore one needs a comparison theorem identifying this Mellin trace with the first Frobenius matrix coefficient. This is a nontrivial crystalline period calculation.

---

## (B) Computing Newton polygons for small primes

For a fixed $p$ and tame character $r$, the computational route is:

1. Compute the Apéry Hasse--Witt matrix $HW(t)$.
2. Apply the multiplicative Mellin projector

$$
HW_r=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^*}\chi_r(t)HW(t).
$$

3. Compute the rank and elementary divisors of the Frobenius matrix.
4. Extract Newton slopes from the valuations of Frobenius eigenvalues.

The criterion is:

- ordinary first break: first slope $=0$;
- nonordinary first break: first slope $>0$.

A small-prime computation is useful, but it cannot by itself prove HTO. The required theorem is a uniform bound:

$$
\sum_{p\le X}|E_p^{sl}|\ll \pi(X).
$$

## (C) Mellin transform of the Hasse--Witt matrix

Yes, the Kummer twist should be obtained by Mellin projection.

If

$$
H(t)=\left(h_{ij}(t)\right),
$$
then the $r$-th twist is

$$
H_r=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^*}\chi_r(t)H(t).
$$

This is the matrix analogue of the scalar identity

$$
b_r=-\sum_t\theta_p(t)t^r.
$$

Thus the zero set $Z_p$ is expected to be contained in the set of twists where the first Hasse invariant vanishes.

The missing step is proving that the relevant matrix entry is not cancelled by the other Frobenius components.

---

## (D) Relation between $E_p^{sl}$ and $Z_p$

The expected picture is:

$$
Z_p \subseteq E_p^{sl}
$$

because a vanishing Mellin coefficient indicates loss of the first unit-root contribution.

But the converse may fail:

$$
H_{p,r}=0
$$
can happen even when the trace is nonzero, because trace is a sum of eigenvalue contributions while ordinarity is a statement about slopes.

Therefore the most realistic computational test is:

1. compute $Z_p$ directly from Apéry recurrence;
2. compute $E_p^{sl}$ from Mellin Hasse--Witt matrices;
3. compare the two distributions.

If

$$
|E_p^{sl}|-|Z_p|=O(1),
$$
then HTO is essentially equivalent to AFP.

---

## Main obstruction

The difficult theorem is not the explicit Hasse--Witt calculation. It is the global average estimate:

$$
\sum_{p\le X}|E_p^{sl}|\ll \pi(X).
$$

This is a horizontal ordinarity problem for a family of Kummer twists. Existing generic ordinarity results do not immediately apply because the twists vary with $p$ and the Apéry family is a fixed arithmetic motive rather than a generic family.

---

## Conclusion

The first-break Hasse scalar route is promising:

- the Mellin transform of the Hasse--Witt matrix gives an explicit computational model;
- small-prime Newton polygon calculations are feasible;
- $Z_p$ should correspond to a subset of first-break nonordinary twists.

The missing ingredient is a theorem of horizontal tame ordinarity for the Kummer-twisted Apéry crystal. This would provide a geometric replacement for Apéry Fourier pseudorandomness and imply the M1 bound.
