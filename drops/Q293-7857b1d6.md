# P3.2 first-zero distribution and the Gallagher short-arc hypothesis

## Referee verdict

There are three separate assertions here, and they must not be conflated.

1. **Prime-aspect distribution of the first coefficient zero.** No theorem currently available in the repository or in the cited Apéry/CFVZ literature proves that
   \[
   \frac{2r_p}{p-1}
   \]
   is equidistributed on \([0,1]\), even after restricting to the doublet primes \(|\mathcal Z_p|=2\). There is no known Sato--Tate law for this statistic. The uniform law is a well-supported **reflected-random-model conjecture**, not a consequence of Picard--Fuchs rank, Lucas congruences, or CFVZ.

2. **The CFVZ polynomial has the wrong zero locus.** If
   \[
   A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j
          =\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
   \qquad \Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1,
   \]
   then roots of \(B_p\) are **evaluation roots in the variable \(t\)**. The Apéry zero positions
   \[
   \mathcal Z_p=\{j:b_j\equiv0\pmod p\}
   \]
   are **coefficient, equivalently finite-Mellin, zeros**. Thus the statement “zero positions are roots of \(B_p\)” is false. Even a complete theorem about the roots of \(B_p\) would not determine \(r_p\).

3. **Gallagher/AMTD requires substantially more than equidistribution.** Qualitative equidistribution of \(h_p/p\), or even a power-saving marginal discrepancy estimate at fixed frequencies, does not imply the short-arc bound. The exact off-diagonal term is a two-prime, moving-frequency bilinear form over near-Farey pairs \(a/p\approx b/q\). What is needed is a quantitative **joint dispersion/large-sieve estimate**, such as the repository's condition (SDC), uniformly at the shrinking scale \(1/N\).

The empirical evidence is nevertheless quite strong: in the audited sample \(p\le 20000\), the 695 genuine doublets pass a uniform-on-the-half-interval diagnostic, and the small-modulus tests show no detectable dependence. This is evidence for the conjectural random model, not a proof of the Gallagher estimate.

# 1. Precise notation and conditioning

Let
\[
\mathcal Z_p=\{0\le j<p:b_j\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]
Reflection gives
\[
b_{p-1-j}\equiv b_j\pmod p.
\]
Thus every noncentral zero occurs in a reflected pair
\[
\{r,p-1-r\},\qquad 1\le r<\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
and the only possible unpaired zero is the center \((p-1)/2\).

The statistic
\[
r_p=\min\mathcal Z_p
\]
needs conditioning:

- it is undefined when \(\mathcal Z_p=\varnothing\);
- it determines the complete noncentral zero set only when \(|\mathcal Z_p|=2\);
- for \(|\mathcal Z_p|\ge4\), it is merely the outermost reflected pair.

For a genuine doublet define
\[
U_p:=\frac{2r_p}{p-1}\in(0,1),
\qquad
H_p:=\frac{h_p}{p-1}
     =\frac{p-1-2r_p}{p-1}=1-U_p.
\]
Replacing \(p-1\) by \(p\) changes either normalized statistic by \(O(1/p)\), so it is irrelevant for weak convergence, although the exact Fourier phase uses denominator \(p\).

The clean equidistribution conjecture is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{\#\mathcal D_2(X)}
\sum_{\substack{p\le X\\|\mathcal Z_p|=2}}
\varphi(U_p)
\longrightarrow
\int_0^1\varphi(u)\,du
}
\tag{ED2}
\]
for every continuous \(\varphi\), where
\[
\mathcal D_2(X)=\{p\le X:|\mathcal Z_p|=2\}.
\]
Equivalently, \(r_p/p\) would be uniform on \([0,1/2]\), and \(h_p/p\) would be uniform on \([0,1]\), along the doublet primes.

No theorem in the current project establishes (ED2).

# 2. Question 1: what is known as \(p\) varies?

## 2.1 There is presently no proved equidistribution theorem

A targeted check of the standard Apéry congruence literature and the current CFVZ paper found no published theorem proving any of the following:

- positive density, or an asymptotic density, for the active primes \(\mathcal Z_p\ne\varnothing\);
- positive density, or an asymptotic density, for the doublet primes \(|\mathcal Z_p|=2\);
- equidistribution of \(r_p/p\) or \(h_p/p\);
- a discrepancy estimate for these normalized positions;
- a Sato--Tate-type limiting law for the first zero.

The repository correctly treats the coefficient-zero law as conjectural. See in particular:

- [`problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex), especially “The two zero loci are in dual spaces” and “The moving-target quantifier trap”;
- [`problems/3.2/oracleB_exploration.md`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/oracleB_exploration.md);
- [`problems/3.2/hasse_franel_descent.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/hasse_franel_descent.tex).

This is a statement about the present literature search, not a proof that no formulation exists under different terminology. But no result cited by the project supplies the required horizontal prime-aspect law.

## 2.2 Why this is not presently a Sato--Tate problem

A classical Sato--Tate theorem concerns Frobenius conjugacy classes in a fixed compatible family and the distribution of a normalized trace or characteristic polynomial. The statistic \(r_p\) is instead:

1. extracted from all \(p\) defining-characteristic coefficients \(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1}\);
2. defined by the nonlinear event \(b_j=0\);
3. then subjected to the nonlinear order statistic “take the first such \(j\).”

No fixed-rank compatible \(\ell\)-adic representation is known whose Frobenius trace is \(r_p/p\). Consequently, “Sato--Tate law for \(r_p/p\)” is presently an analogy, not an available theorem schema.

There is a modular-form statement at the moving center:
\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f)\pmod p
\]
for the relevant weight-four form. But the event \(p\mid a_p(f)\) is a shrinking, same-characteristic divisibility condition. Ordinary Sato--Tate for normalized real/complex traces does not decide it. The repository emphasizes this point in `oracleB_result.tex`.

The natural conjectural law here is better described as a **uniform reflected-orbit/order-statistic law**, not a Sato--Tate law.

## 2.3 What fixed-gap Chebotarev does prove, and why it does not answer this question

For a fixed integer \(h\), the recurrence gives a fixed gap polynomial \(N_h(r)\in\mathbb Z[r]\) such that, away from the boundary,
\[
b_r\equiv b_{r+h}\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
For those fixed \(h\) for which the splitting field of \(N_h\) has the required full hyperoctahedral Galois group, Chebotarev gives a prime-aspect law for the number of roots of \(N_h\bmod p\). This is a genuine theorem in the fixed-polynomial regime.

It does not prove a law for \(r_p\) or \(h_p\), for three independent reasons.

1. **Necessary is not sufficient.** A root of \(N_h\) is a candidate projective return; it need not be an actual pair of Apéry coefficient zeros.
2. **The actual gap moves.** For a doublet, \(h=h_p\) can be of order \(p\). Thus \(N_{h_p}\), its degree, discriminant, splitting field, and effective Chebotarev constants all vary with the same prime being counted.
3. **The quantifiers cannot be interchanged.** Fixed-\(h\) Chebotarev has the form “for each fixed \(h\), let \(X\to\infty\).” The desired theorem requires estimates uniform for \(h\asymp p\) inside a dyadic prime block.

Hence the fixed-gap theorem is structural evidence, not a route to (ED2) without a new uniform-in-\(h\) theorem.

## 2.4 The CFVZ factorization does not locate coefficient zeros

Let
\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j.
\]
The CFVZ theorem states that, according to \(p\bmod24\),
\[
A_p(t)=B_p(t)^2
\quad\text{or}\quad
A_p(t)=(t^2-34t+1)B_p(t)^2.
\]
See Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin,
[“Galois Groups of Apéry-like Series Modulo Primes”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972725100932), also [arXiv:2510.23298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23298).

But
\[
B_p(t_0)=0
\]
means that \(t_0\) is an **evaluation root**. In contrast,
\[
r\in\mathcal Z_p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[t^r]A_p(t)=b_r=0
\]
is a **coefficient condition**.

For \(1\le r\le p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality gives the exact finite-Mellin formula
\[
\boxed{
 b_r=-\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-r}.
}
\tag{Mellin}
\]
Thus the zero position is determined by cancellation among all values
\[
A_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
\]
not by the vanishing set of \(B_p\).

The separation is already visible at \(p=7\):
\[
A_7(t)=1+5t+3t^2+3t^3+3t^4+5t^5+t^6
      =(t-1)^2(t^2+1)^2\pmod7.
\]
No coefficient is zero, so \(\mathcal Z_7=\varnothing\), while \(A_7\) and its square root have the evaluation root \(t=1\).

Therefore the premise

> “The CFVZ factorization means zero positions are roots of \(B_p\)”

must be removed from `proof.tex` or any AMTD argument.

## 2.5 Can the roots of \(B_p\) themselves be analyzed?

They can be studied as a separate problem, but standard fixed-complexity results do not immediately apply:
\[
\deg B_p=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon_p}{2}=\Theta(p).
\]
The polynomial changes with \(p\), in characteristic \(p\), and its degree grows linearly. It is not the reduction of a fixed bounded-degree divisor on the \(t\)-line. If one manufactures the curve \(y^2=B_p(t)\), its genus is \(\Theta(p)\), so a naive Weil bound has a constant growing with \(p\).

The repository's exact computation for all 2260 primes \(5\le p\le20000\) found that the tested \(B_p\) were squarefree and that every irreducible factor had degree one or two; equivalently, in that finite range, all roots lie in \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\). CFVZ Theorem 1.2 itself does not state squarefreeness or this factor-degree pattern, so these remain finite-range observations unless supplied with an independent proof.

Most importantly, even a theorem giving the complete root distribution of \(B_p\) would not give the distribution of \(r_p\). Formula (Mellin) requires the full value function \(A_p(t)\), not merely its root divisor.

# 3. The conjectural reflected-random law

The empirical law becomes clearer when one models reflected **pairs**, rather than individual zeros.

Let \(K_p\) be the number of noncentral reflected zero pairs. The repository data fit
\[
K_p\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(1/2),
\]
with the pair locations conditionally independent and uniform in the lower half. This predicts:

- \(|\mathcal Z_p|=2K_p\), apart from rare center zeros;
- mean zero count approximately \(1\);
- on the doublet sector \(K_p=1\),
  \[
  U_p=\frac{2r_p}{p-1}\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1];
  \]
- on the full active sector \(K_p\ge1\), \(U_p\) is the minimum of \(K_p\) uniform points and is therefore mildly biased toward zero.

The phrase “\(|\mathcal Z_p|\) is Poisson(1)” is only a rough mean-level description. Because of reflection, the literal distribution is concentrated on even integers. The sharper model is
\[
\boxed{K_p\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(1/2),\qquad |\mathcal Z_p|\approx2K_p.}
\]

This distinction matters for interpreting the first zero.

## 3.1 Exact order-statistic prediction for all active primes

Take a Poisson point process of intensity \(\lambda=1/2\) on \([0,1]\), and condition on at least one point. If \(U\) is the first point, then
\[
\Pr(U\le u\mid K\ge1)
 =\frac{1-e^{-\lambda u}}{1-e^{-\lambda}},
\qquad 0\le u\le1.
\]
Its mean is
\[
\mathbb E U
 =\frac1\lambda-\frac{e^{-\lambda}}{1-e^{-\lambda}}
 =0.458505\ldots
\quad(\lambda=1/2),
\]
and hence
\[
\mathbb E(1-U)=0.541495\ldots.
\]
The median is \(U\approx0.4381\), corresponding to
\[
r_p/p\approx0.2191.
\]

This explains an important empirical point: if the reported “mean depth/d” \(0.55\) is the normalized outer-gap/depth variable \(H_p\), then it is almost exactly the reflected Poisson order-statistic prediction \(0.5415\). It is not evidence for edge concentration. On genuine doublets, where \(K_p=1\), the predicted mean is exactly \(1/2\).

# 5. Question 3: qualitative equidistribution does not imply Gallagher

## 5.1 The short arcs are arcs in \(a/p\), not in \(h_p/p\)

This is the first conceptual correction.

For a dyadic block \(P<p\le2P\), define
\[
c_{p,a}=\frac{(\log p)F_p(a)}p,
\qquad
F_p(a)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal Z_p}e_p(ar),
\]
and
\[
A_{P,M}(x)=
\sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P,\ 1\le a<p\\
                 a/p\in(x,x+1/M]_{\mathbb T}}}
 c_{p,a}.
\]
The short-arc target is
\[
\boxed{
\int_{\mathbb T}|A_{P,M}(x)|^2\,dx
 \ll M^{-1+o(1)}Q_P,
}
\tag{SA}
\]
where
\[
Q_P=\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}|c_{p,a}|^2.
\]

The points lying in the short arcs are the Farey points \(a/p\). The gap \(h_p\) appears in the **weight**. On a genuine doublet,
\[
\boxed{
F_p(a)=2e_p\!\left(\frac{a(p-1)}2\right)
       \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi a h_p}{p}\right).
}
\tag{Doublet}
\]

Therefore equidistribution of the one point \(h_p/p\) per prime is not literally equidistribution of the frequencies occurring in Gallagher's integral.

## 5.2 What qualitative equidistribution actually supplies

Qualitative equidistribution of \(H_p=h_p/(p-1)\) says that for every fixed continuous test function \(\varphi\),
\[
\frac1{\#\mathcal D_2(X)}
\sum_{\substack{p\le X\\|\mathcal Z_p|=2}}
\varphi(H_p)
\longrightarrow\int_0^1\varphi(u)\,du.
\]
By Weyl's criterion it is equivalent to cancellation for every **fixed** integer \(m\ne0\):
\[
\sum_{\substack{p\le X\\|\mathcal Z_p|=2}}
e(mH_p)
=o(\#\mathcal D_2(X)).
\]

In (SA), however:

- \(a\) ranges up to \(p-1\);
- the relevant \(a\)'s depend on the short arc \(x\);
- after expanding the square, \(a\) and \(b\) are coupled by
  \[
  \left\|\frac ap-\frac bq\right\|<\frac1M;
  \]
- in reciprocal coordinates, the phases involve moving residues of the form \(kq^{-1}\pmod p\) and \(kp^{-1}\pmod q\).

Fixed-frequency Weyl cancellation says nothing uniform about these moving frequencies.

## 5.3 A rate of marginal equidistribution is necessary but still not sufficient

Suppose one only has a discrepancy estimate
\[
D(X)=\sup_{I\subset[0,1]}
\left|
\#\{p\le X:H_p\in I\}-|I|\#\mathcal D_2(X)
\right|
=o(\#\mathcal D_2(X)).
\]
This controls fixed arcs. An arc of length \(1/M\) has expected population roughly
\[
\frac{\#\mathcal D_2(X)}M.
\]
To obtain relative equidistribution on every such shrinking arc one would need, at minimum,
\[
D(X)=o\!\left(\frac{\#\mathcal D_2(X)}M\right),
\]
which is far stronger than qualitative equidistribution. In the critical regime \(M\asymp P\) and \(\#\mathcal D_2(P,2P]\asymp P/\log P\), the expected number in a single \(1/P\)-arc is below one, so pointwise relative discrepancy is not even the natural formulation. An \(L^2\) large-sieve statement is the correct object.

More importantly, even an excellent one-dimensional discrepancy bound for \(H_p\) does not control correlations between two different primes. The expanded short-arc integral is a second-moment statement. Marginal uniformity does not imply pairwise independence.

A simple general obstruction is that one may alter an equidistributed family on an exceptional set of \(L(X)=o(\#\mathcal D_2(X))\) primes. Qualitative equidistribution is unchanged, but if those \(L(X)\) primes are arranged to have coherent short-arc phases, their off-diagonal contribution is quadratic, of order \(L(X)^2\), rather than linear. The repository's reflected anchored constructions make this obstruction concrete.

Thus the answer is not merely “one needs some rate.” One needs a rate with:

1. uniformity at frequencies growing with the dyadic scale;
2. uniformity under the reciprocal substitutions produced by near-Farey pairs;
3. genuine two-prime decorrelation.

## 5.4 The exact missing bilinear estimate

Expanding (SA) gives the triangular kernel
\[
K_M(t)=\left(M^{-1}-\|t\|\right)_+.
\]
After separating equal primes, the unequal-prime term is
\[
\boxed{
R_{\ne}(P,M)=
\sum_{\substack{P<p,q\le2P\\p\ne q}}
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\sum_{b=1}^{q-1}
K_M\!\left(\frac ap-\frac bq\right)
\frac{(\log p)(\log q)}{pq}
F_p(a)\overline{F_q(b)}.
}
\tag{Cross}
\]
The same-prime terms are bounded by \(O(Q_P/M)\). The precise missing estimate isolated in
[`problems/3.2/oracleA_result.tex`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/oracleA_result.tex)
and
[`problems/3.2/ORACLE_COMM/codex_result_001.md`](https://github.com/xiangyazi24/Ramanujan_Challenge/blob/main/problems/3.2/ORACLE_COMM/codex_result_001.md)
is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}R_{\ne}(P,M)
 \ll M^{-1+o(1)}Q_P.
}
\tag{SDC}
\]

For doublets, inserting (Doublet) shows exactly what must cancel: products
\[
\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi a h_p}{p}\right)
\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi b h_q}{q}\right)
\]
over all near-Farey quadruples \((p,q,a,b)\). This is a two-prime bilinear statistic. The marginal distribution of \(h_p/p\) is only one projection of it.

Accordingly, the logically correct implication is
\[
\boxed{\text{(SDC)}\Longrightarrow\text{short-arc bound},}
\]
not
\[
\text{qualitative equidistribution of }h_p/p
\Longrightarrow\text{short-arc bound}.
\]

## 5.5 Gallagher dilation and the shell \((N,2N]\)

There is also a technical correction already proved in `oracleA_result.tex`. The Fejér coefficient at scale \(N\) satisfies
\[
\gamma_N(N)=\gamma_N(2N)=0,
\]
so the short-arc estimate at scale exactly \(N\) does not by itself control every integer frequency in the shell \(N<k\le2N\).

The valid reduction applies the hypothesis at scale
\[
M=4N.
\]
Then \(\gamma_{4N}(k)\gg N^{-2}\) throughout \(N<k\le2N\), giving
\[
\sum_{N<k\le2N}|D_P(k)|^2
 \ll N^{1+o(1)}Q_P.
\]
Any referee-level use of equation (5.52) should include this dilation, or explicitly assume uniformity in the scale parameter.

## 5.6 Short-arc dispersion versus literal AMTD

The Fourier identity centers each prime by the vertical mean \(Z(p)/p\):
\[
D_P(k)=\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\left(
\mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_p}(k\bmod p)-\frac{Z(p)}p
\right).
\]
Literal shell AMTD centers by the empirical shell mean \(A_p/N\), where clipping can remove residues when \(p>N\). These are not identical quantities.

The short-arc estimate yields a weighted dispersion bound with majorant \(NQ_P\), which the repository proves is already sufficient for the all-index conclusion. It should not be described as “literally AMTD block by block” without this centering qualification.

# 6. What hypotheses would actually suffice?

There are several valid levels of formulation.

## 6.1 Exact analytic hypothesis

Use (SDC) exactly as stated above, uniformly for
\[
\sqrt M<P\le M.
\]
This is the cleanest hypothesis because it is precisely the off-diagonal term left after the exact short-arc identity.

## 6.2 Exact incidence hypothesis

Let \(I_N=(N,2N]\),
\[
X_p(m)=\mathbf1_{\{m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\}},
\qquad
A_p=\sum_{m\in I_N}X_p(m),
\]
and
\[
S=\sum_pA_p,
\qquad
F^{\mathrm{ord}}=
\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{m\in I_N}X_p(m)X_q(m).
\]
The exact no-excess fiber-square condition
\[
F^{\mathrm{ord}}
\le
\frac{S^2-\sum_pA_p^2}{N}
+N^{o(1)}S
\]
implies the centered AMTD variance bound. This is the combinatorial equivalent of saying that cross-prime hit fibers have no exceptional quadratic concentration.

## 6.3 A genuinely strong gap-distribution hypothesis

A theorem about \(h_p\) could imply (SDC), but it would have to be much stronger than (ED2). It would need a uniform bilinear statement for the moving phases generated by all near-Farey pairs. Schematically, it must control
\[
\sum_{p\ne q}
\sum_{|aq-bp|<pq/M}
W_{p,q,a,b}
\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi a h_p}{p}\right)
\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi b h_q}{q}\right)
\]
with square-root/large-sieve cancellation for the relevant weights \(W\).

Calling such a theorem “quantitative equidistribution” is possible, but only if the statement explicitly includes moving frequencies and two-prime correlations. Ordinary discrepancy of \(h_p/p\) does not.

```tex
\begin{conjecture}[Doublet first-zero equidistribution]
Let
\[
\mathcal D_2(X)=\{p\le X:|\mathcal Z_p|=2\}.
\]
For \(p\in\mathcal D_2(X)\), write
\(\mathcal Z_p=\{r_p,p-1-r_p\}\) with
\(r_p<(p-1)/2\). Then, for every continuous
\(\varphi:[0,1]\to\mathbb C\),
\[
\frac1{|\mathcal D_2(X)|}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal D_2(X)}
\varphi\!\left(\frac{2r_p}{p-1}\right)
\longrightarrow\int_0^1\varphi(u)\,du,
\]
provided \(|\mathcal D_2(X)|\to\infty\).
\end{conjecture}
```

This accurately records the empirical first-zero claim. It should not be called Sato--Tate unless an actual Frobenius/monodromy object producing this statistic is constructed.

# 8. Direct answers

## Answer to Question 1

- No theorem is presently known here proving that \(r_p/p\) is equidistributed in \([0,1/2]\), even on the doublet primes.
- There is no established Sato--Tate law for \(r_p/p\). The natural proposed law is uniform and comes from the reflected random-pair model.
- Fixed-gap Chebotarev results do not apply uniformly to the moving first gap \(h_p\asymp p\).
- The roots of \(B_p\) are not the zero positions. Root-distribution results for \(B_p\) would concern a separate evaluation-variable problem and would not, by themselves, control \(r_p\).

## Answer to Question 2

- In the audited range \(p\le20000\), 695 genuine doublets were found.
- Their normalized first positions \(2r_p/(p-1)\) have KS distance \(0.03894\) from uniform, with no statistically visible edge concentration.
- All-active first zeros show the mild left bias expected from taking the minimum when several reflected pairs occur.
- A normalized outer-depth mean near \(0.55\) is almost exactly the reflected-Poisson conditional prediction \(0.5415\); it is not evidence of boundary localization.
- Fixed-modulus and finite short-arc diagnostics show no detectable dependence, but supply no asymptotic rate.

## Answer to Question 3

No. Qualitative equidistribution does not automatically imply equation (5.52). A quantitative rate is necessary, but a marginal rate alone is still insufficient. The required input is a uniform, two-prime, moving-frequency dispersion estimate—most cleanly (SDC)—or an equivalent no-excess fiber-square estimate. The Gallagher step should also use the dilation \(M=4N\) when converting the short-arc integral to the shell \((N,2N]\).

# Bottom line

The empirical first-zero law is a credible and useful conjecture:
\[
\frac{2r_p}{p-1}\mid(|\mathcal Z_p|=2)
\quad\text{looks uniform on }[0,1].
\]
It is not presently a theorem, it is not obtained from the roots of \(B_p\), and it is much weaker than the AMTD/Gallagher input. The correct remaining target is not merely “prove equidistribution of \(r_p/p\),” but rather:
\[
\boxed{
\text{prove uniform two-prime Mellin/Farey dispersion for the doublet phases.}
}
