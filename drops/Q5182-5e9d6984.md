## 2. Claim (1): the actual zero-chain involution

Define

\[
\boxed{\iota_{p,a,b}(u)=p-1-(u+2a+b).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The source proves the actual Apéry palindrome

\[
b_j\equiv b_{p-1-j}\pmod p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For completeness, this can be checked directly from the binomial formula. For \(0\le k\le p-1-r\),

\[
\binom{p-1-r}{k}\equiv(-1)^k\binom{r+k}{k},
\]

\[
\binom{p-1-r+k}{k}\equiv(-1)^k\binom rk
\pmod p.
\]

Terms with \(k>r\) vanish in the second identity, and the missing terms of \(b_r\) with \(k>p-1-r\) have \(p\mid\binom{r+k}{k}\). Squaring proves (2.2).

Reflecting the four labels gives, in increasing order,

\[
\iota(u),\quad
\iota(u)+a,\quad
\iota(u)+a+b,\quad
\iota(u)+2a+b,
\tag{2.3}
\]

because these are respectively

\[
p-1-(u+2a+b),\quad
p-1-(u+a+b),\quad
p-1-(u+a),\quad
p-1-u.
\]

The original nonwrapping bounds imply

\[
0\le\iota(u),\qquad \iota(u)+2a+b\le p-1.
\]

Thus (2.1) maps the actual nonwrapping zero-chain variety for fixed \((p,a,b)\) to itself. Moreover,

\[
\iota(\iota(u))=u.
\]

The fixed-point equation is

\[
2u+2a+b=p-1,
\tag{2.4}
\]

so there is at most one fixed point for each \((p,a,b)\); it can occur only when \(b\) is even.

## 4. Exact fourth-zero equations

Put

\[
F_a(X)=N_a(X),
\]

\[
G_{a,b}(X)=N_b(X+a),
\]

\[
J_{a,b}(X)=N_a(X+a+b).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The exact nonwrapping return criterion gives

\[
\boxed{
F_a(u)=G_{a,b}(u)=J_{a,b}(u)=0\pmod p.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Conversely, if \(u\in\mathcal Z_p\), the displayed interval is nonwrapping, and (4.2) holds, then all four displayed labels are actual zeros.

There is also a same-column formulation. With \(h=a+b\), the return levels from \(u\) are

\[
a,\qquad h,\qquad a+h,
\]

so

\[
N_a(u)=N_h(u)=N_{a+h}(u)=0\pmod p.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The canonical Bézout identity in `proof.tex:700-758` recovers

\[
N_b(u+a)=0
\]

from the pair of levels \(a<h\), and

\[
N_a(u+h)=0
\]

from \(h<a+h\), because all cut-edge factors are units on the displayed nonwrapping interval.

The fourth zero is therefore an exact three-return or Schur-triple condition, not merely another copy of the two-return energy.

However, this fact alone does not improve the exponent. For an arbitrary level set \(A\subset[1,H]\), the number of pairs \((a,h)\) with

\[
a,h,a+h\in A
\]

can be of order \(|A|^2\), the same order as the pair factorial moment. For example \(A=\{2,4,\ldots,2m\}\) has \(\asymp m^2\) such triples. This is only a scoped combinatorial no-go, not an Apéry counterexample.

## 5. Claim (3): two common roots and \(p^2\) in each resultant

The polynomial reflection identity proved in `meso_result.tex:25-82` is

\[
N_d(-X-d-1)=(-1)^{d-1}N_d(X).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Modulo \(p\), equation (2.1) is

\[
\iota(u)\equiv-u-2a-b-1.
\]

Applying (5.1) to the three factors in (4.1) gives

\[
F_a(\iota(u))=(-1)^{a-1}J_{a,b}(u),
\]

\[
G_{a,b}(\iota(u))=(-1)^{b-1}G_{a,b}(u),
\]

\[
J_{a,b}(\iota(u))=(-1)^{a-1}F_a(u).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence \(u\) and \(\iota(u)\) are common roots of both pairs

\[
(F_a,G_{a,b}),
\qquad
(F_a,J_{a,b}).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The reflected **selected masks** are irrelevant here. Equations (5.2) use only the distinguished-\(p\) zero predicates and the exact polynomial reflection identity.

### 5.1 Nonvanishing over \(\mathbb Z\)

Let

\[
\mathcal R_{a,b}
=\operatorname{Res}_X(F_a,G_{a,b}),
\]

\[
\mathcal S_{a,b}
=\operatorname{Res}_X(F_a,J_{a,b}).
\tag{5.4}
\]

The complex roots of \(F_a\) lie in

\[
-a<\Re X<-1.
\]

The roots of \(G_{a,b}\) lie in

\[
-a-b<\Re X<-a-1,
\]

and the roots of \(J_{a,b}\) lie in

\[
-2a-b<\Re X<-a-b-1.
\]

These strips are pairwise disjoint in the two resultant pairs, so

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{a,b}\ne0,\qquad
\mathcal S_{a,b}\ne0.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is valid for every \(a,b\ge2\); no \(H\le\sqrt p\) hypothesis is used.

### 5.2 Exact-degree nullity audit

Let \(A\in M_n(\mathbb Z)\) be either integer Sylvester matrix. If a finite root \(z\in\mathbb F_p\) is common to the two reduced polynomials, the evaluation row

\[
(1,z,z^2,\ldots,z^{n-1})
\]

annihilates the reduced Sylvester matrix on the left. Distinct roots give linearly independent rows by the \(2\times2\) Vandermonde determinant.

Therefore, whenever

\[
\iota(u)\ne u,
\]

the reduction of each Sylvester matrix has nullity at least two over \(\mathbb F_p\). Smith normal form over \(\mathbb Z\) then gives

\[
\boxed{
p^2\mid\mathcal R_{a,b},
\qquad
p^2\mid\mathcal S_{a,b}.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is unaffected by vanishing leading coefficients modulo \(p\): the matrices are the integer Sylvester matrices at their exact characteristic-zero degrees, and a degree drop can only add kernel vectors. This is the same valid mechanism used in `near_bridge_energy.tex:65-125`.

Full support-disjointness implies \(\iota(u)\ne u\), so the Q5155 claim follows. In fact support-disjointness is stronger than necessary: every nonfixed chain, including a partially overlapping one, satisfies (5.6).

At the unique possible fixed chain only one distinct root has been proved, so (5.6) must not be asserted there without an independent multiplicity argument.

**Verdict on (3): true, and slightly stronger than stated.**

## 6. Claim (4): the pencil content — first actual proof repair

Define

\[
\mathscr R_{a,b}(Z)
=\operatorname{Res}_X\bigl(F_a(X),G_{a,b}(X)+ZJ_{a,b}(X)\bigr)
\in\mathbb Z[Z].
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
m=\deg F_a=3(a-1),
\qquad
n=\deg G_{a,b}=3(b-1),
\qquad
q=\max(m,n),
\]

and let \(\ell_a>0\) be the leading coefficient of \(N_a\).

### 6.1 The false equality in the Q5155 proof

When \(a>b\), the generic polynomial \(G+ZJ\) has \(X\)-degree \(m\), but its specialization at \(Z=0\) has degree \(n<m\). Therefore the standard resultant specialization is not literally

\[
\mathscr R_{a,b}(0)=\mathcal R_{a,b}.
\]

The correct root-product calculation is

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr R_{a,b}(0)
=\ell_a^{\,q-n}\mathcal R_{a,b}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the factor is \(1\) when \(a\le b\), and is

\[
\ell_a^{3(a-b)}
\]

when \(a>b\).

A small independently checkable certificate for this specialization issue is

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X(2X^2+1,\,1+ZX^2)=(2-Z)^2,
\]

whereas

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X(2X^2+1,1)=1.
\]

The constant coefficient is \(4\), not \(1\), because the generic second polynomial has degree \(2\) and its \(Z=0\) specialization has degree \(0\).

Equation (6.2), together with (5.5), still proves

\[
\boxed{\mathscr R_{a,b}(Z)\ne0.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

So the Q5155 conclusion survives, but its displayed justification requires this repair. This is the first genuine algebraic defect in that proof.

### 6.2 Content divisibility and the full multiplicity statement

Let

\[
\mathcal C_{a,b}
=\operatorname{cont}_Z\mathscr R_{a,b}(Z)>0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Put

\[
\tau_{a,b}(p)
=\#\{z\in\mathbb F_p:
F_a(z)=G_{a,b}(z)=J_{a,b}(z)=0\}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Over the residue field \(\mathbb F_p(Z)\), every root counted by (6.5) is a common root of

\[
F_a(X),\qquad G_{a,b}(X)+ZJ_{a,b}(X).
\]

Hence the generic Sylvester matrix has residue-field corank at least \(	au_{a,b}(p)\).

Localize \(\mathbb Z[Z]\) at the height-one prime ideal \((p)\). This localization is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer \(p\) and residue field \(\mathbb F_p(Z)\). Smith normal form over that DVR gives

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{a,b}(p)
\le v_p(\mathcal C_{a,b}).}
\tag{6.6}
\]

In particular every four-zero chain gives

\[
p\mid\mathcal C_{a,b},
\tag{6.7}
\]

and every nonfixed reflected pair gives

\[
\boxed{p^2\mid\mathcal C_{a,b}.}
\tag{6.8}
\]

This coefficientwise \(p^2\) statement is stronger than Q5155. It can also be seen directly for corank two. Modulo \(p\), every \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) minor of the pencil Sylvester matrix vanishes, so its adjugate is \(pB(Z)\). Write the determinant as \(pQ(Z)\). The identity

\[
A(Z)B(Z)=Q(Z)I
\]

then shows \(Q\equiv0\pmod p\), because the left side has rank at most \(n-2\) over \(\mathbb F_p(Z)\), while a nonzero scalar matrix has full rank.

### 6.3 Why this still does not save a power

The standard determinant-height calculation gives, for \(a+b\asymp T\), a per-pair bound of the generic form

\[
\log\|\mathscr R_{a,b}\|_1\ll T^2\log(2T).
\]

There are \(O(T^2)\) ordered pairs in the dyadic region \(T\le a+b<2T\). Thus direct height summation has \(T^{4+o(1)}\) capacity. The extra factor \(p\), or even the full valuation inequality (6.6), is an **absolute** divisor carrier.

The selected Palm measure may concentrate on one distinguished characteristic. There is no legal implication from an absolute prime-divisor count to

\[
\sum_xw_x(\cdots)\ll T^{-\delta}M_0
\]

without a selected Carleson or weighted content theorem. In particular one may not divide an absolute resultant estimate by the unknown \(M_0\).

**Verdict on (4): conclusion true; proof repaired by (6.2), and strengthened by (6.6).**

## 7. Claim (5): the two reciprocal divisors coincide identically

Index the four actual moving primes by the equal-gap-square order

\[
\ell_1\leftrightarrow u,\qquad
\ell_2\leftrightarrow u+h,\qquad
\ell_3\leftrightarrow u+a,\qquad
\ell_4\leftrightarrow u+a+h.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The literal reciprocal fibres satisfy

\[
\ell_i r_i\equiv C_x\pmod p,
\qquad C_x\ne0.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The two equal-\(h\) pairs give

\[
r_2-r_1=r_4-r_3,
\]

and therefore

\[
D_h
=(\ell_1-\ell_2)\ell_3\ell_4
 -(\ell_3-\ell_4)\ell_1\ell_2
\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The two equal-\(a\) cross-pairs give

\[
r_3-r_1=r_4-r_2,
\]

and therefore

\[
D_a
=(\ell_1-\ell_3)\ell_2\ell_4
 -(\ell_2-\ell_4)\ell_1\ell_3
\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{7.4}
\]

But expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_h
&=\ell_1\ell_3\ell_4-\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4
  -\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3+\ell_1\ell_2\ell_4,\\
D_a
&=\ell_1\ell_2\ell_4-\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4
  -\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3+\ell_1\ell_3\ell_4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{D_h=D_a}
\tag{7.5}
\]

as an identity in \(\mathbb Z[\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3,\ell_4]\).

The negative signed orientation changes the order of conjugation in \(\Gamma_x^-\), but not the physical label assignment (7.1) or the reciprocal congruence (7.2). Therefore it gives the same divisor.

The reflected labels do not automatically possess literal moving-prime lifts satisfying the selected masks, so reflection supplies no further reciprocal divisor.
