# Q8493 — TGD3 mod-43 logic audit

## 1. Basic implication from an integral certificate to reduction modulo 43

**Classification: proved theorem.**

Let Mx=b be an integer system with coefficients in R = Z[1/q : q<=32]. Every denominator in R is a 43-unit, so the natural reduction map

\[
R \to \mathbf F_{43}
\]

is defined. If x exists over R with Mx=b, reducing every coordinate gives

\[
\bar M\bar x=\bar b
\]

in F_43. Hence an actual Laurent certificate over R implies solvability of the literal mod-43 system.

No converse follows from this argument.

## 2. Landing obstruction: exact conditional statement

Let the literal system be Lx=b and let the Saito landing construction be represented by maps

\[
L \xrightarrow{\pi} S,
\]

where S is the landing coefficient space. A mod-43 landing rank gap obstructs the literal certificate only if the following diagram is an actual commutative diagram over F_43:

```
      literal solutions
          Mx=b
             |
             | pi
             v
      landing solutions
 I f + Σ A_k Phi_k = t
```

The required identity is:

\[
\pi(Mx)=N(\pi(x)),\qquad \pi(b)=t,
\]

or equivalently, at the matrix level, there must exist a matrix P with

\[
PM=NQ,\qquad Pb=t.
\]

Only then does

\[
Mx=b \implies NQx=t
\]

and non-membership of t in the landing image contradict the literal certificate.

**Classification: conditional implication.**

The numerical statement

\[
\operatorname{rank}(A)=42366,
\quad
\operatorname{rank}([A|t])=42367
\]

is an exact finite fact about the stated landing matrix. It does not by itself prove that the landing matrix is a quotient/image of the literal Laurent matrix.

## 3. Smallest counterexample to the invalid converse

The converse

"solvable modulo 43 implies solvable over R"

is false.

Smallest integer matrix example:

\[
M=[43],\qquad b=[1].
\]

The equation

\[
43x=1
\]

has no solution over Z (and no solution over R if 43 is not inverted). But modulo 43 it becomes

\[
0\cdot x=1,
\]

which is also not solvable, so use the minimal example detecting the opposite direction:

\[
M=[43],\qquad b=[43].
\]

Over R it has the solution x=1. This does not give a failure. For failure of the converse with local solvability, take

\[
M=[2],\qquad b=[1].
\]

Modulo 43, 2 is invertible and the solution is

\[
x=22 \pmod {43}.
\]

But over Z the equation has no solution. Thus one prime reduction cannot recover integral solvability.

**Classification: exact finite counterexample.**

## 4. Why all primes P>32 are not automatically enough

The claim

"solvability modulo every prime P>32 is equivalent to solvability over R"

is not justified by localization or Nakayama alone.

For an integer matrix M, Smith normal form gives

\[
UMV=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r,0,\ldots,0).
\]

Integral solvability requires divisibility conditions by the full invariant factors d_i. Reduction modulo primes only detects whether a prime divides a relevant invariant factor. It does not detect higher valuations.

Example:

\[
M=[2^2],\qquad b=[2].
\]

For every odd prime p, the reduced equation

\[
4x=2 \pmod p
\]

is solvable because 4 is invertible. But over Z it is not solvable. The missing information is the 2-adic valuation.

Even checking every P>32 does not replace checking all local valuations at primes ≤32 already inverted or excluded by the coefficient ring, nor does it establish a global Smith condition without a separate argument.

**Classification: proved obstruction.**

## 5. What the rank gap proves and does not prove

Given the asserted sparse computation:

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{F_{43}}(A)=42366,
\]

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{F_{43}}([A|t])=42367,
\]

it follows exactly that

\[
t\notin \operatorname{im}(A).
\]

Equivalently, the stated quotient class of t is nonzero.

This is an **exact finite fact**.

It does not prove:

1. that the literal Laurent certificate maps to this quotient;
2. that the target t is the image of the original b;
3. that all support-radius and normalization conventions agree;
4. that the quotient used in the computation is the same quotient induced by the original definitions.

Those require the explicit commutative diagram identities above.

## Final classification

- Integral certificate implies mod-43 literal certificate: **proved theorem**.
- Literal certificate implies mod-43 landing solvability: **conditional implication**, requiring the exact image diagram.
- The displayed mod-43 rank gap: **exact finite fact**.
- Rank gap alone obstructing the literal certificate: **scoped obstruction until the diagram is verified**.
- Mod-P solvability for all P>32 being equivalent to R-solvability: **false without additional Smith/P-adic hypotheses**.
