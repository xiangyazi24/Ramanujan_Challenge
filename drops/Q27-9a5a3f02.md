# Q27: Variance of variance and the third factorial moment

## 1. The first correction: factorial moments are not central moments

The proposed variance-of-variance idea is useful, but the identity

\[
(K)_3=K(K-1)(K-2)\approx (K-\lambda)^3
\]

is not correct. The exact polynomial identity is

\[
(K-\lambda)^3=K^3-3\lambda K^2+3\lambda^2K-\lambda^3.
\]

Using

\[
(K)_3=K^3-3K^2+2K,
\]

we obtain

\[
(K)_3=(K-\lambda)^3+3(\lambda-1)K^2+(2-3\lambda^2)K+\lambda^3.
\]

After summing over m, the lower terms are controlled by the first two factorial moments. Therefore the centered third moment is not the quantity whose size gives (HM)_3. The relevant quantity is the third factorial cumulant.

This distinction explains the apparent paradox with small \(\lambda\).

For Poisson(\lambda):

\[
E[(K)_3]=\lambda^3,
\]

while

\[
E[(K-\lambda)^3]=\lambda.
\]

Both are correct. The central moment is dominated by single-hit fluctuations; the factorial moment measures genuine triples of distinct primes.

## 2. The exact CRT expansion

Write

\[
K(m)=\sum_p X_p(m),
\]

where

\[
X_p(m)=1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p}.
\]

Then

\[
(K(m))_3=\sum_{p,q,r\ distinct}X_p(m)X_q(m)X_r(m).
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_m(K(m))_3
=
\sum_{p,q,r}\#\{m:m\in[0,X^2),m\bmod p\in Z_p, m\bmod q\in Z_q,m\bmod r\in Z_r\}.
\]

For each triple, CRT gives

\[
\frac{X^2Z(p)Z(q)Z(r)}{pqr}+E_{pqr}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_m(K)_3
=X^2\lambda^3+\sum_{p,q,r}E_{pqr}+\text{diagonal corrections}.
\]

The entire problem is the signed discrepancy sum

\[
\mathcal E_3=\sum_{p,q,r}E_{pqr}.
\]

The variance-of-variance approach is exactly an attempt to prove cancellation in \(\mathcal E_3\).

---

## 3. Why bounding the centered third moment is the wrong target

A Poisson model gives

\[
\sum_m(K-\lambda)^3\sim X^2\lambda.
\]

Since \(\lambda\approx 1/\log X\) or smaller in the shell model, this is much larger than

\[
X^2\lambda^3.
\]

This does NOT contradict (HM)_3. It only shows that central moments are the wrong observable.

The rare-event regime is exactly where factorial moments are designed to remove the one-hit contribution.

---

## 4. Variance of the factorial moment estimator

Define

\[
T_3=\sum_m(K(m))_3.
\]

Under the independent Bernoulli model:

\[
E T_3=X^2\lambda^3.
\]

The variance is obtained from

\[
\mathrm{Var}(T_3)=\sum_{m,n}\mathrm{Cov}((K(m))_3,(K(n))_3).
\]

Expanding gives terms indexed by two triples of primes. The overlap types are:

1. six distinct primes: independent contribution cancels;
2. five distinct primes: one shared prime;
3. four distinct primes: two shared primes;
4. three distinct primes: identical triple.

For Poisson/Bernoulli independence the dominant surviving contribution is the identical-triple term:

\[
\mathrm{Var}(T_3)\asymp X^2\lambda^3,
\]

up to constants depending on normalization. Relative fluctuations are therefore small:

\[
\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(T_3)}}{ET_3}\approx \frac1{X\lambda^{3/2}}.
\]

Thus the Poisson model strongly supports (HM)_3.

## 5. What CRT input is actually needed

The needed statement is not a bound on individual CRT errors. Individual errors are too large:

\[
|E_{pqr}|\le O(1).
\]

There are about

\[
(X/\log X)^3
\]

triples, so absolute summation loses badly.

The required input is a signed cancellation theorem:

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}E_{pqr}=O(X^2\lambda^3).
\]

Equivalently, one needs a triple large-sieve/discrepancy statement for the family of Apéry residue sets.

A sufficient form would be:

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}\left|
\sum_{m<X^2}
\prod_{s\in\{p,q,r\}}
(1_{\Omega_s}(m)-\rho_s)
\right|^2
\ll X^2\lambda^6.
\]

Then Cauchy-Schwarz gives the desired first moment bound.

## 6. Does HM_2 imply HM_3?

No, not formally.

HM_2 controls

\[
\sum_{p,q}E_{pq},
\]

while HM_3 needs

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}E_{pqr}.
\]

Pairwise cancellation does not control third-order correlations. An adversarial family of residue sets can have perfect pair independence and huge triple clustering.

The missing Apéry-specific input is therefore genuinely third-order:

- monodromy independence of three Frobenius conditions;
- a triple character-sum estimate;
- or a direct third-order CRT discrepancy theorem.

---

## 7. Most promising route

The variance-of-variance idea is closer to the truth than a Selberg-sieve argument, because (HM)_3 is a moment statement.

The realistic route is:

1. Define centered local variables

\[
g_p=1_{\Omega_p}-\rho_p.
\]

2. Prove a third-order correlation estimate

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}\left|\sum_m g_pg_qg_r\right|
\ll X^2\lambda^3.
\]

3. Use the exact factorial/central conversion to obtain

\[
\sum_m(K)_3\ll X^2\lambda^3.
\]

For Apéry zeros, this likely requires the geometric input already suggested by the K3 interpretation: independence of the corresponding Frobenius traces across three primes.

Conclusion: the variance-of-variance framework identifies the correct obstruction. It does not reduce (HM)_3 to HM_2, but it reduces the problem to a precise signed triple CRT discrepancy estimate. The Poisson calculation confirms that the target is natural and not too strong; the missing theorem is a genuine third-order independence theorem for Apéry zero sets.
