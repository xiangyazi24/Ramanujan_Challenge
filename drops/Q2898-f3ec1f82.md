# 1. The two exact Apéry solutions and the denominator budget

The committed source uses the two solutions

\[
(a_0,a_1)=(0,6),\qquad (b_0,b_1)=(1,5)
\]

of

\[
(k+1)^3y_{k+1}=P(k)y_k-k^3y_{k-1}.
\tag{3}
\]

Put

\[
c_k:=a_k/6.
\]

Since \(p\ge7\), division by \(6\) is harmless in \(\mathbb Z_p\).  The source proves

\[
d_k a_k\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
 d_k=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,k)^3.
\tag{4}
\]

It also proves the exact Casoratian

\[
a_kb_{k-1}-a_{k-1}b_k=\frac6{k^3},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
c_kb_{k-1}-c_{k-1}b_k=\frac1{k^3}.}
\tag{5}
\]

Now fix \(s=p^a\).  In the fixed-\(q\) range needed here every index occurring in a full block or at its two endpoints satisfies

\[
0\le k\le qs < p^{a+1},
\]

because \(q<p-1\).  Therefore

\[
v_p(d_k)=3\lfloor\log_p k\rfloor\le3a.
\]

From (4), and because \(6\) is a \(p\)-unit,

\[
v_p(c_k)\ge-v_p(d_k)\ge-3a.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{s^3c_k=p^{3a}c_k\in\mathbb Z_p
       \qquad(0\le k\le qs).}
\tag{6}
\]

This is the denominator cancellation needed below.  Nothing is being inferred from a guessed recurrence or from numerical data: the entire denominator budget is paid by the already-proved \(d_ka_k\in\mathbb Z\).

# 2. Explicit normalized Green solution in a shifted block

Take a noninitial full block, so

\[
L=ms,\qquad 1\le m<q.
\]

Define, for all indices needed around this block,

\[
\boxed{
 u^{(m)}_k
 :=m^3s^3\bigl(b_{L-1}c_k-c_{L-1}b_k\bigr).
}
\tag{7}
\]

Because both \(b\) and \(c\) satisfy (3), so does \(u^{(m)}\).  By (6), every value in (7) is in \(\mathbb Z_p\): explicitly,

\[
m^3b_{L-1}(s^3c_k)
 -m^3(s^3c_{L-1})b_k\in\mathbb Z_p.
\]

The normalization is exact.  First,

\[
u^{(m)}_{L-1}=0.
\tag{8}
\]

Second, applying (5) at \(k=L=ms\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
u^{(m)}_L
 &=m^3s^3
   \bigl(b_{L-1}c_L-c_{L-1}b_L\bigr)\\
 &=m^3s^3\frac1{L^3}\\
 &=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

So (7) is the p-adically integral restart solution with exact Dirichlet data

\[
\boxed{u_{L-1}=0,\qquad u_L=1.}
\tag{10}
\]

This is precisely the object that the naive forward recurrence tries to construct by repeatedly dividing by \((L+j)^3\).  Formula (7) proves all those cancellations at once.

For the initial block \(m=0\), use simply

\[
u^{(0)}_k=b_k.
\tag{11}
\]

It already has \(u^{(0)}_0=1\), and the left boundary of the matrix is absent.

# 3. Exact relation with the continuant / gap polynomial

Let \(N_h(X)\) be the committed continuant polynomial,

\[
N_0=0,\qquad N_1=1,
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(X)
 =P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X).
\tag{12}
\]

The project already identifies it as the determinant of the corresponding symmetric tridiagonal block.  For the length-\(j\) segment beginning at \(L\),

\[
D_j(L)
 :=\det J[L,L+j-1]
 =N_{j+1}(L-1).
\tag{13}
\]

The normalized restart identity gives, for \(0\le j\le s\),

\[
\boxed{
 u^{(m)}_{L+j}
 =\frac{N_{j+1}(L-1)}
        {\prod_{t=1}^{j}(L+t)^3}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

For \(m\ge1\), (14) follows either from the committed Green/restart proof or directly because its right side and (7) satisfy the same second-order recurrence with the same initial values (10).  For \(m=0\), it is the familiar identity

\[
N_{j+1}(-1)=(j!)^3b_j.
\]

Combining (6), (7), and (14) yields an explicit divisibility statement that is useful independently of Smith language:

\[
\boxed{
 v_p\bigl(N_{j+1}(L-1)\bigr)
 \ge
 3\sum_{t=1}^{j}v_p(L+t)
 \qquad(0\le j\le s).
}
\tag{15}
\]

Thus every denominator in the local factorial/Green expression is actually cancelled in \(\mathbb Z_p\).  This is the exact denominator statement the factorial-gauge heuristic needs.

# 4. The distinguished block vector

The Jacobi matrix has positive off-diagonal entries, whereas (3) has a minus sign on the backward term.  Therefore introduce the alternating sign locally:

\[
\boxed{
 x^{(m)}_j:=(-1)^j u^{(m)}_{L+j},
 \qquad 0\le j<s.
}
\tag{16}
\]

By (9),

\[
x^{(m)}_0=1,
\]

so \(x^{(m)}\) is primitive, and indeed can be the first column of a \(\mathbb Z_p\)-unimodular basis.

Let \(B_m\) be the \(s\times s\) Jacobi block on indices \(L,\dots,L+s-1\).  For an interior row, (3) gives exact cancellation:

\[
(L+j)^3x_{j-1}+P(L+j)x_j+(L+j+1)^3x_{j+1}=0.
\]

At the left edge, (8) gives exact cancellation as well; the omitted predecessor contributes

\[
L^3u_{L-1}=0.
\]

At the last row only the omitted successor remains.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
B_mx^{(m)}
 =(-1)^{s-1}(L+s)^3u^{(m)}_{L+s}\,e_{s-1}.
}
\tag{17}
\]

Set

\[
\lambda_m:=(-1)^{s-1}(L+s)^3u^{(m)}_{L+s}.
\]

Since \(L+s=(m+1)s\), and \(u^{(m)}_{L+s}\in\mathbb Z_p\),

\[
\boxed{v_p(\lambda_m)\ge3a.}
\tag{18}
\]

This proves (1).

Using (14), there is also a useful exact quotient formula:

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_m
 =(-1)^{s-1}
 \frac{N_{s+1}(L-1)}
      {\prod_{t=1}^{s-1}(L+t)^3}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

For the initial block this reduces to

\[
\lambda_0=s^3b_s,
\]

because

\[
N_{s+1}(-1)=(s!)^3b_s.
\]

Formula (19) is the cleanest interpretation of the new scale-\(a\) direction: the huge determinant of the length-\(s\) block contains all lower-scale factorial divisibility, and after removing the internal product

\[
\prod_{t=1}^{s-1}(L+t)^3,
\]

the remaining boundary quotient is still divisible by the **new** factor \(s^3=p^{3a}\).

# 5. Explicit p-adic row and column bases

Define

\[
C_m=
\begin{bmatrix}
|&|&&|\\
x^{(m)}&e_1&\cdots&e_{s-1}\\
|&|&&|
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{20}
\]

Because the zeroth coordinate of \(x^{(m)}\) is \(1\),

\[
\det C_m=1,
\]

so

\[
C_m\in\mathrm{GL}_s(\mathbb Z_p).
\]

Take

\[
R_m=C_m^{\mathsf T}\in\mathrm{GL}_s(\mathbb Z_p).
\tag{21}
\]

Since \(B_m\) is symmetric, (17) gives both

\[
B_mx^{(m)}=\lambda_me_{s-1}
\]

and

\[
(x^{(m)})^{\mathsf T}B_m
 =\lambda_me_{s-1}^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Therefore every entry in the first row and every entry in the first column of

\[
\boxed{R_mB_mC_m}
\tag{22}
\]

is divisible by \(p^{3a}\).  More explicitly, with the basis order in (20), the distinguished row/column can only couple to the last standard basis vector:

\[
(R_mB_mC_m)_{0,j}=0
\quad(1\le j<s-1),
\]

\[
(R_mB_mC_m)_{i,0}=0
\quad(1\le i<s-1),
\]

while the remaining entries in its row/column are multiples of \(\lambda_m\in p^{3a}\mathbb Z_p\).

Hence modulo \(p^{3a}\), the distinguished direction is literally a zero row and zero column.

This is the promised explicit p-adic row/column isolation.

It does **not** assert that \(\lambda_m\) itself is an elementary divisor or that its valuation is exactly \(3a\).  Extra divisibility may occur, and lower-scale Smith directions remain in the complementary \((s-1)\)-dimensional block.  The statement needed for the selector is the robust one: at least one Smith exponent in this block is \(\ge3a\).

# 6. Why this really implies a Smith direction

Let \(A\in M_s(\mathbb Z_p)\), and suppose \(x\in\mathbb Z_p^s\) is primitive with

\[
Ax\in p^t\mathbb Z_p^s.
\]

Write a Smith decomposition

\[
UAV=\operatorname{diag}(p^{e_1}u_1,\dots,p^{e_s}u_s),
\qquad
0\le e_1\le\cdots\le e_s,
\]

with every \(u_i\) a unit.  The vector \(V^{-1}x\) is primitive, so one of its coordinates is a unit.  Since multiplying by the diagonal sends it into \(p^t\mathbb Z_p^s\), the exponent on at least one unit coordinate is \(\ge t\).  Thus

\[
e_s\ge t.
\]

Applying this to (17) gives one exponent \(\ge3a\) in every full block.

For several blocks, use the stronger direct-summand version.  If \(X\subset\mathbb Z_p^n\) is a primitive rank-\(q\) direct summand and

\[
J_nX\subset p^t\mathbb Z_p^n,
\]

then at least \(q\) Smith exponents of \(J_n\) are \(\ge t\).  Indeed, in Smith right coordinates the image of \(X\bmod p\) still has dimension \(q\).  Every row whose exponent is \(<t\) must vanish on \(X\bmod p\), because its coordinate in every vector of \(X\) is forced to be divisible by \(p^{t-e_i}\), hence by \(p\).  Therefore at least \(q\) Smith rows have exponent \(\ge t\).

Now take the \(q\) vectors (16), extended by zero outside their respective full blocks.  Their first coordinate in each disjoint block is \(1\), so they form a primitive rank-\(q\) direct summand.

Inside a block, (17) is divisible by \(p^{3a}\).  Leakage into a neighboring block comes only through a boundary edge

\[
(ms)^3
\]

or

\[
((m+1)s)^3,
\]

which is also divisible by \(s^3=p^{3a}\).  Hence

\[
J_nX\subset p^{3a}\mathbb Z_p^n.
\]

This proves (2).

# 7. The factorial diagonal gauge: exact identity and exact obstruction

The tempting diagonal scaling can now be audited precisely.

For a block starting at \(L\), define

\[
f_0=1,
\qquad
f_j=\prod_{t=1}^{j}(L+t)^3
 =\left(\frac{(L+j)!}{L!}\right)^3,
\]

and

\[
F_{L,s}=\operatorname{diag}(f_0,\dots,f_{s-1}).
\]

Then there is an exact identity over \(\mathbb Q_p\):

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde B_m:=F_{L,s}B_mF_{L,s}^{-1}
}
\tag{23}
\]

is integral, with

\[
(\widetilde B_m)_{j,j}=P(L+j),
\]

\[
(\widetilde B_m)_{j,j+1}=1,
\qquad
(\widetilde B_m)_{j+1,j}=(L+j+1)^6.
\tag{24}
\]

Thus every denominator in the *matrix entries* cancels exactly.

Moreover, because the superdiagonal is identically \(1\), the minor using rows \(0,\dots,s-2\) and columns \(1,\dots,s-1\) is triangular with determinant \(1\).  Therefore, as an ambient integral matrix,

\[
\widetilde B_m
\sim
\operatorname{diag}(1,\dots,1,\det B_m).
\tag{25}
\]

This looks like the desired one-direction factorization.  But for \(a\ge2\), (23) is **not a Smith equivalence of the original \(\mathbb Z_p\)-lattice**.

The exact lattice index is

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_p(\det F_{L,s})
 &=3\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\sum_{t=1}^{j}v_p(L+t)\\
 &=3\sum_{t=1}^{s-1}(s-t)v_p(L+t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Since \(L=ms\) and \(0<t<s=p^a\),

\[
v_p(L+t)=v_p(t),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\det F_{L,s})
 =3\sum_{t=1}^{s-1}(s-t)v_p(t).
}
\tag{27}
\]

For \(a=1\), every \(1\le t<p\) is a unit, so \(F_{L,p}\in\mathrm{GL}_p(\mathbb Z_p)\); at the first scale the factorial gauge really is a legitimate p-adic Smith change of basis.

For \(a\ge2\), (27) is positive and large.  There is also a basis-independent way to see that no hidden unimodular replacement can make (23) into a Smith equivalence.  Modulo \(p\), the raw length-\(p^a\) block splits at every internal multiple of \(p\) into

\[
p^{a-1}
\]

length-\(p\) irreducible tridiagonal blocks.  Each has one-dimensional kernel, so

\[
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb F_p}B_m=p^{a-1}.
\tag{28}
\]

By contrast, \(\widetilde B_m\) has superdiagonal \(1\), hence any kernel vector is determined by one starting coordinate and

\[
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb F_p}\widetilde B_m\le1.
\tag{29}
\]

For \(a\ge2\), (28) and (29) are incompatible with left/right multiplication by \(\mathrm{GL}_s(\mathbb Z_p)\).  Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
F_{L,s}B_mF_{L,s}^{-1}
\text{ is an integral recurrence gauge, but not a Smith-lattice gauge for }a\ge2.
}
\tag{30}
\]

This is the exact obstruction to the most natural factorial-scaling proof.

The Green vector (7) repairs exactly this defect: it uses the same normalized recurrence solution as the factorial gauge, but (4)--(6) prove that the resulting vector actually belongs to the original \(\mathbb Z_p\)-lattice.

# 8. Factorial gauge and Green function are the same construction after denominator cancellation

The connection is explicit.  Starting from the Dirichlet data

\[
u_{L-1}=0,\qquad u_L=1,
\]

forward recurrence formally gives

\[
u_{L+j}
 =\frac{N_{j+1}(L-1)}
 {\prod_{t=1}^{j}(L+t)^3}.
\]

The factorial gauge stores the numerator

\[
z_j:=\left(\prod_{t=1}^{j}(L+t)^3\right)u_{L+j}
 =N_{j+1}(L-1),
\]

which obeys the denominator-free continuant recurrence

\[
z_{j+1}
 =P(L+j)z_j-(L+j)^6z_{j-1}.
\tag{31}
\]

The problem is that \(F^{-1}z\) need not lie in the standard lattice for an arbitrary integral numerator sequence.

For the **actual Apéry operator**, (7) proves

\[
F^{-1}z=u\in\mathbb Z_p^s
\]

through the entire block.  Equivalently, (15) is exactly the missing denominator divisibility.

So the correct logical order is:

```text
factorial conjugation
    -> denominator-free continuant numerator
    -> NOT a Smith equivalence by itself
    -> Apéry companion + Wronskian
    -> proves numerator lies in factorial sublattice
    -> yields genuine primitive Z_p restart vector
    -> one p^(3a) Smith direction per block.
```

This is substantially stronger than simply observing that the boundary edge \(s^3\) is divisible by \(p^{3a}\): it constructs the primitive direction that reaches that boundary with no p-adic denominator loss.

# 9. What this does and does not settle in the full selector

What is now explicit and proved from committed source:

1. **One scale-\(a\) primitive direction per full block.**  Equations (7), (16), and (17) give it.
2. **No hidden denominator loss.**  Equation (6) uses the proved Apéry denominator \(d_n\), and (15) is the resulting exact continuant divisibility.
3. **Explicit p-adic row/column bases.**  Equations (20)--(22) make the distinguished row and column zero modulo \(p^{3a}\).
4. **The global Smith consequence.**  For \(q\) full \(p^a\)-blocks, at least \(q\) Smith exponents are \(\ge3a\).
5. **Exact diagnosis of factorial scaling.**  It is legitimate at \(a=1\), but for \(a\ge2\) it changes the lattice by (27) and cannot be a Smith equivalence because of the corank mismatch (28)--(29).

What is **not** proved here is a complete recursive Smith normal form, or an exact formula saying that the newly isolated exponent is *exactly* \(3a\).  The scalar

\[
\frac{\lambda_m}{p^{3a}}
 =(m+1)^3u^{(m)}_{(m+1)s}
\]

may itself vanish mod \(p\).  Such extra vanishing is exactly where a coarser target/selector condition should live.  To identify the quotient operator on the distinguished block directions with a literal coarse Jacobi matrix \(J_q\), one still has to compute the boundary amplitudes/pairings modulo \(p\).  The argument above gives the required integral lattice and the correct \(p^{3a}\) extraction; it does not fabricate that final coarse-matrix identification.

That is the smallest honest stopping point given the connector-visible sources.

# Bottom line

The p-adic basis exists.  For \(s=p^a\), \(L=ms\), define \(c=a/6\) and, for \(m\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
 u_k=m^3s^3(b_{L-1}c_k-c_{L-1}b_k),
\qquad
 x_j=(-1)^ju_{L+j}.
}
\]

The proved denominator theorem gives \(s^3c_k\in\mathbb Z_p\) throughout the fixed-\(q\) block, the Wronskian gives

\[
u_{L-1}=0,\qquad u_L=1,
\]

and the recurrence gives

\[
\boxed{
B_mx=(L+s)^3u_{L+s}e_{s-1}
\in p^{3a}\mathbb Z_p^s.
}
\]

Thus

\[
C_m=[x,e_1,\dots,e_{s-1}],
\qquad R_m=C_m^{\mathsf T}
\]

are explicit \(\mathbb Z_p\)-unimodular column/row bases whose distinguished row and column vanish modulo \(p^{3a}\).  Across the \(q\) full blocks this gives \(q\) independent primitive directions, hence at least \(q\) Smith invariant factors divisible by \(p^{3a}\).

The naive factorial conjugation

\[
F B_mF^{-1}
\]

is the right recurrence gauge but the wrong Smith lattice for \(a\ge2\); its lattice defect is exactly

\[
3\sum_{t=1}^{p^a-1}(p^a-t)v_p(t),
\]

and the raw/transformed mod-\(p\) coranks \(p^{a-1}\) versus at most \(1\) prove that no unimodular interpretation of that diagonal scaling is possible.  The Green/Wronskian construction above is precisely the denominator-corrected replacement.
