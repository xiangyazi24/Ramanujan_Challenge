# Q875: weakened top-block energy attack audit

## 1. Target and the exact amount of saving required

The desired statement is

\[
F_N=\sum_{p\neq q}|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|=o(N^2/\log^2 N).
\]

This is strictly weaker than the previous Diagonal-AMTD target

\[
F_N=O(\sum_p|\Omega_p|).
\]

Indeed \(\sum_p|\Omega_p|\) can be as large as \(N^2/\log N\) in the present top-block normalization, so the diagonal estimate would imply a much stronger pair-correlation exclusion than is needed. The correct question is only whether two independent large-prime divisibility events have total energy below the threshold forced by a heavy row.

The Apéry-Lucas relation gives only

\[
p\mid b_m\iff p\mid b_{m-p},
\]

which is a one-prime translation statement. It does **not** imply a joint congruence modulo \(pq\): from

\[
p\mid b_{m-p},\qquad q\mid b_{m-q}
\]

one cannot conclude

\[
pq\mid b_{m-pq}
\]

or any analogous combined recurrence relation.

## 2. Gap decomposition

For \(p,q\in(N,2N]\), write \(d=|p-q|\). Then

\[
|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|
\]

counts \(m\in(N,2N]\) satisfying

\[
p\mid b_m,\qquad q\mid b_m.
\]

The Lucas relation moves the two conditions independently:

\[
p\mid b_{m-p},\qquad q\mid b_{m-q}.
\]

The only natural common parameter is the gap

\[
d=q-p.
\]

However, fixing \(d\) does not create a recurrence modulo \(d\). The number of pairs with a given gap is controlled by prime-gap estimates, but the Apéry divisibility events remain attached to different characteristics.

The formal decomposition is

\[
F_N=2\sum_{d\le N}\sum_{p,p+d\ {m prime}}|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_{p+d}|.
\]

A saving would require an estimate of the form

\[
\sum_{p,p+d\ {m prime}}|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_{p+d}|
=o(N^2/(d\log^2N))
\]

on average over gaps. No unconditional mechanism currently supplies this.

## 3. Fixed-gap energy does not close by itself

For a fixed gap \(d\), one can define

\[
E_d=\sum_{p,p+d\ prime}|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_{p+d}|.
\]

The available information gives only the trivial bound

\[
E_d\le \sum_{p,p+d\ prime}\min(|\Omega_p|,|\Omega_{p+d}|).
\]

Even using the number of prime pairs

\[
\#\{p:p,p+d\in(N,2N]\}\ll N/\log^2N
\]

for typical fixed even \(d\), this leaves the unknown size of the individual Apéry divisibility sets. There is no known bound strong enough to make the sum over \(d\) little-o of \(N^2/\log^2N\).

## 4. Selberg sieve route

A possible route would be to prove an Apéry-specific depletion statement:

> For distinct primes \(p,q\in(N,2N]\), the set of indices \(m\in(N,2N]\) for which \(p\mid b_m\) and \(q\mid b_m\) has density \(o(1/\log^2N)\) uniformly on average over \((p,q)\).

A generic Selberg sieve cannot prove this. It would need a local obstruction showing that simultaneous Apéry divisibility removes a positive proportion of residue classes.

The recurrence

\[
b_n=\text{Apéry recurrence solution}
\]

has characteristic-dependent reductions modulo \(p\). The missing input is a quantitative statement about the joint distribution of the zero sets of these reductions. Without such an input, sieve weights see only arbitrary subsets.

## 5. Characteristic-zero carrier obstruction

A tempting approach is to use the analytic size of \(b_n\). Since

\[
b_n\approx C(1+\sqrt2)^{4n}n^{-3/2},
\]

one might hope that a prime divisor \(p>N\) forces a rare event. But this does not control intersections:

\[
p\mid b_m, q\mid b_m
\]

is simply the assertion that \(pq\mid b_m\). The integer size only gives

\[
\omega(b_m)\ll m,
\]

which is far too weak because the relevant primes are all of size \(N\), and \(b_m\) has exponential size.

No characteristic-zero estimate currently gives the needed pair energy saving.

## 6. A genuinely weaker sufficient lemma

A non-tautological sufficient lemma is the following average pair-depletion statement.

**Lemma (average Apéry pair depletion).** There exists a function \(\eta(N)\to0\) such that

\[
\sum_{\substack{p,q\in(N,2N]\\p,q\ prime\\p\neq q}}
|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|
\le \eta(N)\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}.
\]

This is exactly the desired theorem and therefore not useful. A strictly weaker form must insert an independently checkable structure. A candidate is:

**Candidate weaker lemma.** There exists \(\eta(N)\to0\) such that for every dyadic block,

\[
\sum_{p\in(N,2N]}\sum_{q\in(N,2N],q\neq p}
\frac{|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|}{|\Omega_p|+|\Omega_q|+1}
\le \eta(N)N.
\]

Combined with an independent first-moment estimate

\[
\sum_p|\Omega_p|\ll N^2/\log N,
\]

this would imply the desired energy bound by Cauchy-type amplification. However, proving this normalized correlation estimate remains open; it is a real distribution statement rather than a restatement of \(F_N\).

## 7. Quantifier audit

The needed theorem is:

\[
\forall\epsilon>0,\ \exists N_0,\ \forall N>N_0:
F_N<\epsilon N^2/\log^2N.
\]

The heavy-row contradiction uses only this scale. It does not require:

* uniform bounds for every pair \((p,q)\);
* diagonal domination \(F_N\ll\sum_p|\Omega_p|\);
* independence modulo \(pq\);
* fixed-gap bounds individually for every gap.

A viable proof may therefore average over prime pairs, but it must exploit a genuine Apéry property beyond Lucas periodicity.

## 8. Conclusion

The weakened target is substantially more plausible than Diagonal-AMTD, but no unconditional proof follows from current ingredients. Gap summation, generic sieve, and characteristic-zero growth estimates do not provide the missing saving. The real bottleneck is an Apéry-specific pair-correlation/depletion theorem for reductions modulo two large primes.

The most economical next lemma is not a diagonal estimate but an average normalized pair-depletion result for the family of mod-\(p\) Apéry sequences. Establishing such a lemma would directly attack the correct energy scale.
