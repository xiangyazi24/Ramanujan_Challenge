## 5. Finite Boolean model convention

All failed arrows below are witnessed inside the exact nonnegative interface.

A model consists of finitely many `q`-blocks.  Each block has two displayed `t`-atoms, and centering is over those two atoms.  Thus for a vector `(u,v)`,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(u,v)=\frac{(u-v)^2}{2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If one insists on a complete odd-prime residue block, add a third atom with value `(u+v)/2` after doubling all loads when necessary.  Its block mean is still `(u+v)/2`, and its centered energy remains exactly `(u-v)^2/2`.  Unused residues may be filled by copies of the block mean.

Every “column” below is a distinct lower-prime label.  Its window mask and its actual lower-zero event are Boolean.  The rational density `rho_p` is realized by a finite auxiliary zero table.  For example, `rho=1/4` is the pair density obtained from a zero set of density `1/2`; `rho=N^{-2}` is obtained from a zero set of density `1/N`.  The designated affine sample may hit or miss that zero table adversarially.  No independence is assumed.

All core countermodels are one-sign models:

\[
\chi^+=0.
\]

They therefore satisfy the union-corrected definition exactly, and no issue about the overlap coefficient can rescue a failed arrow.

To make PF2 zero on an active pre-upper block, take the upper selector to vanish there and add a disjoint dummy selected state of load zero.  In the stronger models `F_N` and `Q_N`, the displayed selector is specified explicitly.

Repeating a block `m` times multiplies every displayed variance by `m`.  Consequently each finite separation below yields an asymptotic failed implication by taking `m\to\infty` and choosing an intermediate comparison scale.

## 8. Exact union-overlap sanity models

The variance countermodels are one-sign and therefore cannot exploit a mistake in the overlap correction.  For completeness, here are finite models testing the correction itself.

### 8.1 Perfect sign overlap

Take one lower-prime column with both windows active.  On a four-atom auxiliary table, let

\[
(X^-,X^+)=(1,1)
\]

on one atom and `(0,0)` on the other three.  Then

\[
\rho=1/4,
\qquad
\theta=1/4,
\]

and the true union density is

\[
2\rho-\theta=1/4.
\]

The uncorrected sign sum gives `1/2`, double the truth.

### 8.2 Disjoint signs

On a four-atom table, let `X^-=1` on one atom and `X^+=1` on a different atom.  Then

\[
\rho=1/4,
\qquad
\theta=0,
\]

and the union density is

\[
2\rho=1/2.
\]

These two models show that `theta` can range from zero to `rho` while both marginals remain fixed.  A theorem controlling only `rho` does not determine the overlap correction.

They also show why the corrected baseline changes constants but does not by itself control centered oscillation: one may assign the two overlap patterns to different `t`-rows while preserving every marginal density.

## Final conclusion

The new union-corrected affine baseline is the right object:

\[
R_q(t)=\sum_p
\left(
 \chi^-_{p,q}(t)\rho_p
 +\chi^+_{p,q}(t)\rho_p
 -\chi^-_{p,q}(t)\chi^+_{p,q}(t)\theta_p

But exact union accounting and variance control are different steps.  After centering,

\[
A^\circ=R^\circ+D^\circ.
\]

Therefore WAUD is neither stronger nor weaker than UVD by itself.  It is equivalent to UVD only conditional on TSO, the small structured-`R` oscillation premise.  Any two of UVD, WAUD, and TSO imply the third, and there are no other variance arrows.

PF2 is orthogonal.  It controls multiplicity on actual upper-selected states and closes the marked incidence through state-adaptive Cauchy.  It does not see false-positive affine windows, unselected residues, the structured baseline, or singleton defect oscillation.  It cannot bound either `V_R` or `V_D`, even after adding the marked first mass and a bounded UVD quantity.  The finite model `Q_N` gives the exact obstruction.

Hence the cold comparison is:

```text
UVD                  : one complete pre-upper variance premise;
WAUD + TSO           : a split formulation equivalent in closing power;
PF2                   : a separate post-selected factorial route;
WAUD alone            : incomplete;
TSO alone             : incomplete;
PF2 -> R or defect    : false.
```

The only possible new gain from WAUD is arithmetic: the defect may be easier to estimate after subtracting the exact union baseline.  That gain is not supplied by PF2, by interval nesting, or by the overlap correction itself.
