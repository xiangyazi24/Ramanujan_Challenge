## Verdict

There is a clean unconditional theorem, but it does **not** contain a new Apéry-specific horizontal power saving.

Let `I` be any interval of `L` consecutive integers and let the active primes be any subset of the primes `P < p <= 2P` (so prime-level low-order pruning is harmless).  Put

\[
Z_p=\{r\in\mathbf F_p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},\qquad
z_p=|Z_p|,\qquad \rho_p=z_p/p,
\]
\[
M_{p,m}=1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p},\qquad
K(m)=\sum_{P<p\le2P}M_{p,m},\qquad
T=\sum_{m\in I}K(m).
\]

Then unconditionally

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in I}\left(K(m)-\frac TL\right)^2
\le (L-1+4P^2)
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
}
\tag{1}
\]

If `L >= 4P`, then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in I}\left(K(m)-\frac TL\right)^2
\le 2\left(1+\frac{4P^2}{L}\right)T.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Finally the **Apéry recurrence** gives the genuinely arithmetic absolute form

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in I}\left(K(m)-\frac TL\right)^2
\ll (L+P^2)\frac{P^{2/3}}{\log P}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

The same theorem holds after replacing each `Z_p` by any pruned subset `Z_p^\sharp \subseteq Z_p`, with `z_p` replaced by `|Z_p^\sharp|`; the recurrence bound remains valid because `|Z_p^\sharp| <= Z(p)`.  If the manuscript's low-character-order pruning is implemented by deleting frequencies rather than zero residues, one uses the corresponding projected Fourier coefficients; the large-sieve step is unchanged.

Consequences by interval length are immediate:

* if `L \asymp P^2`, then the second moment is `O(T)`; in the notation of the question this is stronger than `P^{1/3-\delta}T` with any fixed `\delta<1/3` (and corresponds to `\delta=1/3` at the exponent level);
* if `L \asymp P^{5/3}`, then the unconditional theorem is exactly
  \[
  V(I):=\sum_{m\in I}(K(m)-T/L)^2\ll P^{1/3}T;
  \]
* more generally, the desired `P^{1/3-\delta}T` follows from the generic large sieve whenever
  \[
  L\gg P^{5/3+\delta};
  \]
* at the critical length `L \asymp P^{5/3}`, **no positive `\delta` is currently proved** from the Apéry recurrence, the finite-field differential equation, the zero-count theorem, or the value-collision theorem.

Thus the correct answer depends on the **length** of the parabolic interval, not merely on its location inside `|m| \ll P^2`.  The parabolic location restriction by itself does not improve (1)--(2).

There is also no known actual-Apéry counterexample to a power saving at the critical length.  What can be proved is a sharp obstruction: the presently proved recurrence/ODE information is vertical and, after the exact common-frequency expansion, it controls precisely the diagonal Fourier mass but not the signed near-diagonal coupling between two different characteristics.  I make that statement exact below.

# 1. Exact common-frequency identities

Write

\[
e_p(x)=e^{2\pi i x/p},\qquad
E_p(y,u)=\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(yb_r-ur).
\]

Also define the prime Ramanujan kernel

\[
c_p(t)=\sum_{y\in\mathbf F_p^\times}e_p(yt)
       =p\,1_{t=0}-1.
\]

Hence, pointwise in `r`,

\[
1_{b_r=0}=\frac1p\sum_{y\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(yb_r)
          =\frac{1+c_p(b_r)}p.
\tag{4}
\]

Define the additive Fourier transform of the actual zero set by

\[
F_p(u)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(-ur).
\]

From (4), for every `u != 0`,

\[
\boxed{
F_p(u)=\frac1p\sum_{y\in\mathbf F_p}E_p(y,u)
      =\frac1p\sum_{y\in\mathbf F_p^\times}E_p(y,u),
}
\tag{5}
\]

because `E_p(0,u)=\sum_r e_p(-ur)=0` for `u != 0`.

Now center at the **true zero density**

\[
g_p(m)=M_{p,m}-\rho_p.
\]

Fourier inversion gives the exact common-frequency expansion

\[
\boxed{
g_p(m)=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p^\times}F_p(u)e_p(um).}
\tag{6}
\]

Combining (5) and (6),

\[
\boxed{
g_p(m)=\frac1{p^2}
\sum_{u\ne0}\sum_{y\ne0}E_p(y,u)e_p(um).}
\tag{7}
\]

This is the exact `E_p`/Ramanujan-kernel expansion requested in the question.  No probabilistic model, Weil estimate, or sheaf interpretation has entered.

## Lemma 1 (exact coefficient energy)

For every prime `p`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{u\ne0}\left|\frac{F_p(u)}p\right|^2
=\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
}
\tag{8}
\]

### Proof

Parseval for the indicator `1_{Z_p}` gives

\[
\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}|F_p(u)|^2=pz_p.
\]

The zero frequency is `F_p(0)=z_p`.  Hence

\[
\sum_{u\ne0}|F_p(u)|^2=pz_p-z_p^2,
\]

and division by `p^2` gives (8).  QED.

This identity is important: there is no room to improve the total nonzero-frequency `\ell^2` mass by proving a better pointwise vertical estimate for `E_p(y,u)`.

# 2. The unconditional cross-prime large-sieve theorem

## Lemma 2 (spacing of prime-denominator frequencies)

For distinct pairs `(p,u) != (q,v)` with

\[
P<p,q\le2P,\qquad 1\le u<p,\quad 1\le v<q,
\]

the points `u/p` and `v/q` in `\mathbf R/\mathbf Z` satisfy

\[
\left\|\frac up-\frac vq\right\|_{\mathbf R/\mathbf Z}
\ge \frac1{pq}\ge\frac1{4P^2}.
\tag{9}
\]

### Proof

For `p=q`, distinct numerators are separated by at least `1/p`.  For `p!=q`, equality modulo `1` would force `uq=vp` (or the corresponding wrapped equality), impossible because `0<u<p`, `0<v<q` and `p,q` are distinct primes.  The numerator of the reduced difference on the circle is therefore a nonzero integer, so its absolute value is at least `1/(pq)`.  QED.

## Lemma 3 (standard additive large sieve)

If points `\alpha_j\in\mathbf R/\mathbf Z` are `\delta`-separated, then for every interval `I` of `L` consecutive integers,

\[
\sum_{m\in I}\left|\sum_j a_j e(m\alpha_j)\right|^2
\le (L-1+\delta^{-1})\sum_j|a_j|^2.
\tag{10}
\]

This is the usual sharp-spacing form of the additive large sieve (equivalently the dual Hilbert-inequality form).  Translation of `I` only multiplies each coefficient by a unit phase, so the bound depends on `L`, not on the location of `I`.

## Theorem 4 (unconditional Apéry-prime second moment)

With the definitions above,

\[
\sum_{m\in I}\left|K(m)-\sum_p\rho_p\right|^2
\le (L-1+4P^2)\sum_p\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
\tag{11}
\]

Consequently (1) holds with the actual sample mean `T/L`.

### Proof

By (6),

\[
K(m)-\sum_p\rho_p
=\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{u\ne0}
\frac{F_p(u)}p\,e\!\left(m\frac up\right).
\]

Apply Lemma 3 to the frequencies `u/p`.  Lemma 2 permits `\delta=1/(4P^2)`, and Lemma 1 evaluates the coefficient square-sum exactly.  This proves (11).

Now write

\[
\mu=\sum_p\rho_p,\qquad \bar K=T/L.
\]

Since `\bar K` is the sample mean,

\[
\sum_{m\in I}(K(m)-\bar K)^2
=\sum_{m\in I}(K(m)-\mu)^2-L(\bar K-\mu)^2
\le\sum_{m\in I}(K(m)-\mu)^2.
\]

Together with (11), this is (1).  QED.

## Lemma 5 (comparison with the actual incidence mass)

If `L>=4P`, then

\[
\frac L2\sum_p\rho_p\le T\le2L\sum_p\rho_p.
\tag{12}
\]

### Proof

For one modulus `p`, every residue class occurs either `floor(L/p)` or `floor(L/p)+1` times in an interval of `L` consecutive integers.  Therefore

\[
\lfloor L/p\rfloor z_p
\le\sum_{m\in I}M_{p,m}
\le(\lfloor L/p\rfloor+1)z_p.
\]

Since `p<=2P` and `L>=4P`, one has `L/p>=2`; hence

\[
\lfloor L/p\rfloor\ge L/(2p),\qquad
\lfloor L/p\rfloor+1\le2L/p.
\]

Sum over `p`.  QED.

Combining (1), `rho_p(1-rho_p)<=rho_p`, and the lower bound in (12) gives (2).

# 3. Where the Apéry recurrence actually improves the theorem

Proposition `prop:zp-bound` of `proof.tex` gives, uniformly for primes `p>=7`,

\[
z_p\ll p^{2/3}.
\]

Hence, by the Chebyshev/PNT upper bound for primes in a dyadic interval,

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\rho_p
\ll P^{-1/3}\,\frac P{\log P}
=\frac{P^{2/3}}{\log P}.
\tag{13}
\]

Substitution into (1) proves (3):

\[
V(I)\ll(L+P^2)P^{2/3}/\log P.
\]

This is the strongest unconditional theorem I can prove from the checked current recurrence/ODE input.  It is genuinely Apéry-specific in its **absolute scale** through (13), but the horizontal factor `1+P^2/L` is still the generic large-sieve factor.

For log weights one gets the equally explicit analogue.  Let

\[
K_{\log}(m)=\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)M_{p,m}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{m\in I}|K_{\log}(m)-\mu_{\log}|^2
\le(L-1+4P^2)
\sum_p(\log p)^2\rho_p(1-\rho_p)
\ll(L+P^2)P^{2/3}\log P.
\tag{14}
\]

Thus the logarithmic prime weights cost only the expected dyadic `log P` factor; they do not repair the missing horizontal power saving.

# 4. Why the stronger value-collision theorem does not improve (1)

The manuscript proves the nontrivial recurrence/finite-field value-distribution estimate

\[
E(p)=\sum_a N_p(a)^2=O(p^{7/4}),
\qquad N_p(a)=\#\{r:b_r=a\}.
\]

It is natural to hope that this might shrink the common-frequency coefficients in (7).  The exact orthogonality identities show why it does not.

## Lemma 6 (two exact `E_p` energies)

For every `p`,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{y,u\in\mathbf F_p}|E_p(y,u)|^2=p^3,}
\tag{15}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\sum_{y\in\mathbf F_p}|E_p(y,0)|^2=pE(p).}
\tag{16}
\]

### Proof

Expand (15):

\[
\sum_{y,u}\sum_{r,s}
 e_p(y(b_r-b_s)-u(r-s)).
\]

Orthogonality in `u` forces `r=s`; then both the `y`- and `u`-sums contribute `p`, giving `p^3`.

For (16), orthogonality in `y` counts pairs `(r,s)` with `b_r=b_s`, exactly `E(p)`, with an extra factor `p`.  QED.

So the proved `E(p)=O(p^{7/4})` controls the **`u=0` slice** of the two-variable transform.  The centered incidence problem (6)--(7) contains only `u!=0`.  Moreover, its total nonzero-frequency mass is already fixed exactly by (8).  Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (5) and then inserting (15) can only recover a weaker generic estimate than the zero-set Parseval identity.

This is a precise obstruction to the proposed route:

> The current recurrence/finite-field differential-equation theorems improve vertical zero/value statistics, but they do not produce a signed relation between `F_p(u)` and `F_q(v)` for `p!=q`.  The common-frequency second moment needs exactly such a relation.

This is stronger than saying “vertical Weil bounds are insufficient”: equations (8), (15), and (16) show **where the available vertical energy lives and why it cannot remove the horizontal clustering factor**.

# 5. The parabolic range is not an unrestricted CRT range

There is an actual-Apéry arithmetic coupling in the positive parabolic range, and it is worth recording because it rules out the wrong kind of CRT counterexample.

## Lemma 7 (parabolic Lucas lift)

Let `P<p<=2P` be prime and let `0<=m<P^2`.  Write

\[
m=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

Then `q<p` and

\[
\boxed{b_m\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.}
\tag{17}
\]

In particular,

\[
m\bmod p\in Z_p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid b_m.
\tag{18}
\]

For distinct primes `p_1,...,p_k` in `(P,2P]` satisfying `M_{p_i,m}=1`,

\[
\boxed{p_1\cdots p_k\mid b_m.}
\tag{19}
\]

If, in addition, the leading digit is pruned so that `p\nmid b_q`, then (18) is an equivalence.

### Proof

Because `m<P^2` and `p>P`,

\[
q=\lfloor m/p\rfloor<P<p.
\]

Thus `m` has exactly the two base-`p` digits `(r,q)`.  Gessel's Apéry--Lucas theorem in `proof.tex` gives (17).  If `b_r=0 mod p`, (18) follows.  Distinct prime divisibilities multiply to (19).  If `b_q` is nonzero mod `p`, (17) gives the converse.  QED.

This is the correct parabolic replacement for an unrestricted CRT model: simultaneous incidences really do force simultaneous large-prime divisibility of the **same Apéry number**.

However, this exact coupling does not by itself yield the requested second moment.

## Lemma 8 (height slack in the parabolic range)

For all `m>=0`,

\[
b_m\le(m+1)64^m.
\tag{20}
\]

Therefore Lemma 7 gives only

\[
K(m)\log P\le\log b_m
\le m\log64+\log(m+1).
\tag{21}
\]

For `m<=P^2`, this is

\[
K(m)\ll P^2/\log P,
\]

which is weaker than the trivial prime-counting bound
`K(m) <= pi(2P)-pi(P) << P/log P`.

### Proof

Use `binom(m,k)<=2^m` and, for `k<=m`, `binom(m+k,k)<=2^{m+k}<=4^m` in

\[
b_m=\sum_{k=0}^m\binom mk^2\binom{m+k}k^2.
\]

Each summand is at most `64^m`, giving (20).  Since every incident prime is at least `P`, (19) implies `P^{K(m)}<=b_m`, hence (21).  QED.

Thus the actual Apéry sequence prevents an arbitrary CRT construction, but the parabolic height has enormous slack: even simultaneous divisibility by **every** prime in `(P,2P]` is not ruled out by size.  Any successful theorem must use more than Lucas plus archimedean height.

For intervals containing negative `m`, Lemma 7 is not invoked; Theorem 4 remains valid without any sign or location restriction.

# 6. The exact parabolic near-diagonal obstruction

Let

\[
I=\{A+1,\ldots,A+L\},\qquad |A|+L\ll P^2,
\]

and define the Dirichlet kernel

\[
D_I(\theta)=\sum_{m\in I}e(m\theta).
\]

Expanding (6) gives the exact two-prime off-diagonal term

\[
\mathcal O_I(P)=
\sum_{\substack{P<p,q\le2P\\p\ne q}}
\frac1{pq}
\sum_{u\ne0}\sum_{v\ne0}
F_p(u)\overline{F_q(v)}\,
D_I\!\left(\frac up-\frac vq\right).
\tag{22}
\]

By (5), this is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_I(P)=
\sum_{p\ne q}\frac1{p^2q^2}
\sum_{\substack{u\ne0,v\ne0\\y\ne0,z\ne0}}
E_p(y,u)\overline{E_q(z,v)}\,
D_I\!\left(\frac up-\frac vq\right).
}
\tag{23}
\]

This is the exact common-frequency form in the variables requested in the question.

Write

\[
a=uq-vp.
\]

For the nonwrapped representative,

\[
\frac up-\frac vq=\frac a{pq}.
\]

Since `pq \asymp P^2`, the full-size part of the Dirichlet kernel is

\[
|a|\lesssim\frac{P^2}{L}.
\tag{24}
\]

Indeed, if `|a|<=pq/(4L)`, then

\[
|D_I(a/pq)|
=\left|\frac{\sin(\pi L a/pq)}{\sin(\pi a/pq)}\right|
\gg L.
\tag{25}
\]

The starting point `A` contributes only the unit phase

\[
e\!\left((A+1)a/(pq)\right);
\]

it does **not** change (25).  Therefore restricting `I` to the parabolic location `|A|\ll P^2` does not remove the cluster of close rational frequencies.  It merely specifies the phase with which those close pairs have to cancel.

This is the rigorous parabolic obstruction.  It is not an unrestricted CRT example: it is an identity for the actual Apéry Fourier coefficients on the actual anchored interval.

At the critical length `L\asymp P^{5/3}`, (24) is the band

\[
|uq-vp|\lesssim P^{1/3}.
\tag{26}
\]

The generic large sieve pays exactly for this width.  A bound `P^{1/3-\delta}T` therefore requires a power saving in the signed sum of the Apéry coefficients over (26).

# 7. The one missing estimate

The proof can be closed by one genuinely horizontal statement.  A convenient exact formulation is the following.

## Missing estimate `(HCF_delta)` (horizontal common-frequency cancellation)

For some fixed `delta>0`, uniformly for the critical parabolic intervals `I` and active prime blocks `P<p<=2P`, prove

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal O_I(P)|
\ll
\left(1+P^{-\delta}\frac{P^2}{L}\right)T,
}
\tag{HCF_delta}
\]

where `\mathcal O_I(P)` is either exact form (22) or (23).

At `L\asymp P^{5/3}` this is

\[
|\mathcal O_I(P)|\ll P^{1/3-\delta}T+T.
\]

The same-prime diagonal is already `O(T)` when `L>=4P`: one period has exact square mass `p\rho_p(1-\rho_p)`, and the incomplete remainder contributes `O(z_p)`; summing and using (12) gives `O(T)`.  Therefore `(HCF_delta)` yields

\[
\sum_{m\in I}(K(m)-T/L)^2
\ll P^{1/3-\delta}T+T,
\]

which is the requested power saving at the critical length.

One may localize `(HCF_delta)` further to the near band (24), since that is where the Dirichlet kernel has size `\asymp L`; but the full form above is cleaner and contains exactly the two-characteristic signed cancellation that is missing.  In `E_p` language it asks for cancellation after **both** Ramanujan projections `\sum_{y\ne0}` and `\sum_{z\ne0}`, with the common rational frequency constrained by `uq-vp`.

No theorem presently checked in `proof.tex` implies `(HCF_delta)`:

* `Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}` determines the total coefficient energy in (8), but supplies no cross-prime phase information;
* `\max_a N_p(a)\ll p^{3/4}` and `E(p)\ll p^{7/4}` control vertical value collisions, with (16) locating that gain at `u=0`;
* the Apéry Picard--Fuchs/Hasse interpretation is single-characteristic and does not relate `(p,u)` to `(q,v)`;
* palindromic symmetry only puts each `F_p(u)` on a known real line after a phase rotation; it does not prove cancellation between different primes;
* Lucas turns an incidence into an actual divisor of `b_m`, but Lemma 8 shows that height has too much slack in the parabolic range.

So `(HCF_delta)` is not a hidden consequence of a vertical Weil estimate.  It is precisely a two-characteristic horizontal theorem.

# 8. What is and is not proved

The dependency ledger is:

```text
Apéry recurrence
  -> gap-polynomial/nonvanishing certificates
  -> Z(p) << p^(2/3)
  -> sum_{p~P} Z(p)/p << P^(2/3)/log P

exact zero detector / Ramanujan kernel
  -> F_p(u) = p^(-1) sum_y E_p(y,u)
  -> exact Parseval mass rho_p(1-rho_p)

prime-denominator spacing
  + additive large sieve
  -> V(I) <= (L+4P^2) sum rho_p(1-rho_p)
  -> V(I) << (1+P^2/L) T              [L >= 4P]

critical L ~ P^(5/3)
  -> V(I) << P^(1/3) T                 [unconditional]

power saving P^(1/3-delta) T
  <- requires HCF_delta                 [OPEN]
```

The value-collision theorem adds

```text
E(p) << p^(7/4)
  -> sum_y |E_p(y,0)|^2 << p^(11/4),
```

but the centered common-frequency problem is `u!=0`, so this arrow does not enter the horizontal closing chain.

## Final answer to the question

1. **Yes**, on intervals whose **length** is comparable to `P^2`, one already has the much stronger unconditional bound `V(I)=O(T)`; no special horizontal Apéry theorem is needed there.
2. **At the critical shorter length `L\asymp P^{5/3}`**, the sharpest theorem currently provable from the checked recurrence/finite-field material is
   \[
   V(I)\ll P^{1/3}T,
   \]
   together with the absolute Apéry-specific estimate
   \[
   V(I)\ll(L+P^2)P^{2/3}/\log P.
   \]
3. There is **no proved `delta>0`** at that critical length.  I do not know an actual-Apéry counterexample, and the manuscript's data strongly predict cancellation rather than failure.
4. The parabolic restriction is arithmetically meaningful: by Lucas, simultaneous incidences force the corresponding product of primes to divide the same `b_m`.  But the exact height bound shows that this fact alone is quantitatively vacuous at `m\ll P^2`.
5. The single missing estimate is `(HCF_delta)`, equivalently a power-saving bound for the signed near-diagonal `uq-vp=O(P^2/L)` part of (22)--(23).  That is the precise point at which a CHLS/operator attack would have to produce new information.

No generic sheaf slogan, vertical Weil bound, or unrestricted CRT counterexample closes or refutes this estimate.
