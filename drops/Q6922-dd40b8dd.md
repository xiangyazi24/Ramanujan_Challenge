## 1. Source conventions and the adjoint audit

The relevant source is Spencer Bloch and Masha Vlasenko, *Gamma functions, monodromy and Frobenius constants*, arXiv:1908.07501, published in *Communications in Number Theory and Physics* 15 (2021), 91--147.

The Apéry operator in their Example 29 is

```text
L = D^3 - t P(D) + t^2(D+1)^3,

P(x)=34x^3+51x^2+27x+5
    =(2x+1)(17x^2+17x+5).
```

The factorization immediately gives

```text
P(-x-1)=-P(x).                                      (1.1)
```

BV Theorem 30 relates Frobenius constants for an operator to a gamma function associated with the formal adjoint.  This is a genuine point that should not be skipped.  In the present case it is harmless, because direct calculation gives anti-self-adjointness.

Using

```text
D^vee=-D,
(t A(D))^vee=A(-D)t=t A(-D-1),
A(D)t^m=t^m A(D+m),
```

one obtains

```text
(D^3)^vee = -D^3,

(-tP(D))^vee
 = -P(-D)t
 = -tP(-D-1)
 = +tP(D),

(t^2(D+1)^3)^vee
 = (1-D)^3 t^2
 = t^2(-D-1)^3
 = -t^2(D+1)^3.
```

Therefore

```text
L^vee=-L.                                            (1.2)
```

Multiplying a differential operator by `-1` changes neither its solution sheaf nor its scalar gamma-function difference equation.  Thus there is no hidden adjoint sign reversal left in the Apéry specialization.  What remains to be aligned is the normalization/path, addressed below.

## 2. Proposition 8 gives exactly the proposed recurrence

BV Proposition 8 says that for

```text
L = p_0(D)+t p_1(D)+...+t^a p_a(D),
```

the associated gamma function satisfies

```text
sum_(j=0)^a p_j(-s-j) Gamma(s+j)=0.                  (2.1)
```

For the Apéry operator,

```text
p_0(x)=x^3,
p_1(x)=-P(x),
p_2(x)=(x+1)^3.
```

Hence

```text
p_0(-s)       = -s^3,
p_1(-s-1)     = -P(-s-1)=P(s),
p_2(-s-2)     = (-s-1)^3=-(s+1)^3.
```

Equation (2.1) is therefore

```text
-s^3 Gamma(s)
 +P(s) Gamma(s+1)
 -(s+1)^3 Gamma(s+2)=0.
```

Multiplying by `-1` gives

```text
s^3 Gamma(s)
 -P(s) Gamma(s+1)
 +(s+1)^3 Gamma(s+2)=0.                              (2.2)
```

Put

```text
q(s)=1-e^(-2*pi*i*s),
Phi(s)=s^3 Gamma(s)/q(s)^3.
```

Since `q(s+1)=q(s)`, substituting

```text
Gamma(s)=q(s)^3 Phi(s)/s^3
```

into (2.2) gives exactly

```text
Phi(s)
 - P(s) Phi(s+1)/(s+1)^3
 + (s+1)^3 Phi(s+2)/(s+2)^3
 = 0.                                                (2.3)
```

Thus the recurrence proposed in the question is correct, including its signs.

Define

```text
Psi(s)=Phi(s+1)/(s+1)^3.                              (2.4)
```

Then (2.3) is equivalently

```text
Phi(s)-P(s)Psi(s)+(s+1)^3 Psi(s+1)=0,                (2.5)

Phi(s+1)=(s+1)^3 Psi(s).                             (2.6)
```

These are the two first-order relations needed for the cell cancellation.

## 3. Exact proof that the corrected cell is one-periodic

Start from

```text
J(s)=s^(-3)Phi(-s)Phi(s)+s^3Psi(-s)Psi(s).           (3.1)
```

Using (2.6),

```text
J(s+1)
 = Phi(-s-1)Psi(s)
   +(s+1)^3 Psi(-s-1)Psi(s+1).                       (3.2)
```

Apply (2.5) at `-s-1`.  Since `P(-s-1)=-P(s)`, it gives

```text
Phi(-s-1)+P(s)Psi(-s-1)-s^3Psi(-s)=0,
```

or

```text
Phi(-s-1)=s^3Psi(-s)-P(s)Psi(-s-1).                 (3.3)
```

Apply (2.5) at `s`:

```text
(s+1)^3Psi(s+1)=P(s)Psi(s)-Phi(s).                  (3.4)
```

Substitution of (3.3) and (3.4) into (3.2) yields

```text
J(s+1)
 = [s^3Psi(-s)-P(s)Psi(-s-1)]Psi(s)
   +Psi(-s-1)[P(s)Psi(s)-Phi(s)]

 = s^3Psi(-s)Psi(s)-Psi(-s-1)Phi(s).                (3.5)
```

Finally, from (2.4),

```text
Psi(-s-1)=Phi(-s)/(-s)^3=-Phi(-s)/s^3.              (3.6)
```

Therefore (3.5) becomes

```text
J(s+1)
 = s^3Psi(-s)Psi(s)
   +s^(-3)Phi(-s)Phi(s)
 = J(s).                                             (3.7)
```

This cancellation is exact.  It also pinpoints the flaw in treating the cell as only

```text
s^(-3)Phi(-s)Phi(s):
```

that first summand is not periodic by itself.  Its shift defect is canceled precisely by the gauged companion term.

The parity is immediate:

```text
J(-s)=-J(s).                                         (3.8)
```

Equations (3.7)--(3.8) hold first on any common domain of definition and then globally by meromorphic continuation.

## 4. Pole order, regularity at one, and the exact residue

BV Corollary 33, specialized to the Apéry case `r=3,d=0`, says after the standard normalization that

```text
Phi(s)
 = s^3 Gamma(s)/(1-e^(-2*pi*i*s))^3
 = sum_(n>=0) kappa_n s^n                         (4.1)
```

near zero.  In particular,

```text
Phi(0)=kappa_0=1.                                    (4.2)
```

Write

```text
Phi(s)=1+a_1 s+a_2 s^2+O(s^3).                       (4.3)
```

Then

```text
Phi(-s)Phi(s)
 =1+(2a_2-a_1^2)s^2+O(s^4),                         (4.4)
```

and hence

```text
s^(-3)Phi(-s)Phi(s)
 =s^(-3)+(2a_2-a_1^2)s^(-1)+O(s).                   (4.5)
```

For the companion term to be `O(s^3)`, one needs `Psi` regular at zero, equivalently `Phi` regular at one.  This is true, but it is not a consequence of the local Taylor statement (4.1) alone.  There are two source-level ways to justify it:

1. BV Remark 32 gives the global meromorphic Frobenius-constant function and explicitly records the finite value

```text
kappa(1)=zeta(3)/6.
```

2. BV Proposition 15 gives a holomorphic Mellin representation for `Phi` on a far-right half-plane, including the positive integers there; recurrence (2.3) then propagates holomorphy left through the positive real axis.

Thus

```text
s^3Psi(-s)Psi(s)=O(s^3),                             (4.6)
```

so `J` has an exact cubic pole at zero with leading coefficient one.  By (3.7), the same exact pole order and leading coefficient occur at every integer.

The companion term contributes no principal part at zero.  Therefore

```text
Res_(s=0) J(s)
 =2a_2-a_1^2
 =Phi''(0)-Phi'(0)^2
 =(log Phi)''(0).                                     (4.7)
```

BV Example 29 gives the exact Apéry Frobenius constants

```text
kappa_0=1,
kappa_1=0,
kappa_2=-pi^2/3,
kappa_3=17*zeta(3)/6.
```

Equivalently, BV Remark 32 writes

```text
Phi(s)=kappa(s)
 =1-(pi^2/3)s^2+(17*zeta(3)/6)s^3+... .              (4.8)
```

Consequently

```text
Res_(s=0)J(s)
 =2*(-pi^2/3)-0
 =-2*pi^2/3.                                         (4.9)
```

A conservative source citation should say that Corollary 33 supplies the gamma/Frobenius generating-function relation, while the numerical value `kappa_2=-pi^2/3` is the separate exact Apéry computation recorded in Example 29 / Remark 32.  Corollary 33 alone does not compute that coefficient.

## 5. Pole set and type-zero growth

### 5.1 No noninteger poles

The BV gamma function `Gamma(s)` is entire.  Thus

```text
Phi(s)=s^3Gamma(s)/q(s)^3
```

can have poles only at the integer zeros of `q(s)`, of order at most three.  The same is true of the shifted function `Psi`.  Therefore `J` has no noninteger pole orbit.  Since its exact behavior at zero is known and `J` is one-periodic, the complete pole set is the integer lattice, with exact order three.

### 5.2 Proposition 15 on the direct positive-real path

BV Proposition 15 gives, for the direct path from `0` to the positive real reflection point `c_0=17-12*sqrt(2)` and for `Re(s)` sufficiently large,

```text
Gamma(s)
 = C R(e^(-2*pi*i*s))
     integral_0^(c_0) f(t)t^(s-1) dt.                 (5.1)
```

In the Apéry `r=3,d=0` case the minimal annihilator is, up to a nonzero normalization,

```text
R(T)=(1-T)^3.                                         (5.2)
```

With the direct-path representative and the normalization `Phi(0)=1`, (5.1) becomes

```text
Phi(s)
 = C' s^3 integral_0^(c_0) f(t)t^(s-1) dt.            (5.3)
```

On the positive real path use the real logarithm, so

```text
|t^(sigma+i*tau-1)|=t^(sigma-1).                      (5.4)
```

For any sufficiently far-right fixed strip of width at least two, Proposition 15's endpoint integrability therefore gives a bound independent of `tau` for the integral in (5.3).  Hence

```text
Phi(s)=O((1+|Im(s)|)^3)                               (5.5)
```

uniformly on that strip.

The width-two point matters: recurrence (2.3) uses both `Phi(s+1)` and `Phi(s+2)`.  Solving it backwards gives

```text
Phi(s)
 = P(s)/(s+1)^3 Phi(s+1)
   -(s+1)^3/(s+2)^3 Phi(s+2).                         (5.6)
```

On any fixed vertical strip, for sufficiently large `|Im(s)|`, both rational coefficients in (5.6) are uniformly bounded.  A finite number of backward shifts therefore propagates (5.5) to every fixed strip needed for `s`, `-s`, and their unit translates.  In particular,

```text
Phi(s)=O((1+|Im(s)|)^3),
Psi(s)=O(1)                                           (5.7)
```

on vertical tails in a fundamental strip, and the same bounds hold at `-s`.  Consequently

```text
J(s)=O((1+|Im(s)|)^3)                                (5.8)
```

away from fixed small neighborhoods of its integer poles.

After subtracting the periodic principal-part model, the remainder is entire, one-periodic, and polynomially bounded on a fundamental strip.  Its nonzero Fourier coefficients vanish by shifting the integration line to the appropriate vertical infinity.  This is exactly the `type 0` input used in the periodic-meromorphic classification.

### 5.3 Path and generator caveats

The positive-real/direct-path qualification is load-bearing for the simple estimate (5.4).  A path with nonzero argument or winding can introduce factors of the form

```text
exp(-Im(s)*arg(t))
```

and therefore apparent positive exponential type.

BV gamma functions also have the usual generator ambiguity

```text
Gamma(s) -> lambda*e^(2*pi*i*m*s)Gamma(s),

m in Z.
```

The direct positive-real path selects the `m=0` representative used in (5.3).  In any event, the cell itself is invariant under the exponential gauge: the factors at `s` and `-s` cancel.  A scalar `lambda` multiplies `J` by `lambda^2`, and `Phi(0)=1` fixes that scalar.

Thus there is no branch obstruction to `J`; one must merely state which representative is used when asserting polynomial growth for `Phi` separately.

## 6. The missing but explicit BV--repository Jost identification

This is the only point at which I would not simply write “proved” unless the repository already contains the lemma.

### 6.1 The repository's dominant solution is the BV Frobenius coefficient sequence

The repository defines

```text
F_0(s)=1,
F_1(s)=P(s)/(s+1)^3,

(s+n+1)^3 F_(n+1)(s)
 =P(s+n)F_n(s)-(s+n)^3F_(n-1)(s).                    (6.1)
```

If

```text
u(t,s)=t^s sum_(n>=0) a_n(s)t^n
```

is the normalized Frobenius series for `L`, coefficient extraction from `L u=0` gives exactly (6.1), with the same initial conditions.  Hence

```text
a_n(s)=F_n(s).                                        (6.2)
```

At `s=0`,

```text
F_n(0)=b_n.                                           (6.3)
```

Assume the locally uniform dominant Jost limit used in the repository,

```text
phi_J(s)=lim_(n->infinity) F_n(s)/b_n.                (6.4)
```

### 6.2 The gauge is `c_0^s`

BV Lemma 24 identifies the Frobenius constants for the direct path to the closest positive reflection point `c_0` with the asymptotic coefficient-ratio constants.  In generating-function form, its logarithmic convolution is multiplication by `c_0^s`.  Thus the precise relation is

```text
Phi(s)=kappa(s)=c_0^s phi_J(s),

c_0=17-12*sqrt(2).                                    (6.5)
```

The direction of the gauge is important.  Since `kappa_1=0`, (6.5) predicts

```text
phi_J'(0)=-log(c_0)=log(17+12*sqrt(2)),
```

which is the expected dominant-root connection term.

### 6.3 The companion has the same gauge

The exact finite identity in the repository is

```text
G_n(s)=F_(n-1)(s+1)/(s+1)^3.                          (6.6)
```

Since

```text
b_(n-1)/b_n -> c_0,                                   (6.7)
```

the companion Jost limit is

```text
gamma_J(s)
 =lim G_n(s)/b_n
 =c_0 phi_J(s+1)/(s+1)^3.                             (6.8)
```

On the BV side,

```text
Psi(s)
 =Phi(s+1)/(s+1)^3
 =c_0^(s+1)phi_J(s+1)/(s+1)^3
 =c_0^s gamma_J(s).                                   (6.9)
```

Therefore the gauge cancels in both products:

```text
Phi(-s)Phi(s)=phi_J(-s)phi_J(s),
Psi(-s)Psi(s)=gamma_J(-s)gamma_J(s).                  (6.10)
```

So the repository's actual limiting cell

```text
s^(-3)phi_J(-s)phi_J(s)
 +s^3 gamma_J(-s)gamma_J(s)                           (6.11)
```

is exactly the BV cell (3.1), not merely proportional to it.

This is the clean bridge that was absent from the earlier one-term audit.  To make it fully formal, the repository needs:

```text
[BV-JOST-ID]

1. F_n(s) is the normalized BV Frobenius coefficient a_n(s).
2. F_n(s)/b_n converges locally uniformly to phi_J(s).
3. BV Lemma 24 applies to the same direct path and normalization.
4. Phi(s)=c_0^s phi_J(s).
5. G_n(s)=F_(n-1)(s+1)/(s+1)^3 and hence
   Psi(s)=c_0^s gamma_J(s).
```

Items 1 and 5 are exact algebra.  Item 4 is the direct generating-function content of BV Lemma 24 once items 2--3 are aligned.  Local uniform convergence through two derivatives is enough for the residue calculation; full local uniform convergence gives the meromorphic cell identity.

Corollary 33 alone does not prove `[BV-JOST-ID]`: it identifies the BV gamma function with the Frobenius-constant generating series, not automatically with an independently constructed Poincare--Perron/Jost limit.

## 7. Periodic-meromorphic classification and the final closed form

Let

```text
A(s)=pi^3 cot(pi s)csc^2(pi s).
```

It is odd and one-periodic, has a cubic pole with leading coefficient one at every integer, and has zero residue.  Since `J` has residue

```text
c=-2*pi^2/3,                                          (7.1)
```

the function

```text
E(s)=J(s)-A(s)-c*pi*cot(pi s)                         (7.2)
```

is entire, odd, one-periodic, and polynomially bounded in a fundamental vertical strip.  The Fourier-shift argument forces `E` to be constant, and oddness forces that constant to be zero.  Therefore

```text
J(s)
 =pi^3 cot(pi s)csc^2(pi s)
  -(2*pi^3/3)cot(pi s).                               (7.3)
```

Equivalently,

```text
J(s)
 =(pi^3/3)
   cos(pi s)(cos(2*pi s)+2)/sin^3(pi s).              (7.4)
```

This also preserves the algebraic zero-location correction from Q6880.  The nonreal zeros satisfy

```text
cos(2*pi*s)=-2,
```

so on `Re(s)=1/2` their height is

```text
|Im(s)|=arcosh(2)/(2*pi)
       =log(2+sqrt(3))/(2*pi),                        (7.5)
```

not `log(2)/(2*pi)`.
