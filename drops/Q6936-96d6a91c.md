# Verdict

There is an exact nonnegative two-dimensional second-kind kernel with phase
maximum

```text
rho = 17 - 12 sqrt(2),
```

namely the square of the Legendre second-kind solution at `x=3`.  However,
that kernel belongs to the symmetric-square Legendre/Delannoy module, not to
the actual normalized P2.5 module.

For the specific polynomial

```text
H_n(z) = 4F3(n+4,n+4,-n-1/2,-n; 7/2,7/2,4; z),
```

I obtain a sharp obstruction to the requested direct construction:

1. `H_n` has a genuine Jacobi Rodrigues core, but the remaining diagonal
   multiplier is **not** a positive moment sequence; its first `2 x 2` Hankel
   determinant is strictly negative for every `n >= 0`.
2. Consequently no representation of the natural form

   ```text
   H_n(z) = integral J_n(-zt) dmu_n(t),   dmu_n >= 0,
   ```

   exists, for either possible terminating Jacobi factor.
   The same obstruction excludes a nonnegative two-variable product kernel
   depending only on `t1*t2`.
3. The monic `H_n` sequence is not a scalar orthogonal-polynomial sequence:
   its multiplication recurrence is not three-term.
4. It is not even the step-line type-II family for two fixed measures: the
   exact `n=3` multiplication formula has a forbidden `H_0` term.
5. The direct positive coefficient saddle of `H_n(1)` is the dominant branch
   `17+12 sqrt(2)`, while the positive tangent tail has the stated rate `16`.
   A nonnegative Euler/Beta averaging cannot turn either into the reciprocal
   branch `rho`; two cancellations are required.

Thus the answer is:

> **No single positive Euler/Markov transform of `H_n` can produce the actual
> minimal P2.5 solution.**  The smallest viable structure is a rank-three
> system with two independent Cauchy channels—equivalently a signed or
> determinantal two-functional construction—after a nontrivial contiguous or
> coboundary transformation.  `H_n` itself is not the required two-weight
> step-line polynomial.

The exact calculations and a standalone SymPy verifier are below.

# 1. The classical part is Jacobi, not Racah/Wilson

A Racah or Wilson polynomial is a terminating `4F3(1)` in which the polynomial
variable occurs in an opposite upper-parameter pair such as

```text
a+ix, a-ix
```

or

```text
-x, x+constant.
```

Here `z` is the hypergeometric argument, not an upper parameter.  Therefore
`H_n(z)` is not a Racah/Wilson polynomial in `z`.  The standard definitions are
recorded in DLMF §18.25 and §18.26.

There are nevertheless two natural terminating Gauss factors, corresponding
to the two distinct lower parameters `4` and `7/2`.

## 1.1 The `c=4` Jacobi factor

Define

```text
J_n^(4)(z) = 2F1(-n,n+4;4;z).
```

Then

```text
J_n^(4)(z)
 = n!/(4)_n P_n^(3,0)(1-2z)
 = z^(-3)/(4)_n d^n/dz^n [ z^(n+3)(1-z)^n ].       (1.1)
```

## 1.2 The `c=7/2` Jacobi factor

Define

```text
J_n^(7/2)(z) = 2F1(-n,n+4;7/2;z).
```

Then

```text
J_n^(7/2)(z)
 = n!/(7/2)_n P_n^(5/2,1/2)(1-2z),                (1.2)
```

with the usual Jacobi Rodrigues formula and positive weight

```text
z^(5/2) (1-z)^(1/2) dz,   0<z<1.
```

So the question is not whether a Jacobi core exists—it does.  The issue is
whether the remaining hypergeometric factors can be supplied by a positive
Euler moment.

# 2. Exact one-dimensional positive-moment obstruction

Write

```text
H_n(z) = sum_{k=0}^n h_{n,k} z^k.
```

## 2.1 Attempt based on `J_n^(4)`

The coefficient of `z^k` in `J_n^(4)(-z)` is positive.  If there were a
positive measure `mu_n` such that

```text
H_n(z) = integral J_n^(4)(-zt) dmu_n(t),           (2.1)
```

then coefficient comparison would force its moments to be

```text
mu_{n,k}
 = (-1)^k (-n-1/2)_k (n+4)_k / (7/2)_k^2.         (2.2)
```

In particular,

```text
mu_{n,0} = 1.
```

For any positive measure on the real line, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

```text
mu_0 mu_2 - mu_1^2 >= 0.
```

Here direct simplification gives

```text
mu_{n,0} mu_{n,2} - mu_{n,1}^2
 = -4 (n+4)(2n+1)(64n^2+288n+569) / 194481
 < 0                                                     (2.3)
```

for every natural `n`.

Hence (2.1) is impossible.

## 2.2 Attempt based on `J_n^(7/2)`

The analogous required moments are

```text
nu_{n,k}
 = (-1)^k (-n-1/2)_k (n+4)_k
   / ((7/2)_k (4)_k).                              (2.4)
```

Again,

```text
nu_{n,0} nu_{n,2} - nu_{n,1}^2
 = -(n+4)(2n+1)(34n^2+153n+320) / 35280
 < 0.                                               (2.5)
```

Thus the other natural Jacobi Euler lift is also impossible.

## 2.3 Scope of the obstruction

Equations (2.3) and (2.5) rule out any nonnegative kernel of the form

```text
integral J_n(-z T(omega)) dmu(omega),   dmu >= 0,
```

because the coefficients would be moments of the real random variable
`T(omega)` and its first Hankel determinant would have to be nonnegative.

They also rule out a nonnegative two-variable kernel of the product form

```text
integral integral J_n(-z x y) w(x,y) dx dy,
w(x,y) >= 0,                                      (2.6)
```

since `xy` again defines one scalar positive moment sequence.

This is a precise obstruction to a **single positive Euler/Beta integral**.
It does not rule out a signed measure, a matrix-valued measure, or a
multiple-Cauchy determinant.

The `2 x 2` Hankel matrices in (2.3)--(2.5) have negative determinant and
positive upper-left entry, hence signature `(1,1)`.  Therefore at least two
positive channels are needed even to realize the required diagonal multiplier
as a difference/determinant of positive moments.

# 3. Exact beta--Hadamard representation

Although a positive scalar Euler transform fails, there is a completely
explicit no-Barnes representation that shows exactly where the sign/cancellation
enters.

Put

```text
F_n(z) = 2F1(-n,n+4;4;z),

G_n^[n](z)
 = sum_{k=0}^n
     (-n-1/2)_k (n+4)_k / ((7/2)_k k!) z^k.
```

Let `odot` denote coefficientwise (Hadamard) multiplication.  Since

```text
(5/2) integral_0^1 t^k (1-t)^(3/2) dt
 = k!/(7/2)_k,
```

coefficient comparison gives the exact identity

```text
H_n(z)
 = (5/2) integral_0^1 (1-t)^(3/2)
     (F_n odot G_n^[n])(zt) dt.                  (3.1)
```

The remaining Hadamard product is a Cauchy projection:

```text
(F odot G)(w)
 = (1/(2 pi i)) contour F(xi) G(w/xi) dxi/xi.    (3.2)
```

Thus a direct Euler reduction of `H_n` naturally gives one positive beta
variable **plus an oscillatory/complex Cauchy variable**, not a nonnegative
one- or two-dimensional kernel.  This agrees exactly with the negative Hankel
determinants above.

# 4. `H_n` is not a scalar or two-weight orthogonal family

Let `P_n(z)` be the monic normalization of `H_n(z)`.

For a scalar quasi-definite orthogonality functional, Favard's theorem requires

```text
z P_n in span(P_{n+1},P_n,P_{n-1}).
```

But exact arithmetic gives

```text
z P_2
 = P_3
   - (2284/1323) P_2
   + (820693/307328) P_1
   - (869527/352800) P_0.                       (4.1)
```

The nonzero `P_0` coefficient rules out scalar orthogonality, even for an
indefinite scalar functional.

For a normal step-line type-II family for two fixed measures, the recurrence is
four-term:

```text
z P_n in span(P_{n+1},P_n,P_{n-1},P_{n-2}).
```

At `n=3` this span does not contain `P_0`, whereas

```text
z P_3
 = P_4
   - (6653/3267) P_3
   + (56735303/11664000) P_2
   - (19148431/2116800) P_1
   + (2354197243/272160000) P_0.               (4.2)
```

So `H_n` itself is not the required two-measure step-line polynomial.

For completeness, the `n=4` formula also has

```text
[P_0] zP_4 = -169070489377/3920400000 != 0,    (4.3)
```

which rules out a standard three-measure/five-term step-line interpretation.
The verifier below checks the full multiplication rows through any requested
finite height; through `n=8` every lower-degree component is nonzero.  That
finite observation is not asserted as an all-`n` theorem.

This matters structurally: an eventual two-functional Markov representation of
the **actual P2.5 recurrence** cannot use `H_n` directly.  It must first apply a
nontrivial contiguous/coboundary transformation.

# 5. Exact dominant saddle of the positive polynomial

The coefficients satisfy

```text
h_{n,k+1}/h_{n,k}
 = (n-k)(n+1/2-k)(n+k+4)^2
   / ((k+7/2)^2(k+4)(k+1)).                    (5.1)
```

For `k/n -> s` with `0<s<1`,

```text
h_{n,k+1}/h_{n,k}
 -> ((1-s^2)/s^2)^2.                           (5.2)
```

The associated logarithmic coefficient phase is

```text
Phi(s)
 = 4 atanh(s) + 2s log((1-s^2)/s^2),          (5.3)
```

because `Phi'(s)` is the logarithm of (5.2).  The unique interior maximum is

```text
s_* = 1/sqrt(2).
```

At this point the logarithmic term in (5.3) vanishes and

```text
exp(Phi(s_*))
 = ((1+s_*)/(1-s_*))^2
 = (3+2sqrt(2))^2
 = 17+12sqrt(2).                               (5.4)
```

Thus the coefficientwise positive polynomial selects the dominant branch,
not the minimal branch.

The upper-parameter tangent tail in the question is also coefficientwise
nonnegative and has exact scalar rate `16`.  Any representation obtained by
integrating that tail against a fixed nonzero nonnegative Euler/Beta kernel
retains at least that rate by the elementary lower-bound half of Laplace's
principle.  It cannot have rate

```text
17-12sqrt(2) < 1.
```

Therefore the desired minimal solution necessarily uses cancellation.  This is
not a technical failure of a particular beta substitution; it is forced by
positivity.

# 6. The concrete positive two-dimensional kernel with the correct phase

There is a canonical second-kind model with exactly the required phase.
Let `q_n` be the real Legendre second-kind solution at `x=3`, normalized by

```text
q_0 = (1/2) log 2,
q_1 = (3/2) log 2 - 1,

(n+1)q_{n+1} = 3(2n+1)q_n - n q_{n-1}.        (6.1)
```

For every `n >= 0`, the classical positive integral is

```text
q_n
 = integral_0^infinity
     (3 + 2sqrt(2) cosh u)^(-n-1) du.          (6.2)
```

This is the integer-degree specialization of the standard Legendre-Q integral
representation; see DLMF §14.25.

Squaring gives the explicit nonnegative two-dimensional kernel

```text
q_n^2
 = integral_0^infinity integral_0^infinity
     [1 / ((3+2sqrt(2)cosh u)
           (3+2sqrt(2)cosh v))]^(n+1)
     du dv.                                    (6.3)
```

Its phase is

```text
Psi(u,v)
 = 1 / ((3+2sqrt(2)cosh u)
        (3+2sqrt(2)cosh v)).                   (6.4)
```

Since `cosh u >= 1`, the unique maximum is at `(u,v)=(0,0)` and

```text
max Psi
 = 1/(3+2sqrt(2))^2
 = (3-2sqrt(2))^2
 = 17-12sqrt(2).                               (6.5)
```

So (6.3) is exactly the requested kind of positive kernel and phase geometry.

Moreover `U_n=q_n^2` satisfies the exact symmetric-square recurrence

```text
(n+3)^2(2n+3) U_{n+3}
 -(2n+5)(35n^2+140n+131) U_{n+2}
 +(2n+3)(35n^2+140n+131) U_{n+1}
 -(2n+5)(n+1)^2 U_n
 = 0.                                           (6.6)
```

Its Poincare roots are precisely

```text
17+12sqrt(2), 1, 17-12sqrt(2).
```

This explains why the Legendre model is so compelling.

# 7. Why the positive Legendre kernel is not the P2.5 minimal solution

Shared Poincare roots are not enough.  Substitute `U_n=q_n^2` into the exact
normalized order-three Ore operator from Q6920.  At `n=0`, with `L=log 2`, the
residual is

```text
-139117152291882
+401868330022836 L
-290234535701562 L^2.                          (7.1)
```

This is nonzero: `log 2` is transcendental and the displayed polynomial is not
the zero polynomial.

Even an affine scalar prefactor does not repair the mismatch.  For

```text
y_n = (a n+b) q_n^2,
```

the coefficients of `1` and `L` in the `n=0` Ore residual give a `2 x 2`
linear system in `(a,b)` with determinant

```text
-260951107796329865036945760 != 0.             (7.2)
```

Hence `a=b=0` is the only affine-gauge solution.

The repository's earlier exact determinant/formal-exponent analysis gives the
same structural message at matrix level: the P2.5 module is not the natural
rational symmetric-square Delannoy gauge.  Formula (6.3) is therefore a model
with the correct phase, not the hidden P2.5 minimal solution.

# 8. Minimal structure still possible

The obstructions delimit the viable construction sharply.

## What is impossible

- one positive scalar Euler/Beta moment applied to either natural Jacobi core;
- a positive two-variable kernel depending only on one product variable;
- a scalar fixed-measure Markov function for `H_n`;
- a two-fixed-measure step-line representation with `H_n` itself as the type-II
  polynomial;
- the unmodified positive Legendre-Q square.

## What remains possible

A successful no-Barnes construction must have at least two independent moment
channels and one cancellation determinant.  Concretely, the smallest form is a
rank-three fundamental vector

```text
(P_n(z), Q_n^(0)(z), Q_n^(1)(z)),

Q_n^(j)(z) = integral P_n(t)/(z-t) dmu_j(t),
```

or an equivalent mixed-type/matrix-valued moment system.  The minimal scalar is
then a `2 x 2` minor that cancels the dominant and neutral channels, leaving the
`rho` channel.

The negative Hankel determinant proves that the two channels cannot collapse to
one nonnegative measure.  The order-three P2.5 recurrence proves that two
channels are the minimum system dimension compatible with its three modes.
But equations (4.1)--(4.2) prove that the polynomial entering this system is not
`H_n` without a nontrivial contiguous/coboundary change of basis.

So the smallest concrete next ansatz is:

```text
1. start from the Jacobi Rodrigues polynomial J_n^(7/2);
2. introduce a 2 x 2 matrix-valued or signed moment functional realizing the
   diagonal multiplier (2.4);
3. solve for a contiguous 3 x 3 transformation whose first component is the
   actual normalized P2.5 scalar recurrence;
4. take the two-channel Cauchy minor and seek a real-domain deformation to a
   kernel whose phase is (6.4).
```

A plain positive Euler integral cannot skip steps 2--3.

def H(N):
    return s.expand(sum(
        s.rf(-N, j) * s.rf(-N-s.Rational(1,2), j)
        * s.rf(N+4, j)**2
        / (s.rf(s.Rational(7,2), j)**2 * s.rf(4, j) * s.factorial(j))
        * z**j
        for j in range(N+1)
    ))


def monic_H(N):
    p = H(N)
    return s.expand(p / s.Poly(p, z).LC())

    """Coefficients c_j in z P_N = sum_{j=0}^{N+1} c_j P_j."""

        cs[j]*basis[j] for j in range(N+2))), z)
    equations = [s.Eq(c, 0) for c in residual.all_coeffs()]

# Final conclusion

A positive two-dimensional kernel with the desired saddle exists explicitly,
but it is `Q_n(3)^2` and solves the wrong symmetric-square recurrence.  For the
actual hypergeometric polynomial `H_n`, the first Hankel determinant proves
that no direct nonnegative Jacobi/Euler lift exists.  The multiplication
recurrences further prove that `H_n` is neither a scalar orthogonal family nor
the two-weight step-line family needed for a rank-three Markov system.

Therefore the actual P2.5 minimal solution cannot be obtained from one positive
Euler integral for `H_n`.  It requires at least two independent Cauchy/moment
channels with a signed or determinantal cancellation after a nontrivial
contiguous/coboundary transformation.
