# 2. Why the global \(\Delta^2\) factor is impossible

Set

\[
H_r(Y)=\prod_{i=0}^{r}(Y-\lambda_i),
\qquad
K(Y)=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_i)=H_r(Y)T_r(Y).
\]

The folded field identity is

\[
\boxed{
z^p-z=\frac{u(z)}2K(\lambda_z).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Also

\[
4\lambda_z+1=u(z)^2,
\qquad 4\lambda_m+1=0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_z-\lambda_m=\frac{u(z)^2}{4}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Because

\[
M(Y)=(Y-\lambda_m)H_r(Y)T_r(Y)^2,
\]

substitution gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 uM(\lambda_z)
 &=u\frac{u^2}{4}H_r(\lambda_z)T_r(\lambda_z)^2\\
 &=\frac{u^2}{2}T_r(\lambda_z)
   \left(\frac u2H_r(\lambda_z)T_r(\lambda_z)\right)\\
 &=\frac{u^2}{2}T_r(\lambda_z)(z^p-z),
\end{aligned}
\]

which is (0.4).  Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{u(z)M(\lambda_z)}{(z^p-z)^2}
 =\frac{u(z)}{H_r(\lambda_z)}
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

as a rational function.  At the simple lower/target roots, the right side has a pole unless \(W_V\) contributes an unrelated cancellation.  A second global copy of \(z^p-z\) therefore cannot follow from the actual modulus.

The same factorization gives the orders immediately:

* at \(z=r\), \(u(r)\ne0\) and \(T_r(\lambda_r)\ne0\), hence one zero;
* at \(z=r+1<m\), \(T_r(\lambda_z)\) gives one extra unramified zero, hence two;
* at \(z=m\), \(u^2\) gives two zeros on top of the one zero of \(z^p-z\), hence three.

# 3. Quotient calculus with all denominator factors

Write

\[
R(Y)=M(Y)W_V(Y),
\qquad
D(z)=v_{z-1}v_zv_{z+1},
\]

and

\[
F(z)=\mathcal L(t/V)(z)=\frac{N(z)}{D(z)},
\qquad
N(z)=u(z)R(\lambda_z).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The denominators are nonzero at the nodes below because \(AV\equiv1\pmod M\) makes \(V\) nonzero at every root of \(M\), and all three folded neighbors occurring below are such roots.

Since

\[
\lambda'=u,\qquad u'=2,\qquad \lambda''=2,\qquad \lambda'''=0,
\]

at any point where \(R(\lambda_z)=0\),

\[
\boxed{N'=u^2R',}
\tag{3.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{N''=u^3R''+6uR',}
\tag{3.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{N'''=u^4R'''+12u^2R''+12R'.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Here all \(R\)-derivatives are \(Y\)-derivatives evaluated at \(Y=\lambda_z\).

Put

\[
D_j=v_{j-1}v_jv_{j+1},\qquad a_j=2j+1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D'_j={}&a_{j-1}V'(\lambda_{j-1})v_jv_{j+1}\\
&+a_jV'(\lambda_j)v_{j-1}v_{j+1}\\
&+a_{j+1}V'(\lambda_{j+1})v_{j-1}v_j.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.5}
\]

If \(N(j)=0\),

\[
F'(j)=\frac{N'(j)}{D_j},
\tag{3.6}
\]

\[
F''(j)=\frac{N''(j)}{D_j}
       -2\frac{N'(j)D'_j}{D_j^2}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

If also \(N'(j)=0\),

\[
\boxed{
F'''(j)=\frac{N'''(j)}{D_j}
        -3\frac{N''(j)D'_j}{D_j^2}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

These are the only quotient-rule identities needed below.

\[

F'(r)

\tag{4.1}

\[
\boxed{

# 7. Does the third jet give an equation independent of \(AV-1=MS\)?

Not in the sense needed for the descent.

There are two parallel quotient factorizations.

## 7.1 Riccati quotient

After the established divisibility,

\[
R_V=MW_V.
\]

At a doubled root \(x\),

\[
R_V''(x)=M''(x)W_V(x),
\]

\[
\boxed{
R_V'''(x)=M'''(x)W_V(x)+3M''(x)W_V'(x).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

So the second and third jets successively recover the value and derivative of the quotient \(W_V\).

## 7.2 Bezout quotient

Likewise

\[
B:=AV-1=MS.
\]

At a doubled root,

\[
B''(x)=M''(x)S(x),
\]

\[
\boxed{
B'''(x)=M'''(x)S(x)+3M''(x)S'(x).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
S(x)=\frac{B''(x)}{M''(x)},
\qquad
S'(x)=\frac{B'''(x)-M'''(x)S(x)}{3M''(x)}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

At a simple root \(y\),

\[
\boxed{
S(y)=\frac{B'(y)}{M'(y)},
\qquad
S'(y)=\frac{B''(y)-M''(y)S(y)}{2M'(y)}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

There is a logical distinction worth keeping precise:

* the WZ identity is extra structure beyond the bare congruence \(AV\equiv1\pmod M\), so a differentiated WZ identity is not literally a formal consequence of the congruence alone;
* but in (0.1), \(W_V\) is the quotient of the folded Riccati numerator by \(M\), so differentiating (0.1) merely evaluates the next coefficient of that quotient;
* after the corresponding differentiated WZ/Bezout equation is written, the new unknowns are precisely the next quotient jets \(W_V'\) and \(S'\).  One equation solves for one quotient jet.  It does not create a homogeneous zero.

Therefore the third jet is **not an independent missing boundary equation** of the sort Q1594 needed.  To obtain a contradiction one would need an additional relation eliminating \(W_V'\) or \(S'\) without defining it.  The false global \(\Delta^2\) factor was doing that job implicitly; once it is removed, the descent stops.

# 13. Reusable corrected theorem

> **Full-inverse pulled-back Riccati jet theorem.**  Let \(p=2m+1\ge5\), \(0\le r<m\), \(\lambda_z=z(z+1)\), \(u=2z+1\), and
> \[
> M=(Y-\lambda_m)\prod_{i=0}^{r}(Y-\lambda_i)
>                 \prod_{i=r+1}^{m-1}(Y-\lambda_i)^2.
> \]
> Suppose \(V\) is nonzero at the roots of \(M\) and
> \[
> F(z)=\frac{u(z)M(\lambda_z)W(\lambda_z)}
> {V(\lambda_{z-1})V(\lambda_z)V(\lambda_{z+1})}.
> \]
> Then the pulled-back source has structural multiplicity one at the target, two at every unramified doubled tail node, and three at the midpoint.  The exact first non-forced jets are (4.1), (5.3), and (6.2), and the doubled-node third jet is (5.5).  Globally,
> \[
> uM(\lambda_z)=\frac{u^2}{2}T_r(\lambda_z)(z^p-z),
> \]
> so there is no second global copy of \(z^p-z\).  At a doubled node the third derivative determines the quotient slope \(W'\); it is not a homogeneous vanishing condition.  At the midpoint the third derivative determines only \(W(\lambda_m)\).

Applied to Problem 3.2, this theorem removes the central premise of Q1594.  The surviving higher-jet route would require a genuinely new identity relating the quotient slopes to the degree-descended combination; differentiating the existing Riccati/Bezout factorizations one more time does not provide it.
