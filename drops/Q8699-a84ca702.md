# Q8699 carrier audit

## Verdict

The Newton carrier identity is correct as a congruence selector, but the proposed height reduction and quadratic compression do not follow. The exact obstruction is that the carrier is a truncated binomial transform whose coefficients retain the dominant Apéry singularity. Changing the finite-difference center changes the presentation but not the underlying exponential type unless a new cancellation theorem is proved.

## 1. Exact shell selector

Let
\[
I_K(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{K}\binom{t}{j}\Delta^jB_0,
\qquad K=\lfloor n/4\rfloor .
\]

Newton interpolation gives the exact identity
\[
B_u=\sum_{j=0}^{u}\binom uj\Delta^jB_0
\]
for every integer \(u\ge0\). If \(p\) satisfies
\[
3n/4<p\le n,
\qquad u=n-p,
\]
then
\[
0\le u<n/4\le K,
\]
so the truncated sum is actually the full Newton sum:
\[
I_K(u)=B_u.
\]

Modulo \(p\),
\[
\binom nj\equiv\binom{n-p}{j}=\binom uj\pmod p
\]
for \(0\le j<p\), because \(u<p\) and the numerator factors differ by multiples of \(p\). Since \(K<p\), every occurring index is legal. Hence
\[
I_K(n)\equiv I_K(u)=B_u\pmod p.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{p\mid I_K(n)\iff p\mid B_{n-p}.}
\]

Endpoints: for \(p=n\) one has \(u=0\) and the same argument gives \(I_K(0)=B_0=1\); for the lower endpoint excluded by the strict inequality no extra case occurs.

## 2. Height audit

Write
\[
F(t)=\sum_{j\ge0}\Delta^jB_0\,t^j.
\]
The exponential generating relation is
\[
\sum_j\Delta^jB_0\frac{z^j}{j!}=e^{-z}\sum_mB_m\frac{z^m}{m!}.
\]
The singularities of the Apéry generating function occur at the characteristic roots
\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
\qquad \beta=17-12\sqrt2=\alpha^{-1}.
\]
The finite Newton carrier therefore inherits exponential type controlled by \(\alpha\). The truncation at \(K=n/4\) removes only the high finite-difference tail; it does not remove the dominant singularity.

Thus the sharp generic bound remains of the form
\[
\log |I_K(n)|=\Theta(n)
\]
with exponential base determined by the same Apéry singularity. A proof of a smaller base would require an explicit cancellation in the transformed coefficients, not present in Newton truncation alone.

## 3. Shifted differences \(\Delta_c=E-c\)

The proposed identity
\[
J_c(n)=\sum_{j\le K}\binom njc^{K-j}\Delta_c^jB_0
\]
comes from rewriting the Newton basis around the eigenvalue \(c\). Modulo primes \(p\nmid c\), the same Lucas/Fermat argument preserves the shell selector after clearing the common power of \(c\).

However optimizing \(c\) is a spectral problem. The transform is a change of basis for the same recurrence. The characteristic polynomial is
\[
\lambda^2-34\lambda+1,
\]
so shifting by \(c\) changes the polynomial coefficients but not the spectral radius of the underlying sequence.

In particular \(c=17\) centers the recurrence:
\[
\lambda=17\pm12\sqrt2
\]
become symmetric around zero, but the radius remains
\[
|17+12\sqrt2|.
\]
It does not reduce the coefficient growth to \(12\sqrt2\). The quantity \(12\sqrt2\) is the off-center gap, not the dominant eigenvalue.

## 4. Quadratic interpolation attempt

The operator
\[
E^2-34E+1
\]
is the characteristic operator. Over \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)\), the two roots are
\[
17\pm12\sqrt2.
\]
Splitting by Frobenius in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)\) can separate the two eigenspaces, but it does not lower the degree of the shell selector.

The reason is exact: a second-order annihilator acts on the two-dimensional recurrence state. The shell condition asks for divisibility of one scalar coordinate at many unrelated primes. After projection to an eigenspace, the remaining coefficient is still a linear form in the same state. No new prime-independent relation appears.

The saturated situation is:

- the recurrence gives a rank-two state space;
- Newton or quadratic interpolation produces linear functionals on that space;
- CRT across shell primes keeps these local conditions independent.

Therefore a quadratic carrier can only compress if it proves an additional correlation among the projected coefficients. The characteristic equation alone supplies no such relation.

## Exact residual problem

The remaining nontrivial statement would have to be a coefficient-correlation theorem of the form:

> For the bounded family of transformed coefficients produced by the Newton or quadratic basis, the values modulo shell primes satisfy a new cross-prime algebraic relation of height smaller than the generic spectral height.

This is strictly narrower than the original carrier construction. Without such a theorem, the finite-difference and quadratic transforms are reparameterizations of the Apéry two-dimensional recurrence, not new compression mechanisms.

## Final conclusion

The Newton selector is an exact and useful carrier:
\[
p\mid I_K(n)\iff p\mid B_{n-p}.
\]

But:

1. its height remains exponential with the Apéry dominant singularity;
2. choosing \(c=17\) does not replace the base by \(12\sqrt2\);
3. quadratic interpolation by \(E^2-34E+1\) is a state decomposition, not a height reduction;
4. the only possible escape is an additional bounded-coefficient correlation theorem, not contained in the recurrence or Frobenius splitting itself.
