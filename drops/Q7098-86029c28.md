# Exact boundary/content factor for shifted Apéry gap resultants

## 1. What can be extracted universally

Let

\[
S_{d,r}=\operatorname{Res}_X(N_d(X),N_r(X+d)).
\]

The correct universal factor is not obtained by removing every prime dividing a broad carrier.  The canonical factor is the part forced by the Euclidean chain and the endpoint evaluations.  Define the shifted Euclidean sequence

\[
R_0=N_r(X+d),\qquad R_1=N_d(X),
\]

and recursively

\[
R_{i+1}=\operatorname{rem}(R_{i-1},R_i),
\]

with contents removed at each step:

\[
R_{i-1}=Q_iR_i+c_iR_{i+1}.
\]

Then the resultant recursion gives

\[
\operatorname{Res}(R_{i-1},R_i)=c_i^{\deg R_{i+1}}
\operatorname{Res}(R_i,R_{i+1})
\]

(up to sign).  Therefore every forced prime power comes from the contents of the Euclidean remainders and from leading coefficient drops.  This gives an intrinsic definition

\[
C_{d,r}=\prod_i \operatorname{cont}(R_{i+1})^{e_i}
\]

where the exponents \(e_i\) are exactly the determinant exponents accumulated by the resultant recursion.

This factor is universal: it depends only on the recursion and not on accidental common roots modulo primes.

---

## 2. Endpoint contribution

For the Apéry recurrence the endpoint identity is

\[
N_h(-s)=(-1)^{s-1}b_{s-1}b_{h-s}((s-1)!(h-s)!)^3.
\]

Hence any Euclidean remainder which specializes to an endpoint produces forced factors

\[
 b_j,\qquad (j!)^3.
\]

For adjacent shifts the chain collapses completely and gives

\[
|\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+1})|
 =\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(j!^3b_j)^6.
\]

The exponent 6 is not accidental: it is the multiplicity with which the endpoint divisor occurs in the determinant recursion.

For general \((d,r)\), the same mechanism produces a product over the boundary indices hit by the Euclidean chain.  Explicitly, if the chain visits endpoint indices \(E(d,r)\), then

\[
C_{d,r}
=\prod_{j\in E(d,r)}(j!^3b_j)^{m_j(d,r)},
\]

where

\[
m_j(d,r)=v_{j}\left(\text{the determinant multiplier of the Euclidean step}
\right).
\]

This is the exact recursion-level formula. A closed form for arbitrary \((d,r)\) would require solving the full remainder pattern, which is not currently available.

---

## 3. Large primes \(p>H\)

For a prime larger than every boundary index, factorial content disappears:

\[
v_p((j!)^3)=0\qquad (j<p).
\]

Therefore the only universal contribution is from Apéry factors:

\[
v_p(C_{d,r})=\sum_{j\in E(d,r)}m_j(d,r)v_p(b_j).
\]

In particular, for \(p>H\), the boundary factor is exactly computable by evaluating the endpoint indices appearing in the Euclidean chain.

There is no legitimate reason to remove all primes in a carrier set
\(U_H\).  Such stripping removes both:

1. forced boundary factors;
2. genuine interior intersection factors.

Only the first category belongs in \(C_{d,r}\).

---

## 4. Integrality of the reduced resultant

After exact division,

\[
S_{d,r}^{\rm prim}=S_{d,r}/C_{d,r}
\]

is an integer.

Reason: every Euclidean resultant identity is an identity in \(\mathbb Z[X]\).  The content factors are precisely the integer divisors introduced by pseudo-division. Removing them leaves the primitive resultant, which lies in \(\mathbb Z\).

However, a uniform small-height bound is not automatic. The remaining height satisfies the standard resultant estimate

\[
\log |S_{d,r}^{\rm prim}|
\ll (\deg N_d+\deg N_r)\,(h(N_d)+h(N_r)),
\]

so the height is controlled polynomially/exponentially in the degrees, but not by a constant independent of \(d,r\).

---

## 5. Does an interior common root give an extra prime?

Yes, after exact boundary division.

Let \(p\) be a prime and suppose

\[
N_d(\alpha)\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad
N_r(\alpha+d)\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

where \(\alpha\) is not a boundary root generated by the Euclidean endpoint chain.

Then the two primitive polynomials have a nontrivial gcd modulo \(p\). Therefore

\[
p\mid \operatorname{Res}(N_d,N_r(X+d)).
\]

Because the boundary factor has already removed exactly the forced common divisors,

\[
p\mid S_{d,r}/C_{d,r}.
\]

This statement is valid only after exact content division. Broad carrier stripping destroys precisely this implication.

---

## 6. Multiple roots and overlap

If the common root has multiplicity \(m\), then

\[
v_p(S_{d,r})\ge m
\]

provided the leading coefficients remain nonzero modulo \(p\).

The correct algorithm must therefore compute the gcd chain modulo \(p\), not merely test whether \(p\) divides a resultant.

Overlaps between boundary factors are handled by the Euclidean multiplicities \(m_j(d,r)\). One must not multiply endpoint formulas independently, because the same endpoint divisor can appear in several remainder stages.

---

## 7. Leading coefficient drops

If the leading coefficient of some remainder vanishes modulo \(p\), the naive resultant recursion loses information. The repair is standard subresultant theory:

1. compute the primitive subresultant sequence;
2. record each leading-coefficient valuation;
3. include those valuations in \(C_{d,r}\).

Thus the certified factor is

\[
C_{d,r}=C_{\rm endpoint}C_{\rm content}C_{\rm lc},
\]

where:

- \(C_{\rm endpoint}\) comes from the Apéry endpoint identities;
- \(C_{\rm content}\) comes from pseudo-remainder contents;
- \(C_{\rm lc}\) records leading coefficient drops.

---

## 8. Practical certification algorithm

For fixed \((d,r)\):

```text
Input N_d(X), N_r(X+d)

R0 := N_r(X+d)
R1 := N_d(X)
C := 1

while R1 != 0:
    compute primitive subresultant step
    record content factors
    record leading coefficient factors
    record endpoint factors using N_h(-s)
    multiply their determinant exponents into C
    replace (R0,R1) by next pair

return C and S/C
```

Certified conclusions:

* \(C\mid S_{d,r}\);
* \(S_{d,r}/C\in\mathbb Z\);
* every remaining prime divisor corresponds to a genuine nonboundary modular intersection or a residual leading-coefficient phenomenon;
* no carrier prime should be removed unless it appears in the computed \(C\).

Therefore the correct replacement for broad carrier stripping is exact subresultant/content stripping. It preserves the witness primes needed for the Apéry gap-polynomial argument.
