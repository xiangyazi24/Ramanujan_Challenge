# HM_8 audit: eighth factorial moment expansion and the missing correlation estimate

## 1. Exact CRT/Fourier expansion

Let

\[
K_X(m)=\sum_{X<p\le 2X}1_{b_m\equiv0\pmod p}
\]

with the prime set \(\mathcal P_X\), and write \(\lambda_X\) for the expected sparse density scale. The factorial moment is

\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_8
=\sum_{(p_1,\ldots,p_8)\in\mathcal P_X^{\underline 8}}
\#\{m<X^2: b_m\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\ (1\le i\le8)\}.
\]

For a fixed ordered tuple of distinct primes, CRT identifies the condition with a subset of
\(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z\), \(Q=\prod_i p_i\). If

\[
Z_p=\{a\in\mathbb F_p:b_a=0\},
\]

then the number of residues is

\[
N(p_1,\ldots,p_8)=|Z_{p_1}\times\cdots\times Z_{p_8}|=\prod_i |Z_{p_i}|
\]

only when the sequence \(m\mapsto b_m\bmod p\) is independent under CRT. The actual interval count is

\[
\frac{X^2}{Q}N(p_1,\ldots,p_8)+E(p_1,\ldots,p_8).
\]

The error is the obstruction.

Using additive characters,

\[
1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p}=\sum_{r\in\mathbb F_p}\widehat{1_{Z_p}}(r)e_p(rm),
\]

so

\[
\sum_{m<X^2}\prod_{i=1}^8 1_{Z_{p_i}}(m)
=
\sum_{r_i\in\mathbb F_{p_i}}
\prod_i\widehat{1_{Z_{p_i}}}(r_i)
\sum_{m<X^2}e_Q\left(m\sum_i r_iQ/p_i\right).
\]

The zero frequency gives the expected main term. All nonzero frequency vectors produce the mixed-prime correlation error.

## 2. A sufficient explicit inequality for HM_8

A sufficient statement is:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{(p_i)\in\mathcal P_X^{\underline8}}
\sum_{r\ne0}
\left|\prod_{i=1}^8\widehat{1_{Z_{p_i}}}(r_i)\right|
\left|\sum_{m<X^2}e_Q(m\alpha(r))\right|
\le X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^8
}
\]

where \(\alpha(r)=\sum_i r_iQ/p_i\). This is essentially the weakest Fourier formulation: it asks only for the total mixed nonzero-frequency contribution, not pointwise cancellation.

Equivalently, with discrepancy

\[
D(p_1,\ldots,p_8)=\sup_I\left|
\#\{m\in I:b_m=0\ (p_i\ \forall i)\}-|I|\prod_i\frac{|Z_{p_i}|}{p_i}\right|,
\]

it suffices that

\[
\sum_{(p_i)}D(p_1,\ldots,p_8)\le X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^8.
\]

## 3. Sparse normalization

The diagonal term is

\[
X^2\sum_{(p_i)}\prod_i\frac{|Z_{p_i}|}{p_i}.
\]

Since \(|Z_p|/p\approx\lambda_X/\pi(X)\), this gives

\[
X^2\lambda_X^8
\]

up to the usual prime counting factors. Thus HM_8 requires that correlations are no larger than the independent sparse model prediction.

## 4. Frequencies supported on subsets of the eight primes

The Fourier sum splits according to

\[
S=\{i:r_i\ne0\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,8\}.
\]

The cases \(|S|=1\) require one-prime incomplete sums. Cases \(|S|=2,\ldots,7\) require mixed-prime correlations. The hardest term is \(|S|=8\):

\[
\sum_{r_1\ne0,\dots,r_8\ne0}
\prod_i\widehat{1_{Z_{p_i}}}(r_i)
\sum_{m<X^2}e_Q(m\alpha(r)).
\]

A bound for individual K3/Franel trace functions at a single prime does not control this product correlation.

## 5. What the Apery Hasse--Franel formulas provide

The available formulas give finite-field descriptions such as

\[
b_m\bmod p = \text{finite-field hypergeometric/Hasse--Franel expression}
\]

and permit Deligne-type square-root bounds for individual trace sums of the form

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(x)F(x).
\]

They do not directly imply

\[
\sum_{m<X^2}\prod_{i=1}^8 1_{b_m=0\bmod p_i}
\approx
X^2\prod_i |Z_{p_i}|/p_i.
\]

Orthogonality reduces the problem to the displayed mixed Fourier sums, but no currently established bound gives the required eighth-order independence.

A large sieve could prove HM_8 if one had a family estimate of the form

\[
\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_8}
\sum_{r_i\ne0}
\left|\prod_i\widehat{1_{Z_{p_i}}}(r_i)\right|^2
\ll X^{o(1)}\lambda_X^8,
\]

combined with the incomplete geometric sum bound. Such an inequality is not supplied by the existing Apery congruences.

## Conclusion

HM_8 is not proved by the current Hasse--Franel/K3 machinery. The precise missing input is the quantitative correlation estimate:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_8\in(X,2X)}
D(p_1,\ldots,p_8)
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^8
}
\]

or equivalently the mixed eight-prime Fourier bound above. This is a concrete eighth-order independence statement for sparse Apery zero sets. Deligne bounds for individual prime traces and CRT orthogonality alone do not establish it; a genuine mixed-prime large-sieve or trace-correlation theorem is still required.
