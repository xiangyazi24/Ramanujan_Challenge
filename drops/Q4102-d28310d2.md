# Q4102 — AD1 actual-flag arithmetic versus the corrected quartic

## Verdict

**No closure from the present inverse-row arithmetic.**  On an actual AD1 flag the prime/integrality constraints do sharpen the bare ordering substantially, but they do **not** by themselves force the sign of the corrected quartic and they do not produce the exponentially accurate four-ratio matching needed by the C4 packing step.

The exact new arithmetic normal form is:

\[
x:=b-a=6m,
\qquad
y:=d-c=78m+84k,
\qquad z:=c-b,
\qquad p=z+42t,
\tag{1}
\]

with \(m\ge1\), \(y>0\), and integers \(k,t\).  In these parameters the four inverse rows are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{edge}&q&u\\ \hline
ac=P&z+49t-m&z+6m-a-1\\
bc=M^+&z+48t&6t-a-6m-1\\
bd=P&z+49t-13m-14k&z+72m+84k-a-1\\
ad=M^+&z+48t-12m-12k&6t-a-12m-12k-1.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]

Actual selectedness is the four extra predicates

\[
q_e\text{ prime},\qquad q_e\mid b_{u_e}.
\tag{3}
\]

No pair of the four \(u_e\)'s is forced equal by (1)--(2), and no repeated selected upper zero is forced.  Thus the classification does not collapse the AD1 determinant to a two-row Casoratian.

The key point is that (1)--(2) still leave a genuine free parameter \(k\), and the inverse-row legality region intersects both asymptotic sign regions from Q4091.  Consequently any fixed-sign theorem for **actual** AD1 flags must use a new correlation among the four selectedness predicates (3), not merely the affine/integrality geometry.

I do **not** claim an unconditional infinite family of actual selected flags of both signs: that would require proving simultaneous Apéry-zero selectedness along an infinite prime family, which is a new arithmetic theorem not supplied by the current C4 classification.  What is proved below is the exact reduction and the precise remaining theorem.

## 1. Exact prime/integrality consequences

Put

\[
x=b-a>0,\qquad z=c-b>0,\qquad y=d-c>0.
\]

Then the four physical gaps in (4) are

\[
c-a=z+x,\quad c-b=z,\quad d-b=z+y,\quad d-a=z+x+y.
\tag{5}
\]

### Integrality

From (4),

\[
z+x\equiv p\pmod6,\qquad z+y\equiv p\pmod6,
\tag{6}
\]

and

\[
z\equiv p\pmod7,\qquad z+x+y\equiv p\pmod7.
\tag{7}
\]

Hence

\[
y-x\equiv0\pmod6,\qquad x+y\equiv0\pmod7.
\tag{8}
\]

### Odd upper primes

For the P pair,

\[
q_{ac}-q_{bd}={y-x\over6},
\tag{9}
\]

and for the M+ pair,

\[
q_{bc}-q_{ad}={x+y\over7}.
\tag{10}
\]

Since every upper prime satisfies \(q_e>p>7\), all four \(q_e\) are odd.  Therefore both differences (9)--(10) are even:

\[
y\equiv x\pmod{12},\qquad x+y\equiv0\pmod{14}.
\tag{11}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{y\equiv13x\pmod{84}.}
\tag{12}
\]

Also the four gaps (5) are odd, so \(x\) is even.

### The mod-3 obstruction forces \(3\mid x\)

From the P integrality in (6), modulo 3,

\[
p\equiv z+x\pmod3.
\tag{13}
\]

Because \(7\equiv1\pmod3\) and \(8\equiv2\pmod3\),

\[
q_{bc}\equiv2p-z\equiv z+2x\pmod3.
\tag{14}
\]

Using \(y\equiv x\pmod3\),

\[
q_{ad}\equiv2p-(z+x+y)\equiv z\pmod3.
\tag{15}
\]

If \(x\not\equiv0\pmod3\), then the three residues

\[
z,\quad z+x,\quad z+2x
\]

are all three residue classes modulo 3.  One of \(p,q_{bc},q_{ad}\) would then be divisible by 3, impossible because all exceed 3.  Hence

\[
\boxed{6\mid x.}
\tag{16}
\]

Write \(x=6m\).  Combining (12) with (16) gives

\[
\boxed{y=78m+84k}
\tag{17}
\]

for some integer \(k\).  Finally (6)--(7) now give \(p\equiv z\pmod{42}\), so

\[
\boxed{p=z+42t.}
\tag{18}
\]

Substitution of (17)--(18) into (4) gives exactly the table (2).

A useful affine identity is also

\[
6(q_{ac}+q_{bd})-7(q_{bc}+q_{ad})+2p=0.
\tag{19}
\]

Nothing in (1)--(2) forces \(k\) to have one sign or to lie on a single side of the Q4091 four-ratio barrier.

## 2. What selectedness actually adds

Actual selectedness adds, edge by edge,

\[
q_e\mid b_{u_e},\qquad 0\le u_e<q_e.
\tag{20}
\]

The inequalities \(u_e\ge0\) are exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
a&\le z+6m-1,\\
a&\le 6t-6m-1,\\
a&\le z+72m+84k-1,\\
a&\le 6t-12m-12k-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

Equivalently in the original variables,

\[
c\ge2a+1,\qquad d\ge2b+1,
\tag{22}
\]

\[
c+6b\le p-7,\qquad d+6a\le p-7.
\tag{23}
\]

There is a small but exact strengthening from the divisibility \(q_e\mid b_{u_e}\).  Let

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
\qquad
\ell_p=\left\lfloor{\log p\over\log\alpha}\right\rfloor+1.
\tag{24}
\]

The Stieltjes representation used in Q4091 has support in \([0,\alpha]\) and total mass \(b_0=1\), so

\[
b_u\le\alpha^u.
\tag{25}
\]

Since \(q_e>p\) and \(q_e\mid b_{u_e}\), one has \(b_{u_e}\ge q_e>p\).  Thus

\[
\boxed{u_e\ge\ell_p}
\tag{26}
\]

for every selected edge.  Consequently

\[
c\ge2a+1+\ell_p,\qquad d\ge2b+1+\ell_p,
\tag{27}
\]

and

\[
c+6b\le p-7(\ell_p+1),\qquad
 d+6a\le p-7(\ell_p+1).
\tag{28}
\]

These are only \(O(\log p)\) tightenings of the affine legality region.  They do not create an \(O(p)\) relation between \(x\) and \(y\), and they do not identify any two of the four selected indices in (2).

In particular, selectedness as currently stated gives four separate Apéry-zero predicates

\[
\begin{aligned}
z+49t-m&\mid b_{z+6m-a-1},\\
z+48t&\mid b_{6t-a-6m-1},\\
z+49t-13m-14k&\mid b_{z+72m+84k-a-1},\\
z+48t-12m-12k&\mid b_{6t-a-12m-12k-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]

A sign theorem would therefore have to extract a **four-edge correlation** from (29).  No one-edge reflection or Casoratian identity turns (29) into such a correlation.

## 3. The corrected AD1 determinant and its exact sign barrier

Retain Q4091's notation

\[
R_v=7p-1-v,\qquad L_v=p+v,\qquad H_v=14p-1-v.
\]

The actual corrected determinant is

\[
\Delta_1=
 b_{H_c}b_{L_b}b_{R_b}b_{R_d}
-b_{R_a}b_{R_c}b_{H_d}b_{L_a}.
\tag{30}
\]

Let

\[
\Gamma_h(n)={b_{n+h}\over b_n}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal Q_1:={b_{H_c}b_{L_b}b_{R_b}b_{R_d}
\over b_{R_a}b_{R_c}b_{H_d}b_{L_a}}
={\Gamma_y(H_d)\Gamma_x(L_a)
\over\Gamma_y(R_d)\Gamma_x(R_b)}.
\tag{31}
\]

Thus \(\Delta_1\) has the sign of \(\mathcal Q_1-1\).

The first Apéry asymptotic from Q4091 gives

\[
\log\mathcal Q_1
={3\over2}\log
{R_aR_cH_dL_a\over H_cL_bR_bR_d}
+O(p^{-1}).
\tag{32}
\]

The numerator-minus-denominator of the index product in (32) factors **exactly** as

\[
\boxed{
\Psi:=R_aR_cH_dL_a-H_cL_bR_bR_d
=7pL_aR_a\,y-(R_b-L_a)R_dH_c\,x.
}
\tag{33}
\]

Hence any normalized family on which \(|\Psi|\ge\eta p^4\) for some fixed \(\eta>0\) has, for all sufficiently large \(p\),

\[
\operatorname{sgn}\Delta_1=\operatorname{sgn}\Psi.
\tag{34}
\]

So the question becomes whether the actual inverse-row constraints force \(\Psi\) to one side.

They do not at the affine-legality level.

## 4. Both asymptotic sign regions survive the exact legality inequalities

Scale

\[
A={a\over p},\qquad X={x\over p},\qquad Z={z\over p},\qquad Y={y\over p}.
\]

Ignoring only the vanishing \(O(\log p/p)\) margins in (27)--(28), the selectedness-compatible interior is

\[
X+Z>A,\qquad Z+Y>A+X,
\tag{35}
\]

\[
7A+7X+Z<1,\qquad 7A+X+Z+Y<1.
\tag{36}
\]

The normalized leading sign functional from (33) is

\[
\psi(A,X,Z,Y)
=7(1+A)(7-A)Y
-(6-2A-X)(7-A-X-Z-Y)(14-A-X-Z)X.
\tag{37}
\]

There are open subsets of (35)--(36) with either sign.  For example:

* at
  \[
  (A,X,Z,Y)=(0.01,0.01,0.05,0.45),
  \]
  all four inequalities are strict and \(\psi>0\);
* at
  \[
  (A,X,Z,Y)=(0.01,0.05,0.10,0.05),
  \]
  all four inequalities are strict and \(\psi<0\).

The exact congruences (16)--(18) are fixed-modulus lattice conditions.  They can be met by \(O(1)\) perturbations of large integer points approaching either interior point, so they do not remove either open sign region.  The \(\ell_p=O(\log p)\) strengthening in (27)--(28) is also negligible on this scale.

This proves the precise scope:

> **Inverse-row geometry, integrality, odd-prime parity, the mod-3 prime obstruction, and the size consequence of selectedness do not force an AD1 sign.**

What remains untested by this geometric argument is the genuinely arithmetic condition (29): the four specific upper primes must actually divide the four specific Apéry values.  It is logically possible that those four simultaneous zero conditions select only one sign region, but that would itself be a new four-edge Apéry-zero correlation theorem.

Accordingly I cannot honestly give an unconditional infinite family of **actual selected** AD1 flags of both signs.  Producing one would require proving infinitely many simultaneous instances of (29), which is substantially stronger than the current classification theorem.  A finite scan would not repair this gap and is not used here.

## 5. No exponential matching comes from the current arithmetic constraints

Use the Q4091 normalized Turán gaps

\[
E_y=1-{b_{H_d}b_{R_c}\over b_{H_c}b_{R_d}},
\qquad
E_x=1-{b_{L_b}b_{R_b}\over b_{L_a}b_{R_a}}.
\tag{38}
\]

Strict log-convexity gives

\[
0<E_x,E_y<1,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_1=b_{H_c}b_{R_d}b_{L_a}b_{R_a}(E_y-E_x).
}
\tag{39}
\]

The selectedness conditions (29) are congruence/divisibility statements.  They give no upper bound for \(|E_y-E_x|\).  The only immediate metric consequence is the lower index bound (26).

They actually make the uniform exponential target slightly **harder**.  Put

\[
S=29p-3-c-d.
\tag{40}
\]

Adding (28) gives

\[
c+d+6(a+b)\le2p-14(\ell_p+1),
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
S\ge27p+11+14\ell_p+6(a+b).
}
\tag{41}
\]

Thus the leading uniform rate remains at least \(27\log\alpha\) per \(p\); selectedness does not lower it.

If \(\Delta_1\ne0\), the already-known support divisibility \(p^4\mid\Delta_1\) gives only a lattice **lower** bound on \(|E_y-E_x|\); it does not supply the upper bound needed for packing.

## 6. Smallest additional theorem now required

There are two logically different targets.

### Sign only

To prove a fixed sign on actual AD1 flags, the missing theorem is simply a selected-flag exclusion:

\[
\boxed{
\exists\sigma\in\{\pm1\}\ \forall\text{ actual AD1 flags},
\quad \sigma(E_y-E_x)>0.
}
\tag{42}
\]

Equivalently, all actual selected quadruples (29) must lie on one side of (31).  Nothing in (1)--(29) proves (42).

### C4 packing height

A sign theorem is nowhere near enough.  If the allowed logarithmic height is \(H_{\rm pack}(T)\), then by (39) the exact necessary-and-sufficient four-ratio theorem is

\[
\boxed{
0<\sigma(E_y-E_x)
\le
{\exp(H_{\rm pack}(T))
\over b_{H_c}b_{R_d}b_{L_a}b_{R_a}}
}
\tag{43}
\]

for every actual AD1 flag.

Since

\[
b_{H_c}b_{R_d}b_{L_a}b_{R_a}
=\alpha^S p^{-6}e^{O(1)},
\]

(43) is

\[
\boxed{
|E_y-E_x|
\le
\exp\!\bigl(-S\log\alpha+6\log p+H_{\rm pack}(T)+O(1)\bigr).
}
\tag{44}
\]

For a polylogarithmic target,

\[
H_{\rm pack}(T)=O((\log T)^A),
\]

this becomes

\[
\boxed{
|E_y-E_x|
\le
\exp\!\left(
-(29p-3-c-d)\log(17+12\sqrt2)
+O((\log T)^A)
\right).
}
\tag{45}
\]

By (41), uniformly on actual selected flags this requires at least

\[
\boxed{
|E_y-E_x|
\le
\exp\!\left(
-(27\log(17+12\sqrt2)+o(1))p
\right).
}
\tag{46}
\]

Equivalently, with \(\Gamma_h(n)=b_{n+h}/b_n\), the smallest four-ratio statement is

\[
\boxed{
{\Gamma_y(H_d)\Gamma_x(L_a)
\over\Gamma_y(R_d)\Gamma_x(R_b)}
=1+\varepsilon,
}
\tag{47}
\]

with a fixed nonzero sign for \(\varepsilon\) and

\[
\boxed{
|\varepsilon|
\le
\exp\!\bigl(-S\log\alpha+6\log p+H_{\rm pack}(T)+O(1)\bigr).
}
\tag{48}
\]

This is exactly the Q4091 exponential matching, now restricted to the **actual AD1 selected set**.  The inverse-row prime/integrality constraints do not improve the exponent; their only quantitative effect is the lower bound (41), which slightly increases the required cancellation.

## Final theorem

> **Actual-AD1 inverse-row reduction.**  Let \(p>7\) and let \(a<b<c<d<p\) be an actual aligned-directed AD1 C4 with edges \(ac=P,bc=M^+,bd=P,ad=M^+\), inverse rows (4), prime upper rows, and selectedness \(q_e\mid b_{u_e}\).  Then there are integers \(m\ge1,k,t\) and \(z=c-b>0\) such that (1)--(2) hold.  In particular \(6\mid(b-a)\) and
> \[
> d-c\equiv13(b-a)\pmod{84}.
> \]
> Every selected index satisfies \(u_e\ge\ell_p\), giving (27)--(28) and (41).
>
> These constraints do not algebraically order the two Q4091 cross-ratios: the affine legality region, including the exact fixed-modulus constraints and the \(O(\log p)\) selectedness margins, has open components on both sides of the asymptotic sign barrier (33).  Therefore a fixed sign for **actual** flags, if true, must come from a new correlation among the four Apéry-zero predicates (29).  No such correlation follows from the one-edge selectedness theorem.
>
> Likewise no useful height saving follows.  The exact missing packing theorem is the actual-flag four-ratio estimate (43)--(48), requiring exponential matching at rate \(S\log(17+12\sqrt2)\), uniformly at least \((27\log(17+12\sqrt2)+o(1))p\).

So the positive attack does not close AD1: the arithmetic classification narrows the lattice sharply, but it neither forbids one asymptotic sign region nor supplies the beyond-all-orders matching.  The next genuinely new step is a four-selected-zero correlation theorem, not another affine manipulation of the inverse rows.
