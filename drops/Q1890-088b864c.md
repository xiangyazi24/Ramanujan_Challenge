# P3.2 alternative route audit

## Result

The proposed universal Bezout support statement

\[
A_m e_m+B_mY_m=C_m,\qquad \operatorname{Supp}(C_m)\subseteq 3d_m\prod_{t=2}^m X_t
\]

is **not verified by the continuant identities alone**. The natural Desnanot--Jacobi candidate does not produce such a support restriction: the determinant minors introduce the full boundary coefficient of the continuant recurrence, not only the final correction term \(3d_m\prod X_t\).

In particular, a gcd observation between \(e_m\) and \(Y_m\) is insufficient: it only shows that some combination exists over the coefficient field, not that the Bezout constant has the claimed prime support.

## Structured recurrence check

Write the recurrence as a tridiagonal determinant recurrence

\[
D_{t+1}=a_tD_t-b_tD_{t-1},
\]

with

\[
a_t=4t(68t^2+3),\qquad b_t=(2t-1)^6.
\]

The standard continuant identity gives

\[
D_{r+1}D_{r-1}-D_r^2
\]

as a product of the off-diagonal terms \(b_i\) times the corresponding complementary minors. The correction term in \(Y_m\), however, is a first-order deformation

\[
\prod X_t-\sum_t6(t^2-1)d_t\prod_{s\neq t}X_s,
\]

so its Jacobi relation contains all \(d_t\) contributions, not only \(d_m\).

Therefore the only possible way the desired support could hold is through an additional telescoping invariant cancelling the earlier \(d_t\)'s. No such invariant follows from the continuant recurrence.

## Small-case obstruction

The first nontrivial cases already show the required identity cannot be obtained from the bare continuant/Jacobi relation. The recurrence produces boundary terms involving

\[
(2r-1)^6X_{r-1}
\]

at every stage. These terms survive into the determinant minors. The claimed support set only contains the last \(d_m\) and the product of \(X_t\), so it is too small unless an extra arithmetic identity relating all earlier \(d_t\)'s is supplied.

## Conclusion

The alternative route should therefore be rejected in its current form:

* no verified Desnanot--Jacobi invariant with the stated support was found;
* a Bezout identity over the coefficient ring does not imply the required support condition;
* the missing step would have to be a new telescoping identity eliminating all \(d_2,\ldots,d_{m-1}\) terms.

The implication \(e_m=0\Rightarrow Y_m\neq0\) still needs a different invariant (for example the centered Green-function/endpoint route), rather than the proposed restricted-support Bezout identity.
