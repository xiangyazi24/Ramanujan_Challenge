# Mellin-transform sheaves and defining-characteristic independence

## Main conclusion

The Mellin-transform sheaf idea is the correct geometric reformulation of the Apéry zero problem, but Katz-Laumon equidistribution over \ell-adic coefficients still does not directly control the congruence

\[
T_p(r)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The obstruction is not the rank of the Mellin sheaf. Even if the Mellin transform has bounded rank and conductor, the zero condition is a crystalline/finite-field condition on the reduction of the trace, not an \ell-adic trace-vanishing condition.

## The Mellin transform sheaf

Let \(\mathcal H\) be the Apéry trace sheaf. Its multiplicative Fourier transform is a sheaf \(\mathcal M=FT_!\mathcal H\) on the dual torus.

At a multiplicative character \(\chi_r(x)=x^r\), the trace is

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{\chi_r}|\mathcal M)=\sum_x\theta_p(x)\chi_r(x)=T_p(r).
\]

Under the usual non-degeneracy hypotheses of Laumon's Mellin transform theory, \(\mathcal M\) is generically lisse of the same generic rank as \(\mathcal H\), with conductor controlled by the original conductor and singularities. Thus the expected rank-2 statement is plausible.

## Why this is insufficient

Katz equidistribution gives distribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes of \(\mathcal M\) in its geometric monodromy group. It controls quantities such as

\[
\frac{T_p(r)}{p^{w/2}}
\]

as a complex trace.

The Apéry zero condition is instead

\[
T_p(r)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p},
\]

where \(\mathfrak p\) is a prime above \(p\). This depends on the integral lattice and the crystalline Frobenius, not merely on the compact monodromy class.

A small complex trace does not imply high p-adic valuation. Conversely, a trace can vanish modulo p while having a generic archimedean size.

## Relevant p-adic theories

The closest existing frameworks are:

1. **Crew, Tsuzuki, Kedlaya p-adic differential equations:** overconvergent F-isocrystals and p-adic cohomology give crystalline realizations of trace functions.

2. **Abe's p-adic Langlands correspondence for curves:** gives p-adic companions and L-functions for overconvergent F-isocrystals.

3. **Wan's Dwork trace formulas and p-adic estimates:** control slopes and Newton polygons of exponential sums.

4. **Katz's theory of p-adic cohomology and rigid local systems:** provides p-adic analogues of monodromy calculations.

However, none of these currently gives a general Sato-Tate theorem for reductions of trace functions modulo the defining characteristic.

## What theorem would be needed

One needs a defining-characteristic equidistribution statement:

For the Mellin F-isocrystal \(\mathcal M\), as \(p\to\infty\),

\[
\Pr_r(\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_r|\mathcal M)\equiv0\pmod p)
=\frac{\#Z_p}{p}+o(1/p).
\]

Equivalently, one needs control of the reduction of Frobenius traces in the residue field of the same characteristic.

This is stronger than Deligne equidistribution because it mixes:

- varying characteristic;
- crystalline Frobenius;
- Mellin transform;
- zero loci of trace functions.

## Consequence for three-characteristic independence

If such a theorem existed with uniform error, then the distribution of Apéry zeros would satisfy the required independence:

\[
\Pr(r|b_{CRT(z_p,z_q)})\sim |Z_r|/r.
\]

The Mellin transform removes the earlier difficulty that \(z_p\) is an implicit root: it packages the whole zero set as the trace-zero locus of a fixed geometric object. But the final step remains a new p-adic equidistribution problem.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Mellin sheaves identify the correct object, but no known crystalline Katz theorem proves the needed independence.}}
\]
