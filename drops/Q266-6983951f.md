# P3.2 Apéry: the variance approach to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

## Verdict

The scale
\[
\operatorname{Var}\asymp \frac{X^2}{\log^3 X},
\qquad
\operatorname{sd}\asymp \frac{X}{\log^{3/2}X},
\]
is the right scale in a **random-shift / independently translated zero-set model**, under a mild regularity assumption on the sizes \(|\mathcal Z_p|\). After correcting the overlap calculation, it is a clean and rigorous probabilistic statement.

It does **not**, by itself, prove the deterministic Apéry statement \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) for the fixed interval \([0,M)\). There are two distinct gaps:

1. The numbers
   \[
   N_T=\left|\bigcap_{p\in T}A_p\right|
   \]
   are deterministic. The expression \(M\prod_{p\in T}|\mathcal Z_p|/\prod_{p\in T}p\) is only an “expectation” after one specifies a randomization.
2. A variance estimate over random shifts proves that almost every shift is good. It gives no reason that the particular shift \(0\), corresponding to the actual Apéry interval, is good. A single arithmetically distinguished shift may be exceptional.

Moreover, the quoted disjoint-triple covariance \(\sim-X^{-2}\) cannot be combined with the claimed total variance. There are about \((X/\log X)^6\) disjoint pairs of triples, so such a covariance would contribute
\[
\asymp -\frac{X^4}{\log^6X},
\]
not \(O(X^2/\log^3X)\). Any cancellation that reduces this to the desired scale is itself a major unproved mixed-prime correlation statement.

The structural correlation one must exclude is real: many different triples, including disjoint triples, can have their selected CRT class represented by the **same integer** \(m_0<M\). The anchored-star construction already recorded in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` is precisely such an obstruction.

## 1. The natural randomization and the corrected covariance calculation

Write
\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p:X<p\le 2X\},
\qquad z_p=|\mathcal Z_p|,
\qquad d_p=\frac{z_p}{p},
\qquad \lambda=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}d_p.
\]
Let
\[
P_X=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_X}p
\]
and choose a shift \(H\) uniformly modulo \(P_X\). Define, for an unordered prime triple \(T\),
\[
N_T(H)=\sum_{0\le m<M}\prod_{p\in T}
  \mathbf 1_{\mathcal Z_p}(m+H\bmod p).
\]
The coordinates \(H\bmod p\) are independent and uniform as \(p\) varies. Equivalently, this independently translates every zero set modulo its prime.

For every triple \(T\), exactly
\[
\mathbf E_H N_T(H)=M\prod_{p\in T}d_p.
\]
This identity does not require \(\prod_{p\in T}p>M\); the latter condition is useful for bounding the range and variance of \(N_T(H)\).

Let
\[
S(H)=\sum_{T\in\binom{\mathcal P_X}{3}}N_T(H).
\]
The ordered third factorial moment is \(6S(H)\), so the distinction changes only an absolute factor in the variance.

### Disjoint triples

If \(T\cap U=\varnothing\), then \(N_T(H)\) and \(N_U(H)\) are functions of disjoint sets of independent coordinates \((H\bmod p)\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}_H(N_T,N_U)=0.}
\]
Thus the natural model gives zero, not \(-X^{-2}\), for disjoint triples.

### One shared prime

Let
\[
T=\{p,a,b\},\qquad U=\{p,c,d\}.
\]
Put
\[
B_p(h)=\#\{0\le m<M:m+h\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\}.
\]
Conditional on \(H\bmod p\), the remaining random coordinates occurring in \(N_T\) and \(N_U\) are disjoint. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_H(N_T,N_U)
=d_ad_bd_cd_d\,\operatorname{Var}_H(B_p).
\]
Every residue class modulo \(p\) occurs either \(\lfloor M/p\rfloor\) or \(\lceil M/p\rceil\) times in \([0,M)\). Consequently the range of \(B_p\) has length at most \(z_p\), and
\[
\operatorname{Var}(B_p)\ll z_p^2.
\]
When the \(z_p\)'s are bounded, this gives
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}_H(N_T,N_U)\ll X^{-4},}
\]
not \(X^{-3}\).

### Two shared primes

Let
\[
T=\{p,q,a\},\qquad U=\{p,q,b\},
\]
and define
\[
B_{p,q}(h_p,h_q)=
\#\{0\le m<M:
 m+h_p\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p,
 m+h_q\bmod q\in\mathcal Z_q\}.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_H(N_T,N_U)
=d_ad_b\,\operatorname{Var}(B_{p,q}).
\]
For the project normalization \(M=X^2\), one has \(pq>M\). Thus every selected residue pair has at most one representative, so
\[
0\le B_{p,q}\le z_pz_q,
\qquad
\mathbf E B_{p,q}=M d_pd_q.
\]
In particular
\[
\operatorname{Var}(B_{p,q})
\le \mathbf E B_{p,q}^2
\le z_pz_q\,\mathbf E B_{p,q}
= M\frac{z_p^2z_q^2}{pq}.
\]
For bounded \(z_p,z_q\), this is \(O(1)\), and hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}_H(N_T,N_U)\ll X^{-2}.}
\]
This part of the proposed scale is correct.

### The same triple

Since \(pqr>M\),
\[
0\le N_T(H)\le z_pz_qz_r.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}N_T
\le z_pz_qz_r\,\mathbf E N_T
=M\frac{z_p^2z_q^2z_r^2}{pqr}.
\]
For bounded zero counts this is \(O(X^{-1})\).

### Aggregate scale

Let \(L=|\mathcal P_X|\asymp X/\log X\). In the bounded-zero-count model, the four overlap types give

| shared primes | number of pairs | covariance size | aggregate size |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | \(\asymp L^3\) | \(O(X^{-1})\) | \(O(X^2/\log^3X)\) |
| 2 | \(\asymp L^4\) | \(O(X^{-2})\) | \(O(X^2/\log^4X)\) |
| 1 | \(\asymp L^5\) | \(O(X^{-4})\) | \(O(X/\log^5X)\) |
| 0 | \(\asymp L^6\) | exactly \(0\) | \(0\) |

Thus the diagonal term dominates and
\[
\operatorname{Var}_H S(H)\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^3X}.
\]

A useful weighted version, without pretending all \(z_p\)'s equal one, is obtained by setting
\[
\eta_2=\sum_p\frac{z_p^2}{p},
\qquad
Z_2=\sum_p z_p^2.
\]
The preceding argument gives, up to absolute combinatorial constants,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_H S(H)
\ll M\eta_2^3+M\lambda^2\eta_2^2+Z_2\lambda^4.
}
\]
Hence the claimed \(X^2/\log^3X\) scale also requires some control such as
\[
\eta_2\ll X^{o(1)}\lambda,
\qquad
Z_2\ll X^{1+o(1)}\lambda.
\]
It does not follow from the single first-moment parameter \(\lambda\) alone if zero mass can be concentrated in a few unusually large \(\mathcal Z_p\)'s.

Under these regularity assumptions,
\[
\mathbf E_H(6S(H))
=M\sum_{p,q,r\ {m distinct}}d_pd_qd_r
\le M\lambda^3,
\]
and the standard deviation is of Poisson size
\[
\sqrt{M\lambda^3}.
\]
For \(M=X^2\) and \(\lambda\asymp1/\log X\), this is exactly
\[
\frac{X}{\log^{3/2}X}
=o\!\left(\frac{X^2}{\log^3X}\right).
\]
So the probabilistic calculation is sound after these corrections.

## 2. Why this does not prove the fixed Apéry moment

Chebyshev would show that, for a uniformly random shift \(H\),
\[
6S(H)=\mathbf E(6S(H))+o(M\lambda^3)
\]
for all but a vanishing proportion of shifts. It says nothing pointwise about \(H=0\).

This is not a minor technicality. The period \(P_X\) is exponentially large in \(X\). A single bad shift, or even polynomially many bad shifts, is invisible to a global mean-square estimate modulo \(P_X\). The actual Apéry interval may in principle be one of these exceptional shifts.

The clean countermodel is a common anchor. Choose \(m_0<M\) and, initially, put
\[
\mathcal Z_p^*=\{m_0\bmod p\}
\]
for a set of active primes. Then every active prime marks \(m_0\). Since \(pq>M\), every pair intersection has exactly the single representative \(m_0\), and since \(pqr>M\), every triple has exactly the same representative:
\[
N_{\{p,q,r\}}(0)=1.
\]
Yet its volume prediction is only
\[
\frac{M}{pqr}\asymp \frac1X.
\]
Thus all triple rounding errors have the same positive sign, including errors belonging to disjoint triples.

If there are \(L\) active primes concentrated near \(X\), then
\[
\lambda\sim\frac{L}{X},
\qquad
F_2=(L)_2\sim L^2\sim M\lambda^2,
\]
while
\[
F_3=(L)_3\sim L^3,
\qquad
M\lambda^3\sim\frac{L^3}{X}.
\]
Thus the second factorial moment can be asymptotically at its Poisson scale while the third factorial moment is larger than its Poisson scale by a factor \(\asymp X\).

The stronger construction in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` replaces the singleton by the reflection-invariant pair
\[
\mathcal Z_p^*=\{a_p,p-1-a_p\},
\qquad a_p=m_0\bmod p,
\]
after deleting finitely many exceptional primes. It retains the common anchor and also satisfies the currently available reflection, endpoint, gap, interval, fiber-energy, and bounded-row-codegree constraints. It gives
\[
F_3\gg \frac{X^3}{\log^3X}
\]
while \(M\lambda^3\asymp X^2/\log^3X\).

Therefore the missing phenomenon is not merely an uncontrolled \(\pm1\) boundary error. It is **cross-prime selection alignment**: the chosen zero fibers can make the CRT representatives of many tuples coincide on a common row.

## 3. If the intended covariance is over a random integer \(m\)

There is another possible interpretation. Define
\[
I_T(m)=\prod_{p\in T}\mathbf1_{A_p}(m)
\]
and choose \(m\) uniformly from \([0,M)\). Then, for disjoint triples \(T,U\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_m(I_T,I_U)
=
\frac{|\bigcap_{p\in T\cup U}A_p|}{M}
-rac{N_TN_U}{M^2}.
\]
The first term is a six-prime intersection. Summing it over disjoint \(T,U\) is essentially a sixth factorial-moment quantity. CRT uniqueness only says that an individual six-prime residue tuple has at most one representative; it does not place that representative randomly below \(M\).

Hence, under the random-\(m\) interpretation, the desired covariance bound assumes a sixth-order correlation estimate that is substantially stronger than \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). It is not supplied by the CRT observation.

## 4. A variance statement that really would imply \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

The project already has the right deterministic reduction in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex`. For ordered \(p\ne q\), write
\[
J_{p,q}=|A_p\cap A_q|,
\]
\[
T_{p,q}
=\sum_{m\in A_p\cap A_q}
  \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}
  \mathbf1_{A_\ell}(m).
\]
Then exactly
\[
F_3:=\sum_{m<M}(K_X(m))_3
=\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}.
\]
Let
\[
\rho_\ell=\frac{|A_\ell|}{M},
\qquad
\mu_{p,q}=\sum_{\ell\notin\{p,q\}}\rho_\ell,
\qquad
w_{p,q}=J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}.
\]
The finite-marginal pair--Palm theorem proves
\[
\sum_{p\ne q}(T_{p,q}-w_{p,q})_+
\le F_3
\le
\sum_{p\ne q}(T_{p,q}-w_{p,q})_+
+O(M\lambda^3).
\]
Thus the arithmetic obstruction is exactly the total positive conditional overload of third primes on actual two-prime loci.

A genuine deterministic variance route would be the chi-square estimate
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{Palm}}(X)
:=
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\\w_{p,q}>0}}
\frac{(T_{p,q}-w_{p,q})^2}{w_{p,q}}
\ll X^{o(1)}M\lambda^3.
}
\tag{PV}_3
\]
Indeed, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\sum_{p\ne q}(T_{p,q}-w_{p,q})_+
\le
\left(\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}\right)^{1/2}
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{Palm}}(X)^{1/2}.
\]
Also
\[
\sum_{p\ne q}w_{p,q}
\le
\left(\sum_\ell\rho_\ell\right)
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}
\ll \lambda F_2
\ll M\lambda^3
\]
by the proved second factorial moment. Therefore \((\mathrm{PV})_3\) implies
\[
\sum_{p\ne q}(T_{p,q}-w_{p,q})_+
\ll X^{o(1)}M\lambda^3,
\]
and hence \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

This is the clean way to salvage the variance idea. It is a variance estimate for the **actual Apéry incidence system**, not for random translations. It asks for Poisson-scale conditional variance of the number of third-prime extensions after conditioning on a two-prime hit. Proving it requires precisely the mixed-prime arithmetic that the current CRT argument lacks.

## 5. Interpretation of the numerical ratios

The values
\[
R_2=1.0006,
\qquad
R_3=0.975
\]
are very encouraging evidence that the actual Apéry zero sets are not exhibiting an anchored pileup at \(X=1000\). They are not, however, a proof of a uniform statement in \(X\), and a near-perfect second moment does not rule out third-moment spikes, as the common-anchor model shows.

For diagnostics, the denominator should preferably be the exact distinct-prime finite-marginal Bernoulli baseline rather than the Poisson power. If
\[
\mu=\sum_p\rho_p,
\qquad
s_j=\sum_p\rho_p^j,
\]
then the ordered independent-Bernoulli baseline is
\[
B_3^{\mathrm{fin}}
=M\sum_{p,q,r\ {m distinct}}\rho_p\rho_q\rho_r
=M(\mu^3-3\mu s_2+2s_3).
\]
The ratio to \(M\lambda^3\) is naturally a little below one because \(\lambda^3\) includes repeated prime labels, which cannot occur in a factorial moment, and because \(\rho_p\) absorbs the exact one-column boundary counts. Thus part of the observed \(0.975\) deficit may be a benign Bernoulli-versus-Poisson correction.

The most proof-relevant numerical statistic would be
\[
\frac{\mathcal V_{\mathrm{Palm}}(X)}{M\lambda^3},
\]
together with the upper tail of \(K_X(m)\). The computations already recorded in `problems/3.2/hm3_exploration.md`—small maximum \(K_X\), no visible reflection-orbit pileup, and a conditional third-extension rate close to \(\lambda_X\)—all support this picture.

## Conclusion

- Your proposed variance size is essentially correct for a random-shift model, but the covariance bookkeeping should be corrected to
  \[
  0,\quad O(X^{-4}),\quad O(X^{-2}),\quad O(X^{-1})
  \]
  for zero, one, two, and three shared primes, respectively, when \(|\mathcal Z_p|=O(1)\).
- A covariance \(\sim-X^{-2}\) for disjoint triples is incompatible with the claimed aggregate variance unless one assumes a huge unproved cancellation.
- Random-shift concentration proves only that almost every shift is good; it does not control the fixed shift defining the Apéry problem.
- CRT rounding errors can correlate perfectly across triples through a common representative \(m_0\). The anchored-star proposition in `hm3_result.tex` shows that the currently known primewise and pairwise constraints do not exclude this.
- A viable variance proof of \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) should target the deterministic pair--Palm estimate \((\mathrm{PV})_3\) above, or an equivalent mixed-prime discrepancy theorem.
