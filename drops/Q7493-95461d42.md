# Verdict

For a fixed quotient `q`, reflection does **not** create a second independent congruence.  It creates the same zero condition at the reflected index.  This is the first structural fact that any proposed characteristic-zero carrier has to respect.

There is nevertheless a completely honest Apéry-specific characteristic-zero carrier:

\[
C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}
 :=\operatorname {lcm}_{m\in\mathcal M_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}}
   \gcd\!\left(m,b_{n-qm},b_{(q+1)m-n-1}\right),
\tag{1}
\]

where `m` ranges over the integer points for which both displayed Apéry indices are in `[0,m-1]`, with the unique centered point removed.  Every off-center dangerous prime divides this integer.  Moreover

\[
 C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}\mid \operatorname {lcm}(1,\ldots,\lfloor n/q\rfloor),
\qquad
 \log C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}\le \frac nq+o(n).
\tag{2}
\]

Thus (1) is a genuine reduction, but (2) is only **linear** height.  I do not find a proof in the current tracked repository, or from the natural identities below, that

\[
\log C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}=o_q(n).
\tag{3}
\]

Proving (3) would be a real new arithmetic theorem about the distinguished Apéry initial vector and would settle the fixed-`q` target radical.  It is not a disguised recurrence/continuant statement.

The positive tactical observation is an exact Lucas descent for the prime factors of (1).  If a prime `ell >= 5` divides one of its local gcd factors and `m=k ell`, then simultaneous divisibility either comes from the original lower Apéry digit `b_{n mod ell}`, or from a new pair of Apéry zeros whose indices sum to `k-1`.  This really uses the distinguished Apéry solution.  It is the one route here that I would keep alive.

By contrast, the following natural carrier classes fail sharply.

* **Strong divisibility:** false for the Apéry numbers already at `b_2=73`, `b_4=33001`, where `gcd(b_2,b_4)=1`.
* **Wronskian/Green/continuant:** reflection holds for *every* `p`-integral solution of the Apéry recurrence, including the companion.  Hence the cross-Casoratian/continuant vanishes modulo `p` for every reflected pair, dangerous or not.  It is nonselective.
* **Reflected generating functions:** the antisymmetric coefficient `b_s-b_r` is universally divisible by `p`; for every off-center pair its characteristic-zero height is already at least
  \[
  \log|b_s-b_r|
  \ge \frac{2\log 2}{q+1}\,n-O_q(\log n).
  \tag{4}
  \]
  The same linear lower bound holds for the obvious sum/product carriers.
* **Same-variable constant-term fusion:** `r+s=p-1`, so fusing exponents produces `b_{p-1}`, but reflection gives `b_{p-1} \equiv b_0=1 (mod p)`.  The most obvious fusion is a `p`-adic unit, not a carrier.
* **Fixed-rank Green/resultant compression:** the tracked Green kernel reduces the bridge to a coefficient index, not to a bounded algebraic parameter; the tracked Cartier-defect proposition proves that the direct polynomial degree is `Omega(p)` and that the first Frobenius boundary simply stores the original Apéry Hasse coefficient.  This blocks the natural bounded-degree resultant route, not every conceivable nonlinear resultant.

There is also an exact scoped no-go.  Any nonzero **reflection-universal** carrier which is forced to be divisible by every prime in the fixed-`q` reflection interval, without using the distinguished condition `p | b_r`, has

\[
 \log|U_{n,q}|
 \ge \left(\frac{1}{q(q+1)}+o_q(1)\right)n.
\tag{5}
\]

The constant is just the prime-number-theorem mass of the quotient interval.  This applies to globalizations of the universal reflected-difference, common-kernel, and transfer/continuant constructions.  It is an exact linear obstruction, not the vague statement that “cross-prime cancellation is hard.”  It does **not** rule out (1), an Apéry-polynomial resultant, a higher `p`-adic lift, or another genuinely nonlinear distinguished-vector construction.

So the strategic conclusion is: **do not spend more time trying to extract a second mod-`p` equation from reflection itself.**  The clean live target is the nonlinear carrier (1), especially its Lucas descent described in Section 8 below.  I do not claim the all-index conjecture.

# 1. Exact affine geometry of a reflected pair

Fix `n` and `q>=1`.  For a prime `p>sqrt(n)` in quotient class `q`, write

\[
n=qp+r,
\qquad 0\le r<p,
\qquad s=p-1-r.
\tag{6}
\]

Then

\[
p=r+s+1
\tag{7}
\]

and eliminating `p` gives the exact affine equation

\[
\boxed{(q+1)r+qs=n-q.}
\tag{8}
\]

Equivalently, if one uses the candidate modulus itself as an integer parameter `m`, define

\[
r_m=n-qm,
\qquad
s_m=(q+1)m-n-1.
\tag{9}
\]

Then

\[
r_m+s_m=m-1.
\tag{10}
\]

The condition `0<=r_m,s_m<m` is equivalent to

\[
 m>\frac{n}{q+1},
\qquad
 m\le \frac nq,
\tag{11}
\]

with the obvious integer interpretation.  The additional problem condition is `m>sqrt(n)`.

For a fixed `q` and `n -> infinity`, the `sqrt(n)` condition is eventually redundant because `n/(q+1)>sqrt(n)`.

## Endpoints

There are two affine endpoints.

* `r=0` occurs only at `p=n/q` when that is an integer.  It is never dangerous because `b_0=1`.
* `s=0` occurs only at `p=(n+1)/(q+1)` when that is an integer.  It is never dangerous for the same reason.

Thus every dangerous off-center pair has

\[
1\le r,s\le p-2.
\tag{12}
\]

For `p>=7`, `p` is odd, so `r+s=p-1` is even.  Therefore the off-center gap

\[
h=|r-s|
\tag{13}
\]

is a positive **even** integer; in particular `h>=2`.

The boundary `p=sqrt(n)` must remain excluded.  There `n` can equal `p^2`, the base-`p` expansion changes from the two-digit strict triangle, and the large-prime denominator scaling used elsewhere in the manuscript changes as well.

Primes `2,3,5` are irrelevant to the fixed-`q` asymptotic considered here.  The reflection proposition in the tracked manuscript is stated from `p>=5`; restricting all statements below to `p>=7` avoids the exceptional initial prime `5`, and the omitted finite primes cost only `O(log n)` in any global radical comparison.

The endpoint `q=p-1` causes no algebraic singularity: under `p>sqrt(n)` one always has `q<p`; for the fixed-`q` asymptotic eventually `p` is much larger than `q`.

# 2. The centered case is one prime and should be removed immediately

The center is

\[
r=s=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\tag{14}
\]

Substituting into `n=qp+r` gives

\[
\boxed{
 p_c=\frac{2n+1}{2q+1},
 \qquad
 r_c=s_c=\frac{n-q}{2q+1}.}
\tag{15}
\]

Hence for fixed `n,q` there is at most one centered candidate modulus, and therefore at most one centered dangerous prime.  It exists only if the displayed quantities are integers and `p_c` is prime (and it must also satisfy `p_c>sqrt(n)`).

Its entire contribution to the target radical is at most

\[
\log p_c\le \log n.
\tag{16}
\]

So the center is `O(log n)` and is not part of the real problem.  Everything below concerns `r != s`.

# 3. Why reflection does not give two conditions

The tracked manuscript proves the stronger reflection statement:

> every `p`-integral solution `u` of the Apéry recurrence satisfies
> `u_{p-1-j} = u_j (mod p)`.

In particular it holds both for the distinguished Apéry solution `b` and for the companion solution `c` used in the Green formula.

Thus, for the affine reflected pair,

\[
b_s\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\tag{17}
\]

Consequently

\[
p\mid b_r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_s.
\tag{18}
\]

The simultaneous divisibility

\[
p\mid\gcd(b_r,b_s)
\tag{19}
\]

therefore has mod-`p` codimension **one**, not two.  Reflection copies the same condition to a second index; it does not supply an independent equation.

This is why every approach which treats `b_r=0` and `b_s=0` as two unrelated constraints overcounts the information.

# 4. Strong divisibility fails at the first useful test

A Lucas or elliptic-divisibility style strategy would be powerful if one had a characteristic-zero law such as

\[
\gcd(b_r,b_s)\mid b_{\gcd(r,s)}^{O(1)}
\tag{20}
\]

or even the basic implication `d|m => b_d|b_m`.  The Apéry numbers do not have this property.

Indeed

\[
b_2=73,
\qquad
b_4=33001,
\tag{21}
\]

and

\[
33001=452\cdot73+5,
\qquad
\gcd(73,33001)=1.
\tag{22}
\]

Thus `2|4` but `b_2` does not divide `b_4`, and even

\[
\gcd(b_2,b_4)\ne b_{\gcd(2,4)}.
\tag{23}
\]

So there is no strong-divisibility compression of `gcd(b_r,b_s)` to a smaller Apéry index.  This does not rule out a special theorem for the affine complementary pairs (8), but it removes the standard divisibility-sequence mechanism.

# 5. Wronskian and Green kernel: exact reason the route is nonselective

Let `u=min(r,s)`, `v=max(r,s)`, and `h=v-u>0`.  The tracked Green identity gives, for the companion solution `c`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N_h(u)}{\prod_{j=1}^{h}(u+j)^3}
   =b_u c_v-c_u b_v.}
\tag{24}
\]

Since `v<=p-1`, every factor in the denominator of (24) is a `p`-adic unit.

But reflection holds for **both** solutions:

\[
b_v\equiv b_u,
\qquad
c_v\equiv c_u
\pmod p.
\tag{25}
\]

Therefore

\[
b_u c_v-c_u b_v\equiv0\pmod p
\tag{26}
\]

for every reflected pair, whether or not `p|b_u`.  Hence

\[
\boxed{p\mid N_h(u)}
\tag{27}
\]

is a universal reflection consequence and is not target-selective.

This is stronger than saying that the continuant route is “hard.”  It says exactly what is wrong: after the second solution is introduced, the initial vector cancels and the resulting bridge is an operator-level invariant.  Reflection of every solution forces that invariant to vanish at every candidate prime.

At a dangerous zero, the ordinary Wronskian at the endpoint instead says that the companion/neighbor state is a unit modulo `p`; it supplies no additional factor of `p`.  Thus the Wronskian gives either a universal bridge zero or a unit statement, never a second target divisor.

The tracked `gap_kernel_green.tex` makes the other obstruction explicit: even though the Green differential equation has fixed order and fixed singular locus, the bridge length remains the coefficient index `[z^{h-1}]`.  A naive algebraization has order growing with `h`.  The affine relation (8) does not turn this moving coefficient index into a fixed-degree resultant.

# 6. Reflected generating functions have an explicit linear height floor

Let

\[
H_p(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1} b_jT^j.
\tag{28}
\]

Reflection is exactly the self-reciprocity congruence

\[
T^{p-1}H_p(T^{-1})\equiv H_p(T)\pmod p.
\tag{29}
\]

The coefficient at a reflected pair gives

\[
p\mid b_s-b_r
\tag{30}
\]

for **every** candidate prime, not just a dangerous one.  One might try to use the nonzero integer `b_s-b_r` itself as a characteristic-zero carrier.  Off center this has provably linear logarithmic height.

## Lemma: exact elementary lower bound

For `0<=a<m`,

\[
b_m-b_a\ge b_m-b_{m-1}
        \ge \binom{2m}{m}^2.
\tag{31}
\]

Indeed, in

\[
b_m=\sum_{k=0}^{m}
\binom mk^2\binom{m+k}{k}^2,
\tag{32}
\]

every old summand with `k<=m-1` is at least the corresponding summand at index `m-1`, and the new `k=m` summand is exactly `binom(2m,m)^2`.

Since the central binomial coefficient is the largest coefficient in `(1+X)^{2m}`,

\[
\binom{2m}{m}\ge \frac{4^m}{2m+1}.
\tag{33}
\]

Thus

\[
\log(b_m-b_a)
\ge 4m\log2-2\log(2m+1).
\tag{34}
\]

For a reflected off-center pair, `m=max(r,s)>=(p-1)/2`.  Therefore

\[
\log|b_s-b_r|
\ge 2(p-1)\log2-O(\log p).
\tag{35}
\]

Finally `n=qp+r<(q+1)p`, so `p>n/(q+1)`.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
\log|b_s-b_r|
\ge \frac{2\log2}{q+1}\,n-O_q(\log n).}
\tag{36}
\]

This is an unconditional **linear** lower bound for the most natural reflected characteristic-zero carrier.

The same lower bound applies to `b_s+b_r` and to `b_sb_r`, because each is at least `b_{max(r,s)}` and `b_m>=binom(2m,m)^2`.

So no raw linear combination/product obtained by merely exposing the two reflected Apéry values is an `o_q(n)`-height solution.  A useful carrier must perform genuinely nonlinear arithmetic compression, such as a gcd, before taking height.

# 7. The constant-term formula: the obvious fusions fail

The tracked Cartier audit uses the exact positive Laurent polynomial

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)
=\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz},
\tag{37}
\]

whose support has 22 points and for which

\[
b_j=\operatorname {CT}\Lambda^j.
\tag{38}
\]

This is genuinely distinguished-Apéry structure, so it is the correct place to look for an escape.  But the two immediate fusions do not work.

First,

\[
r+s=p-1.
\tag{39}
\]

If one identifies the Laurent variables and combines exponents, one gets

\[
\operatorname {CT}\Lambda^{r+s}=b_{p-1}.
\tag{40}
\]

Reflection at `j=0` gives

\[
\boxed{b_{p-1}\equiv b_0=1\pmod p.}
\tag{41}
\]

Thus the same-variable fusion is actually a `p`-adic **unit** at every candidate prime.  It cannot carry the dangerous prime.

Second, the affine relation

\[
(q+1)r+qs=n-q
\tag{42}
\]

does not repair this.  Constant term is not multiplicative in the exponent:

\[
(\operatorname {CT}\Lambda^r)^{q+1}
(\operatorname {CT}\Lambda^s)^q
\ne
\operatorname {CT}\Lambda^{n-q}
\tag{43}
\]

in general.  Using independent Laurent variables preserves the two constant terms, but then it simply gives products such as `b_r b_s`, whose height is already linear by Section 6.  Identifying variables destroys the independent coefficient conditions.

So a constant-term proof would need a new **coefficient-level** operation, not exponent fusion.

The tracked `gap_kernel_cartier_defect.tex` points in the same direction.  Its first Frobenius boundary is exactly the original Hasse coefficient `b_{a-1}`, and its direct truncation has degree at least `p-a-2`.  In other words, the known Cartier compression faithfully stores the target data rather than replacing it with an independent bounded-complexity invariant.

# 8. The honest Apéry-specific carrier and its exact digit descent

The preceding failures suggest that one should compress **arithmetically**, not linearly.

Define the full integer candidate set

\[
\mathcal M_{n,q}
=\left\{m\in\mathbb Z:
 m>\sqrt n,
 0\le n-qm<m
\right\}.
\tag{44}
\]

For `m in M_{n,q}` put

\[
r_m=n-qm,
\qquad
s_m=(q+1)m-n-1,
\tag{45}
\]

and

\[
d_m=\gcd(m,b_{r_m},b_{s_m}).
\tag{46}
\]

Remove the centered integer `m_c=(2n+1)/(2q+1)` if it exists, and define

\[
\boxed{
C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}
=\operatorname {lcm}_{\substack{m\in\mathcal M_{n,q}\\r_m\ne s_m}}d_m.}
\tag{47}
\]

This is a characteristic-zero integer built from the distinguished Apéry values and the affine relation.  It does not require `m` to be prime.

## Carrier property

If `p` is an off-center dangerous prime in quotient class `q`, then `m=p` belongs to `M_{n,q}` and

\[
p\mid p,
\qquad p\mid b_{r_p},
\qquad p\mid b_{s_p}.
\tag{48}
\]

Hence `p|d_p|C_{n,q}^{off}`.  Therefore, if

\[
R_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}
=\prod_{\substack{p>\sqrt n,\ \lfloor n/p\rfloor=q\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p},\ r_p\ne s_p}}p,
\tag{49}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{R_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}\mid C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}.}
\tag{50}
\]

The two affine endpoints cause no contamination: if `r_m=0` or `s_m=0`, then `b_0=1`, so `d_m=1` automatically.

The centered prime, if it exists and is dangerous, is appended separately and costs at most `log n`.

## Unconditional height

Because `d_m|m`,

\[
C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}
\mid \operatorname {lcm}\{m:m\le n/q\}
=\operatorname {lcm}(1,\ldots,\lfloor n/q\rfloor).
\tag{51}
\]

Hence, writing `psi` for the Chebyshev function,

\[
\boxed{
\log C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}
\le \psi(n/q)
=\frac nq+o_q(n).}
\tag{52}
\]

This is the strongest automatic bound I see: linear, not sublinear.

Therefore

\[
\log C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}=o_q(n)
\tag{53}
\]

is a precise new sufficient target.  Unlike the universal continuant conditions, (53) visibly depends on the actual Apéry initial vector.

## Exact prime-factor descent inside the carrier

There is additional structure which may make (53) attackable.

Let `ell>=5` be prime and suppose

\[
\ell\mid d_m.
\tag{54}
\]

Since `ell|m`, write

\[
m=k\ell.
\tag{55}
\]

Write the base-`ell` decomposition of `n` as

\[
n=t\ell+a,
\qquad 0\le a<\ell.
\tag{56}
\]

Then (45) becomes

\[
r_m=(t-qk)\ell+a,
\tag{57}
\]

and

\[
s_m=((q+1)k-t-1)\ell+(\ell-1-a).
\tag{58}
\]

Set

\[
u=t-qk,
\qquad
v=(q+1)k-t-1.
\tag{59}
\]

The candidate inequalities ensure `u,v>=0`, and

\[
\boxed{u+v=k-1.}
\tag{60}
\]

Apéry--Lucas and reflection modulo `ell` give

\[
b_{r_m}\equiv b_u b_a\pmod\ell,
\tag{61}
\]

\[
b_{s_m}
\equiv b_v b_{\ell-1-a}
\equiv b_v b_a
\pmod\ell.
\tag{62}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\ell\mid b_{r_m},b_{s_m}
\iff
\bigl(\ell\mid b_a\bigr)
\ \lor\ 
\bigl(\ell\mid b_u\ \text{and}\ \ell\mid b_v\bigr).}
\tag{63}
\]

This is the most useful tactical identity I found.

The first branch is precisely the original distinguished lower-digit condition

\[
\ell\mid b_{n\bmod\ell}.
\tag{64}
\]

The second branch is a new simultaneous-zero problem, but the two indices now satisfy

\[
u+v=k-1=\frac m\ell-1.
\tag{65}
\]

Thus composite contamination in the characteristic-zero carrier descends from scale `m` to scale `m/ell`.

This is a real arithmetic descent and is not present in the universal recurrence carrier.  Unfortunately it is not yet a proof of (53): the second branch in (63) is another two-zero problem, and no tracked theorem bounds the total mass of its iterates strongly enough for every `n`.

This is where I would put further effort if the goal is to continue this line.

# 9. A rigorous linear no-go for the universal carrier class

The preceding honest carrier escapes the following theorem because its divisibility depends on the actual integers `b_r,b_s`.  The theorem only kills constructions whose divisibility is universal once the reflection geometry is imposed.

## Proposition (fixed-`q` reflection-universal height floor)

Fix `q`.  Let `U_{n,q}` be a nonzero integer with the property that every prime

\[
p>\sqrt n,
\qquad
\frac{n+1}{q+1}<p<\frac nq,
\tag{66}
\]

apart from at most `O_q(1)` explicitly excluded endpoint/center primes, divides `U_{n,q}` **without using the distinguished condition** `p|b_{n-qp}`; that is, the divisibility follows only from operator-level recurrence/transfer/reflection identities valid for every `p`-integral solution.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\log|U_{n,q}|
\ge
\left(\frac1{q(q+1)}+o_q(1)\right)n.}
\tag{67}
\]

### Proof

For fixed `q` and sufficiently large `n`, the lower endpoint in (66) is larger than `sqrt(n)`.  Hence the product of all nonexcluded primes in (66) divides `U_{n,q}`.  Removing `O_q(1)` primes changes its logarithm by only `O_q(log n)`.  By the prime number theorem,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\frac{n+1}{q+1}<p<n/q}\log p
&=\vartheta(n/q)-\vartheta((n+1)/(q+1))+O_q(\log n)\\
&=\left(\frac1q-\frac1{q+1}\right)n+o_q(n)\\
&=\frac{n}{q(q+1)}+o_q(n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{68}
\]

Any nonzero integer divisible by that prime product has absolute value at least the product, proving (67).  `square`

## Scope

This proposition applies directly to a global carrier assembled from:

* the reflected difference `b_s-b_r`, whose `p`-divisibility does not use a zero;
* the cross-Casoratian/continuant (24), because reflection of both basis solutions already forces (27);
* common-kernel or transfer determinants that express the same first-row reflection identity;
* any resultant which eliminates only those universal transfer equations.

It is the fixed-`q` analogue of the primorial saturation already recorded in the tracked `q32_adjoint_obstruction.py` for another interpolation carrier.

It does **not** apply to (47), because a prime divides its local factor only when the distinguished Apéry value actually vanishes.  Nor does it rule out a higher `p`-adic lift or a genuinely Apéry-specific resultant.

The quantifier is important: (67) is for each **fixed** `q` as `n->infinity`.  If `q=q(n)->infinity`, the constant `1/(q(q+1))` tends to zero; this theorem then no longer excludes an `o(n)` carrier.  That is consistent with the manuscript's quotient reduction, where bounded quotients are the difficult regime.

# 10. Modular parametrization: why the standard use does not change the index problem

The classical modular interpretation of the Apéry generating function is valuable for Frobenius and Hasse information, but it does not turn `(b_n)` into a strong-divisibility or Hecke-multiplicative sequence in the coefficient index.

The point relevant here is simple.  A modular parametrization rewrites the generating function in a modular parameter; the integers `b_j` remain Taylor coefficients in that parameter.  They are not being identified with Fourier coefficients of a fixed normalized Hecke eigenform for which one could invoke multiplicativity at the indices `r,s`.

Therefore the usual modular machinery does not supply a characteristic-zero identity of the shape

\[
p\mid b_r,b_s
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid C_{n,q},
\qquad
\log|C_{n,q}|=o_q(n).
\tag{69}
\]

Any modular argument which merely reproves the mod-`p` palindrome is still inside the universal class of Section 9 and pays the linear primorial floor when globalized.

A genuinely new modular route would have to produce a norm, congruence ideal, or special-value integer tied to the **distinguished coefficient vector**, with both the divisibility (69) and an independent Archimedean `o(n)` height estimate.  No such statement is present in the current tracked repository, and I do not infer one from modularity alone.

# 11. Resultants: one valid local construction, but no sublinear globalization

There is a natural distinguished-Apéry polynomial

\[
B_m(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}
\binom mk^2\binom{m+k}{k}^2X^k,
\qquad B_m(1)=b_m.
\tag{70}
\]

If `p|b_r` and `p|b_s`, then `X=1` is a common root modulo `p` of `B_r` and `B_s`.  Hence, whenever the characteristic-zero resultant is nonzero,

\[
\boxed{
p\mid\operatorname {Res}_X(B_r,B_s).}
\tag{71}
\]

This is a bona fide Apéry-specific local carrier and therefore should not be dismissed by the universal no-go.

However, it does not currently solve the fixed-`q` problem.

1. The resultant depends on the moving pair `(r,s)`, hence on the candidate prime.  One still has to globalize over a linear set of possible pairs.
2. One must first prove that `Res(B_r,B_s)` is nonzero for every relevant pair; that is not automatic from `B_r(1),B_s(1)`.
3. The trivial resultant against `X-1` is just
   \[
   \operatorname {Res}(B_m,X-1)=B_m(1)=b_m,
   \tag{72}
   \]
   returning to the linear-height carriers of Section 6.
4. A Sylvester determinant for `B_r,B_s` has degrees growing with `r,s`; there is no tracked identity reducing it to bounded rank or sublinear height.

The tracked Green/Cartier result gives a rigorous no-go only for the **bounded-degree/fixed-rank** version of this idea: direct coefficient extraction has degree `Omega(p)` and the Frobenius defect stores the Hasse coefficient itself.  I do not claim that all nonlinear Apéry-polynomial resultants are impossible.

A reduced resultant whose prime support can be compared to the local gcd `d_m` in (46), together with a sharp global height estimate, would be a legitimate new escape.

# 12. What remains genuinely live

After this audit I would rank the remaining options as follows.

## Live A: the gcd/lcm carrier

Prove

\[
\log C_{n,q}^{\mathrm{off}}=o_q(n)
\tag{73}
\]

for the explicit integer (47).  This is the cleanest characteristic-zero formulation I know that both carries every dangerous prime and visibly uses the distinguished Apéry vector.

The digit descent (63) gives a concrete starting point: separate prime factors into the true lower-digit branch and the scale-reduced two-zero branch, then try to prove that repeated second-branch descent has sublinear total log mass.

## Live B: an Apéry-specific reduced resultant

Use the polynomials (70) or the constant-term model to construct a nonzero local resultant whose large prime content contains `d_m`, then show that its **reduced** content over all affine pairs has `o(n)` height.  No such reduction is currently proved.

## Live C: a genuinely higher `p`-adic reflection defect

Modulo `p`, reflection is operator-level and hence nonselective.  The first place where the distinguished vector could provide new information is a higher lift (for example, a reflection defect modulo `p^2` or a mixed Cartier jet) for which the coefficient of the defect vanishes specifically on `b_r=0`.  The tracked Cartier-defect theorem shows that the naive first lift is circular, but it does not exclude a new mixed construction.

# 13. Final scoped conclusion

For the fixed-`q` reflected pair, the center costs only `O(log n)`.  Off center, the apparent simultaneous divisibility

\[
p\mid b_r,
\qquad p\mid b_s
\tag{74}
\]

is only one mod-`p` condition because reflection holds for the entire two-dimensional recurrence solution space.  Consequently the natural Wronskian, transfer, common-kernel, and reflected generating-function carriers are universal rather than target-selective.  Their globalizations have the exact primorial height floor

\[
\left(\frac1{q(q+1)}+o_q(1)\right)n,
\tag{75}
\]

and the direct reflected coefficient already has the explicit local lower bound

\[
\frac{2\log2}{q+1}n-O_q(\log n).
\tag{76}
\]

That is the rigorous natural-class no-go.

The affine relation does, however, give the explicit nonlinear characteristic-zero carrier (47), and Lucas gives the exact scale descent (63) for its prime factors.  Those two formulas are the part of this audit that is not merely negative.  At present they yield only the linear upper bound (52), not the required `o_q(n)` estimate.

Accordingly, **the Apéry gcd conjecture is not proved here**.  A successful continuation must establish new arithmetic cancellation or sparsity for the distinguished gcd/lcm carrier, a reduced Apéry-specific resultant, or a genuinely new higher `p`-adic/constant-term invariant.
