# Q578 — Pascadi reduction for the Apéry CMRD operator

## Executive conclusion

The CMRD sum is **close to**, but not currently reducible in a black-box way to, the Kloosterman matrix treated by Pascadi.

The key point is:

\[
S(A;m)=\sum_{q<r\ {m prime}} e_m(A(qr)^{-1}),\qquad m=p\ell
\]

is a bilinear reciprocal sum. After completion it does produce Kloosterman sums, but the resulting object is not exactly

\[
\sum_{n_1,n_2}\alpha_{n_1}\beta_{n_2}{\rm Kl}(n_1n_2;c)
\]

with fixed coefficients. The coefficient \(A=h(\ell z-pz')\) moves with the modulus and with the Mellin zero sets. This is the missing compatibility condition.

The optimistic part is that CMRD averages over \(p,\ell\). This extra averaging is probably the correct way around the square-root barrier, but it requires a new "moving-modulus Kloosterman large sieve" rather than a direct application of Pascadi.

---

# (A) Reduction to Pascadi's Kloosterman matrix

## 1. The reciprocal bilinear form

Ignore the prime restriction temporarily and write

\[
S(A;m)=\sum_{q,r} \alpha_q\beta_r e_m(A\bar q\bar r).
\]

Set

\[
 n=qr.
\]

The phase depends only on the product:

\[
 e_m(A(qr)^{-1})=e_m(A\overline n).
\]

A multiplicative convolution of the coefficient sequences gives

\[
S(A;m)=\sum_n c_n e_m(A\bar n),
\]

where

\[
c_n=(\alpha*\beta)(n).
\]

For unrestricted integers this is a natural multiplicative convolution. For primes, however,

\[
c_n=\Lambda_2(n)
\]

(the semiprime indicator, essentially a two-fold von Mangoldt convolution).

This is already a warning: Pascadi's framework starts with arbitrary coefficients multiplying Kloosterman sums, whereas here the coefficients are sparse prime convolutions.

---

## 2. Completion

Use additive Fourier inversion on \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\):

\[
e_m(A\bar n)=\frac1m\sum_{t\bmod m}K_m(A,t)e_m(tn),
\]

where

\[
K_m(A,t)=\sum_{x\in(\mathbb Z/m)^*}e_m(A\bar x+tx)
\]

is the Kloosterman sum.

Therefore

\[
S(A;m)=\frac1m\sum_{t\bmod m}K_m(A,t)\widehat c(t).
\]

This is the correct Kloosterman decomposition.

The obstacle is that

\[
\widehat c(t)=\sum_{q,r\ {m prime}}e_m(tqr)
\]

is a prime bilinear exponential sum, not a bounded coefficient sequence.

Thus the reduction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{CMRD}\rightarrow\text{Kloosterman transform + prime bilinear dual}
}
\]

but not yet

\[
\boxed{
\text{CMRD}\rightarrow\text{Pascadi theorem directly}.
}
\]

---

## 3. Relation to Pascadi

Pascadi studies bilinear forms of the shape

\[
\sum_{m,n}\alpha_m\beta_n Kl(mn;c).
\]

After completion our kernel is

\[
Kl(A,t;m).
\]

The parameter \(A\) is harmless if fixed. The problem is that in CMRD:

\[
A=h(\ell z-pz')
\]

varies over

- the two moduli \(p,\ell\);
- the two zero sets \(Z_p,Z_\ell\);
- the Fourier frequency \(h\).

So Pascadi gives the correct local analytic technology, but the Apéry application needs a uniform family version.

---

# (B) Does averaging over p,ℓ help?

Yes. This is likely the decisive feature.

Pointwise in \(m=p\ell\), the variables \(q,r\) have length

\[
X/\log X\approx \sqrt m/\log X,
\]

which is below the usual bilinear threshold.

But CMRD asks for

\[
\sum_{p,\ell\sim X}S(A_{p,\ell};p\ell).
\]

The moduli are not arbitrary: they are products of two primes of the same size.

This creates three possible averaging mechanisms.

## 1. Reciprocity in the modulus variables

For \(m=p\ell\),

\[
(qr)^{-1}\pmod{p\ell}
\]

splits as

\[
(qr)^{-1}\pmod p,
\qquad
(qr)^{-1}\pmod\ell.
\]

Expanding the phase gives

\[
e_p(A\bar q\bar r)e_\ell(A\bar q\bar r).
\]

The primes \(p,\ell\) themselves become oscillatory variables. This is extra cancellation absent in a fixed-modulus theorem.

## 2. Large sieve over moduli

A possible target is

\[
\sum_{p,\ell}\left|S(A_{p,\ell};p\ell)\right|^2
\ll X^{o(1)}L^6.
\]

This is exactly a composite-modulus Type-II large sieve.

## 3. Avoiding absolute values too early

The CMRD expression already has the signed sum

\[
\sum_{p\ne\ell}\sum_{z,z'}S(h(\ell z-pz');p\ell).
\]

Taking

\[
\sum_{p,\ell,z,z'}|S(A;p\ell)|
\]

throws away the modulus cancellation.

The correct object is an averaged operator, not a collection of pointwise estimates.

# (C) What is the completed dual sum?

Starting with

\[
S(A;m)=\sum_{q,r} \Lambda(q)\Lambda(r)e_m(A\bar q\bar r),
\]

complete in \(q\):

\[
\sum_q e_m(A\bar q\bar r)
=\frac1m\sum_u K_m(A\bar r,u)\widehat W(u).
\]

Then complete in \(r\):

\[
K_m(A\bar r,u)
\]

itself Fourier transforms into another Kloosterman object.

The final structure is schematically

\[
\frac1{m^2}
\sum_{u,v}
K_m(A,u)K_m(v,u)\widehat{\Lambda W}(v)\widehat{\Lambda W}(u).
\]

The dual weights are therefore

\[
\widehat{\Lambda W}(u)
\]

or semiprime analogues.

## Can these be handled?

Partially.

For prime-supported sums one can use:

- Vaughan identity;
- Heath-Brown identity;
- Type I/II decompositions;
- Bombieri-Vinogradov input for ordinary primes.

However, the modulus is

\[
m=p\ell\sim X^2,
\]

so standard BV does not reach the required range. The modulus is exactly where a new bilinear Kloosterman estimate is needed.

The difficulty is not the Kloosterman side; it is the simultaneous presence of:

1. composite modulus \(p\ell\);
2. prime-supported coefficients;
3. moving coefficient \(A_{p,\ell}\).

---

# (D) Is there a modulus-average large sieve?

## Existing results

Several nearby theories exist.

### 1. Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel

FKM develop large sieve inequalities for trace functions over finite fields.

Relevant principle:

\[
\sum_{q\le Q}|\sum_n \alpha_n t_q(n)|^2
\]

can be controlled when \(t_q\) comes from bounded-conductor sheaves.

CMRD fails the hypothesis because

\[
A\mapsto e_{p\ell}(A(qr)^{-1})
\]

is not a fixed trace-function family in the modulus aspect.

---

### 2. Kowalski–Michel–Sawin

KMS handle bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums and obtain strong bounds in ranges unavailable to classical large sieve.

This is very close technically.

But their setting has a fixed modulus and fixed Kloosterman family. CMRD requires averaging over

\[
m=p\ell
\]

with moving \(A\).

---

### 3. Pascadi

Pascadi is the closest match.

The expected reduction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{CMRD}
\rightarrow
\text{bilinear Kloosterman form}
\rightarrow
\text{non-abelian amplification}
}
\]

However, one additional theorem is needed:

> a uniform Pascadi-type estimate for Kloosterman matrices with composite moduli \(p\ell\) and coefficients \(A=h(\ell z-pz')\) averaged over \(p,\ell\).

This is not currently in the theorem statement.

---

# Concrete proposed intermediate theorem

The right replacement for full CMRD is probably:

## Averaged reciprocal Kloosterman theorem (ARK)

For \(p,\ell\sim X\), \(p\ne\ell\), and coefficients

\[
A_{p,\ell}=h(\ell z-pz'),
\]
with \(z\in Z_p,z'\in Z_\ell\), prove

\[
\sum_{p,\ell}
\left|
\sum_{q,r\sim X}\alpha_q\beta_r
e_{p\ell}(A_{p,\ell}(qr)^{-1})
\right|^2
\ll X^{o(1}}L^6.
\]

A theorem of this shape would immediately imply CMRD by Cauchy-Schwarz plus ZM₂.

---

# Final answers

## (A)

Yes, CMRD has a Kloosterman expansion, but not directly in Pascadi's input form. Completion produces

\[
S(A;m)=m^{-1}\sum_t Kl(A,t;m)\widehat c(t),
\]

where \(\widehat c(t)\) is a prime bilinear transform.

## (B)

Modulus averaging is probably the key missing ingredient. It introduces cancellation in \(p,\ell\), unavailable pointwise.

## (C)

The dual sums involve Kloosterman transforms of prime bilinear weights. Vaughan/Heath-Brown handle the prime weights, but the composite-modulus square-root barrier remains.

## (D)

No existing theorem directly proves the required bound. The closest tools are:

- Pascadi: closest local Kloosterman technology;
- Kowalski–Michel–Sawin: bilinear Kloosterman framework;
- Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel: trace-function large sieve;
- Drappeau and related dispersion methods.

The missing theorem is a **moving-coefficient composite-modulus Kloosterman large sieve**.

That is the precise analytic formulation of CMRD.
