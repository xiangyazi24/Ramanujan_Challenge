# P3.2 — Minimal input for AMTD dispersion

Repository state inspected: `problems/3.2/proof.tex` on `main` at commit `f914983116049a10ced0844172d7e7f99a0eb238`, especially Hypothesis `hyp:amtd` / `hyp:ap-bdh` and Proposition `prop:ap-bdh-suffices`.

## Executive answer

1. **Yes: a uniform R2 row bound implies AMTD immediately.** The proposed conclusion is correct, but one displayed intermediate inequality is not: in general
   \[
   \sum_m |B_P(m)-\mu|\le 2S,
   \]
   not necessarily \(\le S\). The clean proof is the exact identity
   \[
   V^\circ=\sum_m B_P(m)^2-\frac{S^2}{N}
   \le \bigl(\max_m B_P(m)\bigr)S.
   \]

2. **R1 is not needed to deduce AMTD from R2.** However, **AMTD by itself does not imply \(W(n)=o(n)\)** because centered variance does not control the mean \(S/N\). In the current proposition, the unconditional input \(z_p=O(p^{2/3})\) supplies the needed first-moment bound on \(S\); R1 would be a stronger replacement, not an additional requirement. If R2 itself is available for the same block-count function used in \(W\), then R2 proves \(W(n)=o(n)\) directly, without AMTD, R1, or a bound for \(S\).

3. **Yes, a pure prime-factorization statement is sufficient**, and a polylogarithmic bound is stronger than the required \(N^{o(1)}\) bound. There is, however, a convention issue: the current `proof.tex` detector is the lower-digit condition \(p\mid b_{m\bmod p}\), while literal divisibility \(p\mid b_m\) also includes leading-digit zeros. By the Apéry–Lucas congruence, the literal factorization statement dominates the lower-digit R2, but is not generally equivalent to it.

4. **There is no theorem of this strength for arbitrary polynomial-coefficient recurrences.** The class is far too broad: \(u_n=n!\) satisfies \(u_{n+1}=(n+1)u_n\), yet every prime in \((n/2,n]\) divides \(u_n\). For Fibonacci numbers, the standard primitive-divisor, largest-prime-factor, and aggregate-prime-divisor theorems do not give the requested fixed-term dyadic upper bound. An elementary rank-of-apparition argument gives only about \(n^{1/2+o(1)}\), not \(n^{o(1)}\).

5. **The Apéry supercongruences are vertical in one prime and do not by themselves control simultaneous divisibility by many different primes.** They are useful when \(p\mid n\) and for valuations, but in the range \(p>\sqrt n\) at most one such prime divides the index. The relevant condition for almost all candidate primes is instead the moving-target Lucas condition. Bounding its simultaneous occurrence requires a genuinely cross-prime input such as R2, AMTD/large-sieve dispersion, or cross-prime algebraic rigidity.

## 0. A normalization that matters

Let
\[
I_N=(N,2N],\qquad |I_N|=N,
\]
and let \(B_P(m)\ge 0\) be the block incidence count. Define the **actual total mass**
\[
S_0(P,N):=\sum_{m\in I_N}B_P(m)
          =\sum_{P<p\le 2P}|\Omega_p|.
\]
The centered mean must be \(\mu=S_0/N\).

For the lower-digit detector
\[
\Omega_p=\{m\in I_N:m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\},
\qquad z_p=|\mathcal Z_p|,
\]
one has
\[
|\Omega_p|\le z_p\left(\left\lfloor\frac Np\right\rfloor+1\right),
\]
and therefore
\[
S_0(P,N)
\le \left(\frac NP+1\right)\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p.
\]
The expression with \(z_p(\lfloor N/p\rfloor+1)\) is generally an **upper bound**, not an exact equality, because different residue classes can occur either \(\lfloor N/p\rfloor\) or \(\lceil N/p\rceil\) times in the translated interval \((N,2N]\).

This distinction is harmless for the existing upper bounds, but it matters in the variance identity: center at the actual mass \(S_0/N\), not at an upper envelope for \(S_0\). Below I write \(S=S_0\).

There is a second convention issue. In the current repository, \(B_P(m)\) is built from the lower-digit events \(m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\). If one instead defines
\[
B_P^{\mathrm{fact}}(m)=\#\{P<p\le2P:p\mid b_m\},
\]
then \(S=\sum_p|\Omega_p|\) remains true with the newly defined \(\Omega_p\), but its column sizes are no longer given just by \(z_p\), because a higher base-\(p\) digit can also be a zero digit.

## 1. R2 implies AMTD

Put
\[
M(P,N):=\max_{m\in I_N}B_P(m),
\qquad \mu=\frac SN.
\]
Since \(B_P(m)\ge0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
V^\circ(P,N)
&=\sum_{m\in I_N}(B_P(m)-\mu)^2\\
&=\sum_{m\in I_N}B_P(m)^2-2\mu S+N\mu^2\\
&=\sum_{m\in I_N}B_P(m)^2-\frac{S^2}{N}\\
&\le \sum_{m\in I_N}B_P(m)^2\\
&\le M(P,N)\sum_{m\in I_N}B_P(m)\\
&=M(P,N)S(P,N).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the following uniform form of R2 is enough:
\[
\forall\varepsilon>0,\qquad
M(P,N)\ll_\varepsilon N^\varepsilon
\]
uniformly over all admissible dyadic blocks \(P\). It gives
\[
V^\circ(P,N)\ll_\varepsilon N^\varepsilon S(P,N),
\]
which is exactly AMTD in the usual \(N^{o(1)}\) notation. If \(S=0\), then \(B_P\equiv0\) and the conclusion is trivial.

### What is wrong with the proposed \(L^1\) step?

The inequality
\[
\sum_m|B_P(m)-\mu|\le \sum_m B_P(m)=S
\]
is false in general. For example, for the three values \((0,0,3)\), one has \(S=3\), \(\mu=1\), and the left side is \(4\).

The always-valid estimate is
\[
\sum_m|B_P(m)-\mu|\le 2S.
\]
Indeed, the total positive deviation equals the total negative deviation, and each is at most \(S\). Also \(0\le\mu\le M\), so \(\max_m|B_P(m)-\mu|\le M\). This alternative route gives \(V^\circ\le2MS\). The exact second-moment identity above improves the constant to \(1\).

A ready-to-insert lemma is:

```tex
\begin{lemma}[Row maximum dominates centered dispersion]
Let $I$ be a finite set of size $N$, let $B:I\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$,
and put $S=\sum_{m\in I}B(m)$ and $M=\max_{m\in I}B(m)$.
Then
\[
  \sum_{m\in I}\left(B(m)-\frac SN\right)^2
  \le MS.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding around the mean gives
\[
  \sum_m\left(B(m)-\frac SN\right)^2
  =\sum_m B(m)^2-\frac{S^2}{N}
  \le\sum_m B(m)^2
  \le M\sum_mB(m)=MS.
\]
\end{proof}
```

One indexing adjustment: because `proof.tex` uses \(m\in(N,2N]\), a hypothesis written as \(\max_{m\le N}\) should be applied with \(2N\), or reformulated directly on the shell. This does not change \(N^{o(1)}\), but the quantifiers should be explicit.

## 2. What AMTD needs, what R1 supplies, and why R2 bypasses both

There are three logically different kinds of information:

- **column mass / first moment:** control of \(z_p\), \(\sum z_p\), and hence \(S\);
- **dispersion:** AMTD controls fluctuations of the row counts around their mean;
- **row maximum:** R2 controls every individual row directly.

AMTD gives for every \(m\in I_N\)
\[
B_P(m)
\le \frac SN+\sqrt{V^\circ(P,N)}
\ll \frac SN+N^{o(1)}\sqrt S.
\]
Consequently, a bound on \(S\) is needed if AMTD is the only pointwise mechanism. Centered variance alone cannot control a large constant mean: an incidence system with
\[
B_P(m)\equiv L
\]
has \(V^\circ=0\), hence satisfies AMTD perfectly, while every row still contains \(L\) primes.

Let
\[
A(P):=\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p.
\]
For the lower-digit detector,
\[
S(P,N)\le\left(\frac NP+1\right)A(P).
\]
The existing unconditional estimate \(z_p=O(p^{2/3})\) yields
\[
A(P)\ll \frac{P^{5/3}}{\log P},
\qquad
S(P,N)\ll \frac{NP^{2/3}}{\log P}
\]
in the range used by `prop:ap-bdh-suffices` (with harmless endpoint modifications near \(P\asymp N\)). This is exactly the first-moment input used in the repository proof.

R1,
\[
A(P)=\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p\ll \frac P{\log P},
\]
would be much stronger. It would give roughly
\[
S(P,N)\ll\frac N{\log P}
\]
for \(P\le N\), and AMTD would then give a substantially better pointwise estimate. Therefore:

- R1 is **not required in addition to** the current \(z_p=O(p^{2/3})\) input;
- R1 could **replace** that input as a stronger first-moment hypothesis;
- some first-moment control is required if one assumes only AMTD;
- R1 alone still does not control worst-case clustering and therefore does not imply R2 or the all-\(n\) conclusion.

### R2 proves the weighted target directly

Suppose R2 bounds the exact block detector relevant to \(W\):
\[
B_P(n)\le n^{o(1)}
\]
uniformly for all dyadic \(P\) between \(\sqrt n\) and \(n\). Then
\[
W_P(n)\le B_P(n)\log(2P)\le n^{o(1)}\log n.
\]
There are only \(O(\log n)\) dyadic blocks, so
\[
W(n)
\ll n^{o(1)}(\log n)^2
=n^{o(1)}
=o(n).
\]
A bounded valuation multiplicity, such as the factor at most \(3\) for primes above \(\sqrt n\) in the current GCD argument, changes nothing.

Thus R2 is not merely sufficient for AMTD; for the pointwise weighted target it is stronger than needed and bypasses the AMTD-to-pointwise step entirely. The logical picture is:

```text
uniform R2  ───────────────►  AMTD
     │
     └─────────────────────►  W(n)=o(n) directly

AMTD + a first-moment bound on S  ─►  W(n)=o(n)
AMTD alone                       ─╳►  W(n)=o(n)
R1 alone                         ─╳►  worst-case row control
```

## 3. A clean prime-factorization formulation of R2

A shell-adapted subpolynomial formulation is
\[
\tag{R2-fact}
\forall\varepsilon>0,\qquad
\max_{N<m\le2N}\;
\max_{\sqrt N<P\le N}
\#\{p\text{ prime}:P<p\le2P,\ p\mid b_m\}
\ll_\varepsilon N^\varepsilon.
\]
If one wants a concrete stronger conjecture, one may state that there are absolute constants \(C_0,C\) such that
\[
\max_{N<m\le2N}\;
\max_{\sqrt N<P\le N}
\#\{p:P<p\le2P,\ p\mid b_m\}
\le C_0(\log(2N))^C.
\]
Equivalently, an all-initial-segments version is
\[
\max_{1\le m\le N}\;
\max_{\sqrt N<P\le N}
\#\{p:P<p\le2P,\ p\mid b_m\}
\le C_0(\log(2N))^C.
\]
The use of \(\log(2N)\), rather than \(\log m\), avoids a meaningless right side at \(m=1\) and makes the uniformity transparent. One can insert \(p\le m\) if only primes relevant to the GCD are desired; omitting it is a stronger statement.

The polylogarithmic form certainly implies \(N^{o(1)}\), but it is stronger than the minimal requirement. The analytically natural formulation is the \(\varepsilon\)-form in (R2-fact).

### Relation to the detector actually used in `proof.tex`

Gessel's Apéry–Lucas congruence says that if
\[
m=m_0+m_1p+\cdots+m_sp^s,
\]
then
\[
b_m\equiv\prod_{i=0}^s b_{m_i}\pmod p.
\]
Hence
\[
p\mid b_{m\bmod p}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid b_m.
\]
Therefore (R2-fact) implies the lower-digit R2 used by the AMTD incidence system.

The converse is false in general. In the two-digit range \(m=qp+r<p^2\),
\[
b_m\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
\]
Literal divisibility \(p\mid b_m\) can arise either from the lower digit \(p\mid b_r\) or from the leading digit \(p\mid b_q\). The current project deliberately separates these cases, and proves that a pure leading-digit zero does not contribute to the relevant GCD in the situation covered by `lem:leading-vanish`.

In the very top block \(p>m/2\), one has \(q=1\) and \(b_1=5\). Thus for \(p\ge7\),
\[
p\mid b_m\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid b_{m-p}.
\]
There the factorization and lower-digit formulations coincide. In lower blocks, (R2-fact) is a genuine strengthening because it also counts leading-digit factors that the GCD proof may discard.

A basic height estimate does not approach R2. Since the binomial formula gives \(\log b_m=O(m)\), if \(k\) distinct primes at least \(P\) divide \(b_m\), then
\[
P^k\le b_m,
\qquad
k\le\frac{\log b_m}{\log P}=O\!\left(\frac m{\log P}\right).
\]
For \(P>\sqrt m\), this is only \(O(m/\log m)\), far larger than \(m^{o(1)}\).

## 4. What recurrence-sequence theory currently gives

### 4.1 No theorem for arbitrary polynomial recurrences

The statement is false for the class of all sequences satisfying polynomial-coefficient recurrences. The first-order recurrence
\[
u_{n+1}=(n+1)u_n,\qquad u_0=1,
\]
has \(u_n=n!\). Every prime in \((n/2,n]\) divides \(u_n\), so the dyadic count is
\[
\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)\asymp\frac n{\log n},
\]
not \(n^{o(1)}\).

Thus any theorem must impose substantial additional hypotheses: at least controlled height, nondegeneracy, and usually a divisibility-sequence or algebraic-group structure. Being P-recursive by itself is not enough.

### 4.2 Fibonacci numbers

For \(F_n\), the immediate size bound is still only
\[
\#\{P<p\le2P:p\mid F_n\}
\le\frac{\log F_n}{\log P}
=O\!\left(\frac n{\log P}\right),
\]
which is \(O(n/\log n)\) for \(P>\sqrt n\).

The classical and modern literature contains strong but differently directed results:

- Bilu–Hanrot–Voutier prove that every Lucas or Lehmer term of index \(n>30\) has a primitive divisor. This is an **existence/lower-bound** theorem, not an upper bound for the number of factors in one dyadic block.
- Stewart and subsequent explicit refinements prove strong **lower bounds for the greatest prime factor** of a term of a nondegenerate binary recurrence.
- Murty–Séguin–Stewart estimate the number of distinct primes dividing **one of the first \(N\) terms** of a nondegenerate binary recurrence. This is an aggregate-in-index statement, not a uniform fixed-term dyadic upper bound.

I am not aware of a published theorem implying
\[
\max_{\sqrt n<P<n}
\#\{P<p\le2P:p\mid F_n\}=n^{o(1)},
\]
and the standard results above do not imply it. It should be treated as a strong additional hypothesis/open-type problem rather than as a routine consequence of linear-recurrence theory.

### 4.3 An elementary Fibonacci benchmark: \(n^{1/2+o(1)}\)

Let \(z(p)\) be the rank of apparition of \(p\) in the Fibonacci sequence. For \(p\ne2,5\),
\[
p\mid F_n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z(p)\mid n,
\]
and
\[
z(p)\mid p-\left(\frac5p\right).
\]
Fix a divisor \(d\mid n\), and count primes \(p\in(P,2P]\) with \(z(p)=d\). There are two elementary bounds.

First, all such primes divide \(F_d\), so their product divides \(F_d\); hence their number is
\[
O\!\left(\frac d{\log P}\right).
\]
Second, each satisfies \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod d\), so the number of candidates in an interval of length \(P\) is
\[
O\!\left(\frac Pd+1\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
K_d(P)\ll
\min\left\{\frac d{\log P},\frac Pd+1\right\}.
\]
Splitting at \(d=\sqrt{P\log P}\) and summing over \(d\mid n\) gives
\[
\#\{P<p\le2P:p\mid F_n\}
\ll \tau(n)\left(\sqrt{\frac P{\log P}}+1\right).
\]
For \(P\le n\), this is at most \(n^{1/2+o(1)}\). It is a useful structural improvement over the height bound, but it remains far from \(n^{o(1)}\). Reaching R2 would require new cancellation or rigidity beyond rank-of-apparition congruences and the divisor bound \(\tau(n)=n^{o(1)}\).

## 5. What the Apéry supercongruences do—and do not do

For primes \(p\ge5\), the supercongruence
\[
b_{mp^k}\equiv b_{mp^{k-1}}\pmod{p^{3k}}
\]
implies in particular
\[
b_{mp^k}\equiv b_m\pmod p.
\]
Thus if \(n=mp^k\) with \(p\nmid m\), then
\[
p\mid b_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid b_m.
\]
This is strong **vertical information for a fixed prime**: it strips powers of \(p\) from the index and can also control higher \(p\)-adic valuations.

It does not substantially constrain the number of distinct primes \(p\in(P,2P]\) dividing a fixed \(b_n\), for the following reason. In the pointwise range \(p>\sqrt n\), at most one prime \(p\) in the block can divide \(n\). Thus the supercongruence directly addresses at most one candidate prime. In the shell convention \(m\in(N,2N]\), \(p>\sqrt N\), the number of such primes dividing \(m\) is still \(O(1)\). All the other candidate primes satisfy \(p\nmid n\), where the displayed supercongruence imposes no index divisibility condition.

For those primes, the relevant relation is the Lucas congruence. If \(p>\sqrt n\), write
\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]
Then
\[
b_n\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p,
\]
so
\[
p\mid b_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q\in\mathcal Z_p\ \text{or}\ r\in\mathcal Z_p.
\]
For the lower-digit detector, the condition is simply
\[
n\bmod p=r\in\mathcal Z_p.
\]
This is a **moving-target, cross-prime problem**: both the residue \(n\bmod p\) and the zero set \(\mathcal Z_p\) vary with \(p\).

Small cardinality of each \(\mathcal Z_p\) is not enough. The adversarial construction already recorded in `proof.tex` makes this explicit: for one chosen \(n_0\), sets of size two can be chosen so that \(n_0\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\) for every prime \(p\), even while preserving the reflection symmetry. Hence even the hypothetical bound \(z_p\le2\), plus reflection, would not imply R2 without some cross-prime nonalignment principle.

The supercongruences and the one-prime recurrence geometry can therefore help with:

- primes dividing the index;
- repeated powers of one prime;
- valuations and vertical lifting;
- the internal structure of each individual zero set \(\mathcal Z_p\).

They do **not**, by themselves, control how the zero sets for different primes line up at one fixed integer \(n\). To prove R2 one needs a horizontal input, for example:

- AMTD or a large-sieve/dispersion theorem strong enough to control row maxima;
- a cross-prime resultant or algebraic-independence mechanism for the zero conditions;
- a direct theorem on the large-prime factorization of \(b_n\);
- a high factorial-moment estimate of the kind already discussed elsewhere in the project.

## Recommended formulation for the paper

The cleanest way to organize the hypotheses is to state the row-max lemma unconditionally and then distinguish two alternative sufficient inputs.

```tex
\begin{hypothesis}[Apéry dyadic row sparsity (R2)]
For every $\varepsilon>0$, uniformly for dyadic $I_N=(N,2N]$
and $\sqrt N<P\le N$,
\[
  \max_{m\in I_N} B_P(m) \ll_\varepsilon N^\varepsilon.
\]
\end{hypothesis}
```

Then record:

1. R2 implies AMTD by \(V^\circ\le (\max B_P)S\).
2. R2 implies the lower-digit weighted contribution is \(n^{o(1)}\) directly.
3. The existing route remains useful as a weaker, more genuinely dispersion-based alternative:
   \[
   \text{AMTD}+z_p=O(p^{2/3})\Longrightarrow W(n)=o(n).
   \]
4. R1 is an optional stronger first-moment hypothesis for the AMTD route, not a prerequisite for the implication R2 \(\Rightarrow\) AMTD.

If the intention is specifically a pure factorization conjecture, call it `R2-fact` and state separately that Gessel–Lucas gives
\[
R2\text{-fact}\Longrightarrow R2\text{-lower-digit}.
\]
This avoids silently identifying two incidence systems that are equal only in the top block.
