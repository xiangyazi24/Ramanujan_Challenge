# Q7165: P32 variance overlap route

## 1. Expansion of the second moment

Write

\[
C_p(q)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal C_p(q)}1,
\]

where \(\alpha=(x;a,b,c)\) is a carrier-clean primitive off-center four-return chain with total span
\[
s=a+b+c\le H,\qquad H=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor.
\]

The variance contribution is bounded by
\[
\sum_q C_p(q)^2
=\sum_{\alpha,\beta:\,q(\alpha)=q(\beta)}1.
\]

Split pairs into:

1. identical pairs (diagonal);
2. overlapping return intervals;
3. reflection-related pairs;
4. separated pairs.

The question is whether (2) alone is small enough for weak-DPLS.

---

## 2. Overlapping pairs: deterministic structure

Suppose
\[
[x,x+s], [y,y+t]
\]
overlap. Because each chain has four zero positions
\[
x,z_1,z_2,z_3,x+s
\]
with three primitive gaps, the union has at most seven zero positions and span at most
\[
2H.
\]

The possible overlap patterns are therefore finite:

- one common return point: 6 or 7 zero positions;
- two common return points: 5 or 6 zero positions;
- containment/reflection degeneracies.

The correct deterministic reduction is not a generic gcd-content assertion. Instead use the continuant identities already present in `proof.tex`:

\[
N_{u+v}(x)=N_u(x+v)N_v(x)-x\text{-shift correction},
\]

and the reflection law
\[
N_h(x)=\pm (x+h-1)^6N_h(-x-h+1)
\]
(in the appropriate normalized form).

For a fixed overlap pattern, the two chains impose a bounded collection of polynomial equations in the gap variables. Since all gaps are \(\le H\), the number of possible integer gap patterns is
\[
O(H^6)
\]
for six free gaps and
\[
O(H^5)
\]
for five free gaps.

However this is a count over formal patterns, not yet over primes.

---

## 3. Arithmetic prime bound for a fixed overlap pattern

A fixed pattern gives simultaneous congruences
\[
F_1(x)=F_2(x)=\cdots=0\pmod p,
\]
where each \(F_i\) is a continuant polynomial of degree comparable to the number of remaining steps.

The safe bound without an unproved content hypothesis is obtained by eliminating \(x\):

\[
R=\operatorname{Res}_x(F_i,F_j).
\]

Either:

- the resultant is identically zero, which must be handled by an exact common-factor/reflection analysis; or
- every admissible prime divides a fixed nonzero integer \(R\).

For non-identically-zero resultants, the height estimate gives
\[
\log |R| \ll H\log H.
\]

Therefore the number of prime divisors contributed by one pattern is
\[
\omega(R)\ll H.
\]

This gives the aggregate overlap estimate
\[
\sum_{X<p\le 2X}E_{\rm ov}(p)
\ll H^7X^{o(1)}+E_{\rm refl},
\]

where the first term comes from
\[
\#\text{patterns}=H^6
\]
and \(O(H)\) possible prime divisors.

With \(H=X^{1/2}\), this is
\[
X^{7/2+o(1)},
\]

which is far above the required weak-DPLS scale
\[
X^{2+o(1)}/\log X.
\]

Therefore raw overlap counting is insufficient.

---

## 4. Reflection cases

Reflection is special because it can identify a chain with its mirror. These are not generic overlaps.

After quotienting by the reflection involution, the remaining equations lose one degree of freedom. The reflection contribution is bounded by the number of short palindromic continuants:

\[
E_{\rm refl}\ll H^3X^{o(1)}.
\]

This is negligible:
\[
H^3=X^{3/2},
\]

so reflection is not the obstruction.

---

## 5. Why overlap does not close weak-DPLS

The obstruction is not a failure of the continuant identities. The identities only compress a chain after the long return segment is already specified. In the overlap case there are still many possible local gap configurations.

The bad exponent is structural:

\[
\#(5\text{-}7\text{ zero unions})\approx H^6.
\]

Even granting a strong arithmetic saving of one prime-divisor factor, one obtains only
\[
H^6X^{o(1)}=X^{3+o(1)},
\]

which misses the target by a factor of \(X\).

No proof based only on bounded-gap overlap equations can reach
\[
X^{2+o(1)}/\log X.
\]

A new input must exploit cancellation/variance or control separated pairs.

---

## 6. Residual separated-pair term

After removing diagonal, reflection, and overlap contributions, the remaining term is

\[
\mathcal E_{\rm sep}(X)
=
\sum_{X<p\le2X}
\sum_q
\sum_{\substack{\alpha\ne\beta\\q(\alpha)=q(\beta)\\\operatorname{dist}(\alpha,\beta)>H}}
1.
\]

Here the starts satisfy
\[
L=|x-y|\gg H.
\]

The addition law

\[
N_{x+1+h}(-1)=N_{x+2}(-1)N_h(x)-(x+1)^6N_{x+1}(-1)N_{h-1}(x+1)
\]

cannot eliminate this term. It only factors a long return into:

- a short prefix coefficient;
- a long continuation \(N_h(x)\).

The unknown continuation still has length \(L\), and its vanishing is exactly the separated long-return event. There is no bounded-gap replacement involving only the two short chains.

---

## 7. Projective-state warning

The equality is
\[
q(\alpha)=q(\beta)\in \mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p),
\]
not necessarily
\[
\pi_x=0.
\]

The state is basis invariant. A coordinate zero depends on the chosen solution basis. Therefore arguments that replace a projective collision by two scalar zero conditions are invalid unless the verifier has fixed the canonical basis and proved invariance.

---

## 8. Final conclusion

The sharp conclusion is:

* Reflection pairs are controllable: \(\ll H^3X^{o(1)}\).
* Generic overlap pairs reduce to finitely many continuant/resultant problems, but the deterministic exponent is at best
  \[
  H^6\cdot H\cdot X^{o(1)}=H^7X^{o(1)}=X^{7/2+o(1)},
  \]
  without an additional structural theorem.
* Therefore overlap alone does **not** satisfy weak-DPLS.
* The unresolved term is the separated-pair energy \(\mathcal E_{\rm sep}(X)\); it requires a genuinely new input (transfer-matrix mixing, projective equidistribution, or an averaged determinant argument), not another bounded-gap continuant identity.
