# 3. Coefficient zeros are not Hasse roots

The row event is

\[
r\in\mathcal Z_s
\iff [a^r]A_s(a)=b_r=0\pmod s.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The root-orbit event is

\[
B_s(\alpha)=0,
\qquad \alpha\in\mathbb F_{s^2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

These are dual kinds of data. Equation (2.1) says that \(b_r=0\) removes one
coordinate in a triangular basis. It supplies no distinguished evaluation
point \(\alpha\).

## Exact actual example at \(s=7\)

The actual Apéry Hasse polynomial is

\[
A_7(a)=1+5a+3a^2+3a^3+3a^4+5a^5+a^6.
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
\boxed{
A_7(a)=(1-a+a^2-a^3)^2
      =(a-1)^2(a^2+1)^2\pmod7.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus \(B_7\) has the root \(1\in\mathbb F_7\) and the two roots of
\(a^2+1\) in \(\mathbb F_{49}\), while **none** of the coefficients of
\(A_7\) is zero. Hasse roots exist independently of coefficient marks. The
local package therefore does not define a map

\[
\{r:b_r=0\}\longrightarrow\{\alpha:B_s(\alpha)=0\}.
\]

This does not refute a special marked-shell theorem. It proves that such a
theorem is additional and cannot be inferred from (2.2)–(2.4).

## Theorem 5.1 — tensor vanishing

Let \(A\) be killed by \(q\) and \(C\) killed by \(r\), with
\(\gcd(q,r)=1\). Choose \(u,v\in\mathbb Z\) with \(uq+vr=1\). Then

\[
a\otimes c
=(uq+vr)(a\otimes c)
=u(qa\otimes c)+v(a\otimes rc)=0.

\[
\boxed{A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}C=0.}
\tag{5.1}

no nonzero cross-characteristic tensor algebra on which to take the desired

\tag{5.2}

# 7. Audit of the proposed local-to-global routes

## 7.1 Order-two ODE

The ODE canonically reconstructs \(B_s\) in each characteristic. It changes
the coefficients of the separate equations \(P_s(X_s)=0\), but it supplies no
equation involving \(X_q\) and \(X_r\) for \(q\ne r\). The quotient basis in
Section 6 remains valid.

The common integer supplies coefficient indices \(m\bmod s\), not Hasse-root
coordinates. CRT allows the residue indices to vary independently before the
special zero-set condition is imposed. Thus sharing \(m\) does not create a
root relation.

## 7.2 Franel pullback

Equation (2.1) identifies \(b_r\) as a shell coordinate. A zero coordinate
does not imply that \(K_s\), \(A_s\), \(B_s\), or the basis element
\(\Psi_{s,r}\) vanishes at a canonically selected point. Isolating a coordinate
requires a dual linear functional; the pullback does not show that this
functional is evaluation at a Hasse root.

A selector-content product can encode an OR over marked shell rows, but unless
one proves additional cancellation its content is charged to the original
marked coefficients and retains the same height problem. It is a selector,
not a seven-characteristic compression.

## 7.3 Norm over all roots

A norm over \(\mathbb F_s[a]/(B_s)\) has local degree
\(\deg B_s\asymp X\). More importantly, no visible implication says that a
coefficient zero makes a chosen evaluation polynomial vanish at a root. The
norm either returns a power of the original scalar coefficient or introduces
unmarked root phantoms.

## 7.4 Norm over a marked factor

This is the best local variant. It removes the large local degree but not the
tensor-zero, CRT-separation, or rank-product obstructions. A marked factor is
necessary for a root route and still not sufficient.

## 7.5 Resultants of characteristic-zero truncations

The ODE produces canonical rational truncations, but a root of the
\(q\)-truncation modulo \(q\) and a root of the \(r\)-truncation modulo \(r\)
are not reductions of a common algebraic root. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Res}(B_q^\sharp,B_r^\sharp)
\]

has no forced divisibility from the two coefficient-zero events. Reducing
\(B_r^\sharp\) modulo \(q\) tests a polynomial unrelated to the event in
characteristic \(r\).

## 7.6 Evaluation at the common integer

The implication

\[
b_{m\bmod s}=0\pmod s
\]

does not imply \(B_s(m)=0\pmod s\). The actual \(p=7\) example already
separates evaluation roots from coefficient marks. Hence
\(\prod_sB_s(m)\) is not a carrier.

## 7.7 Product of marked Apéry coefficients

This is exactly (V1). It is valid, nonzero, retains all seven labels, and is
the strongest unconditional product formula found. Its terminal defect is
linear logarithmic height.
