# 3. Actual coefficient/evaluation separation

## Theorem 3.1 — a coefficient zero is not a Hasse root condition

The event defining an Apéry row is

\[
r\in\mathcal Z_s
\iff [a^r]A_s(a)=b_r=0\pmod s.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The root-orbit package concerns

\[
B_s(\alpha)=0,
\qquad \alpha\in\mathbb F_{s^2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equations (3.1) and (3.2) live in dual coordinate systems.  The Franel
pullback (2.1) reinforces this distinction: \(b_r=0\) says that the basis
coordinate of \(\Psi_{s,r}\) in \(K_s^2\) vanishes.  It does not say that
\(K_s\), \(A_s\), or \(B_s\) vanishes at a point determined by \(r\).

### Exact actual example at \(s=7\)

The Apéry row is

\[
A_7(a)
=1+5a+3a^2+3a^3+3a^4+5a^5+a^6.
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
\boxed{
A_7(a)=(1-a+a^2-a^3)^2
      =(a-1)^2(a^2+1)^2\pmod7.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the actual normalized square root has the root \(1\in\mathbb F_7\)
and the two roots of \(a^2+1\) in \(\mathbb F_{49}\), while **none** of the
seven coefficients of \(A_7\) is zero.  Root orbits exist regardless of
whether the coefficient mark exists.  Consequently there is no canonical
map

\[
\{r:b_r=0\}\longrightarrow\{\alpha:B_s(\alpha)=0\}
\]

provided by (2.2), (2.4), reciprocity, or the ODE alone.

This does not disprove a special marked-shell theorem.  It proves that such a
theorem would be genuinely additional and cannot be silently inferred from
“the roots lie in \(\mathbb F_{s^2}\).”

## Theorem 3.2 — abstract reciprocal squares retain triangular coefficient freedom

The checked-in `hasse_square_no_go.tex` proves that, for a normalized
reciprocal polynomial

\[
B(a)=1+x_1a+\cdots+x_{d-1}a^{d-1}+a^d,
\]

and \(H(a)=\Delta(a)^\varepsilon B(a)^2\), the map

\[
(x_1,\ldots,x_h)\mapsto([a]H,\ldots,[a^h]H)

is a triangular polynomial automorphism whenever \(h<d/2\).  The diagonal
entries are \(2\).  Hence reciprocity and the square shape alone permit an

The order-two ODE distinguishes the actual \(B_s\) from this abstract family,
but it remains a one-characteristic recurrence.  To affect the fresh
seven-label term one still needs a theorem saying how the distinguished ODE
solutions in six or seven **different** characteristics interact.  No such
interaction is present in the local recurrence.

## Theorem 4.1 — low-digit product carrier

Every Apéry number \(b_u\) is a positive integer.  Therefore the integer in
(V1) is nonzero.  Each of the seven distinct primes divides its associated

\[
p^2q_1q_2q_3r_1r_2r_3
\mid C^{\rm low}_{p;\mathbf q,\mathbf r}(m,n),
\tag{4.1}
\]

so the squarefree seven-label divisibility (V2) follows.

This is already better than the generic carrier \(b_mb_n\): every index in
(V1) is \(<2X\), regardless of the size of \(m,n<X^2\).

## Theorem 4.2 — height ledger

The elementary Apéry sum gives

\[
0<b_u
=\sum_{k=0}^u\binom uk^2\binom{u+k}{k}^2
\le (u+1)64^u.
\tag{4.2}
\]

There are eight factors in (V1), counting the output twice, and every index is
less than \(2X\).  Therefore

\[
\log C^{\rm low}_{p;\mathbf q,\mathbf r}(m,n)
\le16X\log64+O(\log X)
=O(X).
\tag{4.3}
\]

For a single common point \(m=n\), one can remove one repeated output factor
if only squarefree divisibility is desired; the scale remains \(O(X)\).

Conversely, the \(k=u\) summand gives

\[
b_u\ge\binom{2u}{u}^2
\ge\frac{16^u}{(2u+1)^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence a residue \(u\ge cX\) forces

\[
\log b_u\ge cX\log16-O(\log X).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The available local facts do not imply that all eight selected residues have
sum \(o(X)\).  Reflection replaces \(u\) by \(s-1-u\), but this only puts one
representative in a half interval of length \(\asymp X\); it does not make the
representative sublinear.  Thus the exact low-digit carrier stops precisely
at the linear-height wall.

## Theorem 4.3 — common-index carrier

The Apéry Lucas law says that if

\[
m=m_0+m_1s+\cdots+m_ks^k,

\[
b_m\equiv\prod_i b_{m_i}\pmod s.
\tag{4.6}

In particular a low-digit hit implies \(s\mid b_m\).  Applying this to the

\[

\[
\log|b_mb_n|=O(m+n)=O(X^2).

The common integer therefore provides exact cross-characteristic

# 5. Why local trace/norm does not couple characteristics

The most tempting route is to attach to each prime \(s\) a root algebra

\[
E_s=\mathbb F_s[a]/(P_s(a)),
\qquad P_s\mid B_s,
\qquad \deg P_s\in\{1,2\},
\]

then tensor the six fresh algebras and take a trace or norm.  This construction
fails before any height estimate.

## Theorem 5.1 — distinct-characteristic tensor vanishing

Let \(A\) be a \(\mathbb Z\)-algebra killed by \(q\) and \(C\) a
\(\mathbb Z\)-algebra killed by \(r\), with \(\gcd(q,r)=1\).  Choose integers
\(u,v\) with \(uq+vr=1\).  For every pure tensor,

\[
\begin{aligned}
a\otimes c
&=(uq+vr)(a\otimes c)\\
&=u(qa\otimes c)+v(a\otimes rc)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}C=0.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
E_q\otimes_{\mathbb Z}E_r=0
\qquad(q\ne r).
\]

There is therefore no nonzero finite algebra over which one can take the
proposed seven-characteristic tensor norm directly.

## Theorem 5.2 — CRT gives a product, not a coupling

For

\[
N=pq_1q_2q_3r_1r_2r_3,
\]

CRT gives

\[
\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z
\cong
\mathbb F_p\times\prod_i\mathbb F_{q_i}\times\prod_j\mathbb F_{r_j}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The right side has seven orthogonal idempotents.  Any finite étale algebra,
trace, or norm constructed componentwise remains a seven-tuple.  If every
local marked element vanishes, the CRT tuple is simply zero.  A nonzero
integer lifting it is an arbitrary multiple of \(N\); choosing \(N\) itself is
the tautological label product and carries no information about how often the
configuration occurs.

This is the exact distinction:

- **tensor product:** would couple, but is zero across distinct
  characteristics;
- **CRT direct product:** is nonzero, but keeps the characteristics separated;
- **characteristic-zero lift:** can couple, but requires a new compatible
  horizontal object not supplied by the local identities.

# 6. Exact separated-orbit rank computation

Suppose, optimistically, that the missing marked-coupling source supplies a

\[
P_s(X)\mid B_s(X)

\mathcal A_S
=K[X_s:s\in S]/(P_s^\sharp(X_s):s\in S).
\tag{6.1}

## Theorem 6.1 — product-rank theorem

Because each relation is monic and uses a different variable, repeated
Euclidean division gives the basis

\[
\left\{
\prod_{s\in S}X_s^{e_s}:
0\le e_s<\deg P_s
\right\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\dim_K\mathcal A_S=\prod_{s\in S}\deg P_s.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

No nonzero \(K\)-linear relation among these standard monomials follows from
the local equations.

For six quadratic factors,

\[
\dim_K\mathcal A_S=2^6=64.
\tag{6.3}
\]

If a quadratic output factor is retained as a seventh channel, the dimension
is

\[
2^7=128.
\tag{6.4}
\]

If a marked irreducible factor and its reciprocal are distinct, closing the
local algebra under reciprocity replaces degree \(d_s\) by

\[
e_s=d_s+\deg P_s^*,
\]

so the reciprocal-closed rank is \(\prod e_s\), never smaller than (6.2).

## Exact trace-pairing certificate

For a reciprocal quadratic

\[
X_s^2-t_sX_s+1=0,
\]

multiplication in the basis \((1,X_s)\) gives the trace Gram matrix

\[
G_s=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&t_s\\
t_s&t_s^2-2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det G_s=t_s^2-4.
\tag{6.5}

\[
G_{q_1}\otimes G_{q_2}\otimes G_{q_3}

of rank \(64\), and

\[
\det G
=\prod_{s\in S}(t_s^2-4)^{32}.
\tag{6.6}

Including the output gives rank \(128\) and exponent \(64\) in the determinant.
The owned verifier checks this exact matrix rank and determinant formula.

# 7. Why the ODE does not reduce the mixed rank

The order-two ODE has two genuine consequences:

1. it canonically selects the actual \(B_s\) among abstract reciprocal square
   roots;
2. it permits exact reconstruction and verification in each characteristic.

It does **not** add an equation involving \(X_q\) and \(X_r\) for distinct
characteristics.  Algebraically, it changes the coefficients of the separate
relations

\[
P_q^\sharp(X_q)=0,
\qquad P_r^\sharp(X_r)=0,
\]

but it leaves the ideal separated by variables.  The quotient basis in
Section 6 remains valid.

The common integer does not alter this conclusion.  It supplies residue
indices

\[
r_s=m\bmod s.
\]

CRT says that, before imposing the special zero-set membership, the tuple
\((r_s)_s\) is an arbitrary element of the product of residue fields.  The
index \(r_s\) is a coefficient label for \(A_s\); it is not the Hasse root
coordinate \(X_s\).  Thus no equation among the \(X_s\) follows from sharing
\(m\).

A successful use of the ODE would need a new theorem of the form

\[
b_r=0\pmod s
\Longrightarrow
\text{a canonically selected ODE root orbit }P_{s,r}\mid B_s,
\tag{7.1}
\]

with a selection rule that is compatible across \(s\).  This is exactly the
missing marked-root bridge, not a consequence of order two by itself.

# 8. Why the Franel pullback does not provide the bridge

The identity

\[
K_s(x)^2=\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}b_r\Psi_{s,r}(x)
\]

is unusually strong: it is an exact change of basis.  But its strength points
in the wrong direction for a root norm.

A zero \(b_r=0\) removes one basis coordinate.  It does not imply

\[
K_s(\xi)=0,
\qquad
\Psi_{s,r}(\xi)=0,
\qquad
\text{or}
\qquad
B_s(\phi(\xi))=0
\]

for a canonically determined \(\xi\).  Linear independence of the shells
prevents one coordinate from being recovered as a fixed evaluation functional
on the other coordinates.

One can package an OR over shell rows by multiplying linear selector factors
and taking content.  The exact content is then the product of the row gcds.
After intersecting with the marked Apéry value, this is phantom-free, but the
result is still charged to the original \(b_r\)'s and has quadratic aggregate
height in the worst case.  That construction is an exact selector, not a
cross-characteristic compression.

Therefore a “Franel norm” must add one of two new facts:

- a bounded-degree evaluation point selected by a zero coordinate, or
- cancellation/anti-concentration after averaging many shell coordinates.

Neither is present in (2.1).

# 9. Audit of the natural norm/resultant constructions

## 9.1 Norm over all roots of \(B_s\)

Taking

\[
\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb F_s[a]/(B_s)}U_{s,r}(a)
\]

is meaningful only after choosing a polynomial \(U_{s,r}\).  If \(U\) is built
so that its norm is the coefficient \(b_r\), the norm merely returns
\(b_r^{\deg B_s}\) or a scalar multiple and worsens height.  If \(U\) is an
evaluation selector, coefficient zero does not force \(U\) to vanish at a
root, so the norm has false positives or no divisibility.

Using all roots also has local degree \(\deg B_s\asymp X\), so a sixfold
resultant has enormous multidegree before one addresses height.

## 9.2 Norm over a marked degree-\(\le2\) factor

This is the best possible local version.  It removes the degree problem, but
Sections 5–6 show the remaining obstruction:

- there is no nonzero tensor algebra over \(\mathbb Z\);
- a CRT product has no mixed norm;
- an arbitrary characteristic-zero lift has rank \(64\) or \(128\) and no
  mixed relation.

Thus a marked factor is necessary for the root route, but not sufficient.

## 9.3 Resultant of characteristic-zero ODE truncations

The two ODE solutions do give canonical rational polynomials obtained by
truncation.  However, a root of the \(q\)-truncation modulo \(q\) and a root of
the \(r\)-truncation modulo \(r\) are not reductions of one common root.
Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Res}(B_q^\sharp,B_r^\sharp)
\]

has no forced divisibility from the two marked coefficient events.  Reducing
\(B_r^\sharp\) modulo \(q\) tests a polynomial unrelated to the \(r\)-event.
The common integer is a coefficient index and supplies no common evaluation
variable for the resultant.

## 9.4 Evaluating \(B_s\) at the common integer

The implication

\[
b_{m\bmod s}=0\pmod s
\]

does not imply \(B_s(m)=0\pmod s\).  The \(p=7\) example already separates
these notions.  Therefore

\[
\prod_s B_s(m)
\]

is not a carrier.

## 9.5 Product of the marked Apéry coefficients

This is exactly (V1).  It is valid, nonzero, retains every label, and is the
strongest unconditional product formula found.  Its terminal defect is the
linear logarithmic height.

# 10. Short-output-gap partial result

There is one genuine partial route when the two points \(m,n\) are close in
the output characteristic.

Let

\[
u=m\bmod p,
\qquad v=n\bmod p,
\qquad h=|u-v|
\]

in a nonwrapping orientation.  If \(p\mid b_u,b_v\), the Apéry transfer
continuant gives

\[
N_h(u)=0\pmod p.
\tag{10.1}
\]

For prescribed additional output zeros, fixed-gap resultants provide nonzero
characteristic-zero carriers.  The checked-in resultant estimates have

\[
\log|\operatorname{Res}(N_h(X),N_k(X+\cdots))|
=O((h+k)^2\log(h+k)).
\tag{10.2}
\]

Consequently a dyadic sector with every relevant output separation at most
\(H\) can be charged to a resultant bank of logarithmic height

\[
O(H^3\log H).
\tag{10.3}
\]

Taking \(H=X^{1/3-\eta}\), for example, gives \(o(X)\) height for the
**output-prime support**.

This does not close the fresh seven term.  It controls only \(p\), while the
six fresh labels remain independent.  It is nevertheless the strongest
rigorous localization available from the existing gap machinery:

> Any genuinely new seven-characteristic obstruction may be restricted to
> pairs whose output residues have separation \(X^{1/3-o(1)}\) or larger,
> unless a sharper shared-resultant mass estimate is proved.

\[

 g_{q_1}(m)g_{q_2}(m)g_{q_3}(m).
\tag{12.1}
\]

Define the exact output-Palm contrast

\[
\Delta_p^{[3]}

\frac1{Mz_p}\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)Y_{-p}^{[3]}(m),
\tag{12.2}
\]

with the manuscript’s finite-window intercept inserted if (4.3) uses
\(f_p-z_p\) in a different order.  Squaring (12.2) produces:

- identical triples;
- triples sharing one or two nonoutput labels;

The fresh class retains exactly

\[
p,q_1,q_2,q_3,r_1,r_2,r_3.

# 13. Strongest plausible algebraic intermediate lemma

A root-based proof of (12.3) first needs the following bridge.

> **[MARKED-HORIZONTAL-COVER].**  There exists a fixed finite-type
> characteristic-zero cover \(\mathscr X\to\mathbb A^1\), of degree

> every actual hit \(b_r=0\pmod s\), one can select a point
> \(x_{s,r}\in\mathscr X(\mathbb F_{s^2})\) whose image is a reciprocal
> Frobenius orbit factor of \(B_s\), and this selection is compatible with
> reduction from \(\mathscr X\), not chosen independently in every

The compatibility clause is essential.  Merely saying

\[
\exists\alpha_{s,r}\in\mathbb F_{s^2}:B_s(\alpha_{s,r})=0

separated.  A fixed cover would supply a common characteristic-zero algebra in

> **[SEVENFOLD-TRANSVERSALITY].**  The two disjoint triples of selected points

statement follows from the presently visible ODE/root facts, and the exact

For an analytic route, [R3-QPALM-\(L^2\)] is strictly cleaner: it states only
