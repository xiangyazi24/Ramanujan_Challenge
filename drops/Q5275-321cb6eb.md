# 8. Why no other circular complement works

There are three distinct geometries which must not be conflated.

### 8.1 Same-prime translated Gram

If the two actual \(q\)-zeros are at integer separation \(\delta\), the legal continuant is \(N_\delta\) on the nonwrapping interval between them.  When \(\delta>q/2\), replacing it by \(q-\delta\) would connect the upper zero to a translated lower zero across the singular index \(q\).  The recurrence coefficient at the seam is not a unit.  There is no source-equivalent implication

\[
q\mid N_{q-\delta}(\cdots)
\]

from the two original zeros.

The committed source's bound \(A_q(\delta)\le q-\delta\) for \(\delta>q/2\) is only endpoint counting.  It is not a complementary gap polynomial.

### 8.2 Palindrome

Simultaneously reflecting both zeros

\[
r\mapsto q-1-r,\qquad r'\mapsto q-1-r'
\]

preserves \(|r'-r|\).  Reflecting only one zero produces a separation involving \(r+r'\), which depends on the base point.  It therefore destroys the fixed translated-vector coordinate used in CD9 and the fixed slope used in CD10.

### 8.3 Cross-prime centered endpoint

The endpoint slack

\[
s=c_p+c_q-|g|
\]

is different.  It is an exact coordinate on the *ambient support boundary* of the source convolution.  After the change of variables, palindromy converts the endpoint source pair to the literal low-index pair \((i,s-i)\), and the CRT phase becomes a fixed constant plus the new slope \((s+1)\bar p\).  That is why Section 5 is legal while a circular \(q-|\delta|\) substitution is not.

For a genuine power-size macroscopic centered gap

\[
|g|=N^\gamma,\qquad \frac12<\gamma<1,
\]

\begin{proposition}[Global translated actual-zero Gram]
\label{prop:ad-source-global-translation-gram}
For $q\asymp_vN$, put
\[
 x_q(k)=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal K_q}(k),
\]
extended by zero to $\mathbf Z$.  Let $I\subset\mathbf Z$ be an interval
with $|I|\ll G$, and let
\[
 y_g(k)=m_g(k)x_q(k+g),\qquad |m_g(k)|\le1.
\]
Then, for every $p\asymp_vN$,
\[
 \sum_{g\in I}|\langle x_p,y_g\rangle|^2
 \ll
 Z(p)\min\{Z(q)+G^2,\ GZ(q),\ Z(q)^2\}.
\]
The estimate is uniform under arbitrary bounded source masks contained in
$m_g$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

\[
 a_q(\delta)=\sum_{n\in\mathbf Z}x_q(n)x_q(n+\delta).
\]
If $0<|\delta|<q$, every term of $a_q(\delta)$ gives two actual

 a_q(\delta)\ll|\delta|.

 \sum_{\delta\in\mathbf Z}a_q(\delta)=Z(q)^2

 Z(q)+O(G^2),\qquad O(GZ(q)),\qquad Z(q)^2.

Schur's test and $\|x_p\|_2^2=Z(p)$ give the result.
\end{proof}

## Final conclusion

The current \(\sqrt N\) boundary had two logically different parts.

- The **same-prime translated-zero Gram** did not actually stop there; the stable all-gap continuant theorem extends it, and the exact total autocorrelation gives stronger rows for very large \(G\).
- A **legal small complement** exists only near the absolute centered-gap endpoint.  Reparametrizing by endpoint slack before PS14 gives a new nonempty macroscopic deletion, including endpoint rows whose original gap is \(\asymp N\).

What survives is the true middle: a row with both its centered gap and its endpoint slack larger than \(\sqrt N\).  On that sector the strongest unconditional scalar source estimate above still misses the target by at least \(99/400-o(1)\) at the first macroscopic corner.  Closing it requires an actual-vector signed covariance theorem such as (OPEN-MAC); none of the existing same-prime gap polynomial, circular endpoint count, coloring, or completed-source positive moment statements supplies that covariance.
