# P3.2 Clausen polarization audit

## Verdict

The proposed bilinear Clausen formula is structurally correct only after introducing the **full Clausen square-root sequence**, not from the Apéry square-root sequence alone. The exact identity is a polarization identity for the quadratic form whose diagonal is the CFVZ Clausen square.

Let

\[
B_n(z)=\sum_{k=0}^n D(n,k)z^k
\]

be the CFVZ square-root polynomial. The Clausen identity has the form

\[
B_n(z)^2={}_3F_2(\cdots;z),
\]

and the Apéry coefficient is the specialization

\[
b_n=[z^n]B_n(z)^2=\sum_k D(n,k)^2.
\]

The kernel in the question is

\[
K(r,s)=\sum_kD(r,k)D(s,k).
\]

It is the Gram pairing of the coefficient vectors of the two square roots.

## Exact polarization formula

Introduce the two-variable Clausen polarization

\[
\mathcal C_{r,s}(x,y)
 :=\frac{B_r(x)B_s(y)+B_r(y)B_s(x)}2 .
\]

Taking the coefficient pairing at the diagonal specialization gives

\[
\boxed{
K(r,s)=\frac12\left(\langle B_r,B_s\rangle+\langle B_s,B_r\rangle\right)
}

where

\[
\langle B_r,B_s\rangle
 =\sum_k[z^k]B_r(z)\,[z^k]B_s(z).
\]

Since the coefficient vectors are finite, this is simply

\[
\boxed{
K(r,s)=\operatorname{Pol}_{r,s}(B^2)
}
\]

where `Pol` means replacing the quadratic diagonal term
\(B_n^2\) by the bilinear cross term \(B_rB_s\).

Equivalently, if

\[
Q_n=\sum_kD(n,k)^2
\]

is the Clausen diagonal, then the polarization operator satisfies

\[
Q_{r+s}-Q_r-Q_s
\]

only after the underlying coefficient grading is shifted; it is not literally \(K(r,s)\) without this grading correction.

## Important obstruction

The naive formula

\[
K(r,s)=\frac{B_{r+s}-B_r-B_s}{2}
\]

is false. The Clausen identity squares a single hypergeometric series. It does not identify the mixed coefficient pairing of two different square roots with the square of the index sum.

The correct object is the bilinear Clausen form, not the scalar sequence \(b_n\).

## p=181 test: (r,s)=(19,47)

The computational test should compare

\[
K(19,47)=\sum_kD(19,k)D(47,k)\pmod{181}
\]

with the polarized Clausen expression obtained from the coefficient vectors of

\[
B_{19},B_{47}\in\mathbf F_{181}[z].
\]

The two quantities agree identically because the polarization is a coefficient-level identity:

\[
\sum_k d_{19,k}d_{47,k}
=
\left\langle(d_{19,k})_k,(d_{47,k})_k\right\rangle .
\]

No additional supercongruence or CFVZ input is required for this equality.

However, this does **not** imply the desired nonvanishing theorem. The missing step remains proving that the bilinear Gram entry is nonzero modulo p. The Clausen square gives the diagonal information

\[
\sum_kD(r,k)^2=b_r,
\]

but it does not prevent two different coefficient vectors from becoming orthogonal modulo p.

## Final status

- Clausen gives a bilinear Gram/polarization interpretation of `K(r,s)`: **PROVED**.
- A formula expressing `K(r,s)` only through scalar values `B_r,B_s` without coefficient data: **FALSE**.
- The p=181 check is a verification of the coefficient-level polarization identity, not a proof of `K(19,47)\neq0`.
- The remaining P3.2 obstruction is exactly the possible modular orthogonality of distinct CFVZ coefficient vectors.
