# Mellin anti-concentration for the Apéry sequence

## Executive conclusion

The proposed Mellin/Fourier route correctly identifies the object, but the usual Fourier bounds do not prove AFP.  The obstruction is that AFP is a statement about a **pinned coefficient fiber**
\[
\#\{r:b_r=0\},
\]
while the available transform estimates control global spectral energy.

The missing theorem is not an ordinary Weil bound for \(H_p(t)\), but a horizontal defining-characteristic anti-concentration theorem for the tame-character family.

## (A) Does the polynomial transform \(H_p(t)\) control zero coefficients?

No direct duality exists.

\[
H_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r t^r
\]
encodes evaluations of the coefficient vector. Its roots are values of the generating polynomial, whereas AFP concerns which coefficients vanish.

The only universal relation is linear algebra: many zero coefficients force sparsity of the polynomial, but roots of a polynomial over \(\mathbf F_p\) do not detect sparse coefficients. Conversely, a polynomial with many nonzero coefficients can have many roots.

Thus the root set of \(H_p\) gives information about the generating function geometry, not directly about \(Z_p\).

## (B) Dwork Frobenius does not give an O(1) root count

The Dwork relation
\[
H_p(Z)=\bar F(Z)/\bar F(Z^p)
\]
is a Frobenius statement about the generating function. Its zeros correspond to a Frobenius degeneration locus in the parameter space.

However, the relevant supersingular loci in families need not have O(1) points over \(\mathbf F_p\). The Legendre family is the standard warning: the number of supersingular parameters is of order \(p\), with class-number fluctuations.

The Apéry family has extra rigidity, but that rigidity has to be proved; it does not follow from non-isotriviality alone.

## (C) Fourier anti-concentration

A Halász-style inequality is not strong enough. Writing
\[
\widehat\beta(t)=H_p(t),
\]
one gets bounds of the form
\[
\#\{r:b_r=0\}\lesssim p\frac{\max|H_p|^2}{\|\beta\|_2^2}.
\]
Even a square-root estimate
\[
|H_p(t)|\ll \sqrt p
\]
gives only a bound of order \(p\), not O(1).

The reason is that square-root cancellation is the generic scale for a trace function; AFP requires exceptional anti-concentration of one value.

## (D) Does multiplicative Dwork structure help?

Yes, but not through the ordinary Fourier transform.

The congruence
\[
b_{mp+r}\equiv b_m b_r\pmod p
\]
means that the sequence is controlled by Cartier/Frobenius operators. This suggests studying the unit-root splitting and the tame-character parameter space rather than additive frequencies.

The correct target is a statement like:

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\binom{|Z_p|/2}{2}\ll \pi(X),
\]

or a stronger horizontal Mellin anti-concentration theorem controlling simultaneous failures of the unit root for different twists.

## (E) Can crystalline coefficient complexity solve it?

Not currently through a general theorem.

Crystalline theory controls:

- Frobenius slopes,
- unit-root subspaces,
- overconvergent eigenvectors,
- p-adic differential equations.

It does not automatically bound the number of vanishing Taylor coefficients of a Frobenius eigenfunction.

A successful theorem would need a bridge:

\[
\text{Frobenius eigenvector structure}
\Longrightarrow
\text{coefficient anti-concentration}.
\]

No general result of this form is known for Apéry-type Dwork families.

## Correct formulation of the missing theorem

The desired result is not
\[
\max_t |H_p(t)|\ll \sqrt p,
\]
but rather a horizontal statement over primes and twists:

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\#\{j_1\ne j_2:
 b_{j_1}\equiv b_{j_2}\equiv0\pmod p\}
\ll \pi(X).
\]

Equivalently: different tame Mellin branches should not simultaneously lose their unit roots except with bounded frequency.

This is the TCMA/ZM2 mechanism. The Dwork/Frobenius viewpoint is the right geometric framework, but the missing step is a branch-coupling theorem, not a one-variable Fourier estimate.
