## 1. The exact recurrence object

Write

\[
P(u)=(2u+1)(17u^2+17u+5)
     =34u^3+51u^2+27u+5.
\]

The Apéry sequence satisfies

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

On a regular mod-\(p\) window, clear the factorials by

\[
\beta_n=(n!)^3b_n.
\]

Then

\[
\beta_{n+1}=P(n)\beta_n-n^6\beta_{n-1},
\tag{1.2}
\]

or, with \(v_n=(\beta_n,\beta_{n-1})^t\),

\[
v_{n+1}=M(n)v_n,
\qquad
M(n)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(n)&-n^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det M(n)=n^6.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Every transfer matrix is invertible away from the cut. This gives the no-consecutive-zero fact: a nonzero solution cannot have two adjacent zeros in a regular window.

Define the gap continuants by

\[
N_0(X)=0,
\qquad
N_1(X)=1,
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(X)=P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X).
\tag{1.4}

\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1).
\tag{1.5}

\[
\beta_x=0,
\qquad
\beta_{x+1}=1,

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{x+h}=N_h(x).}
\tag{1.6}

Thus a zero return at distance \(h\) is not merely bounded by a degree capacity: its exact equation is

N_h(x)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.
\tag{1.7}

For the distinguished Apéry solution, if \(b_x=0\), then \(b_{x+1}\ne0\), so after scaling it is exactly the local solution in (1.6). Hence every pair of actual zeros satisfies (1.7).

## 2. Exact chain criterion: the first place different gaps meet

Let

\[
d_1,\ldots,d_m\ge2,
\qquad
s_0=0,
\qquad
s_j=d_1+\cdots+d_j.

Define the shifted segment continuants

\[
F_j(X)=N_{d_j}(X+s_{j-1})
\qquad(1\le j\le m).
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proposition 2.1 — exact regular chain criterion

Let \(x+s_m<p\), with the whole interval regular. For the normalized local solution \(\beta_x=0,\beta_{x+1}=1\),

\[
\boxed{
 \beta_{x+s_j}=0\text{ for every }1\le j\le m
 \iff
 F_j(x)=0\text{ for every }1\le j\le m.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The same equivalence holds for the actual Apéry solution after assuming \(b_x=0\).

#### Proof

For the first segment this is (1.6). Suppose the solution has reached a zero at \(x+s_{j-1}\). Invertibility and no consecutive zeros give

\[
\beta_{x+s_{j-1}+1}\ne0.
\]

After dividing by this nonzero scalar, the recurrence restarted at \(x+s_{j-1}\) is again the normalized local recurrence. Therefore

\[
\beta_{x+s_j}=0
\iff
N_{d_j}(x+s_{j-1})=0.
\]

Induction proves (2.2). This is the transfer-matrix form of the source's restart identity. ∎

\mathcal E_p(H)\ll H^{8/5-\eta}
\Longrightarrow
Z(p)\ll p^{3/5-\eta/6}.}
\tag{9.3}

### 9.1 Propagation to the hostile rectangle's pair scale

\[
\#\text{blocks}\asymp T^{2/5},
\qquad
\#\text{states per block}\asymp Z^2/L,
\qquad
K\asymp T^{2/5}/L.

Its pair mass is therefore

\[
T^{2/5}\cdot\frac{Z^2}{L}\cdot K^2.
\tag{9.4}

With \(Z=T^{3/5}\), this is

\[
\asymp\frac{T^{12/5}}{L^3}.

Under (9.3),

\[
Z^2\ll T^{6/5-\eta/3},

and (9.4) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\ll\frac{T^{12/5-\eta/3}}{L^3}.}
\tag{9.5}
\]

Thus the fixed-power gain at the pair scale is exactly

\[
\boxed{T^{-\eta/3}.}

For the source-natural conjectural exponent \(\beta=3/2\),

\[
\eta=\frac85-\frac32=\frac1{10}.

Therefore

\[
Z(p)\ll p^{7/12}

and the pair-scale saving is

\boxed{T^{-1/30}.}
\tag{9.6}

This is a genuine fixed power, unlike the determinant-height inequalities in §7.

## 10. The weakest non-equivalent lemma needed only for the shared block

A uniform pointwise theorem such as \((MG)_\eta\) is stronger than necessary. Q5921 needs \(K\) primes in one short interval, and every such prime carries a zero set of size \(\asymp T^{3/5}\). By (8.7), every block prime is therefore triple-energy heavy.

### Short-block actual-triple rarity

For every fixed legal shell and every interval

\[
I\subset[c_1T,c_2T],
\qquad
|I|\ll T^{2/5},
\]

require

\[
\boxed{
\#\left\{
 p\in I\cap\mathbb P:
 \mathcal T_p(\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor)
 \ge c\,p^{4/5}
\right\}
=o\!\left(\frac{T^{2/5}}{\log T}\right)}
\tag{SBATR}
\]

for every fixed \(c>0\).

Then a Q5921 block of

\[
K\asymp T^{2/5}/\log T
\]

primes is impossible, because every one of its primes belongs to the set counted in `(SBATR)`.

This lemma is:

* genuinely multi-gap — it counts triples inside one \(\mathcal Z_p\);
* characteristic-correct — every \(\mathcal T_p\) is formed entirely in one \(\mathbb F_p\);
* non-equivalent to the upper-state target — it counts all triple-rich primes in every short interval, without using \(q,t\), the selected upper zero, or the rectangle's block assignment;
* weaker than a uniform \(H^{8/5-\eta}\) energy theorem.

A quantitative form

\[
\#\{p\in I:\mathcal T_p(\sqrt p)\ge c p^{4/5}\}
\ll \frac{T^{2/5-\delta}}{\log T}
\tag{10.1}
\]

would delete all but a \(T^{-\delta}\) fraction of the required block primes. An averaged projective-energy form such as

\[
\sum_{p\in I\cap\mathbb P}\mathcal E_p(\sqrt p)
\ll
\frac{T^{2/5}}{\log T}\,T^{4/5-\eta/2}
\tag{10.2}
\]

would contradict the rectangle's forced lower bound

\[
\sum_{p\in P_b}\mathcal T_p(\sqrt p)
\gg K T^{4/5}
\asymp\frac{T^{6/5}}{\log T}
\tag{10.3}
\]

by the factor \(T^{\eta/2}\).

This is the weakest clean repair I can isolate without repackaging the desired upper-state conclusion itself.

## 11. The first stronger untested higher-order constraint

The checked source isolates a stronger distinguished-orbit possibility. Let

\[
z_1<z_2<\cdots<z_M
\]

be the actual zeros in \([0,p)\). Consider four consecutive zeros

\[
z_i<z_{i+1}<z_{i+2}<z_{i+3}.
\]

### Four-zero rigidity hypothesis

There exists a fixed \(\eta_0>0\) such that every such four-zero window with

\[
z_{i+3}-z_i\le\eta_0\sqrt p
\]

contains an adjacent reflection pair

\[
z_j+z_{j+1}=p-1.
\tag{11.1}
\]

This would imply

\[
\boxed{Z(p)\ll\sqrt p.}
\tag{11.2}
\]

#### Proof

Put \(g_i=z_{i+1}-z_i\). The span of the \(i\)-th four-window is

\[
w_i=g_i+g_{i+1}+g_{i+2}.
\]

Each gap occurs in at most three such sums, so

\[
\sum_i w_i\le3(p-1).
\]

Hence only \(O(\sqrt p)\) windows can have span larger than \(\eta_0\sqrt p\).

In a reflection-stable ordered set, at most one reflected pair can be adjacent: every other reflected pair encloses the points closer to the center. That unique adjacent reflected pair belongs to at most three four-windows. Therefore all but \(O(\sqrt p)\) four-windows are impossible, giving \(M=O(\sqrt p)\). ∎

At the rectangle scale this improves \(Z=T^{3/5}\) to \(Z=T^{1/2}\). Since the pair mass is quadratic in \(Z\), it saves

\[
\boxed{T^{-1/5}}
\]

relative to \(T^{12/5}\).

However, this hypothesis cannot be a theorem about arbitrary local recurrence solutions. The source has formal primitive off-center four-return chains. Any valid proof must use the fact that the zero line is visited by the **distinguished Apéry solution**, not merely by some solution of the same second-order recurrence.

This is the first concrete higher-order constraint I would test after the two-gap \(8/5\) threshold.

## 12. Bounded-order constraints are nonempty: exact finite checks

The first determinant is a real restriction, but it is not universally prohibitive. The source records split common-root examples, including a two-gap chain at \(p=71\) and a four-return local chain at \(p=128047\).

The following exact modular recurrence checker is independently reproducible. It uses no floating point and tests the normalized local solution, not the distinguished Apéry initial condition.

```python
def P(n: int) -> int:
    return 34*n**3 + 51*n**2 + 27*n + 5


def local_zero_offsets(p: int, x: int, last_offset: int) -> list[int]:
    """Zeros of the normalized local cleared recurrence.

    beta_x = 0, beta_{x+1} = 1, and
    beta_{n+1} = P(n) beta_n - n^6 beta_{n-1} (mod p).
    The caller should keep x + last_offset < p.
    """
    previous = 0                 # beta_x
    current = 1                  # beta_{x+1}
    zeros = [0]
    for h in range(1, last_offset):
        n = x + h
        following = (P(n)*current - pow(n, 6, p)*previous) % p
        previous, current = current, following
        if current == 0:
            zeros.append(h + 1)
    return zeros


assert local_zero_offsets(71, 33, 4) == [0, 2, 4]
assert local_zero_offsets(128047, 42375, 164) == [0, 41, 127, 164]
```

For the first assertion, \(33\equiv-5/2\pmod{71}\), and both

\[
N_2(33)=0,
\qquad
N_2(35)=0
\]

hold modulo \(71\). Thus \(71\mid\mathcal R_{2,2}\).

These examples prove only that bounded local return patterns and split resultants are nonempty. They do **not** construct actual Apéry zero sets of size \(p^{3/5}\), a block of \(K\) partner primes, or any asymptotic family.

An exact Sage check of the first determinant is:

```sage
R.<X> = PolynomialRing(ZZ)
P = lambda U: 34*U^3 + 51*U^2 + 27*U + 5

N = [R(0), R(1)]
for h in range(1, 6):
    N.append(P(X+h)*N[h] - (X+h)^6*N[h-1])

R22 = N[2].resultant(N[2](X+2))
assert R22 % 71 == 0
assert N[2](33) % 71 == 0
assert N[2](35) % 71 == 0
```

Again, this is a finite exact certificate, not asymptotic evidence.

## Final conclusion

The earliest exact Apéry-recurrence obstruction omitted by Q5921 is not another moment and not another per-gap degree bound. It is the **same-root shifted-continuant condition for two consecutive gaps in one \(\mathbb F_p\)**. Its first finite-dimensional certificate is

\[
p\mid\operatorname{Res}_X(N_d(X),N_e(X+d)).
\]

For a translated block, deriving this separately at every prime yields exact common-radical divisibilities in \(\mathbb Z\), including the all-gap chain carrier (5.6). Those conditions sharply delete gaps \(o(K)\), but their known height bounds leave logarithmic or larger slack at

\[
K\asymp T^{2/5}/\log T,
\qquad
Z\asymp T^{3/5}.
\]

Accordingly, the rectangle is **not yet recurrence-compatible**, but bounded-order determinant height does **not** prove it impossible and gives no fixed power at the hostile scales.

The first exact missing implication is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{all separate gap capacities}
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{one common shifted-continuant root chain}.}
\]

The weakest clean non-equivalent lemma that kills the shared block is `(SBATR)`. The source-natural power theorem is the strict two-gap energy bound

\[
\mathcal E_p(H)\ll H^{8/5-\eta},
\]

whose exponent propagation is exactly

\[
Z(p):\ p^{3/5}\longmapsto p^{3/5-\eta/6},
\qquad
\text{pair scale}:\ T^{12/5}\longmapsto T^{12/5-\eta/3}.
\]

If that route survives, the first higher-order distinguished constraint to test is the short four-consecutive-zero reflection rigidity, not another marginal moment or another cross-characteristic identification.
