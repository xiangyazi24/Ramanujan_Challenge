# Q1763 — delta-source equation audit

## 1. Substitution and factorization

Start from

\[
(t+\tfrac12)^3\nu_{t+1}-t(34t^2+\tfrac32)\nu_t+(t-\tfrac12)^3\nu_{t-1}=0
\]

and put

\[
\nu_t=(t^2-1)C_t.
\]

Using

\[
(t+1)^2-1=t(t+2),\qquad (t-1)^2-1=t(t-2),
\]

we obtain

\[
t\{(t+\tfrac12)^3(t+2)C_{t+1}-(34t^2+\tfrac32)(t^2-1)C_t+(t-\tfrac12)^3(t-2)C_{t-1}\}=0.
\]

Therefore for \(t\neq0\),

\[
HC_t=0,
\]

where

\[
HC_t=(t+\tfrac12)^3(t+2)C_{t+1}-(34t^2+\tfrac32)(t^2-1)C_t+(t-\tfrac12)^3(t-2)C_{t-1}.
\]

The sign is positive in the first and third terms and negative in the middle term; no additional minus is introduced by the \((t^2-1)\) factor.

## 2. The source at t=0

At the fixed center, the recurrence no longer gives the equation by division by \(t\). Directly:

\[
HC_0=(\tfrac12)^3(2)C_1-(\tfrac32)(-1)C_0+(-\tfrac12)^3(-2)C_{-1}.
\]

Hence

\[
HC_0=\frac14(C_1+C_{-1})+\frac32 C_0.
\]

Using the stated centered values

\[
C_0=-A_m,
\]

and

\[
C_{\pm1}=3A_m-2Q_{center},
\]

we get

\[
\frac14(C_1+C_{-1})=\frac12(3A_m-2Q_{center})
=\frac32A_m-Q_{center},
\]

while

\[
\frac32C_0=-\frac32A_m.
\]

The \(A_m\) terms cancel and therefore

\[
HC_0=-Q_{center}.
\]

Consequently the global equation on \(\mathbb F_p\) is exactly

\[
\boxed{HC=-Q_{center}\,\delta_0}.
\]

## 3. Sign audit at p=19,37

The only possible sign ambiguity is the source term. The local computation above fixes it.

For p=19 and p=37, substituting the finite-field centered Apéry row gives:

- the off-center equations \(HC_t=0\) for every \(t\neq0\);
- the center defect equals \(-Q_{center}\), not \(+Q_{center}\).

Thus the delta source has coefficient \(-Q_{center}\) in both checks. A formula with \(+Q_{center}\delta_0\) fails the center equation.

## 4. Self-adjoint form

Let

\[
a_t=(t+\tfrac12)^3(t+2),
\]

and

\[
c_t=(t-\tfrac12)^3(t-2).
\]

The operator is a Jacobi operator:

\[
(HC)_t=a_tC_{t+1}-b_tC_t+c_tC_{t-1}.
\]

A diagonal scaling \(C_t=s_t y_t\) makes it symmetric iff

\[
a_t\frac{s_{t+1}}{s_t}=c_{t+1}\frac{s_t}{s_{t+1}},
\]

or

\[
\left(\frac{s_{t+1}}{s_t}\right)^2=\frac{c_{t+1}}{a_t}.
\]

Here

\[
c_{t+1}=(t+\tfrac12)^3(t-1),
\]

so the required ratio is

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac{s_{t+1}}{s_t}\right)^2=\frac{t-1}{t+2}}.
\]

This is the formal self-adjoint scaling. It is valid away from the singular endpoints where the factors vanish. The telescoping product is

\[
s_t^2\propto\prod_j\frac{j-1}{j+2},
\]

which collapses to a rational factorial ratio.

## 5. Green identity and remaining obstruction

For the scaled symmetric operator \(\widetilde H\), let \(G(t,0)\) denote the Green kernel satisfying

\[
\widetilde H G(\cdot,0)=\delta_0.
\]

The delta equation gives

\[
y_t=-Q_{center}G(t,0).
\]

Therefore

\[
R=\sum_t\frac1{C_t}
\]

would require converting the reciprocal sum of the original solution into a linear functional of the Green kernel. The operator identity alone does not provide this conversion.

Equivalently, the determinant route is:

\[
G(t,0)=\frac{\operatorname{cof}(\widetilde H)_{t0}}{\det'(\widetilde H)},
\]

where \(\det'\) is the reduced determinant after removing the zero mode (if present). The missing step is an identity expressing

\[
\sum_t\frac1{C_t}
\]

as either a cofactor sum or a logarithmic derivative

\[
\frac{d}{d\lambda}\log\det(\widetilde H+\lambda V)\big|_{\lambda=0}
\]

for a suitable diagonal perturbation \(V\).

So the verified result is:

\[
\boxed{HC=-Q_{center}\delta_0}
\]

with the exact self-adjoint multiplier

\[
\boxed{s_{t+1}/s_t=\sqrt{(t-1)/(t+2)}}.
\]

The unresolved part is not the delta-source equation; it is the determinant/cofactor functional that turns this Green solution into the forbidden reciprocal sum \(R\).
