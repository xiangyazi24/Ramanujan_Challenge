# Cross-prime Frobenius independence for the Apéry problem

## Executive verdict

I audited the current `main` branch at commit `3b32157484571a9d1997bf89c703c810e9843609`, especially `problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex`, `oracleC_result.tex`, `toric_fiber_k3.tex`, `toric_fiber_sym2.tex`, `proof.tex`, and `AVENUES.md`.

The state of the art is sharply negative for the exact question, but the reason is more precise than “non-abelian monodromy is difficult.” There are several distinct notions of independence that are often conflated:

1. **Independence of the realization prime** \(\ell\) in a compatible system is well developed.
2. **Joint Sato–Tate for two representations at the same rational prime** is known in many non-CM cases, effectively and unconditionally.
3. **Product monodromy for several sheaves over one finite field**, followed by averaging over the finite-field parameter or over characters, is also well developed.
4. **A fixed object evaluated at shifted rational primes \(p\) and \(p+h\)** is not covered by any of those theories. Even coarse product-Sato–Tate behavior for successive Frobenius angles of one fixed non-CM elliptic curve is currently posed as a conjecture.
5. The Apéry target is harder still: the repository's actual marker \(b_j\bmod p\) is a defining-characteristic reduction of a **Kummer–Mellin Frobenius supertrace with a character depending on \((p,j)\)**. It is not the Frobenius trace of the single K3 fiber \(X_{1/j}\).

Consequently:

- There is **no theorem** of the requested fixed-shift form for a non-CM weight-2 newform.
- The proposed \(p^{-2}\) scale is not the natural scale for weight-2 newforms. Hasse's bound turns the two congruences into two exact trace-zero conditions, each of expected size \(p^{-1/2}\), so their independent trace scale is \(p^{-1}\), conditional on the two integers being prime.
- There is **no two-prime Katz–Sarnak theorem** giving independence of \(\mathrm{Frob}_p\) and \(\mathrm{Frob}_{p+h}\) for a fixed K3 surface.
- For the Apéry problem, the realistic route is a new two-stage theorem: first, an integral same-characteristic product-monodromy/anti-concentration theorem for the marked Mellin coordinate; second, a genuinely horizontal large-sieve or dispersion theorem for the deterministic characters \(j_p=n\bmod p\). The second stage is essentially the missing LGTQ-3 input.

## 0. Two corrections to the stated “key insight”

### 0.1 Sparse support gives a trivial Fourier bound, not Fourier decay

For the unnormalized transform

```math
\widehat{1_{Z_p}}(a)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(-ar),
```

one certainly has

```math
|\widehat{1_{Z_p}}(a)|\leq Z(p).
```

This is useful bookkeeping, but it is not “decay” in the usual harmonic-analytic sense: the nonzero-frequency bound can be as large as the zero-frequency coefficient \(\widehat{1_{Z_p}}(0)=Z(p)\). There is no spectral gap and no cancellation as \(a\neq0\).

More importantly, the current repository does **not** prove \(Z(p)=O(1)\). Its unconditional theorem is \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\). The computation through \(p\leq10^6\) finds \(Z(p)\leq12\), mean approximately one, and a good Poisson fit for \(Z(p)/2\); this is compelling evidence for bounded moments, not a uniform theorem. A Poisson law itself has unbounded support, so it does not predict a uniform absolute bound over all primes.

Thus the correct conclusion is:

> Granting bounded moments, or even a hypothetical uniform bound for \(Z(p)\), the remaining obstruction is cross-modulus correlation. But sparse support alone does not prove the single-prime input currently needed in every unconditional argument.

### 0.2 The Apéry coefficient marker is not the trace of the fiber \(X_{1/d}\)

The current exact construction in `oracleC_result.tex` is

```math
\Lambda(x,y,z)=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz},
\qquad
\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^j=b_j.
```

For \(0\leq j\leq p-2\), it defines

```math
c^{\mathrm{tor}}_{p,j}
=-\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}\Lambda(x,y,z)^j
\equiv b_j\pmod p.
```

After choosing the Teichmüller character \(\omega_p\), the characteristic-zero lift is

```math
\widetilde c^{\mathrm{tor}}_{p,j}
=-\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{alt}}
\left(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
R\Gamma_c(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{\omega_p^j})\right).
```

The variety \(U\) and map \(\Lambda\) are fixed, but the Kummer character and its cyclotomic coefficient field vary with \((p,j)\). Grouping the torus points by \(a=\Lambda(x,y,z)\) shows that \(b_j\bmod p\) is a multiplicative Mellin coefficient of the **entire K3 fiber-count function** \(a\mapsto\mu_p(a)\). It is not the trace of the one fiber with parameter \(a=1/j\).

The integral distinction is visible already at \((p,j)=(11,5)\):

```math
b_5=819005=11\cdot74455,
\qquad
\widetilde c^{\mathrm{tor}}_{11,5}=-33.
```

Both vanish after reduction modulo 11, but their first quotient digits differ:

```math
74455\equiv7\pmod{11},
\qquad -3\equiv8\pmod{11}.
```

So even the correct marked Frobenius lift agrees only modulo \(p\), not modulo \(p^2\). This matters whenever one tries to interpret \(b_j/p\) as a Frobenius digit.

## 1. A taxonomy of independence results

| Regime | Typical statement | Status | Relevance to LGTQ-3 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Compatible-system \(\ell\)-independence | For fixed good \(r\), the polynomial of \(\mathrm{Frob}_r\) is independent of \(\ell\neq r\); monodromy groups have common rank/root data or a common form | Strong theorems | Low |
| Joint Sato–Tate, same prime | \((\theta_p(f_1),\theta_p(f_2))\) has product distribution as the **same** \(p\) varies | Known for twist-inequivalent forms | Low |
| Product monodromy, one finite field | Several trace functions or translates over \(\mathbf F_p\) have square-root cancellation when the sheaves are Goursat-independent | Known and powerful | High for a local lemma, insufficient globally |
| Character-average Mellin equidistribution | Average over all good characters of \(\mathbf F_q^\times\), with \(q\) fixed or tending to infinity | Katz; Forey–Fresán–Kowalski | High for character averages, not deterministic characters |
| Shifted rational primes | Joint law of \(\mathrm{Frob}_p\) and \(\mathrm{Frob}_{p+h}\) for one fixed object | Conjectural in general | Directly relevant |
| Deterministic cross-modulus sampling | A fixed \(n\) selects \(j_p=n\bmod p\), and one asks for decorrelation across \(p\) | No general geometric theorem | Exactly LGTQ-3's regime |

The fourth and fifth rows are not consequences of the first three.

A useful finite-quotient formulation makes the obstruction transparent. Fix an auxiliary \(\ell\) and a finite quotient of a Galois representation. Conditions on \(\mathrm{Frob}_p\) and \(\mathrm{Frob}_{p+h}\), with both \(p\) and \(p+h\) prime, form a **Chebotarev prime-tuple problem**. Ordinary Chebotarev counts one varying prime; it does not count two primes constrained by a fixed additive shift. An asymptotic product law would require a Hardy–Littlewood-type theorem with Chebotarev conditions. In the Apéry problem the auxiliary modulus also changes with the observation prime, producing a still harder “moving-field diagonal.”

## 2. Question (1): non-CM weight-2 eigenforms

### 2.1 The proposed event collapses to simultaneous supersingularity

Let \(f\) be a weight-2 non-CM newform with rational integral coefficients, equivalently an elliptic curve up to isogeny, and let \(A_f(r)\) be its unnormalized Frobenius trace. For a good prime \(r\),

```math
|A_f(r)|\leq2\sqrt r.
```

Fix \(h\). If \(p\) and \(q=p+h\) are prime and \(p\) is sufficiently large, then

```math
p\mid A_f(p) \iff A_f(p)=0,
```

and also

```math
p\mid A_f(q) \iff A_f(q)=0,
```

because \(|A_f(q)|<p\). If the second congruence is instead taken modulo \(q\), the conclusion is the same. Thus the proposed event is, asymptotically,

```math
A_f(p)=A_f(p+h)=0,
```

with both arguments prime: two supersingular primes separated by the fixed gap \(h\).

There is also an elementary parity point. If \(h\) is odd, then apart from the pair involving 2, \(p\) and \(p+h\) cannot both be prime. Only even \(h\) gives a nontrivial asymptotic problem.

For a newform with non-rational coefficient field, “modulo \(p\)” must be replaced by reduction at a prime \(\lambda\mid p\), and the direct integer Hasse-collapse needs a norm or embedding-sensitive reformulation.

### 2.2 The independent scale is \(p^{-1}\), not \(p^{-2}\)

The Lang–Trotter heuristic for a fixed trace \(t\), including \(t=0\), is

```math
\#\{p\leq x:A_f(p)=t\}\sim C_{f,t}\frac{\sqrt x}{\log x}.
```

Consequently, among primes near \(x\), the local probability of the exact value \(A_f(p)=0\) is of order \(x^{-1/2}\), not \(x^{-1}\). This is forced by the Hasse interval: there are only \(O(\sqrt p)\) possible integral traces.

If the two trace-zero events at \(p\) and \(p+h\) behaved independently, their conditional trace probability would be

```math
\asymp p^{-1/2}(p+h)^{-1/2}\asymp p^{-1}.
```

If one also samples the primality of the two shifted integers from all integers, the Hardy–Littlewood factor contributes approximately \((\log p)^{-2}\). The expected number in a dyadic block \([X,2X]\) is then approximately \((\log X)^{-2}\). Summing over dyadic blocks converges, so the strongest naive heuristic predicts only finitely many fixed-gap simultaneous supersingular pairs. This is a heuristic, not a theorem.

The \(p^{-2}\) heuristic would be appropriate for two genuinely uniform residue conditions modulo \(p\). Weight-2 modular traces are not uniform residues modulo \(p\); their Archimedean support is much smaller.

### 2.3 No theorem of the requested form is known

The obstacles already appear before asking for a pair.

- The single-curve Lang–Trotter asymptotic for \(A_f(p)=0\) is open.
- Elkies proved infinitely many supersingular primes for every elliptic curve over \(\mathbf Q\), but not the Lang–Trotter order of growth and not a fixed-gap correlation theorem.
- Zywina's `Bounds for the Lang–Trotter conjectures` improves conditional upper bounds using smoothed Chebotarev, but does not prove the asymptotic or shifted independence.
- Mok and Zheng's `Pseudorandomness of Sato–Tate Distributions for Elliptic Curves` explicitly formulates product-Sato–Tate behavior of successive Frobenius angles as a **conjecture** and provides numerical evidence. That conjecture is macroscopic; exact trace zero is a much thinner lattice-scale event.

There is a particularly important local-global mismatch. For \(q\neq p\), the mod-\(p\) representation satisfies

```math
\operatorname{tr}\overline\rho_p(\operatorname{Frob}_q)
\equiv A_f(q)\pmod p.
```

So for each fixed \(p\), the condition on \(q\) is a Chebotarev condition in the \(p\)-division field. But the condition at the observation prime \(p\) is not obtained from \(\operatorname{Frob}_p\) in that same representation: the \(p\)-adic representation is ramified at \(p\). Letting \(p\) vary therefore changes both the finite extension and the prescribed shifted prime \(q=p+h\). Existing uniform Chebotarev theorems do not handle this diagonal.

### 2.4 What the strongest nearby results actually prove

Thorner's Theorem 1.2 in `Effective forms of the Sato–Tate conjecture` states that for two twist-inequivalent non-CM newforms \(f_1,f_2\) and fixed intervals \(I_1,I_2\),

```math
\left|
\pi_{f_1,f_2,I_1,I_2}(x)
-\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}(I_1)\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}(I_2)\pi(x)
\right|
\ll
\pi(x)
\frac{\log(k_1q_1k_2q_2\log\log x)}{\sqrt{\log\log x}}.
```

Here **both forms are evaluated at the same prime \(p\)**. It gives independence of two representations, not independence of one representation at \(p\) and \(p+h\).

The April 2026 preprint of Kumar–Kumari–Mishra extends effective joint Sato–Tate from rectangles to broad planar regions for two twist-inequivalent forms, but it remains a same-prime theorem.

Gillman–Kural–Pascadi–Peng–Sah prove a Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem and bounded gaps for primes whose normalized Frobenius trace lies in a fixed Sato–Tate interval. Chentouf–Cossaboom–Goldberg–Miller prove analogous bounded-gap results for joint Sato–Tate intervals. These theorems give infinitely many **some bounded gaps**, not one prescribed \(h\), and they require fixed intervals of positive Sato–Tate measure, not the shrinking singleton \(A_f(p)=0\).

**Answer to (1): no.** There is no theorem with the requested fixed-shift asymptotic independence, and the stated \(p^{-2}\) probability is not the correct modular-form scale.

## 3. Question (2): K3 surfaces and rank-three orthogonal monodromy

### 3.1 What a generic rank-three heuristic would say

For a pure weight-two rank-three factor with integral trace \(A_p\), the Weil bound is

```math
|A_p|\leq3p.
```

The normalized trace \(A_p/p\) lies in a fixed compact interval. If its lattice values behaved locally like a smooth continuous density, then the exact condition \(p\mid A_p\) would select only the finitely many values \(A_p=mp\), and a one-prime probability of order \(1/p\) would be plausible. Two independent such trace conditions would then have order \(1/p^2\).

This is only a local-limit heuristic. Ordinary Sato–Tate controls fixed intervals. A congruence modulo the observation prime is a shrinking target of width about \(1/p\), far below the resolution of known effective Sato–Tate estimates.

Moreover, the monodromy group alone does not determine the local lattice law. In the Apéry K3's split Shioda–Inose description, the repository proves

```math
\theta_p(a)=a_p(E_x)^2-p-\varepsilon_a p.
```

Modulo \(p\), this becomes \(a_p(E_x)^2\). Thus \(p\mid\theta_p(a)\) is equivalent, for large \(p\), to the elliptic trace-zero event \(a_p(E_x)=0\), whose natural scale is \(p^{-1/2}\), not \(p^{-1}\). Symmetric-square structure can therefore change the naive orthogonal trace heuristic.

### 3.2 Existing K3 Sato–Tate results are vertical or one-prime

Saad's `Explicit Sato-Tate type distribution for a family of K3 surfaces` treats a one-parameter Picard-rank-19 family over one finite field. If \(A_\lambda(p)\) denotes its normalized trace, then for \(-3\leq a<b\leq3\),

```math
\left|
\frac{\#\{\lambda\in\mathbf F_p:A_\lambda(p)\in[a,b]\}}{p}
-\frac1{4\pi}\int_a^b f(t)\,dt
\right|
\leq110.84\,p^{-1/4}.
```

This is an explicit \(O(3)\) law, but the averaging variable is \(\lambda\in\mathbf F_p\) at a single prime. Its error \(p^{-1/4}\) is also much larger than a \(1/p\)-scale atom.

Chen and Shen prove effective Sato–Tate distributions for surfaces arising from products of elliptic curves, including a K3 family, by effective **same-prime** joint Sato–Tate for the elliptic factors. Again, no shifted-prime correlation appears.

Chao Gu's December 2025 preprint proves potential automorphy for K3 surfaces over totally real fields with geometric Picard rank at least 17 under the stated hypotheses. Potential automorphy can provide analytic continuation, one-prime Sato–Tate, and Rankin–Selberg tools. It does not by itself create an additive-shift Euler product or a theorem for \((p,p+h)\).

### 3.3 \(\ell\)-independence of monodromy is not probabilistic independence of primes

Larsen–Pink and successors prove that the ranks, formal characters, root data, or related structures of the algebraic monodromy groups are independent of \(\ell\) under suitable compatible-system hypotheses. Amadio Guidi develops decomposition-based independence results. Hui proves, for suitable pure compatible systems with connected monodromy, the existence of a common coefficient-field form of the connected monodromy groups and, in the absolutely irreducible case, of their tautological representations.

These are structural theorems about the algebraic envelope of the representations. They do not say that two Frobenius conjugacy classes at additively related primes are independent. In particular:

- Frobenius elements are individually defined only up to conjugacy.
- There is no canonical shared eigenbasis for \(\mathrm{Frob}_p\) and \(\mathrm{Frob}_{p+h}\).
- Membership in the same group \(O(3)\) supplies the ambient support, not a product measure for a fixed shift.
- No Rankin–Selberg \(L\)-function has coefficients equal to a general product \(F(\mathrm{Frob}_p)G(\mathrm{Frob}_{p+h})\).

**Answer to (2): there is no known fixed-shift independence theorem for Frobenius conjugacy classes of a fixed K3 surface in \(O(3)\), and existing K3 Sato–Tate or potential-automorphy results do not reach the defining-characteristic shrinking target.**

## 4. Question (3): what Katz–Sarnak and its extensions do and do not give

### 4.1 Katz's Mellin equidistribution theorems

Katz's `Convolution and Equidistribution` proves a powerful but different kind of result.

- **Theorem 7.2** fixes a finite field \(k\) and an arithmetically semisimple, pure convolution object. As extensions \(E/k\) grow through a prescribed degree class, the Frobenius conjugacy classes attached to the good characters of \(E^\times\) become equidistributed in the relevant compact group/component.
- **Theorem 28.1** permits a sequence of finite fields \(k_i\) whose cardinalities tend to infinity, together with objects having the same Tannakian monodromy group and uniform complexity bounds. The empirical distributions over the good characters in each \(k_i^\times\) converge to the compact-group law.

Chapter 28 is sometimes described as “horizontal” because the finite fields may be prime fields \(\mathbf F_{p_i}\) with \(p_i\to\infty\). But its quantifiers are crucial:

```text
for each field k_i: average over essentially all good characters of k_i^×;
then let |k_i| tend to infinity.
```

It does **not** give a joint law for one selected character in \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) and one selected character in \(\mathbf F_q^\times\). It also does not select the deterministic character

```math
\chi_{p,n}=\omega_p^{\,n\bmod p},
```

and it does not control the reduction of a cyclotomic Frobenius trace modulo the defining characteristic.

Forey–Fresán–Kowalski greatly generalize this framework to arithmetic Fourier transforms on connected commutative algebraic groups, proving generic vanishing, a convolution Tannakian category, and equidistribution. Their framework remains a finite-field/character-average theorem; it does not supply the two-characteristic deterministic selection needed here.

### 4.2 There is a genuine multi-point theorem over one finite field

Suppose \(K_1,\ldots,K_r\) are trace functions of geometrically irreducible sheaves over the **same** \(\mathbf F_p\), and the sheaves have no accidental geometric isomorphisms or dualities among their translates. Goursat–Kolchin–Ribet product-monodromy criteria can identify the monodromy of the direct sum with a product. Deligne's Riemann Hypothesis then gives square-root cancellation in centered sums of products. Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel's `A study in sums of products` packages this into a form useful in analytic number theory.

Schematically, after subtracting invariant contributions, one obtains estimates of the shape

```math
\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}
K_1(x+h_1)\cdots K_r(x+h_r)
=O_{\mathrm{cond}}(p^{1/2}).
```

This is the closest established non-abelian analogue of the desired independence. It is extremely relevant to a **same-prime parameter average**, for example averaging \(j\) over all characters at fixed \(p\).

It still does not prove LGTQ-3, for three reasons:

1. LGTQ-3 fixes \(n\), hence selects one \(j_p=n\bmod p\) per prime rather than averaging \(j\) in one field.
2. The Apéry zero is a defining-characteristic reduction of a cyclotomic trace, not merely a fixed continuous class function of the complex compact-group conjugacy class.
3. Passing from one field to two characteristics requires a horizontal dispersion theorem; Deligne's square-root cancellation occurs separately inside each field.

### 4.3 “Pair correlation of Sato–Tate sequences” usually averages another variable

Balasubramanyam and Sinha prove pair-correlation statistics for Hecke angles after averaging over spaces of non-CM newforms. This is a powerful family average, not a theorem for one fixed form along shifted primes.

Mok and Zheng's work is especially diagnostic: they formulate pseudorandomness of the sequence of Frobenius angles of one fixed non-CM elliptic curve as conjectural. Thus even the macroscopic two-prime product law is not part of the proved Katz–Sarnak package, let alone the \(1/p\)-scale defining-characteristic event.

**Answer to (3): there are multi-representation and multi-parameter extensions of Katz–Sarnak, but no theorem that turns them into fixed-shift independence across rational primes.**

## 5. Question (4): the exact Apéry geometry and why compatibility does not couple two hits

### 5.1 The two relevant cohomological objects are different

The K3 fiber calculation in `toric_fiber_k3.tex` gives, for nonzero parameter \(a\),

```math
\mu_p(a)=p^2-6p+12+\theta_p(a),
```

where the generic primitive trace \(\theta_p(a)\) has dimension three. The file `toric_fiber_sym2.tex` identifies it with a symmetric-square/Asai trace on the relevant quadratic cover.

The coefficient marker, however, is

```math
b_j\equiv-\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\mu_p(a)a^j\pmod p,
```

up to the endpoint conventions recorded in `oracleC_result.tex`. Therefore a zero \(b_j\equiv0\pmod p\) can arise from cancellation among all fibers. No theorem in the repository identifies it with the vanishing of \(\theta_p(1/j)\), or with any trace of the single fiber \(X_{1/j}\).

### 5.2 At two primes there is no one compatible system carrying both markers

At \((p,j)\), the correct object is

```math
\mathcal M_{p,j}
=R\Gamma_c(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{\omega_p^j}).
```

At \((q,j')\), it is

```math
\mathcal M_{q,j'}
=R\Gamma_c(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_q},
\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{\omega_q^{j'}}).
```

They live in different characteristics, use characters of different orders, and naturally have different cyclotomic coefficient fields. They are related by the fixed integral map \(\Lambda\), but they are not simply two Frobenius classes of one fixed rank-three compatible system.

For fixed \(n\), the selections

```math
j=n\bmod p,
\qquad
j'=n\bmod q
```

are deterministic and correlated through the same integer. Katz's character average says nothing about this thin diagonal selection.

### 5.3 Why automorphy of the K3 factor is not enough

The Sym\(^2\)/Asai description is valuable because it can determine the local monodromy group, exceptional correspondences, and possible tensor decompositions. Automorphy or potential automorphy of the rank-three factor could then provide one-prime Sato–Tate and analytic estimates for coefficients of its standard and Rankin–Selberg \(L\)-functions.

But the desired product has the schematic form

```math
1_{\{b_{n\bmod p}=0\bmod p\}}
\,1_{\{b_{n\bmod q}=0\bmod q\}},
```

with two changing characteristics and two changing Kummer characters. It is not a coefficient of a standard Euler product. Rankin–Selberg theory naturally correlates two representations at the same prime or multiplicative indices; it does not encode the additive relation between two rational primes or the deterministic CRT diagonal in \(n\).

The CM proof of concept succeeds because a rank-one Hecke character converts Frobenius into scalar ideal-theoretic data with abelian reciprocity and multiplicativity. Non-CM monodromy may predict stronger randomness, but it removes exactly the scalar mechanism that makes the CM proof accessible.

## 6. The theorem package that could realistically prove the Apéry cross-prime estimate

### Stage A: an integral marked local system, not only a complex Sato–Tate class

The repository has already constructed the correct modulo-\(p\) marked scalar. The next geometric task is to put it into a form with uniform integral control:

1. construct a canonical integral/crystalline or Witt-vector realization of the Mellin object;
2. identify the reduction coordinate whose zero locus is exactly \(b_j\equiv0\pmod p\);
3. determine its arithmetic and geometric monodromy uniformly in the Kummer character;
4. if quotient digits \(b_j/p\) are needed, compute the lift modulo \(p^2\).

Ordinary \(\ell\)-adic monodromy with fixed \(\ell\neq p\) sees the characteristic-zero conjugacy class but not automatically the reduction at the prime above \(p\).

### Stage B: same-characteristic product monodromy for shifted characters

For fixed \(p\) and shifts \(h_1,h_2\), study the tuple

```math
(\mathcal M_{p,j},\mathcal M_{p,j+h_1},\mathcal M_{p,j+h_2})
```

as \(j\) varies. A useful theorem would prove that, outside the already identified reflection/diagonal correspondences, its geometric monodromy is the full product of the three individual groups. This is a concrete Goursat–Kolchin–Ribet problem.

A successful result would give character-average independence at one prime and would rigorously justify the “fully transverse” label. It could also identify every exceptional relation that must be removed before a global sieve.

This stage is plausible with current geometric technology, although the defining-characteristic integral coordinate is harder than an ordinary complex trace function.

### Stage C: horizontal dispersion for the deterministic residues

Define the centered incidence function

```math
X_p(m)=1_{\{m\bmod p\in Z_p\}}-\frac{Z(p)}p.
```

The genuinely new input is a bound that couples distinct moduli, for example a moment estimate of the schematic form

```math
\sum_{m\in I_N}
\left|\sum_{P<p\leq2P}X_p(m)\right|^{2k}
\ll
N\,(Ck\,\lambda_P)^k+o(N),
```

uniformly in the critical range and with \(k>6\), where

```math
\lambda_P=\sum_{P<p\leq2P}\frac{Z(p)}p.
```

A multilinear shifted version must incorporate the three zero conditions and the maximum over \(h_1,h_2\leq C\log N\).

Complete CRT averaging would give independence over a full period of length \(pq\). In LGTQ-3 the interval in \(m\) is much shorter than the product of the relevant large primes, so that complete-period argument is unavailable. The classical large sieve carries an \(N+P^2\) barrier; when \(P\) is comparable with \(N\), the \(P^2\) term is fatal. A proof must exploit Apéry-specific bilinear cancellation rather than only the sizes of the sets \(Z_p\).

This is the precise content of the repository's “marked two-characteristic crystalline Mellin dispersion” residual. It is not supplied by monodromy alone.

### Stage D: high moments and the maximum over short shifts

An average over \(n\), or an average over \((h_1,h_2)\), is materially easier than the pointwise maximum in LGTQ-3. The project's existing observation that one fixed factorial moment of order \(k>6\) would suffice is consistent with the natural strategy:

1. prove local product monodromy and bounded local moments;
2. prove a horizontal second moment or Barban–Davenport–Halberstam estimate;
3. amplify to a fixed high moment;
4. absorb the \(O((\log N)^2)\) choices of \((h_1,h_2)\) by Markov/union bounds;
5. use the exact transversality classification to discard algebraic diagonals.

## 7. Feasibility assessment

| Proposed input | Present status | Likely value for Apéry |
|---|---|---|
| Compatible-system equality of Euler factors | Available | Necessary background, no cross-prime saving |
| \(\ell\)-independence/common monodromy group | Available in broad settings | Helps identify the correct group, no shifted-prime law |
| Automorphy/potential automorphy of rank-three K3 factor | Partial, improving | Gives one-prime analytic tools; does not address the marked Mellin diagonal |
| Same-prime product monodromy for three shifted Kummer twists | Not yet proved for this marker | Best concrete geometric next theorem |
| Defining-characteristic local anti-concentration at scale \(1/p\) | Open | Essential |
| Fixed-shift \(p,p+h\) Frobenius independence | Open even in simpler non-CM settings | Too strong as a first target |
| Average-\(n\) two-characteristic dispersion | Open | Most realistic global bridge to LGTQ-3 |
| Pointwise LGTQ-3 with max over logarithmic shifts | Open | Requires high-moment strengthening of the preceding line |

The overall feasibility verdict is:

- **Existing Katz–Sarnak, Sato–Tate, compatible-system, and automorphy theorems do not imply LGTQ-3.**
- **A local product-monodromy theorem for the Apéry Mellin object is a realistic and worthwhile subproblem**, but it will not close the conjecture by itself.
- **The decisive new result must be a horizontal cross-modulus dispersion theorem.** This is an analytic-arithmetic statement about a deterministic CRT diagonal, not merely a geometric equidistribution theorem.
- A direct fixed-\(h\) pointwise theorem modeled on \(\mathrm{Frob}_p,\mathrm{Frob}_{p+h}\) is substantially beyond the current frontier. An averaged theorem in \(n\), the shifts, or the prime block is the credible first target.

## 8. Direct answers to the four questions

### (1)

No theorem is known of the stated fixed-shift form for a non-CM weight-2 eigenform. For rational coefficients, Hasse's bound converts both congruences into exact trace-zero conditions. The independent trace scale is \(p^{-1}\), not \(p^{-2}\), conditional on both arguments being prime. Even the single Lang–Trotter asymptotic is open, and product-Sato–Tate for successive primes of one fixed curve is currently conjectural.

### (2)

For K3 surfaces, there are strong one-prime, vertical-family, and monodromy-structure results, including explicit \(O(3)\) laws. There is no theorem giving independent Frobenius conjugacy classes at the fixed shifted primes \(p\) and \(p+h\). \(\ell\)-independence of algebraic monodromy groups is a different statement.

### (3)

Katz–Sarnak has genuine multi-object extensions: same-prime joint Sato–Tate, product monodromy for several sheaves over one finite field, and character-average Mellin equidistribution as the field grows. None provides a joint law for one deterministically selected character in characteristic \(p\) and another in characteristic \(q\), nor the defining-characteristic reduction needed for Apéry zeros.

### (4)

For Apéry, the currently proved marker is the Kummer–Mellin supertrace attached to \((p,j)\), not the trace of \(X_{1/j}\). A viable proof requires:

1. uniform integral monodromy for that marked Mellin object;
2. product monodromy for the three shifted characters at one prime;
3. an Apéry-specific horizontal large-sieve/dispersion theorem for \(j_p=n\bmod p\) across distinct characteristics;
4. high moments to handle the maximum over \(h_1,h_2\leq C\log n\).

No existing automorphic or geometric theorem supplies item 3. It is the real cross-prime bottleneck.
