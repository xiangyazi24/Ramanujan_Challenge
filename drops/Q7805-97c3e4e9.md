# \(p=7\) unit-root matrices: what exists, what does not, and one fully explicit corrected computation

## Verdict

The requested table

\[
\Phi_{7,r},\qquad \Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{7,r},\qquad 0\le r\le6,
\]

does **not** exist in the checked-in construction.  Producing seven numerical matrices would repeat precisely the dictionary error corrected in Q7799:

- \(r\) is an Apéry coefficient/Mellin-character index;
- \(t\) is a geometric point of the Apéry K3 base;
- the rank-three K3 crystal and its fiberwise Frobenius are indexed by \(t\), not by \(r\).

The repository contains two related but different objects.

1. **Geometric K3 object.**  At a nonzero geometric fiber \(t\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), `toric_fiber_sym2.tex` constructs a weight-two factor \(V_t\).  At a generic fiber it has rank three and reciprocal polynomial
   \[
   P_t(T)=(T-p)(T^2-a_tT+p^2),
   \]
   with ordinary slopes \((0,1,2)\).  A fiberwise projector 
   \(\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{p,t}\) is legitimate.

2. **Marked Mellin object.**  For \(1\le r\le p-2\), `lemmaBprime_result.tex` constructs a semisimplified effective Frobenius **characteristic factor**
   \[
   (T-p)(T^2-a_{p,r}T+p^3),
   \qquad a_{p,r}\equiv b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
   \]
   This has slopes \((0,1,3)\) when \(b_r\not\equiv0\pmod p\).  The repository does not construct an integral rank-three crystalline basis with a matrix \(\Phi_{p,r}\); it gives the characteristic factor and trace identities.  The resonant indices \(r=0,p-1\) are not covered by this nontrivial-character rank-three statement.

A characteristic polynomial permits an arbitrary companion matrix, but that is not “the Frobenius matrix in the hypergeometric connection basis.”  A basis change conjugates the linearized matrix, and before linearization a change of basis acts by the semilinear gauge rule

\[
A'(t)=G(t)^{-1}A(t)\,\sigma(G(t)).
\]

No such basis \(G\), Frobenius lift, and comparison matrix are fixed in the repository for an \(r\)-indexed crystal.

Nevertheless, one can give two useful explicit outputs:

- the complete \(p=7\) calculation showing why \(r\ne t\);
- an entirely explicit projector for the **corrected geometric fiber \(t=3\)**, in a stated rational-canonical basis.

## 1. The coefficient calculation at \(p=7\)

The first seven Apéry numbers are

\[
1,\ 5,\ 73,\ 1445,\ 33001,\ 819005,\ 21460825.
\]

Modulo \(7\),

\[
\boxed{(b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_6)\equiv(1,5,3,3,3,5,1)\pmod7.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{Z_7=\varnothing.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

In particular, the calculation in the question is correct:

\[
1445=7\cdot206+3,
\qquad 7\nmid b_3.
\]

But this is a statement about the Mellin coefficient indexed by \(r=3\); it is not, by itself, a geometric-fiber ordinarity statement.

### The geometric Hasse polynomial gives a different test

The geometric Hasse polynomial is

\[
H_7(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{6}b_rt^r
=1+5t+3t^2+3t^3+3t^4+5t^5+t^6
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_7[t].
\]

Its values are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
t&0&1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
H_7(t)&1&0&4&1&4&1&2.
\end{array}
\tag{1.3}
\]

So the geometric fiber \(t=1\) is nonordinary even though \(Z_7\) is empty.  This is a concrete \(p=7\) disproof of the identification

\[
r\in Z_p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{the geometric fiber }t=r\text{ is nonordinary}.
\]

Also \(t=0\) is a boundary/singular parameter, not one of the nonzero K3 fibers in `toric_fiber_k3.tex`.

The exact point-count formulas in `toric_fiber_sym2_verify.py` give, for the six nonzero geometric parameters,

\[
\bigl(\theta_7(1),\ldots,\theta_7(6)\bigr)
=(0,-10,1,-10,1,9),
\tag{1.4}
\]

with cohomological dimensions

\[
(2,2,3,2,3,3).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The rank drops at \(t=1\) and at the two branch parameters \(t=2,4\).  Hence even after deliberately reinterpreting \(r\) as a geometric parameter, there cannot be seven rank-three matrices.

## 2. The maximum matrix information supplied by the marked \(r\)-indexed factor

For \(1\le r\le5\), write

\[
P^{\mathrm{Mell}}_{7,r}(T)
=(T-7)(T^2-a_{7,r}T+343),
\qquad a_{7,r}\equiv b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

If one merely wants a companion representative of this known semisimplified characteristic factor, one may choose

\[
C_{7,r}(a_{7,r})=
\begin{pmatrix}
7&0&0\\
0&0&-343\\
0&1&a_{7,r}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is **not** the hypergeometric connection matrix.  It is just one matrix having the required characteristic polynomial.

Reduction modulo \(7\) uses only \(a_{7,r}\equiv b_r\):

\[
\overline C_{7,r}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&1&b_r
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For \(b_r\ne0\), its rank-one unit spectral idempotent is

\[
\overline P^{\mathrm{comp}}_{7,r}
=b_r^{-1}\overline C_{7,r}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&b_r^{-1}&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus the only mod-\(7\) companion-basis data determined by the Apéry residues are

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
r&1&2&3&4&5\\ \hline
b_r&5&3&3&3&5\\
b_r^{-1}&3&5&5&5&3.
\end{array}
\tag{2.5}
\]

There is no corresponding row for \(r=0,6\), because those are the trivial/resonant Kummer characters, outside the stated rank-three middle-extension factor.

To lift (2.4) to an actual \(7\)-adic idempotent, one needs the full \(7\)-adic trace \(a_{7,r}\), not merely its residue \(b_r\).  If

\[
\alpha_r^2-a_{7,r}\alpha_r+343=0,
\quad v_7(\alpha_r)=0,
\qquad
\beta_r=343/\alpha_r,
\]

then the companion-basis projector would be

\[
P^{\mathrm{comp}}_{7,r}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&-\beta_r/(\alpha_r-\beta_r)&-343/(\alpha_r-\beta_r)\\
0&1/(\alpha_r-\beta_r)&\alpha_r/(\alpha_r-\beta_r)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

But (2.6) is still only the projector of the chosen companion representative.  It is not a horizontal projector in a constructed \(r\)-indexed crystal.  The exact values \(a_{7,r}\), the prime above \(7\), and the comparison basis would all have to be fixed before a numerical table could be claimed.

## 4. A completely explicit corrected calculation at the geometric fiber \(t=3\)

This is the nearest honest replacement for the requested \(r=3\) matrix.

For \(p=7,t=3\),

\[
D_t=t^2-34t+1\equiv6\pmod7,
\]

so the elliptic cover is inert.  The exact finite-field formulas in `toric_fiber_sym2_verify.py` give

\[
\mu_7(3)=20,
\qquad
\theta_7(3)=20-(7^2-6\cdot7+12)=1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently, the quadratic-extension elliptic trace is

\[
a_{49}(E_x)=8.
\]

The geometric rank-three Frobenius polynomial is therefore

\[
\boxed{
P_{7,3}^{\mathrm{geom}}(T)
=T^3-T^2+7T-343
=(T-7)(T^2+6T+49).}
\tag{4.2}
\]

A block rational-canonical representative is

\[
\boxed{
\Phi^{\mathrm{can}}_{7,t=3}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
7&0&0\\
0&0&-49\\
0&1&-6
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This matrix has slopes \((0,1,2)\).  Let 

\[
\alpha^2+6\alpha+49=0,
\qquad \alpha\equiv1\pmod7,
\]

be the unit root, and put

\[
\beta=49/\alpha=-6-\alpha,
\qquad
\delta=\alpha-\beta.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\alpha=-3+\frac12\sqrt{-160},
\qquad
\delta=\sqrt{-160},
\]

where the square root is chosen congruent to \(1\pmod7\).  The exact rank-one projector in the basis of (4.3) is

\[
\boxed{
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur,can}}_{7,t=3}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&-\beta/\delta&-49/\delta\\
0&1/\delta&\alpha/\delta
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

It satisfies

\[
\Pi^2=\Pi,
\qquad
\Phi\Pi=\Pi\Phi,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}\Pi=1.
\]

### Numerical \(7\)-adic entries

Hensel lifting gives

\[
\alpha\equiv
1\pmod7,
\quad43\pmod{49},
\quad288\pmod{343},
\quad2003\pmod{2401}.
\]

Modulo \(7^4=2401\),

\[
\beta\equiv392,
\qquad
\delta\equiv1611,
\qquad
\delta^{-1}\equiv155.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur,can}}_{7,t=3}
\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&1666&2009\\
0&155&736
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod{2401}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Reducing further modulo \(7\),

\[
\boxed{
\overline\Pi^{\mathrm{ur,can}}_{7,t=3}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&1&1
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Because the other eigenvalues have valuations \(1\) and \(2\), while the unit root is a \(7\)-adic unit, this is exactly the Dwork spectral limit

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\left(\Phi^{\mathrm{can}}_{7,t=3}\right)^{n!}
=
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur,can}}_{7,t=3}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

The divisor \(p^{n!\cdot0}\) in the question is \(1\) and has no effect.

The qualification remains essential: (4.3)--(4.7) concern the geometric fiber \(t=3\), not the Apéry Mellin coefficient \(r=3\).

## 5. Rank of the tensor-square

There are three different notions here.

### 5.1 Literal tensor product of rank-one projectors

If 

\[
\Pi_t:V_t\to V_t,
\qquad
\Pi_{t'}:V_{t'}\to V_{t'}
\]

are rank-one projectors, then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}(\Pi_t\otimes\Pi_{t'})=1.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

For \(t\ne t'\), there is no canonical matrix “diagonal” to subtract: the two operators act on different fibers.  In factorial-moment language, excluding \(t=t'\) already removes the index diagonal, and every remaining off-diagonal tensor summand is still rank one.

### 5.2 Ordered connected square of the ambient rank-three local system

For the same rank-three polarized local system \(V\),

\[
V\otimes V
=\langle q^{-1}\rangle
\oplus\Lambda^2V
\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2_0V,
\]

with ranks

\[
1+3+5=9.
\]

Removing the scalar polarization/duality line gives

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}=8.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the **ordered connected square** from Q7799.

### 5.3 Symmetric trace-free connected square

If one first keeps only the symmetric/duality-even part, then

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2V
=\langle q^{-1}\rangle\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2_0V
\]

has rank \(6=1+5\).  Removing the scalar trace line leaves

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}=5.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus:

- literal \(\Pi_t\otimes\Pi_{t'}\): rank \(1\);
- ambient ordered connected square: rank \(8\);
- ambient symmetric trace-free square: rank \(5\).

These ranks must not be interchanged.  The rank-\(5\) and rank-\(8\) objects are representation-theoretic connected pieces of the ambient rank-three system; they are not the matrix rank of the tensor product of two unit-root idempotents.

## 6. What would be required to answer the original request literally

A genuine table of \(\Phi_{7,r}\) would require all of the following new data.

1. Construct the \(r\)-indexed Kummer/Mellin cohomology object, not merely its semisimplified characteristic factor.
2. Fix the coefficient field, the prime \(\mathfrak p\mid7\), the Teichmüller character, and the Frobenius lift.
3. Fix an integral/overconvergent crystalline basis and its comparison with the hypergeometric realization.
4. Compute the full \(7\)-adic traces \(a_{7,r}\) to a stated precision; \(b_r\bmod7\) is insufficient.
5. Treat the resonant characters \(r=0,6\) separately.
6. Prove that the resulting projectors vary horizontally and are compatible with the tensor construction.

Until those steps are carried out, a seven-row numerical matrix table would be a table of arbitrary companion representatives, not matrices of the Apéry crystal asserted in the question.

## Bottom line

The coefficient calculation \(7\nmid b_3\) is correct, but it does not define a rank-three geometric Frobenius matrix \(\Phi_{7,3}\).  The strongest honest numerical statement presently available is the corrected geometric computation (4.3)--(4.7).  Its projector is explicitly

\[
\Pi^{\mathrm{ur,can}}_{7,t=3}
\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&1666&2009\\
0&155&736
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod{7^4},
\]

but this is a rational-canonical representative for the geometric fiber \(t=3\), not an \(r=3\) Mellin projector.  For two distinct genuine rank-one projectors, the literal tensor product has rank one; the ranks \(8\) and \(5\) arise only after passing to the ordered or symmetric connected square of the ambient rank-three local system.
