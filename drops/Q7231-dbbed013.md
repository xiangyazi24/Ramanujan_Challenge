# Verdict

I pinned the audit to public `main` commit
`2bee8355b4dcbc534c53f9cf2ec3dba17c066b31`.

There is an exact transfer-matrix algebraization of every fixed-length
condition

\[
N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0,
\]

but the direct algebraic complexity grows with the word length.  More
precisely:

1. for fixed \(G\), \(N_G(u)\) is one matrix entry of a product of \(G-1\)
   nonautonomous \(2\times2\) matrices;
2. an exact unrolled transfer variety uses \(4G+1\) scalar variables and
   equations of degree at most \(7\);
3. eliminating the intermediate matrices gives the polynomial \(N_G(u)\)
   of exact degree \(3(G-1)\);
4. the determinant of the transfer product has \(G-1\) distinct singular
   parameters when \(G<p\), so the direct transfer local system has a
   divisor whose complexity is already linear in \(G\);
5. at \(G\asymp\sqrt p\), the standard Deligne bound attached to the direct
   polynomial or rational-function detector is of order
   \(G\sqrt p\asymp p\), hence is no better than the trivial complete-sum
   bound; most of the far range has \(G\gg\sqrt p\).

Thus **the transfer word itself does not produce a bounded-complexity
variety or sheaf to which black-box Lang--Weil/Deligne can be applied**.
The exact finite-field interpolation that makes \(G\) an ordinary algebraic
coordinate has degree \(O(p)\), and its degree-only Betti/genus bounds are
far too large.

There is, however, a useful positive compression which does not seem to be
recorded in the cited sections.  After dividing by the factorial gauge, the
whole family of continuants is the Taylor-coefficient kernel of a single
rank-three spectral deformation of the Apéry Picard--Fuchs operator.  Its
pole divisor in the generating variable is independent of both recurrence
indices.  This is a genuine bounded-complexity **differential** object, but
it does not make the Taylor index \(G\) an algebraic parameter and does not
by itself supply an ℓ-adic trace sheaf or a point-counting theorem.

Finally, one must not turn the direct degree calculation into a universal
no-go theorem for every possible geometric realization.  The repository's
`oracleC_result.tex` proves exactly why that inference is unsafe: the direct
transfer word for \(b_j\) has growing degree, yet \(b_j\) has a different
fixed toric Kummer realization of uniformly bounded cohomological
complexity.  What is missing here is a comparable **two-point gap-kernel**
realization together with an integral moving-character/large-sieve theorem.
Uniform cohomological dimension alone would still not count reductions that
vanish modulo \(p\).

# 1. Exact incidence and the nonwrapping domain

Write

\[
P(t)=(2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5)
\]

and use the repository convention

\[
N_1(u)=1,\qquad N_2(u)=P(u+1),
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(u)=P(u+h)N_h(u)-(u+h)^6N_{h-1}(u).
\]

The proposition in `far_bridge_incidence.tex` gives the exact count

\[
A_p^{\mathrm{nw}}(K)
 =\sum_{s=2}^{\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor}
  \sum_{G=K+1}^{p-1-s}
  \#\left\{x:
  \begin{array}{l}
  0\le x\le p-1-s-G,\\
  N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0
  \end{array}
  \right\}.
\]

Put \(u=x+s\).  In the displayed nonwrapping range,

\[
0\le x<x+s<x+s+G<p.
\]

Every denominator used in the projective transfer is therefore a unit.
This matters: the unrestricted affine majorant in the same file also allows
values of \(u\) for which a transfer word crosses the singular parameter,
where the polynomial identity remains valid but the interpretation as a
word in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) does not.

The count is of actual \(\mathbf F_p\)-roots.  As the repository stresses,

\[
\#\{x\in\mathbf F_p:N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\}
\]

is not equal in general to

\[
\deg\gcd_{\mathbf F_p[X]}(N_s(X),N_G(X+s)),
\]

because the latter also counts nonsplit irreducible factors.

# 2. Exact transfer encoding and its degree

Use the matrices from `FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex`:

\[
M_t=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&(t+1)^6\\
-1&P(t)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det M_t=(t+1)^6.
\]

The exact transfer-product lemma and the dictionary
\(N_h(r)=U_{h-1}(r+1)\) give, for \(G\ge2\),

\[
T_G(u):=M_{u+G-1}M_{u+G-2}\cdots M_{u+1},
\]

\[
\boxed{N_G(u)=e_2^{\mathsf T}T_G(u)e_2=(T_G(u))_{22}.}
\]

For \(G=1\), the empty product is the identity and its lower-right entry is
\(N_1=1\).  In algorithmic form the construction is literally

```text
T := I_2
for j = 1,...,G-1:
    T := M_{u+j} T
return T_22
```

The same repository lemma proves

\[
\deg N_G=3(G-1)
\]

over \(\mathbf Q\).  Indeed, if \(\lambda_m\) is the leading coefficient
of \(U_m\), then

\[
\lambda_0=1,\qquad \lambda_1=34,
\qquad \lambda_{m+1}=34\lambda_m-\lambda_{m-1}.
\]

The characteristic roots \(17\pm12\sqrt2\) are positive, so no leading
term cancels.  This is an exact degree statement, not an upper bound coming
from a generic matrix-product estimate.

The determinant is equally explicit:

\[
\boxed{
\det T_G(u)=\prod_{j=2}^{G}(u+j)^6.
}
\]

For \(G<p\), these are \(G-1\) distinct points of the geometric \(u\)-line.
On the nonwrapping integer interval none is encountered, but a transfer
sheaf on the whole line must see the growing puncture divisor.  The
singular full-cycle step is the case \(t=-1\); \(M_{-1}\) has rank one.
Consequently there is no invertible full-period monodromy with which to
replace a long word by the inverse of its short complement.  Reflection
also preserves the gap length \(G\); it does not replace \(G\) by \(p-G\).
This is consistent with `full_cycle_bridge_reciprocity.tex`, where the
full-cycle multiplication reduces to the reflection cocycle and gives no
second bridge equation.

# 3. A completely explicit transfer variety

For a fixed \(G\), introduce matrices

\[
T_j=
\begin{pmatrix}a_j&b_j\\c_j&d_j\end{pmatrix}
\qquad(0\le j\le G-1)
\]

and impose \(T_0=I_2\) and

\[
T_{j+1}=M_{u+j+1}T_j
\qquad(0\le j\le G-2).
\]

In scalar equations this is

\[
a_{j+1}=(u+j+2)^6c_j,
\qquad b_{j+1}=(u+j+2)^6d_j,
\]

\[
c_{j+1}=-a_j+P(u+j+1)c_j,
\qquad d_{j+1}=-b_j+P(u+j+1)d_j.
\]

The largest total degree is \(7\).  Before imposing the return equation,
this scheme is the graph of a deterministic polynomial recursion and is
isomorphic to the \(u\)-line.  Adding

\[
d_{G-1}=0
\]

cuts out the divisor \(N_G(u)=0\).  Thus an exact fixed-\(G\) encoding has

\[
1+4G\quad\text{scalar variables},
\]

\[
4G+1\quad\text{scalar equations including the endpoint},
\]

and local equation degree at most \(7\).  Unrolling both the short and long
words costs \(O(s+G)\) variables and equations.

This construction makes the obstruction precise.  One may keep the local
degree bounded only by allowing the ambient dimension and the number of
equations to grow with \(G\); eliminating the path variables gives a
one-variable equation whose degree is exactly \(3(G-1)\).

## Fixed-complexity algebraic-family no-go

Here is the rigorous scope of the degree obstruction.

Suppose one asks for a single ordinary algebraic family with the following
properties:

- a fixed number \(n\) of ambient and auxiliary variables;
- defining equations of degree at most a fixed \(D\);
- an ordinary algebraic parameter \(g\);
- at \(g=G\), the scheme-theoretic image on the \(u\)-line is exactly the
  natural divisor \(V(N_G)\), with bounded projection multiplicity.

A zero-dimensional scheme in a fixed affine space cut out by equations of
degree at most \(D\) has length bounded by \(D^{O(n)}\), and elimination or
projection cannot make the degree unbounded while \(n,D\) and the
projection multiplicity remain fixed.  But

\[
\operatorname{length}V(N_G)=\deg N_G=3(G-1)\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Therefore no such fixed-dimensional, bounded-degree algebraic family can
have the natural \(N_G\)-divisors as its fibers.  Equivalently, in any
direct ordinary-algebraic encoding, at least one of the ambient dimension,
the equation degrees, or the projection multiplicity must grow with \(G\).

This is a no-go theorem for exact algebraic fibers produced from the
transfer word.  It is **not** a theorem that no unrelated trace or period
construction can encode the same reductions; Section 8 below explains the
important distinction.

# 4. Making \(G\) an algebraic coordinate over \(\mathbf F_p\)

There is also a direct exact three-variable encoding, but it is
\(p\)-dependent and has degree linear in \(p\).

For \(1\le h\le p-1\), put

\[
\delta_h(T)=1-(T-h)^{p-1}\in\mathbf F_p[T].
\]

On \(\mathbf F_p\), this is the Kronecker delta at \(h\).  Define

\[
\mathscr N_p(U,T)
 =\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}\delta_h(T)\,\overline{N_h}(U).
\]

Then, for every \(t\in\mathbf F_p^\times\),

\[
\mathscr N_p(u,t)=N_t(u).
\]

Consequently the affine scheme

\[
\mathcal C_p:
\quad
\mathscr N_p(X,S)=0,
\qquad
\mathscr N_p(X+S,G)=0
\]

contains exactly the desired equalities on its
\(\mathbf F_p\)-points with \(S,G\ne0\).  The inequalities
\(2\le S\le\sqrt p\) and \(G>K\) are then imposed externally on the standard
integer representatives.

The formal degree bounds are

\[
\deg_T\mathscr N_p\le p-1,
\qquad
\deg_U\mathscr N_p\le3(p-2),
\]

and hence

\[
\deg_{\mathrm{tot}}\mathscr N_p\le4p-7.
\]

Reducing each \(N_h(U)\) modulo \(U^p-U\) lowers the degree in \(U\) to at
most \(p-1\), but the total degree is still \(O(p)\), and the resulting
polynomial agrees only on \(\mathbf F_p\), not on extension fields.  It is
therefore not the reduction of one fixed bounded-degree model over
\(\mathbf Z\).

A black-box Lang--Weil/Deligne estimate based only on these degrees cannot
reach the target.  Even in the optimistic case that a projective closure is
a smooth geometrically irreducible complete-intersection curve of degrees
\(d_1,d_2\asymp p\), its arithmetic genus has scale

\[
1+\frac12d_1d_2(d_1+d_2-4)=O(p^3),
\]

so the corresponding degree-only Weil error is \(O(p^{7/2})\), worse than
the ambient trivial bound.  Singular or reducible behavior can only require
additional component control.  A miraculous low-genus decomposition is not
logically excluded, but it is precisely extra Apéry-specific structure; it
does not follow from the interpolation construction.

The same tradeoff appears in a bounded-degree selector construction: one
can compute every prefix matrix \(T_j(u)\) for \(j<p\), add one-hot variables
selecting \(G\), and use only quadratic selector equations, but this uses
\(O(p)\) variables and \(O(p)\) transfer equations.  Thus one may trade
large degree for large dimension, not obtain bounded complexity.

# 5. Direct sheaf conductor and the square-root threshold

There are two natural direct sheaf detectors, and both inherit linear
complexity.

First, the polynomial detector uses Artin--Schreier sheaves pulled back by
\(N_G\).  At good reductions in the range where the degree is below \(p\),
the Swan conductor at infinity is governed by

\[
\deg N_G=3(G-1).
\]

Second, the factorial-gauge return coefficient is

\[
R_G(u)=
\frac{N_G(u)}{\prod_{j=2}^{G}(u+j)^3}.
\]

Over characteristic zero the endpoint identity recorded in
`oracleC_result.tex` shows that the \(G-1\) apparent finite poles are not
cancelled.  More intrinsically, the full transfer product has determinant

\[
\prod_{j=2}^{G}(u+j)^6,
\]

so the direct invertible transfer local system is naturally defined on a
line with \(G-1\) punctures.  For every \(G<p\), those punctures are distinct
in characteristic \(p\).  At good reductions, a direct additive-character
sheaf therefore has conductor \(\asymp G\).

The standard complete-sum estimate for a rank-bounded sheaf of conductor
\(C\) is of order \(C\sqrt p\).  Hence the direct construction reaches the
trivial scale precisely at

\[
G\asymp\sqrt p:
\qquad C\sqrt p\asymp G\sqrt p\asymp p.
\]

The far range in the repository begins at
\(K\asymp\sqrt p/\sqrt{\log p}\) and extends to \(G\asymp p\).  Near its
lower edge a degree-only bound can save at most a square root of a logarithm;
at \(G\ge\sqrt p\) it is already trivial.  Moreover, even a hypothetical
\(O(1)\) estimate for each fixed pair \((s,G)\) would sum to
\(p^{3/2}\), since there are \(\asymp\sqrt p\) short indices and
\(\asymp p\) long indices.  The needed \(O(p^{1+o(1)})\) result must exploit
the **joint** three-parameter geometry or a genuine large-sieve effect; it
cannot come from termwise Deligne alone.

Bad reductions can lower the degree or cancel some marked-function poles,
so the preceding conductor statement should not be misread as a lower bound
for every individual pair \((p,G)\).  What is rigorous and uniform is that
the direct transfer model has a divisor with \(G-1\) moving degeneracy
points and exact characteristic-zero output degree \(3(G-1)\).  Therefore
black-box conductor bounds for this model necessarily carry \(G\)-dependence;
there is no uniform \(O(1)\)-conductor theorem available from the transfer
word.

# 6. The strongest positive compression: a fixed rank-three differential kernel

The recurrence admits a substantially better local compression than the
unrolled word.  It is useful because it identifies exactly what a future
geometric construction would have to geometrize.

For a parameter \(a\), define

\[
V_m(a)=\frac{U_m(a)}{(a+1)_m^3},
\qquad
(a+1)_m=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(a+j),
\qquad V_{-1}(a)=0.
\]

Then \(V_0=1\), and direct division of the \(U\)-recurrence gives

\[
(a+m+1)^3V_{m+1}(a)
 =P(a+m)V_m(a)-(a+m)^3V_{m-1}(a).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
F_a(z)=\sum_{m\ge0}V_m(a)z^m,
\qquad \theta=z\frac{d}{dz}.
\]

Multiplying (6.1) by \(z^{m+1}\), summing for \(m\ge0\), and using
\(V_{-1}=0\) gives the exact differential equation

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_aF_a=0,
}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_a
 =(	heta+a)^3-a^3
  -zP(	heta+a)
  +z^2(	heta+a+1)^3.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

If

\[
\mathcal L_0=	heta^3-zP(	heta)+z^2(	heta+1)^3
\]

is the Apéry Picard--Fuchs operator, then conjugation by \(z^a\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_0(z^aF_a)=a^3z^aF_a.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the gap kernel is a spectral family for one fixed third-order
operator.  The coefficient of \(	heta^3\) in (6.2) is

\[
1-34z+z^2,
\]

so the associated rank-three differential system has a pole/singular
divisor contained in

\[
z=0,\qquad z^2-34z+1=0,\qquad z=\infty,
\]

independent of \(a\) and \(m\).  The parameter \(a\) enters with bounded
polynomial degree.  This is a genuine \(O(1)\)-complexity de Rham family.

For the long bridge, take

\[
a=u+1,\qquad m=G-1.
\]

Then

\[
N_G(u)=U_{G-1}(u+1)
      =((u+2)_{G-1})^3V_{G-1}(u+1).
\]

On a nonwrapping interval all factors in the Pochhammer symbol are units, so

\[
\boxed{
N_G(u)=0
\iff
V_{G-1}(u+1)=0.
}
\]

There is also an exact two-point interpretation.  Let \(b,c\) be the two
solution rows used to define the projective orbit, with

\[
b_nc_{n-1}-b_{n-1}c_n=-\frac1{n^3}.
\]

For integers \(a\ge1\), the gap criterion gives

\[
\boxed{
V_m(a)
 =a^3\bigl(b_{a-1}c_{a+m}-c_{a-1}b_{a+m}\bigr).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Hence \(V_m(a)\) is the normalized two-point Green/Casoratian kernel of the
Apéry recurrence.

Equations (6.2)--(6.4) are the strongest bounded-local-complexity
construction I can prove.  They do **not** yet produce the desired finite
field geometry:

- \(m=G-1\) is a Taylor-coefficient index, not a coordinate on the
  \(z\)-line;
- extracting the \(m\)-th coefficient is an order-\(m\) jet operation, which
  reintroduces growing complexity if it is algebraized naively;
- a differential module over \(\mathbf Q(a,z)\) is not automatically an
  \(\ell\)-adic sheaf with Frobenius traces equal to these coefficients;
- even a bounded-rank Frobenius realization for each character \(m\) would
  not automatically be a single sheaf on an algebraic \(m\)-line.

For \(a=0\), \(V_m(0)=b_m\), and the repository has a separate fixed toric
constant-term/Kummer realization.  Extending that construction to the
full two-point kernel (6.4) would require an integral geometric realization
of the companion/logarithmic solution as well as compatibility in both
indices.  No such theorem is present in the repository.

# 7. Resultants do not repair the growing-index problem

The separated-root-strip proposition in `meso_result.tex` proves that

\[
\mathcal R_{s,G}
 =\operatorname{Res}_x(N_s(x),N_G(x+s))
\]

is a positive nonzero integer for every \(s,G\ge2\).  The norm estimates
used in `near_bridge_energy.tex` extend without change to arbitrary
\(s,G\):

\[
\log\|N_h\|_2\ll h\log(2h),
\]

and translation plus the standard resultant inequality give

\[
\boxed{
\log|\mathcal R_{s,G}|
 \ll sG\log(2(s+G)).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let ν_p(s,G) denote the number of actual common
\(\mathbf F_p\)-roots.  Smith normal form gives

\[
\nu_p(s,G)\le v_p(\mathcal R_{s,G}),
\]

including the cases in which a leading coefficient drops modulo \(p\).
For \(X<p\le2X\), \(s\le H\asymp\sqrt X\), and the far range
\(G<p\le2X\), summing (7.1) gives only

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{X<p\le2X}
 \sum_{s\le H}
 \sum_{G<p}
 \nu_p(s,G)\\
&\qquad\le
 \frac1{\log X}
 \sum_{s\le H}\sum_{G\le2X}
 \log|\mathcal R_{s,G}|\\
&\qquad\ll
 \sum_{s\le H}\sum_{G\le2X}sG
 \ll H^2X^2
 \asymp X^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The desired dyadic scale is

\[
X^{2+o(1)}/\log X.
\]

Thus fixed-pair resultants lose essentially one full power of \(X\).  The
near-bridge proof succeeds only because both indices are at most
\(K\ll\sqrt X\); it does not give a uniform compression of the long index.
Likewise, the fixed-family Chebotarev proposition in
`FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex` explicitly keeps the family
\(U_1,\ldots,U_H\) fixed while \(p\to\infty\).  It cannot be interchanged
with \(G\) growing like a power of \(p\) without a new uniform theorem.

# 8. Why this is not a universal sheaf no-go

The direct-transfer obstruction has a strict logical boundary.
`oracleC_result.tex` proves that the Apéry number itself satisfies

\[
b_j\equiv
-\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}\Lambda(x,y,z)^j
\pmod p
\]

for one fixed Laurent polynomial \(\Lambda\).  The corresponding Kummer
objects have rank one and uniformly bounded total cohomology, even though
the direct transfer word producing \(b_j\) has degree growing linearly with
\(j\).  Therefore

> growing transfer degree rules out bounded complexity of the direct word;
> it does not rule out a different period/trace realization.

The same file also identifies two further obstacles which apply verbatim to
the proposed use here.

First, a bounded family indexed by the character \(j\) is not a single
sheaf on a \(j\)-line.  Deligne's pointwise bounds do not automatically give
a bilinear or large-sieve estimate as the character moves.

Second, the equality is integral only after reducing a cyclotomic Frobenius
trace modulo a prime above \(p\).  A trace may be nonzero in characteristic
zero while its reduction is zero modulo \(p\).  Archimedean or \(\ell\)-adic
Deligne bounds on the trace do not count those divisibility events.  An
integral vertical estimate is required.

For the gap problem one would need both indices of the Green kernel
(6.4), not merely the distinguished solution \(b_j\).  No fixed toric,
Kummer, or trace realization of \(V_m(a)\) with the required integral
compatibility is currently proved.

# 9. The minimal missing uniform statements

There are two precise ways to state what would make the geometric strategy
work.  Neither is merely a restatement of \(A_p^{\mathrm{nw}}\).

## 9.1 Finite-cover form on the long-return relation

Let

\[
\mathcal R_p
 =\{(u,G):0\le u<u+G<p,\ N_G(u)=0\}.
\]

A sufficient algebraic theorem is:

> **Uniform gap-kernel algebraization.**  For every \(\varepsilon>0\),
> there is an algebraic curve \(C_p\subset\mathbf A^2_{u,G}\), of total
> degree at most \(p^\varepsilon\), containing \(\mathcal R_p\), with no
> vertical component \(u=u_0\).  Equivalently, each vertical fiber has at
> most \(p^\varepsilon\) points.

The short-return mass satisfies, by the exact degree cap,

\[
\sum_{s=2}^{\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor}
\#\{x:0\le x<p-s,\ N_s(x)=0\}
\le3\sum_{s=2}^{\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor}(s-1)
\ll p.
\]

Each short pair ends at one \(u=x+s\).  The finite-cover statement would
therefore imply

\[
A_p^{\mathrm{nw}}(K)\ll p^{1+\varepsilon}
\]

uniformly in \(K\).  This is stronger than necessary but is a clean,
one-dimensional theorem about the recurrence index \(G\).

The direct transfer curve has vertical/output degree \(3(G-1)\), not
\(p^{o(1)}\) uniformly over \(G<p\), so it does not supply this statement.

## 9.2 Bounded-Betti joint incidence form

A more flexible Lang--Weil formulation is:

> **Uniform incidence complex.**  For every \(p\), there is a curve (or a
> bounded complex of curves) \(\mathcal C_p/\mathbf F_p\) and a map to
> \(\mathbf A^3_{x,s,G}\) whose image contains every nonwrapping incidence
> triple, whose point fibers have size \(p^{o(1)}\), and for which both the
> number of geometric components and the total compactly supported Betti
> number are \(p^{o(1)}\).

Deligne would then give

\[
\#\mathcal C_p(\mathbf F_p)
 \ll p^{o(1)}p+p^{o(1)}\sqrt p
 =p^{1+o(1)}.
\]

Since `far_bridge_incidence.tex` gives

\[
E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K)
 \le B_p^{[4]}(K)
 \le A_p^{\mathrm{nw}}(K),
\]

this would imply

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}E_p^{\mathrm{far}}(K)
 \ll
 \frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
\]

This is the exact dyadic bookkeeping: there are
\(O(X/\log X)\) primes in the interval, and each contributes
\(X^{1+o(1)}\).

In sheaf language, the corresponding missing theorem is not merely
"bounded conductor for each fixed \(G\)."  It is a **joint, integral,
moving-index trace theorem** for the normalized kernel \(V_m(a)\): a fixed
geometric realization compatible in both \(a\) and \(m\), plus a
large-sieve or divisibility estimate for the reductions of its Frobenius
matrix coefficients.  The rank-three differential compression (6.2) is a
natural starting object, but no comparison of this strength is currently
available.

# 10. Final classification

## Proved here

- the exact transfer entry
  \(N_G(u)=e_2^{\mathsf T}M_{u+G-1}\cdots M_{u+1}e_2\);
- an exact degree-\(7\), \(O(G)\)-variable path variety;
- exact output degree \(3(G-1)\) and determinant
  ​\(\prod_{j=2}^G(u+j)^6\);
- impossibility of a fixed-dimensional, bounded-degree ordinary algebraic
  family whose fibers are the natural \(N_G\)-divisors;
- an exact \(p\)-dependent three-variable interpolation of degree \(O(p)\);
- failure of black-box degree/conductor estimates at the square-root scale;
- the rank-three spectral generating equation (6.2), its fixed singular
  divisor, and the two-point kernel identity (6.4);
- the \(X^3\) ceiling obtained by summing the existing nonzero resultants in
  the far range.

## Not proved, and not implied by transfer matrices

- a fixed finite-type variety on an ordinary \(G\)-line encoding all gap
  returns;
- a bounded-conductor \(\ell\)-adic sheaf whose trace zeros are exactly the
  reductions \(N_G(u)=0\) jointly in \((u,G)\);
- a Lang--Weil/Deligne estimate with constants uniform for
  \(G\asymp\sqrt p\) or \(G\asymp p\);
- an integral moving-character large sieve converting a bounded toric or
  cohomological realization into a bound for mod-\(p\) vanishing.

Accordingly, the transfer-index algebraization route is **rigorously
blocked in its direct form**, but not universally refuted.  The smallest
honest remaining geometric target is a bounded-degree/Betti algebraization
of the two-point normalized kernel \(V_m(a)\), or an equivalent integral
large-sieve theorem for that kernel.  Without that new input,
Lang--Weil/Deligne cannot improve the current far-incidence bounds.
