## Result

A complete reduction of the reciprocal trace to a new nonzero unit factor was not obtained. The bounded candidate search below gives a precise no-go for the most direct reflected-half-row contiguous candidate.

The natural candidate was to set

\[
b_{m-t}=b_m u_t,
\]

and attempt to derive a first-order hypergeometric quotient for the reciprocal sum

\[
R=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{(m-j)^2-1}{b_j}
\]

from a finite-field contiguous relation of the form

\[
\frac{(m-j)^2-1}{b_j}=F(j+1)-F(j)
\]

with

\[
F(j)=\frac{P(j)b_{m-j}}{Q(j)b_j}
\]

where P,Q have degree at most 4. Clearing denominators produces an exact polynomial identity. Solving the coefficient equations over Q gives only the trivial solution.

Therefore this particular telescoping ansatz does not prove Sigma_p != 0.

## Exact bounded ansatz searched

The searched certificate was:

\[
F(j)=\frac{P_4(j)b_{m-j}}{Q_4(j)b_j},
\]

with

\[
P_4,Q_4\in\mathbf Q[j],\quad \deg P_4,\deg Q_4\le4.
\]

After substituting the Apéry recurrence and clearing all denominators, the coefficient matrix has full column rank after removing the unavoidable common scaling of P and Q. The nullspace consists only of the zero certificate.

No identity of this form telescopes the reciprocal trace.

## Why this is not a proof of impossibility

This excludes only the stated bounded rational contiguous certificate. It does not exclude:

* higher degree rational certificates;
* certificates involving derivatives of a spectral deformation;
* nonlocal finite-field hypergeometric identities;
* identities using the WZ seam term directly.

Hence the remaining lemma is strictly smaller:

> Find a nontrivial finite-field contiguous relation for the reciprocal Apéry trace that is not representable as a degree-4 reflected-row rational telescope.
