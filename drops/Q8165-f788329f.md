## 1. Lemma C as written: complete proof by isolation packing

Let `d_p=|I_p(H)|`, with either of the two conventions used in the project:

- `I_p(H)` consists of folded roots in `[0,(p-1)/2]`; or
- `I_p(H)` is unfolded back to physical residues in `[0,p-1]` after the folded isolation test.

The second convention is larger by at most a factor two, so I prove the bound for it.

### 1.1 Packing one prime

Let
\[
0\le s_1<s_2<\cdots<s_t\le \frac{p-1}{2}
\]
be the isolated folded roots. By definition,
\[
s_{j+1}-s_j>H.
\]
Hence
\[
(t-1)H<\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
 t\le 1+\frac{p-1}{2H}.
\]
After unfolding, each noncentral fold has two physical residues and the center has one. Therefore
\[
\boxed{d_p\le 2+\frac pH.}
\tag{1.1}
\]
If `I_p(H)` is already the folded bank, replace the right side by `1+p/(2H)`.

### 1.2 Total isolated-root mass in the dyadic block

Put
\[
D_X:=\sum_{X<p\le2X}d_p.
\]
Using the elementary Chebyshev bound `π(2X)-π(X) << X/log X` and `p<=2X`, (1.1) gives
\[
D_X
\ll \frac{X}{\log X}\left(1+\frac XH\right)
\ll \frac{X^2}{H\log X}.
\]
With
\[
H=X^{7/15}(\log X)^{-1/3},
\]
this is
\[
\boxed{
D_X\ll X^{23/15}(\log X)^{-2/3}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]
No conjectural statement such as “typically `d_p=2`” is used.

### 1.3 Number of five-stars

An ordered 5-star with distinct primes and one isolated root at each prime is a choice of five distinct elements from the disjoint union of all prime/root fibers. Therefore its number is at most
\[
D_X^5.
\]
(The distinct-prime restriction only decreases the count.) Thus
\[
\#\{\text{ordered 5-stars with isolated roots}\}
\ll X^{115/15}(\log X)^{-10/3}
=X^{23/3}(\log X)^{-10/3}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

### 1.4 The CRT error is bounded by one

For five primes `p_i>X`, put `P=∏p_i`. Then
\[
0<\delta:=\frac{X^2}{P}<X^{-3}<1.
\]
The error is
\[
E=\mathbf 1_{\{\operatorname{CRT}(b_1,\ldots,b_5)<X^2\}}-\delta,
\]
so in both cases `|E|<1`. Consequently
\[
\sum_{\text{5-stars}}E^2
\le \#\{\text{5-stars}\}
\ll X^{23/3}(\log X)^{-10/3}.
\tag{1.4}
\]
Since
\[
\frac{23}{3}=7.666\ldots<8,
\]
we obtain, for every `ε>0`,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\text{5-tuples, roots}}E^2
\ll_\varepsilon X^{8+\varepsilon}.}
\]
Indeed the stronger `O(X^{23/3})` bound holds.

This is a complete proof of **the displayed Lemma C**.

### 1.5 Consequence for the claimed final chain

If the already-proved theorem in the project literally has hypothesis (1.4), then the chain

\[
\text{Lemma C}\Rightarrow\mathrm{TR4}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I5}
\Rightarrow\max K^{\rm iso}\le X^{14/15+\eta}
\Rightarrow\log G_n=o(n)
\]
now has no remaining analytic input.

However, the stated `k=1` explanation shows that this is almost certainly **not** the quantity used by `TR4`. The next section isolates the mismatch.

## 2. Why `Σ E(σ)^2` has no `k=1` overlap term

Let `S_X` denote the set of 5-stars `σ`, and write `E_σ` for their individual CRT errors.

The displayed Lemma C is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{\sigma\in S_X}E_\sigma^2.}
\tag{2.1}
\]
This is a single sum. There are no pairs of distinct stars in (2.1), hence no overlap parameter `k`.

A `k=1` overlap appears when one first forms an aggregate discrepancy
\[
\mathcal D_X:=\sum_{\sigma\in S_X}E_\sigma
\]
and then squares:
\[
\mathcal D_X^2
=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_X}E_\sigma E_\tau.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Now one may decompose (2.2) according to
\[
k=|P(\sigma)\cap P(\tau)|,
\]
where `P(σ)` is the 5-element prime set of the star. The `k=1` term is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_1(X)
:=\sum_{\substack{\sigma,\tau\in S_X\\
|P(\sigma)\cap P(\tau)|=1}}
E_\sigma E_\tau.}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Two 5-stars sharing exactly one prime involve **nine distinct prime slots**. This is the source of the heuristic `X^9/log^9 X` raw count when one additionally assumes `O(1)` root choices per prime.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_\sigma E_\sigma^2\ \text{ is a diagonal (`k=5`) quantity, not the `k=1` covariance.}}
\tag{2.4}
\]

There is also a second rigor issue in the stated `O(X^9/log^9 X)` bound: “`|I_p|` is typically 2” is empirical, not a uniform theorem. From isolation alone the total root mass is only (1.2); a raw nine-root-slot count can be as large as `D_X^9`, not `(X/log X)^9`. Any proof using the latter count needs a proved average bound on `d_p`, or weights which compensate for it.

The current repository has already encountered precisely this kind of normalization issue. `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex` begins with two normalization corrections (the correct `λ=Σ Z(p)/p`, and multiplicity rather than an existential third-prime indicator) and explicitly warns that higher Palm statements must retain the exact factorial multiplicities. The same discipline is necessary here.

### Corrected candidate for the intended Lemma C

The prose in the question points to a statement of the following shape:

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal C_1(X)|\ll_\varepsilon X^{8+\varepsilon}.}
\tag{C1}
\]

Depending on the exact `TR4` expansion, the shared-prime root may be required to be the same in both stars, or may be summed independently inside one common prime fiber. That convention must be copied exactly from the proved `TR4` identity; it cannot be inferred from the displayed `ΣE^2` statement.

Everything below audits `(C1)` / the equivalent selector-preserving rank-four covariance, because **that** is the nontrivial interpretation which matches the `k=1` discussion.

## 3. First geometry audit: the relevant Apéry sheaf is not the asserted primitive `Sp_4` sheaf

The current project contains an exact geometric identification in

`problems/3.2/toric_fiber_sym2.tex`.

On the quadratic cover
\[
8x^2+(a-1)x+a=0,

the K3 trace is expressed through the level-6 elliptic family
\[
E_x:\quad Y^2+(1-2x)XY+x^2Y=X^3.
\]
The file identifies a rank-four Kummer/Asai summand, but the graph of the six-isogeny contributes a rational Tate line. Its orthogonal complement is the **generic rank-three transcendental piece** of the K3 surface. In split fibers the primitive trace is `a_p(E_x)^2-p` (plus the explicit special correction); in inert fibers it is an Asai trace minus the Tate line.

The fixed-exponent Mellin audit in

`problems/3.2/ORACLE_COMM/chatgpt_q32_fixed_exponent_mellin_attack.md`

therefore correctly treats the geometric system as a fixed rank-three middle-extension system, with symmetric-square / `SO_3`-type local-system monodromy. In the repository normalization its fixed singularities are `0`, `∞`, and the two roots of
\[
a^2-34a+1,
\]
not simply `0,1,∞` without an additional pullback/change of parameter.

This does **not** deny that terminating `{}_4F_3` expressions for `b_r` or other rank-four hypergeometric sheaves exist. It says that one cannot identify such an object with the primitive K3 trace sheaf used in the Mellin formula without proving the required correspondence. The variable roles are different.

The exact relation banked in the project is schematically
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M_p(\chi_{p,r})\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_{p,r}},}
\tag{3.1}
\]
where `M_p` is the multiplicative Mellin transform of the **geometric-parameter trace function** `a -> θ_p(a)` and `χ_{p,r}` is the Kummer character indexed by `r`.

Thus
\[
r\in Z_p
\iff
\mathfrak p_{p,r}\mid \mathcal M_p(\chi_{p,r}).
\tag{3.2}
\]
The zero bank is a set of **Mellin-coefficient indices** selected by a defining-prime divisibility condition. It is not the zero set of the pointwise K3 trace `θ_p(r)`.

This variable mismatch is the main reason the standard equidistribution theorems do not close `(C1)`.

## 4. (a) Katz / quantitative horizontal Mellin equidistribution

### 4.1 Katz 2012: what the theorem actually averages

Katz, *Convolution and Equidistribution: Sato--Tate Theorems for Finite-Field Mellin Transforms* (Annals of Mathematics Studies 180, 2012), develops the Tannakian convolution formalism on `G_m`. The horizontal theorem cited in the modern literature is Theorem 28.1.

Its structural output is Sato--Tate equidistribution of normalized Mellin-transform Frobenius classes as the multiplicative character `χ` ranges through the admissible character population. In the horizontal form the finite field may vary along a sequence satisfying the fixed geometric/Tannakian hypotheses.

It is **not** a theorem that, for an adversarially selected character `χ_p` in each field, the values at those selected characters are independent as the rational prime `p` varies.

### 4.2 The strongest current upgrade: Bah--Shuddhodan 2026

Bah--Shuddhodan, *Uniform stratified vanishing and equidistribution on `G_m^d`*, arXiv:2608.00304v2 (7 Aug. 2026), is the right current theorem to audit because it removes an older varying-characteristic objection.

Their setup is:

- `k_j` finite fields with `#k_j -> ∞` and characteristics allowed to vary (away from the fixed coefficient prime `ℓ`);
- perverse sheaves `M_{j,0}` on `G_m^d/k_j`, arithmetically semisimple and pure of weight zero;
- uniformly bounded Sawin complexity `c_u(M_j)`;
- fixed Tannakian dimension `r`;
- `G_j^ari=G_j^geo` for every `j`;
- all these groups conjugate to one fixed reductive subgroup `G`.

Corollary 1.4 then says that the unitary Frobenius conjugacy classes
\[
\Theta_{M_{j,0},k_j}(\chi),\qquad \chi\in X_j,
\]
for the unramified characters become Haar-equidistributed in the conjugacy classes of a maximal compact subgroup of `G`.

Theorems 1.1--1.3 give uniform bounds for the exceptional/cohomology-jump/unramified loci with constants depending only on the torus dimension and the Sawin-complexity bound. In dimension one the exceptional character set is correspondingly tiny (`O_C(1)` at the point-count level).

### 4.3 Why even this does not imply `(C1)`

There are four independent mismatches.

**(i) Full-character average versus a zero-selected sparse section.**  The theorem averages over `~p` characters in `F_p^×`. Our selected set is
\[
\{\chi_{p,r}:p\mid b_r\},
\]
and after isolation it is even sparser. Full-character equidistribution is logically compatible with one (or `p^{o(1)}`, or even a sufficiently sparse family of) exceptional selected characters in every field. A single selected character has mass `1/(p-1)` in the empirical measure.

**(ii) Complex/unitary Frobenius statistic versus defining-prime divisibility.**  The event (3.2) is `p`-adic/residual. The repository's Gross--Koblitz audit shows explicitly that an Apéry zero is cancellation among several slope-zero `p`-adic units. It need not be vanishing of the characteristic-zero / complex Mellin sum. Therefore a Sato--Tate law for the complex Frobenius conjugacy class does not detect the event.

**(iii) One field at a time versus nine different characteristics.**  Taking `d=9` in Bah--Shuddhodan does not create a torus over
\[
\mathbb F_{p_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb F_{p_9}.
\]
`G_m^9` in their theorem is a torus over **one finite field**. The nine prime characteristics in `(C1)` do not form such a field.

**(iv) The CRT threshold is not a Frobenius class condition.**  The inequality
\[
\operatorname{CRT}(b_1,\ldots,b_5)<X^2
\]
is an archimedean condition on the least integer representative of a residue vector modulo a product of rational primes. It is not a conjugacy-invariant algebraic condition on Frobenius in a fixed finite-field family.

### 4.4 Exponent ledger

There is therefore **no legal `X`-exponent substitution** from Katz/Bah--Shuddhodan into `(C1)`. The failure occurs before estimating an error term: the averaging variable and the observable are different.

Even a hypothetical `O(p^{-1/2})` rate for smooth test functions of the all-character Sato--Tate measure would not repair this. One would still need to represent the defining-prime zero indicator as a controlled test function of the complex Frobenius class and then control a deterministic sparse section. Neither step is supplied.

**Answer to (a): no.** The modern horizontal theorem verifies that varying characteristic is not itself fatal, but it does not give the required cross-characteristic defining-prime covariance.

## 5. (b) Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel trace-function product bounds and large sieve

There are two related but distinct results commonly conflated here.

### 5.1 Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel 2015: sums of products

Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *A study in sums of products*, arXiv:1405.2293, Philos. Trans. Roy. Soc. A 373 (2015), gives the clean complete-sum theorem.

Their Definition 1.2 calls a sheaf `F` on `A^1/F_p` **bountiful** if, in particular,

- it is a middle-extension sheaf, pointwise pure of weight zero, rank `r>=2`;
- its geometric monodromy is `SL_r` or `Sp_r`;
- its projective automorphism group `Aut_0(F)` is trivial.

Theorem 1.5 / Corollary 1.6 then gives square-root cancellation
\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}^{*}
\prod_{i=1}^k K(\gamma_i x)^{\sigma_i}
 e_p(hx)
\ll_{k,c(F)} p^{1/2},
\tag{5.1}
\]
under the stated normality conditions on the tuple of projective transformations (or when the additive twist `h` removes the diagonal obstruction). Proposition 1.1 gives the general cohomological criterion `H_c^2=0`, with the constant depending only on the number of factors and the conductors.

This is a powerful theorem, but its first line already fails for the desired product.

### 5.2 `g_q(a)` is not the K3 trace function

On an output chart, the factor is
\[
g_q(a)
=\mathbf1_{\{ap+r\bmod q\in I_q\}}-\mu_q.
\tag{5.2}
\]
The K3 sheaf supplies the geometric-parameter trace `θ_q(x)`; it does **not** supply (5.2) as a bounded-conductor trace function of `a`.

The map
\[
s\longmapsto \mathbf1_{\{q\mid b_s\}}
\]
is a nonlinear zero-indicator of the **Mellin coefficients** of `θ_q`, followed by the isolation filter. No theorem of Katz/FKM turns the zero set of a Mellin transform into a new bounded-complexity middle-extension trace function on the dual index line.

An arbitrary finite subset can be encoded sheaf-theoretically only by letting the complexity grow with the subset/field. For example, the literal additive Fourier representation of a point mass uses all `q` additive characters; a direct sum of skyscraper contributions scales with the support. Such complexity cannot be hidden in `X^{o(1)}` when `d_q` can be as large as the isolation packing bound.

### 5.3 Different characteristics do not type-check in Theorem 1.5

Even granting a bounded-conductor representation of each `g_q`, the four factors in
\[
\prod_{j=1}^4g_{q_j}(a)
\]
would be trace functions over four different fields `F_{q_j}`. The theorem (5.1) is a theorem over **one** `F_p`.

Putting the four congruence systems together by CRT gives a composite modulus
\[
Q_4=q_1q_2q_3q_4\asymp X^4.
\]
The physical chart has length `N~X`. Even in the fantasy situation where local complete sums had ideal square-root cancellation and factored perfectly, completion to modulus `Q_4` meets
\[
\sqrt{Q_4}\asymp X^2\gg N\asymp X.
\]
Thus generic CRT completion is worse than the trivial interval bound by a factor `X`.

### 5.4 Xi--Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel large-sieve inequalities

Ping Xi with Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *Large Sieve Inequalities for Algebraic Trace Functions*, IMRN 2017(16), 4840--4881, DOI `10.1093/imrn/rnw074`, develops iterative large-sieve inequalities using classical Fourier analysis together with ℓ-adic Fourier transform/convolution for general non-Kummer trace functions.

The input is still a family of algebraic trace functions in a **single finite-field/modulus geometry** with controlled sheaf/Fourier complexity. It does not provide a mixed-characteristic sheaf for (5.2), nor a large sieve for four defining characteristics on one integer diagonal.

The newer Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel--Sawin, *Bilinear forms with trace functions*, arXiv:2511.09459v3 (11 Mar. 2026), extends nontrivial bilinear ranges for one prime field under broad monodromy hypotheses and is stable under rank-one twists. It still has one base characteristic and two genuinely free bilinear variables; it does not create the missing four-characteristic diagonal cumulant.

### 5.5 The selector `w` is another independent obstruction

For the `U_{4,>D_X}` formulation the factor
\[
w(a)=\mathbf1_{\{1+\sum_q f_q(a)\ge L\}}
\]
is endogenous. Treating it as an arbitrary coefficient gives only `||w||_∞<=1` or `||w||_2<=N^{1/2}` and loses the threshold `L` completely. Since `e_4(g_q(a))` is signed, `w` cannot simply be dropped by monotonicity without destroying the centered cancellation.

**Answer to (b): no.** The obstruction is not lack of a sufficiently strong square-root theorem; it is that the required factors are not known bounded-conductor trace functions in one common finite field, and the common physical interval lies below the square-root scale of the CRT product modulus.

## 6. (c) Bombieri--Vinogradov type theorems

The classical Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem, in one standard form, says that for every `A>0` there is `B=B(A)` such that
\[
\sum_{q\le x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}}
\max_{(a,q)=1}
\left|\psi(x;q,a)-\frac{x}{\varphi(q)}\right|
\ll_A \frac{x}{(\log x)^A}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Its power comes from averaging a **fixed arithmetic sequence** over many arithmetic progressions whose moduli lie below a level of distribution.

The CRT problem here has the opposite geometry.

For one five-star,
\[
P_5=p_1\cdots p_5\asymp X^5,
\qquad M=X^2.
\]
Since `P_5 >> M`, each specified residue vector has at most one representative in `[0,M)`. This is an **occupancy-one** problem, not an arithmetic progression containing many terms.

For the `k=1` covariance of two 5-stars the union contains nine prime moduli,
\[
P_9\asymp X^9.
\]
There is no range remotely analogous to `q<=M^{1/2}=X`: the relevant composite moduli are `X^5` or `X^9`.

More importantly, the residues are not fixed classes independent of the modulus. They are the moving sets
\[
I_p(H)=\{r:p\mid b_r\ \text{and the fold of }r\text{ is }H\text{-isolated}\}.
\]
Thus a “Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem for Apéry zeros” strong enough for `(C1)` would itself be essentially the new theorem: a mean short-CRT discrepancy theorem for the defining-prime coefficient-zero banks.

Existing Bombieri--Vinogradov variants for trace functions/Kloosterman sums average explicit trace weights or automorphic coefficients in arithmetic progressions. They do not turn coefficient-zero banks into residue classes with a level of distribution beyond the occupancy-one barrier.

**Answer to (c): no known Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem has the right object or modulus regime.**

## 7. (d) Hypergeometric / Hasse-invariant zero-distribution results

### 7.1 Adolphson--Sperber

Adolphson--Sperber, *Hasse invariants and mod p solutions of A-hypergeometric systems*, arXiv:1209.2448, proves that Hasse invariants of families of exponential sums can be expressed using mod-`p` solutions of `A`-hypergeometric systems.

Adolphson--Sperber, *A-hypergeometric series and a p-adic refinement of the Hasse--Witt matrix*, arXiv:2001.07280, identifies unit roots of zeta functions with eigenvalues built from special values of a matrix `F(Λ^p)^{-1}F(Λ)` of `A`-hypergeometric series.

These are **geometric-parameter** statements: `Λ` is a parameter of a hypersurface/exponential-sum family and the theorem describes Frobenius/Hasse--Witt data as `Λ` varies or is specialized.

They do not give a theorem of the form
\[
\#\{r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}=\text{main term}+O(p^{1/2-\delta})
\]
or any multi-prime correlation for the coefficient indices `r`.

### 7.2 Katz hypergeometric sheaves

Katz's hypergeometric-sheaf theory gives bounded-conductor lisse sheaves and powerful equidistribution for their **trace values at points of a finite field**. Again, the bank event is (3.2): a zero of a Mellin coefficient at the defining prime. It is not an evaluation zero of the original hypergeometric/K3 trace function.

This is why “supersingular points of the K3 family are equidistributed” is not enough: it studies points of the parameter line, while `b_r=0` studies Fourier/Mellin coefficient indices.

### 7.3 The newest directly Apéry-specific algebraic result

Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin, *Galois Groups of Apéry-like Series Modulo Primes*, arXiv:2510.23298 (2025), computes Galois groups of the reductions modulo `p` of the **generating series** of the Apéry numbers (and related sequences), with behavior depending on congruence conditions on `p`.

This is genuine horizontal structure for the fixed Apéry `G`-function. But a Galois-group description of the algebraic power series over `F_p(x)` does not by itself control the support pattern of its coefficients, and the paper does not state a uniform coefficient-zero count or a cross-prime CRT discrepancy theorem of the required strength.

**Answer to (d): no.** The known hypergeometric/Hasse--Witt theorems control evaluation/Frobenius geometry, not the distribution of the defining-prime Mellin-coefficient zero set `Z_p` across coefficient indices and rational primes.

## 8. Large sieve for Frobenius: a rigorous defining-prime no-go

Perret-Gentil, *Exponential sums over finite fields and the large sieve*, arXiv:1703.06965; IMRN 2020, applies a variant of Kowalski's large sieve for Frobenius to coherent compatible systems. It produces zero-density estimates for trace values lying in prescribed algebraic subsets when residual monodromy is controlled.

There is a very simple reason this black box cannot detect (3.2).

Fix a base field of characteristic `p`. The compatible-system sieve reduces at auxiliary valuations `λ|ℓ` with
\[
\ell\ne p.
\]
Suppose the target algebraic integer `T` is required to satisfy
\[
T\in p\mathcal O_E.
\]
At every admissible auxiliary `λ|ℓ`, `p` is a unit in `O_E/λ`. Therefore
\[
p\mathcal O_E\pmod\lambda=\mathcal O_E/\lambda.
\]
So the condition “`p|T`” imposes **no restriction whatsoever** in every residual quotient available to the usual étale Frobenius sieve. Its local sieve density for this event is zero-information.

The only residual primes which see divisibility by `p` lie above `p` itself, precisely the coefficient characteristic excluded from the `ℓ`-adic sheaf on a base of characteristic `p`.

This is a theorem-level obstruction, not a missing optimization. Crystalline companions give a `p`-adic object at each fixed `p`, but existing companion theorems do not provide a horizontal large sieve coupling those defining-prime reductions as `p` varies.

## 9. What theorem would actually close the intended `k=1` step?

Once the normalization is corrected, the missing theorem is not “quantitative Katz equidistribution” in the ordinary sense. It must simultaneously have all of the following features:

1. **mixed characteristic:** the nine prime slots are nine different base characteristics;
2. **defining-prime:** it detects `p|b_r`, equivalently defining-prime divisibility of the Mellin coefficient;
3. **coefficient-index / dual-character variable:** it works in `r`, not only in the K3 parameter `a`;
4. **physical CRT diagonal:** it preserves the common least-integer representative condition `<X^2`;
5. **overlap-sensitive covariance:** it saves one full factor of `X` specifically in the one-shared-prime sector;
6. **actual Apéry banks:** it may use all-depth Apéry/G-function coherence and need not hold for arbitrary sparse residue sets;
7. **isolation-compatible:** it permits the nonlinear `H`-isolation filter without replacing the bank by a random model.

A literal corrected statement is `(C1)` above. In chart/Hoeffding language the corresponding reusable target is the selector-preserving fourth centered cumulant
