# Q565 — Can averaged AKD₃ replace pointwise AKD₃?

## Summary

The conclusion is: **yes, the proof architecture can probably be weakened, but not all the way to an arbitrary L² estimate.** The useful replacement is a weighted second moment or a mixed moment estimate adapted to the third-prime sum. A bare Cauchy–Schwarz conversion from L² to L¹ loses too much.

The key distinction is:

- Q554's near-injection gives a true geometric Parseval bound after averaging over the Fourier phase.
- Fixed Apéry phases remove that averaging.
- The remaining problem is not the size of individual correlations, but controlling their collective contribution in the factorial-moment expansion.

## (A) Second-moment AKD₃

Suppose

\[
\sum_{p\ne p'} |C(p,p')|^2 \ll \pi(X)^2P^2.
\]

The desired estimate is

\[
\sum_{p\ne p'} |C(p,p')|\ll \pi(X)^2P.
\]

Cauchy gives

\[
\sum |C(p,p')|
\leq \pi(X)\left(\sum |C(p,p')|^2\right)^{1/2}
\ll \pi(X)^2P.
\]

Carefully counting the number of prime pairs, there is **no extra \(\sqrt{\pi(X)}\) loss** if the proposed normalization is exactly the one above. The loss appears only if the second moment is normalized incorrectly.

Therefore a genuine pairwise second moment is essentially as good as AKD₃.

The real question is whether Q554's argument can prove this fixed-phase second moment.

## (B) Averaging over the third prime

This is more promising than pointwise AKD₃.

In the factorial moment expansion, the third prime is not an arbitrary external variable. It appears through another Fourier factor. Instead of bounding

\[
|C(p,p')|
\]

uniformly, one can attempt to bound

\[
\sum_{p''}|C(p,p')D(p'')|.
\]

The natural object is then a triple correlation. The CRT map

\[
(q,r,s)\mapsto(qrs)^{-1}\pmod{pp'p''}
\]

has much more expansion than the semiprime map. This suggests that a direct third-moment dispersion argument may be easier than proving all pair correlations individually.

## (C) Conditioning on M1/M2

This is likely the weakest sufficient target.

M1 controls the expected number of zero primes. M2 controls pair collisions at a single prime. The off-diagonal AKD₃ term only needs to be smaller than the natural third factorial moment scale.

Therefore the required statement may be

\[
\text{off-diagonal}=o(X^2\lambda^3)
\]

rather than the absolute AKD₃ normalization.

This suggests replacing AKD₃ by a conditional decorrelation estimate after removing the exceptional primes with unusually large \(|Z_p|\).

## (D) Direct CRT cancellation

This is the most attractive alternative.

The M1+M2+AKD₃ decomposition isolates correlations, but the original CRT counting problem has a larger symmetry. The triple product map may have enough multiplicity control that one can apply Parseval directly to the complete expression.

The obstacle is that the Fourier coefficients are not random phases. They are fixed traces of the Apéry recurrence. A direct proof would need either:

1. a uniform Fourier L² statement for these trace functions, or
2. a replacement for phase averaging using monodromy/equidistribution.

## (E) Can Q554 near-injection alone prove second moment AKD₃?

Probably **not by itself**.

The near-injection proves the combinatorial statement:

\[
(q,r)\mapsto(qr)^{-1}\pmod{pp'}
\]

has bounded multiplicity.

This is enough when the coefficients are averaged over all phases because Parseval removes the coefficients. For fixed Apéry coefficients one needs an additional input:

\[
\sum_a |\widehat Z_p(a)|^2 \text{ behaves generically}
\]

or a correlation bound between the Apéry Fourier vectors for different primes.

The missing ingredient is therefore not more geometry of CRT; it is a fixed-coefficient Fourier pseudorandomness theorem.

## Recommended replacement target

The most realistic hierarchy is:

1. Prove Q554 phase-averaged RMS bound (done).
2. Prove fixed-phase second moment:

\[
\sum_{p,p'}|C(p,p')|^2\ll \pi(X)^2P^2.
\]

3. Insert this directly into the factorial-moment argument.

This avoids proving pointwise AKD₃ and avoids a full automorphic BV theorem.

## Relation to automorphic input

An Apéry BV theorem is not currently known in the required form. Existing modularity results concern the underlying differential motive/modular form, but the zero detector

\[
1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}
\]

is nonlinear and does not directly correspond to a standard automorphic coefficient sequence.

The minimal new theorem needed is therefore not a full BV theorem, but a **fixed-phase bilinear Fourier estimate for Apéry trace functions**.

This is substantially weaker and aligns with the Q554 near-injection mechanism.
