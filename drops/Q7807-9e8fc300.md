# Apéry ratio energy: exact Sidon data and the \(E_2\)-weighted completion

## Verdict

There are three clean conclusions.

1. **The proposed “minimum \(z^3\)” normalization is not the Sidon minimum.** For a set \(Z\subset\mathbb F_p^\times\) of size \(z\), the multiplicative-Sidon floor is
   \[
   \boxed{E_\times(Z)\ge 2z^2-z,}
   \]
   provided there are enough nonidentity ratios for equality to be possible. The value \(z^3\) is instead the energy of a multiplicative subgroup (or a coset), not the minimum.

   In the exact scan through \(p\le1000\), there are **68 primes with \(Z(p)\ge2\)**, and every one of them attains
   \[
   \boxed{E_\times(Z_p)=2Z(p)^2-Z(p).}
   \]
   Equivalently, every ordered off-diagonal ratio \(k/k'\), \(k\ne k'\), is distinct. This includes all nine \(z=4\) examples and both \(z=6\) examples; it is not merely the automatic two-point phenomenon.

2. The \(W_p\) of Q7798 is not intrinsic to one zero set: it depends on the dyadic prime block and on \(L=X^2\). I computed each \(p\le500\) in its unique power-of-two block
   \[
   X<p\le2X,
   \qquad L=X^2.
   \]
   For the mature blocks the exact Q7798 centered trivial-character contraction is tiny compared with the diagonal scale:
   \[
   \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
   X&\#\{p\le500:Z(p)\ge2\}&
   \displaystyle\frac{\sum_p|C_p|}{\sum_pD_p}&
   \displaystyle\frac{\sum_pC_p}{\sum_pD_p}\\ \hline
   128&13&0.00619799&-0.000730519\\
   256&16&0.00447977&\phantom{-}0.00243890
   \end{array}
   \]
   Here
   \[
   C_p=\sum_{a\ne a'}W_p(a,a')
       \bigl(T_p(a'/a)-\tau_p^\times\bigr),
   \qquad
   D_p=T_p(1)\operatorname{tr}W_p.
   \]
   Thus the sum-of-absolute-values version of the Q7798 \((\mathrm{ARD}_{E_2})\) ratio is already below \(0.7\%\) in those blocks. The signs are mixed, so this statistic is not identically the negative Palm excess reported by DeepSeek; it is a different completed Fourier mode.

3. **Squarefreeness and root coprimality of the Hasse square root \(B_p(t)\) give no nontrivial bound on this index energy.** They concern algebraic roots of \(B_p\), while \(Z_p\) consists of zero **coefficient indices** of
   \[
   A_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_mt^m
   =\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
   \qquad \Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1.
   \]
   The exact zero-index polynomial does encode the energy, but only tautologically through dilation gcds. There is no resultant or discriminant transfer from the stated root conditions to a useful energy estimate.

All statements labelled “data” below are finite computations, not asymptotic inferences.

# 1. Correct energy normalization

For \(h\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), put

\[
M_p(h)=\#\{(x,y)\in Z_p^2:x=hy\}.
\]

Since \(0\notin Z_p\), the multiplicative energy may be written exactly as

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_\times(Z_p)
&=\#\{(a,b,c,d)\in Z_p^4:ab=cd\}\\
&=\sum_{h\in\mathbb F_p^\times}M_p(h)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The identity follows by grouping a solution \(ab=cd\) according to
\(h=a/c=d/b\).

Now

\[
M_p(1)=z,
\qquad
\sum_{h\ne1}M_p(h)=z(z-1).
\]

If every off-diagonal ordered pair has a distinct ratio, then each nonzero
\(M_p(h)\), \(h\ne1\), equals \(1\), and hence

\[
E_\times(Z_p)=z^2+z(z-1)=2z^2-z.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Conversely, equality in (1.2) forces all off-diagonal ratio multiplicities to be \(1\). Thus this is exactly the multiplicative-Sidon condition.

For comparison:

| \(z\) | Sidon floor \(2z^2-z\) | \(z^3\) | observed energy |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 6 | 8 | 6 |
| 4 | 28 | 64 | 28 |
| 6 | 66 | 216 | 66 |

So the Apéry data are not merely below \(z^3\); they attain the true minimum.

\[

\]

using only unit denominators \(1,\ldots,p-1\). For every prime it also checked:

- \(b_{p-1}=1\);

# 3. Exact \(E_2\)-weighted \(W_p\) computation

## 3.1 Why a dyadic normalization is necessary

In Q7798, \(W_p\) is constructed from the other primes in

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{q:X<q\le2X\},
\qquad L=X^2.
\]

It is therefore not meaningful to ask for \(W_p\) without specifying \(X\). For the table below I used the canonical finite convention:

> For each prime \(p\le500\), choose the unique power of two \(X\) satisfying \(X<p\le2X\), and use the complete Q7798 block \(\mathcal P_X\) with \(L=X^2\).

This puts \(p=257,\ldots,499\), for example, in the \(X=256\) computation.

## 3.2 Exact physical-space inversion of the completion coefficient

Q7798 defines, for \(a,u\in\mathbb F_p^\times\),

\[
\beta_{p,a}(u)
=\sum_{\substack{q,\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p\}\\q\ne\ell\\
(q\ell)^{-1}=u\bmod p}}
\frac1{pq\ell}
\sum_{\substack{k\bmod pq\ell\\k=a\bmod p}}
D_{L,pq\ell}(k)
F_q\!\left(k(p\ell)^{-1}\right)
F_\ell\!\left(k(pq)^{-1}\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Expanding the two auxiliary Fourier factors and applying CRT orthogonality gives a much faster but still exact formula. Define

\[
H_{p,u}(m)
=\#\left\{
(q,\ell):
\begin{array}{l}
q,\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p\},\ q\ne\ell,\\
m\bmod q\in Z_q,\ m\bmod\ell\in Z_\ell,\\
(q\ell)^{-1}=u\bmod p
\end{array}
\right\},
\tag{3.2}
\]

where \((q,\ell)\) is ordered. Then

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{p,a}(u)
=\frac1p\sum_{0\le m<L}H_{p,u}(m)e_p(-aum).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus all \(eta_{p,a}(u)\) are obtained by one length-\(p\) DFT for each \(u\), after binning the exact two-prime intersections by \(m\bmod p\). No loop over \(k\bmod pq\ell\) is needed.

The Q7798 Gram matrix is then

\[
\boxed{
W_p(a,a')
=\frac1{p-1}\sum_{u\ne0}
\beta_{p,a}(u)\overline{\beta_{p,a'}(u)}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The computation formed every entry of this \((p-1)\times(p-1)\) positive-semidefinite matrix.

For the ratio kernel, put

\[
T_p(h)=pM_p(h)-z^2,
\qquad
\tau_p^\times
=p\frac{z(z-1)}{p-2}-z^2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The centered off-diagonal contraction and diagonal scale are

\[
C_p
=\sum_{a\ne a'}W_p(a,a')
 \bigl(T_p(a'/a)-\tau_p^\times\bigr),
\tag{3.6}
\]

\[
D_p
=T_p(1)\sum_aW_p(a,a)
=(pz-z^2)\operatorname{tr}W_p.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The per-prime comparison below is \(C_p/D_p\). The Q7798 sum-of-absolute-values gate uses \(\sum_p|C_p|/\sum_pD_p\), not the sum of the individual ratios.

# 4. What squarefree \(B_p\) does—and does not—say

## 4.1 Root geometry versus coefficient-index geometry

The repository identity is

\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_mt^m
=\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Squarefreeness of \(B_p\) and

\[
\gcd(B_p,t\Delta)=1
\tag{4.2}
\]

say that the algebraic roots of \(B_p\) are simple and avoid \(0\) and the two singular roots of \(\Delta\). They do not say that the coefficient convolution in \(B_p^2\), or in \(\Delta B_p^2\), cannot cancel at a multiplicatively structured collection of indices.

At most, (4.2) gives trivial endpoint information such as a nonzero constant term. It does not control ratios among the zero coefficient indices.

## 4.2 Exact zero-index polynomial encoding

Define the polynomial whose roots really are the zero indices:

\[
Q_p(X)=\prod_{r\in Z_p}(X-r)\in\mathbb F_p[X].
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because \(Z_p\) is a set and \(0\notin Z_p\), \(Q_p\) is squarefree and coprime to \(X\). For every \(h\in\mathbb F_p^\times\),

\[
\boxed{
M_p(h)=\deg\gcd\bigl(Q_p(X),Q_p(hX)\bigr).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed, a common root \(y\) is exactly an index with \(y\in Z_p\) and \(hy\in Z_p\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
E_\times(Z_p)
=\sum_{h\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
\deg\gcd\bigl(Q_p(X),Q_p(hX)\bigr)^2.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is a correct algebraic encoding, but it is tautological. Constructing \(Q_p\) has already required deciding which coefficients of \(A_p\) vanish. No known identity transfers squarefreeness of \(B_p\) to small dilation gcds of \(Q_p\).

## 4.3 A degree-matched counterexample to any root-only implication

The failure is structural, not merely a missing proof. Fix \(arepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) and put

\[
d=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

For generic \(c\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), set

\[
B(t)=1+ct^d.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Then:

- \(p\nmid d\), so \(B\) is squarefree;
- \(B(0)=1\), so \(\gcd(B,t)=1\);
- excluding at most two choices of \(c\) makes \(\gcd(B,\Delta)=1\);
- the degree is matched so that
  \[
  \deg\bigl(\Delta^\varepsilon B^2\bigr)=p-1.
  \]

But \(B^2\) has only the three possible exponents \(0,d,2d\), and multiplication by \(\Delta\) creates at most nine supported exponents. Thus the zero-coefficient index set of

\[
A(t)=\Delta(t)^\varepsilon B(t)^2
\]

has size at least \(p-9\). By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[
E_\times(Z(A))
\ge \frac{|Z(A)|^4}{p-1}
\asymp p^3,
\tag{4.7}
\]

far above the Sidon scale \(O(p^2)\).

Therefore even the **exact degree**, squarefreeness, and coprimality to \(t\Delta\) permit extremely high zero-index ratio energy. Any theorem for the actual Apéry \(Z_p\) must use more than the root geometry of \(B_p\): it must exploit the special coefficient recurrence, Cartier structure, or another genuinely coefficient-sensitive identity.

# 6. Theorem/no-go ledger

### Exact identities

- \(E_\times(Z)=\sum_hM(h)^2\).
- The Sidon floor is \(2z^2-z\), not \(z^3\).
- Q7798’s completion coefficient has the physical-space formula (3.3).
- \(W_p\) is the Gram matrix (3.4).
- The zero-index polynomial encodes energy through (4.4)–(4.5).

### Finite data only

- All 68 Apéry sets with \(Z(p)\ge2\), \(p\le1000\), are multiplicatively Sidon.
- The \(E_2\)-weighted absolute centered ratio is \(0.00620\) at \(X=128\) and \(0.00448\) at \(X=256\).
- No asymptotic conclusion is inferred from these scans.

### Rigorous scoped no-go

- Squarefreeness and coprimality of \(B_p\) to \(t\Delta\), even with the correct degree, do not imply a useful bound on multiplicative energy of the zero coefficient indices.
- Unweighted local Sidonicity cannot by itself prove \((PP3)\), because the anchored two-point star is locally Sidon at every prime.
- The relevant next theorem remains the **weighted, cross-prime** ratio-dispersion statement \((\mathrm{ARD}_{E_2})\), together with control of the nontrivial multiplicative-character modes in Q7798’s full completion identity.
