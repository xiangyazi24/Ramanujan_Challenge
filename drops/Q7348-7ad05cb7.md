# Q7348 — Weakest horizontal upper-state fibre theorem for quotient-six C4

## Verdict

At commit `a955d03d26f2a14c3a35fa8386c96554be44e226`, the state-adaptive threshold in the marked-incidence owner is algebraically correct, after making five points literal:

1. on the plus branch the marked upper index must be reflected from `v` to
   \[
   y=q-1-v;
   \]
2. the state must retain the branch/orientation tag, or the two branches must be estimated as separate strata;
3. a lower condition `p | b_n` is the vanishing of a **coefficient convolution** of the canonical Hasse–Franel square root `B_p`, not the vanishing of the raw coefficient of `B_p`;
4. every branch gate and every index window must remain in the fibre definition;
5. the horizontal statistic must count distinct lower primes inside one actual occupied upper state. A resultant across the two characteristics `p` and `q` is neither needed nor legitimate.

Let
\[
L=\log T,
\qquad
\mathfrak B=\frac{T^2}{L^2}.
\]
The total weighted C4 contribution is `o_J(T^2/L)` exactly when the unweighted number of marked incidences is `o_J(\mathfrak B)`.

For an actual occupied branch-tagged upper state `s=(\sigma,q,y)`, let `f_s` be the number of admissible lower primes `p=q-d`. Put
\[
U=\#\{s:f_s>0\},
\qquad
F_\vee=\sum_{s:f_s>0}(f_s)_2
       =\sum_s f_s(f_s-1).
\]
Then
\[
\sum_s f_s\le U+\sqrt{UF_\vee}.
\]
Consequently the exact state-adaptive second-moment repair is
\[
\boxed{
F_\vee=o_J\!\left(\frac{\mathfrak B^2}{U}\right)
=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^4}{U\log^4T}\right).
}
\tag{USF2}
\]
The current upper-zero bound gives
\[
U\ll \frac{T^{8/5}}L,
\]
so the uniform worst-case specialization is
\[
\boxed{F_\vee=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-3}).}
\tag{USF2-worst}
\]
If each pair is weighted by `log p_1 log p_2`, the equivalent thresholds are
\[
\boxed{
F_\vee^{\log}
=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^4}{U L^2}\right),
\qquad
F_\vee^{\log}=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-1})
\text{ under }U\ll T^{8/5}/L.
}
\tag{USF2-log}
\]

The logically weakest fibre statement is the first-moment average
\[
\frac1U\sum_s f_s=o_J\!\left(\frac{\mathfrak B}{U}\right),
\tag{USF1}
\]
but this is merely the target regrouped by upper state. The weakest clean **noncircular horizontal theorem accessible to collision methods** is therefore `(USF2)`, or, still more weakly, its stratum-adaptive version
\[
\boxed{
\sum_\nu\sqrt{U_\nu F_\nu}=o_J(\mathfrak B).
}
\tag{USF2-strat}
\]
Here the occupied states may be partitioned by branch, `q`-scale, window position, load, or any other deterministic data. In particular
\[
\sqrt{U_-F_-}+\sqrt{U_+F_+}=o_J(\mathfrak B)
\]
is weaker than first pooling the branches and charging artificial cross-branch pairs.

A bounded fibre is far stronger than required. With the current `U` bound, a uniform estimate
\[
f_s\ll T^\delta L^\lambda
\]
closes whenever
\[
\delta<\frac25,
\]
and at the endpoint `delta=2/5` it needs
\[
\lambda<-1,
\]
or precisely `f_s=o(T^{2/5}/L)`. The average weighted fibre threshold at the current worst-case `U` is only
\[
\frac1U\sum_s\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_s}\log p=o_J(T^{2/5}).
\]

Vertical improvement in `Z(p)` can close C4 directly if it is much stronger than what is known: the exact lower-pair injection gives
\[
N_\vee\le 2\sum_{p\in I}Z(p)(Z(p)-1).
\]
Thus the vertical second-mass condition
\[
\sum_{p\in I}Z(p)^2=o(T^2/L^2)
\]
is sufficient. A pointwise bound `Z(p)\ll T^\alpha L^{-\sigma}` closes for `alpha<1/2`; at `alpha=1/2` it needs `sigma>1/2`. But vertical cardinalities do not imply the horizontal distribution theorem `(USF2)` at the natural scale. They contain no information about whether many different lower characteristics land on the same marked upper zero. Only vertical bounds so strong that the entire lower-pair inventory is already subcritical can bypass this missing horizontal input.

There is therefore no fatal mismatch in the proposed reparameterization after reflecting the plus upper zero and using the convolution functional below. The first exact failed implication is instead
\[
[X^n]\,\Delta(X)^{\epsilon_p}B_p(X)^2=0
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
[X^n]B_p(X)=0.
\]
The Hasse–Franel factorization provides exact coordinates for the fibres, but no horizontal anti-concentration across the varying polynomials `B_p`.

## 1. Canonical Hasse–Franel coefficient functional

For every odd prime `ell>13`, put
\[
A_\ell(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}b_nX^n\in\mathbb F_\ell[X].
\]
The factorization in `problems/3.2/hasse_franel_descent.tex` has the canonical normalized form
\[
A_\ell(X)=\Delta(X)^{\epsilon_\ell}B_\ell(X)^2,
\qquad
\Delta(X)=1-34X+X^2,
\tag{1}
\]
where `epsilon_ell` is `0` or `1`, and the sign of the square root is fixed by
\[
B_\ell(0)=1.
\]
Write
\[
B_\ell(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m_\ell}\beta_{\ell,j}X^j,
\]
and extend `beta_{ell,j}` by zero outside `[0,m_ell]`. Define the canonical quadratic convolution
\[
K_\ell(n)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
\beta_{\ell,j}\beta_{\ell,n-j}.
\tag{2}
\]
The coefficient functional detecting the Apéry zero is
\[
\Phi_\ell(n)
=[X^n]\Delta(X)^{\epsilon_\ell}B_\ell(X)^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Explicitly,
\[
\Phi_\ell(n)=
\begin{cases}
K_\ell(n),&\epsilon_\ell=0,\\[2mm]
K_\ell(n)-34K_\ell(n-1)+K_\ell(n-2),&\epsilon_\ell=1.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
For every `0<=n<ell`,
\[
\Phi_\ell(n)=b_n\pmod\ell.
\tag{5}
\]
Hence
\[
\ell\mid b_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi_\ell(n)=0
\qquad(0\le n<\ell).
\tag{6}
\]

This is the correct use of `B_ell`. The raw coefficient `beta_{ell,n}` is not the detector. Even in the square case `epsilon_ell=0`, for an index in the initial range one has schematically
\[
K_\ell(n)
=2\beta_{\ell,n}
 +\sum_{1\le j\le n-1}
  \beta_{\ell,j}\beta_{\ell,n-j},
\tag{7}
\]
so `K_ell(n)=0` can be a genuine convolution cancellation. The normalization `B_ell(0)=1` removes the sign ambiguity, and the convolution itself is sign-invariant in any event.

## 2. Literal minus branch

The minus branch starts with
\[
d=a+t+1,
\qquad
p=q-d,
\qquad
r=7t+6a+6,
\]
with the literal gates
\[
\begin{gathered}
q>13\text{ prime},\quad q\mid b_t,\quad
p\in I=(T/7,T/3],\\
d>T/J,\quad a,t\ge0,\quad r<p,
\quad T<6p+r\le2T.
\end{gathered}
\tag{8}
\]
Set the upper-state coordinate
\[
y=t.
\]
Then
\[
a=d-y-1,
\qquad
r=y+6d,
\tag{9}
\]
and the archimedean window becomes exactly
\[
6p+r=6(q-d)+y+6d=6q+y.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus every minus incidence lies over an upper zero satisfying
\[
q\mid b_y,
\qquad
T<6q+y\le2T.
\tag{11}
\]
For fixed `(q,y)`, put `d=q-p` and define
\[
i_-(p;q,y)=q-p-y-1,
\qquad
j_-(p;q,y)=y+6q-6p.
\tag{12}
\]
The exact minus fibre is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_-(q,y)=\{p:\;&p,q\text{ prime},\ p\in I,
\ d=q-p>T/J,\\
&i_-(p;q,y)\ge0,
\quad 0\le j_-(p;q,y)<p,\\
&\Phi_p(i_-(p;q,y))=0,
\quad\Phi_p(j_-(p;q,y))=0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The inequalities in (13) are exactly `a>=0` and `r<p`. They also ensure both lower indices are in `[0,p-1]`, so (6) is being used inside its valid coefficient range.

The inverse map is literal:
\[
t=y,
\qquad
a=i_-(p;q,y),
\qquad r=j_-(p;q,y),
\qquad d=q-p.
\tag{14}
\]
No occurrence or mark is lost.

The two lower zeros give the gap-polynomial consequence
\[
p\mid N_{g_-}(i_-),
\qquad
g_-=j_--i_-=5d+2y+1.
\tag{15}
\]
For fixed `(q,y)`, however, both `g_-` and `i_-` vary with `p`; (15) is not a fixed-polynomial divisor problem in `p`.

## 3. Literal plus branch and the necessary reflection

The plus branch starts with
\[
a=6d-1-z,
\qquad
v=z+7d,
\qquad
p=q-d,
\]
with
\[
\begin{gathered}
q>13\text{ prime},\quad q\mid b_v,\quad
p\in I,\quad d>T/J,\quad d\equiv0\pmod2,\\
z,a\ge0,\quad q>v,
\quad T<7q-1-v\le2T.
\end{gathered}
\tag{16}
\]
If one declares `v` itself to be the common upper coordinate, the two branches do **not** have the same window. The required correction is to reflect the upper zero modulo `q`:
\[
y=q-1-v.
\tag{17}
\]
Since `q>v`, one has `0<=y<q`; by the Apéry palindrome,
\[
q\mid b_v
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q\mid b_y.
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover
\[
7q-1-v=6q+y,
\tag{19}
\]
so the upper window is again precisely
\[
T<6q+y\le2T.
\tag{20}
\]

With `d=q-p`, solve the original variables from `(q,y,p)`:
\[
\begin{aligned}
z&=v-7d=q-1-y-7d
   =p-6d-y-1\\
 &=7p-6q-y-1,\\[1mm]
a&=6d-1-z
   =12d+y-p\\
 &=12q-13p+y.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Define
\[
i_+(p;q,y)=7p-6q-y-1,
\qquad
j_+(p;q,y)=12q-13p+y.
\tag{22}
\]
The exact plus fibre is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_+(q,y)=\{p:\;&p,q\text{ prime},\ p\in I,
\ d=q-p>T/J,
\ d\equiv0\pmod2,\\
&i_+(p;q,y)\ge0,
\quad j_+(p;q,y)\ge0,\\
&\Phi_p(i_+(p;q,y))=0,
\quad\Phi_p(j_+(p;q,y))=0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
For odd primes `p,q`, the parity gate is automatic because `d=q-p` is even, but it has been retained to make (23) literally equivalent to the owner.

The lower indices satisfy
\[
i_++j_+=6d-1.
\tag{24}
\]
The inequality `i_+>=0` gives `p>6d`, and hence
\[
0\le i_+,j_+<p.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus the coefficient tests in (23) again lie in the valid Hasse range. The inverse is
\[
z=i_+,
\qquad a=j_+,
\qquad v=q-1-y,
\qquad d=q-p.
\tag{26}
\]

Reflecting the second lower zero modulo `p` gives
\[
p-1-a=z+h,
\qquad
h=p-6d=7p-6q,
\tag{27}
\]
so the plus pair can equivalently be written
\[
p\mid b_z,b_{z+h},
\qquad
p\mid N_h(z).
\tag{28}
\]
Again, for fixed `(q,y)` both `h` and `z` vary with `p`.

\mathscr Y_T=

## 5. Exact occurrence identity

For `sigma in {-,+}`, put
\[
f_\sigma(q,y)=\#\mathcal P_\sigma(q,y),
\qquad
w_\sigma(q,y)=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_\sigma(q,y)}\log p.
\tag{32}
\]
Let
\[
\mathscr S^\circ=
\{(\sigma,q,y):(q,y)\in\mathscr Y_T,\ f_\sigma(q,y)>0\},
\qquad
U=|\mathscr S^\circ|.
\tag{33}
\]
The branch tag is part of the state. This is not cosmetic: pooling `f_-+f_+` creates cross-branch collision terms that are irrelevant to either original sum and makes the proposed horizontal theorem strictly stronger.

The maps in (14) and (26) prove a bijection between:

* literal marked C4 prime occurrences in the two radicals; and
* triples `(sigma,q,y,p)` with `(sigma,q,y)` in `S^circ` and `p` in `P_sigma(q,y)`.

Therefore, exactly,
\[
N_\vee=
\sum_{(\sigma,q,y)\in\mathscr S^\circ}f_\sigma(q,y),
\tag{34}
\]
and
\[
C^-(T,J)+C^+(T,J)=
\sum_{(\sigma,q,y)\in\mathscr S^\circ}w_\sigma(q,y).
\tag{35}
\]
Because `p in (T/7,T/3]`,
\[
(L-\log7)N_\vee
\le C^-+C^+
\le(L-\log3)N_\vee.
\tag{36}
\]
Hence
\[
C^-+C^+=o_J(T^2/L)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N_\vee=o_J(T^2/L^2)
=o_J(\mathfrak B).
\tag{37}
\]

## 6. Occupied-state bound

Every occupied branch-tagged state projects to an actual upper zero `(q,y)`. Consequently
\[
U\le2\sum_{q\in I}Z(q).
\tag{38}
\]
The current banked average bound is
\[
\sum_{q\in I}Z(q)\ll \frac{T^{8/5}}L,
\tag{39}
\]
so
\[
U\ll U_0:=\frac{T^{8/5}}L.
\tag{40}
\]
Relative to the incidence budget,
\[
\frac{U_0}{\mathfrak B}
\ll \frac{L}{T^{2/5}}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{41}
\]
Thus the occupied states themselves are already negligible. Only repeat occupancy of the same upper state remains.

The word **occupied** is essential. If `U` is replaced by all potential pairs `(q,y)` in the geometric window, the resulting threshold is needlessly stronger and loses the state-adaptive gain introduced at commit `a955d03d...`.

## 7. State-adaptive collision theorem

For a state `s=(sigma,q,y)`, abbreviate `f_s=f_sigma(q,y)` and define the ordered off-diagonal fibre energy
\[
F_\vee=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S^\circ}(f_s)_2
      =\sum_s f_s(f_s-1).
\tag{42}
\]
Then
\[
\sum_s f_s^2=N_\vee+F_\vee.
\tag{43}
\]
Cauchy gives
\[
N_\vee^2
\le U(N_\vee+F_\vee).
\tag{44}
\]
Solving the quadratic inequality,
\[
N_\vee
\le\frac{U+\sqrt{U^2+4UF_\vee}}2
\le U+\sqrt{UF_\vee}.
\tag{45}
\]
Because `U=o(mathfrak B)`, condition `(USF2)` implies
\[
N_\vee=o_J(\mathfrak B),
\]
and hence the weighted target by (37).

This proves the state-adaptive threshold, including the exact logs:
\[
\frac{\mathfrak B^2}{U}
=\frac{T^4}{U L^4}.
\tag{46}
\]
Substituting only the worst-case bound (40) gives
\[
\frac{T^4}{U_0L^4}
=T^{12/5}L^{-3}.
\tag{47}
\]
If the actual number of occupied states is smaller than `U_0`, the admissible collision energy increases by the factor `U_0/U`. This is the precise state-adaptive refinement.

### Weighted form

Put
\[
F_\vee^{\log}
=\sum_s
 \sum_{\substack{p_1,p_2\in\mathcal P_s\\p_1\ne p_2}}
 (\log p_1)(\log p_2).
\tag{48}
\]
Uniformly on `I`,
\[
F_\vee^{\log}\asymp L^2F_\vee.
\tag{49}
\]
Thus `(USF2)` is equivalent, up to absolute constants, to
\[
F_\vee^{\log}
=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^4}{UL^2}\right).
\tag{50}
\]
At `U=U_0`, this is
\[
F_\vee^{\log}=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-1}).
\tag{51}
\]

### Independent diagonal check

One may instead apply weighted Cauchy directly to `w_s`. Then
\[
\sum_sw_s^2=D_\vee^{\log}+F_\vee^{\log},
\]
where
\[
D_\vee^{\log}
=\sum_{\text{occurrences}}(\log p)^2.
\]
The banked unmarked count
\[
N_{\rm unmarked}\ll_JT^{11/5}L^{-3/5}
\]
gives
\[
D_\vee^{\log}\ll_JT^{11/5}L^{7/5}.
\tag{52}
\]
With (40),
\[
U_0D_\vee^{\log}
\ll_JT^{19/5}L^{2/5}
=o\!\left(\frac{T^4}{L^2}\right),
\tag{53}
\]
because the ratio is `T^{-1/5}L^{12/5}`. Thus the weighted diagonal is already subcritical; (50) is genuinely the only new quadratic term.

## 8. The weakest stratum-adaptive form

Let the occupied state set be partitioned arbitrarily:
\[
\mathscr S^\circ=\bigsqcup_\nu\mathscr S_\nu.
\]
Put
\[
U_\nu=|\mathscr S_\nu|,
\qquad
F_\nu=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S_\nu}(f_s)_2,
\qquad
N_\nu=\sum_{s\in\mathscr S_\nu}f_s.
\]
Applying (45) in each stratum gives
\[
N_\vee
\le U+\sum_\nu\sqrt{U_\nu F_\nu}.
\tag{54}
\]
Since `U=o(mathfrak B)`, the sufficient condition is precisely
\[
\sum_\nu\sqrt{U_\nu F_\nu}=o_J(\mathfrak B).
\tag{55}
\]
This is weaker than the one-block condition because
\[
\sum_\nu\sqrt{U_\nu F_\nu}
\le\sqrt{\left(\sum_\nu U_\nu\right)
          \left(\sum_\nu F_\nu\right)}.
\]
Natural strata include the branch, the upper-prime scale, the position of `y` in the window, lower-prime load, or any scale on which a horizontal estimate has a different strength.

For the two literal branches, the cleanest version is
\[
\sqrt{U_-F_-}+\sqrt{U_+F_+}=o_J(\mathfrak B).
\tag{56}
\]
It preserves every occurrence while charging no nonexistent minus/plus collision.

## 9. Fibre threshold ledger

Suppose more generally that
\[
U\ll T^uL^{-\upsilon}.
\tag{57}
\]
Then the exact scales are:

| statistic | sufficient scale |
|---|---:|
| average unweighted fibre `(1/U) sum f_s` | `o(T^(2-u) L^(upsilon-2))` |
| average weighted fibre `(1/U) sum w_s` | `o(T^(2-u) L^(upsilon-1))` |
| off-diagonal energy `F_vee` | `o(T^(4-u) L^(upsilon-4))` |
| weighted energy `F_vee^log` | `o(T^(4-u) L^(upsilon-2))` |
| collision RMS `(F_vee/U)^(1/2)` | `o(T^(2-u) L^(upsilon-2))` |

For the current worst-case `(u,upsilon)=(8/5,1)`, this becomes:

| statistic | current sufficient scale |
|---|---:|
| average unweighted fibre | `o(T^(2/5)/L)` |
| average weighted fibre | `o(T^(2/5))` |
| `F_vee` | `o(T^(12/5)L^(-3))` |
| `F_vee^log` | `o(T^(12/5)L^(-1))` |
| collision RMS | `o(T^(2/5)/L)` |

### 9.1 Bounded fibres

If
\[
\max_s f_s=O_J(1),
\]
then
\[
N_\vee\ll_JU_0\ll_JT^{8/5}/L
\]
and
\[
C^-+C^+\ll_JT^{8/5}.
\]
The ratio to `T^2/L` is `T^{-2/5}L`, which tends to zero. This is much stronger than needed.

### 9.2 Uniform `T^delta` fibres

If
\[
\max_sf_s\ll_JT^\delta L^\lambda,
\tag{58}
\]
then
\[
N_\vee\ll_JT^{8/5+\delta}L^{\lambda-1},
\]
so the ratio to `mathfrak B` is
\[
T^{\delta-2/5}L^{\lambda+1}.
\tag{59}
\]
Therefore:

* every fixed `delta<2/5` closes, regardless of a fixed log loss;
* at `delta=2/5`, one needs `lambda<-1`;
* the exact endpoint statement is
  \[
  \max_sf_s=o_J(T^{2/5}/L).
  \]

### 9.3 Average first moment

The condition
\[
\frac1U\sum_sf_s=o_J(\mathfrak B/U)
\tag{60}
\]
is both necessary and sufficient. It is the weakest possible fibre statement, but substituting the definition of `N_vee` shows that it is exactly the original incidence target. It is therefore not a reduction and should not be advertised as a new theorem.

An average `O_J(1)` fibre does close and is weaker than a bounded maximum, but proving it still requires horizontal information.

### 9.4 Average collision fibre

The condition
\[
\frac1U\sum_s(f_s)_2
=o_J\!\left(\frac{\mathfrak B^2}{U^2}\right)
\tag{61}
\]
is exactly `(USF2)`. Equivalently,
\[
\left(\frac1U\sum_s(f_s)_2\right)^{1/2}
=o_J(\mathfrak B/U).
\tag{62}
\]
Unlike a maximum bound, it permits a sparse set of very large fibres. For example, one exceptional fibre may be as large as `o(mathfrak B/sqrt(U))`, much larger than the uniform endpoint `mathfrak B/U`. This is why `(USF2)` is the weakest natural noncircular theorem in the current pair-counting architecture.

## 10. Cold audit of vertical `Z(p)` improvements

There are two separate questions:

1. can sufficiently strong vertical information close C4 directly? — **yes**;
2. does a plausible vertical improvement imply the horizontal upper-state theorem? — **no**.

### 10.1 Exact injection into lower zero pairs

Every minus occurrence injects into a branch-tagged ordered pair of distinct lower `p`-zeros `(a,r)`. Indeed
\[
a+r=7d-1,
\qquad
d=\frac{a+r+1}{7},
\qquad
y=d-a-1,
\qquad q=p+d.
\tag{63}
\]
Every plus occurrence injects into `(z,a)`, with
\[
z+a=6d-1,
\qquad
d=\frac{z+a+1}{6},
\qquad
y=p-6d-1-z,
\qquad q=p+d.
\tag{64}
\]
The plus indices are distinct because their sum is odd. Thus, with all original windows merely reducing the count,
\[
\boxed{
N_\vee\le2\sum_{p\in I}Z(p)(Z(p)-1).
}
\tag{65}
\]
This gives the exact vertical second-mass criterion
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in I}Z(p)^2=o(T^2/L^2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C^-+C^+=o_J(T^2/L).
}
\tag{66}
\]

If
\[
Z(p)\ll T^\alpha L^{-\sigma}
\quad(p\in I),
\]
then, since `#(I cap primes)<<T/L`,
\[
N_\vee\ll T^{1+2\alpha}L^{-1-2\sigma}.
\tag{67}
\]
The ratio to `mathfrak B` is
\[
T^{2\alpha-1}L^{1-2\sigma}.
\tag{68}
\]
Therefore `alpha<1/2` closes. At `alpha=1/2`, one needs `sigma>1/2`, or the exact little-o bound
\[
Z(p)=o(T^{1/2}L^{-1/2}).
\tag{69}
\]
A bare square-root bound `Z(p)<<T^{1/2}` still misses one logarithm.

More generally, if
\[
\sum_{p\in I}Z(p)\ll T^{1+c}L^{-1-\rho},
\qquad
\max_{p\in I}Z(p)\ll T^\alpha L^{-\sigma},
\]
then
\[
N_\vee
\ll T^{1+c+\alpha}L^{-1-\rho-\sigma}.
\tag{70}
\]
This closes when `c+alpha<1`; on the power boundary it needs `rho+sigma>1`.

### 10.2 Upper-state vertical information alone

The upper count controls only `U`:
\[
U\le2\sum_{q\in I}Z(q).
\]
A fixed upper state has at most `O_J(T/L)` candidate lower primes. Therefore
\[
N_\vee\ll_J U\,T/L.
\tag{71}
\]
Even the conjectural average bound
\[
\sum_{q\in I}Z(q)=O(T/L)
\]
gives only
\[
N_\vee=O(T^2/L^2),
\]
not the required little-o. Upper vertical information alone closes through (71) only if
\[
\sum_{q\in I}Z(q)=o(T/L).
\tag{72}
\]
Thus an average bounded `Z(q)` does not supply the missing saving.

### 10.3 Why vertical cardinalities do not force a fibre theorem

Knowing the numbers `Z(p)` says how many lower zero positions each characteristic owns. It says nothing about the affine landing map
\[
(p,i,j)\longmapsto(q,y).
\]
As an information-content countermodel, fix one admissible plus state `(q,y)`. For every lower prime `p` in its admissible interval, place zeros at the two affine positions
\[
i_+(p;q,y),\qquad j_+(p;q,y)
\]
(and add their reflected partners if reflection is required). Then every lower zero set has bounded cardinality, yet
\[
f_+(q,y)\asymp T/L.
\]
This is not asserted to be an actual Apéry configuration. It proves that vertical cardinalities, reflection, and the windows do not logically imply a bounded or `T^delta`, `delta<1`, upper fibre.

Notice that this aligned example may still be harmless for the target because it has very small `U`. That is exactly why the state-adaptive threshold is the right one: a large fibre is allowed when very few states are occupied.

One can quantify how strong vertical data must be before it forces `(USF2)` without any alignment theorem. Let
\[
M=2\sum_{p\in I}Z(p)(Z(p)-1),
\qquad
P_0\ll_JT/L
\]
be, respectively, the total lower-pair inventory and the trivial maximum number of candidate lower primes in one fibre. Then
\[
N_\vee\le M,
\qquad
U\le M,
\qquad
F_\vee\le P_0M.
\]
Hence
\[
UF_\vee\le P_0M^2.
\tag{73}
\]
Vertical information alone therefore guarantees `(USF2)` only at the sufficient scale
\[
M=o\!\left(\frac{\mathfrak B}{\sqrt{P_0}}\right)
=o(T^{3/2}L^{-3/2}).
\tag{74}
\]
Under a pointwise power bound for `Z(p)`, this requires roughly `alpha<1/4` (at `alpha=1/4`, an additional log saving is needed). This is far stronger than the direct vertical closure threshold `alpha<1/2`.

So the horizontal theorem is a sufficient mechanism, not something that follows automatically from a plausible improvement of `Z(p)`.

## 11. Why the gap polynomial and `B_p` factorization stop here

For each fixed lower characteristic, the two lower zero tests imply a gap-polynomial zero:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{minus: }&p\mid N_{5d+2y+1}(d-y-1),\\
\text{plus: }&p\mid N_{p-6d}(p-6d-y-1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{75}
\]
This retains the lower pair mark. It does not yield the required horizontal bound because, inside one upper fibre, changing `p` changes simultaneously:

* the characteristic;
* the polynomial `N_h` itself;
* its degree;
* the evaluation point;
* the Hasse square root `B_p` and all its coefficients.

There is no fixed integer polynomial whose prime divisors enumerate the fibre. A resultant between an object over `F_p` and an object over `F_q` would erase the characteristic labels and is not a valid common-root argument.

Likewise, (3)–(6) give an exact expression for each lower zero, but the implication
\[
\Phi_p(i)=\Phi_p(j)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta_{p,i}=\beta_{p,j}=0
\tag{76}
\]
is false. The first failed step in a raw-`B_p` sparsity attack is already (76): the equations are quadratic convolution cancellations. Nor does a vertical coefficient-zero bound for one `B_p` compare the varying systems as `p` runs through a fixed upper fibre.

Thus the Hasse–Franel descent successfully reparameterizes the exact C4 fibre, but it does not supply the needed cross-characteristic horizontal anti-concentration.

## 12. Insert-ready theorem

### Theorem (state-adaptive Hasse–Franel upper-state fibre closure for quotient-six C4)

Fix `J>0`, let `T -> infinity`, put `L=log T`, `I=(T/7,T/3]`, and `mathfrak B=T^2/L^2`. For each prime `ell>13`, let
\[
A_\ell(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}b_nX^n
=\Delta(X)^{\epsilon_\ell}B_\ell(X)^2
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_\ell[X],
\]
with `Delta=1-34X+X^2` and `B_ell(0)=1`, and define
\[
\Phi_\ell(n)=[X^n]\Delta(X)^{\epsilon_\ell}B_\ell(X)^2.
\]
Let `Y_T` be the set (29). For `(q,y) in Y_T`, define `P_-(q,y)` by (13) and `P_+(q,y)` by (23), retaining all displayed primality, parity, index, `d>T/J`, and archimedean gates. Put
\[
f_\sigma(q,y)=|\mathcal P_\sigma(q,y)|,
\]
let
\[
U_\sigma=\#\{(q,y):f_\sigma(q,y)>0\},
\qquad
F_\sigma=\sum_{q,y}(f_\sigma(q,y))_2.
\]
Then:

1. The two maps (14) and (26) are bijections from the literal minus and plus marked incidences to the corresponding fibre occurrences. In particular,
   \[
   N_\vee=\sum_{\sigma,q,y}f_\sigma(q,y)
   \]
   and
   \[
   C^-(T,J)+C^+(T,J)\asymp L N_\vee.
   \]

2. One has
   \[
   U_-+U_+
   \le2\sum_{q\in I}Z(q)
   \ll T^{8/5}/L
   =o(\mathfrak B).
   \]

3. If
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sqrt{U_-F_-}+\sqrt{U_+F_+}=o_J(\mathfrak B),
   }
   \tag{HF-USF2}
   \]
   then
   \[
   C^-(T,J)+C^+(T,J)=o_J(T^2/L).
   \]

4. In particular, with `U=U_-+U_+` and `F_vee=F_-+F_+`, it is sufficient that
   \[
   F_\vee=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^4}{U L^4}\right).
   \]
   Using only the currently banked upper-zero count, it is sufficient that
   \[
   F_\vee=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-3}).
   \]
   Equivalently, for the logarithmically weighted ordered collision sum, it is sufficient that
   \[
   F_\vee^{\log}
   =o_J\!\left(\frac{T^4}{U L^2}\right),
   \]
   or, uniformly under the current `U` bound,
   \[
   F_\vee^{\log}=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-1}).
   \]

### Proof

Parts 1 and 2 are the literal substitutions and the upper-zero projection proved above. For each branch, Cauchy gives
\[
N_\sigma
\le U_\sigma+\sqrt{U_\sigma F_\sigma}.
\]
Summing the two inequalities, using `U_-+U_+=o(mathfrak B)`, and applying `(HF-USF2)` gives `N_vee=o_J(mathfrak B)`. Equation (36) then gives the weighted conclusion. The one-block and weighted formulations follow from Cauchy and `log p asymp L` on `I`.

## 13. Claim boundary

What has been proved here is an exact fibre reparameterization and an exact closure theorem. It does **not** prove `(HF-USF2)` unconditionally.

The smallest remaining arithmetic lemma is a horizontal collision estimate for two distinct lower primes `p_1,p_2` that:

* land on the same branch-tagged marked upper zero `(q,y)`;
* satisfy their own two canonical convolution equations `Phi_{p_i}(i_i)=Phi_{p_i}(j_i)=0`;
* retain `p_i=q-d_i`, every `d`, parity, index, and archimedean window;
* is bounded at the state-adaptive scale in `(HF-USF2)`.

Vertical `Z(p)` improvements, one-characteristic gap-polynomial bounds, and the Hasse–Franel square factorization do not by themselves supply that horizontal collision estimate. The first exact failed implication is the attempted replacement of a convolution zero by a raw `B_p` coefficient zero, followed by the unjustified comparison of those raw coefficients across varying characteristics.
