# 4. Upper reflection saturation theorem

## Theorem 4.1 — first-order upper recurrence mark is purely structural

Let \(q\ge7\) be prime and suppose
\[
0<t<\frac{q-1}{2},
\qquad
q\mid b_t.
\]
Put
\[
\bar t=q-1-t,
\qquad
H=\bar t-t=q-1-2t.
\]
Then:

1. \(H\ge2\) is even;
2. \(q\mid b_{\bar t}\) by Apéry reflection;
3. \(q\mid N_H(t)\) by the gap transfer;
4. there is an integral polynomial \(M_H(X)\) with
   \[
   N_H(X)=(2X+H+1)M_H(X);
   \]
5. at the actual state,
   \[
   \boxed{N_H(t)=qM_H(t)};
   \]
6. hence the divisibility in item 3 yields no information about \(M_H(t)\).

### Proof

Items 1 and 2 are immediate from the definition of \(H\), oddness of \(q\), and the reflection congruence
\[
b_{q-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod q.
\]
Because
\[
0<t<\bar t<q,
\]
the transfer from \(t\) to \(\bar t\) crosses no denominator divisible by \(q\). Both endpoints vanish mod \(q\), and no-consecutive-zero gives a unit adjacent state, so (1.9) gives
\[
q\mid N_H(t).
\]

Since \(H\) is even, (1.10) says that \(N_H\) is odd around the center
\[
X=-\frac{H+1}{2},
\]
which gives the exact factor (1.11):
\[
N_H(X)=(2X+H+1)M_H(X).
\]
Finally
\[
2t+H+1
=2t+(q-1-2t)+1
=q.
\]
Therefore
\[
N_H(t)=qM_H(t).
\]
The mod-\(q\) vanishing of \(N_H(t)\) is thus automatic from the affine relation between the reflection endpoints; it does not constrain \(M_H(t)\). QED.

## Consequence

The most tempting marked carrier
\[
N_H(t)\,N_{h_\sigma}(x_\sigma)
\]
does satisfy
\[
pq\mid N_H(t)N_{h_\sigma}(x_\sigma),
\]
but after removing the universal upper factor \(q\) it becomes
\[
M_H(t)N_{h_\sigma}(x_\sigma),
\]
and no \(q\)-divisibility remains. The \(p\)- and \(q\)-parts are completely separated. This carrier is therefore no stronger than multiplying a lower carrier by the already-known upper prime.

# 5. Exact desingularization law: what would be needed at second order

The preceding theorem identifies a tautology. The companion identity identifies exactly what lies one order deeper.

Write
\[
a_m=A_m/d_m,
\qquad
\gcd(A_m,d_m)=1,
\tag{5.1}
\]
and define the q-stripped endpoint values
\[
c_t=b_t/q,
\qquad
c_{\bar t}=b_{\bar t}/q.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Applying (1.7) to the upper reflection interval gives
\[
N_H(t)
=
\frac{1}{6}
\left(\frac{\bar t!}{t!}\right)^3
\bigl(a_{\bar t}b_t-b_{\bar t}a_t\bigr).
\tag{5.3}
\]
Substitute
\[
b_t=qc_t,
\qquad
b_{\bar t}=qc_{\bar t},
\qquad
N_H(t)=qM_H(t),
\]
and cancel \(q\) in characteristic zero. This yields
\[
M_H(t)
=
\frac{1}{6}
\left(\frac{\bar t!}{t!}\right)^3
\bigl(a_{\bar t}c_t-c_{\bar t}a_t\bigr).
\tag{5.4}
\]
After clearing the reduced companion denominators,
\[
\boxed{
6d_td_{\bar t}M_H(t)
=
\left(\frac{\bar t!}{t!}\right)^3
\bigl(d_tA_{\bar t}c_t-d_{\bar t}A_tc_{\bar t}\bigr).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

All the clearing factors are \(q\)-units:

- \(6\) is a q-unit because \(q\ge7\);
- every factor in \(\bar t!/t!\) is strictly below \(q\);
- the standard companion denominator divides an lcm of integers below its index, so \(q\nmid d_t d_{\bar t}\);
- if \(q\mid b_m\) with \(0<m<q\), the Casoratian
  \[
  a_mb_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m=6/m^3
  \]
  shows \(a_m\) is a q-unit; hence \(q\nmid A_m\).

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
q\mid M_H(t)
\iff
 d_tA_{\bar t}c_t
\equiv
 d_{\bar t}A_tc_{\bar t}\pmod q.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
q^2\mid N_H(t)
\iff
\frac{c_t}{a_t}
\equiv
\frac{c_{\bar t}}{a_{\bar t}}\pmod q,
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
where the ratios are interpreted using q-unit companion values.

This is the exact missing second-order relation. It is a projective equality of the two lifted zero states. Mod-q reflection knows only
\[
b_t\equiv b_{\bar t}\equiv0\pmod q;
\]
it says nothing about their quotients \(c_t,c_{\bar t}\) modulo q.

Thus a recurrence proof that stops at first-order vanishing cannot create a primitive upper mark.

uA_6+vA_{12}
\]
to vanish modulo q from selectedness alone, independent of a further relation between those two jet coordinates, one would need
\[
ub_6+vb_{12}=0,
\]
\[
6ub_6+12vb_{12}=0
\pmod q.
\]
Outside the fixed exceptional primes dividing \(6b_6b_{12}\), this forces
\[
u=v=0.
\]
So the first q-adic lift does not supply a nonzero homogeneous relation among exactly the two rows which every plus foreign prime divides.

The simplest determinant illustrates the issue:
\[
D_+(q,t):=b_{12}A_6-b_6A_{12}.
\tag{8.6}
\]
Every plus foreign prime divides \(D_+\), but (8.5) gives
\[
\boxed{
D_+(q,t)
\equiv
-6b_6b_{12}\dot b_t
\pmod q.
}
\tag{8.7}
\]
Thus selectedness does **not** make q divide the p-carrier. To repair the q-congruence one must add the derivative correction
\[
6b_6b_{12}\dot b_t,
\]
but that correction is not forced to be divisible by the lower prime p. The q-mark and p-mark again split into different summands/factors.

This is a structurally different elimination failure from the reflection-factor failure:

```text
reflection continuant failure:
  q is present, but only as a universal central factor.

mod-q^2 block failure:
  after dividing q, the two p-divisible lifted rows span a two-coordinate upper jet;
  no nonzero homogeneous combination kills both coordinates.
```

Going to a higher q-adic order only helps if one proves a new relation reducing the upper jet rank **and** that relation is compatible with the same p-divisible rows. Merely increasing q-adic precision does not automatically create such compatibility.

This agrees with the earlier project-wide warning that the Apéry block transfer is rank-one at mod q and acquires memory/jet terms at higher precision rather than a free second modular zero condition.

# 10. Separated resultants and the gamma = 4 height wall

The exact separated resultant is
\[
\mathcal R_{h,k}
:=
\operatorname{Res}_X\bigl(N_h(X),N_k(X+h)\bigr).
\tag{10.1}
\]
The continuant Bézout/concatenation identities show that, away from restart boundary factors, repeated same-characteristic returns reduce to such separated collisions. The safe Smith-nullity majorant is
\[
r_p(h,k)
\le
v_p(\mathcal R_{h,k}),
\tag{10.2}
\]
where \(r_p\) is the affine gcd degree after the standard saturation.

Degree and coefficient-height bounds give
\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1),
\qquad
\deg N_k=3(k-1),
\]
and Sylvester/Hadamard gives
\[
\boxed{
\log|\mathcal R_{h,k}|
\ll hk\log(h+k).
}
\tag{10.3}
\]
For one fixed h-row,
\[
\sum_{k\le K}\log\Sigma_{\rm aff}(h,k)
\ll hK^2\log(h+K).
\tag{10.4}
\]
The exact return-difference closure from the continuant concatenation law turns two returns at \(k<\ell\) into another separated collision at \((k,\ell-k)\), but its scalar height is
\[
\sum_{\ell\le K}\sum_{k<\ell}
 k(\ell-k)\log\ell
\asymp
K^4\log K.
\tag{10.5}
\]
This is the known quartic, \(\gamma=4\), moving-resultant height barrier.

## Why the upper mark does not change gamma

A successful marked argument would have to do at least one of the following:

1. remove one genuinely free gap parameter;
2. reduce one of the two resultant degrees by a positive proportion/power;
3. force a large primitive common content that can be divided out while retaining every foreign lower prime occurrence.

The upper recurrence supplies none of these.

Its only unconditional factor is
\[
2t+H+1=q,
\]
a single linear structural factor. After removing it, no q-root condition remains. Keeping it changes a logarithmic height by only \(O(\log q)\) per selected state. It does not change the \(hk\) bidegree in (10.3), and hence does not change the quartic sum in (10.5).

So the marked recurrence route has
\[
\boxed{\gamma_{\rm usable}=4,}
\]
not \(<4\).

If a future marked desingularization lowered the primitive moving-resultant ledger from \(K^4\) to \(K^{4-\delta}\), the gain would be \(K^\delta\). At the q6 cutoff scale
\[
K\asymp
\frac{T^{2/5}}{\xi(T)(\log T)^{6/5}},
\]
a fixed \(\delta\) corresponds to a power gain \(T^{2\delta/5-o(1)}\). Thus a genuine drop of the quartic exponent is the right kind of improvement. The present upper mark gives \(\delta=0\).

# 12. Characteristic, division, exceptional-fibre, and multiplicity audit

## 12.1 Different characteristics

The lower condition is modulo p; the upper condition is modulo q. Ordinary polynomial resultants require a common root in one residue field. They cannot be applied directly across \(p\ne q\).

A CRT reformulation in \(\mathbf Z/(pq)\) does not change this: it produces a product of two residue-field components, not a single common-root condition.

## 12.2 Division by q

The fixed-state values
\[
b_{6q+t}/q,
\quad
b_{12q+t}/q,
\quad
b_{q-1-t}/q
\]
are integers because selectedness plus Lucas/reflection supplies one q-factor.

There is no right to divide by another q unless a q-adic lift theorem supplies it. The upper gap theorem shows exactly why: \(N_H(t)\) has one structural q-factor, but \(M_H(t)=N_H(t)/q\) need not be q-divisible.

## 12.3 Division by p

For lower gap transfer, all indices lie below p, so denominator factors are p-units. If one divides actual characteristic-zero Apéry values by p after using a zero condition, the resulting p-stripped quotients are new variables; the ordinary mod-p zero recurrence does not determine them.

## 12.4 Central upper zero

If
\[
t=(q-1)/2,
\]
then \(H=0\). There is no nontrivial reflection interval and hence no upper gap continuant to exploit. This is a separate exceptional fibre. The present argument does not assume that central selected states are negligible; they must remain in the old carrier unless another theorem removes them.

## 12.5 Fixed small exceptional primes

The q6 Lucas equivalences use fixed quotient digits \(6,12,13,1\). Primes dividing the relevant fixed Apéry coefficients, conventionally absorbed into the fixed exceptional factor such as \(5b_6b_{13}\), must be removed before treating those coefficients as units. They contribute only the already-recorded fixed exceptional support, not an asymptotic family.

## 12.6 Lower-gap parity

Because \(d=q-p\) is even, both lower gaps in (3.7) are odd. Thus the upper reflection factor has no lower analogue on the prescribed sign pair. This rules out a hidden lower central-factor cancellation in the generic q6 geometry.

## 12.7 Occurrence multiplicity

For a fixed \((q,t,\sigma)\), one lower prime is one support occurrence. A higher valuation \(p^e\mid G_\sigma\) does not create e different rows. Likewise the same q reused by many lower primes is one upper selected state, not many independent q resources.

If the same p occurs in both signs, the raw minus-first tie-break from Q4156/Q4158 can partition it set-theoretically; this has no effect on the algebraic obstruction.

## 12.8 Resultant infinity/boundary factors

The separated-resultant machinery must retain the standard projective/infinity and restart-boundary saturation. A prime dividing a raw resultant may come from degree drop or a boundary factor rather than an affine common root. None of those exceptional factors helps here: the fatal upper q-factor is itself exactly such a universal reflection/center factor and has to be removed before claiming a primitive marked root.

# 15. Smallest repair premise that could yield a strict exponent improvement

There is an important distinction between a premise that merely repairs (14.1) and one that actually improves the foreign-prime count.

## 15.1 Merely repairing the upper recurrence is not sufficient

Suppose one somehow proves q-divisibility of a desingularized upper scalar \(U(q,t)\). If \(U(q,t)\) depends only on the fixed upper state, then it is constant across all foreign lower primes attached to that state. Multiplying
\[

still leaves every p in exactly the old factor \(G_\sigma\). The logarithmic capacity for foreign primes is unchanged up to an additive statewise term.

Therefore a second-order upper theorem alone does not solve the incidence problem.

## 15.2 Minimal useful repair: primitive marked content/coupling

A genuinely useful repair must force an upper-selected factor **inside the primitive lower carrier**, not beside it.

One clean formulation is the following.

### Premise MARKED-PRIMITIVE-CONTENT(δ)

For each legal noncentral selected state/sign, construct a primitive integer carrier
\[
\mathfrak C_\sigma(q,t)

with these properties:

1. every legal foreign lower prime occurrence divides \(\mathfrak C_\sigma(q,t)\);
2. \(\mathfrak C_\sigma\) is obtained from the recurrence/continuant/resultant construction after **all universal reflection, restart, degree-drop, and fixed Lucas factors are saturated**;
3. the q-selected relation is used in the primitive factor itself, not by multiplying an external q-divisible scalar;
4. in the moving-gap ledger its total logarithmic height satisfies a strict power improvement

   \boxed{

   \ll
   K^{4-\delta}(\log T)^{O(1)}

   \tag{15.1}
   \]
   for some fixed \(\delta>0\), instead of the present \(K^4\) barrier;
5. occurrence multiplicity is preserved: repeated use of one q or one p cannot be counted as new primitive factors.

This is the smallest quantitative repair that directly attacks the known \(\gamma=4\) wall.
