# Kowalski's Frobenius sieve, Katz Mellin equidistribution, and AFP

## Executive verdict

The proposed shortcut does **not** follow from the Kowalski large sieve for Frobenius, and the obstruction is sharper than the fact that the character set varies with \(p\).

There are two decisive mismatches.

1. **The ordinary Frobenius sieve uses many auxiliary residue characteristics λ above primes ℓ≠p.** AFP asks whether a Mellin trace is divisible by the **defining prime** \(p\). For the algebraic condition
   
   \[
   \mathscr M_{p,r}\in p\mathcal O_E,
   \]
   
   reduction modulo every auxiliary λ∤p is vacuous, because \(p\) is a unit modulo λ. Thus the Frobenius-sieve denominator is literally zero: there are no nontrivial local conditions to combine.

2. **Katz's Mellin equidistribution is archimedean/macroscopic, whereas AFP is crystalline/microscopic.** Katz does have a character-aspect theory for finite-field Mellin transforms, so the phrase “Katz only treats the geometric parameter \(t\)” is too pessimistic. But it controls normalized complex Frobenius conjugacy classes against fixed test functions. The event
   
   \[
   v_p(\mathscr M_{p,r})>0
   \]
   
   is a same-characteristic \(p\)-adic slope or congruence condition of expected frequency \(1/p\). It is not a fixed complex conjugacy condition and is not detected by weak Sato–Tate equidistribution.

There are also three important corrections to the proposed setup.

- The standard theorem matching the phrase **“large sieve for Frobenius”** is due to **Emmanuel Kowalski** (2005/2006, and Chapter 8 of his 2008 book), not a 2002 Kowalski–Michel theorem under that name.
- The project has not identified \(b_r\) as the trace of the stalk of one rank-two sheaf at a “character point.” The underlying Apéry differential equation is rank three and is of symmetric-square type; the Mellin coefficient is an alternating trace on the **global twisted cohomology**. The rank of the original local system and the rank of this Mellin cohomology are different notions.
- The current repository only banks the implication
  
  \[
  \text{unique unit root}\Longrightarrow v_p(\mathscr M_{p,r})=0.
  \]
  
  Thus a zero is contained in the locus where the twisted crystal fails to have a unique unit root. The claimed equivalence “\(b_r=0\) iff there is no unit root” is not presently established and is generally too strong: several unit roots may cancel in the trace.

The right missing statement is therefore not a classical ℓ-adic Frobenius sieve. It is an **averaged crystalline Mellin anti-concentration theorem**:

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{p\le X}^{\mathrm{good}}
 \#\left\{r\bmod (p-1)/\{r\sim p-1-r\}:
          v_{\mathfrak p_p}(\mathscr M_{p,r}^{\mathrm{cris}})>0
   \right\}
 \ll \pi(X).
}
\tag{CMAC}
\]

After the exact comparison

\[
\mathscr M_{p,r}^{\mathrm{cris}}\equiv u_{p,r}b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p},
\qquad u_{p,r}\in\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p_p}^{\times},
\]

this is AFP, up to the harmless central self-reflected character and finitely many boundary twists.

The pointwise bound requested in part (E), with one absolute \(C\) for every \(p\), is **strictly stronger than AFP**. In the notation of `proof.tex`, it is Hypothesis \(Z\), and the project's Poisson model predicts that it is false: \(Z(p)\) should be unbounded, with extreme order about \(2\log\log p\). The natural theorem to seek is the averaged bound `(CMAC)`, not a uniform pointwise constant.

# 1. Three different Frobenius objects must not be conflated

Let \(U\) be the smooth locus of the Apéry pencil and let ℓ≠p. There are three layers.

## 1.1 Geometric-parameter Frobenius

For an ℓ-adic local system ℓ-adic sheaf \(\mathcal V_\ell\) on \(U/\mathbf F_p\), the stalk trace is

\[
\tau_p(t)=\operatorname{Tr}⁡\left(\operatorname{Frob}_t\mid
      (\mathcal V_\ell)_{\bar t}\right),
\qquad t\in U(\mathbf F_p).
\]

Large geometric monodromy gives vertical Sato–Tate equidistribution of these stalk classes as \(t\) varies. This is the ordinary geometric parameter.

## 1.2 Character-parameter Mellin Frobenius

For a multiplicative character χ of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), let \(\mathcal L_\chi\) be the Kummer sheaf. The Mellin transform is a **global cohomological trace**

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr M_{p,\chi}
  &:=\sum_{t\in U(\mathbf F_p)}\tau_p(t)\chi(t)\\
  &=\sum_i(-1)^i
    \operatorname{Tr}⁡\left(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
      H_c^i(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
             \mathcal V_\ell\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
    \right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.1}
\]

up to the fixed convention for geometric versus arithmetic Frobenius and the finitely many removed boundary points.

This is the object treated by Katz's theory of finite-field Mellin transforms. The Frobenius conjugacy class lives in the Tannakian monodromy group attached to the Mellin/convolution category. It is **not** simply the stalk matrix of the original rank-two local system.

In the Apéry project, the source-faithful object is additionally of symmetric-square type: the rank-three Apéry Picard–Fuchs connection has connected geometric monodromy of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\operatorname{SL}_2\simeq\operatorname{SO}_3\) type. A rank-two description may be useful after a proved descent to the underlying elliptic family, but it cannot be assumed at the Mellin-cohomology level.

## 1.3 Defining-characteristic crystalline Frobenius

To ask whether \(p\mid b_r\), one needs a \(p\)-adic/crystalline realization and a prime \(\mathfrak p_p\mid p\) in the coefficient field. The desired comparison is

\[
\mathscr M_{p,\omega_p^{-r}}^{\mathrm{cris}}
   \equiv u_{p,r}b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p},
\tag{1.2}
\]

where \(u_{p,r}\) is a unit and the exceptional boundary characters are explicit.

The event in AFP is therefore

\[
\mathfrak p_p\mid\mathscr M_{p,\omega_p^{-r}}^{\mathrm{cris}},
\tag{1.3}
\]

or a sufficient slope condition on

\[
H^1_{\mathrm{rig},c}
   (U,\mathcal V_p^\dagger\otimes\mathcal K_{\omega_p^{-r}}).
\]

This third layer is not controlled by knowing the complex distribution of the first or second layer.

# 2. Part (A): why the Frobenius large sieve does not give AFP

## 2.1 The actual Kowalski theorem

A convenient precise version is Theorem 2.7 of Perret-Gentil's *Exponential Sums Over Finite Fields and the Large Sieve*, which adapts Kowalski's sieve to compatible systems. For a coherent family \((\mathcal F_\lambda)_\lambda\), auxiliary valuations λ, and conjugacy-invariant subsets

\[
\Omega_\lambda\subset G(\mathbf F_\lambda),
\]

it gives schematically

\[
\frac{
 \#\{x\in U(\mathbf F_q):
      \rho_\lambda(\operatorname{Frob}_x)\notin\Omega_\lambda
      \text{ for every }\lambda\in\Lambda_L\}
}{q}
\ll
\left(1+\frac{L^B}{q^{1/2}}\right)\frac1{P(L)},
\tag{2.1}
\]

where

\[
P(L)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_L}
      \frac{|\Omega_\lambda|}{|G(\mathbf F_\lambda)|}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For \(G=\operatorname{SL}_n\),

\[
B=\frac{2n^2+n-1}{2};
\]

thus \(B=9/2\) for \(\operatorname{SL}_2\).

The mechanism is important: one global algebraic condition must imply **nontrivial restrictions modulo many independent auxiliary λ**. The denominator \(P(L)\) accumulates those local exclusions.

## 2.2 For \(p\)-divisibility, every auxiliary local condition is vacuous

Grant, optimistically, that all Mellin traces lie in the ring of integers \(\mathcal O_E\) of one number field \(E\). Let

\[
A_p=p\mathcal O_E.
\]

For an auxiliary valuation λ above ℓ≠p, \(p\) is a unit in \(\mathcal O_\lambda\). Hence

\[
(A_p\cap\mathcal O_\lambda)\bmod\lambda
   =\mathbf F_\lambda.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently the usual sieve choice

\[
\Omega_\lambda
 =\{g\in G(\mathbf F_\lambda):
          \operatorname{tr}(g)\notin (A_p)_\lambda\}
\]

is empty. Therefore

\[
P(L)=0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is not a weak estimate or a conductor loss. The sieve has **no local information at all**.

The only residue characteristic that sees \(p\mid\mathscr M_{p,r}\) is λ above \(p\). But the ℓ-adic construction of \(\mathcal F_\lambda\) requires ℓ≠p, and a single defining-characteristic place would not provide the many independent sieving moduli needed by (2.1). One must replace ℓ-adic monodromy by a genuinely crystalline theorem.

This is the cleanest reason the proposed Kowalski argument cannot work.

## 2.3 Even algebraic trace equality would not yield \(O(1)\)

Suppose instead the target were the algebraic equality

\[
\mathscr M_{p,r}=0
\]

in a fixed number field. That condition reduces nontrivially modulo every auxiliary λ, so the Frobenius sieve does apply in principle. But it still does not give an \(O(1)\) count on a parameter set of size \(q=p\).

Optimizing (2.1) at

\[
L\asymp q^{1/(2B)}=q^{1/9}
\]

for \(\operatorname{SL}_2\) gives, at best,

\[
\#\{x:\mathscr M_x=0\}
   \ll q^{1-1/(2B)}\log q
   =q^{8/9}\log q,
\tag{2.5}
\]

not \(O(1)\).

Thus the claim

> “trace zero is codimension one, and the character parameter space is zero-dimensional, so the count is \(O(1)\)”

is not a consequence of the large sieve.

There are two elementary reasons.

- A zero-dimensional scheme may have degree \(p-1\). The finite étale scheme representing the \(p-1\) characters has complexity growing with \(p\); dimension alone says nothing about its number of points.
- The preimage of a codimension-one subset under an arbitrary map from \(p-1\) points may contain all \(p-1\) points. One needs quantitative transversality or equidistribution, not a dimension subtraction.

If one somehow had classes \(g_{p,r}\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\) uniformly distributed with microscopic accuracy, then

\[
\frac{\#\{g\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p):\operatorname{tr}g=0\}}
     {|\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_p)|}
 =\frac1p+O(p^{-2}),
\]

and \(p-1\) samples would indeed predict about one zero. But proving an \(O(1)\) error in this sampling is essentially the desired theorem. Standard Deligne square-root discrepancy would give an error of order \(p^{1/2}\) in the count.

### Answer to (A)

The CMRD/AFP character family can be phrased in Frobenius language, but the classical large sieve for Frobenius cannot see defining-characteristic divisibility. Its auxiliary reductions are vacuous, and even for exact algebraic trace zero its quantitative output is far weaker than \(O(1)\).

# 3. Part (B): can one build a universal \((p,\chi)\)-family?

## 3.1 Fixed character order is manageable

For a fixed integer \(N\), Kummer characters of order dividing \(N\) can be organized over a fixed base such as

\[
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z[1/N,\zeta_N],
\]

and one may study the corresponding finite collection of twists at primes \(p\equiv1\pmod N\). Standard compatibility and Chebotarev ideas can then address this **fixed-order slice**.

This does not cover AFP. AFP uses all characters of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), whose orders range up to \(p-1\). The union over \(N=p-1\) has growing degree, growing coefficient fields, and is not a finite-type parameter scheme of uniformly bounded complexity.

## 3.2 The full character group is not a fixed zero-dimensional variety

One can encode the characters after choosing identifications, but the resulting finite étale object has degree \(p-1\). The disjoint union over all \(p\) is better viewed as a pro-/stacky character object rather than a fixed finite-type variety. Any direct Weil-bound argument would carry constants or Betti numbers growing with that degree.

The phrase “zero-dimensional parameter space” therefore hides exactly the complexity that must be controlled.

## 3.3 Katz already supplies the correct categorical substitute

Katz's *Convolution and Equidistribution* does not require one to force the character group into an ordinary geometric parameter line. It builds a Tannakian category using convolution, and twisting by \(\mathcal L_\chi\) gives Frobenius classes whose traces are the finite-field Mellin transforms. Forey–Fresán–Kowalski extend this arithmetic Fourier-transform framework to general connected commutative algebraic groups.

So there **is** a satisfactory universal language for the character aspect at the ℓ-adic/complex level.

What is absent is a companion theorem with all of the following simultaneously:

1. varying defining characteristic \(p\);
2. all \(p-1\) tame characters;
3. a uniform integral \(p\)-adic/crystalline realization;
4. comparison (1.2) with the Apéry coefficient;
5. microscopic control of the reduction modulo \(p\) or of the first Newton-slope break.

Constructing a formal moduli object for \((p,\chi)\) would not by itself prove item 5.

### Answer to (B)

Fixed-order Kummer slices can be put into a usual compatible family. The complete family of all characters as \(p\) varies is not a fixed finite-type base of bounded complexity. Katz's Mellin category is the right replacement for archimedean character equidistribution, but a new **crystalline character-aspect theorem** is still required for AFP.

# 4. Part (C): what Katz equidistribution does and does not say

## 4.1 Katz does treat the character parameter

For a suitable perverse sheaf \(\mathcal N\) on \(\mathbf G_m\), Katz studies the cohomology of

\[
\mathcal N\otimes\mathcal L_\chi
\]

as χ varies. The Frobenius conjugacy classes on these cohomology groups equidistribute in a maximal compact subgroup of the relevant Tannakian monodromy group. This is precisely a Sato–Tate theorem for finite-field Mellin transforms.

Thus the correct comparison is:

- geometric Sato–Tate: vary \(t\in\mathbf F_q^	imes\) in a stalk trace;
- Mellin Sato–Tate: vary χ in the global twisted cohomological trace.

The second is the one relevant to Apéry coefficients.

## 4.2 Weak complex equidistribution cannot detect \(p\)-divisibility

Katz's theorem controls averages

\[
\frac1{q-1}\sum_\chi f(\Theta_{q,\chi})
\]

for fixed continuous class functions \(f\) on a compact group, with errors governed by Deligne's Riemann hypothesis and the complexity of the corresponding representation.

AFP asks for

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{v_p(\mathscr M_{p,\chi})>0\}},
\]

which is not such a fixed class function.

Three distinctions matter.

1. **Complex trace zero is not \(p\)-adic trace divisibility.** An algebraic integer can have normal or even maximal complex absolute value while being divisible at a prime over \(p\).
2. **A continuous Sato–Tate law has no atom at zero.** The heuristic probability \(1/p\) does not come from the continuous \(SU(2)\) trace measure; it comes from a separate conjecture that the integral Mellin trace is uniformly distributed modulo \(p\).
3. **The required scale shrinks with \(p\).** Even if one replaced divisibility by a complex window of width \(1/p\), approximating its indicator requires test functions of growing frequency. Standard equidistribution errors deteriorate and are much larger than the expected main term \(O(1)\).

A square-root error in the normalized character average is \(O(p^{-1/2})\); after multiplying by \(p-1\), it is \(O(p^{1/2})\). AFP needs control at absolute scale \(O(1)\) per prime on average.

## 4.3 The repository's Jacobi counterexample is decisive

`proof.tex` records a fixed-complexity counterexample. Fix \(d\ge2\) and, for primes \(p\equiv1\pmod d\), let ψ be a multiplicative character of order \(d\). The rank-one Kummer sheaf has Mellin transforms

\[
M_j=J(\omega_p^{-j},\psi).
\]

The Gross–Koblitz/Stickelberger valuation formula and the Gauss-sum factorization of Jacobi sums give

\[
v_p(M_j)=1
\qquad
\text{for }j\ge\frac{(p-1)(d-1)}d.
\]

Hence at least

\[
\frac{p-1}{d}-1
\]

characters are \(p\)-divisible, even though the sheaf has fixed rank, fixed conductor, and the usual square-root Weil size.

This example does not prove that an Apéry-specific \(SL_2\) theorem is false. It proves something exactly relevant here:

> bounded conductor, purity, monodromy, and Katz equidistribution do not by themselves imply bounded \(p\)-divisibility of Mellin transforms.

The missing input must use special crystalline arithmetic of the Apéry system.

### Answer to (C)

Katz supplies the correct **character-aspect complex equidistribution**, but it does not imply AFP. A new theorem must control the reduction of the Mellin Frobenius trace at the same prime that defines the finite field.

# 5. Part (D): Plancherel explains the heuristic, not the zero count

Let

\[
M_r=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\tau_p(t)\omega_p(t)^{-r}.
\]

Multiplicative Plancherel gives

\[
\sum_{r\bmod p-1}|M_r|^2
   =(p-1)\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}|\tau_p(t)|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If \(\tau_p\) is normalized to size \(1\), the right side is of order \(p^2\). If one uses the unnormalized weight-\(1\) trace of size \(p^{1/2}\), it is of order \(p^3\). This normalization issue does not affect the conclusion.

## 5.1 An \(L^2\) identity cannot upper-bound the number of zeros

Zeros contribute nothing to (5.1). A vector can have a prescribed \(L^2\)-norm while having almost all coordinates zero. To prove that almost every Mellin coefficient is nonzero, one needs a small-ball theorem, a high-moment theorem, or a structural nonvanishing theorem.

Even a fourth moment normally gives only a lower bound on the support via Cauchy–Schwarz. Obtaining

\[
|\operatorname{supp}(M)|=p-O(1)
\]

requires near-optimal information at every scale, not ordinary Plancherel.

## 5.2 The exact residual Fourier identity

Over \(\mathbf F_p\), additive orthogonality gives

\[
Z(p)
 =\sum_{r\bmod p-1}\mathbf 1_{\{b_r=0\}}
 =\frac{p-1}{p}
  +\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
             \sum_{r\bmod p-1}e_p(ub_r).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus a pointwise \(Z(p)=O(1)\) theorem would follow from the genuinely two-dimensional cancellation

\[
\sum_{u\ne0}\sum_r e_p(ub_r)=O(p).
\tag{5.3}
\]

A separate square-root estimate \(O(p^{1/2})\) for each fixed \(u\) gives only \(O(p^{1/2})\) after (5.2), because there are \(p-1\) values of \(u\). This is the “second Fourier layer” already isolated in `proof.tex`.

For AFP, the corresponding averaged target is

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\frac1p
 \sum_{u\ne0}\sum_r e_p(ub_r)
 \ll \pi(X).
\tag{5.4}
\]

No ordinary automorphic large sieve gives (5.4) from the first Mellin \(L^2\)-norm.

## 5.3 The random heuristic remains compelling

If the residues \(M_r\bmod p\) behaved as independent uniform elements of \(\mathbf F_p\), then

\[
\Pr(M_r=0)=\frac1p,
\qquad
\mathbf E[Z(p)]\sim1.
\]

Reflection \(r\leftrightarrow p-1-r\) pairs the noncentral events, so the number of reflected zero pairs has mean \(1/2\), explaining the project's \(\operatorname{Poisson}(1/2)\) model.

What must be proved is precisely the missing assertion “the deterministic Mellin coordinates are uniform modulo the defining prime.” Neither complex Sato–Tate nor Plancherel supplies it.

Calling the \(M_r\) “Hecke eigenvalues on \(GL_1\)” is also potentially misleading. They are Fourier coefficients on the finite abelian group \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), and they are Frobenius traces of Kummer-twisted cohomology, but there is no fixed global \(GL_1\) automorphic representation whose Hecke eigenvalues are indexed by \(r\), nor a fixed Euler product in the \(r\)-variable.

### Answer to (D)

The \(1/p\) heuristic and the Poisson prediction are correct random-model expectations. Making them rigorous requires a defining-characteristic small-ball or factorial-moment theorem for Mellin traces. Katz/Kowalski ℓ-adic equidistribution and the automorphic \(L^2\) large sieve stop one full conceptual layer earlier.

# 6. Part (E): the precise missing theorem

The proposed pointwise statement should be modified in four ways.

1. Replace the stalk trace of a rank-two sheaf by the alternating Frobenius trace on the actual Kummer-twisted global cohomology.
2. Include a crystalline realization and a fixed comparison at a prime over \(p\).
3. Use “failure of a unique unit root” only as a sufficient majorant unless the converse is separately proved.
4. Replace a uniform pointwise constant by an average over \(p\), because AFP is an average theorem and the project's Poisson model predicts that the pointwise constant is false.

## 6.1 Minimal trace formulation

Let \(\mathcal V=(\mathcal V_\lambda)_\lambda\) be the integral compatible system associated with the primitive Apéry motive on \(U\), and assume it has an overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal companion \(\mathcal V_p^\dagger\) at every good \(p\). For

\[
\chi_{p,r}=\omega_p^{-r},
\]

put

\[
\mathscr M_{p,r}^{\mathrm{cris}}
 =\sum_i(-1)^i
  \operatorname{Tr}⁡\left(
   \operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
   H^i_{\mathrm{rig},c}
     (U,\mathcal V_p^\dagger\otimes\mathcal K_{\chi_{p,r}})
  \right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

First prove the integral comparison

\[
\mathscr M_{p,r}^{\mathrm{cris}}
   \equiv u_{p,r}b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}
\tag{6.2}
\]

for units \(u_{p,r}\), with only \(O(1)\) explicitly listed exceptional characters.

Then the exact AFP-level theorem is:

> **Crystalline Mellin anti-concentration, first moment (CMAC\(_1\)).** There is an absolute constant \(C\) such that
> 
> \[
> \sum_{p\le X}^{\mathrm{good}}
> \#\left\{
>  r\in\{1,\ldots,(p-3)/2\}:
>  v_{\mathfrak p_p}(\mathscr M_{p,r}^{\mathrm{cris}})>0
> \right\}
> \le C\pi(X).
> \tag{6.3}
> \]

The center \(r=(p-1)/2\) is added separately. By reflection, (6.2)–(6.3) give

\[
\sum_{p\le X}Z(p)\ll\pi(X),
\]

which is AFP/Hypothesis \(\bar Z\).

This formulation is nearly tautologically equivalent to AFP once (6.2) is proved, but it identifies the exact geometric structure a proof must exploit: the varying-character Newton slopes of one Apéry-specific overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal.

## 6.2 Stronger slope-majorant formulation

Let

\[
E_p=\left\{r:
H^1_{\mathrm{rig},c}
 (U,\mathcal V_p^\dagger\otimes\mathcal K_{\chi_{p,r}})
\text{ does not have exactly one slope-zero eigenvalue}
\right\}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The current project records that a unique unit root forces the Mellin trace to be a \(p\)-adic unit. Hence

\[
\{r:p\mid b_r\}\subseteq E_p
\tag{6.5}
\]

once the comparison is fixed. Therefore the stronger theorem

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le X}^{\mathrm{good}}|E_p|\ll\pi(X)
}
\tag{CUMA}
\]

would imply AFP.

What is **not** currently justified is replacing (6.4) by “there is no unit root” or asserting equality in (6.5). Multiple slope-zero eigenvalues can have a trace divisible by \(p\) through cancellation.

## 6.3 The natural Poisson strengthening

Let

\[
\nu_p=\mathbf 1_{\{p\mid b_{(p-1)/2}\}},
\qquad
K_*(p)=\frac{Z(p)-\nu_p}{2}.
\]

The project predicts the factorial-moment law

\[
\frac1{\pi(X)}\sum_{p\le X}(K_*(p))_k
   \longrightarrow \left(\frac12\right)^k
\qquad(k\ge1\text{ fixed}).
\tag{6.6}
\]

The case \(k=1\) gives AFP with the predicted mean \(Z(p)\to1\). This is the appropriate rigorous form of the Poisson heuristic.

## 6.4 What a “Frobenius theorem” strong enough to imply the pointwise claim would look like

Suppose one could construct defining-characteristic classes

\[
g_{p,r}\in G(\mathbf F_p)
\]

whose trace-zero condition is exactly \(p\mid b_r\). A pointwise \(O(1)\) result would follow from the microscopic Chebotarev estimate

\[
\left|
 \#\{r:g_{p,r}\in C_p\}
 -(p-1)\frac{|C_p|}{|G(\mathbf F_p)|}
\right|
\le C,
\tag{6.7}
\]

for

\[
C_p=\{g:\operatorname{tr}g=0\}.
\]

For \(G=\operatorname{SL}_2\), the main term in (6.7) is \(1+O(1/p)\). But (6.7) is a new **microscopic defining-characteristic Mellin Chebotarev theorem**. It is much stronger than Katz's weak equidistribution and is not supplied by Kowalski's sieve.

Moreover, the project expects the pointwise conclusion of (6.7) to fail for the actual coefficient-zero process. The averaged version (6.3) is the correct target.

# 7. Why the pointwise theorem is probably the wrong endpoint

The theorem proposed in the question is

\[
\#\{r\bmod p-1:
 \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}\mid
 \mathcal F\otimes\mathcal L_{\omega^r})\equiv0\pmod p\}
\le C.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Even after repairing the cohomological object and the \(p\)-adic comparison, (7.1) is Hypothesis \(Z\), not AFP. The manuscript's exhaustive computation up to \(10^6\) gives mean \(Z(p)\approx1\) but record values \(2,4,6,8,10,12\), and the reflected-pair distribution closely follows \(\operatorname{Poisson}(1/2)\). A Poisson law has unbounded support, predicting

\[
\max_{p\le Y}Z(p)\sim2\log\log Y.
\]

This is evidence, not a proof, but it makes a universal \(C\) an implausible strategic target.

The averaged theorem

\[
\sum_{p\le X}Z(p)\ll\pi(X)
\]

is both consistent with the Poisson model and exactly what AFP asks for.

# 8. A realistic proof program

The Frobenius/Mellin viewpoint remains valuable, but the order of work should be changed.

## Step 1: freeze the exact integral Mellin comparison

Prove a source-faithful version of (6.2), including:

- the exact Apéry local system or symmetric-square/Asai factor;
- the cohomological degree and boundary terms;
- the Teichmüller-character convention;
- the coefficient field and prime \(\mathfrak p_p\);
- the reflection involution;
- the center character.

Without this, “trace of the twist” may refer to the wrong Frobenius object.

## Step 2: compute the generic Hodge polygon of every tame twist

The toric calculation in `proof.tex` gives slopes

\[
a,\ a+1,\ a+2,\ a+3
\]

with multiplicities \(1,3,3,1\) for the relevant twist parameter \(a=r/(p-1)\). Determine exactly which first Hasse coordinate controls the existence and uniqueness of the unit root.

## Step 3: isolate the exceptional slope locus as an Apéry-specific object

One needs either:

- a uniformly bounded-degree normalized tame Hasse polynomial after a nontrivial Gauss/Jacobi normalization; or
- an average bound for nonordinary tame twists; or
- an explicit Gross–Koblitz valuation formula whose exceptional intervals cancel in the Apéry symmetric-square combination.

The direct unnormalized bounded-degree interpolation cannot work: `proof.tex` records a linear-complexity obstruction, and `hasse_square_no_go.tex` shows that reciprocal squareness alone does not restrict early coefficient zeros.

## Step 4: prove an average, not a supremum

Target `(CMAC_1)` or `(CUMA)`. An average over \(p\) permits rare primes with several zero pairs and matches the empirical distribution. Fixed-order Chebotarev calculations may contribute after decomposing the character family by order, but the error must remain summable as the order grows.

## Step 5: use Katz only after the \(p\)-adic exceptional set is defined

Katz/Forey–Fresán–Kowalski may control complex monodromy and rule out geometric degeneracies of the Mellin transform. A separate comparison theorem must then show that the crystalline exceptional locus is cut out by an algebraic condition of uniformly controlled complexity. The Jacobi example proves that this comparison is not automatic.

# 9. Direct answers

## (A)

No. The Frobenius large sieve combines reductions modulo many auxiliary λ∤p. The condition \(p\mid\mathscr M_{p,r}\) reduces to the whole residue field at every such λ, so the sieve denominator \(P(L)\) is zero. Codimension one in \(SL_2\) does not imply an \(O(1)\) preimage on a degree-\(p\) zero-dimensional parameter set. Even for algebraic trace equality, the standard \(SL_2\) sieve gives roughly \(p^{8/9}\log p\), not \(O(1)\).

## (B)

A fixed-order Kummer family can be put on a fixed scheme, but the collection of all characters of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) for varying \(p\) has growing degree and is not a uniformly bounded finite-type parameter variety. Katz's Mellin Tannakian formalism is the correct universal character-aspect language at the ℓ-adic level. It does not provide defining-characteristic slope anti-concentration.

## (C)

Katz does have a direct equidistribution theorem for finite-field Mellin transforms as the character varies. It controls complex normalized Frobenius conjugacy classes. AFP concerns divisibility at \(p\), a crystalline event of expected density \(1/p\). Weak Sato–Tate equidistribution and square-root errors do not resolve that scale.

## (D)

Plancherel gives the correct total Mellin energy but no upper bound for the zero count. The random-mod-\(p\) heuristic predicts mean \(1\), and reflection gives Poisson mean \(1/2\) for zero pairs. To prove it one needs a second-layer residual cancellation theorem such as (5.4), or the crystalline factorial-moment law (6.6). The repository's Jacobi-sum example shows that fixed conductor and Katz equidistribution alone cannot supply this.

## (E)

The stated pointwise \(C\) theorem is not presently justified and is likely false for the Apéry coefficient-zero process. The precise missing AFP theorem is the averaged crystalline Mellin anti-concentration statement `(CMAC_1)`, after proving the exact comparison (6.2). A stronger sufficient geometric formulation is the averaged unique-unit-root-failure bound `(CUMA)`.
