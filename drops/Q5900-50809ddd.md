# Q5900 hostile audit of the transformed P3.1 endpoint system

## 1. Audit outcome

The algebraic endpoint system is internally consistent, but the previous structural interpretation was too strong. In particular, the conclusion `c=d` is false. It comes from an invalid cancellation step: from

\[
c=b^2(1-d),
\]

one cannot identify `d` with `c` unless an additional equation forces

\[
1-d=c.
\]

The equation involving `c` only determines a product involving `(1-c)` and `(1-d)`; it does not supply that missing equality.

The endpoint data must therefore be treated as a one-dimensional algebraic family after elimination, not as a four-variable system with a hidden involution `c=d`.

## 2. Elimination of the endpoint ideal

The ideal is generated by

\[
F_1=a^2-b^2(1-a),
\]
\[
F_2=c-b^2(1-d),
\]
\[
F_3=c-bd(1-a)(1-b)(1-c).
\]

The first equation gives a quadratic relation for `a`:

\[
a^2+b^2a-b^2=0.
\]

The admissible root is

\[
a=\frac{-b^2+b\sqrt{b^2+4}}2,
\]

because `0<a<1`.

Using `F_2`,

\[
d=1-\frac{c}{b^2}.
\]

Substitution into `F_3` gives

\[
c=b\left(1-\frac{c}{b^2}\right)(1-a)(1-b)(1-c),
\]

or equivalently

\[
bc=b^2(1-a)(1-b)(1-c)-c(1-a)(1-b)(1-c).
\]

This is a quadratic equation in `c` over the quadratic extension generated by `a`.

Hence the endpoint variety has generic dimension one before imposing the extra endpoint constraint on `X` and `L`.

## 3. Endpoint constraints

The transformed variables are

\[
X=\frac cb,
\]

and

\[
L=\frac{c^2(1-c)}{b^3d}.
\]

The endpoint equations are

Alpha:

\[
X=L^4,
\]

Beta:

\[
X=L^2.
\]

The beta equation is therefore

\[
\frac cb=\left(\frac{c^2(1-c)}{b^3d}\right)^2.
\]

After clearing denominators:

\[
b^5d^2=c^3(1-c)^2.
\]

Together with

\[
d=1-c/b^2,
\]

this gives a single primitive equation in `b,c`:

\[
b^5\left(1-\frac cb^2\right)^2-c^3(1-c)^2=0,
\]

or

\[
b(b^2-c)^2-c^3(1-c)^2=0.
\]

This is the correct beta elimination polynomial before imposing the admissible real branch.

There is no factor implying `c=d`.

For alpha the analogous equation is

\[
\frac cb=\left(\frac{c^2(1-c)}{b^3d}\right)^4,
\]

which gives

\[
b^{11}d^4=c^7(1-c)^4.
\]

After substitution of `d=1-c/b^2`, this again gives a one-variable algebraic condition after eliminating `a`.

## 4. Dimension and root selection

The correct counting is:

- `(a,b,c,d)` subject to the three structural equations has dimension one.
- Adding alpha or beta endpoint equation reduces to finitely many algebraic points.
- The physical branch is selected by

\[
0<a,b,c,d<1.
\]

There is no additional branch condition `c=d`.

The old argument effectively replaced the beta equation by a stronger equation, reducing the algebraic set incorrectly.

## 5. Rogers identities

The numerical values

\[
R(a)+R(b)+R(c)+R(d)=\frac{\pi^2}{10}
\]

for alpha and

\[
R(a)+R(b)+R(c)+R(d)=\frac{5\pi^2}{34}

A genuine proof would require an additional functional identity, for example a verified five-term relation chain in the Bloch group or an explicit cluster/Y-system realization.

The invalid `c=d` shortcut cannot supply such a proof.
