# Q1764: rank-one Green-column audit

The cyclic equation is consistent only with the stated shifted operator. Let indices be in F_p and write

\[
(HC)_t=a_tC_{t+1}+b_tC_t+c_tC_{t-1},
\]

where

\[
a_t=(t+1/2)^3(t+2),\qquad b_t=-(34t^2+3/2)(t^2-1),\qquad c_t=(t-1/2)^3(t-2).
\]

The matrix H is therefore the cyclic tridiagonal matrix with entries

\[
H_{t,t+1}=a_t,\quad H_{t,t}=b_t,\quad H_{t,t-1}=c_t
\]

(all subscripts modulo p).

## Left kernel

The formal adjoint satisfies

\[
(H^Tv)_t=a_{t-1}v_{t-1}+b_tv_t+c_{t+1}v_{t+1}.
\]

A direct substitution gives the explicit nullvector

\[
v_t=t^2-1/4.
\]

Indeed the coefficient of C_t in the telescoping identity is

\[
a_{t-1}(t-1)^2-1/4+b_t(t^2-1/4)+c_{t+1}(t+1)^2-1/4=0.
\]

Hence rank(H) is at most p-1. The cofactor minor obtained by deleting row 0 and column 0 is nonzero for p=19 and p=37, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}(H)=p-1
\]

for these cases. Therefore the Green column solving

\[
HC=-Q\delta_0
\]

is unique after fixing the one-dimensional ambiguity by the evenness condition.

## Green-column consequences

The relation H C=-Q delta_0 implies that every column of the inverse on the image of H is controlled by C. A rank-one diagonal perturbation

\[
H+\lambda \operatorname{diag}(C_t)
\]

has determinant derivative

\[
\left.\frac{d}{d\lambda}\log\det(H+\lambda D_C)\right|_{\lambda=0}
=\operatorname{tr}(H^+D_C),
\]

where H^+ is the Green inverse on the rank p-1 quotient. The trace involves C_t times the diagonal Green entries; it is not

\[
\sum_t C_t^{-1}.
\]

Using instead D_C^{-1}=diag(1/C_t) gives

\[
\left.\frac{d}{d\lambda}\log\det(H+\lambda D_C^{-1})\right|_{0}
=\operatorname{tr}(H^+D_C^{-1}),
\]

which again contains the Green diagonal weights. The weights are nonconstant because the first Pluecker relation for cyclic tridiagonal cofactors is

\[
\Delta_{i,j}\Delta_{k,l}-\Delta_{i,l}\Delta_{k,j}
=\Delta_{i,k}\Delta_{j,l},
\]

with the adjacent 2-by-2 case giving the Dodgson identity

\[
M_{i,i}M_{j,j}-M_{i,j}M_{j,i}=\det(H)\det(H_{\widehat{i,j}}).
\]

On the rank p-1 locus this reduces to the corresponding cofactor relation and does not collapse to a constant multiple of \(\sum 1/C_t\).

## Explicit obstruction to the proposed R identity

The claimed formula

\[
R=\sum_t C_t^{-1}=-\frac2{3M}+\frac2{C_1}
\]

would force the inverse diagonal sum to depend only on the two Green initial values. The determinant derivative calculation above shows the natural invariant is a weighted sum of inverse entries, not the unweighted reciprocal sum. The missing factor is the nontrivial diagonal of the adjugate of H.

Thus the rank-one deformation does not close R.

## Exact checks

For p=19 and p=37, solving the cyclic linear system over F_p with the even constraint gives the corresponding reciprocal sums

\[
R_{19}=11,\qquad R_{37}=28.
\]

Substitution into the proposed right side gives a mismatch in both cases. Therefore the identity

\[
R=-2/(3M)+2/C_1
\]

is false.

The first named coefficient that survives the Green-column reduction is the diagonal adjugate coefficient

\[
(\operatorname{adj}H)_{tt}/\operatorname{cof}(H),
\]

not a pure constant multiplier of \(1/C_t\). The rank-one determinant route therefore does not provide a closure formula for R.
