# 2. Independent re-audit of the WZ certificate

Let

\[
T(z,k)=
\left(\frac{(-z)_k(z+1)_k}{(k!)^2}\right)^2,
\qquad
P(z)=34z^3+51z^2+27z+5,
\tag{2.1}
\]

and, for \(k\ge1\), define

\[
\Omega(z,k)=
\frac{4k^4(2z+1)
      (2k^2-3k-4z(z+1))}
     {(k+z)^2(k-z-1)^2}
T(z,k),
\qquad
\Omega(z,0)=0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Although (2.2) is displayed rationally, it is a polynomial: \(T(z,k)\)
contains the square factors \((k+z)^2\) and \((k-z-1)^2\).

## 2.1 Exact quotient identities

Direct cancellation gives

\[
\frac{T(z+1,k)}{T(z,k)}
=\left(\frac{z+k+1}{z-k+1}\right)^2,
\tag{2.3}
\]

\[
\frac{T(z-1,k)}{T(z,k)}
=\left(\frac{z-k}{z+k}\right)^2,
\tag{2.4}
\]

and

\[
\frac{T(z,k+1)}{T(z,k)}
=\frac{(k-z)^2(k+z+1)^2}{(k+1)^4}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Put

\[
y=z(z+1),\qquad x=2z+1,\qquad
\mathcal D=(k+z)^2(k-z-1)^2.
\]

Using (2.5), the first boundary ratio simplifies to

\[
\frac{\Omega(z,k+1)}{T(z,k)}
=4x(2k^2+k-1-4y).
\tag{2.6}
\]

After multiplying by \(\mathcal D\), the claimed WZ relation is exactly the
following polynomial identity in \(\mathbf Z[z,k]\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(z+1)^3(z+k+1)^2(z+k)^2
-P(z)\mathcal D
+z^3(z-k)^2(k-z-1)^2\\
&\quad=
4x(2k^2+k-1-4y)\mathcal D
-4k^4x(2k^2-3k-4y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Expanding the two sides proves (2.7); the verifier checks this identity over
`QQ[z,k]`, without any finite-field substitution. Dividing back by the
visible common denominator yields

\[
\boxed{
\Omega(z,k+1)-\Omega(z,k)
=(z+1)^3T(z+1,k)-P(z)T(z,k)+z^3T(z-1,k).}
\tag{2.8}
\]

The sign in Q8200 is therefore correct.

# 3. The \(k=N+1\) boundary and the \(p\)-special seam

Let \(p\) be odd and

\[
N=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
Y=z(z+1),
\qquad
x=2z+1.
\]

The nodes \(a=0,\ldots,N-1\) give

\[
\prod_{a=0}^{N-1}(Y-a(a+1))=x^{p-1}-1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The last node satisfies

\[
Y-N(N+1)=Y+\frac14=\frac{x^2}{4}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
4^{N+1}\prod_{a=0}^{N}(Y-a(a+1))
=x(x^p-x).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Here \(4^N=2^{p-1}=1\), so \(4^{N+1}=4\).

Wilson's theorem gives

\[
(N!)^2=(-1)^{N+1},
\]

and therefore

\[
((N+1)!)^2
=(N+1)^2(N!)^2
=\frac14(-1)^{N+1}
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

All factorials are units because \(N+1<p\), including \(p=3\). Combining
(3.2) and (3.3) yields

\[
\boxed{T(z,N+1)=x^2(x^p-x)^2.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

At \(k=N+1=1/2\) in \(\mathbf F_p\), the rational prefactor in (2.2)
simplifies, after its removable factor is cancelled, to

\[
-\frac4x.
\]

Consequently

\[
\Omega(z,N+1)=-4x(x^p-x)^2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Since

\[
x^p-x=2(z^p-z),
\]

(3.5) is

\[
\boxed{
\Omega(z,N+1)
=-16(2z+1)(z^p-z)^2.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The apparent division by \(x\) at \(x=0\) is removable in (3.5); the final
expression is polynomial.

Now sum (2.8) for \(0\le k\le N\). Since \(\Omega(z,0)=0\) and

\[
\mathcal B_p(z)
=\sum_{k=0}^{N}T(z,k)
=F_p^{\mathrm{sp}}(z(z+1)),
\]

one obtains the exact seam

\[
\boxed{
(z+1)^3\mathcal B_p(z+1)-P(z)\mathcal B_p(z)
+z^3\mathcal B_p(z-1)
=-16(2z+1)(z^p-z)^2.}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus Q8200's WZ certificate, boundary sign, factor \(16\), and endpoint
\(N+1\) all survive.

## 3.1 Centered form and uniqueness

With \(x=2z+1\) and

\[
f_p(x)=\mathcal B_p\left(\frac{x-1}{2}\right),
\]

multiplying (3.7) by \(8\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(x+1)^3f_p(x+2)
-2x(17x^2+3)f_p(x)\\
&\qquad +(x-1)^3f_p(x-2)
=-32x(x^p-x)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}

If a nonzero polynomial \(g\) has leading term \(cx^d\), the degree-
\(d+3\) coefficient on the homogeneous left side of (3.8) is

\[
(1-34+1)c=-32c.
\]

For odd \(p\), \(32\) is a unit. The homogeneous operator is injective on
all polynomials, in particular on the even polynomials of degree at most
\(2p-2\). Q8200's uniqueness claim therefore also survives.

# 4. Exact node derivative normalization

Let

\[
\lambda_n=n(n+1),
\qquad 0\le n\le N.
\]

For \(k\le n\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi_k(\lambda_n)
&=\frac{\prod_{a=0}^{k-1}(n-a)(n+a+1)}{(k!)^2}\\
&=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For \(k>n\), the polynomial \(\phi_k^2\) has a double zero at
\(\lambda_n\), so both its value and first derivative vanish there.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{F_p^{\mathrm{sp}}(\lambda_n)=b_n.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For \(k\le n\), logarithmic differentiation gives

\[
\frac{\phi_k'(\lambda_n)}{\phi_k(\lambda_n)}
=\sum_{a=0}^{k-1}\frac1{(n-a)(n+a+1)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The exact partial fraction identity is

\[
\frac{2n+1}{(n-a)(n+a+1)}
=\frac1{n-a}+\frac1{n+a+1}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since

\[
(F_p^{\mathrm{sp}})'(\lambda_n)
=2\sum_{k=0}^n
\phi_k(\lambda_n)^2
\sum_{a=0}^{k-1}\frac1{\lambda_n-\lambda_a},
\]

multiplying by \((2n+1)/2\) and applying (4.4) gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{2n+1}{2}
(F_p^{\mathrm{sp}})'(\lambda_n)
=D_n.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is the correct normalization. There is no missing factor of \(2\): the
formal index derivative is \(g_n=2D_n\), whereas the spectral derivative in
(4.5) is multiplied by \((2n+1)/2\).

If \(p>2n+1\), then every harmonic denominator is at most \(2n<p\), and
\(2(2n+1)\) is a \(p\)-unit. Hence

\[
\lambda_n\text{ is repeated}
\iff
p\mid b_n
\text{ and }
p\mid\operatorname{num}(D_n).
\tag{4.6}
\]

At the midpoint \(n=N\), \(2n+1=p=0\). Equation (4.5) then forces
\(D_N=0\) modulo \(p\), but it gives no information about
\((F_p^{\mathrm{sp}})'(\lambda_N)\). The midpoint must remain separate.

For \(p=11,n=4\), equations (V1)--(V2) are now an explicit hostile
regression of (4.5).

# 5. The fixed integer \(E_n\)

Put

\[
g_n=2D_n.
\]

Differentiating the characteristic-zero Apéry recurrence gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(n+1)^3g_{n+1}-P(n)g_n+n^3g_{n-1}\\
&\quad=P'(n)b_n
-3(n+1)^2b_{n+1}-3n^2b_{n-1}
=:S_n,
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

with

\[
g_0=0,\qquad g_1=12.
\]

Define the discrete Wronskian

\[
W_n=(n+1)^3(b_ng_{n+1}-g_nb_{n+1}).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Multiplying (5.1) by \(b_n\), subtracting \(g_n\) times the homogeneous
Apéry recurrence, and cancelling the middle terms gives

\[
\boxed{W_n-W_{n-1}=b_nS_n,\qquad W_0=12.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

For \(n\ge1\), define

\[
\boxed{E_n=W_{n-1}+3n^2b_{n-1}b_n.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Expanding \(W_{n-1}\) gives the exact Bézout identity

\[
\boxed{
E_n
=n^3b_{n-1}g_n
+n^2b_n(3b_{n-1}-ng_{n-1}).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Telescoping (5.3) eliminates every \(g_j\). The result is the integer formula

\[
\boxed{E_1=27,}
\tag{5.6}
\]

and, for \(n\ge2\),

\[
\boxed{
E_n=-3
+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}P'(j)b_j^2
-6\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}(j+1)^2b_jb_{j+1}.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Thus \(E_n\in\mathbf Z\) is defined using only ordinary Apéry numbers. The
old float-dependent verifier did not establish (5.4)--(5.7), but these
identities follow exactly from the two recurrences and are checked by the new
rational verifier.

## 5.1 Exact pointed equivalence

Let \(p>2n+1\) and suppose \(p\mid b_n\). Then:

- \(p\nmid2n\);
- every denominator of \(D_n\) is a \(p\)-unit;
- \(p\nmid b_{n-1}\).

For the last assertion, if \(b_{n-1}=b_n=0\pmod p\), the recurrence at
\(n-1\), whose pivots are units because \(p>n\), propagates backward to
\(b_0=0\), contradicting \(b_0=1\).

Reducing (5.5) at the target gives

\[
E_n\equiv n^3b_{n-1}g_n
=2n^3b_{n-1}D_n\pmod p.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Every displayed multiplier is a unit. Hence

\[
\boxed{
 p>2n+1,\ p\mid b_n
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(p\mid E_n\iff p\mid\operatorname{num}(D_n)\bigr).}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\text{pointed simplicity for all }n
\iff
\operatorname{rad}\gcd(b_n,E_n)
\text{ has no prime factor }>2n+1
\text{ for every }n.}
\tag{5.10}
\]

This is an exact reduction, not a proof of the right side. Q8200 did not—and
Q8206 does not—prove pointed simplicity.

# 6. Corrected saturated-gcd theorem

Write, over \(\mathbf F_p[Y]\),

\[
F=F_p^{\mathrm{sp}},
\qquad
\psi=\prod_{a=0}^{N-1}(Y-\lambda_a),
\]

\[
\mathcal C=\psi F'-2\psi'F,
\qquad
G=\gcd(F,\psi),
\qquad
q=F/G,
\qquad
c=\mathcal C/G.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The polynomial \(\psi\) is squarefree. The divisibility \(G\mid\mathcal C\)
follows from the local calculation below.

## Theorem 6.1

For every odd prime \(p\), with all gcds monic,

\[
\boxed{\gcd(q,c)=\gcd(F,F').}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
F_p^{\mathrm{sp}}\text{ is squarefree}
\iff
\gcd(q,c)=1.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

## Proof with the complete exponent cases

Work in the local DVR at an irreducible factor \(\pi\). Put

\[
a=v_\pi(F),
\qquad
\epsilon=v_\pi(\psi)\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Because \(\deg F=p-1<p\), whenever \(a\ge1\) the multiplicity coefficient
\(a\) is nonzero in \(\mathbf F_p\), and

\[
v_\pi(F')=a-1.
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Case \(\epsilon=0\)

Here \(\psi\) is a unit.

- If \(a=0\), then \(q\) is a unit and both gcd exponents are zero.
- If \(a\ge1\), the two terms in
  \(\mathcal C=\psi F'-2\psi'F\) have valuations \(a-1\) and at least \(a\).
  Hence
  
  \[
  v_\pi(\mathcal C)=a-1.
  \]
  
  Since \(G\) has exponent zero,
  
  \[
  v_\pi(q)=a,\qquad v_\pi(c)=a-1,
  \]
  
  so the gcd exponent is \(a-1\).

### Case \(\epsilon=1\)

Choose a local parameter \(t\) and units \(u,v\) with

\[
\psi=tu,\qquad F=t^av.
\]

For \(a\ge1\), direct differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C
=(a-2)uvt^a
+t^{a+1}(uv'-2u'v).}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The complete cases are:

- \(a=0\): \(q\) is a unit; the gcd exponent is zero.
- \(a=1\): \(v_\pi(\mathcal C)=1\). After dividing by the single factor in
  \(G\), both \(q\) and \(c\) are units; the gcd exponent is zero.
- \(a=2\): the first term in (6.5) cancels, so
  \(v_\pi(\mathcal C)\ge3\), possibly \(+\infty\). After division by \(G\),
  
  \[
  v_\pi(q)=1,\qquad v_\pi(c)\ge2,
  \]
  
  and the gcd exponent is \(1=a-1\).
- \(3\le a\le p-1\): \(a-2\) is a unit, so
  \(v_\pi(\mathcal C)=a\). After division by \(G\), both \(q\) and \(c\)
  have exponent \(a-1\).

Thus in every case the exponent in \(\gcd(q,c)\) is

\[
\max(a-1,0),
\]

which is exactly the exponent in \(\gcd(F,F')\). This proves (6.2).

The exceptional \(a=2\) line includes the only delicate \(p=3\) possibility;
no division by \(a-2\) is made there. This is the corrected version of the
malformed/incomplete exponent prose in Q8200. The theorem survives.

# 7. What the correction does not prove

The exact identities above establish neither requested global theorem.

## 7.1 Full squarefreeness

Equation (6.3) is an exact criterion. It does not prove

\[
\gcd(F_p^{\mathrm{sp}},(F_p^{\mathrm{sp}})')=1
\]

for every odd prime. A multiple midpoint root or an off-node multiple root is
not governed by the strict pointed statement.

## 7.2 Pointed simplicity

Equation (5.10) is an exact characteristic-zero reduction. It does not prove
that \(\gcd(b_n,E_n)\) is supported on primes at most \(2n+1\).

## 7.3 Finite evidence

The corrected verifier contains an exact counterexample search and a hostile
small-prime regression. Its output for a finite bound is evidence only. No
finite absence of failures is used in Sections 2--6.

# 9. Final ruling

The Q8200 verifier was not exact and its completion claim is retracted. The
smallest exposed failure is \((p,n)=(11,4)\), where the bad code returns \(8\)
and exact arithmetic returns \(3\).

After independent re-audit:

- the WZ certificate and \(p\)-special seam survive with their original sign;
- the \(k=N+1\) boundary is exactly
  \(-16(2z+1)(z^p-z)^2\);
- the node normalization is exactly
  \((2n+1)(F_p^{\mathrm{sp}})'(\lambda_n)/2=D_n\);
- the integer \(E_n\) and its large-prime pointed equivalence survive;
- the saturated-gcd theorem survives after the local exponent cases are
  stated correctly.

No all-prime squarefreeness proof, no all-\(n\) pointed-simplicity proof, and
no exact counterexample to those mathematical statements is obtained. The
remaining pointed problem is still the fixed smooth-gcd assertion for
\(\gcd(b_n,E_n)\); full squarefreeness additionally includes midpoint and
off-node multiplicity.
