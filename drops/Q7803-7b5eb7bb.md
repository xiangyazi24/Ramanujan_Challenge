# Verdict

There are two corrections before applying trace-function technology.

1. The complete nontrivial-character mode is **not** itself a K3 Hecke eigenvalue.  It is a Mellin transform of the *support of the vanishing coefficients* \(b_r\bmod p\), and the full CED kernel is a two-point twisted correlation whose second Mellin transform factors through products of these support transforms.
2. The actual zero-support kernel currently has growing rank/conductor and reducible abelian monodromy.  It therefore does **not** satisfy the Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Sawin hypotheses.  The pure rank-five K3 connected tensor piece would have bounded complexity and plausible gallant monodromy, but it is the wrong observable: it lives over the geometric K3 parameter \(t\), whereas \(r\in Z_p\) is a coefficient/Mellin index.

For the range question, put \(Y\asymp X^2\) for the semiprime scale.  Then
\[
 p\asymp X\asymp Y^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \frac vp\asymp X\asymp Y^{1/2}.
\]
Thus the modulus is **exactly at exponent \(1/2\)** on the total semiprime scale—at the classical Bombieri–Vinogradov endpoint, though logarithmically outside the usual \(Y^{1/2}(\log Y)^{-A}\) formulation.  Factor-by-factor it is much worse: \(p\asymp q\asymp\ell\), so the modulus has exponent \(1\), not \(1/2\), relative to either prime factor.

The present CED route is therefore blocked by the construction/complexity of the correct Mellin detector, not by a fixed-power exponent obstruction in the semiprime variable.

# 0. Mandatory dictionary correction

The subsequent audit of Q7786 must be imposed here.  The Apéry index \(r\) is not a point of the K3 base.

The repository has the Hasse polynomial
\[
 H_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m t^m,
\]
and, for \(1\le r\le p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality gives
\[
 \boxed{
 b_r=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}H_p(t)t^{-r}
 }
 \qquad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
 \tag{0.1}
\]
Hence

- \(t\) is a geometric K3/modular parameter;
- \(H_p(t)=0\) is a nonordinary-fiber condition;
- \(r\) labels a Mellin/Kummer character \(t\mapsto t^{-r}\);
- \(b_r=0\) is cancellation of the global Mellin coefficient (0.1).

Consequently there is no currently constructed rank-three fiber Frobenius matrix \(\Phi_{p,r}\) whose unit-root projector satisfies
\[
1_{b_r=0}=1-\operatorname{Tr}\Pi^{\mathrm{ur}}_{p,r}.
\]
That formula from Q7786 is not valid.  A legitimate \(r\)-indexed object would require a Kummer/Mellin pushforward such as
\[
 R\Gamma_c\bigl(U_p,\mathscr U_p\otimes\mathcal L_{\omega^{-r}}\bigr),
 \tag{0.2}
\]
together with a comparison theorem identifying the relevant Frobenius datum with (0.1).  Its rank and conductor are not generally three and have not been established in the repository.

The checked-in object \(S_{p,j}^{(1)}\) in `problems/3.2/lemmaBprime_result.tex` is a Kummer-twisted cohomological trace congruent to \(b_j\).  Even granting that trace realization, the condition \(S_{p,j}^{(1)}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p}\) is nonlinear in the trace, and its support transform below is not another eigenvalue of that cohomology.

# 1. Exact nontrivial-character kernel

Put
\[
 z_p(r)=1_{r\in Z_p},
 \qquad
 F_p(a)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(ar),
 \qquad
 e_p(x)=e^{2\pi i x/p}.
\]
Since \(b_0=1\), one has \(0\notin Z_p\), so all Mellin sums may be taken on \(\mathbf F_p^\times\).

For a multiplicative character \(\chi\bmod p\), define
\[
 \boxed{
 S_p(\chi)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}\chi(r).
 }
 \tag{1.1}
\]
Let
\[
 \tau_p(\chi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\chi(x)e_p(x),
 \qquad \tau_p(1)=-1.
\]
For nontrivial \(\chi\), \(|\tau_p(\chi)|=p^{1/2}\), and additive/multiplicative Fourier inversion gives the exact identity
\[
 \boxed{
 S_p(\chi)
 =\frac{1}{\tau_p(\bar\chi)}
   \sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\bar\chi(a)F_p(a).
 }
 \tag{1.2}
\]
There is also a completely explicit, but analytically bad, Fermat-detector formula.  Extend \(b_r\) modulo \(p\) and use
\[
 z_p(r)=1-b_r^{p-1}.
\]
Since \(\sum_{r\ne0}\chi(r)=0\) for nontrivial \(\chi\),
\[
 \boxed{
 S_p(\chi)
 =-\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\chi(r)b_r^{p-1}.
 }
 \tag{1.3}
\]
Formula (1.3) makes the main complexity issue visible: direct zero detection uses degree \(p-1\), not bounded degree.

For reference, character orthogonality gives
\[
 \sum_{\chi\bmod p}|S_p(\chi)|^2=(p-1)Z(p),
 \tag{1.4}
\]
and hence
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\chi\bmod p\\\chi\ne1}}|S_p(\chi)|^2
 =(p-1)Z(p)-Z(p)^2.
 \tag{1.5}
\]
These are exact average identities, not individual cancellation bounds.

## 1.1 The full CED twisted kernel is larger than \(S_p(\chi)\)

In the Q7798 notation, for a completion character \(\eta\bmod p\), the local twisted ratio kernel is
\[
 T_p(h;\eta)
 =\sum_{w\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
   \eta(w)F_p(w)\overline{F_p(hw)}.
 \tag{1.6}
\]
For nontrivial \(\eta\), expansion of the two additive Fourier sums gives
\[
 \boxed{
 T_p(h;\eta)
 =\tau_p(\eta)
  \sum_{\substack{r,s\in Z_p\\r\ne hs}}
       \bar\eta(r-hs).
 }
 \tag{1.7}
\]
Thus the nontrivial CED mode is a twisted difference-incidence sum on \(Z_p\times Z_p\), not merely the one-point sum (1.1).

A second multiplicative Fourier transform diagonalizes (1.7).  For every character \(\chi\bmod p\), with the convention \(\tau_p(1)=-1\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{h\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\chi(h)T_p(h;\eta)
 =\chi(-1)\tau_p(\chi)\tau_p(\eta\bar\chi)
  S_p(\bar\chi)S_p(\bar\eta\chi).
 }
 \tag{1.8}
\]
For \(\eta=1\), this reduces to the familiar ratio-energy diagonalization.  Indeed, if
\[
 M_p(h)=\#\{(x,y)\in Z_p^2:x=hy\},
\]
then
\[
 T_p(h;1)=pM_p(h)-Z(p)^2,
 \tag{1.9}
\]
and for nontrivial \(\chi\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_h\chi(h)T_p(h;1)=p|S_p(\chi)|^2.
 }
 \tag{1.10}
\]
Equations (1.7)–(1.10) are the precise relation between the trivial ratio mode, the nontrivial completion modes, and \(S_p(\chi)\).

## 1.2 It is not a Hecke eigenvalue

No.  There are three separate reasons.

First, \(S_p(\chi)\) is a support statistic:
\[
 S_p(\chi)
 =\sum_r\chi(r)\,
 1_{\{b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}}.
 \tag{1.11}
\]
It depends only on whether a Frobenius/Mellin trace vanishes modulo the defining prime.  This is a nonlinear operation on the trace and is not itself a Frobenius trace of the same K3 representation.

Second, the geometric K3 family is indexed by \(t\), while \(r\) is the Mellin exponent in (0.1).  The pure rank-three K3 trace at a fiber and the coefficient-zero indicator in \(r\)-space are different observables.

Third, there is an index-group mismatch.  The Kummer character \(t\mapsto t^{-r}\) is indexed additively by \(r\bmod(p-1)\), whereas \(\chi(r)\) in (1.1) uses the multiplicative structure of \(r\in\mathbf F_p^\times\).  There is no natural operation “the K3 Hecke eigenvalue at \(\chi\)” that turns one into the other.

At most, after constructing the missing Mellin pushforward (0.2), one could hope to realize \(S_p(\chi)\), or a related centered quantity, as a complete twisted cohomological sum.  It would still be a complete trace sum, not one Hecke eigenvalue.

# 2. Exact trace-function complexity and the FKM–S gate

The phrase “the trace function” currently hides three distinct candidates.  They have very different status.

## 2.1 The bare completion character

If the \(v=q\ell\) dependence is only
\[
 K(v)=\eta(v),
\]
then \(K\) is the trace of a rank-one Kummer sheaf.  Under the usual conductor convention it has
\[
 \operatorname{rank}=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Sing}=\{0,\infty\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{cond}=3.
 \tag{2.1}
\]
This is bounded complexity, but it does not solve the Type-II problem:
\[
 \eta(q\ell)=\eta(q)\eta(\ell),
\]
so for separable coefficients the bilinear form factors exactly.  Moreover a rank-one Kummer sheaf is not *gallant* in the Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Sawin sense: its connected monodromy is abelian, not simple, and it has no nonabelian quasisimple core.

The stability under rank-one twists added in arXiv:2511.09459v3 says that a sheaf which is already gallant remains usable after an appropriate rank-one twist.  It does not make a bare Kummer sheaf gallant.

## 2.2 The exact additive zero-set transform \(F_p\)

There is a literal sheaf realizing \(F_p(a)\):
\[
 \mathscr F_p
 =\bigoplus_{r\in Z_p}\mathcal L_{\psi(rx)}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]
It has
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(\mathscr F_p)=Z(p),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Swan}_\infty(\mathscr F_p)=Z(p).
 \tag{2.3}
\]
With the standard conductor
\(\operatorname{rank}+\#\operatorname{Sing}+\sum\operatorname{Swan}\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{cond}(\mathscr F_p)=2Z(p)+1.
 }
 \tag{2.4}
\]
More importantly, (2.2) is a direct sum of rank-one Artin–Schreier sheaves.  Its geometric monodromy is reducible and abelian.  Thus it is neither bounded-complexity uniformly in \(p\) nor gallant.

## 2.3 The exact nontrivial ratio kernel

Normalize (1.7) by its Gauss sum.  As a function of \(h\), it is the trace of the middle extension of
\[
 \mathscr T_{p,\eta}
 =\bigoplus_{(r,s)\in Z_p^2}
   \mathcal L_{\bar\eta(r-sh)}.
 \tag{2.5}
\]
Let
\[
 R_p=\left|\left\{\frac rs:r,s\in Z_p\right\}\right|.
\]
Then the exact geometric data are
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(\mathscr T_{p,\eta})=Z(p)^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Sing}(\mathscr T_{p,\eta})
 \subseteq (Z_p/Z_p)\cup\{\infty\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Swan}=0.
 \tag{2.6}
\]
Under the same standard conductor convention,
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{cond}(\mathscr T_{p,\eta})
 =Z(p)^2+R_p+1
 \le 2Z(p)^2+1.
 }
 \tag{2.7}
\]
In particular the generic rank alone is exactly \(Z(p)^2\).  With the proved bound \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\), the worst-case complexity is
\[
 O(p^{4/3}).
 \tag{2.8}
\]
Again, (2.5) is a direct sum of rank-one Kummer pullbacks, so its monodromy is reducible and abelian.  It fails both the bounded-complexity and gallant-monodromy hypotheses.

The quantitative dependence on complexity in the general FKM–S theorem is not harmless here; the paper explicitly does not claim polynomial dependence in full generality.  A complexity growing like a positive power of \(p\) cannot be inserted into the theorem and absorbed into \(p^{o(1)}\).

## 2.4 The pure K3 connected tensor is bounded—but is the wrong function

On the geometric K3 base, the duality-even connected tensor square is
\[
 \mathcal C_2=\operatorname{Sym}^2_0(V)
 \simeq\operatorname{Sym}^4(H),
 \tag{2.9}
\]
of rank \(5\).  It has a fixed finite singular set and bounded conductor, and the expected geometric monodromy is the irreducible \(\operatorname{Sym}^4\) image of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), whose connected projective group is simple.  After a genuine global monodromy proof, this is the type of bounded-complexity gallant sheaf to which FKM–S could apply; a Kummer twist would not be an obstacle in v3.

But (2.9) is a sheaf in the geometric variable \(t\).  It does not have trace
\(1_{b_r=0}\), \(F_p(a)\), or (1.7) in the coefficient index \(r\).  The missing operation is precisely the defining-characteristic unit-root/Mellin pushforward, including its boundary and Hasse-divisor contributions.  No bounded-rank, bounded-conductor sheaf realizing that observable is currently constructed.

The situation can be summarized as follows.

```text
kernel/object                         rank/complexity          FKM–S?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
eta(v) (bare Kummer)                  rank 1, conductor 3      No: not gallant; factors
F_p(a) (zero-set Fourier transform)   rank Z(p), cond 2Z+1     No: reducible, growing
T_p(h;eta) (twisted ratio kernel)     rank Z(p)^2, O(Z^2)      No: reducible, growing
Sym^4(H) pure K3 connected piece      rank 5, O(1)             Geometrically plausible,
                                                               but wrong observable
missing unit-root/Mellin detector     unknown                  Not yet available
```

## 2.5 Additional analytic mismatch with the literal FKM–S theorem

The FKM–S Type-II theorem applies to bilinear forms of the form
\[
 \sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}
 \alpha_m\beta_n K(m^bn^c),
 \tag{2.10}
\]
where \(K\) is the trace of a gallant and light \(\ell\)-adic sheaf over \(\mathbf F_p\), \(\ell\ne p\), and the coefficients are separated.

The exact CED coefficient \(\beta_{p,a}(u)\) from Q7798 already contains

- the interval completion \(D_{L,pq\ell}(k)\);
- the two auxiliary factors \(F_q\) and \(F_\ell\);
- the congruence \((q\ell)^{-1}=u\pmod p\);
- joint dependence on \(q,\ell,k\).

It is not yet written as a controlled sum of separated forms (2.10).  Thus even a future bounded-complexity projector sheaf would require one more analytic reduction that separates the \(q\)- and \(\ell\)-weights without losing the CED saving.

The exact FKM–S theorem therefore does **not** currently close the nontrivial modes.  It is the correct analytic template only after both of the following are proved:

1. a bounded-complexity gallant \(r\)-indexed Mellin detector for the actual Apéry zero event;
2. a separation of the completed \(E_2\) weights into admissible Type-II forms.

# 3. Exact exponent comparison for \(v=q\ell\)

Let
\[
 X<q,\ell,p\le2X,
 \qquad
 v=q\ell.
\]
Then
\[
 X^2<v\le4X^2
 \tag{3.1}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac X2<\frac vp<4X.
 }
 \tag{3.2}
\]
Also \(X<\sqrt v\le2X\), so
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac12<\frac{p}{\sqrt v}<2,
 \qquad
 \frac12<\frac{v/p}{\sqrt v}<2.
 }
 \tag{3.3}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\log p}{\log v}
 =\frac12+O\!\left(\frac1{\log X}\right),
 \qquad
 \frac{\log(v/p)}{\log v}
 =\frac12+O\!\left(\frac1{\log X}\right).
 }
 \tag{3.4}
\]
This is the exact power comparison.

## 3.1 On the total semiprime scale

If \(Y\asymp X^2\) denotes the size of the semiprime sequence, classical Bombieri–Vinogradov is uniform for moduli of size roughly
\[
 Q\le Y^{1/2}(\log Y)^{-A}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]
Our modulus satisfies
\[
 p\asymp X\asymp Y^{1/2}.
 \tag{3.6}
\]
Therefore it is

- **not beyond Bombieri–Vinogradov by any fixed power**;
- exactly at exponent \(1/2\);
- logarithmically outside the standard log-shaved statement (3.5) when the entire dyadic interval \(p\asymp\sqrt Y\) is retained.

Thus an endpoint-strength BV/BDH theorem for the appropriately weighted semiprime convolution would have the right power range.  The current CED weights are much more structured than the plain semiprime indicator, so ordinary BV cannot simply be quoted.

## 3.2 On either prime-factor scale

Relative to \(q\asymp X\) or \(\ell\asymp X\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\log p}{\log q}=1+O\!\left(\frac1{\log X}\right),
 \qquad
 \frac{\log p}{\log\ell}=1+O\!\left(\frac1{\log X}\right).
 }
 \tag{3.7}
\]
Prime-level Bombieri–Vinogradov for the \(q\)-variable alone would only reach modulus exponent \(1/2\), while the actual modulus has exponent \(1\).  It is larger by a factor of order \(X^{1/2}\) on the power scale.  The same is true for \(\ell\).

This is why one must preserve the bilinear convolution \(v=q\ell\).  Applying BV separately to one prime factor is genuinely out of range.

## 3.3 Relation to the FKM–S Type-II length range

Here the two bilinear lengths are
\[
 M\asymp N\asymp X\asymp p,
 \qquad MN\asymp p^2.
 \tag{3.8}
\]
So the raw bilinear product is in a very long Type-II regime.  The explicit “below Pólya–Vinogradov” clause of FKM–S Theorem 1.3(2),
\[
 10p^{3/(2l)}\le N\le p^{1/2+3/(4l)},
 \tag{3.9}
\]
does not literally take \(N\asymp p\) as written.  Long intervals must be completed or partitioned and the resulting losses checked.  Numerically, however, \(MN\asymp p^2\) is far above the nontriviality threshold; the difficult issue is not that the variables are too short.  It is the absence of the correct bounded-complexity gallant kernel and the nonseparable CED weights.

# 4. Final answers to the three questions

1. **Explicit kernel.**
   \[
   S_p(\chi)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}\chi(r)
   =\tau_p(\bar\chi)^{-1}\sum_{a\ne0}\bar\chi(a)F_p(a)
   =-\sum_{r\ne0}\chi(r)b_r^{p-1}
   \]
   for nontrivial \(\chi\).  The full CED mode is (1.7), and its second Mellin transform is the product formula (1.8).  It is **not** a K3 Hecke eigenvalue.

2. **Trace complexity / FKM–S.**  The exact zero-set transform has rank \(Z(p)\) and conductor \(2Z(p)+1\); the exact twisted ratio kernel has rank \(Z(p)^2\) and conductor \(Z(p)^2+R_p+1\le2Z(p)^2+1\).  Both are reducible sums of rank-one sheaves, with growing complexity, so they fail the gallant/light bounded-complexity hypotheses.  The bounded rank-five pure K3 tensor is geometrically suitable but does not realize the coefficient-zero detector.  Hence **the current nontrivial CED modes do not fit FKM–S**.

3. **Range.**  On the total semiprime scale \(v\asymp X^2\), both \(p\) and \(v/p\) have exponent \(1/2\): the modulus is at the BV endpoint, logarithmically beyond the usual log-shaved formulation but not beyond by a fixed power.  Relative to either factor \(q\) or \(\ell\), the modulus has exponent \(1\), so factorwise BV is decisively out of range.
