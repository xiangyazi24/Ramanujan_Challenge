# Adversarial audit of the quadruple-corank exceptional-prime argument

## 1. What the quadruple estimate actually gives

Let

$$E_U(H,X)=\{p\in(X,2X]:p\mid \mathfrak U_H\}.$$

The structural carrier estimate gives only

$$|E_U(H,X)|\ll H^2/\log X.$$

This is an unweighted cardinality statement. The desired quantity is instead

$$\sum_{p\in E_U(H,X)} Z(p).$$

There is no implication

$$|E_U|\ll H^2/\log X \implies \sum_{p\in E_U}Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.$$

The missing factor is exactly the possible concentration of large zero sets on the exceptional primes.

## 2. Generic primes

For primes not dividing the carrier, the reduced resultant argument gives

$$\sum_{h_1+h_2+h_3\le H}\log\gcd(S^*_{h_1,h_2},S^*_{h_2,h_3})\ll H^{3+o(1)}.$$

A prime p counted by four zeros contributes roughly

$$\binom{Z(p)}4$$

quadruples. Hence after removing the exceptional carrier primes one obtains a generic bound of the shape

$$\sum_{p\notin E_U} Z(p)^4 \ll X^{o(1)}H^{3+o(1)} + \text{boundary losses}.$$

The block-boundary losses come from the incomplete representation of four zeros near the ends of the p-period. They are lower order provided H=o(X), because each block contributes O(H^3) possible gap triples and the number of boundary blocks is O(1).

This controls the generic contribution.

## 3. Exceptional primes are not controlled

For p dividing U_H we only know

$$|E_U|\ll H^2/\log X.$$

Using the established pointwise estimate

$$Z(p)\ll p^{2/3},$$

one obtains only

$$\sum_{p\in E_U}Z(p)
 \ll |E_U|X^{2/3}
 \ll \frac{H^2X^{2/3}}{\log X}.$$

At the claimed choice H=\sqrt X this becomes

$$\ll X^{5/3}/\log X,$$

which is larger than the required

$$X^{3/2+o(1)}.$$

Therefore the stated exceptional-prime count does not prove the claimed quadruple-corank bound.

## 4. Minimal missing lemma

A sufficient replacement is a weighted exceptional-carrier lemma:

$$\boxed{\sum_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid \mathfrak U_H}} Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}}$$

for H about sqrt(X).

Equivalent useful forms are, for example,

$$\sum_{j\le H}\sum_{p\mid b_j}Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)},$$

plus the analogous estimate for the extra carrier factors U_j(17).

This is substantially stronger than the existing average-zero information. It is essentially a restricted version of the pre-existing problem: control the average size of Z(p) on a thin but arithmetically selected set of primes.

## 5. Does the condition p|b_j force smaller Z(p)?

Not from the current lemmas.

The fact that j\in Z(p) gives one known zero of the zero set. The root-strip and gap-polynomial arguments give bounds on the whole set, but they do not show that a zero at a small residue reduces the number of other residues.

A hypothetical prime could have

$$j\in Z(p),\qquad |Z(p)|\asymp p^{2/3},$$

without contradicting any stated theorem. Hence no saving follows merely from membership in the carrier.

## 6. Saturation stripping issue

Deleting all prime powers whose prime divides U_H is logically dangerous if used without a separate argument.

If p divides U_H and also divides a resultant because of a genuine common root, then passing from S to S* removes exactly that p-adic contribution. The reduced gcd estimate then says nothing about that prime.

This is not a contradiction: the purpose of saturation is to move those primes into the exceptional set. But the exceptional set must then be handled with a weighted estimate. Removing them and retaining only an unweighted count loses precisely the information needed.

## Conclusion

The generic quadruple-corank argument is compatible with the claimed estimate, but the exceptional-prime step has a quantifier gap.

Known lemmas prove:

$$|E_U|\ll H^2/\log X,$$

and

$$\sum_{p\notin E_U}\text{(quadruple contribution)}\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.$$

They do not prove:

$$\sum_{p\in E_U}Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}.$$

The missing ingredient is a weighted exceptional-carrier bound. Without it, the claimed global quadruple-corank improvement is not established.
