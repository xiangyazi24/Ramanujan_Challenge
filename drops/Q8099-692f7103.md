# Actual coefficient coherence behind I5-R

## Verdict

I do not find a cross-characteristic theorem coming from the universal
square-root recurrences that closes I5-R.  The recurrences do give exact and
useful arithmetic identities, but after every carry, fold, endpoint, and
normalization is tracked, their prime support is exactly the old Apéry
coefficient support.

The strongest unconditional conclusions are the following.

1. **Exact universal convolution.**  Away from one explicitly treated defect
   center, an isolated hit is exactly the vanishing modulo \(p\) of a quadratic
   convolution in the universal \(c_n\)- or \(d_n\)-sequence.  After clearing
   denominators, that convolution is a \(p\)-unit multiple of the same integer
   \(b_h\).  It is not a second scalar.

2. **Exact common-index carrier.**  If
   \(m=a_pp+r_p<X^2\), then \(a_p<X<p\) and
   
   \[
   b_m\equiv b_{a_p}b_{r_p}\pmod p.
   \]
   
   Hence every actual hit \(p\mid b_{r_p}\) divides the one integer \(b_m\).
   Together with the Franel--Newton shell this gives a nonzero common carrier
   of logarithmic height \(O(X\log X)\).  Its height is still larger than
   \(L\log X\) by the power \(X^{1/15-\eta}\) in the only nontrivial range
   \(0<\eta<1/15\).

3. **A genuine quotient-digit reduction.**  If the quotient digit also
   vanishes, \(p\mid b_{a_p}\), and its folded zero is the same orbit as the
   selected folded zero, then the prime is determined by a divisor of \(m\).
   There are only \(X^{o(1)}\) such primes.  Otherwise the quotient zero is a
   second folded zero at distance \(>H\), producing only a long-gap
   certificate of height \(O(X\log X)\).  This does not close the star.

4. **Isolation supplies a nondivisibility, not a fifth equation.**  For a
   retained folded height \(h\), the product of all short-gap certificates has
   
   \[
   \log |D_{h,H}|=O(H^2\log X)
   =O\!\left(X^{14/15}(\log X)^{1/3}\right)=o(L).
   \]
   
   An isolated prime satisfies \(p\nmid D_{h,H}\).  This efficiently removes
   nearby returns, but it cannot be used as a prime-divisible companion.

5. **Exact local-algebra barrier.**  On the recurrence module, after all
   neighbor values required by isolation have been inverted, the marked locus
   is the reduced principal divisor \(b_h=0\).  Every identity forced for all
   recurrence germs is a multiple of \(b_h\).  The canonical Apéry companion
   is a unit there by the Wronskian.  Higher derivatives, local
   subresultants, and recurrence determinants therefore certify
   transversality; they do not give a second target valuation.

6. **Five characteristics remain componentwise.**  A five-point divided
   difference or determinant lives over five different residue fields.  CRT
   gives a direct product, not one field with five marked points.  Recurrence
   rank-two minors are either identically zero over characteristic zero or are
   not divisible by the five primes.  The only uniformly prime-divisible
   construction is product-like and has the old height.

The smallest clean additional theorem that closes I5-R is a **target-safe
primitive companion**: a nonzero integer \(\mathfrak C_X(m)\), built from the
*distinguished* Apéry normalization, for which all but \(O(H^2)\) isolated
hit primes at \(m\) divide \(\mathfrak C_X(m)\), and

\[
\log |\mathfrak C_X(m)|
\le X^{1-\delta+o(1)},
\qquad
\delta>\frac1{15}-\eta.
\tag{V}
\]

Indeed, (V) makes a point with \(K_{\rm iso}(m)\ge L\) impossible.  This is
strictly weaker in height than the current \(O(X\log X)\) shell carrier by
exactly the missing power.  An aggregate fivefold primary-saturated theorem
can replace (V), but it must encode the same new first-primary or horizontal
information; a second copy of the coefficient equation is not enough.

# 3. Lucas, quotients, folds, and every carry case

Gessel's Lucas congruence and (1.3) give the exact two-digit formula

\[
\boxed{b_m\equiv b_{a_p}b_{r_p}\pmod p.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Hence an actual row hit always satisfies

\[
p\mid b_{r_p}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid b_m.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The converse is false: \(p\mid b_m\) may be caused by \(p\mid b_{a_p}\).
This quotient channel cannot be silently deleted.

Reflection gives

\[
b_{r_p}\equiv b_{p-1-r_p}\equiv b_{h_p}\pmod p,
\tag{3.3}
\]

so a hit also divides the fixed integer \(b_{h_p}\).

## 3.1 Direct and reflected quotient formulas

There are exactly two orientations:

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{direct:}&r_p=h_p,&m=a_pp+h_p,\\[2mm]
\text{reflected:}&r_p=p-1-h_p,&m=(a_p+1)p-1-h_p.
\end{array}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\text{direct:}\quad p\mid m-h_p,
\qquad
\text{reflected:}\quad p\mid m+h_p+1.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For \(a_p>0\) these become

\[
p=\frac{m-h_p}{a_p}\quad\text{or}\quad
p=\frac{m+h_p+1}{a_p+1}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Substituting (3.6) into the \(c_n/d_n\) recurrence does not create a
polynomial relation in \(p,a_p,m\): the prime occurs in the *index* of a
P-recursive sequence.  Encoding all indices \(<X\) by interpolation or by a
transfer product costs degree \(\asymp X\) and logarithmic height
\(O(X\log X)\), exactly the shell scale.

## 3.2 Endpoint residues

The residues \(r_p=0\) and \(r_p=p-1\) cannot be hits:

\[
b_0=1,
\qquad b_{p-1}\equiv b_0=1\pmod p.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Consequently an immediate shift \(m\mapsto m\pm1\) never crosses a base-\(p\)
digit boundary at a hit.  Nevertheless its value can vanish through the
quotient digit, as follows.

## 3.3 Shift and carry audit

For \(|t|<p\), Lucas gives

\[
b_{m+t}\equiv
\begin{cases}
 b_{a_p}b_{r_p+t},&0\le r_p+t<p,\\
 b_{a_p+1}b_{r_p+t-p},&r_p+t\ge p,\\
 b_{a_p-1}b_{r_p+t+p},&r_p+t<0,\ m+t\ge0.
\end{cases}
\pmod p.
\tag{3.8}
\]

All quotient indices in (3.8) remain below \(p\).  Therefore row isolation
only controls the second factor.  It does **not** imply that physical
neighbors \(b_{m+t}\) are units unless the relevant quotient coefficient
\(b_{a_p-1},b_{a_p},b_{a_p+1}\) is also a unit.

In particular, no derivative or finite-difference argument may replace
“isolated coefficient zero in the \(p\)-row” by “simple zero of the physical
sequence at \(m\)” without first removing the quotient-zero channel.

## 3.4 When the quotient digit is also zero

Assume

\[
p\mid b_{a_p},
\qquad p\mid b_{r_p}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Fold \(a_p\) to

\[
\alpha_p=\min(a_p,p-1-a_p).
\]

If \(\alpha_p=h_p\), then \(a_p=r_p\) or
\(a_p=p-1-r_p\).  These two cases give

\[
a_p=r_p:\qquad m=a_p(p+1),\qquad p=\frac m{a_p}-1,
\tag{3.10a}
\]

\[
a_p=p-1-r_p:\qquad m=(a_p+1)(p-1),\qquad
p=\frac m{a_p+1}+1.
\tag{3.10b}
\]

Thus, for fixed \(m\), the same-orbit quotient channel contains at most

\[
2\tau(m)=X^{o(1)}
\tag{3.11}
\]

primes.

If \(\alpha_p\ne h_p\), isolation gives

\[
d_p:=|\alpha_p-h_p|>H.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Both first-half positions are actual zeros, so the ordinary Apéry gap
polynomial gives

\[
p\mid N_{d_p}(\min(\alpha_p,h_p)).
\tag{3.13}
\]

The exact degree is \(3(d_p-1)\), and the standard transfer bound gives

\[
\log\left|N_{d_p}(\min(\alpha_p,h_p))\right|
=O(d_p\log X)\le O(X\log X).
\tag{3.14}
\]

The polynomial and its evaluation vary with \(p\).  Equation (3.13) is a
long-gap row certificate, not a cross-prime carrier.  This is the strongest
rigorous use of the quotient digit furnished by (3.1).

# 4. The exact common carriers

Put

\[
Y_m=m(m+1),
\qquad
\lambda_h=h(h+1),
\qquad
B_X(Y)=\prod_{0\le h<X}(Y-\lambda_h).
\]

The elementary factorization

\[
Y_m-\lambda_h=(m-h)(m+h+1)
\tag{4.1}
\]

contains the complete direct/reflected carry ledger.

For every \(p\in(X,2X]\), the folded residue \(h_p\) satisfies \(h_p<X\),
and (3.5) gives

\[
p\mid B_X(Y_m).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus \(B_X(Y_m)\) is universal: it contains every window prime, not only an
Apéry hit.  If \(m\ge X\), then

\[
\boxed{
B_X(Y_m)
=\prod_{h=0}^{X-1}(m-h)(m+h+1)
=\frac{(m+X)!}{(m-X)!}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

If \(m<X\), the factor \(h=m\) makes \(B_X(Y_m)=0\), so this carrier gives
no height information in the low-anchor sector.

Let \(S_{X-1}(Y)\) be the normalized Franel--Newton prefix from the WIP
interface, with

\[
S_{X-1}(\lambda_h)=b_h
\qquad(0\le h<X).
\tag{4.4}

After multiplying by the standard factorial denominator, write
\(\widetilde S_{X-1}\in\mathbb Z[Y]\).  Because every \(p>X\) is a unit on

\[
p\mid \widetilde S_{X-1}(Y_m).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The value is nonzero: at \(Y_m\), the Newton terms are

\[
\phi_k(Y_m)=\binom mk\binom{m+k}{k},

with the terms beyond \(m\) equal to zero when \(m<X\), and all relevant
terms nonnegative.  Standard binomial bounds give

\[
\log\left|\widetilde S_{X-1}(Y_m)\right|=O(X\log X)
\qquad(m<X^2).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Every isolated hit therefore divides each of

\[
b_m,\qquad B_X(Y_m),\qquad \widetilde S_{X-1}(Y_m),
\tag{4.7}
\]

where the nonzero common carrier of best uniform height is the shell value
(or an appropriate gcd involving it).

The height ledger is

\[
\log b_m\le m\log64+O(\log m)=O(X^2),
\tag{4.8}
\]

\[
\log |B_X(Y_m)|=O(X\log X)\quad(m\ge X),
\tag{4.9}
\]

\[
\log |\widetilde S_{X-1}(Y_m)|=O(X\log X),
\tag{4.10}
\]

while an individual folded coefficient has logarithmic height \(O(X)\).

Since a point of load \(K\) has \(X^K\) dividing \(b_m\), (4.8) gives the
useful but insufficient range reduction

\[
K\ge L
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m\gg L\log X.
\tag{4.11}
\]

The shell carrier gives only \(K=O(X)\).  In the nontrivial range (1.1),

\[
\frac{X\log X}{L\log X}=X^{1/15-\eta},
\tag{4.12}
\]

which is the exact missing power.

## 4.1 Fixed folded height multiplicity

For a fixed \(m,h\) with \(m\ne h\), every associated prime divides one of
the two nonzero integers \(m-h\) or \(m+h+1\), each of size \(O(X^2)\).
Each can have at most two distinct prime factors larger than \(X\).  Hence at
most four window primes use that same folded height.

The sole exception is the direct low-anchor case

\[
m=h<X,
\tag{4.13}
\]

where \(m-h=0\).  Then all such primes divide the one moving integer \(b_m\).
This exception is exactly the moving-anchor quantifier obstruction identified
in `oracleB_result.tex`; it is not covered by the fixed-anchor proposition
with \(m\) independent of \(X\).

# 5. What isolation contributes

For an interior first-half height \(h\), define the short-return obstruction

\[
D_{h,H}
=\prod_{1\le d\le H}N_d(h)
 \prod_{1\le d\le\min(H,h)}N_d(h-d).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The positivity/root-strip results for \(N_d\) make this a nonzero integer.
If a second folded Apéry zero occurs within distance \(H\), the associated
prime divides \(D_{h,H}\).  Therefore an isolated prime satisfies

\[
\boxed{p\nmid D_{h,H}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The degree-\(3(d-1)\) transfer bound gives

\[
\log |D_{h,H}|
\ll\sum_{d\le H}d\log X
\ll H^2\log X.
\tag{5.3}
\]

At the prescribed scale,

\[
H^2\log X
=X^{14/15}(\log X)^{1/3}=o(L).
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is a genuine power-level gain, but its sign is wrong for a carrier:
(5.2) says that a target prime avoids a small integer.  Once universal
reflection collisions have been folded/saturated, an isolated singleton has
no remaining short-gap equation.  No product formula converts (5.2) into a
new integer divisible by the target prime.

# 6. Exact recurrence-local no-go

The coefficient sequence \(b_n\) satisfies the order-two Apéry recurrence.
On an interval strictly inside \([0,p-1]\), every transfer denominator is a
\(p\)-unit.  Use the two consecutive state coordinates

\[
x=u_h,\qquad y=u_{h+1}.
\]

Every nearby value of an arbitrary recurrence germ has the form

\[
u_{h+t}=\alpha_t(h)x+\beta_t(h)y
\tag{6.1}
\]

with coefficients in a localization of \(\mathbb F_p\).  Localize further by
inverting all values required to be nonzero by isolation.  The resulting
coordinate ring has the form

\[
R=\mathbb F_p[x,y,U^{-1}],
\tag{6.2}
\]

where \(U\) is a product of the relevant linear forms.  The marked locus is

\[
x=0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Since a nonzero germ cannot have two consecutive zero values, \(y\) is a
unit on this locus.  Consequently

\[
R/(x)\cong\mathbb F_p[y,U(0,y)^{-1}]
\]

is a domain, and

\[
\sqrt{(x)}=(x).
\tag{6.4}
\]

**Therefore every polynomial identity that is forced by the recurrence and
isolation for all marked germs is a multiple of the original marked
coordinate.**  Clearing transfer denominators merely multiplies by \(p\)-units.
This covers local derivatives, finite differences, transfer minors, and
local subresultants built only from the recurrence module.

The distinguished Apéry companion makes the transversality explicit.  The
Wronskian in `proof.tex` is

\[
a_{h+1}b_h-a_hb_{h+1}=\frac6{(h+1)^3}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

At a hit \(b_h=0\pmod p\), with \(h<(p-1)/2+1\),

\[
-a_hb_{h+1}\equiv\frac6{(h+1)^3}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus both the companion value and the neighboring Apéry value are units.
The canonical rank-two determinant does not vanish; it proves that the
marked divisor is reduced.

The two universal square-root series do not supply two independent
companions: they satisfy

\[
C_-=C_+/\sqrt\Delta.
\]

They are gauge-related descriptions selected by the quadratic character of
\(p\), not two simultaneous target equations.

The conclusion of this section is deliberately scoped.  It says that the
**operator and isolation alone** cannot close I5-R.  It does not rule out a
new identity using the exact distinguished initial normalization of the
universal series.

# 7. Why five-prime determinants and resultants do not couple

Let \(p_1,\ldots,p_5\) be distinct isolated hit primes with folded heights
\(h_1,\ldots,h_5\).

## 7.1 Divided differences

Even when the integer heights are distinct, the data are

\[
b_{h_i}=0\pmod{p_i},
\qquad 1\le i\le5.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Modulo \(p_i\), the other four integers \(b_{h_j}\) are unrestricted.  Hence
a five-point divided-difference numerator is not forced to vanish modulo
\(p_i\).  The Vandermonde differences \(|h_i-h_j|<X<p_i\) are units, which
removes denominators but adds no zero.

If several \(h_i\) coincide, Section 4.1 handles the multiplicity; the
low-anchor exception reduces to the one integer \(b_m\), not a divided
difference.

## 7.2 Rank-two recurrence determinants

For recurrence state vectors \(v_h=(b_h,b_{h+1})\), every \(3\times3\)
minor is identically zero over characteristic zero because the state space is
two-dimensional.  Such a zero integer cannot carry primes.  A \(2\times2\)
minor involving \(h_i,h_j\) reduces modulo \(p_i\) to

\[
-b_{h_i+1}b_{h_j},
\]

which is not forced to vanish.  Making the whole \(i\)-th row divisible by
\(p_i\) requires multiplying it by \(b_{h_i}\), returning a diagonal/product
carrier.

The same statement applies to transfer vectors from the \(c_n\)- or
\(d_n\)-recurrence: the coefficient condition is the quadratic convolution
(2.6±), not vanishing of one transfer coordinate.

## 7.3 CRT and tensor products

CRT gives

\[
\mathbb Z/(p_1\cdots p_5)\mathbb Z
\cong\prod_{i=1}^5\mathbb F_{p_i}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

A determinant or subresultant over the right side is evaluated
componentwise.  It is not one determinant over a field containing five
marked points.

Equivalently, if \(A_i\) is a \(p_i\)-torsion root or coefficient algebra,
then for \(i\ne j\)

\[
A_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A_j=0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Indeed, choose \(u,v\) with \(up_i+vp_j=1\) and apply this identity to every
pure tensor.  A nonzero mixed norm requires a new characteristic-zero
horizontal lift.

## 7.4 Subresultants and discriminants

At the selected shell node, after the universal reflection collision is
saturated, \(B_X\) has a simple local factor.  Hence the selected local gcd
with the shell prefix has multiplicity one.  Higher principal subresultants
can vanish because of *other far-away coefficient zeros*, but that is a
vertical zero-count statistic and does not distinguish the selected star.

Isolation does not imply \(S_{X-1}'(\lambda_h)\ne0\).  The derivative is a
weighted linear combination of all shell values, not a neighboring
coefficient.  The Wronskian

\[
B_X'S_{X-1}-B_XS_{X-1}'
\]

at \(\lambda_h\) is \(B_X'(\lambda_h)b_h\), exactly the old scalar.  After
saturating reflection, \(B_X'(\lambda_h)\) is a unit.  Higher derivatives
have no forced target valuation.

## 7.5 Height of generic elimination

The input parameters are

\[
\deg B_X=X,
\qquad
\deg S_{X-1}=X-1,
\qquad
h(B_X),h(S_{X-1})=O(X\log X).
\]

A Sylvester/Nullstellensatz certificate pays the product of the growing
degrees and has natural logarithmic height \(O(X^2\log X)\).  The exact
resultant is better but tautological:

\[
\operatorname{Res}(B_X,S_{X-1})
=\prod_{h=0}^{X-1}S_{X-1}(\lambda_h)
=\prod_{h=0}^{X-1}b_h
\tag{7.4}
\]

up to the declared normalization.  Its logarithmic height is
\(O(X^2)\), and it is the old product of coefficients.

# 8. Exact-operator countermodel for recurrence-only transforms

The following scoped construction shows that the failure in Section 6 is
sharp.  It is **not** a counterexample to the distinguished Apéry sequence;
it changes the initial germ while retaining the exact Apéry operator.

Fix \(0<\eta<1/15\), put

\[
L=\lceil X^{14/15+\eta}\rceil,
\qquad
h_0=\lfloor X/3\rfloor.
\]

Then \(H<h_0\), and every \(p\in(X,2X]\) has
\(p>2h_0+1\), so the folded physical residue of \(m=h_0\) is \(h_0\).
Let

\[
D=D_{h_0,H}.
\]

By (5.3), only \(O(H^2)\) window primes divide \(D\).  Since

\[
L+H^2=o(X/\log X),
\]

choose \(L\) distinct primes in \((X,2X]\) not dividing \(D\).

Use the factorial-scaled exact Apéry recurrence

\[
U_{n+1}=P(n)U_n-n^6U_{n-1},
\qquad
P(n)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5).
\tag{8.1}
\]

For each selected \(p\), evaluation at \(h_0<p\) is a nonzero linear
functional of the initial pair \((U_0,U_1)\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).  Choose a
nonzero vector in its kernel.  The next value is nonzero by invertibility of
the transfer.

CRT glues these projective local vectors to one integer pair \((A,B)\) modulo

\[
Q=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{selected}}p.
\]

No selected prime divides both coordinates.  Divide by
\(\gcd(A,B)\) to make the pair primitive without changing any selected local
projective germ.  One may take

\[
\log\max(|A|,|B|)\le\log Q
=O(L\log X)=o(X).
\tag{8.2}
\]

The zero sets of every germ of the Apéry recurrence have the exact reflection
\(n\leftrightarrow p-1-n\): reflect the recurrence using
\(P(-n-1)=-P(n)\), match the two center values, and propagate by invertible
transfer.  If the chosen germ had another folded zero within \(H\) of
\(h_0\), the two-zero theorem would force \(p\mid D\), contrary to the prime
selection.  Thus every selected zero is \(H\)-isolated.

At the common physical point \(m=h_0\), this one primitive recurrence germ has

\[
K_{\rm iso}(h_0)\ge L.
\tag{8.3}
\]

The terms through \(n\le X\) have logarithmic height \(O(X\log X)\) by
(8.1).  Hence the exact order-two operator, reflection, actual gap
polynomials, primitive initial content, subexponential initial height, and
perfect isolation do not imply I5-R uniformly over recurrence germs.

A successful use of \(c_n/d_n\) must therefore use their **distinguished
normalization**, not just their common operator, degree, denominator, or
local-transfer consequences.

# 9. Literature audit with parameter substitution

No standard theorem I found has the required moving-coefficient,
moving-characteristic quantifiers.

1. **Gessel's Lucas theorem** applies exactly and yields (3.1).  Its output is
   the common integer \(b_m\), whose height is \(O(m)\), not a sub-\(L\)
   companion.  Primary source: Ira Gessel,
   [“Some congruences for Apéry numbers,” JNT 14 (1982), 362--368](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(82)90071-3).

2. **Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin** give the local
   square/quadratic-times-square factorization and the universal truncations.
   Their theorem is prime-by-prime; it does not identify coefficient zeros
   with evaluation roots or couple different characteristics.  Primary
   source: [arXiv:2510.23298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23298), published as
   [doi:10.1017/S0004972725100932](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972725100932).

3. **Skolem--Mahler--Lech and its P-recursive variants** concern zeros in
   characteristic zero of one fixed sequence.  The Bell--Burris--Yeats theorem
   assumes, in its stated form, a recurrence whose last polynomial coefficient
   is a nonzero constant.  The factorial-scaled Apéry recurrence has trailing
   coefficient \(-n^6\), and I5-R asks for reductions modulo a characteristic
   that moves with the index.  Primary source:
   [Bell--Burris--Yeats, MPCPS 153 (2012), 235--247](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004112000114).

4. **Subspace-theorem gcd bounds** such as Bugeaud--Corvaja--Zannier concern
   fixed multiplicatively independent bases (or fixed \(S\)-unit varieties).
   Here the pair of polynomials and the selected indices grow with \(X\), and
   \(m\mapsto h_p\) changes with the same prime being counted.  Substituting
   the present parameters gives no uniform constant.  Primary source:
   [Math. Z. 243 (2003), 79--84](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-002-0449-z).

5. **Arithmetic Nullstellensatz** bounds retain the degree product.  With
   degrees \(X,X-1\) and input height \(O(X\log X)\), they live at the
   \(O(X^2\log X)\) scale, not \(X^{14/15+\eta}\).  Primary source:
   [Krick--Pardo--Sombra, arXiv:math/9911094](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9911094).

6. **G-function Padé theorems** control denominators and Diophantine
   approximation to fixed values of fixed G-functions.  They do not bound the
   set of moving primes for which a moving coefficient coordinate vanishes.
   Primary source: D. V. and G. V. Chudnovsky,
   [LNM 1135 (1985), 9--51](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0074600).

The exact-operator construction in Section 8 explains why an operator-only
G-function theorem cannot be enough.

# 10. The smallest additional arithmetic theorem

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X^{\rm iso}(m)
=\{p\in(X,2X]:p\mid b_{m\bmod p},\ h_p\text{ retained and isolated}\},
\]

and \(K_{\rm iso}(m)=|\mathcal P_X^{\rm iso}(m)|\).

A concise sufficient input is the following.

> **Target-safe primitive companion (TSPC).**  There are
> \(\delta>1/15-\eta\) and nonzero integers
> \(\mathfrak C_X(m)\), constructed from the distinguished Apéry
> coefficient system, such that uniformly for \(m<X^2\):
> 
> \[
> \#\{p\in\mathcal P_X^{\rm iso}(m):p\nmid\mathfrak C_X(m)\}
> \ll H^2,
> \tag{10.1}
> \]
> 
> and
> 
> \[
> \log|\mathfrak C_X(m)|
> \le X^{1-\delta+o(1)}.
> \tag{10.2}
> \]

Indeed,

\[
K_{\rm iso}(m)-O(H^2)
\le \omega_X(\mathfrak C_X(m))
\le\frac{\log|\mathfrak C_X(m)|}{\log X}.
\tag{10.3}
\]

But

\[
\frac{H^2}{L}	o0,
\qquad
\frac{X^{1-\delta+o(1)}/\log X}{L}	o0
\]

precisely when \(\delta>1/15-\eta\).  Thus no point reaches the I5-R tail;
this closes I5-R more strongly than required.

The current shell carrier has \(\delta=0\).  The missing theorem must save

\[
X^{1/15-\eta+o(1)}
\tag{10.4}
\]

in characteristic-zero height.

In terms of the universal square roots, TSPC would have to use a genuinely
new **first-primary coefficient relation**.  At a hit, the zeroth-order
quadratic convolution in (2.6±) is already \(b_h\).  Neighbor
nonvanishing only says that the marked divisor is simple.  What is missing is
a target-safe primitive scalar—built, for example, from the first divided
\(p\)-adic convolution of the *actual normalized* \(c_n/d_n\)—whose reduction
vanishes coherently for changing \(p\), is not a multiple of \(b_h\), and has
the height (10.2).

An aggregate primary-saturated fivefold theorem can be weaker than TSPC: it
may attach carriers only to tail-selected ordered five-tuples and control
their total primitive height.  But it must still provide a valuation not
contained in the principal local ideal \((b_h)\).  Without that extra
first-primary or horizontal relation, it is only a repackaging of the five
divisibilities.

# Final ruling

The universal \(c_n/d_n\) recurrence supplies exact coefficient coherence,
but that coherence is vertical:

\[
p\mid b_h
\iff
p\mid\text{one explicit universal quadratic convolution at }h.
\]

After clearing \(p\)-unit denominators, the convolution is the old integer
\(b_h\).  Lucas moves every hit to the one common integer \(b_m\), while the
Franel--Newton shell compresses its window-prime support to a common value of
height \(O(X\log X)\).  Quotient zeros either form an \(X^{o(1)}\)
same-orbit sector or produce only a long-gap equation.  Isolation contributes
the nondivisibility of a carrier of height \(O(H^2\log X)=o(L)\), and the
Wronskian proves that the local mark is transverse.

Therefore none of the tested transforms—Lucas alone, quotient substitution,
universal recurrence convolution, recurrence resultants, five-prime
determinants, neighbor derivatives, saturated subresultants, or standard
G-function/gcd theorems—beats \(L=X^{14/15+\eta}\).  The exact remaining gap
is a characteristic-zero companion height saving of
\(X^{1/15-\eta+o(1)}\), or an equivalent tail-selected fivefold
primary-saturated cancellation theorem using the distinguished Apéry
normalization across changing characteristics.
