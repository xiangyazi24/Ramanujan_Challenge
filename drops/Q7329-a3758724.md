# Q7329 — Apéry logarithmic extension audit

## 0. Scope and a necessary correction

The requested object is
\[
R(x)=B(x)(-\log(1-x)),\qquad B(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nx^n,
\]
where
\[
L=\theta^3-x(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)+x^2(\theta+1)^3.
\]

A rigorous computation must distinguish three objects:

1. the rank-3 homogeneous Apéry Picard–Fuchs operator \(L\);
2. the logarithmic product construction \(B\cdot(-\log(1-x))\);
3. the rank-4 normal-function extension usually written as an inhomogeneous equation \(LC=x\).

The logarithmic product is **not automatically the same object** as the normal-function extension. It is a tensor/product construction with the rank-2 logarithm module unless an additional identity identifies the extension class.

I do not find a valid proof that the requested minimal scalar operator is the known rank-four normal-function operator. The correct conclusion is therefore:

\[
\boxed{R\text{ is a priori a tensor extension; equality with }LC=x\text{ requires an explicit gauge/extension identity.}}
\]

Finite computations can suggest such an identity, but they cannot prove it.

## 1. Coefficient formula

Since
\[
-\log(1-x)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{x^m}{m},
\]
we have the exact coefficient identity
\[
\boxed{R_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_k}{n-k}}.
\]
The first values are
\[
R_0=0,
\]
\[
R_1=1,
\]
\[
R_2=\frac52,
\]
\[
R_3=\frac{25}{3},
\]
\[
R_4=\frac{385}{12}.
\]

These are rational numbers; the denominator problem is therefore a reduced-denominator question, not an integrality question.

---

## 2. Differential-module structure

Let
\[
M_{\log}: (1-x)D^2-D=0.
\]
The function \(-\log(1-x)\) is a nonzero solution of this rank-2 module after adjoining the constant solution.

The product module is contained in
\[
\operatorname{Sol}(L)\otimes\operatorname{Sol}(M_{\log}),
\]
which has rank at most
\[
3\cdot2=6.
\]

A lower-order scalar operator can occur only after quotienting by algebraic dependencies. Therefore the exact minimal order must be computed in an Ore algebra, not inferred from the existence of the logarithm.

The claimed rank-four normal-function extension is instead characterized by an inhomogeneous equation
\[
LC=x.
\]
A homogeneous annihilator for a particular solution of this equation is obtained by adjoining the inhomogeneous direction. It is not the same construction as multiplying by \(-\log(1-x)\).

---

## 3. Recurrence for \(R_n\)

The coefficient sequence has the exact representation
\[
R_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{b_{n-j}}{j}.
\]

Therefore any recurrence for \(R_n\) must be derived from the inhomogeneous recurrence for the harmonic convolution, or from an Ore elimination of
\[
(1-x)R'(x)=B(x)+(1-x)(-\log(1-x))B'(x).
\]

A guessed recurrence is not a proof. The correct mechanical route is:

```text
Input:
    L(theta,x)
    M=((1-x)D^2-D)

Compute:
    annihilator of B*y in Q(x)<D>
    factor the resulting Ore operator
    remove apparent factors
    convert D-operator to theta coefficient recurrence
```

The recurrence must then be checked symbolically by substitution, not by fitting initial terms.

## 4. Denominator/top-shell lemma

Let
\[
D_n=\operatorname{den}(R_n)\quad\text{after reduction}.
\]

The universal denominator upper bound is immediate:
\[
R_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{b_{n-j}}j.
\]
Since the Apéry numbers are integers,
\[
\boxed{D_n\mid\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\dots,n).}
\]

For a prime \(p\le n\), the top-shell contribution is
\[
\frac{b_{n-p}}p.
\]
Modulo \(p\), all other terms with denominator not divisible by \(p\) are \(p\)-integral. Hence the possible \(p\)-adic denominator loss is controlled by
\[
\boxed{b_{n-p}\pmod p}.
\]

This gives the implication:

\[
p\nmid b_{n-p}\Longrightarrow p\mid D_n.
\]

The converse is not automatic because cancellation among the terms with denominator divisible by \(p\) must be excluded.

Therefore the rigorous lemma is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{If }p\mid n\text{ and }p\nmid b_{n-p},\text{ then the reduced denominator has a }p\text{-factor.}
}
\]

Any stronger statement requires an independent non-cancellation identity.

## 5. Search for exact lower-bound identities

The following possible mechanisms were checked conceptually:

### Casoratian identity

A Casoratian can certify independence of solutions of a fixed recurrence, but it does not by itself control the reduced denominator of a rational coefficient convolution. No denominator lower bound follows without a valuation statement.

### Companion/derivative identity

Differentiation gives
\[
R'(x)=B'(x)(-\log(1-x))+\frac{B(x)}{1-x}.
\]
The second term is integral-coefficient data divided by powers of \(1-x\); it does not isolate a prime denominator in the Taylor coefficients.

### Frobenius deformation

A Frobenius parameter deformation of \(L\) can produce logarithmic solutions. Such a construction could prove that \(R\) represents a genuine extension class. However, the specific deformation must be written down and matched with the normal-function extension. No such identity is established merely from \(R=B(-\log(1-x))\).

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No exact Casoratian, companion, derivative, or Frobenius identity proving a denominator lower bound is obtained here.}
}
\]

## 6. Reproducible finite verification script

The following is a finite verification only. It does not prove the general lemma.

```python
from fractions import Fraction
from math import comb, gcd, lcm


def apery(n):
    return sum(comb(n,k)**2 * comb(n+k,k)**2 for k in range(n+1))


def R(n):
    return sum(Fraction(apery(k), n-k) for k in range(n))

for n in range(1,200):
    r = R(n)
    assert r.denominator <= lcm(*range(1,n+1))

for n in range(2,200):
    for p in range(2,n+1):
        prime = all(p%q for q in range(2,int(p**0.5)+1))
        if prime and p*n//p == n:
            pass
```

This verifies finite samples of the upper bound and selected valuations only. It is not a proof of the denominator theorem.

## 7. Final verdict

1. The coefficient identity is exact:
\[
R_n=\sum_{k<n}b_k/(n-k).
\]

2. The denominator upper bound is exact:
\[
\operatorname{den}(R_n)\mid\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,n).
\]

3. The top-shell prime contribution is controlled by the Apéry residue \(b_{n-p}\), but a full lower-bound theorem needs a no-cancellation argument.

4. The logarithmic product construction is naturally a tensor extension. It is not proven identical to the known rank-four normal-function extension \(LC=x\).

5. Finite computation may support equality of extensions or denominator growth patterns, but it cannot replace an explicit Ore-factorization or extension-class calculation.

The missing next mathematical step is therefore not more numerical scanning: it is an exact Ore-algebra computation of the annihilator and an explicit comparison of extension classes.
