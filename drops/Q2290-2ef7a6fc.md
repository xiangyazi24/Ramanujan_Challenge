The decisive adjacent identity exists, and it is stronger than the requested nonvanishing statement.

Let

\[
D(n,k)=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k},
\qquad
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^nD(n,k)^2,
\]

and

\[
K(r,s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(r,s)}D(r,k)D(s,k).
\]

Normalize the Apéry companion by

\[
a_0=0,\qquad a_1=6,
\]

with the same recurrence as \(b\). Thus the companion called \(c\) in
`problems/3.2/gap_kernel_green.tex`, normalized by \((c_0,c_1)=(0,1)\), is
exactly

\[
c_n=\frac{a_n}{6}.
\]

For every integer \(r\ge1\), one has the exact integer identity

\[
\boxed{6K(r,r-1)=b_r+b_{r-1}.}
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently, if \(p>3\), \(1\le r\le p-2\), and

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

then the companion Casoratian gives

\[
b_{r-1}a_r=\frac6{r^3}\pmod p,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r-1)
 =\frac{b_{r-1}}6
 =-\frac{(r+1)^3}{6r^3}b_{r+1}
 =\frac1{r^3a_r}
 =\frac1{6r^3c_r}
 \ne0\pmod p.}
\tag{2}
\]

Equivalently, the adjacent kernel has the literal unit certificate

\[
\boxed{r^3a_rK(r,r-1)=1.}
\tag{3}
\]

The forward adjacent value satisfies

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+1)=\frac{b_{r+1}}6
        =-\frac1{(r+1)^3a_r},}
\tag{4}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{(r+1)^3a_rK(r,r+1)=-1.}
\tag{5}
\]

This proves the weighted assertion exactly. If

\[
W_r:=\sum_{k=0}^{r}D(r,k)^2\frac{k}{r+k},

then over \(\mathbf Q\)

\[
\boxed{W_r=\frac{5b_r-b_{r-1}}{12}.}
\tag{6}

Therefore, on \(b_r=0\),

\[
\boxed{
W_r
=-\frac{b_{r-1}}{12}

=\frac{(r+1)^3}{12r^3}b_{r+1}
e0\pmod p.}
\tag{7}

# 1. Fraction-free proof of the adjacent identity

Put

\[
d_k=D(r,k),\qquad e_k=D(r-1,k),
\]

with \(e_r=0\) and \(d_{-1}=0\). Elementary binomial ratios give

\[
e_k=\frac{r-k}{r+k}d_k,
\tag{8}
\]

and, for \(1\le k\le r\),

\[
\frac{d_k}{d_{k-1}}
 =\frac{(r-k+1)(r+k)}{k^2}.
\tag{9}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
(r-k+1)^2d_{k-1}^2
=\frac{k^4}{(r+k)^2}d_k^2.
\tag{10}
\]

The exact termwise certificate is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&r^2\bigl(6d_ke_k-d_k^2-e_k^2\bigr)\\
&\qquad=4\Bigl((r-k)^2d_k^2
 -(r-k+1)^2d_{k-1}^2\Bigr).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{11}
\]

Indeed, by (8),

\[
6d_ke_k-d_k^2-e_k^2
=d_k^2\left(
6\frac{r-k}{r+k}-1-\frac{(r-k)^2}{(r+k)^2}
\right)
=\frac{4(r^2-2k^2)}{(r+k)^2}d_k^2.
\]

On the other hand, (10) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(r-k)^2d_k^2-(r-k+1)^2d_{k-1}^2\\
&\quad=d_k^2\left((r-k)^2-\frac{k^4}{(r+k)^2}\right)\\
&\quad=\frac{r^2(r^2-2k^2)}{(r+k)^2}d_k^2,
\end{aligned}
\]

which is (11).

Sum (11) over \(k=0,\ldots,r\). Its right side telescopes to zero. The left side becomes

\[
r^2\left(
6\sum_{k=0}^rd_ke_k
-\sum_{k=0}^rd_k^2
-\sum_{k=0}^re_k^2
\right).
\]

The three sums are respectively

\[
K(r,r-1),\qquad b_r,\qquad b_{r-1}.
\]

Since this is an integer equality and \(r\ge1\), division by the nonzero integer \(r^2\) gives

\[
6K(r,r-1)=b_r+b_{r-1}.
\]

The certificate (11) itself is fraction-free, so it can also be reduced directly modulo any prime.

K(r,r-1)

   \left(1-\frac{2k}{r+k}\right)\\

b_r-2W_r=\frac{b_r+b_{r-1}}6.

# 3. Companion Casoratian and Green normalization

The companion-normalized Green formula in the repository is

\[
\frac{N_G(u)}{\prod_{j=1}^G(u+j)^3}
=b_uc_{u+G}-c_ub_{u+G}.
\tag{14}
\]

Since \(a=6c\), this is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
b_ua_{u+G}-a_ub_{u+G}
=6\frac{N_G(u)}{\prod_{j=1}^G(u+j)^3}.}
\tag{15}
\]

For \(G=1\), \(N_1=1\). At \(u=r-1\), equation (15) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
b_{r-1}a_r-a_{r-1}b_r=\frac6{r^3}.}
\tag{16}
\]

At a target \(b_r=0\), it reduces to

\[
\boxed{b_{r-1}a_r=\frac6{r^3}.}
\tag{17}
\]

Because \(p>3\) and \(0<r<p\), the right side is a unit. Therefore both

\[
b_{r-1}\ne0,\qquad a_r\ne0
\]

follow simultaneously. This is stronger than invoking the no-consecutive-zero lemma: the Casoratian supplies an explicit inverse.

Combining (17) with (13) gives

\[
K(r,r-1)=\frac{b_{r-1}}6=\frac1{r^3a_r},
\]

which proves (2)--(3).

In the \(c_0=0,c_1=1\) normalization, (17) reads

\[
r^3b_{r-1}c_r=1,
\]

and therefore

\[
K(r,r-1)=\frac1{6r^3c_r}.
\]

This is the exact normalization of the companion-coordinate observation in Q2278.

# 4. Exact formula involving \(b_{r+1}\)

The Apéry recurrence at index \(r\) is

\[
(r+1)^3b_{r+1}=P(r)b_r-r^3b_{r-1}.
\tag{18}
\]

Using (1), one obtains the exact rational identity valid before imposing \(b_r=0\):

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r-1)
=\frac{(P(r)+r^3)b_r-(r+1)^3b_{r+1}}{6r^3}.}
\tag{19}
\]

On the target \(b_r=0\), this becomes

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r-1)
=-\frac{(r+1)^3}{6r^3}b_{r+1}.}
\tag{20}
\]

The Green formula at \(u=r,G=1\) gives

\[
b_ra_{r+1}-a_rb_{r+1}=\frac6{(r+1)^3}.
\tag{21}
\]

Thus, on \(b_r=0\),

\[
\boxed{a_rb_{r+1}=-\frac6{(r+1)^3}.}
\tag{22}
\]

This proves directly that \(b_{r+1}\) is also a unit, and substituting (22) into (20) again gives

\[
K(r,r-1)=\frac1{r^3a_r}.
\]

Finally, applying (1) with \(r+1\) in place of \(r\), and using symmetry of \(K\), gives

\[
6K(r,r+1)=b_r+b_{r+1}.
\]

Hence on \(b_r=0\),

\[
K(r,r+1)=\frac{b_{r+1}}6=-\frac1{(r+1)^3a_r},
\]

which proves (4)--(5) and the stated Racah propagation ratio

\[
K(r,r+1)=-\frac{r^3}{(r+1)^3}K(r,r-1).
\]

\boxed{
K(r,r-1)
=\frac1{r^3a_r}
 +\frac{a_r+a_{r-1}}{6a_r}\,b_r.}

Thus the target formula is not a guessed congruence. It is the specialization of an exact rational identity:

b_r=0

Equations (19) and (23) are the requested two exact descriptions, respectively in the adjacent primary coordinate \(b_{r+1}\) and the companion coordinate \(a_r\).

# 7. What this does—and does not—prove for arbitrary \(s\)

Fix \(r\), assume \(b_r=0\), and put

\[
Y_h:=K(r,r+h).
\]

The Racah recurrence in the second index is

\[
\begin{aligned}
(r+h+1)^3Y_{h+1}
={}&A_{r,h}Y_h-(r+h)^3Y_{h-1},
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

where

\[
A_{r,h}
=(r+h+1)^3+(r+h)^3
 +2(2r+2h+1)r(r+1).
\tag{25}
\]

Define the normalized Racah propagator by

\[
C_0=0,\qquad C_1=1,
\]

and the same recurrence (24). Since

\[
Y_0=K(r,r)=b_r=0,\qquad
Y_1=K(r,r+1)\ne0,
\]

uniqueness gives

\[
\boxed{K(r,r+h)=K(r,r+1)C_h.}
\tag{26}
\]

Equivalently, define the cleared Racah continuant

\[
R_0=0,\qquad R_1=1,
\]

\[
\boxed{
R_{h+1}=A_{r,h}R_h-(r+h)^6R_{h-1}.}
\tag{27}
\]

Then

\[
C_h=\frac{R_h}{\prod_{j=2}^{h}(r+j)^3},
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)
=-\frac1{(r+1)^3a_r}
 \frac{R_h(r)}{\prod_{j=2}^{h}(r+j)^3}.}
\tag{28}
\]

All displayed denominator factors are units on a nonwrapping interval \(r+h<p\). Since the first scalar in (28) is now a proved unit,

\[
\boxed{K(r,r+h)=0\iff R_h(r)=0.}
\tag{29}
\]

This is the exact gain from the present attack: it removes every uncertainty about the initial amplitude and isolates the sole remaining factor.

Now let \(s=r+h\) be a second Apéry zero. The repository Green formula (15) gives

\[
6\frac{N_h(r)}{\prod_{j=1}^{h}(r+j)^3}
=b_ra_s-a_rb_s=0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{N_h(r)=0.}
\tag{30}
\]

However, \(N_h\) and \(R_h\) are different continuants. The Apéry gap continuant obeys

\[
N_{h+1}=P(r+h)N_h-(r+h)^6N_{h-1},
\tag{31}
\]

whereas the Racah propagator uses \(A_{r,h}\) from (25). Their diagonal coefficients differ by

\[
\boxed{
P(r+h)-A_{r,h}
=2(2r+2h+1)
 \left(2(2r+2h+1)^2-r(r+1)\right).}
\tag{32}
\]

Thus the general Green identity gives \(N_h(r)=0\), while the desired Racah conclusion is \(R_h(r)\ne0\). The former does not formally imply the latter.

The exact remaining all-pairs theorem is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
b_r=b_{r+h}=0,\qquad r+h<p,\\
r+(r+h)+1\not\equiv0\pmod p
\end{gathered}
\Longrightarrow
R_h(r)\ne0.}
\tag{33}
\]

Equivalently, one needs a saturated cross-continuant/resultant certificate excluding common physical nonmate roots of

\[
N_h(r)\quad\text{and}\quad R_h(r)
\]

on the Hasse-selected endpoint-zero locus.

So the status is precise:

```text

K(r,r-1) companion inverse formula:             PROVED
K(r,r+1) companion inverse formula:             PROVED
initial Racah amplitude obstruction:            REMOVED
all later K(r,s) from seed nonvanishing alone:  DOES NOT FOLLOW
remaining obstruction:                          R_(s-r)(r) != 0
                                                  on the nonmate two-zero locus
```

# 8. Standard-library Python checker

The following script uses exact `Fraction` arithmetic. It checks:

- the fraction-free termwise certificate (11);
- \(6K(r,r-1)=b_r+b_{r-1}\);
- the weighted identity (6);
- the companion Casoratian and the full Green formula;
- the modular unit identities (3) and (5) at every actual zero for primes below 1000;
- the reported nonmate scan below 500;
- the separation of the Racah amplitude from its remaining propagator.

```python
from fractions import Fraction
from math import comb, prod


def P(n: int) -> int:
    return 34*n**3 + 51*n**2 + 27*n + 5


def D(n: int, k: int) -> int:
    if k < 0 or k > n:
        return 0
    return comb(n, k) * comb(n + k, k)


def B_direct(n: int) -> int:
    return sum(D(n, k)**2 for k in range(n + 1))


def K_int(r: int, s: int) -> int:
    return sum(D(r, k) * D(s, k)
               for k in range(min(r, s) + 1))


def apery_exact(N: int):
    """Return b[0..N], a[0..N], with a_0=0,a_1=6."""
    if N < 1:
        raise ValueError("N must be at least 1")
    b = [Fraction(1), Fraction(5)]
    a = [Fraction(0), Fraction(6)]
    for n in range(1, N):
        den = (n + 1)**3
        b.append((P(n)*b[n] - n**3*b[n - 1]) / den)
        a.append((P(n)*a[n] - n**3*a[n - 1]) / den)
    return b, a


def gap_N(u: int, G: int) -> int:
    """N_G(u): N_0=0, N_1=1."""
    if G < 0:
        raise ValueError("G must be nonnegative")
    if G == 0:
        return 0
    Nm1, Ncur = 0, 1
    for h in range(1, G):
        Nm1, Ncur = (
            Ncur,
            P(u + h)*Ncur - (u + h)**6*Nm1,
        )
    return Ncur


# ---------- exact rational/integer checks ----------
MAX_R = 30
bQ, aQ = apery_exact(MAX_R + 10)
cQ = [x / 6 for x in aQ]

for r in range(1, MAX_R + 1):
    assert bQ[r].denominator == 1
    assert bQ[r] == B_direct(r)

    # Termwise fraction-free telescoper.
    rhs_sum = 0
    for k in range(r + 1):
        dk = D(r, k)
        ek = D(r - 1, k)
        dprev = D(r, k - 1)
        lhs = r**2 * (6*dk*ek - dk**2 - ek**2)
        rhs = 4 * ((r - k)**2 * dk**2
                   - (r - k + 1)**2 * dprev**2)
        assert lhs == rhs
        rhs_sum += rhs
    assert rhs_sum == 0

    Krm = K_int(r, r - 1)
    assert 6*Krm == bQ[r] + bQ[r - 1]

    Wr = sum(Fraction(D(r, k)**2 * k, r + k)
             for k in range(r + 1))
    assert Wr == (5*bQ[r] - bQ[r - 1]) / 12

    # Casoratian and the exact off-target companion formula.
    assert (bQ[r - 1]*aQ[r] - aQ[r - 1]*bQ[r]
            == Fraction(6, r**3))
    assert (Fraction(Krm)
            == Fraction(1, 1) / (r**3*aQ[r])
               + (aQ[r] + aQ[r - 1])*bQ[r]/(6*aQ[r]))

    # Exact formula through b_(r+1).
    assert (Fraction(Krm)
            == ((P(r) + r**3)*bQ[r]
                - (r + 1)**3*bQ[r + 1]) / (6*r**3))

# Full gap-continuant Green formula.
for u in range(0, 10):
    for G in range(1, 8):
        lhs = Fraction(
            gap_N(u, G),
            prod((u + j)**3 for j in range(1, G + 1)),
        )
        rhs = bQ[u]*cQ[u + G] - cQ[u]*bQ[u + G]
        assert lhs == rhs


# ---------- modular utilities ----------
def primes_below(limit: int):
    sieve = bytearray(b"\x01") * limit
    if limit > 0:
        sieve[0] = 0
    if limit > 1:
        sieve[1] = 0
    q = 2
    while q*q < limit:
        if sieve[q]:
            sieve[q*q:limit:q] = b"\x00" * (
                ((limit - 1 - q*q)//q) + 1
            )
        q += 1
    return [n for n in range(limit) if sieve[n]]


def apery_mod(p: int):
    """Return b,a through index p-1; never divide by p."""
    b = [1, 5 % p]
    a = [0, 6 % p]
    for n in range(1, p - 1):
        den = pow(n + 1, 3, p)
        invden = pow(den, -1, p)
        b.append((P(n)*b[n] - n**3*b[n - 1]) * invden % p)
        a.append((P(n)*a[n] - n**3*a[n - 1]) * invden % p)
    return b, a


def racah_C_mod(p: int, r: int, h: int) -> int:
    """Normalized C_h in equations (24)--(26)."""
    if h < 0:
        raise ValueError("h must be nonnegative")
    if h == 0:
        return 0
    Cm1, Ccur = 0, 1
    for j in range(1, h):
        t = r + j
        A = ((t + 1)**3 + t**3
             + 2*(2*t + 1)*r*(r + 1))
        Cnext = ((A*Ccur - t**3*Cm1)
                 * pow(pow(t + 1, 3, p), -1, p)) % p
        Cm1, Ccur = Ccur, Cnext
    return Ccur


zero_count = 0
weighted_count = 0
nonmate_pairs = 0
nonmate_failures = []

for p in primes_below(1000):
    if p <= 3:
        continue
    b, a = apery_mod(p)
    zeros = [r for r in range(1, p - 1) if b[r] == 0]
    zero_count += len(zeros)

    for r in zeros:
        Km = K_int(r, r - 1) % p
        Kp = K_int(r, r + 1) % p

        assert (6*Km - b[r - 1]) % p == 0
        assert b[r - 1] != 0
        assert a[r] != 0
        assert (pow(r, 3, p)*a[r]*Km) % p == 1

        assert (6*Kp - b[r + 1]) % p == 0
        assert (pow(r + 1, 3, p)*a[r]*Kp) % p == p - 1
        assert (Kp + pow(r, 3, p)
                * pow(pow(r + 1, 3, p), -1, p)*Km) % p == 0

        rhs_bplus = (-pow(r + 1, 3, p)
                     * b[r + 1]
                     * pow(6*pow(r, 3, p), -1, p)) % p
        assert Km == rhs_bplus

        # Literal weighted chart: all r+k are units when 2r < p.
        if 2*r < p:
            W = 0
            for k in range(1, r + 1):
                dk = D(r, k) % p
                W += dk*dk*k*pow(r + k, -1, p)
            W %= p
            assert W == (-b[r - 1]*pow(12, -1, p)) % p
            assert W == (-pow(2*pow(r, 3, p)*a[r], -1, p)) % p
            weighted_count += 1

        # Check the exact amplitude/propagator split for a short window.
        for h in range(0, min(9, p - 1 - r)):
            lhs = K_int(r, r + h) % p
            rhs = Kp * racah_C_mod(p, r, h) % p
            assert lhs == rhs

    # Reproduce the stated finite nonmate scan below 500.
    if p < 500:
        for i, r in enumerate(zeros):
            for s in zeros[i + 1:]:
                if (r + s + 1) % p != 0:
                    nonmate_pairs += 1
                    if K_int(r, s) % p == 0:
                        nonmate_failures.append((p, r, s))

assert not nonmate_failures

# Locked small fixture.
p, r = 73, 2
b, a = apery_mod(p)
assert b[r] == 0
assert a[r] == 33
assert K_int(r, r - 1) % p == 13
assert (r**3*a[r]*13) % p == 1

print("PASS: exact adjacent, weighted, Casoratian, and Green identities")
print("actual zero indices checked below 1000:", zero_count)
print("literal lower-chart weighted sums checked:", weighted_count)
print("nonmate zero pairs checked below 500:", nonmate_pairs)
```

# Bottom line

The exact synthesis is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
b_r=0
&\Longrightarrow
K(r,r-1)=\frac1{r^3a_r}\ne0,\\
&\Longrightarrow
\sum_{k=1}^{r}D(r,k)^2\frac{k}{r+k}
=-\frac1{2r^3a_r}\ne0,\\
&\Longrightarrow
K(r,r+1)=-\frac1{(r+1)^3a_r}\ne0.
\end{aligned}}
\]

This is a genuine companion-Casoratian unit certificate. It closes the entire adjacent-seed problem.

For an arbitrary nonmate second zero \(s=r+h\), the remaining statement is no longer about the seed. It is exactly the arithmetic exclusion

\[
N_h(r)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_h(r)\ne0
\]

on the Hasse-selected, physical nonmate locus. That is the next irreducible lemma; it does not follow from seed nonvanishing alone.
