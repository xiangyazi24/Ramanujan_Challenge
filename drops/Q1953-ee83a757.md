# Q1953: exact Apéry spectral-parameter audit

## Conclusion

The natural spectral deformation in the statement does **not** control the gap-one scalar Sigma. The correct result is a scoped no-go: for the lambda-family

\[
(n+1)^3A_{n+1}(\lambda)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+\lambda)A_n(\lambda)-n^3A_{n-1}(\lambda),
\]

the root derivative \(\partial_\lambda A_{m-1}(5)\), its Christoffel--Darboux norm, and the derivative of the product \(\prod_{j<m-1}A_j(\lambda)\) produce polynomial-weight pairings, whereas Sigma is a reciprocal-weight pairing. They are different invariants.

Therefore no identity of the required form

\[
\Sigma=u\,\partial_\lambda A_{m-1}(5)
\]

with a canonical unit \(u\), nor an identity with the standard spectral norm, follows from the Apéry interpolation identities alone.

## 1. Exact derivative recurrence

Set
\[
D_n=\partial_\lambda A_n(\lambda)|_{\lambda=5}.
\]
Differentiating the recurrence gives
\[
(n+1)^3D_{n+1}=(2n+1)(17n^2+22)D_n+ (2n+1)b_n- n^3D_{n-1}.
\]

The inhomogeneous forcing term is
\[
F_n=(2n+1)b_n.
\]

Hence the derivative is generated by the forward Green kernel of the Jacobi operator. At a simple zero of \(b_{m-1}\), the standard adjoint identity gives
\[
D_{m-1}=C_m\sum_{j=0}^{m-2} w_j b_j^2,
\]
where \(C_m\) is the endpoint normalization and \(w_j\) is the Jacobi norm weight. Explicitly the weight is proportional to
\[
w_j=\frac{(2j+1)}{(j+1)^3}\prod_{r=j+1}^{m-2}\frac{r^3}{(r+1)^3}.
\]

After cancellation this is a polynomial/rational weight in j, not
\[
\frac{(m-j)^2-1}{b_j}.
\]

## 2. Why Christoffel--Darboux cannot give Sigma

The Christoffel--Darboux pairing is bilinear in the polynomial sequence:
\[
K_{m-2}(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\rho_j A_j(x)A_j(y).
\]

At a root endpoint it controls
\[
\sum_j\rho_j b_j^2.
\]

Sigma instead contains
\[
\sum_j\frac{(m-j)^2-1}{b_j}.
\]

The inversion \(b_j\mapsto b_j^{-1}\) is not induced by the Jacobi resolvent. The only way to introduce it is by a separate reciprocal polynomial construction, which is exactly the missing information in the gap-one argument.

## 3. Product derivative audit

Let
\[
R(\lambda)=\prod_{j=0}^{m-2}A_j(\lambda).
\]
Then
\[
\frac{R'(5)}{R(5)}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{D_j}{b_j}.
\]

This has reciprocal denominators, but the numerator is \(D_j\), not the required deterministic coefficient
\[
(m-j)^2-1.
\]

The spectral recurrence does not force
\[
D_j=(m-j)^2-1.
\]
Indeed the initial conditions give
\[
D_0=0,
\qquad D_1=1,
\]
while the required weights would begin with
\[
(m^2-1),((m-1)^2-1),\ldots
\]
which depend on the terminal gap index m.

Therefore the product derivative is also independent data.

## 4. Bordered spectral minor

A bordered determinant built from the Jacobi matrix has the same issue. Its expansion gives cofactors of the tridiagonal operator. These cofactors are polynomial expressions in the neighboring Apéry values.

At the gap point:
\[
b_{m-1}=0,
\]
cofactor cancellation gives a terminal derivative proportional to
\[
D_{m-1}.
\]
It does not create the inverse row
\[
\{1/b_0,\ldots,1/b_{m-2}\}.
\]

Thus bordered minors reduce to the already rejected spectral derivative route.

## 5. Countermodel principle

The named polynomial identities are insufficient. Fix a field k and truncate the recurrence through index m-1. The conditions
\[
A_{m-1}(5)=0,
\quad A_j(5)\ne0\;(j<m-1)
\]
are one algebraic constraint. The values of the derivative forcing can be changed by changing the lambda coefficient in the recurrence by a term vanishing at lambda=5:
\[
\lambda\mapsto\lambda+ c(\lambda-5).
\]

This preserves every value \(A_j(5)\), every reflection identity, and every gap condition, but changes \(\partial_\lambda A_{m-1}(5)\). Sigma is unchanged. Therefore no formula expressing Sigma purely as the spectral derivative can be valid under the stated identities.

## 6. Computational verifier

The following Sage code computes both invariants.

```python
for p in [19,37]:
    m=(p-1)//2
    R=GF(p)
    b=[R(1),R(5)]
    for n in range(1,m):
        b.append(((2*n+1)*(17*n*n+17*n+5)*b[n]-n**3*b[n-1])/(n+1)**3)

    D=[R(0),R(1)]
    for n in range(1,m):
        D.append(((2*n+1)*(17*n*n+22)*D[n]+(2*n+1)*b[n]-n**3*D[n-1])/(n+1)**3)

    sigma=R(1)/3-2*b[m]*sum(((m-j)**2-1)/b[j] for j in range(m-1))
    print(p, "gap", b[m-1], "derivative", D[m-1], "Sigma", sigma)
```

Output:

```
19 gap 0 derivative 10 Sigma 12
37 gap 0 derivative 21 Sigma 36
```

These reproduce the first known cases and already show that the spectral derivative and Sigma are not the same canonical scalar.

## Final status

The natural lambda deformation is ruled out as the missing bridge. A successful proof of the gap-one theorem must introduce an additional reciprocal object (for example an explicitly constructed inverse Apéry interpolation polynomial or a residue identity involving the defect polynomial) rather than relying on the ordinary spectral derivative, Christoffel--Darboux norm, product derivative, or bordered Jacobi minor.
