# Q879 audit: does `c_n | J_n` hold for all prime powers?

## Conclusion

The proposed all-prime-power divisibility statement

\[
 c_n=\operatorname{cont}\left(\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}T^k\right)\mid
 J_n=\gcd(L_n/n,b_n,X_n)
\]

is **not currently derivable from the known carrier identities**, and the available structure gives a strong obstruction to the most natural proof routes. The computation through `n <= 2000` is evidence for a true divisibility phenomenon, but it is not explained by an integral linear-combination identity of the requested type.

The missing point is the coefficient vector of `P_n`: it controls shifted Apery values

\[
 A_k=(L_n/k)b_{n-k},\qquad 1\le k\le n,
\]

whereas `J_n` depends on the unshifted pair `(b_n,X_n)` and on `L_n/n`. A valid proof needs a unimodular (or explicitly bounded-index) transfer between these lattices.

## What follows from the polynomial content definition

For every prime p,

\[
v_p(c_n)=\min_{1\le k\le n}\left(v_p(L_n/k)+v_p(b_{n-k})\right).
\]

Therefore `c_n | J_n` is equivalent to the pair of inequalities

\[
\min_k(v_p(L_n/k)+v_p(b_{n-k}))\le v_p(L_n/n),
\]

and

\[
\min_k(v_p(L_n/k)+v_p(b_{n-k}))\le v_p(b_n),\quad
\min_k(v_p(L_n/k)+v_p(b_{n-k}))\le v_p(X_n)
\]

for every prime p.

The first inequality is plausible because the k=n coefficient equals `(L_n/n)b_0`, but it only gives

\[
v_p(c_n)\le v_p(L_n/n).
\]

It does not give the `b_n` or `X_n` divisibilities.

## Attempted characteristic-zero transfer

The desired identity would have to produce integer coefficients `u_k,v_k` such that

\[
b_n=\sum_k u_k(L_n/k)b_{n-k},
\]

and

\[
X_n=\sum_k v_k(L_n/k)b_{n-k}.
\]

No such identity follows from the Apéry recurrence. The recurrence is a second-order relation with polynomial coefficients in n; expressing the terminal value from the shifted values introduces denominators coming from the recurrence coefficients. The resulting transfer matrix is rational, not integral.

The determinant obstruction is visible already from the recurrence companion matrix. A length-n transfer has determinant containing products of recurrence denominators. Clearing them introduces factors comparable to powers of n and does not preserve content divisibility.

Thus the recurrence route cannot prove `c_n | gcd(b_n,X_n)` without an additional saturation theorem.

## p-adic minimum valuation route

A possible route would be to show that for every p there is a coefficient index k satisfying

\[
v_p((L_n/k)b_{n-k})\le v_p(b_n),
\]

and similarly for `X_n`.

For p>sqrt(n), this is essentially already known from the target-support theorem: the valuation is controlled by the Apéry-Lucas residue and the carriers agree.

The difficulty is exactly p<=sqrt(n). For these primes, `v_p(L_n/k)` can remove several powers of p depending on k, while the shifted values `b_{n-k}` have unrelated p-adic valuations. There is no monotonicity statement

\[
v_p(b_{n-k})\ge v_p(b_n)
\]

or the reverse.

Hence a minimum-valuation proof requires a new small-prime depletion lemma.

## Why the derivative-content identity does not automatically help

The identity

\[
 c_n\,\operatorname{cont}((P_n/c_n)')=L_n
\]

implies

\[
c_n\mid L_n.
\]

Combining with `c_n|J_n` would imply

\[
c_n\mid \gcd(L_n/n,b_n,X_n).
\]

However the reverse height consequence is not new unless one proves that the quotient

\[
J_n/c_n
\]

has a controllable size. The observed quotients 5, 25, and 19 show that equality fails and therefore the derivative-content identity alone does not identify the missing factor.

The combined inequality would be new only if one obtains an independent upper bound

\[
\log(J_n/c_n)=o(n).
\]

No such bound follows from the derivative identity.

## Scoped obstruction

The following class of certificates cannot prove the theorem:

**Integral shift-lattice certificates.** Suppose one only uses integer linear combinations of the coefficient vector

\[
((L_n/1)b_{n-1},\ldots,(L_n/n)b_0)
\]

with determinant ±1 (or determinant bounded independently of n).

Such transforms preserve content up to a bounded factor. They cannot create `b_n` and `X_n` divisibility unless those quantities already lie in the saturated lattice generated by the shifted coefficients. The Apéry recurrence transfer matrix has non-unit determinant, so this saturation is an extra arithmetic statement, not a formal consequence.

Therefore a successful proof needs genuinely new input: either

1. a small-prime saturation theorem for the shifted coefficient lattice;
2. an explicit integral adjoint identity for the Apéry recurrence;
3. a direct p-adic depletion theorem for all p<=sqrt(n).

## Status of the carrier connection

The relation between the two carriers is presently:

- `c_n` and `J_n` have identical large-prime support and valuation by existing theorems.
- Computation suggests `c_n | J_n` globally.
- No known recurrence, generating-function, or derivative-content identity upgrades the large-prime statement to all prime powers.
- If `c_n|J_n` is proved, it becomes a genuinely useful new carrier only when combined with an independent bound on `J_n/c_n`; otherwise it is another representation of the same height problem.

The missing edge is therefore not a formal change of basis but a small-prime integral saturation theorem.
