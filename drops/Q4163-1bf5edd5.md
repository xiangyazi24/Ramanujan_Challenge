# 10. Central versus far reflection sectors

For an oriented state put

\[
\delta(q,t):=|q-1-2t|.
\tag{10.1}

The central complementary-plus theorem controls the following pair type:

```text
one numerical lower prime p
occurring across the two reflected branches t and q-1-t
of one typed upper orbit,
with delta <= D.
```

The pair moment `(3.1)` controls a different pair type:

```text
two distinct lower primes p_1 != p_2
inside one fixed oriented state (q,t).
```

Therefore the central complementary deletion cannot be inserted into `(9.2)` as a global deletion. It controls an intersection of branch supports, whereas the present obstruction is concentration inside one branch.

The crude central selected-state estimate

\[
\#\{(q,t):q\mid b_t,\ \delta(q,t)\le D\}
\ll_J\frac{T}{L}(D^{2/3}+1)
\tag{10.2}
\]

also does not control total edge mass: multiplying by the pointwise fixed-state divisor capacity `O(T/L)` is supertarget for every growing `D`.

At the exact midpoint `t=(q-1)/2`, there is one oriented branch, not two. The minus window is empty in the macroscopic regime, but the plus graph can remain. This changes constants only.

The support obstruction in Section 12 can be placed with

\[
\delta(q,t)\asymp T,
\]

so a far-reflection restriction alone does not solve `(MARKED-FAR-BIRICH)`.

# 11. Exact computation

The computation used only the Apéry recurrence modulo each prime:

\[

I first compared the recurrence output with the defining binomial sum

\[

\]

for every prime `7<=p<=47`; every zero set agreed exactly.

The core Wolfram Language census was:

# 12. Sharp support counterconfiguration

The following is an abstract arithmetic-support model, not a claim that the Apéry sequence realizes it. Its purpose is to show exactly which new theorem is needed.

Put

\[
R_0:=T/L,
\qquad
Z_0:=T^{3/5}L^{-3/10},
\qquad
M_0:=R_0/Z_0.
\tag{12.1}

Partition `R_0` lower-prime labels into `M_0` clusters, each containing `Z_0` primes.

For every cluster choose:

```text
Z_0 common quotient-6 values n_k;
Z_0 common sign-companion values c_j
  (quotient 13 in plus, quotient 1 in minus);
Z_0^2 selected upper cells (n_k,c_j).
```

For a lower prime `p_i` in that cluster declare actual lower zeros

\[
r_{i,k}:=n_k-6p_i,
\qquad
a_{i,j}:=c_j-\lambda p_i,
\qquad
\lambda=13\text{ or }1.
\tag{12.2}

Choose the affine boxes so all digits are legal and a fixed positive proportion away from every endpoint. For plus, for example, one may place

\[
n_k\approx6.9p,
\qquad
c_j\approx13.1p,
\]

which gives `q≈1.03p`, a legal positive upper digit, and reflection gap `asymp T`. Reconstruct and declare the corresponding upper prime and zero mark for every cell.

Every selected cell is adjacent to all `Z_0` lower primes in its cluster. The ledgers are:

\begin{aligned}

&\asymp Z_0<T^{2/3},\\

# 13. Why the standard alternatives do not close the residual

## 13.1 Hall or flow

The graph here is upstream of packing. The affine cluster can be completed with private C4 edges in each lower characteristic. Matching and flow constraints do not reduce its raw arithmetic edge count. Reusing Hall deficiency would be circular and would address the wrong graph.

## 13.2 Assigned support

Deterministic physical OR assignment is a subset of the raw union. Replacing raw support by assigned support may prove a statement about a chosen packing, but it does not bound the authoritative raw carrier from above. The raw tie-break `(1.10)` is the correct multiplicity ledger.

## 13.3 Same-characteristic SF2

The banked second factorial moment counts two zeros modulo one fixed prime. In `(4.8)` the obstruction is two different lower characteristics sharing one upper cell. It is a cross-characteristic product of two overlap channels.

## 13.4 Scalar determinants and gcd heights

For one pair of lower primes, the cross determinant is a positive nonzero integer divisible by `p_1p_2`, but its logarithmic height is `O(T)`. It can carry `T/L` shell primes and gives no aggregate codegree saving. The fixed-state gcd radical has the same first-order limitation.

## 13.5 One overlap channel

The marginals `(5.3)` are already sharp at the marked `T^{11/5}L^{-8/5}` scale. Bounding `J_6` or `J_lambda` separately cannot control their aligned product. Section 12 saturates both marginals simultaneously.

## 13.6 Central-band complementarity

That theorem controls one prime repeated across reflected branches. It says nothing about two primes in one oriented branch and therefore cannot bound `(4.8)`.
