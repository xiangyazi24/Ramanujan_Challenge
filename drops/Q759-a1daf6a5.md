# Small-g packet bottleneck: can a macroscopic target set force large g?

## Conclusion

The packet certificate gives a strong conditional height saving for large divisor parameter g, but it does **not** force a positive fraction of a hypothetical macroscopic target set onto g→∞. The obstruction is that the certificate is an upper bound on the contribution of large-g packets, not a lower bound on how much mass must enter them.

A complete admissible saturation model exists in which all target mass is carried by g=1 packets while every large-g packet has negligible total radical.

Therefore the remaining bottleneck is entirely the g=1 layer.

---

## 1. The known packet bound

For the q=1 packet decomposition, write

\[
J_{n,g,D}^{\mathrm{branch}}
\]

for the branch corresponding to divisor parameter g and auxiliary parameter D. The established certificate is

\[
\log H(J_{n,g,D}^{\mathrm{branch}})
\leq \frac{X}{g}\log(6g^2).
\]

Summing over

\[
g\geq \Omega_X,
\]

with \(\Omega_X\to\infty\), gives

\[
\sum_{g\geq\Omega_X}\log\operatorname{rad}(J_{n,g,D}^{\mathrm{branch}})=o(X).
\]

Hence large g is harmless once it is reached.

The missing implication would be:

\[
|K|\geq \epsilon X/\log X
\Longrightarrow
\text{many elements have }g\to\infty.
\]

This implication is false from the packet bounds alone.

---

## 2. Divisor-lattice counting does not force large g

The parameter g comes from the divisor structure of the affine incidence relation. A typical count has the shape

\[
\sum_{g\leq X} N_g,
\]

where \(N_g\) counts possible packets with parameter g.

Divisor estimates can show that large g contributes fewer packets, but they do not show that small g cannot contain a positive density of incidences.

A saturation model is:

\[
N_1=\epsilon X/\log X,
\]

and

\[
N_g=0\qquad(g>1).
\]

This satisfies all lattice-counting constraints and all large-g radical estimates. Therefore divisor counting alone cannot exclude g=1 concentration.

---

## 3. Average order of gcd(n-1,p-1)

One might hope that

\[
g\mid \gcd(n-1,p-1)
\]

forces g to grow on average because gcd values have nontrivial distribution.

However the relevant question is not the average gcd over all primes. It is the gcd distribution restricted to the target incidence set:

\[
(p,n)\in K.
\]

The target set may be highly biased.

The model

\[
K=\{(p,n):g(p,n)=1\}
\]

is compatible with all generic gcd averages. Average-order theorems give no anti-concentration statement for this adversarial subset.

Thus:

\[
\mathbb E\gcd(n-1,p-1)
\]

cannot force

\[
g\rightarrow\infty
\]

inside K.

---

## 4. Direct/reflected linear laws

The direct and reflected laws give two branches, schematically

\[
n=p+r,
\]

and

\[
n=(p-h)+(r+h).
\]

They create packet structure but do not change the divisor parameter.

The laws imply that certain prime incidences are paired, but a paired incidence can remain inside the g=1 branch.

A model with

\[
g(p,n)=1
\]

for every target incidence preserves both linear laws. Therefore these laws alone do not generate large-g mass.

---

## 5. Explicit admissible saturation model

Define an abstract target incidence graph:

- vertices are admissible pairs \((p,n)\);
- assign every target edge the label \(g=1\);
- assign no edges to \(g>1\).

Require only:

1. the direct relation holds:

\[
n=p+r;
\]

2. the reflected relation holds when applicable:

\[
n=(p-h)+(r+h);
\]

3. packet height bounds hold.

All three conditions are satisfied because they impose no contradiction with g=1.

The target size can be

\[
|K|=\epsilon X/\log X,
\]

while

\[
\sum_{g\geq\Omega_X}\log\operatorname{rad}(J_g)=0.
\]

Therefore large-g forcing is not a consequence of the current structure.

---

## 6. What would actually suffice?

A sufficient theorem would be a g-distribution statement:

\[
\sum_{g\leq G}N_g=o(X/\log X)
\]

for every fixed G.

Equivalently, the small-g contribution must be negligible:

\[
\#\{(p,n)\in K:g(p,n)\leq G\}=o(X/\log X).
\]

No divisor-lattice estimate currently gives this because it requires information about the Apéry divisibility condition, not merely the incidence geometry.

---

## 7. Weakest genuinely Apéry-specific statement needed for g=1

The remaining target is therefore:

> **g=1 anti-concentration statement:** the set of q=1 Apéry prime incidences with trivial divisor parameter has submacroscopic size.

In a form suitable for the proof:

\[
\#\{p\in(X/2,X]:p\mid b_n,\ g(p,n)=1\}
=o(X/\log X).
\]

This cannot follow from:

- divisor counting;
- average gcd estimates;
- direct/reflected linear identities;
- packet height bounds.

It requires an Apéry-specific input, such as a new cancellation theorem for the zero set of \(b_r\bmod p\), a stronger Lucas distribution theorem, or a structural restriction on the g=1 packets.

---

## Final answer

Large g is already controlled: if a target incidence enters g→∞, the packet height gives the desired sublinear radical contribution.

But a macroscopic target set can consistently live entirely at g=1. The current q=1 packet certificate does not force mass migration to large g.

The weakest missing theorem is therefore not a large-g theorem. It is a genuine Apéry statement eliminating or sparsifying the g=1 packet layer.
