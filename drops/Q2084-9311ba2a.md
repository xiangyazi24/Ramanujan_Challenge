\tag{59}
$$

Consequently `(AS6AG)` implies

$$
\sum d^\star_{\beta,x,h}
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{60}
$$

This is a strict algebraic reduction:

- the row `m` and six prime labels have disappeared;
- only three lower-index tags `(beta,x,h)` remain;
- the six local prime conditions are encoded by one common-root gcd;
- extraneous roots are allowed, so the statement is stronger than the canonical count.

## 9.1 Present unconditional height is far too large

The trivial inclusion `J|mathcal P_beta` gives

$$
\log|J_{\beta,x,h}(0)|
\le\log\operatorname{rad}(b_\beta)
\le\log b_\beta
=O(\beta).
\tag{61}
$$

There are `O(N^3)` admissible triples `(beta,x,h)`, so direct summation gives only

$$
O(N^4).
\tag{62}
$$

Grouping through the tail factor alone is better but still insufficient.  From `(44)`, if its roots are `R_i`, then

$$
|G_{x,h}(0)G_{x,h}(3h)|
=\prod_i|R_i(R_i-3h)|
\tag{63}
$$

and this divides the corresponding squarefree prime-divisor products from `b_x` and `b_(x+3h)`.  Thus its logarithm is `O(N)`, and summing only over `(x,h)` costs `O(N^3)`.  What is missing is an Apéry-specific theorem controlling how many different reflection loci `H_(beta,h)` can hit the same shifted tail root while preserving all four reflection labels.

Canonical tags alone do not provide that theorem, and I do not identify the stated reuse factor `128` with such a bound.

# 10. Why scalar resultants do not improve `(48)`

One might try

$$
\operatorname{Res}_T\left(H_{\beta,h}(T),G_{x,h}(T+\beta-x)\right).
\tag{64}
$$

For a canonical record the two polynomials have an exact common **integer** root, so `(64)` is zero.  It is therefore not a nonzero integer carrier.

Using resultants of the six original polynomials pairwise goes in the other direction and loses the common-root label: the same rational prime can divide two scalar resultants through different roots.  This is precisely the component-misalignment obstruction already proved in `gcdtail_result.tex`.

The monic gcd `J` is the correct object because it keeps the aligned root itself.  Principal subresultants merely encode the degree of this gcd; they do not provide a smaller nonzero marked scalar unless a new theorem controls their height or content.

# 11. Relation to the first-Bockstein saturation

Q2081 shows that for one reflection edge, passing to the first divided reflection state gives no new primitive marked factor after the universal row content is removed.  In the present notation this applies separately to the `(p,P)` and `(q,Q)` pieces of `H_(beta,h)`.

Therefore the full six-label cycle does **not** acquire connectivity by multiplying two first-Bockstein edge carriers and appending a tail factor: that is the excluded separable construction.

The new coordinate `(48)` is qualitatively different.  It asks whether the root selected by the four-label reflection locus also lies, after the shear `T -> T+beta-x`, in the two-label tail divisor locus.  This is a nonlinear root-incidence question.

There are therefore two distinct candidates beyond the saturated linear layer:

1. the **geometric asynchronous curvature**

$$
(m-1)x-3\beta v,
$$

which is low height but unmarked;

2. the **selectedness-sensitive aligned gcd**

$$
J_{\beta,x,h},
$$

which is marked and connected but for which the needed aggregate height theorem is open.

A higher (second) Bockstein determinant of the Q2081 type could in principle further refine `H` before intersecting it with `G`; no such identity is presently proved to force a saving on the six-label family.

# 12. Final theorem

## Theorem — asynchronous six-label factorization and first connected gate

Fix `N<m<=2N` and a canonical record with six distinct selected top primes

$$
p,\ q=p+h,\ Q,\ P=Q+2h,\ s,\ r=s+3h,
$$

and lower indices as in the question.  Then:

1. `h` is even, `3h<N`, and if `3` does not divide `h` then `m=1 (mod 3)`.

2. With `T=P`, all six labels are the affine forms `(41)` and `m=T+beta`.

3. The signed-gap transfer word is not a closed path in the Apéry transfer groupoid.  In the normalized gauge it satisfies

$$
\det\Omega
=\left(\frac{v\beta y}{u\alpha x}\right)^3,
$$

with curvature numerator

$$
u\alpha x-v\beta y
=h((m-1)x-3\beta v).
$$

No one of the six affine label forms divides the primitive bracket.  Hence this low-height holonomy defect is not a selected-prime carrier.

4. The exact six-label selectedness locus is the monic gcd `J_(beta,x,h)` in `(3)`.  Its valid roots are in bijection with canonical records.

5. The full locus factors exactly as

$$
J_{\beta,x,h}(T)
=\gcd^{\rm monic}\left(
H_{\beta,h}(T),
G_{x,h}(T+\beta-x)
\right).
$$

Thus the first two reflection edges and the final `(r,s)` pair remain locally separable **until their common-root intersection**.  That final gcd is the first nonseparable nonlinear selectedness coordinate.

6. The filtered endpoint product `(7)` is nonzero and equals the exact product of all six prime labels over all valid roots.  Its logarithmic height is bounded by `(55)`.

7. No unconditional aggregate little-oh bound for this carrier follows from the currently banked recurrence, reflection, first-Bockstein, or divisor-polynomial height estimates.  A sufficient strictly smaller algebraic theorem is `(AS6AG)`.

So the complete cycle does supply a genuinely new object beyond the two saturated reflection carriers, but the gain is **incidence structure rather than an already-small height**.  The concrete next target is not another product/lcm or fixed-return continuant: it is an Apéry-specific height/degree theorem for the aligned intersection

$$
\gcd\left(H_{\beta,h}(T),G_{x,h}(T+\beta-x)\right),
$$

or a higher-Bockstein refinement of `H` whose common-root intersection with the tail locus has summably small mass.
