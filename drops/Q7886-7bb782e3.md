# Hostile audit of the four-puncture Apéry K3 Mellin interface

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
X=\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_p},\qquad
S=\{0,\alpha,\beta,\infty\},\qquad
U=X\setminus S,
\]

where \(\alpha,\beta\) are the two geometric roots of
\(D(t)=t^2-34t+1\), let

\[
j:U\hookrightarrow X,
\]

and let \(\mathcal V=\mathcal V_{\mathrm{prim}}\) denote the rank-three
primitive local system of the Beukers--Peters Apéry K3 pencil. For
\(1\le r\le p-2\), write

\[
\chi=\omega^{-r},\qquad
\mathcal F_r=\mathcal V\otimes\mathcal L_\chi .
\]

The four draft claims have the following status.

| draft claim | audit verdict |
|---|---|
| \(\dim H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r)=6\) | **Correct, after stating the actual hypotheses.** It follows from tame GOS and the vanishing of \(H_c^0,H_c^2\). The included repository files do not themselves package the global local system and its local inertia in a theorem, so this is not presently a proved numbered repository statement. |
| “the middle-extension cohomology has dimension \(2\)” | **Correct for one precise object:** \(IH^1(X,\mathcal F_r):=H^1(X,j_*\mathcal F_r)=H^0(X,j_{!*}(\mathcal F_r[1]))\). It is false if this is confused with the open compactly supported group above. |
| its Frobenius trace is \(-b_r\pmod p\) after Tate/boundary corrections | **False or at least sign-ambiguous as written.** The exact toric Mellin sum reduces to \(-b_r\). The ordinary Frobenius trace on the rank-two \(IH^1\) group reduces to **\(+b_r\)**, provided one proves the missing branch-stalk comparison. The value \(-b_r\) is the alternating trace of the **unshifted** middle-extension complex, or equivalently the point-sum. The open \(H_c^1\) trace has an additional rank-four branch-boundary term. |
| the open trace has weights \(\le3\), hence \(O(p^{3/2})\), and this alone gives no zero-count saving | **Correct.** More precisely, \(H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r)\) has dimension six and weights at most three, while the rank-two intersection cohomology is pure of weight three. Neither Archimedean bound controls divisibility at the defining prime. |

The most serious publication gap is claim (3). The repository proves an
exact Mellin congruence for the **fiber-count function** \(\mu_p(a)\) and
constructs pointwise primitive traces \(\theta_p(a)\). It does not, in the
files named in the question, construct the global middle-extension sheaf,
identify its two branch stalks with the specially deflated traces
\(\theta_p(\alpha),\theta_p(\beta)\), and then carry out the trace-formula
comparison with all signs fixed. That missing comparison is standard in
shape--it should be a Picard--Lefschetz/invariant-cycles argument--but it is
not a harmless typographical omission.

# 1. What the repository actually proves

The following three statements are genuinely present in the cited source.

## 1.1 Exact toric Mellin coefficient

`problems/3.2/toric_mellin_square.tex`, equation
`eq:toric-fiber-Mellin`, proves, for \(1\le j\le p-2\),

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\mu_p(a)a^j=-b_j
 \quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

This is an exact finite-field identity. It is not initially a statement
about a rank-three local system on the \(a\)-line.

## 1.2 Pointwise K3 trace and deflation

`problems/3.2/toric_fiber_k3.tex`, equation
`eq:toric-K3-deflated-trace`, proves

\[
\boxed{
 \mu_p(a)=p^2-6p+12+\theta_p(a),}
\tag{1.2}
\]

where \(\theta_p(a)\) is a weight-two Frobenius trace on a space of
dimension

\[
3-\mathbf 1_{a=1}-\mathbf 1_{D(a)=0}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The proof exhibits a generic rank-19 rational divisor lattice. At \(a=1\)
it exhibits an additional rational Tate class, and at a branch value
\(D(a)=0\) it exhibits an additional exceptional class. Thus
\(\theta_p(a)\) is a **pointwise deflated trace whose dimension jumps**.
It is not, without further work, the trace function of a lisse rank-three
sheaf extended across the singular fibers.

## 1.3 Symmetric-square/Asai description

`problems/3.2/toric_fiber_sym2.tex` proves exact elliptic formulas for
\(\theta_p(a)\), including separate formulas on the split, inert, and
branch loci. In particular, it identifies the generic primitive factor as
a rank-three complement inside a symmetric-square/Asai construction.
This is strong evidence for, and essentially the geometric source of, the
rank-three local system \(\mathcal V\). But the file does not state or prove
the complete sheaf-theoretic package needed below: a global
\(j_*\mathcal V\), its local inertia invariant spaces, the arithmetic
Frobenius action on those spaces, and comparison of its branch stalks with
the pointwise spaces used to define \(\theta_p(a)\).

## 1.4 What is *not* proved

The included source does **not** prove a pointwise congruence

\[
H_p(a):=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_ma^m
 \stackrel{?}{\equiv}
 \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_a\mid\mathcal V_a)
 \pmod p.
\tag{1.4}
\]

`hasse_franel_descent.tex` identifies \(H_p\) as the relevant Hasse
polynomial and proves its cover-adapted factorization. A Hasse--Witt scalar
is the Cartier action on a Hodge line; it is not the full primitive
Frobenius trace. Nothing in the three toric files licenses replacing one by
the other pointwise.

The valid route from \(b_r\) to K3 traces is the **Mellin identity**
(1.1) together with the exact point-count decomposition (1.2), not the
unproved pointwise assertion (1.4).

# 2. Local monodromy from the Picard--Fuchs operator

The local-inertia calculation can be made explicit. The rank-two operator
whose symmetric square is the Apéry rank-three operator is, in the notation
of `hasse_franel_descent.tex`,

\[
L_+=4\vartheta^2
 -2t(68\vartheta^2+34\vartheta+5)
 +t^2(2\vartheta+1)^2,
\qquad \vartheta=t\frac d{dt}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

In ordinary derivatives this is

\[
4t^2D(t)y''
 +4t(1-51t+2t^2)y'
 +t(t-10)y=0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

At a root \(a\) of \(D\), using \(a^2=34a-1\),

\[
\frac{4a(1-51a+2a^2)}{4a^2D'(a)}
 =\frac{17a-1}{2(17a-1)}=\frac12.
\]

Hence the local exponents of the rank-two system are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &0&\alpha&\beta&\infty\\ \hline
L_+&(0,0)&(0,\tfrac12)&(0,\tfrac12)&(\tfrac12,\tfrac12).
\end{array}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The Beukers--Peters/Peters geometry identifies the primitive rank-three
variation with the symmetric square of this rank-two variation. The
rank-three exponents are therefore

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &0&\alpha&\beta&\infty\\ \hline
\operatorname{Sym}^2L_+
 &(0,0,0)&(0,\tfrac12,1)&(0,\tfrac12,1)&(1,1,1).
\end{array}
\tag{2.4}
\]

At \(0\) and \(\infty\), the repeated rank-two exponent is logarithmic;
the rank-two monodromy is one Jordan block, up to the scalar \(-1\) at
infinity. Its symmetric square is one rank-three unipotent Jordan block.
At \(\alpha,\beta\), the singularity is an ordinary \(A_1\) degeneration;
Picard--Lefschetz gives a reflection. Thus, over a geometric algebraic
closure,

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 x&\text{eigenvalues on }\mathcal V&
 \dim \mathcal V^{I_x}&\operatorname{Swan}_x(\mathcal V)\\ \hline
0&(1,1,1),\ J_3&1&0\\
\alpha&(1,1,-1)&2&0\\
\beta &(1,1,-1)&2&0\\
\infty&(1,1,1),\ J_3&1&0.
\end{array}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Primary geometric sources for this Apéry family are Beukers--Peters,
*A family of K3 surfaces and \(\zeta(3)\)*, J. Reine Angew. Math. 351
(1984), 42--54, [doi:10.1515/crll.1984.351.42](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1984.351.42),
and Peters, *Monodromy and Picard--Fuchs equations for families of K3
surfaces and elliptic curves*, Ann. Sci. ENS 19 (1986), 583--607,
[doi:10.24033/asens.1519](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.1519).
The Picard--Lefschetz reflection statement is the theorem of SGA 7 II,
Exposé XV; see [SGA 7 II, LNM 340, pp. 165--196](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0060505).

Now twist by the nontrivial Kummer sheaf \(\mathcal L_\chi\). It is
unramified at \(\alpha,\beta\), and its tame local characters at \(0\) and
\(\infty\) are nontrivial inverses of one another. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 x&0&\alpha&\beta&\infty\\ \hline
 d_x:=\dim(\mathcal F_r^{I_x})&0&2&2&0\\
 \operatorname{Swan}_x(\mathcal F_r)&0&0&0&0.
\end{array}}
\tag{2.6}
\]

This is the local table required for both dimension claims. Notice that
at \(0,\infty\) the nontrivial Kummer scalar kills every invariant; no
assumption about semisimplicity there is being smuggled into GOS.

**Repository gap.** The table (2.5), although standard and recoverable
from the displayed differential operator plus the K3 geometry, is not
proved as an \(\ell\)-adic inertia theorem in the three included toric
files. A publication should either add this argument or cite the precise
comparison theorem that transports the geometric monodromy to the chosen
\(\ell\)-adic local system.

# 3. The open compactly supported group: dimension six

For a lisse sheaf \(\mathcal F\) of rank \(n\) on a curve
\(U=X\setminus S\), Grothendieck--Ogg--Shafarevich gives

\[
\chi_c(U,\mathcal F)
=n(2-2g(X)-|S|)-\sum_{x\in S}\operatorname{Swan}_x(\mathcal F).
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is SGA 5, Exposé X, formula 7.2; see
[SGA 5, LNM 589](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0096802).
For \(g=0\), \(|S|=4\), \(n=3\), and the tame table (2.6),

\[
\boxed{\chi_c(U,\mathcal F_r)=-6.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Two vanishings are needed before converting this Euler characteristic into
a dimension.

1. \(H_c^0(U,\mathcal F_r)=0\). A section of a lisse sheaf on a connected
   nonproper curve cannot have nonempty proper support.
2. By Poincaré duality,

   \[
   H_c^2(U,\mathcal F_r)
   \simeq H^0(U,\mathcal F_r^\vee(1))^\vee.
   \tag{3.3}
   \]

   The right side is zero: a global invariant vector would in particular
   be invariant under inertia at \(0\), but the dual Kummer character is
   nontrivial there. Duality and the trace formula may be found in SGA
   4\(\frac12\), respectively *Dualité* and *Rapport sur la formule des
   traces*, [LNM 569](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0091516).

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\dim H_c^1(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},\mathcal F_r)=6.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This proves claim (1), with its missing assumptions exposed.

A useful warning is that inertia-invariant dimensions do **not** enter the
open compact-support formula (3.1); only Swan conductors do. Invariant
dimensions enter when passing from \(j_!\) to \(j_*\).

# 4. The middle extension: exact object and dimension two

There are three different complexes in play.

## 4.1 Open extension by zero

\[
R\Gamma(X,j_!\mathcal F_r)=R\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal F_r).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Unshifted, its only nonzero cohomology is the six-dimensional group in
degree one. With the perverse shift suppressed explicitly,

\[
R\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal F_r[1])
 \simeq H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r)[0].
\tag{4.2}
\]

## 4.2 Classical middle-extension sheaf

On a smooth curve, the classical middle-extension sheaf is the ordinary
constructible sheaf \(j_*\mathcal F_r\), whose boundary stalk is

\[
(j_*\mathcal F_r)_{\bar x}=\mathcal F_r^{I_x}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

One must not replace it by the entire derived object \(Rj_*\mathcal F_r\).
In perverse notation,

\[
\boxed{j_{!*}(\mathcal F_r[1])=(j_*\mathcal F_r)[1].}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The intermediate extension formalism is BBD, *Faisceaux pervers*,
Astérisque 100 (1982), §1.4; a primary-source scan is available from the
[IAS publication page](https://publications.ias.edu/deligne/paper/396).

There is a short exact sequence of ordinary sheaves

\[
0\longrightarrow j_!\mathcal F_r
 \longrightarrow j_*\mathcal F_r
 \longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{x\in S}i_{x*}\mathcal F_r^{I_x}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Since the total boundary-stalk dimension is

\[
\sum_{x\in S}d_x=0+2+2+0=4,
\tag{4.6}
\]

we obtain

\[
\chi(X,j_*\mathcal F_r)
=\chi_c(U,\mathcal F_r)+4=-2.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Equivalently, the Artin-conductor form of GOS gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi(X,j_*\mathcal F_r)
&=2\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal F_r)
 -\sum_{x\in S}
  \bigl(3-d_x+\operatorname{Swan}_x\bigr)\\
&=6-(3+1+1+3)=-2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Furthermore,

\[
H^0(X,j_*\mathcal F_r)=H^0(U,\mathcal F_r)=0,
\tag{4.9}
\]

because the local inertia at \(0\) has no fixed vector. The long exact
sequence of (4.5), together with \(H_c^2=0\), gives

\[
H^2(X,j_*\mathcal F_r)=0.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
IH^1(X,\mathcal F_r)
:=H^1(X,j_*\mathcal F_r)
=H^0\bigl(X,j_{!*}(\mathcal F_r[1])\bigr),
\qquad
\dim IH^1=2.}
\tag{4.11}
\]

Moreover, (4.5) yields the Frobenius-equivariant exact sequence

\[
\boxed{
0\longrightarrow
 \mathcal B_r
 \longrightarrow H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r)
 \longrightarrow IH^1(X,\mathcal F_r)
 \longrightarrow0,}
\tag{4.12}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal B_r
=\bigoplus_{x\in\{\alpha,\beta\}}\mathcal F_r^{I_x},
\qquad \dim\mathcal B_r=4.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Thus the six-dimensional open group consists of a rank-four branch-boundary
piece and the rank-two middle-extension quotient.

## 4.3 Mellin-perverse formulation on \(\mathbf G_m\)

Let

\[
j_0:U\hookrightarrow\mathbf G_m,
\qquad
\mathcal P:=j_{0,!*}(\mathcal V[1]).
\tag{4.14}
\]

The coefficient-indexed Mellin complex is correctly written

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{p,r}^{\mathrm{ME}}
=R\Gamma_c\bigl(\mathbf G_m,
 \mathcal P\otimes\mathcal L_\chi\bigr).}
\tag{4.15}
\]

Because the Kummer twist makes the inertia invariant spaces at \(0\) and
\(\infty\) vanish, extension across those two points is clean. Therefore

\[
\mathcal C_{p,r}^{\mathrm{ME}}
\simeq IH^1(X,\mathcal F_r)[0]
\tag{4.16}
\]

and has dimension two. This is the precise version of claim (2).

# 5. Exact trace bookkeeping and the sign error

Fix geometric Frobenius and choose the Kummer convention in which the stalk
trace at \(a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\) is \(\chi(a)=\omega(a)^{-r}\).
Define the exact cyclotomic Mellin sum

\[
M_{p,r}:=
 \sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\mu_p(a)\chi(a).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since \(\chi\) is nontrivial, (1.2) gives the exact characteristic-zero
identity

\[
M_{p,r}=\sum_{a\ne0}\theta_p(a)\chi(a).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Reducing at the Teichmüller prime above \(p\), equation (1.1) applied to
\(p-1-r\) gives

\[
M_{p,r}\equiv-b_{p-1-r}\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The Apéry reflection congruence in `proof.tex` gives
\(b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\), so

\[
\boxed{M_{p,r}\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is the exact repository-level Mellin congruence. It is a congruence
for \(M_{p,r}\), not yet a formula for the ordinary trace on the
rank-two group (4.11).

## 5.1 Middle-extension stalk trace

Let

\[
t_p(a):=
 \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_a\mid(j_*\mathcal V)_{\bar a}\bigr)
\tag{5.5}
\]

at every rational point \(a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\). At an ordinary smooth
fiber away from \(a=1\), the intended identification is
\(t_p(a)=\theta_p(a)\). At \(a=1\), the lisse rank-three stalk still
contains the extra rational Tate line exhibited in
`toric_fiber_k3.tex`, so

\[
\boxed{t_p(1)=\theta_p(1)+p.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

At a rational branch point \(D(a)=0\), define the comparison defect

\[
e_p(a):=\theta_p(a)-t_p(a),
\qquad
E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}
:=\sum_{\substack{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\\D(a)=0}}
 e_p(a)\chi(a).
\tag{5.7}
\]

This notation deliberately exposes the missing theorem. Standard local
invariant cycles for an \(A_1\) degeneration should give \(e_p(a)=0\),
but the included files do not write down that comparison.

From (5.2), (5.6), and (5.7),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}t_p(a)\chi(a)
=M_{p,r}+p-E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

The Grothendieck--Lefschetz trace formula for \(j_*\mathcal F_r\) gives

\[
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}t_p(a)\chi(a)
=\sum_i(-1)^i
 \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
 H^i(X,j_*\mathcal F_r)\bigr).
\tag{5.9}
\]

The degree-zero and degree-two groups vanish, so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid IH^1(X,\mathcal F_r)\bigr)
=-M_{p,r}-p+E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}.}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Modulo \(\mathfrak p\),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid IH^1)
\equiv b_r+\overline E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}.}
\tag{5.11}
\]

Thus, after the missing branch comparison
\(E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}=0\), the ordinary rank-two Frobenius trace is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid IH^1)
\equiv +b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.}
\tag{5.12}
\]

The sign \(-b_r\) belongs instead to the point-sum/alternating trace:

\[
\operatorname{STr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
 R\Gamma(X,j_*\mathcal F_r)\bigr)
=\sum_a t_p(a)\chi(a)\equiv-b_r
\tag{5.13}
\]

when \(E^{\mathrm{br}}=0\). Shifting to the perverse complex
\(j_{!*}(\mathcal F_r[1])\) changes the supertrace sign and places
\(IH^1\) in degree zero. A draft that calls both quantities “the Frobenius
trace” without fixing the shift has not stated a theorem.

## 5.2 Open compact-support trace and the branch term

Let

\[
B_{p,r}:=
\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid\mathcal B_r\bigr).
\tag{5.14}
\]

If \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf F_p\), then

\[
B_{p,r}=t_p(\alpha)\chi(\alpha)+t_p(\beta)\chi(\beta).
\tag{5.15}
\]

If the two branch points are conjugate over \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\), Frobenius
interchanges the two geometric summands of \(\mathcal B_r\), and its trace
on their direct sum is zero:

\[
B_{p,r}=0.
\tag{5.16}
\]

Taking traces in (4.12) gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
 H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r)\bigr)
=B_{p,r}-M_{p,r}-p+E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}.}
\tag{5.17}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid H_c^1)
\equiv b_r+\overline B_{p,r}+\overline E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}
\pmod{\mathfrak p}.}
\tag{5.18}
\]

The open group does not recover \(b_r\) without subtracting the branch
boundary. This is not a hypothetical concern. `oracleC_exploration.md`
records the exact example \(p=31\): the two branch points are \(14,20\),
\(H_{31}(14)=H_{31}(20)=7\), and the full Mellin coefficient vanishes at
\(r=8,22\), while deleting the two branch values changes the Mellin sum by
\(11\ne0\pmod{31}\).

## 5.3 The missing branch theorem

To set \(E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}=0\) in a publication, one must prove all of
the following.

1. The generic rank-19 divisor subspace used in
   `toric_fiber_k3.tex` glues to a lisse quotient over \(U\).
2. At each \(A_1\) degeneration, Picard--Lefschetz identifies
   \((j_*\mathcal V)_{\bar a}=\mathcal V^{I_a}\) with the correct
   rank-two quotient of the resolved special fiber.
3. This identification is Frobenius equivariant, including the arithmetic
   twist visible in the branch formula of `toric_fiber_sym2.tex`.
4. Under this identification, the trace is precisely the pointwise
   \(\theta_p(a)\) used in (1.2).

The cited files establish the geometry needed to attempt this proof, but
not the comparison itself. Until it is written, claim (3) is not a
repository theorem.

# 6. Weights and the sharp available bounds

The primitive K3 local system \(\mathcal V\) is pure of weight two, and a
tame Kummer sheaf is pure of weight zero. Hence \(\mathcal F_r\) is pure
of weight two.

Deligne's theorem on weights gives

\[
H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r)
\quad\text{mixed of weights at most }3.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Since its dimension is six,

\[
\boxed{
\left|\sigma\operatorname{Tr}
 (\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r))\right|
\le6p^{3/2}}
\tag{6.2}
\]

for every complex embedding \(\sigma\) of the cyclotomic coefficient
field.

By purity of intermediate extension,

\[
j_{!*}(\mathcal F_r[1])
\quad\text{is a pure perverse sheaf of weight }3.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Because \(X\) is proper, its degree-zero hypercohomology--equivalently
\(IH^1(X,\mathcal F_r)\)--is pure of weight three. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\left|\sigma\operatorname{Tr}
 (\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid IH^1(X,\mathcal F_r))\right|
\le2p^{3/2}.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The branch-boundary space has dimension four and weight two, so

\[
|\sigma B_{p,r}|\le4p.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The primary weight references are Deligne,
*La conjecture de Weil II*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 52 (1980), 137--252,
[doi:10.1007/BF02684780](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02684780), and BBD,
Astérisque 100, §5.3, for purity of intermediate extension.

These bounds prove only Archimedean size control. The zero event is

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p,
\tag{6.6}
\]

i.e. divisibility of a cyclotomic algebraic integer by the selected prime
above the **same defining characteristic**. A nonzero algebraic integer of
size \(O(p^{3/2})\) may certainly be divisible by \(p\). The repository's
concrete example is \((p,r)=(11,5)\): the lifted toric Mellin trace is
\(-33\ne0\), but it reduces to zero and \(11\mid b_5\).

Therefore claim (4) is correct, including its limitation: the Weil bound
alone gives no saving for the number of indices with residual trace zero.

# 7. Publication-quality corrected proposition

## Proposition (four-puncture Kummer twist of the primitive Apéry system)

Let \(p\ge5\), \(\ell\ne p\), and let

\[
j:U=\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_p}\setminus
 \{0,\alpha,\beta,\infty\}\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_p}.
\]

Assume \(\mathcal V\) is the rank-three pure weight-two primitive local
system of the Apéry K3 pencil, with geometric local monodromy the symmetric
square of (2.1). For \(1\le r\le p-2\), let
\(\mathcal F_r=\mathcal V\otimes\mathcal L_{\omega^{-r}}\). Then:

1. \(\mathcal F_r\) is tame and

   \[
   \bigl(
   \dim\mathcal F_r^{I_0},
   \dim\mathcal F_r^{I_\alpha},
   \dim\mathcal F_r^{I_\beta},
   \dim\mathcal F_r^{I_\infty}
   \bigr)=(0,2,2,0).
   \]

2. \(H_c^0(U,\mathcal F_r)=H_c^2(U,\mathcal F_r)=0\) and

   \[
   \dim H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r)=6.
   \]

3. \(H^0(\mathbf P^1,j_*\mathcal F_r)\) and
   \(H^2(\mathbf P^1,j_*\mathcal F_r)\) vanish, while

   \[
   \dim H^1(\mathbf P^1,j_*\mathcal F_r)=2.
   \]

   Equivalently,

   \[
   R\Gamma_c\bigl(\mathbf G_m,
    j_{0,!*}(\mathcal V[1])\otimes\mathcal L_{\omega^{-r}}\bigr)
   \simeq H^1(\mathbf P^1,j_*\mathcal F_r)[0].
   \]

4. There is a Frobenius-equivariant exact sequence

   \[
   0\to
   \mathcal F_r^{I_\alpha}\oplus\mathcal F_r^{I_\beta}
   \to H_c^1(U,\mathcal F_r)
   \to H^1(\mathbf P^1,j_*\mathcal F_r)
   \to0.
   \]

5. The open group is mixed of weights at most three; the middle-extension
   group is pure of weight three. Hence their Frobenius traces are bounded
   respectively by \(6p^{3/2}\) and \(2p^{3/2}\) under every complex
   embedding.

6. Let \(M_{p,r}\) be (5.1). The repository proves

   \[
   M_{p,r}\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
   \]

   If \(E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}\) and \(B_{p,r}\) are defined by
   (5.7) and (5.14), then the exact trace identities are

   \[
   \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid IH^1)
   =-M_{p,r}-p+E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}},
   \]

   \[
   \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid H_c^1)
   =B_{p,r}-M_{p,r}-p+E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}.
   \]

   In particular, after proving the still-missing branch comparison
   \(E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}=0\), the **ordinary** middle-extension trace is
   congruent to \(+b_r\), while the alternating trace of the unshifted
   middle-extension complex is congruent to \(-b_r\).

### Proof

Items 1--4 are the local-monodromy calculation (2.6), GOS
(3.1), the two vanishing arguments, and the exact sequence (4.5). Item 5
is Deligne's weight theorem and purity of intermediate extension. Item 6
combines the repository identities `eq:toric-fiber-Mellin` and
`eq:toric-K3-deflated-trace` with the Grothendieck trace formula and the
explicit \(a=1\) Tate correction. No pointwise identification of the Hasse
polynomial with the primitive trace is used. \(\square\)

---

# 8. Limitation paragraph

The corrected proposition does **not** establish that
\(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\) is the vanishing of a characteristic-zero Frobenius
trace. It is the reduction of a Mellin trace at the defining prime. It also
does not identify the Hasse polynomial \(H_p(t)\) with the primitive K3
trace function. Finally, the included repository source has not yet proved
the branch invariant-cycle comparison
\(E_{p,r}^{\mathrm{br}}=0\). Until that comparison is added, the strongest
unconditional statement from the cited files is the exact toric Mellin
congruence (5.4), together with the abstract dimension and weight
calculation for the postulated primitive local system. The
\(O(p^{3/2})\) Weil bound is valid but cannot, by itself, bound
\(Z(p)=\#\{r:p\mid b_r\}\).
