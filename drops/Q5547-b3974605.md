# CFVZ square roots: exhaustive audit of the boundary-telescoping escape hatch

## Executive verdict

Let

$$
F(t)=\sum_{r\ge 0}B_rt^r,
\qquad D(t)=1-34t+t^2,
$$

and define

$$
S_+(t)=F(t)^{1/2}=\sum_{n\ge0}s_n^+t^n,
\qquad
S_-(t)=\left(\frac{F(t)}{D(t)}\right)^{1/2}
       =\sum_{n\ge0}s_n^-t^n.
$$

Q5532 proved that the normalized CFVZ root modulo $p$ is the appropriate first-half truncation of $S_+$ in the square branch and of $S_-$ in the discriminant-times-square branch. Hence

$$
B_r=\sum_{i=0}^r s_i^+s_{r-i}^+
\tag{E+}
$$

in the first branch, while, with

$$
c_r^-:=\sum_{i=0}^r s_i^-s_{r-i}^-,
$$

one has

$$
B_r=c_r^- -34c_{r-1}^-+c_{r-2}^-.
\tag{E-}
$$

The sharply targeted verdict is:

> **The normalized square-root recurrence has a genuine Christoffel--Darboux identity, but it is a same-index identity at two spectral parameters. It does not telescope the reversed-index convolution $s_i s_{r-i}$. For the reversed convolution, the natural Lagrange concomitant has an exact compatibility equation with no rational solution, by a translation-by-two divisor obstruction. The most general uniform rational adjacent-state concomitant is also impossible: it would force a quadratic--linear algebraic differential relation for the square-root period, whereas the connected derived differential Galois group contains $\mathrm{SL}_2$.**

There is an explicit parity-split **hypergeometric weighted Green identity**, but its Pochhammer length and divisor complexity grow linearly with $r$. It is not an $O(1)$-boundary formula for the unweighted convolution and supplies no low-height cross-prime certificate.

The first bounded outer telescoper is exactly the original symmetric-square Apéry recurrence

$$
(r+1)^3B_{r+1}
=(34r^3+51r^2+27r+5)B_r-r^3B_{r-1}.
\tag{A}
$$

The associated order-three differential operator is minimal. At $B_r=0$, (A) gives only the already known neighbor relation. Two zeros give the existing gap-continuant certificate and no transverse condition on the two-dimensional square-root state.

Therefore this escape hatch gives:

- no exponent below $2/3$ for $|Z_p|$;
- no new zero-pair or factorial-moment estimate;
- no new forbidden configuration beyond recurrence-only consequences;
- and no fixed-$n$ cross-prime certificate.

The narrowest useful statement still open is an actual-return anti-concentration bound

$$
\mathcal A_p(H)
:=\#\{(r,h):1\le h\le H,\ B_r\equiv B_{r+h}\equiv0\pmod p\}
\ll H^{2-\eta}p^{o(1)}
\tag{AC}
$$

for one fixed $\eta>0$. Such a theorem would break the $2/3$ exponent. Boundary telescoping does not prove it.

## 2. A genuine Christoffel--Darboux identity for the wrong sum

The recurrence has an exact self-adjoint gauge.

### Proposition 2.1 — self-adjoint normalization

Set

$$
h_n^{(\varepsilon)}
=(2n+1)^\varepsilon\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n},
\qquad s_n=h_n^{(\varepsilon)}y_n,
\tag{2.1}
$$

and

$$
p_n=(n+1)(2n+2\varepsilon+1).
\tag{2.2}
$$

Then

$$
p_ny_{n+1}+p_{n-1}y_{n-1}+12y_n
=17(p_n+p_{n-1})y_n.
\tag{2.3}
$$

#### Proof

One has

$$
\frac{h_{n+1}}{h_n}
=\frac{2n+2\varepsilon+1}{2(n+1)},
\qquad
\frac{h_{n-1}}{h_n}
=\frac{2n}{2n+2\varepsilon-1}.
$$

Substitution in (1.6), followed by division by $2h_n$, gives

$$
p_ny_{n+1}+p_{n-1}y_{n-1}=V_ny_n.
$$

Finally,

$$
V_n=17(p_n+p_{n-1})-12.
$$

This proves (2.3). ∎

Introduce $Y_n(\lambda)$ by

$$
p_nY_{n+1}(\lambda)+p_{n-1}Y_{n-1}(\lambda)+12Y_n(\lambda)
=\lambda(p_n+p_{n-1})Y_n(\lambda).
\tag{2.4}
$$

The actual normalized coefficient is $y_n=Y_n(17)$.

### Proposition 2.2 — exact same-index CD identity

For $N\ge0$, with $p_{-1}=0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(\lambda-\mu)
\sum_{i=0}^N(p_i+p_{i-1})Y_i(\lambda)Y_i(\mu)\\
&\qquad =p_N\bigl(
Y_{N+1}(\lambda)Y_N(\mu)
-Y_N(\lambda)Y_{N+1}(\mu)
\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
$$

#### Proof

Multiply (2.4) at $\lambda$ by $Y_i(\mu)$, multiply the equation at $\mu$ by $Y_i(\lambda)$, subtract, and sum. The off-diagonal terms telescope. ∎

This is a real positive identity, but it misses the target because:

1. both factors use index $i$, whereas the convolution uses $i$ and $r-i$;
2. its weight is $p_i+p_{i-1}$, whereas the normalized convolution weight is $h_i h_{r-i}$;
3. setting $\lambda=\mu=17$ gives the derivative/norm limit, still with the same index.

So the fixed second-order recurrence has CD structure, but not a CD formula for $c_r$.

## 3. Forward and reversed recurrences in the summation index

Fix $r$, and set

$$
u_i=s_i,
\qquad
v_i=s_{r-i}.
\tag{3.1}
$$

Then

$$
A_i u_{i+1}=B_i^{\rm sq}u_i-C_i u_{i-1},
\tag{3.2}
$$

while reversal gives

$$
C_{r-i}v_{i+1}=B_{r-i}^{\rm sq}v_i-A_{r-i}v_{i-1}.
\tag{3.3}
$$

The leading and trailing coefficients are exchanged. This is the exact reason that the same-index CD identity does not transfer automatically.

Consider the natural cross-Casoratian

$$
\mathcal W_i
=X_i u_{i+1}v_i-Y_i u_i v_{i+1}.
\tag{3.4}
$$

Substitution of (3.2)--(3.3) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal W_i-\mathcal W_{i-1}
={}&\left(\frac{X_iB_i^{\rm sq}}{A_i}
          -\frac{Y_iB_{r-i}^{\rm sq}}{C_{r-i}}\right)u_iv_i\\
&+\left(Y_{i-1}-\frac{X_iC_i}{A_i}\right)u_{i-1}v_i\\
&+\left(\frac{Y_iA_{r-i}}{C_{r-i}}-X_{i-1}\right)u_iv_{i-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
$$

This is the requested symbolic test. The three local bilinear monomials are independent for arbitrary solution states, so the cross terms vanish exactly when

$$
Y_{i-1}=\frac{X_iC_i}{A_i},
\qquad
X_{i-1}=\frac{Y_iA_{r-i}}{C_{r-i}}.
\tag{3.6}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
Y_i=\frac{X_{i+1}C_{i+1}}{A_{i+1}},
\tag{3.7}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\frac{X_{i+1}}{X_{i-1}}
=\frac{A_{i+1}C_{r-i}}{C_{i+1}A_{r-i}}
=\left(
\frac{(i+2)(2r-2i+2\varepsilon-1)}
     {(2i+2\varepsilon+1)(r-i+1)}
\right)^2.}
\tag{3.8}
$$

When (3.8) holds,

$$
\mathcal W_i-\mathcal W_{i-1}
=\Omega_i u_iv_i,
\tag{3.9}
$$

where

$$
\Omega_i
=\frac{X_iB_i^{\rm sq}}{A_i}
-\frac{X_{i+1}C_{i+1}B_{r-i}^{\rm sq}}
       {A_{i+1}C_{r-i}}.
\tag{3.10}
$$

Thus the classical Lagrange problem is reduced exactly to (3.8).

## 4. No rational or polynomial cross-Casoratian

### Theorem 4.1 — two-step divisor-orbit obstruction

For every integer $r\ge0$, in both branches ε = 0 and ε = 1, equation (3.8) has no nonzero rational solution in $i$. Uniformly, there is no nonzero $X(i,r)\in\overline{\mathbf Q}(i,r)$ satisfying (3.8). Hence no polynomial or rational two-term CD concomitant has difference equal to a rational multiple of $s_i s_{r-i}$.

#### Proof

For a nonzero rational function $X(i)$, the divisor of

$$
\frac{X(i+1)}{X(i-1)}
$$

has total multiplicity zero on every orbit of the translation $i\mapsto i+2$. A zero or pole of $X$ at $\alpha$ contributes at $\alpha-1$ and $\alpha+1$ with opposite signs, so the sum on each $2\mathbf Z$-orbit telescopes.

The right side of (3.8) has double zeros at

$$
i=-2,
\qquad i=r+\varepsilon-\frac12,
$$

and double poles at

$$
i=-\varepsilon-\frac12,
\qquad i=r+1.
$$

The integer zero and pole differ by $r+3$. The half-integer zero and pole differ by $r+2\varepsilon$. Exactly one of these two differences is even:

- for ε = 0, they have parities $r+3$ and $r$;
- for ε = 1, they have parities $r+3$ and $r+2$.

Thus one divisor pair lies in one translation-by-two orbit and the other does not. The unmatched orbit has nonzero total multiplicity, contradicting the necessary orbit balance of a rational shift quotient. ∎

This obstruction occurs before specifying $\Omega_i$. It excludes not only $\Omega_i=1$, but every rationally controlled weight in the natural two-term Lagrange ansatz.

## 5. The exact parity-split hypergeometric Green identity

Equation (3.8) does have solutions separately on the even and odd subsequences. For $e\in\{0,1\}$ and $i=e+2k$,

$$
\boxed{
X_{e+2k}
=X_e\left[
\frac{
\bigl((e+3)/2\bigr)_k
\bigl((-2r+2e-2\varepsilon+3)/4\bigr)_k
}{
\bigl((2e+2\varepsilon+3)/4\bigr)_k
\bigl((e-r)/2\bigr)_k
}
\right]^2.}
\tag{5.1}
$$

Indeed, substitute $i=e+2j+1$ in (3.8) and multiply for $j=0,\ldots,k-1$. With

$$
Y_i=\frac{X_{i+1}C_{i+1}}{A_{i+1}},
$$

this gives the exact weighted Green identity (3.9).

This is the strongest positive output of the boundary calculation. It is not useful for $Z_p$:

- it weights the convolution by $\Omega_i$, rather than $1$;
- even and odd indices have independent seed constants;
- the Pochhammer length is $\asymp r$ in the critical range;
- zeros and poles run through $\asymp r$ affine factors;
- standard denominator clearing has degree $\asymp r$ and logarithmic height $O(r\log r)$;
- modulo a prime comparable with $r$, endpoint degeneracies require separate normalization.

Thus (5.1) is a valid Green identity, but its coefficients encode the full convolution length. It cannot become a fixed low-degree Casoratian or a common low-height integer across primes.

## 6. The complete adjacent-state certificate equation

The preceding ansatz is not the most general local bilinear concomitant. Normalize

$$
a_i=\frac{B_i^{\rm sq}}{A_i},
\quad d_i=\frac{C_i}{A_i},
\qquad
\bar a_i=\frac{B_{r-i}^{\rm sq}}{C_{r-i}},
\quad \bar d_i=\frac{A_{r-i}}{C_{r-i}}.
\tag{6.1}
$$

Put

$$
U_i=\binom{u_i}{u_{i-1}},
\qquad
V_i=\binom{v_i}{v_{i-1}}.
$$

Then

$$
U_{i+1}=M_iU_i,
\qquad
M_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
a_i&-d_i\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$

and

$$
V_{i+1}=N_iV_i,
\qquad
N_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
\bar a_i&-\bar d_i\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6.2}
$$

For

$$
\Phi_i=U_i^TK_iV_i,
\qquad
K_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
x_i&y_i\\z_i&w_i
\end{pmatrix},
$$

the equation

$$
\Phi_{i+1}-\Phi_i=\omega_i u_iv_i
\tag{6.3}
$$

is equivalent to

$$
\boxed{M_i^TK_{i+1}N_i-K_i=\omega_iE_{11}.}
\tag{6.4}
$$

Writing $K_{i+1}=(x,y;z,w)$ gives

$$
a_i\bar a_i x+a_i y+\bar a_i z+w-x_i=\omega_i,
\tag{6.5a}
$$

$$
-\bar d_i(a_i x+z)-y_i=0,
\tag{6.5b}
$$

$$
-d_i(\bar a_i x+y)-z_i=0,
\tag{6.5c}
$$

$$
d_i\bar d_i x-w_i=0.
\tag{6.5d}
$$

These are the complete symbolic certificate equations for every adjacent-state bilinear concomitant.

## 7. Differential-module obstruction to every uniform rational boundary collapse

### Theorem 7.1 — no bounded rational order-zero telescoper

There is no uniformly bounded-complexity rational solution of

$$
M_i^TK_{i+1}N_i-K_i=E_{11}
\tag{7.1}
$$

for all $i,r$, apart from bounded exceptional loci. More generally, no nonzero polynomially weighted convolution of $s_i s_{r-i}$ collapses uniformly to finitely many boundary values through a rational finite-width concomitant, unless the weighted convolution is identically zero.

#### Step 1: a telescoper would give a linear boundary formula

Summing (7.1) from $i=0$ to $r$ gives

$$
c_r=\Phi_{r+1}-\Phi_0.
\tag{7.2}
$$

At either endpoint, one factor is among $s_{-1}=0,s_0=1$. The right side is therefore a rational linear combination of finitely many $s_{r+j}$. The second-order recurrence reduces any fixed-width boundary window to $s_r,s_{r-1}$. After clearing denominators,

$$
Q(r)c_r=P_0(r)s_r+P_1(r)s_{r-1}
\tag{7.3}
$$

for polynomials $P_0,P_1,Q$, up to a finite initial correction.

#### Step 2: generating functions give a quadratic--linear differential relation

For the square branch, $c_r=[t^r]S_+^2$. Summing (7.3) gives an identity of the shape

$$
Q(\theta)S_+^2=L_0(t,\theta)S_++H(t),
\tag{7.4}
$$

where $L_0$ is linear in derivatives and $H$ accounts for finitely many initial indices.

For polynomial weights, the exact identity

$$
\sum_{r\ge0}
\left(\sum_{i=0}^r i^a(r-i)^b s_i s_{r-i}\right)t^r
=(\theta^aS)(\theta^bS)
\tag{7.5}
$$

shows the same thing. The second-order equation reduces every higher derivative to a linear combination of $S$ and θS. Thus a boundary collapse forces a nonzero polynomial relation of degree at most two between $S$ and θS over $\overline{\mathbf Q(t)}$.

For the discriminant branch, a direct telescoper for (1.8) gives a quadratic expression for

$$
D S_-^2=F
$$

equal to a linear boundary expression in $S_-$ and θ$S_-$.

#### Step 3: the derived Galois group contains $\mathrm{SL}_2$

CFVZ use

$$
t=\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x}
$$

and a period $h(x)$ satisfying

$$
F(t(x))=(1+x)h(x)^2.
\tag{7.6}
$$

In the proof of **Lemma 2.3**, $h$ is algebraically gauged to

$$
g(y)={}_2F_1\left(\frac13,\frac23;1;y\right),
\qquad
y=\frac{27x^2}{(1-2x)^3}.
\tag{7.7}
$$

This Gauss equation is irreducible because none of

$$
\frac13,\quad\frac23,\quad1-\frac13,\quad1-\frac23
$$

is an integer. Its local monodromies at $y=0$ and $y=1$ contain nontrivial unipotents. Irreducibility forces their fixed lines to be distinct. Two nontrivial unipotent subgroups with distinct fixed lines generate a Zariski-dense subgroup of $\mathrm{SL}_2$. Hence the connected derived differential Galois group contains $\mathrm{SL}_2$.

Algebraic pullback and algebraic gauge preserve this connected derived group. Both $S_+$ and $S_-=S_+/\sqrt D$ therefore carry the same $\mathrm{SL}_2$ obstruction.

If a nonzero relation

$$
R(S,\theta S)=0
$$

existed, applying the derived $\mathrm{SL}_2$ group would make $R$ vanish on the orbit of a nonzero solution vector. That orbit is Zariski dense in the two-dimensional vector space, so $R$ would be the zero polynomial. Hence

$$
S,\theta S
\quad\text{are algebraically independent over}\quad
\overline{\mathbf Q(t)}.
\tag{7.8}
$$

This contradicts Step 2. ∎

The theorem excludes, in both branches:

- an unweighted $O(1)$-boundary collapse;
- a polynomial or rational multiple of the summand collapsing to the boundary;
- any fixed-width bilinear concomitant in neighboring square-root states;
- the directly discriminant-filtered version;
- and a bounded number of rational pole corrections.

It does not exclude finite-range certificates whose degrees or pole divisors grow with $r$; formula (5.1) exhibits that unavoidable growth.

## 8. A bounded number of interior exceptional terms does not help

The proposed escape hatch also allowed a bounded number of exceptional summands, such as a central term on one parity class.

Let

$$
\lambda=17+12\sqrt2.
$$

Substitution of $s_n\sim\mu^nn^\alpha$ into (1.6) gives

$$
\mu^2-34\mu+1=0,
\qquad
\alpha=\varepsilon-\frac32
$$

at the dominant root $\mu=\lambda$. Thus

$$
s_n^+\sim \kappa_+\lambda^n n^{-3/2}
\left(1+\frac{a_1}{n}+\cdots\right),
\tag{8.1+}
$$

and

$$
s_n^-\sim \kappa_-\lambda^n n^{-1/2}
\left(1+\frac{b_1}{n}+\cdots\right),
\tag{8.1-}
$$

with nonzero constants. The Apéry coefficients satisfy

$$
B_n\sim \kappa_B\lambda^n n^{-3/2}
\left(1+\frac{c_1}{n}+\cdots\right).
\tag{8.2}
$$

For a genuine interior exceptional index $i=\alpha_0r+O(1)$, $0<\alpha_0<1$,

$$
s_i^\varepsilon s_{r-i}^\varepsilon
=\lambda^r r^{2\varepsilon-3}
\left(C_0+\frac{C_1}{r}+\cdots\right).
\tag{8.3}
$$

The exponent class modulo integers is zero: it is $-3$ in the plus branch and $-1$ in the minus branch. Rational factors, finite shifts, and the fixed filter $1-34E^{-1}+E^{-2}$ change exponents only by integers.

Boundary values $s_{r+j}^\varepsilon$ lie in a half-integer exponent class, and $B_r$ lies in $-3/2+\mathbf Z$. Consequently finitely many interior products cannot combine with boundary terms to produce $B_r$. On either parity subsequence, finite cancellation leaves an integer-power asymptotic series; it cannot become a half-integer series. If the interior sector cancels to all orders, it contributes nothing to the dominant target and one is back to the forbidden pure boundary formula.

Exceptions at bounded distance from $0$ or $r$ are boundary terms already covered by Theorem 7.1.

## 9. The minimal outer telescoper is the original symmetric square

The Apéry operator is

$$
\mathcal L_A
=\theta^3
-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\tag{9.1}
$$

One has

$$
\mathcal L_A(S_+^2)=0,
\qquad
\mathcal L_A(D S_-^2)=0.
\tag{9.2}
$$

### Proposition 9.1 — minimality

Over $\overline{\mathbf Q(t)}$, the minimal scalar differential operator annihilating

$$
F=D^\varepsilon S_\varepsilon^2
$$

has order three.

#### Proof

The rank-two square-root module has connected derived group containing $\mathrm{SL}_2$. Its symmetric-square representation is irreducible of dimension three. The orbit of a nonzero square spans that representation. Any scalar differential operator annihilating the square must therefore have solution-space dimension at least three. Equation (9.1) supplies an operator of order three, so the bound is sharp. The algebraic factor $D$ in the minus branch is only a gauge. ∎

In coefficient language, (9.1) is exactly

$$
(r+1)^3B_{r+1}
=(34r^3+51r^2+27r+5)B_r-r^3B_{r-1}.
\tag{9.3}
$$

The order audit is:

```text
summation variable i:
    no uniform rational order-zero boundary telescoper;

outer coefficient index r:
    minimal differential telescoper order = 3;
    corresponding P-recurrence shift order = 2 (three terms);
    the recurrence is exactly the Apéry recurrence.
```

At $B_r=0$, this gives only

$$
(r+1)^3B_{r+1}=-r^3B_{r-1}.
\tag{9.4}
$$

If $B_r=B_{r+h}=0$, propagation gives the existing gap polynomial $N_h(r)$ of degree $3(h-1)$. The square-root representation adds no second independent polynomial at the same pair of zeros. The observable $B_r$ is nonlocal in the square-root state, involving all $r+1$ products in (E+) or (E-).

## 10. Dedicated check of the discriminant-times-square branch

Here

$$
F=D S_-^2,
\qquad
B_r=c_r^- -34c_{r-1}^-+c_{r-2}^-.
$$

There are three possible variants.

### 10.1 Telescope $c_r^-$, then apply $D$

Theorem 7.1 rules out the boundary formula for $c_r^-$, and Theorem 4.1 applies with ε = 1.

### 10.2 Telescope the filtered summand directly

A direct certificate is still a fixed finite-shift bilinear expression in $S_-$-states. Its generating series is $D S_-^2$. A boundary collapse gives a quadratic--linear relation in $S_-$ and θ$S_-$, forbidden by the $\mathrm{SL}_2$ argument.

### 10.3 Exploit cancellation of the dominant pole by $D$

Multiplication by $D$ cancels the leading singular part of $S_-^2$ and produces the Apéry $n^{-3/2}$ scale. This is already the gauge $S_-=S_+/\sqrt D$; it creates no new local concomitant. Section 8 shows that finitely many interior exceptional products remain in the integer asymptotic sector after the filter and cannot replace the half-integer Apéry sector.

Thus the discriminant factor changes the fixed outer filter, not the telescoping obstruction.

## 11. Tests at one zero, two zeros, and different primes

### 11.1 One zero

A boundary formula

$$
B_r=R_0(r)s_r+R_1(r)s_{r-1}
$$

would turn $B_r=0$ into a projective line in the two-dimensional square-root state. Theorems 4.1 and 7.1 rule out such a line. The event remains full Cauchy cancellation.

### 11.2 Two zeros at one prime

If $B_r=B_{r+h}=0$, the only bounded local relation is the symmetric-square recurrence. It yields the same gap continuant $N_h(r)$ already used in the $p^{2/3}$ proof. There is no lower-degree square-root Casoratian and no second transverse equation.

### 11.3 Fixed $n$, different primes

For a top-half target prime, $r_p=n-p$ and $p\mid B_{r_p}$. A useful cross-prime identity would need a common characteristic-zero integer of subexponential height, or uniformly low-degree local state conditions. The only explicit Green coefficient found, (5.1), has length and divisor complexity growing with $r_p$. Different primes use different lengths and reductions. Clearing it produces no common low-height integer.

A certificate solved separately in each finite field by backward recursion merely stores the partial convolution. It has no bounded-degree characteristic-zero lift and cannot be compared across primes.

## 12. Narrowest anti-cancellation lemma still open

Define the actual short-pair incidence

$$
\mathcal A_p(H)
=\#\left\{(r,h):
\begin{array}{l}
0\le r<r+h<p,\\
1\le h\le H,\\
B_r\equiv B_{r+h}\equiv0\pmod p
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{12.1}
$$

The current degree argument gives only

$$
\mathcal A_p(H)\ll H^2.
\tag{12.2}
$$

The packing proof can be written

$$
|Z_p|
\le \left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil+\mathcal A_p(H).
\tag{12.3}
$$

Therefore, for any fixed $\eta>0$, a theorem

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A_p(H)
\ll H^{2-\eta}p^{o(1)}
}
\tag{12.4}
$$

in the critical range would give, with $H=p^{1/(3-\eta)}$,

$$
|Z_p|
\ll p^{(2-\eta)/(3-\eta)+o(1)},
\tag{12.5}
$$

and

$$
\frac{2-\eta}{3-\eta}<\frac23.
$$

This is weaker than full equidistribution or a full moment theorem. It asks only that actual convolution-zero pairs occupy a power-saving fraction of the ambient roots allowed by all short gap continuants. The audit above shows that such a result must be genuine anti-cancellation or return-direction information; it cannot come from collapsing each coefficient to $O(1)$ square-root boundary values.

## 13. Final proof audit

```text
PROVED HERE
-----------
1. Exact self-adjoint gauge for both square-root recurrences.
2. Exact same-index Christoffel--Darboux identity.
3. Exact reversed-index Lagrange certificate equations.
4. No rational/polynomial two-term CD certificate, by a
   translation-by-two divisor-orbit obstruction.
5. Explicit parity-split hypergeometric weighted Green identity.
6. Exact 2x2 matrix equation for every adjacent-state bilinear
   concomitant.
7. No uniform rational order-zero boundary telescoper, by the
   SL2 differential-module obstruction.
8. O(1) interior exceptional terms cannot repair the collapse.
9. The minimal outer differential telescoper has order 3 and is
   exactly the Apéry symmetric-square operator.
10. The discriminant-times-square branch has the same obstruction.

NOT OBTAINED
------------
1. Any new exponent below 2/3 for |Z_p|.
2. Any nontrivial zero-pair or factorial-moment estimate.
3. Any new forbidden zero configuration.
4. Any transverse local square-root-state condition at B_r=0.
5. Any fixed-n cross-prime low-height certificate.

PRECISE SURVIVING TARGET
------------------------
A power-saving actual-return estimate
    A_p(H) << H^(2-eta) p^o(1)
for one fixed eta>0.
```

The boundary-telescoping escape hatch is exhausted. The fixed second-order CFVZ square root has useful self-adjoint and hypergeometric structure, but the coefficient-zero event lives in its symmetric-square convolution. The only bounded closure of that convolution is the original Apéry recurrence. Any improvement requires a genuine anti-cancellation theorem.
