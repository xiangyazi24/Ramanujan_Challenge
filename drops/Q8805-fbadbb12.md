# Definitive analysis: bilinear large sieve for Apéry moving zero sets

## Executive conclusion

The bilinear formulation is the correct analytic reformulation of LGTQ-3, but the current technology does not directly apply. The obstruction is not the prime sum itself; it is that the coefficient family

\[
f(p,r)=1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}
\]

is not a fixed-modulus trace-function family. The recurrence orbit has conductor growing with the interval length. Therefore DFI, Deshouillers-Iwaniec, FKM, Vaughan, and Heath-Brown methods do not automatically produce the needed square-root cancellation.

The most realistic next theorem is not the pointwise bound
\[
\max_n S(n)=o(P/\log P),
\]
but a stronger-than-L2 averaged statement: a fourth moment / large-values estimate for the moving zero set.

---

# 1. DFI bilinear forms

Classical DFI estimates apply to sums

\[
\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}a_mb_n e(\alpha mn)
\]

or more generally to oscillatory phases whose arithmetic dependence factors through a controlled modulus.

The Apéry sum is

\[
T(u,n)=\sum_{p\sim P} e_p(u b_{n\bmod p}).
\]

Writing \(n=pk+r\) gives

\[
e_p(u b_r),
\]

but the residue variable is coupled to the modulus. There is no fixed level N modular form or automorphic coefficient behind the summand.

The effective "level" is roughly p for each p, and the conductor of the orbit segment
\(r\mapsto b_r\pmod p\) is of size comparable with p. This is precisely outside the standard DFI framework.

---

# 2. Kloosterman analogy

There is a superficial analogy:

\[
S(m,n;c)=\sum_x e_c(mx+n\bar x)
\]

and

\[
e_p(u b_r)
\]

are both finite-field oscillations.

However Kloosterman sums have a fixed algebraic phase in x. Deligne theory gives square-root cancellation because the sheaf has bounded conductor.

The Apéry orbit phase is generated by a length-r non-autonomous matrix product. The corresponding sheaf, if constructed, has conductor increasing with r.

Thus there is no known Kloosterman replacement theorem.

---

# 3. Fouvry-Kowalski-Michel trace function technology

FKM applies when one has trace functions

\[
t_p(x)=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_x|\mathcal F_p)
\]

with bounded conductor and controlled monodromy.

For Apéry numbers modulo p, one would need a family

\[
r\mapsto b_r\pmod p
\]

represented by a bounded-complexity l-adic sheaf.

The recurrence gives instead a product

\[
M(r-1)\cdots M(0),
\]

whose complexity grows with r. Unless one finds a hidden Frobenius compression or a finite-state quotient, FKM does not apply.

---

# 4. Vaughan / Heath-Brown identities

These identities decompose prime sums such as

\[
\sum_{p}a_p
\]

into Type I and Type II bilinear sums.

They help when the coefficient sequence \(a_n\) is defined for integers n independently of the prime modulus.

Here

\[
a_p=e_p(u b_{n\bmod p})
\]

changes its definition with p. Prime factorization identities do not remove this moving-modulus dependence.

They may help after a successful transformation to a fixed-modulus object, but not before.

---

# 5. The conductor obstruction

This is the central issue.

For fixed p, the recurrence orbit is

\[
v_r=M(r-1)\cdots M(0)v_0.
\]

A polynomial phase has bounded complexity. A trace function has bounded conductor. The Apéry orbit has complexity roughly proportional to r.

Therefore the naive expectation

\[
|T(u,n)|\ll P^{1/2+\epsilon}
\]

is currently beyond available technology.

A proof would require one of:

1. a hidden algebraic factorization reducing the orbit to bounded complexity;
2. a new large sieve theorem for non-autonomous SL(2) recurrence orbits;
3. strong independence between Frobenius conjugacy classes of the Apéry Picard-Fuchs family.

---

# 6. What can realistically be proved next?

## Almost-all n

Yes. This is the natural continuation of the existing L2 result.

The second moment gives

\[
\sum_n S(n)^2 \ll N(P/\log P)^{1+o(1)}.
\]

The next target is a fourth moment:

\[
\sum_n S(n)^4\ll N(P/\log P)^{2+o(1)}.
\]

This would imply

\[
\#\{n:S(n)>L\}\ll N L^{-4}(P/\log P)^2.
\]

This is the correct replacement for pointwise control.

## Most primes p

Also plausible. Since

\[
|Z_p|=O(p^{2/3})
\]

is known and empirical size is constant, exceptional primes could potentially be isolated using the modular form relation and Frobenius statistics.

## L2 improvement

The next meaningful improvement is not a better constant in L2. It is a large-values estimate:

\[
\#\{n:S(n)>V\}
\]

with power savings in V.

---

# 7. Type I / Type II information

## Type I

Available.

Known inputs:

- Lucas congruence:
\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
\]

- block law:
\[
p^3a_{qp+r}\equiv b_r a_q\pmod p.
\]

- bounds on \(|Z_p|\).

These give good control when one variable is averaged.

## Type II

Missing.

The required Type II statement is essentially:

For separated ranges of primes and moduli,

\[
\sum_{p,q} \alpha_p\beta_q
1_{b_{n\bmod pq}=0}
\]

must exhibit cancellation.

This is exactly equivalent to controlling correlations of independent Apéry recurrence orbits modulo different primes.

No existing sieve theorem supplies this.

---

# Final verdict

The bilinear large sieve viewpoint is the right endpoint formulation, but the missing ingredient is a new theorem:

> A large sieve inequality for moving-modulus Frobenius-recursive trace families.

Classical tools fail for a precise reason:

- DFI: requires fixed modular oscillation.
- Deshouillers-Iwaniec: requires Kloosterman-type bounded conductor.
- FKM: requires bounded-conductor trace functions.
- Vaughan/Heath-Brown: cannot remove the moving modulus.

The highest-value next attack is therefore:

1. prove a fourth-moment bound for \(S(n)\);
2. reinterpret the recurrence orbit as an l-adic family with controlled conductor;
3. exploit the Apéry Picard-Fuchs/K3 geometry to obtain cross-prime independence.

The bilinear formulation has identified the exact missing theorem, but it does not currently provide a proof route by existing large sieve technology.
