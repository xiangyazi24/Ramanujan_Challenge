# Q3284 — exact lattice classification of the `C*` excess

## Executive verdict

The exact abstract invariant is simple:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
=[\tau(\mathcal K):\tau(\mathcal R^*)]
=[\mathcal K:\mathcal R^*+(\mathcal K\cap\ker\tau)] .}
\]

More precisely,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K/(\mathcal R^*+(\mathcal K\cap\ker\tau))
\simeq \mathbf Z/(\Gamma/\Delta)\mathbf Z .}
\]

Here `K` is the full integral relation lattice among the two slope families and `R*` is the sublattice generated by the three-row Bezout relations whose `T`-values are the `C*_{s,K}`. Thus `Gamma/Delta` is exactly the **T-visible saturation defect** of those relations.

This does **not** lead to an unconditional small bound. The three-row carrier has at least three missing free directions globally: one internal direction on each slope and one cross-slope selector direction. The special hypergeometric vector `e_K` controls only the finite saturation defect of the *full adjacent-relation lattice*. It does not control the free direction lost when the adjacent values `V_K` are replaced by the `C*_{s,K}`.

In fact there is a decisive counterexample mechanism: fix the actual q=6 hypergeometric `e`-vectors, the two intervals, all Bezout choices, and even prescribed selector values `B_4^H,B_7^H`. For every `M>=1` one can choose integral `T_K` satisfying all stated abstract hypotheses with

\[
D_4=D_7=1,\qquad \Delta=1,\qquad M\mid\Gamma.
\]

Therefore no theorem depending only on `e`, the recurrence coefficients, endpoints, or selectors can force `Gamma/Delta` to divide a fixed endpoint expression, a fixed product of local gcds, or a fixed polynomial in `m,H`; its height is abstractly unbounded.

What *is* exact is the missing complementary coordinate. With

\[
\alpha_{s,K}=X_s+2K+1,\qquad
 g_{s,K}=\gcd(\alpha_{s,K},2),
\]

define

\[
\boxed{
W_{s,K}:=
\frac{(\alpha_{s,K}+2)V_K-\alpha_{s,K}V_{K+1}}
     {g_{s,K}}.}
\]

Then `(C*_{s,K},W_{s,K})` is a unimodular transform of `(V_K,V_{K+1})`, so

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(C^*_{s,K},W_{s,K})=
\gcd(V_K,V_{K+1}).}
\]

This isolates the smallest genuinely new Apéry input: one complementary `T`-mode on each slope and one cross-slope selector mode. Once those three modes are controlled, the rest is forced into explicit hypergeometric/Bezout products of short logarithmic height.

## 1. One-slope relation lattices

Fix a slope `s` and write its legal interval as

\[
I_s=[a,b]\cap\mathbf Z,
\qquad a=A_s,\quad b=B_s.
\]

Let

\[
F_s=\bigoplus_{K=a}^{b}\mathbf Z\delta_K
\]

with homomorphisms

\[
\varepsilon_s(\delta_K)=e_K,
\qquad
\tau_s(\delta_K)=T_K.
\]

Since `gcd_K e_K=1`, `epsilon_s` is surjective. Hence

\[
\mathcal K_s:=\ker\varepsilon_s
\]

is a saturated direct summand of `F_s`, of rank `b-a`.

For `a<=K<=b-1`, define the adjacent recurrence relation

\[
r_{s,K}:=(X_s+K)\delta_K+(K+1)\delta_{K+1}.
\]

The recurrence

\[
(K+1)e_{K+1}=-(X_s+K)e_K
\]

says precisely that `r_{s,K}` lies in `K_s`, and

\[
\tau_s(r_{s,K})=(X_s+K)T_K+(K+1)T_{K+1}=V_K.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal R_s^{\rm adj}:=
\langle r_{s,K}:a\le K\le b-1\rangle_{\mathbf Z}.
\]

Now put

\[
\alpha_K=X_s+2K+1,
\qquad g_K=\gcd(\alpha_K,2)
          =\gcd(\alpha_K,\alpha_K+2),
\]

and choose integers `u_K,v_K` satisfying

\[
u_K\alpha_K+v_K(\alpha_K+2)=g_K.
\]

The preceding displayed line is simply the hypothesis `u_K alpha_K + v_K(alpha_K+2)=g_K`; no new symbol is intended before `u_K`.

Define, for `a<=K<=b-2`,

\[
c_{s,K}:=u_Kr_{s,K}+v_Kr_{s,K+1}.
\]

Then

\[
\tau_s(c_{s,K})=u_KV_K+v_KV_{K+1}=C^*_{s,K},
\]

and

\[
\mathcal R_s^*:=
\langle c_{s,K}:a\le K\le b-2\rangle
\subset
\mathcal R_s^{\rm adj}\subset\mathcal K_s.
\]

The occurrence of `g_K` has a direct three-row meaning. If

\[
q_K=(e_K,1,T_K),
\]

then

\[
(X_s+K)q_K+(K+1)q_{K+1}
=(0,\alpha_K,V_K),
\]

\[
(X_s+K+1)q_{K+1}+(K+2)q_{K+2}
=(0,\alpha_K+2,V_{K+1}),
\]

and the chosen Bezout combination gives

\[
(0,g_K,C^*_{s,K}).
\]

## 2. `D_s`, `G_s`, and `Gamma_s`

The map

\[
\Phi_s:F_s\to\mathbf Z^2,
\qquad
\Phi_s(x)=(\varepsilon_s(x),\tau_s(x))
\]

has image the row lattice `L_s`. By hypothesis,

\[
L_s=\mathbf Z(1,B_s^H)+\mathbf Z(0,D_s).
\]

Since `epsilon_s` is onto, the pure vertical subgroup is

\[
L_s\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbf Z)
=\{0\}\times\tau_s(\mathcal K_s),
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{\tau_s(\mathcal K_s)=D_s\mathbf Z.}
\]

This is also the usual gcd-of-`2x2`-minors interpretation of `D_s`.

Define

\[
G_s:=\gcd_{a\le K\le b-1}V_K,
\qquad
\Gamma_s:=\gcd_{a\le K\le b-2}C^*_{s,K}.
\]

Because

\[
\mathcal R_s^*\subset
\mathcal R_s^{\rm adj}\subset
\mathcal K_s,
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{D_s\mid G_s\mid\Gamma_s}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma_s}{D_s}
=rac{\Gamma_s}{G_s}\frac{G_s}{D_s}.}
\]

The two factors are different in nature:

* `G_s/D_s` is a finite saturation defect of the complete adjacent recurrence lattice;
* `Gamma_s/G_s` is the loss caused by retaining only one linear combination of each adjacent pair of `V`'s.

The first is controlled by the hypergeometric `e`; the second is not.

## 3. Exact Smith index supplied by the hypergeometric `e`

Relative to the basis `delta_a,...,delta_b`, the adjacent relations form the bidiagonal matrix

\[
\mathcal A_s=
\begin{pmatrix}
X_s+a & a+1 \\
& X_s+a+1 & a+2 \\
&&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&X_s+b-1&b
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Deleting column `j`, `a<=j<=b`, gives the maximal minor

\[
M_{s,j}=
\prod_{i=a}^{j-1}(X_s+i)
\prod_{i=j}^{b-1}(i+1)
\]

up to sign. Hence

\[
\boxed{
I_s:=\gcd_{j=a}^{b}|M_{s,j}|
      =[\mathcal K_s:\mathcal R_s^{\rm adj}].}
\]

Proof: the rational row span of `A_s` is `ker(epsilon_s) tensor Q`; since `K_s` is saturated, the index of the row lattice in `K_s` is the gcd of the maximal minors.

The signed cofactor vector

\[
z_j=(-1)^{j-a}M_{s,j}
\]

satisfies

\[
(i+1)z_{i+1}=-(X_s+i)z_i.
\]

Because the given `e`-vector is the primitive integer solution of this same first-order recurrence,

\[
\boxed{z_j=\pm I_s e_j}
\]

with one global sign. Thus `I_s` is precisely the Smith/content scalar behind the primitive hypergeometric vector

\[
e_K=(-1)^K\binom{X_s+K-1}{K}/\operatorname{content}.
\]

Applying `tau_s` gives a surjection

\[
\mathcal K_s/\mathcal R_s^{\rm adj}
\twoheadrightarrow
D_s\mathbf Z/G_s\mathbf Z,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\frac{G_s}{D_s}\mid I_s.}
\]

Prime by prime this is completely explicit:

\[
\boxed{
v_p(I_s)=
\min_{a\le j\le b}
\left(
\sum_{i=a}^{j-1}v_p(X_s+i)
+
\sum_{i=j}^{b-1}v_p(i+1)
\right).}
\]

For nonnegative cutoffs,

\[
\boxed{
I_s\mid\prod_{i=a}^{b-1}(i+1)=\frac{b!}{a!},}
\]

and also

\[
I_s\mid\prod_{i=a}^{b-1}|X_s+i|.
\]

Thus

\[
\log(G_s/D_s)\le \log I_s
\le O((b-a)\log(b+1)).
\]

This is the strongest unconditional consequence of the special `e_K` needed below.

## 4. Exact one-slope free-mode defect

Identify the adjacent relation lattice with

\[
N_s:=\bigoplus_{K=a}^{b-1}\mathbf Z f_K,
\qquad f_K\mapsto r_{s,K}.
\]

The `C*` relations correspond to the sublattice

\[
B_s:=\langle
u_K f_K+v_K f_{K+1}:a\le K\le b-2
\rangle,
\]

where the intended first coefficient in that display is `u_K`; equivalently, and unambiguously,

\[
\boxed{
B_s=\langle u_Kf_K+v_Kf_{K+1}:a\le K\le b-2\rangle.}
\]

Define

\[
\lambda_s(f_K)=V_K.
\]

Then

\[
\lambda_s(N_s)=G_s\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\lambda_s(B_s)=\Gamma_s\mathbf Z.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma_s}{G_s}
=[\lambda_s(N_s):\lambda_s(B_s)]
=[N_s:B_s+(N_s\cap\ker\lambda_s)].}
\]

There are `b-a` adjacent basis vectors but only `b-a-1` three-row generators. Hence one free mode is missing whenever the latter have their expected rank. In particular, the ordinary index `[N_s:B_s]` is infinite: there is no finite Smith determinant that can by itself bound `Gamma_s/G_s`.

## 5. The omitted coordinate `W` and unimodularity

For `a<=K<=b-2`, define the complementary relation

\[
\omega_{s,K}:=
\frac{\alpha_K+2}{g_K}r_{s,K}
-
\frac{\alpha_K}{g_K}r_{s,K+1}.
\]

Then

\[
W_{s,K}:=\tau_s(\omega_{s,K})
=
\frac{(\alpha_K+2)V_K-\alpha_KV_{K+1}}{g_K}.
\]

Relative to `(r_{s,K},r_{s,K+1})`, the two relations `(c_{s,K},omega_{s,K})` have coefficient rows

\[
(u_K,v_K),
\qquad
((\alpha_K+2)/g_K,-\alpha_K/g_K).
\]

Their determinant is

\[
-\frac{u_K\alpha_K+v_K(\alpha_K+2)}{g_K}=-1.
\]

Hence the transformation is unimodular:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf Zc_{s,K}+\mathbf Z\omega_{s,K}
=
\mathbf Zr_{s,K}+\mathbf Zr_{s,K+1}.}
\]

Applying `tau_s`,

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(C^*_{s,K},W_{s,K})
=\gcd(V_K,V_{K+1}).}
\]

Taking all `K`,

\[
\boxed{
\gcd\bigl(
\{C^*_{s,K}\}_K\cup\{W_{s,K}\}_K
\bigr)=G_s.}
\]

Let

\[
\Omega_s:=\gcd_K W_{s,K}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\gcd(\Gamma_s,\Omega_s)=G_s.}
\]

Since the global `Gamma` divides `Gamma_s`, this also gives

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_s)=\gcd(\Gamma,G_s).}
\]

Thus the internal free-mode defect visible to the global carrier is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,G_s)}
=
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_s)}.}
\]

### Dependence on the Bezout choice

Every other Bezout solution is

\[
 u'_K=u_K+t\frac{\alpha_K+2}{g_K},
\qquad
 v'_K=v_K-t\frac{\alpha_K}{g_K}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{C^{*\prime}_{s,K}=C^*_{s,K}+tW_{s,K}.}
\]

So `Gamma` itself is not generally invariant under a change of the chosen Bezout coefficients. The pair `(C*,W)` is the unimodularly complete local datum.

## 6. Combined two-slope lattice: exact formula for `Gamma/Delta`

Set

\[
F=F_4\oplus F_7,
\]

\[
\varepsilon(x_4,x_7)
=\varepsilon_4(x_4)+\varepsilon_7(x_7),
\qquad
\tau(x_4,x_7)
=\tau_4(x_4)+\tau_7(x_7),
\]

and

\[
\mathcal K=\ker\varepsilon,
\qquad
\mathcal R^*=\mathcal R_4^*\oplus\mathcal R_7^*.
\]

The combined row lattice is generated by

\[
(1,B_4^H),\ (0,D_4),\ (1,B_7^H),\ (0,D_7),
\]

or equivalently by

\[
(1,B_4^H),\ (0,D_4),\ (0,D_7),
(0,B_7^H-B_4^H).
\]

Its pure vertical subgroup is therefore

\[
\{0\}\times\Delta\mathbf Z,
\]

where

\[
\Delta=\gcd(D_4,D_7,B_7^H-B_4^H).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\tau(\mathcal K)=\Delta\mathbf Z.}
\]

By definition,

\[
\boxed{\tau(\mathcal R^*)=\Gamma\mathbf Z.}
\]

Hence `Delta | Gamma` and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
=[\Delta\mathbf Z:\Gamma\mathbf Z]
=[\tau(\mathcal K):\tau(\mathcal R^*)].}
\]

Moreover

\[
\mathcal K/(\mathcal K\cap\ker\tau)
\simeq\Delta\mathbf Z,
\]

and the image of `R*` is `Gamma Z`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K/(\mathcal R^*+(\mathcal K\cap\ker\tau))
\simeq
\mathbf Z/(\Gamma/\Delta)\mathbf Z.}
\]

This is the requested exact abstract lattice/index formula.

### Where the three missing directions come from

If `n_s=|I_s|` and `N=n_4+n_7`, then

\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal K=N-1,
\]

while

\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal R^*
\le(n_4-2)+(n_7-2)=N-4.
\]

So `K/R*` has free rank at least three. In the usual nondegenerate legal q=6 ranges the `C*` chains have their expected rank, and the corank is exactly three: one missing adjacent mode for each slope and one cross-slope relation.

For the cross direction, choose `w_s in F_s` with

\[
\varepsilon_s(w_s)=1,
\qquad
\tau_s(w_s)=B_s^H.
\]

Then

\[
h_\times=(-w_4,w_7)\in\mathcal K,
\qquad
\tau(h_\times)=B_7^H-B_4^H.
\]

## 7. Exact prime-wise decomposition of the excess

Put

\[
E_\times:=B_7^H-B_4^H.
\]

A valuation calculation gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
=\operatorname{lcm}\left(
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_4)},
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_7)},
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,E_\times)}
\right).}
\]

Indeed, if `a=v_p(Gamma)`, `d_s=v_p(D_s)`, and `e=v_p(E_x)`, both sides have `p`-valuation

\[
a-\min(d_4,d_7,e).
\]

Now `G_s/D_s | I_s`, and Section 5 gives

\[
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,G_s)}
=rac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_s)}.
\]

It follows that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}
\mid
I_s\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_s)}.}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
\mid
\operatorname{lcm}\left(
I_4\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_4)},
I_7\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_7)},
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,E_\times)}
\right).}
\]

This is a sharp structural classification. It is not yet a numerical bound because the three displayed residual factors are precisely the three uncontrolled free modes.

## 8. Decisive counterexample mechanism

### Theorem

Fix the two slope intervals, each with at least three rows; fix the primitive nonzero recurrence vectors `e_K` (in particular, the actual q=6 hypergeometric vectors); fix all Bezout choices; and fix arbitrary integers `beta_4,beta_7` to serve as the selector values.

For every integer `M>=1` there exist integral values `T_K` on both slopes such that

\[
L_s=\mathbf Z(1,\beta_s)+\mathbf Z(0,1),
\]

so `D_s=1` and `B_s^H` may be taken to be `beta_s`, while every `C^*_{s,K}` is divisible by `M` and at least one is nonzero. Hence

\[
\Delta=1,
\qquad
M\mid\Gamma.
\]

### Proof

For one slope, let

\[
S_s=\operatorname{Sat}_{\mathcal K_s}(\mathcal R_s^*)
=\mathcal K_s\cap(\mathcal R_s^*\otimes\mathbf Q).
\]

Since `rank R_s^* <= n_s-2 < n_s-1 = rank K_s`, `S_s` is a proper saturated sublattice. Hence

\[
\mathcal K_s=S_s\oplus C_s
\]

for a nonzero free complement `C_s`.

Choose a nonzero `r in R_s^*`. Since `S_s` is free, choose a homomorphism

\[
\ell_s:S_s\to\mathbf Z
\]

with `ell_s(r) != 0`. Choose a primitive element `y_s in C_s` and define a homomorphism on `C_s` taking `y_s` to `1`. Define

\[
\tau_s|_{S_s}=M\ell_s,
\qquad
\tau_s(y_s)=1,
\]

and extend over a basis of `C_s`. Then

\[
\tau_s(\mathcal K_s)=\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\tau_s(\mathcal R_s^*)\subset M\mathbf Z,
\]

with a nonzero value on `r`.

Because `e` is primitive, `K_s` is a direct summand of `F_s`. Choose `w_s` with `epsilon_s(w_s)=1`; then

\[
F_s=\mathcal K_s\oplus\mathbf Zw_s.
\]

Extend `tau_s` by setting

\[
\tau_s(w_s)=\beta_s,
\]

and finally define

\[
T_K=\tau_s(\delta_K).
\]

The image row lattice contains `(1,beta_s)` and `(0,1)`, so it is exactly

\[
\mathbf Z(1,\beta_s)+\mathbf Z(0,1).
\]

Thus `D_s=1`. Do this independently for `s=4,7`. Then every `C*` is a multiple of `M`, at least one is nonzero, and

\[
\Delta=\gcd(1,1,\beta_7-\beta_4)=1.
\]

Therefore `M | Gamma/Delta`. QED.

### Consequence

This counterexample keeps the hypergeometric `e`, endpoints, Bezout choices, and selector values fixed. Hence no bound depending only on those data can exist. In particular, from the stated premises alone there is no fixed polynomial `P(m,H)` with

\[
\Gamma/\Delta\mid P(m,H),
\]

nor any uniform sublinear logarithmic-height estimate. The observed actual ratios `1,2,11,...` do not imply a finite abstract classification.

## 9. One explicit complementary anchor per slope

Although the abstract excess is unbounded, one additional scalar on a slope gives an explicit determinant bound.

Fix `j in [a,b-2]`. Form the square matrix whose rows, relative to the adjacent basis `r_{s,a},...,r_{s,b-1}`, are all the `c_{s,K}` together with the one complementary row `omega_{s,j}`. Its determinant has absolute value

\[
\boxed{
H_{s,j}=
\left|
\prod_{i=a}^{j-1}u_i
\prod_{i=j+1}^{b-2}v_i
\right|.}
\]

Reason: `(c_{s,j},omega_{s,j})` is a unimodular replacement of `(r_{s,j},r_{s,j+1})`; after that replacement the remaining left and right blocks are triangular.

Let `gamma` divide every `C^*_{s,K}`. Apply the adjugate identity to the augmented square matrix and to the primitive vector `(V_K)/G_s`. Prime by prime one obtains

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\gamma}{\gcd(\gamma,G_s)}
\mid
H_{s,j}
\frac{\gamma}{\gcd(\gamma,W_{s,j})}.}
\]

Combining with `G_s/D_s | I_s` gives, for `gamma=Gamma`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}
\mid
I_sH_{s,j}
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,W_{s,j})}.}
\]

Thus the sole remaining scalar defect at that anchor is exactly

\[
\Gamma/\gcd(\Gamma,W_{s,j}).
\]

In particular, the sufficient premise

\[
\Gamma\mid W_{s,j}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}
\mid I_sH_{s,j}.}
\]

If `Gamma` divides every `W_{s,K}`, then `Gamma | G_s` and the `H` factor is unnecessary:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}\mid I_s.}
\]

## 10. Explicit Bezout product for the q=6 slope chains

Because

\[
\alpha_{K+1}=\alpha_K+2,
\]

the parity of `alpha_K` is constant along a slope interval.

### Even parity

If `alpha_K` is even, then `g_K=2` and the coherent choice

\[
 u_K=-1,
\qquad
 v_K=1
\]

satisfies the Bezout equation. Therefore

\[
\boxed{H_{s,j}=1}
\]

for every anchor.

### Odd parity

If `alpha_K` is odd, put

\[
t_K=\frac{\alpha_K+1}{2}.
\]

Then `t_{K+1}=t_K+1`, and the coherent choice

\[
 u_K=t_K,
\qquad
 v_K=-(t_K-1)
\]

satisfies

\[
 u_K\alpha_K+v_K(\alpha_K+2)=1.
\]

On the legal positive ranges, the anchor determinant is independent of `j` and equals

\[
\boxed{
H_s=
\prod_{i=a}^{b-3}|t_i|
=
\prod_{i=a}^{b-3}
\left|\frac{X_s+2i+2}{2}\right|.}
\]

The empty product is `1` when `b-a=2`.

This can also be read as the top determinantal divisor of the coefficient matrix of the `C*` chain. Its maximal minors are

\[
\prod_{i=a}^{j-1}u_i
\prod_{i=j}^{b-2}v_i,
\qquad a\le j\le b-1.
\]

After factoring

\[
P=\prod_{i=a}^{b-3}t_i,
\]

the remaining factors are consecutive integers

\[
t_a-1,t_a,t_a+1,\ldots,t_{b-2},
\]

whose gcd is `1`, so the determinantal divisor is exactly `|P|`.

Thus, under one anchor congruence `Gamma | W_{s,j}`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}
\mid
I_s
\prod_{i=a}^{b-3}
\left|\frac{X_s+2i+2}{2}\right|
}
\]

in odd parity, while in even parity only `I_s` remains.

This is a genuine explicit product-of-local-linear-factors theorem, but it is conditional on controlling the missing `T` mode.

## 11. Global conditional divisor and height bound

Let

\[
E_\times=B_7^H-B_4^H.
\]

Suppose that for the actual Apéry `T_K` there are anchors `j_4,j_7` and explicit integers `R_4,R_7,R_x` such that

\[
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,W_{4,j_4})}\mid R_4,
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,W_{7,j_7})}\mid R_7,
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,E_\times)}\mid R_x.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
\mid
\operatorname{lcm}
\bigl(
I_4H_{4,j_4}R_4,
I_7H_{7,j_7}R_7,
R_x
\bigr).}
\]

A particularly clean sufficient premise is

\[
\Gamma\mid W_{4,j_4},
\qquad
\Gamma\mid W_{7,j_7},
\qquad
\Gamma\mid(B_7^H-B_4^H),
\]

which yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
\mid
\operatorname{lcm}
(I_4H_{4,j_4},I_7H_{7,j_7}).}
\]

For nonnegative cutoffs,

\[
I_s\mid B_s!/A_s!,
\]

and the coherent Bezout `H_s` is either `1` or a product of `B_s-A_s-2` linear factors. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\log(I_sH_s)
