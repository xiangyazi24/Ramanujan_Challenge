# Q3284 — exact classification of the `C*` carrier defect

## Verdict

There is a clean exact abstract answer, but it is negative for the hoped-for unconditional compression.

Let `R*` be the lattice generated by the three-row Bezout relations whose `T`-values are the numbers `C*_{s,K}`, and let `K` be the full integral relation lattice among all rows from slopes 4 and 7. Then

\[
\boxed{\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
 = [\tau(K):\tau(R^*)]
 = [K:R^*+(K\cap\ker\tau)]}
\]

and in fact

\[
\boxed{
K/(R^*+(K\cap\ker\tau))\simeq
\mathbf Z/(\Gamma/\Delta)\mathbf Z .}
\]

Thus `Gamma/Delta` is exactly the **`T`-visible saturation defect** of the three-row carrier lattice. It is not the ordinary saturation index of `R*`: the latter has free corank at least three. In the nondegenerate legal case those three missing free directions are exactly

1. one internal direction on the slope-4 interval;
2. one internal direction on the slope-7 interval;
3. one cross-slope selector direction.

The special hypergeometric vector `e_K` controls a finite torsion defect of the *full adjacent-relation lattice*, but it does **not** control the missing free direction left after replacing the adjacent values `V_K` by the `C*_{s,K}`. Consequently, from the hypotheses in the question alone there is no bound at all on `Gamma/Delta`, even with the two `e`-vectors fixed to the actual q=6 hypergeometric vectors and even with the selector values `B_s^H` prescribed. For every integer `M` one can choose integral `T_K` satisfying all stated lattice hypotheses with

\[
D_4=D_7=1,\qquad \Delta=1,\qquad M\mid\Gamma.
\]

So `Gamma/Delta` is **not** forced to divide any fixed endpoint expression, any product depending only on the local recurrence coefficients, or any polynomial in `m,H`; its logarithmic height can be made arbitrarily large with `e`, the intervals, and the selector values held fixed.

There is, however, a sharp complementary quantity. Put

\[
\alpha_{s,K}=X_s+2K+1,
\qquad g_{s,K}=\gcd(\alpha_{s,K},2),
\]

and define

\[
W_{s,K}:=
 \frac{(\alpha_{s,K}+2)V_K-\alpha_{s,K}V_{K+1}}{g_{s,K}}.
\]

Then `(C*_{s,K},W_{s,K})` is a unimodular transform of `(V_K,V_{K+1})`. Hence

\[
\boxed{\gcd(C^*_{s,K},W_{s,K})=\gcd(V_K,V_{K+1})}
\]

and over a whole slope interval

\[
\boxed{
\gcd\bigl(\{C^*_{s,K}\}_K\cup\{W_{s,K}\}_K\bigr)
 =\gcd_K V_K .}
\]

So `W` is exactly the information discarded by `C*`, not another heuristic carrier.

The smallest new Apéry-specific input must therefore control three scalar modes: one `W`-complement on each slope and the cross-slope selector mode. A single explicit `W` anchor on each slope already gives a controlled local-product loss; if the missing modes are themselves `Gamma`-divisible, the remaining excess is forced into an explicit hypergeometric Smith product of logarithmic height `O(L_s log(|X_s|+B_s))`, with `L_s=B_s-A_s`. In a length-`O(H)` short window this is of the desired `O(H^2+(log m)^2)` scale. Without those missing scalar constraints, no such theorem follows.

## 1. Relation lattices

Fix one slope `s`, and abbreviate its cutoff interval by

\[
I_s=[a,b]\cap\mathbf Z,
\qquad a=A_s,\quad b=B_s.
\]

Let

\[
F_s:=\bigoplus_{K=a}^{b}\mathbf Z\,\delta_K,
\]

and define homomorphisms

\[
\varepsilon_s:F_s\to\mathbf Z,
\qquad \varepsilon_s(\delta_K)=e_K,
\]

\[
\tau_s:F_s\to\mathbf Z,
\qquad \tau_s(\delta_K)=T_K.
\]

Because `(e_K)` is primitive, `epsilon_s` is onto. Hence

\[
K_s:=\ker\varepsilon_s
\]

is a saturated direct summand of `F_s` of rank `b-a`.

For `a <= K <= b-1`, put

\[
a_{s,K}:=(X_s+K)\delta_K+(K+1)\delta_{K+1}.
\]

The recurrence

\[
(K+1)e_{K+1}=-(X_s+K)e_K
\]

says exactly that `a_{s,K} in K_s`, and

\[
\tau_s(a_{s,K})=(X_s+K)T_K+(K+1)T_{K+1}=V_K.
\]

Write

\[
R_s^{\rm adj}:=\langle a_{s,K}:a\le K\le b-1\rangle_{\mathbf Z}.
\]

For `a <= K <= b-2`, abbreviate

\[
\alpha_K:=X_s+2K+1,
\qquad g_K:=\gcd(\alpha_K,2)=\gcd(\alpha_K,\alpha_K+2),
\]

choose integers `u_K,v_K` with

\[
u_K\alpha_K+v_K(\alpha_K+2)=g_K,
\]

and define

\[
c_{s,K}:=u_Ka_{s,K}+v_Ka_{s,K+1}.
\]

Then

\[
\tau_s(c_{s,K})=u_KV_K+v_KV_{K+1}=C^*_{s,K}.
\]

Thus the three-row carrier lattice is

\[
R_s^*:=\langle c_{s,K}:a\le K\le b-2\rangle_{\mathbf Z}
 \subset R_s^{\rm adj}\subset K_s.
\]

There is also a useful explanation for `g_K`. Let

\[
\sigma\!\left(\sum x_J\delta_J\right):=\sum x_J.
\]

Then

\[
\sigma(a_{s,K})=X_s+2K+1=\alpha_K,
\]

so

\[
\sigma(c_{s,K})
=u_K\alpha_K+v_K(\alpha_K+2)=g_K.
\]

Equivalently, if the three consecutive lifted rows are written as

\[
q_K=(e_K,1,T_K),
\]

then

\[
(X_s+K)q_K+(K+1)q_{K+1}=(0,\alpha_K,V_K),
\]

\[
(X_s+K+1)q_{K+1}+(K+2)q_{K+2}
=(0,\alpha_K+2,V_{K+1}),
\]

and their Bezout combination is

\[
(0,g_K,C^*_{s,K}).
\]

This is the exact three-row origin of `C*`.

## 2. `D_s` is the full relation-value gcd

The row map

\[
\Phi_s:F_s\to\mathbf Z^2,
\qquad
\Phi_s(x)=(\varepsilon_s(x),\tau_s(x))
\]

has image `L_s`. By hypothesis

\[
L_s=\mathbf Z(1,B_s^H)+\mathbf Z(0,D_s).
\]

Because `epsilon_s` is onto, the pure vertical part is

\[
L_s\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbf Z)
=\{0\}\times\tau_s(K_s).
\]

The displayed Smith form therefore gives

\[
\boxed{\tau_s(K_s)=D_s\mathbf Z.}
\]

This also proves directly that `D_s` is the gcd of all `2 x 2` minors of the rows `(e_K,T_K)`.

Define

\[
G_s:=\gcd_{a\le K\le b-1}V_K,
\qquad
\Gamma_s:=\gcd_{a\le K\le b-2}C^*_{s,K}.
\]

Since

\[
R_s^*\subset R_s^{\rm adj}\subset K_s,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{D_s\mid G_s\mid\Gamma_s.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma_s}{D_s}
=\frac{\Gamma_s}{G_s}\frac{G_s}{D_s}.}
\]

The two factors are fundamentally different:

- `G_s/D_s` is a finite saturation defect of the full adjacent recurrence relations; the hypergeometric form of `e_K` controls it exactly.
- `Gamma_s/G_s` is the defect caused by deleting one free adjacent mode and retaining only the `C*` combinations; `e_K` does not control it.

## 3. Exact hypergeometric/Smith index for the adjacent relations

The relations `a_{s,K}` form the rows of the bidiagonal `(b-a) x (b-a+1)` matrix

\[
\mathcal A_s=
\begin{pmatrix}
X_s+a & a+1 \\
& X_s+a+1 & a+2 \\
&&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&X_s+b-1&b
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For `j in [a,b]`, delete column `j`. The resulting maximal minor is, up to sign,

\[
M_{s,j}:=
\prod_{i=a}^{j-1}(X_s+i)
\prod_{i=j}^{b-1}(i+1).
\]

Hence the top determinantal divisor is

\[
\boxed{
I_s:=\gcd_{j=a}^{b}|M_{s,j}|.}
\]

Because the legal interval has nonzero recurrence coefficients, `mathcal A_s` has rank `b-a`. Its rational row space is `ker(epsilon_s) tensor Q`; since `K_s` is the integral saturation of that rational space,

\[
\boxed{[K_s:R_s^{\rm adj}]=I_s.}
\]

There is a direct hypergeometric interpretation. The signed cofactor vector

\[
z_j:=(-1)^{j-a}M_{s,j}
\]

spans the one-dimensional integer nullspace of `mathcal A_s` and satisfies

\[
(i+1)z_{i+1}=-(X_s+i)z_i.
\]

The primitive integer solution of this recurrence is precisely the given `e`-vector. Hence, for one global sign,

\[
\boxed{z_j=\pm I_s e_j.}
\]

This is the exact Smith meaning of the content normalization in

\[
e_K=(-1)^K\binom{X_s+K-1}{K}/\operatorname{content}.
\]

Applying `tau_s`, the quotient map gives a surjection

\[
K_s/R_s^{\rm adj}\twoheadrightarrow
D_s\mathbf Z/G_s\mathbf Z.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\frac{G_s}{D_s}\mid I_s.}
\]

This is a genuine uniform theorem.

Prime by prime,

\[
\boxed{
v_p(I_s)=
\min_{a\le j\le b}
\left(
\sum_{i=a}^{j-1}v_p(X_s+i)
+
\sum_{i=j}^{b-1}v_p(i+1)
\right).}
\]

When `a >= 0`, `I_s` divides the left-end minor, so

\[
\boxed{
I_s\mid\prod_{i=a}^{b-1}(i+1)=\frac{b!}{a!}.}
\]

It also divides the right-end minor

\[
I_s\mid\prod_{i=a}^{b-1}|X_s+i|.
\]

Thus, for `1 <= a < b`,

\[
\log\frac{G_s}{D_s}
\le\log I_s
\le\log\frac{b!}{a!}
\le(b-a)\log b.
\]

This is the strongest unconditional compression supplied by the special hypergeometric `e_K`. It does **not** bound `Gamma_s/G_s`.

## 4. The exact missing mode in `C*`

Identify `R_s^{adj}` with the free lattice

\[
N_s:=\bigoplus_{K=a}^{b-1}\mathbf Z f_K,
\qquad f_K\longmapsto a_{s,K}.
\]

The bidiagonal rows are rationally independent, so this is an isomorphism onto `R_s^{adj}`.

Under this identification, `R_s^*` corresponds to

\[
B_s:=\langle
u_K f_K+v_K f_{K+1}:a\le K\le b-2
\rangle,
\]

where the first coefficient here is the original integer `u_K` (the formula is simply `u_K f_K+v_K f_{K+1}`). To avoid any notational ambiguity, equivalently write

\[
\boxed{B_s=\langle u_K f_K+v_K f_{K+1}:a\le K\le b-2\rangle.}
\]

Define

\[
\lambda_s:N_s\to\mathbf Z,
\qquad \lambda_s(f_K)=V_K.
\]

Then

\[
\lambda_s(N_s)=G_s\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\lambda_s(B_s)=\Gamma_s\mathbf Z.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma_s}{G_s}
=[\lambda_s(N_s):\lambda_s(B_s)]
=[N_s:B_s+(N_s\cap\ker\lambda_s)].}
\]

The matrix of `B_s` has only `b-a-1` rows in a free lattice of rank `b-a`. Thus there is one free missing mode (exactly one in the nondegenerate case). The ordinary index `[N_s:B_s]` is infinite. This is why a finite Smith/content calculation on the `C*` rows alone cannot classify the excess.

## 5. The complementary carrier `W` is unimodularly exact

Define

\[
\omega_{s,K}:=
\frac{\alpha_K+2}{g_K}a_{s,K}
-\frac{\alpha_K}{g_K}a_{s,K+1}.
\]

Its `T`-value is

\[
\boxed{
W_{s,K}:=\tau_s(\omega_{s,K})
=\frac{(\alpha_K+2)V_K-\alpha_KV_{K+1}}{g_K}.}
\]

Compare `(c_{s,K},omega_{s,K})` with `(a_{s,K},a_{s,K+1})`. The coefficient matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
u_K&v_K\\
(\alpha_K+2)/g_K&-\alpha_K/g_K
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where the first entry is `u_K`. Written without a potentially confusing glyph, the first row is exactly `(u_K,v_K)`. Its determinant is

\[
-\frac{u_K\alpha_K+v_K(\alpha_K+2)}{g_K}=-1.
\]

Hence it is unimodular, and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf Zc_{s,K}+\mathbf Z\omega_{s,K}
=\mathbf Za_{s,K}+\mathbf Za_{s,K+1}.}
\]

Applying `tau_s`,

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(C^*_{s,K},W_{s,K})
=\gcd(V_K,V_{K+1}).}
\]

Taking all `K`,

\[
\boxed{
\gcd\bigl(
\{C^*_{s,K}:a\le K\le b-2\}
\cup
\{W_{s,K}:a\le K\le b-2\}
\bigr)
=G_s.}
\]

So `W` is exactly the omitted coordinate.

This also exposes Bezout-choice dependence. Every other solution is

\[
u'_K=u_K+t\frac{\alpha_K+2}{g_K},
\qquad
v'_K=v_K-t\frac{\alpha_K}{g_K},
\]

where the first displayed symbol is `u'_K`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{C^{*\prime}_{s,K}=C^*_{s,K}+tW_{s,K}.}
\]

Thus `Gamma` itself need not be invariant under a change of Bezout choices. The pair `(C*,W)`, in contrast, is the unimodularly complete local datum.

For later use, define

\[
\Omega_s:=\gcd_{a\le K\le b-2}W_{s,K}.
\]

Since `Gamma_s=gcd_K C^*_{s,K}` and the gcd of all `C*` and all `W` is `G_s`,

\[
\boxed{\gcd(\Gamma_s,\Omega_s)=G_s.}
\]

Because the global `Gamma` divides `Gamma_s`, it follows prime by prime that

\[
\boxed{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_s)=\gcd(\Gamma,G_s).}
\]

So the global one-slope free-mode loss is measured exactly by

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,G_s)}
=
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_s)}.}
\]

## 6. Combined two-slope lattice and the exact quotient

Let

\[
F:=F_4\oplus F_7,
\]

with

\[
\varepsilon(x_4,x_7)=\varepsilon_4(x_4)+\varepsilon_7(x_7),
\qquad
\tau(x_4,x_7)=\tau_4(x_4)+\tau_7(x_7),
\]

and put

\[
K:=\ker\varepsilon,
\qquad
R^*:=R_4^*\oplus R_7^*\subset K.
\]

The combined row lattice is generated by

\[
(1,B_4^H),\ (0,D_4),\ (1,B_7^H),\ (0,D_7),
\]

or equivalently by

\[
(1,B_4^H),\ (0,D_4),\ (0,D_7),\
(0,B_7^H-B_4^H).
\]

Hence its pure vertical subgroup is

\[
\{0\}\times
\gcd(D_4,D_7,B_7^H-B_4^H)\mathbf Z.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\tau(K)=\Delta\mathbf Z.}
\]

By definition of `Gamma`,

\[
\boxed{\tau(R^*)=\Gamma\mathbf Z.}
\]

This reproves `Delta | Gamma` and yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
=[\Delta\mathbf Z:\Gamma\mathbf Z]
=[\tau(K):\tau(R^*)].}
\]

The first isomorphism theorem gives

\[
K/(K\cap\ker\tau)\simeq\Delta\mathbf Z,
\]

and the image of `R*` is `Gamma Z`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
K/(R^*+(K\cap\ker\tau))
\simeq
\Delta\mathbf Z/\Gamma\mathbf Z
\simeq
\mathbf Z/(\Gamma/\Delta)\mathbf Z.}
\]

This is the requested exact abstract lattice/index formula.

### Rank anatomy

Let `n_s=|I_s|` and `N=n_4+n_7`. Since the combined `e`-list is primitive,

\[
\operatorname{rank}K=N-1.
\]

But `R_s^*` has at most `n_s-2` generators, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}R^*\le N-4.
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{rank}(K/R^*)\ge3.
\]

In the legal nondegenerate case the `C*` rows have full possible rank and equality holds. The three free directions are the two one-slope missing modes and one cross-slope relation.

Concretely, choose `w_s in F_s` with

\[
\varepsilon_s(w_s)=1,
\qquad
\tau_s(w_s)=B_s^H.
\]

Then

\[
h_\times:=(-w_4,w_7)\in K
\]

and

\[
\tau(h_\times)=B_7^H-B_4^H.
\]

This is the cross mode absent from `R_4^* direct-sum R_7^*`.

## 7. Exact prime-by-prime decomposition of the global excess

Put

\[
E_\times:=B_7^H-B_4^H.
\]

Since `Delta | Gamma`, valuation-by-valuation one gets

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
=\operatorname{lcm}\left(
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_4)},
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_7)},
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,E_\times)}
\right).}
\]

Indeed, if

\[
a=v_p(\Gamma),\quad d_s=v_p(D_s),\quad e=v_p(E_\times),
\]

then the left side has valuation

\[
a-\min(d_4,d_7,e),
\]

while the lcm has valuation

\[
\max\bigl(\max(0,a-d_4),\max(0,a-d_7),\max(0,a-e)\bigr),
\]

which is the same number.

Using `G_s/D_s | I_s` and the exact `Omega_s` identity above gives the useful unconditional isolation

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}
\mid
I_s\,
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_s)}.}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
\mid
\operatorname{lcm}\left(
I_4\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_4)},
I_7\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,\Omega_7)},
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,E_\times)}
\right).}
\]

This is not yet a numerical bound, because it deliberately displays the three uncontrolled scalar defects. It is nevertheless a sharp classification of *where* every excess prime can come from. There is no fourth source.

## 8. Parametrized counterexample with the hypergeometric `e` fixed

The rank defect gives a uniform counterexample mechanism.

### Theorem

Fix:

- the slope-4 and slope-7 intervals, each of length at least three;
- the two primitive nonzero integer vectors `e_K` satisfying the stated recurrence, including if desired the actual q=6 hypergeometric vectors;
- all chosen Bezout pairs `(u_K,v_K)`;
- arbitrary prescribed selector integers `beta_4,beta_7`.

Then for every `M>=1` there exist integer values `T_K` on both slopes such that

\[
L_s=\mathbf Z(1,\beta_s)+\mathbf Z(0,1),
\]

so `D_s=1` and one may take `B_s^H=beta_s`, while every `C^*_{s,K}` is divisible by `M` and at least one is nonzero. Hence

\[
\Delta=1,\qquad M\mid\Gamma.
\]

Thus `Gamma/Delta` is unbounded while `e`, the intervals, the Bezout choices, and the selector values are fixed.

### Proof

For one slope let

\[
S_s:=\operatorname{Sat}_{K_s}(R_s^*)
=K_s\cap(R_s^*\otimes\mathbf Q).
\]

Since `rank R_s^* <= n_s-2` and `rank K_s=n_s-1`, `S_s` is a proper saturated sublattice of `K_s`. Hence

\[
K_s=S_s\oplus C_s
\]

for a nonzero free complement `C_s`.

Choose nonzero `r_s in R_s^*`. Since `S_s` is free, choose a homomorphism

\[
\ell_s:S_s\to\mathbf Z
\]

with `ell_s(r_s) != 0`. Pick a primitive element `y_s in C_s` and define a homomorphism on the complement taking `y_s` to `1`. Set

\[
\tau_s|_{S_s}:=M\ell_s,
\qquad
\tau_s(y_s):=1,
\]

and extend over a basis of `C_s`. Then

\[
\tau_s(K_s)=\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\tau_s(R_s^*)\subset M\mathbf Z,
\]

with at least one nonzero value.

Because `e` is primitive, `K_s` is a direct summand of `F_s`. Choose `w_s` with `epsilon_s(w_s)=1`; then

\[
F_s=K_s\oplus\mathbf Zw_s.
\]

Extend `tau_s` by prescribing

\[
\tau_s(w_s)=\beta_s,
\]

and define

\[
T_K:=\tau_s(\delta_K).
\]

The row lattice contains `(1,beta_s)` and `(0,1)`, so

\[
L_s=\mathbf Z(1,\beta_s)+\mathbf Z(0,1)
\]

and `D_s=1`. Repeat independently for both slopes. All `C*` values are multiples of `M`, at least one is nonzero, and

\[
\Delta=\gcd(1,1,\beta_7-\beta_4)=1.
\]

Therefore `M | Gamma/Delta`. QED.

### Consequences

This counterexample can use the exact hypergeometric q=6 `e`-vectors. Hence no theorem based only on

- the recurrence for `e`;
- primitivity/nonvanishing of `e`;
- cutoff endpoints;
- the selector values;
- the local Bezout gcds `g_K`;

can imply any finite bound for `Gamma/Delta`.

In particular there is no universal statement

\[
\Gamma/\Delta\mid P(m,H)
\]

for a fixed nonzero polynomial `P`, no divisor bound by a fixed endpoint expression, and no sublinear logarithmic-height bound from those data alone. Given any proposed fixed divisor expression, choose `M` with a prime factor not dividing it; given any proposed height bound, choose `M` larger than its exponential.

The observed actual values `1,2,11,...` therefore cannot be promoted to a finite classification without an Apéry-specific statement about `T_K`.

## 9. One explicit complementary scalar per slope

The previous theorem also gives a minimality statement: in the nondegenerate case `K/R*` has free rank three. Leaving any one of those free modes uncontrolled permits the same direct-summand modification of `tau`, inserting an arbitrary new factor. Thus a genuine Apéry theorem must control three independent scalar modes.

The `W_{s,K}` give a natural explicit choice for the two internal modes.

Fix one slope and one anchor `j in [a,b-2]`. Form the square matrix whose rows are

- all `c_{s,K}`, `a <= K <= b-2`, and
- the single complementary relation `omega_{s,j}`.

Relative to the basis `a_{s,a},...,a_{s,b-1}`, its determinant has absolute value

\[
\boxed{
H_{s,j}:=
\left|
\prod_{i=a}^{j-1}u_i
\prod_{i=j+1}^{b-2}v_i
\right|.}
\]

The proof is immediate from the unimodular pair `(c_{s,j},omega_{s,j})`: replace that pair by `(a_{s,j},a_{s,j+1})`; the remaining left and right bidiagonal blocks are triangular and contribute the displayed products.

Let `gamma` be any positive integer dividing every `C^*_{s,K}`. Applying the adjugate identity to the square matrix gives the more general bound

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\gamma}{\gcd(\gamma,G_s)}
\mid
H_{s,j}\,
\frac{\gamma}{\gcd(\gamma,W_{s,j})}.}
\]

Indeed, prime by prime, the common divisibility available in the augmented output vector is `gcd(gamma,W_{s,j})`; after dividing the input vector `(V_K)` by its content `G_s`, primitivity and the adjugate force the remaining common factor to divide the determinant. The displayed extra factor is exactly the part of `gamma` not seen by the anchor.

Combining with `G_s/D_s | I_s`, and taking `gamma=Gamma`, yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}
\mid
I_sH_{s,j}
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,W_{s,j})}.}
\]

Thus one scalar bound on

\[
\Gamma/\gcd(\Gamma,W_{s,j})
\]

is enough. The particularly clean sufficient premise

\[
\Gamma\mid W_{s,j}
\]

reduces this to

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}
\mid I_sH_{s,j}.}
\]

If `Gamma` divides **all** `W_{s,K}`, then `Gamma | G_s` by the global gcd identity and the whole free-mode loss disappears:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}\mid I_s.}
\]

## 10. Explicit local product for the natural Bezout choice

Because `alpha_{K+1}=alpha_K+2`, the parity of `alpha_K` is constant along a slope interval.

### Even `alpha_K`

Then `g_K=2`, and one may choose

\[
u_K=-1,\qquad v_K=1,
\]

where the first symbol is `u_K`. Indeed `-alpha_K+(alpha_K+2)=2`. Hence

\[
H_{s,j}=1
\]

for every anchor. Therefore a single congruence `Gamma | W_{s,j}` implies

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}\mid I_s.}
\]

### Odd `alpha_K`

Then `g_K=1`. Put

\[
t_K:=\frac{\alpha_K+1}{2}.
\]

Since `t_{K+1}=t_K+1`, choose

\[
u_K=t_K,
\qquad
v_K=-(t_K-1),
\]

again with the first symbol meaning `u_K`. The Bezout identity is

\[
t_K\alpha_K-(t_K-1)(\alpha_K+2)=1.
\]

On the legal positive q=6 ranges, no degenerating zero coefficient is encountered. The anchor determinant simplifies, independently of `j`, to

\[
\boxed{
H_s=
\prod_{i=a}^{b-3}|t_i|
=
\prod_{i=a}^{b-3}
\left|\frac{X_s+2i+2}{2}\right|.}
\]

The empty product is `1` when `b-a=2`.

For completeness, this is also the top determinantal divisor of the `(b-a-1) x (b-a)` coefficient matrix of the `C*` relations. Its maximal minors are

\[
\prod_{i=a}^{j-1}u_i
\prod_{i=j}^{b-2}v_i,
\qquad a\le j\le b-1.
\]

Factoring

\[
P=\prod_{i=a}^{b-3}t_i
\]

leaves the consecutive factors

\[
t_a-1,t_a,t_a+1,\ldots,t_{b-2},
\]

whose gcd is `1`; hence the determinantal divisor is exactly `|P|`.

Thus a single Apéry-specific anchor congruence gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,D_s)}
\mid
I_s
\prod_{i=a}^{b-3}
\left|\frac{X_s+2i+2}{2}\right|.}
\]

This is the strongest simple local-product theorem available from one added scalar congruence. It is not unconditional.

## 11. Conditional global divisor and height bound

Let

\[
E_\times=B_7^H-B_4^H.
\]

Suppose that for the actual Apéry `T_K` one proves, for anchors `j_4,j_7`, bounds

\[
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,W_{4,j_4})}\mid R_4,
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,W_{7,j_7})}\mid R_7,
\]

and a cross bound

\[
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,E_\times)}\mid R_\times.
\]

Then the exact lcm formula and the one-anchor determinant lemma give

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
\mid
\operatorname{lcm}
\bigl(I_4H_{4,j_4}R_4,\ I_7H_{7,j_7}R_7,\ R_\times\bigr).}
\]

In particular it divides their product.

The cleanest sufficient version is

\[
\Gamma\mid W_{4,j_4},
\qquad
\Gamma\mid W_{7,j_7},
\qquad
\Gamma\mid E_\times,
\]

which yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta}
\mid
\operatorname{lcm}
\bigl(I_4H_{4,j_4},I_7H_{7,j_7}\bigr).}
\]

For nonnegative cutoffs,

\[
I_s\mid B_s!/A_s!,
\]

and for the natural Bezout choices `H_s` is either `1` or the displayed product of linear factors. Therefore

\[
\log(I_sH_s)
\ll
(B_s-A_s)\log(B_s+1)
+
(B_s-A_s)\log(|X_s|+B_s+2),
\]

so, with `L_s=B_s-A_s`,

\[
\boxed{
\log(I_sH_s)=O\bigl(L_s\log(|X_s|+B_s+2)\bigr).}
\]

Whenever the q=6 legal window has `L_s=O(H)` and `|X_s|+B_s` polynomially bounded in `m+H`, this is

\[
O(H\log(m+H))
\subset O(H^2+(\log(m+H))^2).
\]

Thus the desired short logarithmic height becomes available after the three missing scalar modes are controlled. Before that, the counterexample theorem rules it out.

The odd-parity product has degree growing with the interval length, so the abstract argument does not yield a fixed-degree polynomial in `m,H`; it yields a product with controlled logarithmic height.

## 12. What must be proved for the actual q=6 Apéry `T_K`

The `e`-side is already exhausted, in the precise sense that its full adjacent Smith contribution is `I_s`. The remaining task is an Apéry-specific identity constraining the three free `T`-modes.

A concrete minimal target is to prove small bounds for exactly these three quantities:

\[
\boxed{
R_4^\mathrm{miss}:=
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,W_{4,j_4})},
\qquad
R_7^\mathrm{miss}:=
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,W_{7,j_7})},
\qquad
R_\times^\mathrm{miss}:=
\frac{\Gamma}{\gcd(\Gamma,B_7^H-B_4^H)}.}
\]

Here `j_4,j_7` can be any convenient legal anchors; changing them only changes the explicit determinant factors `H_{s,j_s}`. If one can show these three missing defects divide low-height expressions, the preceding theorem immediately gives a low-height divisor for `Gamma/Delta`.

A stronger but especially simple target is

\[
\Gamma\mid W_{4,j_4},
\qquad
\Gamma\mid W_{7,j_7},
\qquad
\Gamma\mid B_7^H-B_4^H.
\]

Such congruences would have to come from actual Apéry structure — for example a second recurrence for `T`, an endpoint/reflection identity, or a common hypergeometric antiderivative. They do not follow from the first-order recurrence for `e`.

Why are three scalar inputs minimal? In the nondegenerate case `K/R*` has free rank three. If fewer than three independent free directions are constrained, the direct-summand construction of Section 8 varies `tau` on an unconstrained direction while preserving everything already fixed and inserts an arbitrary factor `M`. So no argument using fewer independent `T`-constraints can succeed abstractly.

## 13. Interpretation of the observed factors `1,2,11,...`

The observations are compatible with several distinct sources:

1. the finite adjacent Smith defect `G_s/D_s`, which divides `I_s`;
2. the one-slope free-mode defect, equivalently failure of the omitted `W` data to share all `Gamma`-divisibility;
3. the cross-slope selector defect `Gamma/gcd(Gamma,B_7^H-B_4^H)`;
4. additional finite coefficient-chain factors from a particular noncanonical Bezout choice.

Thus `2` and `11` may be genuine residual saturation factors in the actual q=6 cells, but the present hypotheses do not restrict the list to those values or to any finite set. The fact that `Gamma` is only a sufficient carrier is exactly why it can exceed `Delta`.
