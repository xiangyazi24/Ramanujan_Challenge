# Verdict: option (3) — a rigorous scoped obstruction

I do **not** find a complete every-\(n\) proof of
\[
\log \operatorname{rad}\gcd\!\left(b_n,\prod_{p\le n}p\right)=o(n),
\]
and I do not find a primary theorem that can currently be inserted to obtain one.  What can be proved cleanly is a fairly sharp obstruction to the most natural modular/supercongruence route:

> **After the smooth-radical reduction, the unresolved events are lower-digit events \(p\mid b_r\) with \(0<r<p\), simultaneously for many different large primes \(p\).  The classical Apéry Lucas law and the standard vertical supercongruence tower are tautological or inapplicable on exactly these lower digits.  The Hasse--Franel/K3/symmetric-square structure gives strong one-characteristic structure, but its verified outputs are evaluation/trace statements or a one-prime coefficient factorization; none supplies the mixed-characteristic positional correlation required by the every-index radical.**

This is a scoped obstruction, not an impossibility theorem for all future modular methods.  A genuinely horizontal modular/crystalline theorem could still solve the problem.  The current manuscript's fixed high-moment criterion identifies exactly what such a theorem would have to control.

# 2. First obstruction: the standard Apéry supercongruence is blind on the residual digit

Let \(p>\sqrt n\) and write
\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]
Then automatically \(q<p\).  Gessel's Lucas congruence gives
\[
b_n\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.\tag{6}
\]
The current paper has already disposed of the companion/quotient channel at total cost \(O(n^{2/3})\); equation (1) leaves precisely the lower-digit indicator
\[
\mathbf1_{p\mid b_r},\qquad r=n\bmod p.
\]

## Theorem 2.1 (vertical-Frobenius blind spot)

For every summand of \(M(n)\):

1. \(r\neq0\).  Indeed \(b_0=1\), so \(r=0\) never contributes.
2. Since \(0<r<p\), the base-\(p\) expansion of \(r\) has one digit.  Therefore the Lucas theorem applied to \(b_r\) is the tautology
   \[
   b_r\equiv b_r\pmod p.
   \]
3. The standard Apéry tower
   \[
   b_{mp^s}\equiv b_{mp^{s-1}}\pmod{p^{3s}},\qquad p\ge5,\ s\ge1,\tag{7}
   \]
   has no instance whose left side is \(b_r\), because every index on the left of (7) is divisible by \(p\), whereas \(0<r<p\).

Consequently, the combination of the multiplicative Lucas theorem and the standard vertical supercongruence tower gives **no new condition at all** on the residual event \(p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\) appearing in \(M(n)\).

### Proof

Only the third point requires comment.  If \(r=mp^s\) for \(s\ge1\), then \(p\mid r\); since \(0<r<p\), this is impossible.  The other two points are immediate. ∎

This explains precisely why “use Beukers supercongruences” does not by itself attack the remaining radical.  The tower is very strong *vertically* in a fixed \(p\)-adic digit tree, but §4.2a has already reduced the problem to the bottom nonzero digit.

### Fixed endpoint refinements also have negligible scope

Beukers and later authors prove additional congruences at special shifted indices.  Even if a congruence completely settled finitely many fixed endpoint residue classes, that does not touch the mass in (1).

For example, fix an integer \(c\).  If a large prime \(p>\sqrt n\) has
\[
n\bmod p=p-c,
\]
then \(p\mid n+c\).  Therefore
\[
\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt n\\n\bmod p=p-c}}\log p
\le \log|n+c|=O(\log n).\tag{8}
\]
The same holds for any fixed finite set of endpoint offsets.  Thus special congruences at residues such as \(0\) or \(-1\pmod p\), useful though they are, can remove only \(O(\log n)\) from the dangerous large-prime mass.  The hard residues are the moving interior values \(1\le r\le p-2\).

# 3. Second obstruction: the Hasse--Franel theorem is an exact one-prime coordinate identity, not coefficient anti-concentration

The current file `problems/3.2/hasse_franel_descent.tex` contains the exact identity
\[
A_p(a)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_ma^m,
\qquad
K_p(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}f_mx^m,
\]
with \(f_m=\sum_k\binom mk^3\),
\[
\phi(x)=\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x},
\qquad
\Psi_{p,m}(x)=x^m(1-8x)^m(1+x)^{p-1-m},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
K_p(x)^2=(1+x)^{p-1}A_p(\phi(x))
       =\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m\Psi_{p,m}(x).
}\tag{9}
\]
Because \(\Psi_{p,m}\) has exact \(x\)-adic order \(m\), these \(p\) polynomials are triangularly independent.  Hence
\[
b_m=[K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,m}},
\qquad
Z(p)=\#\{m:[K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,m}}=0\}.\tag{10}
\]
This is a useful exact reformulation, but it exposes the difficulty rather than solving it: the vanishing is a *coordinate* condition in a \(p\)-dependent basis.

## Primary factorization theorem checked

Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin, **“Galois groups of Apéry-like series modulo primes,” Bull. Aust. Math. Soc. 114 (2026), 65--78, DOI 10.1017/S0004972725100932**, Theorem 1.2, states exactly that there is \(B_p\in\mathbb F_p[t]\) such that
\[
A_p(t)=
\begin{cases}
B_p(t)^2,
 &p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},\\
(t^2-34t+1)B_p(t)^2,
 &p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.
\end{cases}\tag{11}
\]
The same paper explicitly records the Franel pullback
\[
f_\alpha(t)=(1+x)h(x)^2,
\qquad
 t=\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x},\tag{12}
\]
and the Gessel \(p\)-Lucas law.

So (9)--(11) are source-faithful.

## Proposition 3.1 (the square factorization alone cannot bound \(Z(p)\))

The structural conclusion “\(A_p\) is a square of degree at most \(p-1\)” is compatible with a linear number of zero coefficients.

Indeed, in a square branch choose
\[
B(t)=1+t^2+t^4+\cdots+t^{2h},
\qquad 4h\le p-1.
\]
Then \(B(0)=1\), \(\deg B^2\le p-1\), but every odd coefficient of \(B(t)^2\) vanishes.  Thus a family satisfying the bare square/degree conclusion can have \(\gg p\) zero coefficients.

This is **not** an Apéry counterexample and does not refute the full CFVZ structure.  It proves the narrower logical point needed here: Theorem 1.2's factorization, by itself, gives no coefficient-sparsity theorem.  Any improvement of \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\) from (11) must use extra arithmetic of the *specific* square root \(B_p\), not merely its existence or the root locus of \(A_p\).

The current manuscript does exactly the right thing by deriving recurrences for the two normalized square roots.  But those remain one-prime recurrences, and no cited primary theorem currently turns them into the cross-prime condition (3).

# 4. Third obstruction: K3 / symmetric-square trace control is in the evaluation aspect, while \(p\mid b_j\) is a defining-characteristic Mellin zero

The current toric files prove much more geometry than the classical K3 attribution alone.

`toric_fiber_k3.tex` proves, for \(p\ge5\) and \(a\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), an exact K3 point-count formula with a deflated primitive trace
\[
\mu_p(a)=p^2-6p+12+\theta_p(a),\tag{13}
\]
where the transcendental part has rank at most three and the corresponding character sum is uniformly \(O(p)\) (the file records the explicit bound \(5p+8\)).

`toric_fiber_sym2.tex` then identifies \(\theta_p(a)\) on the quadratic cover
\[
8x^2+(a-1)x+a=0
\]
with an elliptic symmetric-square/Asai trace.  In the split case,
\[
\theta_p(a)=a_p(E_x)^2-p-\epsilon_ap,
\]
and in the inert case,
\[
\theta_p(a)=\chi_p(-3)a_{p^2}(E_x)-p-\epsilon_ap,
\]
with the stated branch correction when \(a^2-34a+1=0\).

These are strong pointwise trace formulas.  But the coefficient event is the *multiplicative Mellin transform* of the whole fiber function.  The current paper records, for \(1\le j\le p-2\),
\[
\boxed{
 b_j=-\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}\mu_p(a)a^j
}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.\tag{14}
\]
Therefore
\[
p\mid b_j
\iff
\sum_{a\ne0}\mu_p(a)a^j\equiv0\pmod p.\tag{15}
\]

A pointwise Weil bound such as \(|\theta_p(a)|\ll p\) does not prevent an exact Mellin coefficient from vanishing.  More generally, an \(L^\infty\) bound on a function never controls the support of its Fourier/Mellin transform: even a nonzero bounded function may have many zero Fourier coefficients.  Deligne-type bounds for a *twisted Mellin sum* control its complex/\(\ell\)-adic magnitude at the natural square-root scale; they do not say that the resulting algebraic integer is nonzero modulo the defining characteristic \(p\).

The repository has a concrete live cancellation already at \((p,j)=(11,5)\).  Thus the K3 collapse and symmetric-square formula do not even imply “all nontrivial Mellin coefficients are nonzero,” much less a uniform cross-prime radical bound.

The classical geometric source is correctly cited as **F. Beukers and C. A. M. Peters, “A family of K3 surfaces and \(\zeta(3)\), J. reine angew. Math. 351 (1984), 42--54, DOI 10.1515/crll.1984.351.42**.  That source validates the K3 origin of Apéry's differential equation.  The exact toric compactification, rank-19 deflation, and elliptic trace identities used above are stronger calculations proved in this repository; I do not attribute those exact formulas to Beukers--Peters.

# 5. The modular-parametrization / excellent-Frobenius route remains prime-local

A modern primary formulation makes the scope particularly clear.  F. Beukers, **“Supercongruences using modular forms,” arXiv:2403.03301, current version dated March 22, 2026**, studies congruences of the form
\[
F_p(t_0)\equiv \vartheta_p\pmod{p^s},\qquad s=2,3,
\]
for a \(p\)-truncation \(F_p\) and a local unit root.  With the excellent Frobenius lift \(t^\sigma(q)=t(q^p)\), the basic power-series mechanism is
\[
\frac{F(t)}{F(t^\sigma)}\equiv F_p(t)\pmod{p^s}.\tag{16}
\]
Modulo \(p\), Beukers explicitly notes that this becomes
\[
F(t)\equiv F_p(t)F(t^p)\pmod p,\tag{17}
\]
which is equivalent to the Lucas digit property.

This is exactly the issue in Theorem 2.1: (16) is a powerful Frobenius statement **at one prime**, but the residual event in (1) is the coefficient at a single bottom digit \(0<r<p\).  Modulo \(p\), the Frobenius recursion stops there.  Raising the congruence to \(p^2\) or \(p^3\) strengthens vertical information but does not by itself relate
\[
[b_{r_p}\bmod p]
\quad\text{and}\quad
[b_{r_q}\bmod q]
\]
for distinct primes \(p\ne q\) selected by the same integer \(n\).

This is the missing horizontal direction.

There is also an important terminology trap.  The Apéry generating function has a modular parametrization of the form
\[
F(t(q))=\Theta(q),
\]
but the Apéry numbers \(b_n\) are the coefficients in the **\(t\)-expansion** of \(F\); they are not the ordinary Hecke \(q\)-Fourier coefficients \(a_n(\Theta)\).  Consequently, theorems on prime factors of Fourier coefficients of a fixed Hecke eigenform do not apply to \(b_n\) merely from modularity.

# 6. A rigorous cross-prime obstruction: even \(Z(p)\le1\) is insufficient if all one knows is row cardinality

The current manuscript correctly warns that a strong single-prime zero-count theorem is not the all-index theorem.  Here is a clean quantifier-preserving demonstration of that logical gap.

## Proposition 6.1 (singleton rows can carry linear pointwise mass)

There exist sets
\[
E_p\subset\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
\qquad(p\text{ prime})
\]
with
\[
|E_p|\le1\quad\text{for every }p,
\]
and an infinite sequence \(N_j\to\infty\) such that
\[
\sum_{\sqrt{N_j}<p\le N_j}
 \log p\;\mathbf1_{N_j\bmod p\in E_p}
\ge (1/2+o(1))N_j.\tag{18}
\]

### Proof

Choose \(N_1<N_2<\cdots\) so rapidly that the intervals
\[
I_j=(N_j/2,N_j)
\]
are pairwise disjoint; for example require \(N_{j+1}>2N_j\).  For each prime \(p\in I_j\), define
\[
E_p=\{N_j-p\};
\]
for all other primes take \(E_p=\varnothing\).

Because \(p>N_j/2\),
\[
0<N_j-p<p,
\]
and
\[
N_j\bmod p=N_j-p.
\]
Thus every prime in \(I_j\) contributes at \(n=N_j\).  Hence the left side of (18) is at least
\[
\sum_{N_j/2<p<N_j}\log p
=\vartheta(N_j)-\vartheta(N_j/2)+O(\log N_j)
=(1/2+o(1))N_j
\]
by the prime number theorem. ∎

This artificial model is **not** claimed to satisfy the Apéry recurrence, Hasse--Franel identity, or K3 geometry.  Its role is precise: it proves that a theorem whose final output is only a separate bound on each \(|Z(p)|\)—even the dream bound \(Z(p)\le1\)—cannot imply the all-index radical.  One must also control the *locations* of the zeros across different primes.

That is exactly what (3) measures.

The same example can be made reflection-symmetric by adding the reflected residue to each \(E_p\), at the cost of replacing the bound \(1\) by \(2\).  So cardinality plus a local reflection symmetry is still not a horizontal theorem.

# 7. The exact modular theorem that would be enough is mixed-characteristic, not another one-prime trace bound

Using (10), define
\[
X_p(m)=
\mathbf1_{[K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,m\bmod p}}=0}.
\]
Then
\[
K_X(m)=\sum_{X<p\le2X}X_p(m).
\]
Therefore the current \((\mathrm{HM})_8\) target can be rewritten entirely in Hasse--Franel language as
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m<X^2}
\left(
 \sum_{X<p\le2X}
 \mathbf1_{[K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,m\bmod p}}=0}
\right)_8
\ll
X^{2+o(1)}
\left(\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{Z(p)}p\right)^8.
}\tag{19}
\]
By the theorem already in `proof.tex`, (19) would imply
\[
\log G_n\ll n^{11/12+o(1)}
\]
for every \(n\), hence the conjecture.

Equation (19) shows exactly why the verified geometry has not closed the problem.  CFVZ gives one polynomial \(A_p\) in \(\mathbb F_p[t]\) for each prime; the K3/Sym\(^2\) trace formula gives one trace sheaf/fiber problem in characteristic \(p\); the excellent Frobenius lift gives one \(p\)-adic dynamical relation.  The factorial moment in (19), however, asks for an eight-prime correlation among *defining-characteristic coefficient-zero events* at residues selected by one common integer \(m\).

A valid modular breakthrough therefore needs one of the following genuinely new ingredients:

1. an **integral/horizontal carrier** whose reduction simultaneously controls the coordinates \([K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,r_p}}\) for several distinct primes;
2. a mixed-characteristic large-sieve/monodromy theorem that directly proves a Palm contraction
   \[
   F_{t+1}(X)\ll X^{o(1)}\lambda_XF_t(X),
   \qquad 2\le t\le7;
   \]
3. or some stronger Apéry-specific fixed-index theorem that bounds
   \[
   \#\{X<p\le2X:p\mid b_{m\bmod p}\}
   \]
   uniformly in \(m<X^2\) at the needed scale.

None of the primary results checked here contains such a mixed-prime statement.

# 8. Known arithmetic prime-factor theorems do not bridge the gap

There is directly relevant prior work: **F. Luca and I. E. Shparlinski, “Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers,” J. London Math. Soc. 78 (2008), 545--562, DOI 10.1112/jlms/jdn031.**  Their main result states that for a set of positive integers \(n\) of asymptotic density \(1\),
\[
\omega(b_n)>c_0\log\log\log n
\]
and
\[
P(b_n)>c_0(\log n\log\log n)^{1/2},
\]
where \(P\) is the largest prime factor.

This is genuine Apéry-specific arithmetic, but it does not imply the desired statement for two independent reasons:

- it is an **almost-all** theorem, not an every-\(n\) theorem;
- a lower bound for one large prime factor gives no upper bound on
  \[
  \sum_{\substack{p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}\log p.
  \]
  A linear mass of additional \(n\)-smooth prime divisors may coexist with that large factor.

So this literature does not provide option (2) in the requested sense.

Likewise, mere holonomicity cannot supply the needed smooth-part theorem.  For example \(a_n=n!\) is P-recursive, yet every prime \(p\le n\) divides \(a_n\), so
\[
\log\operatorname{rad}\gcd\!\left(n!,\prod_{p\le n}p\right)
=\vartheta(n)\sim n.
\]
Thus any applicable “smooth parts of holonomic coefficients” theorem would need hypotheses much stronger than holonomicity itself.  The user's central-binomial warning makes the analogous point even within the Lucas/hypergeometric world.

# 9. What is and is not proved by this attack

## Proved / verified here

1. The historical §4.2a equivalence was recovered exactly: \(\mathcal S(n)=M(n)+O(n^{2/3})\), with the explicit one-sided error \(0\le\mathcal S-M\le12n^{2/3}\).
2. For every dangerous lower-digit event in \(M(n)\), \(0<r=n\bmod p<p\).  The Lucas recursion is tautological at \(r\), and the standard \(p^{3s}\) supercongruence tower does not apply to \(b_r\).
3. Any fixed finite family of endpoint residue classes contributes only \(O(\log n)\) to the large-prime radical, so special-index supercongruences cannot control the main mass.
4. CFVZ Theorem 1.2 and its exact two-branch factorization were checked against the primary 2026 paper.
5. The square factorization alone does not imply coefficient anti-concentration; explicit squares of the allowed degree can have linearly many zero coefficients.
6. The current K3/Sym\(^2\) theorem controls the evaluation/trace aspect, while \(p\mid b_j\) is an exact defining-characteristic Mellin-zero condition.
7. A purely single-prime zero-count bound, even \(Z(p)\le1\), is logically insufficient without cross-prime positional control; Proposition 6.1 gives an explicit construction with linear pointwise mass.
8. The manuscript's fixed high-moment condition is precisely the missing horizontal theorem; its Hasse--Franel rewrite is (19).

## Not proved

- No every-\(n\) estimate \(\mathcal S(n)=o(n)\).
- No improvement here over the existing unconditional \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\).
- No new \((\mathrm{HM})_k\) beyond the manuscript's proved \(k=2\) case.
- No theorem asserting that all possible modular/crystalline approaches must fail.  The obstruction applies to the **currently verified prime-local outputs** and to arguments that extract only single-prime row size/evaluation bounds from them.

# 10. Recommended theorem-level next target

The modular route should not spend its next effort on another bound of the form \(Z(p)\ll p^\alpha\) unless that bound is strong enough to reduce the moment order *and* is paired with a proved cross-prime moment.  The highest-value next statement is the first genuinely horizontal contraction, e.g.
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_3
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
}\tag{20}
\]
This is the first unproved Palm step beyond (4).  It would not by itself finish the conjecture, but unlike another K3 pointwise trace bound it attacks the correct quantifier: one integer \(m\), several distinct primes.

A modular/K3 proof of (20) would need to turn the exact identity
\[
[K_p^2]_{\Psi_{p,r}}=b_r
\]
into a common arithmetic object for triples of characteristics.  If no such horizontal carrier exists, that is the precise reason the natural modular/supercongruence route stalls.
