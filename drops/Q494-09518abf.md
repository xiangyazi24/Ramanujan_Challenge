# P3.2: direct CRT counting for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

## Executive verdict

The proposed random-CRT calculation contains the **right local probability** but applies it to the **wrong global target**.

For one fixed triple of primes \(p,q,s\asymp X\) and one fixed residue triple, a uniformly distributed CRT representative in \([0,pqs)\) would indeed satisfy

\[
\Pr\{m<X^2\}=\frac{X^2}{pqs}\asymp \frac1X.
\]

However:

1. In the project, \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) ranges over **all distinct primes in the dyadic block**
   \[
   \mathcal P_X=\{p:X<p\le 2X\},
   \]
   not merely triples of consecutive ambient primes.
2. There are \(\asymp (X/\log X)^3\) such prime triples, not \(O(X/\log X)\).
3. Therefore the random model predicts
   \[
   \asymp \frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}
   \]
   CRT representatives below \(X^2\), not zero of them.
4. This is exactly the scale on the right side of \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). Thus \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) is an **aggregate upper bound at the random scale**, not a statement that every three-prime CRT representative exceeds \(X^2\).
5. The proposed stronger statement is already false for the actual Apéry data in the repository. At \(X=4096\), the exact CRT enumerator finds \(1203\) unordered residue triples below \(X^2\), equivalently \(7218\) ordered contributions, while the random main scale is
   \[
   X^2\lambda_X^3=7608.237134.
   \]
   The ratio is \(0.948708600\), and the observed maximum is \(K_X(m)=4\). See [`hm3_exploration.md`](../problems/3.2/hm3_exploration.md).
6. Bounded cardinality, the CRT formula, and reflection symmetry do **not** imply equidistribution. The repository already contains a reflection-invariant bounded-size countermodel in which all primes align on one integer and \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) fails by a factor of order \(X\); see [`hm3_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex).

So the corrected conclusion is:

> The direct CRT approach is a valid way to *formulate* \((\mathrm{HM})_3\), and the random calculation recovers exactly its desired scale. But there is no black-box equidistribution theorem that proves it for the moving Apéry zero sets. The missing input remains an Apéry-specific mixed-characteristic, cross-prime correlation estimate.

One preliminary qualification is also important: the current paper proves \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\), while \(Z(p)=O(1)\) is an empirically compelling but presently unproved strengthening. The conclusions below grant the proposed \(O(1)\) assumption when discussing its consequences; the exact \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) formulation itself uses the actual values \(Z(p)\) and does not require that assumption.

## 1. The exact target in the repository

Put

\[
H=X^2,\qquad
K_X(m)=\#\{p\in\mathcal P_X:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(p)}p.
\]

The third factorial moment is

\[
F_3(X)=\sum_{0\le m<H}(K_X(m))_3,
\]

and \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) is

\[
\boxed{
F_3(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
}
\]

For \(p<q<s\) and \((r,t,u)\in Z_p\times Z_q\times Z_s\), let

\[
[r,t,u]_{p,q,s}\in[0,pqs)
\]

be the canonical CRT representative. Since \(pqs>X^3>H\), each residue tuple contributes either zero or one integer below \(H\). Define the unordered direct count

\[
C_3(X)=
\sum_{p<q<s}
\sum_{\substack{r\in Z_p\\t\in Z_q\\u\in Z_s}}
\mathbf 1_{\{[r,t,u]_{p,q,s}<H\}}.
\]

Then exactly

\[
\boxed{F_3(X)=6C_3(X).}
\]

Thus proving the correct direct-counting estimate

\[
C_3(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3
\]

is equivalent, up to the constant \(6\), to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). By contrast, proving \(C_3(X)=0\) would assert \(K_X(m)\le2\) for every \(m<X^2\), a dramatically stronger assertion contradicted by the computed \(K_X(m)=3,4\) examples.

### The random main term

Write \(z_p=Z(p)\) and

\[
\sigma_j=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\left(\frac{z_p}{p}\right)^j.
\]

If each CRT point is uniform in its modulus, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E C_3(X)
&=H\sum_{p<q<s}\frac{z_pz_qz_s}{pqs}\\
&=\frac H6\left(\lambda_X^3-3\lambda_X\sigma_2+2\sigma_3\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Equivalently, the ordered expectation is

\[
\mathbb E F_3(X)
=H\left(\lambda_X^3-3\lambda_X\sigma_2+2\sigma_3\right).
\]

Under \(z_p=O(1)\) and a positive average density of zeros,

\[
\lambda_X\asymp\frac1{\log X},\qquad
\sigma_2\ll\frac1{X\log X},\qquad
\sigma_3\ll\frac1{X^2\log X},
\]

so

\[
\mathbb E C_3(X)\sim\frac{X^2\lambda_X^3}{6}
\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\]

This is why the computation \(1203\) versus \(7608.237134/6=1268.039522\ldots\) at \(X=4096\) is exactly what the independence heuristic predicts.

### Why “consecutive” does not reduce \((\mathrm{HM})_3\)

There are two possible meanings:

* If \(p_1,p_2,p_3\) are consecutive in the **ambient list of all primes**, they form only a tiny subset of the triples in \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). Controlling that subset leaves all nonconsecutive triples untreated.
* If they are consecutive among the primes that hit a fixed integer \(m\), then every row with \(K_X(m)\ge3\) certainly contains such a triple, but the selected prime list depends on \(m\). Globally, an arbitrary triple \(p<q<s\) can be consecutive in the hit set of its own CRT representative. This interpretation does not reduce the candidate count to \(O(X/\log X)\).

For ambient consecutive primes in a dyadic block, the random expected number of lower-tail hits would indeed be

\[
\asymp \frac{X}{\log X}\cdot\frac1X
=\frac1{\log X}.
\]

That tends to zero **in expectation in the random ensemble**, but it neither implies a deterministic eventual-zero theorem nor controls \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). In fact, if disjoint dyadic blocks behaved independently, the heuristic expectations are comparable to \(1/j\) for \(X=2^j\); since \(\sum_j1/j\) diverges, the corresponding Borel--Cantelli heuristic points toward infinitely many blocks containing a consecutive-triple hit, not eventual absence.

Finally, the approximation \(p_1p_2p_3\asymp X^3\) requires \(p_i\asymp X\). If the phrase “consecutive primes \(p_i\le X\)” is interpreted literally over the whole range below \(X\), then many triples satisfy \(p_1p_2p_3\le X^2\), in which case every canonical CRT representative automatically lies below \(X^2\). The dyadic localization in the repository is essential.

## (A) Can the random-CRT heuristic be made rigorous?

### Not from boundedness or CRT linearity alone

The CRT map

\[
\prod_i\mathbb Z/p_i\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/(p_1p_2p_3)\mathbb Z
\]

is a bijection when the full residue spaces are used. That does **not** make its restriction to three bounded subsets random. A bounded subset can be placed in an arbitrarily correlated way across primes.

The simplest obstruction is to fix one integer \(m_0<H\) and set

\[
a_p=m_0\bmod p,\qquad Z_p^*=\{a_p\}.
\]

Then for every prime triple the residue choice \((a_p,a_q,a_s)\) has CRT representative exactly \(m_0\), so every triple is “bad.” To impose the Apéry reflection symmetry, replace this by

\[
Z_p^*=\{a_p,p-1-a_p\}.
\]

The same aligned choice \(a_p,a_q,a_s\) still has representative \(m_0\).

This is not merely a toy objection. The anchored-rainbow-star construction in [`hm3_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex) chooses \(m_0=\lfloor3X^2/4\rfloor\) and essentially these two-point sets. It additionally satisfies:

* reflection invariance;
* no consecutive residues;
* avoidance of endpoint residues;
* the proved interval zero-count shape;
* the universal second factorial-moment bound;
* a uniform \(O(1)\) row-codegree bound;
* the available primewise fiber/energy bounds; and
* the necessary gap-polynomial pair certificate.

Nevertheless \(K_X^*(m_0)\asymp X/\log X\) and

\[
F_3^*(X)\gg\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3},
\]

which exceeds the \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) scale by a factor of order \(X\). It also defeats the ambient-consecutive-prime version, because all but \(O(1)\) primes in the block can be active at the same \(m_0\).

Therefore there can be no theorem of the form

> “bounded moving residue sets in consecutive primes have equidistributed CRT representatives.”

Any valid theorem must impose a substantive horizontal condition tying together how the actual Apéry zero fiber is selected at different residue characteristics.

### The exact Fourier form of the missing input

For fixed distinct \(p,q,s\in\mathcal P_X\), put \(M=pqs\),

\[
S_p(v)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(vr),
\qquad
\widehat I_H(h)=\sum_{0\le n<H}e_M(-hn).
\]

If \(\overline{qs}\) denotes the inverse of \(qs\pmod p\), and similarly cyclically, Fourier inversion on \(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\) gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
N_{p,q,s}(H)
=\frac1M\sum_{h\bmod M}\widehat I_H(h)
S_p(h\overline{qs})
S_q(h\overline{ps})
S_s(h\overline{pq}),
}
\]

where \(N_{p,q,s}(H)\) is the number of zero-residue tuples whose CRT representative is below \(H\). The \(h=0\) term is

\[
\frac{H z_pz_qz_s}{pqs}.
\]

Thus a proof by direct equidistribution would have to control, after summing over \(p,q,s\), the nonzero-frequency expression

\[
\sum_{p<q<s}
\frac1{pqs}
\sum_{0\ne h\bmod pqs}
\widehat I_H(h)
S_p(h\overline{qs})
S_q(h\overline{ps})
S_s(h\overline{pq}).
\]

This makes the real difficulty visible:

* \(z_p=O(1)\) gives only the trivial pointwise bound \(|S_p(v)|\le z_p=O(1)\);
* cancellation must occur **horizontally across primes**;
* the phases contain modular inverses of products of the other two primes, giving a trilinear Kloosterman-fraction shape; and
* the coefficients \(S_p(\cdot)\) themselves are moving, prime-dependent transforms of the Apéry zero sets.

A power-saving estimate for this trilinear expression, with the actual Apéry coefficients, would make the heuristic rigorous. No currently available theorem supplies it merely from bounded cardinality or from the known single-prime geometry.

### The pair-first form is cleaner and exactly matches the repository frontier

Because \(pq>H\), each pair \((r,t)\in Z_p\times Z_q\) has at most one representative \(a_{p,q}(r,t)<H\). It contributes to a third prime \(s\) precisely when

\[
a_{p,q}(r,t)\bmod s\in Z_s.
\]

This is the exact enumeration used by [`hm3_explore.py`](../problems/3.2/hm3_explore.py). Analytically, define

\[
\Omega_p=\{0\le m<H:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\]

\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|,
\qquad
T_{p,q}=\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\sum_{s\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}
\mathbf1_{\Omega_s}(m),
\]

and

\[
\lambda_{p,q}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}
\frac{Z(s)}s.
\]

The repository proves the exact identity

\[
F_3(X)=\sum_{p\ne q}T_{p,q}
\]

and the equivalence

\[
\boxed{
(\mathrm{HM})_3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{p\ne q}
\bigl(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}\bigr)_+
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
}
\]

See [`hm3_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex). This positive pair--Palm excess is the sharp form of the missing arithmetic: after conditioning on an actual two-prime CRT hit, third primes must not be positively overloaded in aggregate.

This target is weaker and more realistic than termwise equidistribution for every prime triple. A successful direct-counting proof should aim at this aggregate excess, not at proving every individual count is zero.

## (B) Does Bombieri--Vinogradov, or a generalization, apply?

No black-box form of Bombieri--Vinogradov applies to this problem.

A classical Bombieri--Vinogradov estimate controls an average such as

\[
\sum_{d\le Q}
\max_{(a,d)=1}
\left|
\psi(Y;d,a)-\frac{Y}{\varphi(d)}
\right|,
\qquad
Q\lesssim Y^{1/2}(\log Y)^{-B},
\]

where a long arithmetic sequence—usually the primes up to \(Y\)—is counted in fixed residue classes modulo \(d\).

The present problem has a different geometry:

1. The integer interval has length \(H=X^2\), while the three-prime modulus is \(M=pqs\asymp X^3\). Thus a prescribed class modulo \(M\) has at most one representative in the interval. There is no long progression to average inside each modulus.
2. The objects being averaged over are the moduli \(p,q,s\) themselves, while the allowed residue set \(Z_p\) changes with \(p\).
3. After fixing a pair hit \(m=a_{p,q}(r,t)\), the third-prime condition is
   \[
   a_{p,q}(r,t)\bmod s\in Z_s.
   \]
   This is a **moving target depending on the modulus \(s\)**, not a fixed class \(a\pmod s\) arising from a fixed numerator or fixed polynomial.
4. Requiring \(p,q,s\) to be consecutive primes only imposes a prime-gap condition. It supplies no cancellation for the moving residues and makes the prime variables more dependent rather than less.

Even Elliott--Halberstam-type conjectures or the Bombieri--Friedlander--Iwaniec/Maynard theorems for primes in progressions to large moduli concern the distribution of the prime sequence in prescribed arithmetic progressions. They do not automatically estimate a sparse CRT incidence built from characteristic-dependent coefficient-zero sets.

The large sieve also cannot prove the desired result from arbitrary \(O(1)\)-size sets: the anchored-star model is an explicit counterexample satisfying the generic second-moment constraints. A large-sieve or dispersion argument can work only after one inserts an additional structural estimate for the actual coefficients \(S_p(v)\) or for the conditional third-prime loads.

So Bombieri--Vinogradov is relevant only at the level of **methodological analogy**—average discrepancies over moduli—not as an applicable theorem.

## (C) Is this a three-prime Linnik--Goldfeld problem? What literature is closest?

There is no standard problem called the “three-prime Linnik--Goldfeld problem” that directly matches this setup.

### Goldfeld's fixed-shift problem is different

Goldfeld's 1969 paper studies primes \(p\) for which the fixed-shift integer \(p+a\) has a large prime factor. The shift \(a\) is fixed globally, so the problem can be connected to primes in arithmetic progressions. In the Apéry problem, the target \(Z_s\) changes with \(s\), and there is no fixed integer or fixed polynomial whose roots produce all the residue sets. Thus the decisive uniformity in Goldfeld's setting is absent here.

### The closer classical name is Linnik--Skubenko

If “Linnik--Goldfeld” was intended to refer to equidistribution of modular roots, the closer classical problem is the **Linnik--Skubenko problem**: equidistribution of roots of fixed quadratic equations as the modulus varies. The crucial word is *fixed*. Those roots come from one global algebraic equation, which allows automorphic and Kloosterman-sum methods.

The Apéry CRT problem is better described as:

> a trilinear moving-target CRT discrepancy problem, or a mixed-characteristic pair--Palm correlation problem.

### Closest useful literature

The following works give the relevant analytic templates, but none applies without first expressing the Apéry zero indicators in a compatible fixed-structure form.

1. **Bombieri, large sieve / Bombieri--Vinogradov framework.**  
   E. Bombieri, “On the large sieve,” *Mathematika* **12** (1965), 201--225. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300005313).

2. **Fixed quadratic roots modulo prime moduli.**  
   W. Duke, J. B. Friedlander, H. Iwaniec, “Equidistribution of roots of a quadratic congruence to prime moduli,” *Annals of Mathematics* **141** (1995), 423--441. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118527).  
   This is the closest positive precedent for equidistribution of a bounded number of residues attached to each prime, but those residues are roots of a fixed quadratic congruence.

3. **Linnik--Skubenko / Weyl sums for fixed quadratic roots.**  
   W. Duke, J. B. Friedlander, H. Iwaniec, “Weyl Sums for Quadratic Roots,” *International Mathematics Research Notices* 2012, no. 11, 2493--2549. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnr112).

4. **Bilinear Kloosterman fractions.**  
   W. Duke, J. B. Friedlander, H. Iwaniec, “Bilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions,” *Inventiones Mathematicae* **128** (1997), 23--43. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220050135).

5. **Trilinear Kloosterman fractions.**  
   S. Bettin, V. Chandee, “Trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions,” *Advances in Mathematics* **328** (2018), 1234--1262. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2018.01.026).  
   This is analytically the nearest shape to the three-prime Fourier expression above. The obstruction is that its coefficient sequences are arbitrary but external, whereas here one still needs enough cancellation after inserting the prime-dependent zero-set transforms and the varying interval/modulus weights.

6. **Primes in progressions to large moduli.**  
   E. Bombieri, J. B. Friedlander, H. Iwaniec, “Primes in arithmetic progressions to large moduli,” *Acta Mathematica* **156** (1986), 203--251. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02399204).  
   J. Maynard, “Primes in Arithmetic Progressions to Large Moduli III: Uniform Residue Classes,” *Memoirs of the AMS* **306** (2025), no. 1544. These results demonstrate how much extra structure is needed to go beyond the classical level of distribution; they do not handle arbitrary moving zero fibers.

7. **The fixed-shift Goldfeld paper.**  
   M. Goldfeld, “On the number of primes \(p\) for which \(p+a\) has a large prime factor,” *Mathematika* **16** (1969), 23--27. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300004575).

The conceptual lesson from this literature is consistent: boundedly many residues per modulus become tractable when they arise from a fixed algebraic or automorphic object whose exponential sums can be controlled uniformly. The current Apéry zero sets are coefficient-zero loci in the defining characteristic; no fixed bounded-conductor horizontal object is presently known that yields the required three-prime phases.

## (D) Does palindromic symmetry help?

It gives an exact and useful pairing, but only a constant-factor structural fact—not the required equidistribution.

Suppose

\[
Z_p=p-1-Z_p,
\]

so that \(r\in Z_p\) implies \(r^*=p-1-r\equiv-1-r\pmod p\). If

\[
m=[r_1,r_2,r_3]_{p_1,p_2,p_3}
\]

and all three residues are reflected, then the reflected CRT representative \(m^*\) satisfies

\[
m^*\equiv-1-m\pmod{p_i}\quad(i=1,2,3).
\]

Consequently, with \(M=p_1p_2p_3\),

\[
\boxed{m^*=M-1-m.}
\]

Thus reflection pairs the lower interval

\[
[0,H)
\]

with the upper interval

\[
(M-H,M).
\]

Since \(M\asymp X^3\gg H=X^2\), a lower-tail hit is paired with a point very near the top of the CRT period. This proves symmetry of the distribution about \((M-1)/2\), but it does not force the lower-tail count to vanish and does not create cancellation in a positive counting problem. At best it reduces an exhaustive computation by a factor of approximately two.

Partial reflections of one or two coordinates generate the other members of a \(2^3\)-orbit, but there is no corresponding deterministic spacing theorem that keeps all of them away from \([0,H)\).

On the Fourier side, reflection gives

\[
S_p(a)=e_p(-a)\overline{S_p(a)},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\overline{S_p(a)}=e_p(a)S_p(a).
\]

Hence \(e_p(a/2)S_p(a)\) is real for odd \(p\). This constrains the phase of each local Fourier coefficient, but it supplies no saving in its magnitude and no cross-prime cancellation by itself.

Most decisively, the anchored-star countermodel is already reflection-invariant. It places one chosen member of every reflected pair on the same \(m_0<X^2\), so reflection is fully compatible with maximal lower-tail alignment. Therefore palindromicity can help only when combined with a genuinely Apéry-specific identity that couples the selected reflection orbits across different primes.

The computation in [`hm3_exploration.md`](../problems/3.2/hm3_exploration.md) is consistent with this conclusion: reflection-side patterns are well balanced and full-reflection partners lie near the opposite end of the CRT period, but this supports the random model rather than proving it.

## 2. What a viable direct-counting theorem should say

There are three increasingly strong possible targets.

### Target 1: the exact \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) upper bound

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p<q<s}
\sum_{r\in Z_p,t\in Z_q,u\in Z_s}
\mathbf1_{\{[r,t,u]_{p,q,s}<X^2\}}
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
}
\]

This is the correct direct CRT formulation. An asymptotic with the random main term would be stronger but is not necessary.

### Target 2: the pair--Palm positive-excess bound

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}
\bigl(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}\bigr)_+
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
}
\]

By the theorem already proved in [`hm3_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex), this is equivalent to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). It isolates the exact missing assertion: actual pair CRT loci must not attract too many third primes.

### Target 3: an Apéry-weighted trilinear Kloosterman-fraction estimate

After smoothing the cutoff and applying Fourier inversion, prove power cancellation in the nonzero-frequency trilinear sum involving

\[
S_p(h\overline{qs})S_q(h\overline{ps})S_s(h\overline{pq}).
\]

This would be a genuine new analytic theorem. Generic Kloosterman-fraction bounds suggest the architecture, but one must first exploit special information about the horizontal family \(S_p\); treating these coefficients as arbitrary cannot rule out the anchored-star alignment.

A useful intermediate theorem would be an averaged conditional estimate, not a uniform estimate for every pair:

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}
\left(
\frac{T_{p,q}}{J_{p,q}}-\lambda_{p,q}
\right)_+
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3,
\]

with the summand interpreted as zero when \(J_{p,q}=0\). This is just the pair--Palm condition written as a conditional overload, and it avoids demanding impossible termwise accuracy when the expected count for a single triple is below one.

## Final answers to (A)--(D)

**(A)** The heuristic cannot be made rigorous from CRT linearity, \(Z(p)=O(1)\), consecutiveness, or reflection. There are explicit bounded reflection-invariant moving sets whose CRT values all align. For the actual Apéry sets, rigor would require a new horizontal three-prime correlation theorem; the exact pair--Palm excess in the repository is the sharp target.

**(B)** Bombieri--Vinogradov does not apply. It controls a long arithmetic sequence in fixed residue classes averaged over moduli. Here the product modulus exceeds the interval, each CRT class contributes at most one point, and the target residue sets move with the primes. Large-modulus AP theorems do not remove this mismatch.

**(C)** This is not a standard Linnik--Goldfeld problem. Goldfeld's fixed-shift problem is structurally different. The closest literature is the Linnik--Skubenko/DFI theory of fixed quadratic roots and the DFI/Bettin--Chandee theory of bilinear or trilinear Kloosterman fractions. Those works provide a template only after one finds fixed horizontal structure for the Apéry zero transforms.

**(D)** Palindromicity sends \(m\mapsto M-1-m\), pairing the lower and upper tails, and gives a local Fourier phase constraint. It supplies at most a constant-factor simplification. The reflection-invariant anchored-star obstruction proves that it cannot by itself yield any \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) saving.

## Recommended disposition of this avenue

Retain the **direct CRT enumerator and pair-first expansion**; they are exactly the right computational and algebraic representations. Drop the proposed goal “every CRT value is \(>X^2\)” and drop the restriction to ambient consecutive primes as a route to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). The next theorem should be formulated as either the pair--Palm positive-excess estimate or an equivalent Apéry-weighted trilinear Kloosterman-fraction bound.
