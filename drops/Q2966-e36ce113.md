# 10. The smallest exact missing lemma

The next useful statement cannot be “one more homogeneous Apéry solution”: an order-two scalar recurrence has only a rank-two homogeneous module. The smallest genuine escape is a **non-split sparse extension**.

One precise formulation is the following.

> **Sparse non-split jet commonizer lemma.** Construct a p-independent augmented transfer system
> \[
> \binom{Y_{k+1}}{Z_{k+1}}
> =
> \begin{pmatrix}
> M_k&0\\
> N_k&M_k
> \end{pmatrix}
> \binom{Y_k}{Z_k},
> \]
> together with p-independent gauges `G_k`, such that:
> 1. the transformed cocycle
>    \[
>    N_k+M_kG_k-G_{k+1}M_k
>    \]
>    is supported on `O(1)` indices;
> 2. the resulting right boundary scalar `Gamma_r` is nonzero and satisfies
>    \[
>    p>r,\ p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow p\mid\Gamma_r;
>    \]
> 3. `Gamma_r` is not a local multiple of `D_r` and its target vanishing is not equivalent to `p|D_{r-2}` or `p|P(r-1)`;
> 4. after the fixed-q substitution `r=n-qp`, the moving family is commonized by one integer `Gamma_q(n)` with
>    \[
>    \log|\Gamma_q(n)|=o(n)
>    \]
>    (polynomial height would be stronger).

Condition 1 is the exact sparse-defect requirement. Condition 2 is the desired second target zero. Condition 3 excludes the two local tautologies proved above. Condition 4 is the Lucas/height requirement needed for P3.2.

The Gessel derivative is the natural first candidate for the non-split cocycle `N_k`, but its source is currently distributed and its known energy commonizers fail the required height.
