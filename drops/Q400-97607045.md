# Horizontal large sieve inequalities for Apéry zero-set Fourier transforms

## Executive verdict

There is an exact Fourier formulation of pinned row discrepancy, and it makes the hierarchy of missing estimates very clear.

1. For each pair of primes, the CRT error is a bilinear pairing of the two Fourier packets
   \[
   \left(\frac{S_p(h)}p\right)_{h\ne0},
   \qquad
   \left(\frac{S_q(k)}q\right)_{k\ne0}
   \]
   through the Dirichlet kernel at the near-Farey difference \(h/p-k/q\).

2. For fixed \(p\), the pinned row sum \(Y_p\) pairs the \(p\)-packet with the superposition of all \(q\)-packets. Squaring and summing over \(p\) produces a **three-prime, four-Fourier-variable form**: the prime \(p\) is repeated, while \(q\) and \(r\) are the two arms of the pinned star.

3. The ordinary additive large sieve controls only the first-level quadratic form
   \[
   c^*G^*Gc
   =\sum_{n\in I}\left|\sum_pc_pg_p(n)\right|^2.
   \]
   PRD asks for
   \[
   \|(G^*G)^\circ\mathbf1\|_2^2,
   \]
   where \((\cdot)^\circ\) means that the diagonal is removed. This is one spectral level higher: it is a fourth-order statistic of the centered incidence columns.

4. A marginal theorem saying that the noncentral doublet location \(r_p/p\) is equidistributed is not enough. PRD requires growing-frequency, modular-inverse-coupled, **multi-prime** decorrelation. A Sato--Tate-type weak-convergence theorem is therefore not the missing input.

5. The closest useful theorem statement is a weighted horizontal large sieve for the error matrix:
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sum_p\left|\sum_{q\ne p}c_q\varepsilon_{pq}(I)\right|^2
   \ll
   N\Lambda_{2,N}
   \sum_q\frac{z_q^2}{q}|c_q|^2,
   }
   \tag{HLS-PRD}
   \]
   where
   \[
   \Lambda_{2,N}=\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le N}\frac{z_p^2}{p}.
   \]
   Taking \(c_q=1\) gives PRD whenever \(\Lambda_{2,N}=O(1)\). In the doublet channel, \(z_p=2\), this is the random-model scale.

6. No Kuznetsov formula, automorphic spectral decomposition, or bounded-conductor trace-function realization is currently known for the functions \(S_p(h)\). The realistic BFI analogue would first require a new **crystalline/Mellin sum-of-products theorem** for the Apéry coefficient-zero detector, and only then an analytic dispersion argument.

Thus the answer to the last question is decisive:

\[
\boxed{
\text{PRD requires a genuinely new multi-prime Fourier estimate,}
\text{ not a one-prime equidistribution theorem.}
}
\]

Three normalization points should be fixed at the outset.

## 0.1 The raw zero count is not proved uniformly bounded

Reflection implies

\[
r\in Z_p\Longleftrightarrow p-1-r\in Z_p.
\]

Thus noncentral zeros occur in pairs, but this is a parity statement, not an absolute bound. Rare midpoint zeros give odd cardinality. The current unconditional result in the manuscript is a polynomial bound such as \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\), while the computations show a thin small-valued distribution. Any theorem using \(z_p=O(1)\) must therefore be understood either as:

- a theorem for the doublet/bounded-cardinality channel; or
- a conditional theorem with a harmonic second-mass hypothesis such as \(\Lambda_{2,N}=O(1)\).

## 0.2 The exact PRD convention can be symmetric or triangular

Let

\[
\mathcal P_N=\{p\text{ prime}:\sqrt N<p\le N\}.
\]

For a fixed \(p\), let \(\mathcal Q_p\) denote either

\[
\mathcal P_N\setminus\{p\}
\]

or the oriented set

\[
\{q\in\mathcal P_N:q>p\}.
\]

All formulas below hold with either choice. The symmetric convention makes the matrix notation cleaner; the triangular convention is the one used in some versions of PRD.

## 0.3 The theoretical main term leaves endpoint axes

Let \(I\) be an interval of \(N\) consecutive integers. Put

\[
f_p(n)=\mathbf1_{Z_p}(n\bmod p),
\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{z_p}{p},
\qquad
g_p(n)=f_p(n)-\rho_p.
\]

Define

\[
d_p(I)=\sum_{n\in I}f_p(n),
\qquad
\beta_p(I)=d_p(I)-N\rho_p.
\]

Then

\[
|\beta_p(I)|\le z_p.
\]

Centering by \(z_p/p\) is natural arithmetically, but it leaves these boundary terms. Centering by the empirical mean \(d_p(I)/N\) removes them exactly. Since the PRD in the question uses \(Nz_pz_q/(pq)\), the exact Fourier formula must retain the boundary-frequency axes. They should not be silently dropped.

# 1. Exact Fourier expansion of one pair error

Write

\[
e_m(x)=e^{2\pi i x/m}
\]

and define, with the sign convention in the question,

\[
S_p(h)=\sum_{a\in Z_p}e_p(ha),
\qquad h\in\mathbb F_p.
\]

Thus \(S_p(0)=z_p\), and finite Fourier inversion gives

\[
\boxed{
 f_p(n)=\frac1p\sum_{h\bmod p}S_p(h)e_p(-hn).
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

For an interval \(I\), define the ordinary Dirichlet kernel

\[
D_I(\theta)=\sum_{n\in I}e(-n\theta).
\tag{1.2}
\]

If \(I=[0,N)\), then

\[
D_I(\theta)
=e\!\left(-\frac{N-1}{2}\theta\right)
 \frac{\sin(\pi N\theta)}{\sin(\pi\theta)}
\]

with the usual continuous interpretation at integers.

For distinct primes put

\[
A_{pq}(I)=\sum_{n\in I}f_p(n)f_q(n),
\qquad
\varepsilon_{pq}(I)=A_{pq}(I)-\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Because \(p,q>\sqrt N\), one has \(pq>N\), but the Fourier identity below does not need that inequality.

## 1.1 Product-modulus form

Let \(Q=pq\), and let

\[
\widehat{1_I}^{(Q)}(t)=\sum_{n\in I}e_Q(-tn).
\]

Write \(\bar q_p=q^{-1}\bmod p\) and \(\bar p_q=p^{-1}\bmod q\). Multiplying (1.1) for \(p\) and \(q\), and identifying the two local frequencies with one frequency modulo \(pq\), gives

\[
\boxed{
A_{pq}(I)
=\frac1{pq}\sum_{t\bmod pq}
 \widehat{1_I}^{(pq)}(t)
 S_p(t\bar q_p)S_q(t\bar p_q).
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The term \(t=0\) is exactly \(Nz_pz_q/(pq)\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{pq}(I)
=\frac1{pq}\sum_{\substack{t\bmod pq\\t\ne0}}
 \widehat{1_I}^{(pq)}(t)
 S_p(t\bar q_p)S_q(t\bar p_q).
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

This is the cleanest exact formula for the full theoretical-center error. Frequencies divisible by \(p\) or by \(q\) are present; they are precisely the one-prime boundary axes.

## 1.2 Separated-frequency form and the boundary decomposition

Removing the zero frequency from (1.1) gives

\[
 g_p(n)
 =\frac1p\sum_{h\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
 S_p(h)e_p(-hn).
\tag{1.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\beta_p(I)
=\frac1p\sum_{h\ne0}S_p(h)D_I(h/p).
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

For the genuinely two-frequency part, put

\[
C_{pq}(I)=\sum_{n\in I}g_p(n)g_q(n).
\]

After replacing the \(q\)-frequency by its negative, one obtains the near-Farey form

\[
\boxed{
C_{pq}(I)
=\frac1{pq}
 \sum_{h\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb F_q^\times}
 S_p(h)\overline{S_q(k)}
 D_I\!\left(\frac hp-\frac kq\right).
}
\tag{1.8}
\]

Expanding \(f_p=\rho_p+g_p\) gives the exact relation

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{pq}(I)
=C_{pq}(I)+\rho_p\beta_q(I)+\rho_q\beta_p(I).
}
\tag{1.9}
\]

Thus the product-modulus formula (1.5) decomposes into:

- the interior frequencies \(h\ne0\), \(k\ne0\), giving \(C_{pq}\);
- the \(p\)-axis, giving \(\rho_q\beta_p\);
- the \(q\)-axis, giving \(\rho_p\beta_q\).

If empirical centering is used instead, with

\[
\widetilde g_p(n)=f_p(n)-\frac{d_p(I)}N,
\]

then

\[
\sum_{n\in I}\widetilde g_p(n)\widetilde g_q(n)
=A_{pq}(I)-\frac{d_p(I)d_q(I)}N.
\]

The difference between this empirical covariance and \(\varepsilon_{pq}\) is an explicit rank-two-plus-rank-one boundary matrix:

\[
\varepsilon_{pq}
=\widetilde C_{pq}
 +\rho_p\beta_q+\rho_q\beta_p
 +\frac{\beta_p\beta_q}{N}.
\tag{1.10}
\]

This is useful for numerical linear algebra, but the full PRD theorem must either control (1.10) or work directly with (1.5).

# 2. Exact Fourier expansion of the pinned row \(Y_p\)

Let

\[
Y_p(I)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}\varepsilon_{pq}(I).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Define

\[
\Lambda_p=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}\rho_q,
\qquad
B_p=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}\beta_q(I),
\tag{2.2}
\]

and, for \(h\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), define the horizontal superposition

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_p(h)
=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}\frac1q
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb F_q^\times}
 \overline{S_q(k)}
 D_I\!\left(\frac hp-\frac kq\right).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Summing (1.8) and (1.9) over \(q\) gives

\[
\boxed{
Y_p(I)
=\rho_pB_p+\beta_p(I)\Lambda_p
 +\frac1p\sum_{h\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
  S_p(h)\mathcal H_p(h).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

The last term is the genuine pinned Fourier interaction. Write it as

\[
Y_p^\circ(I)
=\frac1p\sum_{h\ne0}S_p(h)\mathcal H_p(h).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_p|Y_p|^2
=\sum_p\left|
Y_p^\circ+\rho_pB_p+\beta_p\Lambda_p
\right|^2.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Thus a proof may either keep all three terms together through (1.5), or prove a core estimate for \(Y_p^\circ\) plus separate boundary-row estimates.

## 2.1 The pinned three-prime expansion

Squaring (2.5) first gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p|Y_p^\circ|^2
=\sum_p\frac1{p^2}
 \sum_{h,h'\ne0}
 S_p(h)\overline{S_p(h')}
 \mathcal H_p(h)\overline{\mathcal H_p(h')}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Substituting (2.3) gives the fully expanded form

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\sum_p|Y_p^\circ|^2
&=\sum_p\sum_{q,r\in\mathcal Q_p}
 \frac1{p^2qr}
 \sum_{\substack{h,h'\in\mathbb F_p^\times\\
                  k\in\mathbb F_q^\times,
                  \ell\in\mathbb F_r^\times}}
 S_p(h)\overline{S_p(h')}
 \overline{S_q(k)}S_r(\ell)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times
 D_I\!\left(\frac hp-\frac kq\right)
 \overline{
 D_I\!\left(\frac {h'}p-\frac\ell r\right)}.
\end{aligned}}
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

This is the exact load-bearing form. It has:

- one pinned prime \(p\);
- two arm primes \(q,r\);
- two frequencies at the pinned characteristic;
- one frequency at each arm;
- two short-interval kernels sharing the same \(p\).

The terms \(q=r\) give the self energy. The terms \(q\ne r\) are exactly the shared-one-prime correlations observed numerically. A negative aggregate shared-prime contribution is cancellation inside (2.8); it is not a consequence of CRT alone.

## 2.2 Matrix interpretation

Let \(G\) be the \(|I|\times|\mathcal P_N|\) centered incidence matrix

\[
G(n,p)=g_p(n).
\]

Then

\[
C=G^*G,
\qquad
C_{pq}=C_{pq}(I).
\]

Let \(C^\circ=C-\operatorname{diag}(C)\). Ignoring the explicit low-rank boundary matrix for one sentence, the symmetric PRD core is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p|Y_p^\circ|^2
=\|C^\circ\mathbf1\|_2^2
=\mathbf1^*(C^\circ)^2\mathbf1.
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

This identity explains why the ordinary large sieve is not automatically PRD.

- The ordinary large sieve controls
  \[
  c^*Cc=\|Gc\|_2^2.
  \]
- PRD controls
  \[
  \|C^\circ\mathbf1\|_2^2.
  \]

The latter is a squared Gram operator with its large diagonal removed. It is a fourth-order statistic of the incidence columns.

# 3. What the classical additive large sieve actually gives

The common general form of the additive large sieve is the following: if points \(\alpha_j\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) are separated by at least \(\delta\), then

\[
\sum_j\left|\sum_{n\in I}a_ne(n\alpha_j)\right|^2
\le (N-1+\delta^{-1})\sum_{n\in I}|a_n|^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For the reduced fractions

\[
\frac hp,
\qquad
P<p\le2P,
\qquad
1\le h<p,
\]

the spacing is \(\gg P^{-2}\). Therefore the classical constant is \(N+O(P^2)\).

Now use the exact packet Parseval identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}|S_p(h)|^2
=pz_p-z_p^2.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For arbitrary coefficients \(c_p\), the factorization (1.6) and the dual additive large sieve yield the unconditional dyadic estimate

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in I}
 \left|\sum_{P<p\le2P}c_pg_p(n)\right|^2
\ll
(N+P^2)
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|c_p|^2
\left(\frac{z_p}{p}-\frac{z_p^2}{p^2}\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is a genuine horizontal large sieve for the first-level Fourier packets, and it is valid for arbitrary sets \(Z_p\).

## 3.1 Why it stops at the square-root boundary

When \(P\asymp\sqrt N\), one has \(N+P^2\asymp N\), which is the natural scale.

When \(P\gg\sqrt N\), the term \(P^2\) dominates. At \(P\asymp N\), the loss is a full factor of order \(N\). This is exactly the regime where the interval is much shorter than the Farey resolution scale.

No information about the Apéry recurrence has entered (3.3); it uses only packet energy. Hence it cannot distinguish the actual Apéry family from the reflected anchored-star countermodel.

## 3.2 The current repository's short-arc target

`problems/3.2/oracleA_result.tex` sharpens the formulation. With

\[
c_{p,h}=\frac{(\log p)S_p(h)}p,
\]

it defines a short-arc sum over fractions \(h/p\) and proves an exact triangular/Fejér-kernel identity. The cross-prime part is

\[
R_{\ne}(P,M)
=
\sum_{p\ne q}
\sum_{h,k}
K_M\!\left(\frac hp-\frac kq\right)
\frac{(\log p)(\log q)}{pq}
S_p(h)\overline{S_q(k)},
\tag{3.4}
\]

where

\[
K_M(t)=\left(M^{-1}-\|t\|\right)_+.
\]

The exact missing first-level estimate isolated there is a one-sided near-Farey cancellation bound of the form

\[
\operatorname{Re}R_{\ne}(P,M)
\ll M^{-1+o(1)}Q_P,
\tag{3.5}
\]

with \(Q_P\) the packet \(L^2\) mass.

This is the closest existing formulation to a horizontal large sieve for the Apéry packets. It is not proved. If proved, it gives a strong weighted dispersion theorem and closes P3.2 by the alternate route documented in that file.

However, (3.5) is still a first-level scalar quadratic form. PRD asks for a pinned \(TT^*\) version: after fixing one prime \(p\), the near-Farey interactions with all \(q\)'s must have controlled \(L^2\) energy as \(p\) varies. That is exactly (2.8).

# 4. The natural PRD-level horizontal large sieve

Let \(E(I)\) be the matrix indexed by \(\mathcal P_N\) with

\[
E_{pq}(I)=
\begin{cases}
\varepsilon_{pq}(I),&p\ne q,\\
0,&p=q.
\end{cases}
\]

For a bounded-cardinality channel define

\[
w_p=\frac{z_p^2}{p},
\qquad
\Lambda_{2,N}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_N}w_p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The robust random-subset variance bound naturally uses \(z_p^2/p\). If \(z_p\) is uniformly bounded, this is equivalent up to constants to the more intuitive weight \(z_p/p\). In the doublet channel, \(z_p=2\), the two weights differ only by a factor of two.

## Proposed theorem: horizontal large sieve for pinned CRT errors

For every complex coefficient sequence \((c_q)\), every interval \(I\) of length \(N\), and either the symmetric or triangular adjacency convention,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_N}
\left|
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}c_q\varepsilon_{pq}(I)
\right|^2
\ll
N\Lambda_{2,N}
\sum_{q\in\mathcal P_N}w_q|c_q|^2.
}
\tag{HLS-PRD}
\]

This is the clean reusable analogue of a large-sieve inequality.

Taking \(c_q=1\) gives

\[
\sum_p|Y_p(I)|^2
\ll N\Lambda_{2,N}^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\Lambda_{2,N}=O(1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sum_p|Y_p(I)|^2=O(N).
\]

The random-residue theorem in the project predicts precisely this scale.

## 4.1 The logical minimum is weaker

The full coefficient-uniform inequality `(HLS-PRD)` is stronger than P3.2 needs. The literal minimum is its specialization

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p\left|\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}arepsilon_{pq}(I)\right|^2
\ll N.
}
\tag{PRD-scalar}
\]

Equivalently, by (1.5), the literal minimum is

\[
\sum_p
\left|
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}
\frac1{pq}
\sum_{\substack{t\bmod pq\\t\ne0}}
\widehat{1_I}^{(pq)}(t)
S_p(t\bar q_p)S_q(t\bar p_q)
\right|^2
\ll N.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is just PRD written in Fourier variables. It is the weakest exact theorem statement, but it offers little structural guidance. `(HLS-PRD)` is the natural reusable target because it allows arbitrary arm coefficients \(c_q\), exactly as a classical large sieve does.

## 4.2 Why a first-level frame bound is not enough

Suppose one somehow proves the ideal first-level inequality

\[
\sum_{n\in I}\left|\sum_pc_pg_p(n)\right|^2
\ll N\sum_p\rho_p|c_p|^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

This says that the degree-normalized columns form a bounded frame. It controls \(G^*G\) in one weighted operator norm.

PRD needs the off-diagonal square \((G^*G)^\circ{}^2\). The large diagonal entries

\[
\|g_p\|_2^2\asymp N\rho_p
\]

must be removed before squaring. A crude use of the operator norm of \(G^*G\) retains those diagonals and, for primes near \(\sqrt N\), loses roughly a factor \(N^{1/2}\). Therefore a first-level frame theorem does not automatically yield the random-scale PRD bound.

The needed theorem is specifically about the **off-diagonal Gram operator**, or equivalently the shared-prime form (2.8).

# 5. Which cancellation in \(S_p\) is actually needed?

## 5.1 Primewise pointwise bounds are irrelevant

For every set \(Z_p\),

\[
|S_p(h)|\le z_p.
\]

When \(z_p=2\), this is sharp for many frequencies. It contains no arithmetic information.

The primewise Parseval identity (3.2) is nontrivial algebraically but universal combinatorially. It also holds for the anchored-star model. Thus neither the \(L^\infty\) norm nor the individual \(L^2\) norm distinguishes Apéry.

## 5.2 Fixed-frequency vertical cancellation is insufficient

A statement such as

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}S_p(h)\ll P^{1/2+o(1)}
\]

for every fixed integer \(h\) would still not address the kernel in (1.5) or (1.8). The relevant frequencies are

\[
tq^{-1}\bmod p,
\qquad
tp^{-1}\bmod q,
\]

or, in the separated form, pairs \((h,k)\) constrained by

\[
\left\|\frac hp-\frac kq\right\|\lesssim\frac1N.
\]

They vary with both moduli and reach the full finite-field frequency range. Fixed-\(h\) vertical cancellation does not survive these moving inverses.

## 5.3 First-level horizontal cancellation controls total pair dispersion

The near-Farey form (3.4) is the right first-level horizontal object. Cancellation in

\[
S_p(h)\overline{S_q(k)}
\]

when \(h/p\) and \(k/q\) lie in the same short arc controls

\[
\sum_{p,q}c_p\overline{c_q}C_{pq}.
\]

This is sufficient for a Barban--Davenport--Halberstam-type second moment of the row count.

## 5.4 PRD needs pinned second-level horizontal cancellation

PRD asks for cancellation in

\[
S_p(h)\overline{S_p(h')}
\overline{S_q(k)}S_r(\ell)
\]

weighted by two kernels sharing \(p\), exactly as in (2.8).

Thus the answer to “vertical, horizontal, or both?” is:

- the final theorem must be **horizontal and multi-prime**;
- vertical one-prime information may be part of a proof;
- but no collection of fixed-frequency marginal estimates is sufficient by itself.

A useful proof architecture may start from a geometric vertical theorem and then use dispersion to convert it into (2.8). The deliverable, however, is still a shared-prime horizontal estimate.

# 6. The doublet channel and the cosine packet

Suppose

\[
Z_p=\{r_p,p-1-r_p\},
\qquad
0<r_p<\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

The formula in the question needs a centering phase. Put

\[
\nu_p=2r_p+1.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_p(h)
&=e_p(hr_p)+e_p(h(p-1-r_p))\\
&=2e_p(-h/2)
 \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi h\nu_p}{p}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently, using the reflected gap

\[
\delta_p=p-1-2r_p,
\]

one may write the same packet as a known center phase times

\[
\cos(\pi h\delta_p/p).
\]

The cosine is real after the deterministic palindromic rotation; the packet itself is not literally \(2\cos(2\pi r_ph/p)\) in the unshifted coordinate.

## 6.1 Exact product-modulus doublet formula

Substituting (6.1) into (1.5) gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_{pq}(I)
&=\frac4{pq}
\sum_{\substack{t\bmod pq\\t\ne0}}
\widehat{1_I}^{(pq)}(t)
 e_{pq}(-t/2)\\
&\qquad\times
\cos\!\left(
 \frac{\pi(t\bar q_p)\nu_p}{p}
\right)
\cos\!\left(
 \frac{\pi(t\bar p_q)\nu_q}{q}
\right),
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.2}
\]

with the ordinary representatives of the finite-field frequencies understood inside the cosines. This is the exact doublet kernel, including the boundary axes.

The PRD expression is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p\left|
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}
\frac4{pq}
\sum_{t\ne0}
\widehat{1_I}^{(pq)}(t)e_{pq}(-t/2)
C_p(t\bar q_p)C_q(t\bar p_q)
\right|^2,
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

where

\[
C_p(u)=\cos(\pi u\nu_p/p).
\]

A sum of the individual pairwise squares

\[
\sum_{p<q}|\varepsilon_{pq}|^2
\]

captures only the self term. It misses the shared-prime terms

\[
\varepsilon_{pq}\overline{\varepsilon_{pr}},
\]

which are exactly the decisive negative/repulsive contribution seen in the computation. Therefore the square must remain **outside the sum over \(q\)**, as in (6.3).

## 6.2 Why equidistribution of \(r_p/p\) is not enough

The repository computation through \(p\le20{,}000\) is consistent with

\[
\frac{2r_p}{p-1}
\]

being uniform on \([0,1]\) within the doublet subfamily. This is strong evidence for the reflected-random marginal model.

Even a proof of that marginal equidistribution would control only averages of fixed-complexity test functions

\[
\Phi(r_p/p).
\]

Formula (6.2) requires much more:

1. Fourier frequencies grow with \(p\) and range through all nonzero residues.
2. The frequency at \(p\) is multiplied by \(q^{-1}\bmod p\).
3. The frequency at \(q\) is multiplied by \(p^{-1}\bmod q\).
4. The interval kernel chooses a moving short arc depending on \(pq/N\).
5. Squaring PRD couples \((p,q)\) to \((p,r)\).
6. The desired inequality must tolerate arbitrary coefficients \(c_q\) if one wants a genuine large-sieve theorem.

This is analogous to the distinction between:

- weak convergence of one-dimensional marginals; and
- a restriction/large-sieve inequality uniform over high-frequency test functions.

The latter never follows formally from the former.

A deterministic sequence can have uniform marginals and still possess a sparse subsequence with coherent pair or triple correlations. The anchored-star constructions in the project make this logical obstruction explicit.

## 6.3 A doublet-specific horizontal large sieve

Let \(\mathcal D_N\subseteq\mathcal P_N\) be the doublet primes. A clean doublet theorem would be

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in\mathcal D_N}
\left|
\sum_{\substack{q\in\mathcal D_N\\q\ne p}}
 c_q\varepsilon_{pq}^{\mathrm{dbl}}(I)
\right|^2
\ll
N\Lambda_{D,N}
\sum_{q\in\mathcal D_N}\frac{|c_q|^2}{q},
}
\tag{D-HLS}
\]

where

\[
\Lambda_{D,N}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal D_N}\frac1p
\]

and harmless factors of \(2\) or \(4\) may be absorbed in the constant. Taking \(c_q=1\) gives doublet PRD.

This is not a consequence of the empirical uniformity of \(r_p/p\). It is the quantitative joint statement that the empirical observation suggests.

# 7. Comparison with BFI dispersion and Kloosterman spectral theory

There is a genuine formal analogy with Bombieri--Friedlander--Iwaniec dispersion.

## 7.1 What happens in BFI-type arguments

A classical dispersion proof typically:

1. centers an arithmetic-progression count;
2. expands an \(L^2\) norm;
3. applies Cauchy--Schwarz, Poisson/Voronoi summation, or reciprocity;
4. obtains phases containing modular inverses;
5. recognizes Kloosterman sums or Kloosterman fractions;
6. uses the Kuznetsov trace formula, Deshouillers--Iwaniec estimates, Weil bounds, or modern bilinear/trilinear Kloosterman estimates.

The crucial point is that Kloosterman sums belong to a coherent automorphic/spectral family. Their correlations across moduli are not arbitrary.

Relevant primary examples include:

- [Bettin--Chandee, *Trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00769);
- [Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *Algebraic trace functions over the primes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6043);
- [Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel--Sawin, *Bilinear forms with trace functions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459).

## 7.2 The Apéry kernel has the analytic shape but not the spectral object

The Apéry formulas contain the same two analytic signals:

- modular inverses \(q^{-1}\bmod p\) and \(p^{-1}\bmod q\);
- near-Farey localization through \(D_I(h/p-k/q)\).

However, the amplitudes

\[
S_p(h)=\sum_{a\in Z_p}e_p(ha)
\]

are not currently known to be:

- Fourier coefficients of an automorphic form in the variables \((p,h)\);
- Kloosterman sums of a fixed rank;
- trace functions of a uniformly bounded-conductor \(\ell\)-adic sheaf as \(p\) varies;
- values in a compatible system to which a Kuznetsov or Frobenius large sieve directly applies.

The Ahlgren--Ono newform controls only the central coefficient

\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\pmod p.
\]

It does not encode the noncentral coefficient-zero positions and therefore does not furnish a spectral expansion of \(S_p(h)\).

Likewise, the factorization

\[
A_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{\epsilon_p}B_p(t)^2
\]

is in the evaluation variable \(t\). Its roots are not the coefficient-zero indices \(Z_p\). The degree of \(B_p\) is \(\Theta(p)\), so even the evaluation-root family has growing complexity. It cannot be inserted as a bounded-rank Kloosterman substitute.

## 7.3 The plausible geometric replacement

There is an exact additive zero detector:

\[
\mathbf1_{\{b_r=0\bmod p\}}
=\frac1p\sum_{s\bmod p}e_p(sb_r).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore, for \(h\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{
S_p(h)
=\frac1p\sum_{s\bmod p}
 \sum_{r\bmod p}e_p(sb_r+hr).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The \(s=0\) term vanishes when \(h\ne0\). Formula (7.2) identifies the kind of new geometric input that would be useful.

One would need a theorem roughly of the following type:

> For every fixed number of transformed copies of the Apéry coefficient function, all complete sums of products arising after dispersion have square-root cancellation outside an explicitly classified diagonal locus, with conductor/complexity bounds uniform in \(p\).

Concretely, after Cauchy--Schwarz and completion, one expects sums resembling

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}
\psi_p\!\left(
ax+\sum_{j=1}^m\epsilon_js_j\,u_p(\gamma_jx)
\right),
\tag{7.3}
\]

where \(u_p\) is a crystalline/Hasse coordinate encoding the distinguished Apéry coefficient orbit and the \(\gamma_j\) are the fractional-linear transforms generated by the dispersion process. A Goursat--Kolchin--Ribet/product-monodromy theorem would then be needed to classify the exceptional tuples.

This is the “crystalline sum-of-products” route already isolated in the project. The modern trace-function papers cited above provide an analytic and geometric template **after** a bounded-complexity trace object exists. They do not construct that object for the Apéry coefficient-zero detector.

Thus there is presently no direct analogue of the Kuznetsov spectral input. Constructing or replacing it is the arithmetic heart of the problem.

# 8. What is the minimal new ingredient?

There are three useful levels of answer.

## Level 1: exact logical minimum

The exact logical minimum is the scalar Fourier PRD estimate (4.3):

\[
\sum_p|Y_p(I)|^2\ll N.
\]

This is sufficient and, within the PRD route, essentially tautologically necessary.

## Level 2: clean reusable analytic theorem

The cleanest reusable input is `(HLS-PRD)`:

\[
\sum_p\left|\sum_qc_q\varepsilon_{pq}(I)\right|^2
\ll
N\Lambda_{2,N}\sum_q\frac{z_q^2}{q}|c_q|^2.
\]

This is a true horizontal large-sieve statement. It:

- has arbitrary coefficients;
- detects the anchored star;
- has the random-residue scale;
- contains the shared-prime covariance cancellation;
- yields PRD by taking \(c_q=1\).

## Level 3: structural theorem from which the large sieve might follow

A plausible structural input is a bounded-complexity crystalline/Mellin realization plus a uniform sum-of-products/product-monodromy theorem strong enough to run a BFI/FKM-style dispersion argument.

This is substantially more than a Sato--Tate theorem. A Sato--Tate law controls one-prime conjugacy classes against fixed test functions. PRD requires:

- defining-characteristic coefficient zeros;
- growing frequencies;
- two modular inverses;
- three varying primes after squaring;
- short-interval kernels;
- arbitrary coefficient stability if formulated as a large sieve.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{One-prime equidistribution is evidence.}
\quad
\text{Multi-prime Fourier dispersion is the missing theorem.}
}
\]

# 9. Direct answers to the six questions

## 1. Precise Fourier expansion

The exact pair formula is (1.5):

\[
\varepsilon_{pq}(I)
=\frac1{pq}\sum_{t\ne0}
\widehat{1_I}^{(pq)}(t)
S_p(tq^{-1})S_q(tp^{-1}).
\]

The exact separated form is (1.9), with the core bilinear pairing (1.8). The pinned row is (2.4), and its core squared norm is the three-prime form (2.8).

The bilinear form that appears at first level is the near-Farey kernel

\[
\frac1{pq}\sum_{h,k}
S_p(h)\overline{S_q(k)}
D_I(h/p-k/q).
\]

PRD is the pinned \(TT^*\) form obtained by squaring this after summing over \(q\).

## 2. Is there an analogous horizontal large sieve?

There is an unconditional first-level inequality, (3.3), with constant \(N+P^2\). It is useful only up to the square-root denominator scale.

The appropriate PRD-level analogue is `(HLS-PRD)`. It is not currently proved. The short-arc theorem `(3.5)` in `oracleA_result.tex` is the closest first-level candidate already formulated in the repository.

## 3. What nontrivial input about \(S_p\) is needed?

Not a pointwise bound. Not merely Parseval. Not fixed-frequency vertical cancellation.

The needed input is cancellation in inverse-twisted, near-Farey products of different characteristics; for PRD, it must persist after a second \(L^2\) expansion with one shared prime. In formula form, it is the bound (2.8) at scale \(O(N)\).

## 4. Does equidistribution of \(r_p/p\) give a doublet horizontal sieve?

No. It gives only a marginal law for fixed-complexity tests. The exact doublet packet has the shifted form (6.1), and the exact CRT kernel is (6.2). PRD requires the square of the sum over \(q\), as in (6.3), with frequencies twisted by modular inverses and growing with the moduli.

A theorem strong enough to imply `(D-HLS)` could reasonably be called quantitative joint equidistribution, but it would already be a horizontal large-sieve theorem—not ordinary equidistribution.

## 5. Is there a BFI/Kuznetsov analogue?

There is an analytic analogy but no existing spectral input. The modular inverses and short kernels are BFI-shaped. The missing amplitude family \(S_p(h)\), however, has no known Kuznetsov formula or bounded-conductor trace-sheaf realization.

The plausible replacement is a new crystalline sum-of-products theorem followed by modern trace-function/dispersion techniques. Existing Kloosterman and trace-function theorems do not apply directly.

## 6. Minimal new ingredient

The minimal exact input is scalar PRD in the Fourier form (4.3). The clean analytic input is `(HLS-PRD)`. A Sato--Tate-type one-prime theorem is not enough; one needs a genuinely new multi-prime Fourier estimate.

# Final research recommendation

For the PRD route, the next theorem should be named and attacked in the following form:

> **Apéry horizontal pinned large sieve.** Uniformly for intervals \(I\) of length \(N\), and for all coefficients \(c_q\), the error matrix of the Apéry least-digit zero sets satisfies `(HLS-PRD)`.

The proof program should then be separated into two modules.

1. **Arithmetic-geometric module:** construct a bounded-complexity realization, or prove directly the required complete sum-of-products bounds for the Apéry coefficient-zero detector under all transforms generated by dispersion.
2. **Analytic module:** apply a BFI/Type-III/trace-function dispersion argument to convert those complete correlation bounds into `(HLS-PRD)`.

Marginal doublet equidistribution, Ahlgren--Ono modularity, and primewise Fourier norms should remain supporting evidence and local structure. None is the missing theorem by itself.
