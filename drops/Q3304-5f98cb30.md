# 3. The exact quotient theorem

## Theorem 3.1 — endpoint-times-complementary-difference bound

Define

\[
R=\gcd_{K=a}^{b-3}(\bar W_{K+1}-\bar W_K),
\tag{3.1}
\]

with the usual convention that the gcd is nonnegative. Define

\[
L=
\begin{cases}
1,&X\text{ odd},\\[2mm]
\operatorname{lcm}_{K=a}^{b-3}\lambda_K,&X\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Also define the stronger weighted carrier

\[
Q=\gcd_{K=a}^{b-3}
\begin{cases}
\bar W_{K+1}-\bar W_K,&X\text{ odd},\\[1mm]
\lambda_K(\bar W_{K+1}-\bar W_K),&X\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\eta\mid Q\mid LR.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

If `X` is odd, `(2.9)` and `(2.12)` give

\[
W_{K+1}-W_K
=-\frac{A_K+2}{2}(C^*_{K+1}-C^*_K).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Because `eta` divides every `bar C_K`, it divides every difference on the right. The coefficient `(A_K+2)/2` is integral. Hence

\[
\eta\mid \bar W_{K+1}-\bar W_K
\]

for every `K`, proving `eta|Q=R`.

Now suppose `X` is even. A direct expansion of `(2.11)-(2.12)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_K(W_{K+1}-W_K)
=(A_K+2)(C^*_{K+1}-C^*_K).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

After division by `G`, `eta` divides the right-hand side and therefore

\[
\eta\mid\lambda_K(\bar W_{K+1}-\bar W_K)
\tag{3.7}
\]

for every `K`. This proves `eta|Q`.

For `Q|LR`, fix a prime `ell` and write `r_K=bar W_(K+1)-bar W_K`. Then

\[
v_\ell(Q)=\min_K\bigl(v_\ell(\lambda_K)+v_\ell(r_K)\bigr)
\]

in the even case. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_\ell(Q)
&\le \max_Kv_\ell(\lambda_K)+\min_Kv_\ell(r_K)\\
&=v_\ell(L)+v_\ell(R).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `Q|LR`. The odd case is immediate. QED.

### Primewise interpretation

For every prime `ell`,

\[
\boxed{
v_\ell(\gamma/G)
\le v_\ell(L)+v_\ell(R).
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus if `ell` does not divide the affine endpoint lcm `L`, every extra `ell`-adic copy must occur in the complementary-difference gcd `R`.

This is already stronger than the abstract Q3284 index formula: it uses the coherent q=6 chain, not merely the relation-lattice ranks.

# 8. What is proved, what is finite evidence, and what remains

1. `G_s | gamma_s` for the coherent `C*` chain.
2. The exact divisor
   \[
   \gamma_s/G_s\mid Q_s\mid L_sR_s.

4. Every extra prime is supported on an affine endpoint coefficient or the short actual Apéry Casoratian gcd.
5. Non-target q=6 core primes are excluded by `(7.3)`.

## Exact finite certificate for the supplied row

The equalities `(6.1)-(6.16)` are exact integer computations from the Apéry recurrence. They explain the observed `5`; they are not used to infer an asymptotic theorem.

## Not proved

1. That all actual extra primes belong to a fixed finite bounded set.
2. That `gamma_s/G_s` divides one fixed-degree polynomial in `(m,H)`.
3. That the short-Casoratian gcd `R_s` has `exp(o(m))` height.
4. That the Ore gcd in Section 5 is `1`, or that its cleared scalar `rho_s` has the needed height, without running the unavailable exact local operator certificate.
5. That the appearances seen in finite scans exhaust the actual q=6 behavior.

The quantitatively precise remaining estimate is

\boxed{
\log^+\left|
\frac{
\gcd_K(U_{K+2}J_{K+1}-U_{K+1}J_K)
}{\epsilon_sG_s}

\tag{8.1}

plus control of the endpoint-lcm contribution when one asks about all primes rather than only the q=6 slope-4 core. An Ore certificate `(5.7)` is a concrete route to `(8.1)`; a nontrivial Ore gcd would be the exact obstruction.
