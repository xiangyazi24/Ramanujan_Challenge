# P3.2 mirror-saturated first lift: exact mod-p residual

Source audit was against `main` at `47fa0e653f52c4a71e9a8c26b31ca9f66f6bbe86`. The two source facts used below are the normalized Apéry recurrence/reflection (`scripts/apery_zeros.py`, `problems/3.2/UNDERSTANDING.md`) and the exact moving-gap Green/Casoratian formula in `problems/3.2/gap_kernel_green.tex`.

## Result

Let \(p\) be an odd prime and let

\[
0\le s<\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad p\mid b_s,
\]

be the folded residue of an actual fixed-\(q\) target. Put

\[
t=p-1-s,\qquad d=t-s=p-1-2s.
\]

Thus \(d\ge2\) is even. (The exceptional centered residue \(s=(p-1)/2\) has \(d=0\), hence no nontrivial mirror gap \(N_d\); it is a separate case.)

Define the \(p\)-integral transverse derivative

\[
\boxed{
J_s
:=2\sum_{k=0}^s
\binom{s}{k}^2\binom{s+k}{k}^2
\bigl(H_{s+k}-H_{s-k}\bigr)
}
\]

with \(H_0=0\). Equivalently, for

\[
\mathcal B_s(X)=\sum_{k=0}^s\binom{X}{k}^2\binom{X+k}{k}^2,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{J_s=\mathcal B_s'(s).}
\]

Let \(c_0=0,c_1=1\) be the standard companion solution of

\[
(n+1)^3y_{n+1}=P(n)y_n-n^3y_{n-1},
\qquad
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]

Finally put

\[
\Pi_{s,d}:=\prod_{j=1}^d(s+j)^3
=\left(\frac{t!}{s!}\right)^3.
\]

The exact first-lift formula is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N_d(s)}p
\equiv
\Pi_{s,d}\,c_s\,J_s
\pmod p.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Both \(\Pi_{s,d}\) and \(c_s\) are units modulo \(p\) at a target. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
p^2\mid N_d(s)
\iff
J_s\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{2}
\]

So the extra \(p\)-adic lift is **not forced** by targethood. More importantly, the private quotient \(b_s/p\) cancels from the mirror-saturated first lift. The exact residual is the transverse derivative \(J_s\), up to the explicit unit \(\Pi_{s,d}c_s\).

This is genuinely target-selective and reusable across primes: after the target condition \(p\mid b_s\) has selected the prime, mirror saturation upgrades to \(p^2\mid N_d(s)\) exactly when the same prime also annihilates the fixed characteristic-zero rational \(J_s=\mathcal B_s'(s)\) (whose denominator is a \(p\)-unit in this range).

## 1. Exact moving-gap identity and denominator check

The repository proves, for \(G\ge1\),

\[
\frac{N_G(u)}{\prod_{j=1}^G(u+j)^3}
=b_uc_{u+G}-c_ub_{u+G}.
\tag{3}
\]

Taking \(u=s,G=d\), so \(u+G=t=p-1-s\), gives

\[
N_d(s)=\Pi_{s,d}(b_sc_t-c_sb_t).
\tag{4}
\]

Every factor \(s+1,\dots,t\) lies in \(\{1,\dots,p-1\}\), so

\[
p\nmid\Pi_{s,d}.
\tag{5}
\]

The companion values \(c_j\), \(j<p\), are \(p\)-integral: the recurrence constructs them using only division by \(1^3,2^3,\dots,j^3\), all \(p\)-units. Hence (4) may be reduced modulo \(p\) or \(p^2\) in \(\mathbf Z_{(p)}\) without any hidden denominator problem.

The banked reflection law

\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p
\tag{6}
\]

therefore gives \(p\mid b_t\) whenever \(p\mid b_s\). Equation (4) then shows \(p\mid N_d(s)\), so

\[
\widehat N_d(s):=N_d(s)/p\in\mathbf Z
\]

is well defined.

## 2. The companion has the same reflection parity

A point that matters for the first lift is the sign of the reflected companion. It is **even**:

\[
\boxed{c_{p-1-r}\equiv c_r\pmod p\qquad(0\le r<p).}
\tag{7}
\]

Here is a recurrence/Casoratian proof.

The polynomial satisfies the exact symmetry

\[
P(-1-X)=-P(X).
\tag{8}
\]

Over \(\mathbf F_p\), let \(R\) act on recurrence solutions by

\[
(Ry)_r=y_{p-1-r}.
\]

Using the recurrence at index \(p-1-r\), (8), and

\[
(p-r)^3\equiv-r^3,\qquad (p-1-r)^3\equiv-(r+1)^3\pmod p,
\]

one obtains exactly the recurrence at index \(r\) for \(Ry\). Thus \(R\) is a linear involution on the two-dimensional solution space. The existing reflection theorem says \(Rb=b\).

Write

\[
Rc=\alpha b+\beta c.
\]

The companion Casoratian from the source is

\[
W_n(b,c):=b_{n-1}c_n-c_{n-1}b_n=\frac1{n^3}.
\tag{9}
\]

For \(1\le n\le p-1\), using (6),

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_n(b,Rc)
&=b_{n-1}c_{p-1-n}-c_{p-n}b_n\\
&=b_{p-n}c_{p-1-n}-c_{p-n}b_{p-1-n}\\
&=-W_{p-n}(b,c)\\
&=-\frac1{(p-n)^3}
\equiv\frac1{n^3}
=W_n(b,c)\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence \(eta=1\). Since \(R^2=1\),

\[
c=R^2c=R(\alpha b+c)=2\alpha b+c.
\]

Because \(p\) is odd and \(b\ne0\), \(\alpha=0\). This proves (7).

In particular, at the mirror endpoints,

\[
\boxed{c_t\equiv c_s\pmod p.}
\tag{10}
\]

## 3. Reflection to precision \(p^2\)

Now compute \(b_t=b_{p-1-s}\) one order deeper from the exact Apéry binomial formula

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n
\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\tag{11}
\]

### Terms with \(k>s\)

If \(s<k\le t\), then

\[
\binom{t+k}{k}=\binom{p-1-s+k}{k}
\]

has exactly one factor \(p\) in its numerator and none in \(k!\): indeed \(k<p\), and the numerator interval crosses \(p\) exactly once and remains below \(2p\). Therefore

\[
p\mid\binom{t+k}{k},
\]

so its square makes the whole \(k\)-term in (11) vanish modulo \(p^2\).

Thus only \(0\le k\le s\) survive modulo \(p^2\).

### Terms with \(0\le k\le s\)

All integers appearing below lie in \(\{1,\dots,p-1\}\), so every reciprocal is legitimate in \(\mathbf Z_{(p)}\). We have

\[
\begin{aligned}
\binom{p-1-s}{k}
&=(-1)^k\binom{s+k}{k}
\prod_{j=1}^k\left(1-\frac p{s+j}\right)\\
&\equiv
(-1)^k\binom{s+k}{k}
\left(1-p\sum_{j=1}^k\frac1{s+j}\right)
\pmod{p^2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\binom{p-1-s+k}{k}
&=(-1)^k\binom{s}{k}
\prod_{j=1}^k\left(1-\frac p{s+1-j}\right)\\
&\equiv
(-1)^k\binom{s}{k}
\left(1-p\sum_{j=1}^k\frac1{s+1-j}\right)
\pmod{p^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

Squaring and multiplying gives, with

\[
T_{s,k}=\binom{s}{k}^2\binom{s+k}{k}^2,
\]

\[
\binom{t}{k}^2\binom{t+k}{k}^2
\equiv
T_{s,k}\left[
1-2p\bigl(H_{s+k}-H_{s-k}\bigr)
\right]
\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{14}
\]

Summing (14), and using the vanishing of all \(k>s\) terms, yields

\[
\boxed{
b_t\equiv b_s-pJ_s\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{15}
\]

This also proves that \(J_s\) is exactly the transverse first-order defect of reflection.

To identify it as a derivative, differentiate a summand of \(\mathcal B_s(X)\). At \(X=s\),

\[
\frac d{dX}
\log\left(
\binom Xk^2\binom{X+k}{k}^2
\right)
=2(H_{s+k}-H_{s-k}),
\]

so indeed

\[
\boxed{J_s=\mathcal B_s'(s).}
\tag{16}
\]

The denominator of \(J_s\) divides a product of integers at most \(2s\le p-1\), hence is prime to \(p\). Thus \(J_s\bmod p\) is unambiguous.

## 4. Cancellation of the private quotient

Write

\[
b_s=p\beta,\qquad b_t=p\beta_t
\]

with \(eta,eta_t\in\mathbf Z\). Equation (15) gives

\[
\boxed{\beta_t\equiv\beta-J_s\pmod p.}
\tag{17}
\]

Divide (4) by \(p\):

\[
\widehat N_d(s)
=\Pi_{s,d}(\beta c_t-c_s\beta_t).
\tag{18}
\]

Now use (10) and (17):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat N_d(s)
&\equiv
\Pi_{s,d}
\left(\beta c_s-c_s(\beta-J_s)\right)\\
&=\Pi_{s,d}c_sJ_s
\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is (1). The private quantity \(eta=b_s/p\) has disappeared exactly.

## 5. The remaining factors are units at every target

We already have \(p\nmid\Pi_{s,d}\). It remains to check \(c_s\).

Since \(s\ne0\) at a target (\(b_0=1\)), apply (9) at \(n=s\):

\[
b_{s-1}c_s-c_{s-1}b_s=\frac1{s^3}.
\]

Modulo \(p\), targethood \(b_s\equiv0\) gives

\[
b_{s-1}c_s\equiv s^{-3}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{c_s\in\mathbf F_p^\times.}
\tag{19}
\]

Combining (1), (5), and (19) proves the exact equivalence (2):

\[
p^2\mid N_d(s)
\iff p\mid J_s.
\]

There is therefore no automatic mirror \(p^2\)-lift.

## 6. Exact Apéry counterexamples

These are not finite evidence for a theorem; they are exact counterexamples to the proposed universal \(p^2\)-lift, evaluated from formula (1).

### Counterexample 1: \(p=73,\ s=2\)

The exact Apéry value is

\[
b_2=73,
\]

so \(p=73\) is a genuine target at \(s=2\). Here

\[
d=72-4=68,\qquad t=70.
\]

The derivative is

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_2
&=2\left[
36\left(H_3-H_1\right)
+36\left(H_4-H_0\right)
\right]\\
&=2(30+75)=210
\equiv64\pmod{73}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The companion recurrence gives

\[
c_2=\frac{117}{8}\equiv42\pmod{73}.
\]

Wilson gives

\[
\frac{70!}{2!}\equiv18\pmod{73},
\qquad
\Pi_{2,68}\equiv18^3\equiv65\pmod{73}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N_{68}(2)}{73}
\equiv65\cdot42\cdot64
\equiv31\not\equiv0\pmod{73}.
}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{v_{73}(N_{68}(2))=1.}
\]

### Counterexample 2: \(p=17,\ s=3\)

Here

\[
b_3=1445=5\cdot17^2,
\]

so the endpoint itself already has a **double** \(17\)-adic zero. Nevertheless the mirror bridge does not lift twice. We have

\[
d=16-6=10,\qquad t=13.
\]

Compute

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_3
&=2\left[
144\left(H_4-H_2\right)
+900\left(H_5-H_1\right)
+400H_6
\right]\\
&=2(84+1155+980)\\
&=4438
\equiv1\pmod{17}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The companion recurrence gives

\[
c_3\equiv16\pmod{17}.
\]

Also

\[
\frac{13!}{3!}\equiv9\pmod{17},
\qquad
\Pi_{3,10}\equiv9^3\equiv15\pmod{17}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N_{10}(3)}{17}
\equiv15\cdot16\cdot1
\equiv2\not\equiv0\pmod{17}.
}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{v_{17}(N_{10}(3))=1,}
\]

even though \(v_{17}(b_3)=2\). This cleanly rules out any explanation in which the mirror first lift is forced by the endpoint quotient \(b_s/p\) or by extra divisibility of \(b_s\).

## 7. What the mirror-saturated condition really is

The local mirror mechanism is now exact:

1. Targethood \(p\mid b_s\) plus ordinary reflection forces the opposite endpoint \(p\mid b_{p-1-s}\).
2. Hence the mirror gap continuant has one forced factor \(p\).
3. At precision \(p^2\), reflected endpoint values satisfy

   \[
   \frac{b_{p-1-s}}p
   \equiv
   \frac{b_s}p-J_s
   \pmod p.
   \]

4. The companion is reflection-even, \(c_{p-1-s}\equiv c_s\), so the two copies of \(b_s/p\) cancel in the Green determinant.
5. The residual is exactly the unit multiple

   \[
   \boxed{
   \widehat N_d(s)
   \equiv
   \left(\frac{(p-1-s)!}{s!}\right)^3
   c_s\,\mathcal B_s'(s)
   \pmod p.
   }
   \]

Therefore the genuinely target-selective **mirror-saturated first-lift condition** is

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_s
\quad\text{and}\quad
p\mid\mathcal B_s'(s).}
\tag{20}
\]

The second condition is transverse to the zero condition rather than a restatement of it. It is independent of the private quotient \(b_s/p\), and all normalizing denominators are \(p\)-units in the folded range.

For the fixed-\(q\) program this is the useful output: every folded target prime supplies one forced mirror factor, while a second factor occurs exactly at a simultaneous zero of the Apéry value and this explicit derivative. Any attempt to exploit mirror saturation across primes should therefore study the common-prime support of

\[
b_s\quad\text{and}\quad J_s=\mathcal B_s'(s),
\]

not the quotient \(b_s/p\). The two exact counterexamples above show that no unconditional \(p^2\) lift can be banked.
