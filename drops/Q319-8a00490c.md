# Claim III: complete unconditional proof

## Verdict

Claim III has a short proof from the **continuant determinant**, with no irreducibility hypothesis and no root-location conjecture for the core factors.

In fact one gets the stronger exact classification

\[
\boxed{\quad
\widetilde W_k\!\left(-n-\frac12\right)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k=2n,
\qquad k\ge 2,\ n\ge 1.
\quad}
\]

Taking \(j=2n\) gives immediately

\[
\widetilde W_k\!\left(-\frac{j+1}{2}\right)\ne0
\qquad(k\ne j).
\]

Thus this proves not only the even-even case but the whole of Claim III, including the odd-\(k\), even-\(j\) case, without using irreducibility of \(f_k\) or \(h_k\).

There are two important corrections to the proposed route:

1. When \(j\) and \(k=2m\) are both even,
   \[
   c=\frac{(j-k)^2}{4}=\left(\frac{j-k}{2}\right)^2
   \]
   is a **positive integer square**, not an arbitrary positive rational.
2. The statement that the core polynomials have no positive real roots is false. In fact \(h_{2m}\) has positive real roots for every \(m\ge2\); those roots lie between the positive integer squares, so they do not contradict Claim III.

## 1. Normalize to the repository polynomial

Let \(N_k=-\widetilde W_k\). Then

\[
N_0=0,\qquad N_1=1,
\]

and

\[
N_{k+1}(x)=P(x+k)N_k(x)-(x+k)^6N_{k-1}(x),
\]

where

\[
P(t)=(2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5)
    =34t^3+51t^2+27t+5.
\]

As in `meso_result.tex`, let \(D_k(x)\) be the \((k-1)\times(k-1)\) symmetric tridiagonal matrix

\[
(D_k(x))_{ii}=P(x+i),
\qquad
(D_k(x))_{i,i+1}=(x+i+1)^3.
\]

Expansion along the last row gives

\[
\det D_k(x)=N_k(x).
\]

Hence it suffices to classify when \(D_k(-n-\tfrac12)\) is singular.

## 2. Scale the half-integer matrix

Fix integers \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge1\), and put

\[
H_{k,n}=8D_k\!\left(-n-\frac12\right).
\]

For row \(i\), set

\[
z_i=i-n.
\]

Then the diagonal entry in that row is

\[
8P\!\left(z_i-\frac12\right)
 =272z_i^3+12z_i=:a_{z_i},
\]

and the entry joining the rows with labels \(z\) and \(z+1\) is

\[
8\left(z+\frac12\right)^3=(2z+1)^3=:b_z.
\]

Thus the row labels are the consecutive integers

\[
1-n,\ 2-n,\ldots,\ k-1-n.
\]

Also

\[
\det H_{k,n}=8^{k-1}N_k\!\left(-n-\frac12\right),
\]

so scaling does not affect singularity.

## 3. The positive half-blocks

For \(s\ge1\), let \(B_s\) be the \(s\times s\) tridiagonal matrix indexed by \(1,\ldots,s\), with

\[
(B_s)_{tt}=a_t=272t^3+12t,
\qquad
(B_s)_{t,t+1}=b_t=(2t+1)^3.
\]

Let \(B_0\) be the empty matrix, with determinant \(1\).

Each \(B_s\) is positive definite. Indeed, in an interior row \(t\ge1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_t-b_{t-1}-b_t
&=272t^3+12t-(2t-1)^3-(2t+1)^3\\
&=256t^3>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

At an endpoint one of the off-diagonal terms is absent, so the inequality is stronger. Hence \(B_s\) is symmetric, strictly diagonally dominant, and has positive diagonal; therefore it is positive definite.

Define

\[
g_0=0,
\qquad
g_s=(B_s^{-1})_{11}\quad(s\ge1).
\]

### Lemma: \(g_s\) is strictly increasing

Write

\[
B_{s+1}=
\begin{pmatrix}
B_s&b_se_s\\
b_se_s^{T}&a_{s+1}
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where \(e_s\) is the last standard basis vector. Since \(B_{s+1}\) is positive definite, its Schur complement

\[
\sigma_s=a_{s+1}-b_s^2(B_s^{-1})_{ss}
\]

is positive. The block inverse formula gives

\[
g_{s+1}-g_s
 =\sigma_s^{-1}b_s^2
   \bigl((B_s^{-1})_{1s}\bigr)^2.
\]

The corner entry is nonzero; explicitly,

\[
(B_s^{-1})_{1s}
 =(-1)^{s-1}\frac{b_1b_2\cdots b_{s-1}}{\det B_s}\ne0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{g_0<g_1<g_2<\cdots.}
\]

## 4. Half-integer zero classification

We now prove

\[
N_k\!\left(-n-\frac12\right)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k=2n.
\]

### Case 1: \(n\ge k\)

All row labels satisfy \(z_i\le-1\). The same diagonal-dominance computation, after multiplying by \(-1\), shows that \(H_{k,n}\) is negative definite. In particular it is nonsingular.

### Case 2: \(1\le n\le k-1\)

There is exactly one row with label \(z=0\), namely row \(i=n\). Put

\[
L=n-1,
\qquad
R=k-1-n.
\]

The labels to the left of the zero row are

\[
-L,-L+1,\ldots,-1,
\]

and the labels to its right are

\[
1,2,\ldots,R.
\]

Reverse the order of the left block. Since

\[
a_{-t}=-a_t,
\qquad
b_{-t-1}=-b_t,
\]

the left block becomes \(-B_L\), while the right block is \(B_R\). The two entries adjacent to the central row are \(b_{-1}=-1\) and \(b_0=1\). Hence, after a simultaneous row-and-column permutation,

\[
H_{k,n}\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
-B_L&-e_1&0\\
-e_1^T&0&e_1^T\\
0&e_1&B_R
\end{pmatrix},
\]

with empty blocks omitted when \(L=0\) or \(R=0\).

Take the Schur complement of

\[
\operatorname{diag}(-B_L,B_R).
\]

It is

\[
0-
\begin{pmatrix}-e_1^T&e_1^T\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}-B_L^{-1}&0\\0&B_R^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}-e_1\\e_1\end{pmatrix}
=g_L-g_R.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\det H_{k,n}
 =\det(-B_L)\det(B_R)(g_L-g_R).
}
\]

The two block determinants are nonzero. Since \(g_s\) is strictly increasing,

\[
\det H_{k,n}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L=R.
\]

Finally,

\[
L=R
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n-1=k-1-n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k=2n.
\]

This proves the classification theorem.

## 5. Deduction of Claim III

Let \(j\ge2\) be even and write \(j=2n\). Then

\[
-\frac{j+1}{2}=-n-\frac12.
\]

The theorem gives

\[
\widetilde W_k\!\left(-\frac{j+1}{2}\right)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k=j.
\]

Therefore, for every \(k\ge2\) with \(k\ne j\),

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde W_k\!\left(-\frac{j+1}{2}\right)\ne0.
}
\]

This is exactly Claim III.

### Translation to the even core \(h_{2m}\)

Let \(k=2m\), \(j=2n\), and

\[
q=m-n=\frac{k-j}{2}.
\]

Then

\[
-\frac{j+1}{2}
=-\frac{2m+1}{2}+q.
\]

Since

\[
\widetilde W_{2m}(r)
 =\bigl(2r+2m+1\bigr)
   h_{2m}\!\left(\left(r+\frac{2m+1}{2}\right)^2\right),
\]

we obtain at this point

\[
\widetilde W_{2m}\!\left(-\frac{j+1}{2}\right)
 =2q\,h_{2m}(q^2).
\]

If \(j\ne2m\), then \(q\ne0\), and Claim III yields

\[
\boxed{h_{2m}(q^2)\ne0.}
\]

Thus the required evaluation points are precisely the nonzero integer squares.

## 6. The central cofactor is nonzero

For completeness, the same matrix argument also proves

\[
h_{2m}(0)\ne0.
\]

At the center \(n=m\), one has \(L=R=m-1\). The Schur complement is zero, but the two side blocks are invertible, so \(H_{2m,m}\) has nullity exactly one.

Let

\[
H_m(y)=8D_{2m}\!\left(y-m-\frac12\right).
\]

At \(y=0\), its derivative has diagonal entries

\[
816z^2+12
\]

and off-diagonal entries

\[
6(2z+1)^2.
\]

For an interior row labelled \(z\),

\[
816z^2+12
-6(2z-1)^2-6(2z+1)^2
=768z^2\ge0,
\]

with equality only at \(z=0\). The derivative matrix is connected, has positive diagonal, is diagonally dominant, and has strict inequality in every noncentral row; hence it is positive definite. Therefore the unique zero eigenvalue of \(H_m(0)\) crosses zero with nonzero derivative. The determinant has a simple zero at \(y=0\).

Since

\[
\widetilde W_{2m}\!\left(y-\frac{2m+1}{2}\right)
 =2y\,h_{2m}(y^2),
\]

a simple zero at \(y=0\) is equivalent to \(h_{2m}(0)\ne0\).

This recovers the exact multiplicity-one central factor without irreducibility.

## 7. Why “no positive real roots” cannot be the proof

The determinant formula also gives the exact sign, when \(1\le n\le k-1\):

\[
\operatorname{sgn}
 N_k\!\left(-n-\frac12\right)
 =(-1)^{n-1}\operatorname{sgn}(2n-k).
\]

Indeed, \(\det B_L,\det B_R>0\), while

\[
\operatorname{sgn}(g_L-g_R)=\operatorname{sgn}(L-R)
 =\operatorname{sgn}(2n-k).
\]

Now take \(k=2m\) and use the centered coordinate

\[
F_m(y)=N_{2m}\!\left(y-m-\frac12\right).
\]

For \(q=1,\ldots,m-1\), set \(n=m-q\). Then

\[
\operatorname{sgn}F_m(q)=(-1)^{m-q}.
\]

At \(q=m\), all row labels are positive, so \(F_m(m)>0\), which is the same sign formula with exponent \(0\). Thus the signs at

\[
1,2,\ldots,m
\]

alternate. By the intermediate value theorem, \(F_m\) has a positive real zero in every interval

\[
(q,q+1),\qquad q=1,\ldots,m-1.
\]

Since \(F_m(y)\) is \(y\) times the even core (up to the harmless global factor and sign), \(h_{2m}\) has a positive real zero in every interval

\[
\boxed{(q^2,(q+1)^2),\qquad q=1,\ldots,m-1.}
\]

So \(h_{2m}\) does have positive real roots; the relevant fact is that none occurs **at an integer square**.

The analogous “no positive roots” assertion for the odd cores is also false. Already for \(k=3\), in the centered variable \(u=y^2\), one has, up to the normalization sign,

\[
1155u^3-765u^2+219u-25,
\]

whose value is \(-25\) at \(u=0\) and \(584\) at \(u=1\). It therefore has a positive real root in \((0,1)\).

Irreducibility would exclude a **rational** evaluation point from being a root, but it does not exclude positive irrational real roots. The determinant proof above is stronger because it proves exactly the rational-square nonvanishing needed here for every \(k\).

## 8. Recommended manuscript lemma

The clean statement to record is:

> **Half-integer zero lemma.** For every \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge1\),
> \[
> N_k(-n-1/2)=0\iff k=2n.
> \]
> Equivalently, for every even \(j\ge2\), the only gap polynomial vanishing at \(-(j+1)/2\) is \(N_j\).

This lemma subsumes both parity cases of Claim III and removes all dependence on the unproved all-level irreducibility conjecture.
