# P3.2 direct integer anti-star attack

## Verdict

I do **not** obtain a genuinely subexponential characteristic-zero carrier from the direct neighboring-value / Wronskian / linear-Bézout lane.

I do obtain a sharp theorem-level obstruction for what is, in this lane, the maximally stripped immediate-neighbor carrier.  Put

\[
D_n^{\rm nbr}:=\gcd\!\bigl(b_n,b_{n-1}b_{n+1}\bigr),
\qquad
C_n^{\rm nbr}:=\frac{b_n}{D_n^{\rm nbr}}\in\mathbf Z_{>0}.
\tag{1}
\]

For fixed `q`, every sufficiently large target prime in the quotient cell divides `b_n` and divides neither adjacent Apéry value.  Hence

\[
\boxed{R_q(n)\mid C_n^{\rm nbr}.}
\tag{2}
\]

So `(1)` is an honest, explicit, characteristic-zero anti-star carrier: it removes from `b_n` every prime-power contribution that can be removed merely because it is visible in one of the two immediate neighbors, while retaining every aligned target prime.

The obstruction is that this carrier is still exponentially large.  In fact

\[
\boxed{
\log C_n^{\rm nbr}
\ge (\log 27-3)n-o(n)
=0.295836\ldots\,n-o(n).
}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus even the two-sided immediate-neighbor stripping cannot yield `e^{o(n)}`.

The proof uses only the exact Apéry Wronskian, Lucas, no-consecutive-zeros, and a single positive summand of the Apéry binomial formula.  No finite scan enters.

There is a second exact no-go.  In the generic rank-two recurrence module, every **linear** characteristic-zero combination of `b_(n-1),b_n,b_(n+1)` whose vanishing on the locus `b_n=0` is forced by the recurrence is already a multiple of `b_n`.  Explicitly, for polynomial coefficients `A,B,C`,

\[
A(n)b_{n-1}+B(n)b_n+C(n)b_{n+1}
\]

vanishes formally whenever `b_n=0` iff

\[
A(X)=X^3H(X),\qquad C(X)=(X+1)^3H(X),
\]

and then the whole expression is

\[
\boxed{(B(X)+P(X)H(X))\,b_n.}
\tag{4}
\]

The same statement holds for any fixed-width linear window after expressing it in the two-dimensional recurrence state.  Therefore a smaller linear Bézout/Green-style carrier cannot be obtained from the characteristic-zero recurrence alone.  If a nonproportional linear form vanishes for all fixed-`q` targets, the missing divisibility must come from a **new q-specific arithmetic statement**, not from recurrence algebra.

The strongest concrete nonlinear survivor remains the repaired two-normalized-energy numerator from Q2995.  If

\[
F_n=F(n,\kappa_{n-1},\kappa_{n-2})=\frac{U_n}{V_n}
\]

is reduced, the previous split repair gives

\[
\boxed{
R_q(n)\mid \operatorname{rad}(5b_q)\,P(n-1)\,U_n,
}
\tag{5}
\]

with all gap-two targets absorbed by `P(n-1)` and the fixed exceptional primes absorbed by `rad(5b_q)`.  Moreover `F_n>0` and has polynomial ordinary size.  Thus the smallest surviving direct-integer premise in this lane is simply

\[
\boxed{\log U_n=o(n),}
\tag{6}
\]

and a sufficient projective formulation is

\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{den}F(n,\kappa_{n-1},\kappa_{n-2})=o(n).
}
\tag{7}
\]

No current source proves `(6)` or `(7)`.  The exact factorization

\[
F_n=(n-1)^6(x-x_0)
\Bigl((x+x_0)((n-2)\kappa_{n-2}-3A(n))+3(n-1)^2\Bigr),
\]

with

\[
x=\frac{b_{n-2}}{b_{n-1}},
\qquad
x_0=\frac{P(n-1)}{(n-1)^3},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
x-x_0=-\frac{n^3b_n}{(n-1)^3b_{n-1}}}
\tag{8}
\]

shows why.  The small ordinary value of `F_n` is not a free small integer: it contains the exponentially large `b_n` factor behind rational cancellation.  The one-neighbor version of the argument below even gives

\[
\log\frac{b_n}{\gcd(b_n,b_{n-1})}
\ge (\log27-3/2)n-o(n),
\tag{9}
\]

so any subexponential reduced numerator in `(5)` must use cancellation deeper than the immediate neighbor.  That is exactly the unresolved projective-energy denominator cancellation, not something supplied by the ordinary recurrence or Wronskian.

So the direct result is:


# 1. Fixed-`q` target primes really are removed from neither neighbor

For definiteness put

\[
\mathcal T_q(n)
=
\left\{p\text{ prime}:\frac{n}{q+1}<p\le\frac nq,
\quad p\mid b_{n-qp}\right\},
\qquad
R_q(n)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal T_q(n)}p.
\tag{10}
\]

Write

\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

A target has `p|b_r`.  The endpoints `r=0` and `r=p-1` cannot be targets because

\[
b_0=1,
\qquad
b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p.
\]

For fixed `q`, the finite set of primes dividing `b_q` is harmless.  Once `n` is large enough that a target prime is not one of those fixed primes, Lucas gives

\[
b_n\equiv b_qb_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{11}
\]

For the lower neighbor,

\[
b_{n-1}\equiv b_qb_{r-1}\pmod p
\]

when `r>1`, with `b_0=1` at `r=1`.  No-consecutive-zeros gives `p\nmid b_{r-1}`.  Thus

\[
p\nmid b_{n-1}.
\tag{12}
\]

Similarly, for `r\le p-2`, Lucas and no-consecutive-zeros give

\[
p\nmid b_{n+1};
\tag{13}
\]

at the last possible digit `r=p-2`, the upper neighbor has low digit `p-1` and hence value `1 mod p`.

Therefore every sufficiently large fixed-`q` target satisfies

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_n,\qquad p\nmid b_{n-1}b_{n+1}.}
\tag{14}
\]

The finitely many primes dividing `b_q` can always be multiplied into a fixed `q`-dependent factor and do not affect `o(n)` questions.

Equation `(14)` immediately proves `(2)` for the integer `(1)`.

# 3. A common adjacent prime cannot lie above the half-way point

We need one additional exact support fact.

## Lemma 3.1

For `m` sufficiently large and every prime `ell>=7` with

\[
ell>m/2,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{\ell\nmid\gcd(b_m,b_{m-1}).}
\tag{17}
\]

### Proof

If `ell>m`, then `m<ell`, so `b_(m-1)` and `b_m` are two consecutive entries in the first characteristic block.  The no-consecutive-zero lemma excludes simultaneous vanishing modulo `ell`.

Now assume

\[
m/2<\ell\le m.
\]

Then

\[
m=\ell+s,
\qquad 0\le s<\ell.
\]

If `s=0`, Lucas gives

\[
b_m=b_\ell\equiv b_1b_0=5\not\equiv0\pmod\ell
\]

for `ell>=7`.

If `s>=1`, Lucas gives

\[
b_m\equiv b_1b_s=5b_s,
\qquad
b_{m-1}\equiv5b_{s-1}\pmod\ell.
\]

If both vanished, `b_s` and `b_(s-1)` would be consecutive zeros in the first block, impossible.  This proves `(17)`.  QED.

Applying the same lemma with `m=n+1` controls `g_n^+`.

# 4. The maximally stripped immediate-neighbor carrier is still exponential

From `(15)` and Lemma 3.1, only primes `ell<=n/2` can contribute to `g_n^-` apart from finitely many fixed small primes.  Therefore

\[
\log g_n^-
\le
3\sum_{\ell\le n/2}
\left\lfloor\log_\ell n\right\rfloor\log\ell
+O(\log n).
\tag{18}
\]

Write `theta` and `psi` for the Chebyshev functions.  Since

\[
\sum_{\ell\le n/2}
\left\lfloor\log_\ell n\right\rfloor\log\ell
=
\theta(n/2)+\psi(n)-\theta(n),
\tag{19}
\]

(the terms of prime-power exponent at least two are all based at primes `<=sqrt n<=n/2`), the prime number theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
\log g_n^-\le\frac32n+o(n).
}
\tag{20}
\]

Similarly

\[
\boxed{
\log g_n^+\le\frac32n+o(n).
}
\tag{21}
\]

Now put

\[
D_n^{\rm nbr}=\gcd(b_n,b_{n-1}b_{n+1}).
\]

Primewise,

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_\ell(D_n^{\rm nbr})
&=\min\{v_\ell(b_n),v_\ell(b_{n-1})+v_\ell(b_{n+1})\}\\
&\le
\min\{v_\ell(b_n),v_\ell(b_{n-1})\}
+
\min\{v_\ell(b_n),v_\ell(b_{n+1})\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
D_n^{\rm nbr}\mid g_n^-g_n^+
\]

at the level of prime valuations, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log D_n^{\rm nbr}\le3n+o(n).
}
\tag{22}
\]

It remains to lower-bound `b_n` without using its full asymptotic.  Keep only the positive summand with

\[
k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor.
\]

Stirling gives, uniformly for this choice,

\[
2\log\binom nk
+2\log\binom{n+k}{k}
=n\log27+O(\log n).
\tag{23}
\]

For even `n=2m` this is especially transparent:

\[
\begin{aligned}
2\log\binom{2m}{m}
+2\log\binom{3m}{m}
&=4m\log2
 +2m(3\log3-2\log2)+O(\log m)\\
&=6m\log3+O(\log m)\\
&=n\log27+O(\log n).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\log b_n\ge n\log27-O(\log n).
}
\tag{24}
\]

Combining `(1)`, `(22)`, and `(24)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\log C_n^{\rm nbr}
\ge(\log27-3)n-o(n).
}
\tag{25}
\]

Since

\[
\log27-3=0.295836866\ldots>0,
\]

`C_n^nbr` is exponentially large.  This proves `(3)`.

The same proof with only `g_n^-` gives the stronger one-sided estimate

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{b_n}{\gcd(b_n,b_{n-1})}
\ge(\log27-3/2)n-o(n),
}
\tag{26}
\]

and similarly with `b_(n+1)`.

So neither choosing the better side nor stripping both sides reaches subexponential height.

# 5. Exact local no-go for linear recurrence/Bezout carriers

The preceding theorem addresses gcd-stripping.  There is also a formal obstruction to a different common attempt: choose a linear combination of the three neighboring values which vanishes modulo every target.

Work over `Q(X)` with a generic solution `u_j` of

\[
(X+1)^3u_{X+1}=P(X)u_X-X^3u_{X-1}.
\]

At one step regard

\[
Y=u_{X-1},\qquad Z=u_X
\]

as free state coordinates.  Then

\[
u_{X+1}
=\frac{P(X)Z-X^3Y}{(X+1)^3}.
\tag{27}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal L
=A(X)u_{X-1}+B(X)u_X+C(X)u_{X+1},
\qquad A,B,C\in\mathbf Q[X].
\tag{28}
\]

Substituting `(27)`,

\[
\mathcal L
=
\left(A-\frac{CX^3}{(X+1)^3}\right)Y
+
\left(B+\frac{CP(X)}{(X+1)^3}\right)Z.
\tag{29}
\]

Suppose the vanishing of `L` on the zero locus `u_X=0` is to follow **formally from the recurrence alone**, i.e. for arbitrary nonzero predecessor state `Y`.  Then the coefficient of `Y` in `(29)` must vanish identically:

\[
A(X)(X+1)^3=C(X)X^3.
\tag{30}
\]

Since `X^3` and `(X+1)^3` are coprime in `Q[X]`, there is `H(X) in Q[X]` such that

\[
A(X)=X^3H(X),
\qquad
C(X)=(X+1)^3H(X).
\tag{31}
\]

Substitution back into `(28)` and use of the recurrence gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L=(B(X)+P(X)H(X))u_X.
}
\tag{32}
\]

This proves the claimed local linear no-go.

### Fixed wider windows

For any fixed `h`, every value

\[
u_{X-h},\dots,u_{X+h}
\]

is a `Q(X)`-linear combination of the two state coordinates `(Y,Z)` after inverting only the usual recurrence coefficients.  Therefore a fixed-width linear functional restricts on `Z=0` to one scalar multiple of `Y`.  If that restriction vanishes identically, the functional is proportional in the recurrence module to `Z=u_X`.

So the conclusion `(32)` is not special to width one.

### What this does and does not rule out

It rules out a recurrence-universal linear Bézout trick.  If `(30)` is **not** an identity, then for an actual target prime `p`, with `b_n=0` and `b_(n-1)` a unit, the congruence `L_n=0 mod p` forces

\[
\boxed{
p\mid (n+1)^3A(n)-n^3C(n).}
\tag{33}
\]

Thus a successful nonproportional linear form would already manufacture a new fixed-`n` integer on the right of `(33)` divisible by every target prime.  Proving that divisibility is precisely the new q-specific arithmetic input one is looking for; it is not supplied by the recurrence.

This is why ordinary neighboring Green rows, linear continuant identities, and linear Bézout combinations do not close the anti-star problem by themselves.

The theorem is deliberately scoped to **linear recurrence-module constructions**.  It does not rule out nonlinear gcds of many Jacobi minors, nonlinear normalized energies, resultants, or other genuinely new arithmetic invariants.

# 6. Wronskian and companion-value carriers stop at a linear bound

The Wronskian itself is

\[
W_n=\frac6{n^3}.
\]

After the standard denominator clearing it becomes the structural integer

\[
\frac{6L_n^3}{n^3},
\]

whose logarithm is

\[
3n+o(n).
\]

So the Wronskian gives excellent **valuation control** but not a subexponential target selector.

For example,

\[
\gcd(b_n,d_na_n)
\mid \frac{6d_n}{n^3}
\tag{34}
\]

by the same determinant identity.  This is an exact characteristic-zero gcd container, but `(34)` only supplies a linear-height upper bound.  Any attempt to turn the Casoratian into a smaller target carrier must add a second arithmetic selection mechanism beyond the Wronskian itself.

C_n^{\rm nbr}=\frac{b_n}{\gcd(b_n,b_{n-1}b_{n+1})},
