# 1. Exact normalization

Let

```text
Z_p={r in F_p^*: b_r=0 mod p},
z_p=|Z_p|,
F_p(a)=sum_(r in Z_p) e_p(a r).
```

The centered transform on `F_p^*` is

```text
G_p(a)=F_p(a)+z_p/(p-1),       a != 0,
G_p(0)=0.
```

Equivalently, put

```text
g_p(r)=1_(r in Z_p)-z_p/(p-1),  r != 0,
g_p(0)=0.
```

Then `G_p` is the unnormalized additive Fourier transform of `g_p`. Hence

```text
sum_(a in F_p^*) G_p(a)=0,                         (1.1)
```

and finite Parseval gives

```text
E_p
 :=sum_(a in F_p^*) |G_p(a)|^2
 =p z_p (1-z_p/(p-1))
 <=p z_p.                                          (1.2)
```

Write

```text
w_p=log p/log N.
```

On a dyadic block `p~N`, define the weighted zero mass

```text
T_N=sum_(p~N) w_p z_p.
```

Then

```text
sum_(p~N) w_p E_p << N T_N,                        (1.3)

sum_(p~N) w_p << N/log N.                          (1.4)
```

The high-frequency block is

```text
C_K
 =sum_(k~K, p!=ell)
    omega_(p,ell,k)
    G_p(k ell^(-1)) G_ell(k p^(-1)),               (1.5)

omega_(p,ell,k)

```

There is one Fourier-inversion factor only. For `p,ell~N`, the factor


# 2. Exact reciprocal decomposition

Fix distinct primes `p,ell`, put

```text
q=p ell,
```

and let

```text
bar_ell = ell^(-1) mod p,
bar_p   = p^(-1) mod ell.
```

The elementary additive reciprocity identity is

```text
bar_ell/p + bar_p/ell = 1/(p ell) mod 1.           (2.1)
```

For integer lifts `x` modulo `p` and `y` modulo `ell`, (2.1) gives

```text
e_p(k bar_ell x)e_ell(k bar_p y)
 =e_q(k y)e_p(k(x-y)bar_ell).                      (2.2)
```

Expanding the uncentered product,

```text
F_p(k bar_ell)F_ell(k bar_p)
 =sum_(x in Z_p) sum_(y in Z_ell)
    e_q(k y)e_p(k(x-y)bar_ell).                    (2.3)
```

This is the load-bearing identity.

## 2.1 Common-state diagonal

When the two lifted states agree,

```text
x=y,
```

the reciprocal phase disappears:

```text
e_q(k y)e_p(k(x-y)bar_ell)=e_q(kx).                (2.4)
```

There is no Kloosterman sum, no inverse map, and no `ell`-oscillation left.

## 2.2 Genuine reciprocal off-diagonal

When

```text
x!=y mod p,
```

the phase retains

```text
e_p(k(x-y)bar_ell),                                (2.5)
```

and completion in `ell^(-1) mod p` is possible.

Thus every proposed reciprocal estimate must first split

```text
C_K = C_K^diag + C_K^off + C_K^cent.              (2.6)
```

Here:

```text
C_K^diag:
  x=y zero states in (2.3);

C_K^off:
  x!=y zero states in (2.3);

C_K^cent:
  the three terms introduced by
  G_p=F_p+z_p/(p-1).
```

Applying completion directly to the unsplit expression hides the fact that its phase is singular on `x=y`.

# 3. Reflection produces a second common state, not extra oscillation

Suppose one physical row `R` is a zero modulo both primes:

```text
R in Z_p intersect Z_ell,
0<=R<min(p,ell).
```

Apéry reflection also gives

```text
p-1-R in Z_p,
ell-1-R in Z_ell.
```

Although those standard representatives differ, they are reductions of the same integer lift

```text
-R-1.
```

Consequently the two common states contribute

```text
e_q(kR),
e_q(-k(R+1)).                                    (3.1)
```

Both are diagonal in the sense of (2.4). Reflection therefore doubles the dangerous common-state packet; it does not turn it into a reciprocal phase.

The two cross orientations do contain inverses. For example,


```text
e_p(-k(N+1)bar_ell)e_ell(kN bar_p)
 =e_p(-k(2N+1)bar_ell)e_q(kN).                     (3.3)
```

Equations (3.2)-(3.3) are the pieces on which inverse completion genuinely acts.

# 4. The common-state section is rigorously positive

Take the standard interval transform

```text
K_N(theta)=sum_(N<n<=2N)e(n theta).                (4.1)
```

Consider one prime pair with

```text
q=p ell=alpha N^2+o(N^2),
K=L N+o(N),
L>1 fixed.
```

For the common direct/reflected states `N` and `-N-1`, define

```text
D_(q,K)(N)
 =sum_(K<k<=2K)
    [N^2/q]
    [K_N(k/q)/H_K]
    [e_q(kN)+e_q(-k(N+1))].                       (4.2)
```

Since `K>N`, one has

```text
H_K=N/L+o(N).
```

A two-dimensional Riemann-sum limit gives

```text
lim_(N to infinity) (1/N) Re D_(q,K)(N)
 =Phi(alpha,L),                                    (4.3)
```

where

```text
Phi(alpha,L)
 =(L/alpha) integral_(L to 2L)
   [ integral_(0 to 1) cos(2 pi u t/alpha) dt
    +integral_(2 to 3) cos(2 pi u t/alpha) dt ] du. (4.4)
```

The intervals have a direct physical meaning:

```text
reflected state:
  n-(N+1) lies in [0,N-1];

direct state:
  n+N lies in [2N+1,3N].
```

Choose

```text
L=m alpha
```

with `m` a positive integer. Changing variables `u=alpha v` gives

```text
Phi(alpha,m alpha)
 =m alpha [I_m(0,1)+I_m(2,3)],                    (4.5)

I_m(a,b)
 =integral_(m to 2m) integral_(a to b)
    cos(2 pi v t) dt dv.                          (4.6)
```

Put

```text
J_c(m)
 =integral_(m to 2m) sin(2 pi c v)/(2 pi v) dv.
```

Twice integrating by parts, using that `m` and `c` are integers, gives

```text
J_c(m)
 =1/(8 pi^2 c m)+O_c(m^(-3)).                     (4.7)
```

Therefore

```text
I_m(0,1)=J_1(m)
 =1/(8 pi^2 m)+O(m^(-3)),                         (4.8)
```

while

```text
I_m(2,3)=J_3(m)-J_2(m)
 =-1/(48 pi^2 m)+O(m^(-3)).                       (4.9)
```

Combining (4.5), (4.8), and (4.9),

```text
Phi(alpha,m alpha)
 =5 alpha/(48 pi^2)+O_alpha(m^(-2)).              (4.10)
```

Hence, for every sufficiently large fixed integer `m`,

```text
Phi(alpha,m alpha)>0.                              (4.11)
```

Continuity makes this positivity uniform when `p/N` and `ell/N` range in a sufficiently narrow fixed interval around `sqrt(alpha)`.

This proves a signed, not merely absolute, obstruction. A common direct/reflected row contributes a positive amount of order `N` per ordered prime pair on a legitimate block `K>N`.

If the convention `k~K` includes both signs, the negative-frequency half is the complex conjugate and doubles the real contribution.

# 5. A reflection-symmetric family saturating the Schur estimate

The following construction is an obstruction to the analytic mechanism. It is not asserted to be an Apéry row.

Fix

```text
beta=3/2
```

and choose a sufficiently small fixed `eta>0`. Let

```text
P_N={p prime: beta N<p<=(beta+eta)N},
M_N=|P_N|.
```

The prime number theorem gives

```text
M_N~eta N/log N.                                  (5.1)
```

For every `p in P_N`, define

```text
Z_p^model={N,p-1-N}.                               (5.2)
```

For all large `N`, these two residues are distinct, nonzero, nonadjacent, and avoid `p-1`. They are exactly interchanged by reflection.

The centered reciprocal transform is

```text
G_p^model(a)
 =e_p(aN)+e_p(-a(N+1))+2/(p-1),   a!=0,
G_p^model(0)=0.                                    (5.3)
```

It satisfies exactly

```text
sum_(a!=0) G_p^model(a)=0,                        (5.4)
```

and

```text
sum_(a!=0)|G_p^model(a)|^2
 =2p(1-2/(p-1)).                                   (5.5)
```

Thus the model satisfies all of the generic TCUS inputs. Its zero mass is

```text
T_N^model
 =sum_(p in P_N) w_p |Z_p^model|
 asymp 2M_N
 asymp N/log N,                                    (5.6)
```

which is far below the banked upper bound

```text
N^(8/5)/(log N)^(4/5).                             (5.7)
```

Choose a sufficiently large fixed integer `m`, put

```text
alpha_0=beta^2=9/4,
K=floor(m alpha_0 N).                              (5.8)
```

Then `K>N`.

## 5.1 Positive same-state main term

The two same-state orientations in (5.3) are exactly the states treated in Section 4. Taking `eta` small after fixing `m`, (4.10) gives a constant `c_m>0` such that every ordered pair `p!=ell` in `P_N` contributes

for all sufficiently large `N`.

Therefore

```text
Re C_K^same
 >=c_m N M_N(M_N-1).                              (5.10)
```

## 5.2 The two inverse cross orientations are lower order

For the direct/reflected orientation, equation (3.2) supplies the inverse phase

```text
e_p(k(2N+1)ell^(-1)).                              (5.11)
```

Fix `p`. The residues of the primes `ell in P_N`, `ell!=p`, are distinct modulo `p`, because the prime interval has length below `p`. Also `p` does not divide `2N+1` in this block.

For every prefix `L` of the ordered prime set, additive orthogonality gives

```text
sum_(k mod p)
 |sum_(ell in L)e_p(k(2N+1)ell^(-1))|^2
 =p |L|.                                           (5.12)
```

The interval `K<k<=2K` contains only `O_m(1)` complete `p`-periods. Hence

```text
sum_(K<k<=2K)
 |sum_(ell in L)e_p(k(2N+1)ell^(-1))|^2
 <<_m p |L|.                                       (5.13)
```

The remaining amplitude in (3.2), including

```text
N^2/(p ell),
logarithmic weights,
K_N(k/(p ell))/H_K,
e_(p ell)(-k(N+1)),
```

has uniformly bounded magnitude and bounded total variation as a function of `ell/N` on the fixed narrow interval. Abel summation, (5.13), and Cauchy-Schwarz therefore give, for each `p`,

```text
sum_(K<k<=2K)
 |sum_(ell in P_N,ell!=p)
   amplitude_(p,ell,k)
   e_p(k(2N+1)ell^(-1))|
 <<_m N sqrt(M_N).                                (5.14)
```

Summing over `p` gives

```text
C_K^cross
 <<_m N M_N^(3/2).                                (5.15)
```

The other cross orientation is identical. Since `M_N` tends to infinity,

```text
N M_N^(3/2)=o(N M_N^2).                           (5.16)
```

## 5.3 Centering corrections are negligible

Each centering constant is `2/(p-1)=O(1/N)`. The terms with one centering constant contribute at most

```text
O(M_N^2),                                          (5.17)
```

and the term with two constants is smaller. Thus

```text
C_K^cent=o(NM_N^2).                               (5.18)
```

Combining (5.10), (5.15), and (5.18),

```text
Re C_K^model
 >=c N M_N^2                                      (5.19)
```

for one fixed `c>0` and all sufficiently large `N`.

By (5.1),

```text

 >> N^3/log^2 N.                                  (5.20)
```

On the other hand, because `K~N` and `T_N^model~N/log N`, the accepted Schur bound is

```text
 asymp N^3/log^2 N.                               (5.21)
```

So the model saturates the Schur estimate up to a fixed constant.

# 6. What this says about the actual Apéry functions

The model does not replace the Apéry recurrence. Its purpose is to prove a precise implication failure.

For the true zero sets, define the common-row multiplicity

```text
M_P(r)
 =sum_(P<p<=2P)
   (log p/log P) 1_(p|b_r),                       (6.1)
```

and its ordered two-characteristic energy

```text
H_2(P;J)
 =sum_(r in J)
   [M_P(r)^2
    -sum_(P<p<=2P)(log p/log P)^2 1_(p|b_r)].     (6.2)
```

Here `J` may be the state packet on which the section kernel has one sign; reflection supplies the corresponding negative lifts automatically.

The exact decomposition (2.3) shows:

```text
C_K^diag is a weighted Fourier section of H_2(P;J). (6.3)
```

The trivial bound is

```text
H_2(P)
 <=(number of primes in the block)
   sum_r M_P(r)
 <<(P/log P) T_P.                                 (6.4)
```

Substitution of (6.4) is exactly the Schur estimate.

Neither the characteristic-zero height of one Apéry number nor the banked `T_P` estimate improves (6.4). Indeed,

```text
# {p~P:p|b_r}
 <=log|b_r|/log P
 =O(P/log P)                                      (6.5)
```

for `r~P`, which is the same scale as the total number of primes in the block. The product of all primes in one narrow block has logarithm `Theta(P)`, so a private or common clique of such primes is compatible with exponential coefficient height.

Thus the actual remaining arithmetic question is not a reciprocal exponential-sum estimate. It is:

```text
How many different characteristics can vanish at the same
characteristic-zero Apéry coefficient, on average over the coefficient? (6.6)
```

This is exactly the horizontal singleton-clique obstruction already visible in the current sparse-exception map, now identified inside TCUS as the nonoscillatory diagonal.

# 7. The smallest replacement lemma

At `K~P`, the Schur scale has the form

```text
P * (P/log P) * T_P.                              (7.1)
```

The common-row diagonal has scale

```text
P * H_2(P).                                       (7.2)
```

Therefore a power `3/5` saving requires, at minimum,

```text
H_2(P)
 <<P^(2/5+o(1)) T_P/log P.                        (7.3)
```

A pointwise sufficient version is

```text
max_r M_P(r)
 <<P^(2/5+o(1))/log P.                            (7.4)
```

These are genuinely new Apéry-specific two-characteristic statements. They do not follow from M1, the one-prime zero-count theorem, reflection, or Parseval.

Keeping the logarithms from the restored shell estimate in the question, write

```text
E_high(N)
 <<Delta_N N T_N log N.                           (7.5)
```

Since

```text
T_N <<N^(8/5)/(log N)^(4/5),                      (7.6)
```

closure needs

```text
Delta_N
 =o(N^(-3/5)(log N)^(-1/5)).                     (7.7)
```

Against the trivial common-row ratio in (6.4), the corresponding log-exact diagonal target is

```text
H_2(P)
 =o(P^(2/5) T_P/(log P)^(6/5)),                   (7.8)
```

up to the harmless fixed number of prime and frequency dyadic decompositions used by the shell restoration.

A weighted packet formulation closer to the exact section is:

```text
Q32-TCUS-COMMON-ROW(P,K):

sum_r sigma_(P,K)(r)
 [M_P(r)^2-diag_1(P,r)]

 =o(P^(-3/5)(log P)^(-1/5))
  * (P/log P)T_P,                                 (7.9)
```

where `sigma_(P,K)(r)` is the bounded signed section weight obtained from (4.4), and `diag_1` removes equal primes. Section 4 shows that `sigma` is positive on fixed packets, so (7.9) cannot be obtained solely by summation by parts in `r`.

The off-diagonal reciprocal task can then be stated separately:

```text

control (2.5) after imposing x!=y.                 (7.10)
```

The present mechanism addresses (7.10), not (7.9).
