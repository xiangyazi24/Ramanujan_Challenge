# The Apéry Mellin fibre is rank two and symplectic; mod-​\(p\) trace vanishing is nonordinary, not torsion

## Executive conclusion

At the repository state `e94ba1d`, the warning in Q854 is justified as far as its criticism of the *raw* compactly supported cohomology on the four-punctured line goes, but its resulting rank-six conclusion is not the rank of the Mellin object used by Katz.

The correct input is the **middle extension on ​\(\mathbf G_m\)** of the rank-three transcendental Apéry K3 local system. Its only interior singularities are the two roots of

```text
Delta(t) = t^2 - 34 t + 1.
```

At each of those two points the rank-three local monodromy is a semisimple reflection with eigenvalues

```text
1, 1, -1,
```

so the middle-extension stalk has dimension two and the drop is one. Katz's Euler--Poincare formula therefore gives Mellin dimension

```text
dim M_p(chi) = 1 + 1 = 2,
```

not six. The six-dimensional space in Q854 is the raw cohomology of the lisse restriction to

```text
P^1 minus {0, infinity, alpha, beta};
```

it includes four boundary dimensions which the middle extension retains as invariant stalks at `alpha,beta` and therefore does not count in the Mellin fibre.

For every nontrivial multiplicative character \(\chi\) and every prime outside a fixed finite bad set, the actual unnormalised Mellin fibre is

\[
 \mathcal M_p(\chi)
 =H_c^1\!\left(\mathbf G_{m,\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
       \mathcal V_p\otimes\mathcal L_\chi\right),
\]

where \(\mathcal V_p\) denotes the middle extension of the rank-three transcendental K3 sheaf. It has:

```text
generic cohomological rank:  2
weight:                       3
duality:                      alternating pairing into Q_l(-3)
determinant of geom. Frob_p:  p^3
normalised geometric group:  Sp_2 = SL_2
normalised arithmetic group: SL_2
characteristic polynomial:   X^2 - T_p(chi) X + p^3.
```

For \(1\le r\le p-2\), choose the Teichmuller character \(\omega_p\), put

\[
 \chi_r=\omega_p^{-r},
 \qquad
 T_{p,r}=\operatorname{Tr}
    (\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid\mathcal M_p(\chi_r)),
\]

and take the prime \(\mathfrak p_{p,r}\) of the cyclotomic coefficient field selected by the Teichmuller embedding. Then the exact integral comparison is

\[
 \boxed{T_{p,r}\equiv b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_{p,r}}.}
\]

Thus

```text
b_r = 0 mod p
    iff  T_(p,r) = 0 mod mathfrak p_(p,r)
    iff  the rank-two weight-three Mellin Frobenius has no slope-zero root.
```

This is a genuine positive theorem: the Apéry zero set is exactly the nonordinary locus of a rank-two weight-three Mellin fibre.

However, it does **not** put Frobenius into a finite-order locus. The reason is decisive. The integral Frobenius determinant is \(p^3\), so reduction at the same prime above \(p\) is singular. When \(T_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p}\), the residual characteristic polynomial is

```text
X^2 mod mathfrak p,
```

not the characteristic polynomial of a trace-zero element of \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\). Dividing by \(p^{3/2}\) produces an \(SL_2\)-class archimedeanly, but that normalisation is not integral at the same residue characteristic.

An explicit allowed non-torsion class is

\[
 P_p(X)=X^2-pX+p^3.
\]

Its two roots have complex absolute value \(p^{3/2}\), its determinant is \(p^3\), its trace is divisible by \(p\), and its Newton slopes are \(1,2\). After normalisation its trace is \(p^{-1/2}\). A torsion \(SL_2\)-class has trace equal to a sum of roots of unity, hence an algebraic integer, whereas \(p^{-1/2}\) is not an algebraic integer. Therefore this class has infinite order while satisfying every rank, weight, determinant, self-duality, and trace-divisibility condition above.

There is no second variable characteristic coefficient: in rank two the other coefficient is the fixed determinant \(p^3\). The precise missing scalar is the **divided trace**

\[
 U_{p,r}=T_{p,r}/p\pmod{\mathfrak p_{p,r}}.
\]

The zero condition determines only that this quotient exists. It imposes no condition on \(U_{p,r}\). A second congruence

```text
U_(p,r) = 0 mod mathfrak p_(p,r),
```

i.e. \(T_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod{p^2}\), would be genuinely new Frobenius-jet information. Traces of \(F^2\), exterior powers, and symmetric powers do not supply it: they are polynomial consequences of \(T_{p,r}\) and \(p^3\).

Consequently the exact rank-two calculation supplies a new structural theorem but no pointwise bound for

\[
 \{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_{n-p}\}.
\]

A bound for that diagonal requires cross-prime control of the divided traces \(U_{p,n-p}\), or another independent low-height scalar. Rank, purity, determinant, and full \(SL_2\) monodromy alone permit the bad congruence at every selected prime.

# 1. The fixed rank-three Apéry K3 local system

Let \(\theta=t\,d/dt\). The Apéry period satisfies

\[
 \mathcal L_3
 =\theta^3
 -t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
 +t^2(\theta+1)^3.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

In ordinary derivatives this is

\[
 t^2(1-34t+t^2)y'''
 +t(3-153t+6t^2)y''
 +(1-112t+7t^2)y'
 +(t-5)y=0.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Beukers--Peters construct the corresponding family of K3 surfaces, and Peters identifies the rank-three transcendental variation and its monodromy. The equation is the symmetric square of

\[
 t(t^2-34t+1)y''
 +(2t^2-51t+1)y'
 +\frac{t-10}{4}y=0.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

This exact symmetric-square description immediately separates the two objects which were repeatedly conflated in the earlier notes:

```text
parameter-side object:
  rank 3, weight 2, orthogonal, geometric group SO_3;

character-Mellin fibre:
  cohomology of the rank-3 object twisted by one Kummer character;
  rank 2, weight 3, symplectic, geometric group SL_2.
```

The second line is not a rank-two sub-local-system of the first. It is a degree-one cohomology group on the parameter curve.

## 1.1 Exact local monodromy

Put

\[
 \alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
 \qquad
 \beta=17-12\sqrt2,
 \qquad
 \alpha\beta=1.
\]

The singularities of (1.1) are

```text
0, infinity, alpha, beta.
```

At `0`, the indicial polynomial is \(\rho^3\), so the rank-three monodromy is one unipotent block `Unip(3)`. By inversion the same is true at infinity.

At a root \(a\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\), the coefficient of \(y'''\) has a simple zero. Writing the first two leading coefficients in (1.2) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [t^2(1-34t+t^2)]'_{t=a}
   &=2a(17a-1),\\
 a(3-153a+6a^2)&=3a(17a-1).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Hence the finite-point indicial equation is

\[
 \rho(\rho-1)\left(\rho-\frac12\right)=0.
\]

Equivalently, in the rank-two square root (1.3) the exponents are \(0,1/2\). They are distinct modulo integers, so the local monodromy is semisimple, with rank-two eigenvalues \(1,-1\). Its symmetric square has eigenvalues

```text
1, -1, 1.
```

Therefore

\[
 \dim \mathcal V^{I_\alpha}
 =\dim \mathcal V^{I_\beta}=2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{drop}_\alpha
 =\operatorname{drop}_\beta=1.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

This one-line drop calculation is the load-bearing correction to Q854.

## 1.2 Inversion

Equation (1.1) is exactly reciprocal. If \(f(z)\) is a solution and \(z=1/t\), then

\[
 y(t)=t^{-1}f(1/t)
\]

is again a solution. Indeed, applying (1.1) and using

\[
 \theta_t(t^{-1}f(1/t))
 =-t^{-1}(\theta_z+1)f(z)
\]

gives

\[
 \mathcal L_{3,t}\bigl(t^{-1}f(1/t)\bigr)
 =-t\,\mathcal L_{3,z}f(z).
 \tag{1.5}
\]

Geometrically this is the parameter inversion correspondence of the Apéry K3 family. Thus, for the middle-extension sheaf,

\[
 \iota^*\mathcal V\simeq\mathcal V,
 \qquad \iota(t)=1/t.
 \tag{1.6}
\]

The K3 cup-product pairing is symmetric and has values in \(\mathbf Q_\ell(-2)\):

\[
 \mathcal V\otimes\mathcal V\longrightarrow\mathbf Q_\ell(-2).
 \tag{1.7}
\]

# 2. The actual Mellin object and its rank

Let

\[
 U=\mathbf G_m\setminus\{\alpha,\beta\},
 \qquad j:U\hookrightarrow\mathbf G_m,
\]

and let

\[
 \mathcal G=j_{!*}(\mathcal V|_U)
\]

be the middle-extension sheaf on \(\mathbf G_m\). For a nontrivial character \(\chi\) of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), let \(\mathcal L_\chi\) be the associated Kummer sheaf and define

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal M_p(\chi)
   =H_c^1\!\left(
      \mathbf G_{m,\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
      \mathcal G_p\otimes\mathcal L_\chi
      \right).}
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Equivalently, in Katz's perverse notation set

\[
 \mathcal N=\mathcal G(3/2)[1].
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Then the normalised Mellin fibre is Deligne's fibre functor

\[
 \omega_\chi(\mathcal N)
 =H_c^0(\mathbf G_m,
         \mathcal N\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
 =\mathcal M_p(\chi)(3/2).
\]

## 2.1 Rank two, not six

Katz records the Euler--Poincare formula for a middle extension \(\mathcal G[1]\) on \(\mathbf G_m\):

\[
 \dim\omega(\mathcal G[1])
 =\operatorname{Swan}_0(\mathcal G)
  +\operatorname{Swan}_\infty(\mathcal G)
  +\sum_{x\in\mathbf G_m}
       (\operatorname{drop}_x+\operatorname{Swan}_x).
 \tag{2.3}
\]

The Apéry sheaf is tame everywhere. Its only singularities *inside* \(\mathbf G_m\) are \(\alpha,\beta\), and (1.4) gives one drop at each. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\dim\mathcal M_p(\chi)=1+1=2.}
 \tag{2.4}
\]

This holds for every nontrivial \(\chi\). Such a character removes the unipotent invariant character at both `0` and `infinity`, so it is a good Mellin character in Katz's sense.

Q854 instead applied

\[
 \chi_c(U,\mathcal V)=3(2-4)=-6
\]

to the *lisse restriction on the four-punctured line*. That computes a different group. At each of \(\alpha,\beta\), the middle extension inserts the two-dimensional inertia-invariant stalk. The four inserted dimensions change six to two:

```text
raw lisse H_c^1 dimension       = 6
minus invariant stalks 2 + 2   = 4
middle-extension Mellin rank   = 2.
```

## 2.2 Weight and determinant

The K3 transcendental sheaf is pure of weight two. A clean degree-one Mellin cohomology is therefore pure of weight three. Thus all complex conjugates of the two Frobenius eigenvalues have absolute value

\[
 p^{3/2}.
\]

Poincare duality first pairs the `chi` fibre with the `chi^{-1}` fibre:

\[
 H_c^1(\mathcal G\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
 \times
 H_c^1(\mathcal G\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi^{-1}})
 \longrightarrow\mathbf Q_\ell(-3).
\]

Inversion (1.6) identifies the second space with the first because

\[
 \iota^*\mathcal L_{\chi^{-1}}\simeq\mathcal L_\chi.
\]

The pairing on \(\mathcal V\) is symmetric, while cup product in cohomological degree one is alternating. Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal M_p(\chi)\times\mathcal M_p(\chi)
 \longrightarrow\mathbf Q_\ell(-3)
 \quad\text{is alternating and perfect}.}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

In dimension two the similitude multiplier equals the determinant, so for geometric Frobenius

\[
 \boxed{
 \det(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid\mathcal M_p(\chi))=p^3.}
 \tag{2.6}
\]

## 2.3 Full monodromy

The middle extension is geometrically irreducible because the parameter-side geometric monodromy is the irreducible standard representation of \(SO_3\).

A nontrivial multiplicative translate would have to preserve the interior singular set \(\{\alpha,\beta\}\). Over an algebraic closure, if multiplication by \(a\) preserves that set, comparison of products gives \(a^2=1\). Except in characteristics `2` and `17`, the value `a=-1` does not preserve the sum \(\alpha+\beta=34\). Thus, outside a fixed finite set of primes, no nontrivial multiplicative translate exists.

Now apply Katz, *Convolution and Equidistribution*, Theorem 14.1. The normalised object \(\mathcal N\) is:

```text
pure of weight zero;
geometrically irreducible;
arithmetically self-dual;
of Tannakian dimension two;
not isomorphic to a nontrivial multiplicative translate.
```

Katz's theorem gives

\[
 \boxed{
 G_{\rm geom}(\mathcal N)
 =G_{\rm arith}(\mathcal N)
 =SL_2=Sp_2.}
 \tag{2.7}
\]

This is exactly the same theorem Katz uses in Theorem 15.3 for \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathrm{Leg})\), but the dimension calculation here is

```text
two reflection points, each of drop 1,
```

rather than one point of drop two.

The excluded characteristics form a fixed finite set and have no effect on any asymptotic diagonal problem. Rank two, purity, pairing, and determinant remain the correct structural description at every good prime; only the uniform full-monodromy conclusion needs the finite exclusion.

# 3. Frobenius trace and the Apéry Mellin coefficient

Let

\[
 A_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m t^m\in\mathbf F_p[t].
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Stienstra--Beukers identify this as the Hasse--Witt polynomial of the Apéry K3 Picard--Fuchs crystal. One direct constant-term derivation uses the standard Apéry Laurent polynomial \(\Phi\), for which

\[
 b_m=\operatorname{CT}(\Phi^m).
\]

The Hasse coefficient is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{CT}(1-t\Phi)^{p-1}
 &=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}
   \binom{p-1}{m}(-t)^m\operatorname{CT}(\Phi^m)\\
 &\equiv\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m t^m
   =A_p(t)\pmod p,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

because \(\binom{p-1}{m}\equiv(-1)^m\pmod p\).

Let

\[
 a_p(t)=\operatorname{Tr}
   (\operatorname{Frob}_t\mid\mathcal G_{p,\bar t}).
\]

The Stienstra--Beukers crystalline construction together with the Katz--Messing comparison gives, at a chosen prime above \(p\),

\[
 a_p(t)\equiv A_p(t)\pmod{\mathfrak p}
 \tag{3.3}
\]

for the middle-extension trace function. At a conifold point this means the trace on the inertia-invariant middle-extension stalk; using the middle extension is precisely what makes (3.3) the correct global trace function rather than a smooth-fibre-only assertion.

For \(\chi_r=\omega_p^{-r}\), the Grothendieck trace formula and the clean rank-two cohomology give

\[
 T_{p,r}
 =-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}a_p(t)\chi_r(t).
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Reduction at the Teichmuller prime sends \(\chi_r(t)\) to \(t^{-r}\). Hence

\[
 T_{p,r}\equiv
 -\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-r}\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]

For \(1\le r\le p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality gives

\[
 \sum_{t\ne0}A_p(t)t^{-r}
 =\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m\sum_{t\ne0}t^{m-r}
 \equiv-b_r\pmod p.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{T_{p,r}\equiv b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.}
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Equivalently, using the toric realization and projection formula, the same trace is the primitive part of the Kummer sum

\[
 -\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
       \chi_r(\Phi(x,y,z)).
\]

The nonprimitive Tate summands have zero Mellin fibre for nontrivial \(\chi_r\). Reducing the displayed sum directly gives (3.5) by torus orthogonality and the unique-interior-lattice-point property of the Apéry Newton polytope.

Thus the degree-two local factor is

\[
 \boxed{
 P_{p,r}(X)
 =X^2-T_{p,r}X+p^3.}
 \tag{3.6}
\]

# 4. The fixed rank-three K3 fibre is a different object

It is useful to state the fixed-fibre characteristic polynomial because it explains the tempting but invalid torsion analogy.

If a rank-two elliptic fibre has Frobenius roots \(\pi,\bar\pi\), with

\[
 \pi\bar\pi=p,
 \qquad a=\pi+\bar\pi,
\]

then its symmetric square has eigenvalues

\[
 \pi^2,\quad p,\quad\bar\pi^2.
\]

Writing

\[
 A=a^2-p,
\]

its rank-three characteristic polynomial is

\[
 \boxed{
 X^3-A X^2+pA X-p^3.}
 \tag{4.1}
\]

After division by \(p\), an \(SO_3\)-class has eigenvalues

```text
1, z, z^(-1).
```

If its **exact normalised trace** is zero, then

```text
1 + z + z^(-1) = 0,
```

so \(z\) is a primitive cube root and the class is torsion. In the special symmetric-square formula, the congruence \(p\mid A=a^2-p\) forces \(p\mid a\); the elliptic Hasse bound then forces \(a=0\) for \(p>4\), giving the order-two normalised symmetric-square class with eigenvalues \(1,-1,-1\).

None of this applies to \(b_r\):

```text
b_r is not the trace of the fixed rank-three K3 fibre at t=r;
b_r is the mod-p reduction of the trace of the rank-two Mellin cohomology.
```

The characteristic polynomial relevant to \(b_r\) is (3.6), not (4.1).

# 5. What `b_r = 0 mod p` actually forces

Fix the selected prime \(\mathfrak p\mid p\) and write \(v=v_{\mathfrak p}(T_{p,r})\). Since the determinant has valuation three, the Newton polygon of

\[
 X^2-T_{p,r}X+p^3
\]

gives:

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 v&\text{Frobenius slopes}\\ \hline
 0&\{0,3\}\\
 1&\{1,2\}\\
 v\ge2&\{3/2,3/2\}.
 \end{array}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 b_r\equiv0\pmod p
 \iff v_{\mathfrak p}(T_{p,r})\ge1
 \iff\mathcal M_p(\chi_r)\text{ has no slope-zero eigenvalue}.}
 \tag{5.2}
\]

This is the exact positive arithmetic lemma supplied by the Mellin calculation: Apéry zeros are the nonordinary character twists of this rank-two weight-three object.

But (5.2) does not imply torsion.

## 5.1 Why the residual trace-zero argument is invalid

The normalised Frobenius class is obtained by dividing eigenvalues by \(p^{3/2}\), and lies in \(SL_2\) over a characteristic-zero coefficient field. At the prime over \(p\), however, this division is not integral.

Before normalisation, reduction of (3.6) under the zero condition gives

\[
 P_{p,r}(X)\equiv X^2\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

This is a singular residual operator. It is not an element of \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\). It is therefore irrelevant that an *invertible* trace-zero element of \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\) satisfies \(g^2=-I\).

The same-residue-characteristic determinant collapse is the exact place where the torsion argument fails.

## 5.2 Explicit non-torsion class in the correct group

For every prime \(p\), consider

\[
 P_p(X)=X^2-pX+p^3.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

Its discriminant is \(p^2(1-4p)<0\), so its roots are complex conjugates. Their product is \(p^3\), hence each has absolute value \(p^{3/2}\). Thus (5.4) is a perfectly admissible pure weight-three symplectic Frobenius polynomial.

It has

```text
trace       = p,
determinant = p^3,
slopes      = 1 and 2,
trace mod p = 0.
```

After normalisation by \(p^{3/2}\), the determinant is one and the trace is

\[
 p^{-1/2}.
\]

If this normalised class were torsion, its eigenvalues would be roots of unity, so its trace would be an algebraic integer. But \(p^{-1/2}\) is not an algebraic integer: its norm from \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt p)\) is \(-1/p\). Hence the class has infinite order.

A concrete matrix is

\[
 F_p=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&-p^3\\
 1&p
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Every two-by-two matrix satisfies

\[
 F_p^{\mathsf T}JF_p=(\det F_p)J,
 \qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

so this matrix lies in the correct two-dimensional symplectic-similitude group with multiplier \(p^3\). Since the actual normalised monodromy group is the full \(SL_2\), no proper-group constraint excludes this conjugacy-class type.

This is the requested structural counterexample inside the correct monodromy group.

# 6. There is no independent second characteristic coefficient

For a rank-two Frobenius operator \(F\), all characteristic data are

\[
 T=\operatorname{Tr}F,
 \qquad D=\det F=p^3.
\]

The determinant is fixed for every character. Thus the zero condition changes only the trace.

Other natural traces are polynomial consequences:

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(F^2)=T^2-2p^3,
 \tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Sym}^2F)=T^2-p^3.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

If \(T=pU\), then

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Tr}(F^2)&=p^2(U^2-2p),\\
 \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Sym}^2F)&=p^2(U^2-p).
 \end{aligned}
\]

So the zero condition does force divisibility by \(p^2\) of these two derived traces. But that divisibility is tautological: it contains no information beyond \(p\mid T\), and it cannot provide a second low-height container.

Modulo \(p^2\), the characteristic polynomial is

\[
 X^2-pU_{p,r}X\pmod{p^2},
 \qquad
 U_{p,r}=T_{p,r}/p\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

The scalar \(U_{p,r}\) is unconstrained by rank, weight, determinant, duality, or monodromy. The example (5.4) has

```text
U_(p,r) = 1 mod p.
```

Therefore the exact missing local datum is:

\[
 \boxed{U_{p,r}=T_{p,r}/p\pmod{\mathfrak p}.}
 \tag{6.4}
\]

A genuinely stronger congruence would be

\[
 U_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p},
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 T_{p,r}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p^2}.
 \tag{6.5}
\]

This is the first Frobenius jet beyond the Hasse invariant. Nothing in the characteristic polynomial supplies (6.5).

There is a useful special-case calibration. If \(T_{p,r}\) were a rational integer and (6.5) held, then the Weil bound

\[
 |T_{p,r}|\le2p^{3/2}
\]

would force \(T_{p,r}=0\) for \(p>4\), and the normalised class would indeed have eigenvalues \(\pm i\), hence order four. In the actual moving-character problem, however, \(T_{p,r}\) lies in a cyclotomic coefficient field whose degree varies with the order of \(\chi_r\). Divisibility at the one Teichmuller prime above \(p\) does not propagate to the conjugate primes. This is exactly the diagonal Galois movement audited in Q368.

# 7. Consequence for the fixed-`n` diagonal

For \(n/2<p\le n\), put \(r=n-p\). The exact translation of the top-half event is

\[
 p\mid b_{n-p}
 \iff
 \mathfrak p_{p,n-p}\mid T_{p,n-p}.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

The rank-two theorem converts the problem into a horizontal count of nonordinary Mellin twists:

\[
 \#\left\{
 p:\frac n2<p\le n,
 \ \mathcal M_p(\omega_p^{p-n})
 \text{ is nonordinary}
 \right\}.
 \tag{7.2}
\]

It does not bound this set. Indeed, the assignment

\[
 P_{p,n}(X)=X^2-pX+p^3
\]

for every selected prime satisfies all the following local conclusions of the genuine Apéry calculation:

```text
rank 2;
pure weight 3;
determinant p^3;
symplectic self-duality;
normalised class in SL_2;
trace divisible by p;
nonordinary slopes {1,2};
non-torsion.
```

Thus those invariants alone are compatible with selecting every prime in the top-half interval. This does not construct actual Apéry Frobenius classes; it proves rigorously that no deduction using only the calculated rank, group, weight, determinant, and first trace congruence can give a diagonal bound.

A usable arithmetic completion would need one of the following genuinely additional inputs.

1. **A divided-trace theorem.** Prove a second congruence controlling
   \(U_{p,n-p}\), ideally \(U_{p,n-p}=0\) or membership in a fixed finite set.

2. **A second independent Frobenius scalar.** Construct an algebraic integer
   \(S_{p,r}\), not a polynomial in \(T_{p,r}\) and \(p^3\), such that
   simultaneous vanishing eliminates \(U_{p,r}\).

3. **A global low-height interpolation.** Produce nonzero integers \(C_n\) with

   \[
   \prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_{n-p}}}p\mid C_n,
   \qquad \log|C_n|=o(n),
   \]

   by showing that the local divided traces are reductions of one
   characteristic-zero object of subexponential height.

The determinant, \(\operatorname{Tr}(F^2)\), and
\(\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Sym}^2F)\) cannot play the role of \(S\), because they are already functions of the first trace.

# 8. Final verdict

The exact calculation requested in Q5572 is positive and resolves the rank ambiguity:

```text
INPUT K3 SHEAF
  rank 3, weight 2, orthogonal, SO_3;
  singularities 0, infinity, alpha, beta;
  local monodromy at alpha,beta = diag(1,1,-1).

MELLIN FIBRE AT chi = omega^(-r)

  determinant p^3;
  normalised G_geom = G_arith = SL_2;
  characteristic polynomial X^2 - T_(p,r) X + p^3;
  T_(p,r) = b_r mod the Teichmuller prime.
```

The resulting arithmetic lemma is exact:

```text
b_r = 0 mod p
  iff the Mellin fibre is nonordinary.
```

The hoped-for torsion lemma is false. The congruence is imposed before weight normalisation, at the same prime which kills the determinant. The residual operator is nilpotent rather than an invertible trace-zero \(SL_2\)-element. The polynomial

```text
X^2 - p X + p^3
```

is an explicit pure, symplectic, nonordinary, infinite-order counterexample in the correct group.

The precise missing scalar is the divided trace

```text
U_(p,r) = T_(p,r)/p mod p,
```

or an independent scalar which controls it. Until such a scalar is linked across different residue characteristics, the exact Mellin theorem reorganises the zero condition but does not furnish a p-independent low-height divisibility container.
