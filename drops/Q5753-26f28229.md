# Same-K cancellation theorem: exact Smith rank-one reduction

## Theorem

Let `Y=(Y_j)_{j\ge0}` be any integer sequence. For finitely many intervals

\[
I_i=[d_i,d_i+L_i]
\]

let

\[
A_i=G_{d_i,L_i}(Y)
\]

be the primitive Newton carrier with the interpolation property

\[
q-1\in I_i\implies A_i\equiv Y_{q-1}\pmod q.
\]

Let

\[
J=\bigcap_i I_i.
\]

Then for every prime `q` with `q-1 in J`,

\[
A_i\equiv Y_{q-1}\pmod q
\]

for every `i`.

Therefore for any integers `lambda_i`,

\[
E=\sum_i\lambda_i A_i
\]

satisfies

\[
E\equiv(\sum_i\lambda_i)Y_{q-1}\pmod q.
\]

In particular, if

\[
\sum_i\lambda_i=0,
\]

then

\[
q\mid E
\]

for every sequence `Y` and every prime node in the common interval.

## Proof

The first assertion is exactly the Newton endpoint congruence. If `q-1` is a node of `I_i`, interpolation evaluates the carrier at that node modulo `q`; all other Lagrange basis terms contain a factor `q`, while the distinguished basis coefficient is one modulo `q`. Hence

\[
G_{d_i,L_i}(Y)\equiv Y_{q-1}\pmod q.
\]

The linear combination follows immediately by summing congruences. If the coefficient sum is zero, the distinguished scalar disappears and the resulting divisibility is universal: it contains no information about whether `q` divides `Y_{q-1}`.

## Corollary: cancellation is exactly inside the Smith no-go

The relation

\[
A_i+H_i=S_K
\]

with the same packet `S_K` means that a same-K cancellation is precisely a linear relation with coefficient sum zero:

\[
\sum_i\lambda_i A_i
=-\sum_i\lambda_i H_i.
\]

The cancelled quantity `S_K` is not a new target observable. After cancellation the remaining rows are finite-difference rows. Their local Smith form at a common target prime is

\[
\operatorname{diag}(1,q,\ldots,q),
\]

so the forced powers of `q` are presentation content. After primitive saturation the only remaining target-sensitive coordinate is the original alias scalar `Y_{q-1}`.

Thus same-K cancellation does not escape the rank-one theorem.

# Union-of-interval attempts

Suppose the union of intervals is larger than the common intersection. To force coverage, one may multiply rows by primorial factors. Let `P_i` be a product of primes in `I_i` that are not in the common core. A constructed row has the form

\[
B_i=P_iA_i.
\]

For a prime `q` in the missing part of another interval, target divisibility is obtained only because `P_i` already contains `q`. It is coefficient-forced, not state-forced.

The height cost is unavoidable:

\[
\log |P_i|=\sum_{q\in I_i\setminus J}\log q.
\]

For a block of length `N` with primes of size comparable to `M`, this is

\[
\asymp N\log M
\]

per full interval. Therefore a cover by such primorial-weighted rows has total height at least the total covered interval length times `log M`.

This is larger than the required `o(N)` block ledger and cannot prove the desired radical bound.

# Exact quantifier statement

The only possible escape would require a theorem of the following form:

For a family of same-K packets, there exist integer combinations `E_alpha` such that

1. every target prime in the desired block divides some `E_alpha`;
2. the total coefficient height satisfies

\[
\sum_\alpha \log\|E_\alpha\|=o(H);
\]

3. after local Smith saturation, the reductions of the `E_alpha` contain a second distinguished-state scalar independent of `Y_{q-1}`.

The theorem above proves condition (3) cannot come from linear same-K cancellation alone. The only remaining possibility would be a genuinely nonlinear arithmetic identity among the actual Apéry shell values, not a consequence of the Newton packet relation.

# Verdict

The corrected beta-Pade same-K route is fully contained in the existing Smith rank-one no-go under the stated hypotheses. Exact cancellation of the shared packet produces universal divisibility, not target divisibility. Covering unions by coefficient primorials merely transfers the target factors into explicit height, with linear block-scale cost.

Therefore no new horizontal cancellation theorem is obtained from `A_i+H_i=S_K` alone. A future escape must prove a new arithmetic relation for the distinguished Apéry state, not exploit the same-K Newton presentation.
