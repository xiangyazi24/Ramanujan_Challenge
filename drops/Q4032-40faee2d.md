## 1. Audit of A: fixed nonzero transfer residues

Fix all outer discrete data: the physical order cell/fibre, \(q,r,m\), and
\(\epsilon\). Thus

\[
 d_2=d_1-\epsilon\Delta,\qquad \Delta=m/10,
\]

and the coefficients \(\alpha,eta_0\) in

\[
 H(d_1)=(-1)^r(\alpha d_1+\epsilon\beta_0)
\]

are fixed while the shell shift \(d_1\) runs.

For \(c\in\mathbf F_q^\times\), the banked congruence gives

\[
 H+c d_1d_2\equiv d_1d_2(c-E)\pmod q. \tag{1}
\]

On the actual-leaf domain, \(q\nmid d_1d_2\). Indeed, this unit property is
already implicit in the banked biconditional

\[
 q\nmid H\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E\not\equiv0\pmod q
\]

together with \(H\equiv-d_1d_2E\pmod q\). Therefore (1) gives the exact
biconditional

\[
 E\equiv c\pmod q
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H+c d_1d_2\equiv0\pmod q. \tag{2}
\]

This unit check is indispensable. Without it, (2) would be the first fatal
flaw: if \(q\mid d_1d_2\), the right side of (1) vanishes for every \(E\).
That case is excluded on the banked actual-leaf domain.

After substituting \(d_2=d_1-\epsilon\Delta\), the polynomial in (2) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_c(D)
 &=(-1)^r(\alpha D+\epsilon\beta_0)
   +cD(D-\epsilon\Delta)\\
 &=cD^2+
   \bigl((-1)^r\alpha-c\epsilon\Delta\bigr)D
   +(-1)^r\epsilon\beta_0 . \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Its leading coefficient is \(c\ne0\) in \(\mathbf F_q\). Consequently it is
an honest degree-two polynomial, even if its linear or constant coefficient
vanishes, and has at most two roots in \(\mathbf F_q\). Changing the sign,
parity, physical order cell, or any bounded fibre merely changes the lower
coefficients or introduces an \(O_J(1)\) number of copies; it cannot cancel the
quadratic coefficient.

The shell for \(d_1\) has length \(<q\), so each residue class modulo \(q\)
occurs at most once in each fixed shell fibre. Hence, for every fixed
\(c\ne0\), there are at most two actual shell shifts in that fibre. More
generally, for any single set

\[
 S_q\subseteq\mathbf F_q^\times,\qquad |S_q|\le R,
\]

fixed at the level at which the shell shift runs,

\[
 \#\{d_1:E(d_1)\bmod q\in S_q\}\le 2R                  \tag{4}
\]

per fixed outer fibre. No probabilistic independence is needed: (4) holds for
every set of size \(R\). The same proof even permits the set to depend on the
fixed tuple \((q,r,m,\epsilon,\text{fibre})\). What is impermissible is a new
singleton set chosen separately for every running \(d_1\).

In a balanced dyadic block there are \(O_J(X^2)\) admissible \((r,m)\) tuples
(the restriction \(m\in20\mathbf Z\) only improves the constant), and only
\(O_J(1)\) physical/sign fibres. Weighting by \(\log q\) and using the
Chebyshev bound for primes in the \(q\)-shell,

\[
 \sum_{q\ \mathrm{in\ shell}}\log q\ll T,
\]

(4) yields

\[
 W_A(X;S)\ll_J R\,T\,X^2.                              \tag{5}
\]

Thus A is an unconditional occurrence-level deletion. It counts actual shell
shifts, not merely distinct primes or unlabelled algebraic solutions.

## 2. Audit of B: endpoint divisibility

Work inside the unmarked residual, so

\[
 E=K_2-MK_1\not\equiv0\pmod q.                         \tag{6}
\]

If \(q\mid K_1K_2\), at least one endpoint is divisible by \(q\). They cannot
both be divisible, because then (6) would give \(E\equiv0\pmod q\). Hence

\[
 q\mid K_1K_2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 (q\mid K_1)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(q\mid K_2).          \tag{7}
\]

No invertibility assumption on \(M\) is needed. By the banked endpoint
criterion,

\[
 q\mid K_1K_2
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 q\mid b_r b_{r+m}.                                    \tag{8}
\]

### Why the \(q\mid K_2\) half is still bounded by \(k_r(q)\)

Fix \(q,r,m,\epsilon\) and the bounded physical fibre. Project an actual pair
to its smaller first leaf. The first leaf fixes \(d_1\); then

\[
 d_2=d_1-\epsilon m/10
\]

fixes the second shell position. Thus the projection has fibre size one, or
at worst an \(O_J(1)\) size coming from the already separated physical labels.
In particular,

\[
 \#\{\text{B-pairs over }q,r,m\}\ll_J k_r(q).           \tag{9}
\]

The proof of (9) never requires \(q\mid K_1\). If instead \(q\mid K_2\), that
condition merely filters the uniquely reconstructed second leaf. Therefore
the same total smaller-first-leaf multiplicity \(k_r(q)\) remains valid.

There is one precise definitional trap. If somebody were to redefine
\(k_r(q)\) as

\[
 \#\{\text{first leaves satisfying }q\mid K_1\}
\]

rather than the banked count of all admissible first leaves, then (9) would
not control the \(q\mid K_2,\ q\nmid K_1\) case. Under the stated
smaller-first-leaf meaning, that flaw is absent.

Using \(k_r(q)\ll_J r\), (8), and counting distinct prime divisors with their
\(\log q\) weight gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 W_B(X)
 &\ll_J
   \sum_{r,m}\ \sum_{q\mid b_r b_{r+m}} r\log q\\
 &\le
   C_J\sum_{r,m}r\log\operatorname{rad}(b_r b_{r+m})\\
 &\le
   C_J\sum_{r,m}r\bigl(\log b_r+\log b_{r+m}\bigr)\\
 &\ll_J
   \sum_{r,m}r(2r+m)
 \ll_J X^4.                                             \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]

Primes outside the physical \(q\)-shell are harmlessly overcounted. A prime
dividing both \(b_r\) and \(b_{r+m}\) is also harmlessly overcounted, since by
(7) its actual unmarked contribution is empty. Thus (10) preserves prime and
occurrence multiplicities correctly.

Let

\[
 \mathcal B_0:=\frac{\lambda T^2}{L\eta},\qquad
 Y:=\mathcal B_0^{1/4}.                                 \tag{11}
\]

Equation (10) gives deletion for

\[
 X\le c_JY                                               \tag{12}
\]

with a sufficiently small constant \(c_J>0\), or equivalently
\(X\ll_JY\). Therefore the proposed fourth-root scale is correct. The literal
assertion that every block with \(X\le Y\) fits a budget whose constant is
exactly one does not follow from an \(O_J(X^4)\) estimate; one must absorb
\(C_J\) into the cutoff or into the slack parameter \(\eta\). If the ledger
demands an \(o(\mathcal B_0)\), use \(X=o(Y)\) or one further slowly growing
slack. This is a constant/slack correction, not a fatal flaw in B.

## 3. Relation to the close-offset divided-midpoint theorem

B does not discover a new algebraic locus: on the range where the existing
close-offset divided-midpoint theorem applies, both arguments delete the same
endpoint-divisibility subfamily

\[
 \{q\mid K_1K_2\}=\{q\mid b_r b_{r+m}\}.                \tag{13}
\]

Hence B is duplicate on that overlap. Its genuine gain is range, not locus:
the divisor-log proof (10) uses only balancedness, \(k_r(q)\ll_Jr\), and
\(\log b_n\ll n\); it does not use the old close-offset hypothesis. It
therefore extends the deletion to balanced offsets outside the old theorem's
close range. If a particular block already lies entirely inside that close
range, B adds no new deletion there.

## 4. Smallest conditional gateway after A and B

After B, every surviving unmarked pair satisfies

\[
 q\nmid K_1K_2,\qquad E\in\mathbf F_q^\times.            \tag{14}
\]

The weakest next structural input is not equidistribution and does not need a
ratio theorem. It is a small-list compression statement for the residue
\(E\), with one list fixed for each full shell-shift fibre. A sharp gateway is:

```text
SMALL-LIST(E; R, Exc).
For every outer tuple
    tau = (q,r,m,epsilon,physical fibre),
choose one set S_tau subset F_q^x with |S_tau| <= R. The aggregate weighted
mass, summed over all tau, of surviving pairs satisfying E mod q notin S_tau
is at most Exc(X).
```

A common list \(S_q\) for all \(r,m\) is stronger than necessary; tuple-level
lists already suffice because (3)--(4) are uniform after the outer tuple is
fixed. Combining this gateway with A and B gives the exact block estimate

\[
 W_{\mathrm{block}}(X)
 \ll_J X^4+R\,T\,X^2+\operatorname{Exc}(X).              \tag{15}
\]

Thus the minimal quantitative conditions for deletion at budget
\(\mathcal B_0\) are, up to \(J\)-constants,

\[
 X^4\ll\mathcal B_0,\qquad
 R\ll\frac{\lambda T}{L\eta X^2},\qquad
 \operatorname{Exc}(X)\ll\mathcal B_0.                  \tag{16}
\]

At the fourth-root boundary \(X\asymp Y\), the middle condition becomes

\[
 R\ll\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{L\eta}}
 \asymp
 \frac{T^{1/5}}
 {\xi(T)^{1/2}L^{11/10}\eta^{1/2}}.                     \tag{17}
\]

The decisive quantifier is that \(S_\tau\) is one set for the whole running
shift fibre. Allowing a separate singleton
\(S_{\tau,d_1}=\{E(d_1)\}\) for every pair would make the assertion vacuous
and would not permit summation by (4).

Accordingly, the first genuinely conditional gateway remaining after these
two unconditional deletions is a bounded-image/small-list theorem for the
nonzero, nonendpoint transfer residue \(E=K_2-MK_1\pmod q\), possibly with a
controlled aggregate weighted exceptional set. No stronger mixing or full
equidistribution statement is required for this stage.
