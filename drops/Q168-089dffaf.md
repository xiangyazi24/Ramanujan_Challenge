# Q168 — Directly sieving the large prime factors of the Apéry number \(b_n\)

## Executive verdict

There are two important corrections before evaluating the proposed approaches.

First, the literal fixed-saving statement is already true for a reason much simpler than the height of \(b_n\). If

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)=\#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n\},
\]

then

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
\le \pi(n)-\pi(n/2)
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac n{\log n}.
\]

Thus one may take, for example, **ε = 3** in

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)\le(3.52-\varepsilon)\frac n{\log n}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(n\). No Apéry structure is needed.

Second, one fixed saving does **not** imply the conjecture. Every estimate

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)\le c\frac n{\log n}
\qquad(c>0\text{ fixed})
\]

still permits

\[
B_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
:=\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}\log p
\asymp n.
\]

What is required is zero density inside the available primes:

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
=o\!\left(\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)\right)
=o\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right).
}
\]

After this correction, the substantive conclusion is:

> None of the proposed generic tools—supercongruences, divisor bounds, Erdős–Kac, Rosser–Iwaniec sieve, or Lucas-sequence prime-factor technology—currently proves this pointwise zero density. The Apéry structure converts the problem into a moving cross-prime incidence problem, and the missing input is precisely cross-prime anti-alignment.

For \(p\in(n/2,n)\), the Lucas law gives the exact reformulation

```text
p | b_n    <==>    p | b_{n-p}    <==>    n-p in Z_p       (p >= 7).
```

Hence

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
=\#\{p\in(n/2,n):n-p\in Z_p\}.
\]

A direct sieve would have to prove that the moving diagonal \(p\mapsto n-p\) cannot land in many different zero sets \(Z_p\). That is essentially the atom-tail/factorial-moment theorem already isolated in the project; classical sieve theory does not provide it for free.

## 1. The correct elementary scales

Let

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^4,
\qquad
\lambda=\log\alpha=3.525494348\ldots .
\]

The Apéry recurrence implies

\[
\log b_n=\lambda n+O(\log n).
\]

The height argument therefore gives

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)\log(n/2)
\le\log b_n
=\lambda n+O(\log n),
\]

so

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
\le(\lambda+o(1))\frac n{\log n}.
\]

This is valid but not the best trivial bound for **distinct** prime factors. There are only

\[
\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac n{\log n}
\]

candidate primes. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
\le\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac n{\log n}.
}
\]

Equivalently, every fixed

\[
0<\varepsilon<\lambda-\frac12
=3.025494348\ldots
\]

gives

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
\le(\lambda-\varepsilon)\frac n{\log n}
\]

for sufficiently large \(n\).

There is a useful notation distinction:

\[
\omega_I(N)=\#\{p\in I:p\mid N\}
\]

counts distinct prime divisors, whereas

\[
\Omega_I(N)=\sum_{p\in I}v_p(N)
\]

counts them with multiplicity. Prime supply directly bounds \(\omega_I\); the height bound directly controls \(\Omega_I\). The question is stated with \(\omega\), so \(1/2\), not \(3.52549\), is the natural trivial coefficient.

For the logarithmically weighted radical,

\[
B_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
\le\vartheta(n)-\vartheta(n/2)
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n.
\]

Since \(\log(n/2)\le\log p\le\log n\) throughout the window,

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)=o(n/\log n)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B_{\mathrm{top}}(n)=o(n).
\]

A bound with any fixed positive coefficient remains linear after logarithmic weighting and cannot establish \(e^{o(n)}\).

## 2. Why height is extremely far from the desired sparsity

Let

\[
Q_n=\prod_{n/2<p\le n}p.
\]

The prime number theorem gives

\[
\log Q_n
=\vartheta(n)-\vartheta(n/2)
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n.
\]

But

\[
\log b_n=(3.525494348\ldots)n+O(\log n).
\]

Therefore

\[
\log Q_n^7=(3.5+o(1))n<\log b_n
\]

for sufficiently large \(n\). Size alone is compatible not merely with every prime in \((n/2,n]\) dividing \(b_n\), but with every such prime occurring to exponent seven. This does not assert the divisibility; it demonstrates how blind the height argument is.

More generally, suppose hypothetically that every top-window hit satisfied

\[
v_p(b_n)\ge\kappa.
\]

Then height would imply only

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
\le\left(\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}+o(1)\right)\frac n{\log n}.
\]

For \(\kappa\le7\), this is weaker than prime supply. A fixed \(\kappa\ge8\) would yield a fixed saving below \(1/2\), but still not little-\(o\). To solve the conjecture from multiplicities alone, one would need \(\kappa(n)\to\infty\) for essentially all hits. Neither the known congruences nor the computations suggest this.

The same quantitative warning applies to a large prime factor outside the window. A factor of polynomial or mildly superpolynomial size uses only \(o(n)\) logarithmic mass. To force the top radical to be \(e^{o(n)}\) by accounting for outside factors, one would need to prove that outside factors consume

\[
(\lambda-o(1))n
\]

of the logarithmic mass of \(b_n\). That is essentially another formulation of the desired theorem.

## 3. Exact Lucas reformulation of a top-window hit

Let \(p\in(n/2,n)\) and write

\[
n=p+r,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

The base-\(p\) digits of \(n\) are \(1,r\). Gessel's Lucas congruence gives

\[
b_n=b_{p+r}\equiv b_1b_r=5b_r\pmod p.
\]

Thus, for \(p\ge7\),

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid b_{n-p}.
}
\]

The endpoint \(p=n\) is not a hit for \(p\ge7\), since

\[
b_p\equiv b_1b_0=5\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Consequently, up to the finite prime \(5\),

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
=\sum_{n/2<p<n}\mathbf1_{Z_p}(n-p).
}
\]

Changing variables \(r=n-p\) gives the equivalent shifted-divisor form

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
=\sum_{0\le r<n/2}
\mathbf1_{\mathbb P}(n-r)
\mathbf1_{\,n-r\mid b_r}.
}
\]

This is Goldbach-like: \(n-r\) must be prime and must divide the different integer \(b_r\). It is not a conventional factor-counting problem on one generic integer.

There is one small direct structural consequence. If \(p=n-r\mid b_r\), then \(b_r\ge p>n/2\). Since

\[
\log b_r=\lambda r+O(\log r),
\]

we obtain

\[
r\ge\frac{\log n}{\lambda}+O(\log\log n).
\]

Thus hits are excluded from an \(O(\log n)\)-length interval immediately below \(n\). This removes only \(o(n/\log n)\) candidate primes and leaves the coefficient \(1/2\) unchanged.

## 4. Audit of the proposed methods

### 4.1 Supercongruences and “multiplicative structure”

The Beukers-type congruence

\[
b_{mp^s}\equiv b_{mp^{s-1}}\pmod{p^{3s}}
\]

is powerful when the **index is divisible by \(p\)**. A top-window hit has

\[
n=p+r,
\qquad 0<r<p,
\]

so \(p\nmid n\). The only multiple of \(p\) in the range is \(n=p\), and that case is not a hit because \(b_p\equiv5\pmod p\).

The Apéry sequence is also not multiplicative or a divisibility sequence in the integer sense. There is no analogue of

\[
m\mid n\Longrightarrow b_m\mid b_n,
\]

nor a factorization of \(b_n\) indexed by divisors of \(n\). The \(p\)-Lucas law is multiplicative in base-\(p\) digits **modulo \(p\)**, not an integer factorization.

A stronger \(p\)-adic block law might control \(v_p(b_{p+r})\) in terms of \(b_r\), but a fixed valuation gain would still face the quantitative obstruction of Section 2.

**Verdict:** the known supercongruences are off the relevant top-window locus, and fixed multiplicity control would not prove the required little-\(o\) statement.

### 4.2 Divisor bounds

Let \(	au(N)\) be the divisor function. Since every distinct top-window prime doubles the number of divisors,

\[
2^{K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)}\le\tau(b_n).
\]

The sharp generic maximal-order estimate is

\[
\log\tau(N)
\le(\log2+o(1))\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}.
\]

Putting \(N=b_n\) yields

\[
\log\tau(b_n)
\le(\lambda\log2+o(1))\frac n{\log n},
\]

hence

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
\le(\lambda+o(1))\frac n{\log n}.
\]

Thus generic divisor theory merely recovers the height scale and is weaker than prime supply.

A sequence-specific theorem such as

```text
tau(b_n) = exp(o(n/log n)) for every n
```

would solve the problem. But this would be a very strong global factorization theorem about Apéry numbers. It is not a routine consequence of being hypergeometric or holonomic. The factorial product structure lies in the summands

\[
\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\]

and summation destroys the product decomposition needed for standard divisor arguments.

**Verdict:** generic divisor bounds give no saving. A sufficient Apéry-specific divisor bound would itself be a major new theorem, essentially as difficult as the target.

### 4.3 Erdős–Kac for \(b_n\)

The random-integer heuristic predicts the right qualitative answer in the top window, but classical Erdős–Kac cannot simply be transferred to this sparse deterministic sequence. The fixed-prime local statistics are not those of random integers.

Set

\[
z_p=|Z_p|.
\]

For \(0\le m<p^k\), the Lucas product law shows that \(p\nmid b_m\) exactly when every base-\(p\) digit avoids \(Z_p\). Therefore

\[
\#\{0\le m<p^k:p\nmid b_m\}=(p-z_p)^k,
\]

and

\[
\frac1{p^k}\#\{0\le m<p^k:p\mid b_m\}
=1-\left(1-\frac{z_p}{p}\right)^k.
\]

If \(z_p>0\), this density tends to \(1\) as \(k\to\infty\). A fixed active prime divides \(b_m\) for almost every sufficiently long base-\(p\) digit string. This differs radically from the random-integer probability \(1/p\).

The top window is special because \(n<2p\): the high digit is fixed at \(1\), and only \(r=n-p\) varies. The relevant local density is \(z_p/p\). If the observed mean \(z_p\approx1\) persists, the heuristic mean becomes

\[
\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\frac{z_p}{p}
\sim\frac{\log2}{\log n},
\]

which matches the computation very well. But proving this uniformly for moving primes \(p\asymp n\), and proving an upper tail for **every** \(n\), is the hard part.

Even a full Erdős–Kac theorem for \(\omega(b_m)\) as \(m\le N\) varies would normally be a normal-order or density-one result. It could leave an infinite sparse exceptional sequence \(n_j\) with large \(K_{\mathrm{top}}(n_j)\). The project already has density-one control; the unresolved problem is the pointwise exceptional tail.

The directly relevant theorem of Luca–Shparlinski (2008) proves, for a density-one set of indices, the lower bounds

\[
\omega(b_n)>c_0\log\log\log n,
\qquad
P(b_n)>c_0(\log n\log\log n)^{1/2}.
\]

These results use the recurrence and Lucas congruences, but they go in the opposite direction and are far below the top-window scale.

**Verdict:** the heuristic is compelling, but a classical Erdős–Kac theorem neither follows from current structure nor would by itself settle the all-\(n\) conjecture.

### 4.4 Rosser–Iwaniec or Selberg sieve

A combinatorial sieve needs an ensemble and distribution data. In standard notation one must estimate, for squarefree \(d\),

\[
A_d(X)=\#\{m\le X:d\mid b_m\}
=Xg(d)+r_d,
\]

with a multiplicative density \(g\) and a sufficiently strong average bound for the remainders \(r_d\).

For one fixed \(n\), there is no ensemble: the question asks for prime divisors of the single integer \(b_n\). Treating \(p\) as the sieve variable does not help, because \(p\mid b_n\) is not a congruence-class condition on \(p\); it is membership in an unknown divisor set.

If one dualizes and averages over \(n\), the one-prime density is governed by \(Z_p\). But a sieve requires joint information. For distinct \(p,q\asymp X\), simultaneous hits are periodic modulo \(pq\asymp X^2\), while the \(n\)-interval has length only \(X\). There is no complete period. The \(O(1)\) boundary error per CRT class becomes dominant after summing over many pairs and tuples. This is precisely the pair-to-third-moment barrier recorded in `problems/3.2/proof.tex` and `problems/3.2/DOCTRINE.md`.

Knowing only

\[
g(p)=\frac{z_p}{p}
\]

is insufficient. A sieve would need estimates for

\[
\#\{m\in I:m\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i}\text{ for every }i\}
\]

uniformly for products of large co-varying primes. Such estimates are the desired cross-prime independence theorem.

In the project's language, the sieve needs the same input as a sufficiently high factorial moment \((\mathrm{HM})_k\), or the pointwise atom-tail estimate

\[
\max_m K_X(m)\ll\lambda_X X^{o(1)}.
\]

The sieve does not manufacture this input; it consumes it.

**Verdict:** classical sieve theory repackages the missing joint-distribution statement and gives no independent shortcut.

### 4.5 Fibonacci and Lucas analogies

The useful prime-factor theory for Fibonacci and Lucas numbers rests on structures absent here:

\[
\gcd(F_m,F_n)=F_{\gcd(m,n)},
\qquad
m\mid n\Longrightarrow F_m\mid F_n,
\]

cyclotomic decompositions of \(\alpha^n-eta^n\), ranks of apparition, and primitive divisors. Bilu–Hanrot–Voutier proved that every Lucas or Lehmer term with index \(n>30\) has a primitive divisor. Stewart later proved a lower bound of the shape

\[
P(u_n)>n\exp\!\left(\frac{\log n}{104\log\log n}\right)
\]

for the greatest prime factor of the relevant Lucas/Lehmer cyclotomic component.

These arguments are powerful because prime divisors of primitive components have orders tied to \(n\), often forcing congruences such as \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod n\). Apéry numbers satisfy a polynomial-coefficient recurrence, not a constant-coefficient Binet formula; they are not a strong divisibility sequence and have no known cyclotomic factorization.

Moreover, primitive-divisor and greatest-prime-factor results are mostly lower bounds on the presence or size of factors. A factor of size

\[
n\exp(\log n/(104\log\log n))
\]

uses only \(O(\log n)\) logarithmic mass, negligible compared with \(\log b_n\asymp n\). Even a perfect Apéry analogue would not give a fixed linear saving in the top radical.

The “Luca (2001)” attribution in the question should be checked against a precise reference. I could not match the stated formulation to a standard theorem; it may be a conflation with the 2001 primitive-divisor theorem of Bilu–Hanrot–Voutier or later work of Stewart. In any case, an upper bound only of order \(O(n/\log n)\) is the scale already supplied for \(b_n\) by generic divisor theory and remains insufficient.

**Verdict:** the Fibonacci technology depends on strong divisibility and cyclotomic order constraints that are not presently available for Apéry numbers.

### 4.6 The modular-form connection

The central congruence

\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f)\pmod p
\]

controls only the central zero. For a top hit \(r=n-p\), centrality means

\[
n-p=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

so

\[
p=\frac{2n+1}{3}.
\]

Thus, for fixed \(n\), the modular form controls at most one candidate prime. Extending the interpretation to arbitrary \(r\) leads to the moving-character Mellin problem already identified in the project, not to one fixed modular form that can be sieved conventionally.

## 5. A logical obstruction: local zero-set information permits maximal alignment

The gap-polynomial and zero-count results are strong prime by prime, but they do not couple different primes. This can be made rigorous as a model obstruction.

Fix a large \(n\). For each prime \(p\in(n/2,n)\), put

\[
r_p=n-p
\]

and define an artificial reflection-symmetric set

\[
Y_p=\{r_p,p-1-r_p\}.
\]

Discard the \(O(1)\) primes for which a point is an endpoint, the two points coincide, or they are adjacent. Then:

- \(Y_p\subset\{1,\ldots,p-2\}\);
- \(Y_p\) is invariant under \(r\mapsto p-1-r\);
- \(|Y_p|=2\), far stronger than the proved \(O(p^{2/3})\) bound;
- it has no consecutive elements;
- for every fixed gap \(h\ge2\), there is at most one start \(r\) with \(r,r+h\in Y_p\), so it satisfies the numerical gap cap \(1\le3(h-1)\).

Nevertheless \(r_p=n-p\in Y_p\) for every retained prime, and therefore the diagonal hit count is

\[
\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac n{\log n}.
\]

This is not a model for the actual Apéry sets; it is a logical insufficiency result. It proves that no argument using only

```text
|Z_p| bounds,
reflection,
endpoint exclusion,
no adjacent zeros,
and the numerical per-gap root cap
```

can yield even a fixed saving below the ambient coefficient \(1/2\). A proof must use an Apéry-specific relation coupling the **locations** of roots as \(p\) varies.

Even a bounded average

\[
\sum_{p\le x}|Z_p|\ll\pi(x)
\]

does not fix this. Put \(Y_p\) on a positive-density subset of primes and take \(Y_p=\varnothing\) on the rest. The average zero count remains \(O(1)\), while the selected primes may align on one diagonal and give \(c\,n/\log n\) hits for a fixed \(c>0\). Marginal statistics cannot control pointwise cross-prime alignment.

This is the direct-factor version of the “star” obstructions recorded in the campaign notes.

## 6. What the specific Apéry structure presently gives

The project has established or recorded:

1. the Lucas digit law, turning a top divisor into \(n-p\in Z_p\);
2. reflection, endpoint exclusion, no adjacent zeros, and gap-polynomial rigidity;
3. the unconditional local bound \(|Z_p|=O(p^{2/3})\);
4. second-moment control \((\mathrm{HM})_2\), yielding strong density-one results;
5. extremely sparse computation.

In particular, `problems/3.2/bn_bigscan_report.md` records through \(n=2{,}000{,}000\)

\[
\max K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)=3,
\]

with mean approximately \(0.0530\). Over primes through \(2{,}000{,}000\), the mean of \(|Z_p|\) is approximately \(1.0012\), and the observed distribution is close to the Poisson model.

What is not known is a pointwise theorem preventing one diagonal \(r=n-p\) from selecting roots for many different primes. The known structural facts are vertical, prime-by-prime information; the missing theorem is horizontal across primes.

The most directly relevant published factorization result is Luca–Shparlinski's density-one lower bound on \(\omega(b_n)\) and \(P(b_n)\). Delaygue's valuation and \(p\)-Lucas results are also congruential rather than pointwise upper bounds for large prime divisors.

I do not know of a published theorem improving

\[
K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
\le\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac n{\log n}
\]

for every \(n\). The computation suggests a vastly stronger truth—probably \(K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)=n^{o(1)}\), and perhaps a very small polylogarithmic bound—but current arithmetic tools do not prove it.

## 7. What a successful direct sieve would actually require

### Target A: pointwise diagonal sparsity

Prove, for some \(\delta>0\), uniformly for \(n\asymp N\),

\[
\#\{p\in(n/2,n):n-p\in Z_p\}
\ll\frac N{(\log N)^{1+\delta}},
\]

or more strongly

\[
\max_{n\asymp N}K_{\mathrm{top}}(n)\le N^{o(1)}.
\]

This is the cleanest direct theorem and agrees with the project's atom-tail target.

### Target B: a sufficiently high factorial moment

Prove, for one fixed sufficiently large \(k\), the independence-scale estimate for

\[
\sum_m(K_X(m))_k.
\]

The manuscript records that one fixed \(k>6\), together with \(Z_p\ll p^{2/3}\), suffices for the full conjecture. This is a sieve-compatible route, but its content is exactly the missing multi-prime correlation bound.

### Target C: an actual sieve level of distribution

For squarefree products of distinct primes in the relevant block, prove

```text
number of simultaneous Apéry-zero congruences
= expected product density + error,

with the sum of all required errors = o(main scale).
```

That would permit Rosser–Iwaniec or Selberg machinery. Proving this distribution theorem is the breakthrough; applying the sieve afterward is routine.

### Target D: a global factorization theorem

Any of

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)=o(n),
\]

\[
\tau(b_n)=\exp(o(n/\log n)),
\]

or a factorization \(b_n=C_nD_n\) with \(C_n\) free of top-window primes and

\[
\log C_n=(\lambda-o(1))n
\]

would solve the problem. These are valid direct arithmetic targets, but no current hypergeometric or modular-form theorem supplies them.

Targets A or B are the most realistic within the current project because they use the finite-field zero geometry already developed rather than requiring a new integer factorization theory for \(b_n\).

## 8. Final assessment

| Proposed idea | What it yields | Pointwise \(o(n/\log n)\)? |
|---|---|---:|
| Height of \(b_n\) | \((\lambda+o(1))n/\log n\) | No |
| Number of available primes | \((1/2+o(1))n/\log n\) | No, but already proves the requested fixed saving |
| Supercongruence \(b_{mp^s}\) | Valuation control when \(p\mid n\) | No; generic top hits have \(p\nmid n\) |
| Generic divisor bounds | Recover the height scale | No |
| Apéry-specific divisor theorem | Would work if extremely strong | No such theorem known |
| Erdős–Kac heuristic | Predicts a tiny Poisson mean | Heuristic or density-one, not all-\(n\) |
| Rosser–Iwaniec sieve | Requires joint distribution as input | Not independently |
| Fibonacci/Lucas methods | Use strong divisibility and cyclotomic factors | Structure absent for \(b_n\) |
| Modular form \(a_p(f)\) | Controls the unique central candidate | At most one prime per \(n\) |
| Gap polynomial and \(|Z_p|\) | Strong prime-by-prime rigidity | No cross-prime anti-alignment |

The clean conclusion is:

> The requested fixed saving below \(3.52\) is already trivial: the coefficient is at most \(1/2+o(1)\) by prime counting. But this does not advance the conjecture. No currently known direct factorization or classical-sieve theorem yields zero density among the top-window primes. The specific Apéry structure converts the problem into the moving diagonal \(n-p\in Z_p\), and proving that this diagonal cannot align for many primes is exactly the missing cross-prime theorem.

The productive target is therefore:

```text
POINTWISE APÉRY DIAGONAL SIEVE

Prove uniformly as n -> infinity that

  #{p in (n/2,n) : n-p in Z_p} = o(n/log n),

preferably N^{o(1)} on each dyadic shell.
```

That theorem would be genuinely new and would close the top-window problem. The five generic routes do not presently bypass it.
