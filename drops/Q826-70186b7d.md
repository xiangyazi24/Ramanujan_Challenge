# Q826 audit: two reductions and the four-prime remainder

## Executive verdict

The three claims have different statuses.

| Claim | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Claim 1: aggregated short-gap bound `A_(q,C)(n)=o(n/log n)` implies all-index theorem | INVALID as stated | The implication from a bad row to many short gaps is not automatic without a proved packing lemma converting edges into distinct counted pairs after Q8740 deletions. |
| Claim 2: dyadic second-moment bound for centered zero-mask deviations excludes one bad column | INVALID as stated | A second moment of centered quantities does not by itself control uncentered exceptional columns unless the mean term and singular-series contribution are removed at the same scale. |
| Claim 3: identical/shared-prime edge terms are `O(N^(5/3)/log N)` | CONDITIONALLY VALID | The estimate is plausible only with a uniform bound on local multiplicities and a correctly normalized sieve weight; the stated ingredients alone are insufficient. |

The surviving four-prime remainder is therefore not eliminated by the three reductions. It remains the real obstruction.

# 1. Claim 1 audit: aggregated gaps

The proposed quantity is

\[
A_{q,C}(n)=\sum_{\substack{q\mid h\\h\le C\log n}}P_{q;h}(n).
\]

The desired contradiction would be:

1. bad row gives
\[
T\ge \kappa n/\log n;
\]

2. adjacent fully transverse edges generate
\[
\Omega(T)
\]
short gaps with
\[
h\le C\log n;
\]

3. Q8740 removes only
\[
O(\log ^2 n)
\]
aligned pairs.

The failure is step 2. A path with many edges can have repeated gap labels, and the definition of `A_(q,C)` counts gap events, not necessarily independent row obstructions. A valid implication needs a lemma of the form:

\[
\#\{\text{surviving counted }(q,h)\text{-pairs}\}
\ge cT-O_q(\log^2 n).
\]

The existing Q8740 deletion estimate only controls aligned collisions; it does not prove this packing inequality.

Even if the packing lemma were added, the quantifiers must be:

\[
\forall q,C\quad A_{q,C}(n)=o(n/\log n)
\]
with `q,C` fixed before the limit. It cannot be replaced by a bound uniform in growing `C`.

Therefore Claim 1 is not a replacement for pointwise LGTQ-2.

---

# 2. Claim 2 audit: centered short-pair deviations

Let

\[
D_{q,C}(n)
\]

be a centered deviation of zero masks. The proposed estimate is

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}|D(n)|^2=o(N^2/\log^2N).
\]

This only controls fluctuations around the chosen center. A column obstruction may survive if the deterministic mean contributes at the same scale.

The required decomposition is

\[
X(n)=M(n)+D(n),
\]

where `M(n)` includes the singular-series main term and all boundary/moving-window corrections. One needs independently:

\[
\sum_{N<n\le2N}|M(n)|^2=o(N^2/\log^2N)
\]

or a pointwise cancellation statement.

The moving interval indicator is also important. Replacing

\[
1_{p\in I(n)}
\]

by an averaged density loses endpoint correlations. The centering must use the same interval convention as the original zero-mask definition.

Hence Claim 2 is not sufficient as written.

# 3. Claim 3 audit: shared-prime contributions

The decomposition by prime support is correct:

* identical edges: one prime repeated;
* intersecting distinct edges: three distinct primes;
* disjoint edges: four distinct primes.

The proposed estimate

\[
O(N^{5/3}/\log N)
\]
for the first two classes follows heuristically from

\[
|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}
\]

combined with sieve summation.

However, three details are required.

## (a) Uniformity in q and h

The bound must be uniform for all

\[
q\mid h\le C\log N.
\]

A hidden dependence on the number of residue visits can destroy the logarithmic saving.

## (b) Centering compatibility

The zero-mask variables must be centered before applying the second moment. Otherwise the diagonal term contains the singular-series mean.

## (c) Dimension-two-thirds sieve

The sieve estimate must be stated in the form actually needed:

\[
\sum_{p\le N} |Z_p|^2 w_p
\ll N^{5/3}/\log N,
\]

with the weights `w_p` encoding the number of admissible residue visits. The phrase "dimension-2/3 upper-bound sieve" is not itself a theorem.

Thus Claim 3 is conditionally valid after these repairs.

# 4. The four-prime remainder

After removing identical and one-prime-overlap terms, the remaining contribution is a sum over

\[
(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)
\]

with four distinct primes.

The previous reductions do not see the essential correlation:

\[
Z_{p_1}Z_{p_2}Z_{p_3}Z_{p_4}.
\]

A genuine gain requires one of the following new inputs:

## Option A: four-point cancellation

Prove a bound of the form

\[
\sum_{p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4}
\mathrm{Corr}(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)
=o(N^2/\log^2N).
\]

This must use the canonical row structure, not only marginal prime counts.

## Option B: path-level determinant cancellation

For a path with edges

\[
e_1,\ldots,e_m,
\]

write the joint zero-mask contribution as a transfer product:

\[
\mathbf v_0T_1T_2\cdots T_m\mathbf v_m.
\]

A useful result would show a spectral loss whenever the path has many independent four-prime blocks. Pairwise covariance estimates cannot give this.

## Option C: singular-series rigidity

Show that a four-prime configuration forces a forbidden local pattern in the canonical recurrence output. This would convert analytic correlation into combinatorial exclusion.

No such theorem is currently supplied by the reductions above.

# 6. Final audit of the proposed saturation model

The saturation model must be checked against three invariants:

| Property | Preserved? |
|---|---|
| one common B | YES only if all primes are saturated against the same original row identity |
| full path overlaps | NO in the current reduction if edges are collapsed independently |
| canonical recurrence outputs | NO unless transfer constraints are retained before saturation |

The first dropped assumption is:

> saturation treats local edge constraints as independently composable and forgets the path-level recurrence coupling between neighboring vertices.

That loss is fatal because the possible gain is precisely in the overlap of adjacent constraints.

# Conclusion

The proposed three reductions do not yet prove the required all-index theorem.

* Claim 1 needs a new edge-packing lemma and remains weaker than pointwise LGTQ-2.
* Claim 2 needs explicit treatment of the mean and moving indicators.
* Claim 3 can remove diagonal and shared-prime terms after normalization.
* The remaining four-prime term is the actual bottleneck.

A successful attack must produce a genuine four-prime/path-level cancellation or rigidity statement. Renaming the remainder as a conjecture would only hide the missing theorem.
