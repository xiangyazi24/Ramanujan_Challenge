Q6679 - structure of Disc(N_h), local-strip asymptotics, and the Eisenstein filter

# Executive verdict

There are three main conclusions.

1. The strip decomposition changes the proposed superfactorial coefficient by a
   factor of nine. There are three roots in each strip, hence nine differences
   between each pair of strips, and every difference is squared in the
   discriminant. If n=h-1, the inter-strip main term is therefore

     18 sum_{1<=i<j<=n} log(j-i)
       = 18 log G(n+1),

   where G is the Barnes G-function, not 2 log G(n+1).

2. After including the leading coefficient L_h=lc(N_h), the robust two-term
   asymptotic is

     log |Disc(N_h)|
       = 9 n^2 log n
         + (6 log lambda - 27/2) n^2
         + O(n log n),

     lambda = 17+12 sqrt(2).

   Under the expected uniform two-sided cell/Jost expansion, the error sharpens
   to an explicit linear local-lattice correction plus O(log n):

     log |Disc(N_h)|
       = (6n-2) log L_h
         + 18 log G(n+1)
         + kappa_cell n
         + O(log n).

   The limiting strip triple is not the three roots of P. Those are the
   decoupled deformation start. The endpoint local triple is the set of three
   zeros in one fundamental cell of the two-sided limiting Evans/Jost function

     K_infty(z)=phi(-z)phi(z)+z^6 gamma(-z)gamma(z).

3. The centered reflection descent gives an exact discriminant identity. It is
   the useful valuation theorem for the Eisenstein campaign. Put

     d=3h-3,
     epsilon_h=1 if h is even, and 0 if h is odd,
     m=(d-epsilon_h)/2,

   and normalize

     H_h(T)=2^d N_h((T-h-1)/2)
           =g_h T^{epsilon_h} M_h(T^2),

   where M_h is primitive, with leading coefficient a_h and constant e_h.
   Then

     2^{d(d-1)} Disc(N_h)
       =g_h^{2d-2} (-4)^m a_h e_h^{1+2 epsilon_h}
        Disc(M_h)^2.                                      (E)

   Consequently, at every odd prime ell,

     v_ell Disc(N_h)
       =(2d-2)v_ell(g_h)+v_ell(a_h)
        +(1+2epsilon_h)v_ell(e_h)
        +2v_ell Disc(M_h).                               (V)

   Thus the entire Montes discriminant contribution is even. Valuation-one
   primes are forced to come from the much cheaper centered endpoint
   coefficients a_h,e_h, with Montes used only to certify that Disc(M_h) is an
   ell-adic unit.

There is no known Schur-type product formula for this family. The precise
obstruction is that both the diagonal and the off-diagonal of the tridiagonal
pencil depend on X. Differentiation does not reduce N_h' modulo N_h to a scalar
multiple of N_{h-1}; it retains derivative states. The adjacent-resultant law is
therefore insufficient to close the derivative resultant.

# 1. Exact root-cluster factorization

Let

  n=h-1,
  d=3n,
  L_h=lc(N_h).

By the banked localization theorem, the roots can be labeled

  alpha_{i,a},
  1<=i<=n,
  1<=a<=3,

so that alpha_{i,1},alpha_{i,2},alpha_{i,3} lie in the strip

  -i-1 < Re X < -i.

Put the strip center and local offsets

  c_i=-i-1/2,
  u_{i,a}=alpha_{i,a}-c_i.

Then

  alpha_{i,a}-alpha_{j,b}
    =(j-i)+(u_{i,a}-u_{j,b}).                            (1.1)

The standard polynomial discriminant is

  Disc(N_h)
    =L_h^{2d-2}
     product_{(i,a)<(j,b)}(alpha_{i,a}-alpha_{j,b})^2.

Split it into the intra-strip and inter-strip pieces:

  Disc(N_h)
    =L_h^{6n-2}
     product_{i=1}^n Delta_i
     product_{1<=i<j<=n} D_{i,j},                        (1.2)

where

  Delta_i
    =product_{1<=a<b<=3}(u_{i,a}-u_{i,b})^2,             (1.3)

  D_{i,j}
    =product_{a=1}^3 product_{b=1}^3
       ((j-i)+(u_{i,a}-u_{j,b}))^2.                     (1.4)

Equation (1.2) is exact. The squarefreeness theorem says every Delta_i and every
D_{i,j} is nonzero.

The number of inter-strip differences for a fixed pair i<j is 3*3=9. After
squaring, its leading separation contribution is

  (j-i)^18.

Thus

  D_{i,j}
    =(j-i)^18 R_{i,j},                                  (1.5)

  R_{i,j}
    =product_{a,b}
      (1+(u_{i,a}-u_{j,b})/(j-i))^2.                    (1.6)

Taking absolute logarithms gives the exact decomposition

  log|Disc(N_h)|
    =(6n-2)log|L_h|
     +18 sum_{i<j}log(j-i)
     +sum_i log|Delta_i|
     +sum_{i<j}log|R_{i,j}|.                            (1.7)

Since

  product_{1<=i<j<=n}(j-i)
    =product_{r=1}^{n-1} r^{n-r}
    =1! 2! ... (n-1)!
    =G(n+1),                                             (1.8)

we obtain

  log|Disc(N_h)|
    =(6n-2)log|L_h|
     +18 log G(n+1)
     +E_local(h),                                        (1.9)

where

  E_local(h)
    =sum_i log|Delta_i|+sum_{i<j}log|R_{i,j}|.           (1.10)

This is the correct exact starting point. The proposed coefficient 2 in front
of the superfactorial is the one-root-per-strip coefficient; the present family
has three roots per strip and therefore coefficient 18.

# 2. The limiting local triple

The local triple is determined by the endpoint a=1 pencil, not by P alone.

Fix the pole -j and write

  X=-j+z,
  r=j-1,
  s=h-j.

The exact two-block identity banked in the earlier cell analysis is

  f_h(-j+z)
    =z^{-3}F_r(-z)F_s(z)+z^3 G_r(-z)G_s(z).             (2.1)

A zero of N_h away from the pole is therefore a zero of

  K_{r,s}(z)
    =F_r(-z)F_s(z)+z^6 G_r(-z)G_s(z).                  (2.2)

Let b_n be the Apéry solution. The two-sided Poincare-Perron/Jost expansion has
the form

  F_n(z)/b_n -> phi(z),
  G_n(z)/b_n -> gamma(z),                               (2.3)

locally uniformly, together with the derivatives needed for simple-root
continuation. Hence, when r,s both tend to infinity,

  K_{r,s}(z)/(b_r b_s)
    -> K_infty(z)
     :=phi(-z)phi(z)+z^6 gamma(-z)gamma(z).              (2.4)

Let

  zeta_1,zeta_2,zeta_3

be the three simple zeros of K_infty in one fundamental cell, say

  0<Re z<1,

with one real zero and one conjugate pair. Put

  eta_a=zeta_a-1/2.                                     (2.5)

Then the limiting intra-strip discriminant is

  Delta_infty
    =product_{a<b}(eta_a-eta_b)^2.                      (2.6)

This is the correct local constant. It is not

  Disc(P)=-459.

The three roots of P at the decoupled deformation parameter are only the
starting triple

  0,
  +/- i sqrt(51)/34

relative to the cell midpoint. Turning on the off-diagonal coupling deforms
that triple to the three zeros of K_infty.

A useful infinite-lattice correction is obtained by comparing two copies of the
limiting triple k cells apart:

  R_k^{infty}
    =product_{a,b}
      |1+(eta_a-eta_b)/k|^2.                            (2.7)

Because

  sum_{a,b}(eta_a-eta_b)=0,

one has

  log R_k^{infty}=O(k^{-2}).                            (2.8)

Thus the convergent linear constant is

  kappa_cell
    =log|Delta_infty|
     +sum_{k>=1}log R_k^{infty}.                        (2.9)

Under a uniform bulk-root expansion with summable edge errors, (1.10) becomes

  E_local(h)=kappa_cell n+O(log n).                     (2.10)

The O(log n) term is natural: although log R_k=O(k^{-2}), the weighted sum
sum_{k<n}(n-k)log R_k contains a secondary sum k log R_k of harmonic size.

Important status distinction:

  - The strip count and squarefreeness are banked exact theorems.

  - Formula (1.9) is exact.

  - Formula (2.10) requires the uniform two-sided root convergence, including
    quantitative control near both ends. The earlier transfer asymptotics make
    this the right statement, but the complete all-h error estimate is not yet
    in the banked proof package.

  L_1=1,

  L_h

  log G(n+1)
    =(n^2/2)log n-(3/4)n^2

  log|Disc(N_h)|

  log|Disc(N_h)|

# 5. Exact centered discriminant identity

This is the main valuation theorem.

Let

  d=3h-3,
  epsilon=1 if h is even and 0 if h is odd,
  m=(d-epsilon)/2.

Define the integral centered polynomial

  H_h(T)=2^d N_h((T-h-1)/2).                            (5.1)

Reflection makes H_h even for odd h and odd for even h. Write its primitive
square descent as

  H_h(T)=g_h T^epsilon M_h(T^2),                        (5.2)

where

  M_h(Y)=a_h Y^m+...+e_h

is primitive over Z and g_h>0 is the removed content/scalar.

The affine change in (5.1) gives

  Disc(H_h)=2^{d(d-1)} Disc(N_h).                       (5.3)

For a general degree-m polynomial M(Y)=aY^m+...+e, direct root pairing gives

  Disc(M(T^2))
    =(-4)^m a e Disc(M)^2,                              (5.4)

  Disc(T M(T^2))
    =(-4)^m a e^3 Disc(M)^2.                            (5.5)

The factor a is essential; formulas omitting it are valid only for monic M.
Scaling by g_h contributes g_h^{2d-2}. Therefore

  2^{d(d-1)} Disc(N_h)
    =g_h^{2d-2}(-4)^m a_h e_h^{1+2epsilon}
      Disc(M_h)^2.                                      (5.6)

This proves (E).

At an odd prime ell,

  v_ell Disc(N_h)
    =(2d-2)v_ell(g_h)
     +v_ell(a_h)
     +(1+2epsilon)v_ell(e_h)
     +2v_ell Disc(M_h).                                 (5.7)

At ell=2,

  v_2 Disc(N_h)
    =(2d-2)v_2(g_h)
     +2m+v_2(a_h)
     +(1+2epsilon)v_2(e_h)
     +2v_2 Disc(M_h)
     -d(d-1).                                           (5.8)

The odd-prime squareclass is especially simple:

  v_ell Disc(N_h)
    ==v_ell(a_h e_h) mod 2.                             (5.9)

The content exponent 2d-2 and the Montes discriminant exponent 2 are even.

This explains all three small cases:

  h=2:
    M_2(Y)=17Y+3,
    a_2=17,
    e_2=3,
    epsilon=1,
    Disc(N_2)=-3^3*17.

  h=3:
    a_3=1155=3*5*7*11,
    e_3=-1600,
    and the remaining factor is a square.

  h=4:
    a_4=9809=17*577,
    e_4=-120339=-3^3*4457,
    and the remaining factor is again a square.

# 6. Consequences for ell-adic valuations and Montes

## 6.1 What Montes actually supplies

If M_h is irreducible and theta is one of its roots, then

  Disc(M_h)
    =Disc(O_{Q(theta)}) [O_{Q(theta)}:Z[theta]]^2.       (6.1)

At a fixed ell, a complete Montes computation supplies

  - the factorization of M_h over Q_ell;

  - the local index valuation;

  - the residual degrees and ramification indices;

  - the local different/discriminant contribution.

If M_h is reducible, the polynomial discriminant splits into factor
discriminants and squared pairwise resultants. Either way, all of it enters
(5.7) multiplied by 2.

Thus the Montes campaign is not the source of the odd squareclass of
Disc(N_h). It determines the even-depth part.

## 6.2 Banked local patterns

The presently banked patterns are first-layer structures, not a closed formula
for v_ell Disc(N_h):

  - At ell=3, N_h is an exact Frobenius cube modulo 3 before primitive
    normalization. Hence the reduction is maximally inseparable and the
    3-adic discriminant valuation is large. Ordinary first-order Newton polygons
    are deliberately blind; higher Montes types are required.

  - At ell=2, the four-step degeneration and periodic content loss force
    separate residue classes of h. Again, the first reduction frequently
    vanishes and the next 2-adic layer is decisive.

  - At ell=17, the leading coefficient has the Lucas valuation law banked in
    the qI/Montes campaign. That constrains a_h and g_h, but by itself does not
    determine the residual discriminant valuation.

The coefficient recurrence is periodic in h modulo ell, so an ell-step transfer
matrix exists. This does not make v_ell Disc(N_h) periodic in h: polynomial
degrees grow, type depths can grow, and the index depends on higher congruence
layers. Periodicity of a complete type tree would be a new theorem, not a formal
consequence of coefficient periodicity.

## 6.3 Valuation-one filter

For an odd prime ell, (5.7) gives a cheap exact prefilter.

If h is even, epsilon=1. A simple divisor of e_h contributes 3, not 1. Therefore

  v_ell Disc(N_h)=1

can occur only if

  v_ell(a_h)=1,
  ell does not divide g_h e_h Disc(M_h).                 (6.2)

If h is odd, epsilon=0. Then valuation one can occur if exactly one endpoint
has a simple ell-factor:

  v_ell(a_h)+v_ell(e_h)=1,
  ell does not divide g_h Disc(M_h).                    (6.3)

Thus the machine does not need to factor Disc(N_h) first. It should factor the
much smaller endpoint integers a_h,e_h and then ask Montes only whether
Disc(M_h) is an ell-adic unit.

This is the requested prediction mechanism for candidate pairs (h,ell).

# 7. Eisenstein protocol for W_h

The zero-fiber law from Q6672 has the shape

  mathcal V_h(0)
    =-27 L_h^4 F_h^2 Disc(N_h),                         (7.1)

up to the project content/sign normalization, where

  F_h=product_{r=0}^{h-1}(r!)^3 b_r.

The L_h^4 and F_h^2 factors are squares at every odd prime. The only odd
squareclass comes from 3 and from Disc(N_h), hence ultimately from a_h e_h by
(5.9).

A scalable ordinary-Eisenstein campaign should run as follows.

1. Construct H_h and its primitive descent

     H_h=g_h T^epsilon M_h(T^2).

2. Factor only a_h and e_h.

3. Generate candidate odd primes using (6.2)-(6.3).

4. At each candidate ell, run a local Montes/discriminant-unit check for M_h.
   Reject ell immediately if

     v_ell Disc(M_h)>0.

5. Form the primitive W_h and test the actual Eisenstein conditions:

     ell does not divide lc(W_h),
     ell divides every nonleading coefficient,
     ell^2 does not divide W_h(0).                       (7.2)

6. Record content removal explicitly. The raw constant valuation is not the
   primitive constant valuation.

7. If ordinary Eisenstein fails, test the shifted polynomials W_h(S+c) for a
   small certified set of c modulo ell, then the complete Newton polygon and
   residual polynomials.

The endpoint filter is necessary, not sufficient. There is no reason a prime
that ramifies simply in N_h must divide every interior coefficient of W_h.
Ordinary Eisenstein is therefore expected to be sporadic; the main benefit of
(5.7) is that it makes the search cheap and exhaustive.

# 8. Is a closed product formula plausible?

## 8.1 The Schur mechanism

Schur's classical resultant formula concerns recurrences of the form

  r_n(x)=(a_n x+b_n)r_{n-1}(x)-c_n r_{n-2}(x),          (8.1)

with a_n,b_n,c_n independent of x. The derivative can be reduced, using a
ladder/differential identity, to a controlled combination of neighboring
polynomials. The adjacent resultant then closes into a product of recurrence
coefficients.

Modern extensions by Ulas, Turaj, and Matsumura retain an analogous closure:
a prescribed recurrence plus a derivative/contiguity identity reduces the
derivative resultant to already controlled adjacent resultants.

The Dilcher-Stolarsky Chebyshev formulas are even more specialized: they exploit
constant-coefficient Chebyshev recurrences and linear forms in two neighboring
Chebyshev polynomials.

## 8.2 Exact obstruction here

The Apéry continuant is

  N_{h+1}
    =P(X+h)N_h-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}.                          (8.2)

Both coefficients depend on X, and their degrees are 3 and 6. Differentiating
gives

  N_{h+1}'
    =P'(X+h)N_h+P(X+h)N_h'
     -6(X+h)^5N_{h-1}-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}'.                 (8.3)

Modulo N_{h+1}, the right side still contains

  N_h',
  N_{h-1}'.

It does not collapse to

  scalar(X,h)*N_h

or to a fixed linear combination of N_h,N_{h-1}. Therefore the exact adjacent
resultant

  Res(N_h,N_{h-1})

cannot be converted into Res(N_h,N_h') by Schur's one-line induction.

The tridiagonal pencil says the same thing geometrically. At a simple root
alpha,

  N_h'(alpha)
    =product(nonzero eigenvalues of J(alpha))
      * v_alpha^* J'(alpha) v_alpha,                    (8.4)

up to normalization of the null vector v_alpha. For an ordinary Jacobi matrix
xI-A, one has J'(x)=I, and Christoffel-Darboux/norming-constant identities close
the product over roots. Here J'(x) is a nonconstant tridiagonal matrix; the
quadratic form in (8.4) depends on the root eigenvector. The product of these
norming factors is precisely the missing information.

Thus the literature gives analogies and techniques, but no existing theorem
matches (8.2) directly.

Relevant primary references for the comparison are:

  - K. Dilcher and K. B. Stolarsky, "Resultants and discriminants of Chebyshev
    and related polynomials", Trans. AMS 357 (2005), 965-981,
    DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-04-03687-6.

  - H. Matsumura, "A generalization of formulas for the discriminants of
    quasi-orthogonal polynomials with applications to hypergeometric
    polynomials", arXiv:2303.15934; Ramanujan J. 64 (2024), 835-856.

Neither theorem supplies a product formula for a polynomial-dependent
tridiagonal pencil of the present type.

## 8.3 What a genuine closed form would require

One of the following new identities would suffice:

  [DERIVATIVE-LADDER]
  q_h(X)N_h'(X)
    =A_h(X)N_h(X)+B_h(X)N_{h-1}(X)

with B_h having an explicitly evaluable resultant;

or

  [PENCIL-NORM-PRODUCT]
  product_{N_h(alpha)=0} v_alpha^*J'(alpha)v_alpha

has a closed product expression in the recurrence coefficients.

No such identity is presently banked. The exact formula (5.6) is therefore the
best structural replacement: it isolates the non-square endpoint factors and
pushes the remaining arithmetic into the lower-degree centered discriminant
Disc(M_h).

# 10. Final answers

(a) Least-confident step.

The least-confident step is the sharpened local asymptotic

  E_local(h)=kappa_cell(h-1)+O(log h).

The strip theorem and squarefreeness do not alone imply a uniform lower bound on
all intra-strip separations or summable convergence of the root offsets. The
limiting Evans/Jost function and the coefficient 18 of the superfactorial are
clear, but a proof of the linear correction requires a quantitative root
continuation theorem uniform in the two block lengths and near both edges.

The exact identities (1.2), (5.6), and the small-case factorizations do not depend
on that analytic step.

(b) Likely blind spot.

The main blind spot is conflating three different discriminants:

  1. the standard polynomial discriminant Disc(N_h);

  2. the derivative resultant Res(N_h,N_h'), which differs by the leading
     coefficient and a sign;

  3. the number-field discriminant computed from a defining polynomial, which
     differs by the square of the index.

The centered identity shows why this matters: Disc(M_h) appears squared, while
the odd squareclass comes from the centered leading and constant coefficients.
A campaign that factors only the raw discriminant, or reads a Montes field
discriminant as the polynomial discriminant, will misidentify the
valuation-one/Eisenstein candidates.

A secondary blind spot is the factor-of-nine error in the strip superfactorial:
three roots per strip create nine cross differences per strip pair before the
Vandermonde square.
