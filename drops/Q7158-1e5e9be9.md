# P32 anchored long-short continuant R2 audit

## 1. Setup and the exact continuant identity

Let
\[
D_n(x)=N_n(x),\qquad D_0(x)=0,\quad D_1(x)=1,
\]
with the Apéry divided recurrence giving a second-order continuant recurrence in the shift variable. The relevant transfer matrices are
\[
M_j(x)=\begin{pmatrix}P(x+j)&-(x+j)^6\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
\binom{N_{x+1+h}(-1)}{N_{x+h}(-1)}
= M_{x+1}(-1)\cdots M_{x+h}(-1)
\binom{N_{x+1}(-1)}{N_x(-1)}.
\]

The continuant addition formula is the standard determinant identity. Writing the solution with initial column \((N_{x+1}(-1),N_x(-1))\), one obtains an unconditional integer identity of the form
\[
N_{x+1+h}(-1)
= N_{x+2}(-1)N_h(x)+E_{x,h}N_{x+1}(-1),
\]
where the error coefficient is an integer polynomial/continuant in x of degree controlled by h. The sign is determined by the determinant convention; with the convention above the coefficient of the first term is positive and the determinant contribution gives
\[
E_{x,h}= -\,\widetilde N_h(x)
\]
for the companion continuant \(\widetilde N_h\). Thus the exact statement is
\[
\boxed{
N_{x+1+h}(-1)=N_{x+2}(-1)N_h(x)-N_{x+1}(-1)\widetilde N_h(x).
}
\]

No specialization to a zero has been used.

## 2. Reduction modulo the anchored zero

If
\[
N_{x+1}(-1)=0,
\]
then the second term disappears and
\[
N_{x+1+h}(-1)=N_{x+2}(-1)N_h(x).
\]

Using
\[
N_{x+1}(-1)=(x!)^3b_x,
\]
we get, modulo a prime p with x<p,
\[
b_x=0\pmod p \implies N_{x+1}(-1)=0\pmod p.
\]
Therefore
\[
N_{x+1+h}(-1)\equiv N_{x+2}(-1)N_h(x)\pmod p.
\]

Since
\[
N_{x+2}(-1)=(x+1)!^3b_{x+1},
\]
the extra factor is a neighboring Apéry value, not a new independent object.

## 3. Comparison of gcds

For p>x+h,
\[
(x!)^3,((x+h)!)^3
\]
are p-units. Hence
\[
p\mid \gcd(N_{x+1}(-1),N_{x+1+h}(-1))
\]
is equivalent to
\[
p\mid \gcd(b_x,b_{x+h}).
\]

The continuant identity gives instead
\[
p\mid b_x \implies
p\mid N_{x+1+h}(-1) \Longleftrightarrow
p\mid N_{x+2}(-1)N_h(x).
\]
This splits into

1. the neighboring-zero branch \(p\mid b_{x+1}\);
2. the genuine gap branch \(p\mid N_h(x)\).

The second branch is exactly the usual short return condition. The identity does not remove it.

## 4. Three-gap chains

Suppose
\[
b_r=b_{r+a}=b_{r+a+b}=b_{r+a+b+c}=0\pmod p.
\]
Applying the identity at each anchored point gives factors
\[
N_a(r),\ N_b(r+a),\ N_c(r+a+b).
\]
However these are merely the original short continuant conditions rewritten after multiplying by factorial units.

The proposed compression would require a new gcd relation such as
\[
gcd(N_{r+1}(-1),N_h(r))
\]
being substantially smaller than the corresponding
\[
gcd(b_r,b_{r+h}).
\]
But for p>r+h,
\[
N_{r+1}(-1)=(r!)^3b_r
\]
and factorials are invertible modulo p, so the two conditions have identical support.

Therefore no H-saving or x-saving parameter reduction follows.

## 5. Height and content analysis

The identity is useful over integers because it separates a long endpoint from a short continuant. The coefficient sizes satisfy roughly
\[
\log |N_h(x)|=O(h\log(x+h)),
\]
so the resultant/content bounds are not improved: the height still contains the full long parameter x and short parameter h.

The factorization does not lower the dimension of the gcd problem. It only changes coordinates:

\[
\text{moving gcd of Apéry values}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{endpoint continuant gcd}.
\]

## 6. Localization argument showing the no-go

After localizing at p>x+h,
\[
(x!)^3\in \mathbb F_p^\times.
\]
Hence
\[
N_{x+1}(-1)=0
\iff b_x=0.
\]
The map from Apéry zeros to endpoint continuant zeros is therefore an isomorphism of zero conditions. Any claimed new congruence derived only from the addition identity would descend to a statement already true for arbitrary second-order recurrences with the same continuant representation.

Consequently the anchored identity cannot distinguish the Apéry recurrence from a generic continuant sequence.

## 7. Final verdict

The anchored long-short identity is exact and useful as bookkeeping:

* it gives a clean factorization of a long endpoint after conditioning on an anchored zero;
* it separates the neighbor-zero branch from the short-return branch;
* it can simplify notation in gcd computations.

But it does **not** provide a new arithmetic constraint.

The missing ingredient remains a genuine estimate on
\[
\#\{r: b_r=b_{r+h}=0\pmod p\}
\]
or higher-order moving gcd/codegree sums. The continuant addition law alone is tautological after localization and cannot save an H or x factor.
