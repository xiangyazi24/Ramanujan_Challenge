# Shifted-carrier implication audit

## 1. Correct Kummer reduction

Let

$$q=p^e\mid m,\qquad n=Nq+t,\qquad m=Kq,$$

and let $j$ range over the shifted Newton window $h\le j<h+M$. The only possible rows with zero $p$-valuation in the shifted content modulo $q$ are those satisfying

$$j\equiv t\pmod q.$$

Writing such a row as

$$j=aq+t,$$

and using the complementary form

$$W(n,h,j)=(-1)^{h+M-1-j}{n-h\choose j-h}{n-j-1\choose h+M-1-j},$$

with $m=H-h+1$ and $E=h+M-1$, the second binomial has lower entry $E-j$ and complement

$$ (E-j)+(m-1)=n-j-1. $$

Therefore the exact digit stripping gives

$$v_p(W(n,h,aq+t))
=v_p{\left(K+\left\lfloor\frac{M-1}{q}\right\rfloor\right)\choose N-a}
 +v_p{N-a-1\choose K-1}.$$

All rows with $j\not\equiv t\pmod q$ have positive valuation and cannot be the obstruction rows for a $p^e$-content argument.

## 2. What would be needed for the implication

The desired statement is

$$p^e\mid A(n,h)\Longrightarrow p^e\mid b_{n\bmod p^e}.$$

Since

$$A(n,h)=\gcd(\Gamma(n,h),m),$$

this means that every row

$$W(n,h,j)b_j$$

must be divisible by $p^e$ whenever $q=p^e\mid m$.

For the rows $j=aq+t$, the Kummer formula reduces the question to whether the valuation deficit in the binomial coefficient product can be compensated by the Apéry valuation of

$$b_{aq+t}.$$

The shifted problem therefore requires a suffix ladder, not only the unit-row argument. The ladder would have to prove:

$$v_p(W(n,h,aq+t))+v_p(b_{aq+t})\ge e$$

for every admissible $a$, and then transfer the resulting divisibility to $b_t$ through the Mellit--Vlasenko block factorization.

## 3. Suffix-row ladder

The rows with $a=N$ are the top suffix rows. Their second factor is

$$ { -1\choose K-1},$$

which is zero outside the combinatorial range and therefore they do not generate a new unit obstruction.

For $a<N$, the two Kummer terms are exactly the carry counts of

$$\binom{K+\lfloor(M-1)/q\rfloor}{N-a}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\binom{N-a-1}{K-1}.

A successful ladder would descend in $a$:

$$a=N-1,N-2,\ldots,0,$$

showing that whenever the binomial product loses a factor of $p$, the corresponding Apéry block has the missing factor. The final step would identify the terminal block with

$$b_t=b_{n\bmod p^e}.

## 4. Audit result

The implication is **not proved by the current shifted data**. The observed cases

$$
(302,31,121,11),\ldots,(786,31,363,11)
$$

show compatibility with

$$p^2\mid b_{n\bmod p^2}\quad\text{and}\quad p^2\mid b_n,$$

but they only test $e=2$ and a finite endpoint range.

The missing theorem is the full suffix-row ladder. The Mellit--Vlasenko block theorem for the unshifted content cannot be applied directly because the shifted window changes the admissible digit prefixes and the second binomial carries the shifted complement.

No exact counterexample is known from the supplied scan; however, no fully quantified implication can be claimed without proving the ladder.

## 5. Conclusion

The shifted analogue reduces to a precise finite statement:

$$\forall a\text{ with }aq+t\in[h,h+M),\quad
v_p(W(n,h,aq+t))+v_p(b_{aq+t})\ge e,$$

followed by a Mellit--Vlasenko descent from $b_{aq+t}$ to $b_t$.

The endpoint handling is now clear: rows outside $j\equiv t\pmod q$ are automatically non-unit; rows inside that congruence class require the suffix-row ladder. The current evidence supports the conjecture but does not constitute a proof of

$$p^e\mid A(n,h)\Rightarrow p^e\mid b_{n\bmod p^e}.$$
