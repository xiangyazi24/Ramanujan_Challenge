# Q1112: shifted Newton carrier large-prime support audit

## 1. Setup

Let
\[
M=\lfloor n/2\rfloor,
\qquad
W_{n,h,j}=(-1)^{h+M-1-j}\binom{n-h}{j-h}\binom{n-j-1}{h+M-1-j},
\]
for \(h\le j<h+M\), and
\[
\Gamma_{n,h}=\gcd_j W_{n,h,j}b_j.
\]
Assume throughout \(p\ge 7\), \(p^2>n\), and write
\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]
Since \(p^2>n\), every relevant binomial has at most two base-p digits and Kummer reduces the valuation question to a single carry test.

The proposed exact statement is not correct as stated: the observed threshold \(h=\lceil r/2\rceil+1\) for even \(q\) is a sufficient pattern in the scanned range, but it is not the invariant description. The invariant is the p-unit support of the shifted Newton row.

## 2. Exact p-unit condition for W

Put
\[
a=j-h,\qquad 0\le a<M.
\]
Then
\[
W_{n,h,j}=(-1)^{M-1-a}\binom{n-h}{a}\binom{n-h-a-1}{M-1-a}.
\]
The first factor is p-unit exactly when subtraction of \(a\) from \(n-h\) produces no borrow in base p.

Write
\[
h=up+s,\quad 0\le s<p.
\]
For the tested range one has \(h<p\), so \(u=0\) and the relevant effect is the low digit shift \(r-h\). The first binomial gives the condition
\[
a\le r-h
\]
when \(h\le r\), with the complementary no-borrow case after the forced low-digit borrow when \(h>r\).

The second binomial contributes an independent Kummer condition. Its top is
\[
n-h-a-1,
\]
and its bottom is
\[
M-1-a.
\]
The p-unit set is therefore
\[
U_{n,h}=\{j=h+a:0\le a<M,\;\kappa_p(n-h,a)=0,\;\kappa_p(n-h-a-1,M-1-a)=0\},
\]
where \(\kappa_p(x,y)\) denotes the number of base-p carries in the addition form of Kummer's theorem.

This is the exact answer. Any closed form must be derived from these two simultaneous carry constraints; the first binomial alone gives the wrong support when h introduces a borrow.

## 3. Consequence for Gamma

For a large prime \(p\), because all coefficients outside \(U_{n,h}\) are divisible by p,
\[
p\mid \Gamma_{n,h}
\]
if and only if
\[
p\mid b_j\quad\text{for every }j\in U_{n,h}.
\]
Thus the problem is a support intersection problem, not a single-index problem.

The empirical phenomenon can be restated as:

- for odd q, \(U_{n,0}\) contains the unique large-prime obstruction index \(r\);
- for even q, the shifted rows can remove all smaller residue classes until the first surviving unit index is the observed \(r\)-class at \(h=\lceil r/2\rceil+1\).

However, the implication
\[
p\mid b_r,\ q\text{ even}\implies p\mid\Gamma_{n,\lceil r/2\rceil+1}
\]
is not a theorem from the support calculation alone. It requires proving
\[
U_{n,\lceil r/2\rceil+1}\subseteq\{j:p\mid b_j\}.
\]
The Kummer description shows why the finite data is plausible, but does not collapse the support to the single residue index without an additional Apéry-Lukas argument.

## 4. Does p | Gamma imply p | b_r?

For p^2>n, this is also not automatic from Newton support. What is true is:

\[
p\mid\Gamma_{n,h}
\Longrightarrow
p\mid b_j\ \forall j\in U_{n,h}.
\]

To conclude \(p\mid b_r\), one needs a theorem that every nonempty large-prime unit set contains an index congruent to r modulo p and that all other surviving indices are Apéry-equivalent to r modulo p.

The required ingredients would be:

1. exact Kummer classification of \(U_{n,h}\);
2. Lucas reduction
\[
b_{ap+r}\equiv b_a b_r\pmod p;
\]
3. proof that the remaining unit indices all have a factor \(b_r\).

The scans strongly support this, but the implication is not proved by the Newton algebra alone.

## 5. Aggregate carrier

Define
\[
C_n=\operatorname{lcm}_{0\le h\le\lceil n/2\rceil}\Gamma_{n,h}.
\]
The lcm combines all shifted rows, so its large-prime radical is the union of all shifted supports. The data suggests that
\[
\operatorname{rad}_{>\sqrt n}(C_n)
\]
is exactly the all-q target radical.

The direction that is immediate is:

- every target prime appearing in some shifted row appears in C_n;
- finite scans show no extra primes for bounded h.

The missing proof obligation is again the exact support theorem for all h. A proof must show that every large prime surviving any shift is an Apéry target prime and that every target prime has at least one shift exposing it.

## 6. Height/divisibility bounds

There is no evidence for a bound substantially below exponential growth without new cancellation. The crude bound follows from
\[
|W_{n,h,j}|\le 2^n
\]
and the Apéry growth
\[
 b_j=\exp(O(j)),
\]
so
\[
\log C_n=O(n).
\]

An exact sharper bound would require exploiting gcd cancellation across rows. The shifted construction may reduce the radical while leaving the archimedean height exponential. The available data does not imply a polynomial or subexponential height theorem.

## Conclusion

The correct theorem-level formulation is:

\[
\boxed{p\mid\Gamma_{n,h}\iff p\mid b_j\text{ for all }j\in U_{n,h},}
\]

with \(U_{n,h}\) defined by the simultaneous Kummer carry conditions from both binomial factors.

The observed rule

- odd q: h=0,
- even q: h=\lceil r/2\rceil+1,

is a strong experimental corollary of the support geometry, but not yet an exact theorem. The remaining mathematical step is an Apéry-Lukas collapse theorem for every shifted unit set. The characteristic-zero aggregate C_n plausibly has the full target radical, but an exact radical identity and any height improvement beyond \(\exp(O(n))\) require that additional support theorem.
