# 6. Sustained recurrence attack

The remaining question is whether the second zero can be converted into a source theorem which bounds the foreign support in `(5.9)`.  The following derivation tests the literal continuant mechanisms.

## 6.1 Local two-zero coordinates and the exact slopes

For a candidate foreign prime \(p'\), put

\[
a'=n-p'.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
p'=n-a'
\tag{6.2}
\]

and

\[
r'=B-6p'
=B-6(n-a')
=6a'+7t+6.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the two local zero tests are exactly

\[
p'\mid b_{a'},
\qquad
p'\mid b_{6a'+7t+6}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The direct ordered gap is

\[
h_5=r'-a'
=5a'+7t+6
=5(q-p')+2t+1.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Reflection modulo \(p'\) replaces \(r'\) by

\[
r'^*=p'-1-r',
\tag{6.6}
\]

and the signed reflected gap relative to \(a'\) is

\[
r'^*-a'=8p'-7q.
\tag{6.7}
\]

These are the literal minus slopes \(5\) and \(8\).  If the relevant two zeros are in increasing order, the source gap identity gives respectively

\[
p'\mid N_{h_5}(a')
\tag{6.8}
\]

or the corresponding slope-8 reflected evaluation.

This is a genuine use of both zero conditions.  It does not count foreign primes, however, because as \(p'\) varies:

```text
the defining characteristic changes from F_{p'} to F_{p''};
the starting point a'=n-p' changes;
the gap index h_5 or h_8 changes with slope 5 or 8;
the evaluated characteristic-zero integer changes.
```

The source’s `prop:column` fixes one characteristic and one base point while varying the gap level.  Here each characteristic contributes only one prescribed two-zero pair.  A bound of `O(L^(2/3))` zero levels in each fixed column therefore gives no bound on the number of different characteristics which each contribute one hit.

## 6.2 Exact failure of the fixed high-index continuant

A natural attempt is to avoid the moving local index and connect the two fixed high indices

\[
n
\quad\text{and}\quad
B=n+h,
\qquad
h=B-n=5q+2t+1.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Let

\[
D_h(m):=\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(m+j+1)^3.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Clearing the recurrence gives an integral transfer identity of the form

\[
D_h(m)b_{m+h}
=N_h(m)b_{m+1}+E_h(m)b_m,
\qquad E_h(m)\in\mathbb Z[m],
\tag{6.11}
\]

where \(N_h\) is the source gap polynomial.  In the ordinary nonwrapping range, all factors of \(D_h(m)\) are units and `(6.11)` is the usual gap certificate.

For a legal q6 minus lower prime \(p'\), the situation is the opposite.  The raw window gives

\[
p'>\frac B7>\frac{n+1}{2}.
\tag{6.12}
\]

The second inequality follows from

\[
2B-7(n+1)=5q+9t>0.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Also \(p'\le n\), and

\[
B-2n=4q+3t+2>0.
\tag{6.14}
\]

Hence

\[
n+2\le2p'<B.
\tag{6.15}
\]

The factor \((2p')^3\) therefore occurs in `(6.10)`, so

\[
p'\mid D_h(n).
\tag{6.16}
\]

Now suppose only the row-1 condition \(p'\mid b_n\).  Write

\[
n=p'+a',
\qquad0\le a'\le p'-2;
\tag{6.17}
\]

the upper bound follows again from `(6.12)`.  Gessel--Lucas and no-consecutive-zeros give

\[
b_n\equiv5b_{a'}\equiv0\pmod{p'},
\qquad
b_{n+1}\equiv5b_{a'+1}\not\equiv0\pmod{p'}.
\tag{6.18}
\]

Reduce `(6.11)` at \(m=n\) modulo \(p'\).  The left side vanishes because of `(6.16)`, and the last term vanishes because \(p'\mid b_n\).  Since \(b_{n+1}\) is a unit,

\[
\boxed{p'\mid N_{B-n}(n).}
\tag{6.19}
\]

Crucially, `(6.19)` was obtained **without using** \(p'\mid b_B\).

### Fixed-span restart-erasure lemma

For every legal raw-minus prime \(p'\) satisfying the row-1 zero,

\[
\boxed{
p'\mid b_n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p'\mid N_{B-n}(n).
}
\tag{6.20}
\]

Thus the first fixed high-index continuant is a carrier for the whole first-row support.  It is not a detector for the intersection with the row-6 support.  Divisor or height estimates for this integer discard precisely the condition the question asks us to retain.

This is the first exact failure of the fixed-span continuant route.  It is caused by a real restart factor, not by a loose height estimate.

After the structural crossing is deflated, the remaining local certificate is `(6.8)` or its reflected slope-8 analogue, which again depends on \(p'\).  The attack has therefore returned exactly to the moving-characteristic horizontal support problem.

## 6.3 Why the anchor does not deplete foreign support

Fix an actual anchor \(p\).  It gives

\[
p\mid G_-(x).
\tag{6.21}
\]

Remove its complete primary part:

\[
G_-^{(p)}(x)
:=G_-(x)/p^{v_p(G_-(x))}.
\tag{6.22}
\]

For every foreign prime \(p'\ne p\), exactly

\[
\boxed{
v_{p'}(G_-^{(p)}(x))=v_{p'}(G_-(x)).}
\tag{6.23}
\]

Thus the anchor’s exact Lucas branch and any finite amount of its divided \(p\)-adic data do not, by themselves, reduce the foreign support.  They describe the \(p\)-component of one fixed integer; the partner question concerns different primary components.

This statement does not assert arithmetic independence of the actual Apéry values.  It identifies the precise datum which a new theorem must control: the distribution of foreign prime divisors of the distinguished fixed integer \(G_-(x)\) **under the selected-anchor weighting**.

## 6.4 Resultant, Bezout, and Casoratian audit

A same-characteristic gap resultant is useful only when two endpoint conditions are visible in one residue field.  For two different foreign primes \(p_1,p_2\), the two local certificates live in

\[
\mathbb F_{p_1}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathbb F_{p_2}.
\]

Their prescribed roots are not common roots of one polynomial pair over one field.  A resultant in the numerical prime variable therefore has no pair-detection implication unless the local conditions are first lifted to a single characteristic-zero object.  The canonical such object is already \(G_-(x)\).

The honest mixed integer for two candidates is, for example,

\[
\mathcal B(p_1,p_2)
:=
b_{a(p_1)}b_{r(p_2)}
-b_{r(p_1)}b_{a(p_2)}.
\tag{6.24}
\]

If both candidates are actual two-zero leaves, each term is separately divisible by \(p_1p_2\), so

\[
p_1p_2\mid\mathcal B(p_1,p_2).
\tag{6.25}

But this pays the two marked-prime factors term by term and has no subcritical height.  It is partner-dependent and gives no bound on the number of partners.

The Apéry Casoratian controls the two solution coordinates at one index.  It does not bound

\[
\gcd(b_n,b_B)
\]

at two separated moving indices.  Transfer Bezout identities reduce a pair of zeros within one fixed characteristic to a local continuant; as `(6.5)`--`(6.8)` show, that continuant varies with the defining prime.  Combining the separate local identities into one integral determinant returns to the common gcd or to a product carrying every marked prime.

Accordingly, no source resultant, Bezout, Wronskian, or Casoratian estimate yields `(5.14)`.

# 8. Scoped non-implication model

The following is a method-classification result, not an Apéry counterexample.

Fix an abstract cell containing \(N\) candidate prime labels, with

\[
N\asymp\frac{\Lambda}{\log T}.
\]

For each label \(\ell\), take a local residue-pair space and two zero-row events \(U_\ell,V_\ell\) having the source-compatible ambient sizes

\[
\mathbb P(U_\ell)\ll T^{-1/3},
\qquad
\mathbb P(V_\ell)\ll T^{-1/3},
\qquad
\mathbb P(U_\ell\cap V_\ell)\ll T^{-2/3}.
\]

Take the product of these local spaces across the distinct prime labels.  Then every finite ambient joint moment factors at the independence scale, so in particular all one-prime marginals and ambient second moments are as strong as the source inputs require.

Choose one distinguished product point lying in every \(U_\ell\cap V_\ell\), and let the “selected upper mark” and actual Palm law be supported on that point.  Declare all \(N\) labels actual anchors there.  Then

\[
M=N,
\qquad
D=N(N-1)\asymp M\frac{\Lambda}{\log T}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus, for every fixed \(\eta>0\),

\[
\frac{D}{M\Lambda T^{-\eta}}
\asymp
\frac{T^\eta}{\log T}\to\infty,
\tag{8.2}
\]

and

\[
\frac{D}{MH}
\asymp
\frac{Y^{1/3}}{\log T}\to\infty.

One may also assign only one prescribed two-zero pair in each varying characteristic.  This is far below the pointwise \(|Z_\ell|\ll\ell^{2/3}\) allowance and below every fixed-characteristic column/collision bound, while still filling the horizontal cell with varying-characteristic hits.

# 10. Insertion-ready exact reduction

The following is suitable for a q6/Palm source section after aligning the local names for the selected-state ledger and fixed saturation.

```latex
\begin{lemma}[Minus q6 fixed-index serialization]
\label{lem:q6-minus-fixed-index}
Let $x=(q,t,\ldots)$ be a selected minus state and put
\[
 n=q-1-t,\qquad B=6q+t.
\]
Let $p$ be a lower-shell prime outside the fixed coefficient ledger and
satisfying the raw minus window $B/7<p\le n$.  Put
\[
 a_p=n-p,\qquad r_p=B-6p.
\]
Then $0\le a_p,r_p<p$ and
\[
 p\mid b_{a_p},\ p\mid b_{r_p}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p\mid b_n,\ p\mid b_B.
\]
Moreover the selected upper mark implies $q\mid b_n$ and $q\mid b_B$.
Consequently, after complete $q$-primary and fixed-ledger saturation,
every actual minus leaf $p$ divides the normalized fixed integer
$G_-(x)=\gcd(b_n,b_B)^{\rm sat}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $n=p+a_p$ and $B=6p+r_p$, Gessel--Lucas gives
$b_n\equiv b_1b_{a_p}=5b_{a_p}\pmod p$ and
$b_B\equiv b_6b_{r_p}\pmod p$.  The fixed ledger removes the prime
support of $5b_6$.  Reflection gives
$b_n=b_{q-1-t}\equiv b_t\pmod q$, while Gessel--Lucas gives
$b_B=b_{6q+t}\equiv b_6b_t\pmod q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Minus joint-two-zero Palm reduction]
\label{prop:q6-minus-joint-palm}
Let $A_x(p)$ be the literal actual minus-leaf indicator and let
$C_x^{2Z}(p)$ be the deterministic raw-window indicator of
$p\mid G_-(x)$ before the final residual, primitive, depth and
assignment masks.  For translated length-$\Lambda$ cells define
$D_-(\Lambda)$ by the ordered actual second factorial moment.  Then
\[
 D_-(\Lambda)\le
 \frac1\Lambda\sum_{\tau,x,j}
 \sum_{\substack{p\ne\ell\\p,\ell\in I_j^\tau}}
 A_x(p)C_x^{2Z}(\ell).
\]
Writing $g_\ell=|Z_\ell|^2/\ell^2$, the right side equals
\[
 \mathcal C_{-,\rm Palm}^{2Z}(\Lambda)
 +O_J\!\left(M_-(T)\Lambda T^{-2/3}/\log T\right),
\]
where
\[
 \mathcal C_{-,\rm Palm}^{2Z}(\Lambda)
 =\frac1\Lambda\sum_{\tau,x,j}
  \sum_{\substack{p\ne\ell\\p,\ell\in I_j^\tau}}
 A_x(p)\bigl(C_x^{2Z}(\ell)-W_x(\ell)g_\ell\bigr).
\]
Therefore any estimate
\[
 \mathcal C_{-,\rm Palm}^{2Z}(\Lambda)
 \ll_J M_-(T)\Lambda T^{-\eta}
\]
with fixed $\eta>0$ gives a fixed-power Palm bound; if
$\eta\ge\beta/3$ and $\Lambda=Y^{5/6}$, it gives
$D_-(\Lambda)\ll_JM_-(T)Y^{1/2}$.
\end{proposition}
```
