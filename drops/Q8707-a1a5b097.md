# Q8707 audit: WZ certificate requirement

## Verdict

No exact WZ certificate has been produced here. Therefore the claimed all-prime theorem remains **unproved** by this audit. The previous argument invoking an unspecified WZ decomposition was not a proof: a valid certificate must give an explicit rational function R(u,j), an identity

\[
F(u,j)=\Delta_j R(u,j),
\]

and verify both boundary terms. Without these data the divisibility by p^3 is not established.

## What is required

For

\[
A(u,j)=\binom uj^2\binom{u+j}{j}^2,
\]

one must expand the difference

\[
B_{p+u}-5\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}A(p+u,j)
\]

through order p^2 after the standard binomial expansions. The resulting coefficient of p^2 (or the corresponding normalized finite sum modulo p) must be represented as

\[
\sum_j F(u,j)=\sum_j (R(u,j+1)-R(u,j)).
\]

The certificate data are:

1. the exact summand F(u,j);
2. a closed rational expression R(u,j);
3. proof that the identity holds for every admissible j;
4. boundary verification

\[
R(u,0)=0,
\qquad
R(u,u+1)=0
\]

(or the appropriate finite endpoints after truncation).

No such R has been supplied or derived in the present argument.

## Endpoint u=0

The case u=0 must be checked independently. It reduces to the endpoint congruence

\[
B_p\equiv 5B_0=5\pmod {p^3}
\]

if that endpoint theorem is available. It cannot be inferred from a missing WZ certificate for the general u case.

## Conclusion

The statement

\[
B_{p+u}\equiv 5\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p+u}{j}^2\binom{p+u+j}{j}^2\pmod {p^3}
\]

may be true, but the requested proof obligation is an explicit finite telescoping certificate. Since no explicit \(R(u,j)\) and no verified boundary cancellation are available, the theorem must be recorded as open in this line of attack rather than called standard or valid.
