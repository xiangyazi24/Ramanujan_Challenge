\[
\log\gcd(b_r,K_r)=o(r)

or into the needed dyadic estimate

\[
\sum_{R<r\le 2R}\log \operatorname{rad}_{>2R}\gcd(b_r,K_r)=o(R^2).
\tag{T}

- P-recursiveness / holonomicity,
- being coefficients of G-functions,
- exponential coefficient height,
- reduced denominators supported on primes `<= n`, and
- even algebraic independence of the two G-functions (or, separately, nonsplitting of a homogeneous/inhomogeneous difference-module extension).

\[
(1+o(1))R^2.

Among precise existing results, the closest unconditional theorems are Grieve--Wang's moving-target Subspace Theorem, Xiao's extension to almost `S`-units, and Yasufuku's 2026 codimension-two blowup inequality for arbitrary rational points. All three fail a decisive hypothesis for the level-6 Apéry/Eichler pair. ## 2. What the primary gcd theorems actually say

I searched the primary literature in the directions requested: gcds from Schmidt/Subspace methods, moving targets, linear recurrences, almost units, blowups/Vojta, G-function coefficient arithmetic, and E-function algebraic independence.

### 2.1 Bugeaud--Corvaja--Zannier: cyclic torus orbits

Bugeaud, Corvaja and Zannier prove that if nonzero integers `a,b` are multiplicatively independent, then for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
\log\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)<\epsilon n
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`.

Primary reference:

- Y. Bugeaud, P. Corvaja, U. Zannier, *An upper bound for the G.C.D. of \(a^n-1\) and \(b^n-1\)*, Math. Z. 243 (2003), 79--84.

This is a moving-prime theorem, not a fixed-prime theorem. But the decisive geometry is a cyclic orbit in a fixed algebraic torus. The Apéry index orbit is not of this form.

### 2.2 Corvaja--Zannier / Levin: polynomial gcds on a fixed finitely generated torus

Corvaja--Zannier prove gcd/height inequalities for coprime polynomials evaluated on a finitely generated subgroup of `G_m^2`; Levin extends this to higher-dimensional algebraic tori.

Primary reference:

- P. Corvaja, U. Zannier, *A lower bound for the height of a rational function at S-unit points*, Monatsh. Math. 144 (2005), 203--224, arXiv:math/0311030.

Again, the primes entering the **gcd** may move. What is fixed is the finite-rank multiplicative group containing the **arguments**. There is no representation of `(b_r,K_r)` as fixed-degree coprime polynomial evaluations on such a fixed torus.

So using these theorems here would be exactly the invalid substitution warned against in the question.

### 2.3 Ru--Vojta moving targets and Grieve--Wang

Ru--Vojta's moving-target Subspace Theorem allows the hyperplanes/polynomials to move, provided their heights are slow compared with the point height and the moving point is nondegenerate over the field generated by the moving coefficients.

Grieve--Wang extract a gcd theorem from this framework.

Primary reference:

- N. Grieve, J. T.-Y. Wang, *Greatest common divisors with moving targets and consequences for linear recurrence sequences*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 373 (2020), 8095--8126, DOI `10.1090/tran/8220`, arXiv:1902.09109.

Their Theorem 1.2 has the following relevant hypotheses:

- a **fixed finite** set of places `S`;
- maps `u_i(alpha)` taking values in the fixed `S`-unit group;
- moving coprime polynomials of **fixed degree**;
- at least one polynomial has nonzero constant term;
- moving target heights are `o(max h(u_i(alpha)))`;
- otherwise one falls into a moving translated proper algebraic subgroup.

Their “linear recurrence sequence” means an exponential polynomial

\[
F(n)=\sum_i f_i(n)\alpha_i^n,
\tag{8}
\]

that is, a constant-coefficient linear recurrence (possibly with polynomial multiplicities), not a P-recursive sequence with polynomial-in-`n` recurrence coefficients.

**Audit for Apéry:** fails the torus/S-unit representation. The Apéry recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}
\]

is P-recursive, not an instance of (8), and the repository contains no fixed finite-rank torus parametrization of `(b_r,K_r)` with slow moving coefficients. Encoding `b_r` or `K_r` themselves as moving coefficients would give target height comparable to the point height, violating the slow-height hypothesis and making the theorem circular.

### 2.4 Xiao 2024: almost S-units

Zheng Xiao extends the toric gcd method from exact `S`-units to points whose outside-`S` height is a sufficiently small fraction of total height.

Primary reference:

- Z. Xiao, *Greatest common divisors for polynomials in almost units and applications to linear recurrence sequences*, Math. Z. 306 (2024), article 61, DOI `10.1007/s00209-024-03453-4`, arXiv:2110.01751.

In the form relevant here: for fixed coprime `f,g`, every `epsilon>0` admits `delta>0` and a proper Zariski-closed exceptional set so that the gcd is `<= epsilon h` for points satisfying

\[
h_{\bar S}(P)<\delta h(P).
\tag{9}
\]

This is the closest unconditional theorem that could plausibly be made pointwise for a non-toric sequence.

A natural shift puts the Apéry gcd into exactly this shape:

\[
P_r=(b_r+1,K_r+1)\in \mathbf G_m^2,
\qquad
f(X,Y)=X-1,
\quad g(X,Y)=Y-1.
\tag{10}
\]

Then `f(P_r)=b_r` and `g(P_r)=K_r`.

But there is no proof -- and no reason supplied by characteristic-zero transversality -- that `(b_r+1,K_r+1)` is an almost-`S`-unit point for one fixed finite `S`. This is an **arithmetic height condition**, not a functional-independence condition.

### 2.5 Silverman under Vojta, and Yasufuku 2026 unconditionally

Silverman interprets gcd as a height relative to an exceptional divisor on a blowup and derives strong inequalities from Vojta's main conjecture.

Primary reference:

- J. H. Silverman, *Generalized greatest common divisors, divisibility sequences, and Vojta's conjecture for blowups*, Monatsh. Math. 145 (2005), 333--350, arXiv:math/0407415.

Yasufuku proved an unconditional weaker codimension-two version in April 2026:

- Y. Yasufuku, *GCD inequalities arising from codimension-2 blowups*, Bull. London Math. Soc. 58 (2026), e70349, DOI `10.1112/blms.70349`.

This theorem is worth auditing exactly because it is newer than most standard gcd surveys.

For our shifted point

\[
P_r=[1:b_r+1:K_r+1]\in\mathbf P^2
\]

choose

\[
F_1=X_1-X_0,\qquad F_2=X_2-X_0.
\tag{11}
\]

Their intersection is the point `[1:1:1]`, a codimension-two complete intersection, and it avoids the coordinate vertices, so Yasufuku's geometric hypotheses are satisfied.

For `n=2`, `d_1=d_2=1`, his explicit constant may be taken arbitrarily above

\[
\epsilon_2(1,1)=\sqrt6-2\approx0.44949.
\tag{12}
\]

The conclusion has the form

\[
\log\gcd(b_r,K_r)
< (\sqrt6-2+\eta)h(P_r)
+\sum_{v\notin S}\lambda_v(X_0X_1X_2=0,P_r)
\tag{13}
\]

outside a proper Zariski-closed set.

For integral coordinates `[1:b_r+1:K_r+1]`, the boundary term is precisely measuring outside-`S` prime content of the shifted coordinates. It is not known to be `o(r)`. Worse, even if it vanished identically, the unconditional coefficient in (12) is a fixed positive number, so (13) would still only give `O(r)`, not `o(r)`.

Silverman's Vojta-conditional version allows an arbitrary `epsilon h(P)` term, but the same outside-`S` boundary term remains. Therefore even Vojta does not close the Apéry problem unless one also proves a sublinear boundary-height statement and controls the exceptional locus.

### 2.6 G-function denominator theorems

Fischler--Rivoal prove strong denominator structure for Taylor coefficients of G-functions, unconditionally for geometric G-operators and conditionally in general on a standard `p`-adic G-operator conjecture.

Primary reference:

- S. Fischler, T. Rivoal, *On the denominators of the Taylor coefficients of G-functions*, Kyushu J. Math. 71 (2017), 287--298, DOI `10.2206/kyushujm.71.287`, arXiv:1606.00706.

Their result controls common denominators of initial Taylor coefficients by products built from `lcm(1,...,bn)` and a fixed exponential factor. It does **not** give an upper bound for gcds of primitive numerators of two independent G-functions.

For the present problem we already have an even sharper support statement for `kappa_r`: no prime factor of `d_r` exceeds `r`. The missing information is therefore on the **numerators**, exactly where the G-function denominator theorem is silent.

### 2.7 E-function algebraic-independence theorems

Beukers' refined Siegel--Shidlovskii theorem characterizes algebraic relations among values of E-functions at nonzero algebraic points from functional relations.

Primary reference:

- F. Beukers, *A refined version of the Siegel--Shidlovskii theorem*, Ann. of Math. 163 (2006), 369--379, DOI `10.4007/annals.2006.163.369`.

This is a theorem about **values at fixed algebraic arguments**, not common prime divisors of same-index Taylor coefficients. Passing to an E-function/Borel transform does not convert coefficient divisibility into a hypothesis of Siegel--Shidlovskii.

So the E-function route does not touch (T).

## 3. Explicit counterexample: algebraically independent G-functions with quadratic fresh common mass

This rules out the hoped-for abstract theorem in the strongest clean way I know.

### Theorem 3.1

For an integer `A>=1`, define

\[
a_n(A)=\frac1n\binom{2n}{n}A^n,\qquad n\ge1,
\tag{14}
\]

and

\[
H_A(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n(A)z^n.
\tag{15}
\]

Then:

1. `H_A` is a G-function and `(a_n(A))` is first-order P-recursive;
2. the reduced denominator of `a_n(A)` divides `n`, hence has no prime factor `>n`;
3. `H_1` and `H_2` are algebraically independent over `C(z)`; in fact their logarithmic extension classes are independent over their common algebraic cover;
4. if `N_n(A)` denotes the primitive numerator of `a_n(A)`, then

\[
\sum_{R<n\le2R}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>2R}\gcd(N_n(1),N_n(2))
\ge (1+o(1))R^2.
\tag{16}
\]

In particular, algebraic independence of G-functions plus P-recursiveness plus high-prime-free denominators does **not** imply (T).

### Proof

The ratio is rational:

\[
\frac{a_{n+1}(A)}{a_n(A)}
=\frac{2A\,n(2n+1)}{(n+1)^2},
\]

so

\[
(n+1)^2a_{n+1}(A)=2A\,n(2n+1)a_n(A).
\tag{17}
\]

Thus the sequence is hypergeometric and P-recursive.

The closed form is

\[
H_A(z)
=2\log\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-4Az}}.
\tag{18}
\]

Indeed differentiating the series gives

\[
H_A'(z)=\frac1z\left((1-4Az)^{-1/2}-1\right).
\]

The coefficients have exponential size, their denominators divide `lcm(1,...,n)`, and (18) is D-finite; hence `H_A` is a G-function.

For algebraic independence, put

\[
s_1^2=1-4z,\qquad s_2^2=1-8z,
\qquad
u_i=\frac{2}{1+s_i}.
\]

On the common algebraic cover

\[
L=\mathbf C(z,s_1,s_2),
\]

we have `H_1=2 log u_1` and `H_2=2 log u_2`. Over the point `z=0` there is a branch with `(s_1,s_2)=(-1,+1)`: there `u_1` has a simple pole while `u_2` is a unit. There is another branch `(s_1,s_2)=(+1,-1)` where `u_2` has a simple pole while `u_1` is a unit.

Analytic continuation around the first point translates `H_1` by a nonzero integral multiple of `4 pi i` and leaves `H_2` unchanged; around the second it translates `H_2` independently. If a nonzero polynomial relation

\[
Q(z,H_1,H_2)=0
\]

existed, repeated continuation around the first loop would give infinitely many translates of `H_1` as roots of a fixed polynomial in its first argument, forcing the relation to be independent of `H_1`. The second loop then forces independence of `H_2`, a contradiction. Thus `H_1,H_2` are algebraically independent over `L`, hence over `C(z)`.

Equivalently, the residue vectors of `d u_1/u_1` and `d u_2/u_2` are independent, so the logarithmic Picard--Vessiot extension has additive differential Galois group `G_a^2`.

Now let

\[
c_n=\binom{2n}{n}.
\]

Because the reduced denominator of `a_n(A)=c_nA^n/n` divides `n`, any prime `p>n` dividing `c_n` divides the primitive numerator `N_n(A)` for every fixed `A`.

For every prime

\[
n<p\le2n,
\]

we have `v_p(c_n)=1`. Hence, for `R<n<=2R`, every prime

\[
2R<p\le2n
\]

divides both `N_n(1)` and `N_n(2)`. Writing `theta(x)=sum_{p<=x} log p`,

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>2R}\gcd(N_n(1),N_n(2))
\ge \theta(2n)-\theta(2R).
\]

By the prime number theorem, uniformly on `R<n<=2R`, `theta(x)=x+o(R)`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{R<n\le2R}[\theta(2n)-\theta(2R)]
&=\sum_{n=R+1}^{2R}(2n-2R)+o(R^2)\\
&=R^2+R+o(R^2)\\
&=(1+o(1))R^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (16). `square`

### Consequence

A theorem whose “independence” input lives only in the characteristic-zero differential/G-function category cannot prove the Apéry fresh gcd bound. Finite-place coefficient content is invisible to that category.

## 4. Even nonsplitting of a homogeneous/inhomogeneous extension is insufficient

The previous example uses two algebraically independent G-functions. There is an even closer model for the actual “period plus Eichler extension” language.

Let

\[
b_n=\binom{2n}{n},
\qquad
k_n=b_n H_n,
\tag{19}
\]

where `H_n=1+1/2+...+1/n`.

The period satisfies

\[
(n+1)b_{n+1}-2(2n+1)b_n=0.
\tag{20}
\]

Since `H_{n+1}=H_n+1/(n+1)`, the extension coordinate satisfies the **same homogeneous operator with a hypergeometric source**:

\[
(n+1)k_{n+1}-2(2n+1)k_n=b_{n+1}.
\tag{21}
\]

This extension does not split over `Q(n)`: if `k_n=q(n)b_n` with rational `q`, then

\[
q(n+1)-q(n)=\frac1{n+1},
\tag{22}
\]

but the right side has a nonzero discrete residue on its integer pole orbit, so no rational `q(n)` exists. Equivalently, the harmonic antidifference is not rational. This is the standard rational-summation obstruction.

Nevertheless the denominator of `k_n` divides `lcm(1,...,n)`, and every prime `p>n` dividing `b_n` also divides the primitive numerator of `k_n`. Consequently the same calculation as in Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
\sum_{R<n\le2R}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>2R}
\gcd\bigl(b_n,\operatorname{num}(k_n)\bigr)
\ge(1+o(1))R^2.
\tag{23}
\]

So even a **genuinely nonsplit P-recursive inhomogeneous extension with small-prime denominators** can carry maximal fresh common mass.

This example does not have the special residual affine transversality proved for the Apéry pair; that extra local structure is real and valuable. But there is currently no theorem converting that local codimension-two statement into a global moving-prime incidence estimate. Such a conversion is precisely the missing lemma, not something supplied by the abstract nonsplitting of the extension.

## 5. Why the new reflected-tail sequence e_n does not supply the missing arithmetic input

There are two separate tests.

### 5.1 It does not create a third vanishing condition at a common Apéry/Eichler prime

From (7), for a prime `p>n`,

\[
p\mid b_n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W_{n+1}\equiv W_n\pmod p,
\tag{24}
\]

because all denominators in `F_{n-1}` are `p`-units.

The common target `p|b_n,K_n` is equivalent to `p|b_n,Xi_n` by (2), so it imposes `Xi_n=0`; it imposes **no condition** on `W_n`. In particular, the new inhomogeneous recurrence does not yield

\[
p\mid b_n,K_n \Longrightarrow p\mid e_n
\]

or any equivalent third-coordinate zero.

A new bridge would have to be proved; it is not a formal consequence of the two source equations.

### 5.2 Its source has quadratic fresh radical mass

Take `(E-source)` and restrict to

\[
R<n\le2R-2.
\]

Then `n+2<=2R`, so every prime `p>2R` is a unit in the denominator `n+2`.

For every prime

\[
2R<p\le2n+1,
\]

we have `p>n+1`, hence

\[
p\mid \binom{2n+1}{n}.
\]

Therefore these primes survive in the primitive numerator of `F_n`, regardless of the quadratic prefactor. Hence

\[
\sum_{R<n\le2R-2}
\log\operatorname{rad}_{>2R}\operatorname{num}(F_n)
\ge
\sum_{R<n\le2R-2}
[\theta(2n+1)-\theta(2R)]
=(1+o(1))R^2.
\tag{25}
\]

Thus the source itself already contains deterministic fresh-prime mass at the forbidden scale.

Moreover, for any fixed finite set of primes `S`, the `S`-part of a central binomial coefficient has only `O_S(log n)` logarithmic size (for each fixed `p`, `v_p binom(2n,n)=O(log n)`), while

\[
\log\binom{2n+1}{n}^2=n\log16+O(\log n).
\]

So its outside-`S` height is `Theta(n)`. The source is not even asymptotically close to an `S`-unit.

**Conclusion on e_n:** it is structurally excellent for reflection/Green identities, but it is arithmetically the wrong object for Xiao/Grieve--Wang style gcd bounds. To become useful for the common-prime problem, one would need a new identity in which the central-binomial source cancels before forming the integer certificate.

## 6. A scoped conditional theorem that really would apply

The closest existing theorem that can be made rigorous for the shifted Apéry pair is Xiao's almost-unit result. It gives a clean statement of the exact missing arithmetic hypothesis.

### Conditional Theorem 6.1 (almost-S-unit Apéry criterion)

Let

\[
P_r=(b_r+1,K_r+1)\in\mathbf G_m^2(\mathbf Q)
\]

for all sufficiently large `r`, and assume:

**(H1) Linear projective height.** There is `C>0` such that

\[
h(P_r)\le Cr.
\tag{26}
\]

**(H2) Fixed-S finite-place primitivity.** For one fixed finite set of places `S`,

\[
h_{\bar S}(P_r)=o(r).
\tag{27}
\]

**(H3) Sparse exceptional intersections.** For every proper Zariski-closed set `Z` arising from Xiao's theorem for `f=X-1,g=Y-1` and any fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
\#\{R<r\le2R:P_r\in Z\}=o(R).
\tag{28}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{R<r\le2R}\log\operatorname{rad}_{>2R}\gcd(b_r,K_r)=o(R^2).
\tag{29}
\]

If, stronger, every sufficiently large `P_r` avoids the exceptional sets, then

\[
\log\gcd(b_r,K_r)=o(r).
\tag{30}
\]

### Proof

Fix `epsilon>0`. Xiao gives a `delta>0` and a proper Zariski-closed exceptional set `Z_epsilon` such that for points outside `Z_epsilon` satisfying

\[
h_{\bar S}(P)<\delta h(P),
\]

we have

\[
\log\gcd(X(P)-1,Y(P)-1)<\epsilon h(P).
\]

By (H2), this applies to all sufficiently large nonexceptional `P_r`. By (H1), each such row contributes at most `C epsilon r`, so over a dyadic block its total is `O(epsilon R^2)`.

The exceptional rows are `o(R)` by (H3). Their gcd is at most the smaller coordinate height, hence `O(R)` per row by (H1), so their total is `o(R^2)`.

Since `epsilon` is arbitrary, the total gcd logarithm is `o(R^2)`. The fresh radical in (29) is smaller, proving (29). If there are eventually no exceptional rows, the same argument rowwise gives (30). `square`

### Hypothesis audit

- `(H1)` is a standard G-function/P-recursive type height statement and is consistent with the observed linear row height of the primitive numerator. It is not the conceptual obstruction.
- `(H3)` is a genuine arithmetic-independence/zero-set statement. Residual affine transversality does not presently imply it in characteristic zero.
- **`(H2)` is the fatal missing hypothesis.** It says that after the harmless shift by 1, almost all arithmetic height of both coordinates is supported on one fixed finite set of primes. Nothing in the level-6 differential/Eichler independence implies this. The central-binomial source of `e_n` provably fails the analogous condition maximally.

Thus this conditional theorem is useful mainly as a diagnosis: the Subspace-Theorem machinery asks for finite-place primitivity of a kind we do not have.

## 7. The narrowest new Diophantine lemma that would close the actual dyadic aggregate

The most faithful formulation avoids introducing a fake fixed `S`.

For `p>2R`, define the common-zero incidence multiplicity

\[
m_R(p)=\#\{r:R<r\le2R,\ p\mid b_r,\ p\mid K_r\}.
\tag{31}
\]

Then the target is exactly

\[
\mathcal W_R
=\sum_{p>2R}m_R(p)\log p.
\tag{32}
\]

(The use of `rad` is rowwise, so the same prime occurring in two rows is counted twice, which is why `m_R(p)` appears.)

### Lemma 7.1 (subquadratic incidence certificate)

Suppose that for every sufficiently large `R` one can construct a nonzero integer `Delta_R` such that

\[
v_p(\Delta_R)\ge m_R(p)
\qquad\text{for every prime }p>2R,
\tag{33}
\]

and

\[
\log|\Delta_R|=o(R^2).
\tag{34}
\]

Then (T) holds.

### Proof

From (33),

\[
\mathcal W_R
=\sum_{p>2R}m_R(p)\log p
\le\sum_p v_p(\Delta_R)\log p
\le\log|\Delta_R|,
\]

which is `o(R^2)` by (34). `square`

This may look elementary, but it isolates the precise thing the natural row resultants fail to provide. A rowwise resultant of logarithmic height `Theta(r^2)` is already too large **for one row**; multiplying such certificates over a block is vastly worse. What is needed is a genuinely **multirow** determinant/Fitting/Bezout object in which the common-prime incidences are recorded before exponential row heights are multiplied.

The canonical adjacent identity (1) is exactly the right scale normalization for attempting such a construction, but Q7694 already showed that its primitive row carrier by itself does not produce a hidden subquadratic certificate.

### Equivalent orbit-sieve form

Q7736's affine transfer suggests the analytic version. For dyadic prime scale `P>=2R`, put

\[
N_R(P)=
\sum_{\substack{R<r\le2R\\P<p\le2P\\p\mid b_r,K_r}}
\log p.
\tag{35}
\]

A bound of the form

\[
N_R(P)\ll \frac RP+R^{1-\delta}
\tag{36}
\]

with one fixed `delta>0`, **uniformly in the prime scale `P` up to the exponential height range of the rows**, closes (T): there are only `O(R)` dyadic prime scales, so the error sums to `O(R^{2-\delta})`, while the `R/P` main terms are summable.

The exact ambient codimension-two density (3) predicts much better than (36), but no theorem presently transfers that ambient density to the actual nonautonomous Apéry orbit.

I regard (33)--(34) and (36) as the two clean formulations of the same missing phenomenon: finite-place independence across the moving prime support.

## 8. What should and should not be pursued next

The direct “find a general G-function gcd theorem” route is closed in its naive form by Theorem 3.1 and the nonsplit extension example (19)--(23).

The useful remaining directions are much narrower:

1. **Apéry-specific multirow arithmetic.** Use the unit-Casoratian normalization and affine transfer to build a Fitting determinant whose height is `o(R^2)` before taking rowwise primitive numerators.
2. **Uniform residual orbit sieve.** Prove a prime-scale-uniform estimate like (36), not merely ambient codimension two.
3. **A cancellation identity involving e_n.** The new `e_n` can help only if its base-16 source cancels in a determinant/Green combination. Using the source itself cannot work because of (25).

What should not be used as a substitute:

- the two observed pairs `(17,13)` and `(2237,492)`;
- a fixed finite `S` chosen after seeing the common primes;
- a theorem for constant-coefficient linear recurrences applied to the P-recursive Apéry sequence;
- algebraic/differential independence alone;
- G-function denominator control alone;
- the fact that the residual common-zero locus has codimension two, without an orbit-distribution theorem.

## 9. Final answer to the question

**No existing primary theorem located in the G-function, E-function, P-recursive, arithmetic differential-equation, or moving-target Subspace-Theorem literature applies to the level-6 Apéry/Eichler pair strongly enough to prove `log gcd(b_r,K_r)=o(r)` or the desired dyadic `o(R^2)` fresh mass.**

This is not merely because one technical hypothesis has not yet been checked. A blanket theorem from characteristic-zero G-function/P-recursive independence is impossible: Theorem 3.1 gives algebraically independent G-functions with denominator prime support `<=n` and fresh common radical `(1+o(1))R^2`; (19)--(23) gives the same obstruction for a genuinely nonsplit homogeneous/inhomogeneous P-recursive extension.

For the actual Apéry pair, the extra residual affine transversality is real, but the missing theorem is exactly a **moving finite-place orbit-independence/height theorem**. The sharp closing statements are Lemma 7.1 (subquadratic incidence certificate) or the uniform affine-orbit sieve (36).

The reflected-tail `e_n` does not currently provide that missing theorem. Its Green increment vanishes automatically on `b_n=0`, so it supplies no third zero; and its central-binomial-square source has deterministic fresh radical mass `(1+o(1))R^2`, so it is not a low-content or almost-`S`-unit auxiliary sequence.

That is the strongest theorem-level conclusion that survives a full hypothesis audit.
