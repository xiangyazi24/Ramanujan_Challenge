W_3(r)\ne0\pmod p.

\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

identifies the relevant object uniquely as the **gap-indexed terminal mixed Casoratian**

This is the polynomial denoted \(\mathscr S_h\) in Q2365. In the indexing of Q2362, where

\[

It should not be confused with the smaller difference remainder

# 2. Recurrences and the affine spectral split

Put

\[
P(t)=34t^3+51t^2+27t+5
=(2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5),

and

The two continuants are normalized by

\[

\[

\tag{2.1}

and

\[

\tag{2.2}

\qquad n=r+m.

&=2(2n+1)\bigl(8n(n+1)+2-\lambda_r\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}

The mixed-Casoratian telescope from Q2365 is

\[
\boxed{
W_h=
\sum_{m=1}^{h-1}
\Delta_mN_m\mathcal R_{m-1}
\prod_{j=m+1}^{h-1}(r+j)^6.

\tag{2.4}

Define

\[
X_{m,h}(r)
:=N_m(r)\mathcal R_{m-1}(r)
  \prod_{j=m+1}^{h-1}(r+j)^6.

Then the requested split is obtained term by term:

\[
\boxed{

:=\sum_{m=1}^{h-1}(2r+2m+1)X_{m,h}(r),

# 6. Exceptional-prime and physical audit

A nonzero characteristic-zero resultant does **not** by itself prove the finite-field theorem at primes dividing that integer. Those primes must be checked.

The complete prime-field root audit is:

| \(p\) | common roots of \(N_3=W_3=0\) in \(\mathbf F_p\) | endpoint residues | status |
|---:|:---|:---|:---|
| \(2\) | \(r=1\) | \((b_1,b_4)=(1,1)\) | excluded characteristic; not a zero pair |
| \(5\) | \(r=3\) | \((b_3,b_6)=(0,0)\) | degenerate/wrapping: \(r+2=0\), \(r+3>p-1\) |
| \(7\) | none in \(\mathbf F_7\) | — | no physical index root |
| \(61\) | \(r=8,49\) | \((b_8,b_{11})=(12,7)\), \((b_{49},b_{52})=(7,12)\) | neither is a zero pair |
| \(73\) | \(r=70=-3\) | \((b_{70},b_{73})=(0,5)\) | endpoint \(r+3=p\); wrapping and not a double zero |

The root sets agree with the Q2355 audit of \(N_3=\mathcal R_2=0\). This is forced by (4.1), except for the possible adjacent factor \(N_2\); its only extra resultant prime is \(5\), already represented by the degenerate root \(r=-2\).

Consequently no prime supports a physical nonwrapping triple

\[
0<r<r+3<p,
\qquad
b_r=b_{r+3}=0,
\qquad
N_3(r)=W_3(r)=0.
\]

This proves (V2).

# 7. The ratio equation really occurs off the marked locus

The common-continuant point

\[
p=61,\qquad r=8
\]

is the decisive diagnostic. It satisfies

\[
N_3(8)=\mathcal R_2(8)=0\pmod{61}.
\]

The intermediate values are

\[
N_2(8)=7,
\qquad
\mathcal R_1(8)=12,
\qquad
(r+2)^6=10^6=27\pmod{61}.
\]

Using (3.9),

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_3(8)
&=(19)(27)+(21)(7)(12)\\
&=25+56\\
&=20\pmod{61}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(V_3(8)\ne0\). Directly from (3.10),

\[
U_3(8)=13\pmod{61}.
\]

Also

\[
\lambda_8=8\cdot9=72=11\pmod{61}.
\]

Since

\[
(2V_3(8))^{-1}=40^{-1}=29\pmod{61},
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U_3(8)}{2V_3(8)}
=13\cdot29
=11
=\lambda_8
\pmod{61}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

So the rational-function equation is not intrinsically incompatible with a common continuant root. It is realized exactly. What fails is the Apéry mark:

\[
b_8=12\ne0,
\qquad
b_{11}=7\ne0\pmod{61}.
\]

This shows why a recurrence-only proof cannot stop at the equation \(U_h=2\lambda_rV_h\). The mark \(b_r=0\) must enter essentially.

# 8. What the \(h=3\) computation proves—and what it does not

For gap three, the fixed-gap route is complete:

\[
\boxed{
\text{characteristic-zero gcd}
+\text{ resultant factorization}
+\text{ exceptional-prime audit}
\Longrightarrow
\text{no physical common root}.}
\]

In particular, at a physical Apéry double zero,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_r\ne\frac{U_3(r)}{2V_3(r)}
}
\]

whenever the quotient is defined; if \(V_3=0\), the gcd calculation still excludes \(W_3=0\), so no separate chart is needed.

For general \(h\), however, the logical situation remains:

1. \(W_h=U_h-2\lambda_rV_h\) is an exact and useful affine-spectral split.
2. The equation \(U_h=2\lambda_rV_h\) is still equivalent to \(W_h=0\); it is not an independent endpoint condition.
3. A characteristic-zero gcd equal to \(1\) only reduces the problem to primes dividing the integer resultant.
4. The fixed-gap resultants in Q2356 acquire large new prime factors rapidly as \(h\) grows.
5. Therefore the degree heuristic does not become an all-gap proof. One still needs either a uniform resultant-prime exclusion or a genuinely marked identity involving \(b_r\).

The exact gap-three conclusion is nevertheless strong: the proposed rational-function obstruction is completely understood, including the one unmarked point showing that the ratio equality itself is genuinely attainable.
