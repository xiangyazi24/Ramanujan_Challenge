\tag{V1}

\boxed{\log|C_y|\ll D\log T.}
\tag{V2}

q_1q_2\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4\mid b_J.

\[
E_y:=\frac{b_J}{q_1q_2}\in\mathbb Z.

Then all four lower primes divide the single integer

\[
U_y:=\gcd(E_y,C_y),
\tag{V3}

\[
\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4\mid U_y,
\qquad
\omega_{\asymp T}(U_y)\ll D.
\tag{V4}

\[

\tag{V5}

\[
\boxed{(\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4)^2\mid K_2.}
\tag{V6}

\[
\log|K_2|\ll 2D\log T.
\tag{V7}

\boxed{
(N_d,N_e):S_d^\infty

\tag{V8}

Equivalently, the restart identity says that once the first return at level `d` is present and the next level is a unit, the later return at `d+h` is the first return times the restarted short carrier at level `h`.  Thus a second long-return equation does **not** give another primitive direction.  It is exactly the already present return plus the short colour block.

For the four-zero lower word relevant here, the gaps have the form

\[
(c,\ g_\ell,\ h),
\tag{V9}

## 2. The strongest common endpoint radical

Because `q_1,q_2` are distinct from every lower prime, define

\[
E_y:=b_J/(q_1q_2).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4\mid E_y.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The endpoint integer itself is macro-height:

\[
\log|E_y|\le\log|b_J|\ll T.
\tag{2.3}
\]

It is therefore much too large to beat the selected-grid lower-prime cap.  Intersecting it with the short carrier gives the optimal scalar compression:

\[
U_y:=\gcd(E_y,C_y).
\tag{2.4}

\[
\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4\mid U_y,
\qquad U_y\mid C_y,
\tag{2.5}

\omega_{\asymp T}(U_y)

## 3. Role-specific endpoint gcds and the union/intersection dichotomy

For the two common indices define

\[
G_m:=\gcd(U_y,b_m),
\qquad
G_{m'}:=\gcd(U_y,b_{m'}).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
\ell_1\ell_2\mid G_m,
\qquad
\ell_3\ell_4\mid G_{m'}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Because both divide `U_y`, their least common multiple satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{lcm}(G_m,G_{m'})\mid U_y.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the **union** of the two disjoint lower-prime supports is already compressed into the one short carrier `U_y`.  Equation `(3.3)` is the best possible scalar union operation: it keeps all four primes and costs only `O(DL)` height.  But it has only first-power information.

The opposite operation, the gcd,

\[
\gcd(G_m,G_{m'}),
\tag{3.4}
\]

sees only lower primes that divide **both** `b_m` and `b_{m'}`.  Those are exactly the one-prime-overlap/same-pair directions already removed from the final event.  On the disjoint rectangle `(3.4)` is allowed to have no shell prime at all.

This is the first simple algebraic dichotomy:

```text
intersection operation (gcd/resultant common root):
    can be small,
    but loses the disjoint primes;

union operation (lcm/product):
    keeps all four disjoint primes,
    but is bounded only by the same common carrier C_y.
```

There is no scalar operation that simultaneously enjoys the smallness of the intersection and the support preservation of the union without a new codegree theorem.

## 4. A genuine one-integer square carrier — and why it gives zero saving

The most favorable repeated-divisibility construction uses the common endpoint factor once and each role value once:

\[
K_2
=\gcd\!\left(U_y^2,\ E_y b_m b_{m'}\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `ell in P`,

\[
v_\ell(E_yb_mb_{m'})\ge2,
\]

and for `ell in P'` the same is true using `b_{m'}`.  Since every lower prime divides `U_y`,

\[
\boxed{
(\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4)^2\mid K_2.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Yet

\[
K_2\mid U_y^2\mid C_y^2,

so

\[
\log|K_2|\ll2DL.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\#\{\ell\asymp T:v_\ell(K_2)\ge2\}
\le\frac{\log|K_2|}{2\log T+O(1)}
\ll D.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The factor `2` cancels exactly.

K_r\mid U_y^r,

\frac{\log|K_r|}{r\log T}
\le
\frac{r\log|U_y|}{r\log T}

\tag{4.5}

Thus any repetition produced merely by taking more copies of the same short carrier is homogeneous of degree one in both valuation and logarithmic height.  It cannot give a power or logarithmic saving.

\tag{4.6}

so the small `D` scale is lost entirely.

## 5. Lucas and the prefix gap carrier do not add an independent valuation

Fix one lower prime `ell` in the role `P` and let

\[
c=|m-J|.
\]

On each canonical quotient chart, Gessel--Lucas has the form

\[
b_{k\ell+r}\equiv b_k b_r\pmod\ell,
\tag{5.1}
\]

with the fixed quotient digit `k` among the ledger's finite set (`1,6,13` on the WORDCV namespaces).  After removing the finite set of primes dividing the fixed coefficient `b_k`, `(5.1)` is an **equivalence of zero conditions**.  It transports `ell|b_J,b_m` to two lower-row zero residues.  It does not supply another factor of `ell`.

The two residues are separated by the literal prefix gap `c`.  The gap lemma therefore gives an evaluated primitive carrier

\[
F_m=N_c(B_m),
\qquad
\ell_1\ell_2\mid F_m,
\tag{5.2}
\]

with the reflected orientation differing only by sign.  Likewise

\[
F_{m'}=N_{c'}(B_{m'}),
\qquad
\ell_3\ell_4\mid F_{m'}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Here `c,c'<ell` on the nonwrapping lower word.  By the source content theorem no shell lower prime divides the coefficient content of `N_c` or `N_{c'}` in this range.

But `(5.2)` is **not a third independent congruence in addition to** `ell|b_J,b_m`.  The source gap formula is exactly

\[
b_{r+c}=C_c(r)b_{r+1}
\quad\text{when }b_r=0,
\tag{5.4}
\]

with the cleared numerator `N_c`.  The no-consecutive-zero theorem makes `b_{r+1}` a unit on the retained locus.  Consequently

\[
\boxed{
(b_r=0,\ b_{r+c}=0)
\iff
(b_r=0,\ N_c(r)=0)
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

modulo the lower prime after denominator/content removal.

Thus `F_m` **replaces** the second endpoint equation; it does not add a new primitive valuation to it.  Multiplying `b_mF_m` can of course create an artificial square divisor, but it simultaneously counts the same recurrence relation twice and doubles scalar height when one compresses it through a carrier power.

Reflection is equally non-additive: the two forms

\[
N_c(x),
\qquad
(-1)^{c-1}N_c(-x-c-1)
\]

are the same polynomial value after the reflected change of coordinate.  They cannot be charged as two independent carrier factors.

## 6. The four-zero chain and the exact continuant dependence

For one lower characteristic, the full lower word on this event contains four relevant zeros.  After fixing orientation they can be written

\[
x_0<x_1<x_2<x_3,
\]

with consecutive gaps

\[
x_1-x_0=c,
\qquad
x_2-x_1=g_\ell,
\qquad
x_3-x_2=h.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The first gap comes from the endpoint/common-index pair; the last is the selected-colour gap.  The middle gap depends on the lower characteristic.

The naive hope is to use the two long returns from one root, e.g.

\[
N_{g_\ell}(x_1)=0,
\qquad
N_{g_\ell+h}(x_1)=0,
\]

as two additional independent conditions and then add the short carrier.  The restart identity kills this hope exactly.  Since the next level is a unit on the nonconsecutive/primitive locus,

\[
N_{g_\ell+h}(x_1)
=
N_{g_\ell+1}(x_1)
N_h(x_1+g_\ell)
\pmod\ell.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
N_{g_\ell}(x_1)=N_{g_\ell+h}(x_1)=0
\iff
N_{g_\ell}(x_1)=N_h(x_2)=0.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The integral Bezout form is stronger.  With

\[
S_d(x)=\prod_{j=2}^d(x+j),
\]

the source proves

\[
N_{e-1}(x+1)N_d(x)-N_{d-1}(x+1)N_e(x)
=S_d(x)^6N_{e-d}(x+d),
\tag{6.4}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
(N_d,N_e):S_d^\infty
=(N_d,N_{e-d}(x+d)):S_d^\infty.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is an equality of saturated ideals, not a heuristic.  The later return contains no new primitive component beyond the first return and restarted block.

The corresponding characteristic-zero resultant factorization is

\[
\boxed{
|\operatorname{Res}(N_d,N_e)|
=\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\bigl((j!)^3b_j\bigr)^6
\,|\mathcal R_{d,e-d}|.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus the apparent extra factors in the two-long-return resultant are exactly endpoint/cut-edge structural content plus **one** separated-block resultant.  The source explicitly notes that the subtraction step does not iterate after this split.

This is the precise algebraic dependence that collapses the hoped-for repeated carrier.

## 7. Universal content and reflection-center audit

### 7.1 Coefficient content of `N_h`

The source proves

\[
p\mid\operatorname{cont}(N_h)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h\ge2p
\tag{7.1}
\]

for shell primes `p>=7`.  Since every gap used here is below the defining lower prime, a retained lower shell prime is not coefficient content.

### 7.2 Boundary/cut-edge content

N_h(-r)
=(-1)^{r-1}b_{r-1}b_{h-r}
((r-1)!)^3((h-r)!)^3.
\tag{7.2}

The source remarks that the full boundary array is a **rank-one tensor**.  This explains why endpoint factors recur in raw resultants: they are products of the same Apéry boundary values, not transverse new certificates.

The central factor is structural.  The canonical central-deflation lemma removes it before forming the primitive aligned content.  Keeping both the raw and deflated equations as separate divisibility conditions would double-count the same factor.

## 8. Why the aligned quadruple content cannot be shared by the four lower primes

The aligned-content construction in `atom_tail_section.tex` is genuinely stronger than an ordinary scalar gcd because it records that the **same starting point** is a common root of several gap polynomials.  If a fixed gap triple `(a,b,c)` has `t` distinct starts modulo one prime, the content valuation is at least `t`.

It does not apply here with one fixed gap triple across the four lower characteristics.

For `(6.1)`, `c` and `h` are fixed after `m` and `y` are fixed, but

\[
g_\ell=A_\tau\ell-B_\tau(y,m)
\tag{8.1}
\]

with nonzero integer slope `A_tau`.  Consequently

\[
\ell\ne\ell'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
g_\ell\ne g_{\ell'}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The actual primitive statement is therefore

\[
\ell\mid\mathcal C^*_{c,g_\ell,h},
\tag{8.3}
\]

one **different** aligned-content integer for each lower characteristic.

It is false to replace `(8.3)` by

\[
\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3\ell_4
\mid\mathcal C^*_{c,g,h}
\]

for one fixed `g`.  Doing so would transpose a same-characteristic theorem across different primes.

Could Lucas reduce `g_ell` modulo `ell`?  No.  Lucas is a congruence for the Apéry index `n` in `b_n`.  The parameter `g` in `N_g` is the **length of a transfer product** and satisfies its own recurrence

\[
N_{g+1}(x)=P(x+g)N_g(x)-(x+g)^6N_{g-1}(x).
\]

There is no p-Lucas law that identifies the polynomial family member `N_{g_ell}` with `N_{g_0}` merely because `g_ell\equiv g_0\pmod\ell`.

This is the second exact quantifier obstruction.

## 9. Eliminating the lower characteristic: why every row collapses to the same constant root

The moving-zero formulas are affine in the lower characteristic.  Introduce an integer variable `X` standing for that lower prime and write the short carrier before evaluation as

\[
\mathscr C_y(X)=N_h(a_\tau X+A_y),
\qquad a_\tau\ne0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

For an actual lower prime `ell`, evaluation modulo `ell` sets `X=ell=0`.  Hence

\[
\mathscr C_y(\ell)
\equiv\mathscr C_y(0)=C_y
\pmod\ell.
\tag{9.2}
\]

That is exactly why all lower characteristics land on the same evaluated colour carrier.

Likewise the endpoint/common-index gap gives an affine polynomial

\[
\mathscr F_m(X)=N_c(a_mX+B_m)
\tag{9.3}
\]

with

\[
\mathscr F_m(\ell)
\equiv\mathscr F_m(0)=F_m
\pmod\ell.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Thus every lower prime in role `m` makes `X=0` a common root modulo `ell` of the two reductions

\[
\mathscr C_y(X),\qquad\mathscr F_m(X).
\tag{9.5}
\]

This formulation exposes a tempting resultant route.  One can form

\[
R_m=\operatorname{Res}_X(\mathscr C_y,\mathscr F_m).
\tag{9.6}
\]

Every lower prime in role `m` divides `R_m` (away from the already split leading/content exceptions).

But `(9.6)` is never a better carrier than the constant-term gcd: the same prime already divides

\[
\gcd(C_y,F_m).
\]

The resultant merely eliminates a common root which is known in advance to be `X=0`.

More quantitatively, after the fixed chart/reflection split the two continuants have degrees

\[
d_c=3(c-1),
\qquad
d_h=3(h-1)
\tag{9.7}
\]

(up to the one central deflation), and their affine starts differ by a translation of size `Theta(T)` in the common endpoint variable.  A standard resultant-height estimate gives

\[
\log|R_m|\ll c h L
\tag{9.8}
\]

plus the structural factors already removed in `(7.2)`--`(7.3)`.  Hence its shell-prime divisor bound is `O(ch)`, while the simple constant-term intersection already gives

\[
\omega_{\asymp T}(\gcd(C_y,F_m))
\ll\min(D,c).
\tag{9.9}
\]

Thus the resultant is not the missing compression.

There is an exact union/intersection obstruction at this polynomial level too.  To retain primes from **either** role one must use

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X
(\mathscr C_y,\mathscr F_m\mathscr F_{m'}).
\]

But resultants are multiplicative:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}
(\mathscr C_y,\mathscr F_m\mathscr F_{m'})
=
\operatorname{Res}(\mathscr C_y,\mathscr F_m)
\operatorname{Res}(\mathscr C_y,\mathscr F_{m'}).
}
\tag{9.10}
\]

So the union eliminant is literally the product of the two role eliminants; there is no cross term and no height saving.

Conversely, a pencil

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X
(\mathscr C_y,\mathscr F_m+T_0\mathscr F_{m'})
\]

has coefficient content divisible by a lower prime only when that prime makes the same root satisfy **both** role equations.  That is the one-prime-overlap sector, not the disjoint rectangle.

This is perhaps the sharpest algebraic statement of the obstruction:

```text
product resultant preserves the disjoint union but factors;
pencil/common-root content is genuinely transverse but sees only the intersection.
```

## 10. Why more equations at the same root do not create p^2

A possible escape would be to put endpoint, prefix, colour, and long-return equations into one larger Sylvester/pencil matrix and hope that several vanishing equations force several powers of the same lower prime.

The aligned-content normalization formula in `atom_tail_section.tex` rules out that inference.  For one normalized branch `alpha`, the p-adic content contribution is the local length

\[
\ell_{\mathbb Z_p}
\bigl(\mathcal O_\alpha/(G(\alpha),J(\alpha))\bigr).
\tag{10.1}
\]

On the primitive simple locus, if both values vanish to first order, `(10.1)` is `1`.  It is a **minimum/intersection length**, not the sum of the number of equations written down.

Equivalently, the elementary corank statement is

\[
t\text{ distinct common roots}
\Longrightarrow
v_p(\text{content})\ge t.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Our role incidence contributes one start in one lower field.  Adding more algebraically dependent equations at that same start does not turn `t=1` into `t=2`.

The final disjointness makes this especially binding.  A lower prime in `P` is not forced to occur in the `m'` role, and a lower prime in `P'` is not forced to occur in the `m` role.  Thus no lower prime is forced to contribute two distinct common roots or two independent role branches.  The only sector where such a repeated-prime mechanism could occur is the one-prime-overlap sector, which is already closed.

Derivative-degenerate/nontransverse roots could raise `(10.1)`, but the rectangle does not force that degeneracy.  Any argument relying on it leaves the simple-root subevent untouched.

## 11. Occurrence multiplicity: product versus gcd/lcm

The raw fourth-factorial envelope contains `R^2` because one lower pair can carry as many as

\[
r_P\le R=T^{1/2+o(1)}
\]

common indices.  A characteristic-zero radical does not consume a prime when the same prime pair is reused at another index.

For one pair `P`, form the honest product

\[
B_P:=\prod_{m\in R_y(P)}b_m.
\tag{11.1}
\]

Then

\[
(\ell_1\ell_2)^{r_P}\mid B_P.
\tag{11.2}
\]

So occurrence-to-valuation amplification is algebraically real.  But there are only two ways to combine it with the common carrier.

### Keep the short height

\[
\gcd(U_y,B_P)\mid U_y.
\]

Its valuation is a **minimum**.  On the valuation-one primitive cell it records only one copy of `ell_1ell_2`, regardless of `r_P`.

### Keep all `r_P` valuations

\[
\gcd(U_y^{r_P},B_P)
\]

can carry `ell_1^{r_P}ell_2^{r_P}`, but

\[
\log U_y^{r_P}=r_P\log U_y\ll r_PD L.
\]

The factor `r_P` cancels when divided by the forced valuation cost `r_P\log T`.

The same cancellation occurs for two disjoint roles.  Products preserve the occurrence multiplicity and multiply height; gcd/lcm compress height and replace valuation sums by minima/maxima.  There is no intermediate operation in the current theorem bank.

This is why the single common integer cannot attack the `R^2` part of the fourth-factorial envelope.

## 14. Scalar-interface saturation check

The following is only a countermodel to a proof using **only the exported scalar divisibility/height interfaces**; it is not a claim about the actual Apéry sequence.

\[

and put

\[

\[

All repeated-carrier constructions `(4.1)`--`(4.5)` are saturated: the same prime powers of `C` are reused for every private common index.  Nothing in scalar height prevents that reuse.

Again, this is not finite evidence for actual Apéry saturation.  It proves only that a successful proof must use additional recurrence information which correlates the **role subsets across different common indices**, not merely another integer divisible by the same lower primes.

## Bottom line

The common-endpoint radical **can** be compressed with the short colour carrier into one nonzero integer of height `O(D log T)`, and one can even manufacture a square (or any fixed power) of all four lower primes in one integer.  This does not help asymptotically: the required carrier power multiplies logarithmic height in the same proportion, and the same short-carrier prime powers can be reused by all `R^2` common-index occurrences.

The recurrence does not hide another independent factor.  After structural saturation, the continuant Bezout/restart identity identifies the second long return with the restarted short block; reflected formulas are identical up to sign; Lucas only transports the zero; the small aligned-content certificate has a middle gap that moves with the lower characteristic; and a union resultant factors into the two role resultants.  These are exact algebraic dependencies, not heuristic independence claims.

So this direct characteristic-zero attack does **not** beat the raw fourth-factorial envelope.  The unresolved input remains a genuinely joint four-characteristic, two-common-index codegree theorem at the stated `T^{-6s}L^{-14/5}` saving.
