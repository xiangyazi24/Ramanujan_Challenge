# Square-target incidence: exact determinant and large-sieve obstruction

## Verdict

**No bound of the requested strength follows from the square-target formulation, reflection, and the known cardinality bound alone.**  The transformation

\[
m\bmod p\in Z_p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(2m+1)^2\bmod p\in W_p
\]

is exact, but its apparent conic geometry is reducible: over each prime it is just the union of the two affine rays

\[
2m+1\equiv \pm(2r+1)\pmod p.
\]

Consequently:

1. the direct product-of-primes argument has a fixed factor \(X^3\) of Archimedean slack **per prime**;
2. the p-adic determinant method has only one incidence at each distinct prime, so it gets no quadratic determinant valuation;
3. the ordinary large sieve is at its critical \(Y=Q^2\asymp X^2\) scale and controls an average \(L^2\) norm, not an isolated aligned point;
4. most decisively, the repository already contains an explicit **anchored rainbow star** with reflection-invariant two-point sets for which every \(W_p\) is the singleton \(\{y_0^2\bmod p\}\), and hence
   \[
   K(m_0)=\pi(2X)-\pi(X)-O(1)\sim \frac X{\log X}.
   \]
   This model also satisfies substantially stronger local sparsity and bounded-codegree conditions.

Thus a Bombieri--Pila/Heath-Brown or arithmetic-large-sieve proof using only this square incidence geometry is mathematically impossible.  A successful argument must use a genuinely Apéry-specific **cross-prime anti-alignment theorem** not encoded by the sets' sizes or their being square targets.

The exact obstruction is already formalized in

```text
problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex
```

as Proposition `prop:hm3-anchored-star`.

## 1. Exact normalization: the square map is only the reflection quotient

Let

\[
\iota(r)=p-1-r.
\]

Then

\[
2\iota(r)+1\equiv -(2r+1)\pmod p.
\]

Moreover,

\[
(2r+1)^2\equiv(2s+1)^2\pmod p
\]

implies \(r=s\) or \(r=\iota(s)\).  Therefore the map

\[
Z_p/\langle\iota\rangle\longrightarrow W_p,
\qquad [r]\longmapsto(2r+1)^2
\]

is a bijection.

There is a small correction to the inequality in the question.  If

\[
\epsilon_p=1_{\{(p-1)/2\in Z_p\}},
\]

then the exact formula is

\[
\boxed{|W_p|=\frac{Z(p)+\epsilon_p}{2}\le\frac{Z(p)+1}{2}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The fixed point contributes \(w=0\).  This does not alter the \(O(p^{2/3})\) scale.

Define the root set

\[
\Omega_p=\{a\in\mathbb F_p:a^2\in W_p\}.
\]

Because \(Z_p\) is reflection-invariant,

\[
\boxed{\Omega_p=\{2r+1:r\in Z_p\},\qquad |\Omega_p|=Z(p).}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus for \(y=2m+1\),

\[
K(m)=\sum_{X<p\le2X}1_{\Omega_p}(y).
\tag{1.3}
\]

The square formulation has not reduced the number of allowed residue classes: it has merely quotiented the reflection pair in \(W_p\) and then restored both square roots in \(\Omega_p\).

## 2. The product divisibility is quantitatively tautological

Assume

\[
1\le y<4X^2
\]

and let \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\in(X,2X]\) be bad.  Choose the least residues

\[
0\le w_i<p_i,
\qquad y^2\equiv w_i\pmod{p_i},
\qquad w_i\in W_{p_i}.
\]

Then

\[
y^2-w_i=p_iq_i
\]

with

\[
|q_i|\le\frac{y^2+2X}{X}<16X^3+2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Set

\[
P=\prod_{i=1}^kp_i,
\qquad
F(T)=\prod_{i=1}^k(T^2-w_i).
\]

The exact identity is

\[
\boxed{F(y)=P\prod_{i=1}^kq_i.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Hence

\[
X^k<P\le |F(y)|\le(16X^4+2X)^k,
\tag{2.3}
\]

and the quotient in (2.2) can be as large as \(O(X^{3k})\).  Increasing \(k\) multiplies the modulus gain and the Archimedean loss by the same factor; it never changes the unfavorable ratio.

The coefficient height is equally explicit.  Since \(0\le w_i<2X\),

\[
H(F)\le(1+2X)^k,
\]

but evaluation at \(y\asymp X^2\) costs \(y^{2k}\asymp X^{4k}\).  The elementary zero-forcing condition

\[
P>|F(y)|
\]

would require \(X^k>X^{4k+o(k)}\), which is impossible for every positive \(k\).

### Linear factorization improves the slack only from \(X^3\) to \(X\)

Choose a lift \(z_i=2r_i+1\) with \(0<z_i<2p_i\).  Since

\[
p_i\mid y^2-z_i^2=(y-z_i)(y+z_i),
\]

there is a sign \(\varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}\) such that

\[
y-\varepsilon_i z_i=p_i\ell_i,
\qquad
|\ell_i|\le4X+4.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
P\mid\prod_{i=1}^k(y-\varepsilon_i z_i),
\]

but

\[
\left|\prod_{i=1}^k(y-\varepsilon_i z_i)\right|
\le(5X^2)^k.
\tag{2.5}
\]

This still leaves a factor \(X^k\) of slack.  Formula (2.4) is exactly the two-ray quotient-cell reduction in another notation; the degree-two conic has split into two lines.

### The CRT lift becomes \(y^2\) itself

Let \(W\in[0,P)\) be the CRT lift satisfying

\[
W\equiv w_i\pmod{p_i}\quad(1\le i\le k).
\]

But every \(w_i\equiv y^2\pmod{p_i}\), so

\[
W\equiv y^2\pmod P.
\]

For \(k\ge5\) and \(X>16\),

\[
P>X^5>16X^4>y^2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{W=y^2.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Thus the many moving targets do not produce a new small global integer.  Their CRT combination simply reconstructs the integer \(y^2\) that was used to select them.

## 3. Why the determinant method cannot amplify these congruences

The p-adic determinant method gains strength when **many points reduce to the same point or low-dimensional jet modulo one fixed prime**.  If \(s\) points cluster modulo \(p\), a determinant of monomials can acquire a valuation growing quadratically in \(s\), while its Archimedean height grows only according to the Hilbert function of a fixed variety.

Here the situation is the opposite:

- there is one integer \(y\);
- each prime \(p_i\) supplies one row-specific congruence;
- the modulus changes from row to row;
- the polynomial \(T^2-w_i\) also changes from row to row.

Each \(p_i\) therefore contributes at most one factor \(p_i\) to the natural block determinant/product.  There is no \(p_i^{s(s-1)/2}\) gain because \(s=1\) at every prime.  The linear growth

\[
\log P=k\log X+O(k)
\]

cannot beat either the quadratic-factor height \(4k\log X+O(k)\) or even the split-linear height \(2k\log X+O(k)\).

There is also no fixed bounded-degree Archimedean curve.  Lifting the congruence gives

\[
y^2-w=pq.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For fixed \(y\), this is a rational surface in \((p,q,w)\), not a curve.  Without the membership condition \(w\in W_p\), every prime \(p\in(X,2X]\) gives a point on (3.1), namely

\[
w=y^2-p\left\lfloor\frac{y^2}{p}\right\rfloor.
\]

So the ambient algebraic geometry itself genuinely contains \(\asymp X/\log X\) prime points.  All sparsity is hidden in the moving arithmetic condition \(w\in W_p\), and that condition is not represented by a fixed polynomial of bounded degree in \((p,w)\).

Equivalently, one may form

\[
H_p(T)=\prod_{w\in W_p}(T^2-w),
\]

but its degree is \(2|W_p|=O(X^{2/3})\), its coefficients vary with \(p\), and there is no common curve across primes.  Bombieri--Pila and Heath-Brown determinant estimates do not turn a family of unrelated, growing-degree polynomials into a cross-prime incidence theorem.

This is the exact determinant obstruction: **the necessary superlinear p-adic determinant valuation is absent, while the target-defining polynomial is neither fixed nor bounded-degree.**

## 4. A concrete counterexample: the anchored square star

The obstruction is not merely failed numerology.  Fix

\[
M=X^2,
\qquad
m_0=\left\lfloor\frac{3M}{4}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
y_0=2m_0+1.
\]

Delete the primes \(p\in(X,2X]\) dividing

\[
E=(m_0-1)m_0(m_0+1)(m_0+2)(2m_0+1).
\]

Only \(O(1)\) primes are deleted, because \(|E|\ll X^{10}\) and every deleted prime exceeds \(X\).

For every remaining prime put

\[
a_p=m_0\bmod p,
\qquad
Z_p^*=\{a_p,p-1-a_p\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

These sets are reflection-invariant and have size two.  The deletion ensures that they avoid the endpoint residues, their two points are distinct, and they are not consecutive.  They also satisfy the strong interval bound

\[
|Z_p^*\cap I|\le2\le2|I|^{2/3}
\]

for every nonempty interval \(I\).

Their square targets are

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_p^*
&=\{(2a_p+1)^2,(2(p-1-a_p)+1)^2\}\pmod p\\
&=\{y_0^2\bmod p\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore every active prime is bad at \(m_0\):

\[
\boxed{K^*(m_0)=\pi(2X)-\pi(X)-O(1)\sim\frac X{\log X}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This exceeds the desired \(X^{2/3+o(1)}/\log X\) by a factor \(X^{1/3-o(1)}\).

The repository proves more about this same model in `hm3_result.tex`:

- its global centered second moment has the natural large-sieve scale;
- every pair of distinct rows has codegree at most seven;
- it can satisfy the necessary reflection/gap certificates;
- it can be extended to a reflection-invariant value map with fibers of size at most two.

Thus even adding the currently available local sparsity, pair-codegree, and second-moment information does not repair the square-incidence route.

A minimal finite check of the construction is:

```python
from math import isqrt

def primes(a, b):
    sieve = bytearray(b + 1)
    out = []
    for n in range(2, b + 1):
        if not sieve[n]:
            if n >= a:
                out.append(n)
            if n <= isqrt(b):
                sieve[n*n:b+1:n] = b'\x01' * (((b - n*n)//n) + 1)
    return out

X = 1000
m0 = 3 * X * X // 4
y0 = 2 * m0 + 1
E = (m0-1)*m0*(m0+1)*(m0+2)*(2*m0+1)
K = 0
for p in primes(X+1, 2*X):
    if E % p == 0:
        continue
    a = m0 % p
    Z = {a, p-1-a}
    W = {(2*r+1)**2 % p for r in Z}
    assert W == {y0*y0 % p}
    assert (y0*y0 % p) in W
    K += 1
print(K)
```

This is a counterexample to the **methodological implication**, not a claim that these artificial sets are Apéry zero sets.  It proves that any successful proof must use additional Apéry-specific information that excludes the anchored star across different characteristics.

## 5. The exact arithmetic large-sieve bound and its failure at \(L^\infty\)

Let

\[
S_p=|\Omega_p|=Z(p),
\qquad
\lambda=\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{S_p}{p}.
\]

The known pointwise estimate \(S_p\ll p^{2/3+o(1)}\) gives

\[
\lambda\ll\frac{X^{2/3+o(1)}}{\log X}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Use the unnormalized Fourier transform

\[
\widehat{1_{\Omega_p}}(h)
=\sum_{a\in\Omega_p}e_p(-ha).
\]

Then, pointwise in \(y\),

\[
K(y)-\lambda
=\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac1p
\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}\widehat{1_{\Omega_p}}(h)e_p(hy).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The fractions \(h/p\) occurring in (5.2) are distinct and \(1/(4X^2)\)-separated.  The additive large sieve therefore gives, for \(Y\le4X^2\),

\[
\sum_{y\le Y}|K(y)-\lambda|^2
\ll (Y+4X^2)
\sum_{p}\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}
\left|\frac{\widehat{1_{\Omega_p}}(h)}p\right|^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\sum_{h=0}^{p-1}|\widehat{1_{\Omega_p}}(h)|^2=pS_p,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{y\le Y}|K(y)-\lambda|^2
\ll X^2\lambda
\ll\frac{X^{8/3+o(1)}}{\log X}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Taking one term out of this sum yields only

\[
K(y)\le\lambda+O\!\left(\frac{X^{4/3+o(1)}}{\sqrt{\log X}}\right),
\tag{5.5}
\]

which is worse than the trivial \(K(y)\le\pi(2X)-\pi(X)\).

This is not an artifact of Cauchy--Schwarz.  The anchored-star family has a natural-scale global centered second moment while retaining the isolated spike (4.3).  An \(L^2\) large sieve cannot rule out one exceptional integer.

### CRT capacity shows the same \(2/3\) barrier

For distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_t\), let

\[
N(p_1,\ldots,p_t)
=\#\{1\le y\le Y:y\bmod p_i\in\Omega_{p_i}\ \forall i\}.
\]

CRT gives the exact general bound

\[
N(p_1,\ldots,p_t)
\le\left(\frac{Y}{P}+1\right)\prod_{i=1}^tS_{p_i},
\qquad P=\prod_{i=1}^tp_i.
\tag{5.6}
\]

For \(p_i\asymp X\), \(Y\asymp X^2\), and \(S_{p_i}\ll X^{2/3+o(1)}\),

\[
N(p_1,\ldots,p_t)
\ll\left(X^{2-t}+1\right)X^{2t/3+o(t)}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

When \(t=3\), the target-class capacity is already

\[
X^{2t/3}=X^2,
\]

exactly the size of the ambient \(y\)-interval.  For \(t\ge3\), (5.7), after the trivial cap by \(Y\), gives no power saving at all.  This is the occupancy-one barrier in its sharp square-target form: **the exponent \(2/3\) makes three independent target sets large enough to cover the whole \(X^2\)-interval.**

## 6. What additional lemma would actually prove the desired maximum

A fixed-order determinant or fixed-order moment is insufficient.  To control the maximum at the mean-cardinality scale one needs a logarithmic-order cross-prime large-deviation estimate.

Put

\[
B_X=\frac{X^{2/3+o(1)}}{\log X},
\qquad
F_t(X)=\sum_{\substack{1\le y\le4X^2\\y\text{ odd}}}(K(y))_t.
\]

A concrete sufficient theorem is:

\[
\boxed{
F_t(X)\le 4X^2(CB_X)^t
\quad\text{for every }1\le t\le c\log X,
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

with absolute \(C,c>0\).

Indeed, suppose \(K(y_0)\ge4CB_X\).  Choose an integer

\[
t>\log_2(4X^2)
\]

with \(t\le c\log X\).  Since \(B_X\gg X^{2/3-o(1)}/\log X\gg\log X\), for large \(X\) one also has \(t\le K(y_0)/2\).  Hence

\[
(K(y_0))_t\ge\left(\frac{K(y_0)}2\right)^t
\ge(2CB_X)^t.
\]

But (6.1) would imply

\[
(2CB_X)^t\le4X^2(CB_X)^t,
\]

or \(2^t\le4X^2\), contradicting the choice of \(t\).  Therefore

\[
\max_yK(y)\le4CB_X.
\]

The content of (6.1) is precisely a **logarithmic-order mixed-prime CRT discrepancy theorem for the actual Apéry zero sets**.  It rules out the anchored star and every similar isolated alignment.  No standard determinant method or ordinary arithmetic large sieve supplies it.

## Bottom line

The square variable is useful as a clean notation for the reflection quotient, but it creates no new curvature or low-height carrier:

\[
y^2\equiv(2r+1)^2\pmod p
\]

splits immediately into the two affine rays already present in the quotient-cell formulation.  The proposed polynomial product gives only

\[
X^k\le P\mid F(y),
\qquad |F(y)|\le X^{4k+o(k)},
\]

and therefore no bound on \(k\).  The determinant method cannot amplify because each prime sees only one point, and the large sieve cannot pass from natural \(L^2\) control to an \(L^\infty\) statement in the presence of an isolated anchored spike.

So the exact answer is:

> **The square-against-moving-target formulation does not admit the requested \(X^{2/3+o(1)}/\log X\) determinant or standard large-sieve bound.  The missing input remains an Apéry-specific cross-characteristic anti-alignment/high-moment theorem such as (6.1).**
