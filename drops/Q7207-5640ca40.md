# Q7207: aligned-content route audit for weak projective dispersion

## 1. Setup and the possible route

Let a primitive off-center short return chain be represented by a root/alignment event for the Apéry projective sequence. For a fixed prime `p`, write `m_p(q)` for the number of primitive four-return windows carrying projective state `q`, and

\[
M_p=\sum_q m_p(q).
\]

The proposed route is to replace the long-bridge analysis by a direct estimate of `M_p`, using same-root aligned content quantities

\[
C_{a,b,c}
\]

and their primitive part `C^*`. The key question is whether these quantities control the first moment `M_p` strongly enough, or whether they can control the second moment

\[
\sum_q m_p(q)^2.
\]

The conclusion is negative with the current `AC^{tr}` input: it controls aligned root concentration in a first-moment sense, but it does not supply the missing projective-state second moment without a new non-diagonal tensor estimate.

---

## 2. What a same-root content bound actually gives

For fixed gap data `(a,b,c)`, a primitive chain requires simultaneous vanishing of the corresponding transfer continuants. If a common root occurs with multiplicity `r`, the valuation/content decomposition gives schematically

\[
C_{a,b,c}=\prod_\alpha (x-\alpha)^{e_\alpha},
\]

with primitive content

\[
C^*_{a,b,c}=\prod_\alpha (x-\alpha)^{e_\alpha^*}.
\]

A bound of the form

\[
\sum_{\alpha} e_\alpha \le B(a,b,c)
\]

implies only

\[
\#\{x:C_{a,b,c}(x)=0\}\le B(a,b,c),
\]

and hence after summing over gap triples,

\[
M_p\le \sum_{a,b,c} B(a,b,c).
\]

This is a first moment estimate. It counts chain occurrences but does not remember whether two chains land in the same projective state.

---

## 3. Relation to projective energy

The relevant quantity after reflection reduction is

\[
\overline V_p\le 7\overline M_p+E_p^{sep}.
\]

A direct mass bound would need something like

\[
\sum_{p\sim X} M_p^2\ll X^{2+o(1)}/\log X,
\]

or at least a saving over the trivial Cauchy bound.

However, `M_p` itself satisfies only

\[
M_p=\sum_q m_p(q),
\]

while the bad contribution is

\[
\sum_q m_p(q)^2.
\]

The implication

\[
\text{root multiplicity bound}\Rightarrow \text{state energy bound}
\]

fails because many different roots/gap configurations can correspond to the same projective state.

---

## 4. Why a tensor/pencil upgrade does not work automatically

One might try to square the aligned-content relation:

\[
(C_{a,b,c})^2
\]

or use a tensor product of two pencils. This introduces pairs of gap triples

\[
(a,b,c),(a',b',c').
\]

The diagonal locus

\[
(a,b,c)=(a',b',c')
\]

is harmless, but the genuine difficulty is the large off-diagonal aligned-root locus. A naive expansion has roughly

\[
H^6
\]

formal gap choices, and after summing over roots the diagonal contribution behaves like the forbidden `H^4`-scale term.

Therefore `AC^{tr}` cannot simply be tensorized to produce the required second moment.

---

## 5. Exact obstruction: parity/root alignment

The obstruction is not polynomial degree alone. It is that the same projective state condition is a quotient condition:

\[
[b_x:c_x]=[b_y:c_y].
\]

A common root of transfer polynomials records equality of two positions, but it does not record the complete orbit label of the resulting state.

Two chains may share:

* the same root of a transfer continuant,
* the same local aligned-content factor,
* the same reflection class,

while their middle bridge structure differs. The aligned content therefore detects root coincidence but not state collision multiplicity.

This is exactly the parity/root-alignment gap left after the reflection quotient.

---

## 6. Conditional theorem that would close the route

A sufficient new hypothesis would be a genuine second-moment aligned-content estimate:

\[
\sum_{a,b,c,a',b',c'}
\#\{x:
C^*_{a,b,c}(x)=C^*_{a',b',c'}(x)=0
\}
\ll H^{2+o(1)}.
\]

More precisely, after removing identical gap triples and reflection copies, one needs

\[
\sum_p\sum_q m_p(q)^2
\ll X^{2+o(1)}/\log X.
\]

Such an estimate is stronger than `AC^{tr}` and is a new non-concentration theorem.

---

## 7. Final verdict

`AC^{tr}` gives a useful first-moment control on primitive same-root aligned content:

\[
C^*\text{-valuation bounds}\Rightarrow M_p\text{ bounds}.
\]

It does not imply

\[
M_p\text{ bounds}\Rightarrow E_p^{sep}\text{ bounds},
\]

because the missing information is concentration among projective states.

The existing route therefore cannot eliminate long-bridge analysis. The minimal missing inequality is a second-moment non-concentration statement for aligned roots/projective states, not another first-moment content estimate.
