# Tensor-square decomposition is real, but it does not detect the zero fiber

## Executive verdict

The Clebsch–Gordan calculation is correct for the rank-three symmetric-square Apéry local system, but the proposed implication

```text
tensor-square cancellation  =>  ZM2
```

is **not valid by itself**.

There are four separate issues.

1. The standard Apéry K3 local system in `proof.tex` has rank three and connected geometric monodromy of
   \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathrm{SL}_2\simeq\mathrm{SO}_3\) type. A rank-four \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) object, if used elsewhere in the project, is a different auxiliary representation and has a different tensor decomposition.
2. \(\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal F\) linearizes the **square of a trace at a geometric point**. The Apéry numbers \(b_r\bmod p\) are instead Mellin coefficients of the geometric trace function. Products of two Mellin coefficients live naturally on an external product \(\mathcal F\boxtimes\mathcal F\), and only after character orthogonality can a diagonal tensor square appear.
3. The function \(H_rH_s\) does not detect the event \(H_r=H_s=0\). Its zero set is the **union** of the two coordinate zero sets. Exact zero detection over \(\mathbf F_p\) is the degree-\(p-1\) operation
   \[
   \mathbf 1_{\{x=0\}}=1-x^{p-1}.
   \]
   Thus a tensor square controls a quadratic moment, not the pinned zero-fiber moment.
4. Bounded rank, conductor, rigidity, and large monodromy do not imply that a trace function has only \(O(1)\) zero Mellin coefficients. The rank-two rigid Legendre/Deuring example has fixed geometric complexity and \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) monodromy, yet exactly half of its nontrivial defining-characteristic Mellin coefficients vanish.

What the tensor square **can** do is valuable: it identifies the invariant pairing, isolates the reflection/duality diagonal, and gives square-root cancellation for low-degree trace correlations away from self-twist loci. To reach \(\mathrm{ZM}_2\), one still needs a **pinned residual trace-zero sieve** or an equivalent two-point same-characteristic anti-concentration theorem.

The corrected hierarchy is

```text
Clebsch–Gordan + product monodromy
+ pinned residual trace-zero sieve at the 1/p^2 scale
        => ZM2 = O(1)
        => AFP.
```

Tensor-square cancellation alone supplies only the first line.

# 1. First correct the empirical second moment

Let
\[
u_p=\#\left\{1\le r\le \frac{p-3}{2}:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\right\}
\]
be the number of noncentral reflection orbits of zeros, and let
\[
\varepsilon_p=
\mathbf 1_{\{b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv0\pmod p\}}\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The reflection law gives
\[
|Z_p|=2u_p+\varepsilon_p.
\]

The Poisson law observed in the computations is the law
\[
u_p\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(1/2),
\]
not \(|Z_p|\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(1/2)\). Ignoring the very rare central term,
\[
\mathbf E|Z_p|=2\cdot\frac12=1,
\]
and
\[
\mathbf E|Z_p|^2
=4\,\mathbf E(u_p^2)
=4\left(\frac12+\frac14\right)
=3.
\]
Thus an empirical value near \(2.81\) is not suspicious; it is naturally converging toward the reflection-corrected prediction \(3\), not toward \(0.75\).

There is also a structural decomposition of the ordered off-diagonal pairs. When \(\varepsilon_p=0\),
\[
|Z_p|(|Z_p|-1)
=2u_p+4u_p(u_p-1).
\]
Here:

- \(2u_p\) is the forced graph \(s=p-1-r\) of the reflection involution;
- \(4u_p(u_p-1)\) consists of genuinely transverse pairs of distinct reflection orbits.

So removing only \(r=s\) is insufficient. There is a second noncancellative diagonal
\[
s=p-1-r.
\]
After folding the involution, this becomes the ordinary diagonal.

If
\[
D_p=\binom{u_p}{2},
\]
then, for \(\varepsilon_p=0\),
\[
|Z_p|^2=4u_p^2=4u_p+8D_p.
\]
With the central term restored, the discrepancy is only an \(O(1)\)-per-prime correction. Consequently

```text
average ZM2 bounded
```

is, up to an automatic \(O(\pi(X))\) term, essentially the same pair-orbit target as

```text
sum_p D_p = O(pi(X)).
```

Thus ZM2 is a clean reformulation of Target A, but not a representation-theoretic bypass of it.

# 2. The correct tensor decompositions

## 2.1 Rank-three symmetric-square object

Let \(V\) be a rank-two system. The general \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) Clebsch–Gordan formula is
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2V\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2V
\simeq
\operatorname{Sym}^4V
\oplus
\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\otimes\det V\bigr)
\oplus
(\det V)^2.
\]
After determinant/Tate normalization to \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), this becomes
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Sym}^2V\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2V
\simeq
\operatorname{Sym}^4V\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2V\oplus\mathbf 1
}
\]
with ranks \(5+3+1=9\).

Equivalently,
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(\operatorname{Sym}^2V)
\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^4V\oplus\mathbf 1,
\qquad
\bigwedge^2(\operatorname{Sym}^2V)
\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^2V.
\]

The one-dimensional summand is the invariant quadratic pairing. Arithmetically it may be a Tate or finite-order twist rather than a literally constant sheaf before normalization.

This is the decomposition relevant to the rank-three Apéry K3 variation described in `proof.tex` and `toric_fiber_sym2.tex`.

## 2.2 A genuine rank-four \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) object

If \(W\) is instead the standard four-dimensional representation of \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\), then
\[
W\otimes W
=\operatorname{Sym}^2W\oplus\bigwedge^2W
\simeq V_{2\omega_1}\oplus V_{\omega_2}\oplus\mathbf 1,
\]
with dimensions
\[
10+5+1=16.
\]

Therefore “\(\mathrm{Sp}_4\)” and “\(\mathrm{SL}_2\) via \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\)” are not interchangeable descriptions of one representation. The project must first specify which cohomological object is being tensored.

# 3. What Deligne cancellation from the decomposition really gives

Let \(t_{\mathcal F}(x)\) be the geometric trace function of the rank-three sheaf on the geometric parameter \(x\). After weight normalization,
\[
t_{\mathcal F}(x)^2
=t_{\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal F}(x)
=t_{\operatorname{Sym}^4V}(x)
+t_{\operatorname{Sym}^2V}(x)
+1.
\]
Hence for a nontrivial Kummer character \(\eta\), and assuming the two nontrivial summands have no geometrically constant constituent after twisting,
\[
\sum_{x\in U(\mathbf F_p)}
\eta(x)t_{\mathcal F}(x)^2
=O_{\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal F)}(\sqrt p)
\]
after the appropriate Weil normalization. The invariant line contributes
\[
\sum_x\eta(x)=0
\]
for nontrivial \(\eta\), while it supplies the expected main term for \(\eta=1\).

This is a genuine and useful theorem. It controls a **quadratic trace correlation in the geometric variable**.

It also has an exact Mellin-autocorrelation interpretation. Write
\[
M_p(r)=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
 t_{\mathcal F}(x)\chi_r(x),
\]
where, in the project normalization, the reduction of \(M_p(r)\) is a unit multiple of \(b_r\). Then character orthogonality gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_r M_p(r)M_p(r+h)
=(p-1)\sum_x
 t_{\mathcal F}(x)t_{\mathcal F}(x^{-1})
 \chi_h(x^{-1}).
}
\]
If inversion identifies \(\iota^*\mathcal F\simeq\mathcal F\), the right side is a Mellin coefficient of the tensor-square trace function, and Clebsch–Gordan plus Deligne controls it for nonexceptional \(h\).

So tensor-square theory can prove statements of the form

```text
low-degree autocorrelations of the Mellin values cancel,
except on dual/self-twist diagonals.
```

That is not the same as cancellation of
\[
\mathbf1_{\{M_p(r)=0\}}\mathbf1_{\{M_p(s)=0\}}.
\]

---

# 4. The main category error: Mellin coefficients are not stalk traces on an \(r\)-line

The geometric Apéry sheaf lives in a parameter such as \(x\) or \(t\). The coefficient index \(r\) labels a Kummer character. Schematically,
\[
M_p(r)
=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
R\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_r})
\right),
\qquad
M_p(r)\equiv c_{p,r}b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.
\]

Therefore
\[
M_p(r)M_p(s)
\]
is the trace on a tensor product of two **global Mellin cohomology groups**. By Künneth it is naturally associated with
\[
R\Gamma_c\!\left(
U\times U,
(\mathcal F\boxtimes\mathcal F)
\otimes
(\mathcal L_{\chi_r}\boxtimes\mathcal L_{\chi_s})
\right).
\]
It is not, before a diagonal restriction or an orthogonality collapse, the cohomology of
\[
\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal F
\]
on the original curve.

Thus the assertion

> \(P(r,s)=H_p(r)H_p(s)\) is a rank-nine trace function on the character plane

requires a new construction: a bounded-complexity sheaf over a genuine algebraic parameter space whose points are the tame characters. The repository currently has bounded geometry **for each twist**, but not a fixed bounded-conductor sheaf on an additive \(r\)-line with trace \(r\mapsto b_r\).

# 5. A product of traces is not a pair-zero detector

Even granting a trace realization in \(r\), the product
\[
H_p(r)H_p(s)
\]
does not detect simultaneous zeros. Indeed,
\[
H_p(r)H_p(s)=0
\]
means
\[
H_p(r)=0\quad\text{or}\quad H_p(s)=0,
\]
not “and.”

The exact defining-characteristic detector is
\[
\delta_0(x)=1-x^{p-1}.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbf1_{\{H_p(r)=0\}}
\mathbf1_{\{H_p(s)=0\}}
=
\bigl(1-H_p(r)^{p-1}\bigr)
\bigl(1-H_p(s)^{p-1}\bigr).
\]

The degree \(p-1\) is unavoidable: a nonzero polynomial that vanishes on every element of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) has degree at least \(p-1\). In tensor language, exact zero detection by polynomial moments requires tensor powers whose order grows with \(p\), not merely \(\mathcal F^{\otimes2}\).

The equivalent additive-character formula is
\[
\mathbf1_{\{H=0\}}
=\frac1p\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}\psi_p(aH).
\]
For the pair count,
\[
|Z_p|^2
=\frac1{p^2}
\sum_{a,b\in\mathbf F_p}
\sum_{r,s}
\psi_p\bigl(aH_p(r)+bH_p(s)\bigr).
\]
This makes clear that a successful argument must control **all** additive frequencies coherently. The tensor-square decomposition controls a low-degree polynomial class function; it does not perform this Fourier inversion at the pinned value zero.

---

# 6. Why individual Deligne bounds still miss the required scale

Suppose, much more strongly than is presently available, that for every nonzero \(a\),
\[
\left|\sum_r\psi_p(aH_p(r))\right|\ll\sqrt p.
\]
Then the one-point zero detector gives only
\[
|Z_p|\ll\sqrt p.
\]
The factor \(1/p\) in Fourier inversion is canceled by the \(p-1\) nonzero frequencies.

For the two-point detector, a bounded-conductor two-variable sheaf would normally give a Deligne bound of size \(O(p)\) for each nontrivial frequency pair \((a,b)\), because the parameter space has \(p^2\) points. Summing over \(p^2\) frequencies and dividing by \(p^2\) still gives only
\[
O(p),
\]
not \(O(1)\).

Therefore even a hypothetical bounded-conductor sheaf for every fixed \((a,b)\) would not close ZM2 through termwise absolute values. One needs one of the following:

- cancellation **across the frequency family** \((a,b)\);
- a direct geometric description of the pinned fiber as a bounded zero-dimensional scheme;
- or a residual Frobenius sieve that counts the trace-zero conjugacy condition without expanding the delta function frequency by frequency.

This is the same pinned-fiber obstruction isolated in Q605.

---

# 7. “Bounded conductor implies \(O(1)\) trace zeros” is false

Conductor controls complete sums, Betti numbers, and ramification. It does not make the trace function a rational function of bounded degree, and it does not bound the size of a prescribed value fiber.

The exact rank-two Legendre example already disproves the proposed principle in the character direction. Put
\[
m=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
D_p(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\binom mn^2t^n.
\]
This is the Deuring Hasse polynomial of the rigid rank-two Legendre sheaf, whose geometric monodromy is \(\mathrm{SL}_2\). For \(1\le j\le p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality gives
\[
\widehat D_p(j)
:=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}D_p(t)t^{-j}
=
\begin{cases}
-\binom mj^2,&1\le j\le m,\\[2mm]
0,&m<j\le p-2.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus
\[
\#\{j:\widehat D_p(j)=0\}=\frac{p-3}{2}
\]
despite fixed rank, fixed singularity data, rigidity, and large \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) monodromy.

Likewise, pulling a large-monodromy sheaf back by \(t\mapsto t^2\) produces an even trace function; all odd Mellin characters then vanish identically. Fixed conductor is compatible with \(\gg p\) exact Mellin zeros.

Katz’s bounded set of bad Kummer characters is a different notion. It bounds characters for which the forget-supports map or cohomological dimension behaves exceptionally. It does **not** bound characters for which the Frobenius trace is zero, and still less those for which a generally nonzero algebraic trace is divisible by the defining prime \(p\).

# 8. Is there an \(O(1)\)-conductor Apéry sheaf on the character torus?

There are three distinct statements.

1. **Original geometric sheaf.** The Apéry K3/elliptic local system on its geometric parameter curve has bounded rank and conductor. Yes.
2. **One fixed Kummer twist.** Grothendieck–Ogg–Shafarevich gives a uniformly bounded Mellin-cohomology dimension outside a bounded structural exceptional set. Yes.
3. **All \(p-1\) tame characters as points of one parameter variety.** The repository does not have a fixed \(\mathbf F_p\)-sheaf on an \(r\)-line whose trace is \(b_r\) and whose conductor is bounded independently of \(p\). No such theorem is presently known.

Katz’s convolution/Mellin formalism gives a Tannakian family of character fibers, but that is not the same as a bounded-degree regular function on the cyclic index set. Moreover, the relevant event is reduction modulo the **same** prime that defines the field. Standard \(\ell\)-adic equidistribution keeps \(\ell\ne p\) fixed and controls continuous class functions; it does not resolve this diagonal same-characteristic trace-zero event.

Q564’s flat Newton polygon and the \(p-1\) tame branches are consistent with this obstruction: the naive analytic/algebraic interpolation in the character direction has complexity growing with \(p\).

So the answer to the conductor question is:

```text
The original Apéry sheaf has O(1) conductor.
The function r -> b_r on the full tame-character set is not known
as a bounded-conductor trace function on a fixed character torus.
Even bounded conductor would not, by itself, bound its zero fiber.
```

# 9. What the invariant line and product monodromy can still buy

The tensor-square viewpoint is not useless. It identifies exactly the structural correlations that must be removed.

## 9.1 Pairing/reflection diagonal

For a self-dual object, an invariant can appear in a product of twists only when the two characters are dual, up to a finite Kummer self-twist:
\[
\chi_s\simeq\chi_r^{-1}\otimes\chi_0.
\]
In the Apéry situation the principal such locus is
\[
s=p-1-r,
\]
which is exactly the palindromic reflection. This is why folding to \(u_p\) is the correct first step.

## 9.2 Away from self-twist loci

If one proves that two distinct folded twists have product geometric monodromy, then the external tensor product has no invariant line. Deligne/Katz can then give cancellation for polynomial trace correlations of those two twists.

This is an important ingredient for a future Frobenius sieve, because it supplies the analogue of pairwise independence. It still does not turn the trace-zero indicator into a bounded-degree trace correlation.

## 9.3 Low moments versus an atom

Clebsch–Gordan controls low moments such as
\[
\sum_r M_p(r)M_p(r+h).
\]
ZM2 is a moment of the **atom at zero**:
\[
\sum_r\mathbf1_{\{M_p(r)=0\}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sum_{r,s}\mathbf1_{\{M_p(r)=0\}}\mathbf1_{\{M_p(s)=0\}}.
\]
Low polynomial moments do not determine one distinguished atom. That is the remaining anti-concentration gap.

# 10. The additional input actually needed

A tensor-square proof of ZM2 would need a theorem substantially stronger than ordinary Deligne cancellation. A useful target can be stated as follows.

Let \(I_p\) be the folded character set, and let \(\Phi_{p,r}\) be an integral Frobenius object with
\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi_{p,r})=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
\]
One needs
\[
\sum_{p\le X}
\#\left\{(r,s)\in I_p^2:r\ne s,
\operatorname{tr}\Phi_{p,r}=\operatorname{tr}\Phi_{p,s}=0
\right\}
\ll\pi(X).
\]

A Frobenius-sieve route would require all of the following.

1. **A universal integral Mellin family** over the tame-character branches, not merely one sheaf for each fixed twist.
2. **A same-characteristic trace comparison** identifying the residual trace-zero condition with \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\).
3. **Product monodromy** for distinct folded twists, with all duality and Kummer self-twist diagonals explicitly removed.
4. **A residual trace-zero density computation**, heuristically \(\asymp1/p\) for one trace and \(\asymp1/p^2\) for two independent traces.
5. **An averaged large-sieve/Chebotarev error at the atomic scale.** Ordinary square-root error is much larger than the expected \(O(1)\) count per prime.
6. **Uniformity as the residual characteristic varies.** Standard fixed-\(\ell\) monodromy theorems do not give this diagonal \(\ell=p\) statement.

This is precisely a pinned two-point residual anti-concentration theorem—essentially TCMA2/Target A in geometric language.

Two alternative routes would also work:

- construct a bounded-degree algebraic map from a genuine character parameter curve whose zero fiber is zero-dimensional; Q564/Q609 give strong evidence that the naive interpolation does not have this property;
- exploit the order-two transfer recurrence directly, using gap/return certificates and projective orbit mixing. This is currently the most concrete Apéry-specific route because it addresses the index variable without pretending it is the geometric K3 parameter.

# 11. Direct answers to (A)–(D)

## (A) Decomposition

Yes, for \(\mathcal F=\operatorname{Sym}^2V\) one has, after determinant normalization,
\[
\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal F
\simeq
\operatorname{Sym}^4V\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2V\oplus\mathbf1.
\]
For a standard rank-four \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) representation the corresponding decomposition is \(10\oplus5\oplus1\). These are different objects.

The trivial component is the pairing. It produces the main term in same-point quadratic moments and identifies the dual/reflection exceptional locus. It does not itself count coefficient zeros.

## (B) Pair correlation and the value \(2.81\)

\(H_p(r)H_p(s)\) is not the pair-zero indicator. Moreover, \(b_r\) is a Mellin coefficient, not a stalk trace of the original rank-three sheaf in an \(r\)-variable.

The observed Poisson law is for \(u_p=|Z_p|/2\), so the predicted full second moment is
\[
\mathbf E|Z_p|^2=3.
\]
Hence \(2.81\) is compatible with the model.

## (C) Clebsch–Gordan and off-diagonal cancellation

It gives square-root cancellation for nontrivial Mellin transforms of the **quadratic geometric trace function**, and hence for certain autocorrelations of the Mellin values. It also removes invariants away from the duality graph.

It does **not** imply
\[
\sum_{r\ne s}
\mathbf1_{\{b_r=0\}}\mathbf1_{\{b_s=0\}}=O(1).
\]
That implication needs a zero detector plus a family-level residual sieve.

## (D) Additional input and conductor

The required extra input is a pinned same-characteristic two-point equidistribution/large-sieve theorem at density \(1/p^2\), with product monodromy and reflection/self-twist diagonals removed.

The original geometric sheaf has bounded conductor. The full character-index function \(r\mapsto b_r\) is not known to arise from a bounded-conductor sheaf on a fixed character torus, and bounded conductor alone would not imply an \(O(1)\) zero set.

# Final assessment

Q617 correctly observed that ZM2 implies AFP, but its proposed geometric bridge should be downgraded from an implication to a research interface.

The accurate statement is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{tensor-square decomposition and Deligne cancellation}\\
\text{control low-degree trace correlations and isolate reflection;}\\[1mm]
\text{a new pinned residual two-point sieve is still required for ZM2.}
\end{gathered}}
\]

The tensor square is therefore a useful **ingredient**, not the missing zero-count theorem itself.
