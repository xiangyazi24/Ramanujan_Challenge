# Verdict

The fixed \(q\)-primitive two-value Apéry gcd is **not remotely of full Apéry height in the completed range**. It is usually \(1\), and when it is nontrivial it is normally a product of one or two small primes.

I completed an exact-integer census for every actual selected state

\[
17\le q\le 10{,}000,
\qquad q\in\mathbb P,
\qquad 0\le t<q,
\qquad q\mid b_t,

with both raw signs. This gives \(1{,}171\) selected states and \(2{,}342\) exact sign records. The largest Apéry index evaluated was \(128{,}706\); the largest exact Apéry integer used had \(654{,}599\) bits. Every gcd, every \(q\)-primary removal, every fixed-prime saturation, and every admissible-window divisibility test was exact.

The size-accounting route consequently looks **genuinely promising**, especially on the minus raw-window and M5-capable sectors. The fixed gcd is tiny throughout this range even though the individual Apéry values have hundreds of thousands of bits.

# 1. Exact object computed

Let

\[
b_0=1,\qquad b_1=5,
\]

and

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}
=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\]

Fix one actual selected state \(x=(q,t)\). Put

\[
N_6=6q+t,
\qquad
N_+=12q+t,
\qquad
N_-=q-1-t.
\]

The raw two-value gcds are

\[
G_+^{\rm raw}(q,t)=\gcd(b_{N_6},b_{N_+}),
\]

\[
G_-^{\rm raw}(q,t)=\gcd(b_{N_6},b_{N_-}).
\]

Every power of the selected upper prime \(q\) is removed. Then every power of the fixed saturated coefficient primes is removed.

The exact fixed values and prime supports used by the scanner are

```text
b_1  = 5
b_6  = 21460825
b_12 = 12073365010564729
b_13 = 364713572395983725

prime support b_1  = {5}
prime support b_6  = {5, 858433}
prime support b_12 = {12073365010564729}
prime support b_13 = {5, 17, 315493, 2720027329}
```

Accordingly,

```text

  = {2,3,5,7,17,315493,858433,2720027329}

E_plus, full fixed ledger
  = E_plus union {12073365010564729}

E_minus
  = {2,3,5,7,858433}
```

The reported \(G_+\) uses the full fixed ledger. The program also retains the lower-row-only version and audits the difference. Through \(q=10{,}000\), stripping the \(b_{12}\) prime changes **zero** plus records, so the numerical conclusions are invariant under that convention choice.

Thus, explicitly,

\[
G_\sigma(q,t)
=
\left(G_\sigma^{\rm raw}(q,t)\right)_{
qE_\sigma\text{-primitive}}.
\]

No large primitive radical is stripped. Small prime factors not belonging to the fixed ledger remain in \(G_\sigma\).

# 2. Literal admissible-window audit

For each exact prime factor \(p<q\) of \(G_\sigma(q,t)\), the scanner tests the literal raw quotient residues

\[
r_6=6q+t-6p,
\]

and

\[
r_+=12q+t-13p,
\qquad
r_-=q-1-t-p.
\]

It retains \(p\) only when:

```text
p is prime and p>=17;
p lies in the exact raw sign window;
0<=r_6,r_sigma<p;
p divides b_{r_6} and b_{r_sigma};
the fixed residual/exceptional exclusions hold;
for plus, the raw-minus-first tie-break is applied at the same (q,t,p).
```

The exact result through \(q=10{,}000\) is

\[
\boxed{
\text{raw plus leaves}
=
\text{raw minus leaves}
=
0.
}
\]

So the maximum number of admissible-window prime divisors is \(0\), not merely \(1\). There is no two-prime witness to report.

This is a finite theorem about the scanned range, not an asymptotic theorem.

# 7. Exact finite size certificate for lower-fibre uniqueness

The scan gives the stronger direct result that the admissible support is empty. It also supplies a pure size proof of uniqueness throughout the completed range.

Every admissible lower prime satisfies the raw lower bound

\[
p>\frac{6q+t}{7}>\frac{6q}{7}.
\]

Two distinct admissible primes would therefore force

\[
G_\sigma(q,t)>\left(\frac{6q}{7}\right)^2.
\]

For \(q\le5{,}000\), the augmented scanner checks the exact state-dependent lower bound and certifies every record individually.

For \(5{,}000<q\le10{,}000\),

\[
\left(\frac{6q}{7}\right)^2>18{,}000{,}000,
\]

while the global completed-range maxima are

\[
G_+\le253{,}783,
\qquad
G_-\le10{,}013,
\qquad
G_-^{\rm raw-window}\le1{,}159.
\]

Hence two admissible primes are impossible by size alone. Therefore:

> **Finite restricted-radical spacing theorem through \(10{,}000\).** For every actual selected state and either raw sign in the completed range, the fixed saturated two-value gcd has at most one admissible-window prime divisor. In fact it has none.

The exact direct factor check and the independent size comparison agree.

# 8. Consequence for route selection

Before this computation, two sharply different possibilities remained:

```text
A. G is commonly of exponential Apéry height.
   Then size accounting is hopeless.

B. The two affine values have an exceptionally small q-primitive gcd.
   Then restricted-radical spacing may be approachable by size.
```

The completed data strongly select **B as the finite-scale description**.

The individual values \(b_{6q+t}\) and \(b_{12q+t}\) have logarithms of order \(q\), but their saturated gcd is normally \(1\) and never exceeds six decimal digits through \(q=10{,}000\). The q-free common part therefore exhibits massive cancellation/sparsity not visible from separate value heights.

This makes the following theorem a serious target:

\[
\boxed{
G_\sigma(q,t)
<
\left(p_{\min,\sigma}(q,t)\right)^2
}
\]

for every actual selected state, where \(p_{\min,\sigma}\) is the first admissible prime in the raw window. This inequality alone proves Q6-SLUNI.

A weaker asymptotic bound such as

\[
G_\sigma(q,t)\le q^{2-\delta}
\]

for one fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly outside an actual marked-mass exceptional set, would already prove uniqueness for large \(q\). The data are far below even that threshold.

What is still missing is a proof mechanism. The earlier scalar-height and Fitting no-gos say that one cannot compress a *known set of endpoint primes* below their radical information floor. They do not bound the fixed gcd itself from above and do not preclude a horizontal gcd theorem. The present computation shows that such an upper-bound theorem would match the observed arithmetic.

My route verdict is therefore:

```text
plus size-accounting route:       promising, unproved
minus raw-window route:           very promising, unproved
M5-capable minus route:            strongest finite evidence, unproved
claim of global bounded G:         not justified
claim of Q6-SLUNI theorem:         not justified beyond q<=10000
```
