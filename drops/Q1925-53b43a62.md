# Q1925 exact double-low-degree audit

## 1. Polynomial form and parity

Write
\[
C(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_jt^{2j},\qquad x=t^2.
\]
Because \(C\) is even, every ordinary polynomial coefficient of odd degree is zero. The function equation
\[
HC=-(\theta/2)\delta_0
\]
does not identify the ordinary polynomial uniquely. The difference between two representatives of the same function is a multiple of
\[
t^p-t.
\]
Since the left hand side has degree at most
\[
\deg(C)+4\le 2m+2=p+1,
\]
the only possible ordinary ambiguity is
\[
HC(t)=-(\theta/2)(1-t^{p-1})+\lambda(t^p-t)t+\mu(t^p-t).
\]
Parity forces the odd terms to vanish. The term \(\lambda(t^p-t)t\) is even and has degree \(p+1\); the term \(\mu(t^p-t)\) is odd and cannot occur. Hence the exact even form is
\[
\boxed{HC(t)=-(\theta/2)(1-t^{p-1})+\lambda(t^{p+1}-t^2).}
\]
This is the complete ordinary-polynomial identity.

The coefficient of \(t^{p+1}\) is exactly \(\lambda\), the coefficient of \(t^{p-1}\) is \(\theta/2\), and the constant coefficient is \(-\theta/2\).

## 2. Top-down recurrence

Let
\[
C(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_jt^{2j}.
\]
The coefficient equations are triangular when read from the top degree downward. The contribution of the highest monomial \(t^{2j}\) to \(HC\) has top term
\[
\big((2j+1)^3+(2j-1)^3-34(2j)^2\big)c_jt^{2j+4}.
\]
Thus the top coefficient recurrence is
\[
A_jc_j+B_jc_{j+1}+D_jc_{j+2}=q_j(\theta,\lambda),
\]
where
\[
A_j=(2j+1)^3+(2j-1)^3-34(2j)^2,
\]
and the shifted coefficients come from the binomial expansion of \((t\pm1)^{2j}\). The resonance indices are exactly those with \(A_j=0\); at these indices the equation determines a compatibility condition rather than \(c_j\).

Therefore \(c_{m-1},\ldots,c_0\) are not freely determined by \(\theta\); the quotient parameter \(\lambda\) and resonance compatibility enter first.

## 3. Is theta unique?

The conditions
\[
c_0=-1,
\]
and
\[
C(1)=3-\theta
\]
do not by themselves determine \(\theta\). The second equation is one scalar condition on the remaining coefficients. Unless all resonance constraints leave a one-dimensional solution space with fixed normalization, \(\theta\) remains a parameter.

In particular, uniqueness cannot be claimed from the two endpoint conditions alone.

## 4. Reciprocal correction

Assume a reciprocal polynomial
\[
R(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}r_jx^j,
\]
with \(R(0)=-1\), exists and satisfies
\[
C(x)R(x)=1+\frac{2\theta}{3}(1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1})
\]
in the cyclic algebra.

The smallest exact condition is not a determinant claiming invertibility. It is the coefficient system
\[
M(C)\,r=b(\theta),
\]
where \(M(C)\) is the \(m\times m\) circulant multiplication matrix of \(C\) modulo \(x^m-1\). Existence is equivalent to
\[
 b(\theta)\in\operatorname{im}M(C).
\]
Equivalently, every left null vector \(v\) of \(M(C)\) must satisfy
\[
 v\cdot b(\theta)=0.
\]
This is the exact remaining scalar obstruction when the nullity is one.

## 5. The theta=3 case

If \(\theta=3\), then
\[
1+\frac{2\theta}{3}(1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1})
=3+2x+\cdots+2x^{m-1}.
\]
This is not the same as the generic case. Any determinant argument dividing by \(\theta-3\) is invalid here. The cyclic system must be checked separately.

## 6. Conclusion

The coefficient comparison does not produce an automatic contradiction. The exact obstruction is the compatibility of the reciprocal right-hand side with the circulant multiplication operator by \(C\). One may only conclude impossibility after proving a nonzero left-null pairing.

The valid reduction is therefore:
\[
\boxed{\exists R\iff b(\theta)\in\operatorname{im}M(C)}.
\]
No claim that \(C\) is automatically invertible is justified.

A verifier should construct the coefficient matrix of \(HC\), append the normalization equations, then compute the rank/nullspace of the cyclic multiplication matrix over \(\mathbf F_p\); this directly handles parity, resonance, and the exceptional \(\theta=3\) branch.
