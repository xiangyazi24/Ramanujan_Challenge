## 2. Exact mass-capacity extremal theorem

Let the finite capacities be sorted as

```text
c_1 >= c_2 >= ... >= c_N >= 0.
```

Put

```text
M_*:=min(floor(M),sum_i c_i),
C_j:=sum_(i<=j)c_i.
```

Choose `j` so that

```text
C_j <= M_* < C_(j+1),
```

with the obvious endpoint conventions, and put

```text
r:=M_*-C_j.
```

### Theorem 2.1 — exact variable-capacity maximum

For every integer load vector satisfying

```text
0<=A_i<=c_i,
sum_i A_i<=M,
```

one has

```text
boxed:
sum_i f_K(A_i)
 <= sum_(i<=j) f_K(c_i)+f_K(r).                        (2.1)
```

Equality is attained by

```text
A_i=c_i  for i<=j,
A_(j+1)=r,
A_i=0    afterward.
```

#### Proof

The discrete increments

```text
f_K(a+1)-f_K(a)
```

are nondecreasing in `a`: they are zero up to the threshold and then grow linearly. Thus `f_K` is an increasing discrete convex function with `f_K(0)=0`.

The displayed filled vector majorizes every vector with the same total mass and the coordinate caps `c_i`: for every prefix `m`, its first `m` coordinates have sum

```text
min(M_*,sum_(i<=m)c_i),
```

the largest possible prefix sum. Karamata's inequality, or the elementary operation of moving one unit from a smaller partially filled coordinate to a larger nonfull coordinate, proves `(2.1)`.

This is the exact load-distribution theorem. No entropy argument can improve it without adding information that excludes the cap-filled vector.

### Uniform capacity

If `c_i<=C`, let

```text
n=floor(M_*/C),
r=M_*-nC.
```

Then

```text
boxed:
T_K(A)<=n f_K(C)+f_K(r).                               (2.2)
```

Since `f_K(a)/a` is increasing on the active range,

```text
boxed:
T_K(A)<=M_* f_K(C)/C.                                  (2.3)
```

The coefficient `f_K(C)/C` is optimal: take all nonzero loads equal to `C`, apart from a possible final remainder.

### Exact layer-cake form

If

```text
N_h:=#{x:A_x>=h},
```

then

```text
T_K(A)=2 sum_(u>=1) u N_(K+u+1).                       (2.4)
```

The cap-filled vector is also the extremizer for the entire nested tail profile in `(2.4)`. Applying Markov separately at every `h` and then summing can manufacture a logarithm, but it cannot beat `(2.2)`; the separate Markov inequalities are not simultaneously sharper than the majorizing profile.

## 6. Why the lower zero moments do not improve the right tail

View the inverse leaves as a bipartite simple graph:

```text
left vertices:   numerical lower primes p;
right vertices:  exact pre-upper states x=(q,t,tau);
edge p--x:       Y_(p,q)(t)=1.
```

Then

```text
A_x = degree of the right vertex x.
```

For one lower prime, inverse fibre one bounds its left degree by the number of available ordered lower-zero pairs, hence schematically by `O((Z(p))_2+Z(p))`. The banked lower zero moments therefore control the sum of left degrees and related left moments.

They do not prevent every left vertex from using one of its allowed pairs to hit the same right vertex. More generally, they do not prevent many right vertices from sharing essentially the same set of lower primes. This is exactly the star obstruction.

No deterministic inequality can turn a left-degree moment into a right-degree entropy gain without a restriction on the neighborhoods. Such a restriction would be a new horizontal cross-characteristic theorem, not an entropy lemma.

## 7. A q6 integral-window countermodel at the banked scales

```text
1<=j<=R,                                               (7.3)
```

and put `t_j=0`. A fixed macroscopic interval contains `asymp T/L` primes, so `(7.3)` is possible because `R=o(T/L)`.

Define the common lower-prime interval


with the exact floor endpoints moved inward by `O(1)`. By `(7.1)`, it has positive fixed length. Let

```text
P={p prime:p in I_P},
C=|P| asymp_(J,epsilon) T/L.                           (7.5)
```

For every `p in P` and every state `j`, put

```text
d_j=q_j-p,
a_j=d_j-1=q_j-p-1,
r_j=6d_j=6(q_j-p).                                     (7.6)
```

All exact minus predicates hold:


Thus, if `a_j` and `r_j` are lower zeros modulo `p`, `(p,q_j,0)` is a legal minus inverse leaf.

The four coordinate types in `(7.7)` are affine functions of `p`, with slopes

```text

Z_p^sharp has no consecutive elements;
every surviving leaf has both required lower zeros;
|Z_p^sharp|<=4R.                                       (7.8)
```


the pointwise bank `Z(p)<<p^(2/3)` is respected with room to spare.

The aggregate lower-zero moments satisfy


Thus the first and second lower-zero moment scales are both respected.

Let `A_j` be the surviving load at state `(q_j,0)`. Since only `O(R^2)` of the initial `RC` edges were deleted and `R=o(C)`,


## 8. What new input would improve the bound

Any one of the following would be genuinely new and would break the extremizer:

1. **A support-blind mixed radical moment**

   ```text
   sum_x A_x(C_x-1)
   ```

   at a scale smaller than the cap-filled model. This charges false-positive radical support and is not implied by the actual pair moment.

2. **A radical-capacity tail theorem** controlling the sorted capacities, for example a strong estimate for

   ```text
   sum_(x:C_x>u) C_x
   ```

   uniformly in `u`.

3. **A right-state entropy theorem** proving entropy strictly above the minimum `log(M/C)` forced by the cap.

4. **A higher pre-upper load moment**, such as the `PRE-F3` or `PRE-F4` thresholds isolated in Q7556.

5. **A horizontal zero-pair distribution theorem** preventing one deterministic q6 affine section from hitting the varying lower zero sets for nearly every lower prime.

The present banks contain none of these.

## Final answer

The exact deterministic shifted-tail bound is the cap-filling majorization formula `(2.1)`. With a uniform radical capacity it becomes `(2.2)`, and with the banked pre-upper factorial moment its sharp theorem-facing form is

```text
sum_x((A_x-K)_+)_2
 <= [((C-K)_+)_2/(C)_2] F2_pre.                        (8.1)
```

At the current scales this is asymptotically best possible. To reach `o(Phi_T)`, the shift must satisfy

```text
C-K=o(T^(9/10)(log T)^(-17/10)),
```

equivalently `(C-K)/C=o(T^(-1/10)(log T)^(-7/10))`. A fixed-fraction or mesoscopic shift does not gain even one logarithm.

The lower-zero moments constrain the supply of leaves per lower prime but not their concentration among upper states. Exact q6 primality, integrality, and window restrictions do not change that conclusion: the construction in Section 7 realizes the cap-filled extremizer at all current marginal scales.

Therefore the proposed deterministic entropy/load-distribution route is negative. The missing object is not another inequality between the existing moments; it is a new mixed radical-capacity or horizontal-distribution theorem.
