# Exact affine telescoper, and the obstruction to a useful local one

## Verdict

There is an exact fixed-radius identity, but it is the already-visible global endpoint identity in local disguise.  Put

\[
 k_s:=\sum_{t<s}P_tj_t=a_0-a_s,
 \qquad c_s:=a_s+k_s=a_0.
\]

Then \(c_s\) is constant and

\[
 \boxed{
 P_s\det(j_s,k_s)
 =B_{s+1}-B_s-P_sE_s,
 \qquad
 B_s:=\det(c_s,a_s)=\det(k_s,a_s).
 }
 \tag{1}
\]

Thus (68.110) has the formal solution

\[
 R=0,\qquad u_{s,0}=-P_s.
\]

However, \(B_s=\det(a_0,a_s)\), so summing (1) gives exactly (68.99)/(68.117) and returns the exponentially tall endpoint determinant.  Prefix augmentation therefore makes the curvature a coboundary only by retaining the global cumulative integration constant.

More strongly, the Ore modules give a precise no-go theorem:

> **No prefix-free local curvature primitive exists.**  In the universal prefix-augmented direct sum of the fourteen ray modules, no identity of the form (68.110) can have \(B_s\) in the bounded-shift tensor/exterior module generated only by the ray states.  Any such identity must retain a cumulative/prefix coordinate (equivalently the conserved column \(c=a+k\)).

At a target prime, eliminating that cumulative coordinate integrally has a moving Smith/Fitting factor \(p\).  Dividing by this factor produces a primitive difference row which is no longer target-forced.  Consequently the current Ore modules cannot produce a boundary packet that is simultaneously

1. genuinely local,
2. target-preserving, and
3. obtained with only fixed bad factors.

This is a module-theoretic obstruction, not merely the statement that a finite-state recurrence exists.

## 1. Exact algebra of the prefix extension

Retain the notation of (68.94)--(68.100):

\[
 a_s-a_{s+1}=P_sj_s,\qquad E_s=\det(j_s,a_s).
 \tag{2}
\]

Define the prefix and conserved column

\[
 k_s=\sum_{t<s}P_tj_t,\qquad c=a_s+k_s=a_0.
 \tag{3}
\]

Then

\[
 k_{s+1}=k_s+P_sj_s,\qquad a_{s+1}=a_s-P_sj_s.
 \tag{4}
\]

For

\[
 B_s=\det(c,a_s)=\det(k_s,a_s),
 \tag{5}
\]

one obtains

\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_{s+1}-B_s
 &=\det(c,a_s-P_sj_s)-\det(c,a_s)\\
 &=P_s\det(j_s,c)\\
 &=P_s\det(j_s,a_s)+P_s\det(j_s,k_s)\\
 &=P_sE_s+P_s\det(j_s,k_s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

This proves (1) with no recurrence input at all.  Summing from \(s=0\) to \(r-1\) gives

\[
 \sum_{s<r}P_s\det(j_s,k_s)
 =\det(a_0,a_r)-\sum_{s<r}P_sE_s,
 \tag{7}
\]

which is exactly the curvature part of (68.99).  Hence adding the prefix to the state does not by itself produce a small boundary: it merely stores the left endpoint \(a_0\) in the conserved mode \(c=a+k\).

## 2. The affine-prefix obstruction

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the finite direct sum of the certified ray recurrence modules needed for \(Y\) and \(W\), after passing to the rational Ore field so that finitely many positive and negative shifts are legitimate.  The fourteen scalar ray operators have orders

\[
 3,3,2,2,3,3,3,3,2,3,3,2,3,3,
 \tag{8}
\]

whose sum is \(38\).  The \(W\) construction uses only finitely many fixed moment translates, so it enlarges this by a fixed factor only.  Let \(\mathcal L_s\) denote the module generated by a bounded window of this ray state and its tensor/exterior powers.  Crucially, \(\mathcal L_s\) contains neither the free prefix \(k_s\) nor the conserved cumulative column \(c\).

Consider an arbitrary finite Laurent Ore stencil

\[
 U=\sum_{|q|\le R}u_{s,q}S^q,
 \qquad
 J_U:=\sum_{|q|\le R}u_{s,q}j_{s+q}.
 \tag{9}
\]

For every fixed \(q\), write

\[
 k_{s+q}=k_s+D_{s,q},
 \tag{10}
\]

where \(D_{s,q}\in\mathcal L_s^2\).  For \(q>0\), \(D_{s,q}\) is the finite sum

\[
 D_{s,q}=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}P_{s+i}j_{s+i};
 \tag{11}
\]

for \(q<0\) the analogous finite backward sum is used.  Since

\[
 a_{s+q}=c-k_s-D_{s,q},
 \tag{12}
\]

we have the exact decomposition

\[
\begin{aligned}
 U(E)_s
 &=\sum_qu_{s,q}\det(j_{s+q},a_{s+q})\\
 &=\det(J_U,c)-\det(J_U,k_s)+L_U,
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

with

\[
 L_U=-\sum_qu_{s,q}\det(j_{s+q},D_{s,q})\in\mathcal L_s.
 \tag{14}
\]

Now suppose that an identity

\[
 P_s\det(j_s,k_s)
 =B_{s+1}-B_s+U(E)_s
 \tag{15}
\]

has \(B_s\in\mathcal L_s\), i.e. a genuinely local boundary packet with no prefix or conserved cumulative coordinate.  Then \(B_{s+1}-B_s\) and \(L_U\) lie in \(\mathcal L_s\).  Moving (13) to the left of (15) gives

\[
 \det(P_sj_s+J_U,k_s)-\det(J_U,c)\in\mathcal L_s.
 \tag{16}
\]

The prefix extension treats \(c\) and \(k_s\) as independent affine coordinates over the local ray state.  Equivalently, the arbitrary-realization argument used in (68.102) lets the initial carrier and the successive \(J\)-packets be prescribed independently.  Therefore the coefficients of \(c\) and \(k_s\) in (16) must vanish separately:

\[
 J_U=0,\qquad P_sj_s+J_U=0.
 \tag{17}
\]

Subtracting gives

\[
 P_sj_s=0.
 \tag{18}
\]

This is impossible in the torsion-free rational direct sum of ray modules: \(P_s\ne0\), and the \(J\)-output is not the zero module morphism.  Thus (15) has no prefix-free local solution for any fixed \(R\), or indeed for any finite \(R\).

This proof is independent of the order bound.  The order-\(38\) state only ensures that \(\mathcal L_s\) is finite rank; it cannot remove the two independent affine coefficients in (16).

### Exact normal-form certificate

A computer certificate does not require building the huge tensor LCLM.  Reduce all shifted \(j_{s+q}\) in the block direct sum of the fourteen certified modules.  For a proposed stencil \(U\), the two affine coefficients are exactly

```sage
JU   = normal_form(sum(u[q] * shift(j, q) for q in range(-R, R+1)))
eq_c = JU
eq_k = normal_form(P*j + JU)
```

A prefix-free telescoper requires both `eq_c == 0` and `eq_k == 0`.  Their difference is

```sage
normal_form(P*j)
```

which is nonzero.  This is an exact Ore normal-form obstruction valid for every ansatz radius \(R\); no numerical search cutoff is involved.

## 3. What prefix augmentation actually proves

The augmented transfer is triangular.  If \(x_s\) is a finite ray state, \(j_s=\ell_sx_s\), and \(x_{s+1}=T_sx_s\), then

\[
 \binom{x_{s+1}}{k_{s+1}}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 T_s&0\\
 P_s\ell_s&I_2
 \end{pmatrix}
 \binom{x_s}{k_s}.
 \tag{19}
\]

In the exterior state, (1) is therefore a legitimate one-step WZ law.  But its primitive is the affine mode

\[
 B_s=\det(k_s,a_s)=\det(a_0,a_s).
 \tag{20}
\]

The resulting boundary is not a bounded-width shell packet.  It carries the same global column \(a_0\) throughout the block and, after summation, is the tall endpoint carrier already measured in Section 68.10.  In cohomological language, the curvature becomes exact only in the affine extension \(\widehat{\mathcal V}\); it is not exact in the local tensor/exterior module of \(\mathcal V\).

Thus the finite-state construction succeeds formally and fails arithmetically at exactly the point identified in (68.117).

## 4. Target-local Smith obstruction

The preceding no-go is over the rational Ore field and rules out a boundary depending only on local ray states.  One might still hope to use several target-preserving cumulative outputs to solve for the affine mode \(c\) integrally.  The target-local Smith form rules this out with only fixed bad factors.

Fix a good target prime \(p\), and let \(V\) be a free localized recurrence-state lattice.  Let

\[
 \tau_p\in V^\vee
 \tag{21}
\]

be the primitive marked-node functional.  An observation \(\lambda\in V^\vee\) is universally target-preserving if

\[
 \tau_p(v)\equiv0\pmod p
 \Longrightarrow
 \lambda(v)\equiv0\pmod p
 \quad\text{for every }v.
 \tag{22}
\]

Over \(\mathbb F_p\), the annihilator of the hyperplane \(\ker\bar\tau_p\) is the line spanned by \(ar\tau_p\).  Hence the exact module of target-preserving observations is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal P_p=\mathbb Z_{(p)}\tau_p+pV^\vee.
 }
 \tag{23}
\]

Consequently, if \(m\) target-preserving observation rows are rationally independent, their reduction modulo \(p\) has rank at most one.  Their local Smith exponents satisfy

\[
 e_1\ge0,\qquad e_i\ge1\quad(2\le i\le m),
 \tag{24}
\]

and every full \(m\)-row minor is divisible by at least \(p^{m-1}\).

For the present \(Y/W\) pair this applies twice:

* every Newton carrier \(A_s(Y)\) retaining the marked node reduces to the same marked value;
* the exact Ore--CRT construction of \(W\) is universally aliased to \(Y\) at every candidate prime, so the \(W\)-rows do not add a second target-fiber direction.

At a target, both coordinates of every \(a_s\) vanish modulo \(p\).  Therefore all target-safe cumulative observations needed to recover the conserved column \(c\) are collinear modulo \(p\).  A rank-two recovery determinant necessarily contains \(p\).  Since \(p\) varies with the target, this is not a fixed bad factor.

Dividing that determinant by \(p\) does not solve the problem.  It replaces a target-preserving row by its first divided increment, and the exact first-Pascal-quotient calculation shows that this divided coordinate is free modulo \(p\) after targetness is imposed.  Thus there are only two choices:

1. keep the moving \(p\)-factor and preserve target vanishing; or
2. divide by \(p\), obtain a primitive/unit row, and lose target vanishing.

The desired curvature telescoper needs both properties simultaneously, which the current module does not supply.

## 6. Why tensor/exterior closure does not help

Taking tensor and exterior powers linearizes every bilinear expression:

* \(E_s\) becomes a linear output on an exterior state;
* \(\det(j_s,k_s)\) becomes a linear output on the mixed ray-prefix tensor state;
* \(B_s\) in (1) becomes a linear output on the cumulative exterior state.

But linearization does not remove the affine extension.  The conserved mode \(c=a+k\) remains a trivial two-dimensional quotient of the augmented state.  Formula (13) shows that every shifted edge \(E_{s+q}\) contains the same two affine coefficients \(J_U\wedge c\) and \(-J_U\wedge k_s\).  A local boundary has neither coefficient, forcing the contradictory pair (17).

Equivalently, the curvature is the transgression of the prefix extension class.  Tensor/exterior closure moves the problem from a bilinear recurrence to a linear recurrence on a larger state, but the extension class survives unchanged.  A finite-state recurrence is therefore necessary for bookkeeping and irrelevant to the missing integral splitting.

The fact that all \(91\) pairwise scalar gcrds of the fourteen ray operators are units reinforces this conclusion: there is no literal common scalar solution whose removal could absorb the affine mode.  A distinguished initial-vector relation could still exist, but it would be new Apéry arithmetic, not a consequence of the Ore modules.

## 8. Scope of the obstruction

The no-go theorem applies to an identity that is a module identity, i.e. one valid throughout the solution module generated by the certified ray recurrences.  It does not logically exclude an accidental identity satisfied only by the distinguished Apéry initial vector.

Such an exception would have to provide new information beyond the recurrence operators.  Concretely, one needs one of the following:

1. an integral divided observation
   \[
   \widetilde W_s=
   \frac{W_s-\lambda_sY_s}{\text{target factor}}
   \]
   which remains target-zero and is independent of \(Y_s\) modulo \(p\); or
2. a distinguished endpoint identity expressing the conserved column \(c=a_0\) by bounded-width shell packets with denominators supported only on fixed primes; or
3. a twisted-de-Rham/constant-term relation that kills the affine extension class before target specialization.

The current \(W\) does not provide (1): its candidate-prime alias to \(Y\) makes the target-fiber output rank one.  Universal Newton/Pascal identities do not provide (2): division by the common-node factor exposes a free first quotient.  The fourteen ray recurrences do not provide (3): they control the difference state, not the cumulative integration constant.

The precise obstruction is the nonsplit target-local cumulative/prefix extension, equivalently its rank-one target observability/Smith class.  Progress now requires a new distinguished Apéry divided observation or an independent arithmetic endpoint identity, not a larger finite-state or exterior recurrence.
