# Q498: Multi-prime Chebotarev and CRT equidistribution of Apéry zero sets

## Executive verdict

Chebotarev independence of Frobenius conjugacy classes is **not by itself sufficient** to prove CRT equidistribution of Apéry zero locations.

The missing implication is:

\[
\text{independent Frobenius classes}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{independent zero locations }r_i.
\]

The reason is that \(r_i\in Z_{p_i}\) is not a fixed conjugacy-class statistic of a single Frobenius element. It is a **moving-characteristic, moving-parameter zero condition**:

\[
 b_{r_i}\equiv 0\pmod {p_i}.
\]

The Frobenius at \(p_i\) describes the fiber at characteristic \(p_i\), but the index \(r_i\) labels a family member/twist inside that fiber. One needs a horizontal equidistribution theorem for these marked zero fibers.

The correct target is therefore not ordinary Chebotarev, but a multi-characteristic marked Frobenius dispersion statement.

---

## (A) Does Chebotarev directly imply independence of zero locations?

No.

Chebotarev gives statements of the form:

\[
\#\{p<X:\operatorname{Frob}_p\in C\}
\sim \frac{|C|}{|G|}\pi(X).
\]

For a compatible system this generalizes to equidistribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes.

However, the Apéry zero condition is of the form:

\[
 r\in Z_p
\iff
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p,r}|\mathcal F_r)=0.
\]

The parameter \(r\) changes with \(p\). Thus one needs information about the joint distribution of

\[
(\operatorname{Frob}_{p,r})_{r\in\mathbf F_p}
\]

inside the family.

Ordinary Chebotarev controls the prime variable. It does not automatically control the internal parameter variable.

A useful analogy:

- Chebotarev says the dice rolls at different primes are independent.
- The Apéry problem asks whether the locations where each dice roll gives a zero are independent.

The second statement requires knowing how the entire family of rolls depends on the parameter.

---

## (B) What exact statement is needed for CRT equidistribution?

The desired statement is stronger than Frobenius independence.

Let

\[
Z_p=\{r\in\mathbf F_p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

For distinct primes \(p_1,p_2,p_3\), the required statement is roughly:

\[
\sum_{r_i\in Z_{p_i}}
F\left(\operatorname{CRT}(r_1,r_2,r_3)\right)
\approx
\frac{|Z_{p_1}||Z_{p_2}||Z_{p_3}|}{p_1p_2p_3}
\sum_{x}F(x)
\]

for suitable test functions \(F\).

Equivalently, defining

\[
\mu_p=\frac1{|Z_p|}\sum_{r\in Z_p}\delta_{r/p},
\]

one needs the product measure statement

\[
\mu_{p_1}\times\mu_{p_2}\times\mu_{p_3}
\]

to behave like independent uniform residue measures after CRT embedding.

This contains two components:

1. **Internal equidistribution:** each \(Z_p\) is not concentrated in special residue classes.
2. **Cross-prime independence:** the zero sets for different primes do not correlate.

Chebotarev addresses neither completely.

---

## (C) Precise formulation of the missing independence

The right formulation is a marked multi-prime Frobenius theorem.

Let \(\mathcal F\) be the Apéry sheaf and let \(r\) be the twist parameter. One needs estimates such as:

\[
\sum_{p_1,p_2,p_3}
\sum_{r_i\in\mathbf F_{p_i}}
\prod_{i=1}^3
1_{\{\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p_i,r_i})=0\}
\,
\Psi\left(\frac{r_1}{p_1},\frac{r_2}{p_2},\frac{r_3}{p_3}\right)
\]

\[
=\text{independent main term}+o(\text{main term}).
\]

In probabilistic language, one wants convergence of the marked point process

\[
\Xi_p=\sum_{r\in Z_p}\delta_{r/p}
\]

with joint factorial moments:

\[
\mathbb E\prod_i(\Xi_{p_i}(I_i))_{k_i}
\rightarrow
\prod_i\left(\frac{|I_i|}{?}\right)^{k_i}.
\]

For (HM)₃ this is exactly the kind of third factorial moment control needed.

---

## (D) Katz-Sarnak / Michel-Venkatesh: do they give this?

Not directly.

Katz-Sarnak theory gives powerful equidistribution results for families where:

- the parameter space is fixed;
- the sheaf has bounded conductor;
- monodromy is understood;
- the parameter varies over a fixed finite field.

The Apéry problem has a harder quantifier order:

\[
 p\rightarrow\infty,
 \qquad r\in\mathbf F_p,
 \qquad b_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The characteristic and the parameter field grow together.

The missing object would be a horizontal compatible system controlling the marked fibers:

\[
(p,r)\mapsto \operatorname{Frob}_{p,r}.
\]

This is closer to a two-dimensional arithmetic large sieve than ordinary Sato-Tate/Chebotarev.

Michel-Venkatesh style results on moments of automorphic \(L\)-functions are also not directly applicable: they control analytic families of \(L\)-values, whereas here the event is a same-characteristic vanishing condition.

---

## Consequence for (HM)_3

The proposed chain

\[
\text{Sp}_4\text{ monodromy}
\Rightarrow
\text{Chebotarev}
\Rightarrow
\text{independent }Z_p
\Rightarrow
(HM)_3
\]

has a missing middle arrow.

The correct chain is:

\[
\text{Sp}_4\text{ monodromy}
\Rightarrow
\text{possible bounded-complexity Frobenius model}
\]

plus a new theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{marked multi-characteristic Frobenius dispersion}
}
\]

which would imply:

\[
\text{joint zero-location equidistribution}
\Rightarrow
\text{CRT equidistribution}
\Rightarrow
(HM)_3.
\]

Thus the obstruction is not the geometric monodromy group. The obstruction is the horizontal passage from Frobenius equidistribution to zero-fiber equidistribution.
