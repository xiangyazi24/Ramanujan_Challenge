# Q8532 — ATUP3 unpaired reflection divisor audit

## Status

No unconditional arity-two divisor certificate for the unpaired sign-simple sector was obtained. The surviving statement is an exact obstruction to the proposed reflection-only divisor witness route.

## Route examined

Let a thin row have

\[
K_X(m)=3,
\qquad m<X^2,
\qquad p<q<\ell,
\]

and let the three hit primes have their two Apéry zero residues reflected by
\[
r\mapsto p-1-r.
\]

The eight CRT vertices are obtained by choosing independently the three reflections. In the paired sector, two low vertices permit the identity
\[
D_T=2\binom{t_T}{2},
\]
which counts incidences between a low vertex and a second reflected representative. This identity fundamentally uses the existence of a second low vertex.

For a sign-simple cube there is exactly one low vertex. Therefore every candidate certificate must arise from comparing the unique low vertex with one of the seven high vertices, not from low-low collisions.

## Failure of the reflection-only divisor witness

Write a pair-reflected vertex changing the q and ell coordinates as
\[
m_{q\ell}=m+\Delta_{q\ell}.
\]
The CRT equations imply only
\[
\Delta_{q\ell}\equiv 0\pmod q,
\qquad
\Delta_{q\ell}\equiv0\pmod \ell,
\]
so
\[
q\ell\mid \Delta_{q\ell}.
\]
This is a valid divisibility statement, but it is not an incidence certificate of the required strength. The quotient
\[
|\Delta_{q\ell}|/(q\ell)
\]
depends on the unrestricted choice of CRT lift and has no forced small divisor whose prime support is bounded by the window primes.

The same argument applies to the three pair-reflected vertices. Reflection geometry supplies congruence divisors, but does not supply a bounded-multiplicity divisor family.

## Exact obstruction

Consider the proposed certificate:

"Every sign-simple cube has a pair-reflected difference D with a divisor d(D) such that (i) d(D) is composed only of window primes, (ii) d(D) has bounded multiplicity over all cubes, and (iii) d(D) gives the required \(X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_2^3\) summation."

This certificate is false as a consequence of reflection geometry alone. The reflection construction determines residues modulo p,q,ell but leaves the integer representatives free up to adding multiples of the product modulus. Hence two formal CRT families can have identical reflection data and arbitrarily different pair-reflected differences.

Therefore any proof of this form requires extra arithmetic information beyond reflection symmetry.

## Survivor checks

The exact survivors

* \((256,25874;307,367,463)\),
* \((256,51931;283,347,367)\),
* \((512,98926;659,857,953)\),

all satisfy the same obstruction pattern: the reflected pair representatives provide the expected q- and ell-divisibility, but no second low representative exists from which a bounded incidence count can be formed.

No identity reducing these examples to the paired-sector count was found; using \(2\binom{t_T}{2}\) here would incorrectly insert a nonexistent second low vertex.

## Smallest surviving theorem

The strongest reflection-only statement proved is:

For every thin sign-simple cube and every two-coordinate reflection vertex,
\[
\operatorname{rad}(q\ell)\mid (m_{q\ell}-m),
\]
with analogous statements for the three pairs. This is an exact congruence statement, not an ATUP3 counting bound.

## Required additional datum

To complete ATUP3 one needs an arithmetic input not contained in reflection geometry, for example:

1. a uniform bound on the quotient of reflected CRT differences;
2. a recurrence-derived restriction on the allowed CRT lifts;
3. a new pair-correlation estimate for actual Apéry zero residues;
4. an independent anti-concentration theorem for the three-prime intersection rows.

Without such a datum, the reflection cube alone cannot lower the unpaired sector to an arity-two divisor count.

## Claim

The accepted paired-sector divisor identity cannot be extended to the sign-simple sector. The missing ATUP3 estimate is not resolved by unpaired reflection geometry; the remaining gateway is an additional arithmetic rigidity statement about actual Apéry zero sets.
