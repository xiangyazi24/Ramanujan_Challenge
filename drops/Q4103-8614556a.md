# 2. Three fixed Apéry values at one lower endpoint

If a P row leaves `x` and reaches `y`, then

\[

u=y-2x-1.

Equivalently, for fixed `(p,x,q)`,

\tag{2.2}

\[
\boxed{u_P^{\rm out}=7p-x-6q-1.}
\tag{2.3}

\[
A_{p,x}=6q+u_P^{\rm out}.
\tag{2.4}

Outside a fixed finite exceptional set of primes dividing `b_6`, Lucas gives

\[
q\mid b_{A_{p,x}}
\iff q\mid b_{u_P^{\rm out}}.
\tag{2.5}

## 2.2 Outgoing minus

For a minus row whose canonical anchor/source is `x`, the partner is

\[
\boxed{y_M^{\rm out}=7q-8p+x.}
\tag{2.6}

Its upper digit is again

\[
\boxed{u_M^{\rm out}=7p-x-6q-1,}
\tag{2.7}

A_{p,x}=6q+u_M^{\rm out}.
\tag{2.8}

Thus the two outgoing sign channels are two **tagged copies** of the same upper-prime divisor support of `b_(A_(p,x))`. They must remain tagged because the reconstructed lower partners are different and deterministic sign assignment selects one physical row.

## 2.3 Incoming P

If a P row enters `x` from a smaller source `y`, then

\boxed{y_P^{\rm in}=6q-7p+x,}
\tag{2.9}

\boxed{u_P^{\rm in}=14p-x-12q-1.}
