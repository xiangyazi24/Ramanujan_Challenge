# P32 combinatorial sharpness: a quadratic periodic-gap extremizer

## Verdict

The periodic-gap idea works. After a harmless translation of the packet and a slightly wider reflection separation, it gives an explicit infinite family satisfying every stated axiom and

\[
E_H\ge \frac1{8000}H^2.
\]

Consequently, palindrome symmetry, no adjacency, and the pair caps

\[
A_Z(h)\le 3(h-1)
\]

cannot imply a genuinely subquadratic bound for the number of off-center short four-windows. In particular, those axioms alone cannot prove the actual-Apéry short-window reflection principle. Some higher-order recurrence/orbit input is indispensable.

The point missed by the earlier audits is a scale split:

1. if a difference is smaller than \(m^2\), its block length is less than one period, so the sawtooth sum has fibers of size at most two, with the explicit factor \(R\) harmless;
2. if a difference is at least \(m^2\), the trivial bound “at most one endpoint for each start” is already far below \(3(h-1)\);
3. for cross-reflection differences, fixing the sum \(P_a+P_b\) leaves at most one \(b\) for each \(a\), so the multiplicity is at most the packet size, not its square.

No Sidon packet is needed.

## 1. Construction

For an integer \(k\ge 6\), set

\[
m=100k,\qquad R=\frac m{100}=k,\qquad
N=mR=\frac{m^2}{100},\qquad H=12m.
\]

Define the periodic gap word

\[
g_i=3m+(i\bmod m),\qquad 0\le i<N,
\]

and its partial sums

\[
P_0=0,\qquad P_j=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}g_i
\quad(1\le j\le N).
\]

One period has sum

\[
S=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(3m+r)
  =3m^2+\frac{m(m-1)}2
  =\frac{m(7m-1)}2.
\]

There are exactly \(R\) periods, hence

\[
L:=P_N=RS=\frac{m^2(7m-1)}{200}.
\]

Apply Bertrand's postulate to the integer \(8L+1>1\), and choose a prime \(p\) with

\[
8L+1<p<2(8L+1).
\]

Translate the left packet by \(L\):

\[
X_j=L+P_j\qquad(0\le j\le N),
\]

and reflect it:

\[
Y_j=p-1-X_j=p-1-L-P_j.
\]

Finally put

\[
Z=\{X_0,\ldots,X_N\}\cup\{Y_0,\ldots,Y_N\}.
\]

The translation is cosmetic for all within-packet differences. It merely avoids placing points at \(0\) and \(p-1\), and makes even the cyclic boundary gap large.

## 2. Geometry, palindrome, and the scale \(p>H^2\)

Because \(p-1>8L\), the points occur in the order

\[
L=X_0<\cdots<X_N=2L
 <Y_N=p-1-2L<\cdots<Y_0=p-1-L.
\]

Thus the two packets are disjoint. Reflection sends \(X_j\) to \(Y_j\) and vice versa, so

\[
z\in Z\Longleftrightarrow p-1-z\in Z.
\]

The within-packet gaps are all at least \(3m\). The middle gap is

\[
\Delta:=Y_N-X_N=p-1-4L>4L.
\]

Hence no two elements of \(Z\) are adjacent. In fact, if adjacency were interpreted cyclically, the wrap-around gap would also be

\[
(X_0+p)-Y_0=2L+1>1.
\]

Moreover,

\[
8L=\frac{m^2(7m-1)}{25}.
\]

For \(m\ge600\), one has \(7m-1>3600\), and therefore

\[
8L>144m^2=H^2.
\]

Since \(p>8L+1\), this proves

\[
p>H^2.
\]

As \(k\to\infty\), both \(H\) and the selected primes tend to infinity, so this is an infinite family.

## 3. A two-fiber lemma for short sawtooth sums

For \(1\le \ell<m\), define

\[
F_\ell(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}((r+j)\bmod m),
\qquad 0\le r<m.
\]

Then every value of \(F_\ell\) has at most two preimages.

Indeed, if \(0\le r\le m-\ell\), there is no wrap and

\[
F_\ell(r)=\ell r+\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}2,
\]

which is strictly increasing in \(r\). If \(m-\ell+1\le r\le m-1\), exactly \(r+\ell-m\) terms wrap, and

\[
F_\ell(r)
 =\ell r+\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}2-m(r+\ell-m),
\]

which is strictly decreasing because its slope is \(\ell-m<0\). Thus a target value occurs at most once in each of the two ranges.

For a fixed block length \(\ell<m\),

\[
P_{a+\ell}-P_a=3m\ell+F_\ell(a\bmod m).
\]

Every residue class modulo \(m\) occurs at most \(R\) times among the admissible starts \(0\le a\le N-\ell\), because \(N=mR\) and \(\ell\ge1\). Consequently, for fixed \(\ell<m\) and fixed \(h\),

\[
\#\{a:P_{a+\ell}-P_a=h\}\le 2R.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is the exact place where the repeated-period factor enters; it is \(R\), and it will be absorbed quantitatively.

## 4. Verification of every pair cap

Let

\[
B(h)=\#\{(a,b):0\le a<b\le N,\ P_b-P_a=h\}
\]

be the multiplicity of \(h\) inside one packet.

All within-packet differences are at most \(L\), whereas every cross-packet difference is at least

\[
\Delta>4L.
\]

Hence the within and cross contributions have disjoint ranges.

### 4.1 Small within-packet differences: \(1\le h<m^2\)

If \(B(h)>0\), write \(\ell=b-a\). Since every gap is at least \(3m\),

\[
3m\ell\le h,
\qquad
\ell\le\left\lfloor\frac{h}{3m}\right\rfloor
 <\frac m3<m.
\]

Thus (3.1) applies to every possible \(\ell\). Summing over block lengths gives

\[
B(h)
 \le 2R\left\lfloor\frac{h}{3m}\right\rfloor.
\]

Reflection duplicates each within-left pair exactly once in the right packet, and there are no cross pairs at this scale. Therefore

\[
A_Z(h)=2B(h)
 \le 4R\frac{h}{3m}
 =\frac{h}{75},
\]

because \(R=m/100\). For integer \(h\ge2\),

\[
\frac h{75}\le3(h-1).
\]

For \(h=1\), the count is zero by the already verified gap bound. Hence the cap holds throughout the small range.

Notice that this range never sees a complete period: the possible block lengths satisfy \(\ell<m/3\). Thus there is no hidden \(q\)-sum in a decomposition \(\ell=qm+r\); necessarily \(q=0\).

### 4.2 Large within-packet differences: \(m^2\le h\le L\)

For each starting index \(a\), strict monotonicity of \(P_j\) permits at most one endpoint \(b\) with \(P_b-P_a=h\). Since \(a\le N-1\),

\[
B(h)\le N.
\]

Thus

\[
A_Z(h)=2B(h)
 \le2N=rac{m^2}{50}
 \le h
 \le3(h-1).
\]

This is why repetitions of a period sum, or possible contributions from several values of \(q\), cause no problem: they occur only when the allowed right-hand side is already of order at least \(m^2\).

### 4.3 The empty intermediate range

For

\[
L<h<\Delta,
\]

there are neither within-packet nor cross-packet pairs, so

\[
A_Z(h)=0.
\]

### 4.4 Cross-reflection differences

Every cross pair goes from some \(X_a\) to some \(Y_b\), and its difference is

\[
h=Y_b-X_a
  =p-1-2L-P_a-P_b.
\]

For a fixed \(h\), this is equivalent to

\[
P_a+P_b=p-1-2L-h.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For each fixed \(a\), strict monotonicity of \(P_b\) gives at most one solution \(b\) to (4.1). Therefore the complete cross multiplicity, including both possible ordered index pairs \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\) when they exist, satisfies

\[
A_Z(h)\le N+1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

There is no missing factor \(N+1\): (4.2) already counts all choices of the first index \(a\), with at most one partner for each.

Any such cross difference has \(h>4L\), hence, since \(h\) is integral,

\[
h-1\ge4L.
\]

Also

\[
L=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}g_i\ge3mN,
\]

so \(4L\ge12mN\ge N+1\). Combining this with (4.2),

\[
A_Z(h)\le N+1\le h-1\le3(h-1).
\]

This completes the cap verification for every \(1\le h\le p-1\):

\[
\boxed{A_Z(h)\le3(h-1).}
\]

## 5. Exact count of short off-center four-windows

The left packet has \(N+1\) points, hence \(N-2\) consecutive four-point windows. The span of the window starting at \(X_j\) is

\[
X_{j+3}-X_j=g_j+g_{j+1}+g_{j+2}.
\]

Since every \(g_i\le4m-1\),

\[
X_{j+3}-X_j
 \le3(4m-1)
 =12m-3
 <H.
\]

The reflected right packet contributes another \(N-2\) windows with the same spans.

The full ordered set has a unique adjacent pair fixed setwise by reflection, namely

\[
(X_N,Y_N)=(2L,p-1-2L),
\qquad X_N+Y_N=p-1.
\]

This is the centered adjacent pair. Every window using points from both packets contains the middle gap \(\Delta>4L\). Since \(4L>H\), no such window has span at most \(H\). Thus every short window lies wholly in one packet and contains no centered adjacent pair.

Consequently the count is exact:

\[
E_H=2(N-2)
   =\frac{m^2}{50}-4.
\]

As \(H^2=144m^2\),

\[
\frac{E_H}{H^2}
 =\frac1{7200}-\frac1{36m^2}.
\]

For \(m\ge600\), in particular \(m^2\ge2000\), so

\[
\frac1{36m^2}\le\frac1{72000}
 =\frac1{7200}-\frac1{8000}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{E_H\ge\frac1{8000}H^2.}
\]

## 6. Sharpness consequence

There is also an immediate universal quadratic upper bound from the pair caps. Map a consecutive four-window

\[
(z_i,z_{i+1},z_{i+2},z_{i+3})
\]

to its endpoint pair \((z_i,z_{i+3})\). For a fixed span \(h\), this is an injection into the pairs counted by \(A_Z(h)\). Hence

\[
E_H
 \le\sum_{h=1}^{H}A_Z(h)
 \le3\sum_{h=1}^{H}(h-1)
 =\frac{3H(H-1)}2.
\]

The construction above supplies a matching lower bound in exponent:

\[
\frac1{8000}H^2\le E_H\le\frac32H^2.
\]

Thus the \(H^2\) scale is genuinely sharp under exactly the stated combinatorial axioms. In particular:

* no universal \(o(H^2)\) estimate follows from pair caps plus palindrome;
* those axioms do not even force \(E_H=0\) when \(H<\sqrt p\);
* the current repo's short-window reflection principle can only be an actual-Apéry theorem, using recurrence/projective-orbit information not encoded by the pair caps.

The translation by \(L\) was included only to avoid endpoint distractions. If one keeps the original packet beginning at \(P_0=0\), the same small/large difference argument works with a correspondingly smaller separation condition; the essential correction is the scale split above, not the translation.
