# Prime-averaged distinguished-state hitting: exact target, obstruction, and replacement observable

## 1. Verdict

At repository main commit `a8159d911d92b2e5089d4cc8643f3b6f33b01827`, the distinguished-state condition is the genuinely new input.  The local continuant equations see only that four indices lie in one projective fibre; they are invariant under changing the basis of the two-dimensional solution space.  The assertion that this common fibre is the Apéry fibre \(q=0\) is not basis-invariant.  Consequently:

* averaging the target point \(q\) gives an **exact** \(1/(p+1)\) factor;
* averaging a change of solution basis gives the same exact factor;
* none of the presently available identities de-randomizes that average to the fixed Apéry line;
* additive characters, a projective large sieve, Chebotarev, or bilinear completion would close the problem only after one proves a new long-prefix/short-return independence estimate;
* no such estimate follows from standard bounded-conductor technology, because the prefix depth is \(x+1\asymp p\), while the exact primitive-chain selector itself has complexity up to \(H\asymp\sqrt p\).

There is, however, a clean exact large-sieve-shaped lemma which would give the requested bound.  It is stated and exponent-checked in Section 4 below.  The best fail-closed conclusion is:

> **No unconditional \(1/p\) distinguished-state saving is currently available.**  The natural diagonal-size projective variance estimate would suffice, but proving it is essentially the missing orbit-coupling theorem.  Replacing exact primitive chains by the low-degree local algebra makes the set algebraic, but then the unavoidable diagonal is at the \(X^{7/4+o(1)}\) scale rather than the \(X^{3/2+o(1)}\) scale.

The strongest unconditional bound already present in the repository remains
\[
 \sum_{X<p\le 2X} A_p \le \sum_{X<p\le2X} Z(p)
 \ll rac{X^{8/5}}{\log X},
\]
where \(A_p\) is the number of actual primitive off-center short chains.  No square-root average is proved here.

\[

with \(a,b,c\ge2\), no centered adjacent pair, the gap vector not all equal, and \(p

\[

is the repository's structural carrier.  Thus the return offsets in \([0,s]\) are exactly
\(\{0,a,a+b,s\}\).  Put
\[

 M_p=\sum_{q\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)}C_p(q)=|\mathscr S_p|,

\[

### Distinguished projective large sieve (DPLS)

For \(H_p=\lfloor\sqrt pfloor\),
\[
 oxed{

\[

\[

has degree growing with \(p\), reproducing the same conductor problem.

### The \(7/4\) diagonal after algebraic relaxation

There is an important distinction between the exact primitive set \(\mathscr S_p\) and its low-degree algebraic superset.  If primitivity is dropped and one sums over all tuples \((x,a,b,c)\) satisfying the three local equations, then a fixed \(x\) can occur with many gap triples.  Let \(\widetilde M(X,H)\) be that formal multiplicity over \(X<p\le2X\) and \(a+b+c\le H\).

The fully deflated pencil content gives unconditionally
\[
 \widetilde M(X,H)
 \ll rac1{\log X}
   \sum_{\substack{a,b,c\ge2\\a+b+c\le H}}
   (a+b+c)^2\log(2a+2b+2c)
 \ll rac{H^5\log(2H)}{\log X}.
 	ag{5.4}
\]
Indeed, a common root contributes to the \(p\)-adic valuation of the nonzero pencil content, and the generic Sylvester-height bound is
\(O(s^2\log s)\) for \(s=a+b+c\).

At \(H=\sqrt X\), (5.4) is \(X^{5/2+o(1)}\).  A positive second-moment argument at its unavoidable diagonal scale \(X\widetilde M\) then gives, after the same Cauchy--Schwarz step,
\[
 \left(rac{X\widetilde M(X,\sqrt X)}{\log X}ight)^{1/2}
 =X^{7/4+o(1)},
 	ag{5.5}
\]
which misses the target by a power \(X^{1/4}\).  To recover \(X^{3/2}\), one would need a factor \(X^{1/2}\) below this positive diagonal bound.

This is the precise primitivity--algebraicity tradeoff:

* retaining exact primitivity gives \(M_p\le p\), so a diagonal-size large sieve would close the problem;
* replacing primitivity by the three low-degree equations gives algebraic access, but its diagonal is too large;
* inclusion--exclusion over all intermediate return times restores primitivity only at complexity exponential in the number of possible intermediate gaps.

## 6. Finite-field projective mixing and large-sieve audit

The projective formulation makes the missing input particularly transparent.  The vector
\[
 (C_p(q))_{q\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)}
\]
may have arbitrary concentration from the viewpoint of the local continuant equations.  Those equations determine which indices return to the same state, but not which state is labelled \(0\) by the chosen Apéry basis.

The artificial \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\)-average (3.2) controls only the mean.  It gives no bound on the variance (4.1).  An arbitrary nonnegative vector with mass \(M_p\) can have variance as large as \(M_p^2\), and group transitivity does not rule this out.

Nor is there a random walk to which an expander theorem can be applied.  The recurrence transfer is a deterministic, nonautonomous product
\[
 T_x=A_{x-1}A_{x-2}\cdots A_0,
\]
with \(A_n\) depending on \(n\).  Enlarging the state by the time coordinate makes the evolution autonomous, but the time coordinate advances deterministically and supplies no spectral gap.  Standard expansion in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\) concerns random or suitably averaged words, not this one prescribed word of length \(x\).

Finally, marginal mixing of all \(\pi_x\) would not be enough.  The set \(\mathscr S_p\) is endogenous: it is selected by three future returns of the same orbit.  What is needed is mixing **conditioned on those returns**, exactly the variance statement (DPLS).  The known generic PGL identities and codegree bounds do not provide this conditioned mixing.

## 7. Chebotarev audit

Chebotarev is useful for the root statistics of one fixed \(N_h\), as already exploited in the repository.  It is not the right mechanism for simultaneous short-gap returns.

For fixed \((a,b,c)\), consider
\[
 F=N_a(x),\qquad G=N_b(x+a),\qquad J=N_c(x+a+b).
\]
The root-strip theorem shows already that \(F\) and \(G\) have no common root over characteristic zero.  Hence the common-root scheme of \(F,G,J\) has empty generic fibre over \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z\).  Its points modulo \(p\) occur only at **vertical bad primes**, namely primes dividing a nonzero resultant or pencil content.  They are not Frobenius classes in a horizontal finite étale cover.  Thus applying Chebotarev to the simultaneous root event is a category error: it is a divisor-of-a-fixed-algebraic-integer problem, not a splitting-density problem.

Trying to add the distinguished condition does not repair this.  The prefix identity is
\[
 N_{x+1}(-1)=(x!)^3b_x.
 	ag{7.1}
\]
For \(x<p\), the factorial is a unit modulo \(p\), so \(q_x=0\) is equivalent to
\[
 N_{x+1}(-1)=0\pmod p.
\]
But the subscript \(x+1\) is the depth of the continuant.  It is not evaluation of one fixed-degree polynomial at the small-gap root \(x\).  As \(x\) varies up to \(p\), the degree, height, transfer length, and any known field or sheaf complexity all grow with \(p\).  There is therefore no fixed number field or bounded-degree cover whose Frobenius simultaneously records the short-gap root and the prefix hit.

Even an effective Chebotarev theorem for a family of growing fields would face discriminants and degrees growing with \(x\); at \(x\asymp p\asymp X\), its error term is far larger than a \(1/p\) saving.  The more fundamental obstruction remains that the short common-root event itself is vertical.

## 9. Precise obstruction, summarized

There are four separate obstructions, and they should not be conflated.

1. **Basis symmetry.**  The local return equations are \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\)-invariant; the label \(q=0\) is fixed by the initial data.  The exact \(1/(p+1)\) average cannot be de-randomized by invariant identities.

2. **Growing prefix conductor.**  Detecting \(q_x=0\) requires the depth-\(x\) quantity \(N_{x+1}(-1)\), with \(x\) as large as \(p\).  All available algebraic or sheaf realizations have complexity growing with \(p\), so standard Deligne/trace-function bounds are ineffective.

3. **Vertical, not Chebotarev, short algebra.**  Simultaneous shifted continuant roots occur at primes dividing resultants or pencil contents.  They are special-fibre intersections, not Frobenius events in a fixed extension.

4. **Primitivity--algebraicity diagonal.**  Exact primitive chains have one start per index and a diagonal-size large sieve would close the target.  The low-degree algebraic relaxation has total multiplicity \(H^{5+o(1)}\), whose positive character diagonal gives \(X^{7/4+o(1)}\) at \(H=\sqrt X\).  No standard method presently keeps the low multiplicity and bounded algebraic complexity simultaneously.

This identifies the precise missing statement: a conditioned, prime-averaged equidistribution of the fixed Apéry line among exact primitive return fibres, or equivalently a \(1/X\) average bound for the long--short gcd observable (8.2).

## 10. Recommended fixed-initial-data observables

Two observables avoid the phantom-state ambiguity completely.

### 10.1 The actual short-window count

The repository already defines \(E_{\mathrm{sw}}(p;\eta)\), the number of four consecutive actual Apéry zeros of span at most \(\eta\sqrt p\) with no centered adjacent pair, and proves
\[
 Z(p)<3\eta^{-1}\sqrt p+6+E_{\mathrm{sw}}(p;\eta).

\[

 \ll rac{X^{3/2+o(1)}}{\log X}
 	ag{10.2}

is already sufficient.  It is less algebraic than a resultant, but it is exactly the fixed-orbit quantity and cannot count phantoms.

For arithmetic work, use (8.1), or its window-prime part

 	ag{10.3}

It does not pretend that the prefix is fixed-degree, and it does not lose the common root or the distinguished initial line.

## 11. Final assessment

* **Additive characters:** exact detector, but no bounded-conductor complete-sum estimate; the needed collision estimate is (5.2) or (DPLS).
* **Multiplicative characters:** do not oscillate at zero; the Fermat replacement has degree \(p-1\).
* **Projective mixing/large sieve:** the exact diagonal-size target (DPLS) closes the problem, but the deterministic nonautonomous orbit and return-conditioned selector lie outside standard expander/large-sieve hypotheses.
* **Chebotarev:** applies to roots of one \(N_h\), not to the vertical simultaneous-root primes, and cannot absorb the depth-\(x\) prefix.
* **Bilinear completion:** reduces exactly to the long--short gcd sum (8.2), but no available Type-II estimate controls its \(b_x\) phase.

Therefore the honest outcome is negative for all four proposed off-the-shelf mechanisms.  The mathematically sharp next lemma is (DPLS); the arithmetically sharp replacement is (DH)/(10.4).  Either would give the desired \(X^{3/2+o(1)}/\log X\) bound, but each is a new Apéry-specific input rather than a consequence of generic PGL identities, fixed-degree resultants, or standard Chebotarev.
