# P3.2 exact audit of the simplified plus-star carrier

## Verdict

The proposed carrier is **correct for edge-incidence multiplicity**, with one important wording correction.

For a fixed upper state `(q,u)`, put

\[
n_{q,u}:=6q+u,
\qquad
N_{q,u}:=12q+u,
\]

\[
G^+_{q,u}:=\gcd\!\bigl(b_{n_{q,u}},b_{N_{q,u}}\bigr),
\qquad
A^+_{q,u}:=
\frac{G^+_{q,u}}{\gcd(G^+_{q,u},q)}.
\tag{V.1}
\]

If a true legal plus-secondary edge in this star has lower prime `p`, then

\[
\boxed{p\mid A^+_{q,u}.}
\tag{V.2}
\]

At fixed `(q,u)`, the lower prime `p` determines the complete edge, so distinct edges in one star have distinct lower primes. Consequently, if `E^+_{q,u}` is the set of true plus-secondary edges in that star, then

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{e\in E^+_{q,u}}p_e\mid A^+_{q,u}.
}
\tag{V.3}
\]

The exact local valuation is stronger. For an edge `e` with lower prime `p_e`,

\[
\boxed{
v_{p_e}(A^+_{q,u})
=
\min\!\left\{
 v_{p_e}(b_{6q+u}),
 v_{p_e}(b_{12q+u})
\right\}
\ge1.
}
\tag{V.4}
\]

Products over prescribed stars preserve repeated occurrences of the same lower prime across different stars:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{e\in E^+_{J,T}\\p_e=\ell}}
\min\!\left\{
 v_\ell(b_{n_e}),v_\ell(b_{N_e})
\right\}
\le
v_\ell\!\left(\mathfrak A^+_{J,T}\right),
}
\tag{V.5}
\]

where `\mathfrak A^+_{J,T}` is the product of the star carriers defined in §6. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{e\in E^+_{J,T}:p_e=\ell\}
\le v_\ell(\mathfrak A^+_{J,T}).
}
\tag{V.6}
\]

Thus the proposed construction preserves **one valuation unit per edge**, including repeated use of one lower prime in different stars.

The wording correction concerns the upper prime. What is proved is

\[
\boxed{v_q(G^+_{q,u})\ge1,}
\tag{V.7}
\]

not necessarily `v_q(G^+_{q,u})=1`. Since `q` is prime, (V.7) implies the exact integer identity

\[
\boxed{\gcd(G^+_{q,u},q)=q,}
\tag{V.8}
\]

so (V.1) strips **exactly one** `q`-adic layer. It may leave further powers of `q`. Gessel--Lucas and reflection alone do not prove that there are no further powers.

The simplified carrier is strictly cleaner than the Q3744 carrier: it removes the candidate product `\prod b_{r_p}` entirely. It is not, however, proved to be pointwise smaller than the Q3744 gcd, and it does not by itself prove the needed global height estimate. The remaining arithmetic target is

\[
\boxed{
\log \mathfrak A^+_{J,T}
=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^2}{\log T}\right).
}
\tag{V.9}
\]

No present Lucas/reflection argument supplies (V.9).

## 1. Exact upper-star identities

Let

\[
n=6p+r=6q+u,
\qquad q=p+d>p,
\tag{1.1}
\]

with legal digits

\[
0\le r<p,
\qquad
0\le u<q.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Subtracting the two representations of `n` gives

\[
\boxed{r-u=6d.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The plus secondary index is

\[
a=L_+=r+6d-p.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Using (1.3),

\[
a=u+12d-p=u+12q-13p.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Hence the lifted index is

\[
\begin{aligned}
13p+a
&=13p+u+12q-13p\\
&=12q+u.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
N:=13p+a=12q+u=n+6q.
}
\tag{1.6}
\]

For fixed `(q,u)`, both indices

\[
n=6q+u,
\qquad
N=12q+u
\tag{1.7}
\]

are independent of the lower neighbor `p`. This is the complete reason an upper-state star can use one two-value gcd.

The inverse formulas are

\[
\boxed{
 d=q-p,
 \qquad
 r=n-6p,
 \qquad
 a=N-13p.
}
\tag{1.8}
\]

Thus `(q,u,p)` determines `d,r,a,n,N`, and hence determines the edge. In particular:

> **Uniqueness in one star.** A fixed upper state `(q,u)` contains at most one plus-secondary edge with a given lower prime `p`.

## 2. Four Lucas congruences give `pq` in both values

Assume the true-edge conditions

\[
p\mid b_r,
\qquad
q\mid b_u,
\qquad
p\mid b_a.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Apply (0.1) to the row index `n` in the two characteristics.

Modulo `p`, since `n=6p+r`,

\[
b_n\equiv b_6b_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Modulo `q`, since `n=6q+u`,

\[
b_n\equiv b_6b_u\equiv0\pmod q.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Because `p` and `q` are distinct primes,

\[
\boxed{pq\mid b_n.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Now apply (0.1) to the lifted index `N`.

Modulo `p`, since `N=13p+a`,

\[
b_N\equiv b_{13}b_a\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Modulo `q`, since `N=12q+u`,

\[
b_N\equiv b_{12}b_u\equiv0\pmod q.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{pq\mid b_N.}
\tag{2.7}
\]

No unit assertion about `b_6,b_{12},b_{13}` is needed: the selected digit already makes each product zero. Reflection is also not needed at this final stage. It is an upstream tool for obtaining or interpreting the secondary zero; once `p\mid b_a` is given, the carrier proof is purely Lucas-theoretic.

Equations (2.4) and (2.7) immediately imply

\[
\boxed{pq\mid G^+_{q,u}.}
\tag{2.8}
\]

This is the whole local coverage theorem in one line.

## 3. Exact valuation ledger and the upper-prime caveat

For any prime `\ell`,

\[
v_\ell(G^+_{q,u})
=
\min\!\left\{
 v_\ell(b_{6q+u}),
 v_\ell(b_{12q+u})
\right\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since `q` is prime,

\[
v_\ell\!\left(\gcd(G^+_{q,u},q)\right)
=
\begin{cases}
1,&\ell=q\text{ and }q\mid G^+_{q,u},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence the exact valuation formula for the stripped carrier is

\[
\boxed{
 v_\ell(A^+_{q,u})
 =
 \min\!\left\{
 v_\ell(b_{6q+u}),
 v_\ell(b_{12q+u})
 \right\}
 -\mathbf 1_{\ell=q}\mathbf 1_{q\mid G^+_{q,u}}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

On a true edge, (2.8) gives `q\mid G^+_{q,u}`, so

\[
\boxed{
 v_q(A^+_{q,u})
 =
 \min\!\left\{
 v_q(b_n),v_q(b_N)
 \right\}-1
 \ge0.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This proves exactly what the one-layer normalization does.

### What is not proved

The assumptions show only

\[
\min\{v_q(b_n),v_q(b_N)\}\ge1.
\]

They do not show that this minimum equals one. Thus the statement

```text
v_q(Gplus) = 1
```

is **not justified** by Gessel--Lucas and reflection. The correct exact statements are (V.7)--(V.8): `q` occurs at least once, and `gcd(Gplus,q)` is exactly the integer `q`, so one layer is removed.

### Why no lower prime is removed

For every lower edge prime `p`,

\[
p<q,
\qquad p\ne q.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Therefore the subtraction term in (3.3) is zero at `\ell=p`, and

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(A^+_{q,u})
 =v_p(G^+_{q,u})
 =\min\{v_p(b_n),v_p(b_N)\}
 \ge1.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Dividing by `q` cannot remove any `p`-factor because `p` and `q` are coprime.

On a selected upper state, one can equivalently write

\[
\boxed{
 A^+_{q,u}
 =\gcd\!\left(\frac{b_n}{q},\frac{b_N}{q}\right).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Indeed, if `q\mid x,y`, then `\gcd(x,y)=q\gcd(x/q,y/q)`.

## 4. One star records every distinct lower edge

Let `E^+_{q,u}` be the set of true plus-secondary edges with fixed upper state `(q,u)`. For each edge `e`, let `p_e` be its lower prime and define

\[
\omega_e^{\mathrm{row}}
:=
\min\!\left\{
 v_{p_e}(b_{6q+u}),
 v_{p_e}(b_{12q+u})
\right\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

By §1, distinct edges in `E^+_{q,u}` have distinct lower primes. By (3.6),

\[
v_{p_e}(A^+_{q,u})=\omega_e^{\mathrm{row}}\ge1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since the `p_e` are pairwise distinct, their prime-power contributions multiply independently:

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{e\in E^+_{q,u}}
 p_e^{\omega_e^{\mathrm{row}}}
\mid A^+_{q,u}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular, (V.3) follows.

This is exactly the desired “one per edge” result **inside one star**. No candidate lower-prime product is needed.

### Valuation scope

The weight in (4.1) is not automatically the original digit valuation `v_{p_e}(b_{r_e})`. Ordinary Gessel--Lucas proves only the first divisibility layer

\[
p_e\mid b_{r_e}\Longrightarrow p_e\mid b_n.
\]

It does not, by itself, prove

\[
v_{p_e}(b_n)\ge v_{p_e}(b_{r_e}).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore the simplified carrier unconditionally preserves:

- one unit for every edge;
- in fact the common valuation `\omega_e^{\mathrm{row}}` of the two fixed star values;

but it is not yet a theorem that it preserves the entire higher `p`-adic multiplicity of the original lower zero `b_{r_e}`. The exceptional-edge count needs only the first item.

## 5. Products across stars preserve repeated lower-prime use

Let `\mathcal U` be any prescribed finite collection of upper states and define

\[
\mathfrak A^+(\mathcal U)
:=\prod_{(q,u)\in\mathcal U}A^+_{q,u}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Fix a prime `\ell`. Every true edge with lower prime `\ell` belongs to one unique upper state `(q_e,u_e)`. Within that star, `q_e\ne\ell`, so (3.6) contributes at least one `\ell` to that factor. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_\ell\bigl(\mathfrak A^+(\mathcal U)\bigr)
&=\sum_{(q,u)\in\mathcal U}v_\ell(A^+_{q,u})\\
&\ge
\sum_{\substack{e:\ (q_e,u_e)\in\mathcal U\\p_e=\ell}}
\omega_e^{\mathrm{row}}\\
&\ge
\#\{e:(q_e,u_e)\in\mathcal U,\ p_e=\ell\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This proves (V.5)--(V.6).

A possible concern is that `\ell` may be the **upper** prime of some other star, where one `\ell` layer is stripped. This causes no cancellation. Valuations of a product are sums of the nonnegative valuations of its factors. The edge with lower prime `\ell` contributes inside its own star factor, whose upper prime is different from `\ell`; stripping in a separate upper-`\ell` factor does not subtract from that contribution.

The global operation must be the **product** (5.1). Replacing it by a gcd or lcm generally loses edge multiplicity: if the same lower prime occurs in `k` different stars, the product contains at least `\ell^k`, whereas an lcm need contain only one `\ell` layer.

## 6. A fully prescribed shell carrier

It is useful to formulate the carrier without inspecting any selected edge.

Fix an outer row shell, for example

\[
T<n\le2T,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and fix a prescribed gap set `\mathscr D_{J,T}\subset\mathbf Z_{>0}`. In the current macroscopic application one may take

\[
\mathscr D_{J,T}
=\{d:T/J<d\le T/21\},
\tag{6.2}
\]

or replace it by one prescribed dyadic block `D<d\le2D`.

For a prime `q` and `0\le u<q`, set

\[
n(q,u)=6q+u,
\qquad
N(q,u)=12q+u.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Define the purely geometric candidate lower-prime fibre

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P^+_{J,T}(q,u)
:=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l|l}
p\text{ prime}
&
q-p\in\mathscr D_{J,T},\\[-1mm]
&\displaystyle\frac{6q+u}{7}<p\le\frac{12q+u}{13}
\end{array}
\right\}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The interval in (6.4) is exactly the plus-legality interval. Indeed, with

\[
r=6q+u-6p,
\qquad
a=12q+u-13p,
\tag{6.5}
\]

one has:

- `r<p` iff `p>(6q+u)/7`;
- `a\ge0` iff `p\le(12q+u)/13`;
- `r\ge0` is automatic from `p<q`;
- `a<p` follows from `p>(6q+u)/7`, because
  \[
  14p>2(6q+u)\ge12q+u;
  \]
- `p<q` follows from `p\le(12q+u)/13<q`, since `u<q`.

Now prescribe the upper-star set

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal U^+_{J,T}
:=
\left\{
(q,u):
\begin{array}{l}
q\text{ prime},\ 0\le u<q,\\
T<6q+u\le2T,\\
\mathcal P^+_{J,T}(q,u)\ne\varnothing
\end{array}
\right\}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

This set uses only primes and deterministic inequalities. It does not use `p\mid b_r`, `q\mid b_u`, or `p\mid b_a`.

The fully prescribed shell carrier is

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak A^+_{J,T}
:=
\prod_{(q,u)\in\mathcal U^+_{J,T}}
\frac{
\gcd(b_{6q+u},b_{12q+u})
}{
\gcd\!\bigl(\gcd(b_{6q+u},b_{12q+u}),q\bigr)
}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Every true plus-secondary edge in the prescribed shell lies in one fibre (6.4), and §§2--5 give, for every prime `\ell`,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{e\in E^+_{J,T}:p_e=\ell\}
\le v_\ell(\mathfrak A^+_{J,T}).
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Since every lower prime in the row shell satisfies `p>n/7>T/7`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
|E^+_{J,T}|\log(T/7)
&\le \sum_e\log p_e\\
&=\sum_\ell
\#\{e:p_e=\ell\}\log\ell\\
&\le\sum_\ell v_\ell(\mathfrak A^+_{J,T})\log\ell\\
&=\log\mathfrak A^+_{J,T}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus the exact remaining height target is (V.9), which would imply

\[
|E^+_{J,T}|
=o_J(T^2/\log^2T).
\tag{6.10}
\]

The construction proves coverage and multiplicity, not (V.9).

## 7. Rigorous comparison with the Q3744 carrier

For the same fixed upper state, Q3744 used

\[
\boxed{
C^{\mathrm{old}}_{q,u}
:=
\gcd\!\left(
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P^+_{J,T}(q,u)}b_{r_p},
\frac{b_N}{\gcd(b_N,q)}
\right),
\qquad
r_p=6q+u-6p,
\quad N=12q+u.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The new carrier is

\[
\boxed{
C^{\mathrm{new}}_{q,u}
:=A^+_{q,u}
=
\frac{\gcd(b_n,b_N)}{\gcd(\gcd(b_n,b_N),q)},
\qquad n=6q+u.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

On a true upper-selected state, `q\mid b_u`, so `q` divides both `b_n` and `b_N`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
C^{\mathrm{new}}_{q,u}
=\gcd(b_n/q,b_N/q),
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

whereas

\[
\boxed{
C^{\mathrm{old}}_{q,u}
=\gcd\!\left(
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P^+_{J,T}(q,u)}b_{r_p},
 b_N/q
\right).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### 7.1 Coverage

For a true edge with lower prime `p`:

- Q3744 uses `p\mid b_{r_p}` and `p\mid b_N`;
- the new carrier uses `p\mid b_n` from Lucas and `p\mid b_N`.

Since `p\ne q`, division by `q` is harmless in both constructions. Both therefore preserve one lower-prime unit per edge.

### 7.2 Multiplicity across stars

Both constructions must be multiplied over stars. In both cases, fixed `(q,u,p)` determines one edge, so the same valuation-sum proof gives full edge-incidence multiplicity. The new proof is shorter because no candidate-product valuation has to be tracked.

### 7.3 Higher local valuation

The old natural edge weight is bounded below by

\[
\min\{v_p(b_{r_p}),v_p(b_N)\},
\]

while the new exact weight is

\[
\min\{v_p(b_n),v_p(b_N)\}.
\]

Gessel--Lucas modulo `p` proves that both are at least one, but it gives no general inequality between `v_p(b_{r_p})` and `v_p(b_n)`. Therefore neither carrier is presently proved to dominate the other at higher `p`-adic order.

### 7.4 No pointwise divisibility ordering

The first arguments of the two gcds are respectively

\[
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P^+_{J,T}(q,u)}b_{r_p}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b_n/q.
\]

No current Lucas or reflection identity says that either integer divides the other. Consequently there is no proved pointwise relation

\[
C^{\mathrm{new}}_{q,u}\mid C^{\mathrm{old}}_{q,u}
\quad\text{or}\quad
C^{\mathrm{old}}_{q,u}\mid C^{\mathrm{new}}_{q,u}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

The simplification is structural, not a theorem that every new numerical carrier is smaller.

### 7.5 Deterministic height ceilings

The new carrier does have a shorter immediate ceiling:

\[
C^{\mathrm{new}}_{q,u}\le b_n/q
\qquad\text{on a selected upper state},
\tag{7.6}
\]

whereas Q3744 gives only

\[
C^{\mathrm{old}}_{q,u}\le b_N/q.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Using the elementary Apéry bound

\[
b_k\le(k+1)64^k,
\tag{7.8}
\]

and `N=n+6q\le2n`, one obtains

\[
\log C^{\mathrm{new}}_{q,u}\le n\log64+O(\log n),
\tag{7.9}
\]

\[
\log C^{\mathrm{old}}_{q,u}\le N\log64+O(\log N)
\le2n\log64+O(\log n).
\tag{7.10}
\]

This is a genuine improvement in the uniform per-star ceiling. It is not enough to prove the shell estimate (V.9), because the number of prescribed stars is large and the actual gcd cancellation remains uncontrolled. Conversely, the old candidate product can make its actual gcd much smaller than its crude ceiling, so (7.9)--(7.10) still do not establish pointwise ordering.

### 7.6 Practical and logical comparison

| feature | Q3744 carrier | simplified carrier |
|---|---|---|
| fixed values in gcd | candidate product and `b_N` | only `b_n` and `b_N` |
| candidate lower-prime enumeration inside scalar | required | absent |
| true-edge `p` coverage | primary digit plus lift | row Lucas plus lift |
| one edge per `p` in a star | yes | yes |
| repeated `p` across stars | product preserves it | product preserves it |
| one upper `q` layer stripped | yes | yes |
| exact `v_q(G)=1` needed | no | no |
| full lower-zero valuation proved | no without an extra lift theorem | no without an extra lift theorem |
| global subcritical height proved | no | no |

Thus the new carrier **supersedes Q3744 as the cleaner incidence carrier**, but not as a proved height theorem.

## 8. Optional stronger stripping

For lower-edge counting only, one may remove the entire upper-prime primary part

\[
A^{+,\circ}_{q,u}
:=
\frac{G^+_{q,u}}{q^{v_q(G^+_{q,u})}}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is also fully prescribed and still satisfies

\[
v_p(A^{+,\circ}_{q,u})=v_p(G^+_{q,u})\ge1
\]

for every lower edge prime `p\ne q`. Even if the same numerical prime `q` occurs as a lower prime in another star, its contribution is in that other factor and is unaffected by stripping the upper-`q` factor here.

The question's one-layer carrier (V.1) is therefore valid but not maximally stripped. Keeping only the one-layer normalization is harmless and matches Q3744's convention.

## 9. Final audit answers

1. **Does every distinct lower prime in a true plus-secondary star divide `Aplus`?**  
   **Yes.** In fact (V.4) gives its exact retained valuation in the two fixed star values.

2. **Is there at most one edge per lower prime inside a fixed star?**  
   **Yes.** The inverse formulas (1.8) recover the entire edge from `(q,u,p)`.

3. **Do products over prescribed stars preserve full edge multiplicity?**  
   **Yes at the edge-incidence level.** Equation (V.6) counts repeated use of one lower prime across distinct stars. The global operation must be a product, not a gcd or lcm.

4. **Does `q` divide `Gplus`?**  
   **Yes, at least once**, because `q` divides both `b_{6q+u}` and `b_{12q+u}`.

5. **Is `v_q(Gplus)=1` proved?**  
   **No.** The exact statement is `v_q(Gplus)\ge1`; nevertheless `gcd(Gplus,q)=q`, so the proposed denominator strips exactly one layer.

6. **Can stripping that layer remove a lower prime?**  
   **No**, because every lower prime satisfies `p<q` and hence `p\ne q`.

7. **Is the shell carrier selected-edge independent?**  
   **Yes.** Formula (6.7), indexed by the geometric set (6.6), is completely prescribed before testing any Apéry zero.

8. **Does this finish the fixed-`J` counting theorem?**  
   **Not yet.** It reduces the plus-secondary contribution to the explicit star-gcd height estimate (V.9). That estimate remains a genuinely new arithmetic statement.
