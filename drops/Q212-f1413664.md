# Executive verdict

Among the four proposed directions, **Option 1 is the only genuinely intermediate one**, but it needs two corrections.

1. The existing bound
   \[
   \#\{n\le X:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}
   \ll_\varepsilon (\log X)^2
   \]
   does **not** come from applying a second-moment tail estimate to one random variable. In `problems/3.2/proof.tex`, Theorem `thm:polylog` comes from a local packing argument using the common-bad-prime codegree lemma. Consequently, a formal \(2+\delta\) moment does not automatically turn the exponent \(2\) into \(2-\delta\).

2. In the sparse integer-valued setting, the ordinary moment \(\sum K^{2+\delta}\) is the wrong object. The first genuinely new contribution beyond \((K)_2\) occurs at \(K=3\). The correct interpolation is a **fractional pair-Palm/third-hit statistic**, or equivalently a fractional improvement in the high-level atom tail.

There is nevertheless a substantial unconditional answer to the broad question “can anything stronger than density one be proved?” The banked argument already implies the following variable-threshold estimates:

- for every fixed \(A>0\),
  \[
  \boxed{
  \#\left\{n\le X:\log G_n>\frac{n}{(\log n)^A}\right\}
  \ll_A (\log X)^{4A+2};}
  \]
- more generally, for every fixed \(\alpha\in(2/3,1)\), one obtains a power-saving exceptional set for the bound \(\log G_n\le n^\alpha\).

Thus one can prove a quantitative rate much stronger than \(o(n)\) on an overwhelmingly large set, without any form of \((HM)_3\). What cannot presently be extracted is a uniform rate for **every** integer.

For a new improvement of the fixed-\(\varepsilon\) count itself, the closest concrete target is either:

- a logarithmic saving in the **aggregate common-prime codegree** used by the existing local packing proof; or
- a fractional atom-tail estimate \((AT_\delta)\), which is strictly between \((HM)_2\) and \((HM)_3\) but still requires genuine third-prime arithmetic.

Options 2 and 3, in their stated all-\(n\) forms, are not intermediate: they imply the full conjecture. Option 4 is not a valid Borel–Cantelli argument for a deterministic sequence.

# 1. What the current unconditional proof actually gives

Let
\[
 \mathcal E_\varepsilon(X)
 =\{n\le X:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}.
\]
Theorem `thm:polylog` in `problems/3.2/proof.tex` proves
\[
 |\mathcal E_\varepsilon(X)|
 \ll_\varepsilon (\log X)^2.
\]
Its proof contains considerably more local information than this global statement.

For \(n\in(N,2N]\), let
\[
 t(n)=\#\{p>\sqrt n:\ n\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\}
\]
be the number of lower-digit bad primes. The small-prime and companion-block estimates give, for an absolute \(C_0\),
\[
 \log G_n\le C_0n^{2/3}+3t(n)\log(2n).
 \tag{1}
\]
Hence a fixed-\(\varepsilon\) exception has
\[
 t(n)\gg_\varepsilon \frac{N}{\log N}.
 \tag{2}
\]

Now put such exceptional integers into an interval \(J\subset(N,2N]\) of length
\[
 Y\asymp_\varepsilon \frac{N}{\log N}.
\]
If \(M\) exceptional integers lie in \(J\), the Cauchy–Schwarz lower bound for pair incidences among their bad-prime sets conflicts with the upper bound supplied by Lemma `lem:codegree`, unless
\[
 M=O_\varepsilon(1).
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus the real theorem is:

> Every interval of the critical length \(\asymp_\varepsilon N/\log N\) inside \((N,2N]\) contains only \(O_\varepsilon(1)\) fixed-\(\varepsilon\) exceptions.

There are \(O_\varepsilon(\log N)\) critical intervals in one dyadic shell, so
\[
 \#\bigl(\mathcal E_\varepsilon\cap(N,2N]\bigr)
 \ll_\varepsilon \log N.
 \tag{4}
\]
Summing (4) over the \(O(\log X)\) dyadic shells below \(X\) produces \((\log X)^2\).

This explains an important point: **the exponent 2 is a packing exponent**, not a moment exponent.

The same proof already gives:

- upper Banach density zero;
- finite harmonic weight,
  \[
  \sum_{n\in\mathcal E_\varepsilon}\frac1n<\infty;
  \]
- stretched-exponential spacing of exceptions: if
  \(e_1<e_2<\cdots\) are the \(\varepsilon\)-exceptions, then
  \[
  e_j\ge \exp(c_\varepsilon\sqrt j).
  \tag{5}
  \]

Indeed, (5) follows immediately by inverting
\(j\ll_\varepsilon(\log e_j)^2\).

These conclusions are compatible with an infinite exceptional set. For example, a set containing approximately \(k\) well-separated points in the shell \((2^k,2^{k+1}]\) has counting function \(\asymp(\log X)^2\) and finite harmonic weight. Therefore finite harmonic weight plus the present local spacing cannot by itself improve the logarithmic exponent.

# 2. A variable-threshold theorem available without \((HM)_3\)

The proof of `thm:polylog` can be rerun with a variable height threshold. This yields a useful intermediate theorem that does not seem to be stated explicitly in the current manuscript.

## Proposition: threshold tradeoff

For \(N\) large and a threshold \(H=H(N)\) satisfying
\[
 H\gg N^{2/3},\qquad H\le N,
\]
put
\[
 E(N,H)=\#\{n\in(N,2N]:\log G_n>H\}.
\]
Then the arguments already proved in `proof.tex` give
\[
 \boxed{
 E(N,H)
 \ll
 \min\left\{
   \frac{N^{5/3}}{H},
   \frac{N^4\log N}{H^4}
 \right\}.}
 \tag{6}
\]
Using the later unconditional corank-average estimate recorded in `problems/3.2/atom_tail_section.tex`,
\[
 \sum_{p\le x}Z(p)\ll \frac{x^{8/5+o(1)}}{\log x},
\]
the first term in (6) improves to
\[
 \frac{N^{8/5+o(1)}}{H}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The second term is independent of this average improvement.

## Proof

By (1), after choosing the implicit constant in \(H\gg N^{2/3}\) sufficiently large, every counted \(n\) satisfies
\[
 t(n)\ge T,
 \qquad
 T\asymp \frac{H}{\log N}.
 \tag{8}
\]

The first-moment estimate in the density-one proof is
\[
 \sum_{N<n\le2N}t(n)
 \ll \frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}.
\]
Markov and (8) give the first term of (6). If one inserts the banked average bound \(\sum_{p\le x}Z(p)\ll x^{8/5+o(1)}/\log x\), partial summation gives
\[
 \sum_{N<n\le2N}t(n)
 \ll \frac{N^{8/5+o(1)}}{\log N},
\]
and hence (7).

For the local estimate, let \(J\subset(N,2N]\) have length \(Y\), let \(\mathcal A\) be the set of counted integers in \(J\), and write \(M=|\mathcal A|\). For each relevant prime \(p\), let \(d_p\) be the number of elements of \(\mathcal A\) hit by \(p\). Put
\[
 I=\sum_pd_p.
\]
Then (8) gives \(I\ge MT\). The prime universe has cardinality
\[
 L\ll \frac{N}{\log N}.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
 \sum_p\binom{d_p}{2}
 \ge \frac12\left(\frac{I^2}{L}-I\right).
 \tag{9}
\]
On the other hand, Lemma `lem:codegree` gives
\[
 \sum_p\binom{d_p}{2}
 \ll YM^2.
 \tag{10}
\]
Choose
\[
 Y=c\frac{T^2}{L}
   \asymp c\frac{H^2}{N\log N}
 \tag{11}
\]
with \(c>0\) sufficiently small. Combining (9) and (10), and using \(I\le LM\), yields
\[
 M\ll \frac{L^2}{T^2}\ll \frac{N^2}{H^2}.
 \tag{12}
\]
There are
\[
 O\left(\frac{N}{Y}\right)
 =O\left(\frac{N^2\log N}{H^2}\right)
\]
such intervals. Multiplying by (12) gives
\[
 E(N,H)\ll \frac{N^4\log N}{H^4},
\]
which completes the proof.

## Consequence 1: an almost-all logarithmic rate

Take
\[
 H(N)=\frac{N}{(\log N)^A}
\]
for any fixed \(A>0\). The second term of (6) gives
\[
 E(N,H)\ll_A (\log N)^{4A+1}
\]
in one dyadic shell. Summing over dyadic shells gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \#\left\{n\le X:
   \log G_n>\frac{n}{(\log n)^A}
 \right\}
 \ll_A (\log X)^{4A+2}.}
 \tag{13}
\]

This is a genuine unconditional strengthening of the density-one theorem: for every prescribed fixed power of \(\log n\), the estimate
\[
 \log G_n\le \frac{n}{(\log n)^A}
\]
holds outside a polylogarithmic exceptional set.

It does **not** give the estimate for every \(n\), because the exceptional set may remain infinite.

## Consequence 2: power-rate exceptional sets

For any fixed \(\alpha\in(2/3,1)\), take \(H=N^\alpha\). Combining (6) and (7) gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \#\{n\le X:\log G_n>n^\alpha\}
 \ll_\alpha
 X^{\min(8/5-\alpha,\,4-4\alpha)+o(1)}.}
 \tag{14}
\]
If one uses only the older first-moment estimate in `proof.tex`, replace \(8/5-\alpha\) by \(5/3-\alpha\).

For example, with \(\alpha=2/3+\eta\),
\[
 \#\{n\le X:\log G_n>n^{2/3+\eta}\}
 \ll_\eta
 X^{\min(14/15-\eta,\,4/3-4\eta)+o(1)}.
 \tag{15}
\]

Thus there is already a continuum of quantitative intermediate theorems between the banked deterministic \(O(n^{2/3})\) floor and the fixed-\(\varepsilon\) density-one assertion.

# 3. Why an ordinary \(2+\delta\) moment is not the right interpolation

Let
\[
 K=K_X(m),\qquad
 F_j(X)=\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_j.
\]
The proved statement is
\[
 F_2(X)\ll X^2\lambda_X^2.
\]

In a sparse Poisson or Bernoulli model with mean \(\lambda\ll1\),
\[
 \mathbb E[K^{2+\delta}]\asymp\lambda,
\]
because rows with \(K=1\) dominate. Therefore a bound of scale
\(\lambda^{2+\delta}\) for the ordinary \(2+\delta\) moment is not even compatible with the independent model.

The same warning applies to a centered ordinary moment: a sparse Bernoulli variable still has
\(\mathbb E|K-\lambda|^{2+\delta}\asymp\lambda\).

The correct object must vanish for \(K=0,1,2\). For \(0<\delta<1\), define
\[
 \mathfrak F_{2,\delta}(X)
 =\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_2\,(K_X(m)-2)_+^\delta.
 \tag{16}
\]
Pointwise one has the exact identity
\[
 (K)_2(K-2)_+^\delta
 =\bigl((K)_2\bigr)^{1-\delta}
  \bigl((K)_3\bigr)^\delta.
 \tag{17}
\]
Hence Hölder gives
\[
 \mathfrak F_{2,\delta}(X)
 \le F_2(X)^{1-\delta}F_3(X)^\delta.
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus \((HM)_2+(HM)_3\) implies
\[
 \mathfrak F_{2,\delta}(X)
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^{2+\delta}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Equation (19) is a mathematically clean statement strictly between the second and third factorial moments.

It gives the tail estimate
\[
 n_T(X):=\#\{m<X^2:K_X(m)\ge T\}
 \le
 \frac{\mathfrak F_{2,\delta}(X)}
      {(T)_2(T-2)^\delta},
 \qquad T\ge3.
 \tag{20}
\]
At the level of powers, (19) therefore yields
\[
 n_T(X)
 \ll
 \frac{X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^{2+\delta}}
      {T^{2+\delta}}.
 \tag{21}
\]
This gains approximately \((\lambda_X/T)^\delta\) over the unconditional second-moment tail.

A convenient direct formulation is:

```text
(AT_δ)  There exists δ in (0,1) such that, uniformly for T >= 3,

        n_T(X) << X^{2+o(1)} λ_X^{2+δ} / T^{2+δ}.
```

At \(\delta=0\), this is the proved \((HM)_2\) tail. At \(\delta=1\), it is the \((HM)_3\)-scale tail characterized in `atom_tail_section.tex`. Thus \((AT_\delta)\) is the exact tail interpolation suggested by Option 1.

There is also an even weaker “existence-only” third-hit statistic:
\[
 \mathfrak S_3(X)
 =\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_2\mathbf1_{\{K_X(m)\ge3\}}.
 \tag{22}
\]
It satisfies
\[
 \mathfrak S_3(X)
 \le \mathfrak F_{2,\delta}(X)
 \le F_3(X).
 \tag{23}
\]
In pair-Palm notation,
\[
 \mathfrak S_3(X)
 =\sum_{p\ne q}
   \#\{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q:
          \text{at least one third prime hits }m\}.
 \tag{24}
\]
Thus (22) counts only whether a pair locus receives any third hit; it does not count the multiplicity of third primes. This is the **minimal discrete correlation beyond \((HM)_2\)**.

The key conclusion is nevertheless negative:

> For an integer-valued \(K_X\), every nonzero improvement beyond \((HM)_2\) is already a third-hit problem. A tiny positive \(\delta\) weakens the multiplicity demanded from triple intersections, but it does not turn the problem into a purely pairwise one.

This is exactly what the pair-Palm hierarchy in `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex` formalizes.

# 4. Why present pairwise inputs cannot prove even a tiny \(\delta>0\)

The obstruction in `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex`, Proposition `prop:hm3-anchored-star`, is decisive here.

That construction has all of the following properties:

- every nonempty model zero set has size two;
- reflection symmetry, endpoint exclusion, no consecutive zeros, and the interval zero-count bound hold;
- the universal \((HM)_2\) estimate holds;
- the centered second moment has the natural scale;
- the row-codegree of any two distinct integers is uniformly bounded;
- the numerical fiber and collision-energy consequences used elsewhere in the project can also be imposed;

but one row \(m_0\) is hit by
\[
 K_X(m_0)\asymp \frac{X}{\log X}
\]
primes, while
\[
 \lambda_X\asymp\frac1{\log X}.
\]

Take \(T\asymp X/\log X\). The right side of \((AT_\delta)\) is then
\[
 \frac{X^{2+o(1)}(1/\log X)^{2+\delta}}
      {(X/\log X)^{2+\delta}}
 =X^{-\delta+o(1)},
\]
while the left side is at least one. Thus the model violates \((AT_\delta)\) for **every** \(\delta>0\).

Therefore no positive fractional improvement can be deduced from the current package of:

- one-prime zero-set bounds;
- reflection and no-adjacency;
- \((HM)_2\);
- centered variance;
- bounded row-codegree;
- fiber-energy bounds;
- individual gap certificates.

A proof of any \((AT_\delta)\) must eliminate the isolated giant-atom mechanism. That is already genuinely new arithmetic, even though it is weaker than full \((HM)_3\).

# 5. The most direct route to \((\log X)^{2-\eta}\)

Because the current \((\log X)^2\) theorem is a local packing theorem, the most direct route to the requested exponent saving is not a fractional moment but a logarithmic saving in the **aggregate codegree**.

For a set \(\mathcal A\subset J\subset(N,2N]\), let
\[
 \mathcal P_n
 =\{p>\sqrt N:n\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\}.
\]
The banked proof uses
\[
 \sum_{\substack{m<n\\m,n\in\mathcal A}}
 |\mathcal P_m\cap\mathcal P_n|
 \ll Y|\mathcal A|^2,
 \qquad |J|=Y.
 \tag{25}
\]
Suppose one could prove, for some fixed \(\eta>0\), the strengthened hereditary estimate
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{m<n\\m,n\in\mathcal A}}
 |\mathcal P_m\cap\mathcal P_n|
 \ll
 \frac{Y|\mathcal A|^2}{(\log N)^\eta}}
 \tag{$CD_\eta$}
\]
uniformly for the critical sets \(\mathcal A\).

For fixed-\(\varepsilon\) exceptions, the Cauchy lower bound has scale
\[
 \frac{N}{\log N}|\mathcal A|^2.
\]
Balancing it with \((CD_\eta)\) permits an interval length
\[
 Y\asymp_\varepsilon
 \frac{N}{(\log N)^{1-\eta}},
\]
instead of \(N/\log N\). Hence a dyadic shell contains
\[
 O_\varepsilon((\log N)^{1-\eta})
\]
exceptions, and dyadic summation gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \#\mathcal E_\varepsilon(X)
 \ll_\varepsilon(\log X)^{2-\eta}.}
 \tag{26}
\]

Thus \((CD_\eta)\) is an exact sufficient theorem for Option 1.

Even a slowly growing saving would be meaningful. For example, replacing the right side of (25) by
\[
 \frac{Y|\mathcal A|^2}{\log\log N}
\]
would give an exceptional-set improvement of roughly
\[
 \frac{(\log X)^2}{\log\log X}.
\]
So an \(o(1)\) saving in aggregate codegree is already a genuine result beyond the current theorem, without approaching the full factorial-moment hierarchy.

## Arithmetic content of \((CD_\eta)\)

Lemma `lem:codegree` proves the pointwise \(O(h)\) bound by showing that a prime common to two rows at distance \(h\):

- divides the integer gap value \(N_h(m)\) in the no-wrap case; or
- divides one of the integers \(m+1,\ldots,m+h\) in the wrap case.

The estimate \(O(h)\) is obtained only from the logarithmic heights of these integers. Consequently, \((CD_\eta)\) asks for an average large-prime-factor sparsity theorem for the values
\[
 N_h(m)
\]
and the wrap products. It is not full cross-prime independence of the Apéry zero events. It is a more classical-looking integer-factorization problem, but no such uniform hereditary saving is currently banked.

This is, in my view, the **closest concrete route to the specific exponent \(2-\eta\)** because it modifies exactly one line of the existing proof and has an exact payoff.

# 6. Assessment of the four options

## Option 1: improve the fixed-\(\varepsilon\) exceptional set

**Verdict: closest, after reformulation.**

Two precise targets are available:

1. the direct packing target \((CD_\eta)\), which yields exactly \((\log X)^{2-\eta}\); or
2. the moment-side target \((AT_\delta)\), which gives a fractional gain over the second-moment atom tail.

The second is genuinely weaker than \((HM)_3\), but it is not “almost pairwise”: it still has to control the existence of a third prime on a two-prime locus. The anchored-star construction proves that current pairwise inputs cannot imply it.

A particularly modest first target would be the support-Palm bound
\[
 \mathfrak S_3(X)
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^{2+\delta}
\]
for some \(\delta>0\), or only above the critical levels needed by the all-\(n\) assembly. This ignores third-prime multiplicity and is strictly weaker than \((HM)_3\).

## Option 2: bounded exceptional set

**Verdict: not intermediate.**

For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), a bound
\[
 \sup_X\#\{n\le X:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}<\infty
\]
means that the set of \(\varepsilon\)-exceptions is finite. If this holds for every \(\varepsilon>0\), then
\[
 \frac{\log G_n}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Conversely, the all-\(n\) conjecture implies finiteness of every fixed-\(\varepsilon\) exceptional set. Hence Option 2, with its \(C_\varepsilon\) quantifier, is **equivalent to the all-\(n\) conjecture**.

An exponential moment is one sufficient route, but not a necessary one. Also, “all fixed factorial moments” is not automatically equivalent to an exponential moment unless the constants are controlled uniformly in the order. The exact identity is
\[
 \mathbb E[e^{\theta K}]
 =\sum_{j\ge0}
   \frac{(e^\theta-1)^j}{j!}\,
   \mathbb E[(K)_j].
 \tag{27}
\]
To sum (27), one needs factorial-moment constants with suitable exponential growth in \(j\), not merely a separate \((HM)_j\) statement for each fixed \(j\).

The strongest unconditional substitute currently available is the stretched-exponential spacing (5), not finiteness.

## Option 3: \(\log G_n\le n/(\log n)^\varepsilon\) for all \(n\)

**Verdict: a stronger quantitative all-\(n\) theorem, not a weaker intermediate.**

For any fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), this estimate implies \(\log G_n=o(n)\) for every sufficiently large \(n\), with an explicit rate. It requires a maximum/anti-tail theorem and cannot follow from \((HM)_2\); the anchored-star construction again supplies the abstract obstruction.

However, the **almost-all** version is already provable in a very strong form: equation (13) holds for every fixed \(A>0\). This is probably the cleanest unconditional intermediate theorem to add to the paper now.

## Option 4: Borel–Cantelli

**Verdict: not a valid deterministic upgrade.**

The first Borel–Cantelli lemma concerns events \(E_n\) in one fixed probability space and assumes
\[
 \sum_n\mathbb P(E_n)<\infty.
\]
The deterministic sequence \(B(n)\) does not supply such a probability space. Empirical frequencies on growing intervals are not probabilities of a fixed sequence of events to which Borel–Cantelli can be applied.

More decisively, the paper already proves
\[
 \sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\\log G_n>\varepsilon n}}
 \frac1n<\infty,
\]
but this does not imply that the exceptional set is finite. The set \(\{2^j:j\ge1\}\) is the elementary counterexample:
\[
 \sum_j2^{-j}<\infty
\]
although the set is infinite.

One may randomize a choice of one integer from each dyadic shell and then obtain an almost-sure statement about the randomly selected subsequence. That gives no deterministic information about the unselected integers and cannot prove the all-\(n\) conjecture.

# 7. Recommended hierarchy of next targets

The following order separates what is available now from what requires genuinely new correlation input.

### A. Add the unconditional threshold corollary

Bank equations (6), (13), and (14). They use only existing lemmas and give a substantial quantitative strengthening of the density-one result:

- \(n/(\log n)^A\) outside a polylogarithmic set for every fixed \(A\);
- \(n^\alpha\) outside a power-saving set for every \(\alpha>2/3\).

This is the nearest fully provable result.

### B. Seek any aggregate codegree saving

Try first for
\[
 \sum_{m<n\in\mathcal A}
 |\mathcal P_m\cap\mathcal P_n|
 =o(Y|\mathcal A|^2)
\]
on critical windows. Even this would improve \(O((\log X)^2)\) to \(o((\log X)^2)\). A power of \(\log N\) gives the requested exponent saving.

The needed arithmetic is average large-prime-factor sparsity for gap values, not the full moving-target three-prime theorem.

### C. Formulate the minimal third-hit hypothesis

On the moment side, use \((AT_\delta)\) or the support statistic \(\mathfrak S_3\), preferably only at the high levels used by the all-\(n\) reduction. This is strictly weaker than full \((HM)_3\) and isolates exactly how much third-prime information is required.

### D. Do not target Options 2 or 3 as “intermediate”

Their all-\(n\) formulations already imply the desired conjecture. They are final-goal statements in stronger quantitative form.

# Final answer

There **is** meaningful progress available without \((HM)_3\), but it is of two different kinds:

1. **Already provable:** a strong quantitative almost-all rate. In particular, for every fixed \(A>0\),
   \[
   \#\left\{n\le X:\log G_n>\frac{n}{(\log n)^A}\right\}
   \ll_A(\log X)^{4A+2}.
   \]
   This follows by rerunning the existing codegree-packing proof with a variable threshold.

2. **Closest new open improvement:** a logarithmic saving in aggregate codegree, which would directly yield
   \((\log X)^{2-\eta}\), or a fractional atom-tail estimate \((AT_\delta)\).

A fractional \(2+\delta\) improvement is genuinely weaker than \((HM)_3\), but it is **not** free of triple arithmetic: because \(K_X\) is integer-valued, the first new support beyond \((HM)_2\) is exactly the event that a two-prime CRT locus receives a third hit. The anchored-star obstruction in `hm3_result.tex` shows that no positive \(\delta\) follows from the current one-prime and pairwise package.

So the ranking is:

1. **Option 1, reformulated as \((CD_\eta)\) or \((AT_\delta)\): closest.**
2. **Option 3 in an almost-all form: already provable strongly.**
3. **Option 2: equivalent to the all-\(n\) conjecture when required for every \(\varepsilon\).**
4. **Option 4: Borel–Cantelli does not apply to the deterministic empirical sequence.**
