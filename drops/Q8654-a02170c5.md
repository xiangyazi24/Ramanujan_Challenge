# Q8654 Strategic audit

## 1. Correct counting object

The natural object is not the translation variable alone. A target-regular fibre contains

\[
(t,N,p_1,p_2,p_3,c_1,c_2,c_3)
\]

subject to

\[
N-d_i=c_ip_i,
\qquad z_i-z_1=d_i,
\qquad z_i\in\{u_i,-u_i-1\}.
\]

The parameter \(t=z_1\) has only \(O(X)\) possible values, but this does not imply an \(O(X)\) fibre bound: for each translation there may be many admissible prime triples and products. The free variable \(N\) is the missing dimension in the naive argument.

The unconditional starting bound is therefore a count of admissible rows, not translations.

## 2. Exact equations

For fixed full defect key,

\[
d_i=N_1-N_i=z_i-z_1,
\]

so

\[
N_i=N-d_i=c_ip_i.
\]

Subtracting two coordinates gives

\[
c_1p_1-c_ip_i=d_i.
\]

For fixed prime pair \((p_1,p_i)\), this is a two-variable linear Diophantine equation. It does not determine \(t\). The variable \(t\) enters only through the Apéry-zero conditions

\[
p_i\mid B_{u_i(t)}.
\]

The affine maps are

\[
u_i(t)=s_i(t+d_i+\tau_i).
\]

The condition that the folded anchors are distinct gives only that three affine images do not coincide; it does not create an injective map from prime pairs to translations.

## 3. Pairwise prime-pair gate

For fixed \((p_i,p_j)\), equality of products gives

\[
c_ip_i-c_jp_j=d_j-d_i.
\]

Modulo \(p_i\),

\[
c_jp_j\equiv d_i-d_j\pmod {p_i}.
\]

Modulo \(p_j\),

\[
c_ip_i\equiv d_j-d_i\pmod {p_j}.
\]

Thus the coefficient pair is constrained by CRT. However the CRT residue does not determine the Apéry zero location. It only determines possible coefficients. The remaining multiplicity is exactly the intersection

\[
\{t:p_i\mid B_{u_i(t)},\ p_j\mid B_{u_j(t)}\}.
\]

No independence assumption is available.

## 4. Short CRT is smaller than full injectivity

For a fixed root triple and defects, short CRT fixes

\[
c_1\pmod {p_2p_3}.
\]

Because

\[
p_2p_3>X^2
\]

and \(1\leq c_1<X\), there is at most one admissible coefficient. This is a genuine gate.

But it gives uniqueness of \(c_1\) after the roots are supplied. It does not bound the number of root triples producing the same translation.

Therefore SCRT is a compression step, not a proof of fibre sparsity.

## 5. Best unconditional structural bound

Ignoring Apéry divisibility, the coefficient choices give

\[
X^3
\]

possibilities and shell primes give roughly

\[
\pi(2X)-\pi(X)\asymp X/\log X.
\]

The crude triple-prime bound is therefore enormous:

\[
O(X^3(X/\log X)^3).
\]

Using shell uniqueness improves this. Since

\[
N_i<X^2+X
\]

and every positive \(N_i\) has at most one shell prime factor, each coordinate contributes at most one prime. But this still leaves a count over possible products \(N_i\), of size about \(X^2\), not just over translations.

Hence the unconditional baseline is polynomially larger than \(O(X)\).

## 6. Countermodel showing why translation counting fails

A recurrence-free model can be built as follows. Let the folded zero sets be arbitrary subsets \(Z_p\subset [0,(p-1)/2]\) satisfying:

- no two consecutive elements;
- reflection symmetry;
- the required height bound on the number of roots.

Choose one translation \(t_0\). For many primes choose

\[
u_p(t_0)\in Z_p.
\]

Independently choose coefficients satisfying

\[
N-d_i=c_ip_i.
\]

The resulting incidence model satisfies every frozen combinatorial condition but can have many prime triples over the same \(t_0\).

The missing input is precisely the Apéry recurrence/Frobenius structure controlling correlations between zero sets for different primes.

## 7. What would prove \(J_{dist}=o(X/\log^3X)\)

A sufficient exact gate would be a bound of the form

\[
\sum_t A_1(t)A_2(t)A_3(t)=o(X/\log^3X),
\]

where \(A_i\) counts admissible prime choices in coordinate \(i\).

Equivalently one needs a genuine three-coordinate incidence estimate.

Pairwise affine distinctness alone is insufficient.

## 8. Final audit

The correct framing is:

- count rows/prime triples, not translations;
- use SCRT as a coefficient uniqueness gate;
- do not claim fixed prime pairs determine \(t\);
- do not assume independent Apéry roots;
- identify the missing theorem as a third-order correlation bound for affine Apéry zero sets.

The observed fibre maximum one through \(X=4096\) is strong evidence, but it is not implied by the frozen algebraic constraints alone.
