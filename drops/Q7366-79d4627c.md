# Q7366 finite-difference attack on the interpolation numerator

## Verdict

**NO-GO for the claimed implication.** The finite-difference equation gives a genuine exact identity, but it does not by itself force \(z^3\nmid P_a(z)\pmod q\). The obstruction is that the quotient polynomial only records a small number of endpoint/Taylor constraints, while the condition \(z^3\mid P_a\) is a three-dimensional local condition at a point not appearing in the node recurrence.

## 1. Sign check of the transformed recurrence

Write
\[
P_a(u)=b_{a-u}(-1)^{a-u}(u-1)!(a-u)!.
\]
Substituting the Apéry recurrence
\[
j^3b_j=jA(j)b_{j-1}-j^3b_{j-1}
\]
(or equivalently the standard form
\(j^3b_j-(2j+1)(17j^2+17j+5)b_{j-1}+(j-1)^3b_{j-2}=0\))
with \(j=a-u\), and tracking the factorial/sign ratios gives
\[
(a-u)^4P_a(u+1)+uA(a-u)P_a(u)
+u(u-1)(a-u+1)^2P_a(u-1)=0.
\]
The signs in the stated difference operator are therefore consistent.

## 2. The exact quotient identity

Let
\[
Q_a(z)=\frac{\mathcal D_aP_a(z)}{\prod_{u=2}^a(z-u)}.
\]
The numerator has degree at most \(a+4\), while the divisor has degree \(a-1\), hence
\[
\deg Q_a\le5
\]
from degree counting alone. A further leading-term cancellation lowers this to degree at most four.

The quotient is determined by five values of the remainder data. The useful endpoint evaluations are obtained without division:

At \(z=a\),
\[
\mathcal D_aP(a)=aA(0)P(a)+a(a-1)P(a-1).
\]
Since
\[
A(0)=5,
\]
this becomes
\[
\mathcal D_aP(a)=5aP(a)+a(a-1)P(a-1).
\]
Using
\[
P(a)=b_0(-1)^0(a-1)!=(a-1)!,
\]
and
\[
P(a-1)=-b_1(a-2)!,
\]
with \(b_1=5\), gives
\[
\mathcal D_aP(a)=5a(a-1)!-5a(a-1)(a-2)!=0.
\]
Hence the quotient has a root at the endpoint corresponding to \(z=a\).

A symmetric calculation at the left endpoint gives a second linear constraint involving \(b_a,b_{a-1}\), but not enough constraints to force the first three Taylor coefficients at zero.

## 3. Why the quartic quotient does not prove the target

Suppose
\[
P_a(z)=z^3H(z)\pmod q.
\]
Then
\[
P_a(0)=P_a'(0)=P_a''(0)=0.
\]
Substituting into \(\mathcal D_aP_a\) only yields conditions on
\[
P_a(1),P_a(-1),
\]
and on the coefficients of the quotient \(Q_a\). The recurrence is valid only at the integer nodes \(2,\ldots,a\), so it does not propagate these three zero conditions at zero unless an additional boundary identity is supplied.

The quotient relation reduces the problem to a determinant involving the first Taylor coefficients of \(P_a\), but it does not make that determinant visibly nonzero modulo every prime \(q>a\).

## 4. Reduced determinant formulation

The exact remaining obstruction can be written as
\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
P_a(0)&P_a'(0)&P_a''(0)\\
\mathcal D_aP_a(0)&(\mathcal D_aP_a)'(0)&(\mathcal D_aP_a)''(0)\\
Q_a(0)&Q_a'(0)&Q_a''(0)
\end{pmatrix}
\neq0\pmod q.
\]
The difference equation gives the last row through endpoint interpolation, but no closed evaluation of this determinant in terms of only \(a,b_a,b_{a-1}\) is obtained.

## Conclusion

The finite-difference attack succeeds in producing an exact quartic quotient and endpoint cancellations, but it does **not** prove \(z^3\nmid P_a(z)\pmod q\). The sharp reduction is the above local determinant/nonvanishing problem. A proof would require a new identity controlling the Taylor jet of \(P_a\) at zero, not merely the translated Apéry recurrence.

Status: **NO-GO (current identity insufficient), with a concrete exact determinant reduction.**
