## 2. Coefficient-by-coefficient derivation and the \(m=0\) boundary

Define

\[
D_a=(\theta+a)^3-zP(\theta+a)+z^2(\theta+a+1)^3.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For \(n\ge0\),

\[
[z^n](\theta+a)^3F_a=(a+n)^3V_n(a).
\]

Also,

\[
[z^n]\,zP(\theta+a)F_a
 =
 \begin{cases}
 0,&n=0,\\
 P(a+n-1)V_{n-1}(a),&n\ge1,
 \end{cases}
\]

and

\[
[z^n]\,z^2(\theta+a+1)^3F_a
 =
 \begin{cases}
 0,&n=0,1,\\
 (a+n-1)^3V_{n-2}(a),&n\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]

Therefore

\[
[z^0]D_aF_a=a^3V_0(a)=a^3.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For every \(n\ge1\), using \(V_{-1}=0\) when \(n=1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
[z^n]D_aF_a
&=(a+n)^3V_n(a)
  -P(a+n-1)V_{n-1}(a)\\
&\qquad +(a+n-1)^3V_{n-2}(a)\\
&=0
\end{aligned}
\]

by (1.1) with \(m=n-1\). Hence

\[
\boxed{D_aF_a=a^3.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The same boundary term is visible directly after summation. Multiplying (1.1) by \(z^{m+1}\) and summing over \(m\ge0\) gives

\[
\sum_{m\ge0}(a+m+1)^3V_{m+1}z^{m+1}
=(\theta+a)^3F_a-a^3V_0,
\]

while the two right-hand sums are

\[
zP(\theta+a)F_a
\quad\text{and}\quad
-z^2(\theta+a+1)^3F_a.
\]

Since \(V_0=1\), this again yields (2.3). The missing term in Q7231 is precisely the omitted \(m=0\) boundary contribution.

Equivalently, the sequence identity is

\[
[z^n]D_aF_a=a^3\mathbf 1_{n=0}.
\]

Thus \(F_a\) is naturally a half-line Green/kernel solution with a point source at the initial boundary, not an eigenfunction.

## 3. Exact failure of Q7231 equations (6.2) and (6.3)

Q7231 defines, in effect,

\[
\mathcal L_a^{\mathrm{old}}=D_a-a^3,
\]

where the last \(a^3\) is a zeroth-order scalar multiplication operator, and then claims \(\mathcal L_a^{\mathrm{old}}F_a=0\). In fact,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_a^{\mathrm{old}}F_a
 =D_aF_a-a^3F_a
 =a^3(1-F_a).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is generally nonzero. The error is hidden at \(a=0\), where the forcing happens to vanish and \(F_0\) really does satisfy the usual homogeneous Apéry equation.

Let

\[
D_0=\theta^3-zP(\theta)+z^2(\theta+1)^3.
\]

For fixed integral \(a\), or formally in the corresponding Kummer gauge,

\[
z^{-a}D_0z^a=D_a.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently, for \(Y_a=z^aF_a\),

\[
\boxed{
D_0Y_a=z^aD_aF_a=a^3z^a.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The right side is not \(a^3Y_a=a^3z^aF_a\). Thus the statements

\[
D_0Y_a=a^3Y_a
\]

and “\(Y_a\) is an eigenfunction of \(D_0\) with eigenvalue \(a^3\)” must both be deleted.

For a parameter \(a\), the notation \(z^a\) should moreover be understood as the rank-one Kummer equation \((\theta-a)S=0\), not as a rational algebraic function on an \(a\)-line. Literal \(z^a\) is harmless for each fixed integer \(a\), but it is not one rational function of the pair \((a,z)\).

## 4. Canonical homogeneous augmentation and the order-four equation

### 4.1 Scalar homogenization

Since \(D_aF_a=a^3\) is constant in \(z\),

\[
\boxed{\theta\circ D_a\,F_a=0.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is a homogeneous scalar differential equation of order four. It must be accompanied by the source normalization

\[
D_aF_a\equiv a^3;
\]

otherwise (4.1) allows \(D_aF\) to be an arbitrary constant.

In the \(Y_a=z^aF_a\) gauge, (3.3) gives

\[
(\theta-a)(D_0Y_a)=0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
H_aY_a=0,
\qquad
H_a:=(\theta-a)\circ D_0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the clean corrected replacement for the false eigenvalue equation.

The two scalar order-four formulations are conjugate:

\[
\theta\circ D_a
 =z^{-a}\bigl((\theta-a)\circ D_0\bigr)z^a.
\]

### 4.2 Explicit rank-four homogeneous system in the \(F_a\) gauge

Put

\[
\delta=\theta+a,
\qquad
q(z)=1-34z+z^2,
\]

and

\[
r_2(z)=3z^2-51z,
\qquad
r_1(z)=3z^2-27z,
\qquad
r_0(z)=z^2-5z.
\]

Expanding \(P\) gives

\[
D_a=q\delta^3+r_2\delta^2+r_1\delta+r_0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Adjoin the source coordinate

\[
S=D_aF.
\]

For the desired solution \(S\equiv a^3\), hence \(	heta S=0\). With

\[
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
F\\
\delta F\\
\delta^2F\\
S
\end{pmatrix},
\]

one obtains the homogeneous system

\[
\boxed{
\theta X=A_a(z)X
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

with

\[
A_a(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-a&1&0&0\\
0&-a&1&0\\
-\dfrac{r_0}{q}&-\dfrac{r_1}{q}&-a-\dfrac{r_2}{q}&\dfrac1q\\
0&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The formal branch defined by the recurrence has

\[
X(0)=(1,a,a^2,a^3)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Thus one extra constant-source coordinate turns the third-order affine equation into a rank-four homogeneous connection.

### 4.3 Equivalent Apéry-plus-Kummer system

Let

\[
Y=z^aF,
\qquad
S=D_0Y.
\]

Then \(S=a^3z^a\) and \(	heta S=aS\). With

\[
Z=
\begin{pmatrix}
Y\\
\theta Y\\
\theta^2Y\\
S
\end{pmatrix},
\]

one gets

\[
\boxed{
\theta Z=B_a(z)Z
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

where

\[
B_a(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&0\\
-\dfrac{r_0}{q}&-\dfrac{r_1}{q}&-\dfrac{r_2}{q}&\dfrac1q\\
0&0&0&a
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

This identifies the corrected homogeneous object precisely: it is a rank-four extension of the rank-three Apéry Picard--Fuchs module by the rank-one Kummer module

\[
(\theta-a)S=0.
\]

It is not a rank-three eigenfamily.

### 4.4 What “minimal” can and cannot mean

The rank-four construction is the minimal **canonical augmentation by the source module**: the nonzero forcing term spans a one-dimensional differential module, so retaining it in a homogeneous first-order system adds one state to the rank-three companion system.

One should not silently strengthen this to the claim that \(	heta D_a\) or \((\theta-a)D_0\) is the minimal Ore annihilator of the single series \(F_a\) or \(Y_a\). Ruling out every unrelated homogeneous operator of order at most three would require a separate irreducibility/non-splitting argument not supplied in Q7231 or in the cited section. At \(a=0\), the order certainly drops: \(D_0F_0=0\) already has order three.

## 5. Singular divisor of the corrected rank-four system

In ordinary derivative form, (4.6) is

\[
\frac{dZ}{dz}=\frac1zB_a(z)Z.
\]

The only finite denominators are \(z\) and

\[
q(z)=z^2-34z+1.
\]

The roots of \(q\) are

\[
17-12\sqrt2,
\qquad
17+12\sqrt2.
\]

Moreover, \(B_a(z)\) tends to a finite matrix as \(z\to\infty\), so infinity is also a logarithmic singular point after the change \(w=1/z\). Therefore the natural pole/singular divisor in the \(z\)-line is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_z=\{0,\ 17-12\sqrt2,\ 17+12\sqrt2,\ \infty\},
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

or, over \(\mathbb Q\), the divisor

\[
z(z^2-34z+1)=0
\quad\text{together with }z=\infty.
\]

Applying the extra first-order factor \(	heta-a\), or adjoining the source coordinate, introduces no new \(z\)-singularity: the Kummer factor is singular only at \(0\) and \(\infty\), which were already present.

For the relative \(z\)-connection, there are no finite poles in the parameter \(a\); the matrix depends polynomially on \(a\). If one compactifies the parameter line, \(a=\infty\) is of course a parameter-direction pole. No absolute flat connection in both \(a\) and \(z\) has been constructed here; the rigorous statement is a bounded-complexity **relative** de Rham family in the \(z\)-direction.

## 6. Precisely what bounded-complexity statement survives

The following statement is rigorous.

> **Corrected bounded-complexity differential statement.**  The shifted normalized continuants \(V_m(a)\) are the Taylor coefficients of a distinguished solution of a third-order inhomogeneous equation \(D_aF=a^3\). Equivalently, after adjoining one source coordinate, they are encoded by a rank-four relative logarithmic connection whose matrix entries have bounded degree, whose parameter dependence has bounded degree, and whose \(z\)-singular divisor is the fixed four-point divisor (5.1), independent of \(a\) and \(m\).

In the \(Y\)-gauge (4.7), the rank is \(4\), the numerators have degree at most \(2\) in \(z\), the only denominator is \(zq(z)\), and \(a\) enters linearly in the connection matrix. The source normalization contributes the scalar \(a^3\). This is genuine \(O(1)\) relative de Rham complexity.

What does **not** survive is equally important:

1. \(F_a\) is not a horizontal section of a rank-three homogeneous system \(D_aF=0\).
2. \(z^aF_a\) is not an eigenfunction of \(D_0\).
3. The parameter \(m\) remains a Taylor-coefficient index, not an algebraic coordinate.
4. Extracting \(V_m(a)\) is still an \(m\)-th jet/coefficient operation and can reintroduce complexity growing with \(m\).
5. The distinguished Taylor coefficients have possible resonant denominators at \(a=-1,-2,\ldots,-m\), even though the connection matrix itself has no growing finite \(a\)-divisor.
6. No \(\ell\)-adic sheaf, Frobenius-trace formula, vertical divisibility estimate, Lang--Weil estimate, or far-bridge bound follows from the de Rham system alone.

Thus the correction preserves the useful “fixed local differential complexity” observation, but only after replacing “rank-three spectral family” by “rank-three forced equation / rank-four homogeneous extension.”

## 7. Audit of the two-point Casoratian identity

The companion solutions in `FABLE_SECTION_orbit_energy.tex` satisfy

\[
(n+1)^3y_{n+1}=P(n)y_n-n^3y_{n-1},
\]

with

\[
b_0=1,\quad b_1=5,
\qquad
c_0=0,\quad c_1=1,
\]

and the exact Casoratian

\[
W_n:=b_nc_{n-1}-b_{n-1}c_n=-\frac1{n^3}
\qquad(n\ge1).
\tag{7.1}
\]

For an integer \(a\ge1\), define

\[
d_n=b_{a-1}c_n-c_{a-1}b_n.
\]

As a fixed linear combination of \(b_n,c_n\), the sequence \(d_n\) satisfies the same Apéry recurrence in \(n\). Its two initial values at the restart point are

\[
d_{a-1}=0
\]

and, by (7.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_a
&=b_{a-1}c_a-c_{a-1}b_a\\
&=-W_a\\
&=\frac1{a^3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Set

\[
K_m(a)=a^3d_{a+m}
 =a^3\bigl(b_{a-1}c_{a+m}-c_{a-1}b_{a+m}\bigr).
\]

Then

\[
K_{-1}(a)=0,\qquad K_0(a)=1,
\]

and the recurrence at \(n=a+m\) gives

\[
(a+m+1)^3K_{m+1}(a)
=P(a+m)K_m(a)-(a+m)^3K_{m-1}(a).
\]

These are exactly the recurrence and initial data defining \(V_m(a)\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
V_m(a)
 =a^3\bigl(b_{a-1}c_{a+m}-c_{a-1}b_{a+m}\bigr)
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

for every integer \(a\ge1\) and \(m\ge-1\) for which the displayed sequences are defined.

Therefore:

- the factor is exactly \(a^3\), not \((a+1)^3\) or \((a+m)^3\);
- the sign in Q7231 is correct;
- the endpoints \(a-1\) and \(a+m\) are correct;
- the identity is a discrete integer-index identity, not an algebraic identity for nonintegral \(a\).

The terminology “Casoratian kernel” is exact. “Green kernel” is also reasonable if it is understood as the solution normalized by a zero at \(a-1\) and value \(1\) at \(a\); no additional analytic Green-function theorem is being asserted.

### The \(a=0\) specialization is separate

Formula (7.2) cannot be specialized to \(a=0\): it would require \(b_{-1},c_{-1}\), and the normalization \(a^3\) degenerates. Nevertheless the separate statement

\[
V_m(0)=b_m
\]

is correct. Indeed,

\[
U_m(0)=(m!)^3b_m
\]

has the same initial values and the same \(U\)-recurrence, and division by \((1)_m^3=(m!)^3\) gives \(V_m(0)=b_m\). This fact must not be presented as a consequence of (7.2).

## 8. Exact \(N_G\) identity, unit conditions, and carrier/phantom audit

The FABLE restart dictionary is

\[
N_h(r)=U_{h-1}(r+1).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Take

\[
a=u+1,\qquad m=G-1.
\]

Then (7.2) becomes

\[
V_{G-1}(u+1)
=(u+1)^3\bigl(b_uc_{u+G}-c_ub_{u+G}\bigr).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since

\[
U_{G-1}(u+1)
=\left(\prod_{j=2}^{G}(u+j)\right)^3V_{G-1}(u+1),
\]

(8.1) and (8.2) give the exact lattice identity

\[
\boxed{
N_G(u)
=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{G}(u+j)\right)^3
 \bigl(b_uc_{u+G}-c_ub_{u+G}\bigr).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This also checks all shifts: \(N_G\) corresponds to \(m=G-1\), starts at \(a=u+1\), and compares the orbit points at \(u\) and \(u+G\).

For reduction modulo a prime \(p\), the projective/Casoratian interpretation requires the regular-window condition

\[
\prod_{j=1}^{G}(u+j)\ne0\pmod p.
\tag{8.4}
\]

The nonwrapping range

\[
0\le u<u+G<p
\]

implies (8.4), so there

\[
N_G(u)=0
\iff
b_uc_{u+G}-c_ub_{u+G}=0
\iff
\pi(u)=\pi(u+G).
\]

Outside the regular window, (8.3) must not be read as saying that the Pochhammer product is a genuine polynomial factor of \(N_G(u)\). The determinant on the right is a rational/lattice expression with compensating denominators, and the projective recurrence itself is singular when one of the factors \(u+j\) vanishes modulo \(p\). Thus:

- the product in (8.3) is a denominator-clearing carrier;
- it does not create legitimate “automatic roots” of the polynomial \(N_G\);
- singular-window cases must be excluded or treated by the polynomial recurrence directly, not counted as projective collisions.

This is the exact carrier/phantom audit needed for the Casoratian formula.

# Final conclusion

The \(m=0\) boundary changes the logical nature of the construction. The exact object is

\[
D_aF_a=a^3,
\]

a forced third-order equation. Its natural homogeneous completion is the rank-four Apéry/Kummer extension

\[
(\theta-a)D_0(z^aF_a)=0,
\]

with the same fixed four-point singular divisor. This preserves a bounded-complexity **relative differential** encoding, but destroys the claimed eigenfunction/spectral interpretation and supplies no new control of the growing coefficient index \(G\).

The displayed two-point Casoratian formula is correct, including its \(a^3\) normalization, sign, and endpoints. Its valid use requires \(a\ge1\) and a regular nonwrapping denominator window; \(a=0\) is a separate specialization, and the denominator-clearing product must not be treated as an automatic polynomial factor.
