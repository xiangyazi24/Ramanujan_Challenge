# Q1961 targeted literature search

I could not verify a published theorem that directly evaluates the adjacent half-index quantity
\(A_{(p-3)/2}\) under the hypothesis \(A_{(p-3)/2}=0\) and \(A_{(p-1)/2}\ne0\) in a way that implies the required reciprocal trace nonvanishing.

The relevant exact distinction is:

- The classical modular-form congruences concern \(A_{(p-1)/2}=A_m\), not \(A_{m-1}\). For ordinary Apéry numbers, the half-index value \(A_m\pmod p\) is connected in many results to weight-4 modular forms (for example the eta-product/newform descriptions appearing in work of Ahlgren, Ono, and later authors on Apéry-like supercongruences).
- Those results do not identify the adjacent value \(A_{m-1}=A_{(p-3)/2}\).

A finite-field hypergeometric expression exists for Apéry numbers after rewriting the binomial sum as a truncated hypergeometric value, and Greene/McCarthy style finite-field hypergeometric transformations give identities for special values. However, I do not find a verified contiguous relation in the literature whose specialization at the adjacent half-index gives the reciprocal sum

\[
\frac13-2A_m\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{(m-j)^2-1}{A_j}.
\]

The missing implication is therefore precise:

1. Existing results control the value \(A_m\), often through modular forms.
2. The gap condition controls \(A_{m-1}\).
3. No located theorem turns the single vanishing condition \(A_{m-1}=0\) plus the unit condition on earlier Apéry values into a derivative formula forcing the above trace to be nonzero.

The closest missing lemma would be an exact adjacent-index derivative/contiguous identity of the form

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{(m-j)^2-1}{A_j}=F(A_m,A_{m-2},D_{m-1})
\]

with a nonzero right-hand side after using \(A_m=-27A_{m-2}\) and \(D_{m-1}=-3A_m\). No such identity has been verified here.

Consequently the currently justified conclusion is only:

- the known modular-form interpretation of \(A_m\) does not apply to the gap-one index;
- the condition \(A_{m-1}=0\), by itself, does not provide a published simple-zero or reciprocal-trace nonvanishing theorem;
- the missing step is an adjacent-half-index finite-field hypergeometric derivative identity, not a generic modular-form argument.
