ANSWER Q7458 a0a35761

# Growing-window attack on the full-Newton Apéry residual gcd

## Executive conclusion

Let

\[
P(k)=(2k-1)(17k^2-17k+5)=34k^3-51k^2+27k-5
\]

and

\[
R_s=(n+s)^3A_s-P(n+s)A_{s-1}+(n+s-1)^3A_{s-2},
\qquad
c_S=\gcd(A_0,R_{-S},\ldots,R_S).
\]

I do **not** see a proof of \(\log c_S=o(n)\) from the currently proved recurrence/Newton facts in the repository.  There is a precise reason: modulo every prime power, the homogeneous recurrence transports a free one-dimensional scale through every nonsingular portion of the window, and a singular row \(n+s\equiv0\pmod p\) loses a coupling rather than creating one.  Newton primitivity does not remove that scale.  In fact the obstruction can be made completely integral and Apéry-shaped: for any integer \(M\),

\[
A_s=M b_{n+s}
\]

(where \(b_j\) is the Apéry sequence) has \(R_s=0\) identically, hence

\[
c_S=|M b_n|.
\]

Because the full consecutive Newton weights sum to \(1\), the same values can even be represented by a primitive Newton stencil by taking artificial stencil data constant and equal to \(M b_{n+s}\).  Thus **recurrence propagation + the fact that the Newton row is primitive cannot bound either the support or the exponent of \(c_S\), in any of the ranges \(p\le S\), \(S<p\le\sqrt n\), or \(p>\sqrt n\)**.  What is missing is exactly a statement about the *actual Laurent-shell packet* on which the Newton row acts.

There is, however, a clean growing-window theorem that would close the argument and that is substantially weaker than a cyclotomic annihilator.  The right object is an **integral primitive-window observability matrix**.  If the actual generator vector

\[
G_S=(A_0,R_{-S},\ldots,R_S)^T\in\mathbb Z^{2S+2}
\]

admits an exact factorization

\[
G_S=M_{n,q,S}X_{n,q,S},
\qquad
M_{n,q,S}\in\mathbb Z^{(2S+2)\times r_S},
\qquad r_S\le 2S+2,
\]

with full column rank, and if

\[
h_S=\gcd(X_{n,q,S,1},\ldots,X_{n,q,S,r_S}),
\qquad
D_S=\gcd_{|I|=r_S}\left|\det (M_{n,q,S})_I\right|,
\]

then a direct adjugate argument gives the exact valuation inequality

\[
\boxed{v_p(c_S)\le v_p(h_S)+v_p(D_S)\quad\text{for every prime }p,}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{c_S\mid h_SD_S.}
\]

Therefore the single quantitative estimate

\[
\boxed{\log h_S+\log D_S=O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2))}
\tag{PW}
\]

would suffice.  Taking, for example,

\[
S(n)=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor
\]

gives

\[
\log c_S=O_q(n^{2/3}\log n)=o(n),
\qquad
\log B_S=O(S^2)=O(n^{2/3})=o(n).
\]

This is the weakest useful growing-window target I can isolate from the current architecture: one need not annihilate the \(A_s\) sequence and one need not make any individual shell value small; one only needs a low-rank **common packet** with controlled primitive content and controlled common maximal-minor content.

A still more concrete sufficient form, suggested by the current terminal observability scripts, is

\[
D_S\mid K_{q,S}\prod_{|j|\le C_qS+C_q}(n+j)^{m_{j,S}},
\qquad
\sum_jm_{j,S}=O_q(S^2),
\tag{B}
\]

with

\[
\log K_{q,S}=O_q(S^2\log(S+2)),
\qquad
\log h_S=O_q(S^2\log(S+2)).
\tag{C}
\]

Then

\[
\log c_S\le O_q(S^2\log(S+2))+O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2)),
\]

so again \(S=n^{1/3}\) closes the target-cover argument.  This formulation also controls **prime powers**, not merely radicals.

The current repository is close to this shape but does not yet prove it.  `q32_terminal_family_audit.py` proves exact terminal Newton/difference identities.  `q32_newton_gcd_audit.py` proves the formal local Smith behavior of Newton stencil rectangles.  `q32_lower_terminal_observability.sage` explicitly says that its Freshman-reduced terminal observability calculation remains diagnostic until the unique-decomposition reduction is proved.  `q32_terminal_directional_cofactor_audit.py` distinguishes the proved/computational radical implication from the still-conjectural short-factor divisibility.  The missing step for Q7458 is therefore not another scan: it is a valuation-level, integral, actual-shell version of that observability factorization.

The rest of this note gives the exact propagation formulas, the three prime ranges, the no-go, and the sufficient lemma with its quantitative bookkeeping.

## 1. What \(p^e\mid c_S\) means

Fix a prime \(p\) and put

\[
e=e_p:=v_p(c_S).
\]

Then, by definition,

\[
A_0\equiv0\pmod{p^e},
\qquad
R_s\equiv0\pmod{p^e}\quad(-S\le s\le S).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Write

\[
a_s=(n+s)^3,\qquad b_s=P(n+s),\qquad d_s=(n+s-1)^3.
\]

Thus

\[
a_sA_s-b_sA_{s-1}+d_sA_{s-2}\equiv0\pmod{p^e}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Whenever \(p\nmid n+s\), (1.2) can be solved forward:

\[
\binom{A_s}{A_{s-1}}
\equiv
T_s\binom{A_{s-1}}{A_{s-2}}\pmod{p^e},
\tag{1.3}
\]

where

\[
T_s=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{P(n+s)}{(n+s)^3}&-\dfrac{(n+s-1)^3}{(n+s)^3}\\[4pt]
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\in M_2(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z).
\tag{1.4}
\]

The determinant is

\[
\det T_s=\frac{(n+s-1)^3}{(n+s)^3}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Consequently, if all integers

\[
n+a-1,n+a,\ldots,n+b
\]

are prime to \(p\), then

\[
\det(T_bT_{b-1}\cdots T_a)
=\prod_{s=a}^b\frac{(n+s-1)^3}{(n+s)^3}
=\boxed{\left(\frac{n+a-1}{n+b}\right)^3}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

So propagation across a nonsingular interval is invertible modulo **every power** \(p^e\); there is no loss depending on \(e\).

This telescoping determinant is the exact local fact one would want from recurrence propagation.  It is also exactly why propagation alone cannot bound \(e\): it preserves a free scale.

### 1.1 The regular window has a free scale

Assume for the moment that

\[
p\nmid\prod_{j=-S-1}^{S}(n+j).
\tag{1.7}
\]

Then all transfer matrices needed across the window are invertible.  The central equation \(R_1\equiv0\), together with \(A_0\equiv0\), gives

\[
(n+1)^3A_1+n^3A_{-1}\equiv0\pmod{p^e}.
\tag{1.8}
\]

Thus, because \(n(n+1)\) is a unit, \(A_1\) and \(A_{-1}\) are proportional.  Equations (1.3) then determine all \(A_s\) in \([-S-2,S]\) from one free scalar, say \(A_{-1}\).  Symbolically there are coefficients \(u_s\in\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\), depending only on \(n,p,e\), with

\[
A_s\equiv u_s A_{-1}\pmod{p^e}.
\tag{1.9}
\]

Nothing in (1.1)--(1.9) bounds \(v_p(A_{-1})\), nor does it force the surviving line to be zero.  Increasing \(S\) only adds more equations that the same homogeneous solution already satisfies.

### 1.2 A singular row loses information

Suppose \(n+t\equiv0\pmod p\).  At the singular row \(s=t\),

\[
(n+t)^3A_t-P(n+t)A_{t-1}+(n+t-1)^3A_{t-2}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Since

\[
P(0)=-5,
\]

for \(p\ne5\) this becomes

\[
5A_{t-1}-A_{t-2}\equiv0\pmod p,
\tag{1.10}
\]

and does **not** constrain \(A_t\).  The next row has the coefficient of \(A_{t-1}\) equal to \((n+t)^3\equiv0\), so it starts propagating the newly free \(A_t\) to the right.  Thus a singularity disconnects the two regular transfer chains; it does not create an extra condition on their common scale.  At \(p=5\), the middle coefficient is singular as well and the degeneration is even worse.

For \(p>S\), an interval of length \(2S+1\) contains at most two integers congruent to \(-n\pmod p\).  For \(p>2S+1\), it contains at most one.  Hence the medium- and large-prime regimes have at most a bounded number of singular breaks.  But boundedly many breaks are not enough: each break can add a free local scale.

## 2. Prime-range analysis from the recurrence alone

Write

\[
\log c_S=\sum_p e_p\log p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The requested three ranges behave as follows **before** using actual shell structure.

### 2.1 Small primes: \(p\le S\)

There may be several singular rows \(n+s\equiv0\p\) inside \([-S,S]\), and coefficients can carry nontrivial \(p\)-adic valuation.  Recurrence propagation therefore has both denominator loss and rank breaks.  No uniform bound on \(e_p\) follows.

Even if one somehow proves a support statement saying only primes \(p\le S\) survive, that is not enough.  One would still need

\[
\sum_{p\le S}e_p\log p=o(n).
\tag{2.2}
\]

For example, the trivial bound \(e_p\le \log|A_0|/\log p\) is useless if \(\log|A_0|=\Theta(n)\): a single small prime can carry linear height.

A growing-window proof therefore needs a genuine **valuation** statement at small primes, not a radical statement.

### 2.2 Medium primes: \(S<p\le\sqrt n\)

Here the recurrence is much cleaner geometrically: there are at most two singular rows, and outside them (1.6) gives exact invertible transport modulo \(p^e\).  But the regular pieces still carry free scales.  Hence there is still no recurrence-only bound on \(e_p\).

What would be enough quantitatively is quite weak.  Since

\[
\sum_{p\le x}\log p=O(x),
\]

any uniform estimate

\[
e_p=O_q(S+1)
\quad(S<p\le\sqrt n)
\tag{2.3}
\]

would give

\[
\sum_{S<p\le\sqrt n}e_p\log p
=O_q(S\sqrt n)=o(n)
\tag{2.4}
\]

whenever \(S=o(\sqrt n)\).  Thus the medium range does **not** require exponent \(1\); an \(O(S)\) valuation bound would already suffice.  The issue is that the recurrence supplies no such bound at all.

### 2.3 Large primes: \(p>\sqrt n\)

This is the most revealing range.  For \(p>n+S+1\), every recurrence coefficient \(n+j\) in the window is a unit.  Hence the entire window is regular and (1.6) is invertible.  Nevertheless the free scale in (1.9) remains.

Therefore even arbitrarily large primes cannot be ruled out by recurrence propagation.  In particular, there is no theorem of the form

\[
p>F(n,S)\Longrightarrow p\nmid c_S
\]

that follows from the scalar recurrence and Newton primitivity alone.

If an actual-shell certificate reduces the large-prime divisors to a short product of linear factors in \(n\), then the large-prime exponents become easy: for

\[
p>\sqrt{n+C S+C},
\]

one has

\[
v_p(n+j)\le1\qquad(|j|\le CS+C),
\tag{2.5}
\]

because \(p^2>n+CS+C\).  But (2.5) is useful only *after* a shell-specific determinant has shown that those are the only possible sources.

## 3. Sharp no-go: recurrence + Newton primitivity cannot control common scaling

The obstruction can be stated without any rational-realizability argument.

Let \(b_m\) be the Apéry numbers.  They satisfy

\[
m^3b_m-P(m)b_{m-1}+(m-1)^3b_{m-2}=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Assume \(n>S+2\), so all indices below are nonnegative.  For any nonzero integer \(M\), define

\[
\widetilde A_s=M b_{n+s}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then for every \(s\),

\[
\widetilde R_s
=(n+s)^3\widetilde A_s
-P(n+s)\widetilde A_{s-1}
+(n+s-1)^3\widetilde A_{s-2}=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\gcd(\widetilde A_0,\widetilde R_{-S},\ldots,\widetilde R_S)
=|M b_n|.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Choose any prime \(p\) and put \(M=p^E\).  Then

\[
v_p(c_S)\ge E,
\tag{3.5}
\]

with \(E\) arbitrary.  For each fixed \(n\), one may choose \(p\nmid b_n\) in any sufficiently large prime range, so equality in (3.5) can be made \(E\) at that prime.

This already kills every hoped-for recurrence-only valuation estimate.

### 3.1 Why Newton primitivity does not repair the countermodel

For consecutive nonnegative nodes \(d,d+1,\ldots,d+L\), the repository uses the integer full-Newton weights

\[
w_i=(-1)^i\binom{d+i}{i}\binom{d+L+1}{L-i}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

They are the Lagrange weights for evaluation at \(-1\), so

\[
\sum_{i=0}^L w_i=1.
\tag{3.7}
\]

In particular the row is primitive.  But (3.7) also shows directly that **every integer constant stencil** is fixed by the selector.  If, for each \(s\), artificial raw stencil values are chosen as

\[
Y_{s,d+i}=M b_{n+s}\qquad(0\le i\le L),
\tag{3.8}
\]

then

\[
\sum_{i=0}^Lw_iY_{s,d+i}=M b_{n+s}=\widetilde A_s.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Thus the combination

* scalar Apéry recurrence,
* integrality of the stencil,
* and primitivity/content-one of the Newton row

still admits arbitrary common scaling.  The property violated by (3.8) is exactly the one that has not yet been put into the gcd argument: the entries must be the **actual coupled Laurent-shell values** \(C_{n-q+s}(d+i)\), not arbitrary integers.

This is the sharply scoped obstruction requested in the question.

## 4. The exact primitive-window determinant lemma

Here is the cleanest shell-specific interface that would suffice.

### Lemma PW (integral primitive-window observability)

For fixed \(q\), suppose that for every \(n,S\) in the desired range there exist

* an integer \(r=r_{n,q,S}\le2S+2\),
* a vector \(X=X_{n,q,S}\in\mathbb Z^r\),
* an integer matrix
  \[
  M=M_{n,q,S}\in\mathbb Z^{(2S+2)\times r}
  \]
  of rank \(r\) over \(\mathbb Q\),

such that, in the row order \(A_0,R_{-S},\ldots,R_S\),

\[
G_S=MX.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Put

\[
h=\gcd(X_1,\ldots,X_r)
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
D=\gcd_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,2S+2\},\ |I|=r}
|\det M_I|,
\tag{4.3}
\]

where zero minors may be omitted and \(D>0\) because \(M\) has rank \(r\).

Then

\[
\boxed{c_S\mid hD.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equivalently, for every prime \(p\),

\[
\boxed{v_p(c_S)\le v_p(h)+v_p(D).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
X=hX^{\rm prim},
\qquad
\gcd(X^{\rm prim}_1,\ldots,X^{\rm prim}_r)=1.
\]

Then

\[
G_S=hM X^{\rm prim},
\]

so

\[
c_S=h c'_S,
\qquad
c'_S=\gcd(MX^{\rm prim}).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Fix a row set \(I\) of cardinality \(r\).  Since every selected component of \(MX^{\rm prim}\) is divisible by \(c'_S\), the adjugate identity gives

\[
\operatorname{adj}(M_I)(M_I X^{\rm prim})
=(\det M_I)X^{\rm prim}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Therefore \(c'_S\) divides every integer

\[
(\det M_I)X^{\rm prim}_j.
\]

Taking the gcd over \(j\), and using primitivity of \(X^{\rm prim}\), yields

\[
c'_S\mid\det M_I.
\tag{4.8}
\]

This holds for every full-rank minor, hence

\[
c'_S\mid D.
\]

Multiplying by \(h\) proves (4.4).  Prime-by-prime, if \(e=v_p(c_S)\) and \(t=v_p(h)\), then some coordinate of \(X^{\rm prim}\) is a \(p\)-adic unit, so (4.7) gives

\[
e-t\le v_p(\det M_I)
\]

for every \(I\), and therefore (4.5).

No Smith normal form is being invoked here; (4.7) is the entire argument.

### Why this is the right interface

The shell values themselves may have exponential height.  Lemma PW does not ask them to be small.  It asks only that the **common local packet** through which all \(2S+2\) generators factor have controlled content, and that the common observability rows have a small determinantal divisor.

That is exactly the information recurrence propagation lacks: it kills the free common scale.

## 5. Quantitative form sufficient for \(S=n^{1/3}\)

The following bound is more than enough:

\[
\boxed{
\log h_{n,q,S}+\log D_{n,q,S}
\le C_q S^2\log(n+S+2).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then by (4.4),

\[
\log c_S\le C_qS^2\log(n+S+2).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Take

\[
S=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
S=o(\sqrt n)
\]

and

\[
\log c_S=O_q(n^{2/3}\log n)=o(n).
\tag{5.4}
\]

The assumed target cover gives

\[
\operatorname{Rad}_{\rm target}(n,q)\mid B_Sc_S,
\]

with

\[
\log B_S=O(S^2)=O(n^{2/3})=o(n).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\log(B_Sc_S)=o(n).
\tag{5.6}
\]

So (5.1) is a complete growing-window closure.

More generally, if one proves

\[
\log h_S+\log D_S
=O_q(S^a(\log n)^b),
\tag{5.7}
\]

then any power choice \(S=n^\delta\) with

\[
0<\delta<\min\left(\frac12,\frac1a\right)
\]

(and the obvious logarithmic slack when \(a\delta=1\)) closes the argument.  Thus the local determinant may grow polynomially in \(S\); it need not be fixed-size.

## 6. A more concrete Bézout/short-boundary version

The current terminal observability computations strongly suggest a still more structured target.  Suppose one can prove the exact divisibility

\[
D_{n,q,S}\mid K_{q,S}Q_{n,q,S},
\tag{6.1}
\]

where

\[
Q_{n,q,S}=\prod_{j\in J_{q,S}}(n+j)^{m_{j,S}},
\tag{6.2}
\]

and

\[
J_{q,S}\subset[-C_qS-C_q,C_qS+C_q],
\qquad
\sum_{j\in J_{q,S}}m_{j,S}\le C_qS^2,
\tag{6.3}
\]

while

\[
\log K_{q,S}=O_q(S^2\log(S+2)),
\qquad
\log h_S=O_q(S^2\log(S+2)).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log c_S
&\le \log h_S+\log K_{q,S}
  +\sum_jm_{j,S}\log|n+j|\\
&=O_q(S^2\log(S+2))+O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Again (5.3) gives \(o(n)\).

This form is especially attractive because a polynomial Bézout identity can prove (6.1) without estimating the enormous actual shell values.  Concretely, after removing known boundary factors from a finite family of normalized minors \(F_{\nu,S}(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]\), it would be enough to prove

\[
\gcd_{\mathbb Q[x]}(F_{1,S},\ldots,F_{m,S})=1.
\tag{6.6}
\]

An integer clearing of a polynomial Bézout identity then gives

\[
U_{1,S}(x)F_{1,S}(x)+\cdots+U_{m,S}(x)F_{m,S}(x)=K_{q,S},
\tag{6.7}
\]

for some nonzero integer \(K_{q,S}\).  Evaluating at \(x=n\) shows that the common integer divisor of the normalized minors divides \(K_{q,S}\).  Restoring the explicit boundary factors gives (6.1).

The only quantitative requirement is that the denominator/Bezout constant obey (6.4), or more generally

\[
\log K_{q,S}=o(n)
\]

for the chosen \(S(n)\).

## 7. Prime-power bookkeeping under the sufficient lemma

Assume the concrete form (6.1)--(6.4).  Let

\[
e_p=v_p(c_S),\qquad
\eta_p=v_p(h_SK_{q,S}).
\]

From (4.5) and (6.1),

\[
\boxed{
e_p\le \eta_p+
\sum_{j\in J_{q,S}}m_{j,S}v_p(n+j).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is the valuation-level statement that a support-only theorem lacks.

### 7.1 \(p\le S\)

Summing (7.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{p\le S}e_p\log p
&\le \log(h_SK_{q,S})
 +\sum_jm_{j,S}
   \sum_{p\le S}v_p(n+j)\log p\\
&\le \log(h_SK_{q,S})
 +\sum_jm_{j,S}\log|n+j|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le S}e_p\log p
=O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2)).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

This controls arbitrarily high small-prime powers.  No radical estimate is needed.

### 7.2 \(S<p\le\sqrt n\)

For each fixed shift \(j\),

\[
v_p(n+j)\le\frac{\log(n+CS+C)}{\log p}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Moreover the interval of relevant shifts has length \(O(S)\), so for \(p>S\) only \(O_q(1)\) shifts in that interval can satisfy \(p\mid n+j\).  Thus pointwise

\[
e_p\le\eta_p+O_q(m_{\max,S})
\frac{\log n}{\log p},
\tag{7.5}
\]

where

\[
m_{\max,S}=\max_jm_{j,S}.
\]

The aggregate bound is simpler and stronger for our purpose:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{S<p\le\sqrt n}e_p\log p
\le O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2)).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

If the sharper local structure gives \(m_{\max,S}=O_q(1)\), then (7.5) is already essentially logarithmic at each medium prime.

### 7.3 \(p>\sqrt n\)

For

\[
p>\sqrt{n+C_qS+C_q},
\]

(2.5) gives

\[
v_p(n+j)\le1.
\]

Hence

\[
e_p\le\eta_p+
\sum_{\substack{j\in J_{q,S}\\p\mid n+j}}m_{j,S}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Since \(p>S\) in this range and the shift interval has length \(O(S)\), only \(O_q(1)\) shifts can occur.  Thus a bounded-multiplicity boundary factor gives bounded large-prime exponents.  Even without that refinement, the product bound yields

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p>\sqrt n}e_p\log p
=O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2)).
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

The thin transition interval

\[
\sqrt n<p\le\sqrt{n+C_qS+C_q}
\]

is harmless and is already included in the same product-height bound.

So a single integral observability certificate controls all three prime ranges at once, while retaining the requested local valuation interpretation.

## 8. Why the current exact Newton identities are not yet PW

There are several exact ingredients already in the repository.

### 8.1 Exact shell and Newton rows

`problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_cartier_packet_audit.py` uses the exact Laurent coefficient formula

\[
[x^uy^vz^w]\Lambda^m
=\sum_{t=0}^m
\binom mt\binom m{t-u}
\binom{2m-t}{m-v}
\binom{2m-t}{m-w},
\tag{8.1}
\]

and the full-Newton row

\[
w_i=(-1)^i\binom{d+i}{i}\binom{d+L+1}{L-i}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Those are integral and exact.

### 8.2 Exact terminal finite-difference identity

`q32_terminal_family_audit.py` proves the algebraic identity

\[
G_L-G_{L-1}
=(-1)^L\binom{M+1}{L}B_L,
\tag{8.3}
\]

with the terminal packet \(B_L\) represented directly by a Laurent-coefficient sum.  This is useful because it exposes a known Pascal factor and a more primitive packet.

But (8.3) by itself does not say that the common content of the actual packets \(B_L\) is small.  It separates universal Newton/Pascal content; it does not eliminate actual shell content.

### 8.3 Exact formal stencil Smith behavior

`q32_newton_gcd_audit.py` proves, for the coefficient rows themselves, the local rectangle phenomenon: when a prime-selected node lies in every stencil, the raw boundary rows coincide modulo \(p\), and after dividing consecutive row differences by their single forced \(p\), the resulting coefficient matrix has full rank modulo \(p\).  This is exactly the kind of primitive-row normalization one wants.

However it is a statement about the **Newton coefficient matrix acting on arbitrary node values**.  It cannot rule out the scaled constant-vector countermodel (3.8).  To control \(c_S\), the normalized Newton rows must be composed with an exact low-content description of the actual Laurent-shell node vector.

### 8.4 The current observability scripts identify the missing step

`q32_lower_terminal_observability.sage` is explicit about its status: after a top-half Freshman reduction it constructs a two-component lower raw vector and computes its minors, but the header says that the calculation remains diagnostic until the unique-decomposition lemma and resulting formulas are written out.

That script also constructs an `origin_bezout_constant()` from polynomial coefficient rows whose gcd over \(\mathbb Q[n]\) is \(1\).  This is precisely the mechanism of (6.7): a fixed or controlled integer Bézout denominator can bound pointwise common content.

`q32_terminal_directional_cofactor_audit.py` likewise cleanly separates two assertions:

* the directional-cofactor radical implication checked by the audit; and
* the much stronger short divisibility by the origin content times \((n+2)\cdots(n+7)\), which the code labels **conjectural**.

For Q7458, radical divisibility is not enough.  The needed upgrade is an **integral valuation statement** of the form (4.5) or (7.1), together with a proof of the short/common determinant bound.

## 9. What exact shell lemma is weakest and still sufficient?

There are three equivalent levels of strength.

### Level A: direct small integer certificate

The formally weakest statement is: there is a nonzero integer \(H_{n,q,S}\) such that

\[
c_S\mid H_{n,q,S}
\]

and

\[
\log|H_{n,q,S}|=o(n).
\]

This is tautologically sufficient but gives no proof architecture.

### Level B: valuation observability

A useful minimal shell theorem is:

> There are explicit integers \(h_{n,q,S}\) and \(D_{n,q,S}\), derived from an exact terminal Laurent-shell packet, such that for every prime \(p\),
> \[
> v_p(c_S)\le v_p(h_{n,q,S})+v_p(D_{n,q,S}),
> \]
> and
> \[
> \log h_{n,q,S}+\log D_{n,q,S}
> =O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2)).
> \]

This is exactly enough for \(S=n^{1/3}\).

### Level C: primitive-window factorization

Lemma PW is the strongest structurally informative version, because it derives Level B from the concrete factorization \(G_S=MX\).  It is what I recommend proving.  It automatically handles all prime powers and makes singular recurrence rows irrelevant.

For an implementation/proof target, the weakest exact statement I would put on the critical path is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
G_S=M_{n,q,S}X_{n,q,S},\quad
r_S\le2S+2,\quad
\log\operatorname{cont}(X_{n,q,S})
+\log\delta_{r_S}(M_{n,q,S})
=O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2)),
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

where \(\delta_r\) denotes the gcd of the \(r\times r\) row minors, defined explicitly by (4.3).  No other global property of \(A_s\) is required.

## 10. Can a growing-window determinant beat the cyclotomic height obstruction?

**Yes, in principle, and there is no contradiction.**  But only if the determinant is formed *before* expanding into the full cyclotomic spectral basis.

Suppose an exact local packet gives

\[
r_S=O_q(S)
\]

and the entries of \(M_{n,q,S}\) are integer polynomials/rationally cleared polynomials in \(n\) with

\[
\deg_n M_{ij}=O_q(S),
\qquad
\log H(M_{ij})=O_q(S\log(S+2)).
\tag{10.1}
\]

Then every \(r_S\times r_S\) determinant has, by the elementary determinant expansion/Hadamard bound,

\[
\log|\det M_I(n)|
=O_q(S^2\log(n+S+2)+S^2\log(S+2)).
\tag{10.2}
\]

The gcd \(D_S\) of those determinants is no larger.  Thus \(S=n^{1/3}\) gives sublinear height.

By contrast, the cyclotomic obstruction concerns a global constant-coefficient annihilator that resolves the full spectral support of the sequence \(A_s\).  Its natural order/height grows on the scale reported in the project, \(\Omega(n^2/\log n)\).  If one first constructs that global annihilator and then takes a growing-window determinant of its coefficients, the damage is already done; no choice \(S=o(\sqrt n)\) repairs it.

So the determinant route beats the cyclotomic barrier **iff** it comes from a local common-convolution/terminal-packet quotient of rank \(O(S)\) (ideally fixed rank) whose entries have polynomial height in \(n\).  It must not be a disguised truncation of the global spectral annihilator.

This is an important design criterion for the missing six-generator/S=2 interface and its growing-S generalization.

## 11. A publication-grade conditional theorem

The entire growing-window route can be packaged as follows.

### Theorem (conditional growing-window closure)

Fix \(q\).  Assume:

1. **Target cover.** For every \(S\), the fixed-\(q\) target radical \(T_{n,q}\) satisfies
   \[
   T_{n,q}\mid B_Sc_S,
   \qquad
   \log B_S=O_q(S^2).
   \]

2. **Integral primitive-window shell lemma.** For every \(n,S\), the actual full-Newton Laurent-shell generator vector has a factorization (4.1) with
   \[
   \log h_S+\log D_S
   \le C_qS^2\log(n+S+2).
   \]

Then for

\[
S(n)=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor
\]

one has

\[
\log c_{S(n)}=O_q(n^{2/3}\log n)=o(n)
\]

and

\[
\log T_{n,q}=o(n).
\]

### Proof

Lemma PW gives

\[
c_S\mid h_SD_S,
\]

hence

\[
\log c_S\le C_qS^2\log(n+S+2).
\]

At \(S=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\), this is \(O_q(n^{2/3}\log n)=o(n)\).  Also \(\log B_S=O_q(n^{2/3})=o(n)\).  Since \(T_{n,q}\mid B_Sc_S\),

\[
\log T_{n,q}\le\log B_S+\log c_S=o(n).
\]

No information about the cyclotomic annihilator is used.

## 12. What should be proved next in the repository

The shortest rigorous route is not to increase \(S\) in scans.  It is to prove one exact local factorization theorem.

1. **Freeze the actual terminal node block for all shifts \(|s|\le S\).**  Write each \(A_s\) and each \(R_s\) directly as an integer linear form in the Laurent coefficients using (8.1) and the full-Newton row (8.2).  Keep the common-convolution structure; do not expand into cyclotomic roots.

2. **Perform the exact common packet reduction.**  The goal is an identity over \(\mathbb Z\), or over a localization with an explicitly cleared boundary denominator,
   \[
   G_S=M_{n,q,S}X_{n,q,S}.
   \]
   The rank \(r_S\) must be \(O(S)\), not \(\Theta(n)\).

3. **Prove primitive content of the packet.**  Either prove \(h_S=1\), or prove
   \[
   \log h_S=O_q(S^2\log(S+2)).
   \]
   This is the point at which the artificial constant-stencil countermodel must fail.

4. **Normalize universal Newton/Pascal factors before taking minors.**  The exact identities in `q32_terminal_family_audit.py` and `q32_newton_gcd_audit.py` show why this is necessary.  Otherwise universal candidate-prime factors pollute every minor, exactly as in the interpolation obstruction audited by `q32_adjoint_obstruction.py`.

5. **Take the common maximal-minor gcd, not one enormous determinant.**  Prove a polynomial Bézout identity after removing explicit boundary factors.  Target either
   \[
   D_S\mid K_{q,S}\prod_{|j|\le C_qS+C_q}(n+j)^{m_{j,S}}
   \]
   with total multiplicity \(O(S^2)\), or directly (5.1).

6. **Track the Bézout denominator as \(S\) grows.**  Fixed-width experiments can hide a coefficient explosion.  The proof must record
   \[
   \log K_{q,S}=O_q(S^2\log(S+2))
   \]
   (or any bound satisfying \(\log K_{q,S(n)}=o(n)\)).  This is the quantitative issue that decides whether the growing-window idea really beats the global spectral obstruction.

If Step 2 cannot be achieved with rank \(O(S)\), or if the primitive packet content itself has \(\exp(\Theta(n))\) common scaling, then the growing-S route fails for a structural reason.  The current repository does not establish either failure.

## 13. Final verdict

* The approximate Apéry recurrence gives an exact, elegant transfer law, including the telescoping determinant (1.6), but it **cannot** control \(c_S\).  It propagates a free scale; singular rows weaken propagation further.
* Newton content-one/primitivity also cannot control that scale.  The countermodel \(A_s=M b_{n+s}\), together with constant artificial stencil data and \(\sum w_i=1\), proves this sharply.
* Therefore there is no rigorous prime-range bound on \(v_p(c_S)\) from the presently stated scalar recurrence + Newton-primitivity facts, even for \(p>n+S\).
* The growing-window idea remains viable because it needs much less than a global annihilator.  An exact local primitive-window factorization with
  \[
  \log\operatorname{cont}(X_S)+\log\delta(M_S)
  =O_q(S^2\log n)
  \]
  is sufficient.
* With that lemma, the explicit choice
  \[
  S=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor
  \]
  gives both boundary cost and residual-gcd cost \(o(n)\), and the valuation inequality (7.1) controls small, medium, and large prime powers separately.
* The present `lower_terminal_observability` / `terminal_directional_cofactor` work is pointed at exactly the right local object, but the repository itself labels the crucial reduction/short divisibility as diagnostic or conjectural.  Upgrading it to the integral valuation-level Lemma PW is the missing publication-grade step.

In short: **growing \(S\) can beat the cyclotomic height obstruction, but recurrence propagation is not the engine.  The engine must be a low-rank integral observability quotient of the actual Laurent-shell packet, with a controlled common determinantal divisor.**
