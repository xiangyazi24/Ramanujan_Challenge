# Q580 — Can Kowalski–Michel–Sawin solve the CMRD barrier?

## Executive summary

The short answer is:

**KMS is the closest available technology, but it does not apply directly.** The Apéry CMRD sum has the right reciprocal/Kloosterman shape, but three mismatches remain:

1. the Kloosterman modulus is composite and varying: `m=pℓ`;
2. the Kloosterman parameter `A=h(ℓz-pz')` moves with the modulus;
3. the coefficients are not arbitrary fixed bounded sequences but come from Apéry zero sets and prime support.

The most realistic missing theorem is therefore not a direct KMS application, but an **averaged composite-modulus Kloosterman large sieve with moving coefficients**.

---

## (A) Does KMS apply directly?

Kowalski–Michel–Sawin, *Bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums and applications* (arXiv:1702.03085, Annals of Mathematics 2017), studies bilinear forms of the shape

\[
\sum_{m,n}\alpha_m\beta_n Kl_k(mn;q)
\]

where the modulus `q` is fixed and the Kloosterman trace function is a fixed bounded-conductor object.

The Apéry inner sum is

\[
S(A;m)=\sum_{q<r\atop q,r\ \mathrm{prime}} e_m(A(qr)^{-1}),
\]

with

\[
m=p\ell,\qquad A=h(\ell z-pz').
\]

Complete the q-variable:

\[
\sum_q e_m(Aq^{-1})W(q)
\]

can be written using additive Fourier inversion as

\[
\frac1m\sum_t Kl(A,t;m)\widehat W(t).
\]

For the bilinear q,r sum one obtains schematically

\[
\sum_t Kl(A,t;m)\,C(t),
\]

where `C(t)` is a convolution of the prime weights.

So the formal Kloosterman structure is present.

However KMS requires a fixed trace family. Here:

- modulus `m` varies with `(p,ℓ)`;
- the parameter `A` varies with `(p,ℓ,z,z')`;
- the weights are supported on primes and have arithmetic correlations.

Therefore this is not a direct substitution into KMS Theorem 1.1.

---

## (B) Does the Apéry sheaf satisfy the KMS bounded-conductor condition?

Partially.

The Apéry geometric object has bounded rank and bounded conductor. The monodromy statement gives a genuine bounded-complexity sheaf for the underlying trace function.

This is exactly the setting of Deligne/Katz/Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel:

\[
t(x)=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Fr}_x|\mathcal F)
\]

with bounded conductor.

But CMRD is not simply

\[
\sum_x t(x).
\]

The zero set contribution is

\[
1_{z\in Z_p},
\]

which is a nonlinear condition on Apéry coefficients. The Fourier transform of the zero indicator is not currently known to be a bounded-conductor trace function.

Thus the underlying Apéry motive is acceptable; the moving zero-set coefficients are the obstruction.

---

## (C) Composite and varying moduli

This is the largest mismatch.

KMS is essentially a fixed-modulus theorem. It exploits the spectral theory of the Kloosterman sheaf over one finite field.

For

\[
m=p\ell,
\]
Chinese remainder gives

\[
Kl(A,t;p\ell)=Kl(A\bar\ell,t;p)\,Kl(A\bar p,t;\ell)
\]
(up to the usual normalization conventions).

This factorization is useful because it converts the composite modulus into two prime-modulus pieces.

However the two pieces are coupled:

\[
A=h(\ell z-pz')
\]
contains both primes.

So factorization alone does not reduce CMRD to two independent KMS problems.

Relevant literature:

- Kowalski–Michel–Sawin (2017): fixed modulus bilinear Kloosterman forms.
- Blomer–Milićević, *The second moment of twisted modular L-functions* and related spectral large sieve work: handles varying composite moduli in automorphic settings.
- Kerr–Petrow–Young: composite-modulus Kloosterman and trace-function methods in analytic number theory.

None gives the exact moving-modulus CMRD estimate.

---

## (D) What extra input is minimally needed?

The options:

### (i) Conductor lowering

Probably not the right route.

The modulus `pℓ` is intrinsic because it comes from CRT separation of two primes. There is no obvious quotient that lowers it.

### (ii) Factorization over p and ℓ

Necessary but insufficient.

The identity

\[
Kl(A;t;p\ell)=Kl_p\,Kl_\ell
\]

is useful, but the coefficient coupling remains.

### (iii) Kuznetsov/Voronoi

Potentially useful.

After completion, one obtains classical Kloosterman sums. A spectral method could average over moduli and exploit cancellation across `(p,ℓ)`.

This is closer to the real problem than a pointwise KMS application.

### (iv) The actual missing ingredient

A theorem of the form:

\[
\sum_{p,\ell\sim X}
\left|
\sum_{q,r\sim X}
\alpha_{q,r}
 e_{p\ell}(A_{p,\ell}(qr)^{-1})
\right|^2
\ll X^{o(1)}L^6.
\]

This is a **moving-modulus reciprocal large sieve**.

That is the minimal bridge.

---

## (E) Does a composite-modulus Kloosterman large sieve already exist?

There are related results, but not exactly this theorem.

### KMS

Provides:

\[
\sum_{m,n}\alpha_m\beta_n Kl(mn;q)
\]

for fixed q.

### Deshouillers–Iwaniec

The Kuznetsov large sieve handles sums averaged over moduli:

\[
\sum_c\frac1c
\left|\sum_n a_n Kl(m,n;c)\right|^2.
\]

But the moduli are summed independently, not restricted to products of two primes with moving coefficients.

### Blomer–Milicević

Provides powerful composite-level spectral large sieve estimates, especially for automorphic forms, but not the reciprocal-prime kernel with moving `A_{p,ℓ}`.

### Kerr–Petrow–Young

Develop trace-function/Kloosterman methods over composite moduli. Again, the exact CMRD family is outside their hypotheses.

---

## What would a successful proof look like?

The natural pipeline is:

1. Complete the reciprocal prime sums.
2. Factor the Kloosterman sums modulo `pℓ`.
3. Use an averaged spectral/Kloosterman large sieve over `(p,ℓ)`.
4. Treat the Apéry zero-set coefficients using either:
   - ZM₂,
   - Fourier decay of `Z_p`, or
   - a future sheaf-theoretic realization of the zero indicator.

The desired theorem is therefore:

> **Apéry moving-modulus Kloosterman large sieve.** For `p,ℓ∼X`, the family of reciprocal bilinear sums with coefficients `A=h(ℓz-pz')` satisfies square-root cancellation after averaging over the composite moduli.

This is weaker than a pointwise KMS-type theorem and is exactly matched to CMRD.

---

# Final conclusion

KMS provides the correct local model but not the solution.

\[
\boxed{
\text{CMRD is not a KMS problem; it is a KMS + composite modulus + moving coefficient problem.}
}
\]

The decisive missing theorem is an averaged composite-modulus reciprocal large sieve. The p,ℓ average is likely the feature that makes the problem accessible, but existing KMS/Kuznetsov technology does not yet give it as a black box.
