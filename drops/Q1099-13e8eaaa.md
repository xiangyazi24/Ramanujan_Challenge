## Conclusion

The requested unconditional proof of `log Gamma_n=o(n)` is not currently derivable from the banked facts alone. The exact obstruction can be reduced to one missing divisor-witness statement.

Write

\[
\Gamma_n=\prod_{p\le n}p^{g_p(n)}.
\]

The known facts imply:

1. `g_p(n)=0` for `p>n`.
2. For `n/2<p\le n`, `g_p(n)=1` iff `p|b_{n-p}`.
3. `rad(\Gamma_n)|b_n`.

The missing part is a uniform upper bound on the small-prime-power contribution:

\[
\sum_{p\le n/2} g_p(n)\log p=o(n).
\]

This is the smallest unresolved statement needed after the supplied reductions.

## Attempted divisor-witness reduction

For every prime power `p^a|Gamma_n`, one needs an index `j<M` with

\[
v_p(L_jb_j)<a
\]

unless `p^a` is already controlled by a sublinear exceptional set. Since

\[
L_j=(-1)^{M-1-j}\binom nj\binom{n-j-1}{M-1-j},
\]

Kummer gives

\[
v_p(L_j)=C_p(n,j)+C_p(n-j-1,M-1-j),
\]

where the two terms are carry counts in base `p`.

A successful proof would require:

**Missing Lemma (prime-power witness).** There exists a function `J(n,p,a)<M` such that for every `p^a|Gamma_n`,

\[
v_p(L_{J(n,p,a)}b_{J(n,p,a)})<a,
\]

except for at most `o(n/log n)` prime powers whose total logarithmic mass is `o(n)`.

No supplied Lucas congruence currently proves this statement.

## Why rad(Gamma)|b_n is insufficient

The radical bound gives

\[
\log rad(\Gamma_n)\le \log b_n.
\]

It does not bound exponents. Even if every prime divisor is below `n`, a single contribution `p^{Theta(n/log n)}` would violate the target. Therefore a bound on `b_n` or random-residue assumptions cannot close the argument.

## Kummer route obstruction

The half-cutoff form is favorable because `j<M` permits many carry-free choices. However, the gcd condition requires the same prime power to divide every term:

\[
p^a|L_jb_j\quad\forall j<M.
\]

Kummer controls only the `L_j` component. To remove `p^a` one needs an Apéry congruence forcing some selected `b_j` to be a `p`-adic unit or have insufficient valuation. Existing ordinary Lucas-type information is not enough for all prime powers because carries in `L_j` and valuations in `b_j` can compensate.

## Conditional theorem that would finish the problem

Assume the prime-power witness lemma above. Then

\[
\log\Gamma_n
=\sum_{p\le n}g_p(n)\log p.
\]

Split at `n/2`.

For `p>n/2`, the supplied exact classification gives

\[
\sum_{n/2<p\le n}g_p(n)\log p
\le \sum_{n/2<p\le n}\log p
=O(n),
\]

and the witness condition must sharpen this to `o(n)` by counting the exceptional divisors.

For `p\le n/2`, the missing lemma gives directly

\[
\sum_{p\le n/2}g_p(n)\log p=o(n).
\]

Hence `log Gamma_n=o(n)`.
