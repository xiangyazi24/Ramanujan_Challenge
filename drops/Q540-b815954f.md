# P3.2 — Artin–Schreier zero detectors, tensor products, and the AKD\(_3\) obstruction

## Verdict

Even granting the stated rank-4 Apéry \(\ell\)-adic sheaf \(\mathcal F_p\) on \(\mathbf A^1/\mathbf F_p\) with geometric monodromy \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\), the proposed object

\[
I_p(r)=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
 \psi\!\left(u\,\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_r\mid \mathcal F_p)\right)
\]

is **not automatically an Artin–Schreier pullback sheaf**. There are two separate category errors:

1. an Artin–Schreier sheaf can be pulled back along an **algebraic morphism** \(f:X\to\mathbf A^1\), whereas a Frobenius trace function \(r\mapsto t_{\mathcal F}(r)\) is generally not such a morphism;
2. \(\mathcal F_p\) and \(\mathcal F_q\), for \(p\ne q\), live on schemes in different characteristics, so there is no ordinary tensor product \(\mathcal I_p\otimes\mathcal I_q\) to which one can apply Deligne over a common finite field.

There is a genuine delta-function sheaf when the tested function is algebraic. Its generic rank is zero and its punctual length is the number of geometric zeros. But for the Apéry zero condition, the trace-to-zero operation is nonlinear and does not inherit bounded conductor from the rank-4 sheaf.

Thus the proposed tensor-product argument does **not** presently prove AKD\(_3\). The missing input is precisely a bounded-complexity geometrization of the mod-\(p\) zero indicator, together with a separate horizontal/cross-prime large-sieve theorem.

## 1. First type check: \(\psi(u\,t_{\mathcal F}(r))\) is not defined sheaf-theoretically

For an \(\ell\)-adic sheaf \(\mathcal F\) on a variety over \(\mathbf F_p\), with \(\ell\ne p\), its trace function is

\[
t_{\mathcal F}(r)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_r\mid\mathcal F_{\bar r})
 \in \overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell.
\]

An additive character has type

\[
\psi:\mathbf F_p\longrightarrow \overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^{\times}.
\]

There is no canonical map

\[
\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\longrightarrow\mathbf F_p
\]

with which to form \(\psi(u\,t_{\mathcal F}(r))\). If the traces happen to lie in a number field, one may separately choose an integral model and reduce numerical trace values modulo a prime above \(p\), but that produces a function on \(\mathbf F_p\); it does not produce a pullback of sheaves.

More importantly, the notation

\[
\mathcal L_{\psi(u t_{\mathcal F})}
\]

is not defined from \(\mathcal F\) alone. The Artin–Schreier pullback

\[
f^*\mathcal L_\psi=\mathcal L_{\psi(f)}
\]

requires a regular or rational map \(f:X\to\mathbf A^1\). A trace function is a function on finite-field points arising from Frobenius; it is not, in general, a regular function on \(X\).

This distinction is decisive. The sheaf-function dictionary is linear under direct sums, tensor products, duals, and cohomological pushforwards. It is not closed under the nonlinear operation

\[
t(r)\longmapsto \psi(u\,t(r)).
\]

So the sentence “this is an Artin–Schreier pullback of a trace function” is false unless one has separately proved that the trace values are supplied by an algebraic morphism.

## 2. The correct delta sheaf when \(f\) is an algebraic function

Let \(X/\mathbf F_p\) be a variety and let

\[
f:X\longrightarrow\mathbf A^1
\]

be an algebraic morphism. Put

\[
m_f:\mathbf A^1_u\times X\longrightarrow\mathbf A^1,
\qquad (u,x)\longmapsto u f(x),
\]

and let

\[
\pi:\mathbf A^1_u\times X\longrightarrow X
\]

be projection. Define the geometric Fourier projector

\[
\mathscr D_f
 :=R\pi_!\,m_f^*\mathcal L_\psi(1)[2].
\]

Here \((1)\) is the Tate twist. If

\[
i:Z=f^{-1}(0)_{\mathrm{red}}\hookrightarrow X,
\]

then fiberwise compactly supported cohomology gives

\[
R\Gamma_c\!\left(\mathbf A^1_{ar{\mathbf F}_p},
 \mathcal L_{\psi(u f(x))}\right)
\simeq
\begin{cases}
\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-1)[-2],&f(x)=0,\\
0,&f(x)\ne0.
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\mathscr D_f\simeq i_*\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell.}
\]

Its trace function on every extension \(\mathbf F_{p^n}\) is exactly

\[
t_{\mathscr D_f}(x)=1_{f(x)=0}.
\]

Equivalently, before the Tate twist,

\[
\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_{p^n}}
 \psi\!\left(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf F_{p^n}/\mathbf F_p}
 (u f(x))\right)
 =p^n1_{f(x)=0}.
\]

This is the genuine sheaf-theoretic delta function. It uses the **geometric integral over the whole \(u\)-line**, not merely a direct sum indexed by the \(p\) rational values \(u\in\mathbf F_p\).

### Rank and perversity

If \(X\) is a smooth curve and \(Z\) is finite, then:

- \(\mathscr D_f=i_*\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\) has generic rank \(0\);
- its stalk rank is \(1\) at each geometric zero of \(f\);
- its total punctual length is \(|Z(\bar{\mathbf F}_p)|\);
- it is a perverse sheaf in degree zero, since skyscraper sheaves on a smooth curve are perverse.

Thus it is misleading to assign it “rank one” or “rank \(p\)”. Its relevant size is the length/complexity of its zero support.

### Conductor

For a fixed polynomial or rational function \(f\) of degree \(d\), the relevant sheaf complexity is

\[
\ll d+\deg(\operatorname{pole}(f)),
\]

up to the chosen conductor convention. Nilpotent multiplicities in the scheme-theoretic zero fiber are invisible to the étale sheaf; what matters is the reduced geometric support.

Hence the delta sheaf has bounded complexity if \(f\) has bounded algebraic degree. It does **not** follow that the zero detector of an arbitrary bounded-conductor trace function has bounded complexity.

## 3. Three different “\(u\)-sums” that must not be conflated

### 3.1 Literal direct sum over \(u\in\mathbf F_p\)

If \(f\) is algebraic, one may form

\[
\mathscr E_f
 =\bigoplus_{u\in\mathbf F_p}\mathcal L_{\psi(u f)}.
\]

This is a genuine sheaf, but it has

\[
\operatorname{rank}(\mathscr E_f)=p.
\]

Its conductor is at best of order

\[
\operatorname{cond}(\mathscr E_f)
 \ll p\,\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal L_{\psi(f)}),
\]

apart from minor savings caused by repeated isomorphism classes. There is no automatic bounded-conductor collapse.

Moreover, over \(x\in X(\mathbf F_{p^n})\), its trace is

\[
\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
 \psi\!\left(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf F_{p^n}/\mathbf F_p}
 (u f(x))\right)
 =p\,1_{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf F_{p^n}/\mathbf F_p}(f(x))=0},
\]

which detects vanishing of the **field trace** of \(f(x)\), not vanishing of \(f(x)\). It agrees with the desired delta identity only on \(\mathbf F_p\)-points.

Also, multiplying its base-field trace by \(1/p\) is not a geometrically compatible normalization over all extensions: a Tate twist scales degree-\(n\) Frobenius traces by \(p^{-n}\), not by a fixed \(p^{-1}\).

### 3.2 Geometric integration over the \(u\)-line

The object

\[
R\pi_!\mathcal L_{\psi(u f)}(1)[2]
\]

sums over \(u\in\mathbf F_{p^n}\) at every extension level and is the true delta sheaf

\[
i_*\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell.
\]

This avoids generic rank \(p\), but its complexity is the complexity of the zero fiber of the algebraic map \(f\).

### 3.3 A zero indicator of an arbitrary trace function

For a general trace function \(t_{\mathcal F}:\mathbf F_p\to\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\), there is no canonical analogue of either construction.

One can always make the tautological punctual object

\[
\mathscr T_p
 =\bigoplus_{a\in Z_p}i_{a,*}\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell,
\qquad
Z_p=\{a\in\mathbf F_p:t_{\mathcal F}(a)=0\},
\]

whose trace on \(\mathbf F_p\) is \(1_{Z_p}\). But this construction already uses the zero set. It has

\[
\text{generic rank }0,
\qquad
\text{punctual length }|Z_p|,
\]

and any reasonable Betti/conductor complexity is at least of order \(|Z_p|\). It therefore gives no new bound on \(|Z_p|\) and no uniform conductor theorem.

So the alternatives are:

- literal \(u\)-sum: rank \(p\) and conductor growing with \(p\);
- geometric delta of an algebraic map: generic rank \(0\), complexity equal to the zero-fiber complexity;
- arbitrary trace-function zero set: no canonical bounded-complexity sheaf.

## 4. What \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) monodromy does and does not provide

Geometric monodromy \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) is powerful for **fixed algebraic tensor constructions** of \(\mathcal F\). For example, it controls invariants and correlations arising from

\[
\mathcal F^{\otimes m},\qquad
\operatorname{Sym}^m\mathcal F,\qquad
\wedge^m\mathcal F
\]

when \(m\) is fixed independently of \(p\). This gives fixed-moment equidistribution and Deligne bounds after excluding invariant constituents.

It does not make exact trace-zero detection a fixed tensor construction. If a value \(t\) lies in \(\mathbf F_p\), then functionally

\[
1_{t=0}=1-t^{p-1}.
\]

If \(t\) is a rank-4 representation trace, the obvious tensor realization of \(t^{p-1}\) uses

\[
\mathcal F^{\otimes(p-1)},
\]

of rank

\[
4^{p-1}.
\]

This is not asserted to be minimal; it simply exhibits the basic obstruction: exact delta detection requires algebraic complexity growing with \(p\), whereas \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) monodromy controls fixed-degree representation-theoretic tests.

There is a second, specifically Apéry, mismatch. The condition relevant to \(Z_p\) is

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

This is a mod-\(p\), hence crystalline/unit-root or coefficient-characteristic-\(p\), condition. Deligne’s usual \(\ell\)-adic formalism uses \(\ell\ne p\). Even when an \(\ell\)-adic compatible system has integral Frobenius traces, reducing those numerical traces modulo \(p\) is not an operation on the original \(\ell\)-adic sheaf.

Thus \(\mathrm{Sp}_4\) monodromy of the characteristic-zero/\(\ell\)-adic Apéry sheaf does not by itself control the exact mod-\(p\) zero indicator.

## 5. The tensor product \(\mathcal I_p\otimes\mathcal I_q\) for \(p\ne q\)

This tensor product is not defined in the usual sheaf category. One has

\[
\mathcal I_p\in D^b_c(\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf F_p},\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell),
\qquad
\mathcal I_q\in D^b_c(\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf F_q},\overline{\mathbf Q}_{\ell'}),
\]

on different base schemes and in different characteristics. A tensor product of sheaves is defined when the objects live on the same étale topos. There is no common finite field over which both are sheaves with a single Frobenius action.

The numerical product

\[
I_p(n\bmod p)I_q(n\bmod q)
\]

is perfectly meaningful as a periodic function of an integer \(n\), but that is a CRT object, not a trace function on one finite-field curve.

For a complete period one has the exact identity

\[
\sum_{n\bmod pq}I_p(n\bmod p)I_q(n\bmod q)
 =|Z_p|\,|Z_q|.
\]

For centered indicators,

\[
J_p(a)=1_{Z_p}(a)-\frac{|Z_p|}{p},
\qquad
J_q(b)=1_{Z_q}(b)-\frac{|Z_q|}{q},
\]

CRT gives

\[
\sum_{n\bmod pq}J_p(n\bmod p)J_q(n\bmod q)=0.
\]

For an incomplete interval \(0\le n<M<pq\), the correlation is

\[
\sum_{n<M}I_p(n\bmod p)I_q(n\bmod q)
 =\frac{M|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}
  +\mathcal E_{p,q}(M),
\]

where \(\mathcal E_{p,q}(M)\) is exactly the discrepancy of the CRT lifts of \(Z_p\times Z_q\) in the interval. Bounding this error is a dispersion/large-sieve problem. It is not a direct consequence of Deligne over \(\mathbf F_p\) or \(\mathbf F_q\).

This is the same cross-characteristic obstruction identified in the prime-aspect discussion of Q518.

## 6. What the genuine same-field Deligne correlation theorem says

For comparison, suppose \(\mathcal K\) and \(\mathcal L\) are geometrically semisimple middle-extension sheaves on the same smooth curve over the same field \(k=\mathbf F_s\), normalized to weight zero. Then Grothendieck–Lefschetz and Deligne give a formula of the shape

\[
\sum_{x\in U(\mathbf F_s)}
 t_{\mathcal K}(x)t_{\mathcal L}(x)
 =s\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Frob}_s\mid
 (\mathcal K_{\bar\eta}\otimes\mathcal L_{\bar\eta})^{\pi_1^{\rm geom}}
 \right)
 +O\!\left(B(\mathcal K,\mathcal L)s^{1/2}\right),
\]

where \(B(\mathcal K,\mathcal L)\) is controlled by their conductors/Betti numbers. With complex conjugation in the second trace, replace \(\mathcal L\) by its dual.

If \(\mathcal K\otimes\mathcal L\) has no geometrically constant constituent, the \(s\)-term vanishes and one gets square-root cancellation.

This theorem would be relevant if two bounded-conductor centered Apéry indicator sheaves existed **over the same finite field** and had no common geometric constituent.

It behaves differently for delta sheaves. If

\[
\mathcal K=i_{Z,*}\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell,
\qquad
\mathcal L=i_{W,*}\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell,
\]

then

\[
\mathcal K\otimes\mathcal L
 \simeq i_{Z\cap W,*}\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell
\]

and

\[
\sum_x t_{\mathcal K}(x)t_{\mathcal L}(x)
 =|(Z\cap W)(\mathbf F_s)|.
\]

There is no oscillatory square-root phenomenon: the product simply counts the intersection of two supports.

## 7. What Forey–Fresán–Kowalski actually supplies

Forey–Fresán–Kowalski study arithmetic Fourier transforms of the form

\[
S(M,\chi)
 =\sum_{x\in G(k_n)}t_M(x)\chi(x),
\]

where

- \(G\) is a connected commutative algebraic group;
- \(M\) is a perverse sheaf on \(G\);
- \(\chi\) is a character of \(G(k_n)\).

This is a **linear Fourier transform of the trace function in the argument \(x\)**. For \(G=\mathbf G_a\), it geometrizes sums such as

\[
\sum_x t_M(x)\psi(ux).
\]

The Apéry zero detector instead asks for

\[
x\longmapsto
\frac1p\sum_u\psi\bigl(u\,t_M(x)\bigr),
\]

which is a Fourier transform in the **value** \(t_M(x)\). These are different operations. The second is geometrizable by the universal Artin–Schreier kernel only when \(x\mapsto t_M(x)\) is replaced by an algebraic morphism \(f:X\to\mathbf G_a\).

Therefore Forey–Fresán–Kowalski does not turn an arbitrary trace function into a bounded-conductor delta sheaf. Their formalism becomes relevant only after one already has a perverse sheaf whose trace is the desired indicator or centered indicator.

Two points from their paper are especially relevant:

1. their basic arithmetic Fourier transform is linear in \(t_M\), so it does not justify \(\psi(u t_M(x))\);
2. their Remark 1.4(3) notes that horizontal families as the characteristic varies require quantitative stratified-vanishing input not available in full generality. Thus even the linear theory does not automatically give the cross-prime theorem needed here.

The exact delta calculation above is standard Fourier–Deligne/Laumon theory. Forey–Fresán–Kowalski provides the broader convolution and equidistribution framework once a suitable perverse input object has been constructed.

## 8. Consequence for AKD\(_3\)

The hoped-for argument was:

\[
\text{rank-4 Apéry sheaf}
\Longrightarrow
\text{bounded-rank zero-indicator sheaf}
\Longrightarrow
\text{Deligne bound for }p\ne q.
\]

Both arrows fail:

1. zero detection is nonlinear and does not preserve bounded conductor;
2. sheaves in characteristics \(p\) and \(q\) cannot be tensored into one same-field object.

A correct conditional formulation would require the following genuinely new inputs.

### Input I: bounded-complexity indicator geometrization

Construct, for every \(p\), a perverse complex \(\mathscr J_p\) on \(\mathbf A^1/\mathbf F_p\) whose trace is

\[
t_{\mathscr J_p}(r)
 =1_{Z_p}(r)-\frac{|Z_p|}{p},
\]

with complexity \(p^{o(1)}\), and prove purity plus absence of exceptional Artin–Schreier constituents.

This is not a consequence of the rank-4 sheaf. In substance, it is already a strong uniform theorem about the Apéry zero sets.

### Input II: same-field correlation/Fourier bounds

For each fixed \(p\), use Deligne or Fourier–Deligne to control additive transforms and rational self-correlations of \(t_{\mathscr J_p}\). This is where FKM/Laumon technology would apply.

### Input III: a horizontal cross-prime large sieve

Relate the family \(\{\mathscr J_p\}_p\) across varying characteristics and prove a large-sieve/dispersion estimate for their CRT pullbacks. This is not a tensor-product theorem over a finite field. It requires compatible-system, product-monodromy, or direct CRT discrepancy input.

For the present P3.2 program, the realistic geometric target is therefore not “form \(I_p\otimes I_q\) and invoke Deligne,” but one of:

- construct a crystalline/unit-root algebraic moduli problem in which \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\) is a bounded-degree rank-drop condition;
- prove a uniform sheaf realization of the centered zero indicator, accepting that this is essentially the missing theorem;
- or bypass the indicator sheaf and prove cross-prime decorrelation directly by dispersion/large sieve on the CRT sets.

## 9. Correction to the current Q542 note

The useful part of Q542 is the distinction between Fourier transform in \(r\) and zero detection. However, the statement there that the \(u\)-family can avoid a \(p\)-fold conductor explosion should be made conditional:

> For an algebraic map \(f:X\to\mathbf A^1\), geometric integration over the universal Artin–Schreier kernel collapses to the punctual delta sheaf \(i_{f=0,*}\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\), whose complexity is the complexity of the zero fiber. For a generic trace function \(t_{\mathcal F}\), the pullback \(\mathcal L_{\psi(u t_{\mathcal F})}\) is not defined; the literal direct sum, when definable, has rank \(p\), and the tautological zero-support sheaf has complexity \(|Z_p|\).

With this correction, Q518’s conclusion remains the right one: the bottleneck is the construction of a uniformly controlled sheaf for the Apéry zero indicator, not the formal Fourier transform after such a sheaf is already available.

## Direct answers

### (1) Is \(I_p\) a sheaf, and what is its rank?

- For a general Frobenius trace function: **no canonical Artin–Schreier pullback exists**.
- For an algebraic map \(f\): the literal direct sum over \(u\in\mathbf F_p\) is a rank-\(p\) sheaf with conductor growing at least linearly in \(p\).
- The correct geometric \(u\)-integral is the delta complex \(i_{f=0,*}\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\), of generic rank \(0\) and punctual length equal to the number of geometric zeros; on a curve with finite zero set it is perverse.
- For the actual set \(Z_p\), a tautological skyscraper sheaf exists but has complexity \(|Z_p|\) and therefore proves nothing.

### (2) Is there a Deligne bound for \(I_p\otimes I_q\)?

- For \(p\ne q\): **not in this form**, because the tensor product is not defined over a common finite field.
- For two sheaves over the same finite field: yes, after removing geometrically constant constituents, Grothendieck–Lefschetz plus Deligne gives an \(O(\sqrt{|k|})\) correlation bound with conductor dependence.
- For punctual zero-indicator sheaves, the product counts support intersections rather than exhibiting oscillation.
- The actual cross-prime correlation is a CRT discrepancy problem.

### (3) Does the \(u\)-sum behave as a delta sheaf, and what is its conductor?

- **Yes for an algebraic map \(f\)**, via
  \[
  R\pi_!\mathcal L_{\psi(u f)}(1)[2]
  \simeq i_{f=0,*}\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell.
  \]
  Its complexity is controlled by the reduced zero fiber, hence by \(\deg f\) for bounded-degree \(f\).
- **No such conclusion follows for a trace function**. The zero set can have complexity growing with \(p\), and the bounded conductor of \(\mathcal F\) does not transfer through the nonlinear operation \(t\mapsto1_{t=0}\).
- Forey–Fresán–Kowalski applies to linear arithmetic Fourier transforms of trace functions, not to this nonlinear value-level delta operation.
