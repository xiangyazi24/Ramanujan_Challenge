## 5. Which statistic falsifies saturation fastest?

### 5.1 Actual local `rho` mass — decisive

The sharpness model replaces the actual `rho_p` by its pointwise envelope on essentially every prime in the interval. For a fixed-width interval there are approximately `T/log T` primes, and `rho_p` is allowed to be of size `T^(-2/3)`. With the literal `log p` weight this predicts

```text
A_rho approximately T^(1/3),
N_square approximately T^2/log T,
R2_square approximately T^(8/3)/log T.
```

But (V4) is an equivalence up to four. Therefore the normalized one-prime statistic

\[
\mathfrak a_\rho(T)
:=\frac{A_\rho(T)}{T^{1/3}}
\tag{5.1}
\]

is the earliest possible falsifier. It requires only `Z(p)`, not `q`, `t`, `T_3(p)`, or Fourier data.

To distinguish uniform smallness from concentration on a few primes, also emit

\[
E_\rho(T)
:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_\square}
 (\log p)^2\rho_p^2,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
H_\rho(T)=A_\rho(T)^2/E_\rho(T).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Because `rho_p<=theta_p<=2rho_p`,

\[
\frac14H_\rho\le H_\theta\le4H_\rho.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus `H_rho` already determines whether the cross term has the abstract effective support `T/log T`.

### 5.2 Pair-prime occupancy — already saturated

Let

\[
m_P=|\mathcal P_\square(T)|.
\]

Every row contains all `m_P` lower primes. Hence the exact ordered distinct-pair occupancy is

\[
N_\square(m_P)_2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

There is no hidden moving-window loss. The usual two prime-density logarithms are already present in `m_P^2`; an additional logarithm would require an anomalous shortage of ordinary primes in a fixed interval, not an Apéry phenomenon.

Moreover raw pair occupancy weights every prime equally. The actual cross term (3.5) can be small while (5.5) is maximal if the Apéry weights are concentrated or mostly zero. Therefore pair occupancy is strictly less discriminating than `A_rho` and `H_rho`.

### 5.3 Cubic overlap — absolute-factor range only

The exact cubic statistic changes `2rho_p` to `theta_p=2rho_p-tau_p`. By (2.7),

\[
1\le\frac{\theta_p}{\rho_p}\le2
\]

whenever `rho_p>0`. Even perfect nonzero Fourier coherence in `T_3(p)` cannot create a logarithmic saving in this rectangle. Cubic data remain useful for validating the union baseline, but not for falsifying its scale.

### 5.4 `t`-variation — exactly zero in the sharp core

For each `q`, let

\[
\overline R_q
=\frac1{H_T}\sum_{t=0}^{h}R(q,t).
\]

The exact mean/variance identity is

\[
\sum_{t=0}^{h}R(q,t)^2
=\sum_{t=0}^{h}(R(q,t)-\overline R_q)^2
 +H_T\overline R_q^2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

In the sharp core, `R(q,t)=S_square`, so

\[
\sum_t(R-\overline R_q)^2=0,
\qquad
H_T\overline R_q^2=H_TS_\square^2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

All energy is in the constant mode. Consequently small `t`-variation is not evidence against saturation; it is the exact saturation geometry.

## 6. A rigorous concentration-to-log theorem

The following implication converts the fastest empirical diagnostic into an actual theorem.

### Lemma 6.1 — effective-support transfer

Assume that, for some fixed `delta>0`, the actual Apéry weights on `P_square(T)` satisfy

\[
H_\rho(T)
\ll \frac{T}{(\log T)^{1+\delta}}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal R_{2,\square}^{\log}(T)
\ll \frac{T^{8/3}}{(\log T)^{1+\delta}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

By (V4) and the definition of `H_rho`,

\[
\mathcal R_{2,\square}^{\log}
\le4N_\square A_\rho^2
=4N_\square H_\rho E_\rho.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The banked pointwise theorem gives `Z(p)<<p^(2/3)`, so on this fixed-width interval

\[
\rho_p\ll T^{-2/3}.
\]

Also `log p` is comparable to `log T`, and the elementary prime-counting upper bound gives `m_P<<T/log T`. Therefore

\[
E_\rho
\ll \frac{T}{\log T}
       (\log T)^2T^{-4/3}
= T^{-1/3}\log T.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Likewise

\[
N_\square
=(h+1)|\mathcal Q_\square|
\ll T\frac{T}{\log T}
=\frac{T^2}{\log T}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Insert (6.1), (6.4), and (6.5) into (6.3):

\[
\mathcal R_{2,\square}^{\log}
\ll
\frac{T^2}{\log T}
\cdot
T^{-1/3}\log T
\cdot
\frac{T}{(\log T)^{1+\delta}},
\]

which is (6.2). `QED`

Thus the mathematically testable two-log lemma is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\left(\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_\square}
 (\log p)Z(p)^2/p^2\right)^2}
 {\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_\square}
 (\log p)^2Z(p)^4/p^4}
\ll \frac{T}{(\log T)^3}.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

It would imply

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{2,\square}^{\log}(T)
\ll T^{8/3}(\log T)^{-3},}
\tag{6.7}
\]

a genuine two-log saving over the abstract weighted rectangle scale `T^(8/3)/log T`.

### Lemma 6.2 — local-mass transfer

A still simpler sufficient statement is

\[
A_\rho(T)
\ll T^{1/3}(\log T)^{-\eta}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Then (V4) and (6.5) give

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{2,\square}^{\log}(T)
\ll T^{8/3}(\log T)^{-1-2\eta}.}
\tag{6.9}
\]

For `eta=1`, one logarithm in the actual one-prime Apéry mass produces two logarithms in `R^2`.

Neither lemma is inferred from a finite scan. The scan should report the exact left sides of (6.6) and (6.8) on consecutive dyadic shells; the proof task is then the corresponding uniform bound.
