# Stewart-type theorems for Apéry numbers

## Executive verdict

There is a direct published prime-factor theorem for the Apéry numbers, but it is much weaker than a Stewart theorem for constant-coefficient binary recurrences and it does not address the interval needed in Problem 3.2.

Let

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
P^+(N)=\max\{p:p\mid N\},
\]

and let \(\omega(N)\) denote the number of distinct prime factors of \(N\). Luca and Shparlinski proved that there is an absolute constant \(c_0>0\) such that, for a set of positive integers \(n\) of asymptotic density one,

\[
\boxed{
 \omega(b_n)>c_0\log\log\log n,
 \qquad
 P^+(b_n)>c_0\sqrt{\log n\,\log\log n}.
}
\tag{LS}
\]

This is [Florian Luca and Igor Shparlinski, *Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers*, JLMS 78 (2008), 545–562](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jdn031). Their abstract explicitly says that the method applies to more general sequences satisfying both a second-order recurrence with polynomial coefficients and suitable Lucas-type congruences.

That theorem is the presently relevant “Stewart-type” result for Apéry numbers. It is:

- a **density-one**, not all-\(n\), theorem;
- a **global** prime-factor theorem;
- only polylogarithmic in the largest-prime-factor variable;
- silent about the location of the prime factors.

It does not imply any useful upper bound for

\[
\omega_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)
 :=\#\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n\}.
\]

The classical Stewart–Shorey machinery itself does **not** apply to the Apéry recurrence. Its essential input is a fixed Binet representation

\[
u_n=a\alpha^n+b\beta^n
\]

coming from a constant-coefficient recurrence. The Apéry recurrence is a non-autonomous, polynomial-coefficient recurrence. Its limiting characteristic roots control archimedean growth, but they do not give an exact Binet formula, cyclotomic factorization, rank of apparition, or primitive-divisor structure.

There is also a general obstruction: no theorem depending only on “P-recursive,” “G-function,” “geometric period,” or even “large differential Galois group” can force

\[
P^+(u_n)>\exp(cn^\alpha)
\]

for any fixed \(\alpha>0\). For example,

\[
c_n=\binom{2n}{n}^2
\]

is the coefficient sequence of a non-algebraic elliptic-period G-function with Zariski-dense rank-two monodromy, yet every prime divisor of \(c_n\) is at most \(2n\). Thus extra Apéry-specific arithmetic, not differential Galois theory alone, is indispensable.

Finally, in the top window the project has the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
 \omega_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)
 =\#\{p\in(n/2,n]:n-p\in Z_p\},
 \qquad
 Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},
}
\tag{MD}
\]

apart from the harmless prime \(5\). This is the moving zero diagonal. A theorem bounding it would be a genuinely new horizontal, mixed-characteristic theorem; it is not a standard consequence of any known largest-prime-factor result for recurrence sequences.

## 2. What Stewart's theorem actually assumes

A binary recurrence in Stewart's setting has fixed integers \(r,s\) and

\[
u_n=ru_{n-1}+su_{n-2}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

If \(\alpha,\beta\) are the roots of \(x^2-rx-s\), then

\[
u_n=a\alpha^n+b\beta^n.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The recurrence is non-degenerate when

\[
ab\alpha\beta\ne0,
\qquad
\alpha/\beta\text{ is not a root of unity}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The modern quantitative statements include the following.

1. For a general non-degenerate binary recurrence, Stewart's 2013 result gives, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
   \[
   P^+(u_n)>
   n^{1/2}\exp\!\left(\frac{\log n}{104\log\log n}\right).
   \tag{2.4}
   \]

2. For a Lucas sequence
   \[
   U_n=\frac{\alpha^n-\beta^n}{\alpha-\beta},
   \]
   Stewart proved, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
   \[
   P^+(U_n)>
   n\exp\!\left(\frac{\log n}{104\log\log n}\right).
   \tag{2.5}
   \]

3. Stewart's 2022 paper proves the same bound as (2.5), outside a set of indices of asymptotic density zero, for an arbitrary non-degenerate binary recurrence:
   \[
   P^+(u_n)>
   n\exp\!\left(\frac{\log n}{104\log\log n}\right)
   \quad\text{for density-one }n.
   \tag{2.6}
   \]

See [C. L. Stewart, *On prime factors of terms of binary recurrence sequences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01275), especially the introduction and Theorems 1–2. A uniform explicit Lucas-sequence version is given by [Y. Bilu, S. Gun and H. Hong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09857).

The proof architecture is inseparable from (2.2). It uses:

- homogeneous cyclotomic factors \(\Phi_m(\alpha,\beta)\);
- primitive divisors and congruence restrictions on their prime factors;
- ranks of apparition in Lucas/Lehmer sequences;
- exact valuation formulas tied to the divisibility sequence;
- archimedean and \(p\)-adic linear forms in logarithms.

These are not cosmetic hypotheses. They are the mechanism producing a prime \(p\) whose size is constrained by the index \(n\).

## 3. Why Stewart does not apply to the Apéry cocycle

After division by \((n+1)^3\), (1.1) becomes

\[
b_{n+1}
=\frac{34n^3+51n^2+27n+5}{(n+1)^3}b_n
-\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^3b_{n-1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus the transfer matrix depends on time:

\[
\binom{b_{n+1}}{b_n}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{34n^3+51n^2+27n+5}{(n+1)^3}
&-\dfrac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}\\[1.2ex]
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{b_n}{b_{n-1}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The product is

\[
T_nT_{n-1}\cdots T_1,
\]

not a power \(T^n\) of one fixed matrix. Therefore:

- there are no fixed algebraic numbers \(\alpha,\beta\) for which \(b_n=a\alpha^n+b\beta^n\) exactly;
- the limiting roots \(17\pm12\sqrt2\) describe asymptotic growth only;
- there is no canonical termwise cyclotomic factor \(\Phi_n(\alpha,\beta)\mid b_n\);
- \((b_n)\) is not a divisibility sequence of Lucas type;
- there is no known rank-of-apparition law saying that the indices at which a fixed prime divides \(b_n\) form multiples of one basic index;
- the singular times \(n\equiv-1\pmod p\) and the Lucas digit structure are characteristic-dependent.

Consequently, checking “non-degeneracy” of the limiting characteristic polynomial does not place \((b_n)\) in Stewart's class. It would be a category error to substitute the asymptotic formula

\[
b_n\asymp C\rho^n n^{-3/2}
\]

for the exact Binet formula required in the arithmetic argument.

The Subspace Theorem and linear-forms-in-logarithms methods used for constant-coefficient recurrences likewise do not automatically extend. They exploit sums of a fixed finite set of exponential terms. A P-recursive solution is instead a coefficient sequence of a differential equation, and its exact terms need not be values of a fixed \(S\)-unit expression.

## 4. There is no generic polynomial-coefficient Stewart theorem

The class of polynomial-coefficient recurrences is far too broad for a universal large-prime-factor theorem.

### 4.1 A literal second-order counterexample

The sequence \(u_n=n!\) satisfies

\[
u_{n+2}-(2n+3)u_{n+1}+(n+1)^2u_n=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

All three coefficients are nonzero polynomials, yet

\[
P^+(u_n)\le n.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This example is not a G-function because its coefficients grow factorially, but it already shows that “second-order recurrence with polynomial coefficients” alone has no Stewart consequence.

### 4.2 A geometric G-function counterexample

A much stronger counterexample is

\[
c_n=\binom{2n}{n}^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Its generating function is

\[
C(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n
={}_2F_1\!\left(\frac12,\frac12;1;16z\right),
\tag{4.4}
\]

and satisfies the Picard–Fuchs equation

\[
z(1-16z)C''+(1-32z)C'-4C=0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is a non-algebraic elliptic-period G-function. Its projective monodromy is an arithmetic subgroup of \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbf Z)\), hence Zariski-dense; after determinant normalization its differential Galois group has \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) identity component. Nevertheless,

\[
P^+(c_n)\le2n
\tag{4.6}
\]

because every prime dividing \(\binom{2n}{n}\) is at most \(2n\).

Thus even the conjunction

```text
integral coefficients
+ exponential growth
+ G-function
+ geometric period
+ genuine rank-two Picard–Fuchs equation
+ large differential monodromy
```

does not imply \(P^+(c_n)>\exp(cn^\alpha)\) for any \(\alpha>0\).

There is an even closer K3-style example:

\[
d_n=\binom{2n}{n}^3,
\qquad
\sum_{n\ge0}d_nz^n
={}_3F_2\!\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac12;1,1;64z\right).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The hypergeometric differential equation is a symmetric-square geometric equation of the type appearing in K3-period theory, while still

\[
P^+(d_n)\le2n.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Therefore neither “period of a K3 family” nor differential-Galois largeness is, by itself, a prime-factor theorem for the coefficients.

### 4.3 What differential Galois theory actually controls

Differential Galois theory controls algebraic and differential relations among solutions of the generating-function differential equation. It can establish, for example:

- irreducibility of the differential module;
- the Zariski closure of monodromy;
- algebraic independence of functions or special values;
- the absence of Liouvillian solutions.

It does not encode the factorization of the individual integer coefficient \(b_n\). The predicate

\[
p\mid b_n
\]

varies simultaneously with the coefficient index and the residue characteristic. It is not an algebraic relation among the characteristic-zero solutions of the differential equation.

## 5. The direct Luca–Shparlinski theorem

The key paper is:

> Florian Luca and Igor E. Shparlinski, [*Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers*](https://academic.oup.com/jlms/article/78/3/545/979380), Journal of the London Mathematical Society **78** (2008), 545–562, DOI 10.1112/jlms/jdn031.

Their theorem gives (LS): for density-one \(n\),

\[
\omega(b_n)>c_0\log\log\log n
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
P^+(b_n)>c_0\sqrt{\log n\,\log\log n}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This paper answers two parts of Q162 precisely.

1. **There is an analogue for certain polynomial-coefficient recurrences.** It is not a generic P-recursive theorem. The extra input is a suitable Lucas-type congruence.

2. **For the Apéry sequence, the known analogue is far weaker than the constant-coefficient Stewart theorem.** Its lower bound is polylogarithmic, not \(\exp(cn^\alpha)\), and it holds only on a density-one set of indices.

The distinction between “recurrence” and “recurrence plus Lucas congruences” is essential. The Lucas congruence lets one factor the reduction of \(b_n\) through the base-\(p\) digits of \(n\); this supplies arithmetic information not present in an arbitrary holonomic sequence. Even so, the conclusion is global and density-one.

I found no later published theorem that upgrades (5.2) for the Apéry numbers to

\[
P^+(b_n)>\exp(cn^\alpha)
\tag{5.3}
\]

for any fixed \(\alpha>0\), either for all \(n\) or for density-one \(n\). The subsequent Apéry-like literature located in this audit is predominantly about Lucas congruences, supercongruences, and \(p\)-adic valuations rather than largest prime factors.

For example, Éric Delaygue's [*Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X17007552) proves lower bounds for \(p\)-adic valuations of factorial-ratio multisums and criteria for the \(p\)-Lucas property. These are highly relevant to the local structure of \(Z_p\), but they do not supply a largest-prime-factor or top-window \(\omega\)-bound.

## 6. André's G-function theory does not give coefficient factorization

The standard monograph is Yves André's [*G-Functions and Geometry*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-663-14108-2), published in 1989, not 2004. If a different 2004 André result was intended, the same categorical distinction applies.

The characteristic-zero G-function theory concerns:

- regular-singular differential equations;
- controlled coefficient heights and denominators;
- geometric origin and periods;
- functional and value transcendence or algebraic independence.

These outputs do not impose a lower bound on \(P^+(b_n)\). In particular:

- a denominator theorem controls common denominators, not the prime support of an integral numerator;
- algebraic independence of functions says that no fixed polynomial relation holds among whole functions;
- algebraic independence of values says that certain complex or \(p\)-adic values do not satisfy fixed algebraic relations;
- neither statement controls the moving predicate \(p\mid b_n\).

A later paper that is very close in vocabulary is Adamczewski–Bell–Delaygue, [*Algebraic independence of G-functions and congruences à la Lucas*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.04187), Ann. Sci. ENS 52 (2019), 515–559. It uses Frobenius/Lucas functional equations to prove algebraic independence of G-functions. Its conclusion is still functional algebraic independence, not a factorization theorem for one coefficient.

The elliptic example (4.3) is a concrete no-go theorem for this proposed implication: a geometric, rank-two, large-monodromy G-function may have every coefficient supported on primes \(\le2n\).

## 7. Why exponential growth does not force a large prime factor

From (1.2),

\[
b_n=\exp((3.52549\ldots+o(1))n).
\]

This only says

\[
\sum_p v_p(b_n)\log p=(3.52549\ldots+o(1))n.
\tag{7.1}
\]

It permits the mass on the left to be carried by small primes with large multiplicity or by many moderate primes. For comparison:

- \(2^n\) grows exponentially and has largest prime factor \(2\);
- \(\binom{2n}{n}^2\) grows like \(16^n/(\pi n)\) and has largest prime factor at most \(2n\);
- the K3-style sequence \(\binom{2n}{n}^3\) grows exponentially and also has largest prime factor at most \(2n\).

Therefore an archimedean asymptotic, even one coming from a geometrically irreducible differential equation, cannot by itself yield (5.3).

To prove (5.3) for the specific Apéry sequence would require a new arithmetic theorem excluding smooth Apéry values. Potential tools might involve effective estimates for solutions of the Apéry difference equation modulo large smooth moduli, but no established general theorem presently supplies such an exclusion.

## 8. A largest-prime-factor theorem is nearly orthogonal to the P3.2 target

Suppose hypothetically that one proved

\[
P^+(b_n)>\exp(cn^\alpha),
\qquad 0<\alpha<1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Remove that one prime factor from \(b_n\). The remaining \(K_{\rm top}(n)-1\) distinct top-window primes are each at least \(n/2\), so at best

\[
(K_{\rm top}(n)-1)\log(n/2)
\le \log b_n-\log P^+(b_n).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Using (1.2) and (8.1),

\[
K_{\rm top}(n)
\le
\frac{(\log\rho+o(1))n-cn^\alpha}{\log(n/2)}+1
=\Theta\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right).
\tag{8.3}
\]

The saving is only \(O(n^\alpha/\log n)\), negligible against the linear numerator. Thus even the hoped-for stretched-exponential largest-prime-factor bound would not prove the desired top-window sparsity.

There are two further mismatches.

1. If \(P^+(b_n)>n\), that prime lies **outside** the interval \((n/2,n]\). It consumes some logarithmic height but gives no top-window hit information.

2. A theorem giving a lower bound for \(\omega(b_n)\), such as (5.1), is in the opposite direction from the desired upper bound for \(\omega_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)\).

Only a near-maximal estimate

\[
\log P^+(b_n)=(\log\rho-o(1))n
\]

would force the remaining factor of \(b_n\) to be subexponential by height alone. Nothing remotely of this strength is known, and even such a statement would concern one huge factor rather than the natural zero-set mechanism of the project.

## 9. Exact top-window reformulation via the Lucas congruence

Let \(p\in(n/2,n]\) be a prime and write

\[
n=p+r,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

Gessel's Apéry–Lucas congruence gives

\[
b_{p+r}\equiv b_1b_r=5b_r\pmod p.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Therefore, for \(p>5\),

\[
p\mid b_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_{n-p}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n-p\in Z_p.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Summing over the top window yields (MD):

\[
K_{\rm top}(n)
=\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\mathbf1_{\{n-p\in Z_p\}}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

This identity explains precisely why the existing vertical information does not settle the question.

The project proves strong facts about each individual \(Z_p\):

- \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\);
- reflection symmetry;
- no consecutive zeros;
- gap-polynomial constraints;
- Cartier–Frobenius/Lucas structure;
- pair-energy and moment information.

But (9.3) samples one specially moving location from each different characteristic. An abstract family could have \(|Z_p|=1\) or \(2\) for every prime and still place that root exactly at \(n-p\) for one common \(n\). This is the anchored-star obstruction already identified in the project.

Hence a bound for \(K_{\rm top}(n)\) requires a horizontal statement such as:

- a mixed-characteristic equidistribution theorem for the points \(n-p\in Z_p\);
- a cross-prime CRT correlation estimate;
- a high factorial-moment estimate \((\mathrm{HM})_k\);
- an atom-tail theorem excluding one row shared by many prime columns;
- an integral carrier/resultant whose height couples the actual roots across different characteristics.

None of these follows from a theorem about \(P^+(b_n)\).

## 10. Is any interval-\(\omega\) theorem known?

In the literature located in this audit, **no theorem gives a nontrivial uniform upper bound for**

\[
\omega_{(n/2,n]}(b_n).
\tag{10.1}
\]

The closest results fall into four different categories.

### 10.1 Largest-prime-factor theorems

Stewart, Shorey, Luca, and successors prove that a term of a constant-coefficient recurrence has at least one sufficiently large prime factor, or that certain smooth/pure-power behavior is rare. These do not upper-bound the number of primes in a prescribed interval.

### 10.2 Primitive-divisor theorems

For Lucas and Lehmer sequences, primitive-divisor theory supplies a new prime at a prescribed index. This is again a lower-bound/existence mechanism. The Apéry sequence has no analogous divisibility-sequence structure.

### 10.3 Global \(\omega\)-lower bounds

Luca–Shparlinski prove (5.1) for Apéry numbers. It gives no localization and has the wrong inequality direction for (10.1).

### 10.4 Congruence and valuation theorems

Gessel, Delaygue, Straub, and others give Lucas congruences, supercongruences, and \(p\)-adic valuation information. These are the right local ingredients for defining \(Z_p\), but a new horizontal correlation theorem is required to combine them at the moving arguments \(n-p\).

Accordingly, the following would be a genuinely new theorem:

> **Apéry top-window theorem.** There exists \(\delta>0\) such that, for every sufficiently large \(n\),
> \[
> \omega_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)\ll n^{1-\delta}.
> \tag{10.2}
> \]

Even (10.2) is much weaker than the Poisson-scale prediction, but it would imply

\[
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}\log p
\ll n^{1-\delta}\log n=o(n).
\]

Under the project's current worst-case bound \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\), a fixed high-moment statement

\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_k
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k,
\qquad k>6,
\tag{10.3}
\]

would also close the relevant pointwise route. This is a cross-prime theorem, not a largest-prime-factor theorem.

## 12. Direct answers to the five questions

### 1. Does Stewart's theorem apply?

**No.** Stewart's theorem is for constant-coefficient recurrences with an exact Binet expression in fixed characteristic roots. The Apéry recurrence has polynomial/rational time-dependent coefficients. The dominant limiting root \(17+12\sqrt2\) is not a substitute for the exact fixed-root arithmetic.

### 2. Are there analogous results for polynomial-coefficient recurrences?

There is no generic analogue based only on P-recursiveness, differential Galois theory, or G-function status. Counterexamples (4.1)–(4.8) rule out such a theorem. There is, however, a special Apéry theorem: Luca–Shparlinski prove the density-one bounds (5.1)–(5.2) by using the recurrence together with Lucas-type congruences.

### 3. Does André's G-function theory give prime-factor information?

**Not of the required type.** It controls differential equations, geometry, denominators, and algebraic independence of functions or values. It does not control the prime support of the integral coefficient \(b_n\). The elliptic-period example \(\binom{2n}{n}^2\) shows that even large differential monodromy is compatible with \(P^+(c_n)\le2n\).

### 4. Is a bound \(P^+(b_n)>\exp(cn^\alpha)\) known?

No such bound was located. The direct published Apéry bound is only

\[
P^+(b_n)>c_0\sqrt{\log n\log\log n}
\]

for density-one \(n\). Exponential growth of \(b_n\) does not imply a stretched-exponential largest prime factor.

### 5. Is there a theorem bounding \(\omega_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)\)?

No nontrivial uniform theorem was located. The exact identity (9.3) turns this into the moving zero diagonal across the sets \(Z_p\). Proving a useful upper bound requires new cross-characteristic/horizontal information. Neither a Stewart theorem nor the Luca–Shparlinski global bounds provide it.

---

## Final route assessment

The Stewart route is valuable mainly as a **classification no-go**:

1. classical Stewart–Shorey theorems do not transfer to the Apéry recurrence;
2. broad G-function and differential-Galois hypotheses cannot force large coefficient prime factors;
3. the strongest directly relevant theorem currently available is Luca–Shparlinski's density-one polylogarithmic result;
4. even a hypothetical stretched-exponential lower bound for \(P^+(b_n)\) would not control the number of prime factors in \((n/2,n]\);
5. the live residual is exactly the horizontal moving-diagonal problem (9.3).

Therefore this avenue does not bypass the project's current cross-prime barrier. Any successful theorem must control **many simultaneous top-window divisibility events**, rather than merely produce one large prime divisor of \(b_n\).
