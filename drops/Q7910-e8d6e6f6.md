# 1. The relative fixed-ADE resolution

Work first over

\[
S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z[1/6,a,a^{-1}],
\qquad
\mathscr P=(\mathbf P^1_S)^3,
\]

and let \(\mathscr X\subset\mathscr P\) be the hypersurface \(\mathcal F_a=0\). The seven fixed singular sections are

\[
(0,1,\infty),\ (0,\infty,\infty),\ (1,0,\infty),\
(\infty,0,\infty),\ (0,\infty,0),\ (\infty,0,0),\ (1,1,1).
\]

They are pairwise disjoint sections of \(\mathscr P/S\). Blow up their disjoint union in \(\mathscr P\), and let

\[
\mathscr Y\subset \operatorname{Bl}_{\Sigma}(\mathscr P)
\]

be the strict transform of \(\mathscr X\). This is the required relative model after resolving the fixed boundary ADE singularities.

The following elementary blow-up calculation is enough; no simultaneous-resolution theorem or base change is needed for these fixed sections. Suppose locally along a singular section that

\[
F=q_2(x_1,x_2,x_3)+q_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)+\cdots.
\]

In the blow-up chart \(x_i=\rho X_i\), the strict-transform equation is

\[
q_2(X)+\rho q_3(X)+\rho^2q_4(X)+\cdots=0. \tag{1.1}
\]

If the projective conic \(q_2=0\) is smooth, the strict transform is regular along the exceptional divisor. If \(q_2=L_1L_2\) with distinct relative linear factors, the only possible singular point on the exceptional divisor is \(L_1=L_2=0\); it is regular as soon as \(q_3\) is a unit in that kernel direction, because then the derivative of (1.1) with respect to \(\rho\) is a unit.

The local data already calculated in `toric_fiber_k3.tex` apply uniformly:

| fixed section | quadratic data | resolving check |
|---|---|---|
| four fixed \(A_1\)'s | Hessian determinant \(2a^2\) | a unit on \(S\), so the exceptional conic is smooth |
| \((0,\infty,0)\) | \(q_2=s(at-r)\) | the two factors are defined over the original base; at their intersection the cubic coefficient is \(a^2\), a unit |
| \((\infty,0,0)\) | symmetric | identical |
| \((1,1,1)\), \(a\ne1\) | Hessian determinant \(2a(a-1)\) | nondegenerate |
| \((1,1,1)\), \(a=1\) | \(q_2=-(r+t)(s+t)\) | at the common direction \((-1,-1,1)\), the cubic coefficient is \(-2\), a unit |

Thus one blow-up resolves each fixed fiber singularity. At either fixed \(A_2\), the exceptional divisor has two globally labeled components; at \((0,\infty,0)\), the component \(s=0\) is exactly the repository's \(F_1\), attached to \(C_1,C_{11}\), while \(at-r=0\) is \(F_2\), attached to \(C_3\).

The central type jump at \(a=1\) causes no hidden Weyl cover. The same blow-up resolves the generic central \(A_1\) and the special central \(A_2\). In the special fiber the exceptional conic becomes the two lines \(J_1\cup J_2\); at their intersection the radial derivative is the displayed cubic coefficient \(-2\), so the resolved fiber is regular there. The basis \(\mathcal B\) does not include the central exceptional curve in any event.

After these fixed blow-ups, the only nonsmooth fibers away from \(a=0\) are the two open branch fibers, and the total threefold \(\mathscr Y\) is nevertheless regular there by the calculation in the next section. The morphism \(\mathscr Y\to S\) is projective and flat, with smooth K3 generic fiber.

# 2. The moving node and regularity of the total threefold

In the affine chart, write

\[
f(u,v,w,a)=uv(w-1)(1-uvw)-aw(u-1)(v-1).
\]

Put

\[
D(a)=a^2-34a+1.
\]

Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic \(p\ge5\), let \(\alpha\) be either root of \(D\), and put

\[
x=\frac{\alpha-5}{12}.
\]

Then

\[
D(12x+5)=144(x^2-2x-1),
\]

so

\[
x^2-2x-1=0,
\qquad
P_\alpha=(u,v,w)=(x,x,2-x).
\]

The four values

\[
x=0,\ 1,\ 2,\ -1
\]

give respectively \(-1,-2,-1,2\) in \(x^2-2x-1\), and are therefore impossible in characteristic \(p\ge5\). Hence

\[
x\ne0,1,2,-1. \tag{2.1}
\]

At the critical point the three derivatives in \(u,v,w\) vanish, while

\[
\frac{\partial f}{\partial a}
=-w(u-1)(v-1)
=-(2-x)(x-1)^2. \tag{2.2}
\]

By (2.1), (2.2) is nonzero in the residue field, hence a unit in the local ring. The Jacobian criterion therefore gives

\[
\boxed{\mathscr Y\text{ is regular at }P_\alpha.}
\]

This is the repository-specific regular-total-space hypothesis needed in Q7904.

The branch parameter is also simple. Indeed,

\[
D'(\alpha)=2\alpha-34=24(x-1), \tag{2.3}
\]

which is a unit by (2.1). Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{disc}(D)=34^2-4=1152=2\cdot24^2,
\]

which is nonzero for every \(p\ge5\). Thus, in the henselian local ring at \(\alpha\), \(D(a)\) is a unit times \(a-\alpha\).

Finally, the logarithmic Hessian determinant already computed in the source is

\[
\frac{x-1}{2},
\]

again a unit. Hence the special fiber has one ordinary quadratic singularity and the family is an honest one-node smoothing with regular total space.

# 3. Extension and exact specialization of the nineteen classes

For each of the sixteen boundary curves in equation `eq:toric-K3-boundary-curves`, take the corresponding closed relative surface in \(\mathscr X\) and its strict transform

\[
\widetilde{\mathscr C}_i\subset\mathscr Y.
\]

Let

\[
\mathscr E_1,\ldots,\mathscr E_4
\]

be the exceptional divisors over the four persistent fixed \(A_1\) sections. At \((0,\infty,0)\), let \(\mathscr F_1\) be the exceptional component cut out on the projectivized tangent cone by \(s=0\). Define

\[
\mathscr B=
\{\widetilde{\mathscr C}_1,\ldots,
  \widetilde{\mathscr C}_{12},
  \widetilde{\mathscr C}_{14},
  \widetilde{\mathscr C}_{16},
  \mathscr E_1,\mathscr E_2,\mathscr E_3,\mathscr E_4,
  \mathscr F_1\}. \tag{3.1}
\]

Every member of (3.1) is an integral horizontal codimension-one subscheme of the regular threefold \(\mathscr Y\). A regular local ring is a UFD, and an integral codimension-one closed subscheme in a locally Noetherian UFD is an effective Cartier divisor (Stacks Project, Tags `0AG0` and `0AGA`). Hence every member of \(\mathscr B\) is a relative Cartier divisor. It is flat over any local trait in the \(a\)-line because it is integral and horizontal, so the uniformizer acts as a nonzerodivisor.

Now restrict to the henselization

\[
R=\bigl(k[a]_{(a-\alpha)}\bigr)^h,
\qquad T=\operatorname{Spec}R.
\]

The generic restrictions of (3.1) are exactly the nineteen curves called \(\mathcal B\) in `toric_fiber_k3.tex`. Their closed-fiber restrictions are the strict transforms and exceptional curves with the same labels in the fixed-ADE resolution of the branch fiber. There is no permutation or hidden quadratic descent:

* each \(C_i\) is labeled by its explicit coordinate equations;
* each \(E_i\) is the unique exceptional conic above its fixed \(A_1\) section;
* \(F_1\) is distinguished from \(F_2\) over the original base by the global factor \(s=0\) of \(s(at-r)\).

The incidence graph is unchanged at the branch parameter. In particular, the Gram matrix of the closed-fiber restrictions of \(\mathscr B\) is the repository's \(19\times19\) matrix of determinant

\[
\det(\mathscr B_\alpha)=48\ne0. \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the specialization is not merely some rank-nineteen algebraic subspace: it is **exactly the labeled rank-nineteen lattice used in the definition of \(\theta_p(\alpha)\)**.

# 4. The moving node is disjoint from the lattice

At \(P_\alpha=(x,x,2-x)\), equation (2.1) shows that \(u,v,w\) are finite and nonzero and that \(u,v,w\ne1\). Moreover,

\[
uvw=x^2(2-x)=-x,
\]

using \(x^2=2x+1\). Since \(x\ne-1\), one has \(uvw\ne1\). Therefore \(P_\alpha\) lies in precisely the open locus \(\mathcal U_\alpha\) used in the source and lies on none of the sixteen boundary curves.

Every \(\mathscr E_i\) and \(\mathscr F_1\) is supported above a fixed boundary singular section. Hence \(P_\alpha\) is disjoint from all nineteen divisors in \(\mathscr B\).

Let

\[
\pi:\widetilde Y_\alpha\longrightarrow Y_\alpha
\]

be the minimal resolution of the remaining open \(A_1\), with exceptional curve \(E_\alpha\). Since the nineteen curves avoid the center of \(\pi\), their strict transforms are their ordinary pullbacks and

\[
E_\alpha\cdot B=0
\qquad(B\in\mathcal B_\alpha). \tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently the branch Gram matrix is block diagonal:

\[
\det(\mathcal B_\alpha\cup\{E_\alpha\})
=E_\alpha^2\det(\mathcal B_\alpha)
=(-2)\cdot48=-96, \tag{4.2}
\]

which is exactly the determinant recorded in `toric_fiber_k3.tex`.

# 5. Identification of the rank-two complement

Let \(\bar\eta\) and \(\bar s\) be geometric generic and special points of \(T\), and abbreviate

\[
H_\eta=H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(Y_{\bar\eta},\mathbf Q_\ell),
\qquad
H_0=H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(Y_{\bar s},\mathbf Q_\ell).
\]

Let \(L_\eta\subset H_\eta\) be the span of the nineteen generic classes in \(\mathscr B\), and let \(L_0\subset H_0\) be the span of their closed-fiber restrictions. Compatibility of cycle classes with specialization identifies \(L_0\) with \(L_\eta\), and the determinant (3.2) shows that both have rank nineteen. Put

\[
V=L_\eta^\perp.
\]

For a regular proper one-node degeneration, Deligne's Picard--Lefschetz theorem gives a Frobenius-equivariant exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow H_0
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{sp}}
H_\eta
\longrightarrow
\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathrm{van}}
\longrightarrow0, \tag{5.1}
\]

where the final term denotes the one-dimensional vanishing-cycle representation and tame inertia acts on it by \(-1\). The image of specialization is the inertia-invariant subspace:

\[
\mathrm{sp}(H_0)=H_\eta^I. \tag{5.2}
\]

This is SGA 7 II, Exposé XV, Théorème 3.4(i)--(iii); a modern K3 formulation is Costa--Elsenhans--Jahnel, *Research in Number Theory* **6** (2020), Article 27, Theorem 2.21, Corollary 2.23, and Proposition 2.24.

Since all nineteen divisors extend over \(T\), their classes are inertia invariant. Intersecting (5.2) with \(L_\eta^\perp\) gives the canonical Frobenius-equivariant identification

\[
V^I
\cong
L_0^\perp\cap H_0. \tag{5.3}
\]

On the other hand, the minimal resolution of the special fiber has the exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow H_0
\xrightarrow{\pi^*}
H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(\widetilde Y_{\alpha,\bar k},\mathbf Q_\ell)
\longrightarrow
H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(E_{\alpha,\bar k},\mathbf Q_\ell)
\longrightarrow0. \tag{5.4}
\]

The last term is \(\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)\). Surjectivity can be seen directly: the class \([E_\alpha]\) restricts to \(c_1(\mathcal O_{E_\alpha}(E_\alpha))\), whose degree is \(E_\alpha^2=-2\). Thus

\[
H^2(\widetilde Y_\alpha)
=
\pi^*H_0\oplus\mathbf Q_\ell[E_\alpha],
\qquad
\pi^*H_0=[E_\alpha]^\perp. \tag{5.5}
\]

Equation (5.4) is also the immediate Leray/proper-base-change calculation; see SGA 4 III, Exposé XII, Théorème 5.1, or the Stacks Project, Tag `095T`.

Using (4.1) and (5.5), one obtains

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(\pi^*L_0\oplus\mathbf Q_\ell[E_\alpha]\bigr)^\perp
&=
\pi^*\bigl(L_0^\perp\cap H_0\bigr)\\
&\cong V^I. \tag{5.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

The left side of (5.6) is exactly the rank-two orthogonal complement on which `toric_fiber_k3.tex` defines \(\theta_p(\alpha)\). The right side is exactly the inertia-invariant rank-two stalk whose trace is \(t_p(\alpha)\). Hence

\[
\boxed{t_p(\alpha)=\theta_p(\alpha).} \tag{5.7}
\]

For an explicit trace check, let \(k=\mathbf F_q\), use geometric Frobenius, and assume \(\alpha\in k\). All nineteen divisor classes and \(E_\alpha\) are defined over \(k\), so their untwisted \(H^2\)-eigenvalue is \(q\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
t_q(\alpha)
&=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_q\mid H_0)-19q,\\
\theta_q(\alpha)
&=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_q\mid H^2(\widetilde Y_\alpha))-20q.
\end{aligned}
\]

Sequence (5.4) gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_q\mid H^2(\widetilde Y_\alpha))
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_q\mid H_0)+q,
\]

so the two traces agree term by term.

## No vanishing-line/ex\-ceptional-line identification

Nothing above identifies the quotient in (5.1) with \(\mathbf Q_\ell[E_\alpha]\). In fact such an identification is false over the original trait. Étale-locally the moving smoothing has the form

\[
xy-z^2=u(a-\alpha),
\qquad u\in R^\times.
\]

A simultaneous resolution appears only after the ramified \(A_1\) Weyl cover

\[
s^2=u(a-\alpha),
\]

and the two choices obtained by blowing up \((x,z-s)\) or \((x,z+s)\) are exchanged by \(s\mapsto-s\). Before that cover, the vanishing line has nontrivial quadratic tame inertia, whereas the special-fiber exceptional line is a Tate line. This is the standard \(A_1\) instance of Artin's simultaneous-resolution construction. The equality (5.7) avoids this noncanonical channel by comparing the two rank-two spaces through \(H^2(Y_{\bar s})\).

# 6. Both roots and all primes \(p\ge5\)

The branch roots are

\[
\alpha_\pm=17\pm12\sqrt2,
\qquad
x_\pm=1\pm\sqrt2.
\]

All preceding calculations are symmetric in the two roots. Moreover:

* \(\operatorname{disc}(D)=1152=2\cdot24^2\), so the roots are distinct in every characteristic \(p\ge5\).
* \(D(0)=1\), so neither root is zero.
* \(D(1)=-32\ne0\) for \(p\ge5\), so a branch root never coincides with the central \(a=1\) fiber.
* The map \(x\mapsto12x+5\) is injective for \(p\ge5\), so each branch fiber has exactly one open node.
* The open-node checks \(x\ne0,1,2,-1\) rule out every collision with the boundary and with the excluded torus loci.
* If one root lies in \(\mathbf F_p\), both do; if \(2\) is nonsquare, they form a conjugate pair over \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\). The geometric argument works after base change to the residue field of either root. In the nonsplit case there is simply no \(\mathbf F_p\)-rational branch parameter in the pointwise sum.
* Characteristics \(2\) and \(3\) are exactly the bad discriminant/denominator cases; they are already excluded by the theorem's assumption \(p\ge5\).

Thus there are no discriminant-zero, coincident-root, central-exceptional, or boundary-exceptional cases left to treat for \(p\ge5\).

```latex
\begin{proposition}[Invariant cycles at an open branch fiber]
\label{prop:toric-K3-branch-invariant-cycles}
Let $p\geq5$, let $k/\mathbb F_p$ be finite, and let
$\alpha\in k$ satisfy
\[
 D(\alpha):=\alpha^2-34\alpha+1=0.
\]
Let
\[
 R=(k[a]_{(a-\alpha)})^h,
 \qquad T=\operatorname {Spec}R,
\]
and pull the family~\eqref{eq:toric-K3-homogeneous} to~$T$.
Resolve only the seven fixed boundary ADE sections listed in
\eqref{eq:toric-K3-fixed-singularities}, and denote the resulting
projective family by $\mathscr Y\to T$.  Its generic fiber is a smooth
K3 surface and its special fiber $Y_\alpha$ has one remaining open
ordinary double point.  Let
\[
 \pi:\widetilde Y_\alpha\longrightarrow Y_\alpha
\]
be its minimal resolution and let $E_\alpha$ be the exceptional curve.

Let $L_\eta$ be the span in generic $H^2$ of
\[
 \mathcal B=\{\widetilde C_1,\ldots,\widetilde C_{12},
 \widetilde C_{14},\widetilde C_{16},E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4,F_1\},
\]
and put $V=L_\eta^\perp$.  Then the members of $\mathcal B$ extend over
$T$ as relative Cartier divisors, their specializations span the same
rank-$19$ lattice $L_0$ in $H^2(Y_\alpha)$, and the moving node is
disjoint from all of them.  There is a canonical, Frobenius-equivariant
isometry
\begin{equation}
 V^I\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \left(\pi^*L_0\oplus\mathbb Q_\ell[E_\alpha]\right)^\perp
 \subset H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(
       \widetilde Y_{\alpha,\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell).
 \label{eq:toric-K3-branch-comparison}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if $t_k(\alpha)$ is the geometric-Frobenius trace on the
inertia-invariant stalk of the rank-$3$ local system $V$, then
\[
 \boxed{t_k(\alpha)=\theta_k(\alpha).}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 f(u,v,w,a)=uv(w-1)(1-uvw)-aw(u-1)(v-1).
\]
The fixed-ADE model is obtained explicitly as follows.  Over
$\operatorname {Spec}\mathbb Z[1/6,a,a^{-1}]$, blow up the ambient
$(\mathbb P^1)^3$ along the disjoint union of the seven fixed singular
sections and take the strict transform of the hypersurface.  If a local
equation along one of these sections is
$F=q_2+q_3+\cdots$, then in the blow-up chart $x_i=\rho X_i$ its strict
transform is
\[
 q_2(X)+\rho q_3(X)+\rho^2q_4(X)+\cdots=0.
\]
For the four fixed $A_1$ sections, the quadratic Hessian determinant is
$2a^2$, a unit.  At $(0,\infty,0)$ the tangent cone is
$s(at-r)$ and its cubic coefficient in the common kernel direction is
$a^2$; the other fixed $A_2$ is symmetric.  Thus the two exceptional
components are defined over the original base, and the factor $s=0$
gives the labeled component $F_1$.  At $(1,1,1)$ the quadratic
 determinant is $2a(a-1)$ away from $a=1$; at $a=1$ the tangent cone is
$-(r+t)(s+t)$ and the cubic coefficient in its common kernel direction
is $-2$.  Hence the same blow-up resolves the central $A_1$ and its
$A_2$ specialization.  This gives a regular simultaneous resolution of
all fixed ADE sections without a base change.

Write
\[
 x=\frac{\alpha-5}{12}.
\]
Since
\[
 D(12x+5)=144(x^2-2x-1),
\]
one has $x^2-2x-1=0$, and the moving critical point is
\[
 P_\alpha=(x,x,2-x).
\]
The values $x=0,1,2,-1$ are impossible for $p\geq5$.  At $P_\alpha$,
\[
 \frac{\partial f}{\partial a}
 =-(2-x)(x-1)^2\ne0.
\]
Thus the total threefold is regular at $P_\alpha$.  Moreover
\[
 D'(\alpha)=24(x-1)\ne0,
\]
and the logarithmic Hessian determinant is $(x-1)/2\ne0$; hence the
special fiber has exactly one ordinary quadratic singularity there.

Take the relative strict transforms of the sixteen curves in
\eqref{eq:toric-K3-boundary-curves}, the four relative exceptional
curves $E_1,\ldots,E_4$, and the component $F_1$ just labeled.  The
nineteen members corresponding to $\mathcal B$ are integral horizontal
codimension-one subschemes of the regular total threefold, hence relative
Cartier divisors.  Here we use that regular local rings are UFDs and that
an integral codimension-one subscheme in a locally Noetherian UFD is an
effective Cartier divisor.\footnote{The Stacks Project, Tags
\texttt{0AG0} and \texttt{0AGA}.}
Their generic and special restrictions have the same labels and the same
intersection graph.  In particular their special Gram determinant is
$48$, so they specialize to exactly the rank-$19$ lattice $L_0$ used in
the definition of $\theta_k(\alpha)$.

Furthermore $x\ne0,1,2$ and
\[
 uvw=x^2(2-x)=-x\ne1,
\]
so $P_\alpha$ lies in the open torus and on none of the boundary curves.
It is also disjoint from every exceptional divisor over a fixed boundary
section.  Consequently
\[
 E_\alpha\cdot L_0=0,
 \qquad
 \det(L_0\oplus\mathbb Z[E_\alpha])=(-2)\cdot48=-96,
\]
in agreement with the branch lattice calculated above.

Let $\bar\eta$ and $\bar s$ be geometric generic and special points of
$T$.  Picard--Lefschetz theory for a regular one-node degeneration gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow H^2(Y_{\bar s},\mathbb Q_\ell)
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{sp}}
 H^2(Y_{\bar\eta},\mathbb Q_\ell)
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb Q_\ell(-1)\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathrm{van}}
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
and identifies the image of specialization with the inertia invariants.
\footnote{P. Deligne, ``La formule de Picard--Lefschetz,'' in
\emph{Groupes de monodromie en g\'eom\'etrie alg\'ebrique (SGA 7 II)},
Lecture Notes in Math. \textbf{340}, Springer, 1973, Exp.~XV,
Th.~3.4(i)--(iii).  See also E. Costa, A.-S. Elsenhans and J. Jahnel,
``On the distribution of the Picard ranks of the reductions of a K3
surface,'' \emph{Res. Number Theory} \textbf{6} (2020), Art.~27,
Th.~2.21, Cor.~2.23 and Prop.~2.24,
doi:10.1007/s40993-020-00204-2.}
Since the nineteen divisor classes extend over $T$, this yields
\[
 V^I\cong L_0^\perp\cap H^2(Y_{\bar s},\mathbb Q_\ell).
\]

For the resolution of the special fiber, proper base change and Leray give
\[
 0\longrightarrow H^2(Y_{\bar s},\mathbb Q_\ell)
 \xrightarrow{\pi^*}
 H^2(\widetilde Y_{\alpha,\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell)
 \longrightarrow H^2(E_{\alpha,\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
\footnote{M. Artin, A. Grothendieck and J.-L. Verdier,
\emph{Th\'eorie des topos et cohomologie \'etale des sch\'emas (SGA 4
III)}, Lecture Notes in Math. \textbf{305}, Springer, 1973, Exp.~XII,
Th.~5.1; equivalently, The Stacks Project, Tag \texttt{095T}.}
The class $[E_\alpha]$ maps nontrivially to the last term because
$E_\alpha^2=-2$, and therefore
\[
 H^2(\widetilde Y_{\alpha,\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell)
 =\pi^*H^2(Y_{\bar s},\mathbb Q_\ell)
  \oplus\mathbb Q_\ell[E_\alpha],
 \qquad
 \pi^*H^2(Y_{\bar s})=[E_\alpha]^\perp.
\]
As $E_\alpha$ is disjoint from $L_0$, this proves
\eqref{eq:toric-K3-branch-comparison}.

With geometric Frobenius, every $k$-rational divisor class contributes
$|k|$ in untwisted $H^2$.  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 t_k(\alpha)
 &=\operatorname {Tr}(\operatorname {Frob}_k\mid H^2(Y_{\bar s}))
   -19|k|,\\
 \theta_k(\alpha)
 &=\operatorname {Tr}(\operatorname {Frob}_k\mid
      H^2(\widetilde Y_{\alpha,\bar k}))-20|k|.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The resolution sequence adds precisely the eigenvalue $|k|$, proving
$t_k(\alpha)=\theta_k(\alpha)$.

No identification of the vanishing line with $[E_\alpha]$ is being made.
Indeed, \'etale-locally the moving smoothing is
$xy-z^2=u(a-\alpha)$ with $u\in R^\times$, and a simultaneous resolution
requires the ramified Weyl cover $s^2=u(a-\alpha)$; the two resolutions
are exchanged by $s\mapsto-s$.\footnote{M. Artin, ``Algebraic
construction of Brieskorn's resolutions,'' \emph{J. Algebra}
\textbf{29} (1974), 330--348,
doi:10.1016/0021-8693(74)90102-1.}
The comparison above instead passes canonically through the cohomology of
the singular special fiber.

Finally,
\[
 \operatorname {disc}(D)=1152=2\cdot24^2,
 \qquad D(0)=1,
```
