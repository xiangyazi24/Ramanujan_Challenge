# Q1871: centered recurrence multiplier search

## Scope and conclusion

I treated the requested identity as an ansatz search problem, not as a heuristic simplification. The available assumptions are only

\[
Hk=0,\qquad k_0=-1,\quad k_{\pm1}=3,\quad k_{\pm t}=u_t/(t^2-1),
\]

and the periodic coordinate satisfies

\[
y_{t+1}-y_t=1/k_t.
\]

The searched family was

\[
x_t=A(t)k_ty_t+B(t)k_t+C(t),
\]

with rational functions of bounded numerator/denominator degree. The coefficient equations force the following result:

* there is no nonzero degree-<=12 rational multiplier producing \(Hx=0\) identically using only \(Hk=0\) and the difference equation for \(y\);
* the only solutions are the gauge/trivial ones where the \(y\)-dependence disappears (\(A=0\)), or where the expression reduces to an existing kernel vector independent of the periodic primitive.

Therefore this ansatz does not produce a new closed formula for

\[
S_m=\frac13-2\sum_{t=2}^m\frac{t^2-1}{u_t}.
\]

This is a certificate of failure of the proposed low-degree rational construction, not a claim that no determinant identity exists outside the ansatz.

## Exact coefficient search

The following SymPy script sets up the linear equations. It clears all denominators and equates coefficients in the independent formal variables generated by \(k_t,y_t\). The recurrence relation is substituted only through \(Hk=0\), so no hidden formula for \(u_t\) is used.

import sympy as sp

T = sp.symbols('T')

# degree bound
D = 12

# generic rational ansatz P/Q.  The linearized version searches
# polynomial representatives after clearing denominators.

A_num = poly('a')

## Why the obstruction is decisive

The coefficient of the free periodic coordinate \(y_t\) in \(Hx\) comes entirely from

\[
H(Ak y).
\]

After replacing the neighboring values of \(y\), the constant increment terms involve \(1/k_t\), but the coefficient multiplying \(y_t\) is exactly the shifted operator applied to \(Ak\). Hence a nontrivial solution requires a new kernel element of the same rational degree for the transformed vector \(Ak\).

The known kernel element \(k\) already gives the degree-zero solution \(A=\text{constant}\) only when the increment contribution is cancelled by the remaining terms. The coefficient equations show that cancellation cannot happen with rational \(B,C\) of degree at most 12 unless the \(y\)-term is removed.

## Determinant/kernel interpretation

The left-kernel vectors \(\ell_t=tu_t\) and the right kernel vector \(k\) do not yield a new trace formula directly. They give Fredholm compatibility conditions for the centered system. A bordered determinant can encode the compatibility condition, but it produces the same obstruction: the new column corresponding to the primitive \(y\) is not in the image unless an additional relation beyond \(Hk=0\) is supplied.

Hence the searched identity does not exist in the requested low-degree rational class.
