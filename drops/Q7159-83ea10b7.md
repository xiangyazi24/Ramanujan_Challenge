# Audit of the proposed P32 cluster construction

## Verdict

The construction has a genuine obstruction in item (3). Items (1) and (2) are essentially correct after harmless constant checks, but the claimed global difference bound

\[
 A_Z(h)\le 3(h-1)
\]

is not proved by the proposed counting argument and in fact the argument misses a multiplicity factor of order \(R\). I do not see a valid repair with the stated periodic word. Therefore the construction does **not** currently certify an \(\Omega(H^2)\) extremizer.

The deterministic upper bound from the previous correction remains valid:

\[
E_H\le \sum_{s\le H}A(s)\le \frac{3H(H-1)}2.
\]

This construction does not establish sharpness.

---

## 1. Geometry, no adjacency, palindrome, and size

Let

\[
g_i=3m+(i\bmod m),\qquad 0\le i<mR,
\]

and let \(P_j\) be the partial sums. The smallest gap is \(3m\), hence there is no adjacency because

\[
3m>1.
\]

The reflected copy

\[
 p-1-P_j
\]

is exactly the image under \(x\mapsto p-1-x\), so the resulting set is palindromic.

Also

\[
S=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(3m+i)=3m^2+\frac{m(m-1)}2
=\frac{m(7m-1)}2,
\]

so

\[
L=RS.
\]

With \(R=m/100\),

\[
4L+1=O(m^3).
\]

By Bertrand we can choose \(p\) with

\[
4L+1<p<2(4L+1),
\]

and therefore

\[
p=\Theta(m^3).
\]

Since

\[
H^2=(12m)^2=144m^2,
\]

we have \(p>H^2\) for sufficiently large \(m\).

---

## 2. Number of short consecutive quadruples

The construction has two copies of the length \(mR\) chain. Any three consecutive gaps satisfy

\[
g_i+g_{i+1}+g_{i+2}<4(4m)=16m.
\]

Because

\[
H=12m,
\]

this inequality is not quite enough: the statement "any three successive gaps sum < H" is false with the given constants.

The maximum gap is

\[
4m-1,
\]

so three successive gaps can be as large as

\[
(4m-1)+(4m-2)+(4m-3)=12m-6<H.
\]

Thus the intended estimate is valid; the looser bound above was simply too crude.

There are

\[
2(mR-2)
\]

internal four-point windows. Since

\[
mR=m^2/100,
\]

this equals

\[
\frac{m^2}{50}+O(m)=\Omega(H^2).
\]

The centered window is removed automatically because the two halves are separated by the midpoint reflection and these short windows stay inside one half.

---

## 3. Failure of the proposed difference-cap proof

The key claimed estimate is:

> For a within-half block of length \(\ell=qm+r\), the residue starts repeat at most \(R+1\) times, and this is absorbed into the desired bound.

This is the invalid step.

A fixed block length \(\ell\) occurs at every one of the \(mR-\ell\) possible starting positions. Writing the sum as

\[
qS+F_r(i\bmod m)
\]

only controls the number of possible residues \(i\bmod m\). It does not eliminate the repetitions over the \(R\) periods.

For example, consider \(\ell=m\). Every complete period has the same sum

\[
S.
\]

Therefore the difference

\[
P_{i+m}-P_i=S
\]

occurs for every admissible start \(i\) in the half-chain. The number of such starts is

\[
mR-m+1= m(R-1)+1.
\]

Since \(R=m/100\), this is

\[
\Theta(m^2).
\]

But the required cap at \(h=S\) is only

\[
3(S-1)=\Theta(m^2).
\]

So this single difference is close to saturating the bound, and any additional contributions from the reflected half or endpoint conventions must be controlled exactly. The proposed argument gives no such control.

More importantly, for lengths \(qm+r\), the same phenomenon persists: the \(R\) copies of a residue pattern contribute a factor of approximately \(R\). The statement

\[
F_r \text{ takes values at most twice}
\]

only gives a bound for one period. It does not imply the required global multiplicity bound after multiplying by the number of periods.

---

## 4. Cross-half differences

Cross-half differences do not rescue the argument. A cross-half difference has the form

\[
(p-1-P_a)-P_b.
\]

Their multiplicity is not automatically bounded by \(mR+1\); one needs a separate uniqueness argument for sums

\[
P_a+P_b.
\]

The reflection symmetry actually doubles many such relations. The proposed estimate is therefore also incomplete here.

---

## 5. What is actually proved

The construction proves:

* palindrome;
* no adjacent points;
* \(p>H^2\);
* \(\Omega(H^2)\) short internal quadruples before checking the difference caps.

It does **not** prove:

\[
A_Z(h)\le 3(h-1)\quad\forall h<p.
\]

Hence it is not a valid extremal example.

A successful \(\Omega(H^2)\) construction would need a packet mechanism where every short gap appears only \(O(h)\) times globally, not merely inside a single period. This likely requires either a Sidon-type separation between periods or abandoning exact periodicity.

Therefore the honest conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Only the deterministic upper bound is established; quadratic sharpness remains open.}}
\]
