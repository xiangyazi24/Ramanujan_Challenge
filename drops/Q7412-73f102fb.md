# Singularity--Pade audit of the polylog moment gcd

## Conclusion

The proposed route does not currently give an \(e^{o(n)}\) bound. The obstruction is not the singular expansion itself; it is the arithmetic height of the Padé/Hermite--Padé certificates. The available G-function and Padé theorems control denominator growth at roughly factorial scale, which is too large for the required pointwise gcd estimate.

## 1. Singular expansion

The Apéry generating function satisfies the third order Picard--Fuchs equation attached to the Apéry family. At the dominant singularity

\[
\rho=(17+12\sqrt2)^{-1},
\]

it has a regular singular point with a repeated exponent. The local basis has the form

\[
B(t)=A_0(t)+A_1(t)(1-t/\rho)\log(1-t/\rho)+A_2(t)(1-t/\rho)\log^2(1-t/\rho),
\]

with analytic \(A_i\) near ρ and \(A_2(\rho)\neq0\). Consequently coefficient asymptotics have the shape

\[
b_n=\rho^{-n}\left(\alpha_1 n^{-2}+\alpha_2n^{-2}\log n+O(n^{-3}\log n)\right).
\]

Since \(\operatorname{Li}_s(t)\) is analytic at ρ,

\[
B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t)
\]

has the same singular exponents. Writing

\[
\operatorname{Li}_s(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\ell_{s,j}(1-t/\rho)^j+O((1-t/\rho)^m),
\]

singularity transfer gives

\[
R_s(n)=b_n\left(\ell_{s,0}+\frac{d_{s,1}}n+\cdots+\frac{d_{s,m-1}}{n^{m-1}}+O(n^{-m})\right).
\]

The asymptotic remainder is useful analytically, but it does not control rational denominator cancellation.

## 2. Arithmetic height obstruction

A subtraction of \(m\) asymptotic terms produces a linear form

\[
\Lambda_s(n)=R_s(n)-\sum_{j<m}d_{s,j}b_n n^{-j}.
\]

After clearing denominators one must account for:

* the Apéry recurrence denominators from shifted expressions;
* denominators of Taylor coefficients of \(\operatorname{Li}_s\) at \(\rho\);
* denominators introduced by powers of \(n^{-1}\).

For G-functions, denominator-growth results imply only exponential/factorial-type bounds for coefficient common denominators. A typical bound is of the form

\[
D_N\leq \exp(O(N\log N)),
\]

not \(\exp(o(N))\).

Taking \(m\) growing with \(n\) therefore gives a remainder potentially as small as desired, but the corresponding integer linear form has height at least

\[
\exp(\Theta(m\log m))
\]

in the available constructions. Choosing \(m\asymp n\) makes the height \(e^{\Theta(n\log n)}\), which dominates the desired gcd scale.

## 3. Applicable theorems

The closest relevant results are:

* Siegel--Shidlovsky theory for E-functions: gives algebraic independence statements for E-functions under hypotheses not satisfied here; Apéry's generating function is a G-function, not an E-function.
* Chudnovsky-André G-function denominator theory: gives arithmetic control of coefficients of G-functions but not lower bounds for the specific simultaneous polylogarithmic linear forms needed here.
* Baker's theorem on linear forms in logarithms: applies to logarithms of algebraic numbers. It does not apply to the vector

\[
(\operatorname{Li}_1(\rho),\operatorname{Li}_2(\rho),\operatorname{Li}_3(\rho))
\]

because higher polylogarithms are not covered by Baker's logarithmic form theorem.
* General Padé/Hermite--Padé results for G-functions give construction and analytic error estimates, but not the required four-dimensional lower bound.

No known theorem gives a pointwise lower bound strong enough for

\[
a_0+a_1\operatorname{Li}_1(\rho)+a_2\operatorname{Li}_2(\rho)+a_3\operatorname{Li}_3(\rho).
\]

## 4. Why the gcd argument does not close

Suppose

\[
g_n=\gcd(L_n,U_1(n),U_2(n),U_3(n)).
\]

An integer divisor \(g_n\) would yield congruences after dividing by \(g_n\), but turning them into a contradiction requires a lower bound for a simultaneous linear form in the three polylogarithmic constants.

The missing inequality would have to beat the denominator height:

\[
|\Lambda|>\exp(-o(n))
\]

for coefficients of the size generated by the Padé construction. Existing G-function theory does not supply this.

The precise obstruction is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Pad\'e remainder }\ll e^{-cnm}\text{ is accompanied by coefficient height }e^{\Theta(m\log m)}.}
\]

At the natural choice \(m\asymp n\), this gives an arithmetic cost \(e^{\Theta(n\log n)}\), so no sublinear logarithmic height bound for the gcd follows.

Thus the singularity--Padé route is analytically compatible with the conjecture but does not currently prove

\[
\log\gcd(L_n,U_1,U_2,U_3)=o(n).
\]

A successful proof would require a new arithmetic input: either a strong irrationality measure for the vector of polylogarithm values at ρ, or a direct denominator-cancellation theorem specific to the Apéry recurrence.
