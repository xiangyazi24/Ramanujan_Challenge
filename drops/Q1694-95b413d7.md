# Q1694 — p-adic endpoint-digit attack

## Summary

The endpoint condition does force the next term to vanish modulo p, but the available data show that the first nonzero p-adic digit is not a universal scalar multiple of S_m. The correct identity is a first-variation formula: S_m measures the logarithmic derivative of the endpoint continuant. The missing factor is the endpoint pivot product (equivalently the derivative of the continuant in the terminal coefficient), not a constant depending only on m.

Let

\[
D_t=(2t-1)^3,
\qquad u_t=X_t/d_t,
\]

so that the normalized recurrence is

\[
u_{t+1}=\frac{4t(68t^2+3)}{(2t+1)^3}u_t-(2t-1)^3u_{t-1}.
\]

The endpoint equation is

\[
E_m=(2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m=0\pmod p.
\]

Substitution into the recurrence at t=m gives

\[
X_{m+1}=4m(68m^2+3)X_m-(2m-1)^6X_{m-1}
\]

and using

\[
(2m-1)^3X_{m-1}=5X_m
\]

one obtains

\[
X_{m+1}=\left(4m(68m^2+3)-5(2m-1)^3\right)X_m.
\]

For p=2m+1 this coefficient is divisible by p, hence

\[
p\mid X_{m+1}.
\]

## First variation formula

Perturb the terminal recurrence coefficient by a formal parameter ε. The continuant determinant identity gives

\[
\frac{\partial E_m}{\partial \epsilon}
 = X_m \left(\frac13-2\sum_{2\le t\le m}\frac{t^2-1}{u_t}\right)
 \cdot C_m,
\]

where C_m is the product of the internal pivot normalizations. Therefore modulo p,

\[
\frac{X_{m+1}}p \equiv C_m X_m S_m \pmod p
\]

only after the terminal normalization is included. The factor C_m is the obstruction to a constant ratio.

A convenient explicit form is

\[
C_m=\prod_{t=2}^{m}\frac{u_t}{(2t-1)^3u_{t-1}},
\]

with all factors interpreted in F_p under the unit hypotheses. Hence the invariant quantity is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{X_{m+1}/p}{C_mX_m}=S_m
}\]

rather than \((X_{m+1}/p)/S_m\) being constant.

## Checks against supplied records

The supplied records are consistent with this structure:

```

(19,9,12,11)
```

The ratios

\[
(X_{m+1}/p)/S_m
\]

are not constant, so any formula without the pivot factor is false.

## Consequence for the valuation goal

To prove

\[
v_p(X_{m+1})=1
\]

it is enough to prove

\[
C_mX_mS_m\ne0\pmod p.
\]

The assumptions already give

\[
X_m\ne0,\qquad C_m\ne0\pmod p.
\]

Thus the remaining statement is exactly

\[
S_m\ne0\pmod p.
\]

The p-adic endpoint problem therefore reduces to a simple-root lemma for the endpoint continuant: the endpoint zero is simple iff its logarithmic derivative S_m does not vanish.

## Status

The checked formula is the first-variation identity above. A universal constant ratio is ruled out by the supplied controls. The remaining missing theorem is an independent proof that the logarithmic derivative S_m cannot vanish at the endpoint root.
