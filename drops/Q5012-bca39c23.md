Let

\[
U_{n,k}:=\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{n}\binom{n+2k}{n},\qquad
S_n:=\sum_{k=0}^n U_{n,k},
\]

so that the AESZ #209 holomorphic coefficient in the question is

\[
A_n=\binom{2n}{n}S_n.
\]

There is an exact and unexpectedly simple answer to the Whipple question:

\[
\boxed{
S_n={}_5F_4\!\left(\begin{matrix}
-n,-n,n+1,\frac{n+1}{2},\frac{n+2}{2}\\
1,1,\frac12,1
\end{matrix};1\right)
=(-1)^n{}_4F_3\!\left(\begin{matrix}
-n,n+1,n+1,n+1\\1,1,1

This is precisely a **small-slope Dauguet--Zudilin Whipple pair**.  The large-slope specialization printed in their 2014 paper is not the one needed here; the correct slopes here are only \(0,1,2\).

r_n^{(2)}=S_n\zeta(2)-p_n^{(2)},\qquad
r_n^{(3)}=S_n\zeta(3)-p_n^{(3)},

\[
B_n:=\binom{2n}{n}\bigl(p_n^{(2)}+p_n^{(3)}\bigr)

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{B_n}{A_n}=\zeta(2)+\zeta(3).

The saddle cubics of the two Barnes constructions both map to

## 1. Exact hypergeometric form of the AESZ #209 sum

### Theorem 1: the terminating \({}_5F_4\)

For every \(n\ge 0\),

\[
S_n={}_5F_4\!\left(\begin{matrix}
-n,-n,n+1,\frac{n+1}{2},\frac{n+2}{2}\\
1,1,\frac12,1
\end{matrix};1\right).
\]

### Proof

Use

\[
\binom nk=(-1)^k\frac{(-n)_k}{k!},\qquad
\binom{n+k}{n}=\frac{(n+1)_k}{k!},
\]

and the duplication identity

\[
\binom{n+2k}{n}
=\frac{(n+1)_{2k}}{(2k)!}
=\frac{\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)_k
       \left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)_k}
      {\left(\frac12\right)_k k!}.
\]

Thus

\[
U_{n,k}
=
\frac{(-n)_k^2(n+1)_k
\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)_k
\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)_k}
{(1)_k(1)_k\left(\frac12\right)_k(1)_k}\frac1{k!},
\]

which is exactly the displayed terminating \({}_5F_4(1)\).  \(\square\)

### Theorem 2: the exact Whipple partner

For every \(n\ge 0\),

\[
\boxed{
S_n=(-1)^n{}_4F_3\!\left(\begin{matrix}
-n,n+1,n+1,n+1\\1,1,1
\end{matrix};1\right).
}
\]

### Proof

Use Whipple's terminating transformation

\[
{}_4F_3\!\left(\begin{matrix}
f,1+f-h,h-a,b\\h,1+f+a-h,g
\end{matrix};1\right)
=
\frac{(g-f)_N}{(g)_N}
{}_5F_4\!\left(\begin{matrix}
a,b,1+f-g,\frac f2,\frac{f+1}{2}\\
h,1+f+a-h,\frac{1+f+b-g}{2},\frac{2+f+b-g}{2}
\end{matrix};1\right),
\]

where \(b=-N\).  Substitute

\[
a=b=-n,\qquad f=n+1,\qquad g=h=1,\qquad N=n.
\]

The prefactor is

\[
\frac{(1-(n+1))_n}{(1)_n}=\frac{(-n)_n}{n!}=(-1)^n,
\]

and the right-hand \({}_5F_4\) is the one in Theorem 1.  \(\square\)

### The corresponding Dauguet--Zudilin parameter sets

On the \(\zeta(2)\) side take

\[
a_1=a_2=a_3=a_4=n+1,\qquad
b_1=b_2=b_3=1,\qquad b_4=2n+2.
\]

On the \(\zeta(3)\) side take

\[
\widehat a_0=2n+2,\qquad
\widehat a_1=\widehat a_2=\widehat a_3=n+1,
\]

\[
\widehat b_0=n+2,\qquad
\widehat b_1=1,\qquad
\widehat b_2=\widehat b_3=2n+2.
\]

These are the small-slope parameter trajectories whose coefficients coincide by Whipple.

## 2. Explicit \(\zeta(2)\) companion

For \(n\ge1\), define the Dauguet--Zudilin rational function

\[
R_n^{(2)}(t)
:=\frac{(t+1)_n^3}{(n!)^2(t+n+1)_{n+1}}.
\]

Its poles are at \(t=-(n+1+j)\), \(0\le j\le n\).  The residues are

\[
\boxed{
C_{n,j}:=
\left.(t+n+1+j)R_n^{(2)}(t)\right|_{t=-(n+1+j)}
=(-1)^{n+j}\binom nj\binom{n+j}{j}^3.
}
\]

The Whipple identity above is equivalently

\[
\sum_{j=0}^n C_{n,j}=S_n.
\]

Because \(R_n^{(2)}(t)\) has polynomial part of degree \(2n-1\), write its unique Newton expansion as

\[
R_n^{(2)}(t)
=
\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{C_{n,j}}{t+n+1+j}
+
\sum_{\ell=0}^{2n-1}\gamma_{\ell,n}\binom{t+n}{\ell}.
\]

The coefficients are completely explicit finite differences.  If

\[
P_n(t):=R_n^{(2)}(t)-
\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{C_{n,j}}{t+n+1+j},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{\ell,n}
=\Delta^\ell\bigl[y\mapsto P_n(y-n)\bigr]_{y=0}
=\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-r}\binom{\ell}{r}P_n(r-n).
}
\]

Normalize the harmless overall sign in the Barnes integral so that the coefficient of \(\zeta(2)\) is the positive number \(S_n\).  Dauguet--Zudilin Proposition 1, or a direct residue calculation, then gives

\[
\boxed{
r_n^{(2)}=S_n\zeta(2)-p_n^{(2)},}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
p_n^{(2)}
=
\sum_{j=0}^n C_{n,j}H_j^{(2)}
-
\sum_{\ell=0}^{2n-1}\frac{(-1)^\ell}{\ell+1}\gamma_{\ell,n}.}
\]

Here and below

\[
H_m^{(r)}:=\sum_{a=1}^m\frac1{a^r},\qquad H_0^{(r)}=0.
\]

For \(n=0\), set \(S_0=1\), \(p_0^{(2)}=0\); this is the natural limiting normalization.

The second finite sum is not cosmetic: it is the contribution of the polynomial part of \(R_n^{(2)}\) in the integer-valued Newton basis.  A deformation of only one binomial factor generally misses precisely this boundary term.

## 3. Explicit \(\zeta(3)\) companion

Define

\[
\widehat R_n(t)
:=
\frac{(2t+n+2)_n(t+1)_n}{(t+n+1)_{n+1}^2}.
\]

At the double pole \(t=-(n+1+j)\), let

\[
A_{n,j}:=
\left.(t+n+1+j)^2\widehat R_n(t)ight|_{t=-(n+1+j)}.
\]

A factorial simplification gives the striking identity

\[
\boxed{A_{n,j}=U_{n,j}.}
\]

Let

\[
B_{n,j}:=
\left.\frac{d}{dt}
\left((t+n+1+j)^2\widehat R_n(t)\right)
\right|_{t=-(n+1+j)}.
\]

Taking the logarithmic derivative yields

\[
B_{n,j}=U_{n,j}\Lambda_{n,j},
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{n,j}
=-2H_{n+2j}+2H_{2j}-H_{n+j}+3H_j-2H_{n-j}.
}
\]

Indeed, the four contributions are

\[
-2(H_{n+2j}-H_{2j}),\quad
-(H_{n+j}-H_j),\quad
2H_j-2H_{n-j}.
\]

Dauguet--Zudilin Proposition 3 now gives

\[
\boxed{r_n^{(3)}=S_n\zeta(3)-p_n^{(3)},}
\]

with the completely explicit finite sum

\[
\boxed{
p_n^{(3)}
=
\sum_{j=0}^n U_{n,j}
\left(
H_j^{(3)}+\frac12\Lambda_{n,j}H_j^{(2)}
\right).}
\]

No separate denominator identity is required: the equality of its coefficient with \(S_n\) is exactly the Whipple transformation from Section 1.

## 5. Why the P2.7 cubic appears exactly

This is the strongest structural evidence that the Whipple pair is the correct source, rather than merely another approximation.

### 5.1 The \(\zeta(2)\) saddle

The slope vectors are

\[
\alpha=(1,1,1,1),\qquad \beta=(0,0,0,2).
\]

Dauguet--Zudilin Proposition 2 uses the saddle equation

\[
(\tau-1)^4-\tau^3(\tau-2)=0,
\]

or

\[
2\tau^3-6\tau^2+4\tau-1=0.
\]

At a saddle, the exponential multiplier is

\[
\mu=e^{f_0(\tau)}
=\frac{(\tau-1)^4}{(\tau-2)^2}
=\frac{\tau^3}{\tau-2}.
\]

Eliminating \(\tau\) gives

\[
\boxed{4\mu^3-220\mu^2+8\mu-1=0.}
\]

A short exact verification avoids a resultant.  Let \(\tau_i\) be the roots of

\[
q_2(\tau)=\tau^3-3\tau^2+2\tau-\frac12.
\]

For \(\mu_i=\tau_i^3/(\tau_i-2)\),

\[
\prod_i\mu_i=\frac{(\prod_i\tau_i)^3}{\prod_i(\tau_i-2)}=\frac14.
\]

Using

\[
\mu_i=3\tau_i+4+\frac{17}{2(\tau_i-2)}
\]

and \(q_2'(2)/q_2(2)=-4\), one obtains \(\sum_i\mu_i=55\).  Finally

\[
\sum_i\frac1{\mu_i}=8,
\]

so \(\sum_{i<j}\mu_i\mu_j=(1/4)\cdot8=2\).  These are exactly the elementary symmetric coefficients of the displayed cubic.

### 5.2 The \(\zeta(3)\) saddle

The slopes are

\[
\widehat\alpha=(2,1,1,1),\qquad
\widehat\beta=(1,0,2,2).
\]

Dauguet--Zudilin Proposition 4 gives

\[
(\tau-1)^5-\tau\left(\tau-\frac12\right)^2(\tau-2)^2=0,
\]

or

\[
7\tau^3-20\tau^2+16\tau-4=0.
\]

The exponential multiplier is

\[
\mu=e^{\widehat f_0(\tau)}
=\frac{2(\tau-1)^5}{(\tau-\frac12)(\tau-2)^4}
=\frac{\tau(2\tau-1)}{(\tau-2)^2}.
\]

Again its three values satisfy

\[
4\mu^3-220\mu^2+8\mu-1=0.
\]

For example,

\[
\mu=2+\frac7{\tau-2}+\frac6{(\tau-2)^2}
\]

implies \(\sum\mu_i=55\), while

\[
\frac1\mu=\frac12-\frac4\tau+\frac{9/2}{2\tau-1}
\]

implies \(\sum1/\mu_i=8\), and the product is again \(1/4\).

### 5.3 Exact rate and the \(S_3\) observation

Let \(\mu_0\) be the positive real root and \(\mu_\pm\) the nonreal pair.  Then

\[
|\mu_\pm|^2=\frac{1}{4\mu_0},
\]

so

\[
\frac{|\mu_\pm|}{\mu_0}
=\frac{1}{2\mu_0^{3/2}}
=0.001227\ldots.
\]

Dauguet--Zudilin's propositions imply

\[
\lim S_n^{1/n}=\mu_0,
\]

and

\[
\limsup |r_n^{(2)}|^{1/n}
=\limsup |r_n^{(3)}|^{1/n}=|\mu_\pm|.
\]

Consequently

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\left|\frac{B_n}{A_n}-(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3))\right|^{1/n}
\le \frac{|\mu_\pm|}{\mu_0}.
\]

Equality holds unless the leading complex contributions of the two error forms cancel; the challenge data show the same noncancelled rate.

The irreducibility of the cubic and its nonsquare discriminant give Galois group \(S_3\).  This is consistent with the repository's irreducibility and nonsymmetric-square results for \(L_{27}\): the construction is genuinely rank three and should not be expected to split into a hypergeometric rank-one factor.  The \(S_3\) fact explains why matching the cubic is strong evidence, but it does not by itself identify the finite-\(n\) accessory parameters.

## 6. Derivatives of the three proposed deformations

All formulas below are for the full summand \(T(n,k)=\binom{2n}{n}U_{n,k}\); the outer factor is independent of the deformation parameter.

### 6.1 Deformation (a): shift the two \(\binom nk\) factors

As written in the question, one factor \(\Gamma(n-k+1)\) is missing a square.  The normalized deformation is

\[
T_a(n,k;s)
=T(n,k)
\left[
\frac{\Gamma(n+1+s)\Gamma(k+1)}
     {\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(k+1+s)}
\right]^2.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{T_a'(0)}{T}=2(H_n-H_k),
\]

\[
\frac{T_a''(0)}{T}
=4(H_n-H_k)^2+2\bigl(H_k^{(2)}-H_n^{(2)}\bigr),
\]

and

\[
\left.\frac{d^3}{ds^3}\log T_a(n,k;s)\right|_{s=0}
=4\bigl(H_n^{(3)}-H_k^{(3)}\bigr).
\]

This deformation is symmetric in the two copies of \(\binom nk\), but by itself it is not adapted to the Whipple parameter constraints.

### 6.2 Deformation (b): shift \(\binom{n+k}{n}\)

Set

\[
T_b(n,k;s)
=T(n,k)
\frac{\Gamma(n+k+1+s)\Gamma(n+1)}
     {\Gamma(n+k+1)\Gamma(n+1+s)}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{T_b'(0)}{T}=H_{n+k}-H_n,
\]

\[
\frac{T_b''(0)}{T}
=(H_{n+k}-H_n)^2+H_n^{(2)}-H_{n+k}^{(2)},
\]

and

\[
\left.\frac{d^3}{ds^3}\log T_b(n,k;s)\right|_{s=0}
=2\bigl(H_{n+k}^{(3)}-H_n^{(3)}\bigr).
\]

### 6.3 Deformation (c): Beta-exponent shift

For \(n\ge1\), use

\[
\binom{n+k}{n}=\frac1{nB(k+1,n)}.
\]

Shift the first Beta exponent:

\[
T_c(n,k;s)
=T(n,k)
\frac{\Gamma(n+k+1+s)\Gamma(k+1)}
     {\Gamma(n+k+1)\Gamma(k+1+s)}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{T_c'(0)}{T}=H_{n+k}-H_k,
\]

\[
\frac{T_c''(0)}{T}
=(H_{n+k}-H_k)^2+H_k^{(2)}-H_{n+k}^{(2)},
\]

and

\[
\left.\frac{d^3}{ds^3}\log T_c(n,k;s)\right|_{s=0}
=2\bigl(H_{n+k}^{(3)}-H_k^{(3)}\bigr).
\]

Among (a)--(c), (c) is the closest to the elementary exponent shift in the double integral.  Algebraically, however, the best deformation is one that remains on the Whipple variety.

## 7. A natural Whipple-preserving one-parameter family

A simple deformation that keeps both terminating upper parameters fixed is

\[
\boxed{
S_n(s):={}_5F_4\!\left(\begin{matrix}
-n,-n,n+1+s,\frac{n+1+s}{2},\frac{n+2+s}{2}\\
1,1+s,\frac{1+s}{2},1+\frac{s}{2}
\end{matrix};1\right),
}
\]

and

\[
A_n(s):=\binom{2n}{n}S_n(s).
\]

Then \(A_n(0)=A_n\), and Whipple gives the exact parametric identity

\[
{}_4F_3\!\left(\begin{matrix}
-n,n+1,n+1+s,n+1+s\\1,1,1+s
\end{matrix};1\right)
=
\frac{(-n-s)_n}{n!}S_n(s).
\]

Thus creative telescoping can be performed with symbolic \(s\) without leaving the terminating Whipple family.

Let \(U_{n,k}(s)\) be the \(k\)-th term of \(S_n(s)\), with \(U_{n,k}(0)=U_{n,k}\).  Duplication gives

\[
\frac{U_{n,k}(s)}{U_{n,k}}
=
\frac{(n+1+s)_k}{(n+1)_k}
\frac{(n+1+s)_{2k}}{(n+1)_{2k}}
\frac{(1)_k}{(1+s)_k}
\frac{(1)_{2k}}{(1+s)_{2k}}.
\]

Define

\[
D_{n,k}
=H_{n+k}+H_{n+2k}-2H_n-H_k-H_{2k},
\]

\[
E_{n,k}
=2H_n^{(2)}-H_{n+k}^{(2)}-H_{n+2k}^{(2)}
+H_k^{(2)}+H_{2k}^{(2)},
\]

\[
F_{n,k}
=2\left(
H_{n+k}^{(3)}+H_{n+2k}^{(3)}-2H_n^{(3)}
-H_k^{(3)}-H_{2k}^{(3)}
\right).
\]

Then

\[
U_{n,k}'(0)=U_{n,k}D_{n,k},
\]

\[
U_{n,k}''(0)=U_{n,k}\bigl(D_{n,k}^2+E_{n,k}\bigr),
\]

\[
U_{n,k}'''(0)
=U_{n,k}\bigl(D_{n,k}^3+3D_{n,k}E_{n,k}+F_{n,k}\bigr).
\]

This is the appropriate family on which to run parametric creative telescoping.  It is not yet proved that a fixed combination of its first few derivatives is exactly \(p_n^{(2)}+p_n^{(3)}\); the Barnes formulas in Sections 2--3 show what that combination and its telescoping boundary terms must reproduce.

## 8. One parameter or two?

### One external parameter is enough

The mixed integral is already a one-parameter construction:

\[
J(s):=\int_0^1\!\int_0^1\frac{(xy)^s}{1-xy}\,dx\,dy
=\sum_{m=0}^\infty\frac1{(m+s+1)^2}
=\zeta(2,s+1).
\]

Hence

\[
J(0)=\zeta(2),\qquad J'(0)=-2\zeta(3),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)=J(0)-\frac12J'(0).}
\]

Suppose a finite construction gives

\[
I_n(s)=Q_n(s)J(s)-P_n(s).
\]

Then the corrected combination with coefficient exactly \(Q_n(0)\) is

\[
\boxed{
I_n(0)-\frac12I_n'(0)
+\frac{Q_n'(0)}{2Q_n(0)}I_n(0)
=Q_n(0)(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3))-\mathcal P_n,
}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal P_n
=P_n(0)-\frac12P_n'(0)
+\frac{Q_n'(0)}{2Q_n(0)}P_n(0).
\]

Thus two independent deformation variables are not mathematically necessary.

### Why the exact proof naturally uses two Barnes functions

Dauguet--Zudilin use one rational function with simple poles to generate \(\zeta(2)\), and a second rational function with double poles to generate \(\zeta(3)\).  Whipple proves that their denominators coincide.  This is cleaner than forcing both constants out of a single termwise derivative, because:

1. the \(\zeta(2)\) side has an essential integer-valued polynomial boundary term;
2. the \(\zeta(3)\) numerator contains \(H_j^{(3)}\) and a logarithmic-derivative correction \(\frac12\Lambda_{n,j}H_j^{(2)}\);
3. a naive first/second derivative of one factor does not produce this full expression.

At the integral level one parameter suffices.  At the hypergeometric-certificate level, the Whipple pair is the natural implementation.

### About the requested derivative order

The ansatz using only \(A_n(0),A_n'(0),A_n''(0)\) is too restrictive unless an additional telescoping identity removes all cubic harmonic terms.  In a gamma-Frobenius expansion,

\[
\log\Gamma(1+s)
=-\gamma s+\frac{\zeta(2)}2s^2-\frac{\zeta(3)}3s^3+\cdots,
\]

so \(\zeta(3)\) naturally appears at third order.  A realistic derivative ansatz is

\[
B_n=c_0(n)A_n(0)+c_1(n)A_n'(0)+c_2(n)A_n''(0)
+c_3(n)A_n'''(0)+\text{telescoping boundary terms}.
\]

The Barnes pair avoids having to guess the functions \(c_j(n)\).

## 9. The determinant gauge is not a scalar gauge for \(q_n\)

The repository records

\[
\det C(n)=\frac{R(n)}{R(n+1)}\delta(n),
\]

\[
\delta(n)=\frac{h_{n+1}}{h_n},\qquad
h_n=2^{-20n}\frac{(3)_n^4(4)_n^6}
{(5/2)_n^4(7/2)_n^3(9/2)_n^3}.
\]

This \(h_n\) is a **Casoratian/determinant normalization**.  It is not itself a solution gauge.  In particular,

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}h_n^{1/n}=2^{-20},
\]

while

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}A_n^{1/n}=4\mu_0.
\]

Therefore, for every fixed nonzero polynomial \(P(n)\),

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}|h_nP(n)A_n|^{1/n}
=2^{-20}\cdot4\mu_0
=\frac{\mu_0}{2^{18}},
\]

whereas the challenge denominator has dominant rate

\[
\frac{\mu_0}{64}.
\]

The ratio of these exponential rates is \(2^{12}=4096\).  Consequently

\[
\boxed{q_n\ne \text{constant}\cdot h_nP(n)A_n}
\]

for any fixed polynomial \(P\).

The mismatch is already visible at \(n=1\):

\[
\frac{q_1}{q_0}=0.859569944180336\ldots,
\]

whereas

\[
\frac{h_1A_1}{h_0A_0}
=14\cdot\frac4{1929375}
=2.902494331\times10^{-5}.
\]

A polynomial factor would need a first-step ratio about \(29614.87\), and it still could not repair the exponential mismatch.

### The natural spectral gauges

For the inner Whipple denominator, the P2.7 scale is

\[
\widetilde S_n:=64^{-n}S_n,
\]

because its three exponential multipliers become \(\mu_i/64\).

For the outer AESZ coefficient,

\[
\widetilde A_n:=256^{-n}A_n
=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}\,64^{-n}S_n.
\]

Since

\[
\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}=\frac{(1/2)_n}{n!}
\]

has only subexponential growth, \(\widetilde A_n\) has the same three Poincare multipliers \(\mu_i/64\).

Finally,

\[
\prod_i\frac{\mu_i}{64}
=\frac{1/4}{64^3}=2^{-20},
\]

which exactly explains the limiting determinant \(\delta(n)\to2^{-20}\).  This is a powerful fingerprint, but a determinant fixes only the product of the three multipliers, not the complete operator.

The challenge \(q_n\) should therefore be expected to arise from a rational **basis change or Ore intertwiner applied to the full three-dimensional Barnes/AESZ system**, not by multiplying the holomorphic period by \(h_n\).

## 10. The exact computation still needed for P2.7

The remaining task is finite and checkable.

### Step 1: creative telescoping on the two rational functions

Find polynomials \(P_0(n),\dots,P_3(n)\) and rational certificates \(G_n^{(2)}(t),G_n^{(3)}(t)\) such that

\[
\sum_{i=0}^3P_i(n)R_{n+i}^{(2)}(t)
=G_n^{(2)}(t+1)-G_n^{(2)}(t),
\]

\[
\sum_{i=0}^3P_i(n)\widehat R_{n+i}(t)
=G_n^{(3)}(t+1)-G_n^{(3)}(t).
\]

Equivalently, set \(G_n^{(a)}(t)=g_n^{(a)}(t)R_n^{(a)}(t)\) and solve the rational Gosper equations

\[
\sum_{i=0}^3P_i(n)\frac{R_{n+i}^{(a)}(t)}{R_n^{(a)}(t)}
=
 g_n^{(a)}(t+1)\frac{R_n^{(a)}(t+1)}{R_n^{(a)}(t)}-g_n^{(a)}(t).
\]

The Barnes contour argument then proves that \(S_n,p_n^{(2)},p_n^{(3)}\) are annihilated by the same order-three telescoper \(L_{\rm DZ}\).

### Step 2: compare with \(L_{27}\)

Conjugate by the spectral gauge \(64^{-n}\), or by the outer gauge

\[
256^{-n}\binom{2n}{n},
\]

and test exact equality with \(L_{27}\).  If they are not scalar-gauge equivalent, solve for low-order Ore intertwiners

\[
\boxed{L_{27}U=V L_{\rm DZ}},
\]

with \(U,V\in\mathbb Q(n)\langle E_n\rangle\).  Start with order zero, then order one.  The quadratic coboundary

\[
R(n)=946n^2+4515n+5399
\]

is a strong indication that a rational basis correction is present.

### Step 3: use the same intertwiner on numerator and denominator

The decisive certificate is a common rational operator \(U\) and normalization \(c\) such that

\[
q_n=c\,U\bigl(64^{-n}S_n\bigr),
\]

\[
p_n=c\,U\bigl(64^{-n}(p_n^{(2)}+p_n^{(3)})\bigr).
\]

It is enough to verify the first three values after the operator identity, because both sides then satisfy the same order-three recurrence.  One obtains identically

\[
p_n-(\zeta(2)+\zeta(3))q_n
=-c\,U\bigl(64^{-n}(r_n^{(2)}+r_n^{(3)})\bigr),
\]

which is recessive by the Barnes asymptotics.  This would turn the current 5100-digit numerical connection certificate into an exact theorem.

### Why this is preferable to fitting derivatives directly

An Ore intertwiner preserves the entire period/companion system and automatically carries the exact error form.  Fitting \(q_n\) to a scalar multiple of the holomorphic period, or fitting \(p_n\) to an ad hoc finite derivative combination, does not preserve the connection coefficient and can match a few initial values for the wrong reason.

## 11. Answers to the five specific requests

1. **Natural deformation.**  Of the three isolated shifts, the Beta shift (c) is geometrically closest to the mixed integral.  For creative telescoping, the preferred deformation is the Whipple-preserving \(S_n(s)\) in Section 7.

2. **Derivatives.**  The exact first, second and third derivative formulas for (a), (b), (c), and for the Whipple family are given in Sections 6--7.  First derivatives produce ordinary harmonic differences; second derivatives produce their squares and \(H^{(2)}\); third logarithmic derivatives produce \(H^{(3)}\).

3. **One or two parameters.**  One external parameter suffices because \(J(0)-\frac12J'(0)=\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)\).  The exact hypergeometric proof is cleaner as two Barnes functions with one common Whipple denominator, not as two independent deformation parameters.

4. **Whipple pair.**  Yes.  The exact pair is

   \[
   (-n,n+1,n+1,n+1;1,1,1)
   \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
   \left(-n,-n,n+1,\frac{n+1}{2},\frac{n+2}{2};
   1,1,\frac12,1\right).
   \]

   Its common coefficient is \(S_n=A_n/\binom{2n}{n}\).

5. **Gauge.**  The determinant term \(h_n\) is not a scalar gauge for a single solution.  The relation is necessarily more complex.  The correct object to seek is a rational basis change/Ore intertwiner from the three-dimensional Whipple/Barnes system to the P2.7 companion system.
