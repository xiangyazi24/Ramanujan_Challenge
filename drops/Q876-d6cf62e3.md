# Q876: canonical first-branch attack audit

## Summary

The new reduction is correct: after the off-center branch is removed, the remaining problem is exactly the first-branch quantity

\[
W(n)=\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n,\ p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}\log p.
\]

No currently available fixed-q prefix gcd, convolution-content, or Lucas-pair carrier gives a genuine pointwise bound \(W(n)=o(n)\). The natural divisor-coordinate reformulations either reproduce the same object or introduce a characteristic-zero product whose degree is already of order n. The strongest rigorous conclusion is therefore a scoped obstruction: several proposed certificate classes cannot yield the required saving without a new depletion mechanism.

## 1. Divisor-coordinate form

Write

\[
p=(n-r)/t,\qquad r=n\bmod p,\qquad t=\lfloor n/p\rfloor.
\]

For \(p>\sqrt n\), we have \(t<\sqrt n\) and \(0\le r<p\). Thus

\[
W(n)=\sum_{1\le t<\sqrt n}\sum_{\substack{p=(n-r)/t\\ p\ {m prime}\\ p>\sqrt n\\ p\mid b_r}}\log p.
\]

For fixed t this is a prime-factor sum over the arithmetic slice

\[
 n-r\equiv0\pmod t.
\]

The condition \(p\mid b_r\) is independent of the primality constraint and depends on the residue variable r. The Apéry-Lucas relation only moves the index backwards by p; it does not create a second congruence condition on the same p.

## 2. Fixed-t summation does not save a factor

A tempting route is to bound each t separately and sum over \(t<\sqrt n\). However, the trivial size is already

\[
\sum_{t<\sqrt n}\sum_{r<n/t}\log(n/t-r)
\asymp n,
\]

so any successful argument needs an additional density loss in the set

\[
\{r:p\mid b_r,\ p=(n-r)/t\}.
\]

Known estimates on the number of prime divisors of individual Apéry numbers do not provide this loss uniformly in moving r.

## 3. Product/resultant certificates

Consider a characteristic-zero carrier of the form

\[
P_n(X)=\prod_r (X-(n-r))^{e_r}
\]

or a resultant involving the Apéry polynomial data.

To capture every possible large divisor p of \(n-r\), the certificate must vanish modulo every such p. The degree must therefore cover essentially all r with \(p\mid b_r\). Since r ranges over an interval of length n, a universal resultant has height/degree at least linear in n unless one proves strong cancellations among the r-rows.

Thus a generic resultant construction cannot prove \(W(n)=o(n)\); it only repackages the carrier.

## 4. Holonomic congruence obstruction

The recurrence for \(b_n\) is useful for congruences modulo a fixed p, but here p varies with n. A holonomic identity in the affine variable \(n-r=tp\) would need to control

\[
b_r\pmod{(n-r)/t}
\]

uniformly over all t and all moving moduli. Existing Apéry-Lucas periodicity gives

\[
b_m\equiv b_{m-p}\pmod p,
\]

but does not give a characteristic-zero divisibility polynomial in \(n-r\). Therefore the recurrence alone does not imply a first-branch compression.

## 5. Relation to existing carriers

The fixed-q prefix gcd carrier bounds products such as

\[
\operatorname{lcm}_r\gcd(A_r,b_r),
\]

where \(A_r\) is a collection of affine factors. It already contains the first-branch primes. Applying it again to \(n-r\) is only a renaming of the same carrier.

Likewise, convolution-content carriers aggregate coefficients of a polynomial identity. They can capture shared divisibility but do not distinguish the moving prime condition

\[
p=(n-r)/t.
\]

Hence neither gives a strictly weaker lemma implying \(W(n)=o(n)\) unless an additional depletion statement is supplied.

## 6. A genuinely weaker sufficient lemma

A meaningful intermediate target is:

**Affine depletion lemma.** For every fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly in n,

\[
\#\{(t,r):1\le t<\sqrt n,\ n-r\ {m prime\ multiple\ of}\ t,\ n-r\mid?\ b_r,\ n-r>\sqrt n\}
=o\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right).
\]

More naturally stated with the actual prime:

\[
\sum_{t<\sqrt n}\#\{r: p=(n-r)/t>\sqrt n\ {m prime},\ p\mid b_r\}
=o\left(\frac n{\log n}\right).
\]

This implies \(W(n)=o(n)\) because every summand contributes at most \(\log n\).

This is strictly weaker than the desired logarithmic estimate and is not a tautological rewriting: it asks for a count of successful affine hits rather than their weighted sum.

## 7. Current obstruction

The remaining barrier is a missing Apéry-specific depletion theorem:

- For each fixed p, Lucas gives periodicity.
- For each fixed r, one can study prime divisors of b_r.
- But the attack requires a two-parameter uniform statement for the diagonal family \(p=(n-r)/t\).

Neither short/long prime gap arguments nor Selberg sieve alone create this dependence. A sieve sees the affine prime variable but has no information that \(p\mid b_r\) is rare; Apéry congruences see the divisibility but do not control the moving affine prime.

Therefore the unconditional status is:

**Proved:** off-center contribution is already \(O_q(n^{3/4})\); the remaining problem is exactly \(W(n)\).

**Not proved:** \(W(n)=o(n)\).

**Concrete next lemma:** affine depletion for the diagonal family above.

**Obstruction:** generic resultant, fixed-t divisor summation, and holonomic certificates without a new depletion input cannot supply the required pointwise saving.
