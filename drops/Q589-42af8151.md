# Gap Wronskian and collision bounding

## Summary

The gap Wronskian viewpoint is the correct reformulation of the ZM₂ collision problem, but the conclusion does not follow from existing generic recurrence theory. The main point is that $W(r,s)$ is itself a solution of the Apéry recurrence, and bounding its zeros is essentially the same type of problem as bounding $|Z_p|$ itself.

The useful new structure is that $W(r,s)$ is not an arbitrary solution: it is generated by a transfer matrix and has a forced zero at $s=r$ with a known adjacent value. This may allow an algebraic-geometric zero-counting theorem for the Apéry recurrence family.

---

## (A) Transfer matrix expression for $W(r,s)$

Let

$$
T(s,r)=M_{s-1}\cdots M_r=
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For any solution $u$ of the recurrence,

$$
\binom{u_s}{u_{s-1}}=T(s,r)\binom{u_r}{u_{r-1}}.
$$

Therefore

$$
f_s=af_r+bf_{r-1},\qquad g_s=ag_r+bg_{r-1}.
$$

Hence

$$
W(r,s)=f_rg_s-f_sg_r
$$

becomes

$$
=f_r(ag_r+bg_{r-1})-(af_r+bf_{r-1})g_r
$$

so the $a$ terms cancel and

$$
\boxed{W(r,s)=b(f_rg_{r-1}-f_{r-1}g_r).}
$$

The parenthesis is the local Wronskian at $r-1$:

$$
f_rg_{r-1}-f_{r-1}g_r=-W_{r-1}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{W(r,s)=-b\,W_{r-1}.}
$$

This is the key formula. Collision is equivalent to the upper-right entry of the transfer matrix vanishing:

$$
W(r,s)=0\iff b(T(s,r))=0.
$$

So the collision problem is a transfer-product entry vanishing problem.

The determinant is

$$
\det T(s,r)=\prod_{n=r}^{s-1}-\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}
=(-1)^{s-r}\frac{r^3}{s^3}.
$$

(The factorial expression telescopes; the cube remains only at the endpoints.)

## (B) Bounding the number of collisions

The desired estimate is

$$
\#\{(r,s):W(r,s)=0\}=O(p).
$$

If true, the ratio map

$$
R_r=f_r/g_r
$$

has bounded average multiplicity.

However, proving this from SL₂ monodromy alone is not immediate. Complex monodromy being Zariski dense does not automatically imply that a deterministic finite-field orbit

$$
M_{s-1}\cdots M_r
$$

is equidistributed.

The missing ingredient is a finite-field analogue of a mixing theorem for the polynomial path

$$
r\mapsto M_r.
$$

A possible route is to interpret $b(T(s,r))$ as the trace function of an $\ell$-adic sheaf on the $(r,s)$ parameter surface and apply Deligne-type bounds. This is the two-variable version of the AFP problem.

---

## (C) Gap Wronskian recurrence

For fixed $r$, the sequence

$$
s\mapsto W(r,s)
$$

satisfies the same Apéry recurrence:

$$
(s+1)^3W(r,s+1)=P(s)W(r,s)-s^3W(r,s-1).
$$

The initial values are

$$
W(r,r)=0,
$$

and

$$
W(r,r+1)=W_r=\frac{(-1)^r}{(r+1)^3}.
$$

Therefore every gap Wronskian is a normalized Apéry solution.

This gives a useful reduction:

A bound

$$
\#\{s:W(r,s)=0\}=O(1)
$$

uniformly in $r$

would imply the collision bound immediately:

$$
\sum_r\#\{s:W(r,s)=0\}=O(p).
$$

But this is essentially a stronger form of the original M1/ZM₁ problem, now for all initial conditions rather than one distinguished solution.

---

## (D) Generic initial conditions

For constant coefficient recurrences, the zero set is controlled by the exponential decomposition. The Apéry recurrence is different because the coefficients vary with $s$.

The correct analogue is not Pisano theory but arithmetic differential equations:

- the recurrence corresponds to a rank-2 difference module;
- zeros correspond to intersections of a Frobenius orbit with a hyperplane in the projective bundle;
- the desired statement is a bounded intersection theorem.

For a generic initial vector, one expects few zeros because the projective orbit

$$
[f_s:g_s]
$$

should not repeatedly hit a fixed point.

However, proving this requires a theorem about the finite-field dynamics of the Apéry difference module.

---

## (E) Existing recurrence zero bounds

There is no general theorem saying that a nonconstant-coefficient second-order recurrence over $\mathbb F_p$ has only $O(1)$ zeros.

Known tools:

1. **Skolem-Mahler-Lech type results**: apply to constant coefficients over characteristic zero; they do not directly apply here.

2. **Difference algebra**: gives structural information but not a uniform finite-field zero bound.

3. **Algebraic geometry of sheaves**: gives the closest analogue. If the projective orbit is represented by a nonconstant rational map and the exceptional locus is controlled, one can obtain square-root cancellation or bounded intersection counts.

Thus the needed theorem is closer to:

> A finite-field zero-counting theorem for the projectivized Apéry difference module.

---

# Overall assessment

The gap Wronskian route is promising because it converts ZM₂ into a geometric collision problem:

$$
W(r,s)=0\iff (T(s,r))_{12}=0.
$$

The main advances are:

- the transfer formula isolates one matrix entry;
- the determinant is explicitly controlled;
- every collision sequence is another Apéry solution.

But the final missing theorem remains a strong form of Apéry Fourier pseudorandomness:

$$
\text{projective transfer orbits of the Apéry cocycle have bounded hitting multiplicity.}
$$

This is likely the correct geometric formulation of ZM₂.
