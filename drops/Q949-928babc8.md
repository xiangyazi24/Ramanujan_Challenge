# Q949: exact arithmetic of the codimension-one reciprocal moment content

## 1. Correct primewise formulation

Let

\[
L=\operatorname{lcm}(A_0,\ldots,A_H),\qquad
X=\sum_{m=0}^H(-1)^m\binom Hm\frac L{A_m},
\]
\[
Y=\sum_{m=0}^H(-1)^m\binom Hm m\frac L{A_m}.
\]

For a prime \(\ell\), write

\[
e=v_\ell(L)=\max_m v_\ell(A_m),\qquad
I_\ell=\{m:v_\ell(A_m)=e\}.
\]

Modulo \(\ell\), only the indices in \(I_\ell\) survive after dividing by \(\ell^e\):

\[
\ell^{-e}X\equiv \sum_{m\in I_\ell}(-1)^m\binom Hm u_m\pmod\ell,
\]
\[
\ell^{-e}Y\equiv \sum_{m\in I_\ell}(-1)^m\binom Hm m u_m\pmod\ell,
\]

where \(u_m=(L/A_m)/\ell^{e-v_\ell(A_m)}\) is an \(\ell\)-unit.

Therefore the exact criterion is:

\[
v_\ell(g_H)>0
\iff
\sum_{m\in I_\ell}(-1)^m\binom Hm u_m\equiv0\pmod\ell
\]

and

\[
\sum_{m\in I_\ell}(-1)^m\binom Hm m u_m\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

This is the usable primewise criterion. It is not equivalent to \(\ell\mid H!\), which is already disproved by the small exceptional cases.

## 2. Singleton maximal valuation case

If \(I_\ell=\{r\}\), then

\[
\ell^{-e}X\equiv (-1)^r\binom Hr u_r\pmod\ell,
\]

\[
\ell^{-e}Y\equiv r(-1)^r\binom Hr u_r\pmod\ell.
\]

Hence

\[
v_\ell(g_H)>0
\iff
\ell\mid \binom Hr.
\]

So a singleton maximal prime contributes to the common gcd only when the binomial coefficient kills the surviving residue. In particular, the generic singleton situation gives no contribution to \(g_H\).

For the observed denominator factors this is the opposite phenomenon: singleton maximal primes usually survive in the reduced denominator of the rational moments.

## 3. Bounding the gcd

The problem reduces to bounding primes for which the maximal-valuation support has at least two terms or for which the singleton binomial obstruction occurs.

Split:

\[
g_H=g_{\le H+1}g_{>H+1}.
\]

For \(\ell>H+1\), Lucas implies

\[
\ell\nmid H!,
\]

so

\[
\ell\nmid\binom Hr
\]

for every \(0\le r\le H\). Therefore singleton supports give no gcd contribution for \(\ell>H+1\).

The remaining possibility is a multi-point cancellation

\[
\sum_{m\in I_\ell}c_m\equiv0,
\qquad
\sum_{m\in I_\ell}mc_m\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

This is a two-moment zero condition. Thus an unconditional route to

\[
\log g_H=o(H^2)
\]

is reduced to proving that the number/weight of primes for which the reciprocal Apéry valuation profile has a double zero is subquadratic.

A sufficient exact lemma is:

> For every \(\ell>H+1\), the polynomial
> \(\sum_{m:v_\ell(A_m)=e}c_mT^m\) has no root of multiplicity at least two at \(T=1\) modulo \(\ell\).

This immediately gives no contribution from large primes.

## 4. Why the empirical data is compatible

The factors seen for \(H\le200\) are mostly small primes. This matches the criterion: small primes can have Lucas/binomial cancellations, while large singleton primes cannot enter \(g_H\).

The observed growth

\[
\log g_H=O(H)
\]

would follow from a stronger but still plausible statement:

\[
\sum_{\ell\le H+1}v_\ell(g_H)\log\ell=O(H).
\]

This is weaker than the radical estimates in the same-gap problem because it only controls the two-dimensional common moment content, not the full Apéry radical.

## 5. Finite verifier

The following exact check implements the criterion:

```python
from math import comb, gcd
from sympy import factorint


def gcd_moment(A):
    H=len(A)-1
    L=1
    for a in A:
        L=L*a//gcd(L,a)
    X=sum((-1)**m*comb(H,m)*(L//A[m]) for m in range(H+1))
    Y=sum((-1)**m*comb(H,m)*m*(L//A[m]) for m in range(H+1))
    return gcd(abs(X),abs(Y))
```

## Conclusion

The unconditional conclusion currently supported is:

- the exact gcd criterion is the two linear congruences on maximal valuation classes;
- singleton maximal primes do not enlarge \(g_H\) except through the explicit binomial obstruction;
- large singleton primes are eliminated by Lucas;
- the missing ingredient for a full \(\log g_H=O(H)\) theorem is a multiple-root exclusion (or an equivalent reciprocal-moment nondegeneracy theorem) for multi-point maximal valuation classes.

No claim that the observed scan proves the asymptotic bound is made.
