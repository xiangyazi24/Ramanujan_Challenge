## 1. Setup and an important integrality fact

Write

\[
(r+1)^3b_{r+1}=P(r)b_r-r^3b_{r-1},
\qquad
P(r)=34r^3+51r^2+27r+5,
\]

with \(b_0=1,b_1=5\).  For the first shifted Jacobi/Gessel jet,

\[
g_0=0,\qquad g_1=12,
\]

and

\[
(r+1)^3g_{r+1}-P(r)g_r+r^3g_{r-1}=S_r,
\]

where

\[
S_r=P'(r)b_r-3(r+1)^2b_{r+1}-3r^2b_{r-1}.
\]

Define the Wronskian energy

\[
E_r=r^3\bigl(b_{r-1}g_r-b_rg_{r-1}\bigr),\qquad r\ge1.
\]

A direct subtraction of the two recurrences gives

\[
\boxed{E_{r+1}-E_r=b_rS_r.}
\tag{1}
\]

Since \(E_1=12\), \(b_r\in\mathbb Z\), and \(S_r\in\mathbb Z\), (1) proves

\[
\boxed{E_r\in\mathbb Z\quad\text{for every }r\ge1.}
\tag{2}
\]

This matters: although \(g_r\) need not itself be integral at small primes, the energy is an honest integer, so the cancellation in
\(E_r/b_r^2\) is governed by an ordinary integer gcd.

## 2. Exact projective-height formula

Put

\[
m=r-1,\qquad B=b_m,\qquad E=E_m,
\qquad T_m=\frac{E_m}{b_m^2}.
\]

Let

\[
d_m:=\gcd(E_m,b_m^2),
\]

and write \(T_m=A_m/D_m\) in lowest terms with \(D_m>0\).  Because \(E_m\) is integral,

\[
\boxed{
A_m=\frac{E_m}{d_m},
\qquad
D_m=\frac{b_m^2}{d_m}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

For the multiplicative projective height

\[
H(T_m)=\max(|A_m|,D_m),
\]

we therefore have the **exact** identity

\[
\boxed{
H(T_m)
=D_m\max(1,|T_m|)
=\frac{b_m^2}{d_m}\max\!\left(1,\left|\frac{E_m}{b_m^2}\right|\right).
}
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\log H(T_m)
=2\log b_m-\log d_m
+\log^+\left|\frac{E_m}{b_m^2}\right|.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus, once one uses the established polynomial ordinary-size estimate

\[
\left|E_m/b_m^2\right|\le m^{O(1)},
\]

(5) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\log H(T_m)
=2\log b_m-\log\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)+O(\log m).
}
\tag{6}
\]

Since \(b_m\) has exponential Apéry growth (the dominant characteristic root is \(17+12\sqrt2\)), the desired statement

\[
H(T_m)=\exp(\Omega(m))
\]

is therefore **equivalent, up to the harmless \(O(\log m)\), to a linear saving in the square gcd**:

\[
\boxed{
2\log b_m-\log\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)=\Omega(m).
}
\tag{7}
\]

A convenient stronger sufficient statement would be

\[
\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)=e^{o(m)},
\tag{8}
\]

but (8) is much stronger than necessary.  The exact missing theorem is (7).

## 3. Why the already-known exponential height of \(E_m/b_m\) does not settle this

Let

\[
d_m^{(1)}=\gcd(E_m,b_m),
\qquad
Q_m=\operatorname{den}\left(E_m/b_m\right)
=\frac{b_m}{d_m^{(1)}}.
\]

The normalized denominator is

\[
D_m=\frac{b_m^2}{\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)}.
\]

Prime by prime, if \(e=v_p(b_m)\) and \(a=v_p(E_m)\), then

\[
v_p(Q_m)=\max(e-a,0),
\qquad
v_p(D_m)=\max(2e-a,0).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{Q_m\mid D_m.}
\tag{9}
\]

So there is one immediate positive result:

> **If** the earlier height barrier actually proves that the *reduced denominator* \(Q_m=b_m/\gcd(E_m,b_m)\) is \(\exp(\Omega(m))\), then (9) instantly proves the same exponential lower bound for \(H(E_m/b_m^2)\).

But a theorem saying only that the **projective height** of \(E_m/b_m\) is exponential is not enough.  Indeed

\[
\frac{E_m}{b_m}=b_m\,T_m,
\]

so the raw ratio already has exponential ordinary magnitude whenever \(T_m\) is merely polynomial-sized and not exponentially tiny.  Its projective height can therefore be carried entirely by the numerator even if the normalized ratio has tiny height.  The toy identity \(E=B^2\) makes the logical gap explicit:

\[
H(E/B)=B\quad\text{but}\quad H(E/B^2)=1.
\]

Therefore the raw-height statement transfers only if it contains a denominator lower bound, not from projective height alone.

## 4. Exact large-prime gcd/common-zero identity

The gcd in (6) can be analyzed exactly at primes above the row index.

Let \(\ell>m\) be prime and suppose

\[
e=v_\ell(b_m)>0.
\]

All denominators occurring in the shifted Taylor coefficient \(g_j\) for \(j\le m\) are \(\ell\)-units.  Also the Apéry no-consecutive-zero argument gives

\[
\ell\nmid b_{m-1}.
\]

In \(\mathbb Z_\ell\),

\[
E_m/m^3=b_{m-1}g_m-b_mg_{m-1}.
\tag{10}
\]

Since \(m\) and \(b_{m-1}\) are \(\ell\)-units, reduction of (10) modulo \(\ell\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\ell\mid E_m
\iff
\ell\mid g_m,
\qquad
\text{provided }\ell>m\text{ and }\ell\mid b_m.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the **first layer of cancellation** in \(E_m/b_m^2\) at every large prime is exactly the Jacobi/Gessel common-zero event

\[
\ell\mid b_m,\qquad \ell\mid g_m.
\]

There is also an exact higher-valuation statement.  Put

\[
c=v_\ell(g_m).
\]

If \(c<e\), then the two terms in (10) have unequal valuations and

\[
\boxed{v_\ell(E_m)=c.}
\tag{12}
\]

If \(c\ge e\), write

\[
b_m=\ell^e\beta,
\qquad
g_m=\ell^e\gamma,
\]

with \(\beta\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times\).  Then

\[
\boxed{
 v_\ell(E_m)
 =e+v_\ell\bigl(b_{m-1}\gamma-\beta g_{m-1}\bigr).
}
\tag{13}
\]

Consequently

\[
 v_\ell\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 c,&c<e,\\[2mm]
 e+\min\!\left(e,
 v_\ell(b_{m-1}\gamma-\beta g_{m-1})\right),&c\ge e.
 \end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]

In particular, **full cancellation of the square \(\ell^{2e}\)** requires not just the common zero \(\ell^e\mid g_m\), but the lifted congruence

\[
\boxed{
 b_{m-1}\frac{g_m}{\ell^e}
 \equiv
 \frac{b_m}{\ell^e}g_{m-1}
 \pmod{\ell^e}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

For a simple factor \(e=1\), (15) is a second Bockstein-level condition.  A mod-\(\ell\) common-zero theorem alone does not control it.

This is the exact arithmetic explanation of the empirical phenomenon: enormous reduced denominators mean that most prime-power mass in \(b_m^2\) survives (14), but a scan of common zeros proves no uniform lower bound for that surviving mass.

## 5. The target prime is not a cancellation prime of the normalized ratio

This is a useful index-level point that rules out one tempting shortcut.

The target situation uses

\[
p>r,\qquad p\mid b_r.
\]

No consecutive zeros give

\[
p\nmid b_{r-1}.
\]

But the normalized energy under discussion is

\[
T_r:=\frac{E_{r-1}}{b_{r-1}^2}.
\]

Therefore the target prime itself satisfies

\[
\boxed{p\nmid \operatorname{den}(T_r)}
\tag{16}
\]

and cannot contribute to

\[
\gcd(E_{r-1},b_{r-1}^2).
\]

The cancellations which decide the projective height of \(T_r\) come from **other primes dividing the previous Apéry value** \(b_{r-1}\).  At a prime \(\ell>r-1\), (11)--(15) translate these cancellations into common-zero and lifted-common-zero conditions at the *previous row*.  At primes \(\ell\le r-1\), even that clean target interpretation is unavailable because the shifted-jet denominators need not be \(\ell\)-units.

So a low-degree condition attached to the target prime \(p\mid b_r\) does not by itself bound the gcd which controls the height of \(T_r\).

## 6. Exact low-degree target identity for the normalized ratio

There is nevertheless a very clean reason the normalization is attractive.

Set

\[
C_r=17r^2-16r+4.
\]

First eliminate \(b_{r-2}\) from \(S_{r-1}\).  The Apéry recurrence gives

\[
(r-1)^3b_{r-2}=P(r-1)b_{r-1}-r^3b_r.
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{r-1}
&=P'(r-1)b_{r-1}-3r^2b_r-3(r-1)^2b_{r-2}\\
&=-\frac{3C_r}{r-1}b_{r-1}
 +\frac{3r^2}{r-1}b_r,
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

because

\[
(r-1)P'(r-1)-3P(r-1)=-3C_r.
\]

Now use

\[
E_r-E_{r-1}=b_{r-1}S_{r-1}
\]

and

\[
E_r=r^3(b_{r-1}g_r-b_rg_{r-1}).
\]

After multiplying by \(r-1\) and rearranging one obtains the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&(r-1)r^3b_{r-1}g_r
-(r-1)E_{r-1}
+3C_r b_{r-1}^2\\
&\qquad=
 b_r\Bigl((r-1)r^3g_{r-1}+3r^2b_{r-1}\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{18}
\]

Now let \(p>r\) and \(p\mid b_r\).  Since
\(p\nmid r(r-1)b_{r-1}\), division in \(\mathbb F_p\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{E_{r-1}}{b_{r-1}^2}
\equiv
r^3\frac{g_r}{b_{r-1}}
+\frac{3C_r}{r-1}
\pmod p.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r,\ p\mid g_r
 \iff
 \frac{E_{r-1}}{b_{r-1}^2}
 \equiv\frac{3C_r}{r-1}\pmod p.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Write \(T_r=A_r/D_r\) in lowest terms.  By (16), \(p\nmid D_r\), so (20) is equivalently the **integer low-degree congruence**

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r,\ p\mid g_r
 \iff
 p\mid\bigl((r-1)A_r-3C_rD_r\bigr).
}
\tag{21}
\]

This is exact and uses no explicit \(b_{r-1}\) or \(g_r\) after the rational \(T_r\) has been reduced.

### What (21) does and does not buy

For common-zero detection, (21) is genuinely cleaner.  If one could prove

\[
H(T_r)=r^{O(1)},
\]

then (21) would give a genuinely low-height scalar obstruction.

But the coefficient pair in (21) is precisely \((A_r,D_r)\).  By (4)--(7), proving that pair short is **exactly the unresolved square-gcd problem**.  Conversely, if

\[
H(T_r)=\exp(\Omega(r)),
\]

then the normalization has only hidden the old height inside the reduced projective coordinates of \(T_r\); it has not removed it.

Thus (21) is not itself a height proof.  It identifies the correct normalized carrier and simultaneously shows the exact missing arithmetic input.

## 7. Why target/common-zero support alone is insufficient for the height theorem

Decompose

\[
d_m=\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)
=d_m^{\le m}d_m^{>m}
\]

according to prime support.

For \(d_m^{>m}\), equations (11)--(15) give an exact description:

- a large prime can cancel at all only at a common zero \(b_m\equiv g_m\equiv0\);
- cancellation of the second copy of a prime power requires the lifted congruence (15).

However, a support theorem saying merely that common zeros are rare, or are constrained to a low-degree residue relation, does not bound the **total prime-power weight**

\[
\log d_m^{>m}.
\]

One needs a rowwise weighted statement.

Worse, the factor \(d_m^{\le m}\) is not controlled by the large-target common-zero argument at all.  A proof that all large common-zero primes are absent would still not exclude the logical possibility that most of \(b_m^2\) is cancelled at primes \(\le m\).

Accordingly, the smallest theorem that actually settles the normalized-height question is not a finite common-zero census but the uniform weighted gcd bound

\[
\boxed{
\log\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)
\le 2\log b_m-\delta m+O(\log m)
}
\tag{22}
\]

for some fixed \(\delta>0\).  By (6), (22) is equivalent to the desired
\(\exp(\Omega(m))\) projective height under the polynomial ordinary-size bound.

A more structural sufficient route would be to prove both

\[
\log d_m^{\le m}\le 2\log b_m-\delta_1m
\]

and a weighted common-zero/lift estimate for \(d_m^{>m}\), but no such theorem follows from the current first-order target congruences alone.

This is the sharply scoped stopping point.

## 8. Rewriting the actual fixed-q target law: the old tall coefficient comes back exactly

There are two notions of “target congruence” in this line of work, and they behave differently.

### 8.1 Common-zero selector

For the selector

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad p\mid g_r,
\]

equation (21) is a valid rewrite with no explicit \(b\) or \(g\).  Its usefulness is exactly equivalent to controlling \(H(T_r)\).

### 8.2 Fixed-q first-order endpoint congruence

For the shifted continuant one has, in the target cell \(p>r\),

\[
U_r(qp)=b_r+qp\,g_r+O(p^2),
\]

so if \(p\mid b_r\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U_r(qp)}p
\equiv
\frac{b_r}{p}+qg_r
\pmod p.
}
\tag{23}
\]

Solving (19) for \(g_r\) gives

\[
\boxed{
 g_r\equiv
\frac{b_{r-1}}{r^3}
\left(
T_r-\frac{3C_r}{r-1}
\right)
\pmod p.
}
\tag{24}
\]

Substitution into (23) yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U_r(qp)}p
\equiv
\frac{b_r}{p}
+
\frac{q\,b_{r-1}}{r^3}
\left(
T_r-\frac{3C_r}{r-1}
\right)
\pmod p.
}
\tag{25}
\]

So the normalized ratio does **not** eliminate the tall coefficient from the actual fixed-q target law: it reintroduces \(b_{r-1}\) multiplicatively.

Indeed this is not merely a presentation artifact.  Since

\[
 b_{r-1}T_r=\frac{E_{r-1}}{b_{r-1}},
\]

(25) contains

\[
\boxed{
\frac{E_{r-1}}{b_{r-1}}
-
\frac{3C_r}{r-1}b_{r-1},
}
\tag{26}
\]

so the raw energy ratio from the original height barrier is literally back.

If one instead divides (23) by \(b_{r-1}\), then the jet term becomes short in terms of \(T_r\), but the private quotient becomes

\[
\frac{b_r/p}{b_{r-1}},
\]

which again imports consecutive Apéry data of exponential characteristic-zero size.  Thus the normalization only moves the tall object from the jet term to the private quotient.

With \(T_r=A_r/D_r\), clearing denominators in (24) makes the same point algebraically:

\[
\boxed{
 r^3(r-1)D_r\,g_r
\equiv
 b_{r-1}\bigl((r-1)A_r-3C_rD_r\bigr)
\pmod p.
}
\tag{27}
\]

The clean low-degree scalar from (21) is multiplied by \(b_{r-1}\) as soon as one asks to recover the actual jet contribution \(g_r\).

Hence:

- normalized energy is a legitimate carrier for **common-zero detection**;
- it is not a shell-free replacement for \(g_r\) in the **fixed-q first-order target value**.

## 9. Final conclusion

The strongest rigorous statement I can get from the exact recurrences is therefore:

1. \(E_m\) is integral and
   \[
   H(E_m/b_m^2)
   =\frac{b_m^2}{\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)}
    \max(1,|E_m/b_m^2|).
   \]
   Under polynomial ordinary magnitude, exponential projective height is **equivalent** to the weighted square-gcd saving (22).

2. At every prime \(\ell>m\) dividing \(b_m\), first cancellation occurs iff \(\ell\mid g_m\); full square cancellation requires the stronger lifted Wronskian congruence (15).  This is an exact theorem, not scan evidence.

3. The target prime \(p\mid b_r\) does not divide \(b_{r-1}\), so it does not control the gcd governing the height of \(E_{r-1}/b_{r-1}^2\).  The height problem is a weighted statement about other prime divisors of the previous row, including small primes not covered by the target-unit argument.

4. Therefore the empirical huge reduced numerator and denominator do **not** currently upgrade to a proof of
   \[
   H(E_{r-1}/b_{r-1}^2)=\exp(\Omega(r)).
   \]
   The exact first missing theorem is (22), or any stronger rowwise bound on \(\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)\).

5. The normalized ratio does rewrite the common-zero condition as the exact low-degree congruence (21).  But its coefficient height is exactly \(H(T_r)\), so this does not evade the unresolved height problem.

6. For the actual fixed-q target congruence, substitution forces the factor \(b_{r-1}\) back in, and the identity \(b_{r-1}T_r=E_{r-1}/b_{r-1}\) literally restores the old raw-height carrier.  Thus there is no current rewrite through \(E_{r-1}/b_{r-1}^2\) that simultaneously removes the tall \(b/g\) coefficient and preserves the needed first-order target value.

So the normalized-energy idea reaches a precise arithmetic gate rather than a proof: **prove a linear saving in \(\gcd(E_m,b_m^2)\), or the projective-height barrier remains unclosed; and even if that gate were favorable, the fixed-q target law still needs a separate way to eliminate the multiplicative \(b_{r-1}\).**
