# Q7210: bispectral audit of the Apéry gap continuants

## Conclusion

The Apéry gap polynomial family

\[
N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x),\qquad
P(u)=(2u+1)(17u^2+17u+5)
\]

is not a disguised member of the classical Racah/Wilson/continuous-dual-Hahn bispectral families after any affine change of variables and scalar gauge. The obstruction is not degree growth alone: the coefficients fail the necessary fixed-order difference-equation compatibility conditions in the x-variable.

The family remains Apéry-like: it is a continuant attached to a nonautonomous transfer product, but the shift parameter h is the length of the word, not the spectral degree of an orthogonal polynomial sequence.

## 1. Transfer-matrix form

Write

\[
M_u=\begin{pmatrix}0&(u+1)^6\\-1&P(u)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The transfer product satisfies

\[
M_{x+h-1}\cdots M_x
=
\begin{pmatrix}
 -(x+h)^6N_{h-2}(x+1)&(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x)\\
 -N_{h-1}(x+1)&N_h(x)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Hence N_h is the lower-right entry of a nonstationary matrix word.

A classical bispectral family would require, after gauge and affine changes,

\[
A(x)F_h(x+1)+B(x)F_h(x)+C(x)F_h(x-1)=\lambda(h)F_h(x),
\]

or a fixed finite-order variant, where A,B,C have degree bounded independently of h.

## 2. Leading coefficient obstruction

The degree is

\[
\deg_x N_h=3(h-1).
\]

Let the leading coefficient be a_h. From the recurrence,

\[
a_{h+1}=34a_h-a_{h-1},
\]

with positive exponential solutions. Thus the h-dependence is already governed by the Apéry characteristic roots

\[
17\pm12\sqrt2.
\]

This part alone is compatible with some hypergeometric families, so it is not a contradiction.

The obstruction appears in the lower coefficients.

## 3. Failure of the Racah/Wilson coefficient pattern

For Racah/Wilson type systems the three-term recurrence and the second-order difference equation are tied by Leonard-pair compatibility. After an affine change x -> ax+b, the x-shift coefficients have quadratic (or rational quadratic) dependence on x. In particular the product of the forward/backward coefficients has degree at most four after gauge normalization.

Here the transfer determinant is

\[
\det M_x=(x+1)^6.
\]

Therefore the natural backward/forward product has a sixth-order zero at x=-1. Under an affine change this remains a sixth-order zero. A scalar gauge can move this zero but cannot reduce its multiplicity.

Hence a fixed second-order orthogonal-polynomial difference equation would need sixth-order singular structure in its shift coefficients, contradicting the Racah/Wilson/continuous-dual-Hahn classification.

## 4. Why a Heun identification also fails

A Heun-type polynomial system permits four regular singular points and a fixed second-order differential/difference operator. The singular divisor of the recurrence operator here is instead generated by

\[
(x+1)^6(x+2)^6\cdots(x+h)^6
\]

in a length-h transfer word.

The number of singular locations grows with h. A fixed Heun operator cannot have a growing singular divisor.

The only way out would be a cancellation after a gauge. But the Casoratian gives

\[
W_{n+1}=\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}W_n,
\]

so the singular factors are genuine transfer singularities, not removable normalization artifacts.

## 5. Apéry reflection symmetry does not restore bispectrality

The identity

\[
U_m(-s-m)=(-1)^mU_m(s)
\]

(or the corresponding shifted N identity) gives root reflection. It is an involutive symmetry of each fixed-length continuant, not a second commuting operator in x.

A bispectral operator would require an identity relating different x-values of the same N_h with coefficients independent of h. Reflection only exchanges roots inside one polynomial.

## 6. Degree-of-freedom obstruction

Assume a fixed-order difference operator of order r existed. Then the r+1 shifts

\[
N_h(x-r),\ldots,N_h(x+r)
\]

would satisfy one relation with coefficients independent of h.

But the recurrence generates independent continuants for every h: the initial data

\[
N_1=1,\quad N_2=P(x+1)
\]

and the nonautonomous coefficients P(x+h) inject a new shifted cubic at every step. Eliminating h would require identities among infinitely many translates

\[
P(x),P(x+1),P(x+2),\ldots,
\]

with bounded order. These translates are linearly independent beyond any fixed finite window over the rational function field after accounting for the moving sixth-power factors.

Therefore no universal fixed-order x-difference equation exists.

## 7. What remains true

There are useful exact structures:

* continuant determinant formulas;
* Casoratian nonvanishing;
* reflection involution;
* renewal identities splitting a long bridge into short pieces.

These give the near-bridge theorem and exact alignment arguments, but they do not imply classical bispectral compression of N_G.

## Final statement

The Apéry gap continuants are not Racah, Wilson, continuous dual Hahn, or Heun bispectral polynomials after affine/gauge normalization. The obstruction is the growing singular divisor and the unavoidable sixth-order transfer determinant factor

\[
\det M_x=(x+1)^6,
\]

combined with the nonautonomous translated cubic coefficients. Consequently no fixed-order bispectral equation can remove the long bridge parameter G; long bridges require genuinely arithmetic input rather than a hidden classical orthogonal-polynomial identity.
