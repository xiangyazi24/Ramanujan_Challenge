# P32 companion expansion versus reciprocal moments

## Summary

The proposed route through the rational companion solution does not currently yield a contradiction from
\(R_1(a)=R_2(a)=R_3(a)=0\pmod p\).  The exact obstruction is that the known congruence
\(p^3c_{p+r}\equiv b_r\pmod p\) gives only the reduction of the shifted companion solution modulo one power after the forced factor \(p^3\).  The next coefficients require a full first-order perturbation of the recurrence coefficients and are not determined by the three reciprocal moments alone.

## 1. Correct normalization of the companion solution

Write the Apéry recurrence as

\[
(n+1)^3 y_{n+1}=A(n)y_n-n^3y_{n-1},
\qquad A(n)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5).
\]

Let \(b_0=1,b_1=5\) and \(c_0=0,c_1=1\).  The Casoratian

\[
W_n=b_nc_{n+1}-b_{n+1}c_n
\]

satisfies

\[
(n+1)^3W_n=-n^3W_{n-1}.
\]

Since \(W_0=1\),

\[
W_n=(-1)^n(n!)^3.
\]

This fixes the normalization of every variation-of-constants formula.  Any expansion with a different sign or an extra factor of \(p\) contradicts this identity.

## 2. What the congruence actually implies

For \(0\le r<p\), the established congruence is

\[
p^3c_{p+r}=b_r+p\alpha_r\pmod {p^2}
\]

for some integer residue \(\alpha_r\).  The leading term is not a statement that

\[
p^3c_{p+r}=b_r+O(p^2)
\]

uniformly as an identity in \(p\)-adic integers without specifying the recurrence lifting.  It only fixes the first residue class.

In particular, if \(r=a\), then

\[
p^3c_{p+a}\equiv b_a\pmod p.
\]

This does not contain a reciprocal-moment term because the moments arise from the partial-fraction expansion of the interpolation polynomial

\[
P_a(z)=Q_a(z)\sum_{u=1}^a\frac{b_{a-u}}{z-u},
\]

whereas \(c_{p+a}\) is a global recurrence solution.

## 3. Variation of constants and the missing moments

Using the fundamental pair \((b,c)\), a perturbation of a recurrence value at index \(a\) produces sums of the form

\[
\sum_{j} \frac{b_j}{(j+1)^3W_j}\,\Delta_j.
\]

The kernel contains factorial terms coming from

\[
W_j=(-1)^j(j!)^3.
\]

The local denominators in the interpolation moments,

\[
\frac1{u},\frac1{u^2},\frac1{u^3},
\]

would appear only after a separate partial-fraction identity relating the factorial kernel to the finite set \(u=1,\ldots,a\).  No such identity follows from the companion congruence alone.

Thus the asserted implication

\[
R_1(a)=R_2(a)=R_3(a)=0\pmod p
\Longrightarrow
p\mid\frac{p^3c_{p+a}-b_a}{p}
\]

is not justified.

## 4. Boundary check

The first cases illustrate the normalization.

For \(a=1\),

\[
R_1(1)=R_2(1)=R_3(1)=b_0=1,
\]

so the moment condition already fails.

For the companion solution,

\[
c_0=0,\qquad c_1=1,
\]

and the congruence gives

\[
p^3c_{p}=b_0=1\pmod p,
\]

which is consistent because \(p^3c_p\) is interpreted after cancellation in the rational recurrence solution, not as an ordinary integer divisibility assertion.

## 5. Exact no-go statement

The strongest conclusion obtainable from the current data is:

**Lemma.** The congruence
\[
p^3c_{p+a}\equiv b_a\pmod p
\]
contains only the zeroth-order boundary value of the companion solution.  Any contradiction from simultaneous vanishing of \(R_1,R_2,R_3\) requires an additional identity expressing the first three lifted coefficients of \(p^3c_{p+a}\) as interpolation moments.

**Proof.** The congruence fixes one residue class modulo \(p\).  The coefficients modulo \(p^2,p^3,p^4\) depend on the lifted recurrence coefficients and the normalized Casoratian kernel.  The reciprocal moments are coefficients of the Taylor expansion of \(P_a\) at zero.  No recurrence identity identifies these coefficient systems. Therefore the implication cannot be derived from the stated hypotheses. ∎

## Conclusion

No valid hit-selective valuation contradiction is obtained.  The companion expansion route is not false, but it requires a new theorem: an explicit formula for the next three lifting coefficients of \(p^3c_{p+a}\) in terms of \(R_1,R_2,R_3\) plus boundary terms.  The currently available congruence supplies only the leading coefficient and is insufficient.
