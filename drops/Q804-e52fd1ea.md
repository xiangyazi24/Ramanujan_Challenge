## Quantifier audit

The missing target is:

\[
\max_{q\le 64}\max_{h\le C_1\log n,q\mid h}P_{q;h}(n)=o(n/\log^2 n).
\]

None of the inspected claims establishes this. A result for fixed q and fixed h is insufficient unless it gives an effective bound uniform after inserting all \(h\le C_1\log n\).

Likewise, a bound for isolated direct hits does not automatically include reflected hits or the full LGTQ-2 carrier set.

## Possible overlooked useful statement

The strongest reusable component is the exact fixed-gap compression identity: if a single characteristic contributes many hits at one gap, its zero set is constrained. However, the reports do not prove that the total contribution of all characteristics satisfies a sub-logarithmic saving.

The missing theorem would need a form such as:

\[
\sum_{p\le n/q} \#\{u: p\mid b_u,\ p\mid b_{u+h}\}=o(n/\log^2 n)
\]

uniformly in the allowed h-range.

No audited report proves this statement.

## Growing-q versus power-scale issue

Several reports were dismissed for lacking growing-q uniformity, but the audit shows that this is not the only obstacle. Even at fixed q=1, the needed saving is stronger than the currently proved compression statements. A fixed-gap theorem with only qualitative sparsity would not suffice.

## Conclusion

No existing corpus theorem was found that upgrades the finite computations (21,100 transverse pairs, no repeated fixed fibres) into an asymptotic theorem. The bottleneck remains an explicit uniform fixed-gap Apéry correlation estimate, not merely a missing growing-q argument.
