# Apéry P3.2: exact Kummer linear program and a no-go for factorial-ratio carriers

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
T_n=\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n\},
\]

and

\[
R_n=\prod_{p\in T_n}p.
\]

I do **not** obtain a proof that `log R_n=o(n)`. I do obtain a precise no-go that is substantially broader than the plain central-window calculation.

The conclusions are:

1. For `p=n-r>n/2`, the exact Kummer valuation of every binomial coefficient is
   \[
   v_p\binom nk=\mathbf 1_{\{r<k<p\}}.
   \]
   Hence every signed product of binomial coefficients gives a literal linear program in interval-indicator columns.

2. More generally, for every integral factorial ratio
   \[
   F_n=\prod_i M_i(n)!^{e_i},\qquad e_i\in\mathbb Z,
   \]
   with `M_i(n)=O(n)`, the top-half valuation is
   \[
   v_p(F_n)=\sum_i e_i\Big\lfloor\frac{M_i(n)}p\Big\rfloor.
   \]
   Exact division does not evade the obstruction: integrality says that the final valuation is nonnegative.

3. If a Kummer/factorial carrier is required to contain **every prime in a positive-width subband** `(\alpha n,\beta n]`, then even its final integer value satisfies
   \[
   \log |F_n|\ge \vartheta(\beta n)-\vartheta(\alpha n)
   =(\beta-\alpha)n+o(n).
   \]
   Thus no signed product, multiscale ratio, or exact integral cancellation can give an `exp(o(n))` universal carrier for a positive-width band.

4. At the opposite extreme, the broader signed-ratio class is so expressive that it has **no residual top-half integrality coupling at all**. For `0\le r<n/2`,
   \[
   L_{n,r}:=n-r=(r+1)\frac{\binom n{r+1}}{\binom nr}
   \]
   is an integer factorial ratio and has top-half valuation vector equal to the coordinate vector at the possible prime `p=n-r`. Consequently every nonnegative valuation pattern on the top-half primes is realizable. In particular, if the target set is already known, the optimal carrier is exactly
   \[
   \prod_{p\in T_n}L_{n,n-p}=R_n.
   \]
   Thus target-adaptive signed cancellation does not contradict the universal no-go; it merely encodes the unknown answer.

5. Taking gcds with `b_n` performs exactly that target-adaptive selection:
   \[
   \gcd\!\left(b_n,\prod_{p\in B_n}p\right)
   =\prod_{\substack{p\in B_n\\p\mid b_n}}p.
   \]
   The complementary quotient is the non-target primorial. The identity partitions the universal linear height into target and non-target parts; proving that the target part is `o(n)` is exactly the original problem.

6. The premise `p\mid b_n` does not enter the Kummer linear program. For the distinguished Apéry sum it is an additive cancellation among `p`-adic units. Therefore a conditional carrier theorem
   \[
   p\mid b_n\Longrightarrow v_p(F_n)>0
   \]
   can only come from a genuinely new Apéry-specific congruence showing that all targets lie in a sparse deterministic factorial-support set. Kummer valuations alone cannot produce that implication.

So the answer is a **precise no-go for Kummer-only factorial-ratio carriers**, together with an exact description of why unrestricted signed ratios and gcds become circular. The factorial-ratio lane is closed unless one first proves new arithmetic information about the distinguished Apéry zero positions.

## 2. Exact top-half Kummer valuation

Fix `n` and a prime

\[
\frac n2<p\le n.
\]

Write

\[
r=n-p,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

### Proposition 2.1

For every integer `0\le k\le n`,

\[
\boxed{
 v_p\binom nk=\mathbf 1_{\{r<k<p\}}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Because `p\le n<2p`,

\[
v_p(n!)=1.
\]

Also `k!` contains `p` exactly when `k\ge p`, and `(n-k)!` contains `p` exactly when

\[
n-k\ge p
\iff k\le n-p=r.
\]

Since `2p>n`, the two denominator factorials cannot both contain `p`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_p\binom nk
&=v_p(n!)-v_p(k!)-v_p((n-k)!)\\
&=1-\mathbf 1_{\{k\ge p\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{k\le r\}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is `1` precisely for `r<k<p`, and `0` otherwise. ∎

For the central half `0\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor`, the inequality `k<p` is automatic, so

\[
\boxed{
 v_p\binom nk=\mathbf 1_{\{n-p<k\}}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is the exact step-vector behind every window construction.

## 3. The discrete signed-binomial linear program

Consider a signed product

\[
F_n=\prod_{j=1}^m\binom n{k_j}^{c_j},
\qquad c_j\in\mathbb Z,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and assume that the final rational number is a nonzero integer. Negative `c_j` are allowed; they represent exact division.

For each top-half prime `p=n-r`, Proposition 2.1 gives

\[
\boxed{
 w_p:=v_p(F_n)
 =\sum_{j=1}^m c_j\mathbf 1_{\{r<k_j<p\}}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

If all `k_j\le n/2`, this simplifies to

\[
 w_p=\sum_jc_j\mathbf 1_{\{r<k_j\}}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the columns of the Kummer matrix are nested step functions in `r=n-p`. Signed products merely take integer linear combinations of those columns.

Let `B_n` be any selected set of top-half primes, for example

\[
B_n(\alpha,\beta)=\{p:\alpha n<p\le\beta n\},
\qquad \frac12\le\alpha<\beta\le1.
\]

Let `S_n\subseteq B_n` be the set that the carrier must retain. The exact top-half carrier problem has the linear constraints

\[
\begin{cases}
w_p\ge0,&p\in B_n,\\
w_p\ge1,&p\in S_n.
\end{cases}
\tag{LP}
\]

The first line is forced by integrality of the final integer. The second line is target retention.

Define the top-half logarithmic content

\[
H_{B_n}(F_n)=\sum_{p\in B_n}v_p(F_n)\log p.
\]

Since this is part of the prime factorization of `F_n`,

\[
H_{B_n}(F_n)\le\log|F_n|.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The constraints immediately give the universal lower bound

\[
\boxed{
 \log|F_n|
 \ge H_{B_n}(F_n)
 \ge\sum_{p\in S_n}\log p.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the correct objective function. Archimedean cancellation in a numerator/denominator presentation cannot lower it, because it is computed from the prime factorization of the **final integral value**.

### Corollary 3.1: positive-width universal coverage is linear

If `S_n=B_n(\alpha,\beta)`, then

\[
\log|F_n|
\ge \vartheta(\beta n)-\vartheta(\alpha n)
=(\beta-\alpha)n+o(n).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Hence an integral signed product cannot cover every prime in a fixed positive-width subband with logarithmic height `o(n)`.

This does not depend on the number of scales, the signs of the exponents, or the choice of windows. It applies to the final integer after every exact cancellation.

### Corollary 3.2: partitioning into many bands does not help

Suppose the top half is partitioned into bands `B_{n,1},\ldots,B_{n,J}` and one constructs integral carriers `F_{n,j}` covering every prime in each band. Then

\[
\sum_j\log|F_{n,j}|
\ge\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\log p
=\frac n2+o(n),
\tag{3.7}
\]

up to the harmless endpoint primes already known not to be targets. Multiscale coverage only redistributes the candidate-primorial cost.

## 4. General integral factorial ratios

The preceding calculation is not special to binomial coefficients.

Let

\[
F_n=\prod_{i=1}^s M_i(n)!^{e_i},
\qquad e_i\in\mathbb Z,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
0\le M_i(n)\le Ln
\]

for a fixed `L`, and suppose `F_n` is a nonzero integer for all sufficiently large `n`.

For `p>n/2` and sufficiently large `n`, one has

\[
p^2>Ln\ge M_i(n).
\]

Legendre's formula therefore truncates after its first term:

\[
v_p(M_i(n)!)=\left\lfloor\frac{M_i(n)}p\right\rfloor.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(F_n)=\sum_{i=1}^s e_i\left\lfloor\frac{M_i(n)}p\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This includes:

- arbitrary ratios and signed products of binomial coefficients;
- products involving several linear scales of `n`;
- ordinary integral factorial ratios;
- exact quotients, provided the final value is integral;
- fixed products of multinomial coefficients.

The top-half problem is again a linear program. The matrix entry is now

\[
A_{p,i}=\left\lfloor\frac{M_i(n)}p\right\rfloor,
\]

and the final valuation vector is `Ae`.

### 4.1 Fixed-template continuum form

Assume more specifically that

\[
M_i(n)=\alpha_i n+O(1)
\]

with fixed nonnegative rational numbers `\alpha_i`. Put `x=p/n`. Away from the finitely many breakpoints `x=\alpha_i/j`,

\[
v_p(F_n)=W(x),
\qquad
W(x):=\sum_i e_i\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha_i}{x}\right\rfloor.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the top-half version of the Landau step function used in the theory of integral factorial ratios. The usual Landau/Bober theory packages global integrality into nonnegativity of related floor sums; here only the elementary top-half consequence is needed.

If `F_n` is integral for all large `n`, then

\[
W(x)\ge0
\]

on every open interval of constancy inside `(1/2,1)`. Otherwise the prime number theorem would supply primes `p/n` in an interval where the eventual valuation is negative.

For any fixed `1/2<a<b\le1`, weighted prime number theory gives

\[
\sum_{an<p\le bn}v_p(F_n)\log p
=n\int_a^bW(x)\,dx+o(n).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus if the template is required to cover every prime in `(an,bn]`, then `W(x)\ge1` there and

\[
\boxed{
 \log|F_n|\ge (b-a)n+o(n).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is a fixed-template no-go in the exact Landau-step-function language.

### 4.2 Why signed cancellation does not change the lower bound

A negative exponent may cancel a universal top-half valuation contributed by another factorial. But after all cancellations, the final valuation is the sum in (4.2). If the output is integral and must retain a prime, that final sum is still at least one. Therefore the prime contributes at least `log p` to the final integer.

The no-go is stated for the weakest possible height notion, `log|F_n|`. Any representation height such as

\[
\log(\text{numerator})+\log(\text{denominator})
\]

is at least as restrictive.

## 5. Exact saturation: adjacent ratios generate every top-half coordinate

The universal lower bound does **not** mean that integrality forces top-half primes to occur in large blocks once arbitrary signed ratios are allowed. In fact the opposite is true.

For every integer `r` with

\[
0\le r<\frac n2,
\]

define

\[
L_{n,r}:=n-r.
\]

The adjacent-binomial identity gives

\[
\boxed{
 L_{n,r}
 =(r+1)\frac{\binom n{r+1}}{\binom nr}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The factor `r+1` is itself a factorial ratio `(r+1)!/r!`, so `L_{n,r}` lies in the permitted multiplicative factorial-ratio class. The apparent denominator in the adjacent binomial ratio is cleared exactly.

### Theorem 5.1: full top-half valuation semigroup

Let

\[
I_n=\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n\}.
\]

For every `q\in I_n`,

\[
\boxed{
 v_q(L_{n,r})=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&q=n-r,\\
 0,&q\ne n-r.
 \end{cases}
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently the projection of the integral factorial-ratio monoid onto

\[
\mathbb N^{I_n},
\qquad
F\longmapsto (v_p(F))_{p\in I_n},
\]

is the full nonnegative orthant.

### Proof

Since `r<n/2`,

\[
\frac n2<L_{n,r}\le n.
\]

Take `q>n/2`. If `q\mid L_{n,r}`, then

\[
L_{n,r}=q,
\]

because the next positive multiple `2q` exceeds `n`. Also `q^2>n\ge L_{n,r}`, so the valuation is exactly one. This proves (5.2).

Now prescribe arbitrary nonnegative integers `u_p` for `p\in I_n`. The integer

\[
F=\prod_{p\in I_n}L_{n,n-p}^{u_p}
=\prod_{p\in I_n}p^{u_p}
\]

has top-half valuation vector `(u_p)`. ∎

### Interpretation

This theorem gives the exact limit of an integrality-based no-go.

- If the carrier must be chosen **without knowing which primes are targets**, covering an entire positive-width candidate band costs linear logarithmic height by Corollary 3.1.
- If the target set is already known, the class can isolate every target prime separately and produce the optimal carrier
  \[
  \prod_{p\in T_n}L_{n,n-p}=R_n.
  \]

Therefore unrestricted signed factorial ratios do not possess a hidden coupling that could force a saving. They can encode an arbitrary top-half support pattern. The missing issue is selecting the correct coordinates from the distinguished Apéry condition without already knowing `T_n`.

This is broader than the plain-window obstruction: adjacent signed ratios destroy the block structure completely, but the resulting coordinate selection is exactly the original target problem.

## 6. The conditional linear program is exactly the original radical

Let `B_n\subseteq I_n` be a candidate subband and write

\[
P(B_n)=\prod_{p\in B_n}p.
\]

Suppose we start from `P(B_n)` and allow exact integral cancellation prime by prime. Let `c_p\in\{0,1\}` indicate whether the universal factor `p` is canceled. The remaining carrier has logarithmic height

\[
\sum_{p\in B_n}(1-c_p)\log p.
\]

Target retention requires

\[
c_p=0\qquad(p\in T_n\cap B_n).
\]

The exact optimization problem is therefore

\[
\min\sum_{p\in B_n}(1-c_p)\log p
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
c_p=0\ (p\in T_n\cap B_n),
\quad c_p\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Its unique optimal support is to cancel every non-target and retain every target:

\[
c_p=
\begin{cases}
0,&p\mid b_n,\\
1,&p\nmid b_n.
\end{cases}
\]

The optimum is

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{p\in T_n\cap B_n}\log p.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The basis elements in Theorem 5.1 realize this optimum as an integral signed factorial-ratio product. Thus the conditional factorial-ratio LP is not infeasible; it is **exactly circular**. Its optimum is the quantity P3.2 asks us to bound.

If the cancellation choices must be made obliviously, before using any Apéry-specific information, then the worst possible target set is all of `B_n`, and no factor can be canceled. The cost is the full subband primorial.

If one instead proposes a deterministic sparse support set `S_n\subset B_n` and hopes to prove

\[
T_n\cap B_n\subseteq S_n,
\qquad
\sum_{p\in S_n}\log p=o(n),
\tag{6.3}
\]

then (6.3) would indeed yield a valid low-height factorial carrier. But the first inclusion is a new Apéry-specific theorem about horizontal zero positions. It is not a consequence of the Kummer valuation table or factorial-ratio integrality.

## 7. Gcds with `b_n`: exact target/non-target partition

For a squarefree candidate carrier `P(B_n)`,

\[
\boxed{
 G_n(B_n):=\gcd(b_n,P(B_n))
 =\prod_{\substack{p\in B_n\\p\mid b_n}}p.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The complementary quotient is

\[
\boxed{
 \frac{P(B_n)}{G_n(B_n)}
 =\prod_{\substack{p\in B_n\\p\nmid b_n}}p.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\log P(B_n)
=\log G_n(B_n)
 +\log\frac{P(B_n)}{G_n(B_n)}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

For a positive-width band, the left side is linear in `n`. Proving

\[
\log G_n(B_n)=o(n)
\]

is equivalent to proving that the non-target quotient carries all but `o(n)` of the candidate Chebyshev mass. The product formula does not favor one side of the partition.

### Boolean valuation lemma

At one candidate prime, let

\[
e=v_p(b_n),
\qquad u=v_p(P(B_n))=1.
\]

Products, gcds, lcms, and exact quotients act on valuations through addition, `min`, `max`, and nonnegative subtraction. Any final integral expression that

- has valuation `0` when `e=0`, and
- has valuation at least `1` when `e\ge1`,

has valuation at least

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{e\ge1\}}.
\]

The smallest possible target-retaining, non-target-canceling valuation is exactly that indicator, realized by `gcd(b_n,P(B_n))`. Hence no expression made from these operations can produce an integer strictly smaller in top-half prime content while still containing every target. It may reproduce `R_n`, add pollution, or remove a target; there is no fourth option.

This explains why a gcd with `b_n` is an exact reformulation but not a height estimate.

## 8. Why the premise `p\mid b_n` is invisible to Kummer valuations

The previous sections are valuation-theoretic. The distinguished Apéry sum shows exactly where the missing information lives.

For `p=n-r>n/2` and `p\ge7`, the Gessel--Lucas congruence gives

\[
b_n=b_{p+r}\equiv5b_r\pmod p.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus

\[
p\mid b_n\iff p\mid b_r.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Now inspect the summands of `b_r`. Because `r<p`,

\[
v_p\binom rk=0
\qquad(0\le k\le r).
\]

Also `r,k<p` and `r+k<2p`, so

\[
v_p\binom{r+k}k
=\mathbf 1_{\{r+k\ge p\}}
=\mathbf 1_{\{k\ge p-r\}}.
\]

After squaring,

\[
v_p\left(\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}k^2\right)
=
\begin{cases}
0,&k<p-r,\\
2,&k\ge p-r.
\end{cases}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 b_r\equiv
 \sum_{0\le k<\min(r+1,p-r)}
 \binom rk^2\binom{r+k}k^2
 \pmod p,
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

and **every summand retained in (8.4) is a `p`-adic unit**.

In the upper subband `p>2r`, equivalently `p>2n/3`, all `r+1` Apéry summands are units modulo `p`. In the lower top-half subband, the terms past the carry threshold vanish modulo `p^2`, but the surviving target equation is still cancellation among units.

Thus `p\mid b_n` is not a statement that some factorial atom acquires positive `p`-adic valuation. It is a statement that a distinguished sum of units cancels. This is the exact reason the conditional premise does not enter the factorial-ratio LP.

Any theorem placing every target prime in a sparse Kummer support must relate this additive unit cancellation to a separate factorial step function. No such relation is present in the current repository.

The endpoint cases are also transparent:

- `p=n` gives `r=0` and `b_r=1`;
- `n=2p-1` gives `r=p-1`, and only `k=0` survives in (8.4), so `b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p`.

They remove at most finitely many boundary candidates and do not affect the linear-height theorem.

## 9. Integer-valued Newton combinations

Additive integer-valued combinations are not literally factorial ratios, because cancellation between terms can create new divisibility. They are therefore the only item in the question not completely covered by the valuation LP.

However, three precise statements can be made.

### 9.1 Integer-valuedness alone gives no target implication

A Newton expansion

\[
N_n(x)=\sum_j a_{n,j}\binom xj,
\qquad a_{n,j}\in\mathbb Z,
\]

is integer-valued on integers. That property alone says nothing about

\[
p\mid b_n\Longrightarrow p\mid N_n(\text{chosen argument}).
\]

Such an implication would require a congruence connecting the Newton value to the Apéry unit cancellation in (8.4).

### 9.2 Multiplicative Newton differences are already saturated

The adjacent ratio

\[
(r+1)\frac{\binom n{r+1}}{\binom nr}=n-r
\]

is the multiplicative first-difference coordinate of the binomial/Newton basis. Theorem 5.1 shows that these coordinates isolate every top-half prime. Thus allowing signed multiplicative Newton factors does not create a new compression mechanism; it gives an exact coordinate encoding of arbitrary support.

### 9.3 Current universal additive carriers are already closed in the repository

The live `Q32_SEPARATION_ANALYSIS.md` records the stronger all-degree direct-carrier result: after imposing the full recurrence, the intersection of the moving target ideals evaluates to the unknown ideal `R_n\mathbb Z`; signed Newton cancellation of the central alias forces full candidate-primorial content, and all-degree alias saturation changes the target radical by only a fixed factor. This is consistent with the exact multiplicative saturation proved above.

Therefore the surviving Newton possibility is very narrow: one would need an **initial-state-specific additive identity** producing a nonzero integer `C_n` such that

\[
R_n\mid C_n,
\qquad
\log|C_n|=o(n),
\tag{9.1}
\]

where the smallness comes from a proved cancellation special to the Apéry initial state `(b_0,b_1)=(1,5)`, not from universal integer-valuedness, Kummer windows, or formal recurrence ideals. No such identity is currently known.

I am not counting (9.1) as progress by reformulation. It states exactly what a genuinely new additive escape would have to supply, and the repository's universal Newton constructions do not supply it.

## 10. Precise no-go theorem

The preceding arguments can be summarized as follows.

### Theorem 10.1: universal factorial-ratio carrier no-go

Fix `1/2<\alpha<\beta\le1`. Let `F_n` be any nonzero integer, however represented, such that

\[
p\mid F_n
\qquad
\text{for every prime }\alpha n<p\le\beta n.
\]

Then

\[
\log|F_n|\ge(\beta-\alpha)n+o(n).
\]

In particular, no integral factorial ratio, signed product of binomials, multiscale factorial ratio, or exact integral quotient can universally cover that subband with logarithmic height `o(n)`.

### Theorem 10.2: exact top-half saturation of the broad signed-ratio class

For fixed `n`, every nonnegative valuation vector on the primes `p\in(n/2,n]` is realized by an integral product of adjacent signed binomial ratios with elementary integer denominator clearing. Hence the broad class imposes no cross-prime top-half restriction beyond nonnegativity.

### Theorem 10.3: conditional optimum

For the actual target set `T_n`, the minimum logarithmic height of any integer divisible by every target is

\[
\log R_n,
\]

and this minimum is attained inside the signed factorial-ratio class. Choosing the minimizing factors requires exactly the target indicator `\mathbf1_{p\mid b_n}`.

Thus a factorial-ratio construction conditional on `p\mid b_n` either

1. is universal on a positive-width candidate set and has linear height;
2. uses a new Apéry-specific theorem to place all targets in a sparse deterministic support;
3. or adaptively selects the targets and reproduces `R_n` itself.

There is no Kummer-only fourth case.

## 11. Consequences for the proposed carrier families

### Ratios or signed products of binomial coefficients

Closed by Theorems 3.1 and 5.1. Signed ratios can cancel a window down to individual coordinates, but the coordinate selector is the unknown target set.

### Multiscale factorial ratios

Closed for Kummer-only universal coverage by (4.2)--(4.5). Every retained prime contributes its full `log p` to the final integer. Fixed-template step functions pay the Lebesgue/Chebyshev mass of every covered band.

### Integer-valued Newton combinations

Not covered by multiplicative valuation theory in complete generality, but universal Newton carriers are already saturated in the current repository. A viable escape would need a new Apéry-initial-state additive cancellation theorem of the form (9.1).

### Gcds with `b_n`

They perform the exact Boolean target selection (7.1). Their top-half part is the desired radical, while the complementary quotient is the non-target primorial. No height saving follows from the product formula.

### Dividing out universal non-target content

Algebraically possible:

\[
\frac{P(B_n)}{\prod_{p\in B_n,\ p\nmid b_n}p}
=\prod_{p\in B_n,\ p\mid b_n}p.
\]

But the denominator is defined by the non-target indicator and is equivalent to knowing the target set. Exact integrality is not the issue; target-selective construction and height control are.

## 12. Final conclusion

No low-height carrier is obtained.

What is proved is the strongest clean factorial-ratio no-go available for this question:

- the top-half Kummer valuations form an explicit linear program;
- universal positive-width coverage has unavoidable linear Chebyshev height;
- signed and multiscale exact division cannot lower the final prime-factor contribution;
- unrestricted adjacent signed ratios actually span every top-half valuation coordinate, so they can reproduce the target radical exactly but only by encoding the unknown target set;
- gcds with `b_n` make the same target/non-target partition explicitly;
- the Apéry target condition is additive cancellation among units and is invisible to the factorial valuation vector.

Therefore a proof of

\[
\log R_n=o(n)
\]

cannot come from a better arrangement of Kummer windows, signed factorial ratios, or universal primorial cancellation alone. It must prove new distinguished-Apéry horizontal information: either a sparse deterministic support theorem for the unit cancellations in (8.4), or a nonzero Apéry-specific additive carrier with genuinely sublinear logarithmic height.
