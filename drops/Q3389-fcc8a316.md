# 1. Why the exact regular initial condition matters

Write

\[
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]

At \(n=0\), (9) is not a generic second-order equation.  It reduces to

\[
b_1-5b_0=0.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the solution space that is regular through the singular origin is **one-dimensional**.

For \(n\ge1\) one may introduce the usual companion \(c_n\), for example

\[
c_0=0,\qquad c_1=1,
\]

and the regular-window Casoratian is

\[
b_nc_{n-1}-b_{n-1}c_n=-\frac1{n^3}.
\tag{12}
\]

For a target residue \(0<j<p\), the denominator \(j^3\) is a \(p\)-unit.  Hence \((b,c)\) is a perfectly good **local** basis near \(j\).  But \(c\) does not satisfy the regular singular condition (11).  A determinant argument that silently uses both ordinary columns \(b\) and \(c\) as regular initial data has therefore changed the problem.

This is precisely where the divided problem differs.  Modulo \(p\), the actual Apéry state lies on the regular line.  After dividing a quantity known to vanish mod \(p\), a transverse coefficient may reappear.  That coefficient is first-Witt/Bockstein data.  It is not determined by the fact that the ordinary transverse coefficient was zero.

This is the conceptual meaning of the missing scalar below.

Thus the forward transfer matrix has a denominator with exact valuation

For \(a=1,2\) and odd \(p\), this is exactly \(3\).  Integrality of the Apéry solution says that the numerator in (39) is divisible by \(p^3\).  But division by \(p^3\) consumes three digits:

* to recover \(b_{ap}\bmod p^2\), it must be controlled modulo \(p^5\).

# 6. Why the regular low state cannot determine the missing scalar

Let \(A\) be the localization obtained by inverting the q=6 core-unit denominators.  At a target prime \(p\), \(p\) is a non-zero-divisor in the relevant local ring.

The fundamental first-Witt fact from Q3369 is:

> There is no function of \(x\bmod p\) that recovers \(x/p\bmod p\) for all \(x\in pA/p^2A\).

Indeed every \(x\in pA/p^2A\) has the same ordinary reduction \(0\), whereas

\[
pA/p^2A\xrightarrow{/p}A/pA
\]

is an isomorphism.

Applied here, the ordinary singular equations determine the special-fibre state but not the first divided transverse coordinate.  The exact singular initial condition (11) makes this sharper rather than weaker:

* ordinary regular state: one-dimensional;
* generic companion direction: excluded by (11);
* first-divided transverse direction: can be nonzero even though its ordinary reduction is zero.

This is why a generic second-order fundamental-matrix determinant is not the missing theorem.  The nonregular companion supplies a second ordinary column, but using it as regular initial data violates (11).  The legal second coordinate is the divided/Bockstein lift, and its value is precisely what has not been fixed.

In the endpoint coordinates this lift is \(C_{47,K}\).  In reflected-tail coordinates it is \(\Theta_{47}\).  In source/Green coordinates it is

\boxed{
\beta_p(\Omega)=\Gamma(\Omega)/p\pmod p.

\tag{40}

For the point source this specializes to

\[
\beta_p=\eta=b_j/p\pmod p.

But (42) is explicitly \(p\)-dependent and does not provide a prime-independent \(e^{o(m)}\) characteristic-zero lift.  This is the same obstruction in a different language.

# 10. Final answer to the determinant question

There are two distinct determinant statements, and conflating them is the trap.

### Formal corrected endpoint determinant

\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-2\epsilon\\
5&-20\epsilon
\end{pmatrix}
=-10\epsilon.
\]

This has uniformly bounded \(p\)-adic valuation, and is a unit for every core \(p>5\).

### Actual divided endpoint system

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
T_{7,K}/p\\T_{4,K}/p
\end{pmatrix}
\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-2\epsilon&0\\
5&-20\epsilon&-2
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
\eta\\\widetilde D_a\\C_{47,K}
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod p.
\]

This is a \(2\times3\) system.  The third column is genuine.  The regular singular condition \(b_1=5b_0\) is already responsible for the coefficient \(5\); it does **not** imply \(C_{47,K}=0\).

So the correct theorem-level verdict is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{unit minor, but no closed }2\times2\text{ transport; one first-Witt scalar is missing.}
}
\]

The missing scalar can be represented as

\[
\boxed{C_{47,K}=J_{4,K}-5J_{7,K}},
\]

or by the characteristic-zero reflected tail

\[
\boxed{\Theta_{47}=R_4-R_7},
\]

or by the Green/Bockstein coordinate

\[
\boxed{\beta_p(\Omega)=\Gamma(\Omega)/p\bmod p}.
\]

Until one of these receives a prime-uniform, core-unit-denominator, \(e^{o(m)}\) characteristic-zero lift, the valuation-one q=6 core radical remains open.  No finite scan changes this conclusion.
