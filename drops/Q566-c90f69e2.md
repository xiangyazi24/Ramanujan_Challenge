# Q566 — Apéry Fourier pseudorandomness: what is already elementary, and what is actually missing

## Executive verdict

There is a sharp correction to the proposed target.

1. **For a fixed prime \(p\asymp X\), the semiprime-inverse cosine mixing is already provable, uniformly in the zero location \(r_0\), by one application of finite Fourier orthogonality.** No monodromy, Weil bound, Kloosterman sheaf, or Pólya–Vinogradov inequality is needed.

2. If \(L=\#\{q\text{ prime}:X<q\le 2X,\ q\ne p\}\), then
   \[
   \sum_{q<r}\left|\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi(2r_0+1)\overline{qr}}p\right)\right|^2
   =\frac12\binom L2+O(\sqrt p\,L).
   \]
   Thus the sum is of order \(L^2\), not \(L\). If the \(P\) in the question denotes the number of primes, the displayed \(O(P)\) target is false. If \(P\) denotes the number of prime pairs, it is trivially true, and the meaningful result is the asymptotic average \(1/2\).

3. The origin of \(r_0\) as an Apéry zero is irrelevant to this fixed-\(p\) equidistribution. The result holds for **every adversarially chosen nonzero scalar** \(c\in\mathbb F_p^\times\). Multiplication by \(c=2r_0+1\) does not create the difficulty.

4. The genuine obstruction appears only after a cross-prime square is opened. For \(p\ne\ell\), the phase becomes
   \[
   e_{p\ell}\!\left((\ell z-pz')\overline{qr}\right),
   \qquad z\in Z_p,\ z'\in Z_\ell,
   \]
   with modulus \(p\ell\asymp X^2\), while each prime variable has only \(X/\log X\), just below the square-root scale. This is the real missing theorem.

5. **Nonzero-frequency Fourier cancellation cannot imply M1 or M2.** Those are zero-mode statements because
   \[
   \widehat Z_p(0)=|Z_p|.
   \]
   Consequently, a “single AFP theorem” can be formulated, but it must explicitly include a zero-mode second-moment bound together with cross-modulus Fourier dispersion. The single theorem is a package of two logically independent phenomena.

The correct slogan is therefore:

> The within-prime semiprime mixer is elementary; the missing ingredient is cross-modulus reciprocal dispersion plus zero-mode control.

## 1. Notation

Let
\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},
\qquad L=|\mathcal P_X|.
\]
For fixed \(p\in\mathcal P_X\), put
\[
\mathcal Q_p=\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p\},
\qquad L_p=|\mathcal Q_p|,
\qquad N_p=\binom{L_p}{2}.
\]
Write \(\bar q\) and \(\overline{qr}\) for inverses modulo the modulus currently in use. Define
\[
F_p(a)=\widehat Z_p(a)=\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(az),
\qquad e_m(t)=e^{2\pi i t/m}.
\]

Because \(\mathcal P_X\) lies in an interval of length \(X<p\), distinct primes in \(\mathcal Q_p\) give distinct residues modulo \(p\), hence distinct inverses modulo \(p\). This elementary fact is all that the fixed-\(p\) argument needs.

## 2. The universal local mixing lemma

### Lemma 2.1 — weighted reciprocal-product Fourier bound

Let \(p\) be prime, let \(A,B\subset\mathbb F_p^\times\), and let \(\alpha_x,\beta_y\) be arbitrary complex coefficients supported on \(A,B\). For every \(c\in\mathbb F_p^\times\),
\[
\left|
 \sum_{x\in A}\sum_{y\in B}
 \alpha_x\beta_y e_p(cxy)
\right|
\le \sqrt p\,\|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Extend \(\alpha,\beta\) by zero to all of \(\mathbb F_p\). By Cauchy–Schwarz and additive orthogonality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\sum_x\alpha_x\sum_y\beta_y e_p(cxy)\right|^2
&\le \|\alpha\|_2^2
   \sum_{x\bmod p}\left|\sum_y\beta_y e_p(cxy)\right|^2\\
&=\|\alpha\|_2^2\,p\|\beta\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking square roots proves (2.1). \(\square\)

Apply this with
\[
A=B=\{\bar q:q\in\mathcal Q_p\},
\qquad \alpha=\beta=1.
\]
Then, uniformly for every \(c\ne0\pmod p\),
\[
B_p(c):=\sum_{q,r\in\mathcal Q_p}e_p(c\overline{qr})
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad
|B_p(c)|\le\sqrt p\,L_p.
\tag{2.2}
\]
For unordered distinct pairs, define
\[
B_p^{\triangle}(c)=\sum_{\substack{q<r\\q,r\in\mathcal Q_p}}
 e_p(c\overline{qr}).
\]
Since the phase is symmetric in \(q,r\),
\[
B_p^{\triangle}(c)
=\frac12\left(
 B_p(c)-\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_p}e_p(c\bar q^{\,2})
\right),
\]
and hence
\[
|B_p^{\triangle}(c)|
\le\frac{\sqrt p\,L_p+L_p}{2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

This estimate is universal: it uses neither primality beyond distinct residues, nor any property of the Apéry sequence.

## 3. Answer to (A): the cosine sum

Assume
\[
Z_p=\{r_0,p-1-r_0\}
\]
has two distinct elements. Put
\[
c_p=2r_0+1\pmod p.
\]
Then \(c_p\ne0\pmod p\): otherwise \(r_0=(p-1)/2\), which is the fixed point of \(r\mapsto p-1-r\), so the alleged pair would have only one element.

The palindromic Fourier transform is
\[
F_p(a)
=2e^{-\pi ia/p}
 \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi c_pa}{p}\right),
\]
up to the harmless choice of an integer representative for \(c_pa\). Therefore
\[
|F_p(a)|^2
=4\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi c_pa}{p}\right).
\]
Using
\[
\cos^2(\pi t/p)=\frac12+\frac12\Re e_p(t),
\]
we obtain the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_p
&:=\sum_{\substack{q<r\\q,r\in\mathcal Q_p}}
 \left|\cos\!\left(
   \frac{\pi c_p\overline{qr}}p
 \right)\right|^2\\
&=\frac{N_p}{2}+\frac12\Re B_p^{\triangle}(c_p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Combining (3.1) with (2.3) gives the explicit bound
\[
\boxed{
\left|C_p-\frac{N_p}{2}\right|
\le\frac{\sqrt p\,L_p+L_p}{4}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Since \(p\asymp X\) and \(L_p\asymp X/\log X\),
\[
C_p
=\frac12N_p+O\!\left(\frac{X^{3/2}}{\log X}\right),
\tag{3.3}
\]
while
\[
N_p\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2}.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{C_p}{N_p}
=\frac12+O\!\left(\frac{\log X}{\sqrt X}\right).
\tag{3.4}
\]

### What this says about the proposed \(C\cdot P\) bound

There are two possible meanings of \(P\).

* If \(P=L_p\), the number of available primes, then the requested bound \(C_p\ll P\) is false. In fact \(C_p\sim L_p^2/4\).
* If \(P=N_p\), the number of unordered prime pairs, then \(C_p\le P\) is immediate from \(\cos^2\le1\). The nontrivial conclusion is (3.4), namely an average of \(1/2\) with a power-saving relative error.

### Which tool proves it?

* **Weil/Kloosterman:** unnecessary here.
* **Pólya–Vinogradov:** not the right tool; it concerns one-dimensional character sums, whereas the two free variables permit exact Fourier orthogonality.
* **Direct computation:** useful as a diagnostic, but mathematically superseded by (3.2), which is uniform in \(p\) and \(r_0\).

The important conceptual correction is that the uncentered nonnegative energy is supposed to be large. Cancellation occurs only in
\[
\sum_{q<r}\left(
 \cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi c_p\overline{qr}}p\right)-\frac12
\right),
\]
not in the raw sum of \(\cos^2\)'s.

## 4. Answer to (B): yes locally, but this is not AFP across primes

For every \(h,c\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), (2.2) gives
\[
\left|
\frac1{L_p^2}
\sum_{q,r\in\mathcal Q_p}
 e_p(hc\overline{qr})
\right|
\le\frac{\sqrt p}{L_p}
\ll\frac{\log X}{\sqrt X}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
The same estimate, with an \(O(1/L_p)\) correction, holds for \(q<r\). Consequently the multiset
\[
\{c\overline{qr}\pmod p:q,r\in\mathcal Q_p\}
\]
is additively equidistributed against every fixed Fourier mode, uniformly for every nonzero \(c\).

More quantitatively, for an interval \(I\subset\mathbb F_p\), Erdős–Turán together with (4.1) gives
\[
\frac1{L_p^2}
\left|
 \#\{(q,r):c\overline{qr}\in I\}
 -\frac{|I|}{p}L_p^2
\right|
\ll\frac{(\log X)^2}{\sqrt X}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
The precise logarithm is unimportant here; the key point is a power saving.

### The Apéry zero \(r_0\) plays no role

The claim does not follow from the assertion that \(r_0\) is a zero of a Mellin transform. It follows because \(c_p=2r_0+1\ne0\), and (4.1) is uniform in every nonzero \(c_p\). Even an adversary may choose \(c_p\) after seeing \(p\) and the set of primes.

Thus the heuristic “\(r_0\) and \((qr)^{-1}\) have no algebraic relation” is much weaker than the truth needed locally: **no independence between them is required at all**.

### Why this does not solve AKD\(_3\)

Let
\[
\mathcal A_X(q,r)
=\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_X\\p\notin\{q,r\}}}
 F_p(\overline{qr}\bmod p).
\]
The second moment relevant to Q554/Q565 has the shape
\[
\sum_{q<r}|\mathcal A_X(q,r)|^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Opening the square creates terms with two different output primes \(p\ne\ell\). For \(z\in Z_p\) and \(z'\in Z_\ell\), Chinese remaindering gives the exact identity
\[
 e_p(z\overline{qr})
 \overline{e_\ell(z'\overline{qr})}
 =e_{p\ell}\!\left(
   (\ell z-pz')\overline{qr}
  \right).
\tag{4.4}
\]
For Apéry, \(0\notin Z_p\) because \(b_0=1\). Hence, when \(p\ne\ell\), the coefficient \(\ell z-pz'\) is a unit modulo \(p\ell\).

Now the modulus is
\[
m=p\ell\asymp X^2,
\]
and each prime variable has cardinality
\[
L\asymp\frac{X}{\log X}
=\frac{\sqrt m}{\log X}.
\]
The analogue of (2.1) over \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) gives only
\[
\left|\sum_{q,r}e_m(A\overline{qr})\right|
\le\sqrt m\,L,
\tag{4.5}
\]
whereas the trivial bound is \(L^2\). In this range
\[
\sqrt m\,L\asymp\frac{X^2}{\log X}
>\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2}\asymp L^2,
\]
so (4.5) gives no cancellation at all: the trivial bound wins.

This is the exact square-root barrier. The fixed-\(p\) problem has modulus \(p\asymp X\) and \(L\gg\sqrt p\); the cross-prime problem has modulus \(p\ell\asymp X^2\) and \(L<\sqrt{p\ell}\). Those are analytically different regimes.

The true off-diagonal object is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak O_X(h)
={}&\sum_{\substack{p,\ell\in\mathcal P_X\\p\ne\ell}}
   \sum_{z\in Z_p}\sum_{z'\in Z_\ell}
   \sum_{\substack{q<r\\q,r\in\mathcal P_X\\
                    \{q,r\}\cap\{p,\ell\}=\varnothing}}
 e_{p\ell}\!\left(
  h(\ell z-pz')\overline{qr}
 \right).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
A random-scale estimate is
\[
|\mathfrak O_X(h)|\ll L^3
\tag{4.7}
\]
uniformly for the frequencies \(h\) required by Q554/Q565. This, not (3.2), is the genuinely missing Fourier theorem.

Notice also that one must not take absolute values separately for every \(p,\ell,z,z'\) unless one can prove nearly square-root cancellation in the \(q,r\)-sum. The weakest useful statement preserves cancellation across the entire signed sum (4.6).

## 5. The same-prime diagonal is completely controlled

The local lemma extends immediately from a doublet to an arbitrary zero set.

For fixed \(p\),
\[
|F_p(a)|^2
=|Z_p|+
 \sum_{\substack{z,z'\in Z_p\\z\ne z'}}
 e_p(a(z-z')).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_p
&:=\sum_{\substack{q<r\\q,r\in\mathcal Q_p}}
 |F_p(\overline{qr})|^2\\
&=N_p|Z_p|+
 \sum_{\substack{z,z'\in Z_p\\z\ne z'}}
 B_p^{\triangle}(z-z').
\end{aligned}
\]
Using (2.3),
\[
\boxed{
\left|E_p-N_p|Z_p|\right|
\le
\frac{|Z_p|(|Z_p|-1)}2
 (\sqrt p\,L_p+L_p).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Thus, once
\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}|Z_p|^2\ll L
\tag{5.2}
\]
is known, summing (5.1) over \(p\) gives
\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}E_p\ll L^3.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Indeed, the main term is \(\asymp L^2\sum_p|Z_p|\ll L^3\), and the error is
\[
\ll \sqrt X\,L\sum_p|Z_p|^2
\ll\sqrt X\,L^2
=o(L^3),
\]
because \(\sqrt X/L\asymp\log X/\sqrt X\).

Therefore the \(p=\ell\) diagonal in (4.3) is not missing. Only \(p\ne\ell\) remains.

This matches the warning already present in the repository’s complete \(E_2\)-dispersion discussion: the sparse semiprime variable and its frequency-dependent coefficients prevent a scalar local estimate from being substituted for the required weighted cross-prime operator estimate.

## 6. Answer to (C): four or six zeros

Suppose the palindromic zero set consists of \(u\) reflected pairs,
\[
Z_p=\bigcup_{j=1}^u\{r_j,p-1-r_j\},
\qquad d_j=2r_j+1.
\]
Then
\[
F_p(a)
=2e^{-\pi ia/p}
 \sum_{j=1}^u
 \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi d_ja}{p}\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Thus \(|Z_p|=4\) or \(6\) produces two or three cosine waves. Expanding their square creates only \(O(u^2)\) nonzero frequencies \(d_i\pm d_j\). Formula (5.1) controls all of them at once.

### Fixed \(p\)

For \(u=2\) or \(3\), this is **not conceptually harder** than the doublet case. It merely changes the constant by \(O(u^2)\). The average local energy is
\[
\sum_{q<r}|F_p(\overline{qr})|^2
=N_p|Z_p|+O\!\left(
 |Z_p|^2\sqrt p\,L_p
\right).
\tag{6.2}
\]
There is no need to analyze the individual cosines separately.

### Across different primes

More pairs are not automatically helpful. Their phases can reinforce rather than cancel. In (4.6), the number of phase combinations is \(|Z_p||Z_\ell|\), so the correct control is through first and second moments of \(|Z_p|\), not through a claim that “more cosines smooth the sum.”

If the number of pairs were uniformly bounded, the extra cost would be harmless. Since uniform boundedness is itself unproved, the project must retain the zero-mode moment bounds.

If the reflection involution has its fixed point \((p-1)/2\) in \(Z_p\), one adds one character term to (6.1). The general expansion (5.1) remains valid without modification.

## 7. A logical correction: nonzero Fourier pseudorandomness does not imply M1 or M2

M1 and the pair-zero target depend on
\[
F_p(0)=|Z_p|.
\]
No estimate involving only \(F_p(a)\) for \(a\ne0\) can control this mode in general.

The extreme example is \(Z_p=\mathbb F_p\): every nonzero Fourier coefficient vanishes exactly, while \(|Z_p|=p\). Even after imposing reflection symmetry and excluding adjacent points, probabilistic constructions can have large cardinality and square-root-size nonzero Fourier coefficients. Thus palindromic nonzero-frequency cancellation still does not determine the zero mode.

For the project’s notation \(u_p=|Z_p|/2\) in the even palindromic case,
\[
D_p=\binom{u_p}{2}\le\frac{|Z_p|^2}{8}.
\]
Hence the single zero-mode estimate
\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}|Z_p|^2\ll L
\tag{7.1}
\]
implies both
\[
\sum_p|Z_p|\ll L
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_pD_p\ll L.
\tag{7.2}
\]
The first follows by Cauchy–Schwarz; the second is immediate.

So “AFP implies M1 and M2” is correct only if AFP is defined to include (7.1). It is not a consequence of cosine equidistribution at nonzero frequencies.

## 8. Answer to (D): a single project-ready AFP statement

There are two useful formulations.

### 8.1 The clean one-line Hilbert-energy formulation

For each dyadic \(X\), let \(L=|\mathcal P_X|\). For every frequency \(h\) required by the Q554/Q565 reduction, assert
\[
\boxed{
L^2\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}|F_p(0)|^2
+
\sum_{\substack{q<r\\q,r\in\mathcal P_X}}
\left|
 \sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_X\\p\notin\{q,r\}}}
 F_p(h\overline{qr}\bmod p)
\right|^2
\ll L^3.
}
\tag{AFP\(_2\)}
\]
If Q554 introduces bounded multiplicities or fixed smooth weights, the second term should be replaced by the exact weighted form from that reduction; the scale remains \(L^3\).

This is genuinely a single statement, and its two terms are on the same natural scale.

* The first term gives \(\sum_p|Z_p|^2\ll L\), hence M1 and the pair-zero bound M2/Target A.
* The second term is precisely the semiprime-inverse Fourier second moment requested in Q565.
* Q554’s near-injection transfers this second moment to the relevant CRT configurations.
* The Q565 reduction then gives AKD\(_3\) in its second-moment form.
* Together with the already established deterministic reductions, this yields \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) and then the Apéry GCD conjecture.

The first term can be viewed as the zero-frequency component of AFP, amplified by the number \(\asymp L^2\) of semiprime samples. Thus (AFP\(_2\)) really is a Fourier-energy theorem, but it does not hide the zero mode.

### 8.2 The analytically minimal decomposition

For proving the theorem, the useful split is:

#### Zero-mode moment
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}|Z_p|^2\ll L.
}
\tag{ZM\(_2\)}
\]

#### Cross-modulus reciprocal dispersion
\[
\boxed{
|\mathfrak O_X(h)|\ll L^3,
}
\tag{CMRD}
\]
where \(\mathfrak O_X(h)\) is the explicit signed sum in (4.6), with the exact Q554/Q565 weights if present.

Then:

1. (ZM\(_2\)) gives M1 and M2.
2. Lemma 2.1 and (ZM\(_2\)) give the same-prime diagonal bound (5.3).
3. (CMRD) gives the different-prime off-diagonal.
4. Their sum is (AFP\(_2\)).

This decomposition identifies the genuine research target. The local cosine theorem is already proved and should not be listed as an assumption.

## 9. What kind of theorem could prove CMRD?

CMRD is not naturally a “difference-Galois Chebotarev theorem.” Difference Galois theory may explain algebraic irreducibility of the recurrence, but it does not supply a Frobenius family whose conjugacy classes are being sampled by \(q,r,p,\ell\). The needed result is an analytic large-sieve/dispersion theorem for reciprocal phases over a family of composite moduli, with coefficients supplied by Apéry zero sets.

The promising analytic shape is:

> **Averaged Type-II reciprocal large sieve over \(p\ell\).** Bound the signed average, over comparable primes \(p,\ell\), of bilinear prime-supported sums
> \[
> \sum_{q,r\sim X}\alpha_q\beta_r
> e_{p\ell}(A_{p,\ell}\overline{qr}),
> \]
> in the critical range \(q,r\) of length \(\sqrt{p\ell}/\log X\), allowing the coefficients \(A_{p,\ell}=h(\ell z-pz')\) and the Apéry zero-set weights.

The emphasis must be on **averaging over \(p,\ell\)** and preserving the full signed form. A pointwise estimate for every modulus followed by absolute summation is substantially stronger than Q565 needs and is unlikely to be the optimal route.

### Relation to existing work

As of 19 August 2026:

* Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel, *Algebraic trace functions over the primes* (arXiv:1211.6043), gives powerful estimates when the summand is a bounded-conductor trace function over a fixed finite field. The obstacle identified in `proof.tex` remains: \(1_{b_r=0}\), and hence \(F_p\), is not currently realized as such a uniformly bounded-complexity trace function.

* Kowalski–Michel–Sawin, *Bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums and applications* (arXiv:1511.01636; Annals of Mathematics 2017), breaks the Pólya–Vinogradov barrier for prime-modulus Kloosterman-type trace functions. It is important technology, but it does not directly state CMRD for the moving composite modulus \(p\ell\) and the nonlinear Apéry zero-set coefficients.

* Forey–Fresán–Kowalski, *Arithmetic Fourier transforms over finite fields* (arXiv:2109.11961), proves equidistribution for Fourier transforms of genuine trace functions/perverse sheaves. It does not manufacture a sheaf for the zero indicator, nor does it tensor data living in different characteristics \(p\) and \(\ell\).

* Korolev, *Kloosterman sums with primes to composite moduli* (arXiv:1911.09981), obtains nontrivial one-prime reciprocal estimates for lengths at least roughly \(m^{3/4+\varepsilon}\). Here the available prime length is only \(m^{1/2}/\log X\), so that theorem is outside the needed range.

* Pascadi, *Non-abelian amplification and bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums* (arXiv:2511.08445), is the most relevant recent development: it treats square-root-length bilinear Kloosterman sums for composite moduli and explicitly obtains savings for \(m=p\ell\) with \(p\asymp\ell\). However, CMRD involves the raw reciprocal-product kernel \(e_m(A\overline{qr})\), prime-supported variables, moving coefficients \(A=\ell z-pz'\), and an average over the moduli themselves. A completion/Fourier transform reducing this exact operator to Pascadi’s Kloosterman matrix, with all weights and averages preserved, has not yet been supplied. This is a concrete route to investigate, not a theorem one can presently cite as closing AFP.

* Shparlinski, *On bilinear exponential and character sums with reciprocals of polynomials* (arXiv:1504.03192), handles related bilinear reciprocal phases over prime fields, but not the full moving-composite-modulus operator above.

* The 2026 preprint of Dong–Robles–Zeindler on bilinear Kloosterman fractions (arXiv:2601.00292) should not currently be used as a claimed improvement: its arXiv comment records a missing factor that invalidates the advertised improved bound, although parts of the argument may remain useful.

Thus no cited theorem presently gives CMRD as a black box. Pascadi’s non-abelian amplification is the nearest modern technology, but a new reduction and probably a modulus-average argument are still required.

## 10. Recommended next target

The campaign should replace the broad phrase “prove AFP” by the following exact theorem.

### Cross-modulus Apéry reciprocal dispersion theorem

For dyadic \(X\), \(L=|\mathcal P_X|\), and each required nonzero \(h\), prove
\[
\left|
\sum_{\substack{p\ne\ell\\p,\ell\in\mathcal P_X}}
\sum_{z\in Z_p}\sum_{z'\in Z_\ell}
\sum_{\substack{q<r\\q,r\in\mathcal P_X\\
                 \{q,r\}\cap\{p,\ell\}=\varnothing}}
 e_{p\ell}\!\left(
 h(\ell z-pz')\overline{qr}
 \right)
\right|
\ll L^3.
\tag{10.1}
\]
In parallel, prove the independent zero-mode estimate
\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}|Z_p|^2\ll L.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Equation (10.1) is exactly where a new averaged composite-modulus Kloosterman-fraction theorem would enter. Equation (10.2) is the crystalline/Mellin anti-concentration problem already identified for M1/M2. Combining them gives the one-line (AFP\(_2\)) package.

### A useful computational diagnostic

Computation should distinguish three scales rather than merely plot \(|F_p(a)|\):

1. Verify the local normalized error
   \[
   \frac{1}{\sqrt p\,L_p}
   \sum_{q<r}e_p(c_p\overline{qr}),
   \]
   which is already rigorously bounded.
2. For each \(p\ne\ell\), record
   \[
   S_{p,\ell,z,z'}
   =\sum_{q<r}e_{p\ell}((\ell z-pz')\overline{qr}).
   \]
   This tests whether cancellation is pointwise in the modulus.
3. Record the signed aggregate \(\mathfrak O_X(1)/L^3\). This tests the weaker and more relevant possibility that cancellation appears only after averaging over \(p,\ell,z,z'\).

The second and third diagnostics answer different mathematical questions; absolute values must not be introduced before the third aggregate if the intended theorem is (10.1).

## Final answers to (A)–(D)

**(A)** The stated \(O(P)\) claim is false if \(P\) is the number of primes and trivial if \(P\) is the number of pairs. The sharp centered theorem is
\[
\sum_{q<r}\cos^2\!\left(
 \frac{\pi(2r_0+1)\overline{qr}}p
\right)
=\frac12\binom{L_p}{2}
+O(\!\sqrt p\,L_p),
\]
with the explicit constant in (3.2). It follows from finite Fourier orthogonality, not Weil or Pólya–Vinogradov.

**(B)** Yes: for fixed \(p\), \((2r_0+1)(qr)^{-1}\) is additively equidistributed over prime pairs with Fourier error \(O(\log X/\sqrt X)\), uniformly in \(r_0\). The Mellin origin of \(r_0\) is unnecessary. This gives only the same-prime diagonal, not cross-prime AFP.

**(C)** Two or three cosines are no harder locally; they cost only \(O(|Z_p|^2)\) frequencies and are covered by (5.1). Globally they create \(|Z_p||Z_\ell|\) cross phases, so zero-mode moments remain essential and no automatic extra cancellation follows.

**(D)** A full single theorem can be stated as (AFP\(_2\)). Its zero-frequency term gives M1 and M2, and its semiprime Fourier-energy term gives the Q554/Q565 second-moment input. Analytically, however, it decomposes into the independent zero-mode estimate (ZM\(_2\)) and the genuinely missing cross-modulus reciprocal-dispersion theorem (CMRD). The latter—not the fixed-\(p\) cosine sum—is the new theorem the campaign needs.
