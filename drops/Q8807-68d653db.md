# Q8807 — Can the Apéry modular form constrain the zero sets Z_p?

## Executive verdict

The modular form viewpoint is structurally relevant, but it does **not** currently turn the zero condition
\[
b_d\equiv0\pmod p
\]
into a bounded-complexity Sato–Tate/Frobenius condition. The obstruction is that the Apéry numbers are coefficients of a Picard–Fuchs period in the parameter direction, not traces of powers of one fixed Frobenius representation.

The useful conclusion is:

- the K3 motive explains why reflection, Lucas congruences, and Frobenius phenomena occur;
- the weight-4 newform controls special fibers and global arithmetic;
- but |Z_p| is a moving-parameter coefficient-vanishing problem, not a fixed Frobenius conjugacy-class problem.

Therefore the modular form alone is unlikely to prove |Z_p|=O(1). It may still provide average information.

---

## 1. Correct Frobenius interpretation

A key correction is that there are not fixed eigenvalues
\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
\]
such that
\[
b_d\equiv \alpha^d+\beta^d+\gamma^d\pmod p.
\]

That would make the zero problem a linear recurrence in the exponent d with fixed characteristic polynomial. The Apéry sequence is instead the coefficient sequence of a period solution of a differential equation.

The recurrence
\[
(d+1)^3b_{d+1}=P(d)b_d-d^3b_{d-1}
\]
is a nonconstant-coefficient recurrence. Its transfer matrix
\[
M(d)
\]
changes with d.

Thus
\[
(b_d,b_{d-1})^T=M(d-1)\cdots M(0)(1,5)^T
\]
contains a growing product of local monodromy data.

The complexity grows with d.

---

## 2. What the K3 motive actually gives

The Apéry family has a K3/Picard–Fuchs realization. Frobenius acts on the cohomology of each fiber. The associated weight-4 newform
\[
f=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
\]
records the transcendental part of certain special fibers.

However:

\[
b_d\pmod p
\]
is a coefficient of the period expansion, equivalently a Kummer–Mellin type supertrace over the parameter family. It is not the trace of Frobenius on a single fiber evaluated at d.

The parameter d is the Taylor coefficient index, not a Frobenius power.

Therefore
\[
1_{b_d=0}
\]
is not presently a class function of Frobenius in the Sato–Tate group.

---

## 3. Why the fixed-representation shortcut fails

Suppose hypothetically
\[
b_d=\operatorname{Tr}(F_p^d|V).
\]
Then zeros would be controlled by multiplicative relations among Frobenius eigenvalues.

For example, if eigenvalues modulo p were
\[
\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r,
\]
then
\[
b_d=0
\]
would be an exponential-polynomial equation in d.

This would indeed allow strong bounds on Z_p.

But the actual representation is a varying local system:

\[
M(0),M(1),\ldots,M(d-1).
\]

The tensor power needed to encode the product has rank growing with d. There is no fixed-rank sheaf.

Hence the Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel Frobenius large sieve does not apply directly.

---

## 4. Atkin–Swinnerton-Dyer and Lucas congruences

The congruence
\[
b_{mp+r}\equiv b_m b_r\pmod p
\]
is much more relevant.

It implies
\[
p|b_n
\iff
\text{some base-p digit of n belongs to }Z_p.
\]

This is the automaton structure.

However, it does not reduce the one-digit forbidden set
\[
Z_p^{(1)}
\]
to modular-form coefficients.

The congruence describes how digits combine; it does not identify which digits are forbidden.

---

## 5. Supersingular primes and a_p

The condition
\[
a_p(f)\equiv0\pmod p
\]
corresponds to special Frobenius behavior (supersingular/nonordinary phenomena).

There is a known relation:

- parity of |Z_p| is controlled by the centered fixed point under reflection;
- this is connected with modular-form data.

But this only detects coarse information such as whether the middle point occurs.

It does not determine all zeros.

A prime can be ordinary and still have several Apéry zeros.

---

## 6. Sato–Tate limitation

Sato–Tate controls distributions of normalized Frobenius traces:
\[
a_p/p^{3/2}.
\]

To use it here one would need
\[
1_{b_d=0}
=\phi(\operatorname{Frob}_p)
\]
for a bounded-complexity function \(\phi\).

No such identity is known.

The obstruction is the moving index:
\[
d=n\bmod p.
\]

The event is not:

"prime p has Frobenius in subset C"

but rather:

"prime p has Frobenius data AND the p-dependent coefficient indexed by n mod p vanishes."

This is a moving-target problem.

---

## 7. What might still work

There are two realistic modular directions.

### A. Average over p

One may hope to prove
\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|\ll X
\]
or a variance estimate using the modular interpretation.

This would already support the observed Poisson law.

### B. p-adic Hasse–Witt rigidity

The Cartier operator gives finite-dimensional data controlling the whole coefficient sequence. A theorem showing that the Hasse–Witt matrix has bounded zero-producing states could imply
\[
|Z_p|=O(1).
\]

But such a theorem would be stronger than ordinary Sato–Tate.

---

## 8. Relation to the all-n theorem

For LGTQ-3, the needed statement is not merely
\[
|Z_p|=O(1).
\]

One needs cross-prime anti-concentration:
\[
\#\{p>\sqrt n:n\bmod p\in Z_p\}=o(n/\log n).
\]

Even perfect knowledge of the marginal distribution of each Z_p does not solve this.

The missing theorem is a correlation theorem between:

- the prime p,
- the moving residue n mod p,
- the Apéry Frobenius orbit.

---

# Final verdict

The modular form f explains the arithmetic origin of Apéry zeros but does not currently compress them into a bounded-complexity Frobenius condition.

The answers are:

1. Fixed Frobenius eigenvalue formula: **No**.
2. Sato–Tate class-function reformulation: **No known formulation**.
3. Atkin–Swinnerton-Dyer/Lucas help: **yes for digit structure, no for bounding one-digit zeros**.
4. Supersingular primes: **only coarse parity information**.
5. Modular methods can plausibly improve average bounds, but an O(1) bound for |Z_p| or the all-n GCD theorem requires new rigidity beyond current modular-form technology.

The most promising synthesis is therefore not "Sato–Tate controls Z_p", but rather:

\[
\text{K3 Cartier/Hasse--Witt rigidity}
+
\text{Lucas automaton}
+
\text{large sieve}
\]

where the missing ingredient is a bounded-complexity description of the Cartier states generating zeros.
