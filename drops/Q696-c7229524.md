# Deterministic polynomial PGL2 cocycles and Apéry orbit equidistribution

## Executive verdict

There is currently no theorem saying that a non-autonomous polynomial family of Mobius transformations
\[
M(r)=T(r-1)\cdots T(1)
\]
with bounded-degree rational entries and large generated monodromy must have O(1) hyperplane crossings. The missing input is genuinely arithmetic: one needs cancellation for a deterministic cocycle, not just expansion of a random walk.

The Apéry problem is therefore stronger than existing Bourgain-Gamburd/Katz frameworks. The empirical SL2 monodromy is necessary but not sufficient.

---

## 1. Arithmetic dynamics: what exists and what fails

For a fixed Mobius map
\[
x\mapsto ax+b\over cx+d,
\]
orbit questions reduce to powers of one element of PGL2. Then the orbit length divides the group order and spectral methods apply.

The Apéry situation is different:
\[
x_{r+1}=T(r)x_r,
\]
where the transformation changes at every step. This is a skew-product cocycle over the base map
\[
r\mapsto r+1.
\]

Known arithmetic dynamics results (e.g. Furstenberg-type random products, Bourgain-Gamburd expansion, Pink/Hrushovski-type results on algebraic dynamics) require one of:

- a fixed map;
- a probability distribution on generators;
- averaging over a parameter family.

They do not imply cancellation for one ordered deterministic sequence of generators.

A counterexample exists in principle: bounded-degree rational matrices can be designed so that the orbit follows an arbitrary prescribed path in P1(F_p) while keeping the generated group equal to SL2(F_p). Thus

\[
\langle T(1),\dots,T(p-2)\rangle=SL_2(F_p)
\]
does not imply equidistribution.

The missing hypothesis must constrain the correlation of successive matrices.

---

## 2. Steinberg formulation

For the permutation representation on P1(F_p), let
\[
e_x=\delta_x-{1\over p+1}\mathbf 1.
\]
Then the Steinberg representation is the augmentation quotient and
\[
\langle e_w,g e_v\rangle
=\mathbf 1_{g v=w}-{1\over p+1}.
\]

Therefore for the Apéry orbit,
\[
\sum_r St_{[5:1],[0:1]}(M(r))
=
\#\{r:M(r)[5:1]=[0:1]\}-{p-1\over p+1}.
\]

So the Steinberg reformulation is exact, but it does not add cancellation by itself. The problem is equivalent to bounding one matrix coefficient along one deterministic cocycle.

No general theorem currently bounds
\[
\sum_r \langle v,\rho(M(r))w\rangle
\]
for Steinberg representations with deterministic polynomial cocycles.

## 3. Exponential sums

A natural route is to study
\[
S(\chi)=\sum_r \chi(M(r)[5:1])
\]
for nontrivial characters of P1 or F_p.

For a genuinely algebraic map
\[
r\mapsto f(r),
\]
Weil/Deligne gives square-root cancellation:
\[
|\sum_r\psi(f(r))|\ll \sqrt p.
\]

But here
\[
f(r)=M(r)[5:1]
\]
is not a rational function of bounded degree. The degree of the numerator/denominator of the product grows with r. After p steps it is typically enormous.

Therefore the naive Weil bound does not apply.

A bound
\[
|S(\chi)|\ll \sqrt p
\]
would only imply the usual square-root scale discrepancy. Parseval then gives roughly
\[
|Z_p|\ll\sqrt p,
\]
which is the known dead end.

The observed L2 discrepancy \(p^{0.03}\) is much stronger and suggests additional hidden structure, but no general framework currently upgrades deterministic cocycles from square-root cancellation to near-constant discrepancy.

The correct target is a new theorem of the form:

**Deterministic cocycle Deligne theorem.** A bounded-conductor cocycle with large geometric monodromy has power-saving cancellation in all representation matrix coefficients.

No such theorem is known.

---

## 4. Picard-Fuchs / Sp4 monodromy

The Picard-Fuchs origin is promising but does not immediately solve the finite-field orbit problem.

The differential equation gives an algebraic monodromy representation
\[
\pi_1(U)\rightarrow Sp_4,
\]
which controls Frobenius traces and geometric families.

However the orbit
\[
T(r-1)\cdots T(1)v
\]
is a discrete ordered product in the residue characteristic direction. It is not a Frobenius conjugacy-class average.

Katz's equidistribution theorems control distributions such as
\[
\{\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_x):x\in U(F_p)\},
\]
not a single path obtained by multiplying local transition matrices.

A missing bridge would be an interpretation of the Apéry transfer orbit as a trace function of a bounded-conductor sheaf on an auxiliary variety. That bridge is not presently available.

---

## 5. The Wilson swap identity

The identity
\[
M(p-1)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
is very strong structurally.

It implies the endpoint relation
\[
M(p-1)[5:1]=[1:5],
\]
and gives reflection identities between orbit segments. In particular, left/right sums can be paired.

However a single total-product constraint does not control intermediate statistics. Many paths in SL2 have the same total product but arbitrary intermediate visit distributions.

The swap identity is therefore a symmetry constraint, not an equidistribution theorem.

A useful consequence is that it reduces AFP to half-orbit information, but it does not prove the half-orbit bound.

---

## 6. What extra structure would suffice?

A proof of AFP would likely require one of the following stronger inputs.

### (i) Bounded-degree representation after compression

If one could show
\[
M(r)[5:1]=F(r)
\]
where F has bounded algebraic degree independent of p, then Bezout/Weil would give bounded crossings.

Currently the degree grows with r.

### (ii) p-adic overconvergence

If the orbit coordinate were the reduction of a uniformly overconvergent p-adic analytic function, Strassmann could bound zeros on residue disks.

This is close to the unit-root crystal approach, but requires a uniform radius theorem.

### (iii) Spectral gap for the deterministic cocycle operator

Define the transfer operator
\[
\mathcal L f(x)=\sum_r f(T(r)x).
\]
A spectral gap for the non-constant modes would imply equidistribution.

This is analogous to Bourgain-Gamburd, but the operator is deterministic rather than convolutional.

### (iv) Special Apéry algebraic identity

The most realistic route is an Apéry-specific conservation law, such as:

- a hidden invariant;
- a coboundary identity;
- a strong gap-polynomial restriction;
- a crystalline Mellin anti-concentration theorem.

---

## Final answer to the four questions

1. **Arithmetic dynamics theorem?** No general theorem for deterministic polynomial PGL2 cocycles. Large monodromy alone is insufficient.

2. **Steinberg matrix coefficient bound?** No known theorem. The Steinberg reformulation is exact but does not create cancellation.

3. **Exponential sums?** Weil gives at best square-root cancellation because the product destroys bounded degree. The observed near-constant discrepancy points to new structure, not existing Deligne theory.

4. **Wilson swap identity?** Useful symmetry, but insufficient alone. It constrains the endpoint, not the path.

The Apéry AFP problem is therefore a new deterministic-cocycle equidistribution problem. The missing theorem is not a standard expansion theorem; it is a rigidity/cancellation theorem for one special arithmetic cocycle.
