## 4. Gcd/product-radical attack across several selected offsets

Fix an owner occurrence `(p,J)` and let

\[
A_D(p,J)=\{d:D<d\le2D,\ J+d\in S_p\}.
\]

For each `d in A_D`, let

\[
Q_d(p,J)=\{q:\Gamma_D(p,q;J,d)=1\}.
\]

The next-consecutive-common-prefix marking has an important exact consequence:

\[
\boxed{Q_d(p,J)\cap Q_e(p,J)=\varnothing\quad(d\ne e).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, for fixed `p,q,J` there is at most one **next** common selected prefix. A later common prefix may exist, but it is not a second edge from the same source `J`.

Thus

\[
\deg_{J,D}(p)
=
\sum_{d\in A_D(p,J)}
\sum_{q\in Q_d(p,J)}\log q
\]

is already a union of distinct foreign characteristics. Since every `q in Q_d` divides `N_d(J)`, one may write

\[
\deg_{J,D}(p)
\le
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(P,2P]}
\prod_{d\in A_D(p,J)}N_d(J).
\tag{4.2}
\]

But the height bound gives only

\[
\log\left|\prod_dN_d(J)\right|
\ll Dm_D(p,J)P^{o(1)},
\tag{4.3}
\]

which is exactly the old capacity.

This shows why an ordinary gcd theorem is not the missing input.

- An **upper** bound for `gcd(N_d(J),N_e(J))` says the values have little common prime support. That makes the radical of the product *larger*, not smaller, and is completely compatible with maximal utilization by disjoint prime blocks.
- A useful radical saving would require a theorem forcing **large overlap** among the shell prime factors of many `N_d(J)`, or some other reason the union of their selected shell divisors is small. No such overlap theorem is present.
- We must not assert pairwise coprimality. The continuant is a non-autonomous second-order recurrence; restart converts repeated zeros into shifted bridge zeros, not a Lucas-type identity `gcd(N_m,N_n)=N_gcd(m,n)`. The connected source contains genuine shared factors/center factors, so pairwise coprimality is false as a general heuristic.

There is a second, source-verified warning. `gcdtail_result.tex` gives an explicit component-misalignment example at `p=5683`: the same rational prime divides two scalar separated resultants through **different roots** of the outer polynomial even though the two shifted inner polynomials are coprime. Thus `gcd` of scalar resultants forgets the physical root label. Our statistic (U) is root/physical-start sensitive, so any resultant gcd used here must retain the aligned root `J`; scalar norm gcds are not enough.

Most importantly, the disjoint-block model

\[
Q_d\cap Q_e=\varnothing
\]

satisfies every possible upper bound on cross-offset gcds. Therefore no theorem whose only new content is “different offsets do not share many shell primes” can imply `CCU_theta`; the hostile utilization can live entirely in multiplicity-one fibers.

## 6. Primitive-divisor theory: the foreign primes are imprimitive, but existence is far too weak

There is a useful conceptual observation which makes primitive-divisor language precise.

If `Gamma_D(p,q;J,d)=1`, then `J in S_q`. Let

\[

\]

The medium range has `c<H<D`. By literal first return,

\[
q\mid N_c(J),
\tag{6.1}
\]

while the endpoint/restart gives

\[
q\mid N_d(J).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus every foreign `q` used by the medium edge is an **imprimitive** prime divisor of the value `N_d(J)` with an earlier selected return below `H`.

This sounds promising, but a classical Zsigmondy/Lucas-style theorem would not be enough even if one could transplant it to this non-autonomous continuant.

1. The sequence `d -> N_d(J)` is not a fixed-coefficient Lucas/Lehmer divisibility sequence. The recurrence coefficients vary with `J+d`, and the source has no strong-divisibility law which turns index gcds into value gcds.
2. Existence of one primitive divisor at index `d` removes only one `log q = P^{o(1)}` unit from an available shell radical of size `D=P^{delta}`. The utilization problem needs a **power** defect in a mass of size `D` at each loaded offset, or a power-small weighted population of loaded offsets.
3. Primitive divisors at different offsets actually support the disjoint-block hostile model: they provide new primes rather than forcing reuse.

The theorem which would help is much stronger: a **quantitative primitive-shell dominance / imprimitive-shell sparsity** estimate saying that, after averaging over the exact selected owner-offset measure, the shell logarithmic mass of primes which already have a short literal return is `P^{-theta}` smaller than the full height capacity.

with `theta>3/5` would make the right side `o(1)`, so it would forbid even one foreign shell prime in every cell. The desired theorem must therefore be the **weighted aggregate** (U), exactly as the dispatch suggests: a power-small fraction of owner-offset capacity may be occupied, while individual occupied cells can remain heavy.

No quantitative primitive-part theorem of this strength is present in the synchronized source.

## 7. Ordinary polynomial incidence / large-sieve counting also has zero power headroom

\[

\[

\]

Averaging over `q in (P,2P]` gives a root density of order `d/P`. There are order `P/log P` shell primes, each carrying weight `log q`, so the expected logarithmic shell-divisor mass at one integer `J` is of order

\tag{7.1}

## 8. Why several-offset resultants cannot be salvaged by hidden pairwise coprimality

The tempting argument is:

> many selected offsets `d` give many values `N_d(J)`; perhaps those values are nearly coprime, so a shell prime cannot recur often; hence utilization should be small.

The conclusion is backwards for the present consumer.

For fixed `(p,J)`, the graph already charges each `q` only at its first next-common offset. If the values at different offsets are nearly coprime, then their shell prime factors can be disjoint, and the owner can use **more** distinct foreign characteristics. Near-coprimality maximizes the union radical in (4.2).

Conversely, large shared factors would reduce the number of distinct shell primes available to the owner, but the current source does not force such sharing. And we cannot manufacture it from restart: if `q` divides two `N_d(J)` values, restart produces a shifted bridge zero; it does not say that every pair of offsets must share a large divisor.

This is also why the exact `gcdtail_result.tex` warning matters. Scalar resultants can share a rational prime through different root components, so even observed resultant gcds do not prove the aligned sharing needed by (U).

Therefore I find no valid route from pairwise gcd/resultant estimates to a positive `theta`.
