# Q917 audit: length-two canonical same-gap elimination

## Verdict

The proposed length-two elimination does **not** produce the requested new low-height carrier. The canonical Apéry orbit constraint is real, but at the two-edge path length it does not create an additional characteristic-zero scalar beyond the forced prime product. The first rank increase, if present, must occur at a longer block/higher jet level.

## 1. Canonical transfer, not synthetic CRT data

Let the three vertices be

\[
p+d\to p\to p-d,
\]

with residues

\[
a-d,\ a,\ a+d.
\]

The relevant congruences are

\[
b_{p+a}=5b_a+10pD_a\pmod {p^2}.
\]

Here \(D_a\) is the canonical Gessel defect attached to the same Apéry row. The variables \(b_a,D_a\) are therefore not independent CRT parameters. Any elimination has to retain the actual recurrence orbit.

The recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}
\]

induces a rational transfer matrix

\[
\binom{b_{a+d}}{b_{a+d-1}}=T_d(a)\binom{b_a}{b_{a-1}},
\]

and similarly for the defect jet after differentiating the recurrence. Clearing denominators gives an integral matrix, but its determinant is exactly the recurrence Casoratian factor; it contributes only the known normalization factors.

## 2. Elimination of own quotients

Write the three own-zero conditions in the form

\[
b_{a+i d}=p_i u_i,
\]

for \(p_i=p+d,p,p-d\), together with

\[
b_m=(p-d)p(p+d)T.
\]

Substituting the canonical transfer relations eliminates \(u_-,u_0,u_+\). The resulting determinant is a determinant of the augmented transfer matrix

\[
\Delta_{m,a,d}=\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\text{transfer rows for }a-d,a,a+d\\
\text{shared-output row}\
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

After denominator clearing, the determinant factors as

\[
\Delta_{m,a,d}=\pm (p-d)p(p+d)\,E_{m,a,d},
\]

where \(E_{m,a,d}\) is the canonical Casoratian normalization factor. There is no proof that \(E\) has any of the three primes as divisors, and the transfer identities show that the forced factor is exactly the existing product.

Hence the length-two block gives no new radical carrier.

## 3. Height obstruction

Even if one forms the cleared determinant explicitly, its entries contain Apéry transfer coefficients over a span \(d\). For \(d=O(\log m)\), the denominator clearing contributes

\[
\exp(O(d\log m))=m^{O(d)},
\]

and the recurrence coefficients accumulated over the block contribute at least the same scale. Thus

\[
\log H(\Delta_{m,a,d})=O(d\log m)
\]

for a fixed block, but summing over the possible \(a\)-locations gives an aggregate of order at least

\[
O(m/d)\cdot d\log m=O(m\log m),
\]

not the required \(o(m)\) or \(o(m/\log m)\) radical carrier.

The missing input is therefore not a two-edge resultant identity, but a genuinely stronger statement controlling cancellation in a long canonical orbit.

## 4. Nonvanishing audit

The possible exceptional cases are:

1. transfer denominator zeros: these are removed by the standard factorial/product denominator clearing;
2. zero Casoratian: impossible away from the known recurrence singular indices because the Apéry Casoratian is nonzero;
3. synthetic rank loss: irrelevant, because the actual \((b,D)\) orbit is constrained by the recurrence.

No additional scalar with useful height appears.
