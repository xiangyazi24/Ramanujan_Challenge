# Q794: uniqueness audit for two LGTQ-2 pairs

## Verdict

The finite scan is an empirical uniqueness observation, not a theorem. The proposed recurrence rectangle does not prove uniqueness because the four divisibility constraints live in different characteristics.

For two pairs
\[
(p_i,\ell_i=p_i-d,u_i=n-qp_i,v_i=u_i+h),\quad i=1,2,
\]
we have
\[
u_2-u_1=s=q(p_1-p_2),\qquad v_2-v_1=s.
\]
Both horizontal transfers have the same shift \(h\), while vertical transfers have shift \(s\).

The continuant transfer gives integral identities of the form
\[
\binom{b_{u+h+1}}{b_{u+h}}=M_h(u)\binom{b_{u+1}}{b_u},
\]
with determinant one. Applying this to the rectangle

```
(u1) ---- h ---- (v1)
 |                 |
 s                 s
 |                 |
(u2) ---- h ---- (v2)
```

creates relations among recurrence values, but reductions occur in
\[
\mathbb F_{p_1},\mathbb F_{\ell_1},\mathbb F_{p_2},\mathbb F_{\ell_2},
\]
not one common field.

## Characteristic-zero carrier attempt

Multiplying the four corner defects gives a possible carrier with height
\[
\log |C|=O((h+|s|)\log n).
\]
This is too large to force the four corner primes as distinct divisors. It does not imply an \(O_{q,h}(1)\) bound or uniqueness.

## Shared corners

If \(p_1=\ell_2\), then \(p_2-p_1=d\). This is compatible with primality and does not collapse the remaining three characteristic conditions into a contradiction.

## Crossing order

If \(p_1>p_2\), then \(u_1<u_2\). The transfer rectangle reverses orientation but gives no monotonicity of zero sets.

## Final audit

- At most one LGTQ-2 pair for fixed \((n,q,h)\): not proved.
- \(O_{q,h}(1)\) multiplicity: not proved.
- Finite scan result (q=1..4, complete rows up to p<=1,999,993): valid only as computation.
- Any abstract zero-set counterexample is irrelevant; an actual counterexample requires an extended Apéry scan.

The missing ingredient is a cross-characteristic rigidity theorem coupling the four moving finite fields; recurrence transfer alone is insufficient.
