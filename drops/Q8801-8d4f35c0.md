# The all-​\(n\) quantifier, the polylogarithmic exceptional set, and what is still missing

## Executive verdict

There is a sharp answer to the quantifier question.

1. The official challenge statement
   \[
   G_n=e^{o(n)}
   \]
   means
   \[
   \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log G_n}{n}=0
   \]
   **along the full sequence of positive integers**. It is equivalent to saying that, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), only finitely many \(n\) satisfy \(\log G_n>\varepsilon n\). Finite initial exceptions are immaterial, so “all \(n\)” in project shorthand really means “all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly in the ordinary asymptotic sense.” It does **not** mean “for a density-one set of \(n\).”

2. The current repository has already proved substantially more than the premise in the question states. In the repaired, audited form recorded in `SUBMIT/3.2/FINAL_AUDIT.md`, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),
   \[
   E_\varepsilon(N)
   :=\#\{n\le N:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}
   \ll_\varepsilon (\log N)^2.
   \tag{1}
   \]
   Thus the questions “can one prove \(O(N^{1-\delta})\)?” and “can one prove \(O((\log N)^C)\)?” are already answered **yes**, with the stronger value \(C=2\). This is a very strong partial result.

3. Nevertheless, (1) does not imply the challenge statement. An infinite set such as \(\{2^j:j\ge1\}\) has \(O(\log N)\) elements up to \(N\), finite harmonic weight, and upper Banach density zero, yet it can support spikes of size \(\log G_n\asymp n\) infinitely often. The remaining quantifier gap is therefore exactly
   \[
   E_\varepsilon(N)\ll_\varepsilon(\log N)^2
   \quad\longrightarrow\quad
   E_\varepsilon(\infty)<\infty
   \qquad(\text{for every }\varepsilon>0).
   \tag{2}
   \]

4. Borel–Cantelli proves finiteness in a genuinely random model of the zero sets, and the random tail is overwhelmingly summable. It does not turn the actual deterministic Apéry zero sets into a random realization. The current finite-harmonic-weight theorem is already a deterministic analogue of summability and still permits infinitely many isolated exceptions.

5. None of ordinary GRH, Elliott–Halberstam, generalized Elliott–Halberstam, Hardy–Littlewood prime-tuples, Sato–Tate, or a marginal Poisson law for \(|Z_p|\) implies the all-​\(n\) result by itself. Those conjectures control the prime carrier or one-prime marginals. The missing input is a **quenched, spatial, cross-prime moving-target theorem** for the locations of the Apéry zeros.

6. The repository already isolates two precise sufficient inputs:

   - a single independence-scale factorial moment \((\mathrm{HM})_k\) for one fixed \(k>6\), which gives
     \[
     \log G_n\ll n^{2/3+2/k+o(1)}
     \quad\text{for every }n;
     \tag{3}
     \]
     in particular \((\mathrm{HM})_8\) gives \(\log G_n\ll n^{11/12+o(1)}\);
   - LGTQ-3, equivalently in the current picture an LGTQ-MTD moving-target discrepancy estimate.

7. Without one of these new cross-prime inputs, the strongest audited unconditional statement is:
   \[
   \boxed{
   G_n=e^{o(n)}\text{ outside }O_\varepsilon((\log N)^2)
   \text{ exceptions up to }N,
   }
   \]
   while the strongest general pointwise statement for every \(n\) remains only
   \[
   \log G_n=O(n).
   \tag{4}
   \]
   No pointwise \(e^{n^{1-\delta}}\) bound for a fixed \(\delta>0\) is currently proved; any such bound would already solve the challenge.

## 1. What the challenge statement literally requires

### 1.1 The official wording

The official challenge paper, arXiv:2607.09721, Section 3.2, defines the two Apéry solutions and asks:

> Prove: \(\gcd(d_na_n,d_nb_n)=e^{o(n)}\).

Problem 3.2 appears under the paper’s section titled “The Conjectures.” There is no qualifier such as “for almost all \(n\),” “in density,” or “on average.” The challenge website also says that outcomes will be reported separately as accepted or partial. Nothing in the official statement or the posted clarifications relaxes the ordinary asymptotic quantifier.

The relevant official sources are:

- [The Ramanujan Challenge paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09721)
- [The Ramanujan Challenge website and submission rubric](https://www.ramanujanmachine.com/ramanujan-challenge/)

### 1.2 Exact quantifier expansion

Because \(G_n\ge1\), the statement is
\[
G_n=e^{o(n)}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\log G_n=o(n).
\]
Expanding \(o(n)\), this is
\[
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N_\varepsilon\ 
\forall n\ge N_\varepsilon:
\log G_n\le\varepsilon n.
\tag{10}
\]
Equivalently, for
\[
\mathcal E_\varepsilon
=
\{n\ge1:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\},
\]
one must prove
\[
|\mathcal E_\varepsilon|<\infty
\qquad\text{for every fixed }\varepsilon>0.
\tag{11}
\]
It is enough to verify (11) for \(\varepsilon=1/j\), \(j=1,2,\ldots\).

Thus finite exceptions are allowed automatically. In that limited sense, mathematical usage does interpret an asymptotic assertion as a statement “for all sufficiently large \(n\).” But the quantifier is over the **full tail** of the sequence, not over a density-one subset of that tail.

### 1.3 Density-one convergence is strictly weaker

The current result says, in particular,
\[
\frac{\log G_n}{n}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in natural density}.
\tag{12}
\]
Ordinary convergence (10) implies (12), but not conversely.

A model counterexample makes the distinction exact. Define
\[
H_n=
\begin{cases}
e^n,&n=2^j\text{ for some }j,\\1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then for every \(0<\varepsilon<1\),
\[
\#\{n\le N:\log H_n>\varepsilon n\}=O(\log N),
\]
\[
\sum_{\log H_n>\varepsilon n}\frac1n
=
\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}<\infty,
\]
and the exceptional set has upper Banach density zero. Nevertheless
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log H_n}{n}=1,
\]
so \(H_n\ne e^{o(n)}\).

This is exactly the logical possibility left by the Apéry theorem: rare isolated spikes, not a positive-density obstruction.

### 1.4 What could be accepted by the challenge?

Mathematically, the density-one theorem is not a complete proof of Problem 3.2. The organizers could recognize it as a strong partial result—the website explicitly distinguishes accepted and partial outcomes—but calling it a proof of the stated conjecture would change the statement.

A clean submission description is therefore:

```text
Unconditional partial solution:
for every epsilon > 0,
#{n <= N : log G_n > epsilon n} <<_epsilon (log N)^2.
The full-sequence limit remains open.
```

That is much stronger and more informative than simply saying “density one,” while remaining mathematically exact.

## 2. Exceptional-set structure: what is already proved

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and write
\[
E_\varepsilon(N)
=
\#\{n\le N:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}.
\]

### 2.1 The current theorem already beats every power bound

The repaired theorem is
\[
E_\varepsilon(N)\ll_\varepsilon(\log N)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore:

- for every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\),
  \[
  E_\varepsilon(N)=O_{\varepsilon,\delta}(N^{1-\delta});
  \]
- more strongly, the requested polylogarithmic bound holds with \(C=2\);
- the theorem is far stronger than the bare density-one assertion \(E_\varepsilon(N)=o(N)\).

So the first two bullets in Question 2 are not future targets; they are current theorems.

### 2.2 Finite harmonic weight

Equation (13) immediately implies
\[
\sum_{n\in\mathcal E_\varepsilon}\frac1n<\infty.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, partial summation gives
\[
\sum_{\substack{n\le X\\n\in\mathcal E_\varepsilon}}
\frac1n
=
\frac{E_\varepsilon(X)}X
+
\int_1^X\frac{E_\varepsilon(t)}{t^2}\,dt,
\]
and the integral of \((\log t)^2/t^2\) converges.

This is stronger than zero natural or logarithmic density. It is still compatible with an infinite exceptional set, as the powers-of-two example shows.

### 2.3 Local sparsity

The critical-window proof in `proof.tex`, after the cap-​6 and companion-height repairs, gives the mechanism behind (13): in a dyadic range \((N,2N]\), intervals of length comparable to
\[
\frac{\varepsilon^2N}{\log N}
\]
contain only \(O_\varepsilon(1)\) exceptional integers. The manuscript records the resulting local estimate
\[
\sup_{M\ge0}
\#\{M<n\le M+L:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}
\ll_\varepsilon L^{2/3}+1.
\tag{15}
\]
This rules out dense clusters of exceptions. It does not rule out one isolated exception in each of infinitely many widely separated critical windows.

### 2.4 Why the current proof stops at polylogarithmic cardinality

The codegree argument is a comparison between **two different exceptional integers**. In one critical interval \(J\), let \(\mathcal E_J\) have size \(M\). Every exceptional integer carries many bad primes. Cauchy–Schwarz gives a lower bound for the number of common-prime incidences among pairs of elements of \(\mathcal E_J\), while the gap-polynomial codegree theorem gives an upper bound. This forces
\[
M=O_\varepsilon(1).
\]
Covering \((N,2N]\) by \(O_\varepsilon(\log N)\) critical intervals and then summing over \(O(\log N)\) dyadic ranges gives (13).

But if \(M=1\), the pair-incidence sum is zero. No pairwise codegree estimate can contradict a singleton. This is the exact **isolated-spike barrier**. It is the exceptional-set version of the repository’s broader singleton/certificate-parity obstruction.

### 2.5 What would prove finiteness?

For a fixed \(\varepsilon\), any one of the following would suffice:

1. a global bound \(E_\varepsilon(N)=O_\varepsilon(1)\), since the counting function is monotone;
2. a critical-window bound that is \(o(1)\), hence eventually zero because it is integer-valued;
3. an amplification theorem saying that one exceptional \(n\) forces more than the allowed \(O_\varepsilon(1)\) exceptional neighbors in its critical window;
4. a one-​\(n\) high-moment or moving-target theorem that directly rules out the required large collection of bad primes.

No current recurrence identity gives item 3. The remaining live program is item 4.

Thus “prove \(E\) finite” is not a modest sharpening of (13). Quantifierially, it is the full challenge.

## 3. Borel–Cantelli: what it proves in a random model and why it does not settle Apéry

### 3.1 There is no canonical probability for the actual event

For a fixed integer \(n\), the statement
\[
\log G_n>\varepsilon n
\]
is deterministic. It has probability \(0\) or \(1\) unless one introduces an auxiliary random model—for example, random locations for the sets \(Z_p\), a random integer sampled from an interval, or a random walk in a monodromy group.

Borel–Cantelli is a theorem inside such a probability space. It does not by itself transfer an almost-sure conclusion to the one deterministic sequence supplied by the Apéry recurrence.

### 3.2 In the independent-zero model the tail is summable by an enormous margin

The random calculation is nevertheless instructive. Suppose the local sets have bounded average size and, for fixed \(n\), the events
\[
X_{p,n}
=
\mathbf1_{\,n\bmod p\in Z_p}
\]
behave independently with
\[
\mathbb P(X_{p,n}=1)\asymp\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]
For \(p>\sqrt n\), the expected number of hits is at most constant order if \(|Z_p|\) has bounded mean. Even under the unconditional worst-case bound \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\), the mean is only
\[
\mu_n
\ll
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n}p^{-1/3}
\ll
\frac{n^{2/3}}{\log n}.
\]
An \(\varepsilon\)-exception requires, by (9), on the order of
\[
t_n\asymp_\varepsilon\frac n{\log n}
\]
large-prime hits. A Chernoff bound for independent Bernoulli variables gives
\[
\mathbb P(T_n\ge t_n)
\le
\left(\frac{e\mu_n}{t_n}\right)^{t_n}
\le
\exp(-c_\varepsilon n)
\tag{16}
\]
for large \(n\). Hence
\[
\sum_n\mathbb P(T_n\ge t_n)<\infty,
\]
and the first Borel–Cantelli lemma gives only finitely many exceptional \(n\) almost surely.

So the random model predicts much more than density one: it predicts eventual absence of linear-size spikes.

### 3.3 Why the argument is not a proof

The missing statement in (16) is not a prime number theorem. It is a high-dimensional joint law for the positions of the deterministic sets \(Z_p\) relative to the deterministic targets \(n\bmod p\), simultaneously over many different residue characteristics.

The following data do not imply it:

- \(|Z_p|=O(1)\);
- a Poisson marginal law for \(|Z_p|\);
- no adjacent zeros;
- one-prime Fourier bounds;
- pairwise cross-prime independence;
- ordinary distribution of the candidate primes.

As shown in Q8775, singleton sets can be chosen as
\[
Z_p=\{n\bmod p\}
\]
and hit one prescribed moving target for every prime while retaining size one and no adjacency. Marginal sparsity is therefore logically orthogonal to moving-target alignment.

### 3.4 The current theorem is already a deterministic summability result

Equation (14) is stronger than the usual conclusion “zero density,” yet it does not imply finiteness. Thus invoking Borel–Cantelli after obtaining finite harmonic weight would only rename the gap. An infinite deterministic set may have summable harmonic weight.

One valid probabilistic corollary is this: if one independently samples one integer uniformly from each dyadic interval \((2^j,2^{j+1}]\), then (13) gives a summable probability of hitting \(\mathcal E_\varepsilon\), so the random samples hit it only finitely often almost surely. This is true but irrelevant to whether \(\mathcal E_\varepsilon\) itself is finite.

## 4. Can a sieve make the exceptional intersection empty?

### 4.1 The direct local-condition picture

An exceptional \(n\) must have many primes \(p>\sqrt n\) for which
\[
n\bmod p\in Z_p.
\tag{17}
\]
This looks ideal for a sieve: many simultaneous local conditions should be rare. The difficulty is that (17) is a **hit condition at a moving modulus**, not avoidance of a fixed family of residue classes at every auxiliary prime.

A classical Selberg sieve can use sets \(\Omega_\ell\subset\mathbf F_\ell\) when every candidate must avoid \(\Omega_\ell\) for each small auxiliary \(\ell\). Here the modulus is itself the candidate prime \(p\), and one asks whether a single target belongs to \(Z_p\). There is no multiplicative sieve density obtained by Chinese remaindering the \(Z_p\).

This is why Q8775’s density-only route fails. Even \(|Z_p|=1\) does not prevent perfect alignment.

### 4.2 What the existing codegree sieve does achieve

The repaired codegree theorem compares two nearby integers \(m<n\). A prime that is bad for both forces a gap-polynomial or wrap certificate depending on \(n-m\). The number of such common primes is \(O(n-m)\) at the relevant scale. This is exactly enough to show that many exceptional integers cannot cluster, yielding (13).

It is not enough to rule out one exceptional integer, because the proof has no second integer with which to form a codegree.

### 4.3 The exact high-moment route

The repository formulates the right one-​\(n\) sieve quantity. For a dyadic prime shell, put
\[
K_X(m)
=
\#\{X<p\le2X:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\]
\[
\lambda_X
=
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]
The unconditional zero bound gives
\[
\lambda_X\ll\frac{X^{2/3}}{\log X}.
\]
For a falling factorial \((K)_k=K(K-1)\cdots(K-k+1)\), the hypothesis \((\mathrm{HM})_k\) is
\[
\sum_{0\le m<X^2}(K_X(m))_k
\ll
X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^k.
\tag{18}
\]
Because every summand is nonnegative, (18) gives a pointwise bound for every \(m<X^2\):
\[
K_X(m)
\ll
X^{2/k+o(1)}\lambda_X
\ll
X^{2/3+2/k+o(1)}.
\tag{19}
\]
Summing dyadic shells yields
\[
\log G_n
\ll
n^{2/3+2/k+o(1)}.
\tag{20}
\]
Therefore any fixed \(k>6\) proves the all-​\(n\) conjecture. The current repository proves the \(k=2\) moment, which is far below the threshold. A fixed \(k=8\) is a clean next target and gives exponent \(11/12\).

This is a genuine sieve-theoretic answer to Question 4: **yes, sufficiently strong factorial-moment control makes the intersection empty at the exceptional scale; no, current pairwise information does not reach that moment.**

### 4.4 The LGTQ-3 route

The alternative route uses the internal geometry of a large bad-prime set. If one integer has \(\gg n/\log n\) bad primes, then many triples of those primes have total span \(O(\log n)\). Pigeonholing the two successive gaps produces one pair
\[
h_1,h_2\ll\log n
\]
with a critical number of bad prime triples. This reduces the all-​\(n\) problem to
\[
\max_{h_1,h_2\le C\log n}
T_{q;h_1,h_2}(n)
=o\!\left(\frac n{(\log n)^3}\right).
\tag{21}
\]
The ordinary upper-bound sieve gives only the Hardy–Littlewood scale
\[
T_{q;h_1,h_2}(n)\ll \frac n{(\log n)^3}.
\]
The extra little-​\(o\) must come from the Apéry moving-target condition. Q8775 identifies the exact residual as LGTQ-MTD, a centered spatial discrepancy estimate. Q8785 gives a representation-theoretic reformulation in terms of moving Steinberg coefficients, but not a proof.

Thus the current sieve landscape is:

```text
pair codegree     -> polylogarithmically many exceptional n;
fixed HM_k, k>6  -> no exceptional n for large n;
LGTQ-MTD         -> no exceptional n for large n;
ordinary sieve   -> critical O(n/log^3 n), no little-o.
```

## 5. Which standard conjectures would imply the all-​\(n\) result?

The important conclusion is mostly negative: the usual conjectures about **where primes lie** do not control **where Apéry zeros lie inside each characteristic**.

### 5.1 GRH

#### Dirichlet GRH

GRH for Dirichlet \(L\)-functions improves error terms for primes in arithmetic progressions. It does not address the sets
\[
Z_p=\{d<p:b_d\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]
or their alignment with \(n\bmod p\). Therefore Dirichlet GRH alone does not imply even the needed one-prime moving-target discrepancy, much less all-​\(n\).

#### Artin/automorphic GRH

GRH for Artin or automorphic \(L\)-functions could support effective Chebotarev once one had:

1. a global Galois or sheaf-theoretic realization of the coefficient-zero event;
2. a framed version retaining the marked vector, because the incidence is not conjugation-invariant;
3. bounded or controlled conductor in the moving index;
4. linear disjointness for the product representations needed for several primes;
5. uniformity while the residue characteristic and representation both vary.

Those geometric and independence statements are not consequences of GRH. They are most of the missing Apéry content. Thus “GRH plus the missing representation and independence theorem” could prove the result, but GRH is not the load-bearing assumption.

### 5.2 Elliott–Halberstam and generalized Elliott–Halberstam

EH and GEH concern averages of prime-counting errors over moduli below a level of distribution. The present obstruction has modulus comparable to the range and only one target residue at each modulus—the repository’s occupancy-one barrier. Moreover, the residue set itself depends on the modulus through the Apéry recurrence.

Even level of distribution \(1\) for ordinary primes would not control
\[
\mathbf1_{Z_p}(n\bmod p).
\]
An **Apéry-specific EH theorem**, in which these centered indicators replace ordinary progression errors, would suffice, but that is essentially LGTQ-MTD or an equivalent moving-characteristic large sieve.

### 5.3 Hardy–Littlewood/Dickson prime-tuples

Hardy–Littlewood predicts the number of prime triples
\[
p,\quad p+h_1,\quad p+h_1+h_2
\]
at the scale \(n/(\log n)^3\). This is the carrier on which LGTQ-3 is imposed. It does not supply the required little-​\(o\) factor from the Apéry conditions.

Indeed, Q8772’s CM benchmark shows the danger clearly: finite Hecke or congruence conditions modify the singular-series constant but do not improve the exponent. Hardy–Littlewood alone therefore predicts the critical order, not the needed saving.

Hardy–Littlewood **combined with a joint spatial independence law for the Apéry zeros** would be enough. The second clause is precisely the unsolved part.

### 5.4 Sato–Tate and Chebotarev for the K3 family

Ordinary Sato–Tate or Chebotarev controls conjugacy-invariant Frobenius data as a geometric parameter varies. The repository proves that \(b_d\bmod p\) is a defining-characteristic Mellin coefficient of the whole K3 fiber-count function, not the fiber trace at a parameter \(a=1/d\). Moreover, the proposed incidence condition depends on a marked vector and is not conjugation-invariant.

Thus standard Sato–Tate does not count exact coefficient zeros. A new crystalline/local-limit theorem for the Mellin coefficients would be required.

### 5.5 Bounded \(|Z_p|\) or a Poisson law for its cardinality

Neither is sufficient. Q8775 gives an exact countermodel: a singleton can be placed at every moving target. Even a Poisson law for \(|Z_p|\), together with no adjacency, can be coupled so that the target is hit whenever the set is nonempty.

The needed Poisson assertion must be **spatial and quenched**, not merely a law for cardinalities. It must control root locations relative to all relevant deterministic target sections and jointly across primes.

### 5.6 A genuinely sufficient random-model conjecture

A strong but conceptually clean sufficient conjecture is:

> Conditional on the cardinalities \(|Z_p|\), the locations of the reflection pairs in \(Z_p\) are asymptotically uniform, with joint large-deviation estimates uniform over every moving target \(n\bmod p\) and over all \(n\) in a dyadic range.

With an exponential Chernoff tail as in (16), a union bound over \(n\in[N,2N]\) followed by Borel–Cantelli would prove that every \(\mathcal E_\varepsilon\) is finite.

This is much stronger than marginal Poisson data but describes exactly what the random heuristic is using.

### 5.7 Precise sufficient assumptions already isolated in the project

The clean conditional hierarchy is:

| Assumption | All-​\(n\)? | Consequence |
|---|---:|---|
| \(|Z_p|=O(1)\) | No | Marginal sparsity only |
| Poisson law for \(|Z_p|\) | No | Marginal cardinalities only |
| no adjacent zeros | No | Same-prime local repulsion only |
| Hypothesis \(\bar Z\): \(\sum_{p\le x}Z(p)\ll\pi(x)\) | No | Stronger density-one size bounds, not pointwise control |
| ordinary GRH | No | Prime distribution, wrong object |
| ordinary EH/GEH | No | Prime distribution, wrong modulus/target quantifier |
| ordinary Hardy–Littlewood | No | Gives the critical prime-triple scale |
| spatial joint Poisson with uniform exponential tails | Yes, almost surely | Borel–Cantelli gives finitely many spikes |
| \((\mathrm{HM})_k\) for one fixed \(k>6\) | Yes | Equation (20) |
| LGTQ-3 / LGTQ-MTD | Yes | Contradicts every large bad-prime spike |
| framed bounded-degree orbit transverse to the incidence divisor | Yes | Bézout gives pointwise \(O(1)\) incidence |

Thus no familiar “standard analytic conjecture” alone bridges density one to all \(n\). The genuinely missing content is Apéry-specific spatial independence.

## 6. Strongest unconditional result presently available

### 6.1 Exceptional-set theorem

The strongest banked statement answering the challenge asymptotically is
\[
\boxed{
\forall\varepsilon>0,\qquad
\#\{n\le N:\log G_n>\varepsilon n\}
\ll_\varepsilon(\log N)^2.
}
\tag{22}
\]
Consequences include:

- natural density zero for every fixed exceptional threshold;
- finite harmonic weight;
- no clustering at positive local density;
- \(G_n=e^{o(n)}\) on a density-one set.

This is much stronger than “all but \(O(N^{1-\delta})\)” and is the correct headline for the partial result.

### 6.2 Pointwise theorem for every \(n\)

From the corrected divisibility (6),
\[
G_n\le \frac{6d_n^2}{n^3}.
\]
Since
\[
\log d_n=3\psi(n),
\]
where \(\psi\) is Chebyshev’s function, the prime number theorem gives
\[
\log G_n
\le
6\psi(n)+O(\log n)
=
(6+o(1))n.
\tag{23}
\]
Chebyshev estimates alone give \(O(n)\). Equation (9) gives the sharper structural form
\[
\log G_n\le6M(n)+O(n^{2/3}),
\]
but without moving-target control one only has \(M(n)=O(n)\) pointwise.

Therefore the strongest general all-​\(n\) growth class currently proved is exponential, not subexponential.

### 6.3 Why \(e^{n^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\) is not an intermediate easy target

If a fixed \(\varepsilon<1/2\) satisfied
\[
\log G_n=O(n^{1/2+\varepsilon})
\quad\text{for every }n,
\]
then \(n^{1/2+\varepsilon}=o(n)\), so the challenge would already be solved. The same is true of every pointwise bound \(O(n^{1-\delta})\), \(O(n/\log\log n)\), or indeed any explicit \(o(n)\) function.

For \(\varepsilon>1/2\), the displayed estimate follows trivially from \(O(n)\), but that regime is not subexponential in the required sense. Hence the proposed \(n^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) bound is either trivial when the exponent exceeds \(1\), or essentially the full theorem when it is below \(1\).

### 6.4 What is not currently proved

The audited sources do **not** prove any of the following:

- \(E_\varepsilon(N)=O_\varepsilon(1)\);
- finiteness of \(\mathcal E_\varepsilon\);
- \(E_\varepsilon(N)=O_\varepsilon(\log N)\);
- a pointwise \(\log G_n=O(n^{1-\delta})\) for any fixed \(\delta>0\);
- a pointwise \(\log G_n=O(n/\omega(n))\) for any function \(\omega(n)\to\infty\).

Any of the last two would settle Problem 3.2.

---

## 7. Strategic interpretation of the remaining gap

The density-one quantifier is not a cosmetic issue. It tells us exactly what the current method controls and what it cannot see.

### What is closed

The codegree method proves that exceptional integers cannot occur in large clusters. It has already compressed the possible obstruction from a positive-density set to at most polylogarithmically many points.

### What is open

It remains possible that there is one exceptional integer in infinitely many widely separated ranges. Such a spike is supported internally by many distinct primes but has no neighboring exceptional integer. Pairwise arguments between integers are then silent.

### What theorem would materially advance the project

The next theorem should be internal to a single integer or to its bad-prime set. The two best formulations already present are:

1. **HM8:** prove (18) at \(k=8\). This gives the explicit all-​\(n\) bound \(\log G_n\ll n^{11/12+o(1)}\).
2. **LGTQ-MTD:** prove the centered moving-target discrepancy that forces every LGTQ prime-triple count to be \(o(n/(\log n)^3)\).

A third, more geometric formulation from Q8785 is a bounded-complexity framed Apéry orbit transverse to the Borel incidence divisor. Bézout would then give a constant zero count, but no such bounded-degree orbit is presently known and the Poisson model suggests a universal constant \(|Z_p|\) may itself be false.

The most realistic target remains HM8 or LGTQ-MTD, not another refinement of marginal zero counts.

---

## 8. Direct answers

### 1. Does the challenge literally require all \(n\)?

It requires the full-sequence limit. Finitely many initial exceptions are allowed; an infinite density-zero exceptional subsequence is not. A density-one theorem can be recognized as partial progress but is not a proof of the written statement.

### 2. Can the exceptional-set bound be strengthened?

It already has been strengthened to
\[
E_\varepsilon(N)\ll_\varepsilon(\log N)^2.
\]
Hence every \(O(N^{1-\delta})\) target is already surpassed, and the polylogarithmic target holds with \(C=2\). Finiteness remains open and is equivalent, as \(\varepsilon\) ranges over positive rationals, to the full conjecture.

### 3. Can Borel–Cantelli close the problem?

It closes a suitably independent random model and predicts an exponentially summable exceptional probability. It does not apply directly to the deterministic Apéry sequence. The current theorem already gives finite harmonic weight, which still permits infinitely many exceptions.

### 4. Can a sieve prove the intersection empty?

A classical density sieve cannot, because the conditions are moving-modulus hits. A high factorial-moment sieve can: any \((\mathrm{HM})_k\) with fixed \(k>6\) proves all \(n\). LGTQ-3/LGTQ-MTD is another sufficient sieve-discrepancy formulation. Present pairwise codegree information only proves polylogarithmic sparsity.

### 5. Which standard conjectures suffice?

GRH, EH/GEH, Hardy–Littlewood, Sato–Tate, bounded \(|Z_p|\), and marginal Poisson laws do not suffice individually. One needs an Apéry-specific joint spatial independence statement. HM8, LGTQ-MTD, or a uniform framed effective-Chebotarev theorem strong enough to imply them would suffice.

### 6. What is the strongest unconditional theorem?

For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), all but \(O_\varepsilon((\log N)^2)\) integers \(n\le N\) satisfy \(\log G_n\le\varepsilon n\). The exceptional set has finite harmonic weight. For every individual \(n\), only \(\log G_n=O(n)\) is currently proved. No pointwise sublinear bound is known.

---

## Final conclusion

The density-one result is not merely qualitative; it is already an exceptionally strong **polylogarithmic-exception** theorem. That should be the main unconditional claim in any paper or challenge submission.

But the official Problem 3.2 quantifier is unambiguous:
\[
G_n=e^{o(n)}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E_\varepsilon\text{ is finite for every }\varepsilon>0.
\]
The current theorem allows \(\mathcal E_\varepsilon\) to be infinite and ultra-sparse. Every pairwise, average, and marginal route can miss precisely such isolated spikes. The remaining problem is therefore not to improve density one to “even denser” density one. It is to prove a one-​\(n\), high-order cross-prime anti-concentration theorem.

In the repository’s present language, the cleanest exact targets are
\[
\boxed{(\mathrm{HM})_8}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\boxed{\mathrm{LGTQ\text{-}MTD}}.
\]
Either one changes the quantifier from polylogarithmically many possible spikes to no sufficiently large spikes, and therefore completes the challenge.
