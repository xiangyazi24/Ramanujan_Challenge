ANSWER Q94 978ea043

# The exponent freezes, but the Kummer coefficient system does not

## Executive verdict

The first observation in the question is correct, and the later retraction is not.
For the Teichmüller character on \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), one has the exact
identity

\[
\boxed{\omega_p^{m-p}=\omega_p^{m-1}.}
\]

Indeed, the two exponents differ by \(p-1\). Equivalently, if \([a]\) is the
Teichmüller lift of \(a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), then
\([a]^{p-1}=1\), hence \([a]^p=[a]\) **exactly**, not merely modulo \(p\).
Consequently

\[
\boxed{T_p(m-p)=T_p(m-1)}
\]

with the same character convention as in Q91.

This is a genuine correction to the diagnosis in `drops/Q91-ecfeb84f.md`
and `drops/Q92-d6bac4e4.md`: for fixed \(m\), the exponent is not moving with
\(p\). The Goldbach diagonal \(p+r=m\) disappears at the level of the integer
exponent, leaving the fixed integer

\[
k=m-1.
\]

However, the following stronger conclusion is false:

```text
fixed exponent k  =>  one fixed global Galois character
                  =>  rho_f tensor that character
                  =>  Frobenius trace T_p(k).
```

There are two independent category errors in that implication.

1. The character \(\omega_p^k\) is a character of the **geometric fiber
   group** \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), used to weight the variable
   \(a\) inside a Mellin sum. A Tate, cyclotomic, Dirichlet, or Hecke twist is
   a character of an **arithmetic Galois group**, and it multiplies the whole
   Frobenius trace at a prime by one scalar.
2. Although the integer power \(k\) is fixed, the finite-field character
   \(\omega_p^k\) still changes with \(p\): its domain, coefficient field,
   order, Kummer cover, and normalized Kummer parameter all vary.

The correct local object is

\[
V_{p,k,\ell}
 =H_c^1\!\left(
   U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
   \mathcal F_{p,\ell}\otimes\mathcal L_{\omega_p^k}
  \right),
\qquad \ell\ne p,
\]

or its crystalline/overconvergent analogue when coefficients are taken at
\(p\). Under the rank-two concentration established in the Mellin analysis,

\[
T_p(k)
 =-\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
    \operatorname{Frob}_p\mid V_{p,k,\ell}
   \right)
\]

up to the repository's Frobenius-sign and normalization convention.
This is a Frobenius module over \(\mathbf F_p\). It is not automatically the
restriction to \(G_{\mathbf Q_p}\) of a representation of \(G_{\mathbf Q}\).

There is presently **no known classical finite-dimensional motive or
compatible system over \(\mathbf Q\)** whose \(p\)-th Frobenius trace is
\(T_p(m-p)=T_p(m-1)\) for the varying primes in question. The direct
construction by twisting the fixed Apéry modular representation is
incorrect. On the other hand, the current information does not prove an
absolute nonexistence theorem for every conceivable motive: an accidental
compatible system could exist, but it would require new rationality,
compatibility, bounded-conductor, and horizontal comparison theorems not now
available.

The right corrected description of Route A is therefore:

```text
The moving exponent obstruction disappears.
The remaining obstruction is a fixed-weight, varying-characteristic
Mellin specialization by the tautological Teichmüller character.
```

That is a real simplification, but it is not yet a global motive.

## 1. The exponent calculation

Let

\[
r=m-p.
\]

For every \(a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\),

\[
a^{m-p}=a^{m-1-(p-1)}=a^{m-1}.
\]

Thus, as characters of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\),

\[
\chi_{p,m-p}=\chi_{p,m-1}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The stated range \(m/2<p\le m\) is not needed for the character identity;
it only ensures that \(m-p\) is a convenient nonnegative representative.
There is a minor endpoint correction to the inequality in the question:

\[
0\le m-p\le p-1,
\]

not always \(m-p<p-1\). Equality occurs when \(m=2p-1\). At that endpoint
both \(\omega_p^{m-p}=\omega_p^{p-1}\) and
\(\omega_p^{m-1}=\omega_p^{2(p-1)}\) are trivial. At \(p=m\), both are
again trivial.

Now take the canonical Teichmüller lift

\[
[a]\in\mu_{p-1}\subset\mathbf Z_p^\times,
\qquad [a]\equiv a\pmod p.
\]

By definition,

\[
[a]^{p-1}=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
[a]^p=[a]
\tag{1.2}
\]

as an equality in \(\mathbf Z_p^\times\). The statement in the question that
this may fail as a \(p\)-adic equality is true for a general unit of
\(\mathbf Z_p^\times\), but false for a Teichmüller unit.

Consequently

\[
[a]^{m-p}=[a]^{m-1}
\tag{1.3}
\]

exactly. The same conclusion holds after transporting the \((p-1)\)-st roots
of unity to \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\) for \(\ell\ne p\).

Hence the exact correction to Q91 is

\[
\boxed{
 \tau_m(p)\equiv T_p(m-p)=T_p(m-1)\pmod p.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The congruence with \(\tau_m(p)=b_{m-p}\bmod p\) may still be only a
same-characteristic congruence, depending on the normalization of the lifted
Mellin trace. The equality between the two Mellin labels is exact.

A convention warning is needed only at \(a=0\). A Kummer character is defined
on \(\mathbf G_m\), so the Mellin sum naturally runs over
\(\mathbf F_p^\times\). If a formula extends the summand to \(a=0\), the
extension convention for the trivial character must be stated separately.

## 2. In what sense the character is fixed, and in what sense it still moves

For fixed \(m\), put \(k=m-1\). Then every prime uses the same integer power

\[
\chi_{p,k}=\omega_p^k.
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is a useful fixed-label statement. It does **not** identify the characters
for different primes as one character on one fixed group. The data are

\[
\omega_p^k:
\mathbf F_p^\times\longrightarrow\mu_{p-1}.
\]

As \(p\) changes, all of the following change:

- the source group \(\mathbf F_p^\times\);
- the group of values \(\mu_{p-1}\);
- the order of the character;
- the cyclotomic coefficient field needed to realize the values;
- the Kummer/Lang covering from which the character sheaf is cut out;
- the finite-field cohomology group on which Frobenius acts.

The order is

\[
\operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^k)
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(k,p-1)}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For a fixed nonzero \(k\), this is unbounded as \(p\to\infty\). Thus
"fixed power" is not the same as "fixed finite-order character."

There is an especially clean way to see the distinction using the usual
Kummer parameter in \(\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z\). A classical fixed Kummer
character with parameter

\[
\alpha=\frac jN\in\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z
\]

specializes, for primes \(p\equiv1\pmod N\), to

\[
\omega_p^{j(p-1)/N}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Its order divides the fixed integer \(N\). By contrast,

\[
\omega_p^k
\quad\text{has parameter}\quad
\alpha_p=\frac{k}{p-1}\pmod{\mathbf Z}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The normalized Kummer parameter \(\alpha_p\) moves with \(p\) and has
unbounded denominator. In the original top-half range one can equally write

\[
\alpha_p
 =\frac{m-p}{p-1}\pmod{\mathbf Z}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus the apparent paradox is resolved:

```text
integer exponent label:       k=m-1 is fixed;
point in the Kummer dual:      k/(p-1) is moving.
```

The first fact corrects Q91/Q92. The second explains why their ultimate
conclusion—no evident fixed Kummer motive—does not automatically disappear.

## 3. Four objects denoted informally by “omega” must be separated

The notation in the question conflates several different characters.

### 3.1 The finite-field Teichmüller character

\[
\omega_p^{\mathrm{ff}}:
\mathbf F_p^\times\longrightarrow\mu_{p-1},
\qquad a\longmapsto[a].
\]

This is the character in the Mellin sum. Its argument is the geometric fiber
variable \(a\).

### 3.2 The global \(\ell\)-adic cyclotomic character

\[
\varepsilon_\ell:
G_{\mathbf Q}\longrightarrow\mathbf Z_\ell^\times.
\]

For \(q\ne\ell\), under the arithmetic-Frobenius convention,

\[
\varepsilon_\ell(\operatorname{Frob}_q)=q.
\tag{3.1}
\]

With geometric Frobenius the inverse convention appears. At \(q=\ell\),
\(\varepsilon_\ell\) is ramified, so there is no ordinary unramified
Frobenius value to insert into (3.1).

### 3.3 The Galois Teichmüller part of the cyclotomic character

For a fixed coefficient prime \(\ell\), one may decompose

\[
\varepsilon_\ell
 =\omega_\ell^{\mathrm{Gal}}\,
  \langle\varepsilon_\ell\rangle,
\]

where

\[
\omega_\ell^{\mathrm{Gal}}:
G_{\mathbf Q}\longrightarrow\mu_{\ell-1}
\]

is the Teichmüller lift of the mod-\(\ell\) cyclotomic character. For
\(q\ne\ell\),

\[
\omega_\ell^{\mathrm{Gal}}(\operatorname{Frob}_q)
 =[q\bmod\ell].
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is not the map \(q\mapsto q\) in \(\mathbf Z_\ell^\times\); it is only
the finite-order Teichmüller factor.

### 3.4 A Kummer character sheaf

Given a multiplicative character

\[
\chi:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^\times,
\qquad \ell\ne p,
\]

one obtains a rank-one Kummer sheaf

\[
\mathcal L_\chi
\]

on \(\mathbf G_m/\mathbf F_p\). Its trace at the rational point \(a\) is
\(\chi(a)\). This is a sheaf in the geometric \(a\)-variable.

These objects are related, but they are not interchangeable. In particular,
there is no meaningful expression

\[
\omega_p^{\mathrm{ff}}(\operatorname{Frob}_p),
\]

because \(\omega_p^{\mathrm{ff}}\) has domain \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), not
\(G_{\mathbf Q}\).

There is one subtle relation worth recording. Via local class field theory,
\(\omega_p^{\mathrm{ff},k}\) corresponds to a tame character of local
inertia, and it is the finite Teichmüller part of the \(p\)-adic cyclotomic
character \(\varepsilon_p^k\) on tame inertia. This does **not** make the
Mellin twist a Tate twist: the Mellin construction uses that tame character
as monodromy in the geometric \(a\)-variable, whereas the Tate motive is a
constant coefficient system on that variable.

Moreover, the full cyclotomic character decomposes as

\[
\varepsilon_p^k
 =\left(\omega_p^{\mathrm{Gal}}\right)^k
  \langle\varepsilon_p\rangle^k.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The Mellin character retains only the finite tame Teichmüller part. The
operation "at each prime \(p\), discard the principal-unit part and retain the
Teichmüller factor" is itself \(p\)-dependent and is not a fixed global
algebraic character.

## 4. The correct local cohomological formula

Let \(U/\mathbf F_p\) be the open parameter curve on which the Apéry trace
sheaf is lisse, and let

\[
\mathcal F_{p,\ell}
\]

be the relevant \(\ell\)-adic sheaf whose trace function is
\(\theta_p(a)\). Fix \(\ell\ne p\) and a realization of the character values
in \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\). For

\[
\chi_{p,k}=\omega_p^k,
\]

put

\[
V_{p,k,\ell}
 =H_c^1\!\left(
    U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
    \mathcal F_{p,\ell}\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_{p,k}}
   \right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The Grothendieck--Lefschetz trace formula gives

\[
\sum_{a\in U(\mathbf F_p)}
 \theta_p(a)\chi_{p,k}(a)
 =
\sum_i(-1)^i
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
  H_c^i(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
   \mathcal F_{p,\ell}\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_{p,k}})
 \right).
\tag{4.2}
\]

When the boundary and invariant terms vanish and the Mellin cohomology is
concentrated in degree one, this becomes

\[
T_p(k)
 =-\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid V_{p,k,\ell}).
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the precise meaning of the statement that the Mellin trace is a
Frobenius trace on a rank-two space.

Two hidden dependencies should be restored in the notation used in Q91:

\[
V_{\chi_r}
\quad\text{should really be written}\quad
V_{p,\chi_{p,r},\ell}.
\]

It depends on the residue characteristic \(p\), the character in that
characteristic, and the coefficient realization.

Most importantly,

\[
V_{p,k,\ell}
\]

is naturally a representation of

\[
G_{\mathbf F_p}
 =\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf F}_p/\mathbf F_p),
\]

or simply a vector space with Frobenius. It is not, without an additional
lifting theorem, a representation of \(G_{\mathbf Q_p}\). Therefore the
question

\[
R|_{G_{\mathbf Q_p}}\cong V_{p,k,\ell}
\]

is not literally well typed.

For a global representation \(R_\ell\) unramified at \(p\), the comparison
one would need is instead an equality of Frobenius characteristic polynomials,

\[
\det\!\left(1-X\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid R_\ell^{I_p}\right)
 =
\det\!\left(1-X\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid V_{p,k,\ell}\right).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Producing (4.4) requires a global integral model, specialization/nearby-cycle
comparison, and \(\ell\)-independence. Rank two and an \(SL_2\) geometric
monodromy group do not supply those facts.

## 5. Why \(\rho_f\otimes\varepsilon_\ell^k\) is not the Mellin object

Let

\[
\rho_{f,\ell}:G_{\mathbf Q}\to\operatorname{GL}_2(E_\ell)
\]

be the representation attached to the fixed Apéry modular form. A Tate twist
is

\[
\rho_{f,\ell}(k)
 =\rho_{f,\ell}\otimes\varepsilon_\ell^k
\]

up to the sign convention for \(\mathbf Q_\ell(k)\). At a good prime
\(p\ne\ell\), its trace is

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Frob}_p\mid\rho_{f,\ell}(k)
\right)
 =p^k a_p(f)
\tag{5.1}
\]

under the arithmetic-Frobenius convention, or the corresponding inverse
power under the opposite Tate-twist convention.

By contrast, the Mellin trace is

\[
T_p(k)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
   \theta_p(a)\omega_p(a)^k.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equation (5.1) multiplies one already-formed Frobenius trace by a scalar.
Equation (5.2) changes the coefficient system before cohomology and weights
each geometric fiber point separately. There is no general identity between
the two operations.

The distinction can be summarized as

```text
Tate/Dirichlet twist:
    Tr(Frob_p | R)  ->  scalar(p) * Tr(Frob_p | R)

Kummer-Mellin twist:
    sum_a theta_p(a)  ->  sum_a theta_p(a) * chi_p(a).
```

A weight check reaches the same conclusion. A finite-order Kummer sheaf has
weight zero, so changing \(k\) does not introduce a factor \(p^k\) into the
Weil size of the Mellin trace. A Tate twist changes the weight and necessarily
introduces a power of \(p\). Thus the two constructions cannot be identified
without a major normalization that would itself have to be proved.

Nor does a global Dirichlet or Hecke character repair the problem. If
\(\eta\) is a global character, then

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid\rho_f\otimes\eta)
 =\eta(p)a_p(f)
\]

at unramified primes. Again this supplies one scalar at \(p\), not the
function \(a\mapsto\omega_p(a)^k\) needed inside (5.2).

One can extend \(\omega_p^k\) separately by local class field theory to a
tamely ramified character of \(\mathbf Q_p^\times\), trivial on a uniformizer
and on \(1+p\mathbf Z_p\). For nontrivial \(\omega_p^k\), this local character
has conductor \(p\). A single classical global Hecke character cannot have
these nontrivial local components at infinitely many \(p\), because a Hecke
character has finite conductor. This rules out patching the local Kummer
characters into one ordinary global twist.

## 6. Why a fixed global Kummer sheaf does not give \(\omega_p^k\)

Suppose one had a fixed finite-order Kummer local system

\[
\mathcal K
\]

on \(\mathbf G_m\) over an arithmetic base. Its geometric monodromy factors
through a fixed finite group \(\mu_N\). Every good reduction therefore gives
a character of order dividing the fixed integer \(N\).

For \(k\ne0\), however,

\[
\operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^k)
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(k,p-1)}
 \ge\frac{p-1}{|k|},
\]

so the orders are unbounded. Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No fixed finite-order Kummer sheaf specializes to }
\mathcal L_{\omega_p^k}
\text{ for all large }p.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently, \(\mathcal L_{\omega_p^k}\) is an eigensheaf of the
prime-dependent Lang/Kummer covering

\[
x\longmapsto x^{p-1}
\]

of \(\mathbf G_m/\mathbf F_p\). The covering degree itself grows with \(p\).
A fixed finite cover over \(\mathbf Q\) cannot contain all these eigenspaces.

This should be contrasted with classical Jacobi-sum or Fermat motives. There,
one fixes rational Kummer parameters with bounded denominators; at a prime
\(p\equiv1\pmod N\), the corresponding exponents are proportional to
\((p-1)/N\). Such eigenspaces can be cut out from a fixed degree-\(N\) cover.
Here the exponent is the fixed integer \(k\), so the rational parameter is
\(k/(p-1)\) and no fixed denominator exists.

There is a qualification. The argument proves the failure of the direct
**fixed finite-order geometric Kummer sheaf** construction. It is not by
itself a theorem that no exotic infinite-monodromy sheaf or no unrelated
motive could accidentally have the same traces. A fixed finite-dimensional
\(\ell\)-adic local system of motivic type would still have to be defined over
a fixed coefficient field and satisfy strong bounded-ramification and
compatibility constraints; no such construction is known here.

There is also no contradiction with the fact that the fixed Tate motive has
\(p\)-adic realizations whose tame residual characters contain
\(\omega_p^k\). That fact uses a different coefficient prime at every
residue characteristic and extracts only the tame factor of a local Galois
character. It does not create one fixed geometric Kummer local system on the
parameter torus.

## 7. What a genuine globalization would have to look like

Assume optimistically that the Apéry trace sheaves arise by reduction of a
relative object

\[
\mathcal F
\]

on an open arithmetic parameter scheme

\[
U/\mathbf Z[1/N].
\]

If there were a rank-one arithmetic local system \(\mathcal K_k\) on
\(\mathbf G_m/\mathbf Z[1/N]\) satisfying

\[
(\mathcal K_k)_p
 \cong\mathcal L_{\omega_p^k}
\tag{7.1}
\]

for every good prime \(p\), then one could try to form

\[
\mathcal R_k
 =R^1\pi_!\left(\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal K_k\right)
\tag{7.2}
\]

over the arithmetic base. Its Frobenius trace would be the desired Mellin
sum by the trace formula.

This is the clean conceptual route to a global motive. The missing object is
precisely \(\mathcal K_k\). Equation (6.1) shows that it is not a classical
fixed finite-order Kummer sheaf.

One could weaken (7.1) and ask for a compatible system

\[
R_{k,\ell}:G_{\mathbf Q}\to\operatorname{GL}_d(E_\ell)
\]

such that, for all good \(p\ne\ell\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Frob}_p\mid R_{k,\ell}
\right)
 =\iota_{p,\ell}\bigl(T_p(k)\bigr).
\tag{7.3}
\]

For (7.3) to define a classical compatible system, one needs at least:

1. a fixed number field \(E\) containing all Frobenius traces after descent;
2. a rank \(d\) independent of \(p\) and \(\ell\);
3. characteristic polynomials in \(E[X]\) independent of \(\ell\);
4. purity of a fixed weight;
5. ramification outside a fixed finite set;
6. bounded or controlled conductor;
7. an integral comparison identifying the reduction of the global object
   with the local Mellin cohomology.

The rank-two local calculation supplies only item 2 at the level of each
separate finite field. It does not establish any of the horizontal items
1 or 3--7.

The coefficient-field issue is especially visible. Before possible Galois
descent, \(T_p(k)\) is formed using values in \(\mu_{p-1}\), hence naturally
lies in a cyclotomic field depending on \(p\). It is possible that the total
trace descends to a much smaller field, but that is an additional theorem,
not a formal consequence of the rank-two construction. Demonstrating
unbounded degrees of the actual trace fields would rule out a compatible
system over a fixed number field; conversely, proving rationality would remove
only one obstruction.

## 8. Fixed \(m\) gives only finitely many primes, so the quantifiers matter

For one fixed \(m\), the range

\[
\frac m2<p\le m
\]

contains only finitely many primes. Asking for a motive that matches traces
only at this finite set is not a useful globalization problem. Finite local
data neither determine nor naturally characterize a motive, and an ad hoc
object whose conductor encodes those primes would give no uniform
Chebotarev or Sato--Tate estimate.

The meaningful Route A target must have stronger quantifiers. One would need
one of the following.

### Target A: one motive for each fixed weight

For every integer \(k\ge0\), construct a canonical motive or compatible
system \(M_k\) such that

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid M_k)=T_p(k)
\]

for all but finitely many \(p\), with rank, weight, coefficient field, and
conductor controlled uniformly enough in \(k\).

For the original row \(m\), one would use \(k=m-1\). Since \(k\) grows with
\(m\), uniformity in \(k\) is indispensable.

### Target B: one arithmetic family in a weight parameter

Construct a finite-type or suitably analytic family \(\mathscr M\) whose
specialization at weight \(k\) is \(M_k\). This would be closer to an Iwasawa
or eigenvariety-style object than to one fixed motive.

### Target C: direct horizontal cancellation

Without constructing a motive, prove directly that

\[
p\longmapsto T_p(k)
\]

has the needed cancellation, uniformly in the range \(k\asymp p\) arising
from \(k=m-1\). This is a fixed-integer-label but varying-characteristic
Mellin theorem.

None of these targets follows merely from (1.1).

## 9. Could a larger nonclassical object package all the local spaces?

There are plausible larger categories in which the local spaces can be
packaged more naturally.

### 9.1 The full pro-Kummer tower

Take all power maps

\[
[N]:\mathbf G_m\to\mathbf G_m
\]

simultaneously and form a pro-object. Every finite-field Kummer character is
then an eigenspecialization somewhere in the tower. The local characters
\(\omega_p^k\) can be selected from this pro-system.

The price is that the object is infinite-level and not a finite-dimensional
motive. Standard Euler products, compatible-system theorems, and Sato--Tate
do not apply automatically.

### 9.2 A universal Mellin transform over the character dual

One can regard the Mellin transform as living over a character space and
then evaluate at the point \(\omega_p^k\). The problem is that the selected
point varies with the residue characteristic and does not arise from one
fixed algebraic section over \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z\).

### 9.3 A \(p\)-adic weight-space or unit-root construction

For each fixed \(p\), the Teichmüller character is naturally represented by
a rank-one unit-root/Kummer \(F\)-isocrystal, and powers can be interpolated
in suitable \(p\)-adic character spaces. This is likely the most natural
local framework for the defining-characteristic congruence.

But a \(p\)-adic weight space is attached to one fixed \(p\). Allowing the
coefficient prime and residue characteristic to vary together does not yield
one ordinary \(\ell\)-adic compatible system. A new horizontal crystalline
or adelic Mellin formalism would be needed.

These possibilities explain why it is too strong to say that no conceivable
global object can exist. They also explain why any such object would be much
larger than \(\rho_f\) with a Tate twist.

## 10. Can an unrelated global motive accidentally have the same traces?

Nothing in the present repository proves an absolute nonexistence theorem of
the form

\[
\text{There is no motive }M_k/\mathbf Q
\text{ with trace }T_p(k)\text{ for almost all }p.
\]

To prove such a theorem, one would need to show that the local traces violate
a necessary property of motivic compatible systems. Potential tests include:

1. **Coefficient field.** Do the fields generated by \(T_p(k)\) have
   unbounded degree?
2. **Purity.** Do all conjugates have one fixed Weil weight?
3. **Integrality and determinant.** Are there fixed determinant and
   self-duality laws compatible across \(p\)?
4. **Conductor.** Can the local data arise from a system ramified at only
   finitely many primes?
5. **\(\ell\)-independence.** Do the same Frobenius polynomials appear in
   every realization?
6. **Chebotarev relations.** Do the traces satisfy the algebraic constraints
   imposed by one fixed monodromy group and coefficient field?

The current rank-two and \(SU(2)\)-type statements are vertical statements
inside each finite-field Mellin family. They do not answer these horizontal
questions. Conversely, local traces can lie in the correct numerical Weil
interval without being Frobenius traces of one global motive.

Thus the honest conclusion is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.84\textwidth}
No natural classical global motive is presently constructed, and the naive
Tate/Dirichlet twist is definitely wrong. Absolute nonexistence of every
unrelated accidental motive is not proved by the current data.
\end{minipage}
}
\]

## 11. The corrected research target for Route A

The correction \(T_p(m-p)=T_p(m-1)\) is useful. It removes one apparent
source of complexity and changes the exact missing theorem.

The old formulation was

```text
moving-character problem:
    p -> Mellin component indexed by m-p.
```

The corrected formulation is

```text
fixed integer weight, varying characteristic:
    k=m-1,
    p -> H_c^1(F_p-trace sheaf tensor L_{omega_p^k}).
```

This suggests a sharper target.

### Fixed-weight horizontal Mellin compatibility

For each \(k\), construct a system \(R_k\) and comparison maps such that

\[
\operatorname{charpoly}
 (\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid R_{k,\ell})
 =
\operatorname{charpoly}
 (\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid V_{p,k,\ell})
\]

for all sufficiently good \(p\ne\ell\), uniformly enough as \(k\to\infty\).

A weaker analytic substitute would be a prime-sum theorem for the local
Mellin traces \(T_p(k)\) with uniformity in \(k\).

The main new difficulties are now clearly isolated:

- constructing a horizontal realization of the tautological
  \(\omega_p^k\)-specialization;
- proving descent to a fixed coefficient field;
- comparing the \(\ell\)-adic and defining-characteristic realizations;
- obtaining conductor bounds uniform when \(k=m-1\asymp p\);
- converting the trace estimate to the same-characteristic divisibility
  event \(T_p(k)\equiv0\pmod p\).

The last item remains even if a global compatible system is found. Ordinary
Sato--Tate controls archimedean distributions of normalized traces; it does
not automatically control divisibility of the trace by the same prime \(p\).
Thus a successful globalization would be important but would not by itself
finish the Apéry GCD argument.

---

## 12. Direct answers to the questions

### Is \(\omega_p^{m-p}=\omega_p^{m-1}\)?

Yes, exactly. The difference of the exponents is \(p-1\), and a
Teichmüller value is a \((p-1)\)-st root of unity. The claim
\([a]^p\ne[a]\) for a Teichmüller lift is false.

### Is this one character independent of \(p\)?

It is one fixed **integer power label** \(k=m-1\), but not one character on a
fixed group and not one fixed Kummer parameter. The normalized Kummer
parameter is \(k/(p-1)\), and the character order usually grows with \(p\).

### Is the character a power of the global cyclotomic character?

Only after a limited local-class-field interpretation: it is the tame
Teichmüller part of the \(p\)-adic cyclotomic character on inertia. The full
cyclotomic character and its Tate twist are different objects, and twisting
\(\rho_f\) by them does not form the Mellin sum.

### Does \(\rho_f\otimes\varepsilon^{m-1}\) have trace \(T_p(m-p)\)?

No. Its trace is a power of \(p\) times \(a_p(f)\), whereas \(T_p(m-p)\) is
compactly supported cohomology after a Kummer twist in the geometric fiber
variable.

### What is the correct local representation?

It is the Mellin cohomology Frobenius module

\[
V_{p,m-1,\ell}
 =H_c^1(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
       \mathcal F_{p,\ell}\otimes
       \mathcal L_{\omega_p^{m-1}}),
\]

with the appropriate boundary terms and normalization restored. It is a
\(G_{\mathbf F_p}\)-representation, not automatically a
\(G_{\mathbf Q_p}\)-representation.

### Is there any global motive whose Frobenius traces are these values?

No such motive is presently known or supplied by the standard Kummer/Tate
machinery. The direct fixed-Kummer construction is obstructed by the
prime-dependent, unbounded-order character. An arbitrary accidental motive
has not been logically ruled out, but proving its existence would require a
new horizontal compatible-system theorem; matching only the finitely many
primes \(m/2<p\le m\) is not meaningful for Chebotarev purposes.

---

## Final conclusion

The crucial algebraic correction is

\[
\boxed{
T_p(m-p)=T_p(m-1).
}
\]

So Route A should no longer be rejected because the **exponent** moves with
\(p\). That part of Q91/Q92 was too pessimistic.

What remains moving is the finite-field Kummer realization itself:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\omega_p^{m-1}}
\text{ lives in characteristic }p,
\text{ has generally unbounded order, and is not the reduction of one fixed
finite-order Kummer sheaf.}
}
\]

The Frobenius trace is therefore correctly understood as a trace on a local
Mellin cohomology space

\[
V_{p,m-1},
\]

not as the trace of

\[
\rho_f\otimes\text{(Tate or cyclotomic power)}.
\]

A genuine globalization would require a new universal or horizontal Mellin
construction selecting the tautological Teichmüller point in every residue
characteristic and proving compatible-system descent. Such an object may
exist in a pro-Kummer, crystalline, or character-stack setting, but it is not
currently a classical finite-dimensional motive and does not come for free
from the rank-two Mellin theorem.

Accordingly, the exact refined status is:

```text
Q91 obstruction removed:       m-p is congruent to fixed m-1.
Naive global twist rejected:   Kummer Mellin twist is not a Tate twist.
Classical global motive:       not known.
Absolute nonexistence:         not proved.
New named residual:            fixed-weight horizontal Mellin compatibility,
                               uniform for k=m-1 growing with m.
```
