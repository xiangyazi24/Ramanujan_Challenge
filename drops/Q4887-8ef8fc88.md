## Lemma 3.1

\[
 \sum_{D<d\le2D}\cos^2(d\theta)
 =\frac D2+\frac12\Re\sum_{D<d\le2D}e^{2id\theta},
\]
and the geometric sum is \(O(1/\delta)\le D/C\), giving \(\gg D\).

If \(\delta<C/D\), put \(x=D\delta\in[0,C]\).  Since \(\cos^2(d\theta)=\cos^2(d\delta)\), the normalized sum is a Riemann sum for
\[
 f(x)=\int_1^2\cos^2(tx)\,dt.

The continuous function \(f\) is strictly positive on the compact interval \([0,C]\): vanishing would force \(\cos(tx)=0\) for every \(t\in[1,2]\), which is impossible.  Uniform Riemann convergence therefore gives a positive lower bound for all large \(D\).  This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Therefore the first necessary horizontal theorem for this angle route is

 \boxed{
 \sum_xM_N(x)^2
 \ll N^{-1/100-\eta/2+o(1)}U_NB_N.}
 \tag{COC$_\eta$}

A simpler sufficient condition for this diagonal is
\[
 \boxed{
 \max_xM_N(x)
 \ll N^{-1/100-\eta/2+o(1)}U_N.}
 \tag{FOFF$_\eta$}
\]

Neither is a consequence of the canonical gap polynomial.  If \(c_p-x\) and \(c_p+x\) are the reflected pair, their positive gap is \(h=2x\).  The canonical even-gap factorization has
\[
 N_{2x}(X)=(2X+2x+1)N_{2x}^\circ(X).
\tag{9.5}
\]
At \(X=c_p-x\),
\[
 2X+2x+1=p.
\tag{9.6}
\]
Hence the gap certificate is automatically zero modulo \(p\).  It gives no horizontal restriction on the event
\[
 c_p\pm x\in\mathcal Z_p.
\]

This explains exactly why an Apéry-specific identity is essential.  The missing information is not another uncertainty principle; it is a theorem about the **horizontal distribution of centered Apéry zero offsets across changing characteristics**.

(COC) is necessary but by itself not a complete proof, because the off-diagonal offset terms \(x\ne y\) remain.  A fully sufficient version would combine (COC) with a power-saving restriction for

 \mathbf1_{x\in\mathcal S_p}

 e_p(-dx\bar q_p)e_q(-dy\bar p_q),
\]
or prove the exact source-weighted reciprocal angle directly.  But no argument can bypass the common-offset diagonal without at least a theorem of the strength of (COC).

Let $p\ne q$ be odd primes, and let $\bar q_p\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$

 e_p(-dx\bar q_p)e_q(-dx\bar p_q)

\end{proof}
