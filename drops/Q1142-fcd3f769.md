# Folded-jet nonvanishing audit

## Computational status

The requested finite scan is the first required certificate. The target set is

\[
(p,s): p\text{ odd prime},\quad 0\le s\le (p-1)/2,\quad b_s\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

For each such pair the derivative coordinate is

\[
D_s=\sum_{v=0}^{s}\binom{s}{v}^2\binom{s+v}{v}^2(H_{s+v}-H_{s-v}).
\]

A valid scan must compute the harmonic factors modulo p using inverses of all integers up to 2s. Since

\[
2s\le p-1,
\]

all denominators are units modulo p. Therefore the finite-field expression is well-defined.

I do not have an executed p<=2000 scan transcript in this session, so I do not claim a numerical count or a smallest counterexample. A theorem cannot be inferred from absence of small examples.

## What a proof would have to show

The congruence

\[
b_{pN+s}=b_N(b_s+2NpD_s)\pmod {p^2}
\]

shows that when \(b_s\equiv0\pmod p\), the first nonzero folded term is controlled by \(D_s\). The desired statement is exactly

\[
b_s=0\implies D_s\ne0\pmod p.
\]

Equivalently, the folded jet of the Apéry sequence at a vanishing residue cannot have a double zero modulo p.

## Differentiated recurrence route

Let

\[
B_n(t)=\sum_k \binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(1+t\left(\frac1{n+i}+\frac1{i}\right)\right)
\]

be a formal deformation whose first derivative records the harmonic contribution. The original Apéry recurrence gives a second-order recurrence for the zeroth coordinate. Differentiating gives a coupled recurrence

\[
\mathcal R(B')=\text{explicit forcing from }\mathcal R'(B).
\]

A Wronskian proof would need a canonical companion solution C_n and an identity

\[
W_s=b_sC'_s-b'_sC_s,
\]

with a nonvanishing formula modulo p. The remaining factor is the reduction of this Wronskian modulo p; without it the argument stops at a conditional implication.

## Finite-field obstruction

The only possible obstruction is a simultaneous vanishing of the value and derivative coordinates:

\[
b_s\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad D_s\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

A proof must rule this out using either:

1. a finite-field hypergeometric differential identity showing the derivative is not zero on every zero of the truncated hypergeometric polynomial; or
2. a recurrence/Wronskian identity where the residual factor is explicitly a unit.

The congruence alone does not supply this unit.

    z=0
    for v in range(s+1):
        c=1
        # exact integer computation is simplest for this range
        import math
        c=math.comb(s,v)**2*math.comb(s+v,v)**2
        z=(z+c)%p
    return z


def D_mod(s,p):
    import math
    ans=0
    for v in range(s+1):
        term=math.comb(s,v)**2*math.comb(s+v,v)**2%p
        h=0
        for i in range(s-v+1,s+v+1):
            h=(h+inv(i,p))%p
        ans=(ans+term*h)%p
    return ans

count=0
