# Q584 — Integer GCD structure of Apéry numbers and ZM₂

## Executive summary

The switched form

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|^2
=\sum_{r,s<X}\#\{p>\max(r,s):p\mid\gcd(b_r,b_s)\}
\]
is a very useful reformulation, but it exposes the real difficulty: ZM₂ is a theorem about **large prime divisors of pairwise gcds of a non-linear holonomic sequence**.

The current state is:

* Apéry numbers appear numerically generic: gcds of pairs behave like random integer gcds.
* There is no known theorem proving that \(\gcd(b_r,b_s)\) has no large prime factors for most pairs.
* Corvaja–Zannier theory does not directly apply because Apéry numbers are not constant-coefficient linear recurrences or S-unit sequences.
* The most realistic rigorous route is not a pointwise gcd theorem but an averaged large-prime divisor estimate for the pair sequence.

---

## (A) What are the gcds \(\gcd(b_r,b_s)\)?

The first 50 Apéry numbers can be computed exactly, but there is no simple closed formula for

\[
G(r,s)=\gcd(b_r,b_s).
\]

The observed pattern is consistent with random integers of comparable size:

* many pairs have gcd 1;
* small primes occur occasionally;
* large gcds are extremely rare.

This is exactly what one expects from

\[
\Pr(p\mid b_r\text{ and }p\mid b_s)\approx p^{-2}
\]

for a random model.

The sequence is therefore "generic" in the gcd sense, despite being highly structured algebraically.

The first terms

\[
1,5,73,1445,33001,819005,\ldots
\]

already show no systematic common factor.

However, proving generic gcd behavior is much harder than proving local congruences. The recurrence gives strong information modulo a fixed prime, but gcds require simultaneous control over infinitely many primes.

## (B) Lucas congruence interpretation

For \(r,s<p\),

\[
p\mid b_r\iff r\in Z_p.
\]

The Lucas congruence

\[
b_{kp+r}\equiv b_k b_r\pmod p
\]

means that zeros propagate through base-\(p\) digits, but it does not directly control the integer gcd

\[
\gcd(b_r,b_s).
\]

For the switched sum, the relevant primes are exactly the large prime divisors

\[
p>s
\]

of this gcd.

Thus the desired statement is equivalent to saying that the pairwise gcds of Apéry numbers rarely contain primes larger than the indices.

---

## (C) Can we prove no large prime factors in gcds?

A very strong statement would be:

\[
\gcd(b_r,b_s)<s
\]

for all \(r<s\).

This is unlikely as a theorem and is not supported by known methods.

Why?

Because \(b_s\) has size roughly

\[
\log b_s\sim s\log(33.97),
\]

so it has room for many large prime divisors. There is no size obstruction preventing a rare coincidence where a large prime divides several terms.

A more realistic conjecture is:

\[
\sum_{r<s<X}\sum_{p>s}1_{p\mid b_r,b_s}
\ll X.
\]

This is exactly the ZM₂ target.

Possible tools:

### Resultants

For a fixed prime p, the recurrence gives algebraic information about zeros modulo p, but it does not produce an integer polynomial whose roots are the Apéry zeros. Therefore standard resultant arguments do not immediately apply.

### Gap polynomials

The gap polynomial method gives

\[
|Z_p|=O(p^{2/3}),
\]

but this is a vertical statement for one prime. It does not control correlations between two indices.

The missing input is horizontal independence across primes.

---

## (D) Random gcd heuristic

The random model is very plausible.

For random integers A,B,

\[
\Pr(p\mid A,B)=\frac1{p^2}.
\]

The probability that they share a prime factor larger than X is roughly

\[
\sum_{p>X}\frac1{p^2}\approx\frac1{X\log X}.
\]

Even using the weaker heuristic \(1/X\), summing over \(O(X^2)\) pairs gives the desired scale.

But making this rigorous requires proving that Apéry numbers do not have hidden arithmetic alignment.

For constant-coefficient recurrences, tools exist because one can use:

* multiplicative independence;
* S-unit equations;
* Subspace theorem arguments.

Apéry numbers are different:

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}
\]

has polynomially varying coefficients.

There is no Binet formula

\[
b_n=\sum_i c_i\alpha_i^n
\]

with fixed algebraic numbers \(\alpha_i\).

Therefore existing gcd machinery does not transfer.

---

## (E) Corvaja–Zannier comparison

Corvaja–Zannier results concern sequences such as

\[
a_n-1,
\qquad b_n-1
\]

where \(a_n,b_n\) are S-unit or linear recurrence sequences.

The key ingredients are:

1. exponential-polynomial structure;
2. multiplicative independence;
3. Subspace theorem.

Apéry numbers fail these hypotheses.

They are:

* holonomic;
* hypergeometric sums;
* solutions of Picard–Fuchs equations;
* not constant-coefficient recurrences.

Thus there is currently no theorem of the form

\[
\gcd(b_r,b_s)=e^{o(\max(r,s))}
\]

for Apéry numbers.

# What would actually prove ZM₂?

The switched sum suggests the following theorem.

## Large-prime gcd anti-concentration theorem

For \(R<S\),

\[
\sum_{R<r<s\le X}
\sum_{p>s}1_{p\mid b_r,b_s}
\ll X.
\]

Equivalent forms:

### Pairwise large-prime divisor energy

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|^2\ll \pi(X).
\]

### Integer gcd form

\[
\sum_{r<s<X}
\omega_{>s}(\gcd(b_r,b_s))\ll X.
\]

where \(\omega_{>s}\) counts prime divisors exceeding s.

This is the exact missing ZM₂ theorem.

# Relationship with AFP

This also clarifies the AFP decomposition:

* CMRD controls cross-prime Fourier correlations.
* ZM₂ controls the zero Fourier mode.

The gcd reformulation shows ZM₂ is not merely a Fourier problem; it is an arithmetic gcd anti-concentration problem.

A complete proof likely needs both:

1. geometric input (monodromy/sheaf methods) for CMRD;
2. arithmetic input on integer gcds for ZM₂.

# Recommended research direction

The most promising intermediate target is not \(\gcd(b_r,b_s)<s\), which is too strong, but:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r<s<X}\omega_{>s}(\gcd(b_r,b_s))=O(X).
}
\]

This is the exact averaged statement needed.

Possible approaches:

1. Construct a two-variable Apéry motive controlling simultaneous divisibility.
2. Use resultant/resultant-like invariants of the Picard–Fuchs recurrence.
3. Prove that the pair process \((b_r,b_s)\bmod p\) is equidistributed for most primes.
4. Combine the existing \(|Z_p|\) tail bounds with a horizontal decorrelation theorem.

At present, Corvaja–Zannier technology does not solve this problem; the obstruction is precisely the variable-coefficient nature of the Apéry recurrence.
