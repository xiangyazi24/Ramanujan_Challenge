```text

```

where `X` contains the numerator (`P`) coefficients and `Y` contains the denominator (`Q`) coefficients. The projection dimension is

```text
dim pi_P Ker[X|Y] = (#P columns) - rank[X|Y] + rank[Y].
```

Therefore dimension zero is equivalent to

```text
rank[X|Y] - rank[Y] = #P columns.
```

Equivalently, every kernel vector has zero `P` projection. Hence every modular solution of the Padé equations has numerator coefficients equal to zero in `F_379`.

This conclusion does not depend on choosing a basis vector of the kernel.

## 2. No normalization escape

The possible escape would be that a primitive integral Padé pair `(P,Q)` reduces to the zero vector modulo 379 because all coefficients are divisible by 379. This cannot happen for a primitive integral pair.


be primitive, meaning the gcd of all coefficients of the combined numerator/denominator vector is 1. If every coefficient vanished modulo 379, then 379 would divide every coefficient, contradicting primitivity.

Thus the reduction of `(P,Q)` modulo 379 is a nonzero kernel vector.

Since the numerator projection of every kernel vector is zero, the reduced numerator must satisfy

```text

```

Therefore every primitive integral numerator arising from the `(K,a)` family is divisible coefficientwise by 379.
