# Cold audit of the Franel/Newton asymptotic

## Verdict

The proposed asymptotic is **correct** under the stated hypothesis. I do not find a sign failure or a hidden large-`s` tail. There is, however, one normalization omission in the proposed proof:

\[
\frac{F_{J-s}/(J-s)}{F_J/J}
=8^{-s}\left(\frac{J}{J-s}\right)^2\left(1+O(J^{-1})\right)
\]
for `s=o(J)`, not merely `8^{-s}` times one factor `J/(J-s)`. One factor comes from the `1/m` in the Franel asymptotic `F_m ~ kappa 8^m/m`; the second comes from the explicit division by `k` in `F_k/k`.

This does **not** change the main term. On the contributing range `s=O(t+log J)`, the square factor is

\[
\left(\frac{J}{J-s}\right)^2=1+O((s+1)/J),
\]
which is smaller than the already-present complete-homogeneous perturbation `O(t(s+1)/J)` for `t>=1`.

A clean rigorous estimate is

\[
\frac{g_{J,t}}
{(-1)^{J+1}(F_J/J)a_J^{-(t-1)}(8/9)^t}
=1+O\!\left(
\frac{t(t+1)}J\left(\frac97\right)^t
\right)+o(1),
\]
where the final `o(1)` is a superpolynomially small tail under the same hypothesis. Thus the sufficient condition actually proved is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{t(t+1)}J\left(\frac97\right)^t\to0,
}
\]
which is equivalent to the user's `t^2(9/7)^t/J -> 0` for `t>=1`.

Equivalently,

\[
t\log(9/7)+2\log t-\log J\to-\infty.
\]

This is the sharp condition delivered by the **absolute-error argument below**. I do not claim it is necessary for the truth of the asymptotic; obtaining a larger range would require exploiting cancellation inside the perturbation, rather than bounding perturbations by the absolute `(8/7)^t` sum.

The derived interpolation identity is also correct, including the sign:

\[
[x^{J-1}]R_{J,t+1}
=-[z^t]\frac{S_J(z)}{Q_J(z)}
=\frac{F_J}{(J!)^2}a_J^{-t}
\left(\frac89\right)^t(1+o(1)).
\]

## 1. Exact coefficient formula and normalization

Write

\[
a_i=i(i+1),\qquad u_i=a_i^{-1}=\frac1{i(i+1)}.
\]

From the exact recurrence for `G_J`, coefficient extraction gives, for `t>=1`,

\[
g_{J,t}
=h_t(u_1,\ldots,u_J)
+\sum_{k=1}^J(-1)^{k+1}\frac{F_k}{k}
 h_{t-1}(u_k,\ldots,u_J).
\tag{1}
\]

Factor out the `k=J` term. Put `k=J-s`, `0<=s<=J-1`, and define

\[
C_{s,t}:=\binom{t+s-1}{s},
\]

\[
H_{J,s,t}:=
\frac{h_{t-1}(u_{J-s},\ldots,u_J)}{u_J^{t-1}}.
\]

Then the alternating part of (1), divided by

\[
(-1)^{J+1}\frac{F_J}{J}u_J^{t-1},
\]

is

\[
T_{J,t}:=
\sum_{s=0}^{J-1}(-1)^s A_{J,s,t},
\tag{2}
\]
where

\[
A_{J,s,t}:=
\frac{F_{J-s}/(J-s)}{F_J/J}H_{J,s,t}.
\tag{3}
\]

The target is therefore

\[
T_{J,t}=\left(\frac89\right)^t(1+o(1)).
\tag{4}
\]

---

## 2. Franel input, including the missing square factor

The Franel numbers satisfy the exact recurrence

\[
(m+1)^2F_{m+1}
=\bigl(7m(m+1)+2\bigr)F_m+8m^2F_{m-1}.
\tag{5}
\]

The standard saddle-point/Perron asymptotic is

\[
F_m=\kappa\frac{8^m}{m}\left(1+O(m^{-1})\right)
\qquad(m\to\infty)
\tag{6}
\]
for an absolute constant `kappa>0`. The proof can be obtained directly from Stirling's formula applied to `sum_k binom(m,k)^3`; only the uniform `O(1/m)` expansion is used below.

For `s=o(J)`, (6) gives

\[
\frac{F_{J-s}}{F_J}
=8^{-s}\frac{J}{J-s}\left(1+O(J^{-1})\right).
\]

Therefore, because (3) contains `F_k/k`, not `F_k`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_{J-s}/(J-s)}{F_J/J}
=8^{-s}\left(\frac{J}{J-s}\right)^2
\left(1+O(J^{-1})\right).
}
\tag{7}
\]

This is the normalization correction missing from the proposed proof.

For the deep tail we also use two elementary bounds. First,

\[
F_m\le\left(\sum_j\binom mj\right)^3=8^m.
\tag{8}
\]

Second, (5) gives

\[
\frac{F_{m+1}}{F_m}\ge5\qquad(m\ge1).
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed equality holds at `m=1`; for `m=2` the ratio is `56/10>5`; and for `m>=3` the first positive term on the right of (5), divided by `(m+1)^2F_m`, already exceeds `5`.

## 3. Complete-homogeneous normalization

For `0<=q<=s`,

\[
\frac{u_{J-q}}{u_J}
=\frac{J(J+1)}{(J-q)(J-q+1)}.
\]

Set

\[
\rho_{J,s}:=\frac{u_{J-s}}{u_J}.
\]

All variables in the normalized complete homogeneous polynomial lie between `1` and `rho_{J,s}`. Since a complete homogeneous polynomial of degree `t-1` in `s+1` equal variables `1` is exactly `C_{s,t}`, we have the exact sandwich

\[
C_{s,t}
\le H_{J,s,t}
\le C_{s,t}\rho_{J,s}^{t-1}.
\tag{10}
\]

If `s=o(J)`, then

\[
\log\rho_{J,s}=O((s+1)/J).
\tag{11}
\]

Consequently, whenever `t(s+1)/J=o(1)`,

\[
\boxed{
H_{J,s,t}
=C_{s,t}\left(1+O\left(\frac{t(s+1)}J\right)\right).
}
\tag{12}
\]

This checks the complete-homogeneous normalization exactly. There is no missing extra binomial coefficient: there are `s+1` variables and degree `t-1`, hence

\[
h_{t-1}(1,\ldots,1)=\binom{(t-1)+(s+1)-1}{s}=\binom{t+s-1}{s}.
\]

Combining (7), (12), and

\[
\left(\frac{J}{J-s}\right)^2=1+O((s+1)/J)
\]
shows, uniformly on a local range with `t(s+1)/J=o(1)`,

\[
A_{J,s,t}
=8^{-s}C_{s,t}
\left(1+O\left(\frac{t(s+1)}J\right)\right).
\tag{13}
\]

Thus the omitted square factor is harmless but should be recorded.

## 4. The ideal signed and absolute sums

The generating function

\[
\sum_{s\ge0}C_{s,t}x^s=(1-x)^{-t}
\]
gives immediately

\[
\sum_{s\ge0}(-1)^s8^{-s}C_{s,t}
=\left(1+\frac18\right)^{-t}
=\boxed{\left(\frac89\right)^t},
\tag{14}
\]

whereas

\[
\sum_{s\ge0}8^{-s}C_{s,t}
=\left(1-\frac18\right)^{-t}
=\boxed{\left(\frac87\right)^t}.
\tag{15}
\]

For the perturbation in (13) we need one weighted absolute sum. Differentiating the generating function gives

\[
\sum_{s\ge0}(s+1)C_{s,t}x^s
=\bigl(1+(t-1)x\bigr)(1-x)^{-t-1}.
\]

At `x=1/8`,

\[
\sum_{s\ge0}(s+1)8^{-s}C_{s,t}
\ll (t+1)\left(\frac87\right)^t.
\tag{16}
\]

Hence the total local perturbation in (13) is

\[
O\left(
\frac{t(t+1)}J\left(\frac87\right)^t
\right).
\tag{17}
\]

Relative to the signed main term (14), this is

\[
\boxed{
O\left(
\frac{t(t+1)}J\left(\frac97\right)^t
\right).
}
\tag{18}
\]

This is exactly where the factor `(9/7)^t` enters. It is not a tail effect; it is the cost of controlling a signed cancellation by an absolute perturbation estimate.

## 5. The hypothesis already forces `t=O(log J)`

Assume

\[
E_{J,t}:=
\frac{t(t+1)}J\left(\frac97\right)^t\to0.
\tag{19}
\]

Then eventually `(9/7)^t <= J`, hence

\[
t\le\frac{\log J}{\log(9/7)}.
\tag{20}
\]

So throughout the proof

\[
t=O(\log J).
\tag{21}
\]

This observation makes the large-`s` tail much easier than it first appears.

Take, for definiteness,

\[
L=\left\lceil20(t+\log J)\right\rceil.
\tag{22}
\]

Then `L=O(log J)=o(J)` and `tL/J=o(1)`, so (13) holds uniformly for `0<=s<=L`.

---

## 6. Uniform tail estimate

We now show that replacing the finite sum in (2) by the infinite ideal sum (14) creates an error negligible compared with `(8/9)^t`.

### 6.1 Ideal tail beyond `L`

Put

\[
q_s=8^{-s}C_{s,t}.
\]

For `s>=L>=2t`,

\[
\frac{q_{s+1}}{q_s}
=\frac{t+s}{8(s+1)}\le\frac14.
\tag{23}
\]

Also

\[
C_{s,t}\le2^{s+t}.
\]

Thus

\[
\sum_{s>L}q_s\ll q_L
\le2^t4^{-L}.
\tag{24}
\]

With (22) and (20), the right-hand side is `J^{-A}` times `(8/9)^t` for any fixed prescribed `A` after increasing the harmless constant `20`. Hence

\[
\sum_{s>L}8^{-s}C_{s,t}
=o\left((8/9)^t\right).
\tag{25}
\]

### 6.2 Actual tail with `L<s<=J/(100(t+1))`

On this range `J-s~J`, (6) gives

\[
\frac{F_{J-s}/(J-s)}{F_J/J}\ll8^{-s},
\]
while (10)-(11) give

\[
H_{J,s,t}\ll C_{s,t}
\]
with an absolute implied constant, because `ts/J<=1/100`.

Therefore this part of the actual tail is bounded by a constant times the ideal absolute tail in (25), and is negligible.

### 6.3 Intermediate tail `J/(100(t+1))<s<=J/2`

Here `J-s>=J/2`, so the Franel asymptotic still yields

\[
\frac{F_{J-s}/(J-s)}{F_J/J}\ll8^{-s}.
\]

Also

\[
\rho_{J,s}\le4,
\qquad
C_{s,t}\le2^{s+t}.
\]

Hence by (10)

\[
A_{J,s,t}
\ll8^{-s}2^{s+t}4^t
=4^{-s}8^t.
\tag{26}
\]

Since `t=O(log J)` and

\[
s\ge \frac{J}{100(t+1)},
\]

the sum of (26) over this range is

\[
\exp\left(-\Omega(J/\log J)+O(\log J)\right),
\tag{27}
\]
which is superpolynomially smaller than `(8/9)^t`.

### 6.4 Deep tail `s>J/2`

Write `k=J-s<J/2`. From (8) and the lower half of (6), for all large `J`,

\[
\frac{F_k/k}{F_J/J}
\ll \frac{J^2}{k}8^{-(J-k)}
\ll J^2 8^{-s}.
\tag{28}
\]

Furthermore

\[
\frac{u_k}{u_J}\le J(J+1)\ll J^2,
\]
so (10), now with a crude maximum-variable bound, gives

\[
H_{J,s,t}
\le C_{s,t}(CJ^2)^t
\le2^{s+t}(CJ^2)^t.
\tag{29}
\]

Combining (28)-(29), and using `s>J/2`, yields

\[
A_{J,s,t}
\le \exp\left(-cJ+O(t\log J)\right)
=\exp\left(-cJ+O((\log J)^2)\right).
\tag{30}
\]

Summing `J` such terms remains exponentially small.

Therefore the complete actual tail `s>L` is

\[
o\left((8/9)^t\right).
\tag{31}
\]

This disposes of the proposed proof's most dangerous uniformity issue: the terms with `k` far from `J` cannot re-emerge through the large ratios `u_k/u_J` because `t=O(log J)` while the Franel factor supplies exponential decay in `J-k`.

## 7. Completion of the alternating-sum asymptotic

On `0<=s<=L`, use (13). The signed ideal sum gives (14), the local perturbation is controlled by (17), and both the ideal and actual tails are negligible by (25), (31). Hence

\[
T_{J,t}
=\left(\frac89\right)^t
+O\left(
\frac{t(t+1)}J\left(\frac87\right)^t
\right)
+o\left((8/9)^t\right).
\]

Therefore, under (19),

\[
T_{J,t}
=\left(\frac89\right)^t(1+o(1)).
\tag{32}
\]

Returning to (2), the alternating part of `g_{J,t}` is

\[
(-1)^{J+1}\frac{F_J}{J}u_J^{t-1}
\left(\frac89\right)^t(1+o(1)).
\tag{33}
\]

## 8. The origin term is negligible

Because

\[
\sum_{i=1}^J u_i
=\sum_{i=1}^J\frac1{i(i+1)}
=\frac{J}{J+1}<1,
\]

and every monomial in `h_t` occurs in `(sum u_i)^t` with multinomial coefficient at least `1`,

\[
0<h_t(u_1,\ldots,u_J)
\le\left(\frac{J}{J+1}\right)^t<1.
\tag{34}
\]

On the other hand, from (6), `u_J=1/(J(J+1))`, and `t=O(log J)`, the magnitude of the main term in (33) has logarithm

\[
J\log8-2t\log J+O(t+\log J)
=J\log8-O((\log J)^2),
\]
so it tends to infinity exponentially in `J`.

Thus the origin term in (1) is negligible relative to (33). We obtain

\[
\boxed{
g_{J,t}
=(-1)^{J+1}\frac{F_J}{J}
 a_J^{-(t-1)}\left(\frac89\right)^t(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{35}
\]

This proves the claimed asymptotic.

## 9. What is the sharp condition obtained here?

The proof gives

\[
E_{J,t}
=\frac{t(t+1)}J\left(\frac97\right)^t\to0.
\tag{36}
\]

For `t>=1`, this is equivalent to

\[
\frac{t^2}{J}\left(\frac97\right)^t\to0.
\]

An equivalent logarithmic formulation is

\[
t\log(9/7)+2\log t-\log J\to-\infty.
\tag{37}
\]

In particular, if `t=c log J`, every fixed

\[
c<\frac1{\log(9/7)}
\]

is allowed. At the transition scale, a sufficient form is

\[
t\le
\frac{\log J-2\log\log J-\omega(1)}{\log(9/7)}.
\tag{38}
\]

I would call (36), not merely `t=O(log J)`, the sharp condition **of this proof**.

There is a plausible route to a larger range: the first-order perturbation is roughly proportional to `ts/J`, and the *signed* identity

\[
\frac{\sum_s(-1)^s8^{-s}C_{s,t}s}
{(8/9)^t}
=-\frac{t}{9}
\]

suggests polynomial rather than `(9/7)^t` relative amplification if one can preserve cancellation through the full perturbation. But that requires a new signed stability argument. The present proof does not justify replacing (36) by `t^2/J -> 0`, so I do **not** claim that stronger range.

## 10. Audit of the interpolation coefficient identity

The exact Franel-Apéry/Strehl identity is

\[
b_h
=\sum_{k=0}^h
\binom hk\binom{h+k}{k}F_k.
\tag{39}
\]

For `h<=J`,

\[
\frac1{(k!)^2}
\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(a_h-a_i)
=inom hk\binom{h+k}{k},
\]

because

\[
a_h-a_i=(h-i)(h+i+1).
\]

Hence

\[
S_J(a_h)=b_h\qquad(1\le h\le J).
\tag{40}
\]

Let

\[
Q_J(z)=\prod_{h=1}^J(z-a_h).
\]

Since `deg S_J=deg Q_J=J`, partial fractions give

\[
\frac{S_J(z)}{Q_J(z)}
=\frac{F_J}{(J!)^2}
+\sum_{h=1}^J
\frac{b_h}{Q_J'(a_h)(z-a_h)}.
\tag{41}
\]

For `t>=1`, the constant term at infinity in (41) does not contribute to `[z^t]`. Expanding each pole at `z=0`,

\[
-[z^t]\frac{S_J(z)}{Q_J(z)}
=\sum_{h=1}^J
\frac{b_h}{Q_J'(a_h)a_h^{t+1}}.
\tag{42}
\]

If `R_{J,w}` is the degree `<J` Lagrange interpolation polynomial satisfying

\[
R_{J,w}(a_h)=\frac{b_h}{a_h^w},
\qquad 1\le h\le J,
\]

then its leading coefficient is

\[
[x^{J-1}]R_{J,w}
=\sum_{h=1}^J\frac{b_h/a_h^w}{Q_J'(a_h)}.
\]

Therefore, with `w=t+1`,

\[
\boxed{
[x^{J-1}]R_{J,t+1}
=-[z^t]\frac{S_J(z)}{Q_J(z)}.
}
\tag{43}
\]

Now use

\[
G_J(z)=(-1)^J J!(J+1)!\frac{S_J(z)}{Q_J(z)}.
\]

Thus

\[
-[z^t]\frac{S_J}{Q_J}
=\frac{(-1)^{J+1}}{J!(J+1)!}g_{J,t}.
\tag{44}
\]

Insert (35). The signs cancel. Since

\[
J!(J+1)!=(J+1)(J!)^2,
\qquad a_J=J(J+1),
\]

we get

\[
\begin{aligned}
-[z^t]\frac{S_J}{Q_J}
&=\frac1{J!(J+1)!}
\frac{F_J}{J}a_J^{-(t-1)}
\left(\frac89\right)^t(1+o(1))\\
&=\boxed{
\frac{F_J}{(J!)^2}a_J^{-t}
\left(\frac89\right)^t(1+o(1)).
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{45}
\]

So the derived identity and its sign are correct.

---

## 11. Final audit summary

- **No fatal sign error.** The relative signs are `(-1)^s`, and their ideal sum is `(8/9)^t`.
- **There is a missing normalization factor in the proposed proof:** `(J/(J-s))^2`, not one copy. It is harmless in the allowed range.
- **The complete-homogeneous normalization is correct:** the ideal factor is `binom(t+s-1,s)`.
- **The tail is safe.** The hypothesis already forces `t=O(log J)`; exponential Franel decay beats the worst possible reciprocal-node growth for `s` far from `0`.
- **The origin term is negligible** by the telescoping identity `sum_i u_i=J/(J+1)` and the exponential size of the last-term scale.
- **The exact sufficient condition is** `t(t+1)(9/7)^t/J -> 0`, equivalent to the stated hypothesis.
- **The interpolation leading-coefficient formula is correct**, including the positive final main term in (45).
