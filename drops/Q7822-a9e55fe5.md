# Verdict

This approach does **not** currently give a large-sieve theorem for the Apéry coefficient zeros.  There are three separate obstructions.

1. The proposed sum
   \[
   S_p(\chi)=\sum_{t:H_p(t)=0}\chi(t)
   \]
   is the multiplicative Fourier transform of the **geometric root indicator**
   \(1_{H_p=0}\).  By contrast,
   \[
   b_r=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}H_p(t)t^{-r}
   \]
   is the multiplicative Fourier transform of the **full value function**
   \(H_p(t)\).  These are different observables.  In fact, every geometric
   root contributes zero to the formula for \(b_r\).

2. There is no generic uniform \(O(\sqrt p)\) bound for \(S_p(\chi)\), even
   for nontrivial \(\chi\), from squarefreeness and coprimality.  The relevant
   root scheme is zero-dimensional, and its natural Weil bound is its degree.
   A squarefree degree-\(\asymp p\) polynomial can have almost all of its
   \(\mathbf F_p\)-roots in one character class, giving
   \(|S_p(\chi)|\asymp p\).

3. Mason--Stothers applies to a nontrivial **additive** identity
   \(A+B=C\) of coprime polynomials.  The identity
   \(H_p=\Delta^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^2\) is multiplicative.  It controls the
   divisor in the geometric variable \(t\), not cancellation among the
   coefficients indexed by \(r\).  There are squarefree polynomials \(B\),
   coprime to \(\Delta\), having exactly the same degree pattern for which
   \(\Delta^\varepsilon B^2\) has only \(O(1)\) nonzero coefficients.

The strongest unconditional statement supplied by the root set is an exact
Parseval identity:

\[
\sum_{\chi\bmod p}|S_p(\chi)|^2=(p-1)R_p,
\qquad
R_p=\#\{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times:H_p(t)=0\}.
\]

Thus a typical nontrivial character has size about \(\sqrt{R_p}\), but the
maximum can be \(R_p\).  Poisson tails require higher multiplicative-energy
bounds for the root divisor, and even those would concern the wrong Fourier
transform for the Apéry problem.

The viable target remains a bounded-complexity realization or a direct
anti-concentration theorem for the **value Mellin transform**

\[
M_p(r):=\sum_{t\ne0}\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2t^{-r}
       =-b_r\pmod p,
\]

not merely for the roots of \(B_p\).

# 0. Corrections and logical status in the repository

The checked-in proposition in
`problems/3.2/hasse_franel_descent.tex` proves

\[
H_p(t)=A_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_mt^m
          =\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
\qquad
\Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1,
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\deg B_p=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon_p}{2}.}
\tag{0.1}
\]

Hence the degree is asymptotic to \(p/2\), **not** \(p/3\).

The checked-in factorization proposition identifies \(B_p\) as a truncation
of one of two universal second-order series.  It does not, in its current
form, prove that every \(B_p\) is squarefree or that
\(\gcd(B_p,\Delta)=1\).  I will grant those two properties as hypotheses in
the discussion below; they do not fix any of the three obstructions.

There is also a small root-set correction.  If \(\varepsilon_p=1\) and
\(B_p\) is coprime to \(\Delta\), then

\[
\{H_p=0\}(\mathbf F_p)
=
\{B_p=0\}(\mathbf F_p)
\sqcup
\{\Delta=0\}(\mathbf F_p).
\tag{0.2}
\]

The second set has at most two points.  Therefore replacing the roots of
\(H_p\) by the roots of \(B_p\) changes every character sum by at most two;
it has no effect on the asymptotic conclusions.

For \(1\le r\le p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality gives the exact
coefficient identity

\[
\boxed{
 b_r=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}H_p(t)t^{-r}.}
\tag{0.3}
\]

Here \(t\mapsto t^{-r}\) may be regarded either as the power character in
\(\mathbf F_p\) or, after a Teichmüller lift and an embedding, as a
multiplicative character.  Formula (0.3) is the bridge that must be preserved.

# 1. Root Fourier data and coefficient Fourier data are different

Put

\[
G=\mathbf F_p^\times,
\qquad n=|G|=p-1,
\]

and define

\[
f_p(t)=H_p(t),
\qquad
R_p=\{t\in G:f_p(t)=0\},
\qquad
\rho_p=1_{R_p}.
\]

For a multiplicative character \(\chi\) of \(G\), the proposed sum is

\[
S_p(\chi)=\widehat\rho_p(\chi)
         :=\sum_{t\in G}\rho_p(t)\chi(t).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The Apéry coefficient, however, is

\[
-b_r=\widehat f_p(\omega^{-r})
    :=\sum_{t\in G}f_p(t)\omega(t)^{-r}\pmod p,
\tag{1.2}
\]

where \(\omega\) is the Teichmüller character.  Thus the two transforms are

\[
\boxed{
S_p=\mathcal M(1_{H_p=0}),
\qquad
-b_r=\mathcal M(H_p).}
\tag{1.3}
\]

They should not be interchanged.

Indeed,

\[
f_p(t)=0\quad(t\in R_p),
\]

so the geometric roots make **no contribution at all** to (1.2).  The value
of \(b_r\) is determined by the values of \(H_p\) on the ordinary locus
\(G\setminus R_p\), with their phases.  A cancellation theorem for the root
indicator therefore does not imply anti-concentration of \(b_r\).

Even knowing all character sums \(S_p(\chi)\) reconstructs only the indicator
of the \(\mathbf F_p\)-rational root set.  It does not reconstruct:

- the nonzero values \(B_p(t)^2\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}\);
- irreducible factors of \(B_p\) having no \(\mathbf F_p\)-root;
- the scalar normalization of \(B_p\);
- the Mellin coefficients in (1.2).

This is the same coefficient-versus-fiber distinction isolated in Q7799 and
Q7808.

# 2. Question (1): what Weil actually gives

## 2.1 The trivial character already rules out uniformity in all characters

For the trivial character \(1\),

\[
S_p(1)=R_p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Therefore a bound

\[
S_p(\chi)=O(\sqrt p)
\quad\text{uniformly in all }\chi
\]

would first require the separate theorem \(R_p=O(\sqrt p)\).  Neither the
degree formula nor the factorization provides such a theorem.

The only automatic root-count bound is

\[
R_p\le \deg B_p+2
     =\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon_p}{2}+2
     =\frac p2+O(1).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Hence even the trivial character can naturally be of linear size.

## 2.2 A zero-dimensional Weil bound is a degree bound, not a square-root bound

Assume \(B_p\) is squarefree.  Its reduced zero scheme is
zero-dimensional of degree \(d_p=\deg B_p\).  Restricting a Kummer sheaf
\(\mathcal L_\chi\) to that scheme gives a vector space of dimension at most
\(d_p\).  The trace formula therefore yields only

\[
\boxed{|S_p(\chi)|\le R_p\le d_p+2.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

There is no ambient set of \(p\) points here on which a bounded-complexity
curve sheaf is being summed.  The familiar \(O(\sqrt p)\) cancellation for a
complete one-variable trace sum does not apply merely because the summands
are roots of a polynomial.

## 2.3 Additive completion recovers the degree-dependent bound

Let \(\psi\) be a nontrivial additive character.  For \(x\in\mathbf F_p\),

\[
1_{B_p(x)=0}
=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}\psi(uB_p(x)).
\tag{2.4}
\]

For nontrivial \(\chi\), this gives

\[
S_{B,p}(\chi)
=
\frac1p
\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
 \chi(x)\psi(uB_p(x)).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Under the usual nondegeneracy hypotheses, each inner complete sum has the
Weil--Deligne estimate

\[
\left|
\sum_{x\ne0}\chi(x)\psi(uB_p(x))
\right|
\ll d_p\sqrt p.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Termwise summation over \(u\) therefore gives only

\[
|S_{B,p}(\chi)|\ll d_p\sqrt p.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Since \(d_p\sim p/2\), this is \(O(p^{3/2})\), worse than the trivial
bound (2.3).  Gaining cancellation in the additional \(u\)-sum would be a
new two-variable theorem about this particular family; it is not supplied by
the one-variable Weil bound.

Equivalently, the conductor of the direct root-divisor realization grows with
\(d_p\).  The polynomial is “one fixed polynomial” after \(p\) is fixed, but
it is not a bounded-complexity object as \(p\to\infty\).

## 2.4 Squarefree and coprime do not force character cancellation

Let \(\eta\) be the quadratic character and set

\[
d=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

Choose a set \(A\subset\mathbf F_p^\times\) of cardinality \(d\), avoiding
the at most two roots of \(\Delta\), with all but \(O(1)\) elements quadratic
residues.  Put

\[
B_A(T)=\prod_{a\in A}(T-a).
\tag{2.8}
\]

Then:

- \(B_A\) is squarefree;
- \(\gcd(B_A,\Delta)=1\);
- \(\deg B_A=d\);
- but
  \[
  \sum_{B_A(t)=0}\eta(t)=d-O(1)=\frac p2+O(1).
  \tag{2.9}
  \]

Thus even a **nontrivial** character can have a linear root sum under exactly
the formal hypotheses proposed in the question.

The simplest model is

\[
B(T)=T^{(p-1)/2}-1,
\tag{2.10}
\]

whose roots are all quadratic residues.  It is squarefree, and its quadratic
character sum is \((p-1)/2\).  Replacing the finitely many roots that might
meet \(\Delta=0\) gives (2.8) while changing the sum by only \(O(1)\).

This does not assert that the actual Apéry polynomial behaves adversarially.
It proves that an Apéry-specific \(O(\sqrt p)\) theorem would need genuinely
new modular/cohomological input beyond degree, squarefreeness, coprimality,
and the displayed factorization.

## 2.5 The honest possible theorem

A potentially useful new statement would be

\[
\boxed{
\max_{\chi\ne1}
\left|
\sum_{t:H_p(t)=0}\chi(t)
\right|
\ll p^{1/2+o(1)}.}
\tag{RH-Mellin}
\]

But `(RH-Mellin)` is not a routine Weil corollary.  It is an equidistribution
theorem for a divisor whose degree grows linearly with \(p\).  Even if proved,
it would still need a second comparison theorem connecting the root-indicator
transform to the value transform (1.2).

# 3. Question (2): Turán--Kubilius does not model this observable

## 3.1 The exponent \(t^{-m}\) is a phase, not a hit

For every \(m\), every nonzero root \(t\in R_p\) contributes a character
value \(t^{-m}\).  There is no random subset of roots “hit” by the column
\(m\).  The statistic is a Fourier coefficient

\[
S_p(m)=\sum_{t\in R_p}\omega(t)^{-m},
\tag{3.1}
\]

not a counting function.

If one instead defines the artificial count

\[
N_p(m)=\#\{t\in R_p:t^m=1\},
\tag{3.2}
\]

then

\[
N_p(m)=\#\bigl(R_p\cap\mu_{\gcd(m,p-1)}\bigr).
\]

This is controlled by the divisor structure of \(p-1\), is generally far
from Poisson, and has no direct relation to \(b_m=0\).

## 3.2 The exact finite-group second moment

Let \(R=|R_p|\).  Orthogonality on the character group \(\widehat G\)
gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}|S_p(\chi)|^2=(p-1)R.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Removing the trivial character gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{\chi\in\widehat G\\\chi\ne1}}
 |S_p(\chi)|^2
=R\bigl((p-1)-R\bigr).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\chi\ne1:|S_p(\chi)|\ge T\}
\le
\frac{R((p-1)-R)}{T^2}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

At the natural scale \(T=\lambda\sqrt R\),

\[
\#\{\chi\ne1:|S_p(\chi)|\ge\lambda\sqrt R\}
\le\frac{p-1-R}{\lambda^2}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is a rigorous averaged cancellation statement.  It is only a
Chebyshev tail, not a Poisson tail and not a uniform character bound.

## 3.3 Higher moments are multiplicative energies

The fourth moment is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}|S_p(\chi)|^4
=(p-1)E_\times(R_p),}
\tag{3.7}
\]

where

\[
E_\times(R_p)
=
\#\{(t_1,t_2,t_3,t_4)\in R_p^4:t_1t_3=t_2t_4\}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

More generally, the \(2k\)-th moment counts equal-product configurations of
\(k\) roots on each side.  Gaussian or Poisson-type large deviations require
near-random bounds for this entire hierarchy of multiplicative energies.
Squarefreeness gives no such bounds.

For example, if \(R_p\) is a multiplicative subgroup, then

\[
S_p(\chi)=
\begin{cases}
R,&\chi|_{R_p}=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\tag{3.9}
\]

so a small set of characters has maximal spikes.  This is precisely the
behavior that second-moment information cannot exclude.

## 3.4 Why classical Turán--Kubilius is inapplicable

The Turán--Kubilius inequality concerns additive arithmetic functions on
integers, assembled from prime-power contributions.  Neither

\[
m\longmapsto S_p(m)
\]

nor

\[
m\longmapsto1_{p\mid b_{m\bmod p}}
\]

is an additive arithmetic function of \(m\).

Across a dyadic prime block, the relevant Apéry variable is

\[
K_X(m)=\sum_{X<p\le2X}1_{p\mid b_{m\bmod p}}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Its large deviations require cross-prime factorial moments or Palm
contractions.  That is exactly the `(HM)_k` / `(FOPC)_k` problem developed in
Q7786--Q7810.  It is not converted into Turán--Kubilius by replacing
coefficient-zero sets with the geometric roots of \(B_p\).

Most importantly, (3.3)--(3.8) are moments of
\(\mathcal M(1_{H_p=0})\), whereas the event in (3.10) is a zero of
\(\mathcal M(H_p)\).  Even perfect Poisson statistics for the former would
not imply the latter.

# 4. Question (3): Mason--Stothers sees the wrong structure

## 4.1 “Fixed polynomial” is not bounded complexity

For each fixed \(p\), every finite subset of \(\mathbf F_p\) is the root set
of a single polynomial.  Encoding a varying set by one polynomial therefore
does not itself add rigidity.

Here \(B_p\) changes with \(p\), its degree is \(p/2+O(1)\), and its
coefficients are characteristic-dependent truncations.  Thus it is not a
fixed-degree family to which a uniform bounded-conductor Weil theorem can be
applied.

The fact that the actual \(B_p\) also satisfies a second-order differential
recurrence is genuine extra Apéry structure.  However, no theorem currently
in the repository converts that structure into anti-concentration of the
coefficients of \(\Delta^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^2\).

## 4.2 Mason--Stothers needs an additive coprime identity

The polynomial abc theorem applies to a nontrivial relation

\[
A(T)+B(T)=C(T)
\tag{4.1}
\]

with the three terms pairwise coprime (after removing their common gcd) and
with a separability/derivative condition in positive characteristic.  It then
bounds a maximum degree by the degree of the radical of \(ABC\).

The factorization

\[
H_p=\Delta^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^2
\tag{4.2}
\]

is multiplicative.  Writing

\[
H_p-\Delta^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^2=0
\]

does not create a Mason triple: the two nonzero terms are the same polynomial
up to sign, share every factor, and the third term is zero.  Dividing by the
common factor leaves the tautology \(1-1=0\).

Likewise,

\[
\frac{H_p'}{H_p}
=arepsilon_p\frac{\Delta'}\Delta+2\frac{B_p'}{B_p}
\tag{4.3}
\]

records only root multiplicities.  It does not constrain cancellation in the
coefficient convolution.

## 4.3 A direct counterexample with the exact degree pattern

Set

\[
d=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon}{2}
\]

and choose \(c\in\mathbf F_p^\times\) outside the at most two values for
which \(1+cT^d\) shares a root with \(\Delta\).  Put

\[
B_c(T)=1+cT^d.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Because \(0<d<p\),

\[
B_c'(T)=cdT^{d-1},
\]

and \(B_c(0)=1\), so \(B_c\) is squarefree.  By the choice of \(c\),

\[
\gcd(B_c,\Delta)=1.
\]

It has exactly the degree required in (0.1).  Nevertheless,

\[
B_c(T)^2=1+2cT^d+c^2T^{2d}
\tag{4.5}
\]

has only three nonzero coefficients.  Therefore

\[
\Delta(T)^\varepsilon B_c(T)^2
\tag{4.6}
\]

has at most nine nonzero coefficients.  Its total degree is exactly

\[
2d+2\varepsilon=p-1,
\]

but it has

\[
p-O(1)
\]

zero coefficient indices.

Thus the following package is compatible with almost maximal coefficient
sparsity:

```text
exact degree p-1;
H = Delta^epsilon B^2;
B squarefree;
gcd(B, Delta) = 1.
```

No Mason--Stothers consequence depending only on that package can bound the
number of \(r\) for which \([T^r]H=0\).

## 4.4 Fourier uncertainty gives only a constant lower bound

On \(G=\mathbf F_p^\times\), let

\[
\operatorname{supp}(f_p)=G\setminus R_p.
\]

The elementary finite-group uncertainty inequality gives

\[
|\operatorname{supp}(f_p)|
\,|\operatorname{supp}(\widehat f_p)|
\ge p-1.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since the factorization yields at best

\[
|R_p|\le\frac p2+O(1),
\]

it follows only that

\[
|\operatorname{supp}(\widehat f_p)|\ge2-O(1/p).
\tag{4.8}
\]

In other words, root support alone is consistent with all but \(O(1)\) Mellin
coefficients vanishing.  The sparse family (4.4)--(4.6) makes this obstruction
concrete.

# 5. The strongest theorem that survives from the root approach

Let

\[
R_p^B=\{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times:B_p(t)=0\},
\qquad R=|R_p^B|.
\]

Without any unproved randomness assumption one has:

\[
R\le \deg B_p=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon_p}{2},
\tag{5.1}
\]

\[
|S_p(\chi)|\le R,
\tag{5.2}
\]

\[
\sum_{\chi}|S_p(\chi)|^2=(p-1)R,
\tag{5.3}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{\chi}|S_p(\chi)|^{2k}
=(p-1)
\#\left\{
\prod_{i=1}^kt_i=\prod_{i=1}^ks_i:
 t_i,s_i\in R_p^B
\right\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

These identities provide a valid experimental programme: compute the
multiplicative energies of the geometric nonordinary divisor and test whether
it resembles a random set.  They do **not** yet provide a theorem about
\(Z(p)=\#\{r:p\mid b_r\}\).

A bridge would require an additional statement of one of the following forms.

### Value-Mellin anti-concentration

\[
\boxed{
\#\left\{1\le r\le p-2:
\sum_{t\ne0}\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2t^{-r}=0
\right\}
\ll p^{1-\delta}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Bounded-complexity coefficient realization

Construct an \(r\)-indexed trace object of uniformly bounded rank and conductor
whose Frobenius trace, or a fixed polynomial in it, equals the Mellin
coefficient in (0.3).

### A comparison from value Mellin data to root Mellin data

Prove a quantitative inequality that controls zeros of
\(\mathcal M(H_p)\) by higher moments of \(\mathcal M(1_{H_p=0})\).
No such inequality follows from the factorization, and the examples above show
that it must use additional Apéry-specific information about the nonzero
values of \(B_p\).

For the all-index conjecture, even (5.5) is only a single-prime input.  The
proof still needs cross-prime dispersion/factorial moments to prevent the
remaining coefficient zeros from aligning along one integer column.

# 6. Direct answers

## (1) Is \(S_p(\chi)=O(\sqrt p)\) uniformly by Weil?

**No.**  Including the trivial character, this would require
\(R_p=O(\sqrt p)\).  For nontrivial characters, a sum over a reduced
zero-dimensional scheme has only the degree bound \(O(\deg B_p)\), and
\(\deg B_p\sim p/2\).  Additive completion gives the degree-dependent
\(O(\deg B_p\sqrt p)\), not an absolute square-root bound.  Squarefree
polynomials coprime to \(\Delta\) can have a nontrivial character sum of
size \(p/2+O(1)\).  Any square-root theorem for the actual Apéry divisor
would be new Apéry-specific arithmetic geometry.

## (2) Can Turán--Kubilius give Poisson deviations?

**No, not for the proposed statistic.**  The factor \(t^{-m}\) is a phase,
not a root-hit indicator, and the resulting function of \(m\) is not an
additive arithmetic function.  Parseval gives the exact second moment and
Chebyshev bound (3.3)--(3.6).  Poisson-type tails require control of all higher
multiplicative energies (3.7)--(3.8).  Moreover, those moments concern the
root indicator, not the Apéry coefficient-zero event.

## (3) Does Mason--Stothers constrain the \(r\)-indexed zeros?

**No.**  The identity is multiplicative rather than an admissible additive
abc identity, and Mason controls radicals in the geometric variable, not
coefficient cancellation.  The explicit family
\(B_c=1+cT^d\) is squarefree, coprime to \(\Delta\), has the required degree,
and makes \(\Delta^\varepsilon B_c^2\) have only \(O(1)\) nonzero
coefficients.  Therefore the factorization alone cannot give any nontrivial
bound on the coefficient-zero set.

# 7. Recommended next computation

The geometric-root experiment is still useful as a diagnostic, provided it
is kept logically separate from the coefficient problem.  For each prime,
compute

\[
R_p,
\qquad
\max_{\chi\ne1}|S_p(\chi)|,
\qquad
E_\times(R_p),
\qquad
\sum_\chi|S_p(\chi)|^{2k}
\quad(k=2,3,4),
\]

and compare them with random subsets of the same cardinality.  In parallel,
compute the full value Mellin transform

\[
M_p(r)=\sum_{t\ne0}H_p(t)t^{-r}=-b_r
\]

and measure whether large root-Fourier coefficients correlate at all with
zeros of \(M_p(r)\).  A lack of correlation would close this route
experimentally; a strong reproducible correlation would identify the precise
new comparison theorem that needs proving.
