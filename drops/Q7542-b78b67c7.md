**No nontrivial \(D\)-saving in the CHLS operator norm follows from the proved local passport/decomposition rigidity alone.** This is a logical no-implication statement, not a claim about the actual Apéry family.

The sharp obstruction is an algebraic anchored-star model. Prime by prime, for every sufficiently large \(p\), one can construct a squarefree polynomial \(B_p^*\), coprime to \(T\Delta\), with the *actual* choice of \(arepsilon_p\in\{0,1\}\), such that

\[
A_p^*(T)=\Delta(T)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^*(T)^2,
\qquad [T]A_p^*(T)=0.
\]

The corresponding primitive

\[
H_{p,1}^*(T)=T^{-1}\sum_{m\ne1}\frac{[T^m]A_p^*}{m-1}T^m
\]

has exactly the stated local cover passport:

- degree \(p-1\);
- pole indices \(1,p-2\);
- cubic ramification at every root of \(B_p^*\);
- the two index-\(2\) conifold points when \(arepsilon_p=1\);
- no other ramification;
- tameness;
- in fact **absolute indecomposability** for the elementary reason that its two pole orders are \(1,p-2\).

Thus it satisfies the strongest possible \(r=1\) version of the decomposition conclusion. Yet for an interval \(I\) of \(P\) consecutive integers containing \(1\), every prime \(P<p\le2P\) hits the anchored column \(m=1\), while

\[
d_p(1)=p-1>D
\]

for every \(D<P-1\). Hence

\[
\|M^*\|_{2\to2}^2\ge \pi(2P)-\pi(P)\sim\frac{P}{\log P}.
\]

Taking, for example, \(D=P^\delta\) with \(0<\delta<1\), the proposed CHLS right side

\[
\frac{P}{D^\eta\log P}+P^{1-\eta}
\]

is \(o(P/\log P)\). Therefore **no estimate with any positive power saving in \(D\) can be a formal consequence of the local passport/decomposition outputs**.

There is one essential scope qualification. The synthetic \(B_p^*\) do **not** satisfy the distinguished Apéry second-order ODE. The normalized solution of that ODE is precisely arithmetic information selecting the actual Apéry \(B_p\), so requiring the exact ODE would remove the freedom used in the countermodel. Thus the conclusion is:

> the proved *passport, ramification and decomposition consequences* have zero horizontal force by themselves; any successful use of the exact Apéry ODE must extract a new relation that is uniform **across changing defining characteristics**. That new relation would be the missing arithmetic input, not an automatic corollary of the passport.

\[
\boxed{
F_D(P,I)
\le \frac{T_D(P,I)^2}{P}+P^{o(1)}T_D(P,I),
}
\tag{HD-PC2}

where

\[
a_{p,m}=\mathbf1_{d_p(m)>D}\mathbf1_{b_{m\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p},
\quad
K_D(m)=\sum_{P<p\le2P}a_{p,m},

\[
T_D=\sum_{m\in I}K_D(m),

F_D=\sum_{m\in I}K_D(m)(K_D(m)-1).

hence logarithmic prime mass \(o(P)\) in the block. So **HD-PC2 is already enough for the desired pointwise conclusion after the proved small-\(d\) disposal**.

# 2. What the local passport actually knows

Fix \(p\ge5\) and a fundamental zero \(1\le r\le p-2\). The proved local package describes one rational map

\[
H_{p,r}:\mathbf P^1\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1
\]

in characteristic \(p\). It gives very strong information **conditioned on the event \(b_r=0\)**:

1. the complete ramification multiplicities of \(H_{p,r}\);
2. tameness;
3. restrictions on functional decomposition \(H=F\circ G\);
4. a large collection of cubic critical points if a nontrivial decomposition exists;
5. collapse of the ordinary scalar critical resultant to a power of \(b_r\).

All of these are one-prime implications of the form

\[
(p,r)\text{ is a zero}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal P(p,r),
\]

where \(\mathcal P(p,r)\) is a geometric property inside characteristic \(p\).

CHLS asks for something logically different. It asks, uniformly for a *moving integer \(m\)*,

\[
\#\{P<p\le2P:\ d_p(m)>D,\ b_{m\bmod p}=0\}
\]

to be small in a spectral sense. No local theorem compares the selected points

\[
(p,m\bmod p)
\]

for two different primes \(p\ne q\).

The scalar resultant is especially revealing. Once \(b_r=0\), the identity

\[
\operatorname{Res}(\text{critical equations})=\pm b_r^{\deg B_p}
\]

only says that the resultant vanishes. It does not attach a nonzero integer of controlled height whose distinct prime divisors could be counted. Hence it has no mechanism for distinguishing “this zero occurs at one prime” from “the same moving anchor occurs at every prime in a dyadic block.”

# 3. Algebraic anchored-star countermodel

## 3.1 A zero-linear-coefficient square model

Write

\[
\Delta(T)=T^2-34T+1,
\qquad
n_p=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon_p}{2}.
\]

For \(p\) sufficiently large, I claim that there exists a set

\[
S_p\subset\mathbf F_p^\times,
\qquad |S_p|=n_p,
\]

such that

\[
\sum_{\lambda\in S_p}\lambda=17\varepsilon_p
\tag{3.1}
\]

and such that no root \(-\lambda^{-1}\) of

\[
B_p^*(T):=\prod_{\lambda\in S_p}(1+\lambda T)
\tag{3.2}
\]

lies on \(\Delta=0\).

The forbidden values of \(\lambda\) are exactly the roots in \(\mathbf F_p\) of

\[
\lambda^2+34\lambda+1=0,
\]

so there are at most two.

### Existence of \(S_p\)

Partition \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) into sign-pairs \(\{u,-u\}\). Discard the at most two pairs meeting the forbidden set.

- If the required cardinality has even parity and the target sum is \(0\), take the needed number of clean sign-pairs.
- If the cardinality is odd and the target is \(0\), first take a three-element seed
  \[
  \{t,2t,-3t\},
  \]
  choosing \(t\) so that the three elements and their sign-pairs avoid the finite forbidden set; then fill with clean sign-pairs.
- If the target is \(17\) and the cardinality is even, choose a seed
  \[
  \{x,17-x\}
  \]
  with \(x\) outside the finitely many values that make either seed zero, forbidden, equal, or in the same sign-pair. Then fill with sign-pairs.
- If the target is \(17\) and the cardinality is odd, choose three distinct allowed elements \(x,y,17-x-y\), again avoiding only finitely many bad choices, and fill with sign-pairs.

Since only \(O(1)\) sign-pairs are lost and the number required for the fill is \(\sim p/4\), this succeeds for all sufficiently large \(p\). This is an elementary finite-exclusion argument; no distribution theorem is being used.

Then \(B_p^*\) is squarefree, \(B_p^*(0)=1\), and

\[
\gcd(B_p^*,T\Delta)=1.
\]

Its linear coefficient is \(17\varepsilon_p\). Therefore, setting

\[
A_p^*(T)=\Delta(T)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^*(T)^2,
\]

we have

\[
[T]A_p^*=
\begin{cases}
2[T]B_p^*=0,&\varepsilon_p=0,\\
2[T]B_p^*-34=34-34=0,&\varepsilon_p=1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Also

\[
\deg A_p^*=2n_p+2\varepsilon_p=p-1.
\]

Thus \(r=1\) is a coefficient zero in every characteristic in the synthetic family.

## 3.2 The associated cover has the exact passport

Write

\[
A_p^*(T)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}a_{p,m}^*T^m,
\qquad a_{p,1}^*=0.
\]

Define

\[
H_p^*(T)
:=T^{-1}\sum_{m\ne1}\frac{a_{p,m}^*}{m-1}T^m.
\]

Since \(m-1\ne0\pmod p\) for \(0\le m\le p-1\), \(m\ne1\), this is well-defined, and

\[
(H_p^*)'(T)=T^{-2}A_p^*(T).
\tag{3.4}
\]

The constant coefficient of \(A_p^*\) is \(1\), so \(H_p^*\) has a simple pole at \(0\). The top term of \(A_p^*\) is nonzero, so \(H_p^*\) has pole order \(p-2\) at infinity. Therefore

\[
\deg H_p^*=1+(p-2)=p-1.
\]

At a simple root \(eta\) of \(B_p^*\), the derivative has a zero of order \(2\). Hence the ramification index is exactly \(3\). If \(arepsilon_p=1\), the two distinct roots of \(\Delta\) give simple zeros of the derivative, hence ramification index \(2\).

At infinity the ramification index is \(p-2\), and the pole at \(0\) is unramified. All these indices are prime to \(p\), so the cover is tame.

Finally, Riemann--Hurwitz exhausts the ramification:

\[
2\deg B_p^*+2\varepsilon_p+(p-3)
=(p-1-2\varepsilon_p)+2\varepsilon_p+p-3
=2p-4
=2\deg(H_p^*)-2.
\]

Therefore there are no additional critical points. This is exactly the stated cubic/conifold/two-pole passport.

## 3.3 It is actually indecomposable

There is an elementary proof that a degree-\(n\) rational map with exactly two poles of orders \(1\) and \(n-1\) is indecomposable.

Suppose

\[
H=F\circ G,
\qquad \deg F=a,\quad \deg G=b,\quad ab=n,
\]

and let \(x_0\) be the simple pole of \(H\). Put \(y_0=G(x_0)\). Since \(y_0\) is a pole of \(F\),

\[
1=\operatorname{ord}_{x_0}(H^{-1}(\infty))
=e_G(x_0)\,\operatorname{ord}_{y_0}(F^{-1}(\infty)).
\]

Both factors equal \(1\).

The whole fiber \(G^{-1}(y_0)\) consists of poles of \(H\). Since \(H\) has only two poles, either:

- the fiber contains only \(x_0\), forcing \(b=1\); or
- it also contains the other pole \(x_\infty\). Then the pole order \(n-1\) at \(x_\infty\) equals \(e_G(x_\infty)\), and the total degree of the fiber is
  \[
  b=e_G(x_0)+e_G(x_\infty)=1+(n-1)=n,
  \]
  forcing \(a=1\).

So every decomposition is trivial.

For \(r=1\), this is stronger than the proved Apéry restriction that the outer degree divide

\[
\gcd(1,p-1)=1.
\]

Thus the anchored model does not exploit a decomposable exceptional cover. It lives in the **maximally rigid local case**.

in the synthetic family,

for every row prime \(p\in(P,2P]\).

\|M^*\|_{2\to2}^2

More generally, the local package is compatible with a norm lower bound of order \(P/\log P\) **independent of \(D\)** throughout the full range \(D<P-1\). Therefore no function of \(D\) tending to infinity can appear as a forced saving based only on those local outputs.

# 5. Scope: why this is not an Apéry counterexample

The distinction is important.

The synthetic model preserves:

- the \(\Delta^{\varepsilon}B^2\) factorization shape;
- the correct degree;
- squarefreeness of \(B\);
- coprimality with \(T\Delta\);
- the exact derivative form \(H'=T^{-r-1}A\) for \(r=1\);
- the entire tame passport;
- and even stronger indecomposability than the stated decomposition theorem requires.

It does **not** preserve:

1. the distinguished Apéry second-order ODE for \(B_p\);
2. the fact that all the \(A_p\) arise by reduction of one fixed integral sequence \(b_n\);
3. Apéry--Lucas compatibility across all base-\(p\) digits;
4. any yet-unproved cross-characteristic compatibility of the selected Mellin coordinates.

This is exactly why it proves the desired logical statement and no more.

If one insists on the exact normalized Apéry ODE, the freedom to choose \(B_p^*\) disappears: one is back to the actual local Apéry object. Therefore the remaining question is not “can a passport theorem be sharpened?” but rather:

> **Can the exact Apéry ODE/integral crystal be used to compare the distinguished Mellin-coordinate cancellation events in two different defining characteristics?**

That is a genuinely new cross-prime theorem.

The current repository already records the analogous fixed-anchor quantifier obstruction in `oracleB_result.tex`: for each *fixed* integer \(c\), only finitely many primes can satisfy \(b_{c\bmod p}=0\), because they divide a fixed Apéry integer. That theorem has quantifiers

\[
\forall c\ \exists C_c\ \forall X,
\]

whereas an anchored star chooses \(c=c(P)\) after the dyadic scale. The algebraic model above isolates the same failure at the level of local covers.

# 6. A cross-prime input strictly weaker than CHLS

Full CHLS controls

\[
\sup_{v\ne0}\frac{\|Mv\|_2^2}{\|v\|_2^2}.
\]

This is far more than the all-index radical argument needs.

For \(P<p\le2P\), \(m\in I\), define

\[
a_{p,m}^{(D)}
=\mathbf1_{d_p(m)>D}\mathbf1_{b_{m\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p},
\]

\[
K_D(m)=\sum_{P<p\le2P}a_{p,m}^{(D)},
\]

and

\[
T_D=\sum_{m\in I}K_D(m).
\]

Let

\[
F_D
=\sum_{m\in I}K_D(m)(K_D(m)-1)
=\sum_{\substack{P<p,q\le2P\\p\ne q}}
C_{p,q}^{(D)}(I),
\]

where

\[
C_{p,q}^{(D)}(I)
=\#\{m\in I:
 d_p(m),d_q(m)>D,
 b_{m\bmod p}\equiv b_{m\bmod q}\equiv0\}.
\]

## Proposed minimum input: one-sided high-\(d\) pair correlation

It is enough to prove, uniformly for the relevant \(P,I,D\),

\[
\boxed{
F_D\le\frac{T_D^2}{P}+P^{o(1)}T_D.
}
\tag{HD-PC2}
\]

No lower correlation bound is needed. No absolute value is needed. No pairwise estimate is needed. No control of arbitrary vectors is needed.

This is strictly weaker than CHLS: it controls only the row-all-ones direction \(M^*\mathbf1\), equivalently one scalar second factorial moment.

## 6.1 Proof that HD-PC2 is enough

Because \(I\) has length \(P\) and every row prime satisfies \(p>P\), reduction \(m\mapsto m\bmod p\) is injective on \(I\). Hence the number of incidences in row \(p\) is at most \(Z(p)\). The proved zero count gives

\[
Z(p)\ll p^{2/3},
\]

so

\[
T_D\le\sum_{P<p\le2P}Z(p)
\ll \frac{P}{\log P}P^{2/3}
=\frac{P^{5/3}}{\log P}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The exact variance identity is

\[
\sum_{m\in I}\left(K_D(m)-\frac{T_D}{P}\right)^2
=T_D+F_D-\frac{T_D^2}{P}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Under HD-PC2,

\[
\sum_{m\in I}\left(K_D(m)-\frac{T_D}{P}\right)^2
\le P^{o(1)}T_D.
\]

Therefore, pointwise,

\[
K_D(m)
\le \frac{T_D}{P}+P^{o(1)}\sqrt{T_D}.
\]

Using (6.1),

\[
K_D(m)
\ll \frac{P^{2/3}}{\log P}
+P^{5/6+o(1)}
=P^{5/6+o(1)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the logarithmic mass of the high-\(d\) primes in this block is

\[
\sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\a_{p,m}^{(D)}=1}}\log p
\le K_D(m)\log(2P)
=P^{5/6+o(1)}=o(P).
\]

Combined over dyadic blocks with the already proved small-\(d\) disposal, this is more than enough for the all-index \(o(n)\) radical target.

So the minimum next theorem need not resemble an operator-norm theorem at all.

# 7. An exact trace/correlation formulation

Put

\[
R_{p,D}(I)=\{m\in I:d_p(m)>D,\ b_{m\bmod p}=0\},
\qquad R_p=|R_{p,D}(I)|.
\]

A still more natural sufficient statement is the one-sided centered aggregate

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{P<p,q\le2P\\p\ne q}}
\left(
|R_{p,D}(I)\cap R_{q,D}(I)|
-\frac{R_pR_q}{P}
\right)
\le P^{o(1)}T_D.
}
\tag{AHD2}
\]

Since

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}\frac{R_pR_q}{P}
\le\frac{T_D^2}{P},
\]

AHD2 implies HD-PC2.

This is the cleanest minimum genuinely cross-prime Apéry input I see. It asks only that the *aggregate excess* of two-characteristic intersections over the independent row-density baseline be at shot-noise scale. Negative correlations are free; large positive correlations only need to cancel in aggregate to the extent expressed by this one-sided inequality.

There is also an exact additive-character expansion. For each prime,

\[
\mathbf1_{b_{m\bmod p}=0}
=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
 e_p\!\left(u b_{m\bmod p}\right).
\]

Hence, for \(p\ne q\),

\[
C_{p,q}^{(D)}(I)
=\frac1{pq}
\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
\sum_{v\in\mathbf F_q}
\sum_{m\in I}
 w_{p,D}(m)w_{q,D}(m)
 e_p(u b_{m\bmod p})
 e_q(v b_{m\bmod q}),
\tag{7.1}
\]

where \(w_{p,D}(m)=\mathbf1_{d_p(m)>D}\).

Thus AHD2 is an exact **two-defining-characteristic mixed trace/exponential-correlation inequality**. This is the correct place where a modular/crystalline parametrization would have to enter. It is substantially weaker than estimating every individual \(p,q,u,v\) sum or proving a spectral large sieve.

The project’s existing `oracleB_result.tex` has the same logical object without the high-\(d\) cutoff: its marked two-characteristic Hasse dispersion (MH2) is precisely a centered second-factorial-moment statement. AHD2 is the Q7520-adapted, one-sided version.

## 8.1 Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *Algebraic trace functions over the primes*

\[
\sum_{q\ {m prime}}K(q)V(q/X)
\ll QX(1+p/X)^{1/6}p^{-\eta},
\]

and the unsmoothed analogue has the corresponding \(1/12\) exponent and \(p^{-\eta/2}\). The constants depend on \(\eta\), the test function bounds, and polynomially on \(\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal F)\). ### Why it does not apply

For us there is no proved representation

\[
m\longmapsto\mathbf1_{b_{m\bmod p}=0}

as a bounded-conductor isotypic trace weight. More fundamentally, the event is a coefficient/Mellin zero, not an evaluation trace of a fixed sheaf.

Moreover Theorem 1.5 treats a sum over prime **arguments \(q\)** for one modulus \(p\). AHD2 needs simultaneous correlation between two changing defining characteristics \(p\) and \(q\) at the same integer \(m\). There is no formal substitution that turns one problem into the other.

## 8.2 Perret-Gentil, *Exponential sums over finite fields and the large sieve*

Primary source: C. Perret-Gentil, **Exponential Sums Over Finite Fields and the Large Sieve**, *International Mathematics Research Notices* 2020, no. 20, 7139--7180, [arXiv:1703.06965](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06965).

The Apéry problem varies the defining characteristic itself. No coherent compatible system has been constructed whose Frobenius conjugacy class has a marked coordinate vanishing exactly when

b_j\equiv0\pmod p.

Even the project’s proposed crystalline Mellin object

is currently a specification, not an established coherent system with the integral monodromy and marked-coordinate control required by the theorem. The event also depends on a moving character \(j\), and AHD2 correlates two different defining characteristics. The hypotheses of the published large sieve simply do not match.

# 9. Hurwitz-space monodromy does not supply the missing step

Primary source: D. P. Roberts, A. Venkatesh, **Hurwitz monodromy and full number fields**, *Algebra & Number Theory* 9 (2015), 511--545, DOI [`10.2140/ant.2015.9.511`](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2015.9.511), [arXiv:1401.7379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.7379).

Their setup begins with a Hurwitz parameter

\[
h=(G,C,\nu),
\]

where \(G\) is a finite group, \(C\) is a fixed list of generating conjugacy classes, and \(
u\) records their branch multiplicities. This produces a cover

\[
\mathrm{Hur}_h\longrightarrow\mathrm{Conf}_\nu.
\]

For fixed nonabelian simple \(G\) and fixed \(C\), when the multiplicities \(

### Why it does not apply

The local Apéry passport supplies, for each \(p,r\), **one distinguished cover** in characteristic \(p\). As \(p\) changes:

- the degree \(p-1\) changes;
- the number of cubic branch points is \(\Theta(p)\);
- the Hurwitz parameter is not fixed;
- no theorem identifies the Apéry covers as generic points of a single finite-type Hurwitz family;
- and, even if the geometric monodromy of an ambient Hurwitz space were full, that would describe generic motion as branch points vary, not the arithmetic distribution of one distinguished Apéry point selected in each characteristic.

Most importantly, the event to control is still

\[
[T^r]A_p=0,
\]

not membership of the cover in a conjugacy class of Hurwitz monodromy. Full Hurwitz monodromy therefore does not imply coefficient anti-concentration.

The algebraic star model above demonstrates the logical issue concretely: covers with the same local passport can be chosen so that the distinguished coefficient zero is anchored for every \(p\).

# 11. What a successful modular/crystalline attack would have to add

A real new attack would need to use the exact Apéry ODE not merely to classify each \(B_p\), but to build a **common arithmetic object across characteristics** with a marked coordinate whose vanishing is exactly \(b_j=0\).

The minimum useful output is not Sato--Tate and not full monodromy. It is AHD2, or equivalently a bound on the aggregate nonzero part of (7.1).

Concretely, a viable theorem could have the following form.

> **Marked two-characteristic Apéry correlation lemma.**  There is a realization of the normalized Mellin coefficient \(b_j\bmod p\) as a distinguished integral/crystalline coordinate \(c_{p,j}\) of a fixed-rank arithmetic object, and for every dyadic block \(P<p,q\le2P\), every interval \(I\) of length \(P\), and the deterministic high-\(d\) weights,
> \[
> \sum_{p\ne q}
> \left(
> \#\{m\in I:c_{p,m\bmod p}=c_{q,m\bmod q}=0\}
> -\frac{R_pR_q}{P}
> \right)
> \le P^{o(1)}T_D.
> \]

That would be enough. It does **not** require:

- individual square-root cancellation for every pair \(p,q\);
- equidistribution of all Frobenius conjugacy classes;
- a full CHLS operator norm;
- pairwise independence;
- or control of every Fourier mode.

It is only one aggregate one-sided second-factorial-moment inequality.

No verified published theorem above provides this statement, because none has simultaneously:

1. changing defining characteristic;
2. the moving Mellin character/index \(j=m\bmod p\);
3. a marked *integral* coordinate detecting the defining-characteristic zero event;
4. uniform bounded complexity/monodromy adequate for a large sieve;
5. and two-characteristic correlation at the same moving integer \(m\).

# 12. Tactical conclusion

The local cover program has reached a genuine terminal point for CHLS unless it is coupled to a new horizontal arithmetic theorem.

The strongest rigorous conclusions are:

1. **Passport rigidity gives no \(D\)-saving.** The explicit algebraic star model has the same factorization shape, squarefree/coprime \(B_p\), exact tame passport and even absolute indecomposability, yet \(\|M\|^2\gg P/\log P\) for all \(D<P-1\).
2. **This is not a counterexample to Apéry.** The synthetic family deliberately drops the exact Apéry ODE/global integral compatibility. Those are precisely the only remaining places where extra cross-prime information could live.
3. **Full CHLS is unnecessary.** The one-sided high-\(d\) pair-correlation inequality HD-PC2/AHD2 already gives \(K_D(m)\le P^{5/6+o(1)}\) from the proved \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\), hence \(o(P)\) logarithmic mass per block.
4. **Existing horizontal trace-function large sieves do not apply.** Their exact hypotheses require bounded-conductor trace weights/coherent systems and fixed defining characteristic or fixed-modulus geometry; the Apéry coefficient-zero event has not been placed in such a framework.
5. **Hurwitz full monodromy does not apply.** A passport identifies a stratum, not distribution of the distinguished Apéry point across primes.
6. **The published Apéry defining-characteristic theorem is local.** CFVZ proves the Kummer Galois group/factorization, not coefficient-zero dispersion.

So the next theorem worth trying to prove is not a stronger local decomposition theorem. It is **AHD2**, ideally by constructing the marked crystalline Mellin coordinate already anticipated in `oracleB_result.tex` and proving only its aggregate two-characteristic excess-correlation bound.
