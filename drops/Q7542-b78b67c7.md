# Verdict

**The proved local passport/decomposition rigidity implies no nontrivial \(D\)-saving in the CHLS operator norm by itself.** This is a logical no-implication result, not a counterexample to the actual Apéry family.

There is an explicit algebraic anchored-star model. For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), with the same local choice \(ε_p\in\{0,1\}\), one can choose a squarefree \(B_p^*\) coprime to \(T\Delta\) such that

\[
A_p^*(T)=\Delta(T)^{ε_p}B_p^*(T)^2,
\qquad [T]A_p^*(T)=0.
\]

For the zero index \(r=1\), its primitive

\[
H_{p,1}^*(T)=T^{-1}\sum_{m\ne1}\frac{[T^m]A_p^*}{m-1}T^m
\]

has **exactly** the stated tame passport: degree \(p-1\), pole indices \(1,p-2\), cubic ramification at every root of \(B_p^*\), and two index-\(2\) conifold points when \(ε_p=1\). Riemann--Hurwitz shows there is no other ramification. In fact these maps are indecomposable for an elementary two-pole reason, so the model lies in the maximally rigid local case.

Nevertheless, if an interval \(I\) of \(P\) consecutive integers contains \(m=1\), then every prime \(P<p\le2P\) hits that column and

\[
d_p(1)=p-1>D
\]

for every \(D<P-1\). Hence

\[
\|M^*\|_{2\to2}^2\ge \pi(2P)-\pi(P)\sim\frac{P}{\log P}.
\]

Taking \(D=P^\delta\), \(0<\delta<1\), makes

\[
\frac{P}{D^\eta\log P}+P^{1-\eta}=o(P/\log P),
\]

so no positive \(D\)-saving can be deduced formally from the local passport/decomposition outputs.

The model deliberately does **not** satisfy the distinguished Apéry second-order ODE. That is the exact scope boundary: the normalized Apéry ODE selects the actual \(B_p\), so a theorem exploiting the ODE may still exist, but it would have to extract a new compatibility across changing defining characteristics. Such a compatibility is not contained in the passport.

A much weaker input than CHLS already suffices. Let

\[
a_{p,m}^{(D)}=\mathbf1_{d_p(m)>D}\mathbf1_{b_{m\bmod p}=0},
\qquad
K_D(m)=\sum_{P<p\le2P}a_{p,m}^{(D)},
\]

\[
T_D=\sum_{m\in I}K_D(m),
\qquad
F_D=\sum_{m\in I}K_D(m)(K_D(m)-1).
\]

It is enough to prove the one-sided scalar pair-correlation bound

\[
\boxed{
F_D\le \frac{T_D^2}{P}+P^{o(1)}T_D.
}
\tag{HD-PC2}
\]

Using the already proved \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\), this yields

\[
\max_{m\in I}K_D(m)\le P^{5/6+o(1)},
\]

and therefore \(o(P)\) logarithmic prime mass in the block. HD-PC2 controls only one aggregate second factorial moment; it is strictly weaker than a \(2\to2\) operator-norm estimate.

I found no published horizontal trace-function large sieve, Hurwitz-space monodromy theorem, or defining-characteristic Apéry result whose verified hypotheses imply HD-PC2. The closest results fail at explicit hypotheses, detailed below.

# 2. Why the local theorem has the wrong logical shape

For a zero pair \((p,r)\), the local theorem says

\[
b_r=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal P(p,r),
\]

where \(\mathcal P(p,r)\) is an extremely rigid geometric passport/decomposition statement in characteristic \(p\).

CHLS asks for control of

\[
\#\{P<p\le2P:\ d_p(m)>D,\ b_{m\bmod p}=0\}
\]

uniformly in a moving integer \(m\). This needs information coupling \((p,m\bmod p)\) and \((q,m\bmod q)\) for \(p\ne q\). Nothing in a prime-by-prime passport compares these points.

The ordinary critical resultant makes this especially transparent. When \(b_r=0\),

\[
\operatorname{Res}(\text{all critical equations})=\pm b_r^{\deg B_p}=0.
\]

A zero scalar resultant contains no height or support information that could limit how many different primes share the same moving anchor.

# 3. Explicit algebraic anchored-star model

Put

\[
\Delta(T)=T^2-34T+1,
\qquad
n_p=\frac{p-1-2ε_p}{2}.
\]

We construct \(S_p\subset\mathbf F_p^\times\), \(|S_p|=n_p\), satisfying

\[
\sum_{\lambda\in S_p}\lambda=17ε_p,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and avoiding the at most two roots of

\[
\lambda^2+34\lambda+1=0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then define

\[
B_p^*(T)=\prod_{\lambda\in S_p}(1+\lambda T).
\tag{3.3}
\]

## Existence of \(S_p\)

Partition \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) into sign-pairs \(\{u,-u\}\), and discard any pair meeting the two-element forbidden set from (3.2).

- Target sum \(0\), even cardinality: use clean sign-pairs only.
- Target sum \(0\), odd cardinality: start with a seed \(\{t,2t,-3t\}\), choosing \(t\) outside finitely many forbidden values, then fill with sign-pairs.
- Target sum \(17\), even cardinality: start with \(\{x,17-x\}\), with \(x\) avoiding the finitely many zero/equality/forbidden cases, then fill with sign-pairs.
- Target sum \(17\), odd cardinality: use three distinct allowed elements \(x,y,17-x-y\), then fill with sign-pairs.

Only \(O(1)\) sign-pairs are excluded, whereas the fill needs about \(p/4\) of the roughly \(p/2\) available sign-pairs. Thus the construction works for all sufficiently large \(p\).

By construction \(B_p^*\) is squarefree, \(B_p^*(0)=1\), and

\[
\gcd(B_p^*,T\Delta)=1.
\]

Its linear coefficient is \(17ε_p\). Therefore

\[
A_p^*=\Delta^{ε_p}(B_p^*)^2
\]

has degree \(p-1\), constant coefficient \(1\), and

\[
[T]A_p^*=0:
\]

for \(ε_p=0\) the coefficient is \(2[T]B_p^*=0\), while for \(ε_p=1\) it is \(2\cdot17-34=0\).

## Passport verification

Write

\[
A_p^*=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}a_{p,m}^*T^m,
\qquad a_{p,1}^*=0,
\]

and set

\[
H_p^*(T)=T^{-1}\sum_{m\ne1}\frac{a_{p,m}^*}{m-1}T^m.
\]

All denominators are nonzero mod \(p\), and

\[
(H_p^*)'=T^{-2}A_p^*.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Since \(a_{p,0}^*=1\), \(H_p^*\) has a simple pole at \(0\). Since \(\deg A_p^*=p-1\), it has pole order \(p-2\) at infinity. Thus

\[
\deg H_p^*=p-1.
\]

Every simple root of \(B_p^*\) is a double zero of the derivative, hence a ramification point of index \(3\). If \(ε_p=1\), the two simple roots of \(\Delta\) are simple zeros of the derivative, hence index-\(2\) points. Infinity has index \(p-2\). All indices are prime to \(p\).

The Riemann--Hurwitz contribution is

\[
2\deg B_p^*+2ε_p+(p-3)
=(p-1-2ε_p)+2ε_p+p-3
=2p-4,
\]

which equals \(2(p-1)-2\). Therefore there is no further ramification.

## Indecomposability is automatic

Let a degree-\(n\) rational map have exactly two poles with orders \(1\) and \(n-1\). Suppose \(H=F\circ G\), with \(\deg F=a\), \(\deg G=b\), \(ab=n\).

At the simple pole \(x_0\), the local pole order factors as

\[
1=e_G(x_0)e_F(G(x_0)),
\]

so both indices are \(1\). Every point of the fiber \(G^{-1}(G(x_0))\) is a pole of \(H\). If the other pole is not in that fiber, then \(b=1\). If it is, its local multiplicity is \(n-1\), so

\[
b=1+(n-1)=n,
\]

and \(a=1\). Hence every decomposition is trivial.

For \(n=p-1\), the synthetic \(H_p^*\) is therefore indecomposable. This is stronger than the stated \(r=1\) conclusion “outer degree divides \(\gcd(1,p-1)=1\).”

# 4. Operator-norm obstruction

Let \(I\) be any interval of \(P\) consecutive integers containing \(1\). In the synthetic family every \(P<p\le2P\) has the coefficient zero at \(r=1\), and

\[
d_p(1)=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(1,p-1)}=p-1.
\]

For \(D<P-1\), the \(m=1\) column is all ones. Therefore

\[
\|M^*\|_{2\to2}^2
\ge \|M^*e_1\|_2^2
=\pi(2P)-\pi(P)
\sim\frac{P}{\log P}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For \(D=P^\delta\), \(0<\delta<1\), and every fixed \(\eta>0\),

\[
\frac{P}{D^\eta\log P}+P^{1-\eta}
=o(P/\log P).
\]

Thus the local passport/decomposition package is compatible with a full anchored star even in the largest-\(d\) class. It gives **zero formal \(D\)-saving**.

# 5. Precise scope relative to the actual Apéry family

This is not an Apéry counterexample. The construction preserves the local **outputs** under audit:

- \(\Delta^{ε}B^2\) shape and correct degree;
- squarefree \(B\), coprime to \(T\Delta\);
- derivative primitive \(H'=T^{-r-1}A\) at \(r=1\);
- full tame passport;
- and stronger-than-required indecomposability.

It does not preserve:

1. the distinguished Apéry second-order ODE for \(B_p\);
2. reduction from a single integer sequence \(b_n\);
3. the full Lucas digit law;
4. any cross-characteristic crystalline compatibility not yet proved.

Requiring the exact normalized Apéry ODE removes the synthetic freedom. Therefore this audit does **not** say “the ODE is useless.” It says that any successful use of it must prove a new theorem coupling its solutions as \(p\) varies. That theorem is exactly the cross-prime input missing from the passport.

# 6. Minimum cross-prime input: one scalar second factorial moment

For \(P<p\le2P\) and \(m\in I\), define

\[
a_{p,m}^{(D)}
=\mathbf1_{d_p(m)>D}\mathbf1_{b_{m\bmod p}=0},
\]

\[
K_D(m)=\sum_p a_{p,m}^{(D)},
\qquad
T_D=\sum_mK_D(m),
\]

\[
F_D=\sum_mK_D(m)(K_D(m)-1).
\]

The proposed minimum is the **one-sided** estimate

\[
\boxed{
F_D\le \frac{T_D^2}{P}+P^{o(1)}T_D.
}
\tag{HD-PC2}
\]

This is much weaker than CHLS. CHLS bounds \(M\) on every vector. HD-PC2 controls only the aggregate row-pair overlap, equivalently the all-ones row direction.

## Consequence from the already proved \(Z(p)\) bound

Because \(I\) has length \(P<p\), the map \(m\mapsto m\bmod p\) is injective on \(I\). Thus row \(p\) contains at most \(Z(p)\) hits. Since \(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\),

\[
T_D\ll\sum_{P<p\le2P}p^{2/3}
\ll\frac{P^{5/3}}{\log P}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Exactly,

\[
\sum_{m\in I}\left(K_D(m)-\frac{T_D}{P}\right)^2
=T_D+F_D-\frac{T_D^2}{P}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

HD-PC2 therefore gives

\[
\sum_m\left(K_D(m)-\frac{T_D}{P}\right)^2
\le P^{o(1)}T_D.
\]

Hence

\[
K_D(m)
\le\frac{T_D}{P}+P^{o(1)}\sqrt{T_D}
\le P^{5/6+o(1)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The logarithmic mass in the block is at most

\[
K_D(m)\log(2P)=P^{5/6+o(1)}=o(P).
\]

Together with the proved small-\(d\) contribution, this is enough for the all-index radical argument.

# 7. Even more intrinsic cross-prime form

Let

\[
R_{p,D}(I)=\{m\in I:d_p(m)>D,\ b_{m\bmod p}=0\},
\qquad R_p=|R_{p,D}(I)|,
\]

and \(T_D=\sum_pR_p\). A natural sufficient form is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(
|R_{p,D}(I)\cap R_{q,D}(I)|
-\frac{R_pR_q}{P}
\right)
\le P^{o(1)}T_D.
}
\tag{AHD2}
\]

Since

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}\frac{R_pR_q}{P}\le\frac{T_D^2}{P},
\]

AHD2 implies HD-PC2. Notice how weak it is: no absolute value, no lower bound, no individual \(p,q\) estimate, and no spectral norm.

There is an exact additive-character expansion

\[
\mathbf1_{b_{m\bmod p}=0}
=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(u b_{m\bmod p}),
\]

so for \(p\ne q\), with \(w_{p,D}(m)=\mathbf1_{d_p(m)>D}\),

\[
|R_{p,D}(I)\cap R_{q,D}(I)|
=
\frac1{pq}\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}\sum_{v\in\mathbf F_q}
\sum_{m\in I}
 w_{p,D}(m)w_{q,D}(m)
 e_p(u b_{m\bmod p})e_q(v b_{m\bmod q}).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus AHD2 is an exact aggregate **two-defining-characteristic trace/correlation inequality**. This is the right target for a modular/crystalline attack.

It is also the high-\(d\), one-sided analogue of the marked two-characteristic Hasse dispersion already isolated in `oracleB_result.tex`.

# 8. Published horizontal trace-function theorems: hypothesis audit

## 8.1 Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel

Primary citation: É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel, **Algebraic trace functions over the primes**, *Duke Mathematical Journal* 163 (2014), 1683--1736, DOI [10.1215/00127094-2690587](https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2690587), [arXiv:1211.6043](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6043).

Their Theorem 1.5 assumes that \(K\) is an **isotypic trace weight on \(\mathbf F_p\)** associated with a nonexceptional \(\ell\)-adic sheaf \(\mathcal F\). For a smooth \(V\) with derivative parameter \(Q\), and any \(\eta<1/24\), they prove

\[
\sum_{q\text{ prime}}K(q)V(q/X)
\ll QX(1+p/X)^{1/6}p^{-\eta},
\]

with constants depending on \(\eta\), the test-function norms, and polynomially on \(\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal F)\). Their Remark 1.6 explicitly points out that the useful range assumes bounded conductor.

**Mismatch.** No theorem identifies

\[
m\mapsto\mathbf1_{b_{m\bmod p}=0}
\]

as a bounded-conductor isotypic trace weight. The natural \(H_{p,r}\) has \(\Theta(p)\) cubic critical points, so the obvious cover-based sheaf has growing conductor. More fundamentally, the event is a Mellin **coefficient zero**, not evaluation of a fixed trace function. And Theorem 1.5 sums prime arguments for one modulus \(p\); AHD2 correlates two changing defining characteristics.

Therefore FKM does not apply.

## 8.2 Perret-Gentil large sieve for Frobenius

Primary citation: C. Perret-Gentil, **Exponential Sums Over Finite Fields and the Large Sieve**, *International Mathematics Research Notices* 2020, no. 20, 7139--7180, [arXiv:1703.06965](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06965).

Definition 2.1 requires a coherent family \((\mathcal F_\lambda)_\lambda\) with:

1. a compatible system whose Frobenius characteristic polynomials lie in one number field and are independent of the auxiliary valuation \(\lambda\);
2. arithmetic and geometric monodromy equal to one fixed classical group \(G(\mathbf F_\lambda)\);
3. uniformly bounded conductor.

The large-sieve theorem then sieves Frobenius classes against conjugacy-invariant subsets \(\Omega_\lambda\subset G(\mathbf F_\lambda)\). The introduction explicitly notes that when one only has Larsen--Pink control, constants depend on the defining characteristic \(p\), forcing one to **fix \(p\)** and let \(q=p^e\to\infty\).

**Mismatch.** Our defining characteristic itself varies. No coherent compatible system has been constructed with a marked integral coordinate \(c_{p,j}\) satisfying

\[
c_{p,j}=0\iff b_j=0\pmod p,
\]

uniformly in both \(p\) and the moving Mellin character \(j\). The project’s proposed crystalline Mellin object is a specification of exactly this missing package, not an object to which the theorem can already be applied.

Therefore the Frobenius large sieve does not apply.

## 8.3 Fixed-modulus Kloosterman bilinear forms

Primary citation: E. Kowalski, P. Michel, W. Sawin, **Bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums and applications**, *Annals of Mathematics* 186 (2017), 413--500, DOI [10.4007/annals.2017.186.2.2](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2017.186.2.2), [arXiv:1511.01636](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01636).

Their bilinear estimates concern hyper-Kloosterman trace functions modulo one fixed prime, with the Kloosterman sheaf supplying bounded-complexity geometric correlation estimates.

No bounded-complexity Kloosterman-type sheaf is known whose trace is the Apéry zero indicator. Hence these bounds cannot be substituted into (7.1).

# 9. Hurwitz-space monodromy: why the passport is insufficient

Primary citation: D. P. Roberts, A. Venkatesh, **Hurwitz monodromy and full number fields**, *Algebra & Number Theory* 9 (2015), 511--545, DOI [10.2140/ant.2015.9.511](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2015.9.511), [arXiv:1401.7379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.7379).

Their Hurwitz parameter is

\[
h=(G,C,\nu),
\]

with a finite group \(G\), a fixed list \(C\) of generating conjugacy classes, and branch multiplicities \(
u\). For fixed nonabelian simple \(G\) and fixed \(C\), sufficiently large multiplicities give full alternating/symmetric monodromy for the appropriate Hurwitz cover over configuration space.

**Mismatch.** The Apéry construction gives one distinguished cover for each \((p,r)\). As \(p\) varies, its degree \(p-1\) and number of cubic branch points both vary. No fixed Hurwitz parameter contains all these covers, and no theorem says the distinguished Apéry point is generic/equidistributed in a Hurwitz space. Full geometric monodromy of an ambient family would in any case control variation of branch configurations, not the coefficient condition

\[
[T^r]A_p=0.
\]

The anchored-star construction is a direct counterexample to any inference “same passport implies horizontal anti-concentration.”

# 10. Strongest published defining-characteristic Apéry result

Primary citation: X. Caruso, F. Fürnsinn, D. Vargas-Montoya, W. Zudilin, **Galois Groups of Apéry-like Series Modulo Primes**, *Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society* (2026), DOI [10.1017/S0004972725100932](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972725100932), [arXiv:2510.23298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23298).

Their Theorem 1 determines

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf F_p(t,f_\alpha)/\mathbf F_p(t))
\]

as either the square subgroup of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) or all of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), according to \(p\bmod24\). Their Theorem 2 proves the factorization

\[
A_p=B_p^2
\]

in four classes mod \(24\), and

\[
A_p=(t^2-34t+1)B_p^2
\]

in the other four. The proof uses the Franel substitution

\[
t=\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x}.
\]

This is genuinely Apéry-specific and defining-characteristic. But it is still a theorem **inside one characteristic at a time**. It gives no distribution of coefficient zero indices and no correlation between two primes.

The new ODE/squarefree/passport/decomposition results strengthen that local description substantially, but they do not change this quantifier.

# 11. What a real modular/crystalline attack must prove

A useful modular parametrization must do more than identify the local cover or its monodromy. It must produce a common arithmetic object across characteristics with a distinguished integral coordinate whose vanishing is *exactly* the Mellin coefficient cancellation.

A sufficient theorem would be:

> **Marked two-characteristic Apéry correlation lemma.** There are marked coordinates \(c_{p,j}\) arising from one uniform arithmetic/crystalline construction such that \(c_{p,j}=0\iff b_j=0\pmod p\), and their zero events satisfy AHD2 uniformly for \(P<p,q\le2P\), intervals \(I\) of length \(P\), and the deterministic high-\(d\) cutoff.

This would not require full CHLS, pairwise square-root cancellation, Sato--Tate, or equidistribution of every Frobenius class. It asks only for one aggregate one-sided excess-correlation estimate.

No verified published theorem above supplies the needed combination of:

1. varying defining characteristic;
2. moving Mellin index \(j=m\bmod p\);
3. a marked integral coordinate detecting \(b_j=0\pmod p\);
4. uniform complexity/monodromy adequate for a large sieve;
5. two-characteristic correlation at the same moving integer \(m\).

# 12. Tactical conclusion

The local cover route has reached a real terminal boundary for CHLS unless it is coupled to a horizontal arithmetic theorem.

- **No \(D\)-saving follows from passport/decomposition rigidity.** The explicit algebraic star model retains the full local passport and absolute indecomposability while forcing \(\|M\|^2\asymp P/\log P\).
- **This does not refute the actual Apéry family.** The model drops the exact Apéry ODE/global integral compatibility; those are precisely where new cross-prime information would have to enter.
- **Full CHLS is unnecessary.** HD-PC2/AHD2, a one-sided scalar second-factorial-moment statement, already gives the power-saving \(K_D(m)\le P^{5/6+o(1)}\) using the proved \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\).
- **Published trace-function large sieves do not instantiate it.** Their bounded-conductor/coherent-system/fixed-characteristic hypotheses are not met.
- **Hurwitz full monodromy does not instantiate it.** Passport strata and generic branch-point monodromy do not control the distinguished Apéry coefficient zero.
- **The published Apéry defining-characteristic theorem remains local.** Its Galois/factorization conclusions do not contain two-prime coefficient-zero dispersion.

Therefore the sharp next target is **AHD2**, preferably by extracting from the exact Apéry ODE/modular parametrization the marked crystalline Mellin coordinate already anticipated in `oracleB_result.tex`, and proving only its aggregate two-characteristic excess-correlation bound.
