p\mid E_{n-1}
\iff p\mid g_{n-1}.
\]

The whole square `p^{2u}` cancels iff

\[
\boxed{p^{2u}\mid E_{n-1}.}
\tag{10.2}
\]

By (9.4)--(9.5), this means exactly:

1. `p^u|g_{n-1}`;
2. the normalized post-zero Wronskian lift
   \[
   b_{n-2}\frac{g_{n-1}}{p^u}
   \equiv
   \frac{b_{n-1}}{p^u}g_{n-2}
   \pmod{p^u}.
   \tag{10.3}
   \]

Thus a simple mod-`p` common zero can remove some denominator, but full square cancellation is a strictly stronger `p^{2u}` lift.

## 10.2 `p^t || a=b_(n-2)`

Put

\[
s=v_p(P_1).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
v_p(V_n)=\max(0,2t-2s-v_p(E_{n-2})).}
\tag{10.4}
\]

If `s>=t`, the gap-two coefficient already cancels the whole `a^2` part.  If `s<t`, full cancellation is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{p^{2(t-s)}\mid E_{n-2}.}
\tag{10.5}
\]

Let

\[
j=2(t-s)>0.
\]

If `j<=t` (equivalently `t<=2s`), (9.4) turns (10.5) into the ordinary jet-depth condition

\[
\boxed{p^j\mid g_{n-2}.}
\tag{10.6}
\]

If `j>t` (equivalently `t>2s`), one first needs `p^t|g_{n-2}` and then the genuine extra lift of depth

\[
j-t=t-2s:
\]

\[
\boxed{
 b_{n-3}\frac{g_{n-2}}{p^t}
 \equiv
 \frac{b_{n-2}}{p^t}g_{n-3}
 \pmod{p^{t-2s}}.
}
\tag{10.7}
\]

This separates the gap-two polynomial contribution from the new first-jet lift exactly.

# 12. Exact large-prime part of `V_n`

Define

\[
a_{>n}
:=\prod_{\substack{p>n\\p\mid a}}p^{v_p(a)},
\qquad
b_{>n}
:=\prod_{\substack{p>n\\p\mid b}}p^{v_p(b)}.
\]

By (7.3), their prime supports are disjoint.  Applying (10.1) and (10.4) prime by prime gives the exact factorization

\[
\boxed{
(V_n)_{>n}
=
\frac{a_{>n}^{\,2}}
     {\gcd(a_{>n}^{\,2},P_1^2E_{n-2})}
\cdot
\frac{b_{>n}^{\,2}}
     {\gcd(b_{>n}^{\,2},E_{n-1})}.
}
\tag{12.1}
\]

Here `(V_n)_{>n}` means the factor of `V_n` supported on primes greater than `n`.

This is the strongest clean characteristic-zero divisibility statement I can obtain from the stated tools.  It is better than an unspecified resultant because it identifies exactly which arithmetic factors would have to be bounded.

There is also an all-prime exclusive-support version.  Let

\[
a_{\mathrm{ex}}
=\prod_{\substack{p\mid a\\p\nmid b}}p^{v_p(a)},
\qquad
b_{\mathrm{ex}}
=\prod_{\substack{p\mid b\\p\nmid a}}p^{v_p(b)}.
\]

Then the factor of `V_n` on these exclusive supports is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\frac{a_{\mathrm{ex}}^{\,2}}
 {\gcd(a_{\mathrm{ex}}^{\,2},(n-2)P_1^2E_{n-2})}
\cdot
\frac{b_{\mathrm{ex}}^{\,2}}
 {\gcd(b_{\mathrm{ex}}^{\,2},(n-2)(n-1)^6E_{n-1})}.
}
\tag{12.2}
\]

The only part not compressed into explicit energy gcds is the shared adjacent-prime support governed by Section 6.3 and bounded only partially by the LCM Wronskian (8.3).

# 13. Why no exponential lower bound follows yet

The denominator before reduction has the correct exponential scale.  Standard Apéry asymptotics give

\[
\log b_m
=m\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log m).
\]

Let

\[
\lambda=\log(17+12\sqrt2)\approx3.52549.
\]

Since `a=b_(n-2)` and `b=b_(n-1)`,

\[
\boxed{
\log(a^2b^2)=4\lambda n+O(\log n)
\approx14.10198n+O(\log n).
}
\tag{13.1}
\]

To obtain

\[
V_n\ge e^{cn}
\]

for some `c>0`, one has to prove that at least `cn` of this logarithmic mass survives the exact cancellation formulas.

The current inputs do not do that, for two independent reasons.

## 13.1 No upper theorem for the smooth part of an individual Apéry number

Gessel--Lucas determines divisibility modulo `p` from base-`p` digits, and Delaygue-type results give important **lower** valuation bounds.  They do not give an all-prime upper bound of the form needed to show that

\[
\prod_{p\le n}p^{v_p(b_m)}
\]

has subexponential size for every `m`.  Thus the present theory does not force `a_{>n}b_{>n}` in (12.1) to have exponential logarithmic mass.

A generic integer of size `e^{\lambda n}` can of course be entirely `n`-smooth; one may not rule out that behavior for the particular Apéry values without an Apéry-specific theorem.  This is not a claim that Apéry numbers actually behave that way; it identifies the missing theorem.

## 13.2 The energy gcd is a genuine new arithmetic object

Even when a large prime occurs, (12.1) shows that it can be lost through

\[
\gcd(a_{>n}^2,E_{n-2}),
\qquad
\gcd(b_{>n}^2,E_{n-1}),
\]

plus the explicit polynomial `P_1^2`.  The homogeneous companion Wronskian controls `b` against the homogeneous companion, not `b` against the inhomogeneous first jet.

The exact `p=11` fixture proves that the latter gcd need not be `1`.

Modulo `p`, the residual statement is precisely the first-jet common-zero problem

\[
p\mid b_m,\qquad p\mid g_m.
\]

At higher powers it is the lift (9.5).  No theorem in the stated input bounds the total logarithmic mass of these common zeros and lifts.

Therefore a Bezout or resultant made only from the homogeneous recurrence cannot manufacture the desired exponential denominator bound: after the exact reductions, it would still have to prove a theorem about the inhomogeneous Gessel jet.

# 14. Precise residual premises that would close an exponential bound

The exact formulas make the needed new input explicit rather than leaving a generic warning.

## 14.1 Large-prime formulation

A sufficient theorem is: there exists `delta>0` such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
&2\log a_{>n}+2\log b_{>n}\\
&\quad-
\log\gcd(a_{>n}^2,P_1^2E_{n-2})
-
\log\gcd(b_{>n}^2,E_{n-1})
\ge \delta n
\end{aligned}
\tag{14.1}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`.  By (12.1), this is exactly

\[
\log (V_n)_{>n}\ge\delta n.
\]

One may split (14.1) into two more conceptual premises:

1. **large-prime mass:** `log(a_(>n)b_(>n))` has a positive linear lower bound;
2. **energy non-saturation:** the two energy gcds consume strictly less than twice that mass.

Neither premise is currently proved.

## 14.2 Prime-by-prime first-jet formulation

For `p>n`, define

\[
\mathcal J_{m,p}(j):
\quad p^j\mid E_m,
\]

with (9.4)--(9.5) giving its exact jet interpretation.  Then (0.8) says

\[
\log (V_n)_{>n}
=
\sum_{\substack{p>n\\p^t\parallel a}}
\bigl(2t-2v_p(P_1)-v_p(E_{n-2})\bigr)_+\log p
+
\sum_{\substack{p>n\\p^u\parallel b}}
\bigl(2u-v_p(E_{n-1})\bigr)_+\log p.
\tag{14.2}
\]

Thus a theorem-grade lower bound can equivalently be stated as a positive linear lower bound for the right side of (14.2).  This is the sharp residual condition; there is no hidden recurrence term left.

# 15. Universal/index factors and “endpoint rollover” bookkeeping

It is important not to double-count the visible polynomial factors in the original numerator.

The raw numerator contains `n^3`, but after using the recurrence product identity (5.1), the exclusive-prime cancellation formulas contain only:

- `n-2` once;
- `P(n-1)` twice on the `a` side;
- `n-1` to the sixth power on the `b` side;
- the neighboring energies.

Hence all purely index/endpoint contributions are supported on

\[

and have only `O(log n)` total logarithmic height.  In particular, the `n^3` prefactor must **not** be counted as an independent source of three more powers after the Apéry recurrence has already been used.

Likewise, the exact gap-two rollover is exactly `P(n-1)` by (7.4)--(7.5).  Its contribution is polynomial-height.  The hard exponential issue is elsewhere: small-prime smooth mass and Gessel-energy common-zero/lift mass.
