# Q603 — Gross–Koblitz, Mellin transforms, and the Apéry zero mechanism

## Summary

The Gross–Koblitz formula gives a Gauss-sum expression for the Apéry finite-field hypergeometric object, but it does **not** turn \(b_r\bmod p\) into a single product of \(p\)-adic Gamma values. The vanishing mechanism is therefore not a Gamma factor becoming zero. It is cancellation in a finite Fourier transform of character amplitudes.

The correct structural picture is:

\[
b_r \equiv \frac1{p-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbf F_p^\times}} A(\chi)\chi(-1)^r
\]

for suitable hypergeometric amplitudes \(A(\chi)\). The hard AFP problem is a bound on the number of zeros of this Mellin transform. Bounded conductor alone is insufficient; the relevant object is the special Apéry motive and its additional monodromy/Hodge constraints.

---

## (A) Gross–Koblitz expression

The Apéry numbers satisfy

\[
b_r=\sum_{k=0}^r {r\choose k}^2{r+k\choose k}^2.
\]

For finite-field hypergeometric interpretations one rewrites binomial coefficients using Gauss sums. Let \(\omega\) be the Teichmuller character and \(g(\chi)\) the Gauss sum. Gross–Koblitz gives

\[
g(\omega^{-m})=-\pi^{m}\Gamma_p\left(\frac m{p-1}\right)
\]

(up to the standard normalization of the fractional exponent).

Consequently every hypergeometric coefficient is a product of Gamma values:

\[
\frac{g(\chi^{a_1})\cdots g(\chi^{a_n})}
{g(\chi^{b_1})\cdots g(\chi^{b_{n-1}})}
\]

becomes

\[
\prod_i\Gamma_p(\langle a_i m/(p-1)\rangle)
\Big/
\prod_j\Gamma_p(\langle b_j m/(p-1)\rangle).
\]

For the Apéry motive this gives the \({}_4F_3\)-type expression

\[
{}_4F_3
\left(
\begin{matrix}
1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2\\
1,1,1
\end{matrix};1
\right)_p,
\]

or equivalent parameterizations of the same hypergeometric motive.

However this is a **character sum of Gamma products**, not a single Gamma product.

---

## (B) Explicit Mellin amplitude

The Mellin form is obtained by expanding the finite-field hypergeometric function:

\[
b_r=\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\chi} A(\chi)\chi(t_r).
\]

The amplitude has the form

\[
A(\chi)=
\frac{g(\chi^{1/2})^4}{g(\chi)^3}
\]

with the usual interpretation after choosing a square-root character when it exists, or equivalently through the McCarthy/Greeene hypergeometric normalization.

Using Gross–Koblitz:

\[
A(\chi)
\sim
\frac{
\Gamma_p(\langle m/2(p-1)\rangle)^4
}
{
\Gamma_p(\langle m/(p-1)\rangle)^3
}
\]

up to explicit powers of \(\pi\) and roots of unity.

Thus

\[
b_r=0
\]

means cancellation among all these character amplitudes.

---

## (C) Where does vanishing occur?

A single Gamma factor cannot vanish:

\[
|\Gamma_p(x)|_p=1.
\]

Therefore the Jacobi-style mechanism

\[
v_p(J(\chi_1,\chi_2))\ge1
\]

coming from Stickelberger fractional parts does not directly apply.

The Apéry zero condition is instead:

\[
\sum_\chi A(\chi)\chi(t)^r=0.
\]

This is a Fourier cancellation condition.

The required cancellation is global:

* many independent character terms must align;
* the Hodge slopes constrain their valuations;
* the monodromy controls possible correlations.

This is exactly why the Jacobi counterexample does not automatically transfer.

---

## (D) Can the degree in \(\omega^r\) be bounded?

Formally every function on \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) has an expansion

\[
f(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2} c_j\omega^{jr},
\]

so the naive degree is at most \(p-2\).

The Apéry recurrence does not imply a small Fourier degree. A recurrence of order two with polynomial coefficients controls the evolution in \(r\), but not the additive/Kummer Fourier support.

In particular:

\[
\text{recurrence degree }3
\not\Rightarrow
\text{Fourier degree }O(1).
\]

A small Fourier degree theorem would be essentially equivalent to the desired bounded-zero theorem and is currently the missing input.

---

## (E) Relation with McCarthy \({}_4F_3\)

The correct statement is not

\[
b_r={}_4F_3(...;\omega^r)_p
\]

with the parameter simply equal to \(\omega^r\).

Rather, the Apéry sequence is a moment/Mellin specialization of the underlying hypergeometric motive. One has a trace function \(H_p(t)\) such that

\[
b_r=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}H_p(t)\omega(t)^{-r}.
\]

This is a Kummer Mellin transform.

The McCarthy finite-field hypergeometric function gives the explicit Fourier coefficients of \(H_p\):

\[
H_p(t)= {}_4F_3(\text{Apéry parameters};t)_p.
\]

Therefore the zeros of \(b_r\) are zeros of the Mellin transform of a hypergeometric trace function.

---

## Consequence for AFP

The desired statement

\[
|Z_p|=O(1)
\]

would follow from a theorem of the following form:

> The Mellin transform of the Apéry hypergeometric trace function has uniformly bounded zero multiplicity.

But this cannot follow only from rank, conductor, or purity. The missing ingredient is an Apéry-specific horizontal non-degeneracy theorem:

1. no Stickelberger mass interval as in Jacobi sums;
2. no large character subspace with forced valuation increase;
3. enough monodromy mixing among the Gamma-product summands.

The Gross–Koblitz formula identifies the exact location of the difficulty: AFP is a theorem about cancellation of a hypergeometric Mellin transform, not about zeros of individual Gamma factors.
