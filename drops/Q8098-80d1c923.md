# Exact audit of the reciprocal Hasse-shape I5 countermodel

## Verdict

The proposed family works as an asymptotic I5 countermodel, and it is strictly sharper than the generic reflected/anchored-star countermodel in the intended sense: for every sufficiently large prime it preserves

- the **actual quadratic-character schedule**
  \[
  \varepsilon_p=\frac{1-\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)}2,
  \]
- a normalized reciprocal factorization of the exact Apéry Hasse **shape**
  \[
  A_p^*(t)=\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^*(t)^2,
  \qquad \Delta(t)=1-34t+t^2,
  \]
- \(\deg A_p^*=p-1\), \(B_p^*(0)=1\), and palindromic coefficients,
- reflection of coefficient zeros under \(j\mapsto p-1-j\), and
- on a positive-density set of primes, the common coefficient zero \(j=2\), whose reflected pair folds to the single isolated folded point \(2\).

The coefficient claims can be sharpened substantially:

> **Core theorem.** On the actual \(\varepsilon\)-schedule, for every prime \(p\ge 11\),
> \[
> Z_p^*:=\{0\le j<p:[t^j]A_p^*(t)=0\}
> =
> \begin{cases}
> \{2,p-3\},&\varepsilon_p=0,\\
> \varnothing,&\varepsilon_p=1.
> \end{cases}
> \]
> The only scheduled small exceptions are \(p=5\), where the printed \(\varepsilon=0\) formula is not normalized, and \(p=7\), where it is normalized but has no coefficient zero.

The parenthetical “\(p=17\) is exceptional” is algebraically correct only if one applies the \(\varepsilon=0\) polynomial **off schedule**: that polynomial has zeros
\[
\{2,4,12,14\}\quad\text{mod }17.
\]
But \(\varepsilon_{17}=1\), so the actual scheduled construction uses \(B=G_7\) and has **no** coefficient zero at \(p=17\).

Two optional evaluation-root properties do **not** hold uniformly and should not be advertised as part of the countermodel:

1. The modified \(\varepsilon=0\) square root is non-squarefree exactly at \(p=7\) and \(p=1129\) among normalized scheduled primes \(p\ge7\). At \(p=1129\),
   \[
   \gcd(B,B')=t^2+782t+1.
   \]
   The \(\varepsilon=1\) polynomial \(G_d\) is always squarefree.
2. \(B\) is always coprime to \(t\) once normalized, but \(\gcd(B,\Delta)\ne1\) at \(p=59\) in the \(\varepsilon=0\) branch, and in the \(\varepsilon=1\) branch one has
   \[
   \Delta\mid B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\equiv17,23\pmod{24}.
   \]
   Thus coprimality with \(t\Delta\) fails for infinitely many scheduled \(\varepsilon=1\) primes.

These evaluation-root failures do not affect the coefficient-zero star. They concern zeros of \(B_p^*(t)\) as a polynomial in \(t\), whereas I5 uses indices \(j\) for which \([t^j]A_p^*(t)=0\). This distinction is exactly the warning in `oracleB_result.tex` and `hasse_franel_descent.tex`.

One source-state note: the GitHub-visible `main` branch contains `oracleB_result.tex`, `proof.tex`, and `hasse_franel_descent.tex`, but `problems/3.2/isolated_five_star.tex` is not present on either `main` or `chatgpt-drop`, and repository code search finds no copy. I therefore use the I5 convention stated in the rc5 problem itself: a folded point is isolated in the set \(Y_p=\sigma_p(Z_p)\), while \(I_p(H)\) consists of the physical roots whose folds are isolated. Under that convention the reflected pair contributes two physical roots to \(|I_p(H)|\).

## 1. What is being preserved, and what is not

The repo makes two distinctions that are essential here.

First, the actual Apéry polynomial is
\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1} b_jt^j,
\qquad
\mathcal Z_p=\{j:b_j\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal Z_p\) is a **coefficient/Mellin zero set**. It is not the evaluation root locus of \(A_p\) or of the square root \(B_p\). `oracleB_result.tex` proves this separation explicitly; `hasse_franel_descent.tex` likewise identifies \(b_j\) as coordinates of the Hasse square in a cover-adapted basis, not as evaluations at \(j\).

Second, `hasse_franel_descent.tex` gives much more than the abstract factorization shape for the actual Apéry family. The genuine normalized factors are the reductions of two fixed characteristic-zero formal series
\[
C_+(t)=\sum c_nt^n,
\qquad C_-(t)=\sum d_nt^n,
\]
with
\[
4(n+1)^2c_{n+1}
=2(68n^2+34n+5)c_n-(2n-1)^2c_{n-1},
\]
\[
4(n+1)^2d_{n+1}
=2(68n^2+102n+39)d_n-(2n+1)^2d_{n-1},
\]
so that
\[
A_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2
\]
with \(B_p\) the appropriate truncation of \(C_+\) or \(C_-\).

The countermodel below preserves the schedule, degree, normalization, reciprocity, and square/quadratic-times-square **shape**, but deliberately drops this universal recurrence/ODE constraint. That is the precise remaining actual-Apéry input.

## 2. The exact \(\varepsilon\)-schedule

For primes \(p\ge5\), the reduced residue classes modulo \(24\) are
\[
1,5,7,11,13,17,19,23.
\]
Quadratic reciprocity gives
\[
\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)=+1
\iff
p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},
\]
and
\[
\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)=-1
\iff
p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_p=0\iff p\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24},
\qquad
\varepsilon_p=1\iff p\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}.}
\]
In particular \(\varepsilon_{17}=1\).

By the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions, each half has relative density \(1/2\) among primes.

## 3. The \(\varepsilon=0\) branch: complete coefficient formula

Put
\[
d=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
G_d(t)=1+t+\cdots+t^d,
\]
and
\[
B(t)=G_d(t)-\frac32\bigl(t^2+t^{d-2}\bigr),
\qquad
A(t)=B(t)^2.
\]
For \(p\ge7\), the two altered exponents lie in \([0,d]\), the constant and leading coefficients are both \(1\), and
\[
t^dB(t^{-1})=B(t).
\]
Thus \(B\) is normalized reciprocal of degree \(d\), and \(A\) is reciprocal of degree \(2d=p-1\).

Let
\[
T_d(n)=[t^n]G_d(t)^2
=\begin{cases}
 n+1,&0\le n\le d,\\
 2d-n+1,&d\le n\le2d,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Expanding once gives the complete formula, valid without hidden case assumptions whenever \(d\ge2\):
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
a_n:=[t^n]A(t)
={}&T_d(n)
-3\,\mathbf1_{2\le n\le d+2}
-3\,\mathbf1_{d-2\le n\le2d-2}\\
&+\frac94\mathbf1_{n=4}
+\frac92\mathbf1_{n=d}
+\frac94\mathbf1_{n=2d-4}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.1}
\]
The indicator formula automatically handles overlaps at small \(d\).

For \(d\ge9\), i.e. \(p\ge19\), reciprocity reduces everything to \(0\le n\le d\), where (3.1) becomes
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
n&a_n\\ \hline
0&1\\
1&2\\
2&0\\
3&1\\
4&17/4\\
5\le n\le d-3&n-2\\
d-2&d-7\\
d-1&d-6\\
d&d-1/2.
\end{array}}
\tag{3.2}
\]
The other half is
\[
a_{2d-n}=a_n.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### 3.1 Zero classification for \(p\ge19\)

Modulo \(p\), the entries in (3.2) are nonzero except \(a_2=0\):

- \(17/4\) vanishes only at \(p=17\);
- for \(5\le n\le d-3\), \(3\le n-2<p\);
- \(d-7=(p-15)/2\) can vanish only for a prime divisor of \(15\);
- \(d-6=(p-13)/2\) can vanish only for a prime divisor of \(13\);
- \(d-1/2=(p-2)/2\equiv-1\pmod p\).

Therefore for every prime \(p\ge19\) to which this branch is applied,
\[
\boxed{Z_p^*=\{2,2d-2\}=\{2,p-3\}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### 3.2 Exact small cases and the alleged \(p=17\) exception

On the **scheduled** \(\varepsilon=0\) primes:

- \(p=5\): \(d=2\) and the printed correction hits \(t^{d-2}=t^0\), so
  \[
  B(0)=1-\frac32=-\frac12\ne1.
  \]
  This is the only normalization failure.
- \(p=7\):
  \[
  B(t)=1+3t+3t^2+t^3=(1+t)^3\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_7[t],
  \]
  and
  \[
  A(t)=(1+t)^6,
  \qquad
  ([t^n]A)_{n=0}^6=(1,6,1,6,1,6,1),
  \]
  so \(Z_7^*=\varnothing\).
- \(p=11\):
  \[
  ([t^n]A)_{n=0}^{10}
  =(1,2,0,9,4,10,4,9,0,2,1),
  \]
  hence
  \[
  Z_{11}^*=\{2,8\}=\{2,p-3\}.
  \]

Thus the scheduled statement actually holds for **every \(\varepsilon=0\) prime \(p\ge11\)**.

If one ignores the schedule and applies the same \(d=(p-1)/2\) formula at \(p=17\), then \(17/4=0\) creates one extra reflected pair:
\[
Z_{17}^{*(\mathrm{off\ schedule})}=\{2,4,12,14\}.
\]
This explains the observed exception, but it is irrelevant to the scheduled family because \(\varepsilon_{17}=1\).

## 4. The \(\varepsilon=1\) branch: no coefficient zeros

Now put
\[
d=\frac{p-3}{2},
\qquad
B(t)=G_d(t),
\qquad
A(t)=\Delta(t)G_d(t)^2.
\]
Then \(B(0)=1\), \(B\) is monic reciprocal of degree \(d\), and
\[
\deg A=2+2d=p-1.
\]
Since both \(\Delta\) and \(G_d\) are reciprocal, so is \(A\).

With the same triangular coefficient \(T_d\), the complete formula is
\[
\boxed{
a_n=T_d(n)-34T_d(n-1)+T_d(n-2),
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
where \(T_d(k)=0\) outside \([0,2d]\).

For the first half, including the center of the degree-\(p-1\) reciprocal polynomial,
\[
\boxed{
 a_0=1,
 \qquad
 a_n=-32n\quad(1\le n\le d),
 \qquad
 a_{d+1}=-32d-34=-16p+14\equiv14\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Reflection gives
\[
a_{2d+2-n}=a_n.
\tag{4.3}
\]
For a scheduled \(\varepsilon=1\) prime one has \(p\ge13\). Since \(1\le n\le d<p\), no \(-32n\) can vanish modulo \(p\), and \(14\not\equiv0\pmod p\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{Z_p^*=\varnothing\qquad(\varepsilon_p=1,\ p\ge13).}
\tag{4.4}
\]
There are no scheduled exceptions in this branch.

## 5. Reciprocity and coefficient reflection

For all normalized cases \(p\ge7\):

- \(B(0)=1\) and the leading coefficient of \(B\) is \(1\);
- \(t^{\deg B}B(t^{-1})=B(t)\);
- \(t^2\Delta(t^{-1})=\Delta(t)\).

Hence
\[
t^{p-1}A_p^*(t^{-1})=A_p^*(t),
\]
so coefficientwise
\[
\boxed{[t^{p-1-j}]A_p^*=[t^j]A_p^*.}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Thus the coefficient-zero set is reflection invariant under
\[
j\longmapsto p-1-j.
\]
The pair \(\{2,p-3\}\) is exactly one such reflection orbit.

Again, this says nothing about whether the field element \(t=2\) is an evaluation root of \(B\) or \(A\). It is only the **coefficient index** \(2\) that is marked.

## 6. Evaluation-root extras: squarefreeness

These properties are logically independent of the I5 coefficient zero set, but they can be audited exactly.

### 6.1 The \(\varepsilon=1\) branch is squarefree

Put
\[
n=d+1=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
G_d(t)=\frac{t^n-1}{t-1}.
\]
Since \(1\le n<p\),
\[
(t^n-1)'=nt^{n-1}
\]
has no common root with \(t^n-1\). Thus \(t^n-1\) is squarefree, and deleting its simple factor \(t-1\) leaves \(G_d\) squarefree. Therefore
\[
\boxed{B=G_d\text{ is squarefree for every scheduled }\varepsilon=1\text{ prime}.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### 6.2 Exact squarefree failures in the modified \(\varepsilon=0\) branch

For \(p\ge7\), set \(P(t)=2B(t)\). Since
\[
B(0)=1,
\qquad
B(1)=d-2\equiv-\frac52\not\equiv0\pmod p,
\]
a repeated root \(\beta\) of \(B\) satisfies \(\beta\ne0,1\). Put
\[
Q(t)=(t-1)P(t).
\]
A direct rearrangement gives
\[
Q(t)=t^{d-2}U(t)+V(t),
\tag{6.2}
\]
with the fixed cubics
\[
U(t)=2t^3-3t+3,
\qquad
V(t)=-3t^3+3t^2-2.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Their resultant is
\[
\operatorname{Res}(U,V)=8,
\]
so for odd \(p\), a root of \(Q\) cannot satisfy \(U=V=0\).

If \(\beta\) is repeated, then \(Q(\beta)=Q'(\beta)=0\). Using
\[
d-2=\frac{p-5}{2}\equiv-\frac52\pmod p
\]
and eliminating \(\beta^{d-2}\) from \(Q=0\) and \(tQ'=0\) gives the fixed degree-six condition
\[
F(\beta)=0,
\tag{6.4}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
F(t)=-30+18t+81t^2-158t^3+81t^4+18t^5-30t^6.}
\tag{6.5}
\]
Moreover \(Q(\beta)=0\) gives
\[
\beta^{d-2}=-\frac{V(\beta)}{U(\beta)}.
\]
Squaring and using \(2d=p-1\),
\[
\beta^{p-5}=\frac{V(\beta)^2}{U(\beta)^2},
\qquad
\beta^p=R(\beta):=eta^5\frac{V(\beta)^2}{U(\beta)^2}.
\tag{6.6}
\]
Because \(F\) has coefficients in \(\mathbb F_p\),
\[
F(\beta^p)=F(\beta)^p=0.
\]
Let \(N(t)\in\mathbb Z[t]\) be the numerator obtained after clearing denominators in \(F(R(t))\). Then every repeated-root prime \(p\ge7\) divides the fixed integer
\[
J=\operatorname{Res}(F,N).
\]
Exact integer factorization gives
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
|J|={}&2^{62}3^{66}5^8 7^2 19^2 23^2 41^2 503^2 1129^2\\
&\times
7135396993633257594923603908523^2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.7}
\]
The prime factors in (6.7) that lie on the \(\varepsilon=0\) schedule are
\[
5,\ 7,\ 1129,\ P_0,
\qquad
P_0=7135396993633257594923603908523
\equiv11\pmod{24}.
\]
The low-degree condition (6.2),(6.5), evaluated by modular binary exponentiation modulo \(F\), gives
\[
\gcd(B,B')\ne1\quad\text{at }p=7,1129,
\]
with
\[
B_7=(1+t)^3,
\qquad
\gcd(B_{1129},B_{1129}')=t^2+782t+1,
\]
while at the enormous candidate \(P_0\) the low-degree gcd is \(1\). The case \(p=5\) is already excluded by failure of normalization.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
B_p^*\text{ is squarefree for scheduled }\varepsilon_p=0,\ p\ge7,
\iff p\notin\{7,1129\}.}
\tag{6.8}
\]

This fixed-resultant argument is useful because it proves the claim for all primes; it is not a finite scan over degrees \(\asymp p\).

## 7. Evaluation-root extras: coprimality with \(t\Delta\)

### 7.1 Coprimality with \(t\)

For every normalized case,
\[
B(0)=1,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\gcd(B,t)=1.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### 7.2 Roots of \(\Delta\)

Let \(s^2=2\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\), and put
\[
\alpha=(1+s)^4=17+12s.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha^{-1}=17-12s,
\qquad
\alpha+\alpha^{-1}=34,
\]
so \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) are exactly the roots of
\[
\Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1.
\]
The splitting behavior of \(\Delta\) is therefore the splitting behavior of \(2\).

### 7.3 \(\varepsilon=1\): exact infinite coprimality failure

Here
\[
B=G_d,
\qquad d+1=\frac{p-1}{2}=:n.
\]
If \((2/p)=1\), then \(s\in\mathbb F_p\), and \(\alpha=(1+s)^4\) is a fourth power in \(\mathbb F_p^\times\). Hence
\[
\alpha^n=1.
\]
Since \(\alpha\ne1\), \(G_d(\alpha)=0\), and similarly for \(\alpha^{-1}\). Thus
\[
\Delta\mid G_d.
\]
On the scheduled \(\varepsilon=1\) classes, \((2/p)=1\) exactly for
\[
p\equiv17,23\pmod{24}.
\]

If \((2/p)=-1\), then \(\alpha^p=\alpha^{-1}\), hence \(\alpha^{p+1}=1\). If also \(\alpha^n=1\), then the order of \(\alpha\) divides
\[
\gcd\left(\frac{p-1}{2},p+1\right),
\]
which is \(2\) in the class \(13\pmod{24}\) and \(1\) in the class \(19\pmod{24}\). The possibilities \(\alpha=1\) or \(\alpha=-1\) are excluded for \(p\ge5\) because
\[
\Delta(1)=-32,
\qquad
\Delta(-1)=36.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\gcd(G_d,\Delta)\ne1
\iff p\equiv17,23\pmod{24}
\quad(\varepsilon_p=1).}
\tag{7.2}
\]
In fact \(\Delta\mid G_d\) in those classes.

### 7.4 \(\varepsilon=0\): the unique scheduled failure \(p=59\)

Now \(d=(p-1)/2\) and
\[
B=G_d-\frac32(t^2+t^{d-2}).
\]

If \((2/p)=1\), then \(\alpha\) is a fourth power and
\[
\alpha^d=1.
\]
Hence
\[
G_d(\alpha)=1,
\qquad
\alpha^{d-2}=\alpha^{-2},
\]
and therefore
\[
B(\alpha)
=1-\frac32(\alpha^2+\alpha^{-2}).
\]
Since
\[
\alpha^2+\alpha^{-2}=34^2-2=1154,
\]
we get
\[
B(\alpha)=-1730.
\tag{7.3}
\]
The prime divisors of \(1730\) are \(2,5,173\), and none is a normalized scheduled split \(\varepsilon=0\) prime. Thus there is no failure in the split classes.

If \((2/p)=-1\), then with \(u=1+s\) one has
\[
u^p=1-s=-u^{-1},
\]
so
\[
\alpha^d=u^{2(p-1)}=u^{-4}=\alpha^{-1}.
\]
Thus
\[
G_d(\alpha)=0,
\qquad
\alpha^{d-2}=\alpha^{-3},
\]
and
\[
B(\alpha)
=-\frac32\alpha^{-3}(\alpha^5+1).
\tag{7.4}
\]
Hence \(\Delta\) meets \(B\) iff \(\alpha^5=-1\). Put
\[
x_m=\alpha^m+\alpha^{-m}.
\]
Then
\[
x_0=2,
\qquad x_1=34,
\qquad x_{m+1}=34x_m-x_{m-1}.
\]
A five-step exact calculation gives
\[
x_5+2=45,239,076=(2\cdot3\cdot19\cdot59)^2.
\tag{7.5}
\]
Thus a nonsplit common-root prime must be one of \(2,3,19,59\). Of these, the only scheduled \(\varepsilon=0\) prime is \(59\). Indeed
\[
\Delta(t)\bmod59=t^2+25t+1
\]
and this entire quadratic divides \(B_{59}\).

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\gcd(B_p^*,\Delta)\ne1
\iff p=59
\quad\text{in the normalized scheduled }\varepsilon_p=0\text{ branch}.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Combining (7.1), (7.2), and (7.6), uniform coprimality with \(t\Delta\) is false and should be omitted from the preserved structure.

## 8. Folded isolation and the common anchored star

For every scheduled \(\varepsilon=0\) prime \(p\ge11\),
\[
Z_p^*=\{2,p-3\}.
\]
Under the actual reflection fold
\[
\sigma_p(r)=\min(r,p-1-r),
\]
both roots map to \(2\):
\[
\sigma_p(2)=2,
\qquad
\sigma_p(p-3)=2.
\]
Hence the folded set is the singleton
\[
Y_p^*=\{2\}.
\]
Therefore the folded root \(2\) is \(H\)-isolated **for every \(H\ge0\)**, vacuously: there is no other point of \(Y_p^*\).

Under the rc5 convention that \(I_p(H)\) contains the physical roots whose fold is isolated,
\[
I_p^*(H)=\{2,p-3\},
\qquad |I_p^*(H)|=2
\quad(\varepsilon_p=0,p\ge11),
\]
and
\[
I_p^*(H)=\varnothing
\quad(\varepsilon_p=1).
\tag{8.1}
\]
Thus the exact value of
\[
H=X^{7/15}(\log X)^{-1/3}
\]
plays no role in this model: isolation is automatic.

## 9. \(\Lambda_{\rm iso}\), the high tail, and the exact exponent gap

Let
\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p:X<p\le2X\},
\]
and define the active half
\[
\mathcal P_X^0
=\{p\in\mathcal P_X:\varepsilon_p=0\}.
\]
Write
\[
L_X=|\mathcal P_X^0|.
\]
By PNT in the four residue classes \(1,5,7,11\pmod{24}\),
\[
\boxed{L_X\sim\frac{X}{2\log X}.}
\tag{9.1}
\]
Partial summation gives
\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X^0}\frac1p
\sim\frac{\log2}{2\log X}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Using (8.1),
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{\rm iso}^*
=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|I_p^*(H)|}{p}
=2\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X^0}\frac1p
\sim\frac{\log2}{\log X}.}
\tag{9.3}
\]
In particular
\[
\Lambda_{\rm iso}^*=X^{o(1)}
\]
with the precise logarithmic scale \(1/\log X\).

At the single physical integer \(m=2\), for every \(p>X>2\),
\[
m\bmod p=2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{K_{\rm iso}^*(2;H)=L_X\sim\frac{X}{2\log X}.}
\tag{9.4}
\]

For fixed \(0<\eta<1/15\), the I5 high-tail threshold is
\[
T_X=X^{14/15+\eta}.
\]
Since
\[
\frac{L_X}{T_X}
\sim\frac{X^{1/15-\eta}}{2\log X}\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{9.5}
\]
we have \(K_{\rm iso}^*(2;H)\ge T_X\) for all sufficiently large \(X\).
Thus the I5 left side contains the single contribution
\[
(K_{\rm iso}^*(2;H))_5=(L_X)_5.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{0\le m<X^2\\K_{\rm iso}^*(m;H)\ge T_X}}
(K_{\rm iso}^*(m;H))_5
\ge(L_X)_5
\sim\frac{X^5}{32(\log X)^5}.}
\tag{9.6}
\]

By (9.3), the claimed I5 scale is
\[
1+X^2(\Lambda_{\rm iso}^*)^5
\sim
(\log2)^5\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^5}.
\tag{9.7}
\]
The ratio of the single anchored contribution (9.6) to (9.7) is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{(L_X)_5}{X^2(\Lambda_{\rm iso}^*)^5}
\sim
\frac{X^3}{32(\log2)^5}.}
\tag{9.8}
\]
Thus the model violates an
\[
X^{o(1)}\bigl(1+X^2\Lambda_{\rm iso}^5\bigr)
\]
I5 bound by a factor
\[
\boxed{X^{3+o(1)}.}
\]
Equivalently, the left side is \(X^{5+o(1)}\) while the target scale is \(X^{2+o(1)}\).

This is stronger than merely saturating a generic CRT error: the obstruction survives the actual half-density \(\varepsilon\)-schedule and the normalized reciprocal square/quadratic-times-square shape.

## 10. Exactly which actual-Apéry input is still absent

This construction is **not** an actual Apéry counterexample. It fails the arithmetic input at three equivalent levels.

### 10.1 It is not the reduction of the universal square-root coefficients

In the actual square branch, `hasse_franel_descent.tex` gives
\[
c_0=1,
\qquad c_1=\frac52.
\]
Our \(\varepsilon=0\) candidate has
\[
[t]B^*=1.
\]
The difference is \(3/2\), nonzero modulo every prime \(p\ge5\). Thus no scheduled \(\varepsilon=0\) candidate factor is the actual truncation of \(C_+\).

In the actual defect branch,
\[
d_0=1,
\qquad d_1=\frac{39}{2}.
\]
Our \(\varepsilon=1\) candidate again has \([t]B^*=1\). These agree only accidentally modulo \(p=37\). At \(p=37\), the next actual coefficient is forced by the recurrence:
\[
16d_2=418\cdot\frac{39}{2}-9,
\qquad
 d_2=\frac{4071}{8},
\]
which is not \(1\pmod{37}\). Thus the defect candidate also never satisfies the actual reduced recurrence beyond a finite accidental first coefficient.

### 10.2 It does not satisfy the exact reduced Picard--Fuchs ODE

The recurrences above are exactly the coefficient forms of the fixed second-order operators \(L_+\) and \(L_-\) in `hasse_franel_descent.tex`. Since a normalized formal solution is uniquely determined coefficient by coefficient, the preceding mismatch already proves that \(B_p^*\) is not an exact mod-\(p\) solution/truncation of the actual operator.

This is the decisive missing input. The abstract reciprocal factorization
\[
A_p^*=\Delta^{\varepsilon_p}(B_p^*)^2
\]
is far weaker than being the reduction of the one fixed characteristic-zero solution.

### 10.3 The coefficients of \(A_p^*\) are not one fixed integer Apéry vector

The genuine polynomial is
\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j
\]
with the same integer Apéry numbers \(b_j\) reduced modulo every \(p\). In particular \(b_1=5\) and \(b_2=73\).

Our first coefficient is
\[
[t]A_p^*=2
\quad(\varepsilon_p=0),
\]
and
\[
[t]A_p^*=-32
\quad(\varepsilon_p=1).
\]
The square branch therefore disagrees with the actual vector for every \(p\ge5\). In the defect branch \(-32\equiv5\pmod p\) only at \(p=37\); there
\[
[t^2]A_p^*=-64\equiv10\pmod{37},
\qquad
b_2=73\equiv36\pmod{37},
\]
so the mismatch appears at the next coefficient.

Hence the family is not compatible with “one fixed integer coefficient vector reduced in every characteristic.” This is another way to state the cross-characteristic arithmetic missing from the model.

## 11. Do we need a repair?

No asymptotic repair is needed. The core family already proves the intended no-go:

- for every scheduled \(\varepsilon=0\) prime \(p\ge11\), it has exactly the reflected coefficient-zero pair \(\{2,p-3\}\);
- for every scheduled \(\varepsilon=1\) prime \(p\ge13\), it has none;
- the active primes have density \(1/2\);
- the common root index \(2\) is folded-isolated and creates the \(X^{3+o(1)}\) I5 violation.

The finite defect \(p=5\) can be patched harmlessly if a definition for every \(p\ge5\) is desired, for example by setting \(B_5^*=G_2\) and \(A_5^*=(B_5^*)^2\). This restores normalization/reciprocity/degree and has no coefficient zero. No finite patch can make the printed family uniformly coprime to \(\Delta\), because the \(\varepsilon=1\) branch has \(\Delta\mid G_d\) for every prime \(p\equiv17,23\pmod{24}\). That property should simply be omitted.

Likewise, if one insists on uniform squarefreeness, the isolated prime \(1129\) would require a local perturbation, but squarefreeness is not needed for I5 and is not asserted by the CFVZ factorization theorem cited in `oracleB_result.tex`. The cleanest countermodel therefore leaves the family unchanged and records the exact evaluation-root exceptions.

## 12. Small exact Sage verifier specification

The following verifier is designed to check the coefficient statements, structural identities, optional evaluation-root exceptions, and the fixed-resultant certificate. It does **not** use Apéry evaluation roots in place of coefficient zeros.

from sage.all import *


def eps(p):
    return ZZ((1 - kronecker_symbol(-6, p)) // 2)


def build(p):
    F = GF(p)
    R = PolynomialRing(F, 't')
    t = R.gen()
    D = t**2 - 34*t + 1
    e = eps(p)
    if e == 0:
        d = (p - 1)//2
        B = sum(t**j for j in range(d+1)) \
            - F(3)/F(2) * (t**2 + t**(d-2))
    else:
        d = (p - 3)//2
        B = sum(t**j for j in range(d+1))
    A = D**e * B**2
    return R, t, D, d, e, B, A

    return [j for j in range(p) if A[j] == 0]

    n = f.degree()
    return all(f[j] == f[n-j] for j in range(n+1))

def powmod_poly(a,n,m):

## 13. Final theorem/no-go statement

A concise statement suitable for the rc5 obstruction ledger is:

> **Proposition (reciprocal Hasse-shape anchored I5 countermodel).** For every prime \(p\ge11\), define \(\varepsilon_p=(1-(-6/p))/2\) and
> \[
> B_p^*(t)=
> \begin{cases}
> G_{(p-1)/2}(t)-\frac32\bigl(t^2+t^{(p-5)/2}\bigr),&\varepsilon_p=0,\\
> G_{(p-3)/2}(t),&\varepsilon_p=1,
> \end{cases}
> \]
> and \(A_p^*(t)=\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p^*(t)^2\). Then \(B_p^*(0)=1\), \(A_p^*\) is monic reciprocal of degree \(p-1\), and
> \[
> \{j:[t^j]A_p^*(t)=0\}
> =\begin{cases}
> \{2,p-3\},&\varepsilon_p=0,\\
> \varnothing,&\varepsilon_p=1.
> \end{cases}
> \]
> Consequently the active primes have density \(1/2\), their two physical coefficient zeros fold to the single \(H\)-isolated point \(2\), and at \(m=2\)
> \[
> K_{\rm iso}^*(2;H)\sim\frac{X}{2\log X},
> \qquad
> \Lambda_{\rm iso}^*\sim\frac{\log2}{\log X}.
> \]
> For every fixed \(0<\eta<1/15\), the \(m=2\) term alone gives
> \[
> \sum_{K_{\rm iso}^*\ge X^{14/15+\eta}}(K_{\rm iso}^*)_5
> \ge \frac{X^{5+o(1)}}{32(\log X)^5},
> \]
> whereas
> \[
> 1+X^2(\Lambda_{\rm iso}^*)^5=X^{2+o(1)}.
> \]
> Hence the I5 bound fails by \(X^{3+o(1)}\).
>
> This is **not** an Apéry counterexample: the factors \(B_p^*\) are not reductions of the fixed characteristic-zero solutions \(C_\pm\), do not satisfy the exact reduced Picard--Fuchs recurrences, and the coefficient vectors \(([t^j]A_p^*)_j\) are not reductions of the one fixed integer Apéry vector \((b_j)_j\).

That is the precise logical boundary. Reflection, the actual \(\varepsilon\)-schedule, normalized reciprocity, degree \(p-1\), and the CFVZ square/quadratic-times-square **form** do not exclude the isolated anchored star. The extra input that excludes this explicit family must use the distinguished universal Apéry square-root solution (equivalently its exact ODE/recurrence or an equally strong cross-characteristic coordinate constraint), not merely the abstract factorization geometry.
