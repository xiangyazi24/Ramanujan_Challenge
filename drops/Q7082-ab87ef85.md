# Artin's conjecture and varying Hecke eigenvalues

## Summary

For the CM newform

\[
f(z)=\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4\in S_4(\Gamma_0(8)),
\]

there is currently **no known theorem** giving a lower bound of the form

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)\ge p^{1-\varepsilon}
\]

for almost all ordinary primes \(p\), and there is no established Artin conjecture for a sequence of varying algebraic integers \(a_p\) in this exact sense. The heuristic is plausible, but the available machinery for fixed integers does not transfer directly.

---

## 1. Lower bounds for \(\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)\)

Here \(a_p\) is the Hecke eigenvalue of the fixed weight-4 newform. For ordinary primes

\[
p\nmid a_p,
\]

we can regard \(a_p\bmod p\in \mathbf F_p^\times\) and ask for the multiplicative order

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{\mathbf F_p^\times}(a_p).
\]

For a fixed integer \(A\), Artin's primitive root conjecture predicts that \(A\) is a primitive root modulo a positive density of primes. Conditional on GRH, Hooley proved this for fixed \(A\).

The crucial difference is:

\[
A\quad\text{fixed}
\]

versus

\[
A=a_p\quad\text{varying with }p.
\]

The Galois/Kummer fields used in Artin's argument are fields such as

\[
\mathbf Q(\zeta_n,A^{1/n}),
\]

where the same element \(A\) appears for every prime. For \(a_p\), the relevant fields would vary with \(p\), so the standard Chebotarev argument does not apply.

No unconditional result comparable to

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)>p^\delta
\]

for almost all \(p\) is known for general non-CM newforms or this CM form.

---

## 2. Is there an Artin conjecture for varying sequences?

There are related conjectures, but not a direct analogue with the required strength.

### (a) Frobenius traces of elliptic curves / modular forms

For a fixed elliptic curve one studies

\[
a_p=p+1-\#E(\mathbf F_p),
\]

and asks distribution questions about \(a_p\). Sato--Tate describes the size distribution of \(a_p\), but it does not control

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p).
\]

### (b) Random matrix heuristic

The natural heuristic is that for ordinary primes, the residue

\[
a_p\bmod p
\]

behaves approximately like a random element of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), subject to the modular constraints. For a random element of \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), typically

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)\asymp p
\]

and the probability that the order is small is small.

This suggests

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)=p^{1-o(1)}
\]

as a plausible conjecture.

### (c) CM complication

The form \(\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4\) has CM, so the coefficients are not generic. The CM description expresses \(a_p\) through Hecke characters of an imaginary quadratic field. This gives extra structure, but no known theorem forcing large order modulo \(p\).

## 3. Would a large order bound imply \(Z(p)=O(p^\varepsilon)\)?

Not by itself, but it is very close to the missing dynamical input.

The Apéry projective orbit is governed by Frobenius eigenvalues. In the ordinary case the Frobenius polynomial has a unit root \(\alpha\), and the projective dynamics are controlled by ratios involving \(\alpha\). If

\[
\alpha\in \mathbf F_p^\times,
\]

then the orbit length is related to

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(\alpha).
\]

A large multiplicative order gives a long orbit in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\). Since zeros correspond to hits of a special point (the zero coordinate), a long orbit suggests few hits.

However there are two caveats.

### Caveat 1: order of \(a_p\) versus order of the unit root

The desired dynamical quantity is generally

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(\alpha),
\]

not

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p).
\]

The relation comes from the Frobenius equation

\[
X^2-a_pX+p^{k-1}=0.
\]

Modulo \(p\), this degenerates to

\[
X(X-a_p)=0.
\]

Thus in the ordinary case the nonzero root modulo \(p\) is indeed related to \(a_p\), but the precise orbit statement requires the integral Frobenius/transfer action, not merely the scalar order of \(a_p\).

### Caveat 2: long period does not automatically imply uniform distribution

A cyclic orbit of length \(L\) in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\) can still have biased intersections with a point or subset. One needs an equidistribution statement for the orbit, not only a period lower bound.

The strongest hoped-for implication would be something like:

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(\alpha)>p^{1-\varepsilon}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|Z(p)|\ll p^\varepsilon,
\]

but this requires additional expansion/mixing input.

## Relation to the current Apéry zero-set bounds

The known algebraic bounds in the project, such as

\[
|Z_p|=O(p^{2/3}),
\]

come from gap-polynomial and collision arguments. They do not use Frobenius order.

A hypothetical theorem giving:

1. large Frobenius order;
2. quantitative equidistribution of the projective orbit;
3. control of exceptional CM primes;

would be a fundamentally different route and could plausibly improve the exponent dramatically.

The missing step is therefore not simply an Artin primitive-root statement. The needed theorem is closer to:

> For the Apéry Frobenius cocycle attached to the modular form, the ordinary projective orbit has near-maximal length and power-saving discrepancy for almost all primes.

No theorem of this strength is currently known.

---

## Final answers

**Q1.** No known lower bound of the form \(\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)\ge p^{1-\varepsilon}\) for almost all ordinary primes exists.

**Q2.** There is no established Artin conjecture for varying Hecke eigenvalue sequences with this conclusion. Random-matrix and Sato--Tate heuristics suggest large orders, but they are far from proved.

**Q3.** Such an order bound would be strong evidence toward \(Z(p)=p^{o(1)}\), but an extra orbit-equidistribution theorem is needed; order alone is insufficient.
