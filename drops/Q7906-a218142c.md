ANSWER Q7906 a218142c

# The level-6 supersingular divisor does **not** turn the Apéry coefficient-zero transform into a Brandt trace

## Executive verdict

The proposed route contains one correct modular fact and one fatal category error.

1. Let
   \[
   A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r t^r\in\mathbf F_p[t].
   \]
   After pullback to the level-6 coordinate \(x\), the polynomial \(A_p\) is the square of the Hasse polynomial of the universal elliptic curve with a point of order six. Thus the **roots of the polynomial \(A_p(t)\)** are Fricke-orbits of supersingular level-6 points, with explicit multiplicities.

2. The set needed in Problem 3.2 is instead
   \[
   Z_p=\{r\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}:b_r=0\pmod p\}.
   \]
   Its elements are **coefficient indices**, not roots of \(A_p\), not supersingular parameters, and not ideal classes in a quaternion algebra. The current repository already records this correction in commit
   `677b927e24edd0475f85b69f14fa2776e6598d3a`:
   \(r\in Z_p\) is a vanishing defining-characteristic Mellin trace, not a supersingular fiber parameter.

3. There is an exact supersingular-divisor formula
   \[
   b_r=(-1)^r\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^rD_p),
   \]
   where \(D_p\) is the diagonal operator whose eigenvalues are the inverses of the Fricke-quotient coordinates of all supersingular level-6 points, counted with the correct multiplicities. This is a useful merge-safe lemma. It is **not** a Brandt-matrix formula: \(D_p\) is multiplication by a modular coordinate and does not commute with the Brandt--Hecke algebra.

4. Deuring/Eichler/Pizer/Hijikata formulas do give exact class-number expressions for:

   - the full supersingular level-6 class set;
   - traces of Brandt operators, hence counts of isogenies of a specified norm;
   - the two Fricke-fixed CM points of discriminant \(-24\).

   They do **not** give \(R_p(c)\), because \(R_p(c)\) applies an additive character to the exterior-degree index \(r\) after the nonlinear test \(\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^rD_p)=0\).

5. No known one-prime Deligne, Brandt, or all-character Mellin equidistribution theorem saves the missing factor \(X\). The indispensable input is a genuinely synchronized cross-prime estimate at
   \[
   c=h(q\ell)^{-1}\pmod p,
   \]
   jointly in \(p,q,\ell,h\). An exact sufficient dyadic estimate is stated in Section 8 below.

Therefore the verdict is **negative for the proposed classical trace-formula shortcut**, but with four concrete positive outputs: the exact modular parameter, all supersingular multiplicities, a diagonal/exterior-power formula for \(b_r\), and the precise cross-prime estimate that would close the centered cubic term.

## 1. First correction: three different finite sets are being conflated

For every prime \(p\ge5\), keep the following objects separate.

### 1.1 Supersingular level-6 points

Let \(\mathscr S_{p,6}\) be the geometric supersingular locus on the level-6 modular curve. It is a set of points
\[
P=(E,C_6)
\]
over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\), where \(E\) is supersingular and \(C_6\subset E[6]\) is cyclic of order six.

### 1.2 Roots of the Apéry polynomial

Let
\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r.
\]
Its roots are values of a Fricke-quotient modular parameter \(t\) on \(\mathscr S_{p,6}\). A non-Fricke-fixed orbit contributes a double root; a Fricke-fixed supersingular point contributes a simple root.

### 1.3 Zero coefficients

The set
\[
Z_p=\{r:b_r=0\pmod p\}
\]
is a subset of the **index set** \(\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\). Every coefficient \(b_r\) is a global elementary symmetric function of all the roots of \(A_p\). There is no map
\[
r\in Z_p\longmapsto P_r\in\mathscr S_{p,6}
\]
coming from the Hasse interpretation.

A numerical illustration from the repository is already decisive. For \(p=59\),
\[
Z_{59}=\{9,49\}.
\]
On the other hand, \((-6/59)=1\), so \(A_{59}\) has \(29\) distinct geometric roots, all of multiplicity two, obtained from the \(58\) supersingular level-6 points. The two coefficient indices \(9,49\) are plainly not those \(29\) modular points.

This is not semantic bookkeeping. Every class-number or Brandt route acts on \(\mathscr S_{p,6}\); the transform in the question acts on \(Z_p\).

## 2. Exact level-6 modular parameter and Hasse polynomial

All assertions in this section require \(p\nmid6\), hence \(p\ge5\). The primes \(2,3\) are bad-level exceptions and should be removed once and for all; they are irrelevant in a dyadic interval \((X,2X]\) with \(X\to\infty\).

### 2.1 The level-6 elliptic family

The Franel family can be written as the plane cubic
\[
C_x:\quad XYZ-x(X+Y)(Y+Z)(Z+X)=0,
\]
or, after a birational change of variables, in Tate normal form
\[
E_x:\quad y^2+(1-2x)xy+x^2y=x^3.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The point \((0,0)\) has exact order six on a smooth fiber. For (2.1),
\[
a_1=1-2x,\qquad a_3=x^2,\qquad a_2=a_4=a_6=0,
\]
so
\[
\Delta_E=x^6(1+x)^2(1-8x),
\tag{2.2}
\]
\[
c_4=(1-2x)(1-6x-12x^2-8x^3),
\tag{2.3}
\]
and
\[
j(x)=
\frac{(1-2x)^3(1-6x-12x^2-8x^3)^3}
{x^6(1+x)^2(1-8x)}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Thus the four cusps are
\[
x=0,-1,1/8,\infty
\]
with cusp widths \(6,2,1,3\), respectively. Formula (2.4) is the Beauville family IV in Zagier's table, after his parameter change \(t_{\mathrm B}=-1/x\). Its modular group is \(\Gamma_1(6)\).

There is no contradiction with the notation \(X_0(6)\). Since
\[
(\mathbf Z/6\mathbf Z)^\times=\{\pm1\},
\]
the coarse map \(X_1(6)\to X_0(6)\) has degree
\[
\varphi(6)/2=1.
\]
Hence the coarse curves are isomorphic. The exact moduli problem remains \((E,P_6)\) on \(X_1(6)\), while \(X_0(6)\) remembers only \(\langle P_6\rangle\). This distinction matters when discussing automorphisms, but not for the coarse parameter \(x\).

### 2.2 The Hasse scalar is the Franel polynomial

Put
\[
f_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^3,
\qquad
K_p(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}f_nx^n.
\tag{2.5}
\]
For a smooth plane cubic \(F=0\), the Hasse scalar in the standard residue differential is the coefficient of \((XYZ)^{p-1}\) in \(F^{p-1}\). Here
\[
F=XYZ-x(X+Y)(Y+Z)(Z+X).
\]
Expanding \(F^{p-1}\), using
\[
\binom{p-1}{n}\equiv(-1)^n\pmod p,
\]
and the elementary coefficient identity
\[
[X^nY^nZ^n]\big((X+Y)(Y+Z)(Z+X)\big)^n
=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^3,
\]
gives exactly (2.5), with no unspecified scalar.

Katz--Mazur, *Arithmetic Moduli of Elliptic Curves*, Section 12.4.3, proves that the Hasse invariant has reduced zero divisor equal to the supersingular locus. Because the level \(6\) is prime to \(p\), the level cover is étale over the elliptic moduli stack; because \(\Gamma_1(6)\) is torsion-free, every zero on the coarse level curve is simple. The Hasse invariant has \(q\)-expansion \(1\), so it does not vanish at a cusp.

Also \(\deg K_p=p-1\): indeed
\[
f_{p-1}\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{3k}=1\pmod p.
\]
Consequently:

> **Lemma 2.1 (complete level-6 Hasse divisor).**  For every prime \(p\ge5\), the roots of \(K_p(x)\) in \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\) are exactly the supersingular points of \(X_1(6)\simeq X_0(6)\), every root is simple, and
> \[
> |\mathscr S_{p,6}|=p-1.
> \]

### 2.3 Multiplicity over special \(j\)-invariants

Let \(s_p\) be the number of supersingular \(j\)-invariants over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\), and put
\[
\delta_0=\mathbf1_{p\equiv2\pmod3},
\qquad
\delta_{1728}=\mathbf1_{p\equiv3\pmod4}.
\]
The exact formula is
\[
s_p=\frac{p-1}{12}
+\frac{1-\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)}4
+\frac{1-\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)}3.
\tag{2.6}
\]
For a generic elliptic curve there are \(24\) points of exact order six and, after quotienting by \(\{\pm1\}\), \(12\) coarse level-6 structures. At \(j=1728\), the automorphism group has order four and there are \(6\) level points. At \(j=0\), the automorphism group has order six and there are \(4\) level points. Therefore
\[
12s_p-6\delta_{1728}-8\delta_0=p-1.
\tag{2.7}
\]
This recovers the degree in Lemma 2.1 and gives the exact distinction between all supersingular \(j\)-invariants and all supersingular level-6 points.

## 3. Fricke descent and the exact root multiplicities of \(A_p(t)\)

The Apéry polynomial is not naturally in the coordinate \(x\). Its coordinate is the Fricke quotient
\[
t=\phi(x)=\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The nontrivial deck transformation is
\[
\sigma(x)=\frac{1-8x}{8(1+x)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
The two preimages of \(t\) satisfy
\[
8x^2+(t-1)x+t=0,
\tag{3.3}
\]
whose discriminant is
\[
D(t)=t^2-34t+1.
\tag{3.4}
\]
The repository file `problems/3.2/toric_fiber_sym2.tex` proves more than equality of Picard--Fuchs equations: the quotient of \(E_x\) by its rational six-torsion subgroup is isomorphic, up to the constant quadratic twist \(-3\), to \(E_{\sigma(x)}\). Thus \(\sigma\) is the Fricke involution \(w_6\) on the coarse modular curve, and \(t\) is a Hauptmodul on
\[
X_0(6)^+=X_0(6)/\langle w_6\rangle.
\]

The exact Hasse descent identity proved in `problems/3.2/hasse_franel_descent.tex` is
\[
K_p(x)^2=(1+x)^{p-1}A_p(\phi(x)).
\tag{3.5}
\]
The factor \((1+x)^{p-1}\) is a cusp trivialization factor and is a unit at every smooth supersingular point.

### 3.1 Fricke-fixed points

The fixed points of \(\sigma\) satisfy
\[
8x^2+16x-1=0.
\tag{3.6}
\]
At such a point the six-isogeny becomes a trace-zero endomorphism \(\alpha\) with
\[
\alpha^2=[-6].
\]
Hence the fixed fibers have CM by the maximal order of \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-6})\), of discriminant \(-24\). Their two \(j\)-values are the roots of
\[
H_{-24}(j)=j^2-4834944j+14670139392.
\tag{3.7}
\]
By Deuring's reduction theorem, for \(p\ge5\) these two CM fibers are supersingular exactly when \(p\) is inert in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-6})\), namely
\[
\chi_p:=\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)=-1.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Therefore
\[
\#\operatorname{Fix}(w_6\mid\mathscr S_{p,6})
=1-\chi_p
\in\{0,2\}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
This is the part of the story that genuinely has a class-number/optimal-embedding trace interpretation. On the supersingular permutation module, the Fricke operator has
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(W_6)=1-\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right).
\tag{3.10}
\]
Pizer's and Eichler's trace formulas identify (3.10) with the number of optimal embeddings of the order of discriminant \(-24\) into the relevant Eichler orders.

### 3.2 Root multiplicities after quotienting

At a nonfixed supersingular point, \(\phi\) is unramified and \(K_p^2\) has order two. Hence the corresponding root of \(A_p\) has multiplicity two.

At a Fricke-fixed supersingular point, \(\phi\) has ramification index two. If the corresponding root of \(A_p\) has multiplicity \(m\), then its pullback has order \(2m\), while \(K_p^2\) has order two. Hence \(m=1\).

Thus:

> **Lemma 3.1 (exact Fricke-orbit divisor).**  Let \(p\ge5\), and let \(\chi_p=(-6/p)\). The roots of \(A_p(t)\) are the \(w_6\)-orbits in \(\mathscr S_{p,6}\). An orbit of size two gives a double root; a fixed supersingular point gives a simple root. In particular,
> \[
> \#\operatorname{Supp}(A_p)
> =\frac{p-\chi_p}{2}.
> \tag{3.11}
> \]
> More explicitly:
>
> | \(p\bmod24\) | \(\chi_p\) | double roots | simple roots | distinct roots |
> |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
> | \(1,5,7,11\) | \(+1\) | \((p-1)/2\) | \(0\) | \((p-1)/2\) |
> | \(13,17,19,23\) | \(-1\) | \((p-3)/2\) | \(2\) | \((p+1)/2\) |

Equivalently, with
\[
\epsilon_p=\frac{1-\chi_p}{2},
\]
there is \(B_p(t)\in\mathbf F_p[t]\) such that
\[
\boxed{
A_p(t)=D(t)^{\epsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
\qquad D(t)=t^2-34t+1.}
\tag{3.12}
\]
This is exactly Theorem 1.2 of Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin.

The geometric support of (3.12) is the “marked supersingular locus” that is legitimately present in the problem. It is the root divisor of \(A_p\), not the index set \(Z_p\).

## 4. Exact supersingular-divisor formula for the Apéry coefficients

There is a clean exact formula, but it exposes rather than removes the obstruction.

For \(P\in\mathscr S_{p,6}\), put
\[
t(P)=\phi(x(P)).
\]
No \(t(P)\) is zero because \(A_p(0)=1\). Let
\[
V_p=\bigoplus_{P\in\mathscr S_{p,6}}\overline{\mathbf F}_p[P]
\]
and define the diagonal coordinate operator
\[
D_p[P]=t(P)^{-1}[P].
\tag{4.1}
\]
The nonfixed Fricke pairs appear twice in this basis and fixed points once, exactly matching Lemma 3.1. Since both polynomials below have degree \(p-1\), constant term one, and the same roots with the same multiplicities, (3.5) gives
\[
\boxed{A_p(T)=\det(1-TD_p).}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
b_r=(-1)^r\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^rD_p)
=(-1)^r
\sum_{\substack{I\subset\mathscr S_{p,6}\\|I|=r}}
\prod_{P\in I}t(P)^{-1}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
Z_p=\{r:\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^rD_p)=0\}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Formula (4.4) is the exact answer to “how is \(Z_p\) marked by the supersingular divisor?” It is a vanishing condition on the \(r\)-th elementary symmetric coordinate of the **entire** divisor. It is not a condition on one supersingular point.

A second exact form uses the power sums
\[
s_{p,j}=\operatorname{Tr}(D_p^j)
=\sum_{P\in\mathscr S_{p,6}}t(P)^{-j}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Newton's identities give, for \(1\le r<p\),
\[
\boxed{
r b_r=-\sum_{j=1}^r s_{p,j}b_{r-j}.}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Even if one found a class-number expression for every coordinate power sum \(s_{p,j}\), deciding \(b_r=0\) requires all \(s_{p,j}\) up to \(j=r\), with \(r\) as large as \(p-1\), followed by the nonlinear recurrence (4.6). No bounded collection of Hecke traces is being sampled.

## 5. What the quaternionic and trace formulas actually compute

Let \(B_{p,\infty}\) be the definite quaternion algebra over \(\mathbf Q\) ramified at \(p\) and \(\infty\), and let \(\mathcal R_{p,6}\) be an Eichler order of discriminant \(p\) and level \(6\). Deuring's correspondence with prime-to-\(p\) level identifies
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal R_{p,6})
\longleftrightarrow
\mathscr S_{p,6}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Because \(X_0(6)\) has no elliptic points, every cyclic level-6 object has only \(\{\pm1\}\) as automorphisms, and the class set has cardinality \(p-1\), in agreement with Lemma 2.1.

Let
\[
\mathcal M_{p,6}=\mathbf C[\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal R_{p,6})].
\]
For \((n,6p)=1\), the Brandt matrix \(B_n\) counts degree-\(n\) isogenies respecting the level structure. The Eichler--Hijikata--Pizer formulas compute
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(B_n),
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(W_dB_n),
\tag{5.2}
\]
in terms of optimal embeddings and class numbers of quadratic orders. Pizer's 1976 Theorems 2 and 4 identify these Brandt traces with the corresponding Hecke traces; Hijikata's 1974 theorem gives the explicit \(\Gamma_0(N)\) trace formula.

This machinery yields the following exact positive information.

- \(B_0\), or the mass formula, counts all supersingular level-6 classes.
- \(B_n\) counts isogenies of reduced norm \(n\).
- \(W_6\) detects the CM fixed points and gives (3.10).
- Quaternionic theta series
  \[
  \theta_{P,Q}(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}B_n(P,Q)q^n
  \]
  encode these norm counts and lie in the appropriate weight-two Jacquet--Langlands space of level determined by the Eichler level \(6\) and discriminant \(p\).

They do not evaluate the diagonal coordinate operator \(D_p\).

### 5.1 Rigorous non-Hecke obstruction

Let \(M_f\) be multiplication by any function \(f\) on the supersingular class set:
\[
M_f[P]=f(P)[P].
\]
Then, in the supersingular basis,
\[
\boxed{
[M_f,B_n]_{P,Q}=(f(P)-f(Q))B_n(P,Q).}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Take \(f(P)=t(P)^{-1}\). The prime-to-\(6p\) Hecke orbit of a supersingular level point is the full supersingular isogeny class, while \(t\) is nonconstant. Hence for some \(n\) and some \(P,Q\),
\[
B_n(P,Q)\ne0,
\qquad t(P)\ne t(Q),
\]
and (5.3) is nonzero. Thus
\[
D_p\notin\mathbb T_{p,6},
\tag{5.4}
\]
where \(\mathbb T_{p,6}\) is the commutative Brandt--Hecke algebra generated by the \(B_n\).

This proves a precise negative statement: the operator whose exterior traces are the Apéry coefficients is not one of the operators to which the Eichler--Selberg, Hijikata, or Brandt trace formulas apply.

### 5.2 Why a quaternionic theta lift does not repair this

Pizer's representability theorem lets one feed an arbitrary vector
\[
v=\sum_Pv(P)[P]
\]
into the Brandt theta construction. Taking, for example,
\[
v_{p,c}(P)=\psi_p(c\,t(P))
\]
packages a genuine supersingular coordinate sum into a modular form. But this is only a repackaging:

- \(v_{p,c}\) depends arbitrarily on \(p\) and \(c\);
- it is not a Hecke eigenvector;
- its spectral coefficients are the unknown quantities one hoped to estimate;
- Parseval on the Brandt module gives only \(\|v_{p,c}\|_2^2\asymp p\).

Moreover, \(R_p(c)\) is not the sum of \(\psi_p(c\,t(P))\) over supersingular points. It is an additive transform of the set of exterior degrees for which (4.3) vanishes. A theta lift of the point weights therefore addresses the wrong function even before the spectral-loss issue arises.

### 5.3 Audit table

| Proposed apparatus | Exact output at level 6 | Does it give \(R_p(c)\)? |
|---|---|---|
| Deuring mass/class numbers | all supersingular \(j\)'s or level-6 classes | No: \(Z_p\) is an index set |
| Optimal embeddings | Fricke-fixed CM \(-24\) points | Only the factor \(D(t)^{\epsilon_p}\) |
| Brandt matrices | norm-\(n\) isogeny counts | No: \(D_p\) is non-Hecke |
| Eichler--Selberg/Hijikata | traces of \(T_n\), \(W_dT_n\) | No exterior-degree zero projector |
| Quaternionic theta series | modular packaging of a chosen class-function vector | Formal only; no evaluation or cross-prime saving |
| Hasse/Deligne on the modular parameter | the root divisor of \(A_p(t)\) | No: coefficients are not roots |

## 6. The actual geometric interpretation of \(Z_p\): defining-characteristic Mellin traces

The repository's K3/Mellin construction gives, for the character
\[
\chi_r=\omega_p^r,
\]
a bounded-rank Mellin cohomology object \(C_{p,r}\) with
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid C_{p,r})
\equiv-b_r\pmod p.
\tag{6.1}
\]
The open-locus version has dimension six and the middle-extension version dimension two. Therefore
\[
r\in Z_p
\iff
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid C_{p,r})=0
\quad\text{in the defining residue field}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
This is a family on the **Mellin-character side**, not the original modular parameter line.

There are exact trace-like zero projectors, but each destroys bounded complexity. For example,
\[
\mathbf1_{b_r=0}
=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}\psi_p(ub_r),
\tag{6.3}
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
R_p(c)=
\frac{e_p(c/2)}p
\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}e_p(cr+ub_r).}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Alternatively, Fermat's theorem gives in \(\mathbf F_p\)
\[
\mathbf1_{b_r=0}=1-b_r^{p-1}.
\tag{6.5}
\]
If \(b_r\) is a rank-two Frobenius trace, then
\[
b_r^{p-1}
=\operatorname{Tr}\big(\operatorname{Frob}_p
\mid C_{p,r}^{\otimes(p-1)}\big),
\tag{6.6}
\]
a tensor power of rank \(2^{p-1}\). Equations (6.3)--(6.6) are exact, but they prove that the zero predicate is not represented by a fixed-rank sheaf or fixed automorphic representation.

Likewise, the supersingular formula (4.3) uses \(\wedge^rD_p\), whose dimension is
\[
\binom{p-1}{r}.
\]
Thus both the Mellin and supersingular realizations force rank or complexity growing with \(p\) when one inserts the predicate \(b_r=0\).

## 7. Why the known one-prime inputs do not yield the missing \(X\)

### 7.1 Reflection and Parseval

The reciprocal symmetry
\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p
\]
implies
\[
R_p(c)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(c(r+1/2))\in\mathbf R,
\qquad R_p(-c)=R_p(c).
\tag{7.1}
\]
It also gives the exact Parseval identity
\[
\sum_{c\bmod p}|R_p(c)|^2=pZ(p).
\tag{7.2}
\]
Neither (7.1) nor (7.2) controls the synchronized samples
\[
c=h(q\ell)^{-1}\pmod p.
\]
They average over a free frequency in one field, whereas the cubic term ties three frequencies to the same \(h\) and the same prime triple.

### 7.2 Why “Deligne gives square root” is invalid here

There are two possible objects, and neither satisfies the bounded-conductor hypothesis needed for a uniform square-root theorem.

1. For a genuine supersingular-coordinate sum, the zero-dimensional scheme has degree \(p-1\). Twisting its point set by an Artin--Schreier character gives cohomology of dimension \(\asymp p\). The general trace bound is then of order \(p\), not \(p^{1/2}\), unless additional structure is proved.

2. For the actual \(R_p(c)\), equations (6.4) or (6.6) give a family with \(p\) additive twists or rank \(2^{p-1}\). Interpolating \(r\mapsto b_r\) by a polynomial also has degree up to \(p-1\). Deligne's Weil II applies to a fixed-rank, fixed-conductor sheaf; those hypotheses have not been met.

Thus a bare citation to Weil II is not an estimate for \(R_p(c)\).

### 7.3 Bah--Shuddhodan: correct varying-characteristic theorem, wrong observable

Bah--Shuddhodan, arXiv:2608.00304, Corollary 1.4, permits varying finite-field characteristic. Its hypotheses are:

- uniformly bounded Sawin complexity;
- fixed tannakian dimension;
- purity of weight zero and arithmetic semisimplicity;
- equality of arithmetic and geometric tannakian groups;
- a common reductive tannakian group after conjugacy.

Its conclusion averages the unitary Frobenius classes over essentially all multiplicative characters \(\chi\) in each field. Even granting every hypothesis for the Apéry Mellin object, it does not imply the needed statement for two independent reasons.

- The condition in (6.2) is reduction modulo the same prime that is the base characteristic. Complex/unitary equidistribution does not detect divisibility of a nonzero algebraic trace by a prime over \(p\).
- \(R_p(c)\) is an additive Fourier coefficient of the exceptional character labels. Unweighted equidistribution over all \(\chi\) does not bound
  \[
  \sum_{r:\,\operatorname{Tr}C_{p,r}=0}e_p(cr).
  \]

### 7.4 Perret--Gentil's large sieve excludes the defining prime

Perret--Gentil, IMRN 2020, Theorem 2.7, fixes a base field of characteristic \(p\) and reduces a compatible \(\ell\)-adic family at auxiliary valuations. His Example 4.3 takes
\[
\Lambda_{0,p}=\{\lambda:\lambda\text{ does not lie above }p\}.
\]
Our event is reduction at a valuation above that same defining prime \(p\). Substituting \(\ell=p\) is not an admissible specialization. Varying the base prime does not change this: the forbidden auxiliary prime moves diagonally with the base characteristic.

### 7.5 A rigorous local-data countermodel

The failure of one-prime information can be proved without guessing the behavior of the Apéry sequence.

Let \(M=X^2\), choose an integer \(m_0\in[M/2,M)\), and for every prime \(p\in(X,2X]\) not dividing \(2m_0+1\), put
\[
Z_p^*=\{m_0\bmod p,\ p-1-(m_0\bmod p)\}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Then
\[
R_p^*(c)=2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi c(m_0+1/2)}p\right),
\tag{7.4}
\]
so
\[
|R_p^*(c)|\le2,
\qquad
\sum_c|R_p^*(c)|^2=2p.
\tag{7.5}
\]
This model has reflection, the strongest possible pointwise bound, and exact one-prime Fourier control. Yet every active prime marks the same integer \(m_0\). If \(L\asymp X/\log X\) is the number of active primes, then
\[
\sum_{m<M}(K_X^*(m))_3\ge(L)_3
\asymp\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{7.6}
\]
Meanwhile
\[
\lambda_X^*=\sum_{p\sim X}\frac{|Z_p^*|}{p}
\asymp\frac1{\log X},
\]
so the desired scale is
\[
M(\lambda_X^*)^3
\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{7.7}
\]
The model misses the target by exactly one factor \(X\).

This does not claim that the Apéry sets equal (7.3). It proves a logical statement: any argument whose arithmetic input is only one-prime cardinality, reflection, pointwise Fourier bounds, Parseval, or complete-frequency equidistribution cannot establish the cubic estimate. Some theorem coupling different defining characteristics is mandatory.

## 8. The precise cross-prime estimate that would close the centered cubic term

Let
\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
\qquad M=X^2,
\]
and
\[
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(p)}p.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Since \(p\asymp X\),
\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}Z(p)\asymp X\lambda_X.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For an ordered distinct triple \((p,q,\ell)\), put \(Q=pq\ell\). With the exact centered interval convention fixed in the repository, the fully nonzero Fourier contribution has the form
\[
\mathcal T_X=
\sum_{p,q,\ell\in\mathcal P_X}^{*}
\frac1Q
\sum_{1\le h\le(Q-1)/2}
K_{M,Q}(h)
R_p(h\overline{q\ell})
R_q(h\overline{p\ell})
R_\ell(h\overline{pq}),
\tag{8.3}
\]
where all inverses are taken in the corresponding prime field. In the centered normalization used after pairing \(h\) and \(-h\),
\[
K_{M,Q}(h)
=(-1)^h\frac{\sin(2\pi Mh/Q)}{\sin(\pi h/Q)};
\tag{8.4}
\]
with another interval origin, \((-1)^h\) is replaced by the explicitly determined unit phase, without changing the estimate below.

The kernel satisfies
\[
|K_{M,Q}(h)|\ll\min(M,Q/h).
\tag{8.5}
\]
For \(h\asymp X\), (8.2) and the trivial bound \(|R_p|\le Z(p)\) give
\[
\frac{M}{X^3}\cdot X\cdot
\left(\sum_{p\sim X}Z(p)\right)^3
\asymp X^3\lambda_X^3,
\tag{8.6}
\]
whereas the factorial-moment target is
\[
X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\tag{8.7}
\]
Thus the first effective frequency range is too large by exactly \(X\).

### 8.1 Minimal low-frequency estimate

A clean sufficient estimate for the critical block is
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{p,q,\ell\in\mathcal P_X}^{*}
\frac{X^3}{pq\ell}
\sum_{X<h\le2X}
\frac{K_{M,pq\ell}(h)}M
\prod_{v\in\{p,q,\ell\}}
R_v\!\left(h\overline{(pq\ell)/v}\right)
\right|
\ll X^{3+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.}
\tag{CP3-low}
\]
The absolute-value bound for the left side is \(X^{4+o(1)}\lambda_X^3\); hence (CP3-low) is exactly a factor-\(X\) cancellation. Multiplying by \(M/X^3=X^{-1}\) converts it into the required \(X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3\) contribution to (8.3).

### 8.2 Dyadic form sufficient for the complete kernel

For a dyadic \(1\le H\le4X^3\), put
\[
L_H=\min(X^2,X^3/H).
\]
A sufficient all-range statement is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{p,q,\ell\in\mathcal P_X}^{*}
\frac{X^3}{pq\ell}
\sum_{H<h\le2H}
\frac{K_{M,pq\ell}(h)}{L_H}
\prod_{v\in\{p,q,\ell\}}
R_v\!\left(h\overline{(pq\ell)/v}\right)
\right|\\
&\hspace{8em}\ll
X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3\max(X,H).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{CP3}
\]
The normalization \(K/L_H\) is uniformly bounded because \(pq\ell\asymp X^3\). Multiplying (CP3) by \(L_H/X^3\) gives
\[
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3
\]
for each dyadic block:

- if \(H\le X\), then \(L_H=X^2\) and \(\max(X,H)=X\);
- if \(H\ge X\), then \(L_H=X^3/H\) and \(\max(X,H)=H\).

The logarithmic number of blocks is absorbed into \(X^{o(1)}\). Together with the already separated zero- and mixed-frequency terms, (CP3) implies the desired third factorial-moment estimate.

This is the minimal genuinely new arithmetic statement exposed by the audit. It is not a one-prime estimate. It is a four-variable dispersion theorem coupling
\[
(p,q,\ell,h)
\]
through reciprocal arguments in three different defining characteristics.

No classical Brandt or Eichler--Selberg theorem has these quantifiers.

## 9. Merge-safe statements

The following four lemmas can be inserted into the Problem 3.2 manuscript without claiming the missing cross-prime theorem.

### Lemma A: exact supersingular divisor

For \(p\ge5\), the Franel polynomial
\[
K_p(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}\left(\sum_k\binom nk^3\right)x^n
\]
is the Hasse invariant of (2.1). Its \(p-1\) roots are the complete geometric supersingular locus of \(X_1(6)\simeq X_0(6)\), each with multiplicity one.

### Lemma B: exact Fricke quotient multiplicities

With
\[
t=x(1-8x)/(1+x),\qquad D(t)=t^2-34t+1,
\]
the roots of \(A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r\) are the Fricke orbits of the roots of \(K_p\). Nonfixed orbits give double roots. The two fixed CM \(-24\) points give simple roots exactly when \((-6/p)=-1\). Hence
\[
A_p(t)=D(t)^{(1-(-6/p))/2}B_p(t)^2.
\]

### Lemma C: coefficient/point separation

Let \(D_p\) be the diagonal operator (4.1) on the full supersingular level-6 module. Then
\[
A_p(T)=\det(1-TD_p),
\qquad
b_r=(-1)^r\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^rD_p).
\]
In particular,
\[
Z_p=\{r:\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^rD_p)=0\},
\]
so \(Z_p\) is not a subset of the supersingular class set.

### Lemma D: non-Hecke obstruction

For every Brandt operator \(B_n\),
\[
[D_p,B_n]_{P,Q}=(t(P)^{-1}-t(Q)^{-1})B_n(P,Q).
\]
Since the prime-to-\(6p\) Hecke orbit is the complete supersingular isogeny class and \(t\) is nonconstant, this commutator is nonzero for some \(n\). Therefore \(D_p\), \(\wedge^rD_p\), and the projector onto the vanishing exterior traces are not elements of the classical Brandt--Hecke algebra.

A conditional proposition may then state that (CP3) implies the centered fully nonzero part of \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

## 10. Final ruling

The strongest exact modular statement available is
\[
A_p(T)=\det(1-TD_p)
\]
on the supersingular level-6 module. The strongest exact classical trace statement available is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(W_6)=1-\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right),
\]
which explains the two CM branch roots and the factor \(t^2-34t+1\).

Neither statement turns
\[
R_p(c)=\sum_{r:\,\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^rD_p)=0}
e_p(c(r+1/2))
\]
into a class-number sum, a Brandt coefficient, a Hecke trace, or a bounded-rank trace function. The obstruction is structural, not a missing normalization:

- the modular parameter labels supersingular points;
- \(r\) labels exterior degree/Mellin character;
- the zero test is nonlinear and defining-characteristic;
- the reciprocal frequencies synchronize three different primes.

Accordingly, there is no justified route from the known Deuring, Brandt, Eichler--Selberg, quaternionic theta, Deligne, or all-character Mellin theorems to the missing factor \(X\). The next theorem to prove is (CP3-low), or preferably the dyadic form (CP3). Anything weaker that treats one prime at a time leaves the anchored-doublet obstruction and cannot establish \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).
