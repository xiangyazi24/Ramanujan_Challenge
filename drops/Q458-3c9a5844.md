# Q458 — The average-zero first moment: present theorem, logical role, and the missing monodromy input

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
z_p=|Z_p|,\qquad Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},\qquad
S(X)=\sum_{p\le X}z_p.
\]

The bounded-average statement

\[
\tag{M1} S(X)\ll \pi(X)\asymp \frac{X}{\log X}
\]

is **open**. I found no published theorem, and no theorem in the current repository, improving the exponent obtained by summing the proved pointwise zero-set bound. The present unconditional result is

\[
\boxed{
S(X)\ll \frac{X^{5/3}}{\log X}.}
\]

Using the explicit constant in `problems/3.2/proof.tex`, one may write more precisely

\[
S(X)\le
\left(\frac{3^{7/3}}{10}+o(1)\right)
\frac{X^{5/3}}{\log X}.
\]

Thus there is currently not even a proof of the weaker statement

\[
S(X)=o(X),
\]

which the repository calls `[AVG-ZERO]` and which is the natural Cesàro statement for the top-half channel.

There are, however, three important corrections to the premise of the question.

1. **(M1) is not logically prior to every possible second-moment proof.** It is needed if one insists that the diagonal have the conjectural random scale `O(N)`. A centered second-moment theorem at the larger diagonal scale allowed by the proved bound `z_p\ll p^{2/3}` would still imply all-index P3.2.
2. **(M1) alone does not control the CRT boundary errors.** Even under (M1), the absolute estimate
   \[
   \sum_{p\ne q}O(z_pz_q)
   \le \left(\sum_pz_p\right)^2
   \ll \frac{N^2}{(\log N)^2}
   \]
   is far larger than `N`. The off-diagonal still requires signed cancellation.
3. **(M1) is not equivalent to the average number of primes in `(sqrt n,n]` dividing `b_n`.** Its exact prime-factor interpretation counts primes **larger than the coefficient index**:
   \[
   \boxed{
   S(X)=\sum_{r<X}\#\{p:r<p\le X,\ p\mid b_r\}.}
   \]

The correct hierarchy is therefore

```text
z_p << p^(2/3)                           proved
S(X) = o(X)                              open; average top-half target
S(X) << X/log X                          open; bounded mean / Poisson scale
signed two-prime dispersion              open; horizontal target
all-n P3.2                               open
```

The repository's `SUBMIT/3.2/FINAL_AUDIT.md` also corrects an older campaign note: the unconditional **almost-all** theorem can be obtained by a repaired codegree argument without first proving `[AVG-ZERO]`. So (M1) is a major independent target, but not a prerequisite for the already claimed density-one result.

## 1. What exactly does (M1) say?

By reversing the two finite sums,

\[
\begin{aligned}
S(X)
&=\sum_{p\le X}\sum_{0\le r<p}\mathbf1_{p\mid b_r}\\
&=\sum_{0\le r<X}
  \sum_{r<p\le X}\mathbf1_{p\mid b_r}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since `b_0=1`, the `r=0` term vanishes. Thus

\[
\tag{1.1}
S(X)=\sum_{1\le r<X}\omega_{(r,X]}(b_r),
\]

where `omega_(r,X]` counts distinct prime divisors in that interval.

This is a triangular, cross-characteristic statistic: the integer `b_r` is fixed first, and one asks for its prime divisors `p` larger than `r`. It is not the same statistic as

\[
\omega_{(\sqrt r,r]}(b_r).
\]

### Relation to the top-half average

For

\[
\operatorname{TOP}(n)=
\#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n\},
\]

write `n=p+r`. For `p>n/2`, one has `0\le r<p`, and the Apéry Lucas congruence gives, for `p\ne5`,

\[
b_{p+r}\equiv b_1b_r=5b_r\pmod p.
\]

Hence

\[
\tag{1.2}
\sum_{n\le X}\operatorname{TOP}(n)
=
\#\{(p,r):r\in Z_p,\ r\ge1,\ p+r\le X\}
+O(1).
\]

If `p\le X/2`, every `r<p` satisfies `p+r<X`; conversely every pair on the right has `p\le X`. Therefore the assertions

\[
S(X)=o(X)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sum_{n\le X}\operatorname{TOP}(n)=o(X)
\]

are equivalent after harmless rescaling of `X`. The sharper bound (M1) gives an `O(X/log X)` first moment for this top-half count.

This explains why `[AVG-ZERO]` is valuable. It does **not** make it equivalent to the pointwise all-`n` theorem, and it sees only an averaged incidence channel.

# (A) Is (M1) currently provable? What is the best unconditional bound?

## A.1 The proved pointwise theorem

The gap-polynomial argument in the current manuscript gives, for every prime `p>=7` and every integer `2<=H<=p`,

\[
\tag{A.1}
z_p
\le
\left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil
+\frac32(H-1)(H-2)+O(1).
\]

The mechanism is:

- partition `[0,p-1]` into blocks of length `H`;
- charge the first zero in each block to the number of blocks;
- if two zeros have gap `h<H`, their starting index is a root of the nonzero gap polynomial `N_h`, whose degree is `3(h-1)`;
- sum the degree budgets over `2<=h<H`.

Optimizing at `H\asymp(p/3)^{1/3}` gives

\[
\tag{A.2}
\boxed{
z_p\le \frac{3^{4/3}}2p^{2/3}+O(p^{1/3}).}
\]

This is presently the strongest unconditional theorem in the repository that applies to every prime.

## A.2 Summing the pointwise theorem

From (A.2),

\[
S(X)
\le \frac{3^{4/3}}2\sum_{p\le X}p^{2/3}
+O\left(\sum_{p\le X}p^{1/3}\right).
\]

The prime number theorem and partial summation give

\[
\sum_{p\le X}p^{2/3}
\sim \frac35\frac{X^{5/3}}{\log X},
\qquad
\sum_{p\le X}p^{1/3}
\ll \frac{X^{4/3}}{\log X}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\tag{A.3}
\boxed{
S(X)\le
\left(\frac{3^{7/3}}{10}+o(1)\right)
\frac{X^{5/3}}{\log X}.}
\]

The coarser statement `S(X)\ll X^(5/3)/log X` does not require the prime number theorem; Chebyshev's bound is enough.

I found no unconditional power improvement on (A.3) in the Apéry literature or in the current repository. `problems/3.2/campaign3_questions/q81_avgzero.txt` records the same barrier, and `SUBMIT/3.2/FINAL_AUDIT.md` states that the campaign obtained no exponent improvement.

## A.3 Why (M1) is not logically required before every variance target

Fix an interval `I` of length comparable to `N`, and use a dyadic envelope of primes `sqrt(N)<p<=2N`. Put

\[
X_p(n)=\mathbf1_{n\bmod p\in Z_p},
\qquad
g_p(n)=X_p(n)-\frac{z_p}{p}.
\]

The known pointwise theorem alone gives

\[
\tag{A.4}
\mu_N:=\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le2N}\frac{z_p}{p}
\ll \frac{N^{2/3}}{\log N}
=o\left(\frac N{\log N}\right).
\]

It also gives the diagonal variance scale

\[
\tag{A.5}
\Delta_N
:=\sum_p\sum_{n\in I}|g_p(n)|^2
\ll
\sum_p\left(\frac{Nz_p}{p}+z_p\right)
\ll \frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}.
\]

Suppose one could prove the **centered signed-dispersion estimate**

\[
\tag{A.6}
\sum_{n\in I}
\left|\sum_pg_p(n)\right|^2
\ll N^{o(1)}\Delta_N.
\]

Then, by the trivial `L^2`-to-`L^infinity` inequality,

\[
\max_{n\in I}D_a(n)
\le
\mu_N+N^{o(1)}\Delta_N^{1/2}
\ll
\frac{N^{2/3}}{\log N}+N^{5/6+o(1)}
=o\left(\frac N{\log N}\right).
\]

After the routine dyadic decomposition of the moving prime window, this is enough for all-`n` P3.2.

Thus:

> (M1) is needed to put the diagonal on its conjectural `O(N)` scale, but a covariance theorem at the larger proved diagonal scale would already suffice.

Under (M1), partial summation gives `mu_N=O(1)` and (A.5) improves to `Delta_N=O(N)`. That is an important simplification, not a logical necessity.

Finally, (M1) does not prove (A.6). Taking absolute values of the incomplete-period CRT errors gives only

\[
O\left((\sum_pz_p)^2\right)
=O\left(\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^2}\right),
\]

so the horizontal signed cancellation remains essential.

# (B) What does the Ahlgren–Ono modular congruence contribute?

Ahlgren and Ono proved the central supercongruence

\[
\tag{B.1}
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod{p^2},
\qquad
f_8(z)=\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4.
\]

The form `f_8` is the rational, non-CM weight-4 newform of level `8` (LMFDB label `8.4.a.a`). Together with the reflection

\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p,
\]

this splits the zero count as

\[
\tag{B.2}
z_p=2y_p+c_p,
\qquad
c_p=\mathbf1_{p\mid a_p(f_8)},
\]

where `y_p` is the number of noncentral reflected zero pairs.

The central contribution is already harmless for (M1), without any density theorem:

\[
\tag{B.3}
\sum_{p\le X}c_p\le\pi(X)\ll\frac X{\log X}.
\]

Therefore all of the difficulty in (M1) lies in proving

\[
\sum_{p\le X}y_p\ll\pi(X).
\]

Information about the single central character cannot control those moving noncentral characters.

## A correction concerning “Serre's theorem”

For a weight-2 elliptic modular form, the Hasse bound implies that `p|a_p` essentially forces `a_p=0` for large `p`, and classical results on supersingular primes are relevant. For a weight-4 form,

\[
|a_p(f_8)|\le2p^{3/2},
\]

so `p|a_p` allows `O(sqrt p)` different multiples of `p`. Weak Sato–Tate equidistribution does not resolve this moving-lattice event. Density-one ordinarity for such higher-weight motives is part of the general ordinarity conjectural landscape; it is not an immediate consequence of Serre's classical theorem.

Accordingly, the safe statement is:

> The central zero is the nonordinary-prime condition for `f_8`; its observed rarity is compelling, but no result about this one slice supplies an estimate for all of `Z_p`.

Even granting the strongest possible statement `c_p=0` for all but finitely many primes would change `S(X)` only by at most `pi(X)`.

# (C) Can known Apéry-number technology bound the relevant prime factors?

## C.1 The claimed equivalence with medium prime factors is false

It is true that, for `p>sqrt(n)`, the Apéry Lucas law implies

\[
n\bmod p\in Z_p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_n.
\]

Hence

\[
D_a(n)\le\omega_{(\sqrt n,n]}(b_n).
\]

But this is only a one-way inequality. If `n=qp+r`, then

\[
b_n\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p;
\]

`p|b_n` may come from the leading digit `q`, whereas `D_a(n)` records only the trailing digit `r`.

More importantly, (1.1) shows that (M1) averages

\[
\omega_{(r,X]}(b_r),
\]

not `omega_(sqrt r,r](b_r)`. It is a large-prime-above-the-index problem produced by swapping the top-half incidence sum.

## C.2 What Luca–Shparlinski proves

Luca and Shparlinski prove, for a density-one set of indices `n`, lower bounds of the form

\[
\omega(b_n)>c\log\log\log n,
\qquad
P(b_n)>c(\log n\log\log n)^{1/2}.
\]

These are valuable results about the existence of prime factors. They are in the opposite direction from (M1): the largest-prime lower bound is far below the moving linear threshold `p>n`, and neither theorem upper-bounds the number of prime divisors in a prescribed interval.

## C.3 What Delaygue/Dwork/supercongruence technology proves

Delaygue's Apéry-like theory gives lower bounds for `p`-adic valuations, proves Beukers-type valuation conjectures, and provides criteria for the `p`-Lucas property. Ahlgren–Ono and related supercongruence results identify special truncations with modular coefficients. Rhin–Viola and Zudilin-type methods control rational approximations, denominators, or special congruences.

None of these results gives a uniform upper bound for

\[
\#\{p:r<p\le X,\ p\mid b_r\}
\]

on average over `r`. The obstruction is not estimating a valuation once a prime is known to divide; it is proving that the first divisibility event is rare as both `r` and the residue characteristic move.

## C.4 “Well-distributed sequence” theorems do not apply for free

Recent work of Bharadwaj–Rodgers proves Poisson–Dirichlet laws for large prime factors of arithmetic sequences satisfying positive or full levels of distribution. Such theorems explain what one should expect **after** suitable residue-class equidistribution has been established.

For the Apéry sequence, verifying the required level of distribution would demand estimates for congruences `b_r≡0 mod d` uniformly over many moduli `d`. That is at least as hard as the average-zero problem and, for products of two large primes, meets the same cross-prime issue. It is therefore a useful template, not an off-the-shelf proof of (M1).

### Verdict on (C)

There is currently no Delaygue/Zudilin/Rhin–Viola theorem that yields (M1) or an upper bound of the required type for medium/large prime factors of Apéry numbers.

# (D) What can be proved for a general exponentially growing sequence?

## D.1 Growth alone reaches exactly the trivial barrier

If `log|a_n|\le Cn`, then

\[
\omega_{(\sqrt n,n]}(a_n)
\le \frac{\log|a_n|}{\log\sqrt n}
\ll \frac n{\log n}.
\]

There is no general way to replace this by `o(n/log n)`.

Indeed, the artificial sequence

\[
a_n=\prod_{\sqrt n<p\le n}p
\]

satisfies `log a_n\sim n` but has

\[
\omega_{(\sqrt n,n]}(a_n)
\sim\frac n{\log n}.
\]

Multiplying by a suitable power of `2` can match any prescribed larger exponential growth constant without changing the medium-prime count.

## D.2 The obstruction already occurs for a classical holonomic sequence

For the central binomial coefficients

\[
c_n=\binom{2n}{n},
\]

Legendre's formula gives, for every prime

\[
n/2<p\le2n/3,
\]

\[
v_p(c_n)
=\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{p}\right\rfloor
 -2\left\lfloor\frac n p\right\rfloor
=3-2=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\omega_{(\sqrt n,n]}(c_n)
\ge \pi(2n/3)-\pi(n/2)
\asymp\frac n{\log n}.
\]

So even “integral, hypergeometric, algebraic generating function, holonomic, Lucas congruences” does not imply a subcritical medium-prime count.

For the corresponding row statistic, Kummer's carry criterion gives exactly

\[
Z'_p=\{r:(p+1)/2\le r\le p-1\},
\qquad
|Z'_p|=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

and hence

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z'_p|
\sim\frac{X^2}{4\log X}.
\]

## D.3 General holonomic prime-divisor theorems point the other way

Results on binary holonomic sequences prove lower bounds for the number of distinct primes dividing the product of many terms. Primitive-divisor theorems for Lucas or elliptic divisibility sequences use strong divisibility and constant-coefficient algebraic-group structure absent from the Apéry recurrence.

They do not furnish upper bounds for a moving prime window in one P-recursive sequence.

### Verdict on (D)

There is no general theorem saying that an exponentially growing integer sequence has `o(n/log n)` medium prime factors. Such a theorem is false even for central binomial coefficients. Any proof for Apéry must use a genuine anti-concentration feature special to its mod-`p` dynamics.

# (E) How would “big monodromy” have to enter?

## E.1 What the central-binomial comparison really shows

Both the central binomial and Apéry sequences have:

- hypergeometric descriptions;
- algebraic/diagonal generating functions modulo `p`;
- automatic digit laws;
- Lucas congruences.

The central-binomial zero interval is forced by a simple carry law. Apéry has no observed macroscopic carry interval and instead exhibits reflected Poisson-scale zeros. The missing theorem must distinguish these two mod-`p` dynamical systems quantitatively.

It is better to say that the proof must consume **anti-concentration arising from big monodromy**, rather than merely cite “infinite monodromy.”

## E.2 A new 2026 theorem gives a concrete monodromy shadow—but not (M1)

Caruso, Fürnsinn, Vargas-Montoya and Zudilin compute the Galois group of the Apéry generating series reduced modulo `p`. It is the subgroup of squares in `F_p^*` for

```text
p = 1,5,7,11 mod 24,
```

and all of `F_p^*` for

```text
p = 13,17,19,23 mod 24.
```

Equivalently, for the truncation

\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r,
\]

they prove

\[
A_p(t)=B_p(t)^2
\]

in the first congruence classes, and

\[
A_p(t)=(t^2-34t+1)B_p(t)^2
\]

in the second.

This is a striking algebraic manifestation of the large/infinite-monodromy nature of the Apéry series: the mod-`p` extension degree grows like `p`, unlike the fixed quadratic degree for `(1-4t)^(-1/2)`.

But it does not bound the number of **zero coefficients** of `A_p(t)`. A polynomial square may be sparse, dense, or have many vanishing coefficients. Field-extension degree and coefficient anti-concentration are different invariants. Thus this 2026 theorem supplies exactly the structural discriminator requested in (E), but no implication to (M1) is presently known.

## E.3 The precise theorem monodromy would have to produce

The repository's Kummer/Mellin interpretation identifies `b_r mod p` with a Hasse-invariant/residual Frobenius-trace quantity in a character family. The zero indicator can be written

\[
\tag{E.1}
\mathbf1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}
=\frac1p\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}e_p(tb_r).
\]

Summing over `r` gives

\[
\tag{E.2}
z_p
=1+\frac1p\sum_{t\ne0}
  \sum_{r=0}^{p-1}e_p(tb_r).
\]

Therefore a route from monodromy to (M1) must prove **aggregate cancellation at the defining-characteristic scale**, for example

\[
\tag{E.3}
\sum_{p\le X}\frac1p
\sum_{t\ne0}\sum_{r<p}e_p(tb_r)
\ll \pi(X),
\]

or an equivalent factorial-moment/local-limit theorem for the Hasse-zero labels.

Standard Deligne–Katz equidistribution controls fixed continuous class functions or bounded-complexity trace functions. The test in (E.1) depends on `p` and resolves one residue class at the microscopic mod-`p` scale. In the most direct Artin–Schreier realization, the complexity/conductor grows with `p`; the ordinary square-root theorem is therefore not uniform enough to see an expected `O(1)` zero count.

A successful monodromy program would need four ingredients:

1. **Character-aspect geometric object.** Package the values `b_r mod p` as Hasse invariants or residual traces of the Kummer-twist family.
2. **Big product monodromy.** Rule out fixed relations among distinct character labels and, eventually, across distinct residue characteristics.
3. **Defining-characteristic local limit.** Prove that exact divisibility by `p`, not merely normalized complex trace distribution, has probability `O(1/p)` on average over labels.
4. **Horizontal dispersion.** For all-index P3.2, prove signed two-prime cancellation in the incomplete CRT intervals. Vertical (M1) alone cannot exclude an anchored family `Z_p={n_0 mod p}`.

Ingredient 3 would prove (M1). Ingredient 4 is still needed for the pointwise theorem.

This is the honest way big monodromy must be “consumed”: not as a qualitative group label, but as a uniform local-limit and product-equidistribution theorem whose error is smaller than a constant-sized main term.

# Direct answers

## (A)

**No proof of (M1) is currently available.** The best unconditional bound I found is

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|
\ll \frac{X^{5/3}}{\log X},
\]

obtained by summing the proved pointwise estimate `|Z_p|\ll p^(2/3)`. No unconditional exponent improvement is recorded in the current repository or in the published Apéry literature located in this audit.

(M1) is required for an `O(N)` random-scale diagonal, but not logically for every centered second-moment route. It also leaves the signed off-diagonal cancellation open.

## (B)

The Ahlgren–Ono congruence controls exactly the fixed midpoint. By palindromy,

\[
|Z_p|=2\times(\text{number of noncentral pairs})
+\mathbf1_{p\mid a_p(f_8)}.
\]

The second term contributes at most `pi(X)` even with no theorem on nonordinary-prime density, so it is already harmless for (M1). It gives no control of the noncentral pairs. The assertion that density zero follows directly from Serre is not valid in this weight-4 moving-modulus setting.

## (C)

(M1) is exactly an average count of prime divisors `p>r` of `b_r`, not an average of primes in `(sqrt r,r]`. Delaygue, Ahlgren–Ono, Luca–Shparlinski, Rhin–Viola and Zudilin-type results do not provide the needed upper bound. Existing Apéry prime-factor results are primarily lower bounds or special congruences.

## (D)

There is no general growth-based theorem forcing the medium-prime contribution to be `o(n/log n)`. Central binomial coefficients are a hypergeometric holonomic counterexample with `asymp n/log n` medium prime factors. General holonomic and primitive-divisor results do not apply in the required direction.

## (E)

The central-binomial/Apéry contrast strongly suggests that an Apéry proof must use monodromy-driven anti-concentration. The newly computed large mod-`p` Galois groups of the Apéry generating series are important structural evidence, but they do not control zero coefficients. The missing theorem is a defining-characteristic local-limit theorem for the Kummer-character/Hasse-invariant family; all-index P3.2 additionally needs two-prime CRT dispersion.

---

# Recommended priority

The most economical proof program is to keep two targets separate.

### Vertical target V1

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|\ll \pi(X).}
\]

This would establish the correct bounded first moment and sharpen the average theory. A weaker first breakthrough

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|=o(X)
\]

would already settle `[AVG-ZERO]`.

### Horizontal target H2

For centered indicators `g_p`, prove on every relevant dyadic interval

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{n\in I}g_p(n)g_q(n)
\right|
\ll N^{o(1)}
\sum_p\sum_{n\in I}|g_p(n)|^2.}
\]

H2 plus the already proved `z_p\ll p^(2/3)` is enough for all-index P3.2, even before V1. V1 would reduce H2 to its natural random scale and is likely the cleaner first arithmetic theorem.

So the final diagnosis is:

> (M1) is a major open vertical local-limit problem and the sharp first-moment conjecture, but it is neither the sole missing ingredient nor logically prior to every viable second-moment proof.
