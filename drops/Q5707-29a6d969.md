# Q5707: exact cross-model shell computation, mutation, and height audit

## Executive verdict

The computation gives a much sharper picture than the premise of Q5707.

1. The two normalized models are connected by a genuine Laurent-mutation chain, but the compact map in Q3051 must first be conjugated by the final `x <-> z` monomial chart. The unpermuted identification rejected in Q5702 remains false. For the displayed Gorodetsky model the correct composite map is
   \[
   T(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{x+y}{z},\frac1{y+z},\frac yx\right),
   \qquad
   \Lambda(T(x,y,z))=\Phi(x,y,z).
   \]

2. For the hostile row `M=320,d0=161,L=50` (`n=M+1=321`),
   \[
   \gcd(G_\Lambda,G_\Phi)=179\cdot193\cdot211=7,289,417.
   \]
   Thus the unrestricted two-model gcd is exactly the target product in this row.

3. The origin-free difference
   \[
   D=G_\Lambda-G_\Phi
   \]
   is **not** merely target-divisible. It is divisible by every candidate prime in the block:
   \[
   P=163\cdot167\cdot173\cdot179\cdot181\cdot191\cdot193\cdot197\cdot199\cdot211
    =46,523,697,007,211,181,623,993,
   \]
   and
   \[
   \gcd(D,P)=P.
   \]
   This follows structurally because the two shells have the same Cartier residue at every candidate node, not only at target nodes.

4. The common-origin cancellation does not improve the Archimedean scale: `G_Lambda` and `D` both have 529 decimal digits and 1757 bits. Numerically
   \[
   \log|D|/M=3.8053109901\ldots.
   \]
   The cancellation removes the literal coefficient `b_M`, but it does not remove the dominant noncentral shell mass.

5. A cover-preserving three-stencil gcd produces the sharpest explicit residual found:
   \[
   \gcd(D_{161,50},D_{161,51},D_{160,51})
   =382,974,141,213,537,062,581,838,569,344
   \]
   and it factors completely as
   \[
   2^7\cdot3\cdot13\cdot17\cdot97\cdot P.
   \]
   Hence adjacent stencils collapse the huge `Theta(M)` Archimedean part, but the full candidate primorial survives. This yields height `Theta(H)` when the block width is `Theta(H)`, not `o(H)`.

6. Taking the gcd of all six model carriers from the three cover-preserving stencils is empirically much smaller. In the five audited rows it is the target product except for one extra factor `5` at `n=200`. However, no small-height characteristic-zero Bezout/resultant certificate was found. The numerical gcd is therefore a promising target-blind selector, not yet an Archimedean theorem.

The requested `o(H)` carrier is therefore **not obtained**. The smallest explicit cross-model obstruction is the candidate primorial itself: mutation plus adjacent-stencil gcd reduces the residual to `P` times a small factor, but does not remove `P` without target selection.

### 1.1 Residue-binomial notation

Put
\[
R(N;r,d)=\sum_{\substack{0\le k\le N\\k\equiv r\pmod d}}\binom Nk.
\]

### 1.2 Section 48 model

For
\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz},
\]
the one-fold coefficient formula gives
\[
C_M^\Lambda(d)
 =\sum_{t=0}^M
 \binom Mt\,R(M;t,d)\,R(2M-t;M,d)^2.
\]

The natural second residue is `M-t`; the displayed `M` is exactly equivalent by the involution
\(k\mapsto(2M-t)-k\) and binomial symmetry.

### 1.3 Gorodetsky model

For
\[
\Phi(x,y,z)=
\frac{(x+y)(1+z)(x+y+z)(1+y+z)}{xyz},
\]
expand the four numerator factors. Let:

- `R` be the non-`x` degree in `(x+y+z)^M`;
- `i` be the `x` degree in `(x+y)^M`;
- `beta` be the `y` degree in the `R`-block;
- `delta` be the `y` degree in `(1+y+z)^M`;
- `k` be the combined `z` degree from `(1+z)^M` and the remaining fourth factor.

The three shell congruences are
\[
i\equiv R,\qquad
\beta+\delta\equiv i,\qquad
k\equiv M-R+\beta\pmod d.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_M^\Phi(d)
={}&\sum_{R=0}^M\binom MR
 \sum_{\substack{0\le i\le M\\i\equiv R\ (d)}}\binom Mi
 \sum_{\beta=0}^R\binom R\beta \\
&\qquad\times
 \sum_{\substack{0\le\delta\le M\\\delta\equiv i-\beta\ (d)}}
 \binom M\delta\,
 R(2M-\delta;M-R+\beta,d).
\end{aligned}}
\]

For the hostile range `d>M/2`, each residue sum has only a few terms, so no Laurent expansion is needed.

### 1.4 Primitive Newton carrier

For `d_i=d0+i`,
\[
\omega_i=(-1)^i
 \binom{d_0+i}{i}
 \binom{d_0+L+1}{L-i},
\qquad 0\le i\le L.
\]
The implementation checks
\[
\sum_i\omega_i=1,
\qquad
\gcd_i(\omega_i)=1.
\]
Then
\[
G_F(d_0,L)=\sum_{i=0}^L\omega_i C_M^F(d_0+i).
\]

Because both carriers use weights summing to one,
\[
G_F=b_M+\sum_i\omega_i(C_M^F(d_i)-b_M),
\]
so
\[
D=G_\Lambda-G_\Phi
 =\sum_i\omega_i(C_M^\Lambda(d_i)-C_M^\Phi(d_i))
\]
is exactly origin-free.

## 4. Why `D` contains every candidate prime

Let `p=d_i+1` be any candidate prime and put
\[
r=M-d_i=n-p.
\]
The common Cartier congruence for the two normalized Apéry models is
\[
C_M^\Lambda(d_i)\equiv b_r\pmod p,
\qquad
C_M^\Phi(d_i)\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\]
The primitive Newton weights satisfy
\[
\omega_i\equiv1\pmod p,
\qquad
\omega_j\equiv0\pmod p\quad(j\ne i).
\]
Consequently
\[
G_\Lambda\equiv C_M^\Lambda(d_i)\equiv b_r\pmod p,
\]
\[
G_\Phi\equiv C_M^\Phi(d_i)\equiv b_r\pmod p,
\]
and therefore
\[
D=G_\Lambda-G_\Phi\equiv0\pmod p
\]
for **every** candidate prime, whether or not `p|b_r`.

This separates the two mechanisms:

- `sum omega_i=1` makes the characteristic-zero carrier origin-free;
- equality of the two Cartier residues forces the entire candidate primorial into `D`.

The second mechanism is precisely why the cross-model difference cannot itself be a target selector.

## 6. Genuine Laurent mutation

### 6.1 The mutation chain

Write the Section 48 model as `Lambda_1(X,Y,Z)`. The verified chain is
\[
X=v(1+u)(1+w),\qquad Y=u^{-1},\qquad Z=w^{-1},
\]
which gives
\[
F_B(u,v,w)=
\frac{(1+u)(1+w)(1+v)(1+(1+u)(1+w)v)}{uvw}.
\]

Apply the elementary mutation `v_old=v/(1+u)`:
\[
F_C(u,v,w)=
\frac{(1+u)(1+w)(1+u+v)(1+v+vw)}{uvw}.
\]

Apply the elementary mutation `u_old=u(1+v)` and cancel the common factor `1+v`:
\[
F_A(u,v,w)=
\frac{(1+u)(1+w)(1+u+uv)(1+v+vw)}{uvw}.
\]

Finally use the unimodular monomial chart
\[
u=x,\qquad v=y/x,\qquad w=z/y.
\]
Then
\[
\Lambda_2(x,y,z)=
\frac{(y+z)(1+x)(1+x+y)(x+y+z)}{xyz}.
\]

The displayed Gorodetsky model is the coordinate permutation
\[
\Phi(x,y,z)=\Lambda_2(z,y,x).
\]
Therefore the genuine composite pullback for the displayed `Phi` is
\[
\boxed{
X=\frac{x+y}{z},\qquad
Y=\frac1{y+z},\qquad
Z=\frac yx.}
\]

The compact unpermuted map from Q3051 lands in `Lambda_2`, not directly in the displayed `Phi`; identifying those two without the final `x <-> z` chart is exactly the false pullback rejected in Q5702.

### 6.2 Direct symbolic verification

For
\[
X=(x+y)/z,\qquad Y=1/(y+z),\qquad Z=y/x,
\]
one has
\[
1+X=\frac{x+y+z}{z},
\qquad
1+Y=\frac{1+y+z}{y+z},
\qquad
1+Z=\frac{x+y}{x},
\]
\[
XYZ=\frac{y(x+y)}{xz(y+z)},
\]
and
\[
(1+Y)(1+Z)+XYZ
=\frac{(x+y)(1+z)}{xz}.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda(X,Y,Z)
&=\frac{(x+y)(1+z)(x+y+z)(1+y+z)}{xyz}\\
&=\Phi(x,y,z).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is an identity in the rational function field, not a numerical coincidence.

## 7. Exact torsion-shell correction

For either Laurent polynomial `F`, root-of-unity filtering gives
\[
C_M^F(d)=\frac1{d^3}
\sum_{(x,y,z)\in\mu_d^3}F(x,y,z)^M.
\]

The mutation map
\[
T(x,y,z)=((x+y)/z,1/(y+z),y/x)
\]
does **not** preserve `mu_d^3`. There are two exceptional divisors on the torsion grid:
\[
A_d=\{(x,y,z)\in\mu_d^3:x+y=0\},
\]
where the first image coordinate is zero and leaves the algebraic torus, and
\[
B_d=\{(x,y,z)\in\mu_d^3:y+z=0\},
\]
where the second image coordinate has a pole.

Let
\[
E_d=A_d\cup B_d,
\qquad
\Omega_d=\mu_d^3\setminus E_d.
\]
For odd `d`, `E_d` is empty. For even `d`,
\[
|A_d|=|B_d|=d^2,
\qquad
|A_d\cap B_d|=d,
\qquad
|E_d|=2d^2-d.
\]

For every `M>0`, the exact signed correction is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
d^3\bigl(C_M^\Phi(d)-C_M^\Lambda(d)\bigr)
={}&\sum_{\xi\in\Omega_d}
  \bigl(\Lambda(T\xi)^M-\Lambda(\xi)^M\bigr)\\
&+\sum_{\xi\in E_d}
  \bigl(\Phi(\xi)^M-\Lambda(\xi)^M\bigr).
\end{aligned}}
\]

This includes both the regular-grid defect and all singular torsion points. The only global arithmetic denominator is the root-filter normalization `d^3`. The rational identity `Lambda o T=Phi` cancels the coordinate denominators completely, but the map itself is not defined on `E_d` and does not send the regular torsion grid to a torsion grid.

### 7.1 Explicit singular contribution on `B_d`

On `B_d`, write `y=-z`. Then
\[
\Phi(x,-z,z)=\frac{(z-x)(1+z)}{z^2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{\xi\in B_d}\Phi(\xi)^M
=d^2\sum_{a,b=0}^M
(-1)^a\binom Ma\binom Mb
\mathbf 1_{d\mid a}
\mathbf 1_{d\mid(b-a-M)}.
\]

Also
\[
\Lambda(x,-z,z)
=-\frac{(1+x)(1-z^2)(1-(1+x)z^2)}{xz^2},
\]
so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\xi\in B_d}\Lambda(\xi)^M
={}&d^2
\sum_{b,c=0}^M\sum_{a=0}^{M+c}
(-1)^{M+b+c}
\binom Mb\binom Mc\binom{M+c}{a}\\
&\quad\times
\mathbf 1_{d\mid(a-M)}
\mathbf 1_{d\mid2(b+c-M)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

### 7.2 Exact coefficient form for the full exceptional set

Write
\[
\Lambda^M=\sum_{u,v,w}c_M^\Lambda(u,v,w)x^uy^vz^w.
\]
Then
\[
S_A^\Lambda
=d^2\sum_{u,v,w}
(-1)^u c_M^\Lambda(u,v,w)
\mathbf 1_{d\mid(u+v)}
\mathbf 1_{d\mid w},
\]
\[
S_B^\Lambda
=d^2\sum_{u,v,w}
(-1)^v c_M^\Lambda(u,v,w)
\mathbf 1_{d\mid u}
\mathbf 1_{d\mid(v+w)},
\]
and
\[
S_{A\cap B}^\Lambda
=d\sum_{u,v,w}
(-1)^v c_M^\Lambda(u,v,w)
\mathbf 1_{d\mid(u+v+w)}.
\]

Since `Phi=0` on `A_d` for `M>0`, the complete singular correction is
\[
\sum_{\xi\in E_d}(\Phi(\xi)^M-\Lambda(\xi)^M)
=S_B^\Phi-igl(S_A^\Lambda+S_B^\Lambda-S_{A\cap B}^\Lambda\bigr),
\]
with `S_B^Phi` given by the explicit double sum above.

This is the exact mutation correction requested in part 3. It is not a width-only correction: the regular term contains `Theta(d^3)` non-torsion image evaluations raised to the `M`th power, and the singular terms themselves contain binomial sums of order `M`.

\]
and
\[
|\Phi(x,y,z)|\le2\cdot2\cdot3\cdot3=36.
\]
Therefore
\[
|C_M^\Lambda(d)|\le40^M,
\qquad
|C_M^\Phi(d)|\le36^M.
\]

The exact beta-integral expression for the absolute Newton-weight sum gives
\[
\sum_{i=0}^L|\omega_i|
=\binom{d_0+L+1}{L}(d_0+1)
 \int_0^1 t^{d_0}(1+t)^L\,dt
\le2^L\binom{d_0+L+1}{L}.
\]
Consequently
\[
|D|
\le(40^M+36^M)
 2^L\binom{d_0+L+1}{L},
\]
so
\[
\log|D|\le M\log40+O(L\log M).
\]

The exact rows show this upper scale is qualitatively sharp: `log(abs(D))` is linear in `M`, and common-origin cancellation does not lower it to the block scale.

The adjacent-stencil gcd does remove the linear-in-`M` bulk, but leaves the full candidate primorial. If the candidate interval has width `L=Theta(H)`, then
\[
\log P=\Theta(L)=\Theta(H)
\]
on the ordinary prime-density scale. Thus the explicit residual `K_D=P*S` is a `Theta(H)`-height object, not an `o(H)` object.

The all-six gcd `K_6` is much smaller numerically, but no controlled-height identity was found that forces this. Computing a gcd of enormous evaluated integers is target-blind, but its small observed value is not yet an Archimedean proof; a bounded Bezout/resultant/holonomic certificate is still missing.
