# Audit of the affine all-quotient carrier route

## Verdict

The requested unconditional pointwise saving is not currently derivable from the stated Apéry Lucas/Gessel information. The obstruction is an exact failure of the natural prime-by-prime implication that would be needed to remove a positive fraction of primes.

Write

\[
A_n=\operatorname{lcm}_{0\le s<n/2}\gcd(n-s,b_s).
\]

For a prime p dividing one of the lcm entries we have

\[
p\mid n-s,\qquad p\mid b_s.
\]

Put

\[
q=(n-s)/p.
\]

The available residue information controls b_s according to s modulo p. If s=pa+r, then the proven congruence

\[
b_{p a+r}\equiv b_a(b_r+2\epsilon(r)apD_s)\pmod {p^2}
\]

only gives a necessary reduction when p divides b_r or when additional information on b_a is available. It does not imply that p cannot divide b_s.

## First false implication

The false step is the proposed uniform implication:

> if p divides n-s and p does not divide a small residue obstruction attached to s mod p, then p cannot divide b_s.

This fails because the Lucas factor contains the high digit contribution.

If s=pa+r, then modulo p,

\[
b_s\equiv b_a b_r\pmod p.
\]

Therefore a prime p can divide b_s for two independent reasons:

1. p divides the residue factor b_r;
2. p divides the high-digit factor b_a.

The second mechanism is not controlled by fixing the residue r. Since a=(s-r)/p ranges through values of size comparable with n/p, the quotient q is not bounded and no uniform exclusion interval follows.

## Why the quotient q cannot be discarded

For p|n-s,

\[
s=n-pq.
\]

The admissible s in the lcm are therefore parameterized by all q satisfying

\[
0<n-pq<n/2.
\]

Hence q ranges up to about n/p. Any argument replacing q by a bounded residue variable loses exactly the high Apéry digit a carried by the Lucas recursion.

The only immediate consequence of the recurrence is a recursive decomposition of possible divisors, not an exclusion theorem:

\[
p\mid b_s \Longrightarrow p\mid b_r\ \text{or}\ p\mid b_{(s-r)/p}
\]

under the usual mod-p Lucas split. This is not enough because repeated descent can terminate at many small indices and does not give a pointwise positive-density prime exclusion.

## Relation to the target bounds

The trivial estimate is

\[
\log A_n\leq \log\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)=n+o(n).
\]

To obtain option (iii) in the question one would need a theorem of the form:

for every n, a fixed positive proportion of primes p in a specified interval satisfy

\[
p\mid n-s\implies p\nmid b_s\quad(0\le s<n/2).
\]

The Lucas/Gessel congruence does not supply this. It leaves the recursive high-digit alternative.

## No valid c<1 iteration is obtained

A possible iteration would try to charge every surviving prime divisor p to a smaller Apéry index s/p. But the charging map is not injective: many pairs (p,s) may descend to the same lower index, and the lcm takes the maximum prime powers rather than a sum of independent contributions. Therefore the recurrence alone does not yield a contraction factor c<1.

## Conclusion

The direct carrier attack fails at the first attempted pointwise exclusion lemma. The missing ingredient is a theorem controlling simultaneous prime divisors of the family

\[
\{b_a:a\le n/p\}
\]

uniformly in the quotient q=(n-s)/p. The known Apéry congruences reduce the problem recursively but do not prove the required saving.

Thus no result of type (i), (ii), or (iii) follows from the current exact Lucas/Gessel machinery alone. A successful proof needs a genuinely new prime-divisor statement for individual Apéry numbers, not a further rearrangement of the carrier lcm.
