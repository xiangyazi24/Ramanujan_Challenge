# Q7314 — Heavy plus anti-diagonal: exact landing-dispersion frontier

## Terminal verdict

I do **not** obtain an unconditional proof of

\[
S_H(T,J):=
\sum_{d,h}
 \#\{z:p\mid b_z,b_{z+h},\ q\mid b_{h-1-z}\}
=o_J(T^2/\log^2T)
\]

on the heavy remainder.  I obtain a sharp occurrence-preserving reduction and can identify the first exact failed implication.

Let \(L=\log T\).  After the already-proved light deletions, one may also delete the symmetric endpoint \(a=6d-1-z\) when \(a=o((T/L)^{1/2})\).  On the remaining region, erase only the \(q\)-zero mark and retain every \(p\)-pair occurrence.  If \(V_q(r)\) is the resulting number of actual occurrences landing at the residue \(r\), then the marked count is exactly

\[
S_H=\sum_q\sum_{rmod q}{\bf1}_{q\mid b_r}V_q(r).
\tag{1}
\]

The exact centering

\[
{\bf1}_{q\mid b_r}=\frac{Z(q)}q+\eta_q(r),
\qquad \sum_{rmod q}\eta_q(r)=0,
\tag{2}
\]

closes the density term with a power saving.  The entire obstruction is the centered landing variance

\[
\mathcal V_H
:=\sum_q\sum_{rmod q}
 \left(V_q(r)-\frac{U_q}{q}\right)^2,
\qquad U_q:=\sum_rV_q(r).
\tag{3}
\]

Using the unconditional average zero mass

\[
\sum_{q\asymp_JT}Z(q)\ll_J T^{8/5}L^{-1},
\tag{4}
\]

the target follows from

\[
\boxed{\mathcal V_H=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-3}).}
\tag{LD}
\]

This is not a restatement of the target: \(\mathcal V_H\) is defined on the **unmarked** \(p\)-pair occurrence set and contains no factor \({\bf1}_{q\mid b_r}\).

After removing the diagonal and deterministically close pairs, the smallest remaining repair is an even narrower far-reuse dispersion lemma.  For any slowly growing \(\Omega(T)\), put

\[
K_0=\left\lfloor
 \frac{T^{1/5}L^{-12/5}}{\Omega(T)}
\right\rfloor.
\tag{5}
\]

For the exact unmarked occurrences \(\omega\), carrying their full data \(q,d,p,h,z,r\), define

\[
\mathfrak D_{>K_0}:=
\sum_q
\sum_{\substack{
 \omega_1\ne\omega_2\in\mathscr U_q^H\\
 |d_1-d_2|>K_0}}
\left({\bf1}_{r_1=r_2}-\frac1q\right).
\tag{6}
\]

Then the one-sided estimate

\[
\boxed{
\max(\mathfrak D_{>K_0},0)
=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-3})
}
\tag{HLD}
\]

is sufficient.  The standard, slightly stronger formulation is
\(|\mathfrak D_{>K_0}|=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-3})\).

The **first exact failed implication** is therefore

\[
\sum_{q,r}V_q(r)\ll_JT^{11/5}L^{-3/5}
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal V_H=o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-3}).
\tag{7}
\]

Equivalently, the banked unmarked first moment does not justify replacing the final \(q\)-zero indicator by its density.  That replacement drops the covariance in (2).  Neither reflection, the \(2/3\) subinterval theorem, divisor height, nor the gap polynomial currently controls this covariance.

## 1. Exact reflected variables and a third height deletion

Retain the notation in the question:

\[
r=h-1-z,\qquad p=6d+h,\qquad q=7d+h,
\]

with \(p,q\) prime and all original range and archimedean gates.  Put

\[
a:=6d-1-z.
\tag{8}
\]

Then

\[
z+a=6d-1,
\qquad
p-1-(z+h)=a.
\tag{9}
\]

The Apéry palindrome modulo \(p\) gives

\[
p\mid b_{z+h}\iff p\mid b_a.
\tag{10}
\]

Thus every marked occurrence has the symmetric form

\[
p\mid b_z,b_a,\qquad q\mid b_r,
\qquad z+a=6d-1.
\tag{11}
\]

All three indices \(z,a,r\) carry an independent deterministic height cap, but the caps must be used as alternatives on the same occurrence set, never multiplied.

### Three-height box lemma

On a dyadic box

\[
z\asymp Z,\qquad a\asymp A,\qquad r\asymp R,
\]

the number of marked occurrences satisfies

\[
\boxed{
N_H(Z,A,R)\ll_J\frac1L
\min\!\left\{
 ZR\min(Z,R),
 AR\min(A,R),
 ZA\min(Z,A)
\right\}.}
\tag{12}
\]

#### Proof

For fixed \(z,r\), varying \(d\) gives distinct primes

\[
p=6d+z+r+1,\qquad q=7d+z+r+1.
\]

Every surviving \(d\) gives \(p\mid b_z\) and \(q\mid b_r\).  Since both primes are \(\asymp_JT\), elementary Apéry height gives at most

\[
O_J(\min(z,r)/L)
\]

such \(d\).  Summation over \(O(ZR)\) pairs gives the first term.

For fixed \(a,r\), one has

\[
h=6d+r-a,\qquad p=12d+r-a,\qquad q=13d+r-a.
\]

Every occurrence gives \(p\mid b_a\) and \(q\mid b_r\), so there are
\(O_J(\min(a,r)/L)\) values of \(d\), giving the second term.

For fixed \(z,a\), the equation \(z+a=6d-1\) fixes \(d\).  As \(r\) varies, \(p=6d+z+r+1\) varies injectively, and every occurrence gives
\(p\mid b_z,b_a\).  Hence there are at most
\(O_J(\min(z,a)/L)\) values of \(r\), giving the third term.  No density product occurs.  ∎

A direct consequence is

\[
N_H[a\le A_0]\ll_J \frac{TA_0^2}{L},
\qquad
C_H[a\le A_0]\ll_J TA_0^2.
\tag{13}
\]

Therefore \(a=o((T/L)^{1/2})\) is light, just as the already-deleted \(z\)- and \(r\)-endpoint regions are light.

### Power polytope

Write

\[
Z=T^\zeta L^{-u},\qquad
A=T^\alpha L^{-v},\qquad
R=T^\rho L^{-w}.
\]

Because \(z+a=6d-1\) and \(d\asymp_JT\),

\[
\max(\zeta,\alpha)=1.
\tag{14}
\]

At power level, (12) is subcritical as soon as one of

\[
\begin{aligned}
\zeta+\rho+\min(\zeta,\rho)&<2,\\
\alpha+\rho+\min(\alpha,\rho)&<2,\\
\zeta+\alpha+\min(\zeta,\alpha)&<2
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

holds.  At equality, the relevant logarithmic exponents must contribute more than one additional power of \(L^{-1}\), because (12) contains only \(L^{-1}\) while the incidence target contains \(L^{-2}\).

For example, in the orientation \(\alpha=1\), the genuinely heavy polytope is

\[
\zeta\ge\tfrac12,\qquad
\rho\ge\tfrac12,\qquad
\zeta+\rho+\min(\zeta,\rho)\ge2.
\tag{16}
\]

The corner \((\alpha,\zeta,\rho)=(1,2/3,2/3)\) lies exactly on the height boundary.  This explains why further dyadic splitting of the individual heights removes a substantial fringe but not the central obstruction.

## 2. Exact fixed-row embedding; no characteristic is merged

The archimedean expression simplifies to

\[
m:=42d+6z+7r+6=6q+r=7p-1-z.
\tag{17}
\]

Define a second row

\[
n:=m+6q=12q+r=13p+a.
\tag{18}
\]

At every marked occurrence, Lucas and reflection imply

\[
p\mid b_m,b_n,\qquad q\mid b_m,b_n.
\tag{19}
\]

Indeed, modulo \(q\), the low digit of both \(m=6q+r\) and \(n=12q+r\) is \(r\).  Modulo \(p\),

\[
m=6p+(p-1-z),\qquad n=13p+a,
\]

and \(p-1-z\) and \(a\) are \(p\)-zero positions by reflection.  Thus every occurrence injects into a literal restricted \(K_{2,2}\) between the two rows \(m,n\) and the two characteristics \(p,q\).  This is only an embedding; no resultant between \(p\) and \(q\) is taken.

The important feature is that for fixed \(q,r\), both rows are fixed:

\[
(m,n)=(6q+r,12q+r).
\tag{20}
\]

The different \(p\)-occurrences at that cell are therefore repeated common-prime landings on one row pair.

## 3. Erase exactly one mark and retain every occurrence

For a prime \(q\asymp_JT\) and \(0\le r<q\), let
\(\mathscr U_q^H(r)\) be the set of tuples determined by \(d\) satisfying all of the following:

\[
\begin{gathered}
T/J<d<T/18,\qquad d\text{ even},\\
p=q-d\text{ prime},\quad p\in(T/7,T/3],\\
h=q-7d>0,\qquad z=h-1-r\ge0,\\
a=6d-1-z=13d+r-q\ge0,\\
T<6q+r\le2T,
\end{gathered}
\tag{21}
\]

all heavy-region cutoffs, and

\[
p\mid b_z,b_{z+h}.
\tag{22}
\]

The single condition omitted from \(\mathscr U_q^H(r)\) is \(q\mid b_r\).  Put

\[
V_q(r):=|\mathscr U_q^H(r)|,\qquad
U_q:=\sum_{rmod q}V_q(r),\qquad
U_H:=\sum_qU_q.
\tag{23}
\]

For fixed \(q,r,d\), all of \(p,h,z,a\) are determined.  Thus \(V_q(r)\) counts occurrences, not distinct projections, and (1) is an exact identity.

The banked unmarked zero-pair moment from the quotient-six reduction applies to this set after the \(q\)-mark is erased:

\[
U_H\le U_0\ll_JT^{11/5}L^{-3/5}.
\tag{24}
\]

If (24) is not taken as an available banked input, the present route stops here; the box-height bound alone is \(T^3/L\) in the central region.

## 4. Coherent proof attack: center the last mark

Let

\[
\mathcal Z_q:=\{0\le r<q:q\mid b_r\},
\qquad Z(q):=|\mathcal Z_q|,
\]

and define \(ho_q=Z(q)/q\) and

\[
\eta_q(r):={\bf1}_{\mathcal Z_q}(r)-\rho_q.
\tag{25}
\]

Since \(\sum_{rmod q}\eta_q(r)=0\), (1) becomes the exact identity

\[
S_H=M_H+E_H,
\tag{26}
\]

where

\[
M_H=\sum_q\rho_qU_q,
\tag{27}
\]

and

\[
E_H=
\sum_q\sum_{rmod q}
\eta_q(r)
\left(V_q(r)-\frac{U_q}{q}\right).
\tag{28}
\]

This is centering, not an independence assertion.

### 4.1 The density term is already subcritical

The unconditional pointwise zero bound \(Z(q)\ll q^{2/3}\) gives

\[
\rho_q\ll_JT^{-1/3}.
\]

Using (24),

\[
M_H\ll_JT^{-1/3}U_H
\ll_JT^{28/15}L^{-3/5}.
\tag{29}
\]

Relative to the target,

\[
\frac{T^{28/15}L^{-3/5}}{T^2L^{-2}}
=T^{-2/15}L^{7/5}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{30}
\]

So the formal expected-density term wins by \(T^{2/15}/L^{7/5}\).

### 4.2 The error is exactly a landing-dispersion problem

For each \(q\),

\[
\sum_{rmod q}\eta_q(r)^2
=Z(q)-\frac{Z(q)^2}{q}\le Z(q).
\tag{31}
\]

The canonical average bound in `problems/3.2/atom_tail_section.tex` gives

\[
\mathcal Z_Q:=\sum_{q\asymp_JT}Z(q)\ll_JT^{8/5}L^{-1}.
\tag{32}
\]

Cauchy applied to (28) yields

\[
|E_H|^2
\le \mathcal Z_Q\,\mathcal V_H,
\tag{33}
\]

with \(\mathcal V_H\) defined in (3).  Hence (LD) gives

\[
E_H=o_J(T^2L^{-2}),
\]

which together with (29) proves the incidence target and, after multiplying by \(\log p\asymp L\), the weighted target.

### 4.3 Fourier-equivalent form

For

\[
\widehat V_q(k)=\sum_{rmod q}V_q(r)e_q(-kr),
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\mathcal V_H
=\sum_q\frac1q\sum_{k\ne0}|\widehat V_q(k)|^2.
\tag{34}
\]

Thus the missing statement is exactly a nonzero-frequency energy bound for the affine landing function \(V_q\).  The short-frequency energy theorem for the zero set \(\mathcal Z_q\) does not prove (34): it controls one factor in (28), whereas no current theorem confines \(\widehat V_q\) to those frequencies or controls its high-frequency mass.

## 6. Hostile audit: raw reuse is not the right repair

A first version of the attack might demand

\[
\sum_{q,r}V_q(r)^2=o(T^{12/5}L^{-3}).
\tag{38}
\]

That is unnecessarily strong.  The constant mode of \(V_q\) never contributes to (28).  Indeed,

\[
\sum_rV_q(r)^2
=\sum_r\left(V_q(r)-\frac{U_q}{q}\right)^2
 +\frac{U_q^2}{q}.
\tag{39}
\]

There are \(\asymp T/L\) admissible primes \(q\), each with \(q\asymp T\), so the total number of \((q,r)\)-cells is \(\asymp T^2/L\).  A completely uniform landing function can have raw second moment of scale

\[
\frac{U_H^2}{T^2/L}
\asymp T^{12/5}L^{-1/5}
\tag{40}
\]

when \(U_H\) is at its banked upper scale, far above (38), while its centered variance is zero.  Such a landing distribution would make (28) vanish identically.  Therefore the centered variance (3), not the raw second moment, is the smallest honest target.

## 7. Exact reuse expansion and deterministic removal of close pairs

Let \(\mathscr U_q^H\) denote the full unmarked occurrence multiset for a fixed \(q\), and let \(r(\omega)\), \(d(\omega)\) denote the retained coordinates of an occurrence.  Expanding (3),

\[
\mathcal V_H
=\sum_q
\sum_{\omega_1,\omega_2\in\mathscr U_q^H}
\left({\bf1}_{r_1=r_2}-\frac1q\right).
\tag{41}
\]

This identity retains ordered occurrence pairs.  It neither collapses equal endpoints nor replaces a count by a support.

The diagonal contribution is at most \(U_H\).  For off-diagonal pairs with
\(0<|d_1-d_2|\le K\), the signed contribution is at most the number with \(r_1=r_2\).  Once \(q,r_1,d_1,d_2\) are fixed, both candidate occurrences are uniquely determined.  Hence each first occurrence has \(O(K)\) possible close partners and

\[
\sum_{\substack{
\omega_1\ne\omega_2\\
|d_1-d_2|\le K}}
\left({\bf1}_{r_1=r_2}-\frac1q\right)
\le O(KU_H).
\tag{42}
\]

With \(K=K_0\) from (5),

\[
K_0U_H
\ll_J\frac{T^{12/5}L^{-3}}{\Omega(T)}.
\tag{43}
\]

Equations (36), (41)--(43) prove

\[
\mathcal V_H
\le o_J(T^{12/5}L^{-3})+\mathfrak D_{>K_0}.
\tag{44}
\]

This proves that (HLD) is sufficient and isolates the genuinely new arithmetic range: two well-separated \(d\)-values whose unmarked \(p\)-pairs land at the same \(q\)-residue more often than the uniform \(1/q\) baseline.

A natural stronger but cleaner theorem would be the shot-noise estimate

\[
\mathcal V_H\ll_J U_HT^{o(1)}.
\tag{45}
\]

It also closes, because \(U_HT^{o(1)}=o(T^{12/5}L^{-3})\).  However, (HLD) is the quantitatively smallest remaining statement after the deterministic work above.

## 8. Where the gap polynomial enters, and why it stops

For one unmarked occurrence,

\[
p=q-d,\qquad h=q-7d,\qquad z=h-1-r,
\tag{46}
\]

and the two \(p\)-zeros give the legitimate same-characteristic certificate

\[
p\mid N_h(z).
\tag{47}
\]

Now take a far equality pair in (6).  For \(i=1,2\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_i&=q-d_i,\\
h_i&=q-7d_i,\\
z_i&=h_i-1-r,\\
a_i&=13d_i+r-q,
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]

and

\[
p_i\mid b_{z_i},b_{z_i+h_i},N_{h_i}(z_i).
\tag{49}
\]

The separations are

\[
|p_1-p_2|=|d_1-d_2|,\qquad
|h_1-h_2|=|z_1-z_2|=7|d_1-d_2|,\qquad
|a_1-a_2|=13|d_1-d_2|.
\tag{50}
\]

This does **not** put two gap polynomials in one finite field: the first congruence is modulo \(p_1\), the second modulo \(p_2\).  A resultant between them would be a cross-characteristic artifact and is invalid.  Treating the two divisibilities as independent and multiplying their densities is equally invalid.

At the marked level, \(q\mid b_r\) also gives the reflected zero \(q-1-r\).  That \(q\)-pair is independent of \(d_i\), so its gap polynomial is the same tautological reflection certificate for every landing in the cell.  It cannot distinguish one \(d\) from many \(d\)'s and gives no control of \(V_q(r)\).

Thus (47) is useful for producing the banked unmarked estimate (24), but it supplies no second, same-characteristic equation for the far landing collision.

## 9. Audit of the other tempting closures

### 9.1 Centering a different mark

Fixing \((p,a)\) and erasing \(p\mid b_a\) would leave the two conditions
\(p\mid b_z\) and \(q\mid b_r\).  Repeated landings would then occur in one fixed characteristic \(p\), and a same-\(p\) gap polynomial could be applied to the varying \(z\)'s.

The failure comes one step earlier: the banked \(T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}\) estimate is for the unmarked **\(p\)-zero-pair** occurrence set obtained by erasing the \(q\)-mark.  It does not automatically bound the differently unmarked \((p	ext{-zero},q	ext{-zero})\) set.  Swapping the erased mark is a lost-mark error.  To use this orientation one first needs a separate bound

\[
\sum_{p,a}W_p(a)\ll_JT^{11/5}L^{-3/5}
\tag{51}
\]

for that exact cross-prime unmarked set.  No such transfer follows from symmetry \(z\leftrightarrow a\), because the \(q\)-marker is not symmetric under that swap.

### 9.2 Dropping one row in the \(K_{2,2}\)

From (19), one may retain only \(p,q\mid b_n\) and try to invoke a prime-load second moment at the row \(n\).  This loses the simultaneous divisibility of \(b_m\), as well as the quotient-six/quotient-twelve linkage.  The resulting bound is a localized higher factorial-moment problem over prime divisors of one Apéry number; the current unconditional global second moment does not have the required \(T\)-length localization.  Reintroducing the omitted row after such a bound would multiply marginals.

### 9.3 Using a pair codegree without its landing label

A same-prime zero-pair theorem can count \((p,z,a)\).  A row-pair theorem can count \((m,n,p)\).  Neither count controls how many such objects share the same \((q,r)\).  Projecting away \(q,r\) before applying a codegree theorem allows the same projected witness to be reused by many affine translations.  Formula (41) shows exactly what must remain attached: both occurrence copies, their common \(q\), their two \(d\)'s, and their landing residues.

### 9.4 Low-frequency Fourier energy

The repository's short-window estimate for

\[
F_q(k)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal Z_q}e_q(kr)
\]

controls a bounded frequency window.  In (34), however, \(V_q\) may be concentrated at individual residues, so its Fourier mass may lie at all nonzero frequencies.  There is no justified truncation of \(\widehat V_q\); the high-frequency term is the full landing variance again.  The exact missing implication is a spectral estimate for \(V_q\), not another estimate for \(F_q\).

### 9.5 Dyadic decomposition in \(d\)

For fixed \(J\), \(T/J<d<T/18\), so every nonempty \(d\)-block has \(d\asymp_JT\), and there are \(O_J(1)\) blocks.  Further splitting can localize \(h,z,a,r\), as in (12), but it cannot provide the \(T^{1/5}L^{7/5}\) saving missing from (24).  In the central polytope (16), the landing variance remains the active unknown on every live \(d\)-block.

## 10. A sharp countermodel to the failed implication

This is **not** a counterexample for the Apéry sequence.  It is a counterexample to deriving the target from the scalar information used before (LD).

Take \(\asymp T/L\) formal primes \(q\asymp T\).  For each \(q\), choose a reflected, nonconsecutive, evenly spaced set \(R_q\subset[0,q)\) of size

\[
Z_0\asymp T^{3/5}.
\]

The spacing can be chosen \(\gg T^{2/5}\), so every interval of length \(H\le T\) contains

\[
O(1+H/T^{2/5})=O(H^{2/3})
\]

points.  Thus this model respects reflection, no consecutive points, the \(2/3\) subinterval ceiling, the pointwise \(q^{2/3}\) ceiling, and the aggregate mass \(T^{8/5}/L\).

Set

\[
V_q(r)=
\begin{cases}
K,&r\in R_q,\\
0,&r\notin R_q,
\end{cases}
\qquad
K:=T^{3/5}L^{2/5}.
\tag{52}
\]

Then \(K<T/L\), so it also respects the elementary multiplicity ceiling.  Its unmarked mass is exactly at the banked scale:

\[
\sum_{q,r}V_q(r)
\asymp \frac TL\,T^{3/5}\,T^{3/5}L^{2/5}
=T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}.
\tag{53}
\]

If the marked zero set is \(R_q\), every unmarked occurrence survives:

\[
S_H\asymp T^{11/5}L^{-3/5},
\]

which misses the target by \(T^{1/5}L^{7/5}\).  Its centered landing variance is

\[
\mathcal V_H\asymp
\frac TL\,T^{3/5}\,K^2
=T^{14/5}L^{-1/5},
\tag{54}
\]

larger than the (LD) budget by \(T^{2/5}L^{14/5}\).

This model does not claim that the required \(p\)-side gap-polynomial incidences can be realized by Apéry zeros.  Its purpose is exact: once the \(p\)-side information has been compressed to the valid first-moment bound (24), all remaining vertical and scalar constraints permit complete concentration on the \(q\)-zero cells.  Any proof must therefore use the affine occurrence geometry to establish landing dispersion; no manipulation of the existing marginals can suffice.

## 11. Smallest repair lemma in explicit original variables

For completeness, the repair can be stated without \(V_q\).  Let
\(\mathscr U_q^H\) consist of the exact tuples

\[
\omega=(d,h,z,r,p,q)
\]

satisfying

\[
q=7d+h,\qquad p=6d+h=q-d,\qquad r=h-1-z,
\tag{55}
\]

all prime, interval, parity, archimedean, and heavy gates, and

\[
p\mid b_z,b_{z+h},
\tag{56}
\]

but not requiring \(q\mid b_r\).  Then (HLD), namely

\[
\sum_q
\sum_{\substack{
\omega_1\ne\omega_2\in\mathscr U_q^H\\
|d_1-d_2|>K_0}}
\left({\bf1}_{h_1-1-z_1=h_2-1-z_2}-\frac1q\right)
