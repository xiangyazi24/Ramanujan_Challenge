# Can the Apéry companion sequence create a cross-prime link?

## Executive verdict

There is a decisive correction at the first step:

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_r\ \text{does not imply}\ p\mid a_{r-1}.}
\]

For \(p>3\) and \(1\le r<p\), the Casoratian instead gives

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 a_r b_{r-1}\equiv \frac{6}{r^3}\pmod p,
}
\]

so both \(a_r\) and \(b_{r-1}\) are **units** modulo \(p\), and

\[
\boxed{
 a_r\equiv \frac{6}{r^3b_{r-1}}\pmod p.
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

There is no condition at all on \(a_{r-1}\). The smallest counterexample is

\[
 b_2=73,\qquad a_1=6,\qquad a_2=\frac{351}{4}.
\]

Thus \(73\mid b_2\), but \(73\nmid a_1\). Moreover

\[
 a_2\equiv 33
 \equiv \frac{6}{2^3b_1}\pmod{73},
\]

exactly as (0.1) predicts.

After correcting this premise, the natural companion construction can be worked out completely. Put

\[
 c_n=\frac{a_n}{6},
 \qquad
 F_n(T)=b_n-Tc_n.
\]

For \(r=s+h\), the exact resultant in the marked-fiber parameter \(T\) is

\[
\boxed{
 \operatorname{Res}_T(F_s,F_r)
 =b_sc_r-c_sb_r
 =\frac{N_h(s)}{\prod_{j=1}^{h}(s+j)^3},
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

up to the harmless sign convention for ordering the two linear polynomials. In other words:

> **The canonical companion resultant is exactly the already-known gap kernel.**

It detects two zeros at indices \(s,r\) in the **same characteristic**. It does not detect a \(p\)-zero at \(r\) and a \(q\)-zero at \(s\). Indeed, if

\[
 p\mid b_r,\qquad q\mid b_s,\qquad p\ne q,
\]

then (0.2) gives

\[
 \frac{N_h(s)}{\prod(s+j)^3}
 \equiv b_sc_r\pmod p,
 \qquad
 \frac{N_h(s)}{\prod(s+j)^3}
 \equiv-c_sb_r\pmod q,
\]

and neither residue is forced to vanish. More sharply,

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r\Longrightarrow
 \bigl(p\mid N_h(s)\iff p\mid b_s\bigr),
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

and symmetrically with \(p,r\) replaced by \(q,s\). Thus the resultant is an exact **same-prime double-zero detector**, not a cross-prime detector.

The harmonic-number representation also gives no second congruence. One standard exact formula is

\[
 c_{n,k}
 =H_n^{(3)}
 +\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{k}
 \frac{(-1)^{j-1}}
 {j^3\binom nj\binom{n+j}{j}},
 \qquad
 a_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{n,k}
 \binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
\tag{0.4}
\]

where \(H_n^{(3)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}j^{-3}\). It is not an exact identity of the form
\(a_n=b_n\zeta(3)+\text{rational correction}\); rather, \(a_n/b_n\to\zeta(3)\), and \(b_n\zeta(3)-a_n\) is a small **irrational** remainder.

For every rational number \(T_r\), the Wronskian gives the exact gauge identity

\[
\boxed{
 r^3b_{r-1}(a_r-b_rT_r)-6
 =r^3b_r(a_{r-1}-b_{r-1}T_r).
}
\tag{0.5}
\]

Taking \(T_r=H_r^{(3)}\) shows that the harmonic correction congruence at a zero of \(b_r\) is automatically divisible by \(b_r\). It is a reformulation of the original zero condition, not an independent condition.

There is nevertheless one genuine positive role for the companion sequence, and the repository has already exploited it. For \(n=kp+r<p^2\), the current `proof.tex` proves the block law

\[
\boxed{
 p^3a_{kp+r}\equiv a_kb_r\pmod p.
}
\tag{0.6}
\]

In the top-half channel of the question, \(k=1\), so

\[
\boxed{
 p^3a_m\equiv6b_{m-p}\pmod p.
}
\tag{0.7}
\]

Thus the cleared global companion value has **exactly the same lower-digit event** as \(b_{m-p}\); it supplies no additional equation. For larger quotients it also produces the separate “companion-block” primes \(p\mid\operatorname{num}(a_k)\), and the repository bounds their total logarithmic weight by \(O(n^{2/3})\). Those primes are already harmless. The unresolved cross-prime problem is precisely the remaining lower-digit zero alignment.

The conclusion is therefore:

> **Within the rank-two recurrence algebra, its harmonic formula, the Casoratian, and all natural resultants/discriminants in the companion coordinate, the companion route is terminal: it adds transversality and a unit label, not a second vanishing condition. A successful companion-based route would have to use genuinely new arithmetic beyond this algebra—most plausibly a first-order mod-\(p^2\) Frobenius/digit law whose defect is a fixed low-height object rather than a new free local harmonic or Fermat-quotient parameter.**

The rest of this report proves these claims precisely, identifies the exact no-go theorem, and specifies the narrow surviving experiment.

## 2. Correct normalization and the first false implication

The two Apéry solutions satisfy

\[
(n+1)^3u_{n+1}=P(n)u_n-n^3u_{n-1},
\qquad
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5,
\tag{2.1}
\]

with

\[
(a_0,a_1)=(0,6),
\qquad
(b_0,b_1)=(1,5).
\tag{2.2}
\]

The repository's Wronskian normalization is

\[
\boxed{
 a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

This is equivalent to the sign convention in the question.

Let \(p>3\), \(1\le r<p\), and suppose \(p\mid b_r\). Since the denominator of \(a_r\) divides

\[
L_r^3,
\qquad L_r=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,r),
\]

and \(p>r\), both \(a_r\) and \(a_{r-1}\) are \(p\)-integral. Reducing (2.3) gives

\[
 a_rb_{r-1}\equiv\frac6{r^3}\pmod p.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The right side is a unit. Therefore

\[
 p\nmid a_r,
 \qquad
 p\nmid b_{r-1},
\tag{2.5}
\]

and (0.1) follows.

The term \(a_{r-1}b_r\) vanishes because \(b_r\) vanishes. This supplies **no information whatsoever** about \(a_{r-1}\). The logical error is the familiar mistake

\[
xy\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid x
\]

when \(p\mid y\) is already known.

### 2.1 Minimal counterexample

The first nontrivial Apéry values are

\[
 b_0=1,\quad b_1=5,\quad b_2=73,
\]

\[
 a_0=0,\quad a_1=6,\quad a_2=\frac{351}{4}.
\]

At \((p,r)=(73,2)\),

\[
73\mid b_2,
\qquad
73\nmid a_1.
\]

Moreover

\[
\frac{351}{4}\equiv33\pmod{73},
\]

and

\[
\frac6{2^3b_1}
=\frac6{40}\equiv33\pmod{73}.
\]

So the corrected unit formula is already exact in the first example.

### 2.2 What is true about “the same primes”

There are two different statements, which must not be conflated.

1. **Local index \(r\):** if \(p\mid b_r\), then \(a_r\) is a unit and no shifted companion zero is forced.
2. **Global top-window index \(m=p+r\):** the block law gives
   \[
   p^3a_m\equiv6b_r\pmod p.
   \]
   Hence the cleared global companion has the same hit event as \(b_r\).

The second statement is true and useful for the GCD problem. It is not a new local condition; it is exactly the same lower-digit zero condition transported to index \(m\).

## 3. The companion is the transverse basis vector

Normalize

\[
 c_n=\frac{a_n}{6}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then \((c_0,c_1)=(0,1)\), and

\[
\boxed{
 c_nb_{n-1}-c_{n-1}b_n=\frac1{n^3}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For every prime \(p>n\), the fundamental matrix

\[
\mathcal F_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
 b_n&c_n\\
 b_{n-1}&c_{n-1}
\end{pmatrix}
\]

has determinant

\[
\det\mathcal F_n=-\frac1{n^3},
\tag{3.3}
\]

a unit modulo \(p\). Thus \(b\) and \(c\) form a basis of the two-dimensional solution space modulo every prime above the index range.

At a zero of \(b_r\),

\[
(b_r,c_r)=(0,\text{unit}).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Geometrically, the zero condition pins the projective state to

\[
[0:1]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p).
\]

The companion coordinate \(c_r\) records only the scale of that vector. It proves that the crossing is transverse; it is not another equation.

Relative to arbitrary initial data \((u_0,u_1)\), every solution is

\[
 u_n=u_0b_n+(u_1-5u_0)c_n.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The evaluation row at index \(r\) is therefore

\[
R_r=(b_r-5c_r,c_r).
\tag{3.6}
\]

At an actual Apéry zero,

\[
\boxed{
R_r=c_r(-5,1).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

All actual zeros have the same row direction; the label \(c_r\) is a nonzero scalar. This is exactly the “marked row” identity recorded in `AVENUES.md`.

## 4. The exact companion resultant is the gap kernel

Let \(r=s+h\), \(h\ge1\), and put

\[
D_{s,h}=\prod_{j=1}^{h}(s+j)^3.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The Green-kernel theorem in `gap_kernel_green.tex` proves

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N_h(s)}{D_{s,h}}
=b_sc_r-c_sb_r.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently, for the original normalization,

\[
\boxed{
 a_rb_s-a_sb_r
=\frac{6N_h(s)}{D_{s,h}}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For \(h=1\), this is precisely the adjacent Wronskian.

Now introduce the marked projective fiber

\[
F_n(T)=b_n-Tc_n.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The actual Apéry fiber is \(T=0\). Since \(F_s,F_r\) are linear in \(T\), their resultant is their \(2\times2\) coefficient determinant:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}_T(F_s,F_r)
&=b_sc_r-c_sb_r\\
&=\frac{N_h(s)}{D_{s,h}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is the exact answer to the proposed resultant route.

### 4.1 Same characteristic

If one prime \(\ell>r\) satisfies

\[
\ell\mid b_s,\qquad \ell\mid b_r,
\]

then both marked equations vanish at \(T=0\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), so their resultant vanishes:

\[
\ell\mid N_h(s).
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is exactly the usual gap-polynomial theorem.

### 4.2 Two different characteristics

Suppose instead

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad q\mid b_s,\qquad p\ne q.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Modulo \(p\), (4.2) becomes

\[
\frac{N_h(s)}{D_{s,h}}
\equiv b_sc_r\pmod p.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Because \(c_r\) is a unit at a \(p\)-zero of \(b_r\),

\[
p\mid N_h(s)\iff p\mid b_s.
\tag{4.9}
\]

The hypothesis gives \(p\mid b_r\), not \(p\mid b_s\). Similarly,

\[
q\mid N_h(s)\iff q\mid b_r.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus the natural resultant is generically a unit modulo both \(p\) and \(q\).

In CRT language,

\[
\mathbb Z/pq\mathbb Z\simeq\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_q.
\]

The condition \(b_r=0\) holds only in the first component, while \(b_s=0\) holds only in the second. The two linear forms do not have a common root in either component. There is no reason for their resultant to vanish in the product ring.

## 5. Every natural discriminant reduces to the same brackets

The two-index computation is not an accident. Let

\[
F_i(T)=b_i-Tc_i
\]

for indices \(i\) in a finite set, and assume \(c_{i_0}\) is inverted. Then

\[
c_{i_0}F_i-c_iF_{i_0}
=c_{i_0}b_i-c_ib_{i_0}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Conversely,

\[
F_i
=\frac{c_i}{c_{i_0}}F_{i_0}
+\frac{c_{i_0}b_i-c_ib_{i_0}}{c_{i_0}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence the ideals are exactly equal:

\[
\boxed{
(F_i:i\in I)
=
\bigl(F_{i_0},\ c_{i_0}b_i-c_ib_{i_0}:i\ne i_0\bigr)
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

after localizing at \(c_{i_0}\).

At an actual zero \(b_{i_0}=0\), \(c_{i_0}\) is a unit, and (5.3) becomes

\[
(F_i:i\in I)=(T,\ b_i:i\ne i_0)
\tag{5.4}
\]

up to multiplication by units.

This is the basis-covariant no-gain theorem already summarized in `AVENUES.md`:

> Marking the distinguished fiber contributes exactly one pin \(T=0\); every additional return condition is the ordinary projective minor, hence an ordinary gap condition.

The same conclusion holds for discriminants. If

\[
P_I(T)=\prod_{i\in I}F_i(T),
\]

then, up to powers of the leading coefficients \(c_i\),

\[
\operatorname{Disc}_T(P_I)
\doteq
\prod_{i<j}(b_ic_j-b_jc_i)^2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Every factor is a gap kernel divided by a denominator unit. Thus a discriminant supplies products of same-prime return certificates; it creates no new mixed-characteristic invariant.

Because the solution module has rank two, all higher basis-covariant determinants are generated by these \(2\times2\) brackets. A \(3\times3\) determinant of evaluation vectors vanishes identically. The companion cannot create a third independent recurrence coordinate.

## 6. The harmonic formula and why Wolstenholme does not add a condition

A standard exact Apéry formula is

\[
 a_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}
 \binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
 \left(
 H_n^{(3)}
 +\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{k}
 \frac{(-1)^{j-1}}
 {j^3\binom nj\binom{n+j}{j}}
 \right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus

\[
 a_n=b_nH_n^{(3)}+R_n,
\tag{6.2}
\]

where \(R_n\in\mathbb Q\) is the weighted correction.

### 6.1 The \(\zeta(3)\) wording

The exact relation is not

\[
a_n=b_n\zeta(3)+\text{rational correction}.
\]

Both \(a_n\) and \(b_n\) are rational/integer, while \(\zeta(3)\) is irrational. Rather,

\[
\frac{a_n}{b_n}\longrightarrow\zeta(3),
\]

and

\[
b_n\zeta(3)-a_n
\]

is the small irrational linear-form remainder. Clearing denominators makes \(D_na_n\) integral, where

\[
D_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)^3,
\]

but it does not make \(D_nb_n\zeta(3)\) integral.

### 6.2 Exact harmonic gauge identity

For any rational \(T_r\), start from

\[
r^3(a_rb_{r-1}-a_{r-1}b_r)=6.
\]

Subtract \(r^3b_rb_{r-1}T_r\) from both sides in the useful way to obtain

\[
\boxed{
 r^3b_{r-1}(a_r-b_rT_r)-6
 =r^3b_r(a_{r-1}-b_{r-1}T_r).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Set \(T_r=H_r^{(3)}\). Then

\[
 r^3b_{r-1}R_r-6
 =r^3b_r(a_{r-1}-b_{r-1}H_r^{(3)}).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Therefore every prime \(p>r\) dividing \(b_r\) automatically satisfies

\[
R_r\equiv\frac6{r^3b_{r-1}}\pmod p.
\tag{6.5}
\]

But (6.4) shows that the numerator of the purported new detector is literally \(b_r\) times another rational number. After saturating by \(b_r\), no forced \(p\)-divisibility remains.

This argument is stronger than an analysis of the particular harmonic formula: **any** decomposition

\[
a_r=b_rT_r+\text{correction}
\]

has the same property. At a zero of \(b_r\), changing \(a_r\) by a multiple of \(b_r\) does not change its residue. The forced unit value is Wronskian tautology.

### 6.3 Why Wolstenholme is at the wrong index

Wolstenholme-type congruences concern complete ranges such as \(H_{p-1}\) and generalized harmonic sums at the endpoint \(p-1\). The difficult root index \(r\) is an arbitrary moving integer with \(0<r<p-1\).

The repository proves a stronger structural statement for the recurrence itself: every \(p\)-integral solution is palindromic,

\[
 u_{p-1-j}\equiv u_j\pmod p.
\tag{6.6}
\]

In particular,

\[
a_{p-1-j}\equiv a_j,
\qquad
b_{p-1-j}\equiv b_j,
\qquad
 a_{p-1}\equiv0,
\qquad
 b_{p-1}\equiv1.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Thus the strongest endpoint harmonic congruence produces the companion endpoint zero \(a_{p-1}\equiv0\), precisely where the Apéry number is a unit. At an actual zero of \(b_r\), the companion \(a_r\) is a unit. The two phenomena point in opposite directions.

Alternative presentations of (6.1) may display denominators containing binomial coefficients whose individual terms have nontrivial \(p\)-adic behavior when \(r+k\ge p\). The final rational number \(a_r\), however, has denominator dividing \(D_r\), so it is \(p\)-integral for \(p>r\). Any termwise argument must reproduce the same global cancellation and ultimately the unit identity (0.1).

## 7. The top-window pair block law: the companion condition is identical to the root condition

The current manuscript proves the following. Let

\[
n=kp+r,\qquad 1\le k<p,\qquad0\le r<p,\qquad n<p^2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
 p^3a_n\equiv a_kb_r\pmod p,
 \qquad
 b_n\equiv b_kb_r\pmod p.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The first identity is the companion coordinate of the Cartier–Frobenius block law.

### 7.1 Top half

In the regime in the question,

\[
p>m/2,\qquad m=p+r,
\]

so \(k=1\). Since \(a_1=6\),

\[
\boxed{
 p^3a_m\equiv6b_r\pmod p.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For a second prime \(q>m/2\), put \(s=m-q\). Then

\[
\boxed{
 q^3a_m\equiv6b_s\pmod q.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
p\mid b_r\iff p\mid p^3a_m,
\qquad
q\mid b_s\iff q\mid q^3a_m.
\tag{7.4}
\]

These are not two independent restrictions on \(a_m\). They are the same local zero events rewritten through two different local normalizations of one rational number.

The exact equality

\[
q^3(p^3a_m)=p^3(q^3a_m)
\]

is tautological. After passing to the common integer \(D_ma_m\), one merely recovers that the aligned primes divide the same cleared companion integer—exactly the original GCD setup.

### 7.2 Denominator capacity gives only a linear bound

Write \(a_m=A_m/C_m\) in lowest terms. In the top window, the manuscript obtains, under the stated small-valuation hypothesis,

\[
v_p(a_m)=-3+v_p(b_m).
\tag{7.5}
\]

For a simple hit \(v_p(b_m)=1\), one has \(v_p(a_m)=-2\), so \(p^2\mid C_m\). If \(\mathcal S_m\) is a set of simple aligned top-window primes, then

\[
\prod_{p\in\mathcal S_m}p^2\mid C_m.
\tag{7.6}
\]

But

\[
C_m\mid D_m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,m)^3,
\]

and hence

\[
2\sum_{p\in\mathcal S_m}\log p
\le\log C_m
\le3\psi(m)=3m+o(m).
\tag{7.7}
\]

This permits \(\asymp m/\log m\) top primes. It does not give the required sublinear logarithmic mass. The companion denominator is a faithful recorder of the hard hits, not a mechanism preventing them.

### 7.3 The positive companion-block result

For a general quotient \(k\), (7.1) says that a prime can enter through

\[
p\mid b_r
\]

or through

\[
p\mid\operatorname{num}(a_k).
\]

The manuscript calls the second class companion-block primes. Since the logarithmic height of \(a_k\) is \(O(k)\), their total logarithmic contribution at a fixed \(n\) is \(O(n^{2/3})\). This is a genuine and useful application of the harmonic/denominator structure.

It also shows exactly where that structure stops helping. After companion-block primes are removed, the remaining condition is solely

\[
p\mid b_{n\bmod p}.
\]

For the top-half quotient \(k=1\), there are no companion-block primes at all because \(a_1=6\) is a unit for \(p>3\).

## 8. Why the two-prime resultant cannot be repaired by weighting

Let \(r=s+h\), and let

\[
X=b_s,\qquad
L_h=\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(s+j)^3\right)b_r.
\]

The transfer recurrence writes \(L_h\) as a linear form in \((b_s,b_{s+1})\). The two cross-prime hypotheses are

\[
p\mid L_h,
\qquad
q\mid X.
\]

Over the localization in which all integers up to \(r\) are units, define

\[
I_p=(p,L_h),
\qquad
I_q=(q,X).
\]

Since \(p\) and \(q\) are coprime,

\[
\boxed{
I_p\cap I_q=I_pI_q=(pq,\ pX,\ qL_h,\ XL_h).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is the exact ideal obstruction established in Q42. Every universal recurrence certificate generated by the two local conditions is built from:

1. an explicit \(pq\);
2. \(p\) times the \(q\)-zero value;
3. \(q\) times the \(p\)-zero value;
4. the product of the two large zero values.

Adjoining the companion solution does not alter this elimination ideal. The fundamental matrix has unit determinant, so the companion variables are obtained by an invertible change of state coordinates. They add unit/nonvanishing information but no new generator.

This explains the small-versus-divisible dichotomy:

- the basis-covariant determinant is small enough to collapse to \(N_h(s)\), but it is not divisible by \(p\) or \(q\);
- a weighted expression forced to be divisible by \(pq\) necessarily restores a full-size endpoint term.

For example, if

\[
A=a_sb_r,\qquad B=a_rb_s,
\]

then \(p\mid A\), \(q\mid B\), and \(B-A=6N_h(s)/D_{s,h}\). The weighted combination

\[
qA-pB
\]

is divisible by \(pq\), but writing \(q=p+h\) gives a small determinant term plus \(hA\) or \(hB\), whose height is linear in the large index. Removing that term removes one of the prime divisibilities.

## 9. Companion labels do not distinguish the actual orbit

A possible refinement is to keep not only the projective state \([b_r:c_r]\), but also the scale label \(c_r\). The repository has already tested this route exactly.

If one solution \(u\) has two zeros at \(r,r+h\) in one characteristic, every independent solution \(v\) satisfies the universal transport law

\[
\boxed{
\frac{v_{r+h}}{v_r}
=-\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^3}
{(r+h)^3N_{h-1}(r)}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

The right side is independent of which projective fiber \(u=b-Qc\) is being followed. Products of these multipliers along a return chain give only the direct multiplier—the cocycle law. Thus the labels contain no new return equation.

The exact localization identity is

\[
(B_i-QC_i:0\le i\le t)
=
(C_0Q-B_0,\ B_iC_0-B_0C_i:1\le i\le t)
\tag{9.2}
\]

after inverting \(C_0\). The derivative

\[
\partial_Q(b_r-Qc_r)|_{Q=0}=-c_r\ne0
\]

only says that the distinguished fiber is a simple root in \(Q\). A simple root is a transversality statement, not a second vanishing condition.

The verifier records a particularly sharp obstruction:

\[

where the complete pair

\[
(b_{282},c_{282})=(b_{300},c_{300})\pmod{709}
\]

repeats across a nonreflection gap \(18<\sqrt{709}\), outside the standard small carrier. Its reflected repeat occurs at \(408,426\). Thus even the full normalized label is not injective on short clean projective fibers.

For the I5 route, isolation removes nearby returns in each individual characteristic. It does not convert the unit label into a cross-prime relation. A five-prime marked incidence still has five independent residual zero conditions and five unit labels in five different fields.

## 10. The Green-kernel/Frobenius compression is exact but circular

The repository goes further than a bare recurrence calculation. It packages the moving gap into the normalized Green coefficients

\[
V_m(a)=a^3\bigl(b_{a-1}c_{a+m}-c_{a-1}b_{a+m}\bigr),
\tag{10.1}
\]

whose generating function satisfies a fixed-order inhomogeneous Picard–Fuchs equation.

On the strict characteristic-\(p\) triangle, the first boundary defect is

\[
\boxed{
 p^3V_{p-a}(a)\equiv a^3b_{a-1}\pmod p.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

Cartier extraction reads exactly that Apéry coefficient. On a marked projective fiber \(d_n=b_n-Qc_n\) with \(d_{a-1}=0\), one has

\[
\boxed{
\overline{V_m(a)}=\frac{d_{a+m}}{d_a}.
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

Thus the finite Green tail is simply the normalized future row of the same recurrence. The first Frobenius boundary stores the original Hasse coefficient, and the marked tail stores the original return condition. The direct polynomial degree grows linearly with \(p\).

This does not prove that every possible crystalline/logarithmic construction is useless. It proves that the most direct companion/Cartier completion is a re-encoding of the original coefficient problem.

## 12. Is any genuinely new companion route left?

Yes, but it must leave the mod-\(p\), rank-two recurrence algebra. The smallest plausible survivor is a **first-order lifted block law**.

For a top-half prime \(p\), write

\[
m=p+r,
\qquad
p^3a_m\equiv6b_r\pmod p.
\]

Define the first defect

\[
\mathcal A_{p,m}
=\frac{p^3a_m-6b_r}{p}\pmod p,
\tag{12.1}
\]

and the Lucas defect

\[
\mathcal B_{p,m}
=\frac{b_m-5b_r}{p}\pmod p.
\tag{12.2}
\]

When \(p\mid b_r\), also define

\[
\beta_{p,r}=\frac{b_r}{p}\pmod p.
\tag{12.3}
\]

A mod-\(p^2\) block calculation will express \(\mathcal A_{p,m}\) and \(\mathcal B_{p,m}\) in terms of \(r\), \(m\), \(\beta_{p,r}\), and p-local harmonic/Fermat-quotient data.

There are two possible outcomes.

### 12.1 Terminal outcome

If the lifted law contains one freely varying local scalar—typically \(\beta_{p,r}\) or a p-dependent logarithmic/Fermat-quotient cocycle—then it remains parity-neutral. Each new congruence introduces one new local unknown. This is the failure mode already anticipated in `atom_tail_section.tex` for deeper digit variation.

### 12.2 Breakthrough outcome

A useful theorem would have to eliminate those local scalars and produce a fixed low-height object. Concretely, for aligned primes

\[
p=m-r,\qquad q=m-s,\qquad r-s=q-p,
\]

one would need an integer \(\mathfrak C_{m;r,s}\), independent of choosing residue-field lifts, such that

\[
 p\mid b_r,\ q\mid b_s
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 pq\mid\mathfrak C_{m;r,s},
\tag{12.4}
\]

with either

\[
\log|\mathfrak C_{m;r,s}|\ll (r-s)(\log m)^{O(1)}
\tag{12.5}
\]

or an aggregate height bound strong enough for I5/HM. It must also survive saturation by the obvious factors \(b_r,b_s\); otherwise it is only the original condition in disguise.

No identity currently in the repository has these properties.

### 12.3 A precise experiment

The next companion-specific experiment should therefore be:

1. derive the pair block law one order further, modulo \(p^2\), without numerical interpolation;
2. specialize to \(p\mid b_r\);
3. eliminate \(\beta_{p,r}\) using the lifted Wronskian;
4. determine whether the remaining defect is a fixed rational function of \((m,r)\), or whether a new p-local harmonic/Fermat quotient survives;
5. only in the first case, form the two-prime resultant after substituting \(p=m-r\), \(q=m-s\), and audit its exact height.

The terminal test is strict:

> If one free local unit remains per characteristic, the companion lift has not created cross-prime coherence.

This is much narrower and more informative than searching arbitrary harmonic identities.

## 13. Answers to the concrete questions

### Does \(p\mid b_r\) force \(p\mid a_{r-1}\)?

No. It forces \(a_r\) and \(b_{r-1}\) to be units, with

\[
a_r\equiv6/(r^3b_{r-1})\pmod p.
\]

The example \(73\mid b_2\), \(73\nmid a_1\) is decisive.

### Does the harmonic-number formula restrict the zero primes?

Not at the required level. The exact leading harmonic term is \(b_rH_r^{(3)}\), which vanishes automatically at a zero of \(b_r\). The remaining correction is the Wronskian-forced unit. Identity (6.3) proves that the corresponding divisibility detector factors through \(b_r\). Wolstenholme-type endpoint congruences are already reflected in the palindrome law and occur where \(b_{p-1}\) is a unit.

### Is there a resultant involving \(a_r,a_s,b_r,b_s\)?

Yes, and it is exact:

\[
a_rb_s-a_sb_r=6N_{r-s}(s)/\prod_{j=s+1}^{r}j^3.
\]

Equivalently it is the resultant in the marked fiber parameter of
\(b_s-T(a_s/6)\) and \(b_r-T(a_r/6)\). It is precisely the old gap polynomial. It vanishes for two zeros modulo one prime, not for zeros modulo two different primes.

### Can a discriminant do better?

No within the companion parameter. The discriminant factors into squares of the same pairwise resultants. Multiple marked equations reduce to one fiber pin and the ordinary projective minors.

### Does the same global companion value \(a_m\) link two top primes?

Only tautologically. The block law gives

\[
p^3a_m\equiv6b_{m-p}\pmod p,
\qquad
q^3a_m\equiv6b_{m-q}\pmod q.
\]

The companion denominator records both local hits, but its allowed height is exponential in \(m\), giving only a linear logarithmic bound. It supplies no pairwise separation or independence.

### Is the companion sequence useless?

No. It is essential for:

- the Wronskian and exact local valuations;
- proving that local zeros are transverse;
- the pair block law;
- isolating companion-block primes and bounding their total weight by \(O(n^{2/3})\);
- marking the distinguished projective fiber.

What it does **not** do is turn lower-digit zeros in different characteristics into a common low-height divisor.

## 14. Final assessment

The proposed local implication is false, and after correcting it the natural companion strategy admits an exact saturation/no-go theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{companion resultant}
=\text{gap kernel}
=\text{same-characteristic double-zero certificate}.
}
\]

The companion is nonzero at an Apéry zero. It supplies the transverse coordinate of a rank-two system. Resultants, discriminants, marked-fiber derivatives, and harmonic decompositions all collapse to the existing gap minors or to expressions visibly divisible by the original \(b_r\). Across two characteristics, one prime kills one endpoint and the other prime kills the other endpoint; no small common divisor follows.

The sharp strategic conclusion is:

> **Do not pursue a mod-\(p\) companion resultant as the cross-prime bridge. The only live companion avenue is a mod-\(p^2\) or crystalline logarithmic lift that eliminates every new p-local unit and produces a fixed low-height mixed-characteristic carrier. The first task is to derive that lifted block law and test whether it is parity-neutral.**

Until such a theorem is found, the companion sequence should be viewed as a completed local/valuation tool and a marker for the actual orbit—not as the missing cross-prime coherence mechanism.
