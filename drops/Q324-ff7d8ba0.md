# Executive verdict

The proposed Hilbert-irreducibility route does **not** apply to the Apéry gap factors in the form stated.

There are two independent, decisive obstructions.

1. A fixed polynomial
   \[
   F(U,T)\in \mathbb Q[U,T]
   \]
   has a fixed degree in \(U\), whereas
   \[
   \deg f_k=\frac{3(k-1)}2
   \]
   tends to infinity. Hence no such \(F\) can satisfy \(F(U,k)=f_k(U)\) for infinitely many odd \(k\). The same obstruction rules out a fixed rational function, a fixed finite algebraic cover, or a fixed algebraic function whose fibers contain all roots of the \(f_k\).

2. Even for a genuine fixed-degree family, Hilbert irreducibility gives preservation of irreducibility outside a **thin set**, not automatically outside a finite set. Thin sets can contain infinitely many integers. For example,
   \[
   X^2-T
   \]
   is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(T)\), but it is reducible at every square \(T=m^2\), including infinitely many odd squares. Thus certificates through \(k\le100\) would not close the argument.

There is, however, an exact fixed object behind the variable-length product. If \(N_k=-\widetilde W_k\), define

\[
\Phi(s,x,y)=\bigl(s+1,\,P(s)x-s^6y,\,x\bigr),
\qquad
P(s)=(2s+1)(17s^2+17s+5).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Phi^{k-1}(r+1,1,0)
  =\bigl(r+k,\,N_k(r),\,N_{k-1}(r)\bigr).
}
\]

So the gap polynomial is a coordinate of the \((k-1)\)-st iterate of one fixed polynomial skew product. This is a legitimate uniform reformulation, but \(k\) is the **iteration number**, not an algebraic parameter. The relevant possible theories are stability of iterate-coordinate divisors, difference Galois theory, or a monodromy tower—not classical Hilbert irreducibility.

The repository already contains the parallel analytic compression:

\[
N_k(r)=\bigl((r+2)_{k-1}\bigr)^3[z^{k-1}]F_{r+1}(z),
\]

where \(F_a(z)\) solves a fixed rank-four inhomogeneous Picard–Fuchs system. The repository explicitly records the obstruction: the gap length remains a coefficient index, and algebraizing that coefficient requires a jet whose rank grows with \(k\). The natural finite Borel/Gamma normalizations are also proved non-algebraic.

Finally, for the specific Problem 3.2 use stated in the question, the current repository already proves

\[
\operatorname{Res}_r\bigl(N_d(r),N_e(r+d)\bigr)>0
\qquad(d,e\ge2)
\]

by disjoint complex root strips. Therefore all-\(k\) irreducibility is no longer needed for shifted-resultant nonvanishing.

# 1. Normalize the family

Put

\[
N_k(r)=-\widetilde W_k(r).
\]

Then

\[
N_0=0,\qquad N_1=1,
\]

and

\[
N_{k+1}(r)
 =P(r+k)N_k(r)-(r+k)^6N_{k-1}(r).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The formalized repository definition is exactly this recurrence, with

\[
\deg_r N_k=3(k-1)
\]

in the intended exact-degree normalization.

For odd \(k=2m+1\), reflection gives

\[
N_{2m+1}(r)
 =-f_{2m+1}\bigl((r+m+1)^2\bigr),
\]

up to the harmless sign coming from \(N_k=-\widetilde W_k\), and

\[
\deg_U f_{2m+1}=3m.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus it is cleaner to write

\[
g_m(U)=f_{2m+1}(U),
\qquad \deg g_m=3m.
\]

The question becomes whether the sequence \(g_m\) can be the sequence of fibers of one finite algebraic family over the \(m\)-line.

It cannot.

# 2. No fixed polynomial \(F(U,T)\) can have these fibers

## Proposition 2.1: degree obstruction

There is no polynomial \(F(U,T)\in\mathbb Q[U,T]\) such that

\[
F(U,m)=g_m(U)
\]

for infinitely many nonnegative integers \(m\).

### Proof

Write

\[
F(U,T)=a_0(T)+a_1(T)U+\cdots+a_d(T)U^d,
\qquad a_d\ne0.
\]

For all but finitely many integers \(m\), one has \(a_d(m)\ne0\). Hence

\[
\deg_U F(U,m)=d
\]

for all but finitely many \(m\). But

\[
\deg_U g_m=3m\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Contradiction. ∎

The same proof remains valid if one asks only for odd \(k\), substitutes \(k=2m+1\), permits multiplication by a nonzero scalar depending on \(m\), or applies an affine change of the \(U\)-coordinate depending algebraically on \(m\): none of those operations changes the fact that the \(U\)-degree of a fixed algebraic expression is bounded.

## Corollary 2.2: no fixed rational function

There is no

\[
R(U,T)\in\mathbb Q(U,T)
\]

whose specialization at \(T=m\) is \(g_m(U)\) for infinitely many \(m\), outside the poles of \(R\).

Indeed, write \(R=A/B\) in reduced form. The \(U\)-degrees of \(A\) and \(B\) are fixed. Whenever \(R(U,m)\) is a polynomial, its degree is bounded in terms of those fixed degrees. It cannot equal a polynomial of degree \(3m\).

This directly corrects the statement in the question that “fixing \(r\) and varying \(k\) gives a rational function in two variables.” A polynomial-coefficient recurrence in \(k\) gives a **P-recursive sequence**, not a rational function of \(k\).

There is also an elementary growth witness in the present family. The Green-kernel identity in the repository gives

\[
N_k(0)=U_{k-1}(1)=(k!)^3c_k,
\]

where \(c_k\) is the positive companion solution. Thus even at one fixed value of \(r\), the sequence has factorial-scale growth, which is incompatible with rational dependence on \(k\).

# 3. A stronger no-go: no fixed algebraic function can produce the \(f_k\)

One might weaken the request and ask for a fixed polynomial relation

\[
H(Y,U,T)=0
\]

such that, at \(T=m\), one algebraic branch is the polynomial

\[
Y=g_m(U).
\]

Even that is impossible.

## Proposition 3.1: bounded degree of polynomial branches

Let

\[
H(Y,U,T)\in\mathbb Q[Y,U,T]\setminus\{0\}.
\]

Then there is a constant \(B_H\) such that, for all but finitely many rational \(t_0\), every polynomial \(q(U)\in\overline{\mathbb Q}[U]\) satisfying

\[
H(q(U),U,t_0)=0
\]

has

\[
\deg q\le B_H.
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
H(Y,U,T)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}h_i(U,T)Y^i,
\qquad h_d\ne0,
\]

and let

\[
D=\max_{i<d}\deg_U h_i.
\]

Except for finitely many \(t_0\), the polynomial \(h_d(U,t_0)\) is not identically zero. Let \(M=\deg q\). The term

\[
h_d(U,t_0)q(U)^d
\]

has \(U\)-degree at least \(dM\). Every lower term has degree at most

\[
D+iM\le D+(d-1)M.
\]

If \(M>D\), the \(i=d\) term has strictly larger degree than every other term, so it cannot cancel. Therefore \(M\le D\). ∎

Consequently there is no fixed algebraic function \(Y(U,T)\) whose specializations are the \(g_m(U)\), and no finite-type algebraic cover that contains these polynomials as polynomial sections of unbounded degree.

There is an even simpler version for the roots. A fixed equation

\[
G(U,T)=0
\]

with \(d=\deg_U G\) has at most \(d\) geometric branches over a generic \(T\). The polynomial \(g_m\) has \(3m\) roots counting multiplicity. Since \(3m\to\infty\), all roots of all \(g_m\) cannot be branches of one finite algebraic cover of the \(T\)-line.

Geometrically: a finite morphism to the \(T\)-line has one fixed generic degree. The desired fibers have degrees \(3m\), so they belong to a tower of covers of growing degree, not one cover.

This also shows that an expression such as

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q(T))=S_{3(T-1)/2}
\]

is not well-formed in classical Galois theory. A polynomial over \(\mathbb Q(T)\) has one fixed integer degree \(d\) and one fixed finite Galois group contained in \(S_d\). There is no finite group \(S_{3(T-1)/2}\) with a transcendental symbolic degree.

# 4. What the variable-length product *can* be reformulated as

Although there is no Hilbert parameter \(T=k\), the recurrence admits several exact uniform encodings.

## 4.1 A fixed clocked polynomial map

Let

\[
M(s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(s)&-s^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
\binom{N_k(r)}{N_{k-1}(r)}
 =M(r+k-1)\cdots M(r+1)inom10.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Introduce the clock as a state variable and define

\[
\Phi:\mathbb A^3\to\mathbb A^3,
\qquad
\Phi(s,x,y)=\bigl(s+1,P(s)x-s^6y,x\bigr).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Induction using (1.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
\Phi^{k-1}(r+1,1,0)
  =\bigl(r+k,N_k(r),N_{k-1}(r)\bigr).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the cleanest answer to the “range of the product” issue. The range is absorbed into iteration of one fixed map.

However, (4.3) does **not** make \(k\) an algebraic coordinate. The map

\[
k\longmapsto \Phi^k
\]

is a semigroup iterate, just as \(x\mapsto x^{2^k}\) is an iterate rather than a polynomial in the pair \((x,k)\).

A possible all-\(k\) theorem would have to be a stability statement of the form:

> the pullback of the coordinate divisor \(x=0\) along the section
> \[
> \iota(r)=(r+1,1,0)
> \]
> under \(\Phi^{k-1}\), after quotienting by the forced reflection involution, is arithmetically irreducible for every \(k\).

That is an iterate-divisor or dynamical-stability problem. It is not Hilbert irreducibility.

## 4.2 A fixed Ore/difference operator

Let \(E\) be the shift operator in \(k\), so \(E(y_k)=y_{k+1}\). Equation (1.1) is equivalently

\[
\mathcal L_r N=0,
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_r
 =E^2-P(r+k+1)E+(r+k+1)^6
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

in the Ore ring

\[
\mathbb Q(r,k)\langle E\rangle,
\qquad Ek=(k+1)E.
\]

Thus there is a single rank-two **difference module** over \(\mathbb Q(r,k)\). Its difference Galois group is a legitimate uniform invariant. Proving that this module has no rank-one submodule would exclude hypergeometric factorization of the recurrence operator and would explain the absence of a simple closed form.

But this is not yet a proof that every individual polynomial \(N_k(r)\), or its centered quotient \(f_k(U)\), is irreducible. Difference-module irreducibility concerns invariant solution spaces under the shift; polynomial factorization of one particular solution as a function of \(r\) is a different property. A new bridge theorem would be required.

## 4.3 The universal continuant tower

Define the generic continuants

\[
K_0=1,\qquad K_1=X_1,
\]

and

\[
K_n=X_nK_{n-1}-Q_{n-1}K_{n-2}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equivalently, \(K_n\) is the determinant of the generic tridiagonal matrix with diagonal \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) and products of opposite off-diagonal entries \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_{n-1}\).

The Apéry gap polynomial is the specialization

\[
\boxed{
N_k(r)=K_{k-1}
\left(
\begin{array}{l}
X_i=P(r+i),\\
Q_i=(r+i+1)^6
\end{array}
\right).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

For every fixed \(n\), the generic continuant \(K_n\) is irreducible in its independent variables. One proves this inductively by viewing (4.5) as a primitive linear polynomial in \(X_n\): consecutive continuants are coprime, and the new variable \(Q_{n-1}\) is independent of \(K_{n-1}\).

This generic irreducibility does not solve the Apéry specialization. Formula (4.6) collapses \(2n-1\) independent parameters onto one very special polynomial curve in coefficient space. Specialization can destroy irreducibility; in fact the reflection symmetry deliberately forces the composition

\[
N_{2m+1}(r)=\pm f_{2m+1}\bigl((r+m+1)^2\bigr).
\]

The universal object is therefore the **tower**

\[
K_1,K_2,K_3,\ldots,
\]

or an ind-object with matrix size tending to infinity, not one finite polynomial \(F(U,T)\).

## 4.4 The fixed Green-kernel differential system

The repository gives another exact compression. Let

\[
U_{-1}(a)=0,\qquad U_0(a)=1,
\]

\[
U_{m+1}(a)=P(a+m)U_m(a)-(a+m)^6U_{m-1}(a),
\]

and

\[
V_m(a)=\frac{U_m(a)}{(a+1)_m^3},
\qquad
F_a(z)=\sum_{m\ge0}V_m(a)z^m.
\]

Then

\[
\left((\theta+a)^3-zP(\theta+a)+z^2(\theta+a+1)^3\right)F_a(z)=a^3,
\qquad \theta=z\frac d{dz},
\tag{4.7}

and

\[
\boxed{
N_k(r)=\bigl((r+2)_{k-1}\bigr)^3[z^{k-1}]F_{r+1}(z).
}
\tag{4.8}

Equation (4.7) has fixed order and fixed singular support, independently of \(k\). But (4.8) again places \(k\) in a coefficient-extraction operator. To make the coefficient \([z^{k-1}]\) into an algebraic coordinate by a jet construction requires a jet space of dimension growing with \(k\). This is exactly the “green-jet barrier” already recorded in the repository.

Moreover, [`gap_kernel_borel_gauges.tex`](../problems/3.2/gap_kernel_borel_gauges.tex) proves that the four natural finite Borel/Gamma normalizations of the bivariate gap array are not algebraic over the corresponding rational function field. The lcm-normalized integral escape is even non-D-finite. These results do not rule out every imaginable exotic encoding, but they rule out the standard finite algebraic/period encodings that could feed Hilbert irreducibility.

## 4.5 Tautological interpolation exists, but is useless

For completeness, any sequence of polynomials can be interpolated on the nonnegative integers by an infinite Newton series. If

\[
g_m(U)=f_{2m+1}(U),
\]

then formally

\[
\widehat F(U,T)
 =\sum_{j\ge0}(\Delta^jg)_0(U)\binom{T}{j}
\tag{4.9}
\]

satisfies

\[
\widehat F(U,m)=g_m(U)
\]

for every nonnegative integer \(m\), because the sum truncates at \(j=m\).

But (4.9) is an infinite Newton series, not an element of \(\mathbb Q[U,T]\), \(\mathbb Q(U,T)\), or a fixed finite algebraic extension thereof. Every sequence admits such an interpolation, so it contains no irreducibility information and Hilbert irreducibility does not apply.

Likewise, one can tautologically form

\[
\mathcal G(U,z)=\sum_{m\ge0}g_m(U)z^m.
\]

Even if this generating series were rational or algebraic, irreducibility of the series would not imply irreducibility of its coefficients. The elementary rational series

\[
\frac1{1-Uz}=\sum_{m\ge0}U^mz^m
\]

already has highly reducible coefficient polynomials for every \(m\ge2\).

# 5. What Hilbert irreducibility would actually give

Suppose, contrary to the degree obstruction, that one had a genuine fixed-degree polynomial

\[
F(U,T)\in\mathbb Q[T][U]
\]

that is irreducible and separable over \(\mathbb Q(T)\).

Hilbert irreducibility says that the set of \(t_0\in\mathbb Q\) for which \(F(U,t_0)\) remains irreducible—and, under the usual regularity hypotheses, retains the generic Galois group—contains a Hilbert subset. Equivalently, the exceptional set is thin.

It does **not** say that the exceptional set is finite.

## 5.1 Infinite thin exceptional sets

The simplest counterexample is

\[
F(U,T)=U^2-T.
\]

It is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(T)\), but

\[
F(U,m^2)=(U-m)(U+m)
\]

for every integer \(m\). Thus there are infinitely many reducible integer specializations. Restricting to odd parameters does not help: the odd squares give infinitely many odd exceptions.

Quantitative Hilbert irreducibility theorems bound the **number** of exceptional specializations of height at most \(H\) for a fixed \(F\). They typically imply density zero or a power saving, not a largest exceptional integer. An infinite sequence such as the squares is entirely compatible with such estimates.

Therefore the inference

\[
\text{generic irreducibility}
\Longrightarrow
\text{irreducible for all but finitely many integer }k
\]

is false without substantial extra hypotheses.

## 5.2 Why DDF through \(k\le100\) would still not close the argument

There are two separate gaps.

1. If the exceptional set is merely thin, it may contain infinitely many integers larger than \(100\).
2. Even if a separate theorem proves that the exceptional set is finite, a non-effective finiteness theorem does not imply that all exceptions are below \(100\).

To combine a finite computation with a theoretical finiteness theorem, one needs either

- an explicit height bound for every exceptional integer, or
- an explicit determination of the exceptional set.

Basic Hilbert irreducibility supplies neither.

## 5.3 What upgrades thinness to finiteness

For a fixed cover, bad specializations can be described using rational points on quotient or resolvent covers corresponding to proper subgroups of the generic Galois group. To prove that only finitely many rational specializations are bad, one must show that the relevant quotient curves have sufficiently large genus and then invoke a Diophantine finiteness theorem, while treating genus \(0\) and genus \(1\) quotients separately.

David Krumm’s work on dynatomic polynomials is a useful exact analogy. For each fixed dynatomic level, Hilbert irreducibility gives a thin exceptional set. Some low levels have infinite exceptional sets. Finiteness for other levels requires separate quotient-curve genus calculations and Faltings’s theorem; it does not follow from Hilbert irreducibility alone.

Even that paradigm does not directly transfer here, because the degree and the generic group would change with every \(k\). One would face an infinite tower of larger and larger resolvent covers and would need uniform control of their rational points.

# 6. A legitimate level-by-level Hilbert family—and why it misses the Apéry point

One can introduce a genuine deformation parameter while holding \(k\) fixed. For example, define

\[
N^{(T)}_0=0,\qquad N^{(T)}_1=1,
\]

\[
N^{(T)}_{j+1}(r)
 =P(r+j)N^{(T)}_j(r)
  -T(r+j)^6N^{(T)}_{j-1}(r).
\tag{6.1}
\]

For each fixed odd \(k\), reflection still yields a centered quotient

\[
N^{(T)}_k(r)
 =F_k\left((r+(k+1)/2)^2,T\right)
\]

for a genuine polynomial

\[
F_k(U,T)\in\mathbb Q[U,T].
\]

This is an honest Hilbert setup **for each fixed \(k\)**. One could try to prove

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(F_k/\mathbb Q(T))=S_{n_k},
\qquad n_k=\frac{3(k-1)}2.
\]

Hilbert irreducibility would then show that \(F_k(U,t_0)\) has group \(S_{n_k}\) for \(t_0\) outside a thin set \(E_k\).

But the Apéry polynomial is the preselected specialization

\[
T=1.
\]

Hilbert irreducibility gives no reason that \(1\notin E_k\), let alone that \(1\notin E_k\) for every \(k\). The fixed point \(T=1\) could lie in every moving exceptional set \(E_k\) without contradicting any Hilbert theorem.

The even more elementary family

\[
f_k(U)-T
\]

is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(T)\) for every nonconstant \(f_k\). Hilbert irreducibility then says that most translates \(f_k(U)-t_0\) are irreducible. This plainly says nothing about the target translate \(t_0=0\), whose irreducibility is exactly the original question.

Thus levelwise Hilbert families can be useful for understanding generic deformations, but they do not certify the Apéry specialization.

Let

\[
T_k(r)=M(r+k-1)\cdots M(r+1),
\qquad T_1(r)=I.

Then

\[
T_k(r)e_1=inom{N_k(r)}{N_{k-1}(r)},
\qquad

Since

\[

 =\bigl((r+1)_{k-1}\bigr)^6.
\tag{7.2}

The characteristic polynomial is therefore

\[
\chi_k(\Lambda;r)
 =\Lambda^2-\operatorname{tr}(T_k(r))\Lambda
  +\bigl((r+1)_{k-1}\bigr)^6.
\tag{7.3}

This route fails for three reasons.

1. The sequence \(\chi_k\) is still indexed by a product of variable length. There is no fixed polynomial \(\chi(\Lambda,r,k)\in\mathbb Q[\Lambda,r,k]\) for the same degree-growth reason.
2. As a polynomial in the eigenvalue variable \(\Lambda\), \(\chi_k\) has degree \(2\), so its Galois group over \(\mathbb Q(r)\) is at most \(S_2\). It cannot encode the conjectural \(S_{n_k}\)-action on the \(n_k\) roots of \(f_k\).
3. Irreducibility of a matrix characteristic polynomial does not imply irreducibility of one matrix entry.

For the last point, take

\[
g(r)=(r-1)(r+1)
\]

and

\[
B(r)=
\begin{pmatrix}
g(r)&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
\det(\Lambda I-B(r))
 =\Lambda^2-g(r)\Lambda-1.
\]

Its discriminant

\[
g(r)^2+4=r^4-2r^2+5
\]

is squarefree and hence not a square in \(\mathbb Q(r)\), so the characteristic polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(r)\). Yet the \((1,1)\)-entry

\[

\]

is reducible.

The determinant that is genuinely relevant is instead the growing tridiagonal continuant

\[
N_k(r)=\det\mathsf A_k(r),
\]

where \(\mathsf A_k\) has size \(k-1\). But calling this a characteristic polynomial after a companion linearization only replaces the growing polynomial degree by a growing matrix dimension. It still does not produce a fixed Hilbert family.

# 8. The roots are not branches of one algebraic function of \(k\)

For a fixed algebraic relation

\[
G(U,T)=0,
\]

the number of generic branches \(U(T)\) is the fixed integer \(\deg_U G\). The number of roots of \(f_k\) is

\[
n_k=\frac{3(k-1)}2\to\infty.
\]

Therefore the roots cannot all be branches of one finite algebraic function.

One could imagine countably many branches, with new branches appearing as \(k\) grows. That is an infinite-degree cover or ind-scheme, outside classical Hilbert irreducibility.

There is also no natural root-by-root propagation in the recurrence. If \(\alpha\) is a root of \(N_k\), then (1.1) gives

\[
N_{k+1}(\alpha)
 =-(\alpha+k)^6N_{k-1}(\alpha),
\]

which is generically nonzero. A root at level \(k\) is not carried to a root at level \(k+1\). The recurrence propagates **values**, not individual roots.

Finally, “the branches are not algebraically related” would not logically imply irreducibility even if formalized. Cross-level independence is different from within-level transitivity. A sequence

\[
(U-a_k)h_k(U)
\]

can have roots with no coherent algebraic relation as \(k\) varies while being reducible at every level. To prove \(f_k\) irreducible one must prove that its roots form one Galois orbit at that fixed level. Saying that its Galois group is \(S_{n_k}\) already includes that transitivity, so using the \(S_{n_k}\) expectation to prove irreducibility is circular unless one supplies independent group certificates.

# 9. What a realistic all-\(k\) irreducibility program would look like

The correct methods are recurrence-specific, analogous to Schur- or Filaseta-type irreducibility arguments for polynomial sequences whose degree is their index.

## 9.1 Uniform modular certificates

For each odd \(k\), it is enough to find a prime \(p=p(k)\) such that

\[
f_k(U)\bmod p
\]

is irreducible. More economically, to prove the full group \(S_{n_k}\), one can seek a bounded collection of primes depending on \(k\) whose exact factorization patterns force

- transitivity or primitivity,
- a large prime cycle, and
- an odd permutation or a transposition.

The computational DDF data show that such primes are plentiful for small \(k\). The missing theorem is a uniform construction of suitable primes or factorization patterns from the recurrence.

A plausible concrete target is to derive the recurrence for the centered odd/even cores over \(\mathbb F_p[U]\), then prove that for a prime in a controlled interval or congruence class the reduction has a prescribed pattern. This would be a genuine all-\(k\) theorem; Hilbert irreducibility is not needed.

## 9.2 Newton-polygon exclusion of factor degrees

For each \(k\), compute \(p\)-adic Newton polygons of the primitive centered quotient at primes selected from the arithmetic of \(k\). Dumas’s factor theorem constrains the degrees of possible factors through the horizontal lengths and slope denominators of the polygon.

The observed \(2\)-adic polygons alone are unlikely to finish the job because the displayed slopes are integral, so their denominators do not force large inertia cycles. The repeated blocks may still give strong divisibility restrictions, but one probably needs odd primes tied to factors of centered shifts \(r+j\), or several primes whose allowed factor-degree sets have trivial intersection.

A sharp useful theorem would have the form:

> for every \(k\), the union of Newton-polygon constraints at primes \(p_1(k),\ldots,p_s(k)\) permits only factor degrees \(0\) and \(n_k\).

That directly proves irreducibility.

## 9.3 Difference-Galois plus a new divisor-stability bridge

The fixed Ore operator (4.4) is a natural structural object. One can attempt to prove that its difference Galois group is as large as possible and that the special solution selected by \(N_0=0,N_1=1\) is not contained in a proper algebraic submodule.

This would explain generic complexity, but a second theorem is still needed:

> large difference Galois group implies arithmetic irreducibility of the folded pullback divisors
> \[
> (x\circ\Phi^{k-1}\circ\iota)=0.
> \]

No such implication is currently present in the repository, and it is not a standard black box.

## 9.4 Monodromy of a growing spectral pencil

The continuant determinant realizes \(N_k\) as the determinant of a polynomial tridiagonal matrix of size \(k-1\). After centering and quotienting by the reflection involution, one can linearize the polynomial eigenvalue problem and study the arithmetic monodromy of the resulting spectral cover.

A successful proof would need a uniform argument in the matrix size, for example:

- connectedness/primitivity from the band graph,
- a simple branch point giving a transposition,
- and a long cycle supplied by local behavior at a suitable place.

This is closer in spirit to the observed \(S_{n_k}\) groups than Hilbert irreducibility. But it remains a tower-of-monodromy problem, one level for each \(k\).

# 10. Consequence for the Problem 3.2 dependency graph

The current repository proves, in [`meso_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/meso_result.tex), that every root \(\alpha\) of \(N_d\) satisfies

\[
-d<\operatorname{Re}\alpha<-1.
\]

The roots of \(N_e(r+d)\) lie in the disjoint strip

\[
-d-e<\operatorname{Re}r<-d-1.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_r\bigl(N_d(r),N_e(r+d)\bigr)>0
\qquad(d,e\ge2).
}
\]

This is unconditional and does not use irreducibility or separability. The later Q319 argument also proves the exact half-integer central evaluation classification needed for the even factors.

Therefore, if the only role assigned to all-\(k\) irreducibility was to deduce shifted-resultant nonvanishing, that role should be removed from the dependency graph. The correct implication is

\[
\text{continuant diagonal dominance + reflection}
\Longrightarrow
\text{disjoint root strips}
\Longrightarrow
\text{shifted resultant nonvanishing}.
\]

Irreducibility remains an interesting independent Galois problem and may still be useful for sharper arithmetic information, but Hilbert irreducibility does not provide the missing all-\(k\) theorem.

# 11. Direct answers to the five questions

## 1. Can one define one \(F(U,T)\in\mathbb Q[U,T]\) with \(F(U,k)=f_k(U)\)?

**No.** This is impossible because a fixed \(F\) has bounded \(U\)-degree while \(\deg f_k\to\infty\). The same no-go holds for fixed rational or finite algebraic families.

The exact uniform substitutes are the clocked iterate (4.3), the Ore operator (4.4), the continuant tower (4.6), and the Green-kernel coefficient formula (4.8).

## 2. Would Hilbert irreducibility imply all but finitely many \(f_k\) are irreducible?

**No.** It would imply irreducibility outside a thin set for a genuine fixed-degree family. A thin set can contain infinitely many integers. Even a separate non-effective finiteness theorem would not combine with checks through \(100\) without an explicit height bound.

## 3. Can the product-range dependence be reformulated?

**Yes, but not as a Hilbert parameter.** It is exactly the iteration count of the fixed polynomial map

\[
\Phi(s,x,y)=(s+1,P(s)x-s^6y,x).
\]

Equivalently it is governed by one fixed difference operator. This changes the problem into stability of iterate-coordinate divisors or a difference/monodromy tower.

## 4. Does irreducibility of the transfer-product characteristic polynomial imply irreducibility of \(f_k\)?

**No.** The characteristic polynomial is quadratic in the eigenvalue variable and controls eigenvalues of the \(2\times2\) product. The gap polynomial is one matrix entry. Irreducibility of the former has no general implication for the latter, as the explicit matrix counterexample in §7 shows.

## 5. Do algebraically independent root branches force irreducibility?

**No.** There is no fixed finite set of algebraic branches because the number of roots grows with \(k\), and the recurrence does not propagate roots branchwise. Cross-level independence would not imply within-level Galois transitivity in any case.

# 12. References on what Hilbert irreducibility does and does not supply

- A. Castillo and R. Dietmann, [*On Hilbert's irreducibility theorem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00314). Quantitative bounds for exceptional specializations of a fixed polynomial family.
- M. Paredes and R. Sasyk, [*Effective Hilbert's Irreducibility Theorem for global fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10420). Effective counting of specializations that fail to preserve irreducibility or Galois group.
- D. Krumm, [*A finiteness theorem for specializations of dynatomic polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11152). A particularly relevant example where Hilbert irreducibility gives only thinness, while finiteness requires quotient-curve genus calculations and Faltings; some low-level exceptional sets are infinite.
- T. Monderer and D. Neftin, [*Symmetric Galois groups under specialization*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11856-022-2302-x). Analysis of groups occurring infinitely often under specialization through low-genus subcovers.

# Bottom line

There is no single irreducible \(F(U,T)\in\mathbb Q[U,T]\) waiting to be found: its existence is ruled out by the unbounded degree of the fibers. The variable-length transfer product can be made autonomous only by treating \(k\) as iteration number, and the repository’s fixed differential encoding likewise retains \(k\) as a coefficient index. Both point toward a tower/stability theorem, not Hilbert irreducibility.

For the immediate Ramanujan Challenge application, use the already-proved root-strip resultant theorem. For the independent all-\(k\) Galois conjecture, the most concrete viable routes are uniform modular factorization certificates, multi-prime Newton-polygon exclusion, or a uniform monodromy theorem for the growing continuant pencil.
