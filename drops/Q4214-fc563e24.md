# 8. Rank-sum identity: the full band theorem is exactly a shorter-scale L2 theorem

\tag{8.1}

Equivalently,

=2\sum_{1\le\Delta<\Lambda}
\operatorname{rank}P_{\mathfrak c,\Delta}.
\tag{8.2}

\operatorname{rank}P_{x,j,\Delta}^{\tau}.

\tag{8.3}

Therefore an aggregate rank theorem for the unweighted popular-gap bands is not easier than the actual selected cell second moment. It is **identically the same quantity**.

\boxed{

\tag{8.4}

There is no fixed `T^{-rho}` here.

# 10. Why ordinary dispersion norms do not prove PBSD

There are two natural attempts.

## 10.1 Uncentered Schatten energy

For the row-space band `(2.1)`,

\[
\|\mathbf B_\Delta\|_F^2
\ge
\sum_\mathfrak c r_\mathfrak c(\Delta)^2.
\tag{10.1}
\]

For every rich core, `(4.5)` gives

\[
\sum_{\Delta<D_0}r_\mathfrak c(\Delta)^2
\gg\frac{K_\mathfrak c^3}{\Lambda}
\ge
\frac{H^2}{\Lambda}K_\mathfrak c.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Thus the uncentered Frobenius energy is automatically **large** on every rich set. A power-small uncentered bound would already be a theorem excluding rich sets; it is not supplied by linear algebra.

## 10.2 Centered dispersion

Subtracting a density main term removes the unavoidable contribution `(10.2)`. But the popular-gap theorem only provides a band at the density scale

\[
r_\mathfrak c(\Delta)\asymp K_\mathfrak c^2/\Lambda.
\]

It need not create a large centered remainder. Therefore a strong centered dispersion theorem can coexist with a large DPRM mass.

This is why the usual dichotomy

```text
large correlation => spectral spike,
otherwise dispersion
```

fails here. The correlation is neither a spike nor an excess; it is the mandatory average correlation of a set of density `K/Lambda`.

# 11. The arithmetic rank step and the common obstruction with the module route

The support matrix `(3.2)` has no Apéry values in its entries. To use the recurrence, one must attach nontrivial local state data to each zero.

For an actual zero `p|b_r`, no-consecutive-zero gives

\[
b_{r+1}\not\equiv0\pmod p
\]

in the deep stable range. Thus the first nonzero local recurrence state naturally lives in

\[
\mathbb F_p^2
\]

or in a projective line over `F_p`. For another column `p'=p+Delta`, the corresponding state lives in

\[
\mathbb F_{p'}^2.
\]

There is no canonical linear map

\[
\mathbb F_p^2\longrightarrow\mathbb F_{p'}^2.
\]

If all columns are placed in the semisimple product ring

\[
\mathcal R_S=\prod_{p\in S}\mathbb F_p,
\tag{11.1}
\]

then the central idempotents separate the characteristics. Column relations are componentwise; cross-column cancellation is impossible. The rank is the direct sum of local ranks, exactly as in the support partial-isometry theorem.

To compare columns, one must descend from `(11.1)` to a common diagonal coefficient ring, normally `Z` or `Q`. A nontrivial rank deficiency is then certified by characteristic-zero minors or syzygies. The row content at column `p` contributes the factor `p`; a minor involving columns `p,p'` is exactly a cross-characteristic scalar carrier of the Q4185 type. A growing minor or minimal approximant basis is exactly the growing transfer-module object being attacked in the parallel round.

Therefore:

```text
support matrix over C:
  partial isometry; no arithmetic cancellation.

local recurrence matrix over product F_p:
  columns separated by idempotents; no cross-p cancellation.

common-field arithmetic matrix over Z/Q:
  minors/syzygies are required;
  bounded ones hit the Theta(T) height/restart no-go;
  growing ones are the transfer-module problem.
```

This is the cross-characteristic diagonal-descent obstruction.

## 11.1 Correct use of the two-slope determinants

They do **not** identify `F_p` with `F_{p+Delta}`, do not supply a common phase, and do not lower the rank of `(3.6)`. No transversality claim is used outside this scope.

The popular-gap **support** band is not low rank and is not anomalously correlated. It is a partial isometry at the natural density baseline. The inverse theorem has already forced exactly the amount of rank that any `K`-point subset of a `Lambda`-interval must have.
