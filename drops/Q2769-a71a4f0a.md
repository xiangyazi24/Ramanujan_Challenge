# 5. What consecutive heights actually give

There **is** an exact adjacent-height identity in the current Padé system, but it is a Plücker/Christoffel identity for the primitive numerator polynomials.

\[

The exact cross determinant is
\[
C_{h,a}(x,y)
=
P_{h-1,a}(x)P_{h,a}(y)
-P_{h-1,a}(y)P_{h,a}(x).
\tag{5.1}

The reciprocal-kernel calculation expresses this through a Christoffel/Schur determinant with the full primitive scaling factors retained.

For `a=1`, after the universal basis clearing disappears, the identity is especially transparent:

=(2n+1)\operatorname{Res}_Z(P_{h-1,1},P_{h,1})

\tag{5.2}

# 6. Why there is no direct recurrence for the Smith denominators

The identity
\[
\sum_a c_{h,A,a}P_{h,a}=d_{h,A}\Phi_A

says that `d_{h,A}` is the order/index of the top Newton coordinate in one primitive lattice
\[
L_{h,A}:=\sum_{a=0}^{A}\mathbf Z P_{h,a}
\subset \mathbf Z[Y]_{\le A}.

When `h` is replaced by `h+1`, **all** primitive Padé pairs change because the interpolation/training set changes.  In particular,
\[
L_{h,A}\not\subseteq L_{h+1,A}
\quad\text{and}\quad
L_{h+1,A}\not\subseteq L_{h,A}
\]
in general.  There is no nested-lattice argument giving
\[
d_{h,A}\mid d_{h+1,A}
\quad\text{or}\quad
d_{h+1,A}\mid d_{h,A}.

The Christoffel transform relates the corresponding **rational** orthogonal polynomials, but the primitive scale factors are exactly where the Smith denominators and prefix/lcm content enter.  Clearing those coefficients integrally is the arithmetic problem, not a free consequence of the rational identity.

Thus an asserted integer relation such as
\[
u_h d_{h,A}+v_h d_{h+1,A}=R_h
\tag{6.1}

with a small explicit `R_h` would be genuinely new.  Moreover every safe target prime divides both denominators, so it would necessarily satisfy

p\mid R_h.
\tag{6.2}
\]
If `R_h` had sublinear total height over enough heights, (6.1) would already solve the target-radical problem.  No such relation is presently derived by the Padé Toda/Plücker identities.

# 9. What can safely be gcd'ed across heights

Because Q2758 proves target survival at every legal height, the following operation is valid:
\[
\boxed{
D_{n,q,A}

\tag{9.1}

It is strictly preferable to a product of the denominators, and it can genuinely remove height-specific nuisance primes.

What is **not** currently proved is a useful upper bound for its remaining candidate-window part.

One should also avoid requiring an upper bound for every prime power of the full integer `D_{n,q,A}`.  That is stronger than the target problem.  The target only needs the squarefree physical candidate-window support.

\mathscr D_{n,q,A}

R_q^{\rm safe}(n)\mid \mathscr D_{n,q,A},

# 10. Smallest still-open denominator-gcd theorem

The minimal theorem needed from this route is therefore not
\[
D_{n,q,A}=1
\]
and not even
\[
\log D_{n,q,A}=o_q(n).
\]
Those are unnecessarily strong.

The exact smallest useful denominator-gcd statement is
\[
\boxed{
\log \mathscr D_{n,q,A}
=
\sum_{\substack{
 p\in\mathcal P_{q,A}(n)\\
 p\mid D_{n,q,A}
}}
\log p
=o_q(n).
}
\tag{10.1}
\]
Together with (9.4), this immediately gives the desired sublinear safe target radical.

A cleaner but stronger theorem is
\[
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad} D_{n,q,A}=o_q(n).
}
\tag{10.2}
\]
Still stronger is
\[
\log D_{n,q,A}=o_q(n).
\tag{10.3}
\]
Neither (10.1), (10.2), nor (10.3) is presently proved by the known adjacent-height identities.

The dual-tail divisor (7.1) shows exactly what (10.1) must overcome: the common denominator gcd contains a genuine high-prime lcm shadow of the Apéry prefix, not merely accidental primitive-normalization factors.
