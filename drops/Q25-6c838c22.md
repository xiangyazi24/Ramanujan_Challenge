# Q25: Direct CRT, triple moments, and the route to (HM)_3

## Executive conclusion

The direct CRT expansion is not the right proof mechanism for `(HM)_3`. The obstruction is not a missing estimate: for three moving prime moduli the interval-discrepancy error is genuinely of the same size as a worst-case count of CRT boxes, and no unconditional cancellation is available from the CRT geometry alone.

The promising route is to replace the raw CRT residue distribution by the arithmetic structure of the Apéry zero sets `Z_p`. The natural object is a triple correlation of the trace functions controlling the zero indicators. A sheaf-theoretic/K3 route can in principle produce the needed square-root cancellation, but it must be formulated as a mixed-character-sum problem, not as an elementary CRT discrepancy problem.

---

## 1. Why the naive CRT proof fails

For

\[
K_X(m)=\sum_{X<p\le 2X}1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p},
\]

we have

\[
\sum_m (K_X(m))_3
=\sum_{p,q,r\ distinct}|\{m:m\equiv a\pmod p,\ b\pmod q,\ c\pmod r\}|.
\]

The expected density contribution is

\[
X^2\sum_{p,q,r}\frac{|Z_p||Z_q||Z_r|}{pqr}
\asymp X^2\lambda_X^3.
\]

However, because `pqr > X^2`, each CRT class contributes either zero or one point in `[0,X^2)`. Therefore the individual discrepancy is bounded only by

\[
O(1)
\]

per CRT triple, and summing absolute values gives

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}|Z_p||Z_q||Z_r|
\asymp \left(\sum_p|Z_p|\right)^3
\asymp X^3/(\log X)^3.
\]

This exceeds the desired main term by a factor about `X`.

Therefore an absolute-value CRT estimate cannot prove `(HM)_3`.

---

## 2. Exact CRT error structure

Let

\[
M=pqr
\]

and let `R(a,b,c)` be the CRT representative in `[0,M)`:

\[
R\equiv a\pmod p,\quad R\equiv b\pmod q,\quad R\equiv c\pmod r.
\]

The count in `[0,L)` is

\[
N(a,b,c)=\left\lfloor\frac{L-1-R(a,b,c)}{M}\right\rfloor+1
\]

when `R<L`, and zero otherwise. Equivalently,

\[
N(a,b,c)=\frac LM+\psi\left(\frac{R(a,b,c)}M\right),
\]

where `psi` is a bounded sawtooth function of mean zero.

Hence the total error is exactly

\[
\sum_{a\in Z_p,b\in Z_q,c\in Z_r}
\psi(R(a,b,c)/pqr).
\]

The required estimate is therefore a bound on a triple incomplete character sum over the CRT parametrization.

There is no general theorem saying this is small for arbitrary subsets `Z_p,Z_q,Z_r`. The anchored-rainbow-star counterexamples show that arbitrary sparse sets can correlate perfectly with CRT fibers.

---

## 3. What cancellation would be needed

A sufficient statement would be something like:

\[
\sum_{p,q,r}\left|
\sum_{a\in Z_p,b\in Z_q,c\in Z_r}
\psi(R(a,b,c)/pqr)
\right|
\ll X^2\lambda_X^3.
\]

This is a three-prime analogue of Bombieri--Vinogradov, but the moduli are not fixed and the sets are not residue intervals. It is substantially stronger than standard large sieve estimates.

Large sieve gives cancellation after averaging over arbitrary additive characters, but here the subsets `Z_p` themselves vary with the modulus and can encode the CRT location.

---

## 4. More promising route: Frobenius trace functions

For Apéry numbers the zero condition is not arbitrary. The sets

\[
Z_p=\{r:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]

come from the finite-field reduction of the Apéry Picard--Fuchs/K3 family.

The correct replacement for CRT boxes is a trace function representation:

\[
1_{Z_p}(r)
\]

should be decomposed into Frobenius trace components. Then the triple moment becomes a sum of products of trace functions.

Schematically:

\[
\sum_m(K_X(m))_3
=\sum_{p,q,r}\sum_m
f_p(m)f_q(m)f_r(m).
\]

After Fourier expansion in `m`, one seeks bounds for mixed sums of the form

\[
\sum_{m\in I}T_p(m)T_q(m)T_r(m),
\]

where the `T_p` are Frobenius traces attached to the Apéry K3 sheaf.

Deligne's Riemann hypothesis gives square-root cancellation if the tensor product sheaf has no geometrically trivial component.

Thus the real target is:

**Triple tensor non-degeneracy theorem.** The rank-three tensor product of the Apéry trace sheaves at distinct primes has no invariant subspace except the expected diagonal terms.

Such a theorem would imply `(HM)_3`.

---

## 5. Relation to the existing MC+AT reduction

The Fable R4 route separates the problem into:

* MC: moment-correlation of the gap polynomial cloud.
* AT: anti-concentration preventing exceptional high multiplicity.

The direct CRT approach tries to prove the whole statement in one step. This hides the same obstruction inside CRT sawtooth sums.

In practice MC is the more geometric part and AT is the combinatorial part. The decomposition is useful because:

* K3 monodromy controls correlations.
* Additive combinatorics controls concentration of the remaining exceptional fibers.

Trying to bypass both forces one to prove a stronger hybrid theorem.

---

## 6. Could a pure additive-combinatorics proof work?

Probably not with current tools.

Sum-product and Szemeredi--Trotter estimates apply when the sets live in one ambient field with algebraic incidence structure. Here the sets `Z_p` live in different fields `F_p`, and the collision equation

\[
m=p+a=q+b=r+c
\]

mixes three characteristics.

The natural algebraic object is not an incidence variety over one field, but a family over varying finite fields. This points toward étale cohomology rather than classical incidence geometry.

---

## 7. Recommended proof program

The shortest realistic path is:

1. Prove a sheaf description of the Apéry zero indicator.
2. Establish geometric monodromy of the associated K3 sheaf.
3. Prove irreducibility/nontriviality of the triple tensor products.
4. Apply Deligne bounds to obtain a triple correlation estimate.
5. Handle diagonal/degenerate configurations separately.
6. Deduce `(HM)_3`.

The key missing theorem is therefore not a CRT cancellation lemma. It is a **three-prime Frobenius independence theorem for Apéry zero sets**.

That theorem is exactly the arithmetic randomness principle behind the conjecture: the Apéry zeros behave randomly because their Frobenius origins are independent across primes.
