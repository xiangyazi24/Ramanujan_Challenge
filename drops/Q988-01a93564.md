For fixed quotient q, the full-stencil construction gives integer rows whose content is

\[
c_S(n,q)=\gcd(A_0(n,q),R_{-S}(n,q),\ldots,R_S(n,q)),

\[

\]

Therefore the only missing quantity is not the primitive Smith saturation. It is the arithmetic height of the common content carrying the target primes.

## 2. Nonalternating adjoint test

A dense adjoint combination of the rows has the form

\[
E=\sum_{|j|\le S} a_jR_j(n,q).
\]

After clearing denominators in the hypergeometric/binomial expressions, every such construction inherits the same issue: the clearing factor is supported on the local denominator lattice, while the cancellation needed to make \(\log |E|=o(n)\) would have to come from an Apéry-specific inhomogeneous identity.

The current recurrence identities only produce homogeneous relations. They can lower the formal stencil rank, but they do not prove that the remaining scalar is nonzero after cancellation with \(c_S\).

Thus no valid certificate of type A is obtained.

## 3. Smith/Fitting minor route

The exact Smith identity removes universal saturation factors from the primitive row lattice. It does not remove factors contained in

\[
\gcd(A_0,R_{-S},...,R_S).
\]

Removing those factors would require a theorem distinguishing universal row denominators from actual Apéry target content. No such theorem follows from the abstract recurrence, opposed defects, or exterior minors.

The missing statement would have to be an actual-shell Fitting estimate:

**Missing lemma.** For fixed q there exists \(S(n)=o(\sqrt n)\) such that the Apéry Cartier/Laurent shell satisfies

\[
\log \gcd(A_0(n,q),R_{-S}(n,q),...,R_S(n,q))=o_q(n).

This is strictly stronger than the nonalternating Smith identity and is the remaining obstruction.
