# Q881: Companion divisibility audit

## Conclusion

The available identities do not prove the claimed theorem `c_n | X_n` for all n. The computation through n≤2000 is strong evidence, but the missing step is exactly the small-prime valuation transfer from the coefficient content to the companion solution. The statement should currently be treated as an open divisibility conjecture, not as a consequence of the known Apéry identities.

## 1. What divisibility would require

Write

\[
P_n(T)=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}T^k,
\]

and let

\[
c_n=\gcd_{1\le k\le n}\left(\frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}\right).
\]

For a prime p, proving `c_n | X_n` is equivalent to proving

\[
v_p(X_n)\ge \min_{1\le k\le n}\left(v_p(L_n/k)+v_p(b_{n-k})\right).
\]

For p>sqrt(n), existing Lucas arguments control the support and give the expected large-prime part. The unresolved regime is

\[
p\le \sqrt n,
\]

where the minimum valuation can receive contributions from many different k and no single Lucas residue controls the valuation.

## 2. Why the Casoratian is insufficient by itself

The exact relation

\[
a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}
\]

only gives a determinant relation between two consecutive solutions. Rearranging gives

\[
a_n=\frac{a_{n-1}b_n+6/n^3}{b_{n-1}},
\]

but this introduces division by b_{n-1} and the denominator n^3. To turn this into an integral combination of the coefficients of P_n one needs an additional statement controlling

\[
v_p(b_{n-1})
\]

against the numerator valuation for every p, including p=2,3. No such valuation inequality follows from the Casoratian alone.

## 3. Variation of parameters does not close the gap

A discrete variation-of-parameters expression for a_n has the schematic form

\[
a_n=\sum_j K(n,j)b_j,
\]

where the kernels K contain products of recurrence coefficients and rational factors. Multiplying by L_n^3 clears denominators globally, but it does not imply that the resulting integer combination lies in the ideal generated by

\[
\left\{(L_n/k)b_{n-k}:1\le k\le n\right\}.
\]

The missing statement would be an explicit integral ideal containment

\[
L_n^3a_n\in \left( (L_n/k)b_{n-k}:1\le k\le n\right)\subset\mathbb Z.
\]

This is stronger than the desired divisibility and is not currently established.

## 4. Why `c_n|b_n` plus a determinant identity is not enough

Assume hypothetically

\[
c_n\mid b_n.
\]

The Casoratian gives

\[
a_nb_{n-1}\equiv a_{n-1}b_n+6n^{-3}\pmod {c_n}.
\]

However, reducing modulo c_n requires controlling the inverse of n^3 and the divisibility of 6. For primes p dividing n or p=2,3, the denominator obstruction is unavoidable. Thus the determinant identity cannot imply

\[
c_n\mid L_n^3a_n
\]

without an additional local lemma.

## 5. The exact missing lemma

A sufficient non-tautological lemma is:

**Companion content transfer lemma.** For every n and every prime power p^e,

\[
p^e\mid \frac{L_n}{k}b_{n-k}\quad(1\le k\le n)
\]

implies

\[
p^e\mid L_n^3a_n.
\]

This is strictly weaker than directly proving `c_n|X_n`, because it is a local valuation implication and can potentially be attacked prime by prime using recurrence structure.

## 6. Scoped obstruction

A broad class of determinant-only arguments cannot prove the theorem. Namely, any argument using only:

1. the Casoratian relation;
2. the fact that b_n and a_n satisfy the same second-order recurrence;
3. integrality of L_n^3a_n;

cannot determine the p-adic valuation of X_n from c_n, because the Casoratian leaves an uncontrolled factor of n^3. For primes dividing n, this loses exactly the valuation information needed.

This does not rule out recurrence-based proofs with additional p-adic input; it only rules out a pure determinant transfer.

## Final assessment

The computation `c_n|X_n` for n≤2000 is compatible with the known structure, but no complete proof follows from the currently banked carriers. The next decisive step is not another support theorem; it is a genuine local companion transfer theorem controlling

\[
v_p(L_n^3a_n)
\]

from the coefficient ideal valuation for all p≤sqrt(n), especially p=2,3 and primes dividing n. If obtained, it would combine with the existing content carrier results to produce a new height inequality rather than a reformulation.
