No exact Bezout or creative-telescoping certificate of the requested form is currently obtained. In particular, no identity


The correct conclusion is that the triple-moment route remains an exact reduction problem, not a solved Bezout identity.

## 1. Recurrence summation identity

Let

\[
(j+1)^3b_{j+1}=P(j)b_j-j^3b_{j-1},
\]

where

\[
P(j)=(2j+1)(17j^2+17j+5).
\]

For a rational certificate one would need to telescope expressions of the form

\[
F(j+1)-F(j)=\alpha_1(a,j)b_{a-j}+\alpha_2(a,j)b_{a-j} /j+\alpha_3(a,j)b_{a-j}/j^2+\alpha_4(a,j)b_{a-j}/j^3.
\]

The recurrence only gives a two-step relation between adjacent Apéry terms. After substituting the shifted index, the natural telescoper ansatz has unknown polynomial coefficients satisfying a coupled first-order system. Solving this system with bounded degree does not produce a nonzero boundary-free relation eliminating all three inverse moments.

## 2. Why the obvious Wronskian shortcut fails

The Apéry recurrence has a two-dimensional solution space. A second independent solution can be used to form a discrete Wronskian, but this controls combinations of

\[
\sum c_{a-u}u^{-s}
\]

with coefficients coming from the second solution. It does not produce a relation involving only

\[
(b_{a-1},b_{a-2},\ldots,b_0).
\]

The rank drop example `(a,q)=(84,211)` shows that the universal two-solution moment matrix argument cannot prove triple nonvanishing.

## 3. Boundary terms

Any summation-by-parts certificate must include the endpoints

\[
 u=1,\qquad u=a.
\]

The first endpoint contributes terms involving `b_{a-1}`. The final endpoint contributes the initial data `b_0=1`, `b_1=5` after recurrence reduction. A valid certificate therefore cannot simply prove that a finite difference vanishes internally; it must leave a controlled explicit boundary expression.

No such expression with controlled prime support has been verified.

## 4. Consequence for the triple-zero question

The absence of a certificate does not prove that a prime

\[
q>a,\qquad q\mid R_1(a),R_2(a),R_3(a)
\]

exists. The computational range through `a=3000` remains evidence against such examples.

However, the current tools only establish:

- exact local reductions of the original gcd problem to the inverse moments;
- computational absence of simultaneous triple zeros in tested ranges;
- failure of the generic rank-two argument.

They do not establish a theorem that `C_3(a)` has only small prime factors.

## Final status

**REPACKAGING.**

The Bezout/creative-telescoping attack has not produced a new carrier or a proof of triple nonvanishing. A breakthrough would require an explicit telescoping certificate with checked boundary terms or a new Apéry-specific arithmetic identity controlling the common divisors of the three inverse moments.
