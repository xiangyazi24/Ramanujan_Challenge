# The leading coefficient of the symmetric Apéry polynomial

## Verdict

Put

\[
S_p:=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r,
\qquad d=\frac{p-1}{2}
\]

for an odd prime \(p\).  The row sum is not random and is not merely an
unidentified Dwork period.  It is governed by the weight-three CM newform

\[
f(\tau)=\eta(\tau)^2\eta(2\tau)\eta(4\tau)\eta(8\tau)^2
       =\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_nq^n
       \in S_3(\Gamma_0(8),\chi_{-8}),
\]

which has CM by \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\).  A theorem of Chen Wang and
Zhi-Wei Sun gives the stronger congruence

\[
\boxed{S_p\equiv\lambda_p\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{0.1}
\]

Explicitly,

\[
\boxed{
S_p\equiv
\begin{cases}
4x^2-2p\pmod{p^2},&p=x^2+2y^2
                  \quad(p\equiv1,3\pmod8),\\[2mm]
0\pmod{p^2},&p\equiv5,7\pmod8.
\end{cases}}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Consequently, modulo \(p\),

\[
\boxed{
S_p\equiv
\begin{cases}
4x^2\ne0,&p\equiv1,3\pmod8,\\
0,&p\equiv5,7\pmod8.
\end{cases}}
\tag{0.3}
\]

Combining this with the leading-coefficient identity banked in Q645 gives

\[
\boxed{
\deg Q_p=d
\iff p\equiv1,3\pmod8
\iff \left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)=1,}
\tag{0.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\deg Q_p<d
\iff p\equiv5,7\pmod8
\iff \left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)=-1.}
\tag{0.5}
\]

Thus degree drop occurs for **exactly half of the odd primes**, namely the
primes inert in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\).  The examples \(p=37\) and
\(p=127\) from Q645 are not accidents: they are respectively \(5\) and
\(7\pmod8\).  Likewise \(p=59\) and \(p=193\) are split primes and have full
degree.

The structural interpretation is equally clean.  In the repository's toric
model, \(S_p\) is the Hasse invariant of the special K3 fiber \(a=1\), and
\(\lambda_p\) is the Frobenius trace on its rank-two transcendental motive.
For good \(p\ge5\), if \(\widetilde X_1\) denotes the resolved K3 fiber, then

\[
\boxed{
\#\widetilde X_1(\mathbf F_p)=1+p^2+20p+\lambda_p.}
\tag{0.6}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
S_p\equiv\#\widetilde X_1(\mathbf F_p)-1\pmod p,}
\tag{0.7}
\]

and even

\[
\boxed{
S_p\equiv
\#\widetilde X_1(\mathbf F_p)-1-20p\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{0.8}
\]

So there is a genuine zeta-function/point-count interpretation.

## 1. The exact arithmetic theorem

The Apéry numbers are

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]

Wang--Sun prove, for every odd prime \(p\),

\[
\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}b_n\equiv
\begin{cases}
4x^2-2p\pmod{p^2},&p=x^2+2y^2,\\
0\pmod{p^2},&p\equiv5,7\pmod8.
\end{cases}
\tag{1.1}
\]

This refines an earlier mod-\(p\) evaluation of Zhi-Wei Sun.  Since an odd
prime is represented by \(x^2+2y^2\) exactly when it splits in
\(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\), formula (1.1) is precisely a CM splitting law.

Let

\[
\chi_{-8}(p)=\left(\frac{-8}{p}\right)
            =\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right).
\]

The coefficient of the CM newform above is

\[
\lambda_p=
\begin{cases}
2(x^2-2y^2)=4x^2-2p,&p=x^2+2y^2,\\
0,&\chi_{-8}(p)=-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus (1.1) is exactly \(S_p\equiv\lambda_p\pmod{p^2}\).  In modular-form
notation the quantity sought in part (A) really is an \(a_p\), but to avoid
collision with the Apéry companion sequence \(a_n\) and with elliptic-curve
traces, it is better to call it \(\lambda_p\).

For a split prime, an associated CM elliptic trace can be chosen as
\(t_p=\pm2x\), and then

\[
\lambda_p=t_p^2-2p.
\tag{1.3}
\]

This is the symmetric-square origin of the formula.  It is not, in general,
the raw elliptic trace \(t_p\) itself.

There is also a finite hypergeometric form.  Sun's transformation gives

\[
S_p\equiv
\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}
 \frac{(4k)!}{(k!)^4\,256^k}\pmod p,
\tag{1.4}
\]

with the terms beyond the natural truncation vanishing modulo \(p\).  This is
another incarnation of the same Hasse invariant.

## 2. Consequence for the leading coefficient and degree

Q645 proved that, for

\[
B_p(R)=Q_p(-R(R+1)),
\qquad \deg Q_p\le d=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

the putative top coefficient satisfies

\[
c_{p,d}=[U^d]Q_p(U)
       =(-1)^{d+1}S_p
       \quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Substitution of (0.3) gives the completely explicit answer

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,d}=
\begin{cases}
-4x^2,&p\equiv1\pmod8,\ p=x^2+2y^2,\\
\phantom{-}4x^2,&p\equiv3\pmod8,\ p=x^2+2y^2,\\
0,&p\equiv5,7\pmod8.
\end{cases}}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The first two values are nonzero because \(p\nmid x\).  Therefore (0.4) and
(0.5) are unconditional, not heuristic.

What this does **not** by itself determine is the size of the drop.  At an
inert prime it proves only

\[
\deg Q_p\le d-1.
\]

The displayed factorizations for \(p=37\) and \(127\) show a one-degree drop,
and small examples do the same, but proving
\(\deg Q_p=d-1\) for every inert prime would require a formula for the next
coefficient, equivalently a suitable weighted row sum.  It should not be
silently inferred from the leading-coefficient theorem.

For the random-root model, a bounded one-degree change would not alter the
asymptotic Poisson law: random polynomials of degree \(d\) and \(d-1\), with
both degrees tending to infinity, have the same fixed root factorial moments.
What must be corrected is the conditioning: the polynomial ensemble has two
arithmetic components, split primes of full degree and inert primes with a
forced vanishing top coefficient.

from math import isqrt


def is_prime(n: int) -> bool:
    if n < 2:
        return False
    if n % 2 == 0:
        return n == 2
    return all(n % d for d in range(3, isqrt(n) + 1, 2))


def apery_row(p: int, modulus: int) -> list[int]:
    # b_0,...,b_{p-1}; all recurrence denominators are units mod p^2.
    b = [1 % modulus, 5 % modulus]
    for n in range(1, p - 1):
        middle = (2*n + 1) * (17*n*n + 17*n + 5)
        numerator = middle*b[n] - n**3*b[n - 1]
        b.append(numerator * pow((n + 1)**3, -1, modulus) % modulus)
    return b[:p]


def representation_x2_plus_2y2(p: int):
    for y in range(isqrt(p // 2) + 1):
        x2 = p - 2*y*y
        x = isqrt(x2)
        if x*x == x2:
            return x, y
    return None


primes = [n for n in range(2, 300) if is_prime(n)][:50]
for p in primes:
    S_mod_p2 = sum(apery_row(p, p*p)) % (p*p)
    if p == 2:
        lam = -2
    elif p % 8 in (5, 7):
        lam = 0
    else:
        x, y = representation_x2_plus_2y2(p)
        lam = 4*x*x - 2*p
    assert S_mod_p2 == lam % (p*p), (p, S_mod_p2, lam)
    print(p, S_mod_p2 % p, "full" if p > 2 and lam % p else "drop")

## 4. Why this is a Hasse invariant, not the analytic value \(F(1)\)

Let \(\Lambda\) be the fixed Laurent polynomial in the repository's toric
realization, normalized so that

\[
\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda^r)=b_r.
\]

Define the truncated period/Hasse polynomial

\[
H_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r\in\mathbf F_p[t].
\tag{4.1}
\]

The usual hypersurface Hasse--Witt calculation gives this polynomial
immediately:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{CT}(1-t\Lambda)^{p-1}
&=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}
  \binom{p-1}{r}(-t)^r\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda^r)\\
&\equiv\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r
 =H_p(t)\pmod p,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

because \(\binom{p-1}{r}(-1)^r\equiv1\pmod p\).  The geometric fiber is
\(\Lambda=a\), with period coordinate \(t=a^{-1}\).  Therefore

\[
S_p=H_p(1)
\tag{4.3}
\]

is exactly the Hasse invariant at the special fiber \(a=1\).

This is the correct meaning of “evaluate the generating function at 1.”  The
complex series

\[
F(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n
\]

does not converge at \(t=1\); its radius of convergence is much smaller than
1.  What is being evaluated is the finite characteristic-\(p\) Hasse
polynomial \(H_p(t)\), not the analytic function \(F(t)\).

The CM law now has a standard crystalline interpretation.

- If \(p\equiv1,3\pmod8\), then \(H_p(1)\ne0\).  The special K3 is ordinary;
  the rank-two transcendental Frobenius has slopes \(0,2\), and there is a
  unit root.
- If \(p\equiv5,7\pmod8\), then \(H_p(1)=0\).  The transcendental Frobenius
  has slopes \(1,1\); together with the 20 algebraic slope-one classes, this
  is the supersingular reduction of the special K3.

Thus the vanishing of the leading coefficient is not caused by an
approximately uniform collection of normalized values.  It is forced by the
ordinary/supersingular dichotomy of a CM K3 motive.

### Correction to the proposed unit-root manipulation

If \(f_r=b_r/\alpha_p^r\), then tautologically

\[
S_p=\sum_r\alpha_p^rf_r.
\]

One cannot replace this by

\[
\alpha_p\sum_rf_r
\]

unless \(\alpha_p^r=\alpha_p\) on the relevant indices or another special
identity is proved.  No such simplification follows from Dwork theory.
Moreover, at the inert primes there is **no unit root** \(\alpha_p\) for this
special fiber, so that normalization is unavailable precisely where
\(S_p=0\).

At a split prime, if \(\alpha_p\) denotes the actual crystalline unit root,
then the Frobenius polynomial implies

\[
\alpha_p\equiv\lambda_p\equiv S_p\pmod p.
\tag{4.4}
\]

That is the valid unit-root statement.

## 5. Zeta-function and point-count interpretation

The repository's `toric_fiber_k3.tex` proves that at \(a=1\) there are 20
independent rational Tate classes and a rank-two transcendental complement.
The exact symmetric-square/Shioda--Inose description in
`toric_fiber_sym2.tex` identifies the latter with the CM motive attached to
\(f\).  Hence, for good \(p\ge5\),

\[
Z(\widetilde X_1/\mathbf F_p,T)
=
\frac{1}{
 (1-T)(1-p^2T)(1-pT)^{20}
 \bigl(1-\lambda_pT+\chi_{-8}(p)p^2T^2\bigr)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Taking the first logarithmic coefficient yields

\[
\#\widetilde X_1(\mathbf F_p)
=1+p^2+20p+\lambda_p,
\]

which proves (0.7) and (0.8) after applying (0.1).

The local factor displays the split/inert dichotomy directly:

\[
1-\lambda_pT+\chi_{-8}(p)p^2T^2
=
\begin{cases}
1-(4x^2-2p)T+p^2T^2,&p=x^2+2y^2,\\
1-p^2T^2,&p\equiv5,7\pmod8.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.2}
\]

At an inert prime the two transcendental Frobenius eigenvalues are \(p\) and
\(-p\), so their trace is zero.  This is exactly why the global row sum and
the top coefficient vanish.

There is therefore a precise answer to part (D):

\[
\boxed{
\#\widetilde X_1(\mathbf F_p)\equiv1+\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r\pmod p.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The universal `1` is the contribution of \(H^0\).  All algebraic
\(H^2\)-classes contribute multiples of \(p\), and \(H^4\) contributes
\(p^2\), leaving exactly the rank-two transcendental trace modulo \(p\).

## 6. Lucas blocks and the constant projection

Gessel's multiplicative Lucas congruence gives

\[
b_{mp+r}\equiv b_mb_r\pmod p
\qquad(0\le r<p).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_{mp+r}
\equiv b_mS_p
\equiv b_m\lambda_p\pmod p.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_{mp+r}\equiv
\begin{cases}
4x^2b_m,&p=x^2+2y^2,\\
0,&p\equiv5,7\pmod8.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Iterating over base-\(p\) digits gives the useful exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n=0}^{p^k-1}b_n\equiv S_p^k\pmod p.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus at every inert prime every complete block of length \(p^k\) has zero
sum modulo \(p\).

Calling \(S_p\) the zero-frequency Fourier coefficient, or the projection
onto the constant function, is correct.  Calling it the trace of a diagonal
operator on the \(p\)-dimensional function space is also formally correct but
tautological.  It is **not** naturally the trace of an endomorphism of the
two-dimensional recurrence solution space.  The non-tautological trace is
\(\lambda_p\), the Frobenius trace on the special rank-two K3 motive.

Finally, zero mean does not imply uniform distribution of the values
\(b_r\) in \(\mathbf F_p\).  It is one linear moment only.  Here it is forced
by CM inertness, so it should not be interpreted as evidence that the entire
row is additively equidistributed.

## 7. Which rank-two monodromy group is meant?

Several rank-two and rank-three objects are being conflated.  They have
different groups.

| object | rank | correct generic/special group |
|:---|---:|:---|
| underlying elliptic \(H^1\) local system on the level-six cover | 2 | geometric Zariski closure \(\mathrm{SL}_2\); arithmetic closure lies in \(\mathrm{GL}_2=\mathrm{GSp}_2\), with cyclotomic determinant |
| generic transcendental Apéry K3 local system | 3 | \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\) image of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), namely \(\mathrm{PSL}_2\simeq\mathrm{SO}_3\) |
| rank-four Kummer/Asai summand before removing the isogeny graph | 4 | one rational Tate line plus the generic rank-three transcendental summand |
| rank-two transcendental motive at the special fiber \(a=1\) | 2 | CM: connected monodromy is a torus; the full algebraic group lies in its normalizer and is dihedral-type, not \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) |

Two corrections follow.

1. The generic rank-three Picard--Fuchs system does **not** decompose as
   rank two plus rank one.  It is generically irreducible and is the symmetric
   square of an underlying rank-two system.
2. At the special point \(a=1\), the Picard rank jumps by one.  There the
   specialized rank-three piece does split, after semisimplification, into an
   additional algebraic/Tate line and the rank-two CM transcendental motive.
   This special rank-two representation is precisely the one whose trace is
   \(\lambda_p\).

Therefore the answer to part (F) is:

- **generic underlying rank-two variation:** geometric monodromy
  \(\mathrm{SL}_2\);
- **rank-two piece of the special \(a=1\) K3 fiber:** CM torus/torus
  normalizer, not \(\mathrm{SL}_2\).

This special CM degeneration is exactly what produces the residue-class law
for \(S_p\) and hence the degree drops of \(Q_p\).

## 8. Direct answers to (A)--(F)

### (A)

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r\equiv\lambda_p\pmod{p^2},
\]

where \(\lambda_p\) is the \(p\)-th coefficient of the weight-three,
level-eight CM newform above.  It is the Frobenius trace of the rank-two
transcendental motive of the special K3 fiber \(a=1\).

### (B)

It is not always nonzero.  The first-50-prime table above gives every value.
It is nonzero modulo \(p\) exactly for \(p\equiv1,3\pmod8\), and zero exactly
for \(p\equiv5,7\pmod8\).

### (C)

Yes.  Degree drop occurs at every prime \(p\equiv5,7\pmod8\), hence at a set
of density \(1/2\).  The leading coefficient alone does not determine
whether the drop is always exactly one.

### (D)

Yes.  For the resolved toric K3 fiber at \(a=1\),

\[
\#\widetilde X_1(\mathbf F_p)
\equiv1+\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_r\pmod p,
\]

and the stronger mod-\(p^2\) relation is (0.8).  The entire local zeta factor
is given in (5.1).

### (E)

The block identity is exactly

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_{mp+r}\equiv b_m\lambda_p\pmod p.
\]

At inert primes this constant projection vanishes on every complete block;
at split primes it equals \(4x^2b_m\).  Vanishing of this one projection does
not imply uniformity of the whole row.

### (F)

The generic rank-two elliptic system has geometric monodromy \(\mathrm{SL}_2\),
and its symmetric square gives the rank-three \(\mathrm{SO}_3\) Apéry K3
system.  The rank-two motive occurring only at the special fiber \(a=1\) is
CM and has toric/dihedral monodromy instead.
