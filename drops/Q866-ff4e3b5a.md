# Q866 one-moment denominator cut audit

## Result classification

The requested deliverable is an obstruction, not a completed power-saving theorem. The exact denominator statement is equivalent to proving pointwise retention of almost all cyclotomic prime factors of \(L_n\) in the denominator of

\[
R_n=\sum_{k=1}^n b_{n-k}/k.
\]

The standard available G-function, recurrence, and rational approximation tools do not provide this pointwise assertion because their quantifiers are incompatible with the required statement for every individual \(n\).

## 1. Exact denominator identity

Since \(U_n=L_nR_n\in\mathbb Z\),

\[
C_n=\gcd(L_n,U_n)=L_n/\operatorname{den}(R_n).
\]

Therefore the target estimate is exactly

\[
\log \operatorname{den}(R_n)=\log L_n-o(n),
\]

or equivalently

\[
\log C_n=o(n).
\]

No averaging in \(n\) is sufficient.

## 2. Prime-local obstruction

For a prime \(p\le n\), the factor \(p\mid C_n\) is controlled by

\[
R_n\pmod p.
\]

The known reduction gives for the relevant lower-digit cases

\[
p\mid C_n \Longleftrightarrow p\mid b_{n\bmod p}
\]

up to the already isolated small-prime and boundary contribution.

Thus removing the exceptional support requires a theorem controlling

\[
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n\atop p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}\log p
\]

pointwise in every \(n\).

This is essentially the one-moment carrier problem.

## 3. Why generic G-function denominator theorems do not apply

The sequence

\[
[z^n]B(z)\log(1/(1-z))
\]

is holonomic and satisfies an inhomogeneous recurrence. Such results control:

- common denominator growth of the whole sequence;
- upper bounds on coefficient denominators;
- asymptotic analytic behavior.

They do not imply that for every \(n\), almost every prime dividing \(L_n\) survives in the reduced denominator of the single coefficient \(R_n\).

The missing implication would require converting an upper bound on recurrence denominators into a lower bound on reduced denominators. No such theorem applies without an additional local nonvanishing statement.

## 4. p-adic/Dwork obstruction

A p-adic congruence can prove statements of the form

\[
R_n\equiv F(n,p)\pmod p
\]

or relate coefficients in residue classes. To finish the problem one needs:

\[
\#\{p:\sqrt n<p\le n,\ R_n\equiv0\pmod p\}=o(n/\log n)
\]

with logarithmic weight.

Existing Lucas/Dwork-type congruences do not exclude the possibility that many primes vanish simultaneously for a special residue pattern of \(n\).

## 5. Rational approximation obstruction

A Padé-type argument would need a lower bound on the denominator of the rational number

\[
R_n.
\]

But irrationality-measure style arguments usually control a family of approximants or asymptotic limits. They do not yield a uniform lower bound for every coefficient denominator.

The missing statement is a local prime non-cancellation lemma, not an approximation-growth lemma.

## 6. Sharp remaining lemma

A sufficient lemma strictly weaker than the full original carrier statement is:

**Prime retention lemma.** There exists \(\delta_n=o(1)\) such that for every \(n\),

\[
\sum_{\sqrt n<p\le n\atop p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}\log p
\le \delta_n n.
\]

Together with the proved estimate

\[
0\le \log C_n-W(n)\ll n^{2/3},
\]

this gives

\[
\log C_n\le \delta_n n+O(n^{2/3})=o(n).
\]

Hence the lemma suffices, but it is exactly the remaining pointwise prime-retention input.

## 7. Status

No complete proof of a fixed power saving

\[
\log C_n\le(1-\delta)n+o(n)
\]

is obtained here.

The obstruction is precise: all standard analytic tools available control families of coefficients or average behavior, while Challenge 3.2 requires a pointwise weighted exclusion of prime divisors satisfying

\[
p\mid b_{n\bmod p}.
\]

The next viable attack must therefore produce a new local lemma (for example through explicit gap-polynomial, Cartier, or p-adic digit analysis) that bounds this exceptional prime set for every n.
