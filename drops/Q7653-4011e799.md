# Exact seam law between Apéry reflection and Cartier/Fricke reflection

## Verdict

There **is** an exact transformation law, but it is not a parity law for the Hecke neighbor.

Put

\[
\sigma(r)=p-1-r=:s,
\qquad
\tau(r)=p+1-r=s+2.
\]

The two involutions satisfy

\[
\sigma^2=\tau^2=1,
\qquad
\tau\sigma(r)=r+2,
\qquad
\sigma\tau(r)=r-2.
\tag{V0}
\]

The decisive seam identity is

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr A_r(z\circ\sigma)
=-(\mathscr A z)_{\tau(r)}
}
\tag{V1}
\]

in \(\mathbf F_p\), where

\[
\mathscr A_rz=r^3z_r-P(r-1)z_{r-1}+(r-1)^3z_{r-2}.
\]

Thus ordinary reflection on the **solution row** sends the forcing to the shifted Cartier row \(s+2\), not to \(s\).

For the actual first Hecke extension, let

\[
K(t)=\sum_{r\ge0}\kappa_rt^r,
\qquad
\kappa_0=0,\quad \kappa_1=-36,
\]

so that

\[
LK=-5(g-1)-t,
\]

and, for interior rows \(r\ge2\),

\[
\mathscr A_r\kappa=-5g_r.
\]

On a Hasse target \(p\mid b_r\), the normalized neighbor is

\[
J_1(p,r)=\kappa_r\pmod p.
\]

Define the first Green/Casoratian coordinate

\[
\Xi_r
:=r^3\bigl(b_{r-1}\kappa_r-b_r\kappa_{r-1}\bigr).
\]

The endpoint cocycle is retained exactly:

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_r=-1-5\sum_{m=1}^r g_m b_{m-1}.
}
\tag{V2}
\]

If both \(r\) and \(s=p-1-r\) are Hasse targets, put

\[
D_{p,r}:=r^3b_{r-1}.
\]

Then reflection plus the homogeneous Apéry recurrence gives

\[
\boxed{
D_{p,r}=s^3b_{s-1}\ne0\pmod p.
}
\tag{V3}
\]

Consequently the **exact ordinary-reflection law for the Hecke neighbor** is

\[
\boxed{
D_{p,r}\bigl(J_1(p,s)-J_1(p,r)\bigr)
=-5\,\Sigma_{r,s}\bigl(g_m b_{m-1}\bigr),
}
\tag{V4}
\]

where

\[
\Sigma_{r,s}(x_m)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{m=r+1}^{s}x_m,&r<s,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle-\sum_{m=s+1}^{r}x_m,&s<r,\\[2mm]
0,&r=s.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, on a common row \(b_r=J_1(p,r)=0\),

\[
\boxed{
J_1(p,s)
=-\frac5{D_{p,r}}\,
\Sigma_{r,s}\bigl(g_m b_{m-1}\bigr).
}
\tag{V5}
\]

So a common row **does not reflect to a common row** unless a long Green interval vanishes.  There is no law \(J_1(p,s)=\pm J_1(p,r)\).

For the Green combination

\[
Y^{(p)}:=J_2+7J_1,
\qquad
\mathscr A Y^{(p)}=H_p,
\qquad
H_{p,r}=5\rho_{r,1}-\rho_{r,2},
\]

define

\[
\Upsilon_r
:=r^3\bigl(b_{r-1}Y_r-b_rY_{r-1}\bigr).
\]

Then

\[
\Upsilon_r-\Upsilon_{r-1}=b_{r-1}H_{p,r},
\]

and the exact reflected transformation on two Hasse rows is

\[
\boxed{
D_{p,r}\bigl(Y_s-Y_r\bigr)
=\Sigma_{r,s}\bigl(b_{m-1}H_{p,m}\bigr).
}
\tag{V6}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
D_{p,r}\Bigl[
J_2(p,s)-J_2(p,r)
+7\bigl(J_1(p,s)-J_1(p,r)\bigr)
\Bigr]
=\Sigma_{r,s}(b_{m-1}H_{p,m}).
}
\tag{V7}
\]

This is the precise Green transformation law.  It is a prefix/interval law, not a two-row scalar symmetry.

The Cartier/Fricke law from Q7650 is simultaneously

\[
S_{\tau(r)}(t)=t^{-2}S_r(1/t),
\qquad S_r=\Lambda_rR_p.
\tag{V8}
\]

If the reduced Cartier numerator at row \(r\) is

\[
\mathcal U_r(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{d_p-2}u_jt^j,
\]

then

\[
g_r=u_0,
\qquad
g_{\tau(r)}=u_{d_p-2},
\tag{V9}
\]

and, writing

\[
A_p^{\rm head}=5+q_1,
\qquad
C_p=q_2-q_1^2-5q_1,
\]

\[
H_{p,r}=-u_2+A_p^{\rm head}u_1+C_pu_0,
\tag{V10}
\]

\[
H_{p,\tau(r)}
=-u_{d_p-4}+A_p^{\rm head}u_{d_p-3}+C_pu_{d_p-2}.
\tag{V11}
\]

Thus the shifted partner replaces a **head functional by a tail functional** across a state of exact length

\[
d_p=
\begin{cases}
2p-2,&\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)=1,\\
2p-6,&\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)=-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{V12}
\]

This is the rank obstruction.  The two reflections generate the formal translation \(r\mapsto r+2\), but the fiber transported by that translation has \(2p+O(1)\) state and an affine endpoint cocycle.  It is not a fixed two-dimensional label fiber.

The conclusions are therefore:

* **No bounded-degree condition on an isolated common row follows from the two involutions.**
* **No fixed-\(p\) bound on the number of common \(b=J_1=0\) rows follows from them.**
* **No label-preserving \(r\leftrightarrow s\) two-row determinant follows from the involution composition.**
* There is, however, a different exact label-preserving \(2\times2\) determinant for the second coordinate after one introduces the \(p\)- and \(2p\)-Frobenius blocks of \(J_1\) and the endpoint frame.  It certifies \(J_2\ne0\) at a label already known to be common, but it does not count common labels.

The point \((17,13)\) is already an arithmetic counterexample to any stronger reflected-\(J_1\) law: its reflected Hasse row is \(s=3\), but

\[
\boxed{J_1(17,3)=2\ne0,}
\]

while \(J_1(17,13)=0\).

## 3. The Hecke endpoint cocycle and the exact first Green coordinate

The first normalized Hecke extension is characterized by

\[
K(t)=\sum_{r\ge0}\kappa_rt^r,
\qquad
LK=-5(g-1)-t.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Hence

\[
\kappa_0=0,\qquad \kappa_1=-36,
\]

and, for \(r\ge2\),

\[
\mathscr A_r\kappa=-5g_r.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The exceptional coefficient at \(r=1\) is

\[
\mathscr A_1\kappa=-36=-5g_1-1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The extra \(-1\) is the first-extension endpoint cocycle.  It must be kept when one integrates the recurrence.

Define

\[
\Xi_r=r^3(b_{r-1}\kappa_r-b_r\kappa_{r-1}).
\tag{3.4}

Subtracting \(\kappa_{r-1}(\mathscr A_rb)=0\) from

\[
\Xi_r-\Xi_{r-1}=-5g_rb_{r-1}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

At \(r=1\), \(\Xi_1=-36\).  Since \(g_1=7\), summation yields the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_r=-1-5\sum_{m=1}^r g_mb_{m-1}.

On a target \(b_r=0\), no-consecutive-zero gives \(b_{r-1}\ne0\), and

\Xi_r=r^3b_{r-1}J_1(p,r).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

So the common condition \(b_r=J_1(p,r)=0\) is exactly \(b_r=\Xi_r=0\).

## 4. Exact ordinary-reflection law for \(J_1\)

Let

\[
s=p-1-r.
\]

Modulo \(p\), Apéry reflection gives

\[
b_s=b_r.
\]

Assume \(b_r=0\); then also \(b_s=0\).

The reflected neighboring coefficient is

\[
b_{s-1}=b_{r+1}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

At a target, the homogeneous recurrence gives

\[
(r+1)^3b_{r+1}=-r^3b_{r-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since

\[
s^3=(-r-1)^3=-(r+1)^3\pmod p,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
s^3b_{s-1}=r^3b_{r-1}=:D_{p,r}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This common denominator is a unit.

Using (3.7) at \(r\) and \(s\),

\[
D_{p,r}\bigl(J_1(p,s)-J_1(p,r)\bigr)
=\Xi_s-\Xi_r.
\]

Insert (3.6).  With the signed interval notation from the verdict,

\[
\boxed{
D_{p,r}\bigl(J_1(p,s)-J_1(p,r)\bigr)
=-5\Sigma_{r,s}(g_mb_{m-1}).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the exact transformation law.

If \(r\) is common,

\[
\boxed{
J_1(p,s)
=-\frac5{D_{p,r}}
\Sigma_{r,s}(g_mb_{m-1}).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore ordinary reflection preserves the Hasse target but **does not preserve the transverse label**.  The obstruction is a Green interval of length \(\asymp p\) in the worst case.

At the center \(r=s=(p-1)/2\), (4.4) is the tautology \(0=0\); it gives no automatic vanishing of \(J_1\).

## 5. The operator seam: why one zero of \(J_1\) says nothing about the source

The polynomial satisfies

\[
P(-x-1)=-P(x).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For \(\sigma(r)=p-1-r\) and \(\tau(r)=p+1-r\), a direct substitution gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr A_r(z\circ\sigma)
&=r^3z_s-P(r-1)z_{s+1}+(r-1)^3z_{s+2}\\
&=-(\mathscr A z)_{s+2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr A_r(z\circ\sigma)=-(\mathscr A z)_{\tau(r)}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For \(J_1\), on interior rows,

\[
\mathscr A_r(J_1\circ\sigma)=5g_{\tau(r)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
C^{(1)}_r:=J_1(p,r)+J_1(p,\sigma(r)),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr A_rC^{(1)}
=5\bigl(g_{\tau(r)}-g_r\bigr).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is exactly why one must not set the source to zero from one value \(J_1(p,r)=0\).  Even if \(C^{(1)}_r\) happened to vanish at one row, \(\mathscr A_rC^{(1)}\) contains \(C^{(1)}_{r-1}\) and \(C^{(1)}_{r-2}\).

The ordinary reflection acts on the row; the source lands at the **shifted** row \(s+2\).

Write

\[
\mathcal U_r(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{d_p-2}u_jt^j.

\boxed{
H_{p,r}=-u_2+A_p^{\rm head}u_1+C_pu_0,

\boxed{

\tag{7.4}

\tag{7.5}

## 8. Why the formal shift by two does not propagate targets

The two involutions generate translations:

\[
\tau\sigma(r)=r+2,
\qquad
\sigma\tau(r)=r-2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

If they acted on the same label-preserving fiber, this would indeed be powerful.  They do not.

On a Hasse target \(b_r=0\), Q7650 gives

\[
\boxed{
b_{\tau(r)}=b_{r-2}
=\frac{P(r-1)}{(r-1)^3}b_{r-1}.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Because \(b_{r-1}\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{
b_{\tau(r)}=0
\iff P(r-1)=0\pmod p.}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Likewise, since \(s=\sigma(r)\) is a Hasse target,

\[
b_{\tau(s)}=b_{r+2}=0
\iff P(s-1)=0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Using \(P(-x-1)=-P(x)\),

\[
P(s-1)=-P(r+1),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
b_{r+2}=0\iff P(r+1)=0\pmod p.}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Therefore the only way to turn the formal translation by two into target propagation is to impose the extra cubic condition \(P(r-1)=0\) or \(P(r+1)=0\).  A common \(b=J_1=0\) row does **not** force either condition.

This is the exact point at which the two involutions fail to close on the target locus.

## 9. A different positive result: a label-preserving \(2\times2\) determinant for \(J_2\)

Although the reflection pair does not produce the desired two-row determinant, the high-section source admits a different exact Frobenius-boundary compression.

From Q7650,

\[
H_{p,m}=108g_m+12g_{m+p}-g_{m+2p}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Modulo \(p\), the coefficients of \(\mathscr A\) are periodic under shifts by \(p\).  Hence, if the full first extension is continued through the next Frobenius blocks, define

\[
Z_m^{(p)}
:=J_{1,m+2p}-12J_{1,m+p}-108J_{1,m}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Then coefficientwise

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr A Z^{(p)}=5H_p.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Since \(Y=J_2+7J_1\) satisfies \(\mathscr AY=H_p\),

\[
\mathscr A(Z^{(p)}-5Y)=0.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Let \(a\) be a homogeneous companion normalized by

\[
W_r(b,a)=6.
\]

The endpoint/frame correction is

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_p
=\frac{5W_1(b,Y)-W_1(b,Z^{(p)})}{6}.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

Put

\[
\widetilde Z^{(p)}:=Z^{(p)}+\lambda_pa.
\tag{9.6}
\]

Then \(\widetilde Z^{(p)}-5Y\) is a homogeneous multiple of \(b\).  Therefore, at every common row \(b_r=J_{1,r}=0\),

\[
\boxed{
5J_2(p,r)=\widetilde Z_r^{(p)}.
}
\tag{9.7}
\]

Equivalently the consecutive-row determinant

\[
\boxed{
D_r^{(2)}
:=W_r(b,\widetilde Z^{(p)})
=r^3\det
\begin{pmatrix}
 b_{r-1}&b_r\\
 \widetilde Z_{r-1}^{(p)}&\widetilde Z_r^{(p)}
\end{pmatrix}
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D_r^{(2)}
=30\frac{J_2(p,r)}{a_r}
}
\tag{9.9}
\]

on a common row.

This is a genuine label-preserving transversality determinant, with the endpoint cocycle \(\lambda_pa\) included.  But it does **not** come from composing \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\), and it does not bound the number of common rows.  It imports the independent seam data in the \(p\)- and \(2p\)-blocks.

Thus the correct answer to the determinant part is:

> the two involutions do not supply an \(r\leftrightarrow s\) determinant that counts common labels; a separate Frobenius-block Green boundary supplies a \(2\times2\) determinant that only tests \(J_2\) at a label already known to be common.

## 10. Exact local/module countermodel to a two-involution counting theorem

The no-go can be made algebraic rather than heuristic.

Fix \(p\ge7\) and one Hasse reflection pair \(r,s\).  Take the actual local Apéry row data, so

\[
\mathscr Ab=0,
\qquad b_r=b_s=0,
\qquad b_{r-1},b_{s-1}\ne0.
\]

### 10.1 Cartier module

Let \(d=d_p\ge8\), and take

\[
M_d=\mathbf F_p^{d-1}
\]

with reversal involution

\[
\mathcal F(x_0,\ldots,x_{d-2})
=(x_{d-2},\ldots,x_0).
\]

Define the two source functionals

\[
\ell_g(x)=x_0,
\]

\[
\ell_H(x)=-x_2+A_p^{\rm head}x_1+C_px_0.
\]

Then

\[
\ell_g(\mathcal Fx)=x_{d-2},
\]

\[
\ell_H(\mathcal Fx)
=-x_{d-4}+A_p^{\rm head}x_{d-3}+C_px_{d-2}.
\]

For **arbitrary** four scalars

\[
g_0,H_0,g_1,H_1\in\mathbf F_p,

choose

\[
x_0=g_0,\quad x_1=0,\quad x_2=C_pg_0-H_0,

and

\[
x_{d-2}=g_1,\quad x_{d-3}=0,\quad x_{d-4}=C_pg_1-H_1.

Then

\[
(\ell_g(x),\ell_H(x),
\ell_g(\mathcal Fx),\ell_H(\mathcal Fx))
=(g_0,H_0,g_1,H_1).
\tag{10.1}
\]

So the abstract reciprocal Cartier section imposes **no scalar relation at all** between \((g,H)\) at a row and at its shifted partner.  The head/tail map has rank four.

The ordinary rows \(r,s\) belong to different Cartier orbits

\[
\{r,s+2\},
\qquad
\{s,r+2\},
\]

unless one is at a boundary collision.  Hence their source pairs can be chosen independently in this local reciprocal module while both Cartier involutions are satisfied exactly.

### 10.2 Green recurrences

Given a chosen source value \(g_u\), prescribe any value \(j_u\) and any neighbor \(j_{u-1}=x\).  Then

\[
\boxed{
j_{u-2}
=\frac{-5g_u-u^3j_u+P(u-1)x}{(u-1)^3}
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

makes

\[
\mathscr A_uj=-5g_u
\]

exactly.

Thus one may impose

\[
j_r=0,
\qquad
j_s=\alpha
\]

with arbitrary \(\alpha\), including \(\alpha\ne0\), and solve the local neighboring state.  This models a Hasse row that is common at \(r\) but not at its ordinary reflection.

Likewise, for arbitrary \(H_u\), any prescribed \(y_u\), and any \(y_{u-1}=z\),

\[
\boxed{
y_{u-2}
=\frac{H_u-u^3y_u+P(u-1)z}{(u-1)^3}
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

makes

\[
\mathscr A_uy=H_u.
\]

Putting

\[
J_1=j,
\qquad
J_2=y-7j
\]

satisfies both supplied recurrences exactly.

### 10.3 The \(p^4\) Fricke fiber

Attach independently the direct-jet fiber (2.5).  It satisfies the full cubic-block Fricke involution, including the companion cocycle \(24c/5\).  Its first coordinate is \(\gamma\), not \(j=\kappa\), so this attachment introduces no equation identifying the reflected depth with \(J_1\).

This direct-product local module therefore satisfies:

* the homogeneous Apéry reflection on \(b\);
* the exact shift-two Cartier reversal;
* the affine normalized \(p^4\) direct/reflected cocycle;
* \(\mathscr AJ_1=-5g\);
* \(\mathscr A(J_2+7J_1)=H\);

while allowing \(J_1(r)=0\), \(J_1(s)\ne0\) and arbitrary local second-neighbor values.

This is a countermodel to any deduction from the **two involutions plus the displayed recurrences alone**.  It is not claimed to be a second global Apéry arithmetic object.  A stronger theorem for the actual Apéry section would have to use additional arithmetic relations inside the particular Hasse numerator, beyond the involution/Green module axioms.

## 14. What is and is not proved about counting

The exact ordinary-reflection transform (4.4) says that, even after \(b_r=0\), a second transverse zero at the reflected row requires

\[
\Sigma_{r,s}(g_mb_{m-1})=0.
\tag{14.1}
\]

This is an interval Green condition whose length can be \(\Theta(p)\).  Cartier reciprocity turns each \(g_m\) into a head coordinate paired with a tail coordinate at \(\tau(m)\); it does not turn (14.1) into a bounded-degree polynomial in \(r\).

The two reflections generate the shift by two, but each shift carries a new head/tail or seam datum.  Iterating from \(r\) toward \(s\) therefore accumulates \(\Theta(p)\) state rather than producing a fixed transfer matrix.

The only bounded-degree conditions visible directly from target preservation are the exceptional cubic conditions

\[
P(r-1)=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
P(r+1)=0,
\]

which say that a shifted Cartier partner is itself a Hasse target.  Neither is forced by a common pair, and both supplied common labels violate them.

Therefore the two-involution package gives **no improvement** from the existing transverse zero bound to \(O(1)\), and no fixed-degree resultant for the common-label set.

The determinant (9.8) is different: it is a local separation certificate after the common label is known.  Its nonvanishing can coexist with arbitrarily many common labels, so it does not bound their number.

## 15. Final theorem/no-go statement

For \(p\ge7\), let \(r\in(0,p)\) be a Hasse target and set \(s=p-1-r\), \(r^*=s+2\).  Then:

1. The normalized cubic-block Fricke action is the affine involution
   \[
   (\gamma,\lambda,\nu)
   \mapsto
   \left(-\gamma,\lambda,-\nu+\frac{24}{5}c\right),
   \]
   where \(\gamma_1=12\).  This controls reflected \(p\)-adic depth.

2. The Hecke neighbor is a different extension, with
   \[
   \kappa_0=0,\quad\kappa_1=-36,
   \qquad
   \Xi_r=-1-5\sum_{m=1}^r g_mb_{m-1}.
   \]
   On targets \(J_1=\kappa\), and
   \[
   D_{p,r}(J_1(s)-J_1(r))
   =-5\Sigma_{r,s}(g_mb_{m-1}).
   \]

3. For \(Y=J_2+7J_1\),
   \[
   D_{p,r}(Y_s-Y_r)
   =\Sigma_{r,s}(b_{m-1}H_{p,m}).
   \]

4. The difference operator intertwines the two involutions by
   \[
   \mathscr A_r(z\circ\sigma)
   =-(\mathscr Az)_{\tau(r)}.
   \]
   Hence the reflected Green cocycles are sourced at \(s+2\), not \(s\).

5. Cartier reciprocity identifies those shifted sources with tail, not head, coordinates of a rational state of exact dimension \(2p-2\) or \(2p-6\).  Thus the formal shift-two composition does not close to bounded rank.

6. A separate Frobenius-block summation gives the label-preserving second-coordinate certificate
   \[
   5J_2(r)=\widetilde Z_r^{(p)},
   \qquad
   W_r(b,\widetilde Z^{(p)})=30J_2(r)/a_r,
   \]
   with the endpoint frame \(\lambda_pa\) included.  This certifies transversality but gives no common-root count.

Accordingly, the mismatch between \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\) does **not** secretly produce a bounded-degree common-pair theorem.  Its exact content is a Green interval plus a head-to-tail Cartier cocycle.  The small point \((17,13)\), whose reflected row has \(J_1(17,3)=2\), is already an explicit arithmetic witness that the transverse label is not preserved by ordinary reflection.
