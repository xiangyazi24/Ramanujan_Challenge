# Q7205 (rc6): exact continuant-identity audit

## Audit verdict

The objection is correct.  Under the conventions of
`problems/3.2/proof.tex` at commit
`d924bb6b47acd308742f98ef8f7e07314bbf7298`, Q7202 made two claims that
must be corrected.

1. Splitting a return of length `s+G` after the first short span `s`
   leaves the factor \(N_{s-1}(x)\), not \(N_{s+1}(x)\).
2. The degree-growth discussion in Q7202 is not a proof that an
   Apéry-specific compression identity cannot exist.  It shows why the
   currently used resultant has long-bridge complexity, but shifted Apéry
   coefficients are all specialized polynomials in the same variable and
   may satisfy relations not present in a free-coefficient model.

The rigorous conclusion is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{No Apéry-specific bounded-gap compression identity is known.}}
\]

There is a rigorous generic countermodel showing that identities of the
abstract \(2\times2\) transfer/Casoratian algebra alone cannot infer the
bridge return from the two short endpoint returns.  That generic statement
does **not** prove nonexistence after the special Apéry specialization.

The exact reflection identity is also shifted relative to the formula in
Q7202.  With the present \(N_h\)-convention it is

\[
N_h(-u-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(u).
\]

## 1. Exact conventions from `proof.tex`

The positional continuants are

\[
N_1(u)=1,\qquad N_2(u)=P(u+1),
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(u)=P(u+h)N_h(u)-(u+h)^6N_{h-1}(u).
\]

For \(2\le d<e\), the exact addition formula is

\[
\boxed{
N_e(u)=N_d(u)N_{e-d+1}(u+d-1)
       -(u+d)^6N_{d-1}(u)N_{e-d}(u+d).
}
\tag{A}
\]

Writing

\[
S_d(u)=\prod_{j=2}^{d}(u+j),
\]

the exact Dodgson identity used in `proof.tex` is

\[
N_{d-1}(u+1)N_{d-1}(u)
-N_{d-2}(u+1)N_d(u)
=\prod_{j=2}^{d-1}(u+j)^6,
\tag{D}
\]

and the resulting Bezout identity is

\[
\boxed{
N_{e-1}(u+1)N_d(u)-N_{d-1}(u+1)N_e(u)
=S_d(u)^6N_{e-d}(u+d).
}
\tag{B}
\]

Thus every common-zero equivalence obtained by subtraction is a
**saturated** statement away from \(S_d(u)=0\).  The saturation cannot be
silently omitted: `proof.tex` records the example
\(p=73\), \(\gcd(N_3,N_4)=x+3\), although \(N_1=1\); the spurious common
root is the cut-edge factor \(x+3\mid S_3(x)\).

## 2. Correct first-span/bridge identity

Let the four relevant landmarks of a separated pair be

\[
x<z:=x+s<y:=x+s+G<w:=y+t\le p-1.
\]

For an actual pair of chains in the same projective state \(q\),

\[
\pi_x=\pi_z=\pi_y=\pi_w=q.
\]

Consequently, on these nonwrapping intervals, the return criterion gives

\[
N_s(x)=0,\qquad N_G(z)=0,\qquad N_t(y)=0,
\]

as well as the composite returns

\[
N_{s+G}(x)=N_{G+t}(z)=N_{s+G+t}(x)=0.
\]

This distinction matters: the two short equations \(N_s(x)=0\) and
\(N_t(y)=0\) alone do **not** say that the two chains have the same state.
The shared-state hypothesis supplies \(N_G(z)=0\) directly, or equivalently
supplies the composite return \(N_{s+G}(x)=0\).

Put \(d=s\), \(e=s+G\) in (A).  The exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
N_{s+G}(x)
=N_s(x)N_{G+1}(x+s-1)
 -(x+s)^6N_{s-1}(x)N_G(x+s).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Hence, on the locus \(N_s(x)=0\),

\[
\boxed{
N_{s+G}(x)
=-(x+s)^6N_{s-1}(x)N_G(x+s).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The factor that survives is exactly \(N_{s-1}(x)\).
The appearance of \(N_{s+1}(x)\) in Q7202 was an indexing error.
Indeed, setting \(G=1\) in (2.2) merely gives

\[
N_{s+1}(x)=-(x+s)^6N_{s-1}(x),
\]

because \(N_1=1\); this explains why \(N_{s+1}\) is adjacent to the
calculation, but it is not the factor in the general bridge formula.

### Exact unit conditions

From (2.2), the equivalence

\[
N_{s+G}(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N_G(x+s)=0
\tag{2.3}
\]

on the locus \(N_s(x)=0\) requires

\[
x+s\ne0\pmod p,
\qquad
N_{s-1}(x)\ne0\pmod p.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The second condition is not an extra heuristic primitivity assumption.
Under \(N_s(x)=0\), (D) gives

\[
N_{s-1}(x+1)N_{s-1}(x)
=\prod_{j=2}^{s-1}(x+j)^6.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Therefore both \(N_{s-1}(x)\) and \(N_{s-1}(x+1)\) are units whenever

\[
\prod_{j=2}^{s-1}(x+j)\ne0\pmod p.
\]

For an actual nonwrapping interval
\(0\le x<x+s\le p-1\), every one of

\[
x+2,x+3,\ldots,x+s
\]

lies in \(\{1,\ldots,p-1\}\).  Thus all required factors are units.
Equivalently, the single structural condition

\[
S_s(x)=\prod_{j=2}^{s}(x+j)\ne0\pmod p
\tag{2.6}
\]

suffices.  This is exactly the carrier/cut-edge saturation in
Corollary `cor:sep-block` of `proof.tex`.

The Bezout form displays all factors at once.  With \(d=s\),
\(e=s+G\), (B) is

\[
N_{s+G-1}(x+1)N_s(x)
-N_{s-1}(x+1)N_{s+G}(x)
=S_s(x)^6N_G(x+s).
\tag{2.7}
\]

On \(N_s(x)=0\),

\[
\boxed{
-N_{s-1}(x+1)N_{s+G}(x)
=S_s(x)^6N_G(x+s).
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

Together with (2.5), this proves the exact saturated equivalence

\[
\boxed{
N_s(x)=N_{s+G}(x)=0
\iff
N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0
}
\quad\text{when }S_s(x)\ne0.
\tag{2.9}
\]

No \(N_{s+1}\) factor occurs in this statement.

## 3. Exact audit of the second short return

Now start at \(z=x+s\), split the return of length \(G+t\) after the
bridge of length \(G\), and use \(y=z+G\).  Formula (A) gives

\[
\boxed{
N_{G+t}(z)
=N_G(z)N_{t+1}(y-1)
-y^6N_{G-1}(z)N_t(y).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

On the bridge-return locus \(N_G(z)=0\),

\[
\boxed{
N_{G+t}(z)=-y^6N_{G-1}(z)N_t(y).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the surviving adjacent factor in this orientation is
\(N_{G-1}(z)\), not an index-plus-one continuant.

The required units are

\[
y\ne0\pmod p,
\qquad
N_{G-1}(z)\ne0\pmod p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Under \(N_G(z)=0\), Dodgson gives

\[
N_{G-1}(z+1)N_{G-1}(z)
=\prod_{j=2}^{G-1}(z+j)^6.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Hence (3.3) follows from

\[
S_G(z)=\prod_{j=2}^{G}(z+j)\ne0\pmod p.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For the actual interval \(z<y\le p-1\), this is automatic.  Also,
\(G=1\) cannot occur for a same-state bridge because \(N_1=1\), so the
separated same-state setting automatically has \(G\ge2\).

The corresponding Bezout identity is

\[
N_{G+t-1}(z+1)N_G(z)
-N_{G-1}(z+1)N_{G+t}(z)
=S_G(z)^6N_t(y).
\tag{3.6}
\]

On \(N_G(z)=0\),

\[
\boxed{
-N_{G-1}(z+1)N_{G+t}(z)
=S_G(z)^6N_t(y).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
N_G(z)=N_{G+t}(z)=0
\iff
N_G(z)=N_t(y)=0
}
\quad\text{when }S_G(z)\ne0.
\tag{3.8}
\]

One may instead split the total return from \(x\) at \(s+G\):

\[
N_{s+G+t}(x)
=N_{s+G}(x)N_{t+1}(y-1)
-y^6N_{s+G-1}(x)N_t(y).
\tag{3.9}
\]

On \(N_{s+G}(x)=0\), the unit conditions on the full nonwrapping interval
give

\[
N_{s+G+t}(x)=0\iff N_t(y)=0.
\]

These are consistency identities among returns already supplied by
\(\pi_x=\pi_z=\pi_y=\pi_w\); they do not derive shared-state equality
from the two isolated short windows.

## 4. Reflection audit

The reflection in `projective_variance_reduction.tex` is

\[
\rho(n)=p-1-n,
\]

and a chain \((u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3)\) is sent to

\[
(\rho(u_3),\rho(u_2),\rho(u_1),\rho(u_0)).
\]

For the four landmarks \(x<z<y<w\), write

\[
\widehat x=\rho(w)=p-1-y-t,
\qquad
\widehat z=\rho(y)=p-1-y,
\]

\[
\widehat y=\rho(z)=p-1-x-s,
\qquad
\widehat w=\rho(x)=p-1-x.
\]

Then

\[
\widehat z-\widehat x=t,
\qquad
\widehat y-\widehat z=G,
\qquad
\widehat w-\widehat y=s.
\]

Thus, after restoring left-to-right orientation, reflection swaps the two
short spans \(s\leftrightarrow t\), reverses each short gap triple, and
leaves the bridge length \(G\) unchanged.

The exact polynomial reflection identity is

\[
\boxed{
N_h(-u-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(u).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since congruent arguments may differ by \(p\), (4.1) yields

\[
N_t(\widehat x)
\equiv(-1)^{t-1}N_t(y)\pmod p,
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
N_G(\widehat z)
\equiv(-1)^{G-1}N_G(z)\pmod p,
\tag{4.3}
\]

\[
N_s(\widehat y)
\equiv(-1)^{s-1}N_s(x)\pmod p.
\tag{4.4}
\]

In particular, reflection preserves the bridge zero but supplies no second,
independent bridge polynomial: it sends the same equation to a nonzero
scalar multiple of itself.

For completeness, applying (A) to the reflected pair and splitting after
the reflected first span \(t\) gives

\[
N_{t+G}(\widehat x)
=N_t(\widehat x)N_{G+1}(\widehat x+t-1)
-(\widehat x+t)^6N_{t-1}(\widehat x)N_G(\widehat x+t).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Since \(\widehat x+t=\widehat z=p-1-y\), on
\(N_t(\widehat x)=0\) this becomes

\[
\boxed{
N_{t+G}(\widehat x)
=-(p-1-y)^6N_{t-1}(\widehat x)N_G(p-1-y).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The factor \(N_{t-1}(\widehat x)\) is a unit on the reflected regular
locus.  Its exact relation to the original orientation is

\[
N_{t-1}(\widehat x)
\equiv(-1)^{t-2}N_{t-1}(y+1)\pmod p,
\]

and the latter is a unit under \(N_t(y)=0\) by (D) and the nonvanishing of
the cut-edge product.  Again the long factor is \(N_G\), merely reflected.

The Q7202 formula
\(U_m(-z-m)=(-1)^mU_m(z)\) should therefore not be used without an
explicit definition and reindexing of \(U_m\).  In the actual
`proof.tex` convention, (4.1) is the correct statement.

## 5. What can and cannot be proved about compression

### 5.1 Why the degree argument in Q7202 is insufficient

It is true that

\[
\deg_x N_G=3(G-1),
\]

and that the ordinary resultant involving \(N_G(x+s)\) has height growing
with \(G\).  This proves that the **particular** direct resultant method
pays long-bridge complexity.

It does not prove that every possible Apéry-specific elimination must do
so.  All coefficients

\[
P(x+j),\qquad (x+j)^6
\]

are images of one variable under additive shifts.  The specialization from
a free ring of recurrence coefficients to \(\mathbf Z[x]\) has a large
kernel.  A polynomial of growing degree may reduce drastically on a
special incidence locus, and degree counting does not exclude a hidden
identity in that specialization.

Accordingly, the statements in Q7202 that “no universal elimination
exists” and that “a generic resultant necessarily pays degree \(O(G)\)”
were too strong when read as theorems about the specialized Apéry family.

### 5.2 A rigorous generic countermodel

There is, however, a precise theorem at the level of abstract transfer
algebra.

**Proposition (free-cocycle endpoint returns do not determine the
bridge).**  Fix a field \(k\), a projective point
\(q\in\mathbf P^1(k)\), and positive lengths \(s,G,t\).  Among regular
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(k)\) cocycles, the conditions that the left short block
returns its incoming state and the right short block returns its incoming
state do not determine whether the bridge returns \(q\) to itself.

**Proof.**  Choose coordinates with \(q=[1:0]\).  Take every matrix in the
left and right short blocks to be the identity.  Hence the left block fixes
\(q\), and the right block fixes whatever moving state enters it.  In the
first cocycle take every bridge matrix to be the identity.  In the second,
keep all bridge matrices equal to the identity except one, which is

\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(k).
\]

The short blocks, their lengths, and all endpoint-return statements are
identical in the two cocycles.  In the first bridge, \(q\) returns to
itself; in the second, \(Uq=[1:1]\ne q\).  All steps are nonsingular.
Thus no identity depending only on the two short blocks, their lengths,
and the universal matrix-product/Casoratian laws can characterize the
bridge return.  ∎

The same point can be stated in the generic continuant ring.  Give the
left, bridge, and right Jacobi blocks disjoint algebraically independent
coefficient sets, and let \(K_L,K_B,K_R\) be their generic continuants.
Then

\[
K_B\notin\sqrt{(K_L,K_R)}.
\]

Indeed, specialize the two endpoint blocks to zeros of \(K_L,K_R\) while
leaving the bridge variables free; one can choose the bridge specialization
with \(K_B\ne0\), and another with \(K_B=0\).  This is a genuine
independence theorem for free coefficients.

But neither generic statement settles the Apéry specialization.  The
matrices in the countermodel are not required to equal the prescribed
nonstationary Apéry matrices, and the disjoint coefficient sets in the
free continuant ring become the algebraically related shifts
\(P(x+j),(x+j)^6\).  Therefore the strongest justified project-specific
statement is the following.

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
The addition, Dodgson, Bezout, reflection, and generic
\(2\times2\) transfer identities currently known leave the long bridge
factor \(N_G(x+s)\) (or an equivalent coefficient of the length-\(G\)
transfer product).  No bounded-gap Apéry-specific identity eliminating it
is presently known.  Q7202 did not prove that no such specialized identity
can exist.
\end{minipage}}
\]

## 6. Correct replacement for the erroneous part of Q7202

```text
Let z=x+s and y=z+G.  On the regular nonwrapping locus,

  N_s(x)=N_{s+G}(x)=0
      iff
  N_s(x)=N_G(z)=0.

The exact identity is

  N_{s+G}(x)
    = N_s(x) N_{G+1}(z-1)
      - z^6 N_{s-1}(x) N_G(z).

Under N_s(x)=0, the factor N_{s-1}(x) is a unit by the
Dodgson identity after saturating S_s(x).  The analogous right-hand
identity is

  N_{G+t}(z)
    = N_G(z) N_{t+1}(y-1)
      - y^6 N_{G-1}(z) N_t(y).

Reflection sends (s,G,t) to (t,G,s) and sends N_G(z) to
(-1)^(G-1) N_G(z); it does not produce an independent long equation.
The known identities therefore align the three returns but do not
currently compress the bridge.  Nonexistence of an Apéry-specific
compression identity is not proved.
```

## Final conclusion

The corrected algebra is completely symmetric and saturated:

\[
N_s(x)=N_{s+G}(x)=0
\iff N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0,
\]

\[
N_G(x+s)=N_{G+t}(x+s)=0
\iff N_G(x+s)=N_t(x+s+G)=0,
\]

provided the corresponding cut-edge products are units.  On the actual
nonwrapping separated intervals these unit conditions hold automatically;
in polynomial elimination they must be imposed by saturation.

The surviving factors are \(N_{s-1}\) and \(N_{G-1}\), respectively.
Reflection swaps \(s\) and \(t\), preserves \(G\), and preserves the bridge
zero up to sign.  The far-bridge obstruction remains, but its correct
status is “not compressed by any known identity,” not a proved universal
non-compression theorem for the specialized Apéry recurrence.
