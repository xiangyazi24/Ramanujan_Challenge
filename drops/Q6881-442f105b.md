## 1. Work on squared values and use a logarithmic relative observable

Let

```text
delta_h(x)=N_h(x)/q_h(x)^3,
A_h(x)=q_h(x)N_h'(x)-3q_h'(x)N_h(x).
```

A root of `A_h` is a finite critical point of `delta_h`. Reflection gives

```text
delta_h(-h-1-x)=-delta_h(x),
A_h(-h-1-x)=A_h(x).
```

Hence mirror critical points have opposite critical values and equal squared values. The correct quotient observable is therefore

```text
W_h(x)=delta_h(x)^2.
```

The already proved nonvanishing of critical values permits a local logarithm. On each analytically continued critical branch choose a continuous lift

```text
L_h = Log W_h.
```

For comparisons between unrelated branches, the invariant distance is

```text
d_T(L_1,L_2)=inf_(k in Z) |L_1-L_2+2 pi i k|.
```

This avoids two common mistakes.

1. Absolute critical-value differences are badly scaled near endpoint cells; relative/logarithmic differences are the stable quantity.
2. Principal logarithms can jump by `2 pi i` at a branch cut. A principal-log jump is not a critical-value collision, and a genuine collision can be missed if the lifts are not tracked continuously.

If every two nonmirror quotient critical points have positive `d_T`, then the quotient critical-value polynomial is squarefree. This is stronger than the numerical inequality required by `[CRIT-2H]`.

## 2. Exact central critical points of the limiting cell

Write `x=pi z`. The given function is

```text
Phi(z)
 = (pi^3/3) cos(pi z)(cos(2 pi z)+2)/sin(pi z)^3
 = (pi^3/3) [3 cot(x)csc(x)^2-2 cot(x)].
```

Differentiating gives

```text
Phi'(z)
 = (pi^4/3) [8 csc(pi z)^2-9 csc(pi z)^4]
 = -(pi^4/3) [9-8 sin(pi z)^2]/sin(pi z)^4.             (2.1)
```

Thus, in the fundamental strip, the critical points satisfy

```text
sin(pi z)^2=9/8.
```

Put

```text
a=log(2)/(2 pi),
z_+=1/2+i a,
z_-=1/2-i a.
```

Indeed

```text
cosh(pi a)=cosh(log(2)/2)=3/(2 sqrt(2)),
```

so `sin(pi z_+)=sin(pi z_-)=3/(2sqrt(2))`. Moreover

```text
cos(pi z_+)=-i/(2sqrt(2)),
cos(pi z_-)= i/(2sqrt(2)),
cos(2 pi z_+)=cos(2 pi z_-)=-5/4.
```

Substitution gives the exact critical values

```text
Phi(z_+)=-2 i pi^3/27,
Phi(z_-)= 2 i pi^3/27,                                  (2.2)
```

and hence the common squared value

```text
Phi(z_+)^2=Phi(z_-)^2=-4 pi^6/729.                      (2.3)
```

The critical points are nondegenerate. A second differentiation at them gives

```text
Phi''(z_+)=-128 i pi^5/81,
Phi''(z_-)= 128 i pi^5/81.                              (2.4)
```

In particular

```text
|Phi''(z_+)|=|Phi''(z_-)|=128 pi^5/81.                  (2.5)
```

These identities provide an explicit implicit-function constant for the local critical branches. They also explain why `log 2` must occur in the separation coefficient: it is the imaginary height of the central limiting critical point.

## 3. What the ratio expansion contributes

The stated expansion is

```text
R_n(z)
 = 1-3z/(2n)+[(15/8)z^2+(3C/8)z]/n^2+O(n^(-3)),
C=(19 lambda-2)/(17 lambda-1),
lambda=17+12sqrt(2).
```

The algebraic constant simplifies to

```text
C=2-5sqrt(2)/8,
```

although its exact value will not affect either `kappa`.

Taking a logarithm gives

```text
log R_n(z)
 = -3z/(2n)+[(3/4)z^2+(3C/8)z]/n^2+O(n^(-3)).          (3.1)
```

At `z_+=1/2+i a`, the imaginary part is

```text
Im log R_n(z_+)
 = -3a/(2n)+[3a(2+C)/8]/n^2+O(n^(-3)).                 (3.2)
```

The value at `z_-` has the opposite imaginary part. The leading jump between the two conjugate critical-point factors is therefore `-3a/n`; passing to squared critical values doubles it to `-6a/n`.

Moving one cell transfers one index from the left side of the continuant to the right side. If the two large side lengths are

```text
n=h t+O(1),
m=h(1-t)+O(1),
```

then the first ratio term gives the branchwise logarithmic cell increment

```text
Delta_j L_h
 = 6a(1/m-1/n)+terms of order h^(-2).                  (3.3)
```

At the continuum level, (3.3) is

```text
F'(t)=6a[1/(1-t)-1/t].                                  (3.4)
```

Normalize by `F(1/2)=0`. Integration gives

```text
F(t)=-6a log(4t(1-t)).                                  (3.5)
```

This is the central squared-value log envelope. It is symmetric and strictly convex:

```text
F(1-t)=F(t),
F'(1/2)=0,
F''(t)=6a[t^(-2)+(1-t)^(-2)]>0,                         (3.6)
F''(1/2)=48a=24 log(2)/pi.                              (3.7)
```

The `n^(-2)` term in (3.1) contributes to the next envelope `G(t)/h`. Reflection implies

```text
G(1-t)=G(t),
G'(1/2)=0.                                              (3.8)
```

This is the key cancellation. Without it, `G/h` would contribute at order `h^(-2)` to the central gap and would change the constants. The value of `C` affects `G''` and the eventual remainder threshold, but not the leading parity constants.

Strictly speaking, (3.3)--(3.8) are still formal until the `O(n^(-3))` remainder is made uniform and propagated through the critical-point equation. Section 8 states the exact estimate needed for that step.

## 4. Exact parity calculation

Use the cell grid

```text
t_(h,j)=(j-1/2)/h,
c=1/2.
```

Mirror reflection sends `t` to `1-t`, so mirror grid points represent the same quotient orbit.

### 4.1 Odd height: central split

For odd `h`, the grid contains `c`. After quotienting by reflection, the first distinct orbit is at `c-1/h`. The limiting logarithmic gap is exactly

```text
Delta_odd^(0)(h)
 = F(c-1/h)-F(c)
 = -6a log(1-4/h^2).                                   (4.1)
```

Since `-log(1-x)>=x` for `0<x<1`,

```text
Delta_odd^(0)(h)>=24a/h^2.                              (4.2)
```

Moreover

```text
Delta_odd^(0)(h)
 = 24a/h^2+48a/h^4+O(h^(-6)).                          (4.3)
```

Therefore

```text
kappa_odd=24a=12 log(2)/pi.                            (4.4)
```

### 4.2 Even height: nearest nonmirror pair

For even `h`, the two nearest grid points

```text
c-1/(2h), c+1/(2h)
```

are mirror points and must not be counted as distinct quotient critical points. The first nonmirror comparison is

```text
c-1/(2h)  versus  c-3/(2h).
```

Its limiting logarithmic gap is exactly

```text
Delta_even^(0)(h)
 = F(c-3/(2h))-F(c-1/(2h))
 = 6a log[(1-h^(-2))/(1-9h^(-2))].                     (4.5)
```

For `h>=4`, integrate `1/(1-u)` from `u=h^(-2)` to `u=9h^(-2)` to obtain

```text
Delta_even^(0)(h)>=48a/h^2.                             (4.6)
```

Also

```text
Delta_even^(0)(h)
 = 48a/h^2+240a/h^4+O(h^(-6)).                         (4.7)
```

Therefore

```text
kappa_even=48a=24 log(2)/pi.                           (4.8)
```

The factor of two is purely geometric. In the odd grid the first quotient displacement from the centre is `1/h`; in the even grid the central mirror orbit is at distance `1/(2h)` and the next quotient orbit is at distance `3/(2h)`.

## 5. Quantitative NEAR estimate

Fix the central neighbourhood

```text
|t-c|<=rho,
rho=1/8.
```

For the explicit `F`, useful constants are

```text
m       := min F''(t) = F''(c)=48a=24 log(2)/pi,
mu_rho  := |F'(c-rho)|=32a/5=16 log(2)/(5pi),
M_F3    := sup |F'''(t)|
          =12a[(3/8)^(-3)-(5/8)^(-3)]
          =602112a/3375.                               (5.1)
```

The `M_F3` value is included for a Taylor-form proof, although the exact formulas (4.1) and (4.5) are stronger.

Assume the central critical branch has the rigorous expansion

```text
L_h(t)=F(t)+h^(-1)G(t)+E_h(t),                          (5.2)
G'(c)=0,
|G''(t)|<=M_G2,
|partial_t E_h(t)|<=K_1 h^(-2)                         (5.3)
```

throughout `|t-c|<=rho`.

Then the odd candidate satisfies

```text
Delta_odd(h)
 >= kappa_odd/h^2-B_odd/h^3,
B_odd=M_G2/2+K_1.                                      (5.4)
```

Indeed

```text
|G(c-1/h)-G(c)|<=M_G2/(2h^2),
|E_h(c-1/h)-E_h(c)|<=K_1/h^3.
```

Similarly the even candidate satisfies

```text
Delta_even(h)
 >= kappa_even/h^2-B_even/h^3,
B_even=5M_G2/4+K_1.                                    (5.5)
```

because

```text
|G(c-3/(2h))-G(c-1/(2h))|
 <= (M_G2/2)[(3/(2h))^2+(1/(2h))^2]
 =5M_G2/(4h^2).
```

A sufficient local positivity threshold is

```text
H_pos
 = max(
     6,
     ceil(2B_odd/kappa_odd),
     ceil(2B_even/kappa_even)
   ).                                                   (5.6)
```

For `h>=H_pos`, each candidate gap is at least half its stated leading term.

### 5.1 Why these are the nearest same-branch pairs

The exact `F` is symmetric and strictly convex. Thus its grid increments increase strictly as one moves away from the centre. The first odd increment is (4.1); after removing the even central mirror pair, the first even increment is (4.5).

The perturbation preserves this ordering once

```text
h>=H_order,
H_order
 = max(
     ceil(2M_G2/m),
     ceil(8K_1/m)
   ).                                                   (5.7)
```

To see this, for `t<c` use `-F'(t)>=m(c-t)`, while (5.3) gives

```text
|h^(-1)G'(t)+partial_t E_h(t)|
 <= (M_G2/h)(c-t)+K_1/h^2.
```

At the closest grid point `c-t>=1/(2h)`. The bounds in (5.7) leave at least a fixed fraction of the convex drift. Hence all farther same-branch increments are larger than the parity candidate.

Set

```text
H_NEAR=max(H_pos,H_order).                              (5.8)
```

This completes the NEAR argument, conditional only on the mixed derivative estimate (5.3).

## 6. FAR bulk compactness

The limiting function `Phi` and the central expansion identify only the branch which becomes nearly degenerate at the centre. A global theorem must control every quotient critical branch supplied by the complete cell decomposition.

Let the limiting lifted log-values be

```text
L_alpha(t), alpha in B,
```

on the bulk compact set

```text
K_delta=[delta,1-delta],
delta=1/8.
```

Let `A` denote the central branch from Sections 3--5. Remove the allowed mirror diagonal. The required compactness constants are:

```text
mu_far
 := min |partial_t L_alpha(t)|
```

over portions of each branch outside its listed stationary neighbourhoods, and

```text
eta_cross
 := inf d_T(L_alpha(t),L_beta(s))                      (6.1)
```

over distinct nonmirror branches and over compact branch pieces not handled by a local crossing chart.

The FAR proof requires

```text
mu_far>0,
eta_cross>0.                                            (6.2)
```

These are genuine analytic statements. They do not follow merely from the formula for the central `Phi`.

Assume uniform finite-height approximations

```text
|L_(h,alpha)(t)-L_alpha(t)-h^(-1)G_alpha(t)|<=K_0 h^(-2),
|partial_t L_(h,alpha)(t)-partial_t L_alpha(t)
   -h^(-1)partial_t G_alpha(t)|<=K_1 h^(-2),            (6.3)
```

and define

```text
M_0=max_alpha ||G_alpha||_infinity,
M_1=max_alpha ||partial_t G_alpha||_infinity.
```

A sufficient derivative threshold is

```text
H_der
 = max(
     ceil(4M_1/mu_far),
     ceil(sqrt(4K_1/mu_far))
   ).                                                   (6.4)
```

Then a one-cell same-branch FAR gap is at least

```text
mu_far/(2h).                                            (6.5)
```

A sufficient cross-branch threshold is

```text
H_cross
 = max(
     ceil(8M_0/eta_cross),
     ceil(sqrt(8K_0/eta_cross))
   ).                                                   (6.6)
```

Then every cross-branch FAR gap is at least `eta_cross/2`.

For `h>=H_NEAR`, the central candidate has an upper bound of the form

```text
Delta_central(h)<=C_central/h^2,
C_central=2 kappa_even                                  (6.7)
```

after increasing `H_NEAR` harmlessly. Therefore the FAR gaps are larger than the central candidate once

```text
H_dom
 = max(
     ceil(2C_central/mu_far),
     ceil(sqrt(2C_central/eta_cross))
   ).                                                   (6.8)
```

The bulk FAR threshold is

```text
H_FAR=max(H_der,H_cross,H_dom).                         (6.9)
```

Under (6.2)--(6.3), the parity pair from Section 4 is the globally closest pair among all bulk critical values for `h>=max(H_NEAR,H_FAR)`.

## 7. Endpoint audit

This is not optional. The ratio expansion is an expansion for large side length. It is not uniform in cells for which one side length remains bounded.

A safe decomposition is

```text
bulk:       n,m>=N_*,
left edge:  0<=n<N_*,
right edge: 0<=m<N_*.
```

The bulk estimate may allow `t` to approach zero as long as `n,m>=N_*`; the remaining endpoint families are finitely many indices but infinitely many heights. They cannot be discharged by checking finitely many heights.

For each fixed endpoint index `r<N_*`, one needs a one-sided critical-value expansion and a quantitative separation bound. A sufficient statement is

```text
sep_end(h)>=eta_end h^(-beta),
eta_end>0,
beta<2,                                                 (7.1)
```

for every endpoint critical value against every nonmirror endpoint or bulk value, once `h>=H_end`. The natural expected strength is `beta=0` or `beta=1`; either is much larger than the central `h^(-2)` gap.

If the one-sided expansion has the form

```text
L_(h,r,alpha)
 = A_(r,alpha) log h+B_(r,alpha)+O(h^(-1)),             (7.2)
```

then (7.1) follows from one of the following explicit alternatives:

```text
A_(r,alpha) != A_(s,beta),
```

or, when the slopes agree,

```text
d_T(B_(r,alpha),B_(s,beta))>=eta_0>0,
```

with a certified `O(h^(-1))` remainder. If both leading data agree, that pair needs its own higher-order local chart; it cannot be put into a generic compactness box.

The exact `Phi` chart does not by itself prove (7.1). There is also a degree-count warning: `Phi'` has two roots in a fundamental fixed `z`-cell, whereas `A_h` has degree `4h-4`. Unless the proved cell theorem explicitly accounts for the remaining critical points in another chart, some critical branches leave every fixed compact `z`-window or belong to a different endpoint/scaling chart. Those branches must be included in `eta_cross` or `eta_end` before claiming a global closest pair.

## 8. Branch-switch audit

A change of labels is harmless; a collision is not. The proof must be formulated for unordered critical-point clusters or for branches defined by Riesz disks, not by sorting roots numerically.

There are three cases.

### 8.1 Pure relabelling

Two analytic labels exchange because a chosen coordinate or ordering convention changes. If the critical points remain simple and their squared critical values remain distinct, this has no mathematical effect. Continue the two Riesz projections through the switch.

### 8.2 Transverse limiting value crossing

Suppose two nonmirror limiting log-values satisfy

```text
L_alpha(t_0)=L_beta(s_0) mod 2 pi i
```

but the crossing is transverse. Then `eta_cross=0`, so the compactness argument in Section 6 is invalid. A separate local chart is required. A sufficient quantitative replacement is

```text
d_T(L_(h,alpha),L_(h,beta))
 >= nu_switch/h                                         (8.1)
```

on the discrete grid near the crossing. Since `h^(-1)>>h^(-2)`, such a switch is still safely farther than the central pair.

### 8.3 Tangential crossing

A tangency can produce an `h^(-2)` gap and compete directly with the central pair. It must either be ruled out or added to the finite list of candidate closest pairs with its own exact coefficient. The central `Phi` calculation does not rule out a tangency on another branch.

Thus the cleanest global hypothesis is:

```text
There are no nonmirror limiting squared-value crossings on the bulk compactum.
```

If this is false, the exact substitute is a finite crossing classification with a certified transversal constant `nu_switch`, and a proof that no unlisted crossing has contact order at least two.

## 9. The minimal uniform analytic estimate

The weakest useful estimate can be stated either at the ratio level or directly at the value-increment level.

### 9.1 Ratio-level version

Let

```text
r_n(z)
 := log R_n(z)+3z/(2n)-[(3/4)z^2+(3C/8)z]/n^2.
```

Choose disjoint disks `D_+`, `D_-` around `z_+`, `z_-`, avoiding the poles of the cell function. It is sufficient to prove explicit constants `B_0,B_1,B_2` and `N_0` such that

```text
sup_(n>=N_0,z in D_+ union D_-)
 n^3 (|r_n(z)|+|partial_z r_n(z)|+|partial_z^2 r_n(z)|)
 <=B_0,                                                  (9.1)

sup_(n>=N_0,z in D_+ union D_-)
 n^4 (|r_(n+1)(z)-r_n(z)|
      +|partial_z r_(n+1)(z)-partial_z r_n(z)|)
 <=B_1.                                                  (9.2)
```

The `z` derivatives are needed to move from the cell function to its critical point and critical value. The finite difference in `n` is what supplies one cell derivative and turns a pointwise `h^(-2)` envelope error into an adjacent-cell `h^(-3)` error.

The exact local implicit-function constants begin with

```text
m_Phi=128 pi^5/81.                                     (9.3)
```

For a chosen disk radius `r_*`, put

```text
M_Phi3(r_*)=sup_(|z-z_+|<=r_* or |z-z_-|<=r_*) |Phi'''(z)|,
m_*=m_Phi-r_* M_Phi3(r_*).                             (9.4)
```

Choose `r_*` so that `m_*>0`. Then the usual Newton/Rouche argument gives an explicit critical-point displacement and converts (9.1)--(9.2) into constants `M_G2,K_0,K_1` in Sections 5--6. This is a fixed compact interval/complex-ball certification, not a scan over heights.

### 9.2 Direct, genuinely minimal version

For the central proof, one can bypass a full `C^1` theorem and prove only

```text
|[L_(h,j+1)-L_(h,j)]
  -[F(t_(j+1))-F(t_j)]
  -h^(-1)[G(t_(j+1))-G(t_j)]|
 <=K_mix/h^3.                                           (9.5)
```

This is exactly the strength required. An `O(h^(-2))` bound in (9.5) is not enough, and an `o(h^(-2))` bound without an effective rate does not produce a finite threshold.

For the whole all-height theorem, (9.5) must be accompanied by:

```text
(a) bulk branch compactness: mu_far>0 and eta_cross>0,
    or a finite transversal crossing classification;

(b) endpoint separation: sep_end(h)>=eta_end h^(-beta), beta<2;

(c) an explicit critical-point chart covering every root of A_h.
```

This triple is the minimal missing analytic package.

## 10. Explicit tail threshold and reduction to a finite scan

Assume the constants in Sections 5--9 have been certified. Let

```text
H_IFT     = threshold from the Newton/Rouche inequalities using m_*,B_0,B_1;
H_NEAR    = max(H_pos,H_order);
H_FAR     = max(H_der,H_cross,H_dom);
H_END     = endpoint threshold from (7.1);
H_SWITCH  = 1 if eta_cross>0, otherwise the threshold from (8.1).
```

Then take

```text
H_0=max(H_IFT,H_NEAR,H_FAR,H_END,H_SWITCH).             (10.1)
```

For every `h>=H_0`:

1. all critical points lie in certified branch charts;
2. the odd/even central candidate has a positive gap of order `h^(-2)`;
3. every other same-branch bulk gap is larger by convexity;
4. every FAR same-branch gap is `Omega(h^(-1))`;
5. every cross-branch compact gap is `Omega(1)`, or every classified switch gap is `Omega(h^(-1))`;
6. every endpoint gap is `Omega(h^(-beta))` with `beta<2`.

Hence the parity candidate is the global closest nonmirror pair and is nonzero. All quotient critical values are distinct, so full Morse holds and `[CRIT-2H]` follows.

The remaining work is then exactly the finite check

```text
2<=h<H_0.                                               (10.2)
```

The banked scan through `h=60` closes the theorem only if the certified constants yield `H_0<=61`. If `H_0>61`, the analytic theorem still reduces the problem to a finite check, but the machine scan must be extended to `H_0-1`.

## 11. Why the assumptions in the question do not yet give numerical constants

The phrase

```text
R_n(z)=...+O(n^(-3))
```

is formal unless all of the following are specified:

```text
- the complex z-domain;
- a constant uniform on that domain;
- first and second z-derivative bounds;
- stability under n -> n+1;
- uniformity when the left/right cell ratio varies;
- propagation through the critical-point implicit equation;
- coverage of endpoint and second-chart critical branches.
```

Without those data, `M_G2`, `K_0`, `K_1`, `eta_cross`, `mu_far`, and `eta_end` are not available. Therefore no honest numerical `H_0`, and in particular no proof that `H_0<=60`, can presently be extracted from the stated formal expansion.

The key distinction is:

```text
pointwise asymptotic value control:      O(h^(-2))  -- insufficient;
adjacent-cell/mixed-parameter control:   O(h^(-3))  -- sufficient locally.
```

This is the same inverse-square obstruction seen in the certified margins: the signal is `Theta(h^(-2))`, so a generic `O(h^(-1))` or pointwise `O(h^(-2))` tail cannot close the sign.

> **Conditional all-height critical-value separation theorem.**
> Let the Apéry continuant cell decomposition admit complete analytic critical branches, including endpoint charts. Suppose:
>
> 1. the central branch has the uniform expansion
>    `L_h=F+h^(-1)G+E_h`, where
>    `F(t)=-6a log(4t(1-t))`, `a=log(2)/(2pi)`,
