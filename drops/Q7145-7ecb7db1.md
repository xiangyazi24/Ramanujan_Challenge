## Executive verdict

1. There are three exact realizations, with importantly different
   complexities.

   * As a function of the **index** \(r\), \(b_r\) is a truncated classical
     \({}_4F_3(1)\) polynomial.  Its natural formal degree is
     \(4(p-1)\).  The unique reduced polynomial representing the function on
     \(\mathbf F_p\) has degree at most \(p-1\), but its degree is not bounded:
     below I prove the quantitative lower bound
     \(d_p\geq \log p/\log 34-1\).  Thus the tautological Artin--Schreier
     conductor in the index variable is provably unbounded.

   * The sequence has a **fixed three-variable Laurent-polynomial**
     realization \(b_n=\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda^n)\).  For
     \(0\leq n\leq p-2\), \(b_n\) is exactly the reduction of a Mellin
     character sum of the fixed map
     \(\Lambda:\mathbf G_m^3\to\mathbf A^1\).  This fixed geometric object
     has bounded geometric complexity; the varying index \(n\) is a Kummer
     character, not a base parameter.

   * The generating truncation \(H_p(t)\), not an individual coefficient
     \(b_r\), is the scalar Hasse--Witt section of the Apéry K3 variation in
     the standard period coordinate.  It has exact degree \(p-1\).  The fixed
     K3 local system has bounded rank and conductor, but its characteristic-
     \(p\) Hasse section lives in a \((p-1)\)-st tensor power and therefore has
     growing degree.  These statements are compatible, not contradictory.

2. Consequently, \(b_r\pmod p\) is **not** the Hasse invariant or the local
   Frobenius trace of a fixed family whose geometric parameter is \(r\).
   It is a coefficient of the Hasse section, equivalently a finite Mellin
   coefficient of the family’s point-count/Hasse trace function.  It can be
   realized as a **global** Frobenius trace after a Kummer twist indexed by
   \(r\), but the twist changes with \(r\) and with \(p\).

3. Standard Weil--Deligne bounds control complex absolute values of local
   traces and Mellin character sums.  They do not control the number of
   characters for which the corresponding algebraic integer is divisible by
   the residue-characteristic prime.  In particular, no currently standard
   finite-field hypergeometric, Hasse-stratification, or \(p\)-curvature
   theorem yields \(Z(p)=O(\sqrt p)\), nor the requested prime-average bound.

4. A concrete exact lemma does emerge for a short actual four-zero window.
   Four actual zeros with gaps \((a,b,c)\) force a four-term Mellin relation
   for the fixed Laurent-polynomial family whose sparse amplitude is
   divisible by \((T-1)^3\), has coefficient height \(O((a+b+c)^3)\), and is
   anti-reciprocal exactly on the symmetric gap slice \(a=c\).  This is an
   actual-state certificate, so formal phantom fibers cannot satisfy it merely
   by returning to another projective state.  It does not by itself give a
   zero count; it identifies a precise same-characteristic \(p\)-adic
   nonconcentration problem that is genuinely stronger than Deligne
   cancellation.

## 1. Exact realizations and their true complexity

### 1.1. The truncated hypergeometric polynomial in the index

Put

\[
 \Phi_p(X)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}
   \frac{(-X)_k^2(X+1)_k^2}{(k!)^4}
 \in\mathbf F_p[X].
\]

For \(r\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\),

\[
 \Phi_p(r)=b_r.
\]

Indeed, for \(k\leq r\),

\[
 \frac{(-r)_k(r+1)_k}{(k!)^2}
 =(-1)^k\binom rk\binom{r+k}k,
\]

and for \(k>r\) the factor \((-r)_k\) is zero.  Thus this is the exact
truncated identity

\[
 b_r={}_4F_3\!\left(
 \begin{matrix}-r,-r,r+1,r+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1\right).
\]

Two degree statements must not be conflated.

* As a formal polynomial, \(\deg\Phi_p=4(p-1)\).  The \(k=p-1\) summand is
  the unique summand of degree \(4(p-1)\), and its leading coefficient is
  nonzero.
* As a polynomial **function** on \(\mathbf F_p\), there is a unique
  representative
  \[
    \widetilde\Phi_p(X)\in\mathbf F_p[X],\qquad
    \deg\widetilde\Phi_p=d_p\leq p-1,
  \]
  modulo \(X^p-X\).  A root bound applied to this representative gives only
  \(Z(p)\leq d_p\leq p-1\) unless one proves a substantially smaller upper
  bound for \(d_p\).

The symmetry

\[
 \Phi_p(-1-X)=\Phi_p(X)
\]

is exact.  Evaluating at \(X=r\) gives the reflection law
\(b_{p-1-r}=b_r\pmod p\).  In particular \(b_0=b_{p-1}=1\), so every zero is
in \(1,\ldots,p-2\).

### 1.2. A fixed Laurent polynomial and an exact torus moment formula

Gorodetsky’s three-variable constant-term representation for the
\(\zeta(3)\) Apéry numbers is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda(x,y,z)=
 \frac{(xy+yz+zx+x+z)(xy+yz+zx+x+y+z+1)}{xyz}}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{b_n=\operatorname{CT}_{x,y,z}\Lambda(x,y,z)^n.}
\]

Every coordinate exponent occurring in \(\Lambda\) lies in
\(\{-1,0,1\}\).  Therefore every coordinate exponent in \(\Lambda^n\) lies
in \([-n,n]\).  For \(0\leq n\leq p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality on the
three-dimensional torus then isolates only the zero exponent:

\[
 \boxed{
 b_n\equiv-
 \sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
 \Lambda(x,y,z)^n\pmod p.}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The sign is \((-1)^3=-1\), because
\(\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p^\times}u^m\) is \(-1\) when
\(m\equiv0\pmod{p-1}\) and is zero otherwise.

Let

\[
 N_p(\lambda)=
 \#\{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3:
       \Lambda(x,y,z)=\lambda\}.
\]

Then, for \(1\leq n\leq p-2\),

\[
 \boxed{
 b_n\equiv-
 \sum_{\lambda\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
 N_p(\lambda)\lambda^n\pmod p.}
 \tag{1.2}
\]

This is the cleanest fixed-family realization of the **index**.  The map
\(\Lambda:\mathbf G_m^3\to\mathbf A^1\) is fixed over \(\mathbf Z\), while
\(n\) labels the multiplicative character \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda^n\).

More invariantly, let \(\omega_p:\mathbf F_p^\times\to\mu_{p-1}\) be the
Teichmüller character in a cyclotomic field and choose a prime
\(\mathfrak p\mid p\) for which
\(\omega_p(\lambda)\equiv\lambda\pmod{\mathfrak p}\).  Define

\[
 \mathfrak M_p(n)=
 \sum_{\lambda\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
 N_p(\lambda)\omega_p(\lambda)^n
 =\sum_{\substack{x\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3\\\Lambda(x)\ne0}}
   \omega_p(\Lambda(x))^n.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak M_p(n)\equiv-b_n\pmod{\mathfrak p}.}
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The right side is a Grothendieck trace-formula sum for the pullback of a
rank-one Kummer sheaf by the fixed map \(\Lambda\).  For every nontrivial
character, the Kummer sheaf has only tame ramification at \(0\) and
\(\infty\); its local conductor is independent of the order of the
character.  Thus the **geometric support and ramification are bounded**, even
though the character varies through all orders dividing \(p-1\).

### 1.3. The index-interpolation conductor is provably unbounded

The preceding fixed-torus realization must not be confused with a
bounded-degree polynomial in the variable \(r\).  In fact one can prove a
quantitative obstruction.

**Proposition 1 (logarithmic lower bound for the reduced index degree).**
Let \(d_p=\deg\widetilde\Phi_p\), where
\(\widetilde\Phi_p\) is the unique polynomial of degree at most \(p-1\)
representing \(r\mapsto b_r\) on \(\mathbf F_p\).  Then

\[
 \boxed{d_p\geq\frac{\log p}{\log34}-1.}
 \tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently, for a nontrivial additive character \(\psi\), the
Artin--Schreier sheaf
\(\mathcal L_{\psi(u\widetilde\Phi_p)}\), \(u\ne0\), has Swan conductor at
infinity \(d_p\), and this conductor is unbounded with \(p\).

**Proof.**  Let \(\Delta\) denote the forward-difference operator.  From the
constant-term representation,

\[
 \Delta^m b_0
 =\sum_{j=0}^m(-1)^{m-j}\binom mjb_j
 =\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda-1)^m.
 \tag{1.5}
\]

All coefficients of \(\Lambda\) are nonnegative, its constant coefficient is
\(b_1=5\), and hence every coefficient of \(\Lambda-1\) is nonnegative; its
constant coefficient is \(4\).  Moreover

\[
 (\Lambda-1)(1,1,1)=5\cdot7-1=34.
\]

It follows that

\[
 0<\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda-1)^m\leq34^m.
 \tag{1.6}
\]

If \(d_p=p-1\), (1.4) is immediate.  Otherwise set \(m=d_p+1\leq p-1\).
The points \(0,1,\ldots,m\) are distinct modulo \(p\), and a polynomial of
degree \(d_p\) has zero \(m\)-th forward difference.  Therefore

\[
 p\mid\Delta^m b_0=\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda-1)^m.
\]

The positive integer on the right is at most \(34^m\), so
\(p\leq34^m\), proving (1.4).  \(\square\)

This proposition is useful mainly as a fail-closed conductor audit.  It does
not show \(d_p\asymp p\), but it rules out any hidden bounded-degree
interpolation theorem.

### 1.4. The Hasse polynomial, point counts, and the algebraic generating function

The constant-term formula gives an elementary Hasse--Witt calculation.  In
the standard toric residue trivialization,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{CT}(1-t\Lambda)^{p-1}
 &=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{n}(-t)^n
   \operatorname{CT}\Lambda^n\\
 &\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}b_nt^n
 =H_p(t)\pmod p,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1.7}
\]

because
\(\binom{p-1}{n}(-1)^n\equiv1\pmod p\).  Thus \(H_p\) is the scalar
Hasse--Witt section of the corresponding toric Apéry variation.  Since
\(b_{p-1}=1\),

\[
 \boxed{\deg H_p=p-1.}
 \tag{1.8}
\]

There is also an exact point-count identity on the open torus.  For
\(t\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), summing (1.1) over \(0\leq n\leq p-2\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 H_p(t)=1-N_p(0)+N_p(t^{-1})\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.}
 \tag{1.9}
\]

Indeed,
\(\sum_{n=0}^{p-2}u^n\) equals \(1\) for \(u=0\), equals \(-1\) for
\(u=1\), and is zero otherwise; the final \(+1\) is the term
\(b_{p-1}t^{p-1}\).

Equation (1.9) is an exact fixed-family point-count realization of
**evaluations** of \(H_p\).  It does not identify the coefficient \(b_r\)
with the point count at the parameter \(r\).

Finally, let

\[
 \mathscr F_p(t)=\sum_{n\geq0}b_nt^n\in\mathbf F_p[[t]].
\]

The Lucas congruence \(b_{pn+r}=b_nb_r\pmod p\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr F_p(t)=H_p(t)\mathscr F_p(t)^p,
 \qquad
 \mathscr F_p(t)^{p-1}=H_p(t)^{-1}.}
 \tag{1.10}
\]

This is the exact function-field algebraic realization coming from
\(p\)-curvature/Lucas theory.  It also displays the growing complexity.  The
recent theorem of Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin proves

\[
 \boxed{
 H_p(t)=(t^2-34t+1)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
 \qquad
 \deg B_p=\frac{p-1-2\varepsilon_p}{2},}
 \tag{1.11}
\]

where \(\varepsilon_p\in\{0,1\}\) is explicitly determined by
\(p\bmod24\).  Correspondingly, the Galois degree of
\(\mathbf F_p(t,\mathscr F_p)/\mathbf F_p(t)\) is either
\((p-1)/2\) or \(p-1\).  Thus it is \(\Theta(p)\).  Their theorem proves the
square/quadratic-times-square factorization; it does **not** state that
\(B_p\) is squarefree, so squarefreeness must not be imported from the
repository’s finite computation.

For conductor bookkeeping, the following distinctions are the important
ones.

| object | exact size/degree | conductor statement |
|---|---:|---|
| reduced index polynomial \(\widetilde\Phi_p(r)\) | \(\log_{34}p-1\leq d_p\leq p-1\) | Artin--Schreier Swan at \(\infty\) is \(d_p\), hence unbounded |
| Hasse section \(H_p(t)\) | degree exactly \(p-1\) | \(\mathcal L_{\psi(H_p)}\) has Swan \(p-1\) at \(\infty\) |
| square root \(B_p(t)\) | degree \(\Theta(p)\) | no bounded-degree root-divisor or hyperelliptic-curve model |
| mod-\(p\) generating-function extension | degree \((p-1)/2\) or \(p-1\) | function-field degree grows linearly |
| fixed Apéry K3 transcendental local system | rank \(3\), four Picard--Fuchs punctures | geometric conductor \(O(1)\) away from the fixed bad primes |
| Kummer twist by \(\omega_p^r\) | rank unchanged | adds only fixed tame ramification at \(0,\infty\) |

The third and fourth rows are why “fixed K3 family” does not imply “bounded
complexity of the characteristic-\(p\) Hasse polynomial.”

## 2. What is the fixed algebraic family, and where is the index mismatch?

### 2.1. The classical Apéry K3 pencil

A standard fixed family is the Beukers--Peters family

\[
 V_\varphi:\qquad
 1-\varphi
 \frac{x(1-x)y(1-y)z(1-z)}{1-z+xyz}=0.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

For generic \(\varphi\), it is birational to a K3 surface of Picard number
\(19\).  Its holomorphic period at \(\varphi=0\) is

\[
 \varpi_0(\varphi)=\sum_{n\geq0}b_n\varphi^n,
\]

and its Picard--Fuchs operator is

\[
 \theta^3-
 \varphi(34\theta^3+51\theta^2+27\theta+5)
 +\varphi^2(\theta+1)^3,
 \qquad \theta=\varphi\frac d{d\varphi}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The singular parameter set is fixed: \(0,\infty\), and the two roots of
\(t^2-34t+1\).  The varying transcendental part has rank \(3\); the full
\(H^2\) has rank \(22\).

The Laurent polynomial in Section 1 gives a separate exact torus model of the
same period equation and is sufficient for every finite-field identity used
here.  I do not need, and do not assume without a citation, a specific
birational map from that exact Laurent-polynomial compactification to the
printed affine equation (2.1).

### 2.2. What is and is not a Hasse invariant

For a smooth good fiber at parameter \(t\), \(H_p(t)\) is the scalar by which
absolute Frobenius acts on the one-dimensional \(H^2(\mathcal O)\) line, in
the period normalization above.  Equivalently, it is the first Hasse--Witt
coefficient.  Its zero locus in the **parameter** \(t\) is the nonordinary
locus on the smooth base.

But

\[
 b_r=[t^r]H_p(t),
 \]

not \(H_p(r)\).  For \(1\leq r\leq p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality gives

\[
 \boxed{
 b_r=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}H_p(t)t^{-r}.}
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus \(Z(p)\) is a zero set in the **Mellin-dual character index**.  The
geometric parameter is \(t\); the index \(r\) labels the character
\(t\mapsto t^{-r}\).  This is the exact index/parameter mismatch.

There is a legitimate Frobenius-trace interpretation, but it is global and
twisted.  Let \(\mathcal V_\ell\) denote the fixed rank-three transcendental
local system on the smooth parameter curve and let
\(\mathcal L_{\omega_p^{-r}}\) be the Kummer sheaf.  The Grothendieck trace
formula identifies a Mellin sum of local traces with

\[
 -\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
  \operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
  R\Gamma_c(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
  \mathcal V_\ell\otimes\mathcal L_{\omega_p^{-r}})
 \right).
 \tag{2.4}
\]

After the fixed algebraic/boundary correction, its reduction modulo a prime
above \(p\) is \(b_r\).  Equations (1.2)--(1.3) provide an entirely explicit
version using the fixed torus map \(\Lambda\).

So the accurate answer to Question 2 is:

* **yes**, \(H_p(t)\) is the Hasse--Witt section of a fixed Apéry K3 family;
* **yes**, \(b_r\) is the reduction of a global Frobenius trace of a
  Kummer-twisted fixed family;
* **no**, \(b_r\) is not the Hasse invariant or local fiber trace at the
  parameter \(t=r\);
* **no**, the collection in \(r\) is not supplied by one fixed bounded-
  conductor sheaf on an \(r\)-line.  The sheaf is fixed on the \(t\)-line and
  the character twist changes with \(r\).

The usual pointwise Weil estimate for a smooth K3 fiber is

\[
 \left|\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_t\mid H^2_{\mathrm{et}})
 \right|\leq22p,
\]

and for the rank-three transcendental quotient the corresponding bound is
\(3p\).  These estimates bound point counts/evaluation traces.  They say
nothing directly about how many Mellin coefficients are zero modulo \(p\).

A useful warning is the central index.  The special coefficient
\(b_{(p-1)/2}\) is related to a fixed weight-four modular form because the
character is quadratic.  Generic \(r\) gives a character whose order can grow
with \(p\), so the special modular interpretation does not extend to a fixed
modular form for all \(r\).

## 3. What Weil--Deligne, finite-field hypergeometric, and \(p\)-curvature methods actually give

### 3.1. The strongest standard character-sum statement is archimedean

Let \(U\subset\mathbf P^1\) be the fixed smooth parameter locus.  For a
multiplicative character \(\chi\), put

\[
 S_p(\chi)=
 \sum_{t\in U(\mathbf F_p)}
 \operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_t\mid\mathcal V_\ell)\chi(t).
 \tag{3.1}
\]

The rank, number of singularities, and tame conductor of
\(\mathcal V_\ell\otimes\mathcal L_\chi\) are bounded independently of the
order of \(\chi\).  If the twist has no geometrically constant quotient, then
\(H_c^2\) contributes no main term, and Deligne’s weight theorem together
with Grothendieck--Ogg--Shafarevich gives

\[
 \boxed{|S_p(\chi)|\ll p^{3/2}.}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The exponent \(3/2\) is the square-root saving in a one-parameter sum of
weight-two traces.  If a particular twist has an invariant quotient, its
explicit \(p^2\)-sized main term must first be removed.  This qualification is
part of the theorem, not an optional detail.

After normalizing the weight-two trace by \(p\), (3.2) looks like the usual
\(O(\sqrt p)\) cancellation.  It is nevertheless a bound in complex absolute
value for an algebraic integer in a cyclotomic coefficient field.  The event
relevant here is

\[
 \mathfrak M_p(r)\in\mathfrak p,
 \tag{3.3}
\]

or equivalently \(b_r=0\) in the residue field.  An \(O(p^{3/2})\) bound does
not prevent divisibility by \(p\), and the degree of the cyclotomic field also
grows with \(p\).  Taking norms therefore gives no useful contradiction.

There is an even more elementary obstruction to any black-box implication
“bounded conductor \(\Rightarrow\) few zero Mellin coefficients.”  Let
\(f(t)=\chi_0(t)\) be a nontrivial rank-one Kummer trace function.  It has two
tame singularities and uniformly bounded conductor, but

\[
 \sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}f(t)\chi(t)
 =\begin{cases}p-1,&\chi=\chi_0^{-1},\\0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]

Thus a bounded-conductor trace function can have \(p-2\) exactly vanishing
Mellin coefficients.  Extra monodromy of the Apéry family may make such
extreme behavior implausible, but excluding it requires a specific
nonvanishing/anti-concentration theorem, not Deligne’s bound.

### 3.2. The direct additive-character zero count has growing conductor

Using the reduced index polynomial,

\[
 Z(p)=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
       \sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}
       \psi\bigl(u\widetilde\Phi_p(r)\bigr).
 \tag{3.4}
\]

For \(u\ne0\), the ordinary Weil polynomial bound is

\[
 \left|\sum_r\psi(u\widetilde\Phi_p(r))\right|
 \leq(d_p-1)\sqrt p
 \tag{3.5}
\]

when \(d_p>0\).  Hence this route gives only

\[
 Z(p)\leq1+d_p\sqrt p,
 \tag{3.6}
\]

capped of course by the trivial \(p\).  A bound \(d_p=p^{o(1)}\) would be
useful, and a bounded \(d_p\) would give the desired square-root estimate,
but there is no such upper bound.  Proposition 1 proves in the opposite
direction that \(d_p\to\infty\), quantitatively at least logarithmically.
The formal hypergeometric degree \(4(p-1)\) likewise gives no saving.

### 3.3. Why finite-field hypergeometric sheaves do not remove the mismatch

The classical truncated expression has upper parameters
\(-r,-r,r+1,r+1\).  As \(r\) varies, the corresponding multiplicative
characters vary.  Katz’s fixed finite-field hypergeometric sheaves give
uniform conductor when the character tuple is fixed; they do not turn this
whole same-characteristic character family into one bounded-conductor sheaf
on an \(r\)-line.

Special rational parameters can collapse to fixed characters.  The central
index \(r=(p-1)/2\), for example, gives quadratic characters and is related to
finite-field \({}_4F_3\) and a weight-four modular form.  This is a special
moving index, not a uniform description of all \(r\).

The fixed Laurent-polynomial sum (1.3) is the correct uniform geometric
replacement.  It supplies four facts:

* the defining algebraic map is fixed;
* each individual Kummer twist has bounded tame conductor;
* Deligne controls complex cancellation of each twist;
* exact divisibility by the prime above \(p\), as the twist varies through
  the entire character group, remains uncontrolled.

The fourth item is exactly what \(Z(p)\) asks for.

### 3.4. Hasse roots, \(p\)-curvature, and the recent square theorem

The factorization

\[
 H_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
 \qquad \Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1,
\]

is strong information about the **evaluation divisor** of the Hasse section.
It is not information about zero coefficients.  The repository’s example
at \(p=7\),

\[
 H_7(t)=(t-1)^2(t^2+1)^2,
 \]

while every coefficient is nonzero, already separates the two notions.
Moreover \(\deg B_p=\Theta(p)\), so even a root-count argument on \(B_p\)
has growing complexity.

Equation (1.10) is essentially the full Lucas/\(p\)-curvature algebraicity
statement.  It says that once the first block
\((b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1})\) is known, every later base-\(p\) digit block is
produced multiplicatively.  It does not constrain the support of zeros
inside that first block strongly enough to count them.  Fixed-depth Cartier
operations likewise reorganize those coefficients but do not provide
same-characteristic anti-concentration.

### 3.5. Pointwise and prime-average status

No standard theorem presently yields either

\[
 Z(p)\ll\sqrt p
\]

or

\[
 \sum_{X<p\leq2X}Z(p)\ll X^{3/2+o(1)}
\]

from the realizations above.  The prime average is not an ordinary
large-sieve-for-trace-functions problem: both the residue characteristic and
the Kummer character group vary with \(p\), and the event is divisibility at
a prime above that same \(p\).  A compatible \(\ell\)-adic system with fixed
\(\ell\) controls complex Frobenius distributions; it does not by itself
control these residual \(p\)-adic hyperplanes.

The genuinely missing input is therefore a **same-characteristic Mellin
anti-concentration theorem**, pointwise or averaged over \(p\).  For the
project’s horizontal applications one ultimately needs a joint/two-prime
version, exactly as isolated in `oracleB_result.tex`.  The recent
Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin theorem does not supply such a
statement; it determines the algebraic reduction and Galois group of the
generating series, not the coefficient-zero support of \(H_p\).

## 4. A concrete actual-state lemma for a four-zero window

The following certificate is independent of the recurrence-only projective
fiber and therefore cannot be triggered merely by a phantom return at another
state.

**Theorem 2 (cubic four-zero Mellin certificate).**
Let \(a,b,c\geq1\), put \(s=a+b+c<p\), and suppose

\[
 b_r=b_{r+a}=b_{r+a+b}=b_{r+s}=0\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Necessarily \(1\leq r\) and \(r+s\leq p-2\).  Define

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{a,b,c}(T)={}&-bc(b+c)
 +c(a+b)s\,T^a\\
 &-a(b+c)s\,T^{a+b}
 +ab(a+b)\,T^s.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Then:

1. \(Q_{a,b,c}(T)\) is divisible by \((T-1)^3\) in \(\mathbf Z[T]\), and
   the multiplicity is exactly three:
   \[
     Q_{a,b,c}^{(3)}(1)
     =abc(a+b)(b+c)s\ne0.
     \tag{4.3}
   \]
2. Every displayed coefficient is nonzero modulo \(p\), and its absolute
   value is at most \(s^3\).
3. The four actual zeros force the fixed-torus congruence
   \[
   \boxed{
    \sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
    \Lambda(x,y,z)^r
    Q_{a,b,c}(\Lambda(x,y,z))=0.}
    \tag{4.4}
   \]
   Equivalently,
   \[
   \boxed{
    \sum_{\lambda\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
    N_p(\lambda)\lambda^rQ_{a,b,c}(\lambda)=0.}
    \tag{4.5}
   \]
4. Writing \(Q_{a,b,c}=(T-1)^3R_{a,b,c}\), (4.5) becomes
   \[
    \sum_{\lambda\in\mathbf F_p^\times\setminus\{1\}}
    N_p(\lambda)\lambda^r(\lambda-1)^3
    R_{a,b,c}(\lambda)=0.
    \tag{4.6}
   \]
5. The equivalent Hasse-trace identity is
   \[
    \sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
    H_p(t)t^{-r}Q_{a,b,c}(t^{-1})=0.
    \tag{4.7}
   \]
   If \(Q_{a,b,c}^{\vee}(T)=T^sQ_{a,b,c}(T^{-1})\), then
   \((T-1)^3\mid Q_{a,b,c}^{\vee}(T)\) and
   \[
    \sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
    H_p(t)t^{-(r+s)}Q_{a,b,c}^{\vee}(t)=0.
    \tag{4.8}
   \]
6. On the symmetric gap slice \(a=c\),
   \[
    T^sQ_{a,b,a}(T^{-1})=-Q_{a,b,a}(T).
    \tag{4.9}
   \]
   Thus the certificate is anti-reciprocal on the direct reflection slice.
   If \(s\) is even, it then has the additional factor \(T+1\).  For
   \(a\ne c\), this direct anti-reciprocity is absent.

**Proof.**  Put

\[
 h_0=0,\quad h_1=a,\quad h_2=a+b,\quad h_3=s
\]

and

\[
 D=\prod_{0\leq i<j\leq3}(h_j-h_i)
  =abc(a+b)(b+c)s.
\]

The four coefficients in (4.2) are exactly

\[
 q_i=\frac{D}{\prod_{j\ne i}(h_i-h_j)}.
 \tag{4.10}
\]

For every polynomial \(f\) of degree at most two, the standard barycentric
identity gives

\[
 \sum_{i=0}^3q_if(h_i)=0.
 \tag{4.11}
\]

Taking \(f(X)=1,X,X(X-1)\) yields
\(Q(1)=Q'(1)=Q''(1)=0\), proving \((T-1)^3\mid Q\).  Taking
\(f(X)=X(X-1)(X-2)\), whose leading coefficient is \(1\), gives
\(Q'''(1)=D\), proving exact multiplicity three.

After cancellation in (4.10), each \(q_i\), up to sign, is the product of
the three pairwise differences among the other three \(h_j\)'s.  Every such
difference lies in \(1,\ldots,s<p\).  Hence all \(q_i\) are nonzero modulo
\(p\) and \(|q_i|\leq s^3\).

Now

\[
 \operatorname{CT}\bigl(\Lambda^rQ(\Lambda)\bigr)
 =\sum_{i=0}^3q_i b_{r+h_i}=0\pmod p
\]

by (4.1).  Since every exponent \(r+h_i\) is at most \(p-2\), applying
(1.1) term by term proves (4.4), and grouping by \(\lambda\) proves (4.5).
Because \(r\geq1\), the fiber \(\lambda=0\) contributes nothing.  The cubic
factor proves (4.6).  Equation (2.3) gives (4.7), and reversal gives (4.8).
Finally, when \(a=c\), the support exponents pair to \(s\) and the
coefficients pair with opposite signs, proving (4.9).  \(\square\)

### Why this is the sharp useful output, and why it still does not close the bound

Theorem 2 has three features absent from a continuant-only return certificate.

* It begins with the four values of the distinguished hypergeometric solution,
  not with an arbitrary projective fiber.
* It lives on one fixed algebraic map \(\Lambda\), so its four summands are
  four Kummer twists of a fixed geometric object.  One should view it as a
  four-sparse Mellin combination, not as a degree-\(s\) polynomial trace
  function; the number of twists is fixed.
* Its integral coefficient height is only \(O(s^3)\), and the cubic factor
  \((T-1)^3\) is exact.  On \(a=c\), anti-reciprocity exhibits the direct
  reflection component; off that slice the relation is genuinely
  non-reciprocal.

What it does **not** provide is a lower bound for the \(p\)-adic valuation or
a nonvanishing theorem for the corresponding algebraic Mellin sum.  Deligne
can bound each of the four conjugate character sums in complex absolute
value; it cannot show that their controlled linear combination is a unit at
\(\mathfrak p\).  Moreover, a single congruence such as (4.5) can have
accidental solutions, so replacing the four zero conditions by only (4.5)
and then counting its solutions would create a new phantom problem.

The clean next geometric target is therefore not a generic Weil bound.  It is
a **four-coordinate crystalline Mellin anti-concentration statement**:
for \(s\ll\sqrt p\), the four global Kummer-twist traces at
\(r,r+a,r+a+b,r+s\) cannot all lie in the prime \(\mathfrak p\), except when
the actual zero window contains the reflection-centered adjacent pair allowed
by the project’s short-window principle.  Theorem 2 supplies a cubic-
vanishing, height-controlled linear functional on that four-coordinate
vector, and separates the anti-reciprocal slice \(a=c\) from the generic
slice.  Proving the required joint \(p\)-adic nonconcentration remains new
arithmetic input; it is not contained in standard hypergeometric or
\(p\)-curvature theory.

Combined with the exact combinatorial reduction already recorded in
`AVENUES.md`, such a short-window theorem would give

\[
 Z(p)<3\eta^{-1}\sqrt p+6
\]

(up to the explicitly counted off-center exceptional windows), and hence the
desired dyadic prime-average scale.  Theorem 2 is therefore a concrete bridge
from an actual four-zero window to the fixed K3/torus geometry, but not a
claim that the bridge has already supplied the missing anti-concentration.

## 5. Direct answers to the four requested questions

### Question 1

The exact index polynomial is

\[
 \Phi_p(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}
 \frac{(-X)_k^2(X+1)_k^2}{(k!)^4},
\]

of formal degree \(4(p-1)\).  Its reduced function degree \(d_p\leq p-1\)
satisfies \(d_p\geq\log_{34}p-1\), so its Artin--Schreier conductor is
unbounded.  The exact fixed-map realization is

\[
 b_n=\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^n,
 \qquad
 b_n\equiv-\sum_{x\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}\Lambda(x)^n,
\]

and the exact Hasse/function-field realizations are

\[
 H_p(t)=\operatorname{CT}(1-t\Lambda)^{p-1},
 \qquad
 \mathscr F_p=H_p\mathscr F_p^p.
\]

Here \(\deg H_p=p-1\), \(\deg B_p=\Theta(p)\), and the algebraic extension
degree is \(\Theta(p)\).  Only the underlying rank-three K3 local system and
its individual Kummer twists have bounded geometric conductor.

### Question 2

The fixed geometric family can be taken to be the Beukers--Peters K3 pencil
(2.1), or the exact torus-period model from \(\Lambda\).  The function
\(t\mapsto H_p(t)\) is its scalar Hasse--Witt section.  The number \(b_r\) is
not the Hasse invariant or local trace at \(t=r\); it is the coefficient
\([t^r]H_p\), equivalently the \(r\)-th finite Mellin coefficient.  It is the
reduction of a global Frobenius trace of the Kummer twist indexed by \(r\).
Standard Weil bounds control the local trace and the complex Mellin sum, not
its exact reduction modulo \(p\).

### Question 3

No known standard finite-field hypergeometric, Weil--Deligne, Hasse-root, or
\(p\)-curvature theorem gives \(Z(p)=O(\sqrt p)\) or the requested prime
average.  Character-sum cancellation and zero counting are logically
different: even a bounded-conductor Kummer trace function can have all but
one Mellin coefficient exactly zero.  The direct index-polynomial method has
unbounded conductor, and the Hasse-root method counts the wrong zero locus.
The missing statement is same-characteristic \(p\)-adic Mellin
anti-concentration, with a joint/two-prime strengthening needed for the
project’s horizontal dispersion problem.

### Question 4

Theorem 2 is the sharpest unconditional new lemma obtained here.  It converts
an actual four-zero window into the fixed-family identities (4.4)--(4.8),
with an explicit four-sparse amplitude divisible by \((T-1)^3\), coefficient
height \(O(s^3)\), and exact anti-reciprocity on \(a=c\).  It is immune to the
formal projective phantom-chain counterexamples because it uses the
constant-term/Mellin realization of the distinguished Apéry solution itself.
It does not close the square-root bound without a new \(p\)-adic
nonconcentration theorem.
