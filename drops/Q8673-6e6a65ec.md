# Same-sign \(k\)-key audit: exact moment ledger and endpoint saturation

## Verdict

I audited public `main` at commit
`4783c5345b8ff4dc32ff51b204646994e0c00513`.  The relevant current sources
are `problems/3.2/proof.tex`, `problems/3.2/new_sections.tex`,
`drops/Q8584-30a61ffd.md`,
`problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_endpoint_transfer_smith_audit.py`,
`problems/3.2/ORACLE_COMM/chatgpt_q32_intermediate_cube_arithmetic.md`,
`problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_target_cubic_block_law_audit.py`, and
`problems/3.2/AVENUES.md`.

The audit gives four conclusions.

1. The proposed number
   
   \[
   k\bigl((X-3)_{k-1}+(X-4)_{k-1}\bigr)
   \]
   
   is the exact number of **sign-labelled, box-admissible** same-sign
   full-defect keys.  It is an upper bound, not an exact count, for keys whose
   arithmetic fibres are nonempty.

2. If `J_same,k` is the largest cardinality of one prime-sorted same-sign
   \(k\)-key fibre, then the exact balanced pointwise implication is
   
   \[
   \binom{\lfloor M_X/2\rfloor}{k}
   +\binom{\lceil M_X/2\rceil}{k}
   \le D_{X,k}J_{\mathrm{same},k},
   \qquad
   D_{X,k}=k\bigl((X-3)_{k-1}+(X-4)_{k-1}\bigr).
   \tag{V}
   \]
   
   For fixed \(k\), the fibre scale sufficient for
   \(M_X\log X=o(X)\) is
   
   \[
   J_{\mathrm{same},k}=o(X/\log^kX).
   \]
   
   For growing \(k\), an exponential-rate root condition is required; a bare
   little-\(o\) statement does not suffice.

3. The \(k=4\) endpoint Plücker expression is a universal rank-two identity.
   After dividing the forced prime contents, it is the identical Plücker
   expression in \((c_i,b_i)\).  The same is true for every
   block-multihomogeneous determinant, exterior contraction, or
   vertex-multihomogeneous polynomial in endpoint minors, for every finite
   \(k\), including \(k=k(X)\).

4. Recurrence-state Casoratians, fixed-gap resultants, inhomogeneous transfer
   identities, and all-order block jets lie outside that elementary endpoint
   theorem.  None yields a new estimate for the present fibre.  The first two
   become target-selective only when one characteristic vanishes at more than
   one anchor.  Fixed-depth normalized block jets are locally triangular and
   introduce one fresh divided coordinate per grade.  The all-order object
   escapes only at full residue-block scale, where it reconstructs the Lucas
   block rather than producing a new bounded-height carrier.

Thus \(k=4\) and slowly growing \(k\) provide a useful exact consumer ledger,
but no new unconditional arithmetic estimate follows from the deterministic
later-prime filters or from endpoint determinant geometry.

## 1. Exact same-sign normal form

For a prime-sorted same-sign tuple write

\[
X<p_1<\cdots<p_k\le2X,
\qquad d_1=0,
\qquad d_i=z_i-z_1.
\]

Put

\[
t=z_1,
\qquad N=m-z_1.
\]

Then

\[
z_i=t+d_i,
\qquad
A_i:=m-z_i=N-d_i=c_ip_i.
\tag{1.1}
\]

On the positive branch,

\[
u_i=t+d_i,
\qquad q_i=c_i,
\qquad 2\le z_i\le X-1,
\qquad 1\le c_i\le X-2.
\tag{1.2+}
\]

On the negative branch,

\[
u_i=-t-d_i-1,
\qquad q_i=c_i-1,
\qquad -(X-1)\le z_i\le-3,
\qquad 2\le c_i\le X-1.
\tag{1.2-}
\]

The target-regular masks are

\[
p_i\mid B_{u_i},
\qquad
p_i\nmid B_{q_i}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Write \(B_{u_i}=p_ib_i\).  The endpoint pair is

\[
v_i=(A_i,B_{u_i})=p_i(c_i,b_i).
\tag{1.4}
\]

### Determinism from the first two products

For \(i=1,2\), equation (1.1) gives

\[
c_1p_1-c_2p_2=d_2.
\]

Since \(d_2\ne0\) and \(|d_2|<X<p_2\), reduction modulo \(p_2\) yields

\[
\boxed{c_1\equiv d_2p_1^{-1}\pmod{p_2}.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

Each legal interval for \(c_1\) has length \(X-2<p_2\), so (1.5) has at
most one legal representative.  If it exists, \(N=c_1p_1\) is fixed.
For every later coordinate,

\[
A_i=N-d_i.
\]

The row and offset boxes give

\[
0<A_i\le X^2+X-2.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Two distinct shell primes have product at least
\((X+1)(X+2)>X^2+X-2\), and the square of one shell prime is also larger
than the right side of (1.6).  Hence \(A_i\) has at most one shell-prime
divisor.  If it exists, it determines \(p_i\) and \(c_i=A_i/p_i\), after
which the prime ordering, coefficient box, and quotient-nonvanishing tests
are deterministic.

This proves that the omitted coordinates are determined by
\((t,p_1,p_2)\).  It does **not** bound the number of triples
\((t,p_1,p_2)\) satisfying all zero masks, nor does it inject the fibre into
any smaller set.  Computational singleton observations do not prove such an
injection.

The direct projection gives only

\[
J_X(\kappa)
\le |I_{\operatorname{sgn}\kappa}|
   \binom{\pi(2X)-\pi(X)}2
\ll X^3/\log^2X,
\tag{1.7}
\]

uniformly in \(k\).  Even granting the current pointwise bound
\(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\), the factor \(X\) can only be replaced here by
\(O(X^{2/3})\); this still provides no quantified gain from an additional
anchor.

## 2. Exact key count

Let \(I\) be an interval of \(L\) consecutive integers.  Take ordered
\(k\)-tuples of distinct elements \((z_1,\ldots,z_k)\in I^k\), and retain
only

\[
(d_1,\ldots,d_k)=(0,z_2-z_1,\ldots,z_k-z_1).
\]

### Lemma

The number of ordered difference keys is

\[
\boxed{k(L-1)_{k-1}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Here \((a)_r=a(a-1)\cdots(a-r+1)\), with value \(0\) when \(r>a\).

### Proof

Translate a representative until its minimum is \(0\).  Distinctness makes
the minimum label unique.  Choose that label in \(k\) ways, and assign the
other \(k-1\) labelled coordinates injectively to
\(\{1,\ldots,L-1\}\), giving \((L-1)_{k-1}\) possibilities.  Conversely,
the normalized tuple recovers the difference vector by subtracting its first
coordinate.

For positive offsets \(L_+=X-2\).  For negative offsets \(L_-=X-3\).
Keeping the sign as part of the key gives

\[
\boxed{
D_{X,k}=k\bigl((X-3)_{k-1}+(X-4)_{k-1}\bigr).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is exact for the box-admissible key universe.  Individual folding
bounds relative to the \(p_i\), prime availability, and the Apéry masks can
empty a key.  Hence the realized key count is only bounded above by (2.2).

For later comparison,

\[
D_{X,3}=6(X-4)^2,
\qquad
D_{X,4}=4(X-4)(X-5)(2X-9).
\tag{2.3}
\]

## 3. Exact balanced \(k\)-moment implication

Let \(R_+(m)\) and \(R_-(m)\) be the two same-sign loads, and put

\[
R(m)=R_+(m)+R_-(m),
\qquad
M_X=\max_{X\le m<X^2}R(m).
\]

For a sign-labelled key \(\kappa\), let \(J_X(\kappa)\) be its total number
of prime-sorted \(k\)-tuples over all rows, and define

\[
J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X)=\max_\kappa J_X(\kappa).
\]

Every prime-sorted same-sign \(k\)-subset on a row has one and only one key.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X\le m<X^2}
 \left[
  \binom{R_+(m)}k+\binom{R_-(m)}k
 \right]
 =\sum_\kappa J_X(\kappa).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\sum_m
 \left[
  \binom{R_+(m)}k+\binom{R_-(m)}k
 \right]
\le D_{X,k}J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X).
\tag{3.2}
\]

For integers \(a+b=R\), the function

\[
f(a)=\binom ak+\binom{R-a}k
\]

is minimized at the balanced split.  Indeed,

\[
f(a+1)-f(a)
 =\binom a{k-1}-\binom{R-a-1}{k-1},
\]

which changes sign at the midpoint.  Thus

\[
\binom ak+\binom bk
\ge
\binom{\lfloor R/2\rfloor}k
+
\binom{\lceil R/2\rceil}k.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Apply this at a row attaining \(M_X\) and use (3.2):

\[
\boxed{
\binom{\lfloor M_X/2\rfloor}{k}
+
\binom{\lceil M_X/2\rceil}{k}
\le D_{X,k}J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
(\lfloor M_X/2\rfloor)_k
+(\lceil M_X/2\rceil)_k
\le k!D_{X,k}J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X).
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the exact balanced \(k\)-moment pointwise implication.

### Explicit inversion

Let \(r=\lfloor M_X/2\rfloor\).  The left side of (3.4) is at least
\(2\binom rk\).  If \(r<k\), then \(M_X\le2k-1\).  Otherwise

\[
\binom rk\ge\frac{(r-k+1)^k}{k!}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
M_X\le2k-1+
2\left(\frac{k!D_{X,k}J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X)}2\right)^{1/k}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

## 4. The sufficient fibre condition, including growing \(k\)

For \(k=k(X)\), equation (3.6) gives \(M_X\log X=o(X)\) under

\[
\boxed{k(X)\log X=o(X)}
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\left(k!D_{X,k}J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X)\right)^{1/k}
\frac{\log X}{X}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The exact logarithmic form is

\[
\frac1k\left(
 \log(k!D_{X,k}J_{\mathrm{same},k})
 -k\log\frac{X}{\log X}
\right)\longrightarrow-\infty.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This rate issue matters when \(k\) grows: the assertion
\(k!D_{X,k}J=o((X/\log X)^k)\) by itself is not enough unless its ratio has
an exponentially small \(k\)-th root.

A concrete sufficient form is that there exists \(\omega_X\to\infty\) with

\[
J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X)
\le
\frac{2X^k}
{k!D_{X,k}(\omega_X\log X)^k}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since \(D_{X,k}\le2kX^{k-1}\), the stronger simpler condition

\[
J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X)
\le
\frac{X}
{k\,k!(\omega_X\log X)^k}
\tag{4.5}
\]

also suffices.

For fixed \(k\),

\[
D_{X,k}=(2k+o(1))X^{k-1},
\]

and (4.2) is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
J_{\mathrm{same},k}(X)=o(X/\log^kX).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus \(k=4\) requires \(J_{\mathrm{same},4}=o(X/\log^4X)\).  This is a
stricter scale than the \(k=3\) requirement.  Projection of an extended key
to a shorter key gives only monotonicity of fibre cardinality; it does not
supply the extra factor \(1/\log X\) required at each increment of \(k\).

For \(k=o(\sqrt X)\),

\[
D_{X,k}=2kX^{k-1}\exp(O(k^2/X)),
\tag{4.7}
\]

so slowly growing \(k\) creates no hidden combinatorial saving in the key
count.  If a uniform singleton theorem were proved, a fixed small \(k\)
would already satisfy (4.6).  Finite singleton data therefore cannot be
promoted into a reason to pass to \(k=4\) or growing \(k\).

## 5. General endpoint-content saturation

### Theorem (all-\(k\) block-multihomogeneous saturation)

Let \(R\) be a commutative domain containing the fixed key and row
parameters.  For each \(i\), let

\[
w_i=(x_i,y_i),
\qquad
v_i=r_iw_i,
\]

where \(r_i\ne0\).  Let

\[
F(V_1,\ldots,V_k)
\in R[V_{10},V_{11},\ldots,V_{k0},V_{k1}]
\]

be multihomogeneous of degree \(e_i\) in the \(i\)-th coordinate pair.
Then

\[
\boxed{
F(v_1,\ldots,v_k)
 =\left(\prod_{i=1}^kr_i^{e_i}\right)
  F(w_1,\ldots,w_k).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

After localization at \(r_1\cdots r_k\), an ideal generated by such
carriers becomes exactly the ideal generated by their primitive versions
\(F(w_1,\ldots,w_k)\).  This holds for every finite \(k\), uniformly when
\(k=k(X)\).

### Proof

Use the \(\mathbb Z^k\)-grading in which both coordinates of block \(i\)
have degree one in component \(i\).  Every monomial of \(F\) uses exactly
\(e_i\) coordinates from block \(i\), so (5.1) follows term by term.
After the \(r_i\) are inverted, the substitution \(V_i=r_iw_i\) is an
automorphism of the Laurent polynomial ring, and the displayed content
monomials are units.

Apply this theorem with

\[
r_i=p_i,
\qquad
w_i=(c_i,b_i),
\qquad
v_i=(A_i,B_{u_i}).
\]

The product equations \(A_i=N-d_i=p_ic_i\) may be included in the
coefficient ring; they do not affect the factorization.

### Endpoint minors and Plücker relations

Define

\[
E_{ij}=A_jB_{u_i}-A_iB_{u_j},
\qquad
e_{ij}=c_jb_i-c_ib_j.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{E_{ij}=p_ip_je_{ij}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

At \(k=4\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
&E_{12}E_{34}-E_{13}E_{24}+E_{14}E_{23}\\
&\quad=p_1p_2p_3p_4
 \left(e_{12}e_{34}-e_{13}e_{24}+e_{14}e_{23}\right)=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The primitive bracket is the identical universal Plücker relation for the
\(2\times4\) matrix with columns \((c_i,b_i)^t\).  It is not a nonzero
integer carrying four target primes.

For arbitrary \(k\), introduce minor variables \(Z_{ij}\).  The kernel of

\[
Z_{ij}\longmapsto x_jy_i-x_iy_j
\]

is the Plücker ideal of \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,k)\), generated over
\(\mathbb Z\) by

\[
Z_{ij}Z_{\ell r}-Z_{i\ell}Z_{jr}+Z_{ir}Z_{j\ell}
\qquad(i<j<\ell<r).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus every universal polynomial relation among the \(2\times2\) endpoint
minors is generated by the four-index rank-two quadrics.  If a polynomial
in the minors is multihomogeneous in the vertex grading, it factors the
prescribed power of each \(p_i\), and its primitive part is the identical
polynomial in the \(e_{ij}\).  Increasing \(k\) only supplies more universal
rank-two relations.

If the primitive polynomial is not a universal relation, it remains a
polynomial in \(c_i\) and the uncontrolled quotients
\(b_i=B_{u_i}/p_i\).  The full-defect key gives no short-height bound for
those quotients.

### Scope and the necessary counterexample to an overbroad claim

The theorem covers determinants whose \(i\)-th endpoint row or column is
homogeneous of a fixed degree in \(v_i\), tensor or exterior contractions
with a fixed number of occurrences of each endpoint, vertex-balanced
polynomials in the minors, and standard multihomogeneous resultants or Chow
forms.

It does **not** cover every polynomial in endpoint coordinates.  For
example,

\[
A_i-A_j=d_j-d_i
\]

is inhomogeneous in the two endpoint blocks and survives content removal;
it is precisely part of the frozen product geometry.  A determinant with
rows \((1,A_i,B_{u_i})\) is likewise not block-homogeneous.  Therefore a
saturation theorem for arbitrary endpoint expressions would be false.

Valuations, gcds, division by a coordinate, identities tied to the fixed
global initial state, and all-order transfer objects also lie outside the
theorem.  These exclusions are essential.

## 6. Recurrence-compatible carriers that escape the endpoint theorem

There are exact escapes, but none yields the required fibre estimate.

### 6.1 Cleared recurrence states

Set

\[
Y_n=(n!)^3B_n.
\]

Then

\[
Y_{n+1}=P(n)Y_n-n^6Y_{n-1},
\qquad
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For \(u_i<p_i\), the factorial is a \(p_i\)-unit, so
\(p_i\mid Y_{u_i}\) exactly when \(p_i\mid B_{u_i}\).  The
no-consecutive-zero theorem makes \(Y_{u_i+1}\) a unit modulo \(p_i\).
Consider

\[
s_i=(Y_{u_i},Y_{u_i+1})
\]

and

\[
C_{ij}=Y_{u_i}Y_{u_j+1}-Y_{u_i+1}Y_{u_j}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This state pair is not wholly divisible by \(p_i\), so it escapes Section 5.
But modulo \(p_i\),

\[
\boxed{C_{ij}\equiv-Y_{u_i+1}Y_{u_j}\pmod{p_i}.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Hence \(p_i\mid C_{ij}\) precisely when the same characteristic also
vanishes at \(u_j\).  In that subcase the fixed-gap polynomial applies.  If
one characteristic sees three ordered anchor zeros, the nonzero fixed-gap
resultant in `meso_result.tex` and `near_bridge_energy.tex` supplies its
usual logarithmic charge.

The present fibre does not force any such cross-zero.  On the subcase in
which every cross evaluation is a unit, all relevant \(C_{ij}\) are units
at the defining characteristics.  More anchors do not alter (6.3), and
Plücker identities among the recurrence states are again universal.

### 6.2 Inhomogeneous transfer identities

For fixed parallel offsets, transfer composition gives linear identities
among three or more values \(B_{t+d_i}\).  After writing
\(B_{u_i}=p_ib_i\), they have the form

\[
\sum_i\alpha_i(t,d)p_ib_i=0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The terms have different endpoint multidegrees, so (6.4) escapes Section 5.
It is nevertheless an identity for every recurrence solution, rather than a
relation selected by the target zeros.  The quotients \(b_i\) remain, and
(6.4) is not a nonzero short-height integer divisible by
\(p_1\cdots p_k\).  No current Smith or resultant theorem eliminates these
quotients across the distinct characteristics.

### 6.3 Fixed-depth normalized block jets

`q32_target_cubic_block_law_audit.py` verifies the cubic target block
formulas using

\[
T_u(X)=B_u+XG_u+X^2H_u+X^3K_u
\]

and the companion solution.  These jet coordinates are not all multiplied
by the defining prime, so they escape the elementary row-content theorem.
They still do not yield a cross-prime equation.

At a target digit, \(B_u=pb\) exposes \(b\) as a divided local coordinate.
After transfer denominators and adjacent values are inverted, the next block
equation is triangular and solves for the new divided coordinate.  Passing
from \(p^r\) to \(p^{r+1}\) adds one equation and one new local coordinate.
CRT keeps these local coordinates independent across the distinct primes.
This is the finite-depth local/transfer/CRT saturation recorded in
`chatgpt_q32_intermediate_cube_arithmetic.md`.

Equivalently, normalized jet recurrences exist for every base index.  A
determinant vanishing solely from those recurrences is target-blind; a
determinant retaining the divided coordinates has not eliminated the local
freedom.  Increasing \(k\) does not change this grade-by-grade balance.

### 6.4 All-order block data

An all-order block jet, or a transfer through the complete residue block, is
outside every fixed-depth theorem above.  At full block scale, however, the
Lucas law is

\[
B_{qp+r}\equiv B_qB_r\pmod p.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The complete block therefore records exactly which base coefficients
\(B_r\) vanish.  A finite-depth tower propagates that base information but
places no restriction on it.  Eliminating every fresh jet coordinate
requires an object whose order or dimension grows to the full residue scale.
No bounded-height all-order determinant, Casoratian, or bounded-complexity
moving-index carrier is proved in the repository.  Without such a
compression, the all-order object returns the original base-zero problem
rather than estimating \(J_{\mathrm{same},k}\).

A genuinely global identity coupling different defining characteristics
before CRT would also escape the theorem, but no such carrier is currently
present.

## 7. Does \(k=4\) or slowly growing \(k\) give a genuine new estimate?

No.

- The first two products reduce reconstruction to \((t,p_1,p_2)\), but the
  number of passing triples remains an uncontrolled intersection of
  distinct-characteristic zero masks.
- Every later product is a deterministic yes/no filter.  No current theorem
  gives a quantitative saving for that filter, so the elementary bound is
  independent of \(k\).
- The \(k=4\) endpoint Plücker expression is identically zero and has no
  nonzero primitive carrier.
- All higher endpoint-minor relations are generated by the same universal
  Plücker quadrics.
- Cleared recurrence states and fixed-gap resultants require an extra zero in
  one defining characteristic; the fibre does not force it.
- Fixed-depth block jets introduce as many local coordinates as equations.
- Growing \(k\) changes the consumer according to (4.2), but does not create
  the arithmetic decay required there.  For fixed \(k\), each added anchor
  raises the required logarithmic power in (4.6).

A valid improvement would have to prove a new theorem bounding the number of
\((t,p_1,p_2)\) for which all deterministic shifted products have legal
shell factors and all distinct-characteristic Apéry zero masks hold.  None
of the endpoint determinant, Plücker, finite-transfer, or finite-jet
identities proves that theorem.

## Final answers

### (1) Exact key count

Yes, for the box-admissible sign-labelled key universe:

\[
\boxed{D_{X,k}=k\bigl((X-3)_{k-1}+(X-4)_{k-1}\bigr).}
\]

For arithmetically nonempty fibres it is only an upper bound.

### (2) Exact pointwise implication

The exact implication is (3.4), with explicit inversion (3.6).  For fixed
\(k\), the sufficient fibre scale is

\[
\boxed{J_{\mathrm{same},k}=o(X/\log^kX).}
\]

For growing \(k\), sufficient conditions are (4.1) and (4.2), or the
concrete exponential-rate condition (4.4).

### (3) Saturation theorem

Section 5 proves saturation for every block-multihomogeneous endpoint
carrier and every vertex-multihomogeneous determinant/Plücker carrier,
uniformly for fixed or growing finite \(k\).  At \(k=4\), primitive
saturation leaves the identical universal Plücker zero in \((c_i,b_i)\).
A theorem covering arbitrary inhomogeneous endpoint expressions is false;
the precise scope is stated after (5.4).

### (4) Non-saturated recurrence carriers

Cleared recurrence-state Casoratians and fixed-gap resultants escape the
endpoint theorem but detect only an additional zero in one characteristic.
Inhomogeneous transfer identities escape but retain the divided values.
Fixed-depth normalized block jets escape simple row-content factorization
but are locally triangular and target-blind.  The all-order block jet escapes
the finite-depth no-go only at full Lucas scale, where it reconstructs the
original residue block and has no known bounded-height compression.  Hence
no known escape gives a new estimate for the same-sign \(k\)-fibre.
