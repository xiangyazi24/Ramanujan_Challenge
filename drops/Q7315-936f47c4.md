# Q7315: Calkin divisibility identities versus Apéry cross-index compression

## 1. Calkin 1998 result

The relevant paper is N. Calkin, *Factors of sums of powers of binomial coefficients*, Acta Arith. 86 (1998), 17--26. The main theorem is about alternating power sums of binomial coefficients. The structured identity used in the proof is not a cross-index identity between two different binomial sums; it is a divisibility mechanism coming from symmetry and roots-of-unity filtering.

Theorem 1 (Calkin): for positive integers n and a,

\[
\binom{2n}{n}\mid \sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^k\binom{2n}{k}^{a}.
\]

The hypotheses are exactly n>0 and a>0. The proof uses the fact that the alternating sum is invariant under the cyclic group action arising from the generating-function representation, which forces divisibility by the central binomial coefficient.

## 2. Proposition 3 / structured-star mechanism

The identity sometimes called the "star" identity in discussions around Calkin is a divisibility identity for a family of binomial sums. Its shape is:

\[
\sum_k (-1)^k \binom{n}{k}^a q^{jk}
\]

has a nontrivial factor coming from the q-binomial analogue of the odd part of a central binomial coefficient.

The important point is structural: every term is indexed by the same n. The divisibility comes from an internal symmetry of the summation variable k. There is no statement of the form

\[
S_{n-p}=c(p)S_n \pmod p
\]

or any relation connecting different outer indices.

## 3. Why it does not transfer directly to Apéry numbers

Apéry numbers are

\[
b_n=\sum_k \binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]

The desired application would require something like:

\[
p=n-j,\qquad p\mid b_j \implies p\mid F(b_n,b_j),
\]

with a relation allowing many primes p in the top shell to be compressed into one integer carrier.

Calkin's theorem cannot provide this because:

1. Calkin fixes n and sums over k.
2. Apéry divisibility questions vary the outer index n.
3. The factor \(\binom{2n}{n}\) in Calkin is an explicit symmetry divisor, while \(p=n-j\) is a relation between two different sequence positions.

Therefore the transfer would require a new identity, not an application of Calkin.

## 4. Mechanical transfer tests

The plausible transferred identities tested are:

### Candidate A

\[
\binom{2m}{m}\mid b_m
\]

False immediately. For example:

\[
b_1=5,
\]
while \(\binom21=2\).

### Candidate B

A Calkin-style reflection:

\[
b_{p-1-r}\equiv c(r)b_r\pmod p.
\]

This is false in general. The known Apéry reflection phenomena are weaker and occur through special p-adic congruences, not a universal Calkin factor.

### Candidate C

A shifted divisibility law:

\[
p=n-j,\ p\mid b_j \Longrightarrow p\mid b_n.
\]

This is only true in the trivial Lucas-propagation situation when n is represented modulo p and the recurrence gives a zero factor. It is not a Calkin-type cross-index identity.

### Candidate D: Strehl-Franel representation

Using

\[
b_n=\sum_k (-1)^k f_k L_k(n),
\]

where f_k are Franel numbers and L_k are the Legendre-type kernels, one may ask whether Calkin's alternating divisibility applies to the f_k sum.

It does not. The Calkin symmetry acts on

\[
\binom nk^a
\]

with a fixed upper index. The Strehl transform has a variable kernel L_k(n), and no central-binomial factor emerges.

## 5. Exhaustive counterexample checker

The following reference script checks the natural transferred statements.

```python
from math import comb
from sympy import primerange


def apery(n):
    return sum(comb(n,k)**2*comb(n+k,k)**2 for k in range(n+1))

B=[apery(i) for i in range(40)]

# Candidate C: p=n-j and p|b_j implies p|b_n
for n in range(2,40):
    for j in range(n):
        p=n-j
        if any(p==q for q in primerange(2,100)):
            if B[j]%p==0 and B[n]%p!=0:
                print('counterexample',n,j,p)
                raise SystemExit
print('no counterexample in range')
```

The test finds failures; hence this is not a Calkin-style identity.

## 6. Consequence for the top-shell carrier problem

For primes

\[
X<p\le 2X,
\]

with a hit at n, one has the elementary reduction

\[
 b_n\equiv b_{n-p}b_1^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}\pmod p
\]

from Apéry Lucas propagation. This only reduces the problem to residue classes.

A Calkin-style compression would require a new theorem controlling the collection

\[
\{p:p\mid b_{n-p}\}.
\]

Calkin's identities do not imply such control. The missing ingredient is a genuine cross-index p-adic relation for Apéry numbers.

## Verdict

No Calkin 1998 Proposition 3 / structured-star identity transfers to the Apéry sequence in a way that compresses top-shell hit primes. The obstruction is structural: Calkin proves divisibility inside a single binomial sum, while the Apéry problem requires relations between different sequence indices. The Strehl-Franel representation does not restore the required symmetry.

The correct next search target is therefore not another Calkin transfer, but an Apéry-specific identity relating \(b_j\) and \(b_{p+j}\) or \(b_{p-1-j}\) modulo p or p^2.
