# 10. Strongest unconditional all-three-condition scalar partial

Although scalar height cannot close the problem, there is a precise joint divisibility statement worth recording because it shows exactly what characteristic-zero descent gives for free.

For one actual leaf, define the folded lower indices

\[
j_{6,p}:=\min\{\rho_x(p),p-1-\rho_x(p)\},
\]

\[
j_{\sigma,p}:=\min\{\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p),p-1-\alpha_{\sigma,x}(p)\}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Apéry reflection gives

\[
p\mid b_{j_{6,p}},b_{j_{\sigma,p}}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

For a finite set \(S\) of distinct actual lower primes in one selected state and sign, put

\[
G_p:=\gcd\bigl(b_{j_{6,p}},b_{j_{\sigma,p}}\bigr),
\]

and define the joint folded carrier

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_\sigma(x;S)
:=
 b_t\cdot\operatorname{lcm}_{p\in S}G_p.
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

## Lemma 10.1 — joint folded radical divisibility

For every such \(S\),

\[
\boxed{
q\prod_{p\in S}p
\mid
\mathcal H_\sigma(x;S).
}
\tag{10.4}
\]

Moreover, with

\[
m_p:=\min\{j_{6,p},j_{\sigma,p}\},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal H_\sigma(x;S)
\le
\log b_t+\sum_{p\in S}\log b_{m_p}.
}
\tag{10.5}
\]

### Proof

The upper mark gives \(q\mid b_t\). Equation `(10.2)` gives \(p\mid G_p\), hence every distinct \(p\in S\) divides the lcm in `(10.3)`. Since every legal lower prime is smaller than \(q\), all displayed primes are distinct, proving `(10.4)`.

Also

\[
\operatorname{lcm}_{p\in S}G_p
\le\prod_{p\in S}G_p
\le\prod_{p\in S}b_{m_p},
\]

which proves `(10.5)`. \(\square\)

This is a genuine single integer containing the upper mark and both lower zero conditions. It evades the orthogonal-idempotent obstruction by lifting everything to characteristic zero. But it immediately encounters the scalar-height wall.

On the deep core,

\[
m_p>J_*=Y^{1/6}.
\]

The automatic Apéry-size bound supplied by `(10.5)` has a per-prime scale at least compatible with

\[
\log b_{m_p}\asymp m_p\ge Y^{1/6}\gg\log T.
\]

Thus the only uniform product bound available from `(10.5)` is far too large compared with the \(\log T\) contributed by one shell prime. Any useful improvement would have to prove substantial common lcm compression; that is another form of the growing-module problem. I do not use `(10.5)` to infer a tail.

## 10.2 Exact raw-M5 corollary

For an actual selected minus M5 occurrence, put

\[
d:=q-p,
\qquad
a:=d-t-1,
\qquad r:=6d+t.
\]

M5 is unreflected in both rows and has \(r>a\). Hence

\[
q p
\mid
b_t\gcd(b_a,b_r).
\tag{10.6}
\]

Since

\[
t+a=d-1,
\]

and

\[
\log b_n=\gamma n+O(\log(n+2)),
\qquad
\gamma=\log(17+12\sqrt2),
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\log q+\log p
\le
\gamma(d-1)+O_J(\log(d+2)).
}
\tag{10.7}
\]

In particular, for every fixed \(\eta>0\), all sufficiently large actual M5 leaves satisfy

\[
\boxed{
d\ge\left(\frac2\gamma-\eta\right)\log T.}
\tag{10.8}
\]

This is an unconditional joint three-characteristic exclusion for a restricted sign/type/range. It does not help DPRM: a deep M5 leaf already has

Also, the numerical strength of `(10.7)` is obtained after bounding the gcd by its smaller endpoint, so it does not extract an additional independent saving from the primary lower zero. I therefore do not present it as clearing the requested joint bar.

The corrected minus height law is respected throughout: no uniform \(\Theta(T)\) claim is made on M5. The only uniform deep statement used is the banked lower scale \(Y^{1/6}\gg\log T\).
