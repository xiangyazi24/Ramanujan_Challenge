# P3.2 — shifted cross-prime Apéry zero-set collisions: literature audit

## Verdict

**I do not know of any Katz-type theorem in the literature that gives the required cross-*base-characteristic* correlation for the Apéry coefficient zero sets, and the strongest nearby results do not imply it.** As of August 2026, the exact P3.2 estimate remains a genuinely new horizontal/CRT shrinking-target problem.

There are three different notions of “independence” that look close but must not be conflated:

1. **Katz / Deligne / Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel:** correlations of trace functions while the base field is one fixed `F_p`.
2. **Compatible-system large sieve:** independence as the *auxiliary coefficient prime* `lambda` varies, again with the base characteristic fixed.
3. **CRT equidistribution:** arithmetic mixing of arbitrary local subsets modulo distinct prime factors of a composite modulus.

Your collision belongs to (3), not (1) or (2). The closest general theorem is Emmanuel Kowalski–Kannan Soundararajan, **“Equidistribution from the Chinese Remainder Theorem”**, Adv. Math. 385 (2021), 107776. But its fixed-two-prime-factor theorem requires the local sets to become large; the Apéry model predicts `|Z_p|=O(1)`. For bounded local sets their theorem obtains equidistribution only when the number of prime factors tends to infinity. Moreover, ordinary discrepancy equidistribution is still too coarse for our interval, whose normalized width is about `1/p`.

So **Sp4 monodromy does not presently give the missing theorem**, even if one grants the relevant Sp4 realization of the Apéry motive. Rankin–Selberg does not encode this event, and the ordinary/trace-function large sieve hits the wrong modulus/length scale.

There are also two important corrections to the random-model calculation in the question:

- A pointwise statement `C(p,q)=O(1/q)` cannot literally hold for an integer-valued `C(p,q)` unless almost all pairs have `C(p,q)=0`; `1/q` is an **average expectation**, not a pointwise bound.
- Summing that expectation over all admissible prime pairs does **not** give `O(log log N)`. There are about `p/log p` possible partner primes in the fixed-ratio interval. The correct bounded-zero-set random model predicts off-diagonal size

\[
\asymp \frac{N}{(\log N)^2},
\]

not `O(log log N)`. This is still more than enough: if the diagonal/first moment is `O(N/log N)`, then

\[
E_2(N)=E_1(N)+O\!\left(\frac{N}{(\log N)^2}\right)
       =O\!\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right).
\]

The clean research target is therefore an **averaged two-prime shrinking-target CRT decorrelation theorem**, not a pointwise Katz bound.

# 1. Exact reformulation as a CRT shrinking-target count

Write

\[
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

For `p<q`, a common q=6 integer has

\[
n=6p+r=6q+s,
\qquad r\in Z_p,\ s\in Z_q.
\]

Hence

\[
r-s=6(q-p).
\]

Put

\[
d=6(q-p),\qquad L_{p,q}=7p-6q.
\]

Then `C(p,q)=0` unless `q<7p/6`, and, away from the terminal `N` cutoff,

\[
C(p,q)
=\#\{0\le s<L_{p,q}:s\in Z_q,\ s+d\in Z_p\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This has an exact CRT form. Define

\[
A_{pq}=\{a\bmod pq:a\bmod p\in Z_p,\ a\bmod q\in Z_q\}.
\]

Since a collision integer `n` satisfies

\[
n\bmod p=r\in Z_p,
\qquad
n\bmod q=s\in Z_q,
\]

and the simultaneous q=6 window is

\[
I_{p,q}=[6q,7p),
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{C(p,q)=\#(A_{pq}\cap I_{p,q}).}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Here

\[
|A_{pq}|=|Z_p||Z_q|,
\qquad
|I_{p,q}|=L_{p,q}=7p-6q.
\]

If the CRT points were uniformly distributed modulo `pq`, the natural main term would be

\[
\boxed{
\frac{L_{p,q}}{pq}|Z_p||Z_q|.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

For `p\asymp q\asymp X` and bounded `|Z_p|,|Z_q|`, this is `asymp 1/X`. This is the precise sense in which the random model gives “about `1/q` per pair”.

But note the scale: the ambient modulus is `pq\asymp X^2`, while the interval has length `L\ll X=\sqrt{pq}`. Thus (1.2) is a **shrinking-target problem exactly at or below the square-root-of-modulus scale**.

That is already outside what plain CRT equidistribution gives.

# 2. Correction to the global random-model scale

Let

\[
\mathcal C_6(N)
=\sum_{p<q} C_{p,q}(N)
\]

be the half-off-diagonal collision count, so that the square moment satisfies

\[
E_2(N)=E_1(N)+2\mathcal C_6(N).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The exact terminal overlap length is

\[
L_{p,q}(N)=\max\{0,\min(N+1,7p)-6q\}.
\]

Under the independent random-subset model with prescribed cardinalities

\[
z_p=|Z_p|,
\]

one gets exactly at the level of expectation

\[
\mathbf E\,C_{p,q}(N)
=rac{L_{p,q}(N)z_pz_q}{pq}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

If, for scale estimation, `z_p=1`, then the project’s exact q=6 collision calculation gives

\[
\mathbf E\,\mathcal C_6(N)
\sim
\frac12\log^2(7/6)\,
\frac{N}{(\log N)^2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the *ordered* off-diagonal contribution to `E_2` is predicted to be

\[
2\mathbf E\,\mathcal C_6(N)
\sim
\log^2(7/6)\,
\frac{N}{(\log N)^2}.
\]

The reason the proposed `sum 1/q = O(log log N)` misses the scale is that it effectively counts only one partner `p` for each `q`. In reality the allowed interval

\[
\frac67q<p<q
\]

contains `asymp q/log q` primes. A hit probability of order `1/q` per partner therefore gives an expected number of hits of order `1/log q` for each outer prime, and summing over primes gives `N/log^2 N`.

This correction is useful rather than damaging: the random prediction is still smaller by a full logarithm than the desired `O(N/log N)` second-moment scale.

# 3. The first conceptual mismatch: `Z_p` is not the zero set of a fixed trace function in its geometric variable

For the classical zeta(3) Apéry numbers used in P3.2,

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
={}_4F_3\!\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-n,n+1,n+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

So, literally, this is a terminating `4F3` whose parameters depend on the coefficient index `n`; it is not a single bounded-conductor finite-field hypergeometric trace function `x -> t_p(x)` evaluated at `x=n`.

There are several geometric/hypergeometric realizations of the Apéry motive and related finite-field hypergeometric values. If by “Sp4 monodromy of the Apéry family” one means one of those rank-four realizations, grant it for the sake of argument. The problem remains:

> Monodromy acts on a geometric family parameter over one finite field. Our set `Z_p` is indexed by the **coefficient number** `0<=n<p`, and then the defining characteristic itself changes from `p` to `q`.

That distinction is decisive.

The classical generating function also has the well-known K3/Picard–Fuchs description; its third-order differential equation is related to a symmetric-square structure. Thus one should not identify “the coefficient zero set” with “the zero set of an Sp4 trace function” without an additional theorem constructing exactly that bounded-conductor coefficient-index trace realization.

More concretely, the current project’s Mellin organization expresses `b_r mod p` linearly through a fixed geometric object, but the exact zero detector introduces

```text
e_p(u b_r).
```

Applying an additive character to the **value of a Mellin coefficient** is a nonlinear second Fourier layer. A bounded-complexity formula for `b_r` does not automatically make

```text
r |--> e_p(u b_r)
```

a bounded-conductor trace function uniformly in `p`.

This is why even the one-characteristic exact-zero problem is much subtler than ordinary Sato–Tate equidistribution.

# 4. What Katz/FKM actually gives: very strong, but in one characteristic

The closest “Katz-type correlation theorem” is the Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel sums-of-products theorem:

> É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, Ph. Michel, **A study in sums of products**, Philos. Trans. Roy. Soc. A 373 (2015), 20140309.  
> DOI: https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2014.0309  
> arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.2293

For a bounded-conductor trace function `K` on `F_p`, and outside explicit diagonal configurations, the Goursat–Kolchin–Ribet independence criterion plus Deligne gives square-root cancellation in sums schematically of the form

```text
sum_{x in F_p}
    K_1(gamma_1 x) ... K_m(gamma_m x) e_p(hx)
      << sqrt(p).
```

This is exactly the theorem one would try for a *same-characteristic* shifted correlation.

But all tensor factors live on the same `A^1/F_p`. For `p!=q`, there is no common finite-field fiber carrying one factor in characteristic `p` and one in characteristic `q`. In particular,

```text
Spec(F_p) x_{Spec Z} Spec(F_q) = empty.
```

Passing to `Z/pqZ` does not create mixed monodromy: by CRT,

```text
Spec(Z/pqZ) = Spec(F_p) disjoint_union Spec(F_q).
```

A sheaf there is just separate data on the two components. Complete exponential sums factor into local sums; there is no new Deligne Riemann-hypothesis statement coupling them.

The newest broad bilinear trace-function result makes the same limitation clear:

> É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, Ph. Michel, W. Sawin, **Bilinear forms with trace functions**, arXiv:2511.09459 (2025).  
> https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459

It can beat the Pólya–Vinogradov range for very general trace functions under robust monodromy hypotheses, but still for **one fixed prime modulus/finite field** and a genuine bilinear configuration. It does not estimate

```text
sum_r K_p(r) K_q(r+d),   p != q.
```

So this branch of Katz technology supplies the right language for local independence, but not the cross-base-characteristic theorem P3.2 needs.

# 5. Compatible systems and the large sieve: a different “independence across primes”

The strongest nearby zero-density framework I found is Corentin Perret-Gentil:

> C. Perret-Gentil, **Exponential Sums Over Finite Fields and the Large Sieve**, IMRN 2020, no. 20, 7139–7174.  
> DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rny202  
> arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06965

This applies Kowalski’s large sieve for Frobenius in compatible systems. It proves zero-density estimates for trace functions whose values lie in prescribed algebraic/definable subsets, under monodromy hypotheses including symplectic cases.

This looks tantalizingly close to the question, but its quantifiers are different. The basic setup is:

- fix the base characteristic `p`;
- let `q=p^e` grow;
- vary auxiliary valuations `lambda` of the coefficient field;
- use independence of the residual monodromy representations modulo distinct `lambda`.

For example, Proposition 5.11 of that paper explicitly starts by fixing `p` and setting `q=p^e`, and its hypergeometric discussion says the same methods apply to Katz hypergeometric sums.

Thus the “two-prime product monodromy” in the compatible-system sieve concerns distinct **auxiliary coefficient primes** `lambda,lambda'`, not two distinct **base characteristics** `p,q` in the sense of P3.2.

That distinction is exactly the trap here. Our event is

```text
p | b_r   and   q | b_s,
```

where `p` and `q` simultaneously serve as:

- the base field characteristics,
- the moduli of the zero conditions,
- and parameters entering the moving coefficient indices.

No compatible-system large-sieve theorem I found converts that into independent residual conditions at two auxiliary places.

There is a second scale problem: these zero-density theorems are designed to prove probabilities `o(1)` (often a small power saving), whereas P3.2 wants a **local-limit-scale probability about `1/p`** and then enough uniformity to place the rare point in an interval of relative length about `1/p`. Generic monodromy equidistribution is far too coarse by itself.

So the answer to question 4 is:

> **Sp4 monodromy is useful input, but it is nowhere near sufficient by itself. One would need a new uniform local-limit/dispersion theorem that survives changing the base characteristic and the coefficient/Mellin coordinate simultaneously.**

# 6. The closest theorem in the *correct formal category*: Kowalski–Soundararajan CRT equidistribution

The most relevant general theorem is:

> E. Kowalski, K. Soundararajan, **Equidistribution from the Chinese Remainder Theorem**, Adv. Math. 385 (2021), 107776.  
> DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2021.107776  
> arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12965

They start with arbitrary local sets `A_p` and form the CRT product `A_q` for composite `q`. That is exactly the formal architecture of (1.2).

Their results split into two regimes which explain why P3.2 falls through the gap:

### 6.1 Bounded local sets

When the local sets have bounded size, CRT multiplication can eventually create enough points for equidistribution **if the modulus has sufficiently many distinct prime factors**. Their Theorem 1.6 has a regime in which the number `k` of prime factors tends to infinity. The paper explicitly notes that `k -> infinity` is needed in that setting so that the CRT product contains many points.

Our modulus is always exactly

```text
pq
```

with `k=2`.

### 6.2 Fixed `k=2`

Their Theorem 1.8 can handle any fixed `k>=2`, but under a stronger local-mixing hypothesis. In dimension one they point out that the condition applies, for example, when the local cardinality `rho(p)` tends to infinity.

The Apéry random model is the opposite regime:

```text
rho(p)=|Z_p| = O(1)  (heuristically / on average).
```

Therefore the strongest general CRT theorem does **not** give semiprime equidistribution for these bounded local sets.

And even if one somehow obtained ordinary equidistribution of `A_{pq}/pq` on `R/Z`, P3.2 needs more. The target interval has normalized length

\[
\frac{L_{p,q}}{pq}\asymp\frac1X.
\]

A discrepancy estimate merely saying

\[
\operatorname{disc}(A_{pq})=o(1)
\]

has an error much larger than the desired main term `asymp 1/X`. One would need a **shrinking-target discrepancy bound at scale `1/X`**, averaged over semiprimes with their special linked interval `I_{p,q}`.

This is the strongest literature comparison I would bank for Q3561: **Kowalski–Soundararajan is the nearest theorem by formal structure, and its hypotheses identify almost exactly why the bounded-`Z_p`, two-prime case is missing.**

# 7. Polynomial-root CRT results do not rescue the Apéry case

Kowalski–Soundararajan emphasize roots of congruences as a motivating example. There are much stronger special results for roots of irreducible quadratic congruences (Duke–Friedlander–Iwaniec, Tóth, and later refinements), and there is continuing work on equidistribution of roots/polynomial values.

Those results exploit that the local sets are roots of **one fixed polynomial of bounded degree** and therefore possess very rigid arithmetic parameterizations and automorphic/Kloosterman structures.

The Apéry set

\[
Z_p=\{0\le j<p:b_j\equiv0\pmod p\}
\]

is not known to be the root set in `j` of one fixed bounded-degree polynomial over `Z`. Interpolating `j -> b_j mod p` produces degree growing with `p`, which destroys the bounded-complexity input needed by the standard Weil machinery.

So this important special CRT literature is evidence for what *kind* of extra structure could solve the problem, but it does not currently apply to Apéry coefficient zeros.

# 8. Why even fixed-prime “`Z_p` is random” is not a proved Katz consequence

The statement in the question

> “Katz's equidistribution gives: for fixed `p`, `Z_p` is random in `[0,p-1]`”

is too strong as written.

A particularly revealing Apéry-specific reference is:

> A. Malik, A. Straub, **Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers**, Research in Number Theory 2 (2016), article 5.  
> DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-016-0036-8  
> arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00297

They use Gessel’s Lucas congruences and the palindromic behavior of the first `p` Apéry values. For the classical Apéry sequence they then make a *randomness heuristic*: treat the `(p+1)/2` essentially independent values as random mod `p`. This leads to their Conjecture 6.3 that the proportion of primes dividing no Apéry number is

\[
e^{-1/2}.
\]

That is already a statistic depending only on whether the first block contains any zero. It remains conjectural in this literature. This is strong evidence that exact zero statistics of the coefficient block are **not** currently a routine consequence of Katz monodromy.

A very recent structural paper is:

> X. Caruso, F. Fürnsinn, D. Vargas-Montoya, W. Zudilin, **Galois Groups of Apéry-like Series Modulo Primes**, Bull. Austral. Math. Soc. 114 (2026), 65–78.  
> DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972725100932  
> arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23298

It computes Galois groups of reductions of Apéry-like **generating series** and gives striking prime-dependent algebraic structure. It does not prove the coefficient-index zero-set pair correlation needed here.

So even before asking for the joint distribution of `(Z_p,Z_q)`, one should not bank a theorem saying each `Z_p` individually is a uniformly random subset of `F_p` at exact-zero resolution.

# 9. A project-specific Lucas reformulation: the collision is also a two-large-prime-divisors problem for one Apéry number

Gessel’s Lucas congruence for the classical Apéry sequence says

\[
b_{ap+r}\equiv b_a b_r\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\tag{9.1}
\]

At q=6,

\[
n=6p+r,
\]

so

\[
b_n\equiv b_6 b_r\pmod p.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Therefore, for every prime `p` not dividing the fixed integer `b_6`,

\[
p\mid b_r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_n.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Up to the finite exceptional set `p|b_6`, the q=6 count can thus be written as

\[
K_6(n)
=\#\{p:n/7<p\le n/6,\ p\mid b_n\}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

A two-prime collision is simply

\[
pq\mid b_n,
\qquad
p,q\in(n/7,n/6].
\tag{9.5}
\]

This is a useful conceptual correction. The missing result can be stated without any cross-characteristic trace-language at all:

> control the number of Apéry integers `b_n` having two large prime divisors in the moving interval `(n/7,n/6]`.

For example, a sufficient averaged theorem is

\[
\sum_{n\le N}
\binom{\omega_{(n/7,n/6]}(b_n)}2
\ll \frac{N}{\log N},
\tag{9.6}
\]

while the random model predicts the stronger `N/log^2 N` scale.

I do not know a Rankin–Selberg, sieve-for-recurrences, or primitive-divisor theorem that proves (9.6) for Apéry numbers. This reformulation actually makes clear why “joint Sato–Tate at `p` and `q`” is not automatically the right global object: the two events are divisibility conditions on the **same integer `b_n`**.

# 10. Rankin–Selberg: why it does not see this indicator

Rankin–Selberg methods are powerful when the relevant correlation can be encoded as coefficients/local factors of a tensor-product automorphic representation, for example

\[
\sum_{n\le X} a_f(n)\overline{a_g(n)}
\]

or joint Frobenius traces arising from fixed compatible systems.

Our observable is instead

\[
\mathbf1_{p\mid b_r}\,\mathbf1_{q\mid b_s},
\qquad r-s=6(q-p),
\]

or equivalently the two-large-prime-divisor event (9.5). The indicator is a nonlinear congruence condition; the local character label/index moves with the place; and no fixed automorphic `L`-function is known whose Rankin–Selberg coefficients are these collision indicators.

Ahlgren–Ono and related modular/hypergeometric congruences connect certain special Apéry values to modular forms and finite-field hypergeometric functions. They do not encode the entire moving set `j -> 1_{p|b_j}` uniformly in `p`.

Thus Rankin–Selberg is an analogy for the kind of global independence one might want, not an existing proof tool for this exact statistic.

# 11. Large sieve: why the obvious expansions stop at the critical barrier

The exact zero indicator has the additive-character expansion

\[
\mathbf1_{p\mid x}
=\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p} e_p(ax).
\]

Using (1.1), one gets

\[
C(p,q)
=\frac1{pq}
\sum_{a\bmod p}\sum_{c\bmod q}
\sum_{0\le s<L_{p,q}}
 e_p\!\bigl(a b_{s+d}\bigr)
 e_q\!\bigl(c b_s\bigr).
\tag{11.1}
\]

So a large-sieve/dispersion approach would need cancellation simultaneously in

```text
p, q, a, c, s.
```

A pointwise Weil bound for the inner `s`-sum is not enough, because there are `pq` additive frequencies and absolute summation loses the gain.

Even under the optimistic fiction that the two local functions were bounded-conductor trace functions, form their CRT product modulo `M=pq`. Complete sums would factor and have square-root size

\[
\ll\sqrt{pq}.
\]

Completion of an interval gives a Pólya–Vinogradov-type bound

\[
\ll \sqrt{pq}\log(pq).
\tag{11.2}
\]

But here

\[
\sqrt{pq}\asymp X,
\qquad
L_{p,q}\le p\asymp X.
\]

Thus (11.2) is not even better than the trivial interval-length bound. The q=6 geometry places us exactly at the square-root-modulus barrier.

The classical large sieve has the same qualitative problem: with moduli up to `Q\asymp X` and an index range of length only `X`, its `Q^2` term dominates. The specialized algebraic trace-function large sieve of Xi–Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel is extremely useful in its one-modulus setting, but it does not by itself create mixed-base-characteristic cancellation here:

> P. Xi, É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, Ph. Michel, **Large Sieve Inequalities for Algebraic Trace Functions**, IMRN 2017, no. 16, 4840–4881.  
> DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnw074

So question 5 has a fairly crisp answer:

> **No standard Rankin–Selberg or large-sieve theorem currently gives the required bound. A plausible route would have to be a new averaged dispersion/large-sieve theorem in which one does not take absolute values before exploiting the `(p,q)` average.**

# 12. What theorem would actually close P3.2?

For a dyadic scale `X`, define

\[
\mathcal P_X
=\{(p,q):X<p<q\le2X,\ q<7p/6\}.
\]

The random-model main term is

\[
M_X
:=\sum_{(p,q)\in\mathcal P_X}
  \frac{(7p-6q)|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}.
\tag{12.1}
\]

Under bounded average zero-set mass this is naturally of size `X/log^2 X`.

Any theorem of the form

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{(p,q)\in\mathcal P_X} C(p,q)
\ll \frac{X}{\log X}
}
\tag{12.2}
\]

is already sufficient for the desired global `E_2(N)=O(N/log N)` after dyadic summation, assuming the corresponding first-moment bound.

The random model predicts the stronger

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{(p,q)\in\mathcal P_X} C(p,q)
\asymp M_X
\asymp \frac{X}{(\log X)^2}.
}
\tag{12.3}
\]

A natural “Katz-strength if it existed” statement would be a centered dispersion estimate such as

\[
\sum_{(p,q)\in\mathcal P_X}
\left(
C(p,q)
-\frac{(7p-6q)|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}
\right)
=o\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}\right),
\tag{12.4}
\]

but P3.2 does not need anything that sharp. Even a fixed logarithmic or small power saving below the worst-case overlap concentration would be valuable.

I would name the missing input explicitly as:

> **Two-prime Apéry CRT shrinking-target / horizontal dispersion theorem.**  
> For the first-block Apéry zero sets `Z_p`, prove averaged deconcentration of the CRT products `Z_p x Z_q` in the linked intervals `[6q,7p)` for `q<7p/6`.

This is more precise than saying “need Sp4 equidistribution”. It identifies the exact quantifiers and the shrinking scale that a successful theorem must address.

# 14. Bottom line for the five questions

### 1. Does fixed-`p` Katz equidistribution imply joint distribution of `(Z_p,Z_q)`?

**No.** In fact the premise is too strong: ordinary Katz equidistribution does not by itself prove that the coefficient-index exact zero set `Z_p` is random. Joint `p,q` distribution is an additional cross-base-characteristic problem.

### 2. Is `b_r=0 mod p` and `b_{r+shift}=0 mod q` a standard trace-function correlation?

**No.** Standard trace-function correlations tensor sheaves over one finite field. Here the two congruence conditions live in different base characteristics and the index shifts with the pair of primes.

### 3. Does the random model give only `O(log log N)` off diagonal?

**No.** After summing over all admissible prime partners, the bounded-zero-set random model predicts

\[
\mathcal C_6(N)\asymp N/\log^2 N,
\]

which is still safely below `N/log N`.

### 4. Can Sp4 monodromy prove the required `1/q` correlation?

**Not with any existing theorem I found.** Sp4 can certify geometric genericity/independence in the correct one-field or auxiliary-prime setting, but the required statement is a defining-characteristic local-limit theorem at shrinking-target scale. New input is needed.

### 5. Rankin–Selberg or large sieve?

**No direct theorem.** Rankin–Selberg has no known fixed automorphic object whose coefficients are these moving zero indicators. Ordinary completion/large sieve hits the `sqrt(pq)~L` barrier. The plausible analytic route is a genuinely new **averaged cross-characteristic dispersion inequality** that keeps the prime-pair and zero-detector frequency averages coupled until cancellation occurs.

---

# 15. Recommendation for P3.2

I would **not** spend the next step trying to invoke a black-box Katz/Sp4 theorem: the needed black box does not appear to exist.

The strongest concrete routes are instead:

1. **Exploit the Lucas reformulation (9.4)–(9.5)** and attack two large prime divisors of the single integer `b_n` directly. This may expose arithmetic structure invisible in the `Z_p` language.
2. **Develop a pair-averaged dispersion identity** from the Mellin representation, with the explicit goal of reducing every hard complete sum back to one characteristic before applying FKM/FKMS. Do not first demand a pointwise cross-characteristic trace-function bound.
3. **Use the much weaker sufficient target** (12.2), rather than trying to prove the full random asymptotic. Any substantial deconcentration below the worst-case `N^2/log^2 N` energy can already be useful, and `O(N/log N)` half-off-diagonal is enough for the stated second moment.
4. Treat Kowalski–Soundararajan 2021 as the correct template: what P3.2 would require is essentially a **new semiprime, bounded-local-set, shrinking-target refinement**, with the special Apéry structure supplying what general CRT theory lacks.

That, in my assessment, is the exact current literature boundary.
