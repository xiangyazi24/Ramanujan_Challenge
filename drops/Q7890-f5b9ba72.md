# Q7890 — factorial-gauge dynamics, Mobius iterations, and cross-prime correlations

## 1. Does Mobius-iteration p-adic exponential-sum theory apply?

The short answer is: **only partially, and not in the form needed for the Apery zero problem.** The Ostafe–Shparlinski theory is primarily designed for polynomial dynamical systems

\[
 x_{n+1}=F(x_n),\qquad \deg F\ge 2,
\]

or more generally maps whose iterates have growing algebraic complexity. The key input is that the n-th iterate has rapidly increasing degree/complexity, allowing bounds for sums such as

\[
\sum_{n<N}\chi(F^{\circ n}(a)).
\]

For example:

- Ostafe–Shparlinski, *On the distribution of rational points on varieties in finite fields*, and their subsequent work on polynomial dynamical systems, treat polynomial maps of degree at least two and use degree growth of iterates.
- Ostafe–Shparlinski, *Exponential sums over finite fields and applications*, develops bounds for polynomial/rational dynamical systems where the rational map itself has nontrivial iteration growth.

Our map is different:

\[
\Phi(s,[X:Y])=(s+1,[P(s)X-s^6Y:X]).
\]

The fiber map is a projective linear transformation. For fixed s it is an element of PGL_2(F_p), not a nonlinear self-map of P^1. Therefore there is no degree growth in the fiber variable. The complexity comes from the **non-autonomous time dependence** through the cubic polynomial P(s).

Thus the right object is not a usual Mobius dynamical system

\[
z\mapsto \frac{az+b}{cz+d},
\]

but a product of matrices

\[
M(r)=\begin{pmatrix}P(r)&-r^6\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v_n=M(n-1)\cdots M(0)v_0.
\]

This is closer to exponential sums over polynomial matrix products, trace functions, and l-adic sheaves than to classical Mobius iteration.

The obstruction is important: a fixed Mobius transformation has bounded algebraic complexity, whereas here the matrices vary with the clock. One should not expect a direct application of Ostafe–Shparlinski.

The relevant frameworks are instead:

1. **Deligne/Katz theory of trace functions.** If the matrix product can be realized as the Frobenius trace of a lisse sheaf over the s-line, square-root cancellation becomes possible.

2. **Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel theory of exponential sums involving trace functions.** This is the natural language for sums over algebraic families.

3. **Kowalski's theory of algebraic exponential sums and monodromy groups.** The main question becomes whether the associated sheaf has large geometric monodromy.

The Apery recurrence is therefore not a degree-2 polynomial dynamical system; it is an arithmetic cocycle over the translation action s -> s+1.

## 2. Reformulating the zero condition

Let

\[
B_n=(n!)^3b_n.
\]

Then

\[
B_{n+1}=P(n)B_n-n^6B_{n-1},
\]

with

\[
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]

Define the affine coordinate

\[
z_n=\frac{B_n}{B_{n-1}}.
\]

Then

\[
z_{n+1}=P(n)-\frac{n^6}{z_n}.
\]

The zero event is

\[
b_n\equiv0\pmod p
\iff B_n\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Because n! is invertible for n<p,

\[
B_{n-1}\ne0
\]

whenever the denominator is defined. Therefore

\[
B_n=0
\iff z_n=0,
\]

not infinity. Equivalently, the next step gives

\[
z_{n+1}=\infty.
\]

So the two equivalent hitting descriptions are:

\[
z_n=0
\]

or

\[
z_{n+1}=\infty.
\]

The projective formulation is cleaner because it avoids singular affine coordinates:

\[
[X_n:Y_n]=[B_n:B_{n-1}].
\]

Then Apery zeros are exactly

\[
[X_n:Y_n]=[0:1].
\]

### Can exponential sums over s control this?

Potentially, but a new ingredient is needed. A sum such as

\[
\sum_{s\in F_p}1_{[X_s:Y_s]=[0:1]}
\]

requires controlling the distribution of a non-autonomous PGL_2 cocycle.

A possible route is:

1. Construct an l-adic sheaf whose Frobenius trace encodes

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(M(s)\cdots M(0)).
\]

2. Prove geometric monodromy is sufficiently large.

3. Apply Deligne's Riemann hypothesis.

However, the orbit hitting a point is a nonlinear condition on the cocycle, not merely a trace condition. One likely needs a Fourier expansion of the indicator:

\[
1_{B_s=0}=\frac1p\sum_t e_p(tB_s),
\]

then estimate sums

\[
\sum_s e_p(tB_s).
\]

The difficulty is that B_s is not a polynomial of bounded degree; it is generated by a recurrence of length s. Thus the required cancellation is genuinely beyond standard polynomial exponential sums.

Conclusion:

- The projective formulation is exact.
- The zero event is a point-hitting problem for a PGL_2 cocycle.
- Existing Mobius iteration theory does not directly solve it.
- Trace-function methods are the more plausible replacement.

## 3. CRT and cross-prime independence

For coprime primes p,q the combined state space is

\[
\mathbf P^1(F_p)\times \mathbf P^1(F_q).
\]

The clock is modulo pq because

\[
s\mapsto(s+1)
\]

has period pq on the product.

The combined cocycle factors:

\[
\Phi_{pq}(s)=\left(\Phi_p(s),\Phi_q(s)\right).
\]

Hence the orbit is literally the product orbit.

For a zero event:

\[
E_p(s): [X_s:Y_s]_p=[0:1],
\]

\[
E_q(s): [X_s:Y_s]_q=[0:1].
\]

The simultaneous event is

\[
E_p(s)\cap E_q(s).
\]

CRT alone gives the identity

\[
\#\{s\bmod pq:E_p(s),E_q(s)\}
\]

but it does not give independence.

Independence would require something like

\[
\Pr(E_p\cap E_q)=\Pr(E_p)\Pr(E_q),
\]

which is a statement about correlations of two cocycles driven by the same clock.

The shared clock is the main obstruction. Although the fibers are independent algebraically, the parameter s couples them.

A useful decomposition is:

\[
s=a+pt,
\]

with a mod p and t mod q. Then the p-component depends only on a, while the q-component sees

\[
a+pt\pmod q.
\]

If p is invertible modulo q, this is a permutation of q-times. Therefore for a fixed p-event residue a, the q-clock samples a complete orbit.

This gives a conditional independence heuristic:

\[
\sum_{s\bmod pq}1_{E_p(s)}1_{E_q(s)}
\approx
\frac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}pq
=|Z_p||Z_q|.
\]

But proving an error term requires a mixing statement for the q-cocycle along arithmetic progressions.

The needed theorem would resemble:

For every p-event residue a,

\[
\sum_{t\bmod q}1_{E_q(a+pt)}
=
|Z_q|+O(q^{1/2+\epsilon}).
\]

Such a result is currently unavailable from ordinary CRT arguments.

## 4. Consequences for the atom program

The factorial gauge insight is still very valuable. It changes the problem from a moving recurrence to a fixed cocycle:

\[
\Phi:A^1\times P^1\rightarrow A^1\times P^1.
\]

But the correct research target is not Ostafe–Shparlinski polynomial dynamics. The hierarchy is:

1. **Easy:** exact orbit computation and structural identities.

2. **Medium:** prove non-concentration of the PGL_2 cocycle using algebraic geometry.

3. **Hard:** prove square-root cancellation for cocycle hitting events.

4. **Hardest:** obtain multi-prime independence needed for third/fourth moments.

The cross-prime problem is therefore not blocked by CRT. CRT gives the product decomposition. The missing ingredient is quantitative decorrelation of two copies of the same arithmetic cocycle evaluated at the same clock.

The Q7884 insight identifies the right object: a polynomially parameterized PGL_2 cocycle over the translation dynamical system. It is a better formulation than treating b_n as an isolated recurrence, but it moves the problem into arithmetic monodromy rather than classical polynomial dynamics.
