## 2. Determinant recurrence in both indices

\[

which is (V2).

No step treats the Apéry operator as scalar multiplication; the whole three-term row is used.

## 3. Adjacent Casoratian and the exact Green sum

\Xi_n:=n^3(b_{n-1}\kappa_n-b_n\kappa_{n-1}).

## 4. Minimal denominator clearing and exact p-adic preservation

Write

\[
\kappa_j=u_j/d_j,
\qquad u_j\in\mathbf Z,\quad d_j\in\mathbf Z_{>0},\quad (u_j,d_j)=1.
\]

Then

\[
D_{m,n}
=\frac{b_mu_nd_m-b_nu_md_n}{d_md_n},
\]

but `d_md_n` is not generally the minimal common denominator.  The exact primitive form is (V6): first use

\[
L=\operatorname{lcm}(d_m,d_n),
\]

then

\[
T=b_mu_n(L/d_n)-b_nu_m(L/d_m),
\]

and finally divide numerator and denominator by

\[
h=\gcd(T,L).
\]

This produces

\[
D_{m,n}=\widetilde D_{m,n}/q_{m,n}
\]

in lowest terms.

The Q7655 denominator theorem is

\[
d_j\mid \ell_j^3,
\qquad
\ell_j=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,j).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore if `p>max(m,n)`,

\[
p\nmid d_m d_n L h q_{m,n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Suppose row `n` is a target.  Since the denominator is a `p`-unit, targetness is exactly

\[
p\mid b_n,\qquad p\mid u_n.
\]

In the two terms of `D_{m,n}`, the first has valuation at least `v_p(u_n)` and the second at least `v_p(b_n)`.  Thus

\[
v_p(D_{m,n})
\ge\min(v_p(u_n),v_p(b_n)).
\]

Using (4.2) converts this without loss to the primitive numerator and proves (V7).

If both endpoints are target rows, write

\[
a=v_p(b_m),\quad b=v_p(u_m),\quad
c=v_p(b_n),\quad d=v_p(u_n).
\]

The two terms have valuations at least `a+d` and `c+b`.  Since

\[
\min(a+d,c+b)\ge\min(a,b)+\min(c,d),
\]

we obtain (V8).  This is why a single matching edge can count one p-adic unit for **each** labeled target endpoint, even if the same prime occurs at both endpoints.

## 5. Two fixed anchors give the exact row content

The most useful determinant gcd is obtained before entering the dyadic block:

\[
D_{0,r}=b_0\kappa_r-b_r\kappa_0=\kappa_r=u_r/d_r.
\]

Thus

\[
\widetilde D_{0,r}=u_r.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Likewise

\[
D_{1,r}=5\kappa_r+36b_r
=\frac{5u_r+36d_rb_r}{d_r}.
\]

Since `(u_r,d_r)=1`,

\[
\gcd(5u_r+36d_rb_r,d_r)
=\gcd(5u_r,d_r)
=\gcd(5,d_r)=:e_r.
\]

Therefore

\[
\widetilde D_{1,r}
=\frac{5u_r+36d_rb_r}{e_r}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Here `e_r` is either `1` or `5`.  If `e_r=1`,

\[
\gcd(u_r,5u_r+36d_rb_r)
=\gcd(u_r,36d_rb_r)
=\gcd(u_r,36b_r).
\]

If `e_r=5`, then

\[
\widetilde D_{1,r}=u_r+36(d_r/5)b_r,
\]

and again `(u_r,d_r/5)=1`, so the same gcd is obtained.  Hence the integer identity (V9) holds.

This is stronger than a mod-`p` assertion.  In particular, every prime `p>3` satisfies

\[
v_p\gcd(\widetilde D_{0,r},\widetilde D_{1,r})
=\min(v_p(u_r),v_p(b_r)).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The fixed anchor determinant

\[
D_{0,1}=-36
\]

explains why there is no fresh-prime ambiguity.

For comparison, arbitrary anchors satisfy the Plücker identities

\[
\boxed{
 b_cD_{a,r}-b_aD_{c,r}=b_rD_{a,c},
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 \kappa_cD_{a,r}-\kappa_aD_{c,r}=\kappa_rD_{a,c}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus, modulo a fresh prime at which all denominators are units and `D_{a,c}` is a unit,

\[
D_{a,r}=D_{c,r}=0
\iff b_r=\kappa_r=0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

But if `p|D_{a,c}`, the two moving-anchor minors can share a spurious prime.  The anchors `(0,1)` remove this issue globally for every `p>3` because `D_{0,1}=-36`.

So the determinant gcd construction is completely understood: its fresh part is the original row content, not a new smaller arithmetic object.

## 6. Sharp CM asymptotics for `D_{m,n}`

Q7655 proves, for every fixed expansion order, the coefficient-ratio expansion.  We only need order two:

\[
r_n:=\frac{\kappa_n}{b_n}
=C_{\rm CM}+\frac{c_1}{n}+\frac{c_2}{n^2}+O(n^{-3}),
\tag{6.1}
\]

where

\[
c_1=\frac{5\pi}{\sqrt6\,E_0^2(1-17\rho)}>0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Take distinct integers

\[
R\le m<n\le2R.
\]

Subtracting (6.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_n-r_m
&=c_1\left(\frac1n-\frac1m\right)
+c_2\left(\frac1{n^2}-\frac1{m^2}\right)
+O(R^{-3})\\
&=-c_1\frac{n-m}{mn}\left(1+O(R^{-1})\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The estimate is uniform even when `n-m=1`: the leading term is then `Theta(R^-2)`, while the combined remainder is `O(R^-3)`.

The Apéry asymptotic is

\[
b_k=B\rho^{-k}k^{-3/2}(1+O(k^{-1})),
\tag{6.4}
\]

with

\[
B=\frac{\sqrt6}{2\pi^{3/2}\sqrt{d_1}},
\qquad
d_1=1-\rho^2=2(1-17\rho).
\]

Since

\[
D_{m,n}=b_mb_n(r_n-r_m),
\]

(6.3)-(6.4) give

\[
D_{m,n}
=-B^2c_1(n-m)\rho^{-(m+n)}(mn)^{-5/2}
(1+O(R^{-1})).
\]

Substituting `d_1=2(1-17rho)` gives (V12).

Two consequences matter.

First, for all sufficiently large dyadic blocks,

\[
m<n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_{m,n}<0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus no eventual exact-zero determinant can be used to evade the height argument.

Second, **`C_CM` has vanished**.  The determinant sees the first nonconstant CM correction `c_1`, not the limiting Eichler period.  Therefore any irrationality question about

\[
14\zeta(3)-\frac{20\pi^3}{9\sqrt6}
\]

is irrelevant to this determinant architecture.

For adjacent indices one gets the useful corollary

\[
\Xi_n=n^3D_{n-1,n}
=-K_0\rho^{-(2n-1)}n^{-2}(1+O(n^{-1})).
\tag{6.6}
\]

This gives the exact exponential scale of the integral transverse Casoratian.

## 7. A positive singleton-sensitive matching carrier

Assume first that `R` is even.  Pair the block rows as

\[
(R+1,R+2),\ (R+3,R+4),\ldots,(2R-1,2R).
\]

For an edge `(m,n)`, let `tilde D_{m,n}` be the primitive numerator from (V6), and define

\[
\mathcal C_R
=\prod_{j=1}^{R/2}
|\widetilde D_{R+2j-1,R+2j}|.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Fix `p>2R`.  Every target row lies at one endpoint of exactly one matching edge.  Applying (V7) or (V8) edge by edge gives

\[
v_p(\mathcal C_R)
\ge
\sum_{r=R+1}^{2R}
\min(v_p(b_r),v_p(u_r)).
\tag{7.2}
\]

In particular, if

\[
N_p(R)=\#\{R<r\le2R:p\mid b_r,\ p\mid u_r\},
\]

then

\[
v_p(\mathcal C_R)\ge N_p(R).
\tag{7.3}
\]

So the matching carrier passes the essential Q7661 singleton test: a completely fresh prime occurring at only one row contributes one full p-adic valuation.

For odd `R`, pair `R-1` rows and attach the last row to one neighboring vertex.  This changes every asymptotic below by only `O(R)`.

The carrier is therefore positive and exact.  The issue is its size.

## 8. The matching carrier has sharp quadratic height

For an adjacent edge `(n-1,n)`,

\[
D_{n-1,n}=\Xi_n/n^3.
\]

Let

\[
s_n=\gcd(|\Xi_n|,n^3).
\]

Then the reduced form is

\[
\widetilde D_{n-1,n}=\Xi_n/s_n,
\qquad
q_{n-1,n}=n^3/s_n.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Hence

\[
1\le q_{n-1,n}\le n^3,
\qquad
\log q_{n-1,n}=O(\log R).
\tag{8.2}
\]

From (V13), for each matching edge

\[
\log|\widetilde D_{m,n}|
=(m+n)\log\lambda+O(\log R).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Summing all matching endpoints uses every vertex exactly once:

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{R/2}
\bigl((R+2j-1)+(R+2j)\bigr)
=\sum_{r=R+1}^{2R}r
=\frac{3R^2+R}{2}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal C_R
=\frac{3R^2+R}{2}\log(17+12\sqrt2)
+O(R\log R).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

This proves (V16).

Thus the determinant matching gives exactly what one wants p-adically and exactly what one does **not** want archimedeanly: its height is quadratic with a positive explicit leading constant.

## 9. Scoped lower-bound theorem for every sparse determinant product

The same argument is not specific to adjacent pairing.

Let `E_R` be any collection of unordered pairs `{m,n}` with

\[
R<m,n\le2R,
\]

and assume

\[
|E_R|=O(R).
\]

Suppose every row is incident to at least one edge.  This endpoint-cover condition is necessary for a **formal** product carrier: if a row `r` occurs in no determinant, targetness at `(p,r)` alone gives no reason for `p` to divide the product.

For every edge, (V13) and `|tilde D|=q|D|>=|D|` give

\[
\log|\widetilde D_{m,n}|
\ge(m+n)\log\lambda-O(\log R).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Summing,

\[
\sum_{\{m,n\}\in E_R}(m+n)
=\sum_{r=R+1}^{2R}r\deg_{E_R}(r).
\]

Every degree is at least one, so

\[
\sum_{\{m,n\}\in E_R}(m+n)
\ge\frac{3R^2+R}{2}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Because there are only `O(R)` edges, the accumulated error in (9.1) is `O(R log R)`.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
\log\prod_{e\in E_R}|\widetilde D_e|
\ge
\frac32\log(17+12\sqrt2)R^2-O(R\log R).
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

This is the requested scoped lower-bound/no-go.

It rules out:

- a perfect matching;
- a near-perfect matching;
- a star forest;
- any other `O(R)` edge cover;
- any sparse product in which each row target is covered solely because it is an endpoint of at least one selected determinant.

It does **not** rule out a new arithmetic identity in which a target row forces divisibility of determinants not incident to that row.  No such identity is presently proved.  The statement is therefore exactly scoped to what follows formally from target preservation plus the pair determinants themselves.

## 10. Why gcds do not inherit the product lower bound

A gcd can throw away almost all archimedean size.  Consequently (9.3) is not a lower bound for gcd constructions.

But formal p-adic coverage imposes a different restriction.  If

\[
G=\gcd(\widetilde D_{e_1},\ldots,\widetilde D_{e_s})
\]

is meant to be divisible whenever row `r` is a target, then every determinant in that gcd must be forced divisible by targetness at `r`.  Without an additional cross-row theorem, that means every edge `e_i` must contain `r`.  A multi-edge gcd is therefore naturally a **star gcd** centered at one row.

The two-edge star with fixed external anchors `0,1` is optimal because (V9) is exact:

\[
G_r^{01}
:=\gcd(\widetilde D_{0,r},\widetilde D_{1,r})
=\gcd(u_r,36b_r).
\tag{10.1}
\]

After removing `2,3`,

\[
[G_r^{01}]_{>3}=[\gcd(u_r,b_r)]_{>3}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Thus the block product

\[
\prod_{r=R+1}^{2R}[G_r^{01}]_{>2R}
\]

is exactly

\[
\prod_{r=R+1}^{2R}[\gcd(u_r,b_r)]_{>2R},
\tag{10.3}
\]

which is the zeroth-Fitting product already isolated in Q7661/Q7662.

Therefore the gcd route has no false height penalty, but also no new theorem:

\[
\log(10.3)=o(R^2)
\]

is precisely the unresolved singleton-tail statement.

For moving anchors `(a,c)`, the Plücker identities show the only additional obstruction: primes dividing `D_{a,c}` can enter the two-minor gcd without targetness at `r`.  This is why the fixed anchors `0,1` are arithmetically canonical.

So products and gcds fail for opposite reasons:

- **products:** correct valuations, provably quadratic height;
- **gcds:** potentially tiny height, but the canonical fresh part is exactly the original unknown target content.

## 12. Interaction with the dyadic aggregate

From the proved Apéry zero-count theorem

\[
Z(p)\ll p^{2/3},
\]

Q7661 already gives, on `R<r<=2R`,

\[
\sum_{p\le2R}N_p(R)\log p=O(R^{5/3}).
\tag{12.1}
\]

Thus the only quadratic-scale object is

\[
L_{>2R}(R)
=\sum_{p>2R}N_p(R)\log p.
\tag{12.2}
\]

The matching carrier gives a valid but useless upper bound

\[
L_{>2R}(R)\le\log\mathcal C_R
=\frac32\log(17+12\sqrt2)R^2+O(R\log R).
\tag{12.3}
\]

The fixed-anchor gcd carrier gives instead the exact arithmetic object

\[
\prod_{r=R+1}^{2R}[\gcd(b_r,u_r)]_{>2R},
\tag{12.4}
\]

whose logarithm, with valuations retained, is the zeroth-Fitting mass.  A squarefree version gives the radical mass.  But proving either relevant large-prime mass to be `o(R^2)` is the missing theorem itself.

Therefore the determinant calculation sharpens the architecture but does not solve the singleton tail.

## 13. Final theorem / no-go classification

### Proved here

- Exact two-index recurrences (V1)-(V2).
- Exact adjacent Casoratian and Green/telescoping formulas (V3)-(V5).
- Exact minimal denominator clearing (V6).
- Fresh-prime valuation preservation, including additive endpoint multiplicity (V7)-(V8).
- Exact fixed-anchor primitive gcd
  \[
  \gcd(\widetilde D_{0,r},\widetilde D_{1,r})=\gcd(u_r,36b_r),
  \]
  so its prime-`>3` part is exactly the transverse row content.
- Uniform sharp dyadic CM determinant asymptotic (V11)-(V13), with positive explicit constant `K_0`.
- A positive adjacent-matching carrier that counts every singleton labeled prime.
- Sharp quadratic height asymptotic for that matching carrier.
- A general quadratic lower bound for every sparse endpoint-cover product of primitive `D_{m,n}`.
- Exact Sage verification and primitive-content factorization through `n=500`.

### Not proved, and not claimed

- Irrationality of `C_CM`.
- Any use of an irrationality measure for `C_CM`.
- `o(R^2)` for the fixed-anchor/Fitting gcd product.
- A theorem excluding some entirely new cross-row arithmetic identity that makes a non-endpoint determinant vanish at a target.
- Finiteness of the common-pair set from the `n<=500` census.

### Bottom line

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{Sparse determinant products are genuine target carriers,}\\
\text{but CM forces their log height to be }\Theta(R^2).\\[2mm]
\text{Sparse determinant gcds can be arithmetically small,}\\
\text{but the canonical fixed-anchor gcd is exactly the original}\\
\text{transverse Fitting content, so no new }o(R^2)\text{ theorem results.}
\end{array}}
\]

The determinant route is therefore **closed as an archimedean product-compression strategy**, while the fixed-anchor gcd identity is a useful exact normalization for any future arithmetic attack on the singleton tail.
