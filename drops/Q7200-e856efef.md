# Q7200: Large-prime support of the Apéry continuant resultants

## 1. Object under study

For fixed short gaps `s,G`, the bridge obstruction is encoded by

\[
R_{s,G}=\operatorname{Res}_x(N_s(x),N_G(x+s)).
\]

A prime `p` contributes to the far-bridge argument only through
\[
v_p(R_{s,G})>0,
\]
which means that the two continuants have a common root modulo `p` after the shift. The question is whether the integer resultant has its prime support forced away from the dyadic range `(X,2X]` or concentrated there.

The answer is negative with current general theorems. The available primitive-divisor and smoothness theorems do not match this family.

---

## 2. Primitive divisor theorems: why they do not apply

Classical primitive divisor results (Zsigmondy, Carmichael for Lucas sequences, and later results for Lehmer sequences) require a fixed divisibility sequence or a fixed recurrence in the index whose terms satisfy

\[
a_m\mid a_n \quad (m\mid n).
\]

Typical examples are Lucas terms `U_n`, cyclotomic factors, elliptic divisibility sequences, or polynomial sequences with a known group law.

The Apéry continuants do not satisfy this structure.

Here `h` changes the polynomial itself:

\[
N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x).
\]

The resultant is a two-parameter quantity:

\[
R_{s,G}=R(s,G),
\]

not an indexed term of a divisibility sequence. There is no known identity of the form

\[
R_{s,G_1}\mid R_{s,G_2}
\]

or a cyclotomic decomposition separating primitive factors.

Therefore Zsigmondy-type primitive divisor theorems cannot imply either:

* most prime factors of `R_{s,G}` are small; or
* a positive proportion of prime factors lie near `X`.

---

## 3. Smooth-part estimates: mismatch

Results on smooth values of polynomial sequences (for example Stewart-type bounds for recurrence sequences) require a one-dimensional sequence with controlled growth and usually estimate

\[
P(n)=\prod_{p\le y}p^{v_p(P(n))}.
\]

Here the variable is the pair `(s,G)` and the relevant object is

\[
\operatorname{Res}(N_s,N_G(\cdot+s)).
\]

The hypotheses fail in three places:

1. The degree grows with `s,G`.
2. The coefficients are not generated by a fixed polynomial family of bounded height.
3. The prime divisors being counted are not divisors of one recurrence term but common-root primes of two different transfer polynomials.

A height estimate

\[
\log |R_{s,G}| \ll d(s,G)\log X
\]

only gives an upper bound on the total weighted prime support. It does not distinguish primes below `X` from primes near `X`.

In particular, bit length cannot imply prime-range support: an integer of size `e^H` can have all its mass in primes much smaller than `X`, or contain a single prime of size comparable to `e^H`.

---

## 4. Orthogonal-polynomial/resultant theory

`N_h` behaves like a continuant and has similarities with orthogonal polynomial transfer systems, but the standard large-prime results for orthogonal polynomial resultants concern:

* fixed recurrence coefficients;
* fixed degree families;
* discriminants or adjacent resultants.

The Apéry family has shifted coefficients:

\[
P(x+h), (x+h)^6,
\]

so the pair

\[
(N_s(x),N_G(x+s))
\]

is not a pair of consecutive members of one stationary orthogonal system.

No theorem currently supplies a factorization

\[
R_{s,G}=R_{\mathrm{old}}R_{\mathrm{primitive}}
\]

with a lower bound on the size of primitive prime divisors.

---

## 5. Prime averaging does give the usable statement, but not support localization

The valid unconditional statement is the one already used:

For primes `p` in `(X,2X]`,

\[
N_s(x)=N_G(x+s)=0\pmod p
\]

forces

\[
p\mid R_{s,G}.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{p\sim X} \#\{(s,G,x):p\mid R_{s,G}\}
\le
\sum_{s,G}\sum_{p\sim X}v_p(R_{s,G}) .
\]

The right side is bounded by the logarithmic height:

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}v_p(R_{s,G})\log p
\le
\log |R_{s,G}|.
\]

This is exactly the existing far-bridge mechanism.

To improve it one would need a theorem giving a saving specifically on the dyadic slice `(X,2X]`.

---

## 6. The genuinely missing lemma

The weakest useful new statement is not a primitive divisor theorem. It is the following non-concentration estimate.

**Required residual resultant non-concentration lemma.** For `S,G\le H`, after removing all deterministic carrier factors,

\[
\sum_{s,G\le H}
\sum_{X<p\le2X}
\mathbf 1_{p\mid R^{\mathrm{res}}_{s,G}}
\log p
\ll
X^{2+o(1)}.
\]

The current height argument only gives the same estimate without the restriction `X<p<2X`.

Equivalently, one needs to prove that the residual resultant height is not concentrated on primes in a single large dyadic interval.

This is exactly the missing input for a stronger far-bridge theorem.

---

## 7. Conclusion

No known primitive divisor theorem, smooth-number theorem, or orthogonal-polynomial resultant theorem applies to the Apéry continuant resultants.

The mismatch is structural:

* `R_{s,G}` is a two-parameter shifted-continuant resultant, not a divisibility sequence.
* The family is non-stationary because coefficients shift with `h`.
* Height bounds do not imply prime-range support.

Therefore the correct current status is:

* the existing logarithmic-height far-bridge argument is rigorous;
* no theorem forces large prime support away from `(X,2X]`;
* no theorem forces concentration near `(X,2X]` either;
* the minimal missing ingredient is a dyadic prime non-concentration estimate for the carrier-deflated residual resultants.

Any proof of weak-DPLS using this route must establish that new lemma or replace the resultant method with a genuinely stronger arithmetic input.
