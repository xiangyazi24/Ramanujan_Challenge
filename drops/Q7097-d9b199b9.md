# Q7097: Small zeros and weighted exceptional-prime bounds

## 1. Setup and what the small zero gives

Let
\[
E_H(X)=\{p\in(X,2X]:\exists j\le H,\ p\mid b_j\}.
\]
For a fixed such prime choose a witness \(j\le H\) with \(j\in Z(p)\). The question is whether this extra information forces
\[
|Z(p)|\ll p/H\,\operatorname{polylog}X+H^{1+o(1)},
\]
or at least enough on average to obtain
\[
\sum_{p\in E_H(X)}|Z(p)|\le X^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

The existing tools give a real but limited amount of propagation.

## 2. Reflection and gap propagation

Reflection gives
\[
r\in Z(p)\Longrightarrow p-1-r\in Z(p).
\]
Hence a small zero \(j\) automatically produces the reflected zero
\[
p-1-j.
\]
This removes half of the possible locations but does not reduce the total size of \(Z(p)\): a set of size \(p^{2/3}\) is compatible with containing both endpoints.

Now fix the small zero \(j\). For another zero \(r\), the gap polynomial gives
\[
N_{r-j}(j)=0\pmod p.
\]
Equivalently every additional zero is a root of a polynomial of degree
\[
3(r-j-1)
\]
whose coefficients depend on the gap. This is not a fixed polynomial condition as \(r\) varies. The degree grows with the unknown second zero, so the usual root bound cannot be summed to improve \(Z(p)\).

The endpoint identity
\[
N_h(-s)=(-1)^{s-1}b_{s-1}b_{h-s}((s-1)!(h-s)!)^3
\]
shows that the boundary values are controlled by smaller Apéry numbers, but the known zero \(b_j\equiv0\) only kills those endpoint configurations where one factor equals \(b_j\). It does not force all interior roots into a sparse set.

## 3. Lucas/Cartier block structure

Write a zero as
\[
r=qp+s,\qquad 0\le s<p.
\]
The Cartier congruence
\[
b_{qp+s}\equiv b_qb_s\pmod p
\]
implies that zeros in higher blocks are generated by either

* a zero of the low block: \(b_s=0\), or
* a zero of the quotient index: \(b_q=0\).

For primes \(p>X\) and the present range of interest, the first block is the relevant one because indices below \(p\) are the residues defining \(Z(p)\). A small zero \(j\) therefore only says that one residue class is bad. It does not constrain the remaining bad residues unless one also knows how the orbit \((b_{r-1}:b_r)\) distributes after hitting \((1:0)\).

In particular, the transfer-matrix interpretation allows a trajectory which hits \((1:0)\) early and later has many further hits. The recurrence has no known monotonicity or expansion property preventing this.

## 4. The exact obstruction

There is a concrete combinatorial obstruction: the available statements are compatible with the following model.

Take a subset
\[
Z\subset[0,p-1]
\]
with:

1. no two consecutive elements;
2. symmetry under \(r\mapsto p-1-r\);
3. one prescribed element \(j\le H\);
4. size \(|Z|\asymp p^{2/3}\).

Such sets exist. None of the currently available consequences of the Apéry recurrence rules them out.

The gap-polynomial statement only says that every pair difference \(h\) satisfies an algebraic condition of degree \(O(h)\). The small zero does not lower these degrees. Therefore the known algebraic input cannot distinguish a prime with one early zero and \(p^{2/3}\) further zeros from a generic prime with \(p^{2/3}\) zeros.

So the desired implication
\[
 j\in Z(p),\ j\le H\quad\Rightarrow\quad |Z(p)|\ll p/H+H^{1+o(1)}
\]
is not derivable from the listed facts alone.

## 5. What can still be obtained

The small-zero condition is useful only after adding a genuinely new decorrelation statement. The required form is not another pointwise root count, but a conditional pair-correlation estimate.

A sufficient lemma would be:

> **Small-zero decorrelation lemma.** Uniformly for \(1\le j\le H\),
> \[
> \sum_{p\in(X,2X],\ p\mid b_j}|Z(p)|
> \ll X^{3/2+o(1)}/H.
> \]

Equivalently, after summing over \(j\), one needs to control
\[
\sum_{j\le H}\sum_{p:\,p\mid b_j}|Z(p)|.
\]
The current \(|Z(p)|\ll p^{2/3}\) gives only
\[
\ll H^2X^{2/3},
\]
which is too weak when \(H\sim X^{1/2}\).

A more structural sufficient statement would be:

> For every fixed small gap window \(j\le H\), the number of further zeros \(r\) with \(r-j\) in a range \(R\) is \(\ll R/H+H^{o(1)}\) on average over the exceptional primes.

This is exactly a two-point zero-correlation theorem for the Apéry projective orbit.

## 6. Conclusion

The small zero does not currently amplify the existing \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\) bound. Reflection gives only one forced partner; endpoint formulas only handle special gaps; Lucas factorization only propagates block structure. The missing ingredient is a new anti-clustering theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A zero at a prescribed small residue must decorrelate from later zeros.}
}
\]

Any proof of the desired weighted estimate must therefore introduce new arithmetic input (for example a projective-orbit mixing statement, a two-point resultant estimate with saving, or a conditional large-sieve bound for the zero indicators). The present algebraic data alone has an explicit obstruction and cannot close the weighted sum.
