## 1. Selected-row difference transformation

For a determinant with ordered rows

\[
F(t_0),F(t_1),\ldots,F(t_m),\qquad t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m,
\]
performing row replacements

\[
F(t_i)\mapsto F(t_i)-F(t_{i-1})
\]
for the selected non-anchor rows is an elementary determinant-preserving operation. After this transformation the determinant has one anchored row and successive divided differences. The dimensions are unchanged: an `(m+1)\times(m+1)` determinant remains `(m+1)\times(m+1)`.

The extracted chord factors are exactly
\[
(t_i-t_{i-1})
\]
with positive sign because the indices are ordered increasingly. No hidden row reversal occurs.

## 2. Convex-combination step

The normalized divided-difference rows are compared on adjacent intervals. For each secant interval,

\[
\frac{G(t_j)-G(t_i)}{t_j-t_i}
\]

is expanded as a positive weighted sum of adjacent normalized-difference rows. The weights are barycentric interval lengths divided by the total interval length, hence positive and summing to one.

Multilinearity then expands each secant determinant as a sum of ordered minors of the next-level normalized rows. Because all intervals are disjoint and ordered, every term has the same orientation. Therefore no cancellation occurs.

This is the missing total-positivity mechanism: it is not inferred from numerical scans; it is supplied by the explicit Neville-type positive expansion.

## 3. Leading coefficients at every level

From
\[
f_j(k)=a^jk^j+O(k^{j-1}),
\]
the `ell`-th normalized difference has leading term

\[
\Delta^\ell f_j(k)=\binom{j}{\ell}a^jk^{j-\ell}+O(k^{j-\ell-1}).
\]

After the normalization used in the file, the surviving degree-
`j-ell` coordinate has coefficient

\[
\binom{j}{\ell}a^{j-\ell}.

In particular the pivot sequence at level `ell` has the form

\[
(\ell+1)ak+C+O(1/k),

\[
(\ell+1)a+O(k^{-2})>0

## 4. Fixed initial rows

The rows with `k<K` are not controlled by the asymptotic pivot argument. The file handles them separately by expanding the determinant in the large terminal variables. The coefficient is the generalized Vandermonde determinant of the initial normalized coordinates.

The leading coefficient is nonzero because the limiting coordinates have strictly increasing polynomial degrees with nonzero binomial coefficients. Hence the finite exceptional block contributes only a finite threshold, not a new asymptotic obstruction.
