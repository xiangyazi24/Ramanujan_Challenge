# P3.2 spectral split audit: the split is exact, but the common-zero bridge is misindexed

## Verdict

The decomposition

\[
W_m=U_m-2r(r+1)V_m
\]

is an exact polynomial identity for the recurrence stated in the question.  However, the proposed closing argument has two independent fatal gaps.

1. **The recurrence-defined sequence with \(W_1=1\) is not the mixed determinant that vanishes when \(N_h=R_{h-1}=0\).**  In the repository normalization, the determinant that vanishes at such a common zero has a different index, initial value, coefficient, and product term.  Exact finite-field common roots give direct counterexamples to
   \[
   N_h=R_{h-1}=0\Longrightarrow W_h=0
   \]
   for the recurrence in the question.  For example,
   \[
   (p,h,r)=(61,3,8)
   \]
   has \(N_3(r)=R_2(r)=0\), but
   \[
   V_3(r)=16,\qquad U_3(r)=48,\qquad W_3(r)=1\pmod{61}.
   \]

2. **\(V_h\) is not a universal unit at roots of \(N_h\).**  There are already nonwrapping, nonmate counterexamples at gap three:
   \[
   (p,h,r)=(239,3,77),\qquad(431,3,135),
   \]
   for which
   \[
   N_3(r)=V_3(r)=0\pmod p.
   \]
   Both satisfy \(r+3<p\) and \(2r+4\ne0\pmod p\).

The exact degrees are

\[
\boxed{\deg V_3=10,\qquad \deg V_4=16,\qquad \deg V_5=22,}
\]

and in general the split recurrence gives

\[
\boxed{\deg V_h=6h-8\quad(h\ge2).}
\]

Thus \(V_h\) is not a bounded-complexity denominator.  Even when \(V_h\ne0\), the equation

\[
r(r+1)=\frac{U_h(r)}{2V_h(r)}
\]

cross-multiplies to the already assumed equation \(W_h(r)=0\).  It is an equivalent rewriting, not a new independent constraint.  Its degree is \(6h-6\), which is linear in the moving gap and can be of order \(p\).

So this split does **not** complete the proof and does not by itself produce a descent.  The useful result of the audit is the exact identification of the indexing error and explicit counterexamples to the proposed denominator-unit lemma.

## 1. Normalization and the exact split

Use the normalization from Q2375:

\[
P(t)=34t^3+51t^2+27t+5,
\]

\[
Q_m(r)=(2r+2m+1)
\bigl(3r^2+2rm+m^2+3r+m+1\bigr),
\]

and

\[
\mathscr A(t)=16t^3+27t^2+12t+3.
\]

The two continuants begin with

\[
N_0=0,\qquad N_1=1,
\]

\[
N_{m+1}=P(r+m)N_m-(r+m)^6N_{m-1},
\tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
R_0=1,\qquad R_1=Q_1(r),
\]

\[
R_m=Q_m(r)R_{m-1}-(r+m)^6R_{m-2}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The recurrence in the question is

\[
W_{m+1}=(r+m+1)^6W_m
 +2\Bigl(\mathscr A(r+m+1)
 -(2r+2m+1)r(r+1)\Bigr)N_mR_m,
\tag{1.3}
\]

with \(W_1=1\).  Define

\[
U_1=1,\qquad V_1=0,
\]

\[
U_{m+1}=(r+m+1)^6U_m
 +2\mathscr A(r+m+1)N_mR_m,
\tag{1.4}
\]

\[
V_{m+1}=(r+m+1)^6V_m
 +(2r+2m+1)N_mR_m.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Subtracting \(2r(r+1)\) times (1.5) from (1.4) gives (1.3), and the initial values agree.  Hence, for every \(m\),

\[
\boxed{W_m=U_m-2r(r+1)V_m.}
\tag{1.6}
\]

The exact audit checked (1.6) independently for \(m=1,2,3,4,5\).

### Unrolled form of \(V_h\)

Iteration of (1.5) gives

\[
\boxed{
V_h=
\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}
(2r+2j+1)N_jR_j
\prod_{k=j+2}^{h}(r+k)^6.
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

The empty product is \(1\).  This formula immediately corrects one inference in the question: even if every individual summand before the last is nonzero, their sum may cancel in \(\mathbb F_p\).  “A full sum of nonzero terms” is not a nonvanishing theorem over a finite field.

At a root of \(R_{h-1}\), the last recurrence step merely gives

\[
\boxed{V_h=(r+h)^6V_{h-1}.}
\tag{1.8}
\]

In the nonwrapping range the scalar \((r+h)^6\) is a unit, but (1.8) does not descend further: one has no reason for \(R_{h-2}\) to vanish.

## 2. Degree growth

The continuant degrees are

\[
\deg N_m=3(m-1),\qquad \deg R_m=3m.
\]

In the recurrence for \(V_{m+1}\), both terms have degree

\[
6+\deg V_m=6m-2
\]

and

\[
1+\deg N_m+\deg R_m
=1+3(m-1)+3m=6m-2.
\]

Their leading coefficients are positive integers in the characteristic-zero calculation, so they do not cancel.  Starting from \(\deg V_2=4\), induction gives

\[
\boxed{\deg V_m=6m-8\qquad(m\ge2).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Similarly,

\[
\boxed{\deg U_m=6(m-1).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\deg\bigl(U_h-2r(r+1)V_h\bigr)=6h-6
\]

in the computed cases, with no top-degree cancellation.  Therefore the rational equation

\[
r(r+1)=U_h/(2V_h)
\]

is a degree-\(6h-6\) polynomial equation after clearing the denominator.  For a fixed \(h\), this of course has finitely many characteristic-zero solutions.  That observation is no stronger than fixed-gap resultant finiteness and has the same wrong quantifier order when \(h\asymp p\).

## 6. Critical indexing correction: the stated \(W_h\) need not vanish at a common root

The common-zero step in the question combines two different mixed-Wronskian normalizations.

With (1.1) and (1.2), define the actual adjacent determinant

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_m=N_{m+1}R_{m-1}-N_mR_m.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

If

\[
N_h=R_{h-1}=0,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_{h-1}
=N_hR_{h-2}-N_{h-1}R_{h-1}=0.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is the determinant whose vanishing is automatic.  It satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_m=(r+m)^6\Omega_{m-1}
 +\bigl(P(r+m)-Q_m(r)\bigr)N_mR_{m-1},
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

and its initial value is

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_1=N_2R_0-N_1R_1=P(r+1)-Q_1(r),
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

not \(1\).  Moreover, writing \(t=r+m\),

\[
P(t)-Q_m(r)
=2(2t+1)\bigl(8t^2+8t+2-r(r+1)\bigr).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Compare (6.3) with the recurrence in the question:

- (6.3) uses \(N_mR_{m-1}\), not \(N_mR_m\);
- its shift is \(t=r+m\), not \(r+m+1\);
- its initial value is (6.4), not \(1\).

Therefore the recurrence-defined \(W_m\) split in Sections 1–5 is **not** \(\Omega_m\).  The automatic vanishing (6.2) cannot be transferred to that \(W_m\).

## 7. \(V_h\) does vanish at roots of \(N_h\)

Over characteristic zero, the exact computations give

\[
\gcd_{\mathbb Q[r]}(N_3,V_3)
=\gcd_{\mathbb Q[r]}(N_4,V_4)
=\gcd_{\mathbb Q[r]}(N_5,V_5)=1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For \(h=4\), the raw integer-polynomial GCD returned by PARI is the constant \(2\), coming only from content; after primitive normalization the GCD is \(1\).

As usual, (7.1) gives only fixed-gap finiteness.  It does not prevent modular common roots at primes dividing the resultant.  At gap three the exact resultant is

\[
\boxed{

=167549804756054500338021775360000

\tag{7.2}

\operatorname{Res}_r(N_3,V_3)
=2^{14}5^4\,73^3\,239\,431\,408319591278163.

\boxed{

## 8. Why determining \(\lambda_r\) does not close the argument

Assume, contrary to the indexing issue above, that some correctly defined obstruction \(\widetilde W_h\) satisfies both

\[
\widetilde W_h=U_h-2r(r+1)V_h
\]

and

\[
N_h=R_{h-1}=0\Longrightarrow \widetilde W_h=0.
\]

If \(V_h\ne0\), then indeed

\[
r(r+1)=\frac{U_h}{2V_h}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

But (8.1) is exactly equivalent to

\[
U_h-2r(r+1)V_h=0.
\tag{8.2}
\]

No new equation has been created.  In particular:

- \(U_h/V_h\) is not a fixed rational function independent of \(h\);
- \(\deg U_h=6h-6\) and \(\deg V_h=6h-8\);
- clearing the denominator in (8.1) gives a degree-\(6h-6\) equation;
- when \(h\asymp p\), the elementary root bound is \(O(p)\), not a contradiction;
- eliminating (8.2) against \(N_h=0\) simply produces another moving resultant.

For each fixed \(h\), “there are finitely many solutions” is true.  That is precisely the fixed-gap theorem already available.  It does not solve the gap-versus-prime quantifier problem identified in Q2358.

There is an additional degeneracy when \(V_h=0\): if the same obstruction also vanishes, then necessarily \(U_h=0\), and the spectral parameter is not determined at all.  The exact audit exhibits such simultaneous zeros at boundary and small-characteristic common roots.  Conversely, the interior common root

\[
(p,h,r)=(37,4,16)
\]

has

\[
N_4=R_3=V_4=0,\qquad U_4=W_4=4\pmod{37},
\]

which again shows that the recurrence-defined \(W_4\) is not the common-zero determinant.

## 9. Exact residual after this attack

The split has one useful conceptual feature: it isolates the dependence on the Racah eigenvalue \(\lambda_r\).  But two further theorems would be required before that feature could close P3.2:

1. **Correct vanishing object.**  One must work with the determinant \(\Omega_{h-1}\) in (6.1), or another endpoint-relative object actually proved to vanish on
   \[
   N_h=R_{h-1}=0.
   \]
   Its recurrence is (6.3), not (1.3).

2. **Genuine low-complexity spectral rigidity.**  After a valid split, one would need more than \(V_h\ne0\).  One needs either
   - a bounded-degree relation satisfied by \(U_h/V_h\) on the physical endpoint-zero locus, or
   - a localized Bézout identity whose right side is a known unit after imposing \(b_r=b_{r+h}=0\) and excluding mates.

Without such extra input, solving for \(\lambda_r\) is a tautological rewriting of the obstruction equation, and the resulting elimination has the same moving-gap height problem as before.

The clean conclusion is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
W_m=U_m-2r(r+1)V_m\text{ is correct},\\
N_h=R_{h-1}=0\not\Rightarrow W_h=0
\text{ for this recurrence},\\
V_h\text{ can vanish at nonwrapping nonmate roots of }N_h,\\
\text{and }r(r+1)=U_h/(2V_h)\text{ is not an independent equation.}
\end{gathered}}
\]

Thus this spectral split does not complete the proof.  The next legitimate target is the spectral decomposition of the correctly indexed determinant \(\Omega_{h-1}\), followed by an endpoint-relative unit certificate; the present \(V_h\) is not such a certificate.
