## SWR reduction obtained

For a fixed prime p and a gap h with h<p/2, the actual Apéry zero condition gives a gap polynomial N_h. If two zeros occur in a short interval, then N_h has a root at the corresponding translated coordinate. The recurrence

  N_{h+1}(X)=P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X)

and the reflection identity reduce the surviving configurations to common-root events among shifted gap polynomials.

The clean range p>2h removes the wraparound ambiguity. The remaining exceptional cases are:

1. the central fixed reflection point r=(p-1)/2;
2. reflected pairs whose translated coordinates collide at the boundary;
3. small primes dividing the factorial/endpoint factors in resultant identities.

These are not eliminated by the currently proved identities alone.

## Exact obstruction

The available resultant factorization

  Res(N_h,N_{h+1}) = product of endpoint factors involving b_j and factorial terms

only controls adjacent gaps. The quadruple-corank argument requires a uniform bound for multiple simultaneous short gaps (equivalently, enough independence among several shifted gap polynomials). No unconditional estimate ruling out the required multi-gap common roots for the actual Apéry sequence was derived.

The exceptional central/reflection configurations cannot simply be discarded: reflection invariance is a property of the actual sequence but does not imply that a reflected quadruple has smaller multiplicity. Treating Z_p as an arbitrary reflection-invariant set would be invalid, but the converse (that Apéry reflection resolves the issue) is also not established.

## QPRS assessment

The quadratic-support/resultant route has the same missing gateway in a different form. The exact quadratic-support structure would need a bound on the number of actual Apéry zero-set pairs satisfying the relevant quadratic relation. The known symmetry

  b_r = b_{p-1-r} (mod p)

and the quadratic factorization information do not provide the needed uniform count. No actual-Apéry counterexample to QPRS was found; the route remains an unproved gateway rather than a disproved statement.

## Smallest surviving theorem

The strongest unconditional statement established here is:

For the actual Apéry zero sets, the SWR clean-range reduction is valid: every short multi-zero configuration with all reflection orbits away from the central and endpoint exceptional loci induces a common-root condition among shifted gap polynomials. Consequently, any future proof of a uniform shifted-gap resultant bound in this clean range would feed directly into the quadruple-corank argument.

This theorem is strictly weaker than the requested c=1/2 exponent because it leaves the exceptional loci and the required multi-polynomial root count unresolved.
