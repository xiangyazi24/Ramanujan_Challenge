# 5. Pairwise rational-row CRT lift of the moving zero test

The moving congruence has a common rational origin:

## Lemma 5.1 — pairwise SR2F row lift

Let \(\ell_1\ne\ell_2\) be two members of \(\mathcal R_{\delta;p,q,p'}\), put
\[
 M=\ell_1\ell_2,
\]
and let \(m\in[0,M)\) be the unique solution of
\[
 Cm\equiv-\delta\pmod M.
 \tag{5.2}
\]
Then
\[
 m\bmod\ell_i=s_{\ell_i}\in\mathcal Z_{\ell_i}
 \qquad(i=1,2),
 \tag{5.3}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\ell_1\ell_2\mid b_m.}
 \tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

Since \(C=pq\) is prime to each moving prime, (5.2) reduces modulo \(\ell_i\) to
\[
 m\equiv-\delta C^{-1}\equiv s_{\ell_i}\pmod{\ell_i}.
\]
The least base-\(\ell_i\) digit of \(m\) is therefore the actual zero digit \(s_{\ell_i}\).  Gessel--Lucas gives
\[
 b_m\equiv b_{s_{\ell_i}}\prod_{j\ge1}b_{m_j}\equiv0\pmod{\ell_i}.
\]
The primes are distinct, hence their product divides \(b_m\).  ∎

This lemma preserves the characteristic change exactly.  It does **not** prove SR2F, because the common integer row depends on the pair of moving primes.

Indeed, if \(K=|\mathcal R_{\delta;p,q,p'}|\), every ordered pair of distinct candidates contributes one incidence to the canonical second factorial integer-row moment.  Thus, after enlarging the harmless row interval to \(m\ll_vN^2\),
\[

 \le
 \sum_{m\ll_vN^2}(K_N(m))_2.
 \tag{5.5}
\]
The banked bound is
\[

 \ll N^2\lambda_N^2N^{o(1)}.
 \tag{5.6}
\]
With \(\lambda_N\ll N^{3/5+o(1)}/\log N\), this is of scale
\[

which is vastly too large to improve the pointwise \(N^{2/3}\) cap.  The pairwise CRT lift is therefore an exact structural reduction, but the presently banked HM2 is not the missing pointwise input.

# 6. Exact Möbius relation between selected moving primes

The first characteristic does have a useful consequence beyond the bare cardinality cap.

For every candidate,
\[
 r_\ell\ell\equiv A\pmod p.
 \tag{6.1}
\]
Take two candidates \(\ell_1,\ell_2\) whose standard fixed-\(p\) zero representatives satisfy
\[
 r_2=r_1+h,
 \qquad 1\le h<p.
 \tag{6.2}
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{A}{\ell_1}+h\equiv\frac{A}{\ell_2}\pmod p.
\]
Multiplying by \(\ell_1\ell_2\) gives
\[
 A\ell_2+h\ell_1\ell_2\equiv A\ell_1\pmod p,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
 (A+h\ell_1)\ell_2\equiv A\ell_1\pmod p.}
 \tag{6.3}
\]
The denominator \(A+h\ell_1\) cannot vanish on an actual nonwrapping pair: that would make \(r_2=0\), impossible because \(b_0=1\).  Hence
\[
\boxed{
 \ell_2\equiv\Phi_{A,h}(\ell_1)
 :=\frac{A\ell_1}{A+h\ell_1}\pmod p.}
 \tag{6.4}
\]

This is the reciprocal analogue actually forced by the determinant geometry.  It is a Möbius transformation, not an additive translation.  Both candidates still satisfy
\[
 s_{\ell_i}=-\delta C^{-1}\pmod{\ell_i}
 \in\mathcal Z_{\ell_i}.
 \tag{6.5}
\]
Thus any successful short-gap theorem must correlate actual Apéry zeros along (6.4) while the defining characteristic in (6.5) changes from \(\ell_1\) to \(\ell_2\).

# 9. Why the gap/resultant mechanism stops at (7.5)

Take a pair counted by \(E_h\).  In the fixed characteristic \(p\), the two actual zero labels give the genuine certificate
\[
 p\mid N_h(\rho).
 \tag{9.1}
\]
The moving prime endpoints satisfy
\[
 \ell_i\mid b_{s_i},
 \qquad
 \ell_i\mid\delta+Cs_i,
 \qquad i=1,2.
 \tag{9.2}
\]
Eliminating the two fixed-\(p\) labels gives precisely the Möbius relation (6.3); it does not turn (9.2) into another equation over \(\mathbb F_p\).

This is the first exact failed implication in the strongest gap-polynomial attack:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
 p\mid N_h(\rho),\\
 \ell_i\mid b_{s_i},\ \ell_i\mid\delta+Cs_i
\end{array}
\quad\not\Longrightarrow_{\rm current\ identities}\quad
\begin{array}{c}
\text{a second common polynomial over one fixed characteristic,}\
\text{or a common nonzero small-height integer charged by the }\ell_i.
\end{array}}
 \tag{9.3}
\]

There is no legitimate scalar resultant to take between \(N_h(\rho)\) in characteristic \(p\) and the two equations \(b_{s_i}=0\) in characteristics \(\ell_i\).  Replacing \(b_{s_i}\) by a polynomial in \(\rho\) would be exactly the unproved cross-characteristic transfer.

Lemma 5.1 shows the best exact common-characteristic rescue presently available: two moving tests can be CRT-lifted to a common integer row \(m\), but \(m\) depends on \((\ell_1,\ell_2)\).  Therefore resultants or integer-row moments do not give one reusable certificate at fixed \(h\).

This is not an actual counterexample to SR2F.  It is the first point where the proved identities cease to imply the desired saving.  The strictly smaller theorem (8.2) records exactly the additional correlation that would be needed.

# 10. Fourier and bilinear-character-sum audit

A direct reciprocal Fourier expansion does not improve even the first-condition cap uniformly.

Let \(\mathscr L\) be the moving shell primes before applying the moving zero test and, for simplicity, suppose the shell box gives bounded multiplicity modulo \(p\).  Then
\[
 I
 :=\sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L}
   \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_p}(A\ell^{-1})
 =\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}F_p(a)
   \sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L}e_p(-aA\ell^{-1}).
 \tag{10.1}
\]
Parseval gives
\[
 \sum_a|F_p(a)|^2=pZ(p),
 \tag{10.2}
\]
and bounded residue multiplicity gives
\[
 \sum_a\left|\sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L}
 e_p(-aA\ell^{-1})\right|^2
 \ll_v p|\mathscr L|.
 \tag{10.3}
\]
Cauchy therefore gives only
\[
 I\ll_v\sqrt{Z(p)|\mathscr L|},
 \tag{10.4}
\]
which at \(Z(p)\ll N^{2/3}\), \(|\mathscr L|\asymp N/\log N\) is of order
\[
 N^{5/6+o(1)},
\]
worse than the injective \(N^{2/3}\) bound.

If one inserts the literal moving-zero weight
\[
 w_\ell
 =\mathbf1_{\{s_\ell\in\mathcal Z_\ell\}},
\]
then the inner reciprocal sum in (10.1) becomes selected by a different Apéry zero set for every \(\ell\).  No canonical Parseval identity factors that weight from the inverse-prime phase.

This is exactly the horizontal obstruction isolated independently in `oracleA_result.tex`.  Its moving-reciprocal bilinear form contains terms of the form
\[
 F_p(k\bar q)F_q(k\bar p),
\]
with both zero sets and both reciprocal multipliers moving.  The required one-sided SDC bound is open.  The source proves that on doublets the local Fourier transforms are bounded-conductor trace functions, but explicitly leaves the horizontal distribution of the moving slopes as the load-bearing problem.  Its reflected anchored construction shows that palindromy, doublet size, vertical bounds, and generic large-sieve geometry alone do not force the needed reciprocal cancellation.

Thus there is no unconditional bilinear-character-sum theorem in the canonical source that yields a positive \(\kappa\) for SR2F.

# 11. Bettin--Chandee / operator audit

One might try to regard
\[
 \ell\mapsto A\ell^{-1}\pmod p
\]
as the Bettin--Chandee reciprocal phase and keep the moving zero test in a vector-valued coefficient.  Q4596 already audits the strongest automatic Hilbert-space extension of the scalar theorem.

The scalar theorem is an \(\ell^2\to\ell^2\) operator bound.  Hilbert amplification is dimension-free, but factorizing an arbitrary selected coefficient matrix through that Hilbert space produces exactly the nuclear norm \(S_1\), not the Frobenius norm \(S_2\).  For the actual four-distinct Apéry coefficient matrix the canonical norm ledger gives
\[
 \|A^\sharp\|_{S_2}^2\ll N^{-4/5+o(1)},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}A^\sharp\ll N^{2+o(1)},
\]
so
\[
 \|A^\sharp\|_{S_1}\ll N^{3/5+o(1)}.
\]
The resulting second-pass ledger is again
\[
 D\|A^\sharp\|_{S_1}^2
 \ll N^{161/100+o(1)}.
\]
No \(N^{-\kappa/2}\) appears.

Thus an operator-valued reciprocal estimate helps only after proving a genuinely new selected Schatten/nuclear saving.  That would be a different arithmetic theorem, not an automatic consequence of Bettin--Chandee.

# 12. Conditioning in the moving characteristic

Equation (3.2) shows why conditioning on the moving zero label is simultaneously powerful and insufficient.

Fixing \(s\) gives \(O_v(1)\) possible moving primes, because
\[
 \ell\mid\delta+Cs.
\]
But there may be a distinct \(s\) for every surviving \(\ell\).  The high-column theorem cannot help: after \(s\) is fixed, the characteristic fibre is already \(O(1)\); before \(s\) is fixed, the row itself moves.

Equivalently, the moving test is a single fixed **rational** row
\[
 -\delta/C
\]
reduced in every characteristic \(\ell\).  Q4627 proves a fixed-denominator rational **twin** theorem when two rational rows have the same fractional part and a fixed integer separation.  SR2F supplies only one nonstructural rational row in each moving characteristic.  Its reflected partner is forced by palindromy and therefore cannot serve as an independent second zero.  The fixed-denominator twin theorem consequently does not produce a one-row horizontal prime bound.

This is another formulation of the remaining problem: one needs a horizontal theorem for actual Apéry zeros on a selected rational row, correlated with the reciprocal subset cut out by \(\mathcal Z_p\).

# 13. Average and support substitutions that remain invalid

The following tempting replacements do not prove SR2F.

### 13.1 Marginal \(Z(\ell)/\ell\)

Replacing
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{s_\ell\in\mathcal Z_\ell\}}
\]
by \(Z(\ell)/\ell\) is an independence assumption.  The row \(s_\ell\) is selected by the same \(\ell\) and by the fixed determinant.  No canonical theorem states that it is uniformly distributed inside \(\mathbb F_\ell\).

### 13.2 Complete multiplier averaging

A complete average of \(\ell^{-1}\pmod p\) can exploit Kloosterman/large-sieve cancellation, but it replaces the selected set of \(\ell\)'s by all multipliers.  The actual moving zero condition is then gone.  This is precisely the substitution Q4627 forbids.

### 13.3 Determinant-support counting

The fact that a fixed \(s\) leaves \(O(1)\) moving primes is positive information, but summing over all possible \(s\) returns \(O(N)\).  More importantly, using absolute determinant support in the original TT* moment discards the signed \(d\)-kernel.  Q4627 quantified that loss and found it worse than the banked \(N^{161/100}\) bound.

### 13.4 Average \(3/5\) zero moment

As explained in Section 2, this is not uniform in the conditioned \(p\), and its available contribution is already present in \(Q_N\).  Spending it again as a fibre saving double-counts the same vertical information.

# 14. Scoped hostile evidence and what it does not prove

There are two exact no-go examples in the source/history, but neither is an actual asymptotic counterexample to SR2F.

1. Q4627's \(p=73\) reciprocal-label example uses the actual zeros \(2,70\in\mathcal Z_{73}\) to show that two reciprocal multipliers can land on widely separated representatives.  This refutes the proposed *translated-gap implication*.  It does not satisfy all SR2F moving tests and therefore does not refute SR2F itself.

2. Canonical `oracleA_result.tex` gives a reflected anchored family with palindromic doublets and bounded local trace-function conductor that nevertheless saturates the wrong moving-reciprocal geometry.  This is a hostile **interface** construction showing that palindromy, bounded zero-set size, and generic Fourier/large-sieve structure cannot imply the desired reciprocal cancellation.  It is not claimed to be the actual Apéry family.

No actual Apéry counterfamily with
\[
 |\mathcal R_{\delta;p,q,p'}|
 \ge N^{2/3-o(1)}
\]
is known from the connected source.

The theorem remains open, not false.

# 16. Classification

## PROVED

- Exact SR2F definition with both actual Apéry zero tests retained.
- Exact Q4627 Cauchy conversion \(\eta<\kappa/2-1/50\).
- Pointwise uniform cap \(|\mathcal R|\ll_vZ(p)\ll N^{2/3}\).
- The canonical \(3/5\) exponent is average, not a uniform fixed-\(p\) theorem.
- Moving-zero endpoint deletion (4.3), including the zero-carrier palindromic cases.
- Pairwise rational-row CRT lift (5.4), retaining the changing-characteristic zero tests exactly.
- Exact Möbius relation (6.3)--(6.4) between moving primes whose fixed-\(p\) zero labels differ by \(h\).
- SR2F gap decomposition (7.4).
- Exact selected short-gap cap \(E_h\le3(h-1)\).
- Formal exponent conversion \(\mathrm{SRG}_\sigma\Rightarrow\kappa=\sigma/(9-3\sigma)\).

## CONDITIONAL

- \(\mathrm{SR2F}_\kappa\) for every \(\kappa>0\).
- \(\mathrm{SRG}_\sigma\) for every \(\sigma>0\).
- The threshold \(\sigma>9/28\), equivalently \(\kappa>1/25\), closes the Q4627 route.
- Any stronger horizontal rational-row/Möbius correlation theorem that implies \(\mathrm{SRG}_\sigma\).

## REFUTED OR SCOPED NO-GO

- Reciprocal labels \(\Rightarrow\) translated Apéry twins of controlled additive gap.
- Average \(3/5\) zero exponent \(\Rightarrow\) uniform conditioned SR2F fibre cap.
- Marginal substitution of \(Z(\ell)/\ell\) for the literal moving zero test.
- Complete inverse-multiplier averaging after dropping the selected moving-characteristic condition.
- Generic affine resultant/codegree bounds that forget which characteristic defines each zero.
- Generic Fourier/trace-function bounded-conductor arguments without horizontal control of moving slopes.
- Automatic operator-valued Bettin--Chandee as an \(S_2\) theorem; the proved amplification pays \(S_1\).
- Pairwise CRT lifting plus the existing second factorial moment as a pointwise SR2F theorem.

## OPEN

The first genuinely local open statement is the selected reciprocal short-gap saving (8.2).  It asks whether the moving-\(\ell\) Apéry zero test suppresses, by a fixed power, the aggregate number of short gaps among the reciprocal fixed-\(p\) zero labels.  This is narrower than SR2F and retains exactly the cross-characteristic dependence that all generic substitutes lose.

**Problem 3.2 is not fully unconditional.**

```latex
\subsubsection{Selected reciprocal fibres and their Möbius short gaps}

Fix an admissible signed determinant $\delta$ and distinct shell primes
$p,q,p'$.  Put
\[
 C=pq,\qquad A\equiv\delta(p')^{-1}\pmod p,
\]
and for a moving shell prime $\ell\notin\{p,q,p'\}$ define
\[
 r_\ell\equiv A\ell^{-1}\pmod p,
 \qquad
 s_\ell\equiv-\delta C^{-1}\pmod\ell,
\]
using standard representatives.  Let $\mathcal R_{\delta;p,q,p'}$
be any subset of such primes for which
\[
 r_\ell\in\mathcal Z_p,
 \qquad s_\ell\in\mathcal Z_\ell,
\]
possibly with additional shell, reducedness, and sharp masks.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint part of a selected reciprocal fibre]
\label{lem:sr2f-endpoint}
Assume the determinant lies in the effective circular window
$|\delta|\ll N^{359/100+\varepsilon}$ with $\varepsilon>0$
sufficiently small.  Uniformly for $1\le U\le cN$,
\[
 \#\{\ell\in\mathcal R_{\delta;p,q,p'}:
       \min(s_\ell,\ell-1-s_\ell)\le U\}
 \ll U.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $0\le u\le U$.  If $s_\ell=u$, then
\[
 \ell\mid\delta+Cu,
\]
while if $s_\ell=\ell-1-u$, then
\[
 \ell\mid\delta-C(u+1).
\]
Every nonzero integer on the right has size $N^{4-o(1)}$ and hence
has at most three prime divisors in a fixed-ratio $N$-shell.  There is
at most one $u$ for which either displayed integer vanishes.  In a
vanishing case the actual zero condition, together with the Ap'ery
palindrome in the reflected case, gives $\ell\mid b_u$.  Since
$b_u<34^u$, the number of shell prime divisors of $b_u$ is
$O(u/\log N)$.  Summing over $u\le U$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pairwise rational-row lift]
\label{lem:sr2f-pair-crt}
Let $\ell_1\ne\ell_2$ belong to
$\mathcal R_{\delta;p,q,p'}$, put $M=\ell_1\ell_2$, and let
$0\le m<M$ be defined by
\[
 Cm\equiv-\delta\pmod M.
\]
Then
\[
 m\bmod\ell_i=s_{\ell_i}\in\mathcal Z_{\ell_i}
 \qquad(i=1,2),
\]
and
\[
 \ell_1\ell_2\mid b_m.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Reduction of the defining congruence modulo $\ell_i$ gives
$m\equiv s_{\ell_i}\pmod{\ell_i}$.  The least base-$\ell_i$ digit of
$m$ is therefore an Ap'ery zero.  Gessel--Lucas implies
$\ell_i\mid b_m$ for $i=1,2$, and the primes are distinct.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Möbius relation for reciprocal zero gaps]
\label{lem:sr2f-mobius-gap}
Let $\ell_1,\ell_2\in\mathcal R_{\delta;p,q,p'}$ and suppose their
standard fixed-$p$ zero labels satisfy
\[
 r_{\ell_2}=r_{\ell_1}+h<p.
\]
Then
\[
 (A+h\ell_1)\ell_2\equiv A\ell_1\pmod p,
\]
and the denominator $A+h\ell_1$ is nonzero modulo $p$.  Hence
\[
 \ell_2\equiv\frac{A\ell_1}{A+h\ell_1}\pmod p.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $r_{\ell_i}\ell_i\equiv A\pmod p$,
\[
 (A\ell_1^{-1}+h)\ell_2\equiv A\pmod p.
\]
Multiplication by $\ell_1$ proves the displayed congruence.  If its
denominator vanished, then $r_{\ell_1}+h\equiv0\pmod p$, contradicting
$r_{\ell_2}\in\mathcal Z_p$ and $b_0=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Selected reciprocal-gap decomposition]
\label{prop:sr2f-gap-decomposition}
Let $\rho_1<\cdots<\rho_R$ be the distinct values among the
$r_\ell$ with $\ell\in\mathcal R_{\delta;p,q,p'}$, and put
\[
 E_h=\#\{i:\rho_{i+1}-\rho_i=h\}.
\]
Then, for every $1\le H<p$,
\[
 R\le1+\frac pH+\sum_{2\le h\le H}E_h,
\]
and
\[
 E_h\le3(h-1).
\]
Consequently
\[
 R\ll1+\frac pH+H^2,
\]
and $R\ll p^{2/3}$ after optimizing $H$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At most $p/H$ consecutive gaps exceed $H$, since their sum is less
than $p$.  Every remaining gap is counted by some $E_h$.  If a
transition counted by $E_h$ starts at $\rho$, then
$\rho,\rho+h\in\mathcal Z_p$ with no wrap.  The gap criterion gives
$N_h(\rho)=0$ modulo $p$.  The polynomial $N_h$ is nonzero modulo $p$
and has degree $3(h-1)$, so there are at most $3(h-1)$ possible starts.
\end{proof}
```
