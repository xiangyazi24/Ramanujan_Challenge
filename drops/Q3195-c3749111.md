# Q3195 — p-adic first-jet/Mellit--Vlasenko audit

## Verdict

There is a clean **differentiated Mellit--Vlasenko theorem** if one first has an **integral Frobenius-compatible coefficient deformation** of the Laurent polynomial. In that setting the differentiated ghost expansion really does carry two independent p-adic weights:

1. the usual block defect `length(block)-1`; and
2. the absolute base-p digit position of the unique differentiated block.

The precise marked-block valuation is

```text
v_p(marked summand)
  >= sum_blocks (length(block)-1) + start_position(marked block).
```

That part is theorem-level and follows by running the coefficient-ring MV proof over dual numbers with Frobenius `epsilon -> p*epsilon`.

However, **the canonical Apéry first jet is not supplied by such a coefficient deformation**. Its natural parameter is the shift of the analytic index, `B(m+t)`, whereas MV controls constant terms of integer powers of an integral Laurent polynomial. Passing from an exponent/index shift to a coefficient deformation requires an additional theorem. The formal exponent derivative inserts `log Lambda`, which lies outside the integral Laurent-polynomial coefficient ring; the hypergeometric derivative contains harmonic denominators; and the shifted recurrence has nonunit denominators at exactly the p-adic places one must control.

There is a second, logically independent obstruction: even a valid marked-block expansion gives **lower bounds on individual summands**. The desired estimate

```text
w <= 2 k
```

is an **upper bound on the valuation after summing and cancelling all those summands**. MV's inequalities alone cannot prevent arbitrarily deep cancellation. A primitive suffix term, or equivalently a suffix first-jet Smith/Bezout theorem, is still required.

So the theorem-first answer is:

> **MV alone does not prove `w <= 2k`.** It supplies exactly the right two-weight mechanism for a Frobenius-compatible coefficient jet, but two genuinely new bridges are needed for the canonical energy: (i) an integral realization of the analytic-index/exterior jet in that category, and (ii) a noncancellation/Smith theorem for the changed `k`-digit suffix.

I do not find a theorem-level counterexample to `w <= 2k` in the current repository state, and the stated census is therefore not contradicted. I give below the exact counterexample target for `C=2` and an averaged substitute which would already be sufficient with the borrow-chain bound.

## 1. The canonical analytic-index jet and the exact energy Casoratian

Put

```text
T(x,r) = binom(x,r)^2 * binom(x+r,r)^2
```

and use the terminating/analytic Apéry interpolation

```text
B(x) = sum_{r>=0} T(x,r)
     = _4F_3(-x,-x,x+1,x+1; 1,1,1; 1).
```

At a nonnegative integer `n`, `T(n,r)=0` for `r>n`. More importantly, the factor `binom(x,r)^2` has a double zero at `x=n` when `r>n`, so those tail terms also have zero **first derivative**. Hence

```text
b_n = B(n),
J_n = B'(n)
```

has the finite exact formula

```text
J_n = 2 * sum_{r=0}^n
          binom(n,r)^2 binom(n+r,r)^2
          * (H_{n+r} - H_{n-r}).                    (1.1)
```

Let

```text
P(x) = 34*x^3 + 51*x^2 + 27*x + 5.
```

For the canonical contiguous relation

```text
(x+1)^3 B(x+1) - P(x) B(x) + x^3 B(x-1) = 0,        (1.2)
```

differentiation at `x=m` gives

```text
(m+1)^3 J_{m+1} - P(m) J_m + m^3 J_{m-1} = F_m,    (1.3)
```

because

```text
P'(m) = 102*m^2 + 102*m + 27
```

and therefore

```text
F_m
 = P'(m)b_m - 3*m^2 b_{m-1} - 3*(m+1)^2 b_{m+1}.
```

Now multiply (1.3) by `b_m` and subtract `J_m` times the ordinary Apéry recurrence. This gives the exact telescoping Wronskian identity

```text
b_m F_m
 = (m+1)^3 (b_m J_{m+1} - b_{m+1}J_m)
   - m^3   (b_{m-1}J_m - b_m J_{m-1}).              (1.4)
```

Since `E_0=0` and `E_{m+1}-E_m=b_mF_m`, (1.4) proves

```text
E_n = n^3 (b_{n-1}J_n - b_n J_{n-1}).              (1.5)
```

Thus the primitive energy is exactly the exterior first jet of the analytic-index family. This is the right object to lift p-adically. In particular, it is safer to work with the determinant in (1.5) than with the logarithmic jet `J_n/b_n`, whose denominator is a nonunit at precisely the primes of interest.

A useful dual-number form is

```text
B_n(t) = B(n+t) = b_n + t J_n      (mod t^2),
```

so that

```text
E_n/n^3
 = [t] ( b_{n-1} B_n(t) - b_n B_{n-1}(t) ).        (1.6)
```

Equation (1.6) also makes clear that if one can make the **exterior** jet integral before differentiating, one need not prove integrality of a normalized logarithmic derivative.

## 2. What can actually be differentiated in Mellit--Vlasenko

The repository already contains the needed coefficient-ring precedent in

```text
problems/3.2/research/proofs/Q32_FRANEL_TAIL_DWORK_PROOF.md
```

There the ghost construction is run over a coefficient ring with a Frobenius lift. An indecomposable block `A` of length `ell(A)` satisfies

```text
c_p(A) in p^(ell(A)-1) R,
```

and a block beginning at absolute digit position `s` contributes `phi^s(c_p(A))`. This is stronger information than the scalar specialization because the absolute position is retained by the Frobenius twist.

Here is the exact differentiated version.

### Theorem 2.1 — marked MV block formula over dual numbers

Fix a prime `p`. Let

```text
R_eps = Z_p[epsilon]/(epsilon^2)
```

with Frobenius lift

```text
phi(epsilon) = p*epsilon.
```

Equivalently, write a multiplicative parameter `q=1+epsilon`; then `phi(q)=q^p` modulo `epsilon^2`.

Let

```text
Lambda_eps(X) = Lambda(X) + epsilon * Lambda_dot(X)
```

be a Laurent polynomial with coefficients in `R_eps`, and define

```text
A_n(epsilon) = CT Lambda_eps^n.
```

For a base-p word `W`, let `Pi(W)` be the MV set of contiguous block partitions. Number digit positions from the least significant digit, so a block `C` starts at position `s(C)`. Running the coefficient-ring ghost proof gives

```text
A_W(epsilon)
 = sum_{pi in Pi(W)}
     product_{C in pi} phi^{s(C)} c_p(C;epsilon),    (2.1)
```

with

```text
c_p(C;epsilon) in p^(ell(C)-1) R_eps.               (2.2)
```

Taking the coefficient of `epsilon` yields

```text
[epsilon] A_W(epsilon)
 = sum_{pi in Pi(W)} sum_{C in pi}
       p^{s(C)} phi^{s(C)}([epsilon]c_p(C;epsilon))
       * product_{D in pi, D != C}
           phi^{s(D)} c_p(D;0).                     (2.3)
```

Consequently every marked summand in (2.3) has

```text
v_p(summand)
 >= omega(pi) + s(C),                               (2.4)

omega(pi) = sum_{D in pi} (ell(D)-1).
```

#### Proof

The coefficient-ring MV proof gives (2.1)--(2.2). Because `phi^s(epsilon)=p^s epsilon`,

```text
[epsilon] phi^s f(epsilon)
 = p^s phi^s([epsilon]f(epsilon)).
```

Apply the product rule to (2.1). The factor `p^{s(C)}` is the second weight, and (2.2) contributes the ordinary defect weight for every block. That is exactly (2.3)--(2.4).

### Padding behaves correctly

The user's fixed top-zero convention causes no fake derivative. For a genuine coefficient family,

```text
A_0(epsilon) = CT Lambda_eps^0 = 1,
```

hence the zero block remains

```text
c_p(0;epsilon)=1
```

identically and its marked derivative is zero. Thus forced singleton top-zero blocks cannot carry the mark.

### What sequence does this theorem differentiate?

It differentiates the **coefficient jet**

```text
D_n = [epsilon] CT (Lambda + epsilon Lambda_dot)^n
    = n * CT Lambda^(n-1) Lambda_dot.                (2.5)
```

Formula (2.4) is therefore a real theorem, but it applies to `D_n`, not automatically to `J_n=B'(n)`.

## 3. The canonical Apéry Laurent polynomial does not by itself supply the index jet

The current Apéry Cartier code uses the explicit Laurent polynomial

```text
Lambda(X,Y,Z)
 = X^(-1) Y^(-1) Z^(-1)
   (1+X)(1+Y)(1+Z)
   ((1+Y)(1+Z) + X*Y*Z),                            (3.1)
```

with

```text
CT Lambda^n = b_n.
```

This is implemented and independently coefficient-checked in

```text
problems/3.2/research/scripts/q32_cartier_packet_audit.py.
```

To turn Theorem 2.1 into a theorem about the canonical first jet, one would need an **additional bridge theorem** producing an integral Laurent-polynomial tangent `Lambda_dot` (or a more general integral Frobenius module) for which

```text
J_n = n * CT Lambda^(n-1) Lambda_dot                (3.2)
```

for every `n`, or at least whose exterior determinant agrees with (1.5).

MV itself does not produce such a `Lambda_dot`.

The natural canonical family is instead

```text
mathcal B_m(t) = B(m+t).                             (3.3)
```

It satisfies the shifted-index recurrence

```text
(m+1+t)^3 mathcal B_{m+1}(t)
 - P(m+t) mathcal B_m(t)
 + (m+t)^3 mathcal B_{m-1}(t) = 0,                 (3.4)
```

and `[t]` of (3.4) is exactly the first-jet equation (1.3). But (3.3) is an **index deformation**, not a coefficient deformation of (3.1).

That distinction is the first hard obstruction.

## 4. Precise algebraic reason MV at the special fiber cannot control a derivative

Suppose one knows only the scalar MV theorem at `t=0`:

```text
c_p(C;0) in p^(ell(C)-1) Z_p.                       (4.1)
```

There is no logical implication whatsoever for the derivative. For one block one may take, purely algebraically,

```text
c_p(C;t) = c_p(C;0) + t*u
```

with `u` any prescribed p-adic number. The specialization (4.1) is unchanged while `[t]c_p(C;t)=u` is arbitrary.

Therefore a differentiated block theorem requires the **stronger family statement**

```text
c_p(C;t) in p^(ell(C)-1) R_t                        (4.2)
```

in a coefficient ring carrying a Frobenius lift. The scalar theorem supplied in the question is not enough by itself.

There is another exact distinction. If the parameter has trivial Frobenius, `phi(t)=t`, then differentiation of `phi^s(c)` gives no factor `p^s`. The second weight in (2.4) appears only for a parameter whose Frobenius tangent is

```text
phi(t) = p*t + O(t^2),                              (4.3)
```

as for the multiplicative coordinate `q=1+t`, `q -> q^p`.

Thus the sought `+ digit position` valuation is not a formal consequence of “differentiate MV”; it depends on finding the **right Frobenius-compatible tangent direction**.

## 5. Every nonunit-denominator issue in the canonical index jet

The obstruction is not merely stylistic. Several nonunits appear if one tries to identify (3.3) with a ghost-coefficient deformation.

### 5.1 Generalized binomial / harmonic denominators

Differentiating the hypergeometric summand gives (1.1). Before all cancellations are assembled, the derivative contains

```text
H_{n+r}-H_{n-r}
 = sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n+r} 1/j.                         (5.1)
```

Whenever `p|j`, `1/j` is a p-adic nonunit denominator. The squared binomial factors may cancel such denominators in particular terms or after summation, but that cancellation is **not** the coefficientwise divisibility used by the MV ghost proof. One must prove it inside the family before invoking (2.2).

Equivalently, `binom(x,r)` is integer-valued on integers but is a polynomial with denominator `r!`; integer-valuedness does not imply that its derivative is p-integral. A generic example is

```text
(d/dx) binom(x,p) at x=0 = (-1)^(p-1)/p.
```

So an integer-valued parameter family is not enough; the tangent itself must live in the correct integral coefficient ring.

### 5.2 Formal exponent differentiation inserts `log Lambda`

The tempting formal identity

```text
(d/dt) Lambda^(n+t) at t=0 = Lambda^n log Lambda   (5.2)
```

leaves the MV category. For the explicit (3.1), `log Lambda` contains the logarithm of the Laurent monomial and logarithms of factors such as `1+X`. The latter expand as

```text
log(1+X) = X - X^2/2 + X^3/3 - ...,
```

with denominators divisible by `p` at indices divisible by `p`; the monomial logarithm is not a Laurent polynomial at all. Thus (5.2) is not an element of the integral Laurent polynomial algebra to which the ghost theorem applies.

I am **not** claiming that no more sophisticated crystalline realization of the index jet exists. I am claiming the precise narrower point needed here: such a realization is an additional theorem and does not follow from scalar MV.

### 5.3 The shifted recurrence has a nonunit forward denominator

Solving (3.4) forward divides by

```text
(m+1+t)^3.
```

At `t=0` this is noninvertible in `Z_p` whenever `p|(m+1)`. Those are exactly the indices crossed by base-p borrow chains, so this denominator cannot be dismissed.

The repository's division-free value recurrence avoids this by setting

```text
C_m = (m!)^3 b_m,
C_{m+1} = P(m) C_m - m^6 C_{m-1},                  (5.3)
```

as used, for example, in `problems/3.2/CRON_zp_bigscan.c`. One can similarly scale the jet, but `(m!)^3` then contributes a large known p-adic valuation. Any theorem for the original `w` must subtract that factorial valuation exactly; it cannot silently compare the scaled and unscaled jets.

### 5.4 Dividing by `b_n` is forbidden at the target prime

A logarithmic jet such as

```text
J_n / b_n
```

has the worst possible denominator for the present problem: `b_n` itself. The Casoratian (1.5) is therefore the canonical integral object; any proof that first divides by `b_n` must later recover precisely the valuation it discarded.

### 5.5 Dividing by `n^3` is also not harmless

Equation (1.5) says

```text
E_n = n^3 W_n.
```

At primes dividing `n`, replacing `E_n` by `W_n=E_n/n^3` loses `3v_p(n)`. A p-adic proof must retain the `n^3` factor or account for it explicitly.

### 5.6 Difference quotients need their own Dwork congruence

One might try to define the index derivative p-adically by

```text
(B(n+p^r)-B(n))/p^r.                                (5.4)
```

Division by `p^r` is legal only after proving the numerator is divisible by `p^r` in the appropriate family, and one must then identify the limit with the canonical `B'(n)`.

The Franel proof in `Q32_FRANEL_TAIL_DWORK_PROOF.md` succeeds in an analogous situation precisely because it first proves a coefficient-ring Frobenius congruence and only **then** takes integral p-adic difference quotients. The scalar Apéry MV block valuation does not, by itself, provide the translated-index congruence required for (5.4).

## 6. Why the marked two-weight theorem still does not imply `w <= 2k`

Assume optimistically that the canonical jet had already been placed in the setting of Theorem 2.1. Then a marked partition term has a lower bound

```text
omega(pi) + s(mark).                                (6.1)
```

This is excellent for proving congruences: when two indices agree above the bottom `k` digits, marks wholly in the common high prefix acquire large absolute-position weights, and only the changed suffix can contribute at shallow depth.

But the desired statement is of the opposite logical type.

Let the chosen `h` attain

```text
lambda = min(v_p(b_n),v_p(b_{n-h}))
```

and minimize the common-prefix depth

```text
k = min{j>=1 : floor(n/p^j)=floor((n-h)/p^j)}.
```

In the nontrivial case `v_p(E_n)>=2lambda`, put

```text
X_p = p^(-lambda)  b_n,
Y_p = p^(-2lambda) E_n.                             (6.2)
```

Then

```text
w = min(v_p(X_p), v_p(Y_p)).                        (6.3)
```

Thus `w<=2k` says that **after all block sums have been taken**, `X_p` and `Y_p` cannot both vanish modulo `p^(2k+1)`.

MV only says that each constituent term begins at some minimum valuation. It gives no upper bound on the valuation of their sum. The logical gap is exact: two terms

```text
p^a*u
and
-p^a*u + p^(a+M)
```

both satisfy the lower bound `>=a`, while their sum has valuation `a+M`, with `M` arbitrary. Nothing in the inequality `v_p(c_p(C))>=ell(C)-1` rules out this cancellation.

This is why a second theorem is unavoidable.

### Missing theorem: suffix first-jet Smith/Bezout bound

A useful exact formulation is:

> For the `k` changed base-p digits, after removing the actual endpoint value factor `p^lambda` and the corresponding quadratic energy factor `p^(2lambda)`, the integral suffix value/exterior-jet module has last determinantal divisor of valuation at most `2k`.

Equivalently, one wants a Bezout relation constructed **from the k-digit suffix block algebra**

```text
U * X_p + V * Y_p = p^e,
U,V in Z_p,
0 <= e <= 2k.                                       (6.4)
```

Equation (6.4) immediately gives `w=e<=2k`. The substantive condition is that `U,V,e` are produced from the suffix ghost/jet matrix before knowing the gcd; otherwise (6.4) is just the tautological Bezout description of `w`.

This is the exact place where a `2` can naturally arise: a first jet is a rank-two tangent extension, so a k-digit suffix can plausibly contribute at most two elementary-divisor units per digit. But that sentence is only motivation. The theorem that the corresponding determinant is primitive is not in MV and is not in the current repository.

## 7. Higher p-power inheritance is explicitly unsafe

Nothing above uses the false implication

```text
v_p(b_{c p^k + u}) >= v_p(b_c).
```

The supplied p=5 example already forbids it:

```text
v_5(b_11) = 4,
v_5(b_55) = v_5(b_56) = 3.
```

The current Cartier packet audit contains the same warning in another form: for `p=17`, `r=3`, one has

```text
v_17(b_3)=2,
```

but an individual packet coordinate at the lifted index has only one factor of 17, and the first shell also has only one factor. Thus mod-p Cartier/MV factorization does not promote to termwise inheritance of the full p-adic valuation.

This is exactly why the normalization by the **actual** `lambda` in the question is the correct one. Any proof of `w<=2k` must work after that actual normalization, not replace it by an inherited prefix valuation.

## 8. The precise averaged theorem that would already suffice

Let `P_n` be the relevant primes occurring in the actual value-window carrier, and for each such prime make the question's choice of attaining `h_p` and minimal `k_p`. Then identically

```text
sum_{p in P_n} w_p log p
 <= 2 * sum_{p in P_n} k_p log p
    + sum_{p in P_n} (w_p-2k_p)_+ log p.            (8.1)
```

Therefore the pointwise `w_p<=2k_p` can be weakened to

```text
sum_{p in P_n} (w_p-2k_p)_+ log p = o(n),           (8.2)
```

or to whatever smaller error is required after inserting the already available borrow-chain estimate for `sum k_p log p`.

A particularly concrete route to (8.2) is a single integer carrier.

### Sufficient carrier theorem

Construct a nonzero integer `Delta_n` such that, for every relevant prime,

```text
v_p(Delta_n) >= (w_p-2k_p)_+,                       (8.3)
```

and prove

```text
log |Delta_n| = o(n).                               (8.4)
```

Then

```text
sum_p (w_p-2k_p)_+ log p
 <= log |Delta_n| = o(n).                           (8.5)
```

The natural candidate `Delta_n` would be a primitive determinant/resultant of the **normalized suffix marked-block coefficients**. The marked MV theorem is relevant here because it identifies which suffix coefficients can survive after the `2k` baseline is removed. What MV does **not** supply is the nonzero primitive determinant or the subexponential height (8.4).

This averaged carrier is the clean fallback if the pointwise suffix Smith bound is false.

## 9. Exact counterexample target for `C=2`

There is no need to test any false prefix inheritance. A counterexample to `w<=2k` is exactly a tuple

```text
(p,n,h)
```

such that

1. `h` attains the maximal value-window exponent `lambda`;
2. among attaining `h`, it has minimal `k` in the question's sense; and
3. simultaneously

```text
v_p(b_n) >= lambda + 2k + 1,                        (9.1)
v_p(E_n) >= 2lambda + 2k + 1.                      (9.2)
```

Then `w>=2k+1`.

This is the canonical hostile search target. It does not ask for any valuation of `b_{floor(n/p^k)}`, does not assume full-prefix inheritance, and uses exactly the normalization appearing in the theorem to be proved.

If one wants to falsify the *marked-block proof mechanism* before finding an actual `w` counterexample, the sharper local target is: after factoring the predicted `2k` suffix weight, show that **every** minimal-weight value/exterior-jet minor vanishes modulo `p`. Such an example would refute the required primitive suffix minor while leaving the numerical inequality `w<=2k` possibly true by another mechanism.

## 10. Recommended next theorem, in dependency order

The right next attack is not to differentiate the already-specialized scalar MV identity. It is:

### Step A — build the correct integral tangent

Prove one of the following.

**A1. Coefficient realization.** Find an integral Frobenius-compatible Laurent/crystalline family whose tangent gives `J_n`, i.e. prove (3.2), or whose **exterior** tangent gives `E_n` directly via (1.5).

**A2. Index-jet Dwork theorem.** Work directly with `B(n+t)` and prove a coefficient-ring Dwork congruence strong enough that the relevant p-adic difference quotients are integral and converge to the canonical jet. This is analogous in logic to the derivative step in `Q32_FRANEL_TAIL_DWORK_PROOF.md`, but it is not a formal corollary of the scalar Apéry MV theorem.

The exterior version is preferable because it avoids division by `b_n` and may allow the harmonic/nonunit pieces of `J` to cancel before the p-adic valuation is taken.

### Step B — prove the marked block formula

Once A is in place, Theorem 2.1 gives the two weights essentially for free:

```text
block defect + absolute mark position.
```

### Step C — prove primitive suffix noncancellation

For the bottom `k` changed digits, form the normalized value/exterior-jet suffix matrix and prove a primitive minor / Smith exponent bound `<=2k`. This is the genuinely new arithmetic statement needed for the pointwise theorem.

If C fails pointwise, retain its determinant as the carrier `Delta_n` and prove the averaged height bound (8.4).

## Bottom line

The differentiable ghost theorem one wants is real and has the exact two-weight formula (2.3)--(2.4), but it does **not** presently apply to the canonical analytic-index jet. And even after that bridge is built, the inequality `w<=2k` does not follow from MV lower bounds without a suffix noncancellation/Smith statement.

So `C=2` remains a viable conjectural bound under the stated census, but its load-bearing theorem should be formulated as a **rank-two suffix Smith/Bezout bound**, not as full-prefix valuation inheritance and not as a formal differentiation of the scalar MV expansion.
