# Q806 hostile audit of LGTQ-RECT canonical coupling

## Verdict

The requested stronger conclusion is not currently derivable from the four-prime condition plus the known Apéry-Lucas congruences. The correct audit result is a scoped no-go theorem: congruences modulo the individual prime factors do not produce a global congruence modulo their product without an additional compatibility identity. The existing canonical coupling data are insufficient to force a factor below the Selberg scale.

## 1. What the local identities actually give

For a rectangle with four distinct primes

\[
 p_i=(n-u_i)/q,
\]

and

\[
 p_i\mid b_{u_i},\qquad p_i\mid b_n,
\]

Lucas/Gessel-type lifting gives, for each i separately,

\[
 b_n\equiv b_q(b_{u_i}+2q p_iD_{u_i})\pmod {p_i^2}.
\]

After writing b_{u_i}=p_i z_i this gives

\[
 b_n/p_i\equiv b_q(z_i+2qD_{u_i})\pmod {p_i}.
\]

This is four independent residue statements.

## 2. CRT saturation check

Let

\[
R=\prod_i p_i,
\]

and suppose b_n=RB.

Multiplying the i-th congruence by R/p_i gives

\[
(R/p_i)B\equiv b_q(z_i+2qD_{u_i})\pmod {p_i}.
\]

The left side is meaningful modulo p_i, but the four equations do not imply an equality

\[
B=F(b_q,D_{u_1},...,D_{u_4})
\]

in Z or modulo R.

The missing step would require the four right hand sides to be the reductions of one canonical integer. The variables z_i are independent local lifts. CRT only says that compatible residues determine a class modulo R; it does not produce a small-height representative.

## 3. Vandermonde/cofactor relations do not remove saturation

The weights

\[
w_i=(R/p_i)\bmod p_i
\]

may be expressed using the residue locations u_i. Since

\[
p_i=(n-u_i)/q,
\]

we have

\[
p_j-p_i=(u_i-u_j)/q.
\]

Hence

\[
\prod_{j\ne i}(p_i-p_j)=(-1)^{T-1}q^{-(T-1)}\prod_{j\ne i}(u_i-u_j).
\]

The sign comes from

\[
p_i-p_j=(u_j-u_i)/q.
\]

These are valid interpolation weights. However, Lagrange identities apply to polynomials in u_i. They do not constrain the independent Apéry quotients z_i unless one proves that z_i has low-degree algebraic dependence on u_i.

No such identity is present in the cited files.

## 4. Explicit saturation model

Take four formal primes p_1,...,p_4 and choose arbitrary residues

\[
z_i\in Z/p_iZ.
\]

Define local data by

\[
b_n=R,
\]

and choose

\[
D_i\equiv b_q^{-1}(B(R/p_i)^{-1}-z_i)/(2q)\pmod {p_i}
\]

whenever the inverses exist.

Then every local lifting congruence holds. The same common integer B is used for all four primes, and the Vandermonde weights are fixed by the p_i. Nevertheless B can be chosen with arbitrary size and no divisibility gain beyond R follows.

Thus the data

* one common B,
* four Apéry-Lucas congruences,
* Vandermonde weights,
* canonical D_i values modulo p_i,

do not imply an extra factor.

## 5. What would be sufficient

A genuinely new theorem would need an additional global statement, for example:

\[
\sum_i c_i z_i
\]

is forced to equal a canonical integer of height o(log R), or a polynomial relation among the z_i and D_i of degree independent of T.

Such a statement would be stronger than the current LGTQ-RECT reduction and is not contained in the audited files.

## 6. Small-packet symbolic sanity check

For T=4, the interpolation identities only give relations such as

\[
\sum_i \frac{f(u_i)}{\prod_{j\ne i}(u_i-u_j)}=0
\]

for low-degree f. Substituting f=z or f=D requires z_i or D_i to be values of a common low-degree polynomial. The congruences provide no such polynomial representation.

Therefore the symbolic packet already exhibits the obstruction: local divisibility plus common b_n does not imply global interpolation collapse.

## Conclusion

The correct result is a scoped no-go theorem. The canonical coupling is real, but it is only a collection of CRT-compatible local constraints. Until an additional global interpolation theorem controlling the Apéry quotients z_i exists, LGTQ-RECT cannot be improved below the critical four-linear-form scale by this route alone.
