## 2. The actual fixed toric coefficient vector

The fixed Laurent polynomial already present in the repository is

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)
=\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz},
\tag{2.1}
\]

with

\[
\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^j=b_j.
\tag{2.2}
\]

It is convenient to rewrite it as

\[
\Lambda
=x^{-1}y^{-1}z^{-1}(1+x)(1+y)^2(1+z)^2
 +(1+x)(1+y)(1+z).
\tag{2.3}
\]

For `u=(u_1,u_2,u_3) in Z^3`, define the integral coefficient vector

\[
c_j(u)=[x^{u_1}y^{u_2}z^{u_3}]\Lambda^j.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Let

\[
S=\operatorname{supp}(c_1).
\]

From (2.3), `|S|=18+8-4=22`, and explicitly

\[
\begin{aligned}
c_1(i,j,k)
={}&\mathbf 1_{\{i\in\{-1,0\},\ j,k\in\{-1,0,1\}\}}
   \binom{2}{j+1}\binom{2}{k+1}\\
&+\mathbf 1_{\{i,j,k\in\{0,1\}\}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
\]

In particular

\[
c_1(-1,-1,-1)=1,
\tag{2.6}
\]

so the row

\[
w=(c_1(u))_{u\in S}\in\mathbf Z^{22}
\tag{2.7}
\]

is primitive.

For every `r>=1`, convolution of Laurent coefficients gives the exact characteristic-zero identity

\[
\boxed{
 b_r=c_r(0)=\sum_{u\in S}c_1(u)c_{r-1}(-u).
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

Thus, with

\[
v_r=(c_{r-1}(-u))_{u\in S}\in\mathbf Z^{22},
\tag{2.9}
\]

we have simply

\[
\boxed{b_r=w\cdot v_r.}
\tag{2.10}
\]

There are only 22 terms in this contraction. That fact is exact, but it does **not** turn the zero of the sum into the zero of any one term.

For a surviving packet prime, (1.10) gives `0<=r_p<=p-2`, so the fixed-torus orthogonality theorem applies without endpoint contamination:

\[
-\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}\Lambda(x,y,z)^{r_p}
\equiv b_{r_p}\pmod p.
\tag{2.11}
\]

Therefore the target event is exactly

\[
\boxed{
I_p:=\mathbf 1_{p\mid b_{r_p}}
 =\mathbf 1_{\{w\cdot v_{r_p}\equiv0\pmod p\}}.
}
\tag{2.12}
\]

There is also a useful packet interpretation. Since

\[
\gcd(r_p,p-1)=\gcd(ga,gN)=g,
\tag{2.13}
\]

the Kummer/Teichmuller character with exponent `r_p=ga` has exact order

\[
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(r_p,p-1)}=N.
\tag{2.14}
\]

Thus the surviving target is genuinely a **primitive order-`N` marked character**, and reflection `a -> N-a` is the inverse-character pair.

## 3. Scoped no-go: a primitive fixed row cannot produce a smaller identity-level projector

This is the cleanest exact obstruction to the natural `edge content + toric coefficients` combination.

### Proposition 3.1 (fixed polynomial projector no-go)

Let

\[
L(X)=\sum_{u\in S}c_1(u)X_u\in\mathbf Z[X_u:u\in S].
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because `w` is primitive, `L` is a primitive linear form.

Let `F in Z[X_u:u in S]` be a **fixed** polynomial, independent of `p,r,N,n`, with the identity-level property

\[
L(x)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(x)=0
\qquad
\text{for every }x\in\overline{\mathbf Q}^{22}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then there exists `H in Z[X_u:u in S]` such that

\[
\boxed{F=L H.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently, on the actual Apéry coefficient vectors,

\[
\boxed{F(v_r)=b_r H(v_r).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

So every such universal polynomial upper-carrier contains the old scalar `b_r` as a factor; it cannot be a strictly smaller carrier.

### Proof

Since `w` is primitive, it extends to a unimodular basis of `Z^{22}`. Equivalently there is `U in GL_22(Z)` carrying `L` to the first coordinate `Y_1`.

After this integral change of variables, condition (3.2) says that the transformed polynomial vanishes identically when `Y_1=0`. Therefore every monomial with `Y_1`-degree zero has zero coefficient, so `Y_1` divides the transformed polynomial in `Z[Y_1,...,Y_22]`. Transforming back gives (3.3). Substituting `X=v_r` and using (2.10) gives (3.4). `square`

### Linear corollary

If fixed rows `lambda_1,...,lambda_t in Z^{22}` satisfy

\[
w\cdot x=0\Longrightarrow\lambda_i\cdot x=0
\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbf Q^{22},
\tag{3.5}
\]

then

\[
\lambda_i=k_iw\qquad(k_i\in\mathbf Z).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Hence

\[
\gcd_i(\lambda_i\cdot v_r)
=|b_r|\,\gcd_i|k_i|.
\tag{3.7}
\]

So even allowing several fixed linear projectors and then taking their content gives exactly the old scalar, up to a fixed integer.

### Scope

This proposition does **not** rule out a new relation that holds only on the special trajectory

\[
\{v_r:r\ge1\}\subset\mathbf Z^{22}
\tag{3.8}
\]

and not on the whole hyperplane `L=0`. But proving such a relation would itself be a genuinely new theorem about the toric coefficient trajectory. It is not supplied by primitivity, by the packet identity, or by the edge-content equality.

This is exactly the point at which the obvious projector route stops.

## 6. Scoped DFT/Galois no-go survives the q=1 packet specialization

Here is the exact algebraic reason the packet identity does not convert the marked primitive coordinate into a cheap characteristic-zero scalar.

Fix `N` and let `K=Q(zeta_N)`. Abstract the primitive DFT coordinates as `Y_a`, indexed by

\[
a\in(\mathbf Z/N\mathbf Z)^\times.
\]

The Galois group acts transitively by

\[
\sigma_t(Y_a)=Y_{ta},
\qquad t\in(\mathbf Z/N\mathbf Z)^\times.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Suppose a `Q`-defined scalar polynomial `F` is Galois invariant and is forced to vanish on the marked coordinate hyperplane `Y_a=0`. Then for every `t`, invariance forces it to vanish on

\[
Y_{ta}=0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

In the natural coordinate ring the distinct linear factors therefore all divide `F`. Without using reflection this costs the full primitive orbit `phi(N)`; if the only identification available is the proved inverse pair `a~ -a`, the degree is still at least

\[
\boxed{\frac{\varphi(N)}2.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For the surviving packet,

\[
N=n^{1-o(1)}
\tag{6.4}
\]

and the standard lower bound for Euler's totient gives

\[
\varphi(N)=N^{1-o(1)}=n^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus a rational scalar completion of a single primitive marked coordinate still has degree `n^{1-o(1)}` per long packet. On a `g=1` candidate family with linear possible logarithmic mass, the aggregate orbit-completion cost is the supplied `n^{2-o(1)}` scale, not a sublinear terminal carrier.

This is only the scoped DFT/Galois no-go just described; I am not claiming a universal impossibility theorem for every nonlinear arithmetic invariant.

## 7. The tempting second-moment reduction has a proved diagonal — but is exactly equivalent to SKPMIX_1

For each surviving prime define

\[
Z_p^{\rm prim}
:=\{a\in(\mathbf Z/N_p\mathbf Z)^\times:
      p\mid b_{g_pa}\},
\tag{7.1}
\]

\[
\mu_p:=\frac{|Z_p^{\rm prim}|}{\varphi(N_p)},
\qquad
\delta_p:=I_p-\mu_p.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The supplied mean estimate is

\[
M_n:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_n^*}\mu_p\log p=o(n).
\tag{7.3}
\]

The target mass on survivors is

\[
T_n:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_n^*}I_p\log p
=S_n+M_n,
\tag{7.4}
\]

where

\[
S_n:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_n^*}\delta_p\log p.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus `SKPMIX_1` is exactly `S_n=o(n)`.

Now square it. Put

\[
D_n:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_n^*}\delta_p^2(\log p)^2
\tag{7.6}
\]

and the ordered off-diagonal

\[
C_n:=\sum_{\substack{p,\ell\in\mathcal P_n^*\\p\ne\ell}}
\delta_p\delta_\ell\log p\log\ell.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Exactly,

\[
\boxed{S_n^2=D_n+C_n.}
\tag{7.8}
\]

The diagonal is unconditional. Since `|delta_p|<=1`, (1.13) gives

\[
D_n
\le (\log n)\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_n^*}\log p
=O(n\log n)
=o(n^2).
\tag{7.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
S_n=o(n)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C_n=o(n^2).
}
\tag{7.10}
\]

The reverse implication follows from (7.8)-(7.9); the forward implication follows by subtracting the diagonal.

So the apparently smaller statement

> “prove the diagonal and only estimate the cross-prime off-diagonal at scale `o(n^2)`”

is **not** strictly weaker than `SKPMIX_1`. It is precisely its squared form after an already-negligible diagonal is removed.

The same phenomenon occurs without centering. If

\[
D_n^0:=\sum_p I_p(\log p)^2=O(n\log n)
\tag{7.11}
\]

and

\[
P_n^0:=\sum_{p\ne\ell}I_pI_\ell\log p\log\ell,
\tag{7.12}
\]

then

\[
T_n^2=D_n^0+P_n^0,
\tag{7.13}
\]

so

\[
T_n=o(n)\iff P_n^0=o(n^2).
\tag{7.14}
\]

Thus the uncentered pair-incidence statistic is merely the terminal target squared; it is not a new aggregate premise either.

This also answers the “fewer terms” possibility coming from (2.8): although `b_r` is a 22-term fixed contraction, the exact zero detector over `F_p` is

\[
\mathbf1_{\{b_r=0\}}
=1-b_r^{p-1}
\qquad(b_r\in\mathbf F_p),
\tag{7.15}
\]

or equivalently an additive-character average with `p` characters. Because `p\asymp n`, exact zero detection immediately reintroduces degree/length `Theta(n)`. The 22-term linear representation by itself does not yield a bounded-complexity indicator.

## 8. Why no same-prime `L^2` or coefficient-energy estimate closes the gap

The fixed vector identity controls an exact cancellation

\[
w\cdot v_{r_p}\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{8.1}
\]

A norm bound on `v_r`, a Parseval identity for its DFT, or a Weil bound for a single toric trace controls the **size** of coordinates/traces. The event in (8.1) is instead an anti-concentration question at one residue class. Small or bounded second moment does not prevent a linear number of exact zeros at moving primitive characters.

This is also why the characteristic-zero lifted toric trace is not enough. The repository already records examples where the lifted trace is nonzero although its marked reduction is zero modulo `p`. Thus ordinary complex Frobenius/Sato-Tate equidistribution does not directly estimate the divisibility event. The needed object is the marked `p`-adic/crystalline coordinate together with horizontal control as `p` changes.

## 9. The first genuinely new theorem

The first genuinely new theorem is therefore not another scalar Green identity. It is a **two-characteristic horizontal dispersion theorem** for the marked primitive Kummer condition.

A concrete sufficient quantitative version would be the following.

### HXD_1 (sufficient source-level form)

There exists some fixed `eta>0` such that, uniformly in `n`, for the long surviving packet set,

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{\substack{p,\ell\in\mathcal P_n^*\\p\ne\ell}}
\delta_p\delta_\ell\log p\log\ell
\right|
\ll n^{2-\eta}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Here each `delta_p` is attached to the marked primitive order-`N_p` toric/Kummer coordinate

\[
-\sum_{(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}\Lambda^{g_pa_p}
\equiv b_{g_pa_p}\pmod p.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Then (7.8)-(7.9) give

\[
|S_n|
\ll n^{1-\eta/2}+\sqrt{n\log n}
=o(n),
\tag{9.3}
\]

and (7.3)-(7.4) give `T_n=o(n)`, hence by (1.1)

\[
\log W_1=o(n).
\tag{9.4}
\]

Of course (9.1) is quantitatively stronger than the bare endpoint `C_n=o(n^2)`. Its value is that it is a plausible **source theorem** to prove by arithmetic geometry / horizontal large sieve, rather than a renamed terminal premise.

A merely qualitative assertion `C_n=o(n^2)` is not a new theorem package: by (7.10) it is exactly `SKPMIX_1` in disguise.

At source level, HXD_1 must couple two different characteristics `p` and `ell`, two moving orders `N_p,N_ell=n^{1-o(1)}`, and the packet-linked primitive exponents `a_p,a_ell`. No fixed-prime edge identity, no single-characteristic toric Parseval formula, and no primitive DFT completion over one `N` supplies this coupling.

## 10. Precise boundary of the no-go

What is ruled out by the argument above:

- replacing the exact edge scalar by the same edge content under another normalization;
- adding finitely many fixed linear toric projectors that are universally forced by `b_r=0`;
- adding finitely many fixed polynomial toric projectors whose vanishing is an identity-level consequence of the target hyperplane;
- using only the reflected pair `a,N-a` as if it were two independent primitive samples;
- completing one marked primitive DFT coordinate to a rational scalar without paying the primitive Galois orbit;
- claiming that a proved `O(n log n)` diagonal plus an `o(n^2)` cross term is weaker than `SKPMIX_1`.

What is **not** ruled out:

- a new special identity satisfied only by the actual Apéry coefficient trajectory `v_r` and not by arbitrary vectors on the target hyperplane;
- a genuinely matrix-valued/projective invariant that creates a second independent marked condition before taking scalar content;
- a two-characteristic `p`-adic/crystalline large-sieve theorem;
- a horizontal theorem exploiting a relation between different `N_p`'s that is invisible to the one-`N` Galois orbit.

Any of those would be genuinely new mathematics. The present package does not contain one.
