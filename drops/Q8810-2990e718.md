# Q8810: Sub-Poisson tails of the Apéry shell-prime count

## Executive conclusion

The numerical observation is important, but the interpretation needs one correction: a smaller exponential moment at finite cutoff does not by itself prove a hard cutoff or a genuine asymptotic sub-Poisson law. The most likely explanation is not a single mechanism but a combination of:

1. the exact local structure of the Apéry zero sets $Z_p$;
2. strong higher-order correlations among the congruence events;
3. the fact that the shell primes are not independent random obstacles because they are generated by one global integer $n$.

The right mathematical object is the full correlation hierarchy
\[
M_k=\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_k} \Pr(n\bmod p_i\in Z_{p_i}\ \forall i),
\]
not only the first moment. Proving exponential moments requires controlling all $k$-point correlations.

The observation is therefore a strong guide: the missing theorem is probably a high-order anti-clustering estimate, not a fourth moment estimate.

---

## 1. Why the body is Poisson but the tail is not

For a fixed prime $p$, define
\[
X_p(n)=1_{b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p}.
\]
Then
\[
S(n)=\sum_p X_p(n).
\]

The Poisson heuristic comes from
\[
\mathbb E X_p\approx |Z_p|/p\approx 1/p,
\]
and the approximate independence of finitely many distinct primes under CRT.

However, the tail depends on simultaneous events with many primes:
\[
\mathbb E e^{tS}=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(e^t-1)^k}{k!}\sum_{p_1<\cdots<p_k}\mathbb E(X_{p_1}\cdots X_{p_k}).
\]

The first few terms only see low-order statistics. The observed agreement for $S=0,1,2,3$ says the first correlations are close to independent. The suppression at $S\ge5$ says that high-order intersections are much smaller than the naive CRT product.

This is exactly the signature of a rare-event process with repulsion.

---

## 2. Possible sources of repulsion

### (a) No adjacent zeros

The theorem
\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p\implies b_{r+1}\not\equiv0\pmod p
\]
(and similarly backwards) is a real local exclusion rule.

But by itself it is not enough. Different primes correspond to different residue systems. The implication
\[
n\bmod p\approx n\bmod q
\]
does not directly force adjacent forbidden states.

The mechanism can still contribute indirectly: the Apéry recurrence produces a highly structured forbidden set, not a random subset of density $1/p$.

### (b) Reflection symmetry

The symmetry
\[
r\mapsto p-1-r
\]
means $Z_p$ is not a random subset of residues. It has arithmetic pairing constraints. Such constraints reduce the number of compatible CRT configurations.

### (c) Sieve interpretation

This is the closest analogy. Classical sieve theory studies products
\[
\prod_p(1-X_p)
\]
where local conditions interact through arithmetic structure. Here the variable is the opposite: counting hits rather than avoiding them.

A Selberg-sieve type upper bound for
\[
\sum_n \prod_{i=1}^k X_{p_i}(n)
\]
would directly give the needed exponential moment control.

---

## 3. Can we prove a Poisson-defect bound?

The desired statement would be something like
\[
\mathbb E e^{tS}\le C(t)
\]
or more strongly
\[
\mathbb E e^{tS}\le \exp(C(e^t-1)).
\]

Expanding gives the necessary condition:
\[
\sum_{p_1<\cdots<p_k}\mathbb E(X_{p_1}\cdots X_{p_k})
\le C^k/k!.
\]

Therefore the problem reduces to a uniform bound on all $k$-fold intersections.

The current known information:

- $k=1$: controlled by $|Z_p|$.
- $k=2$: essentially the density-one/L2 theorem.
- $k=3$: the LGTQ-3 bottleneck.
- arbitrary $k$: completely open.

So the exponential moment conjecture is essentially equivalent to solving the full correlation problem.

---

## 4. Is there a hardcore-model analogy?

Only partially.

The hardcore model has a fixed graph and a hard constraint forbidding neighboring occupied vertices. The Apéry situation has a moving arithmetic constraint:

- vertices = primes;
- occupation = $p$ divides $b_{n\bmod p}$;
- incompatibility = arithmetic obstruction between several primes.

The analogy is useful because both produce sub-Poisson tails through exclusion. But there is no known graph whose independence polynomial is the Apéry moment generating function.

A successful proof would likely require discovering this hidden exclusion structure.

---

## 5. Expected shape of the correction

The proposed model
\[
\exp(\lambda(e^t-1)(1-t/t_c))
\]
is probably too simplistic.

For arithmetic rare events, corrections usually appear in the cluster expansion:
\[
\log \mathbb E e^{tS}
=\sum_{k\ge1} C_k(e^t-1)^k,
\]
where $C_k$ are connected correlation coefficients.

Poisson means
\[
C_1=\lambda,\qquad C_k=0\ (k\ge2).
\]

The data suggests
\[
C_k<0
\]
for large $k$.

The right target is therefore not a modified Poisson parameter, but a cluster expansion with negative higher cumulants.

---

## 6. Does this prove the GCD conjecture?

Not yet.

If one proves
\[
\mathbb E e^{tS}\ll_t1
\]
for some fixed $t>0$, then Markov gives
\[
\#\{n\le N:S(n)>V\}\ll N e^{-tV}.
\]
Taking $V=C\log N$ gives only logarithmic maximum control. This would already be far stronger than required.

For the Apéry GCD problem, it is enough to prove
\[
S(n)=o(N/\log N),
\]
and even a very weak exponential tail would imply this.

Thus the exponential moment route is sufficient.

The missing theorem is:

> Uniform high-order anti-clustering for the Apéry congruence events.

---

## 7. Recommended next mathematical targets

The most realistic intermediate statements are:

### Target A: bounded factorial moments

Prove for every fixed $k$:
\[
\mathbb E(S)_k\ll C^k.
\]

This would imply a Poisson-type tail up to any fixed level.

### Target B: growing moments

Prove
\[
\mathbb E(S)_k\ll k!C^k
\]
for $k\le c\log\log N$.

This would already imply
\[
S(n)=O(\log\log N)
\]
pointwise for almost all ranges.

### Target C: full cluster expansion

Show negative connected correlations:
\[
\kappa_k(S)\le0
\]
for large $k$.

This would explain the numerical phenomenon and likely finish the conjecture.

---

## Final assessment

The sub-Poisson tail is likely a real arithmetic phenomenon, not numerical noise. The body being Poisson while the tail collapses is exactly what one expects from hidden repulsion among rare events.

However, the repulsion is not explained by the simple no-adjacent-zero theorem alone. The deeper source is likely the global automaton/Frobenius structure of the Apéry sequence: the sets $Z_p$ are correlated arithmetic objects, not random subsets of residues.

The correct research direction is therefore:

**replace the LGTQ-3 triple correlation problem by a general Apéry cluster expansion problem.**

A proof of uniform exponential moments would immediately imply the desired GCD growth bound. The numerical discovery identifies the likely missing theorem: not small moments, but negative high-order correlations.
