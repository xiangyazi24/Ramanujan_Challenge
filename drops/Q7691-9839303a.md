# Four-companion finite-field state for ordinary Apéry targets

## Verdict

There are two different answers, depending on whether the exact unit relation is quotiented out.

### Literal answer to the question as written: yes, but vacuously

Write

\[
W_d=W_r^{(d)},\qquad d\in\{1,2,3,6\}.
\]

The exact canonical relation is

\[
\boxed{W_1-28W_2+63W_3-36W_6=240.}
\tag{V0}
\]

If the allowed state contains the unit-normalization coordinate `u=240`, then the primitive nonzero ambient linear form

\[
\boxed{
L_{\rm taut}=W_1-28W_2+63W_3-36W_6-u
}
\tag{V1}
\]

vanishes at **every row**, target or not. Thus a one-form family covers every ordinary target. Since there are actual targets, a zero-form family cannot cover them, so the literal minimum is `J=1`.

This is not a carrier. Its characteristic-zero evaluation is identically zero, so multiplying its values gives the zero integer. The homogeneous companion combination

\[
\boxed{
L_{\rm sep}=W_1-28W_2+63W_3-36W_6=240
}
\tag{V2}
\]

is the opposite extreme: for every `p>=7` it is a p-unit, hence it vanishes at **no** target. The unit relation therefore gives either a tautological identity or a separator, not the nonzero arithmetic carrier needed for the ordinary-target problem.

### Proper reading: no finite cover is currently proved

If “nonzero linear form” means nonzero **on the canonical state variety**, i.e. we quotient by (V0), then I do **not** find or prove a finite prime-independent hyperplane cover. I also do not have a theorem that excludes every such fixed family. What can be proved is sharper and useful:

1. The four canonical companions reduce, after the unit relation, to an affine **three-coordinate** state.
2. The entire Fricke-odd sector contains no new ordinary-target information beyond the unit separator and the already-known transverse coordinate `Xi`.
3. Hence any genuinely new bridge to nontransverse targets must come from the two Fricke-even coordinates.
4. Exact Sage through every prime `p<=5000` gives `606` interior Hasse target pairs. The natural coordinate/Fricke library covers only `11`; `595` targets survive it. The small target `(p,r)=(19,8)` already kills the whole natural library at once.
5. Exact degree-`<=8` congruence-lattice interpolation on both the `W` state and the literal four-`kappa` state finds no stable low-height hyperplane: forms fitted to all `339` targets with `p<=2500` have essentially zero predictive coverage on the `267` targets with `2500<p<=5000`. Eight separately fitted `p mod 24` forms cover only one of those `267` targets in union; `(2521,105)` is an explicit miss.
6. This finite computation is **not** a proof of Zariski density or of universal nonexistence. In fact a finite-sample CRT lemma shows why it cannot be: for any finite set of modular target states there is always a nonzero, generally huge, integer coefficient vector defining one hyperplane that contains that finite sample modulo its respective primes.
7. There is a theorem-level height obstruction in the Fricke-odd sector. Every state-sensitive fixed polynomial-coefficient odd form has quadratic dyadic product height. A low-height odd form necessarily forgets the odd state and degenerates to a polynomial/unit expression. The two even coordinates remain the only possible linear escape.

So the four companions do **not presently bridge the nontransverse gap left by BFH**. They reduce the open problem to a much cleaner question: prove a genuine finite cover in the two Fricke-even coordinates, or prove that the ordinary target states are not contained in any fixed finite union of such affine hyperplanes. Neither theorem is in the current repository.

The negative conclusion is deliberately scoped. It does **not** exclude `R`-dependent nonlinear projectors, higher-degree finite-field invariants, or cross-row constructions.

# 7. Why the finite scan cannot prove universal hyperplane nonexistence

This point is essential. It prevents a false “Zariski dense by computation” conclusion.

Let a finite census consist of feature vectors

\[
x_i\in\mathbf F_{p_i}^N.
\]

For one integer coefficient vector `c in Z^N`, the condition

\[
c\cdot x_i=0\pmod{p_i}
\]

defines a full-rank sublattice

\[
\Lambda_i\subset\mathbf Z^N,
\]

because

\[
p_i\mathbf Z^N\subseteq\Lambda_i.
\]

Therefore the finite intersection

\[
\Lambda=\bigcap_i\Lambda_i
\]

is again a full-rank nonzero lattice. In particular:

\[
\boxed{
\text{every finite modular target census admits a nonzero integer hyperplane
that contains the whole finite sample.}
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Usually that vector has huge, accidental CRT height. This is exactly what the full-training lattices are doing.

Consequences:

- the scan through `p<=5000` cannot prove that no fixed integer hyperplane exists;
- it cannot prove Zariski density of the cross-characteristic target image;
- “minimal cover of the finite data” is meaningless without a coefficient-height restriction, since one sufficiently large artificial form already fits any finite sample;
- train/holdout stability is a **guess filter**, not a proof.

Thus I do not infer asymptotics from the census.

A genuine negative theorem needs new arithmetic, e.g. Zariski density/equidistribution of the actual target-state image, or a theorem saying that every fixed low-degree form not generated by the level-6 identities is a p-unit at infinitely many ordinary targets.

No such theorem is presently in the repository.

# 8. A rigorous bounded-family no-go inside the Fricke-odd sector

Although I cannot exclude every fixed affine hyperplane family, the Fricke-odd subproblem *can* be closed structurally.

Let `P,Q,B,C in Z[r]` be fixed polynomials, of degree at most 8 if desired, and consider the most general odd-sector form allowed after also admitting `b_{r-1}` and a unit/constant coordinate:

\[
L_r=P(r)O_1+Q(r)O_2+B(r)b_{r-1}+C(r).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Using (3.5),

\[
\boxed{
L_r=\Gamma(r)\Xi_r+B(r)b_{r-1}+\Delta(r),
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

where

\[
\Gamma=-7P-Q,
\qquad
\Delta=-12P-36Q+C.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Thus no finite family in the odd sector contains information independent of `Xi`, `b_{r-1}`, and a fixed polynomial.

More importantly, its characteristic-zero height has a sharp dichotomy.

The landed CM asymptotics give, with

\[
\lambda=17+12\sqrt2,
\]

\[
\log b_r=r\log\lambda+O(\log r),
\qquad
\log|\Xi_r|=2r\log\lambda+O(\log r).
\tag{8.4}
\]

### Case 1: Gamma is not identically zero

Except at finitely many integer roots of `Gamma`, the exponential `Xi` term dominates the `b` and polynomial terms. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\log|L_r|=2r\log\lambda+O(\log r).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Therefore on a dyadic block

\[
I_R=\{R+1,\dots,2R\},
\]

\[
\boxed{
\log\prod_{r\in I_R}|L_r|
=3\log\lambda\,R^2+O(R\log R).
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

### Case 2: Gamma=0 but B is not identically zero

Then the `b_{r-1}` term dominates the polynomial term and

\[
\boxed{
\log\prod_{r\in I_R}|L_r|
=\frac32\log\lambda\,R^2+O(R\log R).
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

### Case 3: Gamma=B=0

The form is only the fixed polynomial `Delta(r)`. It has small height but has forgotten the companion state completely. The unit separator and tautological identity live in this state-blind alternative after using the exact relation.

So:

> **Odd-sector carrier no-go.** Every fixed polynomial-coefficient Fricke-odd form that genuinely depends on the canonical target state has quadratic dyadic product height. Every subquadratic-height odd form is state-blind after the exact level-6 relations are imposed.

This is theorem-level and does not depend on the finite scan.

It also proves that adding more Fricke-odd Eisenstein companions cannot bridge BFH: the odd sector is already the old transverse `Xi` plus a unit direction.

# 9. Height audit for a hypothetical proper finite cover

Suppose, beyond current knowledge, that proper forms

\[
L_1,\dots,L_J
\]

with fixed `J` did cover every ordinary Hasse target. After p-safe denominator clearing, a naive rowwise carrier would be

\[
\mathcal C_R
=\prod_{r\in I_R}\prod_{j=1}^J
\left|\operatorname{num}_{\rm red}L_j(\text{state}_r)\right|.
\tag{9.1}
\]

For a target `(p,r)`, at least one factor would be divisible by `p`, so this is logically a carrier if all characteristic-zero factors are nonzero.

But fixed level-6 q-series give at most exponential coefficient height in `r`: using (1.3) at any fixed `|q|<1`, coefficients of `A(q)^r` through order `r` are `exp(O(r))`; the Eichler denominator contributes only `lcm(1,...,r)^3=exp(O(r))`. Fixed degree-8 polynomial coefficients add only `O(log r)` to the logarithm. Hence every fixed raw form has primitive numerator height at most `exp(O(r))`, and (9.1) supplies only the generic budget

\[
\boxed{\log\mathcal C_R=O_J(R^2).}
\tag{9.2}
\]

That is not the desired subquadratic saving.

The odd-sector theorem is stronger: for every state-sensitive odd factor, the quadratic scale is forced, not merely an upper bound.

For the two even coordinates `E_1,E_2`, I do **not** prove a matching lower bound for every possible cancellation. The finite scan shows that the obvious even eigen-coordinate hyperplanes are poor target detectors (`E_1` has zero target zeros through `p<=5000`, and `E_2` only one), but a new exact even-sector cancellation identity is not ruled out.

Therefore a positive finite cover would still need a second theorem: **primitive height compression**. The unit relation by itself cannot provide it because its two options are exactly:

```text
W1-28W2+63W3-36W6      = 240   -> separator;
W1-28W2+63W3-36W6-240  =   0   -> zero/tautological carrier.
```

```sage
from sage.all import *

P_MAX = 5000
DS = [1,2,3,6]

def P(n):
    n = ZZ(n)
    return 34*n^3 + 51*n^2 + 27*n + 5

# exact sigma_3 sieve
sig3 = [ZZ(0)]*(P_MAX+1)
for d in range(1,P_MAX+1):
    d3 = ZZ(d)^3
    for m in range(d,P_MAX+1,d):
        sig3[m] += d3

def apery_mod_targets(p):
    Fp = GF(p)
    b = [Fp(1),Fp(5)]
    for n in range(1,p-1):
        b.append((Fp(P(n))*b[n]-Fp(n)^3*b[n-1]) / Fp(n+1)^3)
    assert b[0] == 1 and b[p-1] == 1
    return b,[r for r in range(1,p-1) if b[r] == 0]

def q_objects_mod(p,prec):
    Fp = GF(p)
    PS = PowerSeriesRing(Fp,'q',default_prec=prec)
    q = PS.gen()

    # A=q/t=prod_{3 not | a}(1+q^a)^12
    A = PS(1)
    for a in range(1,prec):
        if a % 3 != 0:
            A *= (1+q^a)^12
            A = A.add_bigoh(prec)

    # E=eta(2)^7 eta(3)^7/(eta^5 eta(6)^5)
    E = PS(1)
    for m in range(1,prec):
        e = -5 + 7*(m%2==0) + 7*(m%3==0) - 5*(m%6==0)
        if e > 0:
            E *= (1-q^m)^e
        elif e < 0:
            E /= (1-q^m)^(-e)
        E = E.add_bigoh(prec)

    H = (1-q*A.derivative()/A).add_bigoh(prec)
    return A,(E*H).add_bigoh(prec)

def target_state(p,r,bprev,A,EH):
    Fp = GF(p)
    base = (EH*A^r).add_bigoh(r+1)
    W = {}
    for d in DS:
        kap = Fp(0)
        scale = Fp(240)/Fp(d)^3
        for m in range(1,r//d+1):
            kap += (scale*Fp(sig3[m])/Fp(m)^3) * base[r-d*m]
        W[d] = Fp(r)^3*bprev*kap

    assert W[1]-28*W[2]+63*W[3]-36*W[6] == Fp(240)
    return tuple(ZZ(W[d]) for d in DS)

samples = []
for p in prime_range(7,P_MAX+1):
    p = ZZ(p)
    b,targets = apery_mod_targets(p)
    if not targets:
        continue
    A,EH = q_objects_mod(p,max(targets)+1)
    for r in targets:
        W = target_state(p,ZZ(r),b[r-1],A,EH)
        samples.append((p,ZZ(r),ZZ(b[r-1])) + W)

print(len(samples))       # 606
print(len(set(s[0] for s in samples)))  # 248
```

For an exact integer lattice of forms satisfying a training set of modular equations, the update used in the audit is:

```sage
def constraint_lattice(train, feature):
    n = len(feature(train[0]))
    B = identity_matrix(ZZ,n)

    for s in train:
        p = ZZ(s[0]); Fp = GF(p); f = feature(s)
        h = [Fp(sum(B[i,j]*ZZ(f[j]) for j in range(n)))
             for i in range(n)]
        pivot = next((i for i,x in enumerate(h) if x != 0),None)
        if pivot is None:
            continue

        inv = 1/h[pivot]
        K = zero_matrix(ZZ,n,n)
        row = 0
        for i in range(n):
            if i == pivot:
                continue
            K[row,i] = 1
            z = ZZ((-h[i]*inv).lift())
            if z > p//2:
                z -= p
            K[row,pivot] = z
            row += 1
        K[row,pivot] = p
        B = K*B

    return B.LLL(delta=0.99)
```

Again, LLL here is a discovery/anti-overfit tool, not a proof of universal nonexistence.

# 12. Final scoped conclusion

The canonical four-companion state does give a clean new structural reduction, but not the hoped-for ordinary-target hyperplane passport.

The exact level-6 algebra says

\[
\boxed{
\text{four companions}
=\text{unit separator}
\oplus\text{transverse }\Xi
\oplus\text{two Fricke-even coordinates}.
}
\]

The first summand cannot vanish at a target; the second is exactly the already-known proper transverse subset. Therefore **all possible new linear information for ordinary nontransverse targets is concentrated in the two even coordinates**.

No natural even/coordinate hyperplane cover survives even the small target `(19,8)`, and the complete `p<=5000` degree-8 train/holdout experiment gives no stable fixed family. But a finite scan cannot rule out arbitrarily large CRT-engineered coefficients, so I do not claim a Zariski-density theorem that has not been proved.

The next mathematically meaningful target is consequently one of these two statements:

1. **positive:** derive from the level-6 even Eisenstein/Eichler identities a proper fixed finite family of forms in `(Xi,E1,E2,b_{r-1})` that covers every Hasse target and then prove a nonzero subquadratic primitive-height compression; or
2. **negative:** prove that the ordinary target image in the two even coordinates is not contained in any fixed finite union of bounded-degree affine hyperplanes (for example by a genuine cross-characteristic density/equidistribution theorem).

Until one of those is established, the four-companion state is a useful separator/decomposition, not a BFH bridge for all ordinary Apéry targets.
