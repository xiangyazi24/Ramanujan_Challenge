\tag{7.1}
\]
for which
\[
 \ell\mid A_x(h_\ell).
\]
On this residue, (V2) gives
\[
 B_x(h_\ell)\equiv-\Delta\overline P\pmod\ell.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{h\bmod\ell}
 \mathbf1_{\{\ell\mid A_x(h)\}}
 \left(
  \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}(B_x(h))
  -\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell
 \right)\\
&\qquad=
 \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}
 \!\left(-\Delta\overline P\pmod\ell\right)
 -\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.3}
\]
This is an identity, not an estimate.

For any interval \(I\subset\mathbb Z\), the left side without balancing is exactly
\[
 \#\{h\in I:h\equiv h_\ell\pmod\ell\}
 \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}
 \!\left(-\Delta\overline P\pmod\ell\right).
\tag{7.4}
\]
The admissible displacement interval has length \(O(N)\), while \(\ell\asymp N\), so the multiplicity in (7.4) is \(O(1)\).  There is no long complete interval over which the moving-zero indicator becomes its density.

Fourier-expanding the two factors does not alter this conclusion.  The divisibility delta function has all frequencies and collapses the \(h\)-sum to (7.2).  Any successful large-sieve theorem must therefore be a new horizontal estimate for the selected points
\[
 -\Delta_x\overline{P_x}\pmod\ell
\]
under the actual Palm root weights.  Standard one-variable large sieve or the canonical first zero moment does not give such a pointwise selected estimate.

# 8. Exact long-tail candidate set and the smallest average premise

For every root \(x\) and shell prime \(\ell\) outside the fixed primes, define
\[
 t_x(\ell)=
 \left[\Delta_x\overline{p'_x\ell}\right]_{P_x}
 \in[0,P_x),
\tag{8.1}
\]
\[
 h_x(\ell)
 =\frac{t_x(\ell)\ell-t_{0,x}\ell_{0,x}}{P_x}.
\tag{8.2}
\]
The numerator in (8.2) is divisible by \(P_x\).  Let \(\mathcal C_x^{>H}\) be the set of such primes satisfying

```text
ell is in the literal shell and distinct from p,q,p',ell_0;
all non-moving degree-side deletion conditions hold;
t_x(ell) mod p lies in Z_p;
|h_x(ell)| > H.
```

The moving zero test is not included in the definition of \(\mathcal C_x^{>H}\).  Retaining it explicitly, the exact long degree is
\[
 \boxed{
 d_{>H}(x)
 =\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal C_x^{>H}}
  \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}
   \!\left(-\Delta_x\overline{P_x}\pmod\ell\right).}
\tag{8.3}
\]
This formula has every literal actual zero condition in its correct location:

- the selected \(p,q,p',\ell_0\) conditions and masks are in \(w_x\);
- the additional fixed-\(p\) condition is in \(\mathcal C_x^{>H}\);
- the additional moving-\(\ell\) condition is the indicator in (8.3);
- no additional \(q\)-condition is invented.

Subtracting and adding the vertical density gives the identity (V10).  Since \(\mathcal C_x^{>H}\) is a subset of the shell,
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal C_x^{>H}}\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell
 \le
 \sum_{\ell\asymp N}\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell
 \ll N^{3/5+o(1)}.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Multiplying by \(w_x\) and summing,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak L_{1,>H}(F)
 \le{}&N^{3/5+o(1)}\mathfrak M_0(F)\\
 &+\sum_xw_x
 \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal C_x^{>H}}
 \left(
  \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}
   \!\left(-\Delta_x\overline{P_x}\pmod\ell\right)
  -\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell
 \right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.5}
\]
The second line is the whole unresolved arithmetic.

At \(H=N^{3/5}\), the one-sided bound (V11) is therefore the smallest new average premise after removing a term already controlled unconditionally.  An absolute-value version would be stronger than necessary.  A direct premise
\[
 \mathfrak L_{1,>N^{3/5}}(F)
 \ll N^{3/5+o(1)}\mathfrak M_0(F)
\]
is equivalent for the consumer but conceals the free mean term; (V11) isolates only the genuinely missing correlation.

# 9. Why a second selected root is not a free repair

One might try to compare two selected roots before introducing the degree copy.  This does not remain the first factorial target.

- Giving \(\ell_1\) its own actual \(q\)-selector changes the copy from unselected to selected.
- Pairing two roots introduces two Palm weights and produces a second moment or a TDW-type collision rather than \(\mathfrak L_1\).
- For one fixed determinant, both rooted identities have the same characteristic-zero value \(t\ell\); their apparent second linear relation is affine-dependent on (V2).
- Across different determinants or outer rows, the comparison recreates the four-row determinant/resultant problem audited in Q4858.

Thus “root twice” is not a legal way to prove the one-root selected--unselected degree bound.  It either duplicates an actual selector or changes the consumer norm.

\begin{lemma}[Literal selected-root carrier]
\label{lem:q4886-selected-root-carrier}
Fix a literal selected Palm root
$x=(\mathbf b,\delta,\ell_0)$ with
$\mathbf b=(\sigma,p,q,p')$, put $P=pq$ and
$\Delta=\sigma\delta$, and let $\ell_1\ne\ell_0$ be an additional
literal two-test degree prime.  Let $t_i\in[0,P)$ and
$\widetilde t_i'\in\mathbb Z$ satisfy
\[
 p'\ell_i t_i-P\widetilde t_i'=\Delta
 \qquad(i=0,1).
\]
Then there is a unique $h\in\mathbb Z$ such that
\[
 t_1\ell_1=t_0\ell_0+Ph,
 \qquad
 \widetilde t_1'=\widetilde t_0'+p'h.
\]
Put
\[
 A_x(h)=t_0\ell_0+Ph,
 \qquad
 B_x(h)=\widetilde t_0'+p'h,
\]
and
\[
 \nu(m)=
 \begin{cases}
 m,&m\ge0,\\
 -m-1,&m<0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
 p'A_x(h)-PB_x(h)=\Delta,
 \qquad
 \ell_1\mid A_x(h),
\]
\[
 p'\ell_1\mid b_{\nu(B_x(h))},
 \qquad
 p\mid b_{A_x(h)/\ell_1},
\]
and therefore
\[
 \ell_1\mid
 \gcd\!\left(A_x(h),b_{\nu(B_x(h))}\right).
\]
Moreover
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_h(A_x,B_x)=-\Delta,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Res}_h(A_x,-B_x-1)=\Delta-P,
\]
and neither resultant is divisible by $\ell_1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Subtracting the two determinant identities gives
\[
 p'(t_1\ell_1-t_0\ell_0)
 =P(\widetilde t_1'-\widetilde t_0').
\]
Since $(P,p')=1$, the displayed integer $h$ exists and is unique.
The literal degree tests say that $t_1\bmod p$ is a $p$-zero and
$B_x(h)\bmod\ell_1$ is an $\ell_1$-zero; the selected root gives the
common $p'$-zero.  Multiplicative Lucas proves the asserted divisibilities
when $B_x(h)\ge0$, and reflection followed by Lucas proves them when
$B_x(h)<0$.  The resultant formulas follow from
$p'A_x-PB_x=\Delta$.  Finally, modulo $\ell_1$ one has
$\Delta\equiv-PB_x(h)$; the actual moving zero is neither $0$ nor $-1$,
so both $\Delta$ and $\Delta-P$ are units modulo $\ell_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform rooted displacement range]
\label{prop:q4886-rooted-displacement-range}
With the literal Palm weights $w_x$ and selected-rooted first factorial
moment, let $\mathfrak L_{1,\mathcal H}$ denote the contribution for which
the rooted displacement belongs to a finite set
$\mathcal H\subset\mathbb Z$.  Then
\[
 \mathfrak L_{1,\mathcal H}(F)
 \ll
 \min\!\left\{|\mathcal H|,N^{2/3}\right\}
 N^{o(1)}\mathfrak M_0(F).
\]
In particular, uniformly for $1\le H\ll N$,
\[
 \mathfrak L_{1,\{|h|\le H\}}(F)
 \ll
 \min\{H,N^{2/3}\}N^{o(1)}\mathfrak M_0(F).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $x,h$, every additional moving prime divides the same nonzero
integer $A_x(h)=O(N^3)$, so only $O(1)$ primes in a fixed $N$-shell are
possible.  This gives $O(|\mathcal H|N^{o(1)})$ primes per root.
Independently, the map
\[
 \ell\longmapsto
 \Delta\overline{p'\ell}\pmod p\in\mathcal Z_p
\]
has $O(1)$ shell multiplicity, so the total degree is
$O(Z(p)N^{o(1)})=O(N^{2/3+o(1)})$.  Take the smaller bound, multiply by
$w_x$, and sum over the roots.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Complete rooted displacement collapse]
\label{lem:q4886-complete-h-collapse}
Retain the notation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:q4886-selected-root-carrier}.  For every shell prime
$\ell\nmid Pp'$, one has
\[
 \sum_{h\bmod\ell}
 \mathbf1_{\{\ell\mid A_x(h)\}}
 \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}(B_x(h)\bmod\ell)
 =
 \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}
 \!\left(-\Delta\overline P\pmod\ell\right).
\]
More generally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{h\bmod\ell}
 \mathbf1_{\{\ell\mid A_x(h)\}}
 \left(
  \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}(B_x(h)\bmod\ell)
  -\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell
 \right)\\
&\qquad=
 \mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_\ell}
 \!\left(-\Delta\overline P\pmod\ell\right)
 -\frac{Z(\ell)}\ell.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $P$ is invertible modulo $\ell$, exactly one residue
$h_\ell\bmod\ell$ satisfies $A_x(h_\ell)\equiv0\pmod\ell$.  The identity
$p'A_x-PB_x=\Delta$ then gives
$B_x(h_\ell)\equiv-\Delta\overline P\pmod\ell$.  Substitution proves both
formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{aligned}

 \le{}&N^{3/5+o(1)}\mathfrak M_0(F)\\

 \left(

Multiplication by the nonnegative Palm weights and summation proves the
