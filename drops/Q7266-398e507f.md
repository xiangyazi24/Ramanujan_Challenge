# The lcm-cube Green gauge: integrality, non-holonomicity, and the finite-field obstruction

## Verdict

Write

\[
L_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),\qquad L_0=1,
\]

and retain the Green-kernel normalization from
`problems/3.2/gap_kernel_green.tex`:

\[
U_{-1}(a)=0,\quad U_0(a)=1,\quad
U_{m+1}(a)=P(a+m)U_m(a)-(a+m)^6U_{m-1}(a),
\]

\[
V_m(a)=\frac{U_m(a)}{(a+1)_m^3},\qquad
(a+1)_m=\prod_{j=1}^m(a+j).
\]

The exact universal `qL/qI` gauge from the denominator theorem is

\[
\boxed{
q_L(n)=2L_n^3,\qquad q_I(a,m)=(a+1)_m^3,
}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_m(a)=q_L(a+m)V_m(a)
       =\frac{2L_{a+m}^3}{(a+1)_m^3}U_m(a).
}
\tag{1}
\]

The factor audit is important.

* The factor `2` is the one supplied by the universal Apéry denominator theorem for an arbitrary solution with integral initial data.
* A factor `6` also works using only the weaker statement recorded in `proof.tex`, because its numerator solution has initial value `a_1=6`; it is a safe but nonminimal project-native fallback.
* Omitting the factor `2` requires the stronger sequence-specific assertion `L_n^3 c_n in Z`. That assertion may be proved separately, but it is not what the universal theorem says and must not be silently substituted for it.

For the odd primes relevant to Problem 3.2, the factor `2` is a unit, so all three normalizations have the same strict-triangle zero locus once their integrality has been justified.

The conclusions are:

1. `Xi_m(a)` in (1) is integral and exponentially bounded.
2. Its bivariate generating series is **provably non-D-finite/non-holonomic**.
3. Consequently it is not an ordinary rational/algebraic diagonal and not a period function of a fixed finite-rank algebraic family.
4. The present argument does **not** prove differential transcendence; differential algebraicity remains undecided.
5. On every strict triangle `a+m<p`, `Xi_m(a)` is a nonzero scalar multiple of `U_m(a)` modulo `p`. Hence all nonwrapping bridge and short-chain zero incidences are exactly unchanged.
6. The lcm gauge does not preserve fixed complexity. Its prime-power jumps force almost maximal interpolation degree modulo `p`, and its integral height is only exponential, which is too large to save a dyadic prime average.

Thus the gauge is useful for integrality and height bookkeeping, but it is not a finite-field compression mechanism.

## 1. Exact reconstruction and the denominator theorem

Let `b,c` be the two normalized solutions used in the Green-kernel files:

\[
(b_0,b_1)=(1,5),\qquad (c_0,c_1)=(0,1).
\]

The project numerator sequence, denoted here by `A_n` to avoid conflict with the starting index `a`, has

\[
(A_0,A_1)=(0,6),\qquad A_n=6c_n.
\]

The Casoratian formula in `gap_kernel_green.tex` is

\[
V_m(a)=a^3\bigl(b_{a-1}c_{a+m}-c_{a-1}b_{a+m}\bigr)
\qquad(a\geq1).
\tag{2}
\]

The denominator theorem used in Q7255 is the classical universal form quoted by van der Poorten. If `u_n` is any solution of the normalized Apéry recurrence with `u_0,u_1 in Z`, then

\[
\boxed{2L_n^3u_n\in\mathbf Z\qquad(n\geq0).}
\tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, after putting `W_n=(n!)^3u_n`, so that

\[
W_{n+1}=P(n)W_n-n^6W_{n-1},
\]

the divisibility statement is

\[
(n!)^3\mid 2L_n^3W_n.
\]

This is exactly why `q_L(n)` is `2L_n^3`, rather than merely `L_n^3`.

### Integrality of the whole array

Let `n=a+m`. For `a>=1`, multiply (2) by `2L_n^3`:

\[
\Xi_m(a)
=a^3\left(
 b_{a-1}\,(2L_n^3c_n)
 -b_n\,(2L_n^3c_{a-1})
\right).
\tag{4}
\]

The first parenthesized quantity is integral by (3). For the second one,

\[
2L_n^3c_{a-1}
=\left(\frac{L_n}{L_{a-1}}\right)^3
  (2L_{a-1}^3c_{a-1})\in\mathbf Z,
\]

because `a-1<=n` implies `L_{a-1}|L_n`. Since every `b_j` and `a^3` is integral, (4) proves

\[
\boxed{\Xi_m(a)\in\mathbf Z\qquad(a\geq1,m\geq0).}
\]

At `a=0`, the Green file gives `V_m(0)=b_m`, so

\[
\Xi_m(0)=2L_m^3b_m\in\mathbf Z.
\]

This proves integrality on the full nonnegative quadrant.

### Relation to the factor `6`

Using only the statement written in `proof.tex`, namely

\[
L_n^3A_n\in\mathbf Z,
\]

one gets the alternative integral array

\[
6L_{a+m}^3V_m(a)
=a^3\left(
 b_{a-1}L_{a+m}^3A_{a+m}
 -b_{a+m}L_{a+m}^3A_{a-1}
\right).
\]

That is a correct fallback, but it is not the exact universal `q_L` normalization. The factor `2` in (1) is the sharper reconstruction.

## 2. Exponential boundedness

Every normalized Apéry solution satisfies

\[
u_{r+1}=\frac{P(r)}{(r+1)^3}u_r
          -\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^3u_{r-1}.
\]

For `r>=0`,

\[
0<\frac{P(r)}{(r+1)^3}\leq34,
\]

because

\[
34(r+1)^3-P(r)=51r^2+75r+29>0.
\]

Hence

\[
|u_{r+1}|\leq34|u_r|+|u_{r-1}|.
\]

It follows, for an absolute constant `C_0`, that

\[
|b_r|+|c_r|\leq C_0 35^r.
\tag{5}
\]

Putting `n=a+m` in (2) and using `a<=n` gives

\[
|V_m(a)|\leq C_1 n^3 35^{2n}.
\tag{6}
\]

The elementary Chebyshev estimate

\[
\log L_n\leq n\log4
\]

gives `L_n^3<=64^n`. Combining this with (6), and absorbing `n^3` into an exponential, yields an absolute constant `C` such that

\[
\boxed{|\Xi_m(a)|\leq C^{a+m}.}
\tag{7}
\]

For example, one may take a deliberately coarse `C=4*10^7`. Thus the bivariate series below converges in a nonempty bidisc; the obstruction found later is arithmetic/holonomic, not divergent growth.

## 3. What the gauge does to the recurrence

Put

\[
r_n=\frac{L_n}{L_{n-1}}\qquad(n\geq1).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
r_n=
\begin{cases}
q,&n=q^e\text{ for a prime }q\text{ and }e\geq1,\\
1,&n\text{ is not a prime power}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{8}
\]

Let `n=a+m`. Gauging the normalized Green recurrence gives

\[
\boxed{
(n+1)^3\Xi_{m+1}(a)
=r_{n+1}^3P(n)\Xi_m(a)
-n^3(r_nr_{n+1})^3\Xi_{m-1}(a).
}
\tag{9}
\]

Thus the original Green recurrence has polynomial coefficients, while the integral gauge has coefficients that switch at every prime power. The denominator has been removed at the cost of inserting the arithmetic sequence (8). This is the first indication that integrality and fixed algebraic complexity are competing goals here.

## 4. The gauged bivariate generating series

Define

\[
\mathfrak X(s,z)
=\sum_{a\geq1}\sum_{m\geq0}\Xi_m(a)s^az^m.
\tag{10}
\]

Its zeroth `z`-slice is

\[
\boxed{
[z^0]\mathfrak X(s,z)
=2\sum_{a\geq1}L_a^3s^a.
}
\tag{11}
\]

If

\[
H(t)=\sum_{n\geq0}L_n^3t^n
\]

and `mathcal G(s,z)=sum V_m(a)s^az^m` is the ungauged Green series from `gap_kernel_bivariate.tex`, then formally

\[
\mathfrak X(s,z)
=2\operatorname{CT}_u
 H(u)\,\mathcal G(s/u,z/u).
\tag{12}
\]

Formula (12) is only a total-degree Hadamard/constant-term description. It is not a rational diagonal representation, because the new factor `H` is itself non-holonomic.

The ungauged series is on the opposite side of the divide: `B` and `C` are D-finite, the kernel in `gap_kernel_bivariate.tex` is built from them by products and Euler derivatives, and the displayed positive-part operation is a constant-term/diagonal extraction. Standard holonomic closure therefore makes the ungauged `mathcal G` D-finite. The lcm gauge destroys that property.

## 5. Prime-power spikes prove non-P-recursiveness

Set

\[
A_n=2L_n^3.
\]

By (8),

\[
\frac{A_n}{A_{n-1}}
=
\begin{cases}
q^3,&n=q^e,\\
1,&n\text{ is not a prime power}.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]

### Theorem

The sequence `(A_n)` is not P-recursive. Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{n\geq0}A_ns^n
\]

is not D-finite.

### Proof

Assume that for some `r>=0` there are polynomials `R_0,...,R_r in Q[X]`, not all zero, such that

\[
\sum_{j=0}^rR_j(n)A_{n+j}=0
\qquad(n\geq N).
\tag{14}
\]

Clear denominators so that the `R_j` have integer coefficients.

#### Step 1: flat blocks

For each `i=1,...,r`, choose two distinct primes `ell_i,ell_i'`, with all these primes mutually distinct, and impose

\[
n\equiv-i\pmod{\ell_i\ell_i'}.
\]

The Chinese remainder theorem gives an infinite arithmetic progression of such `n`. For every sufficiently large member of it, each `n+i` is divisible by two distinct primes and hence is not a prime power. Therefore

\[
A_n=A_{n+1}=\cdots=A_{n+r}.
\]

Equation (14) gives

\[
\sum_{j=0}^rR_j(n)=0
\]

for infinitely many `n`. Hence the polynomial

\[
S(X)=\sum_{j=0}^rR_j(X)
\]

vanishes identically.

#### Step 2: isolate one prime jump

Fix `k in {1,...,r}`. For every nonzero offset

\[
d\in\{1-k,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,r-k\},
\]

choose two new distinct primes `ell_d,ell_d'>r+1`, all mutually distinct, and prescribe

\[
q\equiv-d\pmod{\ell_d\ell_d'}.
\]

The resulting CRT residue class is coprime to the product modulus, because `0<|d|<=r`. Dirichlet's theorem supplies infinitely many primes `q` in this residue class. For such a sufficiently large `q`, every `q+d` in the displayed offset set has two distinct prime divisors and is not a prime power.

Put `n=q-k`. The only prime-power jump among

\[
n+1,\ldots,n+r
\]

occurs at `n+k=q`. Thus, for a common nonzero value `B`,

\[
A_{n+j}=B\quad(j<k),
\qquad
A_{n+j}=q^3B\quad(j\geq k).
\]

Substitution in (14), followed by use of `S=0`, gives

\[
(q^3-1)\sum_{j=k}^rR_j(q-k)=0.
\]

There are infinitely many such primes `q`, so

\[
T_k(X):=\sum_{j=k}^rR_j(X)
\]

is the zero polynomial. Taking `k=r,r-1,...,1` gives successively

\[
R_r=R_{r-1}=\cdots=R_1=0,
\]

and `S=0` then gives `R_0=0`, contradicting the assumed recurrence. This proves the theorem. `square`

### Corollary: the bivariate series is non-holonomic

Sections/coefficient extractions of a multivariate D-finite series are D-finite. If `mathfrak X` were D-finite, its section (11) would be D-finite, contradicting the theorem. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathfrak X(s,z)\text{ is not D-finite and not holonomic}.}
\tag{15}
\]

This is a proof, not an asymptotic heuristic; the contradiction uses exact prime-power jumps, CRT, and Dirichlet's theorem.

## 6. Diagonal, period, and differential-algebraic classification

The classification supported by (15) is as follows.

| Class | Status for `mathfrak X` | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Rational/algebraic | No | Algebraic series are D-finite. |
| D-finite / holonomic | No | The `m=0` section is the non-P-recursive lcm-cube sequence. |
| Ordinary diagonal of a fixed rational function | No | Rational diagonals are D-finite (Lipshitz closure). |
| Diagonal of a fixed algebraic/D-finite series | No | Diagonals of D-finite series are D-finite. |
| Period function of a fixed finite-rank algebraic family | No | Gauss-Manin/Picard-Fuchs gives a finite-order linear differential equation. |
| Differentially algebraic | Undecided | Non-D-finite does not imply differential transcendence. |

The word “period” must be scoped. Every integer coefficient is trivially a Kontsevich-Zagier period as a number. What is excluded is a **single fixed algebraic family** whose period function has `mathfrak X` as its local expansion. Such a family would give a Picard-Fuchs equation and hence D-finiteness.

The prime-power argument does not settle differential algebraicity. Nonlinear differential equations can support non-P-recursive coefficient sequences and even natural-boundary behavior. No differential polynomial for `H` or `mathfrak X` is known, but the argument above does not prove that none exists.

## 7. Reductions modulo `p`: algebraic but not uniformly simple

The boundary reductions illustrate why finite automata do not repair the situation. For every prime `p>=5`,

\[
A_n=2L_n^3\equiv0\pmod p\qquad(n\geq p).
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{n\geq0}A_ns^n\pmod p
\]

is a polynomial of degree at most `p-1`. It is automatically algebraic over `F_p(s)` and its coefficient sequence is automatically `p`-automatic. Thus Christol-type reduction arguments give no contradiction at all.

What fails is **uniform complexity**. In fact the direct polynomial complexity is nearly maximal.

### Near-maximal interpolation-degree theorem

Let

\[
\mathcal P(p)=\#\{q^e: q\text{ prime},\ e\geq1,\ 2\leq q^e\leq p-1\}.
\]

For `p>=11`, suppose `Q_p(X) in F_p[X]` satisfies

\[
Q_p(n)=A_n\pmod p\qquad(0\leq n<p).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\deg Q_p\geq p-\mathcal P(p).}
\tag{16}
\]

Indeed, put

\[
D_p(X)=Q_p(X)-Q_p(X-1).
\]

At every `n in {1,...,p-1}` that is not a prime power, (13) gives `D_p(n)=0`. On the other hand,

\[
D_p(2)=A_2-A_1=14\not\equiv0\pmod p
\]

for `p>=11`, so `D_p` is not the zero polynomial. It has at least `(p-1)-mathcal P(p)` distinct roots, whence

\[
\deg Q_p-1\geq(p-1)-\mathcal P(p).
\]

Finally,

\[
\mathcal P(p)=\pi(p-1)+O(\sqrt p\log p),
\]

so

\[
\deg Q_p\geq p-O(p/\log p).
\tag{17}
\]

Thus a polynomial encoding of even the `m=0` boundary has asymptotically full degree. Any direct Artin-Schreier, polynomial-map, or bounded-degree affine-family realization of this gauge has conductor/degree growing with `p`.

This theorem is deliberately scoped: it rules out direct bounded-degree regular-function encodings and, together with non-holonomicity, fixed linear-recursive encodings. It does not by itself rule out every imaginable nonlinear or exotic sheaf whose trace happens to agree on `F_p`-points.

## 8. Strict-triangle unit equivalence

Let `p` be an odd prime and suppose

\[
a,m\geq0,\qquad a+m<p.
\]

Every integer from `1` through `a+m` is a `p`-unit. Hence `L_{a+m}` and `(a+1)_m` are both nonzero modulo `p`, and (1) gives

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_m(a)\equiv
\varepsilon_p(a,m)U_m(a)\pmod p,
\qquad
\varepsilon_p(a,m)=
2\left(\frac{L_{a+m}}{(a+1)_m}\right)^3
\in\mathbf F_p^\times.
}
\tag{18}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_m(a)=0\pmod p
\iff
U_m(a)=0\pmod p
}
\qquad(a+m<p).
\tag{19}
\]

This is the exact useful theorem supplied by the gauge.

### The strict inequality is essential

The first seam already shows why (18) cannot be extended across `a+m=p`. From `gap_kernel_bivariate.tex`, for every prime `p>=5`,

\[
v_p(c_p)=-3,\qquad p^3c_p\equiv1\pmod p.
\]

Since `V_{p-1}(1)=c_p` and `L_p=pL_{p-1}`,

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_{p-1}(1)
=2L_p^3c_p
=2L_{p-1}^3(p^3c_p)
\equiv2L_{p-1}^3\not\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Thus the visible factor `L_p^3` does not make the gauged coefficient vanish: it exactly cancels the denominator spike of `c_p`. Reduction of the factors separately is invalid at the seam.

## 9. Far-bridge incidence is exactly invariant

Recall

\[
N_h(x)=U_{h-1}(x+1).
\]

In the nonwrapping range used in `far_bridge_incidence.tex`,

\[
2\leq s\leq H_p,
\quad K+1\leq G\leq p-1-s,
\quad0\leq x\leq p-1-s-G.
\]

For the first bridge, take

\[
(a,m)=(x+1,s-1),
\]

whose total degree is `x+s<p`. For the second bridge, take

\[
(a,m)=(x+s+1,G-1),
\]

whose total degree is `x+s+G<p`. Applying (19) twice gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
N_s(x)=0\pmod p
&\iff \Xi_{s-1}(x+1)=0\pmod p,\\
N_G(x+s)=0\pmod p
&\iff \Xi_{G-1}(x+s+1)=0\pmod p.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{21}
\]

Therefore the exact incidence identity can be rewritten as

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
A_p^{\mathrm{nw}}(K)
={}&\sum_{s=2}^{H_p}\sum_{G=K+1}^{p-1-s}
\#\{x:\ 0\leq x\leq p-1-s-G,\\
&\hspace{55mm}
\Xi_{s-1}(x+1)=\Xi_{G-1}(x+s+1)=0\pmod p\}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{22}
\]

Equation (22) is not a new upper bound: it is exactly the old set with each equation multiplied by a unit. In particular, the existing estimate

\[
A_p^{\mathrm{nw}}(K)\ll p^{3/2}\log p
\]

is neither improved nor weakened by the gauge.

The coefficient index `G-1` still grows to order `p`, and the direct finite-field representation of the lcm factor has degree almost `p` by (16). Hence the gauge does not turn (22) into a bounded-complexity incidence variety to which a uniform Lang-Weil/Deligne estimate can be applied.

## 10. Selected short-chain mass is also invariant

A four-consecutive-occurrence window with successive gaps `h_1,h_2,h_3` is certified by the three corresponding nonwrapping gap equations. Whenever the whole window lies in `{0,...,p-1}`, every one of these equations is in a strict triangle. Replacing each `U` or `N` equation by its `Xi` version therefore preserves the selected window set exactly.

Consequently all quantities built only from these occurrence windows and external filters—among them

\[
C_p(q),\quad M_p,\quad
\sum_qC_p(q)^2,\quad
E_p^{\mathrm{sep}},\quad B_p^{[4]}(K)
\]

are unchanged. In particular, the unconditional short-chain estimate

\[
\sum_qC_p(q)^2\leq30pH
\]

remains exactly the same estimate after gauging. At `H=floor(sqrt p)`, the dyadic `X^{5/2}/log X` scale is not lowered.

## 11. Why exponential integrality does not save the prime average

One might try to reverse the order of summation over integer tuples and primes: if a prime `p` contributes, then it divides one or two integral `Xi` values. The exponential height (7) is too large for this to save anything.

For a fixed tuple of total size `O(X)`, and nonzero integers `M_1,M_2` equal to the relevant gauged values,

\[
\#\{X<p\leq2X:p\mid M_1,\ p\mid M_2\}
\leq\frac{\log\gcd(|M_1|,|M_2|)}{\log X}
\ll\frac{X}{\log X}.
\tag{23}
\]

But `X/log X` is already the order of the total number of primes in the dyadic interval. Equivalently,

\[
\prod_{X<p\leq2X}p=\exp((1+o(1))X),
\]

which is fully compatible with the bound `|M_i|<=exp(O(X))`. A single exponentially sized integer can, at the level of height alone, be divisible by every prime in the interval.

Thus an arithmetic reversal would need genuinely stronger input, such as

\[
\log\gcd(M_1,M_2)=o(X),
\]

or subexponential values, or cancellation after summing tuples. Integrality plus exponential boundedness supplies none of these.

## 12. Sharp scoped obstruction and usable theorem

The results above can be packaged as follows.

### Theorem (lcm-gauge invariance and complexity obstruction)

Let `Xi` be defined by (1).

1. `Xi_m(a)` is an integer and satisfies `|Xi_m(a)|<=C^{a+m}` for an absolute `C`.
2. For every odd prime `p` and every `a+m<p`,
   
   \[
   \Xi_m(a)=0\pmod p\iff U_m(a)=0\pmod p.
   \]
3. Hence every nonwrapping far-bridge root count and every selected short-chain occurrence count is exactly invariant under the replacement `U -> Xi`.
4. The generating series `mathfrak X` is non-D-finite/non-holonomic.
5. Any polynomial over `F_p` representing its `m=0` boundary on all residues has degree at least `p-mathcal P(p)=p-O(p/log p)`.
6. The exponential height gives at best the trivial `O(X/log X)` bound for dyadic prime divisors of a fixed tuple.

Therefore no argument that merely clears denominators, reduces the resulting integer array modulo `p`, and treats it as a fixed-complexity algebraic family can improve the far-bridge or short-chain bounds. Any improvement must use arithmetic specific to the Apéry orbit—coefficient nonconcentration, conditioned incidence, a new common-root theorem, or a genuinely bounded-conductor realization not obtained from the lcm scalar.

## Final classification

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{property} & \text{status}\\ \hline
\Xi_m(a)\in\mathbf Z & \text{proved}\\
|\Xi_m(a)|\leq C^{a+m} & \text{proved}\\
\mathfrak X\text{ D-finite/holonomic} & \text{false}\\
\mathfrak X\text{ rational/algebraic diagonal} & \text{false}\\
\mathfrak X\text{ fixed algebraic period function} & \text{false}\\
\mathfrak X\text{ differentially algebraic} & \text{not decided here}\\
\text{strict-triangle zero equivalence} & \text{exact}\\
\text{far-bridge gain from the gauge alone} & \text{none}\\
\text{short-chain mass gain from the gauge alone} & \text{none}\\
\text{uniform polynomial/sheaf complexity} & \text{destroyed by lcm spikes}
\end{array}}
\]

The arithmetic gauge repairs global boundedness of the coefficients, but it does so by importing the prime-power support of `lcm(1,...,n)`. That support is precisely what makes the gauged series non-holonomic and the finite-field interpolation essentially full degree. The denominator problem is solved; the incidence problem is not compressed.
