# Apéry Hasse-zero Fourier audit

## 1. Normalization of the Fourier quantities

For a prime $p$ write
\[
Z_p=\{r\in\mathbf F_p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},\qquad Z(p)=|Z_p|,
\]
and
\[
F_p(h)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(hr)-Z(p)\mathbf1_{h=0}.
\]
For $h\neq0$ this is the ordinary additive Fourier transform of the zero divisor. Parseval gives exactly
\[
\sum_{h\in\mathbf F_p}|F_p(h)|^2
=pZ(p)-Z(p)^2
\le pZ(p).
\]
No improvement follows from the size estimate $Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}$ alone.

The fourth moment is the additive energy identity
\[
\sum_h|F_p(h)|^4
=p\#\{(a,b,c,d)\in Z_p^4:a+b=c+d\}-O(Z(p)^4).
\]
Thus a useful estimate would require an Apéry-specific bound
\[
E_+(Z_p):=\#\{a+b=c+d\}
\ll Z(p)^{2+\eta}
\]
with some saving over the trivial $Z(p)^3$ bound.

## 2. What the Hasse/Picard-Fuchs structure actually supplies

The Hasse polynomial description gives that $Z_p$ is the zero divisor of a special algebraic function and gives:

* reflection $r\mapsto p-1-r$;
* the Picard-Fuchs differential equation satisfied by the associated family;
* degree/conductor information for the Hasse polynomial;
* the bound $Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}$.

These facts do not by themselves construct a sheaf whose trace function is
\[
h\mapsto F_p(h).
\]
The variable $h$ is an additive Fourier variable of an indicator of a zero locus. It is not the parameter of the original Picard-Fuchs sheaf. Therefore Deligne square-root cancellation cannot be invoked without an additional geometric construction.

## 3. Required sheaf statement if a Fourier estimate were true

A usable theorem would need a middle-extension sheaf $\mathcal G$ on $\mathbf A^1_h$ such that
\[
F_p(h)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_h|\mathcal G)
\]
up to explicit exceptional frequencies.

One would then need:

\[
\operatorname{cond}(\mathcal G)=O(1),
\]
no large geometrically constant quotient, and an explicit treatment of frequencies fixed by reflection.

The generic Deligne conclusion would be
\[
|F_p(h)|\ll p^{1/2}
\]
for $h$ outside a bounded exceptional set. No such sheaf is currently supplied by the Hasse polynomial ODE.

## 4. Why the zero divisor obstructs the naive estimate

The obstruction is that $Z_p$ is a highly structured algebraic subset rather than a random set. Reflection gives
\[
r\in Z_p\Longrightarrow p-1-r\in Z_p.
\]
Hence additive relations occur in forced families:
\[
r+(p-1-r)=p-1.
\]
Every zero contributes a reflected partner, already producing $\gg Z(p)$ additive collisions. This alone does not destroy a square-root energy bound, but it shows that the correct model is not a generic subset.

More seriously, any special subfamily with unusually large repeated gaps can create
\[
E_+(Z_p)\gg Z(p)^3,
\]
and the present Hasse/Picard-Fuchs input does not exclude this.

## 5. Consequence for HM3 Palm estimates

The diagonal triple contribution has the schematic form
\[
\Delta_p\sim \frac p{Z(p)}(E_p)_+^2.
\]
Fourier expansion of CRT intersections would require bounds of the shape
\[
\sum_{p\in(X,2X]}\frac p{Z(p)}(E_p)_+^2
\ll X^2\lambda^3
\]
or a stronger intermediate inequality implying it.

A plausible Fourier route would need an estimate such as
\[
\sum_{p\in(X,2X]}
\frac1{Z(p)}\sum_{h\ne0}|F_p(h)|^4
\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^3,
\]
but Parseval only gives
\[
\sum_h|F_p(h)|^4\le Z(p)^2\sum_h|F_p(h)|^2
\ll pZ(p)^3,
\]
which is far too weak.

## 6. Verdict

No Apéry-specific fourth-moment or cross-prime Fourier estimate follows from the currently available Hasse ODE, reflection symmetry, and $p^{2/3}$ zero bound.

The weakest additional lemma that would close the Fourier route is one of:

1. **Additive-energy lemma:** uniformly
\[
E_+(Z_p)\ll Z(p)^{2+o(1)};
\]

2. **Fourier fourth-moment lemma:**
\[
\sum_{h}|F_p(h)|^4\ll pZ(p)^{2+o(1)};
\]

3. **Sheaf realization lemma:** construct a bounded-conductor sheaf giving $F_p(h)$ as a trace function with all exceptional frequencies classified.

Without one of these, the Hasse divisor geometry does not close the HM3 output-Palm estimate. The missing input is not another use of Parseval; it is genuine additive anti-concentration of the Apéry zero divisor.
