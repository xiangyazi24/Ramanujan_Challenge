# Stepanov attack on recurrence-orbit phases

## Verdict

The viable route is PARTIAL: the Weyl-sum reduction is sharp, but a classical Stepanov argument does not close without a new source of Frobenius-like relations. The best target is not an auxiliary polynomial on the raw orbit in A^4, but an auxiliary polynomial on the return/coincidence variety generated by N_h.

## (i) Configuration map and auxiliary polynomial formulation

On the chart b_r b_{r+1} != 0 write P_r=(r,x,y,u)=(r,b_r,b_{r+1},u_r). Put

A(r)=(34r^3+51r^2+27r+5)/(r+1)^3,
B(r)=r^3/(r+1)^3.

Then

Phi(r,x,y,u)=(r+1, y, A(r)y-B(r)x, u+6/((r+1)^3xy)).

The additive phase is the u-coordinate. A Stepanov auxiliary polynomial would be a polynomial Psi in coordinates of several iterates, with

Psi(P,Phi(P),...,Phi^m(P))

having high multiplicity along the orbit.

The desired conclusion would be:

If deg(Psi)<=E and multiplicity is k, then either the number of orbit points is O(E/k) or the phase sum has cancellation p^{-delta}.

The missing ingredient is the analogue of y^p-y. For multiplicative subgroups, Frobenius preserves the algebraic set and supplies many relations. Here Phi is nonautonomous and does not satisfy Phi^p=Phi. The Lucas relation gives a coarse global relation but collapses at one-digit scale.

The alternative is the coincidence variety. Let

C={(r,d):N_d(r)=0}.

An auxiliary polynomial A(r,d) of bidegree (R,S) can impose multiplicity k on C. Bezout gives roughly

#C <= (R*S)/k

unless C lies on a lower-degree component. Thus a contradiction to a D^2 collision count requires a degree budget with RS << D^2 and k large. The addition law is the only plausible relation supply because it gives identities among neighboring heights.

For D=sqrt(p)L, a useful lemma would need to show that any component supporting T(D) points has degree at least D^{eta}. Current addition-law identities do not yet provide this.

## (ii) Comparison with subgroup Stepanov methods

The subgroup case uses:

1. autonomous closure x -> gx;
2. many polynomial relations x_i x_j^{-1};
3. Frobenius compatibility of the defining variety.

The Apery orbit has only a replacement candidate:

N_{h+d}(x)=N_d(x+h)N_{h+1}(x)-(x+h+1)^6N_{d-1}(x+h+1)N_h(x).

This is a difference-closed return relation, not a group law. It controls collisions but does not automatically generate enough independent vanishing conditions.

Therefore importing subgroup Stepanov directly is invalid.

## Sharp auxiliary-polynomial lemma target

A sufficient theorem would be:

For some fixed epsilon>0, every nonzero auxiliary polynomial A(r,d) of bidegree at most (D^a,D^b) satisfying

ord_(r,d)(A) >= k

at every point with D/2<d<=D and N_d(r)=0 must satisfy

k <= C(D^{a+b}/D^{2-epsilon}).

Equivalently, multiplicity exceeding this threshold forces A to vanish identically on the coincidence variety, which is impossible.

Combined with the standard Stepanov amplification this would give Sigma(t,s)<<p^{1-delta}.

## Feasibility verdict

Not currently proved. The obstruction is not degree growth alone; it is the lack of an independent relation family. The addition law supplies propagation but may leave a large hidden component corresponding to genuine recurrence self-similarity.

## Machine-checkable experiments

1. Compute elimination ideals for

N_d(r)=0,
N_{d+1}(r)=0,
N_{d+2}(r)=0

and measure whether common components occur over Q.

2. Compute the rank of the span of shifted addition-law relations

N_{h+d}-N_d N_{h+1}+...

for h,d<=32.

3. Search for low-degree A(r,d) vanishing on all roots for small primes and compare dimensions with random point sets.

4. Compute whether the collision variety has unexpectedly small degree after compactification.

## Route verdict

A direct Stepanov-on-Phi proof is blocked. The only plausible Stepanov route is Stepanov-on-the-coincidence-variety, where the addition law must be upgraded into a genuine low-degree closure theorem.

Least confidence: the proposed Bezout threshold for the two-variable auxiliary polynomial; it assumes the compactified coincidence variety has no hidden low-degree components.

Blind spot: the question may underestimate the possibility that the correct invariant is not a polynomial relation but a rank-drop condition in the recurrence's exterior-square/transfer representation. A linear-algebraic invariant could supply the missing Frobenius substitute.
