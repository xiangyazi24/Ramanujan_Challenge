# Dwork overconvergent unit root and AFP

## Main conclusion

The Dwork unit-root direction is the strongest p-adic route, but one must correct one crucial point: overconvergence in the geometric parameter does **not automatically imply** overconvergence in the Kummer character parameter. The missing theorem is a horizontal/Iwasawa-type overconvergence statement for the character tower.

If such a theorem holds with bounded Weierstrass degree on the character disks, then AFP follows immediately by Strassmann. At present this is the genuine missing bridge.

## (A) Geometric Dwork theory

For the Apéry Picard-Fuchs crystal, Dwork theory gives an overconvergent F-crystal

\[
F: H^2_{cris}(X_t/W) \to H^2_{cris}(X_t/W).
\]

On an ordinary residue disk, the Frobenius matrix has a unit-root eigenvalue

\[
\alpha(t) \in \mathcal O(D)^\times.
\]

The important fact is not merely analyticity: overconvergence gives a radius of convergence strictly larger than the naive unit disk. Consequently the zero set of an analytic function is controlled by its Weierstrass degree.

For a fixed geometric parameter t, this theory is powerful.

However AFP is not asking about

\[
t\mapsto \alpha(t).
\]

It asks about

\[
r\mapsto \alpha(\omega^r),
\]

where r parametrizes a tame Kummer twist.

These are different directions.

## (B) Character-direction variation

The Kummer twist corresponds to tensoring by rank-one local systems

\[
\mathcal L_{\omega^r}.
\]

Thus the family is indexed by the character group

\[
\widehat{\mathbb F_p^\times}.
\]

The natural parameter space is not the original geometric moduli space but the character torus.

The desired statement would be:

\[
\alpha(r)\in \mathbb Z_p\langle T\rangle
\]

on each residue disk with uniformly bounded Weierstrass degree.

This is stronger than ordinary Dwork overconvergence.

The Dwork congruence

\[
b_r\equiv b_{r+p^s(p-1)}\pmod {p^{s+1}}
\]

shows p-adic continuity in the character direction, but continuity alone gives no zero bound.

A Lipschitz function on Z_p can have infinitely many zeros.

The missing input is analytic continuation plus degree control.

## (C) Does geometric overconvergence transfer to characters?

Not directly.

The geometric family is a smooth family of varieties. The Kummer character family is a family of local systems obtained by twisting Frobenius traces.

The character tower is closer to:

- Mellin transforms of overconvergent F-isocrystals;
- Iwasawa modules;
- p-adic Fourier transforms.

A possible bridge is the p-adic Mellin transform of overconvergent F-isocrystals. If the Mellin transform remains overconvergent in the character variable, then the unit root function becomes a bounded-degree analytic function.

The theorem needed is roughly:

"The unit-root part of the Mellin transform of the Apéry overconvergent F-crystal has uniformly bounded Newton polygon in the weight variable."

This is not a standard consequence of Katz's geometric overconvergence theorem.

## (D) Iwasawa interpretation

There is a strong analogy with p-adic L-functions.

A p-adic L-function is an analytic function

\[
L_p(T)\in \Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[T]].
\]

Its zero structure is controlled by Weierstrass preparation:

\[
L_p(T)=p^\mu P(T)U(T).
\]

The number of zeros is controlled by the degree of P, the lambda invariant.

The Apéry character function would ideally produce an analogous element

\[
A(T)\in \mathbb Z_p[[T]],
\]

with

\[
A((1+p)^r-1)=\alpha(r).
\]

Then AFP would follow from

\[
\deg P=O(1).
\]

But unlike Kubota-Leopoldt L-functions, no known Ferrero-Washington type theorem controls the lambda invariant of this hypergeometric/Iwasawa object.

So the analogy is useful but not yet a theorem.

## (E) Expected computational pattern

The relevant congruence to test is

\[
v_p(b_r-b_{r+p-1}).
\]

Dwork-type congruences predict at least

\[
v_p(b_r-b_{r+p-1})\ge 1.
\]

More generally:

\[
r\equiv r'\pmod {p^s(p-1)}
\implies
b_r\equiv b_{r'}\pmod {p^{s+1}}.
\]

This implies the character function is p-adically analytic only if the stronger Mahler coefficient decay holds.

The key numerical object is therefore not b_r itself but its character-direction Mahler expansion:

\[
\alpha(r)=\sum_n a_n\binom rn.
\]

The desired bound is:

\[
v_p(a_n)\to\infty
\]

fast enough that the first nonzero coefficient modulo p occurs at bounded n.

That first nonzero index is exactly the Weierstrass degree.

## Final assessment

1. Geometric Dwork overconvergence: known and strong.

2. Character-direction overconvergence: plausible but the missing theorem.

3. Dwork congruences alone: insufficient; they give continuity, not bounded zeros.

4. Iwasawa analogy: very promising. AFP would follow from a bounded lambda invariant for the Apéry Mellin unit-root function.

5. The most concrete next step is to compute the character-direction Mahler coefficients of the unit-root approximation and test whether the first nonzero coefficient modulo p has uniformly bounded index.

This gives a precise formulation of the remaining p-adic obstacle:

**Horizontal Dwork theorem for the Apéry Mellin unit-root character family.**

Proving this would convert the observed Poisson(1/2) zero statistics into a Strassmann theorem proof of AFP.
