# Orbit discrepancy and the AFP frontier

## 1. L2 versus L1: what the collision statistic really gives

The observed collision law

\[
C(p)=\sum_b N_b^2=2p+o(p)
\]

is exactly the Parseval scale. Writing the indicator of the target fiber by additive characters gives

\[
\sum_{a\neq0}|S(a)|^2\ll p^2,
\]

which is the optimal second moment. However AFP needs control of one specific fiber, namely the Steinberg orbit count for the target [0:1]. A generic conversion from L2 to L∞ loses a factor of \(\sqrt p\):

\[
|N_b-1|\leq \sqrt{p}\,D_2.
\]

Therefore random-function-level L2 is not by itself enough to prove bounded fibers unless one has a genuinely arithmetic strengthening.

The relevant missing estimate is an L1 or cancellation estimate for the trace expansion:

\[
\sum_a |S(a)|=O(p),
\]

or, equivalently, a direct bound for the single target coefficient.

## 2. Why Erdős–Turán/Vinogradov is insufficient alone

Erdős–Turán gives discrepancy bounds of the shape

\[
D_\infty \ll \frac1H+\sum_{1\leq h\leq H}\frac{|\widehat\mu(h)|}{h}.
\]

It is useful when one controls many Fourier modes. The Apéry orbit problem is different: the target is one exceptional point in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\). A global discrepancy estimate does not prevent a single point from having excess mass.

Thus the required theorem is not a generic equidistribution theorem but a pointwise exceptional-fiber estimate.

## 3. Reformulation through the Steinberg representation

Let

\[
N_{[0:1]}=\#\{r:M(r)[5:1]=[0:1]\}.
\]

After subtracting the uniform term, this is the Steinberg matrix coefficient

\[
\sum_r \operatorname{St}_{[5:1],[0:1]}(M(r)).
\]

Square-root cancellation would give

\[
O(\sqrt p),
\]

which is the natural Deligne scale for a trace function. But AFP requires essentially

\[
O(1).
\]

So the key point is correct: AFP is not merely an equidistribution statement. It asks for a special cancellation phenomenon beyond the generic Riemann-hypothesis scale.

## 4. Possible mechanisms for the extra cancellation

A generic l-adic sheaf argument is unlikely to give O(1). The plausible mechanisms are Apéry-specific:

1. **Hidden coboundary.** If the Steinberg observable satisfies

\[
\operatorname{St}(M(r))=F(r+1)-F(r),
\]

then the complete sum telescopes and becomes O(1). This matches the earlier hidden conservation-law direction.

2. **Monodromy constraint.** The Mellin/transfer sheaf may have a special self-duality or extra symmetry forcing the relevant projection of the trace function to vanish.

3. **Recurrence rigidity.** The matrices are not an arbitrary SL₂ walk: they come from the Apéry differential equation. A second-order recurrence may impose algebraic relations among orbit hits that random functions do not satisfy.

4. **Reflection plus Casoratian structure.** The zero set has the involution \(r\mapsto p-1-r\), and the recurrence has a nontrivial determinant invariant. A pointwise cancellation theorem may have to exploit both.

## 5. Can proving D₂=O(p^{-1/2}) prove AFP?

Care is needed. The statement

\[
D_2=O(p^{-1/2})
\]

only implies

\[
|N_b-\frac{p-1}{p+1}|=O(1),
\]

because

\[
\sqrt{p+1}D_2=O(1).
\]

So yes: a genuine L2 theorem with the optimal constant scale would imply AFP. But proving this L2 theorem is already essentially equivalent to proving square-root cancellation for the full Steinberg component.

The remaining gap is that standard Deligne gives square-root cancellation for fixed-rank sheaves, while the Steinberg representation has dimension \(p\). One needs either a uniform high-rank argument or a structural reduction to bounded rank.

## 6. Conclusion

The orbit data is extremely strong evidence that the Apéry orbit behaves like a random function, not like a random walk. But the proof bottleneck is now sharply identified:

\[
\boxed{\text{AFP requires a special O(1) cancellation for one Steinberg coefficient.}}
\]

The next realistic route is not improving generic discrepancy bounds. It is to search for an Apéry-specific identity making the Steinberg observable a coboundary, or for a hidden symmetry reducing the Steinberg coefficient to a bounded-rank trace sum.
