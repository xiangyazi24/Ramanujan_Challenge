# 1. First consequences of the large-lower-prime window

Assume

\[
\frac m7<p<q,
\qquad m=6q+u,
\]

and put

\[
d:=q-p>0.
\]

Then

\[
7(q-d)>6q+u
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{q-u>7d.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Also

\[
m=6p+r,
\qquad
\boxed{r=u+6d},
\tag{1.2}
\]

and (1.1) is exactly what is needed for

\[
\boxed{0\le r<p.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Therefore the base-`p` leading digit of `m` is **exactly 6**. Apéry--Lucas gives

\[
b_m\equiv b_6b_r\pmod p.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Outside the fixed exceptional set `Sigma_0`, `b_6` is a `p`-unit, so every large lower prime dividing either stripped gcd satisfies

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_r.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

This is the common first zero in all four branches.

# 2. Minus gcd: digit 1 versus reflection

Consider

\[
k=q-1-u.
\]

Since `q=p+d`,

\[
\boxed{k=p+(d-1-u).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

There are exactly two cases.

## 2.1 `u<d`: quotient digit 1, true minus secondary zero

Now

\[
L:=d-1-u\ge0.
\]

Because `d<p`, also `L<p`, and

\[
k=p+L<2p.
\]

Thus the base-`p` leading digit of `k` is **1**, and Lucas gives

\[
b_k\equiv b_1b_L=5b_L\pmod p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since `p` is outside `Sigma_0`, `5` is a unit. Therefore

\[
p\mid b_k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_L.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Together with (1.5), this is exactly the legal minus secondary-zero pair. Moreover

\[
\boxed{r+L=7d-1.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

So the two `p`-zeros lie on the prescribed anti-diagonal of length `7d`.

Write the pair in increasing order as `a<b`. Then

\[
a+b=7d-1,
\qquad a,b<p.
\]

If `a<b`, the transfer identity (0.2) gives

\[
p\mid N_{b-a}(a),
\]

which is one factor of `mathscr K_{7d}`. If `a=b`, then `p|b_a`, which is exactly the central factor in (V.1). Hence

\[
\boxed{p\mid\mathscr K_{7d}.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

This proves the first line of (V.2).

## 2.2 `u>=d`: no secondary digit; reflect the second zero

Now (2.1) gives

\[
k\le p-1.
\]

Since `p|S^-_{q,u}`, we have `p|b_k`. Reflect inside characteristic `p`:

\[
p\mid b_{p-1-k}.
\]

But

\[
p-1-k
=p-1-(p+d-1-u)
=\boxed{u-d}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Set

\[
s:=u-d\ge0.
\]

Then

\[
r=u+6d=s+7d.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Both endpoints lie below `p`, so the exact transfer identity yields

\[
\boxed{p\mid N_{7d}(u-d).}
\tag{2.8}
\]

This proves the second line of (V.2).

The important point is that **every** large lower prime in the minus stripped gcd falls into one of these two symbolic branches. The finite scan is not needed, and the second branch does not assume that a true minus-secondary example exists.

# 5. Why this does not yet prove `61`-smoothness

The pushed adjacent-resultant theorem says

\[
\left|\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+1})\right|
=
\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(j!^3b_j)^6.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is powerful when one knows that the **same residue class** is a root of both `N_h` and `N_{h+1}` modulo `p`. Then `p` is forced into the explicit right-hand side.

Q3752 gives only one continuant congruence at a time:

\[
p\mid N_{7d}(u-d)
\]

or

\[
p\mid N_{6d}(u+6d),
\]

or one anti-diagonal two-zero condition encoded by `mathscr K_H`.

The condition `q|b_u` lives in **characteristic q=p+d**, not in characteristic `p`. It does not supply a second polynomial root modulo `p`, so it cannot simply be fed into (5.1). This is the exact reason I do not see a legitimate fixed resultant whose prime support is uniformly small.

Likewise, real-root interlacing and nonvanishing of `N_h mod p` control the number/location of roots as a polynomial in `X`; they do **not** bound the prime factors of the integer value `N_h(s)`.

Therefore the experimental bound “max stripped prime factor 61 through q<1800” remains a conjectural arithmetic phenomenon, not a consequence of the current continuant toolkit.

# 6. The two literal smoothness loopholes outside the lower-prime theorem

The theorem above completely handles the prime range relevant to a lower endpoint of a quotient-six edge. It does **not** prove that the *entire integer* `S^-_{q,u}` or `S^+_{q,u}` is smooth, for two separate reasons.

## 6.1 The vertical prime `p=q`: one stripped layer does not rule out `q` again

Write

\[
\eta_u:=b_u/q.
\]

Define the exact first-digit lifts

\[
\lambda_a(q,u)
:=\frac{b_{aq+u}-b_ab_u}{q}
\qquad(a=6,12),
\tag{6.1}
\]

and the reflection lift

\[
\rho(q,u)
:=\frac{b_{q-1-u}-b_u}{q}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

These are integers because the corresponding mod-`q` Lucas/reflection congruences hold.

Then

\[
q\mid S^-_{q,u}
\]

is equivalent to the two first-jet congruences

\[
\boxed{
b_6\eta_u+\lambda_6(q,u)\equiv0\pmod q,
\qquad
\eta_u+\rho(q,u)\equiv0\pmod q.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Similarly

\[
q\mid S^+_{q,u}
\]

is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
b_6\eta_u+\lambda_6(q,u)\equiv0\pmod q,
\qquad
b_{12}\eta_u+\lambda_{12}(q,u)\equiv0\pmod q.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

So a literal proof that the stripped gcd has no large `q` factor needs a nondegeneracy theorem for these first jets. The scan can test (6.3)--(6.4), but cannot prove them nonzero.

This does **not** hurt the star-edge carrier of Q3750: an edge lower prime always has `p<q`, so one forced `q` layer is all that needs to be removed for edge multiplicity. It matters only for the stronger statement “the whole stripped integer is smooth.”

## 6.2 Prime factors `p>q`

A prime factor of an integer Apéry value can exceed its index, so `p>q` is not formally impossible.

If `q<p<=m/6`, one can still obtain short continuant conditions. For example, putting `e=p-q>0` and assuming `6e<=u`, the minus gcd gives

\[
p\mid N_{7e}(u-6e)
\tag{6.5}
\]

after reflecting `k=q-1-u` in characteristic `p`.

For the plus gcd, if `12e<=u` the two residual zeros are separated by `6e`; if `6e<=u<12e`, quotient digit `11` enters and reflection produces a `6e-1` anti-diagonal. Once `p>m/6`, the leading digit of `m` drops below `6` and more fixed-digit cases appear.

Thus the elegant `6/1/12/13` four-way theorem is exactly the **lower-prime** theorem. A literal all-prime smoothness statement requires additional cases above `q`.

# 7. Precise conjecture exposed by the reduction

The computation suggests the following stronger statement, but it should be kept explicitly conjectural.

> **Lower-prime smoothness conjecture.** There is an absolute constant `B` (the experiment suggests `B=61`) such that for every prime `q`, every `0<=u<q/7` with `q|b_u`, and every prime
> \[
> (6q+u)/7<p<q,
> \]
> if `p` divides either `S^-_{q,u}` or `S^+_{q,u}`, then `p<=B`.

By (V.2)--(V.3), outside the fixed digit-prime set this would follow from the four nondivisibility statements

\[
q-d\nmid\mathscr K_{7d}
\quad(u<d),
\tag{7.1}
\]

\[
q-d\nmid N_{7d}(u-d)
\quad(u\ge d),
\tag{7.2}
\]

\[
q-d\nmid\mathscr K_{6d}
\quad(q-u\le13d),
\tag{7.3}
\]

\[
q-d\nmid N_{6d}(u+6d)
\quad(q-u>13d),
\tag{7.4}
\]

for `d=q-p` in the forced range

\[
1\le d<(q-u)/7.
\tag{7.5}
\]

The first and third are exactly the minus/plus secondary-zero sectors; the second and fourth are the reflected/digit-12 same-characteristic sectors.

This is a materially sharper target than “factor the stripped gcd”: it identifies the exact finite continuants that a large lower-prime counterexample must divide.

# 8. Regression plan (evidence only)

A useful checker should record the symbolic branch before any factorization result is inspected.

For each prime `q` in the test range and each `u<q/7` with `q|b_u`:

```text
m = 6*q + u
k = q - 1 - u
N = 12*q + u

for each prime p with m/7 < p < q:
    d = q-p
    r = u+6*d
    assert 0 <= r < p

    if p divides gcd(b_m,b_k)/q:
        if u < d:
            assert p divides Kanti(7*d)       # or the exact corresponding N-factor
        else:
            assert p divides N_{7*d}(u-d)

    if p divides gcd(b_m,b_N)/q:
        if q-u <= 13*d:
            assert p divides Kanti(6*d)
        else:
            assert p divides N_{6*d}(u+6*d)
```

Separately test the vertical `q^2` conditions (6.3)--(6.4), and separately log prime factors `p>q` rather than silently classifying them as lower edges.

The observed maximum prime factor `61` and the absence of secondary examples are useful regression facts, but neither is used as a theorem.

# Bottom line

The exact unconditional gain is not a proof of constant smoothness. It is the four-way divisor reduction

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{stripped gcd}&\text{digit/reflection branch}&\text{forced prescribed divisor}\\ \hline
S^-&u<d\ (6,1)&\mathscr K_{7d}\\
S^-&u\ge d\ (6,\mathrm{reflection})&N_{7d}(u-d)\\
S^+&q-u\le13d\ (6,13)&\mathscr K_{6d}\\
S^+&q-u>13d\ (6,12)&N_{6d}(u+6d).
\end{array}}
\]

Every legal large lower-prime factor outside the fixed digit-prime set must occur in exactly one of these recurrence channels. Proving that these special values avoid `p=q-d` is the precise next theorem; the `q<1800` scan is evidence for it, not a proof.
