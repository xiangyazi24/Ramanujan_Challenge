# Deterministic adjacent-gap attack: a recurrence-level CRT obstruction

## Verdict

I do **not** get an \(o_q(n)\)-height product/telescope from the adjacent target gaps. Instead there is a clean scoped obstruction which is stronger than the already-known statement that the ordinary continuant is a unit on a pure-cross pair.

For every fixed \(q\), and for every sufficiently large \(n\), the **exact factorial-normalized Apéry recurrence itself** admits an integer solution, with one common integral initial state, for which **every prime**

\[
\frac{n}{q+1}<p\le \frac nq
\]

is a target at the prescribed index \(r_p=n-qp\). Moreover:

* the construction obeys all recurrence-universal gap/continuant identities;
* it obeys the exact reflection row identity modulo every \(p\);
* it has no two consecutive zeros in the nonsingular range;
* along the infinite specialization \(n=m+q\) with \(m\) prime, every selected prime has
  \[
  \gcd(p-1,n-q)=1,
  \]
  so all targets are in the maximally nearly-primitive, common-kernel-\(1\) sector;
* after ordering these primes, \(\gg_q n/\log n\) adjacent edges have prime gap \(O(\log n)\), and all but \(O_q((\log n)^2)\) of those edges are pure cross.

Thus adjacency, small total adjacent-gap budget, exact transfer algebra, reflection, the gap polynomials, and nearly-primitive/common-kernel data are **jointly compatible with worst-case horizontal alignment** unless one uses arithmetic that selects the distinguished Apéry initial vector \((b_0,b_1)=(1,5)\).

There is also an unavoidable height consequence. In this recurrence countermodel the radical of all target primes has

\[
\log R_{n,q}
=artheta(n/q)-artheta(n/(q+1))
=\frac{n}{q(q+1)}+o_q(n).
\]

Hence no recurrence-universal adjacent-edge telescope can produce a **nonzero** integer divisible by the whole target radical and of logarithmic height \(o_q(n)\): such an integer would already have absolute value at least \(R_{n,q}\).

So the new deterministic conclusion is a no-go, but it is sharply scoped:

> **Any successful adjacent-gap compression for the real Apéry sequence must visibly use the distinguished initial state (or equivalent Cartier/WZ/global arithmetic). A proof that survives replacement of \(b\) by an arbitrary solution of the same Apéry recurrence cannot work.**

This does **not** disprove an Apéry-specific adjacency telescope, and it does not solve the all-index gcd conjecture.

# 2. Exact recurrence normalization

Write

\[
P(k)=34k^3+51k^2+27k+5.
\]

Every solution \(u_k\) of the Apéry recurrence satisfies

\[
(k+1)^3u_{k+1}=P(k)u_k-k^3u_{k-1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Put

\[
V_k=(k!)^3u_k.
\]

Then (2.1) becomes the **integral** recurrence

\[
\boxed{
V_{k+1}=P(k)V_k-k^6V_{k-1}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For \(k\ge1\), its state transfer is

\[
\binom{V_{k+1}}{V_k}
=A_k\binom{V_k}{V_{k-1}},
\qquad
A_k=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(k)&-k^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{2.3}
\]

with

\[
\det A_k=k^6.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Hence, modulo a prime \(p\), every transfer \(A_k\) with \(1\le k<p\) is invertible.

Let \(X=(V_1,V_0)^t\). For every \(r\ge0\) there is an integral row \(L_r\in\mathbb Z^{1\times2}\) such that

\[
V_r=L_rX.
\tag{2.5}
\]

If \(r<p\), then \(L_r\not\equiv0\pmod p\). For \(r=0,1\) this is immediate; for \(r\ge2\) it follows because the product of the matrices (2.3) from the initial state to index \(r\) is invertible modulo \(p\).

This elementary observation is the key horizontal freedom that an adjacent-gap proof has to defeat.

# 3. Exact CRT recurrence-star theorem

## Theorem 3.1 — arbitrary prescribed target column for the Apéry recurrence

Fix \(q\ge1\) and \(n\), and let

\[
\mathcal P_{n,q}
=
\left\{
 p\ge5\text{ prime}:
 \frac{n}{q+1}<p\le\frac nq
\right\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For every \(p\in\mathcal P_{n,q}\), put

\[
r_p=n-qp.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then \(0\le r_p<p\). There exists a nonzero integral initial vector

\[
X=(V_1,V_0)^t\in\mathbb Z^2
\tag{3.3}
\]

such that the integer sequence generated by (2.2) satisfies

\[
\boxed{
p\mid V_{r_p}\qquad(p\in\mathcal P_{n,q}).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently, for \(u_r=V_r/(r!)^3\),

\[
u_{r_p}\equiv0\pmod p
\tag{3.5}
\]

because \(r_p!\) is a \(p\)-unit.

### Proof

For one \(p\), the nonzero row \(L_{r_p}\) has a one-dimensional kernel

\[
K_p=\ker(L_{r_p}:\mathbb F_p^2\to\mathbb F_p).
\]

Choose any nonzero \(X_p\in K_p\). Let

\[
R=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_{n,q}}p.
\]

Apply ordinary coordinatewise CRT to the two coordinates of the \(X_p\)'s. There exists \(X\in\mathbb Z^2\) with

\[
X\equiv X_p\pmod p
\qquad(p\in\mathcal P_{n,q}).
\]

Then

\[
V_{r_p}=L_{r_p}X
\equiv L_{r_p}X_p
=0\pmod p.
\]

Since every \(X_p\) is nonzero, the CRT lift \(X\) cannot be the zero vector modulo every prime, and one may choose a nonzero integral representative. This proves (3.4). \(\square\)

## Remark 3.2 — this is one common global solution, not one solution per prime

The point is important. We do **not** select a different recurrence sequence for every characteristic. CRT is applied only once to the two initial coordinates. After that there is one integer recurrence sequence \(V_0,V_1,V_2,\ldots\) satisfying the exact Apéry recurrence in characteristic zero, and its reductions hit all the prescribed local zero lines simultaneously.

The initial state depends on \(n,q\); that is exactly what makes this a countermodel to recurrence-universal horizontal selection and not a counterexample to the distinguished Apéry sequence.

# 4. The Smith-index version: why local transfer algebra cannot compress the column

The preceding construction has an exact lattice formulation.

Define

\[
\Phi_{n,q}:\mathbb Z^2
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P_{n,q}}\mathbb F_p,
\qquad
X\longmapsto
\bigl(L_{r_p}X\bmod p\bigr)_p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Every component \(L_{r_p}:\mathbb F_p^2\to\mathbb F_p\) is onto because it is a nonzero linear functional. Given arbitrary residues \(y_p\in\mathbb F_p\), choose a local vector \(X_p\) with \(L_{r_p}X_p=y_p\), then CRT the two coordinates. Thus

\[
\Phi_{n,q}\text{ is surjective.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
[\mathbb Z^2:\ker\Phi_{n,q}]
=
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_{n,q}}p.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the global Smith content of the marked zero conditions. Any multiplication by a nonsingular transfer matrix in the range \(k<p\) is merely an invertible local change of basis. It can move the local zero row from one index to another, rewrite it by a continuant, or express it through a Casoratian, but it does not lower the local index \(p\).

This is the cross-prime analogue of the repository's existing local-SNF warning. Tensor/symmetric-power rank amplification cannot turn these independent local index-\(p\) conditions into a smaller global lattice index without adding a genuinely new row that selects the distinguished initial state.

# 5. Reflection does not rescue the recurrence-star

The current `full_cycle_bridge_reciprocity.tex` proves, for the factorial-normalized solution rows in characteristic \(p\), the pointwise reflection relation

\[
\mathbf v_{p-1-r}
=\rho_r\mathbf v_r,
\qquad
\rho_r=(-1)^{r+1}(r!)^{-6}\in\mathbb F_p^\times.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Taking the two standard initial-state basis solutions identifies the evaluation rows themselves:

\[
\boxed{
L_{p-1-r}\equiv\rho_rL_r\pmod p.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore the CRT solution of Theorem 3.1 automatically satisfies

\[
V_{r_p}\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_{p-1-r_p}\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{5.3}
\]

So the countermodel is not evading the reflection symmetry by choosing arbitrary local rows. It lies on the exact reflected return line furnished by the recurrence.

Likewise, it has no two consecutive zeros in \(0,\ldots,p-1\). If

\[
V_j\equiv V_{j+1}\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

then the state \((V_{j+1},V_j)\) is zero. The invertible transfers (2.3) propagate this backward to the initial state, contradicting \(X_p\ne0\). The \(j=0\) case is immediate.

Thus adding reflection and no-consecutive-zeros does not select the Apéry initial state.

# 7. Adjacent gaps in the recurrence-star: linearly many short pure-cross edges

Order the primes in the arc:

\[
p_1<p_2<\cdots<p_K,
\qquad
h_i=p_{i+1}-p_i.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The associated zero indices decrease by

\[
r_{p_i}-r_{p_{i+1}}
=q h_i.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The total prime-gap budget is exactly bounded by the arc length:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}h_i
=p_K-p_1
\le
\frac nq-\frac{n}{q+1}
=rac{n}{q(q+1)}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

and hence

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}(r_{p_i}-r_{p_{i+1}})
\le\frac{n}{q+1}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

The prime number theorem gives, for fixed \(q\),

\[
K
=\pi(n/q)-\pi(n/(q+1))
=\left(\frac1{q(q+1)}+o_q(1)\right)
\frac{n}{\log n}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Fix \(A>2\). By (7.3), the number of edges with

\[
h_i>A\log n
\]

is at most

\[
\frac{n}{Aq(q+1)\log n}.
\]

Combining with (7.5), a positive fixed proportion of all adjacent edges satisfies

\[
h_i\le A\log n.
\tag{7.6}
\]

The only remaining question is whether reflection or same-characteristic returns make most of these edges degenerate. They do not.

## Lemma 7.1 — the universal degenerate-edge bound

Let \(p<\ell=p+h\) be two target primes in the fixed-\(q\) arc, and put

\[
s=n-q\ell,
\qquad H=qh.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Then

\[
p\mid V_{s+H},
\qquad
\ell\mid V_s.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Call the edge degenerate if either

\[
p\mid V_s
\quad\text{or}\quad
\ell\mid V_{s+H};
\tag{7.9}
\]

otherwise pure cross.

Let \(N_H(x)\) be the integral gap continuant, with

\[
N_0=0,\quad N_1=1,\quad
N_{H+1}(x)=P(x+H)N_H(x)-(x+H)^6N_{H-1}(x).
\tag{7.10}
\]

The factorial-normalized transfer identity is

\[
V_{x+H}
=N_H(x)V_{x+1}
-(x+1)^6N_{H-1}(x+1)V_x.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Because there are no consecutive zeros, two zero endpoints in one characteristic force the corresponding \(N_H\) to vanish. Therefore:

* if \(p\mid V_s,V_{s+H}\), then \(p\mid N_H(s)\). Since
  \[
  s=n-q(p+h)\equiv n-qh\pmod p,
  \]
  we get
  \[
  p\mid N_{qh}(n-qh);
  \tag{7.12}
  \]
* if \(\ell\mid V_s,V_{s+H}\), then \(s=n-q\ell\equiv n\pmod\ell\), so
  \[
  \ell\mid N_{qh}(n).
  \tag{7.13}
  \]

Thus every degenerate gap-\(h\) edge supplies a large prime divisor of the fixed nonzero integer

\[
D_{q,h}(n)
=N_{qh}(n-qh)N_{qh}(n).
\tag{7.14}
\]

The root-strip/nonvanishing result used in `near_bridge_energy.tex` ensures that these positive arguments do not hit a characteristic-zero root. The elementary recurrence/tridiagonal estimate gives

\[
\log|D_{q,h}(n)|\ll_q h\log n.
\tag{7.15}
\]

Every endpoint prime is \(>n/(q+1)\), hence has logarithm \(\asymp_q\log n\). Therefore \(D_{q,h}(n)\) has only \(O_q(h)\) distinct possible endpoint primes in the arc. An endpoint can occur in at most two ordered adjacent edges of a fixed gap. Hence

\[
\boxed{
M_{q,h}^{\rm deg}(n)\ll_q h.
}
\tag{7.16}
\]

Summing over \(h\le A\log n\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{h\le A\log n}M_{q,h}^{\rm deg}(n)
=O_{q,A}((\log n)^2).
}
\tag{7.17}
\]

This is exactly the deterministic degenerate-edge estimate `(PC.13)` from the surviving historical separation audit, but note that its proof is recurrence-universal: it applies to the CRT solution as well as to the distinguished Apéry sequence.

## Corollary 7.2 — dense short pure-cross adjacency is recurrence-compatible

By (7.5)–(7.6), the recurrence-star has

\[
\gg_{q,A}\frac n{\log n}
\]

short adjacent target edges. By (7.17), only \(O_{q,A}((\log n)^2)\) can be degenerate. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
A_{q,A\log n}^{\times}(n)
\gg_{q,A}\frac n{\log n}
}
\tag{7.18}
\]

for the CRT recurrence-star.

Along \(n=m+q\) from Section 6, the same statement holds **inside the common-kernel-\(1\), maximally nearly-primitive sector**.

This is the promised scoped countermodel to adjacency selection.

# 8. Why multiplying adjacent certificates is intrinsically anti-compressive

Suppose one somehow finds, for every adjacent target edge, a nonzero integer \(C_i\) satisfying

\[
p_i p_{i+1}\mid C_i.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then the naive product obeys the exact lower bound

\[
\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}|C_i|
\ge
p_1p_K\prod_{i=2}^{K-1}p_i^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Writing

\[
R=\prod_{i=1}^Kp_i,
\]

this is

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}|C_i|
\ge\frac{R^2}{p_1p_K}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\log\left|\prod_iC_i\right|
\ge2\log R-O(\log n).
\tag{8.4}
\]

So an ordinary edge product double-counts every interior target prime. It is worse than simply using one integer divisible by the radical.

The gap-polynomial upper-height bookkeeping points in the same direction. A length-\(qh\) continuant evaluated at an argument of size \(O(n)\) costs

\[
O_q(h\log n)
\]

in logarithmic height. Summing this over adjacent edges and using only (7.3) gives

\[
O_q\!\left(\log n\sum_i h_i\right)
=O_q(n\log n),
\tag{8.5}
\]

not \(o(n)\).

Even an ideal normalization that removed the whole \(\log n\) factor and cost only \(O_q(h_i)\) per edge would give merely

\[
O_q\left(\sum_i h_i\right)=O_q(n),
\tag{8.6}
\]

still not little-oh. The fact that adjacent gaps telescope geometrically gives a **linear** budget, not a sublinear one.

Therefore a useful telescope would need genuine arithmetic cancellation between successive edges, not just the identity

\[
\sum h_i=p_K-p_1.
\]

The next section shows that no such cancellation can be recurrence-universal.

# 9. The decisive nonzero-height obstruction to any recurrence-universal telescope

Assume there were a deterministic construction \(\mathcal T\) with the following properties for a fixed \(q\):

1. its input is a solution of the Apéry recurrence together with an ordered fixed-\(q\) target set;
2. its proof uses only recurrence-universal transfer/gap/continuant/Smith/reflection identities and the target-zero assertions at the prescribed indices;
3. it produces a nonzero integer \(C_{n,q}\) such that
   \[
   \prod_{p\in T_{n,q}}p\mid C_{n,q};
   \tag{9.1}
   \]
4. uniformly for those inputs,
   \[
   \log|C_{n,q}|=o_q(n).
   \tag{9.2}
   \]

Apply it to the CRT recurrence-star of Theorem 3.1. Its target set is the entire prime arc, so

\[
R_{n,q}^{\rm star}
=\prod_{n/(q+1)<p\le n/q}p.
\tag{9.3}
\]

By the prime number theorem in Chebyshev form,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log R_{n,q}^{\rm star}
&=\vartheta(n/q)-\vartheta(n/(q+1))\\
&=\frac nq-\frac{n}{q+1}+o_q(n)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{n}{q(q+1)}+o_q(n)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.4}
\]

But (9.1) and \(C_{n,q}\ne0\) imply

\[
\log|C_{n,q}|
\ge\log R_{n,q}^{\rm star}
=\frac{n}{q(q+1)}+o_q(n),
\tag{9.5}
\]

contradicting (9.2).

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No recurrence-universal adjacent-gap telescope can have the desired }o_q(n)\text{ height.}
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

This obstruction does not care how ingenious the cancellation is. It applies after all intermediate denominators, products, Plücker relations, reflected multipliers, or common factors have been cancelled. The only assumptions retained at the end are that the output is a **nonzero integer**, contains every target prime, and the construction remains valid for every solution satisfying the same local recurrence identities.

That is strictly stronger than observing that a particular pure-cross continuant is a local unit.

# 10. Why this is not the old pure-cross-unit no-go

The historical pure-cross computation says that for a genuine pure-cross adjacent pair the ordinary length-\(qh\) continuant \(N_{qh}(s)\) is a unit in both moving characteristics. That kills the most obvious same-prime return certificate.

The present obstruction says something different:

* grant a hypothetical new edge invariant, even one not equal to \(N_{qh}\);
* grant arbitrary telescoping between consecutive edges;
* grant reflection and nearly-primitive/common-kernel information;
* grant exact Smith/Fitting manipulations;
* require only that the argument be universal for the rank-two Apéry recurrence.

Then the CRT recurrence-star forces its output to contain an arc radical of linear logarithmic size. Therefore the desired \(o(n)\) output is impossible.

So the issue is no longer “we have not found the right continuant.” The issue is that **recurrence-universal local algebra has no way to know that the initial vector is the Apéry vector**.

# 12. A useful “unit test” for future adjacency identities

The obstruction gives a very cheap fail-closed test for any proposed adjacent-gap lemma.

Suppose one derives a candidate identity from the recurrence, gap continuants, reflected transfers, Plücker/Casoratian relations, or Smith normal forms and claims that, whenever the prescribed target zeros occur, a low-height integer is divisible by the target radical.

Before doing any global height analysis, replace \(b\) by an arbitrary solution \(V\) of (2.2).

* If the identity and divisibility proof still go through from the zero hypotheses alone, the CRT recurrence-star applies, and an \(o_q(n)\)-height conclusion is impossible.
* If the argument breaks, identify exactly the first step where it uses \(b_0=1,b_1=5\), the constant-term representation \(b_r=\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^r\), a Cartier numerator, a WZ certificate, or another global property of the distinguished Apéry solution. **That step is the actual new arithmetic content.**

This separates potentially live identities from recurrence repackagings immediately.

# 13. What an adjacent-gap proof would now have to look like

The obstruction leaves one narrow possibility open: an identity which is genuinely **initial-state selective**.

A successful statement could have the form

\[
R_{n,q}^{\rm Ap}\mid C_{n,q}^{\rm Ap},
\qquad
0<|C_{n,q}^{\rm Ap}|,
\qquad
\log|C_{n,q}^{\rm Ap}|=o_q(n),
\tag{13.1}
\]

where the construction of \(C_{n,q}^{\rm Ap}\) uses something unavailable for the CRT recurrence-star. Examples of logically sufficient extra input would be:

1. a Cartier/WZ numerator identity tied to \(b_r=\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^r\) which couples successive **different characteristics** before taking absolute height;
2. a genuinely new marked row whose evaluation on the Apéry initial vector is controlled but whose evaluation on a generic initial vector is not;
3. a fixed-\(q\) holonomic-gcd theorem for the actual Apéry coefficient and a factorial-ratio carrier, of the type already isolated in the repository's `(HD.4)`/`(HD.5)` lane;
4. a quotient-digit or higher \(p\)-adic relation that is compatible across the adjacent primes and is not a coordinatewise CRT condition.

In contrast, the following cannot be the missing ingredient by themselves:

* \(\sum h_i=O_q(n)\);
* \(\sum qh_i=O_q(n)\);
* reflection;
* no consecutive zeros;
* the degree \(O(qh)\) of \(N_{qh}\);
* maximal Kummer order;
* common kernel \(g=1\);
* local transfer Smith normal form;
* tensor/symmetric-power amplification of the same recurrence row.

The CRT star satisfies all of those relevant local constraints while carrying a linear-height prime radical.

---

# 14. Consequence for the square-root/all-index barrier

For the actual problem, a forbidden large-prime contribution can first be localized to a fixed quotient \(q\). The historical `(PC.15)` reduction is therefore still a valid and very focused *target theorem*: after nearly-primitive pruning, enough targets force \(\gg n/\log n\) adjacent pure-cross pairs with gap \(O(\log n)\).

What the present audit changes is the interpretation of that target.

It is **not** plausible that `(PC.15)` will fall from a clever multiplication of the universal gap polynomials along the adjacent chain. The total-gap identity only lowers the parameter count from “all pairs” to one path; it does not lower the path's arithmetic information content. A dense path still carries one independent index-\(p\) condition at every vertex, and the global Smith index is their full product.

Thus the adjacent-gap idea does remove the old \(H^4\log H\) all-pairs height explosion, but it stops at a more fundamental wall:

\[
\boxed{
\text{one independent local prime condition per adjacent-chain vertex.}
}
\]

To cross the square-root barrier, one must show that the **specific Apéry initial vector** cannot lie on such a dense collection of CRT zero lines. That is exactly the different-characteristic alignment problem, not a consequence of the sum of adjacent gaps.

---

# 15. Final adjudication

The proposed adjacent-gap telescope does not presently yield a positive lemma. There is, however, an exact deterministic obstruction which is strong enough to close the **recurrence-universal adjacency-product/telescope class**:

> For fixed \(q\), CRT on the two integral initial coordinates of the factorial-normalized Apéry recurrence constructs one global integer solution targeting every prime in the \(q\)-arc. Along \(n=m+q\) with \(m\) prime, all those primes have common kernel \(1\) and maximal Kummer order. Reflection is automatic from the recurrence's row reciprocity. The universal gap-polynomial estimate shows that all but \(O((\log n)^2)\) of the \(\gg n/\log n\) short adjacent edges are pure cross. The target radical has logarithmic size \(n/(q(q+1))+o(n)\). Therefore no nonzero recurrence-universal telescope divisible by the full target radical can have logarithmic height \(o_q(n)\).

This does **not** claim the Apéry sequence realizes the countermodel. It proves precisely why adjacency does not select genuine Apéry targets: the missing selector is the distinguished initial state/global constant-term arithmetic.

Accordingly I would not spend another cycle searching for a pure continuant telescope unless the candidate identity visibly contains a term that vanishes or simplifies specifically at \(X=(5,1)\) (equivalently \(b_0=1,b_1=5\)) and fails for a generic initial state. That is the necessary escape hatch from the obstruction above.

**Status: scoped no-go proved; all-index conjecture remains open.**
