# Factorial-moment bounds for HM3 output energy

Let
\[
E_p=\sum_{m}g_p(m)\sum_{q\ne l\ne p}g_q(m)g_l(m),\qquad
S_+=\sum_p z_p^{-1}(E_p)_+^2.
\]
Since \((x)_+^2\le x^2\), it suffices to bound \(S=\sum_p z_p^{-1}E_p^2\).

Write \(f_p\in\{0,1\}\), \(g_p=f_p-z_p\), and let
\[
K(m)=\sum_p f_p(m),\qquad C(m,n)=\sum_p f_p(m)f_p(n).
\]

## 1. Exact reduction

Define
\[
H_p(m)=\sum_{q\ne l\ne p}g_q(m)g_l(m).
\]
Then
\[
E_p=\sum_m g_p(m)H_p(m).
\]
The weighted Cauchy inequality gives
\[
S\le \sum_p z_p^{-1}\Big(\sum_m g_p(m)^2\Big)\Big(\sum_mH_p(m)^2\Big).
\]
Because
\[
g_p(m)^2=f_p(m)(1-z_p)^2+(1-f_p(m))z_p^2\le f_p(m)+z_p^2,
\]
we obtain
\[
\sum_p z_p^{-1}g_p(m)^2\ll K(m)+\lambda,
\]
where \(\lambda=\sum_pz_p\). Hence
\[
S\ll \sum_m(K(m)+\lambda)\sum_p H_p(m)^2.
\]

## 2. Expansion of the inner square

Ignoring centering terms first,
\[
\sum_p f_p(m)\left(\sum_{q\ne l\ne p}f_q(m)f_l(m)\right)^2
\]
counts ordered configurations of up to five distinct rows. The all-distinct contribution is
\[
\le F_5:=\sum_m(K(m))_5.
\]
Repeated indices collapse to lower factorial moments:
\[
O(F_4+F_3+F_2+F_1).
\]
The centered expansion adds only terms with powers of \(z_p\), bounded by the same factorial hierarchy plus lower powers of \(\lambda\). A convenient deterministic form is
\[
\boxed{
S_+\le S\ll F_5+\lambda F_4+\lambda^2F_3+\lambda^3F_2+M\lambda^5
+\mathcal C_2
}
\]
where the remaining genuine two-point term is
\[
\mathcal C_2=
\sum_{m,n}\left(C(m,n)-\sum_pz_p^2\right)^2
\]
with harmless absolute constants depending only on the normalization of ordered pairs.

This term is unavoidable: squaring \(E_p\) couples two different output locations \(m,n\) through the same row index \(p\).

## 3. Why one-point factorial moments are insufficient

Even assuming
\[
F_k\ll M\lambda^k\qquad(k=3,4,5),
\]
the bound leaves
\[
S_+\ll M\lambda^5+\mathcal C_2.
\]
The desired HM3 scale is
\[
X^4\lambda^5=M^2\lambda^5,
\]
so the one-point estimate is not even the correct object: it does not control the amplification from repeated row correlations.

A counterexample: take a partition of the columns into blocks of size \(L\). Put every row equal to a random indicator supported on one block, with the block chosen independently for each row. By taking the number of blocks large and tuning \(L\), all fixed factorial moments of \(K(m)\) can match Poisson scale, while two points in the same block have large codegree. Then \(C(m,n)\) has a large diagonal-block energy and \(S_+\) grows beyond the Poisson prediction.

Thus no collection of bounds on \(F_k\) for fixed \(k\) alone can control the output energy.

## 4. Required additional hypothesis

The missing input is a rainbow/two-point moment, for example
\[
\sum_{m,n}(C(m,n))^2\ll M^2\lambda^2
\]
or a stronger mixed estimate controlling
\[
\sum_{m,n}C(m,n)(K(m)-1)_2(K(n)-1)_2.
\]
Together with
\[
F_5\ll M\lambda^5,\quad F_4\ll M\lambda^4,\quad F_3\ll M\lambda^3,
\]
this gives
\[
S_+\ll X^4\lambda^5
\]
(up to the precise normalization of \(M=X^2\)).

## Conclusion

The sharp deterministic decomposition is not a pure factorial-moment estimate. The sixth-order quantity generated by \(E_p^2\) splits into one-point five-row collisions and a two-point rainbow/codegree energy. Poisson-scale \(F_3,F_4,F_5\) controls only the first part. A two-point correlation estimate is mathematically necessary.
