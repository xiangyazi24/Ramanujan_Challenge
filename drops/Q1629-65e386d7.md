With the repository conventions stipulated in the question,

\[
A=\frac{P_m}{Y-\lambda_r},\qquad AU-1=GE,
\]
\[
T=\prod_{r<j<m}(Y-\lambda_j),\qquad F=-UE,\qquad C=F\bmod T,
\]
\[
\nu_m=\left(\frac{F-C}{T}\right)(\lambda_m),
\]

the exact center-free description of the missing scalar is

\[
\boxed{\nu_m=F[\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_m].}
\]

Equivalently, writing

\[
S(Y)=T(Y)(Y-\lambda_m)=\prod_{j=r+1}^{m}(Y-\lambda_j),
\]

one has the finite Green/barycentric identity

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m
 =\sum_{j=r+1}^{m}\frac{F(\lambda_j)}{S'(\lambda_j)}
 =-\sum_{j=r+1}^{m}
 \frac{U(\lambda_j)E(\lambda_j)}{S'(\lambda_j)}
 =-\sum_{j=r+1}^{m}
 \frac{E(\lambda_j)}{A(\lambda_j)S'(\lambda_j)}.
}
\tag{G}
\]

The last equality uses only the radical-root relation
\(A(\lambda_j)U(\lambda_j)=1\).  It does **not** use the defining center equation and it does **not** use the full inverse `V`.

There is also a coefficient-at-infinity form which is a useful target for a sparse-radical attack:

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m=[Y^{-1}]_\infty\frac{F(Y)}{S(Y)}
      =-[Y^{-1}]_\infty\frac{U(Y)E(Y)}{S(Y)}
      =-[Y^{-1}]_\infty\frac{A(Y)U(Y)^2-U(Y)}{G(Y)S(Y)}.
}
\tag{R}
\]

Thus the requested next object can be reduced to a **single explicit coefficient functional of the radical inverse \(U\)**.  This is substantially sharper than introducing a new unspecified jet.

The midpoint value \(C(\lambda_{m-1})\) is only one term of (G).  Isolating it gives

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m=
\frac{C(\lambda_{m-1})}{S'(\lambda_{m-1})}
+\mathcal R_m,
}
\tag{M}
\]

where the complementary source is the completely explicit finite sum

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_m=
\sum_{\substack{j=r+1\\j\ne m-1}}^{m}
\frac{F(\lambda_j)}{S'(\lambda_j)}
=-\sum_{\substack{j=r+1\\j\ne m-1}}^{m}
\frac{E(\lambda_j)}{A(\lambda_j)S'(\lambda_j)}.
}
\tag{O}
\]

This is the precise minimal obstruction left by the midpoint-center no-go: without a *new* Apéry/WZ identity collapsing \(\mathcal R_m\), the controlled midpoint endpoint does not close the problem.  In the locked datum supplied in the question,

\[

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_m=11\in\mathbf F_{17}.}

So the missing contribution is genuinely off-midpoint in that canonical record.  Any proposed homogeneous reduction in which the midpoint source is the only source is dead on this lock.

## 1. Conventions and the distinction that must not be blurred

I use exactly the conventions in the question:

* \(P_m\) is the canonical polynomial of degree **\(2m\)**.
* \(A=P_m/(Y-\lambda_r)\), hence \(\deg A=2m-1\).
* \(U\) is the **radical inverse** selected modulo \(G\), with
  \[
  AU-1=GE.
  \]
* `V`, the full inverse from the larger/nonradical modulus used elsewhere in P3.2, is a different object and never enters the identities below.
* \(T=\prod_{r<j<m}(Y-\lambda_j)\).
* \(F=-UE\).
* \(C\) is the remainder of \(F\) modulo \(T\), equivalently the unique polynomial of degree \(<\deg T\) agreeing with \(F\) at the simple roots \(\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_{m-1}\).
* \(K=(F-C)/T\), so \(\nu_m=K(\lambda_m)\).
* \(J=(E+AC)/T\).

No Q1610/Q1621 convention for a full inverse or for a different endpoint scalar is imported here.

## 2. Exact divided-difference identity

Let

\[
x_i=\lambda_{r+i}\quad (i=1,\ldots,n),\qquad n=m-r.
\]

The roots of \(T\) are \(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}\), and

\[
F-C=TK.
\]

Because \(C\) is precisely the interpolation polynomial of degree \(<n-1\) for the values of \(F\) on \(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}\), the Newton interpolation remainder at \(x_n=\lambda_m\) is

\[
F(x_n)-C(x_n)
=T(x_n)F[x_1,\ldots,x_n].
\]

Dividing by \(T(x_n)\ne0\) gives

\[
\boxed{\nu_m=F[\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_m].}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This already removes the center construction completely.  It also gives the exact Newton recurrence

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m=
\frac{
F[\lambda_{r+2},\ldots,\lambda_m]
-F[\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_{m-1}]
}{\lambda_m-\lambda_{r+1}}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Equation (2.2) is a genuine divided-difference recurrence, but I would **not** call it an Apéry recurrence yet: to deserve that name in this project one still needs a canonical recurrence/telescoper for the shifted divided differences on the right.  No such certificate is being assumed.

## 3. Green/barycentric formula

For distinct nodes \(\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_m\), the standard formula for the top divided difference is

\[
F[\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_m]
=\sum_{j=r+1}^{m}
 \frac{F(\lambda_j)}{
 \prod_{\substack{k=r+1\\k\ne j}}^{m}(\lambda_j-\lambda_k)}.
\]

Since the denominator is \(S'(\lambda_j)\), this is the first equality in (G).

At every canonical node in this radical block, \(G(\lambda_j)=0\).  Evaluating

\[
AU-1=GE
\]

there gives

\[
A(\lambda_j)U(\lambda_j)=1.
\]

Hence

\[
F(\lambda_j)
=-U(\lambda_j)E(\lambda_j)
=-\frac{E(\lambda_j)}{A(\lambda_j)},
\]

which yields the final version of (G):

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m=-\sum_{j=r+1}^{m}
\frac{E(\lambda_j)}{A(\lambda_j)S'(\lambda_j)}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is the clean Green identity I recommend attacking.  Its summand

\[
\omega_j=-\frac{E(\lambda_j)}{A(\lambda_j)S'(\lambda_j)}
\]

is the natural object on which to seek a WZ telescoper.  A successful WZ step would have to exhibit

\[
\omega_j=\Delta_j R_j+\text{explicit boundary source},
\]

with enough cancellation to leave a controllable endpoint.  The \(p=17,r=3\) lock says that such a certificate cannot reduce merely to the midpoint value: when that midpoint contribution vanishes, a residual source equal to 11 remains.

## 4. Sparse-radical coefficient formula

The divided difference has an equivalent residue/coefficient representation.  Since

\[
S(Y)=\prod_{j=r+1}^{m}(Y-\lambda_j),
\]

the coefficient of \(Y^{-1}\) in the Laurent expansion of \(F/S\) at infinity is the sum of the finite residues, i.e. exactly the barycentric sum:

\[
\nu_m=[Y^{-1}]_\infty\frac{F(Y)}{S(Y)}.
\]

Using \(F=-UE\) and \(E=(AU-1)/G\),

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m
=-[Y^{-1}]_\infty\frac{U(AU-1)}{GS}
=-[Y^{-1}]_\infty\frac{AU^2-U}{GS}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is a closed formula involving only the radical inverse \(U\) and the canonical polynomials \(A,G,S\).  It is therefore a legitimate next target for the project’s sparse-radical machinery and, unlike a differentiated endpoint equation, introduces no new unknown derivative.

There is also a completely explicit coefficient expansion.  If

\[
F(Y)=\sum_{k\ge0}f_kY^k
\]

and \(n=m-r=\deg S\), then

\[
\frac1{S(Y)}
=Y^{-n}\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 h_\ell(\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_m)Y^{-\ell},
\]

where \(h_\ell\) is the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m=
\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 f_{n-1+\ell}
 h_\ell(\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_m),
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

with only finitely many nonzero terms.  Any special sparsity of \(U\), \(E\), or \(F=-UE\) can now be inserted directly into (4.1) or (4.2), without changing the degree-\(2m\) definition of \(P_m\).

## 5. What the midpoint controls — and exactly what it does not

The elementary endpoint equation is

\[
T(\lambda_m)\nu_m
=F(\lambda_m)-C(\lambda_m).
\]

Since \(A(\lambda_m)U(\lambda_m)=1\),

\[
F(\lambda_m)=-\frac{E(\lambda_m)}{A(\lambda_m)}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m
=-\frac{E(\lambda_m)/A(\lambda_m)+C(\lambda_m)}{T(\lambda_m)}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

So, beyond the local root data at \(\lambda_m\), the single missing scalar is **\(C(\lambda_m)\)**.  The already controlled quantity is instead \(C(\lambda_{m-1})\).  Those are different linear functionals of the interpolation remainder.

Indeed Lagrange interpolation gives

\[
C(\lambda_m)
=T(\lambda_m)
\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-1}
\frac{F(\lambda_j)}
{(\lambda_m-\lambda_j)T'(\lambda_j)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Substituting (5.2) into (5.1) reproduces (G).  Isolating the midpoint term gives

\[
\nu_m
=\frac{F(\lambda_m)}{T(\lambda_m)}
-\frac{C(\lambda_{m-1})}
{(\lambda_m-\lambda_{m-1})T'(\lambda_{m-1})}
-\sum_{j=r+1}^{m-2}
\frac{F(\lambda_j)}
{(\lambda_m-\lambda_j)T'(\lambda_j)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equations (M)/(O) are the symmetric form of the same statement.

There are therefore two precise conclusions.

1. **Yes**, \(\nu_m\) is expressible from canonical boundary values, in the exact finite Green formula (G).
2. **No**, the already controlled midpoint value by itself does not close the scalar unless one proves an additional canonical identity collapsing the complementary source (O).

At the polynomial-interpolation level this failure is structural whenever \(\deg T\ge2\): evaluation at \(\lambda_{m-1}\) does not determine evaluation at \(\lambda_m\) on the space of degree-\(<\deg T\) remainders.  For example, adding a multiple of \(Y-\lambda_{m-1}\) preserves the midpoint value and changes the value at \(\lambda_m\).  That observation alone is only a linear-algebra obstruction, not a claim about the narrower canonical family.  The supplied canonical \((17,3)\) lock is the relevant family-level warning: the midpoint source can actually vanish while \(\nu_m\) is nonzero.

I would therefore regard either of the following as the **minimal extra scalar** needed after the midpoint no-go:

\[
C(\lambda_m),
\]

or, equivalently,

\[
\mathcal R_m
=-\sum_{\substack{j=r+1\\j\ne m-1}}^{m}
\frac{E(\lambda_j)}{A(\lambda_j)S'(\lambda_j)}.
\]

The second form is preferable for an Apéry/WZ attack because it exposes all sources and their canonical weights.

## 6. The exact `J` identity and why differentiating it does not close

Put

\[
K=\frac{F-C}{T}.
\]

From the definitions,

\[
J+AK
=\frac{E+AC}{T}+A\frac{F-C}{T}
=\frac{E+AF}{T}.
\]

But \(F=-UE\) and \(AU=1+GE\), so

\[
AF=-(AU)E=-E-GE^2.
\]

Therefore the exact polynomial/rational Green identity is

\[
\boxed{
J+AK=-\frac{G}{T}E^2.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

At \(Y=\lambda_m\), \(G(\lambda_m)=0\), and (6.1) gives precisely

\[
\boxed{J(\lambda_m)=-A(\lambda_m)\nu_m,}
\]

with the sign stated in the question.

A tempting next step is to differentiate (6.1).  At \(\lambda_m\) it gives

\[
J'(\lambda_m)
+A'(\lambda_m)\nu_m
+A(\lambda_m)K'(\lambda_m)
=-\frac{G'(\lambda_m)}{T(\lambda_m)}E(\lambda_m)^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

But (6.2) introduces the new unknown \(K'(\lambda_m)\).  That is exactly an unspecified next jet, which the question rules out.  Unless an independent WZ/Apéry identity evaluates that derivative, differentiating the endpoint equation merely moves the obstruction one order upward.  I therefore reject (6.2) as a solution route.

For completeness, one can differentiate the *known radical inverse identity* at each simple root without introducing an unknown object:

\[
(AU)'(\lambda_j)=G'(\lambda_j)E(\lambda_j),
\]

hence

\[
U(\lambda_j)E(\lambda_j)
=\frac{U'(\lambda_j)+A'(\lambda_j)U(\lambda_j)^2}{G'(\lambda_j)}.
\]

Substitution in (G) yields the closed first-derivative boundary formula

\[
\boxed{
\nu_m
=-\sum_{j=r+1}^{m}
\frac{U'(\lambda_j)+A'(\lambda_j)U(\lambda_j)^2}
{G'(\lambda_j)S'(\lambda_j)}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Here \(U'\) is the derivative of the already known radical polynomial \(U\), not a new jet variable.  Still, (4.1) is cleaner for a coefficient attack.

## 7. What a genuinely new Apéry/WZ relation must accomplish

The algebra above already supplies a center-free Green identity and a sparse-radical coefficient formula.  What is **not** yet supplied is a new canonical WZ certificate.  Such a certificate must compress

\[
\omega_j=-\frac{E(\lambda_j)}{A(\lambda_j)S'(\lambda_j)}
\]

into a telescoping difference plus explicit endpoints.  In particular, it must explain the nonzero residual 11 in the \((p,r)=(17,3)\) lock even though the midpoint term vanishes.

A relation that merely rewrites

\[
\nu_m=F[\lambda_{r+1},\ldots,\lambda_m]
\]

or names the complementary sum \(\mathcal R_m\) is **not** by itself a nonvanishing theorem.  It is a reduction of the target to an explicit linear functional.  Likewise, asserting \(\mathcal R_m\ne0\) because \(\nu_m\ne0\) in the locked case would be tautological.  The useful next theorem would instead evaluate (or telescope) (3.1)/(4.1) from the canonical recurrence for \(P_m\), \(U\), or the node weights, and thereby prove the needed value/nonvanishing independently of the center equation.

The cleanest WZ target is therefore (3.1); the cleanest sparse-coefficient target is (4.1).
