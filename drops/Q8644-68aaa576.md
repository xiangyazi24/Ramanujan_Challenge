# Repeated folded-anchor sector audit

## 1. Rigid consequences of a repeated folded anchor

Fix a signed nondegenerate full-defect label. Write the signed representatives in the usual form

\[
z_i(t)=s_i(t+d_i+\tau_i),
\]

with folded anchor \(u_i(t)=\min(z_i(t),-1-z_i(t))\). If two indices \(a,b\) have the same folded anchor, then they cannot have the same sign: equal signs would force equal signed representatives and hence equal defect labels, excluded by nondegeneracy. Therefore

\[
s_b=-s_a,
\qquad z_a+z_b=-1.
\]

The difference is fixed by the defect label:

\[
z_b-z_a=d_b-d_a+\tau_b-\tau_a=:\Delta .
\]

Solving the two linear equations gives

\[
z_a=\frac{-1-\Delta}{2},\qquad z_b=\frac{-1+\Delta}{2}.
\]

Hence the repeated-anchor event does not leave a free translation parameter. It fixes the two signed residues and therefore fixes the anchor

\[
u=\left|z_a+\frac12\right|-rac12
\]

(in the equivalent folded convention). Thus for a fixed label there are at most the finite number of translations compatible with these residue equations (in the standard normalization at most the two opposite-sign translations corresponding to the two possible lifts).

This is the exact rigidity available from the folded-anchor identity.

## 2. Prime conditions after rigidity

After fixing the translation, the two repeated-anchor primes satisfy

\[
p\mid B_u,\qquad q\mid B_u,
\]

and their coefficient equations are

\[
N-d_a=\alpha p,
\qquad N-d_b=\beta q,
\]

with \(\alpha,\beta\) in the allowed coefficient ranges (positive branch \(1\leq \alpha,eta<X\), negative branch shifted to \(2\leq\alpha,eta<X\)). Subtracting gives

\[
\beta q-\alpha p=d_b-d_a.
\]

Equivalently, after folding, the fixed odd anchor relation is

\[
\beta q-\alpha p=2u+1
\]

up to the sign convention. Therefore the repeated-anchor count is exactly the anchored pair divisor problem:

\[
A_2(u;X)=\#\{(p,q,\alpha,\beta):
 p,q\in(X,2X],\ p\ne q,\ p,q\mid B_u,
\beta q-\alpha p=2u+1\}.
\]

No further cancellation follows from the repeated-anchor geometry alone.

## 3. Strongest unconditional fixed-label conclusion

The unconditional reduction is

\[
J_{\rm rep}(s,d_2,d_3)\leq C+A_2(u;X),
\]

where the constant term accounts for the finitely many translation choices and endpoint cases. This is sharp at the level of the frozen hypotheses.

A bound \(O(1)\) or \(X^{o(1)}\) would require a nontrivial theorem on pairs of shell prime divisors of a single Apéry value. The fixed label does not itself provide such a theorem.

## 4. Why an O(1) proof cannot follow from the current data

An adversarial incidence model is immediate. Fix an index \(u<X\) and choose a hypothetical integer \(B_u\) having shell prime divisors

\[
p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_M\in(X,2X].
\]

For each pair \((p_i,p_j)\) choose coefficients satisfying

\[
\beta_{ij}p_j-\alpha_{ij}p_i=2u+1
\]

whenever the coefficient ranges permit it. The repeated-anchor constraints see only the divisibility and the linear equation; they do not impose an additional contradiction. Thus the abstract model can have many repeated-anchor fibres while preserving the fixed-label conditions.

The missing input is precisely a bound on \(A_2\), not a translation argument.

## 5. Final conclusion

The repeated folded-anchor sector is genuinely reduced, but not solved. The exact endpoint is:

\[
\boxed{J_{\rm rep}\ll 1+\max_u A_2(u;X).}
\]

The fixed full-defect label gives rigidity of translation and anchor, but it does not force \(O(1)\) or \(X^{o(1)}\). Proving the desired fibre estimate requires an independent anchored pair divisor estimate (APG or an equivalent result). No resultant in the sequence index is available because \(B_U\) is a sequence, not a polynomial in \(U\).
