# From \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) to pointwise anti-concentration: exact limits of the moment and sieve methods

## Terminal verdict

Let

\[
M=X^2,\qquad
K(m)=K_X(m),\qquad
\lambda=\lambda_X,
\]

and suppose

\[
S_2:=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_2\le C M\lambda^2.
\tag{0.1}
\]

The three answers are as follows.

1. **No bootstrap from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) alone is possible.** In fact, the factorial second moment gives the sharper pointwise estimate
   
   \[
   \boxed{
   \max_{m<M}K(m)
   \le \frac{1+\sqrt{1+4CM\lambda^2}}2
   \le 1+\sqrt C\,X\lambda.
   }
   \tag{0.2}
   \]
   
   The often-quoted \(X\sqrt\lambda\) bound comes from the **raw centered or raw second moment**; the factorial moment is stronger at a high-load point. Nevertheless, (0.2) is sharp from quadratic information: one may place a spike of height \(\asymp X\lambda\) at one row while preserving the correct first moment and the bound (0.1). Its \(k\)-th factorial moment is then \(\asymp (X\lambda)^k\), larger than the \((\mathrm{HM})_k\) target by \(X^{k-2}\).

2. **Restricting to \((X,2X]\) does not, by itself, save a factor \(X\).** For an interval \(I\) of length \(H\), CRT gives the exact generic estimate
   
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sum_{m\in I}(K(m))_2
   \le H\lambda^2+4X^2\lambda^2.
   }
   \tag{0.3}
   \]
   
   When \(H=X\), the boundary/discrepancy term is still \(X^2\lambda^2\). This is not an artifact of the proof. A short-interval version of the repository's anchored rainbow star can put \(\asymp X/\log X\) active columns through one prescribed point of \((X,2X]\), while retaining reflection symmetry, no consecutive residues, two-point zero sets, the necessary gap-polynomial certificate, and the full \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) estimate. It then contributes \(\asymp X^2/\log^2X\) pairs inside the short interval, rather than the expected \(X/\log^2X\).

   The factor-\(X\) saving does hold **on average over the \(X\) disjoint blocks of length \(X\)** in \([0,X^2)\), but there is no implication for the distinguished block \((X,2X]\).

3. **A Turán--Kubilius or quadratic Selberg-sieve argument cannot produce an all-\(m\) bound from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\).** The existing first- and second-moment estimates already imply the exact Turán--Kubilius-type variance bound
   
   \[
   \sum_{m<M}(K(m)-\mu)^2
   \ll M(\lambda+\lambda^2),
   \qquad
   \mu=M^{-1}\sum_{m<M}K(m).
   \tag{0.4}
   \]
   
   This controls almost all rows, not the maximum. The anchored star even satisfies the natural centered scale \(O(M\lambda)\) while having a point of load \(\asymp X/\log X\).

   The exact Brun identity is
   
   \[
   \sum_{m\in I}\binom{K(m)}k
   =\sum_{p_1<\cdots<p_k}
     \left|I\cap\Omega_{p_1}\cap\cdots\cap\Omega_{p_k}\right|.
   \tag{0.5}
   \]
   
   Thus a sieve of degree \(k\) needs the \(k\)-fold intersection data, i.e. an \((\mathrm{HM})_k\)-type input. The case \(k=2\) cannot manufacture the cases \(k\ge3\).

The exact surviving routes are therefore:

- high factorial moments, uniformly up to an order tending to infinity;
- a Poisson-scale exponential-moment estimate;
- a fixed-origin short-interval cross-prime discrepancy theorem;
- or a new deterministic cross-prime certificate of small height.

The last route is obstructed by the certificate-parity phenomenon: the current gap polynomials require two zero incidences at the **same** prime, whereas a high-load atom supplies one incidence at each of many primes.

## 2. What \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) really gives pointwise

### 2.1 First moment and active-column bound

Put

\[
\Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p\}.
\]

For one residue \(z\bmod p\), its number of representatives in \([0,M)\) differs from \(M/p\) by less than one. Hence

\[
\bigl||\Omega_p|-MZ(p)/p\bigr|\le Z(p).
\]

Summing over \(X<p\le2X\),

\[
\left|\sum_{m<M}K(m)-M\lambda\right|
\le\sum_pZ(p)
\le2X\lambda.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus the exact finite mean satisfies

\[
\mu:=\frac1M\sum_{m<M}K(m)
=\lambda+O(\lambda/X).
\tag{2.2}
\]

There is also a completely elementary pointwise estimate. Every prime counted by \(K(m)\) is active, and therefore

\[
K(m)\le\#\{p:Z(p)>0\}
\le\sum_pZ(p)
\le2X\lambda.
\tag{2.3}
\]

So the order \(X\lambda\) is already available without \((\mathrm{HM})_2\). The quadratic moment changes the constant, not the scale.

### 2.2 Sharp factorial-moment pointwise estimate

Let

\[
T=\max_{m<M}K(m),
\]

and choose a row \(m_0\) with \(K(m_0)=T\). Since every summand is nonnegative,

\[
T(T-1)\le\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_2\le CM\lambda^2.
\]

Solving the quadratic inequality gives

\[
T\le\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4CM\lambda^2}}2
\le1+\sqrt C\,X\lambda.
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is the strongest direct pointwise consequence of the factorial second moment.

The raw second moment is

\[
\sum_mK(m)^2
=\sum_m(K(m))_2+\sum_mK(m).
\]

Using (2.1),

\[
\sum_mK(m)^2
\le CM\lambda^2+M\lambda+2X\lambda.
\tag{2.5}
\]

This gives

\[
T\ll X\sqrt{\lambda+\lambda^2},
\tag{2.6}
\]

which is the source of the \(X\sqrt\lambda\) estimate. For \(\lambda<1\), (2.4) is stronger once \(T\ge2\).

### 2.3 Exact level-set estimate

For \(T\ge2\), let

\[
\mathcal A_T=\{m<M:K(m)\ge T\}.
\]

Then

\[
|\mathcal A_T|\,T(T-1)
\le\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_2,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal A_T|
\le\frac{CM\lambda^2}{T(T-1)}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

To rule out even one exceptional row, the right side must be less than one. This again requires

\[
T\gtrsim X\lambda.
\]

Repeated applications of Markov or Chebyshev do not change this threshold: they repeatedly apply the same quadratic budget to the same possible spike.

## 3. Why the moment method cannot bootstrap itself

### 3.1 A deterministic saturation theorem

The obstruction does not depend on any subtle number theory.

Fix a parameter range

\[
0<\lambda\le\frac12,
\qquad X\lambda\to\infty,
\]

and choose a fixed \(0<c<\sqrt C\). Put

\[
T=\lfloor cX\lambda\rfloor.
\]

On a set of \(M=X^2\) rows, define an integer-valued load by

\[
K(m_0)=T,
\]

put \(K=1\) on \(\lfloor M\lambda\rfloor-T\) other rows, and put \(K=0\) elsewhere. Then

\[
\sum_mK(m)=\lfloor M\lambda\rfloor,
\]

while

\[
\sum_m(K(m))_2=T(T-1)\le c^2M\lambda^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus this load has the correct first-moment scale and satisfies \((\mathrm{HM})_2\), yet

\[
\max K\asymp X\lambda.
\]

For every fixed \(k\ge3\),

\[
\sum_m(K(m))_k=(T)_k\asymp(X\lambda)^k.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The independence-scale target is

\[
M\lambda^k=X^2\lambda^k.
\]

The ratio between (3.2) and that target is

\[
X^{k-2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore no inequality of the form

\[
(\mathrm{HM})_2\Longrightarrow(\mathrm{HM})_k
\]

can hold on the basis of moment algebra alone.

### 3.2 The best formal higher-moment consequence is circular

For every integer \(k\ge2\),

\[
(K(m))_k
=(K(m))_2(K(m)-2)_{k-2}
\le(K(m))_2\,T^{k-2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_m(K(m))_k
\le T^{k-2}S_2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Using only (2.4) in (3.4) yields

\[
\sum_m(K(m))_k
\ll_k (X\lambda)^k.
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is sharp by the spike construction and loses precisely \(X^{k-2}\) against \((\mathrm{HM})_k\). Equation (3.4) is the repository's moment-collapse inequality read in the reverse direction: it becomes useful only after an independent bound on \(T\) has been proved.

### 3.3 Why exponential Markov does not help

For \(u>-1\), the factorial expansion is

\[
(1+u)^{K(m)}
=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{u^k}{k!}(K(m))_k.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus a Chernoff or exponential-Markov argument requires control of all factorial moments appearing in (3.6). Knowing only the coefficient at \(k=2\) leaves the rest of the exponential moment unrestricted. A spike of height \(T\) costs only \(T^2\) in \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) but contributes \((1+u)^T\) to (3.6).

There is no analytic continuation, interpolation, or log-convexity argument that supplies upper bounds for the missing moments. The usual \(L^p\)-norm monotonicity gives at best

\[
\sum_mK(m)^k
\le\left(\sum_mK(m)^2\right)^{k/2},
\]

which is again the spike-scale bound, not the independence-scale bound.

### 3.4 What all high moments would give

Suppose that for one fixed \(k\ge2\),

\[
\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_k
\le C_kX^{2+o(1)}\lambda^k.
\tag{3.7}
\]

If \(T\ge2k\), then

\[
(T)_k\ge(T/2)^k,
\]

and hence

\[
T
\le2C_k^{1/k}X^{2/k+o(1)}\lambda.
\tag{3.8}
\]

If estimates (3.7) are known for every fixed \(k\), then for each \(\varepsilon>0\) one may first choose a fixed \(k>2/\varepsilon\) and obtain

\[
T\le O_\varepsilon(1)+\lambda X^{\varepsilon+o(1)}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Thus all fixed moments do imply \(T=X^{o(1)}\). To obtain a Poisson-size maximum such as \(O(\log X/\log\log X)\), one needs estimates uniform for \(k\) growing with \(X\), not merely one theorem for each fixed \(k\).

### 3.5 Random restriction of the prime set does not bootstrap the estimate

The CRT proof of \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) works for every subfamily of primes. One might try to randomly thin the prime set and reapply the theorem. This does not improve the scale because both sides are homogeneous of degree two in the thinning density.

The structured star below makes this exact. For every subfamily \(\mathcal Q\) of its active primes,

\[
K_{\mathcal Q}(m_0)=|\mathcal Q|,
\]

while

\[
\lambda_{\mathcal Q}\asymp\frac{|\mathcal Q|}{X}.
\]

Consequently

\[
(K_{\mathcal Q}(m_0))_2
\asymp X^2\lambda_{\mathcal Q}^2.
\]

So even a version of \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) uniform over **all prime subfamilies** cannot eliminate the spike. Random sampling, entropy decrement, or dyadic splitting of the prime set needs an additional nonquadratic input.

## 4. A structured no-go model with the known one-prime properties

The abstract spike above already proves the formal impossibility. The current repository contains a much stronger obstruction: `prop:hm3-anchored-star`.

For completeness, its relevant features are:

- every active \(Z_p^*\) has exactly two elements;
- \(Z_p^*=p-1-Z_p^*\);
- there are no consecutive marked residues;
- every subinterval contains at most two marked residues;
- the universal \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) estimate holds;
- the centered second moment is \(O(M\lambda)\);
- distinct rows have uniformly bounded common-prime codegree;
- nevertheless one row has load \(\asymp X/\log X\), and the third factorial moment is too large by a factor \(X\).

This is the hypergraph obstruction in its sharp form. The columns \(\Omega_p\) are hyperedges, \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) controls the total number of ordered pairs of hyperedges through rows, and a star is exactly the configuration that concentrates the entire quadratic budget at one center.

The model is not claimed to be the actual Apéry family. Its role is logical: a proof from the currently listed one-prime properties plus \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) is impossible. A successful proof must use a genuinely cross-prime property of the actual Apéry zeros.

## 5. Restriction to a short interval

Let \(I\) be an interval of \(H\) consecutive integers, and put

\[
S_2(I)=\sum_{m\in I}(K(m))_2.
\]

### 5.1 Exact generic CRT bound

For distinct primes \(p,q\in(X,2X]\), define

\[
J_I(p,q)
=\#\{m\in I:m\bmod p\in Z_p,\ m\bmod q\in Z_q\}.
\]

For each \((a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q\), CRT gives one residue class modulo \(pq\). In an interval of length \(H\), that class occurs at most

\[
\frac{H}{pq}+1
\]

times. Therefore

\[
J_I(p,q)
\le Z(p)Z(q)\left(\frac{H}{pq}+1\right).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Summing over ordered distinct pairs,

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_2(I)
&=\sum_{p\ne q}J_I(p,q)\\
&\le H\sum_{p\ne q}\frac{Z(p)Z(q)}{pq}
  +\sum_{p\ne q}Z(p)Z(q)\\
&\le H\lambda^2+\left(\sum_pZ(p)\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(\sum_pZ(p)\le2X\lambda\),

\[
\boxed{
S_2(I)\le H\lambda^2+4X^2\lambda^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For the full interval \(H=X^2\), the two terms have the same scale, giving the constant-size bound recorded as `prop:hm2`. For \(H=X\), the `+1` term in (5.1) dominates by a factor \(X\).

This is geometrically inevitable. Since

\[
pq\asymp X^2\gg H=X,
\]

a given residue pair has expected occupancy \(H/(pq)\asymp1/X\), but its actual occupancy is zero or one. The entire question is whether the rare ones are dispersed across the \(X\) short blocks or concentrated in the distinguished block.

### 5.2 What follows by averaging over blocks

Partition \([0,X^2)\) into the \(X\) half-open blocks

\[
I_j=[jX,(j+1)X),\qquad 0\le j<X.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{X-1}S_2(I_j)
=S_2([0,X^2))
\le CX^2\lambda^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac1X\sum_jS_2(I_j)
\le CX\lambda^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

In particular, for every \(A>1\), all but at most \((C/A)X\) blocks satisfy

\[
S_2(I_j)\le AX\lambda^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

So the desired factor-\(X\) saving is available for a random block or for most blocks. It is not available for the fixed block \((X,2X]\) without an origin-typicality theorem.

### 5.3 A short-interval anchored star

The failure for a distinguished block can be made exact while retaining the known one-prime structure.

Choose

\[
m_0=\lfloor3X/2\rfloor\in(X,2X]
\]

and put

\[
E=(m_0-1)m_0(m_0+1)(m_0+2)(2m_0+1).
\]

For each prime \(p\in(X,2X]\) with \(p\nmid E\), define

\[
a_p=m_0\bmod p,
\qquad
Z_p^*=\{a_p,p-1-a_p\},
\tag{5.5}
\]

and set \(Z_p^*=\varnothing\) for the deleted primes.

Only \(O(1)\) window primes divide \(E\), because \(|E|=X^{O(1)}\) and every such prime exceeds \(X\). Let \(L\) be the number of remaining primes. Then

\[
L\sim\frac{X}{\log X}.
\]

The deletion ensures that the two residues in (5.5) are distinct and avoid the endpoint residues. Their difference is a nonzero even integer, so they are not consecutive. Reflection symmetry is built in. Every nonempty interval contains at most two marked residues.

Moreover, every active prime marks \(m_0\), so

\[
K^*(m_0)=L.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The density is

\[
\lambda^*
=2\sum_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\nmid E}}\frac1p
\asymp\frac1{\log X}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

More elementarily,

\[
\frac{L}{X}\le\lambda^*\le\frac{2L}{X}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

For the full interval, every ordered pair of columns has at most four common CRT witnesses, so

\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K^*(m))_2
\le4L(L-1)
\le4X^2(\lambda^*)^2.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus the proved full \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) bound holds.

Inside the short interval, however, the single point \(m_0\) contributes

\[
S_2^*((X,2X])
\ge L(L-1).
\tag{5.10}
\]

By (5.8),

\[
X(\lambda^*)^2\le\frac{4L^2}{X},
\]

and hence

\[
\frac{S_2^*((X,2X])}{X(\lambda^*)^2}
\ge(1-o(1))\frac X4.
\tag{5.11}
\]

This is exactly the missing factor \(X\).

The construction also satisfies the necessary gap-polynomial relation for its reflected pair. Let

\[
u=\min(a_p,p-1-a_p),
\qquad
g=p-1-2u.
\]

Then \(g\ge2\) is even. The reflection identity

\[
N_g(-u-g-1)=(-1)^{g-1}N_g(u)
\]

has left argument congruent to \(u\pmod p\), while \((-1)^{g-1}=-1\). Therefore

\[
N_g(u)\equiv-N_g(u)\pmod p,
\]

and hence

\[
N_g(u)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{5.12}
\]

So even the known dependency imposed by the gap-polynomial family does not prevent the short star.

Again, this is an abstract zero-set family, not a counterexample involving the actual Apéry numbers. It proves that the desired short-interval estimate cannot follow from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\), reflection, no consecutive zeros, the \(O(p^{2/3})\) zero-count bound, or the necessary one-prime gap certificates.

### 5.4 What is special about the actual interval \((X,2X]\)

For the actual Apéry numbers, the short interval has a useful exact interpretation. Let

\[
X<m\le2X,
\qquad X<p\le2X.
\]

Since \(m<2p\), the base-\(p\) expansion of \(m\) has either one digit or two digits with leading digit one.

- If \(m<p\), the condition \(m\bmod p\in Z_p\) is exactly \(p\mid b_m\).
- If \(p\le m<2p\), write \(m=p+r\). Apéry--Lucas gives
  \[
  b_m\equiv b_1b_r=5b_r\pmod p.
  \]
  Since \(p\ge7\), the condition \(r\in Z_p\) is again exactly \(p\mid b_m\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_X(m)=\#\{p\in(X,2X]:p\mid b_m\}
\qquad(X<m\le2X).
}
\tag{5.13}
\]

Consequently

\[
\prod_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_m}}p\mid b_m,
\]

and

\[
K_X(m)\log X\le\log b_m.
\tag{5.14}
\]

The manuscript uses an exponential growth bound \(\log b_m\ll m\), so (5.14) gives only

\[
K_X(m)\ll\frac{X}{\log X}.
\tag{5.15}
\]

This is the ordinary divisor-size bound and is of the same scale as the total number of window primes. A sieve-theoretic improvement of (5.15) would have to prove that the window radical of the **specific single integer** \(b_m\) is much smaller than its size permits. That is precisely the cross-prime anti-concentration problem, not a consequence of interval length.

### 5.5 The exact missing local theorem

Write

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_I
=\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_{a\in Z_p}\sum_{b\in Z_q}
\left(
\#\{m\in I:m\equiv a\pmod p,\ m\equiv b\pmod q\}
-\frac{H}{pq}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.16}
\]

Then

\[
S_2(I)
=H\sum_{p\ne q}\frac{Z(p)Z(q)}{pq}+\mathcal E_I.
\tag{5.17}
\]

The desired local second moment for \(H=X\) is equivalent, up to the harmless diagonal omission, to the one-sided bound

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{(X,2X]}
\ll X^{1+o(1)}\lambda^2.
}
\tag{5.18}
\]

This is a fixed-origin pair-witness equidistribution theorem. The full \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) gives only

\[
\mathcal E_I\ll X^2\lambda^2.
\]

Even if (5.18) were proved, it would imply only

\[
\max_{X<m\le2X}K_X(m)
\ll1+X^{1/2+o(1)}\lambda,
\tag{5.19}
\]

not the Fable target \(\lambda X^{o(1)}\). Thus the short second moment would be valuable but would not finish the pointwise problem by itself.

## 6. Turán--Kubilius and sieve formulations

### 6.1 The existing theorem already gives a Turán--Kubilius variance

Let \(\mu\) be as in (2.2). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
V
&:=\sum_{m<M}(K(m)-\mu)^2\\
&=\sum_mK(m)^2-M\mu^2\\
&=S_2+\sum_mK(m)-M\mu^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Dropping the last nonpositive term and using (0.1) and (2.1),

\[
\boxed{
V\le CM\lambda^2+M\lambda+2X\lambda.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

For \(\lambda\le1\),

\[
V\ll M\lambda.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is exactly the scale of a Turán--Kubilius inequality for a sum of rare prime indicators.

Chebyshev gives

\[
\#\{m<M:|K(m)-\mu|\ge U\}
\le\frac{CM\lambda^2+M\lambda+2X\lambda}{U^2}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is an almost-all result. To force the right side below one requires

\[
U\gtrsim X\sqrt\lambda.
\]

For large positive deviations, the factorial estimate (2.7) improves the threshold to \(X\lambda\), but not farther.

The anchored star in `hm3_result.tex` satisfies even (6.2), while retaining a row of load \(\asymp X/\log X\). Hence a perfect variance theorem cannot imply the desired maximum.

This is not peculiar to the Apéry sequence. Classical Turán--Kubilius theorems establish normal order or almost-all concentration; they do not exclude exceptional integers with unusually many prime factors.

### 6.2 Exact Brun moment identity

For every finite interval \(I\) and integer \(k\ge1\),

\[
\binom{K(m)}k
=\sum_{p_1<\cdots<p_k}
 \prod_{i=1}^k\mathbf1_{\Omega_{p_i}}(m).
\]

Summing over \(m\in I\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in I}\binom{K(m)}k
=\sum_{p_1<\cdots<p_k}
\left|I\cap\Omega_{p_1}\cap\cdots\cap\Omega_{p_k}\right|.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

For \(T\ge k\),

\[
\mathbf1_{\{K(m)\ge T\}}
\le\frac{\binom{K(m)}k}{\binom Tk},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\#\{m\in I:K(m)\ge T\}
\le
\frac{1}{\binom Tk}
\sum_{p_1<\cdots<p_k}
\left|I\cap\Omega_{p_1}\cap\cdots\cap\Omega_{p_k}\right|.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

For \(k=2\), this is exactly (2.7). A Selberg or Brun sieve weight of degree \(k\) needs the right side of (6.4), which is precisely the \(k\)-th factorial moment. There is no sieve identity that replaces the unknown \(k\)-fold intersections by pair intersections with a favorable upper bound: a star has all pair, triple, and higher intersections at the same center.

Thus a sieve-theoretic argument based only on \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) is not a separate route. It is the quadratic moment method in different notation.

### 6.3 The generic additive large-sieve barrier

Define the centered periodic column

\[
g_p(m)=\mathbf1_{m\bmod p\in Z_p}-\frac{Z(p)}p.
\]

Let

\[
F_p(a)=\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(az).
\]

Fourier inversion gives

\[
g_p(m)
=\frac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}F_p(-a)e_p(am).
\tag{6.6}
\]

The reduced fractions \(a/p\), with \(X<p\le2X\), are separated on the circle by at least

\[
\delta\ge\frac1{4X^2}.
\]

The additive large sieve on an interval \(I\) of length \(H\) therefore gives

\[
\sum_{m\in I}\left|\sum_pg_p(m)\right|^2
\le(H-1+4X^2)
\sum_p\frac1{p^2}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}|F_p(a)|^2.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Parseval on \(\mathbb F_p\) gives

\[
\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}|F_p(a)|^2
=pZ(p)-Z(p)^2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in I}|K(m)-\lambda|^2
\le(H-1+4X^2)\lambda.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

For \(H=X\), the \(4X^2\) spacing term dominates. Thus the generic large sieve again gives an \(X\sqrt\lambda\) pointwise threshold and no factor-\(X\) short-interval saving.

To improve (6.8), one must exploit cancellation among the actual Fourier coefficients \(F_p(a)\) across different primes, replacing the generic \(X^2\) large-sieve constant by an effective constant on the scale \(H\) for the relevant off-diagonal form. That is a new Apéry-specific cross-prime theorem. It is not contained in \((\mathrm{HM})_2\), whose proof uses only the absolute CRT count.

### 6.4 Why the larger sieve does not bound one high-load row

Gallagher's larger sieve bounds the size of a set of integers whose reductions occupy few residue classes modulo many primes. A singleton occupies one residue class modulo every prime, so the theorem is vacuous for the set \(\{m_0\}\). More generally, the set of high-load rows does not have one fixed collection of witnessing primes: different rows may be hit by disjoint prime sets.

One may pigeonhole a common \(k\)-subset of witnesses, but doing so produces exactly (6.5). At \(k=2\), the CRT intersection can contain one point, which is enough to support a star. To exclude the point, one must pass to high \(k\) or exhibit an arithmetic incompatibility among its witnessing primes.

### 6.5 The only immediate pointwise sieve bound

On \((X,2X]\), identity (5.13) gives

\[
K_X(m)=\omega_{(X,2X]}(b_m).
\]

The product of the distinct hit primes divides \(b_m\), so

\[
X^{K_X(m)}<\prod_{p\mid b_m,\,X<p\le2X}p\le b_m.
\]

This proves

\[
K_X(m)\le\frac{\log b_m}{\log X}\ll\frac X{\log X}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

No ordinary upper-bound sieve improves (6.9) for every \(m\) without information on the arithmetic structure of the particular values \(b_m\). An integer of exponential size is allowed, by size alone, to have \(\asymp X/\log X\) distinct prime divisors of size \(X\).

### 6.6 The certificate alternative and its obstruction

A deterministic pointwise proof could proceed as follows. For every high-load row \(m\), construct a nonzero integer \(C(m)\) such that

\[
\prod_{p\text{ hit at }m}p\mid C(m)
\]

and

\[
\log|C(m)|\le\lambda X^{o(1)}\log X.
\]

Then

\[
K(m)\le\frac{\log|C(m)|}{\log X}
\le\lambda X^{o(1)}.
\]

The obvious certificate \(C(m)=b_m\) is too large, giving only (6.9). The current gap-polynomial certificates \(N_h(m)\) are small enough only when one prime hits two rows separated by \(h\). A single atom of load \(T\) gives one incidence at each of \(T\) primes, not two incidences at any one prime.

The CRT vanishing-ideal calculation in Q11 makes the obstruction exact: a polynomial modulo \(pq\) vanishing whenever one prescribed zero condition holds modulo \(p\) and one holds modulo \(q\) splits into independent local factors. The prime gap \(|p-q|\) is not the index gap in \(N_h\). Thus the known gap-polynomial algebra cannot compress a one-row high-load event into a small cross-prime certificate.

## 7. What additional moment input would suffice

### 7.1 Growing factorial moments

Suppose that for every

\[
1\le k\le k_*(X)
\]

one has the uniform local estimate

\[
\sum_{m\in I}(K(m))_k
\le H(C\lambda)^k.
\tag{7.1}
\]

At a point of load \(T\), choose \(k=\lfloor T/2\rfloor\), assuming this is at most \(k_*(X)\). Then

\[
(T)_k\ge(T/2)^k,
\]

so (7.1) gives

\[
\left(\frac{T}{2C\lambda}\right)^{T/2}
\le H.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
T\log\frac{T}{2C\lambda}
\le2\log H.
\tag{7.3}
\]

For \(H=X^2\) and \(\lambda=X^{o(1)}\), this yields

\[
T\ll\frac{\log X}{\log\log X},
\]

provided the moment range reaches that order. This is the genuine moment-method route to pointwise anti-concentration.

### 7.2 Exponential moment

An equivalent sufficient input is a Poisson-scale Laplace bound

\[
\sum_{m\in I}e^{\theta K(m)}
\le H\exp\bigl(C\lambda(e^\theta-1)\bigr)
\tag{7.4}
\]

for a suitable range of \(\theta>0\). Markov and optimization then give

\[
\#\{m\in I:K(m)\ge T\}
\le H\left(\frac{eC\lambda}{T}\right)^T.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Taking \(T\) slightly larger than \(2\log X/\log\log X\) makes the right side less than one. But expanding (7.4) in powers of \(e^\theta-1\) recovers all the factorial moments. It cannot be derived from the quadratic coefficient alone.

### 7.3 A local second moment alone is still insufficient

Even the hoped-for short estimate

\[
S_2((X,2X])\ll X^{1+o(1)}\lambda^2
\]

would give only

\[
\max_{X<m\le2X}K_X(m)
\ll1+X^{1/2+o(1)}\lambda.
\]

Thus the local pair theorem and the atom-tail theorem are distinct milestones. The former removes the CRT boundary loss at order two; the latter requires either high-order distribution or a pointwise certificate.

## 8. Direct answers

### Question 1

No. The exact consequence of \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) is

\[
\max K_X\le1+\sqrt C\,X\lambda_X,
\]

which is sharper than \(X\sqrt{\lambda_X}\) but still polynomial. It is sharp: a single spike of height \(\asymp X\lambda_X\) satisfies the correct first moment and \((\mathrm{HM})_2\), while making every higher factorial moment too large by the factor \(X^{k-2}\).

Markov, Chebyshev, random prime thinning, \(L^p\) interpolation, and exponential Markov cannot bootstrap the bound because each would require information about higher factorial coefficients that is absent from \((\mathrm{HM})_2\).

### Question 2

The short restriction gives a factor \(X\) **on average over blocks**, but not for the fixed interval \((X,2X]\). The exact generic bound is

\[
S_2(I)\le |I|\lambda_X^2+4X^2\lambda_X^2.
\]

For \(|I|=X\), the second term dominates. A short anchored star satisfying the current one-prime structural constraints concentrates \(\asymp X^2/\log^2X\) pair incidences at one point of \((X,2X]\), proving that no factor-\(X\) theorem follows from the present inputs.

For the actual Apéry family, the required new statement is the fixed-origin discrepancy estimate (5.18).

### Question 3

The current first and second moments already provide a Turán--Kubilius-type variance estimate, but it is an almost-all theorem and cannot control the maximum. A quadratic Selberg or Brun sieve is exactly the \(k=2\) instance of (6.5). To obtain a pointwise result, one needs:

- \(k\)-fold intersection estimates for growing \(k\);
- a Poisson exponential moment;
- a genuinely improved cross-prime large sieve at the fixed origin;
- or a small deterministic certificate divisible by every hit prime.

The generic additive large sieve has the unavoidable spacing constant \(X^2\), and the obvious divisor certificate \(b_m\) gives only \(O(X/\log X)\). Neither closes the atom tail.

## Final named residual

The clean residual is:

> **Fixed-origin growing-order Apéry atom-tail estimate.** Prove either
> \[
> \sum_{m<X^2}(K_X(m))_k
> \le X^{2+o(1)}(C\lambda_X)^k
> \]
> uniformly for \(k\) up to a quantity tending to infinity, or construct for every row \(m\) a nonzero cross-prime certificate whose logarithmic height is \(\lambda_XX^{o(1)}\log X\) and which is divisible by every window prime counted by \(K_X(m)\).

The proved \((\mathrm{HM})_2\), even supplemented by short-interval restriction, ordinary large-sieve dispersion, reflection, no consecutive zeros, gap-polynomial dependencies, and bounded two-row codegrees, does not imply this residual.
