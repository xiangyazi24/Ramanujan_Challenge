## Main obstruction theorem

For every fixed integer \(K\), the presently proved local package is compatible with a column containing \(K\) targets. More precisely, one can build, for arbitrarily large centers \(n\), a finite characteristic-by-characteristic data package with \(K\) selected primes in \((n/2,n]\) satisfying all of the following properties simultaneously:

1. the Lucas target relation \(p\mid b_n\Longleftrightarrow n-p\in Z_p\) at the selected entries;
2. exact reflection symmetry \(r\in Z_p\Longleftrightarrow p-1-r\in Z_p\);
3. \(|Z_p|=2\), hence every known vertical bound, including \(|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\);
4. no consecutive zeros;
5. every selected cross-prime pair is pure cross in the sense of PC.1--PC.2;
6. the only same-characteristic pair is the reflected pair, whose continuant divisibility is supplied by the universal reflection factor;
7. all central-binomial candidate valuations are exactly one;
8. all PC.3, PC.5, PC.8, PC.12, PC.19, PC.20, and PC.21 upper bounds remain true.

Thus no formal consequence of Lucas support, reflection, nonconsecutivity, continuants after reflection saturation, the Casoratian, vertical zero-count bounds, the fixed-gap prime-pair sieve, or the central-binomial carrier can imply a universal numerical bound on \(|T_n|\).

This is stronger than the elementary observation that sparse rows may align in one column. The model respects the full **pure-cross decomposition** and the exact reflected-continuant saturation which PC.1--PC.21 identify as the surviving arithmetic geometry.

The model is not claimed to be the actual Apéry sequence. Its theorem-level purpose is precise: an absolute bound, if true, must use arithmetic of the distinguished Apéry initial state that is absent from the current local package. In particular it must compare quotient or jet data in different characteristics, or produce a global low-height integer whose divisibility cannot be programmed independently prime by prime.

The computation through roughly two million is fully consistent with this diagnosis. It gives strong evidence that multiplicity four is rare, not evidence that it is impossible. # 1. Exact top-half formulation

For

\[
 b_m=\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj^2\binom{m+j}j^2,
\]

Gessel--Lucas gives, for \(p>n/2\) and \(n=p+r\),

\[
 b_n\equiv b_1b_r=5b_r\pmod p.
\]

For \(n>10\), every such prime is greater than five, hence

\[
 \boxed{
 T_n=\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_{n-p}\}.
 }
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The boundary \(r=p-1\), equivalently \(n=2p-1\), is never a target because

\[
 b_{p-1}\equiv b_0=1\pmod p.
\]

The exact central-binomial identity is

\[
 \boxed{
 \prod_{p\in T_n}p
 =\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}
   \gcd\!\left(b_n,\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\right).
 }
 \tag{1.2}
\]

This carrier is selective only after intersecting with \(b_n\). Before that intersection, every nonboundary candidate prime occurs exactly once. Consequently it provides no mechanism for bounding the number of selected primes by a constant.

Take two targets \(p<\ell\) and write

 \qquad

Then

 p\mid b_{s+h},

The pair is degenerate if either

\[
 p\mid b_s
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \ell\mid b_{s+h},

and otherwise pure cross.

The current note proves:

- for each exact gap \(h\),
  \[
  M_h^{\rm deg}(n)\ll h;
  \]
- on the pure-cross locus, the ordinary gap continuant is a unit in both characteristics;
- for every fixed even \(h\le A\log n\),
  \[
  M_h^\times(n)

  \[
  O_A(n/\log n),

- a forbidden linear-size spike would force many adjacent pure-cross pairs or many close same-kernel pure-cross pairs.

These are powerful reductions for proving \(o(n/\log n)\). They do not approach an absolute bound. A column with four targets has only six pairs. All six may be pure cross, all six gaps may be large, and every PC estimate remains vacuous at that scale.

In particular, PC.3 does not bound \(|T_n|\): it bounds only degenerate pairs of a specified gap. PC.12 does not do so either: for fixed \(h\) its right side tends to infinity. PC.20 and PC.21 are asymptotic depletion criteria at the \(n/\log n\) scale, not finite-multiplicity theorems.

# 3. A PC-compatible arbitrary-multiplicity model

The following theorem formalizes the obstruction without merely repeating a generic row/column example.

## Theorem 3.1 -- reflected pure-cross stars of arbitrary finite size

Fix \(K\ge1\). For arbitrarily large integers \(n\), there exist distinct primes

\[
 n/2<p_1<\cdots<p_K\le n
\]

and reflected two-point sets

\[
 Z_{p_i}=\{r_i,p_i-1-r_i\},
 \qquad
 r_i=n-p_i,
 \tag{3.1}
\]

such that:

1. \(0\le r_i<p_i\) and \(r_i\in Z_{p_i}\), so every \(p_i\) is a target at column \(n\);
2. the two elements of each \(Z_{p_i}\) are distinct and nonconsecutive;
3. for \(i\ne j\), neither endpoint selected by characteristic \(p_i\) is an endpoint selected by characteristic \(p_j\) after reduction into the common top-half window;
4. every pair \((p_i,p_j)\) is pure cross;
5. the only same-row difference is the reflected difference
   \[
   p_i-1-2r_i,
   \]
   and its unsaturated continuant has the universal factor
   \[
   2r_i+(p_i-1-2r_i)+1=p_i;
   \]
6. every central binomial coefficient \(\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\) contains each \(p_i\) exactly once.

### Proof

By the prime number theorem, the interval \((n/2,n]\) contains more than \(3K\) primes for all sufficiently large \(n\). Select primes greedily while excluding the following finitely many relations:

\[
 2r_i=p_i-2,
 \qquad
 2r_i=p_i,
 \tag{3.2}
\]

which are exactly the two possible nonconsecutivity failures, and, for previously selected \(p_j\),

\[
 p_i=2n+1-2p_j
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 p_j=2n+1-2p_i.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

For each previously selected prime, (3.3) excludes at most one new prime. The two relations in (3.2) exclude at most two further values. Hence a greedy choice of \(K\) primes is possible once the interval contains more than \(3K\) primes.

Now put \(r_i=n-p_i\). Reflection and the target condition are built into (3.1). If \(p_i<p_j\), the pure-cross lower index is

\[
 s=n-p_j=r_j,
\]

and the opposite endpoint is \(s+(p_j-p_i)=r_i\). Degeneracy in characteristic \(p_i\) would require

\[
 r_j=p_i-1-r_i,
\]

which is equivalent to the first relation in (3.3). Degeneracy in characteristic \(p_j\) gives the symmetric relation. These were excluded, so every pair is pure cross.

The reflected difference identity in part 5 is immediate. It is exactly the universal factor isolated in the repository's reflection-continuant audit; therefore the same-row continuant condition contains no extra selective information. Finally, because \(p_i>n/2\), the numerator \(n!\) contains one copy of \(p_i\), while both denominator factorials are below \(p_i\), except at the nontarget boundary \(n=2p_i-1\), already excluded. This proves part 6. ∎

## Corollary 3.2

No theorem using only the predicates and divisibility conclusions listed in Theorem 3.1 can prove \(|T_n|\le C\) for an absolute constant \(C\).

### Scope

This is a logical obstruction, not an Apéry counterexample. The sets in (3.1) need not equal the actual zero sets of the Apéry sequence. The conclusion is that the current package has no finite-multiplicity content after reflection saturation and pure-cross reduction.

A positive absolute-bound proof must therefore use at least one input not represented above, for example:

- a cross-characteristic equation between the divided quotients
  \[
  b_{n-p}/p\pmod\ell,
  \qquad
  b_{n-\ell}/\ell\pmod p;
  \]
- a fixed global algebraic object whose reductions contain all selected rows but whose degree is absolutely bounded;
- a characteristic-zero height inequality specific to the Apéry initial state;
- a genuinely pointwise theorem for old prime factors of one Apéry value.

No such theorem is currently present in the repository.

# 4. Why the Casoratian and continuants cannot give a constant

For a same-characteristic pair of zeros, a second-order recurrence eliminates the local state and produces a gap continuant. In a pure-cross pair, the two zero equations live in different fields. Writing

\[
 b_{s+h}=pu,
 \qquad
 b_s=\ell v,
\]

gives

\[
 ub_s-vb_{s+h}=huv.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The quotients \(u\) and \(v\) are not bounded-height quantities. PC.6 proves more than failure: the ordinary same-prime continuant is a unit in both characteristics. Hence adding a third or fourth target does not create an overdetermined local system. It creates more independent characteristic factors in

\[
 \prod_{p\in T_n}\mathbb F_p.
\]

A determinant involving all targets becomes divisible by the target product only by inserting cross-cofactors or the product itself. Its exterior degree and forced prime multiplicity increase together. After saturation, no extra factor remains.

This explains why a putative \(|T_n|\le3\) proof cannot come from a fixed \(4\times4\) Casoratian built from the known local rows. The four rows are not four equations over one field; they are one equation in each of four unrelated fields.

# 5. Why the vertical bound cannot imply a constant

The proved estimate

\[
 |Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}
\]

is a one-characteristic statement. Even replacing it by the hypothetical optimal bound

\[
 |Z_p|\le2
\]

would not bound \(|T_n|\), because Theorem 3.1 uses exact reflected doublets.

The stronger fixed-gap vertical repulsion

\[
 C_p^{\rm full}(h)\le3(h-1)
\]

also does not help at fixed column multiplicity. A four-target column may have six distinct large gaps; each row still has only one reflected internal gap.

Thus a deterministic absolute bound would have to be horizontal from the start. Improving the exponent \(2/3\), proving exact doublets for almost all active rows, or classifying all same-row gaps does not by itself address four different characteristics meeting at one \(n\).

# 6. No applicable named theorem currently yields \(O(1)\)

The exact top-half question is

\[
 \#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_n\}=O(1).
 \tag{6.1}
\]

It is stronger than every pointwise little-oh target currently isolated in the project. The available theorem families miss it as follows.

1. **Subspace-Theorem gcd results.** These control gcds of fixed-dimensional \(S\)-unit or constant-coefficient recurrence orbits. Apéry numbers are polynomial-coefficient recurrent, and the moving prime set is not a fixed \(S\).
2. **Primitive-divisor theorems.** These produce new primes; they do not upper-bound old primes in one term or relate the sums \(p+(n-p)\) across different indices.
3. **Diagonal/G-function reduction modulo \(p\).** Algebraicity or automaticity modulo each prime is vertical. It does not compare selected coefficients in different characteristics.
4. **Prime-pair sieve.** For one gap it gives \(O(n/\log^2n)\), not \(O(1)\), and summing logarithmic gaps returns \(O(n/\log n)\).
5. **Holonomic operator coprimality.** Even coprime characteristic-zero operators can have exponentially large termwise gcd; the standard example \(2^n,6^n\) already disproves such an implication.

Therefore option (B) is presently unavailable.

# 8. Reproducible segmented exact search

The most direct exact enumeration uses

\[
 p\in T_n
 \Longleftrightarrow
 b_{n-p}\equiv0\pmod p,
 \qquad 0\le n-p<p.
\]

For fixed \(p\), compute the Apéry recurrence modulo \(p\) for

\[
 0\le r\le \min(p-1,N-p),
\]

and emit the sparse hit pair

\[
 (n,p)=(p+r,p)
\]

whenever \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\). Reflection allows the recurrence scan to stop at \((p-1)/2\): a zero at \(r\) also emits the reflected column

\[
 n=p+(p-1-r)=2p-1-r.
\]

The recurrence step is

\[
 b_{r+1}\equiv
 \frac{(34r^3+51r^2+27r+5)b_r-r^3b_{r-1}}
 {(r+1)^3}\pmod p,
\]

valid for \(r+1<p\). This is exact and avoids binomial summation in the main scan.

## 8.1 Storage design

Do not allocate a vector of prime lists for every column. Emit fixed-width records

```plain text
uint64 key = (uint64_t(n) << 32) | uint32_t(p)
```

into one file per prime block. The zero rows are sparse, so output size is proportional to the number of hits rather than \(N\pi(N)\).

After all blocks finish:

1. externally sort records by `(n,p)`;
2. remove duplicate reflected-center records;
3. scan equal-`n` runs;
4. write every run of length at least four to a candidate file;
5. independently verify every candidate.

The prime blocks are independent and can be distributed across machines. Each file should contain a header with

```plain text
magic, version, N, p_lo, p_hi, record_count, SHA256(payload)
```

so interrupted or corrupted segments fail closed.

## 8.2 Exact C++ core

```cpp
#include <algorithm>
#include <cstdint>
#include <fstream>
#include <iostream>
#include <stdexcept>
#include <vector>

using u64 = std::uint64_t;
using u32 = std::uint32_t;
using u128 = unsigned __int128;

static u64 mul_mod(u64 a, u64 b, u64 p) {
    return static_cast<u64>((u128)a * b % p);
}

static u64 pow_mod(u64 a, u64 e, u64 p) {
    u64 out = 1;
    while (e) {
        if (e & 1) out = mul_mod(out, a, p);
        a = mul_mod(a, a, p);
        e >>= 1;
    }
    return out;
}

static u64 inv_mod(u64 a, u64 p) {
    if (a % p == 0) throw std::runtime_error("nonunit inverse");
    return pow_mod(a, p - 2, p);
}

static u64 P(u64 r, u64 p) {
    u64 r2 = mul_mod(r, r, p);
    u64 r3 = mul_mod(r2, r, p);
    return (
        mul_mod(34 % p, r3, p)
        + mul_mod(51 % p, r2, p)
        + mul_mod(27 % p, r, p)
        + 5
    ) % p;
}

static void emit_key(std::ofstream& out, u64 n, u64 p) {
    if (n >= (u64(1) << 32) || p >= (u64(1) << 32)) {
        throw std::runtime_error("use a 128-bit record format above 2^32");
    }
    u64 key = (n << 32) | p;
    out.write(reinterpret_cast<const char*>(&key), sizeof(key));
    if (!out) throw std::runtime_error("write failed");
}

static void scan_prime(u64 p, u64 N, std::ofstream& out) {
    if (p < 7 || p > N) return;

    // b_0=1, b_1=5. Neither is zero for p>=7.
    u64 bm1 = 1 % p;
    u64 b0 = 5 % p;

    const u64 half = (p - 1) / 2;
    const u64 max_r = std::min<u64>(half, N - p);

    for (u64 r = 1; r <= max_r; ++r) {
        // Current b0 is b_r.
        if (b0 == 0) {
            const u64 n_direct = p + r;
            if (n_direct <= N) emit_key(out, n_direct, p);

            const u64 rr = p - 1 - r;
            const u64 n_reflected = p + rr;
            if (rr != r && n_reflected <= N) {
                emit_key(out, n_reflected, p);
            }
        }

        if (r == max_r) break;

        const u64 r3 = mul_mod(mul_mod(r, r, p), r, p);
        u64 numerator = mul_mod(P(r, p), b0, p);
        const u64 subtract = mul_mod(r3, bm1, p);
        numerator = (numerator + p - subtract) % p;

        const u64 rp1 = r + 1;
        const u64 denom = mul_mod(mul_mod(rp1, rp1, p), rp1, p);
        const u64 bp1 = mul_mod(numerator, inv_mod(denom, p), p);

        bm1 = b0;
        b0 = bp1;
    }
}
```

The driver should obtain primes from a segmented Eratosthenes sieve and assign contiguous prime-index blocks, not equal-width integer blocks, to workers. This balances the recurrence work more accurately.

## 8.3 Independent verification

A candidate run must be rechecked without trusting the recurrence scan or reflection emission. For every reported pair `(n,p)`:

1. deterministically test primality of `p` with a proven 64-bit Miller--Rabin base set;
2. check `n/2 < p <= n`;
3. set `r=n-p` and compute
   \[
   b_r=\sum_{k=0}^r\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}k^2\pmod p
   \]
   using the hypergeometric term ratio;
4. require the sum to be zero;
5. independently compute the recurrence values `b_r mod p` and require agreement;
6. sort and deduplicate the primes;
7. require at least four distinct verified primes.

The independent summation is:

```cpp
static u64 apery_sum_mod(u64 r, u64 p) {
    if (r >= p) throw std::runtime_error("verification requires r<p");
    u64 term = 1;
    u64 sum = 1;
    for (u64 k = 0; k < r; ++k) {
        const u64 a = (r - k) % p;
        const u64 b = (r + k + 1) % p;
        const u64 d = (k + 1) % p;
        u64 ratio = mul_mod(a, b, p);
        ratio = mul_mod(ratio, inv_mod(mul_mod(d, d, p), p), p);
        ratio = mul_mod(ratio, ratio, p);
        term = mul_mod(term, ratio, p);
        sum += term;
        if (sum >= p) sum -= p;
    }
    return sum;
}
```

This verifies the original Apéry sum, not merely a derived zero bank.

## 8.4 Complexity

The arithmetic work is

\[
 \sum_{p\le N} O(\min(p,N-p)),
\]

or about half that after reflection. It is quadratic up to logarithms, so a material extension beyond two million is a substantial computation. The search is nevertheless embarrassingly parallel over primes and has very low communication volume.

Useful engineering improvements are:

- Montgomery multiplication for each prime;
- batch inversion of `(r+1)^3` over blocks, or update an inverse factorial state;
- vectorized recurrence for several primes of similar size;
- checkpoint every fixed number of primes;
- compress emitted `n-p` deltas with variable-byte coding;
- merge with an external radix sort;
- rerun every candidate segment on a second implementation.

No new numerical range is claimed in this answer because the dispatch forbids local Python/code-interpreter/sandbox execution and no external compute job was run.

# 9. What would make an absolute bound credible

An absolute bound is not logically impossible, but it currently lacks a mechanism. A credible route would need a theorem of one of the following forms.

## 9.1 A bounded-degree global eliminant

Construct a nonzero integer polynomial or algebraic number \(F_n\) of degree and logarithmic height `polylog(n)` such that every target prime is a distinct prime divisor of a fixed nonzero norm `Norm(F_n)`. Then the number of top-half prime divisors would be bounded by the degree or by `log|Norm(F_n)|/log(n/2)`. No current carrier has this height: `b_n` and the central binomial coefficient have linear exponential height, while pure-cross eliminants retain endpoint Apéry values.

## 9.2 A uniform four-characteristic relation

Show that any four targets force a same-characteristic promotion, for example one degenerate pair or one second zero not explained by reflection. PC.6 shows that the ordinary continuant cannot do this. Such a theorem would have to involve divided quotient jets or a new global symmetry.

## 9.3 A fixed-state old-prime theorem

Prove that an Apéry value has at most `C` prime divisors in `(n/2,n]`. This would be much stronger than the desired `o(n/log n)` theorem and is not implied by any known primitive-divisor or G-function result.

## 9.4 A motive with bounded global exceptional set

Realize the moving zero condition for all primes as reduction of finitely many characteristic-zero algebraic points. Then four hits might violate a bounded intersection theorem. Existing mod-`p` Hasse-polynomial and diagonal descriptions are families whose marked coefficient changes with `p`; they do not provide such a fixed finite set.

Until one of these structures is found, `|T_n|=O(1)` should not be used as an intermediate lemma.

# 10. Correct strategic target

The data make a constant bound tempting, but the theorem actually needed for P3.2 is far weaker:

\[
 |T_n|=o(n/\log n).
\]

PC.20--PC.21 reduce this to depletion of adjacent pure-cross logarithmic-gap pairs after pointwise pruning. The exact-gap Selberg estimate already proves a little-oh for each fixed `h`; the remaining loss occurs only after summing the logarithmic gap range.

That frontier has two advantages over an absolute-bound conjecture:

1. it matches the combinatorial amplification forced by a hypothetical counterexample;
2. it asks only for an averaged Apéry-specific saving over ambient prime-pair counts, not a prohibition of every four-hit column.

A first four-hit example would refute a bound of three but would not damage P3.2. Conversely, failing to find one far beyond two million would still not prove an absolute bound. The productive use of the search is to map the pure-cross multiplicity and gap distribution and to test proposed horizontal identities, not to substitute computation for the pointwise theorem.

# Final conclusion

There is presently no theorem-level basis for `|T_n|=O(1)`. The known inputs admit arbitrary finite reflected pure-cross stars and therefore contain no absolute-multiplicity information. The observed maximum three is an important computational fact, but it is not structurally explained.

The next rigorous avenues are:

- run the segmented exact search above to seek a four-hit column and independently verify it;
- search quotient or higher-jet data on existing triples for a genuine four-characteristic eliminant;
- continue the PC.21 route, where any averaged Apéry saving over logarithmic gaps would advance the actual theorem.
