# Q8660 rc5 audit: heavy-row refinement

## 1. Heavy-row setup

Let a heavy row have regular load
\[
R(m)\geq 5.
\]
A distinct-anchor full-defect triple contributes three target primes on the same row. By the stated refinement, such a triple has at least two surplus primes beyond the three primes used in its defining triple.

The only viable charging mechanism is therefore not the triple itself but the surplus configuration.

Let \(S(m)\) be the set of surplus shell primes on the row. For a candidate triple \(T\), choose a canonical ordered pair
\[
(s_1(T),s_2(T))\in S(m)^2
\]
by a deterministic rule (for example the two smallest surplus primes in the fixed ordering). A successful certificate needs a bound
\[
\#\{T:(s_1(T),s_2(T))=(p,q)\}=O(1).
\]
Without this bounded multiplicity statement the charging is only a relabelling of the heavy-row moment.

## 2. Pair CRT uniqueness

For a fixed anchor and two surplus primes p,q, the row equations give
\[
\alpha p\equiv d_1\pmod q,
\]
with \(\alpha\) in the short window \([1,X)\). Since \(pq>X^2\), the pair determines the short coefficient whenever it exists. Thus a surplus pair determines at most one CRT coefficient.

However this only controls the affine coefficient. It does not yet determine the third target prime. The remaining prime is constrained by a separate Apéry divisibility condition
\[
r\mid B_{u(t)}.
\]
Hence pair-CRT alone gives
\[
\text{multiplicity}\leq \#\{r:r\text{ survives the Apéry condition}\},
\]
which is not an unconditional constant.

## 3. HM2/F2 obstruction

Charging every heavy triple to a surplus pair gives
\[
J_{\rm dist,\geq5}\leq \sum_{p,q} M(p,q),
\]
where \(M(p,q)\) is the number of compatible third primes.

The available second moment controls only
\[
\sum_m(K(m))_2,
\]
not the conditional third-prime multiplicity inside a fixed pair class. Bounding
\(M(p,q)\) by the total number of third hits reintroduces a third moment:
\[
\sum_{p,q}M(p,q)=\sum_m(K(m))_3.
\]
Therefore a direct F2 charging argument is circular unless one proves a genuine pair rigidity lemma.

## 4. Five-prime short CRT gate

A stronger certificate would use the two surplus primes together with the original three primes. The five primes impose multiple independent short CRT relations. The desired conclusion is:

For fixed four primes, at most O(1) choices of the fifth prime occur.

This would reduce the heavy fibre to a four-prime gate. But proving it requires a new arithmetic input: the congruences must force a small integer relation among the five primes or among their Apéry lower digits. Existing Lucas regularity only propagates known divisibilities and does not create such a relation.

## 5. Pointwise conclusion

The heavy-row hypothesis removes low-load noise but does not by itself provide an injective certificate. The following implication is valid:

*If every heavy full-defect triple has a canonical surplus pair of bounded multiplicity, then*
\[
J_{\rm dist,\geq5}\ll F_2,
\]
and the existing HM2 bound finishes the estimate.

The missing statement is exactly the bounded multiplicity lemma.

## Theorem-level no-go for the current charging route

**Theorem.** The heavy-row refinement alone cannot prove
\[
J_{\rm dist,\geq5}=o(X/\log^3X).
\]
More precisely, any proof that charges a heavy triple only to a surplus pair and invokes HM2 must establish a new pair-rigidity theorem. Without it, the charge multiplicity is an uncontrolled third-prime count and is equivalent to a hidden third moment.

Thus the heavy-row refinement reduces the problem to a fourth/fifth-prime gate, but does not close the all-distinct fibre by itself. The obstruction is a quantifier failure: the available estimates are global moment bounds, whereas the required statement is pointwise bounded multiplicity for every heavy row.
