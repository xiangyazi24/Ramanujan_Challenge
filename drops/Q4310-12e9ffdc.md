# Q4310 — reconciliation of the fixed-gcd census with the alleged linear height law

## Verdict

The conflict is real, and it resolves decisively as follows.

> **The census is measuring the carrier relevant to Q6-SLUNI. The campaign sentence that this fixed primitive gcd has logarithmic height \(\Theta(q)\) is wrong.**

More precisely, the repository proves linear logarithmic height for the **individual Apéry values** at affine indices, and in other rounds for certain **endpoint permanents, continuants, and scalar carriers**. From the individual-value asymptotic it obtains only the trivial upper bound

\[
\log G_\sigma(q,t)=O(q)
\]

for the fixed primitive gcd. It never proves a matching lower bound. The later campaign prose silently promoted the height of the operands, or the size of an available upper envelope, into the actual height of their gcd. That implication is invalid: two integers with exponentially large Archimedean size can have gcd \(1\).

The exact Q4284 source makes this completely explicit. It defines the same fixed-state gcd and then uses only

\[
G_+(q,t)\le \frac{b_{6q+t}}q,
\qquad
G_-(q,t)\le \frac{b_{q-1-t}}q.
\]

Its linear “height deficit” is the gap between this crude **upper bound** and the two-prime threshold. It is not a theorem that the gcd itself has linear logarithmic height.

There is one convention distinction worth preserving. Q4300 writes the merely \(q\)-free raw gcd, whereas the Q4289 census removes both the complete \(q\)-primary part and the finite fixed row-normalization ledger. Those two integers can differ by powers of fixed primes. But every admissible lower prime is of size comparable to \(q\), hence outside that fixed ledger. They have exactly the same relevant shell support, so the census integer is the correct carrier for Q6-SLUNI. Even if an unsaturated raw gcd had large fixed-prime powers, that would not be evidence against size accounting for the admissible radical.

There is therefore no crossover scale at which the census should begin seeing a proved \(\Theta(q)\) law. No such law for the primitive gcd exists. The Apéry operands already exhibit their linear logarithmic growth very strongly in the computed range; the primitive gcd simply does not inherit it.

The current repository has advanced beyond the \(q\le 10{,}000\) census quoted in the question. At commit `fcd66eefb193c714012187659c819380612523ed`, `drops/Q4289-8fb970e6.md` records an exact extension through \(q\le 15{,}000\): \(1{,}727\) selected states, \(3{,}454\) sign records, Apéry values up to \(984{,}546\) bits, maximum saturated gcd \(253783\) on plus and \(33847\) on minus, and an exact state-by-state size certificate below the two-prime threshold for every record. Thus size accounting really does prove Q6-SLUNI—and in fact vacancy—through the completed finite range. What remains open is the uniform theorem.

## 1. The objects that were conflated

Fix a prime \(q\) and a selected digit \(0\le t<q\) with \(q\mid b_t\). Put

\[
N_6=6q+t,
\qquad
N_+=12q+t,
\qquad
N_-=q-1-t.
\]

There are five distinct objects in the campaign. They must not be called by one common word “gcd height.”

### 1.1 The individual common Apéry values

These are

\[
b_{N_6},\qquad b_{N_+},\qquad b_{N_-}.
\]

With

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
\qquad
\gamma=\log\alpha,
\]

the standard Apéry asymptotic gives

\[
b_n=c_A\alpha^n n^{-3/2}(1+O(n^{-1})),
\]

and hence

\[
\log b_n=\gamma n-\frac32\log n+O(1).
\]

At the relevant affine indices this is genuinely \(\Theta(q)\). This statement is correct.

### 1.2 The raw two-value gcd

The raw characteristic-zero gcds are

\[
N_+(q,t)=\gcd(b_{6q+t},b_{12q+t}),
\]

\[
N_-(q,t)=\gcd(b_{6q+t},b_{q-1-t}).
\]

The selected upper zero forces

\[
q\mid N_\sigma(q,t).
\]

Thus the only automatic lower bound is

\[
\log N_\sigma(q,t)\ge \log q.
\]

Nothing here gives \(\Omega(q)\). The operand asymptotic gives only

\[
\log N_\sigma(q,t)
\le
\min\{\log b_{N_6},\log b_{N_\sigma}\}
=O(q).
\]

### 1.3 The merely \(q\)-free raw gcd

Q4300 defines

\[
G_\sigma^\circ(q,t)
=
\frac{N_\sigma(q,t)}{q^{v_q(N_\sigma(q,t))}}.
\]

This removes the selected upper prime but retains possible powers of fixed coefficient primes. Every admissible lower prime divides \(G_\sigma^\circ\).

Again, the only general Archimedean conclusion is

\[
0\le \log G_\sigma^\circ(q,t)=O(q).
\]

The lower endpoint \(0\) is real: the \(q\)-free gcd may be \(1\).

### 1.4 The fully normalized fixed carrier used by Q4284 and Q4289

Let \(E_\sigma\) be the finite fixed row-normalization ledger. The census reports

\[
\widetilde G_\sigma(q,t)
=
\bigl(N_\sigma(q,t)\bigr)_{qE_\sigma\text{-primitive}},
\]

meaning that every power of \(q\) and every power of every prime in \(E_\sigma\) is removed.

The exact ledgers in Q4289 are

```text

  = {2,3,5,7,17,315493,858433,2720027329}

E_plus, full fixed ledger
  = E_plus union {12073365010564729}

E_minus
  = {2,3,5,7,858433}
```

The reported plus value uses the full fixed ledger. Through \(q\le15{,}000\), adding the \(b_{12}\) prime to the plus ledger changes no record.

This is the integer whose exact census is tiny. It is also the fixed primitive gcd named in Q4284. Every varying admissible lower prime \(p\asymp q\) divides \(\widetilde G_\sigma\), because such a prime is not in the fixed ledger.

The whole-size relation between the two normalized conventions is

\[
G_\sigma^\circ
=
\widetilde G_\sigma
\prod_{e\in E_\sigma}e^{a_e(q,t)}
\]

for some nonnegative exponents \(a_e(q,t)\). Thus a statement about the **whole numerical size** of \(G_\sigma^\circ\) is not literally the same as a statement about \(\widetilde G_\sigma\). But their prime support in every sufficiently large admissible shell is identical:

\[
\operatorname{Rad}_{S_J(q)}(G_\sigma^\circ)
=
\operatorname{Rad}_{S_J(q)}(\widetilde G_\sigma).
\]

This is the equality relevant to Q6-SLUNI.

### 1.5 Edge permanents, continuants, and union carriers

The objects denoted in earlier rounds by \(B_e\), endpoint bilinears, transfer determinants, Fitting invariants, or products over matched edges are different integers. Their height laws can be linear in \(q\), linear in \(T\), or at least \(Y^{1/6}\), depending on sign and fold type. Those results are not contradicted by the gcd census.

An edge permanent can be enormous while the gcd of the two fixed Apéry values is tiny. Conversely, a scalar union carrier can be forced to contain a product of many endpoint primes even when no individual fixed-state gcd has large height. None of those carrier-height theorems supplies a lower bound for \(\widetilde G_\sigma(q,t)\).

The correct source ledger is therefore:

```text
individual Apéry values at affine indices:       log height Theta(q)
raw endpoint permanents/continuants:              their proved sign-dependent laws
scalar/Fitting union carriers:                    marked-prime information floor
raw two-value gcd N_sigma:                        log N_sigma = O(q), N_sigma >= q
q-free gcd G_sigma^circ:                          log G_sigma^circ = O(q), may be 0
fixed saturated carrier G_tilde_sigma:            log G_tilde_sigma = O(q), may be 0
admissible shell radical:                         unknown asymptotically
```

The campaign sentence “the fixed gcd has logarithmic height \(\Theta(q)\)” incorrectly replaced the last two `O(q)` entries by `Theta(q)`.

\le

The exact two-prime barrier in that file is

\[
P_0(q,t)
=
\left\lceil\frac{6q+1+t}{7}\right\rceil,
\]

because every admissible lower prime satisfies \(p\ge P_0(q,t)\). If two distinct admissible primes divide the fixed gcd, then

\[
G_\sigma(q,t)\ge pp'\ge P_0(q,t)^2.
\]

Q4284 subtracts \(2\log P_0\) from the displayed **upper bound** for \(\log G_\sigma\). The result is linear in \(q\), and the file calls it an “available excess” or height-budget deficit. That calculation proves only:

> The elementary inequality \(G_\sigma\le b_n/q\) is exponentially too weak to establish \(G_\sigma<P_0^2\).

> The actual \(G_\sigma\) is exponentially large.

Formally, the invalid promotion is


A positive gap between an upper bound and a target is not a lower bound for the object. If \(G_\sigma=1\), the same Q4284 upper-bound calculation still produces the same linear “available excess.”

Thus Q4284 itself is not refuted. Its negative conclusion about the **crudeness of that particular upper bound** is correct. The later campaign interpretation of the calculation as an actual height theorem is the error.

The same correction applies to the standalone radical-spacing summary in the supplied campaign context. Positivity and growth of the individual Apéry numbers control the size of each operand and support the endpoint arguments, but they do not force a large gcd.

## 6. There is no theoretical crossover scale

The proposal that \(q\le10{,}000\) might simply be too small for the “deep-interior \(\Theta(q)\) regime” does not survive the source audit.

### 6.1 The Apéry operand asymptotic is already visible

At \(q\le15{,}000\), the largest required Apéry value has nearly one million bits. There is no failure to reach the exponential-growth regime of the sequence. The individual values are already astronomically large and fully consistent with

\[
\log b_n\sim \gamma n.
\]

Their gcd remains tiny after the selected and fixed normalizations.

### 6.2 “Deep interior” is a condition on a hypothetical leaf, not a lower bound on every gcd

The DPRM deep-interior conditions constrain the offset, folded depth, zero-set complexity, and geometry of an **actual hypothetical lower prime**. They do not assert that every selected state has such a prime, nor do they force the fixed gcd to contain an exponentially large common factor.

In the censused range there are geometrically nonempty raw minus windows and M5-capable states, but no actual leaf. Conditional edge-height laws are therefore vacuous on those states. They cannot predict the size of a leaf-free primitive gcd.

### 6.3 No constants or onset theorem exist

A genuine assertion

\[
\log \widetilde G_\sigma(q,t)\ge c q
\]

for some \(c>0\) and all sufficiently large selected states would require both a proven positive constant and a proven threshold \(q_0\). No source establishes either.

Without such a theorem, asking where the census should cross over has no mathematical answer. Any numerical scale would be invented. The finite data are compatible with permanent smallness, polylogarithmic growth, subpolynomial growth, or a sparse later exceptional jump. They are not compatible with claiming that a known theorem forces a forthcoming transition.

### Crossover verdict

> **There is no justified crossover scale, reachable or otherwise. The alleged eventual \(\Theta(q)\) behavior belongs to the operands or to other carrier objects, not to the primitive fixed gcd.**

## 7. The correct unconditional target

Since the census carrier is the Q6-SLUNI carrier, the exact pointwise size target is immediate.

Let \(p_1(q,t,\sigma)<p_2(q,t,\sigma)\) be the two smallest primes allowed by the deterministic raw window before consulting the actual gcd support. Then

\[
\widetilde G_\sigma(q,t)<p_1p_2
\]

is sufficient for Q6-SLUNI. A clean slightly stronger version is

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde G_\sigma(q,t)<P_0(q,t)^2,
\qquad
P_0(q,t)=
\left\lceil\frac{6q+1+t}{7}\right\rceil.
}
\]

Any one of the following uniform theorems would therefore close Q6-SLUNI:

```text
whole primitive gcd, exact threshold:
  G_tilde_sigma(q,t) < P_0(q,t)^2

whole primitive gcd, power-saving form:
  G_tilde_sigma(q,t) << q^(2-eta)
  for some fixed eta>0

observed-shape strengthening:
  G_tilde_sigma(q,t) = q^o(1)

support-targeted form:
  Rad_{S_J(q)}(G_sigma^circ) < P^2
```

The first three are statements about the whole normalized gcd. The fourth is the weaker Q4300 shell-radical target.

### 7.1 Is an observed-shape smallness theorem more tractable?

As a matter of logical strength, no. A theorem such as

\[
\widetilde G_\sigma=q^{o(1)}
\]

or bounded/polylogarithmic \(\widetilde G_\sigma\) controls all retained small primes and their exponents, even though Q6-SLUNI cares only about two primes in one high shell. It is strictly stronger than the support-blind shell-radical inequality. One cannot declare it easier merely because the finite data look good.

As a proof route, however, it is now substantially better motivated than the campaign had recognized. The census exhibits a very specific phenomenon:

```text
individual operands: hundreds of thousands to nearly one million bits
primitive fixed gcd: usually 1, otherwise a small sparse product
```

That is the signature one would expect if there is an unexploited exact Euclidean descent, a low-complexity resultant identity, or a horizontal valuation restriction for the fixed pair of affine indices. Such a theorem would bypass all growing-support compression machinery because it works directly with one fixed characteristic-zero gcd.

The appropriate research hierarchy is therefore:

1. **Search first for an exact fixed-state gcd-collapse mechanism.** Derive support-blind integer combinations of the two Apéry values, after the selected \(q\)-copy and fixed normalization are removed, whose remainder has polynomial or subquadratic size. A successful Euclidean/continuant descent could explain the repeated small-prime support.
2. **Target the exact subquadratic whole-gcd inequality** \(\widetilde G_\sigma<P_0^2\), not necessarily boundedness. This is already enough and is much weaker than proving the observed constant-scale pattern.
3. **Retain the Q4300 shell-radical theorem as the minimal fallback.** If irrelevant small-prime powers prevent a whole-gcd estimate, control only the deterministic admissible shell radical.

The data do not prove that step 1 or step 2 is attainable. They do prove that the previous reason for refusing to try them—an alleged theorem that the primitive gcd has linear logarithmic height—was invalid.
