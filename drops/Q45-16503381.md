# Poisson exponential moment for the Apéry atom process

## Summary

A direct Selberg-sieve proof of a full Poisson exponential moment appears unlikely in its naive form. The reason is that the exponential moment contains all high-order correlations simultaneously:

\[
E_t(X)=\sum_{m<X^2}\prod_{p\in(X,2X]}(1+(e^t-1)1_{\Omega_p}(m)).
\]

Expanding this product gives

\[
E_t/X^2=\sum_k\frac{(e^t-1)^k}{k!}\,\mathbb E[(K_X)_k].
\]

Therefore a Poisson bound is essentially an all-orders factorial moment theorem. A direct proof must replace the separate (HM)_k estimates by a uniform control of this entire generating function.

## 1. The correct exponential parameter

For the Poisson model with mean \(\lambda_X\),

\[
\mathbb E e^{tK}=\exp(\lambda_X(e^t-1)).
\]

The maximum follows from Markov:

\[
\#\{m:K(m)\ge L\}\le X^2\frac{E_t/X^2}{e^{tL}}.
\]

Optimizing requires choosing \(t\) growing slowly. A fixed \(t\) only gives \(K\ll \lambda e^t/t\), which is useless because \(\lambda\to0\). The useful regime is \(t\asymp \log L\), leading to the Poisson extreme-value scale

\[
L\sim \frac{\log X}{\log\log X}.
\]

Thus the target is much stronger than any fixed finite moment estimate.

## 2. Product expansion and what must be proved

Write

\[
\Omega_p=\{m:m\bmod p\in Z_p\}.
\]

Then

\[
E_t(X)=\sum_m\prod_p(1+u1_{\Omega_p}(m)),\qquad u=e^t-1.
\]

Expanding:

\[
E_t/X^2=\sum_{S\subset\mathcal P}u^{|S|}
\frac{1}{X^2}\#(\cap_{p\in S}\Omega_p).
\]

For independent events,

\[
\#(\cap_{p\in S}\Omega_p)/X^2\approx\prod_{p\in S}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

The missing ingredient is therefore a uniform upper bound:

\[
\#(\cap_{p\in S}\Omega_p)\le (1+o(1))X^2\prod_{p\in S}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}
\]

for all relevant sets \(S\), not just fixed \(|S|\).

## 3. Can Selberg sieve do this?

Classical Selberg sieve handles

\[
\#\{n\le x:n+h_i\not\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]

where local conditions are residue classes modulo primes. It gives upper bounds for intersections of many congruence exclusions.

Here the situation is reversed:

* each prime has a sparse exceptional set \(Z_p\),
* we need upper bounds for simultaneous membership in exceptional sets,
* the exceptional sets move with p and come from Apéry dynamics.

A direct application gives only a generic sieve bound. It sees \(|Z_p|\), but not the recurrence structure. The result is roughly of the form

\[
\#(\cap_{p\in S}\Omega_p)\ll X^2\prod_{p\in S}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}
\]

with losses depending on the dimension and sieve level. These losses are fatal when summed over all \(|S|\).

Therefore Selberg sieve alone is not the correct mechanism.

## 4. The promising replacement: a weighted correlation theorem

The right statement is not individual (HM)_k but a generating-function correlation estimate:

For \(|u|\le u_0\log X\), prove

\[
\sum_{m<X^2}\prod_p(1+u1_{\Omega_p}(m))
\le X^2\exp((1+o(1))u\lambda_X).
\]

This is a "cluster expansion" statement. It says all connected correlations are negligible.

Define connected correlations by Möbius inversion on subsets:

\[
\kappa(S)=\frac{\#(\cap_{p\in S}\Omega_p)}{X^2}-\prod_{p\in S}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

Need:

\[
\sum_S |u|^{|S|}|\kappa(S)|/\text{normalization}=o(1).
\]

This is stronger and cleaner than proving each HM separately.

## 5. Gap-polynomial route

The gap polynomial machinery is naturally adapted to connected correlations.

A k-fold collision means

\[
m=p_i+r_i
\]

for k primes. Eliminating m gives relations among residues. For k=3 this gives the gap polynomial cloud. For general k one obtains a higher-dimensional variety.

The expected strategy:

1. Show generic transverse configurations have square-root cancellation.
2. Show degenerate configurations are controlled by resultants of gap polynomials.
3. Sum over exceptional primes separately.

This is essentially a geometric sieve rather than classical sieve.

## 6. K3/sheaf interpretation

The Apéry recurrence gives a two-dimensional Frobenius orbit. The condition

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\]

is a hitting condition in the projective orbit.

A triple correlation asks for simultaneous hits for different Frobenius parameters. The natural object is a fiber product of the corresponding K3/F-crystal systems.

If the monodromy is sufficiently large, Deligne/Katz machinery predicts square-root cancellation in the trace functions controlling correlations.

The obstacle is that one needs uniformity in the number of primes in the product. Fixed k is accessible in principle; exponential moments require controlling k growing like \(\log X/\log\log X\).

## 7. Recommended path

The most realistic hierarchy is:

1. Prove (HM)_3. This already proves the GCD conjecture.
2. Prove bounded exponential moments for fixed \(t\):

\[
E_t\le X^2\exp(C\lambda e^t).
\]

3. Upgrade using cluster expansion methods to obtain the Poisson extreme-value law.

A full Erdos-Kac/Poisson theorem is likely substantially harder than the GCD conjecture itself.

The exponential moment is therefore not a shortcut around (HM); it is a compressed form of infinitely many high-moment statements. The useful breakthrough would be a structural correlation theorem for Apéry zero sets, probably via the gap-polynomial geometry plus Frobenius/K3 monodromy, rather than a black-box Selberg sieve.
