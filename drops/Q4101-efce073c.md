# 1. Canonical source/sink topology

After ordering four distinct lower zeros as

\[
0\le a<b<c<d<p,
\]

the source/sink natural-arrow type is

```text
a --P--> b <-M+-- c --P--> d --M+--> a.
```

Equivalently, its four assigned physical rows are

\[
P(a,b),\qquad M^+(c,b),\qquad P(c,d),\qquad M^+(d,a).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The natural directed graph has unique source `c` and unique sink `b`.

For `0<=x<y<p`, the exact plus row is

\[
q_P(x,y)=\frac{7p-y+x}{6},
\qquad
u_P(x,y)=y-2x-1,
\tag{1.2P}
\]

with natural arrow `x -> y`. For an `M+` arrow from a larger tail `x` to a smaller head `y`,

\[
q_M(x,y)=\frac{8p-x+y}{7},
\qquad
u_M(x,y)=\frac{p-x-6y}{7}-1.
\tag{1.2M}
\]

The exact common row index at a natural source `x` is

\[
\boxed{n_x:=7p-x-1.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Indeed, for either sign,

\[
6q+u=n_x.
\tag{1.4}
\]

For plus this follows from

\[
6q_P=7p-y+x,
\qquad
u_P=y-2x-1,
\]

and for `M+` from

\[
7q_M=8p-x+y,
\qquad
7(u_M+1)=p-x-6y.
\]

This common `n_x` is the load-bearing arithmetic feature of the source. At the sink, by contrast, the two incident rows do not share their first common index.

# 2. Exact minimal lower-star sum

Fix the fully filtered physical row set at lower prime `p`.

For one lower zero `x`, let

\[
\mathscr P_{p,x}
:=\{\text{retained assigned }P(x,y)\text{ rows with }y>x\},
\]

\[
\mathscr M_{p,x}
:=\{\text{retained assigned }M^+(x,y)\text{ rows with }y<x\},
\tag{2.1}
\]

and put

\[
d_P^{\rm out}(p,x):=|\mathscr P_{p,x}|,
\qquad
d_M^{\rm out}(p,x):=|\mathscr M_{p,x}|.
\tag{2.2}
\]

A row in either set is uniquely determined by its upper prime `q`: once `(p,x,q)` is fixed, (1.2P) or (1.2M) recovers the other lower endpoint and (1.3)--(1.4) recover the upper digit.

The two sets are disjoint as physical rows. If the same `(p,x,q)` satisfied both inverse descriptions, it would have the same

\[
(n,q,u)=(7p-x-1,q,7p-x-1-6q)
\]

and hence would be one physical q6 row with two possible certificates. The deterministic minus-first OR assignment retains it in exactly one sign class.

## 2.1 Completion-aware matching number

Let `B^{src}_{p,x}` be the bipartite graph with vertex classes

\[
\mathscr P_{p,x},\qquad \mathscr M_{p,x},
\]

where a pair is joined precisely when it occurs as the two source rows of at least one fully filtered source/sink C4.

If `\mathcal C` is an edge-disjoint C4 packing, the cycles in `\mathcal C` having source `x` give a matching in `B^{src}_{p,x}`. Two such cycles cannot use the same source row because that would be a shared physical edge. Therefore

\[
\#\{C\in\mathcal C:\operatorname{source}(C)=x\}
\le \nu(B^{src}_{p,x}),
\tag{2.3}
\]

where `nu(B)` denotes matching number.

Define the analogous sink completion graph `B^{snk}_{p,y}` from the retained incoming plus and incoming `M+` rows at a sink `y`. Every source/sink cycle contributes to one source matching and one sink matching, so

\[
\boxed{
\nu_{\rm SS}(p)
\le
\min\left\{
\sum_x\nu(B^{src}_{p,x}),
\sum_y\nu(B^{snk}_{p,y})
\right\}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus the smallest completion-aware local-pair sum is

\[
\mathcal M_{\rm SS}(T)
:=
\sum_p
\min\left\{
\sum_x\nu(B^{src}_{p,x}),
\sum_y\nu(B^{snk}_{p,y})
\right\}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

An `o(T^2/L^2)` estimate for (2.5), with `L=log T`, deletes the family.

## 2.2 Smallest completion-blind marginal envelope

The completion predicate in `B^{src}` already remembers part of the desired C4. If one retains only the two lower-star degrees, the exact local maximum matching ceiling is

\[
\nu(B^{src}_{p,x})
\le
\min\{d_P^{\rm out}(p,x),d_M^{\rm out}(p,x)\}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

This is best possible from the two marginals: a complete bipartite completion graph contains a matching of size exactly the smaller degree. Therefore the smallest source functional depending only on the marginal lower stars is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm src}(T)
:=
\sum_{p,x}
\min\{d_P^{\rm out}(p,x),d_M^{\rm out}(p,x)\}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

There is an analogous `\mathcal L_snk`. The smallest two-sided marginal envelope is

\[
\sum_p\min\{\mathcal L_{\rm src}(p),\mathcal L_{\rm snk}(p)\}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

For arithmetic purposes the source side is the useful one, because it has the shared index (1.3). The sink side can only reduce the combinatorial envelope; no existing congruence gives it a stronger common-value carrier.

The frequently written raw star-pair count

\[
\mathcal P_{\rm src}(T)
:=
\sum_{p,x}
 d_P^{\rm out}(p,x)d_M^{\rm out}(p,x)
\tag{2.9}
\]

is a valid but strictly larger sufficient quantity. For positive integral degrees,

\[
\min(d_P,d_M)\le d_Pd_M.
\]

Consequently a theorem for (2.9) is stronger than needed. The exact minimal marginal target is the `L^1` overlap (2.7), equivalently (V1).

# 4. Exact positive Apéry carrier

Fix one actual mixed source pair at `(n,p)`, with upper primes

\[
q_P\in\mathscr P_{p,x},
\qquad
q_M\in\mathscr M_{p,x}.
\]

## 4.1 The lower prime is a row hit

Since `p|b_x`, reflection gives

\[
p\mid b_{p-1-x}.
\]

But

\[
n=7p-x-1=6p+(p-1-x).
\]

The Apéry--Lucas congruence therefore gives

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_n.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently, `p` is a quotient-six hit on row `n`.

## 4.2 Both upper primes hit the same integer

For either physical row,

\[
n=6q+u,
\qquad q\mid b_u.
\]

Apéry--Lucas gives

\[
\boxed{q_P\mid b_n,
\qquad q_M\mid b_n.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

No cancellation of `b_6` is required: this is the forward implication from the actual upper zero.

The upper primes are distinct. Equality `q_P=q_M` would give the same `(n,q,u)` physical row in both sign classes, contradicting deterministic one-sign assignment. Since both satisfy `q>p`, the three primes are pairwise distinct. Combining (4.1)--(4.2),

\[
\boxed{pq_Pq_M\mid b_n.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This proves (V3).

## 4.3 Occurrence-preserving reconstruction

From `(n,p)` recover `x=7p-n-1`. From a typed plus prime `q_P`, recover its other lower endpoint

\[
y_P=7p+x-6q_P.
\tag{4.4P}
\]

From a typed `M+` prime `q_M`, recover

\[
y_M=7q_M-8p+x.
\tag{4.4M}
\]

Thus the map

\[
(p,x;P\text{-row},M^+\text{-row})
\longmapsto
(n;p,q_P,q_M;P,M+)
\tag{4.5}
\]

has fibre one. The source inequalities give

\[
y_M<x<y_P,
\]

so the three lower zeros are also distinct.

Let `H_6(n)` be the complete quotient-six hit set in the prescribed shell and let `K_6(n)=|H_6(n)|`. Every raw mixed source pair is a typed triple in `H_6(n)`, with `p` the unique smallest prime. A bare unordered triple permits at most two assignments of the two larger primes to the `P` and `M+` roles. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_{\rm src}(T)
=\sum_{n,p}P(n,p)M(n,p)
\le2\sum_n\binom{K_6(n)}3.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the exact positive row-triple reduction. It is stronger than merely noting that two rows share a lower zero: all three prime characteristics divide one fixed Apéry value.

Suppose a source matching at `(n,p)` has size `m`. It uses `m` distinct plus rows and `m` distinct `M+` rows. The corresponding `2m` upper primes are pairwise distinct: within one type, `q` determines the physical row, and the two sign classes are disjoint.

# 8. Why the sink does not add a second common value

At a sink `y`, an incoming plus row and an incoming `M+` row use different one-edge common indices.

For the plus row, the head-side common value is

\[
H_y:=14p-1-y.
\]

For the `M+` row, it is

\[
L_y:=p+y.
\]

The lower prime `p` divides both values, and

\[
H_y+L_y=15p-1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

But the plus upper prime is forced only on `b_(H_y)`, while the minus upper prime is forced only on `b_(L_y)`. Relation (8.1) is a reflection relation modulo `p`; it is not a reflection relation modulo either foreign upper prime unless an additional divisibility such as `q|15p` holds, which it does not for distinct macroscopic shell primes.

Therefore the sink pair supplies no second all-three-prime common Apéry value. The source carrier (4.3) is the strongest exact common-index consequence available from the one-edge congruences.

# 10. Final theorem and exact obstruction

## Theorem — mixed source/sink dominance

Let `nu_SS(p)` be the maximum number of pairwise physical-edge-disjoint, fully filtered source/sink C4s at lower prime `p`. Define `P(n,p),M(n,p)` by (3.2). Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p\nu_{\rm SS}(p)
\le
\sum_{n,p}\min\{P(n,p),M(n,p)\}.
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

For every raw mixed source pair counted by `P(n,p)M(n,p)`, its three distinct primes satisfy

\[
\boxed{pq_Pq_M\mid b_n,}
\tag{10.2}
\]

and the typed pair is recovered from `(n,p,q_P,q_M)`.

The strongest current unconditional estimate for the right side of (10.1) is (V5), which does not reach `o(T^2/L^2)`.

## First exact obstruction

The existing Apéry tools establish the support implication

```text
mixed source pair
    => three distinct quotient-six primes divide one b_n.
```

They do not establish an occurrence-sensitive mixed-degree estimate. The same prime divisor of `b_n` can be reused by many `(n,p)` lower anchors, while edge-disjointness acts only on physical rows inside one lower-prime graph. No Lucas, reflection, common-index, zero-moment, subinterval, or current resultant theorem converts that reuse into additional valuation or characteristic-zero height.

Accordingly, the smallest missing arithmetic input is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm mix}(T)
=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^2}{L^2}\right).
}
\]

A stronger but more conventional sufficient target is the typed row-triple moment

\[
\sum_{n,p}P(n,p)M(n,p)
=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^2}{L^2}\right),
\]

or the still larger full row-triple moment `sum_n binom(K6(n),3)`. Neither is currently proved unconditionally.

Hence the source/sink family has an exact positive common-row carrier and a strictly smaller `L^1` closure target, but **the present congruence bank does not close it**.
