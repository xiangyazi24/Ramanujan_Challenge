# Verdict

**There is a sharp positive reduction, but the currently banked Apéry congruences and row geometry do not prove the required deletion.**

Let `L=log T`. The smallest completion-blind global quantity attached only to the two mixed lower stars is the `L^1` overlap

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm mix}(T)
:=
\sum_n\sum_p \min\{P(n,p),M(n,p)\},
}
\tag{V1}
\]

where:

- `P(n,p)` counts fully filtered assigned-plus rows on quotient-six row `n` with lower prime `p` and natural source anchor `x=7p-1-n`;
- `M(n,p)` counts fully filtered assigned-`M+` rows with the same `(n,p,x)`.

Every physical-edge-disjoint source/sink C4 packing injects, source by source, into matchings between these two row sets. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p \nu_{\rm SS}(p)
\le \mathcal L_{\rm mix}(T).
}
\tag{V2}
\]

A completion-aware matching sum can be slightly smaller, but (V1) is the smallest possible functional depending only on the two star degrees: a complete bipartite completion graph has matching number exactly `min(P,M)`.

The exact positive arithmetic carrier is

\[
\boxed{
pq_Pq_M\mid b_n}
\tag{V3}
\]

for every mixed source pair, where `q_P` and `q_M` are its two upper primes. The three primes are distinct quotient-six hits on the same row, and the typed pair is recovered from `(n,p,q_P,q_M)`. Thus the larger raw mixed-pair moment is a typed row-triple subfamily:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{\rm mix}(T)
:=\sum_{n,p}P(n,p)M(n,p)
\le 2\sum_n\binom{K_6(n)}3.
}
\tag{V4}
\]

Nevertheless, the strongest unconditional estimate currently obtained for the minimal sum (V1) is only

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm mix}(T)
\ll_J
\frac{T^{11/5}}{L^{8/5}}+
\frac{T^2}{L}.
}
\tag{V5}
\]

The ratios of these two terms to `T^2/L^2` are

\[
T^{1/5}L^{2/5}
\quad\text{and}\quad
L,
\tag{V6}
\]

so neither is little-oh. The first exact obstruction is multiplicity: `b_n` pays the distinct upper primes used inside one source matching, but the same row value and the same prime factors may be reused by many different lower anchors `(n,p)`. Lucas and reflection provide support; they do not provide the valuation or mixed-codegree amplification required to pay those occurrences.

The smallest new arithmetic input is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n,p}\min\{P(n,p),M(n,p)\}
=o_J\!\left(\frac{T^2}{L^2}\right).
}
\tag{MIXED-SOURCE-L1}
\]

This is strictly stronger than the desired C4 deletion because it counts all mixed source matchings, including pairs with no sink closure. The still stronger typed row-triple estimate `\mathcal T_mix=o(T^2/L^2)` would also suffice, but is not the smallest sum.

No finite scan or independence heuristic is used.

# 1. Exact source/sink geometry

After ordering the four distinct lower zeros as

\[
0\le a<b<c<d<p,
\]

the source/sink type is

```text
a --P--> b <-M+-- c --P--> d --M+--> a.
```

Its four assigned physical rows are

\[
P(a,b),\qquad M^+(c,b),\qquad P(c,d),\qquad M^+(d,a).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The natural directed graph has unique source `c` and unique sink `b`.

For a plus arrow `x -> y` with `x<y`,

\[
q_P(x,y)=\frac{7p-y+x}{6},
\qquad
u_P(x,y)=y-2x-1.
\tag{1.2P}
\]

For an `M+` arrow from a larger tail `x` to a smaller head `y`,

\[
q_M(x,y)=\frac{8p-x+y}{7},
\qquad
u_M(x,y)=\frac{p-x-6y}{7}-1.
\tag{1.2M}
\]

At a natural source `x`, both signs have the same row index

\[
\boxed{n_x=7p-x-1=6q+u.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

For plus, this follows by adding the two formulas in (1.2P); for `M+`, it follows from (1.2M). This common `n_x` is the load-bearing source identity. The two rows incident to a sink do not share their first common index.

# 2. The exact minimal global sum

Fix the fully filtered physical row set at one lower prime `p`. For a lower zero `x`, let

\[
\mathscr P_{p,x}
:=\{\text{retained assigned }P(x,y)\text{ rows with }y>x\},
\]

\[
\mathscr M_{p,x}
:=\{\text{retained assigned }M^+(x,y)\text{ rows with }y<x\},
\tag{2.1}
\]

and put

\[
d_P(p,x)=|\mathscr P_{p,x}|,
\qquad
d_M(p,x)=|\mathscr M_{p,x}|.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For fixed `(p,x)` and sign, the upper prime `q` determines the other lower endpoint and the upper digit. The two row sets are disjoint: if one `(p,x,q)` satisfied both sign certificates, then it would have the same physical state

\[
(n,q,u)=(7p-x-1,q,7p-x-1-6q),
\]

and deterministic minus-first assignment would retain it in exactly one sign class.

## 2.1 Completion-aware form

Let `B^{src}_{p,x}` be the bipartite graph with vertex classes

\[
\mathscr P_{p,x},\qquad\mathscr M_{p,x},
\]

where a pair is joined exactly when it occurs as the two source rows of at least one fully filtered source/sink C4.

Cycles in an edge-disjoint packing having source `x` form a matching in this graph, because two such cycles cannot share either source row. Therefore

\[
\#\{C:\operatorname{source}(C)=x\}
\le \nu(B^{src}_{p,x}).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Define the analogous sink completion graph. Then

\[
\boxed{
\nu_{\rm SS}(p)
\le
\min\left\{
\sum_x\nu(B^{src}_{p,x}),
\sum_y\nu(B^{snk}_{p,y})
\right\}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is the smallest completion-aware local-pair sum. It already remembers which pairs close to a C4.

## 2.2 Completion-blind lower-star form

If only the two lower-star marginals are retained, then

\[
\nu(B^{src}_{p,x})
\le \min\{d_P(p,x),d_M(p,x)\}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

This is sharp from the marginals: a complete bipartite completion graph has a matching of size exactly the smaller degree. Hence the smallest source functional depending only on the lower-star degrees is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm src}(T)
:=
\sum_{p,x}\min\{d_P(p,x),d_M(p,x)\}.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

There is an analogous sink quantity, and one may take the smaller source/sink capacity prime by prime. The source side is the useful arithmetic side because of (1.3).

The raw star-pair count

\[
\mathcal P_{\rm src}(T)
:=\sum_{p,x}d_P(p,x)d_M(p,x)
\tag{2.7}
\]

is valid but stronger than necessary. Since integral positive degrees satisfy

\[
\min(d_P,d_M)\le d_Pd_M,
\]

an estimate for (2.7) overpays high-degree stars.

## 2.3 Row-index form

The variables `(p,x)` and `(n,p)` are equivalent via

\[
n=7p-x-1,
\qquad x=7p-n-1.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Define `P(n,p),M(n,p)` to count the two filtered row sets in this parametrization. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm src}(T)
=
\sum_{n,p}\min\{P(n,p),M(n,p)\},
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

which is (V1).

# 3. Exact common-row Apéry carrier

Fix one mixed source pair at `(n,p)`, with upper primes `q_P,q_M`.

Since `p|b_x`, reflection gives

\[
p\mid b_{p-1-x}.
\]

But

\[
n=7p-x-1=6p+(p-1-x),
\]

so Apéry--Lucas gives

\[
\boxed{p\mid b_n.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For either upper row,

\[
n=6q+u,
\qquad q\mid b_u,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{q_P\mid b_n,
\qquad q_M\mid b_n.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is a forward Lucas implication; no cancellation of `b_6` is required.

The upper primes are distinct. Equality would produce one physical state `(n,q,u)` in both sign classes, contrary to deterministic one-sign assignment. Both upper primes exceed `p`, so (3.1)--(3.2) give

\[
\boxed{pq_Pq_M\mid b_n.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The map is occurrence-preserving. From `(n,p)` recover

\[
x=7p-n-1.
\]

From a typed plus prime recover

\[
y_P=7p+x-6q_P,
\tag{3.4P}
\]

and from a typed `M+` prime recover

\[
y_M=7q_M-8p+x.
\tag{3.4M}
\]

Thus `(n,p,q_P,q_M;P,M+)` recovers the mixed source pair, and

\[
y_M<x<y_P.
\]

Let `H_6(n)` be the full prescribed quotient-six hit set and `K_6(n)=|H_6(n)|`. Every raw mixed source pair is a typed triple in `H_6(n)`, with `p` the unique smallest prime. A bare unordered triple has at most two assignments of its two larger primes to the two signs. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n,p}P(n,p)M(n,p)
\le2\sum_n\binom{K_6(n)}3.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the exact positive common-index reduction.

# 4. Unconditional attempt from the selected-row estimate

Let

\[
E_P(T)=\sum_{n,p}P(n,p),
\qquad
E_M(T)=\sum_{n,p}M(n,p).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since

\[
\sum_i\min(a_i,b_i)
\le\min\left\{\sum_i a_i,\sum_i b_i\right\}
\le\frac12\sum_i(a_i+b_i),
\]

we have

\[
\mathcal L_{\rm mix}(T)
\le\frac{E_P(T)+E_M(T)}2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The exact common-integer q-mate map and the currently proved additive-large-sieve estimate give

\[
E_P(T)+E_M(T)
\ll_J
\frac{T^{11/5}}{L^{8/5}}+
\frac{T^2}{L}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

All committed row-local filters only shrink the left side. Therefore (4.2)--(4.3) prove (V5).

But

\[
\frac{T^{11/5}L^{-8/5}}{T^2L^{-2}}
=T^{1/5}L^{2/5},
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
\frac{T^2L^{-1}}{T^2L^{-2}}=L.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus the best current row-incidence theorem remains supercritical. No better estimate follows from the two marginal row counts: a support with equal plus and minus degree at every active source has

\[
\min(P,M)=\frac{P+M}{2}.

# 5. Unconditional attempt from characteristic-zero height

Suppose a source matching at `(n,p)` has size `m`. It uses `m` distinct plus rows and `m` distinct `M+` rows. Their `2m` upper primes are pairwise distinct and all divide `b_n`. Hence

\[
\prod_{q\text{ used}}q
\mid\operatorname{rad}(b_n).
\]

Since every shell prime has logarithm comparable to `L` and `log b_n=O_J(T)`,

\[
2mL\ll_J\log\operatorname{rad}(b_n)\le\log b_n\ll_JT,
\]

so only

\[
m\ll_J T/L.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The corrected first zero moment is

\[
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}Z(p)
\ll_J\frac{T^{8/5}}{L^{4/5}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Summing (5.1) over all possible lower-zero anchors gives at best

\[
\mathcal L_{\rm mix}(T)
\ll_J\frac{T^{13/5}}{L^{9/5}},
\tag{5.3}
\]

which is worse than (V5) and exceeds the target by `T^(3/5)L^(1/5)`.

Grouping equal `n` does not fix the loss. Different lower primes give different anchors `x=7p-n-1`, but one upper hit `q|b_n` may be used by physical rows with many different lower primes. Edge-disjointness acts inside one lower-prime graph and does not prohibit this cross-`p` reuse. The corresponding upper-prime products therefore do not multiply into `rad(b_n)` with occurrence multiplicity.

This is the first exact obstruction: characteristic-zero height controls distinct support of `b_n`, not the number of lower-star incidences reusing that support.

# 6. Unconditional attempt from lower-zero moments and resultants

A mixed source pair has three ordered lower zeros

\[
y_M<x<y_P.
\]

The inverse maps (3.4P)--(3.4M) show that this ordered triple reconstructs the two source rows. Therefore the raw mixed-pair moment satisfies

\[
\sum_{n,p}P(n,p)M(n,p)
\le\sum_p\binom{Z(p)}3.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Even granting the current cubic envelope

\[
\sum_p Z(p)^3
\ll_J T^{14/5}L^{-2/5},
\tag{6.2}
\]

this misses `T^2/L^2` by `T^(4/5)L^(8/5)`. Combining (6.1) with the first moment by Cauchy returns only the old `T^(11/5)L^(-3/5)` second-moment ceiling. The q-mate theorem saves one logarithm and returns (V5), but it does not change the power of `T`.

The three lower zeros also give the usual two-gap continuant/resultant conditions at characteristic `p`. Those are subcritical only when two charged gaps lie in the already-banked small-gap range. In the surviving source/sink sector both gaps may be macroscopic, and the safe resultant height `O(hk log(h+k))` is supercritical after summation. In addition, one lower prime can support many matched source pairs, while no existing theorem amplifies its resultant valuation by the matching multiplicity.

Thus the current recurrence resultants do not prove (MIXED-SOURCE-L1).

# 7. The sink gives no second three-prime common value

At a sink `y`, the incoming plus row and incoming `M+` row use different one-edge common indices:

\[
H_y=14p-1-y,
\qquad
L_y=p+y.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The lower prime divides both, and

\[
H_y+L_y=15p-1.
\tag{7.2}
\]

But the plus upper prime is forced only on `b_(H_y)`, and the minus upper prime only on `b_(L_y)`. Equation (7.2) is a reflection relation modulo `p`; it is not a reflection relation modulo either distinct macroscopic upper prime. Hence the sink supplies no second common Apéry value carrying all three primes. The source carrier (3.3) is the strongest one-edge common-index consequence.

# 9. Final theorem and obstruction

## Theorem — mixed source/sink dominance

For the fully filtered source/sink family,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_p\nu_{\rm SS}(p)
\le
\sum_{n,p}\min\{P(n,p),M(n,p)\}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Every raw mixed source pair counted by `P(n,p)M(n,p)` satisfies

\[
\boxed{pq_Pq_M\mid b_n,}
\tag{9.2}
\]

with three distinct primes, and the typed pair is recovered from `(n,p,q_P,q_M)`.

The strongest current unconditional estimate for the right side of (9.1) is (V5), which is not `o(T^2/L^2)`.

## First exact obstruction

The existing Apéry tools prove

```text
mixed source pair
    => three distinct quotient-six primes divide one b_n.
```

They do not prove an occurrence-sensitive mixed-degree estimate. The same prime factor of `b_n` may be reused by many `(n,p)` lower anchors, while the packing's edge-disjointness is local to one lower-prime graph. No current Lucas, reflection, common-index, zero-moment, subinterval, or resultant theorem converts that reuse into added valuation or subcritical characteristic-zero height.

Accordingly, the smallest missing theorem is precisely (MIXED-SOURCE-L1). A stronger sufficient target is

\[
\sum_{n,p}P(n,p)M(n,p)
=o_J(T^2/L^2),
\]

or the still larger full row-triple moment `sum_n binom(K6(n),3)`. Neither is currently proved unconditionally.

Thus the source/sink family has an exact common-row carrier and a strictly smaller `L^1` closure target, but **the present congruence bank does not close it**.
