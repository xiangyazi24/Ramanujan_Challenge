Equivalently, in the new truncation inequality one may take the truncation height to be \(A_*\); the high-load tail is then empty and the low-load term is \(A_*U\).  Thus the threshold \(B/U_0=T^{2/5}L^{-6/5}\) is exact for this route.

## 2. Exact fixed-state q6 formula

Put

\[
P_0:=\left\lfloor T/7\right\rfloor+1,
\qquad
P_1:=\left\lfloor T/3\right\rfloor,
\qquad
h:=\left\lfloor T/J\right\rfloor.
\]

Fix an upper prime \(q\), a residue \(0\le t<q\), and assume

\[
T<6q+t\le2T.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For a lower prime \(p<q\), write \(d=q-p\).  The strict long-gap condition is exactly

\[
Jd>T
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\le q-h-1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The common lower endpoint of both signs is

\[
L_{q,t}
:=\max\left\{
P_0,
\left\lfloor\frac{6q+t}{7}\right\rfloor+1
\right\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The exact sign upper endpoints are

\[
U^-_{q,t}
:=\min\{P_1,q-h-1,q-t-1\},
\tag{2.4-}
\]

\[
U^+_{q,t}
:=\min\left\{
P_1,q-h-1,
\left\lfloor\frac{12q+t}{13}\right\rfloor
\right\}.
\tag{2.4+}
\]

Thus the two candidate-prime windows are

\[
\mathcal W^-_{q,t}
=\{p\ {m prime}:L_{q,t}\le p\le U^-_{q,t}\},
\]

\[
\mathcal W^+_{q,t}
=\{p\ {m prime}:L_{q,t}\le p\le U^+_{q,t}\}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Empty intervals contribute nothing.  Every inherited finite exceptional-prime or owner mask can only delete primes from these intervals.

For minus, the two literal lower indices are

\[
a_p=q-p-t-1,
\qquad
r_p=6q-6p+t.
\tag{2.6-}
\]

For plus, in the common reflected upper coordinate, the two literal lower indices are

\[
z_p=7p-6q-t-1,
\qquad
w_p=12q-13p+t.
\tag{2.6+}
\]

The window inequalities ensure that all four relevant indices lie in \([0,p-1]\) in their respective signs.

Write \(\zeta_p(u)=1_{\{p\mid b_u\}}\).  The sign leaves are

\[
Y^-_{p,q}(t)
=1_{\mathcal W^-_{q,t}}(p)\,\zeta_p(a_p)\zeta_p(r_p),
\tag{2.7-}
\]

\[
Y^+_{p,q}(t)
=1_{\mathcal W^+_{q,t}}(p)\,\zeta_p(z_p)\zeta_p(w_p),
\tag{2.7+}
\]

and the numerical-prime Boolean union is

\[
Y_{p,q}(t)
=Y^-_{p,q}(t)+Y^+_{p,q}(t)-Y^-_{p,q}(t)Y^+_{p,q}(t).
\tag{2.8}
\]

Finally,

\[
\boxed{A_q(t)=\sum_pY_{p,q}(t).}
\tag{2.9}
\]

### Exact inverse fibre one

For a fixed \((p,q,t)\), the indices in (2.6-) or (2.6+) are determined.  Conversely, an ordered minus zero pair \((a,r)\) reconstructs

\[
d=(a+r+1)/7,
\qquad
t=d-a-1,
\qquad q=p+d,
\]

and an ordered plus zero pair \((z,w)\) reconstructs

\[
d=(z+w+1)/6,
\qquad
t=p-1-z-6d,
\qquad q=p+d.
\]

Hence a fixed numerical prime \(p\) contributes at most one unit to the Boolean-unioned state \((q,t)\):

\[
Y_{p,q}(t)\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{2.10}
\]

This is the precise content of fibre one.  It is **not** injectivity after \(p\) is forgotten.  Distinct characteristics

\[
p_1,p_2,\ldots
\]

may all map to the same common state \((q,t)\), and \(A_q(t)\) counts all of them.

## 3. What the subinterval theorem actually gives

The committed one-characteristic theorem is:

> For every prime \(p\ge7\) and every interval
> \(I\subseteq\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\),
> \[
> \#(\mathcal Z_p\cap I)=O(|I|^{2/3}).
> \tag{3.1}
> \]

This is powerful when many queried indices lie in one fixed characteristic.  It does not control the present maximum, because at fixed \((q,t)\) we ask only one prescribed pair in each of many different characteristics.

For example, in the plus channel the query for characteristic \(p\) is

\[
(z_p,w_p)
=(7p-6q-t-1,\ 12q-13p+t).
\]

Equation (3.1) permits both points to be zeros for every prime \(p\) in the candidate interval.  It only says that the rest of \(\mathcal Z_p\) cannot be too dense in a short interval.

A block decomposition does not repair this.  Partition a candidate \(p\)-interval into blocks of length \(H\).  On one such block, each affine index in (2.6) ranges over an interval of length \(O(H)\).  Applying (3.1) separately in every characteristic gives at best

\[
O(H^{2/3})
\]

available zeros per characteristic, while the state asks for only one prescribed index in that characteristic.  Since \(H^{2/3}\ge1\), this is weaker than fibre one.  Applying it to both coordinates gives an \(O(H^{4/3})\) pair allowance per characteristic, again weaker than the Boolean bound \(1\).

Optimizing over \(H\) therefore returns the one-prime-per-characteristic estimate and no more.

The same failure can be phrased through complete-plane density.  For each fixed \(p\), the affine map from \((d,t)\bmod p\) to either lower pair is invertible, so

\[
\frac1{p^2}\sum_{d,t\bmod p}X^\sigma_{p,d}(t)
=\frac{Z(p)^2}{p^2}
\ll p^{-2/3}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

But a fixed common state chooses the single point

\[
(d,t)=(q-p,t)
\]

in the \(p\)-plane.  As \(p\) changes, the plane changes.  An average over every point of each plane does not bound one adversarially aligned point from every plane.  Supplying that horizontal anti-alignment is exactly the missing theorem.

## 6. The exact missing lemma

Define the two sign counts at a legal common state by

\[
H^-_{q,t}(T,J)
:=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal W^-_{q,t}}
\zeta_p(q-p-t-1)
\zeta_p(6q-6p+t),
\tag{6.1-}
\]

\[
H^+_{q,t}(T,J)
:=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal W^+_{q,t}}
\zeta_p(7p-6q-t-1)
\zeta_p(12q-13p+t).
\tag{6.1+}
\]

The exact Boolean union satisfies

\[
A_q(t)
=H^-_{q,t}+H^+_{q,t}-H^\cap_{q,t}
\le H^-_{q,t}+H^+_{q,t},
\tag{6.2}
\]

where \(H^\cap\ge0\) counts numerical primes satisfying both signs.

### Logically weakest missing statement

The precise pointwise lemma needed by the maximum route is:

> **Q6-HORIZONTAL-MAX.**  For every fixed integer \(J\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> \sup_{\substack{q\ {m prime},\ 0\le t<q\\
> T<6q+t\le2T}}
> \left(H^-_{q,t}(T,J)+H^+_{q,t}(T,J)\right)
> =o_J\!\left(T^{2/5}L^{-6/5}\right).
> }
> \tag{6.3}
> \]

All shell, exceptional-prime, lift, and owner masks may be retained inside the two sums.  By (6.2), (6.3) proves (V), and Section 1 closes C4.

Statement (6.3) is exact but nearly names the desired maximum.  A more arithmetic and naturally scaled target is the following.

### Natural density-scale theorem

> **Q6-HORIZONTAL-PAIR-SIEVE\(_{1/3}\).**  For every fixed \(J\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> \sup_{(q,t)\ {m legal}}
> \left(H^-_{q,t}(T,J)+H^+_{q,t}(T,J)\right)
> \ll_{J,\varepsilon}T^{1/3+\varepsilon}.
> }
> \tag{6.4}
> \]

For any fixed \(\varepsilon<1/15\),

\[
\frac{T^{1/3+\varepsilon}}
     {T^{2/5}L^{-6/5}}
=T^{-1/15+\varepsilon}L^{6/5}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus (6.4) implies (6.3) with a genuine power margin.

The exponent \(1/3\) is not arbitrary.  The full-plane zero-pair density in one characteristic is at most \(p^{-2/3}\), and a legal fixed-state window contains \(O(T/L)\) candidate primes.  Independent-density scaling therefore predicts

\[
(T/L)T^{-2/3}=T^{1/3}/L.
\tag{6.6}
\]

The harmless loss \(T^\varepsilon L\) in (6.4) still leaves the exact \(1/15\) power slack to the maximum threshold.

### Why this lemma is genuinely new

Neither inverse fibre one nor (3.1) compares different defining characteristics.  Same-characteristic gap continuants and resultants constrain several zeros modulo one fixed \(p\).  The sums (6.1) instead require uniform cancellation or scarcity along

\[
p\longmapsto
\bigl(p,\ L_1(p;q,t),\ L_2(p;q,t)\bigr),
\]

where the finite field, recurrence coefficients, and queried residue pair all move with \(p\).

A proof would need one of the following genuinely horizontal inputs:

- a prime-as-characteristic large sieve for the actual Apéry zero indicator;
- a Dwork/Lucas prefix-state theorem giving uniform decorrelation of the two affine residues as \(p\) varies;
- a resultant or monodromy construction whose conductor remains controlled after the q6 affine substitutions.

No such theorem is presently banked.  Treating \(Z(p)^2/p^2\) as a probability at the chosen diagonal point is exactly the unjustified step exposed by the pile in Section 5.
