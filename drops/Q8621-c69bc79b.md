# Q8621: recurrence-aware collision audit

## 1. Characteristic-zero parameterization

Fix signs and nondegenerate defects. Write the first anchor as
\[
 t=z_1,\qquad N=c_1p_1.
\]
The defect equations give
\[
 c_ip_i+z_i=c_1p_1+z_1,
\]
so
\[
 c_ip_i=N-d_i,\qquad z_i=t+d_i.
\]
For a fixed triple of primes this leaves the factorization conditions
\[
 N=a_1p_1,
\quad N-d_2=a_2p_2,
\quad N-d_3=a_3p_3,
\]
with shell constraints
\[
 X<p_i\le 2X,
\]
and with the additional root constraints
\[
 B_{u_i}\equiv0\pmod {p_i}.
\]
The SCRT statement only controls the congruence class of the coefficient $c_1$ modulo $p_2p_3$. It does not by itself bound the number of characteristic-zero lifts of the factorization equations.

The natural collision object is therefore not a shifted gcd but the intersection of three divisibility varieties:
\[
 p_i\mid N-d_i,
\qquad
p_i\mid B_{\phi_i(t)}.
\]
Here $\phi_i(t)$ is the affine folded-root coordinate determined by the sign and reflection convention.

## 2. Repeated-anchor sector

A repeated anchor means that two candidate solutions share one of
\[
 p_i,
\quad c_ip_i,
\quad N-d_i,
\quad (p_i,u_i).
\]
The smallest exact equations are obtained by subtracting two instances:
\[
 (N-d_i)-(N'-d_i)=a_ip_i-a'_ip_i,
\]
so a shared prime gives
\[
N-N'=p_i(a_i-a'_i).
\]
For two shared prime anchors one obtains a divisor condition
\[
\gcd(N-N',N-d_i,N-d_j)>1.
\]
The characteristic-zero gate is therefore:
\[
 \prod_{r\in\mathcal R}|N-N'|\quad\text{has only }O(N^\epsilon)\text{ admissible divisors},
\]
where $\mathcal R$ is the set of repeated shell primes.

This is the place where positive product uniqueness enters: without the positivity of the coefficients and the shell interval, a reflected-star model can generate many formal factorizations. The shell constraints restrict possible divisors of $N-d_i$ to a short interval, but they do not yet give a subpower count.

## 3. All-distinct-anchor sector

For all-distinct anchors the first nontrivial relation comes from the three Apéry conditions. A collision between two translations gives
\[
B_{u_i(t)}\equiv0\pmod {p_i},
\qquad
B_{u_i(t')}\equiv0\pmod {p_i}.
\]
The required resultant gate is therefore
\[
 R_i(t,t')=
\operatorname{Res}_U(B_U, B_{U+(t'-t)\alpha_i})
\]
for the appropriate affine shift $\alpha_i$ after reduction modulo the shell prime.
A proof would need a characteristic-zero lower bound showing that this resultant is nonzero and has height small enough that the number of prime divisors in the shell is subpolynomial.

The actual Apéry recurrence gives the useful nondegeneracy mechanism:
\[
(n+1)^3B_{n+1}=P(n)B_n-n^3B_{n-1}.
\]
The Casoratian with the companion solution implies that two independent solutions cannot have the same zero pattern on a long interval. Thus an attempted descent must convert
\[
B_u\equiv B_v\equiv0\pmod p
\]
into a bounded-index relation. The missing step is a uniform resultant estimate for the recurrence polynomials.

## 4. Reflection does not solve the problem

The congruence
\[
B_{p-1-u}\equiv B_u\pmod p
\]
explains the reflected-root pairs. It does not by itself bound simultaneous reflected choices at three different primes. The reflected-star model fails because it treats the roots as arbitrary subsets stable under reflection.

The Apéry exclusion is precisely the recurrence constraint: the zero set is not an arbitrary reflection-invariant set. It must arise from the second-order recurrence, its Casoratian, and the finite Hasse invariant structure. Any proof must use one of these; reflection alone is insufficient.

## 5. Smallest plausible theorem gate

A sufficient recurrence-aware statement would be:

For fixed nonzero defect vector and signs, every collision of two valid translations gives either

1. a divisor of one of finitely many nonzero characteristic-zero quantities
\[
N-N',\quad N-d_i-N'+d_i,\quad R_i(t,t'),
\]
with height $X^{O(1)}$, or

2. an impossible recurrence coincidence excluded by the Apéry Casoratian.

If the first case holds, the divisor bound gives at most $X^{o(1)}$ possibilities only after proving that the relevant resultant family has bounded degree and nonvanishing. A generic divisor estimate for $N-d_i$ alone is not enough: an integer of size $X^2$ may have $X^{o(1)}$ divisors, but there are $X^2$ possible values of $N$.

Thus the missing gate is not an elementary S-unit estimate. It is a uniform nonvanishing/height theorem for the translated Apéry recurrence resultants.

## 6. Obstruction and conclusion

The finite scans showing singleton fibers are evidence but not a proof. The unresolved statement is exactly:

> translated Apéry-root constraints force the SCRT characteristic-zero collision variety to have only subpower many integral points in the shell box.

The current algebra reduces the problem to explicit divisor/resultant objects, but the all-X subpolynomial estimate does not follow from Lucas congruence, reflection, SCRT, or positive factorization alone. A proof requires a new recurrence-specific input: either a fixed-index Casoratian/resultant bound or an S-unit theorem whose hypotheses are verified for the Apéry zero polynomials.
