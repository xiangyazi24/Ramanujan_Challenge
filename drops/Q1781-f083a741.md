# Q1781 — corrected root-residue audit

## 1. Residue sign correction

For \(\alpha\notin\mathbf F_p\),
\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}\frac1{x-\alpha}
=-\frac{\left(X^p-X\right)'|_{X=\alpha}}{\alpha^p-\alpha}
=\frac1{\alpha^p-\alpha}.
\]
Hence for squarefree \(C\), with roots \(\alpha\),
\[
R=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}\frac1{C(x)}
=\sum_{C(\alpha)=0}\frac1{C'(\alpha)(\alpha^p-\alpha)}.
\]
The sign in Q1776 was reversed.

## 2. Root equations from the interpolation defect

Write
\[
B=qC,\qquad q(X)=(X-m)^2-1,
\]
and
\[
L(B)=(X^p-X)Q_2(X).
\]
At a root \(\alpha\) of \(C\), one has \(B(\alpha)=0\), while
\[
B'(\alpha)=q(\alpha)C'(\alpha).
\]
Therefore the value equation is
\[
L(B)(\alpha)=0,
\]
and the differentiated equation is exactly
\[
L(B)'(\alpha)=-Q_2(\alpha)+(\alpha^p-\alpha)Q_2'(\alpha).
\]
The left side expands as
\[
L(B)'(\alpha)=L(qC)'(\alpha),
\]
which is a linear combination of the shifted values
\(C(\alpha+1),C(\alpha),C(\alpha-1)\) and shifted derivatives.

The crucial obstruction is that the two equations at a single root do not eliminate these shifted data. The value equation removes the central term proportional to \(C(\alpha)\), but the derivative equation introduces the independent variables
\[
C'(\alpha+1),\ C'(\alpha),\ C'(\alpha-1).
\]
No scalar multiple of the two equations isolates
\(1/(C'(\alpha)(\alpha^p-\alpha))\).

## 3. Transfer-matrix obstruction

The shifted relation can be written as a first-order two-component system
\[
V(\alpha+1)=T(\alpha)V(\alpha),\qquad
V=(C,C')^T.
\]
Multiplying around the \(p\)-cycle gives a monodromy matrix
\[
T_p=\prod_{a\in\mathbf F_p}T(a).
\]
The Green/residue term would require a linear functional extracting the reciprocal Wronskian factor
\[
(C'(\alpha)(\alpha^p-\alpha))^{-1}.
\]
The monodromy identity only gives polynomial relations among the components of \(V\); it does not invert the root derivative. Therefore no Bethe/Stieltjes telescoping identity follows from the cyclic transfer alone.

## 4. Exceptional roots

The only exceptional cases are:

* \(q(\alpha)=0\), i.e. \(\alpha=m\pm1\), impossible because these are already the removed singleton zeros and are not roots of \(C\).
* Multiple roots of \(C\). In that case the simple residue formula is replaced by Hasse-derivative/resultant terms. Those terms involve higher derivatives of \(C\), so they strengthen rather than remove the derivative obstruction.

## 5. Finite checks

For the canonical computations at \(p=19\) and \(p=37\), direct finite-field interpolation gives the expected nonzero defect polynomial and the residue sum \(R\) from
\[
R=-[X^{p-1}]C^{-1}
\]
is not determined by the endpoint/root equations alone. Substitution into the root formula agrees with the coefficient computation. The attempted telescoping expression from the Q1776 route does not reproduce the values.

## Conclusion

The corrected residue identity is valid, but the proposed elimination route fails at a precise algebraic point: the pair
\[
L(B)(\alpha)=0,
\qquad
L(B)'(\alpha)=-Q_2(\alpha)+(\alpha^p-\alpha)Q_2'(\alpha)
\]
contains shifted derivative degrees of freedom. The cyclic transfer matrix preserves this freedom through its monodromy and gives no identity collapsing the residues to a telescoping sum. Thus the root-residue attack does not prove \(R\neq0\); its rigorous output is the derivative/monodromy obstruction above.
