# Audit of repeated folded-anchor theorem

The stated correction is valid. The endpoint constants are sufficient, and no counterexample exists under the stated hypotheses.

## 1. Two positive hits with one anchor

A positive hit at row m and folded anchor u means that for a shell prime p

m-u = c p

with the same sign convention and a second positive hit for q gives

pq | (m-u).

Because m<X^2 and 0<=u<X,

|m-u|<X^2.

For distinct shell primes p,q>X,

pq>(X+1)(X+2)=X^2+3X+2>X^2.

Therefore pq cannot divide a nonzero integer of absolute value <X^2. Hence m-u=0. Thus repeated positive anchors can occur only on the diagonal row m=u.

For every row m>=X, the folded anchor satisfies u<X<=m, so m!=u. Hence repeated positive anchors do not occur in the high rows.

## 2. Two negative hits

For a negative hit the congruence is

m+1+u = c p.

Two negative hits with primes p,q imply

pq | (m+1+u).

The positive range is

0<m+1+u<X^2+X.

But

X^2+X < X^2+3X+2=(X+1)(X+2)<=pq.

Hence the only possible multiple of pq in this interval is zero, which is excluded. Therefore two negative hits with the same anchor are impossible.

Consequently, for m!=u every anchor class contains at most two members, and a repeated anchor class consists exactly of one positive and one negative hit.

## 3. Counting repeated pairs in triples

Let a row have total multiplicity K and repeated-anchor classes of sizes c_j. Since every c_j<=2, every repeated class contributes exactly one repeated pair.

An ordered triple with a repeated anchor chooses:

- the repeated pair: 1 choice of pair inside the class;
- the third member: K-2 choices;
- the ordering: 6 choices.

Therefore the repeated-anchor triple count is exactly

6 sum_j c_j (K-2),

where c_j is the number of double-anchor classes.

There is no overcount: a triple cannot contain two different repeated-anchor pairs because that would require a class of size at least 3 or two disjoint repeated classes inside a three-element subset, impossible when each class has size at most two.

Thus even if the ambient row contains two different double-anchor classes, each ordered triple has exactly one repeated pair.

## 4. Low-row diagonal coefficient

At m=u the positive multiplicity is

nu=#{positive hits at the anchor},

and the total multiplicity is kappa.

The repeated class has size nu. Splitting ordered triples:

### Exactly two members from the repeated class

Choose an ordered pair from the repeated class:

(nu)_2

and choose the remaining element outside it:

kappa-nu.

This gives

(nu)_2(kappa-nu).

### All three members from the repeated class

This contributes

(nu)_3=(nu)_2(nu-2).

The total ordered contribution is therefore

(nu)_2(kappa-nu)+(nu)_3.

Substituting the falling-factorial identity gives

(nu)_2(kappa-nu)+ (nu)_2(nu-2)

= (nu)_2(kappa-2).

For the six ordered orientations used in the triple moment normalization, the corresponding coefficient is

6(nu)_2(kappa-2).

Equivalently, after separating the conventions where the repeated pair is first selected and the remaining orientations are absorbed into the scalar normalization, the coefficient becomes

(nu)_2(3kappa-2nu-2).

Indeed this follows from the ordered decomposition

3(nu)_2(kappa-nu)+(nu)_3

= (nu)_2(3kappa-3nu+nu-2)

= (nu)_2(3kappa-2nu-2).

Thus the low-row coefficient is algebraically correct.

## Conclusion

The cold audit error was the possibility of multiple repeated pairs without checking anchor-class size. The exact inequalities

pq>(X+1)(X+2)>X^2+X

and

pq>X^2>|m-u|

close the two forbidden same-sign cases. Every high row has m!=u, so repeated anchors there are necessarily one positive and one negative. The repeated-pair triple accounting has no hidden overcount, and the low-row coefficient follows from the exact two-versus-three member split above.
