# Tactical algebra audit: divided Apéry returns and companion labels

## Verdict

The exact endpoint formula, with the Wronskian orientation in the question, is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{v_{r+h}}{v_r}
=-\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^3}
        {(r+h)^3N_{h-1}(r)}.}
\]

Every factor shown here is nonzero under the hypotheses.  The minus sign is real; it comes from evaluating the stated Wronskian at the left endpoint.

For four returns, multiplying the three adjacent ratios and comparing with direct transport produces only the cocycle/telescoping identity.  After substituting the continuant formula, one gets the exact conditional factorization

\[
N_{r_3-r_0-1}(r_0)
=(r_1r_2)^6
 N_{r_1-r_0-1}(r_0)
 N_{r_2-r_1-1}(r_1)
 N_{r_3-r_2-1}(r_2),
\]

but this is not an additional constraint: it is forced by composing the same transfer maps.  Applying a multiplicative character cannot turn it into new information.

For the finite projective fibre \(u=b-qc,\ v=c\), the change from \((b,c)\) to \((u,v)\) is a determinant-one shear.  It leaves the Wronskian constant unchanged, and every return-label transport identity has exactly the same form for every \(q\).  Thus the companion-label route does not distinguish \(q=0\) from \(q\ne0\).  The supplied \(p=709,q=67\) repeat and the committed \(p=1297\) projective phantoms are counterexamples to any claim that the resulting label law characterizes the Apéry-zero fibre.

The minimal symmetry-breaking datum is a marked Apéry line, equivalently one marked covector/initial condition.  Concretely,

\[
L(s):=s_1-5s_0,
\qquad L(b-qc)=-q.
\]

Hence \(q=0\) is exactly the non-
\(\mathrm{GL}_2\)-invariant condition \(L(u)=0\).  Any successful argument must use such an external anchoring (or an equivalent arithmetic structure selecting \(b\)); it cannot be manufactured from segmented versus direct companion transport.

## 1. Exact endpoint ratio

Work over \(F=\mathbb F_p\).  Because

\[
0<r<r+h<p,
\]

all indices and all denominator factors used below are units in \(F\).  Independence of \(u,v\) implies \(K\ne0\).

### 1.1. Consecutive zeros are impossible

If \(u_n=u_{n+1}=0\) at nonsingular indices, then the stated Wronskian at \(n+1\) gives

\[
v_{n+1}u_n-v_nu_{n+1}=0=\frac K{(n+1)^3},
\]

contradicting \(K\ne0\).  Therefore the hypotheses \(u_r=u_{r+h}=0\) force

\[
h\ge2,\qquad u_{r+1}\ne0,\qquad u_{r+h-1}\ne0.
\]

### 1.2. Cleared forward transport

For \(t\ge1\), put

\[
D_t(r):=\prod_{j=2}^{t}(r+j)^3,
\]

with the empty product \(D_1(r)=1\).  Under \(u_r=0\), induction on \(t\) gives

\[
\boxed{D_t(r)u_{r+t}=N_t(r)u_{r+1}.}\tag{1}
\]

The base cases are

\[
u_{r+1}=N_1(r)u_{r+1}
\]

and

\[
(r+2)^3u_{r+2}=P(r+1)u_{r+1}=N_2(r)u_{r+1}.
\]

For the induction step, apply the recurrence at \(n=r+t\):

\[
(r+t+1)^3u_{r+t+1}
=P(r+t)u_{r+t}-(r+t)^3u_{r+t-1}.
\]

Multiplying by \(D_t(r)\), using
\(D_t(r)=(r+t)^3D_{t-1}(r)\), and invoking the induction hypotheses gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{t+1}(r)u_{r+t+1}
&=\bigl(P(r+t)N_t(r)-(r+t)^6N_{t-1}(r)\bigr)u_{r+1}\\
&=N_{t+1}(r)u_{r+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

At the return \(u_{r+h}=0\), equation (1) and \(u_{r+1}\ne0\) imply

\[
N_h(r)=0.
\]

Applying (1) with \(t=h-1\) gives

\[
u_{r+h-1}
=\frac{N_{h-1}(r)}{D_{h-1}(r)}u_{r+1}.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular \(N_{h-1}(r)\ne0\), since the other three factors in (2) are nonzero.

### 1.3. Wronskian evaluation at the two endpoints

At \(n=r+1\), the Wronskian convention in the question gives

\[
v_{r+1}u_r-v_ru_{r+1}=\frac K{(r+1)^3},
\]

hence

\[
-v_ru_{r+1}=\frac K{(r+1)^3}.
\tag{3}
\]

At \(n=r+h\), it gives

\[
v_{r+h}u_{r+h-1}-v_{r+h-1}u_{r+h}
=\frac K{(r+h)^3},
\]

hence

\[
v_{r+h}u_{r+h-1}=\frac K{(r+h)^3}.
\tag{4}
\]

Dividing (4) by (3), then using (2), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{v_{r+h}}{v_r}
&=-\frac{(r+1)^3u_{r+1}}
          {(r+h)^3u_{r+h-1}}\\[2mm]
&=-\frac{(r+1)^3D_{h-1}(r)}
          {(r+h)^3N_{h-1}(r)}\\[2mm]
&=-\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^3}
          {(r+h)^3N_{h-1}(r)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves the claimed formula, including the sign and every cubic factor.  Notice that \(K\) cancels: the ratio is intrinsic to the return block, not to the normalization of the fundamental pair.

## 2. Four returns: direct transport adds no equation

Let

\[
r_0<r_1<r_2<r_3,
\qquad h_i:=r_{i+1}-r_i\quad (i=0,1,2),
\qquad H:=r_3-r_0.
\]

Define

\[
R(r,h):=-\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^3}
               {(r+h)^3N_{h-1}(r)}.
\]

The endpoint formula says

\[
\frac{v_{r_{i+1}}}{v_{r_i}}=R(r_i,h_i),
\qquad
\frac{v_{r_3}}{v_{r_0}}=R(r_0,H).
\]

But therefore

\[
R(r_0,H)
=R(r_0,h_0)R(r_1,h_1)R(r_2,h_2)
\tag{5}
\]

is merely

\[
\frac{v_{r_3}}{v_{r_0}}
=\frac{v_{r_1}}{v_{r_0}}
 \frac{v_{r_2}}{v_{r_1}}
 \frac{v_{r_3}}{v_{r_2}}.
\]

There is no unused compatibility condition here.

Substituting the explicit expressions into (5) and observing that

\[
\prod_{n=r_0+1}^{r_3-1}n^3
=\left(\prod_{n=r_0+1}^{r_1-1}n^3\right)
 r_1^3
 \left(\prod_{n=r_1+1}^{r_2-1}n^3\right)
 r_2^3
 \left(\prod_{n=r_2+1}^{r_3-1}n^3\right)
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
N_{H-1}(r_0)
=(r_1r_2)^6
 N_{h_0-1}(r_0)
 N_{h_1-1}(r_1)
 N_{h_2-1}(r_2).}
\tag{6}
\]

Equation (6) is exact, but it is conditional on the three return equations
\(N_{h_i}(r_i)=0\).  It is the scalar shadow of block-matrix composition, not a fourth independent restriction.

More generally, for \(m\) consecutive return blocks with internal return times
\(r_1,\ldots,r_{m-1}\), the same cancellation gives

\[
N_{r_m-r_0-1}(r_0)
=(-1)^{m-1}
 \left(\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}r_j^6\right)
 \prod_{i=0}^{m-1}N_{r_{i+1}-r_i-1}(r_i).
\tag{7}
\]

The signs in (7) are another useful check: two blocks give a minus sign; the three blocks belonging to four return times give the plus sign in (6).

### No cross-ratio survives

Put

\[
\rho_{ij}:=\frac{v_{r_j}}{v_{r_i}}.
\]

Then every path comparison is the cocycle law

\[
\rho_{ik}=\rho_{jk}\rho_{ij}.
\]

For example, the natural four-index expression is identically

\[
\frac{\rho_{02}\rho_{13}}{\rho_{03}\rho_{12}}
=
\frac{(v_{r_2}/v_{r_0})(v_{r_3}/v_{r_1})}
     {(v_{r_3}/v_{r_0})(v_{r_2}/v_{r_1})}
=1.
\]

This is not a projective cross-ratio carrying new geometry; it is cancellation in a one-dimensional multiplicative cocycle.

Likewise, applying a multiplicative character to (6) only applies that character to an already exact equality.  For the quadratic character, the internal factor \((r_1r_2)^6\) disappears, leaving

\[
\chi\bigl(N_{H-1}(r_0)\bigr)
=
\chi\bigl(N_{h_0-1}(r_0)
           N_{h_1-1}(r_1)
           N_{h_2-1}(r_2)\bigr),
\]

but this is not a new character obstruction.  For a general character one retains the known character of the sixth-power factor; the conclusion is still merely the character image of (6).

## 3. The finite Apéry fibre is invisible to this label law

Let \(b,c\) denote the two solutions normalized by

\[
(b_0,b_1)=(1,5),
\qquad
(c_0,c_1)=(0,1).
\]

At a finite projective fibre \(q\), set

\[
u=b-qc,\qquad v=c.
\]

As a change of solution basis this is

\[
(u,v)=(b,c)
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
-q&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is \(1\).  Directly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_nu_{n-1}-v_{n-1}u_n
&=c_n(b_{n-1}-qc_{n-1})
  -c_{n-1}(b_n-qc_n)\\
&=c_nb_{n-1}-c_{n-1}b_n.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the Wronskian constant \(K\) is exactly independent of \(q\).

At a finite fibre,

\[
u_n=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b_n=q\,c_n,
\]

and \(c_n\ne0\).  If the same fibre returns at \(r\) and \(r+h\), then \(u_r=u_{r+h}=0\), so the whole derivation in Sections 1 and 2 applies.  Neither the return equation \(N_h(r)=0\) nor the companion ratio formula contains \(q\).

There is also an explicit companion-gauge explanation.  Replacing

\[
v\longmapsto v'=\alpha v+\beta u,\qquad \alpha\ne0,
\]

gives, at every return time \(r_i\),

\[
v'_{r_i}=\alpha v_{r_i}.
\]

Hence all label ratios are unchanged.  Scaling \(u\) changes neither its return set nor these ratios.  The only gauge-invariant companion-label data are therefore the return multipliers already calculated by the continuants.

### The supplied \(p=709\) example

Under the stated exact repeat at \(282\) and \(300\) in the finite fibre \(q=67\), one has \(u_{282}=u_{300}=0\).  If “exact solution-vector repeat” means the stated ordered pair of distinguished and companion values repeats, then also \(c_{300}=c_{282}\), so

\[
\frac{c_{300}}{c_{282}}=1.
\]

The endpoint formula does not explain this by a special \(q=0\) phenomenon.  It merely rewrites it as

\[
1
=-\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=283}^{299}n^3}
        {300^3N_{17}(282)}
\pmod{709}.
\]

The reflected return times are

\[
709-1-300=408,
\qquad
709-1-282=426,
\]

so the sorted four-return chain is

\[
282<300<408<426
\]

with gaps \(18,108,18\).  Direct-versus-segmented transport yields only

\[
N_{143}(282)
=(300\cdot408)^6
 N_{17}(282)N_{107}(300)N_{17}(408)
\pmod{709},
\]

which is precisely (6).  Carrier cleanliness does not alter this conclusion: it removes known degeneracies but does not break the solution-space shear symmetry.

### The committed \(p=1297\) phantoms

The repository verifier records a carrier-clean nonzero projective state at
\(p=1297\), start \(360\), with four returns having gaps \((5,20,10)\), and verifies the reflected chain as well.  Since the state is explicitly asserted finite and nonzero, it is a \(q\ne0\) example satisfying exactly the same continuant and companion-label laws as a four-return chain in the Apéry fibre.

The \(p=709\) exact repeat makes the failure stronger: even a particularly rigid absolute-looking repeat can occur away from the Apéry fibre once a normalization has been fixed.

A caveat is important.  If one permits full transport back to the marked initial basis and simply computes \(q=b_r/c_r\), then of course one can test \(q=0\).  But that uses the missing marked-basis datum explicitly; it is not a consequence of comparing companion labels along return blocks.

## 4. Clean no-go lemma

### Lemma (companion-label data add nothing to return transport)

Let \(F\) be a field, let \(S\) be the two-dimensional solution space of a nonsingular second-order linear recurrence on an index interval, and let \(0\ne u\in S\).  Suppose

\[
r_0<r_1<\cdots<r_t
\]

are zeros of \(u\).  Let \(v\in S\setminus Fu\).  Then:

1. \(v_{r_i}\ne0\) for every \(i\).
2. For every \(i<j\), the ratio
   
   \[
   \rho_{ij}:=\frac{v_{r_j}}{v_{r_i}}
   \]
   
   is unchanged under every companion gauge change
   \(v\mapsto\alpha v+\beta u\) with \(\alpha\ne0\).
3. The ratios obey
   
   \[
   \rho_{ik}=\rho_{jk}\rho_{ij}.
   \]
   
   Consequently every identity obtained only by comparing two factorizations of transport through the return times is a consequence of this cocycle law.
4. For the divided Apéry recurrence, each \(ho_{ij}\) is exactly the continuant expression proved in Section 1.

#### Proof

For each index \(n\), let

\[
\operatorname{ev}_n:S\to F,\qquad s\mapsto s_n
\]

be evaluation at \(n\).  At a return time \(r_i\), the Wronskian and \(K\ne0\) imply \(v_{r_i}\ne0\), so \(\operatorname{ev}_{r_i}\) is a nonzero functional.  Since \(u_{r_i}=0\), its kernel contains \(Fu\); both are one-dimensional, hence

\[
\ker(\operatorname{ev}_{r_i})=Fu.
\]

All return evaluations therefore descend to nonzero functionals on the same one-dimensional quotient \(S/Fu\).  Thus they are proportional:

\[
\operatorname{ev}_{r_j}=\rho_{ij}\operatorname{ev}_{r_i}.
\]

Evaluating this equality on \(v\) gives
\(ho_{ij}=v_{r_j}/v_{r_i}\).  Replacing \(v\) by
\(\alpha v+eta u\) multiplies both endpoint values by \(\alpha\), proving gauge invariance.  Composing proportionalities proves the cocycle law.  The explicit Apéry formula is Section 1.  ∎

### No-go consequence

For a fixed return configuration, the entire gauge-invariant companion-label data are already determined by the recurrence and the return blocks.  The labels supply no extra bit identifying which projective solution line \(Fu\) produced the returns.

In particular, any proposed \(q=0\) test that uses only

* the return indices;
* the equations \(N_h(r)=0\);
* direct and segmented transfer matrices;
* the Wronskian constant in a basis-covariant way; and
* companion values only through gauge-invariant ratios or character images of those ratios

must do one of two things:

1. reduce to the cocycle identities above and therefore hold for every finite fibre that has the same pattern of returns; or
2. introduce an external normalization/reference line, in which case it is no longer a pure companion-label argument.

This is the appropriate no-go statement.  It does **not** claim that no special arithmetic property of the Apéry solution can ever distinguish it.  It says specifically that segmented-versus-direct return transport and its companion labels cannot do so.

## Minimal extra datum that can distinguish \(q=0\)

The projective group acts transitively on solution lines.  Therefore \(q=0\) has no invariant meaning until one marks the Apéry line \(Fb\), or equivalently marks an affine origin in the projective pencil generated by \(b,c\).

The smallest concrete anchoring is one nonzero covector whose kernel is \(Fb\).  With the repository normalization, take

\[
L(s):=s_1-5s_0.
\]

For \(u=b-qc\),

\[
(u_0,u_1)=(1,5-q),
\qquad
L(u)=(5-q)-5=-q.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{q=0\iff L(u)=0.}
\]

Equivalently, one may mark \(b\) and use

\[
\det\!\begin{pmatrix}u_0&b_0\\u_1&b_1\end{pmatrix}
=u_0b_1-u_1b_0=q.
\]

Both formulations explicitly break the 
\(\mathrm{GL}_2\) symmetry.  They also show why fixing only \(v=c\) and the Wronskian \(K\) is insufficient: the determinant-one shear
\(b\mapsto b-qc\) leaves both of those data unchanged.

An arithmetic substitute would have to canonically select the same marked line—for example, a boundary condition, integral lift, positivity property, or other global structure proved to characterize \(b\).  Merely adding more return ratios, reflected returns, character evaluations, or alternative parenthesizations of the transfer product cannot provide that missing datum.

## Final assessment

The companion-label route is closed in the proposed form.

* The exact ratio is the boxed formula in Section 1.
* Four-return multiplication gives the exact continuant factorization (6), but it is a telescoping identity and nothing more.
* The determinant-one shear \(b\mapsto b-qc\) makes the entire transport/label formalism \(q\)-blind.
* The supplied \(p=709,q=67\) repeat and the committed \(p=1297\) phantoms rule out attempts to reinterpret the universal label law as an Apéry-fibre signature.
* A genuine discriminator must import a non-
  \(\mathrm{GL}_2\)-invariant anchoring such as the marked Apéry line \(L(s)=0\).  That is extra arithmetic input, not hidden content of the telescoping transport identity.
