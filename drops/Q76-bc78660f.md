# Gross--Koblitz approach to Apéry Mellin zeros

## Summary

The Gross--Koblitz idea is conceptually close to the right p-adic object, but the proposed exponential sum is not directly a Gauss-sum problem. The key obstruction is that the Mellin transform is already a multiplicative Fourier coefficient of the K3 trace function, while the zero condition is an additive condition on the value of that coefficient.

## 1. Multiplicative Fourier decomposition

Write
\[
\theta_p(a)=\sum_j c_j\omega^j(a).
\]
Then
\[
T_p(r)=\sum_a\theta_p(a)\omega^r(a)
=(p-1)c_{-r}.
\]
This is simply multiplicative character orthogonality. It does not introduce Gauss sums. The identity
\[
T_p(r)=T_p(p-1-r)
\]
is the reflection symmetry, not a new analytic estimate.

The Gross--Koblitz formula applies when a quantity has already been expressed through Gauss sums
\[
g(\chi)=-\pi^s\prod_i\Gamma_p(\alpha_i).
\]
The Mellin coefficient of a general K3 trace sheaf is not automatically such a Gauss sum.

## 2. Where Gross--Koblitz could enter

The correct place is a hypergeometric realization of the K3 trace. If the Apéry K3 family admits an explicit finite-field hypergeometric expression, then its Mellin coefficients may become combinations of Jacobi sums or Gauss sums. In that case Gross--Koblitz gives p-adic valuations of the individual pieces.

However, the resulting object is usually a p-adic expression for the trace, not immediately a bound for
\[
S(t)=\sum_r e_p(tT_p(r)).
\]

## 3. The additive Fourier detector

The zero indicator is
\[
1_{T_p(r)=0}=\frac1p\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}e_p(tT_p(r)).
\]
Hence the desired estimate requires cancellation in
\[
S(t)=\sum_r e_p(tT_p(r)).
\]
This is an additive character sum whose phase is a Mellin coefficient. It is a nonlinear exponential sum over the family of Mellin values.

Gross--Koblitz controls p-adic size of Gauss sums; it does not by itself give archimedean cancellation of additive phases of Mellin coefficients.

## 4. Dwork trace formula perspective

A possible route would be:

1. Express the Apéry K3 family as a rigid hypergeometric F-crystal.
2. Express Mellin coefficients using Dwork cohomology.
3. Interpret \(S(t)\) as a trace of Frobenius on a larger exponential-sum sheaf.
4. Prove geometric irreducibility and large monodromy.
5. Apply Deligne/Katz bounds.

This would be a new sheaf construction. The difficulty is shifted from the original Mellin sheaf problem to the exponential sheaf obtained after composing with the additive character.

## 5. Would this improve the known \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\) bound?

Potentially yes, but not from Gross--Koblitz alone. A square-root type estimate
\[
|S(t)|\ll p^{1/2}
\]
would be much stronger than currently available and would imply strong zero-density information.

But proving such an estimate requires exactly the missing geometric input: understanding the monodromy of the transformed exponential sheaf.

## 6. Strategic conclusion

The Gross--Koblitz idea is not a dead end. It identifies a promising bridge:

\[
\text{Apéry K3 trace}
\rightarrow
\text{finite-field hypergeometric expression}
\rightarrow
\text{Gauss/Jacobi sums}
\rightarrow
\text{Dwork cohomology}
\rightarrow
\text{exponential-sum cancellation}.
\]

The missing theorem is not a gamma identity; it is a Deligne-type cancellation theorem for the additive exponential transform of the Mellin sheaf. This is essentially the same defining-characteristic gap in a different formulation.
