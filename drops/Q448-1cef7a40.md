# Q448: Hypergeometric motive interpretation of b_{floor(alpha p)} mod p

## Executive summary

The proposed HGM interpretation is promising for the fixed Apéry congruences, but the step from

  b_{floor(alpha p)} mod p

with varying alpha to a Katz-Sarnak family is not currently justified. The crucial distinction is:

- Apéry numbers form one fixed Picard-Fuchs solution.
- The index floor(alpha p) changes the Frobenius situation; it does not automatically produce a geometric family of motives M_alpha.

The alpha=1/2 identity is a genuine modular-form congruence, but it should not be interpreted as evidence that all alpha give a one-parameter family with known monodromy.

## 1. General alpha and finite-field hypergeometric expressions

For alpha=1/2, Ahlgren-Ono gives

  b_{(p-1)/2} = a_p(f_8) (mod p).

This comes from special geometry: the middle cohomology of the Apéry K3 family has a modular factor.

For general alpha=a/b, Dwork/Katz theory often gives finite-field hypergeometric descriptions of truncated hypergeometric sums at rational arguments. However:

  b_{floor(ap/b)} mod p

is not automatically the trace of a new hypergeometric motive with parameters depending on a/b.

The natural parameters of the Apéry differential equation remain fixed. The moving quantity is the truncation index, not the underlying differential equation.

Therefore the statement

  b_{floor(alpha p)} = Tr(Frob_p | M_alpha)

is a conjectural geometric upgrade, not a consequence of the Apéry 4F3 representation alone.

## 2. Sage HypergeometricMotives

Sage's HypergeometricMotives machinery computes Euler factors for genuine hypergeometric data:

  H = HGM(parameters)

It is useful for testing known motives, for example the Apéry modular form factors.

However it does not create a motive from the integer sequence value b_r. To use it here one would need:

1. an explicit hypergeometric sheaf/motive whose trace equals b_r mod p;
2. proof that this sheaf varies algebraically with alpha;
3. a comparison theorem identifying the trace.

Without this input, Sage cannot verify the proposed family.

## 3. The Apéry hypergeometric parameters

The Apéry recurrence corresponds to a rank-3 Picard-Fuchs equation. It is not simply the classical

  _4F_3(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2;1,1,1;z)

family evaluated at a moving point.

There are related hypergeometric descriptions, but the Apéry operator has its own singularity structure and geometric realization. The modular identity at (p-1)/2 is a special fiber phenomenon.

Changing (p-1)/2 to r=floor(alpha p) changes the truncation level. It does not correspond merely to replacing z by r/p.

## 4. Computational tests p=73 and p=19

For

  p=73, r=2,

and

  p=19, r=8,

we know

  b_r = 0 mod p.

An HGM trace calculation could only be meaningful after specifying the actual sheaf. There is currently no canonical HGM object called "the Apéry motive at alpha=r/p".

Thus a zero trace cannot yet be computed in the proposed sense.

A useful computational program would instead be:

1. enumerate candidate hypergeometric sheaves with local data matching the Apéry operator;
2. compute their Frobenius traces;
3. compare against b_r mod p for many rational alpha.

## 5. Monodromy question

If a genuine family M_alpha existed, then Katz-Sarnak philosophy would become relevant. The expected monodromy would likely be a large subgroup of a symplectic or orthogonal group because Apéry geometry comes from K3-type cohomology.

But the missing ingredient is the family itself.

The fixed Apéry motive has known geometric monodromy. A family indexed by alpha has not been constructed.

The statement

  #{alpha : Tr(Frob_p,M_alpha)=0} ~ C/p

is therefore a prediction, not a theorem currently available for Apéry zeros.

## 6. Relation to Lang-Trotter

Fouvry-Murty and David-Pappalardi average over an algebraic family of elliptic curves:

  E_t : t in an integral parameter space.

The parameter is geometric and the Frobenius representations form one compatible family.

The Apéry situation differs:

- alpha=n/p depends on the prime p;
- alpha is rational with denominator p;
- no algebraic parameter space of motives M_alpha is known.

Hence those theorems cannot be applied directly.

## 7. Literature status

The relevant literature does establish pieces:

- Katz: hypergeometric sheaves and monodromy groups.
- Rodriguez-Villegas: hypergeometric motives.
- Long, Tu, Yui and others: modularity of Apéry-like recurrences.
- Ahlgren-Ono: the half-index Apéry congruence.
- Roberts-Rodriguez-Villegas: hypergeometric supercongruences.

However, a theorem identifying the entire collection

  {b_{floor(alpha p)} mod p}_alpha

with a one-parameter hypergeometric motive family appears not to be known.

## Consequence for P3.2

The Katz-Sarnak route would be extremely powerful if the missing family construction existed. It would imply roughly

  sum_{p in (sqrt(n),n]} 1/p = O(1)

and hence bounded average D_a(n).

But the current rigorous status is:

- alpha=1/2: proven modular motive.
- finitely many rational alpha: possible individual modular/hypergeometric interpretations.
- full varying-alpha family: open.

The next realistic step is not applying Lang-Trotter, but constructing the geometric object whose Frobenius traces are exactly the shifted Apéry zeros.
