# Q495: Probabilistic model for (HM)_3

## Executive summary

The probabilistic model is the correct heuristic, but the proposed route has a major gap:

```
Sato--Tate for Frobenius traces
        !=
Equidistribution of zero locations r with b_r = 0 mod p.
```

The former controls the distribution of the values of the trace function.  The latter is a statement about the zeros of a family of finite-field trace functions as the Kummer parameter varies.  It requires a separate geometric monodromy theorem for the parameter family.

The most plausible proof architecture is:

1. construct the sheaf whose trace function is

   b_r mod p,

   with r interpreted as a multiplicative character parameter;
2. prove large geometric monodromy in the r-parameter family;
3. apply Deligne/Katz equidistribution plus a zero-counting theorem;
4. combine with a multi-prime independence theorem (Chebotarev on the product monodromy group).

This is substantially stronger than ordinary Sato--Tate.

---

## (A) From Sato--Tate to the distribution of Z_p

For the Apéry family we have

\[
Z_p=\{r\in[0,p-1]:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

The known Sp_4 statement says roughly:

\[
\mathrm{Frob}_p \in G(\mathbb F_p),\qquad G\simeq Sp_4,
\]

becomes equidistributed in the compact form USp(4).  Therefore the normalized trace

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)}{p^{3/2}}
\]

has the Sato--Tate distribution.

However, b_r is not the trace at a random prime. It is a trace after a Kummer twist:

\[
r\longmapsto \chi_r\longmapsto \operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p|V\otimes\chi_r).
\]

Thus the relevant object is not a single Frobenius distribution, but the family

\[
\{\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p,V\otimes\chi_r):r\in\mathbb F_p^*\}.
\]

A conditional equidistribution statement would look like:

For any interval I in the trace space,

\[
\#\{r: \operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p,V\otimes\chi_r)\in I\}
\]

is asymptotic to the corresponding Haar measure.

This is a Katz-style theorem for a one-parameter family of twists, not the usual Katz--Sarnak theorem over primes.

For the zero set, one additionally needs control of the exact trace-zero fiber:

\[
\#Z_p=\#\{r:T_p(r)=0\}.
\]

This is a Lang--Weil type problem for the zero locus of a trace function, not a direct consequence of Sato--Tate.

---

## (B) Independence across primes

Chebotarev gives independence of Frobenius elements for a fixed algebraic representation:

\[
(\mathrm{Frob}_{p_1},\mathrm{Frob}_{p_2},\mathrm{Frob}_{p_3})
\]

is asymptotically distributed according to the product Haar measure on the monodromy group.

The subtle point is the passage

\[
\mathrm{Frob}_p\mapsto Z_p.
\]

This is a many-to-one nonlinear operation:

\[
\mathrm{Frob}_p \mapsto \{r:T_p(r)=0\}.
\]

Independence survives only if the three prime parameters are governed by a product monodromy group. One needs that there are no hidden geometric correlations between the Kummer twist families at different primes.

The required statement is essentially a Chebotarev theorem for the product sheaf:

\[
\mathcal V_{p_1}\times\mathcal V_{p_2}\times\mathcal V_{p_3}
\]

with geometric monodromy

\[
G^3.
\]

Then the zero sets become asymptotically independent random variables.

This is plausible because the primes are distinct, but it is not automatic from single-prime Sato--Tate.

---

## (C) Can this prove (HM)_3?

Yes, conditionally. The needed chain is:

### Step 1: zero-location equidistribution

Prove that for every p,

\[
Z_p\subset\mathbb F_p
\]

behaves like a Poisson process of intensity 1:

\[
\Pr(|Z_p|=k)\approx e^{-1}/k!.
\]

More strongly, for fixed bounded test functions f,

\[
\sum_{r\in Z_p}f(r/p)
\]

should converge to the uniform measure prediction.

### Step 2: prime independence

For distinct primes:

\[
(Z_{p_1},Z_{p_2},Z_{p_3})
\]

should converge to independent copies.

### Step 3: CRT anti-clustering

If each residue triple is random, then

\[
\operatorname{CRT}(r_1,r_2,r_3)
\]

is uniform modulo

\[
M=p_1p_2p_3\asymp X^3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Pr(\operatorname{CRT}<X^2)
=\frac{X^2}{M}
\asymp X^{-1}.
\]

This gives exactly the required saving.

---

## What theorems are actually needed?

The missing ingredients are:

### 1. Katz geometric monodromy theorem

Need the geometric monodromy group of the Kummer-parameter sheaf:

\[
\mathcal F_r.
\]

The known Sp_4 monodromy of the Picard--Fuchs equation is not enough.

### 2. Deligne equidistribution

Applied to the parameter variety:

\[
r\in\mathbb G_m.
\]

Need cancellation for sums such as

\[
\sum_r e(\lambda b_r/p).
\]

### 3. Zero counting for trace functions

Need bounds of the form

\[
\#\{r:T_p(r)=0\}=O(1)
\]

and stronger distributional versions.

This is closer to results of Katz on exceptional sets of trace functions.

### 4. Product Chebotarev

Need independence of the three prime sheaves:

\[
G_{p_1,p_2,p_3}=G^3.
\]

---

## Important obstruction

The current computational fact

\[
|Z_p|\in\{0,1,2,3\}
\]

is already much stronger than what generic trace-function theory predicts. A random trace function over F_p usually has about p^{1/2} zeros unless it has special geometry.

Therefore the real theorem needed is not merely equidistribution. It is a **rigidity theorem** explaining why the Apéry Kummer family has a bounded zero set.

The universal twist/palindrome theorem helps: it gives a reflection constraint

\[
r\leftrightarrow p-1-r,
\]

but it does not by itself give spatial randomness.

---

## Final assessment

(A) Sato--Tate alone does not imply uniform Z_p locations. A Kummer-family Katz theorem is needed.

(B) Chebotarev independence should survive after passing to Z_p, but only after proving product monodromy for the parameterized sheaves.

(C) The probabilistic proof of (HM)_3 is viable in principle, but it requires two deep inputs:

1. zero-location equidistribution for the Apéry Kummer family;
2. independence of these families across primes.

The CRT step is then immediate.

Among all approaches considered, this is the cleanest conceptual route: it directly matches the observed Poisson statistics and the X^{-1} CRT density. The difficult part is proving that the Apéry zero sets really behave like independent random Frobenius zero sets.
