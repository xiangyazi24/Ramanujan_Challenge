The proposed lcm lemma is **correct**, with the natural conventions

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,0)=1,\qquad \psi(0)=0.
\]

There is no hidden p-adic failure. The application is also correct for the **squarefree product of the target primes**, and the asymptotic improvement is genuine: the old product estimate costs \(O(H\log n)\), whereas the lcm estimate costs

\[
O\!\left(H\log\frac nH+H+\log n\right)
\]

for \(H\ge1\), so every \(H=o(n)\) is removable. For \(H=0\) the cost is simply \(O(\log n)\).

The signs in both quotient branches are correct. In the midpoint specialization \(D=2,c=1\), one indeed has

\[
h=2s-p=-(2g+1),
\]

and the two divisible integers are exactly

\[
2n+1+2g\quad\text{and}\quad 2n+1-2g.
\]

Thus any midpoint cutoff \(G=o(n)\) gives an \(o(n)\) logarithmic contribution from the squarefree target primes with \(g\le G\); the previous sufficient condition \(G\log n=o(n)\) was unnecessarily strong.

# 1. The interval-lcm divisibility lemma

Let

\[
L(A,K)=\operatorname{lcm}(A,A+1,\ldots,A+K),\qquad A\ge1,\ K\ge0,
\]

and put

\[
\Lambda_K=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,K),
\]

with \(\Lambda_0=1\). The claim is

\[
\boxed{
L(A,K)\mid A\binom{A+K}{K}\Lambda_K.
}
\]

This is true prime by prime.

Fix a prime \(\ell\). Write

\[
e=\max_{0\le i\le K}v_\ell(A+i)
\]

and, for \(j\ge1\), let

\[
N_j=\#\{0\le i\le K:\ell^j\mid A+i\}.
\]

Since

\[
A\binom{A+K}{K}
=\frac{A(A+1)\cdots(A+K)}{K!},
\]

we have

\[
v_\ell\!\left(A\binom{A+K}{K}\right)
=\sum_{j\ge1}\left(N_j-\left\lfloor\frac K{\ell^j}\right\rfloor\right).
\tag{1}
\]

For every positive integer \(m\), any block of \(K+1\) consecutive integers contains at least \(\lfloor K/m\rfloor\) multiples of \(m\). Hence

\[
N_j\ge \left\lfloor\frac K{\ell^j}\right\rfloor
\]

for every \(j\), so every summand in (1) is nonnegative.

Now let

\[
r=v_\ell(\Lambda_K).
\]

For \(K\ge1\), this is characterized by

\[
\ell^r\le K<\ell^{r+1},
\]

with \(r=0\) when \(\ell>K\). For each

\[
r<j\le e,
\]

we have \(\ell^j>K\), so

\[
\left\lfloor\frac K{\ell^j}\right\rfloor=0.
\]

But an element of the interval attaining valuation \(e\) is divisible by every \(\ell^j\) with \(j\le e\), so \(N_j\ge1\). Therefore the terms with \(r<j\le e\) contribute at least \(e-r\) to (1). Thus

\[
v_\ell\!\left(A\binom{A+K}{K}\right)\ge e-r.
\]

Adding \(v_\ell(\Lambda_K)=r\) gives

\[
v_\ell\!\left(A\binom{A+K}{K}\Lambda_K\right)\ge e
=v_\ell(L(A,K)).
\]

Since this holds for every prime \(\ell\), the claimed divisibility follows.

For \(K=0\), the statement is simply

\[
L(A,0)=A\mid A\binom A0=A,
\]

so the convention \(\Lambda_0=1\) handles the endpoint exactly.

# 2. The logarithmic bound and Chebyshev \(\psi\)

For an integer \(K\ge1\), the Chebyshev function satisfies the exact identity

\[
\psi(K)=\sum_{p^a\le K}\log p
=\log\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,K).
\]

Hence the divisibility lemma gives

\[
\boxed{
\log L(A,K)
\le \log A+\log\binom{A+K}{K}+\psi(K).
}
\]

At \(K=0\), define \(\psi(0)=0\), and the same formula reads \(\log A\le\log A\).

The estimate

\[
\psi(K)=O(K)
\]

is the classical elementary Chebyshev bound; no prime number theorem is being smuggled into the argument.

Also, for \(1\le K\le A+K\),

\[
\binom{A+K}{K}\le\left(\frac{e(A+K)}K\right)^K,
\]

so

\[
\log L(A,K)
\le \log A
+K\log\frac{e(A+K)}K
+O(K).
\tag{2}
\]

This is the source of the entropy-scale saving over the raw product bound.

# 3. Exact quotient signs

Fix an outer integer \(n\), a target prime \(p\), and write

\[
n=qp+r,\qquad 0\le r\le p-1,
\]

with

\[
s=\min(r,p-1-r).
\]

Let

\[
h=Ds-cp
\]

for fixed \(D\ge1\) and an integer \(c\) (which may depend on the target; the following identities do not require it to be uniform).

There are two branches.

## Direct branch

Here \(r=s\), so

\[
n=qp+s.
\]

Therefore

\[
Dn-h
=D(qp+s)-(Ds-cp)
=(Dq+c)p.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{p\mid Dn-h.}
\]

## Reflected branch

Here

\[
r=p-1-s,
\]

so

\[
n=qp+p-1-s=(q+1)p-1-s.
\]

Hence

\[
D(n+1)+h
=D((q+1)p-s)+(Ds-cp)
=(D(q+1)-c)p.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{p\mid D(n+1)+h.}
\]

At the fixed point \(s=(p-1)/2\), the direct and reflected descriptions coincide; both divisibility identities are valid. There is no seam sign error here.

# 4. Squarefree target divisibility by the short-interval lcm

Assume now

\[
|h|=|Ds-cp|\le H.
\]

Because \(h\) is an integer, if \(H\) is not integral one may harmlessly replace it by \(\lfloor H\rfloor\). So take \(H\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}\).

In the direct branch, the divisible integer \(Dn-h\) lies in

\[
[Dn-H,Dn+H].
\]

In the reflected branch, the divisible integer \(D(n+1)+h\) lies in

\[
[D(n+1)-H,D(n+1)+H].
\]

For fixed \(D\) and \(H=o(n)\), all these integers are positive for sufficiently large \(n\).

Let \(\mathcal T\) be the set of distinct target primes satisfying the small-\(h\) condition, and let

\[
R=\prod_{p\in\mathcal T}p
\]

be their squarefree product. Every \(p\in\mathcal T\) divides at least one integer in the union of the two blocks. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
R\mid
\operatorname{lcm}\Big(
[Dn-H,Dn+H]\cup[D(n+1)-H,D(n+1)+H]
\Big).
}
\tag{3}
\]

This is exactly a **squarefree** statement. From \(p\mid Dn-h\) or \(p\mid D(n+1)+h\) one gets one factor of \(p\), not an arbitrary higher power. So one must not silently upgrade (3) to a claim about unrestricted \(p\)-adic multiplicities. If a separate project lemma supplies a uniform bounded valuation multiplicity, then multiplying the radical bound by that constant is legitimate, but it is a separate input.

There is a slightly cleaner bound than treating the two blocks separately. Their union is contained in the single hull interval

\[
[Dn-H,D(n+1)+H].
\]

Thus, putting

\[
A=Dn-H,\qquad K=2H+D,
\]

we have

\[
R\mid L(A,K).
\tag{4}
\]

This also avoids any semantic issue with calling the two blocks "consecutive": for small \(H\) and \(D>1\) they need not be adjacent, but their fixed-width hull is harmless.

# 5. The asymptotic cost for \(H=o(n)\)

Apply (2) to

\[
A=Dn-H,\qquad K=2H+D,
\]

so that

\[
A+K=D(n+1)+H.
\]

For fixed \(D\), if \(H=o(n)\), then \(K=o(n)\). For \(H\ge1\),

\[
K=O_D(H)
\]

and

\[
\log\binom{A+K}{K}
\le K\log\frac{e(A+K)}K
=O_D\!\left(H\log\frac nH+H\right).
\]

Also

\[
\psi(K)=O(K)=O_D(H),
\qquad
\log A=O_D(\log n).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log R
=O_D\!\left(H\log\frac nH+H+\log n\right).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Since \(H=o(n)\), write \(x=H/n\to0\). Then

\[
\frac1nH\log\frac nH=x\log\frac1x\longrightarrow0,
\]

and of course \(H/n\to0\) and \(\log n/n\to0\). Hence

\[
\boxed{\log R=o(n).}
\]

For \(H=0\), (3) says the targets divide the lcm of the two singleton values \(Dn\) and \(D(n+1)\), so

\[
\log R=O_D(\log n)=o(n).
\]

Thus the displayed \(H\log(n/H)\) formula should simply be read with \(H=0\) handled separately; there is no mathematical problem at that endpoint.

# 6. The two specializations

## 6.1 Endpoint labels: \(D=1,c=0\)

Now

\[
h=s.
\]

Hence

\[
|h|\le H\iff s\le H.
\]

The divisibility identities become

\[
p\mid n-s
\]

in the direct branch and

\[
p\mid n+1+s
\]

in the reflected branch. Therefore every folded endpoint label \(s\le H\) is captured by the short-interval lcm. If \(H=o(n)\), its squarefree logarithmic target mass is \(o(n)\).

At \(H=0\), this says precisely that the \(s=0\) targets divide either \(n\) or \(n+1\), so their radical divides \(\operatorname{lcm}(n,n+1)\).

## 6.2 Midpoint gaps: \(D=2,c=1\)

Let

\[
p=2m+1,\qquad g=m-s.
\]

Then

\[
h=2s-p
=2(m-g)-(2m+1)
=-(2g+1).
\]

So

\[
\boxed{|h|=2g+1.}
\]

If \(G\ge0\) is integral and

\[
H=2G+1,
\]

then

\[
g\le G\iff |h|\le H.
\]

Moreover the two branch identities are exactly the quotient-geometry formulas. In the direct branch,

\[
2n-h=2n+2g+1=(2q+1)p,
\]

while in the reflected branch,

\[
2(n+1)+h=2n+1-2g=(2q+1)p.
\]

Thus every target with \(g\le G\) divides the relevant short-interval lcm, and if

\[
G=o(n),
\]

then \(H=2G+1=o(n)\), so (5) gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{p\ \text{target}\\g(p)\le G}}\log p=o(n)
}
\]

for the squarefree target set.

So yes: **any \(G=o(n)\) is removable.** The old hypothesis \(G\log n=o(n)\) came from bounding by the product of all integers in the short window and is not intrinsic.

# 7. Why this is genuinely stronger than the product bound

The naive argument bounds the squarefree target product by a raw product such as

\[
\prod_{|j|\le H}(Dn+j),
\]

whose logarithm is

\[
O(H\log n).
\]

That only gives \(o(n)\) when

\[
H\log n=o(n).
\]

The lcm lemma removes the enormous repetition coming from common small prime powers among neighboring integers. After the \(K!\) cancellation encoded in the binomial coefficient, the cost is instead

\[
H\log(n/H)+O(H+\log n).
\]

This is strictly stronger in the intermediate regime. For example,

\[
H=\frac n{\log n}
\]

gives a raw product cost of order \(n\), whereas the lcm bound gives

\[
O\!\left(\frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}\right)=o(n).
\]

More generally, \(H=o(n)\) is exactly enough for the entropy term
\(H\log(n/H)\) to be \(o(n)\).

# 8. No contradiction with the far-gap mass lower bound

There is no contradiction. The two statements concern complementary regions.

The lcm result says:

> targets whose associated linear form \(h\) lies in an \(o(n)\)-sized window have only \(o(n)\) squarefree logarithmic mass.

It says nothing comparable when \(H\asymp n\). Indeed if \(H=\alpha n\) with fixed \(\alpha>0\), then the binomial term in (2) is generally of order \(n\), so the lcm argument no longer gives an \(o(n)\) bound.

Therefore any existing far-gap lower-bound argument obtained by subtracting a removable near-gap region from a hypothetical large total mass is only **strengthened**: the near/far cutoff may now be pushed from the old range \(G\log n=o(n)\) all the way to any prescribed \(G=o(n)\). The residual lower bound is then forced farther out into the complementary gap range. Nothing here proves that the far-gap contribution itself is small.

One should keep the quantifiers precise. The result proves removability for every chosen sublinear cutoff \(G(n)=o(n)\); it does not by itself provide an \(o(n)\) estimate for a window of linear width \(cn\), nor does it by itself identify a fixed positive linear gap at which the remaining mass must sit.

- **p-adic bookkeeping:** correct; the \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)\) factor supplies the low valuation levels, and the binomial factor supplies the levels above the interval-length scale.
- **\(K=0\):** correct with \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,0)=1\) and \(\psi(0)=0\).
- **Chebyshev function:** \(\log\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)=\psi(K)\) exactly, and \(\psi(K)=O(K)\) is elementary.
- **Direct sign:** \(p\mid Dn-h\), correct.
- **Reflected sign:** \(p\mid D(n+1)+h\), correct.
- **Midpoint formula:** \(h=-(2g+1)\), correct.
- **Squarefree product:** divides the short-interval lcm; higher \(p\)-powers require a separate valuation input.
- **\(H=0\):** logarithmic cost \(O(\log n)\); the expression \(H\log(n/H)\) should not be used literally at zero.
- **Asymptotic:** for fixed \(D\), every \(H=o(n)\) gives \(o(n)\) log-lcm cost.
- **Strength over product bound:** genuine and substantial.
- **Far-gap lower bound:** no contradiction; the result enlarges the removable near-gap regime but does not control linear-scale far gaps.
