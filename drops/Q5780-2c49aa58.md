# P3.2 strategic route audit: two-carrier gcd problem

## 1. What the new identity actually gives

The new Ore-CRT construction is qualitatively different from the earlier fixed-characteristic recurrences. The important output is not the recurrence alone but a pair of global integers attached to the same Newton block.

Let
\[
T(J)=\delta b,\qquad T(b)=0,
\]
with the shell realization
\[
Z_n(d)=\sum_{j=0}^4 c_j(n)(C_{n+j}(d-1)+C_{n+j}(d+1)).
\]
For a target prime \(p\) with \(n/2<p\le n\) and \(p\mid b_{n-p}\), the Cartier reduction gives
\[
p\mid Z_n(p-1).
\]
The point of the CRT identity is that the constant-origin contribution cancels before reduction; this is a genuine global second carrier.

After Newton projection on a block \((D,D+N]\), we obtain integers
\[
A=G_{D-1,N}(C_{n-1}),\qquad B=G_{D-1,N}(Z_n),
\]
and every moving target prime in the block divides both A and B.

Thus the remaining problem is purely an integer-gcd height problem:
\[
\log\gcd(A,B)=o(n).
\]

The individual heights of A and B being exponential is not itself an obstruction. The question is whether their common divisor is forced to be small.

---

## 2. Formal consequences already available

The following implication is rigorous from the stated identities:

**Lemma 2.1 (global carrier divisibility).** Every target prime in the Newton interval divides \(\gcd(A,B)\).

This is stronger than the old Casoratian/simultaneous-zero mechanism. The prime is not required to annihilate two neighboring recurrence values; it divides two explicitly constructed integers.

However, nothing in the Ore identity alone implies an upper bound on \(\gcd(A,B)\). The missing step is an arithmetic independence theorem for the two projected shell carriers.

---

## 3. Candidate route I: holonomic resultant in the shell variable

The most promising next lemma is a resultant statement.

Define the shell generating objects before Newton projection:

\[
F_n(X)=C_n(X),\qquad H_n(X)=Z_n(X).
\]

A sufficient theorem would be:

**Target lemma (shell resultant bound).** There exists a nonzero polynomial resultant certificate
\[
R_n=\operatorname{Res}_X(F_n(X),H_n(X))
\]
(or an explicitly normalized Ore-Casoratian analogue) such that

1. every common prime divisor of the projected carriers \(A,B\) divides \(R_n\);
2. \(\log |R_n|=o(n)\).

If proved, this immediately gives
\[
\log\gcd(A,B)\le \log|R_n|+O(\log n)=o(n).
\]

The hard part is (2). A naive resultant of degree-growing shell polynomials will probably have exponential Mahler height, so the correct object is likely not the raw polynomial resultant but the primitive resultant after removing the universal hypergeometric factors.

---

## 4. Candidate route II: multiple Ore-CRT variants

Consider
\[
T_i=T+X_i\operatorname{lclm}(P,A_{\mathrm{Ap}}).
\]

Each gives another carrier
\[
B_i.
\]

The desired statement is:

**Finite-output gcd lemma.** For some fixed number r of variants,
\[
\operatorname{rad}\gcd(A,B_1,\ldots,B_r)
\]
has logarithmic height \(o(n)\).

The advantage is algebraic. A common prime divisor would imply a common zero of several independent shell projections. If the corresponding determinant has bounded holonomic rank, it may force the prime into a fixed exceptional set.

The danger is that the variants are all generated from the same Ore module and may differ only by a multiple of the Apéry operator. A first CAS test should compute the rank of the span of the projected carriers modulo a generic prime and measure whether the new outputs are genuinely independent.

---

## 5. Candidate route III: primitive content of the two-output Newton module

This is the closest analogue of a Bezout argument.

Let \(\mathcal M_n\) be the Z-module generated by the Newton projections of the shell vectors appearing in C and Z. A sufficient theorem is:

**Primitive content lemma.** The primitive content of the rank-two module generated by
\[
(A,B)
\]
satisfies
\[
\log \operatorname{cont}(A,B)=o(n).
\]

Equivalently, after removing the maximal common integer factor forced by the two shell modules, the remaining lattice determinant is subexponential.

This is likely approachable through Smith normal form of the finite holonomic moment matrix.

---

## 6. Most likely CAS target

The smallest concrete object to search next is not another recurrence. It is the Smith determinant of the two projected shell columns.

Construct the matrix whose columns are:

\[
(C_{n+j}(d-1)+C_{n+j}(d+1))_{j=0}^4,
\]
and
\[
Z_n(d).
\]

After Newton projection, compute the primitive 2-column determinant over several block lengths.

The desired output is an identity of the form

\[
U_n A+V_n B=E_n,
\]

where

\[
\log|E_n|=o(n)
\]

or where E_n has only a controlled exceptional prime support.

This is the exact analogue of a Bezout certificate.

---

## 7. Load-bearing obstruction

The remaining obstruction is not the old "vertical recurrence" problem. The new construction has overcome that.

The surviving obstruction is:

**Unknown shell arithmetic independence.**

The relation
\[
T(J)=\delta b
\]
proves that the second carrier detects the same target set. It does not prove that the two shell projections are arithmetically transverse.

A countermodel would be possible if the two projected integers satisfied
\[
B=\lambda_n A
\]
with \(\lambda_n\in\mathbb Q\) of exponential denominator. This would preserve all target divisibilities while giving no gcd reduction. The Ore identities alone do not exclude this.

Therefore the missing theorem must be a transversality theorem for the actual Apéry shell coefficients, not a recurrence theorem.

---

## Verdict

The two-carrier construction is the first plausible route to the P3.2 radical bound. The correct next theorem is:

> **Primitive two-shell resultant theorem:** after removing universal Cartier factors, the determinant/content of the Newton-projected pair \((C_{n-1},Z_n)\) is subexponential.

A successful proof can come from:

1. a normalized holonomic resultant in the shell variable;
2. a Smith determinant bound for a finite-rank moment lattice;
3. a bounded number of independent Ore-CRT variants.

The Ore-CRT identity itself is complete. The only remaining mathematical gap is proving arithmetic transversality of the two global shell carriers.
