# Random prefix products in SL₂(F_p) — pointed orbit statistics

## Executive summary

The random model is well understood: prefix products of independent uniform elements of SL₂(F_p) give a Markov chain on P¹(F_p) with a spectral gap, and occupation counts over p steps have Poisson(1)-type statistics. The Apéry transfer-matrix path is much harder: it is an algebraic deterministic walk, not a random walk on an expander. Generation of SL₂(F_p) is far from sufficient.

The right missing theorem for Apéry is an algebraic-walk analogue of a mixing/large-sieve statement for the sequence

\[
M_r=T(r)\cdots T(1).
\]

A proof of such a theorem would essentially imply the desired smallness of Z_p.

---

## (A) Random independent prefix products

Let

\[
X_r=M_rv.
\]

If g_r are independent Haar-uniform in SL₂(F_p), then X_r is the standard random walk on the Schreier graph

\[
SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\curvearrowright \mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p).
\]

The stationary distribution is uniform:

\[
|\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)|=p+1.
\]

For any fixed w,

\[
\mathbb E N_w=\sum_{r=1}^n\Pr(X_r=w).
\]

After the initial mixing transient,

\[
\Pr(X_r=w)=\frac1{p+1}+O(\lambda^r),
\]

where λ<1 is the spectral radius. Therefore for n=p-1:

\[
\mathbb E N_w=\frac{p-1}{p+1}+O(\log p)=1+o(1)
\]

(the O(log p) term comes only from the burn-in window).

For the variance,

\[
\operatorname{Var}N_w
=\sum_r\operatorname{Var}(1_{X_r=w})
+2\sum_{r<s}\operatorname{Cov}(1_{X_r=w},1_{X_s=w}).
\]

The spectral gap gives

\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(1_{X_r=w},1_{X_s=w})|\ll \lambda^{s-r}.
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Var}N_w=1+O(\log p/p)+O(1),
\]

so the variance stays bounded. A Poisson(1) limit follows from standard rare-event Markov-chain arguments when the chain is sufficiently mixing compared with the observation length.

Thus the random statement is essentially true.

---

## (B) Deterministic algebraic walks

The Apéry case is fundamentally different.

We have

\[
T(r)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(r)/(r+1)^3&-r^3/(r+1)^3\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and

\[
M_r=T(r)\cdots T(1).
\]

Even if

\[
\langle T(1),\dots,T(p-2)\rangle=SL_2(\mathbf F_p),
\]

this only says the available matrices generate the group. It says nothing about the ordering

\[
T(1),T(2),\dots,T(p-2).
\]

A deterministic word in generators can have extremely non-random behavior even in an expander group.

The relevant object is not the Cayley graph but the algebraic sequence

\[
r\mapsto M_r.
\]

This is closer to:

- polynomial or rational walks on algebraic groups;
- trace-function sequences;
- Deligne/Katz equidistribution for parameterized families.

One needs bounds such as

\[
\#\{r:M_rv=w\}=1+o(1)
\]

or at least

\[
\sum_w\left(N_w-\frac np\right)^2=o(p).
\]

These are not consequences of Bourgain-Gamburd.

---

## (C) Expander versus algebraic walk

The implication

\[
\text{generates }SL_2(\mathbf F_p)
\Rightarrow
\text{expander}
\]

applies to the random walk using a fixed generating set.

The Apéry path instead uses a word

\[
T(p-2)\cdots T(1)
\]

where each letter is used once.

This is analogous to a non-backtracking/algebraic walk, but existing expander theory does not automatically control it.

The missing input would be an estimate of the form:

\[
\left|\sum_{r\le p}\phi(M_rv)\right|
\ll p^{1/2+\epsilon}
\]

for all nontrivial functions φ on P¹(F_p).

For Apéry, this is exactly the type of cancellation expected from the underlying Picard-Fuchs/K3 geometry.

---

## (D) Conditioned random bridges

Suppose instead we take a random path conditioned on

\[
M_{p-1}=S
\]

for a fixed involution S.

Because the group walk has mixed long before time p, conditioning only affects a bounded number of degrees of freedom near the endpoints. The bulk behaves like independent samples from the stationary distribution.

Therefore one expects

\[
N_w\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(1).
\]

More precisely, the bridge occupation field has the same local statistics as an unconditioned chain away from the endpoints.

The Apéry identity

\[
M(p-1)=\text{swap}
\]

is therefore compatible with randomness, but it does not prove it.

---

## (E) Fibonacci versus Apéry

For Fibonacci,

\[
M_r=T^r.
\]

The orbit is a cyclic subgroup orbit in PGL₂(F_p). Therefore the number of visits is controlled by the period.

Apéry has no such periodicity:

\[
M_r=T(r)\cdots T(1)
\]

with changing T(r).

The possible replacement is not quasi-periodicity but an algebraic equidistribution theorem.

The analogue of periodicity is:

\[
\{M_rv:r\in\mathbf F_p\}
\]

should behave like a trace-function orbit.

A successful theorem would be something like:

\[
\sum_{r\in I}\psi(\text{coordinate}(M_rv))
\ll |I|^{1/2}p^{o(1)}.
\]

Then the return count follows by Fourier expansion.

---

# Consequence for the Apéry zero problem

The transfer-matrix criterion gives

\[
p\mid b_N
\iff
M_Ne_1\in\{x=0\}\subset \mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p).
\]

Therefore Z_p is exactly a pointed return set of the Apéry algebraic walk.

The desired theorem is not a group-generation theorem. It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Apéry algebraic walk has square-root cancellation on }\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p).
}
\]

If proved, it would imply

\[
|Z_p|=O(p^{1/2+\epsilon})
\]

and with stronger uniformity could potentially give O(1)-type bounds.

So the correct analogy is:

- Fibonacci: periodic orbit;
- random SL₂ walk: spectral mixing;
- Apéry: deterministic algebraic orbit requiring a Katz-type equidistribution theorem for prefix products.

The missing theorem is a genuine new piece of arithmetic dynamics, not standard expander theory.
