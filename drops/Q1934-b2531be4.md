# Q1934 P3.2 adjugate derivative

Let \(T_m\) be the square low-even matrix representing the operator \(H\) on the even polynomial basis modulo \(t^p-t\), with \(p=2m+1\). Assume

a\(\det T_m=0\) and
\[
\ker T_m=\langle C\rangle,\qquad C=k-\theta(\delta_1+\delta_{-1}).
\]

## 1. Adjugate perturbation

For any square matrix \(T\), Jacobi's formula gives
\[
\frac{d}{dz}\det(T+zE)\bigg|_{z=0}=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(T)E).
\]

When \(\operatorname{rank}(T_m)=m-1\),
\[
\operatorname{adj}(T_m)=\lambda\, r\ell^{\mathsf T},
\]
where
\[
T_m r=0,\qquad \ell^{\mathsf T}T_m=0.
\]

Taking the normalization \(r=C\), the derivative becomes
\[
\det'(0)=\lambda\,\ell^{\mathsf T}E C.
\]

Choose vectors \(u,v\) satisfying
\[
\ell^{\mathsf T}u=1,\qquad v^{\mathsf T}C=1,
\]
and perturb by the rank-one matrix
\[
T_m(z)=T_m+zuv^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Then
\[
\det T_m(z)'|_{z=0}=\lambda.
\]

Thus every nonzero scalar multiple of the adjugate normalization can be realized by a rank-one perturbation.

## 2. Perturbation giving the reciprocal trace

Let the evaluation functional be
\[
\rho(f)=f(1)=f(-1),
\]
and write the reciprocal trace quantity as
\[
S=\ell^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}_{\mathrm{eval}}u
\]
with the chosen left/right null-vector normalization. A concrete perturbation is
\[
E=\frac{1}{S}u v^{\mathsf T}
\]
so that
\[
\det'(0)=\frac{\lambda}{S}.
\]
Equivalently, absorbing the unit \(\lambda\) into the normalization of \(E\), one obtains
\[
\det'(0)=S^{-1}.
\]

Hence the adjugate construction does not prove \(S\neq0\); it converts that question into the nonvanishing of the corresponding scalar pairing.

## 3. Does the structured matrix force simplicity?

No. Tridiagonal/evaluation structure alone does not imply that the zero eigenvalue is simple.

The required condition is
\[
\dim\ker T_m=1
\]
together with
\[
\ell^{\mathsf T}C\neq0.
\]
A structured matrix may have a one-dimensional kernel but still have a vanishing adjugate pairing for a particular functional. Simplicity of the algebraic eigenvalue requires
\[
\operatorname{rank}(T_m)=m-1
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(T_m)E)\neq0
\]
for some chosen transversal perturbation.

## 4. Explicit matrix form

Let the even basis be
\[
(1,t^2,t^4,\ldots,t^{2m-2}).
\]
Then
\[
T_m=(a_{ij})_{0\le i,j<m},
\]
where
\[
a_{ij}=[t^{2i}]H(t^{2j})
\]
after reduction modulo \(t^p-t\).

A rank-one perturbation is explicitly
\[
T_m(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
 & & \\ 
 &T_m&\\
 & &
\end{pmatrix}
+zuv^{\mathsf T},
\]
with entries
\[
(T_m(z))_{ij}=a_{ij}+z u_i v_j.
\]

The determinant derivative is
\[
\boxed{
\frac d{dz}\det T_m(z)|_0
=\sum_{ij}\operatorname{cof}_{ij}(T_m)u_iv_j
}
\]
and, under rank \(m-1\), equals the null-vector pairing above.

## 5. Smaller missing lemma

If the intended conclusion is equivalent to \(S\neq0\), the missing statement is strictly smaller than invertibility of \(T_m\):

> **Missing lemma.** The left null functional of \(T_m\) does not annihilate the evaluation-normalized kernel vector \(C=k-\theta(\delta_1+\delta_{-1})\); equivalently the adjugate coefficient of the evaluation direction is nonzero.

Formally:
\[
\ell^{\mathsf T}E C\neq0.
\]

This is exactly the scalar obstruction detected by the adjugate derivative, and no stronger determinant computation is needed.
