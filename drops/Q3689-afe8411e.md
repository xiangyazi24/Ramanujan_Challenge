\[
(Lx)_n=(n+1)^3x_{n+1}-P(n)x_n+n^3x_{n-1},
\qquad
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.

\[
B_n=(n!)^3b_n,\qquad Z_n=(n!)^3z_n.

\[
Z_{n+1}-P(n)Z_n+n^6Z_{n-1}=(n!)^3F_n.
\tag{0.1}

In the factorial-gauge determinant gate from the question, the consecutive-row determinant is, up to the fixed orientation sign,

\[
\boxed{((n-1)!)^6F_n.}
\tag{0.2}
\]

For a selected quotient-six digit `0<n<p`, `(n-1)!` is a `p`-unit. Thus the selected prime is seen by the gate **exactly through the residue of `F_n`**.

The screened candidates behave as follows.

   \[
   Lz=a(n)b_n
   \tag{0.7}
   \]
   does vanish at every selected digit and has an integral factorial-gauge realization when `a(n)` is integral. But it succeeds for exactly the forbidden reason: its source lies in the principal ideal generated by the original Apéry coordinate.    - torus-coordinate derivatives are exact constant-term divergences, hence give the zero sequence/source and no gate;

> **Fixed-width linear-source no-go.** Any fixed-width linear source built from the Apéry orbit, with rational-function coefficients in the index, reduces by the order-two recurrence to
> \[
> F_n=U(n)b_n+V(n)b_{n-1}.
> \]
> If it vanishes formally on the entire Apéry zero fiber `b_n=0`, then `V=0`; hence
> \[
> \boxed{F_n=U(n)b_n.}
> \]
> Thus every recurrence-formal fixed-width linear source satisfying the desired zero condition collapses to the principal source the question is trying to avoid.

In particular, the span of the three named canonical directions has no hidden escape: for rational functions `alpha,beta,gamma`,

\[
F_n=\alpha(n)R_n+8\beta(n)(2n+1)+\gamma(n)b_n
\tag{0.8}
\]

restricts on the formal zero fiber to

F_n\big|_{b_n=0}
=(2n+1)\left(
\alpha(n)\frac{5n}{3(n+1)^2}b_{n-1}+8\beta(n)
\right).
\tag{0.9}
\]

Away from the central residue, `b_{n-1}` is the free nonzero zero-fiber coordinate. Universal vanishing forces

\[
\alpha=\beta=0,

There is also a useful quotient-six strengthening: if `r=(p-1)/2` then

m=6p+r=\frac{13p-1}{2},

A genuine escape must leave the fixed-width linear Apéry-orbit category: a nonlocal Green/Lucas-Bockstein orbit, a nonlinear/bilinear Frobenius quantity, or some new arithmetic source whose vanishing uses more than the formal two-state zero fiber.

# 2. Factorial gauge and why the source is the whole gate

Write

\[
(Lx)_n=(n+1)^3x_{n+1}-P(n)x_n+n^3x_{n-1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

If `Lz=F` and

\[
Z_n=(n!)^3z_n,
\]

then multiplying (2.1) by `(n!)^3` gives

\[
\boxed{
Z_{n+1}-P(n)Z_n+n^6Z_{n-1}=(n!)^3F_n.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For the homogeneous Apéry column

\[
B_n=(n!)^3b_n,
\]

this is the monic integral recurrence

\[
B_{n+1}-P(n)B_n+n^6B_{n-1}=0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The pushed factorial-gauge fundamental matrix has determinant

\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
B_n&D_n\\
B_{n-1}&D_{n-1}
\end{pmatrix}
=-((n-1)!)^6.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The third-column expansion therefore converts its recurrence defect into the gate (0.2). For a quotient-six digit `r<p`, the factorial term is a `p`-unit. Hence one cannot manufacture selectedness from factorial content: after primitive normalization, the question is simply whether

\[
F_r=0\pmod p.
\tag{2.5}
\]

This reduction makes the source screen exact.

# 3. General nearest-neighbor zero-fiber lemma

Before testing named candidates, there is a universal two-state calculation.

Let

\[
F_n=A(n)b_{n+1}+B(n)b_n+C(n)b_{n-1},
\qquad A,B,C\in\mathbf Q(n).
\tag{3.1}
\]

On the formal zero fiber `b_n=0`, the recurrence gives

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=-n^3b_{n-1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
F_n\big|_{b_n=0}
=\left(C(n)-A(n)\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}\right)b_{n-1}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus `F` vanishes identically on the zero fiber if and only if

\[
C(n)(n+1)^3=A(n)n^3.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Write

\[
A(n)=(n+1)^3H(n),
\qquad C(n)=n^3H(n).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_n
&=H(n)\bigl((n+1)^3b_{n+1}+n^3b_{n-1}\bigr)+B(n)b_n\\
&=\boxed{(H(n)P(n)+B(n))b_n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
\]

So a nearest-neighbor linear source which vanishes for the formal reason `b_n=0` is necessarily principal.

## 3.1 Fixed-width extension

The same statement holds for every fixed-width linear source

\[
F_n=\sum_{j=-h}^{h}A_j(n)b_{n+j},
\qquad h=O(1).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Repeated use of the order-two recurrence reduces (3.7), over `Q(n)`, to

\[
F_n=U_h(n)b_n+V_h(n)b_{n-1}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

On the formal zero fiber,

\[
F_n=V_h(n)b_{n-1}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Since the predecessor is the free state coordinate, formal zero-fiber vanishing means `V_h=0`, hence

\[
\boxed{F_n=U_h(n)b_n.}
\tag{3.10}
\]

This is the key scoped no-go. It does **not** rule out an arithmetic identity which vanishes only on the distinguished actual Apéry zero subset for a subtle non-formal reason. It does rule out getting the desired property merely by another fixed-width recurrence/WZ linear combination.

# 4. Candidate (i): Racah curvature is central-only

Take

\[
R_n=\frac{5(n+1)b_{n+1}-13(2n+1)b_n+5nb_{n-1}}3.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Use the recurrence to eliminate `b_{n+1}`:

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Multiplying (4.1) by `3(n+1)^2` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
3(n+1)^2R_n
={}&5P(n)b_n-5n^3b_{n-1}\\
&-13(2n+1)(n+1)^2b_n
+5n(n+1)^2b_{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Now

\[
P(n)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5),
\]

and

\[
5P(n)-13(2n+1)(n+1)^2
=(2n+1)(72n^2+59n+12),
\tag{4.4}
\]

while

\[
5n\bigl((n+1)^2-n^2\bigr)=5n(2n+1).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
3(n+1)^2R_n
=(2n+1)\bigl((72n^2+59n+12)b_n+5nb_{n-1}\bigr).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Let `p>=7` and let `r` be an interior selected digit:

\[
0<r<p-1,
\qquad p\mid b_r.
\tag{4.7}
\]

No-consecutive-zero gives

\[
p\nmid b_{r-1}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Because `3(r+1)^2` is a `p`-unit, (4.6) yields

\[
\boxed{
R_r\equiv
\frac{5r(2r+1)}{3(r+1)^2}b_{r-1}\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Since `r` and `b_{r-1}` are units,

\[
\boxed{p\mid R_r\iff 2r+1\equiv0\pmod p.}
\tag{4.10}
\]

With `0<=r<p`, this means

\[
\boxed{r=(p-1)/2.}
\tag{4.11}
\]

So the Racah curvature source is not merely unproved: it is **exactly wrong on every noncentral selected zero**.

## 4.1 Strong quotient-six death certificate

For a quotient-six representation

\[
m=6p+r,
\]

condition (4.11) is equivalent to

\[
2m+1=13p.
\tag{4.12}
\]

If a second distinct prime `q` at the same `m` also had central digit `u=(q-1)/2`, then

\[
2m+1=13q.
\]

Hence `p=q`, contradiction.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Racah curvature cannot vanish at both ends of any distinct-prime q6 edge.}
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

This is an unconditional algebraic no-go; no scan is involved.

## 4.2 Height

The cleared Racah row uses coefficients

\[
5(n+1),\quad -13(2n+1),\quad5n,
\]

plus the fixed denominator `3`, which is a core unit for primes `>5`. Its primitive **coefficient** height on `n<=T` is only

\[
O(\log T).
\tag{4.14}
\]

But the source **value** is not small. With

\[
\alpha=17+12\sqrt2,
\qquad
\log b_n=n\log\alpha+O(\log n),
\]

one has at leading order

\[
R_n\sim \frac n3(5\alpha-26+5\alpha^{-1})b_n.
\]

Since `alpha+alpha^{-1}=34`,

\[
5\alpha-26+5\alpha^{-1}=144,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{R_n\sim48n\,b_n.}
\tag{4.15}
\]

Thus `log|R_n|=Theta(n)`. Even if its zero behavior were useful, inserting its numerical values as independent matrix coefficients would not be a low-height route.

# 6. Candidate (iii): spectral derivative literally works, but is principal

There is one literal positive construction.

Introduce a spectral deformation

\[
L_\lambda=L-\lambda a(n)
\tag{6.1}
\]

and a family `b^(lambda)` satisfying

\[
L_\lambda b^{(\lambda)}=0,
\qquad b^{(0)}=b.
\]

Differentiating at `lambda=0` gives a spectral derivative `z` with

\[
\boxed{Lz=a(n)b_n}
\tag{6.2}
\]

(up to the harmless sign convention in (6.1)).

If `a(n) in Z[n]` and the derivative initial data are integral after factorial gauge, then

\[
Z_n=(n!)^3z_n
\]

satisfies the monic integral recurrence

\[
\boxed{
Z_{n+1}-P(n)Z_n+n^6Z_{n-1}=a(n)B_n.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus this is a genuinely `p`-independent integral third-column construction.

At any selected digit,

\[
p\mid b_r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid a(r)b_r.
\tag{6.4}
\]

So in the **literal** sense candidate (iii) passes the requested vanishing test at both characteristics.

But it is exactly the principal-source failure:

\[
F_n\in(b_n).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The determinant gate is

\[
((r-1)!)^6a(r)b_r.
\tag{6.6}
\]

For a valuation-one selected zero and `p\nmid a(r)`,

\[
v_p\bigl(((r-1)!)^6a(r)b_r\bigr)=1.
\tag{6.7}
\]

This is the same target factor already present in `b_r`; there is no second independent Smith equation. If `p|a(r)`, the extra factor is imposed by the coefficient polynomial, not by the selected Apéry event, and must be treated as structural content rather than cross-characteristic capacity.

The problem is even clearer globally. Quotient-six Lucas gives, for `m=6p+r`,

\[
b_m\equiv b_6b_r\pmod p.
\tag{6.8}
\]

So all quotient-six target primes already divide the common scalar `b_m` (apart from fixed small-prime issues). A principal spectral source merely repackages this old common carrier. Its height is

\[
\boxed{
\log|b_m|=m\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log m),
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

which is linear, not `o(m)`.

If `deg a=d` is fixed, the primitive **coefficient** height of `a(r)` is only `O_d(log T)`, but the evaluated source moment has

\[
\log|a(r)b_r|=\Theta(r).
\tag{6.10}
\]

So candidate (iii) is a useful control: it proves that integrality of a spectral derivative is not the missing issue. The missing issue is **non-principality**.

# 7. Standard WZ / constant-term derivatives

The pushed repository gives the exact Laurent polynomial

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz}
\tag{7.1}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^n=b_n.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This lets us test several canonical infinitesimal deformations without guessing.

## 7.1 Torus-coordinate derivatives are identically zero

Let

\[
\delta=\alpha x\partial_x+\beta y\partial_y+\gamma z\partial_z.
\]

Constant term kills a logarithmic torus derivative:

\[
\operatorname{CT}(\delta H)=0
\tag{7.3}
\]

for every Laurent polynomial `H`.

An infinitesimal coordinate rescaling of `Lambda` therefore gives

\[
\frac d{dt}\Big|_{t=0}
\operatorname{CT}\Lambda(e^{\alpha t}x,e^{\beta t}y,e^{\gamma t}z)^n
=
\operatorname{CT}\delta(\Lambda^n)=0.
\tag{7.4}
\]

So the most canonical toric derivative is an exact divergence. It produces no third direction and no selected gate.

## 7.2 Scalar dilation is maximally transverse

Take

\[
\Lambda_t=e^t\Lambda.
\]

Then

\[
z_n:=\frac d{dt}\Big|_{0}\operatorname{CT}\Lambda_t^n
=n b_n.
\tag{7.5}
\]

A direct commutator calculation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
L(nb)_n
&=(n+1)^3(n+1)b_{n+1}-nP(n)b_n+n^3(n-1)b_{n-1}\\
&=(n+1)^3b_{n+1}-n^3b_{n-1}\\
&=P(n)b_n-2n^3b_{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.6}
\]

At a selected interior digit,

\[
\boxed{
L(nb)_r\equiv-2r^3b_{r-1}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Thus the simplest constant-term spectral scaling fails as strongly as possible.

## 7.3 Additive constant deformation also fails

Take

\[
\Lambda_t=\Lambda+t.
\]

Then

\[
z_n=n b_{n-1}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

For `n>=2`, at a zero `b_n=0`, the recurrence at `n-1` gives

\[
(n-1)^3b_{n-2}=P(n-1)b_{n-1}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
L(nb_{n-1})_n\big|_{b_n=0}
&=\left(
-nP(n)+\frac{n^3P(n-1)}{(n-1)^2}
\right)b_{n-1}\\
&=\frac{n}{(n-1)^2}
\Bigl(n^2P(n-1)-(n-1)^2P(n)\Bigr)b_{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.10}
\]

Using

\[
P(n-1)=34n^3-51n^2+27n-5,
\]

the bracket is

\[
\boxed{
-34n^4+68n^3-7n^2-17n-5.
}
\tag{7.11}
\]

Therefore this source can vanish at only a fixed quartic exceptional residue set modulo each prime; it is not forced to vanish on Apéry zeros.

## 7.4 The genuine index derivative is also transverse

Differentiating the continuously shifted recurrence in the index gives the standard inhomogeneous index jet `g` with source

\[
F_n^{\rm idx}
=P'(n)b_n-3n^2b_{n-1}-3(n+1)^2b_{n+1}.
\tag{7.12}
\]

At `b_n=0`, use

\[
b_{n+1}=-\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}b_{n-1}
\]

to obtain

\[
\boxed{
F_n^{\rm idx}\big|_{b_n=0}
=-\frac{3n^2}{n+1}b_{n-1}.
}
\tag{7.13}
\]

This is a unit at every interior selected digit for primes `>3`. Thus the index/Witt derivative is not the desired third-column source either.

## 7.5 The classical Apéry WZ harmonic numerator is homogeneous

The standard Apéry numerator `a_n` satisfies the **same homogeneous recurrence** as `b_n`:

\[
La=0.
\tag{7.14}
\]

Its Casoratian is

\[
a_n b_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}.
\tag{7.15}
\]

At a selected digit `p|b_r`, no-consecutive-zero gives `b_{r-1}` a unit, so

\[
\boxed{a_r\not\equiv0\pmod p.}
\tag{7.16}
\]

Therefore the canonical harmonic WZ companion is **anti-selective**: its two-column determinant is a unit at a selected zero. As a third-column source it has `F=0`, so the three-column source determinant is identically zero for all indices and carries no selected information.

This exhausts the most standard WZ/constant-term infinitesimal directions available directly from the pushed formulas.

# 8. Hasse / square-root extensions do not give a formal source condition

The current repository contains an exact Hasse no-go (`drops/Q7190-4d44ec84.md`). In odd characteristic, write

\[
H(t)=\Delta(t)^\epsilon B(t)^2,
\qquad
\Delta(t)=t^2-34t+1,
\]

with reciprocal

\[
B(t)=1+x_1t+\cdots+x_{d-1}t^{d-1}+t^d.
\]

For every first-half coefficient `i<d/2`,

\[
H_i=2x_i+Q_i(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1}).
\tag{8.1}
\]

The Jacobian of the first `m` coefficient map is triangular with determinant

\[
2^m\ne0.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Hence the map is a polynomial automorphism: the first-half Hasse coefficients may be prescribed arbitrarily in the abstract reciprocal-square family.

Consequently no identity derived **only** from Hasse squareness, reciprocity, and the condition `H_i=0` can force an independent derivative/source coefficient to vanish. Such a theorem would impose a relation on coordinates which (8.1) shows are free.

This does not rule out extra arithmetic of the **actual** Apéry Hasse point. It says that any successful Hasse source must import new Frobenius/recurrence information beyond the standard square/reciprocity package.

# 9. Why the central factor cannot be exploited cross-characteristically

Both the Racah and affine Green sources contain the factor

\[
2n+1.
\]

It is tempting to hope that the two endpoint digits in a q6 collision might both land on this factor. They cannot.

Let

\[
m=6p+r=6q+u,
\qquad p<q,
\qquad 0\le r<p,\quad0\le u<q.
\tag{9.1}
\]

If

\[
2r+1=p,
\]

then

\[
2m+1=12p+2r+1=13p.
\tag{9.2}
\]

If simultaneously

\[
2u+1=q,
\]

then

\[
2m+1=13q.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus `p=q`. Therefore every genuine distinct-prime q6 edge has at least one endpoint at which **both** the Racah and affine Green sources are units (assuming the endpoint itself is selected and interior).

This kills the only common non-principal zero factor visible in candidates (i) and (ii).
