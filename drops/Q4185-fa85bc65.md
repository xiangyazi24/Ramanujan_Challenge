# 12. The more natural viable object: the joint quotient-row incidence matrix

The scalar obstruction is why Q4182's independent continuation is better phrased as a matrix problem.

For a lower prime `p`, define

\[

\[

with the exact raw sign windows/tie-break inserted.

For one `Y`-cell `I`, form the growing matrix

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_{\sigma,I}

\tag{12.1}

Then

\[
\nu_{u,I}=\sum_{p\in I}H_\sigma(u,p),

and the off-diagonal row energy is exactly

\sum_u\nu_{u,I}(\nu_{u,I}-1).
\]

Thus OMCD is a banded `L^2`/codegree theorem for the **joint** two-slope incidence matrix. It keeps both lower characteristics separate instead of forcing them into a scalar resultant.

\[

\ll_J

This is precisely Q4182's one-scale OMCD theorem. It can exploit cancellation/orthogonality **before** the pair incidences are converted to absolute scalar heights.

lies exactly in the tangent direction of one fixed marked state. The upper mark therefore does not by itself oscillate along the cell. A proof must use genuine two-characteristic arithmetic decorrelation, not merely the existence of the mark.

# Bottom line

The small cell gap **does create short transfer polynomials**, but it does not create a short arithmetic carrier. The only degree-one two-slope eliminant is the universal fixed-state affine equation. After that equation, q-primary content, fixed Lucas factors, and every restart/denominator factor are saturated, the first occurrence-preserving scalar is the endpoint bilinear carrier `(BIL)`, whose deep-cell logarithmic height remains `Theta(T)`.

The reason is exact: `p` and `p'` are different characteristics. A short `6h`/`ch` continuant knows how to transfer within one characteristic, but the opposite endpoint zero belongs to the other characteristic. The moment one restores occurrence preservation, identity `(RST-sat)` puts the full endpoint Apéry values back, and bounded exteriorization collapses only to the saturated restart factor `(DES)`.

Therefore the desired

\[
\mathcal E^{\rm cell}_{\sigma,\mathrm{off}}
\ll_J
Y M_\sigma T^{-\rho}
\]

remains a genuinely new two-characteristic dispersion theorem. The first scalar class still worth attacking is the **linear-size growing-prefix marked syzygy module** left open by Q4166, but for OMCD the cleaner target is the **joint quotient-row incidence matrix** `(12.1)`: it is the first object that grows with the cell while preserving every marked occurrence and still leaves room for cancellation before scalar height saturation.
