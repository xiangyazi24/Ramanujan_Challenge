# Fixed-row Apéry gcd carrier audit

## Verdict

The fixed-row reduction is correct as a congruence selector, but the proposed conclusion that it yields a small carrier is not currently proved. The natural recurrence/Smith analysis gives a precise saturation obstruction: recurrence-generated combinations can recover the forced endpoint content, but do not automatically produce a new small Bezout combination unless one proves an additional primitive-content theorem for the Apéry state.

## 1. Exact Lucas/Kummer selector

Let

\[
A_i=B_i\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i},\qquad 0\le i\le K.
\]

For a prime

\[
n-K\le p\le n,
\]

write

\[
u=n-p.
\]

Since \(K=\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), one has \(0\le u\le K<p\).

For \(i\ne u\), Lucas applied to \(\binom ni\) gives a zero digit carry because

\[
n=p+u
\]

has base-p digits \((1,u)\), while \(i\le K<p\) has digits \((0,i)\). Thus

\[
p\mid \binom ni
\]

unless \(i=u\).

At \(i=u\), Lucas gives

\[
\binom{p+u}{u}\equiv1\pmod p.
\]

The second binomial is

\[
\binom{p-1}{K-u},
\]

and since \(K-u<p\), Lucas gives

\[
\binom{p-1}{K-u}\equiv(-1)^{K-u}\pmod p.
\]

Therefore

\[
A_u\equiv (-1)^{K-u}B_u\pmod p.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{p\mid C(n,K)\iff p\mid B_{n-p}.}
\]

The endpoint conventions are exact:

- the left endpoint \(p=n-K\) corresponds to \(u=K\);
- the right endpoint \(p=n\) is not prime except the irrelevant case \(n\) prime;
- every admissible \(u\) satisfies \(0\le u\le K<p\).

## 2. What the gcd carrier actually gives

The gcd

\[
C(n,K)=\gcd(A_0,\ldots,A_K)
\]

is a universal integer containing the top-quarter shell primes. This is a useful compression because the number of possible target primes is only about \(K\).

However, the desired estimate

\[
\log C(n,K)=o(n)
\]

requires proving that all other common content is small.

The obvious recurrence input is the Apéry recurrence

\[
(n+1)^3B_{n+1}=P(n)B_n-n^3B_{n-1}.
\]

It produces linear relations among nearby \(B_i\), hence among nearby \(A_i\), after multiplying by explicit binomial factors. These relations have coefficients of factorial/binomial size. Without cancellation they only show

\[
\log C(n,K)=O(n),
\]

not \(o(n)\).

## 3. Smith-form obstruction

Consider the map

\[
\Phi:\mathbb Z^{K+1}\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
(e_i)\mapsto A_i.
\]

The gcd is the first Smith invariant of this one-row matrix.

Any explicit Bezout identity

\[
\sum_i r_iA_i=C
\]

requires coefficients \(r_i\) whose denominators cancel every local prime obstruction.

At a shell prime \(p=n-u\), all rows except the singular row are automatically zero modulo \(p\), while the \(u\)-row is the unique surviving coordinate:

\[
(A_0,\ldots,A_K)\equiv
(0,\ldots,(-1)^{K-u}B_u,\ldots,0)\pmod p.
\]

Thus the local Smith form has one distinguished coordinate and no forced cross-index relation. The prime appears because one coordinate vanishes, not because the entire vector has a hidden small relation.

This is the same endpoint-ideal saturation phenomenon:

\[
\bigcap_p(p,A_{\ne u})
\]

is already the product of the independent local conditions.

## 4. Adjacent recurrence combinations

Using the recurrence, any adjacent elimination has the form

\[
\alpha_iB_{i+1}+\beta_iB_i+\gamma_iB_{i-1}=0
\]

with polynomial coefficients in \(i\). After inserting the binomial weights, one obtains combinations

\[
R_i=\sum_j r_{ij}A_j.
\]

Their content is controlled by the same singular indices. At a prime \(p=n-u\), only the coefficient of the \(u\)-state survives modulo \(p\). Therefore a recurrence-generated relation cannot remove the endpoint prime unless the recurrence coefficients introduce a second independent vanishing condition on the same state.

No such second condition follows from the scalar recurrence alone.

## 5. Sharp residual theorem needed

A genuine improvement would require one of the following stronger statements:

1. A primitive-content theorem:

\[
\gcd(A_0,\ldots,A_K)=\text{(top-quarter shell product)}\times e^{o(n)}.
\]

2. A small Bezout certificate:

\[
\sum_i r_iA_i=G(n)
\]

with

\[
\log |r_i|,\log|G(n)|=o(n).
\]

3. A recurrence invariant giving a second independent state functional at the same singular index.

The present recurrence supplies only a rank-two state, not a second shell-prime-sensitive coordinate.

## Final conclusion

The fixed-row construction is an exact and valid reduction:

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{n-K\le p\le n,\ p\mid B_n}p
\mid C(n,K).
}
\]

But proving

\[
\log C(n,\lfloor n/4\rfloor)=o(n)
\]

is a new content problem. Adjacent recurrences, ordinary Smith forms, and recurrence-generated Bezout combinations saturate at the independent local endpoint conditions unless supplemented by a genuinely new primitive-content or cross-index correlation theorem.
