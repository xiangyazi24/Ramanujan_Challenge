# p-adic / adelic audit for the all-index Apéry GCD problem

## Verdict

I do **not** get an adelic product-formula proof of
\[
\log\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,m),b_m)=o(m)
\]
or of the equivalent moving-zero bound in the current project. I do get one new local inequality and two precise no-gain statements which, taken together, sharply delimit what a Frobenius/regulator route would have to add.

The category correction in the historical `CRON_FRESH_EYES_pointwise.md`, Appendix AP.1, is exactly right and is essential:

* the Apéry numbers `b_n` are controlled by the **pure order-3 MUM Picard–Fuchs/K3 module**;
* the rational companion `a_n` is **not** a second MUM/logarithmic solution of that order-3 equation;
* if `A(t)=sum a_n t^n`, then the Apéry operator satisfies `L A = 6t`; after normalizing `C=A/6`, `L C=t`;
* therefore the natural homogeneous rank-4 differential module obtained by adjoining the source has local exponents `0,0,0,1`, not four zero exponents. It is a mixed extension / Green-function / prospective normal-function object, not the order-4 MUM object to which the familiar `alpha_3 ~ zeta_p(3)` formulas apply.

On the **pure** side, Beukers--Vlasenko/Dwork-crystal theorems propagate Frobenius information along `p`-power dilations and control unit-root/Hasse--Witt structures, but a bad event `p | b_r` with `0<r<p` is only the vanishing of one coefficient inside an otherwise ordinary period object. It does not force a Frobenius slope jump or a small unit root.

On the **mixed** side there is a genuine extra local congruence in the hard top window. If `p>=7`, `n/2<p<n`, `r=n-p`, and `t=v_p(b_n)>=1`, then
\[
\boxed{
 v_p\!\left(5p^3a_n-6b_n\right)\ge \min(t+1,3).
}
\]
For `t=1,2` this is a true one-power amplification of the bad prime. But the coefficient `p^3` is place-dependent. A single rational scalar that produces this cancellation simultaneously at many bad top-window primes must itself contain those primes to the third power; its logarithmic height pays at least three times the very prime mass one is trying to bound. Hence the ordinary product formula gives no saving.

Finally, at the residue coordinate `r<p` the obstruction is even stronger: if `p|b_r`, then
\[
\boxed{v_p(a_r)=v_p(b_{r-1})=0.}
\]
Thus every integral linear form `a_r-lambda b_r`, with `lambda in Z_p`, is a p-unit. So a proposed p-adic normal-function linear form is **not small at the coefficient where the zero occurs**, unless an additional place-dependent Tate/Frobenius normalization is inserted.

This leaves one possible route open, but it is much narrower than “use p-adic zeta values”: construct the actual rank-4 Apéry mixed F-isocrystal/normal function, prove that the discrete `a_n` is its integral horizontal section in the required normalization, and prove an **adelic height theorem** whose local term at `p` detects `p|b_r` without paying the same prime in the coefficient. None of the primary sources checked supplies that comparison or height theorem.

# 2. Correct differential-module category

Let `theta=t d/dt`. The standard Apéry differential operator is
\[
L=\theta^3
-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\]
For
\[
B(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n,
\]
the recurrence gives
\[
LB=0.
\]
For
\[
A(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nt^n,
\]
the same recurrence holds for coefficients beyond the initial source, while the `t^1` coefficient is `6`; hence
\[
\boxed{LA=6t.}
\]
Equivalently, for `C=A/6`,
\[
\boxed{LC=t.}
\]

So `A` and `C` are not in the three-dimensional homogeneous solution space of `L`. A convenient homogeneous closure is
\[
(\theta-1)L\,C=0,
\]
because `(theta-1)t=0`. Its indicial polynomial at `t=0` is
\[
(\rho-1)\rho^3.
\]
Thus the four local exponents are
\[
\boxed{0,0,0,1,}
\]
not `0,0,0,0`. This rank-4 closure is therefore **not MUM of order 4**.

A first-order realization can be made on the state
\[
(C,\theta C,\theta^2C,t),
\]
where the last coordinate satisfies `theta t=t`. Its homogeneous rank-3 part is the Apéry K3 Picard--Fuchs module and the source coordinate is rank one. Calling the resulting object a mixed extension is algebraically justified. Calling it specifically the geometric normal function or syntomic regulator attached to an explicit cycle is still a **program**, not a theorem currently proved in this repository.

This distinction immediately blocks a common shortcut: one cannot take a formula for the fourth MUM Frobenius constant `alpha_3`, identify it with `zeta_p(3)`, and declare it to control `a_n`.

# 3. What Beukers--Vlasenko actually proves

Primary source checked: F. Beukers and M. Vlasenko, *Frobenius structure and p-adic zeta function*, arXiv:2302.09603, Advances in Mathematics 480 (2025), 110512.

## 3.1 Proposition 1.3: shape of Frobenius at a MUM point

Their Proposition 1.3 assumes an **order `n` MUM-type differential operator** which already has a p-adic Frobenius structure. In the standard MUM basis `y_0,...,y_{n-1}`, it gives constants
\[
\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}\in\mathbf Z_p
\]
such that
\[
\mathcal A(y_i(t^p))
=p^i\sum_{j=0}^i\alpha_jy_{i-j}(t),
\qquad i=0,\ldots,n-1,
\]
with `alpha_j=A_j(0)` and `alpha_0=1`.

For the pure Apéry operator, `n=3`. Therefore this proposition has only
\[
\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2.
\]
There is **no `alpha_3` slot** in the pure order-3 module.

The same paper explicitly motivates `alpha_3 ~ zeta_p(3)` from **order-4 Calabi--Yau MUM operators**. That is a different category from the homogeneous closure `(theta-1)L` above, whose exponent `1` prevents it from being order-4 MUM.

## 3.2 Theorems 1.4 and 1.5 are special families, not a coefficient theorem

Theorem 1.4 treats their simplicial family, with `p>n+1`, and expresses the MUM Frobenius constants through Morita's p-adic Gamma function. Theorem 1.5 treats the hyperoctahedral family, assumes the constructed order-`n` MUM operator is irreducible over `Q(t)[theta]`, and takes `p>n`. For `n=4` it gives `alpha_3=-zeta_p(3)/3` in that family.

Neither theorem says that a Taylor coefficient `b_m` of another Picard--Fuchs period has valuation controlled by `zeta_p(3)`, and neither treats the inhomogeneous Apéry companion `LC=t`.

**Conclusion:** the Beukers--Vlasenko 2025 result gives no legal implication
\[
p\mid b_m\Longrightarrow v_p(\text{a p-adic zeta/Frobenius constant})>0.
\]
Any such implication would require an additional coefficient-to-Frobenius comparison theorem.

# 4. Dwork crystals: exact applicability and the moving-index mismatch

## 4.1 Dwork Crystals I, Theorem 5.3

Primary source checked: Beukers--Vlasenko, *Dwork Crystals I*, IMRN 2021, 8807--8844, DOI `10.1093/imrn/rnaa119`.

Theorem 5.3 assumes:

1. an open subset `mu` of the Newton polytope in their sense;
2. a p-adically complete base ring `R`;
3. invertibility of the Hasse--Witt matrix `beta_p(mu)` in `R`.

Then all `beta_{p^s}(mu)` are invertible, and the Cartier matrix `Lambda_sigma` satisfies, for all `m,s>=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\beta_{mp^s}(\mu)
\equiv
\Lambda_\sigma\,\beta_{mp^{s-1}}^\sigma(\mu)
\pmod{p^s}.
}
\]
For `m=1` this gives the usual p-adic limit formula for the Cartier/Frobenius matrix.

## 4.2 Dwork Crystals I, Theorem 5.7

Under the same p-adic completeness and Hasse--Witt invertibility assumptions, Theorem 5.7 controls coefficient vectors under **p-power dilation**:
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf a_{p^s\mathbf k}
\equiv
\Lambda_\sigma\mathbf a_{p^{s-1}\mathbf k}^\sigma
\pmod{p^s}.
}
\]
It also gives the corresponding connection congruence.

This is powerful vertically in the exponent, but the hard event in our problem is
\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad 0<r<p.
\]
There is no p-adic descent `r -> r/p`; `r` is already in the first digit. The theorem therefore does not amplify the moving zero.

## 4.3 A concrete pure-crystal mismatch

The repository has a fixed Laurent-polynomial realization
\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz}
\]
with
\[
\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^m=b_m.
\]
For `f=1-t\Lambda`, the holomorphic-period Hasse--Witt entry has, modulo `p`, the form
\[
\operatorname{CT}(1-t\Lambda)^{p-1}
\equiv
\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j\pmod p,
\]
because `(-1)^j binom(p-1,j) congruent 1 mod p`.

Its constant coefficient is `b_0=1`. Hence this Hasse--Witt power series is a unit at the MUM point regardless of whether one particular interior coefficient `b_r` vanishes modulo `p`.

So
\[
p\mid b_r
\]
is **not** by itself a non-ordinarity statement. It is a coefficient/jet vanishing inside a period object whose Hasse--Witt leading term remains a unit. A slope or unit-root product formula cannot be triggered merely by the coefficient zero.

## 4.4 Dwork Crystals III does not supply the missing amplification

Primary source checked: Beukers--Vlasenko, *Dwork Crystals III: From Excellent Frobenius Lifts Towards Supercongruences*, IMRN 2023, 20433--20483, DOI `10.1093/imrn/rnad101`.

Theorem 7.3 assumes a **completely symmetric** Laurent polynomial `g`, an odd prime avoiding the explicit symmetry/lattice/vertex denominators, `f=1-tg`, and invertibility of both first and second Hasse--Witt determinants. It constructs the unique excellent Frobenius lift for which
\[
\mathscr C_p(1/f)
\equiv
\frac{F(t)}{F(t^\sigma)}\frac1{f^\sigma}
\pmod{p^2\mathscr F_2^\sigma}.
\]

The authors then explicitly state that although coefficient supercongruences can be derived, they were **unable** to prove the desired `p^{2s}` strengthening of the truncation-ratio congruence; that is formulated as Conjecture 7.5 in their special families.

Thus even the excellent-lift machinery does not presently give a theorem of the form needed here for arbitrary moving indices `r<p`, let alone a cross-prime product inequality.

# 5. First rigorous obstruction: a bad residue makes the companion a p-unit

This is the cleanest local no-gain statement.

## Lemma 5.1 — residue-unit obstruction

Let `p>=5` be prime and `1<=r<=p-2`. If
\[
p\mid b_r,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{v_p(a_r)=0,\qquad v_p(b_{r-1})=0.}
\]
Consequently, for every `lambda in Z_p`,
\[
\boxed{v_p(a_r-\lambda b_r)=0.}
\]

### Proof

Because `r<p`, the known denominator bound for `a_j` shows that `a_r,a_{r-1}` are p-integral. The no-two-consecutive-zeros lemma in the current manuscript gives
\[
p\nmid b_{r-1}.
\]
The Wronskian is
\[
a_rb_{r-1}-a_{r-1}b_r=\frac6{r^3}.
\]
Since `p>=5` and `r<p`, the right side is a p-unit. The second term on the left belongs to `p Z_p`; hence `a_rb_{r-1}` is a p-unit. Since both factors are p-integral, both are units. Finally `lambda b_r in pZ_p`, whereas `a_r` is a unit, so `a_r-lambda b_r` is a unit. QED.

### Meaning

At the exact coefficient where the pure period vanishes mod `p`, the inhomogeneous companion is **as far as possible from p-adically small**. Thus an integral local linear form in `(a_r,b_r)` cannot supply an extra p-factor.

This is already enough to reject any argument of the shape

> `p|b_r`; therefore the p-adic normal-function value `a_r/b_r` or `a_r-lambda_p b_r` is small.

In fact `a_r/b_r` has negative valuation.

# 6. There is nevertheless a genuine +1 at the fixed top-window index

The residue-unit obstruction does not mean the mixed extension is completely inert. The `p^3` denominator normalization at the **fixed index** `n=p+r` creates one genuine extra congruence.

## Lemma 6.1 — mixed top-window amplification

Let `p>=7`, `n/2<p<n`, and put `r=n-p`, so `1<=r<=p-2`. Suppose
\[
t=v_p(b_n)\ge1.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(5p^3a_n-6b_n)\ge\min(t+1,3).
}
\]
If `t<=2`, moreover
\[
\boxed{v_p(a_n)=t-3.}
\]

### Proof

Put
\[
X=p^3a_n,\qquad Y=p^3a_{n-1}.
\]
The proved pair block law with high digit `q=1` gives
\[
(X,b_n)\equiv b_r(6,5)\pmod p,
\]
and at `n-1=p+(r-1)`,
\[
(Y,b_{n-1})\equiv b_{r-1}(6,5)\pmod p.
\]
Because `p|b_n`, Gessel's law gives `p|b_r`; no consecutive zeros gives `p\nmid b_{r-1}`. Hence `b_{n-1}` is a p-unit and
\[
Y-\frac65 b_{n-1}\in p\mathbf Z_p.
\]

Multiply the Wronskian at index `n` by `p^3`:
\[
Xb_{n-1}-Yb_n=\frac{6p^3}{n^3}.
\]
Since `p\nmid n`, the right side has valuation `3`. Rearranging,
\[
\left(X-\frac65b_n\right)b_{n-1}
=
\left(Y-\frac65b_{n-1}\right)b_n
+\frac{6p^3}{n^3}.
\]
The first term on the right has valuation at least `t+1`; the second has valuation `3`; and `b_{n-1}` is a unit. Thus
\[
v_p\left(X-\frac65b_n\right)\ge\min(t+1,3),
\]
which is the stated inequality after multiplying by `5`.

If `t<=2`, in
\[
Xb_{n-1}=Yb_n+6p^3/n^3,
\]
the first right-hand term has valuation exactly `t` because `Y` is a unit, while the second has valuation `3`. Their valuations differ, so there is no cancellation; hence `v_p(X)=t`, i.e. `v_p(a_n)=t-3`. QED.

### What is new here

The exact valuation `v_p(a_n)=t-3` for `t<=2` is already in the current manuscript. The displayed strengthened linear-form inequality
\[
v_p(5p^3a_n-6b_n)\ge\min(t+1,3)
\]
is the useful extra formulation from this audit: it isolates the **one additional p** supplied by the mixed companion.

It is not yet a global bound, because the normalizing coefficient is `p^3` and therefore changes with the place.

# 7. Second rigorous obstruction: a fixed scalar pays for every amplified prime

The following quantifies why the preceding +1 does not automatically become an adelic product-formula gain.

## Proposition 7.1 — coefficient-height no-gain

Fix `n`. Let `S` be a set of primes `p` in `(n/2,n)` such that
\[
t_p=v_p(b_n)\in\{1,2\}.
\]
Let `mu in Q^*` be a p-unit for every `p in S` (for example `mu=6/5`). Let `c in Q^*` be a **single scalar independent of p**. Suppose that for every `p in S`,
\[
v_p(ca_n-\mu b_n)>t_p.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{v_p(c)=3\quad(p\in S),}
\]
and consequently, writing `c=u/v` in lowest terms,
\[
\boxed{
\log\max(|u|,|v|)\ge3\sum_{p\in S}\log p.
}
\]

### Proof

For `p in S`, Lemma 6.1 gives
\[
v_p(a_n)=t_p-3,
\qquad v_p(\mu b_n)=t_p.
\]
The two summands in `ca_n-mu b_n` have valuations
\[
v_p(c)+t_p-3,\qquad t_p.
\]
For their difference to have valuation **strictly bigger than `t_p`**, the two summands must first have equal valuation `t_p`; otherwise the ultrametric inequality gives the smaller of the two valuations. Hence `v_p(c)=3`. Therefore the reduced numerator of `c` is divisible by `prod_{p in S}p^3`, proving the height bound. QED.

### Consequence

A standard product-formula strategy based on one rational linear form cannot win. Every bad top-window prime at which the linear form gains an extra p must already be inserted to the third power into the global coefficient. The coefficient height is at least three times the target logarithmic prime mass.

The local choice `c=p^3` in Lemma 6.1 evades the hypothesis precisely because it is **not one global coefficient**; it is a different coefficient at every place. But then there is no ordinary product formula tying the local forms together.

This is a scoped no-go, not a theorem against all motivic height constructions. A genuinely global mixed motive can have local Tate normalizations without arising from one rational scalar. What is missing is a global motivic/Arakelov height whose local contribution is provably our extra p while its global height is `o(n)`. None of the cited Frobenius or syntomic theorems supplies such a height.

# 8. Why p-adic zeta/regulator constants do not themselves have an adelic product formula of the required kind

A usual product formula applies to one nonzero algebraic number (or to a global line/section with an adelic metric) viewed at all places. By contrast,
\[
\zeta_p(3),\quad \alpha_j(p),\quad \text{syntomic regulator}_p(\xi)
\]
are **p-dependent local realization data**. The number `zeta_p(3)` is not the p-adic embedding of one fixed nonzero algebraic number as `p` varies. Therefore one cannot sum its p-adic valuations and invoke the elementary algebraic product formula.

A global motivic extension can of course possess compatible realizations at all primes. But compatibility of realizations is not the same thing as an inequality
\[
\sum_{p\in S}v_p(\text{local extension coordinate})\log p
\le h_{\infty}(\text{one small global section}).
\]
Such an inequality is precisely the missing global-height input.

The residue-unit lemma makes the required comparison especially stringent: the most obvious discrete local coordinate `a_r` has valuation zero at every bad residue. Thus even after constructing a normal function, one still must prove that the **correct regulator coordinate is not simply `a_r` modulo an integral pure-period correction**.

# 9. Asakura and syntomic-regulator work: what it does and does not give

## 9.1 Asakura 2023

Primary source checked: M. Asakura, *New p-adic hypergeometric functions and syntomic regulators*, Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux 35 (2023), 393--451, DOI `10.5802/jtnb.1250`, arXiv:1811.03770.

Its first main theorem (Theorem 1.1, restated as Theorem 3.3 in the paper) concerns logarithmic-type p-adic hypergeometric functions and proves Dwork-like truncation congruences under explicit hypergeometric parameter and Frobenius-lift hypotheses. Its second main result (Theorem 1.2 / Corollary 4.10 in the published paper) identifies **special values at a geometric fiber** of those logarithmic-type functions with syntomic regulator pairings for explicit symbols on hypergeometric curves, under the paper's hypotheses such as `p` avoiding the parameter denominators and an ordinary/unit-root condition at the chosen fiber.

This is excellent methodological evidence that an inhomogeneous/logarithmic object can be a syntomic regulator. It is **not** a theorem that the Taylor coefficient `a_m` of the Apéry Green solution is such a regulator, nor that `p|b_m` forces extra valuation of that regulator.

## 9.2 Asakura--Hagihara

Primary source checked: M. Asakura and K. Hagihara, *Frobenius structure on hypergeometric equations, p-adic polygamma values and p-adic L-values*, arXiv:2307.08940 (later Tunisian J. Math.).

Their Theorem 1.2 (cf. Theorem 5.5) assumes a projective smooth family over a number field with an extension at `z=0` whose central fiber is a relative simple normal-crossing divisor. It further assumes a de Rham submodule `M` decomposes as a finite direct sum of hypergeometric modules `H_{P(a;1)}` with rational parameters `0<a_i<1`, denominators prime to `p`. Under the `z -> z^p` Frobenius, the log-crystalline Frobenius matrix is described in an algebra generated by algebraic numbers, logarithmic values, and specified p-adic L-values.

Again this describes Frobenius on a **specific hypergeometric/log-crystalline submodule**. The Apéry inhomogeneous extension `LC=t` is not supplied as one of these direct-sum hypergeometric modules, and the theorem contains no coefficient comparison `p|b_m => ...`.

## 9.3 Asakura--Miyatani

Primary source checked: M. Asakura and K. Miyatani, *Milnor K-theory, F-isocrystals and syntomic regulators*, arXiv:2007.14255, J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 23 (2024), 1357--1415.

Their Theorem 2.23 constructs, under the paper's geometric hypotheses on a smooth affine base and a smooth family with a good compactification/relative normal-crossing boundary and the required de Rham conditions, a symbol map from Milnor K-theory into **1-extensions in a category of filtered F-isocrystals**. Theorem 2.25 gives an explicit formula, and Theorem 3.7 compares the construction with the log-syntomic symbol map.

This supports AP.1's categorical language: regulator classes naturally live as extensions. But to use it here one would still have to do all of the following:

1. exhibit an actual Milnor-K/regulator class on the Apéry K3 family whose filtered F-isocrystal extension is the differential module `LC=t` in the discrete normalization used by `a_n`;
2. identify its Frobenius extension coordinate with a formula involving the coefficients `(a_m,b_m)`;
3. prove that `p|b_m` forces an additional positive local height/valuation;
4. globalize those local heights with a sublinear global height.

None of those four implications is a theorem in the cited source.

# 10. Comparison with AP.1 / the mod-p^3 invariant in the repo

The historical Appendix AP correctly observes that the first genuinely p-dependent scalar in the companion depth expansion appears later than the universal mod-p behavior, and records the experimentally/propositionally checked residue
\[
\kappa_p\equiv -\frac23B_{p-3}\pmod p
\]
in its chosen normalization.

That is interesting as a candidate **local extension coordinate**, but two cautions are mandatory.

First, Q6509 itself correctly says that the exact `Z_p` identity identifying this scalar with a geometric mixed-Frobenius entry has **not** been constructed. Only a residue-class/Kummer-branch comparison was established there.

Second, even if one proved
\[
\kappa_p = c\,\zeta_p(3)
\]
in a fixed p-adic normalization, this would still be a family of local numbers indexed by `p`. It would not by itself yield a product formula for the set of primes satisfying `p|b_{n mod p}`. One also needs an implication linking that moving coefficient zero to the valuation of the extension coordinate.

The residue-unit lemma is a useful hostile test for any proposed formula: if the proposed extension coordinate reduces to `a_r-lambda_p b_r` with `lambda_p in Z_p`, it is a unit at every bad residue and therefore cannot amplify the zero.

# 11. Product-formula ledger

The possible routes can now be classified cleanly.

### Pure Frobenius / unit-root route

Local datum: slopes, unit-root eigenvalues, Hasse--Witt matrix.

Bad event: one coefficient `b_r` vanishes mod `p`.

Status: **mismatch**. Dwork Crystals I, Theorems 5.3 and 5.7, require/propagate Hasse--Witt invertibility and p-dilated indices; they do not turn a first-digit coefficient zero into a slope jump. The holomorphic Hasse--Witt entry still begins with `1`.

### Pure MUM p-adic-zeta route

Local datum: MUM Frobenius constants `alpha_j`.

Status: **wrong category for the companion**. Beukers--Vlasenko Proposition 1.3 on the order-3 Apéry pure module has no `alpha_3`; the rank-4 closure of `a` is not MUM.

### Mixed normal-function / syntomic route

Local datum: off-diagonal Frobenius extension coordinate.

Status: **correct category, missing comparison theorem**. Asakura-type work shows how such coordinates can arise from regulators in explicit hypergeometric geometry, but no source identifies the Apéry discrete companion with such a regulator in the normalization required here.

### Elementary mixed linear form

Local datum: `5p^3 a_n-6b_n`.

Status: **one real extra p**, Lemma 6.1.

Globalization: **fails for a standard product formula** because `p^3` is place-dependent; a single scalar achieving the same cancellation pays height at least `3 sum log p`, Proposition 7.1.

# 12. Exact missing theorem that would reopen the route

A p-adic/adelic strategy would become genuinely new if one could prove the following sort of statement.

> **Required mixed-height theorem.** There exists a global rank-4 extension `E` of the Apéry K3 motive by a rank-one motive, a global section `nu_n` attached functorially to the coefficient index `n`, and local integral coordinates `ell_{p,n}` such that:
>
> 1. the de Rham horizontal equation of `E` is the actual normalized inhomogeneous equation `LC=t`;
> 2. for every sufficiently large prime `p>sqrt(n)` with `p|b_{n mod p}`,
>    \[
>    v_p(\ell_{p,n})\ge1
>    \]
>    (preferably an extra factor beyond the tautological `v_p(b)`);
> 3. the local `ell_{p,n}` are realizations of **one global section**, not unrelated p-dependent scalars;
> 4. its global/archimedean height satisfies
>    \[
>    h(\nu_n)=o(n).
>    \]

Then an adelic product formula/height inequality could force
\[
\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt n\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}\log p=o(n).
\]

The current literature supplies pieces of item 1's *language* and examples of Frobenius/regulator computations, but not items 1--4 for the Apéry companion. Proposition 7.1 shows that the naive choice of a single rational linear form cannot satisfy item 4.

# 13. Final assessment

There is a useful positive local fact:
\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_n,\quad n/2<p<n
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 v_p(5p^3a_n-6b_n)\ge2
}
\]
(and more precisely `>=min(v_p(b_n)+1,3)`). This is the exact amount of local amplification exposed by the Wronskian plus the proved pair block law.

But it does **not** presently convert to a global product-formula saving. At the smaller residue index the companion is a p-unit; at the fixed index the extra cancellation requires a local `p^3` Tate normalization; and a single rational coefficient reproducing that normalization across many primes has height at least three times the bad-prime mass.

Therefore the scoped verdict is:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{pure Frobenius: no gain; naive mixed product formula: no gain;}
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\textbf{mixed normal-function Frobenius remains viable only with a new global-height comparison theorem.}
}
\]

In particular, no checked theorem of Beukers--Vlasenko, Dwork-crystal theory, or Asakura's syntomic-regulator work currently permits replacing the moving coefficient condition `p|b_m` by a p-adic zeta/regulator valuation and summing those valuations adelically. Doing so without constructing the comparison would be precisely the category error AP.1 warned against.
