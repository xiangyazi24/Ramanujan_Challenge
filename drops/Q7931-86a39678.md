# 1. The branch trait and the exact family

Let \(k\) be a finite field of characteristic \(p\ge5\), let

\[
D(a)=a^2-34a+1,
\]

and let \(\alpha\in k\) be a root. Work over the henselian discrete valuation ring

\[
R=(k[a]_{(a-\alpha)})^h,
\qquad T=\operatorname{Spec}R.
\]

The family is the strict closure in

\[
\mathscr P=(\mathbf P^1_T)^3
\]

of

\[
f(u,v,w,a)=uv(w-1)(1-uvw)-aw(u-1)(v-1)=0.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Its homogeneous equation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_a={}&U_1V_1(W_1-W_0)
 (U_0V_0W_0-U_1V_1W_1)\\
&-aU_0V_0W_0W_1(U_1-U_0)(V_1-V_0)=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Since

\[
D(0)=1,
\qquad D(1)=-32,
\]

both \(a\) and \(a-1\) are units in \(R\). Also

\[
D'(\alpha)=2\alpha-34
\]

is a unit for every \(p\ge5\), because

\[
\operatorname{disc}(D)=1152=2\cdot24^2
\]

is nonzero in those characteristics. Thus \(a-\alpha\) is a local parameter and \(D(a)\) is a unit times \(a-\alpha\).

The seven fixed sections are

\[
\begin{gathered}
(0,1,\infty),\ (0,\infty,\infty),\
(1,0,\infty),\ (\infty,0,\infty),\\
(0,\infty,0),\ (\infty,0,0),\ (1,1,1).
\end{gathered}
\tag{1.3}
\]

They are pairwise disjoint sections of \(\mathscr P/T\). Over the branch trait the first four and the last are persistent \(A_1\)'s; the middle two are persistent \(A_2\)'s.

Let \(\Sigma\) be their disjoint union, let

\[
\widehat{\mathscr P}=\operatorname{Bl}_{\Sigma}(\mathscr P),

and let \(\mathscr Y\subset\widehat{\mathscr P}\) be the strict transform of \(\mathscr X\). These are the only fixed blow-up centers used below.

# 2. The five fixed \(A_1\) sections: exact equations and one-blow-up regularity

The following equations are obtained from (1.1) after choosing the displayed local coordinates and clearing the indicated unit denominators. In every row \(r=s=t=0\) is the fixed section.

|---|---|---|---|

| \((1,0,\infty)\) | \(u=1+r,v=s,t=1/w\) | \((1+r)s(1-t)(t-(1+r)s)-atr(s-1)\) | \(-s^2+st+art\) |

The Hessian determinants of the first four quadratic forms are \(2a^2\); the determinant for the central form is

\[
2a(a-1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

All are units in \(R\), including when \(p=7\).

Here is the full blow-up check. Write one of these local equations as

\[
F=q_2(r,s,t)+q_3(r,s,t)+q_4(r,s,t)+\cdots,
\]

where each \(q_i\) is homogeneous of degree \(i\) in the ideal \((r,s,t)\), with coefficients in \(R\). Blow up that ideal. In the \(r\)-chart, \(s=rS,t=rT\), and the strict-transform equation is

\[
q_2(1,S,T)+r q_3(1,S,T)+r^2q_4(1,S,T)+\cdots=0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

On the exceptional divisor \(r=0\), a singular point would satisfy

\[
q_2(1,S,T)=\partial_Sq_2(1,S,T)
=\partial_Tq_2(1,S,T)=0.
\]

Euler's identity for the homogeneous quadratic form, valid because \(2\) is a unit, would then force its remaining projective derivative to vanish. This contradicts the nonzero Hessian determinant. The same argument applies in the \(s\)- and \(t\)-charts. Away from the exceptional divisor the blow-up is an isomorphism. Therefore one blow-up of each of the five \(A_1\) sections makes the strict transform regular along that section.

No appeal to an unnamed simultaneous-resolution theorem is hidden here: equation (2.2) and the unit determinants give every chart.

F_{5,s}=aT-R,

# 4. Properness, flatness, and completeness of the regularity check

The blow-up \(\widehat{\mathscr P}\to\mathscr P\) is projective, and \(\mathscr P/T\) is proper, so \(\widehat{\mathscr P}/T\) is proper. The strict transform \(\mathscr Y\) is closed in it, hence proper over \(T\).

Since \(R\) is a DVR and the uniformizer does not vanish at the generic point, every local ring of \(\mathscr Y\) is torsion free as an \(R\)-module. A torsion-free module over a valuation ring is flat; see Stacks Project, Lemma 15.22.10, Tag `0539`. Thus

\[
\mathscr Y\longrightarrow T
\]

is flat.

The preceding charts prove regularity over every fixed section. Outside those sections and the moving branch point, the source's Jacobian computation gives smooth fibers, so some derivative in \(u,v,w\) is a unit and the total space is regular. A regular local ring is a UFD (Stacks Project, Lemma 15.123.2, Tag `0AG0`), so each height-one prime is principal. This proves the requested properties separately:

# 9. The precise specialization and resolution sequences

Let \(\bar\eta\) and \(\bar s\) be geometric generic and special points of \(T\). Put

\[
H_\eta=H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(Y_{\bar\eta},\mathbf Q_\ell),
\qquad
H_s=H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(Y_{\bar s},\mathbf Q_\ell),

with \(\ell\ne p\). Let \(L_\eta\) and \(L_s\) be the spans of the nineteen generic and special divisor classes, respectively. The relative Cartier divisors give compatible cycle classes, and (7.1) proves

\[
\operatorname{rank}L_\eta=
\operatorname{rank}L_s=19,
\qquad
\operatorname{sp}(L_s)=L_\eta.
\tag{9.1}

## 9.1 Picard–Lefschetz specialization

In Tate-twisted form, Deligne's one-node exact sequence is

\[
0\longrightarrow H_s(1)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{sp}}
H_\eta(1)
\xrightarrow{c\mapsto\langle c,\delta\rangle}
\mathbf Q_\ell(\chi_{\rm van})
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{9.2}
\]

where \(\delta\) is the vanishing cycle, \((\delta,\delta)=-2\), and the restriction of \(\chi_{\rm van}\) to tame inertia is the nontrivial quadratic character. Untwisted, the quotient is

\[
\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)\otimes\chi_{\rm van}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The Picard–Lefschetz formula says that tame inertia acts by the reflection in \(\delta\); consequently

\[
\operatorname{sp}(H_s)=H_\eta^I=\delta^\perp.
\tag{9.4}
\]

These are SGA 7 II, Exposé XV, Théorème 3.4(i), (ii), and (iii). Costa–Elsenhans–Jahnel, *Research in Number Theory* 6 (2020), Article 27, Theorem 2.21, Corollary 2.23, and Proposition 2.24 give the same one-node formulation for K3 surfaces.

Put

\[
V=L_\eta^\perp\subset H_\eta.
\]

Because all nineteen classes extend and are inertia invariant, (9.1)–(9.4) give the canonical decomposition-group-equivariant identification

\[
\boxed{
V^I\cong L_s^\perp\cap H_s.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

The left and right sides have dimension \(2\).

## 9.2 Resolution of the singular special fiber

For the contraction \(\pi:\widetilde Y_\alpha\to Y_\alpha\),

\[
R^0\pi_*\mathbf Q_\ell=\mathbf Q_\ell,
\qquad R^1\pi_*\mathbf Q_\ell=0,
\qquad R^2\pi_*\mathbf Q_\ell=i_*\mathbf Q_\ell(-1),
\]

where \(i\) is the node. Proper base change is SGA 4 III, Exposé XII, Théorème 5.1. The Leray edge sequence gives

\[
0\longrightarrow H_s
\xrightarrow{\pi^*}
H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(\widetilde Y_{\alpha,\bar k},\mathbf Q_\ell)
\longrightarrow \mathbf Q_\ell(-1)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9.6}
\]

The last map is surjective because the class \([E_\alpha]\) restricts to a class of degree

\[
E_\alpha^2=-2\ne0
\]

on \(E_\alpha\). Hence (9.6) splits canonically after choosing the geometric exceptional class:

\[
H^2(\widetilde Y_\alpha)
=\pi^*H_s\oplus\mathbf Q_\ell[E_\alpha],
\qquad
\pi^*H_s=[E_\alpha]^\perp.
\tag{9.7}
\]

Using (8.1), equations (9.5) and (9.7) yield

\[
\boxed{
V^I\ \cong\
\left(\pi^*L_s\oplus\mathbf Q_\ell[E_\alpha]\right)^\perp
\subset H^2(\widetilde Y_\alpha).
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

The right side is exactly the rank-two branch complement used to define \(\theta_q(\alpha)\).

# 10. Frobenius conventions and vanishing of the branch defect

Let \(k=\mathbf F_q\) contain \(\alpha\). Denote by

\[
\Phi_q:x\longmapsto x^{1/q}
\]

the **geometric, point-counting Frobenius**. This is the Frobenius in the Grothendieck–Lefschetz formula

\[
\#Z(\mathbf F_q)=
\sum_i(-1)^i\operatorname{Tr}
(\Phi_q\mid H^i_{\mathrm{et}}(Z_{\bar k},\mathbf Q_\ell)).
\]

It acts on \(\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)\) by \(q\). Hence every \(k\)-rational divisor class in untwisted \(H^2\) has eigenvalue \(q\). The arithmetic Frobenius \(\varphi_q=\Phi_q^{-1}\) would act by \(q^{-1}\) on those same untwisted Tate lines and is **not** the convention used in the point-count formula of `toric_fiber_k3.tex`.

Define the invariant-cycle trace by

\[
t_q(\alpha)=
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_q\mid H_s)-19q.
\tag{10.1}
\]

The branch-resolution trace in the source is

\[
\theta_q(\alpha)=
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_q\mid H^2(\widetilde Y_\alpha))-20q.
\tag{10.2}
\]

The exceptional curve is defined over \(k\), so (9.6) gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_q\mid H^2(\widetilde Y_\alpha))
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_q\mid H_s)+q.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Substituting (10.3) into (10.2) gives

\[
\boxed{t_q(\alpha)=\theta_q(\alpha).}
\tag{10.4}
\]

Thus, with \(E_{\rm br}=t_q-\theta_q\) (or with the opposite sign convention),

\[
\boxed{E_{\rm br}(\alpha)=0.}
\]

## Why there is no quadratic-character correction

The quadratic character has not disappeared; it is located correctly. Étale-locally at the moving node, the regular smoothing has the form

\[
XY-Z^2=u(a-\alpha),
\qquad u\in R^\times.
\tag{10.5}
\]

A simultaneous resolution of the moving \(A_1\) appears after the ramified Weyl cover

\[
s^2=u(a-\alpha),
\]

by blowing up either \((X,Z-s)\) or \((X,Z+s)\). The deck involution interchanges the two choices. This is the local \(A_1\) case of Artin's construction of Brieskorn resolutions (M. Artin, *J. Algebra* 29 (1974), 330–348, DOI `10.1016/0021-8693(74)90102-1`).

Accordingly, the vanishing quotient in (9.2) carries a nontrivial quadratic tame character. But that quotient has no inertia invariants. The special exceptional class \([E_\alpha]\), by contrast, is a \(k\)-rational Tate class in the resolution. Equations (9.5) and (9.8) compare the two rank-two spaces **through \(H_s\)**; they never identify the vanishing line with the exceptional line. Therefore no quadratic sign can enter (10.4).

# 11. Both branch roots and nonsplit primes

Geometrically,

\[
\alpha_\pm=17\pm12\sqrt2,
\qquad
x_\pm=1\pm\sqrt2.
\]

The argument is symmetric in the two roots. For every \(p\ge5\):

- the roots are distinct because the discriminant is nonzero;
- neither root is \(0\) or \(1\);
- each corresponding \(x\) avoids \(0,1,2,-1\);
- each branch fiber has exactly one open node;
- the node is disjoint from the fixed ADE locus and from every member of \(\mathcal B\).

If \(2\) is a square in \(\mathbf F_p\), both roots and both nodes are \(\mathbf F_p\)-rational and (10.4) uses \(q=p\). If \(2\) is nonsquare, the roots form a conjugate pair over \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\); the same proof applies over \(q=p^2\). There is then no \(\mathbf F_p\)-rational branch parameter in the original pointwise sum.

# 12. Corrected repository verdict

The source's fiberwise statements—ADE types, attachments, determinant \(48\), open-node disjointness, and determinant \(-96\)—are consistent. What was missing was the relative bridge.

A merge-safe statement is:

> Over the henselization at a root of \(a^2-34a+1\), blow up the seven fixed singular sections of the ambient \((\mathbf P^1)^3\)-bundle and take the strict transform. The five \(A_1\) sections are resolved because their quadratic Hessians have unit determinant; the two \(A_2\) sections are resolved in one blow-up by the explicit charts (3.4)–(3.8) and (3.12)–(3.13). The resulting total threefold is regular, proper, and flat. The nineteen labeled divisors extend as flat horizontal Cartier divisors with Gram determinant \(48\), avoid the moving node, and specialize to the exact lattice used in \(\theta\). SGA 7 II, Exp. XV, Th. 3.4 and SGA 4 III, Exp. XII, Th. 5.1 then give the Frobenius-equivariant identification (9.8), hence \(E_{\rm br}(\alpha)=0\).

No correction to the family or to the definition of \(\theta\) is required. The correction needed in the repository is to insert this relative proposition and its chart proof; the current text does not itself justify the branch-defect closure.
