# Q439 — Arithmetic nature of the smallest Apéry zero

## Executive verdict

There is a precise arithmetic description of \(r_p\), but no known closed formula for it in terms of a familiar scalar invariant of \(p\).

```text
r_p is best viewed as the first vanishing Kummer–Mellin coefficient
of the Frobenius trace function of the Apéry K3 pencil.

It is not presently known to be:
- a function of one Frobenius angle;
- a function of one Dwork unit-root eigenvalue;
- a root determined by the Newton polygon;
- a p-adic analytic function of the varying prime p; or
- a period of the recurrence modulo p.
```

The main conclusions are as follows.

1. The elementary hypergeometric formula is

   \[
   b_r
   ={}_4F_3\!\left(
   \begin{matrix}-r,-r,r+1,r+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1
   \right).
   \]

   Thus \(r\) occurs in the **parameters**, while the hypergeometric argument is \(1\). The proposed value \({}_4F_3(\cdots;r/p)\) is not the Apéry number \(b_r\).

2. The current repository's toric model gives, for \(0\le r\le p-2\),

   \[
   b_r\equiv
   -\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
   \Lambda(x,y,z)^r
   \pmod p,
   \]

   for one fixed Laurent polynomial \(\Lambda\). After grouping this power sum by fibers of \(\Lambda\), \(b_r\) is a multiplicative Mellin coefficient of the rank-three K3 Frobenius trace function. Equivalently, it is the reduction of a Frobenius supertrace with a Kummer character \(\omega_p^r\).

3. Consequently, a clean intrinsic description is

   \[
   \boxed{
   r_p=\min\{r\ge1:
   \operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{sup}}
   (\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid\mathcal H_{p,r})
   \equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}\},
   }
   \]

   where \(\mathcal H_{p,r}\) is the fixed K3/toric cohomology twisted by the Kummer character \(\omega_p^r\), up to the repository's fixed sign convention. This is an exact motivic reformulation, not a closed formula.

4. The midpoint \(r=(p-1)/2\) is exceptional because \(\omega_p^r\) is the quadratic character and is self-dual. That special fixed-order character admits the Ahlgren–Ono modular evaluation

   \[
   b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod{p^2}.
   \]

   A general non-midpoint zero corresponds instead to a Kummer character whose order

   \[
   \frac{p-1}{\gcd(r,p-1)}
   \]

   usually grows with \(p\). No fixed modular form is known whose \(p\)-th coefficient determines that first vanishing character.

5. The Dwork assertions in the question require correction. The adjacent ratio \(b_p/b_{p-1}\) is not the Apéry unit root. In fact Lucas and reflection give

   \[
   b_p\equiv5\pmod p,
   \qquad
   b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p,
   \]

   so

   \[
   \boxed{b_p/b_{p-1}\equiv5\pmod p.}
   \]

   Standard Dwork unit-root formulas use ratios of **truncation polynomials at lengths \(p^s-1\)**, evaluated at a geometric parameter, not two adjacent Apéry coefficients.

6. The Newton polygon cannot locate \(r_p\). For every \(r\le(p-1)/2\), every summand

   \[
   \left(\binom rk\binom{r+k}{k}\right)^2,
   \qquad 0\le k\le r,
   \]

   is a \(p\)-adic unit. Hence \(p\mid b_r\) is cancellation among \(r+1\) terms of exactly the same slope. There is no distinguished lowest-slope term. The repository's Gross–Koblitz audit reaches the same conclusion in the all-character formulation.

7. There is a categorical distinction between two uses of the Hasse invariant. If

   \[
   H_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r\pmod p
   \]

   is the first Dwork truncation in the standard period coordinate, then

   - the nonordinary/supersingular **fiber locus** is cut out by \(H_p(t)=0\) as a polynomial in the geometric variable \(t\);
   - the set \(Z_p\) records which **coefficients** of \(H_p(t)\) vanish.

   These are different sets. Newton polygons of the fibers govern the first, not the support pattern of the coefficients.

8. Existing p-adic and motivic theory therefore explains why the midpoint is special and supplies a cohomological interpretation for every \(r\), but it does not prove the observed equidistribution of

   \[
   \alpha_p=\frac{2r_p+1}{p}.
   \]

   Such a theorem would require horizontal cancellation for a family of moving Kummer characters together with control of divisibility by the residue characteristic. Standard Sato–Tate, ordinary Deligne bounds, and Newton-polygon information do not provide that.

## 1. What can be proved directly about \(r_p\)?

### 1.1 Exact hypergeometric formulation

Using

\[
\frac{(-r)_k}{k!}=(-1)^k\binom rk,
\qquad
\frac{(r+1)_k}{k!}=\binom{r+k}{k},
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{
 b_r
 =\sum_{k=0}^r
 \frac{(-r)_k^2(r+1)_k^2}{(k!)^4}
 ={}_4F_3\!\left(
 \begin{matrix}-r,-r,r+1,r+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1
 \right).
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 r_p=\min\left\{r\ge1:
 {}_4F_3\!\left(
 \begin{matrix}-r,-r,r+1,r+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1
 \right)\equiv0\pmod p
 \right\}.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

This answers possibility (a) exactly, but it does not simplify the arithmetic: the character parameters vary with \(r\), and the truncation length is \(r+1\).

### 1.2 Two elementary but genuine restrictions

First, a fixed index can occur for only finitely many primes. If \(r_p=r\), then

\[
p\mid b_r.
\]

Thus every prime with \(r_p\le R\) divides the fixed integer

\[
\prod_{r=1}^{R}b_r.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The example \(p=73,r_p=2\) is exactly this kind of vertical phenomenon, since \(b_2=73\). It should not be interpreted as evidence that \(r_p\) is given by a smooth function of \(p\).

Second, the known exponential growth of the Apéry numbers gives a logarithmic lower bound. Let

\[
\lambda=17+12\sqrt2.
\]

There is an absolute \(C>0\) with

\[
b_r\le C\lambda^r.
\]

Since \(p\mid b_{r_p}\) and \(b_{r_p}>0\),

\[
p\le b_{r_p}\le C\lambda^{r_p},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
 r_p\ge\frac{\log p}{\log(17+12\sqrt2)}-O(1).
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Together with reflection, the presently unconditional pointwise range is essentially

\[
\frac{\log p}{\log(17+12\sqrt2)}-O(1)
\le r_p\le\frac{p-1}{2}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

No general pointwise law of substantially greater precision is known.

### 1.3 Transfer-orbit interpretation

Let \(v_n=(b_n,b_{n-1})^T\). Away from the singular step, the recurrence gives a nonautonomous transfer orbit

\[
v_{n+1}=A(n)v_n.
\]

Then \(r_p\) is the first time that the selected coordinate of the selected solution vector meets the hyperplane

\[
e_1^Tv_r=0.
\tag{1.6}
\]

This is not a spectral invariant of the transfer system. Eigenvalues, determinants, and conjugacy classes do not determine the zeros of one chosen matrix coefficient without the initial vector and eigenvector data.

The recurrence also is not ordinarily periodic modulo \(p\): its coefficient matrix has a singular step at \(n=p-1\). The first block is palindromic, and the global sequence is then governed by the Lucas digit law. Thus \(r_p\) is a minimal bad base-\(p\) digit, not the period of the recurrence.

## 2. The strongest known motivic description: a Kummer–Mellin zero

### 2.1 The fixed toric model

The current repository constructs the fixed Laurent polynomial

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz}
\tag{2.1}
\]

with

\[
\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^r=b_r.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For \(0\le r\le p-2\), the exponent box of \(\Lambda^r\) and torus orthogonality give

\[
\boxed{
 b_r\equiv
 -\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
 \Lambda(x,y,z)^r
 \pmod p.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let \(U\) be the complement of the zero and pole divisor of \(\Lambda\), and let \(\mathcal K_{\omega_p^r}\) be the Kummer sheaf attached to the Teichmüller character \(\omega_p^r\). The cyclotomic lift of (2.3) is a Frobenius supertrace on

\[
R\Gamma_c\!\left(
U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{\omega_p^r}
\right).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The geometric space and its divisor are fixed; only the Kummer character varies. Thus each individual twist has uniformly bounded geometric cohomological complexity.

### 2.2 Mellin transform of the K3 trace function

Grouping the toric points by the value \(a=\Lambda(x,y,z)\) converts (2.3) into a multiplicative Fourier transform in \(a\). In the primitive K3 normalization used by the repository, one obtains

\[
M_p(r)
:=\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
\theta_p(a)\omega_p(a)^r,
\qquad
M_p(r)\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p},
\tag{2.5}
\]

for interior exponents, where \(\theta_p(a)\) is the rank-three primitive K3 Frobenius trace of the fiber \(\Lambda=a\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 r_p=\min\{r\ge1:v_{\mathfrak p_p}(M_p(r))>0\}.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

This is the closest existing answer to “what arithmetic function is \(r_p\)?” It is the first vanishing coefficient of the multiplicative Fourier transform of a K3 trace function.

Two warnings are essential.

- The vanishing is **after reduction modulo a prime above \(p\)**. The characteristic-zero Frobenius trace need not be zero. The repository records, for example, a lifted trace equal to \(-33\) at a coefficient that is zero modulo \(11\).
- The exponent \(r\) is a character/Mellin coordinate, not the geometric fiber parameter \(a\).

Thus a single Frobenius angle at a single fiber cannot determine \(r_p\). The full trace function

\[
a\longmapsto\theta_p(a)
\]

across all fibers determines the whole coefficient row by Mellin inversion, but that is \(p-1\) pieces of information, not one scalar.

## 3. Why the midpoint has a modular form and the other zeros do not

Put

\[
m=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

The Kummer character at the midpoint is

\[
\omega_p^m=\varphi_p,
\]

the quadratic character. It is its own inverse, and its order is fixed independently of \(p\). This is precisely the extra symmetry behind the finite-field \({}_4F_3(1)\) evaluation and the Ahlgren–Ono supercongruence

\[
\boxed{
 b_m\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod{p^2},
 \qquad
 f_8=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a nonmidpoint index \(r\), let

\[
\chi_{p,r}=\omega_p^r.
\]

Reflection corresponds to inversion:

\[
r\longleftrightarrow p-1-r,
\qquad
\chi_{p,r}\longleftrightarrow\chi_{p,r}^{-1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus when \(z_p=2\), the two zeros form one inverse-character orbit. The condition is

\[
M_p(\chi_{p,r_p})\equiv
M_p(\chi_{p,r_p}^{-1})\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The order of this character is

\[
d_p=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(r_p,p-1)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Unless \(d_p\) remains in a fixed finite set, these twists do not belong to one fixed modular form or one fixed bounded-order hypergeometric motive. They form a moving family with increasing cyclotomic field and character order.

There may be additional isolated modular evaluations at special rational character exponents, such as characters of fixed order \(3,4,6\), but that does not cover the arbitrary first zero. I found no theorem in the classical Apéry/K3 literature or in the current repository expressing a general nonmidpoint \(r_p\) as the trace of a fixed modular or automorphic object.

### 3.1 Why the gap-polynomial machinery does not locate the pair

For \(z_p=2\), define the reflected gap

\[
h_p=p-1-2r_p.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then

\[
\alpha_p=\frac{2r_p+1}{p}=1-\frac{h_p}{p}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The project's even gap polynomial has the forced reflection factor

\[
2r+h+1.
\]

At the reflected pair,

\[
2r_p+h_p+1=p\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Hence the gap equation vanishes automatically. It proves that a zero has a reflected companion, but it gives no further arithmetic equation selecting the location \(r_p\). Fixed-gap Chebotarev results therefore do not imply the distribution of \(r_p\), because here \(h_p\) grows with \(p\) and the relevant factor is the forced one.

## 4. Corrections to the proposed Dwork unit-root formula

### 4.1 The ratio \(b_p/b_{p-1}\) is constant modulo \(p\)

The Apéry Lucas congruence gives

\[
b_p\equiv b_1b_0=5\pmod p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Reflection gives

\[
b_{p-1}\equiv b_0=1\pmod p.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_p}{b_{p-1}}\equiv5\pmod p.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

So this ratio cannot encode either the varying coefficient \(a_p(f_8)\) or the zero location \(r_p\).

### 4.2 What a Dwork ratio actually looks like

Let

\[
F(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n
\]

be the period solution, and write

\[
F_s(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{p^s-1}b_nt^n.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Dwork-type congruences, in the form proved for Laurent-polynomial constant terms by Mellit and Vlasenko, lead on the ordinary domain to a limit of the form

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal U_p(t)
=\lim_{s\to\infty}
\frac{F_s(\widetilde t)}
     {F_{s-1}(\widetilde t^{\,p})},
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

with the standard model-dependent normalization. Here \(t\) is a **geometric parameter**, and \(\widetilde t\) is a suitable \(p\)-adic lift. At a Teichmüller point, \(\widetilde t^p=\widetilde t\).

The first truncation

\[
H_p(t)=F_1(t)\pmod p
=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r\pmod p
\tag{4.6}
\]

is the scalar Hasse–Witt/Hasse-invariant coordinate in the usual period normalization. Formula (4.5) is defined where this Hasse invariant is nonzero.

This shows exactly what the unit-root theory knows:

- one value \(\mathcal U_p(t_0)\) is one Frobenius eigenvalue at one fiber;
- the full function \(t\mapsto\mathcal U_p(t)\), reduced modulo \(p\), contains the Hasse invariant and hence indirectly its entire coefficient vector;
- extracting \(r_p\) from the full function requires recovering all coefficients of \(H_p(t)\), which is essentially the original problem.

There is no compression from one unit-root eigenvalue to the smallest vanishing coefficient.

### 4.3 The modular-form unit root is also normalized differently

For the two-dimensional Galois representation attached to the weight-four newform \(f_8\), the good-prime local polynomial is

\[
X^2-a_p(f_8)X+p^3.
\tag{4.7}
\]

At an ordinary prime, one root is a \(p\)-adic unit and the other has valuation \(3\). The unit root satisfies

\[
\alpha_p^{\mathrm{unit}}\equiv a_p(f_8)\pmod p,
\tag{4.8}
\]

not

\[
1+a_p(f_8)/p+\cdots.
\]

Indeed, when \(p\nmid a_p(f_8)\), the quantity \(a_p(f_8)/p\) is not even \(p\)-adically integral. Moreover, a unit-root subspace does not exist at a nonordinary prime in the same sense.

The parenthetical attribution to Sato–Tate also needs care: Sato–Tate controls the real distribution of

\[
a_p(f_8)/(2p^{3/2}),
\]

not the microscopic divisibility condition \(p\mid a_p(f_8)\). Bounds for nonordinary primes require separate integral Galois/Chebotarev input.

## 5. Newton and Hodge polygons do not see coefficient locations

### 5.1 Fiber Newton polygon versus coefficient support

A K3 surface has \(22\)-dimensional \(H^2\). For the generic rank-\(19\) Apéry K3 pencil, the moving transcendental part has rank \(3\); the remaining classes are algebraic. Thus a notation such as

\[
1,\alpha_1,\alpha_2
\]

can at best describe a normalized rank-three moving factor, not the complete \(H^2\) spectrum.

For a K3 surface of finite formal Brauer height \(h\), the crystalline slopes are

\[
1-1/h\quad(h\text{ times}),
\qquad
1\quad(22-2h\text{ times}),
\qquad
1+1/h\quad(h\text{ times}).
\tag{5.1}
\]

In the ordinary case \(h=1\), these are

\[
0,\quad1\ (20\text{ times}),\quad2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For a supersingular K3 surface, all slopes are \(1\).

These polygons classify the valuations of Frobenius eigenvalues of a **fiber**. They do not record which coefficients in a period expansion vanish.

### 5.2 The coefficient-zero event is equal-slope cancellation

The failure of the slope approach is already visible in the defining sum. If

\[
0\le r\le\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

then for every \(0\le k\le r\),

\[
r+k<p.
\]

Therefore neither

\[
\binom rk
\quad\text{nor}\quad
\binom{r+k}{k}
\]

is divisible by \(p\). Every term

\[
T_{r,k}=\left(\binom rk\binom{r+k}{k}\right)^2
\tag{5.3}
\]

has valuation zero. Hence

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r
 \iff
 \sum_{k=0}^r \overline{T}_{r,k}=0
 \quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p,
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

where every \(\overline T_{r,k}\) is nonzero.

Thus the Newton polygon is completely flat on the relevant packet. It cannot distinguish a zero from a nonzero value.

The repository's Gross–Koblitz/Morita calculation gives the same diagnosis for all \(r\): after reflection, there are

\[
\rho+1,
\qquad
\rho=\min(r,p-1-r),
\]

minimum-slope unit terms, and

\[
p\mid b_r
\]

is precisely cancellation of their residues. No interior index has a unique minimum-valuation term.

Higher \(p\)-adic data could of course determine the individual units and therefore their sum. But supplying those units is essentially equivalent to evaluating \(b_r\) itself. The slopes alone provide no location theorem.

### 5.3 Spectral data versus a chosen matrix coefficient

This can be stated abstractly.

- The unit root and Newton polygon are conjugacy-invariant spectral data of Frobenius.
- The coefficient \(b_r\) is a matrix coefficient of a chosen horizontal solution in a chosen period coordinate.
- Matrix-coefficient zeros depend on eigenvectors, the initial vector, and the chosen coordinate, none of which are determined by the spectrum alone.

Therefore no formula for \(r_p\) in terms of only the eigenvalues or their slopes should be expected without an additional highly special identity.

## 6. Is \(r_p\) p-adic analytic in \(p\)?

Not in the literal sense proposed.

A \(p\)-adic analytic function is normally defined on a domain inside one fixed field \(\mathbf Q_p\). Here both the field and the finite character group change when the prime changes. There is no canonical common \(p\)-adic disc on which the map

\[
p\longmapsto r_p
\]

would live.

At a fixed prime, one can regard

\[
r\longmapsto M_p(r)
\]

as a function on the finite character group

\[
\widehat{\mathbf F_p^\times}\cong\mathbf Z/(p-1)\mathbf Z.
\]

One may try to interpolate Kummer twists on a \(p\)-adic weight space. Even if such an interpolation is constructed, three additional problems remain:

1. the exponents \(0,\ldots,p-2\) are torsion-character labels, not points approaching one fixed analytic variable as \(p\) varies;
2. the relevant condition is divisibility of a trace by the same residue characteristic \(p\);
3. selecting the **least** vanishing representative is a discontinuous order statistic.

No bounded-Weierstrass-degree interpolation theorem for these moving traces is currently available in the repository or the cited Dwork literature.

## 7. What would be required to prove equidistribution?

Let

\[
\mathcal P_2(X)=\{p\le X:z_p=2\}
\]

and, for \(p\in\mathcal P_2(X)\), put

\[
\alpha_p=\frac{2r_p+1}{p}\in(0,1).
\]

The desired theorem is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\#\{p\in\mathcal P_2(X):\alpha_p\in I\}}
     {\#\mathcal P_2(X)}
\longrightarrow |I|
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

for every interval \(I\subset[0,1]\).

### 7.1 Exact Fourier criterion

For \(z_p=2\), define the zero-set Fourier transform

\[
F_p(m)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e^{2\pi imr/p}.
\]

Since

\[
Z_p=\{r_p,p-1-r_p\},
\]

a direct calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
F_p(m)
=2e^{-\pi im/p}\cos(\pi m\alpha_p).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Therefore (7.1) is equivalent to the horizontal cosine-moment statements

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{\#\mathcal P_2(X)}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_2(X)}
 e^{\pi im/p}F_p(m)
\longrightarrow0
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

for every fixed integer \(m\ge1\).

This is a useful precise target: equidistribution of the smallest zero is a prime-aspect cancellation theorem for the Fourier transforms of the actual zero sets.

### 7.2 Why standard Sato–Tate is insufficient

Sato–Tate controls conjugacy classes or normalized complex traces of a **fixed compatible system**. Here the event is

\[
M_p(r)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}
\]

for a character \(\omega_p^r\) whose order and coefficient field vary with \(p\), followed by minimization over \(r\).

Complex equidistribution does not by itself control reduction modulo the varying residue characteristic. Divisibility by \(p\) is not a continuous condition in the Sato–Tate topology.

### 7.3 The random-motive heuristic

The motivic description nevertheless explains the data very naturally.

There are about \(p/2\) inverse-character pairs

\[
\{\chi,\chi^{-1}\}.
\]

If the reductions of the corresponding Kummer-twisted traces behaved like approximately independent uniform residues in \(\mathbf F_p\), then each pair would vanish with probability about \(1/p\). Hence

\[
\mathbb E\left[\frac{z_p}{2}\right]\approx\frac12,
\]

and the number of zero pairs would approach a Poisson law of mean \(1/2\). Conditional on exactly one zero pair, its character position would be approximately uniform, giving uniform \(\alpha_p\).

This simultaneously predicts

- mean \(z_p\approx1\);
- Poisson behavior of \(z_p/2\);
- reflection pairing; and
- equidistribution of the unique pair location.

The repository computations strongly support this model. What is missing is an integral horizontal independence theorem for the Kummer-twisted traces.

### 7.4 A sufficient new theorem

One possible theorem strong enough for (7.1) would be a character-aspect large sieve of the following qualitative form. For every fixed nonconstant test function \(\Phi\) of mean zero,

\[
\sum_{p\le X}
\sum_{1\le r<(p-1)/2}
\Phi\!\left(\frac{2r+1}{p}\right)
\mathbf1_{\{M_p(r)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}\}}
=o(\pi(X)).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Together with a second-factorial-moment estimate excluding multiple zero pairs, this would imply the desired conditional equidistribution.

Proving (7.4) appears to require all of the following:

1. a uniform integral model for the Kummer-twisted K3 cohomologies as the character order grows;
2. large geometric and arithmetic monodromy after reduction;
3. a large sieve for Frobenius in which both \(p\) and the Kummer character vary;
4. control of the mod-\(p\) trace-zero condition, not merely a complex Weil bound;
5. enough joint independence to handle the “first zero” conditioning.

No existing theorem cited in the project supplies this package.

## 8. Direct answers to the four questions

### Question 1: Is \(r_p\) a known arithmetic function of \(p\)?

No known elementary or classical arithmetic function gives \(r_p\). The exact known descriptions are:

\[
r_p=\min\{r:p\mid b_r\},
\]

\[
r_p=\min\{r:{}_4F_3(-r,-r,r+1,r+1;1,1,1;1)\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]

and, most structurally,

\[
r_p=\min\{r:M_p(\omega_p^r)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}\}.
\]

The last is a Kummer–Mellin/Frobenius interpretation. It is the best current motivic answer.

### Question 2: What determines the nonmidpoint zeros?

They are vanishing reductions of nonquadratic Kummer-twisted K3 Frobenius traces. The midpoint corresponds to the one fixed quadratic character and therefore admits the modular form \(f_8\). General nonmidpoint characters have varying, usually unbounded order. No fixed modular form is known to return their position, and the position itself is an order statistic rather than a trace.

### Question 3: Does Dwork theory express \(r_p\) through a unit root and higher data?

Not through one unit-root eigenvalue or its slopes. The full Dwork unit-root **function** contains the Hasse invariant and hence, after complete coefficient extraction, the row \((b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1})\). But that extraction is not a closed formula and offers no known compression. The proposed adjacent ratio is not the unit root and is congruent to \(5\).

### Question 4: Is there a Newton/Hodge polygon interpretation?

There is a Newton/Hodge interpretation for the ordinary or supersingular status of each geometric K3 fiber. There is no known Newton-polygon interpretation for the positions of zero coefficients in the Hasse polynomial. On the relevant half-row, every hypergeometric summand has the same slope, so the zero event is invisible to the polygon and is controlled entirely by residue cancellation.

## 9. Most useful next experiments

The following computations would distinguish the plausible motivic scenarios.

1. **Character-order census.** For every \(p\) with \(z_p=2\), record

   \[
   d_p=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(r_p,p-1)}.
   \]

   If most \(d_p\) grow with \(p\), then a finite catalog of fixed-order modular motives cannot explain the zeros.

2. **Horizontal Fourier moments.** For fixed \(1\le m\le M\), compute

   \[
   A_m(X)=
   \frac1{\#\mathcal P_2(X)}
   \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_2(X)}
   e^{\pi im/p}F_p(m).
   \]

   Equidistribution predicts \(A_m(X)\to0\).

3. **Stratify by midpoint data.** Compare the distribution of \(\alpha_p\) after conditioning on congruence classes of \(p\), the sign and size of \(a_p(f_8)\), and any ordinary-height invariant. Lack of correlation would directly refute a one-angle explanation.

4. **Separate vertical exceptions.** Remove primes dividing \(\prod_{r\le R}b_r\) for a slowly growing \(R\). This prevents examples such as \((p,r)=(73,2)\) from distorting the bulk distribution.

5. **Verify the toric Mellin normalization at scale.** For sampled primes and every zero index, compute the lifted Kummer trace and confirm that the vanishing is only modulo \(\mathfrak p_p\), not usually an exact trace zero.

6. **Test rare-event independence.** For two disjoint normalized character intervals \(I,J\subset(0,1)\), compare the count of primes having zero pairs in both intervals against the product prediction. This is the second-factorial-moment input needed before conditioning on \(z_p=2\).

## 10. Bottom line for P3.2

The arithmetic geometry supplies a compelling organizing principle:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Apéry coefficient index}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{Kummer character}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{Mellin coefficient of the K3 trace family}.
}
\]

The midpoint is modular because it is the unique nontrivial self-inverse character. A nonmidpoint zero is a mod-\(p\) vanishing of a moving higher-order Kummer moment. Its location is not determined by one Frobenius angle, one unit root, or one Newton polygon.

Thus existing Dwork/K3 theory gives enough structure to formulate the equidistribution problem precisely, but not enough to prove it. The missing theorem is horizontal and integral: equidistribution and approximate independence of the mod-\(p\) vanishing events for Kummer-twisted Frobenius traces as both the prime and the character vary.

That is closely aligned with the missing cross-prime phase-cancellation input already isolated elsewhere in P3.2.
