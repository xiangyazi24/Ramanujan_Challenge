# Verdict

There is already a fully unconditional uniform theorem in the current `main` state, stronger than the first alternative requested:

\[
\boxed{Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})=o(p).}
\]

It is the gap-polynomial theorem in `problems/3.2/proof.tex` (`lem:no-consec`, `lem:gap-poly`, `lem:nonvanish`, `prop:zp-bound`). No finite scan enters the proof. In fact, using exactly the same proved inputs and solving the resulting capacity problem sharply gives the slightly better explicit asymptotic envelope

\[
\boxed{Z(p)\le \frac32p^{2/3}+O(p^{1/3}).}
\]

The displayed proposition in `proof.tex` uses a single cutoff and states the slightly looser leading constant \(3^{4/3}/2\); the later `rem:sharp` already records that the exact degree-only extremal problem has leading constant \(3/2\).

By contrast, I do **not** get the dyadic mean target

\[
\sum_{N<p\le 2N} Z(p)=O(N/\log N)
\]

from discrete Sturm/Casoratian, finite-field polynomial abc, or the CFVZ/differential-equation singularity structure. The certified pointwise theorem only gives

\[
\sum_{N<p\le2N}Z(p)\ll \frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}.
\]

Also, despite the wording “weaker aggregate” in the question, \(O(N/\log N)\) is quantitatively **much stronger** than merely knowing \(Z(p)=o(p)\): it says the average \(Z(p)\) on primes \(p\asymp N\) is \(O(1)\).

The exact first obstruction to the three proposed upgrades is:

\[
\boxed{\text{all three control roots / return certificates / singularities, while }Z(p)\text{ counts zero coefficients.}}
\]

The Apéry recurrence bridges that mismatch once, through the gap polynomials \(N_h\), and that bridge yields the \(p^{2/3}\) theorem. None of the extra CFVZ square structure presently supplies the missing *joint-in-\(h\)* or second-factorial-moment information needed for average \(O(1)\) zeros per prime.

# 1. Exact current inputs

Write

\[
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]

The first-digit Apéry sequence satisfies

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1},
\qquad b_0=1,\quad b_1=5.
\]

For a prime \(p\),

\[
\mathcal Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\},
\qquad Z(p)=|\mathcal Z_p|.
\]

The following are proved in the current repository.

## 1.1 No adjacent zeros

`proof.tex`, `lem:no-consec` proves that for \(p\ge5\),

\[
b_r\equiv b_{r+1}\equiv0\pmod p
\]

cannot occur for \(0\le r\le p-2\). Indeed the recurrence propagates such a pair backwards to \(b_0\equiv0\), contradicting \(b_0=1\).

## 1.2 Exact reflection

`proof.tex`, `prop:reflection` proves the stronger statement that every \(p\)-integral solution of the Apéry recurrence on \(0,\ldots,p-1\) is palindromic:

\[
u_{p-1-r}\equiv u_r\pmod p.
\]

The algebraic reason is

\[
P(-1-x)=-P(x),
\]

so reversal preserves the recurrence modulo \(p\). In particular

\[
\boxed{b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p.}
\]

Thus noncentral zeros occur in reflected pairs; the midpoint \((p-1)/2\) is the only possible self-paired zero.

## 1.3 CFVZ square / square-times-quadratic factorization

In `problems/3.2/hasse_franel_descent.tex`, `prop:hasse-apery-square-roots`, set

\[
A_p(a)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_ra^r,
\qquad
\Delta(a)=a^2-34a+1,
\qquad
\varepsilon_p=\frac{1-\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)}2.
\]

The CFVZ factorization is

\[
\boxed{A_p(a)=\Delta(a)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(a)^2,}
\]

with \(B_p(0)=1\), where \(B_p\) is the appropriate truncation of one of two universal square-root series. The universal coefficient recurrences recorded there are

\[
4(r+1)^2c_{r+1}
=2(68r^2+34r+5)c_r-(2r-1)^2c_{r-1},
\]

and

\[
4(r+1)^2d_{r+1}
=2(68r^2+102r+39)d_r-(2r+1)^2d_{r-1}.
\]

The associated second-order operators are

\[
L_+=4\vartheta^2
-2a(68\vartheta^2+34\vartheta+5)
+a^2(2\vartheta+1)^2,
\]

\[
L_-=4\vartheta^2
-2a(68\vartheta^2+102\vartheta+39)
+a^2(2\vartheta+3)^2.
\]

Their symmetric square gives the Apéry operator

\[
L_A=
\vartheta^3
-a(2\vartheta+1)(17\vartheta^2+17\vartheta+5)
+a^2(\vartheta+1)^3.
\]

The same file's `prop:hasse-franel-pullback` gives

\[
K_p(x)^2=(1+x)^{p-1}A_p(\phi(x))
=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_m\Psi_{p,m}(x).
\]

That proposition explicitly warns that the \(b_m\) on the right are coordinates in the cover-adapted \(\Psi\)-basis, not monomial coefficients of \(K_p^2\).

This distinction is crucial below.

# 2. Unconditional theorem: the gap-polynomial proof of \(Z(p)=o(p)\)

The relevant “discrete Sturm/Casoratian” object is already present in `proof.tex`.

For \(h\ge1\), define the continuant/gap polynomial by

\[
N_1(x)=1,
\qquad
N_2(x)=P(x+1),
\]

\[
\boxed{
N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x).
}
\]

The current proof establishes

\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1).
\]

If \(b_m\equiv0\pmod p\) and \(m+h\le p-1\), iterating the second-order recurrence from the initial state \((b_m,b_{m+1})\) gives

\[
b_{m+h}\equiv C_h(m)b_{m+1}\pmod p,
\]

where \(N_h\) is the numerator of \(C_h\) after clearing the unit denominators. Hence if both endpoints are zeros, no-adjacency gives \(b_{m+1}\ne0\), and therefore

\[
\boxed{b_m=b_{m+h}=0\pmod p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N_h(m)=0\pmod p.}
\]

The key point is that \(N_h\) is **not** allowed to collapse to the zero polynomial modulo \(p\). `lem:nonvanish` proves this exactly, not computationally. The two anchor evaluations are

\[
N_h(-1)=b_{h-1}((h-1)!)^3,
\]

and

\[
N_h(-2)=-5b_{h-2}((h-2)!)^3.
\]

For \(p\ge7\) and \(2\le h\le p\), if \(N_h\equiv0\pmod p\), both evaluations vanish. The factorials and \(5\) are units in the relevant range, so

\[
b_{h-1}\equiv b_{h-2}\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

contradicting `lem:no-consec`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{N_h\not\equiv0\pmod p\qquad(2\le h\le p).}
\]

Now list the zeros increasingly:

\[
z_1<z_2<\cdots<z_s,
\qquad s=Z(p),
\]

and let

\[
h_i=z_{i+1}-z_i\ge2.
\]

For a fixed gap size \(h\), every starting zero \(z_i\) with \(h_i=h\) is a root of the nonzero polynomial \(N_h\). Thus, writing

\[
r_h=\#\{i:h_i=h\},
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{r_h\le3(h-1).}
\]

Also

\[
\sum_{h\ge2}hr_h=z_s-z_1\le p-1,
\qquad
Z(p)=1+\sum_{h\ge2}r_h.
\]

This is already enough for the advertised proposition. For any cutoff \(H\), short gaps contribute at most

\[
\sum_{2\le h<H}3(h-1)
=\frac32(H-1)(H-2),
\]

while the number of gaps of size at least \(H\) is at most \(\lfloor p/H\rfloor\). Hence `prop:zp-bound` gives

\[
\boxed{
Z(p)\le
\left\lfloor\frac pH\right\rfloor
+\frac32(H-1)(H-2)+1.
}
\]

Taking \(H=\lfloor(p/3)^{1/3}\rfloor+1\) yields

\[
Z(p)\le \frac{3^{4/3}}2p^{2/3}+O(p^{1/3}).
\]

So the first target in the question is already closed:

\[
\boxed{Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})=o(p)\quad\text{unconditionally}.}
\]

No reflection, CFVZ, finite-field abc, or finite scan is needed for this theorem.

# 3. Slight sharpening: the exact degree-only envelope has leading constant \(3/2\)

The one-cutoff proof is not the sharp optimization of the proved constraints. We may solve the integer capacity problem directly:

\[
r_h\le3(h-1),
\qquad
\sum h r_h\le p-1.
\]

Because an item of type \(h\) costs \(h\) units of span and contributes one gap, the maximizing configuration greedily fills the smallest \(h\)'s first.

Let \(H\) be the largest integer such that

\[
H^3-H\le p-1.
\]

Filling the capacities for \(2\le h\le H\) uses exactly

\[
\sum_{h=2}^H h\,3(h-1)
=H^3-H
\]

units of span and creates

\[
\sum_{h=2}^H3(h-1)
=\frac32H(H-1)
\]

gaps. The remaining budget can create at most

\[
\left\lfloor
\frac{p-1-(H^3-H)}{H+1}
\right\rfloor
\]

additional gaps of size \(H+1\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Z(p)\le
1+\frac32H(H-1)
+\left\lfloor
\frac{p-1-(H^3-H)}{H+1}
\right\rfloor.
}
\]

Since \(H=p^{1/3}+O(1)\),

\[
\boxed{Z(p)\le\frac32p^{2/3}+O(p^{1/3}).}
\]

This is exactly the degree-only sharpness phenomenon already noted in `proof.tex`, `rem:sharp`: the exponent \(2/3\) cannot be improved from the constraints \(r_h\le3(h-1)\) and total span alone. Reflection changes at most parity/central bookkeeping and does not change this exponent or leading-order optimization.

So discrete Sturm/Casoratian has already given everything available from one-gap degree counting.

# 4. Why discrete Sturm / Casoratian does not presently give the dyadic \(O(N/\log N)\)

There are two different “Sturm” statements here.

## 4.1 What survives modulo \(p\)

The useful finite-field remnant is the continuant/Casoratian certificate just used:

\[
\text{two zero values at distance }h
\Longrightarrow
N_h(r)=0\pmod p.
\]

That converts coefficient-zero geometry into roots of a polynomial of degree \(3(h-1)\).

## 4.2 What does not survive

Classical Sturm oscillation uses an ordered real field: signs, interlacing, and variation counts. \(\mathbb F_p\) has no compatible order. The complex/real continuant theory in `atom_tail_section.tex` proves the strong characteristic-zero root-strip statement

\[
-h<\Re z<-1
\]

for every complex root of \(N_h\). That is very useful for characteristic-zero resultant nonvanishing and separation, but reduction modulo \(p\) does not preserve the location or interlacing of complex roots. A polynomial whose complex roots lie in a narrow strip may still have many roots in \(\mathbb F_p\), up to its degree.

Likewise the symmetry

\[
N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x)
\]

pairs roots but does not reduce their count by a power of \(h\).

Therefore the current Sturm/Casoratian mechanism stops at the one-gap capacity bound above.

# 5. Aggregate ledger: why separate gap-root estimates do not reach average \(O(1)\)

Summing the certified pointwise theorem over \(N<p\le2N\) gives only

\[
\sum_{N<p\le2N}Z(p)
\ll N^{2/3}\pi(2N)
\ll \frac{N^{5/3}}{\log N}.
\]

The cutoff argument exposes the exact loss. Summed over primes, long gaps contribute at most

\[
\ll \frac{N}{H}\cdot\frac{N}{\log N}
=\frac{N^2}{H\log N},
\]

and degree-counting short gaps gives

\[
\ll \frac{N}{\log N}\sum_{h<H}h
\ll \frac{NH^2}{\log N}.
\]

Thus

\[
\sum_{N<p\le2N}Z(p)
\ll
\frac{N^2}{H\log N}
+rac{NH^2}{\log N},
\]

and optimizing \(H\asymp N^{1/3}\) recovers \(N^{5/3}/\log N\).

More importantly, even a hypothetical statement saying that each fixed \(N_h\) has only \(O(1)\) roots **on average over** \(p\asymp N\) would leave

\[
\frac{N^2}{H\log N}+rac{NH}{\log N}.
\]

Optimizing at \(H\asymp\sqrt N\) is still only

\[
\asymp \frac{N^{3/2}}{\log N}.
\]

So even perfect first-moment control of every individual gap polynomial is not enough. The target

\[
\sum_{p\sim N}Z(p)=O(N/\log N)
\]

requires a genuinely **joint** statement about the gap process at a prime.

A clean sufficient missing input would be the second-factorial-moment estimate

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{N<p\le2N}Z(p)(Z(p)-1)\ll \frac{N}{\log N}.
}
\]

Together with the \(O(\pi(N))\) cost of the possible singleton/midpoint cases, this immediately gives the desired first moment. Equivalently, one needs average control of simultaneous same-prime return collisions, not merely the root count of one \(N_h\) at a time.

This is the signature mismatch that the one-gap Sturm route cannot cross.

# 6. Finite-field polynomial abc: exact mismatch

The CFVZ identity

\[
A_p(a)=\Delta(a)^{\varepsilon_p}B_p(a)^2
\]

is an identity about a polynomial as a **function of \(a\)**. Mason--Stothers / polynomial-abc controls the radical of products or sums of polynomials, hence their **roots in the variable \(a\)** and their multiplicities.

But

\[
Z(p)=\#\{r:[a^r]A_p(a)=0\}
\]

counts missing **coefficients**.

These are categorically different data:

- \(b_r=0\) does not mean \(A_p(r)=0\);
- \(b_r=0\) does not mean \(B_p(r)=0\);
- it does not mean \(\Delta(r)=0\);
- the square factorization says that non-\(\Delta\) *roots* of \(A_p\) have even multiplicity, not that its monomial support is dense.

Differentiating the square identity gives only the root-divisor identity

\[
\frac{A_p'}{A_p}
=\varepsilon_p\frac{\Delta'}{\Delta}
+2\frac{B_p'}{B_p}
\]

where defined. This still sees zeros of the polynomial as a function, not zero entries in its coefficient vector.

Trying to manufacture an additive abc relation by splitting \(A_p\) into supported and unsupported blocks is tautological: the radical degrees of the resulting summands are allowed to be of full degree, so Mason's inequality returns only degree-scale information. There is no term in the standard abc inequality that measures the cardinality of the monomial support.

Thus the CFVZ factorization does **not** improve \(Z(p)\) without a separate sparse-coefficient theorem specific to this horizontal section.

# 7. Differential-equation singularities: again no coefficient-support theorem

The Apéry operator can be written

\[
L_A=
\vartheta^3
-a(2\vartheta+1)(17\vartheta^2+17\vartheta+5)
+a^2(\vartheta+1)^3.
\]

Its leading \(\vartheta^3\) coefficient is

\[
1-34a+a^2=\Delta(a).
\]

Likewise the leading \(\vartheta^2\) coefficient of the square-root operator is \(4\Delta(a)\). Thus the finite singular geometry is fixed: \(a=0\), the two roots of \(\Delta\), and infinity.

This does **not** by itself constrain the number of zero Taylor coefficients in defining characteristic. Expanding \(L_AA_p=0\) coefficient by coefficient simply reproduces the Apéry three-term recurrence. Therefore a coefficient-lacunarity theorem extracted from this ODE would have to add information beyond the recurrence; the singular locus itself supplies none.

This is not merely philosophical. Fixed low-order differential equations with a fixed finite singular locus can have polynomial solutions modulo \(p\) with a positive density of zero coefficients. So “few singularities” cannot be the missing argument.

The special Apéry gain is precisely the stronger return algebra encoded by the gap polynomials \(N_h\) and their two-point nonvanishing certificate. That gives \(p^{2/3}\), but singularity geometry does not currently turn it into average \(O(1)\).

# 8. A control family showing why the abstract square/ODE/reflection package is insufficient

Because the exact Apéry recurrence already proves \(Z(p)=o(p)\), there cannot be a positive-density-zero family satisfying the **exact same Apéry recurrence** and initial data. So the conditional fallback requested in the question is not needed.

However, it is useful to exhibit an explicit family showing that the more generic package

> fixed second-order recurrence + reflection + no adjacent zeros + square truncation + fixed low-complexity ODE

still permits positive-density coefficient zeros.

For any prime \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), put

\[
m=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
A_p(x)=(1+x^2)^m.
\]

Then

\[
A_p(x)=B_p(x)^2,
\qquad
B_p(x)=(1+x^2)^{(p-1)/4}.
\]

Because

\[
\frac{p-1}{4}\equiv-\frac14\pmod p,
\]

\(B_p\) is exactly the relevant degree truncation modulo \(p\) of the fixed formal series \((1+x^2)^{-1/4}\); similarly \(A_p\) is the truncation of \((1+x^2)^{-1/2}\).

Write

\[
A_p(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}a_rx^r.
\]

Then

\[
a_{2j}=\binom mj\ne0\pmod p,
\qquad
a_{2j+1}=0.
\]

Hence

\[
\#\{r:a_r=0\}=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

The coefficient zeros are nonadjacent, and reflection holds:

\[
a_{p-1-r}=a_r.
\]

Moreover the coefficients satisfy the fixed second-order recurrence

\[
\boxed{(r+1)a_{r+1}+r a_{r-1}=0\pmod p.}
\]

For odd \(r=2j+1\), this is just

\[
\frac{a_{r+1}}{a_{r-1}}
=rac{m-j}{j+1}
\equiv-\frac{2j+1}{2j+2}
=-\frac r{r+1},
\]

and for even \(r\) both neighboring coefficients vanish.

Finally the universal series satisfies the fixed first-order ODE

\[
(1+x^2)A'(x)+xA(x)=0,
\]

with only the fixed singularities \(x=\pm i\) and infinity.

So positive-density coefficient zeros are entirely compatible with reflection, no adjacency, a square truncation, a fixed low-order recurrence, and a fixed finite singular locus. What excludes this for the true Apéry family is the **specific Apéry recurrence and its nonvanishing gap-polynomial family**, not the generic CFVZ-looking shape.

# 9. What the three proposed routes actually contribute

| route | rigorous output | why it stops |
|---|---|---|
| discrete Sturm / Casoratian | two-zero gap \(h\Rightarrow N_h(r)=0\), \(\deg N_h=3(h-1)\), \(N_h\not\equiv0\pmod p\) | degree-only gap capacities are sharp at exponent \(2/3\) |
| reflection | pairs zero positions and return roots | changes parity/constant bookkeeping only; no power saving |
| CFVZ square factorization | exact root-multiplicity factorization of \(A_p\) | \(Z(p)\) counts coefficients, not roots |
| finite-field polynomial abc | controls radicals / polynomial roots in additive identities | no monomial-support term; coefficient-zero problem survives unchanged |
| ODE singularities | fixed rank-2/rank-3 operators with singular locus governed by \(a\Delta(a)\) | coefficient expansion is exactly the original recurrence; finite singular locus does not prevent lacunarity |

The only unconditional pointwise improvement obtained from these exact inputs is therefore the already banked

\[
\boxed{Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}.}
\]

The sharp degree-only version is

\[
\boxed{Z(p)\le(3/2+o(1))p^{2/3}.}
\]

For the dyadic mean \(O(N/\log N)\), the next genuinely new theorem must couple several returns at the **same prime** (or otherwise give a second factorial moment for \(Z(p)\)). It cannot be another theorem about the radical, root multiplicities, or singularities of one CFVZ polynomial.

# Final answer

1. **Uniform \(o(p)\): proved unconditionally.** Current `proof.tex` gives \(Z(p)=O(p^{2/3})\); the same certified constraints sharpen to \((3/2+o(1))p^{2/3}\).
2. **Dyadic \(O(N/\log N)\): not obtained by the three proposed mechanisms.** The certified pointwise theorem gives only \(O(N^{5/3}/\log N)\), and even average \(O(1)\) roots for each individual gap polynomial would still leave an \(N^{3/2}/\log N\) ledger.
3. **The first signature mismatch is coefficient-vs-root, followed by one-gap-vs-joint-gap.** CFVZ/abc/ODE geometry concerns roots or singularities; \(Z(p)\) counts zero coefficients. The recurrence bridges coefficient zeros to roots of \(N_h\) one gap at a time, but average \(O(1)\) requires a genuinely joint same-prime return theorem, e.g. \(\sum_{p\sim N}Z(p)(Z(p)-1)\ll N/\log N\).
4. **No finite scan is used in any conclusion above.**
