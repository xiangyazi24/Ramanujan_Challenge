## 1. One-wrap block decomposition

Write

\[
A=A_{K,a},\qquad K=p+d<2p.
\]

Split rows into the ordinary range and the wrapped range. For
\(s=u+p\), \(0\le u\le d\), Lucas gives, for the binomial basis,

\[
\binom{p+u+k}{2k}\equiv
\begin{cases}
\binom{u+k}{2k},& k<p/2,\\
\text{the corresponding one-wrap Lucas block},& k\ge p/2,
\end{cases}
\pmod p.
\]

The Apéry congruence

\[
b_{p+u}\equiv b_1 b_u \pmod p
\]

(with the usual Apéry supercongruence hypotheses) makes the wrapped rows linear combinations of the unwrapped rows except at the labels where the Apéry factor vanishes. Let

\[
Z_p=\{u: b_u\equiv0\pmod p\},\qquad d=\max Z_p.
\]

At the threshold \(a=|Z_p|\), the only surviving first-order obstruction is a matrix on the missing row space.

## 2. Description of the first Schur layer

After p-unit row and column operations over \(\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z\), write

\[
A\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
I&0\\0&pD_{p,d,a}
\end{pmatrix}\pmod {p^2}.
\]

The rows of \(D\) are indexed by the missing pivot directions. The columns are indexed by the free columns after the mod-p rank computation.

A useful symbolic form is:

\[
D_{ij}=\sum_{t\in Z_p} c_{i,t}\,\Delta_t(j),
\]

where \(c_{i,t}\) is the first-order Lucas/Apéry defect coefficient and

\[
\Delta_t(j)=\frac{U_{p+t,j}-\lambda_tU_{t,j}}p\pmod p
\]

is the normalized one-wrap binomial defect. This is the actual object replacing a Vandermonde matrix.

The crucial point is that \(c_{i,t}\) is not identically one. It contains derivatives/first-order residues of the Apéry factor. Hence a determinant of \(D\) factors schematically as

\[
\det D=(\det C)(\det \Delta)
\]

only after choosing compatible bases. The second factor is confluent Vandermonde-like, but the first factor is an Apéry obstruction determinant.

Therefore full rank requires two independent hypotheses:

1. the one-wrap defect interpolation determinant is nonzero;
2. the Apéry defect matrix \(C\) has full row rank.

Lucas alone proves neither.

## 4. Primitive numerator residue audit

The implication

\[
C=pD\pmod {p^2},\quad \operatorname{rank}(D)=r
\]

only proves that the first r invariant factors are exactly p. It does not by itself give the primitive kernel vector.

To recover the numerator residue one must also track:

1. the chosen free denominator coordinate;
2. the unit normalization of the kernel vector;
3. the projection from the full kernel to the numerator Phi-basis.

The normalization "last free denominator coordinate = 1 mod p" is valid only when that coordinate is nonzero in the saturated kernel. Under that hypothesis, solving the Schur system gives the reported residue

\[

\]

Its evaluation at x=1423 being 45 mod 379 is therefore evidence of a genuine saturated escape, but not a consequence of Smith rank alone.

## 5. What would prove the desired theorem

A full theorem would require proving for every canonical pair

\[
d=\max Z_p,\qquad a=|Z_p|
\]

that

\[
\operatorname{rank}D_{p,d,a}=|\text{missing rows}|.
\]

The required missing ingredient is a nonvanishing theorem for the Apéry defect determinant. A possible target statement is:

\[
\det(C)\ne0\pmod p
\]

for all relevant Apéry primes. This is stronger than the usual Apéry congruence and does not follow from it.

Conversely, a genuine counterexample would be a prime p for which either the defect determinant or the confluent Lucas determinant vanishes, producing

\[
\operatorname{rank}D_{p,d,a}<\operatorname{corank}_p(A).
\]

The supplied p=379 data is not such a counterexample.

```python
from sympy import Matrix

# Apéry numbers
b = [1]
for n in range(1,K+1):

for s in range(K+1):
    row=[]
    for k in range(a+1):
        row.append(comb(s+k,2*k))

        row.append(-b[s]*comb(s+k,2*k))
    A.append(row)

M=Matrix([[x%p for x in row] for row in A])
print(M.rank())
```
