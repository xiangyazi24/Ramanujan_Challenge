# One-sided HM3: what it buys, what an L² proof would require, and why an ordinary Selberg sieve is not enough

## Executive verdict

The one-sided reformulation is mathematically important, but its benefit has to be stated precisely.

1. **At the required scale, the aggregate one-sided and aggregate two-sided bounds are essentially equivalent.** If
   
   $$
   D_X:=\sum_{r\in\mathcal P_X}\Delta_r^{\rm per},
   \qquad T_X:=M\lambda_X^3=X^2\lambda_X^3,
   $$
   
   then the exact identity is
   
   $$
   D_X=F_3(X)-B_X,
   \qquad 0\le B_X\ll T_X.
   $$
   
   Hence $D_X\ge -O(T_X)$. Proving $(D_X)_+\ll X^{o(1)}T_X$ automatically gives $|D_X|\ll X^{o(1)}T_X$. The one-sided refinement therefore does not create a new asymptotic saving if “two-sided” only means an $O(T_X)$ bound. It does remove the unnecessary demand for square-root cancellation, $o(T_X)$ cancellation, or prime-by-prime equidistribution.

2. **The proposed Cauchy–Schwarz calculation has its implication reversed.** A second moment can prove the target, but one needs an **upper** bound
   
   $$
   \sum_r (\Delta_r)_+^2
   \ll \frac{T_X^2}{L_X},
   $$
   
   where $L_X$ is the number of relevant third-prime columns. In the empirical regime $\lambda_X\asymp1/\log X$, this becomes
   
   $$
   \sum_r (\Delta_r)_+^2\ll \frac{X^3}{(\log X)^5}.
   $$
   
   The random-model second moment is expected to be only $\asymp X^2/(\log X)^3$, so there is a factor $X/(\log X)^2$ of slack. Thus an L² route is quantitatively plausible.

3. **That L² quantity is not controlled merely by the fourth factorial moment.** Its diagonal contains
   
   $$
   F_5+4F_4+2F_3,
   $$
   
   while its off-diagonal contains two-row, same-prime correlations not represented by any one-row factorial moment. At $X=128$ the actual data have $F_4=0$ but $F_3=60$, already disproving the slogan that the required discrepancy energy is simply a fourth-moment object.

4. **A black-box Selberg upper-bound sieve does not solve the problem from the current inputs.** The relevant triple moduli have size $pqr\asymp X^3=M^{3/2}$, whereas the interval has length $M=X^2$. The sieve needs precisely the missing assertion that only an $M/(pqr)\asymp1/X$ fraction of zero-residue triples have their canonical CRT representative below $M$. The anchored-star family satisfies all existing local and pairwise hypotheses and violates this conclusion, so no generic sieve based only on those hypotheses can work.

5. **The one-sided nature does open genuinely different architectures:** a positive-tail L² estimate, a three-color decoupling followed by a weighted upper sieve, a largest-positive-eigenvalue bound rather than a full operator norm, or an inverse theorem saying that excessive positive mass forces an anchored-star-like arithmetic structure. These require new Apéry-specific cross-characteristic input, but they are weaker and better targeted than full equidistribution.

## 1. The exact aggregate identity already supplies the lower bound

Write

$$
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},\qquad M=X^2,
$$

$$
\beta_r=\frac{|Z_r|}{r},
\qquad
w_r(m)=(K(m)-f_r(m))_2.
$$

Then

$$
W_r=\sum_{m<M}w_r(m),
\qquad
H_r=\sum_{m<M}f_r(m)w_r(m),
$$

and

$$
\Delta_r^{\rm per}=H_r-\beta_rW_r.
$$

Summing over the third prime gives the exact identity

$$
\sum_r H_r=F_3(X):=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_3.
$$

Moreover,

$$
B_X:=\sum_r\beta_rW_r
 =\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}.
$$

The universal second-factorial-moment estimate gives

$$
0\le B_X
\le \lambda_XF_2(X)
\le 4M\lambda_X^3.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{D_X:=\sum_r\Delta_r^{\rm per}=F_3(X)-B_X}
$$

and consequently

$$
-B_X\le D_X\le F_3(X),
$$

$$
\boxed{(D_X)_+\le F_3(X)\le (D_X)_++B_X.}
$$

This has two consequences that should not be conflated.

### 1.1 At the HM3 scale, one-sided and two-sided aggregate bounds coincide

Because the negative side is already bounded by $B_X\ll T_X$, one has

$$
(D_X)_+\ll X^{o(1)}T_X
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|D_X|\ll X^{o(1)}T_X
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F_3(X)\ll X^{o(1)}T_X.
$$

Thus the one-sided observation saves work only relative to stronger proposed statements such as

$$
D_X=o(T_X),
\qquad
|D_X|\ll \sqrt{T_X},
\qquad
\sum_r|\Delta_r|\ll T_X,
$$

or individual equidistribution for every $r$. None of those is required for $(\mathrm{HM})_3$.

### 1.2 The location of the positive part matters

The exact target is

$$
\left(\sum_r\Delta_r\right)_+.
$$

The stronger quantity proposed in the question is

$$
P_X:=\sum_r(\Delta_r)_+.
$$

Only the inequality

$$
(D_X)_+\le P_X
$$

is automatic. Passing to $P_X$ discards cancellation between overloaded and underloaded third-prime slices. A second-moment argument controls this stronger quantity. That can still be a good route, but it partly gives back the advantage supplied by the signed aggregate formulation.

The hierarchy is

$$
\left(\sum_r\Delta_r\right)_+
\le \sum_r(\Delta_r)_+
\le \sum_r|\Delta_r|.
$$

The observed value $D_X=5.69$ at $X=128$ gives no upper bound on either of the last two quantities.

## 2. The correct Cauchy–Schwarz criterion

Put

$$
Q_X^+:=\sum_r(\Delta_r)_+^2,
\qquad
L_+:=\#\{r:\Delta_r>0\}.
$$

Then

$$
P_X^2\le L_+Q_X^+.
$$

The full square sum $Q_X=\sum_r\Delta_r^2$ may replace $Q_X^+$, but is stronger than necessary.

The correct logical use is the contrapositive:

$$
P_X>T_X
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Q_X^+>\frac{T_X^2}{L_+}.
$$

Therefore an upper bound

$$
\boxed{
Q_X^+\ll \frac{X^{4+o(1)}\lambda_X^6}{L_+}
}
$$

rules out failure of $(\mathrm{HM})_3$.

The inequality displayed in the question,

$$
Q_X\ge \frac{X^3}{(\log X)^5},
$$

is not a goal. It is a necessary consequence of a hypothetical failure of the desired positive-sum estimate. To prove the desired estimate, one must prove that $Q_X$ stays **below** that threshold.

### 2.1 Eliminating the unknown number of positive slices

Let

$$
L_X=\#\{r\in\mathcal P_X:|Z_r|>0\}.
$$

Then $L_+\le L_X$, and there are two useful deterministic bounds:

$$
L_X\ll \frac{X}{\log X},
$$

and, since $r\le2X$,

$$
L_X\le\sum_r|Z_r|\le2X\lambda_X.
$$

Hence the exact L² sufficient condition can be weakened to

$$
Q_X^+
\ll
\frac{X^{4+o(1)}\lambda_X^6}
     {\min\{X/\log X,\,2X\lambda_X\}}.
$$

Equivalently, one may use either of the convenient sufficient estimates

$$
Q_X^+\ll X^{3+o(1)}\lambda_X^5,
$$

or

$$
Q_X^+\ll X^{3+o(1)}\lambda_X^6\log X,
$$

choosing the better one in the relevant range of $\lambda_X$.

When $\lambda_X\asymp1/\log X$, both have scale

$$
\boxed{Q_X^+\ll \frac{X^{3+o(1)}}{(\log X)^5}.}
$$

### 2.2 The random-model scale has substantial slack

In an ideal independent-row model, let $Y$ be the number of other active columns at a row. The ordered pair weight is $(Y)_2$, and

$$
(Y)_2^2=(Y)_4+4(Y)_3+2(Y)_2.
$$

If $Y$ is approximately Poisson with mean $\lambda_X$, then

$$
\mathbb E (Y)_2^2
=\lambda_X^4+4\lambda_X^3+2\lambda_X^2.
$$

Conditioning on the other columns and treating the $r$-column as Bernoulli of density $\beta_r$ gives heuristically

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\Delta_r)
\approx
M\beta_r
\bigl(\lambda_X^4+4\lambda_X^3+2\lambda_X^2\bigr).
$$

After summing over $r$,

$$
\mathbb E Q_X
\approx
M\bigl(\lambda_X^5+4\lambda_X^4+2\lambda_X^3\bigr)
\asymp M\lambda_X^3
$$

in the sparse regime. Thus one expects

$$
Q_X\asymp T_X=\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3},
$$

whereas Cauchy–Schwarz permits

$$
Q_X\ll\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^5}.
$$

The ratio is

$$
\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}.
$$

So the L² theorem needed for HM3 is much weaker than a sharp variance theorem.

### 2.3 Numerical threshold at $X=128$

The repository's exact $X=128$ data give

```text
number of shell primes       = 23
number of active zero columns = 13
F3                            = 60
F4                            = 0
T_X = X^2 lambda^3            = 64.2 approximately
```

The prompt gives $D_X=5.69$, so the periodic baseline is approximately

$$
B_X=F_3-D_X=54.31.
$$

Using all 23 shell primes, Cauchy–Schwarz would close if

$$
Q_X^+\le\frac{64.2^2}{23}=179.20\ldots.
$$

Using only the 13 active columns, the sufficient threshold is

$$
\boxed{Q_X^+\le\frac{64.2^2}{13}=317.05\ldots.}
$$

The signed number $5.69$ does not determine $Q_X^+$. Computing $Q_X^+$ is therefore the first empirical test of this route.

## 3. Why the discrepancy square is not merely an HM4 quantity

For clarity, retain the periodic baseline and put

$$
g_r(m)=f_r(m)-\beta_r,
\qquad
w_r(m)=(K(m)-f_r(m))_2.
$$

Then

$$
\Delta_r=\sum_{m<M}g_r(m)w_r(m)
$$

and

$$
Q_X=\sum_r\sum_{m,n<M}
 g_r(m)g_r(n)w_r(m)w_r(n).
$$

This splits into a diagonal part $m=n$ and an off-diagonal part $m\ne n$.

### 3.1 The diagonal already reaches factorial order five

At a row with $K(m)=k$, if $f_r(m)=1$, then

$$
w_r(m)=(k-1)_2.
$$

Summing the squared weight over the $k$ active third-prime choices gives

$$
k\bigl((k-1)_2\bigr)^2
=(k)_5+4(k)_4+2(k)_3.
$$

Let

$$
\sigma_2(X)=\sum_r\beta_r^2.
$$

A direct split according to $f_r(m)=1$ or $0$ gives the deterministic diagonal bound

$$
\boxed{
Q_{\rm diag}
\le
F_5+4F_4+2F_3
+
\sigma_2(X)\bigl(F_4+4F_3+2F_2\bigr).
}
$$

The second parenthesis uses

$$
(K)_2^2=(K)_4+4(K)_3+2(K)_2.
$$

Thus the statement “the discrepancy second moment involves the fourth factorial moment” is only partially true. Squaring the **pair multiplicity before testing the third column** gives fourth-order terms. Once the same third-prime indicator is inserted and summed, the hit diagonal carries a fifth prime label and produces $F_5+4F_4+2F_3$.

The $X=128$ data make the point sharply. There are ten rows with $K=3$, so $F_3=10\cdot6=60$, while $F_4=0$. The uncentered active-hit diagonal contribution is

$$
2F_3=120.
$$

Therefore no theorem depending only on $F_4$ can even describe the first nonzero finite example.

### 3.2 The off-diagonal is a two-row correlation problem

The remaining term is

$$
Q_{\rm off}
=
\sum_{m\ne n}
\sum_r g_r(m)g_r(n)w_r(m)w_r(n).
$$

For a fixed $r$, this asks whether two residues $m\bmod r$ and $n\bmod r$ simultaneously lie in $Z_r$, weighted by the pair-witness multiplicities at two different rows. It depends on quantities of the form

$$
|Z_r\cap(Z_r+h)|,
\qquad h=n-m,
$$

but with weights that themselves come from zero sets in other characteristics. No one-row factorial moment $F_j$ controls this term.

A proof of an L² estimate therefore naturally separates into:

1. a **one-row high-multiplicity bound** controlling the diagonal and excluding anchored stars;
2. a **two-row cross-characteristic dispersion bound** controlling the off-diagonal.

The currently available reflection, interval, fiber-energy, and bounded-codegree statements do not supply either bound at the needed scale.

### 3.3 Parseval alone loses the sparsity factor

Let

$$
\widetilde\nu_r(u)=\nu_r(u)-\frac{W_r}{r}.
$$

Then

$$
\Delta_r=\sum_{u\in Z_r}\widetilde\nu_r(u).
$$

Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval give

$$
\Delta_r^2
\le |Z_r|\sum_{u\bmod r}|\widetilde\nu_r(u)|^2.
$$

This is valid but too crude. In a random witness model the centered collision energy is of order $W_r$, so summing the right side gives roughly

$$
\left(\sum_r|Z_r|\right)M\lambda_X^2
\asymp XM\lambda_X^3.
$$

In the regime $\lambda_X\asymp1/\log X$, this is

$$
\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3},
$$

whereas the Cauchy–Schwarz threshold is $X^3/(\log X)^5$. The generic Parseval bound loses two logarithms because it treats $Z_r$ as an arbitrary direction in the full $r$-dimensional residue space. A successful L² proof must exploit the relative orientation of $Z_r$ and the pair-witness measure, not just the energy of either one separately.

## 4. Exact second-moment residuals that would suffice

The one-sided refinement suggests two clean named targets.

### 4.1 Positive L² pair-witness dispersion

Define

$$
\mathcal Q_3^+(X)
:=\sum_{r\in\mathcal P_X}
  \left(H_r-\frac{|Z_r|}{r}W_r\right)_+^2.
$$

Then the following is an exact sufficient condition:

$$
\tag{PW3-L2+}
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_3^+(X)
\ll
\frac{X^{4+o(1)}\lambda_X^6}{L_X}.
}
$$

This implies

$$
\sum_r(\Delta_r)_+\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3
$$

and hence $(\mathrm{HM})_3$.

A more natural but substantially stronger random-scale theorem would be

$$
\mathcal Q_3^+(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
$$

There is no need to aim for this sharper statement initially. The weaker threshold `(PW3-L2+)` has much more room.

### 4.2 A weighted, self-normalized positive energy

Set

$$
b_r=\beta_rW_r.
$$

If $b_r=0$, then $\Delta_r=0$, so define

$$
\mathcal E_3^+(X)
=
\sum_{b_r>0}
\frac{(\Delta_r)_+^2}{b_r}.
$$

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
\sum_r(\Delta_r)_+
\le
\left(\sum_rb_r\right)^{1/2}
\left(\mathcal E_3^+(X)\right)^{1/2}.
$$

Since $\sum_rb_r=B_X\ll T_X$, the condition

$$
\tag{PW3-std+}
\boxed{
\mathcal E_3^+(X)\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3
}
$$

also implies $(\mathrm{HM})_3$.

This normalization is attractive because it compares each overload with its own expected witness mass. In a simple independent model, each summand is of constant order and the whole quantity is about $L_X$, far below $T_X$ in the observed regime. Repeated witnesses at one row inflate the ratio by

$$
\frac{\sum_mw_r(m)^2}{\sum_mw_r(m)},
$$

so `(PW3-std+)` directly detects the anchored-star obstruction.

These are genuinely one-sided second-moment statements: large negative discrepancies are not charged.

## 5. The anchored star calibrates the L² criterion exactly

In the anchored-star model, let

$$
L\asymp\frac{X}{\log X}
$$

active primes all contain the same row $m_0$. Then for every active $r$,

$$
\Delta_r\asymp L^2>0.
$$

Consequently

$$
\sum_r(\Delta_r)_+\asymp L^3
\asymp\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3},
$$

and

$$
Q_X^+\asymp L^5
\asymp\frac{X^5}{(\log X)^5}.
$$

The permitted L² threshold is only

$$
\frac{T_X^2}{L}
\asymp
\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^5}.
$$

Thus the star violates `(PW3-L2+)` by a factor $X^2$. Moreover,

$$
\left(\sum_r(\Delta_r)_+\right)^2
\asymp LQ_X^+,
$$

so it nearly saturates Cauchy–Schwarz. This is useful evidence that the proposed L² residual is not an arbitrary strengthening: it separates the random regime from the known extremal obstruction by a very wide margin and is correctly aligned with that obstruction.

It also proves a no-go statement. Any claimed derivation of `(PW3-L2+)` from only the local constraints already satisfied by the anchored star must contain an error.

## 6. Why a standard Selberg upper-bound sieve does not close HM3

Selberg's sieve is one-sided only **after** one supplies local densities and a level-of-distribution estimate. Here the local density is known, but the required weighted level of distribution is exactly the missing theorem.

### 6.1 Triple-modulus formulation

For squarefree $d$ supported on $\mathcal P_X$, define

$$
A(d)=\sum_{m<M}\prod_{p\mid d}f_p(m),
\qquad
\beta(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}\beta_p.
$$

Then

$$
F_3(X)
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q,r\in\mathcal P_X\\
                 p,q,r\text{ pairwise distinct}}}
A(pqr).
$$

The independent model predicts

$$
A(pqr)\approx M\beta_p\beta_q\beta_r,
$$

and summing these main terms costs at most $M\lambda_X^3$.

But

$$
pqr\asymp X^3=M^{3/2},
\qquad
\frac{M}{pqr}\asymp\frac1X.
$$

For a fixed zero-residue triple $(a,b,c)$, CRT gives one canonical representative modulo $pqr$. Its contribution to $A(pqr)$ is either zero or one according as that representative lies above or below $M$. The expected contribution is only $M/(pqr)\asymp1/X$.

Thus the desired theorem is exactly that, on average over the actual Apéry zero-residue triples, only an $O(1/X)$ fraction land in the very short prefix $[0,M)$ of a period of length $\asymp X^3$.

CRT injectivity alone gives only

$$
A(pqr)\le |Z_p||Z_q||Z_r|.
$$

After summing, this is of order

$$
\left(\sum_p|Z_p|\right)^3
\asymp X^3\lambda_X^3,
$$

which loses exactly the factor $X$ separating the anchored star from HM3.

A Selberg sieve would encode

$$
A(d)=M\beta(d)+R_d.
$$

At the required triple level, controlling the positive weighted sum of the $R_d$ is the pair-witness dispersion problem itself. Calling it a sieve remainder does not make it available.

### 6.2 A quadratic Selberg weight cannot universally majorize the cubic load at the right scale

Suppose an argument uses only pair information and a quadratic majorant in the number $k=K(m)$ of active columns. On the range $0\le k\le L_X$, any quadratic majorant of $(k)_3$ must pay a coefficient of order $L_X$, because at $k=L_X$ one needs

$$
O(aL_X^2)\ge (L_X)_3\asymp L_X^3.
$$

The resulting estimate is essentially

$$
F_3\le (L_X-2)F_2,
$$

which is the existing deterministic envelope and loses a factor of order $X/\log X$. The anchored star saturates this phenomenon. A generic quadratic Selberg form therefore cannot convert the proved second moment into the third-moment scale.

### 6.3 Sieving the pair-witness sequence is circular

One may try to regard the ordered pair weights

$$
a_m=(K(m))_2
$$

as a nonnegative sequence and sieve it by the third-prime conditions. But

$$
\sum_r\sum_{m<M}a_mf_r(m)
=F_3(X)+2F_2(X).
$$

Thus proving that this weighted sequence hits the $r$-th zero set with average density $\beta_r$ would prove HM3 immediately. The required local estimate is not an input; it is the desired conclusion in another normalization.

The same circularity appears in the fixed-$r$ formulation. The weighted local count is $H_r$, and the expected term is $\beta_rW_r$; their difference is exactly $\Delta_r$.

### 6.4 A low-frequency Selberg majorant still needs residue-scale resolution

One-sidedness might suggest majorizing $1_{Z_r}$ by a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial of bandwidth $H$. A standard Selberg/Fejér point majorant has excess average of order $|Z_r|/H$. Its aggregate excess against the witness masses is bounded by

$$
\sum_r\frac{|Z_r|}{H}W_r
\le
\frac{F_2(X)}{H}\sum_r|Z_r|
\ll
\frac{X}{H}M\lambda_X^3.
$$

To keep this within the HM3 scale one needs

$$
H\gg X.
$$

But $r\asymp X$, so the majorant must use essentially the full set of residue frequencies. The one-sided majorant does not reduce the problem to the low-frequency range accessible to ordinary fixed-complexity equidistribution.

### 6.5 The anchored star is a formal sieve no-go

The anchored star has the correct local densities, the universal CRT second moment, reflection, sparse fibers, interval bounds, and bounded row codegrees. Yet all pair-witness mass is overloaded on the third zero sets.

Therefore no sieve theorem whose hypotheses consist only of those properties can imply the needed upper bound. A successful sieve must contain an additional Apéry-specific hypothesis that explicitly excludes coherent cross-characteristic alignment.

## 7. What a genuinely useful one-sided sieve would have to prove

Although a black-box sieve is unavailable, the one-sided formulation suggests a cleaner **decoupled weighted sieve**.

### 7.1 Three-color decoupling

Independently color every shell prime with one of three colors. Let

$$
K_i(m)=\sum_{p\text{ of color }i}f_p(m),
\qquad
\lambda_i=\sum_{p\text{ of color }i}\beta_p.
$$

For every row,

$$
\mathbb E_{\rm color}
\bigl[K_1(m)K_2(m)K_3(m)\bigr]
=\frac{(K(m))_3}{27}.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
F_3(X)
=27\,\mathbb E_{\rm color}
\sum_{m<M}K_1(m)K_2(m)K_3(m).
}
$$

For a fixed coloring, define the pair weight

$$
a_{12}(m)=K_1(m)K_2(m),
\qquad
W_{12}=\sum_{m<M}a_{12}(m).
$$

The important point is that $a_{12}$ is built from colors 1 and 2 and is independent of every individual color-3 column as a formal expression. Pair CRT injectivity gives

$$
W_{12}\le4M\lambda_1\lambda_2.
$$

A one-sided colored pair-sieve theorem of the form

$$
\tag{CPS3}
\boxed{
\left(
\sum_{m<M}a_{12}(m)K_3(m)
-\lambda_3W_{12}
\right)_+
\ll
X^{o(1)}M\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3
}
$$

uniformly, or merely on average over the coloring, would imply $(\mathrm{HM})_3$ after averaging.

This is a better point of entry for Selberg or large-sieve technology than the original $w_r=(K-f_r)_2$ because the weight being tested does not contain the test column. It removes the most immediate self-correlation.

It does **not** solve the arithmetic problem: the anchored star also violates `(CPS3)`. But it converts the target into a standard-looking statement:

> A fixed nonnegative pair-witness sequence generated by two disjoint prime blocks must hit the zero sets of a third disjoint block with at most the expected aggregate density.

That is the cleanest sieve-shaped residual opened by the one-sided observation.

### 7.2 Positive spectral bounds rather than full equidistribution

After coloring, `(CPS3)` is a one-sided trilinear correlation. A two-sided large-sieve theorem would bound the full operator norm of the centered incidence operator. HM3 only needs its largest positive direction.

This suggests seeking:

- a largest-eigenvalue estimate rather than an absolute spectral norm;
- a sum-of-squares or Selberg-weight certificate for an upper majorant;
- a decomposition in which a large negative semidefinite term may simply be retained instead of bounded absolutely.

This is a real advantage of one-sidedness. An anchored star is precisely a large positive eigenvector, so the arithmetic theorem must rule out that positive direction. Large negative correlations need not be controlled.

### 7.3 An inverse theorem for positive overload

A different route is to prove an incidence inverse theorem:

> If the aggregate positive excess is larger than $CM\lambda_X^3$, then a positive proportion of it is supported on a bounded-complexity configuration: one or a few integer rows, a short family of canonical CRT quotients, or a common algebraic orbit shared by many characteristics.

One would then rule out only those structured positive-overload configurations using the Apéry recurrence, resultants, transfer matrices, or a cross-characteristic sheaf argument.

This is weaker than proving all witness residues equidistributed. Underloading and unstructured fluctuations are irrelevant. The anchored-star proposition tells us exactly what the first inverse obstruction should look like.

### 7.4 A geometric upper sieve

If one could construct a bounded-complexity arithmetic family in which

1. pair witnesses form a parameter space over the third characteristic;
2. landing in $Z_r$ is a proper algebraic or Frobenius condition; and
3. no irreducible component of the witness family is contained in that condition,

then a Lang–Weil/Deligne/Katz-type **upper** count could be enough. A matching lower count would not be needed.

This is where one-sidedness could reduce a full equidistribution theorem to a “no dominant component” theorem. At present, however, the Apéry coefficient-zero set is a defining-characteristic Mellin-zero condition, and the canonical CRT representative includes a pair-dependent least-residue/floor operation and the cutoff $m<M$. The current Mellin or Sato–Tate results do not provide the required cross-characteristic parameter space. This remains genuinely new input.

### 7.5 Truncate the witness multiplicity and isolate an anti-star theorem

A practical L² proof may split

$$
w_r(m)=w_r(m)1_{w_r(m)\le R}
       +w_r(m)1_{w_r(m)>R}.
$$

For the bounded part, a weighted large sieve has coefficient energy at most $R W_r$ rather than $\sum_mw_r(m)^2$. The exceptional part can be attacked by an Apéry-specific theorem excluding coherent high-multiplicity rows.

The one-sided target requires only an upper bound for the exceptional positive tail. It does not require a distribution theorem for ordinary rows. This is another meaningful simplification, although the anchored star shows that the exceptional-tail theorem cannot follow from the existing pairwise toolbox.

## 8. What should be measured next

The value

$$
D_X=\sum_r\Delta_r=5.69
$$

at $X=128$ is encouraging for the exact aggregate route, but it cannot distinguish among three very different scenarios:

1. every $\Delta_r$ is individually small;
2. positive and negative discrepancies are both large and cancel;
3. a few overloaded primes are offset by many mildly underloaded primes.

The decisive diagnostics are therefore

$$
P_X=\sum_r(\Delta_r)_+,
$$

$$
Q_X^+=\sum_r(\Delta_r)_+^2,
$$

$$
\mathcal E_3^+(X)
=
\sum_{b_r>0}\frac{(\Delta_r)_+^2}{b_r},
$$

and the dimensionless closure ratio

$$
\mathfrak R_{L^2}(X)
=
\frac{L_XQ_X^+}{T_X^2}.
$$

The L² route closes whenever $\mathfrak R_{L^2}(X)=X^{o(1)}O(1)$.

It is also important to split $Q_X$ into:

- diagonal versus off-diagonal rows;
- rows with $K=3$, $K=4$, and $K\ge5$;
- the largest individual prime contributions;
- colored-block versions of `(CPS3)`.

At $X=128$, the active-column threshold $Q_X^+<317.05$ is concrete. Since $F_4=0$ and the active-hit diagonal begins at $2F_3=120$, this scale is plausible but not automatic. The exact $Q_X^+$ value would immediately tell whether the second-moment strategy deserves priority.

A useful three-way comparison is:

1. the actual Apéry zero sets;
2. independent random translations of zero sets with the same cardinalities;
3. the anchored-star zero sets.

The random model should have $Q_X\asymp T_X$, while the star has $Q_X\asymp X^5/(\log X)^5$. The needed threshold lies far between them.

## 9. Strategic conclusion

The one-sided refinement is useful, but not because it turns HM3 into a routine upper-bound sieve.

### What it genuinely removes

It removes the need to prove:

- lower equidistribution of pair witnesses on $Z_r$;
- absolute discrepancy for each third prime;
- square-root cancellation of the aggregate;
- control of large negative deviations.

At the $O(M\lambda^3)$ scale, the negative aggregate is already bounded by the nonnegative baseline.

### What remains unchanged

One must still exclude a positive coherent alignment of pair witnesses with the third zero sets. The anchored star shows that this is not a consequence of local zero-set sparsity, reflection, CRT injectivity, the second factorial moment, bounded codegrees, or primewise Fourier bounds.

### Best concrete new route

The strongest actionable refinement is:

```text
First target PW3-L2+ or its self-normalized version PW3-std+.
Use a three-color partition to decouple the pair-witness weights from
third-prime columns, and seek a one-sided weighted dispersion theorem
(CPS3). Split bounded witness multiplicities from an exceptional
anti-star tail. Do not attempt to obtain the result from HM4 or from a
black-box Selberg sieve alone.
```

Thus the final answer is:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
The one-sided nature opens upper-tail, positive-spectral, inverse, and
colored weighted-sieve strategies. A second-moment proof is
quantitatively viable after correcting the direction of Cauchy–Schwarz,
and it needs only a much weaker-than-random upper bound. But the required
second moment contains fifth-order diagonal mass and two-row
cross-characteristic correlations, so it is not supplied by the fourth
factorial moment. An ordinary Selberg upper-bound sieve is circular at
the relevant level; a successful sieve must incorporate a new
Apéry-specific weighted level-of-distribution or no-dominant-component
theorem.
\end{minipage}
}
$$
