# Aggregate modular-form audit for the top Apéry radical

## Verdict

There **is** a logically genuine aggregate route, and it does **not** require a separate saving in every quotient block.  The exact weak sufficient statement is a *total-height* statement: construct one or several characteristic-zero integers which collectively absorb all bad primes and whose **sum of logarithmic heights** is `o(n)`.  Individual quotient blocks may be terrible.

However, none of the standard modular-form operations listed in the question gives such a compression for free.  The obstruction is not the quotient decomposition.  It is the following fixed-`n`, varying-`p` geometry:

- for every `p` one is testing a different principal-part coordinate of the same weakly holomorphic form;
- those sampled coordinates are pairwise distinct in the range `p > n^(2/3)`;
- a universal low-degree characteristic-zero selector for these independent congruences necessarily has CRT coefficient height `exp((1-o(1))n)`;
- the opposite choice, a product/norm with tiny defining coefficients, has degree about `n/log n`, and its evaluated height is exactly the sum of the sampled coefficient heights;
- fixed-level Hecke traces replace `U_p` by `T_p` at the cost of the `p^3 V_p` branch; for a pole of order `n` this creates pole order `pn`, so any fixed-level valence/Sturm argument is vastly too expensive;
- `U_p` itself keeps the pole small but lives on a `p`-dependent correspondence/raised level, so there is no single fixed arithmetic surface or fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module on which all of the pole drops become one divisor;
- Borcherds products and arithmetic norms add divisor data; they do not turn the self-modular condition `p | (the p-dependent coefficient)` into a common rational prime divisor of a small integer.

There is also a sharp structural countermodel.  The standard integral Hauptmodul/Faber basis on `Gamma_1(6)` lets one prescribe the negative principal part of an integral weight-4 weakly holomorphic form arbitrarily.  One can therefore make the sampled coefficient at

```text
m_p = p * floor(n/p)
```

equal to `p` for **every** `p` in the top range.  That form has the same weight, level, integral `q`-expansion, pole order, `U_p` pole-drop mechanism, Hecke identities, and valence formalism, but its bad-prime log mass is

```text
theta(n) - theta(n^(2/3)) = n + o(n).
```

Thus no argument using only the generic weakly-holomorphic/Hecke/valence package can prove the desired `o(n)`.  A successful modular proof must use a **special cross-coordinate relation satisfied by the actual Beukers form `Omega_n`**.

The minimal missing theorem is therefore not a per-block `TQ_eta`.  It is an **aggregate horizontal compression theorem** for the actual Beukers principal-part vector.  One clean version is given in Section 11 below.

# 1. Exact principal-part formulation

Write the integral `q`-expansion at infinity as

$$
\Omega_n(q)=\sum_{m\ge -n} a_n(m)q^m,
\qquad a_n(m)\in\mathbf Z,
$$

with `a_n(-n) != 0` because the pole order is exactly `n`.

For `p in P_n`, put

$$
q_p=\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor,
\qquad
r_p=n-pq_p,
\qquad
m_p=pq_p=n-r_p.
\tag{1.1}
$$

Then

$$
1\le q_p\le n^{1/3},
\qquad 0\le r_p<p,
\qquad \frac n2\le m_p\le n.
\tag{1.2}
$$

The last inequality follows from `floor(x) >= x/2` for `x>=1`.

The `q`-series operator is

$$
U_p\Omega_n
 =\sum_j a_n(pj)q^j.
\tag{1.3}
$$

The most negative surviving exponent is `-q_p`, and its coefficient is

$$
c_{n,p}:=[q^{-q_p}]U_p\Omega_n
=a_n(-pq_p)=a_n(-m_p).
\tag{1.4}
$$

By the assumed Beukers congruence,

$$
c_{n,p}\equiv b_{r_p}\pmod p.
\tag{1.5}
$$

Hence the target top-range radical is

$$
R_n
:=\prod_{\substack{p\in P_n\\p\mid b_{r_p}}}p
=\prod_{\substack{p\in P_n\\p\mid c_{n,p}}}p,
\tag{1.6}
$$

and the desired statement in this range is

$$
\log R_n=o(n).
\tag{1.7}
$$

The modular question is therefore exact: can the diagonal family of congruences

$$
a_n(-m_p)\equiv0\pmod p
\tag{1.8}
$$

be compressed into a characteristic-zero object of subexponential height?

# 2. The sampled coordinates are pairwise distinct

This simple fact is the first quantifier obstruction.

## Lemma 2.1

If `p,p' in P_n` and

$$
pq_p=p'q_{p'},
$$

then `p=p'`.

### Proof

Suppose `p != p'`.  Since `p` is prime and divides `p' q_{p'}`, it must divide `q_{p'}`.  But

$$
1\le q_{p'}\le n^{1/3}<n^{2/3}<p,
$$

which is impossible.  Hence `p=p'`.  QED.

Thus the bad-prime conditions inspect pairwise distinct coordinates of the principal-part vector

$$
v_n=(a_n(-1),\ldots,a_n(-n))\in\mathbf Z^n.
\tag{2.1}
$$

If `e_m^*` denotes coordinate extraction, then

$$
c_{n,p}=e_{m_p}^*(v_n),
\qquad m_p\ne m_{p'}\quad(p\ne p').
\tag{2.2}
$$

This is why a generic trace or valence argument has no automatic cross-prime coupling: different primes are not repeatedly testing one scalar.

# 3. What is actually sufficient: an aggregate height theorem

Let the quotient block be

$$
I_{n,q}
=\left\{p\in P_n:
\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor=q\right\}
=\left(\frac n{q+1},\frac nq\right]\cap P_n.
\tag{3.1}
$$

Here `1 <= q <= n^(1/3)`.

A per-block power saving is much stronger than necessary.

## Proposition 3.1 — exact aggregate witness criterion

Suppose that for each `q` there is a nonzero integer `W_{n,q}` such that

$$
p\mid c_{n,p},\ p\in I_{n,q}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid W_{n,q}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

Then

$$
R_n\mid \prod_q W_{n,q},
$$

and therefore

$$
\log R_n
\le \sum_q \log|W_{n,q}|.
\tag{3.3}
$$

Consequently the **single aggregate condition**

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{q\le n^{1/3}}\log|W_{n,q}|=o(n)
}
\tag{AGG}
$$

is sufficient.

There is no requirement that an individual block satisfy any uniform saving.  A sparse set of blocks may contribute `Theta(n^0.99)` or worse, provided the total is `o(n)`.

An even cleaner sufficient theorem is a single scalar:

$$
\boxed{
W_n\in\mathbf Z\setminus\{0\},\qquad
R_n\mid W_n,\qquad
\log|W_n|=o(n).
}
\tag{3.4}
$$

The rest of the audit asks whether standard modular operations naturally manufacture (3.4) or (AGG).

# 4. Exact CRT aggregation: it works arithmetically and misses height by a linear amount

For a block `I=I_{n,q}`, define

$$
P_{n,q}=\prod_{p\in I_{n,q}}p.
\tag{4.1}
$$

Consider the linear CRT scalar

$$
C_{n,q}
=\sum_{p\in I_{n,q}}
\frac{P_{n,q}}p\,c_{n,p}.
\tag{4.2}
$$

If `p in I_{n,q}` is bad, then modulo `p` every summand with index `ell != p` vanishes because `p | P_{n,q}/ell`, while the `p`-summand vanishes because `p | c_{n,p}`.  Thus

$$
p\mid C_{n,q}
\qquad(p\text{ bad in block }q).
\tag{4.3}
$$

If `C_{n,q}=0`, set

$$
\widetilde C_{n,q}=C_{n,q}+P_{n,q}=P_{n,q};
$$

otherwise set `\widetilde C_{n,q}=C_{n,q}`.  Then `\widetilde C_{n,q}` is always nonzero and still absorbs every bad prime in the block.

This is already an exact aggregate construction.  Its problem is height.

The defining coefficient height is at least

$$
\max_{p\in I_{n,q}}\frac{P_{n,q}}p,
$$

so, except for a one-prime block,

$$
\log H_{\rm coeff}(C_{n,q})
=\log P_{n,q}+O(\log n).
\tag{4.4}
$$

Across all blocks,

$$
\sum_q\log P_{n,q}
=\sum_{n^{2/3}<p\le n}\log p
=\vartheta(n)-\vartheta(n^{2/3})
=n+o(n).
\tag{4.5}
$$

Thus the completely formal CRT aggregation lands exactly at linear logarithmic height.  It does not matter whether we aggregate first by `q` or all at once.

Globally, with

$$
P_n^{\rm all}=\prod_{p\in P_n}p,
$$

the scalar

$$
C_n^{\rm CRT}
=\sum_{p\in P_n}\frac{P_n^{\rm all}}p c_{n,p}
\tag{4.6}
$$

absorbs every bad prime, but

$$
\log P_n^{\rm all}=n+o(n).
\tag{4.7}
$$

No quotient-block theorem was used.  The obstruction is pure CRT height.

# 5. Sharp finite-order selector no-go

The previous calculation has an exact polynomial generalization.  This is the cleanest scoped obstruction to traces, bounded determinants, bounded resultants, and finite-order arithmetic-intersection sections that are supposed to work **formally**, without using special relations among the actual coordinates of `Omega_n`.

Let

$$
S=P_n,
\qquad
A=\mathbf Z[X_p:p\in S].
$$

For each prime `p`, the universal condition

```text
p divides the selected coordinate X_p
```

is the ideal

$$
(p,X_p)\subset A.
$$

A polynomial certificate which is automatically divisible by every selected bad prime satisfies

$$
F\in\bigcap_{p\in S}(p,X_p).
\tag{5.1}
$$

## Theorem 5.1 — degree/height selector barrier

Let `0 != F in Z[X_p : p in S]` have total degree `d` and satisfy (5.1).  Let `H(F)` be the maximum absolute value of its integer coefficients.  Then

$$
\boxed{
\log H(F)
\ge
\sum_{p\in S}\log p-d\log n.
}
\tag{5.2}
$$

Hence, since

$$
\sum_{p\in S}\log p=n+o(n),
$$

we have

$$
d=o(n/\log n)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\log H(F)\ge n-o(n).
\tag{5.3}
$$

Conversely, a certificate with

$$
\log H(F)=o(n)
$$

must have

$$
\boxed{
d\ge(1-o(1))\frac n{\log n}.}
\tag{5.4}
$$

### Proof

Take a nonzero monomial coefficient

$$
c_\alpha X^\alpha,
\qquad
X^\alpha=\prod_p X_p^{\alpha_p}.
$$

Fix `p` with `alpha_p=0`.  Reducing `F` modulo `p`, condition

$$
F\in(p,X_p)
$$

says that every monomial not containing `X_p` has coefficient divisible by `p`.  Therefore

$$
p\mid c_\alpha
\qquad\text{for every }p\notin\operatorname{supp}(\alpha).
$$

So

$$
|c_\alpha|
\ge\prod_{p\notin\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)}p.
$$

A degree-`d` monomial involves at most `d` distinct variables, whence

$$
\log|c_\alpha|
\ge
\sum_{p\in S}\log p
-\sum_{p\in\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)}\log p
\ge
\sum_{p\in S}\log p-d\log n.
$$

QED.

### Sharpness

The product

$$
F_\times=\prod_{p\in S}X_p
\tag{5.5}
$$

has coefficient height `1` and degree `|S| ~ n/log n`.  Thus the theorem captures the exact generic dichotomy:

- **low degree** => CRT coefficient height `exp(Theta(n))`;
- **low coefficient height** => degree essentially the number of primes.

### Semantic version

Suppose one only assumes the functional implication

$$
x_p\equiv0\pmod p
\Longrightarrow
F(x)\equiv0\pmod p
\tag{5.6}
$$

for every residue vector `x`, rather than the ideal condition (5.1).  If the degree of `F` in every variable is `< p`, then finite-field polynomial uniqueness gives

$$
F\bmod p\in(X_p).
$$

In particular every fixed-order/bounded-degree construction satisfies the ideal condition for all sufficiently large `n`, because every prime here is `>n^(2/3)`.

So Theorem 5.1 is a genuine no-go for natural finite-order universal traces/determinants/resultants.  It is **not** a no-go for a polynomial identity that holds only on the special Beukers vector `v_n`.

That distinction is precisely where a successful proof could live.

# 6. Literal products and coefficient norms

Because every `c_{n,p}` is already a rational integer, there is no nontrivial field norm across varying primes.  The primes are not Galois embeddings of one algebraic number.  A “norm over `p`” is simply a product.

If every sampled coefficient is nonzero, define

$$
C_n^\times=\prod_{p\in P_n}c_{n,p}.
\tag{6.1}
$$

Then

$$
R_n\mid C_n^\times,
$$

and the height is exactly

$$
\boxed{
\log|C_n^\times|
=\sum_{p\in P_n}\log|c_{n,p}|.
}
\tag{6.2}
$$

If some `c_{n,p}=0`, replace that factor by `p`:

$$
d_{n,p}=
\begin{cases}
|c_{n,p}|,&c_{n,p}\ne0,\\
p,&c_{n,p}=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
C_n^{\times,*}=\prod_{p\in P_n}d_{n,p}.
\tag{6.3}
$$

Then `C_n^{\times,*}>0`, every bad prime divides it, and

$$
\log C_n^{\times,*}
=
\sum_{c_{n,p}\ne0}\log|c_{n,p}|
+
\sum_{c_{n,p}=0}\log p.
\tag{6.4}
$$

Therefore the product route closes the problem **if and only if one proves a genuinely aggregate sampled-coefficient height theorem** of the form

$$
\sum_{c_{n,p}\ne0}\log^+|c_{n,p}|
+
\sum_{c_{n,p}=0}\log p
=o(n).
\tag{6.5}
$$

Since

$$
|P_n|\sim\frac n{\log n},
$$

(6.5) would, in the nonzero case, require the mean sampled logarithmic coefficient size to be

$$
o(\log n).
\tag{6.6}
$$

That is a very strong new theorem.  Generic weakly-holomorphic coefficient bounds of exponential-in-pole-order type do not come remotely close: even an individual estimate

$$
\log^+|c_{n,p}|=O(n)
$$

would only give

$$
\log|C_n^\times|=O(n^2/\log n).
$$

The important logical point is that one cannot prove an `Omega(n)` **lower** bound for the actual product without information on the actual `c_{n,p}`.  The product route is not formally impossible; rather, making it subexponential is itself an Apéry-specific average coefficient-height theorem.

A related tautological construction is

$$
\gcd\left(P_n^{\rm all},\prod_p c_{n,p}\right),
\tag{6.7}
$$

which contains `R_n` but also contains cross-prime false positives (`p` may divide `c_{n,ell}` for `ell != p`).  Its only automatic height bound is

$$
\le P_n^{\rm all}=\exp(n+o(n)),
$$

again exactly linear.

# 7. Hecke algebra audit

## 7.1 Exact extraction by `T_p`

For `p not dividing 6`, on a diamond-character component of weight `4`, the usual Hecke formula is

$$
T_p=U_p+\chi(p)p^3V_p,
\qquad
(V_pf)(q)=f(q^p).
\tag{7.1}
$$

Equivalently, if `f=sum a(m)q^m`,

$$
[q^j]T_pf
=a(pj)+\chi(p)p^3 a(j/p),
\tag{7.2}
$$

with the second term interpreted as zero when `p` does not divide `j`.  On all of `M_4(Gamma_1(6))` one may state the same formula componentwise using the diamond operator.

Now

$$
1\le q_p<p
$$

in the present range.  Hence `p` does not divide `q_p`, and the `V_p` term does not contribute at exponent `-q_p`.  Therefore the target coefficient is visible in characteristic zero:

$$
\boxed{
[q^{-q_p}]T_p\Omega_n
=[q^{-q_p}]U_p\Omega_n
=c_{n,p}.
}
\tag{7.3}
$$

This is the strongest positive Hecke fact in the audit.  We do **not** need to replace `U_p` by `T_p` only modulo `p` at that particular coefficient.

But it still does not aggregate the primes, because the extraction index `q_p` varies with `p`.

## 7.2 Same-`q` block trace

For one quotient block, all `q_p=q`.  Thus

$$
\mathcal T_{n,q}
=\sum_{p\in I_{n,q}}
\frac{P_{n,q}}p T_p\Omega_n
\tag{7.4}
$$

has

$$
[q^{-q}]\mathcal T_{n,q}
=C_{n,q}
\tag{7.5}
$$

from (4.2).  So the CRT witness has an exact Hecke interpretation.

This makes the obstruction especially transparent: **ordinary Hecke trace algebra really can package a whole block, but the necessary universal coefficients already have total CRT log height `n+o(n)` across the blocks.**

A strong per-block theorem is not logically required.  What would suffice is cancellation/extra divisibility proving

$$
\sum_q\log^+|[q^{-q}]\mathcal T_{n,q}|=o(n).
\tag{7.6}
$$

No standard Hecke relation implies (7.6).

## 7.3 Why a single Hecke operator does not align all blocks

A fixed coefficient extractor `[q^{-j}]` applied to `T_p Omega_n` sees `a_n(-pj)`.  To see the desired `c_{n,p}` one must choose

$$
j=q_p.
$$

Thus a sum

$$
\sum_p\alpha_pT_p\Omega_n
$$

at one fixed Fourier coefficient only sees the right target for primes in one quotient block.  Aligning different `q_p` by multiplying a `q`-series by powers of `q` is not a modular operation.

One may use a family of dual coefficient-extraction functionals, but then one has merely rewritten the aggregate witness problem of Section 3.

## 7.4 Hecke multiplicativity does not multiply the target coefficients

For distinct primes `p,l` away from the level,

$$
T_pT_\ell=T_{p\ell}
\tag{7.7}
$$

(with the standard character conventions).  This relation composes correspondences.  It does **not** say

$$
[q^{-q_p}]T_p\Omega_n
\cdot
[q^{-q_\ell}]T_\ell\Omega_n
$$

is a coefficient of `T_{p\ell} Omega_n`.

For the raw `U` operators,

$$
U_pU_\ell=U_{p\ell}.
\tag{7.8}
$$

But if `p,l > n^(2/3)`, then

$$
p\ell>n,
$$

so `U_{p\ell}\Omega_n` has **no negative principal part at all**.  Thus composing two different top-range `U` operators erases the very pole data one is trying to correlate.

This is an exact, not asymptotic, obstruction to the most naive cross-prime Hecke product.

## 7.5 Fixed-level trace pays a huge pole

The benefit of `T_p` is that it returns to the fixed level.  The cost is the second term in (7.1).

On any diamond component carrying the leading pole `q^{-n}`, the `V_p` branch contains

$$
\chi(p)p^3a_n(-n)q^{-pn}.
\tag{7.9}
$$

Hence that component of `T_p Omega_n` has pole order `pn`.

For one top-range prime,

$$
pn>n^{5/3}.
\tag{7.10}
$$

For a linear combination involving many distinct primes, the exponents `-pn` are distinct, so they cannot cancel across different `p`.  If the combination involves a prime `p>n/2`, its pole order is already `>n^2/2`.

For a product of the fixed-level Hecke forms, pole orders add.  Using

$$
\sum_{p\le n}p\sim\frac{n^2}{2\log n},
$$

a product over all top-range primes has polar complexity on the scale

$$
n\sum_{p\in P_n}p
\asymp \frac{n^3}{\log n}.
\tag{7.11}
$$

So fixed-level Hecke multiplication is dramatically worse than the scalar product of the target coefficients.

## 7.6 There is no fixed finite-dimensional Hecke module of pole order `n`

Let

$$
M_4^!(\Gamma_1(6);\le n)
$$

denote weakly holomorphic weight-4 forms with pole order at most `n` at infinity (and the corresponding allowed cusp condition).  The operator `T_p` does not preserve this finite-dimensional filtered piece: its `V_p` branch reaches pole order `pn`.

Thus the usual finite-dimensional Hecke trace/eigenvalue formalism does not directly apply to the family of negative principal-part selectors.  Projecting back to pole order `n` throws away the `V_p` information by hand and is essentially a `U_p`/principal-part projection, which is again `p`-dependent.

This is a genuine quantifier mismatch with ordinary Hecke trace formulas.

# 8. Sturm bounds and valence

## 8.1 Fixed-level `T_p`: pole clearing is necessarily expensive

The group has fixed finite index

$$
[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z):\Gamma_1(6)]=24.
\tag{8.1}
$$

Suppose a fixed-level weakly holomorphic form has a pole of order `L` at infinity.  To turn it into a holomorphic form on the same fixed modular curve, a holomorphic multiplier must supply at least `L` zeros at that cusp.  By the valence formula, the weight of such a multiplier is `Omega(L)` because the level index is fixed.

For `T_p Omega_n`, `L=pn`.  Hence any same-level holomorphic clearing has weight

$$
\Omega(pn),
\tag{8.2}
$$

and a Sturm test on the resulting holomorphic form has a coefficient cutoff of the same order (up to the fixed level constant).

In particular a single top-range prime costs at least `Omega(n^(5/3))` in this fixed-level trace realization.  A global trace containing a prime `>n/2` costs `Omega(n^2)`.

Thus Sturm does not turn the fixed-level Hecke trace into a small witness.

## 8.2 `U_p` keeps the pole small but the level/correspondence varies

The attractive object is `U_p Omega_n`, whose pole order is only

$$
q_p\le n^{1/3}.
$$

But for `p` prime to the level, `U_p` is naturally associated to the `p`-correspondence / raised-level group (one convenient model is `Gamma_1(6) cap Gamma_0(p)`).  The correspondence degree grows with `p` (in the standard model the index over `Gamma_1(6)` is `p+1`).

Therefore the forms for different `p` do not live as one section of one fixed integral modular curve.  A Sturm theorem at each raised level is again **per-prime**; taking a common level for all `p` makes the level astronomically large.

The standard trace back to level `6` is precisely where the Hecke `V_p` branch reappears.

## 8.3 Valence is vertical-prime local, not an aggregate prime count

Modulo a bad prime `p`, the top coefficient of `U_p Omega_n` vanishes, so the pole at infinity drops from `q_p` to at most `q_p-1`.

Valence then reallocates one unit of divisor degree **inside that one characteristic-`p` fiber**.  It does not charge a common characteristic-zero divisor each time another prime has a pole drop.  The next prime uses another correspondence, another residue characteristic, and another selected principal-part coordinate.

The Faber countermodel in Section 10 makes this failure absolute: one can force a pole drop at every top-range prime while retaining the same weight, level, integrality, and valence formalism.

So a bare valence theorem cannot aggregate the primes.

# 9. Arithmetic intersection/product formula formulation

The clean arithmetic-geometric model is the principal-part lattice.

Let

$$
\mathcal V_n\simeq\mathbf A^n_{\mathbf Z}
$$

have coordinates

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n
$$

corresponding to the negative coefficients `a(-1),...,a(-n)`.

The bad condition at `p` is the vertical hyperplane

$$
\mathcal D_{n,p}:
\quad p=0,\quad X_{m_p}=0.
\tag{9.1}
$$

The desired log radical is the sum of the vertical intersection weights of the Beukers point `v_n` with these **different** divisors:

$$
\log R_n
=\sum_{p\in P_n}
\mathbf 1_{v_n\in\mathcal D_{n,p}}\log p.
\tag{9.2}
$$

A standard product formula becomes useful only after these vertical divisors are cut by one global characteristic-zero section `F`.  If `F` is required to contain every selector hyperplane formally, then

$$
F\in\bigcap_p(p,X_{m_p}),
$$

and Theorem 5.1 applies.  Thus a bounded-degree global section has coefficient height `exp(n-o(n))`.

The alternative global section

$$
F=\prod_{p\in P_n}X_{m_p}
\tag{9.3}
$$

has tiny coefficient height but degree `~n/log n`; evaluating it at `v_n` is exactly the product construction of Section 6.

This is the arithmetic-intersection version of the same dichotomy.  Generic Arakelov/product-formula machinery cannot save the missing factor because the vertical divisors are not fibers of one fixed horizontal divisor.

A successful arithmetic-intersection proof would therefore need a **special horizontal relation cutting the Beukers point**, not a generic Bezout estimate.

# 10. Borcherds products and modular norms

There are two separate issues.

## 10.1 Type mismatch

A Borcherds product turns suitable Fourier coefficients into **divisor multiplicities** on an automorphic variety.  Our condition is instead

$$
p\mid c_{n,p},
$$

where the modulus is the same varying prime that indexes the coefficient selector.

Knowing that a divisor multiplicity is a multiple of `p` does not make a value of the Borcherds product a rational integer divisible by `p`.  One needs an additional arithmetic evaluation/norm theorem to bridge those two notions.

So the ordinary Borcherds divisor formula is not, by itself, a carrier for `R_n`.

## 10.2 Products/norms add the very complexity one wants to remove

If one builds a separate automorphic object for every prime and multiplies or norms them, divisor multiplicities/degrees add.  At the scalar level this is exactly Section 6; at the modular-form level the fixed-level `T_p` realization has the much larger pole cost (7.11).

Similarly, taking a norm from a `p`-raised modular curve back to level `6` multiplies the weight by the degree of the covering/correspondence.  Those degrees grow with `p`.  Taking such norms for all top-range primes therefore has aggregate modular weight at least of polynomial size far beyond `o(n)`.

More importantly, a norm of the **form** does not automatically make the norm divisible by `p` merely because one Fourier coefficient of the source is divisible by `p`.

Hence a Borcherds/norm route needs an additional theorem of exactly the missing kind: an arithmetic evaluation whose local valuation is bounded below by the pole-drop indicator and whose archimedean/global height is `o(n)`.

No standard norm identity supplies that.

# 11. Strong structural countermodel: generic modularity cannot prove the result

This is the sharpest scoped no-go.

The group `Gamma_1(6)` is genus zero.  Beukers's standard eta Hauptmodul may be written

$$
y(\tau)
=\frac{\eta(\tau)^4\eta(6\tau)^8}
       {\eta(2\tau)^8\eta(3\tau)^4}
=q+O(q^2).
\tag{11.1}
$$

Set

$$
t=y^{-1}=q^{-1}+c_0+c_1q+\cdots.
\tag{11.2}
$$

Because the eta quotient is a product with integral exponents,

$$
t\in q^{-1}+\mathbf Z[[q]].
$$

Take the ordinary normalized Eisenstein series

$$
E_4(q)=1+240\sum_{m\ge1}\sigma_3(m)q^m,
\tag{11.3}
$$

which is weight `4` already at level `1`, hence also on `Gamma_1(6)`, with integral coefficients.

For `j>=1`,

$$
F_j=E_4t^j
$$

is an integral weight-4 weakly holomorphic form on `Gamma_1(6)` with

$$
F_j=q^{-j}+\sum_{1\le m<j}\alpha_{j,m}q^{-m}+O(1),
\qquad \alpha_{j,m}\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{11.4}
$$

Therefore the matrix taking the integer coefficients of

$$
\sum_{j=1}^n u_jF_j
$$

to its negative principal-part coefficients is unitriangular over `Z`.

## Proposition 11.1 — arbitrary integral principal parts

For every integer vector

$$
(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\in\mathbf Z^n
$$

there is a unique integer vector `(u_1,...,u_n)` such that

$$
F=\sum_{j=1}^n u_jF_j
$$

has principal part

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{PP}_\infty(F)
=\sum_{m=1}^n d_mq^{-m}.
}
\tag{11.5}
$$

This is just backward substitution in a unitriangular integer matrix.

Now choose

$$
d_{m_p}=p
\qquad(p\in P_n),
\tag{11.6}
$$

which is consistent because the `m_p` are pairwise distinct.  Set all other `d_m=0`, except set `d_n=1` if `n` is not one of the sampled `m_p`; if it is sampled, its assigned value `p` is already nonzero.  Then `F` has pole order exactly `n`.

For every `p in P_n`,

$$
[q^{-q_p}]U_pF=d_{m_p}=p\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{11.7}
$$

Thus **every** top-range prime is a pole-drop prime for this integral weakly holomorphic weight-4 form, and the total log mass is

$$
\sum_{p\in P_n}\log p
=n+o(n).
\tag{11.8}
$$

This countermodel preserves all of the following generic structure:

- weight `4`;
- level `Gamma_1(6)`;
- integral `q`-expansion;
- pole order exactly `n`;
- the `U_p` top-principal-part selector mechanism;
- the usual Hecke identities;
- Sturm and valence theorems;
- the same modular curve and same principal-part lattice.

What it does **not** preserve is the special Beukers/Apéry relation

$$
c_{n,p}\equiv b_{r_p}\pmod p.
$$

That is exactly the point.

> Any proof that treats `Omega_n` merely as an integral weight-4 weakly holomorphic form of pole order `n` is impossible.  The proof must exploit the specific arithmetic formula that singles out the Beukers `Omega_n` inside this completely flexible principal-part lattice.

This is stronger than saying a particular estimate looks too large: it is a formal counterexample to the whole generic modularity/valence strategy.

# 12. Why standard Hecke-eigenvalue/Chebotarev intuition does not transfer automatically

It is tempting to compare this with averaging Hecke eigenvalues `a_p`.  The quantifiers are different.

A usual Hecke/Chebotarev statement studies Frobenius at varying `p` in a **fixed** representation, often modulo a **fixed auxiliary prime** `ell`.

Here the condition is

$$
c_{n,p}\equiv0\pmod p,
\tag{12.1}
$$

where

- the modulus itself varies with the Frobenius prime;
- the selected Fourier coordinate also varies with `p`;
- `Omega_n` is weakly holomorphic and depends on the external parameter `n`;
- the selected coefficient is a negative principal-part coefficient, not a Hecke eigenvalue of a fixed cusp form.

This is a vertical/self-divisibility problem, not an ordinary Chebotarev condition in one fixed finite Galois extension.

Even if one rewrites `c_{n,p}` through a Hecke correspondence, the standard relations in the Hecke algebra do not identify all the residue conditions with one conjugacy condition.

Thus existing equidistribution of Fourier/Hecke eigenvalues of an associated holomorphic form does not by itself bound (12.1).

A genuinely new representation-theoretic route would have to produce a fixed bounded-complexity sheaf/representation whose Frobenius invariant at `p` is zero **if and only if** `c_{n,p}=0 mod p`, with conductor/height controlled uniformly in `n`.  That would be a substantive new theorem, not a formal consequence of modularity.

# 13. The minimal missing theorem

The strong per-block `TQ_eta` proposed previously is not necessary.  Here are three progressively more structural aggregate formulations.

## 13.1 Total block-height compression

For each `q<=n^(1/3)`, construct a nonzero integer `W_{n,q}` satisfying the coverage property (3.2), and prove only

$$
\boxed{
\sum_q\log|W_{n,q}|=o(n).
}
\tag{M1}
$$

No individual block estimate is required.

This is the exact weakest block-organized carrier statement.

## 13.2 Finite aggregate modular functionals

Find integral characteristic-zero functionals

$$
\Lambda_{n,1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n,s_n}
$$

on the actual Beukers principal-part data, and an assignment `j(p)`, such that

$$
\Lambda_{n,j(p)}(\Omega_n)
\equiv
u_{n,p}\,c_{n,p}\pmod p,
\qquad
u_{n,p}\in\mathbf F_p^\times,
\tag{13.1}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^{s_n}
\log^+|\Lambda_{n,j}(\Omega_n)|=o(n).
}
\tag{M2}
$$

Then every bad prime divides one of the `Lambda` values, and their product is the desired subexponential carrier.

Theorem 5.1 shows that such functionals cannot be obtained as universal bounded-degree coordinate selectors with small integer coefficients.  Their congruence (13.1) must use special relations among the principal-part coefficients of the actual `Omega_n`.

## 13.3 One horizontal Beukers syzygy

The cleanest target is a single nonzero scalar

$$
W_n=\Lambda_n(\Omega_n)\in\mathbf Z
$$

such that for every `p in P_n`,

$$
p\mid c_{n,p}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid W_n,
\tag{13.2}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\log|W_n|=o(n).}
\tag{M3}
$$

A useful way this could happen is that one fixed small-height expression in all principal coefficients satisfies, **on the Beukers locus only**,

$$
W_n
\equiv
u_{n,p}a_n(-m_p)\pmod p
\qquad\text{for every }p\in P_n,
\tag{13.3}
$$

with `nu_{n,p}` a unit.

The generic principal-part lattice forbids such simultaneous selectors without CRT height.  Therefore (13.3), if true, would be precisely the sought Apéry-specific cross-prime identity.

# 14. What an arithmetic-intersection theorem would have to say

There is a geometric equivalent of (M2)/(M3) which is genuinely aggregate and not per-block.

Let `s_n` be the section corresponding to the Beukers principal-part vector on an integral model of the relevant principal-part bundle.  Let `D_{n,p}` be the vertical divisor (9.1).  A sufficient theorem is

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in P_n}
 i_p(s_n,D_{n,p})\log p=o(n),
}
\tag{AI}
$$

where

$$
i_p(s_n,D_{n,p})\ge1
$$

whenever the top pole drops.

But (AI) is useful only if one derives it from a **single horizontal object of sublinear Arakelov height**.  Applying generic arithmetic Bezout separately to the varying divisors simply reproduces the linear CRT/product cost.

So the minimal new intersection theorem is:

> the `p`-dependent vertical coordinate hyperplanes met by the Beukers section are restrictions of, or are controlled by, a horizontal divisor/line bundle section whose total Arakelov height is `o(n)`.

The Faber countermodel proves that no such horizontal divisor exists for the entire weakly-holomorphic principal-part space.  It must be special to the Beukers family.

# 15. Sharp classification of the proposed mechanisms

## Products of top coefficients

**Exact:** yes, they absorb all bad primes.

**Height:**

$$
\log|\prod c_{n,p}|=\sum\log|c_{n,p}|.
$$

**Status:** conditional route.  It succeeds if one proves the new aggregate sampled-coefficient height theorem (6.5).  No generic modular bound gives `o(n)`.

---

## Linear traces / CRT combinations

**Exact:** yes; (4.2), (4.6), and the Hecke realization (7.4) are explicit.

**Height:** universal coefficients have total/log height `n+o(n)`; Theorem 5.1 proves this is sharp for bounded-degree formal selectors.

**Status:** generic no-go at the required scale.

---

## Hecke algebra relations

**Exact positive fact:**

$$
[q^{-q_p}]T_p\Omega_n=c_{n,p}.
$$

**Failure:** extraction index varies; `T_pT_l` does not multiply those coefficients; `U_pU_l` has no negative principal part for two top-range primes; fixed-level `T_p` introduces pole order `pn`.

**Status:** standard Hecke algebra does not aggregate the target.

---

## Sturm bounds

**Failure mode:** after fixed-level trace, pole clearing needs weight `Omega(pn)` by valence, hence Sturm length of the same scale.  Staying with `U_p` avoids this pole but gives varying level/correspondence and therefore no one-form simultaneous Sturm test.

**Status:** no aggregate saving from ordinary Sturm theory.

---

## Valence

**Failure mode:** each bad prime gives one pole-drop event in a different characteristic-`p` fiber/correspondence.  Valence is a within-fiber divisor identity and has no mechanism to charge those events to one small characteristic-zero divisor.

**Status:** generic no-go, made rigorous by the Faber countermodel.

---

## Borcherds products

**Failure mode:** Fourier coefficients become divisor multiplicities, while the target is divisibility of a coefficient by its indexing rational prime.  A further arithmetic evaluation theorem is required.  Multiplying separate `p`-objects only adds divisor/height complexity.

**Status:** no automatic carrier; possible only with a genuinely new arithmetic norm/evaluation theorem.

---

## Arithmetic norm / product formula

**Failure mode:** there is no Galois norm over the set of primes; literal coefficient norm is the product of Section 6.  A global section selecting the varying vertical hyperplanes obeys the degree/height barrier of Theorem 5.1 unless it uses special Beukers relations.

**Status:** generic no-go; Apéry-specific horizontal compression remains logically open.

---

# 16. Recommended next theorem to attack

The next modular-form task should **not** be another per-`q` bound and should **not** be a raw Hecke trace estimate.

The most informative exact question is:

> **Beukers principal-part compression (BPC).**  Does the special formula defining `Omega_n` impose an integral relation among its principal-part coefficients that is invisible in the full Faber lattice and that reduces every selector `a_n(-m_p) mod p`, for `p>n^(2/3)`, to one of `n^{o(1)}` characteristic-zero scalars whose total logarithmic height is `o(n)`?

A verifier-ready form is:

```text
Input:
  Omega_n(q) = sum_{m >= -n} a_n(m) q^m in Z((q)),
  P_n = {p prime : n^(2/3) < p <= n},
  q_p = floor(n/p),
  m_p = p*q_p,
  c_{n,p} = a_n(-m_p).

Find:
  nonzero integers W_{n,1},...,W_{n,s(n)}
  and j_n : P_n -> {1,...,s(n)}

such that:
  (1) if p | c_{n,p}, then p | W_{n,j_n(p)};
  (2) sum_j log |W_{n,j}| = o(n).
```

This immediately gives

```text
sum_{p>n^(2/3), p<=n, p|b_{n mod p}} log p = o(n).
```

The key audit constraint is:

```text
The proof of (1) must use a relation special to the actual Beukers Omega_n.
If (1) is required for arbitrary integral principal-part vectors, the
selector degree/height theorem forces either exp((1-o(1))n) coefficient
height or degree ~ n/log n.
```

That is the minimal theorem which would make the modular route genuinely new rather than a reformulation of the target.

# 17. Bottom line

The answer to the strategic question is:

**Yes, an aggregate modular route is conceptually possible and does not need a saving in every quotient block.**  It is enough to produce a family of prime-covering characteristic-zero witnesses with

$$
\sum\log|W|=o(n).
$$

But **no**, the standard trace/Hecke/valence/Sturm/Borcherds/norm package supplies such a witness from the presently stated Beukers hypothesis alone.

The strongest exact no-go is twofold:

1. **Selector degree/height barrier.**  Any universal bounded-degree characteristic-zero expression that detects the independent conditions `p|a_n(-m_p)` has coefficient height

$$
\exp(n-o(n)).
$$

To keep defining coefficient height subexponential one needs degree essentially `n/log n`, i.e. the product regime.

2. **Faber principal-part countermodel.**  Integral weight-4 weakly holomorphic forms on `Gamma_1(6)` can realize arbitrary negative principal parts.  In particular one can impose

$$
a(-m_p)=p
$$

for every top-range prime, giving total pole-drop log mass `n+o(n)` while preserving all generic modular/Hecke/valence structure.

Therefore the missing ingredient is sharply isolated:

$$
\boxed{
\text{an Apéry/Beukers-specific cross-coordinate compression identity,}
}
$$

not a generic modular-form theorem and not necessarily a per-block saving.

If such an identity exists, it should manifest as a horizontal syzygy among the principal-part coefficients of the **specific** `Omega_n`, or equivalently as an arithmetic-intersection carrier of total height `o(n)`.  Without that extra structure, every natural aggregate construction either pays linear CRT height, product degree `~n/log n`, or the much larger pole/weight cost of fixed-level Hecke traces.
