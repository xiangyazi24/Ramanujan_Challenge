# 3. Common affine roots are paid by resultant valuation

Let `S_{h,k}` be the full integral Sylvester matrix, formed using the characteristic-zero degrees

\[
d_h=3(h-1),
\qquad
d_k=3(k-1).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
\det S_{h,k}=\pm R_{h,k}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Fix a shell prime `p`.  Since `h,k<p`, the banked nonvanishing theorem gives

\[
\overline{N_h}\ne0,
\qquad
\overline{N_k}\ne0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[X].
\tag{3.3}
\]

Every distinct affine common root contributes a distinct linear factor to

\[
\gcd(\overline F_h,\overline G_{h,k}).

\[
c_p(h,k)
\le
\deg\gcd(\overline F_h,\overline G_{h,k})
\le
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\ker(\overline S_{h,k}).
\tag{3.4}

Now take the Smith normal form of `S_{h,k}` over `\mathbf Z_p`.  Every invariant factor that vanishes modulo `p` contributes at least one to `v_p(det S_{h,k})`.  Therefore

\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\ker(\overline S_{h,k})
\le v_p(R_{h,k}).
\tag{3.5}

Combining (3.4) and (3.5) proves (V1).

# 4. A self-contained uniform resultant-height bound

# 7. Averaging the exact singular series

For one admissible order type, let

\[

\left(1-\frac{\nu_{\tau,\ell}(h,k)}\ell\right)

\[

\le

\prod_{\ell\mid56hk(h+k)}

Define

\[

\tag{7.3}

\ll g(h)g(k)g(h+k).

\[

# 9. Exact obstruction to a full identity-based closure

## 9.1 Restart does not reduce the separated pair

The exact concatenation identity is

\[
\boxed{
N_{h+k}(X)
=N_{h+1}(X)N_k(X+h)
-(X+h+1)^6N_h(X)N_{k-1}(X+h+1).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

At a common root of `N_h(X)` and `N_k(X+h)`, equation (9.1) forces `N_{h+k}(X)=0`.  This is not a third independent equation; it lies in the ideal generated by the first two.

The corresponding Dodgson/Bezout identity converts a nested pair into the separated pair

```text
N_h(X), N_k(X+h),
```

but it does not iterate as a Euclidean algorithm on that separated pair.  The remaining scalar is exactly `R_{h,k}`.

Nonboundary modular common roots really occur.  Already for `h=k=2`, the two shifted continuants have a common affine root modulo `71`.  Therefore no universal coprimality or boundary-only theorem is possible.

## 9.2 A Wronskian gives no extra equation

For

\[
W_{h,k}(X)
:=N_h(X)\frac d{dX}N_k(X+h)
 -N_h'(X)N_k(X+h),
\]

one has `W_{h,k}(x)=0` automatically whenever both original polynomials vanish.  It detects multiplicity only after the first common-root event has already occurred.  Subresultants can refine `c_p>=2`, but they do not remove the first unit of common-root support that dominates the residual.

## 9.3 Why a second black-box Selberg sieve stops

For fixed `(h,k)`, the additional condition supplied by the resultant is

\[
L_{\tau,0}(r)\mid R_{h,k}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is a **large-divisor condition**, not a congruence condition modulo a fixed small sieve modulus.  The divisors of one prescribed integer `R_{h,k}` have no unconditional level of distribution among those `r` for which the two companion affine forms are prime.

The height inequality

\[
\sum_{p\mid R_{h,k}}v_p(R_{h,k})\log p
\le\log|R_{h,k}|
\]

is exactly the unconditional information used in Section 5.  Applying an ordinary Selberg sieve again would require a new distribution theorem for those large resultant divisors; it cannot be obtained from the first sieve remainder terms.

## 9.4 Quantitative failure in the macroscopic cell

For `H,K\asymp T`, (V6) gives only

\[
W(H,K;T)
\ll_J
\min\{T^4\log T,T^4/(\log T)^2\}
=rac{T^4}{(\log T)^2}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

This is far above the current MA4Z budget.  Thus the new theorem is a strict compression of the moderate-product region, not a proof of the whole joint-large-gap residual.
