# Mellin/spectral viewpoint for Apéry zero sets

The Mellin interpretation is a major structural improvement, but it does not by itself solve either the pointwise bound |Z_p|=O(1) or LGTQ-3. Its role is closer to Katz's trace-function technology: it identifies the correct object whose cancellation must be proved.

## 1. What Z_p means spectrally

For j≠0, the quantity
\[
M(j)=\sum_a \theta_p(a)a^j
\]
is a Mellin transform of the K3 trace function. Thus Z_p is a vanishing set of Mellin coefficients, not an arbitrary sparse set. This rules out treating Z_p only by cardinality estimates.

However, Katz equidistribution concerns normalized complex values of trace functions. The condition M(j)≡0 (mod p) is a much finer p-adic congruence condition. Equidistribution alone does not imply that only O(1) characters hit zero modulo p.

## 2. Why O(1) is difficult

Deligne gives square-root cancellation in the size of M(j), but the desired statement requires anti-concentration modulo p. One would need a theorem of the form: the Mellin transform of this K3 sheaf has only boundedly many zero Fourier coefficients over F_p. No general theorem of this strength is known.

The elliptic supersingular polynomial analogy is misleading: there the Hasse invariant is a single algebraic section whose zero divisor controls supersingular fibers. Here the index j is a character of the multiplicative group, and zeros occur in character space. The K3 H^2 is larger and the relevant Hasse-Witt data has more degrees of freedom.

## 3. Consequence for Stage C

The Mellin formula can rewrite the incidence condition, but it does not immediately create analytic cancellation across primes. The obstruction is that the characters involved vary with p and with n mod p. This is a moving-family trace problem, harder than standard Bombieri-Vinogradov.

The promising route is a hybrid: use the Mellin/K3 description to prove stronger correlation estimates for products of incidences. In particular, one wants bounds for sums of products of Mellin transforms over different primes. This would feed directly into high moments.

## 4. Apéry-specific cancellation

The likely missing ingredient is not generic Deligne cancellation but arithmetic rigidity: Dwork congruences, Frobenius compatibility, and monodromy restrictions on the K3 family. These may force correlations between Mellin zeros that are invisible from |Z_p| alone.

Conclusion: the Mellin viewpoint is the correct representation of the problem, but it is a framework for proving the needed cancellation, not yet a proof of bounded zero sets or Stage C. The decisive theorem would be a p-adic Mellin anti-concentration result for this specific K3 sheaf.
