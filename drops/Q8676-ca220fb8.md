# Root-count and majority-sign audit for the same-sign EPC3 fibre

## Verdict

No.  The bound
\[
 Z(p):=\#\{0\le r<p:B_r\equiv0\pmod p\}\le 3p^{2/3},
\]
together with reflection, absence of consecutive roots, and the high-row
majority rule does **not** imply
\[
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log^3X}\right).
\]

The sharp direct consequence of the current fixed-key parameterization and
these marginal root bounds is
\[
 \boxed{
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)
 \le
 \left(\frac32(2X)^{2/3}+\frac12\right)
 \binom{P_X}{2}
 \ll \frac{X^{8/3}}{\log^2X},
 }
 \tag{1}
\]
uniformly in every same-sign EPC3 key \(\kappa\), where
\[
 P_X=\pi(2X)-\pi(X)\sim \frac{X}{\log X}.
\]
The factor \(X^{2/3}\) is the number of possible folded roots for one
anchor prime; the two free shell primes contribute \(P_X^2\).  The
high-row condition does not supply a division by \(H\).

For the prescribed threshold
\[
 H=\max\left(5,\left\lceil\frac{X}{\log^2(2X)}\right\rceil\right),
\]
the same inputs give only
\[
 \#\{m:R(m)\ge H\}
 \ll X^{5/3}\log X.
 \tag{2}
\]
This controls how many rows are heavy, not how many triples in a fixed key
can lie on one heavy row.

More decisively, there is an explicit scale-by-scale zero-incidence model,
using the actual shell primes and the exact plus-row equations, in which
all the stated inputs hold and
\[
 \boxed{
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}
 \ge \left(\frac16+o(1)\right)
       \frac{X}{\log^3X}.
 }
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus even the desired little-\(o\) conclusion is logically false under the
listed inputs alone.  A new cross-prime three-point correlation estimate is
necessary.

## 1. Exact fixed-key geometry

Let
\[
 \mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\}.
\]
For a prime-sorted same-sign triple, write
\[
 p_1<p_2<p_3,
 \qquad d_1=0,
 \qquad d_i=z_i-z_1,
 \qquad t=z_1,
 \qquad N=m-t.
\]
The current normal form is
\[
 z_i=t+d_i,
 \qquad N-d_i=c_ip_i.
 \tag{4}
\]
On the plus branch,
\[
 u_i=t+d_i,
 \qquad q_i=c_i,
\]
and on the minus branch,
\[
 u_i=-t-d_i-1,
 \qquad q_i=c_i-1.
\]
The regular masks are
\[
 p_i\mid B_{u_i},
 \qquad p_i\nmid B_{q_i}.
 \tag{5}
\]

For a fixed key \((\operatorname{sgn},d_2,d_3)\), fixed translation
\(t\), and fixed first two primes \(p_1,p_2\), the short coefficient
congruence determines at most one legal \(c_1\), hence at most one \(N\).
Then \(N-d_3\) has at most one shell-prime divisor because
\[
 0<N-d_3\le X^2+X-2
\]
and the product of two distinct primes larger than \(X\) exceeds this
range.  Consequently
\[
 (t,p_1,p_2)
\]
determines the entire occurrence, including \(p_3\), whenever it exists.
This determinism is an injection; it is not a smallness estimate.

## 2. Folded-root ledger

Reflection partitions the root set modulo \(p\) into orbits under
\[
 r\longleftrightarrow p-1-r.
\]
Let \(F(p)\) be the number of folded root representatives.  There can be
at most one fixed orbit, at \((p-1)/2\), so
\[
 F(p)\le \frac{Z(p)+1}{2}
 \le \frac32p^{2/3}+\frac12.
 \tag{6}
\]
The no-consecutive-roots fact only gives a bound of order \(p\); once
\(Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}\) is known, it does not improve the exponent or any
logarithmic factor in (6).

It is also useful to record the root-mass parameter
\[
 \lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{Z(p)}p.
\]
The pointwise root cap gives
\[
 \lambda_X
 \le 3\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}p^{-1/3}
 \ll \frac{X^{2/3}}{\log X}.
 \tag{7}
\]
This is only a marginal average over primes.

### Total regular incidences

For one folded root and one sign there are at most \(X-2\) admissible
quotients.  Counting both signs,
\[
 I_X:=\sum_{X\le m<X^2}R(m)
 \le 2(X-2)\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}F(p).
 \tag{8}
\]
Using (6),
\[
 I_X
 \le (X-2)\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}(Z(p)+1)
 \ll \frac{X^{8/3}}{\log X}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, since \(p\le2X\),
\[
 \sum_pF(p)
 \le \frac12\left(\sum_pZ(p)+P_X\right)
 \le X\lambda_X+\frac{P_X}{2},
\]
so
\[
 I_X\le 2(X-2)\left(X\lambda_X+\frac{P_X}{2}\right).
 \tag{10}
\]

If \(\mathcal H_X=\{m:R(m)\ge H\}\), then Markov's inequality gives
\[
 |\mathcal H_X|\le \frac{I_X}{H}.
 \tag{11}
\]
For \(H\asymp X/\log^2X\), equations (9)--(11) yield precisely
\[
 |\mathcal H_X|\ll X^{5/3}\log X.
\]
There is no conclusion here about concentration of EPC3 triples inside a
single row or a single key.

## 3. The strongest direct fixed-key inequality from the root cap

Fix a same-sign key \(\kappa=(\sigma,d_2,d_3)\).  Let
\(\mathcal U_p\) denote its folded root representatives.  For fixed
\(p_1<p_2\), the first two root masks require
\[
 u\in\mathcal U_{p_1},
 \qquad u+\sigma d_2\in\mathcal U_{p_2},
 \tag{12}
\]
up to the harmless sign convention in the minus parameterization.  The map
from \(u\) to the translation \(t\) is injective.  Since
\((t,p_1,p_2)\) determines the occurrence, dropping the third-root,
coefficient-box, ordering, and quotient-nonvanishing filters gives the exact
majorant
\[
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)
 \le
 \sum_{p_1<p_2}
 \#\bigl(\mathcal U_{p_1}
          \cap(\mathcal U_{p_2}-\sigma d_2)\bigr).
 \tag{13}
\]
This is already the relevant cross-prime intersection quantity.  If one is
allowed to use only the cardinalities \(F(p)\), then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)
 &\le \sum_{p_1<p_2}\min(F(p_1),F(p_2))\\
 &\le \left(\frac32(2X)^{2/3}+\frac12\right)inom{P_X}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
\]
By the prime number theorem,
\[
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)
 \le
 \left(\frac34\,2^{2/3}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{X^{8/3}}{\log^2X}.
 \tag{15}
\]
A data-sensitive alternative is
\[
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)
 \le P_X\sum_pF(p)
 \le P_X\left(X\lambda_X+\frac{P_X}{2}\right),
 \tag{16}
\]
which has the same worst-case order after (7) is inserted.

The ratio of the bound (15) to the required scale is
\[
 \frac{X^{8/3}/\log^2X}{X/\log^3X}
 =X^{5/3}\log X\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus the marginal root cap misses the target by a polynomial factor, not
merely by a logarithm.

## 4. Why the majority condition does not manufacture a saving

On a high row the retained majority-sign load is at least
\[
 h:=\left\lceil\frac H2\right\rceil.
\]
Every retained EPC3 occurrence therefore has at least \(h-3\) surplus
retained hits on its row.  A natural double count is to attach an
\(\ell\)-subset of those surplus hits to the occurrence.

For a fixed key, the enriched object is determined by
\[
 (t,p_1,p_2;\text{the }\ell\text{ extra primes}),
\]
because \((t,p_1,p_2)\) already determines the row and \(p_3\).  Hence,
for every \(0\le\ell\le h-3\),
\[
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)inom{h-3}{\ell}
 \le
 F_{\max}\binom{P_X}{2}inom{P_X-3}{\ell},
 \tag{17}
\]
where
\[
 F_{\max}=\max_{p\in\mathcal P_X}F(p).
\]
Therefore
\[
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)
 \le
 F_{\max}\binom{P_X}{2}
 \frac{\binom{P_X-3}{\ell}}
      {\binom{h-3}{\ell}}.
 \tag{18}
\]
Since a row has at most \(P_X\) shell-prime hits, \(h\le P_X\), and the
ratio in (18) is at least one.  Thus \(\ell=0\), namely the original
bound (14), is the best member of this entire surplus-incidence family.
For the prescribed threshold,
\[
 P_X\asymp\frac{X}{\log X},
 \qquad
 h\asymp\frac{X}{2\log^2X},
\]
so for fixed \(\ell\)
\[
 \frac{\binom{P_X-3}{\ell}}
      {\binom{h-3}{\ell}}
 \asymp (2\log X)^\ell.
\]
Every surplus hit actually makes the bound worse by one logarithmic factor.
This is the precise failure of the proposed high-row double count.

Likewise, summing over keys gives
\[
 \sum_\kappa J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)
 =\sum_{m\in\mathcal H_X}\binom{S(m)}3,
\]
where \(S(m)\) is the retained load.  An average over keys cannot upper-bound
the largest key.  Concentration into one key is exactly what the marginal
root data do not exclude.

## 5. Explicit abstract configuration at the forbidden scale

This construction is a model of precisely the stated root-set information;
it is **not** a claim that the actual Apéry sequence has these zero sets.
Its purpose is to prove logical insufficiency of the listed inputs.

Fix a sufficiently large integral dyadic \(X\).  Put
\[
 m_X=2X-2
\]
and take the active shell primes
\[
 \mathcal Q_X=
 \left\{p\text{ prime}:\frac{3X}{2}<p\le2X-4\right\}.
 \tag{19}
\]
For \(p\in\mathcal Q_X\), define
\[
 u_p=m_X-p=2X-2-p,
 \qquad
 v_p=p-1-u_p=2p-2X+1.
 \tag{20}
\]
Then
\[
 2\le u_p<\frac X2,
 \qquad
 X<v_p<p,
 \qquad
 u_p<v_p.
 \tag{21}
\]
Define the root sets by
\[
 \mathcal Z_p=\{u_p,v_p\}
 \quad(p\in\mathcal Q_X),
 \qquad
 \mathcal Z_p=\varnothing
 \quad(p\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\mathcal Q_X).
 \tag{22}
\]

These sets can be realized as finite divisibility masks by defining, for
\(0\le n<2X\),
\[
 \widetilde B_n
 =\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal Q_X\\n\in\mathcal Z_p}}p,
\]
with empty product equal to one, and setting \(\widetilde B_1=5\).
Extend the sequence arbitrarily outside this finite range.  Then, for every
shell prime, divisibility by that prime occurs exactly at the prescribed
root residues.

### Verification of every stated input

1. **Root count.**  For every active prime,
   \[
   Z(p)=2\le3p^{2/3};
   \]
   for every inactive prime, \(Z(p)=0\).

2. **Reflection.**  By construction,
   \[
   v_p=p-1-u_p.
   \]

3. **No consecutive roots.**  In fact
   \[
   v_p-u_p=3p-4X+3>\frac X2+3,
   \]
   so the two roots are very far apart.

4. **Exact regular row equation.**  For every active prime,
   \[
   m_X=p+u_p.
   \]
   Hence this is a plus hit with \(q=1\).  The ranges are valid by (21),
   and
   \[
   p\nmid B_1
   \]
   because \(B_1=5\) and \(p>X>5\).  Since \(u_p<v_p\), \(u_p\) is the
   folded representative and every active hit has plus sign.

5. **High-row majority.**  Let
   \[
   K_X=|\mathcal Q_X|.
   \]
   The prime number theorem gives
   \[
   K_X
   =\pi(2X-4)-\pi(3X/2)
   =\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac{X}{\log X}.
   \tag{23}
   \]
   Thus
   \[
   R_+(m_X)=K_X,
   \qquad R_-(m_X)=0,
   \qquad K_X/H\longrightarrow\infty.
   \]
   The deterministic majority rule retains every one of these hits.

### One key has the forbidden multiplicity

Sort three active primes as
\[
 p_1<p_2<p_3.
\]
Their positive-branch offsets are
\[
 z_i=u_{p_i}=m_X-p_i.
\]
Therefore their key is
\[
 d_2=z_2-z_1=p_1-p_2,
 \qquad
 d_3=z_3-z_1=p_1-p_3.
 \tag{24}
\]
Equivalently, the key is determined by the two prime gaps
\[
 a=p_2-p_1,
 \qquad b=p_3-p_1,
 \qquad 0<a<b<\frac X2.
\]
There are at most
\[
 \binom{\lfloor X/2\rfloor}{2}
 \tag{25}
\]
such gap pairs.  All \(\binom{K_X}{3}\) prime triples occur on the same
retained high row, so pigeonholing (24)--(25) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}
 &\ge
 \frac{\binom{K_X}{3}}
      {\binom{\lfloor X/2\rfloor}{2}}\\
 &=\left(\frac16+o(1)\right)
   \frac{X}{\log^3X}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{26}
\]
This proves (3).

The EPC3 determinism is respected exactly.  For a triple in this model,
\[
 t=z_1=m_X-p_1,
 \qquad N=m_X-t=p_1,
 \qquad c_1=c_2=c_3=1,
\]
and
\[
 N-d_i=p_i.
\]
Thus \((t,p_1,p_2)\) recovers \(p_3\) as the unique shell divisor of
\(N-d_3\), exactly as required by the current consumer.

The construction uses only one folded root per active prime.  Consequently,
no refinement of the numerical cap \(3p^{2/3}\) to \(Cp^\theta\), for any
fixed \(\theta>0\), can by itself rule it out.  Even a uniform constant
root cap would not suffice.

## 6. Adaptive thresholds do not repair the argument

One can of course choose \(H>P_X\), making the high-row set empty and
\(J_{\mathrm{MH}}=0\) vacuously.  That choice is unusable for the pointwise
consumer: every row is then put in the low class, whose deterministic bound
is of size \(H\), and the desired pointwise conclusion requires at least
\[
 H\log X=o(X),
 \qquad\text{that is,}\qquad
 H=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right).
 \tag{27}
\]
For every threshold satisfying (27), the model row (23) is eventually high,
because
\[
 K_X\sim\frac{X}{2\log X}.
\]
Hence the lower bound (26) survives every asymptotically useful adaptive
choice of \(H\).  Raising \(H\) enough to delete the obstruction merely
moves the obstruction into the low-row term.

## 7. The exact new correlation that is missing

The root-count theorem controls each vertical marginal
\[
 |\mathcal Z_p|.
\]
The EPC3 fibre asks for a horizontal, mixed-characteristic three-point
correlation across different primes after the affine row maps are imposed.
The countermodel aligns the single queried root of every prime along
\[
 u_p=2X-2-p.
\]
Nothing in reflection, no-consecutive roots, or \(|\mathcal Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}\)
forbids that alignment.

A successful new input must, at minimum, rule out the following concrete
special case uniformly in the two gaps:
\[
 \begin{split}
 \max_{0<a<b<X/2}
 \#\bigl\{p:\;&3X/2<p,\quad p+b\le2X,\\
 &p,\ p+a,\ p+b\text{ prime},\\
 &p\mid B_{2X-2-p},\\
 &p+a\mid B_{2X-2-p-a},\\
 &p+b\mid B_{2X-2-p-b}
 \bigr\}
 =o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log^3X}\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{28}
\]
The regular quotient condition in this special case is automatic because
all three quotients equal one.  Estimate (28) is a genuine cross-prime
prime-gap/Apéry-zero correlation.  It is not implied by any separate bound
on the three root-set cardinalities.

In the full notation, the required arithmetic information is
anti-concentration of the three-point correlation of the retained signed
offset set on a row,
\[
 \sum_z
 1_{S_\sigma(m)}(z)
 1_{S_\sigma(m)}(z+d_2)
 1_{S_\sigma(m)}(z+d_3),
\]
uniformly after summing over rows and imposing the shifted shell-divisor
condition.  Some theorem coupling different characteristics must make this
quantity smaller than its unrestricted prime-gap scale.  Marginal root
counts, even constant root counts, cannot do so.

## Final conclusion

The unconditional ledger is:
\[
 \lambda_X\ll \frac{X^{2/3}}{\log X},
 \qquad
 I_X\ll\frac{X^{8/3}}{\log X},
 \qquad
 |\mathcal H_X|\ll X^{5/3}\log X,
\]
and, for each fixed same-sign EPC3 key,
\[
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}(\kappa)
 \ll\frac{X^{8/3}}{\log^2X}.
\]
The majority condition supplies no favorable factor of \(H\), and every
useful surplus-hit double count loses powers of \(P_X/H\asymp\log X\).
The explicit one-row reflected-root model gives
\[
 J_{\mathrm{MH}}
 \ge\left(\frac16+o(1)\right)\frac{X}{\log^3X}.
\]
Therefore the target little-\(o\) bound cannot be proved from the stated
inputs.  The missing ingredient is an actual mixed-characteristic
three-prime correlation estimate, already in the quotient-one special case
(28).
