# Q8642 — Selberg/larger-sieve audit of a fixed-label Apéry fibre

## Terminal verdict

A classical Brun–Selberg or larger-sieve argument does **not** prove

\[
J_X=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log^3 X}\right)
\]

from the stated fixed-label geometry and shell-factor uniqueness alone.

For the occurrence-counting convention relevant to the falling-third moment—so that two different translations/rows with the same three primes count twice—the strongest direct unconditional bound forced by the stated data is

\[
\boxed{
J_X\ll_D X\,\pi(X,2X]^2
      \ll_D \frac{X^3}{\log^2 X},
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

where \(|d_2|,|d_3|\le D X\) and the implied constant may depend on the fixed defect-size constant \(D\), but not on the label or on \(X\).

The two logarithms in (0.1) are genuine and elementary: after fixing the translation and two distinct shell primes, the two shifted factorizations determine at most one coefficient pair and hence at most one \(N\); the third shell prime, if it exists, is then unique. A third logarithm does **not** follow from a black-box Selberg or large-sieve step. It would require a new level-of-distribution statement for the sparse pair-CRT locus.

More importantly, no logarithmic saving at all comes from the Apéry-root conditions without quantitative information on the root sets

\[
Z_p:=\{u\bmod p:p\mid B_u\}
\]

and without a mixed-characteristic correlation theorem. The elementary height bound only gives

\[
\#\{p\in(X,2X]:p\mid B_u\}
\ll \frac{X}{\log X},
\]

which is the same order as the total number of shell primes. Treating \(p\mid B_{u_i(t)}\) as a local event of probability \(1/p\), or multiplying three putative root densities, is precisely the circular input that the proposed sieve proof is supposed to establish.

Even granting the optimistic but presently additional factorization estimate

\[
\#\{N:\text{all three shifted shell factorizations hold}\}
\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^3 X},
\]

the unrestricted \(O(X)\) translation range leaves

\[
J_X\ll \frac{X^3}{\log^3 X},
\]

still larger than the required \(o(X/\log^3X)\) by a factor \(X^2\). Thus the missing input is not a refinement of the prime number theorem inside the shell; it is a genuine mixed-characteristic Apéry-root transversality statement.

## 1. Exact fixed-label setup

Put \(d_1=0\). Fix a sign pattern and distinct nonzero defects \(d_2,d_3\) satisfying

\[
|d_2|,|d_3|\le D X,
\qquad
 d_2d_3(d_2-d_3)\ne0.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X:=\{p:X<p\le2X,\ p\text{ prime}\}.
\]

For coordinate \(i\), let \(C_i(X)\) be its exact target-filtered coefficient range. In the current signed charts these are subintervals of

\[
1\le c_i<X;
\]

the negative chart may exclude \(c_i=1\). Only the strict inequality \(c_i<X<p_i\) is needed below.

An occurrence in the fixed fibre has data

\[
(p_1,p_2,p_3,t,N)
\]

with distinct \(p_i\in\mathcal P_X\) and

\[
N-d_i=c_i p_i\ne0,
\qquad c_i\in C_i(X),
\tag{1.1}
\]

and with the signed lower-root conditions

\[
p_i\mid B_{u_i(t)}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The same \(t,N\) serve all three coordinates. The physical row is of the form

\[
m=N+t,
\]

with \(m<X^2\).

For a fixed sign chart, each \(u_i(t)\) is an affine signed function of \(t+d_i\), up to the fixed reflection shift. The lower-root and row inequalities therefore put \(t\) in an interval, or a bounded union of intervals, of total cardinality

\[
|\mathcal T_X(d,s)|\ll_D X.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Likewise (1.1), \(p_i\le2X\), and \(c_i<X\) put \(N\) in an interval of length

\[
L_N\ll_D X^2.
\tag{1.4}
\]

No relation in the frozen setup makes \(t\) a function of \(N\) or makes \(N\) a function of \(t\). Any such injection would be an additional theorem.

## 2. Exact shell-factor uniqueness

### Lemma 2.1 — one shell prime per shifted integer

Let \(0<|n|<2X^2+O(X)\). Suppose

\[
n=cp=c'p'
\]

with distinct primes \(p,p'\in\mathcal P_X\) and coefficients

\[
0<|c|,|c'|<X.
\]

Then no such two representations exist.

### Proof

From \(cp=c'p'\) and \(p\ne p'\), primality gives \(p\mid c'\). But

\[
0<|c'|<X<p,
\]

which is impossible. ∎

Thus, for a fixed \(N\), each nonzero shifted integer \(N-d_i\) selects at most one admissible shell prime \(p_i\). This is the exact uniqueness invoked in the question.

### Lemma 2.2 — one coefficient pair for two fixed shell primes

Fix distinct \(p_1,p_2\in\mathcal P_X\). The equation

\[
c_1p_1-c_2p_2=d_2,
\qquad c_i\in C_i(X),
\tag{2.1}
\]

has at most one coefficient pair \((c_1,c_2)\).

### Proof

If \((c_1,c_2)\) and \((c'_1,c'_2)\) are two solutions, then

\[
(c_1-c'_1)p_1=(c_2-c'_2)p_2.
\]

Since \(p_1\ne p_2\), one has

\[
p_2\mid(c_1-c'_1).
\]

But

\[
|c_1-c'_1|<X<p_2,
\]

so \(c_1=c'_1\), and then \(c_2=c'_2\). ∎

Once \(c_1,c_2\) are fixed, so is

\[
N=c_1p_1=d_2+c_2p_2.
\]

Lemma 2.1 then says that the third shifted integer \(N-d_3\) has at most one admissible shell prime.

### Corollary 2.3 — direct unconditional fibre bound

For a fixed translation \(t\), an occurrence injects into the ordered pair \((p_1,p_2)\), up to an absolute permutation constant if the fibre records an unordered prime triple. Hence

\[
J_X
\ll_D
|\mathcal T_X(d,s)|\,|\mathcal P_X|^2.
\]

By the elementary Brun/Chebyshev upper bound

\[
|\mathcal P_X|\ll \frac{X}{\log X},
\]

and (1.3),

\[
\boxed{
J_X\ll_D\frac{X^3}{\log^2 X}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This proof uses the exact coefficient ranges and shell uniqueness; it does not use a heuristic density or an average over labels.

## 3. What happens if the fibre forgets translation multiplicity

There are two different counting conventions, and they must not be conflated.

If a projected fibre records only the prime triple, or equivalently only the corresponding \(N\), and identifies all translations producing the same prime triple, then Lemma 2.2 gives

\[
\boxed{
J_X^{\mathrm{proj}}\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^2 X}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

However, the falling-third moment is

\[
F_3(X)=\sum_{m<X^2}(R_X^\dagger(m))_3.
\]

If one prime triple occurs at two different translations \(t\), it occurs at two different rows \(m=N+t\) and contributes twice to \(F_3\). Therefore the occurrence-counting bound (2.2), not the projected bound (3.1), is the safe one for the accepted full-defect counting inequality unless a separate theorem proves uniqueness of \(t\) for every fixed prime triple and label.

The stated shell-factor uniqueness proves uniqueness of \(p_i\) **after \(N\) is fixed**. It does not prove uniqueness of \(N\) after \(t\) is fixed and does not prove uniqueness of \(t\) after the primes are fixed.

## 4. The first two factorization logarithms are unconditional

For \(i=1,2,3\), define the factorization indicator

\[
A_i(N):=
\mathbf 1_{\{N-d_i=c_ip_i,\ c_i\in C_i(X),\ p_i\in\mathcal P_X\}}.
\]

Lemma 2.1 makes this a \(0/1\) indicator.

For one coordinate,

\[
\sum_N A_i(N)
\le |C_i(X)|\,|\mathcal P_X|
\ll \frac{X^2}{\log X}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For two coordinates, Lemma 2.2 gives

\[
\sum_N A_1(N)A_2(N)
\le |\mathcal P_X|(|\mathcal P_X|-1)
\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^2 X}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since \(A_3\le1\),

\[
\sum_N A_1(N)A_2(N)A_3(N)
\le
\sum_N A_1(N)A_2(N)
\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^2 X}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus two logarithms are genuinely available from prime counting and exact pair uniqueness. A third one is a correlation question, not a formal consequence of adding the third indicator.

## 5. Why the direct three-fold Selberg expansion does not supply the third logarithm

Ignore the Apéry-root conditions and expand the three factorization indicators over shell primes. For distinct \(p_1,p_2,p_3\), the congruences are

\[
N\equiv d_i\pmod{p_i}.
\]

Their combined modulus is

\[
p_1p_2p_3>X^3,
\]

whereas the \(N\)-interval has length \(L_N\ll X^2\). Therefore a fixed prime triple contributes at most one \(N\).

The crude interval estimate is

\[
\#\{N\in I_N:N\equiv d_i\pmod{p_i}\}
\le
\frac{L_N}{p_1p_2p_3}+1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Summing the main terms gives the desired-looking quantity

\[
L_N
\left(\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac1p\right)^3
\ll
\frac{X^2}{\log^3 X}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

But summing the \(+1\) remainders gives

\[
|\mathcal P_X|^3
\ll
\frac{X^3}{\log^3 X},
\tag{5.3}
\]

which is larger than (5.2) by a factor \(X\). Positivity prevents a Brun or Selberg upper-bound weight from cancelling these remainders automatically.

This is also the sieve-level obstruction. The ambient interval has length \(X^2\), so the natural level is at most \(X^2\). The product of three shell moduli is \(\asymp X^3\), beyond that level. Pair products are \(\asymp X^2\), exactly where the direct pair-uniqueness argument works; the third shell modulus crosses the available level.

Consequently, a bound

\[
\sum_N A_1(N)A_2(N)A_3(N)
\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^3X}
\tag{5.4}
\]

would itself be a new distribution statement for the short CRT representatives. It is not the formal output of the fundamental lemma of sieve theory.

A precise additional gate would be:

> **CRT3 level-of-distribution gate.** Uniformly in the admissible nondegenerate defects and coefficient boxes, only \(O(X^2/\log^3X)\) distinct shell-prime triples have their unique CRT representative inside the physical \(N\)-window and inside all three coefficient boxes.

The current uniqueness theorem says “at most one representative per prime triple”; it does not say that only a proportion \(O(1/X)\) of the prime triples land in the physical window.

## 6. An additive large-sieve calculation gives no relative saving

Let

\[
\mathcal N_{12}:=\{N:A_1(N)A_2(N)=1\},
\qquad M:=|\mathcal N_{12}|.
\]

By (4.2),

\[
M\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^2X}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For \(p\in\mathcal P_X\), put

\[
A_p:=\#\{N\in\mathcal N_{12}:N\equiv d_3\pmod p\}
      =\frac{M}{p}+\Delta_p.
\]

The standard additive large-sieve inequality for a set in an interval of length \(L_N\ll X^2\) gives

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}p|\Delta_p|^2
\ll (L_N+X^2)M
\ll X^2M.
\tag{6.2}
\]

By Cauchy–Schwarz and

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac1p\ll\frac1{\log X},
\]

one obtains

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}|\Delta_p|
\ll
X\sqrt{\frac{M}{\log X}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore the large-sieve estimate for the third factorization event is at best

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}A_p
\ll
\frac{M}{\log X}
+
X\sqrt{\frac{M}{\log X}}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

At the largest value allowed by (6.1), the error term is

\[
\ll \frac{X^2}{\log^{3/2}X},
\]

which is larger than the trivial bound

\[
\sum_N A_1(N)A_2(N)A_3(N)\le M
\ll\frac{X^2}{\log^2X}.
\]

More generally, the error in (6.4) is \(o(M)\) only if

\[
M\gg\frac{X^2}{\log X},
\]

whereas the pair locus satisfies the opposite bound \(M\ll X^2/\log^2X\). Thus the ordinary large sieve is operating below the density at which it can produce a relative gain.

This calculation independently confirms that the third logarithm is not available from a black-box large-sieve step on the pair locus.

## 7. The larger-sieve quantifier mismatch

Gallagher’s larger sieve bounds a set when **every element** of that set lies in a small number of residue classes modulo **each modulus in a common family**.

That is not the present situation. A triple-factorization point \(N\) comes with one selected third prime \(p_3(N)\) and satisfies

\[
N\equiv d_3\pmod{p_3(N)}.
\]

For a different shell prime \(q\ne p_3(N)\), there is no restriction on \(N\bmod q\). Thus it is false to insert the local parameter

\[
\nu(q)=1
\]

for every shell prime \(q\). Each point is restricted modulo one varying modulus, not modulo all shell moduli.

Equivalently, the triple locus is a disjoint union of pieces

\[
\mathcal N_{123}=\bigsqcup_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
   \{N\in\mathcal N_{12}:N\equiv d_3\pmod p\},
\]

where disjointness follows from shell-factor uniqueness. Applying a larger sieve separately to a piece with only one effective modulus gives no saving, while applying it to the union as though all congruences held simultaneously reverses the quantifiers.

The same error appears in proposed larger-sieve arguments on the translation variable \(t\): an occurrence uses its own three primes, so the full fibre is not constrained by the root residue sets for every prime in the shell.

## 8. The Apéry-root conditions supply no logarithm without root-density input

For a prime \(p\), write

\[
Z_p:=\{0\le u<p:p\mid B_u\}.
\]

For a fixed prime triple, the admissible translations are

\[
\mathcal T(p_1,p_2,p_3)
:=
\{t\in\mathcal T_X(d,s):u_i(t)\in Z_{p_i}	ext{ for }i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Without a quantitative theorem on \(Z_p\), the only uniform conclusion is

\[
|\mathcal T(p_1,p_2,p_3)|\ll_D X.
\tag{8.2}
\]

A larger sieve cannot improve (8.2) unless one supplies bounds on the number of allowed residue classes. In the notation of a larger-sieve proof, those bounds are exactly \(|Z_p|\), or affine copies thereof. Taking

\[
|Z_p|\asymp1,
\qquad
|Z_p|\ll p^\theta,
\qquad
\text{or}\qquad
|Z_p|/p\asymp1/p
\]

is root-density input, not a consequence of shell-factor uniqueness.

There is also no automatic multiplication of three local densities. Even if each \(|Z_{p_i}|\) were small, affine copies of the three root sets can align on the \(O(X)\)-length translation interval. A product bound requires a mixed-characteristic correlation theorem.

### Height gives no logarithmic prime-aspect saving

The elementary Apéry height estimate is

\[
\log B_u=O(u)=O(X)
\qquad(u=O(X)).
\]

Hence

\[
\#\{p\in\mathcal P_X:p\mid B_u\}\log X
\le\log B_u=O(X),
\]

so

\[
\#\{p\in\mathcal P_X:p\mid B_u\}
\ll\frac{X}{\log X}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

But

\[
|\mathcal P_X|\asymp\frac{X}{\log X}.
\]

Thus (8.3) permits a fixed Apéry value to be divisible by a positive proportion of all shell primes. It gives no factor \(1/\log X\) beyond the prime count already present.

The no-consecutive-zero theorem, if inserted, gives at most a constant-density restriction on \(Z_p\). A constant-density loss is not a logarithmic saving and cannot yield the target.

### Circular local-density substitutions

The following proposed steps are not legitimate without new theorems:

1. assigning probability \(1/p\) to \(p\mid B_{u_i(t)}\);
2. replacing the actual root set by a local sieve density \(|Z_p|/p\ll1/p\);
3. multiplying the three quantities \(|Z_{p_i}|/p_i\) across unrelated characteristics;
4. claiming that the unique shell divisor of \(N-d_i\) is uniformly distributed among shell primes;
5. treating the fibre as occupying \(|Z_p|\) residue classes modulo every shell prime, although each occurrence uses only its selected primes.

Each of these is a form of the missing root-density or mixed-characteristic independence statement.

## 9. Comparison with the required fibre bound

The direct unconditional occurrence bound is

\[
J_X\ll\frac{X^3}{\log^2X}.
\]

The required bound is

\[
J_X=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log^3X}\right).
\]

Their ratio is of order

\[
X^2\log X.
\]

Even if a new Selberg/CRT argument supplied the missing third factorization logarithm,

\[
\#\mathcal N_{123}\ll\frac{X^2}{\log^3X},
\]

the unrestricted translation range would give only

\[
J_X\ll X\cdot\frac{X^2}{\log^3X}
      =\frac{X^3}{\log^3X},
\]

still too large by a factor \(X^2\).

If one projects away translation multiplicity, the direct bound is

\[
J_X^{\mathrm{proj}}\ll\frac{X^2}{\log^2X},
\]

which is still larger than \(X/\log^3X\) by a factor \(X\log X\). Moreover, that projection is not valid for the row-wise falling-third moment without a translation-injectivity theorem.

Thus the target is not one logarithm beyond the reach of a standard sieve. It requires a polynomial mixed-root saving in addition to any logarithmic factorization saving.

## 10. The smallest genuinely new inputs

There are two logically separate missing statements.

### Gate A — third-factor CRT distribution

A theorem of the form

\[
\#\{N:A_1(N)A_2(N)A_3(N)=1\}
\ll\frac{X^2}{\log^3X}
\tag{10.1}
\]

would supply the third factorization logarithm. It must control the unique CRT representatives at moduli \(p_1p_2p_3\asymp X^3\) inside an interval of length \(X^2\). This is not supplied by ordinary shell-factor uniqueness or by the standard large sieve at level \(X^2\).

### Gate B — mixed-characteristic Apéry-root transversality

For every \(N\) satisfying the three factorizations, let \(p_i(N)\) be its uniquely assigned shell primes, and put

\[
T_N:=
\#\{t\in\mathcal T_X(d,s):
  p_i(N)\mid B_{u_i(t)}\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}.
\]

The actual fixed-label fibre is

\[
J_X=\sum_{N:A_1A_2A_3=1}T_N.
\tag{10.2}
\]

After the optimistic Gate A bound, a sufficient genuinely smaller root statement is, for some fixed \(\eta>0\),

\[
\sum_{N:A_1A_2A_3=1}T_N
\ll
X^{-1-\eta}
\#\{N:A_1A_2A_3=1\}.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Then (10.1) and (10.3) give

\[
J_X\ll\frac{X^{1-\eta}}{\log^3X}
=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log^3X}\right).
\]

Statement (10.3) is an average mixed-characteristic incidence theorem: most factorization-compatible prime triples must admit no translation, and the surviving translation intersections must be extremely sparse. It cannot be replaced by three separate cardinality bounds on \(Z_p\) unless an additional independence theorem connects them.

One could alternatively prove a direct injection or resultant theorem that bounds the number of \((t,N)\) in each fixed label by \(X^{o(1)}\). That is the density-free fibre hypothesis itself, not a consequence of a generic Selberg sieve.

## 11. Method-scoped no-go

This audit does not exhibit an actual Apéry counterexample to the desired fibre estimate. It proves a scoped no-go for the proposed method:

* exact shell-factor uniqueness gives at most two unconditional prime-counting logarithms;
* the third shifted factor requires distribution of a sparse pair-CRT set beyond the effective large-sieve level;
* the Apéry-root conditions give no logarithmic saving without quantitative root-set information;
* a larger-sieve argument that inserts small local root densities or common residue restrictions reverses the quantifiers or assumes the missing theorem.

Therefore the fixed-label estimate remains a genuine recurrence-aware, mixed-characteristic arithmetic gate. Classical Brun/Selberg/large-sieve technology, applied only to the supplied factorization geometry and uniqueness, does not reach

\[
\boxed{
J_X=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log^3 X}\right).
}

The named crux is:

```text
VARIABLE-MODULUS ROOT CONDITIONS HAVE NO COMMON LARGER-SIEVE DENSITY
```
