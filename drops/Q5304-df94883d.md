# 9. Hasse multiplicity, subresultants, and deflated discriminants

This is exact but too weak.  At the current hard-gcd threshold

\tag{9.4}

Thus (9.3) is weaker than the trivial fact that there are only `O(T)` admissible `h` values.

\tag{9.5}

So high `g` can occur at every physical `h` without violating the complete root-pair/Hasse multiplicity budget.

A deflated discriminant does not repair this.  It can detect that `h` is a multiple root of a parameter polynomial, but a root of multiplicity `t` gives only a **linear** amount of Smith/Hasse multiplicity without an additional valuation theorem. A corank-`g` common factor gives only linear `p`-adic content valuation in `g` under the current Smith argument.  No `20/93` fixed-p saving follows.

# 10. Distinguished-root pointing: exact, but only one root

Suppose `I_x(a,b)=1`, and let
\[
U_0=Y_{\rm sel}^2
\]
be the selected physical `U` root.  Because the square-selector polynomial is squarefree, the common `U`-gcd
\[

=
\gcd\bigl(

\overline E_{a,h},

\[
\boxed{
G_{p,a,b}(U)=(U-U_0)G_{p,a,b}^{\bullet}(U),
\qquad
\deg G_{p,a,b}^{\bullet}=g_p^\square(a,b)-1.}
\tag{10.1}

This is the exact selected-root deflation.

In the `Y` coordinate it removes the two reflected physical lifts `+/-Y_sel`; in the quotient `U` coordinate they are one root.  The large selected reflection displacement proves only that `U_0!=0` and the two `Y` lifts are distinct.  It does not remove any additional formal root.

Equivalently, if one uses divided differences at the selected affine start, every other common root survives the divided differences.  Factoring the selected start and its reflected start lowers the relevant degrees by only `O(1)`.

Most importantly, the factor `G^bullet` remains a **formal projective** object.  Its roots carry no selected zero predicate and no moving-prime lift mask.  Pointing therefore cannot justify replacing `g-1` by an actual-chain count.

One can go one `p`-adic step further and use the selected simple Hensel root, forming combinations such as

After reduction this can impose a condition involving the first `p`-adic quotients `F(u)/p`, `G(u)/p`.  The current selected masks, however, are mod-`p` zero tests; they contain no theorem controlling these first Hensel quotients uniformly.  Using such a condition would insert a new mod-`p^2` hypothesis.  It is not presently available.

Thus distinguished-root pointing is type-correct but gives no power.

# 12. Why the current source does not imply SLCCR

The failure is not a missing manipulation of an existing resultant.

## 12.1 One-column bounds allow saturation

For each clean pair `(r,s)`, the source gives
\[
\#\{a\le2T:N_a(r)=0\}\ll T^{2/3}.
\]
There is no theorem making the intersection of the two zero-level sets smaller by a power merely because `r-s` is long.  Two abstract clean columns may share all `T^(2/3)` allowed levels while respecting the one-column bound.

The incidence-model construction in `gcdtail_result.tex` already shows that row degrees, the full local `L^(2/3)` column estimate, and even codegree one do not force the desired deep decorrelation.  That model is not an Apéry counterexample, but it proves that a new continuant-specific cross-column input is necessary.

## 12.2 Plancherel stops at the same place

The exact fixed-involution Fourier formula in `gcdtail_result.tex` is
\[
m_{d,r}(p)
=
\frac1p\sum_t e_p(-t)\widehat f_d(t)\widehat f_r(t).
\]
After Cauchy--Schwarz, the current column second moment returns `T^(8/3)`.  The source explicitly records that no power saving appears without a new nonzero-frequency/cross-correlation theorem.  SLCCR is the centered, selected, long-clean version of precisely that missing input.

## 12.3 High `g` is not rare from Hasse degree alone

Equations (9.4)--(9.5) show that the complete Hasse/root-pair degree budget permits the hard threshold `g>T^(7/31)` for all `O(T)` values of the moving parameter.  Thus it is invalid to say “large gcd degree is rare” without a genuinely stronger moment.

## 12.4 Scalar resultants cannot replace the common root

The canonical `p=5683` deep counterexample in `gcdtail_result.tex` proves that two scalar resultants may be divisible by the same prime through different roots of the first continuant.  A gcd of scalar resultants therefore cannot prove SLCCR.  The root label in (7.3) must remain aligned.

# 13. Prime-average / selected-mass audit

Suppose an integer resultant, discriminant, or Fitting content `C_{a,b}` satisfies
\[
g_p^\square(a,b)\le v_p(C_{a,b}).
\]
Then
\[
\sum_pv_p(C_{a,b})\log p\le\log|C_{a,b}|
\]
is an **absolute prime average**.  It does not imply
\[
\sum_xw_xv_{p_x}(C_{a,b})
\ll M_0\times\text{average},
\]
because the selected mass may be concentrated on a worst prime.

There are only two legal conversions here:

1. prove a bound uniform for each fixed selected characteristic `p`, then multiply by `w_x` and sum once; or
2. prove a genuinely selected weighted prime theorem whose left side already contains the exact `w_x`.

No lower bound on `M0` is available, so division of an absolute prime sum by `M0` is forbidden.  Nothing in the discriminant/subresultant attacks above changes this normalization obstruction.
