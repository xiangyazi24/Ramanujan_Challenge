The decisive conclusions are:

1. **A nonzero low-height detector is not merely sufficient; it is an exact reformulation of the pointwise shell-radical problem.** If

   ```math
   \mathcal H_{m,X}
   =\{p:X<p\le 2X,\ p\le m,\ m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
   ```

   and

   ```math
   \mathfrak R_{m,X}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal H_{m,X}}p,
   ```

   then every nonzero detector is a multiple of `\mathfrak R_{m,X}`. Hence

   ```math
   K_X(m)\log X
   \le \log \mathfrak R_{m,X}
   \le K_X(m)\log(2X).
   ```

   Thus a detector of height `o(X)` exists exactly when the shell radical itself has logarithm `o(X)`. No determinant can compress below the product of its distinct required prime divisors.

2. There is a trivial universal detector of height `X+o(X)`:

   ```math
   \gcd\!\left(b_m,\prod_{X<p\le2X}p\right).
   ```

   The Lucas congruence makes every low-digit hit divide `b_m`. The prime number theorem gives the height bound `X+o(X)`. The entire challenge is the strict improvement from `O(X)` to `o(X)`.

3. **The best genuine new detector furnished by the current arithmetic is the exact denominator defect.** Write

   ```math
   L_m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,m),
   \qquad D_m=L_m^3,
   \qquad a_m=\frac{\alpha_m}{E_m}
   ```

   in lowest terms, and put

   ```math
   R_m=\frac{D_m}{E_m}.
   ```

   For every relevant shell prime `p>sqrt(m)`, `p<=m`, with `m=qp+r` and `r\in Z_p`, the vector Lucas law implies

   ```math
   p^3a_m\equiv b_r a_q\equiv0\pmod p.
   ```

   Therefore `v_p(E_m)<=2`, while `v_p(D_m)=3`, so

   ```math
   p\mid R_m.
   ```

   Consequently

   ```math
   \gcd\!\left(R_m,\prod_{X<p\le2X}p\right)
   ```

   is a legitimate global detector for the hard lower-digit shell branch. But no sublinear window-height bound for it is known. Proving such a bound is essentially the original problem in denominator language.

4. **The Casoratian does not compress the detector.** After exact denominator clearing, the apparent certificate is literally a large multiple of `b_m`; the small numerator `6` expresses transversality, not divisibility.

5. **Padé and continued-fraction structure determines the raw adjacent determinant, but primitive reduction divides by exactly the unknown contents.** A Padé-theoretic lower bound on the primitive determinant, or an upper bound on those contents, would already solve the conjecture.

6. **Fixed derivatives and differential operators do not help.** Euler derivatives give `m^j b_m`, with the same zero set and the same exponential height. Shifted derivatives produce neighboring coefficients, which are generically nonzero at an Apéry zero. More generally, the order-two recurrence reduces every bounded finite-shift linear construction to

   ```math
   A(m)b_m+B(m)b_{m+1}.
   ```

   A universal expression vanishing on the hit line must have `B=0`, hence is a multiple of `b_m`.

7. **The K3/Kummer–Mellin realization is local and defining-characteristic.** A hit is the reduction modulo a prime above `p` of a cyclotomic Frobenius supertrace, not the vanishing of that characteristic-zero trace. Taking a norm gives a `p`-specific integer of available height `O(p log p)`, and different shell primes live in different coefficient fields and characteristics. It does not yield one `o(X)` global integer.

8. There are genuine low-height detectors for special branches. For example, a central hit

   ```math
   m\bmod p=\frac{p-1}{2}
   ```

   forces

   ```math
   p\mid 2m+1.
   ```

   Thus `2m+1` is a logarithmic-height detector for the center branch. Likewise, a fixed residue branch `m mod p=c` is detected by `m-c`. The obstruction is that the general Apéry zero moves with `p` and is not known to lie on finitely many such low-height algebraic branches.

The definitive assessment is therefore:

> **None of Ideas 1–5 presently yields the requested detector. The exact denominator defect is the strongest source-faithful candidate, but its required height bound is the unresolved shell-radical statement. The promising research direction is not another raw Casoratian or period determinant; it is a theorem decomposing general zero indices into finitely many low-height algebraic branches plus a negligible residual, or an Apéry-specific mechanism that amplifies a single shell hit into a same-prime multi-return certificate.**

## 1. Notation and the minimal-detector lemma

To avoid the ambiguity between `d_n` and `d_n^3`, write

```math
L_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),
\qquad D_n=L_n^3.
```

Let

```math
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
```

For the GCD problem only primes `p<=m` matter: the repository's Casoratian bound implies that, apart from the fixed primes dividing `6`, a prime larger than `m` cannot divide `G_m`. Accordingly define the relevant shell-hit set

```math
\mathcal H_{m,X}
=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X,\ p\le m,\ m\bmod p\in Z_p\}.
```

Put

```math
K_{m,X}=|\mathcal H_{m,X}|,
\qquad
\mathfrak R_{m,X}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal H_{m,X}}p.
```

If one wants to count the formally larger set allowing `p>m`, every argument below remains true after replacing `\mathcal H_{m,X}` by that set, except that the denominator-defect detector in Section 3 only covers `p<=m`. The primorial-gcd detector in Section 2 covers both versions.

### Proposition 1.1: minimal detector

Let `D_{m,X}` be a nonzero integer such that every `p\in\mathcal H_{m,X}` divides `D_{m,X}`. Then

```math
\boxed{\mathfrak R_{m,X}\mid D_{m,X}.}
```

Consequently

```math
\boxed{
K_{m,X}\log X
\le \log\mathfrak R_{m,X}
\le \log|D_{m,X}|.
}
```

Conversely, `\mathfrak R_{m,X}` itself is a detector, and

```math
\log\mathfrak R_{m,X}
\le K_{m,X}\log(2X).
```

#### Proof

The primes in `\mathcal H_{m,X}` are distinct and pairwise coprime. If each divides the nonzero integer `D_{m,X}`, their product divides it. The logarithmic inequalities follow from `X<p<=2X`.

### Corollary 1.2: exact logical status

Uniformly in the relevant rows,

```math
\exists\ D_{m,X}\ne0:
\quad
p\mid D_{m,X}\ \forall p\in\mathcal H_{m,X},
\quad
\log|D_{m,X}|=o(X)
```

is equivalent to

```math
\log\mathfrak R_{m,X}=o(X),
```

and hence to

```math
K_{m,X}=o(X/\log X).
```

The last equivalence uses both inequalities

```math
K_{m,X}\log X
\le\log\mathfrak R_{m,X}
\le K_{m,X}\log(2X).
```

This is the first conceptual boundary: a low-height detector cannot win by numerical cancellation. The divisibility requirement itself imposes the shell radical as a lower bound. A successful auxiliary construction must prove, through its *independent* height estimate, that the radical is small.

The zero integer must be excluded from the definition; otherwise `D_{m,X}=0` is a vacuous detector with no useful logarithmic height.

## 2. The universal `O(X)` detector and the exact missing saving

Let

```math
P_X=\prod_{X<p\le2X}p.
```

For `m<X^2` and `p>X`, write

```math
m=qp+r,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
```

Then `q<X<p`. Gessel's Lucas congruence gives

```math
b_m\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
```

Therefore every low-digit hit `r\in Z_p` implies

```math
p\mid b_m.
```

It follows that

```math
\boxed{
D^{\rm triv}_{m,X}=\gcd(b_m,P_X)
}
```

is a detector, even for the enlarged hit set allowing `p>m`.

The prime number theorem gives

```math
\log P_X
=\vartheta(2X)-\vartheta(X)
=X+o(X).
```

Hence

```math
\log D^{\rm triv}_{m,X}\le X+o(X).
```

This improves the raw height

```math
\log b_m=\Theta(m)=\Theta(X^2)
```

for rows at the natural scale `m\asymp X^2`, but it stops exactly one order short of the target: it is linear, not sublinear, in `X`.

This also gives a useful diagnostic for every proposed detector. If its only a priori estimate is bounded by the whole window primorial, it has not crossed the barrier. It must exploit Apéry-specific anti-alignment to show that only an `o(1)` fraction of the window prime mass survives.

### 2.1 Why modifying the primorial does not automatically help

One can remove from `P_X` primes known to belong to easy channels—for example fixed small residues, fixed quotient classes, or explicit structural branches. This is useful bookkeeping. But after those removals the logarithm of the remaining primorial is still generically `\asymp X` unless one proves that the hard Apéry hit set occupies only `o(X)` logarithmic mass. That statement is the target itself.

## 3. The exact denominator defect: a real detector, but not yet a low-height one

Write the rational Apéry solution in lowest terms as

```math
 a_m=\frac{\alpha_m}{E_m},
 \qquad \gcd(\alpha_m,E_m)=1,
 \qquad E_m>0.
```

Apéry's denominator theorem says

```math
E_m\mid D_m=L_m^3.
```

Define the exact denominator defect

```math
R_m=\frac{D_m}{E_m}\in\mathbf Z_{>0}.
```

This quantity appeared in Q8802 through the exact factorization of `G_m`.

### Proposition 3.1: lower-digit hits divide the denominator defect

Let `p>=7` satisfy

```math
\sqrt m<p\le m,
\qquad
m=qp+r,
\qquad
0\le r<p,
\qquad
r\in Z_p.
```

Then

```math
\boxed{p\mid R_m.}
```

#### Proof

Since `p>\sqrt m`, one has `q<p`. The vector Lucas law proved in the repository is

```math
\bigl(p^3a_{qp+r},b_{qp+r}\bigr)
\equiv b_r(a_q,b_q)\pmod p,
```

where the first coordinate is interpreted in `\mathbf Z_p`. Since `b_r\equiv0`,

```math
p^3a_m\equiv0\pmod p.
```

Thus

```math
v_p(a_m)\ge-2,
```

so the lowest-term denominator satisfies

```math
v_p(E_m)\le2.
```

Because `p\le m` and `p>\sqrt m`, the least-common-multiple envelope has

```math
v_p(D_m)=v_p(L_m^3)=3.
```

Therefore

```math
v_p(R_m)=3-v_p(E_m)\ge1.
```

### Corollary 3.2: canonical denominator detector

The integer

```math
\boxed{
D^{\rm den}_{m,X}
=
\gcd\!\left(R_m,P_X\right)
}
```

is a detector for all relevant lower-digit shell hits.

This is the strongest positive result in the present audit. It is not a formal multiple of `b_m`; it comes from the integral vector local system and records cancellation in the exact denominator of `a_m`.

### 3.1 Why it does not yet solve the height problem

The available global bound is only

```math
\log R_m\le\log D_m=3\psi(m)=\Theta(m).
```

At `m\asymp X^2`, this is `\Theta(X^2)`. Window restriction gives the same universal linear ceiling as before:

```math
\log D^{\rm den}_{m,X}\le\log P_X=X+o(X).
```

No theorem in the current repository proves

```math
\log\gcd(R_m,P_X)=o(X).
```

But this is a precise and attractive residual: it says that the over-clearance of Apéry's denominator has sublinear logarithmic mass in every shell window.

### 3.2 Relation to `gcd(A_m,b_m)=1`

There are two possible meanings of “numerator,” so distinguish them:

- `\alpha_m` is the reduced numerator of `a_m=\alpha_m/E_m`;
- `V_m=D_ma_m=R_m\alpha_m` is the envelope-cleared integer numerator used in the GCD.

If one proves

```math
\gcd(V_m,b_m)=1
```

for every `m`, then

```math
\begin{aligned}
G_m
&=\gcd(V_m,D_mb_m)\\
&=\gcd(V_m,D_m)\\
&=R_m,
\end{aligned}
```

because `V_m=R_m\alpha_m`, `D_m=R_mE_m`, and `\gcd(\alpha_m,E_m)=1`.

Thus global coprimality would identify the entire GCD with the denominator defect. This is structurally valuable, but it does not bound `R_m`. In fact Proposition 3.1 shows that every hard lower-digit shell hit is forced into `R_m`; the shell problem survives intact.

The Casoratian does not prove this coprimality in the shell. If a prime divides both `V_m` and `b_m`, the cleared Casoratian only forces it to divide

```math
\frac{6D_m}{m^3},
```

which is entirely compatible with a shell prime already present in `D_m`. Q8802 sharpens this to neighboring denominator-pole conditions, but no contradiction is known.

### Assessment

- **Divisibility condition:** yes.
- **Current height bound:** only `O(X)` after window restriction.
- **Status:** the best genuine candidate, but its desired height estimate is an exact residual form of the conjecture.

## 4. Idea 1: the Casoratian

The exact Casoratian is

```math
 a_m b_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m=\frac6{m^3}.
```

Write

```math
 a_j=\frac{\alpha_j}{E_j}
```

in lowest terms. Clearing the exact denominators gives

```math
m^3\alpha_mE_{m-1}b_{m-1}
-m^3\alpha_{m-1}E_mb_m
=6E_mE_{m-1}.
```

Rearrange:

```math
\boxed{
 m^3\alpha_mE_{m-1}b_{m-1}-6E_mE_{m-1}
 =m^3\alpha_{m-1}E_m b_m.
}
```

The left side is indeed divisible by every prime dividing `b_m`. But the identity exposes the failure completely: the proposed certificate is *literally* `b_m` multiplied by a nonzero integer.

For `m>=2`, positivity/nonvanishing of the Apéry companion gives

```math
\left|m^3\alpha_{m-1}E_m b_m\right|\ge b_m.
```

The recurrence has dominant characteristic root

```math
\rho=17+12\sqrt2,
```

and standard Poincaré asymptotics give

```math
\log b_m=m\log\rho+O(\log m).
```

Therefore this Casoratian detector has logarithmic height `\Theta(m)`, hence `\Theta(X^2)` on the natural row scale.

Using the common envelope instead of exact denominators yields the equally tautological identity

```math
\boxed{
 m^3(D_ma_m)b_{m-1}-6D_m
 =m^3(D_ma_{m-1})b_m.
}
```

Again the detector is a large multiple of `b_m`.

### 4.1 The residue-level congruence has the wrong sign of information

At a local zero `b_r\equiv0\pmod p`, with `1<=r<=p-2`, the Casoratian gives

```math
 a_r b_{r-1}\equiv\frac6{r^3}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
```

The no-adjacent-zero theorem ensures that `b_{r-1}` is a unit. Thus the Casoratian says that the complementary coordinate `a_r` is a unit after the appropriate normalization. This is a *transversality* statement. It prevents the whole state vector from vanishing; it does not provide a small vanishing invariant.

To turn it into divisibility one subtracts the right side, and the exact identity then restores the missing factor `b_r`.

### 4.2 Why different residues cannot be combined cheaply

For different shell primes, the hit residue

```math
r_p=m\bmod p
```

varies with `p`. A residue-level Casoratian certificate therefore produces a different integer for each prime. Multiplying those certificates cannot beat the shell radical lower bound from Proposition 1.1 and usually incurs much greater height.

### 4.3 Adjacent primitive determinant

Q8802 gives the exact identity

```math
G_mG_{m+1}\,\delta_m=\Omega_m,
```

where `\delta_m` is the determinant of the two primitive adjacent approximation vectors and

```math
\log\Omega_m=6m+o(m).
```

The shell primes are contained in the content quotient, not in the primitive determinant:

```math
\frac{\Omega_m}{|\delta_m|}=G_mG_{m+1}.
```

Thus extracting a low-height detector from this formula requires controlling exactly the quotient whose subexponential size is the conjecture. The determinant reformulation is clean, but not a compression theorem.

### Definitive assessment of Idea 1

- **Raw Casoratian:** numerator `6`; detects nonvanishing, not hits.
- **Cleared Casoratian difference:** detector, but exactly a multiple of `b_m`, height `\Theta(m)`.
- **Primitive adjacent determinant:** divides out the unknown content; recovering it is equivalent to the GCD problem.
- **Verdict:** fails as a low-height detector.

## 5. Idea 2: Padé and generalized continued fractions

The Apéry pair is generated by a second-order polynomial recurrence and can be viewed through Padé/Hermite–Padé approximation and Apéry's generalized continued fraction. This explains three facts:

1. the two solutions share one recurrence;
2. the linear form

   ```math
   b_m\zeta(3)-a_m
   ```

   is exponentially small; and
3. the raw adjacent Casoratian is explicit.

With the factorial gauge

```math
\mathcal A_m=(m!)^3a_m,
\qquad
\mathcal B_m=(m!)^3b_m,
```

the adjacent determinant at indices `m,m+1` is

```math
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{m+1}\mathcal B_m
-\mathcal A_m\mathcal B_{m+1}
=6(m!)^6
}
```

up to the chosen orientation. With the least-common-multiple gauge it becomes `\Omega_m`.

These are large raw determinants. Their logarithmic sizes are respectively

```math
6m\log m+O(m)
```

and

```math
6m+o(m).
```

At `m\asymp X^2`, neither is close to `o(X)`.

### 5.1 Why excellent real approximation does not imply a small integer detector

The small quantity

```math
b_m\zeta(3)-a_m
```

is a real linear form involving an irrational number. It is not a nonzero integer whose prime divisors can be read off. The product formula does not convert its archimedean smallness into divisibility by shell primes without additional algebraic structure, because `\zeta(3)` is not an algebraic number with finitely many conjugate places available for such an argument.

Modulo `p`, the statement `p\mid b_m` says nothing that makes this real linear form vanish or acquire a controlled rational numerator.

### 5.2 Primitive reduction is exactly the unknown integral saturation

Padé theory operates naturally over a field. It determines approximation order and the determinant of a chosen polynomial basis. The primitive integer columns are obtained only after removing their contents. Q8802 gives

```math
\delta_m
=\frac{\Omega_m}{G_mG_{m+1}}.
```

Thus a Padé theorem proving

```math
\log|\delta_m|=6m-o(m)
```

would prove

```math
\log G_m=o(m).
```

This would be an **integral Padé-saturation theorem**, not a consequence of standard Padé approximation. It is essentially the conjecture in a different language.

### Definitive assessment of Idea 2

- **Padé approximation order:** useful for irrationality and archimedean growth.
- **Raw determinant:** explicit but far too large.
- **Primitive determinant:** exactly equivalent to controlling the unknown contents.
- **Verdict:** no low-height detector with current Padé theory.

Primary background includes Apéry's original construction, Cohen's exposition, and the Padé viewpoints surveyed in the repository; see the references at the end.

## 6. Idea 3: modifying `b_m`

This idea separates into several possibilities.

### 6.1 Taking the window radical

The mathematically optimal modification is

```math
\operatorname{rad}_{(X,2X]}(b_m)
=\prod_{\substack{X<p\le2X\\p\mid b_m}}p
=\gcd(b_m,P_X).
```

It removes multiplicities and all factors outside the shell. It has height at most `X+o(X)`. But the hard hit radical is a divisor of this number, and proving its logarithm is `o(X)` is exactly the desired theorem.

This is not a failure of ingenuity: Proposition 1.1 says no detector can have smaller logarithm than the required radical itself.

### 6.2 Dividing by an explicit gcd

Suppose

```math
c_m=\frac{b_m}{\gcd(b_m,Q_m)}.
```

There are two cases.

1. If `Q_m` contains a shell prime `p` only to remove it, then a typical simple factor `p\parallel b_m` disappears from `c_m`, so `c_m` ceases to detect the hit.
2. If `Q_m` is chosen to avoid every hard shell prime, then dividing by it changes only the cofactor; the shell radical and its minimal logarithmic height remain unchanged.

Choosing `Q_m` so that it removes exactly the non-hit factors while preserving exactly the hit factors requires prior knowledge of the target factorization. That is circular.

### 6.3 Using neighboring terms

Quantities such as

```math
\gcd(b_m,b_{m-1}),
\qquad
\gcd(b_m,b_{m+1}),
```

are not detectors. At a genuine first-block zero, the no-adjacent-zero theorem says the neighboring residues are units. The recurrence is deliberately transverse to the zero line.

Products or resultants of several neighboring terms can detect repeated returns of the *same prime*, but a single shell hit supplies no second return.

### 6.4 Using the denominator envelope

The quantity

```math
\gcd(b_m,D_m)
```

detects every prime `p<=m` dividing `b_m`, but its available height is `O(m)`, much worse than the window primorial truncation. Restricting it to `P_X` returns the universal `O(X)` detector.

### 6.5 Would global numerator coprimality help?

As shown in Section 3.2, proving

```math
\gcd(D_ma_m,b_m)=1
```

would imply

```math
G_m=R_m=D_m/E_m.
```

This would eliminate an intrinsic numerator-overlap factor and would be a valuable theorem. It would not prove that the denominator defect has low shell height. Proposition 3.1 shows that every hard hit is already forced into that defect.

### Definitive assessment of Idea 3

- **Best explicit modification:** the window radical/primorial gcd, height `O(X)`.
- **Needed improvement:** exactly the target `o(X)` radical estimate.
- **Coprimality theorem:** useful structural simplification, not a height bound.
- **Verdict:** no noncircular sublinear detector obtained.

## 7. Idea 4: derivative sequences and differential operators

Let

```math
F(t)=\sum_{m\ge0}b_mt^m,
\qquad
\theta=t\frac d{dt}.
```

Then

```math
[t^m]\theta^jF=m^j b_m.
```

For a shell hit, `p\nmid m`: if `p\mid m`, then `m mod p=0`, but `b_0=1`, so it is not a hit. Therefore

```math
p\mid b_m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid m^j b_m
```

for every fixed `j` at the relevant primes.

But

```math
\log|m^j b_m|=\log b_m+O(\log m)=\Theta(m).
```

Euler derivatives preserve the zero set and preserve the exponential height.

### 7.1 Ordinary derivatives shift away from the zero

The coefficient of `F'(t)` at `t^m` is

```math
(m+1)b_{m+1}.
```

At a first-block zero `b_r=0`, the no-adjacent-zero theorem gives

```math
b_{r+1}\ne0\pmod p.
```

Thus ordinary derivatives are generically *nonzero* at a hit. They do not detect the zero.

### 7.2 State-space no-go for bounded linear constructions

Because the coefficient recurrence has order two, every fixed finite-shift linear expression can be reduced, away from its finitely many poles, to

```math
T_m=A(m)b_m+B(m)b_{m+1},
```

with rational functions `A,B` determined by the chosen operator.

The local state space is two-dimensional. The hit locus is the line

```math
b_m=0,
```

and transversality gives `b_{m+1}\ne0` at a first-block hit. A universal local-system expression that vanishes on this entire line must have

```math
B(m)=0.
```

Algebraically, the ideal of the line `x=0` in the state coordinates `(x,y)` is the principal ideal `(x)`. Hence any regular polynomial invariant vanishing for every state with `b_m=0` is a multiple of the `b_m` coordinate.

There is one possible escape: exploit a special arithmetic property of the *distinguished Apéry orbit*, rather than the whole local system, so that `B(m)` itself vanishes modulo every hit prime. But then `B(m)` is already a new global detector. The differential equation alone does not provide it.

### 7.3 The annihilating differential equation

Applying the Picard–Fuchs operator to `F` gives the zero series. This is the forbidden detector `D=0`; it carries no prime-counting information.

Any nonzero remainder obtained by truncation or boundary terms has size governed by the truncation degree and normally retains exponential or factorial height. No bounded-order operator in the current theory produces a nonzero sublinear-height integer divisible by all shell hits.

### 7.4 Parameter derivatives and Hadamard products

For the deformation

```math
b_m(\lambda)
=\sum_k\binom mk^2\binom{m+k}k^2\lambda^k,
```

a zero of `b_m(1)` need not be a zero of

```math
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}b_m(\lambda)\right|_{\lambda=1}.
```

Indeed, transversality heuristics predict the derivative is usually nonzero. Multiplying by another sequence preserves divisibility but increases height; adding or Hadamard-transforming generally loses the zero. A nonlinear gcd construction returns to Idea 3.

### 7.5 Why gap polynomials do not give a single-hit detector

If one prime `p` hits two indices `r` and `r+h`, then the recurrence gives

```math
p\mid N_h(r),
```

where `N_h` is the gap polynomial of degree `3(h-1)`. This is a powerful low-height certificate when `h` is short. It underlies the pairwise codegree results.

But a shell atom consists of one hit at one row for each prime. Cross-prime multiplicity at the same row does not provide two indices for the same prime. This is the repository's certificate-parity obstruction: recurrence certificates begin at two same-prime incidences, while the pointwise atom problem has one incidence per prime.

### Definitive assessment of Idea 4

- **Euler derivatives:** same zeros, same exponential height.
- **Ordinary derivatives:** shift to a nonzero neighbor.
- **Fixed differential operators:** multiple of `b_m`, zero identically, or not a detector.
- **Gap/transfer certificates:** need a second same-prime hit.
- **Verdict:** fails for single shell hits.

## 8. Idea 5: modular forms, K3 periods, and Frobenius matrices

The geometric origin is real and important. The Apéry sequence is a period sequence for the Beukers–Peters K3 family, and the repository constructs the fixed Laurent polynomial

```math
\Lambda(x,y,z)
=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)((1+y)(1+z)+xyz)}{xyz}
```

with

```math
c^{\rm tor}_{p,j}
=-\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}\Lambda(x,y,z)^j
\equiv b_j\pmod p
```

for `0<=j<=p-2`.

This is an exact marked coordinate. It does not immediately globalize to a low-height integer.

### 8.1 The coefficient zero is not one K3-fiber trace

Grouping by the value `a=\Lambda(x,y,z)` shows that `b_j mod p` is a multiplicative Mellin coefficient of the entire fiber-count function. It is not the Frobenius trace of the single K3 fiber with parameter `1/j` or any similarly indexed point.

Therefore a period-matrix determinant at one fiber does not vanish exactly when `b_j` vanishes.

### 8.2 Integral reduction versus characteristic-zero vanishing

The cohomological lift is a cyclotomic integer

```math
\widetilde c^{\rm tor}_{p,j}
=-\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm alt}\!\left(
\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
R\Gamma_c(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{\omega_p^j})
\right).
```

Its reduction at a chosen prime `\mathfrak p|p` equals `b_j mod p`. A hit says

```math
\widetilde c^{\rm tor}_{p,j}\in\mathfrak p,
```

not

```math
\widetilde c^{\rm tor}_{p,j}=0.
```

The repository gives the concrete example

```math
\widetilde c^{\rm tor}_{11,5}=-33\ne0,
\qquad
b_5\equiv0\pmod{11}.
```

Thus ordinary Frobenius trace vanishing, period determinants, and Sato–Tate zero loci are the wrong integral event.

### 8.3 Taking a norm is too expensive and remains prime-specific

One may take

```math
N_{p,j}
=\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbf Q(\mu_{p-1})/\mathbf Q}
 \bigl(\widetilde c^{\rm tor}_{p,j}\bigr).
```

If the lift is nonzero and lies in a prime above `p`, then `p` divides this norm. But:

1. the coefficient-field degree is as large as

   ```math
   \varphi(p-1)=p^{1-o(1)}
   ```

   along many primes;
2. the available bounds for each conjugate are polynomial in `p`, so the resulting a priori estimate is

   ```math
   \log|N_{p,j}|=O(\varphi(p-1)\log p)=O(p\log p);
   ```

3. the norm depends on the individual pair `(p,j)`; and
4. multiplying such local norms over all hits cannot be smaller than the shell radical and normally has far greater height.

Uniform miraculous cancellation in these norms would itself be a new integral anti-concentration theorem. It is not supplied by the K3 realization.

### 8.4 No one-field product across shell primes

Objects at distinct rational primes live in different residue characteristics. There is no single finite field, product variety, or Frobenius element whose trace is the product of all the defining-characteristic hit indicators. Compatible systems identify characteristic polynomials of a fixed motive across auxiliary `\ell`; they do not turn moving mod-`p` Mellin reductions into one global integer of small height.

### 8.5 Period determinant and monodromy

The determinant of a fundamental period matrix is governed by the Wronskian and singular divisor of the Picard–Fuchs equation. It detects degeneration of the local system. A zero of one distinguished Taylor coefficient is not a degeneration: the companion coordinate is nonzero, and the period matrix remains invertible. Thus the period determinant has the same transversality role as the Casoratian.

### 8.6 Hasse polynomial and truncation warning

The polynomial

```math
A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j
```

has geometric factorization properties, but `Z_p` is the set of its zero *coefficients*, not its evaluation roots. The relevant truncation/factor has degree `\Theta(p)`, so even the evaluation-root side has moving complexity. No fixed bounded-degree Hasse divisor detects the coefficient indices.

### Definitive assessment of Idea 5

- **Local marked coordinate:** exact and geometrically bounded-complexity before zero detection.
- **Global integer:** no common one across primes.
- **Norm construction:** available height `O(p log p)` per prime, too large and prime-specific.
- **Period determinant:** detects singularity/transversality, not coefficient zero.
- **Verdict:** no low-height global detector from current K3 or modular-form technology.

## 9. Genuine partial successes: what a detector would have to look like

The negative conclusions above should not obscure several exact positive models.

### 9.1 Central zero branch

Suppose

```math
m\bmod p=\frac{p-1}{2}.
```

Then

```math
2m\equiv-1\pmod p,
```

so

```math
\boxed{p\mid 2m+1.}
```

Therefore

```math
D^{\rm center}_m=2m+1
```

is a detector for every central hit, with

```math
\log|D^{\rm center}_m|=O(\log m)=o(X)
```

when `m<X^2`.

This completely controls the self-reflection branch. Its modular-form interpretation may identify which primes actually occur, but the low-height detector itself is elementary.

### 9.2 Fixed residue branch

For a fixed integer `c`,

```math
m\bmod p=c
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid m-c.
```

Thus `m-c` is a logarithmic-height detector. Moreover, the extra hit condition requires `p|b_c`, so only prime divisors of the fixed nonzero integer `b_c` can occur when `c` is fixed independently of `X`.

### 9.3 Leading-digit branch

If `m=qp+r` and the bad event comes from `p|b_q`, then `p` divides the fixed integer `b_q` for that quotient cell. For small `q`, `b_q` is a low-height detector. This is why fixed or slowly growing quotient classes are much more accessible than the moving low digit.

### 9.4 Same-prime double-return branch

If the same prime hits two nearby indices, a gap polynomial `N_h` provides a certificate of height controlled by `h`. This is the source of the proved pairwise and codegree estimates.

### 9.5 The missing geometry

All successful examples share one feature: the moving pair `(p,r_p)` lies on a fixed low-degree relation with `m`, such as

```math
2m+1\equiv0\pmod p
```

or

```math
m-c\equiv0\pmod p.
```

For a general Apéry zero `r_p`, no finite list of such relations is known.

This suggests the correct global-detector program:

> Decompose the Apéry zero set into finitely many low-height algebraic branches, each giving a polynomial `F_\nu(m)` divisible by its shell primes, and prove that the residual branch has `o(X)` logarithmic mass.

The center is the prototype. Existing K3/monodromy results do not furnish the general branch decomposition.

## 10. A universal local-system no-go theorem

The repeated failures of the Casoratian, derivatives, and period matrices have one common algebraic reason.

Consider a two-dimensional recurrence state

```math
v_m=(b_m,b_{m+1}).
```

Away from singular indices, the recurrence transfer matrix is invertible. The hit condition is the hyperplane

```math
H=\{(x,y):x=0\}.
```

Any polynomial function `F(x,y)` that vanishes on all of `H` belongs to the principal ideal `(x)`. Therefore

```math
F(b_m,b_{m+1})=b_m\,G(b_m,b_{m+1}).
```

The same remains true after adjoining finitely many rational functions of `m`, provided denominators are cleared away from their poles.

Consequently:

> **Any detector obtained universally from the rank-two local system by a bounded algebraic operation and required to vanish for every state on the hit line is a multiple of the hit coordinate.**

It inherits at least the height of `b_m` unless a new arithmetic cancellation special to the distinguished Apéry orbit is proved.

The Casoratian avoids being a multiple only because it does not vanish on the hit line; it becomes a unit relation there. Differential annihilators avoid the conclusion only by vanishing identically. Gap polynomials avoid it by imposing two return conditions, hence codimension two.

This is not a no-go theorem against every imaginable detector. It identifies the required escape hatch precisely: one must use arithmetic information about the distinguished orbit across primes, not merely the local system, its Wronskian, or its Picard–Fuchs equation.

## 12. What would constitute a real advance

The detector viewpoint is valuable because it isolates several sharply stated targets.

### Target A: denominator-defect shell theorem

Prove uniformly for `m<X^2`

```math
\boxed{
\log\operatorname{rad}
\gcd\!\left(
\frac{L_m^3}{E_m},
\prod_{X<p\le2X}p
\right)
=o(X).
}
```

Proposition 3.1 then controls every relevant lower-digit shell hit. This is the most source-faithful detector theorem.

### Target B: finite branch decomposition

Find fixed-complexity relations `F_1,\ldots,F_J` such that, for all but a residual hit set of `o(X)` logarithmic mass,

```math
p\mid F_\nu(m)
```

for some `\nu`, with

```math
\log|F_\nu(m)|=o(X).
```

The center relation `F(m)=2m+1` is the model case.

### Target C: hit amplification

Prove that if one row is hit by many shell primes, then for most of those primes the distinguished Apéry orbit has a second nearby return. Gap-polynomial or aligned-resultant certificates could then charge the primes to integers of controlled height.

The current pairwise theorems run in the opposite direction: they start with two same-prime hits. No theorem presently amplifies cross-prime multiplicity at one row into same-prime multiplicity across rows.

### Target D: integral Padé saturation

Prove directly that the contents removed from consecutive Padé columns have total logarithm `o(m)`, or a shell-local strengthening of that assertion. This is mathematically natural but is essentially Q8802's primitive-determinant reformulation.

### Target E: distinguished-orbit invariant

Construct an algebraic function of the Apéry transfer state that vanishes on the actual Apéry zero states but not on the full projective hit line, and prove its specialization has sublinear height. The universal local-system no-go theorem shows why such an invariant must use arithmetic of the distinguished orbit, not only recurrence algebra.

---

## 13. Final conclusion

There is a simple reason the low-height detector is so powerful and so difficult:

```math
\boxed{
\text{every detector contains the shell radical as a divisor.}
}
```

The obvious `b_m` has excessive archimedean height. Window truncation immediately lowers it to the primorial scale `X+o(X)`, but no general construction lowers it to `o(X)` without proving that the shell radical itself is sparse.

The five proposed mechanisms divide into two classes:

1. **Universal local-system constructions**—Casoratians, derivatives, period matrices, fixed differential operators—either remain multiples of the hit coordinate, become nonzero unit relations on the hit line, or vanish identically.
2. **Integral/geometric refinements**—exact denominator defects, primitive Padé contents, Kummer–Mellin lifts—do detect the right primes, but their desired height estimate is exactly the missing horizontal arithmetic.

The one substantive positive candidate is

```math
R_m=\frac{L_m^3}{E_m}.
```

Every relevant lower-digit shell prime divides it. This converts the problem into an exact-denominator saturation theorem and is the most promising formulation produced by the audit. But current results give no `o(X)` bound for its window radical.

Accordingly:

> **No presently known Casoratian, Padé, derivative, modified-coefficient, modular-form, or K3-period construction yields a nonzero detector of logarithmic height `o(X)`. The detector route remains viable only through a genuinely new horizontal compression theorem—most plausibly a finite low-height branch decomposition, a denominator-defect shell theorem, or a hit-amplification result that unlocks the existing same-prime gap certificates.**
