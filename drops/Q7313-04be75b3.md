# The first-order Gessel identity is true, but it gives no smaller top-shell carrier

## Verdict

Let

\[
b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2.
\]

For every prime \(p\) and every \(0\le m<p\), one has

\[
\boxed{b_{p+m}+5b_{p-1-m}-10b_m\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{1}
\]

This is exactly the result of eliminating the first derivative from Gessel's first-order Lucas congruence at the two high digits \(1\) and \(-1\), using the exact Apéry reflection \(\mathcal A(-1-z)=\mathcal A(z)\).

After the top-window substitution

\[
m=n-p,\qquad r=p-1-m=2p-n-1,
\]

we obtain

\[
W_{n,p}:=b_n+5b_r-10b_m\in p^2\mathbf Z
\tag{2}
\]

for **every** prime \(n/2<p\le n\), independently of whether \(p\mid b_n\). This unconditionality is the central obstruction. The natural local carrier \(W_{n,p}/p\) is divisible by \(p\) for every candidate prime, so aggregating it over the full top interval contains the entire top primorial, of logarithmic size \((1/2+o(1))n\). Dividing by \(p^2\) removes the guaranteed prime, and a hit does not restore it: an exact example has

\[
v_{11}(W_{16,11})=2
\]

even though \(11\mid b_{16}\). The natural quadratic determinant likewise has only the unconditional \(p^2\), not \(p^3\), on a hit; \((p,m)=(17,3)\) is an exact certificate.

The exact determinants and resultants coming from (1) are therefore literal reformulations of (1). Products over all primes contain the full top primorial; products over hit primes use the unknown support and already contain the target radical; CRT linear combinations require coefficients divisible by the product of all the other primes; and the root-polynomial resultant is exactly the target radical. No subexponential-height common integer follows from (1) alone.

A successful carrier would need an additional **hit-selective, cross-prime** relation. It must fail for generic primes in \((n/2,n]\), retain a factor \(p\) after every forced local factor has been divided out, and have a height bound independent of knowing the hit set. The first-order identity supplies none of these three features.

## 2. The first-order Gessel congruence

Let the entire Apéry function be

\[
\mathcal A(z)
={}_4F_3\!\left(\begin{matrix}-z,-z,z+1,z+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1\right)
=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(-z)_k^2(z+1)_k^2}{(k!)^4}.
\tag{3}
\]

For every nonnegative integer \(d\), the sum truncates and

\[
\mathcal A(d)=b_d.
\]

The hypergeometric expression is invariant under \(z\mapsto-1-z\), hence

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(-1-z)=\mathcal A(z)}
\tag{4}
\]

for every complex \(z\).

Put

\[
D_d:=\mathcal A'(d).
\]

Differentiating the terms in (3) at an integer gives the explicit Gessel derivative

\[
D_d
=2\sum_{k=0}^d
 \binom dk^2\binom{d+k}{k}^2
 \bigl(H_{d+k}-H_{d-k}\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]

For a prime \(p\) and \(0\le d<p\), the denominator of \(D_d\) is prime to \(p\). Thus congruences containing \(D_d\) are unambiguous in \(\mathbf Z_{(p)}\).

The first-order Gessel congruence, in the form extended to arbitrary integer high digit, is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(d+pq)\equiv
\bigl(b_d+pqD_d\bigr)\mathcal A(q)\pmod{p^2}
}
\tag{6}
\]

for every prime \(p\), every \(0\le d<p\), and every \(q\in\mathbf Z\). Gessel proved the nonnegative-high-digit form; Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler prove (6) for all integer \(q\).

## 3. Proof of the observed identity

Take \(d=m\) and \(q=1\) in (6). Since \(\mathcal A(1)=b_1=5\),

\[
b_{p+m}
\equiv5b_m+5pD_m\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{7}
\]

Next take \(d=m\) and \(q=-1\). Reflection gives

\[
\mathcal A(-1)=\mathcal A(0)=1
\]

and

\[
\mathcal A(m-p)
=\mathcal A(-1-(m-p))
=\mathcal A(p-1-m)
=b_{p-1-m}.
\]

Therefore (6) gives

\[
b_{p-1-m}
\equiv b_m-pD_m\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{8}
\]

Adding (7) to five times (8) and subtracting \(10b_m\) cancels the derivative:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_{p+m}+5b_{p-1-m}-10b_m
 &\equiv
 (5b_m+5pD_m)+5(b_m-pD_m)-10b_m\\
 &\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (1) for every prime, including \(p=2\). For reference, the two \(p=2\) cases are

\[
73+5\cdot5-10\cdot1=88,
\qquad
1445+5\cdot1-10\cdot5=1400,
\]

both divisible by \(4\).

It is useful to retain the two non-eliminated defects:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
E_{p,m}&:=b_{p+m}-5b_m\equiv5pD_m\pmod{p^2},\\
F_{p,m}&:=b_m-b_{p-1-m}\equiv pD_m\pmod{p^2}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{9}
\]

Then (1) is simply

\[
W_{p,m}=E_{p,m}-5F_{p,m}\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{10}
\]

Thus (1) is not an independent supercongruence: it is the first-jet elimination of the same scalar \(pD_m\).

## 4. The top-shell specialization

Let

\[
\mathcal P_n:=\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n\},
\qquad
S_n:=\{p\in\mathcal P_n:p\mid b_n\},
\]

and

\[
R_n:=\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)
=\prod_{p\in S_n}p.
\tag{11}
\]

For \(p\in\mathcal P_n\), put

\[
m_p=n-p,
\qquad
r_p=p-1-m_p=2p-n-1.
\tag{12}
\]

Then \(0\le m_p,r_p<p\), and (1) becomes

\[
\boxed{
W_{n,p}:=b_n+5b_{r_p}-10b_{m_p}\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.}
\tag{13}
\]

The crucial quantifier is

\[
\textit{(13) holds for every }p\in\mathcal P_n,
\textit{ not only for }p\in S_n.
\tag{14}
\]

For \(p\ge7\), reduction of (7)--(8) modulo \(p\) gives

\[
b_n\equiv5b_{m_p}\pmod p,
\qquad
b_{r_p}\equiv b_{m_p}\pmod p.
\tag{15}
\]

Since \(5\) is a unit modulo \(p\),

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_n
\iff p\mid b_{m_p}
\iff p\mid b_{r_p}.}
\tag{16}
\]

The prime \(5\) is a finite top-window exception and is irrelevant to the asymptotic radical.

On a hit, write

\[
b_n=pN_p,\qquad b_{r_p}=pR_p',\qquad b_{m_p}=pU_p.
\]

Dividing (13) by \(p\) gives the normalized relation

\[
N_p+5R_p'-10U_p\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{17}
\]

More precisely, (7)--(8) give

\[
\boxed{
N_p\equiv5(U_p+D_{m_p}),
\qquad
R_p'\equiv U_p-D_{m_p}\pmod p.}
\tag{18}
\]

For \(p\ge7\), the map

\[
(U,D)\longmapsto(5(U+D),\,U-D,\,U)
\tag{19}
\]

has rank two over \(\mathbf F_p\). Its image is exactly the plane

\[
X+5Y-10Z=0.
\]

Equivalently, the kernel of

\[
\mathbf F_p[X,Y,Z]\longrightarrow\mathbf F_p[U,D],
\quad
(X,Y,Z)\mapsto(5(U+D),U-D,U),
\]

is the principal ideal

\[
(X+5Y-10Z).
\tag{20}
\]

Therefore the first-order theorem gives only one universal relation among the three normalized hit values, namely (17). Any second independent normalized determinant or polynomial relation requires new arithmetic information about \(U_p\) and \(D_{m_p}\).

## 5. A hit does not upgrade \(p^2\) to \(p^3\)

One might hope that the extra condition \(p\mid b_n\) upgrades (13) to \(p^3\mid W_{n,p}\), so that \(W_{n,p}/p^2\) remains a local carrier. This is false.

Take \(p=11\), \(m=5\). Then \(p+m=16\) and \(p-1-m=5\), so

\[
W_{16,11}=b_{16}-5b_5.
\]

The exact Apéry values reduce modulo \(11^3=1331\) as follows:

```text
b_5  = 819005                      ≡ 440 (mod 1331),
b_16 = 10534522198396293262825     ≡ 264 (mod 1331),
b_16 - 5 b_5                       ≡ 726 = 6·121 (mod 1331).
```

Thus

\[
11\mid b_5,\qquad 11\mid b_{16},\qquad
\boxed{v_{11}(W_{16,11})=2.}
\tag{21}
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{W_{16,11}}{11^2}\not\equiv0\pmod{11}.
\]

There is no general hit factor left after dividing by the unconditional \(p^2\).

## 6. Exact determinant audit

### 6.1 The linear determinant is exactly the same identity

From (9),

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}
 b_{p+m}-5b_m & 5\\
 b_m-b_{p-1-m} & 1
\end{pmatrix}
=b_{p+m}+5b_{p-1-m}-10b_m.
\tag{22}
\]

Therefore the most immediate \(2\times2\) determinant is not a new carrier; it is exactly \(W_{p,m}\).

### 6.2 The natural quadratic determinant is unconditional

Another natural elimination is

\[
Q_{p,m}
:=\det\begin{pmatrix}
 b_{p+m} & 5b_m\\
 b_m & b_{p-1-m}
\end{pmatrix}
=b_{p+m}b_{p-1-m}-5b_m^2.
\tag{23}
\]

Write \(x=b_m\) and \(y=pD_m\). Equations (7)--(8) say

\[
b_{p+m}\equiv5(x+y),
\qquad
b_{p-1-m}\equiv x-y\pmod{p^2}.
\]

Since \(y^2\in p^2\mathbf Z_{(p)}\),

\[
Q_{p,m}\equiv5(x+y)(x-y)-5x^2=-5y^2\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{24}
\]

Again, this holds for every prime and every \(m\), not only on hits.

Nor does a hit force \(p^3\mid Q_{p,m}\). On a hit, using (18),

\[
\frac{Q_{p,m}}{p^2}\equiv-5D_m^2\pmod p.
\tag{25}
\]

There is an exact small certificate. Since

\[
b_3=1445=5\cdot17^2,
\]

\((p,m)=(17,3)\) is a hit. Formula (5) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_3
&=2\left(
144\frac7{12}
+900\frac{77}{60}
+400\frac{49}{20}
\right)\\
&=2(84+1155+980)=4438\equiv1\pmod{17}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Q_{17,3}}{17^2}\equiv-5\not\equiv0\pmod{17},
\qquad v_{17}(Q_{17,3})=2.}
\tag{26}
\]

Thus the quadratic determinant also loses the prime after division by its forced \(p^2\).

## 7. Exact resultant audit

Introduce a first-jet variable \(S\) and the two linear polynomials

\[
f_{p,m}(S)=b_{p+m}-5b_m-5S,
\qquad
g_{p,m}(S)=b_m-b_{p-1-m}-S.
\tag{27}
\]

Modulo \(p^2\), both vanish at \(S=pD_m\). Their exact resultant is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}_S(f_{p,m},g_{p,m})
&=(b_{p+m}-5b_m)-5(b_m-b_{p-1-m})\\
&=W_{p,m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]

So the primitive first-jet resultant is literally (1).

If one instead uses \(T=D_m\), namely

\[
\widetilde f(T)=b_{p+m}-5b_m-5pT,
\qquad
\widetilde g(T)=b_m-b_{p-1-m}-pT,
\]

then

\[
\operatorname{Res}_T(\widetilde f,\widetilde g)=pW_{p,m}.
\tag{29}
\]

The extra factor is the explicitly inserted \(p\). Thus the resultant route either returns \(W_{p,m}\) or returns \(pW_{p,m}\); it creates no common low-height carrier.

## 8. Products over primes: full primorial or circular support

Let

\[
M_n:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}p.
\tag{30}
\]

The prime number theorem gives

\[
\log M_n
=\vartheta(n)-\vartheta(n/2)
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n.
\tag{31}
\]

Because \(p^2\mid W_{n,p}\), the integer

\[
C_{n,p}:=\frac{W_{n,p}}p
\tag{32}
\]

is divisible by \(p\) for **every** \(p\in\mathcal P_n\). Consequently, if the natural product is nonzero,

\[
M_n\mid\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}C_{n,p},
\tag{33}
\]

and therefore

\[
\log\left|\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}C_{n,p}\right|
\ge\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n.
\tag{34}
\]

Using \(W_{n,p}\) rather than \(W_{n,p}/p\) gives the stronger but still useless divisibility

\[
M_n^2\mid\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_n}W_{n,p}.
\]

If a factor were exactly zero, the product would be the zero carrier, which gives no upper bound on a radical.

Dividing by \(p^2\) avoids the full primorial but loses the desired local prime, as (21) proves.

Restricting the product to the unknown hit set gives

\[
R_n\mid\prod_{p\in S_n}\frac{W_{n,p}}p,
\tag{35}
\]

but this is circular in two ways:

1. the definition of the product already requires knowing \(S_n\);
2. any nonzero such product has logarithmic height at least \(\log R_n\), and no independent subexponential upper bound is supplied by (1).

An lcm has the same support problem. A gcd has the opposite defect: \(p\) is guaranteed only in its own row, so taking a gcd across rows does not preserve the union of the row primes. The identity

\[
\gcd(b_n,M_n)=R_n
\tag{36}
\]

is exact but is simply the target radical itself.

## 9. Integer linear forms and the CRT height barrier

The tiny coefficients \((1,5,-10)\) in (13) are misleading: the indices in the form depend on \(p\). To turn the row-wise integers into one integer, one needs to keep the \(p\)-row modulo \(p\) while suppressing all other rows. Without new off-diagonal congruences, this is exactly a CRT selector problem.

### Lemma 9.1: universal selector coefficients contain the radical

Let \(P\) be a finite set of distinct primes. For each \(q\in P\), let \(X_q\) be allowed to range independently over \(q\mathbf Z\). Suppose integers \(a_q\) have the property that

\[
p\mid\sum_{q\in P}a_qX_q
\tag{37}
\]

for every \(p\in P\) and every permitted choice of the \(X_q\). Then, for each \(q\in P\),

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{\substack{p\in P\\p\ne q}}p\mid a_q.}
\tag{38}
\]

#### Proof

Fix distinct \(p,q\in P\). Set \(X_q=q\) and set every other \(X_{q'}=0\). Equation (37) gives

\[
p\mid a_qq.
\]

Since \(p\nmid q\), one has \(p\mid a_q\). This holds for every \(p\ne q\), proving (38). \(\square\)

If the variables range over \(q^2\mathbf Z\) and one demands divisibility by \(p^2\), the same proof gives

\[
\prod_{p\ne q}p^2\mid a_q.
\tag{39}
\]

Apply the lemma to \(X_q=C_{n,q}=W_{n,q}/q\). For the full top interval, every nonzero universal selector has

\[
\log\max_q|a_q|
\ge\log M_n-O(\log n)
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n.
\tag{40}
\]

If one first restricts to the hit set, the lower bound becomes

\[
\log\max_q|a_q|\ge\log R_n-O(\log n),
\tag{41}
\]

which puts the unknown target radical directly into the coefficients.

This is precisely what the standard CRT idempotents do: the coefficient selecting the \(q\)-row contains \(M_n/q\), or \(R_n/q\) if the hit set is already known. Hence a CRT linear form is not a smaller carrier.

The lemma is deliberately a black-box statement. Actual off-diagonal Apéry congruences could invalidate the assumption that the other rows are independent modulo \(p\), but proving such congruences would be the genuinely new cross-prime input. They do not follow from (1).

## 10. Root-polynomial resultants reproduce the target exactly

One can encode the hit indices by

\[
P_n^*(X)
:=\prod_{p\in S_n}\bigl(X-(n-p)\bigr).
\tag{42}
\]

Then the most direct resultant is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_X(P_n^*(X),n-X)
=P_n^*(n)
=\prod_{p\in S_n}p
=R_n,}
\tag{43}
\]

up to the harmless resultant sign convention.

Thus the exact root resultant is not merely bounded below by the target radical; it **is** the target radical. Its input polynomial already requires the hit set.

Replacing \(S_n\) by all of \(\mathcal P_n\) gives \(M_n\), whose logarithm is linear by (31).

There is also a structural reason a fixed small resultant is not naturally available. The hit condition is

\[
(n-m)\mid b_m,
\tag{44}
\]

with a modulus that changes with \(m\). A usual resultant detects common roots over one fixed coefficient ring or one fixed residue field. Encoding the moving modulus \(n-m\) inserts the factors \(n-m=p\), and their product is exactly the primorial or the target radical.

## 11. Telescoping products

There are two natural telescoping ideas, and both are exact dead ends.

### 11.1 Telescoping the first-jet defects

Equation (9) gives

\[
E_{p,m}\equiv5F_{p,m}\pmod{p^2},
\qquad
p\mid E_{p,m},F_{p,m}
\tag{45}
\]

for every candidate prime. Ratios \(E_{p,m}/F_{p,m}\) are not generally integers. Clearing denominators restores products of the \(F_{p,m}\), and those products contain the full top primorial because \(p\mid F_{p,m}\) unconditionally.

Even if an index from one row coincides with an index from another row, exact cancellation of Apéry values in a sum does not preserve the modular certificate. A \(q\)-row is divisible by \(q^2\), not by an unrelated \(p\). To preserve every modulus, the cross-row coefficients must be CRT selectors, and Lemma 9.1 applies.

### 11.2 The recurrence-transfer determinant telescopes to a unit

The Apéry recurrence is

\[
(k+1)^3b_{k+1}
=(34k^3+51k^2+27k+5)b_k-k^3b_{k-1}.
\tag{46}
\]

Let

\[
M_k=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{34k^3+51k^2+27k+5}{(k+1)^3}
&-\dfrac{k^3}{(k+1)^3}\\[1.2ex]
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{47}
\]

Then

\[
\binom{b_{k+1}}{b_k}=M_k\binom{b_k}{b_{k-1}},
\qquad
\det M_k=\frac{k^3}{(k+1)^3}.
\]

For \(1\le u<v<p\), the transfer determinant telescopes exactly:

\[
\boxed{
\det(M_{v-1}\cdots M_u)
=\prod_{k=u}^{v-1}\frac{k^3}{(k+1)^3}
=\left(\frac uv\right)^3.}
\tag{48}
\]

This is a \(p\)-adic unit. Thus the automatic determinant telescope of the Apéry recurrence carries no factor \(p\). The zero event is encoded in a state entry or projective incidence, not in the transfer determinant.

## 12. Why a global fixed linear form still returns the original problem

Suppose one seeks a fixed integer

\[
L_n=\sum_j c_{n,j}b_j
\tag{49}
\]

such that every \(p\in S_n\) divides \(L_n\). For the \(p\)-row, the first-order argument only tells us that

\[
p\mid b_n,\quad p\mid b_{m_p},\quad p\mid b_{r_p}.
\]

It says nothing about a generic off-row \(b_j\pmod p\). Therefore a proof based only on the local row must either

- use only the common term \(b_n\), returning the original exponential-height carrier;
- give every off-row coefficient a factor \(p\), and hence accumulate CRT products as in Lemma 9.1; or
- prove new cross-prime congruences controlling the off-row values.

The first two choices give no saving. The third is precisely the missing arithmetic input, not a consequence of (1).

## 13. A precise no-saving statement for the first-order identity

The logically available local data from (1) are row-separable:

\[
W_{n,p}=p^2Z_{n,p},
\qquad p\in\mathcal P_n,
\tag{50}
\]

where the first-order theorem places no condition on \(Z_{n,p}\pmod p\). The hit condition does not change this: (21) shows that \(Z_{n,p}\) can be a \(p\)-unit on a hit.

Consequently, within constructions whose divisibility proof uses only the local facts (50):

1. a product or block-diagonal determinant using one row for each candidate prime contains \(M_n\) or \(M_n^2\), hence has at least linear logarithmic height;
2. division by the full forced \(p^2\) removes the local prime and is not repaired by the hit condition;
3. a universal linear or coupled-determinant selector requires coefficients containing the product of all other primes by Lemma 9.1;
4. a local first-jet resultant is \(W_{n,p}\) or \(pW_{n,p}\);
5. a root resultant using the hit support is exactly \(R_n\);
6. the recurrence determinant telescope is a \(p\)-adic unit.

This is a rigorous obstruction for the proposed first-order constructions. It is not a theorem that no conceivable Apéry carrier exists. A non-row-separable determinant or resultant could succeed only by proving an additional relation among different characteristics. Such a relation would be genuinely new and would have to be audited independently.

## 14. Relation to the current Franel-tail carrier

The cutoff-content construction in

`problems/3.2/research/proofs/Q32_ALL_CUTOFF_BLOCK_AND_LOSS_PROOF.md`

already proves an exact support theorem: after the loss-count and `Once` filter, the \(q=1\) top-shell primes are isolated up to the stated near-square-root nuisance. The file correctly labels the remaining problem as a height theorem.

Identity (13) does not improve that situation:

- the cutoff carrier is hit-selective but lacks a height estimate;
- \(W_{n,p}/p\) has an elementary local height description but is not hit-selective at all;
- combining the two by an intersection, gcd, or CRT selector again either returns the target support or introduces the top primorial.

Thus the first-order identity and the Franel-tail construction are complementary support descriptions, not a route to a smaller common integer.

## 15. What a genuinely useful next identity would have to do

A successful replacement must produce a nonzero integer \(C_n\), defined without knowing \(S_n\), such that

\[
R_n\mid C_n,
\qquad
\log|C_n|=o(n).
\tag{51}
\]

For a local-to-global construction, at least one of the following genuinely new properties is necessary:

1. **Hit selectivity:** after all unconditional powers of \(p\) are divided out, the remaining local quantity is divisible by \(p\) precisely, or at least only, when \(p\mid b_n\).
2. **Cross-prime coupling:** the reductions of the \(p\)-rows and \(q\)-rows satisfy an arithmetic relation allowing one small coefficient system to serve many primes without CRT weights.
3. **A p-independent global object:** a fixed determinant, resultant, content, or norm of subexponential height whose reduction at a hit prime becomes singular for a reason not valid at generic primes.
4. **A second-order theorem with a hit factor:** a congruence of the form
   \[
   \frac{W_{n,p}}{p^2}\equiv b_{m_p}\,H_{n,p}\pmod p
   \]
   or an analogous formula with a controlled factor. The \(p=11\) certificate rules out this formula for \(W\) itself.

The first-order Gessel identity has none of these features.

## 16. Final conclusion

The observed congruence is rigorously proved by the pair

\[
b_{p+m}\equiv5(b_m+pD_m),
\qquad
b_{p-1-m}\equiv b_m-pD_m
\pmod{p^2}.
\]

It is a clean and useful first-jet identity, but it is **unconditional in the prime**. In the top shell it therefore produces a local \(p^2\)-multiple for every candidate prime, not a special witness for \(p\mid b_n\).

Every natural construction requested in the question has an exact death certificate:

| construction | exact outcome | obstruction |
|---|---|---|
| linear \(2\times2\) determinant | \(W_{n,p}\) | literal reformulation of (1) |
| quadratic determinant | \(p^2\mid b_nb_r-5b_m^2\) for all \(p\) | no \(p^3\) on the hit \((17,3)\) |
| first-jet resultant | \(W_{n,p}\) or \(pW_{n,p}\) | same identity, possibly with an inserted \(p\) |
| product over all top primes | contains \(M_n\) or \(M_n^2\) | \(\log M_n=(1/2+o(1))n\) |
| product/resultant over hits | contains or equals \(R_n\) | support already uses the target set |
| CRT linear form | coefficients contain \(M_n/p\) or \(R_n/p\) | coefficient height already contains the radical |
| divide by forced \(p^2\) | no guaranteed \(p\) remains | \(v_{11}(W_{16,11})=2\) |
| recurrence determinant telescope | \((u/v)^3\) | a \(p\)-adic unit |

Hence (1) does **not** yield

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)=o(n).
\]

It confirms, at mod \(p^2\) resolution, the repository's existing warning: the top-window Gessel conversion is an exact local reformulation, not a height-saving common carrier. Any further progress must introduce a hit-selective second-order invariant or a genuinely cross-characteristic relation.
