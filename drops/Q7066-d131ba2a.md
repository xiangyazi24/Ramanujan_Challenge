# Discriminants of the Apéry polynomials

Let

\[
 b_r(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2\lambda^k.
\]

The discriminant is computed as the resultant

\[
\operatorname{disc}_\lambda(b_r)=(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}\operatorname{Res}_\lambda(b_r,b_r')/\operatorname{lc}(b_r).
\]

The first cases are:

```
r=1:  b_1 = 1+4λ
      disc = 1

r=2:  b_2 = 1+36λ+36λ²
      disc = 1152 = 2^7·3²
```

For r=3,...,10 the same resultant computation gives nonzero integers. In particular every polynomial in this range is squarefree over Q: a zero discriminant would be equivalent to a repeated algebraic root, and no such root occurs.

## Structural observations

1. The nonvanishing is not accidental.  The Apéry polynomials are hypergeometric polynomials

\[
{}_4F_3(-r,-r,r+1,r+1;1,1,1;\lambda),
\]

and satisfy a third-order Picard--Fuchs equation.  A repeated root of b_r would force a degeneration of the corresponding singularity structure.

2. The discriminants are highly composite.  The small primes which occur come from the factorial/binomial structure of the coefficients and from the singular fibers of the Picard--Fuchs family.  They are not expected to be squarefree numbers themselves.

3. Straub's multivariate Apéry interpretation explains many congruence properties of the coefficients, but it does not directly give a closed product formula for the discriminant.  The discriminant is an elimination invariant of the one-variable specialization, and a closed form would require an additional description of the critical values of the associated period map.

## Closed form question

There is currently no simple analogue of the classical discriminant formulas for Legendre or Jacobi polynomials.  The natural expression is

\[
\operatorname{disc}(b_r)=\prod_{b_r'(\alpha)=0} b_r(\alpha)
\]

(up to the leading coefficient factor), so a formula would amount to controlling all critical values of the Apéry hypergeometric polynomial.

The Straub structure is therefore very useful for congruences and arithmetic properties, but it does not by itself collapse the discriminant to a simple product formula.

## Squarefreeness conclusion

For r=1,...,10 the discriminants are all nonzero, hence

\[
\gcd(b_r,b_r')=1\quad\text{in }\mathbb Q[\lambda],
\]

so all ten Apéry polynomials are squarefree over Q.

A natural next step for the project is to compute the exact factorization of these discriminants and compare it with the bad primes of the underlying Picard--Fuchs family.
