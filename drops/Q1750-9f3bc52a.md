# Q1750 audit: canonical interpolation identity and the remaining obstruction

## Summary

The proposed interpolation identity is correct as a finite-field identity once all removable points are treated in the quotient ring of functions on \(\mathbf F_p\). It does not, however, close the singleton-gap argument. The missing step is exactly an explicit evaluation of either

\[
B'(m-1) \quad\text{or}\quad R=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}C(x)^{-1},
\]

for the canonical degree \(<p\) representative. The ordinary Apéry recurrence determines the values \(A_x\), but it does not directly determine the derivative of the canonical interpolation polynomial because replacing a polynomial by its value-equivalent representative modulo \(X^p-X\) changes derivatives.

Therefore the claimed route gives a valid reformulation, not a proof of \(\Sigma\neq0\).

## Identity check

Let

\[
q(X)=(X-(m-1))(X-(m+1))=(X-m)^2-1.
\]

The reflection law is

\[
B(2m-X)=B(X).
\]

Differentiating as an identity of the canonical representative gives

\[
-B'(2m-X)=B'(X).
\]

At \(X=m-1\), this gives

\[
B'(m+1)=-B'(m-1).
\]

Near \(X=m-1\),

\[
B(X)=B(m-1)+B'(m-1)(X-(m-1))+O((X-(m-1))^2).
\]

Since

\[
q(X)=(X-(m-1))(X-(m+1)),
\]

and \(X-(m+1)=-2+O(X-(m-1))\), the removable value is

\[
C^*=\frac{B'(m-1)}{-2}=-\frac{B'(m-1)}2.
\]

The same calculation at \(m+1\) gives the same value.

## Pairing computation

For all non-removable points,

\[
C(x)=\frac{B(x)}{q(x)}=\frac{A_x}{q(x)}.
\]

The reflection orbit pairs \(x\) and \(2m-x\). Since

\[
q(2m-x)=q(x),\qquad A_{2m-x}=A_x,
\]

we get

\[
\sum_{x\notin\{m-1,m+1\}}\frac1{C(x)}
=
2\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{q(j)}{A_j}+\frac{q(m)}{A_m}.
\]

Because

\[
q(m)=-1,
\]

and the two removed points contribute \(2/C^*\),

\[
R=2H-\frac1{A_m}+\frac2{C^*},
\]

where

\[
H=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{q(j)}{A_j}.
\]

Rearranging,

\[
\Sigma=\frac13-2A_mH
\]

becomes

\[
\boxed{\Sigma=-\frac23-A_mR+\frac{2A_m}{C^*}}.
\]

This is the exact claimed identity.

## Why it does not yet prove nonvanishing

The tempting next step is to derive \(C^*\) from the Apéry recurrence. This fails because the recurrence gives the sequence of values

\[
A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{p-1}\in\mathbf F_p,
\]

whereas \(B'(m-1)\) is a property of the unique degree \(<p\) polynomial interpolation lift.

If \(B_1\equiv B_2\pmod{X^p-X}\), then

\[
B_1(x)=B_2(x)\quad(x\in\mathbf F_p),
\]

but generally

\[
B_1'(x)\ne B_2'(x).
\]

Hence any proof must either:

1. control the canonical interpolation coefficients explicitly;
2. compute the reciprocal sum \(R\) by another finite-field method; or
3. find a determinant/resultant identity involving the whole row.

The recurrence alone does not supply (1)--(3).

## Exact obstruction

The reduction

\[
\Sigma=0
\Longleftrightarrow
A_mR= -\frac23+\frac{2A_m}{C^*}
\]

is equivalent to a relation between two global invariants of the interpolation polynomial. No recurrence identity currently known in the project determines this relation.

Thus Q1750 produces a valid algebraic restatement of the singleton-gap obstruction, but not a closure of P3.2.

## Minimal conditional lemma

A sufficient lemma is:

> For every singleton-gap prime \(p=2m+1\), under the unit hypothesis, the canonical interpolation polynomial satisfies
> \[
> 3A_mR-6A_m/C^*+2\neq0.
> \]

Equivalently, any proof of a nonvanishing law for the pair \((R,C^*)\) closes the gap.

At present the interpolation identity narrows the target but does not prove it.
