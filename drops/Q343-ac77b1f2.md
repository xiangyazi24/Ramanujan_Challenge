# Q343 — Can gap-polynomial coprimality prove PTR3?

## Executive conclusion

Pairwise coprimality is a useful **local certificate**, but it does not by itself prove the Pinned Triple-Return Theorem

\[
\sum_{X<p\le 2X}\binom{m_p}{3}\ll \frac{X}{\log X}.
\]

For a fixed pair \((h,k)\), coprimality says that a simultaneous return can occur only at primes dividing one nonzero integer \(R_{h,k}\). The obstruction is that PTR3 involves about \(X^2\) moving pairs \((h,k)\), and the resultants have logarithmic size of order at most \(hk\log(hk)\). Direct divisor accounting therefore gives, at best, an \(O(X^4)\) bound after one uses the full \(p\)-adic multiplicity of the resultant; the cruder bound in the question gives \(O(X^5)\). Neither is remotely close to \(X/\log X\).

The pin condition does not supply a deterministic factor \(1/p\). Proving such a factor is itself a substantial decorrelation statement, and even granting it does not repair the worst-case resultant sum. A successful proof needs a **family-level return-energy or mixing theorem**, not merely pairwise nonvanishing of resultants.

This is the same structural obstruction recorded elsewhere in the P3.2 run log: a local algebraic certificate is real, but summing a two-dimensional family of moving certificates loses the global scale. Below I make that quantitative and state a more plausible missing theorem.

## 1. Exact pinned-return identity

Let \(Z_p^+\) be the chosen half of the noncentral zero set, so that

\[
|Z_p^+|=m_p.
\]

For \(r\in Z_p^+\), let

\[
H_p(r)=\frac{p-1}{2}-r
\]

(with the harmless endpoint convention adjusted as needed), and define the number of later returns from the pin \(r\) by

\[
\nu_p(r)
 =\#\{1\le h\le H_p(r):N_h(f(r))\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

Under the stated gap-polynomial equivalence, this is simply the number of later elements of \(Z_p^+\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\binom{m_p}{3}
 =\sum_{r\in Z_p^+}\binom{\nu_p(r)}{2}.
}
\]

Indeed, every triple is counted once, at its least element. Equivalently, if

\[
T_p(h,k)=\#\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
r\in Z_p^+:\ r+k\le (p-1)/2,\\
N_h(f(r))\equiv N_k(f(r))\equiv0\pmod p
\end{array}
\right\},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\binom{m_p}{3}
 =\sum_{X<p\le2X}\ \sum_{1\le h<k\le (p-1)/2}T_p(h,k).
}
\]

Thus PTR3 is exactly a second factorial moment of return times in the gap variable.

## 2. Part (a): size of a fixed resultant

Work with primitive integral models of the gap polynomials. If the initially defined \(N_j\) is rational, clear denominators and divide by the content first. The finitely or explicitly described primes introduced by denominators and degree drops should be included in the usual bad-prime factor; this does not change the estimates below.

Let \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[x]\) have degrees \(m,n\) and coefficient heights

\[
H(A)=\max_i|a_i|,\qquad H(B)=\max_j|b_j|.
\]

The Sylvester matrix has \(n\) shifted rows containing the coefficients of \(A\), and \(m\) shifted rows containing the coefficients of \(B\). Hadamard's determinant inequality gives

\[
|\operatorname{Res}(A,B)|
 \le \|A\|_2^n\|B\|_2^m
 \le \bigl(\sqrt{m+1}\,H(A)\bigr)^n
      \bigl(\sqrt{n+1}\,H(B)\bigr)^m.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\log|\operatorname{Res}(A,B)|
&\le n\log H(A)+m\log H(B)\\
&\quad+\frac n2\log(m+1)+\frac m2\log(n+1).
\end{aligned}}
\]

Applied to \(N_h,N_k\), this is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\log|R_{h,k}|
&\le d_k\log H_h+d_h\log H_k\\
&\quad+\frac{d_k}{2}\log(d_h+1)
      +\frac{d_h}{2}\log(d_k+1).
\end{aligned}}
\]

Thus, whenever \(d_j\ll j\) and

\[
\log H_j\ll j\log(2j),
\]

one obtains the project-level bound

\[
\boxed{
\log|R_{h,k}|\ll hk\log(2hk).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
|R_{h,k}|\le \exp\!\bigl(O(hk\log(2hk))\bigr).
\]

Under the stronger height hypothesis \(\log H_j\ll j\), the same calculation improves this to

\[
\log|R_{h,k}|\ll hk.
\]

The exact constant in \(d_j\asymp j\)—whether it is the normalization stated in the question or the continuant normalization used elsewhere—does not affect these exponents.

There is an important qualification: degree and height give an **upper bound**, not an asymptotic order of magnitude. Since the resultant is a nonzero integer, the only universal lower bound is

\[
|R_{h,k}|\ge1.
\]

Resultants of large polynomials can equal \(\pm1\). Therefore the rigorous statement needed for divisor budgeting is

```text
log |R_{h,k}| = O(hk log(2hk)),
```

not \(\log|R_{h,k}|\asymp hk\log(hk)\).

## 3. Part (b): what direct summation actually gives

### 3.1 Use the dyadic prime range

For a nonzero integer \(M\), define

\[
\omega_X(M)=\#\{X<p\le2X:p\mid M\}.
\]

Since every such prime contributes at least \(\log X\) to \(\log|M|\),

\[
\boxed{
\omega_X(M)\le \frac{\log|M|}{\log X}.
}
\]

This is the relevant estimate; using \(\log2\) throws away a factor of \(\log X\).

With \(h<k\), one has \(\min(d_h,d_k)\ll h\). Therefore the sum proposed in the question is bounded by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{1\le h<k\le X}
\min(d_h,d_k)\,\omega_X(R_{h,k})
&\ll \frac1{\log X}
 \sum_{h<k\le X}h\,\log|R_{h,k}|\\
&\ll \frac1{\log X}
 \sum_{h<k\le X}h^2k\log(2hk)\\
&\ll X^5.
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\sum_{h<k\le H}h^2k=\frac1{15}H^5+O(H^4).
\]

So the displayed strategy does not give \(O(X/\log X)\); it gives a bound larger by roughly four powers of \(X\).

### 3.2 A useful sharpening: common roots are controlled by \(v_p(R_{h,k})\)

The factor \(\min(d_h,d_k)\) can be removed if one uses the valuation of the resultant rather than merely its set of prime divisors.

Let \(p\) be a good prime for the leading coefficients, and let

\[
e_p(h,k)=\deg\gcd(\overline{N_h},\overline{N_k})
\]

in \(\mathbb F_p[x]\). The nullity modulo \(p\) of the Sylvester matrix is \(e_p(h,k)\). By Smith normal form, a square integer matrix whose reduction has nullity \(e\) has determinant divisible by \(p^e\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
e_p(h,k)\le v_p(R_{h,k}).}
\]

The number \(\rho_p(h,k)\) of distinct common roots in \(\mathbb F_p\) satisfies

\[
\rho_p(h,k)\le e_p(h,k)\le v_p(R_{h,k}).
\]

It follows that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\rho_p(h,k)
 \le \frac{\log|R_{h,k}|}{\log X}.
}
\]

This is stronger than

\[
\rho_p(h,k)\le\min(d_h,d_k)\,1_{p\mid R_{h,k}}.
\]

If \(f\) is a fixed polynomial of degree \(\delta\), every fiber of \(f\) has at most \(\delta\) elements unless \(f\) becomes constant modulo \(p\). For a rational \(f\), the same statement holds with a fixed numerator-denominator degree after poles are removed. Thus one may safely use a fixed fiber constant \(D_f\); global injectivity should not be assumed unless \(f\) has actually been proved to be a permutation map modulo the primes in question.

Dropping the pin and all interval restrictions, the valuation estimate gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{X<p\le2X}\sum_{h<k\le X}
 \#\{r:N_h(f(r))=N_k(f(r))=0\}\\
&\qquad\le \frac{D_f}{\log X}
 \sum_{h<k\le X}\log|R_{h,k}|\\
&\qquad\ll \frac{D_f}{\log X}
 \sum_{h<k\le X}hk\log(2hk)\\
&\qquad\ll D_fX^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the best elementary resultant accounting improves \(X^5\) to \(X^4\), but PTR3 asks for \(X/\log X\). Under the stronger bound \(\log|R_{h,k}|\ll hk\), this becomes \(O(X^4/\log X)\), which is still off by three powers of \(X\).

The bad leading-coefficient or denominator primes can be folded into a modified certificate such as

\[
R_{h,k}\,\operatorname{lc}(N_h)\operatorname{lc}(N_k)
\]

and have the same kind of height budget. They do not alter the obstruction.

### 3.3 Multiplying all certificates does not help

One could combine all pairwise certificates into

\[
\mathcal R_H=\prod_{1\le h<k\le H}R_{h,k},
\]

or equivalently use the discriminant of \(\prod_{h\le H}N_h\). But

\[
\log|\mathcal R_H|
 \ll \sum_{h<k\le H}hk\log(2hk)
 \ll H^4\log H.
\]

So the single global integer has exactly the same \(H^4\) dyadic divisor budget. Packaging the resultants together does not create cancellation.

### 3.4 Why coprimality plus height cannot logically suffice

There are even degree-one families with modest height growth that exhibit maximal modular clustering. For a dyadic block \(H\le h<2H\), set

\[
M_H=\prod_{H<p\le2H}p,
\qquad L_h(x)=x-hM_H.
\]

The polynomials \(L_h\) are pairwise coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), and

\[
\log H(L_h)=O(H)=O(h).
\]

Yet for every prime \(p\in(H,2H]\), every polynomial in that whole block reduces to \(x\), so every pair has the common root \(0\) modulo \(p\). One can place such constructions in disjoint dyadic blocks to obtain an infinite family.

This example is not meant to model the Apéry recurrence. It shows precisely that **pairwise coprimality and ordinary height control alone do not prevent a prime from dividing many moving resultants simultaneously**. Any successful argument must use additional structure of the Apéry continuant family.

## 4. Part (c): the pin does not automatically give \(1/p\)

Let

\[
C_p(h,k)=\{x\in\mathbb F_p:N_h(x)=N_k(x)=0\}.
\]

The actual pinned contribution is

\[
T_p(h,k)=\#\{r\in Z_p^+: f(r)\in C_p(h,k),\ r+k\le(p-1)/2\}.
\]

The unconditional deterministic estimate is only

\[
\boxed{
T_p(h,k)\le \min\bigl(m_p,\,D_f|C_p(h,k)|\bigr).
}
\]

There is no set-theoretic inequality of the form

\[
T_p(h,k)
 \ll \frac{z_p}{p}\,D_f|C_p(h,k)|.
\]

Such an estimate says that the Apéry zero set is equidistributed relative to the exceptional common-root sets \(C_p(h,k)\). That is a new mixing theorem, not a consequence of coprimality.

In fact, the independence heuristic is especially delicate here: the gap-polynomial criterion was derived to describe **returns from a pinned zero**. Thus the pin and the return equations come from the same recurrence orbit; they are not three unrelated random polynomial equations.

There is also a circularity issue in replacing \(z_p/p\) by \(1/p\). The density is \(z_p/p\), and knowing that it behaves like \(1/p\) on the needed average scale is already close to knowing that \(z_p\) has bounded first moments—the consequence PTR3 is intended to deliver.

Even granting the dream bound

\[
T_p(h,k)\ll \frac{D_f}{p}\rho_p(h,k),
\]

and using the sharp valuation estimate, one would get only

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\sum_{h<k\le X}T_p(h,k)
&\ll \frac{D_f}{X\log X}
 \sum_{h<k\le X}\log|R_{h,k}|\\
&\ll D_fX^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Using the cruder \(\min(d_h,d_k)\omega_X(R_{h,k})\) estimate would give \(O(X^4)\). Therefore a single heuristic factor \(1/p\) from the pin does not close the direct resultant sum.

The random model that predicts PTR3 contains **two** average savings that the worst-case resultant bound discards:

1. For a typical pair \((h,k)\), common-root incidence modulo a given prime should have average size about \(1/p\). Equivalently, divisibility of the moving resultant should occur with density about \(1/p\), with only \(O(1)\) common roots when it occurs.
2. Conditional on a common root, the corresponding preimage should satisfy the pin with another probability about \(1/p\).

Thus a pinned incidence for a fixed \((p,h,k)\) should have mean about \(1/p^2\). There are \(\asymp p^2\) gap pairs, so the expected contribution is \(O(1)\) per prime and hence \(O(X/\log X)\) over the dyadic prime interval. Resultant magnitude alone proves neither of these average \(1/p\) savings.

## 5. Part (d): the right moment formulation

A probabilistic or moment method is conceptually the right replacement, but it must be a **uniform family-level theorem**.

For \(H\le p/2\), define

\[
\nu_{p,H}(x)=\#\{1\le h\le H:N_h(x)=0\},
\]

and

\[
\rho_p(h,k)=\#\{x\in\mathbb F_p:N_h(x)=N_k(x)=0\}.
\]

Then the unpinned collision energy has the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{1\le h<k\le H}\rho_p(h,k)
 =\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\binom{\nu_{p,H}(x)}{2}.
}
\]

For a random family with about one root per polynomial, the natural scale is

\[
\sum_x\binom{\nu_{p,H}(x)}{2}
 \asymp \frac{H^2}{p}+H.
\]

When \(H\asymp p\), this is \(O(p)\). Summed over \(p\in(X,2X]\), the natural unpinned scale is therefore \(X^2/\log X\), not the \(X^4\) supplied by resultants.

A clean sufficient package for PTR3 would be the following.

```text
(GR2) Gap-return second moment:

  Σ_{X<p≤2X} Σ_{1≤h<k≤p/2} ρ_p(h,k)
      ≪ X^2 / log X.

(PIN-MIX) Pin/common-root decorrelation:

  Σ_{X<p≤2X} Σ_{1≤h<k≤p/2} T_p(h,k)
      ≪ Σ_{X<p≤2X} (1/p)
           Σ_{1≤h<k≤p/2} ρ_p(h,k)
        + X/log X.
```

Since \(p\asymp X\), these two estimates immediately imply PTR3. They isolate the two missing \(1/p\)-type savings:

- GR2 controls modular overlap of the whole moving family \(\{N_h\}\);
- PIN-MIX controls correlation of those overlaps with the Apéry pin set.

Pairwise coprimality contributes only the support statement

\[
\rho_p(h,k)>0\Longrightarrow p\mid R_{h,k};
\]

it does not imply either GR2 or PIN-MIX.

## 6. Plausible routes to the missing family theorem

### 6.1 Use the recurrence as a projective return process

For fixed \((p,x)\), the continuant recurrence has the form

\[
N_{j+1}(x)
 =P(x+j)N_j(x)-(x+j)^6N_{j-1}(x).
\]

Thus

\[
\binom{N_{j+1}(x)}{N_j(x)}
 =
\begin{pmatrix}
P(x+j)&-(x+j)^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{N_j(x)}{N_{j-1}(x)}.
\]

The condition \(N_j(x)=0\) is a hit of one projective point. The quantity

\[
\sum_x\binom{\nu_{p,H}(x)}{2}
\]

is therefore a return-energy statistic for a family of nonautonomous projective orbits. A spectral-gap, expansion, or large-sieve theorem for these matrix products could plausibly yield GR2. This uses substantially more than \(\gcd_{\mathbb Q}(N_h,N_k)=1\): coprimality only says that two return equations do not coincide identically in characteristic zero, whereas GR2 asks that their reductions rarely align throughout a growing finite-field family.

A useful target at a single prime would be an incidence estimate of the shape

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\binom{\nu_{p,H}(x)}{2}
 \ll p^{o(1)}\left(H+\frac{H^2}{p}\right).
\]

For \(H\asymp p\), this is \(p^{1+o(1)}\), the correct unpinned scale.

### 6.2 Seek a renewal/difference collapse

The raw certificates are indexed by two independent variables \((h,k)\). Divisor counting becomes plausible only if the renewal identity or transfer matrices reduce a double return to a certificate depending on substantially fewer parameters—for example, mostly on \(k-h\), a bounded projective state, or a low-complexity conjugacy class.

The desired structural statement would look schematically like

```text
N_h(x) = N_k(x) = 0 mod p
    ⇒ p divides C_τ,

where the number of possible types τ up to gap X is O(X^{1+o(1)}),
not O(X^2), and the total dyadic prime budget of the C_τ is small.
```

The currently available pairwise resultants retain the full two-dimensional pair \((h,k)\), so they do not provide this collapse.

### 6.3 Prove a large sieve for the resultant family, not a size bound

The relevant statement is not an upper bound for each \(|R_{h,k}|\), but an average modular-incidence estimate such as

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\sum_{h<k\le X}ho_p(h,k)
 \ll \frac{X^2}{\log X}.
\]

This says that prime divisors of the moving resultant family occur at their natural density and do not concentrate on common primes. The degree-one block example above shows that such a theorem cannot follow from heights alone. It would have to exploit the special continuant recurrence, monodromy, or a suitable bilinear large sieve.

### 6.4 Direct three-point correlation

One can bypass resultants and study

\[
C_p(h,k)=\sum_r
1_{b_r\equiv0}
1_{b_{r+h}\equiv0}
1_{b_{r+k}\equiv0}.
\]

PTR3 is a restricted sum of these correlations over \(h,k\). The random main scale for a fixed pair is only about \(1/p^2\), so a pointwise square-root error for each \((h,k)\) is far too large. What is needed is an average-over-\((h,k)\) third-factorial-moment estimate, a bilinear/trilinear large sieve, or a sheaf-theoretic result with cancellation after the gap variables are summed. In other words, this is a sparse Poisson/moment problem, not a fixed-shift character-sum problem.

## 7. What the existing resultants do prove

They do give genuine fixed-gap and very-small-gap exclusion.

If

\[
\log|R_{h,k}|<\log X,
\]

then no prime \(p>X\) can divide \(R_{h,k}\), so that gap pair contributes nothing in \((X,2X]\). From

\[
\log|R_{h,k}|\ll hk\log(2hk),
\]

all pairs satisfying

\[
hk\log(2hk)\le c\log X
\]

are excluded for a sufficiently small constant \(c\). In particular, one can exclude triples whose two gaps from the pin are both at most

\[
L\asymp \sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}}.
\]

Equivalently, apart from the controlled bad primes, every interval of this logarithmic length contains at most two representatives of \(Z_p^+\). This is a valid local-spacing consequence, but it is much weaker than the already targeted power bounds and does not control the total number of long-gap triples.

## Final answers to (a)–(d)

**(a)** For primitive integral models,

\[
\log|R_{h,k}|
\le d_k\log H_h+d_h\log H_k
 +\frac{d_k}{2}\log(d_h+1)
 +\frac{d_h}{2}\log(d_k+1).
\]

With \(d_j\ll j\) and \(\log H_j\ll j\log(2j)\), this is

\[
\log|R_{h,k}|\ll hk\log(2hk).
\]

This is an upper bound, not a two-sided order-of-magnitude statement.

**(b)** No. The sum written in the question is \(O(X^5)\) after using the dyadic divisor estimate. Replacing the crude root bound by the stronger inequality

\[
\#\{	ext{common roots mod }p\}\le v_p(R_{h,k})
\]

improves the direct method to \(O(X^4)\), still far from \(O(X/\log X)\).

**(c)** No automatic factor \(z_p/p\), and certainly no automatic \(1/p\), is available. Such a factor is precisely a pin/common-root decorrelation theorem. Even if a \(1/p\) factor were granted, the worst-case resultant budget would still give about \(X^3\), not PTR3.

**(d)** A moment method is the right conceptual route, but it needs a new uniform theorem such as GR2 plus PIN-MIX, or a direct pinned return-energy bound. The most plausible structural approaches are a transfer-matrix return large sieve, a renewal identity that collapses the two gap parameters, or a genuinely averaged three-point correlation theorem. Explicit estimation of individual resultants can remain a supporting tool for bad-prime support and small gaps, but it cannot be the main global summation argument.

The decisive next lemma should therefore be formulated at the family level:

```text
Pinned gap-return energy, dyadic form.

Σ_{X<p≤2X} Σ_{r∈Z_p^+}
  binom( #{1≤h≤(p-1)/2-r : N_h(f(r))=0 mod p}, 2 )
    ≪ X/log X,

proved through mixing/large-sieve structure of the h-recurrence,
not by summing individual resultant divisor bounds.
```
