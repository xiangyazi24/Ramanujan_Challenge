# 2. Exact rank-`H` charging to shifted resultants

Set

\[
t_\sigma:=(m-H)_+.
\tag{2.1}
\]

After deleting the `H` largest upper primes, the tail consists of

\[
q_1,\ldots,q_{t_\sigma}.
\]

For every `1\le i\le t_\sigma`, choose the next `H` larger neighbors

\[
q_{i+1},\ldots,q_{i+H}.
\]

This produces exactly

\[
H t_\sigma
\tag{2.2}
\]

ordered tail-partner incidences for the star.

Fix one such incidence `(i,j)`. The three positions

\[
z,\ a_i,\ a_j
\]

are pairwise distinct genuine Apéry zeroes modulo `p`. Sort them as

\[
x<x+r<x+r+s<p.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Reflection/no-wrap transfer gives

\[
p\mid N_r(x),
\qquad
p\mid N_s(x+r).
\tag{2.4}
\]

For

\[
S_{r,s}:=\operatorname{Res}_X
\bigl(N_r(X),N_s(X+r)\bigr),
\tag{2.5}
\]

we therefore have

\[
\boxed{p\mid S_{r,s}.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

The existing Sylvester ledger is

\[
c_p(r,s)
:=
\#\{x\bmod p:N_r(x)=N_s(x+r)=0\}
\le v_p(S_{r,s}),
\tag{2.7}
\]

with

\[
\log|S_{r,s}|\ll rs\log(r+s).
\tag{2.8}
\]

For fixed `(p,x,r,s)` and fixed sign, there are at most three choices for which point of the sorted triple was the primary `z`; after that the two secondaries and both upper primes are fixed. Thus the map from tail-partner incidences to marked resultant roots has an absolute bounded fibre. Pooling the two signs changes only that constant.

Consequently the rank condition gives the exact combinatorial inequality

\[
\boxed{
H E_{\rm tail}(T)
\ll
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}Z(p)(Z(p)-1)(Z(p)-2)
\ll
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}Z(p)^3.
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

This is the real `H` gain available from the cubic ledger.

## 2.1 Using all larger neighbors

\begin{aligned}

&=\frac{(m-H)(m+H-1)}2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.10}

\boxed{

 t_\sigma(m_\sigma+H-1)

\sum_{p\asymp_JT}Z(p)^3.

\tag{2.11}
\]

For a dense star with `m\gg H`, the true charge per tail edge is of order `m`, not merely `H`. But dividing a cubic zero count by `m\asymp Z(p)` returns a quadratic zero count. It does not create a new power saving.

# 4. Exact `6/7` dilation changes only constants in the resultant budget

depending on the location of the primary `z`.

# 5. What prime spacing proves, and what it does not prove

Let

\[
Y_r:=q_{i+r}-q_i.
\]

The elementary parity bound is

\[
Y_r\ge2r.
\tag{5.1}
\]

One may rigorously do somewhat better without assuming a prime number theorem in short intervals. Brun--Titchmarsh gives, uniformly in the relevant range,

\[
r
\le
\pi(q_i+Y_r)-\pi(q_i)
\ll
\frac{Y_r}{\log Y_r},
\]

unless `Y_r` is already a fixed positive proportion of `T`, in which case the following lower bound is trivial. Inverting gives

\[
\boxed{Y_r\gg r\log r.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
a_{i+r}-a_i
\gg
\lambda_\epsilon r\log r.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

At the threshold rank,

\[
a_{i+H}-a_i
\gg
\lambda_\epsilon H\log H.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Under the standard subpower assumption `log Omega=o(log T)`,

\[
H\log H
\asymp
\frac{T^{2/5}}{L^{1/5}\Omega(T)}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is a **lower** bound on the span. A block-resultant argument needs an **upper** bound placing many forced partners inside a short coordinate block. Prime spacing supplies the opposite inequality.

In particular, it is not legitimate to assert uniformly that the `H`-th larger prime lies within `O(H log T)` of `q_i`. Such an upper bound would be a lower-density theorem for primes in every relevant short interval. Nothing of that strength is available here.

Even if a lower cutoff `D` is inserted into the resultant sum, it does not help. For `c=12` or `14`,

\[
\sum_{\substack{D<d\le B\\c\mid d}}d
=
\frac{B^2-D^2}{2c}+O(B).
\tag{5.6}

At the optimizing scale below, (5.5) gives only `D\asymp B/\Omega`. Hence (5.6) retains a proportion

\[
1-O(\Omega^{-2})

of the full weighted gap budget. The rank-spacing restriction therefore removes the cheapest small gaps and leaves essentially the entire resultant-height exponent.

# 7. Why the cubic `H` gain is not a net threshold gain

For one prime `p`, a fixed sign and ordered pair `(z,a)` determine at most one upper prime. Therefore

\[
\sum_{z\in Z_p}
\sum_{\epsilon\in\{+,-\}}
m_\epsilon(p,z)
\le
2Z(p)(Z(p)-1).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Since deleting leaves only decreases this count,

\[
E_{\rm tail}(T)
\le
2\sum_{p\asymp_JT}Z(p)(Z(p)-1).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Using (0.3) gives (V3):

\[
E_{\rm tail}(T)
\ll_J
T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}.
\tag{7.3}

This is better than (6.9) by

\frac{T^{12/5}L^{4/5}\Omega}
     {T^{11/5}L^{-3/5}}
=
T^{1/5}L^{7/5}\Omega.

The reason is exact. At zero scale `Z(p)\asymp M`, a dense star has degree `m\asymp M`. Charging all larger neighbors gives about `M` resultant triples per tail edge. Thus the cubic ledger has a formal `H` gain, but it does **not** improve the best existing tail envelope.

# 8. The middle-scale saturation

\[
M_*:=T^{3/5}L^{1/5}.

Consider the numerical extremal profile

\[
N_*\asymp\frac{T}{L}

Z(p)\asymp M_*.

This is not asserted to occur for the Apéry sequence. It is an admissible profile for the present inequalities, and therefore proves that those inequalities alone do not imply a smaller tail.

\tag{8.1}

\tag{8.2}

\asymp

Every currently named geometric/support condition permits this scale:

1. **No consecutive zeroes and exact dilation.**  
   Even spacing by `12` or `14` uses total length `O(M_*)=o(T)`.

2. **Prime spacing.**  
   The rigorous minimum span for `M_*` primes is of order at least `M_*\log M_*`, but

   \asymp
   T^{3/5}L^{6/5}

   \]
   while `\log b_n\ll_JT`. Thus `b_n` has enough linear support capacity.

The unresolved issue is therefore not ordinary spacing. It is whether actual Apéry zeroes can correlate with the same-sign affine prime conditions densely at this middle scale. The current ledger has no theorem ruling that out.

\prod_{i=1}^m q_i^{m-1}.
\tag{9.1}

Even the rank-`H` tail charging requires powers of many `q_i` of order up to `H`. The selected-row statement supplies only

not

v_{q_i}(b_n)\ge H
\quad\text{or}\quad
v_{q_i}(b_n)\ge m-1.

Consequently the common row is a support carrier of capacity one per distinct upper prime. It has no proved pair capacity and cannot be multiplied into the lower resultant valuation gain.

# 10. Why choosing two of the `H` partners does not give a usable `H^2`

For each tail secondary `a_i`, choosing two of its `H` larger secondaries gives

\[
\binom H2
\asymp H^2
\]

triples of secondary zeroes. These triples omit the primary `z`.

A fixed secondary triple can be reused by as many as `Z(p)` possible primary zeroes, and the current prime-spacing/resultant ledger gives no bound improving that multiplicity. Thus the safe global charge is quartic:

\[
H^2E_{\rm tail}(T)
\ll
\sum_{p\asymp T}Z(p)^4,
\tag{10.1}

not cubic.

\ll
T^{2/3}

T^{52/15}L^{-2/5},

\[
E_{\rm tail}(T)
\ll
T^{8/3}L^2\Omega^2,
\tag{10.2}

which is worse than both (6.9) and (7.3).

Recovering the primary from a secondary triple would require using the absolute upper-prime shift, not merely the prime differences. That is precisely a new correlation theorem between the lower zero set and the affine upper-prime conditions; it is not contained in the gap-resultant ledger.

\sum_{\epsilon\in\{+,-\}}
\sum_{p\asymp_JT}
\sum_{z\in Z_p}
\binom{m_\epsilon(p,z)}2.
\tag{11.2}
\]

For every integer `m>H`,

\[
\binom m2-\binom H2
=
\frac{(m-H)(m+H-1)}2
\ge
H(m-H).
\tag{11.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_{\rm tail}(T)
\le
\frac{\mathcal W(T)}{H}.
}
\tag{11.4}
\]

Hence the estimate

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W(T)
\ll_J
\frac{H T^2}{L^2\omega(T)}
=
\frac{T^{12/5}}
     {L^{16/5}\Omega(T)\omega(T)}
}
\tag{11.5}
\]

for any `\omega(T)\to\infty` is sufficient.

A genuinely resultant formulation of the same missing theorem is the following.

> **Star-compressed multi-gap determinantal divisor.** There are prescribed integers `\mathfrak D_+(T),\mathfrak D_-(T)` such that
> \[
> v_p\!\left(\mathfrak D_\epsilon(T)\right)
> \ge
> \sum_{z\in Z_p}
> \binom{m_\epsilon(p,z)}2
> \]
> for every shell prime `p`, while
> \[
> \log|\mathfrak D_+(T)\mathfrak D_-(T)|
> \ll_J
> \frac{H T^2}{L\omega(T)}
> =
> \frac{T^{12/5}}
>      {L^{11/5}\Omega(T)\omega(T)}.
> \]

The valuation inequality would preserve the full occurrence multiplicity of all same-sign star wedges. The height inequality would then turn it into (11.5) after division by `\log p\asymp L`.

This is genuinely stronger than multiplying the existing pairwise `S_{r,s}`:

- the current product does give one `p`-unit for each marked pair;
- but its height is the sum of all corresponding `rs\log(r+s)` costs;
- the needed theorem compresses those costs at a common primary star while retaining the pairwise valuation exponent.

Without such a star-local multiplicity/height theorem, or an equally strong affine-prime/Apéry-zero correlation estimate, the threshold tail does not follow.

# Final conclusion

The rank-`H` neighbor condition can be converted rigorously into `H` shifted-resultant divisibilities, and the exact `6/7` dilation is fully compatible with that conversion. But:

- the product of `H` resultants has `H` times the height;
- prime spacing supplies a lower span, whereas the block method needs an upper span;
- the modulo `12/14` restriction has only constant density;
- the common `b_n` value pays each upper prime once, not once per pair;
- and the optimal block scale is governed by the middle zero density
  \(Z(p)\asymp T^{3/5}(\log T)^{1/5}\), where the cubic ledger collapses back to the existing second-moment envelope.

Thus the present package cannot prove the threshold tail. The sharp current obstruction is the middle-scale contribution of order

\[
T^{11/5}(\log T)^{-3/5},
\]

and the first natural sufficient replacement is the star-wedge multiplicity estimate (11.5), or its compressed multi-gap determinantal-divisor form above.
