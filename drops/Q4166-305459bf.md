# 2. Uniqueness of the fixed one-digit common lifts

This is the first new structural lemma.

## Lemma 2.1 — fixed-quotient common-lift classification

Let \(u\) be one of the two actual lower zero digits in a sign component, allowing replacement by its reflection \(p-1-u\). Consider a one-digit Lucas lift

\[
m=kp+u
\]

with a fixed nonnegative integer quotient \(k\), independent of the foreign prime in the fixed \((q,t)\)-fibre. Suppose the same integer is, for every legal \(p\) in that fibre, a selected q-digit lift

\[
m=\ell q+t
\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
m=\ell q+\bar t
\]

with fixed nonnegative \(\ell\).

Then on a noncentral state the only possibilities are

\[
\boxed{r\xrightarrow{(k,\ell)=(6,6)}t,}
\]

\[
\boxed{a_+\xrightarrow{(k,\ell)=(13,12)}t,}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{a_-\xrightarrow{(k,\ell)=(1,0)}\bar t.}
\]

No reflected orientation gives another nonnegative fixed quotient.

### Proof

Use \(p=q-d\).

For \(r=t+6d\), equality with the t-branch gives

\[
(k-\ell)q+(6-k)d=0.
\]

For it to hold throughout the foreign-prime fibre, the coefficient of \(d\) must vanish, so \(k=6\), and then \(\ell=6\).

Equality with the reflected q-digit gives

\[
(k-\ell-1)q+(6-k)d+2t+1=0.
\]

Again \(k=6\), leaving

\[
(5-\ell)q+2t+1=0.
\]

Since

\[
0<2t+1<q
\]

on a noncentral state, no integer \(\ell\) solves this.

For the reflected primary

\[
z=\bar t-7d,
\]

a match to the reflected q-digit gives

\[
(k-\ell)q-(k+7)d=0,
\]

forcing \(k=-7\), impossible. The t-branch has the same impossible \(d\)-coefficient, with an additional nonzero \(2t+1\) term.

For plus,

\[
a_+=t+13d-q.
\]

Matching to the t-branch gives

\[
(k-1-\ell)q+(13-k)d=0,
\]

hence \(k=13,\ell=12\). Matching to the reflected q-digit leaves, after \(k=13\),

\[
(11-\ell)q+2t+1=0,
\]

which has no solution. The reflected plus zero is

\[
p-1-a_+=q+\bar t-14d;
\]

its reflected-branch match forces \(k=-14\).

For minus,

\[
a_-=d-t-1.
\]

Matching to the reflected q-digit gives

\[
(k-\ell-1)q+(1-k)d=0,
\]

hence \(k=1,\ell=0\). Matching to the t-branch gives, after \(k=1\),

\[
(1-\ell)q-(2t+1)=0,
\]

which has no solution because \(0<2t+1<q\). The reflected minus zero is

\[
p-1-a_-=q+t-2d,
\]

and its t-branch match forces \(k=-2\).

This exhausts the two zeros, both reflection orientations and both selected q-digits. QED.

## Consequence

Within the complete fixed-quotient one-digit Lucas class there are exactly **two** common p/q rows in either sign. There is no third fixed common row available for a three-sample exterior annihilation of the divided upper jet.

This is an occurrence statement: the two rows are fixed by \((q,t,\sigma)\), not chosen per foreign prime. Allowing a quotient depending on the chosen \(p\) leaves the fixed-carrier class and is part of the growing-prefix continuation packet in Section 13.

# 13. Degree/height ledger and the quartic threshold

\[

\ll D(h)D(k)\log K

\[

then

\[
\sum_{h,k\le K}D(h)D(k)
\asymp

The current continuant and every first tangent family have \(\alpha=1\), hence

# 14. The delimited impossibility theorem

## Theorem 14.1 — no primitive \(K^{4-\delta}\) gain in the fixed-jet/bounded-transfer Apéry class

Let \(\mathscr A_{\rm fix}\) be the class of universal symbolic constructions obtained by a bounded number of the following operations:

1. use the two actual lower q6 zero coordinates and reflection;
2. use fixed nonnegative one-digit Lucas quotient lifts which are common to p and q throughout one fixed upper-state fibre;
3. divide exactly the forced target copies and localize at fixed shell-unit coefficients;
4. use the exact desingularized upper reflection relation (U2-aff);
5. use the proved regularized block jets through q^3;
6. use the rank-two recurrence transfer, bounded subresultants, bounded exterior/symmetric/tensor powers, Wronskians, norms and Fitting/content ideals;
7. saturate all universal reflection, cut-edge, restart, denominator, leading-coefficient and fixed-Lucas factors;
8. finally require one characteristic-zero carrier or moving polynomial which contains every legal foreign p occurrence with the prescribed occurrence multiplicity.

Do **not** allow an unbounded number of transfer rows or coefficients chosen separately for each foreign p after the final carrier is formed.

Then, on every legal noncentral residual state outside the fixed exceptional primes:

- the common one-digit p/q lift rank is exactly two;
- the upper divided-jet elimination ideal in those two rows is zero;
- the first moving tangent polynomial has degree exactly \(3h\);
- the first exterior cancellation is entirely the saturated determinant \(S_h^6\);
- bounded functorial connection operations add no independent target-selective row.

Therefore every surviving moving polynomial has linear degree in the moving gap whenever it contains the old lower occurrence set, and every separated-resultant ledger obtained in this class has quartic K-exponent. In particular there is no fixed \(\delta>0\) such that

\[
\sum\log|R^{\rm marked}_{h,k}|
\ll
K^{4-\delta}(\log K)^{O(1)}.
\]

### Scope guard

This theorem does **not** rule out:

1. an accidental arithmetic common factor of the actually evaluated Apéry integers which is not forced by the symbolic identities above;
2. a construction using a number of transfer rows growing linearly with h;
3. a new independent module not functorially generated by the rank-two Apéry connection;
4. a horizontal distribution theorem proving that the actual state-dependent contents are rarely large.

Those are genuinely outside \(\mathscr A_{\rm fix}\), not loopholes inside the proof.

# 15. Exact next class: linear-size growing-prefix marked subresultants

The previous theorem shows that another fixed determinant, another fixed exterior power, or another q-adic precision level is not the next useful experiment. The first algebraic class which escapes the proof is one where the **number of transfer rows grows with the lower gap**.

The reason is quantitative. A bounded number of recurrence rows can cancel only a bounded amount of principal degree, or else their determinant reduces to the rank-two boundary invariant. To make

\[
D(h)\ll h^{1-\varepsilon},
\]

one must cancel essentially all but a sublinear portion of the \(3h\) principal degree. That needs a growing-prefix syzygy, not a bounded-order minor.

Define the saturated transfer-prefix module

\[
\mathcal M_{h,m}(X)
=\operatorname{span}
\{N_h(X),N_{h+1}(X),\ldots,N_{h+m}(X)\}
\]

together with the matching denominator rows \(A_h,\ldots,A_{h+m}\), where \(m=m(h)\).

The next useful regime is

\[
\boxed{m(h)\asymp h,}
\]

not \(m=O(1)\). A prefix \(m=o(h)\) can at best remove \(o(h)\) leading coefficients and still leaves \(D(h)\asymp h\), hence gamma 4.

The exact continuation packet is as follows.

## Packet C — exact success criterion

