# Apéry reduced-denominator holes: exact identities, failed product routes, and a new sufficient stability lemma

## Verdict

I do **not** obtain a proof of

\[
\log \operatorname{rad}_{n/2<p\le n}\Delta_n=o(n)
\]

for every \(n\). The identity supplied in the question,

\[
v_p(\Delta_n)=\min(v_p(b_n),3)
\qquad (p\ge7,\ n/2<p\le n),
\]

is already the exact local description. The explicit companion sum and the factorial-scaled determinant explain why the local denominator hole occurs, but they do not currently compress the product of holes for different primes.

There is, however, a precise new sufficient lemma that is genuinely stronger than the target and is naturally adapted to the reduced denominators \(C_n\): **subexponential adjacent denominator variation**. Define

\[
T_n:=\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(C_n,C_{n+1})}{\gcd(C_n,C_{n+1})}.
\]

Then one has the unconditional divisibility

\[
\boxed{\Delta_n^2\mid T_{n-1}T_n.}
\]

Consequently, the following distinguished-state statement would prove the desired top-half estimate:

> **Adjacent denominator stability lemma.** For the Apéry companion solution \((a_0,a_1)=(0,6)\),
> \[
> \log T_n=o(n).
> \]

Indeed, it would give

\[
2\log\Delta_n\le \log T_{n-1}+\log T_n=o(n).
\]

This is not merely another notation for \(\Delta_n\): \(T_n\) measures **all** primewise changes between two adjacent reduced denominators, including changes unrelated to a top-half hole. It is therefore a strictly stronger regularity assertion. I prove the divisibility and all reductions below. What remains missing is an Apéry-specific theorem controlling the cancellation pattern in the explicit companion sum strongly enough to establish this adjacent stability.

## 1. Exact reduced-denominator algebra

Write

\[
a_n=\frac{A_n}{C_n},\qquad (A_n,C_n)=1,\quad C_n>0,
\]

and put

\[
L_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,n),\qquad D_n=L_n^3,
\qquad P_n=D_na_n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Since \(C_n\mid D_n\), reduction of the fraction \(P_n/D_n\) gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{C_n=\frac{D_n}{\gcd(D_n,P_n)}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently, for every prime \(p\),

\[
c_p(n):=v_p(C_n)
=v_p(D_n)-e_p(n),
\qquad
e_p(n):=\min\{v_p(D_n),v_p(P_n)\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus the reduced denominator is obtained from the universal lcm denominator by deleting exactly the prime powers canceled by the distinguished numerator \(P_n\).

For \(p>n/2\), away from the harmless endpoints where \(p=n\) or \(p=n+1\),

\[
v_p(D_{n-1})=v_p(D_n)=v_p(D_{n+1})=3.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Hence in the top-half window

\[
c_p(m)=3-\min(3,v_p(P_m)),\qquad m=n-1,n,n+1.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The denominator hole is therefore exactly a local peak of canceled content in the integer sequence \(P_m\).

## 2. The explicit companion sum and where the cancellation occurs

The companion solution has the classical finite formula

\[
\boxed{
 a_n=
 \sum_{k=0}^n
 \binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
 \left(
 H_n^{(3)}+
 \sum_{m=1}^k
 \frac{(-1)^{m-1}}
 {2m^3\binom nm\binom{n+m}{m}}
 \right),
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

where \(H_n^{(3)}=\sum_{m=1}^n m^{-3}\). It gives \(a_0=0\), \(a_1=6\), and satisfies the Apéry recurrence, hence equals the distinguished companion solution.

Formula (2.1) proves \(D_na_n\in\mathbb Z\), but its termwise denominators are much larger than the reduced denominator \(C_n\). The key point is that the removal of a top-half factor \(p\) from \(C_n\) is caused by cancellation **across the full sum**.

For \(p\ge7\), \(n=p+r\), \(0\le r<p\), the audited block calculation gives

\[
\boxed{p^3a_{p+r}\equiv 6b_r\pmod p.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since \(p^3\mid D_n\) and \(v_p(D_n)=3\), this is equivalently

\[
P_n/p^3\equiv 6b_r\pmod p
\]

after removing the other \(p\)-adic units in \(D_n\). Thus

\[
p\mid P_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_r
\qquad (n/2<p\le n),
\tag{2.3}
\]

with the valuation refined by the theorem in the question to

\[
3-c_p(n)=\min(v_p(b_n),3).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The neighboring indices are ordinary at the same prime, producing the pattern

\[
(c_p(n-1),c_p(n),c_p(n+1))
=(3,\,3-s,\,3),
\qquad s=\min(v_p(b_n),3),
\tag{2.5}
\]

for a top-half target. Therefore the hole depth is not a missing termwise denominator; it is the exact amount of global \(p\)-adic cancellation in (2.1).

This identifies why a naive product formula fails. Individual summands in (2.1) have factorial and harmonic denominators with explicit products, but reduction to \(C_n\) depends on their signed sum. No known factorization turns that signed cancellation into a product of local factors indexed by \(k\) or \(m\).

## 3. Factorial coordinates and the complete universal determinant

Set

\[
X_n=\frac{(n!)^3a_n}{6},\qquad Y_n=(n!)^3b_n.
\]

Then \(X_n,Y_n\in\mathbb Z\), with

\[
(X_0,X_1)=(0,1),\qquad (Y_0,Y_1)=(1,5),
\]

and both satisfy

\[
U_{n+1}=P(n)U_n-n^6U_{n-1},
\qquad P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The Casoratian is exactly

\[
\boxed{X_{n+1}Y_n-X_nY_{n+1}=(n!)^6.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is the full product formula supplied by the second-order recurrence. It controls common content of two adjacent state vectors, but it does not control the content of one distinguished coordinate strongly enough to bound \(\Delta_n\).

Indeed, putting

\[
f_n=\left(\frac{n!}{L_n}\right)^3,
\]

we have

\[
D_na_n=\frac{6X_n}{f_n},\qquad D_nb_n=\frac{Y_n}{f_n},
\]

and therefore

\[
\gcd(D_na_n,D_nb_n)
=\frac{\gcd(6X_n,Y_n)}{f_n}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The unexplained common content is exactly the original Apéry gcd. The determinant (3.2) is already smooth and explicit; taking more adjacent determinants does not generate an independent small integer divisible by every top-half hole prime.

## 4. Exact valuation formula for \(\Delta_n\)

For any positive integers \(C_{n-1},C_n,C_{n+1}\), define

\[
\Delta_n=
\frac{\gcd(C_{n-1},C_{n+1})}
{\gcd(C_{n-1},C_n,C_{n+1})}.
\]

At a prime \(p\), writing

\[
x=c_p(n-1),\qquad y=c_p(n),\qquad z=c_p(n+1),
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
v_p(\Delta_n)
=\min(x,z)-\min(x,y,z)
=(\min(x,z)-y)_+.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus \(\Delta_n\) records exactly the isolated downward holes of the primewise denominator profile.

For the Apéry solution and top-half primes, (2.5) gives

\[
v_p(\Delta_n)=s=\min(v_p(b_n),3),
\]

recovering the theorem assumed in the question.

## 5. The adjacent-transition product

Define

\[
T_n:=\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(C_n,C_{n+1})}
{\gcd(C_n,C_{n+1})}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Primewise,

\[
\boxed{v_p(T_n)=|c_p(n+1)-c_p(n)|.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the multiplicative symmetric difference between consecutive denominators.

### Theorem 5.1

For every sequence of positive integers \(C_n\),

\[
\boxed{\Delta_n^2\mid T_{n-1}T_n.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

At a prime \(p\), use \(x,y,z\) as above. By (4.1),

\[
v_p(\Delta_n)=(\min(x,z)-y)_+.
\]

If this is zero, the desired valuation inequality is trivial. Otherwise \(x>y\) and \(z>y\), so

\[
2v_p(\Delta_n)
=2(\min(x,z)-y)
\le (x-y)+(z-y)
=|x-y|+|z-y|.
\]

By (5.2), the right side is

\[
v_p(T_{n-1})+v_p(T_n).
\]

Hence

\[
2v_p(\Delta_n)\le v_p(T_{n-1}T_n)
\]

for every prime \(p\), proving (5.3). \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2

If

\[
\log T_n=o(n),
\tag{5.4}
\]

then

\[
\log\Delta_n=o(n),
\]

and therefore

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{n/2<p\le n}\Delta_n=o(n).
\]

This is the promised sufficient lemma.

## 6. Equivalent curvature form

Define the weighted positive denominator curvature

\[
\mathcal K_n
:=\sum_p
\bigl(c_p(n-1)+c_p(n+1)-2c_p(n)\bigr)_+
\log p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Whenever \(v_p(\Delta_n)>0\), both neighboring valuations exceed the middle one, and

\[
2v_p(\Delta_n)
\le c_p(n-1)+c_p(n+1)-2c_p(n).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{2\log\Delta_n\le \mathcal K_n.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Also, by \((u+v)_+\le |u|+|v|\),

\[
\mathcal K_n
\le \log T_{n-1}+\log T_n.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus either of the following would suffice:

\[
\mathcal K_n=o(n),
\tag{6.4}
\]

or the stronger adjacent stability (5.4).

For top-half primes, using (1.4), curvature of \(c_p\) is the negative of curvature of the truncated numerator content

\[
e_p(m)=\min(3,v_p(P_m)).
\]

Hence the actual-state statement needed is a bound on the total positive local peak of the cancellation content:

\[
\sum_{n/2<p\le n}
\bigl(2e_p(n)-e_p(n-1)-e_p(n+1)\bigr)_+
\log p=o(n).
\tag{6.5}
\]

This formulation is useful because \(P_n\) is the explicit integer obtained from (2.1), not an arbitrary recurrence solution.

## 7. Why ordinary log-convexity or subadditivity is insufficient

A real-variable inequality for the sizes \(C_n\), such as

\[
C_n^2\le C_{n-1}C_{n+1}
\quad\text{or}\quad
C_n^2\ge C_{n-1}C_{n+1},
\]

controls only the signed sum

\[
\log C_{n-1}+\log C_{n+1}-2\log C_n
=\sum_p
(c_p(n-1)+c_p(n+1)-2c_p(n))\log p.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The desired quantity is the sum of the **positive parts** prime by prime. Large negative curvature at unrelated primes can cancel large positive top-half curvature in (7.1). Therefore ordinary Archimedean log-convexity does not imply (6.4).

Likewise, the universal bound \(C_n\mid L_n^3\) gives only

\[
\log C_n\le 3\psi(n)=3n+o(n),
\]

and hence at best \(\log T_n=O(n)\). This is exactly the wrong scale: the full top-half primorial has logarithm \((1/2+o(1))n\), so linear denominator variation is compatible with all candidate primes being holes.

A successful convexity theorem must therefore be **primewise** or must control the total variation (5.2), not merely the scalar size of \(C_n\).

## 8. Why the explicit companion sum does not yet prove stability

The summand in (2.1) is hypergeometric times a harmonic correction. After multiplying by \(D_n\), every term is integral, but the reduced numerator \(P_n\) is a signed sum of exponentially many-sized integers. A top-half target prime is detected by the first residual digit

\[
p^3a_{p+r}/6\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\]

At a target, that digit vanishes. The next digit is not forced to vanish: the repository already records examples of exact valuation one. Therefore the cancellation is one-dimensional and isolated; it does not automatically propagate to a second independent equation or to a product factor shared across different primes.

The following tempting approaches fail for precise reasons.

1. **Termwise denominator product.** It bounds a common denominator before summation, namely \(D_n\), but \(C_n\) is obtained only after global cancellation. It cannot distinguish a target hole.

2. **Neighboring Casoratians.** They yield (3.2), whose right side is already supported on small primes. They constrain simultaneous content of adjacent states but do not force a small carrier for the middle-row cancellation peak.

3. **Scalar log-convexity.** It controls the signed curvature (7.1), not its primewise positive part.

4. **Density-one data.** A sparse exceptional sequence of \(n\) may still have linear top-half hole product. No density-one theorem implies the pointwise statement.

5. **Generic recurrence denominator propagation.** The current repository already contains arbitrary-hole countermodels for normalized second-order recurrences. Any proof of (5.4) must use the special finite sum (2.1), the Apéry initial state, or an equivalent arithmetic-geometric structure.

## 9. A sharper version of the missing lemma

The full adjacent stability lemma may be stronger than necessary. It is enough to control only primewise upward cancellation jumps in \(P_n\).

Define

\[
J_n^+
:=\prod_p p^{(e_p(n+1)-e_p(n))_+},
\qquad e_p(n)=v_p(\gcd(D_n,P_n)).
\tag{9.1}
\]

For primes that do not cross a jump of \(D_n\),

\[
(c_p(n)-c_p(n+1))_+
=(e_p(n+1)-e_p(n))_+.
\]

In particular, every top-half hole prime contributes to both \(J_{n-1}^+\) and the reverse jump at \(n\). Thus a still sufficient actual-state theorem is

\[
\boxed{
\log J_{n-1}^++\log J_n^- =o(n),
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

where

\[
J_n^-:=\prod_p p^{(e_p(n)-e_p(n+1))_+}.
\]

Equivalently, it suffices to prove a sublinear weighted total variation for the truncated cancellation content

\[
\sum_p |e_p(n+1)-e_p(n)|\log p=o(n).
\tag{9.3}
\]

This is a concrete theorem about the explicit integers \(P_n=D_na_n\). It is stronger than P3.2 but materially different: it asks that the set of primes whose cancellation depth changes between adjacent Apéry companion numerators has subexponential product.

A plausible route to (9.3) would require one of the following genuinely new inputs:

- a factorization or arithmetic-intersection formula for the primitive numerator of (2.1) whose boundary divisor changes by \(e^{o(n)}\) per step;
- a primewise subadditivity theorem for denominators of this specific Hadamard \(G\)-function;
- a two-index resultant whose value has height \(e^{o(n)}\) and contains every prime where the cancellation depth changes;
- an arithmetic-geometric model in which \(C_n\) is an integral lattice index and adjacent lattices differ by a subexponential elementary divisor.

No such theorem is presently established in the repository or in the standard Apéry/Beukers denominator theory.

## 10. Final status

The exact chain now available is

\[
\text{top-half target}
\Longleftrightarrow
p\mid b_n
\Longleftrightarrow
\text{a cancellation peak in }P_n
\Longleftrightarrow
\text{a denominator hole in }C_n
\Longleftrightarrow
p\mid\Delta_n.
\]

The explicit companion sum proves the first cancellation digit, and the reduced-denominator formula (1.1) turns it into an exact hole. The recurrence determinant gives the complete universal adjacent product identity. None supplies horizontal compression across different primes.

The clean new sufficient lemma is:

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(C_n,C_{n+1})}
{\gcd(C_n,C_{n+1})}=o(n)
\quad\text{for the distinguished Apéry companion.}
}
\]

Together with \(\Delta_n^2\mid T_{n-1}T_n\), it would prove the desired result immediately. Establishing it requires new Apéry-specific control of global denominator cancellation; ordinary recurrence theory, scalar log-convexity, lcm bounds, and density-one information do not suffice.
