# Q385: CRT equidistribution of Apéry zero residues

## Executive conclusion

The proposed route does **not** follow from reflection, the \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\)-factorization, Hecke/Sato–Tate, or the supersingular interpretation presently available in the project.

The most important correction is immediate:

> For an odd prime \(p\), reflection \(r\mapsto p-1-r\) has the unique fixed point \((p-1)/2\). Hence, if \(|Z_p|=1\), then the “single zero” is not a random residue at all:
> \[
> a_p=\frac{p-1}{2}.
> \]

Consequently, for two singleton primes,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(a_p,a_q)=\frac{pq-1}{2}.}
\]
Thus the singleton CRT classes are **maximally structured**, not equidistributed. Their interval indicator is exactly
\[
\mathbf 1_{\{\operatorname{CRT}(a_p,a_q)\le N\}}
=
\mathbf 1_{\{pq\le 2N+1\}}.
\]

For noncentral reflected pairs, reflection gives complementary CRT points, but it does not force the centered interval errors to cancel. The \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\)-root statement concerns **evaluation roots** of the truncated Apéry polynomial, whereas \(Z_p\) is a set of **vanishing coefficients**, equivalently vanishing finite Mellin coefficients. The known Hecke congruence concerns only the central index. Sato–Tate does not control the same-characteristic divisibility event \(p\mid a_p(f)\), and it supplies neither the moving-target uniformity nor the two-prime joint information required by the CRT sum.

The simplest quantifier-correct input is therefore a **two-characteristic centered dispersion theorem for the marked Apéry coefficient-zero sets**. In the notation already isolated in [`oracleB_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex), this is the marked horizontal second-moment estimate
\[
\left|
 \sum_{p\ne q}\sum_m X_p(m)X_q(m)-\frac{T_N^2}{N}
\right|
\ll N^{o(1)}T_N,
\]
or its blockwise analogue over all \(P>\sqrt N\). Equivalently, in the direct CRT/Fourier language, one needs a bilinear large-sieve estimate for
\[
\sum_{p,q}F_p(h\bar q)F_q(h\bar p),
\qquad
F_p(u)=\sum_{a\in Z_p}e_p(ua),
\]
uniformly in the growing frequencies needed to detect the short interval. This is a genuinely horizontal, two-prime theorem. None of the four listed one-prime structures gives it automatically.

## 0. Two preliminary corrections

### 0.1 The singleton model is exceptional

Empirically most nonempty \(Z_p\) have size \(2,4,6,\dots\), not \(1\). Reflection forces every noncentral zero to occur with its partner \(p-1-r\). A singleton can occur only at the center \((p-1)/2\).

Thus the notation “write \(a_p\) for the single zero” describes precisely the **central nonordinary slice**, not a generic model for the Apéry zero sets.

### 0.2 An \(O(N)\) energy estimate is sufficient, not equivalent

A bound
\[
\sum_{n\le N}B(n)^2\ll N
\]
implies
\[
\max_{n\le N}B(n)\ll \sqrt N,
\]
which is far stronger than the required \(o(n/\log n)\) bound and is therefore sufficient for P3.2 after the usual prime-weight bookkeeping.

The converse is false in general: a pointwise estimate \(B(n)=o(n/\log n)\) need not imply an \(O(N)\) second moment.

# 1. What reflection symmetry actually gives

Let
\[
\iota_p(a)=p-1-a\equiv -1-a\pmod p.
\]
For \(a\in Z_p\), \(b\in Z_q\), put
\[
x(a,b)=\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(a,b)\in[0,pq).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{x(\iota_p(a),\iota_q(b))=pq-1-x(a,b).}
\]
Indeed, \(-1-x(a,b)\) has the required two reflected residues.

If
\[
y=x(\iota_p(a),b),
\]
then similarly
\[
x(a,\iota_q(b))=pq-1-y.
\]
Hence the four CRT points attached to two reflected pairs split into two complementary pairs:
\[
\{x,pq-1-x\},
\qquad
\{y,pq-1-y\}.
\]

## 1.1 Singleton zeros are completely deterministic

If \(|Z_p|=1\), reflection gives
\[
a_p=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]
For \(x=\operatorname{CRT}(a_p,a_q)\),
\[
2x+1\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad
2x+1\equiv0\pmod q.
\]
Thus \(pq\mid 2x+1\). Since \(0\le x<pq\),
\[
1\le2x+1<2pq,
\]
so necessarily
\[
2x+1=pq.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{x=\frac{pq-1}{2}.}
\]

So on the singleton subfamily the proposed Bernoulli variable is actually
\[
\varepsilon_{pq}
=
\mathbf1_{\{pq\le2N+1\}}-\frac{N}{pq}.
\]
There is no residue equidistribution to prove here: the problem becomes a weighted count of pairs of **central nonordinary primes** across the hyperbola \(pq=2N\).

A sufficiently strong quantitative sparsity theorem for those central primes could dispose of this slice separately. But the project’s known congruence
\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f)\pmod p,
\qquad
f=\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4,
\]
turns the singleton condition into the nonordinary event \(p\mid a_p(f)\), and no currently available Sato–Tate theorem gives the needed same-characteristic divisibility estimate.

## 1.2 Complementarity is not cancellation

For one complementary pair \(\{x,pq-1-x\}\), its contribution to the lower interval is
\[
\mathbf1_{\{x\le N\}}
+
\mathbf1_{\{pq-1-x\le N\}}
-
\frac{2N}{pq}.
\]
If \(pq>2N+1\), the two indicators cannot both be \(1\), but the expression can still equal either
\[
1-rac{2N}{pq}
\quad\text{or}\quad
-rac{2N}{pq}.
\]
There is no forced sign cancellation.

Reflection only says that the number of marked CRT points in \([0,N]\) equals the number in the reflected interval
\[
[pq-1-N,pq-1].
\]
It does not say that either number is close to its density \((N/pq)|Z_p||Z_q|\).

The anchored-star construction in [`hm3_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex) is explicitly reflection-invariant. It arranges that one point in every complementary pair lies at the same moving anchor. Thus reflection is fully compatible with maximal positive CRT discrepancy.

## 1.3 Fourier effect of reflection

Define
\[
F_p(h)=\sum_{a\in Z_p}e_p(ha).
\]
Pairing \(a\) with \(-1-a\) gives
\[
e_p(ha)+e_p(-h(1+a))
=
2e_p(-h/2)
\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi h(a+1/2)}p\right).
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{e_p(h/2)F_p(h)\in\mathbf R.}
\]
This removes an inessential phase and kills the antisymmetric part of the zero-set measure. It supplies no useful upper bound for \(|F_p(h)|\), and therefore no cross-prime cancellation by itself.

**Answer to Question 1:** reflection gives an exact complement symmetry and makes singleton CRT points deterministic. It does not force the desired cancellation in \(\sum\varepsilon_{pq}\).

# 2. Why the \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\)-root structure is in the wrong space

To avoid a notation collision, write the truncated Apéry polynomial as
\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j.
\]
The factorization discussed in the project has the form
\[
A_p(t)=\Delta(t)^{\epsilon_p}B_p(t)^2,
\]
with \(\deg B_p=\Theta(p)\), and computations indicate that the irreducible factors of \(B_p\) have degree at most \(2\).

This concerns roots in the **evaluation variable** \(t\). But
\[
Z_p=\{j:b_j=0\pmod p\}
\]
is a set of positions in the **coefficient/Mellin variable** \(j\).

For \(1\le j\le p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
 b_j=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-j}.}
\]
Thus \(b_j\) is a finite Mellin coefficient of the value function \(A_p(t)\). The condition \(j\in Z_p\) is a vanishing Mellin coefficient, not the assertion that \(j\) is a root of \(A_p\) or \(B_p\).

The repository contains a concrete separation at \(p=7\):
\[
A_7(t)=(t-1)^2(t^2+1)^2\pmod7,
\]
while none of its coefficients vanishes, so
\[
Z_7=\varnothing.
\]
See Proposition “Coefficient zeros are not Hasse-polynomial roots” in [`oracleB_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex).

Therefore the conjugate pairing
\[
\alpha\longleftrightarrow\alpha^p
\]
of evaluation roots imposes no presently known constraint on the locations of the Mellin zeros \(j\in Z_p\). Moreover, \(\deg B_p=\Theta(p)\), so even the evaluation-root family is not a fixed bounded-degree family to which a uniform elementary Weil bound can simply be applied.

**Answer to Question 2:** the \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\)-Galois structure does not presently control the CRT distribution of \(Z_p\), because it concerns a different zero locus.

# 3. The Hecke-eigenvalue connection is only the central slice

The known modular congruence is
\[
 b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f)\pmod p,
 \qquad f=\eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4.
\]
It detects whether the **central** residue belongs to \(Z_p\). It does not parameterize the noncentral reflected pairs.

This has three consequences.

1. If \(|Z_p|=1\), then the singleton condition is precisely the central nonordinary condition
   \[
   p\mid a_p(f).
   \]
2. It gives no formula for a generic noncentral zero \(a\in Z_p\).
3. Even on the central slice, ordinary Sato–Tate is not the required theorem.

Sato–Tate controls the distribution of the normalized real numbers
\[
\frac{a_p(f)}{2p^{3/2}}
\]
in fixed real intervals. The event
\[
p\mid a_p(f)
\]
is a shrinking, same-characteristic congruence condition. It is not a fixed interval condition, and in weight \(4\) the divisibility event is not reduced by Deligne’s bound to the single equality \(a_p(f)=0\).

Moreover, the desired CRT estimate is a two-prime statement involving \(p,q\) and modular inverses. Even perfect one-prime information about the distribution of \(a_p(f)\) would not supply the required joint distribution.

**Answer to Question 3:** the Hecke congruence may isolate and perhaps eventually control the rare central/singleton contribution, but it does not address the main noncentral CRT problem. Sato–Tate alone is insufficient even for the central divisibility event.

# 4. Why the supersingular/Sato–Tate heuristic does not apply

There are two distinct notions being conflated.

- For a fixed elliptic curve over \(\mathbf Q\), Sato–Tate varies the prime and studies its Frobenius trace. The curve’s \(j\)-invariant is fixed.
- For a family over \(\mathbf F_p\), a supersingular locus consists of parameter values inside that characteristic. Its distribution is a vertical moduli problem, not the Sato–Tate distribution of one fixed curve.

More importantly, the Apéry index
\[
j\in Z_p
\]
is a Mellin-character index. It is not presently identified with a supersingular \(j\)-invariant or with an evaluation parameter of the K3/elliptic family. The fixed-family route would have to pass through a crystalline Mellin transform and construct a marked coordinate \(c_{p,j}\) satisfying
\[
c_{p,j}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b_j=0\pmod p.
\]
That marked object is precisely what is specified, but not yet constructed with the needed uniformity, in [`oracleB_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex).

Even a hypothetical marginal equidistribution of integer representatives \(j/p\) would not be enough. The CRT phase depends jointly on \(p,q\) through modular inverses, and the anchored star can have highly coherent CRT classes while its one-prime residues appear broadly spread.

**Answer to Question 4:** no. Sato–Tate does not imply that Apéry zero indices divided by \(p\) are equidistributed, and such marginal equidistribution would still not prove the pair CRT estimate.

# 5. Marginal equidistribution cannot control CRT pairs

For one chosen residue \(a_p\) at each prime, let
\[
x_{pq}=\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(a_p,a_q).
\]
For an integer frequency \(h\), CRT factorization gives
\[
\boxed{
 e_{pq}(h x_{pq})
 =e_p(h\bar q\,a_p)e_q(h\bar p\,a_q),}
\]
where
\[
\bar q=q^{-1}\pmod p,
\qquad
\bar p=p^{-1}\pmod q.
\]
For general zero sets,
\[
\sum_{a\in Z_p}\sum_{b\in Z_q}
 e_{pq}\!\left(h\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(a,b)\right)
=
F_p(h\bar q)F_q(h\bar p).
\]

Thus the direct Fourier object is the bilinear sum
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_h(P,Q)
=
\sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\Q<q\le2Q\\p\ne q}}
F_p(h\bar q)F_q(h\bar p).}
\]
The arguments of \(F_p\) and \(F_q\) depend on the **other prime**. A theorem asserting only that \(a_p/p\) is equidistributed, or that \(F_p(h)\) is small for each fixed \(h\), does not control this bilinear inverse-twisted family.

The scale-dependent anchored star shows the logical gap sharply. Choose a moving integer \(m_0=m_0(N)\) and set
\[
a_p=m_0\bmod p.
\]
Then for every prime pair
\[
\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(a_p,a_q)=m_0
\]
as soon as \(m_0<pq\). Hence all CRT points align, regardless of how irregular or apparently spread the individual fractions \(a_p/p\) look. The reflection-invariant version in [`hm3_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex) preserves the local structural constraints already proved for the Apéry model.

Therefore any successful theorem must be genuinely **joint in the two characteristics**.

# 6. The clean direct Fourier target

After dyadically localizing \(p\asymp P\), \(q\asymp Q\), a Fourier or Erdős–Turán treatment of the interval indicator reduces the discrepancy to weighted sums of \(\mathcal S_h(P,Q)\) over a growing frequency range.

A sufficient direct input would be a statement of the schematic form
\[
\mathcal S_h(P,Q)
\ll
(PQ)^{1/2}(\log N)^{-A}
\]
with enough uniformity in

- \(P,Q\ge\sqrt N\),
- the frequencies needed for a sharp or smoothed cutoff,
- the varying zero-set multiplicities,
- and all dyadic boxes,

so that the frequency and box sums are \(o(N)\).

The exact logarithmic exponent and cutoff depend on the chosen smoothing. The invariant content is clear: one needs square-root-type cancellation in a **prime-pair Kloosterman/Mellin sum with moving marked zero sets**.

Reflection turns the factors into real cosine sums after a phase rotation, but it does not prove this estimate. The \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) factorization does not identify the marked Mellin zeros. Sato–Tate does not see the inverse-twisted two-prime sum.

# 7. The simplest quantifier-correct Apéry input

The most robust formulation uses exact finite marginals and avoids all one-column boundary errors.

For the relevant prime set define
\[
\Omega_p=\{0\le m<N:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad
A_p=|\Omega_p|,
\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{A_p}{N},
\]
and
\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|.
\]
Let
\[
K(m)=\sum_p\mathbf1_{\Omega_p}(m),
\qquad
\mu=\sum_p\rho_p.
\]
Then the exact identity is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m<N}(K(m)-\mu)^2
=
\sum_p A_p(1-\rho_p)
+
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(J_{p,q}-\frac{A_pA_q}{N}\right).}
\]

Thus the simplest useful input is
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(J_{p,q}-\frac{A_pA_q}{N}\right)
\right|=o(N),}
\]
or \(O(N)\) if the goal is merely an \(O(N)\) energy bound.

This is not just a renaming of one-prime equidistribution: it asserts that the actual two-prime CRT loci have the correct aggregate finite-marginal mass. It is exactly the horizontal anti-clustering property that excludes an anchored star.

For the top block, [`oracleB_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex) isolates the concrete version
\[
X_p(m)=
\mathbf1_{\{p\le m\}}
\mathbf1_{\{b_{m-p}\equiv0\pmod p\}},
\qquad
T_N=\sum_{p,m}X_p(m),
\]
\[
F_N=
\sum_{p\ne q}\sum_m X_p(m)X_q(m),
\]
and the desired marked two-characteristic dispersion estimate
\[
\boxed{
\left|F_N-\frac{T_N^2}{N}\right|
\ll N^{o(1)}T_N.}
\]
When \(T_N\asymp N/\log N\), this is \(o(N)\). A summable blockwise version over every \(P>\sqrt N\) gives the full moving-target dispersion needed by the analytic reduction.

```text
Minimal arithmetic package
--------------------------
1. Mark the coefficient/Mellin zero b_j ≡ 0 (mod p) inside a fixed
   integral crystalline or sheaf-theoretic object.
2. Prove a horizontal two-characteristic large-sieve/dispersion bound
   for those marked zeros, uniform in the moving character j and in p,q.
3. Insert that bound into the exact finite-marginal identity above.
```

The first step is where the K3/supersingular structure may eventually enter. The second step—not ordinary Sato–Tate—is the decisive missing theorem.

# 8. Answers to the five questions

## 1. Does reflection force cancellation?

No. It gives
\[
x\longleftrightarrow pq-1-x
\]
and makes singleton zeros central. For singleton primes the CRT point is exactly \((pq-1)/2\), so the distribution is deterministic. For doublets it gives two complementary CRT pairs, but a lower-interval indicator is not antisymmetric and no centered cancellation follows. The anchored-star obstruction is reflection-invariant.

## 2. Does the \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\)-Galois structure constrain the CRT distribution?

Not with the currently identified objects. The \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) roots are evaluation roots of \(A_p(t)\) or its square root. The set \(Z_p\) consists of zero Mellin coefficients \(b_j\). These are different zero loci, and the degree of the evaluation polynomial grows linearly with \(p\).

## 3. Can Hecke eigenvalues and Sato–Tate solve it?

Only the center has a known Hecke interpretation. Sato–Tate does not control \(p\mid a_p(f)\) in the required weight-four, same-characteristic sense, and it gives no information about noncentral zeros or pairwise inverse-twisted CRT phases.

## 4. Does the supersingular connection imply \(a_p/p\) is equidistributed?

No. It conflates an evaluation parameter with a Mellin-character index and conflates vertical supersingular loci with Sato–Tate for a fixed curve. Even genuine marginal equidistribution of zero indices would not imply the required two-prime CRT independence.

## 5. What is the simplest Apéry-specific input?

The finite-marginal two-characteristic dispersion estimate
\[
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(J_{p,q}-\frac{A_pA_q}{N}\right)=o(N),
\]
preferably proved through a uniform bilinear bound for
\[
\sum_{p,q}F_p(h\bar q)F_q(h\bar p).
\]
In the project’s geometric language, this is the marked crystalline Mellin dispersion statement from [`oracleB_result.tex`](../problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex). It is the quantifier-correct fixed-family target.

# Bottom line

The direct CRT idea correctly identifies the desired **pairwise horizontal discrepancy**, but the listed structures do not provide it.

\[
\boxed{
\text{One-prime equidistribution is not enough; the missing input is a two-prime marked Mellin large sieve.}
}
\]

Reflection is useful diagnostically—it shows that singleton zeros are central and that the singleton CRT classes are \((pq-1)/2\)—but it does not create cancellation. The \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) and supersingular structures live initially in the evaluation variable, while the Apéry zeros live in the Mellin variable. The Hecke congruence controls only the central slice. A proof must therefore construct and disperse the **marked coefficient-zero condition across two varying characteristics**.
