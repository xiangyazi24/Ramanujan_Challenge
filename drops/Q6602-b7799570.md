\qquad
K=T^z,
\qquad
W=T^w.
\tag{9.1}
\]

On the critical line,

\[
\nu(z)=\frac{14}{5}-3z=2-2d.
\tag{9.2}
\]

### 9.1 Lower zero geometry

For every lower-prime label, place two edge subclusters \(A_p,B_p\), each of size \(\asymp U\), inside an interval of length \(\asymp W\). Separate the two subclusters by a fixed positive fraction of \(W\), and choose their internal spacing \(\asymp W/U=T^{w-d}=T^{d/2}\).

Retain a constant fraction of the pairs

\[
(a,b)\in A_p\times B_p
\]

whose direct rank lies in the prescribed \(D\asymp U\) block. Then:

```text
edge zeros:             U = W^(2/3);
rows per lower prime:   U^2 = T^(2d);
direct rank:            D ~ U = T^d;
direct span:            G ~ W = T^w.
```

Reflect both edge subclusters and add \(K-O(U)\) symmetrically placed central zeros. The direct/reflected rank identity then gives

\[
E_0=K-O(U)\asymp K,
\]

and every reflected-cross physical span is \(p-O(W)\asymp T\). The large spacing also respects no-consecutive-zero and interval-capacity constraints at the exponent level.

With

\[
N=T^{2-2d}=T^{\nu(z)}
\]

lower-prime labels, the lower third moment is exactly critical:

\[
NK^3
=T^{2-2d+3z}
=T^{14/5}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The total rows are

\[
NU^2=T^{2-2d+2d}=T^2.
\tag{9.4}
\]

### 9.2 Exact same-state clustering for `P`

Partition the \(N\) lower-prime labels into

\[
C=T^{1-d}
\]

clusters, each containing

\[
L=T^{1-d}
\]

lower primes. This is possible because \(CL=T^{2-2d}=N\).

Within one cluster write \(p=p_0+\Delta\). Starting from base edge subclusters \(A_0,B_0\), translate them by

\[
A_p=A_0+7\Delta,
\qquad
B_p=B_0+14\Delta.
\tag{9.5P}
\]

For a base pair \((a,b)\), its translated gap is

\[
G_p=(b+14\Delta)-(a+7\Delta)=G_0+7\Delta.
\]

The exact `P` state is therefore

\[
q=\frac{7(p_0+\Delta)-(G_0+7\Delta)}6
  =\frac{7p_0-G_0}{6},
\]

\[
t=(G_0+7\Delta)-1-(a+7\Delta)
  =G_0-1-a.
\tag{9.6P}
\]

Both \(q\) and \(t\) are independent of \(\Delta\). Thus every selected pair state is repeated at all \(L=T^{1-d}\) lower primes in its cluster, exactly matching (5.7P).

### 9.3 Exact same-state clustering for `M+`

The analogous `M+` translation is

\[
A_p=A_0-\Delta,
\qquad
B_p=B_0+7\Delta.
\tag{9.5M}
\]

Then

\[
G_p=G_0+8\Delta,
\]

and

\[
q=\frac{8(p_0+\Delta)-(G_0+8\Delta)}7
  =\frac{8p_0-G_0}{7},
\]

\[
t=\frac{p_0+\Delta-G_0-8\Delta}{7}-1-(a-\Delta)
  =\frac{p_0-G_0}{7}-1-a.
\tag{9.6M}
\]

Again the state is independent of \(\Delta\), exactly matching (5.7M).

The cluster diameter is \(T^{1-d+o(1)}\), while

\[
1-d<w
\]

for \(1/2\le d\le3/5\). Hence these translations fit inside the \(W\)-scale margins.

### 9.4 State counts and upper moments

Choose the base subclusters as equal-step grids. Their differences have \(\asymp U\) values, and every difference diagonal contains \(\asymp U\) pairs. Thus one lower-prime cluster produces

```text
U upper-prime labels q;
U selected digits t at each q;
U^2 selected states;
load L at every selected state.
```

Choose the upper-prime sets from distinct lower-prime clusters to be disjoint. Then

\[
\#\{q\}=CU=T^{1-d+d}=T,
\tag{9.7}
\]

\[
\#\{(q,t)\}=CU^2=T^{1+d},
\tag{9.8}
\]

and every state has exact load

\[
\boxed{k(q,t)=L=T^{1-d}.}
\tag{9.9}
\]

Declare the \(U\) diagonal digits, together with their reflections, to be the upper zero set in the relevant \(O(W)\) interval. Then

\[
\sum_q Z(q)^3
\asymp T\,U^3
=T^{1+3d}
\le T^{14/5},
\tag{9.10}
\]

with equality at \(d=3/5\), i.e. at \(z=2/3\). The upper interval bound is saturated, not violated.

The total marked row count is

\[
(T\text{ upper primes})
\cdot(T^d\text{ selected digits per }q)
\cdot(T^{1-d}\text{ lower primes per state})
=T^2.
\tag{9.11}
\]

The centered pair mass is

\[
T^{1+d}\cdot T^{2(1-d)}
=T^{3-d},
\tag{9.12}
\]

exactly the neutral exponent in (8.5) with \(\eta=0\).

Integrality classes, primality masks, exact cell flags, and bounded physical-source multiplicities can be inserted as constant or \(T^{o(1)}\) thinning in this interface model. This construction is not offered as an existence theorem for actual Apéry zeros; it is a witness that the landed inequalities, reflection, affine maps, and carrier capacities alone leave a critical configuration.

At every selected state, the product of its \(L=T^{1-d}\) lower-prime labels has logarithmic size \(T^{1-d+o(1)}\), so a fixed-state integer of logarithmic height \(O(T)\) can carry all of them to first order. No carrier interface is violated.

## 10. First failed implication

The exact unavailable implication is

```text
K = Z(p) ~ T^z,
edge zero count U <= W^(2/3),
direct rank D ~ T^d,
reflected rank E ~ K,
D + E = K + 1 - 2u,
W and direct span G ~ T^w,
reflected span barG ~ T,
actual P or M+ affine map,
q prime and t in Z(q),
raw-minus-first and every inherited exact mask,
committed lower and upper third zero moments

    do not imply

an independent third lower-zero equation at a prescribed position;
a second legal reflected row in the same cell;
two P/M+ rows at the same lower prime and selected state;
p^2 in a fixed-state carrier;
a bounded cross-lower-prime state fibre;
or a centered selected-state tail with any uniform fixed-power gain.
```

What is proved is narrower and exact:

```text
E ~ K is automatic, with deficit D+2u-1;
barG ~ T is forced but reflection-trivial;
the four-zero orbit has only two independent zeros;
fixed (p,q,t,cell) fibre is one;
fixed-q selected digits lie in an O(W) interval;
a supercritical face forces state load T^(1-d-eta);
the missing consumer has exponent 3-d-2eta.
```

The endpoint \(z=2/3\) is the sharp obstruction. There

\[
d=3/5,
\qquad
B_c=T^{8/5+o(1)},
\qquad
R_{z,\eta}=T^{2/5-\eta},
\]

so the localized window gain merges exactly into the global selected-state threshold and the consumer returns to

\[
T^{12/5-2\eta+o(1)}.
\]

Therefore the corrected `P`-small/`M+` face is not closed by the saturated reflected rank, the third zero moment, reflection, or the exact upper-state maps. The sharp next interface is the face-restricted, cell-separated far selected-state tail (8.9), and the unconditional additional exponent on the complete continuation remains zero.
