# P3.2 — Prime-aspect Bombieri–Vinogradov for Apéry Fourier transforms

## Short answer

There is no existing Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem that directly applies to

\[
S_{q,r}(h)=\sum_{p\in(X,2X]}\widehat Z_p(h(qr)^{-1}\bmod p),
\]

because the summand is not a fixed trace function evaluated at the prime. It is a family of trace functions whose conductor and definition vary with p through the Apéry zero set

\[
Z_p=\{a<bmod p:b_a\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

However, the Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel (FKM) philosophy is the right framework. The missing ingredient is a uniform l-adic sheaf interpretation of the Fourier transform of the zero indicator.

A plausible route is:

1. replace the indicator of Z_p by a Frobenius trace on a sheaf attached to the Apéry hypergeometric motive;
2. prove geometric monodromy and bounded conductor;
3. apply Deligne/FKM large sieve to obtain square-root cancellation in the prime aspect.

This is not currently a theorem for Apéry zeros, but the formulation matches known trace-function technology.

---

## (A) The inner sum is not a classical BV sum

Classical Bombieri–Vinogradov controls sums such as

\[
\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)1_{n\equiv a\pmod q}
\]

uniformly in a modulus range. After Vaughan/large sieve reductions one encounters trace sums of the form

\[
\sum_{p\le x}t(p),
\]

where t is a fixed trace function.

Our object is instead

\[
\widehat Z_p(\alpha)
=
\sum_{a\in Z_p}e_p(\alpha a).
\]

Expanding:

\[
S_{q,r}(h)=
\sum_p\sum_{a<p\atop b_a\equiv0(p)}
 e_p(a h(qr)^{-1}).
\]

The prime p simultaneously determines:

- the finite field F_p;
- the recurrence reduction b_n mod p;
- the zero locus Z_p;
- the additive character e_p.

Therefore this is not of the form f(p)e_p(g(p)) with fixed algebraic f,g.

The obstruction is not the varying phase h(qr)^{-1}; that part is harmless. The obstruction is the moving set Z_p.

---

## (B) Relation to FKM trace functions

The relevant FKM framework is:

\[
t_p(x)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_x|\mathcal F),
\]

where \mathcal F is a fixed l-adic sheaf over an algebraic variety.

If we could construct a sheaf \mathcal F_A on Spec(Z)[1/S] whose fiber over p satisfies

\[
\mathbf 1_{Z_p}(a)=F_p(a)
\]

for a trace function F_p of bounded conductor, then

\[
\widehat Z_p(\alpha)
=
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p}F_p(a)e_p(\alpha a)
\]

would be a finite-field Fourier transform of a trace function.

Deligne's Fourier transform theory would preserve bounded conductor, and FKM results would become applicable.

The Apéry recurrence is especially promising because:

- it comes from a rank-2 hypergeometric/elliptic motive;
- the associated K3 realization has controlled monodromy;
- the Mellin transform description already gives a sheaf whose Frobenius traces recover Apéry coefficients.

The missing step is that

\[
a\mapsto1_{b_a\equiv0(p)}
\]

is a zero-indicator of a sequence, not directly a Frobenius trace.

---

## (C) Large sieve / second moment route

The desired estimate is essentially

\[
\sum_{q,r}|S_{q,r}(h)|^2
\ll
(X/\log X)^3.
\]

Expanding:

\[
\sum_{q,r}|S_{q,r}|^2
=
\sum_{p_1,p_2}
\sum_{q,r}
\widehat Z_{p_1}(h(qr)^{-1})
\overline{\widehat Z_{p_2}(h(qr)^{-1})}.
\]

The diagonal p_1=p_2 contributes

\[
\sum_p\sum_{q,r}|\widehat Z_p(\alpha)|^2.
\]

By Parseval over F_p,

\[
\sum_{\alpha\bmod p}|\widehat Z_p(\alpha)|^2
=p|Z_p|.
\]

Thus the diagonal is compatible with square-root cancellation if |Z_p| is small.

The difficult term is the off-diagonal correlation:

\[
\sum_{q,r}
\widehat Z_p(h(qr)^{-1})
\overline{\widehat Z_\ell(h(qr)^{-1})},
\qquad p\ne\ell.
\]

This is exactly a cross-prime decorrelation problem.

The existing Apéry program has already reduced this type of question to independence of the local objects

\[
(F_p,F_\ell)
\]

coming from the Mellin sheaf. A product-monodromy theorem such as

\[
SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\times SL_2(\mathbf F_\ell)
\]

would imply the needed cancellation heuristically.

Hence HM_3 and the prime-aspect BV estimate are essentially the same missing input.

---

## (D) Is there a sheaf on Spec Z giving Z_p?

Not directly in the current formulation.

There is a sheaf for the Apéry motive, but it gives Frobenius traces such as

\[
a_p(f),\qquad b_n\bmod p,
\]

rather than the subset

\[
Z_p=\{n:b_n=0\}.
\]

The operation

\[
\text{Frobenius trace} \longrightarrow \text{zero set of the trace sequence}
\]

is nonlinear. In particular, zero indicators are not generally l-adic trace functions.

A possible replacement is to study the rank-drop variety:

\[
\Pi_n e_1\equiv(0,*)\pmod p,
\]

where

\[
\Pi_n=M(n-1)\cdots M(0).
\]

This turns Z_p into a hitting problem for an algebraic dynamical system on \mathbf P^1. The sheaf would then live on the orbit space of the transfer matrices rather than directly on Spec Z.

This is closer to the Ostafe–Shparlinski dynamical trace-function setting.

---

## Consequence for P3.2

A full BV theorem would give:

\[
S_{q,r}(h)=O(X^{1/2+o(1)})
\]

for almost all pairs (q,r), which would close the decoupled Weyl-sum argument.

The realistic intermediate theorem is weaker:

\[
\sum_{q,r}|S_{q,r}(h)|^2
\ll
X^{3+o(1)}/(\log X)^3.
\]

This requires only:

1. Parseval control of each Z_p;
2. pairwise decorrelation of Apéry zero sets for p\ne q;
3. a large sieve inequality for the associated Mellin/transfer-matrix sheaves.

Therefore:

- Classical Bombieri–Vinogradov: **not directly applicable**.
- FKM trace-function machinery: **likely the correct technology, but requires constructing the right sheaf**.
- Large sieve second moment: **the most promising near-term target**.
- Existing Apéry motive sheaves: **control b_n traces, not the zero indicators Z_p**.

The missing theorem is a "moving-target Apéry trace large sieve": a uniform bound for correlations of the Fourier transforms of the zero sets Z_p across distinct primes.
