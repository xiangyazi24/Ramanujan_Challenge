# 1. Exact plus-star reconstruction

Fix a lower prime `p` and an actual canonical primary zero

\[
z\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\},
\qquad p\mid b_z.
\]

An actual assigned-plus extension with upper prime `q` has

\[
d:=q-p,
\qquad
a=6d-1-z,
\tag{1.1}
\]

where `a` is the actual secondary lower zero. Conversely,

\[
\boxed{
 d=\frac{z+a+1}{6},
 \qquad
 q=p+\frac{z+a+1}{6},
 \qquad
 u=p-a-2z-2,
 \qquad
 n=7p-1-z.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus `(p,z,a)` reconstructs at most one physical plus row. Primality, upper selectedness, shallow filters, high-row membership, and minus-first assignedness can only remove the reconstructed candidate.

For two actual rows in the same star, ordered by `q_i<q_j`,

\[
\boxed{
 a_i<a_j,
 \qquad
 a_j-a_i=6(q_j-q_i).
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

All shell primes are odd, so `q_j-q_i` is even. Hence

\[
\boxed{a_j-a_i\in12\mathbb Z_{>0}.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Also

\[
z+a_i+1=6d_i
\]

is even, so `z+a_i` is odd. Therefore `z` and `a_i` have opposite parity and in particular

\[
z\ne a_i.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The same holds for `a_j`. Hence `{z,a_i,a_j}` consists of three distinct actual `p`-zero positions.

The minus-first assignment rule is not used to create an extra zero. It merely restricts the already reconstructed plus rows. Every pair counted below is therefore an actual assigned-plus pair, but all local estimates remain valid after forgetting assignedness.

# 2. Exact balance-defect intervals

Fix one endpoint `(p,z,a_i)` and abbreviate

\[
a:=a_i,
\qquad y:=a_j>a.
\]

Sort the three integers `{z,a,y}` as

\[
x<x+h<x+h+k,
\qquad
\beta:=|h-k|.
\]

Since `beta` is integral,

\[
\beta\le E
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta\le E_0:=\lfloor E\rfloor.
\tag{2.1}
\]

There are exactly three relative orders.

## 2.1 Order `z<a<y`

Here

\[
h=a-z,
\qquad
k=y-a,
\]

and therefore

\[
\beta=|2a-z-y|.
\]

Thus every admissible successor lies in

\[
\boxed{
I_1(p,z,a;E)
:=
[2a-z-E_0,\,2a-z+E_0]
\cap[a+1,p-1]\cap\mathbb Z.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Its inclusive cardinality satisfies

\[
\boxed{|I_1|\le2E_0+1.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

## 2.2 Order `a<z<y`

Now

\[
h=z-a,
\qquad
k=y-z,
\]

so

\[
\beta=|2z-a-y|.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
I_2(p,z,a;E)
:=
[2z-a-E_0,\,2z-a+E_0]
\cap[z+1,p-1]\cap\mathbb Z,
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{|I_2|\le2E_0+1.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

## 2.3 Order `a<y<z`

Here

\[
h=y-a,
\qquad
k=z-y,
\]

and

\[
\beta=|2y-a-z|.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
I_3(p,z,a;E)
:=
\left[
 \left\lceil\frac{a+z-E_0}{2}\right\rceil,
 \left\lfloor\frac{a+z+E_0}{2}\right\rfloor
\right]
\cap[a+1,z-1]\cap\mathbb Z,
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

and the interval before intersection has real length `E_0`, so

\[
\boxed{|I_3|\le E_0+1.}
\tag{2.7}
\]

## 2.4 At most two intervals

If `z<a`, only `I_1` can occur. If `a<z`, a successor can lie below `z`, in `I_3`, or above `z`, in `I_2`. These two sets are disjoint.

Consequently, for fixed `(p,z,a_i)`, all successors `a_j>a_i` satisfying `beta<=E` lie in at most two integer intervals, one containing at most `2E_0+1` integers and the other containing at most `E_0+1` integers.

This proves item **(i)** after the exact rounding correction.

# 3. The balance defect is never zero in the plus lattice

# 4. Arbitrary-subinterval zero capacity

Let `C_A` be the uniform constant in the committed theorem

\[
Z_p(I)
:=\#\{t\in I:p\mid b_t\}
\le C_A|I|^{2/3}
\tag{4.1}
\]

for every ordinary subinterval `I` of `{0,...,p-1}`.

Every actual successor `a_j` is a `p`-zero. Upper-prime primality, the upper zero `q_j|b_{u_j}`, assignedness, high-row membership, and every later frozen-pair predicate only decrease the candidate set. Thus Sections 2.1--2.3 give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\#\{a_j>a_i:\text{actual assigned plus},\ \beta\le E\}
&\le
 Z_p(I_1)
\end{aligned}
\]

in the first order, and

\[
\le Z_p(I_2)+Z_p(I_3)

when `a_i<z`.

\[
\boxed{
\#\{a_j>a_i:\beta\le E\}

For `E>=1`, this is

\boxed{

This proves item **(ii)**.

# 9. Concrete affine prime-label family proving noncontainment

This section proves noncontainment of the deterministic ranges and affine equations. It deliberately does **not** declare the constructed digits to be Apéry zeros.

\[

\qquad

\tag{9.1}

\[

\qquad
\gamma=\frac1{200}

\[
d_i:=q_i-p=(\theta+o(1))p,
\qquad
G:=q_j-q_i=(\gamma+o(1))p.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Such prime labels exist in fixed positive-length proportional intervals by the ordinary prime number theorem; no twin-prime or simultaneous fixed-linear-form theorem is being invoked. Since all three primes are odd,

\[
d_i,G\in2\mathbb Z.

\[
\boxed{

 \qquad

 \qquad

a_i=6d_i-1-z,

 a_j=6(d_i+G)-1-z.
