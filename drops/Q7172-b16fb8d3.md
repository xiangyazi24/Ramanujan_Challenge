# P32 DPLS long-transfer R2

## 1. Transfer matrix

The Apéry recurrence used in the project is the second-order recurrence written through the normalized ratio variable.  If

\[
N_n(z)\equiv (n!)^3 b_n(z)
\]

is the anchored continuant numerator, then the one-step transfer is a determinant-one projective matrix after scalar normalization.  In the standard basis used by the proof files it has the form

\[
T_n=\begin{pmatrix}
A_n&-n^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A_n=(2n+1)(n^2+n+1)
\]

(up to a scalar in GL_2, which does not change PGL_2).  Thus a long return is a product

\[
T_{r+L-1}\cdots T_r\in PGL_2(\mathbb F_p).
\]

The separated-pair problem is exactly a correlation problem for these nonautonomous products.

## 2. What elementary identities can and cannot do

Reflection gives

\[
b_{p-1-n}=b_n,
\]

and the Wronskian/continuant identities control determinants of nearby products.  They are sufficient for bounded separation, overlap, and reflection collisions.

They do not remove a gap L comparable with p.  The reason is algebraic: the product

\[
T_{r+L-1}\cdots T_{r}
\]

contains L independent coefficient positions modulo p.  The addition formula

\[
N_{x+1+h}(-1)=N_{x+2}(-1)N_h(x)-(x+1)^6N_{x+1}(-1)N_{h-1}(x+1)
\]

only replaces the long interval by two shorter intervals with shifted base point x.  It does not reduce the number of arithmetic parameters unless one proves new cancellation for the family of shifted products.

Therefore the short-chain algebra gives no deterministic elimination of the separated term.

## 3. Plausible averaged theorem needed

The weakest useful statement is an averaged projective mixing estimate:

For every pair of bounded-gap endpoint conditions defining a short chain, and every projective states a,b,

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}\sum_{L\le p}
\left|
\#\{r:T_{r+L,r}(a)=b\}-\frac{p}{p+1}
\right|^2
\ll X^{1+o(1)}.
\]

A theorem of this strength would imply the required variance estimate after inserting the number of short patterns (H^O(1), H=X^{1/2}) and applying Cauchy--Schwarz.

This is not a direct consequence of currently available Deligne bounds: the family is nonautonomous and the sheaf attached to

\[
(r,L)\mapsto T_{r+L-1}\cdots T_r
\]

has conductor growing with the degree of the product description.

## 4. Relation to existing technology

Possible tools:

* Deligne's Riemann Hypothesis over finite fields controls trace functions of fixed conductor.
* Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel large sieve methods control families of trace functions with bounded conductor.
* Katz's work on hypergeometric sheaves gives strong monodromy results for genuinely hypergeometric families.

However, the Apéry transfer product here is not currently identified with a fixed-conductor l-adic sheaf whose trace is the required projective transition indicator. Therefore these results cannot presently be cited as proving the needed estimate.

The needed statement is a new conjectural "Apéry nonautonomous transfer mixing" theorem.

## 5. Parameter bookkeeping

Let H=\sqrt X.

Number of short chains per state is at most polynomial in H.  The diagonal/overlap terms are bounded by counting configurations with O(H) possible offsets.

The separated contribution has roughly

\[
\sum_{p\sim X} \sum_{r,L} 1_{T_{r+L,r}(q)=q'}.
\]

Without cancellation this is of size

\[
\pi(X)X^2=X^3/\log X,
\]

far above the target

\[
X^2/\log X\cdot X^{o(1)}.
\]

A square-root cancellation theorem in the projective transfer variable is therefore necessary.

## 6. Fail-closed conclusion

No existing theorem located in the standard trace-function literature directly applies to this nonautonomous Apéry PGL_2 product. Reflection, Wronskians, and continuant addition identities do not prove the required long-transfer cancellation.

The narrow rigorous next statement is:

> Prove that the family of Apéry transfer products defines a geometrically irreducible bounded-conductor l-adic sheaf after suitable state-space compression, with bounded projective monodromy. Then Deligne/Katz/Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel machinery would yield the needed averaged mixing estimate.

At present this compression and bounded-conductor assertion is the missing lemma; treating it as known would be an unjustified literature claim.
