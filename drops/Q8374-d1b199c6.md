## 2. The exact extra theorem that would close GH1

### 2.1 Weakest scalar form

The direct statement (GH1-W3)

\[
\sum_m H(m)(k_D(m))_3
\ll X^{o(1)}\Lambda^3\sum_m H(m)
\]

is the weakest clean correlation I can isolate.  It is target-selective in all three senses:

1. it uses only the actual far-`m` range;
2. it uses only the actual GH endpoint weight `H(m)`;
3. it asks only for an upper tail / positive correlation, not absolute discrepancy.

If the last proved pair-CRT reduction is written as `\sum_m H(m)\ll\mathcal B_X`, then (GH1-W3) gives immediately

\[
\mathcal G_X\ll X^{o(1)}\Lambda^3\mathcal B_X,
\]

which is the desired replacement in (1.1).

### 2.2 Localizable triple-Palm form

Suppose the exact prefix labels in the local proof are denoted by `\pi`, with exact indicators

\[
\beta_\pi(m)\in\{0,1\},\qquad
k_D(m)=\sum_\pi\beta_\pi(m).
\]

Let `d_\pi` be the exact finite marginal used for that same far-`m` box (or the manuscript's already normalized prefix density), with

\[
\sum_\pi d_\pi=\Lambda X^{o(1)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
(k_D(m))_3
=\sum_{\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3\ \mathrm{distinct}}
\beta_{\pi_1}(m)\beta_{\pi_2}(m)\beta_{\pi_3}(m).
\]

Define the one-sided weighted triple excess

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak C_{3,H}:={}&
\sum_{\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3\ \mathrm{distinct}}
\Bigg(
\sum_m H(m)\prod_{i=1}^3\beta_{\pi_i}(m)\\
&\hspace{35mm}
-\Big(\prod_{i=1}^3d_{\pi_i}\Big)\sum_m H(m)
\Bigg)_+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{TP3-GH}
\]

**[CONDITIONAL THEOREM]** If

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak C_{3,H}
\ll X^{o(1)}\Lambda^3\sum_mH(m),
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

then (GH1-W3), hence GH1, follows.

Indeed, summing the baseline and the positive excess gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_mH(m)(k_D(m))_3
&\le
\left(
\sum_{\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3\ \mathrm{distinct}}
\prod_i d_{\pi_i}
\right)\sum_mH(m)+\mathfrak C_{3,H}\\
&\le
\left(\sum_\pi d_\pi\right)^3\sum_mH(m)+\mathfrak C_{3,H}\\
&\ll X^{o(1)}\Lambda^3\sum_mH(m).
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the theorem I would target next.  A uniform bound for every triple is stronger than needed.  An absolute-value sum is stronger than needed.  An unweighted HM3 theorem is not aligned with the target weight.  (TP3-GH) is precisely the aggregate positive correlation that the GH1 proof consumes.

If the local unpushed manuscript normalizes the finite prefix marginals differently, only (2.1) should be changed; the proof above is formal.

## 3. Literal masks versus an unmasked autocorrelation

There is a useful analytic majorant, but it must not be mistaken for an identity.

Let

\[
C_g(m):=\sum_s A_m(s)A_m(s+g)
\]

with `A_m` extended by zero outside `[-X,X)`.  If `E_g^{\rm lit}(m)` is the number of **literal masked** edges, then

\[
E_g^{\rm lit}(m)\le C_g(m).
\]

For any simple shift graph, every edge has two endpoints, hence

\[
V_g(m)\le2E_g^{\rm lit}(m)\le2C_g(m).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\left(V_g-\tau\right)_+
\le2\left(C_g-\frac\tau2\right)_+,
\qquad
\tau:=\frac{\widetilde L^2}{4X}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is safe for an upper bound.  The reverse comparison is false in general, and (3.2) changes the centering.  In particular, Fourier analysis of `C_g` does **not** by itself prove a statement for the literal folded-mask support at its manuscript baseline.  A successful energy/spectral route needs a mask-aware transfer with the correct mean, not merely the observation that the unmasked set is pseudorandom.

This distinction matters because positive-part sums are sensitive to a deterministic baseline mismatch.

## 4. Additive energy: exact best-case saving, and why it is too small

This is the one route for which the exponent shortfall can be quantified very cleanly.

Fix `m`, put

\[
S_m=\{s:A_m(s)=1\},\qquad k=|S_m|=k_D(m).
\]

Embed the interval by zero-padding in a cyclic group of size

\[
N=4X+O(1),
\]

large enough that ordinary differences do not wrap.  Then

\[
C_g=|S_m\cap(S_m-g)|,
\qquad
\sum_g C_g=k^2,
\]

and the additive energy is

\[
E(S_m)=\sum_g C_g^2.
\]

With `\mu=k^2/N`, Parseval gives the exact variance identity

\[
\sum_g(C_g-\mu)^2
=E(S_m)-\frac{k^4}{N}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since the centered deviations sum to zero,

\[
\sum_g(C_g-\mu)_+
=\frac12\sum_g|C_g-\mu|
\le
\frac12\sqrt{N}
\left(E(S_m)-\frac{k^4}{N}\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Notice that `N\asymp4X`, so the natural edge baseline is exactly of order `k^2/(4X)`, the GH1 scale.

Now grant the **optimistic random-energy law**

\[
E(S_m)-\frac{k^4}{N}
\ll k^2X^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is already much stronger than what follows from the presently proved Apéry gap bounds.  Then (4.2) gives only

\[
\sum_g(C_g-\mu)_+
\ll kX^{1/2+o(1)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Against the trivial total edge mass `k^2`, the maximal fluctuation saving is therefore

\[
\frac{k}{\sqrt X}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

In the current H16 top-strip regime

\[
L=X^{14/15+\eta+o(1)},
\qquad
\frac1{120}<\eta<\frac2{105},
\]

and at the maximal isolation-density scale `k\asymp L`, (4.5) is

\[
X^{13/30+\eta+o(1)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

GH1 is short by `X^{7/5+o(1)}`.  Thus **even random additive energy leaves**

\[
X^{7/5-(13/30+\eta)+o(1)}
=
X^{29/30-\eta+o(1)}
\tag{4.7}
\]

unrecovered.  In the allowed eta window this residual exponent lies in

\[
\boxed{
\frac{199}{210}<\frac{29}{30}-\eta<\frac{23}{24}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

So additive energy misses by almost a full power of `X` even under a best-case random-energy input.

There are two further losses hidden by this optimistic computation.

* `H(m)` is built from literal **vertex support**, not the unmasked edge count.  A mask-aware mean/energy theorem is needed before (4.2) can be centered at the manuscript baseline.
* Because `k=k_D`, inserting (4.4) into GH1 produces an integrand of size roughly `(k)_3 k\sqrt X`, i.e. a fourth-load moment.  Unless all selected rows are already confined to `k\asymp L`, this has moved the difficulty upward rather than eliminated the three-prefix factor.

**Verdict:** additive energy can be a useful auxiliary regularity lemma, but it cannot close the stated three-prefix deficit.

## 5. Classical large sieve: the `Q^2` barrier and the wrong moment

The Montgomery--Vaughan large sieve has the schematic sharp form

\[
\sum_{q\le Q}\ \sum_{a\bmod q}^{*}
\left|\sum_{n=M+1}^{M+N}c_ne(an/q)\right|^2
\ll (N+Q^2)\sum_n|c_n|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

There are two natural places to try it here.

### 5.1 In the far-`m` variable

For shell moduli `q\asymp X`, the remotely visible pair-Palm normalization uses an `m` interval of length `M=X^2`.  Thus

\[
N\asymp X^2,\qquad Q\asymp X,\qquad N+Q^2\asymp X^2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the critical large-sieve scale.  It reproduces second-order quasi-orthogonality, exactly the level already supplied more sharply by pair CRT.  It does not turn a third factorial load into three sparse density factors.  The repo's anchored-star construction proves that primewise constraints plus the natural second moment do not force the third moment.

If one expands `(k_D)_3` first and then applies Cauchy--Schwarz in order to use (5.1), the square contains **six** prefix incidences.  No sixth-prefix input is presently available; this is strictly worse than (TP3-GH).

### 5.2 In the endpoint `s` variable

Here `N_s\asymp X` while the relevant shell moduli are still `Q\asymp X`, so

\[
N_s+Q^2\asymp X^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The `Q^2` term dominates the interval length by a factor `X`.  Classical large sieve therefore cannot provide the required sparse, pointwise-in-`m` shift decorrelation.

### 5.3 Positive part

Even if a signed Fourier estimate for

\[
\sum_g c_g\left(V_g-\frac{\widetilde L^2}{4X}\right)
\]

were available, GH1 uses the `L^1` positive part over `O(X)` shifts.  Passing from a spectral `L^2` estimate to this positive part costs `X^{1/2}` by Cauchy--Schwarz unless extra structure is proved.  Formula (4.2) is exactly that loss in energy language.

**Verdict:** the classical large sieve is a repackaging of pair/second-moment information at the relevant scales, not a source of the missing `\rho_X^3`.

## 6. Linnik dispersion: where the sixth-order object appears

Linnik's dispersion method is potentially attractive because the shell-prime condition gives a bilinear congruence after the two endpoints are opened.  But the exact GH1 weight is hostile to the standard dispersion closure.

Let the three prefix labels in `(k_D)_3` be opened.  A typical missing term is

\[
\sum_m H(m)
\left(
\beta_{\pi_1}(m)\beta_{\pi_2}(m)\beta_{\pi_3}(m)
-\text{density baseline}
\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Ordinary dispersion centers (6.1), applies Cauchy--Schwarz, and opens the square.  The resulting correlation has two triples of prefix labels, hence up to **six distinct defining-prime conditions**.  Pair CRT controls two labels, not six.

One could hope for a Type-II factorization which reduces those six labels.  The shell divisor part alone has such a factorization: write

\[
m-s=pa,
\]

with `p\in(X,2X]`.  However the exact coefficient also contains

\[
\mathbf 1_{p\mid b_r}
\times
\mathbf 1_{\text{isolated}}
\times
\mathbf 1_{\text{high-Kummer}}
\times
\mathbf 1_{\text{legal orientation}},
\tag{6.2}
\]

with `r` the actual row dictated by the quotient/fold.  This is not a separable divisor-function coefficient.

The formal additive-character detector

\[
\mathbf1_{p\mid b_r}
=\frac1p\sum_{t\bmod p}e_p(tb_r)
\tag{6.3}
\]

does not fix the problem.  The phase `r\mapsto b_r` is a nonautonomous recurrence value, not a fixed-degree rational phase to which the standard bilinear/Kloosterman dispersion machine applies.

Dropping (6.2) restores a tractable divisor problem, but it discards exactly the sparse defining-characteristic density whose three copies are missing.  That cannot close GH1.

**Verdict:** standard dispersion converts the desired third-prefix theorem into a sixth-prefix theorem unless a new arithmetic factorization of the actual Apéry indicator is supplied.  The method is therefore not presently a proof route; the missing input is again (TP3-GH), or something stronger that implies it.

## 7. Kuznetsov / spectral route: a real shifted-convolution skeleton, but the coefficient is not automorphic

There is a genuine shifted-convolution equation inside a pair of endpoint incidences.  If

\[
m-s=pa,
\qquad
m-(s+g)=qb,
\]

then exactly

\[
pa-qb=g.
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is the right algebraic skeleton for delta methods, Kloosterman sums, and the Kuznetsov trace formula.

If the coefficients in `(p,a)` and `(q,b)` were divisor-like or automorphic, the Kuznetsov formula and the Deshouillers--Iwaniec spectral large-sieve technology would be relevant.  But the actual coefficient is the defining-characteristic Apéry predicate plus the isolation/high-Kummer/orientation masks.  There is presently no transform expressing that coefficient as a fixed family of Fourier coefficients or as a bounded-conductor Kloosterman weight.

Treating it as an arbitrary coefficient leaves only its `L^2` norm.  The spectral large sieve then gives an `L^2` result, and the positive part over `g` again costs a square root of the number of shifts.  More importantly, it still leaves `(k_D)_3` outside.

A favorable scale check reinforces the point.  If, counterfactually, opening (7.1) produced one classical Kloosterman modulus of size

\[
c\asymp pq\asymp X^2,
\]

then a pointwise square-root Kloosterman estimate would gain only `X^{1+o(1)}` over the trivial `c` scale.  The GH1 deficit is `X^{7/5+o(1)}`.  Further family cancellation could in principle do more, but GH1 takes a positive part in `g`, so signed cancellation cannot simply be inserted without another `L^2`/dispersion step -- which returns to the sixth-prefix obstruction above.

This scale check is not a claim that the exact GH kernel is Kloosterman; the stronger statement is the opposite: no such exact Kloosterman transform is presently known because of (6.2).

**Verdict:** there is a legitimate shifted-convolution geometry, but no known spectral theorem acts on the actual coefficient.  A new arithmetic transform of `p|b_r` would have to precede Kuznetsov.

## 8. Frobenius / sheaf routes: defining characteristic is the hard wall

This part must be kept exact.  The event in `A_m(s)` is

\[
p\mid b_r
\]

where `p` is the same prime that defines the finite field.  It is not permissible to replace this by a fixed-`\ell` trace condition and quote Deligne.

### 8.1 What Deligne does give

For a fixed finite field of characteristic `p` and an `\ell`-adic sheaf with `\ell\ne p`, Deligne's Weil II gives square-root cancellation for complete trace sums under the standard purity/no-invariant hypotheses.  This is enormously useful for fixed-prime Apéry/K3 trace functions.

It does **not** say that the reduction of the relevant algebraic trace at the prime above the **base characteristic** is nonzero.  A complex Frobenius trace may be perfectly generic and still be divisible by `p`.

### 8.2 Compatible-system large sieve

Perret-Gentil's large sieve for Frobenius works over a fixed finite field of characteristic `p` and varies auxiliary valuations `\lambda\mid\ell` in a compatible system, with `\ell\ne p`.  Its sieve reductions therefore do not include the forbidden diagonal `\ell=p`.

The GH1 event sits precisely on that diagonal for every moving shell prime.  Setting the auxiliary prime equal to the base characteristic is not a specialization of that theorem.

### 8.3 Mellin equidistribution

The repository's exact fixed-exponent Mellin reduction records, in each quotient cell,

\[
M_p(m)\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Forey--Fresan--Kowalski construct the convolution/tannakian Fourier framework and obtain character-average equidistribution.  Bah--Shuddhodan's 2026 uniform stratified-vanishing theorem goes further: bounded-complexity perverse sheaves over finite fields of **varying characteristic** with a common tannakian group are allowed.

That removes the old objection "the characteristic varies".  It does **not** remove the present obstruction.  The target follows one deterministic character/quotient section for each prime and asks whether its trace is zero **after reduction at the prime above that same `p`**.  All-character complex/unitary equidistribution can leave one selected character exceptional for every `p`; its mass is only `1/(p-1)` inside the character family.  It also does not convert complex equidistribution into defining-prime divisibility.

The visible Q7703 audit gives an explicit warning of this type: a lifted trace can be nonzero in characteristic zero while its reduction is zero at `p`.

### 8.4 Even a fantasy primewise square-root theorem is not enough for triple density on `X^2`

There is a useful parameter no-go which is independent of the sheaf-model objection.

Grant the counterfactual best case that every centered prefix indicator admits bounded-complexity complete Fourier sums with square-root cancellation modulo each prime `p\asymp X`.  For three distinct prefix primes the CRT period is

\[
Q_3=p_1p_2p_3\asymp X^3.
\]

Factoring complete sums prime by prime would give an error of size at best

\[
Q_3^{1/2}X^{o(1)}=X^{3/2+o(1)}
\tag{8.2}
\]

for one completed triple correlation.  The independent triple density is

\[
\rho_X^3=X^{-7/5+o(1)}.
\]

On an `m` interval of length `M`, its main term has size

\[
M X^{-7/5+o(1)}.
\]

For (8.2) to be smaller than that main term one needs

\[
\boxed{
M\gg X^{3/2+7/5+o(1)}=X^{29/10+o(1)}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

On the remotely visible pair-CRT scale `M=X^2`, this fails by

\[
X^{9/10+o(1)}.
\]

Thus even a hypothetical **individual** Deligne-quality theorem for each prefix condition would not by itself prove the required triple density on the `X^2` box.  One still needs averaging over triples / a density-sensitive dispersion theorem.  If the unpushed GH1 file uses a different `m` length, (8.3) is the exact threshold to substitute.

**Verdict:** current Frobenius technology has both the wrong observable (fixed `\ell` or character-average versus same-prime divisibility) and, even under a fantasy repair, insufficient individual-completion strength for the triple-density scale.

## 11. What a genuinely successful analytic theorem must look like

The next theorem should not be advertised as "another large sieve" unless it has a density-sensitive **third-order** conclusion.  The minimum useful shape is one of these equivalent-strength target-selective forms.

### Direct weighted form

\[
\sum_mH(m)(k_D(m))_3
\ll X^{o(1)}\Lambda^3\sum_mH(m).
\]

### Aggregate one-sided triple form

\[
\mathfrak C_{3,H}
\ll X^{o(1)}\Lambda^3\sum_mH(m).
\]

A theorem of this kind must use arithmetic not present in pair CRT.  Plausible sources would be:

1. a genuinely defining-characteristic arithmetic identity coupling three prefix primes;
2. a new horizontal `p`-adic/Cartier theorem controlling the selected same-prime sections, not all characters on average;
3. an exact transform of `1_{p|b_r}` into a bilinear/spectral coefficient with uniform conductor and enough averaging over **three** moving primes.

Without one of these, the known methods stop at pair information or at a signed `L^2` statement.

The most important negative lesson is also the simplest: proving that `V_g` is beautifully pseudorandom for every fixed `m` is not by itself GH1.  The missing `X^{7/5}` is attached to the external three-prefix load.  The theorem has to say that the rows on which `H(m)` is large do **not** simultaneously support three prefix incidences at the worst-case `P_X^3` rate.
