# P3.2 endpoint closure: primary-source audit and constructive relative Shioda–Inose attack

## Decisive ruling

The current endpoint package is **not yet closed by the cited literature**.

The local Picard–Fuchs, Jordan, Kodaira, ADE-resolution, and invariant-cycle calculations establish the correct local rank and local inertia data. They do **not** by themselves construct the global algebraic correspondence needed to identify the primitive rank-three sheaf with a symmetric square. The sentence in `problems/3.2/toric_fiber_sym2.tex` saying, in effect, that the displayed rational maps in the literature have graphs which automatically give the Shioda–Inose correspondence after reduction at every `p>=5`, is stronger than the primary sources I could verify.

What the sources actually supply is:

1. a generic complex or function-field Shioda–Inose/Kummer description;
2. explicit elliptic fibrations and formulas for the associated pair of elliptic `j`-invariants;
3. an objectwise Hodge or transcendental-lattice isometry;
4. in the present repository, an explicit level-six elliptic family, a rational six-isogeny, and the constant `-3` twist.

What they do **not** state is a single relative theorem over `Z[1/6]` giving all of:

- a simultaneous relative Nikulin quotient;
- a relative algebraic cycle between the resolved Apéry K3 family and a relative Kummer family;
- a proof that the cycle spreads with no arithmetic denominators beyond `2,3`;
- deck-equivariant descent across the quadratic parameter cover;
- the induced Frobenius-equivariant isomorphism of lisse `Q_l`-sheaves in every residue characteristic `p>=5`.

Therefore the safe publication status is:

> **The rank-19 algebraic projector can be constructed from the relative divisor lattice already present in the project. The remaining symmetric-square identification is conditional on one explicit relative-correspondence lemma. Endpoint assumptions must not be removed until that lemma, including deck descent, is proved.**

The missing lemma is constructive and finite: it can be verified from rational maps, blowup charts, divisor matrices, and a deck-cocycle identity. It is not a new analytic or arithmetic conjecture.

# 1. What the primary sources prove—and what they do not

## 1.1 Beukers–Peters and Peters–Stienstra

F. Beukers and C. A. M. Peters, *A family of K3 surfaces and zeta(3)*, J. reine angew. Math. **351** (1984), 42–54, construct the K3 family underlying the Apéry Picard–Fuchs equation and analyze its periods and geometry.

C. A. M. Peters and J. Stienstra, *A pencil of K3-surfaces related to Apéry's recurrence for zeta(3) and Fermi surfaces for potential zero*, in LNM **1399** (1989), 110–127, identify the relevant base changes and the Apéry–Fermi geometry.

These are the correct sources for the family and its period system. I found no theorem in these sources that states the arithmetic relative-cycle result needed here: neither a cycle over `Z[1/6]` nor a deck-equivariant sheaf isomorphism valid after every reduction `p>=5`.

A coincidence of Picard–Fuchs operators gives an isomorphism of complex local systems after choosing the correct irreducible constituent. It is not, without an algebraic correspondence, a proof of compatibility with Frobenius after reduction.

## 1.2 Clingher–Doran: canonical Shioda–Inose structures over `C`

A. Clingher and C. Doran, *Modular invariants for lattice polarized K3 surfaces*, Michigan Math. J. **55** (2007), 355–393, is a precise source for the complex geometric Shioda–Inose statement.

Relevant exact locations are:

- Theorem 1.1, pp. 356–357: an `M`-polarized complex K3 surface has a canonical involution whose quotient is birational to the Kummer surface of a product of elliptic curves, with the corresponding Hodge-theoretic identification.
- Theorem 3.4, around p. 360, restating Morrison's criterion in terms of a primitive `E_8(-1)^2` embedding.
- Theorem 3.13, around p. 363: the canonical Shioda–Inose structure for the `M`-polarized surface.
- Lemma 3.22 and the subsequent proof, around pp. 367–368: the double-Kummer configuration and the identification of the quotient using lattice theory and global Torelli.

This is an **objectwise complex-algebraic** construction. The proof uses the complex Néron–Severi lattice and Torelli theory to identify the quotient K3 as a Kummer surface. The theorem does not state that the quotient, its resolution, and its graph form a relative algebraic cycle over an arithmetic parameter scheme. “Canonical” in that theorem is a statement in the complex moduli problem; it is not automatically an integral spreading theorem.

Thus Clingher–Doran proves the correct generic complex geometry but not the uniform `p>=5` statement required in P3.2.

## 1.3 Bertin–Lecacheux: the closest explicit source

M.-J. Bertin and O. Lecacheux, *Apéry–Fermi pencil of K3-surfaces and 2-isogenies*, J. Math. Soc. Japan **72** (2020), 599–637, DOI `10.2969/jmsj/80638063`, is the strongest directly relevant primary source.

The exact useful locations are:

- the displayed fibration `#16`, p. 611;
- Proposition 4.1, pp. 614–615;
- Corollary 4.1, p. 616;
- Definition 4.1, p. 616;
- Proposition 5.1, p. 620.

Proposition 4.1 analyzes an elliptic K3 surface with a two-torsion section and identifies the quotient fibration with a Kummer surface associated to a product of elliptic curves. Its proof compares an explicit quotient equation with a Kummer elliptic fibration after adjoining a square root and derives the two symmetric formulas for the elliptic `j`-invariants. Corollary 4.1 records the resulting Kummer product. Proposition 5.1 establishes that translation by the two-torsion section in fibration `#16` gives a Shioda–Inose structure, using the Kummer description and the transcendental-lattice discriminant calculation.

This is enough for the **generic complex/function-field Shioda–Inose statement**. It is not the relative arithmetic theorem needed here, for four concrete reasons.

1. The construction is carried out for the generic complex pencil and uses square-root extensions and coordinate rescalings. The paper does not audit a model over `Z[1/6]`.
2. The proposition identifies quotient fibrations up to an isomorphism over a function-field extension; it does not construct a simultaneous blowup of the eight fixed points over the full parameter base.
3. It does not state a relative cycle in

   ```text
   CH^2(X x_U Kum(E_1 x E_2))_Q.
   ```

4. It does not prove a deck-cocycle identity or a uniform specialization theorem at every `p>=5`.

There is also a useful warning internal to the paper: some coordinate transformations between its fibrations are described as too long and omitted. Consequently, citing the paper cannot substitute for displaying the particular rational map whose graph the manuscript needs.

The sentence currently in `toric_fiber_sym2.tex` should therefore be weakened. Bertin–Lecacheux supplies the generic geometric target and a route to the map; it does not, as cited, prove the exact relative spreading assertion.

## 1.4 Kuwata–Shioda: explicit Kummer fibrations, not the Apéry quotient map

M. Kuwata and T. Shioda, *Elliptic parameters and defining equations for elliptic fibrations on a Kummer surface*, Adv. Stud. Pure Math. **50** (2008), 177–215, gives explicit equations for elliptic fibrations on `Kum(E_1 x E_2)` over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two.

For the fibration used by Bertin–Lecacheux, the relevant location is §2.4, pp. 187–188. In Legendre coordinates the Kummer surface is represented by

and fibration `J_6` has the displayed Weierstrass form

```text

```

This is excellent for a symbolic target check. It does not provide the rational map from the Apéry K3 model to that Kummer model, nor an integral relative spreading of that map.

# 2. The rank-19 projector is already constructible

This part does **not** require the missing Shioda–Inose map.

Let

```text
f : X -> U
```

be the smooth proper resolved K3 family over the chosen parameter open, and suppose the nineteen curves selected in `toric_fiber_k3.tex` are genuinely global relative Cartier divisors

```text
D_1,...,D_19 subset X,
```

not merely fiberwise curves. Let

```text
G_ij = D_i . D_j
```

be their constant relative intersection matrix. The repository computes

```text
det(G)=48.
```

Define the relative codimension-two correspondence

```text
Pi_alg = sum_{i,j=1}^{19} (G^{-1})_{ij}
         [D_i x_U D_j]
         in CH^2(X x_U X)_Q.
```

On `R^2 f_* Q_l`, its action is

```text
v |-> sum_{i,j} (G^{-1})_{ij} (v.D_i) D_j.
```

Hence:

- `Pi_alg(D_k)=D_k` for every `k`;
- `Pi_alg(v)=0` when `v` is orthogonal to every `D_i`;
- `Pi_alg^2=Pi_alg` on `R^2 f_*Q_l`.

Thus

```text
V_prim := ker(Pi_alg : R^2 f_*Q_l -> R^2 f_*Q_l)
```

is a rank-three lisse direct summand wherever `f` is smooth and the divisor matrix has rank nineteen.

Because `Pi_alg` is an algebraic cycle defined over the base, its specialization commutes with geometric and arithmetic Frobenius. This proves the rank-19 projector and the Frobenius-stable primitive quotient uniformly after every good reduction.

### Required check

The argument requires the `D_i` to be global relative divisors with constant intersection matrix. A list of curves on every geometric fiber, even if given by similar equations, is not by itself enough. The relative-resolution proof should record:

1. each strict transform and exceptional component as a Cartier divisor on the total space;
2. flatness over `U`;
3. the constant incidence/intersection matrix;
4. the chosen nineteen classes as globally labelled sections of the relative Picard lattice, or an invariant projector if monodromy permutes them.

Under the premise in Q7990 that the relative resolution of the fixed ADE sections has already been proved, these checks should be short.

### Rational versus integral coefficients

For the requested `Q_l`-sheaf, the determinant `48` causes no problem, including at `l=2,3`, because `G^{-1}` exists over `Q_l`.

An integral `Z_l` projector is a stronger assertion. It is immediate only when `l` does not divide `48`; at `l=2,3` one must compute the saturation of the divisor lattice. The present endpoint theorem needs the rational projector, not this stronger integral refinement.

# 3. Sharp arithmetic base and parameter cover

Put

```text
D(a)=a^2-34a+1.
```

A natural initial smooth parameter base is

```text
U = Spec Z[1/6, a, a^{-1}, D(a)^{-1}].
```

The point at infinity is omitted by using the affine `a`-line. The parameter `a=1` is not a Picard–Fuchs singularity. It may remain in `U` if the proved relative resolution handles the Picard-rank jump there. If the current simultaneous-resolution construction is only written away from `a=1`, first remove it and then prove extension separately; this is a geometric issue, not an additional bad residue characteristic.

The quadratic level-six cover is

```text
8x^2+(a-1)x+a=0,
```

or equivalently

```text
a = x(1-8x)/(1+x).
```

The exact discriminant identity is

```text
D(a)(1+x)^2 = (8x^2+16x-1)^2.
```

Thus an explicit cover on which all relevant fibers are smooth is

```text
C = Spec Z[1/6,
             x,
             1/(x(1+x)(1-8x)(8x^2+16x-1))].
```

The deck involution is

```text
sigma(x) = (1-8x)/(8(1+x)).
```

The level-six elliptic family is

```text
E_x : Y^2+(1-2x)XY+x^2Y=X^3,
```

with

```text
Delta(E_x)=x^6(1+x)^2(1-8x).
```

The arithmetic exclusion `6` is not cosmetic:

- the degree-two Nikulin/Kummer quotient requires characteristic different from two;
- the discriminant of `D` is

  ```text
  34^2-4 = 1152 = 2^7*3^2;
  ```

- the level-six formulas and the identified Kodaira fibers collide or cease to have the required units in characteristic three.

Therefore `p=2,3` are genuinely exceptional. No primary-source or equation audit found a reason to exclude another base prime. However, the assertion that `Z[1/6]` is the **sharp spreading base of the correspondence** still depends on checking every denominator and every base locus in the actual rational map. A generic cycle over `Q(x)` spreads over `Z[1/N]` for some `N`; it does not prove `N=6` until this audit is performed.

# 4. Use the six-isogeny quotient to encode the twist algebraically

The repository contains a particularly efficient constructive route which avoids introducing `sqrt(-3)` into the relative product.

The point

```text
Q=(x,-x) in E_x
```

has exact order six. Let

```text
phi_x : E_x -> C_x=E_x/<Q>
```

be the normalized Vélu six-isogeny. The repository gives a Weierstrass equation for `C_x` and verifies

```text
C_x is isomorphic to E_{sigma(x)}^{(-3)}.
```

The isomorphism is initially displayed over `Q(x,sqrt(-3))`, and the conjugation identity determines the quadratic-twist cocycle. Thus the product

```text
A = E_x x_C C_x
```

and its Kummer surface are already defined over `C`, while the relation to the deck transform records the `-3` arithmetic twist.

This route has three advantages.

1. The six-isogeny and its graph are defined over the arithmetic base after inverting six.
2. On `Q_l`-cohomology, `phi_x` identifies `R^1(C_x)` with `R^1(E_x)`.
3. The cross term becomes

   ```text
   R^1(E_x) tensor R^1(C_x)
      ~= R^1(E_x) tensor R^1(E_x)
      = Sym^2 R^1(E_x) direct_sum wedge^2 R^1(E_x).
   ```

The exterior-square line is the Tate line represented algebraically by the graph of the isogeny. Its orthogonal complement is the desired rank-three symmetric square.

This is the correct motivic explanation of the pointwise formulas in `toric_fiber_sym2.tex`. What is missing is the relative K3-to-Kummer correspondence which transports the original primitive projector to this complement.

# 5. Minimal relative correspondence lemma

The following is the exact lemma which would close the endpoint assumptions.

## Relative Apéry–Shioda–Inose lemma

Let `U`, `C`, `f:X->U`, and `g:E->C` be as above. Let `C_x=E_x/<Q>` and

```text
K = Kum(E x_C C_x) -> C
```

be the relative Kummer surface obtained by blowing up the finite étale two-torsion locus and quotienting by `[-1]x[-1]`.

There exist:

1. a relative symplectic involution

   ```text
   iota : X_C -> X_C
   ```

   induced by translation by the two-torsion section of the explicit fibration `#16`;

2. a finite étale degree-eight fixed locus `Z subset X_C`;

3. a blowup

   ```text
   b : X_tilde=Bl_Z(X_C) -> X_C
   ```

   and a finite flat degree-two quotient

   ```text
   q : X_tilde -> Y=X_tilde/<iota>;
   ```

4. an isomorphism of smooth K3 families over `C`

   ```text
   psi : Y -> K;
   ```

5. a relative algebraic cycle

   ```text
   Gamma = [graph(psi o q)] o [graph(b)]^t
           in CH^2(X_C x_C K)_Q;
   ```

such that the induced map on relative cohomology satisfies

```text
Gamma_* Pi_prim Gamma^* = Pi_K,tr
```

and gives an isomorphism

```text
pr^* V_prim  ~=  Sym^2 R^1 g_* Q_l
```

for every `l` invertible on the base.

Here `Pi_K,tr` is the complement of the algebraic Kummer lattice consisting of the two factor classes, the sixteen exceptional classes, and the graph-of-six-isogeny class.

Finally, there is a deck isomorphism

```text
delta : sigma^*K -> K
```

which swaps the two elliptic factors and applies the simultaneous `-3` twist, and the cycle obeys

```text
(sigma_X x delta)^* Gamma = Gamma.
```

The corresponding cocycle squares to the identity on the Kummer quotient. Hence `Gamma` descends to `U`; on the base its rank-four cross-term descent is the conventionally normalized

```text
chi_{-3} tensor Asai^+(R^1 g_*Q_l),
```

and removing the graph/Tate line gives the rank-three primitive sheaf.

### Why this lemma is sufficient

All maps and cycles in the statement are defined over the arithmetic base. Therefore their actions commute with Frobenius after every specialization `p>=5`. Smooth proper base change then yields the desired Frobenius-equivariant lisse-sheaf isomorphism, not merely equality of traces.

The quotient identities

```text
q_* q^* = 2,
q^* q_* = 1+iota^*
```

show that `q_*` is an isomorphism on the invariant transcendental part after tensoring with `Q_l`. The factor `1/2` needed for an inverse is harmless over `Q_l`.

# 6. What must be checked constructively

The missing lemma can be proved by a finite symbolic computation. The following route avoids all appeals to period equality.

## 6.1 Put the Apéry K3 in fibration `#16`

Starting from the committed `(2,2,2)` equation, exhibit explicit rational functions

```text
(t_fib, X_fib, Y_fib)
```

which put the generic fiber into the fibration `#16` of Bertin–Lecacheux, p. 611. Record the inverse map and all denominators.

The check is a polynomial identity after clearing denominators. Saturate by the product of the stated boundary factors so that components supported entirely in the indeterminacy locus are not mistaken for the graph.

## 6.2 Quotient by the two-torsion translation

For a Weierstrass curve

```text
y^2=x^3+A x^2+B x
```

with two-torsion point `(0,0)`, the quotient by that point is

```text
y^2=x^3-2A x^2+(A^2-4B)x,
```

and the standard two-isogeny is

```text
(x,y) |-> (x+A+B/x, y(1-B/x^2)).
```

Apply this formula to fibration `#16`. Verify directly that translation by `(0,0)` is symplectic on the K3 two-form and enumerate its eight fixed sections. The fixed scheme must be finite étale over `C`; this is stronger than counting eight points on a generic complex fiber.

## 6.3 Resolve the quotient relatively

Blow up the eight fixed sections. In local affine charts verify:

- the lifted involution acts without fixed divisors;
- the invariant ring gives an `A_1` quotient before resolution;
- the quotient after the blowup is smooth;
- the relative canonical two-form remains nowhere vanishing.

This produces the family `Y` and the degree-two correspondence without any Torelli argument.

## 6.4 Identify `Y` with the Kummer surface

Use

```text
A=E_x x C_x,
```

where `C_x` is the explicit Vélu quotient already present in the repository. A direct affine model of its Kummer quotient is obtained from

```text
E_x : y_1^2=f_x(u),
C_x : y_2^2=c_x(v)
```

as

```text
w^2=f_x(u)c_x(v).
```

After resolving the sixteen two-torsion nodes, transform this model to the `J_6` equation of Kuwata–Shioda, §2.4, pp. 187–188. Then compare the quotient fibration from §6.2 with that equation.

The primary source proves that such an isomorphism exists generically. The manuscript must display enough of the coordinate transformation to verify:

```text
psi^*(equation of K)=unit*(equation of Y),
```

and similarly for the inverse.

Matching only the two symmetric `j`-formulas is insufficient: the same `j`-invariants allow quadratic twists, and they do not determine an isomorphism of Kummer families over the base.

## 6.5 Verify the cycle action

Compute pullback and pushforward on the following divisor basis:

- the nineteen divisors used in `toric_fiber_k3.tex`;
- the two factor classes on the Kummer surface;
- the sixteen exceptional curves;
- the graph of `phi_x`.

The Kummer algebraic lattice has generic rank nineteen. Its orthogonal complement is rank three. It is enough to verify that `Gamma_*` is nonzero on the generic holomorphic two-form and that its kernel on `R^2` is exactly the rank-nineteen algebraic part; equal rank then gives the required isomorphism.

A stronger fully explicit check is to compute the matrix of `Gamma_*` on the two divisor lattices and verify the expected factor-of-two scaling of the transcendental intersection form.

## 6.6 Verify deck descent and the twist

The repository proves

```text
C_x ~= E_{sigma(x)}^{(-3)}.
```

Applying `sigma` again gives

```text
C_{sigma(x)} ~= E_x^{(-3)}.
```

Therefore the deck transform of the pair `(E_x,C_x)` is obtained by swapping the factors and twisting both by `-3`. Simultaneously twisting both elliptic factors leaves their Kummer quotient canonically unchanged: in the invariant Kummer coordinates the two twist scalars cancel.

One must nevertheless check the cocycle. Compose the displayed twist/isogeny maps twice and prove that the result is either the identity or `[-1]x[-1]`; the latter also acts trivially on the Kummer quotient. Then verify the same identity for the K3-to-Kummer graph. This is the step which converts a cover-level symmetric square into the asserted base-level Asai/twist descent.

Pointwise split/inert trace formulas are evidence for this descent, not a substitute for the cocycle identity.
