# 1. The determinant formula and the exact reflection law

Put

```text
Q_h(r) = product_(j=1)^h (r+j),
f_h(r) = N_h(r)/Q_h(r)^3.
```

All identities in this section are over `Q(r)` and then reduce modulo every prime on a regular nonwrapping window.

## 1.1 [PROVED] Proof of (D1)

Fix `r` and set

```text
z_m = det(v_r,v_(r+m))
    = b_r*c_(r+m)-b_(r+m)*c_r.
```

As a function of the second index, `z_m` satisfies the same recurrence:

```text
(r+m+1)^3 z_(m+1)
 = P(r+m) z_m - (r+m)^3 z_(m-1).
```

The initial values are

```text
z_0 = 0,
z_1 = b_r*c_(r+1)-b_(r+1)*c_r
    = -W_(r+1)
    = 1/(r+1)^3.
```

Define

```text
E_m(r) = Q_m(r)^3 z_m.
```

Then

```text
E_1 = 1 = N_1,
E_2 = P(r+1) = N_2,
```

and clearing the cubes in the recurrence gives

```text
E_(m+1)
 = P(r+m) E_m - (r+m)^6 E_(m-1).
```

This is exactly the defining recurrence of `N_m`. Hence `E_m=N_m`, proving

```text
Delta_(r,h) = f_h(r) = N_h(r)/Q_h(r)^3.
```

So (D1) is bankable.

## 1.2 [PROVED] Numerator-denominator coprimality

A useful exact boundary value is

```text
N_h(-j)
 = (-1)^(j-1)
   * ((j-1)!*(h-j)!)^3
   * b_(j-1)*b_(h-j),
1<=j<=h.
```

Proof: use `N_h(r)=U_(h-1)(r+1)` and put `r=-j`. In the continuant for
`U_(h-1)(1-j)`, the edge with weight zero splits the path into lengths
`j-1` and `h-j`. Then use

```text
U_m(-m)=(-1)^m U_m(0),
U_m(0)=(m!)^3 b_m.
```

All Apéry numbers on the right are positive integers. Therefore

```text
gcd_Q[r](N_h,Q_h)=1.
```

It follows that `f_h` has exactly the finite poles

```text
r=-1,-2,...,-h,
```

each of order three. Since `deg N_h=3h-3` and `deg Q_h^3=3h`, infinity is a zero of order three.

## 1.3 [PROVED] Reflection changes the sign

The proved continuant reflection gives

```text
N_h(-h-1-r) = (-1)^(h-1) N_h(r).
```

Also

```text
Q_h(-h-1-r) = (-1)^h Q_h(r).
```

Cubing the second identity and dividing yields

```text
boxed: f_h(-h-1-r) = -f_h(r).
```

This is the local determinant version of projective orbit reflection. The determinant changes sign because the reflection reverses the two endpoint vectors.

For equal gaps, substitute `r'=-h-1-r` into the cleared equation:

```text
F_(h,h)(r,-h-1-r)
 = N_h(r) Q_h(-h-1-r)^3
   -N_h(-h-1-r) Q_h(r)^3
 = 2*(-1)^h N_h(r)Q_h(r)^3.
```

Therefore the anti-diagonal is not a component. It meets the curve only at zeros and poles of `f_h`, plus projective boundary points.

# 2. Equal gaps: the diagonal and the residual curve

Since

```text
F_(h,h)(r,r') = -F_(h,h)(r',r),
```

the diagonal `r=r'` is always a factor. The issue is whether the residual factor splits.

Let `G_h` be the geometric monodromy group of the degree `3h` rational map

```text
f_h : P1 -> P1.
```

The components of `f_h(r)=f_h(r')` correspond to orbits of a point stabilizer in `G_h`. Thus

```text
F_(h,h)/(r-r') is absolutely irreducible
```

if and only if `G_h` is 2-transitive.

## 2.1 [PROVED] The exceptional case h=1

Here

```text
f_1(r)=1/(r+1)^3.
```

With `x=r+1`, `y=r'+1`,

```text
F_(1,1)=y^3-x^3
       =(y-x)(y-zeta*x)(y-zeta^2*x)
```

over `Qbar`, where `zeta` is a primitive cube root of unity. Hence

```text
c(1,1)=3.
```

Over `F_p`, the number of `F_p`-defined graph components is

```text
gcd(3,p-1),
```

so it is one for `p=2 mod 3` and three for `p=1 mod 3`.

## 2.2 A decomposition passport

The following lemma is the main small-h tool.

[PROVED] Suppose `N_h` is squarefree and

```text
f_h = A o g,
deg A=a>1,
deg g=b>1,
ab=3h.
```

Then:

```text
b>=3.
```

Every zero of `A` is simple. One such zero has infinity in its `g`-fiber with ramification index three.

Let `u` be the number of simple poles of `A`, and `v` the number of triple poles. Then

```text
a=u+3v.
```

If `u>0`, then `3` divides `b`. A simple pole of `A` has `b/3` preimages under `g`, each with ramification index three; a triple pole has `b` unramified preimages.

Finally, Riemann-Hurwitz for `g` gives

```text
2 + 2bu/3 <= 2b-2,
so u <= 3-6/b.
```

Proof: the zero divisor of `f_h` has one point of multiplicity three, namely infinity, and all other points have multiplicity one. The pole divisor has exactly `h` points, all of multiplicity three. Multiplicities in a composite are products, giving the zero and pole statements. The ramification index three at infinity contributes two to the ramification divisor of `g`; every simple pole of `A` contributes `2b/3`. This proves the inequality.

The stored exact factor certificates imply squarefreeness for `2<=h<=6`: `N_2` has three distinct roots; `N_3,N_5` are irreducible; and `N_4,N_6` are the center line times a distinct squarefree primitive factor.

## 2.3 [PROVED] Indecomposability for h=2

Here `deg f_2=6`. The only possible inner degree after `b>=3` is `b=3`, with `a=2`. Then `a=u+3v` forces `u=2`, but the passport gives `u<=1`. Contradiction.

## 2.4 [PROVED] Indecomposability for h=3

Here `deg f_3=9`, so `a=b=3`. The passport forces `u=0,v=1`. After changing the intermediate coordinate, the outer map is a cubic polynomial and the inner map is

```text
g(z)=constant/Q_3(z),
Q_3(z)=z(z^2-1),
z=r+2.
```

Therefore `N_3` would lie in the span of `Q_3^2,Q_3,1`. Reflection makes `N_3` even and `Q_3` odd, so the `Q_3` term vanishes.

Directly from the recurrence,

```text
N_3
 =1155 z^6-765 z^4+219 z^2-25.
```

If `N_3=alpha Q_3^2+gamma`, the leading term gives `alpha=1155`, which would force the `z^4` coefficient to be `-2310`, not `-765`. Contradiction.

## 2.5 [PROVED] Indecomposability for h=4

The possible inner degrees are `b=3,4,6`.

For `b=3`, one outer pole is simple and one is triple. The simple pole has one totally ramified preimage `q`; the other three Apéry poles form one fiber of the degree-three inner polynomial. Every fiber of a cubic polynomial has the same sum of roots, namely `3q`. In centered coordinates the four poles are

```text
-3/2,-1/2,1/2,3/2,
```

whose total sum is zero. The other three poles have sum `-q`, so `-q=3q`, hence `q=0`, which is not a pole. Contradiction.

For `b=6`, the outer quadratic has two simple poles. Each corresponding inner fiber consists of two points of multiplicity three. Hence

```text
(g-alpha)/(g-beta)
```

has divisor divisible by three and is a cube on `P1`. Thus `g` is a Mobius transform of `u^3` with `deg u=2`. This gives a decomposition of `f_4` with inner degree two, forbidden by the zero passport. Contradiction.

For `b=4`, the outer map is cubic polynomial. Put

```text
z=r+5/2,
Q_4=(z^2-1/4)(z^2-9/4).
```

The inner numerator is linear. Since `N_4` is odd and `Q_4` is even, parity and the top degree force the inner numerator to be proportional to `z`, and force the quadratic term of the outer cubic to vanish. Thus

```text
N_4/z = alpha Q_4^2 + gamma z^2.
```

The first two exact coefficients from the recurrence are

```text
N_4/z = 39236 z^8 -112560 z^6 + lower terms.
```

The leading coefficient forces `alpha=39236`; then `alpha Q_4^2` has `z^6` coefficient

```text
-5*39236=-196180,
```

not `-112560`. Contradiction.

## 2.6 [PROVED] Indecomposability for h=5

The possible inner degrees are `b=3,5`.

For `b=3`, `a=5`, while the pole passport requires `u=2` or `u=5`; both contradict `u<=1`.

For `b=5`, the outer map is cubic polynomial. Put `z=r+3`. The pole polynomial

```text
Q_5=z(z^2-1)(z^2-4)
```

is odd and `N_5` is even. The inner numerator must therefore be even,

```text
A(z)=a z^2+c,
```

and the quadratic term of the outer cubic vanishes. At a pole of `Q_5`,

```text
N_5=constant*A^3.
```

At `z=0,1,2`, the exact boundary formula of Section 1 gives

```text
n_0=64*73^2,
n_1=-216*5*1445,
n_2=13824*33001.
```

Since `n_0!=0`, put `x=A(1)/A(0)`. Then

```text

x^3=n_1/n_0,
```

Reduce this pair of polynomial equations modulo seven. There

so

```text
(x+1)^3=2.
```

Subtracting gives `x^2+x+1=0`, hence `x^3=1`, contradicting `x^3=4`. This contradiction holds over `Fbar_7`, so the characteristic-zero resultant is nonzero.

## 2.7 [PROVED] Indecomposability for h=6

The possible inner degrees are `b=3,6,9`.

For `b=3`, the outer degree-six map has two triple poles. After sending the value of `g(infinity)` to infinity, `g` is a cubic polynomial. The six centered poles

```text
+/-1/2, +/-3/2, +/-5/2
```

must split into two three-point fibers. Two fibers of a cubic polynomial have equal root sums; since the total sum is zero, each triple must have sum zero. After multiplying by two, each triple consists of three odd integers, whose sum cannot be zero. Contradiction.

For `b=9`, the outer quadratic has two simple poles. The ratio of their fibers is a cube, so `g` is a Mobius transform of `u^3` with `deg u=3`. This reduces to the already impossible inner-degree-three decomposition.

For `b=6`, the outer map is cubic polynomial. Put

```text
z=r+7/2,
Q_6=(z^2-1/4)(z^2-9/4)(z^2-25/4).
```

Write the inner numerator as `A=O+d`, with `O` odd and `d` constant. The top-degree parity first eliminates the quadratic even term. The even part of the composite numerator is

```text
c_1*d*Q_6^2
 +c_2*(O^2+d^2)*Q_6
 +c_3*(d^3+3d O^2).
```

If `d!=0`, evaluation at all six roots of `Q_6` gives

```text
O(z)^2+d^2/3 = constant*Q_6(z).
```

With `x=2z`, the normalized right side is

```text
x^6-35x^4+259x^2-225.
```

The square of an odd cubic, plus a constant, has normalized coefficients

```text
x^6+2a x^4+a^2 x^2+constant.
```

The equation `2a=-35` would force `a^2=1225/4`, not `259`. Hence `d=0`. Parity then also forces `c_2=0`, and

```text
A(x/2)=constant*x*(s x^2+1).
```

The boundary values at `x=1,3,5`, reduced modulo seven, are

```text
n_1=5, n_3=1, n_5=5.
```

If the linear coefficient of `A` vanished, the first ratio already fails. Otherwise set

```text
X=(2s+1)/(s+1).
```

The two cubic ratio equations become

```text
X^3=4,
(3X-2)^3=6.
```

They imply

```text
2X^2+X+3=0,
X^3=4X+6.
```

Combining with `X^3=4` gives `X=3`, which does not satisfy the quadratic. Again the contradiction holds over `Fbar_7`.

This completes the small-h indecomposability proof.

## 2.8 [PROVED] The equal-gap factorization for h<=6

For `2<=h<=6`, the degree is `d=3h>=6`. Indecomposability makes the geometric monodromy primitive. Inertia at the value zero contains one 3-cycle: infinity is a zero of order three and all finite zeros are simple. Jordan's theorem therefore gives

```text
A_d <= G_h <= S_d.
```

In particular `G_h` is 2-transitive. Hence

```text
F_(h,h)(r,r')
 =(r-r') R_h(r,r'),
```

where `R_h` is absolutely irreducible over `Qbar` for every `2<=h<=6`.

Conceptually:

```text
h=1 : three graph components;
h=2 : diagonal plus one residual component;
h=3 : diagonal plus one residual component;
h=4 : diagonal plus one residual component;
h=5 : diagonal plus one residual component;
h=6 : diagonal plus one residual component.
```

For odd `h=3,5`, the middle pole is a triple pole. Locally the equation has three branches with tangent ratios `1,zeta,zeta^2`; the diagonal is one branch and the irreducible residual has the other two local branches. This local splitting is not a global component splitting.

# 3. Unequal gaps and the parity-factor audit

## 3.1 [PROVED] Exact geometric criterion

For `h!=h'`, the following are equivalent:

```text
F_(h,h') is absolutely irreducible over Qbar;
Qbar(r) and Qbar(r') are linearly disjoint over Qbar(t),
where t=f_h(r)=f_h'(r');
the joint geometric monodromy is transitive on
Omega_h x Omega_h'.
```

This is the correct general qIRRED criterion. Adjacent coprimality of the numerators is not this criterion: it controls common zeros in one coordinate, not common intermediate covers of two rational maps.

## 3.2 [PROVED] All unequal pairs with h,h'<=6

For `2<=h<=6`, the preceding section gives

```text
A_(3h) <= G_h <= S_(3h).
```

Take distinct `h,h'` in this range. Goursat's lemma says that a proper subdirect product can arise only through a common quotient of `G_h` and `G_h'`. Since the degrees are different, the only possible common nontrivial quotient is `C_2`, when both groups are symmetric. The corresponding sign fiber product is still transitive on

```text
Omega_h x Omega_h'.
```

Indeed, the parity of a permutation sending one prescribed point to another can be changed inside the point stabilizer.

For `h=1`, the geometric group is `C_3`. It has no nontrivial common quotient with `A_d` or `S_d` for `d>=6`. Thus the joint group is the full product.

Therefore

```text
boxed: F_(h,h') is absolutely irreducible for
       1<=h,h'<=6 and h!=h'.
```

## 3.3 [PROVED] Even-even center factors do not split F

Let `h,h'` be even and write

```text
N_h=L_h M_h,       L_h=2r+h+1,
N_h'=L_h' M_h',    L_h'=2r'+h'+1.
```

Then

```text
F_(h,h')
 = L_h M_h D_h' - L_h' M_h' D_h,
D_h=Q_h^3.
```

Neither `L_h` nor `L_h'` divides this polynomial: setting `L_h=0` leaves the second term generically nonzero, and conversely. At the joint center

```text
r=-(h+1)/2,
r'=-(h'+1)/2,
```

the denominators are nonzero and the center roots are simple. The linear part is

```text
constant_1*(r+(h+1)/2)
 -constant_2*(r'+(h'+1)/2),
```

so the point is smooth with one local branch. There is no product of two center components.

The other parity combinations are equally harmless:

- odd-odd centers are triple-pole singularities, with local cubic branches but no forced global component;
- even-odd centers compare a zero with a pole and are not points of the equality curve in the finite affine chart.

Thus the parity factorization affects isolated points and local singularities, not the component main coefficient.

## 3.4 [OPEN] The exact all-h gap

The proof above extends verbatim to all distinct `h,h'` once the following statement is known:

```text
[INDECOMP-SQFREE]
For every h>=2, N_h is squarefree and the rational map
f_h=N_h/Q_h^3 is compositionally indecomposable.
```

Then the same zero-fiber 3-cycle and Jordan theorem give

```text
A_(3h) <= G_h <= S_(3h),
```

and Goursat gives all unequal-pair irreducibility.

Neither

```text
gcd(N_h,N_(h+1))=1
```

nor

```text
N_(2k)=(2r+2k+1)M_(2k)
```

proves compositional indecomposability. A common right compositional factor can exist without producing a common zero or a repeated polynomial factor. Therefore the general qIRRED assertion must remain labelled [GAP-INDECOMP-SQFREE].

# 4. Component counts and the correct point-count scale

Let `c_geo(h,h')` denote the number of absolutely irreducible components over `Qbar`.

For `H<=6`, and conditionally for all `H` under [INDECOMP-SQFREE],

```text
c_geo(h,h') = 1,  h!=h',
c_geo(1,1)  = 3,
c_geo(h,h)  = 2,  h>=2.
```

Therefore, over ordered gap pairs,

```text
sum_(h,h'<=H) c_geo(h,h')
 = H(H-1)+3+2(H-1)
 = H^2+H+1.
```

The geometric average is exactly

```text
1+1/H+1/H^2.
```

Over `F_p`, only the `(1,1)` term changes its `p`-sized coefficient:

```text
c_arith(1,1;p)=gcd(3,p-1).
```

Thus the arithmetic sum of `F_p`-defined component coefficients is

```text
H^2+H-2+gcd(3,p-1).
```

## 4.1 [CORRECTION] The available Weil error is quadratic in the gaps

The projective closure of `X_(h,h')` in `P1 x P1` has bidegree

```text
(d,e)=(3h,3h').
```

A component of bidegree `(d_i,e_i)` has geometric genus at most

```text
(d_i-1)(e_i-1).
```

Summing over components gives the safe bound

```text
sum genus_i <= de = 9hh'.
```

Normalize every component and apply Hasse-Weil. Allowing a coarse explicit correction for singular points and points at infinity gives, for every sufficiently large good prime,

```text
#X_(h,h')(F_p)
 <= c_geo(h,h')*(p+1)
    +18hh'*sqrt(p)
    +9(h+h')^2.
```

The same bound applies to the restricted windows because restriction only removes points.

This is the bankable degree-theoretic bound. A bound of the form

```text
c p + C(h+h') sqrt(p)
```

would require a new genus-collapse theorem. It is not a consequence of transfer matrices, adjacent coprimality, or the parity factorization. In a generic fiber product the genus scale is `(3h-1)(3h'-1)`, so quadratic dependence is the natural one.

Consequently, summing the safe individual Weil errors over `h,h'<=H` gives

```text
O(H^4 sqrt(p)),
```

not `O(H^3 sqrt(p))`. This strengthens, rather than weakens, the conclusion that per-pair Weil is not the desired argument.

## 4.2 [CORRECTION] Full curves do not give the restricted main term

Write

```text
n_h=p-2-h,
#S_H=sum_(h<=H) n_h
    =H(p-2)-H(H+1)/2.
```

The target baseline is

```text
(#S_H)^2/p
 = sum_(h,h'<=H) n_h n_h'/p.
```

A full-curve main term `p` for each off-diagonal pair gives `pH^2`, not this restricted baseline. Their difference is of order `H^3`. At `H=p^(2/3)` this is of order `p^2`, already larger than `#S_H`.

One also cannot repair this by deleting the excluded coordinate strips trivially. The complement of the `h`-window has `O(h)` x-coordinates, and a vertical fiber has degree `3h'`. The resulting deterministic deletion bound is `O(hh')` per pair and `O(H^4)` after summation.

Thus qIRRED plus ordinary complete-curve Weil estimates is not [COINC]. A restricted-box or family-averaged theorem is still required.

# 5. Matching the components to the [COINC] budget

The component accounting is favorable.

For `h!=h'`, the one component supplies the uniform baseline

```text
n_h n_h'/p.
```

For `h=h'>=2`, the diagonal component contributes exactly

```text
n_h.
```

The residual component supplies the off-diagonal random baseline

```text
n_h(n_h-1)/p,
```

up to its family error. Summing the one-component off-diagonal terms and these residual self terms gives

```text
((#S_H)^2-#S_H)/p.
```

Adding the exact diagonals gives

```text
(#S_H)^2/p + #S_H*(1-1/p).
```

This is precisely inside the allowed `K*#S_H` excess.

The special `(1,1)` cube deck transformations contribute at most `O(p)`, also inside `O(#S_H)`.

The forced mirror zeros for even `h` contribute coincidences among the value zero at only `O(H^2)` ordered gap pairs. They are isolated points on the existing curves, not components. Since `H<=p`,

```text
H^2 <= pH << #S_H
```

in the working range, so these points are also inside the linear budget.

Therefore the component prediction is

```text
N_coinc(H)
 = (#S_H)^2/p
   + #S_H*(1-1/p)
   + O(p+H^2)
   + E_family(H,p).
```

The desired [COINC] is exactly the assertion

```text
E_family(H,p) = O(#S_H)
```

uniformly in the growing-gap range, with the restricted windows included.

## 5.1 What would kill the reduction

If a positive proportion `rho>0` of unequal pairs had two `F_p`-defined components, their extra main term would be

```text
rho*p*H^2,
```

whereas the admissible excess is only of size

```text
#S_H about pH.
```

For fixed positive `rho` and growing `H`, [COINC] would fail.

In the extreme hypothetical pattern `c(h,h')=2` for typical unequal pairs, the true main term would be

```text
N_coinc(H)
 about 2*(#S_H)^2/p + O(#S_H),
```

and the second-moment reduction would die.

That is not what reflection or parity produces. The small-gap theorem proves the opposite pattern through `h,h'<=6`: every unequal pair is irreducible, and the only systematic second component is the diagonal in a self-pair. The remaining danger is solely the open possibility of hidden common compositional quotients at larger gaps.

# 6. Answers to the four requested items

1. For `h=h'`, the diagonal is a component. The reflection anti-diagonal is not. For `h=1` there are three cube-deck graph components. For every `2<=h<=6`, there are exactly two components: the diagonal and one absolutely irreducible residual curve.

2. For `h!=h'`, absolute irreducibility is equivalent to transitivity of the joint monodromy, or linear disjointness of the two function fields over `Qbar(t)`. It is proved for every distinct pair with `h,h'<=6`. In general it follows from [INDECOMP-SQFREE], which is not supplied by adjacent coprimality. Even-even center factors do not create components.

3. The exact component average is

```text
1+1/H+1/H^2
```

for `H<=6`, and conditionally for all `H` under [INDECOMP-SQFREE]. The safe point-count error is `O(hh' sqrt(p)+(h+h')^2)`, not `O((h+h')sqrt(p))`. The diagonal main terms and all forced center points fit inside `K#S_H`.

4. The component main term does not exceed the [COINC] budget in the proved small-gap range or under the natural all-h monodromy extension. The predicted asymptotic is

```text
N_coinc(H)
 = (#S_H)^2/p + #S_H + O(p+H^2+E_family),
```

with the harmless correction `-#S_H/p` if the independent off-diagonal baseline is written exactly. The reduction is not dead. What remains is the restricted, family-averaged Frobenius error, plus the all-h indecomposability theorem.

## Least-confident step

The least-confident part is not the anti-diagonal correction or the parity audit; those are exact identities. It is the extension from the fully proved small range `h<=6` to all growing gaps. The exact missing theorem is [INDECOMP-SQFREE]. Even after that theorem, one still needs a restricted-window family estimate; ordinary Hasse-Weil on the individual curves has the wrong total error and the wrong boundary resolution.
