# 1. The unconditional two-ended fractional moment

For \(r\ge1\), define
\[
\mathfrak M_r(D)
:=\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\sum_{J\in S_p}a_p(J)m_D(p,J)^r
\tag{1.1}
\]
and
\[
\mathfrak I_s
:=\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\sum_{J\in S_p}a_p(J)^{-s}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

## Proposition 1 — exact raw moment ladder

For every fixed real \(r\ge1\), uniformly for \(D>H\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak M_r(D)
\ll D^r\mathfrak I_{r-1}P^{o(1)}.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Proof

Fix a characteristic \(p\) and a source \(J\in S_p\).  Let
\[
\mathcal K_{p,J}
:=\{K\in S_p:D<K-J\le2D\}.
\]
For distinct \(K_1<K_2\) in \(S_p\), \(K_2\) is itself an actual \(p\)-zero after \(K_1\).  Hence the literal first return from \(K_1\) occurs no later than \(K_2\):
\[
a_p(K_1)\le K_2-K_1.
\]
Therefore the target intervals
\[
[K,K+a_p(K))\qquad(K\in\mathcal K_{p,J})
\]
have pairwise disjoint interiors.  They lie in
\[
[J+D,J+2D+H),
\]
whose length is \(D+H\ll D\).  Consequently
\[
\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{p,J}}a_p(K)\ll D.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Hölder in the target set gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_D(p,J)^r
&=\left(
\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{p,J}}
 a_p(K)^{(r-1)/r}a_p(K)^{-(r-1)/r}
\right)^r\\
&\le
\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{p,J}}a_p(K)\right)^{r-1}
\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{p,J}}a_p(K)^{1-r}\\
&\ll
D^{r-1}\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{p,J}}a_p(K)^{1-r}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.5}
\]
Multiply by the **source** first-return length \(a_p(J)\), sum in \(J\), and reindex by the target \(K\):
\[
\sum_{J\in S_p}a_p(J)m_D(p,J)^r
\ll
D^{r-1}
\sum_{K\in S_p}a_p(K)^{1-r}
\sum_{\substack{J\in S_p\\D<K-J\le2D}}a_p(J).
\tag{1.6}
\]
For fixed \(K\), the source intervals \([J,J+a_p(J))\) in the inner sum again have disjoint interiors and lie in
\[
[K-2D,K-D+H),
\]
so
\[
\sum_{\substack{J\in S_p\\D<K-J\le2D}}a_p(J)\ll D.
\tag{1.7}
\]
Substitution in (1.6) gives
\[
\sum_{J\in S_p}a_p(J)m_D(p,J)^r
\ll
D^r\sum_{K\in S_p}a_p(K)^{1-r}.
\]
Now multiply by exactly one source weight \(\log p\) and sum the shell primes.  This is (1.3).  \(\square\)

The endpoint cases are worth recording:
\[
r=1:\qquad
\sum_{p,J}(\log p)a_p(J)m_D(p,J)
\ll DM,
\tag{1.8}
\]
which is the integrated replicated-spacing lemma, while
\[
r=2:\qquad
\sum_{p,J}(\log p)a_p(J)m_D(p,J)^2
\ll D^2\mathfrak I_1.
\tag{1.9}
\]
No target multiplicity has been divided out in either identity.

A useful normalized form is obtained by putting
\[
u_D(p,J):=\frac{a_p(J)m_D(p,J)}D.
\]
Then (1.3) is exactly
\[
\sum_{p,J}(\log p)a_p(J)^{1-r}u_D(p,J)^r
\ll
\sum_{p,J}(\log p)a_p(J)^{1-r}.
\tag{1.10}
\]
Thus current first-return geometry proves only an \(O(1)\) Palm \(L^r\) norm for the normalized occupancy \(u_D\).  The saturation model in Section 6 has \(u_D\asymp1\) essentially everywhere.

# 2. Fractional inverse-label packet moments

## Proposition 2 — fractional inverse packet moment

For \(0\le s\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
Z_{J,s}
:=\sum_a\frac{R(J,a)}{a^s}
\ll C_J^{1-s/2}P^{o(1)}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

For any cutoff \(T\ge1\), the primitive packet cap (0.6) gives
\[
\sum_{a\le T}\frac{R(J,a)}{a^s}
\ll P^{o(1)}\sum_{a\le T}a^{1-s}
\ll T^{2-s}P^{o(1)},
\tag{2.2}
\]
while positivity gives
\[
\sum_{a>T}\frac{R(J,a)}{a^s}
\le \frac{C_J}{T^s}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
Take \(T=C_J^{1/2}\).  If this cutoff lies outside the admissible label interval, the missing low or high piece is empty and the same estimate follows after truncation.  Both terms are
\[
\ll C_J^{1-s/2}P^{o(1)}.

\(\square\)

This estimate is sharp on the equal-packet model used below: if there are \(L\) labels, each with logarithmic prime mass \(L\), then \(C=L^2\) and

This section is an **exponent-level interface model**, not an assertion that the Apéry zero sets actually realize the design.  It is enough for a no-go statement: every inequality used in Sections 1--4, plus the stable one-edge and aligned-resultant capacities, is simultaneously compatible with \(P^{12/5}\) edge mass.

\qquad
L=P^{2/5},
\qquad
C=P^{4/5},
\qquad
N=P^{4/5},
\qquad
m_0=\frac DL=P^{1/5}.
\tag{6.1}

The label scale \(L\) is strictly inside the selected window:
\[
P^{77/200}=o(L),
\qquad
L=o(P^{41/100}).
\tag{6.2}
\]
At each of \(N\) active physical starts, use \(L\) admissible first-label packets, each with logarithmic prime mass \(L\).  Thus
\[
C=L^2=P^{4/5}.

The basic bank is then saturated at exponent level:

|---|---:|---:|

selected starts.  Place them at first-return spacing \(\asymp L\) along the \(P\)-scale row.  Since

\tag{6.6}

assign a distinct integer offset \(d\in(D,2D]\) to every block.  Membership of \(p\) in the block at \(d\) is interpreted as the available selected target \(J+d\in S_p\).

\ll DP^{o(1)}.
\tag{6.8}

\quad(\delta>0).

\tag{6.13}
\]
Again, this is not an actual Apéry counterexample.  It identifies exactly the missing cross-characteristic information.

# 7. The smallest new moment statement, and why raw higher moments are the wrong direction

The unconditional identity (1.10) says that, under the natural Palm measure
\[
d\mu_r(p,J)
:=(\log p)a_p(J)^{1-r},
\]
the normalized replicated occupancy
\[
u_D(p,J)=\frac{a_p(J)m_D(p,J)}D
\]
has only an \(O(1)\) \(L^r\) norm.  The saturation model has \(u_D\asymp1\) almost everywhere.  Therefore a raw higher-moment theorem of the form “\(\mathfrak M_r\) is finite/smaller than the trivial pointwise \((D/\mathsf A)^r\) bound” is not enough.  The new theorem must force a **power defect** in this normalized Palm moment.

The clean source-compatible formulation is \((\mathrm{RPM}_{r,\delta})\):
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\sum_{J\in S_p}a_p(J)m_D(p,J)^r
\ll
P^{-\delta+o(1)}D^r
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\sum_{J\in S_p}a_p(J)^{1-r}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]
All membership predicates in \(S_p\) remain literal.  This statement does not insert a resultant candidate start, does not change the endpoint masks, and does not introduce a second outer prime weight.

With the present inverse-label bank, the exact target threshold is (5.9):
\[
\boxed{
\delta>rac{179r-3}{200}+4r\sigma.}
\tag{7.2}
\]
The right side is increasing in \(r\).  Hence:
\[
\boxed{
\text{the cheapest raw-moment defect is }r=1,
\text{ not }r>1.}
\tag{7.3}
\]
If one insists on a genuinely higher fractional moment \(r=1+\rho\), the threshold is
\[
\delta>
\frac{176+179\rho}{200}
+4(1+\rho)\sigma.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Its infimum as \(\rho\downarrow0\) is the first-moment requirement \(22/25+4\sigma\).

This is why I do not recommend pursuing a raw second moment of \(m_D\) as the next source theorem.  The second moment is already algebraically present in the \(P^{243/200}D^2\) cap, and the sharp uniform-spacing model makes every normalized raw moment order one.

A more plausible new statistic couples the replicated multiplicity to **foreign partner utilization**.  Define
\[
u^{\rm edge}_D(p,J)
:=
\frac{\deg_{J,D}(p)}{D\,m_D(p,J)}
\qquad(m_D(p,J)>0).
\tag{7.5}
\]
By the exact \(q\mid N_d(J)\) radical argument,
\[
0\le u^{\rm edge}_D(p,J)\ll P^{o(1)}.
\]
The hostile block model has
\[
u^{\rm edge}_D(p,J)\asymp1
\]
on essentially every used owner occurrence: all \(m_D\) available offsets are filled with a \(D\)-mass partner block.  Thus a Palm tail saying that high utilization has power-small replicated owner mass would be genuinely new cross-characteristic information, unlike a marginal raw moment of \(m_D\).

For example, a source-faithful sufficient form is: for fixed \(\eta,\kappa>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}(\log p)
\sum_{\substack{J\in S_p\\
 u^{\rm edge}_D(p,J)>P^{-\eta}}}
 a_p(J)m_D(p,J)
\ll
P^{-\kappa+o(1)}DM.}
\tag{EUP}_{\eta,\kappa}
\]
Splitting owners into low- and high-utilization pieces and rerunning the literal selector proof gives a selector saving
\[
P^{-\min(\eta,\kappa)/2+o(1)}.
\]
Therefore this particular Palm-tail formulation would need
\[
\min(\eta,\kappa)>rac{22}{25}+4\sigma
\]
to cross the limiting target by itself.  The threshold is large, but the statistic is at least aimed at the actual obstruction: simultaneous saturation of the replicated endpoint count and the exact-offset foreign radical budget.

A theorem which controls the edge mass of the high-utilization owners directly, rather than their replicated one-characteristic mass, could enter linearly and would have the more favorable \(11/25\)-scale threshold.  That is the natural cross-characteristic direction after the present no-go.

# 8. Multiplicity / mask audit

The moment proof and no-go use the literal multiplicities in the following way.

The replicated count \(m_D(p,J)\) is an **unweighted count of same-\(p\) later selected starts**.  Proposition 1 keeps all of those occurrences.  Its first use of interval disjointness is among the target first-return intervals; after target reindexing, its second use is among the source first-return intervals.  No factor \(D/H\), no one-per-\(H\)-block thinning, and no division by an endpoint multiplicity occurs.

The source first label \(a_p(J)\) is kept throughout.  The target labels enter only through the exact target inverse factor \(a_p(K)^{1-r}\) produced by Hölder.  The fractional packet estimate is applied at the same physical start and never replaces an actual selected occurrence by a polynomial root candidate.

For a graph edge at exact offset \(d\), both characteristics have the actual selected endpoints \(J,J+d\).  Thus \(q\mid N_d(J)\) is a direct restart congruence for the actual partner \(q\).  The radical height is applied separately at each exact \(d\).  Summing over the at most \(m_D(p,J)\) offsets used by one owner is precisely what produces \(D m_D(p,J)\); there is no extra factor counting all \(D\) possible offsets after that.

The graph remains simple because the consumer marks the **next consecutive common selected prefix**.  This is what permits \(\deg_{J,D}(p)\le C_J\).  If all future common prefixes were retained as parallel edges, this bound would no longer be valid; the present proof never makes that enlargement.

The logarithmic weights are exact.  \(R,Q_r,R_D\) carry one owner weight \(\log p\).  The degree carries one foreign weight \(\log q\).  The edge mass is therefore one pair \((\log p)(\log q)\).  No step squares \(\log p\), and the moment \(m_D^r\) is a multiplicity moment, not an additional prime weight.

Every selected mask can remain inside \(S_p\) and inside the literal graph edge condition.  Proposition 1 only uses positivity and actual-zero first-return geometry, so deleting occurrences by headroom, far-label, mixed-return, simple-root, residual/valuation, branch/orientation, nonalias, or boundary masks can only decrease its left side.  The exact-offset radical step is invoked only after the edge has passed all pair masks.  Hence no mask is commuted through a signed sum or reconstructed after the majorization.

# Final conclusion

The replicated multiplicity does have a clean unconditional higher/fractional moment theory, but that theory closes **against** the hoped-for saving:
\[
\mathfrak M_r(D)\ll D^r\mathfrak I_{r-1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E_D
\ll
P^{\frac{723}{400}-\frac{3}{400r}+o(1)}D,
\]
and the coefficient is minimized at \(r=1\).  The \(r=2\) endpoint separately reproduces \(P^{243/200}D^2\).  At \(D=P^{3/5}\), the \(L=P^{2/5}\) uniform-spacing/biregular-offset model makes all normalized raw moments order one and carries the full \(P^{12/5}\) mass while respecting the literal one-edge radical capacity, long pair-chain packing, strong far colors, and aligned fixed-label degree/resultant capacity.

So there is no fully unconditional improved selector cap from the requested marginal-moment toolbox.  The smallest raw-moment theorem that would cross the target is the power-defect statement \((\mathrm{RPM}_{r,\delta})\), with exact threshold
\[
\delta>\frac{179r-3}{200}+4r\sigma.
\]
Its cheapest case is \(r=1\), corresponding to the already identified selector saving \(\eta>11/25+2\sigma\).  Any genuinely higher raw moment is more expensive.  A viable next attack should therefore measure **cross-characteristic utilization/codegree across the exact \(m_D\) offsets**, not another marginal moment of \(m_D\) itself.
