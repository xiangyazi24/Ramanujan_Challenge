# P3.2 final assessment

## Verdict

The honest answer is **(d), with a more precise refinement: the missing ingredient is a new arithmetic rigidity theorem, not another resultant estimate**.

The current toolkit is strong enough to prove every local and characteristic-zero statement, but it does not contain a mechanism preventing the following phenomenon:

\[
\gcd(A_r,A_s)\neq 1\pmod p
\]

for degrees \(r,s\) that themselves move with \(p\).  All failed routes are different ways of trying to turn characteristic-zero separation into a moving-characteristic statement, and each loses exactly at this moving-degree step.

## Why the obvious algebraic routes cannot close

A resultant argument would need a statement of the form

\[
p\nmid \operatorname{Res}(A_r,A_s)
\]

for the pair \((r,s)=(r(p),s(p))\).  A fixed pair \((r,s)\) has only finitely many bad primes, but here the resultant itself changes with \(p\).  No bound currently proved controls the prime divisors of this moving family of resultants.

The same obstruction kills Hensel lifting: one can lift a fixed characteristic-zero root modulo sufficiently large primes, but the required threshold depends on the discriminant/resultant of \(A_s\), and \(s\) grows with \(p\).  There is no uniform inequality of the form

\[
p>C(s)
\]

available with \(s\sim p/2\).

## The most plausible genuinely new idea

The only route among (a)--(c) that looks capable of finishing the problem is a new structural theorem of type (b), but stronger than CFVZ.

A concrete target would be a **moving-degree Frobenius rigidity theorem**:

For the Apéry Racah family, prove that if

\[
A_r(T)\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

then every Frobenius orbit of roots is forced onto the special Hasse values

\[
T=\lambda_n=n(n+1),
\]

with the orbit label controlled by the same Apéry recurrence giving \(b_n\).

Equivalently, one wants a theorem that replaces

\[
\text{root of }A_r \pmod p
\]

by

\[
\text{Hasse/Racah parameter attached to an Apéry index }n.
\]

Then a common root of \(A_r,A_s\) would force a common Hasse index, and the already-proved adjacent descent plus recurrence would give the contradiction.

This would be a true new theorem: it is not a resultant bound and it uses the special arithmetic origin of the polynomials.

## Candidate form of a usable theorem

A sufficient statement would be:

> For every prime \(p\) and every \(m<p/2\), every irreducible factor of \(A_m(T)\in\mathbf F_p[T]\) divides a polynomial \(H_{m,p}(T)\) whose roots are exactly the Hasse values arising from the Apéry Frobenius orbit.

Then

\[
\gcd(A_r,A_s)\neq1
\]

would imply a shared Frobenius orbit, hence a shared Apéry zero, contradicting the nonzero-gap structure.

At present there is no proof of this statement.

## What about option (a)?

The strongest realistic algebraic identity already discovered is the adjacent relation

\[
6K(r,r-1)=b_r+b_{r-1}.
\]

It controls neighboring indices but has no known extension to arbitrary \((r,s)\). A hypothetical identity

\[
D(r,s)K(r,s)=F(b_r,b_s,\ldots)
\]

with \(D(r,s)\) explicitly invertible modulo \(p\) would solve the problem, but no such identity is currently visible. Discovering it would itself be the new idea.

## What about option (c)?

A completely different route avoiding \(K(r,s)\neq0\) is possible in principle, but the current reduction already isolates the hard point: bounding the number of large primes dividing the relevant Apéry denominators. Any alternative must replace kernel nonvanishing by another source of prime independence. No such route is currently available.

## Final evaluation

Current status:

- characteristic-zero coprimality: solved;
- adjacent characteristic-p coprimality: solved;
- finite experiments: strongly supportive;
- arbitrary moving pair \((r,s,p)\): open.

The missing theorem is not a technical cleanup. It requires new arithmetic structure connecting the Racah polynomial roots over \(\mathbf F_p\) to the Apéry Frobenius/Hasse geometry.

Therefore the correct answer is:

**(d). Current tools are insufficient. The next breakthrough must be a new Frobenius/Hasse rigidity theorem (most likely a stronger CFVZ-type structure theorem), not another manipulation of the existing kernel or resultant framework.**
