# P3.2 exceptional fibers: the zeroth-order Casoratian is necessarily the mark, and the first non-tautological obstruction is the secant-direction norm

## Executive verdict

I do **not** obtain an all-\(h\), all-\(p\) proof or a marked counterexample from the current state. I obtain the third requested outcome, together with an exact no-go theorem explaining why an ordinary two-solution Casoratian cannot remove the marked condition.

The decisive conclusions are:

1. With the canonical normalization,
   \[
   \boxed{M_{12}(r,h)=-r^3N_h(r).}
   \]
   Thus, after boundary saturation, a root of \(M_{12}\) is exactly a local Apéry Dirichlet return \(N_h(r)=0\). At such a root the normalized local transfer is lower triangular with both diagonal entries units.

2. Let \(\phi^A\) be the local Apéry solution with
   \(\phi^A_{r-1}=1,\phi^A_r=0\). The distinguished Apéry solution \(b\) lies on this local line exactly when \(b_r=0\). Its Casoratian with \(\phi^A\) is
   \[
   \boxed{W_r(b,\phi^A)=r^3b_r.}
   \]
   Since the solution space is two-dimensional, every zeroth-order alternating transfer invariant comparing these two lines is a localized unit multiple of this one. Hence **every ordinary \(2\times2\) line-incidence Casoratian is equivalent to the mark \(b_r\)**.

3. There is an exact distinguished Apéry--Racah Lagrange current
   \[
   \mathcal L_{p,h}(r)
   =2\sum_{j=r+1}^{r+h-1}
     (2j+1)\bigl(\lambda_r-2(2j+1)^2\bigr)b_jK(r,j).
   \]
   A marked failure forces \(\mathcal L_{p,h}(r)=0\). This is a valid non-tautological marked obstruction. However, after imposing the mark and replacing the two distinguished sequences by their locally normalized Dirichlet solutions, its core becomes the automatic local Green identity. Therefore no proof using only the two local continuants can show that the **distinguished** current is nonzero; the global Apéry prefix remains essential.

4. A strictly smaller polynomial obstruction is obtained from the **secant direction** of the Apéry Gram curve. Put
   \[
   M=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad \lambda_n=n(n+1),
   \]
   \[
   d_k(T)=\frac1{(k!)^2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\bigl(T-j(j+1)\bigr),
   \qquad
   v(T)=(d_0(T),\ldots,d_M(T)).
   \]
   For \(X=\lambda_r\), \(Y=\lambda_{r+h}\), and
   \(\Delta=Y-X=h(2r+h+1)\), define
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal J_p(X,Y)
     :=\left\langle\frac{v(Y)-v(X)}{Y-X},
                         \frac{v(Y)-v(X)}{Y-X}\right\rangle
     =\sum_{k=1}^{M}
       \left(\frac{d_k(Y)-d_k(X)}{Y-X}\right)^2 .
   }
   \tag{E1}
   \]
   Every displayed quotient is a polynomial. Thus \(\mathcal J_p\) is defined on rational and non-rational exceptional components without choosing roots.

   At grid points,
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal J_{p,h}(r)
   =\sum_{k=1}^{M}
      \left(
       \frac{D(r+h,k)-D(r,k)}{h(2r+h+1)}
      \right)^2,
   \qquad
   D(n,k)=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}.
   }
   \tag{E2}
   \]

   A marked failure has
   \[
   b_r=b_{r+h}=K(r,r+h)=0,
   \]
   and hence
   \[
   \boxed{
   \text{marked failure}\Longrightarrow
   \mathcal J_{p,h}(r)=0.
   }
   \tag{E3}
   \]
   Therefore proving that \(\mathcal J_{p,h}\) is a unit on the boundary/mate/pole-saturated physical common-root algebra proves the required marked exclusion.

5. This invariant is strictly smaller and is not \(b_r\) under another name. The Apéry form
   \(B_p(T)=\langle v(T),v(T)\rangle\) has degree \(2M\), whereas
   \(\mathcal J_p(X,Y)\) has total degree \(2M-2\). Moreover, at
   \[
   (p,r,h)=(7,1,2)

   \[

   \[
   \mathcal J_{7,2}(1)=1+3^2+2^2=14=0\pmod7.
   \]
   Thus \(\mathcal J=0\) is not equivalent by definition, or even as a universal admissible-grid condition, to \(b_r=0\).

The exact scalar certificate is
\[
\boxed{
1\in
\bigl(N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi^{\rm phys}_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h}\bigr)

This is a genuine reduction: it replaces the original degree-\(2M\) mark by a degree-\(2M-2\) secant-direction norm on the already finite exceptional fiber, works without factoring large irreducible components, and is not a tautological restatement of \(b_r\). # 1. Exact Apéry transfer and Cassini identity

Let
\[
\mathcal T_h(r)=T_{r+h-1}\cdots T_r.
\tag{1.1}
\]
A direct induction gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_h(r)=
\begin{pmatrix}
N_{h+1}(r-1)&-r^3N_h(r)\\
(r+h)^3N_h(r-1)&-r^3(r+h)^3N_{h-1}(r)
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{M_{12}(r,h)=-r^3N_h(r).}
\tag{1.3}
\]
For every solution \(y\),
\[
\mathcal T_h(r)\binom{y_r}{y_{r-1}}
=D_h(r)\binom{y_{r+h}}{y_{r+h-1}}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Taking determinants gives
\[
\boxed{
N_h(r)N_h(r-1)
-N_{h+1}(r-1)N_{h-1}(r)
 =\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^6.
}
\tag{1.5}
\]
Suppose \(N_h(r)=0\) in a boundary-saturated residue field. Then
\[
-N_{h+1}(r-1)N_{h-1}(r)
=\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^6\in K^\times,
\tag{1.6}
\]
so both factors on the left are units. The normalized transfer has the form
\[
D_h(r)^{-1}\mathcal T_h(r)
=
\begin{pmatrix}\nu_A&0\\ *&\omega_A\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\nu_A,\omega_A\in K^\times,
\tag{1.7}
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
\nu_A(r,h)=\frac{N_{h+1}(r-1)}{D_h(r)}.
}
\tag{1.8}
\]
Thus a local return is simple and preserves the Dirichlet line, but it does not select the distinguished global solution.

For the distinguished Apéry solution, (1.4) gives on \(N_h=0\)
\[
\boxed{b_{r+h}=\nu_A(r,h)b_r.}
\tag{1.9}
\]
Since \(\nu_A\) is a unit,
\[
b_{r+h}=0\Longleftrightarrow b_r=0
\tag{1.10}
\]
on the local Apéry return fiber.

# 2. Exact Racah transfer and the two local return multipliers

The Racah kernel
\[
K(r,s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(r,s)}
 \binom rk\binom{r+k}{k}
 \binom sk\binom{s+k}{k}
\tag{2.1}
\]
satisfies
\[
(s+1)^3K(r,s+1)
=\gamma_s(\lambda_r)K(r,s)-s^3K(r,s-1).
\tag{2.2}
\]
The same transfer calculation gives
\[
\mathcal C_h(r;\Lambda)=
\begin{pmatrix}
C_{h+1}(r-1;\Lambda)&-r^3C_h(r;\Lambda)\\
(r+h)^3C_h(r-1;\Lambda)&-r^3(r+h)^3C_{h-1}(r;\Lambda)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
At \(R_{h-1}(r)=C_h(r;\lambda_r)=0\), the Racah Cassini identity again makes both diagonal entries units, and
\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)=\nu_R(r,h)b_r,
\qquad
\nu_R(r,h)=
\frac{C_{h+1}(r-1;\lambda_r)}{D_h(r)}\in K^\times.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
Here \(K(r,r)=b_r\).

Since
\[
K(r,r-1)=\frac{b_r+b_{r-1}}6,
\tag{2.5}
\]
when \(b_r=0\) one obtains
\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)
=-\frac{r^3b_{r-1}}{6D_h(r)}R_{h-1}(r).
}
\tag{2.6}
\]
Therefore a marked failure gives
\[
N_h(r)=R_{h-1}(r)=0.
\tag{2.7}
\]
Conversely, on the local common-root algebra,
\[
\boxed{
 b_{r+h}=\nu_A b_r,
 \qquad
 K(r,r+h)=\nu_R b_r.
}
\tag{2.8}
\]
The local fiber determines \(\nu_A,\nu_R\); the distinguished prefix determines the remaining coordinate \(b_r\).

At \((h,p)=(3,433)\), the roots \(164\) and \(265\) are exchanged by
\[
r\longmapsto p-1-h-r.
\]
They are regular local common-root states, but the supplied computation gives \(b_{164},b_{265}\in\mathbf F_{433}^\times\). Their distinguished states are therefore transverse to the local Dirichlet line. Large irreducible factors cause no conceptual change: (1.7), (2.4), and (2.8) hold in the corresponding finite residue algebra without choosing a rational root.

# 3. Zeroth-order Casoratian no-go theorem

For two Apéry solutions \(y,z\), define
\[
W_n(y,z)=n^3\bigl(y_nz_{n-1}-y_{n-1}z_n\bigr).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Equation (0.2) gives
\[
W_{n+1}(y,z)=W_n(y,z).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let \(\phi^A\) be normalized by
\[
\phi^A_{r-1}=1,\qquad \phi^A_r=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Its exact values are
\[
\boxed{
\phi^A_{r+j}
=-\frac{r^3N_j(r)}{\prod_{i=1}^{j}(r+i)^3}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Thus \(N_h(r)=0\) is exactly \(\phi^A_{r+h}=0\). For the distinguished solution,
\[
\boxed{W_r(b,\phi^A)=r^3b_r.}
\tag{3.5}
\]
Hence, after boundary saturation,
\[
 b_r=0
 \Longleftrightarrow
 W(b,\phi^A)=0
 \Longleftrightarrow
 b\in K\phi^A.
\tag{3.6}
\]
If \(b_r=0\), no-consecutive-zero gives \(b_{r-1}\ne0\), and uniqueness gives
\[
 b_n=b_{r-1}\phi^A_n
\tag{3.7}
\]
throughout the local interval.

Let \(\mathscr S\) be the two-dimensional solution space over the localized residue field. The line
\[
L_r=\{y\in\mathscr S:y_r=0\}
\]
is spanned by \(\phi^A\). Every alternating bilinear form on \(\mathscr S\) is a scalar multiple of \(W\), since
\[
\dim\bigwedge^2\mathscr S=1.
\]
Transfer to another regular index multiplies the coordinate determinant only by a known unit. Therefore:

> **Every nonzero zeroth-order \(2\times2\) transfer/Casoratian invariant testing whether the distinguished Apéry line equals the local Dirichlet line is a localized unit multiple of \(b_r\).**

This is a rank-one exterior-power obstruction, not a normalization problem. The marked incidence has only one zeroth-order Pluecker coordinate.

A companion calculation makes the tautology explicit. Let \(c_0=0,c_1=1\). Then
\[
W(b,c)=-1.
\tag{3.8}
\]
At a marked left zero,
\[
c_r=\frac1{r^3b_{r-1}}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
At a marked right zero, transfer plus (1.6) gives
\[
b_{r+h-1}c_{r+h}=\frac1{(r+h)^3},
\tag{3.10}
\]
which merely reproduces \(W(b,c)=-1\). No extra unit appears.

Thus an all-\(h\) proof must leave the zeroth exterior power: one needs a parameter jet, a distinguished mixed current, a secant/osculating invariant, or another enlarged module.

# 4. The exact distinguished Lagrange current

Put \(y_j=K(r,j)\) and define
\[
\mathcal W_j
=j^3\bigl(b_{j-1}y_j-b_jy_{j-1}\bigr).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Subtracting the Apéry and Racah equations gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_{j+1}-\mathcal W_j
=2(2j+1)
 \bigl(\lambda_r-2(2j+1)^2\bigr)b_jK(r,j).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
If \(s=r+h\) and \(b_r=b_s=0\), summing gives
\[
\boxed{
 s^3b_{s-1}K(r,s)
=2\sum_{j=r+1}^{s-1}
 (2j+1)\bigl(\lambda_r-2(2j+1)^2\bigr)
 b_jK(r,j).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Define the distinguished finite-window invariant
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{p,h}(r)
:=2\sum_{j=r+1}^{r+h-1}
 (2j+1)\bigl(\lambda_r-2(2j+1)^2\bigr)
 b_jK(r,j).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
A marked failure has \(K(r,r+h)=0\), hence
\[
\boxed{
\text{marked failure}\Longrightarrow\mathcal L_{p,h}(r)=0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Thus a unit theorem for \(\mathcal L_{p,h}\) on the saturated common-root algebra is itself an exact transfer/Casoratian route to P3.2.

The limitation is precise. Let \(\phi^R\) be the local Racah Dirichlet solution with
\[
\phi^R_{r-1}=1,\qquad \phi^R_r=0.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Under the mark \(b_r=0\), equations (3.7) and (2.5) give
\[
 b_j=b_{r-1}\phi^A_j,
 \qquad
 K(r,j)=\frac{b_{r-1}}6\phi^R_j.
\tag{4.7}
\]
If also \(N_h=R_{h-1}=0\), both local Dirichlet solutions vanish at \(r+h\). Substituting (4.7) into (4.3) reduces the interior sum to \(b_{r-1}^2/6\) times the ordinary local Green identity, whose boundary current is zero.

Therefore the **locally normalized core** of (4.4) is automatic on a common Dirichlet root. This does not say that the distinguished value \(\mathcal L_{p,h}\) vanishes at an unmarked phantom root; it may well separate the \(p=433\) roots. It says that proving such separation must use the distinguished global prefix, not merely \(N_h,R_{h-1}\) and their local Cassini identities.

For non-rational components, \(b_j\) and \(K(r,j)\) in (4.4) can be replaced by the polynomial Gram evaluations introduced in Section 6, so the current also defines an element of the finite exceptional algebra.

# 5. Audit of the first parameter jet

The standard shifted family satisfies
\[
(n+1+t)^3b_{n+1}(t)
=P(n+t)b_n(t)-(n+t)^3b_{n-1}(t),
\tag{5.1}
\]
with \(b_{-1}(t)=0,b_0(t)=1\). Put
\[
g_n=\left.\partial_tb_n(t)\right|_{t=0}.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
(n+1)^3g_{n+1}={}&P(n)g_n-n^3g_{n-1}+P'(n)b_n\\
&-3(n+1)^2b_{n+1}-3n^2b_{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]
If
\[
F_n=P'(n)b_n-3(n+1)^2b_{n+1}-3n^2b_{n-1}
\]
and
\[
J_n=n^3\bigl(b_ng_{n-1}-b_{n-1}g_n\bigr),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{J_{n+1}-J_n=-b_nF_n.}
\tag{5.3}
\]
At \(b_r=0\), only the single increment at \(r\) vanishes. Neither \(N_h=0\) nor \(R_{h-1}=0\) currently forces \(J_r\), \(J_{r+h}\), or their difference to be a unit or to vanish.

The exact reflection-polynomial construction from the first jet has
\[
H_n(0)=-(n!)^2b_n.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Thus its endpoint zero is exactly the original mark. The jet construction remains promising, but no established identity in the current chain converts \(N_h=R_{h-1}=0\) into a nonzero jet current. Claiming that would rename the mark or assume the missing incidence theorem.

# 6. The first secant transfer and the strictly smaller invariant

Let \(p\) be odd and \(M=(p-1)/2\). Define
\[
c_k=k(k+1),
\qquad
 d_0(T)=1,
\qquad
 d_{k+1}(T)=\frac{T-c_k}{(k+1)^2}d_k(T).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Thus
\[
d_k(T)=\frac1{(k!)^2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(T-j(j+1)).
\]
For every integer label \(n\) modulo \(p\),
\[
\boxed{d_k(\lambda_n)=D(n,k).}
\tag{6.2}
\]
This remains true above the midpoint; the factors implement the reflection/support zeros automatically.

Define
\[
v(T)=(d_0(T),\ldots,d_M(T)),
\tag{6.3}
\]
\[
B_p(T)=\langle v(T),v(T)\rangle
      =\sum_{k=0}^{M}d_k(T)^2,
\tag{6.4}
\]
\[
\mathcal K_p(X,Y)
=\langle v(X),v(Y)\rangle
=\sum_{k=0}^{M}d_k(X)d_k(Y).
\tag{6.5}
\]
At grid points,
\[
\boxed{
 B_p(\lambda_n)=b_n,
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_p(\lambda_r,\lambda_s)=K(r,s).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Put
\[
e_k(X,Y)=\frac{d_k(Y)-d_k(X)}{Y-X}.
\tag{6.7}
\]
This is a polynomial and has the triangular transfer
\[
\boxed{
\binom{d_{k+1}(X)}{e_{k+1}(X,Y)}
=\frac1{(k+1)^2}
\begin{pmatrix}
X-c_k&0\\
1&Y-c_k
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{d_k(X)}{e_k(X,Y)}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]
The initial vector is \((1,0)^T\), so
\[
e_0=0,\qquad e_1=1.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Define the first-secant Gram invariants
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_p(X,Y)
=\langle v(X),e(X,Y)\rangle
=\frac{\mathcal K_p(X,Y)-B_p(X)}{Y-X},
}
\tag{6.10}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_p(X,Y)
=\langle e(X,Y),e(X,Y)\rangle
=\frac{B_p(Y)+B_p(X)-2\mathcal K_p(X,Y)}{(Y-X)^2}.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]
The second quotient is polynomial because its numerator is
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{M}\bigl(d_k(Y)-d_k(X)\bigr)^2.
\]

Set
\[
X=\lambda_r,\qquad Y=\lambda_{r+h},\qquad
\Delta=Y-X=h(2r+h+1).
\tag{6.12}
\]
After gap and mate saturation, \(\Delta\) is a unit. Then
\[
\mathcal T_{p,h}(r)
=\frac{K(r,r+h)-b_r}{\Delta},
\tag{6.13}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_{p,h}(r)
=\frac{b_{r+h}+b_r-2K(r,r+h)}{\Delta^2}
=\sum_{k=1}^{M}
\left(
 \frac{D(r+h,k)-D(r,k)}{\Delta}
\right)^2.
}
\tag{6.14}
\]

If
\[
b_r=0,\qquad N_h(r)=0,\qquad R_{h-1}(r)=0,
\]
then (1.9) and (2.4) give
\[
b_{r+h}=K(r,r+h)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{p,h}(r)=\mathcal J_{p,h}(r)=0.
}
\tag{6.15}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J_{p,h}(r)\ne0}
\tag{6.16}
\]
at every admissible local common root proves the desired exclusion.

Geometrically, a marked failure makes \(v(X)\) and \(v(Y)\) isotropic and mutually orthogonal. Their secant direction is therefore isotropic. The invariant \(\mathcal J\) is exactly its quadratic norm. Thus (6.16) is the **secant-direction anisotropy theorem on the exceptional fiber**.

## Strict degree drop

The polynomial \(d_k\) has degree \(k\), while \(e_k\) has total degree \(k-1\). Hence
\[
\deg B_p=2M,
\tag{6.17}
\]
\[
\deg_{\rm tot}\mathcal T_p=2M-1,
\qquad
\deg_{\rm tot}\mathcal J_p=2M-2.
\tag{6.18}
\]
This is a strict descent in the Newton/osculating filtration.

\[
X=\lambda_1=2,\qquad Y=\lambda_3=12,\qquad \Delta=10\ne0\pmod7.
\]
One has
\[
v(X)=(1,2,0,0),
\qquad
v(Y)=(1,12,30,20),

so
\[

\]
Thus
\[
\mathcal J_{7,2}(1)=1+9+4=14=0\pmod7,

while
\[
b_1=5\ne0\pmod7.

The boundary and mate factors are units. Hence \(\mathcal J=0\) is genuinely not equivalent to the Apéry mark.

On the saturated common-root algebra, (2.8), (6.13), and (6.14) give
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{p,h}
=\frac{\nu_R-1}{\Delta}\,b_r,

\tag{6.19}

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_{p,h}
=\frac{\nu_A+1-2\nu_R}{\Delta^2}\,b_r.
}
\tag{6.20}
\]
This factorization should be retained in any proof attempt. The local exceptional fiber supplies the multiplier defect; the distinguished prefix supplies \(b_r\). A proof that \(\mathcal J\) is a unit must rule out vanishing of this product on every admissible component. Thus the secant invariant is a stronger, lower-degree target, not an already completed proof in disguise.

# 7. Exact exceptional-algebra formulation

Let \(I_{p,h}^{\rm phys}\) be the precise admissible set after boundary and mate conventions, and put
\[
\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}(r)
=\prod_{a\in I_{p,h}^{\rm phys}}(r-a).
\tag{7.1}
\]
For the usual lower-half convention,
\[
I_{p,h}^{\rm phys}
=\{1,2,\ldots,(p-1)/2-h\}.
\]
If the implementation retains both reflected representatives, use that exact grid; none of the algebra changes.

Work in
\[
A_{p,h}=\mathbf F_p[r,\Sigma_h^{-1}].
\tag{7.2}
\]
The physical exceptional algebra is
\[
\boxed{
E_{p,h}^{\rm phys}
=A_{p,h}/(N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
The scalar secant theorem is
\[
\boxed{
\overline{\mathcal J}_{p,h}
\in(E_{p,h}^{\rm phys})^\times.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
1\in
(N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys},\mathcal J_{p,h})
:\Sigma_h^\infty.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
After clearing denominators, this means that there are polynomials
\(U,V,W,A\) and \(m\ge0\) such that
\[
UN_h+VR_{h-1}+W\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}
+A\mathcal J_{p,h}=\Sigma_h^m.
\tag{7.6}
\]

The weaker first-secant ideal condition is
\[
\boxed{
1\in
(N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys},
 \mathcal T_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h})
:\Sigma_h^\infty.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]
It suffices because every marked failure annihilates both first-secant invariants. One may weaken further by adjoining the distinguished current:
\[
\boxed{
1\in
(N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys},
 \mathcal L_{p,h},\mathcal T_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h})
:\Sigma_h^\infty.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]
Each added generator is forced to vanish by a marked failure, so a unit ideal excludes such a failure.

Since the ring is one-variable after specializing \(p,h\), form the saturated physical gcd
\[
G_{p,h}^{\rm phys}
=\gcd(N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}).
\tag{7.9}
\]
Then the scalar target is
\[
\gcd(G_{p,h}^{\rm phys},\mathcal J_{p,h})=1,
\tag{7.10}
\]
and the weaker multi-generator target is
\[
\gcd(G_{p,h}^{\rm phys},
      \mathcal L_{p,h},\mathcal T_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h})=1.
\tag{7.11}
\]
These are marked first-order tests, not generic resultants of \(N_h\) and \(R_{h-1}\).

To test all geometric components, including large irreducible factors, omit \(\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}\) and work in
\[
E_{p,h}^{\rm alg}
=\bigl(A_{p,h}/(N_h,R_{h-1})\bigr)_{\rm red}.
\tag{7.12}
\]
The formulas for \(d_k,e_k,\mathcal T,\mathcal J\) are polynomial. The current \(\mathcal L\) can likewise be written with the polynomial evaluations \(B_p(\lambda_j)\) and \(\mathcal K_p(\lambda_r,\lambda_j)\). Hence all invariants are evaluated directly in each finite field factor, with no rational-root assumption or factor splitting.

For the \(p=433,h=3\) fiber, the immediate exact diagnostic is:

1. form the saturated gcd factor containing \(164,265\);
2. reduce \(\mathcal L_{433,3}\), \(\mathcal J_{433,3}\), and if needed \(\mathcal T_{433,3}\) in that factor;
3. test whether their ideal is the unit ideal.

The known unit values of \(b_{164}\) and \(b_{265}\) prove that these points are harmless. The new reduction asks whether harmlessness is already visible in the distinguished current or one osculating level lower.

# 8. What has and has not been proved

## Proved here

1. The exact transfer identity (1.2), hence \(M_{12}=-r^3N_h\).
2. The Cassini unit identity (1.5), showing a regular local return has unit diagonal multipliers.
3. The exact multiplier factorizations
   \[
   b_{r+h}=\nu_A b_r,
   \qquad
   K(r,r+h)=\nu_R b_r.
   \]
4. The zeroth-order no-go theorem: every ordinary line-incidence Casoratian is a unit multiple of \(b_r\).
5. The exact distinguished mixed current (4.2)--(4.5), and the fact that its locally normalized core is the automatic Green identity.
6. The exact first-secant transfer (6.8).
7. The marked implication
   \[
   b_r=N_h=R_{h-1}=0
   \Longrightarrow
   \mathcal J_{p,h}=0.
   \]
8. The strict degree drop \(2M\to2M-2\) and an explicit admissible example proving that \(\mathcal J=0\) is not equivalent to \(b_r=0\).
9. The exact saturated Bezout/unit certificates (7.5), (7.7), and (7.8), valid for rational and irreducible exceptional components.

## Not proved here

I do not claim
\[
\mathcal J_{p,h}(r)\ne0
\]
or
\[
(\mathcal L_{p,h},\mathcal T_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h})=(1)
\]
for every admissible common root. Those are the new strictly smaller marked-invariant theorems. Nor did I find an exact marked counterexample in the inspected project state. The \(p=433,h=3\) roots are explicitly unmarked and therefore are not counterexamples.

## Final conclusion

A uniform zeroth-order transfer/Casoratian proof cannot exist in the proposed form, because the unique line-incidence Casoratian is \(r^3b_r\). The exact distinguished Apéry--Racah current is a valid marked obstruction, but its locally normalized core is automatic and therefore cannot be controlled from the two local continuants alone. The first genuinely lower-degree obstruction is the secant-direction norm \(\mathcal J_{p,h}\), two degrees below the Apéry form. Proving that this element, or the larger ideal generated by \(\mathcal L_{p,h},\mathcal T_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h}\), is a unit in the saturated physical exceptional algebra proves P3.2. Its nonvanishing is not equivalent by definition to \(b_r\).

This is the appropriate next invariant to evaluate on the known exceptional fibers before attempting another all-gap resultant or statistical argument.
