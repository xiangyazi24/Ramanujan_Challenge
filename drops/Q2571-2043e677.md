# P3.2 exceptional fibers: the zeroth-order Casoratian is necessarily the mark, and the first non-tautological obstruction is the secant-direction norm

## Executive verdict

I do **not** obtain an all-\(h\), all-\(p\) proof or a marked counterexample from the current state.  I obtain the third requested outcome, together with an exact no-go theorem explaining why the desired proof cannot come from an ordinary two-solution Casoratian.

The decisive facts are these.

1. With the canonical normalization,
   \[
   \boxed{M_{12}(r,h)=-r^3N_h(r).}
   \]
   Thus, after boundary saturation, a root of \(M_{12}\) is exactly a local Apéry Dirichlet return \(N_h(r)=0\).  At such a root the normalized local transfer is lower triangular with **both diagonal entries units**.

2. Let \(\phi^{A}_{r,h}\) be the local Apéry solution with
   \(\phi^A_{r-1}=1,\phi^A_r=0\).  The distinguished Apéry solution \(b\) lies on this local line exactly when \(b_r=0\).  Its Casoratian with \(\phi^A\) is
   \[
   \boxed{W_r(b,\phi^A)=r^3b_r.}
   \]
   Since the solution space is two-dimensional, every zeroth-order alternating transfer invariant comparing these two lines is a localized unit multiple of this one.  Consequently, **any ordinary \(2\times2\) transfer/Casoratian incidence test is equivalent to \(b_r\) itself**.  There is no different zeroth-order Casoratian waiting to remove the marked condition.

3. The exact Apéry--Racah Lagrange-current identity does not repair this.  On a common root of \(N_h\) and the Racah continuant, it becomes the Green identity for two local Dirichlet solutions and vanishes for every local common root, including the unmarked exceptional roots.  It therefore cannot distinguish the \(p=433,h=3\) phantom roots from a marked failure.

4. A genuinely smaller, non-definitional marked obstruction is obtained by passing from the point \(v(X)\) on the Apéry Gram curve to its **secant direction**.  Put
   \[
   M=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad \lambda_n=n(n+1),
   \]
   \[
   d_k(T)=\frac1{(k!)^2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\bigl(T-j(j+1)\bigr),
   \qquad
   v(T)=(d_0(T),\ldots,d_M(T)).

   \(\Delta=Y-X=h(2r+h+1)\), define

   \boxed{
   \mathcal J_p(X,Y)
     :=\left\langle\frac{v(Y)-v(X)}{Y-X},
                         \frac{v(Y)-v(X)}{Y-X}\right\rangle
     =\sum_{k=1}^{M}
       \left(\frac{d_k(Y)-d_k(X)}{Y-X}\right)^2 .
   }
   \tag{E1}
   \]
   The quotients are polynomials, so \(\mathcal J_p\) is defined on rational and non-rational exceptional components without choosing roots.

   At grid points this is
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal J_{p,h}(r)
   =\sum_{k=1}^{M}
      \left(
       \frac{D(r+h,k)-D(r,k)}{h(2r+h+1)}
      \right)^2,
   \qquad
   D(n,k)=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}.
   }
   \tag{E2}
   \]

   A marked failure has
   \[
   b_r=b_{r+h}=K(r,r+h)=0.
   \]
   Hence
   \[
   \boxed{
   \text{marked failure}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

   \]
   Therefore the exact sufficient theorem is that \(\mathcal J_{p,h}\) is a unit on the boundary/mate/pole-saturated physical common-root algebra of \(M_{12}\) and \(R_{h-1}\).

5. This is strictly smaller and not merely \(b_r\) under another name.  The Hasse/Apéry form

# 1. Exact Apéry transfer and Cassini identity

Let
\[
\mathcal T_h(r)=T_{r+h-1}\cdots T_r.
\tag{1.1}
\]
A direct induction gives the complete matrix formula
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_h(r)=
\begin{pmatrix}
N_{h+1}(r-1)&-r^3N_h(r)\\
(r+h)^3N_h(r-1)&-r^3(r+h)^3N_{h-1}(r)
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{M_{12}(r,h)=-r^3N_h(r).}
\tag{1.3}
\]
For every solution \(y\),
\[
\mathcal T_h(r)inom{y_r}{y_{r-1}}
=D_h(r)\binom{y_{r+h}}{y_{r+h-1}}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Taking determinants in (1.2) gives the exact Cassini identity
\[
\boxed{
N_h(r)N_h(r-1)
-N_{h+1}(r-1)N_{h-1}(r)
 =\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^6.
}
\tag{1.5}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\det\mathcal T_h(r)
=r^3(r+h)^3\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^6.
\]

Suppose now that \(N_h(r)=0\) in a boundary-saturated residue field.  Equation (1.5) becomes
\[
-N_{h+1}(r-1)N_{h-1}(r)
=\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(r+j)^6\in K^\times.
\tag{1.6}
\]
Thus both factors on the left are units.  The normalized local transfer has the form
\[
D_h(r)^{-1}\mathcal T_h(r)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
u_A&0\\ *&\omega_A\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\nu_A,\omega_A\in K^\times,
\tag{1.7}
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
\nu_A(r,h)=\frac{N_{h+1}(r-1)}{D_h(r)}.
}
\tag{1.8}
\]
Thus the local return is simple and transverse: it preserves the Dirichlet line, but it does not select the distinguished global solution.

For the distinguished Apéry solution, (1.4) gives on \(N_h=0\)
\[
\boxed{
b_{r+h}=\nu_A(r,h)b_r.}
\tag{1.9}
\]
Since \(\nu_A\) is a unit,
\[
b_{r+h}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_r=0
\tag{1.10}
\]
on the local Apéry return fiber.  This is the exact separation between a local return and a marked return.

# 2. Exact Racah transfer and the two local return multipliers

The Racah kernel
\[
K(r,s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(r,s)}
 \binom rk\binom{r+k}{k}
 \binom sk\binom{s+k}{k}
\tag{2.1}
\]
satisfies, as a function of \(s\),
\[
(s+1)^3K(r,s+1)
=\gamma_s(\lambda_r)K(r,s)-s^3K(r,s-1).
\tag{2.2}
\]
The same transfer calculation as above gives
\[
\mathcal C_h(r;\Lambda)=
\begin{pmatrix}
C_{h+1}(r-1;\Lambda)&-r^3C_h(r;\Lambda)\\
(r+h)^3C_h(r-1;\Lambda)&-r^3(r+h)^3C_{h-1}(r;\Lambda)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
At \(R_{h-1}(r)=C_h(r;\lambda_r)=0\), the Racah Cassini identity again makes both diagonal entries units, and
\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)=\nu_R(r,h)b_r,
\qquad
\nu_R(r,h)=
\frac{C_{h+1}(r-1;\lambda_r)}{D_h(r)}\in K^\times.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
Here we used \(K(r,r)=b_r\).

The familiar marked formula follows by using the left Dirichlet initial data.  Since
\[
K(r,r-1)=\frac{b_r+b_{r-1}}6,
\tag{2.5}
\]
when \(b_r=0\) one obtains
\[
\boxed{
K(r,r+h)
=-\frac{r^3b_{r-1}}{6D_h(r)}R_{h-1}(r).
}
\tag{2.6}
\]
Therefore a marked failure indeed gives
\[
N_h(r)=R_{h-1}(r)=0.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Conversely, on the local common-root algebra one has the two multiplier identities
\[
\boxed{
 b_{r+h}=\nu_A b_r,
 \qquad
 K(r,r+h)=\nu_R b_r.
}
\tag{2.8}
\]
These identities are useful diagnostics: the local fiber determines \(\nu_A\) and \(\nu_R\), while the distinguished prefix determines the single global coordinate \(b_r\).

At \((h,p)=(3,433)\), the roots \(164\) and \(265\) are exchanged by the gap reflection
\[
r\longmapsto p-1-h-r.
\]
They are regular local common-root states, but the supplied computation gives \(b_{164},b_{265}\in\mathbf F_{433}^\times\).  Thus their distinguished state is transverse to the local Dirichlet line.  They are exact examples of why a common modular root is not a marked failure.

Large irreducible factors cause no conceptual change: (1.7), (2.4), and (2.8) hold in the corresponding finite residue algebra, without choosing a rational root.

# 3. Zeroth-order Casoratian no-go theorem

For two solutions \(y,z\) of the Apéry equation define
\[
W_n(y,z)=n^3\bigl(y_nz_{n-1}-y_{n-1}z_n\bigr).
\tag{3.1}
\]
A one-line use of (0.2) gives
\[
W_{n+1}(y,z)=W_n(y,z).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let \(\phi^A=\phi^A_{r,h}\) be the local Dirichlet solution normalized by
\[
\phi^A_{r-1}=1,\qquad \phi^A_r=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Its exact values are
\[
\boxed{
\phi^A_{r+j}
=-\frac{r^3N_j(r)}{\prod_{i=1}^{j}(r+i)^3}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
In particular, \(N_h(r)=0\) is exactly \(\phi^A_{r+h}=0\).

For the distinguished solution,
\[
\boxed{
W_r(b,\phi^A)=r^3b_r.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
Hence, after boundary saturation,
\[
 b_r=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 W(b,\phi^A)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 b\in K\phi^A.
\tag{3.6}
\]
If \(b_r=0\), no-consecutive-zero gives \(b_{r-1}\ne0\), and uniqueness gives
\[
 b_n=b_{r-1}\phi^A_n
\tag{3.7}
\]
throughout the local interval.

## No-go theorem

Let \(\mathscr S\) be the two-dimensional solution space over the localized residue field.  The line
\[
L_r=\{y\in\mathscr S:y_r=0\}
\]
is one-dimensional and is spanned by \(\phi^A\).  Every alternating bilinear form on \(\mathscr S\) is a scalar multiple of \(W\), because
\[
\dim\bigwedge^2\mathscr S=1.
\]
Transfer to another regular index multiplies the coordinate determinant only by the known nonzero determinant of the transfer matrix.  Therefore:

> **Every nonzero zeroth-order \(2\times2\) transfer/Casoratian invariant that tests whether the distinguished Apéry line equals the local Dirichlet line is a localized unit multiple of \(b_r\).**

This is not a lack of a clever normalization.  It is a rank-one exterior-power obstruction.  The marked incidence has only one Pluecker coordinate, namely \(b_r\).

A companion-solution calculation makes the tautology explicit.  Let \(c_0=0,c_1=1\).  Then
\[
W(b,c)=-1.
\tag{3.8}
\]
At a marked left zero,
\[
c_r=\frac1{r^3b_{r-1}}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
At a marked right zero, transfer plus (1.6) gives
\[
b_{r+h-1}c_{r+h}=\frac1{(r+h)^3},
\tag{3.10}
\]
which merely reproduces \(W(b,c)=-1\).  No contradiction or extra unit appears.

Thus an all-\(h\) proof must leave the zeroth exterior power: one needs a parameter jet, a secant/osculating invariant, or another genuinely enlarged module.

# 4. The exact Apéry--Racah Lagrange current, and why it is still local

Put
\[
y_j=K(r,j).
\]
Define the mixed current
\[
\mathcal W_j
=j^3\bigl(b_{j-1}y_j-b_jy_{j-1}\bigr).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Subtracting the Apéry and Racah equations gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_{j+1}-\mathcal W_j
=2(2j+1)
 \bigl(\lambda_r-2(2j+1)^2\bigr)b_jK(r,j).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
If \(s=r+h\) and \(b_r=b_s=0\), summing gives
\[
\boxed{
 s^3b_{s-1}K(r,s)
=2\sum_{j=r+1}^{s-1}
 (2j+1)\bigl(\lambda_r-2(2j+1)^2\bigr)
 b_jK(r,j).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
This is the correct uniform transfer/Casoratian identity.

However, on a local common root it does not contain the missing mark.  Let \(\phi^R\) be the Racah Dirichlet solution with
\[
\phi^R_{r-1}=1,\qquad \phi^R_r=0.
\tag{4.4}
\]
When \(b_r=0\), equations (3.7) and (2.5) give
\[
 b_j=b_{r-1}\phi^A_j,
 \qquad
 K(r,j)=\frac{b_{r-1}}6\phi^R_j.
\tag{4.5}
\]
If also \(N_h=R_{h-1}=0\), then both local solutions vanish at \(s\).  Substitution into (4.3) reduces its right side to \(b_{r-1}^2/6\) times the ordinary Green identity for the two local Dirichlet solutions.  That Green identity is already zero solely because both endpoints are Dirichlet.

Therefore (4.3) vanishes at every local common root after the local normalizations are imposed.  It is an exact identity, but it does not distinguish marked and phantom common roots.  In particular, it cannot by itself exclude the \(p=433\) roots.

This also explains why mixed continuant determinants or local spectral splits return the common-root condition rather than the distinguished prefix condition: they live entirely inside the local interval.

# 5. Audit of the first parameter jet

For completeness, the standard shifted Apéry family satisfies
\[
(n+1+t)^3b_{n+1}(t)
=P(n+t)b_n(t)-(n+t)^3b_{n-1}(t),
\tag{5.1}
\]
with \(b_{-1}(t)=0,b_0(t)=1\).  Put
\[
g_n=\left.\partial_tb_n(t)\right|_{t=0}.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
(n+1)^3g_{n+1}={}&P(n)g_n-n^3g_{n-1}+P'(n)b_n\\
&-3(n+1)^2b_{n+1}-3n^2b_{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]
If
\[
F_n=P'(n)b_n-3(n+1)^2b_{n+1}-3n^2b_{n-1}
\]
and
\[
J_n=n^3\bigl(b_ng_{n-1}-b_{n-1}g_n\bigr),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{J_{n+1}-J_n=-b_nF_n.}
\tag{5.3}
\]
At \(b_r=0\), only the single increment at \(r\) vanishes.  Neither \(N_h=0\) nor \(R_{h-1}=0\) currently forces \(J_r\), \(J_{r+h}\), or their difference to be a unit or to vanish.

The exact reflection-polynomial construction from the first jet has the endpoint identity
\[
H_n(0)=-(n!)^2b_n.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Thus its endpoint zero is exactly the original mark.  The jet construction is valuable, but no established identity in the current chain converts the local equations \(N_h=R_{h-1}=0\) into a nonzero jet current.  Claiming otherwise would simply rename \(b_r\) or assume the missing marked incidence theorem.

The secant construction below has a different advantage: its vanishing is forced by a marked failure, it has strictly lower polynomial degree, and it is defined over every exceptional residue algebra.

# 6. The first secant transfer and the smaller invariant

Let \(p\) be odd and \(M=(p-1)/2\).  Define
\[
c_k=k(k+1),
\qquad
 d_0(T)=1,
\qquad
 d_{k+1}(T)=\frac{T-c_k}{(k+1)^2}d_k(T).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Thus
\[
d_k(T)=\frac1{(k!)^2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(T-j(j+1)).
\]
For every integer label \(n\) modulo \(p\),
\[
\boxed{d_k(\lambda_n)=D(n,k).}
\tag{6.2}
\]
This remains true above the midpoint; the factors automatically implement the reflection/support zeros.

Define the Apéry Gram curve and its polarization by
\[
v(T)=(d_0(T),\ldots,d_M(T)),
\tag{6.3}
\]
\[
B_p(T)=\langle v(T),v(T)\rangle
      =\sum_{k=0}^{M}d_k(T)^2,
\tag{6.4}
\]
\[
\mathcal K_p(X,Y)
=\langle v(X),v(Y)\rangle
=\sum_{k=0}^{M}d_k(X)d_k(Y).
\tag{6.5}
\]
At grid points,
\[
\boxed{
 B_p(\lambda_n)=b_n,
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_p(\lambda_r,\lambda_s)=K(r,s).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

For \(X\ne Y\), put
\[
e_k(X,Y)=\frac{d_k(Y)-d_k(X)}{Y-X}.
\tag{6.7}
\]
This is an honest polynomial.  It has a uniform triangular transfer:
\[
\boxed{
\binom{d_{k+1}(X)}{e_{k+1}(X,Y)}
=\frac1{(k+1)^2}
\begin{pmatrix}
X-c_k&0\\
1&Y-c_k
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{d_k(X)}{e_k(X,Y)}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]
The initial vector is \((1,0)^T\).  In particular,
\[
e_0=0,\qquad e_1=1.
\tag{6.9}
\]

There are two first-secant Gram invariants:
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_p(X,Y)
=\langle v(X),e(X,Y)\rangle
=\frac{\mathcal K_p(X,Y)-B_p(X)}{Y-X},
}
\tag{6.10}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_p(X,Y)
=\langle e(X,Y),e(X,Y)\rangle
=\frac{B_p(Y)+B_p(X)-2\mathcal K_p(X,Y)}{(Y-X)^2}.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]
The second equality in (6.11) is polynomial because its numerator is
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{M}\bigl(d_k(Y)-d_k(X)\bigr)^2.
\]

Now set
\[
X=\lambda_r,\qquad Y=\lambda_{r+h},\qquad
\Delta=Y-X=h(2r+h+1).
\tag{6.12}
\]
After gap and mate saturation, \(\Delta\) is a unit.  Equations (6.10)--(6.11) become
\[
\mathcal T_{p,h}(r)
=\frac{K(r,r+h)-b_r}{\Delta},
\tag{6.13}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_{p,h}(r)
=\frac{b_{r+h}+b_r-2K(r,r+h)}{\Delta^2}
=\sum_{k=1}^{M}
\left(
 \frac{D(r+h,k)-D(r,k)}{\Delta}
\right)^2.
}
\tag{6.14}
\]

## Marked implication

If
\[
b_r=0,\qquad N_h(r)=0,\qquad R_{h-1}(r)=0,
\]
then (1.9) and (2.4) give
\[
b_{r+h}=K(r,r+h)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{p,h}(r)=\mathcal J_{p,h}(r)=0.
}
\tag{6.15}
\]
In particular, the scalar condition
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J_{p,h}(r)\ne0}
\tag{6.16}
\]
at every admissible local common root proves the desired marked exclusion.

Geometrically, a marked failure makes \(v(X)\) and \(v(Y)\) isotropic and mutually orthogonal.  Hence their secant direction
\[
\frac{v(Y)-v(X)}{Y-X}
\]
is isotropic.  The invariant \(\mathcal J\) is exactly its quadratic norm.  Thus (6.16) is the **secant-direction anisotropy theorem on the exceptional fiber**.

## Strict degree drop

The polynomial \(d_k\) has degree \(k\), while \(e_k\) has total degree \(k-1\).  Hence
\[
\deg B_p=2M,
\tag{6.17}
\]
\[
\deg_{\rm tot}\mathcal T_p=2M-1,
\qquad
\deg_{\rm tot}\mathcal J_p=2M-2.
\tag{6.18}
\]
This is a strict descent in the Newton/osculating filtration, not a change of notation for the same degree-\(2M\) mark.

Thus
\[
\mathcal J_{7,2}(1)=1+9+4=14=0\pmod7,
\]
while
\[
b_1=5\ne0\pmod7.
\]
All boundary and mate factors here are units.  Hence \(\mathcal J=0\) is genuinely not equivalent to the Apéry mark.

## Return-multiplier factorization on the common fiber

Equations (2.8), (6.13), and (6.14) give, in the saturated common-root algebra,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{p,h}
=\frac{\nu_R-1}{\Delta}\,b_r,
}
\tag{6.19}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_{p,h}
=\frac{\nu_A+1-2\nu_R}{\Delta^2}\,b_r.
}
\tag{6.20}
\]
This factorization should be retained in any proof attempt.  It shows exactly what remains:

- the local exceptional fiber supplies the multiplier defect
  \(\nu_A+1-2\nu_R\);
- the distinguished prefix supplies \(b_r\);
- a proof that \(\mathcal J\) is a unit must rule out cancellation of this product on every admissible component.

Thus the secant invariant is a stronger, lower-degree target, not an already completed proof in disguise.

# 7. Exact exceptional-algebra formulation

Let \(I_{p,h}^{\rm phys}\) be the precise admissible set used after boundary and mate conventions, and put
\[
\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}(r)
=\prod_{a\in I_{p,h}^{\rm phys}}(r-a).
\tag{7.1}
\]
For the usual lower-half convention,
\[
I_{p,h}^{\rm phys}
=\{1,2,\ldots,(p-1)/2-h\}.
\]
If the implementation retains both reflected representatives, use that exact grid instead; none of the algebra below changes.

Work in
\[
A_{p,h}=\mathbf F_p[r,\Sigma_h^{-1}].
\tag{7.2}
\]
The physical exceptional algebra is
\[
\boxed{
E_{p,h}^{\rm phys}
=A_{p,h}/
 (N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
The theorem needed for the scalar secant invariant is
\[
\boxed{
\overline{\mathcal J}_{p,h}
\in(E_{p,h}^{\rm phys})^\times.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
1\in
(N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys},\mathcal J_{p,h})
:\Sigma_h^\infty.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
After clearing denominators, this means that there are polynomials
\(U,V,W,A\) and an integer \(m\ge0\) such that
\[
UN_h+VR_{h-1}+W\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}
+A\mathcal J_{p,h}=\Sigma_h^m.
\tag{7.6}
\]

The weaker first-jet ideal condition is
\[
\boxed{
1\in
(N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys},
 \mathcal T_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h})
:\Sigma_h^\infty.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]
It suffices because every marked failure annihilates both first-secant invariants.  It is weaker than demanding that \(\mathcal J\) alone be a unit and may therefore be the better computational target.

Since the ring is one-variable after specialization to \(p,h\), one may equivalently form the saturated physical gcd
\[
G_{p,h}^{\rm phys}
=\gcd
 (N_h,R_{h-1},\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys})
\tag{7.8}
\]
and test
\[
\gcd(G_{p,h}^{\rm phys},\mathcal J_{p,h})=1
\tag{7.9}
\]
or, for the two-generator version,
\[
\gcd(G_{p,h}^{\rm phys},\mathcal T_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h})=1.
\tag{7.10}
\]
These are **marked first-jet** tests, not generic resultants of \(N_h\) and \(R_{h-1}\).

To test all geometric components, including large irreducible factors, omit \(\Pi_{p,h}^{\rm phys}\) and work in
\[
E_{p,h}^{\rm alg}
=\bigl(A_{p,h}/(N_h,R_{h-1})\bigr)_{\rm red}.
\tag{7.11}
\]
The formulas for \(d_k,e_k,\mathcal T,\mathcal J\) are polynomial, so they are evaluated directly in each finite field factor.  No rational-root assumption or factor splitting is required.  The algebraic version is stronger than P3.2; the physical-grid version is the exact required statement.

For the \(p=433,h=3\) fiber, the immediate exact diagnostic is therefore:

1. form the saturated gcd factor containing the roots \(164,265\);
2. reduce \(\mathcal J_{433,3}\) and, if needed, \(\mathcal T_{433,3}\) in that factor;
3. verify whether the first-jet ideal is the unit ideal.

The known unit values of \(b_{164}\) and \(b_{265}\) prove that these points are harmless.  The new reduction asks whether harmlessness is already visible one osculating level lower.

# 8. What has and has not been proved

## Proved here

1. The exact transfer identity (1.2), hence \(M_{12}=-r^3N_h\).
2. The Cassini unit identity (1.5), showing a regular local return has unit diagonal multipliers.
3. The exact multiplier factorizations
   \[
   b_{r+h}=\nu_A b_r,
   \qquad
   K(r,r+h)=\nu_R b_r
   \]
   on the respective local return fibers.
4. The zeroth-order no-go theorem: every ordinary Casoratian detecting the marked line is a unit multiple of \(b_r\).
5. The exact mixed Lagrange current (4.2) and the fact that it collapses to a purely local Green identity on a common Dirichlet root.
6. The exact first-secant transfer (6.8).
7. The marked implication
   \[
   b_r=N_h=R_{h-1}=0
   \Longrightarrow
   \mathcal J_{p,h}=0.
   \]
8. The strict degree drop \(2M\to2M-2\) and an explicit example proving that \(\mathcal J=0\) is not equivalent to \(b_r=0\).
9. The exact saturated Bezout/unit certificate (7.5), with a two-generator weakening (7.7), valid for rational and irreducible exceptional components.

## Not proved here

I do not claim
\[
\mathcal J_{p,h}(r)\ne0
\]
for every admissible common root.  That is the new strictly smaller invariant theorem.  Nor did I find an exact marked counterexample in the inspected project state.  The \(p=433,h=3\) roots are explicitly unmarked and therefore are not counterexamples.

The answer to the original search is therefore sharp:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
A uniform zeroth-order transfer/Casoratian proof cannot exist in the proposed form, because the unique line-incidence Casoratian is \(r^3b_r\).  The exact mixed Apéry--Racah current is local and vanishes at every common Dirichlet root.  The first genuinely non-tautological marked obstruction is the secant-direction norm \(\mathcal J_{p,h}\), of degree two less than the Apéry Hasse form.  Proving that this element (or the pair \((\mathcal T_{p,h},\mathcal J_{p,h})\)) is a unit in the saturated physical exceptional algebra proves P3.2, and this nonvanishing is not equivalent by definition to \(b_r\).
\end{minipage}
}
\]

This is the appropriate next invariant to compute on the known exceptional fibers before attempting another all-gap resultant or statistical argument.
