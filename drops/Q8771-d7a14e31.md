# Can LGTQ-3 be proved for CM Picard–Fuchs families?

## Executive summary

The CM case is a useful proof of concept, but it does not reproduce the full difficulty of the Apéry K3 problem.  In CM families the Frobenius parameters are controlled by one-dimensional Hecke characters, so the relevant prime conditions become explicit congruences on algebraic integers.  This gives a model for the desired mechanism: convert simultaneous vanishing at nearby primes into an intersection of independent Frobenius conditions.

However, the final Apéry obstruction is not merely missing equidistribution.  The non-CM problem lacks the abelian parameter space on which the Hecke-character argument lives; one needs independence of conjugacy classes in a higher-rank monodromy group.

## (1) Legendre family and the binomial-square sequence

For

\[
E_t:y^2=x(x-1)(x-t),
\]

the Hasse invariant is the Deuring polynomial

\[
H_p(t)=\sum_{d=0}^{(p-1)/2}\binom{2d}{d}^2t^d/16^d.
\]

The coefficient vanishing condition

\[
\binom{2d}{d}\equiv0\pmod p
\]

is completely described by Kummer's theorem.  A carry occurs in adding d+d in base p exactly when some base-p digit of d is at least p/2.

Thus the zero set is not a mysterious Frobenius condition.  It is a union of explicit digit cylinders.

An LGTQ-3 analogue is therefore provable, and in fact much easier than the Apéry case.  If one asks for

\[
p_0,p_1,p_2>\sqrt n,
\qquad
\lfloor n/p_i\rfloor=q,
\]

and requires the three corresponding residues d_i=n-q p_i to lie in Kummer zero sets, the conditions are independent digit restrictions.  For fixed q and logarithmic gaps, the number of admissible triples is bounded by the product of digit densities.

The saving comes from the fact that a random residue has probability about a fixed positive power of p^{-1} of satisfying the necessary carry pattern.  Hence the required o(n/(log n)^3)-type bound follows by elementary digit counting rather than monodromy.

This is a degenerate but clean CM-like example: the Frobenius information has already collapsed into explicit arithmetic.

## (2) Fermat quartic CM example

For the Fermat quartic

\[
x^4+y^4=1,
\]

(or its associated CM elliptic quotient), the Frobenius eigenvalues come from a Hecke character of Q(i):

\[
\alpha_p=\varepsilon(\mathfrak p).
\]

For split primes

\[
p=\pi\bar\pi,
\qquad \pi=a+bi,
\]

one has an explicit formula for the trace.

The proposed condition

\[
\varepsilon(p)\equiv0\pmod p
\]

needs a small correction: a genuine Frobenius eigenvalue is a p-adic unit at good primes, so it is not literally zero modulo p.  The meaningful analogue is usually

\[
a_p\equiv0\pmod p
\]

or a Hasse invariant vanishing condition.

For CM curves this condition becomes a congruence on the Gaussian integer representation of p.  For example, supersingular primes are characterized by the inert/special splitting behavior in the CM field.  Simultaneous conditions at p and p+h can then be attacked by counting lattice points in Gaussian integers satisfying two congruence constraints.

The expected estimate is accessible:

\[
\#\{p,p+h\text{ both exceptional}\}\ll \frac{x}{(\log x)^2}\cdot x^{-\delta}
\]

for suitable fixed h, after translating to prime ideal conditions and applying Chebotarev in compositum fields.

## (3) Weight-2 CM eigenforms

Let

\[
f=\sum a_nq^n
\]

be a CM eigenform.  Then

\[
a_p=\varepsilon(\mathfrak p)+\bar\varepsilon(\mathfrak p).
\]

The condition

\[
a_p\equiv0\pmod p
\]

is a linear relation between a Hecke value and its conjugate.

For a single prime this is a Chebotarev condition in the extension generated by the relevant torsion representation.  For two primes p and p+h, the desired estimate requires independence of the two Frobenius conditions.

For CM forms this independence is available because the Galois representations are induced from characters of an abelian extension.  One studies the compositum of the two relevant ray class fields.  Unless h creates an exceptional algebraic relation, the conditions have codimension two, giving the heuristic

\[
\Pr(a_p\equiv0,
 a_{p+h}\equiv0)=O(p^{-2}),
\]

and certainly the weaker LGTQ-type bound O(1/p).

The important point is that the independence is algebraic: it comes from independence of Hecke characters, not from analytic randomness.

## (4) What breaks in the non-CM case?

The missing ingredient is deeper than ordinary equidistribution.

For CM:

- Frobenius is parameterized by a one-dimensional torus.
- The Galois representation splits after induction from a character.
- Independence reduces to independence of characters.
- Chebotarev applies in explicit abelian extensions.

For non-CM families:

- Frobenius lies in a non-abelian monodromy group.
- There is no Hecke character encoding the trace.
- The condition a_p=0 is a hypersurface condition in a matrix group.
- Correlations between p and p+h require controlling Frobenius in a product of non-abelian representations.

For the Apéry K3 family this is exactly the K3 monodromy obstacle: one needs a statement that the Frobenius classes at nearby primes do not concentrate on special subvarieties.

Thus the missing theorem is closer to a two-prime large sieve / independence theorem for geometric Galois representations than merely an unknown Sato–Tate theorem.

## (5) What CM teaches for Apéry

A successful CM proof gives the correct architecture:

1. Identify the geometric Frobenius condition corresponding to a zero.
2. Translate it into a condition on a parameter space.
3. Prove that two nearby primes impose independent conditions.
4. Convert independence into an anti-concentration estimate.

For CM, step 3 is Hecke-character independence.

For Apéry, the expected replacement is:

- replace the Hecke torus by the K3 monodromy group;
- replace ray class fields by compatible ℓ-adic representations;
- replace character independence by independence of conjugacy classes in the monodromy group.

The CM case therefore supports the proposed LGTQ-3 strategy, but it also identifies the real barrier: Apéry is not hard because Frobenius values are poorly distributed in a one-dimensional parameter. It is hard because the relevant Frobenius variables live in a genuinely non-abelian geometric family.

## Final conclusion

CM families provide a rigorous toy model for LGTQ-3.  The analogue is provable because Hecke characters reduce the problem to explicit arithmetic independence.  The non-CM Apéry case requires the same structural argument at the level of K3 monodromy: a geometric independence theorem for Frobenius conditions in a non-abelian family.
