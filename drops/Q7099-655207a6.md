# Correction of the affine-collision invariant

The proposed invariant based on
\[
C=\gcd(S_0,S_1,\ldots,S_m)
\]
is not valid. The counterexample in the question is decisive:

\[
f=X,\qquad g=X+p.
\]

The affine algebra over \(\mathbf F_p\) is \(\mathbf F_p[X]/(X)\), of length one, and the resultant is
\[
\operatorname{Res}(f,g)=p.
\]
However the first principal subresultant coefficient is a unit (the degree-one Bezout relation is already primitive), hence the gcd of the entire subresultant chain is 1. Thus taking a gcd destroys the valuation information that the resultant carries.

The correct replacement is not a gcd but a degree-stratified determinantal divisor after removing the infinity component.

## Affine presentation matrix

Let
\[
f,g\in\mathbf Z[X],\qquad \deg f=m,\quad \deg g=n.
\]
Introduce the affine Sylvester map
\[
\Phi_{f,g}:\mathbf Z[X]_{<n}\oplus\mathbf Z[X]_{<m}\to \mathbf Z[X]_{<m+n}
\]
by
\[
(A,B)\mapsto Af+Bg.
\]
Its matrix is the Sylvester matrix \(\mathcal S(f,g)\).

The ordinary determinant measures the whole projective intersection, including the point at infinity caused by leading-coefficient vanishing. To remove this contribution, homogenize and saturate by the line at infinity. Equivalently, replace the full Sylvester matrix by the affine Macaulay matrix obtained after the dehomogenized elimination ideal
\[
I^{\rm aff}=(f,g):X_0^\infty\subset \mathbf Z[X].
\]
For a fixed degree cutoff D one obtains a finite presentation matrix
\[
M_D(f,g)
\]
whose maximal minors generate the degree-D affine elimination ideal.

The invariant is the product of the nonzero determinantal divisors in the affine complex:
\[
\Delta_{\rm aff}(f,g)=\prod_D \delta_D,
\]
where \(\delta_D\) is the gcd of all rank-D minors of \(M_D\). This product is prime-independent: the matrices are constructed over \(\mathbf Z\), and no choice depending on p occurs.

## Valuation lemma

Let p be a prime and suppose the affine common-root scheme over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\) contains t distinct reduced points. After base extension to \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\), evaluation at these points gives t independent linear functionals on the cokernel of the affine presentation matrix. Hence
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}(\operatorname{coker}M_D\otimes\mathbf F_p)\ge t
\]
for sufficiently large D.

For an integer matrix M, the product of its determinantal divisors satisfies
\[
 v_p(\Delta(M))
 =\sum_i v_p(d_i),
\]
where d_i are the Smith invariants. The dimension of the mod-p cokernel equals the number of non-unit Smith invariants, and the length of the affine intersection contributes at least one to each reduced point. Therefore
\[
 v_p(\Delta_{\rm aff}(f,g))\ge t.
\]
This is the desired valuation inequality.

The key difference from the failed gcd construction is that determinantal divisors retain the Smith filtration; gcd-ing all subresultants collapses that filtration.

## Tests

### 1. \(f=X, g=X+p\)

The affine presentation is multiplication by p after quotienting by X. The Smith form is
\[
[p].
\]
Therefore
\[
v_p(\Delta_{\rm aff})=1,
\]
matching the single affine root.

There is no infinity contribution.

### 2. \(f=pX+1, g=pX+2\)

The equations have no affine common root modulo p:
\[
1=0,\qquad 2=0
\]
is impossible. The projective homogenizations share the point at infinity because both leading coefficients vanish modulo p. The saturated affine matrix has full rank modulo p, so
\[
v_p(\Delta_{\rm aff})=0.
\]
Thus the degree-drop intersection is correctly ignored.

## Aggregation for Apéry gap polynomials

For
\[
f=N_d(X),\qquad g=N_r(X+d),
\]
with \(d,r\le H<p\), define
\[
A_{d,r}=\Delta_{\rm aff}(N_d,N_r(\cdot+d)).
\]
Then every genuine affine collision modulo p contributes to
\[
v_p(A_{d,r})
\]
with its multiplicity, while primes dividing both leading coefficients only through the infinity component contribute zero.

No prime-dependent selection of minors is required: one fixes all degree strata and takes the product of their determinantal divisors once over \(\mathbf Z\).

## Height

The cost is controlled by the affine Macaulay matrices. The degrees are
\[
\deg N_d=3(d-1),
\]
so for \(d,r\le H\) the matrix size is \(O(H)\). Coefficient heights of Apéry gap polynomials are exponential in H, with
\[
\log \|N_d\|=O(H\log H).
\]
Hadamard bounds for the minors give
\[
\log |A_{d,r}|=O(H^2\log H).
\]
Summing over \(O(H^2)\) pairs gives
\[
\sum_{d,r\le H}\log |A_{d,r}|
 =O(H^4\log H).
\]
Therefore the desired \(H^{3+o(1)}\) total height is not available from the canonical affine determinantal construction alone.

The sharp obstruction is the quadratic matrix size together with quadratic pair count. A bound of order \(H^3\) would require an additional Apéry-specific compression theorem showing that the affine collision modules have a presentation of average size O(1) per pair or that the determinantal divisors factor through lower-degree gap data.

Thus the corrected statement is:

* A canonical prime-independent affine invariant exists: the product of affine determinantal divisors of the saturated Macaulay presentation.
* It satisfies the required valuation inequality.
* It removes infinity-only intersections.
* Its general height is \(O(H^4\log H)\), not \(H^{3+o(1)}\).
* Achieving the stronger GM-LARGE budget requires a new Apéry-specific height reduction, not a generic subresultant argument.
