# Q6759 strip-localization: harmonic toy audit

## Verdict

The fresh reduction is correct: energy is not the right statistic. A dyadic distance strip requires a distance anti-concentration statement, not a stronger global collision count. The harmonic toy gives a clean model showing that the missing input is a Weyl bound for the underlying walk.

The toy chain does not prove the Apery statement, but it isolates the exact analogue needed on the Apery side.

## 1. Energy versus distance strips

Let E(p)=sum_v m_v^2. Counting unordered collision pairs over all distances gives

 total pairs = (E(p)-N)/2.

Therefore even an optimal energy estimate cannot prevent one mesoscopic strip from carrying a positive fraction of all collisions.

For D=sqrt(N)L, the target

 |{(r,r+d): D/2 < d <= D}| << D^(2-eta)

is a statement about the distance coordinate. It is a Fourier anti-concentration statement in the difference variable.

This uses the supplied energy identity and the distinction between P_D and total energy.

## 2. Harmonic toy reflection law

Let H_r=sum_{i=1}^r 1/i mod p.

Using

 1/(p-i) = -1/i mod p,

we get

 H_{p-1-r} = H_{p-1} - sum_{i=p-r}^{p-1}1/i.

Since Wolstenholme gives H_{p-1}=0 mod p,

 H_{p-1-r}=H_r mod p.

Hence the harmonic walk is reflection symmetric.

Consequences:

- If H_{r+d}=H_r, then the reflected pair also collides.
- Reflection couples position and distance, but it does not force a concentration of d into one dyadic strip.

The reflection creates paired geometry, not the required anti-concentration.

## 3. Fixed distance exponential sum

For the toy collision count

 C_d = #{r:H_{r+d}-H_r=0},

orthogonality gives

 C_d = (1/p)sum_t S_d(t),

where

 S_d(t)=sum_r e_p(t sum_{i=1}^d 1/(r+i)).

For t != 0, the phase is the rational function

 f_d(r)=sum_{i=1}^d 1/(r+i).

It has d simple poles. Bombieri-Weil type bounds for rational exponential sums give the shape

 |S_d(t)| <= O(d sqrt(p)).

This is applicable because f_d is not of the form g^p-g plus a constant and its conductor is controlled by the number of poles.

But inserting this into orthogonality gives only

 C_d <= main term + O(d sqrt(p)),

which is weaker than the trivial degree count for the relevant range.

So fixed-d Weil is the wrong direction.

## 4. Dyadic averaging and the bilinear collapse

The strip sum is different.

Sum_{D/2<d<=D} C_d

counts pairs (r,u) with u-r in the strip and

 H_u-H_r=0.

Thus the exponential contribution becomes

 sum_t sum_{r,u:u-r in strip} e_p(tH_u)e_p(-tH_r).

The phase separates.

The only obstruction is the strip indicator.

After smoothing the indicator by additive Fourier expansion, the problem reduces to weighted sums of the form

 sum_t |Sigma_t|^2,

where

 Sigma_t=sum_r w(r)e_p(tH_r).

Therefore a bound

 |Sigma_t| <= p^(1-delta)

uniformly for t != 0 would give power saving for strip collisions.

The exact exponent depends on the smoothing loss, but any fixed delta>0 is qualitatively enough.

## 5. Known input status

The relevant object is the harmonic-walk Weyl sum

 Sigma_t=sum_r e_p(tH_r).

This is not automatically identical to the classical Fermat quotient sums. There are congruence relations between harmonic sums and Fermat-quotient expressions in special ranges, but a direct transfer of Heath-Brown/Ostafe-Shparlinski style bounds requires an explicit transformation.

Therefore the safe statement is:

- Fermat quotient Heilbronn-type estimates suggest the correct technology.
- They do not immediately supply a theorem for the full harmonic walk H_r without an additional bridge.

## 6. Apery-side translation

The exact analogue needed is a Weyl bound for the projective Apery walk.

Let theta_t(r) be a nontrivial additive character of the collision coordinate or an appropriate trace coordinate on P^1. The missing theorem should have the form:

 For every nontrivial frequency t,

 |sum_{r in window} e_p(t * AperyPhase(r))| <= p^(1-delta).

Then the same bilinear strip argument would convert it into distance anti-concentration:

 |{(r,r+d):D/2<d<=D, xi_{r+d}=xi_r}| <= D^(2-eta).

This is the precise Apery-side analogue of the harmonic toy.

## 7. What this says about the current wall

The conclusion agrees with the Strike2 diagnosis: the missing object is not another energy estimate. It is a quenched distance-distribution theorem.

Run rigidity, fixed-height Weil, and degree budgets attack individual distances. The strip theorem requires cancellation after summing many distances.

A successful route must create a separated two-variable phase, just as the harmonic toy does.

## Final answers

(i) Least confident step:

The least confident step is the claim that a known Fermat-quotient/Heilbronn technology can be transferred to the full harmonic walk H_r. The bilinear reduction is formal; the needed Weyl estimate is the hard external input.

(ii) Blind spot in the question:

The question may still be looking too much at collision geometry and not enough at the distance Fourier transform. Even a perfect description of fibers, triangles, or fixed-gap zero sets does not control where the gaps land. The missing invariant is a strip-level Fourier statistic of the gap distribution.
