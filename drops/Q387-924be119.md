# Structural repulsion in CRT pair correlations

## Executive verdict

The proposed `|Y_p|=O(1)` argument does **not** follow from CRT structure, and the attempted reduction to the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions is based on the wrong congruence variable.

The exact conclusions are:

1. For
   \[
   \varepsilon_{pq}
   =\#\{n\in I:n\bmod p\in Z_p,\ n\bmod q\in Z_q\}
    -\frac{|I|z_pz_q}{pq},
   \qquad p\ne q,
   \]
   and
   \[
   Y_p=\sum_{q\ne p}\varepsilon_{pq},
   \]
   the identity
   \[
   \sum_pY_p=2\sum_{p<q}\varepsilon_{pq}
   \]
   is correct. Thus a uniform bound `|Y_p|=O(1)` would indeed imply
   \[
   \sum_{p<q}\varepsilon_{pq}=O(N/\log N)=o(N).
   \]
   It is a valid **sufficient hypothesis**.

2. That hypothesis is far stronger than CRT predicts. In the independent random-zero-set model,
   \[
   \operatorname{Var}(Y_p)\asymp \frac Np,
   \]
   up to the bounded density factor `\lambda_N`. Hence the natural size is
   \[
   |Y_p|\asymp \sqrt{N/p}.
   \]
   For `p\asymp\sqrt N`, this is `N^{1/4}`, not `O(1)`.

3. The negative shared-prime term in the computation is not itself a proof of uniform repulsion. Algebraically it says that the vector of row sums of the error matrix has relatively small `L^2` norm. With the convention used in the table, the reported numbers imply
   \[
   \frac1N\sum_pY_p^2
   =2(0.169)-0.023
   =0.315.
   \]
   This is an excellent random-scale observation, but it is an `L^2` statement, not a pointwise `O(1)` statement.

4. The correct theorem suggested by the data is the **star-energy bound**
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sup_{|I|=N}
   \sum_{p}
   \left|\sum_{q\ne p}\varepsilon_{pq}(I)\right|^2
   \ll N.}
   \tag{STAR-L2}
   \]
   Since there are `\asymp N/\log N` relevant primes, Cauchy--Schwarz then gives
   \[
   \left|\sum_{p<q}\varepsilon_{pq}(I)\right|
   \ll \frac{N}{\sqrt{\log N}}
   =o(N).
   \]
   More generally, `o(N\log N)` on the left of `(STAR-L2)` is sufficient.

5. Proving `(STAR-L2)` is still a genuinely new Apéry-specific cross-prime theorem. When expanded, it is exactly a three-prime shared-anchor correlation. It is not supplied by Siegel--Walfisz, CRT, reflection, or the endpoint companion-matrix identity.

The observed negative covariance is therefore a valuable clue, but the proposed one-line PNT-in-progressions proof does not work.

# 1. Normalize the deterministic error decomposition

Let
\[
\mathcal P_N=\{p\text{ prime}:\sqrt N<p\le N\}
\]
and, to avoid endpoint notation, take an interval `I=[0,N)` of length `N`. Put
\[
f_p(n)=\mathbf 1_{\{n\bmod p\in Z_p\}},
\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{z_p}{p}.
\]
For distinct primes define
\[
A_{pq}(I)=\sum_{n\in I}f_p(n)f_q(n),
\qquad
\varepsilon_{pq}(I)=A_{pq}(I)-N\rho_p\rho_q.
\tag{1.1}
\]
Because `pq>N`, every pair `(a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q` has at most one CRT representative in `I`.

Write
\[
S(I)=\sum_{p<q}\varepsilon_{pq}(I),
\qquad
Y_p(I)=\sum_{q\ne p}\varepsilon_{pq}(I),
\tag{1.2}
\]
where the error matrix is regarded as symmetric with zero diagonal. Then
\[
\boxed{\sum_pY_p(I)=2S(I).}
\tag{1.3}
\]
Thus the last implication in the question is algebraically sound, modulo the factor `2` caused by unordered versus ordered pairs.

There is a second exact identity that explains the reported negative shared-prime contribution. Let
\[
A=\sum_{p<q}\varepsilon_{pq}^2,
\]
and let `C_1` denote the contribution to `S^2` from two distinct edges sharing exactly one prime, including the factor `2` from expanding the square. Then
\[
\boxed{
\sum_pY_p^2=2A+C_1.}
\tag{1.4}
\]
Indeed,
\[
Y_p^2
=\sum_{q\ne p}\varepsilon_{pq}^2
 +2\sum_{q<r\atop q,r\ne p}
   \varepsilon_{pq}\varepsilon_{pr},
\]
and summing over `p` counts every square twice and every shared-vertex edge pair once with coefficient `2`.

If `C_0` is the disjoint-edge contribution, then
\[
S^2=A+C_1+C_0.
\tag{1.5}
\]
So a negative `C_1` is equivalent to partial cancellation between the self energy and the shared-prime cross terms. It is **not** a separate probabilistic mechanism until a theorem bounding the row sums `Y_p` is proved.

## 1.1 What the reported numbers actually imply

Using the table's convention,
\[
A/N=0.169,
\qquad
C_1/N=-0.023.
\]
Equation (1.4) gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac1N\sum_pY_p^2=0.315.}
\tag{1.6}
\]
At `N=5000`, the number of primes in `(\sqrt N,N]` is about `650`, so (1.6) corresponds to an RMS row sum around `1.5`. That is small at this finite scale, but (1.6) does not imply an absolute bound as `N\to\infty`. Since the number of rows of the error matrix is `\asymp N/\log N`, an `O(N)` star energy permits average
\[
Y_p^2\asymp\log N,
\]
and hence an RMS size of order `\sqrt{\log N}`.

This distinction is important: the computation supports `(STAR-L2)`, not necessarily `\sup_p|Y_p|=O(1)`.

Also, unless an averaging measure over intervals or zero sets has been specified, the table is formally a decomposition of a deterministic square, not a covariance decomposition in the probabilistic sense. The terminology is harmless, but the algebraic identity (1.4) is the load-bearing fact.

# 2. The exact fixed-`p` formula

The key quantity can be written without any heuristic.

For `a\in Z_p`, define the number of representatives of the class `a mod p` in `[0,N)` by
\[
L_p(a)
=\#\{j\ge0:a+jp<N\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Since `p>\sqrt N`,
\[
L_p(a)=\frac Np+O(1)
\quad\text{and}\quad
L_p(a)<q
\qquad(q\in\mathcal P_N).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Fix `a\in Z_p` and `b\in Z_q`. The CRT representative congruent to `a mod p` is
\[
c=a+jp,
\]
where `j\in[0,q)` is uniquely determined by
\[
j\equiv (b-a)p^{-1}\pmod q.
\tag{2.3}
\]
It lies in `[0,N)` exactly when `0\le j<L_p(a)`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
A_{pq}([0,N))
=\sum_{a\in Z_p}
  \sum_{0\le j<L_p(a)}
  \mathbf 1_{Z_q}\bigl((a+jp)\bmod q\bigr).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Define the cross-prime row load with `p` removed by
\[
K_{-p}(n)
=\sum_{q\in\mathcal P_N\setminus\{p\}}
  \mathbf 1_{Z_q}(n\bmod q),
\tag{2.5}
\]
and put
\[
\lambda_{-p}
=\sum_{q\ne p}\frac{z_q}{q}.
\tag{2.6}
\]
Summing (2.4) over `q` gives the exact identity
\[
\sum_{q\ne p}A_{pq}
=\sum_{a\in Z_p}
  \sum_{0\le j<L_p(a)}
  K_{-p}(a+jp).
\tag{2.7}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
Y_p
&=\sum_{a\in Z_p}
  \sum_{0\le j<L_p(a)}
  \bigl(K_{-p}(a+jp)-\lambda_{-p}\bigr)\\
&\quad+
\lambda_{-p}
\left(\sum_{a\in Z_p}L_p(a)-\frac{Nz_p}{p}\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.8}
\]
The last line has absolute value at most
\[
z_p\lambda_{-p},
\tag{2.9}
\]
because every `L_p(a)` differs from `N/p` by less than one.

Equation (2.8) is the correct interpretation of `Y_p`:

> `Y_p` is the discrepancy of the full cross-prime incidence load `K_{-p}` when sampled along the short union of progressions
> \[
> a,\ a+p,\ a+2p,\ldots
> \qquad(a\in Z_p).
> \]

It is a **one-prime Palm discrepancy**. Conditioning on a `p`-hit, it asks whether the remaining primes extend that hit with the expected frequency.

This is already a cross-prime problem. CRT merely supplies (2.8); it does not bound its right-hand side.

## 2.1 Singleton notation

If `Z_p={a_p}` and, for simplicity, every `Z_q={a_q}`, put
\[
L_p=L_p(a_p).
\]
Then the number of actual pair hits adjacent to `p` is
\[
H_p
=\#\left\{q:\
((a_q-a_p)p^{-1}\bmod q)<L_p
\right\}.
\tag{2.10}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
H_p
=\sum_{0\le j<L_p}
  \#\{q:(a_p+jp)\bmod q=a_q\}.}
\tag{2.11}
\]
The expected main term is
\[
L_p\sum_q\frac1q
=\frac Np(\log2+o(1)),
\tag{2.12}
\]
not a prime-counting main term `Li(N)/(p-1)`.

# 3. Why this is not a PNT in arithmetic progressions

The proposed congruence
\[
q\equiv c_p\pmod p
\]
does not arise from the CRT hit condition.

For a hit one needs, for some `0\le j<L_p`,
\[
a_q\equiv a_p+jp\pmod q.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Here:

- `q` is the modulus of the congruence, not the residue being tested modulo `p`;
- `a_q` changes with `q`;
- the inverse `p^{-1} mod q` changes with `q`;
- there are `L_p\asymp N/p` possible values of `j`.

Thus (3.1) is a moving divisor/inverse condition. It is not one fixed residue class of primes modulo `p`.

This can be seen particularly clearly from the Fourier expansion. Let
\[
S_q(u)=\sum_{b\in Z_q}e_q(ub)
\]
and
\[
\widehat{1_{L}}(h)=\sum_{0\le j<L}e_q(-hj).
\]
For singleton `a_p` one obtains
\[
\boxed{
A_{pq}-\frac{L_pz_q}{q}
=\frac1q
 \sum_{h\in\mathbb F_q^\times}
 \widehat{1_{L_p}}(h)
 e_q(-ha_pp^{-1})
 S_q(hp^{-1}).}
\tag{3.2}
\]
The relevant phase is an incomplete reciprocal phase involving `p^{-1} mod q` and the Apéry zero-set transform `S_q`. Summing (3.2) over `q` is a Kloosterman/trace-function-shaped dispersion problem, not Siegel--Walfisz.

## 3.1 The quantifier problem with Siegel--Walfisz

Even if the condition were a fixed progression, the quoted use of Siegel--Walfisz would still be invalid in this range.

The uniform Siegel--Walfisz theorem treats moduli
\[
p\le (\log N)^A
\]
for fixed `A`. Here
\[
p>\sqrt N.
\]
Saying “for fixed `p`” does not repair this: a truly fixed prime eventually lies below `\sqrt N` and leaves the relevant range. The asymptotic problem requires `p=p(N)` growing at least as fast as `\sqrt N`.

Bombieri--Vinogradov also does not help directly. Its classical range ends just below the square-root barrier on average over moduli, while the present moduli begin at that barrier. More decisively, it concerns ordinary residue classes of primes, whereas (3.1) contains the moving coefficient zero `a_q` and the inverse `p^{-1} mod q`.

Even a hypothetical very strong PNT for large moduli would not by itself control (3.2).

# 4. The random model rejects the `O(1)` scale

Suppose, as a benchmark, that the sets `Z_q` are chosen independently and uniformly with prescribed cardinalities.

Fix `p` and one residue `a_p`. The `L_p` residues
\[
a_p,\ a_p+p,\ldots,a_p+(L_p-1)p
\pmod q
\]
are distinct because `L_p<q`. If `Z_q` is a uniformly random `z_q`-subset of `\mathbb F_q`, then `A_{pq}` has a hypergeometric distribution with
\[
\mathbb E(A_{pq}\mid a_p)
=\frac{L_pz_q}{q}
\tag{4.1}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Var}(A_{pq}\mid a_p)
=\frac{L_pz_q}{q}
 \left(1-\frac{L_p}{q}\right)
 \frac{q-z_q}{q-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Different `q`-columns are independent in this model. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y_p\mid a_p)
\asymp
L_p\sum_q\frac{z_q}{q}
\asymp
\frac Np\lambda_N,
\tag{4.3}
\]
where
\[
\lambda_N=\sum_{\sqrt N<q\le N}\frac{z_q}{q}.
\]
Under the observed mean-one law, `\lambda_N\asymp\log2`.

Therefore the random scale is
\[
\boxed{
|Y_p|\asymp\sqrt{N/p}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]
In particular:

- `p\asymp N` gives `|Y_p|=O(1)`;
- `p\asymp\sqrt N` gives `|Y_p|\asymp N^{1/4}`.

The conditional mean differs from the main term `Nz_pz_q/(pq)` by only an endpoint error of total size `O(1)`, but the **fluctuation** is much larger. Confusing mean error with random deviation is the source of the proposed constant bound.

Summing the random variances over `p` gives
\[
\mathbb E\sum_pY_p^2
\asymp
N\left(\sum_p\frac{z_p}{p}\right)^2
\asymp N.
\tag{4.5}
\]
This is precisely the scale observed in (1.6).

So the empirical discovery is consistent with random-scale star energy. It is not evidence that every `Y_p` remains bounded.

## 4.1 A plausible pointwise replacement

A much more natural fixed-`p` theorem would be
\[
|Y_p|
\ll
\sqrt{\frac Np}\,(\log N)^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Ignoring the harmless zero-count weights, this would already be sufficient because
\[
\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le N}\sqrt{\frac Np}
\asymp
\frac{N}{\log N}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Thus square-root cancellation in each `p`-star, rather than an absolute constant, is the correct individual benchmark.

The aggregate `(STAR-L2)` formulation is safer because it permits exceptional primes and naturally incorporates varying `z_p`.

# 5. The singleton Apéry case is actually central

There is an additional Apéry-specific correction to the premise `z_p=1` with an arbitrary zero `a_p`.

Reflection gives
\[
r\in Z_p
\Longleftrightarrow
p-1-r\in Z_p.
\]
Therefore, if `z_p=1`, the unique zero must be the fixed point
\[
\boxed{a_p=\frac{p-1}{2}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

If also `z_q=1`, then
\[
a_q=\frac{q-1}{2},
\]
and their CRT representative is not random at all:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}
\left(\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{q-1}{2}\right)
=\frac{pq-1}{2}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]
For the prefix `[0,N)`, this point is counted exactly when
\[
pq\le 2N.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Thus the singleton--singleton contribution for fixed `p` is a count of central-zero primes `q` below the hyperbolic cutoff `2N/p`, not a count of primes in a residue class modulo `p`.

Ahlgren--Ono identifies the central-zero condition through
\[
b_{(q-1)/2}\equiv a_q(f_8)\pmod{q^2}.
\]
Hence centrality is governed by the diagonal divisibility event
\[
q\mid a_q(f_8).
\]
No ordinary Chebotarev or Siegel--Walfisz theorem gives a prime number theorem for that same-characteristic event. Moreover, this controls only the central part; the bulk of the computed zero sets consists of noncentral reflected pairs.

So even the one case where the location `a_q` has an explicit formula does not lead to the proposed AP argument.

# 6. CRT alone allows the opposite behavior

The reflected anchored-star construction from the earlier Q335/Q354 audits directly refutes a CRT-only bound on `Y_p`.

Fix a large `N` and a block of primes
\[
\mathcal Q_N
=\left\{p:\frac{3N}{4}<p<\frac{7N}{8}\right\}.
\]
For each such prime put
\[
a_p=N-p.
\]
Then
\[
N\equiv a_p\pmod p.
\]
For distinct `p,q\in\mathcal Q_N`, one has `N<pq`, so
\[
\operatorname{CRT}_{p,q}(a_p,a_q)=N.
\tag{6.1}
\]
If `Z_p={a_p}`, then
\[
\varepsilon_{pq}([0,N+1))
=1-\frac{N+1}{pq}
=1+O(1/N).
\]
Consequently, if `L=|\mathcal Q_N|`,
\[
Y_p=(L-1)(1+o(1))
\asymp\frac N{\log N}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Adding the reflected mate
\[
p-1-a_p
\]
keeps the same anchor contribution and preserves reflection. By choosing rapidly separated scales `N_j`, these sets can be defined once and for all, so the final countermodel is a universal family `Z_p` depending only on `p`.

Therefore:

- CRT does not force negative shared covariance;
- CRT permits huge **positive** shared covariance;
- reflection does not repair this;
- the small Apéry star energy, if true asymptotically, must come from arithmetic of the common Apéry sequence.

# 7. The correct formalization of the observed repulsion

Let `E_N(I)` be the symmetric error matrix
\[
(E_N(I))_{p,q}
=\begin{cases}
\varepsilon_{pq}(I),&p\ne q,\\
0,&p=q.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
Y(I)=E_N(I)\mathbf1,
\]
and
\[
2S(I)=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}E_N(I)\mathbf1.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The proposed pointwise hypothesis is
\[
\|E_N(I)\mathbf1\|_\infty=O(1).
\tag{7.2}
\]
It is sufficient, but unnecessarily strong.

The data directly suggest instead
\[
\boxed{
\|E_N(I)\mathbf1\|_2^2\ll N.}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
|S(I)|
&=\frac12
 \left|\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}E_N(I)\mathbf1\right|\\
&\le\frac12
 \|\mathbf1\|_2
 \|E_N(I)\mathbf1\|_2\\
&\ll
\sqrt{\frac N{\log N}}\sqrt N
=\frac{N}{\sqrt{\log N}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.4}
\]
This is already `o(N)`.

The minimal scale condition obtainable from this argument is
\[
\sum_pY_p(I)^2=o(N\log N),
\tag{7.5}
\]
while the random and empirical scale is the stronger `O(N)`.

## 7.1 Relation to the negative shared term

By (1.4), `(STAR-L2)` is equivalently
\[
2\sum_{p<q}\varepsilon_{pq}^2
+C_1
\ll N.
\tag{7.6}
\]
Thus the shared-prime cross terms need not individually be negative. What matters is that they cancel any excess of the self energy sufficiently to leave an `O(N)` row-sum square.

This is the rigorous version of the “balance” intuition in the question. But the balance must be **proved in aggregate**; it is not forced by the definition of the errors.

## 7.2 What must be proved arithmetically

Expanding `(STAR-L2)` gives
\[
\sum_p
\sum_{q\ne p}
\sum_{r\ne p}
\varepsilon_{pq}(I)\varepsilon_{pr}(I).
\tag{7.7}
\]
This is a shared-anchor three-prime correlation. Through (2.8), it is the variance over `p` of the discrepancy of the residual load `K_{-p}` along the `p`-zero progressions.

It is therefore closely related to the pair-Palm/extension quantities in

```text
problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex
problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex
```

and to the mixed inverse Fourier sums isolated in the dispersion analyses. It is not a theorem about ordinary primes in progressions.

A plausible analytic route is to insert (3.2), expand the square over `q,r`, and seek a mixed-characteristic sum-of-products estimate for
\[
S_q(hp^{-1})\overline{S_r(kp^{-1})}
\]
after averaging over the common anchor prime `p`. This is exactly where the Apéry Cartier/Mellin structure could matter. No such estimate is currently banked in the repository.

# 8. Direct answers to the four questions

## 8.1 Is `|Y_p|=O(1)` provable from CRT?

**No.** The anchored-star family gives
\[
Y_p\asymp N/\log N.
\]
The negative shared-prime term is not a universal CRT phenomenon.

For actual Apéry sets, `|Y_p|=O(1)` would be a major new cross-prime theorem. It is stronger than the natural random scale and is not implied by the companion endpoint identity, reflection, Lucas, or current gap-polynomial results.

## 8.2 How many `q` have a small CRT representative?

For singleton zero sets the exact count is
\[
H_p
=\#\left\{q:
((a_q-a_p)p^{-1}\bmod q)<L_p
\right\},
\qquad L_p\asymp N/p.
\]
Its density-model mean is
\[
\frac Np\sum_q\frac1q
=\frac Np(\log2+o(1)).
\]
The natural fluctuation is
\[
\asymp\sqrt{N/p},
\]
not `O(1)`.

The correct theorem to seek is either square-root cancellation for every `p`, or the aggregate star-energy estimate `(STAR-L2)`.

## 8.3 Is this a PNT in arithmetic progressions?

**No.** The condition is
\[
a_q\equiv a_p+jp\pmod q
\]
for some short `j`, with `a_q` changing with `q`. It is an incomplete modular-inverse/Apéry-zero condition. There is no fixed class `q\equiv c_p mod p`.

Furthermore, `p>\sqrt N` lies far outside the uniform Siegel--Walfisz range. Treating `p` as fixed changes the quantifiers and removes it from the relevant prime range.

## 8.4 Would a constant bound finish the proof?

**Yes, as a sufficient hypothesis.** With unordered pairs,
\[
2\sum_{p<q}\varepsilon_{pq}=\sum_pY_p.
\]
Thus `|Y_p|\le C` uniformly gives
\[
\left|\sum_{p<q}\varepsilon_{pq}\right|
\ll N/\log N=o(N).
\]
Together with the bounded first-density term, this yields the desired `O(N)` row second moment.

But this is not the right expected theorem. The substantially weaker and empirically matched condition
\[
\sum_pY_p^2\ll N
\]
already finishes the argument by Cauchy--Schwarz.

For the short-interval theorem stated with a supremum over all intervals `I`, either condition must hold uniformly in the interval location. A proof only for `[0,N)` would not automatically give the full translated statement.

# 9. Recommended next target

The discovery should be recorded as the following precise conjecture rather than as a putative Siegel--Walfisz corollary.

## Conjecture STAR-L2 (shared-prime repulsion)

For every interval `I` of length `N`, let
\[
\varepsilon_{pq}(I)
=\#\{n\in I:n\bmod p\in Z_p,\ n\bmod q\in Z_q\}
-\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq},
\]
where `\sqrt N<p,q\le N`. Then
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\sqrt N<p\le N}
\left(
 \sum_{q\ne p}\varepsilon_{pq}(I)
\right)^2
\ll N.}
\tag{STAR-L2}
\]

This conjecture:

- exactly captures the observed negative shared-prime correction;
- is false for the reflected anchored star;
- has the random-model scale `N`;
- implies the required signed pair dispersion with a factor `\sqrt{\log N}` to spare;
- is weaker than a full operator-norm theorem for the pair-error matrix;
- is stronger than the scalar signed sum alone;
- exposes the missing arithmetic as a shared-anchor three-prime correlation.

A useful computational diagnostic is to record, for increasing `N`,
\[
\frac1N\sum_pY_p^2,
\qquad
\max_p\frac{|Y_p|}{\sqrt{N/p}},
\]
and the same quantities in dyadic `p`-ranges. The current `N=5000` decomposition already gives the first ratio indirectly as approximately `0.315`.

## Final conclusion

The negative shared-prime term is a real and promising empirical phenomenon. Its correct mathematical content is
\[
\|E_N(I)\mathbf1\|_2^2\asymp N,
\]
not `\|E_N(I)\mathbf1\|_\infty=O(1)`.

CRT supplies the exact progression identity (2.8), but no cancellation. Siegel--Walfisz is inapplicable because the hit condition is not a fixed arithmetic progression and the anchor modulus lies above the square-root scale. The remaining theorem is an Apéry-specific shared-anchor dispersion estimate, with `(STAR-L2)` as the cleanest formulation suggested by the new data.
