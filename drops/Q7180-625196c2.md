# P32 empirical route selection: random-fiber heuristic for qualified short chains

## Executive summary

The observed data (only 4 reflection-orbit chains through `p <= 50000`, quotient maximum `\bar C=1`) is consistent with the random-fiber model. The correct heuristic is not based on independent starts: the objects are **consecutive four-occurrence windows** in the zero list, after carrier-clean filtering, non-AP filtering, off-center filtering, and reflection quotienting.

Let `\bar M_p` be the number of surviving reflection classes of qualified short chains in `P^1(F_p)`. The natural model is:

\[
\bar M_p \asymp \kappa H^3/p
\]

for `H=\sqrt p`, hence

\[
\bar M_p\asymp \kappa p^{1/2}.
\]

Distributing these chains among `p+1` projective states gives occupancy mean

\[
\lambda_p=\frac{\bar M_p}{p+1}\asymp \kappa p^{-1/2}.
\]

Therefore a collision in one state is rare for one prime. Summed over primes up to `P`, the first quotient collision is predicted when

\[
\sum_{p\le P}(p+1)\Pr(C_p\ge2)
\asymp 1,
\]

which occurs at a polynomial scale around `P^{1/2}` times the chain constant; with realistic constants the first collision is expected only at very large `P`. The theorem-level statement remains only that the current scans found no collision.

---

# 1. Object being modeled

A qualified chain is not an arbitrary gap triple. It is a window

\[
(r_i,r_{i+1},r_{i+2},r_{i+3})
\]

in the ordered occurrence list of a fixed projective state.

The filters are:

1. three consecutive returns;
2. span
\[
(r_{i+3}-r_i)\le H=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor;
\]
3. carrier-clean: no shorter carrier interval contaminates the witness;
4. non-all-equal gap vector;
5. non-AP;
6. off-center (not the reflection-fixed centered case);
7. quotient by reflection.

These filters substantially reduce the naive `H^3` gap count.

---

# 2. Random-fiber model

## 2.1 Number of candidate chains

A generic ordered gap triple satisfies

\[
a,b,c\ge1,\qquad a+b+c\le H.
\]

The number is

\[
\binom{H+2}{3}=\frac{H^3}{6}+O(H^2).
\]

The Apéry projective state condition is a codimension-one condition in `P^1(F_p)`. Treating the endpoint state as random gives probability approximately

\[
\frac1{p+1}.
\]

Reflection quotient contributes a factor `1/2`.

Hence the raw heuristic is

\[
\bar M_p\approx
\eta
\frac{H^3}{12(p+1)},
\]

where `\eta` absorbs:

- carrier-clean probability;
- removal of AP triples;
- off-center survival;
- actual recurrence correlations.

For `H=\sqrt p`:

\[
\boxed{\bar M_p\approx \frac{\eta}{12}\sqrt p.}
\]

This is the relevant scale, not `p` or `pH^3`.

---

# 3. State occupancy distribution

Let

\[
C_p(q)=\#\{\text{qualified chains in state }q\}.
\]

Conditional on `\bar M_p`, the random-fiber model gives a multinomial distribution over `p+1` states.

For a fixed state:

\[
C_p(q)\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_p),
\]

with

\[
\lambda_p=\frac{\bar M_p}{p+1}
\approx \frac{\eta}{12\sqrt p}.
\]

Thus

\[
\Pr(C_p(q)\ge2)
=1-e^{-\lambda_p}(1+\lambda_p)
\sim \frac{\lambda_p^2}{2}.
\]

The expected number of collision states for one prime is therefore

\[
(p+1)\Pr(C_p(q)\ge2)
\sim \frac{\bar M_p^2}{2(p+1)}.
\]

Substituting the model:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E N_{\rm coll}(p)
\asymp \frac{\eta^2}{288}
}
\]

per prime.

This predicts a constant expected number of colliding states per prime only if the constant hidden in `\eta` is large. The empirical observation that collisions are absent through `50000` indicates `\eta` is small or that arithmetic repulsion is significant.

---

# 4. First state with quotient multiplicity >=2

For a fixed prime:

\[
\max_q \bar C_p(q)\ge2
\]

is approximately a birthday event.

The threshold is

\[
\bar M_p\approx \sqrt{p}.
\]

But with the short-window scaling

\[
\bar M_p\approx \frac{\eta}{12}\sqrt p,
\]

we sit below the generic birthday threshold by the constant factor `\eta/12`.

Therefore:

- if `\eta` is genuinely small, uniqueness can persist for many primes;
- if the fibers have only mild repulsion, eventual failure is expected.

Summing over primes:

\[
\sum_{p\le P}\Pr(\exists q:C_p(q)\ge2)
\approx
\frac{\eta^2}{288}\pi(P).
\]

Thus the first failure scale is roughly

\[
\pi(P)\asymp 288/\eta^2.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{P\text{ is around }\exp(O(1/\eta^2)).}
\]

Without estimating `\eta`, no sharper numerical prediction is justified.

---

# 5. Interpretation of the observed scans

The scan through `p<=50000` finding only four reflection-orbit chains and

\[
\max_q \bar C_p(q)=1
\]

means:

1. `\bar M_p` is much smaller than the raw `H^3/(12p)` estimate;
2. qualified chains are strongly correlated with special states;
3. reflection quotient removes the deterministic duplicate but does not explain the scarcity.

The empirical effective constant is closer to a sparse model

\[
\bar M_p=o(\sqrt p)
\]

on currently reachable primes.

---

# 6. p=500000 expectation

For `p=500000`:

\[
H\approx707.
\]

The generic model gives

\[
\bar M_p\approx \frac{\eta}{12}\cdot707.
\]

Even with `\eta=1`, this is only about 59 reflection classes. The mean occupancy is

\[
59/500001\approx1.2\times10^{-4}.
\]

A collision is still unlikely for a single prime.

Therefore a scan to `500000` is a meaningful test of the constant but not a decisive test of eventual uniqueness.

---

# 7. Theorem versus heuristic

## Proven from the scan

- Exact enumeration gives the observed values for scanned primes.
- Consecutive-window counting is the correct combinatorial object.
- Reflection quotient removes the forced partner.

## Not proved

The following are heuristics:

\[
\bar M_p\sim \eta H^3/(12p),
\]

Poisson state occupancy, and the predicted first collision scale.

A proof of pointwise uniqueness would require a deterministic theorem controlling projective fibers:

\[
C_p(q)\le1
\]

for every qualified chain family.

The random model instead predicts eventual failure unless an additional arithmetic rigidity mechanism exists.

---

# Final conclusion

Pointwise quotient uniqueness is **not the natural infinite prediction**. The correct heuristic is:

\[
\boxed{
\bar M_p\ll\sqrt p,
\qquad
\lambda_p\ll p^{-1/2},
\qquad
\text{collisions are rare but eventually expected.}
}
\]

For the P32 proof strategy this is favorable: variance control does not require absolute uniqueness. It requires only a sufficiently small collision energy

\[
\sum_q C_p(q)^2,
\]

and the random-fiber model predicts this remains close to the diagonal term.

The observed `max C=1` should therefore be viewed as evidence for very low collision energy, not evidence for a permanent uniqueness theorem.
