# Transfer-matrix formulation and collision bounds

## 1. Transfer matrix encoding

Write the Apéry continuant recurrence in the standard second-order form
\[
 u_{n+1}=A_n u_n+B_n u_{n-1}.
\]
The corresponding transfer matrix is
\[
 T_n(x)=\begin{pmatrix}A_n(x)&B_n(x)\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
\binom{u_{n+1}}{u_n}=T_n(x)\binom{u_n}{u_{n-1}}.
\]
For the continuant polynomial \(N_h\), with the fixed initial vector \(v_0\), there is a vector functional \(\ell\) such that
\[
N_h(x)=\ell\,T_{h-1}(x)\cdots T_0(x)v_0.
\]
Therefore the collision
\[
N_d(r)=N_e(r+d)=0\pmod p
\]
is exactly
\[
\ell T_{d-1}(r)\cdots T_0(r)v_0=0,
\]
\[
\ell T_{e-1}(r+d)\cdots T_0(r+d)v_0=0
\]
over \(\mathbf F_p\).

Equivalently, the projective vector \(v_0\) is sent by two matrix products into the kernel line of \(\ell\). The zero collision is an intersection of two projective transport conditions.

## 2. Does multiplicity 3 force an algebraic identity?

In general, no. The transfer-matrix representation alone is insufficient.

A product of matrices in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf F_p)\) can have many different words mapping the same starting line to the same target line. This is a projective semigroup phenomenon, not an algebraic dependence phenomenon.

A counterexample: take a finite field \(\mathbf F_q\) and a fixed matrix
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every power fixes the unique eigenline \([1:0]\) in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_q)\). Choosing continuant-like words \(M^a\), \(M^b\), \(M^c\) gives arbitrarily many distinct labels sharing the same left/right projective condition. No low-degree polynomial identity over \(\mathbf Z\) is forced.

Likewise, three shifted pairs can share roots because a projective orbit may return periodically. The obstruction is not necessarily a repeated resultant factor.

Therefore the desired statement is false for arbitrary second-order recurrences.

## 3. What is special about Apéry matrices?

The needed extra input is not merely nonzero resultants over \(\mathbf Z\). One needs a rigidity statement preventing modular projective orbit collapse.

For Apéry matrices the candidates are:

1. **Non-scalar monodromy rigidity.** Show that all relevant products
\[
M_h(x)=T_{h-1}(x)\cdots T_0(x)
\]
have bounded exceptional primes where their projective action becomes degenerate. Outside those primes, three collisions would imply a determinant/discriminant polynomial vanishes.

2. **No short projective periods.** A triple collision would give three transport words with
\[
M_i(r)[v_0]=M_j(r)[v_0]=M_k(r)[v_0]
\]
in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\). If the cross-ratio or commutator invariant attached to the Apéry matrices is nonzero, this cannot happen except when a bounded-degree integer polynomial vanishes modulo \(p\).

3. **Controlled eigenline degeneracy.** A repeated eigenline occurs when
\[
\operatorname{disc}(\operatorname{tr}(M)^2-4\det(M))=0.
\]
For Apéry products this gives an explicit integer polynomial. Its prime divisors are the structural exceptional primes.

## 4. What can actually be proved from the current census

The census (all \(p\le20000\), \(H=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\)) showing

- maximum common-root multiplicity 2,
- maximum left codegree 3,
- no multiplicity 3,

is consistent with a bounded-codegree theorem, but it does not prove one. The missing lemma is a uniform modular rigidity theorem of the following form:

> There exists an absolute constant \(C\) and a finite exceptional prime set \(S\) such that for every \(p\notin S\), every projective line \(L\), and every Apéry transfer family \(M_h\), the number of indices \(h\le H\) with \(M_h(r)L=L_0\) is at most \(C\).

This is stronger than a resultant argument.

## 5. Consequence for Kővári–Sós–Turán

If the above rigidity lemma holds, then

\[
\max m=O(1),\qquad \max \operatorname{codeg}_L=O(1).
\]

The bipartite collision graph therefore has bounded multiplicity and bounded left codegree. Applying Kővári–Sós–Turán gives
\[
E_p(H)\ll H^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

However, without the projective rigidity input, transfer matrices alone allow periodic finite-field counterexamples and no deterministic KST bound follows.

## 6. Recommended next proof target

The shortest viable route is not to prove that every triple collision creates a global polynomial identity. Instead prove:

1. compute the finite collection of possible short-word commutators of Apéry transfer matrices;
2. show their determinants are nonzero integers;
3. remove their finite prime divisors;
4. prove any triple collision forces one of these commutators to vanish on a projective line.

Then the only remaining primes are structural primes, and the census pattern becomes a theorem rather than an observation.
