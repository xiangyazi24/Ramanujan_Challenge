# Mixed-characteristic Apéry pairs: exact transfer theorem, aligned divisor bound, and transverse saturation

## Verdict

There is an exact pair theorem, but it splits sharply into two sectors.

Let

\[
p=n-u,\qquad q=n-v,\qquad h=v-u=p-q,
\]

with

\[
\frac{3n}{4}<q<p\le n,\qquad 0\le u<v<\frac n4,
\qquad p\mid B_u,\quad q\mid B_v.
\]

Define the gap continuants by

\[
N_0(x)=0,\qquad N_1(x)=1,
\]

\[
N_{r+1}(x)=P(x+r)N_r(x)-(x+r)^6N_{r-1}(x),
\qquad r\ge1.
\]

Then the actual Apéry recurrence gives the exact integral identity

\[
\boxed{
N_h(u)B_{u+1}
 =D_h(u)B_v+C_h(u)B_u,
}
\tag{1}
\]

where

\[
D_h(u)=\prod_{j=2}^{h}(u+j)^3,
\qquad
C_h(u)=(u+1)^3N_{h-1}(u+1),
\tag{2}
\]

with the empty product convention for \(h=1\).

This identity yields:

1. **Aligned sector.** One of the two characteristics also kills the other endpoint. Precisely,
   \[
   p\mid B_v\iff p\mid N_h(u),
   \qquad
   q\mid B_u\iff q\mid N_h(u).
   \tag{3}
   \]
   In this sector there are genuine fixed-gap carriers:
   \[
   p\mid N_h(n),\qquad q\mid N_h(n-h),
   \tag{4}
   \]
   and their logarithmic heights are \(O(h\log n)\). Consequently the number of aligned hit-pairs at one fixed gap \(h\) is at most
   \[
   6h+O\!\left(\frac h{\log n}\right).
   \tag{5}
   \]

2. **Transverse sector.** Here
   \[
   p\nmid B_v,\qquad q\nmid B_u,
   \qquad	ext{equivalently}\qquad
   \gcd(pq,N_h(u))=1.
   \tag{6}
   \]
   The remaining exact recurrence identity is the normalized quotient congruence
   \[
   \boxed{
   qD_h(u)\frac{B_v}{q}
   +pC_h(u)\frac{B_u}{p}
   \equiv0\pmod{N_h(u)}.
   }
   \tag{7}
   \]
   This is a real mixed-characteristic identity, but its modulus is a unit at both target primes. It therefore does not make \(p\), \(q\), or \(pq\) divide a new low-height integer.

More strongly, after localizing at \(N_h(u)\), the formal elimination ideal generated by the two hit conditions and the rank-two recurrence is exactly

\[
\boxed{
(p,F_u)\cap(q,F_v)
 =(pq,\ pF_v,\ qF_u,\ F_uF_v),
}
\tag{8}
\]

where \(F_r=(r!)^3B_r\). Its scalar elimination ideal is only \((pq)\), which is coefficient content. Dividing out that forced content leaves the unit ideal. Thus recurrence transport, Bézout identities, linear resultants, and neighboring-state determinants cannot produce a primitive transverse carrier.

The narrow surviving arithmetic problem is therefore a correlation theorem for the **actual normalized Apéry quotients** in (7), or equivalently a bound for the transverse pair count defined in Section 9 below. It cannot be replaced by a same-modulus continuant argument.

## 1. Exact factorial-cleared normalization

Put

\[
F_r=(r!)^3B_r.
\]

Multiplying the Apéry recurrence by \((r!)^3\) gives the integral recurrence

\[
\boxed{
F_{r+1}=P(r)F_r-r^6F_{r-1}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

This gauge is used only to make transfer matrices integral. It introduces no target-prime content here. Indeed,

\[
0\le r\le v<q<p,
\]

so

\[
v_p(r!)=v_q(r!)=0.
\]

Hence, exactly,

\[
\boxed{
 v_p(F_r)=v_p(B_r),\qquad
 v_q(F_r)=v_q(B_r)\quad(0\le r\le v).
}
\tag{10}
\]

The one-step matrix is

\[
M_r=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(r)&-r^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\binom{F_{r+1}}{F_r}=M_r\binom{F_r}{F_{r-1}}.
\tag{11}
\]

Its determinant is \(r^6\). Every index crossed between \(u\) and \(v\) is strictly below \(q\), so every such determinant is a unit modulo both \(p\) and \(q\). There is no hidden \(p^3\)- or \(q^3\)-clearing factor in this low-index transfer.

Induction from (9) gives

\[
\boxed{
F_v=N_h(u)F_{u+1}
 -(u+1)^6N_{h-1}(u+1)F_u.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Dividing by \((u!)^3\), then removing the common factor \((u+1)^3\), gives (1):

\[
N_h(u)B_{u+1}
 =\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(u+j)^3\right)B_v
 +(u+1)^3N_{h-1}(u+1)B_u.
\]

No factorial height remains in this primitive identity.

## 2. Exact same-characteristic alignment criterion

The no-consecutive-zero theorem applies because all indices in question are below both primes.

Since \(p\mid B_u\), one has

\[
p\nmid B_{u+1}.
\tag{13}
\]

Reducing (12) modulo \(p\) gives

\[
F_v\equiv N_h(u)F_{u+1}\pmod p.
\]

The second factor is a unit, so

\[
\boxed{p\mid B_v\iff p\mid N_h(u).}
\tag{14}
\]

For the \(q\)-direction, first suppose \(q\mid B_u\). Then \(q\nmid B_{u+1}\), and reducing (12) modulo \(q\), using \(q\mid B_v\), gives \(q\mid N_h(u)\).

Conversely, suppose \(q\mid N_h(u)\). The full transfer matrix from the state at \(u\) to the state at \(v\) is invertible modulo \(q\). Therefore its row

\[
\left(N_h(u),\;-(u+1)^6N_{h-1}(u+1)\right)
\]

cannot vanish identically modulo \(q\). Once its first entry vanishes, the second is a unit. Since \(q\mid F_v\), (12) then forces \(q\mid F_u\), hence \(q\mid B_u\). Thus

\[
\boxed{q\mid B_u\iff q\mid N_h(u).}
\tag{15}
\]

Equations (14) and (15) prove (3). They also show exactly where a same-modulus continuant is legitimate: it is legitimate only after the same prime is known to annihilate both endpoints.

In particular, the pure mixed-characteristic sector is exactly

\[
\boxed{
 p\nmid B_v,\ q\nmid B_u
 \iff \gcd(pq,N_h(u))=1.
}
\tag{16}
\]

## 3. Genuine fixed-gap carriers for the aligned sector

Because

\[
u=n-p,
\]

integer polynomial evaluation gives

\[
N_h(u)\equiv N_h(n)\pmod p.
\tag{17}
\]

Likewise

\[
q=n-v=n-u-h,
\qquad u\equiv n-h\pmod q,
\]

so

\[
N_h(u)\equiv N_h(n-h)\pmod q.
\tag{18}
\]

Combining these with (14) and (15) yields the exact fixed-row form

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid B_v\iff p\mid N_h(n),
 \qquad
 q\mid B_u\iff q\mid N_h(n-h).
}
\tag{19}
\]

These are actual common integer carriers for all aligned pairs at one fixed spacing \(h\).

### Positivity and height of the carriers

For \(x\ge0\), define \(y_0=0\), \(y_1=1\), and

\[
(x+r+1)^3y_{r+1}=P(x+r)y_r-(x+r)^3y_{r-1}.
\tag{20}
\]

Then

\[
y_h=\frac{N_h(x)}{\prod_{j=2}^{h}(x+j)^3}.
\tag{21}
\]

Let \(s=x+r\). If \(y_r>y_{r-1}\ge0\), then

\[
\begin{aligned}
(s+1)^3(y_{r+1}-y_r)
&=\bigl(P(s)-s^3-(s+1)^3\bigr)y_r
  +s^3(y_r-y_{r-1})\\
&=4(2s+1)^3y_r+s^3(y_r-y_{r-1})>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

Thus \(y_r\) is strictly increasing and positive. Also

\[
P(s)<34(s+1)^3,
\]

so (20) gives \(y_{r+1}<34y_r\). Consequently

\[
\boxed{
0<N_h(x)
 <34^{h-1}\prod_{j=2}^{h}(x+j)^3.
}
\tag{23}
\]

In particular,

\[
\log N_h(n)
 \le(h-1)\bigl(3\log(n+h)+\log34\bigr),
\tag{24}
\]

\[
\log N_h(n-h)
 \le(h-1)\bigl(3\log n+\log34\bigr).
\tag{25}
\]

### Fixed-gap aligned multiplicity

Let \(A_h(n)\) be the number of hit-pairs at gap \(h\) for which at least one cross endpoint also vanishes, namely

\[
p\mid B_v\quad\text{or}\quad q\mid B_u.
\]

Pairs with \(p\mid B_v\) have distinct primes \(p\), all dividing \(N_h(n)\). Pairs with \(q\mid B_u\) have distinct primes \(q\), all dividing \(N_h(n-h)\). Since every such prime exceeds \(3n/4\),

\[
\boxed{
A_h(n)
\le
\frac{\log N_h(n)+\log N_h(n-h)}{\log(3n/4)}.
}
\tag{26}
\]

Using (24) and (25),

\[
\boxed{
A_h(n)
\le6h+O\!\left(\frac h{\log n}\right)
}
\tag{27}
\]

uniformly for \(1\le h<n/4\). This is the promised rigorous spacing-level saving. It is useful for small gaps, although summing the bound over all \(h<n/4\) does not by itself give the required global little-oh estimate.

## 4. The exact transverse quotient identity

Write the known integral quotients

\[
a=\frac{B_u}{p},
\qquad
b=\frac{B_v}{q}.
\tag{28}
\]

Substitution in (1) gives

\[
\boxed{
N_h(u)B_{u+1}
 =qD_h(u)b+pC_h(u)a.
}
\tag{29}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
qD_h(u)b+pC_h(u)a
 \equiv0\pmod{N_h(u)}.
}
\tag{30}
\]

This is the strongest scalar identity obtained by eliminating the middle recurrence state without changing characteristics. It has an \(O(h\log n)\)-height modulus. However, in the transverse sector (16), this modulus is invertible modulo both \(p\) and \(q\). Thus (30) correlates the quotient residues \(a,b\) modulo the continuant; it does not force a target prime to divide the continuant.

Using \(p=q+h\), (30) may be rewritten as

\[
q\bigl(D_h(u)b+C_h(u)a\bigr)
 +hC_h(u)a\equiv0\pmod{N_h(u)}.
\tag{31}
\]

This is a legitimate use of \(p-q=h\), because it is an identity in the integers before reduction. It still leaves one free quotient coordinate.

Any future use of the recurrence at transverse pairs must therefore control the actual distribution of

\[
\left(\frac{B_u}{p},\frac{B_v}{q}\right)
 \pmod{N_h(u)}.
\]

That quotient-distribution input is not present in the ordinary recurrence or in the known fixed-characteristic continuants.

## 5. Exact elimination-ideal saturation in the transverse sector

The negative result can be stated algebraically without heuristics.

Let

\[
X=F_u,\qquad Y=F_{u+1},\qquad Z=F_v,
\]

and put

\[
A=N_h(u),\qquad
C=(u+1)^6N_{h-1}(u+1).
\]

Then (12) is

\[
Z=AY-CX.
\tag{32}
\]

In the transverse sector, \(A\) is a unit modulo \(pq\). Work in

\[
R=\mathbb Z[1/A].
\]

The change of variables

\[
(X,Y)\longleftrightarrow(X,Z),
\qquad
Y=A^{-1}(Z+CX),
\]

is an automorphism of \(R[X,Y]\). Thus the two hit ideals become

\[
\mathfrak p=(p,X),
\qquad
\mathfrak q=(q,Z)
\]

inside \(R[X,Z]\).

Since \(p\) and \(q\) are distinct primes, there exist integers \(r,s\) with \(rp+sq=1\). Hence

\[
\mathfrak p+\mathfrak q=R[X,Z].
\]

The ideals are comaximal, so intersection equals product:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak p\cap\mathfrak q
&=\mathfrak p\mathfrak q\\
&=(p,X)(q,Z)\\
&=\boxed{(pq,\ pZ,\ qX,\ XZ)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]

Setting \(X=Z=0\) in (33) shows that the scalar elimination ideal is

\[
\boxed{
(\mathfrak p\cap\mathfrak q)\cap R=(pq).
}
\tag{34}
\]

Thus every scalar generated formally from the recurrence and the two local hit equations already contains \(pq\) in its coefficients. Saturating by that moving coefficient content gives

\[
(\mathfrak p\cap\mathfrak q):(pq)^\infty=R[X,Z].
\tag{35}
\]

There is no primitive scalar remainder.

The degree-one part is especially transparent. A linear expression

\[
\alpha X+\beta Z+\gamma
\]

belongs to the pair ideal if and only if

\[
q\mid\alpha,\qquad p\mid\beta,\qquad pq\mid\gamma.
\tag{36}
\]

Hence every linear cross-prime carrier explicitly injects the other moving prime into its coefficient. If moving-prime coefficient content is disallowed, the first available generator is \(XZ=F_uF_v\), merely the product of the two original hit values.

### Recurrence countermodel

This ideal calculation has a concrete modular realization. Because \(A\) is a unit modulo both primes, prescribe

\[
(X,Z)\equiv(0,1)\pmod p,
\qquad
(X,Z)\equiv(1,0)\pmod q.
\tag{37}
\]

CRT supplies \(X,Z\pmod{pq}\), and then

\[
Y=A^{-1}(Z+CX)\pmod{pq}
\]

satisfies the exact Apéry recurrence transfer. All lower transfer determinants are units modulo \(pq\), so this state extends to a full solution of the same factorial-cleared recurrence modulo \(pq\).

It has a pure-cross zero at \(u\) in characteristic \(p\) and at \(v\) in characteristic \(q\), with neither cross endpoint vanishing. Therefore no identity valid for every solution of the rank-two recurrence can give an additional transverse scalar divisor.

This countermodel changes the initial state; it is not a counterexample involving the distinguished Apéry sequence \(B_0=1,B_1=5\). Its purpose is exact: any successful transverse theorem must use arithmetic of that distinguished initial state beyond the recurrence module.

## 6. Bézout, resultants, subresultants, and neighboring rows

### 6.1 Linear divisibility resultant

Introduce the quotient variables

\[
X=pa,\qquad Z=qb.
\]

The resultant of the two linear polynomials

\[
pT-X,\qquad qT-Z
\]

is, up to sign,

\[
\operatorname{Res}_T(pT-X,qT-Z)
 =qX-pZ
 =pq(a-b).
\tag{38}
\]

Thus \(pq\) divides this resultant, but only because the opposite prime was inserted into each coefficient. After division by the forced \(pq\), the quotient \(a-b\) has no fixed divisor and no useful height bound.

Adding the recurrence equation (32) and eliminating \(Y\) does not change this conclusion: equation (34) says that the entire scalar elimination ideal is \((pq)\). Higher Sylvester minors and subresultants made solely from these linear divisibility equations and the rank-two transfer cannot leave a primitive target-selective factor.

### 6.2 Neighboring-state determinant is a unit

A particularly tempting determinant is

\[
W_{u,v}=B_uB_{v+1}-B_{u+1}B_v.
\tag{39}
\]

In the transverse sector,

\[
p\mid B_u,\quad p\nmid B_{u+1}B_v,
\]

so

\[
W_{u,v}\equiv-B_{u+1}B_v\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Likewise,

\[
q\mid B_v,\quad q\nmid B_uB_{v+1},
\]

so

\[
W_{u,v}\equiv B_uB_{v+1}\not\equiv0\pmod q.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{v_p(W_{u,v})=v_q(W_{u,v})=0}
\tag{40}
\]

in every transverse pair. Neighboring-row determinants are not merely too large; they carry neither target prime at all. Multiplication by factorial units or any primitive normalization leaves (40) unchanged.

## 7. Binomial/Newton interpolation: an exact pair selector that is CRT-saturated

There is an exact coefficient-primitive integer carrying \(pq\), but it is a universal interpolation of the two independent local conditions rather than a new recurrence correlation.

Let \(\Delta B_r=B_{r+1}-B_r\), and define

\[
\mathcal I_{u,h}(n)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{h}\binom{n-u}{j}\Delta^jB_u
 =\sum_{j=0}^{h}\binom pj\Delta^jB_u.
\tag{41}
\]

Since \(h<p\),

\[
\binom pj\equiv0\pmod p
\qquad(1\le j\le h),
\]

and hence

\[
\mathcal I_{u,h}(n)\equiv B_u\pmod p.
\tag{42}
\]

Since \(p=q+h\) and \(j\le h<q\),

\[
\binom pj=\binom{q+h}{j}\equiv\binom hj\pmod q.
\]

Newton's identity gives

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{h}\binom hj\Delta^jB_u=B_{u+h}=B_v,
\]

so

\[
\mathcal I_{u,h}(n)\equiv B_v\pmod q.
\tag{43}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
pq\mid\mathcal I_{u,h}(n)
\iff
p\mid B_u\ \text{and}\ q\mid B_v.
}
\tag{44}
\]

This is a complete exact mixed-characteristic selector.

However, it satisfies the following universal congruence, without assuming either hit:

\[
\boxed{
 h\mathcal I_{u,h}(n)
 \equiv pB_v-qB_u\pmod{pq}.
}
\tag{45}
\]

Indeed, modulo \(p\), both sides equal \(-qB_u\), while modulo \(q\), both sides equal \(pB_v\). Since

\[
\gcd(h,pq)=1,
\]

(45) proves that (44) is exactly the CRT recombination of the two endpoint divisibilities. It contains no third condition.

### Height ledger

The recurrence and positivity give

\[
0<B_r<34^r.
\tag{46}
\]

Therefore, for \(j\le h\),

\[
|\Delta^jB_u|
 \le\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom jiB_{u+i}
 \le2^j34^v.
\tag{47}
\]

Because \(h<p/3\), the binomial coefficients \(\binom pj\) increase throughout \(0\le j\le h\). Hence

\[
|\mathcal I_{u,h}(n)|
 \le34^v(h+1)2^h\binom ph
 \le34^v(h+1)\left(\frac{2ep}{h}\right)^h.
\tag{48}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\log^+|\mathcal I_{u,h}(n)|
 \le v\log34+h\log\frac{2en}{h}+O(\log h).
}
\tag{49}
\]

This is at best linear in \(n\) over the full top-quarter range, not \(o(n)\) uniformly. More decisively, (45) shows that even an improved Archimedean estimate for this particular interpolation would not constitute a new algebraic coupling: the mod-\(pq\) class is already the tautological CRT class of \(B_u\) and \(B_v\).

## 8. Direct binomial and constant-term formulas do not identify the moduli

The Apéry summand can be written as

\[
B_r
 =\sum_{k=0}^{r}
 \left(
 \frac{(r+k)!}{k!^2(r-k)!}
 \right)^2.
\tag{50}
\]

For \(r\le v\), every factorial argument in (50) is at most \(2v<n/2\), while \(p,q>3n/4\). Hence all factorials in both the \(u\)- and \(v\)-sums are units in both target characteristics. There is no hidden factorial valuation that couples the two hits.

Likewise, in the current constant-term representation

\[
B_r=\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^r,
\tag{51}
\]

the pair conditions are

\[
\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p,
\qquad
\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^v=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_q.
\tag{52}
\]

They live in the product ring

\[
\mathbb Z/(pq)\mathbb Z
 \simeq\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_q.
\tag{53}
\]

A bounded Newton-polytope coefficient vector therefore supplies one local hyperplane in each factor. CRT permits those hyperplanes to be satisfied independently unless a new global relation between the coefficient vectors is proved. Determinants formed only from the two local coefficient equations reproduce the product ideal in (33).

The relation \(p-q=h\) changes integer indices, and it legitimately yields identities such as (31) and (45). It does **not** create a field homomorphism between \(\mathbb F_p\) and \(\mathbb F_q\).

## 9. Explicitly invalid same-modulus steps

The following implications are false and must not appear in a proof.

1. From
   \[
   q\mid B_v,\qquad q=p-h,
   \]
   one may not conclude \(B_v\equiv0\pmod p\). The equality \(q=p-h\) is an integer identity, not an identification of residue fields.

2. From \(q\mid B_v\), one may not use the modulo-\(p\) transfer from \(u\) to \(v\) to deduce \(p\mid N_h(u)\). Equation (14) requires the additional same-characteristic hypothesis \(p\mid B_v\).

3. A congruence proved modulo \(q\) cannot be substituted into a recurrence reduced modulo \(p\). The correct simultaneous ring is the product (53), in which the two coordinates remain independent.

4. Dividing a factorial-cleared identity by a moving prime is legal only after its exact valuation has been established. Here (10) gives that normalization. No Lucas congruence modulo \(p^2\) or \(q^2\) is used.

The modular recurrence countermodel (37) refutes every recurrence-only argument that silently performs one of these identifications.

## 10. Narrowest surviving pair-correlation statement

Let

\[
\mathcal H_n=
\left\{
0\le u<\frac n4:
 p=n-u\text{ is prime},\ p>\frac{3n}{4},\ p\mid B_u
\right\}.
\tag{54}
\]

For each \(h\ge1\), define the transverse fixed-gap count

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_h(n)=\#\{u:\;&u,u+h\in\mathcal H_n,\\
&p=n-u,\ q=n-u-h,\\
&\gcd(pq,N_h(u))=1\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{55}
\]

Let \(A_h(n)\) be the complementary aligned count. Then there is an exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\binom{|\mathcal H_n|}{2}
 =\sum_{1\le h<n/4}\bigl(T_h(n)+A_h(n)\bigr).
}
\tag{56}
\]

The aligned part is reduced to explicit one-variable divisor incidences:

\[
A_h(n)
\le
\omega_{(3n/4,n]}(N_h(n))
+
\omega_{(3n/4,n]}(N_h(n-h)),
\tag{57}
\]

and satisfies the proved bound (27).

The genuinely mixed-characteristic residual is:

> **[TQ-CORR] Transverse quotient correlation.** Prove
> \[
> \sum_{1\le h<n/4}T_h(n)
> =o\!\left(\frac{n^2}{\log^2n}\right),
> \tag{58}
> \]
> using the distinguished Apéry initial state, equivalently using nontrivial distribution of the normalized quotients in (30).

For the original top-quarter multiplicity target, one also needs a summable improvement for the explicit aligned incidence term in (57). A sufficient fully separated residual is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{h<n/4}T_h(n)\\
&\quad+
\sum_{h<n/4}
\left[
\omega_{(3n/4,n]}(N_h(n))
+\omega_{(3n/4,n]}(N_h(n-h))
\right]
=o\!\left(\frac{n^2}{\log^2n}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{59}
\]

This is strictly narrower than the original pair problem:

- the same-characteristic leakage is carried by two explicit integers of height \(O(h\log n)\);
- the transverse part has the exact quotient congruence (30);
- all recurrence-only, Bézout, resultant, neighboring-determinant, Newton-interpolation, and raw CRT constructions are exhausted by the saturation theorem (33)--(45).

## Terminal conclusion

The actual Apéry recurrence does produce a useful cross-prime pair theorem. It gives a low-height fixed-gap divisor and an \(O(h)\) multiplicity bound precisely when one characteristic also kills the opposite endpoint. Outside that aligned sector, the transfer matrix is unimodular in both characteristics and the hit ideals are independent.

The exact transverse identity is (30), not a divisibility \(p\mid N_h(u)\) or \(q\mid N_h(u)\). The formal scalar elimination ideal contains only the forced coefficient factor \(pq\), while the natural neighboring-state determinant is a unit at both primes. The pair Newton carrier (41) is exact, but (45) proves that it is only the CRT recombination of the two original divisibilities.

Therefore no unconditional spacing or sublinear-height transverse carrier follows from the requested architectures alone. The narrow surviving problem is [TQ-CORR]: arithmetic correlation of the actual normalized Apéry quotients across two distinct characteristics, with the aligned continuant incidences handled separately by (57).
