# The most promising bridge: crystalline Mellin sheaves and a p-adic monodromy theorem for zero fibers

## Executive choice

Among the five seeds, the most promising route is not Gross--Koblitz, unit roots, or Hida theory separately. The missing object should be a **crystalline Mellin transform sheaf with a mod-p monodromy theorem**.

The key point is that the current Mellin sheaf

\[
\mathcal F_\ell=\mathrm{FT}_!(\mathcal H_\ell)
\]

lives in the \(\ell\)-adic world. Katz gives geometric monodromy, rank, and complex trace equidistribution. But the condition

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\]

is equivalent to the Frobenius trace landing in the residue hyperplane

\[
T_p(r)\in \mathfrak p_p.
\]

Therefore the missing theorem is a statement about the **reduction of the Mellin sheaf modulo p**, not its complex trace distribution.

The conjectural object is a family of overconvergent F-isocrystals:

\[
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cris}}=\mathrm{FT}^{\mathrm{cris}}_!(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{cris}})
\]

whose Frobenius matrix simultaneously controls:

1. the complex Mellin trace;
2. the p-adic unit-root part;
3. the residue class of the trace modulo p.

A theorem about this object would directly attack cross-prime independence.

# Precise conjecture

## Crystalline Mellin independence conjecture

For the Apéry K3 family there exists an overconvergent F-isocrystal \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cris}}\) on the Mellin parameter line such that:

1. Its Frobenius matrix

\[
\Phi_p(r)\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_p)
\]

has characteristic polynomial

\[
X^2-T_p(r)X+p^3.
\]

2. The reduction

\[
\overline{\Phi}_p(r)
\]

has equidistributed conjugacy classes in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\) outside an exceptional algebraic subset.

3. The trace-zero locus satisfies

\[
\#\{r<p:T_p(r)\equiv0\pmod p\}=O(p^{1/2+\epsilon}).
\]

4. For distinct primes \(p_1,\dots,p_k\), the joint reductions

\[
(\overline\Phi_{p_1}(r),\dots,\overline\Phi_{p_k}(r))
\]

are asymptotically independent.

The last statement is exactly what the LGTQ-3/cross-prime independence problem needs.

# Why this is more promising than Gross--Koblitz

Gross--Koblitz is attractive because it converts Gauss sums into p-adic Gamma values:

\[
\tau(\chi)= -\pi^{s(\chi)}\Gamma_p(s(\chi)).
\]

However the Apéry Mellin transform is not a single Gauss sum. It is a trace function of a rank-two geometric object:

\[
T_p(r)=\sum_a \theta_p(a)a^r.
\]

The zero condition is not usually a Gamma zero. It is a cancellation between many Frobenius eigenvalues.

Gross--Koblitz may describe individual hypergeometric pieces, but it probably cannot see the global zero set \(Z_p\).

The crystalline Mellin sheaf would package all cancellations at once.

# Proof strategy

## Step 1: Construct the crystalline Mellin object

Replace Katz's Fourier transform

\[
\mathrm{FT}_!
\]

by its rigid cohomology analogue:

\[
\mathrm{FT}^{\mathrm{rig}}_!.
\]

The input is the K3 Picard--Fuchs F-isocrystal.

The output is a rank-two overconvergent F-isocrystal whose Frobenius is the p-adic Mellin transform.

This is the missing bridge between:

\[
\ell\text{-adic trace statistics}
\]

and

\[
p\text{-adic residue statistics}.
\]

## Step 2: Prove mod-p monodromy

The required analogue of Katz's theorem is:

\[
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathcal F}_{\mathrm{cris}})
\supseteq \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)
\]

for almost all p.

Then trace-zero matrices form a codimension-one subvariety of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), giving a Lang-Weil estimate:

\[
\#\{g:\operatorname{tr}(g)=0\}
\sim p^2.
\]

After normalization this predicts the correct density of zeros.

More importantly, products of independent Frobenius classes become independent because the geometric monodromy is a product group.

---

## Step 3: Deduce cross-prime independence

For primes \(p,q\), define

\[
Z_p=\{r:T_p(r)=0\pmod p\}.
\]

The desired estimate is a correlation bound:

\[
\#(Z_p\cap Z_q)
\approx
\frac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}.
\]

The crystalline monodromy theorem would replace this with a geometric intersection calculation:

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi_p)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(\Phi_q)=0.
\]

These become independent trace conditions on a product Frobenius space.

# First concrete computation to test

Before proving anything, compute the following invariant.

For each prime p, construct the finite-field Mellin matrices

\[
M_p(r)=
\begin{pmatrix}
* & *\\
* & *
\end{pmatrix}
\]

whose trace is \(T_p(r)\).

Then test whether the generated subgroup

\[
G_p=\langle M_p(0),M_p(1),\dots,M_p(p-1)\rangle
\subseteq \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)
\]

is almost always:

\[
G_p=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p).
\]

This is the finite-field shadow of the desired theorem.

The second test is the decisive one:

For primes p and q compute

\[
C(p,q)=\#\{r<pq:
T_p(r\bmod p)=0,
T_q(r\bmod q)=0\}.
\]

Compare with

\[
\frac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}\cdot pq.
\]

If the error behaves like

\[
O((pq)^{1/2+\epsilon}),
\]

then the conjectural mechanism is visible.

# Relation to the five seeds

## Seed 1: Gross--Koblitz

Useful as a local computation tool, but unlikely to solve independence because zeros are collective cancellations.

## Seed 2: Dwork unit roots

Important ingredient. The unit-root Mellin transform is probably the crystalline realization of the same object. It gives the p-adic analytic interpolation needed for Step 1.

## Seed 3: Overconvergent modular forms

Also relevant. The Apéry form's Coleman family may provide the global construction of the F-isocrystal.

## Seed 4: Frobenius independence

This is closest conceptually. The missing theorem is exactly:

"Chebotarev independence survives after applying the Mellin trace-zero statistic."

The crystalline Mellin object is the mechanism that could make this rigorous.

## Seed 5: Hidden identities

Possible, but less likely. A miraculous identity relating characteristics would be stronger than needed. The geometric route explains why independence should occur.
