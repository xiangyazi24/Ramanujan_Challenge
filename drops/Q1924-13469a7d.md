# Q1924: central gap-one arithmetic attack

## Summary

The natural first-order Bezout ansatz does not produce a certificate with a terminal factor supported only on
\(3d_m\prod_{2\le t\le m}X_t\). The obstruction is that the recurrence gives a clean continuant identity for the reciprocal-sum term, but the endpoint functional \(e_m\) is not the adjoint boundary term of that identity. A genuine certificate requires a second boundary variable (equivalently a two-parameter determinant/minor ansatz).

## Continuant setup

Write the recurrence as

\[
X_{t+1}=a_tX_t-b_tX_{t-1},\qquad a_t=4t(68t^2+3),\quad b_t=(2t-1)^6.
\]

For the tridiagonal determinant \(D_n(\lambda)\) obtained by replacing \(a_t\) by \(a_t+\lambda c_t\), the derivative identity is

\[
D_n'(0)=\sum_{j=2}^n c_j\prod_{i<j}X_i\prod_{i>j}X_i.
\]

Thus choosing \(c_j=6(j^2-1)d_j\) gives exactly the weighted product correction in \(Y_n\). Therefore

\[
Y_n=P_n-D_n'(0)
\]

(up to the harmless convention for the first and last diagonal entries). This is a determinant derivative identity, not yet a Bezout certificate.

## Why the first-order Bezout fails

Assume an identity of the form

\[
A_me_m+B_mY_m=C_m
\]

where \(A_m,B_m\) are generated only by the original first-order continuant recurrence. Expanding backwards in the last recurrence step gives two independent boundary coefficients:

\[
X_m,\qquad X_{m-1}.
\]

The \(Y_m\) contribution cancels the interior derivative terms, but the remaining boundary vector is

\[
\alpha_mX_m+\beta_mX_{m-1}.
\]

The condition \(e_m=0\) kills

\[
(2m-1)^3X_{m-1}-5X_m,
\]

which is one linear combination. The leftover coefficient is not forced to be a multiple of \(d_mP_{m-1}\). Hence the desired support statement cannot follow from a one-boundary ansatz.

## Exact obstruction

The missing variable is the derivative boundary term

\[
Z_m=\partial_\lambda D_m(0)\big|_{\text{right boundary}},
\]

or equivalently the second fundamental continuant associated with the adjacent minor. The pair

\[
(Y_m,Z_m)
\]

forms a closed first-order system, while \(Y_m\) alone does not.

The minimal enlargement is therefore a 2-by-2 determinant identity:

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
D_m(0)&D_m'(0)\\
M_m(0)&M_m'(0)
\end{pmatrix}
\]

where \(M_m\) is the adjacent principal minor. Its determinant is the Desnanot-Jacobi invariant. Only after including \(M_m\) can the endpoint contribution collapse to a product term.

## Proposed next ansatz

Introduce

\[
W_m=u_mY_m+v_mZ_m
\]

with \((u_m,v_m)\) chosen by the adjoint recurrence. The target is then a true telescoping identity

\[
A_me_m+B_mW_m=\pm 3d_m\prod_{t=2}^mX_t.
\]

The coefficients must satisfy the dual recurrence

\[
\binom{u_{m+1}}{v_{m+1}}
=T_m^{-T}\binom{u_m}{v_m},
\]

where \(T_m\) is the transfer matrix

\[
T_m=\begin{pmatrix}a_m&-b_m\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is the smallest enlargement that can produce a real certificate.

## Conclusion

The reciprocal-sum rewrite is not itself a proof: it merely identifies \(Y_m\) as a determinant derivative. The natural first-order Bezout search is obstructed by an unavoidable second boundary component. A valid certificate, if it exists, must use the adjacent minor/Desnanot-Jacobi two-dimensional continuant system. No counterexample to the final arithmetic claim is produced; rather, the obstruction rules out the smaller certificate class.
