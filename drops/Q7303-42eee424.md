# Proof-oriented experiment for cross-prime structure in rare high-load rows

## Executive verdict

The right experiment is **not** another table of \(F_k\), quotient coincidences, or normalized residue correlations.  It should turn every row

\[
 n<X^2,\qquad \mathcal H(n)=\{p\in(X,2X]:b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p\},
 \qquad K(n)=|\mathcal H(n)|\ge3,
\]

into a small exact arithmetic object and ask whether the object has a low-height carrier.

The key horizontalization is elementary but very useful.  For a hit \(p_i\in\mathcal H(n)\), write

\[
 n=q_i p_i+r_i,
 \qquad
 \rho_i=\min(r_i,p_i-1-r_i).
\]

Then

\[
 0\le q_i<X,\qquad 0\le \rho_i<X,
 \qquad b_{\rho_i}\equiv0\pmod{p_i}.
\]

Thus every high-load row supplies two kinds of **common small indices**, \(q_i\) and \(ho_i\), all below \(X\), although the characteristics \(p_i\) vary.  This is the most promising place to look for a horizontal integer identity.  The experiment should build the complete Apéry recurrence-state tensor on the digit pool

\[
 S(n)=\{q_i,ho_i:p_i\in\mathcal H(n)\}\subset[0,X),
\]

then search for a small number of state collisions, quotient-induced returns, or first-order \(p\)-adic labels whose prime support forces a common integer divisor.

This design is consistent with the current repository state:

* `problems/3.2/hm3_explore.py` already has the correct pair-CRT enumeration, but its independent cross-check allocates an \(X^2\)-byte array and stops at small \(X\).
* `problems/3.2/hm3_result.tex` proves that reflection, interval bounds, bounded row codegrees, and necessary gap certificates are compatible with an anchored rainbow star.
* `problems/3.2/distinguished_label_verify.py` proves that a raw companion label transported between known returns is only the universal continuant cocycle.  Therefore companion values alone are not a new invariant.
* `problems/3.2/CRON_zp_bigscan.c` and `CRON_garqi_moments.c` already provide the production-quality division-free/Montgomery kernel needed for the prime scan.
* The current `AVENUES.md` explicitly identifies the quotient-chain and mixed-characteristic directions as the live all-index avenues.

The recommended output is a certificate catalogue of exact carriers and exact failed carriers, not an asymptotic claim.

## Exact recurrence notation

Put

\[
 P(m)=34m^3+51m^2+27m+5.
\]

Use the two cleared Apéry solutions

\[
 B_0=1,\quad B_1=5,
 \qquad
 D_0=0,\quad D_1=1,
\]

both satisfying

\[
 Y_{m+1}=P(m)Y_m-m^6Y_{m-1}.
\]

Then \(B_m=(m!)^3b_m\).  The exact Wronskian is

\[
 B_mD_{m+1}-B_{m+1}D_m=(m!)^6. \tag{1}
\]

For \(h\ge0\), define the gap continuant

\[
 N_0(x)=0,\qquad N_1(x)=1,
\]

\[
 N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x). \tag{2}
\]

A direct recurrence induction gives

\[
 B_xD_{x+h}-D_xB_{x+h}=(x!)^6N_h(x). \tag{3}
\]

Consequently, whenever \(0\le x<x+h<p\),

\[
 [B_x:D_x]=[B_{x+h}:D_{x+h}]\text{ in }\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)
 \iff N_h(x)\equiv0\pmod p. \tag{4}
\]

At an Apéry zero \(0\le r<p\), \(B_r=0\) and \(D_r\ne0\).  Also

\[
 (r+1)^3b_{r+1}\equiv-r^3b_{r-1}\not\equiv0\pmod p. \tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) is important as a normalization, but it also shows that the raw nearest-neighbor ratio at a zero is recurrence-tautological and should not be advertised as a discovery.

# Ranked exact invariants

The first five are direct carrier detectors.  The next two add genuinely new local arithmetic.  The last two are disciplined relation-discovery mechanisms.

## 1. Quotient-to-characteristic zero matrix

For a row with hits \(p_1,\ldots,p_K\), define

\[
 Q_{ij}=\mathbf 1_{\{B_{q_j}\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\}}
       =\mathbf 1_{\{b_{q_j}\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\}}. \tag{6}
\]

The equality is valid because \(q_j<X<p_i\), so \(q_j!\) is a unit modulo \(p_i\).

For each quotient column \(q_j\), let

\[
 \operatorname{Supp}_Q(q_j)=\{p_i:Q_{ij}=1\}. \tag{7}
\]

Then there is an immediate integer carrier:

\[
 \prod_{p_i\in\operatorname{Supp}_Q(q_j)}p_i\mid b_{q_j}. \tag{8}
\]

Also record the minimum number of distinct quotient columns needed to cover a prescribed proportion of the row's hit primes.

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  The star chooses one marked residue and its reflection in each characteristic.  It puts no condition on the actual Apéry integer \(b_{q_j}\), and in particular does not force any off-diagonal or diagonal entry of (6).

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  A column supported on three or more row primes, or a uniformly bounded number of quotient columns covering every prime of every \(K\ge4\) row in several disjoint dyads, is worth attacking.  The reason is not statistical: (8) is already an exact common-divisor statement at an index \(q_j<X\).  The strongest possible signature is a quotient descent, for example a directed cycle \(p_i\mid b_{q_j}\) in which the involved quotient indices become strictly smaller under iteration.

## 2. Canonical-residue cross-zero matrix

Define

\[
 R_{ij}=\mathbf 1_{\{B_{\rho_j}\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\}}. \tag{9}
\]

The diagonal \(R_{ii}=1\) is automatic.  Every off-diagonal entry is new.  For a fixed canonical digit \(s\),

\[
 \prod_{\substack{i:\;R_{ij}=1\\ \rho_j=s}}p_i\mid b_s. \tag{10}
\]

Deduplicate equal digits before measuring support; equality \(ho_j=ho_{j'}\) is itself an exact feature, but should not be counted twice as two different carriers.

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  A star marks its own residue in each column, so it models the diagonal only.  It does not make the actual Apéry sequence vanish at another column's canonical residue.

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  The useful events are:

1. one \(ho_j\) is a zero in several row characteristics, giving (10);
2. one \(p_i\) sees at least three distinct digits among \(\{\rho_j\}\), producing a genuine short same-characteristic zero configuration;
3. a bounded collection of canonical digits covers most or all of a \(K\ge4\) row.

Any of these can be converted into an integer divisibility or a bounded-gap continuant problem.  A mere excess in the total number of off-diagonal entries, without a recurring carrier, is not enough.

## 3. Typed projective-state collision tensor

For every hit characteristic \(p_i\) and every unordered pair \(s<t\) in the digit pool \(S(n)\), compute

\[
 \Delta_i(s,t)=B_sD_t-D_sB_t\pmod{p_i}. \tag{11}
\]

Tag each edge by type `QQ`, `QR`, or `RR`, according to whether \(s,t\) arose as quotient or canonical-residue digits.  By (3),

\[
 \Delta_i(s,t)=0
 \iff p_i\mid N_{t-s}(s). \tag{12}
\]

This tensor includes nonzero projective fibers, not only zero-to-zero returns.

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  The anchored construction specifies a set of marked residues.  Even its optional low-energy value-map extension is not the Apéry transfer orbit and need not satisfy (1)--(4).  In particular, it supplies no prescribed `QQ` or `QR` collision graph.

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  Prioritize exact graph motifs, not edge counts:

* the same typed edge \((s,t)\) is supported by several row primes;
* a collision triangle occurs in one characteristic;
* a `QR` edge joins a quotient digit to a zero digit, forcing the quotient digit into the distinguished zero fiber;
* all \(K\ge4\) rows contain one of finitely many typed motifs, while matched \(K=2\) loci do not.

Do **not** count the state \([B_{\rho_i}:D_{\rho_i}]=[0:1]\) itself as an invariant.  It is just the definition of a hit.  Likewise, once a chain of returns is known, the companion multiplier along it is the universal cocycle already isolated in `distinguished_label_verify.py`.

## 4. Shared-continuant support and minimum-height cover

For \(s<t\) in \(S(n)\), set

\[
 \mathcal P_n(s,t)=\{p_i\in\mathcal H(n):p_i\mid N_{t-s}(s)\}. \tag{13}
\]

The support is read from (11); no large integer need be constructed during the first pass.  Define the gap cost \(c(s,t)=t-s\), and for a budget \(H\),

\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_n(H)=
 \max_{\substack{E\subset\binom{S(n)}2\\
                  \sum_{(s,t)\in E}(t-s)\le H}}
 \left|\bigcup_{(s,t)\in E}\mathcal P_n(s,t)\right|. \tag{14}
\]

Exclude the automatic reflection edge

\[
 (\rho_i,p_i-1-\rho_i), \tag{15}
\]

which is not in the small digit pool anyway and is already realizable by an anchored star.

For every selected edge set \(E\), there is an exact integer divisibility

\[
 \prod_{p_i\in\cup_{e\in E}\mathcal P_n(e)}p_i
 \mid
 \prod_{(s,t)\in E}N_{t-s}(s). \tag{16}
\]

The recurrence gives an elementary bound of the form

\[
 \log |N_h(s)|\le C h\log(X+2) \qquad(s+h<X), \tag{17}
\]

with an explicit absolute \(C\) obtainable by induction.  Therefore a low-cost cover is directly compatible with a size contradiction.

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  The star can satisfy each column's necessary reflection certificate, but it does not force several characteristics to divide one nonreflection continuant \(N_h(s)\), nor does it force a small collection of such continuants to cover its load.

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  The strongest signal is one fixed edge supported by many row primes.  The next strongest is \(\operatorname{Cov}_n(H)=K(n)\) with a tiny \(H\) for every high-load row.  At that point the theorem target is concrete: prove the cover deterministically and combine (16) with (17).  For candidates with modest \(h\), compute the exact multiprecision integer \(N_h(s)\), its bit length, and the exact quotient after division by the supported primes; this produces a finite certificate rather than a heuristic score.

## 5. Quotient-generated extra-return graph

The previous invariants inspect all pairs in \(S(n)\).  This one tests a much smaller, conceptually sharper family in which a quotient becomes a gap.

Define the preregistered gap set

\[
 \mathcal G_Q(n)=
 \{q_j:q_j\ge2\}
 \cup
 \{|q_j-q_k|:|q_j-q_k|\ge2\}. \tag{18}
\]

For each hit \(p_i\), each \(h\in\mathcal G_Q(n)\), and each valid start

\[
 x\in\{\rho_i,\rho_i-h\},
 \qquad 0\le x<x+h<p_i,
\]

test

\[
 N_h(x)\equiv0\pmod{p_i}. \tag{19}
\]

When \(x=\rho_i\), (19) says that \(ho_i+h\) is another actual zero modulo \(p_i\); when \(x=\rho_i-h\), it says that \(ho_i-h\) is another zero.  Remove the case \(h=p_i-1-2\rho_i\), which is merely reflection.

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  The quotient \(q_j\) is determined by the common row and a different modulus.  A synthetic star does not make \(q_j\) a return time in the actual Apéry transfer orbit modulo \(p_i\).

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  A serious signal would be that every \(K\ge4\) row has at least one quotient-generated edge, or that these edges combine with the original zero to force a three- or four-zero chain of total span \(O(q_j+q_k)\).  A recurring exact implication such as

\[
 K(n)\ge4\Longrightarrow
 \exists i,j\;N_{q_j}(\rho_i)\equiv0\pmod{p_i} \tag{20}
\]

would be a sharply falsifiable theorem target.  An isolated occurrence is only a curiosity.

## 6. First \(p\)-adic zero tangent and reflection defect

The zero set records only \(b_{\rho_i}\bmod p_i\).  Lift the same canonical zero to \(p_i^2\).  Define

\[
 \alpha_i=rac{b_{\rho_i}}{p_i}\pmod{p_i}, \tag{21}
\]

and the first-order reflection defect

\[
 \varepsilon_i=
 \frac{b_{p_i-1-\rho_i}-b_{\rho_i}}{p_i}\pmod{p_i}. \tag{22}
\]

Both quotients are integers.  Normalize them by the nonzero local slope

\[
 u_i=(\rho_i+1)^3b_{\rho_i+1}\pmod{p_i}, \tag{23}
\]

and record

\[
 \tau_i=\alpha_i u_i^{-1},
 \qquad
 \eta_i=\varepsilon_i u_i^{-1}
 \quad\text{in }\mathbf F_{p_i}. \tag{24}
\]

Also record the exact indicator \(p_i^2\mid b_{\rho_i}\), equivalently \(	au_i=0\).

These values are inexpensive to obtain from a second cleared recurrence modulo \(p_i^2\).  If \(B_{\rho_i}=p_i\beta_i\pmod{p_i^2}\), then

\[
 \alpha_i\equiv
 \beta_i\,((\rho_i!)^3)^{-1}\pmod{p_i}. \tag{25}
\]

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  The star is a mod-\(p\) zero-set construction.  It has no compatible integral lift, no \(p^2\)-zero multiplicity, and no first-order reflection defect.

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  Look for a fixed low-degree relation involving

\[
 (q_i,\rho_i,\tau_i,\eta_i,[B_{q_i}:D_{q_i}]) \tag{26}
\]

with the same integer coefficients in independent dyads.  A repeated excess of \(	au_i=0\) in high-load rows is also worth checking, but only an exact implication or a relation stable under held-out data would justify a theorem attack.  Raw histograms of \(	au_i\) do not.

## 7. Hypergeometric cancellation tangents

The recurrence state can miss arithmetic carried by cancellation inside the defining sum.  For a canonical zero \(ho\), put

\[
 A_{\rho,k}=\binom{\rho}{k}^2\binom{\rho+k}{k}^2,
 \qquad
 F_\rho(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\rho}A_{\rho,k}z^k. \tag{27}
\]

Then \(F_\rho(1)=b_\rho\equiv0\pmod p\).  Compute the first two logarithmic derivatives

\[
 H_1(p,\rho)=\sum_{k=0}^{\rho}kA_{\rho,k}\pmod p,
\]

\[
 H_2(p,\rho)=\sum_{k=0}^{\rho}k^2A_{\rho,k}\pmod p, \tag{28}
\]

and normalize

\[
 \theta_{a,i}=H_a(p_i,\rho_i)u_i^{-1}\pmod{p_i},
 \qquad a=1,2. \tag{29}
\]

The summands can be generated in \(O(\rho)\) modular operations from

\[
 \frac{A_{\rho,k+1}}{A_{\rho,k}}
 =\left(\frac{(\rho-k)(\rho+k+1)}{(k+1)^2}\right)^2. \tag{30}
\]

Before treating (28) as new, reduce it against known contiguous and recurrence identities symbolically at small indices.  Any universal expression in \(b_{\rho-1},b_\rho,b_{\rho+1}\) is already part of the recurrence and must be discarded.

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  It specifies marked residues but not the cancellation geometry of the Apéry hypergeometric summands.

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  The signal is a rank drop or a fixed low-degree relation among

\[
 q_i,\rho_i,\tau_i,\eta_i,\theta_{1,i},\theta_{2,i},B_{q_i},D_{q_i} \tag{31}
\]

that survives independent dyads.  This would point to a differentiated Hasse--Franel or creative-telescoping identity.  Computing \(H_1,H_2\) only for \(K\ge4\) rows, all rows flagged by invariants 1--6, and a deterministic matched control sample keeps this second tier cheap.

## 8. Universal small-coefficient annihilator

This is the disciplined way to search for a low-degree algebraic relation without interpolating one separately in every characteristic.

Fix **before examining the rare rows** a small monomial dictionary.  For example, take total scalar degree at most two in

\[
 q,\rho,\tau,\eta,\theta_1,\theta_2
\]

and a fixed homogeneous degree one or two in the projective pair

\[
 (X,Y)=(B_q,D_q). \tag{32}
\]

For each hit \(i\), evaluate the dictionary to a row vector

\[
 \Phi_i\in\mathbf F_{p_i}^{d}. \tag{33}
\]

Search for a **single** primitive vector \(c\in\mathbf Z^d\), with a preregistered coefficient bound, such that

\[
 \Phi_i\cdot c\equiv0\pmod{p_i} \tag{34}
\]

for every training hit of every training row.  Algebraically, compute short vectors in the exact lattice

\[
 \Lambda=\{c\in\mathbf Z^d:\Phi_i c\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\text{ for all }i\}. \tag{35}
\]

This can be built incrementally by Hermite/Smith normal form and reduced by LLL.  Validate every candidate, without refitting, on later prime dyads and on matched controls.  Prefer homogeneous forms in \((B_q,D_q)\) so that exceptional cases \(D_q=0\) do not require division.

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  The relation uses actual recurrence, \(p^2\), and hypergeometric labels.  None is part of the star axioms.

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  One nonzero coefficient vector with very small coefficients, exact zero on two or more held-out dyads, and failure on generic actual \(K=2\) controls is enough to justify symbolic elimination.  A relation found only after raising the degree until the number of monomials exceeds the number of samples is interpolation and must be rejected.

## 9. Small-height simultaneous CRT/rational lift

Let \(\ell_i\) be one nontrivial local label, preferably \(	au_i\), \(\eta_i\), or a homogeneous quotient-state label.  Put

\[
 P_n=\prod_{p_i\in\mathcal H(n)}p_i.
\]

CRT always produces \(T\pmod{P_n}\) with

\[
 T\equiv\ell_i\pmod{p_i}. \tag{36}
\]

That existence is **tautological**.  The nontrivial invariant is the minimum rational height

\[
 h_n(\ell)=\min
 \left\{
 \max(|A|,|C|):
 \begin{array}{l}
 C\ne0,\ \gcd(C,P_n)=1,\\
 A\equiv C\ell_i\pmod{p_i}\ \forall i
 \end{array}
 \right\}. \tag{37}
\]

Ordinary rational reconstruction naturally finds heights near \(P_n^{1/2}\), so that scale is not evidence.  Flag only a preregistered gap such as

\[
 h_n(\ell)\le P_n^{1/2-\delta} \tag{38}
\]

for fixed \(\delta>0\), and then require a repeated explicit formula for \(A\) and \(C\) in terms of \(n,q_i,ho_i\).

**Why an anchored star does not automatically satisfy it.**  Arbitrary independently assigned labels have a CRT lift but do not generally have a substantially sub-square-root lift governed by one formula.

**Theorem-worthy empirical signature.**  The same rational function of the small digits reproduces the labels on held-out rows.  Once one proves a height bound \(2|A|,2|C|<P_n\), the congruences can be promoted to an integer identity.  A small lift on one isolated \(K=3\) row is not meaningful.

# Tautology firewall

Every reported candidate should pass the following fail-closed filters.

## CRT tautologies

The relations

\[
 n=q_ip_i+r_i,
 \qquad
 q_ip_i-q_jp_j=r_j-r_i \tag{39}
\]

and every polynomial consequence of them are automatic for arbitrary residue sets.  Formally reduce candidate polynomials in \(n,p_i,q_i,r_i\) modulo the ideal

\[
 I_{\mathrm{CRT}}=\langle n-q_ip_i-r_i:i=1,\ldots,K\rangle. \tag{40}
\]

If the remainder is zero, reject the candidate.  A fast practical check is to evaluate it on random synthetic CRT rows with arbitrary residues before spending any Apéry computation.

The uniqueness of the pairwise CRT representative below \(X^2\) is an enumeration tool, not an arithmetic invariant.

## Reflection tautologies

The facts

\[
 b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p
\]

and the associated return

\[
 N_{p-1-2r}(r)\equiv0\pmod p \tag{41}
\]

are already compatible with an anchored star.  Do not count them, or their products, as new carriers.

## Lucas tautologies

For a hit,

\[
 b_n\equiv b_{q_i}b_{r_i}\equiv0\pmod{p_i} \tag{42}
\]

is automatic because \(b_{r_i}=0\).  The neighboring splice

\[
 b_{q_ip_i+r_i\pm1}\equiv b_{q_i}b_{r_i\pm1}\pmod{p_i} \tag{43}
\]

is also just Lucas when there is no digit carry.  Neither constrains \(b_{q_i}\) by itself.

## Local recurrence and companion tautologies

Equation (5) determines the nearest-neighbor ratio at every simple zero.  Equation (3) determines the companion multiplier after a return is already known.  This is exactly the no-go verified in `distinguished_label_verify.py`.  Only a new collision, a \(p^2\) lift, a hypergeometric tangent, or a cross-characteristic carrier survives this filter.

## CRT label tautology

Every tuple of local labels has a simultaneous CRT representative.  Candidate 9 is nontrivial only through a substantially small lift plus a repeated formula.

# Exact scalable enumeration

Let

\[
 M=X^2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
 \qquad
 S=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}|Z_p|.
\]

The computation has four phases.

## Phase A: exact zero sets

For every \(p\in\mathcal P_X\), run the division-free recurrence for \(B_m\bmod p\) through \(m=p-1\), recording the full sorted zero list \(Z_p\).  Reuse the Montgomery and forward-difference hot loop from `CRON_zp_bigscan.c`, but use the `PairVec`-style output from `CRON_garqi_moments.c` so the residues, not merely the count, are retained.

The exact recurrence work is

\[
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}(p-2), \tag{44}
\]

not \(M\,\pi(X)\).  Independently verify a deterministic set of primes with the original divided recurrence, and verify reflection and absence of adjacent zeros at every prime.

## Phase B: sparse extraction of all rows with \(K\ge3\)

After the zero sets are known, compute the exact prospective costs of two complete methods.

### Method B1: pair-CRT external sort

For each active pair \(p<q\), compute \(p^{-1}\bmod q\).  For every \(r\in Z_p\), \(s\in Z_q\), compute

\[
 a=r+p\left((s-r)p^{-1}\bmod q\right),
 \qquad 0\le a<pq. \tag{45}
\]

Since \(pq>X^2=M\), this residue pair has at most one representative below \(M\).  Emit the compact record

\[
 (a,\operatorname{id}(p),\operatorname{id}(q)) \tag{46}
\]

only if \(a<M\).

The number of residue-pair tests is exactly

\[
 C_2=\sum_{p<q}|Z_p||Z_q|
 =\frac{S^2-\sum_p|Z_p|^2}{2}. \tag{47}
\]

Bucket records by high bits of \(a\), then radix-sort each bucket by \(a\).  A row hit by \(K\) primes produces exactly \(\binom K2\) pair records.  In each equal-\(a\) group:

1. take the union of prime IDs;
2. assert that the edge set is the complete clique;
3. recompute \(a\bmod p\in Z_p\) for every ID;
4. retain the row iff the union has size at least three.

The clique assertion is an excellent fail-closed corruption check.

### Method B2: sparse hit-event external sort

For every \(p\) and \(r\in Z_p\), emit

\[
 (r+tp,\operatorname{id}(p)),
 \qquad 0\le r+tp<M. \tag{48}
\]

The exact number of emitted records is

\[
 E_1=\sum_p\sum_{r\in Z_p}
 \left(1+\left\lfloor\frac{M-1-r}{p}\right\rfloor\right). \tag{49}
\]

External-sort by the first coordinate and retain groups with at least three distinct prime IDs.

Choose B1 when \(C_2\) is smaller and B2 when \(E_1\) is smaller.  Both methods are exact and neither allocates an array indexed by \(0,\ldots,M-1\).  At \(X=10^5\), \(M=10^{10}\), so all row indices and products must be 64-bit; aggregate counters should be 128-bit.

If the selected exact record count exceeds an operational cap, abort with the computed \(C_2,E_1\) and no partial statistical claim.  Do not sample rows silently.

## Phase C: enrich only the rare rows

For every retained row:

1. compute all \(q_i,r_i,ho_i\);
2. build \(Q_{ij}\) and \(R_{ij}\) by binary search in the stored zero lists;
3. build the digit pool \(S(n)\);
4. for every hit characteristic, request recurrence states only at the needed small indices;
5. compute the collision tensor, continuant supports, and quotient-generated return tests;
6. batch all \(p^2\)-lift requests by prime and make one second recurrence pass per used prime;
7. compute the hypergeometric tangents only in the second tier.

The state requests should be batched by characteristic.  For each used \(p\), collect and sort all indices required by all high-load rows involving \(p\), then run \(B,D\bmod p\) once and sample at those indices.  Because every requested \(q_i,ho_i<X<p\), this is at most one additional \(O(p)\) pass per used prime, not one pass per row.

For (19), request the valid endpoints \(ho_i\pm h\) and compare their sampled projective states with the state at \(ho_i\).  This avoids a separate continuant recurrence for every edge; (3) supplies the exact certificate.

For the \(p^2\) pass, use unsigned 128-bit multiplication with modulus \(p^2\).  Record \(B_s/p\bmod p\) only after asserting \(B_s\equiv0\pmod p\).  No large Apéry integer is needed.

## Phase D: controls and relation search

Keep deterministic hash-selected controls from:

* actual \(K=2\) pair-CRT rows, matched by prime sizes, \(Z_p\) sizes, row decile, and digit ranges;
* de-anchored tuples formed from actual Apéry zero sets at the same primes;
* non-high-load rows flagged by one local invariant but not by the others.

Split prime dyads into discovery and validation sets before fitting candidate 8.  Store the full monomial dictionary, coefficient bound, lattice basis, candidate coefficient vector, and validation residues.  A relation may be promoted only if no refitting occurs on the validation dyads.

# Complexity

With external/radix sorting, the core exact work is

\[
 O\!\left(
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}p
 +\min(C_2,E_1)
 +\operatorname{Sort}(A)
 +R_{\mathrm{enrich}}
 \right), \tag{50}
\]

where \(A\le C_2\) for pair-CRT enumeration or \(A=E_1\) for hit-event enumeration, and \(R_{\mathrm{enrich}}\) is proportional to the number of requested recurrence states and second-tier summands.  There is no \(O(X^2\pi(X))\) loop and no \(X^2\)-sized memory object.

using u32 = std::uint32_t;
using u64 = std::uint64_t;
using u128 = __uint128_t;

struct PrimeData {
    u32 p;
    std::vector<u32> zeros;       // sorted, complete Z_p
};

struct PairRec {
    u64 n;
    u32 a, b;                     // prime-table indices, a < b
};

struct Row {
    u64 n;
    std::vector<u32> ids;
};

struct State {
    u64 B, D;
};

static inline u64 mul_mod(u64 a, u64 b, u64 mod) {
    return static_cast<u64>((static_cast<u128>(a) * b) % mod);
}

static u64 pow_mod(u64 a, unsigned e, u64 mod) {
    u64 out = 1 % mod;
    while (e) {
        if (e & 1U) out = mul_mod(out, a, mod);
        a = mul_mod(a, a, mod);
        e >>= 1U;
    }
    return out;
}

static u64 P_mod(u64 m, u64 mod) {
    const u64 x = m % mod;
    const u64 x2 = mul_mod(x, x, mod);
    const u64 x3 = mul_mod(x2, x, mod);
    u64 out = 0;
    out = (out + mul_mod(34 % mod, x3, mod)) % mod;
    out = (out + mul_mod(51 % mod, x2, mod)) % mod;
    out = (out + mul_mod(27 % mod, x, mod)) % mod;
    return (out + 5) % mod;
}

static inline u64 sub_mod(u64 a, u64 b, u64 mod) {
    return a >= b ? a - b : a + mod - b;
}

// Reference kernel.  Replace by the checked Montgomery finite-difference
// loop from CRON_zp_bigscan.c in production.
static std::vector<u32> zero_set(u32 p) {
    std::vector<u32> z;
    u64 prev = 1 % p, cur = 5 % p;
    for (u64 m = 1; m + 1 < p; ++m) {
        const u64 m3 = pow_mod(m, 3, p);
        const u64 m6 = mul_mod(m3, m3, p);
        const u64 next = sub_mod(mul_mod(P_mod(m, p), cur, p),
                                 mul_mod(m6, prev, p), p);
        if (next == 0) z.push_back(static_cast<u32>(m + 1));
        prev = cur;
        cur = next;
    }
    return z;
}

static std::int64_t egcd(std::int64_t a, std::int64_t b,
                         std::int64_t &x, std::int64_t &y) {
    if (b == 0) { x = 1; y = 0; return a; }
    std::int64_t x1 = 0, y1 = 0;
    const std::int64_t g = egcd(b, a % b, x1, y1);
    x = y1;
    y = x1 - (a / b) * y1;
    return g;
}

static u64 inv_mod(u64 a, u64 mod) {
    std::int64_t x = 0, y = 0;
    if (egcd(static_cast<std::int64_t>(a),
             static_cast<std::int64_t>(mod), x, y) != 1)
        throw std::runtime_error("noninvertible CRT modulus");
    x %= static_cast<std::int64_t>(mod);
    if (x < 0) x += static_cast<std::int64_t>(mod);
    return static_cast<u64>(x);
}

// For a large run, send each record directly to an n-bucket file rather than
// retaining the whole vector.
static std::vector<PairRec>
enumerate_pair_records(const std::vector<PrimeData> &P, u64 M) {
    std::vector<PairRec> out;
    for (u32 i = 0; i < P.size(); ++i) {
        if (P[i].zeros.empty()) continue;
        for (u32 j = i + 1; j < P.size(); ++j) {
            if (P[j].zeros.empty()) continue;
            const u64 p = P[i].p, q = P[j].p;
            const u64 pinv = inv_mod(p % q, q);
            for (u32 rp : P[i].zeros) {
                for (u32 rq : P[j].zeros) {
                    const u64 diff = (rq + q - (rp % q)) % q;
                    const u64 t = mul_mod(diff, pinv, q);
                    const u64 n = static_cast<u64>(rp) + p * t;
                    if (n < M) out.push_back({n, i, j});
                }
            }
        }
    }
    return out;
}

static std::vector<Row>
decode_rows(std::vector<PairRec> recs,
            const std::vector<PrimeData> &P) {
    std::sort(recs.begin(), recs.end(), [](const PairRec &x,
                                           const PairRec &y) {
        if (x.n != y.n) return x.n < y.n;
        if (x.a != y.a) return x.a < y.a;
        return x.b < y.b;
    });

    std::vector<Row> rows;
    for (std::size_t lo = 0; lo < recs.size();) {
        std::size_t hi = lo + 1;
        while (hi < recs.size() && recs[hi].n == recs[lo].n) ++hi;
        if (hi - lo >= 3) {
            std::vector<u32> ids;
            std::set<std::pair<u32,u32>> edges;
            for (std::size_t k = lo; k < hi; ++k) {
                ids.push_back(recs[k].a);
                ids.push_back(recs[k].b);
                if (!edges.emplace(recs[k].a, recs[k].b).second)
                    throw std::runtime_error("duplicate pair record");
            }
            std::sort(ids.begin(), ids.end());
            ids.erase(std::unique(ids.begin(), ids.end()), ids.end());
            const u64 K = ids.size();
            if (edges.size() != K * (K - 1) / 2)
                throw std::runtime_error("non-clique equal-n group");

            for (u32 id : ids) {
                const u32 r = static_cast<u32>(recs[lo].n % P[id].p);
                if (!std::binary_search(P[id].zeros.begin(),
                                        P[id].zeros.end(), r))
                    throw std::runtime_error("row/zero-set mismatch");
            }
            if (K >= 3) rows.push_back({recs[lo].n, std::move(ids)});
        }
        lo = hi;
    }
    return rows;
}

// Sample both standard cleared solutions at a sorted set of requested indices.
static std::unordered_map<u32, State>
sample_states(u32 p, std::vector<u32> req) {
    std::sort(req.begin(), req.end());
    req.erase(std::unique(req.begin(), req.end()), req.end());
    if (!req.empty() && req.back() >= p)
        throw std::runtime_error("state request wraps modulo p");

    std::unordered_map<u32, State> out;
    std::size_t at = 0;
    auto emit = [&](u32 index, u64 B, u64 D) {
        while (at < req.size() && req[at] == index) {
            out.emplace(index, State{B, D});
            ++at;
        }
    };

    u64 Bprev = 1 % p, Bcur = 5 % p;
    u64 Dprev = 0,     Dcur = 1 % p;
    emit(0, Bprev, Dprev);
    emit(1, Bcur, Dcur);

    const u32 stop = req.empty() ? 0 : req.back();
    for (u64 m = 1; m < stop; ++m) {
        const u64 m3 = pow_mod(m, 3, p);
        const u64 m6 = mul_mod(m3, m3, p);
        const u64 Bnext = sub_mod(mul_mod(P_mod(m, p), Bcur, p),
                                  mul_mod(m6, Bprev, p), p);
        const u64 Dnext = sub_mod(mul_mod(P_mod(m, p), Dcur, p),
                                  mul_mod(m6, Dprev, p), p);
        Bprev = Bcur; Bcur = Bnext;
        Dprev = Dcur; Dcur = Dnext;
        emit(static_cast<u32>(m + 1), Bcur, Dcur);
    }
    if (at != req.size()) throw std::runtime_error("unsatisfied request");
    return out;
}

static u64 state_minor(const State &x, const State &y, u64 p) {
    return sub_mod(mul_mod(x.B, y.D, p),
                   mul_mod(x.D, y.B, p), p);
}

Each collision edge should contain `(p,s,t,type,minor)` and, when the minor is zero, the gap \(h=t-s\).  A separate verifier should recompute \(N_h(s)\bmod p\) directly from (2), so the state-minor and continuant implementations cross-check each other.

The aggregate report should emphasize:

1. exact carrier records and their supported primes;

4. exact failures of preregistered implications;

A small moment summary may be retained as a regression check, but it is not the scientific output.

# Gates for launching a theorem attack

Finite data should trigger a proof effort only through one of the following concrete gates.

## Carrier gate

There is a fixed family of small-index integers \(b_q\), \(b_\rho\), or \(N_h(s)\) whose prime supports cover every observed high-load row, and the total recurrence-height cost is small enough that (8), (10), or (16) could contradict an explicit height bound.

## Universal-relation gate

One primitive low-degree integer polynomial with small coefficients vanishes exactly in at least two held-out prime dyads, without refitting, and is not in the CRT/reflection/Lucas/recurrence ideal.

## Deterministic-implication gate

The data repeatedly support a statement with theorem-ready quantifiers, such as (20), or a finite list of state-collision motifs covers every \(K\ge4\) row.  The next computation should then seek a counterexample over a larger disjoint dyad, not estimate a frequency.

## Integer-lift gate

A local label has a substantially sub-square-root simultaneous rational lift and the numerator/denominator are identified as explicit low-degree functions.  The proof problem becomes a height estimate promoting the congruence to equality.

No observed near-Poisson ratio, maximum \(K\), motif frequency, or regression slope by itself supports an asymptotic conclusion.  The experiment is successful even if all nine candidates fail: a fail-closed catalogue of exact failures would sharply rule out the current quotient/state/p-adic feature class and prevent another generic moment-table cycle.
