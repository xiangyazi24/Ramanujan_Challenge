 =\deg\operatorname{rad}

\[
 p\mid\mathcal R_{d,e}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 m^{\rm geom}_{d,e}(p)>0

 \begin{array}{c}
 \text{there are a finite }k/\mathbb F_p\text{ and }x\in k\\

The final equivalence uses only that every algebraic element over a finite field lies in a finite extension.  Thus the “nonsplit resultant false positive” from the \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational problem becomes a genuine witness under the quantifier in Q4528.

\[
 (x,d,e)\longmapsto(x,d,d+e)

is injective.

e0\), and the Bezout identity gives

The difference \(f-d\) is at least two by no-adjacent-zeros.  Hence \((x,d,f-d)\) is an energy witness.  These constructions are inverse.  Counting distinct geometric roots proves (5.1).  No root-simplicity assumption is used.

# 6. New exact theorem: transition-energy bootstrap

The following is the main reusable result of this audit.

## Theorem (short-gap transition energy controls a column)

Assume \(p\nmid\mathfrak U_H\).  Let \(k/\mathbb F_p\) be finite, \(z\in k\), and let \(I\) be an interval of \(L\le H\) consecutive levels.  Put

\[
 K=\#\{r\in I:N_r(z)=0\}.
\]

For every integer \(D\ge2\), with \(2D\le L\),

\[
 \boxed{
 K\le \frac{2L}{D+1}
       +\mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(2D)+3.}
 \tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

Write the zero levels in increasing order as

\[
 r_1<r_2<\cdots<r_K
\]

and their consecutive gaps as

\[
 g_i=r_{i+1}-r_i\ge2.
\]

Let \(b\) be the number of gaps \(g_i>D\).  Since the total span is less than \(L\),

\[
 b\le\frac{L}{D+1}.
\]

The gaps at most \(D\) form at most \(b+1\) runs.  Let \(T\) be the number of adjacent pairs of short gaps,

\[
 T=\#\{i:g_i\le D,\ g_{i+1}\le D\}.
\]

A run of \(s\) short gaps contributes \(s-1\) to \(T\).  Hence the total number of short gaps is at most \(T+b+1\), and

\[
 K-1\le b+(T+b+1),
\]

so

\[
 K\le T+2b+2.                                      \tag{6.2}
\]

For every transition counted by \(T\), put

\[
 d=g_i,\qquad e=g_{i+1},\qquad x_i=z+r_i.
\]

Restart at \(r_i\) and then at \(r_i+d\).  The no-adjacent-zero theorem gives the required nonzero restart amplitudes, and therefore

\[
 N_d(x_i)=0,
 \qquad
 N_e(x_i+d)=0.
\]

Thus \(x_i\) is a geometric common root for the pair \((d,e)\), with \(d+e\le2D\).

For a fixed pair \((d,e)\), two different transitions have different starts \(x_i\).  Indeed their level starts differ by a nonzero integer of absolute value less than \(L\le H<p\), so they remain distinct modulo \(p\).  Consequently

\[
 T\le\mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(2D).
\]

Combining this with (6.2) proves (6.1).

## Corollary (exact exponent conversion)

Assume that for some \(0<\delta<2\) and \(A\ge0\), uniformly at every scale \(D\le H\),

\[
 \mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(D)
 \ll D^{2-\delta}(\log H)^A.
\]

Then

\[
 K
 \ll
 L^{\frac{2-\delta}{3-\delta}}
 (\log H)^{\frac{A}{3-\delta}}
 +1.                                                \tag{6.3}
\]

Indeed, optimize (6.1) with

\[
 D\asymp
 L^{1/(3-\delta)}
 (\log H)^{-A/(3-\delta)}.
\]

Since

\[
 \frac{2-\delta}{3-\delta}
 =\frac23-\frac{\delta}{3(3-\delta)},
\]

this proves the requested shape with every fixed

\[
 \eta<\frac{\delta}{3(3-\delta)}
\]

after absorbing the displayed logarithm into a slightly smaller power for large \(L\), and into the permitted polylogarithmic term for small \(L\).

In particular:

\[
 \mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(D)\ll D^{3/2+o(1)}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 K\ll L^{3/5+o(1)},
\]

which gives every fixed \(\eta<1/15\).

Even a theorem

\[

The finite computations in the source, where common-root multiplicities are usually at most two, therefore do not imply any uniform exponent.

is false.  Any proof must retain the root/branch label; scalar norm gcds cannot do it.

## 8.3 Three-polynomial gcds miss singleton branches

For fixed \(d\), write

\[
 q_{d,x}=\#\{e:N_e(x+d)=0\}
\]

on roots \(x\) of \(N_d\).  Then pair energy is

\[
 \sum_x q_{d,x},
\]

whereas aligned three-polynomial gcds measure

\[
 \sum_x\binom{q_{d,x}}2.
\]

Perfect control of the latter does not control branches with \(q_{d,x}=1\).  This is an exact first-moment/second-factorial-moment mismatch internal to the polynomial branches, not a Palm-measure issue.

## 8.4 Codegree and Kővári–Sós–Turán do not settle multiplicity

The source’s bipartite graph on gap labels records whether a pair has at least one common root.  A graph support bound does not count multiple root witnesses.  Conversely, an aligned codegree bound may miss singleton components.  `gcdtail_result.tex` constructs an exact affine-plane incidence model satisfying:

- no adjacent levels;
- the full local \(L^{2/3}\) column cap;
- row degree \(O(h)\);
- pair codegree one;

while still carrying \(\asymp D^2\) deep collision triples.  This is not an actual Apéry construction, but it proves that these interfaces alone cannot yield \((\mathrm{GE})_\delta\).

## 8.5 Plancherel and orbit reformulations stop at the same second moment

The source’s exact involution formula rewrites separated collisions as intersections of reflected root sets.  Cauchy–Schwarz then gives the column second moment, which is \(D^{8/3}\) with current inputs.  The transfer-matrix formulation says a return block preserves a projective line; products of such blocks remain in a Borel stabilizer.  Restart is the cocycle law for these products.  No available nonconcentration theorem for the deterministic Apéry cocycle turns this bookkeeping into a subquadratic incidence bound.

 \ll X^{\frac{28-10\delta}{17-6\delta}+o(1)}.}
 \tag{9.4}
\]

Examples:

\[
 \delta=\frac12:
 \quad \theta=\frac35,
 \quad \gamma=\frac{23}{5},
 \quad \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}=\frac{23}{14};
\]

\[
 \delta=1:
 \quad \theta=\frac12,
 \quad \gamma=\frac92,
 \quad \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}=\frac{18}{11}.
\]

There is an important strategic ceiling.  Since a column count has \(	heta\ge0\), this branch-reuse architecture always has \(\gamma\ge4\).  Even a polylogarithmic column bound merely reaches

\[
 \gamma=4,
 \qquad
 \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}=\frac85.
\]

Therefore:

- any fixed \(\eta>0\) would lower the new \(14/3\) exponent;
- no improvement of the column exponent alone can beat the already existing \(8/5\) theorem through this branch-reuse ledger;
- beating \(8/5\) requires compression of the \(H^4\) branch baseline, not only a better column cap.

# 10. Standing checks

## 10.1 Equivalence check

The geometric energy premise \((\mathrm{GE})_\delta\) is **not** a disguised restatement of the desired column bound.

It is strictly stronger: it charges every algebraic root in every column and every separated gap pair, including roots that are irrelevant to a chosen \(z\).  A system may have only two returns in each column, so the pointwise column theorem is trivial, while \(\asymp D^2\) different columns each carry one pair and the global geometric energy is quadratic.  Thus the column theorem does not imply \((\mathrm{GE})_\delta\).

Conversely, the transition theorem proves that scale-uniform \((\mathrm{GE})_\delta\) implies the column improvement.  This is a genuine sufficient arithmetic premise, not an interpolation certificate chosen after seeing the bad column.

A “local energy” defined only from the transitions of the selected \(z\) would collapse to the target and would not be a new mechanism.  The support-blind global geometric energy avoids that collapse.

## 10.2 Packaging check

The following objects must not be conflated:

1. scalar resultant support \(p\mid\mathcal R_{d,e}\);
2. split \(\mathbb F_p\)-root count using \(X^p-X\);
3. geometric radical gcd degree over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\);
4. p-adic intersection length/content valuation.

For Q4528, item 3 is the correct distinct-witness object.  Item 1 loses the root component and multiplicity.  Item 2 loses nonsplit roots and therefore is not extension-uniform.  Item 4 is stronger and is relevant to aligned-content valuation, but repeated/intersection multiplicity is not needed to count distinct zero levels.

## 10.3 Start and gap multiplicity check

In the proof of (6.1), starts are charged independently only after retaining the triple \((d,e,x)\).  For fixed \((d,e)\), distinct transitions have distinct \(x\), because their integer start difference is nonzero and smaller than \(p\).  No independence between different gap pairs is assumed.  Reuse of one \(x\) by many pairs is exactly what \(\mathcal E^{\rm geom}\) counts.

## 10.4 Finite evidence check

The examples at \(71,1297,5683,7411,\ldots\) are exact finite counterexamples to proposed structural implications.  They are not an asymptotic family and do not refute a fixed positive \(\eta\).  Conversely, the small collision energies observed computationally are not used as a theorem.

The following can be inserted after the finite-extension column theorem.

```latex
\begin{definition}[Geometric separated-block energy]
For a prime $p\nmid\mathfrak U_H$ and $D\le H$, put
\[
 m^{\rm geom}_{d,e}(p)
 =\deg\operatorname{rad}\gcd_{\mathbb F_p[X]}
   \bigl(\overline N_d(X),\overline N_e(X+d)\bigr)
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(D)
 =\sum_{\substack{d,e\ge2\\d+e\le D}}
   m^{\rm geom}_{d,e}(p).
\]
Thus $m^{\rm geom}_{d,e}(p)$ is the number of distinct
common roots over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$.  Since only finitely
many roots occur, $\mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(D)$ is also the maximum
of the corresponding energy over finite extensions of $\mathbb F_p$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Clean geometric column identity]
\label{lem:geom-column-energy}
Assume $p\nmid\mathfrak U_D$ and put
\[
 k_x(D)=\#\{2\le h\le D:\overline N_h(x)=0\},
 \qquad x\in\overline{\mathbb F}_p.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(D)
 =\sum_{x\in\overline{\mathbb F}_p}\binom{k_x(D)}2.
\]
No separability or simple-root hypothesis is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The continuant addition law maps a witness
$N_d(x)=N_e(x+d)=0$ to the two zero levels $d,d+e$.
Conversely, if $N_d(x)=N_f(x)=0$ with $d<f$, then
$S_d(x)\ne0$: otherwise $x=-j$ for some $2\le j\le d$,
and the endpoint factorization of $N_d(-j)$ contradicts
$p\nmid\mathfrak U_D$.  The saturated Bezout identity then gives
$N_{f-d}(x+d)=0$.  No adjacent zero levels occur, so $f-d\ge2$.
The two constructions are inverse on distinct geometric roots.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Short-gap transition-energy inequality]
\label{lem:transition-energy}
Let $k/\mathbb F_p$ be finite, $z\in k$, and let $I$ be an interval
of $L\le H$ consecutive levels.  If
\[
 K_I(z)=\#\{r\in I:N_r(z)=0\},
\]
then for every $D\ge2$ with $2D\le L$,
\[
 K_I(z)\le \frac{2L}{D+1}+\mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(2D)+3.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write the zero levels as $r_1<\cdots<r_K$ and put
$g_i=r_{i+1}-r_i$.  At most $L/(D+1)$ gaps exceed $D$.
The remaining gaps form at most one more run than the number of long
gaps.  Hence
\[
 K\le2L/(D+1)+T+3,
\]
where $T$ is the number of indices with $g_i,g_{i+1}\le D$.
For each such index, restart at $r_i$ and at $r_i+g_i$ to obtain
\[
 N_{g_i}(z+r_i)=0,
 \qquad
 N_{g_{i+1}}(z+r_i+g_i)=0.
\]
For fixed $(g_i,g_{i+1})$, distinct transitions have distinct starts,
since $p>H\ge L$.  Therefore $T\le\mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(2D)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Energy saving implies a column saving]
\label{cor:geom-energy-column-saving}
Suppose that for some $0<\delta<2$ and $A\ge0$ one has, uniformly at
every scale $D\le H$,
\[
 \mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(D)
 \ll D^{2-\delta}(\log H)^A.
\]
Then
\[
 K_I(z)
 \ll
 L^{(2-\delta)/(3-\delta)}
 (\log H)^{A/(3-\delta)}+1.
\]
In particular, after absorbing logarithms, every
\[
 \eta<\frac{\delta}{3(3-\delta)}
\]
is admissible in
$K_I(z)\ll L^{2/3-\eta}+\operatorname{polylog}H$.
\end{corollary}
```

```latex
\begin{openproblem}[Geometric affine gcd tail]
\label{op:geom-gcd-tail}
Do there exist absolute constants $\delta>0$ and $A\ge0$ such that,
for every $H$, every prime $p\nmid\mathfrak U_H$, and every
$D\le H$,
\[
 \sum_{j\ge1}
 \#\left\{\begin{array}{c}
 (d,e):d,e\ge2,\ d+e\le D,\\[1mm]
 \deg\operatorname{rad}\gcd_{\mathbb F_p[X]}
  (\overline N_d(X),\overline N_e(X+d))\ge j
 \end{array}\right\}
 \ll D^{2-\delta}(\log H)^A?
\]
\end{openproblem}
```

The layer-cake sum in this open problem is exactly \(\mathcal E^{\rm geom}_p(D)\).  It is the arbitrary-extension analogue of the split affine gcd-tail in `energy_result.tex`, and it is the first source-compatible new estimate that would prove a fixed positive column saving.
