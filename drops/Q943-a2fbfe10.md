## Verdict

The requested linear lower bound is not proved from the stated identities alone. The correct conclusion is a scoped reduction to an exact arithmetic noncancellation lemma; a proof of that lemma is still required before claiming the boundary no-go.

## Exact moment reduction

For $P(x)=u+vx$, the finite-difference constraint is

$$uS_0+vS_1=0,$$

where

$$S_i=\sum_{m=0}^H(-1)^m\binom Hm\frac{m^i}{A_m}.$$

Hence the rational shape is fixed up to scale:

$$P(x)=\lambda(S_1-S_0x).$$

The value at the boundary point is therefore

$$P(3H+1)=\lambda(S_1-(3H+1)S_0).$$

Any lower bound must control both the denominator-clearing scale $\lambda$ and the possible cancellation in the bracket.

## Node divisibility

The integral construction gives

$$A_m\mid P(m),\qquad 0\le m\le H.$$

If $p\mid A_r$ and $n\equiv r\pmod p$, then because $P$ has integral coefficients,

$$P(n)-P(r)=(n-r)\sum_j c_j\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}n^{j-1-i}r^i,$$

so $p\mid P(n)-P(r)$. Since $p\mid P(r)$, we obtain

$$p\mid P(n).$$

Thus the support implication survives; it does not require the old node-value multiplier.

## Clearing scale and the unresolved point

Let $L_H$ be the least positive integer clearing all monomial coefficients of the rational interpolation polynomial. Then

$$\lambda=L_H,$$

up to the primitive normalization. An exact Smith/cofactor computation is needed because the primitive kernel divides this scale by the gcd of the relevant cofactors.

The sufficient estimate would follow from

$$\log L_H-\log g_H+\log|S_1-(3H+1)S_0|\ge cH-O(\log H).$$

The missing statement is therefore the following precise lemma:

> The primitive denominator clearing factor for the codimension-one Newton--Pade kernel has exponential size after all Smith gcd cancellations, and the boundary linear functional $S_1-(3H+1)S_0$ has no compensating exponentially small cancellation.

Neither the recurrence growth of $A_m$ nor the moment identity alone proves this.
