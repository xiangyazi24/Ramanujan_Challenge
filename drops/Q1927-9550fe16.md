# Gap-one top-coefficient arithmetic audit

## 1. Leading coefficient formula

For an even function on F_p represented by
\[
k(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1} a_jt^j,
\]
the coefficient of \(t^{p-1}\) is determined by the finite-field interpolation identity
\[
a_{p-1}=-\sum_{s\in\mathbf F_p}k(s).
\]
Because k is even and \(k(0)=-1\), this gives
\[
a=-k(0)-2\sum_{t=1}^{m}k(t)=1-6-2\sum_{t=2}^{m}\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{a=-5-2\sum_{t=2}^{m}\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}},\qquad
\boxed{\theta=\frac52+\sum_{t=2}^{m}\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}}.
\]
This is an exact identity; it does not imply either value is nonzero.

## 2. What follows from the ordinary polynomial identity

The function identity \(Hk=0\) means the polynomial representative of Hk is a multiple of \(t^p-t\). It is not legitimate to discard the wrap term. The top coefficient equations constrain the highest coefficients of k and the quotient polynomial Q satisfying
\[
Hk=(t^p-t)Q(t).
\]
The top-degree part of H does determine relations between the leading coefficient of k and Q, but the relation contains the quotient coefficients. Therefore the endpoint recurrence alone does not produce a closed equation forcing \(a\neq0\) or \(a\neq-6\).

For the corrected polynomial \(C=k-\theta d\), the definition removes the \(t^{p-1}\) term because d has coefficient \(-2\) there:
\[
[t^{p-1}]C=a+2\theta=0.
\]
Hence C has degree at most p-3.

## 3. The cases theta=0 and theta=3

The hypotheses give no purely formal contradiction for either value.

* \(\theta=0\) is equivalent to \(a=0\), namely
\[
1-6-2\sum_{t=2}^{m}\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}=0.
\]
No recurrence identity supplied forces this sum away from zero.

* \(\theta=3\) is equivalent to
\[
a=-6,
\]
or
\[
\sum_{t=2}^{m}\frac{u_t}{t^2-1}=\frac12.
\]
Again this is an arithmetic condition, not excluded by the stated endpoint equations alone.

The role of \(\theta=3\) is only that
\[
C(\pm1)=3-\theta
\]
vanishes, so any later cyclic inverse argument must split this case separately.

## 4. Conclusion

The exact top coefficient audit yields the formulas above. A proof of nonvanishing of \(\theta\) or exclusion of \(\theta=3\) requires an additional identity beyond the ordinary polynomial wrap equations. Finite examples cannot establish it. The remaining obstruction is an arithmetic identity for the reciprocal-weighted Apéry values, not a consequence of degree bookkeeping alone.
