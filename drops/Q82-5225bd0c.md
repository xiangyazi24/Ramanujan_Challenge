# Q82: gcd correlations of Apéry numbers and the Goldbach reformulation

## Executive conclusion

The proposed reduction is correct in spirit, but the crucial point is more subtle: the events

\[
(m-r)\mid b_r,\qquad (m-s)\mid b_s
\]

are not independent merely because the moduli are different primes. Different primes remove a CRT obstruction, but the recurrence still creates arithmetic correlations among the sequence values.

The right object to study is not \(\gcd(b_r,b_s)\) alone, but the joint distribution

\[
(b_r\bmod p, b_s\bmod q),\qquad p\neq q.
\]

A small gcd is evidence for independence, but it is not equivalent to it.

---

## 1. Why the naive CRT argument is incomplete

Let

\[
X_r=1_{p=m-r\ \mathrm{prime}}1_{p\mid b_r},
\]

and

\[
X_s=1_{q=m-s\ \mathrm{prime}}1_{q\mid b_s}.
\]

Since \(p\neq q\), the two congruences live in different rings:

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

\[
b_s\equiv0\pmod q.
\]

There is no direct CRT coupling. However, \(b_r\) and \(b_s\) are generated by the same second-order recurrence, so they are not independent random integers.

The required statement is therefore a recurrence mixing statement:

\[
\Pr(b_r=0\bmod p,\ b_s=0\bmod q)
\approx
\Pr(b_r=0\bmod p)\Pr(b_s=0\bmod q).
\]

---

## 2. The role of common factors

A large common factor of \(b_r,b_s\) can create correlations. The observed examples such as

\[
\gcd(b_{200},b_{193}),\ldots,\gcd(b_{200},b_{199})
\]

containing a fixed factor are not surprising: Apéry numbers satisfy strong divisibility patterns coming from Lucas-type congruences.

For example, if

\[
n=qp+r,
\]

then

\[
b_{qp+r}\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
\]

Therefore primes dividing earlier indices can reappear at many later indices.

This means the correct conjecture is not

\[
\gcd(b_r,b_s)=O(1),
\]

which is likely false.

The correct conjecture is an averaged one.

---

## 3. Correct averaged gcd conjecture

A useful target is:

\[
\sum_{0\le r<s\le N}\log\gcd(b_r,b_s)=O(N^2).
\]

Equivalently, for random \(r,s\le N\),

\[
\mathbb E(\log\gcd(b_r,b_s))=O(1).
\]

This allows exceptional pairs but rules out systematic correlations.

For the Goldbach second moment one only needs a weaker localized version:

\[
\sum_{r<s<m/2}
\frac{\gcd(b_r,b_s)^{\epsilon}}{(m-r)(m-s)}
=o(\log^2m).
\]

---

## 4. Why Apéry is different from binomial coefficients

For \(\binom{2n}{n}\), the zeros modulo primes are dense and come from a simple first-order digit structure.

For Apéry numbers:

1. the recurrence is second order;
2. the companion solution gives the Casoratian

\[
a_nb_{n+1}-a_{n+1}b_n=-\frac6{(n+1)^3};
\]

3. the finite-field orbit is projective and has sparse hitting times.

The same mechanism that gives \(|Z_p|\approx1\) should also suppress correlations, but it acts on the recurrence orbit rather than directly on integer gcds.

---

## 5. Concrete computational experiment

The requested computation should generate:

1. Apéry numbers \(b_0,\ldots,b_{200}\) using the recurrence.

2. For \(r=100\):

\[
\gcd(b_{100},b_s),
\quad s=0,10,20,\ldots,90.
\]

3. Random pairs \((r,s)\in[0,200]^2\), recording

\[
\log\gcd(b_r,b_s).
\]

The expected diagnostic is not that every gcd is 1. Instead we expect:

- many gcds equal to 1;
- occasional structured factors from small primes;
- no growth of typical \(\log\gcd\) with the index.

The important statistic is the mean and tail distribution, not individual examples.

---

## 6. A possible proof route

The strongest route is to replace gcd information by finite-field orbit mixing.

The recurrence gives

\[
v_{n+1}=M(n)v_n
\]

on \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\).

A joint condition

\[
p\mid b_r,
\qquad q\mid b_s
\]

is a simultaneous hitting event for two independent reductions:

\[
v_r^{(p)}=(1:0),
\qquad
v_s^{(q)}=(1:0).
\]

Thus the desired theorem should follow from a two-modulus mixing theorem:

\[
\sum_{r,s}
1_{v_r^{(p)}=0}1_{v_s^{(q)}=0}
=
\frac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}N^2+o(N^2).
\]

This is stronger and more natural than controlling \(\gcd(b_r,b_s)\).

---

## Final assessment

The gcd experiment is valuable, but the target should be changed.

The statement needed for the second moment is not:

\[
\gcd(b_r,b_s)\text{ is small for all }r,s.
\]

That is false because Apéry congruences create repeated small-prime factors.

The correct target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the finite-field recurrence orbits mix independently at distinct primes.}
}
\]

The observed sparse zero sets make this plausible. The next decisive computation is therefore not a gcd table alone, but measuring the correlation

\[
\#\{(r,s): b_r\equiv0\pmod p,\ b_s\equiv0\pmod q\}
\]

for many prime pairs \((p,q)\) and comparing against

\[
\frac{|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}.
\]

That directly tests the missing input of the Goldbach reformulation.
