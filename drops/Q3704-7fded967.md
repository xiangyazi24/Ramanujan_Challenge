# 2. Canonical transfer identity and the one-zero wrap lemma

For any solution `u_n` of the Apéry recurrence, put

\[
Q_h(x):=\prod_{j=2}^{h}(x+j)^3.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The exact transfer identity is

\[
\boxed{
Q_h(x)u_{x+h}
=N_h(x)u_{x+1}
-(x+1)^3N_{h-1}(x+1)u_x.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This follows either by induction or from the full transfer matrix

\[
\mathcal T_h(x)=
\begin{pmatrix}
N_{h+1}(x-1)&-x^3N_h(x)\\
(x+h)^3N_h(x-1)&-x^3(x+h)^3N_{h-1}(x)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

## 2.1 Six-side wrap

Let `p` be a quotient-six selected prime and

\[
r=m-6p,
\qquad
0<r<p-1,
\qquad
p\mid b_r.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The endpoint exclusions follow from `b_0=1` and reflection. Put

\[
t=p-1-r.
\tag{2.5}

If

\[
h\ge p-r=t+1,
\tag{2.6}
\]

then the factor with

\[
j=p-r
\]

occurs in `Q_h(r)`, so

\[
p\mid Q_h(r).
\tag{2.7}
\]

Reduce `(2.2)` for the Apéry solution modulo `p`. The left side vanishes by `(2.7)`, and the last term vanishes because `p|b_r`. Hence

\[
N_h(r)b_{r+1}\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{2.8}

The no-consecutive-zero theorem gives `p\nmid b_{r+1}`, therefore

\[
\boxed{p\mid N_h(r).}

Since `m\equiv r (mod p)` and `N_h` has integer coefficients,

\[
\boxed{p\mid N_h(m).}
\tag{2.10}

Taking `h=6d` proves `(0.7)`.

\boxed{p\mid N_h(-m-1).}

Taking `h=7d` proves `(0.8)`.

## 2.3 Why this is one condition, not two

The reflected zero `b_t=0 (mod p)` is the same rank-one local condition as `b_r=0 (mod p)`. The wrap lemma only transports that one condition across the first singular recurrence coefficient. It does not create a second independent Apéry zero equation.

\[
j_6=p-r=t+1,

and

\[
\boxed{m+j_6=7p.}
\tag{2.15}

On the seven side the first singular index is

\[
j_7=p-t=r+1,

\[
\boxed{(-m-1)+j_7=-6p.}

Once the first zero `N_h=0 (mod p)` is reached at a singular seam, the recurrence propagates it forward through the suffix. This is exactly why every larger `d` is automatically hit.

\boxed{13d>p-1\Longrightarrow

This is the exact OR geometry behind the large-gap wrap construction.

\[
g_a(X):=\gcd\bigl(N_a(X),N_{a-1}(X)\bigr).

\gcd(cA,B)\mid |c|\gcd(A,B),

# 4. Exact step-`s` addition identity

The continuant addition formula is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
N_{a+s}(X)
={}&N_a(X)N_{s+1}(X+a-1)\\
&-(X+a)^6N_{a-1}(X)N_s(X+a).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4.1}
\]

It is the path determinant decomposition at the cut after the first `a` steps.

Let

\[
G_{a,s}(X):=\gcd\bigl(N_a(X),N_{a+s}(X)\bigr).
\tag{4.2}
\]

A common divisor of the two left-hand values divides the second term of `(4.1)`, hence

\[
G_{a,s}(X)
\mid
(X+a)^6N_s(X+a)
\gcd\bigl(N_a(X),N_{a-1}(X)\bigr).
\]

Using `(3.5)`,

\[
\boxed{
G_{a,s}(X)
\mid
N_s(X+a)
\prod_{j=2}^{a}(X+j)^6.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This already says that a common factor between two step-`s` samples is either a boundary factor or a root of one fixed length-`s` continuant.

# 8. The smallest universal boundary radical

\tag{8.1}

\mathcal P_6(m,d_0)

\mathcal P_7(m,d_0)

\boxed{
\mathcal P_{\rm wrap}(m,d_0)
:=\operatorname{rad}\bigl(\mathcal P_6(m,d_0)\mathcal P_7(m,d_0)\bigr).

\tag{8.4}

These are completely selected-set-independent: they depend only on `m,d_0` and primality.

\frac m7<p\le
\min\left(\frac m6,\frac{m+6d_0}{7}\right)
\tag{8.5}

for the six seam, and

\max\left(\frac m7,\frac{m+1-7d_0}{6}\right)

\boxed{

# 10. Flagged Jacobi/adjugate data do not restore a second condition

One might try to retain a Jacobi cofactor or an exterior/adjugate flag before removing the boundary factor. The marked-Jacobi identity shows why this does not change the conclusion.

For the factorial-gauge Jacobi path `J_S`,

\[
\det J_S=(S!)^3b_S,
\tag{10.1}
\]

and its adjugate satisfies the exact integral saturation

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{adj}(J_S)
\equiv C_{0,S}\,\mathbf b\mathbf b^T
\pmod{b_S},
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

where `C_{0,S}` is a unit at every prime `p>S` dividing `b_S`.

Thus, on the selected determinant-zero fiber, the entire flagged cofactor matrix is rank one. Principal and off-diagonal cofactors reduce to unit multiples of products `b_rb_t`; they do not define a new transverse selected condition.

The wrap seam has the same local geometry. The transfer loses one direction because the off-diagonal coefficient is divisible by `p`; the zero propagated down the suffix is a one-dimensional defect. Exteriorizing or adjoining an adjugate flag can record that defect, but after saturating its universal boundary factor it cannot manufacture a second independent `p`-rank drop from the same single zero.

This is a **scoped no-go** only. It does not rule out a genuinely nonlinear Apéry invariant, a second independent p-adic integrality condition, or a cross-characteristic orbit not generated by the one-zero wrap module.

# 11. Height ledger for the suffix proposal

There are three distinct height statements, and they must not be conflated.

## 11.1 Raw suffix gcd: coverage, but only a boundary-scale upper bound

From `(7.3)`--`(7.4)`, in `T<m\le2T` with `d_0\asymp D`,

\[
\log|C_6|+\log|C_7|
\le O(D\log T)+O(1).
\tag{11.1}
\]

This is already much better than multiplying every continuant in the suffix, but it is not enough in aggregate. Across `O(T)` rows it gives

\[
O(TD\log T).
\tag{11.2}
\]

## 11.2 Boundary-saturated suffix gcd: excellent height, no selected coverage

Equation `(6.5)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\log|C_6^{(B\perp)}|+\log|C_7^{(B\perp)}|=O(1).
}
\tag{11.3}
\]

Across `T` rows this is `O(T)`, far below the requested scale. But every selected prime whose divisibility was supplied by the wrap lemma lies in the removed boundary set by `(7.6)` or `(7.8)`. So `(11.3)` is not a selected carrier.

## 11.3 Restored large selected-boundary radical: genuine coverage, `O(TD)` scale

The minimal obvious target-independent restoration is `\mathcal P_{\rm wrap}`. In the large-gap dyadic sector,

\[
\log\mathcal P_{\rm wrap}=O(D)
\]

per row and hence

\[
O(TD)=O(T^2)
\]

in aggregate. The actual selected subradical is `(9.4)` and can of course be much smaller—but proving the needed saving is exactly the arithmetic theorem still missing.

So there is no hidden positive `o(T^2/\log T)` theorem in the suffix-gcd algebra itself.

---

# 12. Why the fixed resultants do not rescue multiplicity

The only nonboundary obstruction in `(6.2)` is the fixed integer

\[
R_6R_7.
\tag{12.1}
\]

Its prime support is finite and independent of `m,d_0,T`. Those primes can be paid once and removed from the asymptotic problem.

Conversely, a resultant between the two wrap sides,

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X\bigl(N_{7d}(-X-1),N_{6d}(X)\bigr),
\tag{12.2}
\]

detects a common root of the two factors. The wrap edge supplies an **OR** condition—one side or the other is forced—so `(12.2)` does not give coverage.

A fixed-`d` resultant over row values similarly reproduces a product:
if

\[
H_I(X)=\prod_{m\in I}(X-m),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_X(H_I,G_d)
=\prod_{m\in I}G_d(m),
\qquad
G_d(X)=N_{7d}(-X-1)N_{6d}(X).
}
\tag{12.3}
\]

That is a correct determinant/Smith realization but no height reuse. The suffix gcd is genuinely better than this raw product; `(6.2)` explains exactly why. Unfortunately its saving comes by collapsing onto the same boundary seam that contains the selected prime.

# 13. Smallest remaining gate

The formal continuant part is now exhausted sharply enough that the missing theorem can be stated without the long continuants.

For a dyadic suffix/block define the prescribed squarefree seam product `\mathcal P_{\rm wrap}(m,D)` as in `(8.4)` (with the exact first-wrap bookkeeping appropriate to the block). The scalar gate is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{T<m\le2T}
\log\gcd\bigl(b_m,\mathcal P_{\rm wrap}(m,D)\bigr)
=o(T^2/\log T).
}
\tag{13.1}
\]

On the quotient-six candidate window, `(9.3)` makes this exactly an actual-Apéry selected-boundary anti-concentration statement, not a formal support statement.

There is one multiplicity caveat. If the counting problem charges the same `(m,p)` through several cells/gaps, the squarefree row gcd in `(13.1)` carries only one copy of `p`. Then the correct smallest gate is the corresponding support-independent Fitting/Smith version:

> construct a fixed integral block matrix whose columns are the charged row/cell objects, whose mod-`p` right-kernel dimension is at least the number of charged objects, and prove
> \[
> \log\Delta_{\max}=o(T^2/\log T).
> \tag{13.2}
> \]

The exact two-shared-row matrices from the current dyadic work provide the right valuation semantics, but no subcritical gcd-of-maximal-minors theorem is known.

Equivalently, a successful next input could be any one of:

1. an Apéry-specific large-lcm/nonoverlap theorem for the seam-selected row quotients;
2. a multiplicity-sensitive bound for the gcd in `(13.1)`;
3. a new selected column entry not generated by the ideal `(b_m)` and not removable as a distinct-prime unit witness;
4. a nonlinear or higher-p-adic integrality datum that remains after the wrap boundary is saturated.

What is **not** still missing is another continuant Euclidean manipulation of the same suffix. Equations `(6.2)` and `(6.4)` already show that every such common suffix factor is boundary-supported up to the fixed resultants `R_6,R_7`.

# Bottom line

The proposed suffix-gcd idea has one real algebraic success:

\[
\gcd_{d\ge d_0}N_{sd}(X)
\quad\text{collapses after three samples to}\quad
\text{boundary}\times R_s.
\]

But for the one-zero wrap mechanism the selected prime is **itself one of those boundary factors** (`7p` on the 6-side, `-6p` on the 7-side). Therefore:

- removing universal boundary/factorial content gives an `O(1)`-height residual but destroys selected coverage;
- restoring all possible selected seam primes gives only the universal boundary-prime radical, `O(D)` per row in the large-gap dyadic sector;
- intersecting that radical with actual selectedness is exactly `gcd(b_m,\mathcal P_{\rm wrap})` by Apéry--Lucas;
- no present continuant/resultant/Jacobi flag gives a second independent condition or the required aggregate `o(T^2/\log T)` height.

So this is a **sharp scoped no-go plus an exact reduction**, not a new closure theorem. The smallest remaining gate is the actual-Apéry boundary anti-concentration / multiplicity-sensitive Smith estimate `(13.1)`--`(13.2)`.
