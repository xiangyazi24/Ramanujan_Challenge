# Apéry zero pair correlations across primes

## 1. Random heuristic

Let $z_p=|Z_p|$. The observed law $z_p/2\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(1/2)$ suggests $\mathbb E z_p\approx1$. If the zeros behaved as independent random residues of density $1/p$, then for fixed $h$ the expected number of pairs

$$
\#\{d:b_d\equiv b_{d+h}\equiv0\pmod p\}
$$

would be about

$$
 p\cdot p^{-2}=p^{-1},
$$

not $p^{-2}$. The distinction is that there are $p$ possible starting positions. Therefore

$$
\mathbb E N_h(X)\approx\sum_{p\le X}\frac1p\sim\log\log X.
$$

The stronger prediction $O(1)$ would apply only if one fixed zero location were prescribed in advance.

## 2. The recurrence creates a local obstruction

The Apéry recurrence is

$$
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
$$

For $p\nmid d(d+1)$, if $b_d\equiv0\pmod p$, then

$$
b_{d+1}\equiv -\frac{d^3}{(d+1)^3}b_{d-1}\pmod p.
$$

Hence

$$
b_d\equiv b_{d+1}\equiv0
$$

is equivalent to

$$
b_{d-1}\equiv b_d\equiv0.
$$

But a second-order recurrence with invertible leading coefficients has zero vector propagation: if two consecutive values vanish, every subsequent value vanishes. Since $b_0=1$, this cannot happen. Therefore consecutive zeros are forbidden except at singular indices where a denominator vanishes modulo $p$.

This is the exact structural reason the empirical zero sets have no adjacent elements.

## 3. Fixed-distance correlations inside one prime

For $h=1$ the correlation is essentially zero (away from singular points).

For $h\ge2$, the pair condition is a linear condition on the two-dimensional recurrence state. Write

$$
(b_{d+h},b_{d+h-1})=M_h(d)(b_d,b_{d-1}).
$$

The condition $b_d=b_{d+h}=0$ imposes two linear equations on the state. Generically this has probability about $1/p^2$ for a fixed $d$, giving total probability about $1/p$ after summing over $d$.

Thus the heuristic remains

$$
N_h(X)\approx c_h\log\log X
$$

for generic $h\ge2$, with $c_h$ modified by recurrence degeneracies.

## 4. Cross-prime correlations

For distinct primes $p_1,p_2$, the conditions

$$
p_1\mid b_{n\bmod p_1},\qquad p_2\mid b_{n\bmod p_2}
$$

live in different finite fields. There is no recurrence relation directly coupling the two reductions.

The natural heuristic is independence:

$$
\Pr(p\mid b_{n\bmod p})\approx\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\approx\frac1p.
$$

Therefore

$$
\Pr(p_1,p_2\text{ both hit})\approx\frac1{p_1p_2}.
$$

This is the expected scale behind LGTQ-3: transverse triples should have probability roughly

$$
\frac1{p_1p_2p_3}.
$$

The difficulty is proving this independence, not estimating the marginal density.

## 5. Relation to LGTQ-3

The recurrence gives strong control of internal correlations in a single $Z_p$, including the prohibition of adjacent zeros. This helps eliminate many false configurations.

However LGTQ-3 concerns different primes and different residue classes:

$$
Z_p\ni n\bmod p,
$$

with nearby primes $p,p-h_1,p-h_1-h_2$. The required estimate is a cross-characteristic independence statement.

Possible approaches:

- Mellin/Frobenius methods: express the indicator through the Apéry motive and prove mixed moment cancellation.
- Trace-function methods: prove that the reduction of the zero indicator behaves like a bounded-conductor object.
- Sieve methods: use the no-adjacent-zero property plus average estimates for single hits.

No unconditional theorem currently proves the required cross-prime independence.

## Conclusion

1. The random model predicts $N_h(X)$ of order $\log\log X$ for generic $h$, not $O(1)$.
2. The Apéry recurrence forces a strong local repulsion: adjacent zeros are impossible except singular exceptional indices.
3. For $h\ge2$, correlations should be close to random after accounting for recurrence symmetries.
4. Across different primes the expected probability is multiplicative, about $1/(p_1p_2)$.
5. Proving this cross-prime independence is exactly the missing ingredient for LGTQ-3.
