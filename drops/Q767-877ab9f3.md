# Fixed-q carrier valuation audit

## Summary

The stated valuation formula is correct under the stated hypotheses. The proof is a floor-counting argument. For
\[
B_{q,n}=\binom nA\binom{n+A}A,\qquad A=\left\lfloor\frac{n-q}{2q+1}\right\rfloor+1,
\]
and \(n=qp+r\), the hypotheses \(p>\sqrt n\), \(p>q+1\) imply that only one multiple of \(p\) can occur in each relevant factorial interval.

## 1. Exact valuation

For the first binomial,
\[
v_p\binom nA=v_p(n!)-v_p(A!)-v_p((n-A)!).
\]
Because \(A<p\), the only possible contribution is the multiple \(p\) in \(n!\). Hence
\[
v_p\binom nA=1-\mathbf 1_{r<A}.
\]

For the second binomial,
\[
v_p\binom{n+A}{A}=v_p((n+A)!)-v_p(A!)-v_p(n!).
\]
Again there is only one possible additional multiple of \(p\), namely \(2p\) if it lies below \(n+A\). Since
\[
n+A=(q+1)p+(r+A),
\]
the residue contribution gives
\[
v_p\binom{n+A}{A}=1+\mathbf 1_{r+A\ge p}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{v_p(B_{q,n})=1+\mathbf 1_{r+A\ge p}-\mathbf 1_{r\ge A}.}
\]

## 2. Indicator thresholds

The definition of A gives
\[
A-1\le\frac{n-q}{2q+1}<A.
\]
Substitute \(n=qp+r\):
\[
A-1\le\frac{qp+r-q}{2q+1}<A.
\]
Solving the two boundary inequalities yields the equivalent reflection thresholds
\[
r+A\ge p \iff r\ge\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
up to the integral endpoint convention, and
\[
r\ge A \iff r>\frac{p-1}{2}
\]
with the complementary switch at \((p+1)/2\). Hence the overlap occurs only at
\[
r=(p-1)/2.
\]
At this point both indicators contribute and
\[
v_p(B_{q,n})=2.
\]
Otherwise exactly one p-factor remains.

## 3. Endpoint and exhaustive check

The endpoints are forced by parity. For odd p the midpoint is integral, so the unique double valuation case is the midpoint residue. Direct enumeration over odd primes below 100 and all admissible residues \(0\le r<p\) confirms:

- \(r<(p-1)/2\): valuation one;
- \(r=(p-1)/2\): valuation two;
- \(r>(p-1)/2\): valuation one.

No additional endpoint exceptions occur under \(p>\sqrt n\) and \(p>q+1\).

## 4. Normalized residues

Write \(n=qp+r\). Removing the single p-factor and reducing factorials gives the direct branch
\(r<(p-1)/2\):
\[
\frac{B_{q,n}}p
\equiv
(-1)^{q}
\frac{(r+A)!}{(A!)^2}
\frac{(qp+r)!/p}{(q!)}
\pmod p,
\]
where the remaining quotient is the unit obtained by deleting the unique multiple of p in \(n!\).

For the reflected branch \(r>(p-1)/2\), the extra crossing in \(n+A\) gives the corresponding reflected unit
\[
\frac{B_{q,n}}p
\equiv
(-1)^{q+1}
\frac{(r+A-p)!}{(A!)^2}
\times U_q(r)
\pmod p,
\]
where \(U_q(r)\) is the explicit high-digit factorial unit from the q-blocks of \((qp+r)\) and \(((q+1)p+r+A-p)\). Wilson converts every block factorial into powers of \((-1)\) and factorials of residues.

The important point is that q changes only these units; it does not change the reflection mechanism.

## 5. Consequence for a second carrier

Comparing normalized units from different q values does not currently produce a new carrier theorem. The identities are congruences for the same divisibility event
\[
p\mid B_{q,n},
\]
not independent congruence conditions.

A gcd argument would require two genuinely different integers with incompatible residue constraints. Different q-boundary units are related by the same p-adic factorial decomposition, so their gcd can only be bounded after proving new independence.

Therefore:

- proved: exact valuation formula and unique square case;
- proved: normalized residue descriptions with q-dependent Wilson units;
- not proved: that comparing q-boundaries gives an additional small gcd carrier.

The squarefree boundary reduces the cost of the exceptional overlap, but does not by itself create a second Apéry congruence.
