# Audit conclusion

The proposed fixed-degree extension is **not proved** by the stated induction. The correct scoped conclusion is that the degree-1, degree-2, and degree-3 arguments establish a pattern, but they do not imply a theorem for every fixed degree `d`. The missing ingredient is a genuine discrete total-positivity / extended-Chebyshev theorem for the normalized row family.

## What survives from the setup

The endpoint expansion

\[
c_k=\kappa\gamma^k k^{-3/2}(1+\alpha_1/k+\cdots)
\]

is sufficient to compute any fixed number of terminal asymptotic expansions of

\[
R_k^{(d)}=((M^jc)_k/c_k)_{0\le j\le d}.
\]

For each fixed `j`, repeated use of

\[
(Mw)_k=k(w_k+w_{k-1})
\]

gives an expansion of the form

\[
(M^jc)_k/c_k = (\gamma 2k)^j(1+O(1/k))
\]

with computable lower order terms. This justifies the formal existence of terminal divided differences.

It does **not** imply positivity of every Neville pivot.

## Failure of the proposed induction

The claimed induction requires the implication

"all terminal j-th slopes have increment \(j a+O(k^{-2})>0\)"

\[
\Longrightarrow
\]

"all higher order discrete divided differences have one fixed sign."

This implication is false without an additional total positivity hypothesis.

Positive first-order increments control only the first Neville elimination layer. Higher pivots contain mixed products of lower-order corrections. At degree `d>=4`, the relevant pivot has the schematic form

\[
\Delta_d(k)=V_d(k,H)\,(1+E_d(k,H)),
\]

where `V_d` is the leading generalized Vandermonde factor, but `E_d` is a rational combination of the full asymptotic coefficients of all rows. The endpoint expansion supplies that these coefficients exist; it does not give the sign of `1+E_d` uniformly.

The missing lemma would have to be something like:

> For every fixed `d`, the normalized sequence \(R_k^{(d)}\) is an eventually strictly totally positive kernel (or an oriented discrete extended Chebyshev system).

No such lemma follows from the displayed slope asymptotics.

## Why the finite computations through d=8 do not close the proof

The scan establishes only that the tested finite range has the expected orientation. It cannot replace a proof that every Neville pivot remains positive for all sufficiently large heights. In particular, it does not rule out a sign change caused by a lower-order coefficient appearing at a higher elimination stage.

## Scope of the determinant statement

The statement that can currently be recorded is:

* For `d=1,2,3`, the determinant sign follows from the completed monotonicity/convexity/divided-slope arguments.
* For arbitrary fixed `d`, the determinant sign is a conjectural consequence of eventual total positivity.
* The endpoint expansion proves asymptotic rank, but not orientation of every finite-dimensional minor.

## Consequence for Newton-Pade bounds

The implication

\[
\text{fixed-degree signed kernel}
\Rightarrow
\log |P(n)|\ge 0.450561 n+o(n)
\]

remains valid conditional on the signed-kernel theorem.

The unconditional dependency map should not contain an edge

\[
\text{endpoint expansion}\rightarrow\text{all fixed degree signed kernels}.
\]

The missing edge is

\[
\text{endpoint expansion + explicit total positivity theorem}
\rightarrow
\text{fixed-degree Newton coefficient positivity}.
\]

## Final status

The requested universal fixed-`d` theorem is **not established**. No counterexample at a specific degree `d` is forced by the supplied data; the failure is a missing proof obligation, not a demonstrated falsehood.
