# 3. The exact four-state Plücker identity

For any four vectors in a rank-two module, the six brackets satisfy one Plücker relation.  Applied to

\[
V_t,\ V_\nu,\ V_u,\ V_w,
\]

it is

\[
\boxed{
[t,\nu][u,w]
=[t,w][u,\nu]-[t,u][w,\nu].}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Substituting (2.6) and (2.8) yields a completely explicit integral identity.  Its cleanest orientation-free form is already (3.1).  Written only with restart kernels, it has three order charts.

## 3.1. Chart I: \(d\le\delta<7d\)

Here

\[
t<u<w\le\nu.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
pq\alpha\beta
={}&N_{7d}(t)N_{6d}(u)\\
&-\left(\frac{w!}{u!}\right)^6
  N_\delta(t)N_{\delta-d}(w),
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.2}
\]

where \(w=t+7d\).

## 3.2. Chart II: \(0\le\delta<d\)

Here

\[
t\le u<\nu<w.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
pq\alpha\beta
={}&N_{7d}(t)N_{6d}(u)\\
&+\left(\frac{\nu!}{u!}\right)^6
  N_\delta(t)N_{d-\delta}(\nu),
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.3}
\]

where \(\nu=u+6d\).

## 3.3. Chart III: \(-6d<\delta<0\)

Here

\[
u<t<\nu<w.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
pq\alpha\beta
={}&N_{7d}(t)N_{6d}(u)\\
&-\left(\frac{\nu!}{t!}\right)^6
  N_{-\delta}(u)N_{d-\delta}(\nu).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Every factorial quotient displayed in (3.2)--(3.4) is a product of only \(O(d)\) consecutive integers; there is no rational denominator.

Define the right side in the applicable chart by

\mathcal P_{t,u,d}.

\boxed{

=pq\alpha\beta

Thus the requested nonzero **block-kernel relation indexed by the gap** exists.  It is not a polynomial only in \(m,d\); it retains the edge position \((t,u)\), equivalently one free affine coordinate subject to (1.1).

# 4. The opposite-characteristic residues are unit rescalings

The two proposed cross quantities are

\[
\beta_p:=\beta\bmod p,
\qquad
\alpha_q:=\alpha\bmod q.
\]

Because

\[
q\equiv d\pmod p,
\qquad
p\equiv-d\pmod q,
\]

and \(d\) is a unit at both primes, (2.8) gives immediately

\[
\boxed{
\beta_p
\equiv d^{-1}N_{7d-\delta}(u)\pmod p,}
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_q
\equiv-d^{-1}N_{\delta+6d}(t)\pmod q.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the conceptual obstruction.  For an integer \(K\) divisible by \(q\),

\[
\left(\frac Kq\right)\bmod p
=q^{-1}(K\bmod p),
\qquad p\ne q.
\tag{4.3}
\]

There is no extension class and no loss of a base-
\(q\) digit in (4.3): \(q\) is invertible in \(\mathbf F_p\).  A genuine first Bockstein is division by \(q\) followed by reduction **modulo \(q\)**.  Division by \(q\) followed by reduction modulo \(p\) is merely a unit change of coordinates.

Equivalently,

\[
\mathbf Z/(pq)\cong\mathbf F_p\times\mathbf F_q
\]

splits canonically by CRT.  Once all first vertex-local jets have saturated the two factors, the opposite reductions do not create a new relation between them.

# 6. Precise universal no-go theorem

The negative result must be scoped, because an additional coefficient-specific Apéry identity could in principle exist.  The following class is exactly what the present transfer/jet architecture uses.

## 6.1. Allowed class \(\mathscr C_1\)

An identity belongs to \(\mathscr C_1\) if it is obtained from:

1. the rank-two integral state recurrence and transfer composition;
2. determinant/Casoratian identities and the Plücker relation;
3. integral or localized basis changes whose determinants are units at the endpoint primes;
4. the two existing first divisions
   \[
   [t,\nu]/p,\qquad [u,w]/q;
   \]
5. addition, multiplication, and localization at quantities known to be \(p\)- and \(q\)-units.

It may **not**:

- divide an expression known only to be divisible by \(pq\);
- adjoin a second/higher mixed divided state;
- use a new Apéry-specific algebraic relation among nonadjacent kernels beyond recurrence composition.

This includes canonical transfer rewrites, first divided vertex jets, Wronskians, companion basis changes, and outer rank-two state rewrites with no new mixed quotient.

## 6.2. Explicit free-parameter countermodel

Work over the polynomial ring

\[

\]

Choose four rank-two states

\[

\qquad

V_u=\binom01,
\qquad
V_w=\binom{-q\beta}1.
\tag{6.1}

\[
[t,\nu]=p\alpha,
\qquad
[u,w]=q\beta,
\tag{6.2}
\]

\[
[t,w]=1,
\quad
[u,\nu]=-1,
\quad
[t,u]=1,
\quad
[w,\nu]=-1-pq\alpha\beta.
\tag{6.3}

All the first three cross brackets are units, and the Plücker identity is satisfied identically:

\[
1\cdot(-1)-1\cdot(-1-pq\alpha\beta)

The parameters \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are completely free.  Thus no nonzero polynomial relation between

\[

This countermodel can be realized by an arbitrary second-order transfer system with suitable invertible steps; therefore it rules out every identity that is functorial in rank-two recurrence/transfer algebra. ## 9.2. Quotient-free structural replacement

If adjoining \(\Theta\) is forbidden, the minimum possible escape is one genuinely new independent mixed observation.  Concretely, one needs either:

1. **a second integral mixed block identity** involving the same two cross residues, such that the resulting \(2\times2\) coefficient determinant is a unit at both endpoint primes; or
2. **an Apéry-specific termwise factorization** of the Plücker numerator in which the factors \(p\) and \(q\) are removed before the final subtraction, yielding \(\Theta\) from already named local data; or
3. **an independent outer/companion arithmetic equation** fixing one of the two surviving projective/adjacent-state coordinates without introducing another divided symbol.

Another transfer composition, another Wronskian of the same rank-two state, or another first divided vertex jet stays inside \(\mathscr C_1\) and cannot work.

A sharp acceptance criterion for a positive next lemma is therefore:

> Construct an explicit integer \(J_{t,u,d}\), using only already named undivided short-block kernels and already existing first local jets, such that
> \[
> J_{t,u,d}\equiv\alpha\beta\pmod p,
> \qquad
> J_{t,u,d}\equiv\alpha\beta\pmod q,
> \]
> without defining \(J\) as \(\mathcal P/(pq)\), and prove that no new private divided coordinate occurs in its construction.

Equivalently, produce two quotient-free mixed equations with a unit \(2\times2\) eliminant.  Anything weaker reduces to (3.1) and hence to the private quotient \(\Theta\).
