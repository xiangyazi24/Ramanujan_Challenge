## 1. There is already an exact cross-prime \(L^2\) large sieve

Let \(\mathcal P\) be a set of primes \(p\le Q\), and let
\[
 f_p:\mathbf F_p\longrightarrow\mathbf C
\]
be centered:
\[
 \sum_{x\bmod p}f_p(x)=0.
\]
Write
\[
 \|f_p\|_2^2=\sum_{x\bmod p}|f_p(x)|^2.
\]
Then for arbitrary coefficients \(c_p\),
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{0\le m<M}
 \left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}c_p f_p(m\bmod p)\right|^2
 \le (M-1+Q^2)
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac{|c_p|^2}{p}\|f_p\|_2^2.
}
\tag{1}
\]

This is a genuinely **cross-base-prime** estimate.  It does not require the \(f_p\) to be trace functions.

### Proof

Use the unnormalized finite Fourier transform
\[
 \widehat f_p(a)=\sum_{x\bmod p}f_p(x)e_p(-ax),
 \qquad e_p(y)=e^{2\pi i y/p}.
\]
Centering removes \(a=0\), and Fourier inversion gives
\[
 f_p(m)=\frac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\widehat f_p(a)e\!\left(\frac{am}{p}\right).
\]
The reduced fractions
\[
 \frac ap,\qquad p\in\mathcal P,\quad 1\le a<p,
\]
are all distinct modulo \(1\), and any two are separated by at least \(Q^{-2}\).  The classical additive large sieve therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{m<M}\left|
  \sum_{p}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}
  \frac{c_p\widehat f_p(a)}p e(am/p)
 \right|^2
 \le (M-1+Q^2)
 \sum_p\frac{|c_p|^2}{p^2}\sum_a|\widehat f_p(a)|^2.
\]
Parseval,
\[
 \sum_a|\widehat f_p(a)|^2=p\|f_p\|_2^2,
\]
proves (1).

### The Apéry specialization

Put
\[
 z_p=|\mathcal Z_p|,
 \qquad
 g_p(x)=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal Z_p}(x)-\frac{z_p}{p}.
\]
Then
\[
 \|g_p\|_2^2=z_p-\frac{z_p^2}{p}\le z_p.
\]
For the dyadic block \(X<p\le2X\), with \(M=X^2\) and
\[
 \lambda_X=\sum_{X<p\le2X}\frac{z_p}{p},
\]
(1) yields
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{m<X^2}\left|\sum_{X<p\le2X}g_p(m)\right|^2
 \ll X^2\lambda_X.
}
\tag{2}
\]

This is exactly the natural cross-prime variance scale.

However, it does **not** imply `(HM)_3`.  The anchored-rainbow-star construction in `hm3_result.tex` satisfies a variance bound of this scale, the proved second factorial-moment bound, and even an \(O(1)\) row-codegree bound, while its third factorial moment is
\[
 \gg \frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3}.
\]
Thus the repository already contains a deterministic obstruction showing that no argument using only (1), `(HM)_2`, and primewise sheaf bounds can close the third moment.

## 2. The proposed \(L^{2+\delta}\) inequality is false in general

Consider the exact form suggested in the question, with \(r=2+\delta>2\):
\[
 \sum_{m<M}\left|\sum_p f_p(m\bmod p)\right|^r
 \stackrel{?}{\ll}_r
 M\left(\sum_p\frac{\|f_p\|_2^2}{p}\right)^{r/2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Even with exact centering and uniformly bounded conductor, (3) is false.

### A bounded-conductor trace-function counterexample

For every prime \(p\in(X,2X]\), take
\[
 f_p(x)=e_p(x).
\]
This is the trace function of the rank-one Artin--Schreier sheaf
\(\mathcal L_{\psi(X)}\); it is pure of weight zero, has uniformly bounded conductor, and is exactly centered.  Also
\[
 \frac1p\|f_p\|_2^2=1.
\]
Let
\[
 R=\#\{p:X<p\le2X\}\asymp\frac{X}{\log X}.
\]
Choose a small fixed \(c>0\).  For every \(0\le m\le cX\), the phases
\[
 e(m/p),\qquad X<p\le2X,
\]
all lie in an arc of fixed length less than \(\pi/4\).  Consequently
\[
 \left|\sum_{X<p\le2X}e(m/p)\right|\gg R
\]
uniformly on those \(\asymp X\) integers \(m\).  Taking \(M=X^2\),
\[
 \sum_{m<M}\left|\sum_p f_p(m)\right|^r
 \gg X R^r.
\tag{4}
\]
The right side predicted by (3) is
\[
 X^2R^{r/2}.
\]
Their ratio is
\[
 \frac{XR^r}{X^2R^{r/2}}
 =\frac{R^{r/2}}X
 \asymp
 \frac{X^{\delta/2}}{(\log X)^{1+\delta/2}}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{5}
\]
Therefore (3) fails for every fixed \(\delta>0\).

The counterexample is an **exceptional** Artin--Schreier trace function in the terminology of Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel.  Hence it disproves the theorem as stated; it does not rule out a theorem with strong additional hypotheses excluding every additive rank-one constituent.  But ordinary fixed-prime “nonexceptionality” is still not enough by itself: one would need a genuinely **cross-prime Fourier nonconcentration condition** preventing the low frequencies of the different columns from aligning on the distinguished interval.

### Why interpolation from \(L^2\) does not repair this

For bounded columns,
\[
 \|S\|_{2+\delta}^{2+\delta}
 \le \|S\|_\infty^\delta\|S\|_2^2,
 \qquad S(m)=\sum_p f_p(m).
\]
Since \(\|S\|_\infty\) may be of order \(R\), (1) only gives
\[
 \sum_{m<M}|S(m)|^{2+\delta}
 \ll M R^{1+\delta},
\]
whereas the proposed random scale is \(MR^{1+\delta/2}\).  The missing factor
\(R^{\delta/2}\) is exactly the higher-order cancellation one is trying to prove.

For `(HM)_3`, a moment with exponent strictly between \(2\) and \(3\) is also not automatically enough: converting it to a cubic moment costs a factor comparable to \(\|S\|_\infty^{\,1-\delta}\).  A direct \(L^3\) estimate, a sufficiently strong tail estimate, or the pair--Palm formulation is needed.

## 3. What is true on the full CRT period: independence and Rosenthal

Let
\[
 P=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p.
\]
If \(U\) is uniform modulo \(P\), then the coordinates
\[
 U\bmod p\in\mathbf F_p
\]
are independent by the Chinese remainder theorem.  Hence the centered random variables
\[
 X_p=f_p(U\bmod p)
\]
are independent.  Rosenthal's inequality gives, for every fixed \(r\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
 \frac1P\sum_{u\bmod P}\left|\sum_p f_p(u)\right|^r
 \ll_r
 V^{r/2}+B_r,
}
\tag{6}
\]
where
\[
 V=\sum_p\frac{\|f_p\|_2^2}{p},
 \qquad
 B_r=\sum_p\frac1p\sum_{x\bmod p}|f_p(x)|^r.
\]
The term \(B_r\) is necessary.  In particular, for sparse zero-indicator columns, the third absolute moment of one Bernoulli variable is of the same order as its density; a pure \(V^{3/2}\) bound cannot hold uniformly when \(V<1\).

There is also an exact random-shift consequence.  For any \(M\le P\),
\[
 \frac1P\sum_{U\bmod P}
 \sum_{m<M}\left|\sum_p f_p(U+m)\right|^r
 =\frac MP\sum_{u\bmod P}\left|\sum_p f_p(u)\right|^r,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
 \mathbb E_U\sum_{m<M}|S(U+m)|^r
 \ll_r M(V^{r/2}+B_r).
}
\tag{7}
\]

This is the rigorous form of the “random cancellation” heuristic: a **randomly shifted** interval has the expected moment scale.

But `(HM)_3` concerns the one distinguished interval
\[
 [0,X^2),
\]
i.e. the single shift \(U=0\).  The product period \(P\) is astronomically larger than \(X^2\), and neither (6) nor (7) prevents shift zero from being exceptional.  The Artin--Schreier example above and the repository's anchored-star example both demonstrate this exact failure mode.

Thus the problem is not independence on the CRT probability space.  It is **derandomizing the distinguished short prefix**.

## 4. What the trace-function literature actually varies

The phrase “multi-prime large sieve” is overloaded.  The relevant results fall into distinct classes.

### 4.1 Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *Algebraic trace functions over the primes*

In Theorem 1.5 of this paper, one fixes

- one prime modulus \(p\), and
- one trace weight \(K:\mathbf F_p\to\mathbf C\),

and estimates
\[
 \sum_{q\ \mathrm{prime}}K(q)V(q/X).
\]
Here \(q\) is the **prime argument** of one function modulo the fixed prime \(p\).  The theorem does not correlate trace functions whose base characteristics are different.

Bibliographically, this is Duke Math. J. **163** (2014), 1683--1736, not a 2013 Duke paper:

- arXiv: [1211.6043](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6043)
- DOI: [10.1215/00127094-2690587](https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2690587)

### 4.2 The FKM ternary-divisor Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem

The paper

- Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *On the exponent of distribution of the ternary divisor function*, Mathematika **61** (2015), 121--144,
- arXiv: [1304.3199](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.3199),

is genuinely averaged over prime moduli.  It proves an exponent of distribution beyond \(1/2\) for the specific arithmetic sequence \(d_3(n)\).

However, it is **not** a black-box theorem for arbitrary families \((f_p)_p\).  Trace functions enter after Voronoi/Poisson transformations as very specific Kloosterman-type kernels.  The cross-modulus saving is obtained by combining

- the algebraic geometry of those particular kernels,
- bilinear/dispersion decompositions,
- reciprocity and completion,
- and a detailed treatment of diagonal and exceptional configurations.

It controls a linear arithmetic-progression discrepancy for one fixed sequence.  It does not bound
\[
 \sum_{m<X^2}f_{p_1}(m)f_{p_2}(m)f_{p_3}(m)
\]
for three independently varying base primes and independently varying sheaves.

### 4.3 Xi's large sieve, short-sum theorems, and the 2025 FKMS theorem

The following results are also fixed-characteristic results:

- Ping Xi, with an appendix by Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *Large Sieve Inequalities for Algebraic Trace Functions*, IMRN 2017, 4840--4881, DOI [10.1093/imrn/rnw074](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnw074).
- Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel--Raju--Rivat--Soundararajan, *On short sums of trace functions*, arXiv [1508.00512](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00512).
- Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel--Sawin, *Bilinear forms with trace functions*, arXiv [2511.09459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459) (2025).

Even the “trilinear” theorem in the 2025 paper concerns a kernel such as
\[
 K(j^a m^b n^c)
\]
for one trace function \(K\) over one field \(\mathbf F_q\).  It does not mean a product of trace functions in three different characteristics.

These results could become useful **after** one transforms the Apéry cross-prime problem into an inner complete or bilinear sum over one fixed \(\mathbf F_p\).  No such transformation is currently present.

### 4.4 Kowalski's and Perret-Gentil's large sieve for Frobenius

This is a different meaning of “many primes.”  One fixes

- a base finite field \(\mathbf F_q\) of characteristic \(p\), and
- one compatible system of sheaves,

and varies auxiliary coefficient primes or prime ideals
\[
 \lambda\mid\ell,\qquad \ell\ne p.
\]
Product-surjectivity of residual monodromy at distinct \(\lambda,\lambda'\) supplies the sieve independence.

Relevant references are

- Kowalski, *The large sieve, monodromy and zeta functions of curves*, arXiv [math/0503714](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503714), and
- Perret-Gentil, *Exponential Sums Over Finite Fields and the Large Sieve*, IMRN 2020, arXiv [1703.06965](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06965).

This does not vary the **base characteristic** in the manner needed here.  More importantly, the Apéry event is reduction at the defining prime itself:
\[
 \mathfrak p\mid p,
 \qquad T_{j,p}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]
The specialization \(\ell=p\) is explicitly outside the étale compatible-system sieve.  This is the same diagonal obstruction recorded in `chatgpt_q32_fixed_exponent_mellin_attack.md`.

### 4.5 The newest horizontal Mellin theorem permits varying characteristic, but still does not select the needed section

Bah--Shuddhodan, *Uniform stratified vanishing and equidistribution on \(\mathbb G_m^d\)*, arXiv [2608.00304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00304), proves bounded-complexity Mellin equidistribution along finite fields of varying characteristic, assuming a common tannakian group.

This is genuinely relevant: it removes the old objection that horizontal Mellin equidistribution must keep the characteristic fixed.

But its empirical measure averages over essentially all multiplicative characters \(\chi\) inside each field.  The Apéry application selects one deterministic character/exponent at each prime.  A one-character section has relative mass \(1/(p-1)\) and may be exceptional for every \(p\) without affecting all-character equidistribution.

It also gives complex/unitary Frobenius equidistribution.  It does not detect divisibility by the defining prime \(p\).

### Literature conclusion

As of 2026-08-18, I do not find a theorem whose quantifiers simultaneously allow

1. the base prime \(p\) and the sheaf to vary,
2. one prescribed member of the Kummer/Mellin family at each \(p\),
3. summation over the fixed short prefix \([0,X^2)\), and
4. a moment strictly above two at the random scale.

More strongly, without substantial cross-prime nonresonance hypotheses, such a theorem is impossible by Section 2.

## 5. The Apéry zero event is not the complex trace function

Even a hypothetical cross-prime moment theorem for the normalized complex traces
\[
 p^{-3/2}T_{j,p}
\]
would not immediately address `(HM)_3`.

The zero column is
\[
 \mathbf 1_{\{p\mid b_j\}}
 =\mathbf 1_{\{T_{j,p}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p}\}},
 \qquad \mathfrak p\mid p.
\]
This is a **defining-characteristic residual condition**, not the complex event \(T_{j,p}=0\).  The repository already records a concrete instance
\[
 (p,j)=(11,5),\qquad T_{j,p}=-33\ne0,\qquad T_{j,p}\equiv0\pmod{11}.
\]
Thus complex Sato--Tate or trace-value cancellation does not see the required event.

One may formally write
\[
 \mathbf 1_{\{b_j=0\bmod p\}}
 =\frac1p\sum_{u\bmod p}e_p(ub_j).
\tag{8}
\]
But it is not known that
\[
 j\longmapsto e_p(ub_j)
\]
is a bounded-conductor trace function uniformly in \(p\) and \(u\).  Exponentiating the residual value of a trace is not one of the standard sheaf operations that preserves bounded conductor.  An arbitrary function on \(\mathbf F_p\) can be represented by some sheaf only at the cost of conductor growing with \(p\), which is useless for FKM estimates.

So there are two independent gaps:

1. **cross-prime short-prefix mixing**, and
2. **conversion from a complex Mellin trace to its same-prime divisibility indicator**.

The second gap remains even if the first is solved for \(T_{j,p}\).

## 6. The exact Fourier object behind the three-prime correlation

For the actual zero columns, retain
\[
 g_p(x)=\mathbf1_{\mathcal Z_p}(x)-\frac{z_p}{p}
\]
and define
\[
 \widehat g_p(a)=\sum_{x\bmod p}g_p(x)e_p(-ax).
\]
Then for distinct primes \(p,q,r\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{m<M}g_p(m)g_q(m)g_r(m)\\
 &\quad=
 \frac1{pqr}
 \sum_{\substack{a\bmod p,\ b\bmod q,\ c\bmod r\\a,b,c\ne0}}
 \widehat g_p(a)\widehat g_q(b)\widehat g_r(c)
 D_M\!\left(\frac ap+\frac bq+\frac cr\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
where
\[
 D_M(\theta)=\sum_{m<M}e(m\theta),
 \qquad
 |D_M(\theta)|\le\min(M,\|\theta\|^{-1}).
\]

At \(M=X^2\) and \(p,q,r\asymp X\), the dangerous configurations are the cross-prime near-resonances
\[
 \left\|\frac ap+\frac bq+\frac cr\right\|\lesssim X^{-2}.
\tag{10}
\]
The ordinary large sieve controls pairwise spacing of the individual fractions \(a/p\), which is why the \(L^2\) theorem works.  A cubic estimate requires control of the **additive energy of sums of three prime-denominator frequency packets**.

A fixed-prime Deligne or FKM estimate may bound an individual Fourier transform or a complete product correlation inside one \(\mathbf F_p\).  It does not supply cancellation among the rational frequencies in (10), because those frequencies live in different characteristics.

This is the precise analytic mechanism absent from the literature cited in the question.

## 7. Does the FKM Bombieri--Vinogradov method give any handle at all?

Yes, but only as a **blueprint or local engine**, not as an applicable theorem.

A successful FKM/dispersion attack would need the following new chain.

1. Start from the signed triple-CRT defect or the finite pair--Palm overload.
2. Smooth the interval so that \(D_M\) has rapid decay away from the major arcs.
3. Use reciprocity, Poisson/Voronoi, or another exact transformation to rewrite the cross-prime sum as a bilinear or trilinear form over one fixed finite field.
4. Prove that the resulting kernel is the trace function of a bounded-complexity, nonexceptional sheaf, uniformly outside controlled diagonal strata.
5. Apply FKM/Xi/FKMS fixed-prime correlation estimates to the inner sum.
6. Sum the remaining moduli and prove that the diagonal/major-arc contribution has the `(HM)_3` scale.

This is essentially what makes the ternary-divisor exponent-of-distribution theorem work: the specific Voronoi/Kloosterman structure furnishes Step 3.

For the Apéry zero indicators, no corresponding reciprocity formula is currently known.  The congruence
\[
 T_{j,p}\equiv b_j\pmod{\mathfrak p}
\]
gives a powerful one-prime geometric realization, but it does not convert the product of three residual zero indicators into one fixed-field trace kernel.

Therefore the FKM method gives a **research architecture**, not a present bound on the Apéry three-prime correlation.

## 8. The sharp surviving targets for this project

The broad \(L^{2+\delta}\) conjecture is stronger than necessary and false without extra hypotheses.  The repository has already isolated more precise targets.

### 8.1 Direct signed cubic target

A natural centered target is
\[
 \mathcal C_3(X)
 =\sum_{\substack{p,q,r\in(X,2X]\\p,q,r\ \mathrm{distinct}}}
  \sum_{m<X^2}g_p(m)g_q(m)g_r(m).
\]
A one-sided or absolute estimate of the shape
\[
 \mathcal C_3(X)
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3,
\tag{11}
\]
together with the already controlled diagonal and lower-order terms, would give `(HM)_3`.

In Fourier language, (11) is a signed version of the near-resonance estimate (9)--(10).  This is the most economical “cross-prime trace-dispersion” target.

### 8.2 Exact robust target: finite pair--Palm positive overload

The theorem in `hm3_result.tex` shows that `(HM)_3` is equivalent to
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{p\ne q}
 \left(T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}\right)_+
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
 }
\tag{12}
\]
This formulation absorbs every one-column boundary error and prevents cancellation between overloaded and underloaded anchor pairs.  It is the correct target if a future large-sieve theorem is intended to hold with arbitrary nonnegative pair weights.

### 8.3 A possible but genuinely new short-prefix Rosenthal theorem

A sufficiently strong new theorem would have to look more like
\[
 \sum_{m<M}|S(m)|^r
 \ll_r X^{o(1)}M
 \left(
 V^{r/2}+B_r
 \right),
 \qquad r\ge3,
\tag{13}
\]
for the **actual Apéry columns**, where
\[
 S(m)=\sum_p g_p(m),
 \quad
 V=\sum_p\frac{\|g_p\|_2^2}{p},
 \quad
 B_r=\sum_p\frac1p\sum_x|g_p(x)|^r.
\]
The \(B_r\) term is mandatory, and (13) cannot be a theorem for arbitrary trace functions.  It would require an Apéry-specific hypothesis or proof excluding cross-prime low-frequency concentration.

But proving (13) is probably more than is needed.  The signed target (11) or the pair--Palm target (12) is sharper and better aligned with the empirical cancellation already observed.

## 9. Bottom line

The answer to the question is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
There is a clean cross-prime large sieve at the second moment, obtained from the classical additive large sieve after Fourier expansion of each column.  There is no known trace-function theorem giving the proposed moment above two on the distinguished interval, and the proposed inequality is false for arbitrary bounded-conductor trace functions.  FKM's Duke theorem is single-modulus; the ternary-divisor Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem is a special dispersion theorem, not a black box for varying sheaves.  Kowalski--Perret-Gentil vary auxiliary coefficient primes, not base characteristics, and the newest Bah--Shuddhodan varying-characteristic theorem averages all Mellin characters rather than the selected Apéry section.  Finally, the event \(p\mid b_j\) is same-prime residual divisibility and is not detected by the complex trace itself.
\end{minipage}
}
\]

So the FKM literature does not presently close or materially reduce `(HM)_3` beyond the already available \(L^2\)/pair-correlation layer.  The next genuinely new theorem must control the cross-prime near-resonances in (9), or directly prove the finite pair--Palm overload bound (12), using arithmetic specific to the Apéry Mellin family rather than conductor and monodromy bounds one prime at a time.
