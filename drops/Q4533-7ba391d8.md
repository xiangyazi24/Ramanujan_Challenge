# 2. Exact CRT-row form of the phase

The reciprocal phase has a useful exact integral representative.

## Lemma 2.1 (CRT row)

For distinct primes `p,q`, `0<=r<p`, and `0<=s<q`, let

\[
 j=j_{p,q}(r,s)\in\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\},
 \qquad
 j\equiv(s-r)\bar p\pmod q,
\]

and put

\[
 t=t_{p,q}(r,s)=r+pj.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then `0<=t<pq`,

\[
 t\equiv r\pmod p,
 \qquad t\equiv s\pmod q,
\tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 e\!\left(\frac{dr}{pq}\right)
 e_q\!\left(d(s-r)\bar p\right)
 =e\!\left(\frac{dt}{pq}\right).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Moreover:

- `s=r` if and only if `j=0`, in which case `t=r<min(p,q)`;
- if `s!=r`, then `1<=j<=q-1` and `t>=p`;
- with reflected positions
  \[
  r^\vee=p-1-r,\qquad s^\vee=q-1-s,
  \]
  the CRT representative is
  \[
  t^\vee=pq-1-t.
  \tag{2.4}
  \]

### Proof

The two congruences in (2.2) follow immediately from the definition.  Also

\[
\frac{t}{pq}
 =\frac r{pq}+\frac jq
 \equiv\frac r{pq}+\frac{(s-r)\bar p}{q}\pmod1,
\]

which proves (2.3).  Since `p` is invertible modulo `q`, `j=0` is equivalent to `s-r=0`.  Formula (2.4) follows by reducing `pq-1-t` modulo `p` and `q`.

## Reducedness

The source has `b_0=1`, so `0` is not in any `\mathcal Z_p`.  Thus every actual zero pair in (1.3) has

\[
(t,pq)=1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Consequently `t/(pq)` is a reduced fraction.  This fact will remove every exact zero frequency from the off-row TT* residual.

# 4. Published Kloosterman-fraction input

I use the following literal consequence of Bettin--Chandee, Theorem 1 and Remark 1.  If `A,M_1,M_2>=1`, `theta!=0`, and `alpha_m,beta_n,nu_a` are arbitrary complex coefficients on dyadic intervals, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{a\asymp A}\sum_{m\asymp M_1}\sum_{n\asymp M_2}
 \alpha_m\beta_n\nu_a
 e\!\left(\theta a\frac{\bar m}{n}+f_{a,\theta}(m,n)\right)\\
&\quad\ll_\varepsilon
 \|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2\|\nu\|_2
 \left(1+\frac{|\theta|A+X}{M_1M_2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\qquad\times\left\{
 (AM_1M_2)^{7/20+\varepsilon}(M_1+M_2)^{1/4}
 +(AM_1M_2)^{3/8+\varepsilon}
   (AM_1+AM_2)^{1/8}
 \right\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

provided

\[
 \partial_m f\ll \frac{X}{m^2n},
 \qquad
 \partial_n f\ll \frac{X}{mn^2}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The final theorem has arbitrary coefficients; it does not require the coefficients to be prime-supported, squarefree-supported, or coprime to `theta`.  In our application the sequences are simply set to zero off the desired primes.  The sole denominator coprimality is `(p,q)=1`, automatic for distinct primes.

The smooth factor `\widehat w(Nd/(pq))` is harmless.  On fixed dyadic boxes it is separated by Fourier inversion in the logarithmic variables `(d/D,p/N,q/N)`; the resulting multiplicative phases are absorbed into `nu_d,alpha_p,beta_q` and preserve their `L^2` norms.  The rapidly decaying transform costs `N^\varepsilon` at most.  This is the standard smooth-weight form of (4.1), not a new arithmetic assumption.

# 7. Where the Apéry twin/codegree theorem enters: periodic rows

The Bettin--Chandee deletion above uses the canonical first zero moment.  The stronger audited Apéry twin/codegree theorem enters at the next stage, when the transverse form is squared.

For `0<=t<=L_vN^2`, where `L_v` is a fixed constant larger than every possible `pq/N^2` in the smooth support, put

\[
 \widetilde P_N(t)
 =\{p:v(p/N)\ne0,\ t\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\},
 \qquad
 \widetilde m_N(t)=|\widetilde P_N(t)|.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Unlike `m_N(r)`, this is the periodic incidence degree; `t` need not be smaller than `p`.

## Lemma 7.1 (fixed-multiple extension of the twin bound)

For every fixed `C>0`, the literal proof of `thm:atomtail-twin` gives, uniformly for

\[
0\le x<y\le C N^2,
\qquad 1\le y-x\ll_v\frac N{\log N},
\]

\[
\boxed{
 |\widetilde P_N(x)\cap\widetilde P_N(y)|
 \ll_{C,v} y-x.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof audit

Split the prime support into `O_v(1)` dyadic blocks `X<p<=2X`, with `X\asymp_vN`.  The source codegree dichotomy itself only needs `h=y-x<X`; this holds in the displayed range.  Its type-(A) carrier is the positive integer `N_h(x)`.  For `x<=CN^2`, every entry of its tridiagonal determinant is `O_C(N^6)`, so

\[
\log N_h(x)\ll_C h\log N.
\]

Hence it has `O_C(h)` prime divisors in `(X,2X]`.  In type (B), every prime counted divides one of the integers `y-j<=CN^2`; each such integer has `O_C(1)` prime divisors in `(X,2X]`, and there are `h` possible `j`.  Thus the same proof gives `O_C(h)`.  No recurrence theorem has been extended beyond its hypotheses; only the fixed constant in the source height estimate has changed.

## Lemma 7.2 (periodic high-column tail)

Let

\[
\widetilde S_k=\{0\le t<L_vN^2:\widetilde m_N(t)\ge k\},
\qquad
M_N=\#\{p:v(p/N)\ne0\}.
\]

For `k>=C_v\sqrt{M_N}`,

\[
\boxed{
 |\widetilde S_k|
 \ll_v\frac{N^2M_N^2}{k^3}+\frac{M_N}{k}.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The proof is exactly the Q4524/Q4530 interval argument, now partitioning an ambient interval of length `O_v(N^2)` rather than `O_v(N)`.  The short interval length

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{k^2}{8C_0M_N}\right\rfloor
\]

is still `O(M_N)=O(N/\log N)`, so Lemma 7.1 applies to every pair inside it.

## Lemma 7.3 (periodic fourth moment)

One has

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{0\le t<L_vN^2}\widetilde m_N(t)^4
 \ll_v\frac{N^5}{(\log N)^3}.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Proof

The periodic first mass is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde A_N
 &=\sum_{t<L_vN^2}\widetilde m_N(t)\\
 &\le\sum_{p:v(p/N)\ne0}
 \left(\frac{L_vN^2}{p}+1\right)Z(p)
 \ll_v N\sum_{p\asymp N}Z(p)
 \ll_v\frac{N^{13/5}}{\log N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Put `K_0=C_v\sqrt{M_N}`.  The columns below `K_0` contribute at most

\[
K_0^3\widetilde A_N
\ll_v M_N^{3/2}\widetilde A_N
\ll_v N^{41/10}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

For the higher columns, the discrete fourth-power layer cake and (7.3) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{k>K_0}k^3|\widetilde S_k|
 &\ll_v N^2M_N^2\sum_{K_0<k\le M_N}1
   +M_N\sum_{k\le M_N}k^2\\
 &\ll_v N^2M_N^3+M_N^4
 \ll_v\frac{N^5}{(\log N)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.7}
\]

This dominates (7.6) and proves (7.4).

This fourth moment is not a random model.  Its only arithmetic input is the literal Apéry twin-atom metric codegree.

\boxed{

## Proposition 8.1 (CRT-row diagonal)

\[
\boxed{

 \ll_{v,w,W}DN\log N.}
\tag{8.9}

\[

\[
U_N=\max_{p\asymp N}|u_p|\ll_v\frac{\log N}{N},

\end{aligned}

At `D=N^{41/100}` this is `N^{141/100+o(1)}`.  Thus every same-row concentration allowed by the actual Apéry incidence system is harmless for the first band.

\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak O_{N,D,H}^{\rm offrow}
={}&\sum_{d\ne0}|W(|d|/D)|^2
\sum_{\substack{x,y\in\mathscr X_H\\t_x\ne t_y}}
 u_pu_qu_{p'}u_{q'}\\
&\times
 \widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{p q}\right)
 \overline{\widehat w\!\left(\frac{Nd}{p'q'}\right)}
 e\!\left(d\left(
   \frac{t_x}{pq}-\frac{t_y}{p'q'}
 \right)\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{9.1}
\]

This is not SDC or centered AD-BDH renamed.  It is the precise TT* off-row moment left after the source-proved equal-index theorem, two new near-gap deletions, and the twin-controlled row diagonal have been removed.

## No exact zero frequency

By (2.5), both fractions in (9.1) are reduced.  If

\[
\frac{t_x}{pq}=\frac{t_y}{p'q'}
\quad\text{in }(0,1),
\]

then reducedness gives

\[
pq=p'q',\qquad t_x=t_y.
\]

For prime factors, equality of the products only permits the same ordered pair or its swap; in either case equality of fractions forces the same CRT row.  Such terms belong to (8.8), not (9.1).  Thus (9.1) has no hidden diagonal or zero frequency.

## Near-Farey determinant

Repeated summation by parts in `d` shows that a pair in (9.1) is negligible unless

\[
0<\left|
 \frac{t_x}{pq}-\frac{t_y}{p'q'}
\right|
\ll\frac{N^\varepsilon}{D}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Equivalently, its nonzero integer determinant satisfies

\[
\boxed{
0<|t_xp'q'-t_ypq|
\ll\frac{N^{4+\varepsilon}}D.}
\tag{9.3}
\]

At `D=N^{41/100}` the right side has scale `N^{359/100+epsilon}`.

The source twin/codegree theorem controls

- repeated defining primes at two nearby **additive** rows;
- the high-column tails of one CRT row;
- hence the entire row diagonal (8.9).

It does not control the multiplicative four-denominator determinant (9.3) when `t_x!=t_y`.  Multiplying marginal zero densities would be exactly the forbidden missing conditional theorem.

## Critical exponent

By (8.5), the sufficient endpoint estimate is

\[
\boxed{
 \operatorname{Re}\mathfrak O_{N,D,H}^{\rm offrow}
 \ll N^{159/100-\eta}}
\tag{9.4}
\]

for some fixed `eta>0`; the row diagonal is already much smaller.  This would give

\[
\mathcal S_{D,H}^{\rm far}
\ll N^{2-\eta/2}.
\]

For comparison, before the near-gap subtraction, Q4507 fixed-`d` Parseval gives

\[
 |B_d^{\rm tr}|\ll Q_N,
 \qquad
 Q_N\ll N^{3/5}\log N,
\]

because the equal-index piece is only `N^{3/10+o(1)}`.  Hence

\[
\sum_{d\asymp D}|B_d^{\rm tr}|^2
\ll DQ_N^2
\ll N^{161/100+o(1)}
\tag{9.5}
\]

at the first-band endpoint.  The precise remaining TT* gain is therefore a factor strictly larger than

\[
N^{161/100-159/100}=N^{1/50}.
\]

The Bettin--Chandee near-gap sectors already satisfy the `159/100` budget by (5.13); the CRT-row diagonal satisfies it by (8.9).  The deficit is entirely in the distinct-row near-Farey correlation (9.1).

# 10. Why the attempted full extensions stop

## 10.1 Summing over `d` first

The exact CRT-row form gives a smooth kernel concentrated near `t=0` or `t=pq`.  In the present band its natural endpoint width is

\[
R\asymp\frac{N^2}{D}=N^{159/100}\quad(D=N^{41/100}).
\]

Extending the Q4524 high-column calculation to an interval of length `R` gives

\[
\sum_{t\le R}\widetilde m_N(t)^2
\ll R\frac{N^{13/10}}{(\log N)^{3/2}}+\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^2}.
\]

After the weights and the factor `ND` are restored, this has the scale `N^{23/10+o(1)}`, independent of `D`.  Thus endpoint counting alone is far too weak.  The signed reciprocal oscillation is essential.

## 10.2 Classical Farey spacing

The reduced fractions have denominators `pq\asymp N^2`; classical spacing is `N^{-4}`.  A length-`D` large sieve therefore pays a denominator cost vastly larger than the target.  Removing the exact diagonal does not improve the minimal spacing enough.

## 10.3 Bettin--Chandee beyond the near gaps

For one fixed pair of positions `(r,s)`, Bettin--Chandee supplies the saving used in Proposition 5.1.  Summing its bound over all `O(N)` possible gaps costs a full factor `N`; the first zero moment and the horizontal high-column theorem do not provide an `N^{-1}` replacement.  A vector- or tensor-valued extension strong enough to sum all gaps would itself be new arithmetic and is not contained in the published scalar theorem.

## 10.4 Marginal zero densities

The event in (9.1) conditions simultaneously on four actual zero incidences and a near-Farey determinant.  Neither

\[
Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}
\]

nor the first zero moment permits the product of four marginal densities.  The Q4524 high-column theorem supplies genuine joint information only when rows coincide or are additively close in the same defining characteristic; it does not imply the determinant distribution (9.3).
