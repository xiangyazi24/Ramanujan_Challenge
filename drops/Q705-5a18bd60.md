# L1 exponential sums and the true Apéry-specific mechanism

## Executive conclusion

The proposed L1 route is not the right mechanism. In general, even a perfectly random map
\(x:\mathbf F_p\to\mathbf F_p\) has
\[
\sum_a\left|\sum_r e_p(ax_r)\right|\asymp p^{3/2},
\]
while having only about one zero. Therefore AFP cannot come from a generic Fourier inequality.

The Apéry input has to be the special geometry of the non-autonomous \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\)-cocycle / Picard--Fuchs system.

## 1. Recurrence exponential sums

The ratio
\[
x_{r+1}=\frac{P(r)x_r-r^3}{(r+1)^3x_r}
\]
is a non-autonomous Riccati orbit. There is no general theorem saying that an order-two polynomial-coefficient recurrence has O(1) zeros modulo p.

This is false in general: special recurrences can have many zeros because of hidden symmetries, CM, or factorization. The coefficient degree alone is not a zero bound.

The relevant object is not a scalar recurrence but the projective cocycle
\[
M(r)\in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf F_p),\qquad
[b_r:b_{r-1}]=M(r-1)\cdots M(0)[5:1].
\]
A zero is a hitting event of this orbit on a fixed point of \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\).

## 2. Why holonomicity is insufficient

A hypothetical theorem

> order d + coefficient degree c implies at most f(d,c) zeros mod p

cannot hold in this generality.

The finite-field problem is different from characteristic-zero Sturm theory. Reduction modulo p destroys analytic positivity and interlacing. A holonomic recurrence gives algebraic complexity, but not distribution of orbit points in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\).

## 3. What monodromy adds

The Apéry recurrence is special because its differential equation has large geometric monodromy. This gives two important consequences:

1. The transfer matrices generate a large subgroup (experimentally SL2 over finite fields).
2. The orbit should behave like a random walk on a homogeneous space.

If one could prove a spectral gap / expansion statement for the non-autonomous cocycle, then the hitting probability of the point \([0:1]\) would be about \(1/p\), giving \(O(1)\) zeros on average.

The missing theorem is therefore a cocycle equidistribution theorem, not a recurrence-degree theorem.

## 4. Legendre comparison

The Legendre family illustrates the obstruction. Its many zeros come from an exceptional involution and extra symmetry. Large monodromy alone is not enough; one also needs absence of special subvarieties forcing repeated returns.

For Apéry, the absence of such an involution is evidence for sparse zeros, but it is not yet a proof.

## 5. The right conjectural framework

A plausible finite-field Sturm principle would be:

> For a Picard--Fuchs system with geometrically large monodromy and no exceptional algebraic symmetry, the marked projective cocycle is equidistributed in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\), so hitting a fixed point has the expected frequency.

This is closer to Katz-style monodromy and Deligne equidistribution than to classical Sturm theory.

For Apéry, proving AFP likely requires one of:

- a spectral gap theorem for the transfer cocycle;
- a horizontal Frobenius/Mellin anti-concentration theorem (TCMA2/ZM2 type);
- an algebraic gap-polynomial theorem controlling simultaneous returns.

The empirical bound \(|Z_p|=O(1)\) is therefore a manifestation of arithmetic mixing of the Picard--Fuchs cocycle, not a consequence of low recurrence degree alone.
