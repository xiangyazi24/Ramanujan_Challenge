# Q7228: abstract exponent barrier for the conditioned four-window bridge majorant

## Conclusion

The stated axioms do **not** force
\[
B_p^{[4]}(K)=p^{1+o(1)}.
\]
They permit examples with
\[
B_p^{[4]}(K)\ge p^{3/2-o(1)}.
\]
The obstruction is not a missing estimate: the abstract hypotheses control pair collisions and short fixed-gap multiplicities, but they do not control the number of *compatible long bridges between two independent short return windows*.

Therefore the strongest exponent available from the listed axioms is the trivial fiber-energy scale
\(p^{3/2+o(1)}\), not the desired linear scale.

---

## 1. What the axioms actually give

Let \(m_a=|\pi^{-1}(a)|\). The fiber-square energy assumption is
\[
\sum_a m_a^2\ll p^{3/2}.
\]
For two separated four-occurrence windows, the first window chooses a state \(a\), and the external window must lie in the same fiber. After forgetting internal consecutive structure, the only universal bound is
\[
\sum_a m_a^2\ll p^{3/2}.
\]
The bridge condition
\[
G>K\asymp \frac{\sqrt p}{\sqrt{\log p}}
\]
removes short overlaps but does not reduce this energy scale.

A saving to \(p^{1+o(1)}\) would require an additional mixing statement: roughly, a square-root cancellation between the left and right occurrences after conditioning on the first window. No such statement follows from the given caps.

---

## 2. Countermodel construction

Take a prime \(p\) and choose a fiber decomposition of \(\{0,\dots,p-1\}\) as follows.

Let
\[
M=\lfloor p^{1/2}\rfloor.
\]
Choose \(M\) colors \(c_1,\dots,c_M\). Put approximately
\[
L=\lfloor p/M\rfloor\asymp p^{1/2}
\]
points into each color class. Remaining points are singleton colors.

Then
\[
\sum_a m_a^2
=M L^2+O(p)
\asymp p^{3/2},
\]
so the fiber-square energy axiom is saturated.

The construction can be made reflection symmetric by defining the label on the reflected point by
\[
\pi(p-1-x)=\pi(x).
\]
This only changes constants.

---

## 3. Fixed-gap caps can simultaneously hold

The cap condition requires every interval displacement \(h\) satisfy
\[
\#\{x:\pi(x)=\pi(x+h)\}\le 3(h-1).
\]

The large fibers above are not placed as intervals. Instead, distribute each color class using a Sidon-type placement on the cyclic line.

For each color \(c_i\), choose a set \(S_i\subset \mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) of size \(L\) with bounded difference multiplicity:
\[
|S_i\cap(S_i-h)|\le 3(h-1)
\]
for every nonzero h in the required range.

A greedy difference construction gives such sets because the total forbidden choices at step \(r\) is
\[
O(r^2),
\]
while the available interval length is \(p\). Splitting into \(M\) disjoint translates preserves the cap.

Thus the all-pair fixed-gap caps do not contradict the \(p^{3/2}\) energy.

---

## 4. Four-consecutive windows

The remaining conditions are local:

* span \(\le \sqrt p\),
* gaps at least 2,
* nonconstant triples,
* off-center,
* separated supports.

Inside each large fiber, reserve blocks of length \(O(\sqrt p)\) containing patterns
\[
(x,x+a,x+b,x+s)
\]
with four consecutive occurrences in that fiber.

Because the fixed-gap caps only bound each displacement separately, they do not prevent having \(\asymp p^{1/2}\) such windows distributed through a fiber.

Reserve the reflected copies simultaneously. The reflection quotient identifies the pair but loses only a factor 2.

---

## 5. Size of the bridge count

For a large fiber with \(L\asymp p^{1/2}\) occurrences, the number of ordered pairs of admissible short windows with a bridge longer than \(K\) is
\[
\asymp L^2
\asymp p.
\]
There are
\[
M\asymp p^{1/2}
\]
such fibers, hence
\[
B_p^{[4]}(K)
\asymp M L^2
\asymp p^{3/2}.
\]

The support separation only removes pairs whose windows intersect or approach within \(K\). Since \(K=o(p^{1/2})\), a positive proportion of pairs remain.

Therefore
\[
B_p^{[4]}(K)=p^{3/2-o(1)}
\]
is compatible with every listed abstract hypothesis.

---

## 6. Why the desired Apéry estimate needs extra input

The actual projective Apéry orbit has stronger structure:

* continuant identities,
* Frobenius/Lucas constraints,
* algebraic monodromy,
* recurrence-defined correlations between different gaps.

The abstract axioms erase exactly the information needed. In particular, the near-bridge argument succeeds because the consecutive-window map is injective into \((p,x,s,G)\), but the far bridge count loses this rigidity.

A theorem of the form
\[
B_p^{[4]}(K)\ll p^{1+o(1)}
\]
would require an additional statement such as a cross-gap correlation bound
\[
\sum_x 1_{N_s(x)=0}1_{N_G(x+s)=0}
\ll p^{-1/2+o(1)}\,p
\]
uniformly in \((s,G)\), or an equivalent monodromy/large-sieve theorem. It is not a consequence of the present axioms.

Hence the optimal purely abstract exponent is
\[
\boxed{3/2}
\]
and the claimed \(p^{1+o(1)}\) saving is genuinely Apéry-specific rather than combinatorial.
