# Greene hypergeometric cancellation and AFP

## Executive conclusion

The Gamma-unit representation explains why AFP has the correct random scale, but it does not by itself provide a proof. The missing statement is not a local valuation theorem; it is a horizontal anti-concentration theorem for the family of Mellin parameters.

The random model is accurate:
\[
\Pr(\Theta_{p,r}=0)\approx 1/p,
\]
so the expected number of zeros is about one. However, the Morita Gamma residues are highly correlated, and no existing theorem turns those correlations into the required cancellation bound.

---

## 1. Why the random-unit argument is not yet rigorous

Write
\[
\Theta_{p,r}=\sum_{k=0}^{\rho}\bar U_{p,r,k}^2.
\]
Every summand is a nonzero element of \(\mathbf F_p\), but the family
\[
(\bar U_{p,r,0},\ldots,\bar U_{p,r,\rho})
\]
is not sampled from independent random variables.

A rigorous version would require a theorem of the form:

\[
\#\{r< p:\Theta_{p,r}=0\}=O(1)
\]

or at least

\[
\sum_{p\le X}\#\{r:\Theta_{p,r}=0\}\ll \pi(X).
\]

This is precisely AFP/MLL, rewritten in the Gross--Koblitz coordinates.

---

## 2. Dependence of Gamma units

The units \(\bar U_{p,r,k}\) are constrained by:

* the Gross--Koblitz formula;
* Morita Gamma reflection and multiplication identities;
* the hypergeometric relation between the four Jacobi factors;
* the fact that all terms arise from one Frobenius object.

Therefore the cancellation event is not a random quadratic equation. The variables are coupled by the same crystalline representation.

Known identities control valuations and slopes, but they do not control additive cancellation among unit residues.

---

## 3. The quadratic-form viewpoint

For fixed \(r\),
\[
\Theta_{p,r}=U_0^2+\cdots+U_\rho^2
\]
looks like a quadratic form. However this does not give a root bound because the variables \(U_k\) are not free coordinates.

Chevalley--Warning counts solutions when variables range independently over an affine space. Here there is only one parameter \(r\), and the vector
\[
(U_0,\ldots,U_\rho)
\]
is a highly constrained Gamma trajectory.

The relevant problem is a one-dimensional intersection problem, not a quadratic-form counting problem.

---

## 4. Kloosterman comparison

The Kloosterman Mellin value is essentially
\[
g(\chi)^2,
\]
a single nonzero Gauss product, hence it cannot vanish.

The Apéry Mellin value contains an additional trace summation. Geometrically, this corresponds to taking a Frobenius trace of a higher-rank object. Zero occurs when different Frobenius eigencomponents cancel.

Thus the obstruction is not lack of nonvanishing of the pieces; it is cancellation of a rank-two/rank-four trace.

---

## 5. What Stickelberger gives

Stickelberger theory determines valuations of Gauss and Jacobi sums through carry data. It gives information such as:

* slopes;
* Newton polygons;
* ordinary versus supersingular behavior.

It does not generally give residue-level cancellation estimates such as
\[
\sum_k \bar U_{p,r,k}^2\ne0.
\]

The missing input is horizontal: it compares different tame characters \(r\), not different terms inside one Jacobi sum.

---

## 6. Correct proof target

The natural formulation is:

For the crystalline Mellin family
\[
H_{p,r}=H^1_{rig,c}(U,V\otimes K_{\omega^{-r}}),
\]
prove that simultaneous unit-root failures have bounded density.

Equivalently, prove a two-twist estimate such as
\[
\sum_{p\le X}\binom{|Z_p|/2}{2}\ll \pi(X).
\]

This is TCMA2. It is stronger than individual Deligne bounds and exactly measures whether two independent tame branches can both lose their unit root.

---

## Final answer

The Gross--Koblitz/Morita Gamma formula gives the correct microscopic model for AFP: a sum of many p-adic units should cancel with probability about \(1/p\). But making this rigorous requires a theorem about the horizontal family of Gamma residues or Frobenius twists. Stickelberger, Weil bounds, and quadratic-form counting control local structure and valuations, not the required cancellation probability.

The missing theorem is therefore a **horizontal crystalline Mellin anti-concentration theorem**, not a stronger one-variable Gamma identity.
