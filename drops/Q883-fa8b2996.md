# Q883 audit: multicutoff Legendre–Euler content carrier

## Status

No unconditional proof of `log I_n=o(n)` is obtained from the current coefficient-content formulation. The strongest unconditional statement available from the stated definitions is a support reduction: large surviving primes in `I_n` are constrained to the q=1 Apéry congruence support, but the required valuation estimate for the remaining small-prime and prime-power part is not supplied by Kummer/Lucas alone.

## 1. Large-prime valuation reduction

For

\[
\kappa_{n,J}=\gcd_{J\le k\le n}\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}\binom kJ,
\]

Lucas' theorem gives a necessary condition for a prime `p` with `p>sqrt(2n)` to divide `\kappa_{n,J}`. Since `p^2>2n`, all carries are single-base-p digits. If `p|\kappa_{n,J}`, then for every `k\in[J,n]`:

\[
 v_p\left(\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}\binom kJ\right)>0.
\]

Taking `k=J` and `k=J+1` whenever available forces the corresponding Lucas digit restrictions simultaneously. These restrictions imply that any such prime cannot arise as a free coefficient-content nuisance; it must survive through the Apéry/Legendre transform coefficient profile. Hence the only currently identifiable large-prime support is the known q=1 candidate support:

\[
p\in(n/2,n],\qquad p\mid b_{\min(n-p,2p-1-n)}.
\]

This is a support lemma only; it gives no upper bound on the product of surviving candidates.

## 2. Small primes are not controlled by the cutoff gcd

For `p\le\sqrt{2n}`, Kummer gives

\[
v_p\binom nk=\#\{\text{base-}p\text{ carries in }k+(n-k)\},
\]

and similarly for the other two factors. The gcd over `J\in[\lfloor(n-1)/3\rfloor,\lfloor n/2\rfloor]` does force some carries, but the number of forced carries is not uniformly positive over the whole interval.

In particular, the following tempting statement is false without extra hypotheses:

> For every small prime p, one central cutoff J has a coefficient with zero p-valuation.

A counterexample mechanism is when all admissible `k` in the interval inherit a base-p carry from the fixed digits of `n`; the interval length is insufficient to guarantee a carry-free representative. Therefore the cutoff gcd does not by itself remove all small-prime powers.

## 3. What would be sufficient

A genuinely weaker explicit lemma than `log I_n=o(n)` would be:

**Central-content depletion lemma.** There exists `\delta>0` such that for every `n` and every prime power `p^a\le n`,

\[
\min_{H\le J\le M}v_p(\Gamma_{n,J})\le (1-\delta)\log_p n.
\]

Together with the large-prime support reduction this would imply

\[
\log I_n
\le
O(\sqrt n\log n)+
\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt{2n}\\p\text{ candidate}}}\log p,
\]

reducing the problem to the genuine q=1 candidate bound. This lemma is non-tautological because it asks for valuation depletion at every small prime power.

## 4. Scoped obstruction

The following class of certificates cannot prove the desired estimate:

- integral linear combinations of the rows `\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}\binom kJ`;
- determinant identities whose entries are only these binomial coefficients;
- fixed finite products of such determinants with polynomially bounded coefficients.

Reason: their p-adic valuation is determined by the same Kummer carry data. Whenever a prime p divides all entries because every admissible k forces a carry pattern, every integral transform in this class inherits the same valuation lower bound. Such transforms cannot create a new Apéry-specific depletion mechanism.

This obstruction does not rule out nonlinear Apéry identities, recurrence identities, or transformations involving `F_j`/`b_j` congruences.

## 5. Conclusion

The multicutoff carrier is stronger than a single fixed-q content carrier: it removes many accidental primes experimentally and has a valid large-prime support reduction. However, no proved route currently upgrades this to

\[
\log I_n=o(n).
\]

The missing edge is an Apéry-specific p-adic depletion theorem for the central cutoff interval, not another restatement of the target height inequality. The fixed-q q=1 carrier and the three-cutoff determinant saturation theorem remain logically separate.
