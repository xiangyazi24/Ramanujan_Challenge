# Q797: analytic reduction of fixed-row fixed-gap LGTQ-2

## 1. Exact formulation

Fix q>=1 and C. Let h=qd with 1<=h<=C log n. Write

\[
P_{q;h}(n)=\#\{p: p,p-d\text{ prime},\ p\mid b_{n-qp},\ p-d\mid b_{n-q(p-d)},\ p\nmid b_{n-q(p-d)},\ p-d\nmid b_{n-qp}\}.
\]

Put

\[
u=n-qp,
\qquad v=u+h.
\]

The two diagonal congruences are

\[
b_u\equiv0\pmod p,
\qquad b_v\equiv0\pmod{p-d}.
\]

The cross exclusions remove solutions satisfying either
\[
b_v\equiv0\pmod p
\]
or
\[
b_u\equiv0\pmod{p-d}.
\]

Thus LGTQ-2 is reduced to bounding the number of primes p in the interval p~n/q for which a pair of moving-modulus congruence conditions hold.

The desired estimate is

\[
\max_{1\le d\le C\log n/q}P_{q;qd}(n)=o(n/\log^2 n).
\]

## 2. Linearization of the recurrence condition

Assume the available Apéry-type congruence description gives, for a prime r in the relevant range,

\[
b_m\pmod r = F(m,r),
\]

where F is periodic in m modulo a characteristic period T(r), or equivalently can be expressed by Frobenius traces.

For fixed q and p, the relevant indices are

\[
u=n-qp,
\qquad v=n-q(p-d)=n-qp+qd.
\]

The first condition is a trace condition

\[
\mathrm{Tr}_p(\mathcal F(n-qp))=0,
\]
and the second is

\[
\mathrm{Tr}_{p-d}(\mathcal F(n-qp+qd))=0.
\]

The difficulty is that the modulus is not fixed: the first trace lives modulo p and the second modulo p-d.

## 3. Bilinear reformulation

Introduce weights

\[
A_p(n)=1_{p\mid b_{n-qp}},
\]

and

\[
B_{r}(n)=1_{r\mid b_{n-q r}},
\]

where r=p-d. Then ignoring cross exclusions gives the upper bound

\[
P_{q;h}(n)\leq \sum_{\substack{p\sim n/q\\p,p-d\ prime}} A_p(n)B_{p-d}(n).
\]

Using additive characters,

\[
1_{p\mid b_u}=\frac1p\sum_{a\bmod p}e\left(\frac{a b_u}{p}\right).
\]

Therefore the count becomes a bilinear prime sum

\[
\sum_{p\sim X}\frac1{p(p-d)}
\sum_{a\bmod p}\sum_{c\bmod {p-d}}
 e\left(\frac{a b_{n-qp}}p+\frac{c b_{n-q(p-d)}}{p-d}\right).
\]

Here

\[
X\asymp n/q.
\]

A successful estimate would need cancellation over the prime variable p in a family of exponential sums whose moduli are the moving pair (p,p-d).

## 4. What estimate would close LGTQ-2?

A sufficient new estimate is the following.

For every fixed q,C and every d<=C log X, uniformly in n,

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\le X\\p,p-d\ prime}}
\left(A_p(n)-\alpha_p\right)\left(B_{p-d}(n)-\beta_{p-d}\right)
\ll \frac{X}{\log^{2+\epsilon}X},
\]

for some local densities alpha,beta, with any fixed epsilon>0.

Indeed the main term is then controlled by the expected density and the fluctuation contributes o(X/log^2 X). Since X~n/q, this implies

\[
P_{q;h}(n)=o(n/\log^2 n).
\]

A weaker but still sufficient exponent is any saving

\[
X/(\log X)^2\cdot o(1).
\]

A power saving X^{1-\delta} would of course suffice.

## 5. Why standard large sieve does not directly apply

The classical large sieve controls sums of the form

\[
\sum_{m\le M}\left|\sum_{n\le N}a_n e(mn/q)\right|^2
\]

or families where the moduli q are selected independently of the coefficients.

Here the modulus is coupled to the summation variable:

\[
\text{summation variable}=p,
\qquad \text{modulus}=p.
\]

The phase is schematically

\[
e\left(\frac{aF(n-qp)}p\right),
\]

so the modulus, phase denominator, and prime variable are the same object.

Therefore the ordinary large sieve hypothesis does not match this problem.

A Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem also does not apply: it averages over moduli m with an independent residue problem modulo m, while here the modulus is the prime being counted.

Likewise, Deligne-type trace-function bounds do not immediately apply because one needs a fixed finite-field family or a controlled l-adic sheaf as p varies. The family here changes characteristic with p.

## 6. Possible determinant/trace-function route

A viable route would require constructing a single algebraic family over Z:

\[
\mathcal G_{n,d}(T)\in\mathbb Z[T]
\]

such that for every prime p

\[
b_{n-qp}\bmod p = \mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p|\mathcal G_{n,d}).
\]

Then one needs a two-variable correlation estimate

\[
\sum_{p\le X,p-d\ prime}
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p\mathcal G_{n,d})
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p-d}\mathcal H_{n,d})
\ll X^{1-\delta}
\]

uniformly for

\[
d\le C\log X,\qquad n\le qX.
\]

This is a new shifted-prime correlation theorem for moving-characteristic trace families.

## 7. Comparison with known results

Known average results have a different order of averaging:

- Average in n: one can often use orthogonality because n becomes the free variable.
- Average in p with fixed modulus: standard character sums and trace-function technology apply.
- Average over moduli: Bombieri--Vinogradov applies.

None of these imply

\[
\max_n\max_{d\le C\log n}P_{q;qd}(n)=o(n/\log^2 n).
\]

The missing step is precisely a uniform fixed-n shifted-prime correlation estimate.

## 8. Conclusion

The strongest rigorous reduction currently available is:

\[
\boxed{
LGTQ\text{-}2
\Leftarrow
\sup_{n,d\le C\log n}
\left|
\sum_{p\sim n/q,p,p-d\ prime}
\Phi_{n,d}(p,p-d)
\right|
=o(n/\log^2 n)
}
\]

where

\[
\Phi_{n,d}(p,p-d)
\]
is the centered product of the two Frobenius/character indicators.

The required new theorem is not a routine large sieve estimate. It is a uniform shifted-prime correlation estimate for a recurrence-generated family with moving moduli p and p-d. Establishing such an estimate would close LGTQ-2; no standard theorem currently matches all quantifiers.
