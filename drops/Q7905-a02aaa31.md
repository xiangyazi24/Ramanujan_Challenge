# A sharp two-prime spectral/dispersion reduction for ACF3

## Verdict

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},\qquad
z_p=Z(p),\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{z_p}{p},\qquad
\lambda=\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\rho_p,
\]

and take all starred prime triples to be **ordered and pairwise distinct**, as in the current factorial-moment convention. If the displayed starred sum is instead over unordered triples, every identity below changes only by the constant factor \(3!=6\).

The useful flattening is not a Hilbert--Schmidt estimate on the original three-prime tensor. Normalize each primitive local Fourier vector to unit \(L^2\)-mass, contract the third prime, and obtain a real symmetric matrix whose only free prime indices are \(p,q\). The exact ACF3 sum is then one Rayleigh quotient of this matrix.

The sharp one-sided sufficient estimate is

\[
\boxed{
 \left(\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\sum_H A_H\right)\right)_+
 \ll X^{o(1)}\lambda^2.}
\tag{SPEC}
\]

Here \(A_H\) is the explicit dyadic pair matrix defined below. Since the vector in the Rayleigh quotient has squared norm at most \(4X^2\lambda\), (SPEC) gives

\[
 C_+\le 4X^2\lambda\,
 \left(\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\sum_HA_H\right)\right)_+
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^3.
\]

This is sharper than an absolute operator norm: only the **positive spectral edge** matters. It is also sharper than estimating every triple separately, and it is numerically testable by assembling one matrix of dimension \(\#\mathcal P_X\), not a tensor of dimension \(\#\mathcal P_X^3\).

The weakest one-Cauchy mixed second-moment substitute is

\[
\boxed{
 \left\|\left(\sum_HA_H\right)u\right\|_2^2
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^5,}
\tag{MIX}
\]

with the exact weight vector \(u_p=(pz_p-z_p^2)^{1/2}\). This also closes the target. A dyadic square-function version and the exact \(H\)-dependent powers are given below.

The important negative conclusion is equally precise. Cauchy--Schwarz is harmless as the **single outer duality step** leading to (MIX), but closing the resulting second moment using only the local identities

\[
\sum_a R_p(a)^2=pz_p
\]

necessarily loses at least one power of \(X\) in the present range. The missing input is therefore exactly a signed pair-prime spectral/dispersion inequality such as (SPEC), not another primewise Parseval estimate.

I interpret \(C_+\) as \((C)_+=\max(C,0)\), the positive part of the aggregate centered correlation. If instead \(C_+\) means the sum of the positive parts of the individual \(p,q,\ell\) summands, then cancellation between different triples is forbidden and no global pair-operator reduction can be equivalent to that stronger quantity.

## 1. Primitive local energy and the exact \(Z(p)\)-weights

For \(a\in\mathbf F_p\), the centered real amplitude satisfies

\[
R_p(0)=z_p,
\qquad
\sum_{a\bmod p}R_p(a)^2=pz_p.
\]

Since primitive \(h\) gives a nonzero local frequency at every prime, the relevant local energy is

\[
 e_p
 :=\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}R_p(a)^2
 =pz_p-z_p^2
 =p^2\rho_p(1-\rho_p).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Put

\[
 u_p=\sqrt{e_p},
 \qquad
 \phi_p(a)=\frac{R_p(a)}{u_p}\quad(a\ne0),
\tag{1.2}
\]

and omit a prime with \(e_p=0\). Then

\[
 \sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\phi_p(a)^2=1,
 \qquad
 R_p(a)=u_p\phi_p(a)\quad(a\ne0).
\tag{1.3}
\]

No pointwise estimate for \(R_p(a)\) or \(\phi_p(a)\) has been used. All possible concentration of the local Fourier mass remains inside the actual unit vector \(\phi_p\).

The global primitive energy is

\[
 E_X:=\|u\|_2^2
 =\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}(pz_p-z_p^2).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Because \(X<p\le2X\),

\[
 E_X\le\sum_ppz_p
 =\sum_pp^2\frac{z_p}{p}
 \le4X^2\lambda.
\tag{1.5}
\]

For the actual Apéry zero sets, \(z_p=o(p)\), so \(E_X\asymp X^2\lambda\); only the upper bound (1.5) is needed for the reduction.

## 2. Dyadic \(h\)-bands and the exact kernel scale

Let

\[
 \mathcal D_X=\{2^j:1\le 2^j\le4X^3\}
\]

and, for \(Q=pq\ell\), define

\[
 I_H(Q)=\left\{h\in\mathbf Z:
 H\le h<2H,
 \,1\le h<Q/2,
 \, (h,Q)=1\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

These bands partition the half-range in the formula for \(C\). For \(0<h<Q/2\),

\[
 |\sin(\pi h/Q)|\ge \frac{2h}{Q},
 \qquad
 |\sin(2\pi Mh/Q)|
 \le\min\left(1,\frac{2\pi Mh}{Q}\right),
\]

so

\[
 |K_Q(h)|\ll\min(M,Q/h).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since

\[
 X^3<Q\le8X^3,
 \qquad M=X^2,
\]

put

\[
 \omega_H:=\min\left(X^2,\frac{X^3}{H}\right).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then uniformly for \(h\in I_H(Q)\),

\[
 |K_Q(h)|\ll\omega_H.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus, up to absolute constants,

\[
 \omega_H\asymp
 \begin{cases}
 X^2,&H\le X,\\[2mm]
 X^3/H,&X<H\le4X^3.
 \end{cases}
\tag{2.5}
\]

The transition is the expected first effective scale \(H\asymp Q/M\asymp X\). Define the exact normalized kernel on the band by

\[
 \kappa_{Q,H}(h)=\frac{K_Q(h)}{\omega_H}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Then \(\kappa_{Q,H}\) retains every sign and oscillation of \(K_Q\), while

\[
 |\kappa_{Q,H}(h)|\ll1.
\tag{2.7}
\]

There are only \(O(\log X)=X^{o(1)}\) bands.

## 3. Contract the third prime: the exact two-prime matrix

For \(h\) coprime to \(Q=pq\ell\), write

\[
 a_p(h;q,\ell)=h\overline{q\ell}\pmod p,
 \quad
 a_q(h;p,\ell)=h\overline{p\ell}\pmod q,
 \quad
 a_\ell(h;p,q)=h\overline{pq}\pmod\ell.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For \(p,q\in\mathcal P_X\), define a real matrix \(A_H\) by setting

\[
 A_H(p,p)=0
\]

and, for \(p\ne q\),

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 A_H(p,q)
 := {}&
 \sum_{\substack{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\\
                   \ell\ne p,q}}
 \frac{u_\ell}{pq\ell}
 \sum_{h\in I_H(pq\ell)}
 K_{pq\ell}(h)\\
 &\qquad\qquad\times
 \phi_p\!\left(a_p(h;q,\ell)\right)
 \phi_q\!\left(a_q(h;p,\ell)\right)
 \phi_\ell\!\left(a_\ell(h;p,q)\right).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The matrix is real and symmetric:

\[
 A_H(q,p)=A_H(p,q).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Only \(p,q\) are free matrix variables. The third prime \(\ell\), together with its full arithmetic Fourier profile, has been contracted into the matrix coefficient.

Let

\[
 \widetilde A_H=\omega_H^{-1}A_H.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently, replace \(K_{pq\ell}(h)\) by \(\kappa_{pq\ell,H}(h)\) in (3.2).

Now multiply (3.2) by \(u_pu_q\) and sum over ordered \(p\ne q\). Since \(u_s\phi_s=R_s\) at every primitive local frequency,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 u^{\mathsf T}A_Hu
 ={}&
 \sum_{\substack{p,q,\ell\in\mathcal P_X\\
                   p,q,\ell\ {m distinct}}}
 \frac1{pq\ell}
 \sum_{h\in I_H(pq\ell)}
 K_{pq\ell}(h)\\
 &\qquad\times
 R_p(h\overline{q\ell})
 R_q(h\overline{p\ell})
 R_\ell(h\overline{pq})
 =:C_H.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Therefore the original trilinear expression has the exact flattening

\[
\boxed{
 C=\sum_{H\in\mathcal D_X}C_H
   =u^{\mathsf T}\left(\sum_HA_H\right)u
   =u^{\mathsf T}\left(\sum_H\omega_H\widetilde A_H\right)u.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

There is no Cauchy--Schwarz and no loss in (3.6). It is merely the mode-three contraction of the symmetric cubic tensor.

## 4. The sharp one-sided pair-prime spectral inequality

Put

\[
 A:=\sum_HA_H=\sum_H\omega_H\widetilde A_H.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For any real symmetric matrix,

\[
 u^{\mathsf T}Au\le\lambda_{\max}(A)\|u\|_2^2.
\]

Hence

\[
 C_+\le E_X\,(\lambda_{\max}(A))_+.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Combining (4.2) with (1.5) proves the claimed reduction:

> **One-sided pair spectral estimate.** It is sufficient to prove
> \[
> \boxed{
> (\lambda_{\max}(A))_+
> \ll X^{o(1)}\lambda^2.}
> \tag{P-SPEC}
> \]

Indeed,

\[
 C_+\le4X^2\lambda\cdot X^{o(1)}\lambda^2
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^3.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the weakest test-vector-independent spectral formulation naturally produced by the flattening:

* it bounds only \(\lambda_{\max}\), not the absolute operator norm;
* it treats the full signed matrix \(A\), allowing cancellation between \(h\)-bands;
* it has only the two free prime variables \(p,q\);
* it keeps the actual local Fourier vectors \(\phi_p\), rather than replacing them by pointwise majorants.

Equivalently, (P-SPEC) is the one-sided pair large-sieve inequality

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{p,q\in\mathcal P_X}v_pA(p,q)v_q
 \le X^{o(1)}\lambda^2
      \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}v_p^2
 \quad\text{for every real }v.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a genuine pair-correlation statement. The third prime has already been summed inside \(A(p,q)\), and only a quadratic form in \(p,q\) remains.

The literally weakest statement for the single arithmetic vector \(u\) would replace the universal \(v\) in (4.4) by \(v=u\). That is just the normalized original target. Requiring (4.4) for every \(v\) is the minimal robust strengthening that deserves to be called an operator estimate.

### Dyadic spectral budget

The globally weakest form is (P-SPEC). A bandwise sufficient form follows from

\[
 \lambda_{\max}\!\left(\sum_HA_H\right)
 \le\sum_H\lambda_{\max}(A_H)
 \le\sum_H(\lambda_{\max}(A_H))_+.
\]

Thus it suffices that

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_H\omega_H
  (\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde A_H))_+
 \ll X^{o(1)}\lambda^2.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

More precisely, choose nonnegative band budgets \(\eta_H\) with

\[
 \sum_H\eta_H\le X^{o(1)}.
\]

It is enough to prove

\[
 (\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde A_H))_+
 \ll \eta_H\frac{\lambda^2}{\omega_H}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

With one logarithmic unit of budget per band, (4.6) becomes

\[
\boxed{
 (\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde A_H))_+
 \ll X^{o(1)}
 \begin{cases}
 X^{-2}\lambda^2,&H\le X,\\[2mm]
 H X^{-3}\lambda^2,&X<H\le4X^3.
 \end{cases}}
\tag{4.7}
\]

These are the exact scale targets after removing the elementary size of the Dirichlet kernel.

## 5. The weakest one-Cauchy mixed second moment

Some arithmetic tools naturally produce a second moment rather than a one-sided spectral estimate. Define

\[
 \mathfrak M
 :=\|Au\|_2^2
 =\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
   \left|\sum_{q\in\mathcal P_X}A(p,q)u_q\right|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Using (3.2), the quantity inside the absolute value is explicitly

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (Au)_p
 ={}&\sum_{\substack{q\in\mathcal P_X\\q\ne p}}
 u_q
 \sum_{\substack{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\\\ell\ne p,q}}
 \frac{u_\ell}{pq\ell}
 \sum_{\substack{1\le h<pq\ell/2\\(h,pq\ell)=1}}
 K_{pq\ell}(h)\\
 &\qquad\times
 \phi_p(h\overline{q\ell})
 \phi_q(h\overline{p\ell})
 \phi_\ell(h\overline{pq}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the requested mixed pair-prime second moment: after the \(\ell\)-contraction, it is the \(L^2\)-norm of one pair operator acting in the \(q\)-variable and producing the \(p\)-variable.

One Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 C_+=\bigl(u^{\mathsf T}Au\bigr)_+
 \le\|u\|_2\|Au\|_2
 =E_X^{1/2}\mathfrak M^{1/2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently the exact sufficient estimate is

\[
\boxed{
 \mathfrak M
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^5.}
\tag{P-MIX}
\]

Indeed, (1.5) and (5.3) give

\[
 C_+\ll (X\lambda^{1/2})
          (X^{1+o(1)}\lambda^{5/2})
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^3.
\]

Among estimates obtained by a single outer Cauchy step, (P-MIX) is the weakest global one: it uses only the actual vector \(u\), and it permits cancellation between all dyadic bands before squaring.

### Dyadic square-function form

Put

\[
 \mathfrak M_H
 :=\|\widetilde A_Hu\|_2^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since

\[
 Au=\sum_H\omega_H\widetilde A_Hu,
\]

we have

\[
 \|Au\|_2
 \le\sum_H\omega_H\mathfrak M_H^{1/2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus a useful bandwise condition is

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_H\omega_H\mathfrak M_H^{1/2}
 \ll X^{1+o(1)}\lambda^{5/2}.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

By Cauchy only over the \(O(\log X)\) bands, the slightly stronger square-function estimate

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_H\omega_H^2\mathfrak M_H
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^5}
\tag{5.7}
\]

also suffices. A uniform per-band version of (5.7) is

\[
 \mathfrak M_H
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\frac{\lambda^5}{\omega_H^2}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Using (2.5), the normalized pair-second-moment targets are therefore

\[
\boxed{
 \mathfrak M_H
 \ll X^{o(1)}
 \begin{cases}
 X^{-2}\lambda^5,&H\le X,\\[2mm]
 H^2X^{-4}\lambda^5,&X<H\le4X^3.
 \end{cases}}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Equivalently, without normalizing the kernel,

\[
 \|A_Hu\|_2^2\ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^5
\tag{5.10}
\]

uniformly in \(H\), with the logarithmic number of bands absorbed into \(X^{o(1)}\).

### A stronger, vector-independent mixed estimate

A standard two-sided operator estimate is

\[
 \|\widetilde A_Hv\|_2^2
 \le \nu_H^2\|v\|_2^2
 \quad\text{for every }v,
\tag{5.11}
\]

with

\[
 \sum_H\omega_H\nu_H
 \ll X^{o(1)}\lambda^2.
\tag{5.12}
\]

This implies both (4.5) and (5.6). It is stronger than necessary because it controls the most negative eigenvalue as well as the positive edge. The one-sided quadratic inequality (4.4) is the sharper formulation for \(C_+\).

A Hilbert--Schmidt sufficient condition would be

\[
 \sum_{p,q}|\widetilde A_H(p,q)|^2
 \ll X^{o(1)}\frac{\lambda^4}{\omega_H^2},
\tag{5.13}
\]

but this is substantially stronger than (4.6), since it pays for the entire effective rank of the pair matrix. It should not be adopted as the conjectural target unless numerics show that the matrix is genuinely low rank.

## 6. Why primewise Cauchy and Parseval lose at least one power of \(X\)

The exact centered kernel is the Fourier transform of an interval of length \(2M\). Since \(Q>2M\), Parseval gives

\[
 K_Q(0)=2M,
 \qquad
 \sum_{h\bmod Q}|K_Q(h)|^2=2MQ.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Because \(Q\) is odd and \(|K_Q(h)|=|K_Q(Q-h)|\),

\[
 \sum_{1\le h<Q/2}|K_Q(h)|^2
 =MQ-2M^2
 \le MQ.
\tag{6.2}
\]

On the amplitude side, CRT gives a bijection

\[
 (\mathbf Z/Q\mathbf Z)^\times
 \longleftrightarrow
 \mathbf F_p^\times\times
 \mathbf F_q^\times\times
 \mathbf F_\ell^\times.
\]

Using evenness of every \(R_s\),

\[
 \sum_{\substack{1\le h<Q/2\\(h,Q)=1}}
 R_p(h\overline{q\ell})^2
 R_q(h\overline{p\ell})^2
 R_\ell(h\overline{pq})^2
 =\frac12e_pe_qe_\ell.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore Cauchy--Schwarz for one fixed triple gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |C_{p,q,\ell}|
 &\le \frac1Q
 (MQ)^{1/2}
 \left(\frac12e_pe_qe_\ell\right)^{1/2}\\
 &\le \left(\frac M2z_pz_qz_\ell\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Summing this absolute estimate over triples yields

\[
 |C|
 \le M^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\sqrt{z_p}\right)^3.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Let \(N_X=\#\mathcal P_X\). Since

\[
 \sum_pz_p\le2X\lambda,
\]

we obtain

\[
 \sum_p\sqrt{z_p}
 \le(N_X\sum_pz_p)^{1/2}
 \le(2XN_X\lambda)^{1/2}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Using \(N_X\ll X^{1+o(1)}\), or \(N_X\asymp X/\log X\) if one keeps logarithms, gives

\[
 |C|\ll X^{4+o(1)}\lambda^{3/2},
\tag{6.7}
\]

more precisely

\[
 |C|\ll
 \frac{X^4\lambda^{3/2}}{(\log X)^{3/2}}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The target is \(X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^3\). Thus the ratio of the Cauchy bound to the target is

\[
 X^{2-o(1)}\lambda^{-3/2}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

The currently available zero-set bound \(z_p\ll p^{2/3+o(1)}\) implies

\[
 \lambda\ll X^{2/3+o(1)},
\]

so even at the largest allowed density, (6.9) is at least

\[
 X^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

In the reflected-doublet regime \(z_p=2\), where \(\lambda\asymp1/\log X\), the loss is about \(X^2\).

Hence:

* one outer Cauchy step is a legitimate **reduction** if followed by the new mixed estimate (P-MIX);
* Cauchy in \(h\) for each triple, or a second Cauchy followed only by local Parseval, cannot close ACF3;
* absolute row sums or a bare Hilbert--Schmidt estimate discard exactly the cross-prime signs that must supply the saving.

## 7. Reflection and Plancherel alone cannot imply (SPEC)

The reflected anchored family already constructed in `hm3_result.tex` makes the obstruction spectral.

For \(L\asymp X/\log X\) active primes, take a reflected doublet at every prime, all anchored at the same row \(m_0<M\). Then

\[
 z_p=2,
 \qquad
 \lambda^*\asymp\frac1{\log X},
 \qquad
 E_X^*=\sum_p(2p-4)\asymp\frac{X^2}{\log X}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The raw triple incidence at \(m_0\) contributes \((L)_3\), while all lower-frequency centered strata have total size \(O(M(\lambda^*)^3)\). Therefore

\[
 C^*\gg\frac{X^3}{(\log X)^3}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Let \(A^*\) be the pair matrix produced by the exact flattening. Since \(C^*=(u^*)^{\mathsf T}A^*u^*\),

\[
 \lambda_{\max}(A^*)
 \ge\frac{C^*}{\|u^*\|_2^2}
 \gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}
 \asymp X(\lambda^*)^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The desired spectral scale is only \((\lambda^*)^2\). Thus the abstract reflected family misses (SPEC) by the full factor \(X\), despite satisfying the same reality, evenness, local Parseval identity, reflection symmetry, and natural second-moment scale.

This proves that (SPEC) cannot follow from those local facts alone. It must use arithmetic incompatibility of the actual Apéry Fourier profiles across different primes.

## 8. The non-Cauchy alternative: pair-Palm positive excess

If a signed operator estimate is not approachable, the exact physical-space alternative is already pairwise and one-sided.

Let

\[
 \Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\},
 \qquad f_p=\mathbf1_{\Omega_p},
\]

and for ordered \(p\ne q\), put

\[
 J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|,
\]

\[
 T_{p,q}
 =\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
   \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}f_\ell(m),
\]

\[
 \lambda_{p,q}
 =\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{p,q\}}
   \frac{z_\ell}{\ell}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then

\[
 D_{p,q}:=T_{p,q}-J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}
\tag{8.2}
\]

is the overload of third-prime hits conditioned on the actual \(p,q\) CRT locus.

The exact centered divisor expansion gives

\[
 C=\sum_{p\ne q}D_{p,q}+O(M\lambda^3),
\tag{8.3}
\]

because the difference consists only of the other two pair-density strata, the three one-column strata, and the constant-density stratum; the proved second factorial moment bounds all of them by \(O(M\lambda^3)\).

Therefore the weakest non-Cauchy pair condition for the aggregate positive part is

\[
\boxed{
 \left(\sum_{p\ne q}D_{p,q}\right)_+
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^3.}
\tag{P-PALM-agg}
\]

For the full third factorial moment, the repository's sharper pair--Palm characterization is

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{p\ne q}(D_{p,q})_+
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda^3.}
\tag{P-PALM}
\]

The latter is stronger than (P-PALM-agg), but it is exactly equivalent, up to the already-budgeted \(O(M\lambda^3)\) baseline, to the desired third-factorial-moment estimate.

This is the genuinely non-Cauchy alternative: it preserves the one-sided sign after conditioning on a pair instead of replacing the signed correlation by an \(L^2\) norm.

## 9. What should be tested numerically

For each \(X\), compute \(z_p,R_p,e_p,u_p,\phi_p\), assemble the sparse matrices \(A_H\), and record

\[
 \mathcal R_{\rm ray}
 =\frac{(u^{\mathsf T}Au)_+}{E_X\lambda^2},
\]

\[
 \mathcal R_{\rm spec}
 =\frac{(\lambda_{\max}(A))_+}{\lambda^2},
\]

\[
 \mathcal R_{\rm mix}
 =\frac{\|Au\|_2^2}{X^2\lambda^5},
\]

and, band by band,

\[
 \mathcal R_H^{\rm spec}
 =\frac{\omega_H(\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde A_H))_+}{\lambda^2},
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_H^{\rm mix}
 =\frac{\omega_H^2\|\widetilde A_Hu\|_2^2}
        {X^2\lambda^5}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

The target predicts \(X^{o(1)}\) growth. The gap between \(\mathcal R_{\rm ray}\) and \(\mathcal R_{\rm spec}\) measures how much is lost by asking for a universal test vector; the gap between \(\mathcal R_{\rm spec}\) and a Hilbert--Schmidt ratio measures the effective-rank loss.

A direct implementation outline is:

```text
for p in primes(X,2X]:
    e[p] = p*Z[p] - Z[p]^2
    u[p] = sqrt(e[p])
    phi[p,a] = R[p,a] / u[p]          # a != 0

for dyadic H:
    A_H = zero_matrix(number_of_primes)
    for distinct p,q,l:
        Q = p*q*l
        for h in [H,2H) with h < Q/2 and gcd(h,Q)=1:
            A_H[p,q] += (u[l]/Q) * K_Q(h) \
                * phi[p,h*inv(q*l,p)] \
                * phi[q,h*inv(p*l,q)] \
                * phi[l,h*inv(p*q,l)]

A = sum_H A_H
compute top_eigenvalue(A), norm(A*u)^2, and u^T*A*u
```

The decisive numerical question is not whether individual \(R_p(a)\) have square-root size. It is whether the positive spectral edge of the signed reciprocal pair matrix is at the density scale \(\lambda^2\), rather than the anchored obstruction scale \(X\lambda^2\).

## 10. Final formulation of the missing arithmetic lemma

The cleanest theorem to attack is the following.

> **ACF3 pair-spectral lemma.** For the actual Apéry zero-set amplitudes, let \(A\) be the real symmetric pair matrix (3.2), summed over all dyadic \(h\)-bands. Then
> \[
> \lambda_{\max}(A)
> \ll X^{o(1)}\lambda_X^2.
> \]
> Equivalently, for every real prime coefficient sequence \(v_p\),
> \[
> \sum_{p\ne q}v_pv_qA(p,q)
> \ll X^{o(1)}\lambda_X^2\sum_pv_p^2.
> \]

A weaker actual-vector lemma, still sufficient, is (P-MIX):

\[
 \sum_p\left|\sum_qA(p,q)\sqrt{qZ(q)-Z(q)^2}\right|^2
 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^5.
\]

Either statement replaces the opaque trilinear conjecture by one explicit pair-prime correlation inequality. The spectral form is the sharp one-sided version; the mixed second moment is the weakest standard one-Cauchy version. Neither follows from reflection and local Parseval alone, and any proof must exploit genuine cross-prime arithmetic cancellation in the reciprocal arguments.
