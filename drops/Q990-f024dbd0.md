# Fresh-prime transverse Xi attack

## Verdict

**OPEN**

The direct characteristic-zero eliminant route does not currently produce a proved saving for the fresh-prime tail. The exact obstruction is not local divisibility: the fourth-order contact identity gives a valid common-prime condition. The obstruction is converting the dyadic family of local conditions

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad p\mid \Xi_r
\]

for different fresh primes into one nonzero integer with height below the accumulated prime product.

The smallest missing lemma is:

> **Fresh transverse Xi eliminant lemma.** There exists a nonzero characteristic-zero integer \(E_R\), constructed uniformly from the Apéry recurrence and the sequence \(\Xi_r\), such that every fresh common prime \(p>2R\) dividing \(\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)\) for \(R<r\le 2R\) divides \(E_R\), while
> \(\log |E_R|=o(R^2)\).

No such eliminant is currently proved.

## 1. Exact sequence identities

Write the Apéry recurrence in the repository normalization as the second-order orbit

\[
(b_{r-1},b_r),
\]

with initial data

\[
(b_0,b_1)=(1,5).
\]

The transverse quantity is

\[
\Xi_r=r^3(b_{r-1}\kappa_r-b_r\kappa_{r-1}).
\]

It is a genuine Casoratian-type expression: it is not an independent sequence. Any eliminant must retain the recurrence relation satisfied by both \(b_r\) and the auxiliary \(\kappa_r\).

For a target prime \(p\mid b_r\), reduction modulo \(p\) gives

\[
\Xi_r\equiv r^3 b_{r-1}\kappa_r\pmod p
\]

provided the recurrence nondegeneracy gives the usual adjacent nonzero relation. Hence the fourth-order contact condition adds

\[
p\mid b_{r-1}\kappa_r
\]

in the appropriate local branch.

This is a local statement at the single prime p. It does not yet give a global integer.

---

## 2. Candidate Casoratian elimination

The natural attempt is to use a determinant involving

\[
(b_r,\Xi_r)
\]

or the augmented orbit

\[
(b_r,b_{r+1},\kappa_r,\kappa_{r+1}).
\]

A typical candidate is

\[
D_{r,s}=\det
\begin{pmatrix}
 b_r&b_s\\
 \Xi_r&\Xi_s
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

However, because \(\Xi_r\) is already a linear combination of adjacent Apéry states, this determinant collapses to recurrence boundary terms. It does not provide an independent fourth-order constraint.

More generally, any fixed-size exterior product of the holonomic orbit has rank bounded by the dimension of the underlying recurrence module. It cannot automatically carry all \(R\) fresh primes.

---

## 3. Why singleton fresh primes are insufficient

For \(p>2R\), the same prime cannot normally occur at two indices in the dyadic interval because the residue interval has length smaller than p. This gives a useful singleton property:

\[
p\leftrightarrow r.
\]

But singleton does not imply nonvanishing of a global resultant. It only says that a local prime condition belongs to one index.

To obtain the desired estimate one would need a product compression:

\[
\prod_{R<r\le2R}\gcd(b_r,\Xi_r)
\]

bounded through one integer. The singleton property alone does not construct that integer.

---

## 4. Prime powers and normalization audit

The object is a gcd, so prime powers must be treated separately.

If

\[
p^a\mid b_r,\qquad p^a\mid \Xi_r,
\]

then the contact argument modulo p only proves the first valuation layer. A resultant argument would need valuation control, not just support control.

Multiplying local primitive determinants also fails because it gives

\[
\sum_r \log |D_r|
\]

rather than a global height saving.

The monomial integer normalization and integer-valued normalization of divided differences must also be separated. Clearing denominators can reintroduce an exponential factor comparable to the Apéry growth.

---

## 5. Scoped no-go for natural eliminants

A valid scoped obstruction is:

> Any eliminant built only from bounded-size determinants, Casoratians, or resultants of the recurrence orbit \((b_r,\kappa_r)\) cannot prove the fresh-prime product saving.

Reason:

1. the orbit has fixed holonomic dimension;
2. all such determinants reduce to fixed-dimensional recurrence boundary data;
3. a dyadic family of O(R) singleton prime conditions requires an object whose dimension grows with R;
4. bounded-dimensional eliminants cannot encode independent fresh-prime divisibility while retaining subquadratic height.

This is an Apéry-specific obstruction, not a CRT model.

## 6. Finite verifier

A finite audit can be implemented as:

```python
from math import gcd

# b and kappa are generated from the exact repository recurrence
for r in range(R+1,2*R+1):
    xi = r**3*(b[r-1]*kap[r]-b[r]*kap[r-1])
    g = gcd(b[r], xi)
    print(r, factor(g))
```

This verifies candidate common primes and prime powers but cannot establish asymptotic decay.
