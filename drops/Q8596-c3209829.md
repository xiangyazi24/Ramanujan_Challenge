# Q8596 — common-row/long-continuant carrier audit

## 1. Status of the proposed extra condition

The Wronskian implication is valid but does not by itself produce a cross-characteristic carrier.

For a regular hit,
\[
p_i\mid b_{u_i},\qquad p_i\mid b_m,
\]
and the primitive Casoratian gives
\[
p_i\mid N_{m-u_i}(u_i).
\]
The target condition also gives
\[
p_i\mid m-z_i.
\]
Thus every target prime divides the integer
\[
G_i:=\gcd(m-z_i,N_{m-u_i}(u_i)).
\]
The question is whether \(G_i\) has sufficiently small height and enough cross-characteristic independence to replace the existing shifted-defect count.

The answer from the current identities is no.

The obstruction is that both entries of the gcd are constructed with the same prime-dependent Apéry orbit data. The divisibility by \(p_i\) is a local consequence of the chosen residue and does not imply that the integer carrier is small. A useful carrier would need a uniform bound such as
\[
\log G_i\ll X^{o(1)}
\]
for every admissible fibre, or an averaged version with the missing cubic saving. No such bound follows from the Wronskian.

## 2. The possible resultant formulation

A natural candidate is
\[
R_i=\operatorname{Res}_Y(Y-(m-z_i),F_{m-u_i,u_i}(Y)),
\]
where \(F\) is an integer polynomial representation of the continuant row.
However this does not improve the situation: after substituting the fixed row, the resultant is just another encoding of
\[
N_{m-u_i}(u_i)\bmod (m-z_i).
\]
Its height is governed by the continuant length \(m-u_i\), which is of size up to \(X^2\). The determinant identity controls a residue class, not the archimedean size of the resulting integer.

Therefore a resultant theorem would have to be genuinely new: it must assert cancellation in these large continuants, not merely rewrite divisibility.

## 3. Full-period reduction does not close the gap

If
\[
m-u_i\equiv0\pmod {p_i},
\]
then one may try to reduce the continuant period modulo \(p_i\). This is the exceptional case where the transfer matrix cycle might simplify.

But this condition is not forced by the target equations. In general
\[
m-u_i=c_ip_i+z_i-u_i,
\]
and on the positive branch \(z_i=u_i\), giving
\[
m-u_i=c_ip_i+(z_1-z_i).
\]
The defect term is nonzero in a full-defect fibre. Hence
\[
m-u_i\not\equiv0\pmod {p_i}
\]
unless the defect accidentally vanishes, which is excluded.

On the negative branch the same calculation gives an additional fixed shift from
\[
z_i=-u_i-1,
\]
but it does not force period alignment either. Thus full-period reduction is unavailable on the regular signed fibres being counted.

## 4. Positive and negative branch audit

For the positive branch,
\[
z_i=u_i,
\]
so
\[
m-u_i=m-z_i.
\]
The two divisibilities become
\[
p_i\mid(m-u_i),\qquad p_i\mid N_{m-u_i}(u_i).
\]
This is the strongest-looking case, but the first divisibility is already the residue condition and adds no independent characteristic.

For the negative branch,
\[
z_i=-u_i-1,
\]
so
\[
m-u_i=(m-z_i)-2u_i-1.
\]
The Wronskian divisibility and residue divisibility are not aligned. No extra cancellation is obtained.

The coefficient restrictions
\[
1\le c_i\le X-1
\]
on positive rows and
\[
2\le c_i\le X-1
\]
on negative rows only remove endpoint degeneracies. They do not produce a new carrier.

## 5. What theorem would actually suffice

A strictly smaller non-tautological theorem than the desired triple bound is the following correlation statement.

**Carrier correlation theorem.** For every fixed sign vector and full defect pair \((d_2,d_3)\), define the admissible rows \((p_1,p_2,p_3)\) by the signed equations and require
\[
p_i\mid \gcd(m-z_i,N_{m-u_i}(u_i)).
\]
Then
\[
\#\{(p_1,p_2,p_3)\}\ll X^{o(1)}\lambda_X^3.
\]

This is strictly smaller than the original problem because it asks only for simultaneous large-prime divisors of the three carriers, not all folded-root triples.

Summing over defects would still require an aggregation theorem. The required form is
\[
\sum_{d_2,d_3}\#\text{Carrier}(d_2,d_3)
\ll X^2\lambda_X^3,
\]
not the weaker per-fibre statement multiplied by the trivial number of fibres.

## 6. Exact compatible countermodel

The current inputs allow the following abstract model.

Choose a family of roots \(Z_p\) satisfying:

* reflection symmetry;
* the signed product representation;
* nonzero pair defects;
* the Wronskian divisibility rule.

Assign, for each admissible signed row,
\[
N_{m-u_i}(u_i)\equiv0\pmod {p_i}
\]
by construction whenever
\[
m\equiv z_i\pmod {p_i}.
\]

Then every triple in a chosen defect fibre satisfies all currently used congruence conditions, while the number of triples in one fibre can be made as large as the product-set count allowed by the signed CRT system.

No contradiction arises because the model has not violated any proved condition: reflection, folded roots, Wronskian divisibility, and quotient nonzero conditions are all local statements. What is missing is exactly a cross-characteristic independence statement.

Hence these inputs cannot prove the cubic target.

## Final conclusion

The common-row condition
\[
p_i\mid\gcd(m-z_i,N_{m-u_i}(u_i))
\]
is a valid refinement, but not yet a usable integer carrier. The primitive Wronskian supplies divisibility without a height bound, and full-period reduction fails on regular nonzero-defect fibres.

The next genuine milestone is not another rewriting of the Wronskian. It is a quantified carrier-correlation theorem controlling simultaneous large prime divisors of the three continuant carriers, with exact normalization strong enough to sum over compatible defects. Without that theorem, the signed/product/reflection/Wronskian package remains compatible with higher-than-cubic triple populations.
