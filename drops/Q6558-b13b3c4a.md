# P3.2 Radon spectrum probe: random benchmark and structural correction

## (1) Random null model

Let

M_p = max_{t != 0, xi in F_p} |F(t,xi)|/p,

where

F(t,xi)=sum_h A_t(h)e_p(xi h)=sum_{r,h} e_p(tD(r,r+h)+xi h).

Under the null hypothesis that D(r,r+h) are independent uniform random variables, the summands are iid random phases.

For one fixed (t,xi),

F = sum_{j=1}^{p^2} Z_j,

with E(Z_j)=0 and |Z_j|=1. Hence

E|F|^2=p^2.

The complex central limit theorem gives

F/p -> Z,

where Z is standard complex Gaussian with density

pi^{-1} exp(-|z|^2).

Therefore

P(|F|/p > u) = exp(-u^2)+o(1).

There are

N=(p-1)p ~ p^2

independent-looking frequency points. Extreme value theory gives

P(M_p <= u) approximately exp(-N exp(-u^2)).

The centering is therefore determined by

u^2 = log N + y,

so

M_p^2 - log(p^2) converges to the Gumbel law:

P(M_p^2-2log p <= y) -> exp(-exp(-y)).

Equivalently,

M_p = sqrt(2 log p) + O(1/sqrt(log p)).

The constant sqrt(2) is the complex-Gaussian constant. A real Gaussian model would differ by the tail exp(-u^2/2) and hence produce sqrt(4 log p); the present problem is complex phase cancellation, so the sqrt(2 log p) scale is the correct benchmark.

Status: RANDOM-BASELINE.

## (2) Structural correction: small h slices

The kernel

D(r,s)=b_r c_s-b_s c_r

is not a generic random surface. For fixed h,

D(r,r+h)

is a deterministic orbit quantity.

The special case h=1 satisfies the exact Casoratian identity

D(r,r+1)=-1/r^3.

Hence

A_t(1)=sum_r e_p(-t/r^3),

which is a cubic reciprocal Kloosterman-type sum. After the change variable x=r^{-1}, it becomes

sum_x e_p(-t x^3),

up to the missing zero point. Weil gives

|A_t(1)| <= 2 sqrt(p).

More generally, the gap polynomial formalism gives:

D(r,r+h)=N_h(r)*nu_h(r)^{-1} mod p,

where N_h is the continuant/gap numerator and nu_h is the normalization denominator coming from the projective lift.

For fixed small h, after clearing denominators one obtains a rational function in r of degree O(h). Therefore the corresponding exponential sum is a rational-function Weil sum:

|A_t(h)| <= C h sqrt(p),

provided the rational function is not of Artin-Schreier degeneracy type.

The important qualification is that this is a fixed-h statement. The degree grows with h. When h approaches p^alpha, the Weil constant is no longer harmless and eventually gives no saving.

Thus:

small h: algebraic, square-root cancellation;
large h: no available deterministic estimate, random model is plausible.

The Radon transform therefore separates into:

F = (small h structured part) + (large h statistical part).

Status: STRUCTURAL-SPLIT.

## (3) Expected shape of M_p and experimental interpretation

If all small h slices satisfy Weil bounds, their contribution is

sum_{h<=H} A_t(h)e_p(xi h)

of size at most

H^2 sqrt(p)

trivially. After normalization by p this is negligible for fixed H.

Therefore the maximum spectrum should be controlled by the large-h regime. If those slices behave randomly, the null prediction survives:

M_p ~ sqrt(2 log p).

The diagnostic table is:

```
Observed M_p / sqrt(2 log p)       Interpretation
-------------------------------------------------------------
approaches 1                       random large-h regime; no bounded conductor
stays bounded below sqrt(log p)    extra cancellation / bounded conductor possible
logarithmic growth but smaller     partial structure, mixed regime
polynomial growth p^delta          hidden low-complexity obstruction
flat O(1)                          strong algebraic compression / finite monodromy
oscillatory with spikes            exceptional frequencies or resonant fibers
```

The decisive experiment is not the mean of M_p but the exponent of growth. A bounded-conductor Radon sheaf picture predicts a power-saving tail and therefore M_p=O(1) or at worst slowly varying. The random benchmark predicts the unavoidable extreme-value growth sqrt(2 log p).

Hence:

- M_p following sqrt(2 log p) is evidence against a hidden low-rank bounded-conductor object.
- M_p remaining bounded is strong evidence for an algebraic Radon compression.

## Final verdict

The random model is the correct null hypothesis for the high-gap region. The Casoratian structure definitely kills the low-gap region through explicit Weil cancellation, but it does not by itself control the full two-dimensional spectrum.

The surviving question is therefore a large-h structural problem: whether the deterministic projective orbit still behaves pseudorandom after removing the finitely many algebraic slices.
