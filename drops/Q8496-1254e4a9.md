## Candidate carrier class

Consider a carrier obtained from finitely many polynomial identities in the Apéry diagonal representation

\[
b_r=\sum_j {r\choose j}^2{r+j\choose j}^2
\]

and recurrence relations, by eliminating auxiliary variables with an integer resultant or Bezout combination. Suppose the carrier C satisfies:

1. every clean eight-cube target gives eight distinct primes \(p_i\) dividing \(C\);
2. \(C\neq0\);
3. the coefficients are obtained from bounded-degree elimination of the eight congruence conditions.

The required IQC8 inequality would need

\[
\log |C|=o(\sum_{i=1}^8 \log p_i)
\]

uniformly over all retained cubes.

## Scoped obstruction

Each prime condition is a congruence modulo a different prime:

\[
b_{r_i}\equiv0\pmod {p_i}.
\]

An integer resultant eliminating the common auxiliary parameters multiplies the leading contents contributed by every eliminated equation. The primitive resultant bound gives

\[
\log |C|\geq \sum_i \log p_i-O(\text{normalization terms})
\]

whenever the eight conditions are independent in the elimination variables.

The reason is that reduction modulo each \(p_i\) forces the resultant to vanish, so the product

\[
\prod_i p_i
\]

is contained in the content of the resultant before primitive normalization. Removing common content cannot remove this product unless there is a nontrivial shared factor in the eight congruence equations. Such a factor would be precisely an additional Apéry-specific relation not supplied by the recurrence or diagonal formula.

Therefore a generic resultant carrier reacquires the eight-prime content and cannot have the required sublinear logarithmic height.

## Consequence for the proposed mechanisms

The following implication is valid:

```text
A nonzero carrier C with
  (all clean eight cubes => every p_i | C)
and
  log |C| = o(sum log p_i)
would imply the IQC8 aggregate saving.
```

However, the existence of such a C is not established by:

- an unrestricted resultant.

The obstruction above shows why unrestricted resultants fail: they encode the divisibility by paying essentially the same eight-prime logarithmic mass they are meant to remove.

## Smallest missing lemma

A strictly smaller useful target is:

**Shared-factor lemma (needed for any successful resultant route).**

For a chosen elimination model, prove either:

1. the eight prime congruences share a nontrivial Apéry-specific algebraic factor before resultant formation, or
2. the primitive resultant has a coefficient height bound strictly below the product-of-primes content.

Without one of these two alternatives, the resultant carrier has no IQC8-strength height estimate.

This lemma is not proved here; it identifies the exact missing algebraic point.
