# Full-defect fibre algebra audit

## 1. Identities forced by two solutions

For a solution write

\[
N-d_i=c_i p_i,\qquad N'-d_i=c_i' p_i'.
\]

For \(i=2,3\), subtracting the two defect equations gives the exact identities

\[
N-N'=c_i p_i-c_i'p_i'.
\]

Hence

\[
 c_2p_2-c_2'p_2'=c_3p_3-c_3'p_3'.
\]

Equivalently the two affine factorizations force

\[
 c_2p_2-c_3p_3=c_2'p_2'-c_3'p_3'.
\]

The common value is \(N-d_2-d_3\). The same holds for every fixed sign/defect chart.

For the Apéry conditions one has

\[
p_i\mid B_{u_i(t)},\qquad p_i'\mid B_{u_i(t')}.
\]

The indices are affine:

\[
u_i(t)=s_i(t+d_i+\tau_i),\quad u_i(t')=s_i(t'+d_i+\tau_i).
\]

Therefore the only direct same-prime comparison available would be between affine indices belonging to the same prime. For distinct primes from the two fibres there is no common modulus.

## 2. Attempted determinant elimination

The natural idea is to eliminate \(N,N'\) using

\[
c_2p_2-c_2'p_2'=c_3p_3-c_3'p_3'.
\]

Taking a pair of affine indices gives quantities of the form

\[
H_vB_u-H_uB_v,
\]

where the primitive Casoratian identity gives

\[
H_vB_u-H_uB_v=(u!)^3N_{v-u}(u).
\]

If both \(B_u\) and \(B_v\) vanish modulo the same prime \(p\), then this expression is divisible by \(p\).

The obstruction is that the two solutions only give

\[
p_i\mid B_{u_i(t)},\qquad p_i'\mid B_{u_i(t')},
\]

with generally

\[
p_i\neq p_i'.
\]

Thus a Casoratian between the two affine indices gives no factor of \(p_i\) or \(p_i'\), unless an additional recurrence transport identity connects the two different characteristics. The factorization equation alone does not provide such a connection.

## 3. Height calculation

The algebraic elimination quantity coming only from

\[
c_2p_2-c_2'p_2'=c_3p_3-c_3'p_3'
\]

has size

\[
O(X^2),
\]

because

\[
c_i=O(X),\qquad p_i=O(X).
\]

The difference of two such quantities is therefore a small integer, but it is only divisible by primes that actually divide the same Apéry recurrence expression. The CRT identity itself contributes no shell-prime divisibility.

Hence there is no nonzero integer of height \(X^{O(1)}\) forced to be divisible by three shell primes.

---

## 4. Why the determinant route fails

The missing input is precisely:

\[
\boxed{\text{a mixed-characteristic Apéry transport identity}}
\]

relating

\[
B_{u_i(t)}\pmod {p_i}
\]

and

\[
B_{u_i(t')}\pmod {p_i'}.
\]

The existing primitive Casoratian is a same-modulus statement. It says:

\[
p\mid B_u,\ p\mid B_v
\Longrightarrow
p\mid (u!)^3N_{v-u}(u).
\]

It does not say:

\[
p\mid B_u,\ q\mid B_v
\Longrightarrow
pq\mid F(u,v).
\]

for \(p\neq q\).

Therefore the desired three-prime determinant certificate cannot be derived from the stated inputs.

## 5. Algebraic family showing the gap

Consider abstract data consisting of arbitrary primes

\[
p_i,p_i'\in(X,2X]
\]

and choose coefficients satisfying

\[
c_2p_2-c_2'p_2'=c_3p_3-c_3'p_3'.
\]

For example choose any four products with equal difference and define

\[
N=c_2p_2+d_2=c_3p_3+d_3,
\]

\[
N'=c_2'p_2'+d_2=c_3'p_3'+d_3.
\]

The factorization equations are then satisfied identically.

Now impose only the same-index consequences

\[
B_{u_i(t)}\equiv0\pmod {p_i},
\]

\[
B_{u_i(t')}\equiv0\pmod {p_i'}.
\]

These impose independent congruence conditions in different characteristics. The factorization equations remain unchanged. Thus one obtains a formal family satisfying every stated algebraic relation except the extra recurrence input needed to couple different characteristics.

Consequently any claimed determinant producing a product

\[
p_1p_2p_3
\]

from the two fibres would have to use precisely that missing mixed-characteristic recurrence statement.

---

## 6. Conclusion

The exact elimination gives only

\[
 c_2p_2-c_2'p_2'=c_3p_3-c_3'p_3'.
\]

Its height is polynomial in \(X\), but it carries no forced shell-prime divisibility beyond primes appearing in a common Apéry modulus. The primitive Casoratian cannot convert two unrelated zero congruences into a three-prime divisor.

Therefore the proposed determinant route does not yield an \(X^{o(1)}\) fibre bound from the frozen inputs. The missing theorem would have to be an explicit mixed-characteristic Apéry transport/rigidity statement.
