# Verdict

The proposed second-moment implication is correct **conditionally**: if the aggregate two-prime covariance is \(O(N)\)—in particular, if

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\\ \sqrt N<p,q\le N}}R_{pq}=o(N),
\]

then an \(L^2\)-to-\(L^\infty\) argument gives

\[
B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)=O(\sqrt N)=o(N/\log N).
\]

However, the hypothesis

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\le C\pi(x)
\tag{\(\bar Z\)}
\]

**does not imply** this covariance estimate, even when one also imposes:

- \(z_p\le2\);
- reflection symmetry \(r\in Z_p\Longleftrightarrow p-1-r\in Z_p\);
- no consecutive elements in \(Z_p\);
- the injectivity of \(p\mapsto N\bmod p\) for \(p>\sqrt N\);
- uniformly bounded one-prime Fourier transforms.

There is an explicit reflected anchored-star construction for which \((\bar Z)\) holds, but along infinitely many \(N\),

\[
B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)\asymp \frac{N}{\log N},
\qquad
\sum_{p\ne q}R_{pq}\asymp \frac{N^2}{(\log N)^2}.
\]

Thus no large-sieve, Barban–Davenport–Halberstam, character-sum, or Selberg-sieve argument based only on the primewise masses \(z_p\) and CRT can close the all-\(n\) gap. For the genuine Apéry sets, the desired estimate may still be true, but it is an **additional cross-prime arithmetic theorem**—essentially the moving-anchor/two-characteristic dispersion input already isolated in `problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex` and the anchored-star audit in `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex`.

The good news is that the full Poisson-scale covariance estimate is much stronger than necessary. A more economical sufficient target is the uncentered pair-incidence estimate

\[
F_{2}(N):=
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\\\sqrt N<p,q\le N}}
\#\{m\le N:m\bmod p\in Z_p,\ m\bmod q\in Z_q\}
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^2}\right).
\tag{W2}
\]

Indeed, \((W2)\) already implies \(B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)=o(N/\log N)\). Any power saving \(F_2(N)\ll N^{2-\delta}\) gives the stronger bound

\[
B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)\ll N^{1-\delta/2}.
\]

This weak star-exclusion estimate is the cleanest second-moment target for the stated goal.

# 1. Freeze the scale and write the exact second-moment identity

For a fixed \(N\), let

\[
\mathcal P_N=\{p\text{ prime}:\sqrt N<p\le N\},
\]

\[
\Omega_p=\{1\le m\le N:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\qquad
B_N(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_N}\mathbf 1_{\Omega_p}(m).
\]

The auxiliary function \(B_N(m)\) freezes the prime range at the terminal scale \(N\). It need not equal \(B_{\mathrm{low}}(m)\) for every \(m<N\), but at the one point that matters,

\[
B_N(N)=B_{\mathrm{low}}(N).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Put

\[
a_p=|\Omega_p|,
\qquad
\alpha_p=\frac{z_p}{p},
\qquad
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|.
\]

Each residue class modulo \(p\) occurs \(N/p+O(1)\) times in \([1,N]\), so

\[
a_p=N\alpha_p+\delta_p,
\qquad
|\delta_p|\le z_p.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Let

\[
\mu_N=\frac1N\sum_{m\le N}B_N(m)=\frac1N\sum_pa_p.
\]

Then the exact centered identity is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\le N}(B_N(m)-\mu_N)^2
=
\sum_p a_p\left(1-\frac{a_p}{N}\right)
+
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(J_{p,q}-\frac{a_pa_q}{N}\right).
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

All sums over \(p\ne q\) in this answer are ordered; using unordered pairs changes only a factor of two.

## 1.1 What \((\bar Z)\) gives for the mean

Let

\[
A(x)=\sum_{p\le x}z_p.
\]

Under \((\bar Z)\), Chebyshev’s bound for primes gives \(A(x)\ll_C x/\log x\). Partial summation yields

\[
\lambda_N:=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_N}\frac{z_p}{p}
=
\frac{A(N)}N-rac{A(\sqrt N)}{\sqrt N}
+
\int_{\sqrt N}^{N}\frac{A(t)}{t^2}\,dt
=O_C(1).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Also

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_N}z_p=O_C(N/\log N).
\tag{1.5}
\]

Consequently, by (1.2),

\[
\mu_N
=\lambda_N+O_C(1/\log N)
=O_C(1).
\tag{1.6}
\]

A constant correction is worth recording. If the empirical law strengthens to

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\sim\pi(x),
\]

then the written definition of \(B_{\mathrm{low}}\) predicts

\[
\lambda_N\longrightarrow
\int_{\sqrt N}^{N}\frac{dt}{t\log t}
=\log 2,
\]

not \(1/2\). The value \(1/2\) is the mean of the reflected pair count \(z_p/2\); since each pair contributes two residue positions, the expected mean of \(B_{\mathrm{low}}\) is approximately \(\log 2\). This numerical constant is irrelevant to the pointwise argument.

## 1.2 The user’s \(R_{pq}\) is equivalent to exact covariance at the \(o(N)\) scale

The question defines

\[
R_{pq}=J_{p,q}-N\alpha_p\alpha_q.
\]

Using \(a_p=N\alpha_p+\delta_p\),

\[
\frac{a_pa_q}{N}-N\alpha_p\alpha_q
=\alpha_p\delta_q+\alpha_q\delta_p+\frac{\delta_p\delta_q}{N}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\left|
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(
\frac{a_pa_q}{N}-N\alpha_p\alpha_q
\right)
\right|
\le
2\left(\sum_p\alpha_p\right)
 \left(\sum_p|\delta_p|\right)
+
\frac1N\left(\sum_p|\delta_p|\right)^2.
\]

By (1.4), (1.5), and \(|\delta_p|\le z_p\), this is

\[
O_C(N/\log N)=o(N).
\tag{1.7}
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}R_{pq}=o(N)
\]

is equivalent, up to \(o(N)\), to

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(J_{p,q}-\frac{a_pa_q}{N}\right)=o(N).
\tag{1.8}
\]

## 1.3 Conditional conclusion

The diagonal term in (1.3) is at most

\[
\sum_pa_p=N\mu_N=O_C(N).
\]

Thus (1.8) implies

\[
\sum_{m\le N}(B_N(m)-\mu_N)^2=O_C(N).
\]

Since the summand at \(m=N\) is one nonnegative term of this sum,

\[
(B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)-\mu_N)^2
=(B_N(N)-\mu_N)^2
\le O_C(N),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)=O_C(\sqrt N).}
\tag{1.9}
\]

This is more directly an \(L^2\)-to-\(L^\infty\) inequality than an application of Chebyshev, though Chebyshev with a threshold making the exceptional-set bound less than one is equivalent.

To identify the variance asymptotically with \(\mu_N\), one also wants

\[
\sum_p(a_p/N)^2=o(1).
\]

The average hypothesis alone does not force this, but the project’s unconditional bound \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\) does: for \(p>\sqrt N\), \(z_p/p\ll N^{-1/6}\), and hence the diagonal correction is \(o(1)\). This refinement is not needed for (1.9).

# 2. A rigorous reflected anchored-star counterexample

The following construction proves that \((\bar Z)\) cannot imply the desired covariance estimate by CRT, injectivity, or primewise information alone.

Choose a rapidly increasing sequence of even integers \(N_j\to\infty\), for example with

\[
N_{j+1}>4N_j.
\]

The prime intervals

\[
I_j=\left(\frac{3N_j}{4},\frac{7N_j}{8}\right)
\]

are then pairwise disjoint. For each prime \(p\in I_j\), define

\[
r_p=N_j-p,
\qquad
r_p^*=p-1-r_p=2p-N_j-1,
\]

and set

\[
Z_p=\{r_p,r_p^*\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For primes outside all the \(I_j\), put \(Z_p=\varnothing\).

For sufficiently large \(N_j\), the following all hold:

\[
\frac{N_j}{8}<r_p<\frac{N_j}{4}<p,
\]

\[
\frac{N_j}{2}-1<r_p^*<\frac{3N_j}{4}-1<p-1,
\]

and

\[
r_p^*-r_p=3p-2N_j-1>\frac{N_j}{4}-1>1.
\]

Thus every \(Z_p\):

- consists of two valid nonzero residues in \(\{1,\ldots,p-1\}\);
- is exactly reflection invariant;
- has no consecutive zeros;
- has \(z_p=2\).

In particular,

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\le2\pi(x)
\qquad\text{for every }x,
\tag{2.2}
\]

so \((\bar Z)\) holds with \(C=2\). If one wants any prescribed smaller positive average constant, select only a fixed positive fraction of the primes in each \(I_j\); the same phenomenon remains.

At the anchor \(n=N_j\), every \(p\in I_j\) satisfies \(p>N_j/2>\sqrt{N_j}\) and

\[
N_j\bmod p=N_j-p=r_p\in Z_p.
\]

Therefore

\[
B_{\mathrm{low}}(N_j)
\ge |I_j\cap\mathbb P|
\sim\frac{N_j}{8\log N_j}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Moreover, \(p\mapsto N_j-p\) is injective. Hence this example fully respects the key injectivity observation in the question: all the hit residues are different.

Now take distinct \(p,q\in I_j\). The integer \(m=N_j\) lies in both \(\Omega_p\) and \(\Omega_q\), so

\[
J_{p,q}\ge1.
\]

Since \(z_p=z_q=2\) and \(p,q>3N_j/4\),

\[
\frac{N_jz_pz_q}{pq}
=\frac{4N_j}{pq}
\le\frac{64}{9N_j}.
\]

Thus, for all sufficiently large \(j\),

\[
R_{pq}\ge\frac12.
\]

Writing \(L_j=|I_j\cap\mathbb P|\asymp N_j/\log N_j\), we obtain

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\\p,q\in I_j}}R_{pq}
\ge\frac12L_j(L_j-1)
\asymp\frac{N_j^2}{(\log N_j)^2}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is vastly larger than \(N_j\).

The construction is abstract—it is not claimed to be the genuine Apéry zero family. Its role is logically decisive: it proves that the implication

\[
(\bar Z)+\text{CRT}+\text{injectivity}+\text{reflection}
\Longrightarrow
\sum R_{pq}=o(N)
\]

is false. Any proof for the actual Apéry sets must use an additional arithmetic property that the anchored star violates.

This is precisely the obstruction already recorded in the project:

- `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex` proves the pairwise CRT second-moment envelope and shows the anchored star is sharp for all fixed moments;
- `problems/3.2/oracleB_result.tex` explains the fixed-anchor versus moving-anchor quantifier reversal and isolates the missing two-characteristic Mellin dispersion statement;
- `problems/3.2/DOCTRINE.md` records that \((\mathrm{HM})_2\) is landed but that pointwise atom-tail control remains the live input.

# 3. Why residue injectivity does not control the row load

It is useful to view the data as a \(0\)-\(1\) incidence matrix:

- columns are primes \(p\);
- rows are integers \(m\le N\);
- column \(p\) contains the periodic set \(\Omega_p\);
- the row sum is \(B_N(m)\).

The condition \(p\mapsto N\bmod p\) injective says that, in the row \(m=N\), different columns use different residue labels. It does **not** say that different columns cannot all contain the same row.

The reflected anchored star does exactly this:

\[
N_j\in\Omega_p
\quad\text{for every }p\in I_j,
\]

while the labels \(N_j\bmod p=N_j-p\) are all distinct. Thus injectivity is perfectly compatible with a row of degree \(\asymp N/\log N\).

The first-moment hypothesis controls total column mass. It does not control concentration of those columns on a moving row. Formally,

\[
\sum_{m\le N}B_N(m)=O(N),
\]

so Markov gives only

\[
\#\left\{m\le N:B_N(m)\ge T\right\}
\ll\frac{N}{T}.
\]

At \(T=\varepsilon N/\log N\), this permits \(O_\varepsilon(\log N)\) exceptional moving anchors. The anchored star needs only one. This explains exactly why an average theorem can prove density-one results but cannot by itself prove an all-\(n\) statement.

# 4. Audit of the proposed analytic approaches

## (a) Classical or dual large sieve

Define the centered periodic column

\[
f_p(m)=\mathbf1_{Z_p}(m\bmod p)-\frac{z_p}{p}.
\]

Let

\[
\widehat{Z}_p(h)=\sum_{r\in Z_p}e_p(-hr).
\]

Then

\[
f_p(m)=\frac1p\sum_{h\ne0\bmod p}
\widehat Z_p(h)e_p(hm).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For primes \(p\asymp P\), the additive large sieve gives schematically

\[
\sum_{m\le N}
\left|\sum_{p\asymp P}f_p(m)\right|^2
\ll
(N+P^2)
\sum_{p\asymp P}\frac1{p^2}
\sum_{h\ne0}|\widehat Z_p(h)|^2.
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\frac1{p^2}\sum_{h\ne0}|\widehat Z_p(h)|^2
=\frac{z_p}{p}-\frac{z_p^2}{p^2}
\le\frac{z_p}{p}.
\]

Under \((\bar Z)\),

\[
\sum_{p\asymp P}\frac{z_p}{p}\ll_C\frac1{\log P},
\]

so

\[
\sum_{m\le N}
\left|\sum_{p\asymp P}f_p(m)\right|^2
\ll_C\frac{N+P^2}{\log P}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

In the relevant range \(P>\sqrt N\), the \(P^2\) term dominates. At the top block \(P\asymp N\), (4.2) is of order \(N^2/\log N\), much larger than the \(o(N^2/\log^2N)\) needed even for the weak pointwise goal, and enormously larger than the Poisson-scale \(O(N)\) target.

Sparse-modulus variants may save logarithmic factors in some settings, but no uniform inequality can give a little-\(o\) improvement over the anchored-star scale for arbitrary \(Z_p\), because (2.4) is an explicit counterexample. A useful large-sieve theorem here would therefore have to use a special **mixed arithmetic structure of the Apéry zero positions**, not merely the fact that the denominators are prime or that each \(|\widehat Z_p(h)|\) is small.

Indeed, the anchored star has \(z_p=2\), hence

\[
|\widehat Z_p(h)|\le2
\quad\text{for every }p,h,
\]

but still has the maximal moving-anchor concentration. One-prime Fourier bounds cannot see the obstruction.

## (b) Barban–Davenport–Halberstam

Standard Barban–Davenport–Halberstam theory studies the distribution of a **fixed arithmetic sequence** among residue classes, averaging over moduli and usually over all reduced classes. Even modern versions for general sparse sequences require substantial progression and non-concentration hypotheses.

Here the underlying interval \([1,N]\) is already perfectly understood. The difficulty is that for each product modulus \(pq\), one selects the special CRT classes

\[
(r,s)\in Z_p\times Z_q,
\]

and these classes move with both characteristics. Moreover,

\[
pq>N,
\]

so the interval is shorter than one complete period modulo \(pq\); each selected class contributes either zero or one point. Averaging over all classes modulo \(pq\) would not control an arithmetically selected class that has been arranged to contain the moving anchor \(N\).

Thus an applicable BDH theorem would have to assume or prove exactly the missing fact: that the Apéry-selected CRT representatives land below \(N\) with their expected aggregate frequency. That is the desired dispersion estimate in different notation, not a consequence of \((\bar Z)\).

The recent general-sequence BDH work of Harper makes this distinction explicit: variance asymptotics require progression and non-concentration hypotheses far stronger than a bound on total mass. The reflected anchored star violates the needed non-concentration while satisfying every primewise mass bound in the question.

## (c) Direct character-sum expansion

The exact Fourier formula is useful diagnostically. With

\[
S_N(\theta)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}e(m\theta),
\]

one has

\[
J_{p,q}
=
\frac1{pq}
\sum_{a\bmod p}\sum_{b\bmod q}
\widehat Z_p(a)\widehat Z_q(b)
S_N\!\left(\frac ap+\frac bq\right).
\]

The term \(a=b=0\) is \(Nz_pz_q/(pq)\), so

\[
R_{p,q}
=
\frac1{pq}
\sum_{(a,b)\ne(0,0)}
\widehat Z_p(a)\widehat Z_q(b)
S_N\!\left(\frac ap+\frac bq\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Formula (4.3) makes the missing input exact. One needs cancellation after summing simultaneously over

\[
p,q,a,b,
\]

in a regime where \(pq>N\), and with the sets \(Z_p\) depending on the same characteristic \(p\). Trivial bounds for \(\widehat Z_p\), or even optimal bounds for each individual \(p\), do not control the mixed phases. The anchored star again has uniformly bounded \(\widehat Z_p\) but makes the double sum large and positive.

A successful trace-function route would need at least:

1. a uniformly bounded-complexity geometric realization of the functions \(a\mapsto\widehat Z_p(a)\), or of a suitable marked replacement;
2. compatibility across the two varying characteristics \(p\) and \(q\);
3. a bilinear/dispersion estimate in the ultra-short range \(N<pq\), uniform in the moving marks.

The current Apéry zero indicator is a characteristic-\(p\), coefficient/Mellin vanishing condition; it is not presently known to be a bounded-conductor \(\ell\)-adic trace function to which the standard Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel or Kowalski–Michel–Sawin machinery applies. This is why `oracleB_result.tex` formulates the missing object as a marked two-characteristic crystalline Mellin dispersion theorem.

## (d) Selberg sieve or Brun sieve

Sieve methods use the local densities \(z_p/p\) to estimate how many integers avoid or satisfy collections of congruence conditions on average. They do not, from local density alone, prevent one integer from lying in one selected class for every prime in a large block.

The reflected anchored star is an admissible local system for ordinary sieve bookkeeping. It has the correct small local densities and yet one row has degree \(\asymp N/\log N\). Applying higher-dimensional Selberg weights merely rewrites the need to control factorial moments such as

\[
\sum_{m\le N}(B_N(m))_k.
\]

The crude CRT bounds for these moments are sharp on the anchored star. Hence sieve can be part of a proof only after an independent Apéry-specific level-of-distribution or correlation theorem has excluded the star.

# 5. A simpler sufficient route: do not center

For the stated pointwise goal, estimating the signed covariance to \(o(N)\) is unnecessarily ambitious.

Define the ordered uncentered pair count

\[
F_2(N)
=
\sum_{m\le N}(B_N(m))_2
=
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\\p,q\in\mathcal P_N}}J_{p,q}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

At the terminal row,

\[
(B_{\mathrm{low}}(N))_2
=(B_N(N))_2
\le F_2(N).
\]

Therefore

\[
B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)\le1+\sqrt{F_2(N)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This gives several useful sufficient conditions.

## 5.1 Minimal little-\(o\) target

If

\[
F_2(N)=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^2}\right),
\]

then by (5.2),

\[
B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)=o(N/\log N).
\]

## 5.2 One logarithmic saving

For any fixed \(\eta>0\),

\[
F_2(N)\ll\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^{2+\eta}}
\]

implies

\[
B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)\ll
\frac{N}{(\log N)^{1+\eta/2}}.
\]

## 5.3 Any power saving

For any fixed \(\delta>0\),

\[
F_2(N)\ll N^{2-\delta}
\]

implies

\[
B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)\ll N^{1-\delta/2}.
\]

The first-moment hypothesis gives only the threshold bound

\[
F_2(N)
\le
\sum_{p\ne q}z_pz_q
\le
\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_N}z_p\right)^2
\ll_C\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

There is no little-\(o\) in (5.3), and the anchored star shows this is sharp. Thus the exact new input needed for the weak goal is simply **any strict saving over (5.3)** for the genuine Apéry zero sets.

This is a materially weaker target than

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}R_{pq}=o(N),
\]

which would imply the random-scale estimate \(F_2(N)=O(N)\). The project should therefore distinguish:

- **Poisson-scale dispersion:** \(F_2(N)=O(N)\), or centered covariance \(o(N)\);
- **weak star exclusion:** \(F_2(N)=o(N^2/\log^2N)\);
- **power-saving star exclusion:** \(F_2(N)\ll N^{2-\delta}\).

Only the second is necessary for Q337’s goal, and the third gives the requested \(N^{1-\varepsilon}\) bound.

# 6. What an actual Apéry proof would need

There are three plausible formulations of the missing arithmetic input.

## 6.1 Moving-anchor anti-concentration

Prove directly that for some fixed \(\delta>0\),

\[
\sup_{m\le N}
\#\{p\in\mathcal P_N:m\bmod p\in Z_p\}
\ll N^{1-\delta}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This immediately gives the desired result. It is exactly the type of atom-tail statement highlighted in `DOCTRINE.md`. A fixed-anchor theorem does not suffice because the maximizing \(m=m_N\) may move with \(N\).

## 6.2 Weak two-prime incidence saving

Prove, for some \(\delta>0\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\\p,q\in\mathcal P_N}}
J_{p,q}
\ll N^{2-\delta}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is the clean uncentered route. It asks only that the unique CRT representatives associated with Apéry zero pairs fail to concentrate in \([1,N]\) at the worst possible anchored-star rate.

A logarithmic version of (6.2) is already enough:

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}
\ll\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^{2+\eta}}.
\]

## 6.3 Full centered two-characteristic dispersion

Construct a marked arithmetic object encoding

\[
r\in Z_p\Longleftrightarrow b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\]

and prove

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}
\left(J_{p,q}-\frac{Nz_pz_q}{pq}\right)
=o(N).
\tag{6.3}
\]

This would establish the Poisson-scale second moment and the strong \(O(\sqrt N)\) pointwise bound. It is the most conceptually satisfying route, but also the strongest and deepest.

For a Fourier/trace proof of either (6.2) or (6.3), the indispensable ingredients are:

1. **Geometrization with uniform complexity.** A representation of the marked coefficient-zero/Fourier data by a compatible bounded-complexity object; primewise knowledge of \(z_p\) or of \(|\widehat Z_p|\) is not enough.
2. **Two-characteristic bilinear cancellation.** A theorem controlling the mixed \(p,q\) phases in (4.3), uniformly when \(pq>N\) and the interval is shorter than one period.
3. **Exceptional-locus control.** A proof that the geometric object has no rank-one, additive, multiplicative, or moving-anchor component capable of producing an anchored star. The project’s reflection symmetry should be factored out rather than mistaken for independence.

Without these, the listed analytic tools only reproduce the trivial CRT envelope.

# 7. Direct answers

**Can the proposed second moment close the gap?**  
Yes, if the aggregate covariance estimate is independently proved. The exact moment identity then gives \(B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)=O(\sqrt N)\).

**Can \(\sum R_{pq}=o(N)\) be proved from \(\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll\pi(x)\)?**  
No. The reflected anchored-star construction above satisfies the average hypothesis and the main known primewise structural constraints, but has

\[
\sum R_{pq}\asymp N^2/\log^2N.
\]

**Does injectivity of \(p\mapsto N\bmod p\) help?**  
It is correct but insufficient. The star uses distinct residues in every column and still places all columns through one row.

**Can classical large sieve close it?**  
No. Its \(P^2\) term is far too large in the range \(P>\sqrt N\), and a uniform improvement below the anchored-star scale is impossible without Apéry-specific mixed structure.

**Can BDH close it?**  
Not off the shelf. A BDH theorem applicable here would have to assume or prove non-concentration of the Apéry-selected moving CRT classes, which is essentially the desired result.

**Can direct character sums close it?**  
They give the correct exact formulation (4.3), but require a new cross-prime bilinear cancellation theorem. Individual Fourier bounds are insufficient.

**Can Selberg or Brun sieve close it?**  
Not from local densities alone. Sieve does not exclude a single moving high-load anchor.

**What is the simplest sufficient replacement?**  
Prove any strict saving in the uncentered pair count:

\[
F_2(N)=o(N^2/\log^2N).
\]

A power saving \(F_2(N)\ll N^{2-\delta}\) yields the requested weak pointwise bound \(B_{\mathrm{low}}(N)\ll N^{1-\delta/2}\).

# Bottom line

The second moment is a valid route, but it does **not** turn the prime-average hypothesis \((\bar Z)\) into an all-\(n\) theorem for free. The off-diagonal term is not a technical remainder; it is the entire moving-anchor obstruction. The most economical next target is not the full Poisson asymptotic

\[
\sum R_{pq}=o(N),
\]

but the much weaker star-exclusion estimate

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}
=o(N^2/\log^2N).
\]

That statement is still genuinely arithmetic and cross-prime, but it has enormous slack relative to the random prediction \(F_2(N)=O(N)\). It is therefore the right rigorous reformulation of Q337.

- Emmanuel Kowalski, [*The principle of the large sieve*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610021). This emphasizes that a large-sieve application requires a nontrivial bound for the relevant large-sieve constant; local densities alone do not supply it.
- Adam J. Harper, [*Simple Barban–Davenport–Halberstam type asymptotics for general sequences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19644). The hypotheses explicitly include progression and non-concentration information beyond total mass.
- Étienne Fouvry, Emmanuel Kowalski, and Philippe Michel, [*Algebraic trace functions over the primes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6043). This is representative of the fixed bounded-conductor trace-function technology that would become relevant only after a suitable geometrization of the Apéry marked zeros.
- Emmanuel Kowalski, Philippe Michel, and Will Sawin, [*Bilinear forms with Kloosterman sums and applications*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2017/186-2/p02), *Annals of Mathematics* 186 (2017), 413–500.
- Étienne Fouvry, Emmanuel Kowalski, Philippe Michel, and Will Sawin, [*Bilinear forms with trace functions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459). This is close in analytic spirit, but it still presupposes trace functions with controlled geometric complexity and does not by itself address the same-characteristic moving-zero problem here.
