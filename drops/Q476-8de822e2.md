# Palindromic symmetry for Domb numbers and related Apéry-like sequences

## Executive summary

The computational observation for Domb numbers is very plausible, but the mechanism is not simply “rank 4 implies symmetry”.  For the Apéry ζ(3) sequence the identity

```text
b_{p-1-r} ≡ b_r (mod p)
```

comes from an additional self-duality/involution of the underlying toric or hypergeometric object.  Zariski-dense `Sp_4` monodromy is compatible with this phenomenon, but does not force it by itself.

For Domb numbers there is strong evidence for the same symmetry, and there are toric explanations available, but a complete proof requires exhibiting the relevant involution explicitly.

---

## (A) Domb congruence

The conjectured identity is

```text
D_{p-1-r} ≡ D_r (mod p).
```

for all primes p ≥ 5.

This is consistent with the known structure of Domb numbers:

```text
D_n = Σ_k binom(n,k)^2 binom(2k,k) binom(2n-2k,n-k).
```

A direct proof should follow the same pattern as the Apéry proof:

1. Rewrite D_n as a finite-field hypergeometric value.
2. Apply the Euler/Pfaff transformation for the corresponding finite hypergeometric function.
3. Use the parameter change n -> p-1-r and the congruence

```text
binom(p-1-r,k) ≡ (-1)^k binom(r+k,k) (mod p).
```

The remaining difficulty is that the Domb summand has two coupled binomial factors, so the transformation is not a one-line Lucas argument as it is for Apéry numbers.

Thus:

```text
D_{p-1-r} ≡ D_r (mod p) is very likely true,
but a published elementary proof via termwise Lucas symmetry is not immediate.
```

---

## (B) Constant-term representation

The displayed formula

```text
CT[(1+x)^n(1+y)^n(1+1/x+1/y)^n(1+x+y)^n]
```

is not the standard Domb constant-term representation.

A standard toric form is obtained from the Laurent polynomial

```text
Λ_D(x,y)= (x+x^{-1})(y+y^{-1})(x+y+x^{-1}+y^{-1})
```

(up to monomial changes of variables and normalization), with Domb numbers arising as a diagonal/constant term of powers of a Laurent polynomial.

Equivalent formulations use the diagonal of a rational function associated with the Domb Calabi-Yau threefold.  The important point for symmetry is not the exact presentation but whether the Laurent polynomial has an involutive symmetry.

## (C) Toric involution

For Apéry ζ(3), the key identity is an involution τ satisfying schematically

```text
Λ(τ(x)) = Λ(x)^{-1}
```

so that

```text
CT[Λ^{p-1-r}] ≡ CT[Λ^r] (mod p).
```

For Domb numbers, an analogous involution is expected because the mirror Calabi-Yau has a large automorphism group.  However, the exact statement

```text
Λ_D ∘ τ = Λ_D^{-1}
```

depends on the chosen Laurent polynomial model.

Therefore:

```text
There should be a toric proof, but it is not a formal consequence of the Domb definition alone.
```

The correct route is to find the mirror involution and then reduce modulo p.

## (D) Why Franel numbers behave differently

The Franel numbers

```text
f_n = Σ_k binom(n,k)^3
```

have a rank-2 Picard-Fuchs equation with essentially elliptic (`SL_2`) monodromy.

Rank is not the real dividing line.  The distinction is:

```text
self-dual representation + geometric involution
```

versus

```text
arbitrary infinite monodromy.
```

A rank-2 elliptic family may have a functional equation, but it need not identify the Frobenius trace at r with the trace at p-1-r.

The Apéry ζ(3) and Domb cases have additional involutions coming from their K3/Calabi-Yau geometry.

Thus:

```text
rank 2 does not prevent palindromy,
rank 4 does not guarantee palindromy.
```

The missing ingredient is an involution on the motive.

## (E) General Sp_4 theorem?

No such theorem is currently known.

A plausible conjectural statement is:

Let A_n be an Apéry-like sequence with:

```text
1. a toric representation A_n = CT[Λ^n];
2. a motivic realization with Zariski-dense Sp_4 monodromy;
3. an involution τ with Λ∘τ = Λ^{-1}.
```

Then

```text
A_{p-1-r} ≡ A_r (mod p).
```

The third condition is essential.  Sp_4 density controls equidistribution and independence of Frobenius, not reflection identities.

---

## (F) ζ(2) Apéry numbers

For

```text
a_n = Σ_k binom(n,k)^2 binom(n+k,k),
```

there is no general anti-palindromic identity

```text
a_{p-1-r} ≡ -a_r (mod p).
```

The ζ(2) sequence is associated with a rank-2 elliptic Picard-Fuchs equation.  The relation between r and p-1-r depends on the elliptic curve involution and on the choice of period, and does not generally give a sign change.

Small-prime computations should be used here: if the identity existed universally it would already be visible for p=5,7,11.

The expected situation is:

```text
no universal palindrome,
no universal anti-palindrome.
```

# Structural conclusion

The correct hierarchy is:

```text
Finite monodromy:
    zero sets can have positive density (Theta(p)).

Infinite monodromy without involution:
    zeros should behave randomly; typically O(1) expected.

Infinite monodromy + geometric involution:
    palindromic symmetry is possible.
```

The Domb computation is therefore significant: it is evidence that the Domb motive carries the same hidden reflection symmetry as Apéry ζ(3), not merely evidence for its rank.

The next decisive step is to identify the explicit toric automorphism τ for the Domb Laurent polynomial and prove

```text
Λ_D(τ(x)) = Λ_D(x)^{-1}.
```

That would turn the 667-prime computation into a theorem.
