# Audit verdict: the cross-minor is a genuinely different object, but not yet a proved escape from the Smith obstruction

## 1. Correct p-adic setup

The previous linear-carrier Smith statements do not directly imply the target divisibility of the normalized determinant by cancelling the binomial normalizer modulo p. That cancellation is invalid when

\[
v_p\binom{d+L+1}{L}=1.
\]

The correct argument is valuation-theoretic. Write

\[
E_{d,L}=\frac{A_dB_{d+1}-A_{d+1}B_d}{C_{d,L}},\qquad C_{d,L}=\binom{d+L+1}{L}.
\]

At an interior target node p-1, each carrier satisfies
\[
A_d\equiv A_{d+1}\equiv Y_{p-1}\equiv0\pmod p,
\]
and similarly for B. Therefore the unnormalized determinant has valuation at least 2. Since C has valuation exactly 1, the normalized E retains one factor of p.

At a non-target prime the carrier rows are only rank-one modulo p, giving only the universal determinant factor. Thus the candidate congruence is

\[
E_{d,L}=u_p\,b_{n-p}\,\Delta^{L+1}(Z-MY)_d\pmod p,
\]

with u_p a unit.

## 2. Relation with Sections 55--59

Sections 55--59 study linear maps

\[
Y\mapsto (G_{d,L}(Y))_{d,L}
\]

and their translated-stack Smith invariants. The first layer of the determinant

\[
A_dB_{d+1}-A_{d+1}B_d
\]

is exactly the 2x2 minor of the same carrier matrix, so the universal rank-one reduction is inherited.

However E is not itself a linear carrier. It is a bilinear Pluecker coordinate of the two-dimensional column span generated by Y and Z.

The Section 55--59 Smith form proves:

* universal p-content of linear rows;
* rank-one residue modulo candidate primes;
* determinant divisors of linear stencil matrices.

It does not prove that a bilinear Pluecker coordinate has no additional information.

## 3. Relation with Section 63 no-go

Section 63 blocks bounded-dimensional linear universal-state constructions. It applies to objects of the form

\[
F(n)=\lambda(n)\cdot v(n)
\]

where a single finite-dimensional state is evaluated by a linear functional.

E is instead

\[
E=\det(\mathcal O_Y,\mathcal O_Z)
\]

or an exterior-power observable. Therefore Section 63 does not directly subsume E.

But the escape is only formal. Exterior powers of a fixed-dimensional state are again fixed-dimensional. If the transition matrices for Y and Z have polynomial height control, the same bounded-state obstruction can reappear on the exterior representation.

The missing question is not dimension. It is height.

## 4. Minimal new Apéry lemma required

A sufficient lemma would be:

**Primitive exterior-content lemma.** After removing universal Pascal/binomial factors from the full-margin family

\[
\{E_{d+s,L+t}:0\le s\le r,0\le t\le r\},
\]

there exists a fixed-size determinant divisor

\[
D(n)=\gcd(E_{d+s,L+t})
\]

such that

\[
D(n)\mid R(n)
\]

for an explicit nonzero integer polynomial-height quantity satisfying

\[
\log |R(n)|=o(n).
\]

This is strictly stronger than the existing Smith statements.

## 5. What the data establishes and what it does not

The hostile experiments showing 3000-bit to 40-bit collapse are evidence that the full-margin exterior divisor may have boundary cancellation.

They do not prove that cancellation is forced for all n.

The required proof must exhibit one of:

1. a Bezout chain in the (d,L) lattice reducing all full-margin minors to endpoint minors;
2. a determinantal divisor identity over Z[n];
3. a resultant/Fitting ideal computation whose generator has polynomial height.

Without one of these, E remains a promising nonlinear observable but not an unconditional gcd proof.

## 6. Final classification

| Object | Status |
|---|---|
| Linear carrier Smith invariant | blocked by rank-one alias |
| Cross-minor E | genuinely nonlinear Pluecker observable |
| Section 63 direct no-go | does not formally apply |
| Exterior-state reformulation | may reproduce Section 63 if height grows exponentially |
| Full-margin collapse | strong computational evidence only |
| Needed theorem | primitive exterior-content / endpoint Bezout identity |

Conclusion: the divided cross-minor route is not merely a repackaged linear Smith invariant. The previous no-go does not kill it. But its only possible advantage is a new low-height determinantal-divisor theorem; fixed state dimension alone is insufficient because exterior transition products can carry exponential height.
