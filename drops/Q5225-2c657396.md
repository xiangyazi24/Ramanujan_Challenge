# 2. Approach I: exact \(E/O\) resultant and its saturation

Let
\[
 f_{a,H}(Y)=M_a(Y-H)
 =E_{a,H}(Y^2)+Y O_{a,H}(Y^2),
 \qquad n=3(a-1).
 \tag{2.1}
\]
Because the coefficient of \(Y^{n-1}\) in the shifted polynomial is
\(-nH\ell_a\), the two parity pieces have the full generic degrees in
characteristic zero whenever \(H\ne0\).  The general square identity used
in `thm:meso-diagonal-square` therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname {Res}_Y(f_{a,H}(Y),f_{a,H}(-Y))
 =2^n\ell_a M_a(-H)
   \operatorname {Res}_U(O_{a,H},E_{a,H})^2.
 \tag{2.2}
\]
Reflection gives
\[
 f_{a,H}(-Y)=(-1)^{a-1}M_a(Y+H).
\]
After translating and undoing the dyadic centering,
\[
 \left|
 \operatorname {Res}_Y(M_a(Y-H),M_a(Y+H))
 \right|
 =2^{n^2}\left|
 \operatorname {Res}_x(N_a(x),N_a(x+H))
 \right|.
 \tag{2.3}
\]
Hence, for every integer \(H>a\),
\[
 \boxed{
 2^n|\ell_aM_a(-H)|
 \left|\operatorname {Res}_U(O_{a,H},E_{a,H})\right|^2
 =2^{n^2}
 \left|\operatorname {Res}_x(N_a(x),N_a(x+H))\right|.}
 \tag{2.4}
\]
The root-strip theorem makes \(M_a(-H)\ne0\) and also makes the
same-shift resultant nonzero.  Thus
\[
 \boxed{\gcd_{\mathbb Q[U]}(E_{a,H},O_{a,H})=1.}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

This answers the first requested test.  There is a Bézout relation, but
its scalar is exactly the same-shift resultant factor in (2.4); there is
no new universal vanishing minor beyond the reflection-pair condition.

## 2.1 The subresultant parameter polynomial

Regard \(H\) as an indeterminate and put
\[
 \Theta_a(H)=\operatorname {Res}_U(O_{a,H},E_{a,H})\in\mathbb Z[H].
\]
The leading-term comparison in (2.2)--(2.3) gives
\[
 \deg_H\Theta_a=\frac{n(n-1)}2,
 \qquad
 |\operatorname {lc}_H\Theta_a|
 =2^{n(n-1)/2}\ell_a^{\,n-1}.
 \tag{2.6}
\]
Equivalently, after reduction modulo \(p\), one may form the same
resultant from the actual-degree nonzero polynomial \(\overline M_a\);
this removes any nuisance from a Lucas leading-coefficient drop.

At a value \(H=h\), if the two parity pieces have \(t\) distinct common
roots, the specialized Sylvester matrix has corank at least \(t\).  Over
the DVR \(\mathbb F_p[[H-h]]\), Smith form therefore makes \(h\) a root
of the parameter determinant with multiplicity at least \(t\).  The
resulting universal budget is
\[
 \sum_h t_{a,h}\ll a^2.
 \tag{2.7}
\]
There is also a more elementary split-root proof of the exact bound needed
here, which avoids multiplicity issues entirely and survives all leading
coefficient drops; it appears in Section 3.

The key exponent is therefore fixed: summing (2.7) over
\(a\ll T\) gives only
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{a,h}t_{a,h}\ll T^3.}
 \tag{2.8}
\]
The gate is
\[
 T^{69/31-\eta},
 \qquad
 3-\frac{69}{31}=\frac{24}{31}.
\]
So the universal \(E/O\) subresultant degree is far too large.

There is a useful saturation interpretation.  If one asks only for the
number of \(h\)'s at which the \(E/O\) gcd degree is at least
\(G\), the parameter determinant gives
\[
 \#\{h:t_{a,h}\ge G\}\ll \frac{a^2}{G}.
 \tag{2.9}
\]
At the gate-natural threshold \(G=T^{7/31}\), this becomes nontrivial
against the \(O(T)\) available values of \(h\) only when
\[
 a^2/T^{7/31}<T,
 \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
 a<T^{19/31}.
 \tag{2.10}
\]
Thus the exact subresultant root-counting mechanism saturates at the same
\(19/31\) exponent that has already been crossed by the current wing

# 3. Exact split-root unfolding and a fixed-\(a\) summed triple-gcd theorem

Write
\[
 \epsilon_b=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&b\text{ odd},\\
 1,&b\text{ even},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \widetilde M_b(Y)=M_b(Y)/Y^{\epsilon_b}=A_b(Y^2).
 \tag{3.1}
\]
The division is an identity in \(\mathbb Z[Y]\), by the centered parity
of \(M_b\).

For the Q5208 carrier put
\[
 g_p^\square(a,b)
 =\deg\gcd_{\mathbb F_p[U]}
 \bigl(
 \overline A_b,
 \overline E_{a,h},
 \overline O_{a,h},
 U^{(p-1)/2}-1
 \bigr),
 \qquad h=a+b.
 \tag{3.2}
\]
The last polynomial is squarefree and its roots are exactly the nonzero
squares in \(\mathbb F_p\).

Every such \(U\) has exactly two lifts \(Y=\pm\sqrt U\).  Conversely,
using
\[
 M_a(-Y-h)=(-1)^{a-1}M_a(Y+h),
\]
one obtains the exact equality of split-root counts
\[
 \boxed{
 2g_p^\square(a,b)
 =\deg\gcd_{\mathbb F_p[Y]}
 \bigl(
 \overline{\widetilde M_b}(Y),
 \overline M_a(Y-h),
 \overline M_a(Y+h),
 Y^{p-1}-1
 \bigr).}
 \tag{3.3}
\]
Under the affine change
\[
 Y=2x+a+h+1,
 \]
the three continuants are, up to nonzero powers of two,
\[
 N_b(x+a),\qquad N_a(x),\qquad N_a(x+h).
 \tag{3.4}
\]
Thus (3.3) is exactly a same-root split affine triple gcd.  It is not a
gcd of scalar resultants.

## 3.1 Fixed-\(a\) root-pair injection

Let \(r_p(a)\) be the number of distinct roots of
\(\overline M_a\) in \(\mathbb F_p\).  For a root
\(U\) counted by (3.2), choose \(Y\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) with
\(Y^2=U\).  Then
\[
 \alpha=Y-h,
 \qquad
 \beta=-Y-h
 \tag{3.5}
\]
are two distinct roots of \(\overline M_a\), and
\[
 h=-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2},
 \qquad
 U=\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^2}{4}.
 \tag{3.6}
\]
Consequently the pair \((h,U)\) injects into an unordered pair of
distinct roots of \(\overline M_a\).  The extra equation
\(A_b(U)=0\) only selects a subset of these pairs.  Hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{b\ge2\\a+b<p}}
 g_p^\square(a,b)
 \le\binom{r_p(a)}2
 \le\binom{3(a-1)}2.}
 \tag{3.7}
\]
This is an unconditional **summed aligned triple-gcd bound** retaining the
same split root.

It gives, for \(A\le T\),
\[
 \sum_{\substack{a\le A\\T\le a+b<2T}}
 F_p^{\rm act}(a,b)
 \le2\sum_{a\le A,b}g_p^\square(a,b)
 \ll A^3.
 \tag{3.8}
\]
Thus the purely algebraic reflection-pair route alone deletes
\[
 a\le T^{23/31-\tau}
 \]
at cost \(T^{69/31-3\tau}\).  Section 6 improves this actual-chain
slice further, to \(121/155\), by using the Q5208 actual-return energy.

# 4. Approach II: the three-polynomial \(U\)-Fitting content

The most direct same-root content attached to (3.2) is
\[
 \mathcal P_{a,b}(S)
 =\operatorname {Res}_U
 \bigl(A_b(U),E_{a,h}(U)+S O_{a,h}(U)\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathcal C^U_{a,b}=\operatorname {cont}_S\mathcal P_{a,b}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]
Because (2.5) says \(E_{a,h}\) and \(O_{a,h}\) are coprime over
\(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathcal P_{a,b}(S)\) is not the zero polynomial:
if an irreducible factor of \(A_b\) divided the generic linear
combination \(E+SO\) over \(\mathbb Q(S)\), it would divide both
\(E\) and \(O\).

The Gauss-valuation/Sylvester argument of `lem:qc-aligned` applies
verbatim in the \(U\)-coordinate.  Thus, whenever the reduction of
\(A_b\) is nonzero,
\[
 \#\{U\in\mathbb F_p:A_b(U)=E_{a,h}(U)=O_{a,h}(U)=0\}
 \le v_p(\mathcal C^U_{a,b}).
 \tag{4.2}
\]
The square-root count is a subset of the left side.  This retains the same
root and is therefore immune to the component-misalignment example in
`gcdtail_result.tex`.

The obstruction is the summed height.  The degrees of the three
polynomials are \(O(a+b)\), and the cleared continuant coefficients after
the shift by \(h\) have logarithmic height
\(O((a+b)\log(a+b))\).  The direct Sylvester/Hadamard envelope therefore
gives, uniformly for \(a+b\ll T\),
\[
 \log H(\mathcal P_{a,b})\ll T^2\log T.
 \tag{4.3}
\]
Summed over the \(O(T^2)\) parameter pairs, this yields only
\[
 \sum_{a,b}\log\mathcal C^U_{a,b}
 \le \sum_{a,b}\log H(\mathcal P_{a,b})
 \ll T^4\log T.
 \tag{4.4}
\]
This is only an upper envelope; it is **not** a lower bound on the actual
content, and it does not refute a future content-compression theorem.
It does show exactly where the direct height proof stops.

At fixed \(p\), (4.2) would close the gate if one proved
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{(a,b)\text{ in the saturated core}}
 \log \mathcal C^U_{a,b}
 \ll T^{69/31-\eta+o(1)}\log p.}
 \tag{4.5}
\]
No statement of this strength is currently proved.  In particular, the
existing `AC^{tr}` discussion is not a license to replace (4.5) by a
hypothetical absolute content sum.

There is also no hidden extra determinant from rewriting the word.  A
literal chain gives
\[
 N_h(u)=N_h(u+a)=0,
 \]
so the **same** centered \(Y\) also gives
\[
 M_h(Y-a)=M_h(Y+a)=0.
 \tag{4.6}
\]
But `prop:bezout` identifies the simultaneous systems
\[
 (N_a(u),N_h(u))
 \quad\leftrightarrow\quad
 (N_a(u),N_b(u+a))
\]
after the physical cut-edge saturation, and similarly at the shifted
start.  Thus the four parity equations obtained from the repeated
\(a\)- and repeated \(h\)-blocks are a symmetric presentation of the
same order-two recurrence constraint, not a fourth independent
codimension.

# 5. Approach III: why the square selector gives no automatic character saving

Let
\[
 \mathscr R_{a,b}
 =\{U\in\mathbb F_p^\times:
 A_b(U)=E_{a,h}(U)=O_{a,h}(U)=0\}
\]
and let \(\chi\) be the quadratic character.  Since \(U=0\) is excluded,
\[
 g_p^\square(a,b)
 =\frac12\left(
 |\mathscr R_{a,b}|+
 \sum_{U\in\mathscr R_{a,b}}\chi(U)
 \right).
 \tag{5.1}
\]
There is no universal cancellation in the second term: a zero-dimensional
common-root set can consist entirely of squares.  The current source has
no theorem forcing mixed quadratic characters on these aligned roots.
Thus \(|\sum\chi(U)|\le|\mathscr R_{a,b}|\) is sharp at the level of the
available interfaces.

Equation (3.3) is the structural reason.  The square selector is just the
quotient of an ordinary split \(Y\)-root set by the involution
\(Y\mapsto-Y\).  Without an additional Frobenius-distribution theorem it
can save at most the constant factor two.

The polynomial \(U^{(p-1)/2}-1\) should therefore be used only as a
squarefree root selector.  Feeding its degree \(\asymp p\) into a
Sylvester height computation would introduce a completely spurious
\(p\)-dependent determinant and destroy the \(T\)-scale budget.

There is, however, a useful **geometric** consequence of having the actual
square root \(Y\), proved in Section 7.

# 7. New split-square subregion: short reflection displacement costs \(D^3\)

For a literal chain set
\[
 z_0=u,\quad z_1=u+a,\quad z_2=u+h,\quad z_3=u+a+h
\]
and let \(\iota(x)=p-1-x\).  Put
\[
 Y_0=2u+a+h+1,
 \qquad
 Y=Y_0\pmod p,
 \qquad
 \|Y\|_p=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|Y_0-kp|.
 \tag{7.1}
\]
The present reflection-overlap deletion excludes \(Y=0\).

The four actual zeros produce two **literal zero pairs with the same
signed displacement**:
\[
 (r,s)=(\iota(z_1),z_2),
 \qquad
 (r',s')=(\iota(z_0),z_3)=(r+a,s+a).
 \tag{7.2}
\]
Their signed difference is
\[
 q=s-r=Y_0-p,
 \tag{7.3}
\]
so its circular size is \(\|Y\|_p\).

For a nonzero signed difference \(q\), put
\[
 C_p(q)=\#\{(r,s)\in\mathcal Z_p^2:s-r=q\}.
\]
If \(d=\min(|q|,p-|q|)\le p/2\), the literal gap bound gives
\[
 C_p(q)\ll d.
 \tag{7.4}
\]
Indeed for \(|q|\le p/2\) this is \(A_p(|q|)\le3(|q|-1)\), while
for \(|q|>p/2\) there are at most \(p-|q|=d\) possible starts.

The map from a chain to the ordered pair of ordered zero pairs in (7.2)
is injective: their translation determines \(a\), and then the original
four indices are recovered.  For a fixed circular distance \(d\), there
are only \(O(1)\) possible signed \(q\)'s, each contributing at most
\(C_p(q)^2\ll d^2\) chains.  Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 Q_{\|Y\|\le D}(T)\ll\sum_{d\le D}d^2\ll D^3.}
 \tag{7.5}
\]
This uses only literal zero pairs; no algebraic phantom receives a weight.

Taking
\[
 D=T^{23/31-\tau}
\]
gives
\[
 \boxed{
 Q_{\|Y\|\le D}(T)
 \ll T^{69/31-3\tau}.}
 \tag{7.6}
\]
Thus another gate-safe condition on the surviving physical core is
\[
 \boxed{\|2u+a+h+1\|_p>T^{23/31-\tau}.}
 \tag{7.7}
\]
For the ideal \(11/5\) pre-tent target, the corresponding threshold is
\(D=T^{11/15-o(1)}\).

# 9. Exceptional parities, centers, and leading-coefficient drops

These issues do not change any exponent above, but they are load-bearing
for a correct Fitting interpretation.

## 9.1 \(Y=0\) and \(U=0\)

When \(b\) is even, \(M_b(Y)=Y A_b(Y^2)\).  The physical
\(Y=0\) branch is exactly the central/reflection-overlap branch already
removed in the current source.  Algebraically
\(U^{(p-1)/2}-1\) excludes \(U=0\) automatically.

## 9.2 Outer center roots

When \(a\) is even, \(M_a(0)=0\).  In the reflection-pair equations a
solution using this forced center has
\[
 Y=\pm h,
 \qquad U=h^2.
\]
Thus it is at most one \(U\)-root, hence at most two \(Y\)-lifts, for
each parameter pair.  The total physical contribution of this entire
center locus is therefore
\[
 O(T^2),
\]
which is gate-subcritical by the margin \(7/31\).

The symmetric skipped-\(h\) presentation (4.6) shows the analogous
center locus
\[
 Y=\pm a,
 \qquad U=a^2
\]
when \(h\) is even.  It has the same \(O(T^2)\) total bound.  Thus these
forced parity centers may be removed before any attempt to estimate a
primitive triple-gcd tail.

## 9.3 Leading coefficient drops

The Lucas leading coefficient \(\ell_d\) can vanish modulo the
characteristic.  This does not create an affine root at infinity in the
definition (3.2).  The source's centered coefficient formulas show that,
for \(d<p\), when \(\ell_d=0\) the next allowed centered coefficient is
nonzero (Cassini plus the formula for \(u_d\)); the degree drops by exactly
two.  Thus the \(U\)-degree drops by at most one relative to the nominal
parity degree.

All direct root-count inequalities above use the **actual reduced degree**
and remain valid.  By contrast, a generic-size Sylvester determinant can
pick up extra \(p\)-valuation from such a degree drop.  Therefore a future
content proof must either saturate these structural factors, as the
aligned-corank section does, or work directly with the affine reduced
polynomials.  It is not legitimate to charge the generic infinity defect
as a selected split root.

## 9.4 Structural primes

No structural prime class is discarded in the positive results of this
answer.  The fixed characteristic can divide factorial/Apéry/Lucas
carriers; all literal-root and gap-pair arguments remain valid.  The
structural saturation becomes relevant only if one tries to turn a
Sylvester/Fitting valuation into an upper bound via an integer content.

# 10. What remains after the new deletions

Choose fixed margins
\[
 0<\sigma<26/155,
 \qquad \tau>0,
 \qquad \delta>0,
\]
together with the already-current imbalance margin.  Sections 6--9 show
that, at a total pre-tent cost
\[
 O\!\left(
 T^{69/31-\eta_{\rm new}+o(1)}
 \right),
 \qquad
 \eta_{\rm new}
 =\min\left\{\frac54\sigma,3\tau,\delta,\frac7{31}\right\}>0,
 \tag{10.1}
\]
one can impose all conditions in (R5225), in addition to the Q5208
pair-/anchor-fibre saturation and every canonical source mask.
After the tent the same deletion is
\[
 O\!\left(
 T^{38/31-\eta_{\rm new}+o(1)}M_0
 \right).
\]

On that residual, the exact carrier is the same-root split triple gcd in
(3.3).  A full closure would follow from
\[
 \sum_{(a,b)\in(R5225)}g_p^\square(a,b)
 \ll T^{69/31-\eta+o(1)},
 \tag{10.2}
\]
for some fixed \(\eta>0\), uniformly at the distinguished
characteristic.  Equivalently, because the parameter region is
\(O(T^2)\), one needs an average aligned split-gcd degree below
\[
 T^{7/31-\eta+o(1)}.
 \tag{10.3}
\]

The universal fixed-\(a\) theorem (3.7) gives only \(T^3\) after
summation.  The \(U\)-pencil gives (10.2) only after the unproved content
compression (4.5).  The square character does not lower (3.3).  These are
the first exact failures of the three requested algebraic routes.

A physically sharper equivalent interface is visible from Section 7.
For a middle zero pair
\[
 v=u+a,\qquad w=u+h,
\]
put
\[
 s=\iota(v),\qquad t=w.
\]
An outer extension by \(a\) is exactly the simultaneous occurrence of
\[
 s+a,\qquad t+a
\]
as actual zeros.  Hence the remaining problem is a long-displacement
cross-correlation of two **literal nonwrapping return-level sets** from
the actual anchors \(s,t\), with
\[
 \|t-s\|_p>T^{23/31-\tau}.
\]
A power-saving cross-column theorem on this long-displacement saturated
family would be genuinely different from the absolute pencil-content sum
and would compose with the one-weight Palm consumer without any
normalization change.  No such theorem is presently in the source.

```latex
% Reflection-square arithmetic for the interlaced selected-Palm word a,b,a.
% This block assumes the already-proved actual-return second moment
%   sum_u K_R^+(u)^2 << R^(5/2)
% from the preceding selected-Palm reduction; it does not reprove it.

\begin{lemma}[Reflection-square unfolding and fixed-$a$ sum]
\label{lem:sp-reflection-square-unfolding}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, let $a,b\ge2$, put $h=a+b<p$, and set
\[
 n_d=3(d-1),\qquad
 M_d(Y)=2^{n_d}N_d\!\left(\frac{Y-d-1}{2}\right).
\]
Write
\[
 M_b(Y)=Y^{\epsilon_b}A_b(Y^2),\qquad
 \epsilon_b=\begin{cases}0,&b\text{ odd},\\1,&b\text{ even},\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
 M_a(Y-h)=E_{a,h}(Y^2)+Y O_{a,h}(Y^2).
\]
Define
\[
 g_p^\square(a,b)=
 \deg\gcd_{\mathbb F_p[U]}
 \bigl(
 \overline A_b,\overline E_{a,h},\overline O_{a,h},
 U^{(p-1)/2}-1
 \bigr).
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 2g_p^\square(a,b)=
 \deg\gcd_{\mathbb F_p[Y]}
 \bigl(
 \overline{M_b/Y^{\epsilon_b}},
 \overline M_a(Y-h),
 \overline M_a(Y+h),
 Y^{p-1}-1
 \bigr).}
\]
Under $Y=2x+a+h+1$, the nonzero split roots on the right are exactly
solutions of
\[
 N_a(x)=N_b(x+a)=N_a(x+h)=0
\]
with $Y\ne0$.

If $r_p(a)$ denotes the number of distinct $\mathbb F_p$-roots of
$\overline M_a$, then
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{b\ge2\\a+b<p}}g_p^\square(a,b)
 \le\binom{r_p(a)}2
 \le\binom{3(a-1)}2.}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial $U^{(p-1)/2}-1$ is squarefree and its roots are the
nonzero squares.  Each such root has exactly two lifts $Y=\pm\sqrt U$.
The centered parity identity gives
\[
 M_a(-Y-h)=(-1)^{a-1}M_a(Y+h),
\]
so a common $U$-root lifts to both common $Y$-roots, and conversely.
This proves the first equality.  The displayed affine change gives
\[
 M_b(Y)=2^{n_b}N_b(x+a),\quad
 M_a(Y-h)=2^{n_a}N_a(x),\quad
 M_a(Y+h)=2^{n_a}N_a(x+h),
\]
and $Y^{p-1}-1$ excludes the divided central factor.

For the sum, choose one lift $Y$ of a counted $U$ and put
\[
 \alpha=Y-h,\qquad \beta=-Y-h.
\]
Then $\alpha\ne\beta$ are roots of $\overline M_a$, while
\[
 h=-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2},\qquad
 U=\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^2}{4}.
\]
Thus $(h,U)$ injects into an unordered pair of distinct roots of
$\overline M_a$.  The condition $A_b(U)=0$ only removes pairs, proving
the last bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Shift-parity resultant identity]
\label{prop:sp-shift-parity-resultant}
For integers $a\ge2$ and $H>a$, put $n=3(a-1)$ and write
\[
 M_a(Y-H)=E_{a,H}(Y^2)+Y O_{a,H}(Y^2).
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 2^n|\ell_aM_a(-H)|
 \left|\operatorname{Res}_U(O_{a,H},E_{a,H})\right|^2
 =2^{n^2}
 \left|\operatorname{Res}_x(N_a(x),N_a(x+H))\right|.}
\]
In particular $E_{a,H}$ and $O_{a,H}$ are coprime over $\mathbb Q[U]$.
Moreover, for
\[
 \Theta_a(H)=\operatorname{Res}_U(O_{a,H},E_{a,H})\in\mathbb Z[H]
\]
one has
\[
 \deg_H\Theta_a=\frac{n(n-1)}2,
 \qquad
 |\operatorname{lc}_H\Theta_a|
 =2^{n(n-1)/2}\ell_a^{\,n-1}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the general even--odd resultant identity
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_Y(f(Y),f(-Y))
 =2^n\operatorname{lc}(f)f(0)
   \operatorname{Res}_U(O,E)^2
\]
to $f(Y)=M_a(Y-H)$.  Reflection gives
$f(-Y)=(-1)^{a-1}M_a(Y+H)$.  Translating $Y$ and then using
$Y=2x+a+1$ gives
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Res}_Y(M_a(Y-H),M_a(Y+H))\right|
 =2^{n^2}
 \left|\operatorname{Res}_x(N_a(x),N_a(x+H))\right|.
\]
The root-strip theorem makes the two factors on the right nonzero and
also gives $M_a(-H)\ne0$.  This proves the identity and coprimality.
Comparing the highest powers of $H$ gives the stated degree and leading
coefficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Cross-scale interlaced deletions]
\label{prop:sp-cross-scale-deletions}
Assume the actual nonwrapping return estimate
\[
 \sum_{u\in\mathcal Z_p}K_R^+(u)^2\ll R^{5/2}
\]
for every $R$ in the present short range, and hence the reflected
backward analogue.  Let $Q_a(A;T)$ and $Q_b(B;T)$ count any subfamily of
literal interlaced chains
\[
 u,\quad u+a,\quad u+a+b,\quad u+2a+b
\]
with $T\le a+b<2T$, subject respectively to $a\le A$ and $b\le B$.
Then
\[
 \boxed{Q_a(A;T)\ll(AT)^{5/4},\qquad
        Q_b(B;T)\ll(BT)^{5/4}.}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed $0<\sigma<26/155$, the union
\[
 a\le T^{121/155-\sigma}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 b\le T^{121/155-\sigma}
\]
has cardinality
\[
 O\!\left(T^{69/31-5\sigma/4}ight).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the first bound, a counted chain based at $u$ gives returns of lengths
$a\le A$ and $h=a+b<2T$, so
\[
 Q_a(A;T)
 \le\sum_uK_A^+(u)K_{2T}^+(u)
 \ll(AT)^{5/4}
\]
by Cauchy.

For the second, put $v=u+a$ and $w=v+b$.  For a fixed middle pair
$(v,w)$ the number of outer extensions is at most
$\sqrt{K_{2T}^-(v)K_{2T}^+(w)}$.  If $\mathcal E_B$ is the set of
literal middle zero pairs with $w-v\le B$, Cauchy gives
\[
 Q_b(B;T)^2
 \le
 \left(\sum_{(v,w)\in\mathcal E_B}K_{2T}^-(v)\right)
 \left(\sum_{(v,w)\in\mathcal E_B}K_{2T}^+(w)\right).
\]
The first factor is
\[
 \sum_vK_{2T}^-(v)K_B^+(v)\ll T^{5/4}B^{5/4},
\]
and the second is identical after reflection.  This proves the second
bound.  Finally
\[
 \frac54\left(1+\frac{121}{155}\right)=\frac{69}{31}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Short reflection-displacement deletion]
\label{prop:sp-short-reflection-displacement}
Let $p$ be odd and let a literal nonwrapping interlaced chain be
\[
 z_0=u,\quad z_1=u+a,\quad z_2=u+h,\quad z_3=u+a+h,
 \qquad h=a+b.
\]
Put
\[
 Y_0=2u+a+h+1,\qquad
 \|Y\|_p=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|Y_0-kp|,
\]
and assume the already-deleted case $Y_0\equiv0\pmod p$ is absent.
Then, for every $1\le D<p/2$, the number of such chains satisfying
$\|Y\|_p\le D$ is
\[
 \boxed{O(D^3).}
\]
In particular, with $D=T^{23/31-\tau}$ this subfamily is
\[
 O\!\left(T^{69/31-3\tau}\right).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\iota(x)=p-1-x$ and form
\[
 (r,s)=(\iota(z_1),z_2),\qquad
 (r',s')=(\iota(z_0),z_3)=(r+a,s+a).
\]
All four entries are literal Ap\'ery zeros, and
\[
 s-r=s'-r'=Y_0-p=:q.
\]
If $d=\min(|q|,p-|q|)$, the number $C_p(q)$ of ordered zero pairs with
signed difference $q$ satisfies $C_p(q)\ll d$: for $|q|\le p/2$ this
is the usual bound $A_p(|q|)\le3(|q|-1)$, while for $|q|>p/2$ there
are at most $p-|q|=d$ possible starts.

The chain is recovered from the ordered pair of ordered pairs
$((r,s),(r',s'))$, since their translation is $a$.  For each $d$ there
are only $O(1)$ possible signed $q$ with circular size $d$, so the number
of chains is
\[
 \ll\sum_{d\le D}C_p(q)^2\ll\sum_{d\le D}d^2\ll D^3.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Further saturated interlaced core]
\label{cor:sp-interlaced-further-saturation}
Fix margins
\[
 0<\sigma<26/155,\qquad \tau>0,\qquad \delta>0,
\]
and put $L=T^{7/31-\delta}$.  In addition to every previously proved
selected-Palm deletion, the interlaced residual may be restricted to
\[
 a,b>T^{121/155-\sigma},
 \qquad
 \|2u+a+h+1\|_p>T^{23/31-\tau},
\]
and to parameter pairs satisfying
\[
 F_p^{\rm act}(a,b)>2L,
 \qquad
 g_p^\square(a,b)>L.
\]
The total newly deleted pre-tent mass is
\[
 O\!\left(T^{69/31-\eta+o(1)}\right),
 \qquad
 \eta=\min\left\{\frac54\sigma,3\tau,\delta,\frac7{31}\right\}>0,
\]
and hence its selected-Palm contribution after the $1/h$ tent is
\[
 O\!\left(T^{38/31-\eta+o(1)}M_0\right).
\]
The special center lifts $U=h^2$ (when $a$ is even) and $U=a^2$
(when $h$ is even) may also be removed at $O(T^2)$ pre-tent cost.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The two gap wings are Proposition~\ref{prop:sp-cross-scale-deletions},
and the reflection-displacement sector is
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp-short-reflection-displacement}.  There are
$O(T^2)$ parameter pairs.  Therefore the pairs with
$g_p^\square(a,b)\le L$ contribute at most
$2LT^2=O(T^{69/31-\delta})$ literal starts, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sp-reflection-square-unfolding}; the pairs with
$F_p^{\rm act}(a,b)\le2L$ satisfy the same bound directly.  On the
remaining pairs, $F_p^{\rm act}>2L$ and
$F_p^{\rm act}\le2g_p^\square$ imply $g_p^\square>L$.

For fixed $(a,b)$ a prescribed nonzero value of $U=Y^2$ has at most the
two lifts $Y=\pm\sqrt U$, hence at most two starts.  The forced outer
center of an even $a$ can occur only at $Y=\pm h$, i.e. $U=h^2$; the
symmetric skipped-$h$ center can occur only at $Y=\pm a$, i.e. $U=a^2$.
Their union therefore contributes $O(T^2)$.  The claimed exponent follows
by a finite union of these estimates, and the tent contributes
$T^{-1+o(1)}M_0$.
\end{proof}
```

# Bottom line

The reflection-square carrier is now algebraically pinned down as far as
the current continuant identities permit:

- \(E/O\) is exactly a reflection-pair subresultant, with total fixed-\(a\)
  budget \(O(a^2)\), not an independent third codimension;
- the extra \(A_b\) equation can be packaged in a valid same-root
  \(U\)-Fitting content, but no proved total-content height comes within
the \(T^{69/31}\) budget;
- the split-square selector is exactly the two-to-one quotient of the
  split \(Y\)-root system and has no automatic character cancellation;
- nevertheless, the actual-chain geometry yields the new unconditional
  cutoffs
  \[
  a,b>T^{121/155-\sigma},
  \qquad
  \|Y\|_p>T^{23/31-\tau},
  \]
  together with actual four-fibre and aligned-gcd heaviness at
  \(T^{7/31-\delta}\).

The unresolved object is therefore much narrower: a high-degree,
large-gap, large-reflection-displacement **split common-root** tail.  A
future proof must control that aligned tail itself (or the equivalent
long-displacement cross-column return correlation); scalar resultant gcds,
a generic absolute content envelope, and the bare quadratic-character
selector do not supply the missing \(T^{17/62}\).
