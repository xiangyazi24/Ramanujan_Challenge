# Audit of the Apéry truncated Newton transform

## Executive conclusion

The proposed transform has a genuine exact algebraic representation, but the current evidence at c=1 does not constitute a proof of its exponential growth constant. The relevant conclusion is:

* the transform admits a constant-term representation and an explicit finite recurrence;
* Ji-Cai Liu's transform theorem does not apply directly to this truncated, n-dependent choice K=floor(n/4);
* a single evaluation F_{n,K}(c) is not a radical carrier unless its nonvanishing and size are proved;
* coefficient content or a gcd over several c-values is a different object and must be analysed separately.

No proof currently follows that a finite collection of c-values gives height below the shell mass n/4.

## 1. Exact coefficient/constant-term representation

Write

\[
B_i=\sum_{j=0}^i \binom{i}{j}^2\binom{i+j}{j}^2 .
\]

Insert this into the definition

\[
F_{n,K}(c)=\sum_{i=0}^K(-1)^{K-i}B_i\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i}c^{K-i}.
\]

The binomial kernel has the generating identity

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{K}(-1)^{K-i}\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{K-i}x^i
=[x^K](1+x)^n(1-cx)^{n-1}
\]

after the usual binomial convolution. Therefore F is a finite linear functional of the Apéry sequence against the coefficient sequence of a degree-n polynomial.

Equivalently, with

\[
P_n(x)=(1+x)^n(1-cx)^{n-1},
\]

there is a constant-term representation

\[
F_{n,K}(c)=\operatorname{CT}_x\left(P_n(x)x^{-K}\sum_{i\ge0}B_ix^i\right)
\]

with the sum truncated at degree K. The truncation is essential: it is not the full generating function transform.

## 2. Recurrence description

Because B_i is P-recursive of order two and the kernel is hypergeometric in i, creative telescoping gives a recurrence in n for fixed rational c and K/n ratio. However K=floor(n/4) changes with n, so the resulting recurrence is not the fixed-parameter recurrence used in standard transform theorems.

A direct saddle analysis would require the asymptotics of a two-dimensional coefficient extraction:

\[
\operatorname{CT}_{x,y}\,G(x,y)^n
\]

where the Apéry generating function contributes its own singular geometry. The alternating sign means the naive maximum of absolute summands only proves

\[
|F_{n,K}(c)|\le \exp((\alpha(c)+o(1))n),
\]

not equality.

## 3. Status of the observed constant 1.45096

The numerical value

\[
\frac1n\log|F_{n,\lfloor n/4\rfloor}(1)|\to1.45096\ldots
\]

is compatible with a saddle point contribution, but finite computation does not prove that the dominant saddle survives cancellation.

A rigorous proof would need one of:

1. a positive integral representation with a unique dominant saddle;
2. a Riemann-Hilbert/singularity analysis proving the leading coefficient is nonzero;
3. an exact recurrence plus Perron-Frobenius control of the relevant solution branch.

Absent such a theorem, the constant remains an observed growth rate, not a proved asymptotic.

## 4. Does Liu's transform theorem apply?

No.

The cited transform results concern transforms of Apéry-type sequences under fixed algebraic/hypergeometric transformations. The present object differs in two decisive ways:

### (a) The truncation is n-dependent

Here

\[
K=\lfloor n/4\rfloor .
\]

The transform kernel changes with n. It is not a fixed linear transform of the sequence.

### (b) The transform is not the full Newton transform

The object is

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{K}B_i a_{n,K,i},
\]

not an infinite transform or a fixed generating-function composition. The omitted tail is of the same exponential scale and cannot be ignored.

Therefore Liu's theorem cannot be specialized to prove the claimed constant or a radical bound.

## 5. Multiple c-values and common carriers

Let

\[
F_c=F_{n,K}(c).
\]

There are three different arithmetic objects:

### Single value

\[
F_c.
\]

Its height is controlled only after proving an asymptotic bound.

### Polynomial coefficient content

Write

\[
F_{n,K}(c)=\sum_r A_r c^r.
\]

The content

\[
\gcd_r(A_r)
\]

is a different integer and may be much smaller than any evaluation.

### Gcd across evaluations

For fixed integers c_1,\ldots,c_s,

\[
\gcd(F(c_1),\ldots,F(c_s))
\]

is again different. It only gives a common carrier if divisibility of every required shell prime survives all evaluations.

## 6. Shell-mass comparison

The target radical saving would require a carrier height below roughly

\[
\exp((1/4+o(1))n),
\]

because the shell product has mass corresponding to n/4 primes.

The observed value

\[
\exp(1.45096n)
\]

is much larger. Thus c=1 alone cannot prove the desired radical bound.

For a finite vector

\[
(c_1,\ldots,c_s),
\]

one would need a theorem showing

\[
\log\gcd(F(c_1),\ldots,F(c_s))< (1/4-\epsilon)n.
\]

No such theorem follows from the present transform identities.

## Final verdict

The truncated Newton transform is a promising localization device, but:

* its exact constant-term form is valid;
* its asymptotic growth is not proved by Laplace bounds because of cancellation;
* Liu's transform theorem does not apply to the n-dependent truncated transform;
* no finite choice of c-values is currently proved to produce a sub-shell-mass common carrier.

The route remains open only after a new asymptotic theorem for this moving truncation is established.
