# Extension-field zero bounds and GM-large contribution

## 1. Roots over \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\)

For a nonzero polynomial \(N_h(x)\in \mathbf F_p[x]\) of degree
\(d=3(h-1)\), extension of the base field does **not** increase the
number of roots counted as elements of the field. Therefore

\[
\#\{z\in \mathbf F_{p^2}:N_h(z)=0\}\le d=3(h-1).
\]

This is not a Weil-bound phenomenon; it is the elementary fact that a
nonzero degree \(d\) polynomial over any field has at most \(d\) roots.
The same bound holds over every \(\mathbf F_{p^j}\).

The special Apéry-continuant structure of \(N_h\) could potentially give
stronger information, but the known Gershgorin nonvanishing statement over
complex half-planes does not automatically descend to finite extensions.
A Teichmüller-lift interpretation would require a separate arithmetic
argument controlling the reduction of the zero locus. There is currently no
known general theorem giving fewer than \(3(h-1)\) extension-field roots for
these gap polynomials.

## 2. Resultants and large prime divisors

Let

\[
S_{d,r}=\operatorname{Res}(N_d(x),N_r(x+d)).
\]

A prime \(\ell\) divides \(S_{d,r}\) exactly when the two polynomials have a
common root modulo \(\ell\) (with the usual caveat about leading
coefficients). Thus the resultant packages all possible collision primes.

The crude Hadamard estimate gives

\[
\log |S_{d,r}| \ll \deg(N_d)\deg(N_r)\log H,
\]

where \(H\) bounds coefficients. Hence the number of large prime divisors is
small in the logarithmic sense:

\[
\sum_{\ell>L}v_\ell(S_{d,r})\log \ell\le \log |S_{d,r}|.
\]

For the GM-large term this is the relevant quantity, not the count of prime
factors. Therefore one does not need a sharp classification of extension
roots to control large primes.

## 3. The gcd of two resultant obstructions

For a triple of gaps, the large-prime contribution is controlled by

\[
\sum_{\ell>L}v_\ell(\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3}))\log \ell.
\]

The immediate bound is

\[
\le \log|\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3})|
\le \min(\log|S_{h_1,h_2}|,\log|S_{h_2,h_3}|).
\]

Thus the proposed GM-large mechanism is indeed essentially free once the
saturation computation establishes that the resultant gcds are usually
small. The extension-field question is not the bottleneck for this part.

## 4. Remaining arithmetic bottleneck

The remaining work is not to bound roots over \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\), but to prove
uniform saturation bounds of the form

\[
\log|\gcd(S_{h_1,h_2},S_{h_2,h_3})|=O(1)
\]

(or a sufficiently small average bound) for non-diagonal triples.

If that is available, then all primes above the GM threshold contribute only
through this bounded logarithmic mass, and the large-prime tail does not
require any additional cancellation argument.

**Conclusion:** \(N_h\) has at most \(3(h-1)\) roots over \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\), exactly as over \(\mathbf F_p\). The special determinant structure is interesting but no stronger extension-field zero bound is currently justified. For GM-large, the resultant-gcd logarithmic bound already makes the large-prime contribution essentially free.
