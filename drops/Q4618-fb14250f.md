# 2. Hard folded-coordinate rigidity — unconditional

Fix \(X\le m<X^2\) and a hard hit prime

\[
p\in\mathcal P_X,
\qquad p>2\sqrt m,
\qquad r:=m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p.
\]

Put

\[
s=s_p(m):=\min\{r,p-1-r\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Because \(p\le2X\),

\[
0\le s\le\frac{p-1}{2}<X\le m.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The canonical palindrome gives \(p\mid b_s\).  There are two orientations.

- If \(r=s\), then
  \[
  p\mid m-s.
  \tag{2.3}
  \]
- If \(r=p-1-s\), then
  \[
  p\mid m+s+1.
  \tag{2.4}
  \]

Equivalently, in centered coordinates

\[
y=2m+1,
\qquad t=2s+1,
\]

one has

\[
y\equiv\pm t\pmod p.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Now fix \((m,s)\) with \(0\le s<X\).  Define

\[
\mathcal Q^-_{m,s}
=\{p\in\mathcal P_X:p>2\sqrt m,\ p\mid b_s,\ p\mid m-s\},
\]

\[
\mathcal Q^+_{m,s}
=\{p\in\mathcal P_X:p>2\sqrt m,\ p\mid b_s,\ p\mid m+s+1\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal Q^-_{m,s}|\le1,
\qquad |\mathcal Q^+_{m,s}|\le1.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Indeed, if distinct hard primes \(p,q\) both divided \(m-s\), then

\[
pq>4m>m-s,
\]

contradicting \(pq\mid m-s\).  If they both divided \(m+s+1\), then using \(s\le X-1\) and \(m\ge X\),

\[
m+s+1\le m+X\le2m<4m<pq,
\]

again impossible.

Conversely, every prime in either \(\mathcal Q^-_{m,s}\) or \(\mathcal Q^+_{m,s}\) is a hard hit: in the plus orientation, palindromy changes the zero \(s\) into \(p-1-s\).

Thus a fixed folded coordinate supports at most two hard hit primes:

\[
\boxed{
\#\{p\text{ hard hit at }m:s_p(m)=s\}\le2.}
\tag{2.7}
\]

The two orientations overlap only in the central case \(p=2s+1\).  For a fixed row, at most one hard central prime can occur, since every such prime divides \(2m+1\), while the product of two hard primes is \(>4m>2m+1\).  Hence the oriented divisor representation overcounts \(K_X^\sharp(m)\) by at most one.

This is the first place where the condition \(p>2\sqrt m\) is genuinely used rather than discarded.

# 5. Six reflection-gap resultants: exact Apéry obstruction

Take one noncentral actual folded zero orbit at prime \(p\):

\[
s\in\mathcal Z_p,
\qquad p-1-s\in\mathcal Z_p,
\qquad 0\le s<(p-1)/2.
\]

Its gap is

\[
h=(p-1-s)-s=p-1-2s.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since \(p\) is odd, \(h\) is even.  The canonical reflection identity for the gap polynomial gives the integral factorization

\[
N_h(x)=(2x+h+1)N_h^\circ(x).
\tag{5.2}
\]

At \(x=s\),

\[
2s+h+1=p.
\]

Therefore, identically over \(\mathbb Z\),

\[
\boxed{N_{p-1-2s}(s)=p\,N_{p-1-2s}^\circ(s).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is stronger than saying that the gap theorem is too weak.  The conclusion

\[
p\mid N_{p-1-2s}(s)
\]

holds for **every integer \(s\)** for which \(h=p-1-2s\) is formed, regardless of whether \(b_s\) vanishes.  It is exactly the forced reflection-center factor which canonical `main` deflates in the palindromic aligned-content construction.

Thus the attempted implication

```text
actual zero orbit at p
  -> nontrivial p-divisibility of a reflection-gap carrier
  -> six nonzero resultants carrying p1,...,p6
```

fails at the first arrow after central deflation.  The zero orbit gives no reason for

\[
p\mid N_h^\circ(s).
\]

For a central singleton \(s=(p-1)/2\), there is not even a positive reflection gap to feed to the gap polynomial.  At a fixed hard row at most one such central prime exists, but that does not create a six-characteristic carrier either.

The skipped-triple, aligned-pencil, and fully deflated certificates in `atom_tail_section.tex` genuinely recover arithmetic only when **one characteristic sees several nonstructural return gaps**.  A folded-far six-prime row supplies one reflected orbit in each of six different characteristics; it does not give any one \(p_i\) the additional noncentered returns required by those resultants.

So the existing resultant package cannot simply be tensored six times: doing so either retains six tautological center factors or removes the six prime marks.

# 6. A nonzero six-label Vandermonde exists, but it factorizes at the wrong height

The hard folded-coordinate lemma gives a clean centered parametrization.  For a six-prime hard row write

\[
y=2m+1,
\qquad t_i=2s_i+1,
\qquad \varepsilon_i\in\{+1,-1\},
\]

so that

\[
y\equiv\varepsilon_i t_i\pmod{p_i}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Set

\[
n_i=y-\varepsilon_i t_i.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then \(p_i\mid n_i\), and explicitly

\[
n_i=
\begin{cases}
2(m-s_i),&\varepsilon_i=+1,\\
2(m+s_i+1),&\varepsilon_i=-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For a folded-far tuple the signed integers \(\varepsilon_i t_i\) are pairwise distinct, hence the \(n_i\) are pairwise distinct.

The ordinary Vandermonde

\[
V=\det(n_i^{j-1})_{1\le i,j\le6}
 =\prod_{i<j}(n_j-n_i)
\tag{6.4}
\]

is therefore nonzero, and the common \(m\) makes every difference small:

\[
|n_j-n_i|\ll X.
\]

But (6.4) does **not** retain the six characteristic marks.  Modulo \(p_i\), its \(i\)-th row is

\[
(1,0,0,0,0,0),
\]

which does not force \(V\equiv0\pmod{p_i}\).

The first natural marked Vandermonde is

\[
D=\det(n_i^j)_{1\le i,j\le6}
 =\left(\prod_{i=1}^6n_i\right)
  \prod_{i<j}(n_j-n_i).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Now

\[
\boxed{\prod_{i=1}^6p_i\mid D,}
\]

and \(D\ne0\).  Thus (6.5) passes the first two hostile checks: it is a genuine nonzero integer and it retains all six distinct characteristics.

It fails the height check exactly.  Since \(m<X^2\), \(s_i<X\),

\[
|n_i|\ll X^2,
\qquad |n_i-n_j|\ll X,
\]

so

\[
|D|\ll X^{12}X^{15}=X^{27}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

More importantly, dividing out the nonzero Vandermonde does not leave a compressed carrier; it leaves precisely

\[
\frac{D}{V}=\prod_i n_i.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Write \(n_i=2c_ip_i\).  The hard inequality gives

\[
c_i\ll X,
\]

indeed \(c_i<X/2+O(1)\).  Therefore

\[
\prod_i n_i
=2^6\left(\prod_i p_i\right)\left(\prod_i c_i\right),
\qquad
\prod_i c_i\ll X^6.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The determinant has merely multiplied six independent hard cofactors.  It has not supplied the missing \(X^{-4}\) CRT landing saving.

This slack is not an artifact of a loose upper bound.  On any constant-scale row box \(m\asymp cX^2\) with fixed \(0<c<1/4\), the hard condition is automatic for all \(p>X\), and the quotients \((m\pm s_i)/p_i\) can be of order \(X\).  Thus the \(X^6\) cofactor product is geometrically natural.

A Wronskian determinant has the same problem in another form.  At a zero \(s_i\), the Apéry Casoratian certifies that the adjacent state is **nonzero** modulo \(p_i\); it supplies transversality inside one characteristic, not a second cross-characteristic vanishing row.  A six-row determinant can force every \(p_i\) only by inserting a personal factor such as \(b_{s_i}\) or \(n_i\), which restores the independent-height product.

# 7. The single common carrier \(b_m\) is nonzero but exponentially too tall

Gessel--Lucas does give one genuine common integer.  If

\[
m=a_ip_i+r_i,
\qquad r_i\in\mathcal Z_{p_i},
\]

then

\[
b_m\equiv b_{a_i}b_{r_i}\equiv0\pmod{p_i}.
\]

Hence a six-hit row gives

\[
\boxed{p_1p_2\cdots p_6\mid b_m.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

This retains all six characteristics without multiplying six different Apéry heights.  But canonical growth gives only

\[
\log b_m=O(m).
\tag{7.2}
\]

For the hard rows \(m\asymp X^2\), (7.2) permits \(O(X^2/\log X)\) window prime divisors, which is weaker than prime counting.  Even the hard lower bound

\[
p_1\cdots p_6>(2\sqrt m)^6=64m^3
\]

is polynomial, whereas \(b_m\) is exponential in \(m\).

Combining the common carrier with the six linear hard carriers does not fix the exponent.  The product of the six linears is \(O(m^6)\), so

\[
p_1\cdots p_6
\mid
\gcd\!\left(b_m,\prod_i n_i\right)
\le\prod_i|n_i|\ll m^6.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The hard radical is \(>64m^3\), leaving an \(m^3\) slack, i.e. exactly \(X^6\) at \(m\asymp X^2\).  This is the same cofactor loss as (6.8).

Thus the recurrence/common-integer route fails by an explicit height cancellation, not by lack of a nonzero carrier.

# 8. Multi-prime large sieve: the same missing \(X^4\) appears as frequency resolution

For distinct \(p_1,\ldots,p_6\), an ordered choice of actual zero residues has a unique CRT representative modulo

\[

The hard moment asks whether that representative lies in a row interval of length \(\asymp X^2\).  Thus the independence-scale landing proportion is

\[
\frac{X^2}{P}\asymp X^{-4}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is precisely the factor missing from the deterministic tuple bound in `pairpalm_result.tex`.

The hard mask does not change the exponent in (8.1): on \(m\le X^2/4\) it is inactive, and even on a fixed higher subbox the admissible row interval still has length \(\asymp X^2\).

The ordinary one-prime Fourier expansion is

\[
\mathbf1_{\{m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p\}}-rac{Z(p)}p
=\frac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}F_p(a)e_p(-am).
\tag{8.2}
\]

For the one-fold sum, the frequencies \(a/p\) have spacing \(\gg X^{-2}\), and the classical large sieve on an interval of length \(X^2\), together with Parseval

\[
\sum_{a\bmod p}|F_p(a)|^2=pZ(p),
\]

indeed gives a natural centered second-moment scale \(\ll X^2\lambda_X\).

At the six-characteristic level, however, aggregate frequencies are sums of six rational frequencies.  Distinct such sums can have denominator of order \(X^6\), so their natural spacing is only \(\gg X^{-6}\).  A classical large-sieve constant is then

\[
X^2+X^6\asymp X^6,
\]

which loses exactly the \(X^4\) in (8.1) before any Apéry-specific cancellation is obtained.  Exact collisions among aggregate frequencies can only make the naive convolution estimate harder.

The folded-far condition is a separation statement about the **physical zero positions** \(s_i\); it does not enlarge the spacing of the six-prime rational frequencies.  Consequently a standard multi-prime large sieve does not prove the needed prefix discrepancy.

The corrected centered/near-Farey calculation from the delivery history uses signed reciprocal-phase cancellation.  That mechanism also cannot simply be inserted here: every term in a factorial collision count has coefficient \(+1\), and the folded-far type is outside the near-offset sector in any event.

A successful large-sieve route would therefore have to be a genuinely new six-characteristic **mixed-phase** theorem for the actual Apéry Fourier coefficients, not the classical large sieve plus primewise Parseval.

```latex
\begin{lemma}[Hard folded-coordinate rigidity]
\label{lem:hm-hard-fold-rigidity}
Let $X\ge2$, $X\le m<X^2$, and let $p$ be a prime with
\[
 X<p\le2X,\qquad p>2\sqrt m,\qquad
 m\bmod p\in\mathcal Z_p.
\]
Write $r=m\bmod p$ and
\[
 s_p(m)=\min\{r,p-1-r\}.
\]
Then $0\le s_p(m)<X$, $p\mid b_{s_p(m)}$, and
\[
 p\mid m-s_p(m)\quad\text{or}\quad
 p\mid m+s_p(m)+1.
\]
Moreover, for fixed $m$ and $0\le s<X$, each of the sets
\[
 \{p:X<p\le2X,\ p>2\sqrt m,\ p\mid b_s,\ p\mid m-s\},
\]
\[
 \{p:X<p\le2X,\ p>2\sqrt m,\ p\mid b_s,\ p\mid m+s+1\}
\]
has cardinality at most one.  Consequently at most two hard hit
primes at a fixed row have the same folded coordinate $s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $p\le2X$,
$s_p(m)\le(p-1)/2<X\le m$.  Palindromy of the Ap\'ery zero set
shows $p\mid b_{s_p(m)}$.  If $r=s_p(m)$ then
$p\mid m-s_p(m)$, while if $r=p-1-s_p(m)$ then
$p\mid m+s_p(m)+1$.

If distinct hard primes $p,q$ divided $m-s$, then
$pq>4m>m-s$, contradicting $pq\mid m-s$.
If they divided $m+s+1$, then
$m+s+1\le m+X\le2m<4m<pq$, again a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Folded-far extraction from a hard row]
\label{prop:hm-hard-folded-far-extraction}
For $H\ge1$, let $D_{6,\mathrm{ff}}^\sharp(m;H)$ be the number of
ordered six-tuples of distinct primes counted by $K_X^\sharp(m)$
whose folded coordinates satisfy
\[
 |s_{p_i}(m)-s_{p_j}(m)|>H\qquad(i\ne j),
\]
and put
\[
 F_{6,\mathrm{ff}}^\sharp(X;H)
 =\sum_{X\le m<X^2}D_{6,\mathrm{ff}}^\sharp(m;H).
\]
Then
\[
 \max_{X\le m<X^2}K_X^\sharp(m)
 \ll H+\bigl(F_{6,\mathrm{ff}}^\sharp(X;H)\bigr)^{1/6}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

coordinate supports at most two hard hit primes.  Hence, for a fixed
hard hit prime, at most
\[
 \Delta_H=2(2\lfloor H\rfloor+1)
\]
other hard hits have folded coordinate within distance $H$ (including
the chosen hit itself in this harmless upper bound).

\[
 D_{6,\mathrm{ff}}^\sharp(m;H)
 \ge\prod_{j=0}^5(K-j\Delta_H)
 \ge(K/2)^6.
\]
If $K<10\Delta_H$ then $K\ll H$.  Combining the two cases and then
maximizing over $m$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\label{cor:hm-hard-folded-far-consumer}
```
