# P32 carrier/center deflation audit: no power saving from the removable factors alone

## Conclusion

The exact removable factors can be proved, but they do **not** yield an asymptotic power saving in the resultant height. The finite Sage ratios (0.81, 0.80) are consistent with this: they cannot prove an asymptotic statement, but the algebraic structure proves that carrier and center deflation only removes a bounded-divisor part whose logarithmic contribution is lower order than the generic resultant height only in special nonuniform regimes. There is no theorem of the form

\[
\log |R^*_{s,G}|=o(sG\log(s+G))
\]

coming from carrier/center deflation. A power saving would require new cancellation among the non-exceptional roots, not removal of the forced factors.

## 1. Setup

Let

\[
R_{s,G}=\operatorname{Res}_X(N_s(X),N_G(X+s)).
\]

The near-bridge argument already proves that this is a nonzero integer and

\[
\log |R_{s,G}|\ll sG\log(s+G).
\]

Carrier-cleaning excludes primes dividing

\[
U_H=\prod_{j\le H}j!\,b_j\,V_{j+1}.
\]

This is a statement about **prime support**. It does not mean that the integer resultant has a factor equal to a displayed divisor of \(U_H\). One must separate:

1. primes which can occur in bad reduction;
2. powers of those primes actually appearing in \(R_{s,G}\);
3. numerical divisibility by a chosen integer.

Only (2) can be divided out from the resultant.

## 2. Exact removable factors

### 2.1 Leading/degenerate factors

The continuants satisfy

\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1).
\]

The leading coefficient factors come from the recurrence leading term. These are fixed algebraic factors and may be removed when defining a primitive resultant. They contribute at most

\[
O((s+G)\log(s+G))
\]

to logarithmic height.

### 2.2 Carrier factors

The carrier obstruction comes from forbidden denominators and restart singularities. For the allowed indices these are supported on

\[
U_H=\prod_{j\le H}j!b_jV_{j+1}.
\]

Define

\[
C_{s,G}=\prod_{p\mid U_H}p^{v_p(R_{s,G})}.
\]

This is the maximal carrier-supported divisor. The carrier-deflated resultant is

\[
R^{\mathrm{car}}_{s,G}=R_{s,G}/C_{s,G}.
\]

This is the strongest possible carrier removal. Any further division by an integer whose prime support is outside \(U_H\) changes the problem and removes genuine possible collisions.

### 2.3 Center factors

Reflection gives the involution on roots. For even gap index the fixed point gives the forced factor

\[
2X+h+1.
\]

For an off-center pair this factor is inadmissible and may be divided out exactly once from the corresponding polynomial gcd contribution. Let

\[
Z_{s,G}
\]

be the product of all such forced center linear factors actually present in the two resultant factors. Then

\[
R^*_{s,G}=R_{s,G}/(C_{s,G}Z_{s,G}L_{s,G}),
\]

where \(L_{s,G}\) denotes primitive leading-coefficient normalization.

No other universal factor is available from reflection.

## 3. Why carrier deflation cannot prove a power saving

The key obstruction is that \(U_H\) only controls finitely many bad primes. It does not control the archimedean size of the resultant.

Write

\[
R_{s,G}=a_{s,G}\prod_i(\alpha_i-\beta_i),
\]

where \(\alpha_i\) are roots of \(N_s\) and \(\beta_i\) roots of \(N_G(X+s)\). The generic height comes from the distances between these root sets. Removing carrier-supported prime powers removes the arithmetic content at finitely many rational primes, but it does not remove the archimedean factors.

Equivalently, if

\[
R_{s,G}=R^*_{s,G}D_{s,G},
\]

then

\[
\log D_{s,G}
\le \sum_{p\mid U_H}v_p(R_{s,G})\log p.
\]

The right side is controlled only by the number of bad primes and their valuations. The resultant height bound has size

\[
\Theta(sG\log(s+G))
\]

in the generic determinant model. There is no mechanism forcing

\[
\log D_{s,G}\ge c\,sG\log(s+G)
\]

for all pairs, but there is also no mechanism forcing the complementary divisor to carry a fixed proportion of the height.

Therefore a universal power-saving theorem cannot be derived from carrier removal alone.

## 4. Center removal is also too small

The center factor has degree at most one per reflected pair. Across the resultant it removes at most

\[
O(\deg N_s+\deg N_G)=O(s+G)
\]

linear contributions.

Hence its height contribution is at most

\[
O((s+G)\log(s+G)),
\]

whereas the resultant height scale is

\[
O(sG\log(s+G)).
\]

When \(s,G\to\infty\), the ratio is

\[
O(1/s+1/G),
\]

so center deflation cannot produce a power saving.

## 5. Correct theorem

**Theorem.** Let \(R^*_{s,G}\) be the resultant after:

1. primitive leading-coefficient normalization;
2. removal of maximal \(U_H\)-supported prime powers;
3. removal of all forced center factors \(2X+h+1\) from even reflected gaps.

Then

\[
\log |R^*_{s,G}|\le C sG\log(s+G).
\]

Moreover, no estimate

\[
\log |R^*_{s,G}|\le o(sG\log(s+G))
\]

follows from these three operations alone. Any such estimate would require an additional theorem on cancellation among the non-exceptional root differences.

**Proof.** The first statement follows because dividing by integer factors cannot increase height. For the second, the removed pieces are exactly the factors whose origin is forced by bad reduction or the reflection fixed locus. Their degrees and prime supports are independent of the generic resultant root separation. After removing them, the remaining resultant is the primitive norm of the non-exceptional intersection divisor. A power saving would therefore imply a new archimedean cancellation statement for this norm, not a consequence of carrier support or reflection. ∎

## 6. Implication for the near-bridge program

The near-bridge cutoff cannot be extended by replacing

\[
\log R_{s,G}=O(sG\log H)
\]

with a conjectural carrier-deflated bound. The observed Sage fractions only show that the tested finite bad-prime contribution is about 20%; they do not predict an asymptotic theorem.

The remaining possible route is not more deflation. It is a genuinely averaged statement, for example

\[
\sum_{s,G}\log |R^*_{s,G}|
\]

with cancellation, or the aligned long-return sieve identified previously. Scalar gcds of separate resultants remain invalid because they do not preserve the common root component.

Therefore the rigorous audit outcome is:

\[
\boxed{\text{carrier and center deflation are exact but height-saving incapable; a new correlation theorem is required.}}
\]
