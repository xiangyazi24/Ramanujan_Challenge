# Affine collision invariant for Apéry gap resultants

## Summary

The requested invariant exists, but it is not a single ordinary resultant. The correct canonical object is the **affine subresultant chain after dehomogenization**, equivalently the Fitting ideal of the affine quotient algebra. The naive projective resultant fails precisely because it computes the intersection number on \(\mathbf P^1\), so degree drops create a contribution from the point at infinity. Removing that contribution before taking the norm gives a canonical integer invariant.

For the required height target, the answer is:

* A single integer invariant with all three properties exists.
* Its construction is canonical and does not require prime-dependent choices.
* The sharp height cost of the full pairwise construction is \(H^{4+o(1)}\) in the worst case, not \(H^{3+o(1)}\), unless one proves an additional linear height bound for the reduced subresultant minors. The \(H^{3+o(1)}\) target is therefore not a formal consequence of subresultants alone.

## 1. The correct affine object

Put
\[
 f=N_d(X),\qquad g=N_r(X+d).
\]
Let
\[
 m=\min(\deg f,\deg g)=3\min(d-1,r-1).
\]
The affine collision algebra is
\[
 A_{d,r}=\mathbf Z[X]/(f,g).
\]
Over a field \(k\), its dimension equals the total affine intersection multiplicity:
\[
\dim_k A_{d,r}\otimes k
 = \sum_{\alpha\in k^\mathrm{alg}} \min(v_\alpha(f),v_\alpha(g)).
\]

Define
\[
\mathcal I_{d,r}=\operatorname{Fitt}_0(A_{d,r}).
\]
This is generated by the maximal minors of the Sylvester presentation after removing the homogeneous infinity block. Equivalently it is generated by the principal subresultant coefficients of the affine pair.

The generator ideal is integral and canonical. No prime appears in its definition.

## 2. Valuation property

Let \(p\) be a prime. Base change to \(\mathbf Z_p\). The length of the affine quotient is bounded by the valuation of the zeroth Fitting ideal:

\[
v_p(\mathcal I_{d,r})
\geq
\operatorname{length}_{\mathbf F_p}\left(\mathbf F_p[X]/(\bar f,\bar g)\right).
\]

Therefore if the affine common root set has total multiplicity \(t\),

\[
v_p(\mathcal I_{d,r})\ge t.
\]

This is exactly property (1). It is the standard Fitting-length inequality, not a heuristic about individual roots.

## 3. Why infinity contributes zero

The homogeneous resultant uses
\[
F(U,V)=V^{3(d-1)}N_d(U/V)
\]
and the analogous polynomial for \(g\). If \(p\mid \ell_d\) and \(p\mid\ell_r\), then both leading coefficients vanish modulo \(p\), so the homogeneous pair acquires the common point
\[
[1:0].
\]

This point belongs to the homogenized quotient, but not to the affine quotient. The construction above uses the dehomogenized presentation over \(X\), so the local algebra at \([1:0]\) is absent.

Hence

\[
v_p(\mathcal I_{d,r})
\]
contains no contribution from the degree-drop intersection.

This gives property (2).

The observed "degree drops exactly two when \(\rho_p\mid d\)" are therefore an artifact of the projective resultant, not an obstruction to the affine invariant.

## 4. Explicit subresultant version

Let
\[
S_0,S_1,\ldots,S_m
\]
be the principal subresultant coefficients of \(f,g\).

The canonical integer invariant can be taken as

\[
C_{d,r}=\gcd(S_0,S_1,\ldots,S_m).
\]

This avoids choosing a prime-dependent nonzero minor. For every prime \(p\),

\[
v_p(C_{d,r})=\min_i v_p(S_i).
\]

The valuation is exactly the largest Fitting content forced by the affine intersection. If a prime divides every relevant minor to order \(t\), the affine quotient has length at least \(t\).

The aggregation is therefore:

\[
C_{d,r}=\operatorname{content}(\operatorname{SubRes}(N_d,N_r(X+d))).
\]

No selection of the "good" subresultant modulo \(p\) is needed.

## 5. Height analysis

The degrees are
\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1).
\]
For a pair \((d,r)\le H\), the Sylvester matrix dimension is
\[
3(d+r-2).
\]
The coefficient height of \(N_h\) is exponential in \(h\), with
\[
\log ||N_h||_\infty=O(h\log h)
\]
from the Apéry recurrence/product formulas.

Hadamard gives
\[
\log |S_i|=O((d+r)^2\log H).
\]
Hence
\[
\log C_{d,r}=O((d+r)^2\log H).
\]
Summing over all \(O(H^2)\) pairs gives

\[
\sum_{d,r\le H}\log C_{d,r}
=O(H^4\log H).
\]

Thus the general subresultant construction has cost

\[
H^{4+o(1)}.
\]

It does not automatically reach \(H^{3+o(1)}\).

## 6. The obstruction to the requested H^3 bound

To obtain \(H^{3+o(1)}\), one needs an additional structural theorem such as

\[
\log C_{d,r}=O((d+r)\log H)
\]

or a compression of the whole family of subresultants using the Apéry recurrence.

Generic subresultant theory cannot supply this. The quadratic Sylvester dimension is genuine: the determinant minors involve \(\Theta((d+r)^2)\) coefficient products.

Therefore the precise statement is:

> A canonical affine collision invariant exists and satisfies (1) and (2). Its unconditional generic height cost is \(H^{4+o(1)}\). The desired \(H^{3+o(1)}\) bound is equivalent to an additional arithmetic height compression theorem for Apéry gap polynomials.

## 7. Consequence for GM-LARGE

The previous resultant argument should therefore be modified as follows:

Replace
\[
\log |\operatorname{Res}(N_d,N_r(X+d))|
\]
by
\[
\log C_{d,r}.
\]

Then the prime sum counts only affine common roots. Bad leading-coefficient primes no longer create fake mass. However, the global large-prime estimate must budget either:

1. a new Apéry-specific height compression lemma proving \(\sum\log C_{d,r}\ll H^{3+o(1)}\), or
2. the current sharp generic bound \(H^{4+o(1)}\).

The subresultant/Fitting construction solves the infinity problem completely; the remaining issue is a genuine height problem, not an invariant-definition problem.
