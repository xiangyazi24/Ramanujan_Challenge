# Q586 — Bounded multiplicity of the Apéry ratio map

## Executive summary

The computational discovery is important, but the correct interpretation is:

\[
R_r=f_r/g_r
\]

having multiplicity at most 5 is **not a formal consequence of the Wronskian or SL₂ monodromy alone**. It is an additional arithmetic property of this particular non-autonomous projective orbit.

The Wronskian proves that the map has no adjacent collisions and gives local transversality. It does not prevent many separated orbit points from returning to the same projective point.

The most promising proof route is not a static algebraic curve degree argument. The right object is the finite-field projective cocycle

\[
[v_{r+1}]=M(r)[v_r]\in \mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p),
\]

and bounded multiplicity is a statement that this cocycle has bounded return multiplicity to any projective fiber.

This is a dynamical monodromy statement, stronger than Zariski-density of the generated group.

---

## (A) Wronskian approach

The Wronskian identity

\[
W_r=f_rg_{r+1}-f_{r+1}g_r=\frac{(-1)^r}{(r+1)^3}
\]

immediately gives

\[
R_r\neq R_{r+1}.
\]

Indeed,

\[
f_rg_{r+1}-f_{r+1}g_r=0
\]

is exactly the condition that the two projective points coincide.

However, this is only a nearest-neighbor statement.

For \(r<s\),

\[
R_r=R_s
\]

means

\[
P_sP_r^{-1}
\]

fixes \([v_r]\). The mistake would be to count fixed points of a single matrix. The matrix depends on both \(r,s\), so the argument does not give

\[
\#\{r:R_r=R_s\}\ll1.
\]

A cocycle can have Zariski-dense monodromy and still have exceptional orbit returns.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Wronskian gives transversality, not bounded multiplicity.}}
\]

---

## (B) Algebraic curve / degree argument

A genuine rational map

\[
\phi:\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1
\]

of degree \(d\) has fibers of size at most \(d\).

But the Apéry object is not such a fixed rational map.

The map

\[
r\mapsto R_r
\]

is a sequence generated by a varying transfer matrix:

\[
v_r=M(r-1)\cdots M(1)v_1.
\]

There is no single rational function \(R(x)\in\mathbf F_p(x)\) whose value at integer \(r\) gives \(R_r\).

Thus the curve

\[
R_r=R_s
\]

is not an algebraic correspondence of bounded degree in two variables.

If one interpolates the sequence by a polynomial of degree \(p\), Bezout gives only degree \(p\)-scale bounds, which are useless.

The observed multiplicity \(\approx5\) is therefore evidence of hidden dynamical rigidity, not low algebraic degree.

---

## (C) Wronskian variety viewpoint

The pair \((f_r,g_r)\) satisfies the determinant constraint

\[
f_rg_{r+1}-f_{r+1}g_r=c_r.
\]

This places consecutive pairs on a family of determinant fibers.

However, the collision condition is

\[
[f_r:g_r]=[f_s:g_s],
\]

which compares non-adjacent points. The Wronskian controls the area of one step, not the long-term recurrence of projective directions.

The correct geometric object is a discrete orbit in the moduli space

\[
\mathrm{SL}_2/\mathrm{B}\cong\mathbf P^1.
\]

Bounded multiplicity asks for an orbit-hitting estimate:

\[
\#\{r\le p:[v_r]=x\}=O(1).
\]

This resembles expansion theory for group actions, not algebraic geometry of a fixed curve.

---

## (D) Does SL₂ monodromy imply bounded multiplicity?

No.

This is the key distinction.

SL₂ monodromy says that the subgroup generated by transfer matrices is large:

\[
\overline{\langle M(n)\rangle}=SL_2.
\]

It rules out algebraic degeneracy.

But a deterministic walk on a large group can revisit points many times. For example, a random walk on a finite group has approximately uniform return statistics, but a deterministic sequence generated by a structured word can have large multiplicities.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
SL_2\text{ monodromy}+\text{Wronskian}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{bounded multiplicity}.
}
\]

Additional Apéry-specific arithmetic input is required.

---

## What extra structure could prove it?

### 1. Projective cocycle expansion

The strongest formulation is:

For every \(x\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\),

\[
\#\{0\le r<p/2:[v_r]=x\}\le C.
\]

This is a finite-field expansion statement for the Apéry cocycle.

A possible proof would require:

- large projective monodromy;
- quantitative mixing of the non-autonomous walk;
- exclusion of short relations in the cocycle.

This is beyond ordinary difference Galois theory.

---

### 2. Transfer-product trace method

A collision satisfies

\[
P_{s,r}=M(s-1)\cdots M(r)
\]

fixes a point.

For generic \(P_{s,r}\in SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\), the number of fixed projective points is 0 or 2.

Hence if one could prove that the family

\[
\{P_{s,r}:s-r=h\}
\]

is equidistributed in \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\), then collision counts would be Poisson-scale.

This is the closest analogue of Katz equidistribution, but now for the transfer cocycle rather than a sheaf fiber.

---

## Relation to ZM₂

The computational observation would immediately imply

\[
|Z_p|\le10
\]

because zeros correspond to the projective value \(-5\).

This would prove the strong form

\[
\sum_p |Z_p|^2\ll \pi(X).
\]

However, proving it requires the missing orbit-mixing theorem.

The result is much stronger than what is needed:

- ZM₂ only needs average bounded multiplicity.
- The computation suggests uniform bounded multiplicity.

A realistic hierarchy is:

1. Prove average fiber bound:
\[
\sum_x \#R^{-1}(x)^2\ll p.
\]

2. Deduce
\[
\sum_p|Z_p|^2\ll\pi(X).
\]

3. Only later attempt uniform bound \(\max_x|R^{-1}(x)|=O(1)\).

---

## Final answer

(A) Wronskian proves no adjacent collisions, but cannot bound global multiplicity.

(B) Bezout/Hurwitz does not apply because \(r\mapsto R_r\) is not a fixed algebraic morphism.

(C) The Wronskian variety gives local geometry but not long-range collision control.

(D) The phenomenon is not forced by SL₂ monodromy. It requires additional dynamical information about the Apéry transfer cocycle.

The computational discovery points to the correct missing theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The Apéry projective transfer cocycle has bounded (or at least }L^2\text{-bounded) fiber multiplicity.}
}
\]

This is a horizontal equidistribution theorem for the recurrence orbit, and would directly solve the ZM₂ bottleneck.
