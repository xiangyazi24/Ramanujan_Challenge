# Verdict

**YES: there is a rigorous plus-only, occurrence-preserving arithmetic bound on the common-target multiplicity.** One does not need a speculative joint resultant for the shifted upper primes. The common lower prime already gives a genuinely pairwise continuant carrier between *two distinct common-target occurrences*, and a polynomial root-count/energy argument gives

\[
 m \ll N^{2/3},
\]

where \(N\) is the number of admissible integer values of the plus displacement \(d\) in the shell interval. In particular, over the entire legal plus range, \(N\le \lceil p/6\rceil\), so for \(p\ge 43\)

\[
\boxed{m\le 7\,\lceil p/6\rceil^{2/3}=O(p^{2/3}).}
\]

This counts actual common target occurrences injectively; primality of \(q,q+t\), upper selectedness, the macroscopic shell restrictions, and deterministic minus-first assignment are all extra filters and may be dropped when proving the upper bound.

There is also a fixed two-centre carrier

\[
\boxed{p\mid N_{6t}(z_0)},
\]

so for fixed \((p,t)\) there are at most \(18t-3\) possible residues \(z_0\pmod p\) supporting a lower-zero pair at spacing \(6t\).

What I do **not** obtain from the standard Apéry recurrence/Lucas/reflection package is a non-unary characteristic-zero carrier involving both shifted upper moduli \(q\) and \(q+t\). The first exact obstruction there is modulus drift: after reflection the two upper zero conditions live modulo two different primes, so the one-modulus continuant mechanism cannot be applied across them. The \(O(N^{2/3})\) theorem avoids that obstruction entirely.

# 1. Exact plus normal form

Write

\[
z=z_0,\qquad z_1=z+6t,
\]

and for a common target put

\[
d=\frac{a+z+1}{6},\qquad a=6d-z-1,\qquad q=p+d.
\]

For the first centre,

\[
u_0=p-a-2z-2.
\]

Reflecting the upper selected index modulo \(q\),

\[
q-1-u_0
 =p+d-1-(p-a-2z-2)
 =d+a+2z+1
 =z+7d.
\]

For the second centre,

\[
d_1=d+t,\qquad q_1=q+t,\qquad u_1=u_0-12t,
\]

hence

\[
q_1-1-u_1=(q-1-u_0)+13t=z+7d+13t.
\]

Thus the exact plus common-target conditions imply the clean system

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
p&\mid b_z,\ b_{z+6t},\ b_{6d-z-1},\\
p+d&\text{ is prime},\qquad p+d\mid b_{z+7d},\\
p+d+t&\text{ is prime},\qquad p+d+t\mid b_{z+7d+13t}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1}
\]

This is the useful normalization. Distinct common endpoints \(y\) give distinct \(a=p-1-y\), hence distinct \(d\). Therefore passing from common targets to \(d\)-occurrences loses no multiplicity.

# 2. Gap continuant and exact transfer identity

Put

\[
P(X)=34X^3+51X^2+27X+5.
\]

The Apéry numbers satisfy

\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1}.
\tag{2}
\]

Define the gap continuants

\[
N_0(X)=0,\qquad N_1(X)=1,
\]

\[
N_{h+1}(X)=P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X).
\tag{3}
\]

A direct induction on \(h\) gives

\[
\left(\prod_{j=2}^{h}(r+j)^3\right)b_{r+h}
 =N_h(r)b_{r+1}-(r+1)^3N_{h-1}(r+1)b_r.
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently, if \(0\le r<r+h<p\) and

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad p\mid b_{r+h},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{p\mid N_h(r).}
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, two consecutive Apéry numbers cannot both vanish modulo such a prime: if \(p\mid b_n,b_{n+1}\) with \(0<n<p\), (2) gives \(p\mid b_{n-1}\), and descending reaches \(b_0=1\); the case \(n=0\) is already impossible. Hence \(b_{r+1}\not\equiv0\pmod p\), and (4) gives (5).

Applying (5) to the two fixed centres immediately yields

\[
\boxed{p\mid N_{6t}(z).}
\tag{6}
\]

This is already a genuinely binary centre carrier, not the product of two unary edge conditions.

# 3. Crucial lemma: \(N_h\) is never the zero polynomial mod \(p\) when \(p>h,5\)

This is the point needed to make root counting rigorous; one must not silently assume that a characteristic-zero continuant stays nonzero after reduction modulo \(p\).

For \(h\ge2\), \(N_h(X)\) is the determinant of the \((h-1)\times(h-1)\) tridiagonal matrix with

\[
M_{ii}=P(X+i),\qquad
M_{i,i+1}=M_{i+1,i}=(X+i+1)^3.
\tag{7}
\]

At \(X=-1\), this determinant is exactly the standard scaled Apéry determinant:

\[
\boxed{N_h(-1)=((h-1)!)^3 b_{h-1}.}
\tag{8}
\]

At \(X=-2\), the first off-diagonal link is zero. The determinant splits into the one-by-one left block \(P(-1)=-5\) and the standard positive-index block of size \(h-2\), giving

\[
\boxed{N_h(-2)=-5((h-2)!)^3 b_{h-2}.}
\tag{9}
\]

More generally, the same split and the exact symmetry \(P(-x-1)=-P(x)\) give

\[
N_h(-j)=(-1)^{j-1}((j-1)!(h-j)!)^3b_{j-1}b_{h-j}
\qquad(1\le j\le h),
\tag{10}
\]

but only \(j=1,2\) are needed.

Now let \(p>5\) and \(h<p\). If \(N_h(X)\) were identically zero in \(\mathbf F_p[X]\), then (8), because \((h-1)!\) is invertible modulo \(p\), would give

\[
p\mid b_{h-1}.
\]

Equation (9), because also \(5(h-2)!\) is invertible, would give

\[
p\mid b_{h-2}.
\]

These are consecutive zeros, impossible by (2). Therefore

\[
\boxed{N_h(X)\not\equiv0\pmod p\quad\text{for every }p>5,\ h<p.}
\tag{11}
\]

This removes the main hidden failure mode in the polynomial-root argument.

# 4. Degree of the continuant

In characteristic zero,

\[
\boxed{\deg N_h=3(h-1).}
\tag{12}
\]

To see that there is no leading cancellation, let \(L_h\) be the leading coefficient. Then

\[
L_1=1,\qquad L_2=34,\qquad L_{h+1}=34L_h-L_{h-1}.
\tag{13}
\]

Since \(L_2>L_1>0\), induction gives \(L_{h+1}>33L_h>0\). Hence the degree in (12) is exact.

Combining (11) and (12): for \(p>5\), \(h<p\), the congruence

\[
N_h(x)\equiv0\pmod p
\]

has at most \(3(h-1)\) residues \(x\pmod p\).

# 5. Pairwise carrier between common-target occurrences

Let \(S\) be the set of \(d\)-values coming from actual plus common targets in a fixed shell interval. For each \(d\in S\), (1) gives

\[
p\mid b_{a(d)},\qquad a(d)=6d-z-1.
\tag{14}
\]

Take two distinct common-target occurrences \(d,d+k\in S\), \(k>0\). Their lower-zero indices differ by exactly \(6k\):

\[
a(d+k)=a(d)+6k.
\]

Provided \(6k<p\), all indices are legal, and (5) gives the exact pair carrier

\[
\boxed{p\mid N_{6k}(6d-z-1).}
\tag{15}
\]

For fixed \(k\), define

\[
F_k(D)=N_{6k}(6D-z-1).
\tag{16}
\]

Because \(p>5\), the affine substitution \(X\mapsto6D-z-1\) is an automorphism of \(\mathbf F_p[D]\). By (11), if \(6k<p\), \(F_k\) is nonzero modulo \(p\). By (12),

\[
\deg F_k=3(6k-1)=18k-3.
\tag{17}
\]

Therefore the number of ordered starting positions \(d\) for which both \(d\) and \(d+k\) are common-target occurrences is bounded by

\[
\boxed{E_k:=\#\{d\in S:d+k\in S\}\le18k-3.}
\tag{18}
\]

This is occurrence-preserving: for fixed \(k\), every actual target pair contributes its unique starting \(d\), and distinct legal \(d\)'s are distinct modulo \(p\) because the full legal plus range itself has fewer than \(p\) possible \(d\)-values.

Crucially, (15) is not the product of the two unary carriers associated with one target. It is a new same-prime constraint produced by *two different common-target occurrences*.

# 6. Exact common-target multiplicity bound

Let the shell permit \(N\) consecutive integer values of \(d\) (or take the convex integer hull of the permitted values; dropping holes only enlarges). Put

\[
m=|S|.
\]

Choose any integer \(K\ge1\) with

\[
6K<p.
\tag{19}
\]

Summing (18),

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{K}E_k
 \le\sum_{k=1}^{K}(18k-3)
 =9K^2+6K.
\tag{20}
\]

Partition the \(N\) candidate \(d\)-positions into

\[
B=\left\lceil\frac{N}{K+1}\right\rceil
\]

blocks of length at most \(K+1\). If block \(j\) contains \(m_j\) selected positions, every unordered pair inside that block has distance between \(1\) and \(K\), so by (20)

\[
\sum_j\binom{m_j}{2}\le9K^2+6K.
\tag{21}
\]

Cauchy gives

\[
\sum_j\binom{m_j}{2}
=\frac12\left(\sum_jm_j^2-m\right)
\ge\frac12\left(\frac{m^2}{B}-m\right).
\tag{22}
\]

Hence

\[
m^2-Bm-2B(9K^2+6K)\le0,
\]

and therefore the exact bound is

\[
\boxed{
 m\le
 \frac{B+\sqrt{B^2+8B(9K^2+6K)}}{2},
 \qquad
 B=\left\lceil\frac{N}{K+1}\right\rceil .
}
\tag{23}
\]

A slightly looser but handier form is

\[
\boxed{m\le B+\sqrt{2B(9K^2+6K)}.}
\tag{24}
\]

Now take

\[
K=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor.
\]

For \(N\ge8\), if \(6K<p\), then \(K\ge2\),

\[
B\le N^{2/3}+1\le\frac54N^{2/3},
\]

and

\[
9K^2+6K\le12N^{2/3}.
\]

Substitution into (24) gives

\[
m<\left(\frac54+\sqrt{30}\right)N^{2/3}<7N^{2/3}.
\tag{25}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{m\le7N^{2/3}.}
\tag{26}
\]

For the whole legal plus range, \(0\le a=6d-z-1\le p-1\), so there are at most

\[
N\le\left\lceil\frac p6\right\rceil
\]

possible integers \(d\). For \(p\ge43\), the choice \(K=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\) automatically satisfies \(6K<p\). Hence

\[
\boxed{m\le7\left\lceil\frac p6\right\rceil^{2/3}.}
\tag{27}
\]

Any dyadic/macroscopic shell interval gives the sharper version with its own candidate count \(N\), and every extra actualness condition in (1) only decreases \(m\).

# 7. Fixed two-centre count

Equation (6) and the nonzero-polynomial lemma also give an independent exact centre-pair bound. Since \(6t<p\),

\[
N_{6t}(X)\not\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

and

\[
\deg N_{6t}=18t-3.
\]

Therefore, for fixed \((p,t)\),

\[
\boxed{
\#\{z\pmod p:p\mid b_z,\ p\mid b_{z+6t}\}\le18t-3,
}
\tag{28}
\]

where the left side is restricted to legal \(z,z+6t<p\). This is a genuine two-centre arithmetic carrier and can be multiplied into a later global incidence count without touching the upper primes.

# 8. Why the shifted upper pair does not presently give another continuant

After exact reflection, the two upper conditions are

\[
q=p+d\mid b_w,
\qquad
q+t=p+d+t\mid b_{w+13t},
\qquad
w=z+7d.
\tag{29}
\]

The gap-continuant implication (5) needs two zeros of the same recurrence solution modulo the **same prime**. Here one has

\[
b_w\equiv0\pmod q,
\qquad
b_{w+13t}\equiv0\pmod{q+t},
\]

not two zeros modulo \(q\), and not two zeros modulo \(q+t\). Thus neither

\[
q\mid N_{13t}(w)
\]

nor

\[
q+t\mid N_{13t}(w)
\]

follows. Likewise, a characteristic-zero gcd

\[
\gcd(b_w,b_{w+13t})
\]

is not forced to contain either upper prime: \(q\) is known to divide only the first entry and \(q+t\) only the second. Lucas congruences and Apéry reflection are also single-modulus statements, so they do not repair this mismatch; reflection is already what produced (29).

This is the first exact obstruction to a non-unary joint \((q,q+t)\) carrier from the currently allowed toolkit. It is not an obstruction to the common-target bound, because all common targets share the lower modulus \(p\), and pairwise target gaps restore the same-modulus situation in (15).

# 9. Scope: plus-only versus other sign sectors

The continuant lemmas (4)--(12) are sign-free statements about Apéry zeros. The **application** (1), (14)--(27) is plus-only because it uses the exact plus inverse

\[
a=6d-z-1,
\]

so two target occurrences whose \(d\)-parameters differ by \(k\) have lower-zero indices differing by exactly \(6k\), and because the second centre gives the exact upper shift \(13t\) after reflection.

I do **not** assert (26) with the same parameter \(N\), nor the reflected upper normal form (29), for the minus sector or mixed-sign sectors without separately deriving their inverse affine maps. The abstract pair-gap method can be reused there only after that sector-specific derivation.

# 10. Bottom line for the K_{2,m} question

For a plus-oriented two-centre support at one lower prime \(p\):

1. The centre pair itself forces the fixed binary carrier
   \[
   p\mid N_{6t}(z_0).
   \]
2. Any two distinct common-target occurrences at displacement gap \(k\) force
   \[
   p\mid N_{6k}(6d-z_0-1).
   \]
3. The latter polynomial is nonzero modulo \(p\) for \(6k<p\), with degree \(18k-3\).
4. Summing the resulting root bounds over short target gaps and using a block-energy inequality gives the exact estimate (23), hence \(m=O(N^{2/3})\) and globally \(m=O(p^{2/3})\).
5. This bound is fully occurrence-preserving and remains valid after restoring primality, the two shifted upper selectedness conditions, shell restrictions, and deterministic minus-first assignment.
6. No extra joint upper-pair resultant is justified by recurrence/Lucas/reflection/no-consecutive-zero alone, because the upper zero conditions occur modulo \(q\) and \(q+t\), not one common modulus.

So the useful arithmetic conclusion is **positive**: pure graph packing is unnecessary here; the shared lower prime already forbids a dense plus common-target fan at the quantitative scale \(m\gg N^{2/3}\).
