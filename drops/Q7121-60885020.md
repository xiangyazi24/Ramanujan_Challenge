# Exact audit of primitive, fully deflated four-return chains

This audit uses the gap-polynomial recurrence, reflection law, endpoint formula, restart identity, and continuant Bezout identity in `problems/3.2/proof.tex`, together with the aligned-pencil definitions in `problems/3.2/atom_tail_section.tex`, all at commit `6ba7ed0`.

## Verdict

There **is** a clean exact saturation that encodes the absence of intermediate returns. In fact, after adjoining the natural convention \(N_0=0\), the continuant concatenation identity gives an equality of saturated ideals over \(\mathbb Z[x]\): the three local gap equations can be replaced by the three cumulative-return equations \(N_a(x)=N_{a+b}(x)=N_{a+b+c}(x)=0\).

After fully deflating every even gap, the same equality remains true only on the locus where the three reflection-center linear factors are inverted. This is the correct transverse primitive ideal. For actual Apéry-zero chains, deleting that center locus costs at most three selected consecutive quadruples for each prime, because the reflected zero set has at most one adjacent pair of reflection mates.

What does **not** follow is that the elimination ideal, Fitting divisor, or aligned resultant content of this primitive transverse ideal is supported on the existing carrier
\[
 \mathfrak U_H=\prod_{j\le H}j!\,b_j\,V_{j+1}.
\]
The current continuant identities reduce the problem to a genuinely three-way separated common-root problem, but do not make its localized ideal the unit ideal. Proving support on \(\mathfrak U_H\) would require a new integral Bezout certificate. No such certificate is present in the repository, and no valid derivation of one follows from the one-cut continuant identity.

There is also an exact correction to the motivating data: the factor \(75577\) attached to \((a,b,c)=(3,4,3)\) is **entirely a removed reflection-center factor**. It does not survive the requested full deflation and does not divide the fully deflated aligned content.

Thus the fail-closed answer is:

\[
oxed{
egin{array}{l}
	ext{primitive saturation: proved exactly;}\\
	ext{transverse full-deflation reduction: proved exactly;}\\
	ext{support of the resulting eliminant on }\mathfrak U_H:	ext{ open.}
\end{array}}
\]

## 1. Gap polynomials and full center deflation

Write
\[
 P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5
\]
and use the recurrence
\[
 N_{h+1}(x)=P(x+h)N_h(x)-(x+h)^6N_{h-1}(x),\qquad
 N_0=0,\quad N_1=1.
\]
The reflection identity in `proof.tex` is
\[
 N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x).                 	ag{1}
\]
For even \(h\), set
\[
 \lambda_h(x)=2x+h+1,\qquad N_h^\circ(x)=N_h(x)/\lambda_h(x),
\]
and for odd \(h\) put \(\lambda_h=1\) and \(N_h^\circ=N_h\). Gauss's lemma gives \(N_h^\circ\in\mathbb Z[x]\).

A useful consequence, not stated explicitly in the aligned-pencil subsection, is that **every fully deflated polynomial is reflection-even**:
\[
 N_h^\circ(-x-h-1)=N_h^\circ(x).               	ag{2}
\]
Indeed, for odd \(h\), the sign in (1) is \(+1\); for even \(h\), both \(N_h\) and \(\lambda_h\) change sign. Thus \(N_h^\circ\) has even degree and is a polynomial in a quadratic reflection invariant, for example \(x(x+h+1)\) over \(\mathbb Q\).

Deflation and deletion of the center locus are different operations. Replacing \(N_h\) by \(N_h^\circ\) removes the forced simple factor in characteristic zero, but a prime can still make \(N_h^\circ\) vanish at the center. If the intention is to discard every center-mediated return, one must additionally saturate by \(\lambda_h\).

## 2. Exact continuant identities over \(\mathbb Z[x]\)

The addition formula used in the proof of the Bezout proposition yields the following convenient form. For \(u,v\ge1\),
\[
oxed{
 N_{u+v}(x)
 =N_{u+1}(x)N_v(x+u)
 -(x+u+1)^6N_u(x)N_{v-1}(x+u+1).
}                                                   	ag{3}
\]
This is a polynomial identity over \(\mathbb Z[x]\), not merely a pointwise recurrence over a finite field.

The Bezout identity already recorded in `proof.tex`, with
\[
 S_u(x)=\prod_{j=2}^{u}(x+j),
\]
is
\[
oxed{
 N_{u+v-1}(x+1)N_u(x)-N_{u-1}(x+1)N_{u+v}(x)
 =S_u(x)^6N_v(x+u).
}                                                   	ag{4}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 (N_u,N_{u+v}):S_u^\infty
 =(N_u,N_v(x+u)):S_u^\infty.                       	ag{5}
\]

Identity (4) is useful for arbitrary common-root ideals. For primitive chains, however, (3) gives a stronger and cleaner saturation that does not require adjoining the cut-edge product \(S_u\).

## 3. The exact primitive saturation

Let
\[
 s=a+b+c,\qquad a,b,c\ge2,
\]
and define the local and cumulative ideals
\[
 I_{\mathrm{loc}}
 =igl(N_a(x),\,N_b(x+a),\,N_c(x+a+b)igr),
\]
\[
 I_{\mathrm{cum}}
 =igl(N_a(x),\,N_{a+b}(x),\,N_s(x)igr)
 \subset\mathbb Z[x].
\]
To exclude every intermediate return to the starting projective state, put
\[
 Q_{a,b,c}(x)
 =\prod_{\substack{1\le j<s\\j
otin\{a,a+b\}}}N_j(x).       	ag{6}
\]
The factor \(N_1=1\) is harmless.

### Proposition 1: exact primitive ideal identity

One has the equality of ideals in \(\mathbb Z[x]\)
\[
oxed{
 I_{\mathrm{loc}}:Q_{a,b,c}^{\infty}
 =I_{\mathrm{cum}}:Q_{a,b,c}^{\infty}.
}                                                            	ag{7}
\]

### Proof

Apply (3) first with \((u,v)=(a,b)\):
\[
 N_{a+b}
 =N_{a+1}N_b(x+a)
 -(x+a+1)^6N_aN_{b-1}(x+a+1).                 	ag{8}
\]
Hence \(N_{a+b}\in I_{\mathrm{loc}}\). Applying (3) with \((u,v)=(a+b,c)\) then gives \(N_s\in I_{\mathrm{loc}}\). Therefore
\[
 I_{\mathrm{cum}}\subset I_{\mathrm{loc}}.                   	ag{9}
\]

Conversely, (8) gives
\[
 N_{a+1}(x)N_b(x+a)\in I_{\mathrm{cum}}.                       	ag{10}
\]
Because \(b\ge2\), the index \(a+1\) is strictly between \(a\) and \(a+b\), so \(N_{a+1}\) divides \(Q_{a,b,c}\). Thus
\[
 N_b(x+a)\in I_{\mathrm{cum}}:Q_{a,b,c}^{\infty}.              	ag{11}
\]
Likewise,
\[
 N_{a+b+1}(x)N_c(x+a+b)\in I_{\mathrm{cum}},                   	ag{12}
\]
and \(N_{a+b+1}\mid Q_{a,b,c}\) because \(c\ge2\). This puts the third local generator in the same saturation. Together with \(N_a\in I_{\mathrm{cum}}\), this proves (7). \(\square\)

This identity is stronger than merely saying that the two ideals have the same roots away from cut edges: it is an exact equality of saturated ideals over \(\mathbb Z\).

### Fiberwise meaning

For a field \(k\), the geometric roots of
\[
 (I_{\mathrm{cum},k}:Q_k^\infty)
\]
are exactly the common roots of \(N_a,N_{a+b},N_s\) at which every other \(N_j\), \(1\le j<s\), is nonzero. Because the fiber is zero-dimensional, no closure issue remains: saturation removes precisely the roots on \(Q=0\).

Equivalently one may use the Rabinowitsch ideal
\[
 igl(I_{\mathrm{cum}},\,1-uQ_{a,b,c}igr)\subset\mathbb Z[x,u]. 	ag{13}
\]
Over a field, its points are precisely the primitive-return points, with \(u=Q^{-1}\).

One must not silently identify saturation over \(\mathbb Z\) followed by reduction with saturation after reduction. Extra torsion primes can appear. For pointwise finite-field claims, the safe object is the saturated fiber or the fiber of the Rabinowitsch ideal. The discrepancy primes are part of the arithmetic eliminant one is trying to control.

## 4. Exact identity after full deflation

Define
\[
 \Lambda_{a,b,c}(x)
 =\lambda_a(x)\lambda_b(x+a)\lambda_c(x+a+b)
\]
and
\[
 I_{\mathrm{loc}}^\circ
 =igl(N_a^\circ(x),\,N_b^\circ(x+a),\,N_c^\circ(x+a+b)igr).
\]

### Proposition 2: transverse fully deflated identity

There is an exact equality in \(\mathbb Z[x]\):
\[
oxed{
 I_{\mathrm{loc}}^\circ:(Q_{a,b,c}\Lambda_{a,b,c})^\infty
 =I_{\mathrm{cum}}:(Q_{a,b,c}\Lambda_{a,b,c})^\infty.
}                                                            	ag{14}
\]

### Proof

Since \(N_h=\lambda_hN_h^\circ\), equations (8) and its \((a+b,c)\) analogue show
\[
 I_{\mathrm{cum}}\subset I_{\mathrm{loc}}^\circ.               	ag{15}
\]
Conversely,
\[
 \lambda_aN_a^\circ=N_a\in I_{\mathrm{cum}},
\]
\[
 N_{a+1}\lambda_b(x+a)N_b^\circ(x+a)\in I_{\mathrm{cum}},
\]
\[
 N_{a+b+1}\lambda_c(x+a+b)N_c^\circ(x+a+b)\in I_{\mathrm{cum}}.
\]
The two \(N\)-multipliers divide \(Q_{a,b,c}\), and the three linear multipliers divide \(\Lambda_{a,b,c}\). Saturating proves (14). \(\square\)

Equation (14) is the sharp algebraic statement available from the continuant recurrence.

It is important not to erase \(\Lambda\) from (14). Without inverting the center factors, the cumulative equations \(N_a=N_{a+b}=N_s=0\) need not force the deflated cofactors to vanish at a center. Therefore
\[
 I_{\mathrm{loc}}^\circ:Q^\infty
 \stackrel{?}{=}I_{\mathrm{cum}}:Q^\infty
\]
is not justified and is generally the wrong identity.

## 5. Removing all center gaps costs only \(O(1)\) per prime

For an actual four-zero chain in the non-wrapping range, a centered even gap \(h\) beginning at \(r\) satisfies
\[
 2r+h+1\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Because \(0\le r<r+h\le p-1\), this congruence is the integer equality
\[
 r+(r+h)=p-1.                                                 	ag{16}
\]
Thus its two endpoint zeros are reflection mates.

Write the full zero set as
\[
 u_0<u_1<\cdots<u_{Z(p)-1},\qquad
 u_i+u_{Z(p)-1-i}=p-1.
\]
There is at most one adjacent pair \(u_i,u_{i+1}\) satisfying (16), because it requires \(i+1=Z(p)-1-i\). A fixed adjacent pair belongs to at most three consecutive quadruples. Hence discarding every selected quadruple containing **any** centered gap loses at most three quadruples for that prime.

This is a pointwise \(\mathbb F_p\) statement about the ordered Apéry zero set. It is not a polynomial identity over \(\mathbb Z[x]\). It does, however, justify using the transverse ideal in (14) in a corank count, at an additive \(O(1)\) cost per prime.

## 6. What the structural carrier really controls

For \(p>H\) with \(p
mid\mathfrak U_H\), the existing identities give the following exact protections for all indices at most \(H\):

1. **No degree collapse.** The leading coefficient \(V_h\) of \(N_h\) is nonzero modulo \(p\).
2. **No endpoint pollution.** The endpoint formula
   \[
   N_h(-r)=(-1)^{r-1}b_{r-1}b_{h-r}igl((r-1)!(h-r)!igr)^3
   \]
   excludes a root lying on a cut-edge factor \(x+r\).
3. **No adjacent return levels.** The exact adjacent resultant
   \[
   |\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+1})|
   =\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(j!^3b_j)^6
   \]
   is a unit modulo \(p\).
4. **The reductions \(N_h\) and \(N_h^\circ\) are nonzero polynomials.**

These facts make the restart and saturation manipulations pointwise valid away from \(\mathfrak U_H\). They do **not** show that three separated return equations have no common root.

The precise desired support theorem would be the following. Let
\[
 A_H=\mathbb Z[\mathfrak U_H^{-1}]
\]
and form, for example, the transverse primitive algebra
\[
 B_{a,b,c}=A_H[x,u]\Big/igl(
 N_a^\circ(x),\,N_b^\circ(x+a),\,N_c^\circ(x+a+b),
 1-uQ_{a,b,c}(x)\Lambda_{a,b,c}(x)igr).            	ag{17}
\]
Then support on \(\mathfrak U_H\) is equivalent to
\[
oxed{B_{a,b,c}=0.}                                          	ag{18}
\]
Equivalently, after clearing denominators, one needs an identity
\[
oxed{
 \mathfrak U_H^M
 =A\,N_a^\circ(x)+B\,N_b^\circ(x+a)+C\,N_c^\circ(x+a+b)
  +Digl(1-uQ_{a,b,c}\Lambda_{a,b,c}igr)
}                                                            	ag{19}
\]
for some \(M\ge0\) and \(A,B,C,D\in\mathbb Z[x,u]\).

Identity (19), or a triple-specific version whose right-hand integer has prime support contained in \(\mathfrak U_H\), is the exact missing algebraic certificate. Equations (3), (4), (7), and (14) transform the generators but do not produce the unit on the left of (19).

A determinantal formulation is also possible: after localizing the leading coefficient, the algebra in (17) is finite over \(A_H\), and its zeroth Fitting ideal is generated by maximal minors of a Macaulay presentation. Support on \(\mathfrak U_H\) says this Fitting ideal is the unit ideal. Merely observing that one particular resultant has small content does not establish this.

## 7. Why the present continuant algebra stops short

The Bezout identity reduces a nested pair once:
\[
 (N_d,N_e):S_d^\infty
 =(N_d,N_{e-d}(x+d)):S_d^\infty.
\]
As `proof.tex` explicitly notes, the subtraction does not iterate into a product formula: after one cut, the two continuants occupy separated shifted intervals with opposite orientations. The remaining separated resultant
\[
 \mathcal R_{d,r}=\operatorname{Res}_x(N_d(x),N_r(x+d))
\]
is genuinely new.

Primitive saturation does not change that fact. It deletes roots having extra returns, but it supplies no Bezout combination that kills a primitive separated root.

There is an exact small obstruction to any argument claiming that full deflation plus primitivity already forces structural support at the pair level. At \(p=71\), with \(x=-5/2\),
\[
 N_2^\circ(x)=N_2^\circ(x+2)=0,\qquad N_3(x)
e0\pmod{71}.
\]
Both center factors are units at these two points, so this is a fully deflated, primitive three-return configuration at relative times \(0,2,4\). Moreover \(71
mid\mathfrak U_4\). This does not itself give a four-return counterexample, but it rigorously rules out an induction that would prove the four-return claim by asserting that each primitive separated pair is structural.

The repository also contains exact examples showing why an integer eliminant is stronger than the finite-field chain statement one ultimately needs:

- at \(p=4787\), a separated pair has an irreducible quadratic common factor but no \(\mathbb F_p\)-root;
- at primes where leading coefficients vanish, a resultant can be divisible by \(p\) solely through a common point at infinity.

The \(V_j\)-part of \(\mathfrak U_H\) removes the latter outside the exceptional set, but a nonsplit affine common factor remains visible to an eliminant even though it yields no \(\mathbb F_p\) chain.

Thus even radical support of the full algebraic eliminant on \(\mathfrak U_H\) is a stronger theorem than exclusion of actual primitive Apéry-zero chains.

## 9. Polynomial identities versus pointwise Apéry-zero statements

Three levels must remain separate.

### A. Identities over \(\mathbb Z[x]\)

Equations (1), (3), (4), (7), and (14) are polynomial or ideal identities over the integers. They survive every specialization, subject only to the usual meaning of saturation.

### B. Pointwise statements over \(\mathbb F_p\)

At a point \(\alpha\), deriving
\[
 N_b(\alpha+a)=0
\]
from \(N_a(\alpha)=N_{a+b}(\alpha)=0\) uses division by \(N_{a+1}(\alpha)\), or equivalently the condition \(Q(\alpha)
e0\). Deriving a deflated equation additionally uses the appropriate center factor as a unit. These are pointwise hypotheses, not consequences of a formal quotient unless the stated saturation has been imposed.

For actual zero indices one must also require the non-wrapping range and invertibility of the recurrence denominators. The equations \(N_h(r)=0\) by themselves encode a collision of the projective Apéry orbit,
\[
 \pi_r=\pi_{r+h},
\]
not the assertion \(b_r=b_{r+h}=0\). If \(b_r=0\), then the collision returns to the zero line and produces another zero; without \(b_r=0\), it is only a projective return. The fixed polynomial ideal in the symbolic variable \(x\) necessarily includes these nonzero-fiber projective cycles.

### C. Resultant/content statements

A common \(\mathbb F_p\)-root gives a \(p\)-adic valuation lower bound for the aligned content, as proved in `atom_tail_section.tex`. The converse detects common factors over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\), not necessarily roots in \(\mathbb F_p\), and must also account for leading-coefficient drops. Saturation by \(Q\) is not implemented by simply dividing an ordinary resultant by the resultants with the factors of \(Q\); a Rabinowitsch or Fitting/Macaulay construction is required.

Consequently, the statement
\[
 p\mid E^{\mathrm{prim}}_{a,b,c}\Longrightarrow p\mid\mathfrak U_H
\]
for an integer eliminant \(E^{\mathrm{prim}}\) is stronger than the pointwise theorem needed for selected Apéry-zero quadruples.

## 10. Precise surviving gap

The strongest exact reduction now available is
\[
oxed{
 igl(N_a^\circ(x),N_b^\circ(x+a),N_c^\circ(x+a+b)igr)
 :(Q\Lambda)^\infty
 =igl(N_a(x),N_{a+b}(x),N_s(x)igr):(Q\Lambda)^\infty.
}                                                              	ag{26}
\]

To finish the proposed route, one must prove one of the following genuinely new statements.

### Strong algebraic form

For every \(a+b+c\le H\),
\[
 1\in
 igl(N_a^\circ,N_b^\circ(x+a),N_c^\circ(x+a+b),1-uQ\Lambdaigr)
 \otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z[\mathfrak U_H^{-1}].              	ag{27}
\]
Equivalently, produce the integral certificate (19). This proves support of every algebraic eliminant/Fitting divisor on \(\mathfrak U_H\).

### Weaker counting-relevant form

For \(p>H\), \(p
mid\mathfrak U_H\), and an actual Apéry-zero start \(r\) in the non-wrapping range, there is no \(\mathbb F_p\)-point of the transverse primitive ideal at \(x=r\). This may hold even if (27) fails because of nonsplit common factors or projective-return roots with \(b_r
e0\).

Neither statement follows from the identities currently proved. The exact obstruction is no longer ambiguity about how to encode primitivity: equations (7), (13), and (26) solve that part. The remaining problem is a **primitive three-way separated-return theorem after localization at \(\mathfrak U_H\)**.

That is the precise surviving gap.
