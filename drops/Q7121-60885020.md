## Verdict

There is a clean exact saturation that encodes the absence of intermediate returns. After adjoining the natural convention `N_0 = 0`, the continuant concatenation formula proves an equality of saturated ideals over `ℤ[x]`: the three local gap equations can be replaced by the three cumulative-return equations `N_a(x) = N_{a+b}(x) = N_{a+b+c}(x) = 0`.

After fully deflating every even gap, the same equality remains true on the locus where the three reflection-center linear factors are inverted. This is the correct transverse primitive ideal. For actual Apéry-zero chains, deleting that center locus costs at most three selected consecutive quadruples per prime, because the reflected zero set has at most one adjacent pair of reflection mates.

What does not follow is that the elimination ideal, Fitting divisor, or aligned resultant content of this primitive transverse ideal is supported on the existing carrier

```text
𝔘_H = product_{j <= H} j! b_j V_{j+1}.
```

The current continuant identities reduce the problem to a genuinely three-way separated common-root problem, but do not make its localization the unit ideal. No such certificate is present in the repository, and no valid derivation of one follows from the one-cut continuant identity.

There is also an exact correction to the motivating data: the factor `75577` attached to `(a,b,c) = (3,4,3)` is entirely a removed reflection-center factor. It does not survive the requested full deflation and does not divide the fully deflated aligned content.

## 1. Gap polynomials and full center deflation

Write

```text
P(n) = 34 n^3 + 51 n^2 + 27 n + 5,
N_{h+1}(x) = P(x+h) N_h(x) - (x+h)^6 N_{h-1}(x),
N_0 = 0,  N_1 = 1.
```

The reflection identity in `proof.tex` is

```text
N_h(-x-h-1) = (-1)^(h-1) N_h(x).                 (1)
```

For even `h`, set

```text
lambda_h(x) = 2x + h + 1,
N_h°(x) = N_h(x) / lambda_h(x),
```

and for odd `h` put `lambda_h = 1` and `N_h° = N_h`. Gauss's lemma gives `N_h°` in `ℤ[x]`.

A useful exact consequence is that every fully deflated polynomial is reflection-even:

```text
N_h°(-x-h-1) = N_h°(x).                           (2)
```

For odd `h`, the sign in (1) is positive. For even `h`, both `N_h` and `lambda_h` change sign, so their quotient is invariant. Hence `N_h°` has even degree and is a polynomial over `ℚ` in a quadratic reflection invariant such as `x(x+h+1)`.

Deflation and deletion of the center locus are different operations. Replacing `N_h` by `N_h°` removes the forced characteristic-zero linear factor, but a prime can still make `N_h°` vanish at the center. To discard every center-mediated return one must additionally saturate by `lambda_h`.

## 2. Exact continuant identities over ℤ[x]

The addition formula used in the proof of the repository's Bezout proposition yields, for `u,v >= 1`,

```text
N_{u+v}(x)
 = N_{u+1}(x) N_v(x+u)
   - (x+u+1)^6 N_u(x) N_{v-1}(x+u+1).             (3)
```

This is a polynomial identity over `ℤ[x]`, not merely a pointwise recurrence over a finite field.

The Bezout identity already recorded in `proof.tex`, with

```text
S_u(x) = product_{j=2}^u (x+j),
```

is

```text
N_{u+v-1}(x+1) N_u(x) - N_{u-1}(x+1) N_{u+v}(x)
 = S_u(x)^6 N_v(x+u).                              (4)
```

Consequently,

```text
(N_u, N_{u+v}) : S_u^∞
 = (N_u, N_v(x+u)) : S_u^∞.                        (5)
```

Identity (4) is useful for arbitrary common-root ideals. For primitive chains, identity (3) gives a cleaner saturation that does not require adjoining the cut-edge product `S_u`.

## 3. Exact primitive saturation

Let

```text
s = a+b+c,  with a,b,c >= 2.
```

Define the local and cumulative ideals

```text
I_loc  = (N_a(x), N_b(x+a), N_c(x+a+b)),
I_cum  = (N_a(x), N_{a+b}(x), N_s(x))
```

inside `ℤ[x]`. To exclude every intermediate return to the starting projective state, put

```text
Q_{a,b,c}(x)
 = product of N_j(x) over 1 <= j < s and j not in {a,a+b}.     (6)
```

The factor `N_1 = 1` is harmless.

### Proposition 1: exact primitive ideal identity

```text
I_loc : Q_{a,b,c}^∞ = I_cum : Q_{a,b,c}^∞.                    (7)
```

### Proof

Apply (3) first with `(u,v) = (a,b)`:

```text
N_{a+b}
 = N_{a+1} N_b(x+a)
   - (x+a+1)^6 N_a N_{b-1}(x+a+1).              (8)
```

Thus `N_{a+b}` belongs to `I_loc`. Applying (3) with `(u,v) = (a+b,c)` then gives `N_s` in `I_loc`, so

```text
I_cum is contained in I_loc.                                    (9)
```

Conversely, (8) gives

```text
N_{a+1}(x) N_b(x+a) belongs to I_cum.                           (10)
```

Because `b >= 2`, the index `a+1` lies strictly between `a` and `a+b`; hence `N_{a+1}` divides `Q_{a,b,c}`. Therefore

```text
N_b(x+a) belongs to I_cum : Q_{a,b,c}^∞.                        (11)
```

Likewise,

```text
N_{a+b+1}(x) N_c(x+a+b) belongs to I_cum,                       (12)
```

and `N_{a+b+1}` divides `Q_{a,b,c}` because `c >= 2`. Together with `N_a` in `I_cum`, this proves (7).

This is an equality of saturated ideals over the integers, not only an equality of root sets away from cut edges.

### Fiberwise meaning

For a field `k`, the geometric roots of

```text
I_cum,k : Q_k^∞
```

are exactly the common roots of `N_a`, `N_{a+b}`, and `N_s` at which every other `N_j`, for `1 <= j < s`, is nonzero. Since each relevant fiber is zero-dimensional, saturation removes precisely the roots on `Q = 0`.

Equivalently, use the Rabinowitsch ideal

```text
(I_cum, 1-u Q_{a,b,c}) in ℤ[x,u].                              (13)
```

Over a field its points are exactly the primitive-return points, with `u = Q^{-1}`.

One must not silently identify saturation over `ℤ` followed by reduction with saturation after reduction. Extra torsion primes can appear. For pointwise finite-field claims, the safe object is the saturated fiber or the fiber of the Rabinowitsch ideal. The discrepancy primes are part of the arithmetic eliminant that must be controlled.

## 4. Exact identity after full deflation

Define

```text
Lambda_{a,b,c}(x)
 = lambda_a(x) lambda_b(x+a) lambda_c(x+a+b)
```

and

```text
I_loc° = (N_a°(x), N_b°(x+a), N_c°(x+a+b)).
```

### Proposition 2: transverse fully deflated identity

```text
I_loc° : (Q_{a,b,c} Lambda_{a,b,c})^∞
 = I_cum : (Q_{a,b,c} Lambda_{a,b,c})^∞.                       (14)
```

### Proof

Because `N_h = lambda_h N_h°`, equations (8) and its `(a+b,c)` analogue imply

```text
I_cum is contained in I_loc°.                                  (15)
```

Conversely,

```text
lambda_a N_a° = N_a belongs to I_cum,
N_{a+1} lambda_b(x+a) N_b°(x+a) belongs to I_cum,
N_{a+b+1} lambda_c(x+a+b) N_c°(x+a+b) belongs to I_cum.
```

The two `N` multipliers divide `Q_{a,b,c}`, and the three linear multipliers divide `Lambda_{a,b,c}`. Saturating proves (14).

Equation (14) is the sharp algebraic statement supplied by the continuant recurrence.

It is important not to erase `Lambda` from (14). Without inverting the center factors, the cumulative equations `N_a = N_{a+b} = N_s = 0` need not force the deflated cofactors to vanish at a center. Therefore

```text
I_loc° : Q^∞  ?=  I_cum : Q^∞
```

is not justified and is generally the wrong identity.

## 5. Removing all center gaps costs only O(1) per prime

For an actual four-zero chain in the non-wrapping range, a centered even gap `h` beginning at `r` satisfies

```text
2r + h + 1 = 0 mod p.
```

Because `0 <= r < r+h <= p-1`, this congruence is the integer equality

```text
r + (r+h) = p-1.                                               (16)
```

Thus its endpoint zeros are reflection mates.

Write the full zero set as

```text
u_0 < u_1 < ... < u_{Z(p)-1},
u_i + u_{Z(p)-1-i} = p-1.
```

There is at most one adjacent pair `u_i,u_{i+1}` satisfying (16), since it requires `i+1 = Z(p)-1-i`. A fixed adjacent pair belongs to at most three consecutive quadruples. Hence discarding every selected quadruple containing any centered gap loses at most three quadruples per prime.

This is a pointwise statement over `F_p` about the ordered Apéry zero set. It is not a polynomial identity over `ℤ[x]`. It nevertheless justifies using the transverse ideal in (14) in a corank count, at an additive `O(1)` cost per prime.

## 6. What the structural carrier actually controls

For `p > H` with `p ∤ 𝔘_H`, the repository's identities give the following exact protections for all indices at most `H`.

1. **No degree collapse.** The leading coefficient `V_h` of `N_h` is nonzero modulo `p`.
2. **No endpoint pollution.** The endpoint formula

   ```text
   N_h(-r)
    = (-1)^(r-1) b_{r-1} b_{h-r} ((r-1)! (h-r)!)^3
   ```

   excludes a root on a cut-edge factor `x+r`.
3. **No adjacent return levels.** The exact adjacent resultant is

   ```text
   |Res(N_h,N_{h+1})|
    = product_{j=1}^{h-1} (j!^3 b_j)^6,
   ```

   and is a unit modulo `p`.
4. **The reductions of `N_h` and `N_h°` are nonzero polynomials.**

These facts make the restart and saturation manipulations pointwise valid away from `𝔘_H`. They do not show that three separated return equations have no common root.

The precise desired support theorem can be stated as follows. Let

```text
A_H = ℤ[1/𝔘_H]
```

and form the transverse primitive algebra

```text
B_{a,b,c} = A_H[x,u] /
  (N_a°(x), N_b°(x+a), N_c°(x+a+b),
   1-u Q_{a,b,c}(x) Lambda_{a,b,c}(x)).                       (17)
```

Support on `𝔘_H` is equivalent to

```text
B_{a,b,c} = 0.                                                (18)
```

Equivalently, after clearing denominators, one needs an identity

```text
𝔘_H^M
 = A N_a°(x) + B N_b°(x+a) + C N_c°(x+a+b)
   + D (1-u Q_{a,b,c} Lambda_{a,b,c})                         (19)
```

for some `M >= 0` and `A,B,C,D` in `ℤ[x,u]`.

Identity (19), or a triple-specific version whose integer left side has prime support contained in `𝔘_H`, is the exact missing certificate. Equations (3), (4), (7), and (14) transform the generators but do not produce the unit in (19).

A determinantal formulation is also possible. After localizing the leading coefficient, the algebra in (17) is finite over `A_H`, and its zeroth Fitting ideal is generated by maximal minors of a Macaulay presentation. Support on `𝔘_H` says this Fitting ideal is the unit ideal. Small observed content of one ordinary resultant does not establish this.

## 7. Why the present continuant algebra stops short

The Bezout identity reduces a nested pair once:

```text
(N_d,N_e) : S_d^∞
 = (N_d,N_{e-d}(x+d)) : S_d^∞.
```

As `proof.tex` explicitly notes, the subtraction does not iterate into a product formula. After one cut, the two continuants occupy separated shifted intervals with opposite orientations. The remaining separated resultant

```text
R_{d,r} = Res_x(N_d(x), N_r(x+d))
```

is genuinely new.

Primitive saturation does not change that fact. It deletes roots having extra returns, but supplies no Bezout combination that kills a primitive separated root.

There is an exact small obstruction to any claim that full deflation plus primitivity already forces structural support at the pair level. At `p = 71`, with `x = -5/2`,

```text
N_2°(x) = N_2°(x+2) = 0,
N_3(x) != 0 mod 71.
```

Both center factors are units at these two points. This is a fully deflated primitive three-return configuration at relative times `0,2,4`, and `71 ∤ 𝔘_4`. It is not itself a four-return counterexample, but it rigorously rules out an induction that tries to prove the four-return assertion by claiming every primitive separated pair is structural.

The repository also contains exact examples explaining why an integer eliminant is stronger than the finite-field chain statement ultimately needed:

- at `p = 4787`, a separated pair has an irreducible quadratic common factor but no `F_p` root;
- when leading coefficients vanish, a resultant can be divisible by `p` solely through a common point at infinity.

The `V_j` part of `𝔘_H` removes the latter outside the exceptional set, but a nonsplit affine common factor remains visible to an eliminant even when it gives no `F_p` chain.

Thus even radical support of the full algebraic eliminant on `𝔘_H` is stronger than exclusion of actual primitive Apéry-zero chains.

## 9. Polynomial identities versus pointwise Apéry-zero claims

Three levels must remain separate.

### A. Identities over ℤ[x]

Equations (1), (3), (4), (7), and (14) are polynomial or ideal identities over the integers. They survive specialization, subject to the stated meaning of saturation.

### B. Pointwise statements over F_p

At a point `alpha`, deriving `N_b(alpha+a) = 0` from `N_a(alpha) = N_{a+b}(alpha) = 0` uses division by `N_{a+1}(alpha)`, equivalently the condition `Q(alpha) != 0`. Deriving a deflated equation additionally uses the appropriate center factor as a unit. These are pointwise hypotheses, not consequences of a formal quotient unless the stated saturation has been imposed.

For actual zero indices one must also require the non-wrapping range and invertibility of recurrence denominators. The equations `N_h(r) = 0` by themselves encode a collision of the projective Apéry orbit,

```text
pi_r = pi_{r+h},
```

not the assertion `b_r = b_{r+h} = 0`. When `b_r = 0`, the collision returns to the zero line and produces another zero. Without `b_r = 0`, it is only a projective return. A fixed polynomial ideal in the symbolic variable `x` necessarily includes such nonzero-fiber projective cycles.

### C. Resultant and content statements

A common `F_p` root gives a `p`-adic valuation lower bound for the aligned content, as proved in `atom_tail_section.tex`. The converse detects common factors over the algebraic closure of `F_p`, not necessarily roots in `F_p`, and must account for leading-coefficient drops. Saturation by `Q` is not implemented by merely dividing an ordinary resultant by resultants involving factors of `Q`; a Rabinowitsch or Fitting/Macaulay construction is required.

Consequently, support of a primitive integer eliminant on `𝔘_H` is stronger than the pointwise theorem needed for selected Apéry-zero quadruples.
