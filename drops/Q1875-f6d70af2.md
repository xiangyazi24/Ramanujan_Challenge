# Jordan audit: explicit odd preimage of `a` and Fredholm obstruction

## Result

The claimed Jordan extension does not produce the Apéry reciprocal sum `S`. The first Jordan equation

\[
Hx=a=\delta_1-\delta_{-1}
\]

is solvable in the odd subspace and has an explicit solution, but the next Fredholm obstruction is a pairing with the left kernel and is a boundary-normalization quantity, not `-\sum 1/k_t`.

## 1. Explicit odd solution of `Hx=a`

Write the centered recurrence in flux form. For the odd solution `x_{-t}=-x_t` choose the gauge `x_0=0`. Since the kernel vector satisfies

\[
 k_t=\frac{u_t}{t^2-1},\qquad Hk=0,
\]

the first-order reduction gives

\[
 k_t(x_{t+1}-x_t)-k_{t-1}(x_t-x_{t-1})=1
\]

for the positive half-chain. Summing from `1` to `t-1` gives

\[
 k_{t-1}(x_t-x_{t-1})=t-1+C.
\]

Oddness and the source normalization at `t=1` fix `C=0`, hence

\[
 x_t=\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\frac{j}{k_j},\qquad t\ge 1,
\]

and extend by odd symmetry.

Direct substitution gives

\[
Hx=\delta_1-\delta_{-1}.
\]

This is the unique odd solution after fixing the additive multiple of the odd kernel vector `a` itself.

## 2. Fredholm obstruction for the next equation

The equation

\[
Hy=x
\]

requires orthogonality to every left-kernel vector. With the repository left kernels

\[
\ell^{+}_t=t u_t \;(t>0),\qquad \ell^-_t=t u_t\;(t<0),
\]

one obtains

\[
\langle \ell^+,x\rangle
 =\sum_{t=1}^{m}t u_t x_t
 =\sum_{t=1}^{m}t(t^2-1)k_t
   \sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\frac{j}{k_j}.
\]

This is the exact obstruction. It is not

\[
-\sum_{t=2}^m\frac1{k_t}.
\]

The difference is structural: the obstruction is a weighted double sum involving `k_t/k_j`, while `S` is a single reciprocal sum.

## 3. Why it cannot encode `S`

Using summation by parts, the obstruction reduces to boundary terms plus weighted moments of `k`, not to the reciprocal trace:

\[
\sum_t \ell_t x_t
=\sum_j \frac{j}{k_j}\sum_{t>j}\ell_t .
\]

The inner partial sums are sums of `t u_t`, whereas `S` contains only `1/k_j`. There is no identity from `Hk=0` converting these quantities: the required conversion would need an additional relation involving the reciprocal sequence `1/k_j`, which is not supplied by the kernel equation.

Therefore the proposed Jordan proof route is invalid unless an independent identity is proved.

The comparison is not an equality test that fails because of nonlinear trace behavior; it fails because the two expressions are different Fredholm invariants. The Jordan obstruction is a left-kernel pairing of a particular preimage, whereas `S` is the reciprocal diagonal sum.

## Conclusion

`Hx=a` has an explicit odd solution

\[
\boxed{x_t=\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}j/k_j}
\]

(up to the expected homogeneous freedom), but the obstruction to solving `Hy=x` is

\[
\boxed{\langle\ell,x\rangle
=\sum_t t u_t\sum_{j<t}j/k_j}
\]

and it does not equal or encode `S=-\sum 1/k_t` from the available kernel identities. The proposed Jordan-chain derivation of `S` is therefore a false proof step.
