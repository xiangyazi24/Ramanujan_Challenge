# Inductive irreducibility of the Apéry gap-polynomial cores

## Executive verdict

The three-term recurrence does **not** support an induction of the form

\[
\bigl(C_k\text{ irreducible and }C_{k-2}\text{ irreducible}\bigr)
\Longrightarrow C_{k+2}\text{ irreducible},
\]

where \(C_k\) denotes the relevant odd or even centered core. Coprimality of the previous terms does not repair this: a three-term continuant recurrence controls common factors **between different levels**, but it does not control internal factors of the newly formed polynomial.

The most promising genuinely inductive route is instead:

> **Induct on explicit coefficient congruences and \(p\)-adic Newton-polygon data, then use a Schur–Filaseta factor-degree exclusion argument.**

In other words, the induction invariant should not be “the previous core is irreducible.” It should be a uniform local statement strong enough to rule out every possible proper factor degree at the current level.

My ranking is:

| Route | Assessment | What is missing |
|---|---:|---|
| Coefficient congruences + several Newton polygons | **Best prospect** | A basis in which the recurrence yields a provable valuation pattern of growing length |
| Explicit modular factor-degree patterns at two or more primes | **Second** | A uniform rule producing the primes and patterns from the level \(k\) |
| Identification with a known Schur/Laguerre/Bessel-type family | **High reward, uncertain** | No such identification is presently known; the repository’s bounded bispectral scan is negative |
| Direct induction from irreducibility/coprimality | **No-go as stated** | No factor of \(C_{k+2}\) is forced to descend to \(C_k\) or \(C_{k-2}\) |
| Induction on \(S_n\) Galois groups | **No natural tower** | The splitting fields at consecutive levels are not nested and no root correspondence is known |
| Discriminant/resultant recurrence | **Auxiliary only** | Discriminants detect separability, not transitivity; the derivative recurrence does not close |

There is also an important scope correction. On the current repository branch, all shifted resultants used in the P3.2 “First Lemma” are already nonzero by the complex root-strip theorem in `atom_tail_section.tex` and `meso_result.tex`. Thus all-level core irreducibility is an attractive independent theorem, and may support other arguments, but it is not needed for the existing shifted-resultant nonvanishing.

## 2. Why irreducibility cannot be propagated from the preceding terms alone

### 2.1 A universal counterexample

Even for an ordinary continuant recurrence, consecutive irreducibility and coprimality do not force the next term to be irreducible. Consider the Fibonacci polynomials

\[
G_0=0,\qquad G_1=1,\qquad G_{n+1}=UG_n+G_{n-1}.
\]

Then

\[
G_2=U,\qquad G_3=U^2+1
\]

are irreducible and coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), but

\[
G_4=U^3+2U=U(U^2+2)
\]

is reducible.

Thus no theorem based only on

```text
three-term recurrence + previous irreducibility + previous coprimality
```

can be valid.

### 2.2 What a hypothetical new factor actually implies

Suppose abstractly that

\[
C_{n+1}=A_nC_n-B_nC_{n-1}
\]

and that an irreducible polynomial \(g\) divides \(C_{n+1}\). In the field

\[
K=\mathbb Q[U]/(g)
\]

one gets only

\[
A_nC_n=B_nC_{n-1}.
\tag{2}
\]

If \(g\) is coprime to \(A_nB_nC_nC_{n-1}\), equation (2) says that two nonzero elements of \(K\) have a prescribed ratio. That is entirely consistent. It does not imply that \(g\) divides \(C_n\) or \(C_{n-1}\).

Coprimality is actually what lets one divide in \(K\); it does not create a contradiction.

### 2.3 What the recurrence *does* propagate

Modulo \(C_n\), one has

\[
C_{n+1}\equiv-B_nC_{n-1}\pmod{C_n}.
\]

Consequently, up to the standard leading-coefficient factors,

\[
\operatorname{Res}(C_n,C_{n+1})
=\operatorname{Res}(C_n,B_n)
 \operatorname{Res}(C_n,C_{n-1}).
\tag{3}
\]

This is why adjacent resultants and gcd statements admit beautiful recurrences. The repository’s exact formula

\[
\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_{h+1})
=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(-h)^6
 \operatorname{Res}(N_{h-1},N_h)
\]

is a particularly strong example.

But (3) says nothing about a factorization

\[
C_{n+1}=UV
\]

internal to the new term. This is the precise gap between coprimality and irreducibility.

### 2.4 What a successful direct induction would have to add

A real direct induction would need a new theorem of one of the following forms:

1. every proper factor of \(C_{k+2}\) induces a proper factor of \(C_k\) under a canonical norm or specialization;
2. the splitting field of \(C_k\) embeds canonically into that of \(C_{k+2}\), with a transitive action on the new three roots;
3. every factor of \(C_{k+2}\) violates a numerical invariant already controlled at levels \(k,k-2\).

No such descent map, field tower, or factor invariant is presently visible in the transfer matrices. Claim III provides exact nonvanishing at the half-integer lattice, but a higher-degree factor can avoid all those rational points.

So the answer to Approach 1 is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No: irreducibility and gcd alone do not force the next core to be irreducible.}
}
\]

## 3. Newton polygons: the best route, but not from the leading coefficient alone

### 3.1 Exact top-coefficient information already in the repository

Write the unscaled centered polynomial as

\[
\widehat N_k(Y)
=N_k\!\left(Y-\frac{k+1}{2}\right)
=\ell_kY^{3k-3}+\eta_kY^{3k-5}+\cdots.
\]

The leading coefficients satisfy

\[
\ell_0=0,\quad \ell_1=1,\quad
\ell_{k+1}=34\ell_k-\ell_{k-1}.
\tag{4}
\]

Thus \(\ell_k\) is the Lucas sequence \(U_k(34,1)\). Lemma `nv-centered-coefficients` in `nv_theorem.tex` proves

\[
\boxed{
\eta_k=
\frac{-(k-1)(32k^2-64k-11)\ell_k+5k\ell_{k-1}}{256}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

The same file records the Cassini identity

\[
\ell_{k-1}^2-34\ell_k\ell_{k-1}+\ell_k^2=1.
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(p\) be an odd prime with \(p\mid\ell_k\) and \(p\nmid10k\). Equation (6) gives

\[
\ell_{k-1}^2\equiv1\pmod p,
\]

and (5) gives

\[
\eta_k\equiv\frac{5k\ell_{k-1}}{256}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{7}
\]

In core coordinates, \(\ell_k\) is the leading coefficient and \(\eta_k\) is the next coefficient. Therefore the reciprocal core has constant term divisible by \(p\), but its coefficient of \(U\) is a \(p\)-adic unit.

Its Newton polygon starts with an edge of horizontal length one and integral slope, followed by slope zero. Equivalently, the reciprocal polynomial has a simple \(p\)-adic root by Hensel’s lemma. This is compatible with global irreducibility and gives essentially no restriction on the degree of a rational factor.

This is a useful negative conclusion:

> **A generic prime divisor of the Lucas leading coefficient cannot furnish reciprocal Eisenstein or a one-edge Dumas proof.**

The primitive-divisor theorem for Lucas sequences supplies fresh primes dividing \(\ell_k\) for all sufficiently large \(k\), but the generic fresh prime lands in precisely the weak situation (7). Primitive divisors may still be useful as one local datum in a multi-prime argument; they are not the whole proof.

### 3.2 The central coefficient has the opposite obstruction

For the odd core, in the normalization stated in the question,

\[
f_{2m+1}(0)=\pm(m!)^6b_m^2.
\tag{8}
\]

For every odd prime \(p\),

\[
v_p(f_{2m+1}(0))=6v_p(m!)+2v_p(b_m)
\]

is even. In particular, ordinary Eisenstein at the centered constant term is impossible. A one-edge Newton polygon could still have endpoint height \(2,4,\ldots\), but one would have to prove the required inequalities for **all** intermediate coefficients. Formula (8) by itself does not do so.

Thus both obvious endpoints resist a uniform one-prime proof:

- leading-coefficient primes usually make the next coefficient a unit;
- centered constant-term valuations are highly nonprimitive and even.

### 3.3 The right Newton-polygon invariant is the set of allowed factor degrees

Let \(C(U)\in\mathbb Z[U]\) have degree \(D\). At a prime \(p\), write every slope of its Newton polygon in lowest terms as

\[
\frac{a_i}{b_i},\qquad \gcd(a_i,b_i)=1,
\]

and let the corresponding horizontal length be \(L_i\). Every \(p\)-adic factor supported on that edge has degree divisible by \(b_i\). Hence the degree of any factor over \(\mathbb Q\) lies in the conservative set

\[
\mathcal A_p(C)=
\left\{
 \sum_i b_ie_i:
 0\le e_i\le L_i/b_i,\ e_i\in\mathbb Z
\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]

Factoring the residual polynomials on the edges can make this set much smaller.

A rigorous multi-prime criterion is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\bigcap_{p\in\mathcal P}\mathcal A_p(C)
\cap\{1,\ldots,\lfloor D/2\rfloor\}
=\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C\text{ is irreducible over }\mathbb Q.
}
\tag{10}
\]

This is the correct target for an inductive Newton argument. One does not need one miraculous Eisenstein prime. Several primes can jointly exclude all proper factor degrees.

### 3.4 What should be proved inductively

The recurrence (1) can plausibly prove one of the following stronger statements:

1. exact valuations of the monomial coefficients of \(F_m,H_m\) at selected primes;
2. exact valuations after reversal or a level-dependent translation;
3. a Schur-type congruence in a factorial basis adapted to the centered lattice;
4. a \(\phi\)-Newton polygon, where \(\phi(U)\) is a low-degree polynomial adapted to one of the forced square lattices.

The monomial basis is probably not optimal. The continuant has distinguished cut points at integral \(r\), and Q319 gives distinguished half-integral points. In the integral centered coordinate \(Y\), these become arithmetic lattices of integer squares in \(U=Y^2\). A natural experimental basis is therefore

\[
\Phi_s(U)=\prod_{q=0}^{s-1}(U-q^2),
\tag{11}
\]

or its even-square/odd-square variants. The correct basis may instead be a cubic factorial basis coming from the cut-edge factors \((r+j)^6\). The aim is to make a long block of coefficients acquire transparent products of consecutive integers, exactly as in Schur’s proofs for Laguerre and Bessel polynomials.

The closest literature analogue is not a proof by recurrence of earlier irreducibility. It is the following package:

```text
explicit coefficient formula or congruence
        +
Newton-polygon slope bound
        +
prime in a suitable interval
        =
factor-degree exclusion.
```

That is how the irreducibility of generalized Laguerre, Bessel, and several Wronskian Hermite families is proved.

### 3.5 Two concrete local audits worth doing first

The small structural primes deserve a separate symbolic treatment.

Modulo \(3\),

\[
P(t)\equiv t^3-1=(t-1)^3\pmod3.
\tag{12}
\]

Moreover, (4) gives

\[
\ell_k\equiv0\pmod3\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 3\mid k.
\]

When \(3\mid k\), both terms on the right of (5) are divisible by \(3\), so the reciprocal polygon has a longer initial divisible block than at a generic Lucas prime. The reverse-Eisenstein proof for the \(k=3\) core is the first instance of this phenomenon. The precise \(3\)-adic polygon should be derived for every residue class of \(k\) modulo a power of \(3\).

For even \(k\), \(\ell_k\) is even. The integral normalization \(M_k\), rather than the rationally centered core, is the correct object for a \(2\)-adic audit. It may be possible to handle a large part of the even family by a uniform \(2\)-adic polygon.

These two audits are much more promising than choosing an arbitrary prime divisor of \(\ell_k\).

## 4. Can a DDF witness prime be propagated?

No mechanism of this kind follows from the recurrence.

### 4.1 An irreducible reduction is stronger than rational irreducibility

Let \(C\) have degree \(D\) and Galois group \(G\le S_D\). At a good prime, the factor degrees of \(C\bmod p\) are the cycle lengths of Frobenius. The reduction is irreducible exactly when \(G\) contains a \(D\)-cycle and Frobenius lands in that conjugacy class.

An irreducible polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\) need not have any irreducible reduction. For example, a transitive \(A_4\)-quartic has no good prime with irreducible reduction because \(A_4\) has no \(4\)-cycle.

Thus the empirical statement

\[
\text{“every core has an irreducible reduction at some prime”}
\]

already contains more information than irreducibility itself.

If the Galois group is \(S_D\), Chebotarev predicts density \(1/D\) for irreducible reductions, not density one. The observed DDF witnesses are therefore exactly what an \(S_D\) model predicts, but their existence cannot be inferred from the previous level’s witness.

### 4.2 The witness primes live in unrelated splitting fields

A prime \(p_k\) certifying \(C_k\) is a Frobenius statement in the splitting field \(K_k\). The recurrence does not give an inclusion

\[
K_k\subseteq K_{k+2}
\]

or a map from a conjugacy class in \(\operatorname{Gal}(K_k/\mathbb Q)\) to one in \(\operatorname{Gal}(K_{k+2}/\mathbb Q)\). Consequently, \(p_k\) carries no automatic information about \(C_{k+2}\bmod p_k\), much less about the existence of some new \(p_{k+2}\).

The repository contains a sharp warning about fixed-characteristic propagation: `nv_theorem.tex` proves that over \(\mathbb F_7\), the transfer product degenerates at length \(21\) and

\[
N_{21}\equiv0\pmod7.
\]

Thus a fixed prime can behave regularly for many levels and then become completely singular because the shifted recurrence is periodic modulo that prime.

### 4.3 A more flexible modular certificate

One does not actually need a single irreducible reduction. Let the squarefree factor-degree partition of \(C\bmod p\) be

\[
\lambda_p=(d_{p,1},\ldots,d_{p,t_p}),
\]

and let

\[
\Sigma(\lambda_p)=
\left\{
 \sum_{i\in I}d_{p,i}: I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t_p\}
\right\}.
\]

Every rational factor degree belongs to \(\Sigma(\lambda_p)\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
igcap_{p\in\mathcal P}\Sigma(\lambda_p)=\{0,D\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C\text{ is irreducible}.}
\tag{13}
\]

For a uniform theorem, proving two or three explicit modular patterns may be easier than proving an irreducible reduction. This is the finite-field counterpart of the multi-prime Newton criterion (10).

The recurrence might help derive such patterns for primes tied arithmetically to \(k\). It does not propagate a witness from \(k\) to \(k+2\).

## 5. Can \(S_D\) be proved inductively?

Not with the presently known objects.

Let \(D_k\) be the degree of the core. Then

\[
D_{k+2}=D_k+3.
\]

To induct

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(C_k/\mathbb Q)=S_{D_k}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Gal}(C_{k+2}/\mathbb Q)=S_{D_k+3},
\]

one would need a natural relationship between the two root sets. No such relationship follows from the scalar recurrence, because a root of \(C_{k+2}\) normally makes \(C_k\) and \(C_{k-2}\) nonzero and merely imposes a ratio identity analogous to (2).

The usual proof that a degree-\(D\) polynomial has group \(S_D\) runs in the opposite order:

1. prove irreducibility, hence transitivity;
2. use a Newton polygon or modular factorization to produce a suitable long prime cycle;
3. invoke Jordan’s theorem to obtain \(A_D\);
4. show that the discriminant is not a square to obtain \(S_D\).

Thus \(S_D\) is normally an **upgrade after irreducibility**, not a replacement for it.

A Galois induction would become viable only after constructing a new tower of covers

\[
X_{k+2}\longrightarrow X_k
\]

whose fibers account for the three newly added roots and whose geometric monodromy can be controlled. The Picard–Fuchs/K3 monodromy varies the geometric parameter \(t\), whereas the core roots vary the recurrence starting index \(r\). The existing monodromy representation does not construct this tower.

A clean conditional statement is therefore:

> **Gap–Maeda conjecture.** Every odd and even Apéry core has Galois group \(S_D\) (or at least a transitive group containing \(A_D\)).

This implies all-level irreducibility immediately. GRH alone does not imply the conjecture. Under Gap–Maeda, GRH for the relevant Dedekind zeta functions would make an effective Chebotarev search for a DDF witness possible, but it would not supply the algebraic transitivity statement.

## 6. Discriminants and resultants

### 6.1 What a nonzero discriminant would prove

For a primitive core \(C_k\),

\[
\operatorname{disc}(C_k)\ne0
\]

proves only that the roots are distinct. A squarefree polynomial can have any number of irreducible factors. Thus discriminant nonvanishing is useful preparation for Dedekind/Chebotarev arguments, but it cannot establish irreducibility.

### 6.2 Why the discriminant recurrence does not close

Differentiating

\[
N_{k+1}=P(r+k)N_k-(r+k)^6N_{k-1}
\]

gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{k+1}'={}&P'(r+k)N_k+P(r+k)N_k'\\
&-6(r+k)^5N_{k-1}-(r+k)^6N_{k-1}'.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

At a common root of \(N_{k+1}\) and \(N_{k+1}'\), assuming the displayed factors are nonzero, equations (14) and the original recurrence combine into

\[
\frac{N_k'}{N_k}-rac{N_{k-1}'}{N_{k-1}}
=rac{6}{r+k}-rac{P'(r+k)}{P(r+k)}.
\tag{15}
\]

Equation (15) is a new critical-point equation for the rational function

\[
\frac{P(r+k)N_k}{(r+k)^6N_{k-1}}.
\]

Its resultant is not determined by

\[
\operatorname{disc}(N_k),\quad
\operatorname{disc}(N_{k-1}),\quad
\operatorname{Res}(N_k,N_{k-1}).
\]

New mixed derivative resultants appear at every step. There may be a useful determinant or Wronskian identity, but there is no scalar discriminant recurrence analogous to the adjacent-resultant product formula.

### 6.3 Best use of the discriminant

After irreducibility has been proved, the discriminant is exactly the right invariant for distinguishing

\[
A_D\quad\text{from}\quad S_D.
\]

It should therefore be placed in the Galois-group upgrade stage, not in the primary irreducibility induction.

## 7. The recommended inductive program

### Phase I: find the right coefficient basis

For the integral cores \(F_m,H_m\), compute exact expansions in all of the following bases:

1. monomials in \(U\);
2. reciprocal monomials;
3. \(U\mapsto U+c\) for small and level-dependent \(c\);
4. square-factorial bases such as (11), separated into even and odd square lattices;
5. a cubic factorial basis obtained directly from the cut-edge factors \((r+j)^6\).

For every basis, record the coefficient recurrence induced by (1). The target is a basis in which the \(s\)-th coefficient has a product formula or a divisibility chain involving consecutive linear factors in \(k\) and \(s\).

The repository’s `bispectral_operator_scan.py` rigorously excludes nontrivial bounded-radius, bounded-degree scalar difference eigenoperators within its tested ansatz. This makes a straightforward identification with a classical scalar orthogonal family less likely, but it does not exclude a factorial-basis congruence or a higher-order/matrix-valued structure.

### Phase II: replace DDF hunting by an allowed-degree audit

For each computed core and each small or structurally selected prime \(p\), compute:

- the complete Newton polygon;
- residual polynomials on every edge;
- the allowed degree set \(\mathcal A_p\) from (9);
- the factor-degree subset-sum set \(\Sigma(\lambda_p)\) from (13) when the reduction is squarefree.

Then find a minimal set of primes whose intersection rules out every degree \(1,\ldots,\lfloor D/2\rfloor\).

The important experimental output is not merely

```text
one prime makes the polynomial irreducible,
```

but a symbolic pattern such as

```text
if k lies in this residue/rank class, p gives denominator b_1;
if k lies in the complementary class, q gives denominator b_2;
the resulting allowed-degree intersection is empty.
```

Such a pattern can plausibly be proved by induction from (1).

### Phase III: prove a Gap–Schur package

A useful theorem schema would be:

**Gap–Schur coefficient theorem.** In an adapted basis, the coefficient array \(a_{m,s}\) satisfies a recurrence from (1) and an explicit congruence

\[
a_{m,s}\equiv E_{m,s}\pmod{p^{e_{m,s}}}
\]

for primes in specified ranges or rank classes.

**Gap–Newton slope theorem.** Those congruences imply that every proper factor of the level-\(m\) core has degree in a set \(\mathcal E_m\).

**Prime exclusion theorem.** Elementary prime-distribution or Lucas primitive-divisor input shows

\[
\mathcal E_m\cap[1,D_m/2]=\varnothing
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\).

The DDF certificates through \(k=100\) then dispose of the finite range.

This is exactly the architecture used in the strongest all-degree irreducibility results for Bessel and generalized Laguerre polynomials.

### Phase IV: upgrade to \(S_D\)

Once irreducibility is established, use the existing modular data to seek:

1. a prime with a long prime cycle, obtained from a Newton edge or factorization pattern;
2. a nonsquare discriminant;
3. Jordan’s theorem.

This is where the observed \(S_D\) statistics become useful. Attempting this stage first makes transitivity circular.

## 8. A precise “best next theorem”

The most useful next lemma is not

\[
C_k,C_{k-2}\text{ irreducible}\Rightarrow C_{k+2}\text{ irreducible}.
\]

It is the following factor-degree statement.

> **Uniform local exclusion target.** Let \(C_k\) be the primitive integral odd or even core, of degree \(D_k\). For every \(k>100\), construct an explicit finite set of primes \(\mathcal P_k\), determined from elementary arithmetic data attached to \(k\), such that
> \[
> \bigcap_{p\in\mathcal P_k}\mathcal A_p(C_k)
> \cap[1,D_k/2]=\varnothing.
> \]
> Then \(C_k\) is irreducible.

An even stronger but less likely one-prime target is:

> after a specified reversal, translation, or \(\phi\)-adic expansion, the Newton polygon has one edge of slope \(A/D_k\) with \(\gcd(A,D_k)=1\).

The exact formulas (5)–(8) show that the naïve leading and centered choices do not produce this uniformly. That is why the multi-prime/adapted-basis formulation is the realistic one.

## 9. Literature context

The appropriate precedents are arithmetic irreducibility proofs for explicit polynomial families, not general Picard–Fuchs transfer-matrix theorems.

1. **M. Filaseta and T.-Y. Lam**, “On the irreducibility of the generalized Laguerre polynomials,” *Acta Arithmetica* 105 (2002), 177–182, DOI `10.4064/aa105-2-4`. The proof uses explicit coefficients and prime/valuation arguments.
2. **M. Filaseta and O. Trifonov**, “The irreducibility of the Bessel polynomials,” *J. Reine Angew. Math.* 550 (2002), 125–140, DOI `10.1515/crll.2002.069`. This is the closest model for proving every member of a growing-degree recurrence family irreducible.
3. **F. Hajir**, “On the Galois group of generalized Laguerre polynomials,” *J. Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux* 17 (2005), 517–525, DOI `10.5802/jtnb.505`. Newton polygons are used after irreducibility to force large cycles and \(A_n/S_n\) Galois groups.
4. **C. Grosu and C. Grosu**, “The irreducibility of some Wronskian Hermite polynomials,” *Indagationes Mathematicae* 32 (2021), DOI `10.1016/j.indag.2020.12.004`, arXiv `2007.00065`. Their two main tools are a sharp Newton-slope bound and a Schur-type congruence. This is an especially close methodological analogue because those polynomials also possess nontrivial recurrence relations, yet irreducibility is proved through local coefficient structure rather than induction from prior irreducibility.
5. **N. C. Bonciocat, Y. Bugeaud, M. Cipu, M. Mignotte**, “Irreducibility criteria for sums of two relatively prime polynomials,” *Int. J. Number Theory* 9 (2013), 1529–1539, DOI `10.1142/S1793042113500413`. Their criteria require a large scalar prime-power multiplier. The Apéry recurrence has a polynomial sixth power and equal competing degrees, so their theorem does not apply directly, but it clarifies what extra size/valuation input a coprime-sum argument would need.
6. **Y. Bilu, G. Hanrot, P. Voutier**, “Existence of primitive divisors of Lucas and Lehmer numbers,” *J. Reine Angew. Math.* 539 (2001), 75–122, DOI `10.1515/crll.2001.080`. This supplies fresh primes for the leading-coefficient sequence, although (7) shows that a generic fresh prime is not by itself an irreducibility prime.
7. **L. Bary-Soroker, D. Garzoni, S. Sodin**, “Irreducibility of the characteristic polynomials of random tridiagonal matrices,” arXiv `2502.17218` (2025). Even in a random tridiagonal setting, irreducibility and large Galois groups require substantial global input. There is no general deterministic theorem saying that continuant recurrences preserve irreducibility.

I do not know a published theorem that proves irreducibility of every member of a Picard–Fuchs transfer-polynomial sequence merely from irreducibility of preceding members and Zariski-dense differential monodromy.

## 10. Direct answers to the five proposed approaches

### 1. Does \(f_k\) irreducible and \(\gcd(f_k,f_{k-2})=1\) force \(f_{k+2}\) irreducible?

**No.** This implication is false for three-term polynomial recurrences in general, and the Apéry recurrence supplies no factor-descent map that could make it true here. A successful induction must strengthen the invariant to a local factor-degree exclusion statement.

### 2. Can a prime dividing the leading or trailing coefficient give a Newton proof?

**Possibly, but not by the obvious endpoint argument.** A generic prime \(p\mid\ell_k\) makes the next centered coefficient a unit by (5)–(7), so reciprocal Eisenstein fails. The odd central term has even valuation by (8), so centered Eisenstein also fails at odd primes. The viable route is a longer coefficient block, a translated/\(\phi\)-adic polygon, or several primes.

### 3. Can the DDF witness prime be propagated from \(k\) to \(k+2\)?

**No known mechanism, and none follows formally from the recurrence.** An irreducible reduction is a full-cycle Frobenius statement in a different splitting field at every level. Fixed primes can also encounter periodic degenerations, as \(N_{21}\equiv0\pmod7\) shows. A uniform two- or three-prime factor-degree pattern is a more flexible target.

### 4. Can \(S_n\) be proved inductively?

**Not without a new tower of covers or splitting fields.** There is no natural inclusion of level-\(k\) splitting fields into level-\(k+2\) splitting fields. The practical order is irreducibility first, then long cycles plus discriminant to upgrade to \(A_D/S_D\).

### 5. Can discriminants or resultants close the induction?

**Resultants control inter-level coprimality; discriminants control squarefreeness. Neither controls the number of factors.** The adjacent resultant has a closed recurrence, but differentiating the gap recurrence creates the new critical equation (15), so the discriminant does not satisfy an analogous closed scalar recurrence.

## Final recommendation

The best all-\(k\) attack is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{integral centered cores}\ M_k
\\[2mm]
\Downarrow\ \text{derive coefficient recurrences in an adapted factorial/square basis}
\\[2mm]
\text{uniform Schur-type congruences and }p\text{-adic slope bounds}
\\[2mm]
\Downarrow
\\[2mm]
\text{multi-prime exclusion of every proper factor degree}
\\[2mm]
\Downarrow
\\[2mm]
\text{finite DDF base range }k\le100
\\[2mm]
\Downarrow
\\[2mm]
\text{all-level irreducibility.}
\end{array}
}
\]

So there is a plausible **inductive** proof program, but the induction must act on the coefficient/valuation structure generated by the recurrence—not on irreducibility of the previous two cores.
