# Half-integer pinning audit for aligned Apéry pencils

## Executive conclusion

The half-integer point gives a genuine structural divisor, but it does **not** prove that every aligned resultant content comes from the pinned root. The exact reflection identity forces one factor, while the remaining Sylvester content can still contain contributions from unpinned common roots modulo primes. The observed factor 75577 for `(3,4,3)` is therefore consistent with the pinned carrier, but the claimed equality of all nonstructural content with that carrier requires an additional resultant nonvanishing theorem.

The aligned verifier computes
\[
C_{a,b,c}=\operatorname{cont}_T\operatorname{Res}_x(F,G+TJ),
\]
with the palindromic modification of `G` when `c=a`. The code implements this central deflation explicitly: when `c==a`, the central linear factor is removed before forming the pencil.

## Reflection identity

Let
\[
N_h(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}(x+i)^3+\cdots
\]
be the continuant gap polynomial. The recurrence gives a reflection law of the form
\[
N_h(x)=(-1)^{3(h-1)}N_h(-h-1-x).
\]
Since the degree is `3(h-1)`, the reflection center is
\[
x_h=-\frac{h+1}{2}.
\]
For even `s=a+b+c`,
\[
x_s=-\frac{s+1}{2}
\]
is the fixed point, hence
\[
N_s(x_s)=0.
\]

Now use the continuant composition identity. Splitting a length `s` continuant after the first `a` steps gives
\[
N_s(x)=U_{a,b,c}(x)N_{b+c}(x+a)+V_{a,b,c}(x)N_a(x),
\]
where the transfer coefficients are the two adjacent continuant minors. Evaluating at the reflection point and using the symmetry of the transfer matrix gives
\[
N_{b+c}(x_s+a)=(-1)^{3(b+c-1)}\gamma_{a,b,c}\,N_a(x_s),
\]
where
\[
\gamma_{a,b,c}
=
\frac{\det M_{0:a}(x_s)}{\det M_{a:a+b+c}(x_s)}
\]
is the rational gauge factor coming from the normalization of the two transfer bases.

Equivalently, after clearing denominators,
\[
D_{a,b,c}N_{b+c}(x_s+a)=\pm E_{a,b,c}N_a(x_s),
\]
with `D,E` the adjacent continuant minors. This is the exact integral identity; the scalar is not generally 1.

## What the pinned gcd controls

At the pinned point, the pencil specializes to
\[
F=N_a(x),\qquad G+TJ=N_b(x+a)+T N_{b+c}(x+a).
\]
If a prime `p` divides both rational numerators
\[
A=N_a(x_s),\qquad B=N_b(x_s+a),
\]
then modulo `p` the point `x_s` is a common root of `F` and `G`, and by the reflection identity it is also a common root of `F` and `J`.

Therefore the pinned carrier divides the resultant content:
\[
\gcd(\operatorname{num}(A),\operatorname{num}(B))
\mid C_{a,b,c}
\]
up to the explicit denominator and gauge factors.

This proves a lower bound on the content, not an equality.

## Can all common roots be pinned?

No. The resultant content condition is
\[
p\mid \operatorname{Res}_x(F,G+TJ)
\]
for every `T`. Equivalently, modulo `p`, the polynomials have a common root in the algebraic closure. Nothing in the reflection identity forces that root to equal
\[
x_s\pmod p.
\]

An unpinned root can occur if there exists
\[
r\ne x_s
\]
with
\[
N_a(r)=0,\qquad N_b(r+a)=0,
\]
and the pencil compatibility condition holds. The root strip theorem and degree bounds do not exclude such coincidences modulo primes.

Thus the correct statement is:

* pinned gcd always contributes to `C*`;
* equality of `C*` with the pinned gcd requires a separate unpinned-root exclusion lemma.

No such lemma follows from reflection alone.

## Palindromic case `c=a`

When `c=a`, the second polynomial shares the reflection center. The factor removed in the verifier is the central factor of `N_b` when `b` is even. This changes the statement from
\[
N_b(x_s+a)=0
\]
to the deflated polynomial value
\[
N_b^o(x_s+a).
\]

The pinned divisor becomes
\[
\gcd(\operatorname{num}(N_a(x_s)),
      \operatorname{num}(N_b^o(x_s+a))).
\]
The removed linear factor is structural and should not be counted as an aligned-content prime.

## Height of the pinned carriers

Define
\[
P_H=\prod_{a+b+c\le H}
\gcd\bigl(\operatorname{num}(N_a(x_s)),
\operatorname{num}(N_b(x_s+a))\bigr)^* .
\]

For each triple, the continuant determinant representation gives
\[
\log |N_h(x)|\ll h\log H
\]
for `h\le H`, including the half-integer specialization after clearing denominators. The number of triples is
\[
\#\{(a,b,c):a+b+c\le H\}=O(H^3).
\]
Therefore the direct carrier estimate is
\[
\log P_H
\ll
\sum_{a+b+c\le H}(a+b)\log H
=O(H^4\log H).
\]

There is no cancellation argument in the integer divisor product that improves this automatically to `H^3`. To obtain cubic height one would need a new global divisibility theorem exploiting overlap between different triples.

## Remaining gap

The half-integer pinning mechanism is real and should be added as a structural factor in the aligned-content proof. The unresolved point is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{prove that every nonstructural resultant-content prime has a pinned root.}
}
\]

Reflection proves the pinned implication, but not its converse. Until an unpinned-root exclusion argument is supplied, the 75577 observation is evidence only, not a theorem.
