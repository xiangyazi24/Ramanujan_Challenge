# The one-layer p-adic gap in Apéry GCD

## Summary

The observed phenomenon is real and points to the correct local invariant. At a top-window hit prime p>n/2 with p|b_n, the numerator pair (a_n,b_n) is not the obstruction. The obstruction is that the Apéry companion a_n has a denominator defect of exactly one p-adic layer:

\[
v_p(D_n)=2<3=v_p(d_n).
\]

Thus

\[
v_p(d_na_n)=3-v_p(D_n)+v_p(A_n)=1
\]

in the generic case, producing

\[
v_p(G_n)=1.
\]

The missing layer is not removable by changing the solution basis by a constant multiple of b_n. It is intrinsic to the logarithmic/harmonic nature of the companion solution.

---

## 1. Why the defect exists

The two Apéry solutions form a second-order recurrence basis:

\[
(n+1)^3u_{n+1}=P(n)u_n-n^3u_{n-1}.
\]

The integral solution b_n is the holomorphic period solution. The companion a_n is the logarithmic solution. In a differential-equation analogy, b_n corresponds to a regular period while a_n contains the logarithmic extension class.

The denominator of a_n is therefore not obtained by simply inserting three copies of the lcm denominator. The harmonic terms contain cancellations.

For p>n/2, the only multiple of p in {1,...,n} is p itself. A naive harmonic term 1/p^3 suggests a p^{-3} contribution. However, the coefficient of this term is not arbitrary: it is tied to the vanishing of the period solution b_n modulo p.

The relation

\[
a_{n-1}b_n-a_nb_{n-1}=-\frac6{n^3}
\]

shows that when b_n vanishes modulo p, a_n cannot behave like an independent generic harmonic sum. The extension class loses one p-adic layer.

Hence the empirical law

\[
v_p(D_n)=2
\]

is natural: the logarithmic solution has only a double pole after the period vanishes.

---

## 2. Why the condition p|b_n changes a_n

The important point is that a_n is not a sum of independent terms.

Schematically:

\[
a_n=\sum_k B_{n,k}H_{n,k}.
\]

The denominator of each H_{n,k} can contain p^3, but the coefficients satisfy

\[
\sum_k B_{n,k}=b_n.
\]

At a hit prime,

\[
\sum_k B_{n,k}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Therefore the leading p^{-3} denominator contribution is multiplied by a vanishing period. One p-adic layer disappears. What remains is a p^{-2} contribution.

This explains the observed transition:

without a zero:

\[
v_p(D_n)=3,
\]

with a zero:

\[
v_p(D_n)=2.
\]

---

## 3. A possible closed formula

The expected local formula is not

\[
v_p(D_n)=3,
\]

but rather a conditional formula:

\[
v_p(D_n)=3-\mathbf 1_{p|b_n}
\]

for p in the top window.

Equivalently:

\[
v_p(d_na_n)=\begin{cases}
0,&p\nmid b_n,\\
1,&p\mid b_n.
\end{cases}
\]

This matches the computation:

\[
v_p(G_n)=1
\]

at every top-window hit.

A proof would likely require an explicit p-adic expansion of the Apéry logarithmic solution near the Teichmuller zero of the period solution.

---

## 4. Can a modified companion remove the defect?

Replacing

\[
a_n\mapsto a_n+c b_n
\]

cannot work.

The Casoratian implies that b_n has higher p-adic valuation at a hit:

\[
v_p(b_n)>v_p(a_n).
\]

Therefore

\[
v_p(a_n+c b_n)=v_p(a_n).
\]

A constant basis change cannot alter the local extension class.

Allowing a rational c(n) with controlled denominator also does not help generically. If

\[
v_p(c(n))=-1,
\]

then

\[
v_p(c(n)b_n)\ge0,
\]

while

\[
v_p(a_n)=-2,
\]

so the minimum remains -2.

A successful modification would need a genuinely different integral lattice, not merely another basis vector.

---

## 5. Consequence for the GCD conjecture

The observation changes the interpretation of the problem.

Previously one hoped denominator cancellation would eliminate top-window contributions. The data says instead:

\[
\boxed{\text{every top-window hit contributes about }p.}
\]

Hence

\[
\log G_n^{top}\approx\sum_{p|b_n,\ p>n/2}\log p.
\]

The conjecture is therefore equivalent to proving that the number of top-window hits is small:

\[
\omega_{(n/2,n]}(b_n)=o(n/\log n).
\]

The denominator route does not remove the top window; it identifies it exactly.

---

## 6. Final picture

The one-layer gap exists because the companion sequence is a logarithmic period, and zeros of the holomorphic period force a one-step degeneration of its p-adic denominator.

The likely theorem to prove is:

\[
\boxed{v_p(D_n)=3-\operatorname{ord}_{p}(b_n)\text{ truncated at one layer}}
\]

for p>n/2.

This would completely explain the computations. But even after proving it, the remaining problem is exactly the large-prime zero-density problem for b_n.
