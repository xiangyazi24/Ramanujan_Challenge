# Q872 audit: weighted incidence route

## Verdict

The reduction is correct after the stated source corrections. Claim 1 follows line by line from the variance hypothesis and the zero-count theorem. Claim 2 is also correct: dyadic block control implies the global variance bound by the Hilbert-space triangle inequality. However, the phrase “Diagonal AMTD avoids all cross-block estimates” needs a precise interpretation: it avoids proving cancellation between different prime blocks, but it does not prove that the within-block estimates are independent or automatic.

## Claim 1 audit

Let
\[
I_N=(N,2N],
\]
and let
\[
X_p(m)=1_{p\mid b_{m\bmod p}}
\]
for the actual lower-digit incidence. Then
\[
A_p=\sum_{m\in I_N}X_p(m),
\]
and
\[
B(m)=\sum_p(\log p)X_p(m).
\]

The mean is exactly
\[
\mu=\frac1N\sum_{m\in I_N}B(m)
      =\frac1N\sum_p(\log p)A_p.
\]
No missing normalization occurs here.

The centered variance is
\[
V_w=\sum_m(B(m)-\mu)^2.
\]

Assume
\[
V_w\le N^{o(1)}S_w.
\]
Using
\[
Z(p)=O(p^{2/3}),
\]
we have for every relevant prime
\[
A_p\le (N/p+1)Z(p).
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_p A_p
\ll \sum_{p\le N} (N/p+1)p^{2/3+o(1)}
\ll N^{5/3+o(1)}.
\]

The weighted diagonal quantity satisfies
\[
S_w=\sum_p(\log p)^2A_p(1-A_p/N)
\le \sum_p(\log p)^2A_p
\le N^{5/3+o(1)}.
\]
Hence
\[
V_w\le N^{5/3+o(1)}.
\]

Consequently
\[
|B(m)-\mu|\le \sqrt{V_w}=N^{5/6+o(1)}.
\]

For the mean,
\[
\mu\le N^{-1}\sum_p(\log p)A_p
\le N^{2/3+o(1)}.
\]
Thus
\[
B(m)=N^{5/6+o(1)}
\]
uniformly.

This is sufficient because the lower-digit contribution is exactly the weighted incidence controlling the logarithmic gcd. No extra average over n is used.

## Prime range and endpoints

The sums are over primes in the lower-digit range used by the incidence model. Endpoint conventions do not affect the estimates:

* whether p=N or p=2N is included changes O(log N) terms;
* p>N has no contribution to lower digits in this block formulation;
* small primes contribute below the stated scale and are absorbed in the existing small-prime bounds.

The bound only uses the uniform estimate for Z(p), so no hidden exceptional prime set appears.

## Claim 2 audit

Partition the prime range into dyadic blocks
\[
\mathcal P_i.
\]
The number of blocks is
\[
L=O(\log N).
\]

Define
\[
F_i(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_i}(\log p)(X_p(m)-A_p/N).
\]
Then
\[
B(m)-\mu=\sum_iF_i(m).
\]

The block variance is
\[
s_i^2=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_i}(\log p)^2A_p(1-A_p/N).
\]

Assume
\[
\|F_i\|_2^2\le Cs_i^2.
\]
Then
\[
V_w=\left\|\sum_iF_i\right\|_2^2.
\]
By Cauchy-Schwarz in the finite block index,
\[
\left\|\sum_iF_i\right\|_2^2
\le L\sum_i\|F_i\|_2^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
V_w\le CL\sum_i s_i^2
=CL S_w.
\]
Since
\[
L=O(\log N)=N^{o(1)},
\]
Claim 1 follows.

## Hidden assumptions checked

1. **Mean centering.** Correct: each block subtracts exactly its contribution to the global mean.

2. **Prime powers.** The incidence variable is only prime divisibility. Prime powers are irrelevant at this stage because the target is a logarithmic prime-weighted count. Any prime-power contribution must be handled elsewhere.

3. **A_p/N factor.** Correct: the Bernoulli-variance-shaped term is a formal upper bound; no independence assumption is made.

4. **Cross-block correlations.** They are not estimated. They are bounded by the universal inequality
\[
\|\sum_iF_i\|_2^2\le L\sum_i\|F_i\|_2^2.
\]

5. **AMTD interpretation.** Diagonal AMTD does not prove cross-block cancellation. It makes cross-block cancellation unnecessary by accepting the logarithmic loss from the block count.

## Final verdict

Both claims are valid under their stated hypotheses.

The only non-evidential step is the block hypothesis
\[
\|F_i\|_2^2\le Cs_i^2,
\]
which is the genuine remaining input. Once it is supplied uniformly for every dyadic block, no four-prime condition and no separate cross-block dispersion theorem are needed.

The recovered route is therefore a valid sufficient reduction: the remaining theorem is diagonal AMTD inside each prime scale, not a hidden global cross-block estimate.
