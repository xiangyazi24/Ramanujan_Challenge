# Verdict: NO-GO (for this system alone)

The mixed Cartier/polylog system gives an exact finite-dimensional extension of the Apéry Picard--Fuchs equation, but it does **not** prove that
\[
S_1(a)=S_2(a)=S_3(a)=0\pmod q,\quad q>a,
\]
is impossible. The obstruction is precise: the order-three polylog extension controls the coefficient sequence of the products $B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t)$, whereas the Cartier restart defects controlling $S_s(a)$ are coefficient-extraction functionals after a characteristic-$q$ truncation. No transformation identifies these two coefficient families.

## 1. Differential system

Let
\[
\theta=t\frac d{dt},
\]
and let the Apéry operator be
\[
L=\theta^3-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\]
It annihilates
\[
B(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n.
\]

Define
\[
F_s(t)=B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t),\qquad s=1,2,3.
\]
The polylogarithms satisfy
\[
\theta\operatorname{Li}_s(t)=\operatorname{Li}_{s-1}(t),
\]
with
\[
\operatorname{Li}_0(t)=\frac{t}{1-t},
\qquad
\operatorname{Li}_1(t)=-\log(1-t).
\]

For any differential operator $P(\theta,t)$,
\[
P(F_s)=P(B)\operatorname{Li}_s+[P,B]\operatorname{Li}_s.
\]
Since $L(B)=0$,
\[
L(F_s)=[L,B]\operatorname{Li}_s.
\]
The commutator is obtained by replacing each occurrence of $t\theta$ using
\[
\theta(tg)=t(g+\theta g).
\]
The result is a triangular inhomogeneous system:

\[
L(F_1)=E_1(t),
\]
\[
L(F_2)=E_2(t)+E_1(t)\operatorname{Li}_1(t),
\]
\[
L(F_3)=E_3(t)+E_2(t)\operatorname{Li}_1(t)+E_1(t)\operatorname{Li}_2(t),
\]
where the $E_i$ are explicit rational functions with denominator a power of $(1-t)$ coming only from $\operatorname{Li}_0=t/(1-t)$.

Equivalently, adjoining
\[
Y=(F_1,F_2,F_3,B,1)^T
\]
gives a rank-five regular-singular differential system
\[
\theta Y=A(t)Y
\]
after standard first-order conversion. The extension is upper triangular; the diagonal block is the Apéry Picard--Fuchs block.

## 2. Coefficient recurrence

Write
\[
F_s(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}R_s(n)t^n.
\]
Because
\[
\operatorname{Li}_s(t)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{t^k}{k^s},
\]
we have exactly
\[
R_s(n)=\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{b_{n-k}}{k^s}.
\]

Applying $L$ coefficientwise gives the recurrence
\[
 n^3R_s(n)
-(34(n-1)^3+51(n-1)^2+27(n-1)+5)R_s(n-1)
+(n-1)^3R_s(n-2)
=E_s(n),
\]
where $E_s(n)$ is the coefficient of the explicit source term above.

The homogeneous recurrence is identical to the Apéry recurrence. The only new data is the triangular source.

Boundary conditions are:
\[
R_s(0)=0,
\]
\[
R_s(1)=1,
\]
for all $s\ge1$, because $b_0=1$ and the only contribution is $k=1$.

## 3. Cartier reduction

For a prime $q>a$, the desired quantities are
\[
S_s(a)=\sum_{u=1}^ab_{a-u}u^{-s}\pmod q.
\]

These are exactly
\[
R_s(a)\pmod q
\]
inside the truncated range because every denominator $u$ is a unit modulo $q$.

However, the Cartier operator acts on a power series
\[
\sum c_nt^n
\]
by selecting congruence classes of exponents:
\[
\mathcal C_r\left(\sum c_nt^n\right)=\sum c_{qn+r}^{1/q}t^n.
\]

The coefficient $R_s(a)$ is not a Cartier restart coefficient. It is the coefficient of the product
\[
B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t).
\]

The restart coefficient families appearing in Cartier defects have the form
\[
[t^m]B(t)^q\quad\text{or}\quad[t^{qa+r}]B(t),
\]
whereas
\[
R_s(a)=[t^a]B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t).
\]

There is no identity
\[
[t^a]B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t)
=	ext{Cartier restart coefficient of }B
\]
without an additional transformation.

## 4. What the triangular system can prove

The order-three extension gives only:

If a prime $q$ divides all three moments, then
\[
R_1(a)=R_2(a)=R_3(a)=0\pmod q.
\]

The recurrence then gives three compatible zero solutions modulo $q$ with the same initial source constraints.

But the recurrence is nonhomogeneous. A common zero at one index is not forbidden by uniqueness, because the source terms remain nonzero and the solution can cross zero.

A contradiction would require an additional invariant, for example:

- a Wronskian of the three inhomogeneous solutions;
- a Cartier eigenrelation forcing nonzero determinant;
- an integral positivity statement surviving modulo $q$.

No such identity follows from the Picard--Fuchs operator.

## 5. Why the Cartier defect comparison fails

Known restart-coefficient results concern the Apéry polynomial truncation
\[
H_q(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}b_rt^r.
\]
They study coefficients after Frobenius:
\[
H_q(t)^q\equiv H_q(t^q)\pmod q.
\]

The mixed polylog sequence instead lives in the extension
\[
B(t)\rightarrow B(t)\operatorname{Li}_1(t)\rightarrow B(t)\operatorname{Li}_2(t)\rightarrow B(t)\operatorname{Li}_3(t).
\]

The map between them would have to identify the coefficient functional
\[
[t^a]B(t)\operatorname{Li}_s(t)
\]
with a Frobenius-selected coefficient of $H_q$. No such map is available; the logarithmic singularity of $\operatorname{Li}_s$ at $t=1$ is an additional extension class.

## 6. Consequence for simultaneous zeros

The strongest theorem obtainable from the coupled system alone is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
S_1(a)=S_2(a)=S_3(a)=0\pmod q
\implies
(F_1,F_2,F_3)(t)\text{ has a common zero coefficient at }a
}
\]

inside the polylogarithmic differential module.

There is no contradiction in the module, and no exclusion of $q>a$.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\textbf{The order-three polylogarithmic Cartier system alone cannot prove the absence of common zeros.}}
\]

A successful proof would need an extra arithmetic input beyond the Picard--Fuchs/polylog extension: either an explicit Cartier transformation to the $R_s$ coefficients, or a nonvanishing invariant of the triangular extension.
