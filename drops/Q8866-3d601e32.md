# Pair-witness equidistribution for \(P_{2,3}^{\pm}\): exact formulation, CRT correction, and the required covariance theorem

## Executive verdict

Pair-witness equidistribution is a useful name for the first genuinely new three-prime input, but the formulation in the question needs several corrections before it is mathematically exact.

1. A **prime pair** \((p,q)\) need not have one witness. Since \(pq>M\), each **zero-residue pair** \((z_p,z_q)\) has at most one representative below \(M\), but a prime pair can have as many as \(|Z_p||Z_q|\) distinct witnesses.
2. The displayed formula for \(m_0\bmod r\) is false. A CRT idempotent expression is only congruent to \(m_0\) modulo \(pq\); before reducing modulo a third prime \(r\), one must subtract the actual multiple of \(pq\) that selects the canonical representative. That multiple depends nonlinearly on \((z_p,z_q)\).
3. The cutoff \(m_0<M\) is not a small perturbation. Since

   \[
   \frac{M}{pq}\in\left(\frac14,1\right),
   \]

   it may discard any proportion from almost \(0\) up to almost \(3/4\) of the CRT classes.
4. The residues modulo \(r\) form a **weighted multiset**, not a set of essentially independent samples. If a row \(m\) supports many anchor pairs, every one of those pair witnesses has the same third-prime indicator \(1_{m\bmod r\in Z_r}\). This perfect within-row correlation is precisely the high-moment obstruction.
5. After those repairs, the signed heuristic is conceptually right: in an ideal independent Bernoulli model, the total signed residual has variance of order \(B_3\), hence fluctuation of order \(\sqrt{B_3}\). However, the individual \(\Delta_r\) are not independent even in that model, and the currently proved Apéry structure supplies no covariance bound of the required strength.

The exact missing statement is a three-prime, cross-characteristic dispersion theorem. In ordered notation matching `problems/3.2/pairpalm_result.tex`, it is

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{\substack{r,p,q\in\mathcal P_X\\
                 r,p,q\ \text{pairwise distinct}}}
\ \sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\left(f_r(m)-\frac{|Z_r|}{r}\right)
\right|
\ll X^{o(1)}\sqrt{M\lambda_X^3}.
}
\tag{PW3-sqrt}
\]

Here \(f_r(m)=1_{m\bmod r\in Z_r}\), \(M=X^2\), and
\(\lambda_X=\sum_r |Z_r|/r\). This square-root statement is much stronger than the current \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) target. The weaker one-sided bound of size \(M\lambda_X^3\) already suffices for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).

## 1. Exact witness bookkeeping

Let

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le 2X\},\qquad M=X^2,
\]

and write

\[
s_p=|Z_p|,\qquad
\Omega_p=\{0\le m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},\qquad
f_p(m)=1_{\Omega_p}(m),
\]

\[
A_p=|\Omega_p|,\qquad
\rho_p=\frac{A_p}{M},\qquad
\bar\rho_p=\frac{s_p}{p},\qquad
K(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}f_p(m).
\]

The repository uses ordered prime tuples, so I do the same until Section 7.

For ordered \(p\ne q\), define

\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|.
\]

For each \((a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q\), let \(c_{p,q}(a,b)\in[0,pq)\) be the canonical CRT representative. Since \(pq>M\),

\[
J_{p,q}
=
\#\{(a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q:c_{p,q}(a,b)<M\}.
\]

Thus the correct statement is:

> Each **residue pair** \((p,a;q,b)\) has zero or one witness below \(M\). A prime pair \((p,q)\) has \(J_{p,q}\) witnesses, where \(0\le J_{p,q}\le s_ps_q\).

The assumption \(s_p\in\{0,1,2\}\) is also not valid for the actual Apéry zero sets. Reflection decomposes \(Z_p\) into reflection pairs, with a possible central fixed point, but there may be several pairs. For example, the checked data in `problems/3.2/centered_residual_verify.py` include

\[
Z_{499}=\{67,170,203,295,328,431\},
\]

so \(s_{499}=6\).

### The correct third-prime slice

For a fixed third prime \(r\), define the ordered pair-witness multiplicity in residue class \(u\bmod r\) by

\[
\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{r\}\\p\ne q}}
\#\{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q:m\equiv u\pmod r\}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_{\substack{0\le m<M\\m\equiv u\pmod r}}
(K(m)-f_r(m))_2.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

This identity displays the important multiplicity: a row carrying \(k\) primes other than \(r\) contributes \((k)_2\) copies of the same residue \(m\bmod r\).

Put

\[
W_r=\sum_{u\bmod r}\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_m(K(m)-f_r(m))_2
\]

and

\[
H_r=\sum_{u\in Z_r}\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_m f_r(m)(K(m)-f_r(m))_2.
\]

Then the periodic and finite-marginal discrepancies are

\[
\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}=H_r-\bar\rho_rW_r,
\qquad
\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}=H_r-\rho_rW_r.
\]

These are the exact versions of the quantity in the question.

### Exact aggregate identity

Let

\[
F_3(X)=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_3.
\]

Since a row with \(K(m)=k\) contributes

\[
\sum_r f_r(m)(k-1)_2=k(k-1)(k-2)=(k)_3,
\]

we get

\[
\sum_rH_r=F_3(X).
\]

Also, with

\[
\lambda_{p,q}=\sum_{r\notin\{p,q\}}\bar\rho_r,
\qquad
\mu_{p,q}=\sum_{r\notin\{p,q\}}\rho_r,
\]

we have

\[
\sum_r\bar\rho_rW_r
=
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q},
\]

and similarly for the finite marginals. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}
=
F_3(X)-\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q},
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}
=
F_3(X)-\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

These are exact, with all multiplicities present.

The baseline is already controlled by the repository's second-moment estimate:

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}
\le \lambda_XF_2(X)
\le 4M\lambda_X^3.
\]

Consequently the positive part of the signed residual in (1.2) is equivalent, at the \(M\lambda_X^3\) scale, to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\). A square-root bound such as `(PW3-sqrt)` would be a substantial strengthening, not merely the statement presently needed for the Apéry gcd argument.

### Periodic versus finite marginals

For every \(r\),

\[
|A_r-Ms_r/r|\le s_r,
\qquad
|\rho_r-\bar\rho_r|\le \frac{s_r}{M}.
\]

Thus

\[
\left|
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}-
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}
\right|
\le
\frac1M\sum_rs_rW_r
\le
\frac{F_2(X)}M\sum_rs_r.
\]

Using \(F_2\le4M\lambda_X^2\) and
\(\sum_rs_r\le2X\lambda_X\),

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}-
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}
\right|
\le 8X\lambda_X^3.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

This boundary term is lower order both at the \(M\lambda_X^3\) scale and, in the Poisson regime \(\lambda_X\asymp1/\log X\), at the proposed square-root scale.

### Signed residual versus positive pair-Palm excess

For each ordered \(p\ne q\), let

\[
R_{p,q}
=
\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}(K(m)-2)
-J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}=\sum_{p\ne q}R_{p,q}.
\]

However, the quantity in `hm3_result.tex` is

\[
\mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)
=
\sum_{p\ne q}(R_{p,q})_+.
\]

Hence a notation such as \(P_{2,3}^{\pm}\) must be fixed carefully:

\[
\sum_r\Delta_r
\ne
\sum_{p\ne q}(R_{p,q})_+
\]

in general. Summing over \(r\) permits cancellation among different anchor pairs. A statement that “most occupied pairs have the correct conditional extension rate” is stronger than a bound on the single signed sum.

## 2. The CRT reduction modulo a third prime

Let \(a\in Z_p\), \(b\in Z_q\), and let

\[
\bar p_q\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
\]

be the inverse of \(p\pmod q\). Define

\[
\nu_{p,q}(a,b)
=
[(b-a)\bar p_q]_q\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}.
\]

Then the canonical CRT representative is exactly

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,q}(a,b)=a+p\nu_{p,q}(a,b)\in[0,pq).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,q}(a,b)\bmod r
\equiv
 a+p[(b-a)\bar p_q]_q\pmod r.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is the correct formula. The least-residue operation \([\cdot]_q\) is essential and is nonlinear from the viewpoint of reduction modulo \(r\).

The occupancy condition is

\[
\boxed{
a+p[(b-a)\bar p_q]_q<M.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Equivalently, the \(q\)-residue in (2.1) must lie in an initial interval of length approximately \(M/p\), which ranges between about \(X/2\) and \(X\). Since \(q\in(X,2X]\), this is not a negligible deletion.

### Why the idempotent formula in the question cannot be reduced modulo \(r\)

Let

\[
E(a,b)
=
 aq\bar q_p+bp\bar p_q,
\]

where \(ar q_p\) is the inverse of \(q\pmod p\). We only know

\[
E(a,b)\equiv c_{p,q}(a,b)\pmod{pq}.
\]

The canonical representative is

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,q}(a,b)
=
E(a,b)-pq\left\lfloor\frac{E(a,b)}{pq}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus modulo \(r\),

\[
 c_{p,q}(a,b)
\equiv
 E(a,b)
-pq\left\lfloor\frac{E(a,b)}{pq}\right\rfloor
\pmod r.
\]

The last term does not vanish because \(r\nmid pq\), and the quotient depends jointly on \(a\) and \(b\). Therefore

\[
 c_{p,q}(a,b)\bmod r
\not\equiv
 aq\bar q_p+bp\bar p_q\pmod r
\]

in general.

There is a second notational problem: \(q\bar q_p\) depends on both \(p\) and \(q\), not just on \(p\) and \(r\); likewise \(p\bar p_q\) depends on both anchor primes. There are no separated coefficients \(A_{p,r}\) and \(B_{q,r}\) of the proposed kind.

These two facts invalidate the proposed factorization before any Apéry estimate is considered.

## 3. Are the witness residues typically disjoint from \(Z_r\)?

### Random-model answer

If the \(J_{p,q}\) pair witnesses were independent uniform residues modulo \(r\), then

\[
\mathbb E\#\{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q:m\bmod r\in Z_r\}
=
J_{p,q}\frac{s_r}{r}.
\]

For \(J_{p,q}=O(1)\), \(s_r=O(1)\), and \(r\asymp X\), the probability of at least one hit would be \(O(1/X)\). Thus “usually disjoint” is the correct random heuristic.

### Rigorous answer for the present Apéry input

No such theorem is currently proved. Sparsity of each \(Z_r\) only says that a uniformly random residue is unlikely to hit. It says nothing about whether the deterministic witness residues are uniform.

The anchored-star construction in `hm3_result.tex` and `pairpalm_result.tex` gives an exact no-go. It chooses an integer \(m_*\) and reflection pairs

\[
Z_p^*=\{m_*\bmod p,\ p-1-(m_*\bmod p)\}
\]

for \(L\asymp X/\log X\) active primes. Then \(m_*\) is a witness for every active prime pair and also lies in every active third column.

For a fixed active \(r\), using ordered anchor pairs,

\[
H_r^*\ge (L-1)_2.
\]

Since every active anchor pair has at most four witnesses,

\[
W_r^*\le4(L-1)_2.
\]

Also \(ar\rho_r^*=2/r\le2/X\). Hence, for \(X>8\),

\[
\Delta_r^{*,\mathrm{per}}
\ge
(L-1)_2-\frac2X\,4(L-1)_2
=
\left(1-\frac8X\right)(L-1)_2>0.
\]

All active \(r\) have the same sign, and

\[
\sum_r\Delta_r^{*,\mathrm{per}}\gg L^3.
\]

This family satisfies the known reflection, no-consecutive-zero, subinterval, bounded-fiber, collision-energy, gap-certificate, and pair-CRT constraints recorded in the repository. Therefore none of those inputs can prove witness equidistribution or signed cancellation.

The Poisson law for the **number** of zero pairs does not address this issue. It is unchanged if one moves each reflection pair to an adversarial position. The missing information concerns positions and their coherence across different characteristics.

## 4. The correct Fourier formulation

Write \(e_r(x)=\exp(2\pi i x/r)\), and define

\[
\widehat{Z_r}(h)
=
\sum_{z\in Z_r}e_r(-hz),
\qquad
\widehat\nu_r(h)
=
\sum_{u\bmod r}\nu_r(u)e_r(hu).
\]

From (1.1),

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\nu_r(h)
=
\sum_{m<M}(K(m)-f_r(m))_2e_r(hm).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Fourier inversion gives the exact periodic discrepancy

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}
=
\frac1r\sum_{h=1}^{r-1}
\widehat{Z_r}(h)\widehat\nu_r(h).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

After changing \(h\) to \(-h\), one may put a complex conjugate on either factor according to convention.

Equivalently,

\[
\widehat\nu_r(h)
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q\ne r\\p\ne q}}
\sum_{a\in Z_p}\sum_{b\in Z_q}
1_{c_{p,q}(a,b)<M}
 e_r\bigl(hc_{p,q}(a,b)\bigr).
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the genuine pair-witness exponential sum.

### Why \(S_pS_q\) does not occur

Even without the cutoff, replacing \(c_{p,q}(a,b)\) by the raw idempotent expression \(E(a,b)\) changes the phase by

\[
e_r\left(-hpq\left\lfloor\frac{E(a,b)}{pq}\right\rfloor\right),
\]

which couples \(a\) and \(b\). With the cutoff, the additional factor \(1_{c_{p,q}(a,b)<M}\) also couples them. Therefore (4.3) does not factor into a product of a \(p\)-sum and a \(q\)-sum.

If one artificially discards both problems and defines pair-dependent transforms \(U_{p,q,r}\), \(V_{p,q,r}\), the absolute-value estimate is still only

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}|U_{p,q,r}(h)V_{p,q,r}(h)|
\le
\sum_{p\ne q}s_ps_q
\le
\left(\sum_ps_p\right)^2
\le4X^2\lambda_X^2.
\]

Averaging this over \(r\) only multiplies it by the number of third primes. There is no cancellation after absolute values have been inserted.

### Parseval identifies the next obstruction

Parseval gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{h\bmod r}|\widehat\nu_r(h)|^2
=
r\sum_{u\bmod r}\nu_r(u)^2.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The right side is the collision energy of the weighted pair-witness multiset modulo \(r\). It can range from roughly \(W_r^2/r\) in a uniform situation to \(W_r^2\) when all witnesses lie in one residue class. The anchored star realizes the concentrated behavior.

Primewise Parseval for \(Z_r\),

\[
\sum_{h\bmod r}|\widehat{Z_r}(h)|^2=rs_r,
\]

is universal and does not control the relative phases in (4.2).

### Why a black-box large sieve is circular here

A classical additive large sieve would control Fourier sums of one coefficient sequence \(a_m\) by an expression involving

\[
(M+X^2)\sum_m|a_m|^2.
\]

Here the coefficient sequence

\[
a_m^{(r)}=(K(m)-f_r(m))_2
\]

depends on \(r\). More importantly,

\[
\bigl((K-f_r)_2\bigr)^2
=(K-f_r)_4+4(K-f_r)_3+2(K-f_r)_2.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus its \(L^2\)-energy already contains fourth and third factorial moments. This is exactly the overlap identity audited in `pairpalm_result.tex`. A large-sieve argument based only on (4.4) therefore asks for \(F_3/F_4\)-type control in order to prove \(F_3\); it does not close the base case.

The required new input must control the **mixed orientation** of the witness measure and the Apéry zero measure across distinct characteristics, not merely the Fourier norm of either one separately.

## 5. The reflection-pair cosine and the centered zero coordinate

Suppose, for the moment, that

\[
Z_p=\{z,p-1-z\}.
\]

For a frequency \(a\bmod r\), define the cross-characteristic transform

\[
S_{p;r}(a)
=
e_r(az)+e_r(a(p-1-z)).
\]

Let

\[
d_p=2z-p+1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
S_{p;r}(a)
=
2\exp\left(\frac{\pi i a(p-1)}r\right)
\cos\left(\frac{\pi a d_p}{r}\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This identity is correct, but it does not produce cancellation by itself.

1. The relevant frequency \(a\) is coupled to both anchor primes and the canonical-representative quotient from (2.4).
2. The cutoff \(c_{p,q}(z_p,z_q)<M\) selects zero pairs in a way correlated with the same arithmetic.
3. The prefactor in (5.1), not only the cosine, contributes to the complex phase.
4. In an absolute-value sum, the mean of \(|\cos|\) is positive, so averaging the centered coordinate cannot create cancellation.
5. Even if \(a\) ranged uniformly over all residues, Parseval would give an RMS statement, not the low-frequency, cutoff-weighted bilinear estimate needed here.

### What is known about \(d_p\)?

Reflection gives the sign symmetry \(d_p\leftrightarrow-d_p\). It does not give horizontal equidistribution as \(p\) varies.

Moreover,

\[
|d_p|\le p-1<2r
\]

for \(p,r\in(X,2X]\). Thus reduction modulo \(2r\) is essentially just a repackaging of the signed integer \(d_p\); asking for its equidistribution modulo the varying modulus \(2r\) is a strong statement about the normalized zero location \(z/p\) across characteristics.

Nothing in the present repository proves such a theorem. The gap-polynomial and reflection results are vertical, within one characteristic. The Poisson evidence for \(s_p/2\) controls how many reflection pairs occur, not where they occur. Therefore the proposed equidistribution of

\[
2z-p+1\pmod{2r}
\]

is itself a plausible candidate for new Apéry-specific arithmetic, not an available input.

Even that location theorem would not by itself settle (4.2): one would still need joint equidistribution with the pair-dependent inverse coefficients and with the \(c_{p,q}<M\) cutoff.

## 6. The signed-sum heuristic: what is correct and what is missing

The idea that signed summation over \(r\) can recover a square-root scale is correct as a probabilistic heuristic. Two corrections are essential:

* pair witnesses at the same row are not independent trials;
* the variables \(\Delta_r\) are not independent across \(r\), even in an ideal Bernoulli model.

### Unordered normalization and the factor \(3\)

To match the \(3B_3\) in the question, temporarily use unordered anchors and set

\[
B_2
=
M\sum_{p<q}\rho_p\rho_q,
\qquad
B_3
=
M\sum_{p<q<r}\rho_p\rho_q\rho_r.
\]

For fixed \(r\), the independent pair baseline excluding \(r\) is

\[
b_{2,-r}
=
M\sum_{\substack{p<q\\p,q\ne r}}\rho_p\rho_q.
\]

Then the exact combinatorial identity is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r\rho_r b_{2,-r}=3B_3.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus, if \(\operatorname{Var}\Delta_r\) were approximately
\(ho_rb_{2,-r}\) and the off-diagonal covariance were negligible in aggregate, the total variance would indeed be of order \(3B_3\).

### The individual variance is weighted by squared multiplicity

For unordered anchors define

\[
w_r(m)=\binom{K(m)-f_r(m)}2
\]

and

\[
\delta_r
=
\sum_{m<M}w_r(m)(f_r(m)-\rho_r).
\]

This is one half of the ordered discrepancy, up to the periodic/finite marginal choice.

In an ideal model where, conditional on all other columns, the values \(f_r(m)\) are independent Bernoulli variables of mean \(ho_r\),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\delta_r\mid\{f_p:p\ne r\})
=
\rho_r(1-\rho_r)
\sum_m w_r(m)^2.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

It is **not** generally

\[
\rho_r\sum_mw_r(m)=\rho_rW_r.
\]

The latter approximation requires almost all nonzero \(w_r(m)\) to equal \(1\), meaning that almost no row supports more than one unordered anchor pair. But excluding rows with large pair multiplicity is exactly a third/fourth-moment assertion.

Indeed,

\[
\boxed{
\binom{k}{2}^2
=
\frac14\bigl((k)_4+4(k)_3+2(k)_2\bigr).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore the diagonal variance already sees the \(F_3\) and \(F_4\) tails. In the fully independent sparse model those terms are lower order in \(\lambda_X\), so the heuristic
\(\operatorname{Var}\delta_r\sim\rho_rb_{2,-r}\) is reasonable. For the deterministic Apéry system, using it as a premise would be circular.

### The \(\delta_r\) are correlated even in the ideal Bernoulli model

Let \(r\ne s\), and at one row put

\[
L=\sum_{t\notin\{r,s\}}f_t.
\]

Then

\[
w_r=\binom{L+f_s}{2}=\binom L2+Lf_s,
\qquad
w_s=\binom{L+f_r}{2}=\binom L2+Lf_r.
\]

If \(f_r,f_s\) are independent Bernoulli variables of means
\(a=\rho_r\), \(b=\rho_s\), then conditional on \(L\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\bigl[
(f_r-a)w_r(f_s-b)w_s\mid L
\bigr]
=
a(1-a)b(1-b)L^2.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is positive, not zero. In the sparse independent model, summing (6.4) over \(r,s,m\) is nevertheless only of \(B_3\)-scale, because \(\mathbb E L^2\asymp\lambda_X+\lambda_X^2\). Thus the square-root conclusion remains plausible, but the correct statement is aggregate covariance control, not independence of the \(\Delta_r\).

### The exact covariance condition

A covariance statement requires an actual averaging operator: a probabilistic model, an average over shifts, an average over dyadic parameters, or some other specified ensemble. For a fixed deterministic \(X\), the words “variance” and “covariance” have no literal meaning.

Given an ensemble in which the \(\delta_r\) are centered, one has

\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_r\delta_r\right)
=
\sum_r\operatorname{Var}(\delta_r)
+
2\sum_{r<s}\operatorname{Cov}(\delta_r,\delta_s).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The exact sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r\operatorname{Var}(\delta_r)
+
2\sum_{r<s}\operatorname{Cov}(\delta_r,\delta_s)
\ll X^{o(1)}B_3.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

A stronger, easier-to-state sufficient pair of conditions is

\[
\sum_r\operatorname{Var}(\delta_r)
\ll X^{o(1)}B_3,
\]

\[
\sum_{r\ne s}
|\operatorname{Cov}(\delta_r,\delta_s)|
\ll X^{o(1)}B_3.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Absolute covariance summability is not necessary; only the signed aggregate in (6.6) is required.

If \(L_X\) third primes are active, (6.7) corresponds heuristically to

\[
\text{average diagonal variance}
\asymp \frac{B_3}{L_X},
\qquad
\text{average off-diagonal covariance}
\ll \frac{B_3}{L_X^2}.
\]

For the pointwise Apéry problem, the deterministic replacement for (6.6) is simply `(PW3-sqrt)`. Proving it is the missing theorem; covariance language does not reduce its arithmetic content unless one has an independently justified ensemble and a route from average to pointwise control.

## 7. Scaling audit

In the Poisson-count regime suggested by the repository data,

\[
\lambda_X\asymp\frac1{\log X},
\qquad
B_2\asymp M\lambda_X^2
\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2},
\]

\[
B_3\asymp M\lambda_X^3
\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\]

Hence

\[
\sqrt{B_3}\asymp\frac{X}{(\log X)^{3/2}}.
\]

Let \(L_X\asymp X/\log X\) be the number of relevant shell primes. Without assuming every active prime has exactly two zeros, Cauchy gives

\[
\sum_r\sqrt{\rho_r}
\le
\sqrt{L_X\sum_r\rho_r}
=
\sqrt{L_X\lambda_X}
\asymp\frac{\sqrt X}{\log X}.
\]

Therefore the absolute-value estimate based on the independent individual scale is

\[
\sum_r\sqrt{B_2\rho_r}
\ll
\sqrt{B_2}\sqrt{L_X\lambda_X}
\asymp
\frac{X^{3/2}}{(\log X)^2}.
\]

The gap from \(\sqrt{B_3}\) is therefore

\[
\asymp\sqrt{\frac{X}{\log X}},
\]

rather than exactly \(\sqrt X\). This logarithmic correction does not change the strategic conclusion: absolute values are far too expensive, while a signed variance of order

\[
B_2\sum_r\rho_r
=B_2\lambda_X
\asymp B_3
\]

lands at the desired scale.

## 8. Direct answers to the five questions

### 1. Is the small set of possible \(m_0\bmod r\) values typically disjoint from \(Z_r\)?

Under an independent-uniform heuristic, yes: the expected hit probability per witness is \(s_r/r=O(1/X)\). There is no current theorem proving this for Apéry pair witnesses. Moreover, the displayed four-point formula is not the correct formula for \(m_0\bmod r\), and \(s_p\le2\) is not true in general.

### 2. Are the pair-witness residues equidistributed modulo \(r\)?

Not from any currently banked input. The exact object is the weighted measure \(
u_r\) in (1.1). Its concentration is controlled by row multiplicities \((K-f_r)_2\), so its equidistribution is already a high-moment question. The anchored-star construction makes every \(
u_r\) concentrate at the same residue and gives all \(\Delta_r\) the same positive sign while satisfying the known local Apéry-style constraints.

### 3. Can one bound the proposed product sums on average over \(r\)?

The proposed product factorization is invalid because the canonical CRT quotient and the cutoff couple \(z_p\) and \(z_q\). A trivial absolute-value bound exists but is of full pair-mass size and gives no cancellation. The correct Fourier sum is (4.2). A black-box \(L^2\) or large-sieve estimate encounters the collision energy (4.4), whose coefficient norm contains \(F_3\) and \(F_4\) through (4.5), so it is circular without new arithmetic input.

### 4. Are the centered zero locations \(2z-p+1\pmod{2r}\) equidistributed?

No such result is proved in the current project. Reflection gives only the symmetry \(d\leftrightarrow-d\). The Poisson law for the number of reflection pairs says nothing about their locations. Even a location-equidistribution theorem would still need to be combined with the pair-dependent inverse coefficients and the non-negligible \(c_{p,q}<M\) cutoff.

### 5. Can the signed sum over \(r\) cancel to \(\sqrt{B_3}\), and what correlation condition is needed?

Yes, \(\sqrt{B_3}\) is the natural fully independent fluctuation scale. The factor \(3B_3\) is correct in the unordered convention by (6.1). But the individual variance is governed by squared witness multiplicity, not just witness count, and the \(\Delta_r\) have nonzero covariance even in the ideal model. The exact required condition is the aggregate covariance bound (6.6), after specifying an ensemble; a stronger sufficient condition is (6.7). For the actual deterministic Apéry system, the pointwise replacement is `(PW3-sqrt)`.

## Final assessment

The proposed route has identified the right arithmetic bottleneck but not a derivation of it.

The clean conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Pair-witness equidistribution is equivalent to a genuine
three-prime Apéry dispersion theorem.}
}
\]

It is not supplied by CRT, sparsity, reflection, Poisson zero-count statistics, primewise Fourier bounds, or a standard large sieve. The exact new Apéry input must control the cross-characteristic correlation between

* the weighted pair-witness measure

  \[
  m\longmapsto (K(m)-f_r(m))_2,
  \]

and

* the centered third-prime zero column

  \[
  m\longmapsto f_r(m)-|Z_r|/r,
  \]

with signed cancellation after summing over \(r\).

At the current \((\mathrm{HM})_3\) level, it is enough to prove the positive part of this aggregate residual is \(O(X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^3)\). The proposed \(O(\sqrt{B_3})\) estimate is the sharper Poisson-fluctuation version. Nothing presently proved in the repository rules out coherent same-sign behavior; the anchored star shows exactly why a new horizontal Apéry theorem is indispensable.
