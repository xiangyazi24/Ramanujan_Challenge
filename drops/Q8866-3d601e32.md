# Pair-witness equidistribution: the exact Apéry input behind the \(P_{2,3}^{\pm}\) residual

## Executive conclusion

The proposed route identifies the correct new arithmetic problem, but several structural corrections are needed.

1. A prime pair \((p,q)\) need not have one witness.  Since \(pq>M=X^2\), each **zero-residue pair** \((a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q\) has at most one representative below \(M\), but the prime pair may have as many as \(|Z_p||Z_q|\) witnesses.
2. The displayed formula for the canonical witness modulo a third prime is not valid.  A CRT idempotent expression determines a class modulo \(pq\); reducing it modulo \(r\) before selecting the representative in \([0,pq)\) loses a nonzero multiple of \(pq\bmod r\).
3. The condition \(m<M\) is not a negligible cutoff: for \(p,q\in(X,2X]\),
   
   \[
   \frac{M}{pq}\in\left(\frac14,1\right).
   \]
4. For fixed \(r\), the pair witnesses form a **weighted multiset** modulo \(r\).  A row supporting many anchor pairs contributes the same residue many times.  This within-row multiplicity is exactly the high-moment obstruction.
5. The signed-sum heuristic is sound in an ideal independent model: total fluctuation of order \(\sqrt{B_3}\) is natural.  However, the individual discrepancies are not independent even in that model, and their variances involve squared pair multiplicities, hence third and fourth factorial moments.
6. No theorem currently in the repository proves the required horizontal, cross-characteristic equidistribution.  The anchored-star construction proves that reflection, sparse zero sets, pair CRT injectivity, interval bounds, fiber bounds, and primewise Fourier estimates do not imply it.

The clean deterministic square-root target, in the ordered convention used by `pairpalm_result.tex`, is

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{\substack{r,p,q\in\mathcal P_X\\
                 r,p,q\ \text{pairwise distinct}}}
\ \sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}
\left(f_r(m)-\frac{|Z_r|}{r}\right)
\right|
\ll X^{o(1)}\sqrt{M\lambda_X^3}.
}
\tag{PW3-sqrt}
\]

Here

\[
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},\qquad
\Omega_p=\{m<M:m\bmod p\in Z_p\},
\]

\[
f_p(m)=1_{\Omega_p}(m),\qquad
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
\]

This is stronger than what is needed for \((\mathrm{HM})_3\): a one-sided bound of order \(M\lambda_X^3\) already suffices there.

## 1. Exact witness bookkeeping

Put

\[
s_p=|Z_p|,\qquad
A_p=|\Omega_p|,\qquad
\alpha_p=\frac{A_p}{M},\qquad
\bar\alpha_p=\frac{s_p}{p},
\]

and

\[
K(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}f_p(m).
\]

For ordered \(p\ne q\), let

\[
J_{p,q}=|\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q|.
\]

If \(c_{p,q}(a,b)\in[0,pq)\) is the canonical CRT representative of

\[
m\equiv a\pmod p,\qquad m\equiv b\pmod q,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
J_{p,q}
=
\#\{(a,b)\in Z_p\times Z_q:c_{p,q}(a,b)<M\}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus \(0\le J_{p,q}\le s_ps_q\).  “Exactly one witness” is correct only when the anchor includes the residue pair \((a,b)\), not when it includes only the primes \((p,q)\).

The simplification \(s_p\in\{0,1,2\}\) is also false for the actual Apéry zero sets.  Reflection decomposes \(Z_p\) into pairs, but there may be several pairs.  The checked data in `centered_residual_verify.py`, for example, contain

\[
Z_{499}=\{67,170,203,295,328,431\}.
\]

### The fixed-\(r\) witness measure

For a third prime \(r\), define the ordered pair-witness multiplicity at \(u\bmod r\) by

\[
\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q\in\mathcal P_X\setminus\{r\}\\p\ne q}}
\#\{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q:m\equiv u\pmod r\}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_{\substack{m<M\\m\equiv u\pmod r}}
(K(m)-f_r(m))_2.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

A row with \(k\) other active primes contributes \((k)_2\) copies of one residue.  Hence the relevant object is not an unweighted set of \(J_r\) samples.

Set

\[
W_r=\sum_{u\bmod r}\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_{m<M}(K(m)-f_r(m))_2,
\]

\[
H_r=\sum_{u\in Z_r}\nu_r(u)
=
\sum_{m<M}f_r(m)(K(m)-f_r(m))_2.
\]

The complete-period and exact-finite-marginal discrepancies are

\[
\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}=H_r-\bar\alpha_rW_r,
\qquad
\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}=H_r-\alpha_rW_r.
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Exact aggregate identities

Let

\[
F_3(X)=\sum_{m<M}(K(m))_3.
\]

At a row with \(K(m)=k\), summing over the \(k\) possible third primes gives

\[
\sum_r f_r(m)(k-1)_2=(k)_3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_rH_r=F_3(X).
\]

Writing

\[
\lambda_{p,q}=
\sum_{r\notin\{p,q\}}\bar\alpha_r,
\qquad
\mu_{p,q}=
\sum_{r\notin\{p,q\}}\alpha_r,
\]

we obtain the exact formulas

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}
=
F_3(X)-\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q},
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}
=
F_3(X)-\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}.
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The repository already proves

\[
F_2(X)=\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\le4M\lambda_X^2,
\]

so

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}J_{p,q}\lambda_{p,q}
\le4M\lambda_X^3.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Consequently

\[
\left(\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}ight)_+
\ll M\lambda_X^3
\]

is equivalent, at this scale, to \((\mathrm{HM})_3\).  The square-root target `(PW3-sqrt)` is a much sharper Poisson-fluctuation statement.

The finite and periodic versions differ only by the one-column boundary error.  Since

\[
|A_r-Ms_r/r|\le s_r,
\]

we have

\[
\left|
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}
-
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}
\right|
\le
\frac{F_2(X)}M\sum_rs_r
\le8X\lambda_X^3.
\tag{1.7}
\]

In the empirical Poisson regime \(\lambda_X\asymp1/\log X\), this is lower order even relative to \(\sqrt{M\lambda_X^3}\).

### Global signed residual versus local positive Palm excess

For ordered \(p\ne q\), define

\[
R_{p,q}
=
\sum_{m\in\Omega_p\cap\Omega_q}(K(m)-2)
-J_{p,q}\mu_{p,q}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_r\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}=\sum_{p\ne q}R_{p,q}.
\]

But the finite pair-Palm excess in `hm3_result.tex` is

\[
\mathscr P_3^{\mathrm{fin}}(X)
=
\sum_{p\ne q}(R_{p,q})_+.
\]

Thus cancellation in \(\sum_r\Delta_r\) can suffice for the global \(F_3\) bound, but it does **not** prove that most individual occupied anchor pairs have the correct extension rate.  That “most pairs” assertion is strictly stronger.

## 2. The correct CRT formula modulo a third prime

Let \(a\in Z_p\), \(b\in Z_q\), and let \(ar p_q\) be the inverse of \(p\pmod q\), represented in \([0,q)\).  Put

\[
t_{p,q}(a,b)=[(b-a)\bar p_q]_q,
\]

where \([\cdot]_q\) denotes the least nonnegative residue modulo \(q\).  Then

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,q}(a,b)=a+pt_{p,q}(a,b)\in[0,pq).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,q}(a,b)\bmod r
\equiv
a+p[(b-a)\bar p_q]_q\pmod r.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The occupancy condition is

\[
\boxed{
a+p[(b-a)\bar p_q]_q<M.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Both the least-residue operation and the cutoff couple \(a\) and \(b\).

Now let

\[
E(a,b)=aq\bar q_p+bp\bar p_q
\]

be the usual CRT idempotent expression.  It satisfies only

\[
E(a,b)\equiv c_{p,q}(a,b)\pmod{pq}.
\]

The canonical representative is

\[
\boxed{
c_{p,q}(a,b)
=
E(a,b)-pq\left\lfloor\frac{E(a,b)}{pq}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Because \(r\nmid pq\), the subtracted term does not disappear modulo \(r\).  Therefore the class modulo \(pq\) alone does not determine a residue modulo \(r\); the selected integer representative does.  This invalidates the proposed separated expression

\[
z_pA_{p,r}+z_qB_{q,r}\pmod r.
\]

There is also no coefficient depending only on \(p,r\): the inverse and the canonical quotient depend jointly on both anchor primes.

## 3. Are pair witnesses equidistributed modulo \(r\)?

### Random heuristic

If the \(W_r\) weighted witnesses were independent uniform residues modulo \(r\), then

\[
\mathbb E H_r=W_r\frac{s_r}{r}.
\]

For one witness and \(s_r=O(1)\), the hit probability is \(O(1/X)\), so “usually disjoint from \(Z_r\)” is the correct random prediction.

### What is proved for Apéry zeros

No current theorem proves this prediction.  Sparsity of \(Z_r\) only controls a uniform test residue.  It does not prove that the deterministic residues generated by other Apéry zero sets are uniform.

The anchored-star construction in `hm3_result.tex` gives a decisive no-go.  It chooses one row \(m_*\) and active reflection pairs

\[
Z_p^*=\{m_*\bmod p,\ p-1-(m_*\bmod p)\}
\]

for \(L\asymp X/\log X\) primes.  Then \(m_*\) is a witness for every active pair and is also in every active third column.

For each active \(r\),

\[
H_r^*\ge(L-1)_2,
\qquad
W_r^*\le4(L-1)_2.
\]

Since \(s_r/r=2/r\le2/X\),

\[
\Delta_r^{*,\mathrm{per}}
\ge
\left(1-\frac8X\right)(L-1)_2>0
\]

for large \(X\).  Thus all third-prime discrepancies have the same sign and

\[
\sum_r\Delta_r^{*,\mathrm{per}}\gg L^3.
\]

This family satisfies the local structural constraints currently available in the project.  Hence a new **horizontal Apéry theorem**, excluding this coherent alignment across characteristics, is indispensable.

The observed Poisson law for the number of reflection pairs does not help by itself: it records how many zeros a column has, not where those zeros are or how their positions correlate across primes.

## 4. Exact Fourier formulation

Write \(e_r(x)=\exp(2\pi ix/r)\), and define

\[
\widehat Z_r(h)=\sum_{z\in Z_r}e_r(-hz),
\qquad
\widehat\nu_r(h)=\sum_{u\bmod r}\nu_r(u)e_r(hu).
\]

By (1.2),

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\nu_r(h)
=
\sum_{m<M}(K(m)-f_r(m))_2e_r(hm).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Fourier inversion gives an exact identity, stronger and cleaner than an Erdős-Turán interval bound:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_r^{\mathrm{per}}
=
\frac1r\sum_{h=1}^{r-1}
\widehat Z_r(h)\widehat\nu_r(h).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\widehat\nu_r(h)
=
\sum_{\substack{p,q\ne r\\p\ne q}}
\sum_{a\in Z_p}\sum_{b\in Z_q}
1_{c_{p,q}(a,b)<M}
 e_r\bigl(hc_{p,q}(a,b)\bigr).
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the genuine pair-witness exponential sum.

### Why the proposed product does not occur

The phase in (4.3) contains the quotient in (2.4), and the indicator \(1_{c<M}\) contains the cutoff (2.3).  Both couple \(a\) and \(b\).  Hence the sum does not factor as a \(p\)-transform times a \(q\)-transform.

Even an artificially factorized absolute-value estimate would give only

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}|S_{p;r}S_{q;r}|
\le
\sum_{p\ne q}s_ps_q
\le
\left(\sum_ps_p\right)^2
\le4X^2\lambda_X^2,
\]

which has no cancellation and becomes worse after summing over \(r\).

Parseval gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{h\bmod r}|\widehat\nu_r(h)|^2
=
r\sum_{u\bmod r}\nu_r(u)^2.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The right side is the collision energy of the weighted witness multiset.  It ranges from near \(W_r^2/r\) in a uniform model to \(W_r^2\) under complete concentration.  The anchored star realizes the latter behavior.

A black-box large sieve is therefore circular at the base case.  Its coefficient energy contains

\[
\bigl((K-f_r)_2\bigr)^2
=(K-f_r)_4+4(K-f_r)_3+2(K-f_r)_2,
\tag{4.5}
\]

so an \(L^2\) estimate asks for third- and fourth-moment control in order to prove the third moment.  What is missing is not a primewise Parseval bound, but a mixed phase theorem controlling the relative orientation of \(\widehat Z_r\) and \(\widehat\nu_r\).

## 5. Reflection pairs and the centered zero coordinate

Suppose temporarily that

\[
Z_p=\{z,p-1-z\}.
\]

For a frequency \(a\bmod r\), the cross-characteristic transform is

\[
S_{p;r}(a)
=e_r(az)+e_r(a(p-1-z)).
\]

With

\[
d_p=2z-p+1,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
S_{p;r}(a)
=
2\exp\left(\frac{\pi ia(p-1)}r\right)
\cos\left(\frac{\pi iad_p}{r}\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Reflection supplies only the sign symmetry \(d_p\leftrightarrow-d_p\).  The project contains no theorem showing that

\[
2z-p+1\pmod{2r}
\]

is equidistributed as \(p\) and \(r\) vary.  Since \(|d_p|<2r\), such a statement is essentially a theorem about the normalized location of Apéry zeros across characteristics, not a formal consequence of reflection.

Even that location theorem would not by itself prove (4.2).  It would still have to be joint with

* the pair-dependent inverses in (2.2),
* the canonical-representative quotient in (2.4), and
* the non-negligible cutoff \(c_{p,q}<M\).

Moreover, the absolute-value sum in Question 3 cannot exploit a cosine with mean zero: after absolute values, the average of \(|\cos|\) is positive.

## 6. The signed-sum variance heuristic

The square-root conclusion is correct as an ideal-model heuristic, but the covariance structure is subtler than independent \(\Delta_r\)'s.

### The factor \(3B_3\)

Use unordered anchors for this subsection and define

\[
B_2=M\sum_{p<q}\alpha_p\alpha_q,
\qquad
B_3=M\sum_{p<q<r}\alpha_p\alpha_q\alpha_r.
\]

For fixed \(r\), put

\[
b_{2,-r}
=M\sum_{\substack{p<q\\p,q\ne r}}\alpha_p\alpha_q.
\]

Then exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r\alpha_r b_{2,-r}=3B_3.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus, if the diagonal variance for the \(r\)-slice were approximately \(\alpha_rb_{2,-r}\) and the aggregate off-diagonal covariance were of no larger order, the total variance would indeed be \(O(B_3)\).

### Why witness count alone is not the variance

Let

\[
w_r(m)=\binom{K(m)-f_r(m)}2,
\qquad
\delta_r=\sum_{m<M}w_r(m)(f_r(m)-\alpha_r).
\]

This is the unordered version of \(\Delta_r^{\mathrm{fin}}\).

In the optimistic rowwise Bernoulli model—conditional on all other columns, the values \(f_r(m)\) are independent Bernoulli variables of mean \(\alpha_r\)—one has

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\delta_r\mid\{f_p:p\ne r\})
=
\alpha_r(1-\alpha_r)\sum_mw_r(m)^2.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

It is not generally \(\alpha_r\sum_mw_r(m)\).  That simplification requires almost every nonzero witness multiplicity to equal \(1\).  But

\[
\boxed{
\binom{k}{2}^2
=
\frac14\bigl((k)_4+4(k)_3+2(k)_2\bigr),
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

so squared pair multiplicity already detects the third- and fourth-moment tails.  Using the naive variance formula without first controlling those tails is circular.

The actual fixed-residue model has additional dependence between rows congruent modulo \(r\), so (6.2) is an optimistic simplification, not a theorem about the Apéry columns.

### The third-prime slices are correlated even in the ideal model

Let \(r\ne s\), and at one row write

\[
L=\sum_{t\notin\{r,s\}}f_t.
\]

Then

\[
w_r=\binom L2+Lf_s,
\qquad
w_s=\binom L2+Lf_r.
\]

If \(f_r,f_s\) are independent Bernoulli variables of means \(a,b\), then conditional on \(L\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
(f_r-a)w_r(f_s-b)w_s\mid L
\right]
=a(1-a)b(1-b)L^2.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This covariance contribution is positive, not zero.  In the sparse independent model its aggregate remains of \(B_3\)-order, so the desired square-root scale is still plausible; independence across \(r\) is simply the wrong formal condition.

### The exact covariance condition

Covariance requires a specified averaging ensemble: for example random zero sets, random shifts, or an average over parameters.  For fixed deterministic Apéry data at one \(X\), “variance” is only a heuristic language.

In any ensemble in which the \(\delta_r\) are centered,

\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_r\delta_r\right)
=
\sum_r\operatorname{Var}(\delta_r)
+
2\sum_{r<s}\operatorname{Cov}(\delta_r,\delta_s).
\]

The exact sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_r\operatorname{Var}(\delta_r)
+
2\sum_{r<s}\operatorname{Cov}(\delta_r,\delta_s)
\ll X^{o(1)}B_3.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

A stronger sufficient condition is

\[
\sum_r\operatorname{Var}(\delta_r)
\ll X^{o(1)}B_3,
\]

\[
\sum_{r\ne s}
|\operatorname{Cov}(\delta_r,\delta_s)|
\ll X^{o(1)}B_3.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Absolute covariance summability is not necessary; only the signed aggregate in (6.5) is required.

For the actual deterministic problem, the direct replacement for (6.5) is `(PW3-sqrt)`.  Introducing a probabilistic ensemble is useful only if one can subsequently transfer its average estimate to the fixed Apéry data.

## 7. Scaling check

In the empirical sparse regime

\[
\lambda_X\asymp\frac1{\log X},
\]

one expects

\[
B_2\asymp M\lambda_X^2
\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2},
\qquad
B_3\asymp M\lambda_X^3
\asymp\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^3}.
\]

Hence

\[
\sqrt{B_3}\asymp\frac{X}{(\log X)^{3/2}}.
\]

If \(L_X\asymp X/\log X\) shell primes are active, Cauchy gives

\[
\sum_r\sqrt{\alpha_r}
\le
\sqrt{L_X\sum_r\alpha_r}
\asymp\frac{\sqrt X}{\log X}.
\]

Thus the absolute-value route based on the individual scale \(\sqrt{B_2\alpha_r}\) gives

\[
\sum_r\sqrt{B_2\alpha_r}
\ll
\frac{X^{3/2}}{(\log X)^2}.
\]

Its loss relative to \(\sqrt{B_3}\) is

\[
\asymp\sqrt{\frac{X}{\log X}},
\]

not exactly \(\sqrt X\).  The logarithmic correction does not change the strategic conclusion: absolute values are far too costly, whereas signed variance \(B_2\sum_r\alpha_r\asymp B_3\) lands at the desired scale.

## 8. Answers to the questions

### 1. Is the small set of possible witness residues typically disjoint from \(Z_r\)?

Yes under an independent-uniform heuristic, with hit probability \(s_r/r\) per witness.  No such Apéry theorem is currently proved.  Also, the proposed four-point formula is not the canonical witness formula, and actual \(Z_p\)'s can contain more than one reflection pair.

### 2. Are the pair-witness residues equidistributed modulo \(r\)?

This is exactly the new three-prime problem.  The correct object is the weighted measure \(
u_r\) in (1.2).  Current primewise and pairwise inputs do not control it; the anchored star gives a countermodel satisfying the known local constraints but concentrating every \(
u_r\) at one common row.

### 3. Can the proposed product exponential sums be bounded on average over \(r\)?

The proposed product factorization is invalid because selecting the canonical CRT representative and imposing \(c<M\) couples the two zero residues.  The correct Fourier identity is (4.2).  A trivial absolute-value bound has full pair-mass size, while a black-box \(L^2\) large sieve encounters \(F_3\) and \(F_4\) through (4.5) and is circular.

### 4. Are \(2z-p+1\pmod{2r}\) equidistributed for Apéry zeros?

No result of this kind is presently established.  Reflection gives only sign symmetry.  The Poisson law for \(|Z_p|\) concerns counts, not locations.  Even location equidistribution alone would not handle the inverse coefficients and the cutoff.

### 5. Can the signed sum cancel to \(\sqrt{B_3}\), and what correlation condition is needed?

Yes, \(\sqrt{B_3}\) is the natural independent-model scale, and the factor \(3B_3\) in the unordered normalization follows from (6.1).  But the slices are not independent, and individual variance is weighted by squared witness multiplicity.  The exact ensemble condition is the aggregate covariance estimate (6.5); the stronger absolute-covariance condition (6.6) also suffices.  Pointwise for the deterministic Apéry system, the needed theorem is `(PW3-sqrt)`.

---

## Final assessment

Pair-witness equidistribution is not a consequence of CRT.  It is a precise reformulation of a missing cross-characteristic Apéry dispersion theorem: the weighted pair-witness measure

\[
m\longmapsto(K(m)-f_r(m))_2
\]

must be asymptotically orthogonal, after summing over \(r\), to the centered third-prime column

\[
m\longmapsto f_r(m)-\frac{|Z_r|}{r}.
\]

The weaker one-sided \(O(M\lambda_X^3)\) version closes \((\mathrm{HM})_3\); the proposed \(O(\sqrt{M\lambda_X^3})\) version is the sharper Poisson-fluctuation target.  Nothing currently proved in the repository excludes coherent same-sign alignment, and the anchored-star obstruction shows exactly why genuinely new horizontal Apéry arithmetic is required.
