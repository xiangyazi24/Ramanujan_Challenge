*No presently standard theorem proves any of (A), (B), or (C) at the required scale.*

As formulations of the next missing estimate, I rank them

\[
\boxed{\text{(C) marked arithmetic gcd} \;>\; \text{(A) full Hasse/crystalline period} \;>\; \text{(B) conditional state equidistribution}.}
\]

There is an important qualification. A **raw height proof** of (C) still loses the same factor of \(H\) as the exhausted resultant/content routes. The most plausible engine for proving the correctly formulated version of (C) is therefore the **full-\(p\) marked Hasse/crystalline object** from (A), not ordinary algebraic geometry applied to the zero set of a polynomial. Thus the recommended route is best described as

\[
\boxed{\text{state-marked gcd statement (C), proved—if possible—by a full crystalline Mellin dispersion theorem (A).}}
\]

(B) should be regarded as a possible *consequence* of such a theorem, not as an independent starting point.

The concrete lemma below is not short-window reflection, does not claim that formal projective chains are centered, and allows exceptional off-center actual chains. It is a prime-averaged third factorial-moment estimate for the **distinguished Apéry fiber**. It implies the required average square-root bound by a short deterministic block argument.

# 1. The exact state mark and why it removes every phantom

Let

\[
W_n=b_n c_{n+1}-b_{n+1}c_n.
\]

Using the recurrence for both \(b\) and \(c\),

\[
W_n=\frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3}W_{n-1},
\qquad W_0=b_0c_1-b_1c_0=1.
\]

Hence

\[
W_n=\frac1{(n+1)^3}.
\]

Therefore, over \(\mathbf F_p\), \(W_n\ne0\) for \(0\le n\le p-2\). In particular, every row \((b_n,c_n)\) with \(0\le n<p\) is nonzero, and

\[
b_n=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \pi_n=[0:1].
\]

Now suppose \(0\le r<r+h<p\). By the given continuant criterion,

\[
N_h(r)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \pi_r=\pi_{r+h}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r\ 	ext{and}\ p\mid N_h(r)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p\mid b_r\ 	ext{and}\ p\mid b_{r+h}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

The forward implication is the decisive one: \(p\mid b_r\) pins the repeated projective line to the marked line \([0:1]\), rather than to an arbitrary common fixed line. Thus a tuple

\[
p\mid b_r,\quad
p\mid N_{h_1}(r),\quad
p\mid N_{h_2}(r),\quad
p\mid N_{h_3}(r)
\]

with \(r+h_3<p\) is **exactly** a genuine four-zero tuple

\[
b_r=b_{r+h_1}=b_{r+h_2}=b_{r+h_3}=0\pmod p.
\]

No carrier-clean \(q\ne0\) phantom contributes. This exclusion happens before any elimination, saturation, resultant, content, or Fitting operation. It is therefore not another GL\(_2\)-invariant recurrence identity.

# 2. Ranking the three proposed routes

## 2.1. First: (C), but only as a marked moving-gcd/prime-radical estimate

(C) is the only proposal that inserts the fixed initial state at the exact place where the formal projective model loses it. Equation (1) shows that the four integer quantities

\[
b_r,\quad N_{h_1}(r),\quad N_{h_2}(r),\quad N_{h_3}(r)
\]

form a perfect anti-phantom certificate.

However, retaining \(b_r\) does **not** by itself repair the height problem. Put

\[
V_{r,H}=\prod_{1\le h\le H}N_h(r).
\]

The continuant recurrence gives the elementary height estimate

\[
\log |N_h(r)|\ll h\log(r+h+2),
\]

and therefore

\[
\sum_{r<2X}\log |V_{r,H}|
 \ll XH^2\log X.
\tag{2}
\]

Even for the weaker event “at least three returns occur,” a prime with three distinct returning gaps only gives \(v_p(V_{r,H})\ge3\). Bounding the relevant prime support by \(\log |V_{r,H}|\) therefore yields the \(XH^2\)-scale in (2), whereas the needed state-marked estimate is \(XH\) up to subpowers. At \(H\asymp\sqrt X\), this is exactly the fatal extra factor \(H\).

Using \(b_r\) only through the trivial inequality

\[
\log\gcd(b_r,V_{r,H})\le \log|V_{r,H}|
\]

does not save anything. Using the absolute height of \(b_r\) is worse: \(\log b_r=O(r)\), so summing its height to \(X\) is already of order \(X^2\). Standard generic resultants that eliminate \(r\) simply return to the content/local-length obstruction already identified in Q7133--Q7138.

Thus (C) ranks first because its **statement** is exactly right and phantom-free, not because a known moving-gcd theorem proves it. The missing content is a nontrivial, state-sensitive cancellation theorem for the prime radical of this four-way gcd.

## 2.2. Second: (A), provided “Hasse polynomial” means the full marked crystalline period

Let

\[
A(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n,
\qquad
A_p(t)=\sum_{0\le n<p}b_nt^n\in\mathbf F_p[t].
\]

The full Dwork congruence

\[
\frac{A(t)}{A(t^p)}\equiv A_p(t)\pmod p
\]

retains the distinguished holomorphic Apéry solution. This is genuinely different from a fixed-depth Cartier calculation: it contains \(p\) coefficients and therefore has \(p\)-scale memory.

There is also substantial extra structure. For \(p\ge5\), the recent Beukers--Vlasenko factorization gives

\[
A_p(t)=
\begin{cases}
B_p(t)^2,
 &p\equiv1,7,17,23\pmod{24},\\[2mm]
(t^2-34t+1)B_p(t)^2,
 &p\equiv5,11,13,19\pmod{24}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

This is useful evidence that the marked period descends to rank-two geometry. It is not, however, a coefficient-zero theorem. A square polynomial can have arbitrarily long or prescribed coefficient gaps, and (3) by itself only converts the \(b_n\) into quadratic convolution identities among the coefficients of \(B_p\).

The more fundamental obstruction is that algebraic geometry naturally controls **values** of \(A_p(t)\), whereas \(Z(p)\) concerns **coefficients** of \(A_p\). Coefficient extraction can be rewritten as a multiplicative Mellin transform. Up to the harmless \(n=0,p-1\) boundary, one has a Teichmüller/Mellin expression of the form

\[
b_n\equiv
\frac1{p-1}\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
 [A_p(u)]\,\omega(u)^{-n}\pmod p.
\tag{4}
\]

But \(b_n\equiv0\pmod p\) asks for **\(p\)-adic divisibility** of the crystalline sum in (4). Deligne-type bounds for complex or \(\ell\)-adic trace sums do not imply such divisibility. The characters in (4) also live in a cyclotomic field whose degree grows with \(p\), so an archimedean \(O(\sqrt p)\) bound on each conjugate does not turn divisibility by a prime above \(p\) into ordinary equality. If one instead represents \(n\mapsto b_n\) directly by interpolation, the degree/conductor is of order \(p\), which destroys the uniformity needed for \(H\asymp\sqrt p\).

Therefore a conventional “count points on a bounded-complexity variety and invoke Weil” implementation of (A) fails. The viable part of (A) is narrower and more novel: retain the canonical holomorphic line and prove a **crystalline Mellin large-sieve/dispersion theorem** for its Kummer twists.

## 2.3. Third: (B), because the conditioning is essentially the problem

For a fixed triple \(\mathbf h=(h_1,h_2,h_3)\), let

\[
V_{p,\mathbf h}
 =\{x\in\mathbf F_p:
   N_{h_1}(x)=N_{h_2}(x)=N_{h_3}(x)=0\}.
\]

A formal common root carries a repeated projective line. There are two different notions that can be conflated here:

1. A low-degree *formal* fixed-line coordinate obtained from the short transfer matrices. It is basis/gauge dependent. Equidistributing that coordinate is GL\(_2\)-universal and does not identify the global Apéry line.
2. The genuinely marked coordinate \(b_x/c_x\), whose zero is the fixed initial-state condition. This uses the entire orbit from \(0\) to \(x\) and has \(p\)-scale complexity when \(x<p\).

The phantom scan demonstrates exactly this distinction: recurrence-only common-root data permit many clean primitive chains, while the distinguished orbit rejects them.

There is also no large sample inside a single \(V_{p,\mathbf h}\): for a typical triple the common-root set is tiny. Any useful “equidistribution” must average simultaneously over \(p\), \(x\), and the shifts. Once the coordinate is the true marked coordinate, proving that it avoids \(0\) on these conditioned common-root schemes is nearly the same as proving the desired marked factorial-moment bound. No bounded-conductor sheaf or independent mixing theorem is currently available to break that circularity.

Thus (B) is a good heuristic and a good computational diagnostic, but not presently a proof architecture. A marked crystalline dispersion theorem would imply the required nonconcentration; it is not clear how to obtain the dispersion theorem from (B).

# 3. One concrete lemma that implies the prime-average bound

All \(b_r\) and \(N_h(r)\) in the following statement are the integer sequences/polynomials before reduction modulo \(p\).

For real \(X\ge2\), integers \(r\ge0\) and \(1\le h_1<h_2<h_3\), define the restricted squarefree radical

\[
\mathcal R_X(r;h_1,h_2,h_3)
=
\prod_{\substack{
 p\ \mathrm{prime},\ X<p\le2X,\\
 p>r+h_3,\\
 p\mid b_r,\ p\mid N_{h_1}(r),\ p\mid N_{h_2}(r),\ p\mid N_{h_3}(r)}}p.
\tag{5}
\]

The carrier condition \(p>r+h_3\) is part of the definition; it prevents singular recurrence steps and ensures that (1) applies.

## Marked four-return radical lemma (M4R)

For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there are constants \(C_\varepsilon\) and \(X_\varepsilon\) such that, for every real \(X\ge X_\varepsilon\) and every integer

\[
3\le H\le X^{1/2},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{0\le r<2X}
\ \sum_{1\le h_1<h_2<h_3\le H}
 \log \mathcal R_X(r;h_1,h_2,h_3)
 \le C_\varepsilon X^{1+\varepsilon}H.
}
\tag{M4R}
\]

This is a precise arithmetic-gcd statement. It is not a formal projective assertion and it is not a statement about all solutions of the recurrence.

There is an equivalent finite-field factorial-moment form. Define

\[
R_{p,r}(H)
=
\#\{\,h:
1\le h\le H,\ r+h\le p-1,\ N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod p\,\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Then, by expanding (5) prime by prime,

\[
\sum_{0\le r<2X}
\sum_{h_1<h_2<h_3\le H}
\log \mathcal R_X(r;h_1,h_2,h_3)
=
\sum_{X<p\le2X}(\log p)
\sum_{\substack{0\le r<p\\b_r\equiv0\ (p)}}
\binom{R_{p,r}(H)}3.
\tag{7}
\]

The equality is exact, not an inequality: every choice of three returning gaps is one prime divisor in one restricted radical. By (1), the inner binomial counts choices of three later **actual \(b\)-zeros** within distance \(H\) of the actual zero \(r\).

# 4. Deduction of the average square-root bound from M4R

Fix a prime \(p\in(X,2X]\). Partition the integer interval \([0,p-1]\) into consecutive blocks of \(H+1\) positions. Let \(J_p=\lceil p/(H+1)\rceil\), and let \(k_j\) be the number of \(b\)-zeros in the \(j\)-th block.

For every integer \(k\ge0\),

\[
k\le3+\binom{k}{4}.
\]

Therefore

\[
Z(p)=\sum_jk_j
\le3J_p+\sum_j\binom{k_j}{4}.
\tag{8}
\]

Every four-element zero subset inside one block has a least element \(r\), and its other three elements have distinct offsets

\[
1\le h_1<h_2<h_3\le H.
\]

By (1), that subset contributes to \(\binom{R_{p,r}(H)}3\). Hence

\[
\sum_j\binom{k_j}{4}
\le
\sum_{\substack{0\le r<p\\b_r\equiv0\ (p)}}
\binom{R_{p,r}(H)}3.
\tag{9}
\]

Combining (8), (9), and (7), and using \(\log p\ge\log X\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)
&\le
3\sum_{X<p\le2X}\left(\frac{p}{H+1}+1\right)
+
\frac1{\log X}
\sum_{X<p\le2X}(\log p)
\sum_{b_r\equiv0}\binom{R_{p,r}(H)}3\\
&\ll
\frac{X^2}{H\log X}
+
\frac{C_\varepsilon X^{1+\varepsilon}H}{\log X}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Take \(H=\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{X<p\le2X}Z(p)
\ll_\varepsilon
\frac{X^{3/2+\varepsilon}}{\log X}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

After changing \(\varepsilon\), this is \(X^{3/2+o(1)}\); dividing by the number of primes in the dyadic interval gives mean \(X^{1/2+o(1)}\). Dyadic summation gives the corresponding cumulative estimate up to \(X\).

This deduction uses neither reflection nor a classification of individual four-zero windows. M4R may hold even if infinitely many exceptional off-center actual chains exist. It only requires that marked short clusters have the stated aggregate third factorial moment.

# 5. Why M4R is a plausible *new* target, but not currently proved

M4R has three advantages over the dead routes.

First, its support is exact. The factor \(b_r\) removes all \(q\ne0\) phantom fibers before compression.

Second, it asks only for prime-average control. A finite or thin exceptional set of bad triples/primes is harmless.

Third, it is a squarefree support estimate, not an identity in \(\mathbf Z[x]\). It leaves room for cancellation/orthogonality across primes and shifts that a deterministic resultant height bound discards.

Nevertheless, no standard gcd theorem appears to supply (M4R). The sequence \(b_r\) is a holonomic/G-function coefficient sequence with a variable coefficient recurrence, the shifts grow to \(H\asymp\sqrt X\), and the other entries are continuants whose degrees and heights also grow with \(H\). Existing fixed-map arithmetic-dynamical gcd results and fixed-recursion Subspace Theorem arguments do not provide the necessary uniformity. The generic height estimate (2) shows exactly where a direct proof stops.

The missing mechanism should compress the fixed initial state **\(p\)-adically**, rather than through the enormous integer height of \(b_r\).

# 6. A genuinely different invariant: the marked holomorphic Frobenius line

The recurrence alone supplies a rank-two solution space and is GL\(_2\)-invariant. The extra datum that selects \(b\) is the canonical regular/holomorphic Apéry period at \(t=0\). The proposed invariant is the pair

\[
\boxed{(\ell_{\mathrm{hol}},\Phi_p),}
\]

where \(\ell_{\mathrm{hol}}\) is the integral Hodge line spanned by the normalized holomorphic period \(A(t)\), and \(\Phi_p\) is the full Frobenius on the corresponding Dwork crystal. The scalar Frobenius matrix coefficient on this marked line is represented modulo \(p\) by \(A_p(t)\).

This datum has exactly the required properties:

* It distinguishes \(b=(1,5)\) from a generic linear combination of \(b\) and \(c\).
* It is invariant under changing an auxiliary basis while retaining the marked line.
* It uses the full \(p\)-term Frobenius truncation, so it is not a fixed-depth Cartier law.
* It contains no gauge-universal companion label.
* Its Mellin coefficients recover the coefficient-indexed values \(b_r\pmod p\).

A convenient coordinate-free local detector is the **marked Frobenius congruence length**. Choose the canonical integral Frobenius/Mellin coefficients \(\mu_p(n)\) on \(\ell_{\mathrm{hol}}\), normalized so that

\[
\mu_p(n)\equiv b_n\pmod p
\qquad(0\le n<p).
\]

For a shift triple define

\[
\lambda_p(r;h_1,h_2,h_3)
=
\operatorname{length}_{\mathbf Z_p}
\frac{\mathbf Z_p}
 {(\mu_p(r),\mu_p(r+h_1),\mu_p(r+h_2),\mu_p(r+h_3))}.
\tag{12}
\]

Multiplying the marked line by a \(p\)-adic unit does not change (12). Moreover,

\[
\lambda_p(r;h_1,h_2,h_3)\ge1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b_r=b_{r+h_1}=b_{r+h_2}=b_{r+h_3}=0\pmod p.
\]

This is not the generic Fitting/content construction that eliminated \(x\) and summed local Artin lengths over all formal fibers. Here no variable is eliminated: the length is attached to the canonical Hodge line and is exactly the marked event.

# 7. Plausible proof chain for M4R

The following is a research program, not a currently available theorem. The unsupported step is stated explicitly.

### Step 1: pass from integer height to the marked Frobenius coefficient

For \(p>r+h_3\), replace the condition \(p\mid b_{r+h_i}\) by divisibility of the corresponding canonical Mellin coefficient \(\mu_p(r+h_i)\). This preserves the fixed initial state without paying \(\log b_r\asymp r\).

### Step 2: use coefficient shifts as Kummer twists

In the Mellin representation (4), the shift \(n\mapsto n+h\) is multiplication of the Mellin character by the Kummer character \(\omega^h\). Thus the four coefficients in (12) are four nearby Kummer twists of one marked Frobenius object. This is the correct two-index structure: the index shift is not encoded by a fixed-depth Cartier state, but by a family of character twists of the full \(p\)-scale object.

### Step 3: descend through the square-root geometry

Use (3) to replace the rank-four-looking Apéry Hasse object by the rank-two object underlying \(B_p\), separating the fixed quadratic factor \(t^2-34t+1\) in the four residue classes where it occurs. The already-established self-twist rigidity should then identify the genuine diagonal/reflection Kummer coincidences and prevent a large family of hidden constant twists.

The square factorization alone is insufficient; what is needed here is a compatible **integral crystalline realization** of the square root and of its Kummer twists.

### Step 4: prove a marked crystalline Mellin dispersion inequality

This is the genuinely new step. One needs an integral, prime-varying large-sieve estimate that bounds the support of simultaneous \(p\)-divisibility for four Kummer-twisted Mellin coefficients. In the notation above it must yield

\[
\sum_{X<p\le2X}(\log p)
\sum_{r<p}
\mathbf 1_{\mu_p(r)\in p\mathbf Z_p}
\binom{
\#\{h\le H:\mu_p(r+h)\in p\mathbf Z_p\}
}{3}
\ll_\varepsilon X^{1+\varepsilon}H.
\tag{13}
\]

The two independent shift differences among \(h_1,h_2,h_3\) are where a dispersion argument would have to save the two powers of \(H\) lost by an \(H^3\) enumeration. The diagonal/self-twist terms should be isolated geometrically; the off-diagonal terms would require square-root cancellation in a genuinely **crystalline congruence** correlation.

Ordinary \(\ell\)-adic Deligne estimates do not prove (13), because (13) asks whether a \(p\)-adic Frobenius matrix coefficient lies in \(p\mathbf Z_p\), not for an archimedean bound on that coefficient. This is the exact present gap. One needs a new integral/crystalline large sieve, or an equivalent theorem controlling the support of the local lengths (12).

### Step 5: translate back to M4R

Once (13) is available, (1) identifies its support with the four-way radicals (5), giving M4R and then (11).

This chain genuinely uses the fixed initial state at every stage: the marked Hodge line is never replaced by the full unmarked solution space.

# 8. Exact failure points, summarized

## (A) Pure Hasse-polynomial algebraic geometry

It fails at the passage from geometric value sums to coefficient divisibility. The coefficient index is a Mellin-character parameter of size \(p\); standard bounded-conductor estimates do not control divisibility by the varying residual prime. The square/quadratic-square theorem is strong structure but does not force local coefficient-zero rigidity.

## (B) Equidistribution of the state on common-root schemes

The low-degree formal state is gauge dependent and therefore does not select \(b\). The true marked state uses the full orbit and has \(p\)-scale complexity. Conditioning on three return equations produces tiny, highly selected fibers, so ordinary equidistribution has no independent sample and is essentially equivalent to the desired nonconcentration statement.

## (C) Raw arithmetic gcd/height

It has the correct support, but the elementary continuant height is \(XH^2\log X\), and adding \(b_r\) through a trivial gcd inequality does not produce the required \(XH\) bound. Generic elimination revives the already-failed content/local-length route. The missing ingredient is a nontrivial prime-average theorem for the marked gcd, most plausibly supplied by the full crystalline Mellin object rather than by integer heights.

# 9. Recommendation

The next theorem should be formulated as M4R, not as another recurrence-only short-window theorem. It is exact, falsifiable, phantom-free, and immediately sufficient.

The corresponding research object should be the full marked Frobenius line \((\ell_{\mathrm{hol}},\Phi_p)\) and its Kummer-twisted Mellin coefficients. The decisive question is:

> Can one construct an integral rank-two crystalline/Kummer family for the square root \(B_p\) whose off-diagonal four-Mellin congruence correlations satisfy the dispersion estimate (13)?

A negative answer to that construction would close the Hasse route cleanly. A positive answer would supply precisely the missing \(H^2\) shift cancellation and prove M4R, hence the desired prime-average square-root bound.

At present, step 4 is not justified by a known theorem. Therefore the audit does **not** claim that the target is proved. It identifies (C) as the right state-sensitive lemma, (A) as the only visible mechanism with enough structure to attack it, and (B) as a heuristic consequence rather than an independent route.
