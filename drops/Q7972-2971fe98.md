# Sparse-density audit of the HM3 diagonal triple term

## 1. Normalization of the HM3 quantity

Let
\[
K(m)=\sum_{X<p\le 2X}1_{m\bmod p\in Z(p)},\qquad z_p=Z(p)/p,
\]
and write the centered variables
\[
g_p(m)=1_{m\bmod p\in Z(p)}-z_p.
\]
The HM3 positive-part estimate concerns a third moment of the centered sum, not an arbitrary norm:
\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(\sum_p g_p(m))_+^3
\]
(or its equivalent expansion after removing the lower-order terms). The target scale is
\[
X^2\lambda^3,\qquad \lambda=\sum_p z_p.
\]
Therefore a diagonal contribution must be compared against $X^2\lambda^3$, not against a stronger unnormalized cubic energy.

For a fixed prime triple $p<q<\ell$, the CRT intersection term is
\[
A_{pq\ell}=\sum_{m<X^2}g_p(m)g_q(m)g_\ell(m).
\]
The diagonal-square contribution after expanding the square of the triple term has the shape
\[
D=4\sum_{p<q<\ell}A_{pq\ell}^2
\left(\frac1{Z(p)}+\frac1{Z(q)}+\frac1{Z(\ell)}\right).
\]
This normalization comes from the positive-part diagonalization; replacing it by $\sum A_{pq\ell}^2$ loses the essential $Z(p)^{-1}$ gain.

## 2. What sparse lambda alone gives

Assume only the available inputs:

* $Z(p)\le p^{2/3+o(1)}$;
* $\sum_p z_p=\lambda$;
* $M=X^2$;
* $\sum_m(K)_2\le 5M\lambda^2$.

The first moment gives
\[
\#\{m:K(m)>0\}\le M\lambda.
\]
This does not control the third centered moment because concentration on a much smaller support can compensate for the small support size.

The second moment gives
\[
\sum_mK(m)^2\ll M\lambda^2+M\lambda.
\]
In the sparse range $\lambda<X^{-1/2+o(1)}$, the diagonal $M\lambda$ term is larger than the desired HM3 scale:
\[
M\lambda=X^2\lambda \gg X^2\lambda^3.
\]
Hence the second moment cannot close HM3.

## 3. Maximum multiplicity does not close it

From $Z(p)\le p^{2/3+o(1)}$ we get only
\[
\max_m K(m)\le \sum_p1_{m\bmod p\in Z(p)},
\]
with no nontrivial bound supplied by the hypotheses. Even a hypothetical bound $K(m)\le L$ gives
\[
\sum_mK(m)^3\le L\sum_mK(m)^2,
\]
and to reach $X^2\lambda^3$ one would need
\[
L\ll\lambda,
\]
which is impossible since $K(m)$ is integer-valued while $\lambda<1$ in the sparse regime.

Therefore support size plus maximum multiplicity cannot provide automatic closure.

## 4. Algebraic obstruction: anchored stars

The obstruction is an anchored CRT configuration. Choose a point $m_0$ and a sparse collection of primes $p_1,\dots,p_r$ such that
\[
m_0\bmod p_i\in Z(p_i)
\]
for all $i$.
The contribution of this one atom to the third moment is of order
\[
K(m_0)^3=r^3.
\]
The first and second moment constraints only see
\[
(K(m_0))_2=r(r-1),
\]
so they permit large local concentration provided the number of anchors is small enough.

For actual Apéry sets, such an obstruction is not ruled out by the stated axioms. Reflection symmetry and the no-adjacent-zero property constrain each individual $Z(p)$ but do not prohibit many distinct primes sharing CRT-compatible residues.

Thus there is no theorem deriving HM3 automatically from $\lambda<X^{-1/2+o(1)}$ using only the listed inputs.

## 5. Relation to the claimed bound $D\ll X^3\lambda^3$

A bound
\[
D\ll X^3\lambda^3
\]
compares to the HM3 target as
\[
X^3\lambda^3\le X^2\lambda^3
\]
only when an additional factor $X^{-1}$ is recovered elsewhere. The sparse condition does not supply that factor. Hence the diagonal estimate is not automatically harmless below the threshold.

The threshold $\lambda\gtrsim X^{-1/2}$ is therefore not a proof artifact: it is exactly where this diagonal normalization can be absorbed by the available global scale.

## 6. Weakest missing lemma

The weakest useful additional statement is not a full HM3 theorem. It is a no-heavy-CRT-atom estimate of the form
\[
\sum_{m<X^2}(K(m))_3\ll X^2\lambda^3,
\]
or equivalently a third factorial moment estimate
\[
\sum_m(K(m))_3\ll M\lambda^3.
\]
A more local version sufficient for the diagonal term is
\[
\sum_{p<q<\ell}\frac{A_{pq\ell}^2}{Z(p)}\ll X^2\lambda^3.
\]
This is strictly weaker than controlling all cubic energies and directly targets the missing diagonal obstruction.

## Corrected theorem

The sparse regime $\lambda<X^{-1/2+o(1)}$ is **not automatically closed** by support cardinality, maximum multiplicity, or the first/second moment bounds. The exact HM3 normalization retains a diagonal contribution that can be amplified by anchored CRT stars. A separate anti-concentration lemma controlling triple overlaps (or the normalized diagonal coefficient directly) is required.
