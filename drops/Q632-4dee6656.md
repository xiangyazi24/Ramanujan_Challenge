# Exact bounded Mellin-zero theorems: Kloosterman, Gauss/Jacobi, hypergeometric, and Apéry

## Verdict

There are two different conclusions, depending on what is meant by “the Mellin coefficient modulo \(p\).”

1. **For the raw cohomological Kloosterman Mellin transform**, the exact identity is
   
   $$
   M_{\mathrm{Kl}_2}(\chi)=g(\chi,\psi)^2
   $$
   
   up to the single global sign dictated by the trace-function convention. There are **no hidden \(H_c^0\) or \(H_c^2\) boundary corrections** for a nontrivial twist. Under reduction at the standard totally ramified prime above \(p\), this raw value is divisible by that prime for **every nontrivial character**. Thus the raw Kloosterman transform does **not** have bounded mod-\(p\) zeros.

2. **After the canonical Gross–Koblitz slope normalization**, the Kloosterman transform is a product of \(p\)-adic gamma units and has **no zeros at all**. The same is true for Gauss sums, Jacobi sums after their carry factor is removed, and Katz hypergeometric sums whose Mellin transform is one explicit Gauss-product monomial.

3. There is **no existing black-box theorem** of the form
   
   $$
   \#\{\chi:M(\mathcal F,\chi)\equiv0\pmod p\}\le C(\mathcal F)
   $$
   
   derived merely from bounded rank, bounded conductor, large monodromy, or a trivial multiplicative self-twist group. Katz’s finite set of “bad characters” and the generic-vanishing/equidistribution theorems of Katz and Forey–Fresán–Kowalski concern **cohomological concentration and complex/ℓ-adic equidistribution**, not divisibility of the Frobenius trace by the varying residue characteristic.

4. The explicit Gauss-product theorem does not presently cover Apéry. In the Apéry Gross–Koblitz expansion there are several terms of the same minimal slope. After the common slope is removed, their unit residues can cancel. The event is precisely the same-characteristic condition \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\). Controlling those cancellations is the missing crystalline Mellin anti-concentration theorem already isolated in `problems/3.2/proof.tex`.

So the comparison with Kloosterman is valuable, but it identifies the frontier rather than proving AFP: **Kloosterman is a one-monomial Mellin transform; Apéry is an equal-slope unit sum.**

## 1. “Modulo \(p\)” is not intrinsic for cyclotomic Mellin values

Before comparing the families, one must specify three pieces of data.

Let \(E\) be a coefficient field containing the values of the additive and multiplicative characters, let \(\mathfrak P\mid p\) be the prime selected by the Teichmüller embedding, and let \(v_p\) be normalized by \(v_p(p)=1\).

There are then at least three different operations that are often conflated:

- reduction modulo the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak P\), whose residue field is \(\mathbf F_p\);
- reduction modulo the principal ideal \(p\mathcal O_E\), which in a totally ramified cyclotomic extension is a much higher power of \(\mathfrak P\);
- reduction of a **slope-normalized unit**, obtained after dividing the raw value by its known Gross–Koblitz power of a uniformizer.

For rational integers these distinctions disappear. For Gauss sums and raw Kloosterman Mellin values they do not.

In the standard field \(\mathbf Q_p(\zeta_p,\mu_{p-1})\), choose \(\pi\) by

$$
\pi^{p-1}=-p,
\qquad
\pi\equiv \zeta_p-1\pmod{(\zeta_p-1)^2}.
$$

Then

$$
v_p(\pi)=\frac1{p-1},
$$

and the maximal ideal is generated by \(\pi\). Consequently a number of positive \(p\)-adic slope always reduces to zero modulo \((\pi)\), even when its slope is strictly less than \(1\) and it is not divisible by the ideal \(p\mathcal O_E=(\pi^{p-1})\).

This is the normalization issue behind the apparent Kloosterman contradiction.

## 2. (A) Exact Kloosterman Mellin identity

Fix

$$
\psi(x)=\zeta_p^x
$$

and use the unnormalized two-variable Kloosterman sum

$$
\operatorname{Kl}_2(a;\psi)
 =\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
   \psi\!\left(x+\frac a x\right)
 =\sum_{xy=a}\psi(x+y).
$$

For every multiplicative character \(\chi\) of \(\mathbf F_p^	imes\), direct rearrangement gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
 \chi(a)\operatorname{Kl}_2(a;\psi)
&=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
  \chi(xy)\psi(x+y)\\
&=\left(\sum_{x\ne0}\chi(x)\psi(x)\right)
  \left(\sum_{y\ne0}\chi(y)\psi(y)\right)\\
&=g(\chi,\psi)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
$$

This is the \(n=2\) case of Katz’s multiplicative Fourier-transform formula for Kloosterman sums; see Katz, *Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups*, Chapter 4, especially §4.0 and Scholium 4.0.1.

### 2.1 Cohomological sign and the absence of boundary terms

Katz’s standard Kloosterman sheaf convention has

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_a\mid\mathcal{Kl}_2)
=-\operatorname{Kl}_2(a;\psi).
$$

The Grothendieck–Lefschetz formula gives

$$
\sum_{a\ne0}\chi(a)
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_a\mid\mathcal{Kl}_2)
=
\sum_i(-1)^i
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
H_c^i(\mathbf G_{m,\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
\mathcal{Kl}_2\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
\right).
\tag{2.2}
$$

For a nontrivial irreducible Kloosterman twist,

$$
H_c^0=0,
\qquad
H_c^2=0.
$$

The first vanishes because a compactly supported global section of a nonzero lisse sheaf on \(\mathbf G_m\) is zero. The second is dual to invariants of the dual twist, and those invariants vanish because the twist has no constant quotient.

Therefore, in the unshifted convention,

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid
H_c^1(\mathbf G_m,
\mathcal{Kl}_2\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
\right)
=g(\chi,\psi)^2.
\tag{2.3}
$$

If one defines the trace function as \(+\operatorname{Kl}_2\) instead of Katz’s \(-\operatorname{Kl}_2\), a global minus sign moves between (2.2) and (2.3). Nothing character-dependent changes.

**Thus the proposed formula**

$$
\text{power-weighted sum}
=	ext{cohomological Mellin trace}
+	ext{boundary corrections}
$$

**is not the explanation. There are no such corrections in this case.**

### 2.2 Teichmüller powers versus ordinary integer powers

Equation (2.1) is an identity for a multiplicative character. If

$$
\chi=\omega^r,
$$

then the weight is the Teichmüller lift \(\omega(a)^r\), not the ordinary integer \(a^r\in\mathbf Z\) viewed in characteristic zero.

After choosing \(\mathfrak P\mid p\), one has

$$
\omega(a)^r\equiv a^r\pmod{\mathfrak P},
$$

so the two weights have the same residue. They are not the same algebraic number before reduction.

There is a second issue: \(\operatorname{Kl}_2(a;\psi)\) itself is generally a cyclotomic algebraic integer, not an ordinary integer. Therefore a computation described as

> compute \(\sum_a a^r\operatorname{Kl}_2(1,a;p)\) as an integer and reduce modulo \(p\)

must already be using a different realization—typically a finite-field Hasse/Bessel polynomial, a trace after reduction, or a pre-normalized coefficient sequence. It is not the raw cyclotomic sum in (2.1).

## 3. Correct Stickelberger/Gross–Koblitz valuation

Let

$$
N=p-1,
\qquad
\bar\omega=\omega^{-1}.
$$

Gross–Koblitz gives, for \(0\le j\le N-1\),

$$
g(\bar\omega^j,\psi)
=-\pi^j\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac jN\right),
\tag{3.1}
$$

where Morita’s \(p\)-adic gamma value is a unit. Hence

$$
\boxed{
 v_p\bigl(g(\bar\omega^j,\psi)\bigr)
 =\frac j{p-1}.
}
\tag{3.2}
$$

The value is **not** \(1/2\) for every nontrivial character. The familiar identity

$$
g(\chi,\psi)g(\chi^{-1},\psi)
=\chi(-1)p
$$

only implies that the two complementary slopes sum to \(1\). The individual slope equals \(1/2\) only in the self-dual quadratic case, or in an equivalent symmetric normalization.

For the Kloosterman Mellin transform,

$$
M_j:=M_{\mathrm{Kl}_2}(\bar\omega^j)
=g(\bar\omega^j,\psi)^2
=\pi^{2j}\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac jN\right)^2.
\tag{3.3}
$$

Thus, for every \(1\le j\le p-2\),

$$
M_j\equiv0\pmod{(\pi)}.
$$

So the raw transform has \(p-2\) nontrivial-character zeros modulo the maximal ideal.

If one instead asks for membership in \(p\mathcal O_E=(\pi^{p-1})\), then

$$
M_j\in p\mathcal O_E
\iff 2j\ge p-1,
$$

which gives approximately half of the characters. This is another concrete demonstration that the phrase “modulo \(p\)” must specify the integral lattice and ideal.

### 3.1 Canonical slope normalization

Remove the known Gross–Koblitz slope:

$$
\widetilde M_j
:=\pi^{-2j}M_j
=\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac jN\right)^2.
\tag{3.4}
$$

This is a \(p\)-adic unit for every \(j\). More explicitly, for \(0\le j\le p-2\), one has the standard residue

$$
\Gamma_p\!\left(\frac j{p-1}\right)
\equiv \frac1{j!}\pmod p
$$

up to the harmless sign determined by the convention for \(\pi\) and \(ar\omega\). Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\widetilde M_j
\equiv \frac1{(j!)^2}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
}
\tag{3.5}
$$

The normalized Kloosterman Mellin transform has exactly zero zeros.

The same argument works for the \(n\)-variable Kloosterman sheaf:

$$
M_{\mathrm{Kl}_n}(\chi)=g(\chi,\psi)^n,
$$

so after dividing by \(\pi^{nj}\), the normalized value is a nonzero power of a \(p\)-adic gamma unit.

### 3.2 Likely explanation of the computation

A common defining-characteristic Kloosterman/Hasse normalization is represented by the truncated Bessel polynomial

$$
\mathcal K_p(T)
=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{T^j}{(j!)^2}
\in\mathbf F_p[T].
$$

For \(1\le r\le p-2\), multiplicative orthogonality gives

$$
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
 a^r\mathcal K_p(a)
=-\frac1{((p-1-r)!)^2}\ne0.
\tag{3.6}
$$

Thus this normalized finite-field object has no interior Mellin zeros, exactly as (3.5) predicts. If the computation used this Hasse/Bessel realization, it was computing the slope-normalized transform rather than the raw cyclotomic Kloosterman trace.

## 4. (B) Which bounded-zero results are actually proved?

The correct answer is:

> A complete bounded-zero theorem is available for **explicit single Gauss-product Mellin transforms**, because Gross–Koblitz computes both their slope and their unit residue. There is no comparable general theorem for an arbitrary sheaf with the same rank, conductor, monodromy, and self-twist data.

### 4.1 Gauss sums

For \(1\le j\le p-2\),

$$
g(\bar\omega^j,\psi)
=-\pi^j\Gamma_p(j/(p-1)).
$$

Hence:

- raw reduction modulo \((\pi)\): every nontrivial character is zero;
- normalized reduction after division by \(\pi^j\): every character is nonzero.

So both exact statements are trivial once the normalization is fixed, but they are opposite statements.

### 4.2 Kloosterman sheaves

Katz proves the exact Mellin identity

$$
M_{\mathrm{Kl}_n}(\chi)=g(\chi,\psi)^n.
$$

Therefore:

- raw maximal-ideal zeros: all nontrivial characters;
- Gross–Koblitz-normalized zeros: none.

This is a genuine uniform theorem for the specific Kloosterman family, but it comes from the explicit Fourier identity, not from the abstract fact that \(\mathcal{Kl}_n\) has large monodromy or no nontrivial multiplicative translate. Katz’s Proposition 4.1.5 in *Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups* establishes the relevant translation rigidity; it does not prove the \(p\)-adic nonvanishing by itself.

### 4.3 Jacobi sums

For nontrivial characters with nontrivial product,

$$
J(\bar\omega^a,\bar\omega^b)
=rac{g(\bar\omega^a)g(\bar\omega^b)}
       {g(\bar\omega^{[a+b]_N})},
$$

where \([a+b]_N\) is the residue in \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\). Gross–Koblitz gives

$$
\boxed{
 v_p J(\bar\omega^a,\bar\omega^b)
 =\frac{a+b-[a+b]_N}{N}
 \in\{0,1\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
$$

This is exactly the carry indicator.

For fixed \(b\), the raw Jacobi sum has positive slope for a block of approximately \(b\) values of \(a\). If \(b=N/2\), approximately half the characters are raw zeros. This is the clean model behind the project’s Jacobi/Legendre warning.

After dividing by the appropriate carry factor \(\pi^N=-p\), the remaining gamma quotient is a unit and has no zero. Again:

- raw zeros can have positive density;
- slope-normalized zeros are absent for a single Jacobi monomial.

### 4.4 Katz hypergeometric sums

Katz’s *Exponential Sums and Differential Equations*, §8.2.8, computes the multiplicative Fourier transform of the unnormalized hypergeometric sum as one explicit monomial in Gauss sums. Section 8.2.11 identifies the same product as the Frobenius trace on the relevant compactly supported cohomology. Section 8.16 applies Stickelberger-type valuation analysis.

Schematically, for a hypergeometric datum \((\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta)\),

$$
M_{\mathrm{Hyp}}(\chi)
=C\prod_i g(\alpha_i\chi)
   \prod_j g(\beta_j^{-1}\chi^{-1}),
\tag{4.2}
$$

with an explicit harmless constant \(C\).

Gross–Koblitz gives an explicit piecewise-linear slope \(s(\chi)\), determined by fractional parts/carries, and

$$
\pi^{-s(\chi)(p-1)}M_{\mathrm{Hyp}}(\chi)
$$

is a product of \(p\)-adic gamma units. Therefore the canonically slope-normalized Mellin transform has no zeros.

This is the broadest standard class for which the desired phenomenon is completely proved. But note the key structural hypothesis: **the Mellin transform is one product, not a sum of several products.**

### 4.5 A summary table

| Family | Exact raw Mellin transform | Raw zeros at the maximal ideal | Canonical normalized transform | Normalized zeros |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gauss/Kummer | \(g(\chi)\) | all nontrivial \(\chi\) | \(\pi^{-j}g(\bar\omega^j)= -\Gamma_p(j/N)\) | \(0\) |
| \(\mathrm{Kl}_n\) | \(g(\chi)^n\) | all nontrivial \(\chi\) | gamma unit to the \(n\)-th power | \(0\) |
| single Jacobi sum | \(g(\alpha)g(\beta)/g(\alpha\beta)\) | carry region, often \(\asymp p\) | gamma-unit quotient | \(0\) |
| Katz hypergeometric | one Gauss-product monomial | slope polytope, often \(\asymp p\) | product of gamma units | \(0\) |
| sum of several equal-slope monomials | sum of unit terms after normalization | not controlled by slopes alone | sum of unit residues | generally open |
| Apéry Mellin | several equal-minimal-slope terms | the Apéry zero event | residual unit sum congruent to \(b_r\) | observed sparse; unproved |

## 5. What Katz’s “bad characters” theorem does—and does not—say

There is a nearby theorem that can easily be mistaken for the requested one.

In Katz’s Mellin-transform formalism, a character is called “bad” when the twisted object fails the generic cohomological behavior: for example, compactly supported cohomology is not concentrated in the expected degree, or an exceptional punctual/invariant contribution occurs. Local monodromy bounds the number of such characters, often by a constant depending only on the object.

Katz’s *Convolution and Equidistribution: Sato–Tate Theorems for Finite-Field Mellin Transforms*, especially Theorem 28.1 and Remark 28.3, then proves equidistribution of the normalized Frobenius conjugacy classes as the finite field and character vary, assuming bounded generic rank and a bounded exceptional set.

Forey–Fresán–Kowalski, *Arithmetic Fourier Transforms over Finite Fields: Generic Vanishing, Convolution, and Equidistribution* (arXiv:2109.11961, accepted for Astérisque), greatly extends this generic-vanishing and equidistribution framework.

But

$$
\chi\text{ is cohomologically good}
$$

does **not** imply

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}\mid H_c^1)
ot\equiv0\pmod p.
$$

A good cohomology group can have a trace divisible by \(p\). Indeed the raw Kloosterman example has perfectly controlled one-degree cohomology for every nontrivial twist, yet its trace \(g(\chi)^2\) has positive slope for every nontrivial \(\chi\).

Therefore:

```text
Katz-bad character    = exceptional cohomological geometry;
mod-p Mellin zero     = same-characteristic cancellation/divisibility of a trace.
```

They are different notions.

## 6. The role of the self-twist kernel

Let

$$
K(\mathcal F)
=\{\rho:\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal L_\rho
       \simeq\mathcal F\}
$$

or the corresponding conjugate-twist variant.

A nontrivial self-twist can force the Mellin spectrum into character orbits and can create systematic selection rules. Thus a large self-twist kernel is a legitimate warning sign, and it can explain deterministic families of zeros such as the Legendre phenomenon observed in the project.

However, the converse is not known:

$$
K(\mathcal F)=\{1\}
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\#\{\chi:\widetilde M(\mathcal F,\chi)=0\}=O(1)
$$

by any theorem of Katz, Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel, or Forey–Fresán–Kowalski.

The reason is categorical. Self-twist rigidity is an ℓ-adic geometric-isomorphism statement. After the common \(p\)-adic slopes are removed, a Mellin trace can still be a sum

$$
U_1(\chi)+\cdots+U_s(\chi)
$$

of units. Whether this sum vanishes in \(\mathbf F_p\) is a defining-characteristic cancellation question not encoded by the self-twist group.

Thus trivial self-twist is best regarded as removing **forced** zero families. It does not bound **accidental arithmetic** zeros.

For Kloosterman, the zero-free normalized theorem comes from the much stronger fact that \(s=1\): there is only one gamma monomial. For Apéry, the relevant \(s\) grows or at least exceeds one throughout the interior range.

## 7. Why the explicit Kloosterman proof does not transfer to Apéry

The project’s current \(p\)-adic calculation already exposes the exact distinction.

For an interior Apéry coefficient \(b_r\), after writing the binomial factors using Morita gamma, one obtains

$$
b_r
=\sum_{k=0}^r
 p^{2\mathbf 1_{r+k\ge p}}
 U_{p,r,k}^2,
\qquad U_{p,r,k}\in\mathbf Z_p^\times.
\tag{7.1}
$$

Put

$$
\rho=\min(r,p-1-r).
$$

Exactly \(ho+1\) summands have minimal slope \(0\), and the rest have slope \(2\). Hence

$$
p\mid b_r
\iff
\sum_{k=0}^{\rho}
 \overline{U}_{p,r,k}^{\,2}=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
\tag{7.2}
$$

For every interior \(r\), one has \(ho+1\ge2\). Therefore there is never a unique lowest-slope term. Stickelberger and Gross–Koblitz completely determine the Newton polygon, but that polygon does not decide the zero event.

Compare:

```text
Kloosterman:
    raw Mellin = one Gauss monomial
    -> remove its slope
    -> one gamma unit
    -> never zero.

Apéry:
    raw/geometric Mellin = sum of many Gauss/Jacobi contributions
    -> remove the common minimum slope
    -> several gamma units
    -> possible residue cancellation
    -> exactly b_r = 0 mod p.
```

This agrees with the warning in `problems/3.2/proof.tex`: bounded cohomological complexity does not by itself control \(Z(p)\), and the Jacobi example shows that fixed rank and conductor can coexist with linearly many positive-slope characters.

The missing theorem remains an Apéry-specific estimate for the cancellation set in (7.2), or an equivalent unique-unit-root/Strassmann statement with a uniformly bounded exceptional set.

## 8. (C) The Artin-conductor/Chebotarev interpretation is not correct

### 8.1 Conductor is not divisibility of the trace

The Artin conductor measures ramification of a representation. It controls Euler characteristics, dimensions of twisted cohomology, and the size of the cohomological exceptional set. It does not directly encode whether the trace of Frobenius is divisible by \(p\).

### 8.2 A fixed algebraic integer does not give density \(1/[K:\mathbf Q]\)

If \(\alpha\ne0\) is a fixed algebraic integer, rational primes \(p\) for which some prime above \(p\) divides \(\alpha\) are among the prime divisors of

$$
N_{K/\mathbf Q}(\alpha),
$$

and are therefore finite. Their density is \(0\), not \(1/[K:\mathbf Q]\).

The density \(1/[K:\mathbf Q]\) arises in simple Galois situations for **complete splitting of a prime in a fixed extension**, not for divisibility of a fixed algebraic integer.

### 8.3 The coefficient fields are not bounded in the Kloosterman example

As \(p\) varies, the raw values \(g(\chi)\), \(g(\chi)^2\), and general Mellin traces naturally lie in fields containing \(\zeta_p\) and \((p-1)\)-st roots of unity. Their degrees grow with \(p\). Thus even the premise that all values lie in one bounded-degree number field is false for the basic model.

### 8.4 Varying \(\chi\) at fixed \(p\) is not a Chebotarev family of primes

At fixed \(p\), the characters \(\chi\) are Kummer twists of one object. They are not prime ideals of a fixed number field, and no Frobenius-density statement in a fixed Galois group counts them.

The closest existing “horizontal” theorem is Katz’s Theorem 28.1: it permits a sequence of prime fields and proves Sato–Tate-type equidistribution of normalized Mellin conjugacy classes as the character varies. Forey–Fresán–Kowalski provide a broader generic-vanishing/equidistribution theory on commutative algebraic groups.

These are horizontal in the **complex/ℓ-adic distribution** sense. They do not see reduction modulo the same varying characteristic.

A useful contrast is:

- for a **fixed auxiliary prime ℓ**, a congruence of Frobenius traces modulo ℓ can sometimes be encoded in a fixed residual representation and treated by Chebotarev or a Frobenius large sieve;
- for congruence modulo the **varying characteristic \(p\)**, the relevant object is crystalline/\(F\)-isocrystalline, and there is no fixed residual representation to which ordinary Chebotarev applies.

Thus there is no established “horizontal Chebotarev for varying characters” that yields the desired bounded mod-\(p\) zero theorem.

## 9. What theorem would actually suffice for Apéry?

The project’s two clean sufficient formulations remain the right ones.

### 9.1 Bounded tame-Hasse degree after normalization

Suppose there are an absolute \(D\), units \(u_{p,j}\), and nonzero Laurent polynomials \(Q_p\) of degree at most \(D\) such that the normalized Mellin coefficients satisfy

$$
\widetilde M_{p,j}
=u_{p,j}Q_p(\omega^j)
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
$$

Then

$$
\#\{j:\widetilde M_{p,j}=0\}\le2D.
$$

This is exactly what occurs for Kloosterman with \(Q_p=1\). It is not known for Apéry. Moreover `proof.tex` records a linear-complexity obstruction for the **unnormalized** interpolation, so any such theorem must first discover a nontrivial Gauss/Jacobi normalization.

### 9.2 Bounded nonordinary twists / unique unit root

If the twisted crystalline cohomology

$$
H^1_{\mathrm{rig},c}
(U,\mathcal V_{\mathrm{Ap}}
\otimes\mathcal K_{\omega^{-j}})
$$

has a unique unit-root contribution for all but \(O(1)\) characters, and that unit contributes nontrivially to the Mellin trace, then only \(O(1)\) normalized Mellin coefficients can vanish.

This is a genuine crystalline theorem. Katz’s ℓ-adic generic vanishing does not supply it.

### 9.3 A weaker average theorem is more plausible than a pointwise bound

The empirical Poisson model in `proof.tex` predicts that \(Z(p)\) is likely unbounded, with maximum of order \(2\log\log p\) across primes. Therefore the pointwise statement \(Z(p)=O(1)\) may itself be false even though the first-moment statement

$$
\sum_{p\le X}Z(p)\ll\pi(X)
$$

should hold.

The realistic target is thus an average crystalline anti-concentration theorem, not necessarily a uniform pointwise zero theorem.

## 10. Final answers to (A), (B), and (C)

### (A) Exact relationship for Kloosterman

With the standard unnormalized definitions,

$$
\sum_{a\ne0}\chi(a)\operatorname{Kl}_2(a;p)
=g(\chi)^2,
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid H_c^1)
=g(\chi)^2
$$

up to the global convention sign. There are no \(H_c^0/H_c^2\) corrections for a nontrivial twist.

If \(a^r\) denotes ordinary integer powers rather than Teichmüller powers, the characteristic-zero equality is no longer the same identity; only the reductions agree after choosing \(\mathfrak P\mid p\). If the computation produces bounded zeros, it is almost certainly using a slope/Hasse-normalized finite-field Kloosterman object rather than the raw cyclotomic trace.

### (B) Families with proved bounded zeros

- **Raw Gauss, Kloosterman, Jacobi, and hypergeometric transforms:** bounded-zero statements generally fail; positive-slope zero sets can have size \(\asymp p\).
- **Canonically Gross–Koblitz-normalized single Gauss-product transforms:** zero-free, hence bounded with \(C=0\). This includes Gauss sums, \(\mathrm{Kl}_n\), single Jacobi sums after carry removal, and Katz’s explicit hypergeometric sums.
- **General sheaves, even with bounded conductor and trivial self-twist:** no known theorem of the requested form.
- **Apéry:** observed bounded/sparse zeros, but no proof. The obstacle is equal-slope unit cancellation.

### (C) Horizontal Chebotarev

No standard Chebotarev theorem applies. The number fields and reduction primes vary, fixed-\(p\) characters are twists rather than prime ideals, and same-characteristic divisibility is crystalline. Katz/FFK horizontal Mellin equidistribution is the closest analogy, but it does not bound mod-\(p\) trace zeros.
