# Q32 Hankel determinant / atom-rank amplification

## 1. Moment matrix and universal rank contribution

Write
\[
M_{ij}=U_{i+j+1}(n)=\sum_{k=1}^n w_k z_k^{i+j+1},\qquad
w_k=b_{n-k},\ z_k=L_n/k.
\]
Thus \(H_d\) is the Hankel determinant of the discrete measure
\(\sum_k w_k\delta_{z_k}\).

For a prime \(q\), only atoms with minimal denominator valuation survive after reducing the scaled moments. If \(q^e\le n<q^{e+1}\), write
\(n=aq^e+r\). The surviving residue class consists of
\(k=u q^e\), \(1\le u\le a\). Hence the reduced moment matrix has rank at most \(a\). Therefore the Smith rank defect gives the universal vanishing
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf F_q}(M)\le a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q^{d-a}\mid H_d
\]
for \(d>a\). This is the first universal Smith factor.

More generally, if the surviving atoms are \(m\) distinct nonzero points modulo \(q\), the Hankel Smith normal form has at least
\[
0,1,\ldots,d-m-1
\]
invariant valuation contribution after the usual Vandermonde normalization, giving
\[
v_q(H_d)\ge \binom{d-m}{2}
\]
from the collision/rank filtration. This is the universal rank contribution.

## 2. Generic top-shell case

For \(q>n/2\), one has \(e=0\), \(a=1\), and only the atom \(k=q\) can be q-singular in the usual LCM normalization. The non-target reduction is therefore rank one:
\[
M\equiv c\,(z_i z_j)_{i,j}\pmod q.
\]
Consequently every \(d\times d\) determinant with \(d\ge2\) receives the rank-one Smith contribution
\[
v_q(H_d)\ge \binom{d-1}{2}.
\]
For \(d=2\) this is one universal q factor; for larger d this is the forced determinant amplification.

## 3. Target condition q|b_r

When \(q=n-r\) and \(q\mid b_r\), the unique surviving atom is killed by the Apéry--Lucas congruence. In the top shell this improves the entrywise valuation:
\[
v_q(U_s(n))\ge s.
\]
Hence
\[
v_q(M_{ij})\ge i+j+1.
\]
Factor \(q^i\) from row \(i\) and \(q^j\) from column \(j\), together with the remaining one power from every entry. Equivalently the determinant has valuation
\[
v_q(H_d)\ge
\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}i+\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}j+d
= d(d-1)+d
=d^2.
\]
Therefore the excess over the generic rank-one obstruction is
\[
\Delta_d=d^2-\binom{d-1}{2}
=\frac{d^2+3d-1}{2}.
\]

## 4. Cauchy--Binet height audit

The exact expansion is
\[
H_d=\sum_{|K|=d}\prod_{k\in K}b_{n-k}L_n/k
\prod_{i<j}(L_n/i-L_n/j)^2.
\]
The proposed cancellation must divide by all universal q-factors, not by the size of individual summands.

The Apéry weight contribution cannot be removed. The standard growth estimate
\[
b_m=\exp(\Theta(m))
\]
means that a generic d-subset term contributes
\[
\prod_{k\in K}b_{n-k}
=\exp(\Theta(dn))
\]
for subsets away from the boundary. The number of subsets contributes another factor
\[
\binom nd=\exp(O(d\log n)).
\]
The Vandermonde part contributes at most polynomially in \(L_n\) after logarithmic normalization, but \(\log L_n=\Theta(n)\), so its contribution is also
\[
\exp(O(d^2 n)).
\]
Thus after removing universal Smith factors, the remaining integer has logarithmic height bounded below in the natural scale by
\[
\log |N_d|=\Omega(dn)
\]
coming specifically from the Apéry weight product.

## 5. Can the excess valuation beat height?

The target gain is only
\[
\Delta_d\log q.
\]
In the top shell \(q\asymp n\), so
\[
\Delta_d\log q=O(d^2\log n).
\]
The surviving height obstruction is
\[
\Omega(dn).
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{\log |N_d|}{\Delta_d\log q}
\gtrsim
\frac{dn}{d^2\log n}
=\frac{n}{d\log n}.
\]
To obtain \(o(n)\), one needs \(d\to\infty\), but the determinant construction remains meaningful only while the rank amplification does not exceed the available atom count. Taking \(d=O(\log n)\) gives ratio \(\Omega(n/\log^2 n)\), not a proof of smallness. Taking polynomial d violates the fixed-rank surviving atom regime and increases the Vandermonde height simultaneously.

Hence the Hankel amplification does not currently escape the height barrier.

## 6. Exact obstruction term

The preventing term is not the determinant size alone. It is the Cauchy--Binet weight factor
\[
\boxed{\prod_{k\in K}b_{n-k}}
\]
inside every nonzero minor. Even after saturation by the universal Smith factors, this term contributes exponential height \(\exp(\Theta(dn))\), while the target valuation gain in the top shell is only quadratic in d times \(\log n\).

Therefore the rigorous conclusion is negative:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Hankel atom-rank amplification does not yield an }o(n)\text{ height/excess valuation estimate.}
}
\]
The exact obstruction is the unavoidable Apéry-weight height term in the Cauchy--Binet expansion, not merely the fact that determinants are large.
