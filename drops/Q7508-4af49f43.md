# R1 audit: small-order residue fractions and the Kummer-monodromy scope claim

## Executive verdict

There are two different conclusions.

1. **The arithmetic carrier idea is correct and can be strengthened**, but the proposed parametrization misses a real endpoint and the stated `O(D^2 log(nD))` is not uniformly valid on the stated endpoint range.  The clean statement uses the reduced fraction
   \[
   \frac{j}{p-1}=\frac{a}{d},\qquad 0\le a\le d,
   \]
   with the two exceptional Farey endpoints `(a,d)=(0,1)` and `(1,1)`.  With `M=floor(D)`, every eligible prime can be assigned to exactly one such pair and one gets the exact divisibility
   \[
   \prod_{p\in P}p\mid
   n(n+1)
   \prod_{2\le d\le M}
   \prod_{\substack{1\le a<d\\(a,d)=1}}
   (dn+a).
   \]
   In particular, for `D>=1`,
   \[
   \sum_{p\in P}\log p
   \le
   \log n+\log(n+1)+
   \sum_{2\le d\le M}
   \sum_{\substack{1\le a<d\\(a,d)=1}}
   \log(dn+a),
   \tag{1}
   \]
   and hence, uniformly,
   \[
   \boxed{\displaystyle
   \sum_{p\in P}\log p\le 2D^2\log(2nD).}
   \tag{2}
   \]
   The hypothesis `p>sqrt(n)` is not used anywhere in this proof.  If `D<1`, the eligible set is empty.

2. **The character-order identity is correct, but the proposed sheaf/modularity no-go is too broad.**  If `omega_p` has exact order `p-1`, then
   \[
   \operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^j)=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(j,p-1)}=d.
   \]
   The associated Kummer sheaf has that finite tame inertia order.  However, a fixed geometric variety or fixed Laurent polynomial can perfectly well be paired with a **varying** Kummer coefficient sheaf of unbounded order; indeed that is exactly what the current toric marked-coordinate construction in `problems/3.2/oracleC_result.tex` does.  Also, tame conductor/Swan/rank can stay uniformly bounded while the finite tame character order tends to infinity.  The rigorous no-go is narrower: a fixed finite quasi-unipotent local inertial category has a bounded finite semisimple tame exponent, so it cannot contain Kummer characters of unbounded order as actual local constituents.  Variable Kummer twists, variable/iterated pushforwards, residual reduction at the varying prime, and mere scalar trace identities are outside that argument.

The rest of this report proves these assertions and separates source-verified claims from citations that still need checking.

# 2. Arithmetic audit from scratch

Let `n>=1` be an integer and let `p` be a prime.  Put
\[
j=j_p:=n\bmod p,\qquad 0\le j<p,
\]
and
\[
d=d_p:=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(j,p-1)},
\]
where `gcd(0,p-1)=p-1` as usual.

The first observation is that `d` is always a positive integer.  In particular, no prime satisfies `d<=D` when `D<1`.

## 2.1. Correct reduced-fraction parametrization

### Lemma 2.1

For every prime `p` there is a unique pair of integers `(a,d)` satisfying
\[
1\le d,\qquad 0\le a\le d,\qquad \gcd(a,d)=1,
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\frac{j}{p-1}=\frac{a}{d}.
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover this `d` is exactly
\[
d=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(j,p-1)}.
\]
The endpoints are precisely
\[
 j=0 \Longleftrightarrow (a,d)=(0,1),
 \qquad
 j=p-1 \Longleftrightarrow (a,d)=(1,1).
\tag{5}
\]
For `d>=2` one has the familiar range
\[
1\le a<d,\qquad (a,d)=1.
\tag{6}
\]

### Proof

If `j=0`, (5) is immediate.

Assume `j>0` and put
\[
g=\gcd(j,p-1),\qquad a=j/g,\qquad d=(p-1)/g.
\]
Then `(a,d)=1` and (4) holds.  Since `1<=j<=p-1`, one has `1<=a<=d`.  Equality `a=d` is equivalent to `j=p-1`; in that case `g=p-1`, so `(a,d)=(1,1)`.  Otherwise `a<d`.  Uniqueness is uniqueness of a reduced fraction with the two endpoints included. ∎

### Correction to the proposed statement

The proposed assertion
\[
 j\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 j=\frac{a(p-1)}d,
 \quad 1\le a<d
\]
is **false at `d=1`**.  For example
\[
n=5,\quad p=3,\quad j=2=p-1,
\quad d=1,
\]
and the unique numerator is `a=1=d`, not `a<d`.

Thus there are two `d=1` cases, not one:

| residue | reduced pair | arithmetic meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `j=0` | `(a,d)=(0,1)` | `p | n` |
| `j=p-1` | `(a,d)=(1,1)` | `p | n+1` |

Both give the trivial character `omega_p^j`, since exponents are read modulo `p-1`.

## 2.2. The carrier identity, including both endpoints

Write
\[
n=qp+j,\qquad q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor.
\]
From (4),
\[
dj=a(p-1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
dn+a
 &=d(qp+j)+a\\
 &=dqp+a(p-1)+a\\
 &=p(dq+a).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{p\mid dn+a.}
\tag{8}
\]

This proves that the sign in the proposed carrier is **plus**, not minus.
There is also no positivity issue:
\[
dn+a\ge n\ge1.
\tag{9}
\]
The only possibility `a=0` is `(a,d)=(0,1)`, where the carrier is exactly `n`.

Equation (7) also handles all endpoint cases without fiction:

* `(a,d)=(0,1)`: `n=pq`, so `p|n`;
* `(a,d)=(1,1)`: `n+1=p(q+1)`, so `p|n+1`.

In particular, a prime `p>sqrt(n)` **can divide `n`**.  Then necessarily it is in the first endpoint fiber.  The proof requires no exclusion of this case.

## 2.3. Exact converse and uniqueness of the carrier pair

The carrier condition can be sharpened by retaining the congruence that is lost in the crude product bound.

### Lemma 2.2

Let `d>=2`, `1<=a<d`, `(a,d)=1`, and let `p` be a prime satisfying
\[
p\equiv1\pmod d,
\qquad
p\mid dn+a.
\tag{10}
\]
Then for `j=n mod p`,
\[
j=\frac{a(p-1)}d,
\qquad
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(j,p-1)}=d.
\tag{11}
\]
The analogous endpoint statements are
\[
(a,d)=(0,1)\Longleftrightarrow p\mid n,
\qquad
(a,d)=(1,1)\Longleftrightarrow p\mid n+1
\]
when interpreted through `j=0` and `j=p-1` respectively.

### Proof

Set `t=(p-1)/d`.  Since `d<p`, (10) and invertibility of `d mod p` give
\[
n\equiv -a/d\equiv at\pmod p,
\]
because `dat=a(p-1)=-a mod p`.  The inequalities `0<a<d` give
`0<at<p-1`, so the least residue is exactly `j=at`.  Then
\[
\gcd(j,p-1)=\gcd(at,dt)=t\gcd(a,d)=t,
\]
which proves (11). ∎

Consequences:

* An actual prime has **one and only one canonical reduced pair `(d,a)`**.
* A prime may nevertheless divide several unrelated integers `d'n+a'` if one forgets the indispensable congruence `p≡1 mod d'`.  Thus a proof that simply unions prime divisors of all carriers can overcount a prime.  Overcounting is harmless for an upper bound; the clean proof below instead assigns each prime to its unique canonical pair.
* Distinct primes **can** share one canonical pair.  For example, with `n=17`, the pair `(d,a)=(2,1)` has carrier `2n+1=35`, and both `p=5` and `p=7` are larger than `sqrt(17)` and have that canonical pair.  Therefore the proof must use “the product of the distinct primes in one fiber divides the carrier,” not “there is at most one prime per pair.”

## 2.4. Exact Farey-carrier theorem

For an integer `M>=1`, define
\[
\mathcal F_M
=
\{(a,d):1\le d\le M,\ 0\le a\le d,\ (a,d)=1\}.
\tag{12}
\]
This is the set of reduced Farey fractions in `[0,1]` of denominator at most `M`, written as numerator-denominator pairs.  Its cardinality is
\[
|\mathcal F_M|
=1+\sum_{d=1}^M\varphi(d)
=2+\sum_{d=2}^M\varphi(d).
\tag{13}
\]

### Theorem 2.3 (exact carrier product)

Let `n>=1`, let `D` be real, and let `P` be any set of distinct primes such that `d_p<=D` for every `p in P`.  The additional condition `p>sqrt(n)` may be imposed, but is not needed.

If `D<1`, then `P` is empty.

If `D>=1` and `M=floor(D)`, then
\[
\boxed{
\prod_{p\in P}p\mid
 n(n+1)
 \prod_{d=2}^{M}
 \prod_{\substack{1\le a<d\\(a,d)=1}}
 (dn+a).}
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in P}\log p
\le
\log n+\log(n+1)
+
\sum_{d=2}^{M}
\sum_{\substack{1\le a<d\\(a,d)=1}}
\log(dn+a).}
\tag{15}
\]

### Proof

By Lemma 2.1, every `p in P` has a unique pair `(a_p,d_p) in F_M`.  For each fixed pair `(a,d)`, let `P_{a,d}` be the primes assigned to it.  Equation (8) gives `p|dn+a` for every `p in P_{a,d}`.  Since the primes in a fiber are distinct,
\[
\prod_{p\in P_{a,d}}p\mid dn+a.
\]
Multiply these divisibilities over the disjoint canonical fibers.  The two `d=1` fibers have carriers `n` and `n+1`; the remaining fibers are exactly the double product in (14).  Taking logarithms gives (15). ∎

This exact product is, in my view, the best formulation to put in a paper.  It keeps the endpoint cases visible and allows later arguments to exploit the individual arithmetic of `dn+a` rather than discarding it immediately into a big-O estimate.

## 2.5. Explicit constants

For `d>=2`, `phi(d)<=d-1`.  Therefore
\[
|\mathcal F_M|
\le 2+\sum_{d=2}^M(d-1)
=2+\frac{M(M-1)}2.
\tag{16}
\]
In particular,
\[
|\mathcal F_M|\le M^2\qquad(M\ge2).
\tag{17}
\]
Also, for every `(a,d) in F_M`,
\[
dn+a\le d(n+1)\le M(n+1).
\tag{18}
\]

If `M>=2`, (15), (17), and (18) give
\[
\sum_{p\in P}\log p
\le M^2\log(M(n+1))
\le D^2\log(2nD).
\tag{19}
\]
If `M=1`, the exact bound is simply
\[
\sum_{p\in P}\log p\le\log(n(n+1)).
\tag{20}
\]
Combining the two regimes gives the convenient endpoint-safe bound
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in P}\log p
\le 2D^2\log(2nD)
\qquad(n\ge1,\ D\ge1).}
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, for `1<=D<2`,
`log(n(n+1))<=2 log(2n)<=2D^2 log(2nD)`.

If one specifically wants the original logarithm, then for `nD>=2`,
\[
\log(2nD)\le2\log(nD),
\]
so (21) yields the completely explicit
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in P}\log p
\le4D^2\log(nD)
\qquad(D\ge1,\ nD\ge2).}
\tag{22}
\]
There are slightly better constants in subranges.  For example, if `n>=2` and `D>=1`, then the original shape already holds uniformly with
\[
\sum_{p\in P}\log p\le3D^2\log(nD).
\tag{23}
\]
For `1<=D<2` this follows from (20) and `n+1<=n^2`; for `D>=2`, (19) gives a still smaller constant.

## 2.6. Is `O(D^2 log(nD))` uniformly valid as originally written?

**No, not on the full literal endpoint range.**

There are two separate problems.

### `D<1`

Since every `d_p>=1`, the set `P` is empty.  The left side is zero.  But `log(nD)` may be negative, zero, or even undefined if nonpositive `D` is allowed.  Thus the asymptotic notation is not a well-formed nonnegative uniform majorant on that domain.  The natural statement is simply: `P=empty` for `D<1`, and then assume `D>=1`.

### `n=1`, `D` at or just above `1`

Take `n=1`, `p=2`.  Then
\[
j=1=p-1,\qquad d=1,
\]
so `P={2}` is allowed when `D>=1`.  At `(n,D)=(1,1)`,
\[
\sum_{p\in P}\log p=\log2>0,
\qquad
D^2\log(nD)=0.
\]
Thus no absolute constant can make the literal bound true there.

If `D` is allowed to be real, the failure is not only one isolated point: for every `1<=D<2`, the same prime `2` is allowed, while
\[
D^2\log D\longrightarrow0
\quad(D\downarrow1).
\]
Hence there is no uniform constant on `n=1, D>1` arbitrarily close to `1` either.

The correct fully uniform choices are therefore `log(2nD)`, `1+log(nD)`, or a restriction such as `nD>=2` together with `D>=1`.

## 2.7. The role of `p>sqrt(n)`

It is **not used** in Lemmas 2.1–2.2, Theorem 2.3, or any bound above.  The result is stronger without it.

That condition can still be useful elsewhere.  For example, at most one prime `p>sqrt(n)` can divide `n`, and similarly at most one can divide `n+1`; but no such observation improves the logarithmic carrier estimate enough to change its basic form.

Equation (8) also gives the independent size restriction
\[
p\le dn+a\le D(n+1),
\tag{24}
\]
so all eligible primes automatically lie below `D(n+1)`.  Thus one also has the competing Chebyshev-type estimate
\[
\sum_{p\in P}\log p\le\vartheta(D(n+1))\ll Dn.
\tag{25}
\]
Accordingly one may record the regime-wise bound
\[
\sum_{p\in P}\log p
\ll \min\{D^2\log(2nD),\,Dn\}.
\tag{26}
\]
The first term is the useful one when `D` is small.

## 2.8. Near-optimality: what can and cannot be claimed

The quadratic dependence in the carrier proof is structurally natural because the number of possible reduced pairs is a Farey count:
\[
|\mathcal F_M|
=1+\sum_{d\le M}\varphi(d)
=\frac{3}{\pi^2}M^2+O(M\log M).
\tag{27}
\]
Thus any proof that gives one independent integer carrier for every reduced fraction `a/d` and bounds each carrier only by its size will naturally see `Theta(D^2)` potential carriers.  Keeping (15) rather than replacing every logarithm by the maximum even preserves the exact Farey weighting.

This is **methodological near-optimality only**.  It does **not** prove that the actual maximum over sets of primes satisfying the original conditions is `Omega(D^2 log(nD))`.  The actual primes obey extra restrictions that the crude logarithmic product discards, notably
\[
p\equiv1\pmod d,
\tag{28}
\]
and in the original application `p>sqrt(n)`.  I do not know a construction showing a matching quadratic lower bound with those simultaneous constraints.  A publication should therefore not call the `D^2` exponent globally sharp.

A useful sharper fiber formulation for future work is
\[
P_{a,d}
\subseteq
\{p:\ p\mid dn+a,\ p\equiv1\pmod d\},
\tag{29}
\]
with equality after imposing the defining residue condition as in Lemma 2.2.  Any genuine improvement beyond the Farey-carrier bound should probably exploit (28) or average arithmetic of the linear forms `dn+a`, not merely recount the pairs.

# 3. Publication-ready arithmetic lemma

Here is a compact version suitable for insertion into a manuscript.

> **Lemma (small-order residue characters).**  Let `n>=1`, let `D>0`, and for every prime `p` put `j_p=n mod p` in `[0,p-1]` and
> \[
> d_p=(p-1)/\gcd(j_p,p-1).
> \]
> Let `P` be any set of primes for which `d_p<=D`.  If `D<1`, then `P` is empty.  If `D>=1` and `M=floor(D)`, then
> \[
> \prod_{p\in P}p\mid
> n(n+1)
> \prod_{d=2}^{M}
> \prod_{\substack{1\le a<d\\(a,d)=1}}(dn+a).
> \]
> Consequently
> \[
> \sum_{p\in P}\log p
> \le
> \log n+\log(n+1)+
> \sum_{d=2}^{M}
> \sum_{\substack{1\le a<d\\(a,d)=1}}
> \log(dn+a)
> \le2D^2\log(2nD).
> \]
> In particular, if `nD>=2`, then
> \[
> \sum_{p\in P}\log p\le4D^2\log(nD).
> \]
> These conclusions remain valid without any assumption `p>sqrt(n)`.
>
> **Proof.**  Reduce `j_p/(p-1)` uniquely as `a/d` with `0<=a<=d`, `(a,d)=1`.  The only endpoints are `(0,1)` and `(1,1)`.  Writing `n=qp+j_p` gives
> \[
> dn+a=p(dq+a),
> \]
> so every prime in the canonical `(a,d)`-fiber divides `dn+a`.  The product of the distinct primes in one fiber therefore divides that carrier.  Multiplying over the disjoint fibers proves the product divisibility.  The logarithmic estimate follows from `|F_M|=1+sum_{d<=M}phi(d)`, `phi(d)<=d-1`, and `dn+a<=M(n+1)`. ∎

If the paper already assumes `n>=2`, the final displayed `4` can be replaced by `3` as in (23), but the `log(2nD)` formulation is cleaner and genuinely endpoint-safe.

# 4. The Mellin/Kummer character order

## 4.1. Proved group-theoretic statement

Let
\[
\omega_p:\mathbb F_p^\times\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell^\times,
\qquad \ell\ne p,
\]
be a multiplicative character of exact order `p-1` (for example a chosen Teichmüller lift after embedding the cyclotomic values).  Since `F_p^x` is cyclic of order `p-1`,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^j)
=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(j,p-1)}=d.}
\tag{30}
\]
This includes both trivial-character endpoints `j=0` and `j=p-1`, where `d=1`.  Replacing `j` by `-j`, as often occurs in a Mellin transform, does not change the order.

No sheaf theory is needed for (30).

## 4.2. Proved Kummer-sheaf interpretation

For a finite multiplicative character `chi`, let `L_chi` be the associated rank-one Kummer sheaf on `G_m`.  Under the standard convention its tame inertia character at `0` is `chi`; the character at `infinity` is inverse.  Hence the finite semisimple tame local-monodromy order at either boundary point is
\[
\operatorname{ord}(\chi).
\tag{31}
\]
For `chi=omega_p^j`, this is exactly `d`.

For a pullback `f^*L_chi`, the local character along a divisor where `f` has valuation `m` is `chi^m`; its order is
\[
\frac{d}{\gcd(d,m)}.
\tag{32}
\]
Thus an assertion that the **pullback** still has exact order `d` needs a boundary component whose multiplicity is coprime to `d`; a simple zero or pole suffices.  In the current `oracleC_result.tex` toric object, the displayed zero and pole components of `Lambda` are simple, so exact order `d` is indeed present along those components for nontrivial `chi`.

## 4.3. Conductor is not order

This distinction is essential.  A nontrivial rank-one Kummer sheaf is tame, so its Swan conductor is zero, and common geometric conductor normalizations give the same constant conductor for all nontrivial Kummer characters.  Nevertheless their finite tame inertia orders can be `2,3,4,...` without bound.

Therefore no argument of the form

> “rank, number of singularities, Swan conductor, or Betti complexity is bounded, hence the tame character order is bounded”

is valid.

The repository's `oracleC_exploration.md` §6 is already an explicit instance of this fact.

# 5. Audit of the proposed fixed-sheaf/modular no-go

The proposed inference was roughly:

> the Mellin component uses `omega_p^j`, whose order is `d`; therefore a fixed finite collection of characteristic-zero lisse sheaves or modular eigensystems cannot realize all `(p,j)`, because tame local-monodromy orders are bounded.

The first clause is correct.  The second is **not a theorem until “fixed,” “realize,” and the allowed operations are made precise**.

## 5.1. A rigorous weaker no-go

The following is the statement that the local-monodromy argument genuinely proves.

### Proposition 5.1 (fixed quasi-unipotent local category)

Fix a tame inertia group `I^t` and a finite list of finite-dimensional characteristic-zero `ell`-adic representations
\[
V_1,\ldots,V_r
\]
of `I^t`.  Assume each `V_i` is quasi-unipotent.  Then there exists an integer `E>=1` such that every eigenvalue of the semisimple tame inertia action on every object obtained from the `V_i` by finite direct sums, tensor products, duals, subquotients, and extensions has order dividing `E`.

Consequently, if a rank-one Kummer character of exact order `d` is required to occur as an actual local Jordan-Hölder constituent of such an object, then
\[
d\mid E,
\tag{33}
\]
and in particular `d<=E`.  Hence a single such fixed category cannot contain Kummer constituents of unbounded order.

### Proof

Quasi-unipotence says that for each `V_i` an open subgroup of tame inertia acts unipotently.  Therefore every eigenvalue of a tame generator on the semisimplification of `V_i` is a root of unity.  There are finitely many such eigenvalues among the finite list; let `E` be the least common multiple of their orders.

Direct sums and subquotients do not introduce new eigenvalues.  Tensor products multiply eigenvalues, duals invert them, and both operations stay inside the finite group `mu_E`.  Extensions change only the unipotent/extension data, not the semisimplified characters.  Hence every semisimple tame eigencharacter in the generated category takes values in `mu_E`.  A rank-one constituent of exact order `d` therefore has `d|E`. ∎

This is the clean no-go I would be willing to publish.

### When does quasi-unipotence hold automatically?

For arithmetic `ell`-adic representations of local Galois groups with residue characteristic different from `ell`, Grothendieck's local monodromy theorem gives quasi-unipotence.  Thus Proposition 5.1 applies to the local inertial types of a **genuinely fixed finite list of arithmetic lisse sheaves** once the relevant boundary/inertia groups have been fixed.

It does **not** follow merely from saying “a characteristic-zero lisse sheaf on a geometric punctured curve.”  Over an algebraically closed base one can form rank-one sheaves from arbitrary continuous tame characters with infinite `ell`-adic image; Katz explicitly uses such generic tame characters.  Thus the arithmetic-descent/quasi-unipotence hypothesis matters.

## 5.2. Why this does not imply the broad claim

There are several independent escape hatches.

### A. Fixed geometry with varying Kummer coefficients

A fixed variety, fixed map, fixed divisor, and fixed cohomological construction may be twisted by `L_{omega_p^j}` separately for each `(p,j)`.  Then the geometric object is fixed in one sense, but the coefficient sheaf is not.

That is exactly the current toric construction:
\[
\mathcal L_{p,j}=\Lambda^*\mathcal K_{\omega_p^j}.
\]
Its rank and wild ramification are uniformly bounded while its tame finite order can be `d`.  Proposition 5.1 does not apply because there is no fixed finite list of coefficient local systems.

Thus the statement

> “one fixed geometric object cannot support all orders `d`”

is false if “fixed geometric object” allows a varying Kummer coefficient sheaf.

### B. Variable Kummer twisting

Even starting with one fixed sheaf `F`, the family
\[
F\otimes L_\chi
\]
as `chi` varies contains the varying tame character by construction.  Mellin transforms routinely do exactly this.  Katz's multiplicative Fourier/Mellin formalism treats Kummer tensoring as an external character parameter.

Therefore unbounded order of the Mellin **test character** is not an obstruction to a fixed underlying `F`.

### C. Pushforward can create new orders

If arbitrary variable-complexity pushforwards are allowed, the no-go fails dramatically.  For `m` prime to the base characteristic, consider
\[
[m]:\mathbb G_m\to\mathbb G_m,
\qquad x\mapsto x^m.
\]
Over an algebraic closure this is a finite étale Galois cover with deck group `mu_m`, and after extending coefficients,
\[
[m]_*\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell
\simeq
\bigoplus_{\chi^m=1}L_\chi.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus exact tame characters of order `m` occur in the pushforward of the constant sheaf.  Allowing `m` to vary gives unbounded orders.  Even iterating a fixed power map can make the covering degree and resulting character orders grow.

Hence a no-go allowing pushforward needs a **bounded fixed construction**: finitely many fixed maps applied a fixed number of times, or some other explicit complexity bound.  A finite set of already-constructed pushforwards can of course be absorbed into the finite generating list of Proposition 5.1.

### D. Tensor operations are different from pushforward

For a fixed finite quasi-unipotent generating list, arbitrary tensor powers do **not** create new root-of-unity orders beyond `E`: products of elements of `mu_E` remain in `mu_E`.  Thus tensor/dual/subquotient/extension closure is safe for Proposition 5.1.

This distinction should be stated explicitly if the intended modular/geometric framework permits functorial operations.

### E. Scalar trace identities do not force local constituents

Suppose “realize `(p,j)`” only means

> produce the same single Mellin number, or the same zero/nonzero decision.

Then local monodromy order cannot be read off from that scalar.  Different sheaves, virtual complexes, or cancellations can have identical individual traces.  The monodromy obstruction is categorical: it applies when the Kummer character must occur as an actual local constituent, or when one has enough equality of trace functions to identify semisimplified sheaves by a Chebotarev-type argument.

An isolated congruence or one Frobenius trace is far too weak.  Therefore “cancellation” is not a minor technicality; it changes what can be inferred from the data.

### F. Compatible systems need a local compatibility hypothesis

“Fixed compatible system” is not by itself precise enough for this no-go.  One needs, at minimum, a theorem or hypothesis that the semisimplified tame local inertia characters at the relevant boundary specialize into a single finite group `mu_E` independent of the member of the system.

If such a uniform local-type statement is available, Proposition 5.1 applies fiberwise with the same `E`.

But ordinary compatibility phrased only in terms of Frobenius characteristic polynomials at good closed points should not be silently upgraded to that local assertion without citing an independence-of-`ell`/specialization theorem appropriate to the exact geometric situation.

### G. Reduction modulo the varying prime can destroy the claimed boundedness

A blanket claim that a fixed characteristic-zero compatible object has bounded residual tame orders is false when the coefficient/residual prime is allowed to vary.

The simplest model is the cyclotomic/Tate compatible system.  For the `p`-adic cyclotomic character reduced modulo `p`, its restriction to inertia at `p` acts on `mu_p`.  Since
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p)/\mathbb Q_p)
\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times
\]
and this extension is totally tamely ramified of degree `p-1`, the residual tame character has exact order `p-1`.

Thus unbounded residual tame orders occur in a canonical fixed compatible system.  Any no-go must say whether `p` is the base characteristic, the auxiliary `ell`, the residual coefficient characteristic, or some combination, and must exclude this mechanism if it is not intended.

The current toric construction has another variation of this warning: it keeps auxiliary `ell=2` fixed but lets the cyclotomic coefficient field `Q(mu_{p-1})` vary with `p`, and only then reduces the characteristic-zero trace at a prime above `p`.

### H. “Modular eigensystem” is not enough data

A modular eigensystem can enter through complex automorphic data, an `ell`-adic representation at fixed auxiliary `ell`, a compatible family as `ell` varies, or a residual mod-`p` representation.  These have different local-monodromy behavior.

Therefore the statement

> “a fixed finite collection of modular eigensystems cannot realize all `(p,j)`”

is not justified by the Kummer order calculation alone.  A rigorous modular no-go would need a specified functor from those eigensystems to lisse/local Galois objects, a fixed relevant inertia group or controlled specialization of it, and an assertion that realizing the Mellin component forces the order-`d` Kummer character to occur in local semisimplification.  Without those hypotheses, there is no contradiction.

# 6. A precise cross-prime no-go, if that is what is wanted

The following formulation captures the intended obstruction without overclaiming.

> **Proposition (uniform local-type obstruction).**  Suppose a proposed realization of all Mellin components `(p,j)` comes from a family of characteristic-zero lisse objects with an identified relevant tame inertia at a fixed boundary stratum, and suppose there is an integer `E` independent of `(p,j)` such that every semisimple tame inertia eigencharacter of every allowed object has order dividing `E`.  Suppose further that realizing `(p,j)` requires `L_{omega_p^j}` to occur as a rank-one local constituent (or otherwise forces an inertia eigencharacter of exact order `d=(p-1)/gcd(j,p-1)`).  Then only pairs with `d|E` can be realized.
>
> Consequently no such family realizes all `(p,j)` for which `d` is unbounded.

The proof is immediate from (30).

There is no shortage of pairs of unbounded order.  If `j` is free, take `j=1`, giving `d=p-1`.  Even in the residue form `j=n mod p`, for any fixed `n>=1` and primes `p>n`, one has `j=n` and
\[
d=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(n,p-1)}\ge\frac{p-1}{n}\to\infty.
\tag{35}
\]

The important point is that **the uniform exponent `E` is a hypothesis/consequence of the genuinely fixed local category**, not a consequence of bounded conductor or fixed ambient geometry.

# 7. Consequences for the current Ramanujan_Challenge strategy

## 7.1. What the audit does not rule out

It does not rule out the current fixed toric marked-coordinate construction.  That construction fixes `Lambda` and the ambient complement but varies the Kummer coefficient character with `(p,j)`.  Its uniform Betti/rank/Swan complexity is compatible with unbounded `d`.

It also does not rule out a Mellin transform of a fixed underlying sheaf against varying characters.  That is standard Mellin behavior, not an inconsistency.

## 7.2. What language should be tightened

The phrase in `DOCTRINE.md` that `u^{-m}` is a “FIXED character across all p” is safe if it means a fixed exponent/character-lattice element or a fixed algebraic monomial.  It should **not** be used without qualification to mean one fixed finite-monodromy characteristic-zero Kummer sheaf across all `p`: the Teichmüller realizations have varying finite order.

A safe replacement sentence would be:

> “The Mellin exponent `m` is fixed across `p`; the associated finite-field/Teichmüller Kummer character varies with `p`, so any horizontal sheaf formulation must keep that coefficient variation explicit.”

## 7.3. What can genuinely be ruled out

One can rule out any proposal that simultaneously claims all of the following:

1. a fixed finite list of quasi-unipotent local systems/local inertial types;
2. only tensor/dual/subquotient/extension operations (or another fixed bounded construction that introduces no new finite tame characters);
3. exact realization of `L_{omega_p^j}` as a local constituent; and
4. all pairs `(p,j)` with unbounded `d`.

Those four requirements are inconsistent by Proposition 5.1.

One cannot replace them by the much broader slogan “fixed geometry/modularity cannot do it.”

# 9. Final classification: proved statements, corrections, and unverified citation items

## Proved statements

* The unique reduced parametrization is `j/(p-1)=a/d` with `0<=a<=d`, `(a,d)=1`.
* The two `d=1` endpoints are `(0,1)` for `p|n` and `(1,1)` for `p|n+1`.
* For the canonical pair,
  \[
  dn+a=p(d\lfloor n/p\rfloor+a)>0.
  \]
* A prime has a unique canonical `(d,a)`, though several primes may share one pair.
* The exact Farey-carrier divisibility (14) holds.
* The explicit endpoint-safe mass bound
  \[
  \sum_{p\in P}\log p\le2D^2\log(2nD)
  \]
  holds for all `n>=1,D>=1`.
* The `p>sqrt(n)` assumption is unnecessary for that lemma.
* `ord(omega_p^j)=d`.
* The Kummer sheaf has finite tame local-monodromy order `d`; a pullback along a divisor of multiplicity `m` has order `d/gcd(d,m)`.
* A fixed finite quasi-unipotent tensor/subquotient/extension category has a bounded finite semisimple tame exponent and therefore cannot contain exact Kummer constituents of unbounded order.

## Corrections

* `1<=a<d` is false for the nonzero endpoint `j=p-1,d=1`; one must allow `(a,d)=(1,1)`.
* `j=0` is not to be squeezed into the nonzero parametrization; it is the distinct endpoint `(0,1)`.
* The literal uniform `O(D^2 log(nD))` is not valid at `n=1,D=1`, nor uniformly for real `D downarrow 1` with `n=1`; `D<1` also makes the logarithmic formulation ill-posed despite the empty prime set.
* A carrier `dn+a` may contain several eligible distinct primes, so “one prime per pair” is false.
* Conversely, carrier divisibility without `p≡1 mod d` can count a prime under unrelated pairs; canonical assignment or the congruence removes that ambiguity.
* Bounded rank/Swan/conductor/Betti complexity does not bound the finite tame character order.
* “Fixed ambient geometry” does not mean “fixed Kummer coefficient sheaf.”
* Varying Kummer twists, variable/iterated pushforwards, and residual reduction are not covered by the fixed-local-type no-go.
* A numerical Mellin trace or congruence does not force a Kummer local constituent without much stronger trace-function/sheaf-identification hypotheses.
* The blanket modular-eigensystem no-go should not be stated.

## Citation items still requiring source verification

* Exact SGA 7 I exposé/theorem numbering if used instead of the verified Deligne Weil II §1.7 reference.
* Exact SGA 1 Exposé XIII specialization theorem/corollary numbering if the argument is extended across varying residue characteristics.
* A precise primary Chebotarev reference if a later argument needs to infer semisimplified sheaf equality from equality of full trace functions.

# Bottom line

The arithmetic observation is worthwhile and stronger than the proposed formulation once the two Farey endpoints are handled correctly.  I recommend using the exact product-divisibility lemma (14), with the explicit uniform corollary `2D^2 log(2nD)`, rather than writing an endpoint-fragile bare `O(D^2 log(nD))`.

The modular interpretation needs a substantial scope correction.  The **order identity is genuine**, but it only obstructs a genuinely fixed finite local inertial category in which the Kummer character must appear as an actual constituent.  It does **not** obstruct the current fixed-geometry/varying-Kummer toric construction, a Mellin transform with varying external characters, or general modular/compatible-system constructions that allow residual reduction or varying pushforwards.  Those distinctions should be explicit in any manuscript claim.
