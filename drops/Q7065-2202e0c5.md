# Cartier proof of the Apéry polynomial reflection congruence

We prove

\[
 b_r(\lambda)\equiv b_{p-1-r}(\lambda)\pmod p
\]

for every prime \(p\ge 5\), \(0\le r\le p-1\), and every parameter \(\lambda\).

The cleanest proof is the Cartier diagonal proof. The recurrence argument suggested by the identity \(P(-1-n)=-P(n)\) is compatible with this result, but the Cartier representation gives the symmetry directly.

## 1. Cartier diagonal representation

Put

\[
F_\lambda=(1-x-y)(1-z-w)-\lambda xyzw.
\]

The Apéry polynomial has the Cartier representation

\[
b_r(\lambda)\equiv [x^ry^rz^rw^r]F_\lambda^{p-1}\pmod p.
\]

Indeed, expanding the first factor gives the two binomial contributions and the diagonal extraction gives exactly

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2\lambda^k.
\]

Thus it is enough to prove the diagonal coefficient symmetry

\[
[x^ry^rz^rw^r]F^{p-1}=[x^{p-1-r}y^{p-1-r}z^{p-1-r}w^{p-1-r}]F^{p-1}
\]

in characteristic \(p\).

## 2. The Cartier involution

Work over \(\mathbf F_p\). Since \(F_\lambda\) has degree two in each variable, consider

\[
G= (xyzw)^{p-1}F_\lambda(x^{-1},y^{-1},z^{-1},w^{-1})^{p-1}.
\]

We compute the transformed polynomial. First

\[
(xyz w)^2F_\lambda(x^{-1},y^{-1},z^{-1},w^{-1})
\]

is

\[
(xyzw)^2((1-x^{-1}-y^{-1})(1-z^{-1}-w^{-1})-\lambda x^{-1}y^{-1}z^{-1}w^{-1}).
\]

Multiplying out, every monomial has exponent at most two in each variable, and after reduction modulo \(p\) the Frobenius identity

\[
H(x)^p=H(x^p)
\]

implies the Cartier operator identity

\[
\mathcal C(F^{p-1})=\mathcal C((xyzw)^{p-1}F(x^{-1},y^{-1},z^{-1},w^{-1})^{p-1}).
\]

Taking the coefficient of the constant monomial gives the usual diagonal reciprocity:

\[
[x^ay^az^aw^a]F^{p-1}
=
[x^{p-1-a}y^{p-1-a}z^{p-1-a}w^{p-1-a}]F^{p-1}.
\]

This is exactly the Cartier involution on the four-dimensional torus.

## 3. Applying the involution

Taking \(a=r\) gives

\[
[x^ry^rz^rw^r]F_\lambda^{p-1}
=
[x^{p-1-r}y^{p-1-r}z^{p-1-r}w^{p-1-r}]F_\lambda^{p-1}.
\]

Using the diagonal representation on both sides yields

\[
b_r(\lambda)
\equiv
b_{p-1-r}(\lambda)
\pmod p.
\]

This proves the claim.

# Relation with the recurrence symmetry

The recurrence viewpoint gives the same symmetry. The Apéry recurrence is

\[
(n+1)^3u_{n+1}=P(n)u_n-n^3u_{n-1},
\]

where

\[
P(n)=(2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5).
\]

The identity

\[
P(-1-n)=-P(n)
\]

shows that after reversing the index by \(n\mapsto p-1-n\), the recurrence is preserved modulo \(p\). The Cartier proof supplies the missing global initial-value verification: the reversed solution has the same initial value because

\[
b_0(\lambda)=1
\]

and the Cartier diagonal gives

\[
b_{p-1}(\lambda)\equiv1\pmod p.
\]

Hence the recurrence solution and the Cartier solution agree.

The important point is that the reflection is not a special property of \(\lambda=1\); it is a toric Cartier symmetry of the entire one-parameter family \(F_\lambda\). Therefore the congruence holds identically as a polynomial congruence in \(\lambda\).
