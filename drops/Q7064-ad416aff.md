# Intra-prime separation does not produce the missing T factor

The proposed Selberg large-sieve improvement is a natural direction, but the answer is essentially **no**: the gap condition inside each individual zero set gives at most a constant-factor saving, not a factor that compounds with the number of primes.

## 1. What the separated large sieve actually sees

For a fixed prime p, the points

$$\alpha_{p,r}=r/p,\qquad r\in S_p$$

have spacing at least $2/p$. If we only look inside this one fiber, the large-sieve constant improves from a spacing scale $p^{-1}$ to roughly $(2/p)^{-1}=p/2$. This is only a factor of two.

The moment estimate for $K(m)$ is not controlled by one fiber. It is controlled by the union

$$\mathcal A=\bigcup_{p\in(X,2X]}\{r/p:r\in S_p\}.$$

The relevant minimal spacing in the large sieve is the spacing between **all** points of this union. Two points belonging to different primes satisfy

$$|r/p-s/q|=|rq-sp|/(pq).$$

There is no separation hypothesis preventing this from being as small as $1/(pq)\asymp X^{-2}$. Thus the global $δ$ is still of order $X^{-2}$, and the factor-2 intra-fiber improvement disappears.

## 2. Why it cannot compound over T primes

The desired estimate

$$n_T\ll X^2/T^3$$

would require a third-moment phenomenon. The second factorial moment gives

$$\sum_m (K(m))_2\ll X^2\lambda^2,$$

and hence

$$n_T\ll X^2\lambda^2/T^2.$$

A separated-set large sieve changes the constant in the quadratic estimate. It does not change the degree of the moment. A factor of two in a quadratic norm cannot become a factor $T$ after intersecting T different prime conditions.

The obstruction is that the events

$$m\bmod p\in S_p$$

are indexed by different moduli. Separation is a statement about the geometry of one residue set modulo one p; the tail requires controlling simultaneous incidences across many unrelated moduli.

## 3. What would actually be needed

A successful route must exploit a genuinely higher-order structure, for example:

1. **Third factorial moment:** prove

$$\sum_{m<X^2}(K(m))_3\ll X^2\lambda^3.$$

Then Markov immediately gives

$$n_T\ll X^2\lambda^3/T^3.$$

The missing factor T is exactly the missing third moment.

2. **Cross-prime algebraic constraints:** for triples of primes, show that

$$m\equiv r_i\pmod {p_i},\qquad r_i\in S_{p_i}
$$

has substantially fewer solutions than random CRT predicts. This would need a relation between different Apéry zero fibers, not merely separation inside each fiber.

3. **Use Apéry-specific structure:** reflection symmetry, Lucas propagation, and the projective orbit description of $Z_p$ may give correlations that a general separated-set sieve cannot see.

## 4. Relation to the Apéry zero sets

The condition

$$|Z_p|\ll p^{2/3}$$

already gives the correct one-prime density scale. The no-consecutive property says that each $Z_p$ is sparse and separated, but it does not imply that the family $(Z_p)_p$ behaves like independent random separated sets. The atom problem is precisely about the possible concentration of these fibers along one integer m.

Therefore the Selberg separated-set observation is useful for sharpening constants in existing second-moment estimates, but it is not the mechanism that yields the extra power of T. The missing ingredient has to be a multi-prime (third moment / energy / algebraic independence) estimate.
