# P3.2 — Lucas digits, Mellin diagonalization, and the actual spectral-gap requirement

## Executive conclusion

The proposed additive factorization is indeed false, but there is an exact replacement.

Let
\[
\mu_p(x):=\#\{0\le d<p:b_d\equiv x\pmod p\},\qquad
z_p:=\mu_p(0),\qquad N_p:=p-z_p.
\]
On \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), the distribution of a \(k\)-digit Apéry value is the \(k\)-fold **multiplicative convolution** of \(\mu_p|_{\mathbf F_p^\times}\). Multiplicative characters diagonalize this convolution, so the nontrivial Mellin eigenvalues are
\[
T_p(\chi)=\sum_{d=0}^{p-1}\chi(b_d).
\]
Consequently, every nontrivial character component is multiplied by \(T_p(\chi)^k\).

There are, however, two essential qualifications.

1. A zero digit is absorbing: if one \(b_{n_i}\equiv0\), then \(b_n\equiv0\). Thus the raw additive sum contains an explicit zero-product main term. Spectral decay applies to the **centered nonzero component**, not automatically to the whole sum.
2. A fixed gap \(|T_p(\chi)|\le(1-\delta)N_p\) gives exponential decay in the number of free digits, but for \(p\) growing with \(X\) there may be only two free digits. In that regime a mere constant gap is usually not quantitatively enough; a square-root-scale estimate \(T_p(\chi)\ll\sqrt p\), or a comparably strong spectral-moment estimate, is the useful target.

Thus the answers are:

- **(a)** yes, there is a tensor/convolution decomposition, but only after Mellin expansion; the exact formula is given below;
- **(b)** yes for the centered nonzero distribution, with eigenvalue factor \((T_p(\chi)/N_p)^k\), but not for the zero atom or the principal-character floor;
- **(c)** strict gap is enough only for fixed \(p\) and \(k\to\infty\); \(|T_p(\chi)|<p-1\) is not a useful uniform hypothesis in general;
- **(d)** the ATI3 completion step can be rewritten exactly in radix/Mellin form, but this is not a drop-in replacement for the present hard-prime argument. In the repository's current proof architecture, \(p\le\sqrt n\) is already harmless, whereas the unresolved range \(p>\sqrt n\) has too few free base-\(p\) digits for repeated spectral amplification. The correct role is a hybrid/complementary digit sieve.

## 1. Exact full-block decomposition

Fix a prime \(p\), put \(G=\mathbf F_p^\times\), and extend every multiplicative character \(\chi\in\widehat G\) to \(\mathbf F_p\) by \(\chi(0)=0\), including the principal character for this notation. Then
\[
T_p(\chi)=\sum_{d=0}^{p-1}\chi(b_d)
          =\sum_{x\in G}\mu_p(x)\chi(x),
\qquad T_p(\mathbf 1)=N_p.
\]

For
\[
n=d_0+d_1p+\cdots+d_{k-1}p^{k-1},
\]
the Lucas congruence gives
\[
b_n\equiv \prod_{i=0}^{k-1}b_{d_i}\pmod p.
\]
Define, for \(x\in G\),
\[
\mu_{p,k}(x)
 :=\#\left\{(d_0,\dots,d_{k-1}):
              \prod_i b_{d_i}=x\right\}.
\]
Then \(\mu_{p,k}\) is the multiplicative convolution power
\[
\mu_{p,k}=(\mu_p|_G)^{*_{\times}k},
\]
and Mellin inversion gives the exact formula
\[
\boxed{
\mu_{p,k}(x)
 =\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}
       T_p(\chi)^k\,\overline{\chi(x)}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

The number of zero products is even simpler:
\[
\boxed{
\#\{0\le n<p^k:p\mid b_n\}=p^k-N_p^k.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, \(p\nmid b_n\) precisely when every digit lies outside the one-digit zero set \(Z_p\).

This is already worth separating from the character-sum question: if the application only asks whether \(p\mid b_n\), then the two-state digit automaton

```text
state 0: no digit from Z_p has occurred;
state 1: a digit from Z_p has occurred (absorbing)
```

counts the event exactly. Mellin analysis is needed for the distribution among the nonzero residue classes, not for the zero event itself.

### Additive character sum

Let \(\psi_p(t)=e^{2\pi i t/p}\), let \(a\ne0\), and put
\[
S_{p,k}(a):=\sum_{0\le n<p^k}\psi_p(a b_n).
\]
Separating the zero products and applying (1) to the nonzero products gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{p,k}(a)
&=p^k-N_p^k
  +\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}T_p(\chi)^k
       \sum_{x\in G}\overline{\chi(x)}\psi_p(ax).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the principal character the inner sum is \(-1\); for \(\chi\ne\mathbf1\), it is
\[
\chi(a)\tau(\overline\chi),
\qquad
\tau(\overline\chi):=\sum_{x\in G}\overline\chi(x)\psi_p(x),
\qquad |\tau(\overline\chi)|=\sqrt p.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
S_{p,k}(a)
 =p^k-\frac{p}{p-1}N_p^k
 +\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\substack{\chi\in\widehat G\\\chi\ne\mathbf1}}
      \chi(a)\tau(\overline\chi)T_p(\chi)^k.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Formula (3), not a product of one-digit additive sums, is the exact answer to (a). It is a sum of rank-one tensor components: after Mellin expansion,
\[
\chi\!\left(\prod_i b_{d_i}\right)=\prod_i\chi(b_{d_i}),
\]
so each character component factors digit by digit.

### General rectangular digit restrictions

The tensor statement is slightly more general and is useful for incomplete intervals. Let the \(i\)-th digit range over an arbitrary set \(D_i\subseteq\{0,\dots,p-1\}\). Put
\[
q_i=|D_i|,\qquad
N_i=\#\{d\in D_i:b_d\ne0\},\qquad
T_i(\chi)=\sum_{d\in D_i}\chi(b_d).
\]
For \(a\ne0\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{(d_i)\in\prod_iD_i}
 \psi_p\!\left(a\prod_i b_{d_i}\right)
 =\prod_iq_i-\frac{p}{p-1}\prod_iN_i
 +\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}
   \chi(a)\tau(\overline\chi)\prod_iT_i(\chi).
}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus independent digit restrictions produce a product of their **multiplicative** transforms inside a final sum over \(\chi\).

### Transfer-operator form

Let

```text
(L_d f)(a) = f(a b_d),
A           = sum_{0 <= d < p} L_d,
A_q         = sum_{0 <= d < q} L_d.
```

On a nontrivial multiplicative character,
\[
A\chi=T_p(\chi)\chi,
\qquad
A_q\chi=T_{p,q}(\chi)\chi,
\qquad
T_{p,q}(\chi):=\sum_{d<q}\chi(b_d).
\]
A free digit inserts \(A\), a digit restricted to \([0,q)\) inserts \(A_q\), and a fixed digit inserts \(L_d\). This is the clean operator/tensor formulation of the Lucas automaton.

## 2. What a single-period spectral gap actually gives

Define
\[
M_p:=\max_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}|T_p(\chi)|,
\qquad
\rho_p:=\frac{M_p}{N_p}.
\]
The natural normalization is \(N_p=p-z_p\), not \(p\), because \(N_p=T_p(\mathbf1)\) is the Perron eigenvalue on the nonzero part.

Let
\[
B_{p,k}:=p^k-\frac{p}{p-1}N_p^k,
\qquad
E_{p,k}(a):=S_{p,k}(a)-B_{p,k}.
\]
Then (3) gives
\[
|E_{p,k}(a)|
 \le \frac{\sqrt p}{p-1}
       \sum_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}|T_p(\chi)|^k
 \le \sqrt p\,M_p^k.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, if
\[
M_p\le(1-\delta)N_p,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
|E_{p,k}(a)|\le \sqrt p\,N_p^k(1-\delta)^k.
}
\tag{6}
\]
This is the exponential gain per free digit.

Equivalently, if
\[
\nu_p(x):=\mu_p(x)/N_p\qquad(x\in G),
\]
then the conditional law of the product, given that no zero digit occurs, is \(\nu_p^{*_{\times}k}\), and
\[
\widehat{\nu_p^{*k}}(\chi)
 =\left(\frac{T_p(\chi)}{N_p}\right)^k.
\]
For every \(r\in G\),
\[
\left|
\#\{n<p^k:b_n=r\}-\frac{N_p^k}{p-1}
\right|
\le \frac1{p-1}\sum_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}|T_p(\chi)|^k
<M_p^k,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
\|\nu_p^{*k}-u_G\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \frac12\sqrt{p-2}\,\rho_p^k.
\tag{8}
\]

So the precise statement is:

> A spectral gap gives exponential convergence of the **conditional nonzero product walk** to Haar measure on \(G\), and exponential decay of the centered term \(E_{p,k}(a)\).

It does **not** say that the uncentered sum \(S_{p,k}(a)\) itself decays exponentially.

### The unavoidable main term

The main term is
\[
B_{p,k}=p^k-\frac{p}{p-1}(p-z_p)^k.
\]
There are two sources.

- If \(z_p>0\), then \(p^k-N_p^k\) tuples have a zero factor and each contributes \(\psi_p(0)=1\).
- Even when \(z_p=0\), Haar measure on \(G\) has additive average
  \[
  \frac1{p-1}\sum_{x\ne0}\psi_p(ax)=-\frac1{p-1},
  \]
  so \(B_{p,k}=-p^k/(p-1)\).

A useful elementary estimate is
\[
|B_{p,k}|
 \le p^k-(p-z_p)^k+\frac{p^k}{p-1}
 \le k z_p p^{k-1}+\frac{p^k}{p-1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus, with the project's unconditional \(z_p=O(p^{2/3})\), the main term is still power-saving for bounded \(k\):
\[
B_{p,k}=O(kp^{k-1/3}).
\]
For fixed \(p\) and \(k\to\infty\), however, the zero atom eventually dominates and \(S_{p,k}(a)/p^k\to1\) whenever \(z_p>0\).

### What the empirical square-root scale would imply

Suppose the observed estimate could be proved uniformly:
\[
M_p\le C\sqrt p,
\qquad N_p\asymp p.
\tag{10}
\]
Then (5) gives
\[
|E_{p,k}(a)|\ll C^k p^{(k+1)/2},
\qquad
\frac{|E_{p,k}(a)|}{p^k}\ll C^k p^{-(k-1)/2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Hence two complete free digits already give a \(p^{-1/2}\)-scale centered saving, while three or more digits are much stronger.

For pointwise equidistribution among individual nonzero residues, (7) gives the weaker relative error
\[
\frac{\text{error}}{N_p^k/(p-1)}
 \ll C^k p^{1-k/2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus an \(L^\infty\) square-root bound alone makes the individual residue counts asymptotic starting at \(k\ge3\); for the additive sum, \(k=2\) is already useful because of the Gauss transform.

### A fixed \(\delta\) is not enough when the number of digits is bounded

Even with \(\rho_p\le1-\delta\), (5) only gives the normalized centered estimate
\[
\frac{|E_{p,k}(a)|}{p^k}
 \lesssim \sqrt p\,(1-\delta)^k
\tag{13}
\]
when \(N_p\sim p\). To force this to be \(o(1)\), one needs roughly
\[
k\gg_\delta \log p.
\]
For fixed \(p\), this is harmless. But if \(p\asymp X^{1/2}\), then \(k\) is only about \(2\), so a constant gap does not by itself yield cancellation. This is why the empirical \(O(\sqrt p)\) scale is much more relevant to the proposed ATI3 rearrangement than merely \((1-\delta)p\).

If the available hypothesis is stated as
\[
M_p\le(1-\delta)p,
\]
then it becomes a genuine gap relative to \(N_p\) only after using \(z_p=o(p)\). For example, once \(z_p/p\le\delta/2\),
\[
\frac{M_p}{N_p}
 \le\frac{1-\delta}{1-z_p/p}
 \le1-\frac\delta2.
\]

## 3. The weakest useful single-period input

There are several different meanings of “works,” and their thresholds are different.

### 3.1 Fixed \(p\), arbitrarily many digits: qualitative gap

Let
\[
H_p:=\langle b_d:\ 0\le d<p,\ b_d\ne0\rangle\subseteq G.
\]
Because \(b_0=1\), the support contains the identity. The equality case in the triangle inequality gives
\[
|T_p(\chi)|=N_p
\iff \chi(b_d)=1\quad\text{for every }d\text{ with }b_d\ne0
\iff \chi|_{H_p}=1.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
M_p<N_p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_p=\mathbf F_p^\times.
}
\tag{15}
\]
This is the exact qualitative criterion. It is sufficient for convergence as \(k\to\infty\) with \(p\) fixed, but it gives no useful rate uniform in \(p\). The normalized gap \(1-M_p/N_p\) can be arbitrarily tiny even when the support generates all of \(G\).

### 3.2 Uniform exponential rate: a gap relative to \(N_p\)

For an exponential factor bounded away from \(1\) uniformly in \(p\), the correct hypothesis is
\[
M_p\le(1-\delta)N_p
\tag{16}
\]
with an absolute \(\delta>0\). This is stronger than mere support generation.

### 3.3 A prescribed number \(k\) of digits: a spectral-moment bound is weaker than \(L^\infty\)

The exact robust sufficient input for the additive sum is not necessarily a maximum bound. From (3),
\[
|E_{p,k}(a)|
 \le \frac{\sqrt p}{p-1}\sum_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}|T_p(\chi)|^k.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus a bound for the \(k\)-th spectral mass may allow a small number of exceptional characters and can be strictly weaker than controlling \(M_p\).

There is also an \(L^2\) form. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|E_{p,k}(a)|
 \le
 \frac{\sqrt{p(p-2)}}{p-1}
 \left(\sum_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}|T_p(\chi)|^{2k}\right)^{1/2},
\tag{18}
\]
and Mellin Parseval gives the exact mean-square identity
\[
\sum_{a\in G}|E_{p,k}(a)|^2
 =\frac{p}{p-1}
   \sum_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}|T_p(\chi)|^{2k}.
\tag{19}
\]
For a \(k\)-digit application, (17) or (18) is the genuinely minimal type of one-period input; an \(L^\infty\) spectral gap is merely a convenient sufficient condition.

The histogram identity is
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}|T_p(\chi)|^2
 =(p-1)\sum_{x\in G}\mu_p(x)^2.
\tag{20}
\]
This connects the Q288 collision calculations to the present question. The current unconditional collision-energy estimate in `problems/3.2/proof.tex`, namely \(\sum_x\mu_p(x)^2=O(p^{7/4})\), yields only
\[
\sum_\chi|T_p(\chi)|^2=O(p^{11/4});
\]
it does not rule out a large exceptional \(T_p(\chi)\). An essentially linear collision energy would show square-root size on average over \(\chi\), but a maximum bound would still require an exceptional-character argument or higher moments.

### 3.4 Is \(|T_p(\chi)|<p-1\) enough?

Not in the uniform sense needed here.

- If \(z_p\ge2\), then \(|T_p(\chi)|\le N_p\le p-2<p-1\) is automatic, even if the nonzero support lies in a proper subgroup and there is **no** mixing at all.
- If \(z_p=1\), the condition says \(|T_p(\chi)|<N_p\), so it is only the qualitative fixed-\(p\) gap.
- If \(z_p=0\), then \(N_p=p\), and \(|T_p(\chi)|<p-1\) gives at best a normalized gap of order \(1/p\). Its per-digit gain is roughly \((1-1/p)^k\), negligible when \(k=2\) or \(3\) and \(p\to\infty\).

So strictness is enough only for the statement “for this fixed prime, sufficiently many digits eventually mix.” It is not enough for a uniform power saving in a regime where \(p\) grows with \(X\).

## 4. Can the Lucas property itself prove the one-period gap?

Not by itself. At one digit, the Lucas identity is tautological: it supplies no new information about the list
\[
b_0,b_1,\dots,b_{p-1}\pmod p.
\]
The digit law amplifies a one-period estimate; it does not create that estimate.

A direct quantitative non-concentration identity is
\[
N_p^2-|T_p(\chi)|^2
 =\frac12\sum_{\substack{d,e<p\\b_db_e\ne0}}
   |\chi(b_d)-\chi(b_e)|^2.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus a fixed \(\delta\) requires a positive proportion of pairs whose \(\chi\)-phases are separated by a fixed angle, uniformly for every nontrivial \(\chi\). Merely finding one value outside \(\ker\chi\) proves strictness but can give an extremely small gap.

There is, however, an exact bridge to the ACSOP sums studied elsewhere in this campaign. Put
\[
A_p(a):=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\psi_p(a b_j).
\]
For every nontrivial multiplicative character \(\chi\), exchanging the two sums gives
\[
\boxed{
\tau(\overline\chi)T_p(\chi)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\overline\chi(a)A_p(a).
}
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed, the inner sum over \(a\) vanishes when \(b_j=0\), and equals
\(\tau(\overline\chi)\chi(b_j)\) otherwise.

This identifies the desired \(T_p(\chi)\) as the multiplicative Fourier transform, in the \(a\)-variable, of the one-period ACSOP function. It also explains what would prove the observed square-root scale:

- a pointwise estimate \(A_p(a)=O(\sqrt p)\) alone only gives \(T_p(\chi)=O(p)\) by the triangle inequality;
- if the normalized function \(p^{-1/2}A_p(a)\) is realized by a uniformly bounded-conductor sheaf and its tensor product with every relevant Kummer sheaf has no geometrically trivial constituent, then a complete Mellin bound of size \(O(\sqrt p)\) for the normalized function would give
  \[
  \sum_{a\ne0}\overline\chi(a)A_p(a)=O(p),
  \qquad T_p(\chi)=O(\sqrt p).
  \]

This is a plausible way to connect the Q287 ACSOP/sheaf route to the present digit route. The empirical failure of a “single Gauss-sum monomial” formula does not hurt this strategy; it merely says that the Mellin transform is not collapsing for a trivial algebraic reason. What is still needed is a bounded-complexity sheaf description and exclusion of Kummer exceptional constituents.

A second possible route is a direct multiplicative subgroup/coset non-concentration theorem for the histogram \(\mu_p\), but a fixed \(\delta\) requires more than escape from proper subgroups: high-order characters can vary by very small angles, so one needs quantitative phase dispersion, not only support generation.

## 5. Incomplete cutoffs and the correct radix decomposition

Let
\[
F_{p}(X;a):=\sum_{0\le n<X}\psi_p(a b_n),
\]
and write
\[
X=q p^k+r,\qquad 0\le q<p,\qquad 0\le r<p^k.
\]
Lucas gives the exact recursion
\[
\boxed{
F_p(X;a)
 =\sum_{d=0}^{q-1}F_p(p^k;a b_d)
  +F_p(r;a b_q).
}
\tag{23}
\]
Here \(F_p(Y;0)=Y\). Iterating (23) decomposes \([0,X)\) into at most one digit cylinder per base-\(p\) position. A typical nonzero-prefix cylinder has

- a fixed prefix product \(c\in G\),
- one digit restricted to \([0,q)\), and
- \(\ell\) free lower digits.

Its nontrivial Mellin coefficient is
\[
\chi(c)\,T_{p,q}(\chi)\,T_p(\chi)^\ell.
\tag{24}
\]
If the fixed prefix product is zero, the entire cylinder contributes its cardinality and must be put into the explicit zero main term.

For the aligned block \(X=q p^k\), (4) becomes
\[
\boxed{
F_p(qp^k;a)
 =q p^k-\frac{p}{p-1}N_{p,q}N_p^k
 +\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}
   \chi(a)\tau(\overline\chi)
   T_{p,q}(\chi)T_p(\chi)^k,
}
\tag{25}
\]
where
\[
N_{p,q}:=\#\{0\le d<q:b_d\ne0\}.
\]
This is the exact replacement for “complete to one period and factor.”

A trivial bound \(|T_{p,q}(\chi)|\le q\) is enough when there are many free lower digits, because the repeated factor is \(T_p(\chi)^k\). For a uniform estimate over arbitrary \(X\), however, the lower radix cylinders can have only one or zero free digits. Then a full-period spectral gap alone does not control the boundary sharply. One needs at least one of:

1. uniform incomplete Mellin estimates for
   \[
   T_{p,q}(\chi)=\sum_{d<q}\chi(b_d);
   \]
2. a joint spectral-radius/Fourier-property estimate for the family of partial operators \(A_q\);
3. smoothing or cutoffs aligned with complete \(p^k\)-blocks;
4. a separate direct estimate for the few low-free-digit boundary cylinders.

So the exact digital decomposition exists for every cutoff, but the full-period number \(M_p\) alone does not automatically imply a strong all-prefix estimate.

### If ATI3 retains a linear twist in \(n\)

If the completed sum has the form
\[
S_{p,k}(a,h):=\sum_{n<p^k}\psi_p(a b_n+h n),
\]
then \(n\bmod p=d_0\), so only the least digit carries the \(h\)-twist. Define
\[
T_{p,h}(\chi):=\sum_{d=0}^{p-1}\psi_p(hd)\chi(b_d),
\qquad
U_{p,h}:=T_{p,h}(\mathbf1).
\]
The exact analogue of (3) is
\[
\boxed{
S_{p,k}(a,h)
 =p^k\mathbf1_{h=0}
  -\frac{p}{p-1}U_{p,h}N_p^{k-1}
  +\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\chi\ne\mathbf1}
    \chi(a)\tau(\overline\chi)
    T_{p,h}(\chi)T_p(\chi)^{k-1}.
}
\tag{26}
\]
Thus the linear twist costs one full Mellin power and introduces the mixed one-digit transform \(T_{p,h}(\chi)\). This is the formula to use if ATI3's completion includes the \(h\)-variable from \(S(c,h)\).

## 6. Can ATI3 be moved to \(p\le X^{1/2}\)?

### Formal answer: yes

Any sum over \(n\le X\) can be replaced exactly by the radix recursion (23), and every digit cylinder can be evaluated by (4). For a complete suffix of \(\ell\) free digits, the nontrivial spectrum gains the factor
\[
T_p(\chi)^\ell.
\]
Therefore there is a legitimate multi-digit ATI3 formulation.

### Quantitative answer in the current P3.2 architecture: not as a replacement for the hard step

The repository currently makes two facts explicit.

1. In `problems/3.2/proof.tex`, the entire contribution of primes \(p\le\sqrt n\) is already bounded unconditionally by \(O(\sqrt n)\).
2. `problems/3.2/campaign3_questions/q80_digitsieve.txt` describes the Lucas digit sieve as a **complement** to the large-prime congruence/cancellation argument.

That division is mathematically natural. If \(p>\sqrt X\), then numbers up to \(X\) have at most two base-\(p\) digits, and in a prefix sum there is generally only one fully free lower digit. There is no long product \(T_p(\chi)^k\) to exploit. This is precisely the range that matters for the current all-\(n\) GCD bottleneck.

Conversely, \(p\le\sqrt X\) guarantees at least two digits only for aligned \(p^2\)-blocks. Near \(p\asymp\sqrt X\), an arbitrary endpoint has a remainder whose cylinders may have just one free digit. A fixed gap \(M_p\le(1-\delta)N_p\) is not strong enough there; one needs square-root-scale complete and/or incomplete estimates, or a smoothing argument.

There is also a structural issue beyond one-prime cancellation: the base expansion changes with \(p\). Even strong individual digit-mixing estimates do not by themselves give the cross-prime independence or moving-target discrepancy required by the HM3/LGTQ side of P3.2.

### Recommended hybrid formulation

The digit route is still useful, but the correct package is:

1. **Exact zero-digit DP.** Use
   \[
   p\mid b_n\iff \text{some base-}p\text{ digit of }n\text{ lies in }Z_p.
   \]
2. **Complete Mellin estimate.** Target the empirically suggested
   \[
   \max_{\chi\ne1}|T_p(\chi)|\ll\sqrt p,
   \]
   or a sufficiently strong \(2k\)-th spectral moment for the actual number of free digits.
3. **Incomplete/prefix estimate.** Control \(T_{p,q}(\chi)\), or the corresponding partial transfer operators, whenever the ATI3 cutoff is not aligned with \(p^k\).
4. **Keep the large-prime argument.** Retain the existing top-window/moving-target machinery for \(p>\sqrt X\), where digit amplification cannot iterate.
5. **Use digit mixing in small and intermediate bands.** It can reduce candidate sets, give exact local densities, and potentially supply better inputs to a subsequent cross-prime sieve, even though those primes are not the present source of the \(e^{o(n)}\) obstruction.

A particularly promising bridge is (22): prove the one-period square-root Mellin bound through the ACSOP sheaf, then feed it into (3), (4), and (23). That is stronger and more relevant than first aiming only for a fixed \((1-\delta)\)-gap.

## Bottom line by sub-question

### (a)
No scalar additive product exists. The exact object is the push-forward of a \(k\)-fold tensor measure under multiplication, equivalently a multiplicative-convolution power. Equations (1), (3), and (4) are the exact decompositions. For arbitrary \(X\), use the radix recursion (23).

### (b)
Yes: if \(M_p\le(1-\delta)N_p\), every nontrivial Mellin mode gains \((1-\delta)^k\). This controls the centered nonzero component. The raw sum also contains the explicit main term \(p^k-pN_p^k/(p-1)\), and a constant gap is not quantitatively useful when \(k\) is bounded and \(p\to\infty\).

### (c)
For fixed \(p\), the weakest qualitative condition is \(M_p<N_p\), equivalently that the nonzero digit values generate \(\mathbf F_p^\times\). For a uniform exponential rate, require \(M_p\le(1-\delta)N_p\). For a fixed number of digits, the weaker and more natural input is the spectral-moment bound (17) or (18). The condition \(|T_p(\chi)|<p-1\) is not generally enough; it may even be automatic when \(z_p\ge2\).

### (d)
The ATI3 sums can be rewritten exactly in multi-digit Mellin form. They cannot, in the current P3.2 proof architecture, replace the hard large-prime completion step: \(p\le\sqrt X\) is already negligible in the GCD bound, while \(p>\sqrt X\) supplies too few free digits. The productive route is hybrid: exact digit DP plus Mellin amplification in small/intermediate prime bands, coupled to the existing large-prime/moving-target argument.
