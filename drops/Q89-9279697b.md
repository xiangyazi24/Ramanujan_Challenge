# The difference module approach: does it close the polynomial cocycle expansion gap?

## Executive conclusion

The difference-module idea identifies the correct geometric object, but the hoped-for theorem is not currently available in the required form. Arithmetic difference modules give a Galois theory for the cocycle, and they can encode the running product, but there is no Katz-style theorem saying that Frobenius classes of a non-autonomous difference module at consecutive integer points equidistribute merely from having full difference Galois group.

The missing statement is genuinely a new type of arithmetic-dynamical equidistribution theorem.

## 1. Can a sheaf have stalk equal to the cocycle state?

For a differential equation

$$Y'=A(x)Y,$$

one has a connection and horizontal sections. The solution operator is a path-ordered exponential.

For a difference equation

$$Y(x+1)=M(x)Y(x),$$

the analogue is a difference module: a vector bundle equipped with a semilinear operator

$$\sigma(Y)=M(x)Y.$$

So formally the Apéry recurrence does define a rank-two difference module.

However, there is a crucial distinction:

- the difference module records the transition rule;
- the desired quantity is the accumulated transport from 0 to n.

A stalk of an ordinary l-adic sheaf at n does not naturally contain the ordered product

$$M(n-1)\cdots M(0).$$

The product is a fundamental solution matrix, not a local Frobenius action.

## 2. Why Katz's mechanism does not transfer automatically

Katz equidistribution works because one has a sheaf on a curve over a finite field. At each point x, Frobenius gives a conjugacy class. Deligne's theorem controls sums over x.

The Apéry problem asks about

$$\Pi_n=M(n-1)\cdots M(0).$$

This is not the Frobenius at n of the original difference module. It is the discrete parallel transport from a fixed base point to n.

The analogue would require a theorem controlling the distribution of transport matrices of an arithmetic difference equation.

Such a theorem is not part of standard arithmetic D-module theory.

## 3. Difference Galois group versus orbit distribution

Suppose the difference Galois group is SL_2.

This gives an algebraic independence statement:

- no invariant subspace;
- no reduction to a proper algebraic subgroup;
- full monodromy of the difference equation.

But it does not imply that the particular orbit

$$\Pi_n$$

is equidistributed.

Analogy: a deterministic sequence can have Zariski-dense closure without being equidistributed.

The same obstruction appeared in Q86: monodromy is algebraic information; mixing is analytic information.

## 4. The Apéry-specific situation

The Apéry matrices have special structure:

$$M(n)\in SL_2(\mathbb F_p)$$

with polynomial entries in n. The associated difference module is therefore a polynomial-coefficient sigma-module.

The strongest available route would be:

1. Construct the sigma-module over a suitable arithmetic base.
2. Prove its difference Galois group is SL_2.
3. Relate the transport matrices to trace functions of an auxiliary sheaf.
4. Apply Deligne bounds to correlations of these trace functions.

Step 3 is the hard point. It is not enough to know that the module exists.

## 5. Is there a theorem for finite-field difference modules?

Known difference Galois theories (Hardouin-Singer, Di Vizio, Wibmer, Chatzidakis-Hrushovski) answer questions about algebraic relations and Galois groups.

They do not provide a finite-field analogue of:

"A lisse sheaf with connected monodromy group G has Frobenius classes equidistributed in G."

The reason is structural: Frobenius classes are local objects indexed by geometric points. Difference transport products are global ordered products depending on a base point.

## 6. Lyapunov exponent viewpoint

The Furstenberg theorem for random matrix products does not directly apply.

A deterministic polynomial cocycle has no probability measure on generators. The relevant object is a deterministic skew product

$$n\mapsto (n+1,M(n)\Pi_n).$$

One would need a finite-field analogue of a positive Lyapunov exponent implying spectral mixing.

Such results exist for random cocycles, but not for arbitrary deterministic polynomial cocycles over finite fields.

## 7. Final verdict

The difference-module approach is the correct conceptual language for the Apéry cocycle, but it does not by itself solve the last wall.

What is missing is a theorem of the following form:

**Arithmetic polynomial cocycle equidistribution theorem.**

Let M(x) be a polynomial SL_2-valued cocycle over F_p with full difference Galois group. Then the transport matrices

$$M(n-1)\cdots M(0)$$

become equidistributed as n ranges through F_p (with quantitative error).

No such general theorem is currently known. Proving it would combine ideas from Katz sheaves, expansion, difference Galois theory, and deterministic cocycle dynamics.

For the Apéry GCD problem, the difference module gives the correct framework and may reduce the remaining work to proving a special-case polynomial cocycle mixing theorem for this K3/Apéry sigma-module, but it does not make the proof automatic.
