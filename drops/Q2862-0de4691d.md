# 1. Why (5) is the correct radicalized Smith statement

At a good `p`-adic presentation, Q2841 works in

\[
B=\mathbf Z_p[x]/(F)
\]

and proves the exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow B/GB
 \xrightarrow{\cdot Q}
 B/H_0B
 \longrightarrow B/QB
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]

For a finite `Z_p`-module `M`, let

\[
\mu_p(M)=\dim_{\mathbf F_p}(M/pM).
\tag{14}
\]

This is exactly the number of positive Smith exponents, i.e. the number of
invariant factors divisible by `p`. Therefore, for the affine quotient,

\[
\mu_p(B/GB)=r_p(h,k),\qquad
\mu_p(B/H_0B)=t_p(h,h+k).
\tag{15}
\]

A submodule of an `r`-generated module over a DVR needs at most `r`
generators, and minimal numbers of generators are subadditive in a short
exact sequence. Hence (13) gives

\[
r_p(h,k)\le t_p(h,h+k)
 \le r_p(h,k)+\mu_p(B/QB).
\tag{16}
\]

Equivalently, on trimmed reductions, (1) says

\[
H_0\equiv -QG\pmod F,
\]

so irreducible-factor valuations give directly

\[
\deg\gcd(\bar F,\bar G)
\le
\deg\gcd(\bar F,\bar Q\bar G)
\le
\deg\gcd(\bar F,\bar G)+\deg\gcd(\bar F,\bar Q).
\tag{17}
\]

The second restart identity is what makes `Q` a genuine boundary term. Q2841
proved in `B`

\[
CQ=S_h^6.
\tag{18}
\]

Consequently every affine local component of `B/QB` is supported over
`S_h=0`; off that divisor `Q` is a unit. This yields (7) and (6). More
strongly, for every maximal ideal `m` containing `p,G` but not `S_h`,

\[
\boxed{
(B/GB)_{\mathfrak m}\simeq(B/H_0B)_{\mathfrak m}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

So away from the boundary not only the support but the complete local
algebra, all nilpotent thickness, and every Smith layer are unchanged.

This is exactly the right statement for

\[
\Sigma_{\rm aff}=\prod_i\operatorname{rad}(s_i),
\]

because

\[
v_p\Sigma_{\rm aff}=\mu_p(A_{\rm aff})
=\deg\gcd(\bar F,\bar G).
\tag{20}
\]

It is a different statistic from Q2841's last Smith exponent. For the
present question the relevant invariant is the **number** of positive Smith
layers, not their maximum.

# 2. The boundary term can be counted exactly enough, but only at quartic congestion

Define the logarithmic boundary budget for a fixed first height by

\[
B_h:=\sum_p\beta_p(h)\log p.
\tag{21}
\]

There is an unconditional bound

\[
\boxed{B_h=O(h^2).}
\tag{22}
\]

This uses an exact Apéry boundary factorization, not a generic height bound.
For `2<=j<=h`, the tridiagonal continuant matrix from `meso_result.tex`
splits at `x=-j`, because the off-diagonal entry `(x+j)^3` vanishes. Hence

\[
N_h(-j)=N_j(-j)N_{h-j+1}(-1).
\]

Using reflection and the already proved evaluation

\[
N_m(-1)=b_{m-1}((m-1)!)^3,
\]

one gets the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
N_h(-j)=(-1)^{j-1}
 b_{j-1}b_{h-j}
 ((j-1)!(h-j)!)^3.
}
\tag{23}
\]

Now split primes at `p<=h` and `p>h`.

For `p<=h`, trivially

\[
\beta_p(h)\le \deg N_h=3(h-1),
\]

so Chebyshev's bound `sum_{p<=h} log p=O(h)` gives

\[
\sum_{p\le h}\beta_p(h)\log p=O(h^2).
\tag{24}
\]

For `p>h`, the roots `-2,...,-h` of `S_h` are distinct modulo `p`, and the
sixth power caps the contribution of each root by six. Therefore

\[
\beta_p(h)
\le6\#\{2\le j\le h:p\mid N_h(-j)\}.
\tag{25}
\]

The factorials in (23) are `p`-units for `p>h`, so such a prime must divide
`b_{j-1}b_{h-j}`. Consequently

\[
\sum_{p>h}\beta_p(h)\log p
\le
6\sum_{j=2}^h
 \log\operatorname{rad}_{>h}(b_{j-1}b_{h-j})
\le
12\sum_{m<h}\log b_m.
\tag{26}
\]

The defining binomial sum gives `log b_m=O(m)` (for example
`b_m<=(m+1)64^m` is enough), hence the last expression is `O(h^2)`. This
proves (22).

After summing (5) over the triangle, however, the same first-height boundary
budget is available for every `k`. Its congestion is

\[
\sum_{\substack{h,k\ge2\\h+k<H}} B_h
=
\sum_{h=2}^{H-3}(H-h+O(1))B_h
=O(H^4).
\tag{27}
\]

Thus even the exact boundary accounting only removes the extra logarithm
from a raw `H^4 log H` ledger; it does not produce a positive power saving.
There is no hidden `H^3` consequence here.

Notice also that for the direction actually needed to bound the separated
quantity, (5) gives simply

\[
r_p(h,k)\le t_p(h,h+k),
\tag{28}
\]

with **no** boundary loss. So the fundamental problem is not that the
boundary estimate is sloppy. The problem is that the right side lives at
the same total height.

# 5. Smallest falsifiable next lemma

The smallest next statement I would test is a one-row radical saving. It is
strictly weaker than the full triangular target and directly attacks the
congestion that restart leaves untouched.

> **Row radical saving.** There exists `delta>0` such that, uniformly for
> `h>=2` and `K>=2`,
> \[
> \boxed{
> \sum_{k=2}^{K}
>  \log\Sigma_{\rm aff}(h,k)
> \ll
> h\,K^{2-\delta}(h+K)^{o(1)}.
> }
> \tag{34}
> \]

The raw resultant-height ledger gives only

\[
\sum_{k\le K}\log\Sigma_{\rm aff}(h,k)
\le
\sum_{k\le K}\log|\mathcal R_{h,k}|
\ll hK^2\log(h+K),
\tag{35}
\]

so (34) asks for exactly one genuine power saving in the second index and
nothing more. Summing (34) for `h<=H` immediately gives

\[
\mathcal S(H)\ll H^{4-\delta+o(1)}.
\]

It is also directly falsifiable: for each fixed `h`, compute the trimmed
finite-field gcd degrees (equivalently the radicalized Smith layers) across
`k<=K` and compare the accumulated logarithmic prime weight with `hK^2`.
Unlike a reverse-restart statement, (34) is not already contradicted by the
`p=653` event; it allows isolated same-height returns, but requires them not
to saturate the full row resultant height.

A proof of (34) would have to use new arithmetic of the actual Apéry
recurrence—e.g. a first-return/period restriction for the shifted finite-field
orbit, or a prime-weighted squarefree/resultant estimate. The two restart
identities alone cannot supply it.
