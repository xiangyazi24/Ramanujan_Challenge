# Q8492 — Local three-slope Laurent identity bridge audit

## Verdict

**Conclusion: no exact quantifier-preserving bridge is currently available.** The local three-slope Laurent twisted-Euler identity (verified for h=1,...,4) does not imply any of the three required global premises without an additional theorem controlling uniformity in height, strip length, and multi-shift correlations.

## Classification

- The verified Laurent identities for h=1,...,4 are **exact finite facts**.
- The width-three slope-hole arc extracted from them is a **proved scoped consequence**.
- Any implication from that arc to the top sixteen-point factorial tail, intermediate eight-cube aggregate, or far-strip positive-part estimate is only a **conditional implication**, not a theorem.
- The missing bridge is a **scoped obstruction**.

## Obstruction

The local identity has fixed finite slope depth. A global premise requires estimates after summing over a growing family of residues/shifts:

\[
X\le m<2X,\qquad h\text{-tuples up to }16,
\]

or analogous ranges. The local identity gives cancellation only for a bounded Laurent combination. It does not provide:

1. a uniform bound as h grows;
2. control of exceptional residue classes where all local slope holes align;
3. a positivity-compatible estimate after taking absolute values or positive parts;
4. normalization losses small enough to survive the required powers of X and logarithms.

Therefore no quantifier-preserving implication of the form

\[
\forall h\le 4\;L_h=0 \Longrightarrow \sup_m T_{16}(m)=o(L^{16}/X^{15})
\]

can be derived from the stated local theorem alone.

## Smaller genuine lemma

A strictly smaller valid target is the following.

**Lemma (bounded-width missing-slope exclusion).** Fix h≤4. Let A_h be the finite Laurent support produced by the verified three-slope identity. Then the corresponding slope-hole arc cannot contain all three adjacent slopes simultaneously.

**Proof.** The verified identity gives an exact linear relation among the three Laurent slope contributions. If all three adjacent slope coefficients survived, the relation would force the forbidden nonzero coefficient combination in the finite support calculation. Hence at least one of the three slopes is absent. This is exactly the width-three slope-hole statement already extracted from the identity. ∎

## What would be needed next

A genuine bridge would require a new theorem extending this finite obstruction to a family indexed by the packet size, with explicit losses. At minimum it would need a statement of the form:

```text
For every packet size k, every admissible shift family S of size k,
and every X in the required range, the number of configurations escaping
all Laurent slope holes is bounded by the required factorial-tail exponent.
```

No such theorem follows from the current h≤4 identities.
