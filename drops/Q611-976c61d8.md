# Continued fraction / Möbius walk proof of AFP

## Summary

The Möbius-walk formulation is a very good reformulation of the AFP problem. It identifies zeros of the Apéry sequence with hitting a moving target in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\):

\[
\rho_{r-1}=\frac{(r-1)^3}{c(r)}.
\]

The heuristic giving Poisson(1/2) after folding is exactly the correct random-matrix heuristic. However, the currently available expansion results for random walks on \(PGL_2(\mathbf F_p)\) do not directly apply, because the matrices are a deterministic polynomial cocycle rather than independent random generators.

The key missing theorem is a **deterministic orbit equidistribution theorem for the Apéry transfer cocycle**.

---

# (A) Does large monodromy imply equidistribution?

No, not by itself.

The statement

\[
G_{\mathrm{geom}}=SL_2
\]

means that the Zariski closure of the differential monodromy is large. After reduction modulo \(p\), it implies that the generated subgroup of matrices is often large.

But the trajectory is not a random walk:

\[
\rho_r=M_rM_{r-1}\cdots M_1\rho_0
\]

with

\[
M_r=
\begin{pmatrix}
 c(r)&-(r-1)^3\\
 r^3&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The matrices are correlated through the parameter \(r\).

Large subgroup generation gives:

- irreducibility;
- no invariant finite subset of \(P^1\);
- absence of obvious algebraic obstruction.

It does **not** automatically give:

\[
\#\{r\le p:\rho_r=a\}=1+o(1)
\]

for every target \(a\).

---

# (B) Bourgain-Gamburd/Kowalski type results

The analogy is close but the hypotheses fail.

For a genuine random walk

\[
g_1g_2\cdots g_n
\]

with generators satisfying expansion conditions, one obtains flattening and equidistribution on \(SL_2(\mathbf F_p)\).

Examples include:

- Bourgain-Gamburd expansion for fixed generating sets;
- Gamburd's results on random matrix products;
- Kowalski's large sieve methods for Frobenius and trace distributions.

Here the walk is:

\[
M_1,M_2,\ldots,M_{p-2}
\]

where each step is used exactly once.

This is closer to a **non-autonomous dynamical system** than a random walk.

The missing input would be a statement of the form:

> Polynomially varying products of an arithmetic cocycle with large monodromy have square-root cancellation on projective orbits.

This is not currently a standard theorem.

---

# (C) Continued fraction viewpoint

The continued fraction identity

\[
\rho_r=
\frac{c(r)}{r^3}-\frac{(r-1)^3}{r^3\rho_{r-1}}
\]

is exactly the finite-field analogue of the Apéry continued fraction.

Over characteristic zero, the convergents are controlled by the differential equation.

Over finite fields, the orbit lives in the finite projective line:

\[
P^1(\mathbf F_p),\qquad |P^1(\mathbf F_p)|=p+1.
\]

There are general results for continued fractions over finite fields, but mostly for:

- periodic continued fractions;
- random continued fractions;
- Euclidean algorithms over function fields.

They do not directly cover a Picard-Fuchs recurrence with polynomial coefficients.

The connection with monodromy is nevertheless exact:

continued fraction convergents are projective images of transfer matrices, and transfer matrices are the discrete monodromy representation.

---

# (D) Can one prove the O(1) collision bound?

The desired statement is:

\[
N_p=
\#\left\{r:
M_r\cdots M_1(\rho_0)=\frac{(r-1)^3}{c(r)}
\right\}=O(1).
\]

This is a very strong statement. It is not known from general arithmetic dynamics.

The main obstacles are:

## 1. Moving target

Standard orbit results study

\[
\rho_r=a
\]

for fixed \(a\).

Here the target moves:

\[
a_r=\frac{(r-1)^3}{c(r)}.
\]

This requires cancellation for the intersection of an orbit with an algebraic curve in

\[
\mathbb A^1_r\times P^1_x.
\]

---

## 2. Non-independent matrices

The sequence

\[
M_r
\]

has arithmetic structure. Independence heuristics are excellent numerically, but proving them requires controlling correlations among all partial products.

---

## 3. Singular times

At values where denominators vanish modulo \(p\), the projective dynamics has exceptional behavior. These correspond to singular fibers of the original differential equation.

---

# A more realistic route: trace-function estimates

The collision condition can be rewritten as

\[
W_r=f_{r-1}c(r)-(r-1)^3g_{r-1}=0.
\]

Thus AFP reduces to bounding zeros of a sequence produced by the transfer cocycle.

Instead of discrepancy of the orbit, one should seek:

\[
\sum_r \psi(W_r)=O(p^{1/2})
\]

for nontrivial additive characters \(\psi\).

This is a Deligne/Katz style exponential sum problem.

A square-root cancellation estimate would immediately imply:

\[
|Z_p|=O(1)
\]

by standard amplification.

---

# (E) Discrepancy bounds

The proposed discrepancy relation

\[
|Z_p|\le 1+D
\]

is conceptually correct.

But the optimal discrepancy

\[
D=O(1/p)
\]

is much stronger than known finite-field orbit results.

For random points on \(P^1(\mathbf F_p)\), one expects fluctuations of size about \(p^{-1/2}\), not \(p^{-1}\).

The reason the observed Apéry zeros are still O(1) is that we only need collisions with a moving algebraic target, not uniform discrepancy against every subset.

The right target is a weaker statement:

\[
\sum_r 1_{\rho_r=a_r}=O(1).
\]

This is an incidence estimate, not a discrepancy estimate.

---

# Final assessment

The Möbius walk discovery is probably the most concrete formulation of AFP so far.

The implications are:

1. The Poisson(1/2) law has a natural explanation: random projective hitting probability \(1/p\).

2. Large \(SL_2\) monodromy explains why random behavior is plausible.

3. Existing expansion theorems do not directly prove it because the walk is deterministic and non-autonomous.

4. The likely rigorous route is not Bourgain-Gamburd, but a Katz/Deligne theorem for the transfer cocycle viewed as an arithmetic sheaf.

The missing theorem can be formulated as:

> The Apéry projective cocycle has bounded intersection with the algebraic section \(x=(r-1)^3/c(r)\) over \(\mathbf F_p\).

Proving this would essentially prove AFP and therefore the \((HM)_3\) input for the Apéry GCD conjecture.
