# Sato--Tate for the Apéry weight-4 form

## Short answer

Yes, the Sato--Tate conjecture is proved for the Apéry modular form, but one must distinguish several statements:

1. The qualitative Sato--Tate distribution is known for this specific non-CM newform.
2. The automorphy of symmetric powers needed for the usual proof is available in this GL(2) setting.
3. A strong quantitative error term for the exact triple-prime correlation requires more than merely knowing Sym^2, Sym^3, Sym^4 are automorphic; it requires a careful trace-function analysis and conductor control.

## 1. The Apéry form and Sato--Tate

The Apéry form is the weight-4 level-6 newform associated with the transcendental part of the Apéry K3 family. Its compatible system has Hodge--Tate weights {0,3}. After normalization

\[
\alpha_p=a_p/p^{3/2},
\]

one has \(|\alpha_p|\leq 2\), and the expected Sato--Tate group is SU(2) because the form is non-CM.

For non-CM regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representations of GL(2)/Q, the Sato--Tate theorem follows from the automorphy of sufficiently many symmetric powers. This framework includes the Apéry form.

References:

- Clozel, Harris, Shepherd-Barron, Taylor, potential automorphy results for regular algebraic representations.
- Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris, Taylor, "A family of Calabi-Yau varieties and potential automorphy" and related papers proving symmetric power automorphy inputs.
- Newton and Thorne, symmetric power functoriality results for GL(2).

## 2. Symmetric powers

### Sym^2

Correct. The symmetric square lift

\[
\mathrm{Sym}^2(f):GL_2\rightarrow GL_3
\]

is classical. Gelbart--Jacquet proved automorphy of Sym^2 for cuspidal GL(2) representations.

Therefore

\[
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^2 f)
\]

has analytic continuation and functional equation.

### Sym^3

Also correct. Kim--Shahidi proved the symmetric cube lift:

\[
\mathrm{Sym}^3(f):GL_2\rightarrow GL_4.
\]

Hence

\[
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^3 f)
\]

is automorphic and entire for a non-dihedral cusp form.

Reference:

- H. Kim and F. Shahidi, "Functorial products for GL(2) × GL(3) and the symmetric cube for GL(2)".

### Sym^4

Also essentially correct. Kim proved the symmetric fourth power lift:

\[
\mathrm{Sym}^4(f):GL_2\rightarrow GL_5.
\]

Reference:

- H. Kim, "Functoriality for the exterior square of GL(4) and the symmetric fourth of GL(2)".

Therefore the Apéry form has known analytic symmetric powers through degree four.

## 3. Quantitative Sato--Tate

Knowing Sym^n automorphy gives analytic continuation and bounds for coefficients of symmetric power L-functions. Using the explicit formula, one obtains effective equidistribution estimates of the shape

\[
\#\{p\le x: \theta_p\in I\}=\mu_{ST}(I)\pi(x)+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^A}\right)
\]

for suitable fixed A depending on the available symmetric power estimates.

However, this is not automatically the same as a three-prime CRT correlation estimate. The triple correlation is a product of three independent local trace functions, and the required estimate is closer to a Deligne/Katz bound for a tensor-product sheaf.

## 4. Triple tensor product

For a single SU(2) representation V,

\[
V\otimes V=\mathrm{Sym}^2V\oplus 1.
\]

Hence

\[
V^{\otimes3}=V\otimes(\mathrm{Sym}^2V\oplus1).
\]

Using Clebsch--Gordan:

\[
V\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^2V=\mathrm{Sym}^3V\oplus V.
\]

Therefore

\[
V^{\otimes3}=\mathrm{Sym}^3V\oplus V\oplus V.
\]

This calculation is correct.

There is no trivial representation in the odd tensor power of the standard SU(2) representation. Equivalently,

\[
(V^{\otimes3})^{SU(2)}=0.
\]

Thus the geometric monodromy obstruction coming from a constant summand is absent.

## 5. Does this prove (HM)_3?

Not by itself.

The representation-theoretic statement removes the main obstruction: a trivial summand would create a main term of size comparable to the number of triples. Since it is absent, Deligne's Riemann hypothesis gives square-root cancellation for the relevant trace sums.

But to convert this into the Apéry factorial moment bound one still needs:

1. identify the exact lisse sheaf attached to the Apéry zero indicator;
2. prove its geometric monodromy is the expected SL(2)/Sp(2);
3. prove the tensor product over three independent primes has no constant component;
4. control conductors uniformly in the prime parameters;
5. sum the resulting local bounds over triples.

The symmetric-power automorphy of the modular form is strong evidence, but it is not literally a proof of the moving-modulus CRT correlation estimate.

## 6. Triple product L-function interpretation

The relevant triple product is not simply

\[
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^3 f).
\]

The symmetric cube controls the decomposition of

\[
V^{\otimes3}
\]

under one copy of the Galois group. The CRT problem involves three independent Frobenius variables:

\[
V_p\otimes V_q\otimes V_r.
\]

The absence of invariants is the same representation-theoretic phenomenon, but the analytic object is a multi-variable trace correlation rather than a single Euler product.

## Conclusion

- The Sato--Tate conjecture for the Apéry form is proved.
- Sym^2, Sym^3, and Sym^4 analytic continuation are available by Gelbart--Jacquet, Kim--Shahidi, and Kim.
- The decomposition

\[
V^{\otimes3}=\mathrm{Sym}^3V\oplus V\oplus V
\]

is correct and implies no SU(2)-invariant summand.
- This gives the correct representation-theoretic reason why triple correlations should have square-root cancellation.
- It does not alone prove (HM)_3; the missing step is a uniform geometric/analytic estimate for the three-prime CRT correlation sheaf attached to the Apéry zero sets.
