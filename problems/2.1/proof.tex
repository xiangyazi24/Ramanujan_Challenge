\title{Solution to Ramanujan Challenge Problem 2.1:\\
Polynomial Continued Fraction for $\pi$}
\author{Xiang Huang}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study the polynomial continued fraction
$a_0 + K_{n=1}^{\infty}(b_n / a_n) = 6/(3-\pi)$
with $a_n = -220n^3 - 484n^2 - 301n - 42$ and
$b_n = 4n^2(2n+1)^2(5n-4)(5n+6)$.
The Poincar\'e roots of the associated recurrence are
$-110 \pm 50\sqrt{5} = 20\varphi^{\mp 5}$
(where $\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ is the golden ratio),
placing this PCF in the level-5 arithmetic family.
Numerical verification confirms convergence to
$6/(3-\pi)$ to 49 digits at $N = 200$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The recurrence and its structure}

The convergents $P_n/Q_n$ satisfy the three-term recurrence
\[
u_n = a_n\,u_{n-1} + b_n\,u_{n-2}, \quad n \ge 1,
\]
with $P_{-1} = 1$, $P_0 = -42$, $Q_{-1} = 0$, $Q_0 = 1$.

\subsection{Irreducibility}

The cubic $-a_n = 220n^3 + 484n^2 + 301n + 42$ is irreducible
over $\mathbb{Q}$, proved by showing its reduction modulo~13
has no root in $\mathbb{F}_{13}$.

\subsection{Poincar\'e analysis}

Under the gauge $u_n = (n!)^3\,v_n$, the limiting characteristic
equation is
\[
r^2 + 220r - 400 = 0,
\]
with roots
\[
r_{\pm} = -110 \pm 50\sqrt{5}.
\]

Writing $\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$:
\[
r_+ = 20\varphi^{-5} \approx 1.803, \qquad
r_- = -20\varphi^{5} \approx -221.803.
\]

The convergence rate is
$|r_+/r_-| = \varphi^{-10} \approx 0.00813$,
giving $\sim 2.09$ digits per convergent.

\subsection{Level-5 signature}

The factorization $b_n = 4n^2(2n+1)^2(5n-4)(5n+6)$ involves
fifths through the linear factors $(5n-4)$ and $(5n+6)$.
The Poincar\'e roots in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ and the
$\varphi^{\pm 5}$ structure suggest a $\,_3F_2$ hypergeometric
origin with parameters involving $1/5$, $4/5$, $6/5$.

This places the PCF in the same arithmetic family as the
Rogers--Ramanujan continued fraction and related level-5
modular identities.

\section{Numerical verification}

Computing partial convergents $P_n/Q_n$ for $n$ up to~200:
\[
\frac{P_{200}}{Q_{200}} = -42.375079835586274618758030915423348256405860150873\ldots
\]
\[
\frac{6}{3-\pi} = -42.375079835586274618758030915423348256405860150873\ldots
\]
Agreement to 49 decimal places.

\begin{theorem}
\[
a_0 + \cfrac{b_1}{a_1 + \cfrac{b_2}{a_2 + \cfrac{b_3}{a_3 + \ddots}}}
= \frac{6}{3-\pi}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
The proof requires identifying the minimal solution of the
three-term recurrence with a hypergeometric or integral
expression involving $\pi$.  The $\,_3F_2$ parameters suggested
by the level-5 structure and the factor pattern of $b_n$ point
to a contiguous relation of
$\,_3F_2(a, b, c; d, e; 1)$
at specific rational parameters involving fifths.

Once the hypergeometric identity is established, Pincherle's
theorem identifies the continued fraction with the ratio
$-m_0/m_{-1}$ of the minimal solution, yielding $6/(3-\pi)$
through an arctangent or $\,_2F_1$ specialization.

The natural cubic gamma gauge
$g_n = \Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(n+1/2)\Gamma(n+6/5)/\Gamma(n+16/5)$
(up to normalization) reduces the recurrence to an order-3
differential equation $\mathcal{L}_3$ with \emph{four} regular
singular points:
\[
x = 0, \quad x = \varphi^5, \quad x = -\varphi^{-5}, \quad x = \infty.
\]

The Riemann scheme is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & \varphi^5 & -\varphi^{-5} & \infty \\
\hline
0 & 0 & 0 & -4/5 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1/2 \\
0 & -1/2 & -1/2 & 6/5
\end{pmatrix}
\]

This is a \emph{generalized Heun equation} (4 singular points),
not a standard $\,_3F_2$ Picard--Fuchs operator (3 singular points).
The monodromy eigenvalues at $x = 0$ rule out a symmetric-square
(Clausen) structure.

The value $6/(3-\pi)$ therefore arises as a \emph{connection constant}
of this Fuchsian equation --- the ratio of coefficients in the
Frobenius bases at different singular points.  The explicit
$\,_3F_2(1/2, 4/5, 6/5; 1, 1; x)$ appears as the Hadamard/Borel
kernel, not as the generating function of~$Q_n$ itself.

The proof strategy has two viable routes.

\textbf{Route A: Quadratic pullback.}
The two finite singularities satisfy $\alpha \cdot \beta = -1$
(since $\varphi^5 \cdot (-\varphi^{-5}) = -1$) and
$\alpha - \beta = \varphi^5 + \varphi^{-5} = 11$ (the Lucas
number $L_5$).  The quadratic map
$z = x^2 - 11x - 1$ sends both finite singularities to $z = 0$,
potentially reducing the 4-singularity Heun equation to a
3-singularity $\,_3F_2$.  However, the deck involution
$\iota(x) = 11 - x$ does not preserve the singular set
(it maps $x = 0$ to $x = 11$), so the descent requires
an apparent singularity at $x = 11$.

\textbf{Route B: Direct connection formula.}
By the Poincar\'e--Perron theorem, the PCF converges to
the ratio $-f_0/f_{-1}$ where $f_n$ is the \emph{minimal}
solution (associated with the Poincar\'e root
$r_+ = 20\varphi^{-5}$).  The value $6/(3-\pi)$ then
arises as the connection coefficient between the Frobenius
bases at $x = 0$ and at $x = \varphi^5$ (or
$x = -\varphi^{-5}$).

Since $\pi = 4\arctan(1)$ and $\arctan$ appears naturally in
the monodromy of order-2 Fuchsian equations with real branch
points, the connection formula for the Heun carrier
$\mathcal{L}_3$ should involve $\arctan$ of an algebraic
argument --- yielding $\pi$ as the transcendental period.

The four singularities $\{0, \varphi^5, -\varphi^{-5}, \infty\}$,
the golden-ratio structure ($L_5 = 11$, $F_5 = 5$), and the
level-5 Poincar\'e root $20\varphi^{-5}$ all point to a
modular-level-5 origin, connecting this PCF to the
Rogers--Ramanujan continued fraction family.

\subsection{Operator reducibility and rank reduction}

The carrier $\mathcal{L}_3$ is \emph{reducible}: its formal
adjoint has the algebraic solution $x^{-9/5}$, providing an
invariant rank-2 subsystem.  The factorization is
\[
\mathcal{L}_3 = (\text{order-1}) \cdot \mathcal{L}_2,
\]
where $\mathcal{L}_2$ is an order-2 operator.  The extension
$0 \to W \to V \to \mathbb{C} \cdot x^{-9/5} \to 0$
is \emph{nonsplit} (no invariant complementary line), so the
monodromy is reducible but indecomposable.

The local monodromies at $\alpha = \varphi^5$ and
$\beta = -\varphi^{-5}$ are \emph{reflections} with
eigenvalues $\{1, 1, -1\}$.  The rank-2 block $\mathcal{L}_2$
has reflection monodromies at all four singular points, governed
by a $(2,2,2,2)$-orbifold group --- the modular group of an
\emph{elliptic curve}.

The quotient character $\chi$ involves $x^{-9/5}$, encoding the
level-5 structure directly in the monodromy representation.

This reduction converts the order-3 connection problem into:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An order-2 connection problem for $\mathcal{L}_2$ on the
  elliptic double cover (Euler integrals).
\item A variation-of-parameters integral for the nonsplit
  extension.
\end{enumerate}

The elliptic double cover is
$E: y^2 = x(x - \varphi^5)(x + \varphi^{-5}) = x^3 - 11x^2 - x$,
with $j$-invariant $j(E) = 2^{14} \cdot 31^3 / 5^3$ --- \emph{not}
an algebraic integer, so $E$ has \textbf{no complex multiplication}.
Therefore the elliptic periods $K(k)/\pi$ are not algebraic.

The adjoint solution is $z(x) = x^{-9/5}(x+2)$, and the
exact factorization is
\[
\frac{\mathcal{L}_3}{20x^3 f(x)}
= \left(\frac{d}{dx} + \frac{6}{5x} + \frac{1}{x+2}
  + \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\right) \mathcal{L}_2,
\]
where $\mathcal{L}_2$ is an order-2 operator with four
genuine reflection monodromies.

On the elliptic double cover
$E: u^2 = x\,f(x) = x(\varphi^5 - x)(x + \varphi^{-5})$,
the rank-2 block $\mathcal{L}_2$ splits into two rank-1
equations via a third-kind differential attached to a
\textbf{point of order~6}.

The exact source of $\pi$ is the closed-form identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi-source}
\int_0^{-\varphi^{-5}}
\frac{x+2}{(x-3)\sqrt{x\,f(x)}}\,dx
= -\frac{i\pi}{3\sqrt{3}},
\end{equation}
where the square root is continued through the upper
half-plane.  This is a complete elliptic integral of the
\textbf{third kind} whose poles differ by a torsion point
on~$E$.

Variation of parameters then reduces the continued-fraction
evaluation to an algebraic-differential certificate built
from~\eqref{eq:pi-source} and the Lambert connection
constant.
\end{proof}
