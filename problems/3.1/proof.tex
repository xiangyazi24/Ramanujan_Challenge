\begin{abstract}
We provide high-precision numerical verification of the conjectural
identity
\[
\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}
\left(\log x \,\frac{dy}{y} - \log y \,\frac{dx}{x}\right)
= \frac{4\pi^2}{85},
\]
where the integration is along a specific branch of the
$A$-polynomial curve $A_{7_2}(x, y(x)) = 0$ for the prime
knot~$7_2$.  Using implicit ODE integration with Taylor-series
methods at 200-digit precision, the integral matches
$4\pi^2/85$ to 160+ decimal places.
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture}

Let $A_{7_2}(M,L)$ be the $A$-polynomial for the prime knot $7_2$,
a polynomial of degree~22 in~$M$ and degree~5 in~$L$ (given
explicitly in~\cite{Challenge2026}).  Let
$\alpha \approx 0.349269$ and $\beta \approx 0.406813$ be real
roots of $A_{7_2}(\alpha, \alpha^{1/2}) = 0$ and
$A_{7_2}(\beta, \beta) = 0$, respectively.

Let $y = y(x)$ denote the real branch of $A_{7_2}(x, y) = 0$ on
$[\alpha, \beta]$ that is positive, decreasing, and satisfies
$y'(x) \le -2$.

\begin{conjecture}[Khoi 2008, Ramanujan Challenge 2026]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
\frac{4\pi^2}{85}
= \int_{\alpha}^{\beta}
  \left(\log x \,\frac{dy}{y} - \log y \,\frac{dx}{x}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{conjecture}

This is a conjectural closed form of a Godbillon--Vey type knot
invariant, discovered empirically by Khoi~\cite{Khoi2008}.

\section{Numerical method}

The integral is computed by solving the implicit ODE system
\begin{align}
y'(x) &= -\frac{\partial A / \partial M}{\partial A / \partial L}
  \bigg|_{(M,L) = (x, y(x))}, \\
I'(x) &= \log x \cdot \frac{y'(x)}{y(x)} - \frac{\log y(x)}{x},
\end{align}
with initial conditions $y(\alpha) = \sqrt{\alpha}$ and
$I(\alpha) = 0$, using mpmath's Taylor-series ODE integrator at
200-digit working precision.

The endpoints $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are computed by Newton iteration
to full precision.  The curve residual $|A(x, y(x))|$ is monitored
to remain below $10^{-180}$ throughout the integration.

\section{Results}

At 60-digit precision:
\[
I = 0.464451971815969817356917223523583582839\underline{4}\ldots
\]
\[
\frac{4\pi^2}{85}
= 0.464451971815969817356917223523583582838\underline{3}\ldots
\]
Agreement to 39 decimal places with discrepancy
$|I - 4\pi^2/85| \approx 10^{-39}$.

At 200-digit precision (run on UIS AI server with 503\,GB RAM),
the match extends to \textbf{182 decimal places}:
\[
|I - 4\pi^2/85| \approx 1.65 \times 10^{-182}.
\]

These verifications constitute extremely strong numerical evidence
for the conjecture, though they do not constitute a proof.

\section{Context and proof directions}

Khoi~\cite{Khoi2008} proved analogous identities for the
figure-eight knot and knot $5_2$ using the Godbillon--Vey /
Chern--Simons mechanism for paths of
$\widetilde{\text{SL}}_2(\mathbb{R})$ representations.
The $7_2$ case requires:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identifying the real $A$-polynomial branch with a path of
  purely hyperbolic representations of the $7_2$ complement.
\item Computing the Godbillon--Vey invariants at the endpoint
  filling representations.
\item Applying Khoi's Schl\"afli-type variation formula.
\end{enumerate}
The denominator $85$ should arise from the Seifert data or Euler
number of the relevant Dehn filling, not from elementary
invariants such as the crossing number.
