# Rigor audit of `problems/2.5/proof.tex`

## Executive verdict

The manuscript does **not** currently prove that the CMF converges to Catalan's constant. It contains several exact and essentially correct identities, and the numerical evidence is strong, but the final dependency chain has three theorem-level gaps:

1. The recurrences in Theorem 16 are guessed from 401 terms and checked only on those terms. A finite-prefix check is not an all-`k` annihilation certificate.
2. The positivity proof in Theorem 17 is not a valid effective asymptotic argument.
3. Most decisively, Theorem 18 does not prove that the two `xi = 1` connection coefficients vanish. The claimed rational Casorati projections are not obtained from finite rational linear algebra, and 30-digit agreement cannot prove exact equality.

Theorem 19 is a sound **conditional** averaging argument after minor repairs: if the decomposition, positivity, denominator growth, and `epsilon_k -> 0` are all proved, then it identifies the first normalized column's limit as `G`. It does not repair the missing hypotheses.

The two theorems called “Theorem 8” and “Theorem 9” in the audit request are `thm:eps-conv` and `thm:limit-G`; the current LaTeX counter numbers them Theorems 18 and 19 because of the earlier theorem and corollary environments.

## Direct answer about the 30-digit step

No. Agreement to 30 decimal digits between a rational number `B_j/A_j` and the integral defining `G` is not a proof of equality. At best, a correctly certified computation proves an inequality such as

```text
|B_j/A_j - G| < 10^(-30).
```

There are infinitely many distinct rational numbers in every interval about `G`. Decimal truncations of any real number give immediate counterexamples to the inference “many matching digits implies equality.” Exact arithmetic in the rational endpoint does not change this: it makes the rational number exact, but the comparison with `G` remains an inequality.

There is no separation principle in the manuscript that could turn this small inequality into equality. Such a principle would need, for example, a previously proved discreteness/height theorem for all possible values of the connection coefficient. None is supplied. In fact, if `A_j != 0`, the asserted identity `G = B_j/A_j` would prove that Catalan's constant is rational. That would settle the famous rationality question, and therefore cannot be treated as a routine consequence of a numerical match.

The cited alternating-series bounds do not even certify 30 digits. `S_n` is not defined in the manuscript. If it means the first `n` terms, 270 and 485 terms give a bracket of width on the order of `10^(-6)` (the corresponding exact bounds in the project have width approximately `4.50 * 10^(-6)`). If it means the partial sum through index `n`, the usual parity directions in the displayed inequality are reversed. Either interpretation is far from 30 digits.

There is also an earlier and more structural error. Let `phi_i` be the rational initial-value basis. If `ell_1, ell_2` denote the two asymptotic coefficient functionals at the root `xi = 1`, then

```text
ell_r(h) = sum_i ell_r(phi_i) h(i).
```

The numbers `ell_r(phi_i)` are global connection coefficients between the basis at `k = 0` and an asymptotic basis at infinity. A rational recurrence makes the values `phi_i(k)` rational at finite integer `k`; it does **not** make these connection coefficients rational. Computing coordinates in the initial-value basis is finite rational linear algebra. Projecting that basis onto asymptotic modes is a global analytic connection problem. Lines 643–675 conflate the two.

## Core dependency audit

```text
Theorem 1 (all three CMF ratios -> G)
  |
  +-- Theorem 14: a common limit L exists                 [not fully proved]
  |
  +-- Theorem 19: L = G                                  [valid only conditionally]
        |
        +-- Theorem 15: Delannoy decomposition            [algebraically valid]
        +-- Theorem 17: f(k) > 0                          [not proved]
        +-- Theorem 18: g(k)/f(k) -> G                    [fatal gap]
              |
              +-- Theorem 16: exact k-recurrences         [not proved]
```

Thus the final argument is not circular in Theorem 19 itself, but its load-bearing inputs are unproved. Elsewhere, the text repeatedly presents the numerically observed Catalan connection as if spectral/module matching had already established it; that is not a valid way to supply the missing connection coefficient.

## Theorem-by-theorem audit

### Theorem 1 — Main convergence theorem (lines 61–68)

**Verdict: not proved.** The numerical check at `N = 80` is evidence only. The purported later proof depends on Theorems 14, 16, 17, and 18, all of which have material gaps. Moreover, the Delannoy argument is written for undefined scalar sequences `hat P_N, hat Q_N`, apparently the normalized first column. The manuscript never explicitly connects that result back to all three original columns.

Hidden assumptions include nonvanishing of every `Q_{N,j}`, nonzero dominant denominator coefficient, a common column-independent limit, and the definition/common normalization of the hatted sequences.

### Theorem 2 — Exact moment lift (lines 85–115)

**Verdict: essentially rigorous and exact.** Because `R_0(X)` is affine and the matrices do not depend on `X`, `R_{N,j}` remains affine. Writing

```text
R_{N,j}(X) = Q_{N,j} - (1+X) P_{N,j}
```

makes all four identities immediate from the stated moments and the product-integral identity. The use of the Catalan integral is proved later and can simply be cited forward. The notation `P(-1)-P(t^2)` in line 110 should be `R(-1)-R(t^2)`, but the intended algebra is correct. No numerical verification is needed.

### Theorem 3 — Determinant obstruction (lines 159–178)

**Verdict: incomplete and partly computational.** The matrix `C(n)` is never defined in this manuscript, and the shift-orbit exponent calculation is asserted rather than displayed. If those divisor sums are explicitly derived, they can rigorously obstruct the stated natural twist.

The nullity-zero search for a prescribed denominator and row degree at most 11 proves only failure of that finite ansatz. It does not prove nonexistence of arbitrary rational gauges. “Low-degree” is not defined. The reciprocal determinant rules out a polynomial invertible gauge under the stated determinant equation, but it does not by itself rule out rational gauges.

### Theorem 4 — Integrated elliptic module (lines 190–200)

**Verdict: correct modulo a standard cited fact, but the proof is omitted.** For the standard convention for the complete elliptic integral `K(k)`, `K` satisfies the displayed second-order equation; applying it to `Y' = K/2` gives the third-order equation. The convention for `K` and the standard differential equation should be stated or cited.

The subsequent claim that this is *the* CMF module is not proved by this theorem. Matching rank, singularities, or Poincare roots does not identify a global difference/differential module or its connection constants.

### Theorem 5 — Brafman identity (lines 213–217)

**Verdict: acceptable if invoked as the classical Brafman identity, otherwise unproved.** The formula and radius are consistent, but the theorem has no proof and no precise citation at the point of use. State the convention for `K`, the branch of the square root, and that the identity is used for `|z| < rho` with the endpoint handled as an improper limit.

### Theorem 6 — Catalan integral (lines 222–247)

**Verdict: essentially rigorous, with minor endpoint details.** The substitution derivative and constants are correct. Tonelli/Fubini and the half-angle substitution give

```text
(1/2) integral_0^1 K(k) dk = integral_0^1 arctan(t)/t dt = G.
```

The logarithmic singularity at `k = 1` and the Brafman endpoint should be treated explicitly as improper integrals. Positivity/Tonelli supplies a clean justification.

### Theorem 7 — Double integral corollary (lines 251–264)

**Verdict: essentially correct, with omitted improper-integral justification.** The Euler integral and constants lead to the displayed formula. The corner `(z,u) = (rho,1)` is singular, so the proof should say that the integral is an improper nonnegative integral and invoke Tonelli/monotone convergence.

### Theorem 8 — Clausen single sum (lines 312–315)

**Verdict: plausible exact identity, but no proof or citation is supplied.** This is not a numerical claim; it can be repaired by citing/proving the relevant Clausen identity or by an elementary hypergeometric verification.

### Theorem 9 — Creative-telescoping certificate (lines 317–330)

**Verdict: an exact certificate with an incomplete summation argument.** In the interior range the rational identity can be checked after clearing denominators. However, the allowed range of `n,k` is not stated, and the certificate has apparent singularities at summation boundaries (`n-k+1`, `n-k+2`, and `n+k`). The proof must cancel removable factors first and list the endpoint values before telescoping. “Summing proves” is not sufficient when the displayed certificate is undefined at those endpoints.

### Theorem 10 — Beukers-type error integral (lines 332–342)

**Verdict: incomplete.** Neither the exact error-integral identity nor the bound `O(n sigma^n)` is proved. The big-O has no constant or threshold, and the treatment of `P_n^Leg(sqrt(1-8x^2))` beyond the real square-root range should be explained as a polynomial in `1-8x^2`. This theorem is not used to close the challenge CMF, but it is not established as written.

### Theorem 11 — Scalar recurrence (lines 398–421)

**Verdict: not rigorous.** A recurrence guessed by modular Berlekamp–Massey and checked on 200 terms is still a conjectured recurrence. No coefficients are printed and no symbolic Casorati/elimination identity proves it for all `n`. A modular nullspace computation also requires rational reconstruction and exact verification of the resulting polynomial identity.

There is an internal degree error: `deg c_k = 7(3-k)` gives `(21,14,7,0)` for `k = 0,1,2,3`, not the stated `(28,21,14,7)`. The intended formula appears to be `deg c_k = 7(4-k)`. It is also not established that one common scalar recurrence applies to every terminal column `j`; for a nonautonomous matrix recurrence this does not follow merely from the matrix dimension.

### Theorem 12 — Exact Pochhammer twist (lines 434–448)

**Verdict: exact product formula is credible; “minimal” is unproved.** Factoring the determinant can verify the displayed determinant compatibility and multiplying the factors gives the stated `H_n`. But determinant compatibility alone does not prove uniqueness or minimality of the normalization. Define the relevant normalized transfer and either prove a minimization statement or delete “minimal.”

### Theorem 13 — Formal index obstruction (lines 458–481)

**Verdict: the stated obstruction does not follow.** Formal exponents for rational difference modules are generally invariant modulo integer shifts under rational gauges. Here the target indices `(0,-3,0)` and source indices `(-1,-1,-1)` differ, mode by mode, by integers `(1,-2,1)`. Thus these values do not obstruct a rational matrix/Ore gauge. The inference to an entire `Hom` space being zero is much stronger still and is unsupported.

The entries of `C_1` and the calculation of the indices are also omitted. To prove `Hom = 0`, one needs a genuine rational-intertwiner computation with a complete denominator/degree bound, or a local invariant mismatch modulo integers; the displayed indices provide neither.

### Theorem 14 — Birkhoff convergence (lines 485–519)

**Verdict: incomplete.** The normalized transfer `A_n` is not defined (the manuscript previously used `A` for the initial matrix and `B(n)` for a normalized transfer). A rigorous application of Birkhoff/Perron theory must verify regularity, nonsingularity on the relevant tail, the three asymptotic solutions, and the absence of resonant/logarithmic complications.

Most importantly, the proof assumes without proof that the denominator has a nonzero dominant coefficient `q_0 . w_+(0)` and that all three terminal coordinates see the dominant right vector. Those nonvanishing facts are required for the ratios and formula for `L`. The asymptotic error formula has no explicit constants or onset, so it is non-effective. It could still prove qualitative convergence after all hypotheses and nonvanishing statements are supplied.

The 500-digit computation in lines 521–523 does not establish the Catalan adjoint identity.

### Theorem 15 — Delannoy decomposition (lines 533–545)

**Verdict: algebraically rigorous, but notation is incomplete.** A lower-triangular matrix with nonzero diagonal gives a unique coefficient sequence by induction. However, `hat P_N` and `hat Q_N` are never defined in the manuscript. They appear to mean `P_{N,1}/H_N` and `Q_{N,1}/H_N`; this must be stated, including the chosen column.

### Theorem 16 — `k`-recurrence (lines 549–583)

**Verdict: not proved.** Guessing from 401 exact rational terms and verifying on the same 401 terms is finite empirical verification. A priori holonomicity proves only that *some* P-recursive operator exists; it does not prove that the guessed order-8/order-9 operators annihilate the sequences for all `k`, nor that they are minimal.

Likewise, verifying the inverse Delannoy formula through `k = 20` is not a proof of the formula for all indices. That inverse identity is plausibly provable by binomial inversion and should be proved symbolically.

There is also an ambiguity/inconsistency in the Poincare polynomial statement. The order-8 operators can have `(xi-1)^2(xi+1/8)^6`, while the order-9 LCLM used in Theorem 18 must have `(xi-1)^2(xi+1/8)^7`. The text should distinguish these explicitly.

Publication-grade repair requires the full operators plus exact certificates: derive `L_f f = 0` and `L_g g = 0` from the exact inverse sum and an exact scalar recurrence by creative telescoping/holonomic closure; then exhibit Ore cofactors proving `L = U L_f = V L_g`. Polynomial identities should be checked after denominator clearing, not on a finite sequence prefix. Minimality may be dropped because the limit argument does not need it.

### Theorem 17 — Positivity (lines 587–621)

**Verdict: not proved; the asymptotic argument is neither valid nor effective.** Specific defects are:

- Poincare–Perron applied only to the characteristic roots does not give `f(k) = Ak+B+O((-1/8)^k k^s)` in the repeated-root case. One needs indicial exponents and a Birkhoff–Trjitzinsky/Perron theorem whose hypotheses cover multiplicities and possible logarithmic terms.
- The exponent `rho = 1`, the integer bound `s <= 10`, and the absence of other power/log terms are only numerically inferred.
- Positivity and growth on finitely many computed terms do not imply `A > 0`.
- Positivity does not imply `f(k) >= f(30)`; monotonicity is not proved.
- A big-O term has an unknown multiplicative constant and an unknown onset. It cannot be replaced by the bare expression `8^(-k) k^s`.
- The numerical inequality itself is false: `8^(-30) 30^10` is approximately `4.77 * 10^(-13)`, not less than `10^(-18)`.

An effective tail proof must give explicit rational bounds `A >= a_0 > 0`, `|R_k| <= C 8^(-k) k^s`, and a certified threshold `K_0`, followed by an exact finite check below `K_0`. Preferably, find a direct positive sum/integral representation or an invariant positive cone for `f(k)`; that avoids delicate connection constants.

### Theorem 18 — Convergence of `epsilon_k` to `G` (lines 623–695)

**Verdict: fatal gap.** This is the central missing theorem.

1. It relies on the unproved all-`k` LCLM from Theorem 16.
2. The repeated root `xi = 1` does not by itself imply that the two modes are exactly `k` and `1`. Their formal powers and possible logarithmic/resonant terms must be derived.
3. The rational initial-value basis is confused with the asymptotic mode basis. The projection coefficients are global connection constants and are not shown to be rational.
4. No `alpha_{j,i}`, `A_j`, or `B_j` is printed, and no exact construction of the projection is given. Invertibility of the Casorati matrix for every `k` also requires proving that the recurrence has no relevant singular index.
5. Thirty-digit agreement is not equality; the stated alternating bounds do not provide 30 digits in any case.
6. Even after proving the `xi=1` part vanishes, a big-O bound only proves `epsilon_k -> 0`. The ratio limit `epsilon_{k+1}/epsilon_k -> -1/8` additionally requires identifying a nonzero leading recessive mode (and ruling out exact zero or cancellation among equal-root formal modes). The manuscript explicitly supports this part only by a 12-digit numerical check.

There is no valid conclusion `c_1=c_2=0`, so neither convergence nor the stated ratio is proved.

### Theorem 19 — Limit identification (lines 697–734)

**Verdict: conditionally valid, but not presently applicable.** Under the explicit hypotheses

```text
f(k) > 0,
epsilon_k -> 0,
Qhat_N = sum f(k)B(N,k),
Qhat_N ~ c lambda^N with c > 0 and lambda > 1,
```

the head/tail argument works. A fully formal version should say: given `delta > 0`, choose `K` so that `|epsilon_k| < delta` for every `k > K`; for this fixed `K`, choose `N` so the finite polynomial head divided by `Qhat_N` is below `delta`. Then the normalized error is below `2 delta`. This avoids the nonstandard infinite-tail `max`; `sup` would also work.

The proof must define the hatted sequences, establish the denominator asymptotic with nonzero `c`, and state how the first-column normalized ratio identifies the common limit of all three original columns. Its dependencies on Theorems 17 and 18 are currently fatal, but the averaging lemma itself is not the source of the main gap.

## Other unsupported or misleading assertions

- Lines 268–298 state the desired connection coefficient and convergence mechanism before proving them. Matching Poincare roots, singularities, and an integrated elliptic module does not determine a global connection coefficient. Used as a proof, this would be circular; read only as motivation, it should be labeled conjectural/heuristic.
- The matrix-valued Almkvist–Zeilberger section gives an ansatz but no certificate. It proves nothing about the printed CMF.
- The modular statement in lines 302–308 is uncited and needs its normalization checked. In particular, `eta^2(2 tau) eta^2(4 tau)` has formal modular weight 2, not 3, so the surrounding weight claim is internally suspect. It is not used in the convergence proof.
- The enhanced numerical verification section is valuable evidence but cannot be cited as an exact theorem.

## Effective versus non-effective asymptotics

The exact moment, Brafman, Catalan-integral, and triangular-decomposition steps can be made fully effective. The asymptotic core is not currently effective:

- Theorems 11 and 16 do not first establish exact recurrences.
- Theorem 14 gives qualitative Birkhoff expansions without constants, thresholds, or proved nonzero connection coefficients.
- Theorem 17 needs explicit constants and a threshold because it is used to prove a sign for every index; qualitative asymptotics are insufficient for a finite-check-plus-tail proof.
- Theorem 18 needs an exact zero connection coefficient, not merely an effective small bound at one index. More digits do not solve that problem.
- Theorem 19 itself only needs qualitative convergence and exponential denominator growth; once those hypotheses are rigorously proved, its epsilon–delta proof need not provide optimized constants.

## Specific repair routes for the crux

### Preferred route: an exact integral for the Delannoy coefficient error

First define

```text
Qhat_N = Q_{N,1}/H_N,
Phat_N = P_{N,1}/H_N,
h(k) = g(k)-G f(k).
```

Using the exact inverse Delannoy transform and Theorem 2 gives the finite identity

```text
h(k)
 = sum_{N=0}^k B^{-1}(k,N) (Phat_N-G Qhat_N)
 = - integral_0^1 [(-log t)/(1+t^2)] Psi_k(t) dt,

Psi_k(t) = sum_{N=0}^k B^{-1}(k,N) R_{N,1}(t^2)/H_N.
```

This formula is exact after the inverse identity is proved, because the sum is finite. The missing analytic task is then concrete: derive a closed form or creative-telescoping certificate for `Psi_k`, and prove an integrable bound

```text
integral_0^1 [(-log t)/(1+t^2)] |Psi_k(t)| dt
    <= C 8^(-k) k^s
```

with explicit `C,s,K_0`. That proves `h(k)` is recessive without asserting that `G` is rational. A nonzero leading-term calculation can be added only if the ratio limit `-1/8` is desired; it is not needed for `epsilon_k -> 0`.

### Equivalent generating-function route

Derive exact D-finite equations for `F(z)=sum f(k)z^k` and `H(z)=sum h(k)z^k`. Use an exact integral/period representation to show that the two local `z=1` singular coefficients of `H` vanish. Then apply a proved transfer theorem at the next singularity (`z=-8`) with explicit local expansion. Spectral data alone cannot show this vanishing; the integral continuation is the essential new input.

### If retaining the Casorati language

Construct a rigorously normalized asymptotic basis and its dual concomitant. Treat the projection coefficients as real/complex connection constants, not rational numbers. Prove their pairing with `h` is exactly zero using an integral identity, a conserved bilinear concomitant, or an exact right-factor membership certificate. A finite decimal evaluation may be retained as a check but not as the proof.

One could also use certified approximations only if accompanied by a theorem producing bounds tending to zero for **every** precision—for example, `|pi_j(h)| <= C q^N` for all `N` with `0<q<1`. Since `pi_j(h)` is a fixed real number, arbitrarily shrinking proved bounds imply it is zero. A single 30-digit bound does not.

## Final assessment

The current manuscript is a strong numerical and structural investigation, not an unconditional proof. The answer to the audit question is unambiguous: the 30-digit rational agreement is not rigorous, and Theorem 18 has a genuine global connection-coefficient gap. Theorem 19 is salvageable once that gap, exact recurrence certification, positivity, normalization, and the all-columns bridge are supplied.
