\subsection{Jacobi--Stickelberger analysis of the marked coordinate}
\label{ssec:lemmaA-result}

We use the fixed Laurent polynomial~\eqref{eq:oracleC-Lambda} and the
marked-coordinate identity of
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracleC-marked-coordinate}.  Put
\[
 h=p-1,
 \qquad E_p=\mathbb Q(\mu_h),
 \qquad \chi=\omega^j,
\]
where $\omega\colon\mathbb F_p^\times\to\mu_h$ is the Teichm\"uller
character associated with the chosen prime $\mathfrak p\mid p$ of~$E_p$.
Every multiplicative character, including the trivial character
$\varepsilon$, is extended by zero at~$0$.  We write
\[
 J(A,B)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}A(t)B(1-t).
\]
All character exponents below are read modulo~$h$.

\subsubsection{A1: an exact four-Jacobi skeleton}

\begin{theorem}[Jacobi skeleton]
\label{thm:lemmaA-skeleton}
For $1\leq j\leq p-2$, the cyclotomic lift in
\eqref{eq:oracleC-Frobenius-coordinate} has the exact expression
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde c_{p,j}^{\rm tor}
 =-\frac1h\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}
 J(\omega^{-k},\omega^j)^2
 J(\omega^{-k},\omega^{j+k})^2.
 \label{eq:lemmaA-skeleton}
\end{equation}
Thus the skeleton has one character variable and four Jacobi factors,
all with arguments affine in $(j,k)$.

At $j=0$, let the right side of~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-skeleton}, with
$\chi=\varepsilon$, be denoted by $\mathscr S_{p,0}$.  The exact endpoint
formula is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde c_{p,0}^{\rm tor}
 =\mathscr S_{p,0}-4p^2+14p-13
 =-(p-1)^3.
 \label{eq:lemmaA-endpoint}
\end{equation}
In particular, reduction modulo $\mathfrak p$ gives $b_j$ for every
$0\leq j\leq p-2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a character $A$ and $t\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, finite Mellin
inversion gives
\begin{equation}
 A(1+t)=\frac1h\sum_{\rho}
 \rho(-1)J(\overline\rho,A)\rho(t),
 \label{eq:lemmaA-mellin}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ runs over all multiplicative characters of
$\mathbb F_p^\times$.  Indeed, the Fourier coefficient at~$\rho$ is
\[
 \sum_{t\ne0}A(1+t)\overline\rho(t)
 =\rho(-1)J(\overline\rho,A).
\]

For $x,y,z\ne0$, put
\[
 s=\frac{(1+y)(1+z)}{xyz}.
\]
The marked coordinate factors as
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda(x,y,z)=(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)(1+s).
 \label{eq:lemmaA-factor-Lambda}
\end{equation}
If $j>0$, the summand is already zero when $y=-1$ or $z=-1$.
We may therefore apply~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-mellin} to $s$ and then restore
those zero-contribution values.  With $\rho=\omega^k$, the three
variable sums separate:
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{x\ne0}\chi(1+x)\overline\rho(x)
   &=\rho(-1)J(\overline\rho,\chi),\\
 \sum_{y\ne0}(\chi\rho)(1+y)\overline\rho(y)
   &=\rho(-1)J(\overline\rho,\chi\rho),\\
 \sum_{z\ne0}(\chi\rho)(1+z)\overline\rho(z)
   &=\rho(-1)J(\overline\rho,\chi\rho).
\end{align*}
Together with the factor $\rho(-1)$ in
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-mellin}, the four signs multiply to~$1$.
The outer minus sign in the definition of
$\widetilde c_{p,j}^{\rm tor}$ now gives
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-skeleton}.

This argument includes all degenerate values of~$k$: the identities
\[
 J(\varepsilon,A)=J(A,\varepsilon)=-1,
 \quad J(A,A^{-1})=-A(-1),
 \quad J(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)=p-2
\]
are built into the unnormalized Jacobi sum.  In particular, no
Gauss-sum quotient is being used illegally when a product character is
trivial.

At $j=0$, zero-extension of $\varepsilon$ computes the open-set sum
$\mathscr S_{p,0}=-\#U(\mathbb F_p)$ rather than the prescribed raw
zeroth power.  Equation~\eqref{eq:oracleC-U-count} gives
\[
 \#U(\mathbb F_p)-(p-1)^3=-4p^2+14p-13,
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-endpoint}.  This is the necessary
$0^0=1$ correction; it is not a choice of character convention.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the reduction of every Jacobi factor has an
$O(1)$ formula.  If $a,b\in\{0,\ldots,h-1\}$ and
$\overline J_p(a,b)$ denotes the reduction of
$J(\omega^a,\omega^b)$ modulo~$\mathfrak p$, then
\begin{equation}
 \overline J_p(a,b)=
 \begin{cases}
 -2,&a=b=0,\\
 -1,&\text{exactly one of $a,b$ is zero},\\
 0,&a,b>0\text{ and }h-a>b,\\
 -(-1)^{h-a}\binom b{h-a},
      &a,b>0\text{ and }h-a\leq b.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:lemmaA-Jmodp}
\end{equation}
This follows by expanding $(1-t)^b$: among the powers $t^{a+r}$,
only $a+r=h$ can survive torus orthogonality.  Since
$-h^{-1}\equiv1\pmod p$, equations
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-skeleton} and~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-Jmodp} are an entirely
explicit finite-field formula for~$b_j$.

For $1\leq j\leq p-2$, if
\[
 m=\min(j,h-j),
\]
then only $0\leq k\leq m$ survive modulo~$\mathfrak p$, and on that
range
\begin{align}
 \overline J_p(-k,j)&=(-1)^{k+1}\binom jk,\notag\\
 \overline J_p(-k,j+k)&=(-1)^{k+1}\binom{j+k}k.
 \label{eq:lemmaA-unit-reduction}
\end{align}
Consequently the surviving $k$-th skeleton term is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \binom jk^2\binom{j+k}k^2.
 \label{eq:lemmaA-Apery-summand}
\end{equation}
When $j>h/2$, the omitted terms of the usual Ap\'ery sum have
$j+k\geq p$ and hence $p\mid\binom{j+k}k$.  This also proves directly,
without numerical input, that the reduction of the skeleton is~$b_j$.

The script \texttt{lemmaA\_verify.py} independently compares the
skeleton with the Ap\'ery recurrence for every prime $5\leq p\leq200$
and every $0\leq j\leq p-2$ ($4178$ pairs).  It also compares both with
the direct three-dimensional torus sum through $p=31$.  At
$j=h/2$, it expands
\[
 \eta(2z)^4\eta(4z)^4
 =q\prod_{n\geq1}(1-q^{2n})^4(1-q^{4n})^4
 =\sum_{r\geq1}a_rq^r
\]
and verifies
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde c_{p,h/2}^{\rm tor}\equiv b_{h/2}\equiv a_p\pmod p
 \label{eq:lemmaA-midpoint-anchor}
\end{equation}
for every prime $5\leq p\leq200$, reproducing the modular-form anchor
in Remark~\ref{rem:open}.

\subsubsection{A2: the explicit Stickelberger container}

Choose a prime $\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p$ of $E_p(\mu_p)$ and
normalize $v_{\mathfrak P}(p)=1$.  With
\[
 g(A)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}A(t)\zeta_p^t,
\]
Gross--Koblitz computes this valuation; its restriction to $E_p$ is
the $\mathfrak p$-adic valuation used below.  For an exponent~$a$,
write
\[
 d_h(a)=\frac{[-a]_h}{h}
       =\left\langle-\frac ah\right\rangle,
\]
where $[-a]_h\in\{0,\ldots,h-1\}$ and the fractional part of an
integer is~$0$.  Gross--Koblitz gives
$v_{\mathfrak P}(g(\omega^a))=d_h(a)$.  Including the inverse-character
degeneracy, the resulting formula valid for every $a,b$ is
\begin{equation}
 \nu_h(a,b):=v_{\mathfrak p}\!\left(J(\omega^a,\omega^b)\right)
 =d_h(a)+d_h(b)-d_h(a+b)
 -\mathbf1_{\substack{a\not\equiv0,\ b\not\equiv0\ (h)\\
                       a+b\equiv0\ (h)}}.
 \label{eq:lemmaA-Jvaluation}
\end{equation}
Indeed, away from a trivial product character this is the valuation of
$g(\omega^a)g(\omega^b)/g(\omega^{a+b})$; when the two nontrivial
characters are inverse, $J(A,A^{-1})=-A(-1)$ is a unit and supplies
the last correction.  Formula~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-Jvaluation} also gives
zero in the cases involving a trivial factor.  Its values are always
$0$ or~$1$.

For the remainder of A2 assume $1\leq j\leq p-2$, and let
\[
 T_{j,k}=J(\omega^{-k},\omega^j)^2
          J(\omega^{-k},\omega^{j+k})^2.
\]
Equations~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-skeleton} and
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-Jvaluation} give the requested digit/fractional-part
valuation
\begin{align}
 v(k;j)
 &=2\nu_h(-k,j)+2\nu_h(-k,j+k)\notag\\
 &=2\mathbf1_{\{k>j\}}+2\mathbf1_{\{k>h-j\}},
 \qquad 0\leq k\leq h-1.
 \label{eq:lemmaA-term-valuation}
\end{align}
Thus its minimum is zero and
\begin{equation}
 \{k:v(k;j)=0\}=\{0,1,\ldots,\min(j,h-j)\},
 \qquad
 U_p(j)=1+\min(j,h-j).
 \label{eq:lemmaA-U-explicit}
\end{equation}
This is computable in $O(1)$ operations per~$(p,j)$.

\begin{theorem}[Unique-unit container]
\label{thm:lemmaA-container}
For $1\leq j\leq p-2$, if $U_p(j)=1$, then
$b_j\not\equiv0\pmod p$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Z_p\subseteq
 \{1\leq j\leq p-2:U_p(j)\ne1\}.
 \label{eq:lemmaA-container}
\end{equation}
The endpoints $j=0,p-1$ are absent from $\mathcal Z_p$ because their
marked values are~$1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Jacobi sums are algebraic integers, $-1/h$ is a unit at
$\mathfrak p$, and the minimum in
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-term-valuation} is~$0$.  If exactly one $T_{j,k}$ has
valuation zero, reduction of~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-skeleton} kills every
other term and leaves one nonzero residue.  Therefore the reduction of
$\widetilde c_{p,j}^{\rm tor}$, which is $b_j$ by
\eqref{eq:oracleC-coordinate-equality}, cannot vanish.
\end{proof}

There are two normalization points in this argument.  First, the Gauss
sums live in $E_p(\mu_p)$, but the Jacobi sums lie in~$E_p$; because
$p\equiv1\pmod h$, the residue field at $\mathfrak p$ is
$\mathbb F_p$.  Second, if the valuation is instead normalized by
$v(1-\zeta_p)=1$, every value in
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-Jvaluation}--\eqref{eq:lemmaA-term-valuation} must be
multiplied by~$h$.  No Greene $p^{-1}J$ normalization is used here.

The explicit answer~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-U-explicit} now exposes the
decision gate:
\begin{equation}
 U_p(j)=1+\min(j,p-1-j)\geq2
 \qquad(1\leq j\leq p-2).
 \label{eq:lemmaA-U-never-one}
\end{equation}
Thus the implication in Theorem~\ref{thm:lemmaA-container} is correct
but never triggers on a nontrivial character.  Moreover,
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-Apery-summand} shows that the simultaneous unit terms
reconstruct the original Ap\'ery sum modulo~$p$: cancellation among
unit terms is the original vanishing problem, not a lower-complexity
replacement for it.

It is important here that the $h$ Jacobi summands are a Mellin expansion
of the trace, not the at most $B_\Lambda$ Frobenius eigenvalues in
Proposition~\ref{prop:oracleC-uniform-complexity}.  Their unit count
depends on the chosen integral character expansion and is not a
crystalline slope multiplicity.  The fixed-dimensional cohomological
trace occurs only after the length-$h$ Mellin sum has cancelled.  Thus
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-U-never-one} does not contradict the uniform Betti
bound; it identifies the precise point at which this Jacobi skeleton
fails to produce a unique unit summand and hence gives no
unique-unit-root certificate.

\subsubsection{A3: empirical computation and verdict}

The zero sets were computed from the Ap\'ery recurrence, independently
of the skeleton.  A zero at $j=h/2$ was classified first as
\emph{non-ordinary}; every other zero was classified as a collision of
at least two unit skeleton terms.  The results are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{r|r|r|r|r|r|r}
 bound & primes & $\sum_p Z(p)$ & zero-free & $U=0$ & $U\geq2$ collision
       & non-ordinary \\ \hline
 $200$  & $44$  & $41$  & $25$  & $0$ & $40$  & $1$ \\
 $500$  & $93$  & $95$  & $53$  & $0$ & $94$  & $1$ \\
 $1000$ & $166$ & $165$ & $96$  & $0$ & $164$ & $1$ \\
 $2000$ & $301$ & $283$ & $184$ & $0$ & $282$ & $1$ \\
 $5000$ & $667$ & $608$ & $418$ & $0$ & $606$ & $2$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For $p\leq2000$, the distribution of $Z(p)$ is
\[
 \#\{Z=0,1,2,4,6\}=\{184,1,95,17,4\}.
\]
For the stretch range $p\leq5000$, it is
\[
 \#\{Z=0,1,2,4,6,8\}=\{418,2,199,41,6,1\}.
\]
The non-ordinary points in the stretch range are
\[
 (p,j)=(11,5),\ (3137,1568);
 \qquad a_{11}=-44,
 \quad a_{3137}=207042=66\cdot3137.
\]

The container size is not merely empirical: by
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-U-never-one}, for every $p\geq5$,
\begin{equation}
 \#\{1\leq j\leq p-2:U_p(j)\ne1\}=p-2.
 \label{eq:lemmaA-container-size}
\end{equation}
Therefore the decision-gate verdict is \textbf{S2}.  Generic~$j$ has
multiple unit Jacobi terms (in fact every nontrivial~$j$ does), and all
observed zeros are either non-ordinary midpoint zeros or cancellations
among these unit terms.  This presentation of the crystalline/Jacobi
route alone gives no estimate $|\mathcal Z_p|\ll p^\theta$ with
$\theta<1$.  The unconditional
$Z(p)\ll p^{2/3}$ bound in Proposition~\ref{prop:zp-bound} remains
available, but it comes from the gap-polynomial argument, not from
\eqref{eq:lemmaA-container}.

Finally, the Lemma--0 collapse is fully compatible with this negative
gate.  Corollary~\ref{cor:lucas} gives, for $m\in(N,2N]$ and
$N<p\leq m$,
\[
 b_m\equiv5b_{m-p}\pmod p,
 \qquad
 L_N(m)=\#\{p\in(N,m]:p\mid b_m\}.
\]
Since~\eqref{eq:lemmaA-container-size} excludes no nontrivial affine
target $j=m-p$, it supplies neither the pointwise anti-concentration
required after the collapse nor the centered estimate
\eqref{eq:oracleB-mh2}.  The exact skeleton and its Stickelberger
valuation are therefore complete, but their decision-gate output is
the valid S2 obstruction specified in the task.
