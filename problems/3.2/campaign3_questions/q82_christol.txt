THE ANGLE I WANT YOU TO PUSH (new; the campaign has never used it for this quantity):
The Apery numbers are the diagonal of an explicit multivariate rational function (Straub), so by Furstenberg the generating series B(t) = sum_n b_n t^n is ALGEBRAIC over F_p(t) for every p. By Christol's theorem, the sequence (b_n mod p)_n is therefore p-AUTOMATIC: there is a finite automaton reading the base-p digits of n that outputs b_n mod p. Note this is consistent with — and refines — the Gessel/Lucas congruence b_{mp+r} = b_m b_r (mod p), which says the automaton is essentially multiplicative across digits: b_n = prod_i b_{d_i} (mod p) over the base-p digits d_i of n. Consequently:
   Z := {n : b_n = 0 mod p} is the p-automatic set of integers having at least one base-p digit in Z_p,
and |Z_p| = #(Z ∩ [0,p)) is exactly the number of ZERO COEFFICIENTS, among the first p, of the algebraic power series B(t) mod p. Equivalently, writing the p-Mahler factorization B(t) = B_0(t) B(t^p) with B_0(t) = sum_{r<p} b_r t^r in F_p[t], we have |Z_p| = #{zero coefficients of B_0} and B_0 = B(t)/B(t^p) is a ratio of algebraic functions.

In particular pin down what is actually known about the algebraic equation satisfied by B(t) over F_p(t) for the Apery diagonal: its degree and height as functions of p (Furstenberg's construction, Cartier operator / kernel size, Adamczewski-Bell effective bounds for diagonals — there ARE effective results bounding the algebraicity degree of a diagonal in terms of the ambient rational function and p; state them exactly). This matters because everything below scales with that degree.
(ii) ZERO COEFFICIENTS OF ALGEBRAIC SERIES: what is known about the number of vanishing coefficients of an algebraic power series over F_p? Relevant corpus to check precisely: the Christol-Kamae-Mendes France-Rauzy framework; the theory of the "zero set is p-automatic" (Derksen's structure theorem for zero sets of linear recurrences in char p is the analogous statement for recurrences — is there a diagonal/algebraic analogue?); results bounding the density of zeros of an automatic sequence in terms of the automaton's size (the density of a k-automatic set is computable from the transition matrices; a set with a small automaton has either density 0 with a computable rate, or positive density). The KEY quantitative question: for a series satisfying an algebraic equation of degree D and height H over F_p(t), can one bound #{n < p : coefficient n vanishes} by something like O(D H) or O(poly(D,H)) INDEPENDENT of p? If yes, and if D,H are bounded independently of p for our diagonal, then |Z_p| = O(1) uniformly and [AVG-ZERO] follows immediately with room to spare — and that would be a complete proof of the average version of the conjecture's top window. This is the single most important sub-question; answer it decisively.
(iii) If D or H grows with p, quantify the growth and see what it gives: |Z_p| = O(f(p)) with sum_{p<=X} f(p) = o(X) requires f(p) = o(log p) on average — even a very weak bound suffices.
