[qFAM — family-averaged Weil errors for a discrete growing-genus family at fixed p. life3]
The crux (D6): we need sum_{h,h' <= H} E_{h,h'} = o(pH^2) or better O(pH), where E_{h,h'} = #X_{h,h'}(F_p) - (main term from rational components), and individually |E_{h,h'}| ~ (h+h') sqrt(p). Naive triangle inequality gives H^3 sqrt(p) — useless (worse than trivial for H < p^{1/3}). We need square-root cancellation in the FAMILY aspect: heuristically the Frobenius error angles theta_{h,h'} should behave randomly, giving sum ~ H * sqrt(p) * sqrt(H^2) = H^2 sqrt(p)... even that suffices for H <= p^{1/2}. Assess concretely, with the exact statement you would use: (a) Fouvry-Kowalski-Michel trace-function methods and their conductor limits (our conductor grows like h; sums over r of length p; which FKM ranges apply?); (b) Kowalski's large sieve for Frobenius / Chavdarov families; (c) Hooley-type / Birch swap: exchange the (h,h') sum and the point count to convert the family average into a POINT COUNT ON A HIGHER-DIM VARIETY — but h is a discrete shift length, not an algebraic coordinate. Can the update/transfer structure (G_{m+g} = shifted G_g * G_m, i.e. U_{m+g} composition identities) convert the h-average into an algebraic average? Write the exact identity: for fixed gap difference d = h'-h, X_{h,h+d} varies with h through a SHIFT of the base point s = r+1 -> r+1: the polynomials U_{h-1}(r+1) form a single 2-variable family U_{m}(s) with m discrete. Does the sum over m of Weil errors of the curves U_m(s) = U_{m'}(s') admit a mean-value theorem (Bombieri-Davenport style dispersion in the m-aspect)?
