## 1.5 What replaces Bogolyubov

There is no additive-combinatorial Bogolyubov mechanism here.

- Z(a) is stable under the Galois action χ↦χ^u, not under multiplication or addition in the character group.

- A product of two primitive order-d characters may have much smaller order, so a large order stratum has no useful small-doubling property.

- Fourier inversion identifies the missing rational isotypic components; it does not turn a large zero set into an annihilator subgroup.

The correct analogues are:

1. primitive Fourier inversion — the projectors P_d above;

1. Laurent/Mann — when the vector comes from a fixed bounded-complexity toric or sparse equation;

1. Goursat–Kolchin–Ribet — when it comes from a fixed pair of irreducible Mellin sheaves.

A genuine coset conclusion does follow if the nonzero Fourier support is contained in characters of orders dividing a fixed integer L: then the physical vector is L-periodic in exponent coordinates and is constant on cosets of the annihilator. Positive-density zeros alone do not imply such an order bound. The all-but-K theorem below does.

## 2.4 Correct notion of bounded complexity

“Conductor” alone is not enough.

- All nontrivial Dirichlet characters modulo the prime p have conductor p.

- A rank-one tame Kummer sheaf on \mathbf G_m has two tame singularities independent of the order of the character; its sheaf conductor can therefore stay bounded while its monodromy order tends to infinity.

For this project, bounded twist complexity must include:

1. bounded order of the rank-one character/local system;

1. a fixed compatible model independent of p;

1. a bounded ramification divisor and bounded Swan conductors;

1. for a pullback \mathcal L_\rho(f(t)), bounded degree/divisor complexity of f;

1. for power maps or torus isogenies, bounded matrix entries/degree.

For a pure toric Kummer twist, order plus compatible origin is the decisive datum. For graph twists, order and map complexity are both needed.

## 3.2 Mann’s sparse theorem — CITED

Mann proved that in a nondegenerate rational relation involving s roots of unity, every quotient of two participating roots has order dividing the product of the primes at most s.

Reference: H. B. Mann, “On linear relations between roots of unity,” Mathematika 12 (1965), 107–117.

Consequently, if a difference polynomial has uniformly bounded sparsity, a high-order exact zero forces a partition into degenerate subsums and bounded-order monomial relations. This is another genuine route to bounded Kummer structure.

The Apéry layer difference is dense, with s≈N, so Mann’s bound grows with p and gives no present result. This pinpoints the missing hypothesis rather than merely saying “Laurent should apply.”
