# 4. Approach (b): Adolphson–Sperber and Wan generic ordinarity

Adolphson–Sperber and Wan study fixed algebraic families—typically hypersurfaces or exponential sums with a fixed Newton polytope—and variation in their coefficients. They prove statements of the form:

- the Newton polygon lies above a Hodge polygon;

- a generic Newton polygon exists;

- the ordinary or generic stratum is Zariski open;

- a Hasse polynomial cuts out the complement;

- in many cases the Hasse polynomial is nonzero, so generic ordinarity holds.

Relevant references include:

- A. Adolphson and S. Sperber, Hasse invariants and mod p solutions of A-hypergeometric systems, J. Number Theory 142 (2014), 183–210.

- D. Wan, Newton polygons of zeta functions and L functions, Ann. of Math. 137 (1993), 249–293.

- D. Wan, Variation of p-adic Newton polygons for L-functions of exponential sums, Asian J. Math. 8 (2004), 427–472.

These theorems prove that the nonordinary locus is proper in parameter space. They do not prove that it has few rational points.

On a one-dimensional base, a Hasse invariant is usually a section whose degree grows proportionally to p-1. Therefore its geometric zero divisor can have degree \asymp p. “Zariski thin” here means codimension one, which on a curve means finitely many geometric points, but the number of those points is allowed to grow linearly with p.

## 6.3 Boundedness versus unboundedness

At present:

- no uniform constant C with |Z_p|\le C is known;

- no proof that \sup_p|Z_p|=\infty is known;

- no proof that |Z_p|\gg\log p infinitely often is known;

- no O(1) prime-average bound is known.

The random palindromic model predicts a doubled Poisson law of mean about 1. Under that model, |Z_p| is unbounded over the primes, with the largest value up to P growing on a logarithmic-over-logarithmic scale. But this is probabilistic evidence, not a theorem.

The crucial difficulty is that the sheaf/motive varies with the character \omega^z. Generic z has character order comparable to p. Thus the conductor and field of definition are not uniformly bounded in the naive parameterization.

Deligne equidistribution controls complex or \ell-adic trace values for a fixed bounded-conductor sheaf. Here one asks for divisibility by the residue characteristic p in a family whose character order also grows with p. That is a much more arithmetic condition.

A successful proof would likely need one of the following genuinely new inputs:

1. A bounded-complexity Mellin-family realization. Construct a single geometric object over the character torus whose Frobenius traces specialize to the general-z values with conductor uniform in p.

1. Large monodromy plus a mod-p sieve. Prove that the reductions of the trace values are equidistributed strongly enough to count exact zeros, not merely complex trace intervals.

1. A bispectral transformation. Relate the index variable X=z(z+1) to the geometric \Gamma_0(6) parameter in a way that turns H_p(X) into a pullback of a fixed modular Hasse invariant.

1. A low-degree factorization or norm identity. Express H_p on S_p as a norm from a bounded-degree extension of a polynomial of degree o(p).

No such theorem is presently available.

The desired estimate

- The displayed polynomial is degree p-1 and is an index-interpolation polynomial.
