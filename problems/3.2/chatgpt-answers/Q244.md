# Executive verdict

Let

S(n;X)=\sum_{X<p\le 2X,\ p\mid b_n}v_p(b_n)\log p.

No existing theorem in rational or integral p-adic Hodge theory, prismatic cohomology, Beilinson–Bloch theory, or Perrin–Riou theory implies S(n;X)=o(n) for the Apéry numbers. The standard comparison isomorphisms are intrinsically attached to one residue characteristic p at a time. Integral comparison improves the lattice-level information at that same p, but it does not compare the order of vanishing of a chosen scalar at different primes.

There are two important qualifications.

1. There really are theorems called product formulas for periods, most notably Colmez’s formula/conjecture for CM abelian varieties and its function-field analogues. They mix archimedean and nonarchimedean period data, but only in highly structured CM settings and at the level of normalized period heights/Faltings heights. They do not bound the prime divisors of one Taylor coefficient of a non-CM G-function.

1. There are global categories packaging de Rham data together with Frobenius data at almost all finite places, such as filtered Ogus realizations. These are categorical compatibility objects, not inequalities among the valuations of a selected period coordinate.

Thus the desired estimate is genuinely new mathematics. It is not best viewed as a missing comparison theorem. It is a pointwise prime-divisor-distribution theorem for a geometric recurrence, closer in logical form to a Subspace-theorem/Vojta/gcd estimate or an arithmetic unlikely-intersection theorem. No known assembly of the existing machinery supplies it.

# 0. Two normalizations that matter

## 0.1 The only unconditional product formula for the scalar b_n

Because b_n is a nonzero integer,

\sum_p v_p(b_n)\log p=\log |b_n|.

This is already the global product formula. Consequently

0\le S(n;X)\le \log |b_n|=O(n).

Any improvement from O(n) to o(n) for a dyadic set of primes is not a consequence of the ordinary product formula alone. It asserts that the divisor of the integer b_n cannot concentrate too much in one moving band of finite places. That is a distribution statement.

In Arakelov language, the divisor of one scalar is codimension one. For a codimension-one divisor, global height naturally measures the total local intersection multiplicity, and there is no general reason for the contribution from a specified family of places to be sublinear. The known gcd theorems become sublinear because they impose two sufficiently independent vanishing conditions—geometrically, a codimension-at-least-two intersection or a blowup height. The condition p\mid b_n is only one condition unless one discovers a second independent motivic coordinate tied to it.

## 0.2 b_n is a coefficient, not literally a fixed-fiber period at t=n

For the Beukers–Peters family, the generating function

B(t)=\sum_{n\ge 0}b_nt^n

is a period/Picard–Fuchs solution, and b_n is also a constant term or a period of an n-dependent integrand such as \Phi^n. Standard p-adic Hodge comparison for a family at an algebraic parameter t_0 compares the realizations of the fiber at t_0. It does not directly compare the $n$th Taylor coefficient of a horizontal section across all primes.

One may package b_n geometrically by an n-dependent construction, but then the motive or cohomology class varies with n. This does not invalidate the question, but it removes the most literal interpretation of “one fixed motive evaluated at the fixed point n.” See Beukers–Peters, A family of K3 surfaces and \zeta(3), J. reine angew. Math. 351 (1984), 42–54.

# 1. Rational p-adic Hodge comparison

## 1.1 What the comparison theorem says — theorem

For a finite extension K/\mathbf Q_p and a proper smooth K-variety Y, the de Rham comparison theorem has the form

$$H^i_{mathrm{acute et}}(Y_{overline K},mathbf Q_p)otimes_{mathbf Q_p}B_{mathrm{dR},p}

cong

H^i_{mathrm{dR}}(Y/K)otimes_K B_{mathrm{dR},p},

compatible with Galois action and filtration. In good or semistable reduction there are crystalline or semistable comparisons, schematically

$$H^i_{mathrm{acute et}}(Y_{overline K},mathbf Q_p)otimes B_{mathrm{cris},p}

cong

H^i_{mathrm{cris}}(Y_k/W(k))[1/p]otimes B_{mathrm{cris},p},

with the appropriate Frobenius, monodromy, and filtration structures.

Foundational references include:

- G. Faltings, p-adic Hodge theory, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 1 (1988), 255–299.

- J.-M. Fontaine, Le corps des périodes p-adiques, Astérisque 223 (1994), 59–101.

- T. Tsuji, p-adic étale cohomology and crystalline cohomology in the semi-stable reduction case, Invent. Math. 137 (1999), 233–411.

These are comparison isomorphisms between realizations after extension to a period ring. They are not formulas for the valuation of an individual matrix coefficient or period coordinate.

## 1.2 Why it is one-prime-at-a-time — theorem plus immediate formal consequence

The rings B_{\mathrm{dR},p}, B_{\mathrm{cris},p}, and B_{\mathrm{st},p} are built from the completed algebraic closure of a p-adic local field. The Galois representation is a \mathbf Q_p-representation, crystalline Frobenius acts on a characteristic-p special fiber, and all lattices are \mathbf Z_p-lattices. Replacing p by q changes every one of these objects.

Therefore the comparison theorem provides no map

B_{\mathrm{cris},p}\longrightarrow B_{\mathrm{cris},q}

that could compare v_p(\alpha_p) with v_q(\alpha_q) for chosen period coordinates. Compatible systems of \ell-adic representations say that characteristic polynomials of Frobenius at auxiliary good places have coefficients independent of \ell; they do not identify the integral lattices or valuations of a period value at different residue characteristics.

For the Apéry reduction, the finite hypergeometric or Hasse–Witt description can identify the local condition b_n\equiv0\pmod p with the vanishing of a specific mod-p Frobenius/Hasse–Witt coordinate. A crystalline lift can, in principle, describe v_p(b_n) using higher congruence information at that same p. Nothing in the comparison theorem correlates those higher congruences as p varies.

## 1.3 Fargues–Fontaine does not make the theory adelic — theorem

The Fargues–Fontaine curve geometrizes p-adic Hodge theory for a fixed local field E, hence a fixed residue characteristic. It packages isocrystals, untilts, and period rings beautifully, but it does not put the curves for all rational primes into one number-field object carrying a product formula for scalar periods. See L. Fargues and J.-M. Fontaine, Courbes et fibrés vectoriels en théorie de Hodge p-adique, Astérisque 406 (2018).

A 2026 preprint of S. D. Li-Huerta, Courbes et fibrés vectoriels en théorie de Hodge z-adique globale, develops a global analogue over function fields and reformulates global Langlands phenomena using global G-bundles. This is a significant genuinely global construction, but it is not an all-rational-primes period ring for motives over \mathbf Q, and it proves no bound on prime divisors of a period value.

Likewise, “multivariable p-adic Hodge theory” concerns products or families of p-adic Galois groups at the same residue characteristic; it should not be confused with simultaneous variation over different primes.

## 1.4 The nearest all-place categorical object: filtered Ogus realization — theorem, but not quantitative

Chiarellotto–Lazda–Mazzari construct a filtered Ogus realization of Voevodsky motives over a number field. Roughly, it packages a filtered de Rham realization with Frobenius/rigid realizations at almost all finite places. See B. Chiarellotto, C. Lazda, N. Mazzari, The filtered Ogus realisation of motives, J. Algebra 527 (2019), 348–365.

This answers a categorical version of “can one remember all places at once?” affirmatively. It does not provide:

- a global norm on a chosen period coordinate;

- an inequality involving \sum_p v_p(\text{period})\log p;

- a nonvanishing theorem modulo almost all p for that coordinate; or

- a bound on the number of places where one coordinate meets a divisor.

Indeed, filtered Ogus realization is not fully faithful for mixed motives in general; see B. Chiarellotto and N. Mazzari, Extensions of Filtered Ogus Structures, Appl. Categ. Structures 28 (2020), 143–148. Even complete categorical compatibility would still not automatically imply the desired local-height inequality.

## 1.5 Global Diophantine applications of one fixed p do exist — theorem, but different mechanism

Lawrence–Venkatesh use p-adic period maps, monodromy, and global arithmetic geometry to prove finiteness statements. See B. Lawrence and A. Venkatesh, Diophantine problems and p-adic period mappings, Invent. Math. 221 (2020), 893–999.

This is an important warning against saying that p-adic Hodge theory is “only local” in its applications. It can feed a global Diophantine argument. But Lawrence–Venkatesh fix a suitable prime p and use variation plus monodromy/dimension estimates. They do not derive a relation among p-adic valuations at many distinct primes.

Answer to (1): ordinary comparison, Fargues–Fontaine, and current prismatic/Fargues–Fontaine variants do not give a cross-p product formula for period values. Filtered Ogus theory packages many places but has no relevant quantitative inequality. The 2026 global z-adic function-field construction is also not such a theorem over number fields.

# 2. Beilinson–Bloch, motivic L-values, and p-adic L-functions

## 2.1 What L-functions are naturally present — theorem

The modular form f=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4 has its standard automorphic/motivic L-function L(f,s), and one can form L-functions of tensor or symmetric-power constructions. Their good Euler factors are determined by Frobenius characteristic polynomials, for example by the Hecke eigenvalues a_\ell(f).

But the Apéry number b_n is not the Hecke coefficient a_n(f), not a local Euler factor, and not known to be a critical value of L(f,s) or L(\operatorname{Sym}^2 f,s) for each n. The modular/K3 relation supplies congruences and a geometric differential equation; it does not identify every b_n with an L-value.

Thus a local factor

L_\ell(M,s)=\det(1-\mathrm{Frob}_\ell\ell^{-s}\mid V_r(M)^{I_\ell})^{-1}

encodes Frobenius at \ell on a realization of M. It does not encode v_\ell(b_n) merely because b_n comes from the same geometric family.

## 2.2 What Beilinson and Bloch predict — conjectural in general

Beilinson’s conjectures relate leading terms of motivic L-functions at integers to determinants of regulator maps from motivic cohomology to Deligne cohomology. Bloch’s height conjectures and the Beilinson–Bloch philosophy relate orders of vanishing and derivatives to algebraic cycles and height pairings. Foundational references are:

- A. A. Beilinson, Higher regulators and values of L-functions, J. Soviet Math. 30 (1985), 2036–2070.

- S. Bloch, Algebraic cycles and values of L-functions, J. reine angew. Math. 350 (1984), 94–108.

- S. Bloch, Height pairings for algebraic cycles, J. Pure Appl. Algebra 34 (1984), 119–145, DOI 10.1016/0022-4049(84)90032-X.

These formulas concern a global leading term, regulator determinant, or height pairing. They are not upper bounds on the prime support of an arbitrary scalar period coordinate. Even when a special-value formula is proved, it typically gives an equality up to specified rational/algebraic factors and local Tamagawa terms; it does not imply that only o(n/\log n) primes divide a separate recurrence coefficient.

## 2.3 What Perrin–Riou theory says — fixed-p theorem/conjectural framework

For a chosen prime p, motivic p-adic L-functions interpolate algebraic parts of complex critical values over p-adic characters or twists. Perrin–Riou’s big logarithm and Iwasawa-theoretic formalism relate local p-adic Galois cohomology, Selmer classes, and p-adic L-functions. Principal references include:

- J. Coates and B. Perrin-Riou, On p-adic L-functions attached to motives over Q, Adv. Stud. Pure Math. 17 (1989), 23–54.

- B. Perrin-Riou, Fonctions L p-adiques des représentations p-adiques, Astérisque 229 (1995).

Again, p is fixed. A p-adic L-function can control its own order of vanishing or the size of a Selmer group at that p, under deep hypotheses. It does not compare that order with the corresponding orders for all other primes. There is no known Perrin–Riou element whose specialization is b_n and whose characteristic ideal would yield the desired sum over varying p.

## 2.4 Even a hypothetical L-value identity would not automatically give the desired upper bound

Suppose one discovered an identity

b_n = c_n\,L^*(M_n,m)

with an explicit algebraic normalization. Beilinson–Bloch could then interpret the right side as a regulator or height determinant, conjecturally or in special cases. One would still need to control how the rational number represented by that determinant factors across primes. A global height equality gives the total divisor; it does not give a sublinear upper bound for its contribution from (X,2X].

So an L-value interpretation would be useful new structure, but it would not by itself solve the concentration problem.

Answer to (2): the motive and modular form have natural L-functions, but no known special-value or local-factor formula identifies v_p(b_n). Beilinson–Bloch formulas do not generally yield upper bounds on the number or weighted mass of primes dividing one period value. Perrin–Riou theory remains one-prime-at-a-time.

# 3. Integral p-adic Hodge theory and prismatic cohomology

## 3.1 What BMS and prismatic theory add — theorem

Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze construct an integral cohomology theory for proper smooth formal schemes over \mathcal O_{\mathbf C_p} whose specializations recover or refine crystalline, de Rham, and étale cohomology. See B. Bhatt, M. Morrow, P. Scholze, Integral p-adic Hodge theory, Publ. Math. IHÉS 128 (2018), 219–397.

Bhatt–Scholze then attach a prismatic site to a p-adic formal scheme, producing a cohomology theory that specializes to the standard integral p-adic theories. See B. Bhatt and P. Scholze, Prisms and prismatic cohomology, Ann. of Math. 196 (2022), 1135–1275.

These theories can control:

- integral lattices rather than only rational vector spaces;

- torsion and torsion-freeness comparisons;

- denominators and integral structures in cohomology;

- compatibility of Frobenius, filtration, and étale realizations at a fixed p.

## 3.2 What they do not add — no known theorem

They do not produce a statement of the form

\#\{p: \text{an integral period coordinate vanishes mod }p\}\le \text{small},

or

\sum_{p\in(X,2X]}v_p(\text{period})\log p=o(\text{height}).

The integral comparison may tell us that a period vector lies in a canonical \mathbf Z_p-lattice. It may identify reduction modulo p with a crystalline/Hodge–Witt class. But membership in a lattice is not nonvanishing in the residue lattice. A coordinate can be divisible by p even when the comparison module is perfectly torsion-free.

For b_n, integrality is already known. An integral comparison would have to furnish a new mechanism forbidding a specified coordinate from entering p times the lattice for many p. BMS/prismatic theory has no such zero estimate.

## 3.3 Teichmüller points and the unit boundary

At a fixed p, a Teichmüller lift can be useful because Frobenius acts canonically on it. But as p varies, the lifts [n\bmod p] live in different rings and are not one common adelic point of a single analytic space. Their only universal relationship comes from the original algebraic integer n.

At |t|_p=1, an overconvergent F-isocrystal or a prismatic object may extend a local solution and control its integral lattice. This can overcome a local analytic-domain obstruction. It does not supply a global prime-counting theorem. In particular, local extension to a boundary point does not imply that the value is a p-adic unit, much less that it is a unit for almost all primes in a moving interval.

Answer to (3): integral comparison can refine a local calculation of v_p(b_n) and may be valuable for proving a uniform local multiplicity bound such as v_p(b_n)\le C under additional hypotheses. It does not currently bound how many different primes satisfy v_p(b_n)>0. No BMS/prismatic theorem of the requested form is known.

# 4. Genuine “product formulas for periods”: the CM exception

## 4.1 Colmez — theorem/conjecture in a special CM setting

Pierre Colmez formulated and proved major cases of a product formula for periods of CM abelian varieties, analogous to the product formula for algebraic numbers. The theory relates normalized archimedean and p-adic CM periods and is equivalent to formulas expressing Faltings heights in terms of logarithmic derivatives of Artin L-functions. See P. Colmez, Périodes des variétés abéliennes à multiplication complexe, Ann. of Math. 138 (1993), 625–683.

For CM fields abelian over \mathbf Q, the remaining power-of-two issue in Colmez’s product formula was removed by A. Obus, On Colmez’s product formula for periods of CM-abelian varieties, Math. Ann. 356 (2013), 401–418.

The averaged Colmez conjecture for general CM fields was proved independently through:

- F. Andreatta, E. Goren, B. Howard, K. Madapusi Pera, Faltings heights of abelian varieties with complex multiplication, Ann. of Math. 187 (2018), 391–531.

- X. Yuan, S.-W. Zhang, On the averaged Colmez conjecture, Ann. of Math. 187 (2018), 533–638.

## 4.2 Why Colmez does not imply an Apéry valuation bound

Colmez’s setting has all of the following special features:

- complex multiplication supplies one-dimensional eigenspaces indexed by embeddings;

- the periods are canonical CM period symbols, not arbitrary coordinates of a period vector;

- the formula is normalized and averaged at the level of logarithmic period norms/Faltings height;

- Artin L-functions arise from the CM Galois data.

The relevant Apéry modular form is non-CM, as assumed in the question. The scalar b_n is a Taylor/constant-term coefficient, not a canonical CM period symbol. Even in the CM setting, the formula does not say that a chosen integral period coordinate is divisible by few primes; it evaluates a normalized sum of local logarithms.

Thus Colmez is the closest positive answer to the phrase “product formula for periods,” but not to the desired divisibility theorem.

## 4.3 Function-field analogues — theorem, even closer formally but still inapplicable

Hartl–Singh developed function-field analogues for Drinfeld modules and CM A-motives. In particular they compute valuations of periods at all finite places in terms of Artin L-series in the CM function-field setting. See:

- U. Hartl, R. K. Singh, Periods of Drinfeld modules and local shtukas with complex multiplication.

- U. Hartl, R. K. Singh, Product formulas for periods of CM abelian varieties and the function field analog, J. Number Theory 218 (2021), 62–151.

This shows that a truly all-place valuation formula is mathematically possible when CM and local-shtuka structures rigidify the periods. It also highlights exactly what is missing in the Apéry problem: no CM decomposition and no known Artin-character formula for the coordinate b_n.

# 5. Nearest Diophantine precedents

## 5.1 Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier gcd theorem — unconditional theorem

For multiplicatively independent integers a,b>1, Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier proved that for every \varepsilon>0,

\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)<\exp(\varepsilon n)

for sufficiently large n. See Y. Bugeaud, P. Corvaja, U. Zannier, An upper bound for the G.C.D. of a^n-1 and b^n-1, Math. Z. 243 (2003), 79–84.

Corvaja–Zannier generalized this through the Subspace Theorem to gcd heights of values of coprime functions at S-unit points; see P. Corvaja, U. Zannier, A lower bound for the height of a rational function at S-unit points.

The decisive feature is two coprime functions or two independent multiplicative coordinates. This produces codimension two and lets the Subspace Theorem force sublinear common local height outside exceptional algebraic subsets. There is no analogous second coordinate in the bare condition p\mid b_n.

## 5.2 Divisibility sequences and Vojta — conditional theorem

Silverman applies Vojta’s conjecture on blowups to generalized gcds and divisibility sequences on algebraic groups, obtaining strong conditional bounds. See J. H. Silverman, Generalized Greatest Common Divisors, Divisibility Sequences, and Vojta’s Conjecture for Blowups, Monatsh. Math. 145 (2005), 333–350.

Again, the geometry is a blowup along a higher-codimension intersection or the arithmetic of a group-valued orbit. Apéry numbers are not presently known to be a divisibility sequence on an algebraic group in a way that makes p\mid b_n such an intersection condition.

## 5.3 André’s global theory of G-functions — theorem, but controls different global data

André’s theory identifies geometric G-operators, controls denominators and radii of convergence, and develops global relations among special values. See Y. André, G-Functions and Geometry, Aspects of Mathematics E13, Vieweg, 1989.

The Bombieri–André global-relations philosophy is genuinely adelic: product formulas and p-adic analytic estimates combine with archimedean estimates to bound heights of algebraic parameters satisfying relations among G-function values. But its output is a height/degree bound for the parameter or an algebraic-independence statement. It does not bound the prime support of one integral value or one Taylor coefficient at a p-adic unit boundary point.

The distinction is the same as above: global relations use several functions/solutions and a nontrivial algebraic relation. The scalar divisibility condition b_n\equiv0\pmod p gives one local relation in one residue field.

## 5.4 p-adic period maps and unlikely intersections — theorem/programmatic neighbor

Lawrence–Venkatesh shows that a p-adic period map can turn variation of Galois representations into a global finiteness theorem. Recent work on variations of admissible pairs and p-adic Hodge structures likewise develops p-adic unlikely-intersection frameworks, but at a fixed p.

This suggests a possible architecture: place the Apéry coefficient condition into a family where p\mid b_n forces an intersection with a special Frobenius/Hodge locus, then prove that these loci are sufficiently independent as n and p vary. No theorem currently carries out that architecture or sums the local multiplicities across varying residue characteristics.

# 6. Status table

# 7. What a successful new theorem would actually need

A useful formulation is not merely “an adelic comparison theorem,” but an adelic pointwise divisibility theorem for a geometric G-sequence. At least one of the following extra mechanisms seems necessary.

## 7.1 A second independent coordinate

Find another integral period/solution c_n such that p\mid b_n forces simultaneous smallness or vanishing of two coprime coordinates, except for a controlled exceptional set. Then one could hope to apply a gcd-height theorem or a Vojta/Subspace-theorem argument. The known Apéry companion solution and the discrete Wronskian are natural candidates to test, but a condition on b_n alone does not automatically force the required second vanishing.

## 7.2 A global section and a high-codimension special locus

Construct an integral model of the hypergeometric/K3 variation and interpret p\mid b_n as the intersection of an arithmetic orbit indexed by n with a special Frobenius locus. To beat the height bound, the special locus must effectively have codimension at least two, or the orbit must have strong nonaccumulation properties. A codimension-one divisor alone only recovers O(n).

## 7.3 Uniform control of higher multiplicities plus a separate count of simple zeros

Integral crystalline/prismatic methods might plausibly show that, outside exceptional primes,

v_p(b_n)\le C

or that v_p(b_n)>1 is rare. That would address multiplicities. One would still need an independent theorem bounding

\#\{p\in(X,2X]:b_n\equiv0\pmod p\}.

The finite-hypergeometric description is a good local input for this second stage, but no existing global comparison theorem supplies the count.

## 7.4 A sequence-specific prime-divisor theorem

One could seek a theorem for coefficients of a fixed nondegenerate Picard–Fuchs solution, analogous to primitive-divisor theorems or gcd theorems for recurrences. Apéry’s recurrence is polynomial-coefficient rather than a constant-coefficient linear recurrence, so standard recurrence and algebraic-group methods do not directly apply. Such a theorem would be new arithmetic of G-functions, not a formal consequence of their motivic origin.

# 8. Direct answers to the four questions

## (1) p-adic Hodge comparison

No cross-prime relation is produced. The rational comparison is one-prime-at-a-time, and the period rings are single-p objects. Fargues–Fontaine is also local. Filtered Ogus realization is an all-place packaging theorem but has no product inequality for period values. The new global z-adic Fargues–Fontaine geometry over function fields does not change this for motives over \mathbf Q.

## (2) Beilinson–Bloch / L-values

The K3/newform motive has standard and derived L-functions, and fixed-p p-adic L-functions can be constructed in appropriate settings. No known formula identifies v_p(b_n) with a local Euler factor, Selmer length, regulator determinant, or p-adic L-value. Beilinson–Bloch formulas relate global leading terms to regulators/heights and do not yield upper bounds on the number of prime divisors of one coefficient.

## (3) Integral p-adic Hodge / BMS / prismatic

Integral comparison can constrain denominators, torsion, and lattices at one prime. It may help compute or bound an individual v_p(b_n). There is no known theorem bounding how many primes make an integral period vanish mod p at a fixed algebraic or Teichmüller point. Unit-boundary evaluation does not convert local integrality into global nonvanishing.

## (4) Honest state

A product formula strong enough to imply the desired Apéry estimate is not known. The phrase “product formula for periods” already names Colmez-type CM results, so the desired statement should be formulated more specifically as a pointwise cross-place divisibility or local-height nonconcentration theorem for geometric G-function coefficients.

It is not a standard named open conjecture, but it is a natural new problem. Existing p-adic Hodge and Beilinson machinery does not imply it when “assembled correctly.” If proved, it would most likely be a new Diophantine/arithmetic theorem using the motivic local descriptions as inputs, rather than a new comparison isomorphism between realizations.

# 9. Bottom line for the Apéry gcd program

For the desired unconditional pointwise estimate, this direction does not currently close the gap.

- Rational comparison is too local.

- Integral comparison is still too local.

- L-value/regulator theory targets the wrong scalar.

- Colmez gives a true product formula only because CM supplies far more symmetry than the non-CM Apéry motive has.

- General G-function theory gives global height and denominator control but not prime-divisor nonconcentration at one coefficient.

- The closest successful models—BCZ, Corvaja–Zannier, and Silverman/Vojta—need a second independent function or higher-codimension geometry.

Therefore the missing ingredient is genuinely new. The sharp conceptual target is:

Prove a sublinear local-height/nonconcentration theorem for the divisor of an Apéry coefficient in a moving dyadic set of places, using additional motivic independence beyond the one scalar height.

Without that additional independence, any comparison theorem can at best repackage the tautological identity \sum_pv_p(b_n)\log p=\log b_n.
