## 11. Hostile conclusion

The cross-prime second-neighborhood route is mathematically sharper than the reflection first-moment argument, but its missing step is not a routine expansion lemma.

The exact situation is:

- Each spoke is prime-local and rank one.

- A different prime reaches a leaf only through an additional zero satisfying one of two affine equations.

- A star prime reaches another leaf only through an additional folded zero beyond its center zero.

- Pairwise differences contain no target primes.

- Leaf-to-leaf additive structure is impossible unless n\equiv1\pmod3, and even then it supplies only candidate primes, not Apéry zeros.

- Continuant and Casoratian identities require a common residue characteristic.

- The recurrence module allows arbitrary individual reflection spokes when the initial state varies.

Therefore a genuine proof must establish new arithmetic coherence of the fixed initial state (1,5) across changing primes. The weakest concrete form currently visible is the off-diagonal estimate (10.4), or an equivalent low-height signed Newton–Padé/resultant certificate. Without such an input, a rainbow star with degree \asymp n/\log n and degree-one leaves remains fully compatible with every local identity in the current toolkit.
