# 11. Final assessment

The proportional-degree problem is not settled by the known Apéry asymptotic

That asymptotic determines the principal weighted-Pascal symbol and leads to the explicit comparison rate (0.3). In the exactly solvable model, the rate is strictly positive and primitive normalization only increases the certificate.

For the actual Apéry sequence, however, the factor k^{-3/2}, the alternating subset phase, and the cofactor normalization all enter at the same H scale as \log|P(n)|. Existing positive-weight potential theory stops one asymptotic order too early.

Accordingly:

- Proved: exact kernel equations, exact normalization lemma, uniform entry saddle, exact signed partition functions, and a fully solved positive-rate arithmetic model.

- Not proved: proportional normality, the actual projective limit, positivity of its infimum, or a zero-rate split.

- Best current assessment: the data and the endpoint model favor a positive rate; a proof requires a new strong asymptotic for the oscillatory signed Krawtchouk deformation (6.5)–(6.6), not another raw determinant or Smith bound.

This is the concrete remaining analytic problem for the signed Newton–Padé route.
