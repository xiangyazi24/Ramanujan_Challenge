It covers:

A fixed rational constant-term transformation can evade this lemma only by introducing signs or poles whose residues cancel the entire exponential interior. Merely rewriting the same positive coefficient as a larger positive multidimensional sum cannot help.

Similarly, a fixed terminating hypergeometric transformation with nonnegative summands cannot give a subexponential truncation unless its valid cutoff lies entirely on a zero-entropy face. In the q=1 geometry the cutoff has macroscopic size J~n/3, and all known positive transformations contain an interior positive-entropy point.

This is the precise scope of the obstruction. It does not prove that every signed rational or hypergeometric identity is impossible.
