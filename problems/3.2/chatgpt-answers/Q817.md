# Q817 — The q = 1 Apéry diagonal is a moving Mellin coefficient, not a fixed fiber

## Executive verdict

Let A_m denote the Apéry number

$$
A_m=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}^{2}\binom{m+k}{k}^{2}.
$$

For a fixed outer index n and a prime in the q = 1 range

$$
\frac{2n}{3}<p\le n,
$$

write

$$
n=p+r,\qquad 0\le r<\frac p2.
$$

Gessel's Lucas congruence gives the exact reduction

$$
A_n=A_{p+r}\equiv A_1A_r=5A_r\pmod p.
$$

Consequently, for every such prime with p not equal to 5,

$$
p\mid A_{n-p}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid A_n.
$$

The requested arithmetic-geometric premise then has the following precise status.

1. There is an exact polynomial in the Casimir variable

$$
x=n(n+1)
$$

whose reduction at x equals A_n modulo p.  It is a degree p-1 spectral interpolation polynomial.  This is an identity, not a heuristic.

1. In the canonical Apéry K3 geometry, A_r is the r-th coefficient of the entire Hasse-Witt section, equivalently its multiplicative Mellin mode.  It is not the Hasse invariant of the fiber at parameter r(r+1), and it is not the Frobenius trace of one fixed bounded-complexity motive depending only on the outer integer n.

1. The only fixed rank-two weight-three motive presently obtained from this story is the central quadratic-character slice

$$
r=\frac{p-1}{2}.
$$

It is the level-eight weight-four form of Ahlgren and Ono.  In the q = 1 interval this slice can occur only at

$$
p=\frac{2n+1}{3},
$$

so it contributes at most one prime for each n.  It neither implies nor is implied by the full q = 1 diagonal estimate.  The older fixed-form equivalence therefore has the wrong quantifier and the wrong parameter.

1. Taken literally, the phrase “one fixed n-dependent motive” is too weak: the trivial Tate motive of rank A_n has constant Frobenius trace A_n at every prime.  Its rank is exponential in n, its monodromy is trivial, and it merely repackages the integer-divisor problem.  Thus an absolute nonexistence assertion is false; the meaningful question requires bounded rank, bounded geometric complexity, and uniformity with the height of n.

1. Even under the favorable hypothetical assumption that a rank-two weight-three compatible system existed with trace a_{n,p} satisfying

$$
p\mid A_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid a_{n,p},
$$

present Frobenius sieves do not give the required uniform result.  At a bad prime,

$$
a_{n,p}=p\,m_p,\qquad |m_p|\le 2\sqrt p.
$$

Every auxiliary prime of size at most a constant times sqrt(p) sees every possible trace residue, so its local sieve condition is empty.  To recover the common integer multiplier by the Chinese remainder theorem, one needs a coupled modulus larger than sqrt(p).  Division fields of a generic rank-two system then have degree roughly the fourth power of that modulus, far outside the uniform Chebotarev or large-sieve range.  The square sieve has the same-index obstruction: the relevant square divisor is p^2, where p is also the prime being counted.

The exact no-go is therefore not “arithmetic geometry can never work.”  It is this: all currently available auxiliary-prime sieves whose local moduli are below sqrt(n) lose all information about the moving multiplier, while available effective Chebotarev estimates cannot reach a coupled modulus above sqrt(n) uniformly in a fiber of height about n^2.

A sufficient new theorem is stated in Section 10 below.  It is a uniform, same-multiplier, composite-modulus Frobenius local-limit estimate.  No theorem of that strength is presently available, even for the single fixed non-CM weight-four form arising at the center.

## 1. Exact q = 1 reduction

### Proposition 1.1 — Lucas diagonal

Let n be a positive integer and let p be prime with

$$
\frac{2n}{3}<p\le n.
$$

Put

$$
r=n-p.
$$

Then

$$
0\le r<\frac p2,
$$

and, for p different from 5,

$$
p\mid A_r\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid A_n.
$$

### Proof

The interval inequality gives

$$
n<\frac{3p}{2},
$$

hence

$$
r=n-p<\frac p2.
$$

The base-p expansion of n has two digits,

$$
n=1\cdot p+r.
$$

Gessel's Lucas congruence for the Apéry numbers states that if

$$
m=m_0+m_1p+\cdots+m_sp^s,
$$

then

$$
A_m\equiv\prod_{i=0}^{s}A_{m_i}\pmod p.
$$

Therefore

$$
A_n=A_{p+r}\equiv A_1A_r=5A_r\pmod p.
$$

Since 5 is a unit modulo p, the equivalence follows.  The finitely many primes at most 5 are irrelevant to every asymptotic statement.  ∎

Define the q = 1 bad-prime set and its radical by

$$
\mathcal S_1(n)=\left\{p:\frac{2n}{3}<p\le n,\ p\mid A_n\right\},
$$

and

$$
R_1(n)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal S_1(n)}p.
$$

Because every prime in the product lies between 2n/3 and n,

$$
|\mathcal S_1(n)|\log\left(\frac{2n}{3}\right)
\le \log R_1(n)
\le |\mathcal S_1(n)|\log n.
$$

Consequently,

$$
|\mathcal S_1(n)|=o\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)
$$

is equivalent to

$$
\log R_1(n)=o(n).
$$

The elementary height bound does not give the little-oh.  The standard Apéry asymptotic implies

$$
\log A_n
=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
$$

Since every prime in R_1(n) divides A_n, one obtains only

$$
|\mathcal S_1(n)|
\le \frac{\log A_n}{\log(2n/3)}
=O\left(\frac n{\log n}\right).
$$

The entire problem is to improve this constant-order bound to a little-oh bound uniformly for every n.

## 2. The exact Casimir interpolation

The suggested expression x=n(n+1) is not accidental.  It is the invariant of the reflection

$$
\nu\longmapsto-1-\nu.
$$

However, its natural role is spectral rather than geometric.

### Proposition 2.1 — hypergeometric interpolation

Put

$$
X=\nu(\nu+1).
$$

For every nonnegative integer m,

$$
A_m
={}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}-m,-m,m+1,m+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1
\right).
$$

Moreover,

$$
(-\nu)_k(\nu+1)_k
=(-1)^k\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(X-j(j+1)\right).
$$

### Proof

One has

$$
\binom{m}{k}\binom{m+k}{k}
=(-1)^k\frac{(-m)_k(m+1)_k}{(k!)^2}.
$$

Squaring and summing yields the hypergeometric identity.  The product identity follows term by term from

$$
(-\nu+j)(\nu+1+j)
=j(j+1)-\nu(\nu+1).
$$

∎

For an odd prime p, define

$$
C_p(X)=
\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/2}
\frac{
\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}
\left(X-j(j+1)\right)^2
}{(k!)^4}
\in\mathbf F_p[X].
$$

The top summand has degree p-1, so

$$
\deg C_p=p-1.
$$

### Proposition 2.2 — exact q = 1 Casimir equation

For every prime p greater than 5 in the q = 1 interval,

$$
p\mid A_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C_p\!\left(n(n+1)\right)=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
$$

### Proof

Put r=n-p.  Since

$$
0\le r\le\frac{p-1}{2},
$$

the factor with j=r makes every summand of C_p(r(r+1)) with k>r vanish.  Therefore

$$
C_p(r(r+1))=A_r\pmod p.
$$

Also

$$
n(n+1)\equiv r(r+1)\pmod p.
$$

Proposition 1.1 now gives

$$
C_p(n(n+1))\equiv A_r\equiv 5^{-1}A_n\pmod p.
$$

∎

This is the strongest exact statement supplied by the x=n(n+1) suggestion.  It does not yet make C_p a Hasse invariant.  In particular:

- C_p changes with p;

- its degree grows with p;

- the variable X changes the upper hypergeometric parameters, while the ordinary hypergeometric differential variable is the argument that has already been specialized to 1;

- no fixed Cartier matrix, Laurent-polynomial family, or overconvergent F-isocrystal on an X-line has been constructed whose Hasse scalar is C_p(X).

The degree growth alone is not a disproof: genuine fixed families can have Hasse polynomials of degree proportional to p.  The rigorous point is more specific.  The displayed identity is a terminating spectral interpolation on the quadratic lattice.  It does not identify X as a moduli coordinate of a characteristic-zero family.  Such an identification would require a separate geometric construction, including a fixed integral model, a fixed-rank cohomology group, and a Cartier/Frobenius matrix whose reduction gives C_p.  None follows from the hypergeometric notation.

There is also a standard-hypergeometric obstruction.  Katz's hypergeometric local systems remember exponent parameters modulo integers.  At an integer n, the parameters

$$
-n,-n,n+1,n+1
$$

all have trivial local-monodromy eigenvalue.  The nontrivial information in the terminating sum is the integral termination length and its contiguous extension, not a new fixed hypergeometric local system indexed by n.  Thus the usual fixed-parameter hypergeometric sheaf cannot simply be declared to be the desired M_n.

## 3. Why the literal “fixed motive” question is ill-posed

There is a completely rigorous but useless affirmative answer.

### Proposition 3.1 — the tautological Tate motive

For every n, let

$$
M_n=\mathbf Q(0)^{\oplus A_n}.
$$

Then, at every rational prime p,

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid M_n
\right)=A_n.
$$

Its invariants are:

$$
\operatorname{rank}M_n=A_n,
$$

$$
\operatorname{weight}M_n=0,
$$

$$
\operatorname{cond}M_n=1,
$$

$$
\operatorname{Disc}(\mathbf Q)=1,
$$

and

$$
G_{\mathrm{geom}}(M_n)=\{1\}.
$$

It has no bad primes.  Its exact trace equation is

$$
p\mid A_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid M_n).
$$

The rank has exponential size:

$$
\log\operatorname{rank}M_n
=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
$$

Thus the phrase “one fixed n-dependent motive” by itself cannot be the desired theorem.  It permits the arithmetic integer to be stored directly in the rank.  Chebotarev for this motive has nothing to distribute because the monodromy is trivial and the trace is constant.

A meaningful fixed-fiber premise must at least require:

- rank bounded independently of n;

- a fixed algebraic family over a fixed base;

- conductor or discriminant growing at most polynomially with the height of the parameter;

- a compatible system with uniform big-image and ramification control;

- an exact trace or Hasse equation that is not merely an encoding of A_n.

Under those natural requirements, no fixed x=n(n+1) fiber is currently available, and the canonical Apéry geometry gives a different object.

## 4. The canonical Apéry K3 family: coefficient, not fiber value

A standard three-variable Laurent polynomial for the Apéry numbers is

$$
\Phi(x,y,z)
=
\frac{(x+y)(z+1)(x+y+z)(y+z+1)}{xyz}.
$$

It satisfies

$$
A_m=\operatorname{CT}\!\left(\Phi^m\right),
$$

where CT denotes the constant term.  Consider the toric hypersurface family

$$
\mathcal X_t:\quad 1-t\Phi=0.
$$

After the standard compactification and resolution, this is the Beukers-Peters Apéry K3 variation.

### Proposition 4.1 — Hasse-Witt section

At every prime of good reduction, the scalar Hasse-Witt section is, up to the conventional nonzero unit normalization,

$$
\mathscr H_p(t)
=\operatorname{CT}\!\left((1-t\Phi)^{p-1}\right)
\equiv
\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}A_m t^m
\pmod p.
$$

### Proof

Expanding gives

$$
\operatorname{CT}\!\left((1-t\Phi)^{p-1}\right)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}
\binom{p-1}{m}(-t)^m A_m.
$$

Modulo p,

$$
\binom{p-1}{m}\equiv(-1)^m,
$$

so the two signs cancel.  ∎

The load-bearing distinction is now exact:

$$
A_r=[t^r]\mathscr H_p(t),
$$

whereas the Hasse invariant of a geometric fiber at t=t_0 is

$$
\mathscr H_p(t_0)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}A_m t_0^m.
$$

Therefore

$$
A_r\ne\mathscr H_p(r(r+1))
$$

in general.  Substituting

$$
t=n(n+1)
$$

into the canonical K3 family produces a perfectly legitimate K3 fiber, but its Hasse invariant is the entire polynomial evaluated at that point; it is not the coefficient A_n and does not detect the q = 1 condition.

### Proposition 4.2 — exact Mellin inversion

For

$$
1\le r\le p-2,
$$

one has

$$
A_r
=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
\mathscr H_p(t)t^{-r}
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
$$

### Proof

Insert the expansion of mathscr H_p and use

$$
\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}t^j
=
\begin{cases}
-1,&p-1\mid j,\\
0,&p-1\nmid j.
\end{cases}
$$

For 1<=r<=p-2, only the coefficient with index m=r survives.  ∎

For the q = 1 residue r=n-p, this becomes

$$
t^{-r}=t^{1-n}
$$

on mathbf F_p^times, and hence

$$
A_n
\equiv
-5\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
\mathscr H_p(t)t^{1-n}
\pmod p.
$$

This is the correct geometric statement: the q = 1 condition is the vanishing of a moving Mellin coefficient of the full Hasse-Witt section.

There is an equivalent torus formula.  The Newton polytope of Phi has the origin as its unique interior lattice point.  For

$$
1\le r\le p-2,
$$

this prevents nonzero exponent vectors in r Newt(Phi) from aliasing to the trivial character modulo p-1, and yields

$$
A_r
\equiv
-\sum_{\mathbf u\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
\Phi(\mathbf u)^r
\pmod p.
$$

After Teichmüller lifting, the right side is the reduction of a bounded-dimensional Kummer-character cohomological trace.  The character is

$$
\chi_{p,n}=\omega_p^r=\omega_p^{\,n-1},
$$

where omega_p is the Teichmüller character.  The underlying K3 local system is fixed, but the Kummer character is not.

## 5. Geometric invariants that actually exist

### 5.1 The K3 variation in the geometric parameter t

The transcendental part of the Apéry K3 variation has:

$$
\operatorname{rank}=3,
$$

$$
\operatorname{weight}=2,
$$

and Hodge numbers

$$
(1,1,1).
$$

Its Picard-Fuchs operator is

$$
\mathcal L
=
\theta^3
-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3,
$$

where

$$
\theta=t\frac{d}{dt}.
$$

The singular locus is

$$
t=0,\qquad t=\infty,\qquad t^2-34t+1=0,
$$

so the finite conifold parameters are

$$
t=17\pm12\sqrt2.
$$

The connected geometric monodromy is the rank-three orthogonal group

$$
G_{\mathrm{geom}}^\circ\simeq\operatorname{SO}_3,
$$

and the variation is the symmetric square of a rank-two modular local system whose derived monodromy is SL_2.

These invariants belong to the t-family.  They are not invariants of a putative X=n(n+1) family.  The discriminant factor of the obvious K3 fiber at t=x_n would contain

$$
x_n^2-34x_n+1,
$$

but that fiber's Hasse invariant is not A_n.  Computing its conductor therefore does not answer Q817.

### 5.2 The moving Mellin object

For a generic nontrivial Kummer character, the middle Mellin cohomology of the rank-three K3 local system is rank two, pure of weight three, symplectic, and has connected geometric monodromy SL_2.  This is the source of the observed SU(2) Sato-Tate law on the character axis.

This description is bounded in geometric complexity over one finite field, but it is not one compatible system over Q as p varies.  The order of the selected character is

$$
d_{p,n}
=
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-p)}
=
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-1)}.
$$

It can be of size comparable with p.  A characteristic-zero hypergeometric realization of a character of order d naturally lives over a cyclotomic coefficient field.  The naive restriction of scalars of a rank-two realization has rational rank

$$
2\varphi(d),
$$

and the cyclotomic discriminant is

$$
\left|\operatorname{Disc}\mathbf Q(\zeta_d)\right|
=
\frac{d^{\varphi(d)}}{
\displaystyle\prod_{q\mid d}q^{\varphi(d)/(q-1)}}.
$$

Thus even this attempted descent has rank and coefficient-field discriminant growing with the same prime being counted.  More importantly, the value of d itself changes with p, so these are different motives, not the reductions of one M_n.

The finite-field sheaf has tame Kummer ramification and bounded geometric conductor for generic characters, but that statement does not repair the horizontal quantifier: the character, coefficient field, and residual prime all move together.

## 6. Bounded-order characters account for only finitely many q = 1 primes

The failure of a fixed finite list of hypergeometric motives can be quantified exactly.

### Proposition 6.1 — bounded-order slice lemma

Fix n.  Let p lie in the q = 1 interval, put r=n-p, and suppose r is nonzero.  If the character

$$
\omega_p^r
$$

has order d, then there is an integer j with

$$
1\le j\le\frac d2
$$

such that

$$
p=\frac{dn+j}{d+j}.
$$

Consequently, for every D,

$$
\#\left\{
 p:\frac{2n}{3}<p\le n,
\ \operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^{n-p})\le D
\right\}
=O(D^2),
$$

uniformly in n.

### Proof

The order condition is equivalent to

$$
p-1\mid dr.
$$

Set

$$
j=\frac{dr}{p-1}.
$$

Since

$$
0<r\le\frac{p-1}{2},
$$

one has

$$
1\le j\le\frac d2.
$$

Using r=n-p, the equation

$$
d(n-p)=j(p-1)
$$

rearranges to

$$
(d+j)p=dn+j.
$$

For each pair (d,j) there is at most one candidate value of p.  Summing over d<=D and j<=d/2 gives O(D^2).  ∎

Thus every fixed finite collection of rational hypergeometric slopes is automatically harmless for the q = 1 diagonal.  The difficult primes are precisely those for which the character order grows.

## 7. The central fixed weight-four form

The unique quadratic-character point is

$$
r=\frac{p-1}{2}.
$$

Ahlgren and Ono proved, for every prime p at least 5,

$$
A_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f)\pmod{p^2},
$$

where

$$
f(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
\in S_4(\Gamma_0(8)).
$$

The associated fixed motive has:

$$
\operatorname{rank}=2,
$$

$$
\operatorname{weight}=3,
$$

Hodge-Tate weights

$$
\{0,3\},
$$

conductor

$$
8,
$$

coefficient field

$$
\mathbf Q,
$$

no complex multiplication, no nontrivial inner twist, and local polynomial at every prime p different from 2

$$
1-a_p(f)T+p^3T^2.
$$

Its determinant is the cube of the cyclotomic character.  The connected derived monodromy is SL_2, and its Sato-Tate group is SU(2).  The only rational bad prime in the compatible system is 2.

At a prime for which p divides a_p(f), Deligne's bound gives

$$
a_p(f)=p\,m_p,\qquad |m_p|\le2\sqrt p.
$$

The Newton slopes are

$$
\{0,3\}
$$

when p does not divide a_p(f), generically

$$
\{1,2\}
$$

when p divides a_p(f) exactly once, and

$$
\left\{\frac32,\frac32\right\}
$$

when a_p(f)=0.

This fixed motive nevertheless sees only one possible q = 1 prime.  Indeed,

$$
n-p=\frac{p-1}{2}
$$

is equivalent to

$$
p=\frac{2n+1}{3}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\#\left\{
 p\in\mathcal S_1(n):
 n-p=\frac{p-1}{2}
\right\}\le1.
$$

The fixed-form slice is not merely insufficient; it is pointwise negligible without any density theorem.

For the separate fixed-form question, the desired assertion

$$
\#\{p\le X:p\mid a_p(f)\}
=o\left(\frac X{\log X}\right)
$$

is still not known for this non-CM weight-four form.  Current ordinary-prime theorems for weight-two modular forms or GL_2-type abelian varieties do not cover it, and higher-weight results requiring potentially abelian realizations do not apply to its generic SL_2 monodromy.  Even a future proof of this fixed-form density statement would not control the off-center moving characters required here.

This is the exact repair of the older quantifier error: the form 8.4.a.a is a distinguished diagnostic slice, not an equivalent formulation of the q = 1 or full pointwise problem.
