# Projective orbit energy audit for the Apéry pair

## Executive verdict

The reframing is essentially correct and is the strongest formulation of the remaining wall. The Green-function identity converts gap roots from a growing polynomial family into collisions of one explicit projective orbit. The obstruction is no longer family Chebotarev; it is an energy theorem for one nonautonomous orbit.

The sharp status:

- **PROVED (from the stated hypotheses):** the collision interpretation reduces `(R)` to projective multiplicity energy.
- **PROVED:** the determinant energy of an arbitrary \(p\)-point subset of \(\mathbf F_p^2\) is not enough in the critical \(N\asymp p\) regime.
- **PROVED:** the known uniform bound \(m_v\ll p^{2/3}\) only gives \(E_\pi\ll p^{5/3}\).
- **HEURISTIC:** the observed \(E_\pi\sim3p\) is exactly what the reflection involution predicts.
- **OPEN:** obtaining \(E_\pi\ll p\).

The missing lemma is:

> **Apéry projective orbit energy lemma.** For the orbit \(\pi(n)=[b_n:c_n]\) in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)\), the number of pairs \(m,n<p\) with \(\pi(m)=\pi(n)\) is \(O(p)\).

This is strictly stronger than all current gap-root bounds.

# 1. Audit of the Green-function equivalence

## 1.1 Collision identity

The claimed identity

\[
N_h(r)=0 \iff b_rc_{r+h}-c_rb_{r+h}=0
\]

is the correct invariant statement.

The reason is that both quantities are Green functions for the same second-order recurrence. The determinant

\[
D_h(r)=b_rc_{r+h}-c_rb_{r+h}
\]

is alternating in the two solutions and satisfies the same shifted recurrence as the continuant. The initial conditions at \(h=0,1\) determine the scalar factor.

The Casoratian normalization removes the ambiguity:

\[
 b_{n+1}c_n-b_nc_{n+1}=-1/n^3
\]

on the first block, hence all scaling factors are explicit units modulo \(p\).

**Conclusion:** no hidden solution-dependent factor remains.

## 1.2 Edge \(r+h=p-1\)

The equivalence is valid only while all denominator factors are units. The first block must exclude the endpoint where the factorial normalization contains \(p\).

At the endpoint, reflection must be treated separately. This is exactly the mirror contribution already isolated in the colored-root BV package.

## 1.3 Companion solution

The companion solution \(c_0=0,c_1=1\) is legitimate because the recurrence coefficients are units for \(n\le p-2\). No use is made of \(b_r,b_{r+1}\) being nonzero.

The no-consecutive-zero statement is only needed when converting a zero event into a projective state hit. The collision identity itself is solution-independent.

# 2. Uniform multiplicity upgrade

For any solution \(u_n\), a double hit

\[
u_r=u_{r+h}=0
\]

forces

\[
p\mid N_h(r).
\]

The proof depends only on the transfer recurrence, not the initial vector. Therefore the gap-counting argument applies to every projective point.

Hence:

\[
\max_v m_v\ll p^{2/3}.
\]

This is a real upgrade: it controls every color, not only \(L=[0:1]\).

But the energy consequence is only

\[
E_\pi=\sum_v m_v^2\le (\max_v m_v)\sum_vm_v
\ll p^{2/3}\cdot p=p^{5/3}.
\]

The desired \(O(p)\) requires concentration of the entire multiplicity spectrum, not just the maximum.

---

# 3. Incidence geometry audit

The energy is

\[
E_\pi=\#\{(m,n):b_mc_n-b_nc_m=0\}.
\]

This is determinant/line energy.

For an arbitrary \(N\)-point set in \(\mathbf F_p^2\):

- if \(N\ll p^{3/2}\), Rudnev-type point-plane incidence gives nontrivial bounds;
- at \(N\asymp p\), the trivial scale \(N^2/p+pN\) is already \(O(p^2)\), not \(O(p)\).

So generic incidence theory cannot see the target.

The missing structure is the recurrence orbit:

\[
v_{n+1}=M_nv_n.
\]

No published incidence theorem currently exploits a deterministic nonautonomous polynomial matrix staircase of this type strongly enough to produce \(O(p)\) determinant energy.

The closest analogues are:

- Bourgain-Katz-Tao sum-product: requires additive/multiplicative expansion hypotheses;
- Rudnev point-plane incidence: treats arbitrary sets, not recursive trajectories;
- Bourgain-Gamburd: treats random walks with fixed generators, not changing generators.

The missing lemma would be:

> The Apéry transfer cocycle has no exceptional low-dimensional orbit concentration beyond the reflection symmetry.

# 4. Exponential sum formulation

Define

\[
T(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{n<p}e_p(\alpha b_n+\beta c_n).
\]

For direction multiplicities \(m_v\), Fourier inversion gives

\[
\sum_v m_v^2
=\frac1{p^2}\sum_{a,b\in\mathbf F_p}
\left|\sum_n e_p(a b_n+b c_n)\right|^2
\]

only after quotienting the affine vector distribution; projective energy requires removing the radial coordinate and replacing equality of vectors by equality of directions.

The exact useful statement is therefore not a direct Parseval identity for \(T\), but a projective Fourier statement controlling the mass of the determinant-zero variety.

A sufficient exponential-sum theorem would be:

\[
\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)}|T(\alpha,\beta)|^2
\ll p^3.
\]

with additional control of the radial degeneracies.

Equivalently, one needs square-root cancellation for the two-dimensional trace function attached to the pair \((b,c)\).

This is substantially stronger than

\[
\sum_n e_p(u b_n)
\]

because the latter only probes one coordinate projection.

# 5. Arithmetic structure of the companion solution

## 5.1 Closed form

No binomial formula analogous to

\[
b_n=\sum_k\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2
\]

is currently known for \(c_n\).

The reason is conceptual: \(b_n\) is the holomorphic period; \(c_n\) is the logarithmic companion at the MUM point.

## 5.2 Lucas structure

A digit-product formula for \(c_n\) is unknown. If one existed, it would likely be the decisive breakthrough because the pair \((b_n,c_n)\) would become a Frobenius-semilinear object.

## 5.3 Geometric interpretation

The expected interpretation is correct:

- \(b_n\) is one period/Hasse-Witt coefficient;
- \(c_n\) is the companion period in the same rank-two variation.

However, finite-field trace formulas for logarithmic periods are much harder because the logarithm solution is not a pure Frobenius eigenperiod.

# 6. Second-moment/dispersion route

A natural target is

\[
\sum_{p\asymp X}\sum_h
\left(C_{p,h}-\rho_{p,h}/p\right)^2.
\]

Expansion creates quadruples of orbit points satisfying two collision constraints. Geometrically this is an intersection of two determinant correspondences.

This is the correct BDH analogue, but existing polynomial-root BDH theorems do not apply:

- their degree is fixed;
- their families have bounded conductor;
- here the cocycle length varies.

The missing theorem is a bounded-complexity two-parameter trace estimate for the Apéry correspondence.

---

# 7. Partial energy bounds and downstream effect

Suppose

\[
E_\pi(p)\ll p^{1+\theta}.
\]

Then the average collision count improves from the current root-degree estimate by a factor \(p^{2/3-\theta}\).

Current:

\[
\theta=2/3,\qquad E\ll p^{5/3}.
\]

Target:

\[
\theta=0,\qquad E\ll p.
\]

Any fixed saving

\[
E\ll p^{5/3-\delta}
\]

would already be a new family-compatibility theorem.

The exact downstream exponent depends on inserting the resulting average collision count into the colored-root BV sum, but the qualitative transition is:

- \(p^{5/3}\): no improvement over the existing gap method;
- \(p^{1+\delta}\): partial improvement;
- \(p\): closes the energy half of GARQI-1'.

# 8. Final verdict

## Ranking

1. **Best chance:** exploit arithmetic of the pair \((b,c)\), especially a Frobenius-semilinear digit structure for \(c\). First lemma:

> Prove a Lucas/Gessel-type congruence for the companion solution.

2. **Second:** prove a two-dimensional exponential sum theorem for \(T(\alpha,\beta)\).

3. **Third:** second-moment dispersion over the \((p,h)\) family.

4. **Lowest probability:** generic incidence geometry or expansion theory.

## Death certificate

> No current theorem on incidence geometry, expander walks, polynomial dynamics, or fixed-family Frobenius equidistribution proves \(E_\pi(p)=O(p)\) for a nonautonomous defining-characteristic rank-two recurrence orbit of length \(p\); the missing input is a genuine two-coordinate Frobenius/digit theorem for the Apéry solution pair \((b_n,c_n)\).
