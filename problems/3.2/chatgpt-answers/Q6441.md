# Katz Mellin equidistribution versus defining-characteristic Apéry divisibility

## Executive verdict

There is a numbering correction before the mathematics:

In Katz, Convolution and Equidistribution, AM-180, 27.1 is a lemma in the chapter of exceptional-group examples, not the sequence-of-finite-fields equidistribution theorem. The fixed-object equidistribution theorem is Theorem 7.2, with the connected special case Theorem 7.3 and scalar-trace Corollary 7.4. The result for a sequence of finite fields of growing characteristic is Theorem 28.1.

# 2. Does one global family force the same Mellin group for all large primes?

## 2.1 What ordinary specialization gives

Suppose an auxiliary-prime \ell is fixed and the relevant local systems spread out over

with a smooth relative compactification and relative normal-crossings boundary. The standard specialization theorems for étale and tame fundamental groups—SGA 1, Exposé X, Corollaire 3.9 in the proper smooth setting and Exposé XIII, Corollaire 2.12 in the tame normal-crossings setting—compare the prime-to-residue-characteristic geometric fundamental groups of the generic and special fibers.

Consequently, for a fixed relative lisse tame sheaf, the following ordinary data can be made constant after enlarging N:

- local tame inertia conjugacy classes;

- determinant and orthogonal/symplectic self-duality;

- ordinary geometric monodromy containment;

- irreducibility, provided one also controls the relevant endomorphism spaces by proper or compact-support base change.

For rank three this often permits a direct Apéry-specific proof. Irreducibility, an orthogonal form, a surviving regular unipotent local monodromy element, and the determinant character can force connected component SO_3; the quadratic determinant then distinguishes O_3 from SO_3. A Goursat argument plus exclusion of Kummer mutual twists can determine the joint group.

This is the most concrete same-day route for \mathcal A_\pm: prove the group from its local monodromy separately in each good characteristic, rather than appeal to a black-box specialization of the Mellin category.

## 2.2 Why this is not yet Theorem 28.1’s input

The Mellin–Tannakian group is not merely the ordinary monodromy group of the rank-three sheaf. It is the Tannakian group of the convolution category after quotienting by negligible objects, with fiber functor given by generic twisted compactly supported cohomology.

Passing from a global family to equality

requires all of the following to survive specialization:

1. generic vanishing for the Kummer-twist fiber functor;

1. the generic Mellin dimension;

1. the negligible subcategory and the quotient by it;

1. invariant tensors in convolution powers;

1. Kummer self-twists and mutual twists;

1. determinant and component-group characters.

No theorem in SGA 1 or Katz AM-180 packages these six assertions into (2.1) for a family over \mathbf Z[1/N]. Larsen–Pink/Chin-style independence results do not fill the gap: they concern compatible systems over a fixed base and variation of the auxiliary prime, not the diagonal regime in which the residue characteristic and finite field both vary.

Therefore the honest answer is:

There is no off-the-shelf theorem chain currently cited in the project that takes “one global integral family with generic Mellin group G” directly to “the fiberwise Mellin group is G for every sufficiently large p.”

## 2.3 Minimal new specialization lemma

A sufficient project-level statement is the following.

[GAP-SPEC-MELLIN]. Let \mathcal N be a relative tame pure perverse object on \mathbf G_{m,S}, with generic Mellin–Tannakian group G. After shrinking S, require:

1. uniform generic vanishing and a uniform bound for bad Kummer characters;

2. base-change compatibility for the finite list of convolution/tensor constructions whose invariant lines cut out G inside \mathrm{GL}(V);

3. constancy of the negligible summands in those constructions;

4. constancy of the finite Kummer self-twist and mutual-twist groups;

5. constancy of determinant and component characters.

Then every geometric fiber has Mellin–Tannakian group G.

Why a finite list should suffice is Chevalley’s theorem: a fixed closed subgroup of \mathrm{GL}(V) is the stabilizer of finitely many lines in finite tensor constructions. If the dimensions of these invariant spaces are constructible and no new invariant tensor appears after specialization, the group cannot drop. On the one-dimensional arithmetic base S, a constructible jumping locus missing the generic point is finite.

The unresolved technical point is making this argument inside Katz’s convolution quotient, uniformly over the varying Kummer character groups. That is the genuine content of [GAP-SPEC-MELLIN], not ordinary tame specialization.

# 5. What maximal joint monodromy does and does not buy

Assume the joint Mellin group of the two descended Apéry objects is maximal subject to determinant and self-duality. Then Katz yields:

1. convergence of every fixed normalized trace moment to the corresponding Haar moment;

1. asymptotic independence whenever the joint group is a direct product rather than a diagonal/Goursat subgroup;

1. square-root error for each fixed tensor moment;

1. o(p) exact characteristic-zero trace zeros;

1. effective predictions for low-degree moments and correlations that are immediately machine-checkable.

It does not yield:

1. uniform distribution of A_{p,\chi}\bmod\mathfrak p_p;

1. a bound for |Z_p|;

1. an average of |Z_p| over primes;

1. the census statement F_4=0;

1. any exclusion of a power-law residual tail.

The empirical Poisson law and F_4=0 concern residual anti-concentration and cross-prime anti-alignment. Maximal complex monodromy is compatible with those observations but does not explain them.

# 7. Precise final no-go and the weakest live route

The invisible step can be pinpointed in one line:

Theorem 28.1 controls the left-hand datum. The Apéry event uses the right-hand datum. There is no continuity, approximation, or algebraicity argument that reconstructs the latter from the former at the required scale.

Consequently, proving the largest possible Mellin group is valuable and necessary for the characteristic-zero statistics, but it does not cross the defining-characteristic wall. The weakest live output-side theorem is not “stronger equidistribution in K”; it is the Apéry-specific residual Fourier statement [GAP-RES-WEYL], pointwise or averaged over primes.
