# R3-g — anti-sunflower mechanisms for Apéry divisibility rows

## Executive verdict

There are three materially different conclusions.

1. **Cumulants, Stein couplings, dependency graphs, and statistical-mechanical cluster expansions are real theorem packages, but they are not arithmetic sources of decorrelation.** In every successful number-theoretic use I found, a sieve/Kubilius model, a level of distribution, or an explicit mixing mechanism first supplies the joint laws. In the present top-prime range, the product modulus of two rows is \(pq\asymp X^2\) while the sample interval has length \(X\). A black-box cumulant or Stein theorem does not cross this wall.

2. **The one established analytic architecture that really can beat individual modulus undersampling is Linnik's dispersion method.** Bombieri--Friedlander--Iwaniec and, more recently, Maynard obtain cancellation for moduli beyond the naive square-root barrier by averaging the moduli and exposing a common bilinear/convolution structure, ultimately reducible to Kloosterman, spectral, or bounded-conductor trace-function estimates. Thus a Franel/Mellin-to-dispersion route is not fantasy. But the required first step is missing: coefficient vanishing in the Apéry/Franel square has not yet been converted into a bounded-complexity bilinear kernel. The exact zero indicator currently has defining-characteristic and apparently linear-complexity behavior.

3. **The Lucas calibration is unconditionally soluble.** For a nondegenerate Lucas sequence \(U_n\), if \(\rho(p)\) is the rank of apparition and \(\chi_p=(D/p)\), then

\[
p\mid U_{n-p}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid U_{n-\chi_p}
\qquad (p\nmid 2QD).
\]

Using \(\rho(p)\mid p-\chi_p\), grouping by \(d=\rho(p)\mid n-\chi_p\), and balancing the size bound for \(U_d\) against the progression \(p\equiv\chi_p\pmod d\), one obtains

\[
\max_n\#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid U_{n-p}\}
\ll_U
\bigl(\tau(n-1)+\tau(n+1)\bigr)
\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}
=n^{1/2+o(1)}.
\]

So the easiest nontrivial divisibility-sequence model has a strong anti-sunflower theorem. The decisive structure is not “recurrence” or “motive”; it is the **finite-offset apparition law** \(\rho(p)\mid p-\chi_p\), with only two offsets \(\chi_p=\pm1\).

For Apéry, the most useful ranking is:

| Rank | mechanism | present plausibility | exact missing input |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Franel/Mellin bilinearization + Linnik dispersion | low but genuinely precedented | a bounded-complexity Type-II representation of the centered zero-row function, uniform in the varying prime |
| 2 | new low-height global carrier | low after the project's saturation audits, but immediate payoff | \(C_n\ne0\), \(\log|C_n|=o(n)\), and every top hit prime divides \(C_n\) |
| 3 | finite-offset apparition surrogate | very low for the empirically random \(Z_p\), but sharply falsifiable | each hit admits \(d\mid r\), \(d\mid p-\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\) in a fixed finite set, and \(p\mid C_d\), \(\log|C_d|\ll d\) |
| 4 | horizontal self-characteristic local limit for the K3 Mellin family | conceptually exact, currently Lang--Trotter-level or harder | a uniform-in-\(n\) zero-count theorem for the moving Kummer twist \(\omega_p^{n-1}\) |
| 5 | weighted cumulant/dependency graph | useful wrapper, not a source | a summable tree bound for the cross-prime joint cumulants |
| 6 | larger/inverse sieve | no pointwise force without amplification | prove that one center forces a large family of companion centers |

The rest of this note makes all six statements precise and proves the Lucas theorem.

## 1. Exact anatomy of the cross-prime problem

Let

\[
I=(X,2X]\cap\mathbf Z,
\qquad
X_p(n)=1_{A_p}(n),
\qquad
H(n)=\sum_pX_p(n).
\]

Only \(X/2<p\le2X\) can contribute. For distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\),

\[
A_{p_1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{p_k}
=
\left\{n\in I:n-p_i\in Z_{p_i}\ (1\le i\le k)\right\}.
\]

Consequently the falling-factorial moment is exactly

\[
\boxed{
M_k
=\sum_{n\in I}(H(n))_k
=\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_k}^{\mathrm{distinct}}
 \left|A_{p_1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{p_k}\right|.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus the moment method is not losing anything: an \(h\)-petal sunflower centered at \(n_0\) contributes \((h)_k\) to every \(M_k\) with \(k\le h\).

For two rows there are two equivalent descriptions:

\[
\begin{aligned}
|A_p\cap A_q|
&=\#\{(r,s)\in Z_p\times Z_q:p+r=q+s\in I\}\\
&=\#\{r\in Z_p:r+p-q\in Z_q,\ p+r\in I\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The second line shows the real object: a **cross-characteristic shifted correlation** between \(Z_p\) and \(Z_q\), at shift \(p-q\). The CRT formulation says that prescribed \(r\bmod p\) and \(s\bmod q\) determine one class modulo \(pq\), but \(I\) contains only \(O(X/pq)=O(1/X)\) complete samples of that class. Either formulation exposes the same obstruction.

Put the uniform probability measure on \(I\), and write

\[
\mu_p=\mathbf E_I X_p=\frac{|A_p|}{|I|}.
\]

The pair connected term is

\[
\kappa_I(X_p,X_q)
=\frac{|A_p\cap A_q|}{|I|}-\mu_p\mu_q.
\tag{1.3}
\]

More generally,

\[
\kappa_k(H)=
\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_k}
\kappa_I(X_{p_1},\ldots,X_{p_k}).
\tag{1.4}
\]

A common center in \(k\) specified rows inserts a term at least \(1/|I|\) into the corresponding joint moment. When row sizes are \(O(1)\), the independent baseline is \(O(|I|^{-k})\). In the usual inclusion--exclusion formula for a joint cumulant, the one-block term is therefore of order \(1/X\), while products attached to partitions with two or more blocks are normally \(O_k(X^{-2})\). In other words, the connected correlation is designed to **detect** the sunflower; it does not dissolve it.

There is also an exact Fourier package. Define

\[
R_p(\theta)
=\sum_{\substack{r\in Z_p\\p+r\in I}}e(\theta(p+r)).
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{n\in I}H(n)^2
=\int_0^1\left|\sum_pR_p(\theta)\right|^2d\theta.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The diagonal part is row-local. Every useful gain must come from cancellation among the cross terms \(R_p\overline{R_q}\). Formula (1.5) is a clean target for dispersion, but applying the ordinary large sieve to each row separately loses exactly the needed cross-prime information.

## 2. Question 1: what cumulant/cluster frameworks actually exist?

### 2.1 Sieve moments and Kubilius models: yes, but below a level of distribution

Granville--Soundararajan's sieve proof of Erdős--Kac computes moments in a general sifted sequence. Its arithmetic hypothesis is, schematically,

\[
|\mathcal A_d|=\frac{h(d)}d|\mathcal A|+r_d
\tag{2.1}
\]

for squarefree products \(d\) of the primes occurring in the moment, together with control of the aggregate errors \(r_d\). This is exactly a joint-correlation theorem furnished by a level of distribution. It is highly effective when the relevant products \(d\) remain below that level.

Ford's joint Poisson theorem similarly uses the Kubilius model for sets of “not too large” prime factors of a random integer. His 2024 shifted-prime analogue constructs a Kubilius-type model for prime factors of \(p+a\). These are genuine cross-prime Poisson theorems, but the underlying events are ordinary divisibility events for one integer and inherit multiplicativity plus sieve distribution.

For the Apéry top rows, already at \(k=2\),

\[
d=pq\asymp X^2>|I|.
\]

There is no analogue of (2.1) currently available for the event

\[
p\mid b_{n-p},\qquad q\mid b_{n-q}.
\]

So the standard sieve-moment framework identifies the missing datum but supplies no estimate for it.

### 2.2 Stein--Chen and size-biased coupling: also a wrapper

The Arratia--Goldstein--Gordon theorem and its many descendants bound Poisson approximation errors using local-dependence quantities. Harper's Stein proof of Erdős--Kac succeeds because ordinary prime divisibility can first be coupled to an approximately independent prime-factor model.

Here a size-biased construction would proceed as follows. Choose a row \(p\) with probability proportional to \(\mu_p\), and replace a uniform \(N\in I\) by a random \(N^{(p)}\) having the law of \(N\) conditioned on \(N\in A_p\). One would then need

\[
\sum_q
\mathbf P\bigl(X_q(N^{(p)})\ne X_q(N)\bigr)
\tag{2.2}
\]

uniformly small on average over \(p\). But conditioning on \(N\bmod p\) is a global move: it has no local neighborhood in the index set of primes. Estimating (2.2) is another form of the pair codegree sum (1.2). Thus the coupling does not remove the arithmetic problem; it restates it.

### 2.3 Dependency graphs and cumulant tree bounds

Féray's weighted-dependency-graph theory gives powerful normality and cumulant conclusions when one can prove that joint cumulants are controlled by maximum spanning-tree weights. A directly useful Apéry version would be the following.

> **Weighted cross-prime tree lemma.** There are symmetric weights \(w_X(p,q)\ge0\) and an absolute \(C\) such that
> \[
> \sup_p\sum_qw_X(p,q)\le C,
> \tag{2.3}
> \]
> and, for every tuple of distinct primes and every \(2\le k\le K(X)\),
> \[
> \left|
> \kappa_I(X_{p_1},\ldots,X_{p_k})
> \right|
> \le
> \frac{C^k}{X}
> \sum_{T\in\mathcal T_k}
> \prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(T)}w_X(p_i,p_j).
> \tag{2.4}
> \]

Summing labels along a tree and using Cayley's bound would give

\[
|\kappa_k(H)|\le C_1^k k!
\]

(up to harmless powers of \(k!\)), and the moment--cumulant formula would yield the desired factorial-moment growth for growing \(k\).

This is a precise and dispatchable lemma. It is also essentially the cross-prime theorem: no natural sparse dependency graph is presently visible. If a center has \(h\) petals, the \(h\) participating vertices form a genuinely high-order dependent cluster.

### 2.4 Statistical-mechanical cluster expansions

A polymer formulation can be made literally. Associate to a finite prime set \(S\) the activity

\[
z(S)=\mathbf P_I\left(\bigcap_{p\in S}\{X_p=1\}\right).
\tag{2.5}
\]

The logarithm of the resulting partition function is a sum of connected/Ursell coefficients. Kotecký--Preiss and its refinements prove convergence if the total weighted activity of polymers incompatible with a given polymer is small.

But a sunflower center with \(h\) petals produces \(2^h-1\) nonempty prime subsets \(S\), each with

\[
z(S)\ge\frac1X.
\]

Hence the Kotecký--Preiss small-activity condition is precisely an anti-sunflower condition in disguise. Statistical mechanics supplies excellent bookkeeping after decay is known; it does not prove the decay from row sizes.

### 2.5 Larger and inverse sieve: survives undersampling, but counts sets, not one point

Gallagher's larger sieve is important because it does not require the interval to contain many representatives of every product-modulus class. It uses the product of pairwise differences of a **set of integers** and local bounds on the number of occupied residue classes. Green--Harper's inverse large-sieve program studies structure near the square-root threshold. The July 2026 result of Croot--Yip significantly strengthens the known forced intersection with a quadratic image for large near-extremal sets.

None of these results excludes a singleton. For \(S=\{n_0\}\), every residue-class occupancy bound is perfect. Therefore the larger sieve can enter only after an additional **amplification theorem** saying that one high-multiplicity center forces many companion centers.

### 2.6 Linnik dispersion: the one genuine positive precedent

The CRT wall is not an absolute theorem of impossibility. The dispersion method studies an average over moduli, expands a variance, factorizes the coefficients into Type-I/Type-II pieces, and uses cancellation in the resulting complete or incomplete exponential sums. Bombieri--Friedlander--Iwaniec used this to treat primes in arithmetic progressions to moduli beyond the naive level; Maynard pushed several such mean-value theorems further. In these works the individual progression modulus may be too large for direct equidistribution, but averaging and bilinear structure recover cancellation.

This is the closest existing analogue to the desired pair cumulant. Its non-negotiable input is a common algebraic/bilinear kernel. In the Apéry setting, one would need to turn

\[
1_{b_r\equiv0\pmod p}
=\frac1p\sum_{h\bmod p}e_p(hb_r)
\tag{2.6}
\]

and the Franel-square coefficient structure into Type-II sums whose complete correlations have bounded conductor. At present the canonical polynomial representing \(r\mapsto b_r\pmod p\) has linear degree in the tested range, and the defining-characteristic phase in (2.6) is not an \(\ell\)-adic trace function of fixed conductor.

### 2.7 Answer to Question 1

- **Yes:** connected correlations across primes have been bounded for ordinary additive functions, polynomial-value sieves, shifted-prime factorization, and related settings with a level of distribution or Kubilius model.
- **Yes, in a different sense:** Linnik dispersion has succeeded when each individual product modulus is too large for direct sampling.
- **No matching theorem was found:** there is no black-box cumulant, cluster, dependency-graph, or Stein theorem that bounds the top-prime correlations of the zero sets of one fixed P-recursive/hypergeometric sequence without first proving an arithmetic cross-prime estimate.
- **For Apéry:** even the centered \(k=2\) estimate is a new theorem. Fixed-\(k\) Poisson approximation would still not settle the maximum; the project correctly needs either a growing-order version or a deterministic anti-center mechanism.

## 3. Question 2: rigidity on the resonance line

### 3.1 Two different residue conventions

On a hit,

\[
r=n-p,\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

Therefore

\[
r\equiv n\pmod p.
\tag{3.1}
\]

So the additive-index formulation is indeed

\[
b_{n\bmod p}\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{3.2}
\]

However, when the index is encoded as a multiplicative character \(\omega_p^r\), the exponent is read modulo \(p-1\), and

\[
r=n-p\equiv n-1\pmod{p-1}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the Mellin/Kummer diagonal is \(\chi_p=\omega_p^{\,n-1}\), not \(\omega_p^n\).

### 3.2 What the K3/Mellin description really gives

The useful structural statement is schematically

\[
b_r\pmod p
=\mathcal M_p(\omega_p^r),
\tag{3.4}
\]

where \(\mathcal M_p\) is the finite Mellin transform of the fixed Apéry K3 trace object, with elementary exceptional-character factors suppressed. Along the resonance line, a sunflower center asks for

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M_p(\omega_p^{\,n-1})\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\text{for many }p\asymp n.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is a very clean reformulation. It does not turn (3.5) into a standard fixed-trace-function problem for three reasons.

1. The character group itself varies with \(p\).
2. The selected character \(\omega_p^{n-1}\) is a moving horizontal section through those varying groups.
3. The event is exact divisibility by the residue characteristic. Vertical Sato--Tate controls normalized complex or \(\ell\)-adic traces against continuous test functions; it does not give a local limit at the single residue class zero modulo \(p\).

### 3.3 Why the Franel square does not yet give an evaluation trace function

Write

\[
A_p(T)=\sum_{0\le r<p}b_rT^r.
\]

The verified pullback identity

\[
A_p(t(x))(1+x)^{p-1}=H_p(x)^2
\tag{3.6}
\]

is strong global structure on the coefficient polynomial. But \(b_r=0\) is a **coefficient-vanishing** event after a rational pullback and a square. It is not the same as the vanishing of \(H_p\) at an algebraic point determined by \(r\) or \(n\).

Coefficient extraction from a square is a convolution:

\[
[T^r]H_p(T)^2=\sum_{i+j=r}h_ih_j.
\]

Unless this convolution diagonalizes in a uniformly bounded-complexity transform, the conductor grows with \(p\). One can always interpolate \(r\mapsto b_r\) by a polynomial of degree \(<p\), but that is exactly the vacuous linear-complexity representation already encountered in the project audits.

The correct possible payoff of (3.6) is therefore not “apply Deligne to \(b_{n\bmod p}\).” It is:

> find a transform of the coefficient-zero indicator in which the Franel convolution becomes a bounded number of Type-II kernels, then apply a dispersion estimate across \(p\).

### 3.4 Fixed motive does not imply pointwise anti-alignment

The closest classical warning is Lang--Trotter. For one fixed elliptic curve, Sato--Tate is known, yet the predicted asymptotic for primes with one fixed Frobenius trace remains open in general; even GRH does not settle the basic non-CM conjecture. In several CM cases the fixed-trace problem is tied to Hardy--Littlewood prime-value conjectures for quadratic polynomials. Thus the comparison with a local-limit/Hardy--Littlewood difficulty is legitimate, though not a literal equivalence for Apéry.

The Apéry problem has an additional moving Kummer twist and asks for a bound uniform in the integer \(n\). It should therefore not be advertised as a consequence of geometric monodromy alone.

### 3.5 The exact deterministic alternative: a global carrier

The cleanest anti-alignment principle is elementary.

> **Carrier proposition.** Suppose that for every \(n\) there is a nonzero integer \(C_n\) such that every prime \(p\in(n/2,n]\) with \(p\mid b_{n-p}\) also divides \(C_n\). Then
> \[
> H(n)\log(n/2)\le\log|C_n|.
> \tag{3.7}
> \]
> In particular, \(\log|C_n|=o(n)\) proves the target conjecture.

The proof is just the product of the distinct hit primes.

This explains both the attraction and the ceiling of the project's carrier program. The Franel cutoff carriers, binomial carriers, companion-denominator carrier, Laurent--Cartier cross-model carrier, and index-jet endpoint layers identify the correct support, but the primitive carrier height remains linear or the required subexponential gcd statement is equivalent to the original top-slice problem. More local identities or extra \(p\)-adic multiplicity do not automatically lower the global Archimedean height.

### 3.6 Sharp hierarchy of “sparse \(p\)-dependent sets” results

1. **Arbitrary sparse sets:** no anti-alignment at all. Given \(N\), take \(Z_p=\{N-p\}\) for all \(p\in(N/2,N]\); adding the reflected point preserves reflection symmetry. Every row hits \(N\).
2. **Roots of one fixed polynomial:** very strong anti-alignment. If \(Z_p\subseteq\{r:F(r)=0\pmod p\}\) for fixed nonzero \(F\in\mathbf Z[T]\), then a hit gives \(p\mid F(n-p)\equiv F(n)\pmod p\), hence \(H(n)\le\deg F+O_F(1)\).
3. **Finite-offset rank/apparition systems:** \(n^{1/2+o(1)}\) by the Lucas theorem below.
4. **A global motive with exact trace zero:** no general pointwise theorem; Lang--Trotter is the calibration.
5. **A hypergeometric/coefficient family without rank structure:** can have a macroscopic sunflower; the central-binomial example below gives \(\asymp n/\log n\) hits.

Thus “defined by one global motive” is not yet a theorem-level substitute for either a carrier or an apparition law.

## 4. Cheapest concrete templates

### 4.1 Fixed polynomial sequence: bounded multiplicity

Let \(a_r=F(r)\) with fixed \(F\in\mathbf Z[T]\setminus\{0\}\). For \(p>r=n-p\),

\[
p\mid a_r
\Longrightarrow
p\mid F(n-p)\equiv F(n)\pmod p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid a_{n-p}\}
\le\frac{\log|F(n)|}{\log(n/2)}
\le\deg F+O_F(1).
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the ideal carrier mechanism.

### 4.2 Factorial sequence: zero multiplicity

For \(a_r=r!\), every top prime satisfies \(p>r\), so \(p\nmid r!\). Hence there are no hits. For factorial shifts such as \(r!+1\), this trivial order mechanism disappears; I found no off-the-shelf theorem giving the required uniform diagonal bound. Grimm-type conjectures and covering-congruence constructions are useful warnings about simultaneous prime assignments, not tools that resolve this line.

### 4.3 Central binomial coefficients: a complete negative calibration

Let

\[
a_r=\binom{2r}{r}.
\]

For \(0\le r<p\), Kummer's theorem says

\[
p\mid\binom{2r}{r}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2r\ge p,
\tag{4.2}
\]

because this is exactly the carry condition in adding \(r+r\) in base \(p\). With \(r=n-p\), (4.2) is

\[
2(n-p)\ge p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\le\frac{2n}{3}.
\]

Thus, up to endpoint conventions,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid\binom{2(n-p)}{n-p}\}
=\pi(2n/3)-\pi(n/2)
\sim\frac{n}{6\log n}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

So a concrete hypergeometric/binomial family supports a positive-density-of-primes sunflower. Hypergeometric provenance, palindromy, or a low-degree generating differential equation cannot by itself imply anti-alignment.

## 5. Explicit Lucas calibration theorem

### 5.1 Setup

Let

\[
U_0=0,\qquad U_1=1,\qquad
U_{m+2}=P U_{m+1}-Q U_m,
\tag{5.1}
\]

where \(P,Q\in\mathbf Z\), \(Q\ne0\), \(\gcd(P,Q)=1\), and the sequence is nondegenerate. Put

\[
D=P^2-4Q.
\]

For a prime \(p\nmid2QD\), let \(\rho(p)\) be the rank of apparition: the least positive \(d\) with \(p\mid U_d\). Standard Lucas theory gives

\[
p\mid U_m\iff\rho(p)\mid m,
\qquad
\rho(p)\mid p-\chi_p,
\qquad
\chi_p=\left(\frac Dp\right)\in\{\pm1\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The second relation is explicitly recalled in Sanna's work on the index of appearance:

\[
p\equiv\left(\frac Dp\right)\pmod{\rho(p)}.
\]

Define

\[
H_U(n)=\#\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid U_{n-p}\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### 5.2 Exact diagonal collapse

For every good prime,

\[
\begin{aligned}
p\mid U_{n-p}
&\iff \rho(p)\mid n-p\\
&\iff \rho(p)\mid(n-p)+(p-\chi_p)\\
&\iff \rho(p)\mid n-\chi_p\\
&\iff p\mid U_{n-\chi_p}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
H_U(n)
=
\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm1}
\#\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
p\in(n/2,n],\ p\nmid2QD,\\
(D/p)=\varepsilon,\ p\mid U_{n-\varepsilon}
\end{array}
\right\}
+O_U(1).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is already a major distinction from Apéry: all hits divide one of the two fixed integers \(U_{n-1},U_{n+1}\).

### 5.3 Divisor splitting

Choose \(c_U>0\) so that

\[
\log\max(1,|U_d|)\le c_Ud
\qquad(d\ge1).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Such a constant exists by the Binet formula or directly from the fixed recurrence.

Fix \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\), put \(m=n-\varepsilon\), and group the primes counted in (5.5) by

\[
d=\rho(p)\mid m.
\]

For a fixed divisor \(d\mid m\), two independent bounds hold.

First, all such primes divide \(U_d\), and each is larger than \(n/2\), so

\[
N_{\varepsilon,d}
\le\frac{\log|U_d|}{\log(n/2)}
\le\frac{c_Ud}{L},
\qquad L=\log(n/2).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Second, \(d\mid p-\varepsilon\), so the primes lie in one progression modulo \(d\) inside an interval of length \(n/2\):

\[
N_{\varepsilon,d}
\le\frac{n}{2d}+1.
\tag{5.8}
\]

For any \(1\le y\le n\), split the divisors at \(y\). Since there are at most \(\tau(m)\) divisors in either range,

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_\varepsilon(n)
&\le
\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\d\le y}}
 \frac{c_Ud}{L}
+
\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\d>y}}
 \left(\frac{n}{2d}+1\right)\\
&\le
\tau(m)\left(
\frac{c_Uy}{L}+\frac{n}{2y}+1
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Take

\[
y=\sqrt{\frac{nL}{2c_U}}.
\]

After adjusting the constant for bounded \(n\), (5.9) gives

\[
N_\varepsilon(n)
\le
\tau(n-\varepsilon)
\left(
\sqrt{\frac{2c_Un}{L}}+1
\right).
\tag{5.10}
\]

Combining the signs proves:

> **Theorem (Lucas anti-sunflower).** For every fixed nondegenerate Lucas sequence (5.1),
> \[
> \boxed{
> H_U(n)
> \le O_U(1)
> +\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm1}
> \tau(n-\varepsilon)
> \left(
> \sqrt{\frac{2c_Un}{\log(n/2)}}+1
> \right).}
> \tag{5.11}
> \]
> Consequently
> \[
> H_U(n)=n^{1/2+o(1)}
> =o\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)
> \tag{5.12}
> \]
> uniformly in \(n\).

No GRH, primitive-divisor theorem, or distribution theorem for \(\rho(p)\) is used.

### 5.4 Fibonacci specialization

For Fibonacci numbers \(F_d\), \(D=5\) and

\[
\rho_F(p)\mid p-\left(\frac5p\right)
\qquad(p\ne2,5).
\]

Also \(F_d\le\varphi^d\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Thus one can take \(c_U=\log\varphi\), obtaining

\[
\boxed{
H_F(n)
\le O(1)
+\bigl(\tau(n-1)+\tau(n+1)\bigr)
\left(
\sqrt{\frac{2n\log\varphi}{\log(n/2)}}+1
\right).}
\tag{5.13}
\]

The endpoint \(p=n\), when \(n\) itself is prime, is harmless: \(F_0=0\), so it contributes at most one and is already consistent with (5.4).

### 5.5 What precisely makes Lucas easy

The theorem uses four properties:

1. a rank \(\rho(p)\) with \(p\mid U_r\iff\rho(p)\mid r\);
2. a finite Frobenius-offset set, here \(\chi_p\in\{-1,+1\}\);
3. \(\rho(p)\mid p-\chi_p\);
4. exponential height \(\log|U_d|\ll d\).

The finite offset is decisive. For an elliptic divisibility sequence one typically has a rank dividing \(\#E(\mathbf F_p)=p+1-a_p\); the offset \(1-a_p\) ranges over \(O(\sqrt p)\) values, so the two-carrier collapse is lost. The Apéry zero condition currently has no rank law at all.

## 6. Candidate mechanisms, ranked with one missing lemma each

The scores below mean “chance of producing new progress from the structures already in this repository,” not confidence in the truth of the final Apéry conjecture.

### Rank 1 — Franel/Mellin bilinearization followed by dispersion

**Why first.** Dispersion is the only mature analytic method in this survey that has repeatedly beaten individual CRT undersampling. The Franel square is the only current Apéry identity with a genuine convolutional shape.

Define the centered row function

\[
g_p(r)=1_{Z_p}(r)-\frac{|Z_p|}{p},
\qquad r\in\mathbf F_p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The first dispatch should seek the following.

> **Missing Lemma FD (Franel Type-II lemma).** There are absolute \(J,C\) and a decomposition, derived functorially from (3.6), of the additive-character expansion of \(g_p\) into at most \(J\) bilinear kernels of conductor at most \(C\), uniformly in \(p\), such that the associated variable-modulus Type-II forms satisfy a power-saving dispersion estimate throughout \(MN\asymp X\):
> \[
> \sum_{p\asymp X}\gamma_p
> \sum_{m\asymp M}\alpha_m
> \sum_{\ell\asymp N}\beta_\ell
> K_{p,j}(m,\ell)
> \ll
> X^{1-\delta}(MN)^{1/2}
> \tag{6.2}
> \]
> for some fixed \(\delta>0\), all \(|\alpha_m|,|\beta_\ell|,|\gamma_p|\le1\), and all \(j\le J\).

A successful FD lemma should imply at least the centered pair estimate

\[
\sum_{p\ne q}
\sum_r g_p(r)g_q(r+p-q)\,W\!\left(\frac{p+r}{X}\right)
\ll X(\log X)^{-2-\eta}
\tag{6.3}
\]

for a smooth compactly supported \(W\). Higher moments require multilinear descendants of (6.2), but \(k=2\) is the correct first falsification test.

**Failure terminal.** Prove that every exact representation of the coefficient-zero indicator obtained from the Franel square has conductor/rank \(\gg p^c\), or exhibit a self-characteristic diagonal correlation of size \(p\) that survives all candidate kernels. That would close this route honestly.

### Rank 2 — a genuinely new low-height global carrier

**Why second.** It proves the pointwise theorem immediately and does not need growing moments. But the repository has already shown that a long list of natural carriers have linear height or merely re-encode \(\gcd(P_n,b_n)\).

> **Missing Lemma C (subexponential carrier).** Construct explicit integers \(C_n\ne0\) such that
> \[
> p\mid b_{n-p},\quad n/2<p\le n
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> p\mid C_n,
> \tag{6.4}
> \]
> and
> \[
> \log|C_n|=o(n).
> \tag{6.5}
> \]

A fixed finite family \(C_{n,1},\ldots,C_{n,J}\) is equivalent after taking their product.

**Project-specific warning.** The index-jet/Dwork-crystal tower is vertical at one \(p\). Its \(p^3\) suppression and regulator extension class do not by themselves create an integer of small Archimedean height divisible by many different \(p\). Any new carrier proposal should include its height ledger before further local algebra is developed.

### Rank 3 — finite-offset apparition surrogate

**Why third.** The Lucas proof shows that this is exactly the cheapest deterministic anti-center theorem. It is also likely incompatible with the measured Poisson-like geometry of the Apéry \(Z_p\), so it should be tested and killed quickly if false.

> **Missing Lemma A (finite-offset apparition certificates).** There exist a fixed finite set \(E\subset\mathbf Z\), a fixed multiplicity \(J\), and integers \(C_d\ne0\) with \(\log|C_d|\ll d\), such that for every prime \(p\), every \(r<p\) with \(p\mid b_r\), one can choose \(j\le J\), \(\varepsilon\in E\), and \(d\ge1\) satisfying
> \[
> d\mid r,
> \qquad d\mid p-\varepsilon,
> \qquad p\mid C_d.
> \tag{6.6}
> \]

Then a hit at \(r=n-p\) gives \(d\mid n-\varepsilon\), and the proof of (5.11), summed over \(E\) and \(j\), yields

\[
H(n)\ll X^{1/2+o(1)}.
\]

This lemma is weaker than demanding that every \(Z_p\) be one progression; each hit may choose its own certificate. It is therefore the right formulation to falsify against data.

### Rank 4 — horizontal self-characteristic local limit

**Why fourth.** It is the conceptually exact motivic theorem but asks for new local-limit technology rather than an application of existing Sato--Tate.

> **Missing Lemma HL (horizontal Kummer local limit).** For the rank-two Mellin/Kummer realization \(\mathcal M_p(\chi)\) of the Apéry coefficient family, there is a fixed \(\delta>0\) such that, uniformly for \(X<n\le2X\),
> \[
> \#\left\{
> p\in(n/2,n]:
> \mathcal M_p(\omega_p^{\,n-1})\equiv0\pmod p
> \right\}
> \ll X^{1-\delta}.
> \tag{6.7}
> \]

Even \(X/\log^{1+\eta}X\) would suffice. A Poisson-strength theorem would predict vastly less.

The proof would have to mix three aspects simultaneously: primes varying, Kummer character varying with \(n\), and exact reduction modulo the same prime. Neither vertical Katz equidistribution nor fixed-modulus trace-function bounds supplies this.

### Rank 5 — high-order cumulant/tree expansion

**Why fifth.** This is an excellent organizational endpoint if pair/multilinear dispersion is obtained, but it is circular as an independent idea.

The exact missing lemma is the weighted tree bound (2.3)--(2.4) for some \(K(X)\to\infty\). Under it, weighted dependency-graph theory and the moment--cumulant formula give

\[
M_k\le X C_2^k(k!)^A
\qquad(k\le K(X)),
\tag{6.8}
\]

which combines with the project's deterministic moment endpoint to give \(\max H=X^{o(1)}\).

A size-biased Stein version is equivalent: construct the conditional coupling and prove (2.2) and its higher-order analogues with summable bounds.

### Rank 6 — larger/inverse sieve after center amplification

**Why last.** A single center is invisible to every set-size sieve.

> **Missing Lemma LS (sunflower amplification).** There are functions \(L(h)\to\infty\) fast enough for the larger sieve, and \(Q(h)\), such that if \(H(n_0)\ge h\), one can construct a set \(\mathcal N\subset(X,2X]\) with
> \[
> |\mathcal N|\ge L(h),
> \tag{6.9}
> \]
> while, for at least \(Q(h)\) of the hit primes \(p\), the reduction \(\mathcal N\bmod p\) occupies \(O(1)\) classes contained in translates of \(Z_p\).

Then Gallagher/weighted-entropy inverse-sieve machinery may contradict the existence of \(\mathcal N\). No recurrence, reflection, or Franel identity presently propagates one center to such a set.

## 7. Recommended dispatch order

### Dispatch A — try to build or kill FD at \(k=2\)

Start from the exact identity

\[
1_{b_r=0}=p^{-1}\sum_he_p(hb_r)
\]

and the Franel pullback. Search for a transform in which coefficient extraction becomes a product of two sums with each variable of length about \(p^{1/2}\). For each proposed kernel, compute:

1. geometric rank/conductor as a function of \(p\);
2. complete self-correlation under \((m,\ell)\mapsto(m',\ell')\);
3. whether defining-characteristic diagonal phases produce a main term of size \(p\);
4. the numerical off-diagonal dispersion sum (6.3), separated by \(p-q\) dyadic scale.

Stop if rank/conductor grows linearly or a forced diagonal survives.

### Dispatch B — falsify the apparition surrogate cheaply

For every stored hit \((p,r)\), enumerate

\[
\{d:d\mid r,\ d\mid p-\varepsilon\},
\qquad \varepsilon\in E
\]

for small candidate sets \(E\), beginning with \(\{\pm1\}\), \(\{0,\pm1\}\), and the cusp-width/Atkin--Lehner offsets suggested by the level-six model. Test whether one fixed sequence of candidate carriers \(C_d\) catches every hit. Natural first carriers are the primitive parts of \(b_d\), the companion solution, and the symmetric-square elliptic division polynomials supplied by the modular parametrization.

One uncaught hit kills a proposed finite-offset law. A positive pattern across large \(p\) would be major structure.

### Dispatch C — demand the height ledger first for every carrier

Any new crystal, determinant, cutoff, or resultant carrier should be required to produce simultaneously:

```text
support implication: every top hit p divides C_n;
nonvanishing: C_n != 0;
height: log |C_n| = o(n).
```

A carrier with \(\log|C_n|=cn+o(n)\) gives only a constant fraction of \(n/\log n\), regardless of how elegant its local construction is.

### Dispatch D — bank HL as the honest long-range theorem

Formulate the Mellin/Kummer sheaf and its integral Frobenius trace precisely enough that (6.7) is a literal theorem statement. Then separate:

- vertical complex/\(\ell\)-adic equidistribution;
- a \(p\)-adic local limit for one residue class;
- horizontal variation of \(\omega_p^{n-1}\);
- uniformity in \(n\).

This prevents another Sato--Tate-to-exact-zero overclaim while leaving a clean target for future technology.

## 8. Machine-verification checklist

The following explicit statements are suitable for immediate independent checking.

### 8.1 Pair-line identity

For all stored rows and prime pairs, verify

```text
|A_p intersect A_q|
 = #{r in Z_p : r+p-q in Z_q and p+r in I}.
```

This also audits every endpoint convention.

### 8.2 Central-binomial negative example

For primes \(p\in(n/2,n]\), put \(r=n-p\) and verify

```text
p | binom(2r,r)  <=>  2r >= p  <=>  p <= 2n/3.
```

No large integers are needed; Kummer carry counting suffices.

### 8.3 Lucas exact collapse

For a chosen Lucas sequence and every good prime in the scan:

```text
rho = least d>0 with U_d == 0 mod p
chi = Legendre(D,p)
assert (p-chi) % rho == 0
assert (U_(n-p) == 0 mod p) == ((n-chi) % rho == 0)
assert (U_(n-p) == 0 mod p) == (U_(n-chi) == 0 mod p)
```

Then group hits by \((\chi,\rho)\) and verify both bounds (5.7) and (5.8) separately before checking (5.11).

### 8.4 Fibonacci explicit bound

Use fast doubling for \(F_m\), compute \(\rho_F(p)\), and compare the actual \(H_F(n)\) to the right side of (5.13). The theorem is intentionally loose; the purpose is to audit every sign and endpoint.

### 8.5 Character-index shift

For every hit, verify both congruences

```text
r == n       (mod p)
r == n - 1   (mod p-1)
```

before passing between additive-index and Mellin-character formulas.

## 9. Final answers to the three questions

### Question 1

Cumulant and Poisson-approximation frameworks have worked across primes when a sieve/Kubilius model already controls joint divisibility. Cluster expansions, weighted dependency graphs, and Stein couplings do not independently solve the \(pq\asymp X^2\) undersampling problem. Linnik dispersion is the genuine precedent for beating that barrier, but only after a common factorable kernel is exposed. For Apéry, no such cross-prime estimate is currently known, even at \(k=2\).

### Question 2

The resonance line identifies the additive index with \(n\bmod p\) and the Mellin character with \(\omega_p^{n-1}\). The K3/Mellin description is real and valuable, but exact zero modulo the residue characteristic is a horizontal local-limit event, not a consequence of vertical Sato--Tate. The Franel square is a coefficient/convolution identity, not yet a fixed bounded-conductor evaluation formula. No general theorem says that one integer cannot land in sparse motive-defined sets \(Z_p\) for many top primes. A fixed polynomial carrier or finite-offset rank law would do so; a motive without such extra structure does not.

### Question 3

The Lucas calibration has an unconditional answer:

\[
\max_n\#\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ \rho(p)\mid n-p\}
\ll_U n^{1/2+o(1)}.
\]

The proof is (5.4)--(5.11). This is substantially stronger than the desired \(o(n/\log n)\). Conversely, central binomial coefficients give \(\sim n/(6\log n)\) aligned primes, proving that even a very concrete hypergeometric sequence need not have any anti-sunflower property.

The Lucas theorem therefore calibrates the Apéry gap sharply: what is missing is not a stronger row-size theorem, but an analogue of the finite-offset Frobenius rank relation

\[
\rho(p)\mid p-\chi_p.
\]

Without an apparition law, a subexponential carrier, or a new dispersion/local-limit theorem, the sunflower center remains genuinely unconstrained.
