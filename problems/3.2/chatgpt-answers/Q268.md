# 8. Best assessment and the precise next theorem

## Current status

- Unweighted fixed-frequency horizontal cancellation: open.

- Arbitrary-weight horizontal cancellation: false as stated.

- Pointwise (K_X(n)ll X^{1-delta}): open.

- Average-over-(n) power saving: available from the ordinary large sieve plus the project’s vertical zero bound.

- DFI/FKM applicability: conditional on a new fixed-complexity realization of the zero detector.

## Closest tool

- For the sum (sum e(cz_p/p)), DFI’s quadratic-root theory is the closest conceptual analogue.

- For a hypothetical bounded-conductor realization, FKM/FKM–Sawin trace-function technology is the closest general analytic engine.

- For the actual fixed-(n) incidence problem, the most faithful target is a cross-prime dispersion or small-CRT theorem, not one-point equidistribution alone.

## Exact bottleneck

One needs at least one of the following genuinely new inputs.

1. Fixed algebraic correspondence. Construct a bounded-degree (F(R,P)inmathbf Z[R,P]), or a fixed arithmetic variety/compatible system, whose mod-(p) points encode (Rin Z_p).

1. Bounded-conductor zero detector. Express

1. Direct horizontal dispersion. Prove a two-parameter estimate for

1. Higher CRT non-clustering. Prove a third/fourth factorial-moment bound for the simultaneous small CRT representatives of the sets (Z_p).

At present none is known. The first two would unlock existing analytic machinery; the last two would solve the needed part directly.

Bottom line: this is genuinely open with current technology. It is not blocked by the lack of a marginal exponential-sum theorem of known type; it is blocked by the absence of a fixed-complexity arithmetic description of the moving Apéry zero positions.
