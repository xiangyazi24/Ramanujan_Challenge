## 11.5 Why current determinant identities do not prove it

A Hadamard bound gives only exp(O(n^2)) for balanced determinants. Known integrality and divisibility results for Apéry Hankel determinants go in the wrong direction: they prove large systematic factors, not small archimedean size.

For example, Zhu and Sun prove integrality/divisibility statements for Hankel-type determinants of Apéry numbers; these do not provide a sublinear logarithmic upper bound for the normalized determinants. See Zhu–Sun, “Hankel-type determinants for some combinatorial sequences”.

# 13. Final assessment

The requested direct algebraic attack produces a real hierarchy.

1. Exact but too tall: the Lagrange/central-binomial certificate A_n-I_n.

1. Provably hopeless: direct Gosper antidifferences, rational multipliers, fixed gamma quotients, bounded contiguous relations, and fixed-order Pell corrections.

1. Circular: generic rational approximation with smooth denominators.

1. Still live and sharply formulated: balanced Newton–Padé determinants D_{a,b}(n).

So the next worthwhile symbolic computation is not another fixed contiguous identity. It is:

```plain text
Compute and factor D_{a,b}(n) for balanced a,b;
remove all factors supported on primes <= floor(n/2);
measure the remaining logarithmic height;
then seek a Cauchy–Binet / multi-hypergeometric evaluation.
```

A subexponential normalized determinant would be the desired breakthrough. If the normalized determinants instead have a positive linear or quadratic logarithmic growth rate, that would rigorously close this entire natural certificate class as well.
