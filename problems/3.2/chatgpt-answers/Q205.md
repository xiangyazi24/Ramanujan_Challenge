# 3. Audit of the existing “Deligne + large sieve” theorems

## 3.1 Deligne and Katz: one finite field, geometric parameter or character variation

For a fixed good prime p, Deligne’s Weil II bounds complete sums of fixed-complexity trace functions over \mathbf F_p and their fixed tensor products. Katz’s convolution formalism studies Mellin transforms as multiplicative characters of a fixed finite field—or of its extensions—vary.

These theorems can control:

- parameter-side Frobenius trace moments;

- normalized complex Mellin traces;

- fixed tensor correlations when the relevant monodromy has no invariants.

They do not control the predicate

because that predicate is not a fixed continuous class function of the compact monodromy group and its algebraic complexity grows with p.

## 3.3 Kowalski’s large sieve for Frobenius

Kowalski’s The large sieve, monodromy and zeta functions of curves proves a large-sieve theorem for the average distribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes in arithmetic monodromy groups over finite fields. His broader The principle of the large sieve develops an abstract Frobenius-sieve framework.

Those results require a fixed algebraic family or compatible collection of representations, with local conjugacy conditions of bounded complexity. They are powerful for statements such as “most fibers in a parameter family have generic Frobenius polynomial.”

# Direct answers

## (1) Is there an unconditional AP, short-interval, or discrepancy theorem for z_p?

No known theorem. Chebotarev and Sato–Tate control fixed compatible-system Frobenius data or parameter-side trace functions. The Apéry zero positions are residual zero Mellin coefficients of a degree-growing Hasse polynomial. No power-saving discrepancy for z/p, no AP distribution, and no short-interval theorem is presently available.

## (2) Does a Deligne-plus-large-sieve theorem give the requested averaged second or third moment?

No. Existing theorems require a bounded-conductor detector in a fixed finite-field or fixed-modulus setup. No such detector is known for Z_p across varying p. Moreover, the proposed squared moment is blind to singleton locations, and same-prime Fourier energies do not imply the cross-prime third factorial moment.

## (3) Is the linear sum over p and zero positions amenable to Katz/Deligne?

It is the right analytic observable, but no current Katz/Deligne theorem applies. The field, detector, and coefficient index family all vary with p, and the zero condition is p-adic divisibility rather than a bounded-complexity complex trace function.

## (4) Does fixed-index regularity extend?

For a fixed integer index, the prime set is finite, not Chebotarev-distributed. For an index proportional to p, the term itself changes with p. Fixed-order character subsequences can have a geometric interpretation, but even the central order-two case reduces to the open nonordinary-prime problem for a fixed weight-4 form. It does not yield the needed family-wide equidistribution.

# Bottom line

The averaging-over-p route has not been ruled out, but the missing engine is now precise:

One needs a horizontal theorem for same-characteristic residual Mellin zeros, together with a cross-modulus higher-correlation estimate. Neither ordinary Sato–Tate, Weil II, Katz convolution, the Frobenius large sieve, nor Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel supplies that theorem from the presently known Apéry geometry.

The most immediate correction to the research program is to stop targeting

as a location-equidistribution statistic and target the linear Weyl sums W_X(h) and the explicit three-prime CRT correlation directly.
