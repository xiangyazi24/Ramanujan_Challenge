# Growing Padé zero absorbers: exact bordered-minor ledger and a universal Smith-data no-go

## Verdict

At the latest main commit c962a70e12349babf291b9a6e3f88ef2ebc468a8 (“Prove Padé zero-absorber support lemma”), the local theorem in working-note Sections 159–160 and q32_pade_zero_absorber.py is correct. It removes the positive-density multi-zero pollution of the single a=1 numerator: after taking every numerator degree 0<=a<=A with A>=C H^(2/3), the candidate-window support is exactly the direct q=1 target.

It does not imply an Archimedean saving. I do not obtain

$$
\log \operatorname{rad}_{2H<p\le 3H+1}G_{H,A}(3H+1)=o(H).
$$

What can be proved is a sharp negative theorem and an exact reduction.

1. Every primitive Padé value is a bordered maximal minor divided by the last determinantal divisor. Hence all primitive-normalization and large-prime valuations can be written exactly, without choosing a kernel vector.

1. Smith ideals, Fitting ideals, compound matrices, subresultant chains, Euclidean combinations across a, and geometry-of-numbers arguments that use only the universal interpolation module, its ranks, degrees, nesting, and local zero-set codimensions cannot force an o(H) bound. A CRT construction realizes the entire prime interval (2H,3H+1] as common support while satisfying the strongest local zero-absorber pattern: every relevant prime has exactly one zero node.

1. Consequently the whole-family codimension is a purification mechanism, not a height mechanism. Any proof for the actual Apéry numbers must use arithmetic information that fails in the CRT model.

1. The smallest exact remaining statement is the Apéry bordered-minor excess-content lemma in Theorem 5.1 below. It involves only the canonical interpolation matrices, their last determinantal divisors, and one bordered determinant per degree. It is strictly smaller in algebraic data than estimating the Padé polynomials or their coefficients, but it is still a genuinely new horizontal Apéry theorem; no universal Smith/Fitting inequality proves it.

Thus the valid advance is a rigorous no-go: the new local theorem closes support pollution but cannot, even with a growing family and all universal determinantal algebra, yield the desired global o(H) estimate.

# 1. Canonical interpolation matrices

Fix an integer sequence

$$
\mathbf u=(u_0,\ldots,u_H)\in\mathbf Z^{H+1}.
$$

The Apéry case is u_s=A_s. For 0<=a<=H, put b=H-a and form the integer matrix

$$
\mathcal C_{H,a}(\mathbf u)
=
\left[
\binom{s}{0},\ldots,\binom{s}{a},
-u_s\binom{s}{0},\ldots,-u_s\binom{s}{b}
\right]_{0\le s\le H}.
\tag{1.1}
$$

It has H+1 rows and H+2 columns. A vector

$$
v=(p_0,\ldots,p_a,q_0,\ldots,q_b)^T
\tag{1.2}
$$

lies in its kernel precisely when

$$
P(s)=u_sQ(s),\qquad 0\le s\le H,
\tag{1.3}
$$

where

$$
P(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{a}p_r\binom xr,
\qquad
Q(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{b}q_r\binom xr.
\tag{1.4}
$$

Assume rank C_{H,a}=H+1, as in the normal Padé case. For each column j, let

$$
\Delta_{H,a,j}=(-1)^j
\det\bigl(\mathcal C_{H,a}\text{ with column }j\text{ deleted}\bigr).
\tag{1.5}
$$

Define the last determinantal divisor

$$
\delta_{H,a}:=\gcd_{0\le j\le H+1}\Delta_{H,a,j}>0.
\tag{1.6}
$$

Then

$$
\frac1{\delta_{H,a}}
(\Delta_{H,a,0},\ldots,\Delta_{H,a,H+1})
\tag{1.7}
$$

is, up to sign, the primitive integral kernel vector. This is the exact primitive normalization; no further gcd cancellation is hidden.

# 2. Bordered determinant formula for the evaluated numerator

For an integer n, define the numerator-evaluation row

$$
\ell_{a,n}
=
\left(
\binom n0,\ldots,\binom na,
0,\ldots,0
\right)
\in\mathbf Z^{H+2}.
\tag{2.1}
$$

Let

$$
\mathcal B_{H,a}(\mathbf u;n)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal C_{H,a}(\mathbf u)\\
\ell_{a,n}
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{2.2}
$$

an (H+2) x (H+2) integer matrix, and put

$$
E_{H,a}(\mathbf u;n)
:=\det \mathcal B_{H,a}(\mathbf u;n).
\tag{2.3}
$$

Expanding along the final row gives

$$
E_{H,a}(\mathbf u;n)
=\pm\sum_{r=0}^{a}\binom nr\Delta_{H,a,r}.
\tag{2.4}
$$

Therefore, for the primitive pair,

$$
\boxed{
P_{H,a}^{\rm prim}(n)
=\pm\frac{E_{H,a}(\mathbf u;n)}{\delta_{H,a}}.
}
\tag{2.5}
$$

In particular, for every prime p,

$$
\boxed{
v_p(P_{H,a}^{\rm prim}(n))
=v_p(E_{H,a}(\mathbf u;n))-v_p(\delta_{H,a}).
}
\tag{2.6}
$$

The right side is nonnegative because delta_{H,a} divides every integral linear combination of the maximal minors, including (2.4).

This is the complete large-prime valuation ledger requested in the question. It also shows exactly what compound matrices and Fitting ideals see:

- delta_{H,a} is the generator of the maximal-minor Fitting ideal of coker C_{H,a};

- E_{H,a} is the bordered maximal minor obtained by imposing numerator evaluation at n;

- a prime survives primitive normalization exactly when the bordered determinant has strictly larger valuation than the unbordered maximal-minor content.

For

$$
G_{H,A}(n)=\gcd_{0\le a\le A}P_{H,a}^{\rm prim}(n),
\tag{2.7}
$$

we have the exact identity

$$
\boxed{
v_p(G_{H,A}(n))
=
\min_{0\le a\le A}
\left(
 v_p(E_{H,a}(\mathbf u;n))-v_p(\delta_{H,a})
\right).
}
\tag{2.8}
$$

No adjacent identity is used.

# 3. Recovery of the zero-absorber theorem from the determinant ledger

Take n=3H+1 and a prime p=n-j>2H. Since n=j+p, for every r<=H<p,

$$
\binom nr\equiv\binom jr\pmod p.
\tag{3.1}
$$

Thus every Newton polynomial of degree at most H satisfies

$$
F(n)\equiv F(j)\pmod p.
\tag{3.2}
$$

For the Apéry nodes, let

$$
Z_p(H)=\{s\in\{0,\ldots,H\}:p\mid A_s\},
\qquad z_p(H)=|Z_p(H)|.
\tag{3.3}
$$

The proved local lemma says

$$
P_{H,a}\equiv0\text{ in }\mathbf F_p[x]
\Longleftrightarrow z_p(H)>a,
\tag{3.4}
$$

and, if a=z_p(H), the interpolation-node roots of the nonzero reduced numerator are exactly Z_p(H). Consequently

$$
p\mid G_{H,A}(n)
\Longleftrightarrow
j\in Z_p(H)\text{ or }z_p(H)>A.
\tag{3.5}
$$

If the vertical theorem gives

$$
z_p(H)\le C_0p^{2/3},
\tag{3.6}
$$

then, for example,

$$
A_H=\left\lceil C_0(3H+1)^{2/3}\right\rceil
\tag{3.7}
$$

removes the second alternative. The candidate-window support is then exactly

$$
\{p=3H+1-j>2H:p\mid A_j\}.
\tag{3.8}
$$

This is an exact support theorem. It gives no bound on the number or logarithmic mass of the primes in (3.8).

# 4. Universal interpolation/Smith data cannot give o(H)

## Theorem 4.1 — CRT saturation countermodel

For every H>=1, put n=3H+1 and let

$$
\mathcal P_H=\{p\text{ prime}:2H<p\le3H+1\}.
\tag{4.1}
$$

There exists an integer node vector

$$
\mathbf u^{(H)}=(u_0,\ldots,u_H)
\tag{4.2}
$$

such that for every p in P_H, writing j_p=n-p, one has

$$
Z_p^{\mathbf u}(H)
:=\{s\le H:p\mid u_s\}
=\{j_p\}.
\tag{4.3}
$$

For every normal primitive interpolation family attached to this node vector and every A>=0,

$$
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_H}p
\mid G_{H,A}^{\mathbf u}(n).
\tag{4.4}
$$

Hence

$$
\log\operatorname{rad}_{2H<p\le3H+1}G_{H,A}^{\mathbf u}(n)
\ge \vartheta(3H+1)-\vartheta(2H)
=H+o(H).
\tag{4.5}
$$

### Proof

For each node s, impose the simultaneous congruences

$$
u_s\equiv
\begin{cases}
0\pmod p,&s=j_p,\\
1\pmod p,&s\ne j_p,
\end{cases}
\qquad p\in\mathcal P_H.
\tag{4.6}
$$

The primes are distinct, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives one residue class for each u_s modulo

$$
M_H:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_H}p.
\tag{4.6a}
$$

Choose lifts u_s=u_s^{(0)}+M_Ht_s. For each a, failure of full row rank is the vanishing of all (H+1) x (H+1) minors of \mathcal C_{H,a}. At least one such minor is a nonzero polynomial in the independent variables t_0,\ldots,t_H: for example, outside the imposed residue classes a generic rational node vector has a one-dimensional interpolation kernel. Hence the bad lifts lie in a proper algebraic subset of \mathbf A^{H+1}. There are only H+1 degrees a, so an integral lift exists outside their finite union. We choose such a lift. It satisfies (4.3) and makes every interpolation matrix normal.

Fix p in P_H. Since j_p is a zero node, interpolation gives

$$
P_{H,a}(j_p)\equiv u_{j_p}Q_{H,a}(j_p)\equiv0\pmod p
\tag{4.7}
$$

for every a. By (3.2),

$$
P_{H,a}(n)\equiv P_{H,a}(j_p)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{4.8}
$$

Primitive normalization does not remove this vanishing: the primitive coefficient vector is nonzero modulo p, but its numerator evaluation still vanishes. Thus p divides every member of the family and therefore divides their gcd. Multiplying over p proves (4.4). The prime number theorem gives (4.5). ∎

## Consequences

The countermodel has the strongest possible local purification:

$$
z_p(H)=1
\quad\text{for every candidate prime }p.
\tag{4.9}
$$

Thus the obstruction is not the multi-zero alternative z_p(H)>A; it survives after that alternative has been completely eliminated.

Any theorem derived only from the following universal data is therefore false:

- dimensions and degree bounds of the Padé spaces;

- nesting as a varies;

- ranks of the interpolation matrices over Q or F_p;

- Fitting ideals and Smith forms without an Apéry-specific height estimate;

- compound-matrix identities and Plücker relations;

- subresultant or Euclidean identities valid for every node vector;

- the local implication “one prescribed root imposes one codimension”;

- geometry-of-numbers estimates depending only on the number A+1 of certificates and generic coefficient-height upper bounds.

Indeed all those structures are present in the CRT model, while the candidate-window logarithmic radical is linear in H.

This also explains why a dimension count cannot be converted into an Archimedean gain. The A+1 local conditions live in different residue fields and at different moving nodes. CRT stores the whole prime product in the node data. Codimension over each F_p does not add to a small-height relation over Z.

# 5. The exact Apéry-specific determinantal-divisor lemma

Return now to u_s=A_s and choose A_H as in (3.7).

## Theorem 5.1 — minimal bordered-minor excess-content target

The desired direct q=1 estimate follows from, and in fact is exactly equivalent to,

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{2H<p\le3H+1}
\mathbf 1\!\left[
\min_{0\le a\le A_H}
\bigl(
 v_p(E_{H,a}(A;3H+1))-v_p(\delta_{H,a}(A))
\bigr)\ge1
\right]
\log p=o(H).
}
\tag{5.1}
$$

Here

$$
E_{H,a}(A;3H+1)
=\det\mathcal B_{H,a}\bigl((A_0,\ldots,A_H);3H+1\bigr),
\tag{5.2}
$$

and delta_{H,a}(A) is the gcd of the maximal minors of the unbordered Apéry interpolation matrix (1.1).

Because of the zero-absorber theorem and the vertical bound, the indicator in (5.1) is also exactly

$$
\mathbf1[p=3H+1-j,\ p\mid A_j].
\tag{5.3}
$$

The point of (5.1) is not a relabeling of the support theorem. It removes all Padé-polynomial choices, coefficient growth, projective normalization, and adjacent identities. The remaining object is one precise excess of a bordered maximal minor over the last determinantal divisor, simultaneously across A_H+1 nested presentations.

No smaller universal determinantal statement is available: Theorem 4.1 shows that (5.1) fails maximally for general integer node vectors even when every local zero set has cardinality one. Therefore a proof must exploit a property special to the canonical Apéry vector, such as its recurrence, diagonal representation, modular geometry, or cross-prime arithmetic.

# 6. Why the proposed whole-family mechanisms do not close (5.1)

## 6.1 Smith and Fitting ideals

For each a, the Smith data give exactly

$$
v_p(P_{H,a}^{\rm prim}(n))
=v_p(E_{H,a})-v_p(\delta_{H,a}).
\tag{6.1}
$$

They do not bound how often this difference is positive. Taking the sum, product, intersection, or Fitting ideal across a merely packages the minimum in (2.8). The CRT model makes that minimum positive for every prime in the full candidate interval.

## 6.2 Compound matrices

Exterior powers turn maximal minors into coordinates of a compound vector. A common prime divisor means that each bordered compound coordinate lies in the corresponding unbordered determinantal ideal. Exterior degree multiplies both selective valuation and determinant height. Without an Apéry-specific cancellation estimate, there is no gain growing with A.

## 6.3 Subresultants and Euclidean chains

For each candidate prime, every numerator has the moving root j=n-p; hence every universal resultant or subresultant identity that detects a common root acquires at least one factor p. The CRT construction makes this happen for every candidate prime. Removing universal node factors leaves precisely the excess-content condition (5.1), not a bounded residual integer.

## 6.4 Integer combinations across a

For arbitrary integers c_a,

$$
G_{H,A}(n)\mid\sum_{a=0}^{A}c_aP_{H,a}(n).
\tag{6.2}
$$

A useful argument would need a nonzero combination satisfying

$$
\log\left|\sum_{a=0}^{A}c_aP_{H,a}(n)\right|=o(H).
\tag{6.3}
$$

The dimension A+1 alone does not produce such a combination. In the CRT model every nonzero combination remains divisible by the full candidate primorial unless it vanishes identically, so its absolute value is at least exp(H+o(H)). Any successful Euclidean combination must therefore use special Apéry coefficient relations, not only the number of available numerators.

## 6.5 Geometry of numbers

Pigeonhole or Minkowski can make a linear combination smaller relative to a crude maximum coefficient height, but it cannot cross the lattice covolume forced by (6.2). In the CRT model the image lattice is contained in

$$
\left(\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_H}p\right)\mathbf Z,
\tag{6.4}
$$

so its first nonzero point already has logarithm H+o(H). The growing dimension does not reduce this one-dimensional image covolume.

# 7. Exact primitive-normalization and valuation summary

For the actual Apéry matrices, all normalization questions reduce to the following identities:

$$
\delta_{H,a}
=\gcd_j\Delta_{H,a,j},
\tag{7.1}
$$

$$
(P_{H,a}^{\rm prim},Q_{H,a}^{\rm prim})
=\pm\frac{1}{\delta_{H,a}}
(\Delta_{H,a,0},\ldots,\Delta_{H,a,H+1}),
\tag{7.2}
$$

$$
P_{H,a}^{\rm prim}(3H+1)
=\pm\frac{E_{H,a}(A;3H+1)}{\delta_{H,a}},
\tag{7.3}
$$

and, for p>2H,

$$
p\mid G_{H,A_H}(3H+1)
\Longleftrightarrow
\min_{a\le A_H}
\{v_p(E_{H,a})-v_p(\delta_{H,a})\}\ge1.
\tag{7.4}
$$

The primitive divisor delta_{H,a} must be subtracted degree by degree. Replacing it by a raw determinant, a common upper bound, or an adjacent Smith factor can introduce false prime support. Conversely, once the subtraction is made, there is no additional primitive cancellation.

The local theorem identifies the sign of the excess in (7.4), but not its global frequency. The missing estimate is horizontal across changing primes.

# 8. Strongest correct conclusion

The growing zero-absorber family is a real structural advance: it gives an exact integral family whose common candidate support is the desired direct q=1 set, with no multi-zero pollution once A reaches the proved O(H^(2/3)) vertical threshold.

However, the family does not by itself compress that support into a subexponential integer. Theorem 4.1 proves that no argument based only on universal interpolation, Smith/Fitting, compound, subresultant, Euclidean, or geometry-of-numbers structure can do so. Such structure is compatible with a full prime interval of common support and logarithmic mass H+o(H).

The route can be reopened only by proving an Apéry-specific version of (5.1), or by constructing an explicit nonzero whole-family combination of height exp(o(H)). Either result would be genuinely new horizontal arithmetic. The local codimension theorem alone cannot supply it.
