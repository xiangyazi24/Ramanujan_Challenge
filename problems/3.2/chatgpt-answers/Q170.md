# Executive verdict

The geometric idea buys something real, but it does not currently dissolve the Apéry zero barrier. There are three different averaging problems, and the proposed breakthrough conflates them:

1. Geometric-parameter averaging: vary the K3 parameter (tin U(mathbf F_p)). The rank-three Apéry local system is lisse, pure, geometrically irreducible, and has Zariski-dense orthogonal monodromy. Deligne therefore gives square-root cancellation for fixed-complexity trace functions and their fixed tensor correlations. This part is unconditional.

1. Character/Mellin averaging: vary a multiplicative character ρ of (mathbf F_p^times) and study the Mellin transforms of the parameter-side trace function. Katz’s convolution formalism is designed for this, and it gives complex/ℓ-adic equidistribution once its Tannakian hypotheses are verified. For the raw Apéry Mellin transforms this is a credible, largely geometric program, although I did not find a published Apéry-specific computation of the convolution monodromy group.

1. Residual coefficient zeros: ask whether that Mellin transform is zero modulo the same characteristic (p). This is the event (pmid b_r). It is a (p)-adic divisibility/Newton-slope event, not a fixed continuous class function of the complex Frobenius conjugacy class. Neither Weil II nor Katz’s complex equidistribution controls it.

The sharp conclusion is:

The full rank-three sheaf restores Weil bounds for a pure lift of the Apéry coefficients and for fixed-complexity moments of that lift. It does not presently give an unconditional bound for (|Z_p|), (S_p(h)), residual pair/triple correlations, or the cross-prime (A_3) statistic.

The obstruction is not merely that one carelessly threw away a pure summand. Reduction modulo (p) itself kills the positive-slope part and retains the Hasse/unit-root quotient. The arithmetic question (pmid b_r) is intrinsically about that reduction.

# 4. What Katz’s convolution theorem gives—and what it does not

Katz’s Convolution and Equidistribution studies precisely the finite-field Mellin transform

For the Apéry parameter-side trace function, this framework is genuinely relevant. One would take the middle extension of the normalized rank-three sheaf from (U) to (mathbf G_m), exclude its finite set of bad characters, and study the convolution Tannakian group.

## 4.1 Conditions that are known or plausible

- Lisse on (U): proved geometrically.

- Geometric irreducibility: follows from the rank-three monodromy.

- Purity: proved by Weil II.

- Bounded conductor: yes for the original parameter sheaf and for any fixed finite sequence of standard sheaf operations.

- Original geometric monodromy: (SO_3), proved by the modular/K3 identification.

## 4.2 Conditions that are not automatic

The group in Katz’s Mellin theorem is the convolution Tannakian group of the perverse object, not simply the original (SO_3) monodromy group on the parameter line. One must still determine:

1. the negligible/Kummer constituents;

1. the exact exceptional character set;

1. the convolution group (G_{rm geom,N}) and its standard representation;

1. any autoduality and component-group effects.

I did not find a published Apéry-specific calculation of this package. The modular symmetric-square description makes it plausible that it can be done, but it is not supplied merely by Peters’s theorem.

Nor did I find a published identification of the character-index function (rmapsto b_r) or (rmapsto1_{b_r=0}) with a standard Katz hypergeometric trace function of uniformly bounded conductor. The underlying K3 sheaf may be assembled by modular pullbacks and tensor operations, but that is a parameter-side statement. # 6. The unit-root barrier: real, but slightly misdiagnosed

For the K3 crystal, the generic Newton slopes of the transcendental rank-three piece are

The Hasse–Witt scalar detects the slope-zero line. Beukers–Vlasenko, Dwork Crystals I, Theorem 4.3 and Remark 4.4, make the mechanism explicit:

- when the Hasse–Witt matrix is invertible, the relevant quotient is free of the expected rank;

- Cartier/Frobenius is invertible on that quotient;

- it is divisible by (p) on the complementary formal-derivative submodule.

So modulo (p), the positive-slope complement disappears and the unit-root/Hasse quotient survives.

This yields two conclusions.

## 6.1 Yes, the earlier reduction discarded the pure full trace

At the level of complex/ℓ-adic statistics, retaining only the slope-zero quotient throws away information. The full rank-three trace has excellent Weil bounds and strong fixed-moment equidistribution. Reintroducing it is useful and gives (2.4)–(2.7).

## 6.2 No, that does not make the discarded part relevant to the exact zero event

The congruence (pmid b_r) is defined after reduction modulo (p). At that stage the positive-slope pieces vanish by construction. We are not voluntarily discarding data that the predicate still sees; the predicate itself factors through the slope-zero/Hasse quotient.

The full trace can replace the Hasse scalar modulo (p), but then one immediately reduces the full trace modulo (p), recreating the same projection. Its complex conjugacy class does not determine its (p)-adic divisibility.

This also explains why the phrase “the pure part where Weil lives” is misleading. Weil lives on the whole rank-three sheaf, which is pure of weight (2); the unit-root line is a (p)-adic slope piece of that same pure motive. The tension is between archimedean weight bounds and same-prime integral reduction, not between a pure and an impure motive.

# Direct answers

## (1) Does big monodromy give unconditional average equidistribution relevant to (|Z_p|) or (A_3)?

- Yes for the full Frobenius trace as the geometric parameter varies, and for every fixed tensor/product correlation whose pulled-back sheaf has no unexpected invariant constituent. These sums satisfy square-root cancellation per (p), hence also in a mixed average over (p).

- No known theorem converts this into a bound for (|Z_p|), residual pair/triple correlations, or (A_3). Casting (b_r) as a Mellin transform of the full trace produces a Weil-bounded lift, but (pmid b_r) remains a (p)-adic divisibility condition on that lift.

So the geometric recasting restores Weil-boundedness for the lifted average quantities, not for the exact zero indicators needed by dispersion.

## (2) Does Katz’s Mellin machinery solve (|Z_p|) or (S_p(h))?

- Katz applies naturally to the Mellin transforms of the raw Apéry parameter trace function once the perverse-sheaf and convolution-group hypotheses are verified.

- I found no published Apéry-specific computation showing that the character-index sequence (b_r), its phases (e_p(ab_r)), or its centered zero detector is a lisse, geometrically irreducible, bounded-conductor hypergeometric trace function.

- Even ideal complex equidistribution of the raw Mellin transforms does not count those transforms that vanish modulo (p).

- (S_p(h)) introduces an additive phase in the integer character label and is not the Mellin variable in Katz’s theorem.

Thus Katz supplies a powerful theorem for the wrong level of the transform unless a new residual/integral bridge is proved.

## (3) Is the unit-root barrier merely self-inflicted by throwing away the pure part?

Partly in representation, no in substance. The full sheaf contains much more information and gives unconditional Weil bounds that the rank-one unit-root quotient lacks. But reduction modulo (p) necessarily annihilates the positive slopes, and (b_rbmod p) is itself a Hasse/unit-root statistic. The full trace does not currently control a different quantity sufficient for (A_3); all known exact rewritings reduce it modulo (p) and return to the same barrier.

A genuine escape would require a new (p)-adic residual monodromy theorem, a bounded-conductor realization of the zero detector, or a structural anti-concentration theorem from the new square factorization. Ordinary full-sheaf Sato–Tate/Weil equidistribution alone is not enough.
