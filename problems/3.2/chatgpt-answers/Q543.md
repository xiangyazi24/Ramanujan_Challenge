## 7.5 Pisot numbers

A bounded-house sequence of Pisot numbers with degrees tending to infinity also has height tending to zero, so the same Bilu mechanism applies. Its conjugates cannot asymptotically concentrate near (-1/2); they equidistribute on the unit circle.

Pisot families with growing house, or coupled degree/height comparable to the complexity of (T_n), are not covered by this argument. They belong to the unresolved weighted-resultant problem (WR±).

A reproducible exact test for a proposed minimal polynomial (P) is:

```plain text
1. Construct T_n(c) exactly from the Q533 coefficient formula.
2. Compute R_n = resultant_c(P(c), T_n(c)) in ZZ.
3. Reject the center if R_n = 0.
4. Record log(abs(R_n))/(n*degree(P)).
5. Split n modulo 3 and guess a P-recursive recurrence for R_n only after
```

## Final assessment

The algebraic-center idea is not vacuous: the exact saddle really is much smaller near (-1/2), and the norm construction is arithmetically valid. But every standard way of manufacturing many conjugates—rational approximation, roots of unity, integer shifts, cyclotomic traces, bounded-house Pisot numbers, or Salem numbers—retains a positive exponential floor.

The unrestricted growing-degree case is not settled by classical capacity. It is exactly the moving weighted-resultant problem ((mathrm{WR}pm)). Solving that problem, rather than searching more small quadratic centers, is the next proof-grade step.
