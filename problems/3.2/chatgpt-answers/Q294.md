# 4. What Dwork crystals do give

The Beukers–Vlasenko framework starts with a fixed Laurent polynomial family and constructs a Cartier operator on a Dwork module. When the Hasse–Witt matrix is invertible modulo (p), the corresponding unit-root subcrystal exists and its Frobenius and connection matrices are obtained as limits of explicit coefficient or truncation matrices.

The relevant primary references are:

- F. Beukers and M. Vlasenko, Dwork crystals I: explicit Cartier operators and unit-root crystals for toric hypersurfaces;

- F. Beukers and M. Vlasenko, Dwork crystals II: congruences for truncated period functions;

- F. Beukers and M. Vlasenko, Dwork crystals III: excellent Frobenius lifts and supercongruences.

In Dwork crystals I, the Hasse–Witt matrix is constructed from coefficients of (f^{p-1}); invertibility defines the ordinary locus, and the Cartier operator descends to a finite-rank unit-root subquotient. # 6. Why generic ordinarity does not imply (|Z_p|ll(log p)^A)

Adolphson–Sperber generic invertibility proves that the Hasse–Witt matrix is nonzero on a Zariski-open set. In one parameter, this says the Hasse polynomial is not identically zero.

It gives no useful bound on the number of (mathbf F_p)-rational zeros beyond the degree bound.

A nonzero polynomial of degree (p-1) may vanish at (p-1) field elements. Moreover, in many genuine one-parameter families, the supersingular locus has cardinality proportional to (p). Thus a polylogarithmic root bound is not a generic ordinarity phenomenon; it would be a special anti-concentration theorem for this particular spectral polynomial.

The random-polynomial heuristic

is useful as a model, but it does not follow from a large geometric monodromy group alone. One must control the exact reduction-zero indicator, not only complex or (ell)-adic trace moments.

- prove that every linear factor corresponds to a special endomorphism or torsion condition in a fixed moduli problem.

If the last step succeeds, arithmetic geometry can replace random-polynomial heuristics.

## 12.2 Unit-root lifting

At ordinary points, Dwork ratios give the unit root modulo (p^s). Near a simple zero of (H_p), one can study how the slope filtration changes and whether the zero lifts uniquely.

## 12.3 Excellent Frobenius lifts

If the family admits an excellent Frobenius lift in the sense of Dwork crystals III, one may obtain supercongruences for the truncated values. These can strengthen the gap-polynomial and codegree machinery by controlling valuations, not only residues.

## 12.4 Fixed-fiber horizontal questions

For a fixed algebraic parameter (X_0), the ordinary/nonordinary primes of the associated fiber form a genuine horizontal arithmetic problem. Dwork gives the local Frobenius criterion, while global compatible-system or modularity methods must count the primes. The local and global parts are complementary.

## (2) Does Dwork-crystal theory give the horizontal theorem?

Not from the truncation identity alone.

It becomes applicable after an explicit geometricity certificate identifies (H_p(X)) with the Hasse–Witt matrix of a fixed family. It then gives unit roots, Frobenius, connections, and supercongruences on the ordinary locus.

It does not automatically count the primes for which a growing parameter (X_n=n(n+1)) lands on the nonordinary divisor. For literally fixed (n), the problem is eventually just (pmid b_n), by (5.2).

## (3) Can generic ordinarity prove a polylog vertical root bound?

No. Generic ordinarity proves only that (H_p) is not identically zero. A polylog root bound is a much stronger anti-concentration statement.

The concrete attacks are:

1. transfer matrices and gap polynomials, yielding the natural (p^{2/3}) bound and a precise codegree target;

1. symmetric-square factorization to a rank-two Hasse polynomial;

1. construction of a bounded-conductor additive trace-function model, which would give (O(sqrt p));

1. fixed factorial moments and monodromy for almost-all-prime bounds;

1. a fixed division/p-curvature tower whose Frobenius cycle structure controls linear factors;

1. Dwork analysis of multiplicities and valuation lifting.

The fixed-function viewpoint is therefore a genuine improvement: it supplies exact hypergeometric, recurrence, and potential F-crystal structure. The remaining uniform root-count problem is not solved by Dwork theory alone, but it is now reduced to specific, testable geometric and codegree statements rather than an amorphous moving-character problem.
