# Horizontal location of Apéry hypergeometric zeros

## Executive verdict

I found no existing automorphic, theta-lift, or Waldspurger-type object that locates the off-center zero index

for the Apéry \zeta(3) sequence as p varies.

There are strong automorphic precedents for fixed rational hypergeometric parameters. The central Apéry index

is the prime example: Ahlgren–Ono prove that the corresponding Apéry number is congruent modulo p^2 to the $p$th coefficient of the fixed weight-4, level-8 form

But this works because the finite-field hypergeometric characters are the fixed quadratic character and the trivial character. For a general zero position, the relevant character is \omega^{z_p}, whose order and rational parameter

vary with p and are not confined to a fixed finite set. No compatible motive or fixed automorphic representation is known for this moving family.

The word incomplete is not, by itself, a proof of impossibility. At each individual prime, the moving Kummer character still defines a rank-one tame sheaf of bounded local conductor, and the Apéry Mellin value can be realized in bounded-dimensional cohomology. The real obstruction is horizontal compatibility:

- the character order can grow like p;

- the local monodromy eigenvalues and coefficient fields vary with p;

- the selected index z_p is defined implicitly by a same-characteristic divisibility condition;

- no fixed algebraic or automorphic object is known whose Frobenius data encode z_p.

Known vertical and horizontal Sato–Tate theorems are therefore far from the target. They control normalized traces against fixed continuous test functions. Exact vanishing is a local-limit/Lang–Trotter event, and here the event is more difficult still because the trace object itself changes with the selected character.

Assessment: locating these truncated-hypergeometric zeros with a power saving across primes is genuinely open and would require new mathematics. It is not a routine extension of present Sato–Tate, trace-function, or theta-lift technology.

## 1.2 Correction to the usual attribution

The supersingular-prime literature contains several logically different results.

- Elkies proves infinitude for every elliptic curve over \mathbf Q by CM points, singular moduli, and Deuring lifting: N. Elkies, The existence of infinitely many supersingular primes for every elliptic curve over \mathbf Q, Invent. Math. 89 (1987), 561–567.

- Fouvry–Murty prove strong lower bounds and average Lang–Trotter results: É. Fouvry and M. R. Murty, On the distribution of supersingular primes, Canad. J. Math. 48 (1996), 81–104.

- The familiar x^{3/4}-type upper bounds for a fixed trace belong to effective Chebotarev/Lang–Trotter work under GRH, not to Elkies's CM infinitude argument. A modern reference is D. Zywina, Bounds for the Lang–Trotter conjectures.

So the transferable feature is not simply “theta descent proves a horizontal power saving.” The decisive elliptic feature is that a_p=0 is a fixed-trace condition in one fixed compatible system, with Deuring/class-number formulas available after averaging.

The Apéry zero-position problem currently has neither ingredient.

## 3.3 The truncation is not the deepest obstruction

Many incomplete sums can be completed, Fourier-expanded, or represented by an additional geometric variable. So there is no general theorem that “incomplete implies non-motivic.”

A naive completion of the original k\le z sum would introduce a cutoff whose Fourier complexity grows with p, so it does not itself give a bounded-conductor horizontal object. But this is evidence of the obstruction, not a proof that no more ingenious motive can exist.

Answer to Question 2: incompleteness does not prove impossibility. It blocks the direct fixed-motive constructions presently known, while the decisive formal obstruction is the absence of a compatible bounded-complexity family for the moving characters and their residual zero selector.

# 4. How far Sato–Tate is from the required statement

## 4.1 Katz's vertical equidistribution

For a fixed sheaf over a fixed finite field, Deligne–Katz theory gives square-root cancellation and equidistribution as a geometric parameter varies. Katz's Mellin theory similarly fixes a perverse sheaf and studies its transforms as characters vary, often over larger extension fields.

A precise reference is N. Katz, Convolution and Equidistribution: Sato–Tate Theorems for Finite-Field Mellin Transforms, Princeton, 2012. Its main theorem fixes the sheaf and proves equidistribution of normalized Frobenius conjugacy classes as the extension field grows.

This is extremely strong for moments against fixed class functions. It does not estimate

because divisibility by the characteristic is not a continuous class function of the compact monodromy group.

## 4.2 Horizontal Sato–Tate for one fixed motive

For a fixed non-CM elliptic curve or fixed modular form, horizontal Sato–Tate describes the distribution of normalized coefficients

in fixed intervals as p varies. It does not by itself count the exact event a_p=r.

For elliptic curves, exact a_p=0 remains the Lang–Trotter supersingular-prime problem despite the Sato–Tate theorem. To isolate one lattice point from a continuous distribution, the test interval would have to shrink on the scale p^{-1/2}, far beyond ordinary Sato–Tate uniformity.

For a weight-4 form, |a_p|\ll p^{3/2}. The congruence p\mid a_p allows roughly p^{1/2} possible integer sheets

so archimedean Sato–Tate is even farther from this residual event.

The Apéry problem is harder again: one first varies the Kummer character and then asks which character makes the trace divisible by p.

## 4.3 Average Lang–Trotter results

Fouvry–Murty and later authors prove Lang–Trotter asymptotics on average over large families of elliptic curves. See, for example,

- É. Fouvry and M. R. Murty, On the distribution of supersingular primes;

- I. Shparlinski, On the Lang–Trotter and Sato–Tate conjectures on average for polynomial families of elliptic curves.

The averaging variable supplies enough independent curves that Deuring's class-number formula can be summed effectively. These results do not imply a fixed-curve Lang–Trotter theorem.

For Apéry, averaging over z at one prime is not an analogous replacement: z_p is the output of the vanishing condition, and the desired statement concerns the location of that output across characteristics.

## 4.4 Fouvry–Michel and trace functions over primes

Fouvry–Michel and the later Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel theory obtain cancellation for algebraic trace functions evaluated at prime arguments. The quantifiers are of the form:

```plain text
fix a modulus q and a bounded-conductor trace function K on F_q;
sum K(ell) over rational primes ell.
```

Representative references are:

- É. Fouvry and P. Michel, Sur certaines sommes d'exponentielles sur les nombres premiers, Ann. Sci. ÉNS 31 (1998), 93–130;

- É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel, Algebraic Trace Functions over the Primes, Duke Math. J. 163 (2014), 1683–1736.

The Apéry sum has the reverse quantifiers:

```plain text
for each outer prime p, change the field to F_p,
change the character to omega_p^{z_p},
and select z_p by a residual vanishing equation.
```

There is no fixed trace function to which the prime-sum theorem can be applied.

Answer to Question 3: known Sato–Tate results control coarse normalized distributions for fixed objects, and average Lang–Trotter results exploit an external family average. They do not approach a power-saving count for the implicit moving zero of a truncated hypergeometric sequence.

# Final assessment

The central Apéry term belongs to a fixed modular hypergeometric motive. The general zero location does not.

At present there is:

- no half-integral-weight modular form whose coefficients encode z_p;

- no Waldspurger central-value formula for the event b_{z_p}\equiv0\pmod p;

- no fixed compatible hypergeometric motive for the moving fraction z_p/(p-1);

- no Sato–Tate or trace-function theorem that converts same-characteristic residual vanishing into a horizontal power saving.

So the honest answer is:

Locating the vanishing of the off-center truncated Apéry \,{}_4F_3 across primes is genuinely open. A solution would require a new compatible-system/special-cycle description or a new horizontal residual-Mellin theorem. It is not currently within reach by invoking an existing theta lift or Sato–Tate theorem.
