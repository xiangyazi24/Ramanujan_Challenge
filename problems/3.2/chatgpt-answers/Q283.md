# 1. Why fixed (alpha) gives a fixed hypergeometric datum

Write

[

b_z={}_4F_3!left[

begin{matrix}-z,-z,z+1,z+1\1,1,1end{matrix};1

right]

=

sum_{k=0}^{z}frac{(-z)_k^2(z+1)_k^2}{(k!)^4}.

]

Let (alpha=a/min(0,1)) be reduced, assume (pequiv1pmod m), and set

[

z=alpha(p-1)=frac{a(p-1)}m.

]

In (mathbb F_p),

[

-zequivalpha,qquad z+1equiv1-alpha.

]

Therefore, term by term,

[

frac{(-z)_k^2(z+1)_k^2}{(k!)^4}

equiv

frac{(alpha)_k^2(1-alpha)_k^2}{(k!)^4}pmod p.

]

The right-hand summand vanishes modulo (p) once (k>z), because ((alpha)_{z+1}equiv0pmod p). Hence one may extend the sum to (p-1):

[

boxed{

{}_4F_3!left[

begin{matrix}alpha,alpha,1-alpha,1-alpha\1,1,1end{matrix};1

right]_{p-1}pmod p.

}

]

This is the key fixed-quotient observation. The hypergeometric parameters no longer depend on (p); only the Frobenius prime does.

Equivalently, using a character (A_p) of order (m) with exponent (a), the finite-field character tuple is

[

(A_p,A_p,A_p^{-1},A_p^{-1};varepsilon,varepsilon,varepsilon).

]

This is exactly the fixed-character family discussed in the preceding general-(z) formula.

# 2. Why this does not come from the (Gamma_0(6)) symmetric square

Beukers and Beukers–Peters identify the Apéry recurrence with a Picard–Fuchs equation for a one-parameter K3 family. Beukers’s modular parametrization uniformizes the holomorphic solution using weight-2 modular forms and a Hauptmodul for the genus-zero group related to (Gamma_0(6)). The third-order Apéry operator is a symmetric square of a second-order operator.

Schematically, this says

[

L_{mathrm{Apacute ery},3}simeqoperatorname{Sym}^2 L_2

]

as differential equations in the variable (t), and

[

B(t)=sum_{nge0}b_nt^n

]

becomes a modular form after substituting a modular function (t=t(tau)).

That statement controls:

- the variation of the K3 periods as (t) changes;

- the monodromy of the rank-3 Picard–Fuchs local system;

- congruences and formal-group phenomena for the Taylor coefficients (b_n).

It does not identify the map

[

plongmapsto b_{alpha(p-1)}pmod p

]

with the (p)-th Fourier coefficient of the weight-2 form used in the uniformization. The roles of the variables are different:

- in the Beukers modular picture, (n) is a Taylor-coefficient index at a fixed cusp and (t) is the geometric parameter;

- in the fixed-(alpha) picture, the index itself varies with the residue characteristic, (n=alpha(p-1)), and modulo (p) it is converted into a new ({}_4F_3) datum with rational parameters.

The latter datum is a weight-3 Calabi–Yau-type hypergeometric object, not the original rank-3 K3 symmetric-square variation.

Thus the implication

[

text{Apéry operator is }operatorname{Sym}^2

quadLongrightarrowquad

b_{alpha(p-1)}=a_p(g_alpha)

]

is not valid.

## Exact finite-field trace and the modular quotient

Their Theorem 2 gives the exact identity

[

boxed{

H_p(boldsymbolalpha,boldsymbolbeta;1)

=a_p(f_alpha)+chi_alpha(p)p.

}

]

Thus the complex finite-field hypergeometric lift is not merely congruent to the eigenvalue. It contains:

1. a two-dimensional weight-3 Galois representation (rho_{f_alpha}), attached to the weight-4 eigenform;

1. a one-dimensional Tate piece whose Frobenius trace is (chi_alpha(p)p).

At (alpha=1/2), this is the Ahlgren–Ono evaluation

[

H_p=a_p(eta(2tau)^4eta(4tau)^4)+p.

]

Modulo (p), the Tate term disappears, leaving the Apéry congruence. Kilbourn proves the corresponding stronger supercongruence for the classical central truncated hypergeometric series.

So the desired “fixed quotient per rational (alpha)” is genuinely present for these four (alpha)’s, but it is a consequence of the rigid Calabi–Yau hypergeometric motive, not of the original (Gamma_0(6)) symmetric-square operator.

# 4. Why exactly these denominators are naturally defined over (mathbb Q)

Beukers–Cohen–Mellit call a hypergeometric datum defined over (mathbb Q) when the numerator and denominator root multisets are Galois-stable, equivalently when the corresponding root polynomials are products of cyclotomic polynomials.

For the repeated datum, the numerator roots are

[

zeta_m^a,zeta_m^a,zeta_m^{-a},zeta_m^{-a}.

]

Its root polynomial is

[

P_alpha(X)

=(X-zeta_m^a)^2(X-zeta_m^{-a})^2.

]

Assuming ((a,m)=1), this multiset is invariant under the full Galois group over (mathbb Q) exactly when every unit modulo (m) sends (a) to (pm a). Equivalently,

[

(mathbb Z/mmathbb Z)^times={pm1}.

]

For reduced nontrivial denominators this occurs precisely for

[

boxed{m=2,3,4,6.}

]

This gives a conceptual reason that the repeated-parameter rational cases landing directly in the (mathbb Q)-defined Rodriguez–Villegas list are exactly

[

alpha=frac12,frac13,frac14,frac16

]

up to replacing (alpha) by (1-alpha).

For (m>6), the datum is naturally defined over the real cyclotomic field

[

K_alpha=mathbb Q(zeta_m^a+zeta_m^{-a}),

]

not over (mathbb Q). Katz’s character-sum construction still gives a fixed hypergeometric system for primes splitting appropriately, but there is no automatic two-dimensional descent to (G_mathbb Q). Taking all Galois conjugates produces a much larger representation; its dimension grows with ([K_alpha:mathbb Q]), rather than yielding one classical GL(_2/mathbb Q) modular form.

# 5. What can be claimed for a general rational (alpha)

Let (alpha=a/m) be reduced.

## Fixed compatible hypergeometric object: essentially yes

For primes (pequiv1pmod m), choose the order-(m) character corresponding to (a/m). The character tuple

[

(A,A,A^{-1},A^{-1};varepsilon^3)

]

is independent of (p) as cyclotomic data. Katz’s hypergeometric-sheaf theory supplies the associated compatible finite-field trace formalism. In a motivic formulation, one expects/obtains realizations over the relevant cyclotomic field, subject to the usual care at the singular parameter (t=1).

Thus there is a legitimate fixed hypergeometric motive/sheaf per (alpha).

## Fixed classical Hecke eigenform: generally no known theorem

Nothing in Katz or Beukers–Cohen–Mellit says that an arbitrary rank-4 hypergeometric datum has a two-dimensional GL(_2) quotient. Generic hypergeometric monodromy is large. Modularity by a classical eigenform requires an exceptional rank drop, decomposition, endomorphisms, rigidity, or a separate automorphy theorem.

Long–Tu–Yui–Zudilin succeed because their fourteen fibers at (t=1) are rigid Calabi–Yau threefolds over (mathbb Q), whose relevant two-dimensional (H^3) pieces are modular. This is special geometry, not a formal consequence of fixing rational parameters.

For (m>6), the likely arithmetic possibilities are instead:

- a higher-rank compatible system over (K_alpha);

- automorphy over a number field, potentially Hilbert or higher-rank automorphic rather than classical modular;

- an induced representation over (mathbb Q) of dimension larger than two;

- exceptional isolated (alpha)’s admitting transformations or extra endomorphisms.

There is currently no classification saying that every (alpha=a/m) yields a fixed weight-4 newform, and such a statement would be false in generic monodromy.

# 6. Consequence for the proposed fixed-quotient strategy

The correct hierarchy is:

[

boxed{

begin{array}{c}

text{fixed rational }alpha\

Downarrow\

text{fixed hypergeometric datum }mathrm{HD}_alpha\

Downarrow\

text{fixed compatible hypergeometric trace system over a cyclotomic field}

end{array}}

]

but only in exceptional cases can one continue with

[

boxed{

text{fixed rank-2 quotient}

Longleftrightarrow

text{fixed classical Hecke eigenform}.

}

]

For

[

alpha=1/2,1/3,1/4,1/6,

]

Long–Tu–Yui–Zudilin supply precisely this continuation, including the exact Tate correction. Therefore these four slopes are legitimate fixed modular quotients and can be treated with Hecke-eigenvalue technology.

For a general rational slope, the safe statement is only “fixed hypergeometric compatible system,” not “fixed modular form.” The Apéry (Gamma_0(6)) symmetric-square structure does not bridge that gap.
