# Verdict

I do not find an unconditional published theorem that implies the required pointwise estimate. The direct (q=1) problem reduces exactly to a large-prime radical of a single explicit gcd, but every theorem I could verify either concerns constant-coefficient recurrence orbits, gives a density-one/average conclusion, or gives lower bounds for prime factors rather than an upper bound for the moving diagonal incidence.

The closest theorem by sequence class is Luca–Shparlinski's density-one theorem for Apéry numbers. # 1. Bare arithmetic form

Let

```plain text
A_j = sum_{k=0}^j binom(j,k)^2 binom(j+k,k)^2,
H   = floor((n-1)/3),
B_n = binom(n,H+1).
```

For an integer (mne0), write

```plain text
rad_{>x}(m) = product_{p prime, p|m, p>x} p.
```

Then, for all sufficiently large (n) (in fact once (2n/3>5)), the requested sum satisfies the exact identity

```plain text
S(n)
 = sum_{0<=j<=H, p=n-j prime, p|A_j} log p
 = log rad_{>2n/3}( gcd(A_n,B_n) ).                 (1)
```

## Proof of (1)

Fix (0le jle H), and put (p=n-j). Then

```plain text
3j <= n-1 = p+j-1,
so 2j <= p-1,
and hence p>2j and j<p.                              (2)
```

The Apéry numbers have the (p)-Lucas property

```plain text
A_{ap+b} == A_a A_b (mod p),   0<=b<p.              (3)
```

Using (n=p+j), (A_1=5), and (2), this gives

```plain text
A_n == 5 A_j (mod p).                                (4)
```

For all candidate primes in the asymptotic range, (p>5), so

```plain text
p|A_j  iff  p|A_n.                                   (5)
```

Now (jle H<H+1<p). Lucas's theorem for the binomial coefficient, using the base-(p) digits (n=p+j), yields

```plain text
binom(n,H+1)
 == binom(1,0) binom(j,H+1)
 == 0 (mod p).                                       (6)
```

Also (jle H) is equivalent, for integral (p=n-j), to (p>2n/3). Thus every prime counted by (S(n)) occurs in the right side of (1). Conversely, if (p>2n/3) divides (gcd(A_n,B_n)), then (j=n-p<n/3), hence (0le jle H), and (4) gives (p|A_j). This proves (1).

The condition emphasized in the question is therefore automatic and strict:

```plain text
p=n-j > 2j.                                          (7)
```

It is stronger than what is needed to invert every Apéry transfer matrix up to time (j).

## What (1) gives without a new theorem

Stirling's formula gives

```plain text
log B_n
 = n h(1/3) + O(log n),
h(1/3) = log 3 - (2/3)log 2 > 0.                     (8)
```

Therefore

```plain text
S(n) <= log gcd(A_n,B_n) <= log B_n = O(n),          (9)
```

but the carrier itself has positive linear entropy, so (9) does not give (o(n)). Likewise the Apéry asymptotic has

```plain text
log A_n = n log(17+12 sqrt(2)) + O(log n),           (10)
```

which is also linear. The exact missing estimate is

```plain text
log rad_{>2n/3}( gcd(A_n,binom(n,H+1)) ) = o(n)       (11)
```

for every (ntoinfty), not merely for almost all (n), an average over (n), or infinitely many (n).

# 2. Audit of the nearest published theorems

## 2.1 Luca–Shparlinski: Apéry numbers themselves

Reference. Florian Luca and Igor E. Shparlinski, “Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers,” Journal of the London Mathematical Society 78 (2008), 545–562, DOI 10.1112/jlms/jdn031.

Their main stated conclusion is that there is an absolute (c_0>0) such that, on a set of positive integers (m) of asymptotic density one,

```plain text
omega(A_m) > c_0 log log log m,
P(A_m)     > c_0 (log m log log m)^(1/2).            (12)
```

Their method applies to order-two polynomial-coefficient recurrences with suitable Lucas congruences, so Apéry satisfies the sequence-side hypotheses exactly.

Failure for Q806. Equation (12) is:

- density-one, not pointwise in every (n);

- a lower bound on the number/size of prime divisors of a single (A_m);

- silent about the prescribed moving prime (p=n-j);

- silent about the total weighted mass of incidences over (0le jle n/3).

Even an arbitrarily strong lower bound for (P(A_j)) would not bound how often the particular prime (n-j) divides (A_j).

## 2.2 Luca: prime divisors of binary holonomic sequences

Reference. Florian Luca, “Prime divisors of binary holonomic sequences,” Advances in Applied Mathematics 40 (2008), 168–179, DOI 10.1016/j.aam.2006.12.001.

The theorem assumes a rational sequence satisfying

```plain text
f_0(m)u_{m+2}+f_1(m)u_{m+1}+f_2(m)u_m=0,
f_i in Q[X], not all zero,                           (13)
```

and assumes that the sequence is not eventually a binary constant-coefficient recurrence. It concludes that there is (c>0) such that the product of the numerators and denominators of the nonzero (u_m), (mle N), has at least

```plain text
c log N                                               (14)
```

distinct prime divisors as (Ntoinfty).

The Apéry recurrence satisfies (13), and Apéry is not eventually binary constant-recursive: its (m^{-3/2}(17+12sqrt2)^m) asymptotic is incompatible with a finite exponential polynomial whose polynomial prefactors have nonnegative integral degrees.

Failure for Q806. The conclusion aggregates all terms (u_m) with (mle N), counts distinct primes without weights, and is a lower bound. It supplies no upper bound for one moving diagonal at a fixed (n).

## 2.3 Grieve–Wang: moving targets and recurrence gcds

Reference. Nathan Grieve and Julie Tzu-Yueh Wang, “Greatest common divisors with moving targets and consequences for linear recurrence sequences,” arXiv:1902.09109.

Their Theorem 1.2 concerns fixed-rank (S)-unit points

```plain text
(u_1(alpha),...,u_r(alpha)) in (O_{K,S}^*)^r,        (15)
```

and moving coprime polynomials of fixed positive degrees whose coefficient heights are negligible relative to the heights of the (S)-unit coordinates. The conclusion is a dichotomy on an infinite subset: either the generalized logarithmic gcd is (<varepsilon) times the controlling height there, or the points lie in moving translates of a proper torus subgroup.

Their recurrence ring is explicitly the constant-coefficient/exponential-polynomial ring

```plain text
F(m) = sum_i f_i(m) alpha_i^m,                       (16)
```

with roots in a finitely generated multiplicative group. Theorem 1.6, under (S)-unit-root, torsion-free, local nondegeneracy, and coprimality hypotheses in that recurrence ring, concludes only that there are infinitely many (m) for which

```plain text
log gcd(F(m),G(m)) < epsilon m.                      (17)
```

Hypothesis failure. Apéry is P-recursive, not of the form (16). Its nonautonomous transfer step depends polynomially on (m), and its values are not coordinates of a fixed finite-rank (S)-unit orbit. Moreover (17) is an infinitely-many conclusion, whereas (11) is pointwise for all large (n).

## 2.4 Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier: the closest conclusion shape

Reference. Yann Bugeaud, Pietro Corvaja and Umberto Zannier, “An upper bound for the G.C.D. of (a^n-1) and (b^n-1),” Mathematische Zeitschrift 243 (2003), 79–84, DOI 10.1007/s00209-002-0449-z.

For multiplicatively independent positive integers (a,b), their theorem gives, for every (varepsilon>0),

```plain text
gcd(a^m-1,b^m-1) < exp(epsilon m)                   (18)
```

for all sufficiently large (m).

This has the correct pointwise (exp(o(m))) shape. But its proof uses a fixed algebraic-torus orbit ((a^m,b^m)), so neither hypothesis nor proof mechanism transfers to the index-dependent Apéry matrices. Equation (18) is therefore the closest formal conclusion, not an applicable theorem.

## 2.5 Apéry (p)-adic/G-function results

Éric Delaygue, “Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers,” Compositio Mathematica 154 (2018), 249–274, DOI 10.1112/S0010437X17007552, proves lower bounds for (p)-adic valuations of factorial-ratio multisums and effective (p)-Lucas criteria. These results justify powerful congruence transport such as (3), but they are lower-divisibility statements. They do not upper-bound the set of primes satisfying (p|A_j) on the moving line (p+j=n).

General G-function denominator and height theorems likewise control denominators, analytic continuation, or global heights. I found no theorem converting those properties into the pointwise radical estimate (11).

## 2.6 Arithmetic and affine large sieves

Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak's affine linear sieve starts with a fixed orbit

```plain text
O = Lambda v,   Lambda < GL_d(Z) finitely generated, (19)
```

and a fixed integral polynomial on that orbit; see “Affine linear sieve, expanders, and sum-product,” Inventiones Mathematicae 179 (2010), 559–644, DOI 10.1007/s00222-009-0225-3.

The Apéry state is instead generated by the nonstationary product

```plain text
T_{m-1} T_{m-2} ... T_0,                             (20)
```

with a different matrix at every time. Encoding all (T_m) in a larger fixed system does not give a fixed finite-dimensional integral group orbit: the index (m) itself must evolve and the entries have polynomial growth and changing determinants. Standard recurrence large sieves also average over primes, moduli, or indices. Such average or density-one conclusions cannot imply (11) for every (n).

# 3. Apéry transfer matrices and why order does not couple different primes

The Apéry recurrence is

```plain text
(m+1)^3 A_{m+1}
-(34m^3+51m^2+27m+5)A_m
+m^3 A_{m-1}=0.                                      (21)
```

Put

```plain text
X_m=(m!)^3 A_m,
P(m)=34m^3+51m^2+27m+5.                              (22)
```

Then

```plain text
X_{m+1}=P(m)X_m-m^6X_{m-1},                          (23)
T_m = [[P(m),-m^6],[1,0]],
det(T_m)=m^6.                                        (24)
```

The generating function (F(z)=sum A_m z^m) satisfies the equivalent Apéry differential equation

```plain text
[theta^3
 - z(2theta+1)(17theta^2+17theta+5)
 + z^2(theta+1)^3] F = 0,
theta=z d/dz.                                        (25)
```

The operator (25) controls solutions in one characteristic. It does not itself correlate reductions in different characteristics.

## CRT interpolation lemma

Lemma. Let (j_1,dots,j_t) be nonnegative integers and let (p_1,dots,p_t) be distinct primes with (p_r>j_r). For each (r), prescribe an arbitrary state

```plain text
w_r in F_{p_r}^2.                                    (26)
```

Then there is an integral initial vector (v=(X_1,X_0)in Z^2) whose solution of (23) has the prescribed state at each pair ((j_r,p_r)).

Proof. Let (M_j) be the transfer product taking ((X_1,X_0)) to the chosen two-coordinate state at time (j). Its determinant is a product of sixth powers of integers at most (j), hence

```plain text
det(M_j) != 0 (mod p) whenever p>j.                  (27)
```

Thus (M_{j_r}) is invertible over (F_{p_r}), and the required initial residue is

```plain text
v == M_{j_r}^{-1} w_r (mod p_r).                     (28)
```

The coordinatewise Chinese remainder theorem gives one (vin Z^2) satisfying all congruences (28). ∎

Taking the second coordinate in (26) to be zero or nonzero shows that, among the family of integral solutions of the same order-two holonomic recurrence, an arbitrary finite hit/no-hit pattern can be imposed at arbitrary distinct pairs ((j_r,p_r)) with (p_r>j_r).

For Q806, (7) gives (p_r>2j_r), so the transfer maps are especially far from singular. This helps local propagation but provides no cross-prime dependence. Any proof must use the distinguished low-height initial state

```plain text
(X_0,X_1)=(1,5),                                     (29)
```

not merely the order, the recurrence operator, or invertibility modulo (p).

This explains the failure of a universal low-order resultant. A determinant made solely from a bounded number of transfer rows is a same-characteristic relation. To make every distinct (p=n-j) divide one integer, one must synchronize those relations over (Z); the exact Smith calculation in the current frontier note shows that the natural synchronized lattice has invariant factors

```plain text
(1,R_1(n)),                                          (30)
```

where the second factor already contains the full direct (q=1) radical. Thus the obvious determinant has logarithmic height of the same order as the quantity to be bounded; no compression occurs.

# 4. The precise missing estimate

The problem is not to show that each hit divides some holonomic expression; (1) already puts every hit in one gcd. The missing input is a sublinear-height integer that absorbs many distinct moving primes simultaneously.

A convenient block formulation is the following explicit lemma.

## New sufficient lemma: block determinant compression

Lemma BDC(2/3,3/4). There is an absolute (C>0) such that, for every sufficiently large (n), with

```plain text
L = ceil(n^(2/3)),                                    (31)
```

and every interval (Isubseteq[0,H]) of at most (L) consecutive integers, one can construct from (n), (I), and the integral Apéry transfer system a square integer matrix (M_{n,I}) satisfying

```plain text
1 <= r_{n,I} := size(M_{n,I}) <= C L^(3/4),          (32)
max_{a,b}|(M_{n,I})_{a,b}| <= n^C,                   (33)
det(M_{n,I}) != 0,                                   (34)
```

and, for every (jin I),

```plain text
[p=n-j prime and p|A_j]  =>  p | det(M_{n,I}).       (35)
```

The construction must be independent of knowing in advance which (j)'s are hits; otherwise (35) is tautological.

### BDC implies the target

The candidate primes (p=n-j) are distinct as (j) varies. Therefore (35) gives

```plain text
product_{j in I, n-j prime, n-j|A_j} (n-j)
 | det(M_{n,I}).                                      (36)
```

Hadamard's inequality and (32)–(33) give

```plain text
log|det(M_{n,I})|
 <= r_{n,I}( C log n + (1/2)log r_{n,I})
 = O(L^(3/4) log n).                                  (37)
```

There are (O(n/L)) blocks. Summing (37),

```plain text
S(n)
 <= O((n/L)L^(3/4)log n)
 = O(n L^(-1/4) log n)
 = O(n^(5/6) log n)
 = o(n).                                              (38)
```

Thus BDC(2/3,3/4) is a fully quantified new lemma sufficient to close the direct (q=1) problem. More generally, any block length (L=n^beta) and determinant size (O(L^{1-delta})), with fixed (beta,delta>0) and polynomially bounded entries, would give

```plain text
S(n)=O(n^(1-beta*delta) log n)=o(n).                  (39)
```

The current transfer/Padé/Smith constructions do not meet (32): after saturation, their effective determinant dimension/height is linear in the number or mass of the hits. A proof of BDC must therefore add a genuinely arithmetic relation specific to the Apéry initial state, not another formal consequence of holonomicity.

# 5. Comparison with primitive-divisor and greatest-prime-factor theorems

## Primitive divisors

Bilu–Hanrot–Voutier prove that every (m)-th Lucas or Lehmer number with (m>30) has a primitive divisor: Yuri Bilu, Guillaume Hanrot and Paul M. Voutier, “Existence of primitive divisors of Lucas and Lehmer numbers,” Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik 539 (2001), 75–122, DOI 10.1515/crll.2001.080.

Its hypotheses are constant-coefficient Lucas/Lehmer structure and strong divisibility. Apéry has neither. More importantly, the conclusion is existential and lower-directional:

```plain text
there exists a new prime divisor of u_m.             (40)
```

Q806 asks for an upper bound on the total weight of primes obeying the additional equation (p+j=n).

## Greatest prime factor

C. L. Stewart, “On divisors of Lucas and Lehmer numbers,” Acta Mathematica 211 (2013), 291–314; preprint arXiv:1008.1274, proves for Lucas/Lehmer terms an eventual lower bound of the shape

```plain text
P(u_m) > m exp( log m / (104 log log m) ).            (41)
```

Again, (41) concerns a constant-coefficient divisibility sequence and gives a lower bound for one largest prime. It remains compatible with every candidate prime (n-j) dividing (A_j) for a positive proportion of (j); it supplies no diagonal upper incidence estimate.

The precise conceptual gap is therefore:

```plain text
primitive-divisor / P(u_m) theorems:
    one or more large primes must occur in each term;

Q806:
    the prescribed prime p=n-j must occur for only
    sublinear total logarithmic mass at one fixed n. (42)
```

These are orthogonal directions.

# 6. Final status

The direct (q=1) statement is exactly

```plain text
log rad_{>2n/3}
  gcd( A_n, binom(n,floor((n-1)/3)+1) ) = o(n).       (43)
```

No verified unconditional theorem on P-recursive/G-function sequences, moving-target gcds, fixed recurrence orbits, large sieves, primitive divisors, or greatest prime factors implies (43). The strongest nearby results fail either the sequence hypothesis (fixed (S)-unit/constant-coefficient orbit), the quantifier (density-one, average, or infinitely many indices), or the direction (lower rather than upper prime-factor bounds).

The transfer-matrix CRT lemma proves why holonomic order and the favorable inequality (p>2j) cannot by themselves force cross-prime sparsity. A proof can close the problem by establishing BDC(2/3,3/4), or any quantitatively comparable block low-height determinant/resultant lemma. At present, that is the closest rigorous reduction I can support; I do not claim a proof of BDC.
