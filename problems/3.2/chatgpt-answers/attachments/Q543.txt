It does not prove a positive lower bound for (5.1), for three reasons:

1. (E_{n,tau}) changes with (n).

1. The norm uses the average of (log|T_n|), not the maximum over all conjugates.

1. A small fraction of very large conjugates may compensate, in the algebraic-integrality constraints, for many conjugates in a low-capacity region.

Recent effective versions of Fekete’s theorem and weighted integer-Chebyshev theory quantify support and height restrictions, but they do not directly solve this moving mutual-resultant problem. ## 7.4 Salem numbers

For Salem numbers of degree tending to infinity with bounded house, all but two conjugates lie on the unit circle and the logarithmic Weil height tends to zero. Bilu’s theorem then forces equidistribution on the unit circle. See Yuri Bilu, Limit distribution of small points on algebraic tori.

In the regime where this equidistribution is uniform enough relative to (n), the average rate is again at least (log8). The two real reciprocal conjugates have density (2/d) and do not change the limit.

## 7.5 Pisot numbers

A bounded-house sequence of Pisot numbers with degrees tending to infinity also has height tending to zero, so the same Bilu mechanism applies. Its conjugates cannot asymptotically concentrate near (-1/2); they equidistribute on the unit circle.

Pisot families with growing house, or coupled degree/height comparable to the complexity of (T_n), are not covered by this argument. They belong to the unresolved weighted-resultant problem (WR±).
