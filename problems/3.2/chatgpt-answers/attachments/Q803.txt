# 8. Why the observed b=0 minimum is not yet a theorem

The repository reports an exhaustive exact scan at n=3H+1 for every split and every 4\le H\le40. The denominator-free Newton interpolant is the unique minimum in all those cases. The same audit proves that the Newton value does not divide the other primitive Padé values, so there is no hidden elementary divisibility proof.

Theorem 5.1 proves this comparison only for each fixed denominator strip after H is large: every fixed b has the same positive exponential rate as b=0, with a computable fixed multiplicative constant. It does not compare the constants strongly enough to prove that b=0 is the unique minimum, and it says nothing about b\asymp H or H-b=O(1).

A full proof of the empirical minimum would follow from either of the following genuinely new statements:

1. for every compact K\Subset(0,1), a projective lower rate strictly larger than the b=0 rate; and a matching boundary theorem for fixed a;

1. a direct comparison inequality for the parity determinants in (3.4).

Neither is currently available.
