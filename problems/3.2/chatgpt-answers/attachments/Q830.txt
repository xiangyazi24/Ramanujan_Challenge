# 11. The exact missing zero theorem

The following statement is sufficient and close to minimal.

## Theorem ZR — rightmost zeros and Rice admissibility

There exist \eta>0 and a simple zero

such that:

1. Rightmost pair. The only zeros with real part greater than \alpha-\eta are \rho and \overline\rho.

1. Residue summability. The zero contributions with -1/2<\Re z\le\alpha-\eta are locally finite and their Rice residue sum is O(H^{\alpha-\eta}), together with the first two backward shifts in H.

1. Contour admissibility. For every H, there is a sequence of truncation heights along which the finite Rice contour can be displaced to \Re z=-1/2 and the horizontal/right pieces vanish.

1. Certified location. Interval Newton and an argument-principle count certify the displayed ball for \rho and the absence of another zero with larger real part.

Under Theorem ZR, equations (7.1), (8.1), and (8.6) are rigorous. If the pair is the only zero in the whole half-plane \Re z>-1/2, condition 2 is replaced by the explicit line error O(H^{-1/2}).
