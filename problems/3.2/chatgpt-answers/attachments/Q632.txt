## 2.3 What about algebraic normalization?

Multiplication by a fixed algebraic exponential does not alter the powers of m. Multiplication by any fixed polynomial in m cannot turn the Apéry power m^{-3/2} into a nonnegative integral power. Hence no normalization of the form

enters the Binet class.

For U_s, V_s, or S_s, multiplication by m^2 removes the leading negative power only asymptotically. It does not make the sequence an exact finite power sum: the rational diagonal has logarithmic monodromy and a full inverse-power saddle expansion. The transformed generating function remains non-rational.

An arbitrary sequence-dependent normalization would be vacuous—for example, dividing by the sequence itself—and is not a hypothesis in any gcd theorem. Factorial or gamma normalizations are also unsuitable for the Subspace-Theorem applications: their prime support changes with m, so the resulting points do not lie in one fixed finitely generated multiplicative group or in the S-units for one fixed finite S.

No exact finite power-sum or fixed-S-unit representation is presently known for the Apéry, Newton, or Legendre–Euler sections.

# 3. Exact audit of the existing gcd theorems

## 3.1 Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier

The theorem states that for fixed multiplicatively independent integers a,b\ge2 and every \varepsilon>0,

for all sufficiently large n.

The proof uses the Subspace Theorem after encoding the two powers in the fixed rank-two group generated by a and b. Algebraic dominant roots of a P-recursive asymptotic are not enough: one needs the exact coordinates a^n,b^n.

## 3.2 Fuchs

Fuchs studies linear recurring sequences with exact Binet expressions, in particular simple sequences with finitely many fixed characteristic roots. His theorem concerns divisibility/common factors in the ring of power sums. The Apéry sections have no element in that ring to which the theorem can be applied.

Even the weaker conclusion in Fuchs—an exponent strictly below one relative to the height of one sequence—would not by itself give e^{o(n)} unless supplemented by the stronger multiplicative-independence structure. In any case the class hypothesis already fails.

Grieve–Wang allow moving polynomial targets, but their points remain moving S-unit points and their recurrence applications remain algebraic constant-coefficient linear recurrences.

His almost-S-unit extension still fixes a finite set S and assumes that the non-S height is a controlled fraction of the total height. No such estimate is known for b_n, U_s, V_s, S_s, or the factorial-ratio carriers. Proving it would itself be a strong prime-factor theorem of essentially the type sought here.

## 3.4 G-functions and diagonals

The rational-diagonal descriptions imply:

- D-finiteness/P-recursiveness;

- arithmetic growth and denominator bounds;

- global nilpotence/geometricity in the appropriate cases;

- full saddle expansions.

They do not imply that two values have small gcd. The Fischler–Rivoal theorem controls an integer \delta_N clearing denominators of

for one G-function. It says nothing about

In the present problem all principal sequences are already integral, so the denominator theorem is vacuous at the required step.

## 3.5 Uniformity failure if the dimension grows

One might truncate a saddle expansion after R terms and regard the resulting approximation as a power sum with R auxiliary coordinates. This does not fit the Subspace-Theorem theorems uniformly:

- an exact equality is required, not an exponentially accurate approximation;

- to improve the error with n, the number R grows;

- the ambient torus dimension and group rank then grow;

- exceptional subspaces and constants in the Subspace Theorem are not uniform in that growing dimension.

This is why “infinitely many saddle corrections” is a genuine obstruction, not a technicality.
