# 7. What the literature actually obtains from the constant-term structure

## Gorodetsky

Ofir Gorodetsky, New representations for all sporadic Apéry-like sequences, with applications to congruences, Experimental Mathematics 32 (2023), 641–656.

For the zeta(3) sequence and the other sporadic sequences, the paper obtains constant-term representations, binomial formulas, D3/Lucas congruences, one supercongruence for another sporadic sequence, and Delaygue-type lower valuation bounds. It does not prove additive value equidistribution of b_r mod p or count middle prime factors.

## Samol–van Straten and Mellit–Vlasenko

- Kira Samol and Duco van Straten, Dwork congruences and reflexive polytopes.

- Anton Mellit and Masha Vlasenko, Dwork’s congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial, Int. J. Number Theory 12 (2016), 313–321.

These works explain the Dwork congruences and p-adic analytic continuation/unit-root ratios for constant-term periods. They are congruence and Frobenius-analytic results, not coefficient value-distribution theorems.

## Delaygue

Éric Delaygue, Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers, Compositio Mathematica 154 (2018), 249–274.

Delaygue proves lower bounds for p-adic valuations and criteria for the p-Lucas property, including Beukers’ valuation conjectures for Apéry numbers. Again the bounds are lower bounds driven by base-p digits; they do not give middle-prime counts or valuation upper bounds.

## Malik–Straub, Straub, and finite-state schemes

- Amita Malik and Armin Straub, Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers, Research in Number Theory 2 (2016), article 5.

- Armin Straub, On congruence schemes for constant terms and their applications, Research in Number Theory 8 (2022), article 42.

These works prove Lucas-type congruences and construct algorithmic p-schemes/automata for constant-term sequences. Such schemes can compute or structurally describe b_n mod p^k for a fixed prime. They do not currently have complexity uniform enough in the moving prime p to imply first-block Fourier cancellation.

## Kohen’s fixed-modulus distribution result

Nadav Kohen, A Linear Representation for Constant Term Sequences mod p^a with Applications to Uniform Recurrence (2025 preprint), explicitly includes Apéry numbers among the constant-term sequences covered by his linear-representation framework.

This is a genuine distribution result, but its quantifiers are the opposite of Target A: p is fixed and n -> infinity. In the Lucas case the basic phenomenon is already transparent. If Z_p is nonempty, then almost every sufficiently long base-p digit string contains a digit from Z_p, so b_n=0 mod p has natural density one. If Z_p is empty, no digit product can vanish.

Thus the entire long-term fixed-p distribution is controlled by the first block 0<=r<p; the theorem does not bound that first block as p varies.

Justin Offutt’s Automatic Bounds on Constant Term Sequences Modulo Primes gives automaton-state bounds for univariate Laurent polynomials. It does not presently cover the three-variable Apéry model in the form needed here.

## Galois groups of the generating series modulo p

Xavier Caruso, Florian Fürnsinn, Daniel Vargas-Montoya, and Wadim Zudilin, Galois Groups of Apéry-like Series Modulo Primes, Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society (First View, 2026), compute Galois groups of the reductions modulo p of Apéry-like generating series.

This is strong algebraic information about the generating function over F_p(z). It does not presently imply additive equidistribution of its first p Taylor coefficients.

# 8. Ranked toric directions that are still credible

## 1. Exploit the exact two-digit middle-prime decomposition

This is the strongest immediately usable consequence:

The two branches have different arithmetic geometry and should be estimated separately. The small-digit branch involves large prime factors of earlier terms b_a; the residue branch is a structured affine sampling of Z_p. This decomposition can be combined with gap polynomials, dyadic decomposition in a, and the existing cross-prime dispersion framework.

## 2. Search for a bounded-complexity residual Mellin/Hasse object

The Kummer sums H_{p,r} are geometric lifts of b_r. The correct breakthrough would be a theorem that their first Hasse invariant, as r varies through tame characters, is represented by a bounded-degree section on a character parameter space. That would address divisibility by p directly. Full complex weights alone do not.

## 3. Audit alternative constant-term models for nondegeneracy

The two Gorodetsky models above are face-degenerate. A systematic search among birational/cluster mutations could find a nondegenerate constant-term model. For each candidate one should compute:


A nondegenerate model would give sharp bounds for the associated toric additive and Kummer sums. It would not automatically give S_p(t), but it could improve control of the full Mellin lifts and their fixed moments.

## 4. Use finite-state structure quantitatively across p

Constant-term sequences are p-automatic/p-regular in strong senses. Existing schemes are normally built one prime at a time. A uniform bound on the number of states, transition complexity, and spectral gap for the first p outputs of the three-variable Apéry automaton would be a genuinely new route to value distribution. This is closer to Target A than Adolphson–Sperber because it acts on the coefficient index itself.
