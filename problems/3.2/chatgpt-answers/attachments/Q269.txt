“Automatic along primes” fixes one base and one automaton, then evaluates that one sequence at prime indices. Here the prime p simultaneously changes the characteristic, the base, the automaton, and the residue table, and only one one-digit input is read from each automaton.

The fact that r=n mod p lies in [0,p) does kill the automatic route as a black box. Varying p does not restore the hypotheses of the prime-orthogonality theorems; it removes them.

A preliminary logical point: if n is literally fixed independently of X, then for X>n one has n mod p=n, and the count is eventually zero because only finitely many primes divide the fixed integer b_n. The nontrivial question is a bound uniform in a moving n, as in the middle-prime application.

## 1. What is automatic, and what is not

For each fixed prime p, reduction of the Apéry generating series modulo p is algebraic over F_p(x), hence its coefficient sequence is p-automatic by Christol's theorem. Furstenberg gives the algebraicity of diagonals of rational functions in positive characteristic.

- G. Christol, T. Kamae, M. Mendès France, G. Rauzy, “Suites algébriques, automates et substitutions,” Bull. Soc. Math. France 108 (1980), 401–419, DOI.

- H. Furstenberg, “Algebraic functions over finite fields,” J. Algebra 7 (1967), 271–277, DOI.

- E. Rowland and R. Yassawi, “Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational functions,” J. Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux 27 (2015), 245–288, DOI.

Again, this is orthogonality of a fixed symbolic system in the index variable m. It gives no estimate for a triangular array c_p(r) sampled once in each row.

### Densities along primes

Adamczewski–Drmota–Müllner prove that for any fixed automatic sequence, logarithmic densities along the prime subsequence exist and are computable; they also characterize when ordinary densities exist.

- B. Adamczewski, M. Drmota, C. Müllner, “(Logarithmic) densities for automatic sequences along primes and squares,” Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 375 (2022), 455–499, DOI.

The word “any” quantifies over a sequence chosen before the prime limit. It is not uniform over a family whose base is the running prime.

### Mauduit–Rivat digital methods

The Mauduit–Rivat prime results likewise fix a radix and a digital function before sending the prime-index cutoff to infinity:

- C. Mauduit and J. Rivat, “Sur un problème de Gelfond: la somme des chiffres des nombres premiers,” Ann. of Math. 171 (2010), 1591–1646, DOI.

- C. Mauduit and J. Rivat, “Prime numbers along Rudin–Shapiro sequences,” J. Eur. Math. Soc. 17 (2015), 2595–2642, DOI.

Their methods exploit long fixed-base digit expansions, carry propagation, and Fourier decay across many digit blocks. Here n mod p<p has one base-p digit. Even if one uses the full n with n<X^2 and p~X, it has at most two base-p digits. The number of digit blocks does not tend to infinity.

### Gowers norms

Byszewski–Konieczny–Müllner decompose one fixed automatic sequence into structured and Gowers-uniform parts and obtain uniformity in long intervals of its index.

- J. Byszewski, J. Konieczny, C. Müllner, “Gowers norms for automatic sequences,” Discrete Analysis 2023:4, DOI.

A Gowers norm needs many samples of the same function at additive configurations. Your expression samples one value from each different function c_p. There is no common ambient function on which their theorem can act.

## 4. Why varying the base makes matters worse, not better

The fixed-base proofs use a finite alphabet {0,...,k-1}, a fixed transition monoid, and repeated iteration of the same transitions over words whose lengths grow like log_k N. Their constants and spectral/carry estimates depend on that fixed automaton.

Thus:

1. the alphabet size tends to infinity;

1. the transition table changes with p;

1. the word length is one;

1. only one output is observed from each automaton;

1. there is no fixed symbolic dynamical system or fixed p-kernel being averaged.

The fact that the rational diagonal is defined over the integers gives a common arithmetic origin, but Christol/Furstenberg is a characteristic-by-characteristic statement. Rowland–Yassawi construct automata separately modulo each p^alpha; this is excellent for proving congruences at a fixed prime but supplies no horizontal cancellation as p varies.

Cobham's theorem is also irrelevant: it constrains one sequence that is automatic in two multiplicatively independent fixed bases. Here one has different sequences in different characteristics, not one sequence recognized in many bases.

## 5. The actual bottleneck

To use the automatic literature, one would need to prove something substantially new, for example one of the following:

1. the horizontal indicator

1. the first-block sets

1. the condition b_r=0 mod p admits a bounded-conductor Chebotarev/trace-function parametrization in the pair (p,r);

1. a small-CRT dispersion theorem prevents the residues n mod p from aligning with Z_p for many primes.

None of these follows from automaticity. They are precisely the horizontal arithmetic/equidistribution inputs isolated in the previous Apéry-gcd analysis.

## Final answer

The single-digit subtlety kills the automatic-sequence-along-primes route outright as a black-box argument. The prime in those theorems is the input index of one fixed-base sequence. In your problem it is the varying characteristic/base labeling a different automaton, and n mod p is only a one-symbol lookup. Varying p does not re-enable digit orthogonality; it permits arbitrary first-digit tables and removes the repeated fixed-base structure on which all cited results depend.
