# 9. What extra input would actually make density imply pointwise?

One would need a theorem not supplied by the recurrence alone. Equivalent useful forms include:

1. Incidence expansion: every index incident to K bad primes forces cK-sized incidence at cN other indices, with many primes per index—not merely one prime per index.

1. Small-CRT dispersion: a four-prime or higher-moment bound excluding a common unusually small CRT representative. This is the D4/A_3 route already isolated.

1. A new cross-prime algebraic relation: the conditions

1. A strong divisibility law: something comparable to Lucas sequences, forcing a large radical of G_N into many neighboring G_m. The Apéry sequence explicitly lacks this structure, and (2.7) rules out the most direct version.

Any proof of one of these would be a real breakthrough. But it would not be “density plus recurrence” alone; it would be precisely the missing horizontal non-concentration theorem.

The adversarial construction from the previous round captures the logical issue: sparse, palindromic, nonconsecutive zero sets can all be arranged to contain N mod p for a chosen N, creating one huge spike and clean immediate neighbors. Preventing that arrangement requires using the fact that all sets arise from the single global Apéry sequence—the hard cross-prime arithmetic.
