## Executive verdict

“Not found” means that I checked the named papers and ran targeted searches through the relevant congruence, Lucas-property, Apéry-like, polynomial-recurrence, value-distribution, and Ramanujan-Machine literature. It is not a logically exhaustive proof that no obscure source exists. Publication wording should therefore use “to our knowledge”.

- Ira Gessel, “Some congruences for Apéry numbers,” Journal of Number Theory 14 (1982), 362–368. DOI.

This reduces the question “does p divide some Apéry number?” to the first block: a zero digit B_r≡0 propagates to every index whose base-p expansion contains r. Conversely, if no first-block term vanishes, no term vanishes. It does not bound how many zero digits there can be.

### Beukers and later supercongruence work

Beukers proves deeper congruences relating Apéry numbers and modular forms:

- F. Beukers, “Some congruences for the Apéry numbers,” Journal of Number Theory 21 (1985), 141–155. DOI.

- F. Beukers, “Another congruence for the Apéry numbers,” Journal of Number Theory 25 (1987), 201–210. DOI.

The Osburn–Sahu, Osburn–Sahu–Straub, Amdeberhan–Tauraso, and modern Gessel–Lucas/supercongruence papers likewise establish congruences of individual terms or digit-factorization schemes. I did not find a theorem in this family estimating Z(p) as p varies.

Representative later references include:

- R. Osburn and B. Sahu, “Supercongruences for Apéry-like numbers.” arXiv:0906.3413.

- E. Rowland, R. Yassawi, and C. Krattenthaler, “Lucas congruences for the Apéry numbers modulo p^2.” arXiv:2005.04801.

- A. Straub, “Gessel–Lucas congruences for sporadic sequences,” Monatshefte für Mathematik 203 (2024), 883–898. DOI.

These are structurally important but are not distribution estimates for the first block.

## 1.2 Rowland–Yassawi: fixed-prime automata, not a uniform zero-count bound

Eric Rowland and Reem Yassawi construct finite automata for diagonal sequences modulo prime powers and apply them to Apéry numbers:

- E. Rowland and R. Yassawi, “Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational functions,” Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux 27 (2015), 245–288. DOI; arXiv:1310.8635.

This gives exact decidability and automata for a fixed modulus. It does not produce a p-uniform estimate for the number of zero states among B_0,...,B_{p-1}.

The paper’s Open Question 3.26 asks whether there are infinitely many primes p for which

## 1.3 Malik–Straub: palindrome and zero-free-prime conjecture

Amita Malik and Armin Straub prove Lucas congruences for all sporadic Apéry-like sequences and examine divisibility:

- A. Malik and A. Straub, “Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers,” Research in Number Theory 2 (2016), Article 5. DOI; arXiv:1508.00297.

Their Conjecture 6.3 predicts that the proportion of primes dividing no Apéry number is e^(-1/2). Again, this is a conjectural distribution over primes, not a deterministic bound on Z(p) for each prime.

A potentially confusing point: their Theorem 6.6 proves long terminal blocks of zeros modulo p for certain other Cooper sporadic sequences, notably sequences customarily labelled s_7 and s_18. ## 1.5 Conclusion for part (a)

Result found: structural congruences, automata for fixed p, palindromicity, computations, and conjectures about zero-free primes.

Result not found: a deterministic, uniform-in-p sublinear estimate for Z(p).

Accordingly, a publication-safe claim is:

To our knowledge, Z(p)≪p^(2/3) is the first uniform power-saving upper bound for the number of zeros of the classical Apéry sequence in one base-p digit block.

Do not strengthen this to an unqualified “first ever” without a MathSciNet/zbMATH expert search or author correspondence.

# 4. GCD of the two Apéry solutions

## 4.1 Normalization must be fixed first

There are several related pairs called “the two Apéry solutions”:

1. the integral Apéry number B_n and the rational companion that converges after division;

1. an integer companion obtained by multiplying by lcm(1,...,n)^3 or another denominator-clearing factor;

1. numerator and denominator sequences (p_n,q_n) of an inflated polynomial continued fraction;

1. deflated versions of those sequences.

GCD growth is not invariant under inflation: multiplying both recurrence solutions at level n by a common product changes the gcd by that product. Any theorem must therefore print the exact initial conditions and scaling defining a_n and b_n, and explain which predictable factorial/lcm factor has already been removed.
