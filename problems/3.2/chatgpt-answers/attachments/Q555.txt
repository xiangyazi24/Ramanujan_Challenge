## 6.1 A concrete Bézout-height obstruction

Let a(n),b(n) be fixed nonzero integer polynomials, or more generally integers of size e^{o(n)}. Then:

- if a(n)\ne0 eventually, the term a(n)D_n has exponent 2b and dominates b(n)S_n;

- if a=0, the remaining term has exponent s>0.

Thus every nonzero fixed-complexity same-index combination

has exponential height. In particular, ordinary polynomial Bézout coefficients cannot produce the desired subexponential certificate.

This does not prove that the gcd is exponential. It proves that a successful Bézout identity must use coefficients whose order/degree grows with n and must create exponential signed cancellation.

Existing Subspace-Theorem gcd results for linear recurrence sequences do not apply: D_s and S_s are polynomial-coefficient recurrences with a moving truncation, not fixed constant-coefficient exponential polynomials.

## Reopen D: a new pointwise P-recursive gcd theorem

Prove a theorem tailored to the seven section pairs

using their explicit local singularities, Newton polygons, or a same-index arithmetic norm. Existing gcd theorems for constant-coefficient linear recurrences do not cover this situation.
