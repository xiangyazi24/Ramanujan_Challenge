# 4. Frobenius-parameter Dwork congruence

We use the strong congruence of Samol–van Straten and Mellit–Vlasenko in its coefficient-ring form.

## Lemma 4.1: coefficient-ring form

Let (R) be a (p)-torsion-free (p)-adically complete ring with a Frobenius lift

[

qquad

sigma(r)equiv r^ppmod p.

]

Let (Psiin R[u_1^{pm1},ldots,u_t^{pm1}]), and assume that the origin is the only interior lattice point of its Newton polytope. Put

[

B_m=operatorname{CT}Psi^m.

]

Then, for (a,mge0) and (sge1),

[

oxed{

B_a,sigma(B_{lfloor a/pfloor+mp^{s-1}})pmod{p^sR}.

]

### Why this is the published Dwork proof, not a new hypothesis

For (R=mathbf Z_p) with the identity Frobenius lift, (4.1) is the strong Dwork congruence

[

b_{a+mp^s}b_{lfloor a/pfloor}equiv

b_ab_{lfloor a/pfloor+mp^{s-1}}pmod{p^s}

]

proved by Samol–van Straten and reproved through ghost terms by Mellit–Vlasenko.

Their proof works verbatim over (R). The first ghost identity is obtained from

[

]

All subsequent steps use only:

1. divisibility of the ghost terms by powers of (p);

1. containment of their Newton polytopes;

1. the fact that the origin is the unique interior lattice point;

1. coefficientwise ring operations.

Replacing each occurrence of ordinary coefficient Frobenius by (sigma) changes none of those arguments. Thus (4.1) is the direct Frobenius-ring version of the published proof.

# 8. Tail-ideal theorem and (Gamma_nmid A_n)

Let (w_{J+1},ldots,w_Ninmathbf Z) be arbitrary outer weights, and define the nonconstant coefficient vector

[

C_d=sum_{k=J+1}^{N}w_kA_{k,d}.

]

Using (T),

[

egin{aligned}

sum_{k=J+1}^{N}w_kF_k

&=sum_{k=J+1}^{N}w_ksum_{d=1}^{N}A_{k,d}z_d^{(J)}\

&=sum_{d=1}^{N}C_dz_d^{(J)}.

end{aligned}

]

Therefore

[

oxed{

sum_{k=J+1}^{N}w_kF_kin(C_1,ldots,C_N)subsetmathbf Z.

}

]

This is the tail-ideal theorem.

If the constant coefficient of the transform is

[

C_0=sum_{k=0}^{J}w_kF_k,

]

then the complete weighted sum is

[

sum_{k=0}^{N}w_kF_k

=C_0+sum_{d=1}^{N}C_dz_d^{(J)}.

]

Hence

[

oxed{gcd(C_0,C_1,ldots,C_N)midsum_{k=0}^{N}w_kF_k.}

]

In the truncated Legendre–Euler construction, the complete weighted sum is the Apéry number (A_n), while the gcd on the left is (Gamma_n). Thus

[

oxed{Gamma_nmid A_n.}

]

This holds for every cutoff and every integral outer weight vector for which the coefficient matrix is the one in the question.

# 9. The column lattice is not generally saturated

The theorem does not say that the columns span all of (mathbf Z^{N-J}). They need not.

For (J=1), (N=3),

[

A=egin{pmatrix}

4&-1&0\

0&6&-2

end{pmatrix}.

]

The gcd of its (2	imes2) minors is

[

gcd(24,8,2)=2,

]

so its column lattice has index (2) in (mathbf Z^2). Nevertheless,

[

(F_2,F_3)^T=(10,56)^T

=A(2,-2,-34)^T.

]

Thus tail membership is a special arithmetic property of the Franel vector, not a consequence of saturation.

Likewise, arbitrary integer input sequences fail. For example, replace the Franel values by

[

1,0,1,0,1.

]

The analogous fourth ghost is

[

Q_4(alpha)=-rac{alpha(alpha-1)(alpha^2+alpha-3)}3,

]

so (Q_4'(1)=1/3). The corresponding (z_4) is not integral. This hostile check isolates exactly where the reflexive-Laurent/Dwork input enters.
