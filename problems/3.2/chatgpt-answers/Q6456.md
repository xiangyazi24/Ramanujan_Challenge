# 2. The standard Poisson approximation theorem used

## Arratia–Goldstein–Gordon

**[STANDARD, cited]** R. Arratia, L. Goldstein, L. Gordon, “Two Moments Suffice for Poisson Approximations: The Chen–Stein Method,” *Annals of Probability* 17 (1989), 9–25, Theorem 1; DOI:

https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176991491

Use the total-variation convention

```text
d_TV(mu,nu) = sup_B |mu(B)-nu(B)| = (1/2)||mu-nu||_1.
```

The form needed here is as follows.

Let `(I_alpha)_{alpha in J}` be Bernoulli variables, let

```text
p_alpha = E I_alpha,
W       = sum_alpha I_alpha,
lambda  = sum_alpha p_alpha,
```

and choose a neighborhood `B_alpha subset J` containing `alpha`. Put

```text
b1 = sum_alpha sum_{beta in B_alpha} p_alpha p_beta,

b2 = sum_alpha sum_{beta in B_alpha, beta != alpha}
       E[I_alpha I_beta],

b3 = sum_alpha E | E[ I_alpha-p_alpha
                       | sigma(I_beta : beta notin B_alpha) ] |.
```

Then

```text
d_TV( Law(W), Poisson(lambda) ) <= 2(b1+b2+b3).           (2.1)
```

No reversibility is required.

I will verify all three quantities directly.

# 3. From `L^2` block mixing to pointwise mixing

For `m>=0`, define

```text
d_p(m) = sup_x || delta_x K_p^m - pi ||_TV.              (3.1)
```

## Step 3.1 — point-mass loss is exactly `sqrt p`

**[PROVED]** The density of `delta_x` relative to `pi` is `q 1_{x}`. Hence

```text
|| q 1_x - 1 ||_{L^2(pi)} = sqrt(q-1) = sqrt p.           (3.2)
```

If `mu` has density `g` relative to `pi`, then `mu K_p^m` has density `(K_p^*)^m g`. Since

```text
||(K_p^*)^m-Pi||_{2->2} = ||K_p^m-Pi||_{2->2},
```

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

```text
d_p(m)
 <= (1/2) sqrt p ||K_p^m-Pi||_{2->2}.                    (3.3)
```

## Step 3.2 — BM persists for all larger gaps

**[PROVED]** Since `K_p` is an `L^2(pi)` contraction and `K_p Pi = Pi`, for `m>=L_p`,

```text
K_p^m-Pi = K_p^{m-L_p}(K_p^{L_p}-Pi),
```

so

```text
||K_p^m-Pi||_{2->2}
 <= ||K_p^{L_p}-Pi||_{2->2}.                             (3.4)
```

Combining BM, (3.3), and (3.4),

```text
d_p(m) <= C p^{-1-theta}   for every m>=L_p.             (3.5)
```

This calculation explicitly handles the “`K K^*` is not `K^2`” danger: a bound on `K K^*` is useful because it bounds a singular value of `K`, not because it is an actual two-step transition.

# 4. A two-sided mixing lemma for the `b3` term

The AGG conditional sigma-field contains both the remote past and the remote future. The following elementary lemma supplies the needed estimate.

## Lemma 4.1

Let `(Y_j)_{j in Z}` be a stationary Markov chain with stationary law `pi`, transition kernel `K`, and reversed kernel `K^*`. Suppose

```text
sup_x ||delta_x K^m-pi||_TV <= d,
sup_x ||delta_x (K^*)^m-pi||_TV <= d.                    (4.1)
```

For a bounded function `h:S->[-1,1]`, put `J_i=h(Y_i)` and

```text
G_i = sigma(Y_j : j <= i-m) join sigma(Y_j : j >= i+m).
```

Then

```text
E | E[J_i-EJ_i | G_i] | <= 4d.                           (4.2)
```

### Proof

**[PROVED]** Couple the remote future to an independent stationary future. Conditional on `Y_i=x`, the law of `Y_{i+m}` is `delta_x K^m`, so maximal coupling makes `Y_{i+m}` equal to an independent `pi`-distributed state except on an event of probability at most `d`; using identical later transition randomness couples the entire future tail. Apply the same construction backward in time with the reversed kernel. A union bound shows that the joint law of

```text
(Y_i, remote past, remote future)
```

differs in total variation by at most `2d` from the law in which `Y_i` is independent of both remote tails.

By duality,

```text
E | E[J_i-EJ_i | G_i] |
 = sup_{|H|<=1, H G_i-measurable} E[(J_i-EJ_i)H].
```

The integrand has absolute value at most one after replacing `J_i` by `(J_i+1)/2` and absorbing an inessential factor two. The difference between joint and product expectations is therefore at most `4d`. This proves (4.2). `square`

For the Apéry chain the forward and reverse bounds are the same because

```text
||(K_p^*)^m-Pi|| = ||K_p^m-Pi||.
```

# 7. Renewal interpretation and the exact intensity

Let

```text
T_1 < T_2 < ...
```

be successive visit times to `infinity`.

## Step 7.1 — i.i.d. post-hit excursions

**[PROVED]** Whenever `X_{T_k}=infinity`,

```text
X_{T_k+1}=0
```

deterministically. The increments after time `T_k` are independent of the past because the `U_j` are i.i.d. with replacement. Thus

```text
T_{k+1}-T_k
```

are i.i.d. after the first visit, with the law

```text
1 + tau_{0->infinity}.                                   (7.1)
```

So the visit process is a delayed renewal process.

## Step 7.2 — exact mean excursion length

**[STANDARD: Kac recurrence formula; verification inline]** BM implies mixing, hence irreducibility on the relevant class and uniqueness of `pi`. Kac’s formula gives

```text
E_infinity T_infinity^+ = 1/pi(infinity) = p+1.           (7.2)
```

Because the first step is deterministically `infinity -> 0`,

```text
E_0 tau_{0->infinity} = p.                               (7.3)
```

Thus the renewal intensity is exactly

```text
1/(p+1).                                                  (7.4)
```

Over `floor(tp)` steps, the stationary mean count is exactly

```text
floor(tp)/(p+1) = t p/(p+1)+O_t(1/p).                    (7.5)
```

The restart creates a one-step refractory period, but the probability of any further return within a fixed number of steps is only `O(1/p)` by (1.6). Hence the extremal index is one and the limit is ordinary Poisson, not compound Poisson.

# 8. What HYPOTHESIS CG does and does not provide

Recall CG:

```text
|| K_p K_p^* - Pi - R_p || <= C p^{-delta},
rank(R_p) <= D.                                           (8.1)
```

## Step 8.1 — if `R_p=0`, CG is more than enough

**[PROVED]** Since `K_p Pi=Pi` and `Pi K_p^*=Pi`,

```text
(K_p-Pi)(K_p-Pi)^* = K_p K_p^* - Pi.                    (8.2)
```

Therefore

```text
||K_p-Pi||_{2->2}
 <= C^{1/2} p^{-delta/2}.                                 (8.3)
```

Hence

```text
||K_p^m-Pi|| <= C^{m/2}p^{-m delta/2}.                   (8.4)
```

Choose any fixed integer `m` satisfying

```text
m delta/2 > 3/2 + theta.                                 (8.5)
```

Then BM(theta) holds with a constant block length. A burn-in of `ceil(c log p)` is therefore far more than necessary. For example, choosing

```text
m > 4/delta
```

gives `theta=1/2` after slightly increasing `m`, and the final error is `O_t(p^{-1/2})` up to the sharper `log p/p` term.

## Step 8.2 — rank alone is insufficient

**[PROVED counterexample to the abstract implication]** Split a finite state space into two equal blocks and let `K` send every point to the uniform distribution on its own block. Then `K` is doubly stochastic and

```text
K K^* = K = Pi + R
```

with `rank(R)=1`. Thus (8.1) holds with zero error for every `delta`. But a target contained in one block is never hit from the other block, so no uniform-in-initial-state Poisson theorem is possible.

This counterexample is not claimed to be the Apéry kernel. It proves that the numerical data `(delta, rank R_p)` do not logically control the exceptional modes. Their actual action must be used.

Therefore there is **no formula**

```text
eta = eta(delta, rank R_p)
```

from CG alone.

## Step 8.3 — the minimal sufficient strengthening

A clean operator statement is BM(theta). An equivalent probabilistic statement sufficient for the proof is

```text
sup_x ||delta_x K_p^{L_p}-pi||_TV
 + sup_x ||delta_x (K_p^*)^{L_p}-pi||_TV
 <= C p^{-1-theta},                                      (8.6)
```

with `L_p=O(log p)`, together with the same bound for the burn-in distribution. BM implies (8.6) by (3.3).

This is the exact interface needed by the rare-event proof.

# 9. How classified exceptional modes would imply BM

The following is the natural finite-dimensional completion of CG.

## [HYPOTHESIS EX(D,rho)]

There is a decomposition of the mean-zero space into a contracting bulk and an exceptional space of dimension at most `D`, with a power bound

```text
||K_p^ell-Pi||_{2->2}
 <= A_D [ rho^ell
          + (C_1 p^{-delta/2})^{floor(ell/(D+1))} ],       (9.1)
```

where `0<=rho<1` and `A_D,C_1` are independent of `p`.

The factor `D+1` allows an exceptional mode to pass through a finite chain of classified correspondence modes before entering the CG-contracting bulk. If the exceptional block is actually reducing, `D+1` can be replaced by `1`.

## Step 9.1 — quantitative consequence

**[PROVED from EX]** Put

```text
gamma = -log rho > 0
```

and take

```text
L_p = ceil(c log p).
```

The second term of (9.1) is super-polynomially small in `p`. The first is

```text
rho^{L_p} <= p^{-c gamma}.                                (9.2)
```

Therefore BM(theta) holds for every

```text
theta < c gamma - 3/2,                                   (9.3)
```

provided

```text
c gamma > 3/2.                                           (9.4)
```

The final Poisson exponent may be taken as

```text
eta < min(c gamma-3/2, 1/2).                             (9.5)
```

For example, `c=2/gamma` yields every `eta<1/2`.

If the explicit finite block calculation proves

```text
rho <= 1-kappa/D,
```

then `gamma>=kappa/D`, so

```text
c = 2D/kappa
```

again yields every `eta<1/2`.

This is the correct way rank enters: through a proved escape or contraction rate of the finite exceptional block, not through rank by itself.

## Step 9.2 — nilpotent exceptional block

**[PROVED conditional algebra]** If the classified exceptional modes form a transient/nilpotent block of length at most `D`, then one may take `rho=0`. A constant block length of order

```text
(D+1) ceil(4/delta)
```

already gives BM with `theta=1/2`; the logarithmic burn-in requested in the question is then automatic.

**[PROVED]** Put



# 12. Exact use of with-replacement independence

The annealed proof uses the i.i.d. uniform variables `U_j` essentially at the following places.

1. **[ESSENTIAL] Time homogeneity.** The same kernel `K_p` governs every step, so correlations are expressed by powers `K_p^s`.

2. **[ESSENTIAL] Stationary path construction.** A bi-infinite stationary Markov chain with transition `K_p` is used in the `b3` argument.

3. **[ESSENTIAL] Post-hit renewal.** After a hit, the future increments are independent of the past, so the excursions from `0` are i.i.d.

4. **[ESSENTIAL] Burn-in coupling.** Once two copies agree at the burn-in time, the same independent future increments make their entire future paths agree.

5. **[ESSENTIAL] Remote-tail coupling.** The Markov property and fresh transition randomness allow coupling at time `i+L_p` and then coupling the whole future tail.

6. **[ESSENTIAL] Stationary covariance formula.** The identity

```text
P(X_i=infinity, X_{i+s}=infinity)
  = pi(infinity) K_p^s(infinity,infinity)
```

uses a homogeneous Markov kernel.

7. **[ESSENTIAL] Uniform root bound at every step.** Conditional on the current state, the next `u` is uniform over all `p-1` allowed values, giving the denominator `p-1` in (1.3).

For the true Apéry word, the parameters are used once, without replacement, and in the rigid order

```text
0,1,...,p-2.
```

Then the remaining-parameter set depends on the entire past, the transition kernel is time-inhomogeneous, the excursion laws are not i.i.d., and `K_p^s` no longer represents the relevant correlations. None of those replacements is justified here. That is precisely **[GAP-QUENCHED-ORDER]**.
