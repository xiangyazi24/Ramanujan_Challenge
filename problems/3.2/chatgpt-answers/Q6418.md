## 1.3 Nearby computational literature

Fernando Gouvêa’s Non-Ordinary Primes: A Story, Experimental Mathematics 6 (1997), 195–205, is the classical computational reference. It studies the six level-one eigenforms of weights (12,16,18,20,22,26), using Rankin–Cohen formulas to search to (10^6). It is the source of the familiar random-residue (1/p), hence (loglog x), heuristic. It does not include level (8), weight (4), or the sequence 8.4.a.a.

The Gouvêa–Mazur slope literature is adjacent but addresses a different quantifier: it fixes a residue characteristic (p) and varies weights/forms in a (p)-adic family. See Gouvêa–Mazur, Families of modular eigenforms, Math. Comp. 58 (1992), and the later counterexample of Buzzard–Calegari, arXiv:math/0311361. Those results do not enumerate the varying-prime diagonal (pmid a_p(f)) for one fixed form.

Jin–Ma–Ono, A Note On Non-ordinary Primes, arXiv:1511.06725, proves that one can construct eigenforms nonordinary at any prescribed finite set of primes. It is an existence theorem in the form variable, not a counting theorem for one fixed form.

Booker–Hathi–Mossinghoff–Trudgian, Wolstenholme and Vandiver primes, arXiv:2101.11157, is indeed the right computational genre—extremely sparse congruence primes found by a large specialized scan—but its predicates involve Bernoulli and Euler numbers, not (a_p(8.4.a.a)).

## 2.4 Why the Beukers/Zudilin constraint does not change (1/p) into a huge power saving

Zudilin’s theorem makes nonordinarity look much stronger: every coefficient of a degree (4(p-1)) polynomial must vanish modulo (p). If those were independent conditions, the event would be fantastically rarer than (1/p).

They are not independent. The proof obtains a coefficient-wise congruence in which the whole polynomial is, modulo (p), a fixed rational polynomial factor multiplied by the single scalar (a_p). Thus all coefficient vanishings collapse to the one condition (a_pequiv0pmod p). The Beukers hypergeometric/Apéry realization supplies a powerful test and the parity interpretation in the project, but it does not by itself justify replacing the (1/p) heuristic by (p^{-d}).

## 2.5 Infinitude status

The independent Bernoulli model with probabilities (1/p) predicts infinitely many nonordinary primes almost surely, because (sum_p1/p) diverges. So infinitude is the natural conjectural expectation.

There is currently no analogue of Elkies’s theorem for this weight-four form, and no theorem proving infinitely many nonordinary primes for any particular non-CM form of this type. The complementary ordinary-prime problem is also remarkably hard in the diagonal setting: Zudilin notes that, for a concrete non-CM newform of weight greater than three, even proving infinitely many ordinary primes is presently out of reach. Therefore all (loglog x) assertions here are heuristic, not theorem.

## 4.2 The extra status of (p=11)

The prime (11) has an additional, logically distinct property. Boylan’s classification shows that (11) is an exceptional prime of Type III for (eta(2z)^4eta(4z)^4), meaning the global residual projective image is exceptional. This is restated as Theorem 1.4 in O’Sullivan–Stone–Swati–Jin, Exceptional congruences for eta-quotient newforms, arXiv:2511.16039; published in Ramanujan Journal 69 (2026), article 25, DOI 10.1007/s11139-025-01296-z. The original parallel treatment is Kiming–Verrill, arXiv:math/0311082, published in J. Number Theory 110 (2005), 236–266.

This global exceptional-image property is not synonymous with nonordinarity. A nonordinary prime can still have a large global residual image; the condition is local at the same prime. The coincidence at (11) makes it sensible to regard (11) as a potentially unrepresentative calibration point.
