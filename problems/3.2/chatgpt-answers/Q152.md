# Literature status of primitivity for the Apéry approximants

## Executive verdict

I found no established classical name for the specific assertion

or for the equivalent statement that the Apéry approximant a_n/b_n has no additional exponential cancellation beyond the denominator clearing already forced by d_n.

The closest existing terminology is factorial reduction for polynomial continued fractions. In that language your conjecture says, up to an index shift and a bounded factor coming from the normalization by 6, that the Apéry continued fraction has the minimal possible factorial-reduction gcd: only the classical forced factor

occurs at exponential scale. “Minimal Apéry factorial reduction” or “exponential primitivity of the Apéry approximants” would be accurate descriptive names, but I did not find either as a standard named conjecture.

The several things called a denominator conjecture in the Apéry/zeta/mirror-map literature concern different objects:

- common denominators of coefficients of hypergeometric linear forms in zeta values;

- integrality and root-integrality of mirror maps;

- p-adic valuations or Lucas congruences of one Apéry-like sequence.

They do not assert near-coprimality of the numerator and denominator of the classical Apéry approximants.

The main literature findings are summarized below.

- C. Krattenthaler and T. Rivoal, On the integrality of the Taylor coefficients of mirror maps, Duke Mathematical Journal 151 (2010), 175–218.

- C. Krattenthaler and T. Rivoal, On the integrality of the Taylor coefficients of mirror maps, II, Communications in Number Theory and Physics 3 (2009), 555–591.

## 2.3 Almkvist–Zudilin and Apéry-like tables

- G. Almkvist and W. Zudilin, Differential equations, mirror maps and zeta values, in Mirror Symmetry V, AMS/IP Studies in Advanced Mathematics 38 (2006), 481–515.

This work develops differential equations, mirror maps, and new Apéry-like rational approximations, including examples connected with \zeta(4). The associated lists organize recurrences, differential operators, integrality, and mirror-map data. I found no theorem there controlling the gcd of the two solutions in an Apéry approximation.

## 2.4 Straub–Zudilin and related Apéry-limit work

- A. Straub and W. Zudilin, Sums of Powers of Binomials, Their Apéry Limits, and Franel's Suspicions, International Mathematics Research Notices 2023, 9861–9879.

This paper determines Apéry limits for sums of powers of binomial coefficients and proves a weak version of Franel's recurrence-order conjecture under creative-telescoping assumptions. It does not study the gcd of paired approximants.

Two other nearby arithmetic references are:

- A. Malik and A. Straub, Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers, Research in Number Theory 2 (2016), article 5. They prove Lucas congruences for the known sporadic Apéry-like sequences and derive periodicity and prime-avoidance consequences.

- É. Delaygue, Arithmetic properties of Apéry-like numbers, Compositio Mathematica 154 (2018), 249–274. He proves lower bounds for p-adic valuations of factorial-ratio multisums, including Apéry, Domb, and Franel sequences, and establishes effective p-Lucas criteria.

These are strong one-sequence divisibility theorems, but neither is a paired-gcd theorem.

Conclusion: the “denominator conjectures” do not provide a previously named version of G_n=e^{o(n)}.

# 5. Stewart, Lucas sequences, and the holonomic boundary

There are powerful prime-factor theorems for constant-coefficient binary recurrences.

## 5.1 Lucas and Lehmer sequences

- C. L. Stewart, On divisors of terms of linear recurrence sequences, Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik 333 (1982), 12–31.

- C. L. Stewart, On divisors of Lucas and Lehmer numbers, Acta Mathematica 211 (2013), 291–314.

- Y. Bilu, G. Hanrot and P. M. Voutier, Existence of primitive divisors of Lucas and Lehmer numbers, Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik 539 (2001), 75–122.

Bilu–Hanrot–Voutier prove that every Lucas or Lehmer term of index greater than 30 has a primitive divisor. Stewart proves strong lower bounds for the greatest prime factor; in the 2013 paper the bound has the form

for sufficiently large n, subject to the paper's Lucas/Lehmer hypotheses.

These theorems exploit fixed characteristic roots, divisibility-sequence structure, cyclotomic factorization, and linear forms in logarithms. The Apéry recurrence

has coefficients varying with n and is not a Lucas divisibility sequence. The known proofs do not transfer.

## 5.2 A genuine holonomic result

- F. Luca, Prime divisors of binary holonomic sequences, Advances in Applied Mathematics 40 (2008), 168–179.

Luca considers rational sequences satisfying an order-two recurrence

with polynomial coefficients. Under a nondegeneracy condition excluding eventual constant-coefficient behavior, he proves that the product of the numerators and denominators of the nonzero terms up to index N has at least c\log N distinct prime factors.

This is an aggregate lower bound across many terms. It is not a pointwise upper bound for the prime factors of one term in an interval such as (\sqrt n,n].

## 5.3 Smooth parts in a nearby constant-coefficient model

- N. Balaji and F. Luca, Terms of Lucas sequences having a large smooth divisor, Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 66 (2023), 225–231.

A representative theorem in that paper states that, for fixed a>1, the Kn-smooth part of a^n-1 is a^{o(n)} for almost all n. This is a density-one theorem for a constant-coefficient exponential sequence. It is a useful analogue of your density-one result, but it is neither pointwise nor holonomic in the genuinely variable-coefficient sense.

Literature conclusion: I found no Stewart-style theorem proving a pointwise subexponential (\sqrt n,n]-part for a nontrivial polynomial-coefficient recurrence such as the Apéry sequence.

# 9. Precise answers

## (a) Named conjecture?

Not found. The closest modern concept is factorial reduction of polynomial continued fractions. Your assertion is equivalent, at exponential scale, to minimal factorial reduction for the Apéry continued fraction. The Krattenthaler–Rivoal denominator conjectures and mirror-map integrality conjectures are different.

## (b) Beukers, van der Poorten, and predictable factors?

I found no explicit gcd or near-lowest-terms theorem in the cited classical expositions. The documented predictable common factor is (n!/L_n)^3 in the inflated integer convergents. The bounded factor 6 is a normalization artifact. No general power of 2, binomial factor, or exact small-factor formula for the normalized G_n was found.

## (c) Luca–Shparlinski, Stewart, and upper interval parts?

Luca–Shparlinski prove density-one lower bounds for \omega(b_n) and the largest prime factor. I found no upper bound for the (\sqrt n,n]-part. Stewart's strong pointwise results are for Lucas/Lehmer sequences with fixed characteristic roots. Luca's holonomic theorem is an aggregate lower-bound theorem and does not supply the requested upper bound.

## (d) Any model theorem, and what happens for \binom{2n}{n}?

A density-one smooth-part theorem exists for constant-coefficient models such as a^n-1, but no pointwise theorem of the requested shape was found for a genuinely nonautonomous holonomic sequence. The central-binomial analogue fails:
