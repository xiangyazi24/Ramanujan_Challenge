# T3-2MINUS exponent LP audit

## Executive verdict

The banked inequalities do **not** beat the current \(T_3\ll p^{13/6}\) barrier. The full multi-scale linear program has the same optimum as the one-scale argument:

\[
Q_{p-2}\ll p^{5/3},\qquad T_3=6Q_{p-2}\ll p^{5/3}

is not what follows from the supplied toolkit; the actual optimum remains

\[
\boxed{T_3\ll p^{13/6}}

coming from the global energy tie-in plus the available \(Q_D\) information. No legal cascade of the master inequality improves the fixed point.

The missing exponent is not hidden in LP slack; it requires a genuinely new inequality controlling the balanced two-return core.

# 1. Exponent LP

Write

\[
D=p^\beta,
\quad
Q_D=p^{q(\beta)},
\quad
S_D=p^{s(\beta)},
\quad
M_D=p^{m(\beta)}.
\]

The banked constraints become:

## Multiplicity

From

\[
M_D\le 4D^{2/3},
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{m(\beta)\le \frac23\beta}.
\]

## Local collision energy

From

\[
Q_D\le D^{5/2},
\]

\[
\boxed{q(\beta)\le \frac52\beta}.
\]

## Degree bound

Since

\[
S_1(Y)\le \frac32Y^2,
\]


a direct lag sum gives

\[
\boxed{s(\beta)\le 2\beta}.
\]

## Global energy

The full energy identity gives

\[
S_1(p-2)=\frac{E-N}{2}\ll p^{3/2},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{s(1)\le\frac32}.
\]

## Cauchy

The inequality

\[
S_D^2\le N(S_D+2Q_D)
\]

means at exponent level

\[
2s(\beta)\le \max(1,s(\beta),q(\beta)).
\]

Whenever \(s(\beta)>1\), this forces

\[
\boxed{q(\beta)\ge 2s(\beta)}.
\]

It is a lower coupling, not an upper saving.

## Master inequality

For every pair \(H,D\), with

\[
H=p^\alpha,\quad D=p^\beta,
\quad q_H=\lceil H/D\rceil,
\]

we get

\[
Q_H
\le q_H H S_1(D-1)+q_HS_1(H).
\]

Exponentially:

first term:

\[
\alpha-\beta+\alpha+ s(\beta)
=2\alpha-\beta+s(\beta),
\]

second term:

\[
\alpha-\beta+\max(2\alpha, s(\alpha)).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
q(\alpha)\le
\max(2\alpha-\beta+s(\beta),
\alpha-\beta+\max(2\alpha,s(\alpha)))
}
\]

for every \(0\le\beta\le\alpha\).

The full family of constraints is exactly this continuum.

Assume each proposed improvement by an exponent parameter.

Suppose

\[

At the master step:

\[
2\alpha-\beta+(2-\eta)\beta
=2\alpha+(1-\eta)\beta.

The gain is only

\[
\eta\beta.

Because the optimal scale has β bounded away from 1, this gives a fractional gain.

Cheapness: medium.

\[

The resulting global gain propagates linearly.

Cheapness: **best among purely collision inputs**.

\[
\varepsilon>0

because the interpolation barrier only needs any improvement over sigma=1/2.

# 4. Nonlinear bootstrap checks

## 4.1 Master cascade

Let

\[
q_i=q(\beta_i).
\]

The cascade map induced by the master inequality is

\[
q_{i+1}\le
\max(2\beta_i-\beta_{i+1}+s(\beta_{i+1}),\ldots).
\]

Using

\[
s(\beta)=\min(2\beta,3/2),
\]

one gets the fixed point equation

\[
q=\max(q,13/6).
\]

Hence iteration converges to the same exponent.

No self-improvement occurs.

## 4.2 Cubic identity

The identity

\[
\sum_v m_v^3=T_3+3E-2N
\]

combined with Holder gives

\[
E^{3/2}\le (\sum m_v^3)(\sum m_v)^{1/2}.
\]

Since

\[
E\ll p^{3/2},
\]

this only reproduces

\[
T_3\gg p^2
\]

as a necessary scale. It gives no upper saving.

The reverse Holder direction also closes at the same point:

\[
T_3\le M E
\]

and \(M\) is still too large.

## 4.3 Fiber-size splitting

Split at

\[
m_v\le p^\theta.
\]

Small fibers contribute:

\[
\sum m_v^3\le p^\theta E.
\]

Large fibers contribute by multiplicity:

\[
\#\{v:m_v>p^\theta\}\le p^{3/2-2\theta}.
\]

Multiplying back gives the same fixed point.

Optimizing θ reproduces the LP exponent.

Therefore no nonlinear bootstrap beats the linear program.

# 5. Final answers

## (a) Least-confident step

The least-confident part is not the LP. It is the claim that no hidden nonlinear consequence exists beyond the listed inequalities. In particular, a new algebraic relation between different return lengths could invalidate the LP model.

The current toolkit treats \(C_p(a,g)\) as an incidence table with only marginal constraints. A genuine three-way constraint could break the fixed point.

## (b) Shared blind spot

The likely shared blind spot is the same as in earlier GAP-2D-SQRT rounds:

> the orbit may possess a clock-twisted low-rank object invisible to the marginal collision statistics.

All current inequalities see:

- individual gaps;
- pair collisions;
- fiber multiplicities.

They do not see a possible finite-rank evolution of the ordered transfer word.

If such a bispectral closure exists, the LP is irrelevant. If it does not, the present exponent barrier is likely structural.

**Bank conclusion:** within the audited toolkit, \(13/6\) is the fixed point. The cheapest new theorem is a balanced-core two-return estimate, not another marginal multiplicity improvement.
