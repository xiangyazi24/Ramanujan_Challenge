
## Theorem 4 — exact renewal/addition identity

For all `m,g>=0`,

```text
N_(m+g+1)(s)
 =N_(m+1)(s)N_g(s+m+1)
  -(s+m+1)^6N_m(s)N_(g-1)(s+m+2).                  (2.10)
```

Therefore

```text
N_(m+g+1)(s)
 =N_(m+1)(s)N_g(s+m+1) mod N_m(s).                 (2.11)
```

Algebraically,

```text
(N_m,N_(m+g+1)):N_(m+1)^infinity
 = (N_m,N_g(s+m+1)):N_(m+1)^infinity.              (2.12)
```

Over `F_p`, at every regular root of `N_m`, `N_(m+1)` is nonzero, and hence

```text
N_m(s)=N_(m+g+1)(s)=0
 <=> N_m(s)=0 and N_g(s+m+1)=0.                    (2.13)
```

### Proof

For fixed `m`, both sides of (2.10), as functions of `g`, satisfy the recurrence starting at base `s+m+1`. At `g=0` the identity is tautological. At `g=1` it is exactly the recurrence for `N_(m+2)`. Induction proves (2.10).

At a regular root, the transfer matrix is invertible. The state at time `s+m` is infinity; the next map sends it to zero, so the next denominator `N_(m+1)(s)` is nonzero. Division in (2.11) gives (2.13). The saturated ideal identity is the polynomial version of the same argument. ∎

## Theorem 5 — adjacent coprimality; no renewal divisors

For every `m>=0`,


### Proof

Induct on `m`. A common divisor of `N_m` and `N_(m+1)` divides, by (1.1),

```text
(s+m)^6N_(m-1).
```

By the induction hypothesis it is coprime to `N_(m-1)`, so it must divide `(s+m)^6`. But (2.1) gives

```text
N_m(-m)=(-1)^m(m!)^3b_m!=0
```

over `Q`. Thus `s+m` is not a factor of `N_m`, and the common divisor is a unit. ∎

### Refutation of the proposed product formula

The first two polynomials already give

```text

N_2=P(s+1)P(s)-(s+1)^6,
N_2=-(s+1)^6 mod N_1.
```

Since `P(-1)=-5`, `N_1` and `s+1` are coprime, so `gcd(N_1,N_2)=1`.

Conceptually, a trajectory that hits infinity after `j` steps and again after `m` steps satisfies

```text

N_(m-j-1)(s+j+1)=0.                                 (2.15)
```

This is an intersection of two conditions. A product would encode their union. Nonautonomy shifts the second base point, so there is no dynatomic divisibility lattice analogous to `f^d(x)-x | f^m(x)-x` in autonomous dynamics.

# 5. Exact second moment and the singleton obstruction

For regular roots define

```text
d_H(s)=#{1<=m<=H:s in E_m^reg(p)},

```

Here `P_p(H)` is the number of primitive base points: each base is counted once, at its first return time.

## Theorem 6 — exact chaining second moment

Put

```text
C_p(m,g)
 =#{s in F_p:
      s in E_m^reg(p),
      s+m+1 in E_g^reg(p)}.                          (5.2)
```

Then

```text
Q_p(H)
 =sum_(m>=1,g>=1,m+g+1<=H) C_p(m,g),                (5.3)
```

and

```text
sum_s d_H(s)^2=R_p^reg(H)+2Q_p(H).                  (5.4)
```

### Proof

An unordered pair of return times `m<m'` has a unique representation

```text
g=m'-m-1>=1.
```

Theorem 4 identifies common roots with the event in (5.2). Summing all unordered pairs gives (5.3). Identity (5.4) is `d^2=d+2 choose(d,2)` summed over `s`. ∎

The formula in the question is therefore correct after fixing the ordered/unordered convention: without a factor `2`, the off-diagonal sum must range over ordered pairs.

## 5.1 Random prediction

If shifted root sets were independent, then

```text

Q_p(H)   ~ R_p^reg(H)^2/(2p).                        (5.5)
```

If `H=sqrt(p)` and `R~H`, the expected `Q` is only `O(1)`. Thus the conjectural process is dominated by singleton base points, not by chains with several returns.

## 5.2 Pair independence cannot prove the first moment

There is a sharp combinatorial countermodel. Let `H<=sqrt(p)` and assign to each `m` a reflection-symmetric set `E_m` of size comparable to `m`, respecting

```text

```

but choose all nonforced roots disjoint across `m`. This is possible with total size comparable to `H^2<=p`. Then

```text

```

The chaining law is satisfied vacuously because there are no common roots. This model respects every row-degree bound and the reflection law.

Therefore even the strongest conceivable statement


does not imply `R<<H`.

## 5.3 No dyadic bootstrap from pair correlations alone

Suppose `R(H_0)<<H_0` has already been proved. In the band

```text

```

each new polynomial may put all of its roots at base points unused below `H_0`. The repeated-return count remains zero while the new first-return mass is `O(H_0^2)`. Thus a bootstrap from small to large `H` must separately bound new primitive first returns.

This is the exact point where the proposed Chen-Stein recursion stops. Chen-Stein controls dependency among events once their intensities are known; it does not prove that the sum of the singleton intensities is small.

The composition identity greatly simplifies its meaning. Pairwise nonzero resultants and full Galois groups make fixed pairs accessible.

### Hardest step

At one prime `p`, bound how often `p` divides the entire growing resultant family as `(m,g)` range over a triangular region of size `H^2`. Individual height bounds or nonzero resultants give at best the pointwise degree budget and do not average over the family.

### Status

This route is genuinely aimed at (6.2), but even a complete success leaves clause (6.1) untouched.

## Route C — renewal/Chen-Stein bootstrap

Use (6.6) to imitate the already proved annealed renewal argument.

### Proof skeleton

1. Prove the primitive intensity bound (6.1).
2. Prove a weak shifted-pair bound (6.2), perhaps by a family resultant large sieve.
3. Split every return event into its first event and a later event.
4. Charge first events to `P`, later events to an unordered pair counted by `Q`.
5. Apply Theorem 7.

### Hardest step

Step 1. The renewal identity starts only after the first return and therefore cannot generate its own intensity bound.

### Verdict

This is the cleanest organizational skeleton, but not a self-contained bootstrap. It makes the hard input explicit rather than removing it.

## Route D — full two-parameter sheaf/correspondence

Construct a geometric object whose fibers in the discrete `m` direction encode the primitive `M_m`, and prove a large-sieve or product-monodromy theorem across the family.

### Hardest step

The degree/rank grows linearly with `m`; there is no fixed bounded-conductor sheaf on an algebraic `m`-line. One needs a genuinely new family-compatibility construction rather than a repackaging of each slice.

### Verdict

This is the strongest long route and the natural home of (7.1), but it is not a theorem already implicit in the recurrence.

# 8. Polynomial-space dynamical reformulation

The suggested finite-dimensional reformulation can be made exact, but it does not produce expansion.

Let

```text
A_p=F_p[s]/(s^p-s).
```

Evaluation gives an algebra isomorphism

```text
A_p isomorphic to product_(a in F_p) F_p.            (8.1)
```

Reduce every `N_m` in `A_p`. The recurrence acts on

```text
(N_m,N_(m-1)) in A_p^2
```

by multiplication with `P(s+m)` and `(s+m)^6`.

In the evaluation basis (8.1), multiplication by either polynomial is diagonal. Thus the putative high-dimensional orbit is exactly the direct product of the `p` independent scalar recurrences

```text
(N_m(a),N_(m-1)(a)),        a in F_p.                (8.2)
```

There is no mixing between coordinates and no spectral gap hidden in polynomial space.

Additional obstructions:

1. The state-space dimension is `2p`, not bounded independently of `p`.
2. Before reduction modulo `s^p-s`, the degree grows by `3` at every step. Truncating at `H` gives dimension `O(H)`, which is still growing at the target scale.
3. The event “the polynomial has a root” is not crossing one fixed hyperplane. It is the union of the `p` coordinate hyperplanes

   ```text
   {N(a)=0}, a in F_p,
   ```

   a divisor of complexity `p`.
4. Making the nonautonomous recurrence autonomous by adjoining the clock `m mod p` only produces a triangular skew product; it does not create expansion.

### Verdict on item 3

As a bookkeeping device this reformulation is exact. As an attack it is a dead end unless supplemented by a new theorem that couples the evaluation coordinates. Such a theorem would already be the missing family-compatibility/local-limit input in another language.

# 9. What `[SLIDING-WEIL-L]` does and does not contribute

For

```text
S_m(xi)=sum_(s in F_p)e_p(xi N_m(s)),
```

orthogonality gives

```text
r_m(p)
 =1+(1/p)sum_(xi!=0)S_m(xi),                         (9.1)

R_p(H)
 =H+(1/p)sum_(xi!=0)sum_(m<=H)S_m(xi).              (9.2)
```

## 9.1 Correction to the pointwise-Weil calculation

A Weil bound

```text
|S_m(xi)|<<deg(N_m)sqrt(p)<<m sqrt(p)
```

followed by the triangle inequality gives

```text
r_m<=1+O(m sqrt(p)),
```

not `r_m<=deg N_m`. The elementary polynomial root bound `r_m<=3m` is much sharper. Applying Weil separately to every nonzero `xi` loses a factor of about `sqrt(p)`.

## 9.2 The exact sufficient family exponential-sum statements

Put

```text
A_H(xi)=sum_(m<=H)S_m(xi).
```

Either of the following would suffice:

```text
|sum_(xi!=0)A_H(xi)|<<pH p^epsilon,                 (9.3)
```

or the stronger absolute estimate

```text
sum_(xi!=0)|A_H(xi)|<<pH p^epsilon.                 (9.4)
```

Equation (9.3), however, is exactly the desired root local limit after (9.2); it is a Fourier restatement, not a simplification.

Even the random-size fixed-`xi` estimate

```text
|A_H(xi)| about sqrt(pH)
```

would make the left side of (9.4) about

```text
p sqrt(pH).
```

At `H=sqrt(p)` this is `p^(7/4)`, while (9.4) asks for `p^(3/2+o(1))`. Thus an `m`-average before Weil is not by itself sufficient; one also needs cancellation or flattening in the `xi` direction.

## 9.3 The canonical flattening measure

Define the value-count measure

```text
nu_(p,H)(a)
 =#{(m,s):1<=m<=H, N_m(s)=a}.                       (9.5)
```

Its total mass is `pH`, so uniformity predicts `nu(a)~H`. The root theorem is precisely

```text
nu_(p,H)(0)<<H p^epsilon.                            (9.6)
```

A full family local limit would assert

```text
||nu_(p,H)||_infinity<<H p^epsilon.                  (9.7)
```

The Fourier transform of `nu` is `A_H(xi)`. This is the right “family flattening” formulation.

## 9.4 Why Bourgain-Gamburd flattening is not presently available

Bourgain-Gamburd arguments use repeated convolution in a fixed group, product growth, and nonconcentration in proper subgroups. Here:

- `nu_(p,H)` is not a convolution power;
- the addition formula (2.10) is bilinear and shifts the base `s`;
- the ordered transfer products are strongly correlated sliding products;
- the polynomial-space representation diagonalizes into the independent coordinates (8.2);
- no approximate subgroup or stationary random walk has been identified.

`[SLIDING-WEIL-L]` proves square-root cancellation for each **fixed** sliding length and each nondegenerate linear phase of the transfer matrix. It is valuable for fixed-lag correlations, the annealed theorem, and prospective control of clause (6.2). Its constants grow with `L`, and it does not yield the `L=sqrt(p)` local limit or the primitive intensity clause (6.1).

### Verdict on item 4

The gain must indeed come from inter-`m` structure, but the required statement is not ordinary Weil cancellation. It is the defining-characteristic `L^infinity` flattening (9.6), or an equivalent high-moment/convolution mechanism that has not yet been constructed.
