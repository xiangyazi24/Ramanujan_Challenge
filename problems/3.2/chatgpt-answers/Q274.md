# Verdict

Higher factorial moments are a real sufficient condition but, with the present inputs, a methodological mirage.

They have strictly more consequence than the pair moment: an expected-size bound for a fixed (kge3) would force a polynomial pointwise bound on (K(n)). But they have strictly less automatic CRT leverage: once the product modulus exceeds (X^2), CRT gives uniqueness of a lift, not the expected probability that the unique lift lies below (X^2). Recovering that probability is exactly the missing horizontal equidistribution statement, and the missing factor becomes (X^{k-2}), worse as (k) grows.

So the honest answer is:

- Yes, a proved third/fourth factorial-moment estimate would be a genuine breakthrough and would imply (K(n)ll X^{1-delta}).

- No, the regime does not become favorable merely because (p_1cdots p_k>X^2).

- No, Maynard--Tao, Erdős--Ko--Rado, Matomäki--Radziwiłł, or a standard large sieve supplies this estimate for an arbitrary sparse residue system.

- The proposed (k)-moment theorem should be regarded as a precise restatement of the cross-prime non-clustering conjecture, not as an independent black-box route around it.

# 1. Exact factorial-moment identity

Let

[

mathcal P_X:={ptext{ prime}:X<ple2X},qquad N:=X^2,

]

and

[

A_p:={0le n<N:nbmod pin Z_p}.

]

Then

[

K(n)=sum_{pinmathcal P_X}1_{A_p}(n).

]

For the falling factorial

[

(K)_k:=K(K-1)cdots(K-k+1),

]

we have exactly

[

T_k(X):=sum_{0le n<N}(K(n))_k.

tag{1.1}

]

Expanding,

[

T_k(X)

=

sum_{substack{p_1,dots,p_kinmathcal P_X\text{ordered distinct}}}

 sum_{z_iin Z_{p_i}}

1_{{operatorname{CRT}_{p_1cdots p_k}(z_1,dots,z_k)<N}}.

tag{1.2}

]

Here the CRT representative is taken in ([0,p_1cdots p_k)).

This is the exact object. There is no approximation yet.

Put

[

R_X:=sum_{pinmathcal P_X}|Z_p|,

qquad

A_X:=sum_{pinmathcal P_X}frac{|Z_p|}{p}.

tag{1.3}

]

The random-position prediction is

[

T_k(X)asymp N A_X^k.

tag{1.4}

]

Indeed, for a fixed prime/residue tuple, the expected proportion of representatives below (N) is

[

frac{N}{p_1cdots p_k}asymp X^{2-k}.

]

Summing gives (N A_X^k).

The unconditional CRT bound is instead

[

T_k(X)le R_X^k,

tag{1.5}

]

because each residue tuple contributes at most one lift once (p_1cdots p_k>N).

The ratio between (1.5) and the expected scale (1.4) is roughly

[

frac{R_X^k}{N A_X^k}asymp X^{k-2}

tag{1.6}

]

when (pasymp X).

That is the central audit:

For (kge3), CRT uniqueness leaves an (X^{k-2}) gap. The gap becomes larger, not smaller, as the moment order increases.

# 2. Correction to the proposed “trivial (X^2/log^k X)” scale

Suppose for orientation that (|Z_p|asymp1) on average. Then

[

#mathcal P_Xasympfrac{X}{log X},

qquad

R_Xasympfrac{X}{log X},

qquad

A_Xasympfrac1{log X}.

]

For (k=2), the modulus (p_1p_2asymp X^2=N). The elementary CRT count gives

[

T_2(X)ll R_X^2asympfrac{X^2}{log^2X},

]

which is also the random scale

[

N A_X^2asympfrac{X^2}{log^2X}.

]

This is why the pair moment cannot distinguish random spreading from total positional concentration.

For (kge3), however, the elementary uniqueness bound is

[

T_k(X)ll R_X^kasympfrac{X^k}{log^kX},

tag{2.1}

]

not (X^2/log^kX).

The bound

[

T_k(X)llfrac{X^2}{log^kX}

tag{2.2}

]

is already the desired higher-order equidistribution theorem. It saves the full factor (X^{k-2}) over (2.1).

Thus “the product modulus exceeds the interval” does only this:

- it changes “possibly many lifts” into “zero or one lift”;

- it does not show that only an (X^{2-k}) proportion of residue tuples have their unique lift in the interval.

That proportion is the theorem one still has to prove.

# 3. Adversarial alignment survives every moment

Fix one (n_0<N). For every (pinmathcal P_X), choose a sparse symmetric set containing

[

n_0bmod p.

]

For example, take

[

Z_p^{mathrm{adv}}

=

{n_0bmod p, p-1-(n_0bmod p)},

]

with harmless adjustments in the rare coincident/consecutive cases.

Then

[

]

and therefore

[

T_k(X)ge (K(n_0))_k

asympleft(frac{X}{log X}right)^k.

tag{3.1}

]

This saturates the trivial bound (2.1) for every fixed (k).

So reflection symmetry, no-consecutive zeros, and bounded cardinality do not help at any moment order. The same point (n_0) is simultaneously the small CRT representative for every chosen prime tuple.

The higher moment is therefore more diagnostic than the pair moment:

- the pair moment sees size (X^2/log^2X) both in the random model and in the fully aligned model;

- the third moment distinguishes (X^2/log^3X) from (X^3/log^3X);

- the fourth distinguishes (X^2/log^4X) from (X^4/log^4X).

But diagnosing the alignment does not provide a mechanism to rule it out for the actual Apéry zero sets.

# 5. Is there precedent for higher moments adding information?

## 5.1 Yes, but only when joint correlations are supplied

The cleanest precedent is Gallagher's conditional Poisson law for primes in short intervals:

- P. X. Gallagher, On the distribution of primes in short intervals, Mathematika 23 (1976), 4--9, DOI 10.1112/S0025579300016442.

The method computes factorial moments and, conditional on Hardy--Littlewood prime-(k)-tuple asymptotics, obtains the Poisson distribution. This is directly analogous to the desired program:

- first moment gives the intensity;

- all fixed factorial moments encode non-clustering;

- the Poisson conclusion follows once the (k)-point correlations are known.

But this is not a case where the higher moment is easier than the second. The Hardy--Littlewood (k)-tuple input is precisely the difficult higher-order correlation theorem.

Likewise, Brun/Poisson-sieve arguments work because the joint local density of a fixed finite set of divisibility conditions is multiplicative and can be averaged over an interval long enough relative to the product modulus. In the present problem, for (kge3), the interval is shorter than the product modulus, so the floor-error/least-representative term is the entire count.

## 5.2 Maynard--Tao is not such a precedent

Maynard's multidimensional Selberg sieve is:

- James Maynard, Small gaps between primes, Annals of Mathematics 181 (2015), 383--413, DOI 10.4007/annals.2015.181.1.7.

Its higher dimension improves the optimization of a specially constructed sieve weight over an admissible tuple of fixed shifts. The method still relies on distributional input for primes in arithmetic progressions, such as Bombieri--Vinogradov. It does not prove that arbitrary selected residues have randomly located CRT representatives.

The role of dimension there is:

exploit known local densities more efficiently,

not

manufacture joint equidistribution from cardinality data.

So Maynard--Tao gives no black-box (D_3) or (D_4) for (Z_p).

## 5.3 Matomäki--Radziwiłł is also not a pointwise analogue

Matomäki and Radziwiłł prove cancellation of multiplicative functions in almost all short intervals:

- Kaisa Matomäki and Maksym Radziwiłł, Multiplicative functions in short intervals, Annals of Mathematics 183 (2016), 1015--1056, DOI 10.4007/annals.2016.183.3.6.

Higher uniformity extensions include:

- Matomäki--Radziwiłł--Tao--Teräväinen--Ziegler, Higher uniformity of bounded multiplicative functions in short intervals on average, Annals of Mathematics 197 (2023), 739--857, DOI 10.4007/annals.2023.197.2.3.

These results use multiplicativity, Dirichlet-polynomial decompositions, and averaging over the interval start. They are not sup-norm theorems for an arbitrary sparse system of congruence targets. Their conclusion is deliberately “almost all intervals,” whereas the Apéry problem needs every (n).

They illustrate that higher-order uniformity can be proved with strong algebraic structure and averaging. They do not make the present higher CRT moment automatic.

## 5.4 Erdős--Ko--Rado points the wrong way

The Erdős--Ko--Rado theorem controls intersecting uniform set families under an explicit pairwise intersection hypothesis. It has no theorem saying that a family of CRT conditions cannot all pass through one common integer.

Indeed, the adversarial construction is the analogue of a star: all prime conditions share the common point (n_0). Stars are exactly the extremal objects in Erdős--Ko--Rado theory.

So EKR-type thinking explains why concentration is an extremal configuration; it does not exclude it without an additional non-star hypothesis.

# 6. Does a large sieve become favorable at higher (k)?

No. The standard large-sieve regime becomes worse.

The classical large sieve controls a quadratic form at many well-spaced frequencies with a constant of the shape

[

N+delta^{-1}.

]

For Farey fractions with denominators up to (Q), (delta^{-1}asymp Q^2), giving the familiar

[

N+Q^2.

]

A foundational reference is:

- P. X. Gallagher, The large sieve, Mathematika 14 (1967), 14--20, DOI 10.1112/S0025579300007968.

For a (k)-fold CRT modulus

[

q=p_1cdots p_kasymp X^k,

]

while the interval length is

[

N=X^2.

]

Thus a generic additive large sieve sees a conductor/spacing term on the order of

[

q^2asymp X^{2k},

]

far larger than (N). Tensoring to higher (k) does not improve the inequality; it magnifies the modulus.

Even a hypothetical sparse-moduli improvement cannot hold uniformly for arbitrary chosen residues, because the aligned system (Z_p^{mathrm{adv}}) is an explicit counterexample.

## 6.1 Gallagher's larger sieve does not directly apply

Gallagher's larger sieve bounds a set (Asubset[1,N]) if (Abmod p) occupies few residue classes for many primes. The high-level principle is that a large integer set cannot be simultaneously compressed modulo many primes.

Let

[

B_L:={n<N:K(n)ge L}.

]

There is no reason for (B_Lbmod p) itself to occupy few classes: different (nin B_L) may be certified by completely different sets of primes. A point in (B_L) need not lie in (Z_p) for a fixed positive proportion of all (p).

Pigeonholing a common (k)-subset of certifying primes reduces the problem exactly to (1.2), i.e. to (T_k). It does not create a new larger-sieve input.

## 6.2 No favorable phase transition at (q>N)

When (q>N), each congruence class contains at most one point of the interval. That is sometimes useful after one already knows which classes occur. But here the question is how many of the exponentially many CRT classes have their representative in the initial segment ([0,N)).

The large sieve controls quadratic averages of Fourier coefficients. It does not, for arbitrary selected classes, force their least positive representatives to equidistribute.

So the transition (q>N) is not favorable; it removes multiplicity while exposing the full positional problem.

# 7. The precise missing theorem

The clean formulation is an aggregate small-CRT discrepancy estimate:

[

boxed{

T_k(X)

ll C_k X^2

left(sum_{X<ple2X}frac{|Z_p|}{p}right)^k.

}

tag{D_k}

]

Equivalently, averaged over all distinct prime/residue tuples, the least CRT representative lands below (X^2) with the expected probability

[

asympfrac{X^2}{p_1cdots p_k}.

]

For (k=3) or (4), this is a powerful and concrete target. But it is not weaker than the missing horizontal equidistribution; it is one of its sharpest formulations.

A weaker power-saving version would already help. Suppose

[

T_k(X)ll X^{k-eta}/log^kX

tag{7.1}

]

in the average-(|Z_p|asymp1) setting. Then

[

sup K(n)ll X^{1-eta/k}/log X.

]

Thus any fixed power saving over the trivial (X^k/log^kX) produces a pointwise power saving. One does not need the full random scale (X^2/log^kX).

This may be the most realistic research target:

prove any aggregate power saving in the number of (k)-tuples whose CRT representative is anomalously small.

But such a saving must use genuine cross-prime arithmetic of the Apéry zero sets.

# 8. Direct answers

## (1) Does the higher factorial moment have more room?

Logically yes; arithmetically no.

It is strictly stronger than the pair moment and would yield a pointwise bound. But CRT alone gives (T_kll X^k/log^kX), not (X^2/log^kX). The desired factor (X^{2-k}) is exactly an unproved equidistribution of least CRT representatives.

The adversarial aligned construction defeats every fixed and growing moment.

## (2) Is there a relevant precedent?

There are precedents where factorial moments prove Poisson/non-clustering laws—Gallagher's conditional prime-short-interval theorem is the closest. In all such cases, the higher-order joint correlation input is the hard theorem.

Maynard--Tao, Matomäki--Radziwiłł, and EKR do not establish higher CRT non-clustering for arbitrary sparse residues. Their extra power comes from prime distribution, multiplicativity, averaging, or explicit intersection hypotheses that are absent here.

## (3) Does the large-sieve regime become favorable at higher (k)?

No. Generic large-sieve constants worsen because the product modulus grows like (X^k). The fact that this exceeds the interval length yields uniqueness only. It gives no control over which unique representatives are small.

# Final assessment

Higher moments are not vacuous: (D_3), (D_4), or even a power-saving form of (7.1) would be a genuine breakthrough and would solve the pointwise counting problem at polynomial strength.

But they are not presently an escape from the positional/equidistribution wall. They are a sharper way to state that wall. Unless the Apéry continuants, K3 geometry, or an integer resultant law supplies a specifically multi-prime restriction on the CRT representative, pursuing higher moments by standard sieve/large-sieve technology is a mirage.
