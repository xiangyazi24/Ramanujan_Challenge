# 7. Addition and Dodgson identities: useful for collisions, not isolated moving zeros

The continuant addition law splits an interval into two adjacent blocks. The Dodgson identity is

[

boxed{

-N_{h+1}(x)N_{h+1}(x+1)

Away from the cut-edge points, (7.1) rules out certain simultaneous neighboring zeros. The Bezout/addition identity similarly reduces a common-root condition for (N_d,N_e) to a common-root condition for two separated blocks.

These are powerful for:

- no-consecutive-zero statements;

- pair and triple collision counts;

- codegree bounds;

- exact resultants;

- restart identities.

They do not convert a single congruence (b_requiv0pmod p) into a second independent low-height congruence. At the endpoint, every such identity specializes to products or determinants containing the original (b_r).

This is why the Q338 structure improves density and collision estimates but does not yet control the pointwise moving radical.

# 10. What would constitute genuine progress

A successful Q338-based wall-break would need an identity of a type not presently supplied by the continuant algebra:

1. For each (n,q), a nonzero integer (J_{n,q}) with

1. a product over (q<f(n)log n) whose total logarithmic height is (o(n)); or

1. a second congruence at the same moving residue (r), independent of (b_r), which makes the relevant primes divide a genuinely small resultant.

The current identities do not provide any of these:

- a pair of Casoratians is an invertible coordinate transform;

- an endpoint evaluation is congruent to (b_r);

- a reflection resultant contains (b_r) in its boundary factor;

- addition and Dodgson control repeated zeros rather than singleton zeros.

Therefore the correct conclusion is not that Q338 is useless. It has identified the exact boundary of the method:

[

boxed{

text{The unresolved arithmetic is the horizontal distribution of isolated moving zeros, not the absence of exact continuant identities.}

}

]
