# Problem 3.2: the Q340 Borel cocycle is same-prime/local-time structure, not cross-prime coherence
## Executive verdict
Let
$$
P(t)=34t^3+51t^2+27t+5,
$$
$$
A(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(t)&-t^6\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B(t)=t^{-3}A(t)\in \mathrm{SL}_2
$$
for $`t\ne0`$. Q340 proves
$$
B(t)B(s)^{-1}\in B_{\mathrm{up}}
$$
and, for the interval product
$$
M_h(x)=B(x+h-1)\cdots B(x+1),
$$
the sliding identity
$$
M_h(x+1)=B(x+h)M_h(x)B(x+1)^{-1}.
$$
These are exact and useful, but they do **not** yield a nontrivial two-prime or four-prime CRT discrepancy estimate, and they do not give a pointwise bound for
$$
K_{\mathrm{low}}(n)
=
\#\{p>\sqrt n:p\mid b_{\,n\bmod p}\}.
$$
The precise reason is:
> The transfer identities compare different windows of one orbit over one coefficient field. For fixed $`n`$, two bad primes $`p`$ and $`q`$ impose zero conditions at the two generally different times $`r_p=n\bmod p`$ and $`r_q=n\bmod q`$, in the two different fields $`\mathbf F_p`$ and $`\mathbf F_q`$. There is no common orbit time and no common field in which the same-prime Cassini, Dodgson, or sliding identities can fire.
In fact, if $`p,q>\sqrt n`$ are distinct, then
$$
\boxed{r_p\ne r_q.}
$$
Indeed, equality $`r_p=r_q=r`$ would imply $`pq\mid n-r`$, while $`pq>n\ge n-r`$, which is impossible.
Thus the simultaneous event is intrinsically **asynchronous**:
$$
V_{r_p}\bmod p\in D,
\qquad
V_{r_q}\bmod q\in D,
$$
where $`D`$ is the zero line. It is not the reduction of one product-orbit event at a common integer time.
The sharp universal CRT statement remains
$$
\sum_{m\le N}1_{Z_p}(m)1_{Z_q}(m)
=
\frac{N|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}
+O(|Z_p||Z_q|),
$$
and similarly for four primes. In the critical range $`p,q>\sqrt N`$, the error dominates the main term. Q340 supplies no improvement to this error term.
After the companion channel has been removed, the all-$`n`$ target remains exactly
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_{\,n\bmod p}}}
\log p
=o(n).
}
$$
The weakest new input that would genuinely reopen the Q340 route is a **cross-characteristic synchronization identity**: an explicit subexponential-height object which represents the variable prefix $`V_{n\bmod p}`$ uniformly as $`p`$ varies. A precise sufficient form is given in §8.

# 1. The actual Apéry orbit
Put
$$
W_m=(m!)^3b_m.
$$
Then Apéry's recurrence becomes the cleared recurrence
$$
W_{m+1}=P(m)W_m-m^6W_{m-1}.
$$
For
$$
V_m=
\begin{pmatrix}W_m\\W_{m-1}\end{pmatrix},
$$
one has
$$
V_{m+1}=A(m)V_m.
$$
For $`0\le m<p`$, the factorial $`m!`$ is a unit modulo $`p`$, so
$$
b_m\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
W_m\equiv0\pmod p.
$$
In projective coordinates, write
$$
\pi_m=[W_m:W_{m-1}]\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p),
$$
and let
$$
D=[0:1].
$$
Then
$$
Z_p=\{m: \pi_m=D\}.
$$
The normalized matrix $`B(t)=t^{-3}A(t)`$ has the same projective action as $`A(t)`$. In affine coordinate $`z=W_m/W_{m-1}`$, it acts as
$$
\phi_t(z)=P(t)-\frac{t^6}{z}.
$$
In particular,
$$
\phi_t(0)=\infty.
$$
This is the projective form of the no-consecutive-zero theorem.

# 2. What the Borel-coset identity actually says
Q340 proves
$$
B(t)B(s)^{-1}\in B_{\mathrm{up}}.
$$
The upper Borel fixes $`\infty`$. Equivalently, every $`B(t)`$ has the same preimage of $`\infty`$, namely the zero line $`D`$:
$$
B(t)D=\infty.
$$
This is strong local rigidity, but it is only one-step rigidity. It says that once the orbit hits $`D`$, the next state is forced. It does not say that the orbit hits $`D`$ rarely over long times.
The ordered word is not contained in the Borel. Each step contains the fixed Weyl inversion, and the Borel factors are interleaved with it. Thus the product is not a product of commuting affine transformations.
The sliding identity is also not an ordinary conjugacy:
$$
M_h(x+1)=B(x+h)M_h(x)B(x+1)^{-1}.
$$
The left and right endpoint matrices differ. For a matrix coefficient relevant to a zero condition,
$$
e_1^{\mathsf T}M_h(x+1)e_1
=
\bigl(e_1^{\mathsf T}B(x+h)\bigr)
M_h(x)
\bigl(B(x+1)^{-1}e_1\bigr).
$$
Both boundary vectors move with $`x`$. Therefore a zero of one fixed matrix coefficient of $`M_h(x)`$ is not transported to a zero of the same coefficient at $`x+1`$.
A genuine conjugacy would preserve trace, eigenvalues, and invariant lines. The two-sided sliding relation preserves none of those scalar quantities in a form useful for the zero indicator.
The impossibility of rational diagonal flattening in Q340 is therefore material: one cannot replace the moving boundary vectors by constant ones through a bounded-complexity diagonal gauge.

# 3. Why same-prime gap identities do not become cross-prime identities
Suppose that, in one fixed field $`\mathbf F_p`$,
$$
b_x\equiv b_{x+h}\equiv0\pmod p.
$$
Then the interval transfer from time $`x`$ to time $`x+h`$ has both endpoints on the zero line. This is exactly the situation in which the gap continuant satisfies
$$
N_h(x)\equiv0\pmod p.
$$
Cassini, Dodgson, addition, restart, and resultant identities all exploit this **two-endpoint condition in one field**.
Now take distinct primes $`p,q>\sqrt n`$ and set
$$
r=n\bmod p,
\qquad
s=n\bmod q.
$$
The simultaneous lower-digit event is
$$
p\mid b_r,
\qquad
q\mid b_s.
$$
Assume $`r<s`$ and put $`h=s-r`$. The Q338 Casoratian formula gives
$$
N_h(r)
=C_{r,h}\bigl(a_sb_r-b_sa_r\bigr)
$$
for an explicit nonzero rational/integer factor $`C_{r,h}`$. Reduction gives
$$
N_h(r)\equiv -C_{r,h}b_sa_r\pmod p,
$$
and
$$
N_h(r)\equiv C_{r,h}a_sb_r\pmod q.
$$
Neither expression is forced to vanish. Thus
$$
\boxed{
 p\mid b_r,\ q\mid b_s
 \centernot\Longrightarrow
 p\mid N_h(r),\ q\mid N_h(r),\ 	ext{or }pq\mid N_h(r).
}
$$
This is the exact algebraic failure of the most natural attempt to apply the sliding/gap machinery across primes.
The only automatic product certificate is the tautological one
$$
pq\mid b_rb_s.
$$
Its height is the sum of the heights of two unrelated Apéry values and gives no new pointwise saving.

# 4. The asynchronous CRT obstruction
The ring
$$
\mathbf Z/pq\mathbf Z
\cong
\mathbf F_p\times\mathbf F_q
$$
contains the product orbit at a **common time** $`m`$:
$$
V_m\bmod pq
\longleftrightarrow
(V_m\bmod p,V_m\bmod q).
$$
But the fixed-$`n`$ event uses
$$
(V_r\bmod p,V_s\bmod q),
\qquad r\ne s.
$$
It is therefore not a point of the ordinary product orbit.
One can manufacture a mixed vector using CRT idempotents:
$$
\widetilde V=e_pV_r+e_qV_s,
$$
where
$$
e_p\equiv1\pmod p,\ e_p\equiv0\pmod q,
\qquad
e_q=1-e_p.
$$
Then both components of $`\widetilde V`$ lie on the zero line. But $`\widetilde V`$ is produced by two different words of two different lengths. It is not generated by one bounded-complexity Apéry transfer product.
The corresponding matrix is componentwise
$$
e_pM_r+e_qM_s.
$$
Its construction already contains the two primes and the two asynchronous times. This is merely CRT packaging; it creates no cross-prime cancellation.
This is a useful formal principle:
> Every universal polynomial/rational identity in the transfer matrices is functorial under base change. Over $`\mathbf F_p\times\mathbf F_q`$ it acts componentwise. Such an identity cannot create an off-diagonal relation between time $`r`$ in the first component and time $`s`$ in the second component.
A cross-prime theorem therefore needs an additional global integer relation before reduction. The Borel-coset identity alone is intrinsically characteristic-local.

# 5. Exact two-prime and four-prime CRT formulas
Define the periodic indicator
$$
I_p(m)=1_{\{m\bmod p\in Z_p\}}.
$$
For distinct primes $`p,q`$, each pair $`(u,v)\in Z_p\times Z_q`$ determines one residue class modulo $`pq`$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{m\le N}I_p(m)I_q(m)
=
\frac{N|Z_p||Z_q|}{pq}
+O(|Z_p||Z_q|).
}
$$
The implied constant may be taken to be $`1`$ after summing the endpoint errors for the CRT residue classes.
For four distinct primes $`p_1,\dots,p_4`$,
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{m\le N}\prod_{i=1}^4I_{p_i}(m)
=
\frac{N\prod_i|Z_{p_i}|}{\prod_i p_i}
+O\!\left(\prod_i|Z_{p_i}|\right).
}
$$
These are the sharp universal formulas without information on the locations of the CRT lifts.
In the large-prime range relevant to Problem 3.2,
$$
pq>N
$$
for every two distinct primes $`p,q>\sqrt N`$. Hence
$$
\frac{N}{pq}<1,
$$
and the boundary error is at least as large as the expected main term. The four-prime product is even farther beyond the interval length.
To improve these formulas one needs additive information on the actual locations of the roots, for example uniform control of Fourier coefficients
$$
\sum_{x\in Z_p}e(ax/p),
$$
or a bounded-conductor trace-function realization of the centered indicator. Q340 supplies neither. It controls projective transport, not additive distribution of the hitting times.

# 6. Checks at the two required edge cases
## 6.1 The top-half class $`q=1`$
If $`p>n/2`$, then
$$
r=n-p
$$
and the support law is purely lower-digit because $`a_1=6`$ is a unit:
$$
p\mid G_n
\iff
p\mid b_r.
$$
Lucas gives
$$
b_n\equiv b_1b_r=5b_r\pmod p,
$$
so every top-half bad prime divides the single integer $`b_n`$. Therefore
$$
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_{n-p}}}\log p
\le \log\operatorname{rad}(b_n)
\le\log b_n
=\Theta(n).
$$
This is an exact global certificate, but its height is exponential with a positive linear exponent. It is exactly one $`o(1)`$ factor short of the goal.
The Q340 Borel structure does not lower this height. It rederives the same gap/no-consecutive-zero geometry within each $`\mathbf F_p`$.
## 6.2 The examples $`(n,p)=(26,13),(27,13)`$
Here
$$
26=2\cdot13+0,
\qquad
27=2\cdot13+1.
$$
The lower digits are
$$
b_0=1,
\qquad
b_1=5,
$$
so neither is a zero modulo $`13`$. The divisibility comes from the companion channel
$$
a_2=\frac{351}{4}\equiv0\pmod{13}.
$$
Thus the actual zero-orbit mechanism correctly predicts no lower-digit hit. These primes do not occur in $`\mathcal L(n)`$ after the companion channel is removed.
This is an important audit point: Q340 can only address the lower-digit target. It cannot be used as a proof of the full support law without the separate companion estimate.

# 7. Why the singularity at $`t=0`$ blocks a Floquet shortcut
The normalized cocycle $`B(t)=t^{-3}A(t)`$ is defined only for $`t\ne0`$. Over $`\mathbf F_p`$, the additive translation $`t\mapsto t+1`$ necessarily crosses $`t=0`$ once per full period.
Hence this is not a smooth periodic $`\mathrm{SL}_2`$ cocycle on the entire cyclic orbit $`\mathbf F_p`$. There is a singular cut at every multiple of $`p`$.
A natural attempt to synchronize $`n`$ with $`r=n\bmod p`$ would be to split a length-$`n`$ word into $`q`$ full $`p`$-cycles and one prefix. The singular step prevents an invertible Floquet reduction. The full-cycle cleared matrix is singular rather than an invertible monodromy from which the prefix can be recovered.
This is the global version of the diagonal-flattening obstruction in Q340: there is no bounded-complexity periodic gauge which turns the moving prefix into a fixed matrix power.

# 8. The weakest identity that would genuinely reopen the route
There are three increasingly strong formulations.
## 8.1 Local cocycle reopening: an invariant rational line
A bounded-degree rational covector $`\ell(t)`$ and scalar $`\chi(t)`$ satisfying
$$
\boxed{
\ell(t+1)B(t)=\chi(t)\ell(t)
}
$$
would turn one coordinate of the matrix recurrence into a first-order scalar recurrence. The moving endpoint covectors in the sliding identity would become fixed after gauge.
This is precisely a rank-one submodule of the Apéry difference module—a hypergeometric solution or first-order Ore factor. The Apéry module is not known to possess such a factor, and the Q340 diagonal obstruction is consistent with its absence.
Thus no further consequence should be expected from the Borel coset unless one finds a genuinely new non-diagonal semiconjugacy.
## 8.2 Discrepancy reopening: a bounded-conductor additive realization
For two-prime or four-prime CRT estimates, a sufficient new theorem would be a uniformly bounded-complexity realization of the **actual** centered zero indicator
$$
1_{Z_p}(x)-\frac{|Z_p|}{p}
$$
as a bounded linear combination of trace functions with conductor $`O(1)`$ independent of $`p`$.
That would make uniform additive Fourier and correlation estimates available, and would allow large-sieve/dispersion arguments to improve the CRT boundary error.
The prefix product defining the actual orbit currently has complexity growing with the time $`x`$. Q340's Borel factorization does not compress it into such a fixed sheaf.
## 8.3 Pointwise reopening: a synchronized subexponential certificate
The most direct sufficient identity for the all-$`n`$ theorem is the following.
Construct explicitly a matrix or vector-valued rational function $`S_n(T)`$, with denominators avoiding all primes $`p>\sqrt n`$, such that
$$
\boxed{
S_n(p)V_0
\sim
V_{\,n\bmod p}\pmod p
\qquad(\sqrt n<p\le n),
}
$$
and such that its coefficient height satisfies
$$
\log\|S_n\|=o(n).
$$
Let $`C_n`$ be the numerator of the first coordinate of $`S_n(0)V_0`$. Since $`p\equiv0\pmod p`$,
$$
S_n(p)\equiv S_n(0)\pmod p.
$$
Therefore
$$
p\mid b_{n\bmod p}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\mid C_n.
$$
It would follow that
$$
\mathcal L(n)
\le\log|C_n|
=o(n).
$$
This is a precise cross-characteristic synchronization certificate. Q364 shows that the obvious Q338 Casoratians and endpoint evaluations fail this test: they either have $`\Theta(n)`$ logarithmic height or reduce to unit multiples of the original $`b_r`$ condition.

# 9. Relation to the all-$`n`$ weighted target
For each quotient $`q`$, define
$$
\mathcal L_q(n)
=
\sum_{\substack{n/(q+1)<p\le n/q\\p\mid b_{n-qp}}}
\log p.
$$
Then
$$
\mathcal L(n)=\sum_{1\le q<\sqrt n}\mathcal L_q(n).
$$
The audited quotient reduction shows that, for any slowly growing $`f(n)\to\infty`$ with $`f(n)\le\log n`$, all but
$$
q<f(n)\log n
$$
contribute $`o(n)`$ by prime counting. Thus the real target is
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{q<f(n)\log n}\mathcal L_q(n)=o(n).
}
$$
A uniform bound
$$
K_q(n)
:=
\#\{p\in(n/(q+1),n/q]:p\mid b_{n-qp}\}
\ll (\log n)^C
$$
for all $`q<f(n)\log n`$ would already suffice, since every prime contributes at most $`\log n`$.
Q340 yields no such fixed-$`n`$ estimate. Its consequences are vertical or same-prime:
- no consecutive roots;
- gap-polynomial constraints for two roots of one $`Z_p`$;
- the $`p^{2/3}`$ zero-fiber bound;
- deterministic sliding identities for one characteristic.
Those inputs are enough for density and codegree statements after averaging over $`n`$. They are not enough to eliminate a single rainbow pile-up in which one fixed $`n`$ is hit by many unrelated primes at unrelated residues.
A two-prime or four-prime moment bound would improve exceptional sets, but a finite fixed moment still would not prove the pointwise theorem for every $`n`$. The synchronized certificate of §8.3, or an equivalent pointwise cross-characteristic reciprocity law, is the correct missing object.

# Final assessment
The Q340 structure is not wasted: it explains exactly why the Apéry orbit has strong same-prime rigidity and why generic random-walk expansion arguments are inappropriate. But its Borel-coset and sliding identities are **modulus-local** and **time-synchronous**.
The fixed-$`n`$ problem is **cross-characteristic** and **time-asynchronous**. That mismatch is the exact algebraic wall.
Therefore:
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{Q340 alone gives no nontrivial two- or four-prime CRT discrepancy;}\\
\text{it gives no direct pointwise bound on }K_{\mathrm{low}}(n);\\
\text{the all-}n\text{ target remains }\mathcal L(n)=o(n).
\end{array}
}
$$
The weakest genuinely useful new identity is one that synchronizes the moving prefix $`n\bmod p`$ across characteristics with bounded complexity—either as a bounded-conductor additive trace object or, more decisively, as a subexponential-height global integer certificate.
