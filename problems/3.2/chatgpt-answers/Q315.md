This is why higher energies do not automatically solve the problem. Shkredov's work shows that small higher energies imply Sidon-type or random structure unless a set has a special structured component; that conclusion is entirely compatible with size \sqrt p. See I. Shkredov, Energies and Structure of Additive Sets, EJC 21 (2014), and On an Application of Higher Energies to Sidon Sets, Combinatorica 43 (2023), 329–345.

## No as an absolute barrier to all continuant geometry

An r-return condition is overdetermined: there are r-1 equations in the base z and the gap parameters. If one could prove that the union of all admissible tuples has genuine codimension r-1, so that

The obstacle is that the continuants have degrees and arithmetic complexity growing with the gaps. They are not one bounded-degree algebraic family to which Lang–Weil, Mit'kin-type point counting, or a fixed incidence theorem applies. Forced reflection factors and multiplicative-order resonances also create special components that must be removed uniformly.

# 6. Why Heath-Brown/Bourgain–Garaev methods do not supply the missing gain

The Heath-Brown–Konyagin–Shkredov energy method obtains strong exponential-sum bounds because the relevant sets have multiplicative subgroup structure. The additive energy is not used in isolation; it is coupled to invariance under multiplication and to Stepanov-type algebraic estimates. See:

- D. R. Heath-Brown, An Estimate for Heilbronn's Exponential Sum, in Analytic Number Theory, Progress in Math. 139 (1996), 451–463;

- D. R. Heath-Brown and S. Konyagin, New bounds for Gauss sums derived from $k$th powers, and for Heilbronn's exponential sum, Q. J. Math. 51 (2000), 221–235.

Likewise, finite-field sum-product theorems force expansion only when a set is simultaneously constrained additively and multiplicatively. A representative reference is J. Bourgain and M. Garaev, On a variant of sum-product estimates and explicit exponential sum bounds in prime fields, Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 146 (2009), 1–21.
