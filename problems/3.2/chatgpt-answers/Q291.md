# A rigorous Katz checklist for SU(2) equidistribution of the Apéry Mellin traces

## Executive verdict

The clean route is not to deduce the Mellin monodromy group directly from the rank-three differential monodromy of the Apéry K3 family, and it is not to invoke a Larsen alternative or infer the group from a few numerical moments.

The clean route is Katz's rank-two criterion:

1. construct the normalized middle-extension perverse object N on G_m whose generic Mellin fibre is the claimed two-dimensional primitive Frobenius space;

1. prove that N is geometrically irreducible, pure of weight zero, and arithmetically self-dual in Katz's convolution-Tannakian sense;

1. compute Katz's Tannakian dimension and show that it is exactly 2;

1. rule out every nontrivial multiplicative translate [x↦ax]^*N ≅ N;

1. apply Katz, Convolution and Equidistribution, Theorem 14.1, which then gives

Once this has been established uniformly for the good reductions, Theorem 28.1 of the same book gives the desired prime-aspect vertical Sato–Tate theorem as p→∞; Remark 28.3 is important because it says that no compatibility between the objects over the different finite fields is required beyond the stated uniform bounds and common monodromy group.

The closest worked template in Katz's book is Theorem 15.3, where Katz starts from the rank-three orthogonal sheaf Sym²(Leg), proves that its Mellin object has Tannakian dimension two, verifies autoduality and the absence of nontrivial translates, and concludes that its Mellin monodromy is SL₂. The Apéry writeup should imitate that proof almost line by line.

There are, however, three load-bearing Apéry-specific computations that are not automatic from the known K3 monodromy:

- the exact Euler–Poincaré/local-inertia computation giving Tannakian dimension 2;

- the convolution-autoduality, which requires an inversion functional equation in the Mellin coordinate, not merely the orthogonal pairing on the K3 sheaf;

- the multiplicative-translate test, including all singular points and their local monodromy labels.

Until those three items are archived for the exact primitive object, the statement “the normalized Apéry Mellin traces equidistribute for SU(2)” is a very plausible theorem skeleton, but not yet a complete invocation of Katz.

This is the normalization required in Katz's compact-group formalism. Without it, the same conjugacy classes are obtained after dividing the Frobenius eigenvalues by p^{3/2}.

## 3.3 Semisimplicity

BBD, Théorème 5.3.8, gives geometric semisimplicity for pure perverse sheaves. Katz separately asks for arithmetic semisimplicity.

The simplest way to obtain it here is to prove geometric irreducibility. Any arithmetic invariant subspace is then also geometrically invariant, so the arithmetic object is irreducible and hence semisimple.

If the primitive factor is extracted from a reducible global cohomology group, purity alone gives a semisimple geometric decomposition but does not identify the desired summand over the arithmetic field. One then needs an arithmetic projector or a uniqueness argument based on weights, determinant, and local monodromy.

# 5. Good characters are automatically cofinite

Katz's Lemma 3.3 gives equivalent formulations of a good character. For the present middle-extension object, goodness is the condition that the forget-support morphism is an isomorphism and that the Mellin cohomology is concentrated in the expected perverse degree.

For a tame sheaf, the bad characters are read from the local tame characters at 0 and ∞: a character is bad when its inverse occurs in the relevant local monodromy invariants or coinvariants.

Consequently:

- the set of bad characters is finite for each p;

- its cardinality is bounded by a constant depending only on the generic rank;

- in the crude rank-three estimate, one has at most 2·3=6 bad characters;

- the exact list should be written down from the local inertia decompositions at 0 and ∞.

Thus “the good-character set is cofinite” is not a difficult theorem here. The difficult issue is identifying the correct primitive sheaf and its local monodromy.

# 10. Why the alternatives are less clean

## 10.1 A Larsen alternative

A Larsen or Larsen–Pink alternative is designed to show that a compatible-system monodromy group is large except for algebraic exceptional cases. Here the rank is only two and Katz has already classified the exceptional cases in precisely the needed convolution category.

Using such an alternative would still leave the torus-normalizer and finite cases to be excluded. The translate criterion excludes them directly.

They are not the cleanest proof:

- the second moment proves irreducibility but does not exclude a torus normalizer or finite subgroup;

- several finite binary-polyhedral groups share the first few tensor invariants with SL₂;

- one must prove enough exact correlation moments, not merely observe them numerically.

A moment proof therefore risks reproducing the subgroup classification hidden inside Theorem 14.1.

## 10.3 A unipotent-element proof

A valid alternative would be:

1. show that the Mellin Tannaka group acts irreducibly on a two-dimensional symplectic space;

1. exhibit a nontrivial unipotent element;

1. prove connectedness.

A connected irreducible subgroup of SL₂ containing a nontrivial unipotent is SL₂.

The difficulty is that a unipotent in the original K3 local monodromy is not automatically a unipotent in the Mellin Tannaka group. Again, the translate criterion is usually easier.

# 13. Paper-ready theorem skeleton

Theorem. Let N_p be the normalized primitive middle-extension perverse sheaf attached to the Apéry transcendental variation in the Mellin coordinate. Assume that, for every sufficiently large good prime:

1. N_p is geometrically irreducible and pure of weight zero;

2. dim_Kat(N_p)=2;

3. N_p≅inv^*D(N_p) arithmetically;

4. no nontrivial multiplicative translate of N_p is isomorphic to N_p;

5. its generic rank and number of bad characters are bounded independently of p.

Then

G_{rm geom}(N_p)=G_{rm arith}(N_p)=SL_2

for every sufficiently large good prime. Moreover, as p→∞, the normalized Frobenius conjugacy classes associated with the good characters of F_p^× equidistribute in SU(2). In particular the normalized traces T_p(χ)/p^{3/2} obey the semicircle law (11.2).

## Proof

Items 1–4 put N_p under Katz's Theorem 14.1, using Corollary 8.3 for Lie-irreducibility; hence both Tannaka groups equal SL₂. Item 5 and Katz's Theorem 28.1 give equidistribution as the prime fields grow. The trace statement follows by pushing Haar measure forward under the standard trace map. □
