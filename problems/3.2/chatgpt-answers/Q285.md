# Horizontal interpolation of the slice (omega_p^m): what exists and what does not

Search date: 22 July 2026

## Verdict

There are two different questions, and they have different answers.

1. A mod-(p) congruence carrier can exist in special hypergeometric/diagonal situations. For a fixed integer (m), Gross–Koblitz and Dwork/Hasse–Witt coefficient formulas often convert the character value at (omega_p^m) into a fixed classical hypergeometric or period coefficient, up to a predictable nonzero factor and a power of the Dwork uniformizer. After clearing bounded denominators, one can obtain an integer (c_m) with

1. There is no known horizontal (p)-adic analytic interpolation of the characteristic-zero representations (mathrm{Mellin}_{omega_p^m}) as (p) varies. Dwork crystals, Coleman families, Hida families, and Katz (p)-adic (L)-functions are all constructed after fixing a prime (p). Their character spaces, coefficient fields, Frobenius operators, and Iwasawa algebras are (p)-local. They do not assemble the varying-conductor characters (omega_p^m) into one rigid-analytic family across all primes.

So the honest answer is:

Yes, there may be a fixed integer coefficient carrier at the mod-(p) level; no, the standard interpolation theories do not provide a cross-prime analytic family for the unbounded-order Teichmüller slice.

This is not a theorem that every imaginable future adelic formalism is impossible. It is a statement about the scope of the existing Dwork–Coleman–Hida–Katz machinery.

# 1. Why a carrier can exist even though the slice is not motivic

Let (omega_p) be the Teichmüller character of (mathbf F_p^times), and fix (m) while (ptoinfty). The order of (omega_p^m) is

```plain text
(p-1)/gcd(m,p-1),
```

so the characteristic-zero Kummer twists have growing order, growing coefficient fields, and conductor at the varying prime (p). They are not the local realizations of one fixed compatible system with a fixed finite ramification set.

However, reduction modulo (p) forgets much of that characteristic-zero complexity. The Gross–Koblitz formula expresses a Gauss sum as

```plain text
g_p(omega_bar^j) = - pi^j Gamma_p(j/(p-1)),
pi^(p-1) = -p.
```

See B. Gross and N. Koblitz, “Gauss sums and the p-adic Γ-function,” Ann. of Math. 109 (1979), 569–581.

For fixed (m), the arguments occurring in a bounded hypergeometric Gauss product have the form

```plain text
alpha + linear_function(m)/(p-1),
```

with fixed rational hypergeometric parameters (alpha). After extracting the predictable (pi)-valuation, the remaining (Gamma_p)-units can often be reduced modulo (p) to Pochhammer/factorial ratios in (m). Those ratios are coefficients of a classical hypergeometric period and are independent of (p), after splitting primes into the finitely many residue classes needed to handle the fixed denominators.

This is the mechanism behind many congruences between finite-field or (p)-adic hypergeometric values and truncated classical hypergeometric series. A useful modern reference is D. McCarthy, “Extending Gaussian hypergeometric series to the p-adic setting,” Int. J. Number Theory 8 (2012), 1581–1612: the paper rewrites finite-field hypergeometric expressions using Gross–Koblitz and proves congruences with truncated classical hypergeometric series.

The important logical point is:

A characteristic-zero compatible system is sufficient for a global carrier, but it is not necessary for a mod-(p) coefficient congruence.

A coefficient of one integral period series can be the common mod-(p) shadow of representations that do not themselves form one compatible system.

## Apéry specialization

Under the Mellin/Hasse identity assumed in the question, if

```plain text
Tr(Frob_p | Mellin_{omega_p^m}) ≡ u_p b_m (mod p)
```

with (u_p) a unit for all sufficiently large good primes, then the canonical carrier is simply

```plain text
c_m = b_m.
```

For fixed (m),

```plain text
p | Tr  <=>  p | b_m
```

outside the finite normalization/bad-prime set. This is very strong but also explains why it does not resolve the pointwise Apéry-gcd problem: there (m=nbmod p) varies with (p), so the “single carrier” is not one fixed integer—it is the moving sequence value (b_{nbmod p}), exactly the original horizontal difficulty.

# 2. Dwork and Beukers–Vlasenko

## What they provide

Dwork’s theory is precisely the right source for prime-local congruences between truncated periods, Hasse–Witt matrices, and unit roots. The classical sources include:

- B. Dwork, “p-adic cycles,” Publ. Math. IHÉS 37 (1969), 27–115.

- B. Dwork, “Normalized period matrices II,” Ann. of Math. 98 (1973), 1–57.

Beukers and Vlasenko recast this in terms of Laurent-polynomial coefficient matrices. In Dwork Crystals I, their Hasse–Witt/period matrices (beta_m) are explicitly formed from coefficients of powers of a Laurent polynomial, and (p)-adic limits describe Frobenius on the unit-root crystal:

- F. Beukers and M. Vlasenko, “Dwork Crystals I,” IMRN 2021, 8807–8844.

- F. Beukers and M. Vlasenko, “Dwork Crystals II,” IMRN 2021, 4427–4444.

- F. Beukers and M. Vlasenko, “Dwork Crystals III,” IMRN 2023, 20433–20483.

- A. Mellit and M. Vlasenko, “Dwork’s congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial,” IJNT 12 (2016), 313–321.

Because the Laurent polynomial may be defined over (mathbf Z), its coefficient arrays are genuinely global integers. If the (omega_p^m)-Mellin eigenspace is identified with a particular coefficient entry, that entry is a legitimate carrier (c_m).

## What they do not provide

The analytic constructions are nevertheless made after choosing (p). Their basic rings are (p)-adically complete, the Cartier/Frobenius operator is the (p)-power operator, and the limits run through truncation levels (p^s):

```plain text
fixed p,  s -> infinity,
```

not

```plain text
fixed m,  p -> infinity.
```

Thus Dwork congruences can prove

```plain text
trace_p(m) mod p = coefficient_m mod p
```

when the geometry supplies that identity, but they do not themselves give a horizontal distribution theorem for the primes dividing the coefficient.

Assessment of candidate (a):

- coefficient/Hasse–Witt carrier: yes, case-by-case and often exactly the right mechanism;

- one (p)-adic analytic function interpolating the slice across all (p): no;

- Chebotarev-strength count of primes with vanishing carrier: no.

# 3. Coleman and overconvergent weight interpolation

Coleman families are also fixed-prime objects. For a chosen (p), the weight space is the rigid space of continuous characters

```plain text
Z_p^× -> C_p^×.
```

It is a disjoint union of (p-1) discs, indexed by the Teichmüller character on (mu_{p-1}); analytic variation occurs on the (1+pmathbf Z_p) direction inside a component. See:

- R. Coleman, “p-adic Banach spaces and families of modular forms,” Invent. Math. 127 (1997), 417–479.

- R. Coleman and B. Mazur, The eigencurve, in Galois Representations in Arithmetic Algebraic Geometry (1998), 1–113.

- For an explicit modern formulation of weight space as characters of (mathbf Z_p^times), see the interpolation discussion in J. Jetchev, D. Loeffler, S. Zerbes, “Heegner points in Coleman families,” Proc. LMS 122 (2021), 124–152.

At a fixed (p), the algebraic character (xmapsto x^m) decomposes as

```plain text
x^m = omega_p(x)^m · <x>^m.
```

The finite-field character (omega_p^m) is the tame reduction of this algebraic weight. This observation can explain a mod-(p) congruence with a global coefficient. But the spaces for two different primes (p) and (q) are different rigid spaces over (mathbf Q_p) and (mathbf Q_q); there is no common Coleman disc containing both (omega_p^m) and (omega_q^m).

Coleman’s and Morita’s (p)-adic gamma functions are likewise functions on one (mathbf Z_p). Gross–Koblitz is exact at that prime, but the collection ({Gamma_p}_p) is not one rigid-analytic function over a common nonarchimedean field.

Assessment of candidate (b): fixed-(p) weight interpolation is powerful, but it gives no horizontal coherence across varying (p) beyond coefficient congruences proved by separate global arithmetic input.

# 4. Hida families and big Galois representations

Hida theory fixes a prime (p), a tame level/residual branch, and constructs an ordinary Hecke algebra finite over a (p)-adic Iwasawa algebra. It interpolates weight and (p)-power level/nebentype. A standard reference is:

- H. Hida, “p-adic ordinary Hecke algebras for GL(2),” Ann. Inst. Fourier 44 (1994), 1289–1322.

The control theorem specializes one big (p)-adic representation at arithmetic characters of that one Iwasawa algebra. The “character direction” in Hida/Iwasawa theory is normally a continuous character of a fixed pro-(p) group—cyclotomic, anticyclotomic, or a (p)-adic torus—with tame data fixed on the branch.

Your characters (omega_p^m) vary in the tame group of order (p-1) while the prime itself changes. They are not arithmetic points of one Hida algebra. Even if for every (p) one constructs a Hida family containing a related form, those are separate families and separate big Galois representations.

Assessment of candidate (c): Hida theory does not interpolate this horizontal slice. A global compatible motive could give cross-prime Frobenius coefficients, but the growing-conductor Kummer twists are precisely not one fixed compatible system.

# 5. Katz two-variable p-adic L-functions

Katz’s construction is again local at one chosen prime:

- N. Katz, “p-adic L-functions for CM fields,” Invent. Math. 49 (1978), 199–297.

The multiple variables parametrize algebraic infinity types and characters of a fixed (p)-adic ray-class/Iwasawa group. “Two-variable” usually means cyclotomic and anticyclotomic variation for one (p), not simultaneous variation of the residue characteristic.

Modern extensions make the same locality explicit. For example, Andreatta–Iovita construct, for each triple ((F,K,p)), a (p)-adic (L)-function interpolating twists whose (p)-part of the conductor is (p^n):

- F. Andreatta and A. Iovita, “Katz type p-adic L-functions for primes p non-split in the CM field,” Comment. Math. Helv. 99 (2024), 641–716.

This is variation up a fixed (p)-power conductor tower. It is not variation through the prime-conductor characters (omega_p^m) while (p) changes.

Assessment of candidate (d): Katz’s variables do not supply the requested horizontal carrier, except when an independent congruence identifies the special value with a global coefficient sequence.

# 6. Is the slice “provably outside” interpolation theory?

The right formulation is more cautious.

## What is structurally ruled out

It is outside all standard fixed-(p) interpolation spaces because:

```plain text
coefficient field changes: Q_p -> Q_q,
weight space changes:      W_p -> W_q,
Iwasawa algebra changes:   Lambda_p -> Lambda_q,
tame group changes:        mu_{p-1} -> mu_{q-1},
ramified prime changes:    p -> q.
```

A rigid-analytic function is defined over one nonarchimedean base field; it cannot literally evaluate simultaneously in incompatible (mathbf Q_p)-topologies without additional adelic/global structure.

The twists also fail the usual definition of a compatible system: their conductor includes the moving prime, and the character coefficient fields have unbounded degree.

## What is not ruled out

There is no general impossibility theorem saying that no future adelic, prismatic, lambda-ring, or integral-cohomological construction could package their reductions. In fact, the coefficient-carrier phenomenon demonstrates that an integral package sometimes already exists.

The realistic dichotomy is:

```plain text
A. The Mellin trace is congruent to an entry of a global Hasse/period
   coefficient array.
   -> carrier c_m exists; prove the congruence case-by-case.

B. No such coefficient identity is available.
   -> Dwork/Coleman/Hida/Katz do not manufacture one automatically.
```

# 7. Consequence for the Apéry pointwise problem

For fixed (m), a carrier congruence is almost too strong:

```plain text
p | trace_p(m)  <=>  p | b_m,
```

so there are only finitely many such primes. The difficult regime is instead

```plain text
m = n mod p,
```

where (m) moves over a range comparable to (p). Then the carrier statement becomes

```plain text
p | trace_p(n mod p)  <=>  p | b_{n mod p},
```

which is exactly the original incidence relation. No fixed-(m) interpolation can turn that moving-index problem into Chebotarev for one motive.

Thus the carrier helps with identification, integrality, and congruence precision, but not with the needed horizontal non-clustering. A log-power saving still requires an additional theorem controlling how the prime divisors of the moving coefficient sequence align with (nbmod p).

## Final one-line answer

The only known cross-prime coherence for the unbounded-order slice is a case-specific integral coefficient/Hasse–Witt congruence—often, in the Apéry case, the coefficient (b_m) itself. Dwork explains and strengthens that congruence prime by prime. Coleman, Hida, and Katz interpolate only within a fixed (p)-adic character space and provide no horizontal analytic family or Chebotarev bound as (p) varies.
