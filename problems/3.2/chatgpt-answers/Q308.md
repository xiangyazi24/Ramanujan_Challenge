## 1.3 Cobham does not couple different primes

Cobham's theorem concerns one fixed finite-alphabet sequence which is automatic in two fixed multiplicatively independent bases. Here both the reduction and the base vary with p. There is therefore no Cobham conclusion across primes.

The supercongruences modulo p^k similarly govern lifting and higher-digit propagation. They can show that a zero, once present, has strong descendants; they do not count the one-digit seeds in Z_p.

# 3. Diagonals and prime averages

The diagonal/Hadamard description explains why reductions modulo p^k are automatic and makes their automata computable. It does not create cancellation as p varies.

The obstruction is a quantifier mismatch:

```plain text
automatic/diagonal theory:
    fix p, then describe all n modulo p^k;

desired first moment:
    vary p and count zeros inside a different first block for every p.
```

The automata, alphabets, transition labels and algebraic equations all vary with p. There is no compatible finite automaton over a fixed alphabet that couples the primes.

Estimating this is an average theorem about the large prime divisors of the varying integers b_r. Christol's theorem and diagonal algebraicity contain no prime-divisor distribution input.

This is reflected in the literature. Rowland–Yassawi explicitly ask whether there are infinitely many primes dividing no Apéry number; by Lucas, this is exactly the question whether Z_p=\varnothing for infinitely many p. Malik–Straub go further and conjecture a positive density—empirically e^{-1/2}—of such primes. Those questions would be trivial consequences of a bounded-average theorem, but they remain open.

## Final answer

The automatic/algebraic-mod-p route does not currently bound |Z_p| or its average. In the Apéry case Lucas congruence makes the reason completely explicit: the zero automaton has constant size, while the unknown set Z_p is encoded in its digit labels. The generating series has algebraic degree growing like p, and even bounded degree would not control zero coefficients. Any bound on |Z_p| or on \sum_p|Z_p| requires additional arithmetic input—recurrence geometry, Frobenius residual anti-concentration, or horizontal prime-divisor dispersion—not merely Christol/Furstenberg automaticity.
