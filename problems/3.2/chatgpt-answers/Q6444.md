### 1.3 What a Katz/Mellin framework does—and does not—currently provide

There are two distinct variables that must not be conflated.

- The fiber parameter t of the Beukers–Peters/Stienstra–Beukers K3 family is an algebraic parameter on a fixed curve. Frobenius traces as t varies are the natural domain of trace-function and monodromy methods.

- The coefficient index r in b_r is a Taylor-coefficient/digit variable. It is not automatically a rational point of that parameter curve.

### 3.2 Why the point-counting geometry does not automatically improve on average over p

A Lang–Weil/Deligne average becomes available when the quantity is the point count of fibers of one fixed finite-type scheme or trace function of uniformly bounded conductor. The first-block zero condition is produced by a recurrence orbit of length comparable to p; the degree/complexity of a naive algebraic encoding grows with the iteration length. The existing p^(2/3) argument controls each row by specialized gap/subvariety geometry, but it does not identify |Z_p| as the point count of fibers of one bounded-complexity family over Spec Z.

Consequently there is no automatic “average Lang–Weil improvement.” To obtain one, the proof must expose a fixed arithmetic family whose Frobenius data simultaneously encode the coefficient index and exact residual-characteristic vanishing.

### 3.3 Why existing large sieves do not currently apply

Kowalski’s large sieve for Frobenius and its variants control reductions of a compatible system with known monodromy through fixed auxiliary primes. Perret-Gentil [PG20, arXiv:1703.06965] obtains zero-density estimates for values of trace functions lying in algebraic subsets of cyclotomic integers, under compatible-system and monodromy hypotheses.

Here the modulus defining the event is the same varying prime p as the residue characteristic, and the map r↦b_r mod p has not been realized as a uniformly bounded compatible trace family in r. Thus the required input to the large sieve is precisely the missing theorem, not an automatic consequence of the K3 family.

Even after such a realization, a large sieve for macroscopic conjugacy conditions would not automatically resolve the singleton residual event trace≡0 mod p; one must check that the level of distribution reaches the growing modulus and that conductor constants remain summable.

Current answer to Question 3. Beyond the project’s pointwise consequences displayed in §1.4, I found no published variance or tail theorem for |Z_p|. The natural route is a direct factorial-moment estimate, not a generic variance theorem already in the literature.

## 5. Concrete attack-or-dispatch tests

1. Mellin-integrality test. Construct the proposed Mellin object over Z[1/N], not separately for each p. Prove that conductor/rank are bounded independently of p and that its Teichmüller-character trace reduces to b_r. If the only available object has degree or conductor growing like p, the off-the-shelf Katz route is dispatched.

1. Microscopic test. After obtaining monodromy and normalized equidistribution, identify an integral lattice and prove a congruence local limit of size 1/p. If the argument only integrates continuous class functions, it has not addressed b_r≡0 mod p.

1. Pair-correlation test. Expand F_{2,X} by gaps h and seek a uniform algebraic condition for simultaneous zeros. Degrees, discriminants, and exceptional-prime sets must be summable over all relevant h; a fixed-h Weil bound is insufficient by itself.

1. Numerical discriminator. Alongside S_X log X/X, record

and the histogram of reflection-pair counts. The pair-Poisson model predicts bounded normalized F_{2,X}, E|Z_p|^2≈3, and no-zero frequency near e^(−1/2). Growth of normalized F_{2,X} would falsify GARQI-1′ before further geometric investment.
