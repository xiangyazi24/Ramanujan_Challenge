## Executive correction

The inert-fibre factor in Q6457 was wrong.  The correct constant sign is

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_p=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right),}
\]

not \((2/p)\).  If \(t\in\mathbf F_p\) is inert for the quadratic cover, \(u\in\mathbf F_{p^2}\) is either point above \(t\), and

\[
a_2=p^2+1-\#E_u(\mathbf F_{p^2}),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
T_+(t)=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)a_2-p,
\qquad
T_-(t)=-T_+(t).
}
\]

Equivalently, if \(\alpha,eta\) are the two \(p^2\)-Frobenius eigenvalues on \(H^1(E_u)\), so that

\[
\alpha+\beta=a_2,\qquad \alpha\beta=p^2,
\]

then the half-Frobenius on the rank-three \(+\)-descent has eigenvalues

\[
\boxed{
\left\{
\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)\alpha,
-p,
\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)\beta
\right\}.}
\]

The factor \((-3/p)\) is forced by the explicit isogeny descent.  Relative to the gauge-only answer \((2/p)\), the missing factor is

\[
\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right),
\qquad
\left(\frac2p\right)\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)
=\left(\frac{-12}{p}\right)
=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right).
\]

This is not a coincidence.  The number \(-6\) is exactly the scalar obtained by composing the two deck-conjugate degree-six isogeny comparisons.  The old \(9/8\) norm computation is correct, but it computes only the Kummer-gauge part of the rank-two pullback and does not determine the arithmetic descent map on the symmetric square.

## 2. The canonical symmetric-square descent map

Let

\[
W_u=\operatorname{Sym}^2V_u.
\]

The correct normalization should be made directly on \(W_u\).  There is no need to choose a square root of six in the coefficient field.  Define

\[
\boxed{
C_u:W_{u'}\longrightarrow W_u,
\qquad
C_u=-\frac16\operatorname{Sym}^2(L_u).}
\]

Then

\[
C_uC_{u'}
=\frac1{36}\operatorname{Sym}^2(L_uL_{u'})
=\frac1{36}\operatorname{Sym}^2(-6\,\mathrm{id})
=\mathrm{id}.
\]

Thus \(C_u\) is the involutive deck descent map.  Its scalar on the square of the Hodge line is

\[
-\frac{\kappa_u^2}{6}
=\frac89(1+u)^2,
\]

which is exactly the normalized scalar already printed and checked in `codex_lt_isogeny.py`.

The minus sign is not decorative.  It fixes the \(+\)-descent convention used in the project, for which

\[
\det\mathcal A_+
=\mathbf Q_\ell(-3)\otimes\mathcal K_q.
\]

At an inert point \(\mathcal K_q(\operatorname{Frob}_t)=-1\), so the determinant of the \(+\)-stalk Frobenius must be \(-p^3\).  The normalization above has

\[
\det C_u
=\left(-\frac16\right)^3
  \det(\operatorname{Sym}^2L_u)
=\left(-\frac16\right)^3(\det L_u)^3
=-1,
\]

and therefore gives exactly this determinant.

## 3. Inert fibre: the half-Frobenius calculation

Assume now that \(q(t)\) is nonsquare in \(\mathbf F_p\).  Then

\[
u^p=u'.
\]

Let

\[
F_u:V_u\longrightarrow V_{u'},
\qquad
F_{u'}:V_{u'}\longrightarrow V_u
\]

be the one-step arithmetic Frobenius transports.  Their composite is the \(p^2\)-Frobenius on \(V_u\):

\[
F_{u'}F_u=F_{p^2,u}.
\]

Under the point-count convention,

\[
\det F_u=p,
\qquad
\det F_{p^2,u}=p^2.
\]

### 3.1 Frobenius conjugates the isogeny by the \((-3)\)-character

Put

\[
\epsilon_3=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right).
\]

Since \(w^p=\epsilon_3w\), Frobenius conjugation changes the twist isomorphism \(	au_{u,w}\) by \([\epsilon_3]\).  Therefore

\[
\psi_u^{(p)}=[\epsilon_3]\circ\psi_{u'}.
\]

On \(H^1\), because \([-1]^*\) acts as \(-1\), this becomes

\[
\boxed{L_u^{(p)}=\epsilon_3L_{u'}.}
\]

Functoriality of Frobenius and pullback gives

\[
F_uL_u=L_u^{(p)}F_{u'}
       =\epsilon_3L_{u'}F_{u'}.
\]

The key point is that the \((-3)\)-twist is encountered **once**.  At a split fibre a symmetric square sees its square and the twist disappears.  At an inert fibre the half-Frobenius uses one isogeny comparison before returning to the original stalk, so the character survives.

### 3.2 A rank-two auxiliary operator

Define the endomorphism

\[
R:=L_uF_u:V_u\longrightarrow V_u.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
R^2
 &=L_uF_uL_uF_u\\
 &=L_uL_u^{(p)}F_{u'}F_u\\
 &=\epsilon_3L_uL_{u'}F_{p^2,u}\\
 &=-6\epsilon_3F_{p^2,u}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{R^2=-6\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)F_{p^2,u}.}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{\det R=(\det L_u)(\det F_u)=6p.}
\]

This pair of identities contains the entire sign calculation.

### 3.3 The rank-three half-Frobenius

Under the identification of the \(+\)-descent stalk with \(W_u\), Frobenius is

\[
D=C_u\operatorname{Sym}^2(F_u)
  =-\frac16\operatorname{Sym}^2(L_uF_u)
  =-\frac16\operatorname{Sym}^2R.
\]

Let \(r,s\) be the eigenvalues of \(R\).  If \(\alpha,eta\) are the eigenvalues of \(F_{p^2,u}\), then the formula for \(R^2\) gives

\[
r^2=-6\epsilon_3\alpha,
\qquad
s^2=-6\epsilon_3\beta,
\]

and the determinant formula gives

\[
rs=6p.
\]

The three eigenvalues of \(D\) are therefore

\[
-\frac{r^2}{6}=\epsilon_3\alpha,
\qquad
-\frac{rs}{6}=-p,
\qquad
-\frac{s^2}{6}=\epsilon_3\beta.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}(D)
=\{\epsilon_3\alpha,-p,\epsilon_3\beta\}.}
\]

Taking traces gives

\[
\boxed{
T_+(t)=\operatorname{Tr}D
=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)a_2-p.}
\]

On the lisse locus

\[
\mathcal A_-=\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal K_q.
\]

At an inert point \(\mathcal K_q(\operatorname{Frob}_t)=-1\), so

\[
\boxed{T_-(t)=-T_+(t).}
\]

This proves that the sign is global in \(t\): the only character occurring is the constant twist character \((-3/p)\).

### 3.4 Characteristic polynomial and determinant

The characteristic polynomial is

\[
\boxed{
\det(X-D)
=X^3-(\epsilon_3a_2-p)X^2
 +(p^2-\epsilon_3pa_2)X+p^3.}
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\boxed{\det D=-p^3.}
\]

There are two independent ways to see this:

1. from the eigenvalues,
   \[
   (\epsilon_3\alpha)(-p)(\epsilon_3\beta)
   =-p\alpha\beta=-p^3;
   \]
2. from the normalized correspondence,
   \[
   \det D=(\det C_u)\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2F_u)
   =(-1)p^3.
   \]

Thus the determinant sanity check is unchanged and does not distinguish the two possible common outer signs; the isogeny conjugation does.

## 4. Where the \(9/8\) gauge argument went wrong

### 4.1 What remains correct

The equation \(\phi(u)=t\) is

\[
8u^2+(t-1)u+t=0.
\]

At an inert point the other root is \(u^p\), so

\[
u+u^p=\frac{1-t}{8},
\qquad
uu^p=\frac t8.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathbf F_{p^2}/\mathbf F_p}(1+u)
=(1+u)(1+u^p)=\frac98.}
\]

Consequently the quadratic Kummer line attached to \(1+u\) has degree-two eigenvalue

\[
\left(\frac{9/8}{p}\right)=\left(\frac2p\right).
\]

Every line above is correct.

### 4.2 The invalid inference

The invalid step in Q6457 was the inference

```text
Kummer eigenvalue (2/p)
    therefore
outer sign of the descended Sym^2 half-Frobenius = (2/p).
```

The period identity

\[
S_+(\phi(u))=\sqrt{1+u}\,h(u)
\]

fixes the **geometric** pullback and its Hodge gauge.  It does not, by itself, fix the arithmetic deck descent on

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2H^1(E_u).
\]

That descent also contains the normalized isogeny correspondence

\[
C_u=-\frac16\operatorname{Sym}^2L_u.
\]

At an inert point this correspondence is used once.  Its Frobenius conjugation contains the constant \((-3)\)-twist, and its two-sheet composition is \(-6\), not \(1\).

### 4.3 The missing square class is exactly \(-6\)

The Hodge scalar of the raw isogeny comparison is

\[
\kappa_u=\frac{4(1+u)\sqrt{-3}}3.
\]

The square of the comparison scalar suggested by the period gauge is

\[
\frac{1+u}{1+u'}
=\frac89(1+u)^2.
\]

Their ratio is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa_u^2}{(1+u)/(1+u')}=-6.}
\]

Equivalently, a rank-two square-root comparison of the raw isogeny and the gauge differs by a square root of \(-6\).  Passing from the geometric gauge to the arithmetic descent therefore changes the inert outer-sign character by

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right).}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac{9/8}{p}\right)
\left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)
=\left(\frac2p\right)
 \left(\frac{-6}{p}\right)
=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right).}
\]

This is the same \(-6\) that appears in

\[
L_uL_{u'}=-6\,\mathrm{id}.
\]

Thus it is the actual isogeny-normalization mechanism, not a fitted correction.

A useful conceptual warning is that one should not try to repair the proof merely by saying “twist the rank-two root sheaf by a constant quadratic character.”  A quadratic scalar twist disappears under an ordinary symmetric square.  What was missing is the **arithmetic lifting of the deck involution**, including its similitude normalization and Frobenius conjugation.  The direct rank-three formula

\[
D=-\frac16\operatorname{Sym}^2(L_uF_u)
\]

is canonical and avoids the square-root ambiguity entirely.

## 7. Corrected exact recipe

For every good prime \(p>3\), compute once

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_p=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right).}
\]

Let \(A_p(t)\) be the Apéry truncation modulo \(p\).  For each \(t\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), put

\[
d=q(t)=t^2-34t+1,
\qquad
s=\left(\frac d p\right).
\]

```text
input: good prime p > 3
       Ap(t) = Apéry truncation modulo p
       eps = Legendre(-3,p)

for t in F_p^*:
    d = t^2 - 34*t + 1
    s = Legendre(d,p)

    if s == +1:                         # smooth split fibre
        z = sqrt(d) in F_p
        u1 = (1 - t + z)/16
        u2 = (1 - t - z)/16

        a1 = p + 1 - #E_u1(F_p)
        a2 = p + 1 - #E_u2(F_p)
        f1 = a1^2 - p
        f2 = a2^2 - p

        assert f1 == f2                 # a2 may equal (-3|p)*a1
        Tplus[t]  = f1
        Tminus[t] = f1
        P[t]      = 2*f1

    elif s == -1:                       # inert fibre
        construct F_p2 = F_p[z]/(z^2-d)
        u = (1 - t + z)/16 in F_p2

        a_deg2 = p^2 + 1 - #E_u(F_p2)

        Tplus[t]  = eps*a_deg2 - p      # eps = Legendre(-3,p)
        Tminus[t] = -Tplus[t]
        P[t]      = 0

    else:                               # rational branch fibre
        u = (1 - t)/16
        a = p + 1 - #E_u(F_p)
        f = a^2 - p

        Tminus[t] = p
        Tplus[t]  = f - p               # = a^2 - 2p
        P[t]      = f

    assert Tplus[t] + Tminus[t] == P[t]
    assert Tplus[t]  mod p == Ap(t)
    assert Tminus[t] mod p == s*Ap(t) mod p
    assert abs(Tplus[t])  <= 3*p
    assert abs(Tminus[t]) <= 3*p
```

The inert value is independent of which square root of \(d\) is chosen: replacing \(u\) by \(u^p\) replaces the fibre by its conjugate and leaves the \(p^2\)-point count, hence \(a_2\), unchanged.

The integral virtual trace remains

\[
V(t)=-P(t)+T_-(t)=-T_+(t)
\]

at split, inert, and branch fibres.  Thus all Mellin and inversion formulas from Q6457 remain valid after replacing the inert sign only.  In particular, for \(1\le r\le p-2\), reduction at the chosen prime above \(p\) gives the same Apéry coefficient identity; the \(r=0,p-1\) endpoint aliasing convention is unchanged.

## 8. Exact answer to the “coincidence or mechanism?” question

**[PROVED] It is the actual mechanism.**

The degree-six isogeny contributes the similitude multiplier \(6\).  The constant \((-3)\)-twist needed to identify its quotient with the deck-conjugate fibre makes the two deck comparisons compose as

\[
[-6],
\]

not \([6]\).  The canonical rank-three descent divides the symmetric square by \(-6\):

\[
C_u=-\frac16\operatorname{Sym}^2L_u.
\]

At an inert point, Frobenius conjugates \(L_u\) to \((-3/p)L_{u'}\).  Thus

\[
(L_uF_u)^2=-6\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)F_{p^2,u},
\]

and the normalization by \(-1/6\) cancels the degree while leaving the \((-3/p)\)-character.  The gauge-only value \((2/p)\) is missing precisely the square class \(-6\).
