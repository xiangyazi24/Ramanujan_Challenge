Ramanujan Challenge Problem 3.2, paper proof only, no Lean. Read the
latest main branch and especially Sections 148, 154, 156, and 166 of the
current Codex working note. Work on the remaining direct q=1 fixed-sum
diagonal, but exploit the exact hard-order parametrization rather than
generic interpolation.

For a target prime p=n-j with p>2j, put

  g=gcd(p-1,n-1),  n-1=gD,  j=gk.

Then

  p=1+g(D-k),  gcd(k,D)=1.

The low-order estimate already makes every slice with

  d=(p-1)/g <= n^(1-epsilon)

negligible. Thus the hard range has g<n^epsilon and d near p. Existing
multi-hit examples already have g=1,2,3, so this is not empty.

Find and exploit any Apéry-specific identity that becomes stronger in
this small-kernel parametrization. Candidates include a g-dissection or
multiplication formula for A_{gk}; the Franel rational substitution;
the constant-term/diagonal representation grouped by g-th powers; an
induction/restriction formula for the high-order Kummer Mellin
coefficient; or Dwork congruences along the arithmetic progression gk.
The objective is a pointwise bound for

  sum log(1+g(D-k))

over coprime k<=D/3 satisfying

  1+g(D-k) prime and divides A_{gk},

uniformly for g<n^epsilon, strong enough after summing over divisors
g|n-1. A strictly smaller explicit lemma with verified quantifiers is
also useful.

Do not repeat the O(D tau(n)) low-order count, fixed-field character
equidistribution, or a generic CRT countermodel whose recurrence/initial
state may vary with the prime. Test every proposed identity exactly on
the repository scan data. If small g supplies no extra structure, prove
the first exact obstruction: identify why the g-dissection or induced
character still has the original moving-prime quantifier. Record failed
routes and all load-bearing assumptions. Put formulas in fenced plain
text blocks rather than Notion equation blocks.
