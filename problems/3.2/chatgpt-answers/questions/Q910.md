Ramanujan Challenge Problem 3.2, direct q=1 branch. Seek a TARGET-SPECIFIC p-adic amplification that turns a moving Apéry zero plus many companion zeros into a high power of p dividing one globally small integer.

Definitions:

    A_m = sum_{r=0}^m binom(m,r)^2 binom(m+r,r)^2
    n = 3H+1
    j = n-p,  0 <= j <= H,  2H < p <= 3H+1
    z_p(H) = #{0 <= s <= H : p | A_s}.

The target primes satisfy p|A_j. Ordinary projective resultants are useless because they record any prefix zero and forget j. A fixed-degree anchored Pade determinant is also insufficient: primes with a fixed number of prefix zeros create apparently linear-weight pollution. We need to exploit that j is itself a zero and p+j=n.

Try to prove an exact implication of the following general shape. If p is a target and z_p(H)=z, then either p lies in an explicitly bounded exceptional set, or

    p^f(z) | E_H

for an explicit integer E_H with log|E_H|=O(H polylog H), where f(z) grows at least linearly in z, so high-zero targets have sublinear radical weight. E_H may be a product of marked continuants, Casoratians, recurrence transfer determinants, derivatives at j, subdiscriminants with the marked factor retained, or a factorial-normalized hypergeometric certificate. It must not be an ordinary unmarked resultant whose height simply counts every zero.

Audit p-adic valuations, recurrence singularities, and denominator primes. A determinant containing z zero nodes often only gains p^z while having zH-scale height; quantify whether any compression is genuine. Test the proposed implication symbolically or with exact computations if useful, but do not infer it from data. If the amplification is impossible from the second-order recurrence alone, construct an exact recurrence-compatible countermodel and identify the extra Apéry/hypergeometric identity that would be needed.

The requested output is one complete attempted derivation with a definite verdict and the sharpest proved inequality. Put every essential formula also in fenced plain text.
