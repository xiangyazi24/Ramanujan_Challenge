Ramanujan Challenge Problem 3.2, direct q=1 branch. Develop a theorem-grade FACTORIAL-MOMENT bound for the prefix-zero counts, with special attention to target-conditioned primes.

Let

    A_m = sum_r binom(m,r)^2 binom(m+r,r)^2,
    n=3H+1,
    Z_p(H)={0<=s<=H:p|A_s},
    z_p(H)=|Z_p(H)|,

for candidate primes 2H<p<=3H+1. The growing Newton-Pade family reduces its automatic pollution to z_p(H)>B. Fixed B is likely insufficient numerically; we need B=B(H)->infinity. A mere pointwise z_p(H)=O(p^(2/3)) gives no weighted tail estimate.

Use the exact second-order Apéry recurrence and its gap continuants/Casoratians to bound one or more weighted factorial moments

    M_r(H)=sum_{2H<p<=3H+1} (z_p(H))_r log p,

or the target-conditioned version with p|A_{3H+1-p}. Every pair of zero nodes forces p to divide a gap continuant, but the naive product over all pairs has excessive height. Seek a compressed multi-zero determinant, subdiscriminant, nonintersecting-path identity, or recurrence resultant in which z distinct zero nodes give p to a superlinear power while total logarithmic height is substantially below the naive sum of all gap lengths.

Calculate the exponents and heights explicitly. Optimize Markov's inequality

    sum_{z_p(H)>B} log p <= M_r(H)/(B)_r

with both r fixed and r growing, and determine whether any rigorously derived bound reaches o(H) for some B no larger than the available Pade degree scale O(H^(2/3)). If target conditioning gives an extra marked zero at j=3H+1-p, retain that mark instead of passing to an ordinary resultant that forgets it.

Known traps that must be addressed:

* each gap continuant N_h(s) has large polynomial/factorial height;
* reflection supplies a forced factor p but ordinarily no second p-adic digit;
* ordinary projective resultants count any zero and lose the moving node;
* a generic cardinality or Poisson heuristic is not a proof.

Give the strongest unconditional moment inequality you can actually prove, with every range and normalization explicit. If all continuant factorial moments provably miss the needed exponent, give the sharp optimization/no-go and identify the smallest Apéry-specific compression lemma that would change it. Put every essential formula also in fenced plain text. Do not return a list of possible methods.
