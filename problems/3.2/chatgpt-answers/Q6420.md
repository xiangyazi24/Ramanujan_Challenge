# R3-g-2 — Dispatch A: Franel Type-II bilinearization at k=2

## Executive verdict

Missing Lemma FD is dead in its stated exact-zero form for the standard Franel/Mellin functorial route. The verified pullback supplies a bounded number of bounded-complexity linear Mellin channels for the value b_r mod p. It does not supply a bounded-complexity channel for the nonlinear function 1_(b_r=0 mod p). Once the exact zero detector is inserted, the candidate Type-II kernel is an additive character of a defining-characteristic Frobenius trace. No fixed-rank sheaf operation produces it, and the standard polynomial/tensor realization has complexity growing at least p^(1/3) unconditionally from the project’s all-value collision bound, and canonically has degree p-1.

The positive skeleton is real:

1. the Franel pullback and its quadratic deck involution give two fixed linear Mellin channels;

1. splitting the character exponent r=mL+n, with L≈sqrt(p), would create a genuine balanced Type-II kernel if the nonlinear phase were a uniformly bounded trace function;

1. fixed-degree polynomial observables of the Mellin trace do remain in a bounded-complexity tensor/convolution category and are legitimate calibration targets.

The failure occurs exactly at the map

The death is scoped. It covers every construction tried here that uses a fixed number of Franel pullbacks/pushforwards, deck channels, Kummer twists, Fourier/Mellin transforms, tensor operations of bounded degree, and then a regular/Tannakian function of the resulting trace values. It does not prove that no unexpected, p-dependent Franel identity can exist, and it does not rule out a genuinely new defining-characteristic phase module or a global bad-diagonal inverse theorem. Such an object would be outside the standard bounded-conductor trace-function calculus.

The strongest k=2 statement that survives is therefore:

For every fixed polynomial degree D, polynomial observables of the linear Apéry Mellin trace admit O_D(1) tensor/convolution channels of bounded complexity and have a meaningful trace-function dispersion program. No implication from these fixed-degree moments to the exact zero-pair correlation is presently available, because an exact detector requires D→∞ with p.

## 10. Final verdict and scope

### What is killed

- exact FD for g_p through the standard fixed-degree Franel/Mellin tensor-convolution calculus;

- Weyl differencing as a route to a bounded phase sheaf;

- the degree-two deck cover as a source of two sqrt(p)-length variables;

- a Voronoi transform based only on the four linear singularities;

- the idea that applying multiplicative characters to the value avoids the additive defining-characteristic obstruction;

- Fejér or higher-valuation smoothing at bounded complexity.

### What remains alive

- fixed-degree tensor moments FD(D) as a calibration of the linear sheaf and variable-modulus dispersion machinery;

- a new defining-characteristic phase module whose complexity is proved p^o(1) by a mechanism not expressible as fixed tensor degree;

- a bad-diagonal inverse theorem converting positive-density exact zeros into bounded Kummer self-twists;

- an unexpected cross-prime arithmetic identity that does not factor through the value-functional calculus.

### Precise classification of the death

This is not a theorem that every conceivable Franel-derived representation has large conductor. An ad hoc constructible function can always encode the actual zero set, and a future geometric construction might exploit structure not visible in the Mellin trace value alone. The result is a no-go for the complete family of natural representations tested here and for the standard functorial/Tannakian closure in which present trace-function bilinear theorems operate.
