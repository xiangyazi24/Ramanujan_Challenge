# Independent proof-seeking attempt on Ramanujan Challenge Problem 3.2

## Verdict: deliverable (C)

I do not obtain the full all-(n) proof. I do obtain a sharp exact obstruction to what appears to be the strongest still-plausible “missed identity” route: combine the mod-(p) reflection pair of an Apéry zero with the gap continuant, and eliminate the moving residue by a global resultant.

The obstruction is stronger than “the resultant estimate is weak.” For every noncentral reflection pair, the continuant divisibility furnished by the two zero endpoints is identically generated by a forced central linear factor. Thus the mod-(p) certificate is true for every prime/residue pair of the required shape, even when the residue is not an Apéry zero. Its natural resultant is exactly zero. Dividing out the factor does not repair the route: a concrete genuine zero pair at ((p,t,h)=(17,3,10)) has nonzero quotient modulo (17).

What would reopen the route is not another mod-(p) identity. One needs genuinely new mod-(p^2) information on the normalized values (b_t/p) and (b_{p-1-t}/p), or a second independent certificate not sharing the forced factor.

Before proving this, I audit the denominator and support identities, because otherwise the obstruction could be aimed at the wrong target.

# 1. The strongest exact denominator identity already present

Let

[

L_n=operatorname{lcm}(1,ldots,n),qquad d_n=L_n^3.

]

Define factorial-scaled integer solutions

[

X_n=frac{(n!)^3a_n}{6},qquad Y_n=(n!)^3b_n.

]

The initial values are

[

(X_0,X_1)=(0,1),qquad (Y_0,Y_1)=(1,5),

]

and both satisfy the integral continuant recurrence

[

U_{n+1}=P(n)U_n-n^6U_{n-1},

qquad

P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.

tag{1.1}

]

In particular, (X_n,Y_ninmathbb Z) without invoking any denominator conjecture.

The Wronskian becomes the exact determinant identity

[

boxed{

X_{n+1}Y_n-X_nY_{n+1}=(n!)^6.

}

tag{1.2}

]

Indeed, substituting

[

a_n=frac{6X_n}{(n!)^3},qquad b_n=frac{Y_n}{(n!)^3}

]

into

[

a_{n+1}b_n-a_nb_{n+1}=frac6{(n+1)^3}

]

gives (1.2) exactly.

Now put

[

c_n=left(frac{n!}{L_n}right)^3inmathbb Z.

]

Then

[

d_na_n=frac{6X_n}{c_n},qquad d_nb_n=frac{Y_n}{c_n}.

]

Since both displayed quotients are integers,

[

boxed{

G_n=gcd(d_na_n,d_nb_n)

=frac{gcd(6X_n,Y_n)}{c_n}.

}

tag{1.3}

]

Consequently, the reduced denominator of the rational approximant (a_n/b_n) is exactly

[

boxed{

B_n=frac{Y_n}{gcd(6X_n,Y_n)}

=frac{d_nb_n}{G_n}.

}

tag{1.4}

]

Thus there is indeed a canonical determinant/Smith-normal-form formulation, but it is not a new compression of the gcd. It is exactly the same gcd in factorial coordinates. Identity (1.2) controls one prime at a time through a neighboring determinant; it contains no relation between distinct primes (p) and (q) dividing the same row ((6X_n,Y_n)).

This also explains why a putative “product of binomial terms” formula is unlikely to be hiding in the standard continued fraction. The complete universal determinant is already (1.2), and after removing the predictable factorial content (c_n), the unexplained row content is precisely (G_n).

This is not an impossibility theorem for every conceivable denominator formula. It is an exact audit conclusion: the natural generalized-continued-fraction, determinant, and Smith-normal-form identities do not improve the pointwise problem; they restate it.

# 4. Forced-central-factor obstruction

## Theorem 4.1

For every even (hge2), there is a polynomial (C_h(x)inmathbb Z[x]) such that

[

boxed{

N_h(x)=(2x+h+1)C_h(x).

}

tag{4.1}

]

Consequently, for every odd prime (p) and every integer (t) satisfying

[

h=p-1-2tge2,

]

one has

[

boxed{

pmid N_h(t)}

tag{4.2}

]

with no assumption whatever that (b_tequiv0pmod p).

### Proof

The exact reflection identity is

[

N_h(-x-h)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x-1).

tag{4.3}

]

Put (z=x-1). Then

[

N_h(-z-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(z).

tag{4.4}

]

If (h) is even, this becomes

[

N_h(-z-h-1)=-N_h(z).

]

The involution (zmapsto-z-h-1) has fixed point

[

z=-frac{h+1}{2},

]

so

[

N_h!left(-frac{h+1}{2}right)=0.

]

Thus (2z+h+1) divides (N_h(z)) in (mathbb Q[z]). The linear polynomial (2z+h+1) is primitive, so Gauss's lemma gives the quotient in (mathbb Z[z]), proving (4.1).

If (h=p-1-2t), then

[

2t+h+1=p.

]

Evaluating (4.1) at (t) gives the exact integer identity

[

N_h(t)=pC_h(t),

tag{4.5}

]

which proves (4.2). (square)

## Corollary 4.2: the natural resultant is identically zero

For every even (h),

[

boxed{

operatorname{Res}_xbigl(N_h(x),2x+h+1bigr)=0.

}

tag{4.6}

]

Therefore eliminating the two equations in (3.7) produces no nonzero global integer whose prime divisors can be bounded. The two equations share an exact polynomial component.

## Why this directly attacks adversarial cross-prime alignment

For a fixed (n), write (n=qp+r). After reflecting (r) to (t), relation (3.2) gives

[

Hence collecting these certificates over many primes at the same (n) cannot create a nonzero product/resultant coupling the primes: every certificate lies on the same forced component before any Apéry-zero information is used.

This is stronger than the generic warning that “local symmetry cannot prevent alignment.” It identifies the exact algebraic reason the most natural global resultant cannot see the alignment.

# 7. Information that would reopen the route

Any successful repair must supply information beyond the verified mod-(p) support law. Three concrete possibilities would suffice.

## 7.1 A universal mod-(p^2) reflection law on zero digits

A relation strong enough to force the right side of (6.2) to vanish for every zero digit would give

[

pmid C_h(t),

]

and hence a genuine quotient certificate. Existing mod-(p^2) Lucas theory does not provide this universally. Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler prove mod-(p^2) Lucas congruences only when the base-(p) digits lie in a certain restricted set; equivalently, mod-(p^2) reflection is exceptional rather than automatic.

The example ((p,t)=(17,3)) shows directly that the needed quotient vanishing is not a formal consequence of the known mod-(p) reflection.

## 7.2 A horizontal theorem for normalized zero values

It would be enough to prove that, for every fixed (n), all but (o(n/log n)) of the bad primes satisfy the extra normalized relation making (6.2) vanish. This is a genuinely cross-prime statement about

[

left(frac{b_t}{p},frac{b_{p-1-t}}pright)pmod p,

]

not about (|Z_p|), spacing, or monodromy at one fixed prime.

## 7.3 A second independent certificate

A second polynomial relation

[

M_h(t)equiv0pmod p

]

whose gcd with (2x+h+1) is generically (1), and whose degree/height is controlled uniformly in (h), could be combined with (N_h) by a nonzero resultant. The present endpoint transfer identity supplies only the forced component. One would need an additional arithmetic datum—perhaps a derivative, a neighboring normalized value, or a genuinely two-gap condition—that does not collapse under reflection.

These are sharp reopening conditions: each identifies the exact extra datum required, rather than calling vaguely for “more cancellation.”

# 8. Why recent structural results do not bypass the obstruction

The primary literature checked confirms the following division of labor.

- Gessel's Lucas congruence gives

- Rowland--Yassawi--Krattenthaler obtain Lucas congruences modulo (p^2) only for integers whose digits lie in a specified subset. This is exactly the right depth to matter for (C_h(t)), but not a universal theorem covering every moving zero residue.

- Caruso--Fürnsinn--Vargas-Montoya--Zudilin determine the Galois groups and the square/quadratic-times-square factorization of the mod-(p) Apéry generating polynomial. This is powerful fixed-prime structure, but it supplies neither the normalized endpoint values in (6.2) nor a relation coupling the moving residues for different primes at one fixed (n).

Accordingly, the obstruction above is compatible with the strongest current mod-(p) structure: the issue begins exactly one (p)-adic layer deeper.

# 9. Final conclusion

The exact-identity audit produces the following chain.

1. The reduced denominator and determinant identities are already completely described by (1.2)--(1.4); they do not mix distinct primes.

1. The large-prime support law is exactly two-channel, with all quantifiers valid:

1. The companion channel has logarithmic weight (O(n^{2/3})), so the hard theorem is exactly (2.8).

1. For every noncentral bad residue, reflection creates an even gap (h=p-1-2t), and the pair continuant gives (pmid N_h(t)).

1. But

1. The quotient condition (pmid C_h(t)) is false even for a genuine zero pair: ((p,t,h)=(17,3,10)) gives (C_{10}(3)equiv2pmod{17}).

Therefore the tempting global-resultant route is closed at the exact algebraic point where it was supposed to attack adversarial cross-prime alignment. Reopening it requires mod-(p^2) normalized-zero data or an independent certificate. No presently known denominator, factorial-ratio, Lucas, or fixed-prime Galois identity supplies that information uniformly.

This does not solve Problem 3.2, but it is a proof-grade obstruction rather than another statement that the problem is open.
