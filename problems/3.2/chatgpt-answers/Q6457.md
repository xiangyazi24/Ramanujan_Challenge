# Integral traces of the two Franel–Apéry eigen-descents

## Executive verdict

There are three important corrections to the proposed conventions.

1. **Split fibres behave exactly as expected.** Once the deck correspondence is inserted into the definition of the involution, Frobenius acts on both eigenspaces by an operator conjugate to Frobenius on either source stalk. Thus
   \[
   T_+(t)=T_-(t)=f_p(u)=a_p(E_u)^2-p
   \]
   at every smooth split fibre. Equality of the two numerical values is automatic from the descent datum; equality of traces alone is not the input.

2. **At an inert fibre, point counting over \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\) leaves one common outer sign, but the normalized rank-two pullback fixes it.** If
   \[
   a_2=p^2+1-\#E_u(\mathbf F_{p^2}),\qquad
   \varepsilon_p=\left(\frac2p\right),
   \]
   then the exact integral traces are
   \[
   \boxed{T_+(t)=\varepsilon_p a_2-p,\qquad
          T_-(t)=-\varepsilon_p a_2+p.}
   \]
   Equivalently, if \(\alpha,\beta\) are the \(p^2\)-Frobenius eigenvalues on \(H^1(E_u)\), the eigenvalues on the descended rank-three \(+\) stalk are
   \[
   \boxed{\varepsilon_p\alpha,\ -p,\ \varepsilon_p\beta.}
   \]
   The determinant fixes the middle sign \(-p\), but **does not by itself choose** between the two common outer signs. The factor \(\varepsilon_p=(2/p)\) comes from the Kummer gauge \(\sqrt{1+u}\) in the exact pullback.

3. **The generic Mellin cohomology dimensions are not two for both sheaves.** With the local monodromy stated in the question,
   \[
   \dim H_c^1(\mathbf G_m,\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)=2,
   \qquad
   \dim H_c^1(\mathbf G_m,\mathcal A_-\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)=4
   \]
   for generic \(\chi\). Hence the generic Deligne bounds are
   \[
   |S_+(\chi)|\le 2p^{3/2},\qquad |S_-(\chi)|\le4p^{3/2}.
   \]
   The **residual virtual object** is dimension two because
   \[
   -\phi_*\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal E+\mathcal A_-
   =-\mathcal A_+.
   \]

At a rational branch point \(q(t)=0\), the correct middle-extension traces are
\[
\boxed{T_-(t)=p,\qquad T_+(t)=a_p(E_u)^2-2p,}
\]
not \(T_-(t)=0\). The reduction of \(T_-\) is nevertheless zero, which is why the residual computation did not see this integral correction.

## 0. Frobenius and Tate conventions

I use the project’s point-count convention: on a smooth elliptic fibre over \(\mathbf F_{p^m}\), the operator denoted Frobenius has characteristic polynomial
\[
X^2-a_mX+p^m,
\qquad a_m=p^m+1-\#E(\mathbf F_{p^m}),
\]
and \(\mathbf Q_\ell(-1)\) has Frobenius eigenvalue \(p\) at an \(\mathbf F_p\)-point.

Let
\[
\mathcal V=R^1\pi_*\mathbf Q_\ell,
\qquad
\mathcal W=\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal V.
\]
At a smooth rational source point \(u\), if the elliptic trace is \(a=a_p(E_u)\), then
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_u\mid\mathcal W_u)=a^2-p=:f_p(u).
\]
The rank-three descents are normalized as in the current repository:
\[
\mathcal A_-\simeq\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal K_q,
\]
with Tate factors restored,
\[
\det\mathcal A_+=\mathbf Q_\ell(-3)\otimes\mathcal K_q,
\qquad
\det\mathcal A_-=\mathbf Q_\ell(-3).
\]
Thus at a smooth rational point
\[
\det(\operatorname{Frob}\mid\mathcal A_+)=p^3\chi_2(q(t)),
\qquad
\det(\operatorname{Frob}\mid\mathcal A_-)=p^3.
\]

The notation \(\sigma\) below means the deck action **together with the chosen descent correspondence**. A bare permutation of two unrelated stalk vector spaces is not a well-defined operator.

## 1. Split fibres

Let \(t\in\mathbf F_p\) be smooth and split, with
\[
\phi^{-1}(t)=\{u,u'\}\subset\mathbf F_p,
\qquad u'=\iota(u).
\]
Write
\[
W_0=\mathcal W_u,\qquad W_1=\mathcal W_{u'}.
\]
The normalized symmetric-square isogeny supplies mutually inverse maps
\[
c:W_0\longrightarrow W_1,
\qquad c^{-1}:W_1\longrightarrow W_0.
\]
The deck involution on the pushforward stalk is
\[
\delta(v,w)=(c^{-1}w,cv).
\]
Its eigenspaces are
\[
(W_0\oplus W_1)^+
 =\{(v,cv):v\in W_0\},
\]
\[
(W_0\oplus W_1)^-
 =\{(v,-cv):v\in W_0\}.
\]

Because both points are rational, Frobenius is diagonal:
\[
\Phi(v,w)=(F_0v,F_1w).
\]
Frobenius compatibility of the descent datum says
\[
F_1c=cF_0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\Phi(v,\pm cv)=(F_0v,\pm cF_0v).
\]
Under either identification
\[
W_0\xrightarrow{\sim}(W_0\oplus W_1)^\pm,
\qquad v\longmapsto(v,\pm cv),
\]
Frobenius is exactly \(F_0\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{T_+(t)=T_-(t)=\operatorname{Tr}(F_0)=f_p(u).}
\]

This also gives
\[
P_p(t)=T_+(t)+T_-(t)=2f_p(u).
\]

### Projector calculation

Since \(\Phi\) and \(\delta\) commute,
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi\mid A_\pm)
 =\frac12\left(\operatorname{Tr}\Phi
               \pm\operatorname{Tr}(\delta\Phi)\right).
\]
At a split fibre, \(\delta\Phi\) is off-diagonal on \(W_0\oplus W_1\), so
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\delta\Phi)=0.
\]
Moreover \(F_1=cF_0c^{-1}\), so
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\Phi=2\operatorname{Tr}F_0.
\]
This again yields \(T_+=T_-=f_p(u)\).

A useful subtlety is that the rank-two elliptic traces need not themselves be equal: the explicit degree-six correspondence in `codex_lt_isogeny.py` gives
\[
a_p(E_{u'})=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)a_p(E_u).
\]
The constant quadratic twist disappears after \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\), which is why the rank-three Frobenius operators are conjugate and the \(f_p\)-values agree.

## 2. Inert fibres and the half-Frobenius

Let \(q(t)\) be nonsquare. The two geometric preimages satisfy
\[
u^p=\bar u=\iota(u),
\qquad u\in\mathbf F_{p^2}\setminus\mathbf F_p.
\]
Put
\[
W_0=\mathcal W_u,
\qquad W_1=\mathcal W_{u^p}.
\]
Let
\[
a:W_0\longrightarrow W_1,
\qquad b:W_1\longrightarrow W_0
\]
be the two Frobenius-semilinear transport maps, so that on the induced stalk
\[
\Phi(v,w)=(bw,av).
\]
Use the same descent maps \(c:W_0\to W_1\) and \(c^{-1}:W_1\to W_0\), and set
\[
\delta(v,w)=(c^{-1}w,cv).
\]
The condition \(\delta\Phi=\Phi\delta\) gives
\[
c^{-1}a=bc,
\qquad cb=ac^{-1}.
\]
Define the half-Frobenius operator on \(W_0\)
\[
D:=c^{-1}a=bc.
\]
Then
\[
\Phi(v,cv)=(Dv,cDv),
\]
while
\[
\Phi(v,-cv)=(-Dv,cDv).
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Frob}_t\mid A_+\simeq D,
       \qquad
       \operatorname{Frob}_t\mid A_-\simeq-D.}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{T_-(t)=-T_+(t).}
\]
Also
\[
D^2=ba=\operatorname{Frob}_{p^2}\mid W_0.
\]
This proves the square-root description without making any choice of basis.

### 2.1 What \(\operatorname{Frob}_{p^2}\) alone determines

Let \(\alpha,\beta\) be the eigenvalues of \(p^2\)-Frobenius on \(H^1(E_u)\):
\[
\alpha+\beta=a_2,
\qquad \alpha\beta=p^2,
\qquad a_2=p^2+1-\#E_u(\mathbf F_{p^2}).
\]
Then on \(W_0=\operatorname{Sym}^2H^1(E_u)\),
\[
D^2\text{ has eigenvalues }\alpha^2,p^2,\beta^2.
\]
Hence the eigenvalues of \(D\) are signed choices from
\[
\pm\alpha,\quad\pm p,\quad\pm\beta.
\]
The fact that \(D\) is itself a symmetric square imposes
\[
(\text{first outer sign})(\text{second outer sign})=+1.
\]
The determinant condition
\[
\det D=-p^3
\]
forces the middle eigenvalue to be \(-p\). Thus only two possibilities remain:
\[
\{\alpha,-p,\beta\}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\{-\alpha,-p,-\beta\}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
T_+(t)=-p\pm a_2,
\]
or
\[
\boxed{(T_+(t)+p)^2=a_2^2
       =\operatorname{Tr}(D^2)+p^2.}
\]
Therefore the characteristic polynomial of \(D^2\) and the determinant of \(D\) do **not** determine the remaining sign.

### 2.2 Exact sign from the Kummer gauge

The rank-two Apéry-root system \(\mathcal S_+\) is normalized by the exact pullback relation
\[
\phi^*\mathcal S_+
 \simeq R^1\pi_*\mathbf Q_\ell\otimes\mathcal L_{\chi_2(1+u)}.
\]
This is the sheaf form of
\[
S_+(\phi(u))=\sqrt{1+u}\,h(u).
\]
Let \(B\) be Frobenius on the rank-two descended stalk \((\mathcal S_+)_t\). Then
\[
D=\operatorname{Sym}^2B.
\]
At the degree-two point \(u\), the Kummer eigenvalue is
\[
\eta=\chi_{\mathbf F_{p^2}}(1+u)
    =\chi_{\mathbf F_p}(N_{\mathbf F_{p^2}/\mathbf F_p}(1+u)).
\]
The equation \(\phi(u)=t\) is
\[
8u^2+(t-1)u+t=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
u+u^p=\frac{1-t}{8},
\qquad uu^p=\frac t8,
\]
and
\[
N(1+u)=(1+u)(1+u^p)
       =1+\frac{1-t}{8}+\frac t8
       =\frac98.
\]
Consequently
\[
\eta=\left(\frac{9/8}{p}\right)
    =\left(\frac2p\right)=:\varepsilon_p.
\]

If \(B\) has eigenvalues \(\lambda,\mu\), then
\[
\lambda^2=\varepsilon_p\alpha,
\qquad
\mu^2=\varepsilon_p\beta.
\]
The determinant normalization
\[
\det B=-p
\]
selects \(\lambda\mu=-p\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2B
\text{ has eigenvalues }
\boxed{\varepsilon_p\alpha,\ -p,\ \varepsilon_p\beta.}
\]
It follows that
\[
\boxed{T_+(t)=\varepsilon_pa_2-p,}
\]
\[
\boxed{T_-(t)=-\varepsilon_pa_2+p.}
\]
These are rational integers and satisfy \(|T_\pm(t)|\le3p\).

A useful integral checksum is the characteristic polynomial
\[
\det(X-D)
 =X^3-(\varepsilon_pa_2-p)X^2
  +(p^2-\varepsilon_pp a_2)X+p^3.
\]
Its constant term is \(+p^3\) because \(\det D=-p^3\).

### 2.3 Can the determinant or the mod-\(p\) shadow resolve the sign?

- **Determinant:** no. It fixes the middle root \(-p\), but both outer-sign choices have determinant \(-p^3\).
- **Residual congruence:** the two candidates differ by \(2a_2\). Since
  \[
  T_+(t)\equiv A_p(t)\pmod p,
  \]
  the residual value selects the sign exactly when
  \[
  a_2\not\equiv0\pmod p.
  \]
- **No uniform size threshold exists.** Hasse gives \(|a_2|\le2p\), but \(p\mid a_2\) can occur on supersingular degree-two fibres. In that case both candidates have the same reduction modulo \(p\), so increasing \(p\) does not help.
- **Unconditional resolution:** use the normalized pullback above, giving the fixed sign \(\varepsilon_p=(2/p)\), or compute the normalized correspondence action directly. The residual congruence is a checksum, not a complete sign algorithm on supersingular inert fibres.

The constant \((-3)\)-quadratic twist in the explicit degree-six isogeny does not change this answer: it disappears under \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\), and its value on degree-two Frobenius is a square.

## 3. Weight and Mellin cohomology

### 3.1 Pointwise bounds

For a smooth rational elliptic fibre,
\[
|a_p(E_u)|\le2\sqrt p.
\]
Hence
\[
-p\le a_p(E_u)^2-p\le3p,
\]
so
\[
|f_p(u)|\le3p.
\]
Both \(\mathcal A_+\) and \(\mathcal A_-\) are rank-three, pure of weight two on the lisse open. Therefore every smooth rational trace satisfies
\[
\boxed{|T_\pm(t)|\le3p.}
\]
At a branch point the middle-extension stalks have ranks two and one, giving the sharper bounds \(2p\) and \(p\).

### 3.2 Euler–Poincaré calculation

Let
\[
U=\mathbf P^1_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p}
 \setminus\{0,\infty,\alpha,\beta\}.
\]
For a generic standard Mellin Kummer sheaf \(\mathcal L_\chi(t)\), the twist has no invariant vectors at \(0\) or \(\infty\). All monodromy is tame, so
\[
\chi_c(U,\mathcal A_\pm\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
 =3\,\chi_c(U,\mathbf Q_\ell)
 =3(2-4)=-6.
\]
To pass from \(U\) to the middle extension on \(\mathbf G_m\), add the inertia-invariant stalk dimensions at \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

For \(\mathcal A_+\), local inertia is
\[
\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1),
\]
so
\[
\dim(\mathcal A_+^{I_\alpha})
 =\dim(\mathcal A_+^{I_\beta})=2.
\]
Thus
\[
\chi_c(\mathbf G_m,j_*\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
 =-6+2+2=-2.
\]
For generic \(\chi\), \(H_c^0=H_c^2=0\), hence
\[
\boxed{\dim H_c^1(\mathbf G_m,j_*\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)=2.}
\]

For \(\mathcal A_-\), local inertia is
\[
\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1),
\]
so each branch point has a one-dimensional invariant space. Therefore
\[
\chi_c(\mathbf G_m,j_*\mathcal A_-\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)
 =-6+1+1=-4,
\]
and generically
\[
\boxed{\dim H_c^1(\mathbf G_m,j_*\mathcal A_-\otimes\mathcal L_\chi)=4.}
\]

Accordingly,
\[
\boxed{|S_+(\chi)|\le2p^{3/2},\qquad
       |S_-(\chi)|\le4p^{3/2}}
\]
for generic \(\chi\), after the usual purity normalization and excluding the finite exceptional character set.

The pushforward \(\mathcal A_+\oplus\mathcal A_-\) has generic Mellin dimension \(2+4=6\). The residual expression has dimension two because
\[
-\bigl(\mathcal A_+\oplus\mathcal A_-\bigr)+\mathcal A_-
=-\mathcal A_+.
\]
Thus the correct normalized equidistribution fingerprint for the Apéry residual is
\[
-\frac{S_+(\chi)}{p^{3/2}},
\]
not a new independent two-dimensional transform of \(\mathcal A_-\).

## 4. Branch points and nodal source fibres

## 4.1 Rational branch points \(q(t)=0\)

For \(p>3\), the two roots of \(q\) are distinct. They are rational exactly when \((2/p)=1\). At such a point there is one ramified preimage
\[
u=\frac{1-t}{16}\in\mathbf F_p,
\]
and this source elliptic fibre is smooth. Put
\[
a=a_p(E_u),
\qquad f=a^2-p.
\]
The finite pushforward stalk has trace
\[
P_p(t)=f.
\]

Locally, the rank-two root system has inertia eigenvalues \(1,-1\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(1\oplus(-1))
 =1\oplus(-1)\oplus1.
\]
The \(+\) middle-extension stalk consists of the two invariant square lines. The \(-\) stalk, after twisting by \(\mathcal K_q\), consists of the mixed line. That mixed line is
\[
\det\mathcal S_+\otimes\mathcal K_q
 \simeq\mathbf Q_\ell(-1),
\]
so its Frobenius trace is exactly \(p\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{T_-(t)=p,}
\]
\[
\boxed{T_+(t)=f-p=a^2-2p.}
\]
The exact sum check is
\[
T_+(t)+T_-(t)=a^2-p=P_p(t).
\]
Modulo \(p\),
\[
T_+(t)\equiv a^2\equiv A_p(t),
\qquad
T_-(t)\equiv0=\chi_2(q(t))A_p(t).
\]

This is the main branch correction: the equality
\[
T_-(t)=\chi_2(q(t))T_+(t)
\]
is valid only on the lisse locus \(q(t)\ne0\). Tensoring and then taking middle extension can create an invariant line even though the rank-one Kummer stalk itself has trace zero.

In local-factor form,
\[
L_t(\mathcal A_+,T)^{-1}
 =(1-\alpha_0^2T)(1-\beta_0^2T),
\]
where \(\alpha_0,\beta_0\) are the \(p\)-Frobenius eigenvalues on \(E_u\), while
\[
L_t(\mathcal A_-,T)^{-1}=1-pT.
\]
Their product is the local factor of the rank-three source stalk.

There are at most two such rational points. Their total contribution to an integer Mellin sum is \(O(p)\), hence after division by \(p^{3/2}\) it is \(O(p^{-1/2})\). It is asymptotically lower order but indispensable for exact integral identities and local \(L\)-factors.

## 4.2 Nodal source fibres over \(t=0\) and \(t=\infty\)

The singular source parameters are
\[
u=0,\frac18,-1,\infty.
\]
The first two lie over \(t=0\); the latter two lie over \(t=\infty\). They are multiplicative fibres. For a middle-extension \(H^1\) sheaf, the local invariant line has Frobenius eigenvalue \(\pm1\); after taking \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\), the surviving invariant eigenvalue is \(1\).

Thus each nodal source fibre contributes **one** to the middle-extension symmetric-square trace. Consequently, in the standard normalization,
\[
T_+(0)=T_-(0)=1,
\qquad
P(0)=2,
\]
and similarly at \(t=\infty\).

One must not use
\[
(p+1-\#E_u(\mathbf F_p))^2-p
\]
at a singular source fibre as though it were the trace of the middle-extension rank-three sheaf. For a nodal cubic that raw expression differs from the invariant-cycle trace by a multiple of \(p\).

For the requested sums over \(\mathbf F_p^\times\), \(t=0\) is absent and \(t=\infty\) is not a finite summation point, so these nodal corrections do not alter \(S_\pm(\chi)\). They matter only if one extends the trace table to \(\mathbf P^1\), computes full local \(L\)-functions, or mixes in the zero fibre.

## 5. Exact point-count recipe

The following recipe computes the integral trace tables on \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) for every good prime \(p>3\).

### 5.1 Helper: point count on the displayed elliptic model

For a parameter \(u\) in \(\mathbf F_{p^m}\), count
\[
E_u:\quad y^2+(1-2u)xy+u^2y=x^3.
\]
For each \(x\), put
\[
B_x=(1-2u)x+u^2,
\qquad \Delta_x=B_x^2+4x^3.
\]
Then
\[
\#E_u(\mathbf F_{p^m})
 =1+\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_{p^m}}\bigl(1+\chi_{p^m}(\Delta_x)\bigr),
\]
where the initial \(1\) is the point at infinity.

### 5.2 Trace table

```text
input: good prime p > 3
       Ap(t) = Apéry truncation modulo p
       eps = Legendre(2,p)

for t in F_p^*:
    d = t^2 - 34*t + 1
    s = Legendre(d,p)

    if s == +1:                         # smooth split fibre
        z = sqrt(d) in F_p
        u1 = (1 - t + z)/16
        u2 = (1 - t - z)/16

        a1 = p + 1 - #E_u1(F_p)
        a2 = p + 1 - #E_u2(F_p)
        f1 = a1^2 - p
        f2 = a2^2 - p

        assert f1 == f2
        Tplus[t]  = f1
        Tminus[t] = f1
        P[t]      = f1 + f2             # = 2*f1

    elif s == -1:                       # inert fibre
        construct F_p2 = F_p[z]/(z^2-d)
        u = (1 - t + z)/16 in F_p2

        a_deg2 = p^2 + 1 - #E_u(F_p2)

        Tplus[t]  = eps*a_deg2 - p
        Tminus[t] = -Tplus[t]
        P[t]      = 0

    else:                               # rational branch point
        u = (1 - t)/16
        a = p + 1 - #E_u(F_p)
        f = a^2 - p

        Tminus[t] = p
        Tplus[t]  = f - p               # = a^2 - 2p
        P[t]      = f

    assert Tplus[t] + Tminus[t] == P[t]
    assert Tplus[t]  mod p == Ap(t)
    assert Tminus[t] mod p == s*Ap(t) mod p
    assert abs(Tplus[t])  <= 3*p
    assert abs(Tminus[t]) <= 3*p
```

The inert construction is independent of the choice of the nonsquare square root: replacing \(u\) by \(u^p\) gives the conjugate elliptic fibre and the same degree-two trace.

### 5.3 Mellin sums and inversion

For a multiplicative character \(\chi\), compute in the cyclotomic integer ring
\[
S_\pm(\chi)=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\chi(t)T_\pm(t).
\]
The integral virtual trace in the original two-term formula is
\[
V(t)=-P(t)+T_-(t).
\]
Because \(P=T_++T_-\) integrally,
\[
\boxed{V(t)=-T_+(t)}
\]
at every \(t\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), including rational branch points. Therefore the characteristic-zero Mellin object that should be fingerprinted is simply
\[
M_{\mathrm{res}}(\chi)=-S_+(\chi).
\]

Choose the prime \(\mathfrak p\) above \(p\) for which the Teichmüller character reduces to the identity on \(\mathbf F_p^\times\). Then for
\[
1\le r\le p-2,
\]
\[
-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}T_+(t)\,\omega^{-r}(t)
 \equiv
-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-r}
 \equiv b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]
At \(r=0\) and \(r=p-1\), multiplicative-character aliasing identifies the two exponents; the raw transform sees \(b_0+b_{p-1}\), exactly as recorded in `CRON_pushforward_check_report.md`.

### 5.4 Failure and ambiguity flags

1. **Do not infer the inert sign from \(a_2\) and the rank-three determinant alone.** Those data leave \(-p\pm a_2\). Use \(\varepsilon_p=(2/p)\).
2. **The residual congruence is insufficient at supersingular inert fibres.** If \(p\mid a_2\), both signs reduce to the same value.
3. **Fix the descent labels.** Replacing the descent involution by its negative swaps the symbols \(+\) and \(-\). The project’s labels are fixed by
   \[
   \det\mathcal A_+=\mathcal K_q,\qquad
   \det\mathcal A_-=1
   \]
   after suppressing Tate powers, and by conductors \(9\) and \(11\).
4. **Do not set the integral branch trace of \(A_-\) to zero.** Its value is \(p\); only its reduction is zero.
5. **Do not use smooth-fibre point-count formulas at nodal source parameters.** Use invariant cycles/middle extension there.
6. **Keep Frobenius conventions fixed.** Switching to the inverse-Frobenius convention changes all Tate eigenvalue statements and must be done globally, not pointwise.
7. **The formula is for good primes \(p>3\).** Bad reduction, inseparability, or collision of branch points requires a separate local model.

## 6. Compact case table

For \(t\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), let \(s=\chi_2(q(t))\).

| fibre type | source data | \(P_p(t)\) | \(T_+(t)\) | \(T_-(t)\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| split, \(s=+1\) | \(a=p+1-\#E_u(\mathbf F_p)\), \(f=a^2-p\) | \(2f\) | \(f\) | \(f\) |
| inert, \(s=-1\) | \(a_2=p^2+1-\#E_u(\mathbf F_{p^2})\) | \(0\) | \((2/p)a_2-p\) | \(-(2/p)a_2+p\) |
| branch, \(s=0\) | \(a=p+1-\#E_u(\mathbf F_p)\), \(f=a^2-p\) | \(f\) | \(f-p=a^2-2p\) | \(p\) |

Every row satisfies
\[
T_++T_-=P_p,
\qquad
T_+\equiv A_p\pmod p,
\qquad
T_-\equiv\chi_2(q)A_p\pmod p.
\]
