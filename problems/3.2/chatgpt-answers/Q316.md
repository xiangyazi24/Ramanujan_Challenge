There are three distinct conclusions.

1. An off-diagonal shifted-GCD estimate does not by itself imply

1. The continuants satisfy an exact cross-shifted Dodgson/Bezout identity. For e=d+k, it gives an O(k) bound for the saturated common-root set away from the cut-edge divisor. It does not reduce the full GCD to a fixed boundary factor, it does not iterate as a Euclidean algorithm, and genuine nonboundary common roots occur.

1. The p-step transfer matrix does have bounded-degree conjugacy invariants after writing them in the Artin–Schreier coordinate

Abstractly, periodic gauge changes

The full-cycle matrix does control propagation over complete periods—the usual discrete Floquet role—but the desired statistic lives entirely inside one period. In the present cleared gauge the full-period spectral data are actually nilpotent, making the mismatch especially sharp.

# 4. What would genuinely close the \sqrt p route

The following statement would suffice:

The shifted-GCD approach can contribute only the repeated-root part. To complete it one needs, in addition, one of:

1. a uniform singleton bound

1. a theorem that every noncentral root repeats, apart from O(H(\log p)^C) exceptions;

1. a direct two-variable trace/sheaf formula for the first moment \sum_h|R_h|;

1. a bounded-complexity model for the complete prefix generating function, not merely the full-cycle trace.

The exact Cassini/Bezout identities are valuable: they identify the separated resultants \mathcal R_{d,k} as the correct arithmetic obstruction and show that adjacent rows are almost coprime. But they do not supply the missing first-moment or singleton estimate.

## Final concise answers

(1) An off-diagonal GCD sum \ll H^{1+o(1)} implies

(2) Dodgson gives the exact saturated reduction

and hence an O(k) saturated common-root bound. It does not iterate to an O(1) boundary-only GCD; nonboundary common roots and new separated resultants occur.

(3) The full-cycle trace and determinant are bounded-degree functions of u=z^p-z, but on z\in\mathbf F_p they are both zero, so the cleared full-cycle matrix is nilpotent. Its spectral invariants do not control the zero pattern of the partial products.
