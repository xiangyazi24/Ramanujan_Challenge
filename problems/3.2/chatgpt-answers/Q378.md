# Executive verdict
Let
\[
m=leftlfloorfrac\{n-1\}\{3\}rightrfloor,
qquad
L_m=operatorname\{lcm\}(b_0,b_1,ldots,b_m),
qquad
C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}=gcd(b_n,L_m).
\]
I do **not** know an unconditional proof of
\[
log C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}=o(n),
\]
not even for the top channel (q=1), and I found no published theorem that supplies it. The exact reason is now clearer:
> For every folded pair, Lucas congruence makes (pmid b_n) an automatic consequence of (pmid b_j). Thus (gcd(b_n,b_j)) does not impose two independent divisibility conditions; it is one same-characteristic zero condition transported to the later index.
Consequently:
- the recurrence and Wronskian control **neighboring states, denominators, and valuations in the Apéry convergent gcd**, but not the support of (gcd(b_n,b_j));
- the binomial formula turns (pmid b_j) into cancellation among (p)-adic units and gives no upper bound for such primes;
- Apéry numbers are not a divisibility sequence or a strong divisibility sequence;
- the standard Subspace-Theorem gcd results concern constant-coefficient algebraic linear recurrences, not this polynomial-coefficient recurrence;
- the continuant/resultant certificate loses invertibility exactly because the interval from (j) to (n) crosses a multiple of (p); after clearing denominators, the detected (p)-factor is the forced Lucas factor.
There is, however, one rigorous partial theorem that locates the barrier exactly. Define the large-prime old-part radical through an earlier window (J) by
\[
R_n(J)=
prod_\{substack\{p\>sqrt n\\pmid b_n\\
exists,0le jle J: pmid b_j\}\}p.
\]
Then
\[
boxed\{
log R_n(J)
le
sum_\{jle J\}log b_j
=
frac\{log(17+12sqrt2)\}\{2\}J\^2+O(Jlog J).
\}
tag\{A\}
\]
Hence
\[
J=o(sqrt n)
quadLongrightarrowquad
log R_n(J)=o(n).
\]
This is a genuine unconditional gain. It also shows the exact obstruction: the elementary old-factor argument becomes only linear at (Jasympsqrt n), whereas the folded theorem needs the much larger window (Jasymp n/3). No current recurrence, Wronskian, resultant, or general holonomic theorem crosses that square-root barrier.
The reformulation from [Q359](https://app.notion.com/p/3a67a6c4fa848142bbebc1dc48566183) is therefore valuable, but it is a **new old-prime-part problem**, not yet a solution of the all-(n) problem.

# 1. Radical versus the full lcm-gcd
This distinction is load-bearing.
Let (mathcal B_n) be the lower-channel bad primes (p\>sqrt n) occurring in the support law. Put
\[
R_n\^\{mathrm\{bad\}\}:=prod_\{pinmathcal B_n\}p.
\]
The folding result gives
\[
R_n\^\{mathrm\{bad\}\}mid
operatorname\{rad\}_\{\>sqrt n\}!left(C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}right).
tag\{1.1\}
\]
It need not be equality. The full gcd (C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}) may also contain
1. primes shared with an earlier (b_j) for reasons unrelated to the folded zero representing (nbmod p);
2. prime powers, including powers of small fixed primes;
3. primes below (sqrt n), which are already harmless in the original Apéry-convergent gcd by the Wronskian estimate.
For the original pointwise problem one only needs the weighted contribution
\[
W_n\^\{mathrm\{bad\}\}
=
sum_\{pinmathcal B_n\}v_p(G_n)log p,
qquad
G_n=gcd(d_na_n,d_nb_n).
\]
The established Wronskian estimate gives
\[
v_p(G_n)le6
qquad(p\>sqrt n),
\]
so
\[
W_n\^\{mathrm\{bad\}\}le6log R_n\^\{mathrm\{bad\}\}.
tag\{1.2\}
\]
Thus
\[
log R_n\^\{mathrm\{bad\}\}=o(n)
\]
is sufficient. Proving (log C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}=o(n)) would be stronger, because it additionally controls irrelevant small-prime powers and unrelated old factors. The Wronskian does **not** give a corresponding bound on (v_p(b_n)), so one should not silently identify these two targets.

# 2. Exact Lucas obstruction: the later zero is automatic
Let
\[
n=qp+r,
qquad0le r\<p,
qquad
j=min(r,p-1-r).
\]
There are two cases.
## 2.1 Direct branch
If (j=r), then
\[
n=qp+j.
\]
Gessel's Lucas congruence gives
\[
boxed\{
b_nequiv b_qb_jpmod p.\}
tag\{2.1\}
\]
## 2.2 Reflected branch
If (j=p-1-r), then
\[
n=qp+(p-1-j)=(q+1)p-1-j.
\]
Lucas plus palindromy gives
\[
boxed\{
b_n
equiv b_qb_\{p-1-j\}
equiv b_qb_j
pmod p.\}
tag\{2.2\}
\]
Therefore in both branches
\[
boxed\{
pmid b_jquadLongrightarrowquad pmid b_n.\}
tag\{2.3\}
\]
This implication is unconditional and exact. It is the central obstruction to a pair-gcd attack: for the folded primes, the common divisor of (b_j) and (b_n) is generated by **one** residual equation (b_j=0pmod p), not by two independent equations.
For (q=1), the formulas specialize to
\[
p=n-j
quadtext\{or\}quad
2p=n+1+j,
qquad
0le jlefrac\{n-1\}\{3\},
tag\{2.4\}
\]
and in both cases
\[
b_nequiv5b_jpmod p.
tag\{2.5\}
\]
Thus even the top-half problem does not become an ordinary “two unrelated Apéry terms have a common prime” problem. The common factor is forced by the base-(p) digit structure.
Primary references for the Lucas law are Ira Gessel, *Some congruences for Apéry numbers*, J. Number Theory 14 (1982), 362–368, DOI [`10.1016/0022-314X(82)90071-3`](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(82)90071-3), and Malik–Straub, *Divisibility properties of sporadic Apéry-like numbers*, DOI [`10.1007/s40993-016-0036-8`](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-016-0036-8).
## 2.3 Why this is a rigorous obstruction
For every prime (p) and every first-block zero (j\<p), Lucas produces infinitely many later indices having the same old prime factor: any index whose base-(p) expansion contains the digit (j) has (pmid b_n), unless another digit issue changes only the multiplicity rather than the vanishing.
In particular, when (2jle p-1), taking
\[
n=p+j
\]
gives
\[
jlefrac\{n-1\}\{3\},
qquad
pmid C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}.
tag\{2.6\}
\]
So the folded gcd is designed to collect systematic Lucas-propagated old factors. Primitive-divisor heuristics or generic “different terms should be almost coprime” heuristics are structurally misleading here.

# 3. The recurrence and Wronskian do not bound the old part
The recurrence is
\[
(k+1)\^3b_\{k+1\}=P(k)b_k-k\^3b_\{k-1\}.
\]
Its companion solution satisfies the exact Wronskian identity
\[
a_\{k+1\}b_k-a_kb_\{k+1\}=frac6\{(k+1)\^3\}.
tag\{3.1\}
\]
This has two important consequences already used in the project:
- (a_k) and (b_k) cannot both vanish modulo a prime (p\>k);
- after clearing the Apéry denominators, it bounds (v_p(G_n)).
It does **not** bound (gcd(b_n,b_j)) for separated indices.
A natural cross determinant is
\[
Delta_\{n,j\}:=a_nb_j-a_jb_n.
tag\{3.2\}
\]
If (pmid b_n,b_j), then one might hope that (pmidDelta_\{n,j\}) gives a second certificate. In the target range this fails for two reasons.
1. The rational number (a_n) has denominators supported on primes (le n). Clearing it by (d_n) automatically introduces powers of every candidate prime (ple n), so the resulting integer determinant has a forced (p)-factor even without a new common-zero phenomenon.
2. The companion Lucas law used in the support theorem gives, schematically,
	\[
	p\^3a_nequiv a_qb_rpmod p.
	\]
	At a lower-digit zero this vanishes automatically, exactly as (b_n) does.
Thus the Wronskian controls multiplicity after support is known; it does not reduce the support.

# 4. The continuant/resultant route degenerates at the target primes
For a nonsingular recurrence window, the cleared continuant identity gives
\[
Q_h(j)b_\{j+h\}
=N_h(j)b_\{j+1\}-(j+1)\^3N_\{h-1\}(j+1)b_j.
tag\{4.1\}
\]
When every factor in (Q_h(j)) is a unit modulo (p), two zero endpoints force
\[
pmid N_h(j).
\]
That is the useful fixed-gap pair certificate.
But in the folded problem (j\<n) and the interval from (j) to (n) crosses the index (p) (and, for larger (q), further multiples of (p)). Hence (Q_h(j)) is not a unit. The hypothesis that makes the pair certificate informative is precisely what fails.
For the direct branch,
\[
h=n-j=qp,
\]
so the singular steps occur at (p,2p,ldots,qp). In the reflected branch the interval still crosses (p). After clearing denominators, the resulting continuant or resultant has a forced factor coming from these singular steps. Removing that forced factor recovers the Lucas condition (pmid b_j); it does not leave a second restriction on (p).
There is also a height problem. Away from singularity, a continuant certificate over a gap (h) has degree (3(h-1)), and its integer evaluation has logarithmic height of order at most (O(hlog n)). Here (hasymp n) for each top-channel pair. Even a successful single-pair resultant would therefore naturally have height (exp(O(nlog n))), not (exp(o(n))), and combining certificates over (asymp n) possible earlier indices is much worse.
So resultants help for primes larger than the entire recurrence window; they do not help for the folded primes, which lie **inside** the window and create its singular blocks.

# 5. The binomial formula gives no upper support law
For
\[
b_j=sum_\{k=0\}\^jbinom jk\^2binom\{j+k\}\{k\}\^2,
\]
a folded prime satisfies (jle(p-1)/2). Hence (j+k\<p) for every (0le kle j), so every binomial factor in the sum is a (p)-adic unit.
Therefore
\[
pmid b_j
\]
is cancellation among units; it is not caused by a termwise Kummer carry or a visible factorial factor. Landau/Kummer valuation bookkeeping cannot upper-bound the number of such primes.
The known arithmetic results go in a different direction. Luca–Shparlinski prove density-one **lower bounds** for the number and size of prime factors of one Apéry number:
- Florian Luca and Igor Shparlinski, *Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers*, J. London Math. Soc. 78 (2008), 545–562, DOI [`10.1112/jlms/jdn031`](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jdn031).
Those results do not control which factors of (b_n) occurred in earlier terms, nor do they bound (gcd(b_n,L_m)).
Likewise, Luca's *Prime divisors of binary holonomic sequences*, Adv. Appl. Math. 40 (2008), 168–179, DOI [`10.1016/j.aam.2006.12.001`](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2006.12.001), gives a lower bound for the total number of distinct primes appearing among initial terms of a broad holonomic sequence. It is not an upper bound for the old-prime part of one term.

# 6. Strong divisibility theory does not apply
A strong divisibility sequence satisfies
\[
gcd(u_m,u_n)=u_\{gcd(m,n)\}.
\]
The Apéry sequence fails even ordinary divisibility. Indeed,
\[
b_2=73,
qquad
b_4=33001,
qquad
33001equiv5pmod\{73\},
\]
so
\[
gcd(b_2,b_4)=1
ne b_2,
qquad
b_2nmid b_4.
tag\{6.1\}
\]
Thus the lcm-factorization theory for strong divisibility sequences, such as Nowicki's characterization in [*Strong divisibility and lcm-sequences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2416), has no Apéry specialization.
The same issue blocks primitive-divisor methods for Lucas, Lehmer, or elliptic divisibility sequences. Those theories use an algebraic-group or constant-coefficient recurrence law that makes the index divisibility relation control term divisibility. Apéry's Lucas congruence instead makes old prime factors reappear according to **base-(p) digits**, a different and more flexible mechanism.
---
# 7. Subspace-Theorem gcd results do not match the hypotheses
The classical theorem of Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier proves
\[
loggcd(a\^n-1,b\^n-1)=o(n)
\]
for multiplicatively independent fixed integers (a,b): DOI [`10.1007/s00209-002-0449-z`](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-002-0449-z).
Grieve–Wang and Xiao extend such results to moving polynomials and algebraic **constant-coefficient linear recurrence sequences**:
- Grieve–Wang, DOI [`10.1090/tran/8220`](https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/8220);
- Zheng Xiao, DOI [`10.1007/s00209-024-03453-4`](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-024-03453-4).
Their recurrence applications require an exponential-polynomial representation
\[
u_n=sum_i P_i(n)alpha_i\^n
\]
with finitely many fixed characteristic roots, placing the values in a fixed finite-rank multiplicative group.
The Apéry sequence is P-recursive, not C-finite. Its asymptotic
\[
b_nsim c,(17+12sqrt2)\^n n\^\{-3/2\}
\]
is already incompatible with a nonzero constant-coefficient recurrence, whose dominant terms have nonnegative integral polynomial powers of (n). There is no fixed torus or fixed (S)-unit parametrization to which those gcd theorems apply.
Therefore one should not cite generic “linear recurrence gcd” theorems as proving anything about (C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}).

# 8. A rigorous partial old-factor theorem
Let
\[
R_n(J)=
prod_\{substack\{p\>sqrt n\\pmid b_n\\
exists,jle J: pmid b_j\}\}p.
\]
Since the primes in the product are distinct and each divides at least one (b_j),
\[
R_n(J)mid
operatorname\{rad\}!left(prod_\{j=0\}\^Jb_jright).
\]
Hence
\[
log R_n(J)lesum_\{j=0\}\^Jlog b_j.
tag\{8.1\}
\]
Write
\[
alpha=17+12sqrt2=(1+sqrt2)\^4.
\]
The standard Apéry asymptotic gives
\[
log b_j=jlogalpha+O(log(j+2)).
\]
Summing,
\[
boxed\{
log R_n(J)
le
frac\{logalpha\}\{2\}J\^2+O(Jlog J).
\}
tag\{8.2\}
\]
Also (R_n(J)midoperatorname\{rad\}(b_n)), so the combined elementary bound is
\[
boxed\{
log R_n(J)
le
minleft\{
	nlogalpha+O(log n),
	frac\{logalpha\}\{2\}J\^2+O(Jlog J)
right\}.
\}
tag\{8.3\}
\]
Consequences:
1. If (J=o(sqrt n)), then (log R_n(J)=o(n)).
2. If (J=csqrt n), then
	\[
	log R_n(J)le
	frac\{c\^2logalpha\}\{2\}n+o(n),
	\]
	which is only linear.
3. At the folded window (J=n/3), the product bound is quadratic and (8.3) falls back to the trivial linear bound from (b_n).
For the original convergent gcd, the contribution of the primes in (R_n(J)) is at most six times (8.2), by the Wronskian valuation bound. Thus every sub-square-root folded subwindow is already harmless.
This is the strongest unconditional statement I can derive from the integer old-factor structure alone.

# 9. Why a fixed fraction of the lcm does not help
For fixed (0\<delta\<1/3), define
\[
C_n(delta)
=gcd!left(b_n,operatorname\{lcm\}_\{jledelta n\}b_jright).
\]
The available size bounds are
\[
log C_n(delta)lelog b_n=Theta(n)
\]
and
\[
log C_n(delta)le
sum_\{jledelta n\}log b_j
=Theta(delta\^2n\^2).
\]
Their minimum is still only (O(n)). No fixed (delta\>0) enters the sub-square-root range of Section 8.
More importantly, the Lucas obstruction survives for every fixed fraction. Whenever (pmid b_j) and (n=qp+j) with (jledelta n), the old factor (p) propagates automatically to (b_n). Shrinking (1/3) to another positive constant does not create a second independent congruence.
For (q=1), a purely prime-counting bound gives
\[
sum_\{substack\{n/2\<ple n\\ptext\{ bad\}\}\}log p
le
vartheta(n)-vartheta(n/2)
=frac n2+o(n),
tag\{9.1\}
\]
but this remains linear. For a fixed quotient (q), the entire prime interval has logarithmic weight asymptotic to
\[
frac\{n\}\{q(q+1)\}.
\]
The tail (qge Q) is (O(n/Q)), so large quotients are harmless after taking (Qtoinfty). The hard problem is exactly the finitely many fixed channels, especially (q=1), where the folded index occupies a positive fraction of (n).

# 10. Current weaker global bounds
The completely elementary bound is
\[
log C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}lelog b_n
=nlog(17+12sqrt2)+O(log n).
tag\{10.1\}
\]
This is linear, not sublinear.
For the actual Apéry convergent gcd (G_n), the irrationality-measure bootstrap using Rhin–Viola's bound for (zeta(3)) gives
\[
log G_nle(2.247+o(1))n.
tag\{10.2\}
\]
See Rhin–Viola, *The group structure for (zeta(3))*, Acta Arith. 97 (2001), 269–293, DOI [`10.4064/aa97-3-6`](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa97-3-6). This controls the weighted bad-prime contribution because every lower-channel bad prime divides (G_n), but it is still linear and does not exploit the fold.
No cited theorem improves (10.1) to (o(n)) for (C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}).

# 11. What theorem would actually close the route
The right collision-free target is not necessarily the full gcd. It is the old-part radical estimate
\[
boxed\{
log
prod_\{substack\{p\>sqrt n\\pmid b_n\\
exists,jle(n-1)/3: pmid b_j\}\}p
=o(n).
\}
tag\{11.1\}
\]
An even sharper target keeps the moving linear relation from the fold:
\[
sum_\{q\<sqrt n\}
sum_\{substack\{jle(n-q)/(2q+1)\\
p=(n-j)/qtext\{ prime\}\\pmid b_j\}\}
log p
-
sum_\{q\<sqrt n\}
sum_\{substack\{jle(n-q)/(2q+1)\\
p=(n+1+j)/(q+1)text\{ prime\}\\pmid b_j\}\}
log p
=o(n).
tag\{11.2\}
\]
This moving-target formulation avoids the extra unrelated factors in (C_n\^\{mathrm\{fold\}\}). But it is precisely a theorem about large prime divisors of (b_j) lying on prescribed linear functions of (j). No present Apéry recurrence theorem, binomial valuation theorem, strong-divisibility theorem, resultant bound, or Subspace-Theorem result establishes it.
# Final assessment
- **Can the exact folded gcd currently be proved subexponential?** No known proof.
- **Does (q=1) become easier?** Algebraically no: (b_nequiv5b_jpmod p) makes the common factor automatic.
- **Does a fixed fraction of the lcm help?** No fixed positive fraction reaches the only elementary favorable range (J=o(sqrt n)).
- **Do strong divisibility or primitive-divisor results apply?** No; Apéry numbers fail divisibility and Lucas congruence systematically propagates old factors.
- **Do generic recurrence-gcd theorems apply?** No; their constant-coefficient/S-unit hypotheses fail.
- **What is rigorously new?** The partial theorem (A): the old large-prime radical from indices (j=o(sqrt n)) has logarithm (o(n)). This isolates the square-root old-index threshold as the exact point where genuinely new arithmetic input is required.
