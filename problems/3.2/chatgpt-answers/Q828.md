# Fixed denominator strips: a complete proof

## 1. Exact setup

Let

$$
A_m=\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj^2\binom{m+j}{j}^2,
\qquad
c_k=\Delta^kA_0
     =\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^{k-j}\binom kjA_j.
\tag{1.1}
$$

Put

$$
\lambda=17+12\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^4,
\qquad
\gamma=\lambda-1=16+12\sqrt2,
\tag{1.2}
$$

and abbreviate

$$
s:=\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}
  =1+\frac1\gamma
  =\frac{4+3\sqrt2}{8},
\qquad
\eta:=\frac1{2\gamma}
       =\frac{3\sqrt2-4}{16}.
\tag{1.3}
$$

Thus

$$
1-\eta=\frac{20-3\sqrt2}{16},
\qquad 0<\eta<\frac1{64},
\qquad s>1.
\tag{1.4}
$$

Fix (Hge0) and (0le ble H), and set

$$
a=H-b,
\qquad n=3H+1.
\tag{1.5}
$$

For (kgeellge0), define the Newton multiplication matrix

$$
M_{k,\ell}
 =\binom{k}{\ell}\Delta^{k-\ell}A_\ell
 =\binom{k}{\ell}\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{t}c_{k-\ell+t}.
\tag{1.6}
$$

The denominator coefficients (q_0,ldots,q_b) are determined by

$$
q_b=1,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=0}^bM_{k,\ell}q_\ell=0
\quad(a+1\le k\le H).
\tag{1.7}
$$

For large (H), the square system for (q_0,ldots,q_{b-1}) is nonsingular; this is proved below rather than assumed. Define

$$
d_k:=\sum_{\ell=0}^bM_{k,\ell}q_\ell
\quad(0\le k\le a),
\tag{1.8}
$$

and

$$
\widehat P_{H,b}(x)
 :=\sum_{k=0}^{a}d_k\binom{x}{k}.
\tag{1.9}
$$

The object called (widehat P) in the question is

$$
\widehat P:=\widehat P_{H,b}(3H+1).
\tag{1.10}
$$

## Theorem 1.1 — uniform fixed-strip asymptotic

For every fixed integer (Bge0), there are effective constants (H_Bge1) and (C_B>0) such that, for every (Hge H_B) and every (0le ble B), the system (1.7) has a unique solution and

$$
\left|
\frac{\widehat P_{H,b}(3H+1)}
 {(-1)^b s^b c_{H-b}\binom{3H+1}{H-b}}
 -(1-\eta)^{-b-1}
\right|
\le \frac{C_B}{H}.
\tag{1.11}
$$

Equivalently, uniformly for (0le ble B),

$$
\boxed{
\widehat P
=(-1)^b
\left(\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}\right)^b
c_{H-b}\binom{3H+1}{H-b}
\left(1-\frac1{2\gamma}\right)^{-b-1}
\left(1+O_B(H^{-1})\right).
}
\tag{1.12}
$$

In particular, for (Hge H_B),

$$
\operatorname{sgn}\widehat P=(-1)^b.
\tag{1.13}
$$

The rest of the page proves Theorem 1.1.

# 2. A quantified endpoint expansion for (c_k)

The Apéry numbers have a positive moment density (arphi) on ([0,lambda]):

$$
A_m=\int_0^\lambda x^m\varphi(x)\,dx.
\tag{2.1}
$$

This is Edgar’s moment representation. At the right endpoint, the Frobenius solution used there gives a convergent Puiseux expansion

$$
\varphi(\lambda-u)=u^{1/2}h(u),
\qquad |u|<\delta_0,
\tag{2.2}
$$

where (h) is real analytic and

$$
h(0)=h_0
=\frac{1}{2^{5/4}(1+\sqrt2)^4\pi^2}>0.
\tag{2.3}
$$

Indeed, Edgar normalizes the local solution (v_2(x)=(lambda-x)^{1/2}+O((lambda-x)^{3/2})) and obtains

$$
\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{2^{5/4}(1+\sqrt2)^4\pi^2}\,v_2(x)
\quad(c_0<x<\lambda).
\tag{2.4}
$$

By (1.1) and (2.1),

$$
c_k=\int_0^\lambda(x-1)^k\varphi(x)\,dx
    =\int_{-1}^{\gamma}y^k\varphi(y+1)\,dy.
\tag{2.5}
$$

We now prove the complete expansion directly. No unnamed Watson lemma is used.

## Lemma 2.1 — endpoint expansion with an explicit coefficient formula

Write

$$
h(u)=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}h_pu^p,
\qquad h_p=\frac{h^{(p)}(0)}{p!}.
\tag{2.6}
$$

For (qge0), define the polynomial (E_q(t)) by

$$
\exp\!\left(
-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j+1}}{(j+1)x^j}
\right)
=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}E_q(t)x^{-q}
\tag{2.7}
$$

as a formal series in (x^{-1}). Equivalently,

$$
E_0(t)=1,
\tag{2.8}
$$

and for (qge1),

$$
E_q(t)=
\sum_{m_1+2m_2+\cdots+qm_q=q}
\prod_{j=1}^q
\frac1{m_j!}
\left(-\frac{t^{j+1}}{j+1}\right)^{m_j}.
\tag{2.9}
$$

For example,

$$
E_1(t)=-\frac{t^2}{2},
\qquad
E_2(t)=-\frac{t^3}{3}+\frac{t^4}{8}.
\tag{2.10}
$$

Define

$$
a_m
:=\gamma^{3/2}
\sum_{p+q=m}h_p\gamma^p
\int_0^\infty t^{p+1/2}E_q(t)e^{-t}\,dt.
\tag{2.11}
$$

Then, for every (Nge0),

$$
c_k
=\gamma^k k^{-3/2}
\left(
\sum_{m=0}^{N}a_mk^{-m}
+O_N(k^{-N-1})
\right).
\tag{2.12}
$$

The leading coefficient is

$$
\kappa:=a_0
=h_0\gamma^{3/2}\Gamma(3/2)
=\frac{2^{3/2}}{(1+\sqrt2)\pi^{3/2}}.
\tag{2.13}
$$

Consequently, with (alpha_m=a_m/kappa),

$$
\boxed{
 c_k=\kappa\gamma^k k^{-3/2}
 \left(1+\frac{\alpha_1}{k}+\cdots+
 \frac{\alpha_N}{k^N}+O_N(k^{-N-1})\right).
}
\tag{2.14}
$$

For reference,

$$
\alpha_1
=\frac{3\gamma h_1}{2h_0}-\frac{15}{8}.
\tag{2.15}
$$

### Proof

Choose

$$
0<\delta<\min(\delta_0,\gamma-1).
\tag{2.16}
$$

Split (2.5) into (ylegamma-delta) and (ygegamma-delta). Since (int_0^lambdavarphi=1),

$$
\left|
\int_{-1}^{\gamma-\delta}y^k\varphi(y+1)\,dy
\right|
\le(\gamma-\delta)^k.
\tag{2.17}
$$

This is exponentially smaller than (gamma^kk^{-M}) for every fixed (M).

In the endpoint integral put (u=gamma-y), then (u=gamma t/k). This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_k
&:=\int_{\gamma-\delta}^{\gamma}y^k\varphi(y+1)\,dy\\
&=\gamma^k\left(\frac{\gamma}{k}\right)^{3/2}
\int_0^{\delta k/\gamma}
 t^{1/2}
 \left(1-\frac tk\right)^k
 h\!\left(\frac{\gamma t}{k}\right)dt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.18}
$$

For (0le tledelta k/gamma),

$$
k\log\left(1-\frac tk\right)
=-t-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{t^{j+1}}{(j+1)k^j}
+R_{N+1}(t,k),
\tag{2.19}
$$

where, after decreasing (delta) if necessary,

$$
|R_{N+1}(t,k)|
\le C_{N,\delta}\frac{t^{N+2}}{k^{N+1}},
\qquad
\left(1-\frac tk\right)^k\le e^{-t}.
\tag{2.20}
$$

On (0le tle k^{1/2}), Taylor expansion of the two analytic factors in (2.18), together with (2.7), gives

$$
\left(1-\frac tk\right)^k
h\!\left(\frac{\gamma t}{k}\right)
=e^{-t}
\sum_{m=0}^{N}k^{-m}
\sum_{p+q=m}h_p\gamma^pt^pE_q(t)
+\mathcal R_N(t,k),
\tag{2.21}
$$

with

$$
|\mathcal R_N(t,k)|
\le C'_N k^{-N-1}e^{-t/2}(1+t)^{3N+4}.
\tag{2.22}
$$

On (k^{1/2}le tledelta k/gamma), the bound in (2.20) and analyticity of (h) give an (O(e^{-k^{1/2}/2})) contribution. Hence (2.22) is integrable after multiplication by (t^{1/2}), uniformly in (k). Integrating (2.21), extending the finite upper limit to infinity with an exponentially small error, and adding (2.17) proves (2.12) and the coefficient formula (2.11).

Finally,

$$
\gamma=2^{5/2}(1+\sqrt2)^2,
\qquad \Gamma(3/2)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2},
\tag{2.23}
$$

so (2.3) gives (2.13). ∎

## 2.2 Differentiated form

Define, for real (x>0),

$$
C_+(x):=\int_0^\gamma y^x\varphi(y+1)\,dy.
\tag{2.24}
$$

At integer (k), the omitted part of (2.5) satisfies

$$
|c_k-C_+(k)|\le(1-\varepsilon)^k
\tag{2.25}
$$

for some (arepsilon>0), after combining it with the non-endpoint positive part. Differentiating (2.18) under the integral sign only inserts powers of (log(1-t/k)). The same estimates as above therefore give, for every (N,Jge0),

$$
\frac{d^j}{dx^j}
\left[
\frac{C_+(x)}{\kappa\gamma^xx^{-3/2}}
-\sum_{m=0}^{N}\alpha_mx^{-m}
\right]
=O_{N,J}(x^{-N-1-j})
\tag{2.26}
$$

uniformly for (0le jle J). This differentiated estimate, rather than a merely pointwise Poincaré expansion, is what the determinant argument requires.

For complete effectivity, one may take the constants in (2.22) and (2.26) to depend only on

$$
\delta,\quad
\max_{0\le p\le N+J+2}
\sup_{0\le u\le\delta}|h^{(p)}(u)|,\quad
N,\quad J,\quad\gamma.
\tag{2.27}
$$

All these quantities are finite and computable from the convergent Frobenius series of (h).

# 3. The normalized multiplication columns

For integers (kgeell), put

$$
F_\ell(k):=\frac{M_{k,\ell}}{c_k}
=\binom{k}{\ell}
 \sum_{t=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{t}
 \frac{c_{k-\ell+t}}{c_k}.
\tag{3.1}
$$

For real (x) large, replace (c) by (C_+) in (3.1) and use the polynomial continuation of (inom{x}{ell}). Denote the resulting smooth function by (widetilde F_ell(x)). By (2.25), at integer (ktoinfty),

$$
F_\ell(k)-\widetilde F_\ell(k)=O_B(e^{-\varepsilon k}k^\ell)
\quad(0\le\ell\le B).
\tag{3.2}
$$

For a fixed integer (jge0), define (r_{j,m}) by the formal quotient expansion

$$
\frac{C_+(x-j)}{C_+(x)}
\sim \gamma^{-j}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}r_{j,m}x^{-m},
\qquad r_{j,0}=1.
\tag{3.3}
$$

The coefficients (r_{j,m}) are obtained uniquely by formal division from (2.14). Put

$$
\tau_{\ell,m}
:=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{t}
 \gamma^{-(\ell-t)}r_{\ell-t,m}.
\tag{3.4}
$$

Then

$$
\tau_{\ell,0}
=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{t}\gamma^{-(\ell-t)}
=(1+\gamma^{-1})^\ell=s^\ell.
\tag{3.5}
$$

Write

$$
\binom{x}{\ell}=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell}\beta_{\ell,h}x^h,
\qquad \beta_{\ell,\ell}=\frac1{\ell!}.
\tag{3.6}
$$

Define the polynomial

$$
P_\ell(x)
:=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell}\beta_{\ell,h}
  \sum_{m=0}^{h}\tau_{\ell,m}x^{h-m}.
\tag{3.7}
$$

Thus (P_ell) is exactly the polynomial part at infinity of

$$
\binom{x}{\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\tau_{\ell,m}x^{-m}.
\tag{3.8}
$$

Its degree and leading coefficient are

$$
\deg P_\ell=\ell,
\qquad
[x^\ell]P_\ell(x)=\frac{s^\ell}{\ell!}.
\tag{3.9}
$$

## Lemma 3.1 — polynomial plus symbol remainder

Fix (Bge0), and take

$$
N_B:=2B+4
\tag{3.10}
$$

terms in (2.14). Then there are (X_Bge1) and (E_B^{rm sym}<infty) such that, for every (0leellle B),

$$
\widetilde F_\ell(x)=P_\ell(x)+R_\ell(x)
\quad(x\ge X_B),
\tag{3.11}
$$

and

$$
|R_\ell^{(j)}(x)|
\le E_B^{\rm sym}x^{-1-j}
\qquad(0\le j\le B+1).
\tag{3.12}
$$

### Proof

Insert (2.26) into each of the finitely many quotients in (3.1), perform formal division through order (N_B), and multiply by the degree-(ell) polynomial (inom{x}{ell}). Terms with exponent (x^0,x^1,ldots,x^ell) are precisely (3.7); every remaining term begins at (x^{-1}). Because (2.26) is differentiable through order (B+1), differentiation lowers the order by one each time, giving (3.12). The choice (N_B=2B+4) is more than sufficient: only the first (ellle B) quotient coefficients are needed to determine (P_ell), and the extra (B+4) terms control all differentiated remainders. ∎

This proves, in a precise form, that enough terms of the Poincaré expansion survive the cancellation. The surviving object is not a guessed leading monomial; it is the full polynomial (P_ell), whose leading coefficient is nonzero.

# 4. Consecutive-row determinant cancellation

We need one elementary determinant lemma.

## Lemma 4.1 — divided-difference determinant

Let (0le mle B). Let (x_0<cdots<x_m) be real numbers in

$$
[X-L\log X,\,X+L\log X]
\tag{4.1}
$$

with pairwise spacings at least (1). Suppose

$$
f_\ell(x)=P_\ell(x)+R_\ell(x),
\qquad 0\le\ell\le m,
\tag{4.2}
$$

where (P_ell) is the polynomial in (3.7) and (3.12) holds. Then, uniformly in the nodes,

$$
\det\bigl(f_\ell(x_i)\bigr)_{0\le i,\ell\le m}
=
V(x_0,\ldots,x_m)
\frac{s^{m(m+1)/2}}{\prod_{\ell=0}^{m}\ell!}
\left(1+O_{B,L}(X^{-1})\right),
\tag{4.3}
$$

where

$$
V(x_0,\ldots,x_m)
:=\prod_{0\le i<j\le m}(x_j-x_i).
\tag{4.4}
$$

### Proof

Successive Newton row operations give the exact identity

$$
\det\bigl(f_\ell(x_i)\bigr)
=V(x_0,\ldots,x_m)
\det\bigl([x_0,\ldots,x_i]f_\ell\bigr)_{0\le i,\ell\le m}.
\tag{4.5}
$$

For the polynomials (P_ell), the divided-difference matrix is upper triangular, because

$$
[x_0,\ldots,x_i]P_\ell=0
\quad(i>\ell),
\tag{4.6}
$$

and its diagonal is

$$
[x_0,\ldots,x_\ell]P_\ell
=[x^\ell]P_\ell=\frac{s^\ell}{\ell!}.
\tag{4.7}
$$

Therefore

$$
\det\bigl([x_0,\ldots,x_i]P_\ell\bigr)
=\frac{s^{m(m+1)/2}}{\prod_{\ell=0}^{m}\ell!}.
\tag{4.8}
$$

For the remainder, the generalized mean-value theorem for divided differences and (3.12) give

$$
|[x_0,\ldots,x_i]R_\ell|
\le\frac1{i!}\sup|R_\ell^{(i)}|
\le\frac{E_B^{\rm sym}}{i!}X^{-1-i}(1+o(1)).
\tag{4.9}
$$

Multiply row (i) of the divided-difference matrix by (X^i) and column (ell) by (X^{-ell}). The determinant is unchanged because

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{m}i=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\ell.
\tag{4.10}
$$

The scaled polynomial entries are uniformly bounded on (4.1), while every scaled remainder entry is (O_{B,L}(X^{-1})). Multilinearity of the determinant now gives (4.3).

An explicit admissible error constant is obtained as follows. Let (M_{B,L}) be the supremum, over (Xge X_B), all allowed nodes, and (i,ellle B), of the absolute values of the scaled polynomial divided differences. Then the absolute error in the scaled determinant is at most

$$
(B+1)!(M_{B,L}+1)^B E_B^{\rm sym}X^{-1}.
\tag{4.11}
$$

Dividing by the positive minimum of the finitely many diagonal products in (4.8) gives a fully explicit (O_{B,L})-constant. ∎

The determinant identity (4.3) is the rigorous replacement for the unproved “consecutive rows cancel but enough terms survive” sentence in Q803.

# 5. Normality and the rational cofactor kernel

For (bge1), define the normalized denominator minor

$$
\Delta_{H,b}
:=\det\bigl(F_\ell(a+i)\bigr)_
 {1\le i\le b,\ 0\le\ell\le b-1}.
\tag{5.1}
$$

Apply Lemma 4.1 with the consecutive nodes (a+1,ldots,a+b). Since

$$
V(a+1,\ldots,a+b)
=\prod_{j=1}^{b-1}j!
=\prod_{\ell=0}^{b-1}\ell!,
\tag{5.2}
$$

we obtain, uniformly for (ble B),

$$
\boxed{
\Delta_{H,b}
=s^{b(b-1)/2}\left(1+O_B(H^{-1})\right).
}
\tag{5.3}
$$

In particular, after enlarging (H_B),

$$
|\Delta_{H,b}|\ge\frac12
\quad(1\le b\le B),
\tag{5.4}
$$

so (1.7) has a unique solution. This proves eventual normality on every fixed denominator strip.

Define the normalized cofactor kernel

$$
K_{H,b}(k)
:=\frac{d_k}{c_k}
=\sum_{\ell=0}^bq_\ell F_\ell(k).
\tag{5.5}
$$

Let

$$
\mathcal N_{H,b}(x)
:=\det
\begin{pmatrix}
F_0(a+1)&\cdots&F_b(a+1)\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
F_0(a+b)&\cdots&F_b(a+b)\\
F_0(x)&\cdots&F_b(x)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.6}
$$

The Schur-complement identity is exact:

$$
\boxed{
K_{H,b}(x)=\frac{\mathcal N_{H,b}(x)}{\Delta_{H,b}}.
}
\tag{5.7}
$$

No asymptotic argument enters (5.7).

## Proposition 5.1 — uniform logarithmic-window kernel asymptotic

Put

$$
S_B:=\frac{B(B+1)}2,
\qquad
L_B:=\frac{S_B+7/2}{\log(2\gamma)},
\tag{5.8}
$$

and

$$
R_H:=\left\lceil L_B\log(H+1)\right\rceil.
\tag{5.9}
$$

For every fixed (B), uniformly for (0le ble B) and

$$
0\le r\le R_H,
\tag{5.10}
$$

one has

$$
\boxed{
K_{H,b}(a-r)
=(-1)^b s^b\binom{r+b}{b}
\left(1+O_B(H^{-1})\right).
}
\tag{5.11}
$$

### Proof

For (b=0), (q_0=1), (F_0=1), and (5.11) is exact. Assume (bge1).

The rows in (5.6) are ordered as (a+1,ldots,a+b,a-r). Moving the last row to the first position introduces the sign ((-1)^b). Apply Lemma 4.1 to the increasing nodes

$$
a-r,a+1,\ldots,a+b.
\tag{5.12}
$$

Their span is (O_B(log H)), so the estimate is uniform. Dividing the numerator determinant by (5.3), and using

$$
\frac{V(a-r,a+1,\ldots,a+b)}{V(a+1,\ldots,a+b)}
=\prod_{j=1}^b(r+j),
\tag{5.13}
$$

and

$$
\frac{\prod_{\ell=0}^{b-1}\ell!}
 {\prod_{\ell=0}^{b}\ell!}
=\frac1{b!},
\tag{5.14}
$$

we get

$$
K_{H,b}(a-r)
=(-1)^b s^b
\frac{(r+1)\cdots(r+b)}{b!}
\left(1+O_B(H^{-1})\right),
\tag{5.15}
$$

which is (5.11). ∎

This is the requested uniform asymptotic for the rational cofactor kernel for (rle C_Blog H), with the explicit choice (C_B=L_B).

# 6. A global polynomial cofactor bound

The local asymptotic cannot be used when (r) is comparable to (H). We now prove a separate global bound.

Define

$$
U_B
:=\max_{0\le j\le B}\sup_{k\ge j}
\frac{c_{k-j}}{c_k}.
\tag{6.1}
$$

The supremum is finite by (2.14), and (U_Bge1). Put

$$
E_B:=2^BU_B.
\tag{6.2}
$$

From (3.1), for every (0leellle B) and every (kge0),

$$
0\le F_\ell(k)
\le E_B(k+1)^\ell.
\tag{6.3}
$$

For (bge1), Cramer’s rule applied to (1.7), together with (5.4), gives, for (0leell<b),

$$
|q_\ell|
\le 2b!E_B^b(H+1)^{S_b-\ell},
\qquad
S_b:=\frac{b(b+1)}2.
\tag{6.4}
$$

Indeed, the numerator determinant uses the column degrees

$$
0,1,\ldots,b\quad\text{with }\ell\text{ omitted},
\tag{6.5}
$$

whose sum is (S_b-ell); the Leibniz formula contributes at most (b!) products.

Consequently, for (0le kle a),

$$
\begin{aligned}
|K_{H,b}(k)|
&\le
\sum_{\ell=0}^{b-1}|q_\ell|F_\ell(k)+F_b(k)\\
&\le
\left(2b\,b!E_B^{b+1}+E_B\right)(H+1)^{S_b}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.6}
$$

Thus, with the explicit constant

$$
D_B
:=1+E_B+2B\,B!\,E_B^{B+1},
\tag{6.7}
$$

we have, for every (0le ble B), every (0le rle a), and all (Hge H_B),

$$
\boxed{
|K_{H,b}(a-r)|
\le D_B(H+1)^{S_B}.
}
\tag{6.8}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{
|d_{a-r}|
\le D_B(H+1)^{S_B}c_{a-r}.
}
\tag{6.9}
$$

This is the requested explicit polynomial-in-(H) cofactor bound for all remaining (r). The exponent is

$$
S_B=\frac{B(B+1)}2.
\tag{6.10}
$$

No unproved total-positivity or sign-regularity assertion is used.

# 7. Uniform coefficient and binomial ratios

## 7.1 Global coefficient ratio

Define

$$
w_m:=c_m\gamma^{-m}(m+1)^{3/2}.
\tag{7.1}
$$

By (2.14), (w_mtokappa>0). Hence the effective constants

$$
\underline\kappa:=\inf_{m\ge0}w_m>0,
\qquad
\overline\kappa:=\sup_{m\ge0}w_m<\infty
\tag{7.2}
$$

are finite. Equivalently, choose (m_0) so that

$$
|w_m-\kappa|\le\frac\kappa2
\quad(m\ge m_0),
\tag{7.3}
$$

and take

$$
\underline\kappa
=\min\left(\frac\kappa2,w_0,\ldots,w_{m_0-1}\right),
\qquad
\overline\kappa
=\max\left(\frac{3\kappa}{2},w_0,\ldots,w_{m_0-1}\right).
\tag{7.4}
$$

Put

$$
C_c:=\frac{\overline\kappa}{\underline\kappa}.
\tag{7.5}
$$

Then, for every (0le rle a),

$$
\boxed{
\frac{c_{a-r}}{c_a}
\le C_c(H+1)^{3/2}\gamma^{-r}.
}
\tag{7.6}
$$

This follows immediately from

$$
\frac{c_{a-r}}{c_a}
=\gamma^{-r}
\frac{w_{a-r}}{w_a}
\left(\frac{a+1}{a-r+1}\right)^{3/2}.
\tag{7.7}
$$

For the logarithmic window (0le rle R_H), (2.14) gives the sharper uniform estimate

$$
\boxed{
\frac{c_{a-r}}{c_a}
=\gamma^{-r}
\left(1+O_B\!\left(\frac{r+1}{H}\right)\right).
}
\tag{7.8}
$$

## 7.2 Exact binomial ratio

For (0le rle a),

$$
\frac{\binom{n}{a-r}}{\binom{n}{a}}
=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}
\frac{a-j}{n-a+j+1}
=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}
\frac{H-b-j}{2H+b+j+2}.
\tag{7.9}
$$

Every factor is (<1/2), so

$$
\boxed{
0\le
\frac{\binom{n}{a-r}}{\binom{n}{a}}
\le2^{-r}.
}
\tag{7.10}
$$

When (rle R_H), take logarithms in (7.9). Since (r=O_B(log H)),

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\binom{n}{a-r}}{\binom{n}{a}}
=2^{-r}
\left(1+O_B\!\left(\frac{(r+1)^2}{H}\right)\right).
}
\tag{7.11}
$$

The exact product (7.9), not a heuristic entropy approximation, is used in the tail.

# 8. The valid tail estimate

Using (6.8), (7.6), and (7.10), the part with (r>R_H), normalized by (c_abinom na), is at most

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{H,b}
&:=\sum_{r>R_H}^{a}
 |K_{H,b}(a-r)|
 \frac{c_{a-r}}{c_a}
 \frac{\binom{n}{a-r}}{\binom{n}{a}}\\
&\le
D_BC_c(H+1)^{S_B+3/2}
\sum_{r>R_H}(2\gamma)^{-r}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
$$

Since (R_H=lceil L_Blog(H+1)rceil) and (L_B) is given by (5.8),

$$
(2\gamma)^{-R_H}
\le(H+1)^{-S_B-7/2}.
\tag{8.2}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
T_{H,b}
\le
\frac{D_BC_c}{1-(2\gamma)^{-1}}
(H+1)^{-2}.
}
\tag{8.3}
$$

This is the requested valid tail estimate. The polynomial cofactor loss is paid for explicitly by the logarithmic cutoff; nothing is hidden in an unjustified interchange of limits.

# 9. Completion of the asymptotic

Change variables (k=a-r) in (1.9):

$$
\widehat P_{H,b}(n)
=\sum_{r=0}^{a}
K_{H,b}(a-r)c_{a-r}\binom{n}{a-r}.
\tag{9.1}
$$

For (0le rle R_H), combine (5.11), (7.8), and (7.11):

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{K_{H,b}(a-r)c_{a-r}\binom{n}{a-r}}
 {(-1)^bs^bc_a\binom na}\\
&\qquad=
\binom{r+b}{b}\eta^r
\left(1+O_B\!\left(\frac{(r+1)^2}{H}\right)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.2}
$$

The weighted error is summable, because

$$
G_B(\eta)
:=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
\binom{r+B}{B}(r+1)^2\eta^r
=\left(\eta\frac d{d\eta}+1\right)^2
(1-\eta)^{-B-1}<\infty.
\tag{9.3}
$$

Thus the sum of the errors in (9.2) is (O_B(H^{-1})). The tail (r>R_H) is (O_B(H^{-2})) by (8.3). Also,

$$
\sum_{r>R_H}\binom{r+b}{b}\eta^r
=O_B(R_H^B\eta^{R_H})=O_B(H^{-2}).
\tag{9.4}
$$

Finally, the binomial generating function gives

$$
\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+b}{b}\eta^r
=(1-\eta)^{-b-1}.
\tag{9.5}
$$

Substitution into (9.1) proves (1.11), hence (1.12). ∎

## 11.2 Complete exact verifier

The following Sage program performs no floating-point linear algebra. It computes (A_j), (c_j), the denominator kernel, every numerator coefficient, and (widehat P) over (mathbf Q); only the final display is decimal. It checks every (0le ble8) and every admissible (Hle300), prints selected rows, and reports the worst deviation on each strip.

```python
# Run with: sage q828_exact_check.sage
from sage.all import *

HMAX = 300
BMAX = 8
SHOW_H = [20, 30, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300]

K.<rt2> = QuadraticField(2)
lam = K(17) + K(12)*rt2
gam = lam - 1
s = lam/gam
eta = 1/(2*gam)

# Apéry numbers, exactly.
A = [ZZ(0)]*(HMAX + 1)
A[0] = 1
A[1] = 5
for m in range(1, HMAX):
    num = (34*m^3 + 51*m^2 + 27*m + 5)*A[m] - m^3*A[m-1]
    den = (m+1)^3
    assert num % den == 0
    A[m+1] = num // den

# Forward differences c_k = Delta^k A_0, exactly.
c = []
for k in range(HMAX + 1):
    c.append(sum((-1)^(k-j)*binomial(k,j)*A[j] for j in range(k+1)))
    assert c[-1] > 0


def M(k, ell):
    if ell > k:
        return ZZ(0)
    return binomial(k, ell)*sum(
        binomial(ell,t)*c[k-ell+t] for t in range(ell+1)
    )


def exact_ratio(H, b):
    a = H-b
    n = 3*H + 1

    if b == 0:
        q = [QQ(1)]
    else:
        R = matrix(QQ, [
            [M(k,ell) for ell in range(b)]
            for k in range(a+1, H+1)
        ])
        rhs = vector(QQ, [-M(k,b) for k in range(a+1, H+1)])
        assert R.det() != 0
        q = list(R.solve_right(rhs)) + [QQ(1)]

    d = [
        sum(q[ell]*M(k,ell) for ell in range(b+1))
        for k in range(a+1)
    ]

    # Verify the annihilated high rows exactly.
    for k in range(a+1, H+1):
        assert sum(q[ell]*M(k,ell) for ell in range(b+1)) == 0

    Phat = sum(d[k]*binomial(n,k) for k in range(a+1))
    pred = ((-1)^b * s^b * K(c[a]) * K(binomial(n,a))
            * (1-eta)^(-b-1))
    rho = K(Phat)/pred
    return rho, Phat, q


print("selected exact ratios")
print("H,b,coeff_1,coeff_sqrt2,decimal,error")
for H in SHOW_H:
    for b in range(min(BMAX,H)+1):
        rho, Phat, q = exact_ratio(H,b)
        # rho = rho[0] + rho[1]*sqrt(2), both rational.
        dec = rho.n(80)
        err = abs(dec - 1)
        print(H, b, rho[0], rho[1], dec, err, sep=",")

print("\nworst deviation for each fixed strip, 8 <= H <= 300")
for b in range(BMAX+1):
    worst = RealField(100)(0)
    where = None
    for H in range(max(8,b), HMAX+1):
        rho, _, _ = exact_ratio(H,b)
        err = abs(rho.n(100) - 1)
        if err > worst:
            worst = err
            where = H
    print("b =", b, "max_error =", worst, "at H =", where)
```

# 12. What is corrected relative to Q803

The fixed-(b) conclusion of Q803 survives, but five proof statements must be replaced.

1. Poincaré cancellation. A pointwise expansion of (c_k) is insufficient. The required input is the differentiated expansion (2.26). The cancellation is then evaluated exactly by divided differences, giving (4.3).

1. Tail. The cutoff constant must pay for both polynomial losses. The explicit choice (5.8) gives the valid (O_B(H^{-2})) tail (8.3).
