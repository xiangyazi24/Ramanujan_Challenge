# 1. Inversion on the base and on the degree-two quotient

## 1.1 Three involutions written explicitly

Put

\[
\phi(u)=\frac{u(1-8u)}{1+u}.
\]

The equation \(\phi(u)=t\) is

\[
8u^2+(t-1)u+t=0.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus the deck involution of the degree-two quotient is

\[
d(u)=\frac{1-8u}{8(1+u)}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Indeed the product of the two roots of (1.1) is \(t/8\), and direct substitution gives

\[
d^2=1,
\qquad
\phi(d(u))=\phi(u).
\tag{1.3}
\]

There is a second involution

\[
w(u)=-\frac1{8u},
\tag{1.4}
\]

for which

\[
w^2=1,
\qquad
\phi(w(u))=\phi(u)^{-1}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The verification of (1.5) is one line:

\[
\phi\!\left(-\frac1{8u}\right)
=\frac{1+u}{u(1-8u)}
=\frac1{\phi(u)}.
\]

Moreover

\[
dw=wd,
\qquad
(dw)(u)=\frac{1+u}{8u-1}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

The map \(d\) is the Fricke \(w_6\) deck action in the project normalization. The two other Atkin–Lehner classes \(w_2,w_3\) differ by \(w_6\), so they induce the same involution \(t\mapsto t^{-1}\) on the quotient. In the usual cusp-width labeling, (1.4) is the \(w_2\) lift; if another level-structure convention calls it \(w_3\), all arguments below are unchanged. Replacing \(w\) by \(dw\) only multiplies its action on the negative eigendescent by \(-1\).

The action on the four singular parameters of the Beauville family is

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &0&-1&1/8&\infty\\ \hline
 d&1/8&\infty&0&-1\\
 w&\infty&1/8&-1&0.
\end{array}
\tag{1.7}
\]

This is compatible with the fibre types

\[
I_6,I_2,I_1,I_3
\]

at \(0,-1,1/8,\infty\): \(d=w_6\) pairs \(I_6\) with \(I_1\) and \(I_2\) with \(I_3\), while \(w\) pairs \(I_6\) with \(I_3\) and \(I_2\) with \(I_1\).

## 1.2 The action on the four punctures of the \(t\)-line

Palindromy gives

\[
q(t^{-1})=t^{-2}q(t),
\qquad
\alpha\beta=1.
\tag{1.8}
\]

Hence inversion acts by

\[
0\longleftrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\alpha\longleftrightarrow\beta.
\tag{1.9}
\]

For a pullback local system, a tame generator at \(0\) maps to the inverse of a tame generator at \(\infty\). Therefore the local matrix is \(T_\infty^{-1}\), not literally \(T_\infty\). But

\[
J_3^{-1}\sim J_3,
\]

because every regular unipotent element of \(\operatorname{GL}_3\) has one Jordan block and is conjugate to its inverse. At the branch points the matrices are involutions, so they are equal to their inverses. Thus

\[
\iota^*\mathcal A_+:
\quad J_3,J_3,\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1),
\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1),
\]

and

\[
\iota^*\mathcal A_-:
\quad J_3,J_3,\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1),
\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1)
\]

have exactly the same local conjugacy classes as \(\mathcal A_+\) and \(\mathcal A_-\), respectively.

This local check is necessary but not sufficient. These systems are not physically rigid. Indeed, for a rank-three system with four punctures, the rigidity index is

\[
(2-4)3^2+\sum_s\dim Z(T_s).
\]

For a regular \(J_3\), \(\dim Z(J_3)=3\); for a semisimple involution with multiplicities \(2+1\), the centralizer has dimension \(2^2+1^2=5\). Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rig}
=-18+(3+3+5+5)=-2,
\tag{1.10}
\]

not \(2\). Local monodromy data alone cannot manufacture (E.1). One needs the modular correspondence or an explicit global differential-module conjugacy.

# 2. Construction from the Beauville–Atkin–Lehner geometry

Let

\[
f:\mathcal E\longrightarrow U_u
\]

be the Beauville family

\[
E_u:\quad y^2+(1-2u)xy+u^2y=x^3,
\]

and set

\[
\mathcal F=R^1f_*\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell,
\qquad
\mathcal V=\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal F.
\]

The sheaf \(\mathcal F\) has rank two and weight one; \(\mathcal V\) has rank three and weight two with its natural symmetric form.

## 2.1 Normalized isogeny correspondences

For an Atkin–Lehner involution \(w_Q\) of exact divisor \(Q\mid6\), the modular interpretation gives a cyclic \(Q\)-isogeny

\[
\lambda_Q:E_u\longrightarrow E_{w_Q(u)}
\tag{2.1}
\]

on the universal family, possibly after a quadratic twist caused by the chosen Weierstrass equation. Such a quadratic twist is immaterial after symmetric square:

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal L)
\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal F
\qquad(\mathcal L^2=1).
\tag{2.2}
\]

On \(H^1\), pullback by a degree-\(Q\) isogeny is a symplectic similitude of multiplier \(Q\):

\[
\langle\lambda_Q^*x,\lambda_Q^*y\rangle
=Q\langle x,y\rangle.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
C_Q:=\frac1Q\operatorname{Sym}^2(\lambda_Q^*)
:w_Q^*\mathcal V\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal V
\tag{2.4}
\]

is an isometry for the symmetric form on \(\mathcal V\). If \(\widehat\lambda_Q\) is the dual isogeny, then

\[
\widehat\lambda_Q\lambda_Q=[Q].
\]

Multiplication by \(Q\) acts by \(Q\) on \(H^1\), hence by \(Q^2\) on \(\operatorname{Sym}^2H^1\). The factors \(1/Q\) in (2.4) therefore give the cocycle identity

\[
C_Q\circ w_Q^*C_Q=1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

All of this is defined algebraically over the modular base after inverting \(6\ell\), so the correspondence commutes with geometric and arithmetic Frobenius.

## 2.2 Passing to the \(w_6\)-eigendescents

Use \(C_6\) to linearize the deck action \(d=w_6\). Then

\[
\phi_*\mathcal V=\mathcal A_+\oplus\mathcal A_-,
\tag{2.6}
\]

where \(\mathcal A_+\) and \(\mathcal A_-\) are the \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigendescents. Equivalently,

\[
\mathcal A_-\simeq\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal K_q.
\tag{2.7}
\]

The Atkin–Lehner group at level six is abelian modulo diamond operators. On \(H^1\), a possible commutator is a scalar sign; after \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\), that sign disappears. Thus the normalized operators \(C_6\) and \(C_2\) commute on \(\mathcal V\). Since the rational maps \(d\) and \(w\) commute as in (1.6), \(C_2\) preserves both eigenspaces in (2.6). It therefore descends to

\[
c_\pm:\iota^*\mathcal A_\pm\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal A_\pm.
\tag{2.8}
\]

The normalization (2.5) gives

\[
c_\pm\circ\iota^*c_\pm=1,
\tag{2.9}
\]

and (2.4) shows that \(c_\pm\) are isometries for the orthogonal forms. If the alternate lift \(w_3=w_2w_6\) is used, then \(c_-\) is replaced by \(-c_-\); equations (2.8), (2.9), and the duality sign are unchanged.

This construction shows directly that inversion preserves each eigendescent rather than swapping them. There are also two quick consistency checks:

1. \(\det(\iota^*\mathcal A_+)=\iota^*\mathcal K_q\simeq\mathcal K_q\), whereas \(\det\mathcal A_-=1\), so \(\iota^*\mathcal A_+\) cannot be isomorphic to \(\mathcal A_-\).
2. At a branch point the eigenvalue multiplicities are \(2+1\) for \(\mathcal A_+\) and \(1+2\) for \(\mathcal A_-\). Inversion swaps the two branch points but does not interchange these two multiplicity patterns.

# 3. Independent de Rham construction on the \(t\)-line

The invariant descent \(\mathcal A_+\) is governed by the Apéry rank-three operator

\[
L=\theta^3-tP(\theta)+t^2(\theta+1)^3,
\qquad
P(X)=34X^3+51X^2+27X+5,
\tag{3.1}
\]

with \(\theta=t\,d/dt\). The polynomial satisfies

\[
P(-1-X)=-P(X).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define

\[
(Rf)(t)=t^{-1}f(t^{-1}).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then

\[
R^2=1,
\qquad
R\theta R=-1-\theta,
\qquad
RtR=t^{-1}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Using (3.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
RLR
&=(-1-\theta)^3-t^{-1}P(-1-\theta)
+t^{-2}(-\theta)^3\\
&=-(\theta+1)^3+t^{-1}P(\theta)-t^{-2}\theta^3\\
&=-t^{-2}L.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus \(R\) maps solutions of \(L\) to solutions of \(L\), and \(R^2=1\). This is an explicit rational, involutive isomorphism of the de Rham differential module

\[
\iota^*\mathcal A_{+,\mathrm{dR}}\simeq\mathcal A_{+,\mathrm{dR}}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

No half-integral gauge such as \(t^{1/2}\) occurs. Tensoring (3.6) with the inversion isomorphism of \(\mathcal K_q\) constructed below gives the corresponding de Rham isomorphism for \(\mathcal A_-\).

Equation (3.5) completely settles the Betti/de Rham object. It does not, by itself, identify the chosen integral \(\ell\)-adic Weil structure: that upgrade is supplied by the modular isogeny correspondence of Section 2.

# 4. The determinant local system is inversion-invariant on the nose

Let

\[
Y\longrightarrow U,
\qquad
Y:\ z^2=q(t),
\]

be the quadratic cover defining \(\mathcal K_q\). Its pullback by inversion is

\[
\iota^*Y:\ z^2=q(t^{-1})=rac{q(t)}{t^2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

There is an explicit isomorphism over \(U\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\iota^*Y&\longrightarrow Y,\\
(t,z)&\longmapsto(t,tz).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed,

\[
(tz)^2=t^2q(t^{-1})=q(t).
\]

The inverse is \((t,Z)\mapsto(t,Z/t)\), and applying inversion twice gives the identity. Therefore (4.2) is a genuine descent cocycle defined over \(\mathbf F_p\), and

\[
\iota^*\mathcal K_q\simeq\mathcal K_q
\tag{4.3}
\]

compatibly with Weil structure.

In square-class language,

\[
[\iota^*q]=[q/t^2]=[q]
\quad\text{in}\quad
\mathcal O(U)^\times/\mathcal O(U)^{\times2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The proposed extra sheaf \(\mathcal L_{\chi_2(t)}\) would correspond to multiplying the square class by \(t\), not by \(t^2\). The expression

\[
\sqrt{q(t^{-1})}=\frac{\sqrt{q(t)}}{t}
\]

uses the globally defined rational function \(1/t\); it does **not** require a choice of \(\sqrt t\).

There are also decisive determinant and local-monodromy checks against a Kummer shift. Let \(\mathcal K_t=\mathcal L_{\chi_2(t)}\). If one had

\[
\iota^*\mathcal A_+\simeq\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal K_t,
\]

then, because \(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal A_+=3\),

\[
\det(\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal K_t)
=\mathcal K_q\otimes\mathcal K_t^3
=\mathcal K_q\otimes\mathcal K_t,
\]

contradicting (4.3). Moreover, \(\mathcal K_t\) changes the eigenvalue \(1\) of the \(J_3\) block at \(0\) to \(-1\), while inversion merely replaces the block at infinity by its inverse, still a unipotent block with eigenvalue \(1\).

Finally, (2.7) and (4.3) give

\[
\iota^*\mathcal A_-
\simeq\iota^*(\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal K_q)
\simeq\mathcal A_+\otimes\mathcal K_q
\simeq\mathcal A_-.
\tag{4.5}
\]

# 5. Verdier duality and the exact Tate twist

Each \(\mathcal A_\pm\) is pure of weight two and carries a nondegenerate symmetric pairing

\[
\langle\ ,\ \rangle_\pm:
\mathcal A_\pm\otimes\mathcal A_\pm
\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-2).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The fact that \(\det\mathcal A_+=\mathcal K_q\) does not change the target of this form. It means only that the orthogonal representation has nontrivial determinant character. From (5.1),

\[
\mathcal A_\pm^\vee\simeq\mathcal A_\pm(2).
\tag{5.2}
\]

On a smooth curve the dualizing complex is \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(1)[2]\). Hence

\[
D(\mathcal A[1])
=\mathcal A^\vee(1)[1].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Verdier duality commutes with middle extension, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(N_\pm)
&\simeq j_{!*}(\mathcal A_\pm^\vee(1))[1]\\
&\simeq j_{!*}(\mathcal A_\pm(3))[1]\\
&=N_\pm(3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Combining (5.4) with \(c_\pm:\iota^*\mathcal A_\pm\simeq\mathcal A_\pm\), and extending uniquely through middle extension, gives

\[
\boxed{
N_\pm\simeq\iota^*D(N_\pm)(-3).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the unnormalized form of Katz autoduality. Since a lisse weight-two sheaf shifted by \([1]\) is a perverse object of weight three, set formally

\[
\widetilde N_\pm=N_\pm(3/2).
\]

Verdier duality reverses Tate twists:

\[
D(N(a))=D(N)(-a).
\]

Thus

\[
D(\widetilde N_\pm)
=D(N_\pm)(-3/2)
\simeq N_\pm(3/2)
=\widetilde N_\pm,
\]

and (5.5) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde N_\pm\simeq\iota^*D(\widetilde N_\pm).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

If one prefers not to adjoin a formal half-Tate twist, retain (5.5). On trace functions, the corresponding analytic normalization is division by \(p^{3/2}\).

The correspondence in Section 2 is defined over the finite base and commutes with Frobenius, so (5.5) is compatible with Weil structures. Without that arithmetic correspondence, (3.5) proves only the geometric Betti/de Rham version; an unramified scalar character could then remain in the arithmetic normalization. It would affect only the arithmetic Frobenius torsor, not the geometric symplectic group, and it would not create a Mellin-character shift.

# 6. The sign of the Mellin pairing

Katz's convolution dual is

\[
N^\vee=\iota^*D(N).
\]

For a weight-zero object, an isomorphism \(N\simeq N^\vee\) has sign \(+1\) if the induced form is symmetric and \(-1\) if it is alternating. We now compute the sign rather than infer it from dimension.

Fix a nonexceptional multiplicative character \(\chi\), and write

\[
V_{\pm,\chi}
=H_c^1\!\left(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
\mathcal A_\pm\otimes\mathcal L_\chi\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

For generic \(\chi\), the natural map from compactly supported to ordinary cohomology gives the clean/parabolic fibre used by Katz, and \(H_c^0,H_c^2\) vanish. Inversion gives

\[
\iota^*\mathcal L_\chi=\mathcal L_{\chi^{-1}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For \(x,y\in V_{\pm,\chi}\), define

\[
B_{\pm,\chi}(x,y)
=\operatorname{Tr}_U\!\left(
 x\smile(c_\pm\otimes1)(\iota^*y)
\right).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The two coefficient characters in (6.3) are \(\chi\) and \(\chi^{-1}\), so they cancel. The coefficient pairing (5.1) and the trace map

\[
H_c^2(U,\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-2))
\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-3)
\]

show that

\[
B_{\pm,\chi}:
V_{\pm,\chi}\otimes V_{\pm,\chi}
\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-3).
\tag{6.4}
\]

It is nondegenerate by Poincaré duality and the isomorphism \(c_\pm\).

The symmetry calculation has exactly one minus sign:

1. The coefficient pairing (5.1) is symmetric.
2. Both classes have cohomological degree one, so graded commutativity gives
   \[
   y\smile x=(-1)^{1\cdot1}x\smile y=-x\smile y.
   \]
3. Pullback by the degree-one automorphism \(\iota\) produces no orientation sign in étale cohomology.
4. The cocycle identity \(c_\pm\iota^*c_\pm=1\) and the fact that \(c_\pm\) is an isometry allow the two appearances of \(c_\pm\) to be interchanged without another scalar.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
B_{\pm,\chi}(y,x)=-B_{\pm,\chi}(x,y).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The perverse shift \([1]\) is the categorical expression of this same degree-one cup-product sign. It must not be counted a second time. Inversion contributes no additional sign.

After twisting each Mellin fibre by \((3/2)\), the target in (6.4) becomes the trivial line, and (6.5) is an ordinary symplectic form. Thus both Katz autodualities have sign

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_+=\varepsilon_-=-1.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

This agrees with Katz, *Convolution and Equidistribution*, Chapter 11, Theorem 11.1: in the relevant construction the autoduality sign of the perverse Mellin object is opposite to the sign of the self-dual input sheaf. An orthogonal input therefore yields a symplectic Mellin object.

There is also a useful normalization check at the fixed points of inversion. For \(p>3\), both \(t=1\) and \(t=-1\) lie in \(U\), since

\[
q(1)=-32,
\qquad
q(-1)=36.
\]

If an involutive lift \(c_\pm\) were anti-isometric on the odd-dimensional orthogonal stalk, its \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspaces would have to be totally isotropic dual spaces of equal dimension, impossible in dimension three. Hence the normalized correspondence is isometric, not anti-isometric. This rules out a hidden second sign in (6.5).

# 7. Mellin reflection and the absence of a character shift

Let

\[
f_\pm(t)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_t\mid\mathcal A_\pm)
\]

on the lisse locus. Weil compatibility of \(c_\pm\) gives

\[
f_\pm(t^{-1})=f_\pm(t).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_\pm(\chi)
&=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}f_\pm(t)\chi(t)\\
&=\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}f_\pm(t^{-1})\chi(t)\\
&=\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p^\times}f_\pm(u)\chi^{-1}(u)\\
&=S_\pm(\chi^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For \(\chi=\omega^{-r}\), this is \(r\mapsto p-1-r\), exactly the project's strong reflection.

If instead inversion had introduced \(\mathcal K_t=\mathcal L_{\chi_2(t)}\), the relation would be

\[
S_+(\chi)=S_+(\chi_2\chi^{-1}),
\]

corresponding to

\[
r\longmapsto\frac{p-1}{2}-r.
\]

That is not the universal reflection law. The mod-24 quarter-point phenomena arise from special low-order characters and branch-polynomial reciprocity; they do not alter the object-level inversion cocycle.

# 8. Consequences for the Tannakian groups

The generic Mellin dimensions remain

\[
\dim V_{+,\chi}=2,
\qquad
\dim V_{-,\chi}=4.
\tag{8.1}
\]

The nondegenerate alternating forms (6.5) imply

\[
G_{+,\mathrm{geom}}\subseteq\operatorname{Sp}(V_+)=\operatorname{Sp}_2,
\]

and

\[
G_{-,\mathrm{geom}}\subseteq\operatorname{Sp}(V_-)=\operatorname{Sp}_4.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For the unnormalized weight-three fibres, arithmetic Frobenius is a symplectic similitude of multiplier \(p^3\). Thus one may state

\[
G_{\pm,\mathrm{arith}}\subseteq\operatorname{GSp}(V_\pm)
\]

before weight normalization. After the formal half-Tate normalization, the Weil-compatible pairing is scalar-valued and the normalized arithmetic group lies in the corresponding symplectic group, possibly with a finite Frobenius component/torsor if the half-Tate normalization is not fixed globally. The geometric group is unambiguously symplectic because the cyclotomic multiplier is trivial on the geometric fundamental group.

## 8.1 What remains for \(G_+=\operatorname{SL}_2\)

Since

\[
\operatorname{Sp}_2=\operatorname{SL}_2,
\]

one still needs:

1. geometric irreducibility of the Mellin object in Katz's quotient category;
2. connectedness, or at least Lie-irreducibility, of the geometric Mellin group;
3. infinitude/positive dimension, excluding a finite binary polyhedral subgroup.

Once these are proved, the only connected positive-dimensional irreducible subgroup compatible with the standard two-dimensional symplectic representation is \(\operatorname{SL}_2\).

## 8.2 What remains for \(G_-\)

The containment in \(\operatorname{Sp}_4\) does not distinguish

\[
\operatorname{Sp}_4
\quad\text{from}\quad
\operatorname{Sym}^3(\operatorname{SL}_2)\subset\operatorname{Sp}_4.
\]

After connectedness, infinitude, and Lie-irreducibility are established, the fourth tensor moment is the cheapest separator:

\[
\dim(V^{\otimes4})^{\operatorname{Sp}_4}=3,
\qquad
\dim((\operatorname{Sym}^3W)^{\otimes4})^{\operatorname{SL}_2}=4.
\]

The present autoduality calculation supplies the ambient symplectic group; it does not settle this dichotomy.

# 9. Bad characters and the dimensions \(d_+=2,d_-=4\)

Nothing in the inversion construction changes the Euler–Poincaré calculation.

At \(0\) and \(\infty\), both sheaves have one regular-unipotent block with eigenvalue \(1\). Twisting by a Kummer character \(\chi\) changes the local scalar to \(\chi\) at \(0\) and \(\chi^{-1}\) at \(\infty\). Inertia invariants therefore reappear only when

\[
\chi=1.
\]

At \(\alpha,\beta\), the Kummer character is unramified, so the fixed dimensions remain

\[
\dim\mathcal A_+^{I_\alpha}
=\dim\mathcal A_+^{I_\beta}=2,
\]

and

\[
\dim\mathcal A_-^{I_\alpha}
=\dim\mathcal A_-^{I_\beta}=1.
\]

This gives the already established generic dimensions

\[
d_+=6-2-2=2,
\qquad
d_-=6-1-1=4.
\tag{9.1}
\]

There is no new exceptional character \(\chi_2\). The quadratic Kummer character of \(t\) has local scalar \(-1\) at both \(0\) and \(\infty\), so it kills rather than creates invariants. The finite-branch sign sheaf \(\mathcal K_q\) is not a Kummer character on \(\mathbf G_m\); its ramification is at \(\alpha,\beta\), and it is already built into the distinction between \(\mathcal A_+\) and \(\mathcal A_-\).

Assuming geometric irreducibility, neither \(\mathcal A_\pm\otimes\mathcal L_\chi\) can contain the trivial rank-one sheaf, so no additional \(H_c^0\) or \(H_c^2\) exception appears. Thus the obvious bad-character list remains

\[
\boxed{\{1\}.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Any further exceptional characters would have to arise from a separate global Kummer constituent or convolution-negligibility phenomenon, not from inversion, determinant, or local inertia.
