# Verdict

Yes, the Fermat–Mellin substitution gives an exact toric reduction. No, Ax–Katz, Adolphson–Sperber, Stickelberger, or Katz’s zeta congruence presently evaluates it or improves the known bound on (Z_p).

The exact output is a ((p-2))-dimensional weighted multiplicative-convolution sum:

[

boxed{

sum_{(t_1,ldots,t_{p-2})in(mathbf F_p^times)^{p-2}}

left(prod_{i=1}^{p-2}A_p(t_i)right)

A_p!left((t_1cdots t_{p-2})^{-1}right)-1

pmod p,

}

tag{E}

]

where

[

A_p(t)=sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^rinmathbf F_p[t]

]

is the Apéry Hasse polynomial. Since (0le |Z_p|<p), formula (E) determines the count exactly from that residue.

But the complexity grows with (p): the number of variables is (p-2), the Hasse polynomial itself has length (p), and the sum is a weight rather than an ordinary point count or additive-character sum. The apparent “exact-count shortcut” therefore repackages the same zero-coefficient problem into the ((p-1))-st multiplicative moment of the Hasse function. It does not produce a fixed toric variety or a fixed Newton polyhedron.

A fixed congruence such as (|Z_p|equiv1pmod p) is impossible: for example, (|Z_5|=2), while (|Z_7|=0). Because (|Z_p|<p), such a congruence would force the count to equal (1) at every prime.

# 1. Exact Fermat–Mellin reduction

Let

[

q=p-1,

qquad

G=mathbf F_p^times,

qquad

widehat A_p(chi)=sum_{tin G}A_p(t)chi(t)

]

for a multiplicative character (chiinwidehat G).

For (1le zle p-2), multiplicative orthogonality gives the exact coefficient formula

[

widehat A_p(chi_z)=-b_z,

qquad

chi_z(t)=t^{-z}.

tag{1.1}

]

Indeed,

[

sum_{tin G}A_p(t)t^{-z}

=

sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rsum_{tin G}t^{r-z}

=-b_z.

]

At the trivial character there are two endpoint coefficients:

[

widehat A_p(1)

=-b_0-b_{p-1}=-2,

tag{1.2}

]

using (b_0equiv b_{p-1}equiv1pmod p). Since (q) is even,

[

widehat A_p(1)^q=1=b_{p-1}^q.

]

Consequently the full Fermat sum, including (z=p-1), is exactly the (q)-th Mellin moment:

[

sum_{z=1}^{p-1}b_z^q

=

sum_{chiinwidehat G}widehat A_p(chi)^q

quadtext{in }mathbf F_p.

tag{1.3}

]

Expanding the right side and applying character orthogonality,

[

begin{aligned}

sum_{chi}widehat A_p(chi)^q

&=

sum_{t_1,ldots,t_qin G}

A_p(t_1)cdots A_p(t_q)

sum_{chi}chi(t_1cdots t_q)\

&=

qsum_{substack{t_1,ldots,t_qin G\t_1cdots t_q=1}}

A_p(t_1)cdots A_p(t_q).

end{aligned}

tag{1.4}

]

Put

[

C_p:=

sum_{substack{t_1,ldots,t_qin G\t_1cdots t_q=1}}

prod_{i=1}^{q}A_p(t_i)

=(A_p^{*q})(1),

tag{1.5}

]

the identity coefficient of the (q)-fold multiplicative convolution. Since (qequiv-1pmod p),

[

sum_{z=1}^{p-1}b_z^{p-1}equiv-C_ppmod p.

tag{1.6}

]

Fermat therefore gives

[

boxed{|Z_p|equiv C_p-1pmod p.}

tag{1.7}

]

Eliminating (t_q=(t_1cdots t_{q-1})^{-1}) gives formula (E).

This reduction is completely explicit and applies to any function on a finite cyclic group: the number of zero Fourier coefficients is encoded by the group-order moment of its Fourier transform. It is best viewed as a universal Fourier identity, not yet an Apéry-specific evaluation.

The project’s underlying Mellin identity and general finite-field hypergeometric lift are recorded in Q205 and Q275. Greene’s finite-field hypergeometric formalism is in J. Greene, Hypergeometric functions over finite fields, Trans. AMS 301 (1987), 77–101.

# 2. Why this is not yet an Ax–Katz point count

Ax–Katz concerns the number (N) of common zeros of polynomials

[

f_1,ldots,f_sinmathbf F_p[x_1,ldots,x_n]

]

of degrees (d_1gecdotsge d_s). Its standard bound is

[

operatorname{ord}_p(N)

ge

leftlceil

frac{n-(d_1+cdots+d_s)}{d_1}

rightrceil

]

when the right side is positive. Primary references are:

- J. Ax, Zeroes of polynomials over finite fields, Amer. J. Math. 86 (1964), 255–261;

- N. Katz, On a theorem of Ax, Amer. J. Math. 93 (1971), 485–499.

There are two separate failures here.

## 2.1 (C_p) is a weighted sum, not a zero count

Formula (1.5) sums the values

[

A_p(t_1)cdots A_p(t_q)

]

over the subtorus (t_1cdots t_q=1). Ax–Katz gives divisibility of the number of solutions to equations. It does not evaluate a general (mathbf F_p)-valued weight.

One can decompose the weight by fibers,

[

C_p=sum_{cinmathbf F_p}c,#{mathbf t:F_p(mathbf t)=c},

]

but this creates (p) weighted point counts and does not remove the original problem.

## 2.2 Even the underlying torus equation is at the Ax–Katz threshold

Before eliminating one variable, the multiplicative constraint is

[

t_1t_2cdots t_q-1=0

]

in (n=q) variables and has degree (d=q). Thus

[

n-d=0,

]

so Ax–Katz supplies no positive (p)-divisibility at all, even for the unweighted subtorus count. Adding equations or graph variables to encode the Apéry weight only worsens the degree budget.

Equivalently, if one interpolates (zmapsto b_z) by a polynomial in one (mathbf F_p)-variable, its available degree is of order (p), so the one-variable Ax–Katz bound is again vacuous.

Therefore Ax–Katz does not close (E). It cannot force (|Z_p|) into a small congruence class.

# 3. Adolphson–Sperber: the Newton-polyhedron theory has the wrong object and nonuniform complexity

Adolphson–Sperber refine Chevalley–Warning/Ax–Katz divisibility using supports and Newton polyhedra, and develop cohomological estimates for toric exponential sums. Relevant primary references are:

- A. Adolphson and S. Sperber, (p)-adic estimates for exponential sums and the theorem of Chevalley–Warning, Ann. Sci. ENS 20 (1987), 545–556;

- A. Adolphson and S. Sperber, Exponential sums and Newton polyhedra: cohomology and estimates, Ann. of Math. 130 (1989), 367–406;

- A. Adolphson and S. Sperber, Exponential sums on ((G_m)^n), Invent. Math. 101 (1990), 63–79.

These theorems do not apply as a black box for three reasons.

1. The sum is not an additive-character exponential sum. Formula (E) is

1. The dimension grows with the characteristic. The torus has dimension (p-2), whereas Adolphson–Sperber’s useful uniform conclusions concern fixed dimension and controlled Newton data.

1. The Newton support grows with (p). Each factor is the length-(p) Hasse polynomial (A_p), and there are (p-1) factors. Any associated Newton polytope and cohomology rank therefore grow rapidly with (p). Even a successful nondegeneracy analysis would produce a large (p)-dependent cohomological object, not a fixed exact formula or a horizontal average theorem.

Thus Adolphson–Sperber can describe valuations and weights once one has a controlled toric exponential-sum family. It does not convert the (p)-dependent convolution (E) into a bounded-complexity object.

# 4. Stickelberger and Gross–Koblitz: term valuations do not control cancellation

For (1le zle p-2), let (A_z=baromega^z). The project’s exact Greene/Jacobi lift is

[

T_p(z)

=-frac1{p-1}sum_{chi}

J(A_zchi,barchi)^2

J(A_z^{-1}chi,barchi)^2,

qquad

T_p(z)equiv b_zpmod{mathfrak p}.

tag{4.1}

]

Stickelberger’s theorem determines the (p)-adic valuation and leading term of each Gauss sum; Gross–Koblitz rewrites Gauss sums using the (p)-adic gamma function. A standard modern formula is B. Gross and N. Koblitz, Gauss sums and the (p)-adic (Gamma)-function, Ann. of Math. 109 (1979), 569–581.

This gives useful termwise information. It does not determine

[

v_{mathfrak p}!left(sum_{chi}text{Jacobi-term}_{z,chi}right),

]

because several terms may have the same minimal valuation and cancel. The zero condition is exactly

[

zin Z_p

iff

v_{mathfrak p}(T_p(z))ge1.

tag{4.2}

]

So the missing information is cancellation among the initial forms of the minimal-slope terms as (A_z) varies.

Raising (4.1) to the (p-1) power does not invoke the freshman’s-dream simplification: the exponent is (p-1), not (p). Expanding creates (p-1) independent character variables, and summing over (z) imposes one character-balance constraint. This is the Gauss-sum version of the ((p-2))-dimensional convolution (E).

In principle, a new theorem could use Stickelberger to prove that the minimum valuation is uniquely attained for all but (O(1)) characters (A_z). That would bound (Z_p). No such uniqueness theorem is known here, and the Gross–Koblitz initial-form calculation collapses back to the same truncated Apéry/hypergeometric residue whose zeros are being counted.

# 5. Katz’s “Une formule de congruence pour la fonction ζ” does not count zero coefficients

Katz’s congruence formula is:

- N. Katz, Une formule de congruence pour la fonction ζ, in SGA 7 II, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 340 (1973), 401–438.

It relates the zeta function of a fixed algebraic variety modulo (p) to Frobenius/Cartier action on coherent cohomology—the Hasse–Witt side of the variety.

In the Apéry setup, however,

[

A_p(t)=sum b_rt^r

]

is already the scalar Hasse–Witt polynomial of the family. The statistic (|Z_p|) asks how many coefficients of this polynomial vanish. Katz’s formula controls the zeta function and Frobenius action of a geometric fiber or variety; it does not count zero entries in a chosen coordinate expansion of its Hasse–Witt invariant.

One could formally apply zeta machinery to a newly constructed (p)-dependent convolution variety, but its dimension and cohomology grow with (p). Katz’s determinant formula would then be another exact description of a rapidly growing operator, not a bounded formula for (|Z_p|).

This is the geometric version of the fundamental distinction:

[

text{zero of the Hasse polynomial at a parameter}

quadneqquad

text{zero of one Mellin coefficient of that polynomial}.

]

The former is a standard point/fiber condition; the latter is a Fourier-support statistic.

# 6. Why a prime-by-prime congruence would not prove mean (1)

The numerical law predicts

[

frac1{pi(X)}sum_{ple X}|Z_p|longrightarrow1,

]

with individual values distributed approximately as doubled Poisson. This is a horizontal distribution statement across primes.

Ax–Katz, Adolphson–Sperber, Stickelberger, and Katz’s zeta congruence are primarily prime-by-prime tools. Even a computable exact residue (C_p-1pmod p) does not give its average as (p) varies, because the dimension, polynomial, character field, and Newton data all vary with (p).

# 7. Bottom line

## What is proved by the exact-count route

There is a clean exact identity:

[

boxed{|Z_p|equiv(A_p^{*(p-1)})(1)-1pmod p.}

]

Because (|Z_p|<p), this is an exact algorithm for the count. It can be evaluated in (O(p^2)) operations using a multiplicative FFT/convolution, or directly from the (b_z); it is not presently a theoretical simplification.

## What the named theorems do not give

- Ax–Katz: no positive divisibility; the torus constraint already has degree equal to the number of variables, and the target is weighted.

- Adolphson–Sperber: no fixed-dimensional/nondegenerate toric exponential-sum family; dimension and Newton support grow with (p).

- Stickelberger/Gross–Koblitz: termwise slopes are explicit, but zero detection depends on cancellations among equal-slope terms.

- Katz’s zeta congruence: controls zeta/Hasse–Witt data of a fixed variety, not the number of vanishing coefficients of a (p)-dependent Hasse polynomial.

## Honest status

No cited theorem closes the route, proves the average (|Z_p|to1), or improves the project’s unconditional bound (|Z_p|ll p^{2/3}). The exact expansion is valuable conceptually—it identifies the missing object as a high multiplicative-convolution coefficient—but it does not evade the same-characteristic horizontal cancellation wall.

The only plausible new opening inside this framework is a genuinely new initial-form noncancellation theorem for the Stickelberger/Gross–Koblitz expansion, uniform in the moving character (A_z). That theorem is not presently available and would itself constitute the breakthrough.
