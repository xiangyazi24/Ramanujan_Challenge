# Stepanov multiplicity versus a second-order shift recurrence

## Verdict

The classical p^{2/3} argument is a local jet argument: an ordinary derivation creates multiplicity at each individual zero, and one counts

[

K,|Z|le deg Psi.

]

Your shift operator is nonlocal. The map (tau f(z)=f(z+1)) moves a point instead of increasing its multiplicity. Therefore the stated recurrence, the square Frobenius defect, and

[

gcd(P(z),P(z+1))=1

]

do not by themselves give the classical Stepanov inequality or prove (# Z_pll p^{2/3}).

There is, however, a concrete discrete substitute. Iterating the recurrence produces explicit gap continuants (N_h(z)). If one proves the additional nondegeneracy estimate

[

N_hnotequiv0pmod p,qquad 1le hle p^{1/3},

]

or equivalently

[

#{ain Z_p:a+hin Z_p}ll h,

]

then an elementary close-pair argument gives

[

boxed{# Z_pll p^{2/3}.}

]

That nondegeneracy statement is the exact missing lemma.

# 2. Why the shift equation does not directly adapt

Put

[

## 2.1 A shift is not a derivation

For an ordinary derivation,

[

Psi(a)=Psi'(a)=cdots=Psi^{(K-1)}(a)=0

]

means that ((z-a)^KmidPsi(z)).

For the forward difference (Delta=tau-1), the conditions

[

Psi(a)=DeltaPsi(a)=cdots=Delta^{K-1}Psi(a)=0

]

are equivalent to

[

Psi(a)=Psi(a+1)=cdots=Psi(a+K-1)=0.

]

They give a string of distinct zeros, not one zero of multiplicity (K). Consequently there is no automatic inequality

[

K# Z_pledegPsi.

]

The strings belonging to different roots may overlap almost completely.

## 2.2 What the square defect really gives

Since (F(z)) has a simple zero at every (ainmathbf F_p), the congruence

[

LPin(F^2)

]

controls the value and first derivative of (LP) at every field point. Equivalently, the recurrence is exact in the nonreduced ring

[

mathbf F_p[z]/(F^2),

]

which contains only first jets.

Because

[

F(z+1)=F(z),

]

iterating the shift operator preserves the ideal ((F^2)); it does not produce

[

LPin(F^K)

]

for (Kasymp p^{2/3}). Thus the defect supplies only a two-jet, whereas the Stepanov count needs about (p^{2/3}) jets.

The second-order nature merely replaces a scalar state by

[

begin{pmatrix}P(z)\P(z-1)end{pmatrix}.

]

It does not restore locality.

Finally,

[

gcd(P(z),P(z+1))=1

]

only says that two adjacent field elements cannot both be roots. By itself this gives at best (# Z_ple(p+1)/2).

# 4. The precise missing lemma

The recurrence and adjacent-root coprimality reduce the problem to the following concrete statement:

Gap-continuant nondegeneracy. Prove that, uniformly for

[

1le hle c p^{1/3},

]

the polynomial (N_h(z)) is nonzero modulo (p), or directly prove

[

deggcdbigl(P(z),P(z+h)bigr)ll h.

]

The leading coefficient (ell_h) of (N_h) obeys

[

ell_{h+1}=34ell_h-ell_{h-1},qquad

ell_0=0,quad ell_1=-1.

]

So (pnmidell_h) is a simple sufficient condition, but it need not hold uniformly for all (h). A complete proof should instead establish primitivity of a suitable coefficient, a resultant identity, or a uniform transfer-matrix nondegeneracy theorem.

The square Frobenius defect is enough to derive the exact transfer identity. It does not itself prove this nondegeneracy.

Hence the answer to part (3) is:

[

boxed{

text{The }p^{2/3}text{ bound does not yet follow from the three stated hypotheses alone.}

}

]

It follows immediately once the explicit gap-continuant lemma above is supplied. This is the correct discrete analogue of the nonvanishing lemma in Stepanov's method.
