# Direct Hasse-polynomial control for the Apéry Calabi–Yau family

## Verdict

The direct Hasse-polynomial route is mathematically well targeted, but uniform sublinear root counting for a Calabi–Yau threefold Hasse invariant is presently an open frontier.

Dwork, Katz, Adolphson–Sperber, Beukers–Vlasenko, Huang–Lian–Yau–Yu, and Long–Tu–Yui–Zudilin provide strong and explicit answers to the following questions:

- what the Hasse invariant is;

- why it is a truncated period;

- how it detects the ordinary locus;

- how the unit root is recovered on that ordinary locus;

## 2. Why the elliptic class-number case is exceptional

For elliptic curves, vanishing of the Hasse invariant is equivalent to supersingularity. Supersingular elliptic curves have quaternionic endomorphism algebras, and their isomorphism classes are controlled by maximal orders and optimal embeddings. This produces Deuring/Eichler class-number formulas.

In particular, the number of supersingular j-invariants lying in \mathbf F_p is expressed through imaginary-quadratic class numbers and is therefore of approximately square-root scale. Concrete Hasse-polynomial versions are developed in Brillhart–Morton, Class numbers of quadratic fields, Hasse invariants of elliptic curves, and the supersingular polynomial.

Two warnings are important.

1. Over \overline{\mathbf F}_p, the total number of supersingular j-invariants is itself linear in p—roughly p/12. The class-number phenomenon controls which of them descend to \mathbf F_p.

1. The proof uses the moduli interpretation of elliptic curves and quaternionic endomorphisms. It is not a formal consequence of “being a Hasse invariant.”

The key missing step is therefore a low-complexity sheaf-theoretic representation of the graph or collision relation, not merely a fixed crystal producing H_p.

### 3.2 Frobenius-matrix factorization

Dwork theory gives a full Frobenius matrix before extracting the scalar unit root. A productive explicit attack is:

1. compute the Frobenius/Cartier matrix in a cyclic Picard–Fuchs basis;

1. identify H_p as a specified entry or minor modulo p;

1. search for exact determinant, Cassini, norm, or involution identities among that entry and its shifts;

1. use a second independent minor to control common zeros.

A scalar functional equation such as z\mapsto p-1-z, or a symmetric-square factorization, explains paired roots and evenness. By itself it changes only constants. To improve the exponent, one needs an identity that makes simultaneous roots over many shifts arithmetically rare.

### 3.3 A Calabi–Yau analogue of an Igusa tower

For elliptic curves, Igusa structures trivialize the ordinary p-divisible group and place the supersingular locus inside a well-understood modular compactification. A genuine Calabi–Yau analogue would be a finite cover of the ordinary parameter curve trivializing the unit-root line, with controlled genus and ramification as p varies.

If the nonordinary fibers became Frobenius-fixed or ramification points on a cover of genus p^{o(1)}, Weil bounds could give a sublinear count. No such controlled Igusa tower is currently available for this rank-two weight-three Calabi–Yau motive.

Strassmann’s theorem does not fix this automatically. It bounds zeros of one p-adic analytic function in one disk from its Newton polygon. Here one is counting roots in all p residue classes, and the relevant first truncation has degree growing with p. A useful Strassmann argument would require a p-independent bound on the Weierstrass degree of the full Frobenius coefficient on the entire parameter space; no such bound is currently known.

Higher Hasse–Witt matrices may still help. They can distinguish the exact slope jump at a zero and perhaps provide a second equation whose common zero set is much smaller. That is a concrete computational direction, but not yet an available theorem.

## 5. Sharp answer to the three questions

### (1) Is there a known uniform class-number-type or Dwork zero-count theorem?

No, not for this Calabi–Yau threefold Hasse polynomial. Existing theory identifies H_p, proves generic ordinarity, and computes the unit root on H_p\ne0. The elliptic class-number formulas depend on special modular/quaternionic geometry and have no known CY3 analogue.

### (2) What specific structure could improve p^{2/3}?

The most concrete targets are:

- an average shifted-root estimate for the gap continuants;

- a two-variable bounded-conductor sheaf encoding collisions;

- an exact factorization of the Frobenius matrix giving a second independent vanishing condition;

- a controlled Igusa-type cover of the ordinary locus;

- or higher-order collision determinants with large monodromy.

A reflection symmetry or scalar square factorization alone is insufficient.

### (3) Does the unit-root subcrystal give a usable analytic zero counter?

Not by itself. It gives an explicit p-adic analytic formula on the ordinary locus and breaks down exactly on H_p=0. To turn it into a root bound one would need new uniform control of the full Frobenius matrix across the nonordinary locus—such as a bounded Weierstrass degree, a fixed low-genus parameter cover, or a second Frobenius minor with transverse vanishing.

## Final assessment

The direct Apéry/Hasse-polynomial route is not a dead end. It is the route with the most explicit arithmetic structure. But the next step is not another generic Dwork congruence: it is a new theorem about the distribution of the nonordinary divisor over \mathbf F_p.
