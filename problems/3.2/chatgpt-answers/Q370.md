# The cross-prime bridge after a square-root Apéry zero bound
## Executive verdict
Let
$$
\mathcal P_X=\{p\text{ prime}:X<p\le2X\},
\qquad
Y=\lfloor X^2\rfloor,
$$
$$
X_p(m)=\mathbf1_{\{m\bmod p\in Z_p\}},
\qquad
K_X(m)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}X_p(m),
$$
and
$$
\lambda_X=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}.
$$
There are four conclusions.
1. The proposed total centered variance
$$
\sum_{m<Y}(K_X(m)-\lambda_X)^2\ll X^{2-\eta}
\tag{V$_\eta$}
$$
is **not the right Poisson-scale target**. If the empirical first moment is correct, so that $`\lambda_X\asymp1/\log X`$, then every integer-valued random variable with this mean has variance at least $`\lambda_X(1-\lambda_X)`$; hence the left side is necessarily
$$
\gg \frac{X^2}{\log X}.
$$
Thus (V\$_eta\$) is false for every fixed $`\eta>0`$ in the expected regime. Even perfect two-prime independence leaves a natural diagonal variance of order $`X^2/\log X`$.
1. Consequently, a natural-scale two-prime theorem does **not** bridge to the all-$`n`$ statement by $`L^2\to L^\infty`$. It gives at best
$$
K_X(m)\ll \frac{X}{\sqrt{\log X}},
$$
whereas the weighted dyadic contribution requires
$$
K_X(m)=o\!\left(\frac X{\log X}\right)
$$
uniformly. The variance route would need an unnatural cancellation of its own diagonal, not merely pair independence.
1. After a same-prime bound
$$
|Z_p|\le p^{1/2+o(1)},
\tag{SQ}
$$
the honest weakest robust fixed-order bridge is the fifth factorial moment
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{m<Y}(K_X(m))_5
\ll
Y X^{o(1)}
\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\right)^5.
}
\tag{HM5}
$$
It gives
$$
\max_{m<Y}K_X(m)\ll X^{9/10+o(1)},
$$
and therefore the weighted large-prime contribution is $`o(n)`$ after dyadic summation.
1. I do not prove (HM5), even in the quotient-one slice or after imposing
$$
\gcd(p-1,n-1)=1.
$$
There is a rigorous adversarial obstruction showing that the ordinary large sieve cannot do so: bounded singleton/doubleton sets can be aligned at one integer for $`\asymp X/\log X`$ primes, **with quotient **$`1`$**, primitive selected character, reflection symmetry, and no consecutive residues**. Hence any proof must use genuinely Apéry-specific cross-prime arithmetic.
The exact missing arithmetic object has two equivalent forms:
- a five-prime **small-CRT representative count** for the residual Mellin-zero sets;
- a five-linear form in the additive Fourier transforms
$$
U_p(h)=\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(-hz),
$$
where $`U_p(h)`$ is itself the Fourier transform of the zero locus of the residual Apéry Mellin trace.
This is a new **cross-characteristic residual Mellin dispersion theorem**. Neither vertical $`SU(2)`$ Sato–Tate nor the classical large sieve controls it.

# 1. Exact two-prime variance identity
For complete precision, center by the actual interval means. Put
$$
M_p(Y)=\sum_{m<Y}X_p(m),
\qquad
\mu_p(Y)=\frac{M_p(Y)}Y,
\qquad
\Lambda_Y=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\mu_p(Y).
$$
For distinct primes $`p,q`$, let
$$
C_{p,q}(Y)
=
\#\{m<Y:m\bmod p\in Z_p,\ m\bmod q\in Z_q\}.
$$
Expanding the square gives the exact identity
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
V_2(X;Y)
&:=\sum_{m<Y}(K_X(m)-\Lambda_Y)^2\\
&=D_X(Y)+2\Delta_2(X;Y),
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{1.1}
$$
where
$$
D_X(Y)
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
M_p(Y)\left(1-\frac{M_p(Y)}Y\right),
\tag{1.2}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
\Delta_2(X;Y)
=
\sum_{\substack{p<q\\p,q\in\mathcal P_X}}
\left(
C_{p,q}(Y)-\frac{M_p(Y)M_q(Y)}Y
\right).
}
\tag{1.3}
$$
Since
$$
M_p(Y)=\frac{Y|Z_p|}{p}+O(|Z_p|),
\tag{1.4}
$$
the diagonal has the natural scale
$$
D_X(Y)\asymp Y\lambda_X
$$
when $`|Z_p|/p=o(1)`$ and the first moment is nonzero.
## 1.1 The pair term is a small-CRT discrepancy
At the critical length $`Y=X^2`$, every pair $`p,q\in\mathcal P_X`$ satisfies
$$
pq>Y.
$$
For each $`(r,s)\in Z_p\times Z_q`$, let
$$
[r,s]_{p,q}\in[0,pq)
$$
be its unique Chinese-remainder representative. Then
$$
\boxed{
C_{p,q}(Y)
=
\#\{(r,s)\in Z_p\times Z_q:[r,s]_{p,q}<Y\}.
}
\tag{1.5}
$$
Thus (1.3) is exactly the aggregate discrepancy of the least CRT representatives from uniformity. This, rather than a same-prime collision count, is the arithmetic content of a two-prime theorem.

# 2. Why the proposed power-saving variance is mis-scaled
Let
$$
\overline K_X=\frac1Y\sum_{m<Y}K_X(m)=\Lambda_Y.
$$
Because $`K_X(m)`$ is a nonnegative integer,
$$
K_X(m)^2\ge K_X(m).
$$
If $`0\le\Lambda_Y\le1`$, then
$$
\boxed{
\frac1YV_2(X;Y)
=
\frac1Y\sum_mK_X(m)^2-\Lambda_Y^2
\ge
\Lambda_Y(1-\Lambda_Y).
}
\tag{2.1}
$$
Under the empirical bounded first moment,
$$
\Lambda_Y\asymp\lambda_X\asymp\frac1{\log X},
$$
so
$$
\boxed{
V_2(X;X^2)\gg\frac{X^2}{\log X}.
}
\tag{2.2}
$$
For every fixed $`\eta>0`$,
$$
\frac{X^2}{\log X}>X^{2-\eta}
$$
for all sufficiently large $`X`$. Hence the variance target stated in the question is incompatible with the expected Poisson law.
## 2.1 Pair independence still does not prove the pointwise theorem
Suppose one proves the strongest natural pair statement
$$
\Delta_2(X;X^2)=o(D_X(X^2)).
\tag{2.3}
$$
Then, in the bounded-mean regime,
$$
V_2(X;X^2)\asymp\frac{X^2}{\log X}.
$$
The deterministic inequality
$$
\max_{m<Y}|K_X(m)-\Lambda_Y|\le\sqrt{V_2(X;Y)}
$$
gives only
$$
K_X(m)\ll\frac X{\sqrt{\log X}}.
\tag{2.4}
$$
After multiplication by $`\log X`$, this is $`X\sqrt{\log X}`$, not $`o(X)`$. Thus a natural two-prime variance theorem is valuable distributionally but **cannot be the all-**$`n`$** bridge**.
A second moment can bridge only if one proves an unnaturally small total variance—small enough to cancel most of the Bernoulli diagonal—or adds a separate high-tail theorem. The latter is exactly what factorial moments provide.

# 3. What the ordinary large sieve proves
Define
$$
U_p(h)=\sum_{z\in Z_p}e_p(-hz),
\qquad h\in\mathbf F_p.
$$
Fourier inversion gives
$$
\boxed{
X_p(m)
=
\frac{|Z_p|}{p}
+
\frac1p\sum_{1\le h<p}U_p(h)e_p(hm).
}
\tag{3.1}
$$
Parseval gives
$$
\sum_{h\bmod p}|U_p(h)|^2=p|Z_p|.
\tag{3.2}
$$
Equivalently, for the centered coefficients $`c_{p,h}=U_p(h)/p`$,
$$
\sum_{1\le h<p}|c_{p,h}|^2
=
\frac{|Z_p|}{p}-\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p^2}.
\tag{3.3}
$$
The reduced fractions $`h/p`$, with $`p\in(X,2X]`$ and $`1\le h<p`$, are separated modulo one by at least $`1/(4X^2)`$. Montgomery–Vaughan's large sieve therefore gives
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{m=M+1}^{M+Y}
\left|
K_X(m)-\lambda_X
\right|^2
\le
(Y+4X^2)
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}
\left(
\frac{|Z_p|}{p}-\frac{|Z_p|^2}{p^2}
\right).
}
\tag{3.4}
$$
At $`Y=X^2`$,
$$
V_2\ll X^2\lambda_X.
\tag{3.5}
$$
Under the hypothetical square-root bound (SQ),
$$
\lambda_X
\ll
\frac{X^{1/2+o(1)}}{\log X},
$$
so the black-box result is only
$$
V_2\ll\frac{X^{5/2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
\tag{3.6}
$$
This gives no useful pointwise power saving.
The large sieve is essentially sharp for arbitrary residue selectors. Higher-moment large-sieve variants require additive-energy or phase-correlation input; that input is precisely what is missing here. See:
- H. L. Montgomery and R. C. Vaughan, [*The large sieve*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300004708), *Mathematika* **20** (1973), 119–134.
- R. C. Baker, M. Munsch and I. E. Shparlinski, [*Additive energy and a large sieve inequality for sparse sequences*](https://doi.org/10.1112/mtk.12140), *Mathematika* **68** (2022), 362–399.
The latter improves large-sieve bounds when the relevant sparse frequency/modulus sets have controlled additive energy. It does not create cancellation for an adversarial modulus-dependent phase vector $`U_p(h)`$.

# 4. A rigorous obstruction, even in the quotient-one primitive-character slice
The following construction shows exactly what no ordinary large-sieve argument can exclude.
Fix a small constant $`\varepsilon>0`$. For every sufficiently large $`X`$, choose a prime
$$
\ell\in\left(\frac32X,\frac74X\right)
$$
and put
$$
n_0=\ell+1.
$$
For every prime
$$
X<p\le\ell,
$$
put
$$
r_p=n_0-p.
$$
Then
$$
0\le r_p<p,
\qquad
\left\lfloor\frac{n_0}{p}\right\rfloor=1,
\qquad
n_0\bmod p=r_p.
\tag{4.1}
$$
Moreover,
$$
\gcd(p-1,n_0-1)
=
\gcd(p-1,\ell)=1,
\tag{4.2}
$$
because $`\ell`$ is prime and $`0<p-1<\ell`$.
Define the adversarial sets
$$
Z_p^{\rm adv}=\{r_p\}
$$
or, if reflection symmetry is required,
$$
Z_p^{\rm adv}
=
\{r_p,p-1-r_p\}
$$
with duplicates removed. The two distinct entries are never consecutive, because their difference
$$
p-1-2r_p
$$
is even.
Thus these sets have all of the following properties:
- $`|Z_p^{\rm adv}|\le2`$;
- reflection invariance;
- no consecutive residues;
- every selected prime lies in the quotient-one slice;
- the selected character $`\omega_p^{n_0-1}`$ has full order $`p-1`$ by (4.2);
- every selected prime hits the same integer $`n_0`$.
Consequently
$$
K_X^{\rm adv}(n_0)
\ge
\pi(\ell)-\pi(X)
\asymp\frac X{\log X}.
\tag{4.3}
$$
This has two rigorous consequences.
## 4.1 No generic power-saving variance
For any center of size $`O(1)`$,
$$
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X^{\rm adv}(m)-\lambda)^2
\ge
(K_X^{\rm adv}(n_0)-\lambda)^2
\gg\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^2}.
\tag{4.4}
$$
The right side exceeds $`X^{2-\eta}`$ for every fixed $`\eta>0`$ and all large $`X`$.
## 4.2 No generic HM5
Here
$$
\lambda_X^{\rm adv}\ll\frac1{\log X},
$$
but
$$
\sum_{m<X^2}(K_X^{\rm adv}(m))_5
\ge
(K_X^{\rm adv}(n_0))_5
\asymp\frac{X^5}{(\log X)^5},
\tag{4.5}
$$
whereas the HM5 main scale is only
$$
X^2(\lambda_X^{\rm adv})^5
\ll\frac{X^2}{(\log X)^5}.
\tag{4.6}
$$
The aligned family exceeds it by a factor $`\asymp X^3`$.
This does **not** prove that the actual Apéry sets are aligned. It proves that none of the following, alone or in combination, can establish the required theorem:
- cardinality bounds;
- reflection;
- no consecutive zeros;
- quotient-one restriction;
- primitiveness of the selected Kummer character;
- ordinary large-sieve orthogonality.
The proof must use an arithmetic relation between the actual Apéry zero selectors for different characteristics.

# 5. Exact fifth-moment/CRT formulation
For ordered distinct primes
$$
\mathbf p=(p_1,\ldots,p_5)\in\mathcal P_X^5,
$$
put
$$
P_{\mathbf p}=p_1p_2p_3p_4p_5.
$$
For
$$
\mathbf r=(r_1,\ldots,r_5)
\in Z_{p_1}\times\cdots\times Z_{p_5},
$$
let
$$
[\mathbf r]_{\mathbf p}\in[0,P_{\mathbf p})
$$
be the unique CRT representative. Since
$$
P_{\mathbf p}>X^5\gg Y,
$$
each residue tuple has at most one representative below $`Y`$. Expanding the falling factorial gives the exact identity
$$
\boxed{
\sum_{m<Y}(K_X(m))_5
=
\sum_{\substack{p_1,\ldots,p_5\in\mathcal P_X\\\mathrm{distinct}}}
\#\left\{
\mathbf r\in\prod_{i=1}^5Z_{p_i}:
[\mathbf r]_{\mathbf p}<Y
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.1}
$$
The uniform-distribution prediction is
$$
\frac{Y}{P_{\mathbf p}}\prod_{i=1}^5|Z_{p_i}|.
$$
Thus HM5 is exactly the statement
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{\substack{p_1,\ldots,p_5\in\mathcal P_X\\\mathrm{distinct}}}
\#\left\{
\mathbf r\in\prod_iZ_{p_i}:
[\mathbf r]_{\mathbf p}<X^2
\right\}\\
&\hspace{35mm}\ll
X^{2+o(1)}
\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_X}\frac{|Z_p|}{p}\right)^5.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{5.2}
$$
This is the cleanest combinatorial statement a proof must establish.
## 5.1 Why order five is the first robust order
Let
$$
M_X=\max_{m<Y}K_X(m).
$$
If $`M_X\ge10`$, then
$$
(M_X)_5\ge(M_X/2)^5.
$$
Under HM5,
$$
M_X
\ll
Y^{1/5}\lambda_X X^{o(1)}.
\tag{5.3}
$$
Under (SQ),
$$
\lambda_X
\ll
\sum_{X<p\le2X}p^{-1/2+o(1)}
\ll
\frac{X^{1/2+o(1)}}{\log X}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
M_X\ll\frac{X^{9/10+o(1)}}{\log X}.
}
\tag{5.4}
$$
The corresponding logarithmic prime weight is $`X^{9/10+o(1)}`$. Summing dyadically over $`X\in(\sqrt n,n]`$ is dominated by the largest block and gives $`o(n)`$.
For a natural \$k\$th factorial moment, the same argument yields
$$
M_X\ll X^{2/k+1/2+o(1)}.
$$
A strict power saving requires
$$
\frac2k+\frac12<1,
$$
so the first integer is $`k=5`$.

# 6. Fourier form of the exact missing theorem
Let
$$
G_Y(\alpha)=\sum_{0\le m<Y}e(m\alpha).
$$
Applying (3.1) for five distinct primes gives
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathbf p}(Y)
={}&\frac1{P_{\mathbf p}}
\sum_{h_i\bmod p_i}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^5U_{p_i}(h_i)\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times
G_Y\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^5\frac{h_i}{p_i}\right),
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.1}
$$
where $`C_{\mathbf p}(Y)`$ is the count for the prime tuple in (5.1).
The term $`h_1=\cdots=h_5=0`$ is exactly
$$
\frac{Y}{P_{\mathbf p}}\prod_i|Z_{p_i}|.
$$
Therefore the analytic heart of HM5 is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_5(X)
:={}&
\sum_{\substack{p_1,\ldots,p_5\in\mathcal P_X\\\mathrm{distinct}}}
\frac1{P_{\mathbf p}}
\sum_{\mathbf h\ne\mathbf0}
\left(\prod_iU_{p_i}(h_i)\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times
G_{X^2}\!\left(\sum_i\frac{h_i}{p_i}\right)\\
\ll{}&
X^{2+o(1)}\lambda_X^5.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.2}
$$
Parseval (3.2) controls each $`U_p`$ separately in $`L^2`$. It says nothing about the phases of products
$$
U_{p_1}(h_1)\cdots U_{p_5}(h_5)
$$
near the resonant rational hyperplanes
$$
\left\|\sum_i\frac{h_i}{p_i}\right\|\lesssim X^{-2}.
$$
The aligned construction in Section 4 makes those phases coherent. This is the exact reason an ordinary large sieve cannot prove (6.2).
Recent bilinear trace-function theorems, such as Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Sawin, apply after the coefficients are realized as trace functions of a fixed bounded-conductor sheaf inside one finite field. They do not produce such a realization for the residual zero transforms $`U_p(h)`$ while the characteristic itself varies. See É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel and W. Sawin, [*Bilinear forms with trace functions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09459).

# 7. Insert the Apéry Hasse/Mellin structure
Let
$$
A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r\in\mathbf F_p[t]
$$
be the Apéry Hasse polynomial. For $`1\le r\le p-2`$, multiplicative orthogonality gives
$$
\boxed{
-b_r
=
\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-r}
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.
}
\tag{7.1}
$$
The endpoint coefficients $`b_0`$ and $`b_{p-1}`$ are both nonzero, so they cause no ambiguity for $`Z_p`$.
Let
$$
\mathscr M_p(\chi)
$$
be the integral Frobenius Mellin trace supplied by the primitive Apéry K3 Mellin object. At the Teichmüller prime $`\mathfrak p_p`$,
$$
\mathscr M_p(\omega_p^{-r})
\equiv-b_r\pmod{\mathfrak p_p}.
\tag{7.2}
$$
Hence
$$
Z_p
=
\left\{
1\le r\le p-2:
\mathscr M_p(\omega_p^{-r})\in\mathfrak p_p
\right\}.
\tag{7.3}
$$
The Fourier coefficient in (6.2) is therefore
$$
\boxed{
U_p(h)
=
\sum_{r=1}^{p-2}
 e_p(-hr)
\mathbf1_{\{\mathscr M_p(\omega_p^{-r})\in\mathfrak p_p\}}.
}
\tag{7.4}
$$
Using additive orthogonality in the residue field,
$$
\boxed{
U_p(h)
=
\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
\sum_{r=1}^{p-2}
 e_p\bigl(-hr+u b_r\bigr).
}
\tag{7.5}
$$
Substituting (7.1),
$$
U_p(h)
=
\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
\sum_{r=1}^{p-2}
 e_p\left(
-hr-u\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-r}
\right).
\tag{7.6}
$$
This formula identifies the exact new object:
> One must prove a five-modulus multilinear estimate for the additive Fourier transforms of the **same-characteristic residual zero locus of a finite-field Mellin transform**.
Katz's vertical theorem controls the complex distribution of
$$
\frac{\mathscr M_p(\chi)}{p^{3/2}}
$$
as $`\chi`$ varies. It does not control the discontinuous predicate
$$
\mathscr M_p(\chi)\in\mathfrak p_p,
$$
then Fourier transform that predicate in the exponent, and finally correlate five such transforms over five different residue characteristics.
This is one full nonlinear Fourier layer beyond vertical Sato–Tate.

# 8. The quotient-one and primitive-character restrictions
For a fixed integer $`n`$, write
$$
n=qp+r,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
$$
Because character exponents are taken modulo $`p-1`$,
$$
r=n-qp\equiv n-q\pmod{p-1}.
$$
Thus on the quotient-$`q`$ block the selected Mellin character is
$$
\boxed{
\chi_{p,n}=\omega_p^{-(n-q)}.
}
\tag{8.1}
$$
## 8.1 Exact quotient-one reformulation
For $`q=1`$,
$$
\chi_{p,n}=\omega_p^{-(n-1)}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
K_{X,1}(n)
=
\#\left\{
0\le r<\frac n2:
\begin{array}{l}
p=n-r\in(X,2X]\text{ is prime},\\
p\mid b_r
\end{array}
\right\}.
}
\tag{8.2}
$$
This is a shifted prime-divisor problem, not a standard trace-function sum.
The only elementary exclusion is terminal. The crude estimate
$$
b_r\le(r+1)64^r
$$
shows that, for
$$
r\le\frac{\log X-3\log\log X}{\log64},
$$
one has $`b_r<X`$ for large $`X`$. Hence no prime $`p>X`$ divides $`b_r`$. This removes only an $`O(\log X)`$-length interval next to $`p=n`$ and gives no power saving in (8.2).
## 8.2 Why the primitive-character condition does not help automatically
The condition
$$
\gcd(p-1,n-1)=1
$$
says that $`\omega_p^{n-1}`$ is a generator of the character group. It does not put the twists into one fixed compatible system.
There is also no useful norm amplification. The natural coefficient field contains $`\mathbf Q(\mu_{p-1})`$, and
$$
p\equiv1\pmod{p-1}.
$$
Therefore $`p`$ splits completely in $`\mathbf Q(\mu_{p-1})`$. Divisibility of one Mellin trace at the selected Teichmüller prime contributes only one factor of $`p`$ to its global norm; it does not force all Galois conjugates to vanish at the same prime. The archimedean norm bound is vastly too large to contradict this.
Thus the quotient-one and primitive-character restrictions do not remove the horizontal obstruction.

# 9. The exact theorem a successful proof must supply
The cleanest paper-ready target is the following.
> **Apéry five-prime residual Mellin dispersion.** Uniformly for large $`X`$,
>
> $``$
> sum_\{substack\{p_1,ldots,p_5in(X,2X\]\\mathrm\{distinct\}\}\}
> #left\{
> begin\{array\}\{c\}
> r_iinmathbf F_\{p_i\},quad
> mathscr M_\{p_i\}(omega_\{p_i\}\^\{-r_i\})inmathfrak p_\{p_i\},\\
> \[r_1,ldots,r_5\]_\{p_1,ldots,p_5\}\<X\^2
> end\{array\}
> right\}
> ll
> X\^\{2+o(1)\}
> left(sum_\{X\<ple2X\}frac\{\|Z_p\|\}\{p\}right)\^5.
> $``$
Equivalently, prove the Fourier estimate (6.2) with $`U_p`$ given by (7.4).
This statement is:
- stronger than five separate vertical Sato–Tate theorems;
- stronger than bounded same-prime zero moments;
- not implied by ordinary large sieve;
- not implied by quotient-one or primitive-character restrictions;
- exactly sufficient, together with (SQ), for the all-$`n`$ large-prime theorem.
A plausible proof would need a new ingredient of one of the following forms:
1. a bounded-complexity horizontal realization of the residual zero transform $`U_p(h)`$;
2. a reciprocity formula turning (7.4) into a bilinear/multilinear sum with genuine long variables;
3. a direct theorem that the least CRT representatives of five residual Mellin-zero characters are equidistributed below $`X^2`$;
4. a high-order spectral/large-sieve inequality specialized to the actual Apéry coefficient vectors, with an input that excludes the aligned construction of Section 4.
At present none of these inputs is available.
