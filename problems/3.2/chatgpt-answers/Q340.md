# 7. Why known expansion and sum-product theorems do not close the gap

The relevant classical theorems are powerful but have different inputs.

- Helfgott proves product growth for a set (Asubsetmathrm{SL}_2(mathbf F_p)) that generates the group; see H. Helfgott, Growth and generation in (mathrm{SL}_2(mathbf Z/pmathbf Z)), Annals of Mathematics 167 (2008), 601–623.

- Bourgain–Gamburd prove expansion for random walks on Cayley graphs from a fixed non-elementary generating set; see J. Bourgain and A. Gamburd, Uniform expansion bounds for Cayley graphs of (mathrm{SL}_2(mathbf F_p)), Annals of Mathematics 167 (2008), 625–642.

- Bourgain–Katz–Tao prove growth of (A+A) or (Acdot A) for a set (Asubsetmathbf F_p) of intermediate size; see A sum-product estimate in finite fields, and applications, GAFA 14 (2004), 27–57.

None applies to

[

B(x+h-1)cdots B(x+1)

]

merely because the union ({B(t):tinmathbf F_p^times}) generates a large group.

The word is non-autonomous but completely prescribed. At step (j), the probability measure is the point mass

[

delta_{B(x+j)}.

]

A point mass has no flattening or entropy. Replacing it by the uniform measure on all (B(t)) would create a different random-walk problem.

The variation in (x) also does not create independent step choices: all parameters are translates of the same single (x). The exact sliding identity is

[

boxed{

M_h(x+1)=B(x+h),M_h(x),B(x+1)^{-1}.

}

tag{7.1}

]

Thus neighboring starting points are linked by deterministic endpoint multiplication, not by independent convolution.

The Borel-coset identity (4.1) strengthens this objection: the one-step family has only affine relative motion. Zariski density of the group generated by all steps would say nothing about the distribution of this particular sliding word.

# 9. What the new normal form suggests testing next

The exact identities narrow the next computational/algebraic tests.

## 9.1 Two-step blocks, not one-step generation

Define

[

C(t)=B(t+1)B(t).

]

Because each (B(t)=b(t)w) with (b(t)) upper triangular, (C(t)) is an upper-times-lower Gauss product. Test the Bruhat coordinates of (C(t)) and of products of separated two-step blocks for:

- low-degree common factors;

- exceptional invariant curves;

- collision energy of their projective images;

- extra simplification under (tmapsto-1-t).

This is more informative than testing whether the set of all one-step matrices generates (mathrm{SL}_2).

## 9.2 Use the exact relative affine coordinate

The only nontrivial coordinate in (4.2) is

[

q(t,s)=P(t)(s/t)^3-P(s)(t/s)^3.

tag{9.1}

]

Any sum-product opening must enter through additive/multiplicative non-concentration of this explicit coordinate after block composition. A theorem about a generic matrix family is unnecessary; one can test the exact rational functions generated by (q(x+i,x+j)).

## 9.3 Search for a non-diagonal bounded-degree gauge—but with a precise certificate

The diagonal route is closed. A remaining gauge search should ask for

[

G(t+1)^{-1}B(t)G(t)=C(t)

]

with both (G) and (C) of degree bounded independently of (p), and with (C(t)) regular and invertible on (mathbf F_p^times). The result above gives immediate rejection tests:

- any proposed gauge that merely makes the off-diagonal constant must solve the impossible divisor equation;

- any projective solution using the (t^6) coboundary is necessarily singular on the entire cycle;

- a successful non-diagonal gauge must genuinely mix the two flag directions rather than hide the same scalar cocycle.

# Bottom line

The exact finite-field dynamics are

[

boxed{

B(t)=u(P(t))a(t^3)w,

qquad

phi_t(z)=P(t)-t^6/z,

qquad

B(t)B(s)^{-1}in B_{mathrm{up}}.

}

]

This is a materially simpler cocycle: fixed inversion, affine outer map, and a one-Borel relative family. It also explains why group generation does not imply prefix mixing.

The natural rational flattening fails in the strongest useful sense:

- no rational diagonal (mathrm{SL}_2) gauge exists;

- the projective flattening has unavoidable degree (Theta(p)) and is singular at every finite-field parameter.

Accordingly, no existing Helfgott/Bourgain–Gamburd/Bourgain–Katz–Tao theorem yields the critical-scale aggregate bound. The next serious route is not “prove Zariski density,” but exploit the explicit alternating affine–inversion word—most plausibly through two-step block correlations or a genuinely non-diagonal bounded-degree gauge.
