# Executive verdict

There are sufficient conditions that avoid the cross-prime collision second moment formally. The cleanest is a one-dimensional moving-target gcd estimate along the bounded quotient slices

[

n=qp+r.

]

However, none is presently available for the Apéry sequence. The three proposed broad theories do not close the gap:

- Anatomy of the single integer (b_n): logically sufficient, but in the hard top-prime range it is essentially equivalent to the original zero condition by Lucas congruence. Known Apéry/holonomic prime-factor theorems are lower bounds, density-one statements, or aggregate statements—not the needed pointwise upper bound on the medium-prime radical.

- General G-function/holonomic radical theory: no such theorem can hold at the required strength. The central binomial coefficients, coefficients of the algebraic G-function ((1-4z)^{-1/2}), already have (asymp n/log n) prime divisors in ((sqrt n,n]).

- Reciprocity swapping (r) and (p): no known law does this. The modular-form congruence controls the single central index (r=(p-1)/2), not the full two-variable relation (pmid b_r).

The genuinely different sufficient conditions worth recording are:

1. a subexponential medium-prime radical for (b_n);

1. the moving-target gcd sum stated below;

1. a subexponential-height integer certificate divisible by every bad prime;

1. an essentially optimal irrationality-measure estimate for the specific Apéry approximants.

Of these, (2) is the best new reformulation, but it is an open P-recursive moving-target theorem, not something supplied by Corvaja–Zannier, Bugeaud, Sanna, or Bell–Bruin–Coons.

# 1. The simplest collision-free sufficient condition

Let

[

mathcal B(n)={p:sqrt n<ple n, b_{nbmod p}equiv0pmod p}

]

and

[

W(n)=sum_{pinmathcal B(n)}log p.

]

The project’s Wronskian estimate bounds (v_p(G_n)) by an absolute constant for these primes, so (W(n)=o(n)) is enough.

For (p>sqrt n), write

[

n=qp+r,qquad 0le r<p,qquad q<p.

]

Gessel’s Lucas congruence gives

[

b_nequiv b_qb_rpmod p.

]

Therefore

[

b_requiv0pmod pquadLongrightarrowquad pmid b_n.

]

Define the middle-prime radical

[

R_{mathrm{mid}}(n)

:=prod_{substack{sqrt n<ple n\pmid b_n}}p.

]

Then

[

W(n)le log R_{mathrm{mid}}(n).

]

Hence the following is a completely collision-free sufficient condition:

[

boxed{log R_{mathrm{mid}}(n)=o(n).}

tag{MR}

]

Equivalently, the stronger count condition

[

omega_{(sqrt n,n]}(b_n)=o(n/log n)

]

would suffice.

## Why this does not actually evade the hard top range

Fix (eta>0). For (p>eta n), the quotient (q=lfloor n/prfloor) belongs to the fixed finite set

[

1le qle eta^{-1}.

]

For sufficiently large (n), such a prime (p) is larger than every fixed integer (b_q) in this finite set, hence (pnmid b_q). Consequently, in the top range,

[

pmid b_n

iff pmid b_r,

qquad r=n-qp.

]

Thus the medium-prime anatomy of the single integer (b_n) is not an independent source of information where the pointwise obstruction lives. In the bounded-quotient range it is simply the original condition rewritten using Lucas.

The size bound alone gives only

[

#{sqrt n<ple n:pmid b_n}

le frac{log b_n}{frac12log n}

=left(2log(17+12sqrt2)+o(1)right)frac n{log n},

]

which is exactly the wrong scale: (O(n/log n)), not (o(n/log n)).

# 2. A genuinely different sufficient condition: moving-target gcd sums

This is the cleanest alternative to a collision moment.

For a fixed positive integer (Q), define

[

T_Q(n):=

sum_{q=1}^{Q}

sum_{substack{0le r<n/(q+1)\requiv npmod q}}

loggcd!left(b_r,frac{n-r}{q}right).

tag{2.1}

]

The congruence condition makes ((n-r)/q) an integer.

If (pinmathcal B(n)) and

[

p>frac{n}{Q+1},

]

then (q=lfloor n/prfloorle Q), and with (r=n-qp) one has

[

0le r<frac n{q+1},qquad requiv npmod q,

qquad frac{n-r}{q}=p.

]

Since (pmid b_r), the corresponding summand in (2.1) is at least (log p). Therefore

[

sum_{substack{pinmathcal B(n)\p>n/(Q+1)}}log p

le T_Q(n).

tag{2.2}

]

The remaining primes contribute at most

[

sum_{ple n/(Q+1)}log p

=vartheta!left(frac n{Q+1}right)

le left(1+o(1)right)frac n{Q+1}.

tag{2.3}

]

Hence:

Moving-target sufficient condition. If, for every fixed (Q),

[

T_Q(n)=o_Q(n),

tag{MTG}

]

then (W(n)=o(n)), and therefore the pointwise Apéry gcd conjecture follows.

Indeed, (2.2)–(2.3) give

[

limsup_{ntoinfty}frac{W(n)}nlefrac1{Q+1},

]

and then (Qtoinfty).

This condition does not mention (Z_p), collision energies, or pairs of primes. It asks for a uniform summatory bound on

[

gcd(b_r,L_{n,q}(r)),

qquad L_{n,q}(r)=frac{n-r}{q},

]

where the second argument is a moving linear target.

## Why existing moving-target gcd theorems do not apply

Corvaja–Zannier and their successors work with points in a fixed finite-rank multiplicative group, or with constant-coefficient algebraic recurrence sequences admitting an exact exponential-polynomial representation.

Relevant references are:

- Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier, An upper bound for the G.C.D. of (a^n-1) and (b^n-1), Math. Z. 243 (2003), 79–84.

- Grieve–Wang, Greatest common divisors with moving targets and consequences for linear recurrence sequences, Trans. AMS 373 (2020), DOI 10.1090/tran/8220, arXiv 1902.09109.

- Xiao, Greatest common divisors for polynomials in almost units and applications to linear recurrence sequences, arXiv 2110.01751, published in Math. Z. 306 (2024), article 61.

The Apéry sequence is P-recursive:

[

(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1},

]

not C-finite. It has no known representation

[

b_r=sum_i P_i(r)alpha_i^r

]

with finitely many fixed algebraic roots (alpha_i), and the points entering (MTG) are not fixed-(S) units. Moreover (MTG) is a sum over (asymp n) moving targets, stronger than an individual gcd estimate.

Thus (MTG) is a real alternative research target, but no cited Subspace-Theorem result currently proves it.

A useful equivalent research formulation is to seek, for every fixed (Q), an integer certificate (C_{n,Q}ne0) such that all matching prime factors in (2.1) divide (C_{n,Q}) and

[

log|C_{n,Q}|=o_Q(n).

]

That would compress the diagonal divisibility incidences into one subexponential-height object. No such certificate is presently known; the existing continuant certificates depend on the individual gap/base and do not compress to subexponential total height.

# 3. Candidate (a): anatomy of (b_n)

## What is actually known for Apéry numbers

Luca and Shparlinski prove that for a density-one set of indices,

[

omega(b_n)>c_0logloglog n,

qquad

P(b_n)>c_0(log nloglog n)^{1/2},

]

where (P(m)) is the largest prime factor. See Florian Luca and Igor Shparlinski, Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers, J. London Math. Soc. 78 (2008), 545–562, DOI 10.1112/jlms/jdn031.

These are:

- lower bounds, not upper bounds;

- density-one, not pointwise;

- far below the scale (pasymp n) relevant here.

Florian Luca’s Prime divisors of binary holonomic sequences, Adv. Appl. Math. 40 (2008), 168–179, DOI 10.1016/j.aam.2006.12.001, proves that the product of the numerators and denominators of the first (N) nonzero terms of a suitable second-order holonomic sequence has at least (clog N) distinct prime factors. Again this is an aggregate lower bound and gives no upper bound on medium prime factors of one term.

Sanna-style rank-of-apparition results concern Lucas and related divisibility sequences, where the group law and strong divisibility provide an index attached to each prime. The Apéry numbers have no known strong-divisibility law or rank-of-apparition structure, so those methods do not initialize.

## A decisive no-go for a general holonomic/G-function theorem

Take

[

u_n=binom{2n}{n}.

]

Its generating function is the algebraic G-function

[

sum_{nge0}u_nz^n=(1-4z)^{-1/2},

]

and (u_n) is hypergeometric, hence P-recursive.

For every prime

[

frac n2<plefrac{2n}{3}

]

and sufficiently large (n), one has (p^2>2n), so Legendre’s formula gives

[

v_pbinom{2n}{n}

=leftlfloorfrac{2n}{p}rightrfloor

=3-2=1.

]

Therefore

[

#left{sqrt n<ple n:pmidbinom{2n}{n}right}

ge pi(2n/3)-pi(n/2)

sim frac{n}{6log n}.

]

So even coefficients of an algebraic geometric G-function can have the maximal-order number of medium prime divisors relevant here.

Consequently, no theorem based only on being

- integral,

- P-recursive/holonomic,

- a G-function coefficient,

- a geometric period,

- or exponentially bounded

can imply the desired (o(n/log n)) anatomy. Any successful anatomy theorem must use special Apéry structure substantially stronger than the general G-function framework.

A very large greatest prime factor also does not suffice. To reduce the medium-prime count to little-(o), one needs an upper bound on the entire medium radical, not merely the existence of one very large prime divisor.

# 4. Candidate (b): radical, valuation, and G-function theorems

## G-function denominator theorems control the wrong side

G-function arithmetic gives strong information about common denominators of Taylor coefficients. For example, Fischler and Rivoal study the least common denominators (delta_n) of the first (n) coefficients in On the denominators of the Taylor coefficients of G-functions, arXiv 1606.00706.

But (b_ninmathbb Z), so denominator control is vacuous. These theorems do not bound the radical of the numerator or its distribution across a moving interval of primes.

## Fixed-(S) theorems do not cover a moving set of (asymp n/log n) primes

Bugeaud and Evertse prove that, under hypotheses on a nondegenerate integer constant-coefficient recurrence, the part supported on a fixed finite set (S) satisfies

[

[u_n]_Sle |u_n|^varepsilon

]

for all sufficiently large (n). See (S)-parts of terms of integer linear recurrence sequences, Mathematika 63 (2017), 840–851, DOI 10.1112/S0025579317000298, arXiv 1611.00485.

This does not apply because:

1. Apéry is P-recursive, not constant-coefficient;

1. the relevant set

1. (|S_n|asymp n/log n), so constants depending on (S) are useless.

## ABC points in the opposite direction

The (abc) conjecture and its unconditional substitutes are typically used to prove that values have a large radical, primitive prime divisors, or large prime factors. The present requirement is an upper bound on one selected portion of the radical:

[

prod_{sqrt n<ple n, pmid b_n}p=e^{o(n)}.

]

There is no general abc-free theorem of this form for holonomic or G-function coefficients, and the central-binomial example shows that no broad one can exist.

Even a uniform bound on (v_p(b_n)) would not solve the problem: the obstruction is the number of distinct primes, not their multiplicities.

## Bell–Bruin–Coons is not a prime-factor theorem for D-finite sequences

Bell, Bruin and Coons prove a classification for multiplicative coefficient functions whose generating series is algebraic or D-finite: such a series is rational or transcendental, with strong restrictions in the rational case. See Transcendence of generating functions whose coefficients are multiplicative, Trans. AMS 364 (2012), 933–959, DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-2011-05479-6, arXiv 1003.2221.

The function (nmapsto b_n) is not multiplicative. That paper gives no control of prime divisors of a general D-finite coefficient sequence.

### Verdict on (b)

No existing radical/valuation theorem for G-functions or holonomic sequences avoids the wall. The closest genuinely relevant theorem would be a new proof of (MTG), not a direct application of current G-function arithmetic.

# 5. Candidate (c): reciprocity swapping (r) and (p)

The known modular connection is highly diagonal. Ahlgren and Ono prove a supercongruence relating the central Apéry value

[

b_{(p-1)/2}

]

to the (p)-th Fourier coefficient of a weight-4 modular form. See Scott Ahlgren and Ken Ono, A Gaussian hypergeometric series evaluation and Apéry number congruences, J. Reine Angew. Math. 518 (2000), 187–212, DOI 10.1515/crll.2000.004.

Later generalized-Apéry results similarly treat special indices that are fixed rational multiples of (p-1). These are genuine modular reciprocity phenomena, but they control isolated diagonals such as

[

r=frac{p-1}{2},

]

not arbitrary (0le r<p).

For general (r), the condition

[

pmid b_r

]

already says that (p) is a prime divisor of the fixed integer (b_r). Holding (r) fixed makes the “vertical” problem finite and trivial. The difficulty is that the pointwise problem samples the moving diagonal

[

r=nbmod p.

]

A reciprocity law would have to preserve this diagonal while converting divisibility by (p) into a bounded-complexity condition on (p). No known formula does so.

The existing identities are all same-prime:

- Lucas digit factorization;

- reflection (rleftrightarrow p-1-r);

- finite-field hypergeometric/Mellin descriptions;

- central modular-form supercongruences.

None swaps the residue index and the residue characteristic.

A useful hypothetical target would be a two-variable integer or algebraic object (F(r,p)) of controlled degree/height with

[

pmid b_rquadLongrightarrowquad F(r,p)=0

]

or with all relevant primes dividing a single subexponential-height specialization (F_n). That would yield the compressed certificate described after (MTG). At present, the continuant certificate for two zero positions depends on the individual base and gap and does not produce such a global reciprocity object.

### Verdict on (c)

No known reciprocity law avoids horizontal equidistribution. The weight-4 modular relation is real but far too sparse in (r).

# 6. The only truly orthogonal known formulation

There is one logically independent route: improve the Diophantine approximation lower bound for the specific Apéry convergents.

Let

[

q_n=frac{b_n}{G_n}.

]

Since

[

left|zeta(3)-frac{a_n}{b_n}right|=expleft(-(2log(17+12sqrt2)+o(1))nright),

]

a lower bound of the sequence-specific form

[

left|zeta(3)-frac{a_n}{b_n}right|

ge q_n^{-2-o(1)}

]

would immediately imply

[

G_n=e^{o(n)}.

]

This avoids prime distribution completely. But it is essentially an optimal irrationality-exponent statement. The best published general upper bound remains Rhin–Viola’s

[

mu(zeta(3))le5.51389062ldots,

]

from The group structure for (zeta(3)), Acta Arith. 97 (2001), 269–293, DOI 10.4064/aa97-3-6. As already computed in the project, that yields only an exponential upper bound on (G_n), not (e^{o(n)}).

Thus this is genuinely different, but at least as deep as obtaining irrationality exponent (2) along the Apéry subsequence.

# 7. Focused conclusion

## What actually avoids the collision second moment?

The exact sufficient statement

[

boxed{

forall Qtext{ fixed},quad

sum_{qle Q}

sum_{substack{0le r<n/(q+1)\requiv npmod q}}

loggcd!left(b_r,frac{n-r}{q}right)

=o_Q(n)

}

]

avoids collision moments and proves the pointwise theorem.

It is the best alternative formulation I see. It recasts the problem as a summatory moving-target gcd theorem for a P-recursive sequence.

## Does any existing theory prove it?

No.

- Corvaja–Zannier/Grieve–Wang/Xiao require torus or constant-coefficient recurrence structure.

- Bugeaud–Evertse requires fixed (S) and a C-finite recurrence.

- Luca’s holonomic theorem and Luca–Shparlinski’s Apéry theorem give lower/aggregate/density-one prime-factor information.

- Sanna’s rank-of-apparition methods require divisibility-sequence structure.

- Bell–Bruin–Coons concerns multiplicative coefficient functions, not arbitrary D-finite sequences.

- General G-function arithmetic cannot suffice, as (binom{2n}{n}) supplies a direct counterexample.

- The known modular reciprocity controls only special diagonals in ((r,p)).

## Research recommendation

If the collision route is abandoned, the most coherent new target is not “prime anatomy of holonomic sequences” in general. It is:

Develop a Subspace-Theorem/Diophantine-divisibility analogue for second-order P-recursive sequences against a moving linear target, strong enough to prove (MTG).

A successful proof would be fundamentally different in technique. But absent such a new theorem—or an optimal irrationality-measure breakthrough—every currently known route returns to horizontal zero-set dispersion in one form or another.
