# Algebraic geometry of the Apéry projective-transfer codegree curve

## Executive result

Put

\[
P(U)=34U^3+51U^2+27U+5,
\qquad a(U)=(U+1)^6,
\]

and

\[
\mathscr C_{x,x'}(u,v)
=a(u)(P(v)-x')-a(v)(P(u)-x).
\tag{0.1}
\]

The key birational change of variables is

\[
w=\frac1{u+1},\qquad z=\frac1{v+1},
\qquad X=x+5,\quad X'=x'+5.
\]

Then, away from the two boundary lines \(u=-1,v=-1\), equation (0.1) is exactly

\[
\boxed{f_x(w)=f_{x'}(z)},
\qquad
f_x(W)=34W^3-51W^4+27W^5-(x+5)W^6.
\tag{0.2}
\]

This separated-polynomial form resolves the generic factorization problem.

### Main theorem proved below

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\), and also over every algebraically closed field of characteristic \(p>17\):

1. If \(x,x'\ne-5\) and \(x\ne x'\), then \(\mathscr C_{x,x'}\) is absolutely irreducible.
2. If \(x=x'\ne-5\), then
   \[
   \mathscr C_{x,x}=(u-v)R_x(u,v),
   \]
   where \(R_x\) is absolutely irreducible of bidegree \((5,5)\).
3. If \(x=-5\ne x'\), then \(u+1\) is a component and the complementary component is absolutely irreducible. The component \(u=-1\) contributes nothing to the codegree because \(u=-1\) is excluded. The statement with \(x'\) and \(v\) interchanged is identical.
4. If \(x=x'=-5\), then the projective curve has the four factors/components
   \[
   u=-1,\quad v=-1,\quad u=v,\quad\text{one absolutely irreducible residual component}.
   \]
   Only the last two meet the allowed codegree domain.
5. There is **no reflection-induced curve in the \((x,x')\)-plane**. Apéry reflection gives a time-reversing, \(u\)-dependent anti-conjugacy, not a fixed involution \(x'=\iota(x)\).
6. In \(\mathbf P^1_x\times\mathbf P^1_{x'}\), the parameter-infinity cases are exact:
   \[
   C_p(\infty,x')=C_p(x,\infty)=0\quad(x,x'\ne\infty),
   \qquad
   C_p(\infty,\infty)=(p-1)^2.
   \]

Consequently the exact positive-dimensional exceptional set is

\[
\boxed{
E=\{x=x'\}\cup\{x=-5\}\cup\{x'=-5\}
\cup\{x=\infty\}\cup\{x'=\infty\}.}
\tag{0.3}
\]

The diagonal is the only genuine interior symmetry locus. The two \(-5\) lines and the two infinity lines are degree-drop/endpoint artifacts.

For \(p>17\), \(x,x'\in\mathbf F_p\setminus\{-5\}\), and \(x\ne x'\), the affine curve is absolutely irreducible and

\[
\boxed{C_p(x,x')=p+O(\sqrt p).}
\tag{0.4}
\]

The normalization has genus at most \(7\), so one may take

\[
|C_p(x,x')-p|\le 14\sqrt p+O(1)
\tag{0.5}
\]

with an absolute boundary constant. On the generic diagonal,

\[
\boxed{C_p(x,x)=2p+O(\sqrt p),}
\tag{0.6}
\]

and the non-diagonal residual normalization has genus at most \(5\).

The only item not reduced to displayed hand algebra is the routine verification of the stated absolute-irreducibility criterion in small characteristics \(p\le17\); the project only needs large \(p\). All characteristic-zero claims below are proofs, not experimental factorization guesses.

# 1. Explicit polynomial, degrees and Newton polygon

Let

\[
(c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_6)=(1,6,15,20,15,6,1),
\]

and

\[
(p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3)=(5,27,51,34).
\]

Expanding \(P\), but retaining the antisymmetric structure, gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr C_{x,x'}(u,v)
={}&34\bigl((u+1)^6v^3-(v+1)^6u^3\bigr)\\
&+51\bigl((u+1)^6v^2-(v+1)^6u^2\bigr)\\
&+27\bigl((u+1)^6v-(v+1)^6u\bigr)\\
&+(5-x')(u+1)^6-(5-x)(v+1)^6.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently, the coefficient of \(u^iv^j\) is

\[
\boxed{
[u^iv^j]\mathscr C
=c_i p_j\mathbf 1_{j\le3}
-p_i c_j\mathbf 1_{i\le3}
+x c_j\mathbf 1_{i=0}
-x'c_i\mathbf 1_{j=0}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

This is a completely expanded coefficient table.

## 1.1 Degrees

For finite parameters:

\[
\deg_u\mathscr C=6,
\qquad
\deg_v\mathscr C=6,
\qquad
\deg_{\rm total}\mathscr C=9.
\]

The total-degree-nine part is

\[
34(u^6v^3-v^6u^3)=34u^3v^3(u^3-v^3).
\]

Thus the curve has bidegree \((6,6)\) in \(\mathbf P^1_u\times\mathbf P^1_v\).

## 1.2 Newton polygon

For generic \(x,x'\), the Newton polygon is the pentagon

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Conv}\{(0,0),(6,0),(6,3),(3,6),(0,6)\}.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Its area is \(63/2\), its lattice-boundary count is \(21\), and Pick's formula gives \(22\) interior lattice points.

The bidegree compactification has arithmetic genus

\[
(6-1)(6-1)=25.
\]

The difference \(25-22=3\) is exactly the ordinary triple point at \((u,v)=(\infty,\infty)\) which the toric compactification resolves.

## 1.3 Bihomogenization

Write \(u=U_1/U_0\), \(v=V_1/V_0\),

\[
A(U)=(U_1+U_0)^6,
\]

and

\[
P_h(U)=34U_1^3+51U_1^2U_0+27U_1U_0^2+5U_0^3.
\]

The bidegree-\((6,6)\) closure is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\overline{\mathscr C}_{x,x'}={}&
A(U)V_0^3\bigl(P_h(V)-x'V_0^3\bigr)\\
&-A(V)U_0^3\bigl(P_h(U)-xU_0^3\bigr).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Everything below can be checked directly from (1.4).

# 2. Boundary singularities

Put

\[
s=u+1,\qquad t=v+1,\qquad X=x+5,\qquad X'=x'+5.
\]

Since

\[
P(s-1)=34s^3-51s^2+27s-5,
\]

equation (0.1) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr C
=s^6(34t^3-51t^2+27t-X')
-t^6(34s^3-51s^2+27s-X).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

## 2.1 The point \((-1,-1)\)

If \(XX'\ne0\), the tangent cone is

\[
Xt^6-X's^6.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Over the algebraic closure this is a product of six distinct lines. Hence

\[
\boxed{(-1,-1)\text{ is an ordinary sixfold point},\quad\delta=15.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

There are no other points of the curve on \(u=-1\) or \(v=-1\), because

\[
\mathscr C(-1,v)=(x+5)(v+1)^6,
\qquad
\mathscr C(u,-1)=-(x'+5)(u+1)^6.
\]

If \(x=-5\), the whole line \(u=-1\) becomes a component; if \(x'=-5\), the whole line \(v=-1\) becomes a component. These are precisely the finite endpoint artifacts.

## 2.2 The point \((\infty,\infty)\)

Set

\[
w=1/s,\qquad z=1/t.
\]

Dividing (2.1) by \(s^6t^6\) gives

\[
f_x(w)=f_{x'}(z),
\]

where

\[
\boxed{f_x(W)=34W^3-51W^4+27W^5-(x+5)W^6.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

At \(w=z=0\), the tangent cone is

\[
34(w^3-z^3),
\]

a product of three distinct lines in characteristic different from \(3\). Thus

\[
\boxed{(\infty,\infty)\text{ is an ordinary triple point},\quad\delta=3.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

## 2.3 The remaining points at infinity

At \(u=\infty\), equation (1.4) gives either \(v=\infty\) or

\[
P(v)=x'.
\]

Similarly, at \(v=\infty\) one has \(P(u)=x\).

These points are smooth unless a root of \(P(T)-x\) or \(P(T)-x'\) is multiple. The cubic discriminant is proportional to

\[
68x^2+1,
\tag{2.6}
\]

and similarly for \(x'\). Even when (2.6) vanishes, this creates an additional singularity, not an additional irreducible component.

## 2.4 Generic genus

For \(x,x'\ne-5\), the fixed boundary singularities contribute

\[
15+3=18
\]

to the delta invariant. Therefore every absolutely irreducible member has normalization genus at most

\[
25-18=7.
\tag{2.7}
\]

If no additional interior critical-value collision occurs, the genus is exactly \(7\).

There is no distinguished component at \(u=0\) or \(v=0\). For example, \(u=0\) would be a component only if

\[
P(v)-x'-(5-x)(v+1)^6
\]

were the zero polynomial, which is impossible for every \(x,x'\).

# 3. The degree-six polynomial cover has monodromy \(S_6\)

This section is the main irreducibility input.

Fix an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic \(0\), or of characteristic \(p>17\), and let \(x\ne-5\). Put \(X=x+5\ne0\).

## 3.1 Indecomposability

The polynomial

\[
f_x(W)=W^3(34-51W+27W^2-XW^3)
\]

has degree six and a zero of multiplicity exactly three at \(W=0\).

A nontrivial polynomial decomposition has degree pattern \(6=3\cdot2\) or \(6=2\cdot3\).

### Inner degree two

Suppose \(f=A\circ B\), \(\deg A=3,\deg B=2\). The multiplicity three at \(0\) forces \(B\) to be unramified at \(0\) and \(A\) to have a triple root at \(B(0)\). Hence

\[
f(W)=c(B(W)-B(0))^3=cW^3(\alpha+\beta W)^3.
\]

Comparing the linear coefficient of the cubic factor gives

\[
3\beta/\alpha=-51/34=-3/2,
\qquad \beta/\alpha=-1/2.
\]

Then the predicted quadratic coefficient is

\[
34\cdot3(1/2)^2=51/2,
\]

whereas the actual coefficient is \(27\). This is a contradiction in characteristic different from \(2,3\).

### Inner degree three

Suppose \(\deg A=2,\deg B=3\). Multiplicity three at zero forces total ramification of \(B\) at zero, so

\[
B(W)-B(0)=cW^3.
\]

Then \(A(B(W))\) is a polynomial in \(W^3\), contradicting the nonzero \(W^4\)-coefficient \(-51\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{f_x\text{ is indecomposable for every }x\ne-5.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The geometric monodromy action is consequently primitive.

## 3.2 A transposition is always present

The derivative is

\[
\boxed{
f_x'(W)=3W^2R_x(W),
\qquad
R_x(W)=34-68W+45W^2-2(x+5)W^3.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The point \(W=0\) has ramification index three. The three nonzero critical points are the roots of \(R_x\), counted with multiplicity.

A triple root of \(R_x\) is impossible: comparison with \(c(W-a)^3\) gives inconsistent linear and quadratic coefficient ratios.

If the roots of \(R_x\) are distinct, then either one critical value occurs only once, giving a transposition directly, or all three critical values coincide. The latter is impossible. Indeed, if all three critical values were \(c\), then

\[
f_x(W)-c=\lambda R_x(W)^2.
\]

Leading coefficients force \(\lambda=-1/(4X)\), but the \(W\)-coefficient on the right is

\[
-\frac1{4X}\cdot2\cdot34\cdot(-68)=\frac{1156}{X}\ne0,
\]

whereas \(f_x(W)-c\) has zero \(W\)-coefficient. This excludes the only possible threefold collision for \(p>17\).

If \(R_x\) has one double root and one simple root, then either the simple critical value is separate, giving a transposition, or the two critical values coincide. In the latter case local inertia is a product of a disjoint \(3\)-cycle and transposition; its cube is the transposition.

Thus the primitive monodromy group contains a transposition. A primitive subgroup of \(S_6\) containing a transposition is \(S_6\). Hence

\[
\boxed{G_{\rm geom}(f_x(W)-Y)=S_6\quad\text{for every }x\ne-5.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is uniform in characteristic \(p>17\).

### One explicit specialization check

At \(x=1/2\), so \(X=11/2\),

\[
R_x(W)=-(W-1)(11W^2-34W+34),
\]

and \(f_x(1)=9/2\). Reduction of \(2f_x(W)-9\) modulo the irreducible quadratic \(11W^2-34W+34\) is a nonzero linear polynomial. Hence \(W=1\) is the unique critical point above \(9/2\), providing a literal simple branch value. This independently verifies that the generic monodromy is \(S_6\).

# 4. Generic and off-diagonal absolute irreducibility

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic \(0\) or \(p>17\). Let \(L_x\) and \(L_{x'}\) be the Galois closures over \(k(Y)\) of

\[
f_x(W)-Y,\qquad f_{x'}(Z)-Y.
\]

Assume \(x,x'\ne-5\). By (3.3), both Galois groups are \(S_6\).

The intersection \(L_x\cap L_{x'}\) gives a common quotient of \(S_6\). Since the only normal subgroups of \(S_6\) are

\[
1,\quad A_6,\quad S_6,
\]

the common quotient is one of

\[
1,\quad C_2,\quad S_6.
\]

## 4.1 Trivial common quotient

Then the compositum group is \(S_6\times S_6\), transitive on the \(36\) ordered pairs of sheets. The fiber product is connected and hence absolutely irreducible.

## 4.2 Common quotient \(C_2\)

The compositum group is the parity-matched subgroup

\[
\{(g,h)\in S_6\times S_6:\operatorname{sgn}g=\operatorname{sgn}h\}.
\]

This group is still transitive on \(\{1,\ldots,6\}^2\): for any prescribed images of the two coordinates, one may change the parity of either chosen permutation inside its point stabilizer \(S_5\), because \(S_5\) contains odd elements.

Therefore sharing the discriminant quadratic field does **not** make the codegree curve reducible. This is an important correction to a tempting but false discriminant-only criterion.

## 4.3 Common quotient \(S_6\)

Then \(L_x=L_{x'}\), and the compositum group is the graph of an automorphism of \(S_6\).

- If the automorphism is outer, the two point-stabilizer \(S_5\)'s lie in the two different conjugacy classes of \(S_5\subset S_6\). The outer \(S_5\) is transitive in the natural degree-six action; equivalently the two stabilizers have one double coset. The product action is transitive, so the fiber product remains irreducible.
- If the automorphism is inner, the two degree-six covers are isomorphic over the \(Y\)-line. Hence there is a Möbius transformation \(M\) with
  \[
  f_x(M(Z))=f_{x'}(Z).
  \]
  Both maps have a unique pole, so \(M\) is affine. Both have a unique ramification point of index three above \(Y=0\), namely \(0\), so \(M(Z)=aZ\). Comparing the \(Z^3\) and \(Z^4\) coefficients gives
  \[
  a^3=1,\qquad a^4=1,
  \]
  hence \(a=1\), and then comparison of the degree-six coefficient gives \(x=x'\).

We have proved

\[
\boxed{
x,x'\ne-5,\ x\ne x'\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathscr C_{x,x'}\text{ absolutely irreducible}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

No uncomputed resultant or random-specialization assumption is used here.

# 5. The diagonal factor and its residual

Set \(x'=x\), \(X=x+5\), \(s=u+1\), \(t=v+1\). Equation (2.1) is antisymmetric in \(s,t\), hence divisible by \(s-t=u-v\).

The exact quotient is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
R_x(u,v)={}&34s^3t^3(s^2+st+t^2)\\
&-51s^2t^2(s+t)(s^2+t^2)\\
&+27st(s^4+s^3t+s^2t^2+st^3+t^4)\\
&-X(s^5+s^4t+s^3t^2+s^2t^3+st^4+t^5).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\mathscr C_{x,x}=(u-v)R_x.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The residual has bidegree \((5,5)\) and total degree eight.

Since \(G_{\rm geom}=S_6\), the action is two-transitive. The components of

\[
f_x(w)=f_x(z)
\]

correspond to the orbits of \(S_6\) on ordered pairs of sheets: the diagonal and the off-diagonal orbit. Hence

\[
\boxed{R_x\text{ is absolutely irreducible for every }x\ne-5.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The residual has arithmetic genus \(16\). At \((-1,-1)\) it has an ordinary fivefold point, with \(\delta=10\); at \((\infty,\infty)\) it has an ordinary node, with \(\delta=1\). Therefore its normalization genus is at most

\[
16-10-1=5.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently the diagonal codegree is

\[
C_p(x,x)=2p+O(\sqrt p).
\]

# 6. The Apéry reflection does not create a parameter-plane component

The recurrence satisfies

\[
P(-1-u)=-P(u).
\]

For an actual Apéry orbit, reflection gives

\[
b_{p-1-n}=b_n.
\]

With

\[
x_n=n^3\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_n},
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{x_{p-n}=-\frac{n^6}{x_n}.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define the time-dependent involution

\[
\mathcal I_u(x)=-\frac{u^6}{x}.
\]

Then the one-step maps satisfy the exact **anti-conjugacy**

\[
\boxed{
F_{-u-1}\bigl(\mathcal I_{u+1}(F_u(x))\bigr)=\mathcal I_u(x).}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\mathcal I_{u+1}(F_u(x))=x-P(u),
\]

and

\[
F_{-u-1}(x-P(u))=
\frac{u^6}{-P(u)-x+P(u)}=-\frac{u^6}{x}.
\]

This reverses time and depends on \(u\). It is not a fixed projective involution on the state coordinate.

Directly setting \(v=-1-u\) in the codegree polynomial gives

\[
\mathscr C_{x,x'}(u,-1-u)
=-(u+1)^6(P(u)+x')-u^6(P(u)-x),
\tag{6.3}
\]

which is never the zero polynomial for fixed \(x,x'\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{there is no reflection graph }x'=\iota(x)
\text{ in the reducibility locus}.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is [PROVED], not a failure to identify the correct involution.

# 7. Endpoint and parameter-infinity degenerations

## 7.1 The line \(x=-5\)

If \(x=-5\), then

\[
P(u)+5=(u+1)\bigl(34(u+1)^2-51(u+1)+27\bigr).
\]

Equation (2.1) factors as

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr C_{-5,x'}
=(u+1)\Bigl[
(u+1)^5(34t^3-51t^2+27t-X')
-t^6(34s^2-51s+27)
\Bigr].}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The bracketed factor is absolutely irreducible in characteristic zero. In the \(w,z\) model it is the fiber product of a degree-five polynomial and a degree-six polynomial. The degree-five polynomial has monodromy \(S_5\), while the degree-six one has monodromy \(S_6\); their only possible common nontrivial quotient is \(C_2\), whose parity fiber product is transitive for the same stabilizer argument as in Section 4.

Since \(u=-1\) is excluded from the definition of \(C_p\), the boundary component contributes no codegree points. Hence, for generic \(x'\ne-5\),

\[
C_p(-5,x')=p+O(\sqrt p).
\]

## 7.2 The point \(x=x'=-5\)

Now both \(s\) and \(t\) factor:

\[
\mathscr C_{-5,-5}
=st\bigl[s^5(34t^2-51t+27)-t^5(34s^2-51s+27)\bigr].
\]

The bracket is divisible by \(s-t\), and its residual is absolutely irreducible by two-transitivity of \(S_5\). Thus the projective curve has four components:

\[
u=-1,\quad v=-1,\quad u=v,\quad\text{one residual component}.
\]

The codegree domain sees only the latter two, so

\[
C_p(-5,-5)=2p+O(\sqrt p).
\]

## 7.3 State parameter \(x=\infty\)

Use homogeneous state coordinates \([X_0:X_1]\), with \(x=X_1/X_0\). The projective output is

\[
F_u([X_0:X_1])=
\bigl[(u+1)^6X_0:P(u)X_0-X_1\bigr].
\]

Therefore \(F_u(\infty)=0\) for every allowed \(u\). It follows exactly that

\[
C_p(\infty,x')=0
\]

for finite \(x'\), because \(F_v(x')=0\) would force \(v=-1\), which is excluded. Similarly \(C_p(x,\infty)=0\). Finally

\[
C_p(\infty,\infty)=(p-1)^2.
\]

# 8. Singular/discriminant locus versus reducibility locus

The elimination proposed in the question—solving

\[
\mathscr C=\partial_u\mathscr C=\partial_v\mathscr C=0
\]

—computes the **singular-fiber locus**, not the reducibility locus. These must not be conflated.

In the \(w,z\) model, away from the fixed boundary singularities, an additional singularity occurs when

\[
f_x'(w)=0,\qquad f_{x'}'(z)=0,\qquad f_x(w)=f_{x'}(z).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus it records pairs of parameters having a common critical value. Such a member normally acquires an ordinary node and remains absolutely irreducible by Theorem (4.1).

For machine elimination define

\[
R_x(W)=34-68W+45W^2-2(x+5)W^3
\]

and, at a nonzero critical point,

\[
V(W)=\frac{W^3}{2}(9W^2-34W+34).
\tag{8.2}
\]

The critical-value polynomial is

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak B(x,Y)
=\operatorname{Res}_W
\left(R_x(W),\ 2Y-W^3(9W^2-34W+34)\right).}
\tag{8.3}
\]

It has degree three in \(Y\). The singular-fiber correspondence is

\[
\operatorname{Res}_Y(\mathfrak B(x,Y),\mathfrak B(x',Y))=0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Equation (8.4) contains \((x-x')^3\), but it also has genuine non-diagonal critical-value-collision curves. Those curves are **not new transfer symmetries** and do not create extra irreducible components.

For reference, the discriminant of the nonzero-critical-point cubic is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Disc}_W R_x
=-8(15606x^2+2312x+85).}
\tag{8.5}
\]

The product of the three nonzero critical values is

\[
\boxed{
e_3(x)=
\frac{17^5(68x^2+1)}{36(x+5)^5}.}
\tag{8.6}
\]

Their sum is

\[
\boxed{
e_1(x)=
\frac{N_1(x)}{64(x+5)^5},}
\tag{8.7}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
N_1(x)={}&55488x^4-6399616x^3+32831760x^2\\
&-494700x+2853625.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{8.8}
\]

These formulas are convenient same-day checks for the elimination script. They are not needed for the irreducibility proof.

# 9. Point-count constants on every actual exceptional component

For \(p>17\):

| Parameter locus | Geometric components meeting \(u,v\ne-1\) | Codegree leading term |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(x,x'\ne-5,\ x\ne x'\) | 1 | \(p+O(\sqrt p)\) |
| \(x=x'\ne-5\) | diagonal + one residual | \(2p+O(\sqrt p)\) |
| \(x=-5\ne x'\) | one residual; boundary line excluded | \(p+O(\sqrt p)\) |
| \(x'=-5\ne x\) | one residual; boundary line excluded | \(p+O(\sqrt p)\) |
| \(x=x'=-5\) | diagonal + one residual; two boundary lines excluded | \(2p+O(\sqrt p)\) |
| one of \(x,x'\) is \(\infty\), the other finite | none | \(0\) exactly |
| \(x=x'=\infty\) | whole allowed parameter square | \((p-1)^2\) exactly |

A common critical value, a repeated critical point, or a multiple root of \(P(T)-x\) can make the unique component singular, but does not change its leading constant from \(1\).

There is no item of type (iv): no genuinely new positive-dimensional symmetry correspondence occurs.

# 10. Clean codegree lemma

## Theorem [LEMMA-CODEGREE]

Let \(p>17\), and let

\[
F_{p,u}(x)=\frac{(u+1)^6}{P(u)-x},
\qquad u\in\mathbf F_p\setminus\{-1\}.
\]

For finite \(x,x'\in\mathbf F_p\), put

\[
C_p(x,x')=
\#\{(u,v)\in(\mathbf F_p\setminus\{-1\})^2:
F_{p,u}(x)=F_{p,v}(x')\}.
\]

Then:

1. If \(x,x'\ne-5\) and \(x\ne x'\), the correspondence curve is absolutely irreducible of bidegree \((6,6)\), total degree \(9\), and normalization genus at most \(7\). Hence
   \[
   \boxed{C_p(x,x')=p+O(\sqrt p).}
   \]
   The implied constant is absolute; using the genus bound gives \(14\sqrt p+O(1)\).
2. If \(x=x'\ne-5\), the curve is the union of \(u=v\) and one absolutely irreducible bidegree-\((5,5)\) residual component of normalization genus at most \(5\). Hence
   \[
   \boxed{C_p(x,x)=2p+O(\sqrt p).}
   \]
3. The only additional projective reducibility loci are the degree-drop lines \(x=-5\), \(x'=-5\), and parameter infinity, with the exact behavior listed in Section 9.

Thus the explicit exceptional set is precisely (0.3), and there is no reflection-induced parameter curve.

# 12. Machine-verification script

The following Sage skeleton checks every polynomial identity and performs the requested eliminations. It is deliberately separated into reducibility and singular-fiber computations, because the latter has many non-diagonal components which are not factorization loci.

```python
from sage.all import *

R.<u,v,x,xp> = PolynomialRing(QQ)
P = lambda q: 34*q^3 + 51*q^2 + 27*q + 5
C = (u+1)^6*(P(v)-xp) - (v+1)^6*(P(u)-x)

print("bidegree", C.degree(u), C.degree(v), "total", C.total_degree())
print("diagonal remainder", C.subs(xp=x).quo_rem(u-v)[1])
Rdiag = C.subs(xp=x) // (u-v)
print("Rdiag bidegree", Rdiag.degree(u), Rdiag.degree(v))

# Boundary factors.
print("x=-5 factor", C.subs(x=-5).quo_rem(u+1)[1])
print("xp=-5 factor", C.subs(xp=-5).quo_rem(v+1)[1])

# Birational separated-polynomial model.
S.<w,z,X,Y> = PolynomialRing(QQ)
f = lambda q,A: 34*q^3 - 51*q^4 + 27*q^5 - A*q^6
assert ((u+1)^6*(v+1)^6 *
        (f(1/(v+1),xp+5)-f(1/(u+1),x+5))).simplify_rational() == C

# Critical-value resultant.
T.<w,x,y> = PolynomialRing(QQ)
crit = 34 - 68*w + 45*w^2 - 2*(x+5)*w^3
valeq = 2*y - w^3*(9*w^2 - 34*w + 34)
B = crit.resultant(valeq, w)
print("critical-value degree in y", B.degree(y))
print("critical-point discriminant", factor(crit.discriminant(w)))
print("critical-value discriminant", factor(B.discriminant(y)))

# Singular-fiber locus: this is NOT the reducibility locus.
A.<x,xp,y> = PolynomialRing(QQ)
Bx = B
Bxp = B.subs(x=xp)
J = Bx.resultant(Bxp, y)
print("common-critical-value locus", factor(J))

# Verify the displayed e1/e3 formulas by making B monic in y.
K = FractionField(QQ['x'])
Ky.<y> = PolynomialRing(K)
Bm = Ky(B).monic()
N1 = 55488*x^4 - 6399616*x^3 + 32831760*x^2 - 494700*x + 2853625
assert Bm[2] == -N1/(64*(x+5)^5)
assert Bm[0] == -17^5*(68*x^2+1)/(36*(x+5)^5)

# Finite-field factor scan: the prediction is exactly one factor off diagonal,
# two factors on the diagonal, and only boundary factors at x=-5 or xp=-5.
for p in primes(19,200):
    Fp = GF(p)
    Rp.<u,v> = PolynomialRing(Fp)
    for a in Fp:
        for b in Fp:
            Cp = (u+1)^6*(34*v^3+51*v^2+27*v+5-b) \
                 -(v+1)^6*(34*u^3+51*u^2+27*u+5-a)
            fac = Cp.factor()
            expected = 2 if a == b or a == -5 or b == -5 else 1
            # Inspect rather than assert at boundary intersections.
            if len(fac) != expected:
                print("CHECK",p,a,b,fac)
```

The most useful same-day independent check is the exhaustive finite-field factor scan in the last block. Any off-diagonal, non-\(-5\) factorization for \(p>17\) would contradict the group-theoretic theorem and should be treated as a normalization or software-factor-counting error first.

## Least-confident step

The least familiar—but standard—step is the \(S_6\) outer-automorphism case in Section 4.3: the outer \(S_5\subset S_6\) is transitive in the natural six-point action, so the corresponding graph subgroup has one orbit on the product of the two inequivalent degree-six actions. The order check is

\[
|S_5^{\rm intrans}\cap S_5^{\rm trans}|=20,
\qquad
\frac{120\cdot120}{20}=720=|S_6|,
\]

which proves there is one double coset. This should be machine-checked with an explicit permutation model of the outer automorphism, but it is not an unproved mathematical gap.
