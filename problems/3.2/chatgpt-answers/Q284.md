# Generic ordinarity does not give a uniform bound for the Apéry exceptional characters

## Executive verdict

No theorem of Adolphson–Sperber, Wan, or Gross–Koblitz currently proves

for the Apéry character family.

Granting the asserted rank-two weight-three primitive Mellin factor, these theories can potentially do the following:

- compute or bound the Newton polygon of each fixed character twist from below by a twisted Hodge polygon;

- identify an ordinary locus as a Zariski-open subset of a coefficient parameter space;

- express Frobenius entries and Gauss factors by p-adic gamma values.

They do not bound the number of characters at which the first Hasse invariant vanishes. That final zero-count is an additional anti-cancellation theorem.

The central logical distinction is

The Legendre family already demonstrates this: it is generically ordinary, while its Hasse invariant is the Deuring polynomial of degree (p-1)/2. Thus generic ordinarity is perfectly compatible with a nonordinary divisor whose degree grows linearly with p.

For the Apéry Mellin family, Gross–Koblitz makes the obstruction explicit. After extracting the known powers of the Dwork uniformizer, the first Hasse coefficient is generally a sum of p-adic gamma-unit terms of minimal valuation. Nonordinarity is cancellation in that sum. Gross–Koblitz computes the terms and their valuations; it does not prevent their residues from cancelling for many character indices.

My assessment is therefore:

A constant or logarithmic bound for |Z_p| is not presently a consequence of generic-ordinarity theory and is not known for this Apéry family. Proving it would require a new bounded-complexity formula for the character-side Hasse invariant, followed by a genuine zero-count theorem.

# 1. First audit the cohomological object

The Beukers–Peters K3 variation itself has a rank-three transcendental local system with an orthogonal pairing; it is closely related to a symmetric square of a rank-two elliptic system. See F. Beukers and C. A. M. Peters, A family of K3 surfaces and \zeta(3), Crelle 351 (1984), 42–54, and C. Peters, Monodromy and Picard–Fuchs equations for families of K3-surfaces and elliptic curves, Ann. Sci. ENS 19 (1986), 583–607.

A rank-two weight-three symplectic primitive Mellin factor may arise only after a further Mellin transform, removal of boundary pieces, and extraction of a primitive subquotient. That is not the same object as the original rank-three relative K3 system.

Accordingly, before applying any Newton-polygon theorem one must archive an exact statement of the form

Under these hypotheses, the proposed equivalence is valid:

- if a unit eigenvalue exists and all remaining slopes are positive, the trace is a unit modulo p;

- if no unit eigenvalue exists, every eigenvalue has positive valuation, so the integral trace is divisible by p.

Without the rank-two integral factor and the uniqueness of the slope-zero direction, trace divisibility need not be equivalent to complete nonordinarity; it may detect only failure at the first Hodge break.

The remainder of this answer grants the rank-two formulation and asks what the cited p-adic theories then provide.

## 3.1 Why “all but finitely many” is not O(1) uniformly in p

The exceptional set is cut out by a Hasse polynomial or a collection of Frobenius minors. Their degrees ordinarily depend on p. In Dwork's construction they are extracted from coefficients of powers such as f^{p-1}, so linear growth in p is natural.

The Legendre elliptic family is the basic warning. Its Hasse invariant is the Deuring polynomial

of degree (p-1)/2. The family is generically ordinary, yet the nonordinary divisor has degree proportional to p. Igusa's observation that this Hasse invariant solves a mod-p hypergeometric equation is discussed in A. Adolphson and S. Sperber, Hasse invariants and mod p solutions of A-hypergeometric systems, J. Number Theory 142 (2014), 183–210.

Therefore the logical implication

is false even for a classical rank-two family.

## 3.2 The wrong parameter is being varied

Wan's generic theory varies the coefficients of the Laurent polynomial in M_p(\Delta). The Apéry problem fixes the geometric K3 input and varies a Kummer character

To apply generic-coefficient theory, one would need an algebraic map

whose image represents these twists, together with a bound on the degree of \iota_p and on the degree of the Hasse divisor. No such bounded-degree realization is known. The natural character group has order p-1, and its algebraic complexity changes with p.

Thus even a proof that the ambient coefficient space is generically ordinary would not count the exceptional Apéry characters.

## 4.1 Why the gamma product is not a bounded-degree function of z

As z ranges through 0,\ldots,p-2:

- the arguments \langle\alpha+z/(p-1)\rangle pass through essentially all residue classes modulo p;

- the floor functions in \nu_p(z,a) change at a number of breakpoints that can grow with p;

- the set of auxiliary indices a attaining the minimum changes with z;

- the sum in (4.3) can contain a number of terms growing with p.

Morita's p-adic gamma function is locally analytic, but the sampled points occupy p-1 different residue classes. There is no known single restricted power series of bounded Weierstrass degree whose values are H_p(z) for all z.

Therefore Strassmann's theorem does not yield an O(1) global zero count. To use it, one would first have to prove a uniform analytic interpolation with bounded Weierstrass degree—again the missing new theorem.

## 4.2 Unit-root formulas are still not zero-count formulas

Adolphson and Sperber give unit-root formulas for toric exponential sums in terms of ratios of A-hypergeometric functions:

- A. Adolphson and S. Sperber, On unit root formulas for toric exponential sums.

Such a formula can identify the unit root on the ordinary locus. It does not bound the number of character twists at which the denominator or first Hasse coefficient vanishes.

Wan's proof of Dwork's conjecture similarly concerns p-adic meromorphic continuation of unit-root zeta functions:

- D. Wan, Dwork's conjecture on unit root zeta functions, Ann. of Math. 150 (1999), 867–927.

Meromorphy is not an anti-concentration theorem for the finite set of character indices.

# 5. The exact checklist needed for a constant or logarithmic bound

A proof of |Z_p|\le C by this route would require all of the following.

## 5.1 Exact primitive Frobenius factor

Prove that the character-side object is genuinely rank two and integral, with determinant and Hodge slopes explicitly normalized. Separate it from the full rank-three K3 variation and from any larger global Mellin cohomology.

## 5.2 Trace versus first Hasse invariant

Prove uniformly, including boundary characters, that

The first equivalence needs the exact unit normalization; the second needs first-slope multiplicity one.

## 5.3 A valid Adolphson–Sperber model

Produce an explicit nondegenerate Laurent/hypergeometric model for the primitive rank-two factor, or extend the theory to the relevant degenerate specialization. Compute the twisted Newton polytope and the character-dependent Hodge polygon.

## 5.4 An explicit first Hasse function

Derive H_p(z), not merely the full trace or the lower bound on its valuation. This means identifying every minimal-valuation Gross–Koblitz term and their residues.

## 5.5 Bounded complexity in the character variable

Prove one of the following genuinely new statements:

- H_p(z)=P_p(\omega^z) with \deg P_p\le C;

- H_p(z) is the restriction of a fixed-degree algebraic function on a fixed curve;

- H_p admits a p-adic analytic interpolation with uniformly bounded Weierstrass degree;

- or a direct recurrence/monodromy argument bounds the number of zeros independently of p.

Generic ordinarity supplies none of these.

## 5.6 Noncontainment and exceptional characters

Show that the Apéry character path is not contained in the Hasse divisor, and classify trivial, quadratic, singular, and self-dual characters separately.

Only after Steps 5.1–5.6 would Bézout, Strassmann, or an elementary polynomial root bound give O(1) or O(\log p).

# 6. What can be concluded rigorously today

The cited theories support the following conditional statement:

If the primitive Apéry Mellin twists admit an explicit nondegenerate exponential-sum model with first Hasse invariant H_p(z), then Adolphson–Sperber provides the Hodge lower bound, and Gross–Koblitz can in principle compute H_p(z) term by term. Wan's theory can identify when ordinarity is generic in an ambient coefficient family.

In particular:

- |Z_p|\le C is not presently proved by this method.

- |Z_p|\ll\log p is also not presently proved.

- Generic ordinarity does not even imply o(p) exceptional characters without degree information.

- Gross–Koblitz converts the problem into explicit unit cancellation but does not solve that cancellation problem.

The empirical bounded law for |Z_p| is therefore substantially stronger than the available generic-ordinarity theorems. The new mathematical target is an Apéry-specific character-side Hasse polynomial of bounded complexity, not another general Newton-over-Hodge theorem.
