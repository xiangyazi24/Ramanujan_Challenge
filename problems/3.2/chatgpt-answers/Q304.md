## 1. Density zero of non-ordinary primes: still open

For a rational prime (pnmid 8), the local polynomial is

[

X^2-a_p(f)X+p^3.

]

The form is ordinary at (p) exactly when (pnmid a_p(f)). The conjecture that ordinary reductions have density one is a case of the ordinary-reduction conjecture usually attributed to Serre.

No theorem currently proves

[

#{ple x:pmid a_p(f)}=o(x/log x)

]

for this form, or for a general non-CM rational newform of weight 4.

This remains outside the scope of the strongest recent general results:

- Gouvêa, Non-Ordinary Primes: A Story describes the higher-weight problem and its numerical character.

- Suh, Ordinary primes in Hilbert modular varieties proves strong results in parallel weight 2 and, in higher weight, verifies ordinarity only for motives whose (ell)-adic realizations become abelian on a finite-index subgroup. The representation attached to (8.4.a.a) has generic (mathrm{SU}(2)) monodromy and is not of this type.

- Wang–Zhang, Ordinary primes for GL(_2)-type abelian varieties and weight 2 modular forms, published in 2026, obtains positive-density results for weight-2 forms and associated abelian varieties. It does not cover weight 4.

Thus even the much weaker problem of proving infinitely many ordinary primes for this particular genuinely nonabelian weight-4 motive is not supplied by the cited general theorems. The expected statement is density one ordinary and infinitely many, but the density-one assertion needed here is open.

### Why the usual Chebotarev argument stops at weight 4

At a non-ordinary prime write

[

a_p(f)=p,m_p.

Deligne gives

For weight 2, divisibility by (p) forces the trace to be zero for large (p), a fixed trace condition. For weight 3, only finitely many multipliers can occur. At weight 4, the number of possible integers (m_p) grows like (sqrt p). Chebotarev controls any fixed congruence or fixed trace target, but it does not control this growing union of moving targets.

Complex Sato–Tate likewise does not suffice. The normalized condition is

[

frac{a_p(f)}{2p^{3/2}}

=

frac{m_p}{2sqrt p},

]

so one must detect a (p)-dependent lattice of mesh (p^{-1/2}). Ordinary effective Sato–Tate is not fine enough at that shrinking arithmetic scale.

## 2. What is special about (8.4.a.a)? Not the kind of specialness that solves ordinarity

The exact database invariants are recorded on the LMFDB page for (8.4.a.a):

[

begin{array}{c|c}

text{level} & 8\

text{weight} & 4\

text{coefficient field} & mathbf Q\

text{CM} & text{no}\

text{nontrivial inner twists} & text{none}\

text{twist minimal} & text{yes}\

text{Sato–Tate group} & mathrm{SU}(2)

end{array}

]

It is the unique normalized eigenform in (S_4(Gamma_0(8))), with

[

f(tau)=eta(2tau)^4eta(4tau)^4.

]

Ahlgren and Ono proved that it is the modular form attached to a rigid Calabi–Yau threefold; see Modularity of a certain Calabi–Yau threefold.

These facts point in the generic direction:

- no CM field forces systematic trace vanishing at inert primes;

- no inner twist forces a congruence-class pattern;

- there is no real multiplication or endoscopic splitting restricting (m_p=a_p/p) to a finite set;

- the (mathrm{SU}(2)) Sato–Tate group predicts generic trace variation.

The first coefficients already exclude any simple residue-class zero pattern:

[

a_3=-4,qquad a_5=-2,qquad a_7=24,

]

so primes in the three odd classes modulo 8 are not forced to have zero trace.

### Hypergeometric supercongruences

The central Apéry congruence is special and important, but attribution should be separated by modulus:

- Ahlgren–Ono proved the congruence modulo (p^2); see A Gaussian hypergeometric series evaluation and Apéry number congruences.

- Kilbourn obtained the corresponding extension to modulus (p^3); see An extension of the Apéry number supercongruence.

This identifies the relevant Hasse/unit-root quantity extraordinarily explicitly. It does not provide a horizontal count of the primes at which that quantity vanishes.

## 3. The correct motive is rank two of weight three, not a weight-2 K3 trace

The modular Galois representation attached to (f) is two-dimensional, pure of weight 3, with Hodge–Tate weights

[

{0,3}.

]

It is naturally realized in the middle cohomology of a rigid Calabi–Yau threefold. It is not the (H^2) representation of a fixed K3 surface, whose weight would be 2.

The local Newton polygon makes the distinction precise. Put

[

v=v_p(a_p(f)).

]

For

[

X^2-a_pX+p^3,

]

the Frobenius slopes are

[

begin{cases}

{0,3}, & v=0,\

{1,2}, & v=1,\

{3/2,3/2}, & vge2.

end{cases}

]

For (pge5), Deligne’s bound implies that (vge2) forces (a_p=0). Thus the non-ordinary primes consist of two very different types:

1. the expected generic bad case

1. the genuinely supersingular case

The first type is the main obstacle. It is not a K3 supersingularity condition.

### Why recent K3 theorems do not apply

- Bogomolov–Zarhin prove density-one ordinary reduction for a fixed K3 surface after a finite extension of the number field.

- Hui, 2025 proves density zero for supersingular primes of non-CM abelian varieties and K3 surfaces, with effective upper bounds.

- Shankar–Shankar–Tang–Tayou prove infinitely many Picard-rank jumps and more general exceptional-class results for K3-type motives on GSpin Shimura varieties.

- Ito’s results concern supersingular reduction of CM K3 surfaces and related K3 families.

These theorems concern weight-2 K3 cohomology, Picard-rank jumps, or the fully isoclinic supersingular locus. They do not count the slope-({1,2}) primes of the rank-two Calabi–Yau motive above.

The symmetric-square Picard–Fuchs description of the Apéry family is therefore real at the level of differential equations and K3 variation, but it does not identify (a_p(f)) with the trace of a weight-2 rank-three K3 representation having Weil bound (O(p)). The observed (O(p^{3/2})) trace scale is the correct weight-3 scale.

## 4. Does a unit-root subcrystal help?

At an ordinary prime, the crystalline representation has a unique slope-zero line. Dwork theory can describe its unit root by a hypergeometric quotient. The 2021 work of Long–Tu–Yui–Zudilin, Supercongruences for rigid hypergeometric Calabi–Yau threefolds, is a strong example: it proves unit-root and supercongruence formulas at ordinary primes.

But this does not count the primes where the unit root ceases to exist. In this example,

[

text{unit-root line exists}

quadLongleftrightarrowquad

pnmid a_p(f).

]

Thus a unit-root description converts the question into the vanishing of an explicit Hasse invariant; it does not by itself give a horizontal density theorem for its vanishing.

There is also no bounded-multiplier rescue. At a non-ordinary prime,

[

a_p=p,m_p,

qquad |m_p|le2sqrt p,

]

and the lack of CM or inner twists gives no mechanism forcing (m_p) into a fixed finite set. A finite multiplier set would make Chebotarev plausible; the actual set grows with (p).

## 5. Sharpest honest conclusion and viable routes

For this exact form:

[

boxed{

#{ple x:pmid a_p(8.4.a.a)}=o(x/log x)

text{ is open as of July 2026.}

}

]

The post-2020 results cited in the question do not resolve it. They either:

- concern weight-2 abelian/K3 motives;

- require potentially abelian Galois realizations;

- count the fully supersingular locus rather than the slope-({1,2}) locus;

- or identify unit roots only after ordinarity has already been assumed.

A successful motivic proof would require genuinely new horizontal input, for example:

1. a uniform arithmetic equidistribution theorem for the mod-(p) Hasse invariant of this fixed hypergeometric motive;

1. an effective large-sieve/Chebotarev argument that resolves the moving lattice (a_p/pinmathbf Z);

1. an unexpected algebraic correspondence forcing a finite multiplier set—unlikely given CM = no, inner twists = none, and Sato–Tate group (mathrm{SU}(2));

1. or a direct Apéry/Hasse-polynomial argument controlling the zero positions uniformly in (p).

The fourth route is presently the one most closely matched to the explicit structure of Problem 3.2.
