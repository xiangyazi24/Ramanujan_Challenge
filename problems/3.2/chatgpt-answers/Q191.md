# Gross–Koblitz does not lower the Apéry collision to weight one

## Executive verdict

The proposed route does not close the collision-energy problem.

There are four separate obstructions, any one of which defeats the naive argument.

1. The premise is generally false: the varying Apéry coefficient b_r is not a single product of O(1) Gauss sums. Its finite-field hypergeometric realization is a sum over an auxiliary multiplicative character of products of Gauss/Jacobi sums. Gross–Koblitz rewrites each summand as a power of \pi times p-adic gamma units; it does not remove the auxiliary sum.

1. Weights add under products. A nontrivial Gauss sum has complex absolute value p^{1/2} and Weil weight 1, but a product of three such sums has size p^{3/2} and weight 3. Writing a weight-three number as a product of weight-one factors does not place the product in the Deuring range.

1. Gauss-product values are not rigid or injective. The elementary norm identity

already gives fibers of cardinality \asymp p. Multiplying by one fixed Gauss sum gives a weight-three Gauss product with \asymp p identical values. Hasse–Davenport supplies further relations, and Hasse's proposed classification of all multiplicative relations was disproved by Yamamoto; Murray later produced infinite families of additional sign ambiguities.

1. The \pi-factor is not the surviving obstruction. In the clean Kummer extraction one has

The known factor \pi^r and the gamma unit can be divided out exactly, leaving H_r. It is H_r—a three-dimensional multiplicative character sum, naturally of weight 3—whose reduction is b_r. Thus the outer Gross–Koblitz factor disappears, but the original high-weight cancellation remains intact.

The Gross–Koblitz uniformizer factor can be normalized away. What remains is not a pure weight-one object but the full Apéry Mellin/character sum, or equivalently a sum of Gross–Koblitz gamma-unit terms. Its residual cancellation is exactly the original problem.

## 5.4 Hasse's proposed classification is false

Hasse conjectured that the norm relation and Hasse–Davenport essentially generated all multiplicative relations among Gauss sums. Koichi Yamamoto disproved this, and later work produced infinite families of additional “sign ambiguities.”

- K. Yamamoto, On a conjecture of Hasse concerning multiplicative relations of Gaussian sums, J. Combinatorial Theory 1 (1966), 476–489.

- B. J. Murray, Explicit multiplicative relations between Gauss sums, J. Number Theory 126 (2007), 87–109.

Murray's abstract explicitly notes that Hasse's conjecture is false and gives an infinite class of new sign ambiguities.

Therefore no available classification theorem says that equality of two bounded Gauss products forces equality of their character multisets up to O(1) symmetries.

# 6. Exact answer to the three questions

## (1) Does Gross–Koblitz turn the collision into a weight-one Deligne problem?

No.

- The finite-field hypergeometric formula is an additive sum of Gauss products.

- In the constant-term Kummer formula, one outer weight-one Gauss sum factors out, but division leaves the weight-three sum H_r.

- A product of three weight-one sums is itself weight three.

- Deligne bounds the size of an individual trace or complete character sum; it does not imply injectivity of its values or control reduction collisions.

No.

- Stickelberger fixes valuation/ideal data, not units.

## (3) Does the \pi^{\nu(r)} part survive or cancel?

There are two answers, depending on the representation.

### One Kummer Gauss factor

In (3.3), the entire factor

### Full Greene/McCarthy expansion

There is generally no single \pi^{\nu(r)}. Each auxiliary-character term has its own exponent \nu(r,a). After the minimal exponent is extracted, the residue is an additive sum of gamma units. The condition b_r\equiv0 or b_r\equiv b_s is cancellation among those units.

Thus the “unit-root part” survives in the only sense that matters: it is the whole normalized residual hypergeometric sum. It is not a harmless scalar that factors out of the collision.

# 8. What remains potentially viable

Gross–Koblitz is still useful, but only for a more difficult program.

A viable theorem would have to show that the normalized functions

or the corresponding Greene/McCarthy gamma sums have residual anti-concentration. Possible formulations are:

1. a bounded-conductor sheaf or overconvergent F-isocrystal on a genuine character-parameter space whose residual trace is b_r;

1. a uniform bound for the additive transforms

1. a classification of the additive cancellations in the Apéry hypergeometric character sum, not merely multiplicative identities among individual Gauss factors;

1. an explicit treatment of the quadratic Apéry–Franel pullback showing that its character-side convolution has large monodromy and self-characteristic residual equidistribution.

The last point is important. The Apéry Hasse polynomial is related to a lower-rank Franel/Legendre object through a non-monomial quadratic pullback and gauge. Mellin transform turns a monomial pullback into a simple character substitution, but a non-monomial pullback produces a convolution kernel. Hence even a pure Jacobi-sum formula on the Franel side does not remain a single product in the Apéry coefficient coordinate.

This is a plausible geometric research direction, but it is not a consequence of present Gauss-sum rigidity.
