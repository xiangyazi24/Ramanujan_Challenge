# Executive conclusion

I interpret “for any (delta>0)” as “obtain some fixed positive power saving (Z(p)=O(p^{1-delta})), for any value of (delta) one can prove,” not literally a bound simultaneously for every (delta>0).

Literature verdict as of 19 July 2026: I did not find an unconditional theorem giving (Z(p)=o(p)), either for every prime or on average over primes, for the zeta(3) Apéry numbers. I also did not find a general theorem on zero coefficients of polynomial squares, algebraic series, rational diagonals, or non-autonomous Möbius orbits that would imply such a bound from the structures listed in the question.

The obstruction is substantive:

1. Algebraicity/diagonality gives automaticity and decidability of the zero set, but automatic sets can have essentially arbitrary positive rational densities. In the Apéry case the stronger Lucas structure makes this especially transparent: the entire infinite zero set modulo (p) is determined by the set of bad one-digit symbols (Z(p)), so a tiny automaton can coexist with an arbitrarily large (Z(p)).

1. Knowing that (F=Cg^2), that (g) is squarefree, and that its roots are level-6 supersingular parameters does not control the vanishing of the high-order elementary symmetric functions of those roots. Existing root-multiplicity versus support inequalities give only a constant lower bound on the number of nonzero coefficients.

1. Existing finite-field orbit-distribution theorems concern an autonomous rational map, random walks in a fixed Cayley graph, or words in a fixed finite family of maps. They do not cover the prescribed, time-dependent cocycle (M_n) from the Apéry recurrence, and expansion of the ambient Cayley graph does not imply equidistribution of one deterministic word.

1. Modular/Frobenius interpretations are known at special indices, notably the central index, but I found no interpretation for a positive proportion of the indices (0le n<p) that forces nonvanishing.

The cleanest minimal non-tautological sufficient hypothesis is a power-saving additive-character estimate

[

S_p(a):=sum_{n=0}^{p-1} e_p(a b_n),qquad ainmathbb F_p^times,

]

]

Orthogonality then gives immediately

[

Z(p)=frac1psum_{ainmathbb F_p}S_p(a)

=1+frac1psum_{ane0}S_p(a)

ll 1+p^{1-delta}.

]

An even weaker, and perhaps more approachable, sufficient hypothesis is a collision-energy saving

[

E_p:=#{(m,n)in[0,p-1]^2:b_m=b_n}

=sum_{yinmathbb F_p}N_p(y)^2ll p^{2-eta}.

]

Since (Z(p)^2le E_p), this yields

[

Z(p)ll p^{1-eta/2}.

]

Thus any nontrivial collision saving already solves the requested weakest condition.

# (a) Zero coefficients of squares, algebraic functions, and rational diagonals

## A1. Squares of structured polynomials

Write (w(P)) for the number of nonzero coefficients of a polynomial. Then

[

Z(p)=p-w(F),qquad F(t)=sum_{n=0}^{p-1}b_nt^n.

]

The general literature on sparse powers does not provide a lower bound (w(g^2)gg p^delta), much less (w(g^2)=p-o(p)), from squarefreeness, reciprocity, or a prescribed root field.

Relevant references are:

- Don Coppersmith and James Davenport, “Polynomials whose powers are sparse,” Acta Arithmetica 58 (1991), no. 1, 79–87, DOI 10.4064/aa-58-1-79-87. This studies how cancellation can make powers unexpectedly sparse. It is evidence against any naive principle that squaring a polynomial must create many nonzero coefficients.

- John Abbott, “Sparse squares of polynomials,” Mathematics of Computation 71 (2002), no. 237, 407–413. This gives explicit dense/complete-polynomial phenomena with sparse squares in small degrees. It does not give an asymptotic finite-field bound applicable here.

- Sandro Mattarei, “Root multiplicities and number of nonzero coefficients of a polynomial,” Journal of Algebra and Its Applications 6 (2007), no. 3, 469–475, DOI 10.1142/S0219498807002338, arXiv:math/0512239. Theorem 2 says that in characteristic (p), a nonzero root of multiplicity (k) forces weight at least (prod_i(k_i+1)), where (k=sum k_ip^i). For (F=Cg^2), the relevant multiplicity is only 2, so this gives essentially (w(F)ge3). The degree/distinct-root corollary gives the same constant-scale information because (deg(g^2)/(#text{ distinct roots})=2).

Therefore the known root-multiplicity/support theory does not use the arithmetic nature of the supersingular roots strongly enough. No theorem was found bounding the number of zero coefficients of the square of a supersingular polynomial, a Hasse polynomial, or a reciprocal squarefree polynomial by (o(p)).

### Why supersingular root equidistribution is not enough

If

[

g(t)=prod_{i=1}^d(t-alpha_i),

]

then its coefficients are elementary symmetric functions (e_k(alpha_1,dots,alpha_d)), with (k) ranging up to (dasymp p). Standard equidistribution results for supersingular points control fixed-complexity test functions or fixed moments as (p) varies. They do not control the simultaneous exact vanishing modulo (p) of (O(p)) high-order symmetric functions. This is the precise gap between “the roots are arithmetically special” and “most coefficients are nonzero.”

## A2. Algebraic power series and automatic zero sets

The definitive general theorem is:

- Boris Adamczewski and Jason P. Bell, “On vanishing coefficients of algebraic power series over fields of positive characteristic,” Inventiones Mathematicae 187 (2012), no. 2, 343–393, DOI 10.1007/s00222-011-0337-4, arXiv:1205.4091.

This is a classification theorem, not a sparsity theorem. The converse shows that algebraicity alone cannot force zero density.

Jason P. Bell, “The upper density of an automatic set is rational,” Journal de théorie des nombres de Bordeaux 32 (2020), no. 2, 585–604, DOI 10.5802/jtnb.1135, proves that lower and upper densities of a fixed automatic set are computable rationals and that essentially every allowable rational pair occurs. Again, automaticity is compatible with positive density.

For the Apéry sequence there is an even sharper reason this route cannot bound (Z(p)). The (p)-Lucas congruence gives, for the base-(p) digits (n=n_0+n_1p+cdots+n_rp^r),

[

b_nequiv prod_i b_{n_i}pmod p.

]

Hence

[

b_nequiv0pmod p

iff

text{at least one digit }n_itext{ lies in }Z(p).

]

A two-state automaton recognizes this language: one state means “no bad digit seen,” the other is an accepting sink. The number (Z(p)) is hidden in which of the (p) input symbols trigger the sink, not in the number of states. Thus even an optimal automaton-size theorem says nothing about the proportion of bad one-digit symbols.

Andrew Bridy, “Automatic sequences and curves over finite fields,” arXiv:1604.08241, gives a geometric state-complexity bound of roughly (q^{h+d+g-1}). Eric Rowland, Manon Stipulanti, and Reem Yassawi, “An elementary proof of Bridy’s theorem,” Finite Fields and Their Applications 105 (2025), 102621, DOI 10.1016/j.ffa.2025.102621, gives a new proof. These concern representation complexity, not the distribution of one-digit outputs.

## A3. Rational diagonals and Furstenberg/Deligne

- Boris Adamczewski and Jason P. Bell, “Diagonalization and rationalization of algebraic Laurent series,” Annales scientifiques de l’École Normale Supérieure 46 (2013), no. 6, 963–1004, DOI 10.24033/asens.2207, arXiv:1205.4090, prove quantitative algebraicity bounds for reductions modulo (p) of diagonals. The algebraic degree may be as large as (p^A), with (A) depending on the fixed rational representation.

This does not yield coefficient equidistribution. Deligne-type square-root cancellation applies when the varying index can be represented as the rational points of a bounded-complexity variety carrying a nontrivial bounded-conductor sheaf. A diagonal representation of the generating function does not automatically give such a bounded-complexity geometric family in the index (n). Coefficient extraction at a varying degree and reduction modulo (p) can have complexity growing with (p).

A useful sufficient geometric statement would be the following. Suppose one could realize

[

nlongmapsto b_npmod p,qquad ninmathbb F_p,

]

as a rational map (R_p:mathbb A^1dashrightarrowmathbb A^1) of degree (D_pll p^varepsilon), with every Artin–Schreier pullback (mathcal L_{psi(aR_p)}) geometrically nontrivial. Weil–Deligne would then give

[

]

and hence (Z(p)ll p^{1/2+varepsilon}). No such bounded-complexity realization of the full index map was found. The obvious recurrence product or factorial/binomial description has complexity growing with (p).

# (b) The non-autonomous projective orbit

The projective formulation is exact and promising:

[

theta_n=[b_n:b_{n+1}]inmathbf P^1(mathbb F_p),

]

and (Z(p)) is the number of hits on ([0:1]). The second-order recurrence gives a prescribed cocycle

[

theta_{n+1}=M_ntheta_n,

]

where the entries of (M_n) are rational functions of (n).

I found the following nearby literature:

- Jaime Gutierrez and Igor E. Shparlinski, “Expansion of orbits of some dynamical systems over finite fields,” Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society 82 (2010), no. 2, 232–239, DOI 10.1017/S0004972709001270. This treats an orbit of a fixed rational map (xmapstopsi(x)), including fixed linear-fractional maps, and proves that sufficiently long orbit segments cannot be trapped in short intervals.

- László Mérai and Igor E. Shparlinski, “Sparsity of curves and additive and multiplicative expansion of rational maps over finite fields,” Acta Arithmetica 188 (2019), 401–411, DOI 10.4064/aa180307-20-8, arXiv:1803.02165. The application concerns sequences formed by compositions of maps from a fixed finite collection and proves escape from short intervals or small multiplicative subgroups.

- Jean Bourgain and Alex Gamburd, “Uniform expansion bounds for Cayley graphs of (mathrm{SL}_2(mathbb F_p)),” Annals of Mathematics 167 (2008), no. 2, 625–642, DOI 10.4007/annals.2008.167.625. This gives expansion for reductions of fixed non-elementary generators and for random generators.

None of these yields the required hitting bound:

1. The Apéry maps are time-dependent and prescribed, not iterates of one map.

1. The word (M_{n-1}cdots M_0) is deterministic and highly correlated with the time parameter.

1. Cayley-graph expansion controls a random walk or averaging operator. A single deterministic word can revisit one point often even in an expander unless the word itself satisfies a discrepancy/nonconcentration condition.

1. “Not contained in a short interval/subgroup” is much weaker than an upper bound for the number of visits to one point.

Direct result sought: an equidistribution or point-hitting theorem for a cocycle (M(n)inmathrm{PGL}_2(mathbb F_p)) whose entries are bounded-degree rational functions of (n), along the consecutive times (n=0,dots,p-1). Not found.

Average-over-primes analogue: I did not find a theorem averaging the point-hitting number of such a (p)-dependent non-autonomous cocycle over primes. Most arithmetic-dynamics average results fix a map or an integral orbit first and then reduce it modulo varying primes; that is not this setting.

A plausible route would require proving cancellation in matrix-coefficient sums

[

sum_{n<p}varphi(P_ntheta_0),

qquad P_n=M_{n-1}cdots M_0,

]

for mean-zero test functions (varphi). Merely showing that the matrices (M_n) generate (mathrm{PGL}_2(mathbb F_p)) is insufficient; one also needs quantitative nonconcentration of this specific ordered product sequence.

# (c) Modular and Frobenius interpretations of individual positions

The classical references are:

- Frits Beukers, “Some congruences for the Apéry numbers,” Journal of Number Theory 21 (1985), no. 2, 141–155, DOI 10.1016/0022-314X(85)90047-2.

- Frits Beukers, “Another congruence for the Apéry numbers,” Journal of Number Theory 25 (1987), no. 2, 201–210, DOI 10.1016/0022-314X(87)90025-4.

These and later modular-form work explain special-index congruences, including the central-index relation to the weight-3 form (eta(4tau)^6). I did not verify a theorem number for the exact central-index formula from an accessible full copy, so I do not assign one here.

More recent work on modular forms and Lucas congruences includes Frits Beukers, Wei-Lun Tsai, and Dongxi Ye, “Lucas congruences using modular forms,” Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society 57 (2025), 69–78, DOI 10.1112/blms.13182, arXiv:2408.16616. This explains Lucas congruences for many Apéry-like modular sequences, but it does not identify every position (b_n), (0le n<p), with a Frobenius trace.

Other relevant distribution papers do not answer the fixed-prime question:

- Moubariz Z. Garaev, Florian Luca, and Igor E. Shparlinski, “Catalan and Apéry numbers in residue classes,” Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A 113 (2006), no. 5, 851–865, DOI 10.1016/j.jcta.2005.08.003. For polynomial-recurrence sequences such as Apéry numbers they prove a finite additive-basis result modulo sufficiently large primes. This does not imply that a positive proportion of the first (p) terms are nonzero.

- Florian Luca and Igor E. Shparlinski, “Arithmetic properties of Apéry numbers,” Journal of the London Mathematical Society 78 (2008), no. 3, 545–562, DOI 10.1112/jlms/jdn031. This concerns prime factors of the integer (b_n) for a density-one set of integer indices (n), not the distribution of (b_nbmod p) as (n) runs through one complete block.

- Xavier Caruso, Florian Fürnsinn, Daniel Vargas-Montoya, and Wadim Zudilin, “Galois groups of Apéry-like series modulo primes,” Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society, published online 13 February 2026, 1–14, DOI 10.1017/S0004972725100932, arXiv:2510.23298. Theorem 1.2 proves the square/quadratic-times-square factorization. The article studies the Galois group and factorization pattern, not zero coefficients of (A_p).

### Why Sato–Tate does not directly apply

Sato–Tate normally varies the prime for a fixed motive or modular form and distributes normalized Frobenius traces. Here the desired variable is (n) inside a fixed prime field. The fact that one special (n) gives a Fourier coefficient does not produce a family of fixed-conductor motives indexed by all (ninmathbb F_p).

Likewise, the supersingular interpretation concerns the roots in the parameter (t) of the Hasse polynomial. The numbers (b_n) are its coefficients. Passing from equidistribution of roots to nonvanishing of most coefficients requires uniform control of high-degree symmetric functions; no Sato–Tate theorem supplies that.

Conclusion for (c): modular interpretations for special positions exist; a modular/Frobenius interpretation for all positions, or for a positive proportion sufficient to force (Z(p)=o(p)), was not found.

# (d) Minimal believable hypotheses

## D1. Uniform additive-character cancellation

For (e_p(x)=e^{2pi i x/p}), define

[

S_p(a)=sum_{n=0}^{p-1}e_p(ab_n).

]

Hypothesis H1((delta)). There is a fixed (delta>0) such that

[

max_{ane0}|S_p(a)|ll p^{1-delta}.

]

Then

[

Z(p)=1+frac1psum_{ane0}S_p(a)

le1+frac1psum_{ane0}|S_p(a)|

ll p^{1-delta}.

]

This is substantially weaker than full Sato–Tate or a central limit theorem. It asks only for a power saving in complete additive-character sums.

A weaker averaged version suffices:

Hypothesis H1-avg((delta)).

[

sum_{ane0}|S_p(a)|ll p^{2-delta}.

]

It gives the same conclusion.

## D2. Collision-energy saving

Let

[

N_p(y)=#{0le n<p:b_n=y},

qquad

E_p=sum_yN_p(y)^2.

]

Hypothesis H2((eta)). For some fixed (eta>0),

[

E_pll p^{2-eta}.

]

Then

[

Z(p)=N_p(0)le E_p^{1/2}ll p^{1-eta/2}.

]

This may be the weakest useful research target because it does not single out zero. It says only that the value sequence does not concentrate too heavily in its fibers. By Parseval,

[

E_p=frac1psum_{ainmathbb F_p}|S_p(a)|^2.

]

Thus H2 is an (L^2) character-sum statement, weaker than uniform H1. The random-map prediction is (E_pasymp p), which would imply (Z(p)ll p^{1/2}); the empirical Poisson law is much stronger still.

## D3. Bounded-conductor geometric realization

Hypothesis H3. There is a bounded-dimensional algebraic family over (mathbb F_p) representing (nmapsto b_n), and for every (ane0) the associated additive-character sheaf has conductor (O(p^varepsilon)) and no geometrically trivial summand.

Then Deligne’s Riemann-hypothesis bounds give

[

S_p(a)ll p^{1/2+varepsilon},

]

hence

[

Z(p)ll p^{1/2+varepsilon}.

]

This is a standard, precise geometric hypothesis—not GRH—but the missing work is exactly the construction of a uniform low-conductor sheaf in the index variable.

## D4. Deterministic cocycle discrepancy

A projective-orbit hypothesis sufficient for the target is

[

left|#{n<p:theta_n=x}-frac{p}{p+1}right|ll p^{1-delta}

quadtext{for every }xinmathbf P^1(mathbb F_p).

]

This directly implies the claim at (x=[0:1]). To make it structural rather than a restatement, one would want a theorem deriving this from:

1. Zariski-dense/non-elementary generation by the matrices (M_n);

1. nonconcentration of partial products in proper subgroups;

1. a spectral gap for block-averaging operators; and

1. a discrepancy condition ensuring the prescribed ordered word samples those blocks rather than behaving adversarially.

Conditions 1–3 alone are not enough for one deterministic trajectory. No theorem establishing all four for polynomial-parameter Möbius cocycles was found.

## D5. What GRH would and would not do

GRH by itself is not a natural sufficient hypothesis here. There is no currently identified fixed global (L)-function whose zeros control the distribution of the (p) values (b_0,dots,b_{p-1}). Once a bounded-conductor sheaf or a suitable automorphic family is constructed, one already has finite-field Riemann-hypothesis estimates from Weil/Deligne; classical GRH would not be the main missing ingredient.

# Recommended attack order

1. Collision count first. Try to prove

1. Character sums second. Study (S_p(a)) numerically for all (a) and estimate the observed maximum and second moment. This will distinguish a square-root-cancellation model from weaker power savings.

1. Exploit the recurrence at two times. Equality (b_m=b_n), or simultaneous constraints on the two orbit vectors, may yield an algebraic incidence problem involving the transfer products. A low-degree relation for pairs would be enough for an energy saving even if individual point equidistribution remains inaccessible.

1. Treat the supersingular-square structure as extra arithmetic input, not as a generic square. The needed new theorem would control many elementary symmetric functions of the level-6 supersingular parameters simultaneously. Existing supersingular equidistribution is only fixed-complexity and does not do this.

1. Do not invest first in generic automatic-sequence bounds. The Lucas property shows that the automaton structure merely repackages (Z(p)); it cannot reduce it without a new uniform restriction on the one-digit transition labels.

# Bottom line

- (a) General square/algebraic/diagonal methods: no unconditional (o(p)) coefficient-zero bound found; automaticity is provably too permissive.

- (b) Non-autonomous projective hitting theorem of the required type: not found, including average-over-prime versions.

- (c) Modular interpretations: special indices yes; positive-proportion/all-index interpretation forcing nonvanishing: not found.

- (d) The most economical believable hypothesis is either an (L^2) collision saving (E_pll p^{2-eta}) or additive-character cancellation. A bounded-conductor geometric realization would imply a much stronger (p^{1/2+varepsilon}) bound, but constructing that realization is the substantive open problem.
