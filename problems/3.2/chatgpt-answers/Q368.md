# Executive verdict
The order split is valid and gives a genuine unconditional (o(n)) contribution, but the near-primitive remainder does **not** acquire divisibility through cyclotomic conjugation, Hasse–Davenport, Gross–Koblitz, or an obvious norm.
Let
$$
n=qp+r,\qquad q=\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor,\qquad 0\le r<p,
$$
for a prime (p\>sqrt n), and put
$$
m_q:=n-q,\qquad
g_p:=\gcd(p-1,m_q),\qquad
d_p:=\frac{p-1}{g_p}.
$$
Since (requiv n-qpmod\{p-1\}), (d_p) is exactly the order of the moving Kummer character (omega_p\^r).
The proof-grade conclusions are:
1. For (qle Q) and (d_ple D),
	$$
	\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt n,\ q\le Q\\p\mid b_r,\ d_p\le D}}\log p
\ll QD\,n^{o(1)}.
\tag{0.1}
	$$
	Thus choosing (D=n\^\{1-varepsilon\}/Q) makes this contribution (n\^\{1-varepsilon+o(1)\}=o(n)).
2. On the complementary range (d_p\>D),
	$$
	g_p=\frac{p-1}{d_p}<\frac{p}{D}
\le \frac{Q}{q}n^{\varepsilon}
\le Qn^{\varepsilon}.
\tag{0.2}
	$$
	So the reduction to small character kernel is correct.
3. For the residual sum
	$$
	\mathcal L_{\mathrm{np}}(n;Q,\varepsilon)
:=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n,\ q\le Q\\
    p\mid b_{n\bmod p}\\
    \gcd(p-1,n-q)<Qn^\varepsilon}}
    \log p,
\tag{0.3}
	$$
	no unconditional (o(n)) estimate is currently available. The direct bounds are only
	$$
	\mathcal L_{\mathrm{np}}(n;Q,\varepsilon)
\le
\vartheta(n)-\vartheta\!\left(\frac{n}{Q+1}\right)
=O(n),
\tag{0.4}
	$$
	and, by the Lucas congruence (pmid b_\{nbmod p\}Rightarrow pmid b_n),
	$$
	\mathcal L_{\mathrm{np}}(n;Q,\varepsilon)
\le \log\operatorname{rad}(b_n)\le\log b_n=O(n).
\tag{0.5}
	$$
4. The exact obstruction is stronger than “no theorem is known.” Because (d_pmid p-1), the rational prime (p) splits completely in the character field (mathbf Q(zeta_\{d_p\})). A zero at one chosen prime above (p) has local norm degree one. Galois conjugation moves the trace **and the prime above (p) simultaneously**; it does not create zeros of all conjugate characters at one fixed (p)-adic place.
5. There is an explicit Apéry counterexample to orbit-wide amplification. Modulo (17),
	$$
	(b_0,\ldots,b_{16})
\equiv
(1,5,5,0,4,13,8,8,16,8,8,13,4,0,5,5,1).
\tag{0.6}
	$$
	The zero (r=3) has primitive character order (16). Among the eight primitive indices modulo (16), only (3) and its forced reflection (13=-3) vanish. The other six Galois-conjugate primitive indices do not. Taking (n=20,p=17,q=1) puts this example directly in the near-primitive fixed-(n) regime.
6. Gross–Koblitz removes a predictable nonzero Gauss factor and leaves exactly the Kummer trace whose residue is (b_r). Hasse–Davenport relates products of Gauss factors; it does not turn cancellation in a hypergeometric trace into cancellation for all conjugate characters. There is no amplification across rational primes because the character fields and the traces themselves vary with (p).
Therefore the surviving target is still the horizontal statement
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L(n)=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}\log p=o(n).
}
\tag{0.7}
$$
The order split removes all bounded- or moderately growing-order characters. It does not solve the near-primitive remainder. A proof now needs a genuinely horizontal non-alignment theorem for the marked residual Kummer traces, or a subexponential-height global certificate. Cyclotomic degree alone gives neither.
This sharpens [Q341](https://app.notion.com/p/3a67a6c4fa84813698a9cf66f081d4d4) and [Q362](https://app.notion.com/p/3a67a6c4fa8481cab6c0f258007b38fb). Search status: 23 July 2026.
# 1. The exact elementary order split
Let (p\>sqrt n). Then (q=lfloor n/prfloor\<p), and
$$
r=n-qp\equiv n-q=m_q\pmod{p-1},
\tag{1.1}
$$
because (pequiv1pmod\{p-1\}). Hence
$$
\operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^r)
=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,r)}
=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,m_q)}
=d_p.
\tag{1.2}
$$
Write
$$
p-1=d_pg_p,\qquad m_q=g_ps_p,\qquad \gcd(s_p,d_p)=1.
\tag{1.3}
$$
## 1.1 Small-order candidates
If (d_ple D), then (g_pmid m_q) and
$$
p=1+d_pg_p.
\tag{1.4}
$$
For fixed (q) and (d), the possible values of (g) lie among the divisors of (n-q). Therefore
$$
\#\{p:q(p)=q,\ d_p=d\}\le \tau(n-q),
\tag{1.5}
$$
without using primality or the Apéry condition. Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{p>\sqrt n,\ q\le Q\\p\mid b_r,\ d_p\le D}}\log p
&\le
(\log n)\sum_{q\le Q}\sum_{d\le D}\tau(n-q)\\
&\ll QD\,n^{o(1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.6}
$$
Here the factor (log n) is absorbed into (n\^\{o(1)\}). This establishes (0.1).
More generally, any choice satisfying
$$
QD\le n^{1-\delta}
\tag{1.7}
$$
for a fixed (delta\>0) makes the small-order logarithmic weight (O(n\^\{1-delta+o(1)\})).
## 1.2 The residual kernel bound
If (D=n\^\{1-varepsilon\}/Q) and (d_p\>D), then
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_p
&=\frac{p-1}{d_p}
<\frac pD
\le\frac{n/q}{n^{1-\varepsilon}/Q}\\
&=\frac Qq n^\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.8}
$$
Thus the remaining character is faithful on a quotient of size
$$
d_p=\frac{p-1}{g_p}
>\frac{q}{Q}p\,n^{-\varepsilon+o(1)}.
\tag{1.9}
$$
This is a mathematically meaningful localization: the unresolved twists have very small kernel and very large order.
# 2. Why small kernel alone cannot give (o(n))
The near-primitive condition does not make the candidate prime set sparse.
Take
$$
n_k=2^k+1,\qquad q=1,\qquad m_q=n_k-1=2^k.
$$
For every prime (pin(n_k/2,n_k\]) with (pequiv3pmod4),
$$
\gcd(p-1,2^k)=2,
\qquad
d_p=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\tag{2.1}
$$
Hence these are maximally near-primitive candidates: (g_p=2\<n_k\^varepsilon) for every fixed (arepsilon\>0) and all sufficiently large (k).
The prime number theorem in the progression (3pmod4) gives
$$
\sum_{\substack{n_k/2<p\le n_k\\p\equiv3\ (4)}}\log p
=\frac{n_k}{4}+o(n_k).
\tag{2.2}
$$
This is **not** a counterexample to the desired Apéry theorem, because (2.2) does not impose (pmid b_\{n_k-p\}). It proves the precise limitation of the elementary reduction:
> Any (o(n)) estimate for the residual range must use arithmetic cancellation in the Apéry zero condition. Character order and kernel size by themselves leave a linear amount of prime weight.
# 3. The exact Kummer lift of (b_rbmod p)
Gorodetsky records the three-variable constant-term model
$$
\Phi(x,y,z)
=
\frac{(x+y)(z+1)(x+y+z)(y+z+1)}{xyz},
\qquad
b_j=\operatorname{CT}(\Phi^j).
\tag{3.1}
$$
Its Newton polytope has the origin as its only interior lattice point. See [Gorodetsky, ](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2021.1982080)[*New representations for all sporadic Apéry-like sequences*](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2021.1982080), especially the constant-term table and the Lucas-congruence argument.
Let
$$
p-1=gd,\qquad \mathbf F_p^\times=\langle u\rangle,
$$
and let (zeta_d) be a primitive (d)-th root of unity. For (ain(mathbf Z/dmathbf Z)\^times), define the order-(d) character
$$
\chi_a(u)=\zeta_d^a
\tag{3.2}
$$
and the algebraic integer
$$
H_{p,d,a}
:=
\sum_{(x,y,z)\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
\chi_a\bigl(\Phi(x,y,z)\bigr)
\in\mathbf Z[\zeta_d],
\tag{3.3}
$$
where a nontrivial multiplicative character is extended by zero at (0).
## 3.1 The primes above (p)
Since (dmid p-1),
$$
p\equiv1\pmod d.
\tag{3.4}
$$
Therefore (p) splits completely in
$$
K_d=\mathbf Q(\zeta_d).
$$
The primes above (p) may be indexed by (jin(mathbf Z/dmathbf Z)\^times):
$$
\mathfrak p_j=(p,\zeta_d-u^{gj}).
\tag{3.5}
$$
Every residue degree is one and
$$
(K_d)_{\mathfrak p_j}\simeq\mathbf Q_p.
\tag{3.6}
$$
This is the standard cyclotomic splitting criterion; see Washington, [*Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-1934-7).
## 3.2 Exact reduction formula
Reducing (3.3) modulo (mathfrak p_j) sends
$$
\chi_a(v)\longmapsto v^{gaj}.
$$
Let (\[aj\]_din\{1,ldots,d-1\}) denote the least positive residue. Then
$$
H_{p,d,a}\pmod{\mathfrak p_j}
=
\sum_{(x,y,z)}\Phi(x,y,z)^{g[aj]_d}.
\tag{3.7}
$$
Because
$$
0<g[aj]_d<p-1
$$
and the origin is the only interior lattice point of the Newton polytope, torus orthogonality leaves only the constant term. In three variables,
$$
\boxed{
H_{p,d,a}\equiv-b_{g[aj]_d}\pmod{\mathfrak p_j}.
}
\tag{3.8}
$$
Thus if the selected residue is
$$
r=ga,\qquad \gcd(a,d)=1,
$$
then
$$
p\mid b_r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H_{p,d,a}\in\mathfrak p_1.
\tag{3.9}
$$
Equation (3.8) is the exact integral Kummer formulation needed for the conjugation audit. It is stronger and more elementary than invoking a conjectural motive.
# 4. Galois conjugation moves the zero diagonally
For (cin(mathbf Z/dmathbf Z)\^times), let
$$
\sigma_c(\zeta_d)=\zeta_d^c.
$$
Then directly from the definitions,
$$
\sigma_c(H_{p,d,a})=H_{p,d,ca},
\tag{4.1}
$$
while
$$
\sigma_c(\mathfrak p_j)=\mathfrak p_{jc^{-1}}.
\tag{4.2}
$$
Suppose (pmid b_\{ga\}), equivalently (H_\{p,d,a\}inmathfrak p_1). Conjugating gives
$$
H_{p,d,ca}\in\mathfrak p_{c^{-1}}.
\tag{4.3}
$$
But reducing the left side at that conjugate prime again detects the **same** Apéry coefficient:
$$
H_{p,d,ca}\pmod{\mathfrak p_{c^{-1}}}
=-b_{g[ca\,c^{-1}]_d}
=-b_{ga}.
\tag{4.4}
$$
To prove a new conjugate zero (pmid b_\{gca\}), one would need
$$
H_{p,d,ca}\in\mathfrak p_1,
\tag{4.5}
$$
which does not follow from (4.3).
This is the rigorous obstruction:
> Galois conjugation preserves the vanishing event only after moving both coordinates—character and (p)-adic embedding. The fixed-(p) zero set asks for several characters to vanish at one fixed embedding. These are different statements.
The decomposition group of every (mathfrak p_j) is trivial. There is no nonidentity Galois element that keeps the selected (p)-adic place fixed and propagates the zero around the primitive-character orbit.
# 5. What the cyclotomic norm actually measures
Take the norm of the same algebraic integer:
$$
N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(H_{p,d,a})
=
\prod_{c\in(\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z)^\times}
H_{p,d,ca}.
\tag{5.1}
$$
Because (p) splits completely,
$$
v_p\!\left(N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(H_{p,d,a})\right)
=
\sum_{j\in(\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z)^\times}
 v_{\mathfrak p_j}(H_{p,d,a}).
\tag{5.2}
$$
By (3.8), the primes above (p) that divide (H_\{p,d,a\}) are exactly those for which
$$
p\mid b_{g[aj]_d}.
\tag{5.3}
$$
Consequently the norm does not amplify one zero. It **records the number of primitive-index zeros that already exist**. At the level of support,
$$
\boxed{
\#\{\mathfrak p_j\mid p:\mathfrak p_j\mid H_{p,d,a}\}
=
\#\{c\in(\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z)^\times:p\mid b_{gc}\}.
}
\tag{5.4}
$$
The norm argument is therefore logically circular for the desired purpose: proving a large power of (p) divides the norm is equivalent to proving that many conjugate indices vanish.
## 5.1 Reflection supplies only a constant-size duplication
The known symmetry
$$
b_{p-1-r}\equiv b_r\pmod p
\tag{5.5}
$$
shows that an off-center zero at (ga) forces the zero at (g(d-a)). Thus, in the full cyclotomic field, at least the two primes indexed by (a) and (-a) occur in (5.2).
This is only a factor (p\^2), not (p\^\{varphi(d)\}). In a self-dual normalization whose trace descends to the maximal real field, this conjugate pair is one prime of the real field. Either way, the amplification is bounded independently of (d).
## 5.2 Even an optimal Weil bound gives only the trivial estimate
Suppose, in the most favorable situation, that the normalized Kummer trace satisfies
$$
|\sigma(H_{p,d,a})|\le C p^{3/2}
\tag{5.6}
$$
for every complex embedding. Then
$$
\log\left|N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(H_{p,d,a})\right|
\le
\varphi(d)\left(\frac32\log p+O(1)\right).
\tag{5.7}
$$
If (z) primitive indices vanish, then (p\^z) divides the norm, so (5.7) yields only
$$
z\le\frac32\varphi(d)+O\!\left(\frac{\varphi(d)}{\log p}\right).
\tag{5.8}
$$
This is no better than the total number (arphi(d)) of primitive characters. Near-primitivity makes the field degree large, so it makes the norm-height comparison weaker rather than stronger.
# 6. Equivalent resultant formulation
Write
$$
H_{p,d,a}=F_{p,d,a}(\zeta_d)
$$
with (F_\{p,d,a\}inmathbf Z\[X\]). Then
$$
N_{K_d/\mathbf Q}(H_{p,d,a})
=\pm\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_d,F_{p,d,a}),
\tag{6.1}
$$
where (Phi_d) is the cyclotomic polynomial.
Modulo (p), (Phi_d) splits into distinct linear factors. Therefore
$$
v_p\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_d,F_{p,d,a})
=
\sum_{\xi\in\mu_d^{\mathrm{prim}}(\mathbf F_p)}
 v_p(F_{p,d,a}(\widetilde\xi)),
\tag{6.2}
$$
with Teichmüller lifts (widetildexi). One simple root of (F_\{p,d,a\}bmod p) among the primitive (d)-th roots contributes one factor (p); (k) roots contribute (p\^k). A root at one primitive (d)-th root does not force roots at the other (arphi(d)-1) points.
This is precisely the norm obstruction in polynomial language.
# 7. A general algebraic no-amplification lemma
The obstruction is not peculiar to the chosen formula.
**Lemma.** Let (K/mathbf Q) be a number field in which (p) splits completely:
$$
p\mathcal O_K=\mathfrak p_1\cdots\mathfrak p_e.
$$
For any chosen subset (Ssubseteq\{1,ldots,e\}), there exists (Ainmathcal O_K) such that
$$
v_{\mathfrak p_i}(A)=1\quad(i\in S),
\qquad
v_{\mathfrak p_i}(A)=0\quad(i\notin S).
\tag{7.1}
$$
**Proof.** Apply the Chinese remainder theorem modulo
$$
\prod_{i\in S}\mathfrak p_i^2
\prod_{i\notin S}\mathfrak p_i.
$$
Prescribe (Ainmathfrak p_isetminusmathfrak p_i\^2) for (iin S) and (Aequiv1pmod\{mathfrak p_i\}) otherwise. Then (7.1) holds. (square)
Hence integrality, complete splitting, Galois conjugacy, and an archimedean height bound do not by themselves force orbit-wide divisibility. Any amplification theorem for the actual Apéry traces would have to use an additional identity special to those traces.
# 8. Concrete Apéry counterexample at primitive order (16)
Modulo (17), the recurrence
$$
(r+1)^3b_{r+1}
=(34r^3+51r^2+27r+5)b_r-r^3b_{r-1}
\tag{8.1}
$$
reduces to
$$
(r+1)^3b_{r+1}
=(10r+5)b_r-r^3b_{r-1}.
\tag{8.2}
$$
Starting from (b_0=1,b_1=5), one obtains
$$
(b_0,\ldots,b_{16})
\equiv
(1,5,5,0,4,13,8,8,16,8,8,13,4,0,5,5,1)
\pmod{17}.
\tag{8.3}
$$
Thus
$$
Z_{17}=\{3,13\}.
\tag{8.4}
$$
For (r=3),
$$
\gcd(16,3)=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}(\omega_{17}^3)=16.
\tag{8.5}
$$
The primitive indices modulo (16) are
$$
1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15.
$$
Only (3) and (-3=13) vanish. Hence one primitive zero forces exactly the known reflection mate in this example, not the remaining six Galois-conjugate primitive characters.
Putting
$$
n=20,\qquad p=17,\qquad q=1,\qquad r=3
$$
gives
$$
\gcd(p-1,n-q)=\gcd(16,19)=1.
\tag{8.6}
$$
So this is an actual bad prime in the smallest-kernel regime of the fixed-(n) problem. It is a direct proof that the desired conjugate amplification statement is false for the Apéry family itself.
# 9. Gauss sums, Gross–Koblitz, and Hasse–Davenport
Introduce a nontrivial additive character (psi) of (mathbf F_p) and define
$$
\mathcal S_{p,d,a}
:=
\sum_{w\in\mathbf F_p^\times}
\sum_{x\in(\mathbf F_p^\times)^3}
\chi_a^{-1}(w)\psi\bigl(w\Phi(x)\bigr).
\tag{9.1}
$$
Summing first over (w) gives the exact factorization
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{p,d,a}
=g_p(\chi_a^{-1})H_{p,d,a},
}
\tag{9.2}
$$
where
$$
g_p(\chi)=\sum_{w\ne0}\chi(w)\psi(w)
$$
is a nonzero Gauss sum.
## 9.1 Gross–Koblitz removes, rather than amplifies, the Gauss factor
The [Gross–Koblitz formula](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971226) writes (g_p(chi_a\^\{-1\})) as a known power of a Dwork uniformizer times a (p)-adic gamma unit. Its valuation depends explicitly on the character exponent. Dividing (9.2) by that nonzero factor leaves exactly (H_\{p,d,a\}).
Therefore
$$
p\mid b_{ga}
$$
is cancellation in the unit-level Kummer trace after all predictable Gauss valuations have been removed. Small kernel supplies no additional (p)-adic valuation.
## 9.2 Why Hasse–Davenport does not help
Hasse–Davenport relations control products such as
$$
\prod_{\rho^m=1}g_p(\chi\rho)
$$
in terms of (g_p(chi\^m)) and explicit Gauss factors. They are multiplicative identities for the nonzero factors in (9.2).
The Apéry zero is vanishing of (H_\{p,d,a\}), an **additive trace**. Even if a finite hypergeometric expression expands (H_\{p,d,a\}) as a sum of products of Gauss or Jacobi sums, Hasse–Davenport acts termwise on the products and does not preserve cancellation of the entire sum.
This distinction is visible in the primary formalism:
- Greene, [*Hypergeometric functions over finite fields*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1987-0879564-8), defines finite hypergeometric values by summing Jacobi/Gauss products over an auxiliary character.
- Beukers–Cohen–Mellit, [*Finite hypergeometric functions*](https://doi.org/10.4310/PAMQ.2015.v11.n4.a2), interpret these values as Frobenius traces and point counts.
- Beukers, [*Fields of definition of finite hypergeometric functions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02863), explains the Galois action and integral normalizations.
None of these results states that residual vanishing for one primitive parameter propagates to all of its Galois conjugates at one fixed prime above (p).
# 10. Why reciprocity does not link different rational primes
Classical reciprocity for Gauss and Jacobi sums is effective when the character order and the underlying algebraic Hecke character are fixed while the rational prime varies.
The near-primitive Apéry regime has the opposite quantifiers:
$$
d_p\asymp p/g_p,
$$
so the character order, cyclotomic field, coefficient field, and trace object change with (p). There is no fixed algebraic integer (A_n) whose conjugates contain all the local traces
$$
H_{p,d_p,a_p}.
$$
Hasse–Davenport relates characters in one characteristic or in extensions of that characteristic. It does not relate (H_\{p,d,a\}) to (H_\{p',d',a'\}) for distinct rational primes (pne p').
One may multiply the individual norms, but their archimedean height is then approximately
$$
\sum_p\varphi(d_p)\log p,
\tag{10.1}
$$
which is vastly larger than the lower divisibility weight
$$
\sum_p\log p.
$$
Thus this produces no sublinear certificate.
The fixed-order cases are exactly the cases removed by the small-(d) argument. For a fixed rational slope (r/(p-1)), one may obtain a fixed hypergeometric motive or, in exceptional cases, a fixed modular form. Q341 audits those slices. They do not cover the residual (d_ptoinfty) regime.
# 11. The best unconditional residual bound
For (qle Q), the corresponding prime intervals are disjoint:
$$
\frac{n}{q+1}<p\le\frac nq.
\tag{11.1}
$$
Ignoring the Apéry and kernel conditions gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{np}}(n;Q,\varepsilon)
&\le
\sum_{q\le Q}
\sum_{n/(q+1)<p\le n/q}\log p\\
&=
\vartheta(n)-\vartheta\!\left(\frac{n}{Q+1}\right)\\
&=O(n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.2}
$$
There is no loss from overlap in (11.2): each (p\>sqrt n) determines one (q).
The Lucas congruence supplies the alternative certificate
$$
b_n\equiv b_qb_r\pmod p.
\tag{11.3}
$$
Hence every lower-digit bad prime divides (b_n), and
$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{np}}(n;Q,\varepsilon)
\le\log\operatorname{rad}(b_n)\le\log b_n=O(n).
\tag{11.4}
$$
The natural certificate (b_n) has exponential height. No identity in the Kummer/Gauss-sum formalism lowers that height to (e\^\{o(n)\}).
Therefore the requested proof-grade status is:
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{np}}(n;Q,\varepsilon)=O(n)
\quad\text{unconditionally,}
}
\tag{11.5}
$$
and an (o(n)) bound is open.
# 12. A precise sufficient theorem for the remaining route
A result of the following shape would close the bounded-quotient near-primitive range. Uniformly for (qle Q(n)), suppose
$$
\sum_{\substack{n/(q+1)<p\le n/q\\
       p\mid b_{n-qp}\\
       \gcd(p-1,n-q)<Q(n)n^\varepsilon}}
       \log p
\le
\eta(n)\frac{n}{q(q+1)},
\qquad \eta(n)\to0.
\tag{12.1}
$$
Then summing over (qle Q(n)) gives
$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{np}}(n;Q,\varepsilon)
\le
\eta(n)n
\sum_{q\ge1}\frac1{q(q+1)}
=o(n).
\tag{12.2}
$$
Equation (12.1) is not a consequence of complex Sato–Tate or ordinary Katz equidistribution. It is a same-characteristic residual nonordinary condition in a family whose Kummer character changes with (p). A suitable theorem would be a **marked crystalline Mellin dispersion** or horizontal non-alignment theorem.
An alternative sufficient route would construct integers (C_\{n,q\}ne0) such that every prime in (12.1) divides (C_\{n,q\}) and
$$
\sum_{q\le Q(n)}\log|C_{n,q}|=o(n).
\tag{12.3}
$$
The current norm constructions do not satisfy (12.3): their degrees and heights grow with (d_pasymp p), and they are different integers for different primes.
