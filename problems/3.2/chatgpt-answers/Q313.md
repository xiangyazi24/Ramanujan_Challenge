## 5. Why Katz's theorems do not presently apply

### 5.1 Convolution and Equidistribution

Katz's theorem starts from a fixed perverse sheaf on a fixed commutative algebraic group and studies the distribution of its Fourier or Mellin transforms as the character varies. It proves equidistribution using the Tannakian monodromy group of that fixed object. See Katz, Convolution and Equidistribution, especially Theorem 7.2.

The continuant problem is different:

- h is an iteration time, not a character of a fixed algebraic group;

- T_h(z) is an ordered product of matrices evaluated at z+1,\ldots,z+h-1;

- no fixed perverse sheaf is known whose Mellin transform at character h equals the indicator of N_h(z)=0;

- the complexity of the naive object grows with h.

Thus Katz's equidistribution theorem does not directly furnish the h-average.

### 5.2 Rigid local systems and Gauss–Kloosterman monodromy

Katz's rigid-local-system and Kloosterman-sheaf theories compute the monodromy of a fixed local system from its local singularities and then use Deligne's theorem to control its Frobenius traces. See:

- Katz, Rigid Local Systems;

- Katz, Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups.

They do not turn the graphs of all iterates \Phi^h into one bounded-degree variety.

### 5.3 Goursat–Kolchin–Ribet independence

The Goursat–Kolchin–Ribet mechanism can prove independence of several shifted copies of a fixed sheaf, provided those copies have bounded conductor and are not geometrically isomorphic up to rank-one twists. This is a powerful route for correlation sums.

For the present problem one would first need sheaves \mathcal F_h representing the continuant events with conductor bounded uniformly in h. The obvious \mathcal F_h have conductor \gg h, so the GKR criterion does not solve the prior geometrization problem.

## 6. Why Hall's big-monodromy criterion does not supply the missing estimate

Hall's theorem concerns a strictly compatible system of constructible sheaves of fixed rank on a fixed variety and gives sufficient conditions for geometric monodromy to contain the derived subgroup of the relevant symplectic or orthogonal group. See Chris Hall, Big symplectic or orthogonal monodromy modulo \ell.

The continuant family fails the starting hypothesis in the needed form:

- there is no known fixed finite-type base with coordinate h;

- the natural sheaf complexity grows with h;

- the event is a zero of a matrix coefficient, not merely the distribution of a Frobenius trace;

- even big monodromy of each fixed-h object would give a square-root error in p, which is too large if applied separately for every h.

Big monodromy is therefore at best one ingredient of a future joint-family theorem, not the theorem itself.

The equation

## Final answer to the three questions

### 1. Is there an existing Katz/FKM/Hall/Lang–Weil framework giving the average root bound?

No. Those frameworks require a fixed finite-type family or a fixed bounded-conductor sheaf. The continuant fibers have degree and conductor growing linearly with h, and no bounded-complexity joint (z,h) family is known.

### 2. Is there a vertical bounded-rank reformulation?

Yes: the rank-two translation difference module

or equivalently the skew product \Phi above. But this is a difference-dynamical object, not presently an ordinary bounded-conductor \ell-adic sheaf. Existing Deligne/Katz theorems do not control its finite-time return statistics uniformly for h growing with p.

### 3. Does the known large \mathrm{SO}_3 monodromy of the Apéry local system imply the bound?

No. It is monodromy in the Picard–Fuchs parameter and does not imply quantitative mixing of the coefficient-index translation cocycle. Large monodromy is compatible with highly structured deterministic short words.

The sharp verdict is therefore:

Among concrete attacks, the most realistic are the average shifted-GCD/resultant route and the full-cycle Artin–Schreier descent. Generic monodromy theory alone does not cross the p^{2/3} wall.
