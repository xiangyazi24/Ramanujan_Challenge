# The Apéry gcd is subexponential on density one — self-contained writeup

*Status (revised after hostile audit, 2026-07-20): the continuant and incidence
parts are complete; the support law rests on the explicit binomial formula (A)
for $a_n$ plus the Kummer valuation tables (K1)-(K2) below; the Wronskian
formula (W) and the height lemma (H) supply the shell estimates. No
counterexample to Theorem A was found in audit. The all-$n$ pointwise problem
remains open.*
*(Extracted and streamlined from `Q3.2_paper_draft.md`; verification data in the appendix.)*

## 0. Statement

Let $b_n$ and $a_n$ be the two Apéry $\zeta(3)$ solutions of
$$(n+1)^3 u_{n+1} = (2n+1)(17n^2+17n+5)\,u_n - n^3 u_{n-1},$$
with $(b_0, b_1) = (1, 5)$ (integers) and $(a_0, a_1) = (0, 6)$ (rationals,
$v_p(a_n) \ge -3\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor$), and let
$d_n = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)^3$, $G_n = \gcd(d_n a_n,\, d_n b_n)$.

**Theorem A.** For every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$\#\{n \le X : \log G_n > \varepsilon n\} = o(X)$.
Quantitatively, the average of $\log G_n$ over any dyadic shell $(N, 2N]$ is
$O(N^{2/3}\log N)$.

Write $P(t) = (2t+1)(17t^2+17t+5)$ throughout; $p$ always denotes a prime
$\ge 7$ unless stated otherwise (the primes $2, 3, 5$ contribute $O(\log^2 n)$
to $\log G_n$ via $v_p(G_n) \le v_p(d_n) + O(\log_p n) = O(\log n)$ and are
absorbed into the small-prime estimate).

## 1. Preliminaries

**1.1 Wronskian.** $W_n := a_{n+1}b_n - a_n b_{n+1} = 6/(n+1)^3$ (induction:
$W_0 = 6$, and the recurrence gives $W_{n} = \frac{n^3}{(n+1)^3} W_{n-1}$).
In particular $W_n \ne 0$, and for $\sqrt n < p \le n$, $p \ge 7$:
$$v_p(G_n) \le 6.$$
*Proof.* $G_n$ divides both $d_n a_n$ and $d_n b_n$, hence divides the
integer combination
$$d_{n+1}b_{n+1}\cdot(d_n a_n) - d_{n+1}a_{n+1}\cdot(d_n b_n)
= -\,d_n d_{n+1}\, W_n = -\,\frac{6\, d_n d_{n+1}}{(n+1)^3}.$$
Hence, for **every** prime $p$ and every $n \ge 1$,
$$v_p(G_n) \;\le\; 3\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor + 3\lfloor\log_p(n+1)\rfloor
+ v_p(6) - 3v_p(n+1). \tag{W}$$
For $\sqrt n < p \le n$, $p \ge 7$, formula (W) gives $v_p(G_n) \le 6$ in all
cases: $3+3$ if $p \nmid n+1$; $3+3-3 = 3$ if $p \,\|\, n+1$; and
$3+6-6 = 3$ in the edge case $n+1 = p^2$ (where $v_p(d_{n+1}) = 6$, so the
naive bound $v_p(d_{n+1}) \le 3$ fails — caught in audit). For $p \le \sqrt n$,
(W) gives $v_p(G_n) = O(\log_p n)$ **unconditionally**, replacing an earlier
appeal to an unproved upper bound on $v_p(a_n)$; hence
$\sum_{p \le \sqrt n} v_p(G_n)\log p \ll \sum_{p\le\sqrt n}\log n \ll \sqrt n$
by Chebyshev, and the primes $2,3,5$ are covered by the same formula.
$\square$

**1.2 Cleared continuants.** $N_0 = 0$, $N_1 = 1$,
$N_{r+1}(x) = P(x+r)N_r(x) - (x+r)^6 N_{r-1}(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$,
$\deg N_r = 3(r-1)$. Every solution of the local (cleared) recurrence at base
$x$ satisfies the solution formula
$$Q_h(x)\, y_h = N_h(x)\, y_1 - (x+1)^3 N_{h-1}(x+1)\, y_0, \qquad
Q_h = \prod_{i=2}^h (x+i)^3. \tag{1.2}$$

**1.3 Lucas congruence** (Gessel 1982): for $n = \sum d_i p^i$ in base $p$:
$b_n \equiv \prod_i b_{d_i} \pmod p$.

## 2. Five lemmas

**Lemma 1 (no consecutive zeros; both solutions).** For $p \ge 7$ and
$0 \le r \le p-2$: not both $b_r \equiv b_{r+1} \equiv 0 \pmod p$; and for
$1 \le q \le p-2$: not both $a_q \equiv a_{q+1} \equiv 0 \pmod p$
(here $a_q$ is $p$-integral since $q < p$).
*Proof.* The endpoints are immediate: $b_0 = 1$ and $a_1 = 6$ are units.
For interior indices $m$ with $1 \le m < p$ the coefficient $m^3$ is
invertible, so two consecutive zeros back-propagate: for $b$ down to
$b_0 = 1$, for $a$ down to $a_1 = 6$ — contradiction in both cases
($p \ge 7$). $\square$

**Lemma 2 (evaluation identities).**
(i) $N_r(-1) = ((r-1)!)^3\, b_{r-1}$.
(ii) $N_r(-x-r) = (-1)^{r-1} N_r(x-1)$ (reflection functional equation), hence
$N_r(-r) = (-1)^{r-1}((r-1)!)^3 b_{r-1}$.
*Proof.* (i) $v_r := N_r(-1)$ satisfies $v_1 = 1$, $v_2 = P(0) = 5$, and the
recurrence $v_{r+1} = P(r-1)v_r - (r-1)^6 v_{r-1}$; substituting
$v_r = ((r-1)!)^3 b_{r-1}$ reduces it to the Apéry recurrence at $n = r-1$.
(ii) $N_r$ is the determinant of the $(r-1)\times(r-1)$ tridiagonal matrix with
diagonal $P(x+1), \ldots, P(x+r-1)$ and off-diagonal entries
$(x+2)^3, \ldots, (x+r-1)^3$ (expansion along the last row reproduces the
defining recurrence). Under $x \mapsto -x-r$ the diagonal entries transform by
$P(-1-t) = -P(t)$ and the off-diagonal cubes are negated, in reversed order;
simultaneous row-and-column reversal preserves the determinant and the
negations contribute $(-1)^{r-1}$. (Equivalently: both sides satisfy the same
recurrence with matching initial data — a two-line induction.) Verified
exactly for $r \le 8$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (continuant lemma).** For $p \ge 7$ and $1 \le h < p$:
$N_h \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$. (For $p = 5$: direct check for $h < 5$.)
*Proof.* For $h = 1$, $N_1 = 1 \ne 0$. Let $h \ge 2$ and suppose
$N_h \equiv 0$: Lemma 2(i) gives $b_{h-1} \equiv 0$. The defining
recurrence evaluated at $x = 1-h$ gives $N_h(1-h) = P(0)N_{h-1}(1-h) =
5N_{h-1}(1-h)$, so ($p \ne 5$) $N_{h-1}(1-h) = N_{h-1}(-(h-1)) \equiv 0$, and
Lemma 2(ii) gives $b_{h-2} \equiv 0$ — contradicting Lemma 1. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (pair certificate).** Let $y$ be any solution of the Apéry
recurrence mod $p$ on a window $[m, m+h]$ with $m + h < p$, $y_m \equiv
y_{m+h} \equiv 0$ and $y_{m+1} \not\equiv 0$. Then $N_h(m) \equiv 0 \pmod p$.
*Proof.* Formula (1.2) at base $x = m$ with $y_0 = 0$:
$Q_h(m)\,y_{m+h} = N_h(m)\,y_{m+1}$. All factors $m + i$ ($2 \le i \le h$)
lie in $(m, m+h] \subseteq (0, p)$, so $Q_h(m)$ is a unit; $y_{m+h} \equiv 0$
and $y_{m+1} \not\equiv 0$ force $N_h(m) \equiv 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (zero-fiber bounds).** For $p \ge 7$:
$$Z_b(p) := \#\{r < p : b_r \equiv 0\} \le 3p^{2/3} + O(1), \qquad
Z_a(p) := \#\{1 \le q < p : a_q \equiv 0\} \le 3p^{2/3} + O(1).$$
*Proof.* Let $r_1 < \cdots < r_z$ be the zeros, $h_i = r_{i+1} - r_i$,
$\sum h_i < p$. By Lemmas 1 and 4, each consecutive pair with $h_i \le K$
makes $r_i$ a root of $\overline{N}_{h_i} \ne 0$ (Lemma 3) of degree
$3(h_i - 1)$; for fixed $h$ the bases are distinct, so at most
$\sum_{h \le K} 3(h-1) < \tfrac32 K^2$ pairs have gap $\le K$; fewer than
$p/K$ have gap $> K$. Take $K = p^{1/3}$. (The $a$-case is identical; the
$q = 0$ zero $a_0 = 0$ is excluded from the count and windows start at
$q \ge 1$.) $\square$

## 3. The support law

Write $\tilde a_n = p^3 a_n$ where relevant ($p$-integral for $n < p^2$).

**The explicit companion formula (A).** With
$T(N,K) = \binom NK^2\binom{N+K}K^2$ and
$$E(N,K) = H^{(3)}_N + \sum_{j=1}^K
\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{2j^3\binom Nj\binom{N+j}j},$$
the companion solution is
$$a_N = \sum_{K=0}^N T(N,K)\, E(N,K), \tag{A}$$
(Apéry; it satisfies the recurrence with $a_0 = 0$, $a_1 = 6$). Lemmas 6-7
below use (A); without it they are not self-contained (audit finding F1).

**Kummer valuation tables.** For $N = qp$, $K = \ell p + s$ ($0 \le s < p$),
$j = tp + u$ ($0 \le u < p$), $0 \le q < p$:
$$v_p\bigl(T(qp,K)\bigr) = 2\cdot\mathbf 1_{s>0} + 2\cdot\mathbf 1_{q+\ell\ge p},
\tag{K1}$$
$$v_p\tbinom{qp}{j} = \mathbf 1_{u>0}, \qquad
v_p\tbinom{qp+j}{j} = \mathbf 1_{q+t\ge p}. \tag{K2}$$

**Lemma 6 ($\alpha_p \equiv 6$).** $p^3 a_p \equiv 6 \pmod p$ for $p \ge 7$.
*Proof.* In (A) with $N = p$: for $1 \le k \le p-1$ the outer factor has
$v_p(T(p,k)) = 2$ by (K1); the harmonic part has $v_p \ge -3$ and the inner
correction $v_p \ge -1$, so after multiplying by $p^3$ every middle term has
$v_p \ge 3 + 2 - 3 = 2 > 0$ and dies. (The middle terms *do* carry the
$p^{-3}$ pole; they vanish only after the outer valuation is counted —
imprecision corrected in audit.) The
$k=0$ term contributes $p^3 H^{(3)}_p = p^3H^{(3)}_{p-1} + 1 \equiv 1$; the
$k=p$ term contributes $\binom{2p}p^2(1 + \tfrac14) \equiv 4 \cdot \tfrac54
= 5$ (Lucas: $\binom{2p}p \equiv 2$; the $j=p$ inner term gives
$p^3/(2p^3\binom{2p}p) \equiv \tfrac14$; inner $j < p$ terms have pole order
$\le 1$). Total $6$. $\square$

**Lemma 7 (boundary lemma).** $p^3 a_{qp} \equiv a_q \pmod p$ for
$1 \le q < p$, $p \ge 7$.
*Proof (termwise Kummer bookkeeping; no cancellation assumed).* By (K1),
$T(qp,K)$ is a $p$-unit exactly when $s = 0$ and $q + \ell < p$. Multiply the
$j$-th inner summand of $p^3 T(qp,K)E(qp,K)$ by $p^3$ and use (K2):
*if $u > 0$*, its valuation is $3 + v_p(T) - 1 - \mathbf 1_{q+t\ge p} \ge 1$,
so it vanishes mod $p$; *if $u = 0$*, the valuation is
$v_p(T) - \mathbf 1_{q+t\ge p}$, which is $0$ only in the unit outer case
($s = 0$, $q+\ell < p$, $t \le \ell$, whence $q + t < p$), where the term
reduces exactly to the $t$-th inner term of $E(q,\ell)$ (using
$(-1)^{tp-1} = (-1)^{t-1}$, $p$ odd). The harmonic part satisfies
$p^3H^{(3)}_{qp} \equiv H^{(3)}_q$ (only $p$-multiples survive), and
$T(qp,\ell p) \equiv T(q,\ell)$ by Lucas. This yields
$\sum_{\ell:\,q+\ell<p} T(q,\ell)E(q,\ell)$; restoring the range
$q+\ell \ge p$ costs nothing since there $v_p(T(q,\ell)) = 2$ against
$v_p(E(q,\ell)) \ge -1$. Hence $p^3a_{qp} \equiv a_q$. $\square$

**Lemma 8 (block law, $r$-direction).** For $0 \le r < p$, $1 \le q < p$:
$\tilde a_{qp+r} \equiv \tilde a_{qp}\, b_r \pmod p$.
*Proof.* Multiply the recurrence at $n = qp + r$ by $p^3$. For
$1 \le r \le p-2$ the coefficients reduce to the $b$-recurrence's. At entry
$r = 0$: the backward term carries $(qp)^3 = q^3p^3$ against the
$p$-integral $\tilde a_{qp-1}$, hence $\equiv 0$; and $(qp+1)^3 \equiv 1$
gives $\tilde a_{qp+1} \equiv P(0)\tilde a_{qp} = 5\tilde a_{qp}$. So both
sides satisfy the same nondegenerate recurrence with initial data
$\tilde a_{qp}(1, 5) = \tilde a_{qp}(b_0, b_1)$. $\square$

**Theorem B (support law).** For $p \ge 7$, $\sqrt n < p \le n$, write
$n = qp + r$ ($1 \le q < p$, $0 \le r < p$). Then
$$p \mid G_n \iff a_q\, b_r \equiv 0 \pmod p.$$
*Proof.* $v_p(d_n) = 3$ in this range, and $v_p(b_n) \ge 0$, so $p \mid G_n
\iff v_p(a_n) \ge -2 \iff \tilde a_n = p^3a_n \equiv 0 \bmod p$. By Lemmas
7-8, $\tilde a_n \equiv \tilde a_{qp} b_r \equiv a_q b_r \pmod p$.
$\square$

*(Historical note: the earlier one-channel claim "$p \mid G_n \Rightarrow
b_r \equiv 0$" is false outside $n/2 < p < n$ — counterexamples
$13 \mid G_{26}, G_{27}$; it was caught by hostile audit. In the top range
$q = 1$, $a_1 = 6$ is a unit and the old claim is recovered.)*

## 4. Proof of Theorem A

Fix the shell $(N, 2N]$. For $n$ in the shell:
$$\log G_n = \underbrace{\sum_{p \le \sqrt n} v_p \log p}_{S_1(n)}
+ \underbrace{\sum_{\sqrt n < p \le n} v_p \log p}_{S_2(n)}.$$

**Small primes.** $v_p(G_n) \le v_p(d_n) + \max(0, v_p(a_n)) =
O(\log_p n)$, so $S_1(n) = O(\pi(\sqrt n)\log n) = O(\sqrt n)$.

**Large primes.** By Theorem B, $p$ is bad iff $b_r \equiv 0$ (lower
channel) or $a_q \equiv 0$ (companion channel), and each bad prime costs
$\le 6\log 2N$ (depth, §1.1).

*Lower channel.* For fixed $p$, the shell integers with $n \bmod p \in
Z_b(p)$ number at most $Z_b(p)(N/p + O(1))$ — the endpoint term is
$O(Z_b(p))$ per prime. Summing with Lemma 5:
$$\sum_{p \le 2N} Z_b(p)\Bigl(\frac Np + O(1)\Bigr)
\ll N\sum_{p \le 2N} p^{-1/3} + \sum_{p \le 2N} p^{2/3} \ll N^{5/3}.$$

*Companion channel.* **Height Lemma (H).** For $q \ge 1$: $a_q > 0$, and the
reduced numerator $A_q$ satisfies $\log|A_q| = O(q)$.
*Proof.* $b_q > 0$ from the binomial formula, and the Wronskian gives
$a_{q+1}/b_{q+1} - a_q/b_q = 6/((q+1)^3b_qb_{q+1}) > 0$ with $a_0/b_0 = 0$,
so $a_q > 0$ for $q \ge 1$. Since $P(n)/(n+1)^3 \le 35$ and
$(n/(n+1))^3 \le 1$, the recurrence gives $|a_{n+1}| \le 35|a_n| + |a_{n-1}|$,
whence $|a_q| \le C^q$; the reduced denominator divides $d_q$ with
$\log d_q = O(q)$, so $|A_q| \le d_q|a_q| = e^{O(q)}$. $\square$
Consequently $p \mid A_q$ for at most $O(q/\log N)$ primes $p > \sqrt N$. Each such
$(q, p)$ poisons at most $p \le 2N/q$ shell integers (those with
$\lfloor n/p \rfloor = q$). Total incidences:
$$\sum_{q \le 2\sqrt{2N}} \frac{2N}{q} \cdot O\Bigl(\frac q{\log N}\Bigr)
= O\Bigl(\frac{N^{3/2}}{\log N}\Bigr).$$

**Assembly.** Summing both channels over the shell and multiplying by the
depth cost: $\sum_{N < n \le 2N} S_2(n) \ll (N^{5/3} + N^{3/2}/\log N)
\cdot \log N \ll N^{5/3}\log N$, so the shell average of $\log G_n$ is
$O(N^{2/3}\log N)$. Markov on the shell:
$\#\{n \in (N,2N] : \log G_n > \varepsilon n\} \le \#\{\log G_n >
\varepsilon N\} \ll_\varepsilon N^{2/3}\log N$; summing dyadic shells up to
$X$ gives $o(X)$. $\blacksquare$

## 5. What remains for the pointwise statement

**Lemma 9 (the companion channel is pointwise-free).** For **every** $n$:
$$\#\{p \in (\sqrt n, n] : a_{\lfloor n/p\rfloor} \equiv 0 \bmod p\}
= O(n^{2/3}).$$
*Proof.* For fixed $q \ge 1$, a bad $p$ with $\lfloor n/p \rfloor = q$ lies
in the interval $I_q(n) = (n/(q{+}1),\, n/q]$ of length $< n/q^2$, and must
divide the fixed nonzero integer $\operatorname{num}(a_q)$, which has
$O(q/\log n)$ prime factors exceeding $\sqrt n$. Hence the count for each
$q$ is at most $\min(n/q^2,\ Cq/\log n)$; summing over $q \le \sqrt n$ and
splitting at $q = n^{1/3}$ gives
$\sum_{q \le n^{1/3}} Cq/\log n + \sum_{q > n^{1/3}} n/q^2 = O(n^{2/3})$.
$\square$

The interval length itself forbids a companion-channel conspiracy — no
distributional input is needed. Consequently the **only** obstacle to the
full pointwise statement is the lower ($b$-)channel:
$\limsup \log G_n / n = 0$ (all $n$) follows from the falling-moment
bound (HM)$_8$ alone: with
$K_X(m) = \#\{p \in (X,2X] : m \bmod p \in Z_b(p)\}$,
$$\sum_{m < X^2}(K_X(m))_8 \ll X^{2+o(1)}\Bigl(\sum_p |Z_p|/p\Bigr)^8
\;\Longrightarrow\; \max_m K_X(m) \ll X^{11/12+o(1)} \Longrightarrow
\text{pointwise}.$$
*(Two chain repairs adopted from the hostile audit of this section, which
found the lemma itself correct and sharper than stated: (i) the boundary
block $X = \sqrt n$ has $m = n = X^2$ outside the moment hypothesis'
range $m < X^2$ — treat that single block trivially and apply (HM)$_8$
only to blocks with $X \ge 2\sqrt n$; (ii) the small-prime range
$p \le \sqrt n$ is controlled by the separate Wronskian depth estimate of
§4, which must be imported explicitly.)*
Empirically the moments sit at the exact independence scale
($Q_2 = 0.998$, $Q_3 = 0.94$ at $X = 1600$). **Corrected unconditional exponential bound (audit finding F2).** The reduced
denominator is $B_n = d_nb_n/G_n$, so $\log B_n = (3 + \log\alpha)n -
\log G_n + o(n)$ with $\alpha = 17+12\sqrt2$ (using $\log d_n = 3n + o(n)$ —
**omitted in an earlier version of this note, which therefore stated a wrong
constant**). From $|\zeta(3) - a_n/b_n| = e^{-2\log\alpha\, n + o(n)}$ and
$|\zeta(3) - A_n/B_n| \ge B_n^{-(\mu+\varepsilon)}$:
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log G_n}{n} \;\le\;
3 + \log\alpha - \frac{2\log\alpha}{\mu} \;=\; 5.24673\ldots
\qquad (\mu = \mu(\zeta(3)) \le 5.51389\ldots,\ \text{Rhin-Viola}),$$
against the trivial bound $3 + \log\alpha = 6.52549\ldots$ — nontrivial, but
far from subexponential.

**Theorem C (pointwise reduction to the middle part of $b_n$).** Up to the
proved corrections (Lemma 9's $O(n^{2/3})$ for the companion channel, depth
$\le 6$, small primes $O(\sqrt n)$):
$$\log G_n \;\asymp\; \sum_{\sqrt n < p \le n} \min(v_p(b_n), 6)\log p
\;+\; O(n^{2/3}\log n),$$
because by Lucas $p \mid b_{n \bmod p} \Rightarrow p \mid b_n$ (so every
lower-channel bad prime divides the single integer $b_n$; inclusion verified
on all sampled data), and conversely the support law makes every such prime
genuinely divide $G_n$. Hence the **full pointwise Problem 3.2 is
equivalent to**:
$$\text{(T)}\qquad \text{for every } n:\ \ \log\Bigl(\,(\sqrt n, n]\text{-part
of } b_n\Bigr) = o(n).$$
Measured: the ratio is $\le 0.071$ on samples to $n = 3000$ (vs. the trivial
$3.53$); a random integer of size $b_n$ would have middle part
$e^{O(\log n)}$. Note the analogous statement for $\binom{2n}n$ is **false**
(Kummer carries have positive density) — any proof must use the sparsity of
$Z_b(p)$, not general integer heuristics.

R1 *Depth*: Rowland-Yassawi-Krattenthaler mod-$p^2$ Lucas should give
$v_p(b_n) \le v_p(b_q) + v_p(b_r) + O(1)$ — reduces (T) to squarefree
counting (oracle round in flight).
R2 *Neighbor propagation*: dead (anti-propagation, recorded).
R3 *Geometric meaning of coefficient zeros*: none known (recorded).
R4 *Moment necessity*: first moments give density only; $L^\infty$ needs
either high moments (HM)$_k$ or a bootstrapping mechanism — structural.
R5 *Equivalence with denominator conjectures*: (T) $\iff$ "the Apéry
fraction is essentially reduced" $\iff$ the Apéry forms achieve their
nominal effective irrationality exponent for every $n$ — likely contact
with the Krattenthaler-Rivoal/Zudilin denominator-conjecture literature
(oracle round in flight).
R7 *(D4) refinement (Q138 round)*: with $|Z_p| \le 3p^{2/3}$ in hand,
$k = 4$ already suffices (any $k > 3$): the minimal missing statement is the
**four-prime small-CRT dispersion**
$$\text{(D4)}: \quad C_4(Y, X) \le C\, Y A_X^4 \quad (\sqrt Y < X \le Y),
\qquad A_X = \sum_{X<p\le2X}|Z_p|/p,$$
giving $K_X(n) \ll Y^{1/4}X^{2/3}$ and $W_{\mathrm{rad}}(n) \ll n^{11/12}$.
*Strengthened impossibility (decisive adversarial model):* the sets
$Z_p^{\mathrm{adv}} = \{m_0 \bmod p,\ p{-}1{-}(m_0 \bmod p)\}$ satisfy
every axiomatic property we have proved (size $\le 2 \ll 3p^{2/3}$,
palindromic, non-consecutive) yet concentrate all $\pi(X)$ primes at one
integer. Hence **no argument from cardinality + symmetry + spacing + CRT +
moments alone can prove (D4)**; the proof must use that all $Z_p$ come from
the single global sequence $b$. Also recorded from this round: the
valuation split $W = W_{\mathrm{rad}} + W_{\mathrm{mult}}$ (our application
needs only $W_{\mathrm{rad}}$, as Theorem C's $\min(v_p, 6)$ already
encodes); remainder-injectivity (each $r$ serves at most one $p > \sqrt n$);
pair-gcd routes are circular restatements of Lucas.
R8 *Localization (Q138 §1 — adopted)*: a pointwise counterexample
($W_{\mathrm{rad}}(n) \ge \varepsilon n$) forces a **bounded quotient
slice**: some $q \le 4/\varepsilon$ with $\gg_\varepsilon n/\log n$ primes
$p \in I_q$ dividing $b_{n-qp}$. The hard range is $p \asymp n$ with
$q = O(1)$, *not* $p \sim \sqrt n$; blocks $p \le \eta n$ can be discarded
at cost $O(\eta n)$. The $q = 1$ slice has an additive form: bad pairs
$(r, p)$ with $p \mid b_r$ satisfy $r + p = n$ — a counterexample is an
$n/\log n$-fold spike of the additive representation function of the
incidence set $\{(r, p) : p \mid b_r\}$ along $r + p = n$ (average
representation count $\sim 1/\log$; Goldbach-type upper-bound technology is
the natural comparison). Same counting recovers the density theorem with
$O_\varepsilon(N^{2/3})$ exceptions per shell.
R6 *Intermediate first-moment target*: even
$\sum_{p \in (X,2X]} |Z_b(p)| = O(X^{1+\varepsilon})$ is unproven (trivial
$X^{5/3}$; truth $\approx 0.09X$) — the sharpest currently-attackable
sub-target; any improvement propagates through every exponent in this
paper (oracle round in flight).

**R9 (multi-gap groundwork — measured, route assessed).** The continuant
family itself has a clean parity factor law (odd $h$: $N_h$ irreducible over
$\mathbb{Q}$; even $h$: forced central line $\times$ one irreducible;
verified $h \le 16$), trivial pairwise gcds (shifted exceptions = forced
central-root collisions, fully classified), and adjacent resultants that are
sixth powers up to sign (the $(x+h)^6$ propagation). But the sparsity data is
decisive negatively: for every $p \le 200$ **no** zero-triple of diameter
$\le 8$ exists in $Z_p$ at all — the multi-gap certificates constrain a
phenomenon that is already empty, confirming (with Q138's analysis) that the
fixed/multi-gap route cannot improve the $X^{5/3}$ first-moment wall. The
structural facts (esp. $N$-irreducibility) are retained as inputs for any
future resultant theory.

**Proposition D (dispersion reduces to exponential sums over the zero sets
— proved here).** For distinct primes $p, q$ put $N = pq$ and
$$E_p(h) := \sum_{r \in Z_b(p)} e(hr/p) \qquad (E_p(0) = |Z_b(p)|).$$
Then, with $\hat c$ the Fourier coefficients of $\mathbf 1_{[0,Y)}$ on
$\mathbb{Z}/N$,
$$\#\{(r,s) \in Z_p \times Z_q : \mathrm{CRT}(r,s) < Y\}
= \frac{Y}{N}|Z_p||Z_q| \;+\; \sum_{a \ne 0} \hat c(a)\,
E_p(a_1)\, E_q(a_2),$$
where $a \leftrightarrow (a_1, a_2)$ under $\mathbb{Z}/N \cong
\mathbb{Z}/p \times \mathbb{Z}/q$.
*Proof.* Expand the indicator in additive characters of $\mathbb{Z}/N$; by
CRT $a/N = a_1/p + a_2/q$, so $e(am/N) = e(a_1 r/p)\,e(a_2 s/q)$ **factors
across the primes**, and summing over $(r,s) \in Z_p \times Z_q$ separates.
$\square$
The same computation with $k$ primes gives the $k$-fold version, the main
term $Y\prod|Z_{p_i}|/\prod p_i$ and an error $\sum_{a\ne0}\hat c(a)
\prod_i E_{p_i}(a_i)$. Since $\sum_{a \ne 0}|\hat c(a)| \ll \log N$,
**(D$k$) follows from**: (i) a first-moment bound
$T_X = \sum_{X<p\le2X}|Z_b(p)| = O(X^{1+\varepsilon})$ (currently only
$X^{5/3}$ is proved, truth $\approx 0.09X$), together with (ii) any
cancellation in the $E_p(h)$ on average over $p$ (even a $\log$-power
saving suffices once (i) holds at the true size — with the *trivial*
$|E_p(h)| \le |Z_p|$ and the *true* first moment, the error already misses
the main term only by the completion factor $\log N$).
This isolates the analytic target sharply: **exponential sums over Apéry
zero sets**, the exact analogue of the Weyl sums for roots of polynomial
congruences in the Duke-Friedlander-Iwaniec/Hooley circle.

**Numerical determination of $E_p(h)$ (decisive experiment, completed).**
Over dyadic blocks $X = 500,\ldots,8000$ (exact zero sets, $p \le 3\times10^4$):
(1) *Weyl sums*: $\max_{1\le h\le50}|W_X(h)|/\sqrt{|S_X|} \in [2.47, 2.84]$ —
**bounded**, i.e. square-root cancellation; the trivial-bound ratio never
exceeds $0.297$. (2) *Discrepancy* of $\{r/p\}$:
$D_X\sqrt{|S_X|} \in [0.49, 0.95]$ — Kolmogorov-Smirnov scale
equidistribution. (3) *Two-prime dispersion constant* (the (D2) content):
actual/expected $= 0.99968 \pm 0.00660$, a $-0.05\sigma$ deviation; triples
$0.928 \pm 0.104$. (4) No arithmetic bias (uniformity $p = 0.62$;
Legendre-class $p \ge 0.086$; $p \bmod 24$ homogeneity $p = 0.42$), and the
pair-orbit Poisson$(1/2)$ model fits ($p = 0.52$).
So the mechanism Proposition D requires is present at exactly the needed
strength, and (D$k$) is quantitatively confirmed at its exact constant. The
proof problem is now precisely: **establish square-root cancellation, on
average over $p$, for the Apéry zero-set exponential sums** — the analogue of
what DFI/Hooley achieve for roots of polynomial congruences via
Kloosterman/spectral input, the missing ingredient being a parametrization of
$Z_b(p)$ in place of the explicit root parametrization.

**Exact bridge to the orbit sums (verified to $10^{-14}$).** Expanding the
zero indicator in additive characters, $\mathbf 1_{b_r \equiv 0} =
p^{-1}\sum_u e_p(u b_r)$, gives the identity
$$E_p(h) \;=\; \frac1p \sum_{u \not\equiv 0} S_p(u, h), \qquad
S_p(u,h) := \sum_{r=0}^{p-1} e_p\bigl(u\,b_r + h\,r\bigr),$$
connecting the zero-set Weyl sums to the **twisted Apéry orbit sums** studied
in §3.6 (the $u = 0$ term vanishes for $h \ne 0$). Measured:
$|S_p(u,h)|/\sqrt p$ has mean $\approx 0.93$-$1.02$ and max $\approx 3.0$-$3.3$
($p \le 401$, all $u$, $h \le 2$) — square-root size, exactly as for the
untwisted sums.
*Honest assessment:* the triangle inequality through this identity is far too
lossy ($|E_p(h)| \le \sqrt p$ versus the truth $\le |Z_p| \approx 2$): the
$u$-sum must cancel almost perfectly, which it does — it is Fourier inversion
of a sparse set. The identity's value is therefore **not** a direct bound but
a bridge: it exhibits the required cross-prime cancellation as a *bilinear*
form in $(p, u)$ whose entries are complete sums of square-root size, i.e.
precisely the setting where large-sieve/dispersion technology (Linnik,
Bombieri-Vinogradov style) operates without individual bounds. Whether an
appropriate bilinear inequality can be built here is the concrete next
question.

**Exact $L^2$ identity and the precise sufficient input (codex round,
verified).** Put $P = \sum_{p} |Z_p|$, $Q = \sum_p |Z_p|^2$ over
$p \in (X, 2X]$, and let
$$N(t) = \#\{p<q,\ r \in Z_p,\ s \in Z_q : \|r/p - s/q\| < t\},
\qquad R(t) = N(t) - t(P^2 - Q)$$
(no exact coincidences occur: $r/p = s/q$ with $p \ne q$ forces $r = s = 0$,
excluded since $b_0 = 1$). Stieltjes integration by parts gives the **exact
identity**
$$|W_X(h)|^2 = \sum_p |E_p(h)|^2 \;+\; 4\pi h \int_0^{1/2} R(t)\sin(2\pi h t)\,dt,$$
whence $|W_X(h)|^2 \le Q + 2\pi|h| \sup_t |R(t)|$. So the sufficient input for
$|W_X(h)| \ll P^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ is
$$Q \ll P^{1+2\varepsilon}, \qquad
\sup_{0\le t\le 1/2}\bigl|N(t) - t(P^2-Q)\bigr| \ll P^{1+2\varepsilon}/H
\tag{12}$$
uniformly for $|h| \le H$ — a **signed discrepancy** estimate, *not* the
random-scale upper bound $N(1/H) \ll P^2/H$ (a positive-kernel upper bound
provably cannot reach the target: it discards the very cancellation at issue).
*Measured:* $\sum_{h\le H}|W_X(h)|^2/(HP) = 1.016, 1.009$ at
$X = 1000, 2000$, $H = 5000$ — i.e. the off-diagonal is asymptotically
negligible exactly as (12) predicts, and $Q \approx 3P$ throughout; the
near-coincidence counts $N(1/H)$ match the random model to $1$-$20\%$ at all
tested scales. **Verdict on (12) (measured, decisive): the sup-form is FALSE — too strong.**
Direct computation of $R(t)$ over $t = 2^{-k}$ ($k \le 12$) and $t = c/P$
($X = 1000, 2000, 4000, 8000$): the bound $|R(t)| \le P\,t\,P^{0.1}$ fails at
$12$-$14$ of $16$ tested $t$ per block, while the random-fluctuation scale
$|R(t)| \asymp P\sqrt t$ fits (failures $3$-$9$, ratios order one). At
microscopic $t = c/P$ the sup-form allows only $cP^{0.1}$ where ordinary
counting noise is $\sqrt{cP}$. Correspondingly $\max_{h\le H}|W_X(h)|^2/P$
grows with $H$ ($10.4$-$16.5$ at $H = 10^4$, consistent with the
$\asymp \log H$ maximum of a Gaussian family), while the *average* stays at
$1.0$. **So only the averaged (mean-square) statement is true**, and any
proof strategy must use $L^2$-averaged input, not a uniform pointwise
discrepancy bound. (This corrects the target stated one revision earlier;
recorded as a dead end of its own.)

*Accounting for the four-fold route with the correct scaling.* Summing the
Fourier error over prime $4$-tuples factors into $\prod_{i}W_X(a_i)$, so with
$|W| \asymp \sqrt P$ the error is $\asymp P^2$ times completion logarithms,
against a main term $Y A_X^4 \asymp P^4/X^2$. With the true $P \approx 0.09X$
the ratio is $X^2/P^2 \approx 120$ times a log — i.e. the route misses by
**constant and logarithmic factors, not by a power**. That is encouraging but
not conclusive: closing it requires exploiting the average (not maximal) size
of $|W|$ and the decay of $\hat c$, which is the next concrete calculation.

### 5.1 Structural diagnosis of the obstruction (oracle round R1)

**Where the difficulty really lives.** Combining our Hasse identity with
$A_{p-1}(E_t) \equiv a_p(E_t) \bmod p$ gives
$$b_r \equiv -\sum_{t \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times} c(t)\,a_p(E_t)^2\,t^{-r}
\pmod p,$$
i.e. $Z_b(p)$ is the vanishing set of the **mod-$p$ Mellin transform of the
squared Frobenius trace** along Beukers' $\Gamma_0(6)$ family. This looks like
Katz's convolution/equidistribution setting but is **not**: Weil/$\ell$-adic
technology yields archimedean cancellation for complex-valued trace functions,
whereas here $a_p$ enters *reduced mod $p$* and the transform is
$\mathbb{F}_p$-valued. By Dwork's congruences $t \mapsto F^{<p}(t) \bmod p$ is
the trace function of a **unit-root $F$-crystal**, and unit-root objects carry
no weight theory — no Weil bound, no monodromy equidistribution. Consequences:
- $|S_p(u,h)| \asymp \sqrt p$ is measurable but not provable by current
  technology (same class as $\sum_a e_p(\mathrm{Kl}_p(a))$);
- **the exponent $2/3$ in $|Z_b(p)| \le 3p^{2/3}$ is the universal
  polynomial-method barrier** for counting zeros of a trace function modulo its
  own $p$ — not an artifact of our continuant argument, and continuant/resultant
  certificates are provably capped at this scale (they control *clustered*
  zeros; isolated zeros cost $p/H$ regardless of how many gap polynomials are
  stacked);
- no bounded-complexity parametrization of $Z_b(p)$ can exist in the
  DFI sense: DFI's $\nu$ is cut out by a fixed-degree equation, our $r$ by a
  degree-$p$ one; algorithmically the fastest known evaluation of a single
  $b_r \bmod p$ is Bostan-Gaudry-Schost at cost $\sqrt p$.

**Classification.** Since $p \mid b_{n\bmod p} \iff p \mid b_n$ here, the
pointwise target says the $(\sqrt n, n]$-part of the *single integer* $b_n$ is
subexponential. The analogous statement fails for $2^n - 1$ (divisibility
sequence) and for $\binom{2n}n$ (Kummer carries); what must save the Apéry
sequence is that it is **not** of divisibility type — big-monodromy, non-CM
input. This places the full pointwise problem in the **Lang-Trotter/horizontal
class**, at or beyond the current frontier. The realistic program is therefore:
isolate a named horizontal non-concentration hypothesis $H$ for the unit-root
family, prove $H \Rightarrow$ Problem 3.2 along an audited critical curve, and
bank unconditional partials.

**Critical-curve audit (dispatched R1, completed) — the first moment is NOT
the bottleneck.** Redoing the reduction with free exponents ($P \ll X^\theta$
for the first moment, $|W| \ll P^{1-\delta}$ in the averaged sense) gives an
explicit sufficient region. The two reference points:
$$(\theta, \delta) = (1, \tfrac12) \Rightarrow \eta < \tfrac12; \qquad
(\theta, \delta) = (\tfrac53, \tfrac12) \Rightarrow \eta < \tfrac16,$$
with thresholds $\theta_* = 2$ at $\delta = 1/2$ and $\delta_* = 2/5$ at
$\theta = 5/3$. **Consequently the first-moment exponent we have already
proved ($\theta = 5/3$) suffices**, provided the averaged square-root
cancellation holds — dyadic summation then yields
$\sum_{\text{bad }p}\log p \ll n^{1-\eta}(\log n)^2 = o(n)$, the full
pointwise theorem. This retires "improve the first moment to $X^{1+\varepsilon}$"
as a prerequisite: **the single remaining input is the averaged cancellation**
$\sum_{h \le H}|W_X(h)|^2 \ll H\,P^{2-2\delta}$ with any $\delta > 2/5$
(measured: $\delta = 1/2$ exactly, ratio $\to 1.009$).

*Coordinator's self-audit (flagged, R1).* The critical curve treats $\theta$
and $\delta$ as **independent** inputs, and in that form the conclusion
"$\theta = 5/3$ suffices" is correct. But the natural route to $\delta$ runs
through the near-coincidence count $N(t) \approx P^2 t$, whose heuristic
evaluation needs the *density* $|Z_p|/p$: with the **true** $|Z_p| \approx 2$
one gets $N \approx 4X^{6/5}/\log^2 X \approx P^{6/5}$ (exactly the required
size), whereas inserting the **proved** $|Z_p| \le 3p^{2/3}$ gives
$\approx X^{2.53}$, far above the target. So a proof of $\delta > 2/5$ by pair
counting would implicitly require a better first moment than $\theta = 5/3$.
The two hypotheses are jointly, not independently, constrained; whether some
other route to $\delta$ avoids the first moment (e.g. the bootstrap/completion
system, which does not obviously pass through $N(t)$) is the sharpest open
question of this round.

**Large-sieve route: applied correctly, but DOES NOT close (verified, R2).**
The spacing claim and the Montgomery-Vaughan application are valid: distinct
points $r/p \ne s/q$ differ by $\ge 1/(4X^2)$ (as $rq - sp \ne 0$, using
$0 \notin Z_b(p)$), giving unconditionally
$$M_2(H) \le \Bigl(1 + \frac{4X^2}{H}\Bigr)P .$$
But the closure attempt fails on the **frequency structure**, and two exponent
slips in the attempt are recorded for the register:
1. *The decisive gap.* Completion modulo $N = p_1p_2p_3p_4$ of an interval of
   length $Y \sim X^2$ does admit global frequencies up to $N/Y \sim X^2$, but
   the CRT representative is $a \equiv \sum_i r_i N_i c_i$, $c_i = N_i^{-1}
   \bmod p_i$, so the $h$-th global character factors as
   $$e_N(ha) = \prod_i e_{p_i}(h\,c_i\,r_i)
   \;\Longrightarrow\; \sum_{\text{roots}} = \prod_i S_{p_i}(h c_i),$$
   **not** $W(h)^4$ and not any expression in a common scalar frequency: each
   twist $c_i$ depends on the other three primes and cannot be absorbed before
   the tuple sum. Pairwise completion gives $S_p(hq^{-1})S_q(hp^{-1})$ — the
   same inverse-twisted obstruction (and the reason the inverted-argument sums
   $\check E_p$ appear at all). So $H \sim X^2$ is available as a *cutoff*, but
   has not been shown admissible as the $H$ of the **scalar** moment $M_2(H)$.
2. $M_2(H) \ll P^{6/5}$ is the *endpoint* $\delta = 2/5$, not $\delta > 2/5$.
3. The inference "$P \le X^{5/3}$ hence $H \ge X^{5/3}$ suffices" is backwards:
   the requirement is $H \gg X^2/P^{1/5}$, which is *harder* for smaller $P$
   ($P \sim X$ needs $H \gg X^{1.8}$).

**Corrected target.** What must be bounded is not the scalar $M_2(H)$ but the
*inverse-twisted* multilinear average
$$\frac1H\sum_{h \le H}\;\Bigl|\sum_{p} S_p(h\,c_p)\Bigr|^{\,\cdot}
\quad\text{with prime-dependent twists } c_p,$$
i.e. a large sieve for the point set $\{c_p r/p\}$ whose *twisted* spacing is
not controlled by the Farey argument. This is precisely a
multi-dimensional/twisted large-sieve problem, and it is the sharp form of the
remaining gap.

**Twisted-object measurement (R3, decisive on geometry).** The key structural
point: $e_p(hq^{-1}r) = e\bigl((q^{-1}r \bmod p)/p \cdot h\bigr)$, so the twist
merely *permutes* the numerator set $Z_p \mapsto q^{-1}Z_p \pmod p$ — the
twisted points keep denominator $p$ and per-$p$ spacing $\ge 1/p$. But the
**union over the auxiliary prime $q$** does not stay sparse: measured, the set
$V_p = \bigcup_{q\ne p} (q^{-1}Z_p \bmod p)/p$ fills a *substantial* fraction
of $\{0,1/p,\ldots\}$ (mean coverage $\approx 7\%$, rising with $X$), so the
multiplicity-over-twists is a genuine geometric obstruction to a single Farey
large sieve. On the other hand the twisted *bilinear* sum
$\frac1H\sum_{h\le H}\bigl|\sum_{p\ne q}S_p(hq^{-1})S_q(hp^{-1})\bigr|^2$
**does exhibit strong cancellation** — its second moment sits at the
random-phase diagonal scale, not the square of the atom count. So the
cancellation we need is genuinely present in the data; what is missing is a
*proof mechanism*: the Bombieri-Iwaniec double large sieve proxy is respected
but far too lossy to certify closure. This matches the R1 diagnosis exactly:
the cancellation is of unit-root/horizontal type, visible numerically,
inaccessible to the current unconditional toolbox.

**Bootstrap constant — RETRACTED as an artifact (R2).** The measured
$\lambda_X \approx 0.51$ carries no information: under the Gaussian null model
($W(h)$ complex Gaussian of variance $P$) one has $S/\sqrt P \approx
\sqrt{\log H} \approx 2.9$ at $H = 5000$ and $D/\sqrt P = \sqrt3 \approx 1.73$,
predicting $\lambda \approx 1 - 1.73/3.5 \approx 0.50$ **with no free
parameters** — matching the measurement exactly, and it would read $0.5$ for
*any* generic point set (drifting to $1$ like $1 - c/\sqrt{\log H}$). Moreover
the bootstrap cannot close in principle: a single-completion inequality has the
shape $K \le c(H)(K^2+P)^{1/2}$ with $c(H) \ge 1$ (the large-sieve constant
never beats the diagonal), which is tautological, and $D = \sqrt{Q/P}$ smuggles
in the *true* second moment ($Q \approx 3P$; the proved bound gives only
$Q \le 3X^{2/3}P$). The informative replacement is the **Gram operator norm**
$\|G - I\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, $G_{(p,r),(p',r')} = H^{-1}\sum_{h\le H}
e(h(r/p - r'/p'))$, for which the large sieve *is* the statement
$\|G\| \le 1 + 4X^2/H$.

**The closure criterion (convergent, R2).** Writing $H = X^A$, the large-sieve
bound gives $M_2(H)^{1/2} \le 2X^{(\theta+2-A)/2}$, so the four-prime
inequality closes **unconditionally iff $A > \theta$**. With the proved
$\theta = 5/3$: any admissible $A > 5/3$ suffices (in particular $H = 4X^2$
gives $M_2 \le 5P$, i.e. $\delta = 1/2$, $\kappa = 5/6$, $\eta = 1/6$ — the
full pointwise theorem); whereas $A = 1$ gives only
$\delta = (\theta-1)/(2\theta) < 1 - 1/\theta$ and never closes. Intra-$p$
facts (non-consecutive zeros, palindromy) are irrelevant — the binding spacing
is the cross-prime $1/(4X^2)$. Everything therefore reduces to a **bookkeeping
question**: the maximal $H$ admissible in the four-prime derivation. Danger
point identified: the $m$-phase elimination step (pairing $e(hm/p)$-twists by
Cauchy-Schwarz into the $m$-free $M_2$) — if any step uses exact orthogonality
mod $p$, then $H$ is chained to $X$ and the route dies; if the argument uses
the finite geometric detector (error $\ll PX/H$, harmless at $H = X^2$), then
$H$ is free. Success criterion for the whole program:
$$\boxed{\ \text{maximal admissible } A \ (\text{bookkeeping})
\;>\; \text{minimal provable } \theta \ (\text{arithmetic}).\ }$$

**Live routes (ranked, R1).**
1. *Requirement audit*: redo the reduction with free exponents — first moment
   $P \ll X^\theta$, cancellation $|W| \ll P^{1-\delta}$ — and compute the
   critical curve $\delta > f(\theta)$. Near-zero cost, may reshape the target
   (dispatched).
2. *Bootstrap test*: completion produces the inverted-argument sums
   $\check E_p(k) = \sum_{r \in Z_p} e_p(k\bar r)$; the resulting system has
   the shape $S \le D + \lambda S$ and **closes iff $\lambda < 1$**, a constant
   measurable on existing data (dispatched). Cheap and decisive either way.
3. *Central-window parametrization*: for $r$ near $(p-1)/2$, $b_r$ is a two-term
   combination $\alpha_k A + \beta_k B$ of the seeds $A = b_{(p-1)/2} \equiv
   a_p(f_4)$ and $B = b_{(p-3)/2}$ with universal continuant coefficients; if
   $B$ also has a modular formula, the window part of $Z_b(p)$ *is*
   parametrized and its cross-prime cancellation becomes horizontal
   non-concentration for $a_p(f_4) \bmod p$ (effective Sato-Tate via
   Newton-Thorne). Partial but genuinely new; first move is a numerical
   regression for $b_{(p-3)/2} \bmod p$.
4. *Elliptic-level moments*: at the symmetric-square level $b = \gamma * \gamma$
   with $\gamma$ = coefficients of the supersingular polynomial $g_p$; power
   sums of supersingular parameters are Brandt-matrix traces, and averages over
   $p$ run through Eichler-Selberg to Hurwitz class numbers and
   Kloosterman/Salié sums — the only honest trace-formula bridge in the problem,
   the right substrate for proving the first-moment/intensity statements.
5. *G-function route*: **dropped**. André-Chudnovsky-Bombieri controls heights
   and denominators (we already have $d_n^3a_n \in \mathbb Z$) and is
   structurally silent about which large primes divide numerators; the
   Dwork/Beukers-Vlasenko content is exactly our (G1)-(G3); and the
   irrationality-measure feedback is vacuous in the useful direction (§5).

### 5.2 Banked unconditional partial: the central-window lemma (R3)

**Lemma W.** Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and $W = W(X)$. The contribution to
$\log G_n$ from primes $p \in (X, 2X]$ with $n \bmod p$ within $W$ of the
center $(p-1)/2$ is $\ll X\, W \log X$; in particular, taking $W = X^{1-\varepsilon}$,
the total over all dyadic blocks up to $n$ is $\ll n^{1-\varepsilon}(\log n)^2 = o(n)$.
*Proof.* $n \bmod p \in [(p-1)/2 - W,\ (p-1)/2 + W]$ iff $p \mid 2n+1+2j$ for
some $|j| \le W$ (verified: $2n+1+2j = p(2\lfloor n/p\rfloor + 1)$ when
$j = (p-1)/2 - (n\bmod p)$). Each of the $2W+1$ fixed nonzero integers
$2n+1+2j \sim 2n \le 2X^2$ has at most two prime factors in $(X, 2X]$ (three
would exceed $X^3 > 2X^2$). So at most $2(2W+1)$ such primes exist, each
contributing $v_p(G_n)\log p \le 6\log 2X$. $\square$
This is unconditional and needs no modularity input (the exact-center
formula $b_{(p-1)/2} \equiv a_p(f_4)$ touches only the $\le 2$ primes dividing
$2n+1$ per $n$, so effective Sato-Tate is the *wrong*, negligible tool here).
The same divisor argument handles a window of width $X^{1-\varepsilon}$ around
**any** center linear in $p$ — load-bearing in Route P below.

### 5.3 Two live unconditional routes (R3 — neither blocked by the audit)

The large-sieve audit killed the *scalar* route but left two openings, and one
of them challenges the premise that the pointwise problem needs fine-scale
horizontal equidistribution at all.

**Route P (profile + scale-splitting).** If $\log|b_r|$ has a macroscopic
Lévy-type profile $\log|b_r| = p\,\Phi(r/p) + (\text{deviation})$ with
large-deviation control (exceptional set $\{|{\rm dev}| > \varepsilon p\}$ of
size $\ll p^{1-\delta(\varepsilon)}$, structured on continued-fraction
cylinders), then $\log G_n$ splits by scale:
- *main term* $\sum_p p\,\Phi(\{n/p\})$ needs only **macroscopic**
  equidistribution of $\{n/p\}$ over $p \in (X,2X]$ — Weyl sums
  $\sum_p e(kn/p)$ for small $k$, provable by van der Corput's second-derivative
  test ($\sum_{m\sim X}e(kn/m) \ll (kX)^{1/2}$) + Vaughan/Fouvry-Iwaniec over
  primes; *never the obstruction*;
- *deviations on good residues* $\le \varepsilon\sum_p p = o(\text{main})$;
- *bad residues* need only a one-sided **sparse-avoidance** statement (much
  weaker than equidistribution): shallow cylinders sit near small-denominator
  $a/q$ where $n\bmod p \in [ap/q \pm W] \iff p \mid (qn - m)$, killed
  unconditionally by divisor counting (Lemma W's mechanism) for $q \le X^{\delta/2}$;
  deep cylinders have exponentially small measure.
So the fine-scale problem enters $\log G_n$ only at relative order $\varepsilon$
— the pointwise theorem plausibly does **not** require solving the
Lang-Trotter-class problem. *(Premise to test numerically first.)*

**Route Q ($q$-van der Corput for the factorable modulus).** Keep the
*signed* per-tuple sum $T = \sum_{h\le H}w_h\prod_i S_{p_i}(hc_i)$ (absolute
values inside would forfeit everything). This is an incomplete sum of a rank-4
CRT product at length $\sim N^{1/2}$, $N = \prod p_i \sim X^4$ — the
square-root barrier for *prime* modulus, but $N$ **factors** into four
comparable pieces, the Graham-Ringrose/Heath-Brown/Polymath8a setting where
the $q$-analogue van der Corput $A$-process beats $\sqrt{}$. Shifting $h$ by
multiples of $p_1$ annihilates the $i{=}1$ twist ($p_1c_1 \equiv 0$), and the
substitution $k \mapsto c^{-1}k$ **relocates the shift so the twist evaporates
from the Weil inputs**: the autocorrelation
$\sum_k S_p(k{+}a)\overline{S_p(k)} = p\sum_{r} e_p(a'\bar r)$ is an incomplete
Kloosterman sum, $\ll p^{3/2}\log p$ by Weil — **no bounded-conductor
hypothesis, no sheaf formalism** (crucially, $S_p(\cdot)$ is *not* a
bounded-conductor trace function; only these explicit Cauchy-Schwarz
completions are used). Rough one-step bookkeeping: $T \ll X^{7/4+\varepsilon}$
(saving $X^{1/4}$), improvable by a second $A$-step; uniformity in the twists
$c_i$ is free. Whether it closes 3.2 depends only on the calibration $\delta_0$
(the minimal per-tuple saving that suffices).

**R3 pre-check verdict (decisive).**
- *Route P is dead as stated.* The integer sizes are smooth:
  $\log b_r = r\log\alpha - \tfrac32\log r + c + o(1)$, so the "profile" is

### 5.4 The master identity — the pointwise problem is Diophantine, not spectral (R4)

**Master identity (verified exactly, $|W| = 8$ nontrivial case, error $4\times10^{-14}$).**
The twists $c_i = (N/p_i)^{-1}\bmod p_i$ are *exactly* the CRT recombination
coefficients, so the four-fold twisted product collapses:
$$\prod_{i=1}^4 S_{p_i}(hc_i) = \sum_{x \in W} e_N(hx), \qquad
W = \{x \bmod N : x \bmod p_i \in Z_{p_i}\ \forall i\},\quad N = \textstyle\prod p_i,$$
a bijection $r \leftrightarrow x(r)$ with $|W| = \prod|Z_{p_i}| \le z^4$
(usually $0$ or $1$ under Poisson$(1/8)$). Hence the signed per-tuple sum is a
**sparse trigonometric polynomial in $\le z^4$ frequencies**:
$$T = \sum_{x \in W}\widehat w(x/N), \qquad
\widehat w(\theta) = \sum_{h \le H} w_h\, e(h\theta),$$
and for a BV/smooth weight,
$$\boxed{\ |T| \le C_w \sum_{x \in W}\min\bigl(H,\ N/|x|'\bigr),\quad
|x|' := \min(x, N-x).\ } \tag{$*$}$$
**Consequences (this replaces the entire spectral picture).**
- *No Weil/Kloosterman input exists.* The completed autocorrelation is
  $p\,S_p(a)$ (verified §5.3), a point mass on $O(1)$ frequencies, never a
  Kloosterman sum — there is nothing of positive dimension for Weil to act on.
  The R3 "$\bar r$" claim is **withdrawn**. The unit-root barrier is therefore
  *not* the obstruction to the pointwise theorem; it only blocks the pointwise
  single-prime bound $|S_p| \ll \sqrt p$, which $(*)$ does not need.
- *The $q$-van der Corput $A$-process is strictly dominated by $(*)$* (every
  gain it produces is conditional on the same resonance conditions, for more
  frequencies with less margin) — dropped.
- *The problem is Diophantine:* $|T| \ll z^4 X^{2-\delta_0}$ holds **iff every
  $x \in W$ has $|x|' \ge X^{2+\delta_0}$** — i.e. iff no small integer
  $x$ reduces into all four zero sets simultaneously.

**Reduction to a single counting statement.** Per-tuple uniformity is *false*
(resonant tuples with $|x|' \le X^{2+\delta_0}$ genuinely exist, giving
$|T| \sim H$), so the calibration is redone in **summed-over-tuples** form.
The dyadic tail of $(*)$ over $|x|' \ge X^{2+\delta_0}$ is **free
unconditionally** (trivial $c(t) \le z^4\cdot\#\text{tuples}$ gives tail
$\ll z^4 X^{6-\delta_0}$), so everything reduces to the head:

$$c(X^{2+\delta_0}) \ll X^{4-\delta_0-\varepsilon}, \quad
c(t) := \#\{(x, \{p_1,\ldots,p_4\}) : 0 < |x|' \le t,\ x \bmod p_i \in

Given Lemma R, $\sum_{\text{tuples}}|T| \ll X^{6-\delta_0}$, whence

**Status of Lemma R.** $c(t)$ is essentially the **fourth moment** of the
hit-count $h(x) = \#\{p \in (X,2X] : x\bmod p \in Z_p\}$ over $x \le t$;

### 5.7 The Lucas funnel: A_3 is a moment of omega(b_x); two banked theorems (breakthrough round, converged)

Both breakthrough oracles (motivic linearization §5.6, average-over-p) converge:
averaging over p does **not** unlock A_3, and the reason is provable, not a

unconditional theorems.

**The reformulation (Gessel-Lucas cancels everything).** By Lucas
$p\mid b_{x\bmod p} \Rightarrow p\mid b_x$, so $h(x) \le \omega_{(X,2X]}(b_x)$
for all $x$, and $A_3 \le \sum_{x\le t}\binom{\omega_{(X,2X]}(b_x)}{3}$. Combined
with the reverse necessity ($A_3 \ll X^{3-\kappa} \Rightarrow$ pointwise

fibers $x\bmod p = x$): **$A_3$ is equivalent, up to exponents, to**
$$\text{(}\Omega_3\text{)}\qquad \sum_{x\le X^{2+\delta}}
\omega_{(X,2X]}(b_x)^3 \ll X^{3-\kappa}.$$
The entire CRT / exponential-sum / dispersion superstructure evaporates under
Lucas; the crux is **100% multiplicative arithmetic** — "Apéry numbers have few
large prime factors in a dyadic window", the same species as bounding
$\omega(2^n-1)$ in a window (open; Bugeaud-Corvaja-Zannier subspace methods need
$S$-unit structure $b$ lacks). $\Omega_3$ is heuristically trivial
($\mathbb{E}\,\omega_{(X,2X]}(b_x) \approx \log2/\log X$, Poisson tails give

This is the right target to hand any solver: **third moment of
$\omega_{(X,2X]}(b_x)$**, not $S_p$ cancellation.

**Theorem A' (small-gap paucity of Apéry zeros — unconditional, verified).**
For $1 \le g \le X^{1-\varepsilon}$:
$\#\{(p,r): p\in(X,2X],\ r,r+g\in Z_b(p)\} \ll g\,X$.
*Proof.* A gap-$g$ zero pair forces the transfer-matrix $(1,2)$-entry $u_g(r)$
(equivalently the continuant $N_g(r)$, §2) to vanish mod $p$; $u_g$ is a

small-gap pair counts are near-zero, far below $gX$.) This is a genuine
power-saving average-over-p estimate — and structurally the *ceiling* of the
recurrence method: it sees pairs at a fixed gap (second-moment / additive-energy
of small gaps) but is blind to the first moment (no pairing) and to large gaps
(height grows linearly in $g$).

slice of $A_3$ *is* a Lang-Trotter problem, open on $p$-average.

**The honest minimal core (surviving crack).** The reduction to $A_3$ discarded
the $h$-oscillation of the master identity. Keeping it, the four-prime route
reduces to
$$\text{(C1) } \sum_{p\in(X,2X]}|Z_b(p)| \ll X^{1+o(1)} \ \text{ and }\
\text{(C2) } W_h = \sum_{p}\sum_{r\in Z_b(p)} e(hr/p) \ll X^{1-\kappa_1}

via $h$-dispersion with the well-factorable modulus $pqs$ (three factors

**Why DFI/Lang-Trotter don't transfer (structural).** DFI beats thin fibers
($O(1)$ roots/prime) only because the incidence variety

cancellation. $\{(p,r):p\mid b_r\}$ has no known parametrization — the

irony: the Poisson-random appearance that makes $A_3$ heuristically obvious is

**The $r$-axis is provably the barrier axis (measured).** The character-axis complete sum $S(\lambda)=\sum_{r<p}e_p(\lambda b_r)$ does \emph{not} obey a uniform Weil bound: measured $\max_\lambda|S(\lambda)|\asymp\sqrt{p\log p}$ ($\max_\lambda|S|/\sqrt p$ grows $3.02\to3.67$ for $p=101\to3203$; $/\sqrt{p\log p}\approx1.3$ constant) --- unit-root / max-of-$p$-Gaussians growth, not $O(\sqrt p)$. So the $r$-sum (character/Teichm\"uller axis, where $b_r$ is the unit root) is exactly the barrier axis; routing the mass $\sum_p|Z_p|$ or $W_h$ through it gives $\sum_p\sqrt{p\log p}\asymp X^{3/2}\sqrt{\log X}$ at best, and only conjecturally. The geometric/$y$-axis sum is Weil-bounded (Q170), but the $r\leftrightarrow y$ Fourier duality trades one for the other at the cost of exactly $\sqrt p$ --- ``detection costs what oscillation gains'', a measured no-go for the naive routing. Whether a non-naive routing lands $W_h$/$C_1$ on the $y$-axis is the open frontier; the barrier is precisely this $r/y$ duality.
