# A star-sum proof of the missing H6 lemma

Date: 2026-07-29.  Owner: Codex.

This closes the last mathematical gap recorded in
`Q32_H6_PROOF_PROGRESS_2026-07-29.md`.  The direct four-term
Bernoulli-convolution grind is unnecessary.

Throughout, `p>=11` is prime,

```text
S_j = sum_{k=1}^{p-1} 1/k^j,
H(a_1,...,a_d)
    = sum_{1<=k_1<...<k_d<=p-1}
        1/(k_1^a_1 ... k_d^a_d),
```

and `B_m` denotes the Bernoulli number with `B_1=-1/2`.  Put

```text
beta_0 = B_(p-5)/(p-5),
beta_1 = B_(2p-6)/(2p-6).
```

## The missing lemma

We prove

```text
H(1,4) ==
  (1/2)S_5 - 10 beta_0 + 5 beta_1
  - (1/9)p B_(p-3)^2                         (mod p^2).       (1)
```

We also prove the Euler relation

```text
D == S_5 - 2H(1,4)                           (mod p^2),       (2)
```

where

```text
D=sum_{k=1}^{p-1} H_k^(2)/k^3
```

is inclusive, equation (1) gives

```text
D ==
  20 beta_0 - 10 beta_1
  + (2/9)p B_(p-3)^2                         (mod p^2).       (3)
```

This is exactly the `D`-lemma in the progress note.  The proof below
then verifies H6 directly, so the chain does not depend on the
unexpanded E1 summary in that note.

```text
S_2^2 - 5S_4 - 2pD == 0                      (mod p^3).       (H6)
```

## 1. The only depth-three input

Zhao's general length-three formula
([`Multiple Harmonic Sums I`,
arXiv:math/0301252v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0301252)),
specialized in the display used in the proof of Proposition 3.13
(source label `equ:h141`, published equation (56)), gives

```text
H(1,4,1) ==
  sum_{a=0}^{p-5} B_a B_(p-5-a) + B_(p-3)^2  (mod p).        (4)
```

Indeed, in the short second sum of (56), the terms at `a=p-4` and
`a=p-2` vanish because their Bernoulli indices are odd; only
`a=p-3` remains.  Zhao's Proposition 3.13 states

```text
sum_{a=0}^{p-5} B_a B_(p-5-a)
  == -(2/3)B_(p-3)^2                          (mod p).
```

Therefore

```text
H(1,4,1) == (1/3)B_(p-3)^2                   (mod p).        (5)
```

The strict stuffle product is

```text
S_1 H(1,4)
 = 2H(1,1,4)+H(1,4,1)+H(2,4)+H(1,5).
```

Both weight-six doubles on the right vanish modulo `p`.  For
example, reversal gives `H(1,5)==H(5,1)`, while

```text
S_1S_5=H(1,5)+H(5,1)+S_6==0                  (mod p);
```

the proof for `H(2,4)` is identical.  Since `S_1==0 mod p`,
equation (5) yields

```text
H(1,1,4) == -(1/6)B_(p-3)^2                  (mod p).        (6)
```

Consequently,

```text
V := sum_{k=1}^{p-1} H_k(1,1)/k^4
   = H(1,1,4)+H(1,5)
   == -(1/6)B_(p-3)^2                         (mod p).        (7)
```

This is the key shortcut: the previously unevaluated weighted
Bernoulli convolution is replaced by one published unweighted
convolution and a stuffle identity.

## 2. An independent proof of the Euler relation

Define the additive-triangle sum

```text
G(2,3)=sum_{u,v>=1, u+v<=p-1} 1/(u^2v^3).
```

The exact partial fraction

```text
1/(u^2v^3)
 = 3/[u(u+v)^4] + 1/[u^2(u+v)^3]
   +3/[v(u+v)^4] + 2/[v^2(u+v)^3]
   +1/[v^3(u+v)^2]
```

gives, after summing by `k=u+v`,

```text
G(2,3)=6H(1,4)+3H(2,3)+H(3,2).              (E-a)
```

The stuffle identity

```text
S_2S_3=H(2,3)+H(3,2)+S_5
```

and the standard valuations `S_2==0 mod p`, `S_3==S_5==0 mod p^2`
reduce (E-a) to

```text
G(2,3)==6H(1,4)+2H(2,3)                     (mod p^2).       (E-b)
```

There is a second evaluation.  Put `k=p-v`; the additive triangle
becomes `1<=u<k<=p-1`, and

```text
1/(p-k)^3 == -1/k^3-3p/k^4                  (mod p^2).
```

Therefore

```text
G(2,3)==-H(2,3)-3pH(2,4)==-H(2,3)           (mod p^2),      (E-c)
```

because the even-weight double `H(2,4)` vanishes modulo `p`.
Comparing (E-b) and (E-c), and using that `3` is a unit, proves

```text
H(2,3)==-2H(1,4)                             (mod p^2).
```

Since `D=S_5+H(2,3)` exactly, this proves (2).

## 3. The star-binomial bridge

Consider

```text
A_4=sum_{k=1}^{p-1} (-1)^(k-1) binom(p-1,k)/k^4.
```

The standard binomial identity gives the exact equality

```text
A_4=H_star(1,1,1,1).
```

Newton's identity for the complete homogeneous function gives

```text
A_4 =
 (S_1^4+6S_1^2S_2+3S_2^2+8S_1S_3+6S_4)/24.
```

The usual full harmonic-sum valuations reduce this modulo `p^3` to

```text
A_4 == S_2^2/8 + S_4/4                       (mod p^3).       (8)
```

On the other hand,

```text
(-1)^(k-1) binom(p-1,k)
 = - product_{j=1}^k (1-p/j)
 == -1+pH_k(1)-p^2H_k(1,1)                  (mod p^3).
```

After division by `k^4` and summation,

```text
A_4 ==
 -S_4+p(H(1,4)+S_5)-p^2V                    (mod p^3).       (9)
```

Comparing (8) and (9), then dividing by `p`, gives

```text
H(1,4) ==
 S_2^2/(8p)+5S_4/(4p)-S_5+pV                (mod p^2).      (10)
```

All divisions by `p` here occur only after an equality modulo
`p^3`, so (10) is an equality modulo `p^2`.

## 4. The single-sum coordinates

The classical weight-two congruence is

```text
S_2 == (2/3)pB_(p-3)                         (mod p^2),      (11)
```

and pairing `k` with `p-k` gives

```text
S_5 == 0                                     (mod p^2).      (12)
```

For completeness, the exact second-order coordinate needed for
`S_4/p` is

```text
S_4/p == -8 beta_0+4 beta_1                  (mod p^2).      (13)
```

Here is a short proof.  If `y=k^(p-1)`, then `y==1 mod p` and

```text
15y^4-24y^5+10y^6 == 1                       (mod p^3).
```

Thus

```text
k^(-4) ==
15k^(4p-8)-24k^(5p-9)+10k^(6p-10)           (mod p^3).
```

Faulhaber together with von Staudt--Clausen gives, for each of the
three even exponents `m`,

```text
sum_{k=1}^{p-1} k^m == pB_m                  (mod p^3).
```

Indeed, in the form

```text
sum_(k=1)^(p-1) k^m
 =pB_m+sum_(j=2)^(m+1) [binom(m,j-1)/j]B_(m+1-j)p^j,
```

the `j=2` term vanishes because `B_(m-1)=0`.  The `j=3` term is
`p`-integral because `m-2==-6 mod p-1` and `p>=11`.  For `j>=4`,
von Staudt--Clausen removes at most one `p`.  If `j<p`, the factor
`1/j` removes none, leaving valuation at least `j-1>=3`; if
`j>=p`, then `j<=6p-9<p^2`, so `1/j` removes at most one further
`p`, leaving valuation at least `j-2>=9`.  Thus every omitted term
is in `p^3 Z_p`.

Set

```text
m_i=p-5+i(p-1),       beta_i=B_(m_i)/m_i.
```

Sun's second-order Kummer refinement
([Z.-H. Sun, *Discrete Applied Mathematics* 105 (2000),
equation (1.1)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-218X(00)00184-0))
gives

```text
beta_i == i beta_1-(i-1)beta_0                (mod p^2).
```

The refinement contains the factor `1-p^(p-6)` on `beta_0`; it is
`1 mod p^2` for `p>=11`, which is why the displayed affine form is
valid here.

Substitution at `i=3,4,5` gives the exact coefficient cancellation

```text
15m_3 beta_3-24m_4 beta_4+10m_5 beta_5
 == -8 beta_0+4 beta_1                        (mod p^2),
```

which proves (13).

## 5. Completion

Equations (7), (10), (11), and (13) give

```text
H(1,4)
 == (p/18)B_(p-3)^2
    -10 beta_0+5 beta_1
    -S_5
    -(p/6)B_(p-3)^2
 == -10 beta_0+5 beta_1
    -S_5-(p/9)B_(p-3)^2                     (mod p^2).
```

By (12), `-S_5==(1/2)S_5 mod p^2`, proving (1).  Therefore (3)
follows from the independently proved Euler relation.  Finally,
(11) and (13) give

```text
S_2^2-5S_4
 == (4/9)p^2B_(p-3)^2+40p beta_0-20p beta_1
 == 2pD                                      (mod p^3).
```

Thus H6 is unconditional, and the previously conditional
seventh-grade endpoint reduction may now consume it.

## 6. Primary-source audit

The arXiv v6 TeX of Zhao's paper was checked directly.  Its proof of
Proposition 3.13 states, for even `n`,

```text
H(1,n,1;p-1)
 ==sum_(a=0)^(p-n-1)(-1)^a B_a B_(p-n-1-a)
   +sum_(a=p-n)^(p-2)(-1)^a B_a B_(2p-n-2-a)      (mod p).
```

At `n=4`, the first sum is the convolution in `(4)`.  In the
three-term second range, the endpoints have odd Bernoulli index and
vanish; the middle term is exactly `B_(p-3)^2`.  The same proposition
prints both the convolution congruence and
`H(1,4,1)==B_(p-3)^2/3 mod p`.  Its hypothesis `p>=9` is covered by
`p>=11`.

Sun's printed equation `(1.1)` says, for even `b` not divisible by
`p-1`,

```text
B_(k(p-1)+b)/(k(p-1)+b)
 ==k B_(p-1+b)/(p-1+b)
   -(k-1)(1-p^(b-1))B_b/b                       (mod p^2).
```

Taking `b=p-5` gives exactly the affine law for `beta_i`; here
`p^(b-1)=p^(p-6)==0 mod p^2`.  Thus the two non-elementary inputs
used above match the primary sources with no change of normalization.
