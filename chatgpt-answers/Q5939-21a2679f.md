# Corrected higher-Cartier descent is terminal, not iterative

## Executive verdict

The corrected theorem from Q5919 is an exact and useful **alias formula**, but it does **not** yield the required pointwise descent for

\[
\mathcal R_n=\operatorname{rad}\gcd\!\left(b_n,\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)\right).
\]

The obstruction can be stated sharply.

1. In every safe instance
   \[
   M=ap+s,\qquad d=qp-v,
   \]
   the shell quotient is not merely bounded by the lower quotient: it is exactly
   \[
   t=\left\lfloor\frac M{qp-v}\right\rfloor
     =\left\lfloor\frac aq\right\rfloor=m,
   \]
   and necessarily \(q\le a\). Thus higher shell quotients cannot outrun the outer base-\(p\) digit.

2. For the actual fixed-moment target shell attached to
   \[
   n=Ap+r,\qquad p>\sqrt n,
   \]
   one has \(d=p-1\), so the higher-Cartier quotient is forced to be \(q=1\). Outside a harmless borrow strip \(r<A\), Q5919 becomes the exact integer convolution
   \[
   C_{n-A}(p-1)\equiv
   \sum_{\kappa\in AP}c_A(\kappa)c_{r-A}(-\kappa)
   =b_r\pmod p.
   \]
   Hence the alleged descendant is precisely the original moving Apéry target scalar. There is no index reduction at the level of the target equation.

3. Re-embedding \(p\mid b_r\) at the lower row \(N=p+r\) gives quotient one, and the next application returns the same triple \((N,p,r)\). Thus the descent graph has depth at most one and then an exact fixed point.

4. The terminal quotient-one family already has an explicit primitive lower-packet carrier of **linear** height. With \(Y=\lfloor M/4\rfloor\),
   \[
   \mathcal N_{M,Y}
   =\sum_{j=0}^{Y}\binom Mj\Delta^j b_0
   \]
   carries every prime \(p=M-s\), \(0\le s\le Y\), for which \(p\mid b_s\), and satisfies
   \[
   \left(\frac54\log4-\frac34\log3\right)M-O(\log M)
   \le \log\mathcal N_{M,Y}
   \le M\log40.
   \]
   This is an explicit linear-height ledger, not an \(o(M)\) ledger.

5. Full exterior margins are worse. For every prime \(p>73\), put \(M=2p\). Then
   \[
   p\mid C_{2p}(2p-v)-b_{2p}
   \qquad(1\le v\le p-1).
   \]
   This is an actual first-cell zero segment of length \(p-1\). The corrected lower packet and the origin packet are both \(b_2=73\), so their origin-cancelled descendant is the integer zero. Since \(p\nmid73\), this spike cannot be charged to a prime divisor of any nonzero packet supplied by that Cartier identity.

Therefore the answer to the question is **no**:

> Q5919 removes the shell-domain error and makes the safety bookkeeping exact, but it does not prove a pointwise \(o(n)\) height bound. Safe middle targets terminate at the original quotient-one target, while full margins contain actual exterior state spikes whose lower Cartier difference is identically zero.

This is a no-go for the descent mechanism generated by the proved Cartier identities. It is **not** a proof that \(\log\mathcal R_n\) is linear for infinitely many \(n\), and it makes no pointwise zero-fibre claim. A new cross-characteristic or distinguished-state identity could still prove the desired theorem.

## 2. Exact quotient and safety-strip lemma

The corrected theorem has a stronger elementary consequence which is useful for the global bookkeeping.

### Lemma 2.1

Assume (1.1). Write

\[
a=mq+e,\qquad 0\le e<q.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{q\le a,\qquad t=m=\left\lfloor\frac aq\right\rfloor.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Consequently the safety condition is exactly

\[
\boxed{s+v\left\lfloor\frac aq\right\rfloor<p.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Since \(d\le M\), one has \(t\ge1\). If \(q\ge a+1\), then

\[
qp-v\le ap+s
\]

implies

\[
p\le(q-a)p\le s+v\le s+vt<p,
\]

a contradiction. Thus \(q\le a\), so \(m\ge1\).

Q5919 already gives \(t\ge m\). If \(t\ge m+1\), then

\[
(m+1)(qp-v)\le ap+s=(mq+e)p+s.
\]

After cancellation,

\[
(q-e)p\le s+(m+1)v\le s+tv<p.
\]

But \(q-e\ge1\), another contradiction. Hence \(t=m\). ∎

### Consequences

For fixed \((a,q,v)\), the unsafe top strip in the remainder variable \(s\) has width at most

\[
v\left\lfloor\frac aq\right\rfloor.
\tag{2.3}
\]

If one uses a fixed finite set \(\mathcal V\) of offsets and outer digits \(a\le A\), the total number of unsafe parameter incidences is bounded by

\[
\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}
\sum_{a\le A}\sum_{q\le a}
 v\left\lfloor\frac aq\right\rfloor
\ll_{\mathcal V}A^2\log(2A).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\sum_{q\le a}\left\lfloor\frac aq\right\rfloor
\le a\{1+\log a\}.
\]

At the mesoscopic cutoff \(A=n^{1/3}\), (2.4), even after multiplying by \(\log n\), is

\[
O_{\mathcal V}\!\left(n^{2/3}(\log n)^2\right)=o(n).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus bounded-offset safety failures are not the obstruction. The obstruction is what the formula says on the large safe region.

A full margin has offsets growing with the prime, rather than a fixed \(\mathcal V\). Then (2.4) is unavailable. Section 7 below gives an actual full-margin zero segment.

## 3. Reduction of the smooth radical and the harmless sectors

Put

\[
D_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),
\qquad
\mathcal R_n=\operatorname{rad}\gcd(b_n,D_n),
\]

and

\[
\mathcal M_n=
\prod_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid b_{n\bmod p}}}p.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Section 40 of the authoritative note proves

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal R_n=\log\mathcal M_n+O(n^{2/3}).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The reason is the Apéry--Lucas factorization. For

\[
n=Ap+r,\qquad 0\le r<p,
\tag{3.3}
\]

one has

\[
b_n\equiv b_A b_r\pmod p.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Primes \(p\le n^{2/3}\) cost \(O(n^{2/3})\) in logarithmic radical height. For \(p>n^{2/3}\), one has \(A<n^{1/3}\), and all outer-channel exceptions \(p\mid b_A\) divide

\[
\prod_{A<n^{1/3}}b_A,
\]

whose logarithm is

\[
\le\log40\sum_{A<n^{1/3}}A=O(n^{2/3}).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the only relevant channel is

\[
p\mid b_r,\qquad p>n^{2/3},\qquad A<n^{1/3}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The borrow strip which will occur in the fixed-moment formula is also harmless. For each fixed \(A<n^{1/3}\), the condition

\[
0\le r<A,\qquad p=\frac{n-r}{A}
\]

allows at most \(A\) candidates. Hence

\[
\sum_{\substack{p>n^{2/3}\\0\le n-Ap<A}}\log p
\le
\log n\sum_{A<n^{1/3}}A
=O(n^{2/3}\log n)=o(n).
\tag{3.7}
\]

After (3.2), (3.5), and (3.7), it is enough to understand the safe middle targets with

\[
p>n^{2/3},\qquad A\le r\le p-2,\qquad p\mid b_r.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The endpoint \(r=p-1\) is never a target because

\[
b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p.
\tag{3.9}
\]

## 4. The actual target shell forces \(q=1\) and returns \(b_r\)

For (3.3), the fixed-moment torus reformulation uses

\[
M=n-A=A(p-1)+r
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the marked node

\[
d=p-1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

It gives the exact target equivalence

\[
\boxed{
p\mid C_M(p-1)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid b_r.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In Q5919 notation, (4.2) is

\[
d=1\cdot p-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{q=1,\qquad v=1.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

There is no freedom to choose a higher \(q\) for the marked target shell.

### 4.1 Safe middle: exact recombination to the original target

Assume \(r\ge A\). Then

\[
M=Ap+(r-A),
\]

so in Q5919 notation

\[
a=A,\qquad s=r-A,\qquad q=v=1.
\]

Since \(r\le p-2\),

\[
t=\left\lfloor\frac{A(p-1)+r}{p-1}\right\rfloor=A=m
\]

and the safety inequality is

\[
s+t=(r-A)+A=r<p.
\]

Formula (1.2) therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_M(p-1)
&\equiv
\sum_{\kappa\in AP\cap\mathbb Z^3}
 c_A(\kappa)c_{r-A}(-\kappa)\\
&=\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda^A\Lambda^{r-A})\\
&=b_r
\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The equality in the middle is an equality of integers, not merely a congruence.

Thus on every safe middle target, the “lower Cartier packet” is exactly

\[
\boxed{K_{A,r-A;1,1}=b_r.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the decisive failure of descent. The higher-Cartier packet has lower *ingredients* \(c_A\) and \(c_{r-A}\), but their pairing recombines to the same Apéry number whose divisibility was the original obstruction.

### 4.2 Borrow strip: the theorem correctly refuses to alias

If \(r<A\), the normalized base-\(p\) decomposition is

\[
M=(A-1)p+(p+r-A).
\]

Then

\[
a=A-1,\qquad s=p+r-A,\qquad q=v=1,
\]

while

\[
t=A,\qquad m=A-1.
\]

The safety expression is

\[
s+t=p+r\ge p,
\tag{4.7}
\]

so Q5919 does not apply. This is not a defect: it is the exact borrow layer which was missing from the earlier fixed-domain wording.

Equation (3.7) shows that this strip has total \(o(n)\) logarithmic cost in the remaining large-prime range. Removing it leaves (4.5), where the descent is the identity.

### 4.3 Folding does not make the descent iterative

Reflection gives

\[
b_r\equiv b_{p-1-r}\pmod p.
\]

Writing

\[
j=\min(r,p-1-r)
\]

can lower the displayed index. But re-encoding the same prime at the child row

\[
N=p+j
\tag{4.8}
\]

makes it a quotient-one target. The next fixed-moment application again returns the packet \(b_j\). Thus reflection can choose a smaller representative; it does not create a second descendant.

## 5. Why higher \(q\) packets do not rescue the target

There are three independent reasons.

### 5.1 Safe higher quotients are bounded by the outer digit

Lemma 2.1 gives

\[
q\le a,\qquad t=m=\left\lfloor\frac aq\right\rfloor.
\tag{5.1}
\]

In the smooth-radical range \(p>\sqrt n\), every relevant moment has

\[
a<p.
\]

Hence both lower exponents \(a\) and \(s\) in (1.2) are below \(p\). The same prime cannot be used for another nontrivial base-\(p\) Cartier decomposition of those lower factors.

### 5.2 The marked shell itself has \(q=1\)

Targetness supplies

\[
p\mid C_M(p-1),
\]

not, by itself,

\[
p\mid C_M(qp-v)
\]

for any \(q>1\). Any higher-\(q\) shell which also vanishes at the distinguished Apéry state is an additional alias or state relation, not a consequence of the target scalar alone.

The exact local theorem in Section 63 makes this formal. If \(V\) is a localized recurrence-state lattice and \(\tau_p\in V^\vee\) is the primitive target functional, then all linear observations universally preserved by targetness form

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_p=\mathbb Z_{(p)}\tau_p+pV^\vee.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus every finite family of universally target-preserving rows has rank at most one modulo \(p\). After primitive Smith saturation, it still contains only the original target direction. A second nonzero packet would require a new identity special to the distinguished Apéry state; it is not furnished by Q5919.

### 5.3 The residue channel retains the original factor

For \(v=0\), equation (1.3) gives

\[
K_{a,s;q,0}=b_s C_a(q).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus every residue-channel packet retains \(b_s\) as a common factor. Varying \(q\) shrinks the support from \(aP\) to \(\lfloor a/q\rfloor P\), but it does not split the target scalar into independent conditions.

For \(v>0\), the packet is generally a positive convolution sum, not a product. Divisibility of the sum by \(p\) gives no factor-level descent. Since its two moments are already below \(p\), there is no same-prime Cartier iteration of the summands.

## 6. The descent graph has depth one and then a fixed point

Suppose a state or target prime has been reduced to

\[
p\mid b_u,\qquad 0\le u<p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The natural child row is

\[
N=p+u.
\tag{6.2}
\]

At this row the prime has quotient one and residue \(u\). The fixed moment is

\[
M'=N-1=p+(u-1)
\]

when \(u\ge1\), and its marked shell is again \(p-1\). Q5919 with \(q=v=1\) returns

\[
C_{M'}(p-1)\equiv b_u\pmod p.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus

\[
(N,p,u)\longmapsto(N,p,u).
\tag{6.4}
\]

For \(u=0\), \(b_0=1\), so there is no target.

For an original quotient \(A\ge2\), the first child

\[
N=p+r=n-(A-1)p<n
\]

is a genuine one-step contraction. But (6.4) shows that it cannot be iterated for the same prime. For \(A=1\), it is a fixed point from the start.

Factoring \(b_u\) into other primes does not transfer the original prime \(p\). A height charge for \(p\) must remain attached to an integer divisible by \(p\), and Q5919 supplies no further such descendant after (6.1).

This recovers, with the corrected \(mP\) domain, the depth-one conclusion of Section 63.2.

## 7. Explicit terminal family with linear carrier height

The quotient-one fixed point is not an abstract countermodel. It occurs in the actual Apéry sequence.

For \(0\le Y<M/2\), define

\[
\mathcal T_M^{(1)}(Y)=
\prod_{\substack{0\le s\le Y\\p=M-s\ \mathrm{prime}\\p\mid b_s}}p.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For \(p=M-s>5\), Apéry--Lucas gives

\[
b_M=b_{p+s}\equiv b_1b_s=5b_s\pmod p.
\]

Consequently, apart from the fixed prime \(5\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_M^{(1)}(Y)
=\operatorname{rad}_{[M-Y,M]}(b_M)
=\operatorname{rad}_{[M-Y,M]}
 \gcd\!\left(b_M,\binom M{Y+1}\right),
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

provided \(M-Y>Y+1\).

So the terminal family is exactly a short-interval prime slice of one original Apéry number. The “descent” has not changed the arithmetic problem.

### 7.1 Primitive Newton packet carrier

Define

\[
\mathcal N_{M,Y}
=\sum_{j=0}^{Y}\binom Mj\Delta^j b_0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

For \(p=M-s\) with \(0\le s\le Y<p\),

\[
\binom Mj\equiv\binom sj\pmod p,
\]

and Newton inversion gives

\[
\mathcal N_{M,Y}\equiv
\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom sj\Delta^j b_0=b_s\pmod p.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal T_M^{(1)}(Y)\mid\mathcal N_{M,Y}.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is a single carrier built from finitely many lower Apéry packets. Its height is nevertheless linear.

Since

\[
\Delta^j b_0=\operatorname{CT}(\Lambda-1)^j,
\tag{7.6}
\]

and \(\Lambda-1\) has nonnegative coefficients, constant coefficient \(4\), and total coefficient sum \(39\),

\[
4^j\le\Delta^j b_0\le39^j.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Hence

\[
\binom MY4^Y
\le\mathcal N_{M,Y}
\le\sum_{j=0}^{M}\binom Mj39^j
=40^M.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Take

\[
Y=\left\lfloor\frac M4\right\rfloor.
\]

Stirling's formula gives

\[
\log\binom M{\lfloor M/4\rfloor}
=\left(\log4-\frac34\log3\right)M+O(\log M).
\]

Combining this with (7.8),

\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac54\log4-\frac34\log3\right)M-O(\log M)
\le\log\mathcal N_{M,\lfloor M/4\rfloor}
\le M\log40.
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

Numerically the lower linear coefficient is

\[
\frac54\log4-\frac34\log3=0.9089\ldots.
\]

Equation (7.9) is the requested explicit family with a linear accumulated height ledger. It does not assert that a linear fraction of the candidate primes in (7.1) are actual targets. It proves that the lower-packet carrier naturally produced after terminal descent is not of height \(e^{o(M)}\).

### 7.2 A concrete middle parameter family

There is also a simple infinite family of safe middle parameter rows. Let

\[
p\equiv1\pmod3,\qquad r=\frac{p-1}{3},\qquad n=p+r=4r+1.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Then \(n/2<p<n\), the outer quotient is one, and the fixed moment is

\[
M=n-1=p+r-1.
\]

Q5919 is safe with

\[
a=q=v=t=m=1,\qquad s=r-1,
\]

and gives

\[
C_M(p-1)\equiv b_r\pmod p.
\tag{7.11}
\]

If such a prime is a target, the only descendant supplied by the formula is \(b_r\). Its elementary height is already linear in \(n\): the \(k=r\) summand in the Apéry sum gives

\[
b_r\ge\binom{2r}{r}^2
\ge\frac{16^r}{(2r+1)^2},
\]

while \(b_r\le40^r\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(n-1)\log2-2\log\frac{n+1}{2}
\le\log b_r
\le\frac{n-1}{4}\log40.
}
\tag{7.12}
\]

Again, (7.12) is a height obstruction for the Cartier charge. It is not a claim that infinitely many primes in (7.10) divide \(b_r\).

## 8. Actual full-margin exterior spike with zero descendant

The preceding obstruction concerns marked targets. Full margins contain a stronger, unconditional state obstruction.

Let \(p>73\) be prime and set

\[
M=2p.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For every

\[
1\le v\le p-1,
\]

put

\[
d_v=2p-v.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Then

\[
\frac M2=p<d_v<M,
\]

so every \(d_v\) is in the first cell. In Q5919 notation,

\[
a=q=2,\qquad s=0,\qquad t=m=1.
\]

The safety condition is

\[
s+vt=v<p.
\]

Thus (1.2) and (1.5) give

\[
C_{2p}(2p-v)
\equiv
\sum_{\kappa\in P}c_2(2\kappa)c_0(-v\kappa)
=c_2(0)=b_2=73
\pmod p.
\tag{8.3}
\]

On the other hand, Apéry--Lucas gives

\[
b_{2p}\equiv b_2b_0=73\pmod p.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Subtracting (8.4) from (8.3),

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid W_{2p}(2p-v),
 \qquad
 W_M(d):=C_M(d)-b_M,
 \qquad
 1\le v\le p-1.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

This is an actual exterior zero segment of length \(p-1\), for every prime \(p>73\).

The lower packet in (8.3) is \(b_2=73\); the lower origin packet in (8.4) is also \(b_2=73\). Their origin-cancelled lower descendant is

\[
73-73=0.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Since \(p>73\),

\[
p\nmid73.
\]

Therefore the common exterior factor in (8.5) cannot be charged to a prime divisor of either nonzero lower packet. Charging it to the zero difference is not a height certificate: every prime divides zero, and \(\operatorname{rad}(0)\) has no finite logarithmic ledger.

This family is fully compatible with the target/state separation in Sections 61--63. Here

\[
p\nmid b_{2p},
\]

so these are state-content nuisances, not marked smooth-radical targets. The example answers the “full-margin exterior spike” part of the question: higher-Cartier descent does not classify every such spike by nonzero lower packet prime divisors.

The same construction works more generally with \(M=ap\), \(d=ap-v\), and \(q=a\), whenever the chosen offsets remain in the first cell. The choice \(a=2\) makes the whole range \(1\le v\le p-1\) first-cell and gives the cleanest full segment.

## 9. The strongest inequality obtained from this descent

After discarding the sectors in Section 3, let \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{safe}}(n)\) be the safe moving targets in (3.8). Then

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal R_n
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{safe}}(n)}\log p
+O(n^{2/3}\log n).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

For every term in the sum, Q5919 gives exactly the packet \(b_{n\bmod p}\), and Apéry--Lucas puts the same prime into \(b_n\). Hence

\[
\prod_{p\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{safe}}(n)}p\mid b_n
\]

and the direct height estimate is only

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal R_n
\le n\log40+O(n^{2/3}\log n).
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Of course \(\mathcal R_n\mid b_n\) already gives essentially the same linear bound. The corrected Cartier formula has not improved its scale.

Charging primes separately to the packets \(b_r\) is even weaker:

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{safe}}(n)}\log p
\le
\sum_{p\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{safe}}(n)}\log b_{n\bmod p}
\le
\log40\sum_{p\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{safe}}(n)}(n\bmod p),
\tag{9.3}
\]

which has no sublinear pointwise bound without the missing cross-prime information.

There is also a universal coefficient-content obstruction on a fixed direct middle strip. For example,

\[
\frac{3n}{4}<p\le\frac{4n}{5}
\]

has ambient Chebyshev weight

\[
\vartheta(4n/5)-\vartheta(3n/4)
=\frac n{20}+o(n).
\tag{9.4}
\]

The matched target coordinates in this strip are distinct and have diagonal local valuation. By the exact module law (5.2), any target-blind finite family of universally target-preserving rows retains the corresponding node factors, so its universal coefficient ledger has linear height on this strip.
