This is the closest primary theorem directly about prime divisors of the classical Apéry numbers. It cannot imply the target because:

- it is density one, not pointwise for every m;

## 3.2 Luca: binary holonomic sequences, but cumulative lower-bound

Reference. Florian Luca, “Prime divisors of binary holonomic sequences”, Advances in Applied Mathematics 40 (2008), 168–179, DOI 10.1016/j.aam.2006.12.001.

The paper assumes a rational sequence satisfying a second-order recurrence with polynomial coefficients,

which is not eventually a binary constant-coefficient recurrence. Its main conclusion is that the product of the numerators and denominators of the nonzero terms u_m with m\le N has at least c\log N distinct prime divisors.

The Apéry recurrence lies in the broad recurrence class, but the conclusion is:

- aggregated across all initial terms;

- unweighted;

- a lower bound;

- silent about one term u_N and one moving prime interval.

It therefore supplies no upper bound on R_n^{\mathrm{top}}.

A quantifier warning is important: “almost all primes” in a Lucas theorem concerns the exceptional set of characteristics. It is not an almost-all-index theorem, and neither interpretation is the requested pointwise result.

# 8. Pointwise versus almost-all: exact quantifier ledger

The following distinctions are load-bearing.

- Luca–Shparlinski: density one in the sequence index. Exceptional indices may still contain the entire pointwise obstruction.

- Delaygue: primewise Lucas/valuation statements. “Almost all primes” is a statement about characteristics, not pointwise depletion for each outer index.

- Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier: correct eventual pointwise quantifier, but only for a fixed multiplicative orbit.

# 10. Final literature verdict

Direct answer: no unconditional published theorem located in the searched primary literature implies the pointwise estimate

The negative conclusion is not based on terminology. For each nearest source, the exact theorem was checked against its sequence class, arithmetic direction, and quantifier. The two closest positive templates are:

- Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier: right pointwise asymptotic, wrong fixed-torus class;

- Luca–Shparlinski: right Apéry sequence, wrong direction and density-one quantifier.

The G-function, diagonal, Hadamard-product, denominator, and recent mod-p Galois theories provide valuable structure but no theorem controlling this moving prime radical. The central-binomial example proves that no result based only on membership in those broad classes can possibly suffice.
