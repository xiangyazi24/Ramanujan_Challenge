# Continuant renewal identity: terminal verdict

## Set-theoretic form of the renewal implication

Let S_m = {x in F_p : K_m(x)=0}. For a regular root x in S_m, adjacent coprimality gives K_{m+1}(x) != 0. Therefore the renewal identity implies the exact shifted inclusion/equality

S_{m+g+1} ∩ S_m = {x in S_m : x+m+1 in S_g}.

Equivalently, with translation tau_a(x)=x+a,

S_{m+g+1} ∩ S_m = S_m ∩ tau_{-(m+1)}(S_g).

This is the complete set-theoretic content of renewal. It does not produce new roots from S_m; it identifies which roots of S_m survive into the longer lag.

## No deterministic pumping lemma exists from this identity alone

The renewal identity controls intersections, not unions. A large S_m only implies that there are many candidates on which to test the shifted condition S_g. Without information on correlation between S_m and tau_{-(m+1)}(S_g), the intersection can be empty.

Abstract counterexample:

Take a finite field F_p and choose a subset A of size about p/2. Let B be a translate-disjoint subset with B ∩ A = empty. Set S_m=A and choose S_g so that tau_{-(m+1)}(S_g)=B. Then

S_m ∩ tau_{-(m+1)}(S_g)=empty,

so the renewal identity permits zero long-lag roots generated from S_m.

The adjacent coprimality condition only removes the forbidden case K_{m+1}(x)=0 at x in S_m. It does not impose correlation between these sets. Therefore no self-improving inequality of the form R_{m+g+1} >= f(R_m) follows from renewal plus adjacent coprimality.

## What the identity can prove

The only unconditional deterministic inequality is the intersection bound

Summing over roots gives pairwise correlation counts. It cannot turn a large root count at one lag into a larger root count at later lags.

The identity becomes useful only after adding an independent input such as:

1. a lower bound for correlations |S_m ∩ tau_{-(m+1)}(S_g)|;

1. a family equidistribution theorem for the shifted root sets;

1. a rigidity theorem showing that large S_m forces overlap with many shifted S_g.

All three are extra arithmetic information absent from renewal itself.

## Cross-resultants over Q

Nonvanishing of resultants over Q is not enough at one fixed prime when m,g grow like sqrt(p)L.

A resultant Res(K_m,K_g shifted) != 0 over Q only says that the two polynomials do not share a common algebraic root identically. Mod p, a nonzero resultant may vanish. The prime divisors of the resultant can be large, and when degrees are of order sqrt(p)L the resultant height can be enormous.

Thus the implication

nonzero over Q => no overlap mod p

fails without a quantitative bound excluding p from the resultant divisor set. This is exactly the fixed-p quenched issue.

## Terminal verdict

The renewal identity is a correlation detector, not a pumping engine. Large |Z(K_m)| alone cannot force additional roots at longer lags. Any successful bootstrap must supply a genuinely new arithmetic correlation statement across shifted root sets.

The minimal missing theorem would be a quenched overlap estimate of the form:

for many g in a controlled range,

or a rigidity substitute forcing such overlap. Without such an input, the continuant renewal identity alone cannot beat the degree budget.
