Q6680 - higher fiber moments from S_{3h} monodromy, and why the naive bridge does not break W3

# Executive verdict

The monodromy-to-moment part of the proposed chain is correct, after three
corrections.

1. The m-fold equal-value fiber product is a curve, not an
   (m-1)-dimensional variety. It has m source coordinates and m-1 independent
   equal-value equations.

2. If n=3h and the geometric monodromy of

     f_h(X)=D_h(X)=N_h(X)/q_h(X)^3

   is S_n, then

     M_m(h):=sum_{a in F_p} nu_h(a)^m

   has main term

     b_m(n) p,

   where

     b_m(n)=sum_{j=1}^{min(m,n)} Stirling2(m,j).

   If m<=n, this is the Bell number B_m, equivalently the m-th moment of a
   Poisson(1) random variable. In particular

     B_2=2,
     B_4=15,
     B_6=203,
     B_8=4140.

3. The square-root error is not O_{m}(sqrt p) uniformly in h. The normalized
   j-distinct fiber component has genus Theta_j(n^j). Consequently the honest
   fixed-m estimate is

     M_m(h)=b_m(n)p+O_m(n^m sqrt p+n^m).                (E1)

   For m=2k this is exactly the h^{2k} sqrt p error anticipated in the
   question.

The fatal point is what happens after taking the m-th root:

  R_h <= M_m(h)^{1/m}
      << B_m^{1/m} p^{1/m} + h p^{1/(2m)}.             (E2)

The second term is at least a constant times h, hence it is never better than
(and is usually worse than) the trivial degree bound R_h<=3h-3. There is no
scale on which the Bell main term both dominates the Weil error and improves the
degree bound:

  main term improves degree  =>  p^{1/m}<h  => h^m>p,
  main term dominates error   =>  h^m sqrt p<p => h^m<sqrt p.

These conditions are incompatible.

After summing by Holder one gets, for fixed m,

  S_D
  <<_m B_m^{1/m} D p^{1/m}
       + C_m^{1/m} D^2 p^{1/(2m)}.                     (E3)

At D=sqrt(p)L this becomes

  S_D
  <<_m B_m^{1/m} p^{1/2+1/m} L
       + C_m^{1/m} p^{1+1/(2m)} L^2.                   (E4)

The second term is worse than the trivial O(D^2)=O(pL^2), so one replaces it
by the trivial bound. Letting m or k grow makes the first term approach
p^{1/2+o(1)}, but the error/trivial term remains p^{1+o(1)}. The final exponent
of this pipeline is therefore 1, not 1/2.

Thus:

  FULL S_{3h} MONODROMY FOR ALL h DOES NOT BY ITSELF PROVE W3.

It supplies all fixed moments and their exact Bell main terms, but the conductor
of the higher fiber powers grows at precisely the rate that destroys the
pointwise zero-fiber gain.

There is nevertheless a clean genuine bridge. If, for one fixed m>=7, one could
prove the family-averaged moment estimate

  [AVG-MOMENT-m]
  sum_{h<=D} sum_a nu_h(a)^m <<_m pD                 (E5)

uniformly for D=sqrt(p)L, then

  S_D <<_m D p^{1/m}.                                  (E6)

For the even-moment program, m=8 gives

  S_D << p^{5/8}L = p^{5/8+o(1)} << p^{2/3},           (E7)

and therefore breaks W3. The sixth moment gives only p^{2/3}L, so it misses the
stated W3 target when L tends to infinity. The eighth averaged moment is the
first even moment that wins.

The new residual is therefore not another one-variable monodromy theorem. It is
an h-averaged cancellation theorem for the traces/errors of the higher fiber
powers. That is a two-clock statement and is essentially another coordinate of
the wall.

# 1. Setup and the affine/projective bookkeeping

Write

  q_h(X)=product_{i=1}^h (X+i),
  f_h(X)=D_h(X)=N_h(X)/q_h(X)^3.

As a map P^1 -> P^1, f_h has degree

  n=3h.                                                 (1.1)

Indeed deg(q_h^3)=3h and deg N_h=3h-3.

For a finite value a in F_p, define the full regular affine fiber count

  nu_h(a)=#{x in F_p : q_h(x)!=0 and f_h(x)=a}.         (1.2)

The nonwrapping census quantity R_h is bounded by nu_h(0), because it counts
only a subinterval of the roots of N_h.

It is convenient to add the point x=infinity. Since

  f_h(X) ~ lc(N_h) X^{-3}

at infinity, the point infinity lies over a=0 with ramification index 3. If
bar_nu_h denotes the projective finite-value fiber count, then

  bar_nu_h(a)=nu_h(a)+1_{a=0}.                          (1.3)

Therefore replacing nu by bar_nu changes the m-th moment by at most O_m(n^{m-1}),
and this is absorbed in every error below. I will use the projective cover in
the geometric proof and return to R_h afterward.

The needed good-reduction hypothesis is:

  [MON_h,p]
  - p>3;
  - the degree n=3h is preserved modulo p;
  - the branch-cycle pattern is preserved;
  - the geometric monodromy over Fbar_p is S_n.

For each fixed h, the characteristic-zero S_n theorem implies [MON_h,p] for all
but finitely many p. Uniformity when h grows with p is an additional hypothesis;
it does not follow formally from the characteristic-zero statement.

In the certified range h<=40, the project supplies the required monodromy and
collision geometry at the stated good primes. For an all-h theorem below, assume
[MON_h,p] for every h in the range being summed.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Exact moment constant from monodromy

For an integer m>=1, form the m-fold fiber product over the target:

  Y_{h,m}
    = P^1 x_{f_h,P^1,f_h} P^1 x_{P^1} ... x_{P^1} P^1.
                                                               (2.1)

A rational point over a finite target value is exactly an ordered tuple

  (x_1,...,x_m)

with

  f_h(x_1)=...=f_h(x_m).

Hence

  #Y_{h,m}(F_p over A^1)
    = sum_{a in F_p} bar_nu_h(a)^m.                    (2.2)

The scheme Y_{h,m} is a curve: it sits in (P^1)^m and is cut out by m-1
equalities

  f_h(x_1)=f_h(x_i),  i=2,...,m.                       (2.3)

## 2.1 Components are equality patterns

Let Omega be the n-point geometric generic fiber. The geometrically irreducible
components dominating the target correspond to orbits of the geometric
monodromy group on Omega^m.

For S_n, two ordered m-tuples are in the same orbit exactly when they have the
same equality pattern. Equality patterns are set partitions of {1,...,m}; a
partition with j blocks is possible exactly when j<=n.

Therefore the number of dominant geometric components is

  b_m(n)=sum_{j=1}^{min(m,n)} {m brace j}.              (2.4)

Here {m brace j} is a Stirling number of the second kind. If m<=n, then

  b_m(n)=B_m,                                           (2.5)

where B_m is the Bell number.

This is also Burnside's lemma in the permutation representation:

  b_m(n)
    = dim ((Q^Omega)^{tensor m})^{S_n}
    = average_{sigma in S_n} Fix(sigma)^m.              (2.6)

For n>=m, Fix(sigma) has the first m moments of Poisson(1), hence the Bell
constant.

The same statement may be phrased using distinct tuples. For a partition with
j blocks, its component is the normalization of the cover whose generic fiber
is the set

  Omega^{underline j}

of ordered injective j-tuples. Since S_n is j-transitive for j<=n, that cover is
geometrically connected and therefore geometrically irreducible.

A minor correction to the proposed bookkeeping: one does not need to require
m<=n in order to define the moment or the components. If m>n, one simply uses
the restricted Bell number b_m(n). The ordinary Bell number B_m is valid when
m<=n.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Exact genus growth of the distinct-tuple components

The h-dependence of the error is the decisive issue. In the full-Morse situation
the branch cycles of f_h are explicit.

Let n=3h.

- There are 4h-4 finite nonzero branch values, each with a transposition branch
  cycle.

- Over 0, the point x=infinity has ramification index 3 and all other points are
  unramified. The branch cycle has type

    (3)(1^{n-3}).                                      (3.1)

- Over infinity, the h poles -1,...,-h all have ramification index 3. The branch
  cycle has type

    3^h.                                                (3.2)

The total ramification is

  (4h-4)+2+2h=6h-2=2n-2,

as required for a degree-n map P^1 -> P^1.

Fix 1<=j<=n. Let X_{h,j} be the normalization of the connected cover whose
generic fiber is Omega^{underline j}. Put

  N_j=(n)_{j}=n(n-1)...(n-j+1).                        (3.3)

For a transposition tau acting on injective j-tuples,

  Fix(tau)=(n-2)_{j},

so its permutation index is

  ind_j(tau)
    = [N_j-(n-2)_{j}]/2.                               (3.4)

For the 3-cycle over 0, Burnside on its cyclic group gives

  ind_j(sigma_0)
    = (2/3)[N_j-(n-3)_{j}].                            (3.5)

For sigma_infinity of type 3^h, there are no fixed injective tuples under
sigma_infinity or sigma_infinity^2, so

  ind_j(sigma_infinity)=(2/3)N_j.                      (3.6)

Riemann-Hurwitz now gives the exact genus formula

  2g_{h,j}-2
    = -2N_j
      +(4h-4) [N_j-(n-2)_{j}]/2
      +(2/3)[N_j-(n-3)_{j}]
      +(2/3)N_j                                         (3.7)

or equivalently

  2g_{h,j}-2
    = (2h-2)[N_j-(n-2)_{j}]
      -(2/3)[N_j+(n-3)_{j}].                           (3.8)

For fixed j and n -> infinity,

  g_{h,j}
    = (2/3)(j-1)n^j + O_j(n^{j-1}).                    (3.9)

Thus the n^j conductor growth is genuine, not an artifact of a crude plane
Bezout bound.

There is an equivalent complete-intersection check. In (P^1)^m, the i-th
equality in (2.3) has divisor class

  n(H_1+H_i).

The full fiber product is a complete-intersection curve with arithmetic genus

  p_a(Y_{h,m})
    = 1+n^{m-1}[(m-1)n-m].                             (3.10)

Again the leading size is Theta_m(n^m).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. The fixed-m moment theorem

Decompose ordered tuples by their exact equality partition. A partition with j
blocks contributes a copy of the j-distinct cover X_{h,j}, with finitely many
branch/boundary identifications. Weil's bound on the normalizations, followed by
summing over the {m brace j} partitions, gives

  M_m(h)
    := sum_{a in F_p} nu_h(a)^m

    = b_m(n)p
      + O( E_m(n) sqrt p + E_m(n) ),                   (4.1)

where one may take

  E_m(n)
    << sum_{j=1}^{min(m,n)} {m brace j}(g_{h,j}+n^j)
    <<_m n^m.                                           (4.2)

Consequently

  M_m(h)=b_m(n)p+O_m(n^m sqrt p+n^m).                  (4.3)

This is the exact higher-moment analogue of [L2-FREQ].

For n>=m, the first cases are

  M_2(h)=2p+O(h^2 sqrt p),                              (4.4)

  M_4(h)=15p+O(h^4 sqrt p),                             (4.5)

  M_6(h)=203p+O(h^6 sqrt p),                            (4.6)

  M_8(h)=4140p+O(h^8 sqrt p).                           (4.7)

The constants 2,15,203,4140 are not heuristic. They are the exact numbers of
S_n-orbits on ordered tuples, equivalently Bell numbers.

References for the general mechanism are the standard monodromy/moment formalism
in N. Katz, Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups, especially the
curve cohomology and equal-characteristic equidistribution chapters. The orbit
interpretation is also the elementary Galois correspondence for finite etale
covers; the error in (4.1) is the Weil bound applied to the normalized component
curves. No sheaf-theoretic strengthening removes the n^m conductor here: formula
(3.8) shows that the top distinct-tuple component already has genus
Theta_m(n^m).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Pointwise zero-fiber consequence and the no-overlap obstruction

Since the zero fiber is one summand of M_m(h),

  R_h^m <= nu_h(0)^m <= M_m(h).                         (5.1)

Taking m-th roots in (4.3),

  R_h
    <<_m b_m(n)^{1/m} p^{1/m}
       + n p^{1/(2m)}.                                 (5.2)

With n=3h,

  R_h
    <<_m B_m^{1/m}p^{1/m}
       + h p^{1/(2m)}                                  (5.3)

when m<=3h.

But

  h p^{1/(2m)} >= h.                                    (5.4)

Therefore the error term is never better than the elementary root-degree bound

  R_h<=deg N_h=3h-3.                                    (5.5)

There is a sharper way to see the impossibility. To obtain a strict improvement
from the Bell main term one would need

  p^{1/m}<h,

which means

  h^m>p.                                                (5.6)

To make the Bell main term dominate the Weil error one would need

  h^m sqrt p < p,

which means

  h^m<sqrt p.                                           (5.7)

There is no h satisfying both (5.6) and (5.7).

This remains true if m=2k grows with p. The m-th root of the geometric error is

  (h^m sqrt p)^{1/m}=h p^{1/(2m)},                     (5.8)

which tends to h, not to 1. Growth of Bell numbers and of the implied constants
only makes the estimate weaker.

Thus the sentence

  "S_{3h} handles all k simultaneously"

is correct only at the component-classification level. It is false as a uniform
point-count conclusion: every increase of k also increases the genus/conductor
of the relevant component by another factor comparable to h.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Dyadic and global summation

Let

  S_D=sum_{h<=D} R_h.

Holder and (4.3) give

  S_D^m
    <= D^{m-1} sum_{h<=D} R_h^m
    <= D^{m-1} sum_{h<=D} M_m(h).                       (6.1)

For fixed m, summing the individual moment estimates yields

  sum_{h<=D} M_m(h)
    <<_m B_m pD + sqrt p D^{m+1}.                       (6.2)

Hence

  S_D
    <<_m B_m^{1/m} D p^{1/m}
       + D^2 p^{1/(2m)}.                               (6.3)

The same formula holds on a dyadic block H<h<=2H with D replaced by H.
Choosing m separately on each block does not help, because the second term is
always at least H^2 and the trivial block estimate is O(H^2).

At the target scale

  D=sqrt(N)L ~ sqrt(p)L,

(6.3) becomes

  S_D
    <<_m B_m^{1/m} p^{1/2+1/m}L
       + p^{1+1/(2m)}L^2.                              (6.4)

Replacing the second term by the better trivial estimate gives only

  S_D << pL^2.                                          (6.5)

As m -> infinity, the first term is formally p^{1/2+o(1)}, but the surviving
term is still p^{1+o(1)}. The optimized exponent is 1.

Therefore the proposed higher-moment pipeline, with the honest Lang-Weil error,
does not reach N^{3/4}, N^{2/3}, or any improvement over the trivial census at
the mesoscopic endpoint.

# 7. What [L2-FREQ] alone implies

There is also a direct interpolation showing the same fixed point without using
new fiber products. Since every fiber has size at most n,

  nu_h(a)^m <= n^{m-2} nu_h(a)^2,

so

  M_m(h) <= n^{m-2} M_2(h).                             (7.1)

Using [L2-FREQ],

  R_h
    <= M_m(h)^{1/m}
    << n^{1-2/m}(p+h^2 sqrt p)^{1/m}.                  (7.2)

Even if the error in M_2 is ignored, summing the main part gives

  sum_{h<=D} h^{1-2/m} p^{1/m}
    << D^{2-2/m}p^{1/m}.                               (7.3)

At D=sqrt p L this has p-exponent

  (2-2/m)/2+1/m=1.                                     (7.4)

Thus pure interpolation from the second moment lands exactly at the trivial
exponent for every m. Higher geometric monodromy moments do not alter this
because their conductor error has the matching n^m size.

A set-valued Cauchy estimate has the same limitation. From

  sum_a nu_h(a)=p+O(h),
  sum_a nu_h(a)^2=2p+O(h^2 sqrt p),

one obtains for A subset F_p

  sum_{a in A} nu_h(a)
    <= |A|+O(sqrt(|A|p)+h p^{1/4}|A|^{1/2}).            (7.5)

For A={0}, this is still a square-root bound. The algebraic meaning of 0 is not
used unless one has an additional mechanism transporting the zero fiber to many
other values.

Information-theoretically, [L2-FREQ] alone permits the same distinguished value
to have multiplicity comparable to sqrt p for every h; the remaining values can
still be arranged with total second moment comparable to 2p. Cross-h information
is indispensable.

# 8. The genuine moment-to-census bridge

The positive bridge is an averaged moment theorem, not the individual
Lang-Weil theorem.

[AVG-MOMENT-m]

For some fixed m>=2 and D=sqrt(p)L,

  sum_{h<=D} sum_{a in F_p} nu_h(a)^m <<_m pD.          (8.1)

Then by Holder,

  S_D
    <= D^{1-1/m}
       (sum_{h<=D} nu_h(0)^m)^{1/m}

    <= D^{1-1/m}
       (sum_{h<=D} sum_a nu_h(a)^m)^{1/m}

    <<_m D p^{1/m}.                                     (8.2)

At D=sqrt(p)L,

  S_D <<_m p^{1/2+1/m}L.                               (8.3)

Consequences:

- m=4 gives p^{3/4}L;

- m=6 gives p^{2/3}L, exactly the exponent target but with an unwanted L;

- m=7 gives p^{9/14}L;

- m=8 gives p^{5/8}L and therefore W3 for every L=p^{o(1)}.

Thus the first winning even moment is the eighth moment.

In Bell-main-term language, it would suffice to prove the upper estimate

  sum_{h<=D}
    [M_8(h)-4140p]
    << pD,                                              (8.4)

with bad/boundary h handled separately. Individual monodromy plus Weil gives
only

  sum_{h<=D}|M_8(h)-4140p|
    << sqrt p D^9,                                      (8.5)

which at D=sqrt p is of order p^5, whereas (8.4) asks for order p^{3/2}.
The missing saving is enormous and must come from cancellation as h varies.

This identifies the exact new object:

  [AVG-FIBERPOWER-8]
  a square-root/cancellation theorem in the h-direction for the eighth
  fiber-power trace.

It is a two-parameter family theorem. The one-variable S_{3h} monodromy controls
the invariant space at each fixed h, but not cancellation of the nontrivial
cohomological traces as h runs through an interval. Additive completion in h,
uniform conductor control, or a genuine two-clock monodromy theorem would be
needed.

In other words, the wall has not disappeared; it has moved from the zero fiber
to the h-average of the higher fiber-power error.

# 9. Dependence on the all-h monodromy hypothesis

The conclusions separate cleanly.

## 9.1 Unconditional certified range h<=40

For every fixed m and every certified good prime, the moment theorem (4.3) holds
for 2<=h<=40. This gives a valid higher-moment certificate in that finite range.
It gives no asymptotic census improvement, because

  sum_{h<=40} R_h=O(1)

already follows from deg N_h=3h-3.

## 9.2 Conditional range h<=p^delta

Assume [MON_h,p] for every h<=p^delta. Then (4.3) is uniform with the displayed
O_m(h^m sqrt p) dependence. Nevertheless, the pointwise and summed bounds do not
improve the degree estimate at any delta, because of the incompatibility
(5.6)-(5.7).

Thus extending the S_{3h} theorem from h<=40 to all h is extremely valuable for
[L2-FREQ] and the collision geometry, but it does not by itself prove W3.

## 9.3 A hypothesis that really suffices

All-h S_{3h} plus [AVG-FIBERPOWER-8] does prove W3. Equivalently, one may replace
[AVG-FIBERPOWER-8] by the weaker zero-column moment bound

  sum_{h<=D} nu_h(0)^8 << pD.                           (9.1)

But (9.1) is already an anti-concentration theorem for the special value zero;
it is not a consequence of fixed-h monodromy.

# 10. Final answers to the race questions

(a) Does Mon(f_h)=S_{3h} give Bell/Poisson moments?

Yes. For fixed m,

  sum_a nu_h(a)^m
    = b_m(3h)p+O_m(h^m sqrt p),                         (10.1)

and b_m(3h)=B_m when m<=3h. The main constant is the number of S_{3h}-orbits on
ordered m-tuples, equivalently the restricted Bell number.

(b) Is the higher fiber product irreducible?

Not as a whole. It has one component for every equality partition. The
all-distinct j-tuple component is geometrically irreducible because S_{3h} is
j-transitive. For m<=3h there are B_m dominant components in total.

(c) Is its dimension 2k-1 for the 2k-th moment?

No. It is a curve of dimension 1. Its complexity grows through degree and genus,
not dimension.

(d) Can one choose k dyadically or let k grow to get
sqrt(p)p^{o(1)}?

No, not from individual Lang-Weil estimates. The 2k-th-root error is

  h p^{1/(4k)},                                        (10.2)

which tends to h. At D=sqrt p, the summed error/trivial contribution stays at
exponent 1.

(e) What is the exact win condition?

An averaged m-th moment bound of size pD. The eighth moment is the first even
moment that yields W3 at D=sqrt(p)L with L=p^{o(1)}.

# 11. Least-confident step

The least-confident step is not the Bell constant or the exponent h^m; those are
structural and can be checked independently by the orbit decomposition,
Riemann-Hurwitz, and the complete-intersection genus formula.

The least-confident detail is the completely uniform reduction statement when h
grows with p: characteristic-zero monodromy S_{3h} implies the required
finite-field geometric monodromy for all but finitely many primes for each fixed
h, but the exceptional set can depend on h. A theorem asserted simultaneously
for every h<=p^delta needs a uniform good-reduction certificate. The current
h<=40 certificates supply this only in their recorded range.

This issue does not weaken the no-go. Even granting perfect good reduction and
S_{3h} for every h, the h^m genus term still blocks the bridge.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Likely blind spot

The likely shared blind spot was treating "full monodromy controls every moment"
as though it meant "every moment has a uniform O(sqrt p) error." Full monodromy
controls the invariant/main-term dimension. It does not control the conductor of
the tensor/fiber-power object independently of h and m.

Here that conductor is visible geometrically: the j-distinct component has genus
Theta_j((3h)^j). Once this factor is restored, taking higher roots never beats
the degree bound.

A second blind spot is that the special value zero is arithmetically aligned
across all h. Averaging over values at each fixed h loses exactly that alignment.
Any successful bridge must use cross-h cancellation or a zero-specific algebraic
operation; fixed-h equidistribution, even in all moments, does not see it.
