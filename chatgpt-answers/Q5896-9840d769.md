# Q5896 Terminal boundary packet problem

## Status

The requested \log\operatorname{rad}\gcd(B_-,B_+)=o(n) conclusion is not derivable from the present six-column Smith reduction alone. The actual Apéry constant-term expressions do give a structured common-prime problem, but the available endpoint symmetry is insufficient to force every large common prime into a fixed polynomial in n. The obstruction is that the two endpoint constant terms have different Newton supports after the maximal-margin specialization.

## Endpoint constant-term formulas

Let

[

D=lfloor13n/20rfloor,qquad N=lfloor n/5rfloor,

]

and at maximal margin

[

d=D-m+1,qquad L=N+m-2.

]

The lower endpoint factor is

[

B_- = Delta^L C_{n-1}(d).

]

Using the same Apéry shell constant-term representation as in Sections 57--61, the endpoint coefficient has the form

[

C_{n-1}(a)=operatorname{CT}left(Lambda^{,n-1} X^{-akappa}(X^{-kappa}-1)^Lright),

]

with the constant term taken in all shell variables and the finite \kappa-sum retained. Hence

[

B_- = Delta^Lsum_kappa operatorname{CT}left(Lambda^{n-1}X^{-dkappa}(X^{-kappa}-1)^Lright).

]

At maximality the active side has zero surplus, so this is the clean terminal packet.

The upper endpoint is

[

B_+ = Delta^L C_{n-1}(D),

]

but the corresponding expansion is not the same zero-surplus packet. It lies in the quotient-two/first-cell regime, so after extracting the first nonzero boundary cell one obtains a shifted expression of the form

[

B_+=Delta^Lsum_kappa operatorname{CT}left(Lambda^{n-1}X^{-Dkappa},P_{kappa,L}(X)right),

]

where P_{\kappa,L} is the first-cell quotient-two polynomial. In particular it is not a scalar multiple of (X^{-\kappa}-1)^L.

## Attempted common resultant/WZ relation

A WZ elimination would require a rational certificate relating the two endpoint summands:

[

F_D(kappa)-R(n,X,kappa)F_d(kappa)=Delta_kappa G(n,X,kappa).

]

For this to imply a fixed resultant controlling all large primes, the two summands must have compatible exponent polytopes. They do not.

The difference between the endpoints is the translation

[

X^{-Dkappa}/X^{-dkappa}=X^{-(D-d)kappa}=X^{-(m-1)kappa}.

]

The lower packet has support generated by the Minkowski sum

[

operatorname{Newt}(Lambda^{n-1})+Loperatorname{Newt}(X^{-kappa}-1),

]

whereas the upper packet has the first-cell support

[

operatorname{Newt}(Lambda^{n-1})+operatorname{Newt}(P_{kappa,L})-Dkappa e_X.

]

The extra quotient-two cell introduces boundary exponents not present in the lower packet. Therefore no fixed bounded-degree resultant in n follows from polytope intersection alone.

## Landau-function obstruction

The factorial valuations of coefficients are governed by the Landau functions attached to the actual exponent vectors of \Lambda. A common large prime divisor would have to survive both endpoint Landau inequalities. The two Landau regions overlap along the inherited shell factors, but the quotient-two first cell adds independent inequalities.

Thus the strongest unconditional statement available is:

- primes dividing the common gcd outside the explicit Pascal/Kummer factors must lie in the intersection of two actual Apéry Landau supports;

- this intersection is smaller than either endpoint support;

- however, it is not shown to be bounded by a fixed polynomial in n.

## Coefficient-height audit

The endpoint polynomials have coefficient heights inherited from \Lambda^{n-1} and the binomial expansion of the boundary factor. The naive resultant between the two full endpoint packets has exponential height:

[

log H(operatorname{Res}(B_-,B_+))=Theta(n)

]

in the absence of a new cancellation identity. Therefore it cannot currently yield the desired o(n) radical bound.

After removing explicit Pascal/Kummer factors, the remaining target-selective gcd is a better object, but the same obstruction remains: the quotient-two endpoint contributes genuinely new monomials.

## Conclusion

The terminal packet attack does not currently prove

[

logoperatorname{rad}gcd(B_-,B_+)=o(n).

]

A proof would require a new Apéry-specific identity showing that the quotient-two first-cell contribution is an exact WZ coboundary modulo a bounded-height polynomial factor. The actual \Lambda structure prevents replacing the problem by an arbitrary-array argument, but it also prevents the desired fixed-polynomial-prime conclusion from following automatically.

The concrete obstruction is the non-matching Newton polytope of the maximal zero-surplus packet and the quotient-two first-cell packet.
