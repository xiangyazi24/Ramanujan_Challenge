# Executive verdict

The degree-40 polynomial is the wrong object on which to perform the rational-point proof.

For the standard primitive exact-order factor, the correct answers are:

- F25 has no nonconstant factor over ℚ[b,c]—up to a rational unit, it is the irreducible modular equation defining the Tate-normal-form chart of X₁(25);

- the smooth projective normalization has genus

- the naive degree-40 plane closure has arithmetic genus 741, so it is extremely singular;

- X₁(25)(ℚ) consists of exactly 10 rational cusps;

- “factor into components, observe genus ≥ 2, and finish” is not viable: there is essentially one component, and genus ≥ 2 proves only finiteness, not the point list.

The best Lean architecture is:

```plain text
Tate witness (b,c)
  → explicit birational map to a small X₁(25) model
  → a separately proved theorem that the small model has only its 10 cusps
  → exact cusp-preimage certificates force b=0, b=c, or Δ=0
  → contradiction.
```

Do not formalize resolution of singularities or irreducibility of the 234-term polynomial unless another theorem genuinely needs them. Use F25 only as the input relation for denominator-cleared polynomial certificates.

## Modern explicit models and computations

Two especially useful computational sources are:

- M. Derickx and M. van Hoeij, Gonality of the modular curve X₁(N), J. Algebra 417 (2014), 52–71;

- A. Sutherland and M. van Hoeij, optimized equations for X₁(N) and alternative defining equations.

Derickx–van Hoeij prove that the ℚ-gonality of X₁(25) is 5 and provide Magma-verifiable finite-field/Jacobian computations. Their N=25 data include:

- J₁(25)(ℚ) is finite of odd order;

- reduction at 2 therefore injects J₁(25)(ℚ) into J₁(25)(𝔽₂);

- the smooth curve X₁(25) has no degree-2 places over 𝔽₂;

- its 𝔽₂-rational places are exactly the reductions of the 10 rational cusps.

These facts already imply the rational-point classification. If P ∈ X₁(25)(ℚ), choose a rational cusp C with the same reduction mod 2. Then [P-C] maps to zero in J₁(25)(𝔽₂), hence [P-C]=0 by injectivity. Since a positive-genus curve has injective degree-one Abel–Jacobi map, P=C.

This is a clean modern computational proof, but formalizing a genus-12 Jacobian and its reduction map from scratch is a substantial Lean project.

# 5. Why simple local obstruction is unlikely to finish

A check such as

```plain text
there are no affine noncuspidal zeros of F25 modulo p
```

is not, by itself, a proof over ℚ.

A rational point on the smooth projective curve has a reduction at every good prime, but it may reduce to a cusp. Since rational cusps exist, the finite-field curve is never empty. Even if every 𝔽_p-point is cuspidal, one still needs a formal-immersion or Jacobian-injectivity argument to prove that no rational noncuspidal point lies in a cusp residue disk.

Checking many primes does not repair this without a height bound or a Mordell–Weil sieve. The cusp selected by reduction may vary with p.

A useful finite-field experiment is nevertheless:

```javascript
C := Normalization(ProjectiveClosure(Curve(AffineSpace(Rationals(),2),F25)));
for p in [2,3,7,11,13,17,19] do
    Cp := ChangeRing(C,GF(p));
    printf "p=%o, #C(F_p)=%o\n", p, #Points(Cp);
end for;
```

The crucial warning is that the count must be on the smooth projective normalization, not on the singular affine degree-40 equation.

The plan

```plain text
factor F25
→ prove every component has genus ≥ 2
→ conclude no rational points
```

fails twice:

1. the primitive F25 is already irreducible;

1. genus at least 2 gives only finiteness by Faltings, not a list of rational points.

Even an available formalization of Faltings' theorem would not prove that the finite set consists of cusps.

Moreover, resolving the singularities of the degree-40 plane model in Lean would be much harder than proving the intended modular or birational bridge.

# 7. Two viable rational-point endpoints

## Route A: rank-zero Jacobian plus reduction at 2

This is the cleanest modern mathematical certificate.

Required formal statements:

```plain text
C25 is a smooth projective genus-12 curve over Q;
C25 has good reduction at 2;
J25(Q) is finite of odd order;
reduction J25(Q) → J25(F2) is injective;
C25(F2) is exactly the 10 reductions of rational cusps;
P ↦ [P-C0] is injective.
```

The final proof is only a few lines. The cost is the general genus-12 Jacobian infrastructure and the certificate that J25(ℚ) is finite odd.

This route is attractive only if the FLT development is already acquiring general divisor-class and Jacobian reduction machinery.

## Route B: explicit diamond quotient and covering descent

This better matches the existing explicit N17/N18/N19 style.

The diamond group is

Compute explicit invariant subfields for its order-2 and order-5 subgroups. One intermediate quotient is the genus-4 curve traditionally called Kubert's curve B; modern work identifies it as X_{Δ₁}(25) and notes that it is not bielliptic. See:

- D. Jeon, C. H. Kim, A. Schweizer, Bielliptic intermediate modular curves.

Because this genus-4 quotient is not bielliptic, do not assume it automatically reduces to an elliptic rank-zero computation. Instead, use Sage/Magma to determine which of these is actually cheapest:

- a cyclic degree-5 covering descent over a rational parameter;

- a degree-2 lift from the genus-4 quotient;

- a decomposition of the genus-4 Jacobian into lower-dimensional modular factors;

- a Mordell–Weil sieve on the optimized model.

The desired endpoint is a finite list of quotient rational points plus explicit fiber polynomials. Then Lean only checks rational-function identities, low-genus point classifications, and no-root certificates.
