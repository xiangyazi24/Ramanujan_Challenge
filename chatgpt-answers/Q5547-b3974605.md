# CFVZ square roots: exhaustive audit of the boundary-telescoping escape hatch

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
F(t)=\sum_{r\ge 0}B_rt^r,
\qquad D(t)=1-34t+t^2,
\]

and let

\[
S_+(t)=F(t)^{1/2}=\sum_{n\ge0}s_n^+t^n,
\qquad
S_-(t)=\left(\frac{F(t)}{D(t)}\right)^{1/2}
       =\sum_{n\ge0}s_n^-t^n.
\]

The normalized CFVZ square root modulo \(p\) is the appropriate first-half truncation of \(S_+\) in the square branch and of \(S_-\) in the discriminant-times-square branch. Thus

\[
B_r=\sum_{i=0}^r s_i^+s_{r-i}^+
\tag{E+}
\]

in the first branch, while, with

\[
c_r^-:=\sum_{i=0}^r s_i^-s_{r-i}^-,
\]

one has

\[
B_r=c_r^- -34c_{r-1}^-+c_{r-2}^-.
\tag{E-}
\]

The sharply targeted conclusion is:

> **There is a genuine Christoffel--Darboux identity for the normalized square-root recurrence, but it is in the same coefficient index and at two spectral parameters. It does not telescope the reversed-index convolution \(s_i s_{r-i}\). For the reversed convolution, the natural Lagrange concomitant has an exact compatibility equation whose rational solutions are obstructed by a parity-orbit divisor imbalance. The most general uniform rational adjacent-state concomitant is also impossible: it would force a quadratic--linear algebraic differential relation for \(S_\pm\), whereas the connected differential Galois group is \(\mathrm{SL}_2\).**

There is a parity-split **hypergeometric weighted Green identity**, but its coefficient has Pochhammer length comparable with \(r\). Clearing it has linearly growing pole/degree complexity and logarithmic height of order at least \(r\log r\); it is not an \(O(1)\)-boundary formula for the unweighted convolution and cannot give a low-height cross-prime certificate.

The first nontrivial bounded outer telescoper is therefore not a new identity. It is exactly the symmetric-square Apéry recurrence

\[
(r+1)^3B_{r+1}
=(34r^3+51r^2+27r+5)B_r-r^3B_{r-1}.
\tag{A}
\]

The differential module proves that this is minimal in the relevant sense: the minimal scalar differential operator for \(F=D^\varepsilon S_\varepsilon^2\) has order \(3\), because it is the irreducible symmetric square of an \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-module. At a zero \(B_r=0\), (A) gives only the already known neighbor relation. Two zeros give exactly the existing gap-continuant certificate and no transverse square-root condition.

Consequently this escape hatch yields:

- no exponent below \(2/3\) for \(|Z_p|\);
- no new factorial-moment or collision estimate;
- no forbidden zero configuration beyond what the Apéry recurrence already gives;
- and no fixed-\(n\) cross-prime certificate.

The narrowest useful statement still open is an actual-return anti-concentration estimate

\[
\mathcal A_p(H)
:=\#\{(r,h):1\le h\le H,\ B_r\equiv B_{r+h}\equiv0\pmod p\}
\ll H^{2-\eta}p^{o(1)}
\tag{AC}
\]

for some fixed \(\eta>0\). This alone would break the \(2/3\) exponent. Boundary telescoping does not prove it.

## 2. A real Christoffel--Darboux identity, but for the wrong sum

The second-order recurrence can be put in self-adjoint form exactly.

### Proposition 2.1 — self-adjoint normalization

Set

\[
h_n^{(\varepsilon)}
=(2n+1)^\varepsilon\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n},
\qquad s_n=h_n^{(\varepsilon)}y_n,
\tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
p_n=(n+1)(2n+2\varepsilon+1).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Then

\[
p_ny_{n+1}+p_{n-1}y_{n-1}+12y_n
=17(p_n+p_{n-1})y_n.
\tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

The ratios are

\[
\frac{h_{n+1}}{h_n}
=\frac{2n+2\varepsilon+1}{2(n+1)},
\qquad
\frac{h_{n-1}}{h_n}
=\frac{2n}{2n+2\varepsilon-1}.
\]

Substitution into (1.6), followed by division by \(2h_n\), gives

\[
p_ny_{n+1}+p_{n-1}y_{n-1}=V_ny_n.
\]

A direct calculation gives the useful identity

\[
V_n=17(p_n+p_{n-1})-12,
\]

which is (2.3). ∎

Introduce the spectral family \(Y_n(\lambda)\) by

\[
p_nY_{n+1}(\lambda)+p_{n-1}Y_{n-1}(\lambda)+12Y_n(\lambda)
=\lambda(p_n+p_{n-1})Y_n(\lambda).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The actual square-root coefficient is \(y_n=Y_n(17)\), with the normalized initial data.

### Proposition 2.2 — exact Christoffel--Darboux identity

For every \(N\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(\lambda-\mu)
\sum_{i=0}^N(p_i+p_{i-1})Y_i(\lambda)Y_i(\mu)\\
&\qquad =p_N\bigl(
Y_{N+1}(\lambda)Y_N(\mu)
-Y_N(\lambda)Y_{N+1}(\mu)
\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
\]

where \(p_{-1}=0\).

#### Proof

Multiply (2.4) at parameter \(\lambda\) by \(Y_i(\mu)\), multiply the equation at \(\mu\) by \(Y_i(\lambda)\), subtract, and sum over \(i\). The two off-diagonal terms telescope. ∎

This is the precise positive result one would hope to exploit. It nevertheless misses the target in three independent ways:

1. the summation uses the **same index** \(i\) in both solutions;
2. the target uses the reversed index \(r-i\);
3. the CD weight is \(p_i+p_{i-1}\), whereas the convolution weight after normalization is
   \(h_i h_{r-i}\).

Putting \(\lambda=\mu=17\) does not repair this. It produces the usual derivative/norm form of Christoffel--Darboux, still with the same index. Thus the fixed second-order recurrence really does possess a CD structure, but not the one needed for \(c_r\).

## 3. The reversed recurrence and the exact Lagrange-certificate equations

Fix \(r\), and define

\[
u_i=s_i,
\qquad
v_i=s_{r-i}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then \(u_i\) satisfies the forward recurrence

\[
A_i u_{i+1}=B_i u_i-C_i u_{i-1},
\tag{3.2}
\]

while \(v_i\) satisfies the reversed recurrence

\[
C_{r-i}v_{i+1}=B_{r-i}v_i-A_{r-i}v_{i-1}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The two operators are not the same Jacobi operator: their leading and trailing coefficients are exchanged and evaluated at reflected indices.

Consider the most natural two-term bilinear concomitant

\[
\mathcal W_i
=X_i u_{i+1}v_i-Y_i u_i v_{i+1}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Expanding with (3.2)--(3.3) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal W_i-\mathcal W_{i-1}
={}&\left(\frac{X_iB_i}{A_i}
          -\frac{Y_iB_{r-i}}{C_{r-i}}\right)u_iv_i\\
&+\left(Y_{i-1}-\frac{X_iC_i}{A_i}\right)u_{i-1}v_i\\
&+\left(\frac{Y_iA_{r-i}}{C_{r-i}}-X_{i-1}\right)u_iv_{i-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This identity is the symbolic test: the three displayed monomials are independent for arbitrary local solution states, so their coefficients must be compared separately. No numerical pattern recognition is being used.

To remove the two cross terms one must have

\[
Y_{i-1}=\frac{X_iC_i}{A_i},
\qquad
X_{i-1}=\frac{Y_iA_{r-i}}{C_{r-i}}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
Y_i=\frac{X_{i+1}C_{i+1}}{A_{i+1}},
\tag{3.7}
\]

and the remaining compatibility condition is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{X_{i+1}}{X_{i-1}}
=\frac{A_{i+1}C_{r-i}}{C_{i+1}A_{r-i}}
=\left(
\frac{(i+2)(2r-2i+2\varepsilon-1)}
     {(2i+2\varepsilon+1)(r-i+1)}
\right)^2.}
\tag{3.8}
\]

When (3.8) holds, one gets the exact weighted Green identity

\[
\mathcal W_i-\mathcal W_{i-1}
=\Omega_i u_iv_i,
\tag{3.9}
\]

with

\[
\Omega_i
=\frac{X_iB_i}{A_i}
-\frac{X_{i+1}C_{i+1}B_{r-i}}
       {A_{i+1}C_{r-i}}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Thus the entire natural Lagrange problem has been reduced to the first-order two-step equation (3.8).

## 4. No rational or polynomial two-term CD certificate

### Theorem 4.1 — parity-orbit obstruction

For every integer \(r\ge0\) and either \(arepsilon=0\) or \(1\), equation (3.8) has no nonzero solution

\[
X_i=X(i,r)\in \overline{\mathbf Q(r)}(i)
\]

that is rational in the summation index \(i\). In particular, there is no polynomial or rational two-term Christoffel--Darboux concomitant whose difference is a rational multiple of \(s_i s_{r-i}\).

#### Proof

For a nonzero rational function \(X(i)\), the divisor of

\[
\frac{X(i+1)}{X(i-1)}
\]

has total multiplicity zero on every orbit of the translation \(i\mapsto i+2\). Indeed, if a zero or pole of \(X\) occurs at \(\alpha\), its contribution to the quotient occurs at \(\alpha-1\) and \(\alpha+1\) with opposite signs; summing on each \(2\mathbf Z\)-orbit telescopes.

The right side of (3.8) has, with multiplicity \(2\), zeros at

\[
i=-2,
\qquad
i=r+\varepsilon-\frac12,
\]

and poles at

\[
i=-\varepsilon-\frac12,
\qquad
i=r+1.
\]

The integer zero and pole differ by

\[
(r+1)-(-2)=r+3,
\]

while the half-integer zero and pole differ by

\[
\left(r+\varepsilon-\frac12\right)
-\left(-\varepsilon-\frac12\right)
=r+2\varepsilon.
\]

Exactly one of \(r+3\) and \(r+2\varepsilon\) is even:

- for \(arepsilon=0\), their parities are those of \(r+3\) and \(r\);
- for \(arepsilon=1\), their parities are those of \(r+3\) and \(r+2\).

Hence exactly one of the integer and half-integer divisor pairs lies in one translation-by-\(2\) orbit. The other orbit has nonzero total divisor multiplicity, contradicting the necessary orbit-balance property of a rational shift quotient. ∎

Two points are worth emphasizing.

1. This obstruction is independent of the desired weight \(\Omega_i\). It occurs before asking whether \(\Omega_i=1\), a polynomial, or any other controlled rational function.
2. It applies in both CFVZ branches. The only change between them is \(arepsilon=0\) versus \(1\), and the parity imbalance persists.

So the most classical discrete Green/Lagrange route is exhausted completely: the reversed operator is not rationally gauge-equivalent to the forward operator in the way required by a two-term Casoratian.

## 5. What does exist: a parity-split hypergeometric Green weight

Equation (3.8) can be solved separately on the even and odd subsequences. Let \(e\in\{0,1\}\). For \(i=e+2k\), one obtains

\[
\boxed{
X_{e+2k}
=X_e\left[
\frac{
\bigl((e+3)/2\bigr)_k
\bigl((-2r+2e-2\varepsilon+3)/4\bigr)_k
}{
\bigl((2e+2\varepsilon+3)/4\bigr)_k
\bigl((e-r)/2\bigr)_k
}
\right]^2.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This follows by substituting \(i=e+2j+1\) into (3.8) and multiplying the resulting one-step ratios for \(j=0,\ldots,k-1\).

Together with

\[
Y_i=\frac{X_{i+1}C_{i+1}}{A_{i+1}},
\]

formula (5.1) gives a completely explicit identity (3.9). This is the strongest positive Green identity produced by the attack.

It is not useful for the coefficient-zero problem:

- it telescopes a **hypergeometric-weighted** convolution, not \(c_r\);
- the two parity classes carry independent seed constants \(X_0,X_1\);
- the Pochhammer products have length \(\asymp r\) in the critical range;
- their zeros and poles move through \(\asymp r\) affine factors;
- clearing denominators has degree \(\asymp r\) and logarithmic height \(O(r\log r)\), rather than bounded or logarithmic complexity;
- reduction modulo a prime comparable with \(r\) introduces endpoint and denominator degeneracies that must be normalized separately.

Thus (5.1) is a legitimate special-function identity, but it has exactly the growth one was trying to avoid. It cannot produce a fixed low-degree Casoratian or a subexponential common integer across different primes.

A finite-range identity with coefficients chosen recursively always exists in a vacuous sense: one can encode the partial sums into the coefficients. Formula (5.1) shows where that encoding lives here. Its complexity grows with the full convolution length.

## 6. The general adjacent-state concomitant

The failure above is not an artifact of choosing the cross-Casoratian (3.4). The general local bilinear problem can be written exactly.

Normalize

\[
a_i=\frac{B_i}{A_i},
\quad d_i=\frac{C_i}{A_i},
\qquad
\bar a_i=\frac{B_{r-i}}{C_{r-i}},
\quad \bar d_i=\frac{A_{r-i}}{C_{r-i}}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Set

\[
U_i=\binom{u_i}{u_{i-1}},
\qquad
V_i=\binom{v_i}{v_{i-1}}.
\]

Then

\[
U_{i+1}=M_iU_i,
\qquad
M_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
a_i&-d_i\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and

\[
V_{i+1}=N_iV_i,
\qquad
N_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
\bar a_i&-\bar d_i\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let

\[
\Phi_i=U_i^TK_iV_i,
\qquad
K_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
x_i&y_i\\z_i&w_i
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

A local telescoper

\[
\Phi_{i+1}-\Phi_i=\omega_i u_iv_i
\tag{6.3}
\]

is equivalent to the matrix equation

\[
\boxed{
M_i^TK_{i+1}N_i-K_i=\omega_iE_{11}.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Writing \(K_{i+1}=(x,y;z,w)\), its four scalar equations are

\[
a_i\bar a_i x+a_i y+\bar a_i z+w-x_i=\omega_i,
\tag{6.5a}
\]

\[
-\bar d_i(a_i x+z)-y_i=0,
\tag{6.5b}
\]

\[
-d_i(\bar a_i x+y)-z_i=0,
\tag{6.5c}
\]

\[
d_i\bar d_i x-w_i=0.
\tag{6.5d}
\]

Equations (6.5a)--(6.5d) are the complete symbolic certificate system for every adjacent-state bilinear concomitant. The two-term ansatz of Section 3 is a specialization, not an assumption about the full problem.

## 7. Differential-module obstruction to every uniform rational boundary collapse

The matrix system allows a clean global no-go theorem.

### Theorem 7.1 — no bounded rational order-zero telescoper

There is no family \(K_i(r)\) with entries rational in \(i,r\), of uniformly bounded algebraic complexity, such that

\[
M_i^TK_{i+1}N_i-K_i=E_{11}
\tag{7.1}
\]

for all \(i,r\) away from a bounded exceptional set. More generally, no nonzero polynomially weighted convolution of \(s_i s_{r-i}\) can collapse uniformly to finitely many boundary values of the \(s\)-state by a rational adjacent-state concomitant, unless the weighted convolution is identically zero.

#### Step 1: a telescoper would give a linear boundary formula

Summing (7.1) from \(i=0\) to \(r\) gives

\[
c_r=\Phi_{r+1}-\Phi_0.
\tag{7.2}
\]

At \(i=0\) and \(i=r+1\), one factor of the bilinear product is one of the fixed initial values

\[
s_{-1}=0,
\qquad s_0=1.
\]

Therefore the right side of (7.2) is a rational linear combination of finitely many high-index values \(s_{r+j}\). The second-order recurrence reduces those to \(s_r,s_{r-1}\). After clearing denominators, one obtains a nontrivial identity

\[
Q(r)c_r=P_0(r)s_r+P_1(r)s_{r-1}
\tag{7.3}
\]

with polynomials \(P_0,P_1,Q\), up to a finite initial correction.

For a finite-width concomitant using more shifts, the same reduction gives (7.3): every fixed-width boundary window is two-dimensional.

#### Step 2: generating functions turn (7.3) into a quadratic--linear differential relation

For the square branch \(c_r=[t^r]S_+^2\). Summing (7.3) over \(r\) gives an identity of the shape

\[
Q(\theta)S_+^2
=L_0(t,\theta)S_+ +H(t),
\tag{7.4}
\]

where \(L_0\) is linear in the derivatives of \(S_+\) and \(H\) is a polynomial accounting for finitely many initial indices.

For a polynomial weight in \(i\) and \(r-i\), use the exact identity

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}
\left(\sum_{i=0}^r i^a(r-i)^b s_i s_{r-i}\right)t^r
=(\theta^aS)(\theta^bS).
\tag{7.5}
\]

The second-order equation reduces every higher derivative to a linear combination of \(S\) and \(	heta S\). Hence any claimed polynomially weighted boundary collapse gives a nonzero polynomial relation of degree at most \(2\) between

\[
S
\quad\text{and}\quad
\theta S
\]

over \(\overline{\mathbf Q(t)}\).

The discriminant branch is identical: a direct telescoper for (1.8) gives a quadratic differential expression for

\[
D S_-^2=F
\]

equal to a linear boundary expression in \(S_-,\theta S_-\).

#### Step 3: \(S\) and \(	heta S\) are algebraically independent

CFVZ use the algebraic substitution

\[
t=\frac{x(1-8x)}{1+x}
\]

and a period \(h(x)\) satisfying

\[
F(t(x))=(1+x)h(x)^2.
\tag{7.6}
\]

In the proof of **Lemma 2.3**, \(h\) is algebraically gauged to

\[
g(y)={}_2F_1\left(\frac13,\frac23;1;y\right),
\qquad
y=\frac{27x^2}{(1-2x)^3}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

The Gauss equation in (7.7) is irreducible because none of

\[
\frac13,
\quad\frac23,
\quad1-\frac13,
\quad1-\frac23
\]

is an integer. Its local monodromy at \(y=0\) and \(y=1\) contains nontrivial unipotents. Irreducibility forces their fixed lines to be distinct. Two nontrivial unipotent subgroups with distinct fixed lines generate a Zariski-dense subgroup of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\). Thus the connected differential Galois group is \(\mathrm{SL}_2\).

Algebraic pullback and algebraic gauge do not change the connected derived group. Both \(S_+\) and \(S_-=S_+/\sqrt D\) therefore have connected differential Galois group \(\mathrm{SL}_2\).

If a nonzero algebraic polynomial relation

\[
R(S,\theta S)=0
\]

existed, applying the differential Galois group would make \(R\) vanish on the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-orbit of a nonzero solution vector. That orbit is Zariski dense in the two-dimensional vector space. Hence \(R\) would have to be the zero polynomial. Therefore

\[
S,\theta S
\quad\text{are algebraically independent over}
\quad \overline{\mathbf Q(t)}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

This contradicts the nonzero quadratic--linear relation forced in Step 2. The theorem follows. ∎

### What the theorem excludes

It excludes uniformly, in both branches:

- an unweighted \(O(1)\)-boundary collapse;
- a polynomial or rational multiple of the summand collapsing to the boundary;
- a fixed-width bilinear concomitant involving finitely many neighboring \(s\)-states;
- a direct discriminant-filtered version;
- and any such identity after clearing a bounded number of rational poles.

It does **not** deny the existence of finite-range certificates whose degrees, poles, or recurrence depth grow with \(r\). Formula (5.1) is an explicit example of that unavoidable growth.

## 8. Bounded exceptional terms do not rescue the identity

The user-proposed escape hatch allowed \(O(1)\) exceptional summands, for example a central term when \(r\) has a given parity. Those terms also cannot repair the obstruction.

### Proposition 8.1 — asymptotic sector separation

Let

\[
\lambda=17+12\sqrt2.
\]

The dominant formal solutions of (1.3+) and (1.3-) have asymptotics

\[
s_n^+\sim \kappa_+\lambda^n n^{-3/2}
\left(1+\frac{a_1}{n}+\cdots\right),
\tag{8.1+}
\]

and

\[
s_n^-\sim \kappa_-\lambda^n n^{-1/2}
\left(1+\frac{b_1}{n}+\cdots\right),
\tag{8.1-}
\]

with nonzero constants \(\kappa_\pm\). Meanwhile

\[
B_n\sim \kappa_B\lambda^n n^{-3/2}
\left(1+\frac{c_1}{n}+\cdots\right).
\tag{8.2}
\]

#### Derivation of the exponents

Substitute \(s_n\sim\mu^nn^\alpha\) in (1.6). The leading equation is

\[
\mu^2-34\mu+1=0.
\]

At the dominant root \(\mu=\lambda\), the coefficient of \(1/n\) gives

\[
\alpha=\varepsilon-\frac32.
\]

This yields (8.1+) and (8.1-). The Apéry asymptotic (8.2) is the corresponding symmetric-square coefficient asymptotic.

Now let an exceptional index satisfy

\[
i=\alpha_0r+O(1),
\qquad 0<\alpha_0<1.
\]

Then

\[
s_i^\varepsilon s_{r-i}^\varepsilon
=\lambda^r r^{2\varepsilon-3}
\left(C_0+\frac{C_1}{r}+\cdots\right).
\tag{8.3}
\]

The exponent class modulo integers is therefore \(0\): it is \(-3\) in the \(+\) branch and \(-1\) in the \(-\) branch. Multiplication by rational functions of \(r\), finite shifts, and application of the fixed filter \(1-34E^{-1}+E^{-2}\) change exponents only by integers.

Boundary values \(s_{r+j}^arepsilon\), by contrast, lie in the half-integer exponent class, and the target \(B_r\) lies in the class \(-3/2+\mathbf Z\).

Consequently a finite number of genuine interior exceptional products cannot combine with boundary terms to produce \(B_r\). On each parity subsequence, cancellation of finitely many leading coefficients still leaves an integer-power asymptotic series; it cannot turn into a half-integer series. If all integer-power terms cancel, the exceptional contribution is asymptotically absent and one is back to the forbidden pure boundary formula.

Exceptions at bounded distance from \(0\) or \(r\) are already boundary terms and are covered by Theorem 7.1.

Thus allowing \(O(1)\) central or interior exceptions does not reopen the route.

## 9. The minimal outer telescoper is the original symmetric square

There is, of course, a bounded holonomic relation for the convolution. It is exactly the one already known.

The Apéry operator is

\[
\mathcal L_A
=\theta^3
-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\tag{9.1}
\]

One has

\[
\mathcal L_A(S_+^2)=0,
\qquad
\mathcal L_A(D S_-^2)=0.
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is the symmetric-square construction behind Q5532.

### Proposition 9.1 — minimality

Over \(\overline{\mathbf Q(t)}\), the minimal scalar differential operator annihilating

\[
F=D^\varepsilon S_\varepsilon^2
\]

has order \(3\).

#### Proof

The connected differential Galois group of the rank-two square-root module is \(\mathrm{SL}_2\). Its symmetric-square representation is irreducible of dimension \(3\). The Galois orbit of a nonzero square \(S^2\) spans the whole symmetric-square representation. Therefore any scalar differential operator annihilating \(S^2\) must have solution space of dimension at least \(3\). Equation (9.1) supplies an operator of order \(3\), so the lower bound is sharp. Multiplication by the algebraic factor \(D\) in the minus branch is a gauge and does not change the conclusion. ∎

In coefficient language, (9.1) is precisely the three-term recurrence

\[
(r+1)^3B_{r+1}
=(34r^3+51r^2+27r+5)B_r-r^3B_{r-1}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus the exact order audit is:

```text
summation variable i:
    no uniform rational order-zero boundary telescoper;

outer coefficient index r:
    minimal differential telescoper order = 3;
    corresponding P-recurrence shift order = 2 (three terms);
    this is exactly the original Apéry recurrence.
```

Nothing transverse appears at a zero. If \(B_r=0\), then

\[
(r+1)^3B_{r+1}=-r^3B_{r-1}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

This is the same local relation used by the existing recurrence argument. If \(B_r=B_{r+h}=0\), propagation of (9.3) gives the existing gap polynomial \(N_h(r)\) of degree \(3(h-1)\). The square-root realization does not add a second independent polynomial at the same pair of zeros.

In particular, the apparent drop from a third-order Picard--Fuchs operator to a second-order square-root operator does not lower the zero-pair certificate degree: the observable \(B_r\) is nonlocal in the square-root state, involving all \(r+1\) products in (E+) or (E-).

## 10. Dedicated check of the discriminant-times-square branch

The second CFVZ branch is not a loophole hidden by the unified notation.

Here

\[
F=D S_-^2,
\qquad
D=1-34t+t^2,
\]

so

\[
B_r=c_r^- -34c_{r-1}^-+c_{r-2}^-.
\]

There are three conceivable attacks.

### 10.1 Telescope \(c_r^-\) first, then apply \(D\)

Theorem 7.1 rules out the required rational boundary formula for \(c_r^-\). The parity-orbit obstruction in Theorem 4.1 applies with \(arepsilon=1\).

### 10.2 Telescope the filtered summand directly

A direct certificate is still a fixed finite-shift bilinear expression in the \(S_-\)-states. Its generating series is the quadratic expression \(D S_-^2\). A boundary collapse would give a quadratic--linear differential relation for \(S_-\), again contradicting the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) differential Galois group.

### 10.3 Use cancellation of the dominant pole caused by \(D\)

Indeed \(S_-^2\) has a stronger dominant singularity, and multiplication by \(D\) cancels its leading pole to produce the Apéry \(n^{-3/2}\) coefficient scale. This is already encoded in the gauge \(S_-=S_+/\sqrt D\). It creates no new local bilinear concomitant. Proposition 8.1 also shows that finitely many interior exceptional products remain in the integer asymptotic sector after the \(D\)-filter and cannot replace the half-integer Apéry sector.

Therefore the discriminant factor changes the fixed outer filter but not the telescoping obstruction.

## 11. Two-zero and cross-prime tests

### 11.1 Same-prime two-zero test

Had a boundary identity of the form

\[
B_r=R_0(r)s_r+R_1(r)s_{r-1}
\]

existed, \(B_r=0\) would have defined a projective line in the two-dimensional square-root state. Two zero positions could then have been compared by a \(2\times2\) Casoratian, potentially producing a lower-degree return polynomial.

Theorems 4.1 and 7.1 show that this line does not exist. The condition \(B_r=0\) remains a full Cauchy-cancellation condition, not a local condition on \((s_r,s_{r-1})\). Consequently two zeros yield only the Apéry symmetric-square return condition already encoded by \(N_h\).

### 11.2 Fixed-\(n\), different-prime test

For a top-half target prime one has \(r_p=n-p\) and

\[
p\mid B_{r_p}.
\]

A useful cross-prime certificate would need to turn each condition into divisibility of one common characteristic-zero integer of subexponential height, or at least into low-degree local state conditions that can be eliminated across nearby indices.

The only explicit Green coefficient found here is (5.1). Its Pochhammer length and pole divisor grow with \(r_p\); after clearing denominators its height is of linear-times-logarithmic scale. Different primes use different lengths and different reductions. It therefore supplies no common low-height integer.

A certificate constructed separately modulo each \(p\) by solving the finite matrix equations backwards merely encodes the partial sum. It has no characteristic-zero bounded-degree lift, exactly as Theorem 7.1 predicts, and cannot be compared across primes.

Thus the boundary-telescoping route gives no horizontal certificate.

## 12. The narrowest nontrivial anti-cancellation lemma still open

The exact obstruction suggests a more modest target that is both sufficient and genuinely about convolution cancellation.

For \(1\le H<p\), define the actual short-pair incidence

\[
\mathcal A_p(H)
=\#\left\{(r,h):
\begin{array}{l}
0\le r<r+h<p,\\
1\le h\le H,\\
B_r\equiv B_{r+h}\equiv0\pmod p
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{12.1}
\]

The current gap-polynomial degree argument gives only

\[
\mathcal A_p(H)\ll H^2.
\tag{12.2}
\]

The block-packing argument can be written in the sharper form

\[
|Z_p|
\le \left\lceil\frac pH\right\rceil+\mathcal A_p(H).
\tag{12.3}
\]

Therefore any fixed \(\eta>0\) estimate

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_p(H)
\ll H^{2-\eta}p^{o(1)}
}
\tag{12.4}
\]

uniformly in the critical range would imply, on taking

\[
H=p^{1/(3-\eta)},
\]

that

\[
|Z_p|
\ll p^{(2-\eta)/(3-\eta)+o(1)},
\tag{12.5}
\]

and

\[
\frac{2-\eta}{3-\eta}<\frac23.
\]

This is narrower than a full moment theorem or equidistribution theorem. It asks only that the **actual Apéry convolution zeros** occupy a power-saving fraction of the ambient roots allowed by all short gap continuants.

The present audit shows why such a result must be genuine anti-cancellation or return-direction information. It cannot be obtained by collapsing each convolution coefficient to a bounded number of square-root boundary values.

## 13. Final proof audit

```text
PROVED HERE
-----------
1. Exact self-adjoint gauge for both square-root recurrences.
2. Exact same-index Christoffel--Darboux formula.
3. Exact reversed-index Lagrange certificate equations.
4. No rational/polynomial two-term CD certificate, by a 2-shift
   divisor-orbit parity obstruction.
5. Explicit parity-split hypergeometric weighted Green identity.
6. Exact 2x2 matrix equation for every adjacent-state bilinear
   concomitant.
7. No uniform rational order-zero boundary telescoper, by the
   SL2 differential-module obstruction.
8. O(1) interior exceptional terms cannot repair the collapse,
   by asymptotic exponent-sector separation.
9. The minimal outer differential telescoper has order 3 and is
   exactly the Apéry symmetric-square operator.
10. The discriminant-times-square branch has the same obstruction.

NOT OBTAINED
------------
1. Any new exponent below 2/3 for |Z_p|.
2. Any nontrivial zero-pair or factorial-moment estimate.
3. Any new forbidden zero configuration.
4. Any transverse local condition on the square-root state at B_r=0.

PRECISE SURVIVING TARGET

A power-saving actual-return estimate
    A_p(H) << H^(2-eta) p^o(1)

```

The boundary-telescoping escape hatch is therefore exhausted. The second-order CFVZ square root has a beautiful self-adjoint and hypergeometric structure, but the coefficient-zero event lives in its symmetric-square convolution. The only bounded closure of that convolution is the original Apéry recurrence; obtaining new zero control requires a true anti-cancellation theorem, not another Lagrange identity.
