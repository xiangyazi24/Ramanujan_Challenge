# CFVZ square factorization versus zeros of Apéry coefficients

The Caruso–Fürnsinn–Vargas-Montoya–Zudilin factorization is genuine extra structure, but it does not yield a new bound for the coefficient-zero set. After combining it with reflection and the Apéry recurrence, I obtain the following exact conclusions.

1. The normalized square root is automatically reciprocal or anti-reciprocal.

1. Its coefficients are reductions of one of two fixed characteristic-zero power series, each satisfying an explicit second-order recurrence of degree two.

1. The Apéry coefficients are exact Cauchy convolutions of those square-root coefficients, or a fixed three-term filter of such convolutions.

1. The passage from square-root coefficients to the first half of the Apéry coefficient vector is triangular with diagonal (2). Thus square factorization by itself imposes no local coefficient-zero restriction.

1. Self-reciprocity only adds endpoint identifications. Explicit reciprocal-square countermodels have (p-O(1)) zero coefficients, arbitrarily long zero intervals, or ((p-1)/2) isolated zeros with no consecutive pair.

1. Even after forcing agreement with the actual Apéry recurrence for any prescribed initial range (0le rle K), one can retain (p-O(K)) zero coefficients. Hence any use of the recurrence confined to (K=o(p)) indices cannot extract a zero bound from the square factorization.

1. Using the full Apéry recurrence removes this freedom because the sequence is then uniquely determined. But substituting the convolution representation merely gives a nonlinear full-memory restatement of the same recurrence; it supplies no second hit-specific equation. I find no new exponent below (2/3), no new zero-set moment bound, no new forbidden configuration beyond recurrence-only facts, and no cross-prime certificate.

The strongest positive theorem derived here is the explicit square-root-period package in Section 4. It is potentially useful elsewhere, but it does not presently improve (Z_pll p^{2/3}).
