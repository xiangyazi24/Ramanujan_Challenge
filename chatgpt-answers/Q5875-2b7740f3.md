# Pure-cross sieve gain: exact averaged bound and a unit-resultant obstruction

## Executive verdict

I do not obtain an Apéry-specific saving factor tending to infinity over the ambient dimension-two Selberg sieve.

What can be proved exactly is the following.

Let `a>=1` be fixed, let `d` be either `a` or `a+1`, and let `H<=A log n`.  For a prime pair

```text
ell = p+h,
```

write the relevant two Apéry indices as

```text
j,

x = j-d*h.
```

Thus the two branches in the question are

```text
d=a:     p | b_j,   ell | b_(j-a*h),

d=a+1:   p | b_j,   ell | b_(j-(a+1)*h).
```

Let `B_(a,d)(n;H)` count the pure-cross pairs of either branch, without imposing adjacency.  Then the ordinary Selberg sieve gives the explicit mean bound

```text
B_(a,d)(n;H)
  <= (C_S*C_0/(2*a*(a+1)) + o_(a,A)(1))
     * n*H/log(n)^2,
```

where `C_S` is an absolute effective upper-bound-sieve constant and

```text
C_0 = product_(r>2 prime) (1 + 1/(r*(r-2))) < infinity.
```

Consequently, for `H=A log n`,

```text
B_(a,d)(n;A log n) <<_(a,A) n/log n.
```

This is exactly the critical PC scale.  It gives no function tending to infinity.

The obstruction from the actual Apéry continuant is exact.  Put

```text
k = d*h.
```

For even `k`, write

```text
N_k(x) = (2*x+k+1) C_k(x),
```

where `N_k` is the Apéry gap continuant and the displayed linear factor is the universal reflection factor.  On every pure-cross pair one has

```text
p   does not divide N_k(x),
ell does not divide N_k(x),

p   does not divide 2*x+k+1,
ell does not divide 2*x+k+1,

p   does not divide C_k(x),
ell does not divide C_k(x).
```

Thus, after removing the universal reflection factor, the remaining continuant is a unit in both target fields.  The endpoint resultant is therefore a unit, not a small extra divisor.  The continuant removes the degenerate same-characteristic pairs and then becomes arithmetically invisible on the pure-cross locus.

This has two consequences.

1. In the natural Fourier/bilinear formulation, multiplying by the saturated continuant only makes an invertible change of the local coefficients.  Parseval norms and the large-sieve constant are unchanged.  The Farey-spacing `n^2` term remains.

2. There is an exact recurrence-class countermodel.  For every fixed `a,A>1` and all sufficiently large `n`, one can choose one integral initial state for the same cleared Apéry recurrence such that every prime in the quotient-`a` arc is a target and

```text
Omega_(a,A)(n/log n)
```

of its adjacent gaps at most `A log n` are pure cross, with the saturated continuant a unit at both primes.  This is obtained by choosing the required one-dimensional local kernel independently modulo every prime and using CRT on the two initial coordinates.

Therefore no theorem using only

```text
the Apéry recurrence operator,
reflection,
the gap continuants and their resultants,
local Smith data,
and a black-box large-sieve or Selberg-sieve norm estimate
```

can give the desired mean saving uniformly over recurrence states.

This does **not** disprove a saving for the distinguished Apéry initial state `b_0=1,b_1=5`.  It proves that such a saving must use arithmetic of that fixed state which is absent from the continuant/resultant module: concretely, a cross-prime restriction on the opposite quotient residues or on the actual Fourier transforms of the Apéry zero sets.

# 3. The sharp averaged Selberg bound

Let

```text
X = n/(a+1),

L_a = n/a - n/(a+1) = n/(a*(a+1)).
```

For an even gap `h`, define

```text
S(h) = product_(r|h, r>2 prime) (r-1)/(r-2)
     = product_(r|h, r>2 prime) (1+1/(r-2)).
```

The dimension-two Selberg upper-bound sieve for `m(m+h)` gives, uniformly for `h<=A log n`,

```text
# {p in the quotient-a arc : p and p+h are prime}
 <= C_S*S(h)*(L_a+O_A(log n))/log(X)^2.
```

Here `C_S` is an absolute effective constant.  Therefore

```text
B_(a,d)(n;H)
 <= C_S*(L_a+O_A(log n))/log(X)^2
    * sum_(h<=H, h even) S(h).
```

The mean singular-series bound can be written with an explicit convergent Euler product.  Expand

```text
S(h)
 = sum_(r|h, r odd squarefree)
     product_(q|r) 1/(q-2).
```

Summing multiples of `r` gives

```text
sum_(h<=H, h even) S(h)
 <= (H/2)*C_0 + O(log H),
```

where

```text
C_0
 = sum_(r odd squarefree)
     (1/r) product_(q|r) 1/(q-2)
 = product_(q>2 prime) (1+1/(q*(q-2)))
 < infinity.
```

Hence, for fixed `a,A` and `H<=A log n`,

```text
B_(a,d)(n;H)
 <= (C_S*C_0/(2*a*(a+1)) + o_(a,A)(1))
    * n*H/log(n)^2.
```

For the union of the two branches `d=a,a+1`, multiply the right side by at most two.

At the logarithmic cutoff,

```text
B_(a,d)(n;A log n)
 <= (C_S*C_0*A/(2*a*(a+1)) + o_(a,A)(1))
    * n/log n.
```

This is a proved pointwise bound with the correct critical exponent.  It is not a little-oh theorem.

## Integer-carrier form

Let

```text
D_amb(n,H)
 = product over all ambient pairs in the two branches and h<=H
     of p*(p+h).
```

Every pure-cross pair divides this integer.  The preceding bound gives

```text
log D_amb(n,H)
 <= 2 log(n/a) * B_ambient(n,H)
 <= (C_S*C_0/(a*(a+1)) + o_(a,A)(1))
    * n*H/log n.
```

Thus

```text
fixed h:       log D_amb(n,h) = o(n),

H=A log n:     log D_amb(n,H) = O_(a,A)(n).
```

The Selberg ledger has no unused logarithm.  A saving factor `L(n)->infinity` in the full mean count would be enough, but it is not produced by the ambient sieve.

# 5. Unit theorem on the pure-cross locus

## Theorem 5.1 — saturated continuant unit

Assume

```text
p<ell=p+h,

p   | b_(x+k),
ell | b_x,

p   does not divide b_x,
ell does not divide b_(x+k),

k=d*h,

0<=x<x+k<p<ell.
```

Then

```text
p   does not divide N_k(x),
ell does not divide N_k(x).
```

### Proof for `ell`

If `ell|N_k(x)`, then the transfer identity and `ell|b_x` give

```text
ell | Q_k(x)b_(x+k).
```

Every factor of `Q_k(x)` is below `ell`, hence is an `ell`-unit.  Thus `ell|b_(x+k)`, contradicting pure-crossness.

### Proof for `p`

If `p|N_k(x)`, the transfer identity, `p|b_(x+k)`, and `p not|b_x` force

```text
p | N_(k-1)(x+1).
```

The Dodgson identity is

```text
N_k(x)N_(k-2)(x+1)
 -N_(k-1)(x)N_(k-1)(x+1)
 =-product_(r=2)^(k-1)(x+r)^6.
```

The right side is a `p`-unit because `x+k<p`.  The two asserted divisibilities contradict this identity.  The case `k=1` is immediate from `N_1=1`.

This proves the theorem.

## Corollary 5.2 — the reflection factor is also a unit

On the same pure-cross pair,

```text
p   does not divide 2*x+k+1,
ell does not divide 2*x+k+1.
```

Indeed, in the relevant index range a divisibility by one of the two primes makes the two endpoints a reflected pair in that characteristic.  Reflection would then turn the known zero at one endpoint into a zero at the other endpoint, which is precisely the excluded degenerate case.

Therefore

```text
p   does not divide C_k(x),
ell does not divide C_k(x).
```

This is stronger than a failed divisibility experiment.  The proposed saturated continuant is forced to be nonzero in both fields on every hard pair.

# 6. What the transfer identity becomes after division

Write

```text
b_(x+k)=p*u,

b_x=ell*v.
```

The transfer law becomes

```text
(2*x+k+1) C_k(x) b_(x+1)
 = p Q_k(x)u - ell V_k(x)v.
```

Modulo the two primes,

```text
(2*x+k+1) C_k(x) b_(x+1)
  = -h V_k(x)v               mod p,

(2*x+k+1) C_k(x) b_(x+1)
  = -h Q_k(x)u               mod ell,
```

up to the harmless orientation sign in `V_k`.

Every displayed coefficient other than the opposite quotient residue is a unit.  Thus the saturated continuant does not cut down the local solution set.  It only converts

```text

u = b_(x+k)/p mod ell
```

into another pair of unit coordinates.

These opposite quotient residues are exactly the data left free in Section PC.  Averaging the continuants over `h` does not eliminate them.

# 7. Exact resultant obstruction

Use the local state

```text
y_x=(b_(x+1),b_x).
```

The left endpoint and right endpoint are linear functionals of this state:

```text
L_0(y_x)=b_x,

L_k(y_x)=b_(x+k)
        =[N_k(x)b_(x+1)-V_k(x)b_x]/Q_k(x).
```

Their determinant is

```text
det(L_0,L_k) = -N_k(x)/Q_k(x).
```

All factors of `Q_k(x)` are units in both target characteristics.  By Theorem 5.1, `N_k(x)` is also a unit.  Hence

```text
Res_state(L_0,L_k)
```

is a unit modulo `p` and modulo `ell`.

After reflection saturation the determinant is

```text
-(2*x+k+1)C_k(x)/Q_k(x),
```

and both factors in the numerator remain units.

Therefore a state resultant can detect only a same-characteristic double zero.  On a pure-cross pair, the two equations live in different components of

```text
Z/(p*ell)Z = F_p x F_ell:

right endpoint zero in the F_p component,
left endpoint zero in the F_ell component.
```

Each component contains only one linear equation on a two-dimensional state.  Eliminating the two independent state components gives no off-diagonal parameter equation.  Any nonzero integer resultant which is divisible by both primes must insert at least one of

```text
p or ell explicitly,

the opposite quotient residues u,v,

or a new relation special to the distinguished Apéry initial state.
```

The first is the ambient carrier, the second has no known height control, and the third is the missing theorem.

# 8. Bilinear Fourier sum and why the large sieve does not improve

For each prime put

```text
z_p(r)=1_[p|b_r],

hat_z_p(t)
 = (1/p) sum_(r mod p) z_p(r) exp(-2*pi*i*t*r/p).
```

Parseval is exact:

```text
sum_(t mod p) |hat_z_p(t)|^2 = |Z_p|/p.
```

For the direct branch, the marked index is `j=n-a*p`, hence modulo `p`

```text
j = n mod p.
```

For reflected representatives, the phase is replaced by the corresponding affine phase `-n-1 mod p`; this does not change any norm.

Expanding the bilinear count gives sums of the form

```text
sum_(h<=H)
 sum_(p,ell=p+h prime)
 sum_(u mod p, v mod ell)
   hat_z_p(u) hat_z_ell(v)
   exp(2*pi*i*[u*j/p+v*(j-d*h)/ell]),
```

plus the analogous terms enforcing the two pure-cross nonvanishing conditions.

The fractions with denominators `p,ell` have spacing on the order of `n^(-2)`.  The classical large-sieve constant on an interval of length `O(n)` is therefore

```text
n+n^2,
```

with the `n^2` term dominant.

The saturated continuant supplies only a nonzero multiplier in each local field.  Multiplication by a unit permutes the nonzero finite-field frequencies, and multiplication by a complex phase has modulus one.  Either operation preserves the Parseval norm exactly.  The indicator

```text
1_[C_k(x)=0]
```

is identically zero on the pure-cross locus, while

```text
1_[C_k(x) != 0]
```

is identically one there.

Hence the natural continuant-weighted large-sieve ansatz has exactly the same `L^2` input and exactly the same Farey-spacing constant as the unweighted zero-set sum.  It cannot produce a factor `L(n)->infinity` without a new estimate for the *actual* cross-prime Fourier coefficients.

# 9. A recurrence-class CRT obstruction

The preceding unit calculation has a global counterpart which sharply limits any initial-state-uniform theorem.

Scale an arbitrary Apéry-recurrence solution by

```text
W_m=(m!)^3 y_m.
```

Then

```text
W_(m+1)=P(m)W_m-m^6W_(m-1).
```

For `r<p`, the transfer from the initial state `(W_1,W_0)` to the state at time `r` is invertible modulo `p`: its determinant is a product of sixth powers of integers below `p`.

Fix `n` and all primes in the quotient-`a` arc.  For each prime `p`, choose a nonzero local initial vector in the one-dimensional kernel of the target evaluation

```text
W_(n-a*p)=0 mod p.
```

The two initial coordinates may be chosen simultaneously over the integers by the Chinese remainder theorem.

Thus there is one integral initial state, depending on `n`, for which **every** prime in the quotient arc is a target.

## Pure-crossness of nondegenerate adjacent pairs

Take adjacent target primes `p<ell=p+h`.  The cross endpoint in characteristic `p` is another linear functional on the same local initial state.  Its determinant with the target functional is, up to a unit, the gap continuant

```text
N_(a*h)(n-a*ell)
```

for the direct branch, and the corresponding `N_((a+1)h)` for the reflected branch.

If this continuant is a unit modulo the local prime, then every nonzero vector on the target line has nonzero cross endpoint automatically.  Hence every nondegenerate adjacent pair is pure cross in the CRT-constructed solution.

For a fixed gap `h`, a degenerate pair forces one of the large primes to divide

```text
N_(d*h)(n-d*h) N_(d*h)(n),

d in {a,a+1}.
```

The elementary tridiagonal bound gives, with

```text
Lambda_n=log(118)+6 log(n+1),
```

```text
log [N_(d*h)(n-d*h)N_(d*h)(n)]
 <= 2(d*h-1)Lambda_n.
```

Since every prime in the arc is at least `X=n/(a+1)`, an explicit safe bound is

```text
M_(a,d,h)^deg(n)
 <= 4(d*h-1)Lambda_n/log X.
```

The factor four allows for the two orientations and a prime shared by two neighboring gap-`h` pairs.  Therefore

```text
sum_(h<=H) M_(a,d,h)^deg(n)
 <= 2*d*H*(H+1)*Lambda_n/log X
 = O_(a,A)(log(n)^2)
```

when `H=A log n`.

## There are linearly many short adjacent ambient gaps

Let `K_a(n)` be the number of primes in the quotient arc.  By the prime number theorem,

```text
K_a(n)
 = (1+o_a(1)) L_a/log n,

L_a=n/(a*(a+1)).
```

The sum of all adjacent prime gaps is at most `L_a`.  Hence the number of adjacent gaps exceeding `H` is at most `L_a/H`.  For

```text
H=A log n,

A>1,
```

one obtains

```text
# {adjacent gaps <=H}
 >= [(1-1/A)+o_a(1)] L_a/log n.
```

After deleting the `O_(a,A)(log^2 n)` degenerate pairs, the CRT solution still has

```text
Omega_(a,A)(n/log n)
```

adjacent pure-cross pairs with gap at most `A log n`, and every saturated continuant is a unit at both primes.

## Scope of the obstruction

This proves:

```text
No argument valid for every integral initial state of the Apéry recurrence,
and using only the recurrence, reflection, gap continuants/resultants,
local Smith data, and black-box sieve norms, can prove an averaged
saving factor tending to infinity in PC.21.
```

The constructed initial state varies with `n`; it is not the distinguished Apéry state.  Therefore this is not a counterexample to Problem 3.2.  It precisely isolates the extra input which a successful proof must use.

```python
def P(m):

def apery_values(limit):

        assert remainder == 0
```

```text
   1_[p|b_j]
   1_[ell|b_(j-d*h)]
   1_[pure cross]
```

A weaker adjacent version is PC.21.

The continuant/resultant algebra supplies no candidate for `L(n)`: after saturation it is a unit, and the transfer law exposes the opposite quotient residues

```text

```

The ordinary large sieve likewise supplies no `L(n)`, because these residues or the Fourier transforms of `Z_p` are not related across distinct prime fields by any current theorem.

Thus the exact final ledger is:

```text
proved:
  B_(a,d)(n;H) <<_a nH/log^2 n;
  for H=A log n this is O_(a,A)(n/log n);
  degenerate pairs contribute only O_(a,A)(log^2 n);

proved obstruction:
  on every remaining pure-cross pair the saturated gap continuant and
  its state resultant are units in both target characteristics;
  recurrence-class CRT models attain Omega(n/log n) such pairs;

not proved:
  any factor L(n)->infinity for the distinguished initial state (1,5).
```

Therefore the pure-cross sieve route is not closed by another continuant or by a black-box large sieve.  A successful next step must be a fixed-state, cross-characteristic theorem for the actual Apéry quotient residues or zero-set Fourier coefficients.
