# Verdict

The multi-label construction gives a precise matrix identity, but after saturating universal primorial content it does not produce a new low-height carrier. The obstruction is the same distinguished Apéry column (b_n): multiple quotient labels cancel presentation content but leave a primitive state coordinate.

# 1. Exact multi-label congruence matrix

For fixed (n), let labels (ainmathcal A) be the surviving unique low labels after Section 64 pruning. Put

[

and let (A_a) be a Newton carrier in the quotient-(a) prime block. For a principal prime node (p) in that block,

[

Define the label vector

The block primorials divide the corresponding coordinates of (Z).

[

The common (b_n) coordinate disappears algebraically, but the target congruences imply only one scalar condition per prime. The remaining Smith factor is the primitive quotient of the distinguished state column.

# 3. Rank/content theorem

Let (V) be the localized recurrence state lattice at a good prime (p), and let

[

tau_p(v)=b_{n-ap}

]

be the target functional. Every label observation satisfying the target implication lies in

[

mathbb Z_{(p)}tau_p+pV^vee.

]

Hence a matrix of (m) universally target-preserving rows has Smith exponents

[

(0,e_2,ldots,e_m),qquad e_ige1.

]

In particular every (m)-minor carries at least

[

p^{m-1}

]

which is exactly the universal primorial content. Saturation removes those factors and leaves rank one.

This is the multi-label analogue of the local alias theorem: adding labels increases the presentation rank but not the target rank.

# 4. Why a label Casoratian does not escape

A possible escape is a Casoratian in (a):

[

C_{ij}=b_{a_i}A_{a_i}-b_{a_j}A_{a_j}.

]

But modulo any target prime belonging to label (i),

[

b_{a_i}A_{a_i}-b_nequiv0.

]

The second label is not forced to vanish at the same prime. Thus the resulting determinant either:

1. keeps the target scalar (b_n), or

1. introduces cross-label divisibility conditions not implied by the original problem, or

1. acquires the full primorial Smith content.

No target-preserving determinant with bounded label count removes the final scalar.

# Final conclusion

The exact multi-label determinant exists, and its Smith saturation is computable. After saturation:

1. the common (b_n) column can be algebraically removed;

1. universal primorial factors are removed;

1. the remaining determinant measures only the primitive distinguished-state content.

No proved identity currently forces this residual to have restricted radical (o(n)). The weakest missing lemma is:

[

boxed{

}

]

This is strictly stronger than recurrence observability and is the remaining arithmetic bottleneck.
