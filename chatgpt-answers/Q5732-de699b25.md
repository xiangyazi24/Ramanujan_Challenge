# 2. Ranked routes by realistic unconditional chance

## Rank 1 — Apéry-specific block primitive-content / Padé theorem

Assessment: the only route with a non-negligible realistic chance in the current architecture.

The fixed-moment shell has three decisive advantages:

- all target primes in a quotient block act on one characteristic-zero array;

- the first-cell scalar state has bounded order;

- the commit removes overlap between the marked top target and the actual augmented-state content.

What is missing is a theorem about the primitive interval-prime content of one actual evaluated carrier, not a formal recurrence determinant. The exact lemma in Section 4 below reduces the full target product in a block to one gcd with a Pascal-row binomial coefficient. A proof can therefore use the complete Apéry integral/Padé state, creative telescoping, or a global Bézout/subresultant identity without contradicting the local rank-one no-go.

The required new theorem is open.

## Rank 2 — A new gcd theorem for a very special diagonal paired with a factorial ratio

Assessment: possible only as a genuinely new theorem; a generic P-recursive theorem is false or far too broad.

The useful object would not be “two arbitrary P-recursive sequences.” For example, u_n=n! and v_n=(n+1)! are both P-recursive and have \log\gcd(u_n,v_n)\asymp n\log n. Even operator coprimality by itself does not encode the required primitive arithmetic.

A viable theorem would have to use all of the following special structure simultaneously:

- a geometric diagonal/constant-term representation;

- globally bounded denominators;

- the precise Apéry Picard–Fuchs operator;

- a factorial-ratio second coordinate whose relevant primes occur exactly once;

- a geometric or motivic coprimality condition strong enough to control one coefficient for every index.

I found no published theorem with these hypotheses and conclusion. Developing one would be a major result, but the block lemma below gives a precise test case.

## Rank 3 — Subspace/Ru–Vojta after a new low-height projective compression

Assessment: low.

Existing gcd inequalities compare a gcd height to the height of a projective point plus boundary terms. In the natural Apéry embedding, that height is \Theta(M), while the block budget is N=M^{1/3}. A successful application would first need a new projective compression of height O(N) or smaller that still retains all block targets in codimension at least two. Constructing that compression is essentially the missing arithmetic theorem, and Section 63 shows why bounded local observations cannot provide it.

Thus arithmetic geometry may become useful after a global compression identity is found, but it does not presently create that identity.

## Rank 4 — Direct Padé denominator lower bound

Assessment: low.

The Apéry/Beukers integral and G-function theory explain the D_n^3 clearing denominator and the exponential smallness of the linear form. They do not prevent an isolated large common divisor in the cleared numerator and denominator. Standard irrationality-measure arguments rescale both coordinates and reproduce the same target cancellation. The new block gcd formulation is more selective than the global reduced denominator and is the right place to seek a stronger integral-content theorem.

## Rank 5 — Moving-target Subspace theorems for S-units or constant-coefficient recurrences

Assessment: essentially closed for direct application.

The fixed finite-S, finite-dimensional torus, and constant-coefficient recurrence hypotheses fail. Allowing the prime set, dimension, or degree to grow with n destroys the uniformity that supplies the little-oh estimate.

## Rank 6 — Arithmetic dynamics

Assessment: essentially zero for a direct proof.

The Apéry recurrence can be embedded into a nonautonomous or triangular rational state map after adjoining the index, but the resulting orbit has arithmetic degree 1, is not a polarized degree-d\ge2 dynamical system, and the existing gcd theorems are conditional or operate on the wrong exponential scale.

## Rank 7 — Ordinary fixed-degree products, determinants, or resultants

Assessment: closed unless a new distinguished-state identity is added.

Section 63 already excludes bounded target-preserving local placements. Section 5 below gives an exact resultant factorization for the natural adjacent interpolation polynomials: the resultant contains the target primes only through the literal product of the shared shell values, while the residual high difference is not target-forced.

The conclusion is a small gcd on an infinite subset unless the points lie in a moving translate of a proper algebraic subgroup.

Their recurrence applications, Theorems 1.7 and 1.8, concern algebraic constant-coefficient linear recurrences

- forcing a root-of-unity representation into the theorem makes the number of characters or their degrees grow;

- the moving-target theorem’s infinite-subset conclusion is not an all-n theorem.

Therefore the hypotheses fail before any exceptional-subgroup analysis begins.

## 3.5 Yasufuku’s codimension-two blowup inequality

Yasufuku’s 2026 Theorem 1.2 gives an unconditional gcd inequality for homogeneous forms F_1,F_2 defining a codimension-two complete intersection, under a geometric condition that the center avoids the coordinate points. Outside a fixed proper subvariety, the upper bound contains an explicit multiple of the projective height plus the prime-to-S coordinate-boundary term.

For the naive point [1:A_I:B_I] and forms selecting the last two coordinates, the center is a coordinate point, so the geometric hypothesis fails. A linear change of coordinates can move the center, but then the coordinate boundary becomes a product of affine forms such as 1+A_I, 1+B_I, and their transforms. Its prime-to-S height is of the same large order as the original point. On the shell block, h(A_I)=\Theta(M) while the target budget is N=M^{1/3}. The theorem therefore gives at best a linear-in-ambient-height estimate, not o(N).

This route would require a separate low-height embedding first.

## 3.7 G-function denominator theorems

Fischler–Rivoal prove denominator bounds for Taylor coefficients of G-functions annihilated by a G-operator. In the geometric case relevant to the Apéry Picard–Fuchs equation, their result gives constants b,b_0,C and a fixed exponent depending on the differential-operator order such that a controlled lcm power times C^{n+1}a_n is integral.

This is a strong upper clearing-denominator theorem. It does not give a lower bound for the reduced denominator of one coefficient, and it does not exclude a valuation hole of pattern (1,0,1) at one index. For b_n it is vacuous because b_n is already integral; for the companion it recovers the general type of lcm clearing already present in Apéry’s proof.

Bell–Nguyen–Zannier obtain lower height/denominator growth for nonrational D-finite series only on a positive-density set of indices and at logarithmic scale. That is far weaker than an all-index primitive-denominator statement and cannot exclude the sparse exceptional spikes already permitted by Section 42.

# 7. Explicitly open conjectures and non-results

1. The block primitive-content theorem in Section 4 is open. The exact radical identity is proved; the o(N) bound is not.

1. Vojta’s conjecture is open. Silverman’s and Huang’s strongest relevant forms are conditional and still face height/boundary mismatches.

1. No general all-index gcd theorem for Apéry-type P-recursive/diagonal coefficients was found. Claiming one would be unsupported.

1. The generic-radius/Bombieri–Dwork input in general G-function denominator theory is conjectural, although the geometric Apéry operator falls in an unconditional class. Even the unconditional denominator theorem has the wrong direction for the target gcd.

1. Finite computational nuisance collapse is not a theorem. The exact examples where huge Newton carriers have gcd equal to the target product motivate the primitive-content route but do not imply uniformity.

1. No Katz/equidistribution assertion is used. The missing statement is cross-characteristic and pointwise in the external index; standard fixed-field trace estimates do not supply it.
