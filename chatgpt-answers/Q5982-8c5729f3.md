## 1. Exact logarithmic setup

Put

\[
F=(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)\bigl((1+y)(1+z)+xyz\bigr),
\qquad
\Lambda=\frac{F}{xyz},
\]

and let

\[
\theta_x=x\partial_x,\qquad
\theta_y=y\partial_y,\qquad
\theta_z=z\partial_z.
\]

The three logarithmic critical generators are

\[
g_x=\theta_xF-F,\qquad
g_y=\theta_yF-F,\qquad
g_z=\theta_zF-F.
\]

Writing \(C=(1+y)(1+z)\), direct differentiation gives the exact factorizations

\[
\boxed{
 g_x=(1+y)(1+z)\bigl(x^2yz-(1+y)(1+z)\bigr),

\tag{5}
\]

\[
\boxed{
 g_y=(1+x)(1+z)
 \bigl(xy^2z+(y^2-1)(1+z)\bigr),

\tag{6}
\]

\[
\boxed{
 g_z=(1+x)(1+y)
 \bigl(xyz^2+(z^2-1)(1+y)\bigr).
}
\tag{7}
\]

The reason these are the correct generators for the constant-term problem is the exact identity

\[
\theta_i\Lambda=\frac{\theta_iF-F}{xyz}=\frac{g_i}{xyz}
\qquad(i=x,y,z).
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(H_x,H_y,H_z\) be arbitrary Laurent polynomials and let \(M\ge0\).  Since the constant term of a logarithmic derivative is zero,

\[
0=\operatorname{CT}\theta_i\bigl(\Lambda^{M+1}H_i\bigr).
\]

Expanding by the Leibniz rule and using (8) gives

\[
0=(M+1)\operatorname{CT}
 \left[\Lambda^M\frac{g_i}{xyz}H_i\right]
 +\operatorname{CT}\left[\Lambda^{M+1}\theta_iH_i\right].
\]

Summing over \(i=x,y,z\) yields the logarithmic weight identity

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{CT}\left[
 \Lambda^M\sum_i\frac{g_i}{xyz}H_i
\right]
=-\frac1{M+1}\operatorname{CT}\left[
 \Lambda^{M+1}\sum_i\theta_iH_i
\right].
}
\tag{9}
\]

No specialization, floating-point computation, or genericity assumption enters (5)--(9).

## 2. The exact ranking problem

Let

\[
K=\mathbb Q(a,b,c),\qquad
R=K[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1},Z^{\pm1}],
\]

and write the finite transition as

\[
T=\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z^3}T_sX^{s_1}Y^{s_2}Z^{s_3},
\qquad T_s\in M_3(K).
\]

Rows and columns are indexed by the three source states \(x,y,z\).  A linear ranking is specified by

\[
\omega=(\omega_x,\omega_y,\omega_z)\in\mathbb Q^3,
\qquad
r=(r_x,r_y,r_z)\in\mathbb Q^3,
\]

with rank

\[
\operatorname{rk}(i,m)=r_i+\omega\cdot m,
\qquad i\in\{x,y,z\},\quad m\in\mathbb Z^3.
\]

For every nonzero transition coefficient from state \(i\) at \(m\) to state \(j\) at \(m+s\), strict orientation requires

\[
\omega\cdot s+r_j-r_i>0.
\tag{10}
\]

Because the data are rational, a strict solution can be scaled so that every left side is at least \(1\).  Thus the exact primal linear problem is

\[
Aq\ge\mathbf1,
\qquad
q=(\omega_x,\omega_y,\omega_z,r_x,r_y,r_z)^t.
\tag{11}
\]

The exact Farkas alternative says that (11) is infeasible iff there is a vector

\[
\lambda\ge0,\qquad \lambda\ne0,
\]

such that

\[
\lambda^tA=0,\qquad \lambda^t\mathbf1>0.
\tag{12}
\]

Equivalently, \(\lambda\) is a nonnegative circulation with

- zero net flow at each of the three states, and
- zero total lattice displacement.

This is the symbolic, coefficient-independent formulation.  Coefficients matter only in deciding which support rows are present; once a coefficient is a nonzero rational function, its row is present.

## 4. Gauge invariance on the determinant line

Let

\[
I=(g_x,g_y,g_z).
\]

The transition computed by the three gauge scripts is a Laurent representative of the logarithmic connection induced on the conormal/Koszul quotient.  A tangent Koszul gauge changes the chain-level representative by a null-homotopy; it does not change the induced connection.  In particular, it does not change its determinant-line connection.

Now change the three ideal generators by a finite Laurent unimodular matrix

\[
g'=Ug,\qquad U\in\operatorname{GL}_3(R).
\]

Let

\[
\delta=a\theta_x+b\theta_y+c\theta_z
\]

be the logarithmic weight derivation.  If the connection matrix in the old generators is \(T\), then, up to the harmless sign convention for writing a connection as \(\delta+T\) or \(\delta-T\), the new matrix is

\[
T'=(\delta U+UT)U^{-1}.
\tag{16}
\]

Taking traces gives Jacobi's identity

\[
\operatorname{tr}T'
 =\operatorname{tr}T+rac{\delta(\det U)}{\det U}.
\tag{17}
\]

The units of the Laurent ring are exactly

\[
R^\times=K^\times X^pY^qZ^r.
\]

Therefore

\[
\det U=\kappa X^pY^qZ^r
\]

for some \(\kappa\in K^\times\), and

\[
\frac{\delta(\det U)}{\det U}=ap+bq+cr,
\tag{18}
\]

which has shift \(0\).  Consequently, for every nonzero shift \(s\),

\[
\boxed{
\tau_s(T):=[X^{s_1}Y^{s_2}Z^{s_3}]\operatorname{tr}T
}
\tag{19}
\]

is invariant under every finite Laurent generator change.  It is also invariant under tangent Koszul homotopy because the latter changes only the lift, not the induced determinant-line connection.

The exact support ledger at the two extreme shifts gives

\[
T_{-3e_z}=-\frac L4 E_{yy},
\qquad
T_{+3e_z}=-E_{yy},
\tag{20}
\]

so

\[
\tau_{-3e_z}=-\frac L4,
\qquad
\tau_{+3e_z}=-1.
\tag{21}
\]

These are precisely (3).

A useful single scalar package is

\[
\mathcal W(T)
 =4\tau_{-3e_z}(T)\tau_{+3e_z}(T)
 =L.
\tag{22}
\]

This is the promised gauge-invariant cycle functional.  It is primitive in
\(\mathbb Z[a,b,c]\), and it is not the zero polynomial.

There is also a literal two-step Wilson-loop interpretation.  Starting at the \(y\)-state at lattice point \(m=(a,b,c)\), take the \(-3e_z\) edge and then the \(+3e_z\) edge.  The ordered product is

\[
(-1)\left(-\frac L4\right)=\frac L4.
\tag{23}
\]

Under a local basis change the two edge matrices acquire inverse factors at the intermediate vertex, which telescope; the closed product is conjugated at the starting vertex, so its trace is unchanged.

## 5. Why the invariant forbids every oriented representative

Suppose some finite gauge and generator change produced a representative \(T^{\rm new}\) satisfying a strict ranking.  By (19)--(21), its trace still has nonzero coefficients at both shifts \(-3e_z\) and \(+3e_z\).

A nonzero trace coefficient at a shift means that at least one diagonal transition at that shift is present.  For a diagonal transition the state offset cancels, so strict orientation requires respectively

\[
-3\omega_z>0,
\qquad
3\omega_z>0.
\]

This is impossible.  Equivalently, the nonzero invariant (22) says that a zero-displacement closed transition cycle survives.  Along a strictly ranked cycle every edge raises rank, while return to the same state and lattice point forces total rank change \(0\).  Contradiction.

Thus neither of the following can occur:

- a tangent Koszul gauge that removes the obstruction;
- a Laurent generator change that hides it in off-diagonal entries and leaves an orientable support.

A generator change may redistribute individual matrix entries, but it cannot change the two nonzero-shift trace coefficients.  Hence it must leave some diagonal edge at each of the two opposite shifts.

## 6. The exceptional hyperplane does not rescue a finite symbolic gauge

At a single specialization satisfying

\[
3a+8b-3c+17=0,
\]

the \(-3e_z\) coefficient in (1) vanishes at that one lattice point.  This does not produce a symbolic Laurent gauge:

1. \(L\) is not the zero polynomial, so the edge belongs to the support over \(K=\mathbb Q(a,b,c)\).
2. Cancelling it by a formula that divides by \(L\) is not a finite Laurent polynomial gauge and is singular on the hyperplane \(L=0\).
3. The hyperplane is not stable under the transition shifts:
   \[
   L(a,b,c+3)=L(a,b,c)-9,
   \qquad
   L(a,b,c-3)=L(a,b,c)+9.
   \]
   Thus it does not define a closed transition subsystem.

The requested uniform finite Laurent orientation therefore remains impossible.
