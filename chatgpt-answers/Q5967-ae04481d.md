# 6. Eisenstein constants are both inapplicable and one-sided

Guillaume Rond's survey About Eisenstein's Theorem, arXiv:2412.11738, states the classical result as Theorem 1.1:

If f(x)\in\mathbb Q[[x]] is algebraic, there exists a\ge1 such that af(ax)\in\mathbb Z[[x]].

Equivalently, if f(x)=\sum f_\ell x^\ell, then

This is an upper denominator statement for algebraic power series. The Apéry companion generating function is a transcendental G-function arising from an inhomogeneous Picard–Fuchs equation, so Theorem 1.1 does not apply.

Even in its domain, an Eisenstein constant says that denominators use a fixed finite set of primes with bounded linear growth. It gives no lower bound asserting that those allowed primes or exponents occur. Thus “computing the Eisenstein constant” would not answer the present pointwise question even if the series were algebraic.

For the Apéry K3 operator the rational companion satisfies L(A)=5; it is not simply the homogeneous logarithmic Frobenius coefficient G appearing in the mirror-map expression. Homogenizing it by differentiation recovers the order-four geometric G-operator and hence the upper bound d_n^3a_n\in\mathbb Z, but the mirror-map integrality theorem does not provide a coefficientwise lower bound for C_n.

The more recent motivic theory of Apéry extensions—V. Golyshev, M. Kerr and T. Sasaki, Apéry extensions, J. Lond. Math. It does not state a lower bound of the form \log C_n\ge3n-o(n) for individual Taylor coefficients.

This is a horizontal, moving-characteristic noncancellation theorem for the distinguished integral solution b_n. The following standard inputs do not address it:

1. Generic p-adic radius: bounds how bad denominators can be; it does not force a coordinate to realize the bound.

1. Nilpotent residue / regular singularity: gives a logarithmic normal form and lcm powers; it does not prevent cancellation in the selected initial-state line.

1. Galochkin size: is an operator/module invariant; the event p\mid b_n depends on one solution and one moving coefficient.

1. Eisenstein constants: are upper scaling constants for algebraic series.

1. Mirror-map integrality: proves integrality of \exp(G/F), a nonlinear combination, not denominator maximality of G or A.

1. Sharp lcm exponent: is a prefix or filtration statement. The \operatorname{Li}_3 example shows it is compatible with very small individual denominators.

1. Common-denominator asymptotics: even an exact formula for \operatorname{lcm}_{m\le n}C_m permits the required prime factors to be distributed sparsely among different indices.

There is also a solution-selection obstruction. Operator theorems remain true after replacing a rational companion by another rational solution or by adding a rational multiple of an integral solution. Such a change can alter reduced coefficient denominators by cancellation while leaving the differential operator, local exponents, generic radii, and Galochkin size unchanged. Any theorem strong enough for P3.2 must therefore use the distinguished Apéry initial condition, not only the operator class.
