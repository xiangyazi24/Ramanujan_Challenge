# Adversarial literature audit: fixed-prime tame twists versus T-adic slope theory

Decisive applicability verdict: negative. The cited Liu–Wan, Davis–Wan–Xiao, Liu–Wan–Xiao, Ren–Wan–Xiao–Yu, and Ren results do not put the full set of tame Teichmüller twists ω^(−r), r mod p−1, into one connected T-adic or rigid-analytic family. Their analytic variable is either an additive character of p-power conductor, a wild p-power character, or a modular weight varying inside one fixed tame component. The tame exponent is fixed whenever it appears. None of the checked theorems gives an O(1), O(log p), or o(p) bound for the number of nonordinary tame twists of one fixed sheaf at a fixed prime.

The proposal nevertheless points toward a legitimate object: one may form the separate rigid cohomologies of all Kummer twists and package them over the finite étale tame-character scheme. That package is a product of p−1 blocks, not a one-dimensional analytic family. A Hasse scalar on it is an arbitrary tuple of p−1 values unless a genuinely new cross-component complexity theorem is proved. Thus Newton-versus-Hodge rigidity within a wild family does not count bad tame components.

For the Ramanujan_Challenge state, this audit accepts the exact finite-field Mellin identities and the bounded-conductor object recorded in problems/3.2/CODEX_SPEC_franel_mellin.md and problems/3.2/CODEX_FRANEL_MELLIN.md. It also respects the latter ledger's warning that the two displayed terms geometrically collapse to the residual Apéry constituent and that complex Katz equidistribution does not control reduction modulo the defining prime. Nothing below assumes that the remaining integral/crystalline comparison gaps in that ledger have already been closed.

- DOI: 10.2140/ant.2009.3.489

- Theorem 2.2: the specialized π_ψ-Newton polygon lies above the T-adic Newton polygon.

Substituting “all Teichmüller characters ω^(−r)” for “all nontrivial additive ψ” changes the parameter space and is invalid.

# 2. The tame Teichmüller twists do not form one p-adic analytic family

For odd p, write

```plain text
Z_p^× = Δ × Γ,
Δ = μ_(p−1),
Γ = 1+pZ_p ≅ Z_p.
```

The Iwasawa algebra decomposes as

```plain text
Z_p[[Z_p^×]]
  ≅ Z_p[Δ] completed_tensor Z_p[[Γ]].
```

Because |Δ|=p−1 is a p-adic unit and Q_p already contains the Teichmüller (p−1)st roots of unity, the orthogonal idempotents of the finite group algebra split it:

```plain text
Z_p[Δ] ≅ product_(η ∈ Δ^∨) Z_p.
```

Consequently rigid weight space is a disjoint union

```plain text
W = disjoint_union_(η ∈ Δ^∨) W_η,
```

where every W_η is an open unit disk parameterizing the wild character on Γ, with the tame restriction η fixed.

The characters in the question are

```plain text
ω^(−r) = η_r × 1_Γ.
```

They are the central/trivial-wild point on p−1 different disks. They are not p−1 points in a single disk. An analytic map from a connected affinoid into the finite étale set Δ^∨ is locally constant, so no change of coordinates can turn all η_r into one connected T-adic family.

This is not merely a semantic issue. Weierstrass preparation and boundary-halo degree arguments work on one connected disk or annulus. They can control zeros as the wild coordinate varies on W_η. They do not sum or compare the zero counts at one selected point on every different W_η.

## 2.1 The finite-field group has no wild multiplicative direction

At the original finite-field level,

```plain text
|F_p^×| = p−1.
```

Therefore every multiplicative character of F_p^× has prime-to-p order. There are no nontrivial p-power-order multiplicative characters of F_p^×.

A phrase such as “fix the tame component and vary wild p-power characters” refers to characters of Z_p^×, local units of conductor p^m, or another pro-p group. It does not describe the original set widehat(F_p^×). Relating such a local wild family back to the finite-field Mellin cohomology would itself require a new construction; the Artin–Schreier–Witt T-adic formalism varies an additive Galois character, not a Kummer character of F_p^×.

## 2.2 The disconnected packaging exists but is tautological

All tame characters can be packaged over the finite étale scheme

```plain text
X_p = Spec Z_p[U]/(U^(p−1)−1)
    ≅ disjoint_union_(r mod p−1) Spec Z_p.
```

This is a legitimate “family” only in the disconnected sense. Its ring of functions is

```plain text
Γ(X_p,O_Xp) ≅ product_(r mod p−1) Z_p.
```

Thus every tuple (a_r)_r is a regular section. There is no intrinsic degree or analytic zero-count bound. Choosing a primitive element and writing the unique representative

```plain text
A_p(U) = sum_(j=0)^(p−2) c_j U^j
        mod U^(p−1)−1
```

only gives the tautological degree bound < p−1. A bounded-degree assertion would be new arithmetic content, not a consequence of the existence of X_p.

# 3. Is there a theorem counting nonordinary tame twists?

## 3.1 Answer for the checked literature

No. None of the named papers, nor the nearby twisted-polynomial and 2026 generic-ordinarity papers checked above, proves a bound of the form

```plain text
#{r mod p−1 : the ω^(−r)-twist is nonordinary}
  = O(1), O(log p), or o(p)
```

for one fixed sheaf as r varies at a fixed prime.

This statement is deliberately scoped: no such theorem was found in the checked primary literature, and none of the named theorems has the correct varying parameter. I am not claiming a machine-verifiable theorem that no specialized result exists anywhere in the literature. Any purported countercitation must be checked for the same component issue: does it really vary the tame character, or does it fix the tame character and vary coefficients, conductor, extension degree, or a wild character?

## 3.2 Why “the nonordinary locus is a finite divisor” does not give the desired bound

In a fixed geometric family over a curve in characteristic p, a Hasse invariant is indeed a section of a line bundle and its zero locus is a finite divisor when the section is nonzero. But the line bundle usually contains a Frobenius factor of size p−1; its degree need not be bounded as p grows.

The classical Legendre Hasse polynomial already has degree (p−1)/2:

```plain text
H_p(λ) = sum_(j=0)^((p−1)/2)
         binom((p−1)/2,j)^2 λ^j.
```

Thus “finite divisor” means finite for each fixed p, not degree O(1) uniformly in p.

The present situation is still less favorable: the parameter base is not one fixed curve. It is the p-dependent split scheme X_p with p−1 components. Every subset of its geometric points is the zero set of some regular function. Algebraicity alone supplies no saving.

## 3.3 Katz equidistribution is also not the missing theorem

Katz's multiplicative Mellin-transform theory can produce bounded-rank cohomology, monodromy groups, and equidistribution of normalized complex/ℓ-adic Frobenius conjugacy classes as characters vary. Exact divisibility by the same prime that defines the character group is a self-characteristic local-limit event. It is not a continuous class function of the normalized complex conjugacy class.

Therefore even perfect knowledge of geometric monodromy does not imply

```plain text
Pr_r(trace ≡ 0 mod p) ≈ 1/p
```

without an additional residual anti-concentration theorem. This is the same obstruction already marked [NEGATIVE-KATZ-p] and [GAP-3] in problems/3.2/CODEX_FRANEL_MELLIN.md.

# 4. The closest valid computable object

Let E_p denote the actual fixed residual trace object after the geometric cancellation recorded in the project ledger; before that cancellation one may make the same construction for the two constituents separately. For each tame character η = ω^(−r), the natural cohomological object is

```plain text
C_(p,η)
  = H^1_(rig,c)(G_m/F_p, E_p tensor K_η),
```

or the corresponding overconvergent/rigid cohomology degree dictated by the precise normalization. Its Frobenius characteristic polynomial is

```plain text
P_(p,η)(S) = det(1 − S Frob_p | C_(p,η)).
```

One can package all twists as the block-diagonal direct sum

```plain text
C_p^tame = direct_sum_(η ∈ widehat(F_p^×)) C_(p,η).
```

Equivalently, one may work over the group algebra or the finite étale scheme X_p. After applying the tame idempotents, this universal object is exactly the collection of p−1 separate blocks. It does not create analytic relations among them.

For the scalar in the question, choose the Teichmüller prime 𝔭 above p and put

```plain text
D_p(r) = M_tilde(r) − T_tilde(r),
h_p(r) = D_p(r) mod 𝔭.
```

Then

```plain text
h_p ∈ Γ(X_p,O_Xp) ≅ product_r F_p
```

is the closest unconditional “section over character space,” once the integral trace comparison has been fixed. Its zero set is exactly the desired set. This formulation is correct but tautological.

## 4.1 What a genuine Hasse object would be

If every C_(p,η) admits a compatible integral filtered F-crystal, the Hasse invariant is not automatically its full Frobenius trace. It is typically a determinant or minor of Frobenius/Cartier on a specified Hodge-graded piece, for example a map between one-dimensional lowest-Hodge lines. One obtains a tuple

```plain text
Ha_p = (Ha_(p,η))_η ∈ product_η F_p.
```

Again, this is a section on X_p, but no cross-η degree bound follows.

## 4.2 The exact new theorem that would be needed

A theorem strong enough for the project would have to be genuinely cross-tame-component. A clean target formulation is:

Desired tame-twist nonordinarity theorem. Let E be a fixed compatible system on G_m, of bounded conductor, with specified purity, integral structure, and no nontrivial Kummer self-twist. Suppose the Kummer-twist cohomologies have uniformly controlled Hodge data. Then for some δ>0, uniformly at good primes,

`text

#{η ∈ widehat(F_p^×) : Ha_(p,η)=0}

<<_E p^(1−δ).

`

For the actual scalar rather than a separately defined Hasse minor, one needs the stronger residual statement

```plain text
#{η : D_p(η) ≡ 0 mod 𝔭} <<_E p^(1−δ).
```

No theorem cited above supplies this.

Equivalent kinds of genuinely new input that would suffice include:

- a proof that the Hasse tuple is represented in the tame group algebra by a nonzero element of support or cyclic degree o(p);

- a mod-p local-limit/anti-concentration theorem for Frobenius traces in the tame-character aspect;

- a cross-component Newton–Hodge theorem whose exceptional tame idempotents are controlled uniformly;

- a special algebraic identity for this Apéry/Franel system that constrains the zero set, not merely the complex size of the traces.

The following gaps should remain explicit:

- [GAP-FCRYSTAL] Construct the integral overconvergent F-crystal/rigid cohomology for the precise Mellin object, uniformly in the tame twist, including middle-extension points and the chosen prime above p.

- [GAP-HASSE=TARGET] Prove that D_p(r) mod 𝔭 is the relevant Hasse determinant/minor, up to a nonzero unit. A congruence with the full trace is not enough by itself.

- [GAP-TAME-COUNT] Prove a uniform cross-component zero-count theorem. This is the step that would yield o(p).

Even if the first two gaps are solved, the third does not follow formally.

## 4.3 Fixed tame component plus wild characters: the valid use of T-adic theory

Fix one tame character η. If a suitable local p-adic family is constructed, one can study

```plain text
η κ,     κ ∈ Hom_cont(1+pZ_p,C_p^×),
```

on the single disk W_η. T-adic/halo methods may then control Newton polygons as the p-power conductor of κ grows or as κ approaches the boundary.

That is a valid research direction, but it answers a different question. The original values ω^(−r) are the points

```plain text
(η_r, κ=1),
```

one on each component. A theorem uniform in κ for fixed η does not compare these centers as η varies. Ren's twisted Artin–Schreier–Witt theorem is the exact model: u is fixed and χ varies.

# 5. Audit of “trace zero mod p equals nonordinarity”

The phrase is correct only under a narrow rank/slope normalization. It is false as a general slogan.

## 5.1 The rank-two situation where it is correct

Let an integral rank-two Frobenius module have characteristic polynomial

```plain text
P(X) = X^2 − aX + d,
```

with Frobenius eigenvalues α,β, nonnegative slopes, and

```plain text
v_p(d)=h>0.
```

Assume its Hodge polygon is (0,h), so ordinarity means Newton slopes exactly (0,h)—equivalently, exactly one unit root.

Then

```plain text
p does not divide a
  ⇔ one eigenvalue is a unit
  ⇔ slopes are (0,h)
  ⇔ the module is ordinary,
```

and hence

```plain text
p divides a
  ⇔ there is no slope-zero eigenvalue
  ⇔ the rank-two module is nonordinary.
```

The proof is elementary. If α is a unit and β has positive valuation, then a=α+β is a unit. Conversely, if a is a unit while d has positive valuation, the Newton polygon of X^2−aX+d has one slope zero and one slope h.

For elliptic-curve H^1, d=p and this specializes to the familiar criterion p | a_p for nonordinary/supersingular reduction.

Even here, “nonordinary” need not identify a unique nonordinary Newton polygon without additional polarization/determinant constraints. The safe conclusion is “no unit root,” not automatically a specific supersingular slope pattern.

## 5.2 Failure modes for a general rank-two trace

1. Determinant a unit. If both slopes are zero, unit roots can cancel. The matrix diag(1,−1) is entirely unit-root/ordinary but has trace zero modulo every odd p.

1. The object has not been Tate-normalized. If the minimal Hodge slope is positive, even the ordinary polygon can make every Frobenius eigenvalue divisible by p, so the trace is automatically zero modulo p.

1. More than one possible slope-zero eigenvalue. Cancellation among unit roots destroys the trace criterion. Rank two with Hodge slopes (0,0) is the simplest example.

1. The scalar is not the Frobenius coefficient controlling the lowest Hodge line. A Hasse invariant is a Cartier/Frobenius minor. The full trace agrees with it modulo p only when all other diagonal/eigenvalue contributions have positive valuation and the lowest piece is one-dimensional.

Thus a statement about a “general rank-two cohomology trace” must specify the integral lattice, determinant valuation, Hodge polygon, normalization, and the multiplicity of slope zero.

## 5.3 Failure modes in higher rank and for a difference of traces

In rank at least three, trace zero does not imply absence of unit roots. For example, an ordinary slope pattern (0,0,1) may have unit eigenvalues 1 and −1, whose contributions cancel modulo p.

More importantly for the present setup,

```plain text
M_tilde(r) − T_tilde(r)
```

is initially a difference of two traces. A virtual difference in a Grothendieck group does not have a Newton polygon or an ordinarity notion. It may vanish modulo p because two ordinary traces agree, with no degeneration of either unit-root subspace.

The project ledger finds a geometric cancellation leaving the Apéry constituent, which is useful, but that constituent is recorded as rank three before taking Mellin cohomology. The rank and Hodge slopes of the relevant Mellin cohomology must be computed; a rank-two criterion cannot be imported from the original geometric rank or from numerical trace size.

Therefore the implication

```plain text
D_p(r) ≡ 0 mod 𝔭  ⇒ nonordinary
```

is itself [GAP-HASSE=TARGET] until one proves that, after the correct normalization, D_p(r) is the unique lowest-slope Hasse coefficient of an actual integral rank-two Frobenius module.

# 6. Project-specific conclusion

The rigid/T-adic proposal does not currently improve the analytic bound on the bad r.

What is bankable is the following hierarchy:

1. The fixed-sheaf Mellin formulation gives a bounded-dimensional cohomology object for each individual tame character.

1. The full tame family can be assembled over a finite étale scheme with p−1 components.

1. If a compatible integral F-crystal is built, each component has a computable Newton polygon and possibly a Hasse minor.

1. Existing T-adic rigidity can potentially control wild characters within one fixed tame component.

1. No checked theorem controls how often the central point is nonordinary as the tame component varies.

Accordingly, the proposed inference

```plain text
T-adic rigidity
  ⇒ bounded-degree Hasse section in r
  ⇒ o(p) nonordinary Teichmüller twists
```

breaks at both arrows. The first arrow confuses a connected wild variable with a finite étale tame label. The second assumes a cross-component degree that the geometry does not provide.

Recommended status label for the project: [NEGATIVE-TADIC-TAME-ASPECT]. The named T-adic/eigencurve theorems are inapplicable to the all-Teichmüller-twist count. Retain [GAP-FCRYSTAL], [GAP-HASSE=TARGET], and [GAP-TAME-COUNT] as separate tasks. Only the last task can deliver the required o(p) zero count.
