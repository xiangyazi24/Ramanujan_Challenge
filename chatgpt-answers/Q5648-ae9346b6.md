# Verdict

No. The modularity identity does not imply that a pointwise bound z_p=O(1) must decide exactly which primes satisfy p\mid\gamma_p. It only shows that the central reflection-fixed index may contribute one optional zero. A proof of z_p\le C may simply allow that one exceptional orbit and bound all noncentral reflection orbits uniformly.

The phenomenon rules out only arguments that assert the central value is always nonzero, or that every zero must occur in a two-element reflected pair. It does not rule out bounded zero-fiber bounds, recurrence/continuant arguments away from the center, average L^1 bounds, or cross-prime arguments that isolate the central contribution separately.

# 3. What the nonordinary event actually rules out

The identity rules out the following universal claims unless one separately excludes or accommodates the central index:

1. Universal central nonvanishing: b_{(p-1)/2}\not\equiv0\pmod p for every p.

1. All zeros occur in two-point reflection orbits: a central zero is a singleton orbit.

1. Every target is detected by a nontrivial reflected gap: at the center, the reflected gap is zero.

1. A factor-removal argument that divides out 2r+1-p and then assumes the residual is always a unit at the fold. The modular form supplies genuine primes where the fold value vanishes.

1. Any exact formula asserting z_p is even for every odd prime.

It does not rule out:

- z_p=O(1) with one optional central zero;

- z_p\ll p^\theta for any \theta;

- the current continuant/Casoratian bound for noncentral zeros;

The form \eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4 is the level-8, weight-4 newform attached to the rigid Calabi–Yau threefold studied by Ahlgren–Ono. It is not a CM form. Thus the elementary CM classification—where a_p=0 is determined by inertness in an imaginary quadratic field—does not apply.

For a non-CM newform, general Galois-representation and Sato–Tate results concern the distribution of the normalized real numbers

There are broad results and conjectures on nonordinary primes of modular forms. For non-CM forms of weight at least $2, infinitely many ordinary primes are known in several settings, and density-one ordinarity is expected; some density results are known under additional hypotheses or for fixed residue characteristics. None gives an exact classification of the primes $pmidgamma_p$ for this form, and none is needed for either $z_p=O(1)`$ or the first-moment estimate.

The strongest legitimately relevant conclusion here is therefore:

The central Apéry zero indicator equals the nonordinary-prime indicator for the weight-4, level-8 newform. Its total contribution is at most one per prime and at most one per fixed top-half column. No classification or density theorem for these primes is necessary to prove a bounded zero fiber or the desired average first moment.
