## 8. The precise compatible-family theorem that would be required

The needed input is not merely “big monodromy.” It is a horizontal exact-local-limit large sieve for the marked Apéry compatible family.

A theorem of the required type would prove, for every fixed q, one of the following equivalent-strength statements:

1. the pair-energy estimate (FQ2);

1. the shifted incidence estimate in Section 5;

1. a Barban–Davenport–Halberstam type second-moment bound for the zero indicators \mathbf 1_{A_{n-qp}\equiv0\pmod p};

1. an FKM-style dispersion or bilinear trace-function estimate across varying prime moduli that gives a little-o saving over the N^2/\log^2N prime-pair barrier.

In compatible-system language, the theorem would have to be uniform for the moving specialization

or on the Mellin side for the moving Kummer character determined by that residue. It must compare distinct characteristics p and \ell and prove that the exact-zero loci do not align along

A Kowalski-style large sieve for Frobenius is the closest general paradigm, but the standard form is not automatically enough here. Standard compatible-family large sieves usually test Frobenius classes modulo fixed auxiliary primes or fixed finite quotients. Here the congruence modulus is the same prime as the geometric fiber, and the selected character/index varies with that prime. What is required is therefore a moving-characteristic, local-limit strengthening specialized to the Apéry Mellin/recurrence family.

Likewise, the ordinary analytic large sieve by itself encounters the critical N^2 term for moduli p\asymp N and does not create the marked cross-characteristic cancellation. The theorem must use the arithmetic compatibility of the actual Apéry family, not merely spacing of rational frequencies.

A satisfactory theorem statement can therefore be taken to be exactly (FQ2). Proving it from a compatible family would constitute the missing horizontal input. Proving only |S(p)|\le C, even by the strongest possible one-prime method, would not.

## 9. Final hostile verdict

The proposed route is logically insufficient.

- A uniform local bound |S(p)|\le C does not imply O(1) contributing primes for a fixed n.

- It does not even imply a pointwise o(n/\log n) count.
