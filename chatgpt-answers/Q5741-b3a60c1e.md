# Q5741: unconditional ghost-sector audit

## Theorem status

Let

\[
R_k(M;I)=\prod_{\substack{q\in I\ {m prime}\\q\mid C_M(kq-1)}}q
\]

be the nonprincipal Cartier ghost contribution in a block. The strongest unconditional statement obtained from Sections 61, 63, 64 together with the Q5735 selector audit is:

\[
\boxed{\log R_{\ge 2}(M;I)=o(M)}
\]

for the parts generated by repeated folded nodes, large labels, and Cartier channels whose lower digit is not the principal low-label residual.

The remaining sector is exactly the quotient-one fixed-point family

\[
q\mid b_s,\qquad M=q+s,
\]

and its quotient-two analogue

\[
q\mid b_s,\qquad M=2q+s.
\]

These are not new ghost phenomena: after Cartier reduction they are isomorphic to the original principal Apéry zero-fiber problem at the child index.

---

# 1. Cartier parameterization of ghosts

Write

\[
M=aq+s,\qquad 0\le s<q.
\]

For a ghost node

\[
d=kq-1,
\]

Cartier factorization gives

\[
C_M(kq-1)\equiv 0\pmod q
\]
whenever the nonconstant coefficient shells vanish. The exact digit decomposition gives the sufficient condition

\[
q\mid b_a b_s.
\]

Hence every ghost prime belongs to one of two channels:

1. outer-digit channel: \(q\mid b_a\);
2. residue channel: \(q\mid b_s\).

The block problem is therefore a counting problem for affine relations between q and the small Apéry labels.

---

# 2. Outer-digit channel

Fix a label \(a\). Then

\[
q\mid b_a,
\]

and the ghost prime must satisfy

\[
M=aq+s.
\]

For fixed \(a\), every such q divides one of finitely many affine values determined by the allowed residue range.

By Apéry-Lucas,

\[
b_M\equiv b_a b_s\pmod q.
\]

The Section 63 compression gives

\[
\prod_{q\in S_a}q\mid \operatorname{rad}(b_a).
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{q\in S_a}\log q\le \log b_a\le a\log40.
\]

Summing over low labels

\[
 a\le \log^2 M
\]

produces

\[
\sum_a a\log40=O(\log^6 M)=o(M).
\]

Large labels are already removed by the Section 64 estimate:

\[
\log R_{a>\log^2M}\ll M\frac{\log\log M}{\log M}=o(M).
\]

---

# 3. Residue channel and affine two-form compression

Now assume

\[
q\mid b_s.
\]

The ghost condition gives

\[
M=aq+s.
\]

For fixed residue s this is a quotient-one affine family. Every prime contributes to

\[
\operatorname{rad}\gcd(M-s,b_s).
\]

Thus

\[
\prod_{q:s_q=s}q\mid \operatorname{rad}\gcd(M-s,b_s).
\]

The remaining nonprincipal ghosts are quotient-two folded nodes. Put

\[
M=2q+s.
\]

Then

\[
q=(M-s)/2.
\]

The Section 64 affine sieve applies to the two affine forms

\[
P(t)=P_0-2vt,
\qquad
Q(t)=Q_0+2ut.
\]

For compatible labels \((a,B)\), the number of candidates is

\[
N_{a,B}\le
\frac{M\gcd(a,B)}{2aB\max(a,B)}+1.
\]

The dimension-two Selberg bound gives

\[
\#\{\text{nonprincipal repeated ghosts}\}
\ll
\frac{M\log\log M}{\log^2M}.
\]

Since every prime in this sector is at most M,

\[
\log R_{\rm repeat,ghost}
\ll
\frac{M\log\log M}{\log M}=o(M).
\]

---

# 4. Exact surviving family

The above argument leaves only

\[
q=M-s,
\qquad q\mid b_s,
\]

and

\[
q=(M-s)/2,
\qquad q\mid b_s.
\]

These are terminal Cartier fixed points.

For quotient one:

\[
(M,q,s)\mapsto(q+s,q,s),
\]

which is the same target row.

For quotient two:

\[
(M,q,s)\mapsto(M-q,q,s),
\]

and the child index is smaller but the same prime residue obstruction remains.

No new ghost cancellation occurs.

---

# 5. What remains open

The ghost sector is therefore not the remaining P3.2 obstruction.

The proven decomposition is:

\[
\log R_{\rm ghost}
=o(M)+
\log R_{\rm principal\ low\ label}.
\]

The missing theorem is exactly a horizontal bound for the unique low-label principal residual:

\[
\sum_{q>\sqrt M,\ q\mid b_{M\bmod q}}\log q=o(M).
\]

Cartier, binomial valuations, and affine multiplicity compression remove all nonprincipal copies but do not bound this last one-dimensional Apéry zero fiber.
