# q59 LASTSTAND: fixed-height Sp census and transport audit

## (i) Fixed h <= 32 and fixed p

The certified Sp_{4h-2} geometric monodromy is a statement about the Galois group of the splitting field of N_h over Q. It does not imply a pointwise estimate for one particular prime p.

For a single fixed p, the unconditional information remains:

- N_h is squarefree over Q, so the mod-p root set has no multiplicities except finitely many bad primes coming from the discriminant.
- C_h = #Z_h satisfies the degree budget C_h <= 3(h-1).
- For good primes, Frobenius conjugacy classes in Sp_{4h-2} determine the factorization type of N_h mod p.

Thus Sp monodromy gives Chebotarev statistics in the p-aspect, not a new upper bound for C_h at an adversarial fixed p.

The calibration is the random Frobenius model. If the geometric Galois group is full Sp_{4h-2}, then the average root count is the average number of fixed points of a random Frobenius element acting on the roots. Equivalently, if Omega_h is the root permutation representation, then

E_p(C_h) = average_{g in Sp_{4h-2}} #Fix(Omega_h(g)).

The variance is likewise the second permutation character moment:

Var_p(C_h) = <Omega_h,Omega_h>_Sp - <Omega_h,1>^2.

These are constants depending only on h. They explain the random-floor behavior, but they do not produce decay as h grows. The Strike2 dismissal is therefore correct at fixed height: h <= 32 controls calibration, not mesoscopic growth.

## (ii) Joint small-h census Z_2,...,Z_32

The pairwise coprime critical resultants and Goursat analysis improve the joint p-average picture. They show that the small-height families do not share hidden geometric components.

At fixed p, however, the available deterministic statement is essentially still the intersection injection. If r lies in Z_h intersect Z_k, then xi_r = xi_{r+h} = xi_{r+k}, hence xi_{r+h} = xi_{r+k}. Therefore

Z_h intersect Z_k injects into Z_{|h-k|}

(up to the boundary convention), giving

|Z_h intersect Z_k| <= C_{|h-k|} <= 3|h-k|.

The resultant information does not upgrade this to o(1) or square-root cancellation at a chosen p. It says that an exceptional simultaneous split requires special Frobenius behavior in the combined splitting field, but a fixed p may still realize such behavior.

Hence the nontrivial statement obtained is:

For p outside a finite exceptional set depending on h,k, the joint factorization distribution of (N_h,N_k) is governed by the product-compatible Galois subgroup predicted by the Goursat computation. No unconditional fixed-p improvement over the triangle injection follows.

## (iii) Transport through the addition law

The exact addition law gives a renewal identity, but not a probabilistic renewal process.

Writing the identity at x:

N_{h+d}(x) = N_d(x+h) N_{h+1}(x) - (x+h+1)^6 N_{d-1}(x+h+1) N_h(x).

Therefore

x in Z_{h+d}

if and only if

N_d(x+h) N_{h+1}(x) = (x+h+1)^6 N_{d-1}(x+h+1) N_h(x) mod p.

For h <= 31, the small-height sets enter only through the factors N_h and N_{h+1}. The obstruction is the second term: it couples the new zero event to a correlated ratio

N_d(x+h) / N_{d-1}(x+h+1),

not to independent small-height data.

The recursion therefore does not produce a Markov chain on the indicator sets Z_d. It produces a projective cocycle. The small-height monodromy controls the coefficients of the recurrence, but the state variable needed for transport is the full two-dimensional transfer state, not just the zero/nonzero indicator.

A dyadic average such as

sum_{D/2 < d <= D} C_d

does not improve from the recursion alone. The degree budget gives

sum C_d << D^2.

To get o(D^2), one needs cancellation or anti-concentration for the cocycle state. The certified h <= 32 data does not supply that because the bad configurations are not created by small-height coincidences; they are created by long-range alignment of the transfer product.

This also explains why the padded-word obstruction survives: it models precisely an adversarial alignment of the long transfer states while keeping every bounded-height statistic legal.

## (iv) Verdict

No transport theorem is currently forced by the certified h <= 32 Sp census.

The precise failure mechanism is:

1. Sp monodromy controls Frobenius statistics of finitely many fixed-gap polynomials.
2. The addition law is bilinear, not additive. It mixes two large-gap states with small-gap coefficients.
3. The term (x+h+1)^6 N_{d-1}(x+h+1) N_h(x) preserves long-range correlation instead of randomizing it.
4. Therefore small-height independence does not descend to growing-gap anti-concentration.

A viable new theorem would need a growing-gap statement, for example a quenched bound on the transfer cocycle ratio or a family-averaged cancellation theorem for N_d over d in a dyadic interval. The fixed-height Sp census is a calibration theorem, not the missing mesoscopic theorem.

Least confident step: the exact character-theoretic numerical averages and variances for C_h. The qualitative Chebotarev conclusion is solid, but I have not computed the Sp permutation character decomposition explicitly here.

Blind spot in the question: it may overestimate the leverage of the polynomial recursion. The recursion couples N_d values, but the natural state space is the full companion-matrix orbit; reducing that state to the zero sets Z_d loses precisely the phase information where anti-concentration would have to occur.
