# Hostile audit of the cross-prime Apéry-zero problem

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
B_m=\sum_{k=0}^m\binom{m}{k}^2\binom{m+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:B_r\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]

and

\[
T_n=\{p\text{ prime}:n/2<p\le n,\ n-p\in Z_p\}.
\]

I do **not** find an unconditional proof that

\[
|T_n|=o(n/\log n)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\sum_{p\in T_n}\log p=o(n),
\]

and hence no proof of the full Problem 3.2 pointwise estimate. The observed maximum \(|T_n|=3\) through two million remains compelling numerical evidence, not a theorem.

There is, however, one genuine new cross-prime reduction that comes from the fixed-
\(n\) relation and was not present in the repository in this divisor-sensitive form:

> **Kummer-order divisor sieve.** For fixed \(n\), all candidate primes for which the selected Mellin/Kummer character has anything less than nearly maximal order have total logarithmic weight \(o(n)\), unconditionally. Consequently, the entire unresolved part is concentrated on primes for which
> \[
> \gcd(p-1,n-q)=n^{o(1)},
> \qquad q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor,
> \]
> equivalently the character \(\omega_p^{\,n-q}\) has order \(p^{1-o(1)}\).

For the top half \(q=1\), this says that all primes with large
\(\gcd(p-1,n-1)\) are harmless. The sharp residual top-half estimate is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\ p\mid B_n\\
\gcd(p-1,n-1)\le L_n}}
\log p=o(n),
}
\qquad
L_n=n^{o(1)},
\]

with an explicit permissible choice

\[
L_n=4\Bigl(\max_{m\le n}\tau(m)\Bigr)(\log n)^2.
\]

This is equivalent to the original top-half weighted target because the complementary large-gcd sector is already \(o(n)\).

The hostile conclusion about the proposed mechanisms is:

1. **Casoratians are exact intra-prime second-zero detectors, not cross-prime compressors.** They become useful only after one proves a new off-diagonal incidence or low-height carrier theorem using the exact Apéry cubic coefficient.
2. **Defect-dimension counting is local.** Rank one, rank two, or any fixed finite defect dimension supplies coordinates over each separate \(\mathbf F_p\); it does not couple those coordinates across different primes. Exact mod-\(p^2\) formulas confirm that badness kills only the zeroth state, not the first defect state.
3. **K3/Frobenius and generic ordinarity do not give the required quantifier.** The coefficient \(B_r\) is a moving Mellin coefficient, not the Hasse value of the K3 fiber at \(r(r+1)\). Even a uniform bound on the number of bad characters in each characteristic would still be a row bound and would not control one chosen character per prime.
4. **The polynomial \(H_p(X)\) is an exact spectral interpolation, but the family changes with \(p\).** Reflection is already quotiented out by \(X=r(r+1)\); it does not give a second equation or a multiple root. Generic interpolation, Smith/Fitting, and dimension arguments collapse back to the marked evaluation or to an integer whose height already contains the unknown radical.
5. **The only genuinely live routes are cross-prime routes:** either a vertical-in-\(p\), high-order Kummer/Mellin anti-concentration theorem, or an Apéry-specific recurrence/Casoratian construction of a second nonzero carrier of subexponential height. Both must use structure not preserved by arbitrary bounded-height second-order recurrences.

The row-versus-column implication should therefore remain permanently rejected. A bounded \(|Z_p|\), even \(|Z_p|\le2\) with exact reflection and one Casimir root, is logically compatible with an arbitrarily large \(T_n\).

## 1. Exact reduction and quantifier hygiene

### 1.1 The top-half set is the top-half prime support of one Apéry number

For \(n/2<p\le n\), write

\[
n=p+r,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

The Apéry Lucas congruence gives

\[
B_n=B_{p+r}\equiv B_1B_r=5B_r\pmod p.
\]

Thus, apart from the irrelevant prime \(5\),

\[
\boxed{
p\in T_n\iff p\mid B_n.
}
\]

Therefore

\[
R_n^{\mathrm{top}}
 :=\prod_{p\in T_n}p
 =\operatorname{rad}_{(n/2,n]}(B_n)
\]

and

\[
W_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
 :=\sum_{p\in T_n}\log p
 =\log R_n^{\mathrm{top}}.
\]

Since every such prime satisfies \(\log p=\log n+O(1)\),

\[
W_{\mathrm{top}}(n)=o(n)
\iff
|T_n|=o(n/\log n).
\]

A uniform \(O(1)\) bound would be vastly stronger than necessary.

The elementary carrier \(B_n\) gives only

\[
W_{\mathrm{top}}(n)\le \log B_n
 =n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n),
\]

which is exactly the wrong scale: linear rather than little-oh linear.

### 1.2 Top half is not, by itself, the full current Problem 3.2 residual

The audited support theorem in the project is two-channel. For
\(p>\sqrt n\), write

\[
n=qp+r,
\qquad q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

Then the large-prime support of the project gcd has a companion channel and a lower-digit channel. The companion channel has total logarithmic weight \(O(n^{2/3})\); the unresolved part is

\[
W_B(n)
 :=\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\p\mid B_{\,n\bmod p}}}\log p.
\]

The project also has the uniform valuation bound \(1\le v_p(G_n)\le3\) on this lower-digit channel. Consequently, after the already-controlled small-prime and companion terms,

\[
\log G_n=o(n)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
W_B(n)=o(n).
\]

The top half is the \(q=1\) subproblem of this weighted moving-digit statement. It is a necessary hard core, but a proof of the top half alone must not be advertised as a proof of the full gcd theorem unless the remaining \(q\ge2\) blocks are separately controlled.

## 2. What a bounded zero fiber actually implies

Suppose hypothetically that

\[
|Z_p|\le D
\]

for every prime \(p\). Then, for every \(N\),

\[
\sum_{n\le N}|T_n|
 \le \sum_{p\le N}|Z_p|
 \le D\pi(N),
\]

and similarly

\[
\sum_{n\le N}W_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
 \le \sum_{p\le N}|Z_p|\log p
 \le D\vartheta(N)
 =O(DN).
\]

These are useful **average** statements. They imply that the average count is
\(O(D/\log N)\) and the average logarithmic weight is \(O(D)\). They do not give a pointwise bound at one exceptional \(n\).

### 2.1 Exact reflected countermodel to the inference

Fix an integer \(n\) and an arbitrary set \(S\) of primes in \((n/2,n]\). For each \(p\in S\), put

\[
r_p=n-p,
\qquad
s_p=p-1-r_p=2p-1-n.
\]

Both lie in \([0,p-1]\), and \(s_p=p-1-r_p\). Define abstract rows

\[
Z_p^*=\{r_p,s_p\}
\]

for \(p\in S\), with the two entries merged in the central case, and take
\(Z_p^*=\varnothing\) for other primes. Then

* every row has at most two elements;
* every row has the exact reflection symmetry;
* nevertheless the selected column at \(n\) contains every prime in \(S\).

Thus a uniform row bound plus reflection permits

\[
|T_n|=|S|
\]

as large as the whole prime interval.

### 2.2 The same no-go already survives the Casimir variable

Let

\[
x_n=n(n+1).
\]

For \(p\in S\), take the degree-one polynomial

\[
\widetilde H_{p,n}(X)=X-x_n\in\mathbf F_p[X].
\]

Then

\[
\widetilde H_{p,n}(r(r+1))=0
\]

is equivalent to

\[
(r-n)(r+n+1)=0\pmod p,
\]

so its roots in \([0,p-1]\) are exactly \(r_p\) and \(s_p\). Hence one root in the \(X\)-line, exact reflection, and a degree-one polynomial still allow arbitrary alignment at one \(n\).

This countermodel is not claimed to be the Apéry family. Its purpose is precise: **any argument using only row cardinality, reflection, or the existence of a low-root-count polynomial in \(r(r+1)\) is logically incapable of proving the pointwise theorem.** A successful theorem must use the cross-prime arithmetic of the actual coefficients of \(H_p\), the exact Apéry recurrence, or another structure absent from this model.

## 3. A new unconditional cross-prime lemma: the Kummer-order divisor sieve

The fixed-\(n\) relation does contain one real piece of cross-prime arithmetic. It controls the order of the selected Kummer character.

For a prime \(p\), write

\[
n=qp+r,
\qquad q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor,
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

Let \(\omega_p\) be the Teichmüller character and define

\[
d_{p,n}:=\operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^r)
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,r)}.
\]

Because \(p\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\),

\[
r=n-qp\equiv n-q\pmod{p-1},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
d_{p,n}
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-q)}.
}
\]

This identity is the correct fixed-\(n\) geometric relation: for each quotient block \(q\), the selected character is

\[
\chi_{p,n}=\omega_p^{\,n-q},
\]

but its order generally grows with \(p\).

### 3.1 Fixed-block counting theorem

**Proposition 3.1.** Fix \(n\), an integer \(q\ge1\), and \(D\ge1\). Then

\[
\#\left\{
 p\text{ prime}:\left\lfloor\frac np\right\rfloor=q,
 \ d_{p,n}\le D
\right\}
\le
\tau(n-q)\left(\frac Dq+2\right).
\]

#### Proof

Put

\[
g=\gcd(p-1,r),
\qquad p-1=dg,
\qquad r=jg.
\]

Then

\[
d=d_{p,n},
\qquad 0\le j\le d,
\qquad \gcd(d,j)=1.
\]

Moreover

\[
n-q=q(p-1)+r=(qd+j)g.
\]

Set

\[
h=qd+j.
\]

Then

\[
h\mid n-q,
\qquad qd\le h\le(q+1)d,
\]

and \(p\) is determined by \((h,d)\):

\[
\boxed{
p=1+d\frac{n-q}{h}.}
\]

For a fixed divisor \(h\mid n-q\), the possible \(d\)'s lie in

\[
\frac{h}{q+1}\le d\le\frac hq.
\]

If \(d\le D\), then \(h\le(q+1)D\), and the number of integers in this interval is at most

\[
\frac{h}{q(q+1)}+2\le\frac Dq+2.
\]

Each prime supplies a unique pair \((h,d)\). Summing over the at most
\(\tau(n-q)\) divisors proves the result. The primality condition can only reduce the count. \(\square\)

### 3.2 Sharper top-half parametrization

For \(q=1\), write

\[
g=\gcd(p-1,n-1),
\qquad p-1=dg,
\qquad n-p=jg.
\]

Then

\[
n-1=(d+j)g,
\qquad 0\le j\le d,
\qquad \gcd(d,j)=1.
\]

Putting \(h=d+j\), one obtains the exact parametrization

\[
\boxed{
 h\mid n-1,
 \quad \frac h2\le d\le h,
 \quad \gcd(d,h)=1,
 \quad p=1+d\frac{n-1}{h}.
}
\]

Consequently

\[
\#\{n/2<p\le n:d_{p,n}\le D\}
\le
\sum_{\substack{h\mid n-1\\h\le2D}}\varphi(h)
\le 2D\tau(n-1).
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{p\in T_n\\d_{p,n}\le D}}\log p
\le2D\tau(n-1)\log n.
}
\]

This improves the cruder \(O(D^2)\) bounded-order slice count recorded earlier in the project and makes the dependence on the divisors of \(n-1\) explicit.

The central quadratic character has \(d=2\), hence \(j=1\), \(h=3\), and therefore

\[
p=\frac{2n+1}{3}.
\]

So the central Ahlgren--Ono modular-form slice contributes at most one prime for each fixed \(n\), without any ordinary-prime theorem.

### 3.3 Full large-prime reduction to nearly primitive characters

Let

\[
M(n)=\max_{1\le m\le n}\tau(m).
\]

The classical divisor bound gives

\[
M(n)=n^{o(1)}.
\]

Choose

\[
L_n=4M(n)(\log n)^2.
\]

Consider primes \(p>\sqrt n\), put \(q=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\), and suppose

\[
\gcd(p-1,n-q)>L_n.
\]

Then

\[
d_{p,n}
 =\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-q)}
 <\frac{n}{qL_n}.
\]

Apply Proposition 3.1 with \(D=n/(qL_n)\). The logarithmic weight of **all** such primes, even before imposing the Apéry-zero condition, is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{q<\sqrt n}
 \left(\frac{n}{q^2L_n}+2\right)\tau(n-q)\log n\\
&\qquad\ll
 \frac{nM(n)\log n}{L_n}
 +\sqrt n\,M(n)\log n\\
&\qquad=O(n/\log n)+n^{1/2+o(1)}
=o(n).
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore, unconditionally,

\[
\boxed{
W_B(n)
 =W_{\mathrm{prim}}(n)+o(n),
}
\]

where

\[
W_{\mathrm{prim}}(n)
 :=
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\
 p\mid B_{\,n\bmod p}\\
 \gcd(p-1,n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor)\le L_n}}
\log p.
\]

Every prime in this remaining sum satisfies

\[
\operatorname{ord}(\omega_p^{\,n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor})
\ge\frac{p-1}{L_n}=p^{1-o(1)}.
\]

For the top half,

\[
\boxed{
W_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
 =
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid B_n\\
\gcd(p-1,n-1)\le L_n}}
\log p+o(n).
}
\]

This is the strongest unconditional cross-prime reduction I obtain in this audit.

### 3.4 What the lemma does and does not do

It proves that all fixed-order, bounded-order, and even very substantially sublinear-order character slices are pointwise harmless. Thus proving ordinarity for a finite list of hypergeometric motives, or for every fixed rational character parameter, cannot be the load-bearing step: those slices already contribute \(o(n)\) without such a theorem.

It does **not** explain the observed maximum \(|T_n|=3\). In fact, for a typical prime one expects \(\gcd(p-1,n-1)\) to be small, so the nearly primitive sector is plausibly where most random hits live. The lemma narrows the target sharply; it does not make that target easy.

## 4. Casoratian audit

### 4.1 The exact theorem

Scale a solution of the Apéry recurrence by

\[
X_m=(m!)^3u_m.
\]

Then

\[
X_{m+1}=P(m)X_m-m^6X_{m-1},
\qquad
P(m)=34m^3+51m^2+27m+5.
\]

Let \(\operatorname{row}_m\) be the integral row carrying the initial state to \(X_m\). The canonical determinant identity proved in the project is

\[
\boxed{
\det(\operatorname{row}_a,\operatorname{row}_b)
 =(a!)^6N_{b-a}(a),
\qquad 0\le a<b,
}
\]

where \(N_h(x)\) is the Apéry gap continuant. If \(p>b\) and \(p\mid B_a\), then

\[
\boxed{
 p\mid\det(\operatorname{row}_a,\operatorname{row}_b)
 \iff p\mid B_b.
}
\]

This is a real and useful theorem. It detects a second zero for the **same prime**.

### 4.2 Why reflection does not turn it into a common carrier

For a top-half hit, the two reflected zero positions are

\[
r_p=n-p,
\qquad
s_p=2p-1-n.
\]

Thus \(p\) divides the determinant between rows \(r_p\) and \(s_p\). But both the base point and the gap vary with \(p\):

\[
s_p-r_p=3p-(2n+1).
\]

There is no fixed determinant \(D_n\) known to be divisible by every \(p\in T_n\). Reflection has produced two positions in one row of the incidence matrix; it has not produced a second column condition.

For two distinct hits \(p,q\in T_n\), one would need, for example,

\[
p\mid B_{r_q}
\]

before the \((p,q)\) Casoratian becomes divisible by \(p\). This is an additional cross-incidence, not a consequence of either prime being a hit at its own residue.

### 4.3 The exact missing correlation theorem

Let \(j_p=\min(r_p,s_p)\), and define

\[
C_A(n)=
\#\{(p,q)\in T_n^2:p\ne q,\ p\mid B_{j_q}\}.
\]

The local power-saving bound on \(|Z_p|\) gives an **upper** bound of the form

\[
C_A(n)\ll |T_n|n^{2/3}.
\]

A lower bound

\[
C_A(n)
\ge |T_n|^{1+\delta}n^{-o(1)}
\quad\text{for some }\delta>2/3
\]

would imply \(|T_n|=o(n/\log n)\). This is a valid sufficient theorem, but it is not currently supported by the random-root heuristic: independent sparse rows predict roughly

\[
C_A(n)\asymp |T_n|^2/n,
\]

not a superlinear expansion law. A proof would therefore have to show that any abnormally large star forces a highly non-random Apéry-specific clustering phenomenon.

### 4.4 Decisive coarse-structure no-go

The repository constructs, for any prescribed finite reflected star, a second-order recurrence with

* the same initial state \((1,5)\);
* the same off-diagonal coefficient \(-m^6\);
* the same adjacent determinant \((m!)^6\);
* coefficients of polynomial height;
* no consecutive zeros;
* exactly the prescribed reflected zero pairs modulo the chosen primes.

What the model does not preserve is the exact diagonal coefficient

\[
P(m)=34m^3+51m^2+27m+5
\]

and hence the modular/Picard--Fuchs origin. Therefore no theorem based only on recurrence order, coefficient size, reflection, no-consecutive-zeros, or Casoratian identities can control \(T_n\). Any successful Casoratian proof must use the exact cubic in an essential way.

### 4.5 The genuinely live Casoratian route

A concrete successful theorem would be either:

1. a recurrence-specific expansion theorem proving that a large star necessarily creates many off-diagonal zeros; or
2. a canonical integer \(C_n\ne0\), built from the exact Apéry rows or continuants, such that
   \[
   \prod_{p\in T_n^{\mathrm{prim}}}p\mid C_n,
   \qquad
   \log|C_n|=o(n).
   \]

Generic interpolation of the moving determinants is not enough: the project’s exact interpolation/Fitting analysis shows that universal constructions collapse to the original marked evaluation or retain the full unknown radical in their lattice index. The only remaining possibility is an identity special to the exact Apéry cubic.

## 5. Defect-dimension counting audit

### 5.1 A local defect space is not a cross-prime space

A rank-\(\rho\) Frobenius defect statement says that, for each fixed prime \(p\), the relevant block expansions are governed by \(\rho\) local coordinates in \(\mathbf F_p\). The coordinates for different primes are unrelated unless one constructs a single characteristic-zero object of controlled height whose reductions produce all of them.

CRT allows one independent local scalar, vector, or hyperplane condition at every prime. A fixed local dimension does not prevent one marked equation from being satisfied at every chosen prime.

In lattice language, if the local congruences have product modulus

\[
R=\prod_{p\in S}p,
\]

then the corresponding congruence lattice has index comparable with \(R\). Minkowski may distribute this index among \(\rho\) coordinates, producing vectors of scale \(R^{1/\rho}\), but any nonzero integer certificate divisible by every prime is still at least \(R\). Fixed dimension does not turn linear logarithmic height into little-oh logarithmic height.

### 5.2 The exact mod-\(p^2\) formula shows what goes wrong

The published Gessel--Lucas refinement, in the project’s notation, has the form

\[
B_{qp+r}
\equiv
B_q\bigl(B_r+pqB'_r\bigr)\pmod{p^2},
\qquad 0\le r<p.
\]

If \(p\mid B_r\), put

\[
u_r=B_r/p\pmod p.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{B_{qp+r}}p
\equiv
B_q(u_r+qB'_r)\pmod p.
\]

The badness condition kills the zeroth state \(B_r\pmod p\). It does not kill either of the first states \(u_r\) or \(B'_r\). Two ray descriptions can recover these two quantities; they do not provide two vanishing equations.

The cutoff-array calculation in `Q804.md` gives the same conclusion in a different basis: the first defect is an explicit derivative/Lucas-defect coordinate and is generally nonzero on a bad fiber. Thus “rank two” means two recoverable local states, not codimension two badness.

### 5.3 Higher-order elimination does not supply an Archimedean bound

Even if direct and reflected formulas eliminate all local Frobenius-defect scalars through order \(k\), the output is a congruence describing the quotient \(B_n/p\) or another local residue modulo \(p^k\). Knowing compatible residues for every prime in \(T_n\) is p-adic information. It does not bound the real size of the CRT lift.

To convert defect elimination into the desired theorem, one still needs one of the following genuinely global statements:

* the defect coordinates are reductions of a fixed finite list of integers of total height \(e^{o(n)}\);
* elimination produces a nonzero integer of height \(e^{o(n)}\) divisible by all target primes;
* or the local coordinates satisfy a cross-prime algebraic relation whose resultant has subexponential height.

No such global object is currently known. Dimension counting without it is circular: the product of the local moduli is precisely the radical one is trying to bound.

## 6. K3, Mellin Frobenius, and ordinarity

### 6.1 Coefficient versus fiber value

Let \(\Phi\) be a standard Laurent-polynomial model for the Apéry numbers, so

\[
B_m=\operatorname{CT}(\Phi^m).
\]

For the Beukers--Peters K3 pencil, the mod-\(p\) Hasse--Witt section in its geometric parameter \(t\) is, up to a nonzero normalization,

\[
\mathcal A_p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}B_m t^m.
\]

The residue \(B_r\) is the coefficient

\[
B_r=[t^r]\mathcal A_p(t),
\]

not the fiber value \(\mathcal A_p(r(r+1))\).

Equivalently, for \(1\le r\le p-2\),

\[
B_r
=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}\mathcal A_p(t)t^{-r}.
\]

Thus \(B_r\) is a multiplicative Fourier/Mellin coefficient of the whole Hasse section. Generic ordinarity of the K3 fibers controls zeros of \(\mathcal A_p(t)\) as a function of \(t\); it does not control zeros of its coefficient vector.

The 2026 factorization results for the Apéry generating truncation—square or a fixed discriminant factor times a square, depending on \(p\)—are genuine structure of the polynomial \(\mathcal A_p(t)\). They concern its roots and Galois group in the geometric parameter. They do not imply that the coefficients \(B_r\) are nonzero.

### 6.2 The moving Mellin object

The coefficient \(B_r\pmod p\) has a bounded-dimensional Kummer/Mellin cohomological realization for each fixed pair \((p,r)\). In the relevant rank-two weight-three object, the condition

\[
p\mid B_r
\]

is a nonordinary condition.

For fixed \(n\) and block \(q\), however, the character is

\[
\chi_{p,n}=\omega_p^{\,n-q}
\]

and its order is

\[
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,n-q)}.
\]

In the unresolved sector this order is \(p^{1-o(1)}\). The character field, its order, and any characteristic-zero Kummer realization therefore change with the same prime being counted. Restricting scalars from a cyclotomic coefficient field would have rank and discriminant growing essentially with \(p\).

This is why fixed-motive Chebotarev does not apply. The underlying K3 sheaf is fixed, but the Mellin character and coefficient field are not the reductions of one bounded-complexity compatible system over \(\mathbf Q\).

### 6.3 Why generic ordinarity still does not suffice

There are four distinct statements that are often conflated:

1. **Generic ordinarity in the geometric variable \(t\).** This concerns \(\mathcal A_p(t)\), not its coefficients.
2. **Density-one ordinarity for one fixed motive as \(p\) varies.** The central quadratic-character form is one such slice, but for fixed \(n\) it contributes at most one prime. Moreover, density one for nonordinary primes of the relevant non-CM weight-four form is itself not presently known.
3. **Few nonordinary characters for each fixed \(p\).** Even a uniform \(O(1)\) theorem is only a row bound. The selected characters \(\chi_{p,n}\) can, in principle, be exactly the exceptional ones for every prime.
4. **Equidistribution as the character varies at fixed \(p\).** Katz-type Mellin Sato--Tate controls continuous statistics of normalized traces. Exact divisibility by the residue characteristic is a shrinking p-adic condition, not a continuous class function. It also has the wrong horizontal quantifier: many characters in one field versus one character in each of many fields.

A Legendre-family warning is useful. A family can be generically ordinary while its Hasse polynomial has degree proportional to \(p\), and hence a large nonordinary divisor. Generic ordinarity means the Hasse polynomial is not identically zero; it does not give a bounded number of rational roots.

### 6.4 What geometric theorem would actually be relevant

After Proposition 3.1, the useful geometric target is no longer a theorem about bounded-order characters. It is a **vertical, varying-residue-characteristic estimate for nearly primitive characters**:

\[
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\
\gcd(p-1,n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor)\le L_n\\
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p\mid
\mathcal M_{p,\omega_p^{\,n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor}})
\equiv0\pmod p}}
\log p=o(n).
\]

This is essentially the sharpened target \(W_{\mathrm{prim}}(n)=o(n)\), now expressed in the correct Mellin language. To become a method rather than a restatement, it would need a new cross-characteristic large sieve, a low-height congruence carrier, or a global trace identity uniform in the high character order.

## 7. Audit of the explicit interpolation polynomial

Define

\[
H_p(X)=
\sum_{m=0}^{(p-1)/2}
\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}
 (X-j(j+1))^2}{(m!)^4}
\in\mathbf F_p[X].
\]

The top summand has degree \(p-1\), so

\[
\deg H_p=p-1.
\]

For every \(0\le r<p\),

\[
\boxed{B_r=H_p(r(r+1))\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p.}
\]

For \(r\le(p-1)/2\), this is immediate because the factor with \(j=r\) kills all later summands. For larger \(r\), it follows from reflection and the identity

\[
r(r+1)=(p-1-r)(p-r)\pmod p.
\]

### 7.1 The fixed-\(n\) equation is exact

Put \(x_n=n(n+1)\). If \(n/2<p\le n\) and \(r=n-p\), then

\[
x_n\equiv r(r+1)\pmod p.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
p\in T_n\iff H_p(x_n)=0.}
\]

This is a clean reformulation. It is not yet a cross-prime theorem because the polynomial \(H_p\), its degree, and all of its coefficients change with \(p\).

### 7.2 Reflection is already spent

The map

\[
r\longmapsto X=r(r+1)
\]

is the quotient by the involution \(r\mapsto-1-r\). The reflected pair is one \(X\)-point. Therefore reflection does not imply that \(H_p\) has two roots, a double root, or two independent equations. At the central index the quadratic quotient map ramifies, but that does not make the root of \(H_p\) multiple.

This is the exact reason that the two reflected descriptions cannot be counted as two constraints in a defect-dimension or resultant argument.

### 7.3 Why it is not automatically a K3 Hasse polynomial in \(X\)

The displayed \(H_p(X)\) is a terminating hypergeometric interpolation in the **coefficient index**. The variable \(X\) changes the upper hypergeometric parameters. It is not the geometric parameter \(t\) of the Beukers--Peters pencil.

To call \(H_p\) the Hasse invariant of a fixed characteristic-zero family over an \(X\)-line, one would need to construct:

* a fixed integral family;
* a fixed-rank cohomology group;
* a Cartier/Frobenius matrix whose Hasse scalar is the displayed polynomial;
* and uniform conductor/monodromy control.

No such construction follows from the interpolation identity. Degree growth by itself is not a contradiction—classical Hasse polynomials also have degree proportional to \(p\)—but generic ordinarity would still not give a small rational-root count.

### 7.4 Why interpolation and Fitting methods collapse

At the characteristic-zero integer \(X=n(n+1)\), the infinite hypergeometric series terminates and gives the large integer \(B_n\). Thus the most obvious common lift of the congruences is precisely the already-known carrier \(B_n\).

The project’s exact interpolation, Smith, subresultant, and modular-Siegel analyses establish the following no-go for universal constructions:

* one marked congruence per prime can be aligned by CRT;
* the resulting congruence lattice has index equal to the target radical;
* the marked Fitting ideal collapses to the original marked evaluation;
* a bounded local root count does not lower the height of that evaluation;
* generic fixed-degree combinations of the known interpolation coordinates have exponential height.

Therefore the polynomial \(H_p\) becomes useful only if one discovers an **Apéry-specific cross-prime identity among its coefficients** that produces a second carrier smaller than \(B_n\). No such identity is currently proved.

## 8. What the relation \(p=n-r\) genuinely gives

The fixed-\(n\) relation supplies exactly three rigorous pieces of cross-prime information:

1. **Common support:** every top-half hit is a prime divisor of \(B_n\).
2. **Character exponent:** the selected Mellin character is \(\omega_p^{n-1}\).
3. **Divisor parametrization:** bounded character order forces \(d+j\mid n-1\), giving Proposition 3.1 and its top-half corollary.

It does not force any fixed neighboring Apéry number \(B_{n+h}\), derivative, or companion solution to vanish modulo the same primes. In fact, the no-consecutive-zero property points in the opposite direction. The scalar-endomorphism theorem for the canonical Apéry difference module also rules out a hidden rational same-base endomorphism producing an independent synchronized section.

Reflection gives the second index

\[
2p-1-n,
\]

which still varies with \(p\). It is a same-prime statement, not a common-index statement.

Thus the best exact use of \(p=n-r\) presently available is the new order/divisor sieve above. The remaining nearly primitive sector has not been compressed into a second low-height integer.

## 9. Actual theorems versus heuristics

| Statement | Status | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Apéry Lucas congruence | theorem | Gives \(p\in T_n\iff p\mid B_n\) in the top half. |
| Reflection \(B_{p-1-r}\equiv B_r\pmod p\) | theorem | Pairs roots but supplies only one Casimir point. |
| Exact interpolation \(B_r=H_p(r(r+1))\) | theorem | Sharp local reformulation; no cross-prime coefficient theorem. |
| Canonical Casoratian determinant law | theorem | Detects two zeros for one prime; no automatic cross-prime incidence. |
| Power-saving local zero-fiber bounds in the project | theorem in the current project state | Useful for averages and upper bounds on cross-incidences; not pointwise alignment. |
| Mean \(|Z_p|\approx1\), paired-Poisson law, max \(|Z_p|=12\), max \(|T_n|=3\) through \(2\cdot10^6\) | exact computation on the stated range | Strong evidence, not an asymptotic theorem. |
| Generic ordinarity implies bounded \(|Z_p|\) | false implication | Generic ordinary families can have Hasse divisors of degree \(\asymp p\). |
| Uniform bounded \(|Z_p|\) implies bounded \(|T_n|\) | false implication | Row-versus-column fallacy, even with reflection and one Casimir root. |
| Fixed-form ordinary-prime density solves the moving problem | false quantifier | A fixed-order character is at most a sparse slice; the central slice gives at most one prime per \(n\). |
| High-order Kummer/Mellin traces behave randomly across primes | heuristic | Consistent with the scan, but no vertical local-limit theorem is known. |

### 9.1 Random-root heuristic

If the selected residue behaves like a random position in a row with mean one zero, then for a prime \(p\asymp n\)

\[
\Pr(p\in T_n)\asymp\frac1p.
\]

Hence

\[
\mathbf E|T_n|
 \asymp\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\frac1p
 \sim\frac{\log2}{\log n},
\]

while

\[
\mathbf E W_{\mathrm{top}}(n)
 \asymp\sum_{n/2<p\le n}\frac{\log p}{p}
 \sim\log2.
\]

This predicts exactly the observed qualitative behavior: usually no hit, occasionally one, and very small maxima. It also predicts that different rows are mostly isolated rather than expanding into many cross-incidences. That is why a Casoratian expansion theorem would be a genuinely new rigidity result, not a formal consequence of the random model.

## 10. Sharp residual estimates and the best live route

### 10.1 Sharp top-half residual

Let

\[
L_n=4\Bigl(\max_{m\le n}\tau(m)\Bigr)(\log n)^2.
\]

Then the following are equivalent:

\[
W_{\mathrm{top}}(n)=o(n)
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid B_n\\
\gcd(p-1,n-1)\le L_n}}
\log p=o(n).
}
\]

The omitted primes have selected character order below \(p/L_n\) and total weight \(o(n)\) by Proposition 3.1.

### 10.2 Sharp full large-prime residual

On the project’s current support theorem, the exact remaining estimate for Problem 3.2 can be sharpened to

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt n<p\le n\\
 p\mid B_{\,n\bmod p}\\
\gcd(p-1,n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor)\le L_n}}
\log p=o(n).
}
\]

All complementary large-gcd/low-order character primes have total weight \(o(n)\), the companion channel has weight \(O(n^{2/3})\), and the small-prime contribution is already negligible. This is the cleanest exact missing cross-prime estimate I can formulate after the audit.

### 10.3 Route A: high-order vertical Mellin anti-concentration

The most conceptually matched route is a theorem for the nearly primitive characters

\[
\chi_{p,n,q}=\omega_p^{\,n-q},
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}(\chi_{p,n,q})\ge p/L_n.
\]

It must be horizontal in the prime and sensitive to reduction modulo that same prime. Fixed-field Deligne bounds, ordinary Sato--Tate, fixed-order hypergeometric motives, and standard Chebotarev do not have this arity.

A useful result would be a congruence large sieve or local-limit theorem that gives a logarithmic saving uniformly for the selected high-order character. The theorem need not prove power cancellation; a pointwise log-power saving in the prime count would already suffice. No such theorem is currently available for this moving Mellin family.

### 10.4 Route B: an exact Apéry-specific low-height carrier

The algebraic alternative is to construct a second integer

\[
C_n\ne0
\]

such that

\[
\prod_{\substack{p\in T_n\\
\gcd(p-1,n-1)\le L_n}}p
\mid C_n,
\qquad
\log|C_n|=o(n).
\]

For the full problem, use the analogous union over quotient blocks. The construction could use canonical rows, gap continuants, a marked resultant, or a characteristic-zero interpolation, but it must satisfy two tests:

1. it genuinely uses the exact cubic \(34m^3+51m^2+27m+5\), not merely second-order recurrence identities or polynomial coefficient height;
2. its subexponential height is proved Archimedeanly, not inferred from a local dimension count or from CRT compatibility.

The existing Newton, Padé, Fitting, fixed-order Ore, and constant-gauge constructions fail one of these tests.

### 10.5 Priority recommendation

The best genuinely live program is:

1. **Adopt the nearly primitive-character reduction as the canonical target.** It removes every bounded-order/fixed-motive distraction.
2. **Search for an Apéry-specific congruence carrier in that sector.** The fixed exponent \(n-q\) and small value of \(\gcd(p-1,n-q)\) are the only new uniform arithmetic data available across primes.
3. **Use the Mellin/K3 realization to understand that carrier, not to invoke generic ordinarity.** The desired object must survive varying residue characteristic and character order.
4. **Keep the Casoratian route only if it exploits the exact cubic.** Generic expansion, bounded zero fibers, and local defect rank are closed as standalone routes.

A targeted computational follow-up would stratify every observed zero by

\[
g=\gcd(p-1,r)
\]

and every observed star hit by

\[
\gcd(p-1,n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor).
\]

The theorem above predicts that any genuinely difficult examples lie at small \(g\). Such data would not prove the theorem, but it would directly test whether the remaining high-order sector is the random sector suggested by the Poisson scan or has an exploitable exceptional pattern.

## 11. Final assessment

There is no hidden implication from bounded zero fibers to a pointwise bound. Reflection, Casimir interpolation, generic ordinarity, finite local defect dimension, and Casoratians are all **row-local** until one adds a new cross-prime coherence theorem.

The strongest positive result of this audit is the divisor-sensitive Kummer-order sieve. It proves that fixed-order and low-order Frobenius slices are unconditionally negligible, both in the top half and across all large-prime quotient blocks. The exact obstruction can therefore be stated without noise:

> Control the mod-\(p\) vanishing of the Apéry Mellin trace at one nearly primitive character \(\omega_p^{n-q}\) in each varying characteristic, or construct a nonzero subexponential-height integer divisible by every prime where that trace vanishes.

That is a real sharpening of the frontier, but not a solution. The observed \(|T_n|\le3\) is consistent with a random sparse model; none of the currently proved recurrence, K3, Frobenius, interpolation, or defect identities turns that heuristic sparsity into a pointwise theorem.
