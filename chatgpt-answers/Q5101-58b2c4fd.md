There is no general theorem in Calabi–Yau, difference-module, or Poincaré–Perron theory that transfers recessiveness from the Zudilin/AESZ module to the coprime P2.7 module merely because they have the same characteristic cubic and the same exponential roots.

The reason is structural:

- the Poincaré roots and Birkhoff powers are formal local data at infinity;

In Birkhoff’s analytic theory of difference systems, the missing invariant is precisely the periodic connection or monodromy matrix. Formal characteristic data do not determine it. # 2. Does “the same Calabi–Yau family” change the conclusion?

Only after that phrase is upgraded to an exact geometric statement.

There are several distinct possibilities that are often conflated:

1. two scalar operators have the same numerical fingerprint or AESZ label;

1. two operators have the same singular points;

1. two operators have the same local monodromy conjugacy classes;

1. two operators are cyclic presentations of the same differential module;

1. two objects lie in the same ambient Calabi–Yau geometry, but one is a period and the other a relative period or normal function;

1. one is obtained from the other by an explicit Euler, Hadamard, Fourier–Laplace, or middle-convolution functor.

Only items 4–6 supply a mechanism for transporting connection coefficients.

If two scalar operators are cyclic presentations of the same rank-three local system, then there is a rational or algebraic differential-module isomorphism after the appropriate pullback and gauge. In the coefficient-recurrence setting, an analogous rational difference-module morphism would appear as an Ore intertwiner. The formal-power obstruction and the coprimality computation show that this is not the relationship between L_Z and L_{27}.

They may still arise from the same ambient AESZ geometry through different functors—for example, one from pure periods and one from a regulator or normal-function extension. But then the relevant map must be exhibited.

Known mechanisms include:

- Katz’s middle convolution, with an explicit Riemann–Hilbert action; see Dettweiler–Reiter, arXiv:math/0305311;

- generalized Euler transforms constructing Calabi–Yau Picard–Fuchs systems; see Doran–Malmendier, arXiv:1503.07500;

- relative periods and Abel–Jacobi normal functions satisfying inhomogeneous Picard–Fuchs equations; see Li–Lian–Yau, arXiv:0910.4215.

These theories do not say that two systems with the same Poincaré cubic have related connection matrices. They say that once an explicit functor or geometric cycle is supplied, its effect can be computed.

A useful rigidity test is available in principle. For an irreducible physically rigid local system on a punctured projective line, the full tuple of local monodromy conjugacy classes determines the local system up to isomorphism. But:

- matching singular points is not matching local conjugacy classes;

- the source and target formal exponents differ;

- no rigidity computation for the relevant target generating ODE has established an isomorphism;

- and the current difference modules are demonstrably non-isomorphic.

Thus “same AESZ family” is presently a discovery clue, not a transfer theorem.

If the AESZ identification can be upgraded to an actual geometric construction, the cleanest conceptual proof would be:

This route is deeper than the Beukers certificate but may explain why the constant is the mixed weight-two/weight-three period.

(a) No known CY or difference-equation theorem transfers recessiveness between coprime modules from a common Poincaré polynomial or singular locus. Rigid-local-system theorems apply only after the full local monodromy data and rigidity hypotheses are verified. The different formal powers already show that the source and target are not the same formal module.
