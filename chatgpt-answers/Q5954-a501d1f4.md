# Multi-target reflected carriers: exact saturation and a global transfer/Plücker obstruction

## Verdict

There is **no nonzero gap-height carrier in the natural universal class generated by**

- multilinear determinants of target rows,
- Plücker contractions of reflected endpoint pairs,
- continuant forests obtained by cutting and rejoining second-order transfer products, or
- polynomial/telescoping products of transfer entries,

provided denominator clearing uses only recurrence/edge denominators (hence no inverses of the distinguished solution values).

The obstruction is stronger than the single-pair height estimate.  After the universal central factors
\[
N_{h_i}(u_i)=p_i\widehat N_{h_i}(u_i)
\]
are saturated, the reflected target condition at characteristic \(p_i\) has **one rank-one target slot**, represented by \(b_{u_i}\), where
\[
 u_i=\min(r_i,s_i),\qquad
 v_i=\max(r_i,s_i),\qquad
 h_i=v_i-u_i,
\]
\[
 r_i=n-p_i,\qquad s_i=p_i-1-r_i,\qquad
 p_i=2u_i+h_i+1.
\]
Every fully target-sensitive multilinear contraction contains one such slot for every target.  Transfer telescoping can shorten only the coefficient network; it cannot cancel a leaf slot without either

1. becoming an identically zero Plücker relation,
2. taking that prime from the already universal factor \(p_i\), which is circular, or
3. dividing by \(b_{u_i}\), whose denominator clearing restores the same slot.

More precisely, on either monotone side of the reflection center, if \(p_1<\cdots<p_k\), \(g_j=p_{j+1}-p_j\), and \(s_i<r_i\), then
\[
 u_i=u_1+2\sum_{j<i}g_j,
\]
so the unavoidable target-slot weight is
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{i=1}^k u_i
 =k u_1+2\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(k-j)g_j.
 }
\tag{1}
\]
Adjacent gaps control only \(\sum g_j\), not this weighted sum.  Thus a forest does not turn one exponential slot per target into one slot per gap.

This is a formal obstruction for the stated universal determinant/continuant/transfer constructions.  It does **not** rule out a genuinely arithmetic, nonlinear compression such as a small Bézout certificate for the gcd of the existing time-Casoratians.  Indeed, the current exact audit at \(n=321\) has a 98-bit gcd containing all three targets, but no small-height Bézout relation is known.

## 1. Exact local Casoratian and the smallest denominator clearing

Put
\[
 P(m)=34m^3+51m^2+27m+5.
\]
For the factorially renormalized Apéry recurrence
\[
 B_m=(m!)^3b_m,
\]
we have
\[
 B_{m+1}=P(m)B_m-m^6B_{m-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
Let the continuants be
\[
 N_0(u)=0,\qquad N_1(u)=1,
\]
\[
 N_{h+1}(u)=P(u+h)N_h(u)-(u+h)^6N_{h-1}(u).
\tag{3}
\]
Then every solution of (2) satisfies
\[
 B_{u+h}
 =N_h(u)B_{u+1}-(u+1)^6N_{h-1}(u+1)B_u.
\tag{4}
\]
For the two distinguished original solutions \(a,b\), whose Casoratian is
\[
 a_m b_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m=\frac6{m^3},
\]
(4) gives the exact nonadjacent Casoratian
\[
 \boxed{
 a_{u+h}b_u-a_u b_{u+h}
 =\frac{6N_h(u)}{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{h}(u+j)\right)^3}.
 }
\tag{5}
\]

Let
\[
 M_{u,h}=\prod_{j=1}^{h}(u+j),\qquad
 g_{u,h}=\gcd\bigl(6N_h(u),M_{u,h}^3\bigr).
\]
The **smallest denominator clearing** of (5) is therefore exactly
\[
 \operatorname{num}_{\rm red}
 =\frac{6N_h(u)}{g_{u,h}},\qquad
 \operatorname{den}_{\rm red}
 =\frac{M_{u,h}^3}{g_{u,h}}.
\tag{6}
\]
There is no smaller positive denominator, by the definition of reduced numerator and denominator.

For a top-half target, \(v=u+h<p\), so \(p\nmid M_{u,h}\).  The reflection identity
\[
 N_h(-x-h-1)=(-1)^{h-1}N_h(x)
\tag{7}
\]
shows that, for even \(h\),
\[
 2x+h+1\mid N_h(x)
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[x].
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(h\) is even and \(2u+h+1=p\), hence
\[
 p\mid N_h(u)
\tag{9}
\]
**for every such reflected interval, independently of whether \(p\mid b_u\)**.  Since the denominator in (6) is a \(p\)-unit, the factor \(p\) survives in the reduced numerator.  Dividing it out leaves no consequence of targetness.  This proves the precise saturation asserted in the question.

It is useful to record the endpoint form in the original normalization.  Set
\[
 D_{u,h}=\prod_{t=u+2}^{u+h}t^3,
 \qquad
 c_{u,h}=(u+1)^3N_{h-1}(u+1).
\]
Writing \(N_h(u)=p\widehat N_h(u)\), (4) is equivalent to
\[
 \boxed{
 D_{u,h}b_{u+h}
 =p\widehat N_h(u)b_{u+1}-c_{u,h}b_u.
 }
\tag{10}
\]
Every factor in \(D_{u,h}\) is below \(p\), so it is again a \(p\)-unit.

Modulo \(p\), (10) becomes
\[
 D_{u,h}b_{u+h}\equiv-c_{u,h}b_u\pmod p.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the two reflected endpoint conditions are one condition, not two.  Away from the usual boundary singularities, the target line is
\[
 b_u\equiv0\pmod p,
\tag{12}
\]
and reflection supplies \(b_{u+h}\equiv0\) automatically.  This is the local rank-one fact used below.

## 2. Exact nested transfer factorization

Use the step matrix
\[
 A_m=\begin{pmatrix}P(m)&-m^6\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 X_m=\binom{B_m}{B_{m-1}},
\qquad X_{m+1}=A_mX_m.
\]
For \(a<b\), define
\[
 T[a,b]=A_{b-1}\cdots A_a,
\qquad X_b=T[a,b]X_a.
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
 T[a,c]=T[b,c]T[a,b].
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose \(p_i<p_{i+1}\) are on the side \(s_i<r_i\), and put
\[
 g_i=p_{i+1}-p_i.
\]
The reflected intervals satisfy
\[
 u_{i+1}=u_i+2g_i,\qquad
 v_{i+1}=v_i-g_i,\qquad
 h_{i+1}=h_i-3g_i.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus they are nested and
\[
 \boxed{
 T[u_i,v_i]
 =T[v_{i+1},v_i]\,T[u_{i+1},v_{i+1}]\,T[u_i,u_{i+1}].
 }
\tag{16}
\]
The two annuli have lengths \(g_i\) and \(2g_i\).  Consequently the complete coefficient network for a chain can indeed be assembled from the innermost transfer plus only
\[
 3\sum_i g_i
\]
additional steps.

This is the genuine telescoping opportunity.  The obstruction is that (16) telescopes **matrices**, whereas targetness lives in one scalar leaf slot for each characteristic.  The common middle transfer can be reused, but the target slots cannot.

For targets on the other side of \((2n+1)/3\), reverse the order and obtain the same nested statement.  A target set crossing the center splits into two such chains plus one bridge.  The obstruction below is local to each target and therefore unaffected by that split.

## 3. The target-slot ideal

Work in the universal two-dimensional solution module of (2), localized at the edge factors used in the transfer matrices.  All those edge factors are \(p_i\)-units because the relevant indices are below \(p_i\).

Let \(\ell_i\) denote the universal linear form “value at \(u_i\)”.  In the distinguished specialization it is a fixed nonzero denominator multiple of \(b_{u_i}\).  Since the transfer from the initial state to \(u_i\) is invertible modulo \(p_i\), \(\ell_i\) is a primitive nonzero linear form modulo \(p_i\).

A linear row \(R_i\) made from transfer-generated evaluations is called target-admissible if every entry vanishes modulo \(p_i\) whenever the reflected target condition is imposed.  By (11)-(12), this means that over \(\mathbb F_{p_i}\) every entry vanishes on the hyperplane \(\ell_i=0\).  Therefore every entry has an exact lift
\[
 R_i=p_iA_i+\ell_i C_i.
\tag{17}
\]
Equivalently, the local target ideal is
\[
 J_i=(p_i,\ell_i).
\tag{18}
\]
For distinct primes, these ideals are pairwise comaximal:
\[
 J_i+J_j=(1),
\]
because integers \(a,b\) exist with \(ap_i+bp_j=1\).  Hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \bigcap_{i=1}^kJ_i=\prod_{i=1}^kJ_i.
 }
\tag{19}
\]
This is the global algebraic obstruction.

### Determinant form

Let \(M\) be a \(k\times k\) matrix whose \(i\)-th row is target-admissible.  From (17), multilinearity gives the exact expansion
\[
 \det M
 =\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}}
 \left(\prod_{i\notin S}p_i\right)
 \left(\prod_{i\in S}\ell_i\right)
 \Delta_S,
\tag{20}
\]
for explicit mixed minors \(\Delta_S\).

There are only two possibilities.

1. A target prime \(p_i\) is supplied by the explicit factor in (20).  Then that divisibility is structural, exactly like (8)-(9), and says nothing about targetness.
2. The prime is supplied target-sensitively.  Then the term contains \(\ell_i\).

The component in which **all** primes are target-sensitive is
\[
 \boxed{
 (\ell_1\cdots\ell_k)\det(C_1,\ldots,C_k).
 }
\tag{21}
\]
If the final determinant is nonzero and does not take any prime from a universal \(p_i\)-factor, one target slot survives for every row.

The same proof applies, by induction on the leaves, to any multilinear transfer forest or tensor-network contraction.  Matrix telescoping changes only the coefficient multiplying \(\ell_1\cdots\ell_k\).

### Denominator clearing does not evade (21)

All recurrence denominators are edge products independent of the distinguished solution and are \(p_i\)-units.  Taking the least common denominator of the determinant terms, followed by primitive integer reduction, preserves (17)-(21).  To remove \(\ell_i\) one must invert the solution value itself.  The smallest denominator clearing then contains that same solution value and restores the slot.  Thus a ratio construction does not improve the primitive integer height.

A construction may of course insert \(p_i\) explicitly, for example by adding a row coordinate equal to \(p_i\).  Then the row is visibly divisible by \(p_i\), but the resulting height estimate already contains \(\prod p_i\) and is tautological.

## 4. Height forced by the target slots

The Apéry numbers satisfy
\[
 \log b_m=m\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log(m+2)).
\tag{22}
\]
After primitive edge-denominator clearing, a nonzero fully target-sensitive component has the form
\[
 F_{\rm ts}=(\ell_1\cdots\ell_k)H,
\tag{23}
\]
with \(H\) an integer polynomial.  At the distinguished Apéry specialization, if the carrier is nonzero then \(|H|\ge1\), while each \(\ell_i\) contains \(b_{u_i}\) after undoing its fixed edge normalization.  Hence its normalized logarithmic height obeys
\[
 \boxed{
 \log|F_{\rm ts}|
 \ge \sum_{i=1}^k\log b_{u_i}
 =\left(\sum_{i=1}^ku_i\right)\log(17+12\sqrt2)
 +O(k\log n).
 }
\tag{24}
\]
On the nested side, substituting (1) gives
\[
 \log|F_{\rm ts}|
 \ge
 \left(
 ku_1+2\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(k-j)g_j
 \right)\log(17+12\sqrt2)
 +O(k\log n).
\tag{25}
\]
This is not \(O(\sum g_j)+o(n)\) uniformly.  The gaps are counted with the number of target leaves lying beyond them.  In a bulk target block where \(u_i=\Theta(n)\), (24) is \(\Theta(kn)\).

This is the precise meaning of “one full exponential slot per independent target.”  Near the reflection boundary a slot can be shorter than \(n\), but it is still present, and the total is the weighted quantity (1), not the unweighted sum of adjacent gaps.

## 5. Endpoint Plücker analysis

Put
\[
 v_i=(b_{r_i},b_{s_i}),
\qquad
 E_{ij}=\det(v_i,v_j)
 =b_{r_i}b_{s_j}-b_{r_j}b_{s_i}.
\tag{26}
\]
At the actual targets, \(p_i\mid v_i\) and \(p_j\mid v_j\), hence
\[
 p_ip_j\mid E_{ij}.
\]

The exact endpoint identity (10) displays the structural/target decomposition.  With
\[
 D_i=D_{u_i,h_i},\quad c_i=c_{u_i,h_i},\quad
 N_{h_i}(u_i)=p_iQ_i,
\]
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_iD_jE_{ij}
={}&p_iQ_iD_j b_{u_i+1}b_{u_j}
-p_jQ_jD_i b_{u_j+1}b_{u_i}\\
&+(c_jD_i-c_iD_j)b_{u_i}b_{u_j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
The first two summands take one prime from the universal continuant factor.  The part sensitive to both target conditions is exactly a multiple of
\[
 b_{u_i}b_{u_j}.
\tag{28}
\]
Equation (27) is the two-row instance of (20).

### Three rows

For three two-vectors the only universal exterior relation is
\[
 E_{12}v_3-E_{13}v_2+E_{23}v_1=0.
\tag{29}
\]
Every term is divisible by \(p_1p_2p_3\) at the targets, but the combination is identically zero.  Taking either coordinate does not produce a carrier.

A tempting \(3\times3\) determinant with rows \((b_{r_i},b_{s_i},1)\) fails divisibility: modulo \(p_1\), its first row becomes \((0,0,1)\), leaving \(E_{23}\), which need not be divisible by \(p_1\).  Making the third coordinate target-divisible restores one target slot in that row; using the coordinate \(p_i\) supplies the prime structurally and is circular.

### Four or more rows

Multilinear scalar \(SL_2\)-contractions of two-vectors are generated by products of brackets \(E_{ij}\).  For odd \(k\) there is no scalar contraction without an external vector or a repeated row.  For even \(k\), every monomial is a perfect-matching product.  The first relation at \(k=4\) is the Plücker identity
\[
 E_{12}E_{34}-E_{13}E_{24}+E_{14}E_{23}=0.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus an exact cancellation among the three matchings is either (30), hence zero, or leaves a nonzero matching product with one full pair carrier per matched pair.

More generally, for a graph product
\[
 \prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}E_{ij},
\]
the exponent of \(p_i\) is \(\deg_G(i)\).  A spanning tree repeats primes at internal vertices; a perfect matching uses each prime once but retains \(k/2\) pair carriers.  A Plücker forest cannot turn these into one gap-only scalar.

## 6. Why the pair minors themselves do not telescope

Assume \(p_i<p_j\) and both targets lie on the side \(s<r\).  Write \(g=p_j-p_i\).  The two products in (26) have index sums
\[
 r_i+s_j=p_i-1+2g,
\]
\[
 r_j+s_i=p_i-1-g.
\tag{31}
\]
Their difference is \(3g\).  From (22),
\[
 \frac{b_{r_j}b_{s_i}}{b_{r_i}b_{s_j}}
 =(17+12\sqrt2)^{-3g}\,n^{O(1)}.
\tag{32}
\]
Thus the two terms are not balanced.  The dominant exponential does not cancel, and
\[
 \boxed{
 \log|E_{ij}|
 =(p_i-1+2g)\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
 }
\tag{33}
\]
The gap contributes \(2g\), but there remains a baseline \(p_i-1=\Theta(n)\).  A product over a matching or forest sums these baselines.  This is the global reason the pairwise method is exponentially tall; it is not merely a loose triangle-inequality estimate.

## 7. Exact test at \(n=321\), targets \(179,193,211\)

The reflected data are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
p&r=321-p&s=p-1-r&[u,v]&h\\ \hline
179&142&36 &[36,142]&106\\
193&128&64 &[64,128]&64\\
211&110&100&[100,110]&10.
\end{array}
\tag{34}
\]
The prime gaps are
\[
 g_1=14,\qquad g_2=18,\qquad g_1+g_2=32.
\]
The transfer annuli have total length
\[
 3g_1+3g_2=42+54=96.
\tag{35}
\]
So this triple is an especially favorable test for the proposed telescoping idea.

Nevertheless, the target-slot indices sum to
\[
 36+64+100=200,
\tag{36}
\]
which also follows from (1):
\[
 3\cdot36+2(2\cdot14+1\cdot18)=200.
\]
The exact Apéry values have decimal digit counts
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
m&36&64&100&110&128&142\\ \hline
\operatorname{digits}(b_m)&53&95&150&165&193&214.
\end{array}
\tag{37}
\]
Thus the unavoidable fully target-sensitive slot
\[
 b_{36}b_{64}b_{100}
\]
already has between 296 and 298 decimal digits (between about 981 and 991 bits), before any nontrivial coefficient is included.  This is far larger than a gap-only weight based on \(32\).

Exact recurrence evaluation gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{minor}&\text{index sums}&\text{bit length}&\text{forced target factors}\\ \hline
E_{12}&206\text{ versus }164&1024&179\cdot193\\
E_{23}&228\text{ versus }174&1135&193\cdot211\\
E_{13}&242\text{ versus }146&1206&179\cdot211.
\end{array}
\tag{38}
\]
The tree product \(E_{12}E_{23}\) has bit length \(2159\) and is divisible by
\[
 179\cdot193^2\cdot211=1,406,857,481.
\]
The desired radical is only
\[
 179\cdot193\cdot211=7,289,417
\tag{39}
\]
(23 bits).  The tree has therefore retained more than two thousand bits of exponential endpoint height.

### Comparison with the existing time-Casoratian gcd

Section 68.16 of `Q32_SEPARATION_ANALYSIS.md` records, for this same block, the exact gcd of the ten time-Casoratians:
\[
 2^{34}3^{15}5^5\cdot43\cdot179\cdot193\cdot211,
\tag{40}
\]
a 98-bit integer.  The individual time-Casoratians have thousands of bits.  This is a real global collapse, but it is a **gcd**, not a universal Plücker contraction.  Bézout's identity represents (40) as an integer combination of the ten minors, yet the Euclidean coefficients depend on their full values and currently have no subexponential bound.  The six-column Smith calculations likewise identify determinantal divisors but do not supply a small transfer-local Bézout certificate.

Hence (40) does not contradict the obstruction: gcd/Smith reduction is nonlinear arithmetic after specialization, while (17)-(30) concern universal polynomial/multilinear transfer identities.

## 8. Replay code for the \(n=321\) local and pairwise checks

The following is dependency-free exact integer arithmetic.  It regenerates the Apéry numbers from the recurrence, verifies all reflected target incidences, verifies the universal continuant factor and the minimal Casoratian denominator, and prints the pair-minor bit lengths.

```python
from math import gcd, prod


def P(m):
    return 34*m**3 + 51*m**2 + 27*m + 5


def apery_values(limit):
    b = [1, 5]
    for m in range(1, limit):
        num = P(m)*b[m] - m**3*b[m-1]
        den = (m+1)**3
        assert num % den == 0
        b.append(num // den)
    return b


def continuant(u, h):
    # N_0=0, N_1=1.
    n0, n1 = 0, 1
    for t in range(1, h):
        n0, n1 = n1, P(u+t)*n1 - (u+t)**6*n0
    return n1


n = 321
targets = (179, 193, 211)
b = apery_values(n)
rows = []

for p in targets:
    r = n-p
    s = p-1-r
    u, v = sorted((r, s))
    h = v-u
    assert p == 2*u+h+1
    assert h % 2 == 0
    assert b[r] % p == 0
    assert b[s] % p == 0

    Nh = continuant(u, h)
    assert Nh % p == 0                 # universal reflection factor

    M = prod(range(u+1, v+1))
    g = gcd(6*Nh, M**3)
    red_num = 6*Nh // g
    red_den = M**3 // g
    assert red_num % p == 0
    assert red_den % p != 0            # smallest denominator is a p-unit
    rows.append((p, r, s, u, v, h))

print("rows", rows)
print("target_radical", prod(targets))
print("digits", {m: len(str(b[m])) for m in (36,64,100,110,128,142)})


def E(i, j):
    pi, ri, si, *_ = rows[i]
    pj, rj, sj, *_ = rows[j]
    value = b[ri]*b[sj] - b[rj]*b[si]
    assert value % (pi*pj) == 0
    return value

E12 = E(0, 1)
E23 = E(1, 2)
E13 = E(0, 2)
print("minor_bits", E12.bit_length(), E23.bit_length(), E13.bit_length())
print("tree_bits", (E12*E23).bit_length())
```

Expected final numerical lines are

```text
target_radical 7289417
digits {36: 53, 64: 95, 100: 150, 110: 165, 128: 193, 142: 214}
minor_bits 1024 1135 1206
tree_bits 2159
```

## 9. Consequence for Route A

The desired formula would have to do more than telescope nested transfer matrices.  It would have to eliminate the product of target slots in (21) while remaining nonzero and while not obtaining the primes from the universal factors \(N_{h_i}(u_i)/\widehat N_{h_i}(u_i)=p_i\).  In the universal multilinear/transfer algebra this is impossible.

Therefore:

- a single multi-row determinant does not close the theorem;
- endpoint Plücker forests either vanish identically or retain matching/tree products;
- continuant cutting telescopes coefficient matrices but retains one leaf slot per target;
- transfer ratios require solution-dependent denominators, whose primitive clearing restores those slots;
- multiplying the universal quotients \(N_{h_i}(u_i)\) merely inserts \(\prod p_i\) tautologically.

The remaining plausible positive route is narrower and genuinely arithmetic: turn the small observed gcd such as (40) into a **uniform small-height Bézout certificate**, probably using the special Ore/boundary identities of Sections 68.16-68.18, not only their determinantal divisibility.  Any successful construction must leave the universal multilinear transfer category by exploiting distinguished Apéry coefficients, p-adic quotient information, or a controlled Euclidean/subresultant process across the full family of minors.
