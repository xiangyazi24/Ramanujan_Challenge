## 1. Exact definitions

For any integer sequence \(X=(X_e)\), write

\[
 G_{a,L}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^i
 \binom{a+i}{i}\binom{a+L+1}{L-i}X_{a+i}.                             \tag{3}
\]

Fix the retained core

\[
 I=[D,D+N-1],\qquad D>N,
\]

and an admissible margin \(m\ge2\).  Put

\[
 d_m=D-m+1,\qquad L_m=N+m-2.                                         \tag{4}
\]

Then the two stencils are

\[
 [d_m,d_m+L_m]=[D-m+1,D+N-1],
\]

and

\[
 [D,D+L_m]=[D,D+N+m-2],
\]

whose common node set is exactly the retained core \(I\).

For the two actual arrays, I use the notation of the gauge construction:

\[
 Y_e=C_{n-1}(e),                                                       \tag{5}
\]

and

\[
 W_e=\sum_{j=0}^{4}u_j(n)
       \{C_{n+j}(e-1)+C_{n+j}(e+1)\},                                 \tag{6}
\]

where \({\cal U}=\sum_{j=0}^4u_j(s)S_s^j\) is the primitive integral
order-four origin operator from `q32_doubled_period_gauge_audit.sage`.  Its
scalar identities are

\[
 {\cal U}(J)_s=\mu(s)b_s,
 \qquad
 {\cal U}(b)_s=0,                                                      \tag{7}
\]

with \(\mu\in\mathbb Z[s]\) the degree-30 multiplier called
`multiplier` in the script.

Set

\[
 u_m=(G_{d_m,L_m}(Y),G_{d_m,L_m}(W)),
 \qquad
 v_m=(G_{D,L_m}(Y),G_{D,L_m}(W)).                                     \tag{8}
\]

For \(0\le s<m-1\), define

\[
 B_{m,s}=\binom{d_m+s+L_m+1}{L_m}
        =\binom{D+N+s}{N+m-2},                                        \tag{9}
\]

and

\[
 g_m=\gcd_{0\le s<m-1}B_{m,s}.                                       \tag{10}
\]

The proposed integer is

\[
 H_m=\frac{\det(u_m,v_m)}{g_m}.                                       \tag{11}
\]

The integrality in (11) is not empirical; it follows coefficientwise from the
next identity.

## 2. The exact normalized beta kernel

For a formal variable \(T\), define the row polynomial

\[
 P_{a,L}(T)
 =\sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^i
   \binom{a+i}{i}\binom{a+L+1}{L-i}T^{a+i}
 =T^aQ_{a+1,L}(T).                                                     \tag{12}
\]

For any sequence \(X\), let \(\Phi_X(\sum c_eT^e)=\sum c_eX_e\).  Then

\[
 \Phi_X(P_{a,L})=G_{a,L}(X).                                         \tag{13}
\]

The adjacent Newton identity of Sections 54--56 is equivalent to the following
polynomial identity:

\[
 \boxed{
 P_{a,L}(T)-P_{a+1,L}(T)
 =\binom{a+L+1}{L}T^a(1-T)^{L+1}.}
                                                                            \tag{14}
\]

One may verify (14) either by multiplying out the two beta polynomials or by
applying the last identity in (54.3) to the monomial sequence \(X_e=T^e\).
Summing (14) from \(a=d_m\) to \(a=D-1\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 P_{d_m,L_m}(T)-P_{D,L_m}(T)
 =(1-T)^{L_m+1}\sum_{s=0}^{m-2}B_{m,s}T^{d_m+s}.}
                                                                            \tag{15}
\]

Consequently

\[
 K_m(T):=
 (1-T)^{L_m+1}
 \sum_{s=0}^{m-2}\frac{B_{m,s}}{g_m}T^{d_m+s}
 \in\mathbb Z[T]                                                        \tag{16}
\]

and

\[
 P_{d_m,L_m}-P_{D,L_m}=g_mK_m.                                      \tag{17}
\]

Applying \(\Phi_Y\) and \(\Phi_W\), and using
\(\det(u,u-z)=-\det(u,z)\), proves the exact formula

\[
 \boxed{
 H_m=-\det\left(
   (\Phi_Y(P_{d_m,L_m}),\Phi_W(P_{d_m,L_m})),
   (\Phi_Y(K_m),\Phi_W(K_m))
 \right).}                                                           \tag{18}
\]

This proves \(H_m\in\mathbb Z\) for arbitrary integer arrays \(Y,W\).  It also
shows exactly what has been gained by dividing by \(g_m\): \(K_m\) is the
primitive curvature kernel left after the common-node Pascal content is
removed.

No symmetry of the Laurent coefficient array is used in (12)--(18).

## 3. Exact core-prime ledger

Let

\[
 Q_I=\prod_{\substack{D<p\le D+N\\p\ {m prime}}}p.                  \tag{19}
\]

Assume the admissibility inequality

\[
 L_m=N+m-2<D+1.                                                       \tag{20}
\]

This is the condition used in the large-prime blocks; in particular every core
prime \(p>D\) satisfies \(p>L_m\).

### Lemma 1.  For every core prime \(p\),

\[
 v_p(B_{m,s})=1\quad(0\le s<m-1),
 \qquad v_p(g_m)=1.                                                   \tag{21}
\]

**Proof.**  The numerator interval of \(B_{m,s}\) is

\[
 [D+s-m+3,D+N+s].                                                     \tag{22}
\]

Its lower endpoint is at most \(D+1\), its upper endpoint is at least
\(D+N\), and therefore it contains \(p\).  Its length is \(L_m<p\), so it
contains no second multiple of \(p\).  The denominator \(L_m!\) is a
\(p\)-unit.  This proves (21).  \(\square\)

In particular,

\[
 Q_I\mid g_m,
 \qquad
 \gcd(Q_I,g_m/Q_I)=1.                                                \tag{23}
\]

The interval-prime beta collapse gives

\[
 P_{d_m,L_m}(T)\equiv P_{D,L_m}(T)\equiv T^{p-1}\pmod p.             \tag{24}
\]

Hence

\[
 u_m\equiv v_m\equiv(Y_{p-1},W_{p-1})\pmod p.                        \tag{25}
\]

For the actual doubled-period array, (68.48) gives

\[
 W_{p-1}\equiv\mu(n)Y_{p-1}\pmod p.                                 \tag{26}
\]

If \(p\) is a target, then \(p\mid Y_{p-1}\), and (26) gives
\(p\mid W_{p-1}\).  Therefore both vectors in (8) are divisible by \(p\),
so

\[
 v_p(\det(u_m,v_m))\ge2.                                             \tag{27}
\]

Combining (21) and (27) proves

\[
 \boxed{p\mid H_m\quad\text{for every admissible }m.}                \tag{28}
\]

Taking the product over distinct core targets proves (1).

There is also an exact first-order formula for a non-target core prime.  Reducing
(18) modulo \(p\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 H_m\equiv-\det\left(
  (Y_{p-1},W_{p-1}),
  (\Phi_Y(K_m),\Phi_W(K_m))
 \right)\pmod p.}                                                    \tag{29}
\]

Thus a non-target core prime survives in every \(H_m\) precisely when every
primitive curvature observation in (29) remains on the same line as the marked
value.  Neither beta--Padé nor the scalar alias (26) excludes that event.

The statement in the question that the eleven displayed targets have exactly
valuation two in the raw determinant is therefore an additional actual-state
fact.  The universal theorem is the lower bound (27), not equality.

## 4. Removing the universal doubled-period alias

Put

\[
 X_e=W_e-\mu(n)Y_e.                                                   \tag{30}
\]

By (26), \(X_{p-1}\equiv0\pmod p\) for every core prime.  Applying the
interval-prime collapse to every left and right mask gives

\[
 Q_I\mid G_{d_m,L_m}(X),
 \qquad
 Q_I\mid G_{D,L_m}(X).                                               \tag{31}
\]

Define integers

\[
 R_m^-={G_{d_m,L_m}(X)\over Q_I},
 \qquad
 R_m^+={G_{D,L_m}(X)\over Q_I},                                      \tag{32}
\]

and

\[
 A_m^-=G_{d_m,L_m}(Y),
 \qquad A_m^+=G_{D,L_m}(Y),
 \qquad c_m={g_m\over Q_I}.                                          \tag{33}
\]

Since adding \(\mu(n)\) times the first determinant column to the second does
not change the determinant, (11), (23), and (31) give

\[
 \boxed{
 H_m={A_m^-R_m^+-A_m^+R_m^-\over c_m}.}                              \tag{34}
\]

This is the clean arithmetic residual.  At a target prime, both \(A_m^-\) and
\(A_m^+\) vanish modulo \(p\), while \(c_m\) is a \(p\)-unit.  At a non-target
prime, (34) is a genuine cross-product condition.  The current gauge script
provides no Bezout relation among the four integer sequences
\(A_m^\pm,R_m^\pm\) as \(m\) varies.

## 5. The exact Smith certificate that would prove the desired bound

Let

\[
 A=A_{D,N}=G_{D-1,N}(Y).                                             \tag{35}
\]

By Lemma 65.1,

\[
 \gcd(A,Q_I)=R_I.                                                     \tag{36}
\]

The following elementary proposition is the correct target for an Ore/Sylvester
calculation.

### Proposition 2 (two-row Smith certificate).

Suppose there are integer coefficients \(\lambda_{i,m}\) and an integer matrix

\[
 C_{n,D,N}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{11}&c_{12}\\c_{21}&c_{22}\end{pmatrix}  \tag{37}
\]

such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \sum_m\lambda_{1,m}H_m\\[1mm]
  \sum_m\lambda_{2,m}H_m
 \end{pmatrix}
 =C_{n,D,N}\begin{pmatrix}A\\Q_I\end{pmatrix}.}                     \tag{38}
\]

Put \(\Delta_{n,D,N}=\det C_{n,D,N}\ne0\).  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 {K_I\over R_I}\mid\Delta_{n,D,N}.}                                 \tag{39}
\]

**Proof.**  Let \(k=K_I\).  Since \(k\) divides every \(H_m\), it divides both
entries on the left of (38).  Multiplying (38) by the adjugate of \(C\) gives

\[
 k\mid\Delta_{n,D,N}A,
 \qquad
 k\mid\Delta_{n,D,N}Q_I.                                            \tag{40}
\]

For every prime \(\ell\),

\[
 v_\ell(k)
 \le v_\ell(\Delta_{n,D,N})+
     \min\{v_\ell(A),v_\ell(Q_I)\}
 =v_\ell(\Delta_{n,D,N})+v_\ell(R_I).                               \tag{41}
\]

Equation (1) says \(R_I\mid k\), and (39) follows prime by prime.  \(\square\)

Therefore an exact certificate with

\[
 \Delta_{n,D,N}\mid C_0\prod_{|j|\le6}(n+j)^{C_1}                  \tag{42}
\]

would prove the claim immediately.  More generally, any nonzero fixed-degree
polynomial \(\Delta(n,D,N)\) with \(D,N=O(n)\) has logarithmic height
\(O(\log n)\), which is sufficient for the block argument.

This is the right meaning of an “Ore/Sylvester certificate” here: it must output
the two rows \((\lambda_{i,m})\), the four entries of \(C\), and an exact
factorization of \(\det C\).  A list of evaluated gcds does not supply (38).

## 6. A concrete arbitrary-prime obstruction to the presently certified class

The following counterexample is at the exact Newton/beta-kernel level.  It is
not a generic CRT slogan.

### Proposition 3 (same-core alias does not control the normalized determinant).

Fix \(D,N\), any admissible margin set containing \(m=2\), and an arbitrary
integer \(\mu\).  For every prime \(\ell\), there are integer arrays \(Y,W\)
such that

1. \(Y_{p-1}=1\) and \(W_{p-1}=\mu Y_{p-1}\) for every core prime \(p\), so
   there are no targets and the exact core alias holds;
2. every beta--Padé and Newton identity in Sections 54--56 and 67 holds;
3. \(\gcd_mH_m=\ell\).

**Construction.**  Take

\[
 Y_e=1\quad\text{for all }e,
 \qquad
 W_e=\mu+\ell\,\mathbf1_{e=D-1}.                                    \tag{43}
\]

The perturbation is supported at the single node immediately to the left of the
core, so condition 1 is exact, not merely a congruence.

Every Newton row has coefficient sum one, hence both left and right \(Y\)-values
are one.  The right stencil starts at \(D\), so it never sees the perturbation.
The coefficient of \(W_{D-1}\) in the left row is

\[
 a_m=(-1)^{m-2}
 \binom{D-1}{m-2}\binom{D+N}{N}.                                    \tag{44}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \det(u_m,v_m)=-\ell a_m.                                            \tag{45}
\]

The coefficientwise identity (17) shows \(g_m\mid a_m\), so

\[
 H_m=-\ell\,{a_m\over g_m}.                                         \tag{46}
\]

For \(m=2\),

\[
 a_2=g_2=\binom{D+N}{N},
 \qquad H_2=-\ell.                                                    \tag{47}
\]

Thus every \(H_m\) is divisible by \(\ell\), while one of them is exactly
\(-\ell\); hence \(\gcd_mH_m=\ell\).  Since \(\ell\) is arbitrary, no
fixed polynomial in \(n\) can bound the nuisance gcd in this class.  \(\square\)

This proposition has two consequences.

* The beta--Padé identities, same-radius Smith saturation, and the universal
  point alias cannot prove (2), even when used simultaneously and for all
  margins.
* Any successful proof must use the exact distinguished Laurent coefficient
  state, not only the facts that the two arrays agree projectively at core prime
  nodes.

The counterexample does **not** assert that the actual arrays (5)--(6) behave
this way.  It identifies exactly what a proof must add.

## 7. Why the certified order-four origin operator does not yet add that input

The operator in `q32_doubled_period_gauge_audit.sage` acts in the **moment
index**.  Its theorem-level content is the scalar quotient

\[
 {\cal U}(J)=\mu b,
 \qquad {\cal U}(b)=0.                                                \tag{48}
\]

The margin family acts in the **shell-node index** and changes both the left
endpoint and the stencil length.  Moving (48) through a margin is precisely the
unproved step.  What is certified is only the prime-node congruence

\[
 G(W)\equiv\mu(n)G(Y)\pmod p                                         \tag{49}
\]

and, blockwise,

\[
 Q_I\mid G(W)-\mu(n)G(Y).                                             \tag{50}
\]

The quotient in (50) is the uncontrolled pair \(R_m^\pm\) in (32).  Equation
(48) gives no characteristic-zero recurrence or Bezout identity for that
quotient as \(m\) varies.

The second requested script makes the category mismatch explicit.
`q32_first_cell_ray_telescopers.sage` supplies fourteen separate horizontal ray
operators, of total presented order 38, and verifies pairwise unit greatest
common right divisors.  It does not supply a common scalar horizontal operator
for the complete actual shell, and it does not supply a gauge taking the
order-four moment operator to the margin direction.  In the formal direct-sum
ray presentation, the scalar doubled-period quotient sees only a very small
projection; a large kernel remains.  Clearing denominators and inserting an
arbitrary prime in that kernel preserves all scalar relations while changing
margin determinants.  Proposition 3 is the elementary carrier-level shadow of
this formal kernel.

Thus the first exact obstruction is:

> **Vertical quotient data do not control horizontal state content.**  The
> missing theorem is an actual-state horizontal observability/Smith identity,
> not another factorization of the scalar doubled-period operator.

This is why multiplying Ore operators, taking their ordinary scalar resultant,
or quoting the order-four origin cancellation is not yet a proof of (2).

## 8. The exact computation that would settle the actual-array question

A valid positive certificate can be produced by the following finite exact
procedure.  This is not numerical fitting.

1. For each of the fourteen ray classes, augment the proper-hypergeometric ideal
   used in `q32_first_cell_ray_telescopers.sage` with the moment shift \(S_M\).
   The summand ratios give an exact Ore ideal in
   \(\mathbb Q(M,r,t)\langle S_M,S_r,S_t\rangle\).
2. Reduce the actual arrays (5)--(6), including all five \(u_j(n)\), in the
   direct sum of these ray modules.  Keep the origin coordinate separately and
   verify its cancellation using \({\cal U}(b)=0\).
3. Encode the two beta row polynomials \(P_{d_m,L_m}\) and \(P_{D,L_m}\) as
   exact observation rows.  Use (15)--(18) before any denominator clearing; this
   automatically removes the universal Pascal factor.
4. In the exterior observation module, solve for two syzygy rows giving (38).
   Reduction must be checked against every ray ideal and against the origin
   coordinate.  This is the place where the distinguished initial state, rather
   than the free direct-sum module, must enter.
5. Clear denominators, divide common integer content, and verify the two
   equalities in (38) by exact Ore remainder computations.
6. Factor \(\det C\).  If its irreducible factors are supported on a fixed
   polynomial such as the observed \(\prod_{|j|\le6}(n+j)\), Proposition 2 is
   the proof.

The denominator audit in the existing gauge script is useful at step 5, but it
cannot replace step 4.  Fixed-degree gauge denominators already have
\(O(\log n)\) height; the issue is the numerator/state Fitting ideal.

A machine-checkable certificate should store only

* the two finite syzygy rows \(\lambda_{i,m}\);
* the four polynomial entries of \(C\);
* the cleared common denominator and integer content; and
* the factorization of \(\det C\).

A verifier then recomputes the fourteen Ore remainders and the two matrix
identities.  No shell values of hundreds or thousands of bits are needed in the
certificate.

## 10. Final audited conclusion

The new determinant family is a genuine advance.  The exact theorem currently
available is

\[
 \boxed{
 R_I\mid\gcd_mH_m,
 \qquad
 H_m=-\det(\Phi(P_{d_m,L_m}),\Phi(K_m)),}
\]

with \(K_m\) the primitive beta curvature kernel (16).  The actual computations
show an exceptional collapse consistent with an \(O(\log n)\)-height Smith
determinant.

There is, however, no certified Bezout/Smith identity in the present repo that
implies that collapse.  Proposition 3 proves that beta--Padé, same-radius
saturation, and universal doubled-period aliasing cannot imply it.  The
order-four scalar gauge does not repair the gap because it is vertical, whereas
the uncontrolled quotient is horizontal.

The route closes the large-prime block exactly when the actual ray-state
calculation produces (38).  Proposition 2 then turns the factorization of one
fixed-size determinant into the desired bound with no analytic or probabilistic
input.
