# Q6804: digit/sieve formulation of TOP

## (i) Digit criterion and reduction

The digit statement is correct provided the stated Gessel/Lucas congruence is used in its full form:

b_{mp+r} = b_m b_r mod p, 0 <= r < p.

Writing n in base p,

n=d_0+d_1 p+...+d_k p^k,

iteration gives

b_n = product_i b_{d_i} mod p.

Therefore

p | b_n iff some digit d_i satisfies p | b_{d_i}.

Thus the exact digit sieve is:

p | b_n <=> digits_p(n) intersect Z_p is nonempty.

For p in (n/2,n], n=1*p+(n-p), so the only nontrivial digit is n-p and b_1=5. Hence, except for p=5 which is outside the large top window,

p | b_n <=> n-p in Z_p.

Therefore TOP is exactly

#{p in (n/2,n] : n-p in Z_p}=o(n/log n).

The middle range companion is

M(n)=sum_{sqrt(n)<p<=n/2} log p |R_p(n)|,

where

R_p(n)={r<n/2:r=n mod p, p|b_r}.

For a fixed p, the possible r are an arithmetic progression of step p, so the number of possible residues is bounded by

|R_p(n)| <= 1+n/(2p).

The remaining issue is the weighted sum over p in the middle range.

## (ii) Which sieve framework applies?

The top condition has the form

p in (n/2,n], n-p in Z_p.

The sampled point is affine in p, not a random residue. The target family Z_p changes with p.

Classical Erdos/Lehmer problems usually have a fixed target set A and ask for p with n mod p in A. Here the set itself is arithmetic and prime-dependent. The standard sieve does not directly apply.

The shifted-prime formulation is closer:

p | b_{n-p}.

However, this is not a fixed polynomial F(n-p). The Apery sequence modulo p is a varying characteristic-p object. There is no single integer polynomial whose prime divisibility captures the event.

A large sieve would require a compatible description of Z_p. Typical sufficient inputs would be one of:

1. Frobenius description: Z_p is a union of residue conditions arising from a fixed algebraic Galois representation, with effective Chebotarev and enough independence in p.

2. Orthogonality description: an expansion

1_{r in Z_p}=sum_chi a_p(chi) chi(r)

with coefficients satisfying uniform average bounds.

3. Bilinear cancellation:

sum_{p~X} sum_{r in Z_p} alpha_p beta_r

has square-root type cancellation for structured beta_r.

The current inputs do not provide any of these. The known facts

|Z_p|<=3p^(2/3)

and mean |Z_p| approximately 1

are only marginal information. They prove density-one statements after averaging over n, but they do not control the correlated sampling r=n-p.

The modular/Galois information about the Apery orbit is a plausible route, but the needed output is not merely Frobenius statistics of individual p. It needs a two-variable theorem controlling the pair (p,n-p).

## (iii) Hostile obstruction check

A combinatorial obstruction is possible at the level of currently listed axioms.

One can imagine modifying the sparse sets Z_p while preserving:

- average size about 1,
- reflection symmetry,
- Lucas-style digit recursion,
- local distribution statistics,

but forcing for infinitely many special n that

n-p belongs to Z_p

for a positive fraction of primes p in (n/2,n].

The reason is that TOP is a diagonal sampling problem. Average sparsity does not prevent a sparse family from concentrating on the affine diagonals r=n-p.

Therefore a proof cannot rely only on Poisson statistics or mean |Z_p|.

The Apery-specific input that could kill this construction would have to be genuinely cross-prime: a rigidity theorem saying that the moving family of zero sets cannot align with the affine clock p -> n-p. Current squarefreeness, small fiber bounds, and fixed-p monodromy do not obviously provide that.

Unlike the padded energy construction, the obstruction here is not large fibers inside one p. It is an adversarial correlation across infinitely many different primes.

## (iv) Sharpest proved partial result

Averaging over n gives the available theorem.

For n in an interval [X,2X], the contribution of top-window primes is

sum_n #{p in (n/2,n] : n-p in Z_p}.

For fixed p, the number of n in the range with n-p in Z_p is at most roughly X/p times |Z_p| (plus boundary terms). Hence

average count <= sum_{p~X} |Z_p|/p.

Using the known average |Z_p|=O(1) gives

sum_{n<=X} TOP(n) << X/log X.

Consequently

TOP(n)<< n/log n

for density-one n, with an exceptional set controlled by Markov. More generally, for any A tending to infinity,

#{n<=X:TOP(n)>A n/log n} << X/A.

The same argument with weights gives the corresponding log-p weighted statement.

This is the sharp unconditional output from marginal Z_p information.

The exact remaining gap is:

prove a quenched diagonal anti-concentration estimate

#{p in (n/2,n] : n-p in Z_p}=o(n/log n)

for every n.

Equivalently: rule out arithmetic alignment between the affine residue clock n-p and the prime-indexed Apery zero sets.

Least confident step:

The hypothetical obstruction argument is not a construction inside the genuine Apery system; it only shows that the listed coarse axioms are insufficient. A hidden geometric rigidity theorem could still exist.

Blind spot in the question:

The formulation may underestimate the gap between small random-looking fibers and diagonal control. TOP is not a small-set problem; it is a moving-target correlation problem. The missing theorem is a joint (p,r) distribution theorem for the Apery zero locus, not a sharper bound on |Z_p| alone.
