# Fixed-diagonal thresholds for the reflected Apéry zero sets

## Executive verdict

There are three different statements here, and they should not be conflated.

1. The measured one-prime facts alone imply no nontrivial deterministic bound. Knowing that the mean of (|Z_p|) is about one and that the zeros occur in reflection pairs with pair count close to (operatorname{Poisson}(1/2)) does not control a fixed column (n). One can preserve those marginals and place one reflected pair through a chosen diagonal for every prime whose pair count is nonzero. This gives (|T_n|asymp n/log n) and logarithmic weight (asymp n).

1. Under the natural extra random-position and cross-prime-independence model, an absolute bound three is not asymptotically credible. For fixed (n),

1. For the actual pointwise theorem, the weakest clean correlation order is two, not four. A second falling-factorial collision estimate on each fixed quotient arc already excludes a pointwise spike. The fourth moment appears only if one insists on deriving the result from the currently proved vertical bound (|Z_p|ll p^{2/3}) through a relative small-CRT estimate of the form (S_kll Y A_X^k). In that framework (k=4) is the first integer with (2/3+1/k<1).

The precise missing arithmetic input is therefore a horizontal non-concentration theorem for the actual Apéry zero positions across varying primes: equivalently, a power-saving additive-energy estimate for the affine sets (q p+Z_p), or averaged cancellation for the associated inverse-twisted zero-set Fourier sums. Reflection, row sparsity, same-prime continuants, fixed-gap resultants, and generic interpolation determinants do not supply this input.

A sufficient estimate is

[

quadtext{for every dyadic }N.}tag{HC2-1}

This is the first nontrivial falling-factorial order. A first moment cannot exclude one column carrying all incidences. More generally, for any fixed (kge2),

[

]

is sufficient, but order two is the weakest correlation order.

The natural Poisson prediction is vastly stronger:

[

]

[

The magnitude in (HC2-1) is tuned to the pointwise obstruction: a single diagonal with (cN/log N) hits contributes (asymp c^2N^2/log^2N) to the pair energy. The condition is stronger than logically necessary if many medium-sized columns exist, but it is the weakest clean untruncated second-moment statement that rules out every linear-weight spike.

## 10. What a determinant method would actually have to prove

A collision at fixed (q) is

[

qp+r=qell+s,qquad rin Z_p, sin Z_ell.

]

Same-prime continuants and Casoratians control relations among two or more roots inside one (Z_p). They do not constrain a “rainbow” pileup using one fresh root from each of many primes.

A successful determinant method would need one of the following genuinely new features:

- a bounded-complexity characteristic-zero relation satisfied by every actual pair ((p,r)) with (pmid b_r), strong enough that many collisions force a low-height integer determinant to vanish;

- a recurrence-derived determinant whose prime divisibility records the marked root (r=n-qp), while its archimedean height is (exp(o(n)));

- a rank or codimension estimate for the actual Apéry orbit modulo the product of many distinct primes, not merely for arbitrary interpolation data.

The repository's exact no-go results explain why generic determinant technology is insufficient:

- reflected sparse sets can be CRT-planted at one column;

- fixed-degree Padé numerators retain a positive Poisson tail of high-zero primes as automatic content pollution;

- a growing Padé family can purify the support, but no sublinear height bound is known;

- ordinary resultants forget the marked moving root;

- the marked subresultant chain contracts to the original two-entry Fitting ideal;

- hit-adaptive modular Siegel lattices collapse to divisibility of the single evaluation at (n).

Thus “apply a determinant method” is not yet an arithmetic input. The missing theorem must be Apéry-recurrence-specific and cross-prime.

## 11. Precisely what is missing about the actual Apéry zero positions

The absent ingredient can be stated in any of the following equivalent-strength forms.

### Horizontal additive non-concentration

For every fixed (q), the affine sets

[

q p+Z_p

]

have pair energy (o(N^2/log^2N)) in every dyadic shell, preferably (N^{1+o(1)}).

### Small-CRT dispersion

For distinct varying primes, tuples of actual zero residues have the expected number, or merely a power-saving number, of representatives in intervals much shorter than their product modulus.

### Inverse-twisted Fourier cancellation

The bilinear sums built from

[

E_p(hbarell)E_ell(hbar p)

]

exhibit cancellation on average over (p,ell,h).

### A recurrence-specific cross-modulus certificate

Many congruences

[

pmid b_{n-qp}

]

force a nonzero characteristic-zero integer of height (exp(o(n))) to be divisible by their product.

What is currently available does not imply any of these:

- (|Z_p|ll p^{2/3}) is vertical and permits isolated roots;

- mean (|Z_p|approx1) is empirical, not a theorem;

- reflection controls two residues in one row, not alignment across rows;

- fixed-gap continuants control same-prime pairs, not one marked root per prime;

- fixed-field Chebotarev has the wrong quantifier order;

- standard (ell)-adic weight estimates do not directly control a mod-(p) unit-root coefficient vanishing in its own characteristic;

- no bounded-degree algebraic parametrization of the moving zero positions is known.

That last bridge—from the Apéry recurrence or unit-root crystal to horizontal cancellation across changing characteristics—is the exact arithmetic input missing from the project.

## 12. Average, density-one, and pointwise statements

The quantifiers should be kept explicit.

- A first-moment estimate for (sum_nK_q(n)) gives an average or density-one theorem by Markov. It permits one exceptional column with (asymp N/log N) hits.

- Empirical variance-to-mean near one is evidence for Poisson behavior, but it does not become a pointwise theorem until a deterministic shell moment bound is proved for the actual sets.

- An estimate holding for almost all shells can still miss an infinite exceptional subsequence. (HC2-q) must hold for every sufficiently large dyadic shell.

- The pair-energy estimate upgrades to pointwise because one bad column alone contributes the square of its occupancy.

Finally, the full Problem 3.2 ledger is:

1. primes (plesqrt n): (O(sqrt n)) logarithmic weight by the Wronskian bound;

1. companion (a_q)-channel for (p>sqrt n): already (O(n^{2/3}log n)=o(n)) pointwise in the repository;

1. lower (b_r)-channel: follows from (HC2-q) for every fixed (q), or from the uniform truncated version;

1. large-prime valuations: uniformly bounded by the Wronskian support analysis, so radical control suffices up to an absolute factor.

## Final assessment

The observed bound three is a finite-range manifestation of an inhomogeneous rare-event model, not evidence for a plausible absolute cap. The random prediction is a slowly diverging maximum, with leading order (frac12log N/loglog N).

For a proof, the cleanest minimal target is not a uniform bound on (|Z_p|). It is the fixed-quotient horizontal pair estimate (HC2-q). A theorem as weak as

[

mathcal E_q(N)ll_q N^{2-delta_q}

]

for every fixed (q) would finish the lower channel and hence Problem 3.2. If one works only with the existing (p^{2/3}) zero-fiber bound, then the repository's four-prime small-CRT estimate (D4) is the first relative moment that closes.

The research frontier is therefore sharply located: prove power-saving cross-prime non-concentration for the marked Apéry zero positions, most naturally through inverse-twisted dispersion or an Apéry-specific low-height cross-modulus determinant. No current result in the repository supplies that horizontal input.
