# Adjacent top-half Apéry cutoffs: exact primitive saturation

## Verdict

No new primitive short common carrier is obtained from the stated cutoff data or from adjoining the Apéry three-term recurrence.

There are two load-bearing conclusions.

1. The premise that \(E/p^2\) has logarithmic height \(O((\ell-p)\log n)\) is false. The exact factorization is
   \[
   E=\phi_{M+1}(n(n+1))^2Q_d(n(n+1)),
   \]
   so
   \[
   \frac{E}{p^2}
   =\left(\frac{\phi_{M+1}(n(n+1))}{p}\right)^2Q_d(n(n+1)).
   \]
   The factor \(Q_d\) has reduced rational height \(O(d\log n)\), but \(E/p^2\) itself still has exponential height \(\exp(\Omega(n))\).

2. After the long prefix coordinate is eliminated, the target at \(\ell\) imposes **no algebraic condition at all** on the boundary coordinate. Precisely, the cutoff-target lattice is
   \[
   (S_M,E,S_L)
   =(p\ell t-p^2c,\ p^2c,\ p\ell t),
   \qquad t,c\in\mathbf Z.
   \]
   Its quotient by the long direction \((p\ell,0,p\ell)\) is generated by
   \((-p^2,p^2,0)\), hence has only \(p\)-primary determinantal content. After division by the universal \(p^2\), it has no target-prime content at all.

Consequently, in the clearly defined class consisting of finite linear cutoff-recurrence operations, operandwise-certified gcds, and resultants/subresultants of those linear recurrence equations, followed by primitive saturation of coefficient content and universal boundary factors, the following dichotomy is exact:

> A formally forced \(\ell\)-factor either remains attached to the long carrier \(S_L\) (or to the full Apéry value \(b_n\)), which has exponential height, or it was inserted explicitly in a coefficient. Eliminating the long coordinate removes all primitive \(\ell\)-selectivity.

The pair \((n,p,\ell)=(321,193,211)\) exhibits the second alternative sharply:
\[
E\equiv125\pmod{211},\qquad
E/p^2\equiv87\pmod{211},\qquad
Q_9(n(n+1))\equiv148\pmod{211}.
\]
Thus \(211\) divides none of the normalized boundary quantities. The target equation is the cancellation
\[
S_M\equiv86,\qquad E\equiv125,\qquad S_M+E\equiv0\pmod{211}.
\]

This is a rigorous no-go for the carrier class above. It does not exclude a genuinely new identity involving the distinguished prefix through a growing-order or otherwise non-recurrence algebraic relation.

## 2. Exact two-coordinate form of every bounded cutoff relation

Put
\[
w=\nu_{M+1}=a_{M+1}^2.
\]
For \(j\ge1\), define
\[
r_1=1,
\qquad
r_j=\prod_{t=1}^{j-1}
\frac{(X-(M+t)(M+t+1))^2}{(M+t+1)^4}
\quad(j\ge2),
\tag{2.1}
\]
and
\[
Q_j=\sum_{i=1}^j r_i.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Then (1.2) gives the exact identities
\[
\nu_{M+j}=wr_j,
\qquad
S_{M+j}=S_M+wQ_j.
\tag{2.3}
\]
In particular,
\[
E=wQ_d,
\qquad
S_L=S_M+wQ_d.
\tag{2.4}
\]
This is exactly Section 36's factorization, because
\[
w=\phi_{M+1}(X)^2.
\]

Let
\[
D=\prod_{k=M+1}^{L}k,
\qquad
\mathscr R=\mathbf Z[1/D].
\]
Both \(p\) and \(\ell\) are units with respect to the localization denominator. Equation (2.3) proves:

### Proposition 2.1 — cutoff module

All values obtainable from the interval \(M,\ldots,L\) by finite linear use of the summand recurrence lie in the free rank-two \(\mathscr R\)-module
\[
\mathscr R S_M\oplus\mathscr R w.
\tag{2.5}
\]
Every intermediate prefix has coefficient one on \(S_M\). Thus the actual initial state enters this local module as one additive integration constant; the recurrence never supplies a second equation determining that constant from \(w\).

This is an identity for the actual Apéry summands, not an arbitrary recurrence countermodel.

## 3. Height correction: \(E/p^2\) is not short

The short rational object in Section 36 is
\[
Q_d(X),
\qquad
h(Q_d(X))=O(d\log n).
\tag{3.1}
\]
It is not \(E/p^2\).

Indeed, \(p\mid a_{M+1}\), so \(p^2\mid w\), and
\[
\frac{E}{p^2}
=\left(\frac{a_{M+1}}p\right)^2Q_d(X).
\tag{3.2}
\]
The first factor is exponentially large.

A top-half target prime \(p>5\) cannot equal \(n\): Lucas gives
\(b_p\equiv b_1=5\pmod p\). Hence
\[
m:=M+1=\frac{p+1}{2}\le\frac n2.
\]
Also \(m>n/4\). Since \(n\ge2m\), termwise products give
\[
\binom nm
=\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{n-m+j}{j}\ge2^m,
\tag{3.3}
\]
\[
\binom{n+m}m
=\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{n+j}{j}\ge3^m.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Therefore
\[
a_m\ge6^m,
\qquad
\nu_m\ge36^m.
\tag{3.5}
\]
All summands are positive, so
\[
\boxed{
\log(E/p^2)
\ge m\log36-2\log p
\ge\frac n4\log36-O(\log n).}
\tag{3.6}
\]
Thus \(E/p^2\) has linear logarithmic height. Dividing only by \(p^2\) does not perform the primitive boundary normalization. The low-height coordinate is the reduced rational value \(Q_d(X)\), after the entire boundary square \(a_{M+1}^2\) has been removed.

## 4. Exact target lattice and its Smith saturation

Set
\[
X_0=S_M,
\qquad
Y_0=E,
\qquad
Z_0=S_L.
\]
Then
\[
Z_0=X_0+Y_0,
\qquad
p\mid X_0,
\qquad
p^2\mid Y_0,
\qquad
\ell\mid Z_0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact cutoff-target lattice

For distinct primes \(p,\ell\), the integer solutions of (4.1) are exactly
\[
\boxed{
(X_0,Y_0,Z_0)
=(p\ell t-p^2c,\ p^2c,\ p\ell t),
\qquad t,c\in\mathbf Z.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
The representation is unique.

#### Proof

Write
\[
X_0=pa,
\qquad
Y_0=p^2c,
\qquad
Z_0=\ell b.
\]
The relation \(Z_0=X_0+Y_0\) gives
\[
\ell b=p(a+pc).
\]
Since \((p,\ell)=1\), one has \(p\mid b\), say \(b=pt\). Then
\[
Z_0=p\ell t,
\qquad
X_0=Z_0-Y_0=p\ell t-p^2c.
\]
The converse is immediate. ∎

Therefore the lattice has basis
\[
v_{\rm long}=(p\ell,0,p\ell),
\qquad
v_{\rm bdry}=(-p^2,p^2,0).
\tag{4.3}
\]
The first basis vector is the integration-constant direction; it contains the entire \(\ell\)-selectivity. The quotient by it is
\[
\Lambda_{p,\ell}/\mathbf Z v_{\rm long}
\cong\mathbf Z v_{\rm bdry}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Every determinantal/Fitting divisor of the inclusion of this quotient into the boundary coordinates is supported on \(p\). After dividing by the universal \(p^2\), the generator is \((-1,1,0)\), with no \(p\)- or \(\ell\)-torsion.

This is the exact Smith-form content of the saturation: **eliminating the long direction eliminates every primitive \(\ell\)-factor.**

## 5. Classification of all primitive linear carriers

Let
\[
F=\alpha S_M+\beta E+\gamma S_L,
\qquad
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Substituting (4.2) gives
\[
F=p\ell(\alpha+\gamma)t+p^2(\beta-\alpha)c.
\tag{5.2}
\]
The factor \(p\) is automatic. Universal divisibility by \(\ell\), for every point of the target lattice, is equivalent to
\[
\ell\mid\beta-\alpha.
\tag{5.3}
\]
When \(\beta-\alpha=\ell k\), one has the exact decomposition
\[
\boxed{
F=(\alpha+\gamma)S_L+\ell kE.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This proves the linear no-go.

- The first term retains the long selective carrier \(S_L\).
- The second term receives its \(\ell\)-factor directly from the coefficient \(\ell k\), not from the target conditions.
- If one symbolically eliminates the long coordinate by imposing \(\alpha+\gamma=0\), then after primitive coefficient saturation the remaining cross factor is zero; the unsaturated expression is merely \(\ell kE\).

Equivalently, use the normalized integer \(C=E/p^2\). A linear expression
\[
A S_M+B C
\]
is universally divisible by \(\ell\) exactly when
\[
B\equiv Ap^2\pmod\ell,
\]
and then
\[
A S_M+B C
=A S_L+\ell K C.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Again, the only target-derived \(\ell\)-factor is carried by \(S_L\).

## 6. Elimination ideal: no resultant can create a boundary selector

The preceding lattice statement has a stronger polynomial form.

Work over the coefficient ring \(\mathscr R\) from Section 2 and reduce modulo \(\ell\). Let
\[
s=S_M,
\qquad z=w
\]
be independent coordinates. The exact cutoff graph is
\[
S_L=s+Q_dz.
\tag{6.1}
\]
The \(\ell\)-target ideal is therefore
\[
I_\ell=(s+Q_dz)
\subset(\mathscr R/\ell\mathscr R)[s,z].
\tag{6.2}
\]
Since the coefficient of \(s\) is one,
\[
(\mathscr R/\ell\mathscr R)[s,z]/I_\ell
\cong(\mathscr R/\ell\mathscr R)[z].
\tag{6.3}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
I_\ell\cap(\mathscr R/\ell\mathscr R)[z]=(0).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus **every** boundary value \(z\) is compatible with the \(\ell\)-target: the equation merely chooses
\[
s=-Q_dz.
\]
The \(p\)-target does not change this projection. Introduce the quotient variable \(a\) by \(s=pa\). Since \(p\) is a unit modulo \(\ell\),
\[
(\mathscr R/\ell\mathscr R)[s,z,a]
/(s-pa,\ s+Q_dz)
\cong(\mathscr R/\ell\mathscr R)[z].
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Corollary 6.1 — resultant/subresultant no-go

Take any finite family of polynomial equations obtained from:

1. the exact cutoff recurrence (1.1)–(2.3);
2. the equations \(p\mid S_M\), \(p^2\mid E\), and \(\ell\mid S_L\), represented with quotient variables;
3. finite linear combinations over \(\mathscr R\);
4. elimination by ordinary resultants, subresultants, determinants, or Fitting minors.

After eliminating the integration-constant and quotient variables, any polynomial in the boundary coordinate whose \(\ell\)-vanishing is a formal consequence is either:

- the zero polynomial; or
- divisible by \(\ell\) in its coefficient content.

After primitive coefficient saturation, no nonzero \(\ell\)-selector remains.

This includes every bounded collection of intermediate prefixes, because all of them have the form
\[
S_{M+j}=s+Q_jz.
\]
Adding more such rows produces universal linear relations among the \(Q_j\), but it does not give a second equation in \(s\) at characteristic \(\ell\).

## 7. Exact gcd saturation

The three raw integers satisfy
\[
\gcd(S_M,S_L)
=\gcd(S_M,E)
=\gcd(S_L,E).
\tag{7.1}
\]
Because \(\ell\mid S_L\) and \(p\) is a unit modulo \(\ell\),
\[
\ell\mid E
\iff\ell\mid E/p^2
\iff\ell\mid S_M.
\tag{7.2}
\]
None of these conditions follows from the two target equations.

More generally, let \(H\) be a boundary-only integer obtained from the cutoff recurrence after clearing only denominators that are units at \(p\ell\), and suppose its coefficients have no explicit \(\ell\)-content. If \(\ell\mid H\) were a formal consequence of the target equations, its reduction would define a nonzero element of the elimination ideal in (6.4), which is impossible.

Therefore a gcd such as
\[
\gcd(S_L,H)
\]
has no formally guaranteed \(\ell\)-factor unless:

- \(H\) still contains the symbolic long factor \(S_L\); or
- an \(\ell\)-factor was inserted in the coefficients of \(H\).

For a finite family of operands, a prime is guaranteed to divide their gcd only if every operand lies in the corresponding target ideal. Modulo primitive coefficient content, that ideal is generated by the long form \(S_L\). Hence an operandwise-certified gcd either retains the long carrier or loses the primitive \(\ell\)-factor.

This statement is about guaranteed support. It does not claim that accidental numerical gcds of particular huge integers cannot be small; proving systematic accidental cancellation would itself be a new Apéry-specific theorem outside the present carrier class.

## 8. Why the Apéry three-term recurrence adds no cross equation

The full Apéry values satisfy
\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}-P(n)b_n+n^3b_{n-1}=0,
\tag{8.1}
\]
where
\[
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\]
For any top-half target prime \(q>5\), one has
\[
q\nmid n(n+1).
\tag{8.2}
\]
Indeed:

- If \(q\mid n\), then \(n=q\) because \(q>n/2\); but Lucas gives \(b_q\equiv5\pmod q\).
- If \(q\mid n+1\), then the only possible top-half case is \(n=2q-1\); Lucas and reflection give
  \[
  b_{2q-1}\equiv b_1b_{q-1}\equiv5\pmod q.
  \]

Thus the recurrence transfer matrix is in \({\rm GL}_2(\mathbf F_q)\) at both \(q=p\) and \(q=\ell\). After imposing \(b_n=0\pmod q\), equation (8.1) determines one neighboring coordinate from the other:
\[
(n+1)^3b_{n+1}=-n^3b_{n-1}\pmod q.
\tag{8.3}
\]
There remains one free state coordinate. Algebraically,
\[
\mathbf F_q[b_{n-1},b_n,b_{n+1}]
/(b_n,(n+1)^3b_{n+1}+n^3b_{n-1})
\cong\mathbf F_q[b_{n-1}].
\tag{8.4}
\]
Hence elimination of the neighboring state gives the zero ideal. The recurrence supplies no second scalar condition at either target characteristic.

Across \(p\) and \(\ell\), these are two independent fibers. Their only existing common integer is \(b_n\) itself, which has
\[
\log b_n=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n).
\]
Any recurrence resultant that removes \(b_n\) also removes the only common target equation; what remains is a unit transfer determinant or a universal recurrence identity.

The actual initial state \((b_0,b_1)=(1,5)\) determines the numerical value of the free state, but using that value exactly reconstructs \(b_n\) or the long prefix. It does not create a bounded-gap algebraic relation on \(Q_d\).

Modulo \(193\),

For every \(90\le m\le96\), the base-\(193\) low digit of \(n+m\) is

Lucas therefore gives

This is a degenerate same-characteristic boundary hit, not a new pure-cross carrier.

### 9.2 Pure-cross comparison \((p,\ell)=(193,211)\)

Now
\[
M=96,
\qquad
L=105,
\qquad
d=9.
\]
Modulo \(211\),
\[
n\equiv110.
\]
Put
\[
A_m=\binom nm\binom{n+m}{m}\pmod{211}.
\]
For \(m\le100\), Lucas gives
\[
A_m\equiv\binom{110}{m}\binom{110+m}{m}\pmod{211}.
\]
For \(101\le m\le105\), the second binomial vanishes modulo \(211\).

The nonzero boundary terms are computed exactly from
\[
\frac{A_{m+1}}{A_m}
=\frac{(110-m)(111+m)}{(m+1)^2}\pmod{211},
\tag{9.1}
\]
starting with
\[
A_{100}
=\binom{110}{10}\binom{210}{100}
\equiv79.
\]
The exact table is

| \(m\) | \(A_m\pmod{211}\) | \(\nu_m=A_m^2\pmod{211}\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 97 | 109 | 65 |
| 98 | 172 | 44 |
| 99 | 152 | 105 |
| 100 | 79 | 122 |
| 101–105 | 0 | 0 |

Therefore
\[
E=\sum_{m=97}^{105}\nu_m
\equiv65+44+105+122
\equiv125\pmod{211}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Also
\[
193^2\equiv113\pmod{211},
\qquad
113^{-1}\equiv183\pmod{211},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{E/193^2\equiv125\cdot183\equiv87\pmod{211}.}
\tag{9.3}
\]
In particular,
\[
211\nmid E/193^2.
\]

The primitive boundary factor also fails. Since
\[
\phi_{97}(n(n+1))\equiv A_{97}\equiv109,
\qquad
109^2\equiv65,
\]
and \(65^{-1}\equiv13\pmod{211}\), equation \(E=\phi_{97}^2Q_9\) gives
\[
\boxed{Q_9(n(n+1))\equiv125\cdot13\equiv148\pmod{211}.}
\tag{9.4}
\]
Thus \(211\) divides neither the literal quotient \(E/p^2\) nor the genuinely short rational boundary factor \(Q_9\).

Finally, because \(211\mid S_L\),
\[
S_M\equiv-E\equiv86\pmod{211}.
\tag{9.5}
\]
The target is exactly a cancellation between two nonzero quantities:
\[
86+125=211.
\]
This is the concrete local model of the elimination theorem (6.4).

## 10. Precise scope of the no-go

Define the **primitive cutoff-recurrence carrier class** to consist of expressions obtained as follows:

1. start with the actual values \(S_M,S_{M+1},\ldots,S_L\), the summands \(\nu_{M+1},\ldots,\nu_L\), and optionally the actual neighboring Apéry state \(b_{n-1},b_n,b_{n+1}\);
2. use finitely many linear operations over the localized coefficient ring whose denominators are units at \(p\ell\);
3. form determinants, Fitting minors, ordinary resultants, and subresultants of the resulting linear recurrence equations;
4. form gcds only with prime support certified operandwise from those equations;
5. divide all coefficient content and all universal recurrence/boundary factors before calling the result primitive.

### Theorem 10.1 — saturation for this class

Within this class, a formally forced \(p\ell\)-carrier has one of the following forms.

1. It retains \(S_L\) or \(b_n\), hence retains an actual long/exponential coordinate.
2. Its \(\ell\)-factor is coefficient content inserted explicitly.
3. It becomes zero after the long coordinate and universal factors are saturated.

There is no fourth case producing a nonzero primitive boundary integer of logarithmic height \(O((\ell-p)\operatorname{polylog}n)\) with a target-forced \(\ell\)-factor.

#### Proof summary

- The rank-two cutoff module is (2.5).
- The target lattice splits as (4.3).
- Primitive linear forms are classified by (5.4).
- The boundary elimination ideal is zero by (6.4).
- Operandwise gcd certification reduces to the same ideal.
- Resultants and Fitting minors lie in the corresponding elimination ideal.
- The full Apéry recurrence has a free regular state coordinate by (8.4).

This proves the theorem. ∎

## Final conclusion

The natural adjacent-cutoff bridge has now been saturated exactly.

- The true low-height boundary coordinate is \(Q_d\), not \(E/p^2\).
- The \(\ell\)-target equation is monic in the long prefix \(S_M\) and therefore imposes no condition on \(Q_d\).
- The exact lattice quotient after eliminating the long direction has no \(\ell\)-torsion.
- Linear combinations, operandwise gcds, resultants, subresultants, and the regular Apéry recurrence cannot restore that torsion after primitive saturation.
- The pure-cross example \((321,193,211)\) explicitly has
  \[
  E/p^2\equiv87,
  \qquad Q_9\equiv148
  \pmod{211}.
  \]

Accordingly, no genuine primitive/common carrier exists in the stated recurrence-determinantal class. Reopening the route requires a new distinguished-initial-state identity that supplies a second equation for the prefix coordinate—necessarily outside the bounded cutoff recurrence graph proved here.
