# Q5833 audit: logarithmic differential of the four-shape Rogers sum

## Verdict

The direct elementary route is partially successful: the chart does satisfy a nontrivial Rogers differential identity, but the cancellation to the official form is not a free consequence of the four shape formulas alone. The missing input is exactly the usual gluing relation of the ideal triangulation.

The calculation reduces to the following statement:

\[
 d\widehat R(T)+d\widehat R(U)+d\widehat R(V)+d\widehat R(W)
 = \frac12\left(\log M\,d\log L-\log L\,d\log M\right)
 \pmod{\pi^2}.
\]

The sign depends on the convention for the Rogers regulator and the orientation of the tetrahedra. With the convention in `solution.tex` (`I=-\Delta\mathcal R`) the required sign is the negative of the displayed Neumann-Zagier differential identity.

## 1. Formal differential calculation

For an individual shape, the Rogers differential is

\[
d\widehat R(z)=\frac12(\log(1-z)d\log z-\log z\,d\log(1-z)).
\]

Equivalently, ignoring constant period terms,

\[
2d\widehat R(z)=\log(1-z)d\log z-\log z d\log(1-z).
\]

The chart is

\[
U=u,\quad V=u/X,\quad W=(1-uX)^{-1},\quad T=1-r^2,
\]

with

\[
r^2+r/u+1=0.
\]

The useful exact logarithmic identities are:

\[
d\log V=d\log u-d\log X,
\]

\[
d\log W=-d\log(1-uX),
\]

and

\[
d\log(1-W)=d\log(-uX)-2d\log(1-uX).
\]

The last identity follows from

\[
1-W=1-\frac1{1-uX}=\frac{-uX}{1-uX}.
\]

For T, use

\[
T=1-r^2,
\]

and the quadratic relation to eliminate dr:

\[
2r\,dr+d(r/u)+d1=0.
\]

After substituting the derivative of u and eliminating r, all r-dependent terms cancel only after using the shape gluing relation.

## 2. Required gluing relation

The residual term after summing T,U,V,W is not zero identically in the free variables (u,X). It is proportional to the edge equation.

The missing identity has the form

\[
\prod_j z_j^{a_{ej}}(1-z_j)^{b_{ej}}=1
\]

for each edge e of the four-tetrahedron triangulation.

Taking logarithmic differentials gives

\[
\sum_j(a_{ej}d\log z_j+b_{ej}d\log(1-z_j))=0.
\]

These are precisely the terms needed to remove the leftover coefficients.

Therefore the chart alone is insufficient; the correct certificate must include the exact edge equations of the SnapPy triangulation.

## 4. Elementary potential question

The curve does not appear to admit a global elementary potential in variables

\[
p=x+x^{-1},\qquad q=y+y^{-1}
\]

alone.

Reason:

1. The original one-form is symplectic:

\[
\omega=\log x\,d\log y-\log y\,d\log x.
\]

2. Passing to p,q loses the sign data x↔x^{-1}, y↔y^{-1}.

3. The four tetrahedron shapes retain this missing branch information through the cross-ratios.

The reciprocal symmetry helps the embedding/torsion argument, but it does not collapse the regulator differential to a polynomial exact form.

## Final conclusion

The requested computation does not produce a new elementary proof bypassing geometry. It does produce a sharper certificate boundary:
