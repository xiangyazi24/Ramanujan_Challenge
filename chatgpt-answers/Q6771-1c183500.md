# Q6771 audit: mod-p^2 lift, Gamma_p, and the Heilbronn analogy

## Verdict

The mod-p^2 lift is the correct direction conceptually, but it does not currently produce a Heilbronn-type auxiliary relation strong enough to prove a power saving for Sigma_t = sum_r e_p(t H_r). The route identifies the right missing structure: not merely a difference equation, but a second algebraic relation that reduces the effective complexity of the phase. The harmonic lift has real extra structure (Wolstenholme, Lehmer congruences, p-adic Gamma), but the known identities do not supply the required low-degree curve relation.

The precise obstruction is: the lift HH_r gives a controlled increment law, but not a controlled algebraic relation between HH_r and r analogous to the Fermat-quotient relation used in Heilbronn sums.

## 1. The mod-p^2 lift

Define

HH_r = sum_{n=1}^r n^{-1} mod p^2.

Then

HH_{r+1}-HH_r = (r+1)^{-1} mod p^2.

Reduction modulo p gives the ordinary harmonic walk H_r.

The Newton-Hensel formula is correct:

inv_{p^2}(n) = inv_p(n)  (2 - ninv_p(n)) mod p^2.

However, this is not a new low-complexity relation in the Stepanov sense. It rewrites the increment using an inverse variable, but the inverse variable still has the same pole divisor. After k shifts one accumulates the poles at r+1,...,r+k.

Therefore the mod-p^2 lift removes the ambiguity of lifting, but does not remove pole growth.

## 2. Comparison with Heilbronn

Heilbronn succeeds because the phase n^p mod p^2 contains a multiplicative logarithm:

q_p(ab)=q_p(a)+q_p(b) mod p.

This creates a second equation with low algebraic complexity.

The harmonic walk has the additive logarithmic flavor

H_r = sum_{n<=r} 1/n,

but it is a path integral along an interval, not a homomorphism on a group. The identity

H_{ab} is not determined from H_a and H_b by a bounded-complexity operation.

The analogy is therefore structural but not yet usable.

## 3. p-adic Gamma route

The p-adic Gamma function does encode the product structure:

Gamma_p(x+1)/Gamma_p(x) = -x

away from the exceptional residue classes.

Taking logarithmic derivatives gives identities involving sums of reciprocals. This explains why harmonic sums appear naturally as derivatives of log Gamma_p.

But the required object would be a bounded-degree functional equation of the form:

R(r, H_r, H_{phi(r)}, ...) = 0

with degree independent of p.

Known Gamma_p reflection and multiplication formulas give relations among values at transformed arguments, but after differentiation they involve additional correction terms and do not collapse the whole sequence H_r into a bounded-degree algebraic curve.

So Gamma_p gives a conceptual origin, not yet the missing Stepanov relation.

## 4. What would be enough

A sufficient lemma would be:

There exists a fixed-degree algebraic correspondence C over F_p and an auxiliary variable Y_r derived from HH_r such that

(r,Y_r) lies on C(F_p)

for all r, with C independent of p-degree growth.

Then one could combine:

- the shift relation Y_{r+1}-Y_r = 1/(r+1),

- the bounded-degree algebraic relation,

- Stepanov auxiliary polynomials,

to obtain

This is the exact analogue of the Heilbronn input.

## 5. Why the present lift does not reach it

The obstruction is not Wolstenholme. Wolstenholme gives the global endpoint relation

HH_{p-1}=0 mod p^2.

Lehmer-type congruences give special-point evaluations.

But a Weyl bound requires a relation valid uniformly for every r. Endpoint and distribution identities do not control the full orbit of partial sums.

The missing identity is a bounded-complexity substitute for multiplicative logarithmicity.

## 6. Consequence for the toy strip theorem

The Q6759/Q6761 reduction remains valid: a power saving for

Sigma(t,s)=sum_r e_p(tH_r+sr)

would imply strip anti-concentration.

The lift changes the search target:

not "apply Stepanov to the difference equation",

but

"find a Heilbronn-style mod-p^2 algebraic relation for the harmonic integral."

At present that relation is not known from the available Gamma_p identities.

## Final answers

(i) Least confident step:

The least confident step is the negative conclusion about Gamma_p: a stronger distribution identity or an unexpected finite-difference avatar of the p-adic Gamma multiplication law could still produce the missing bounded-degree relation. The obstruction analysis is stronger than the impossibility claim.

(ii) Blind spot in the question:

The blind spot is treating "log-like" as equivalent to "Heilbronn-like". The decisive feature in Heilbronn is not logarithmic intuition but an algebraic homomorphism relation with bounded complexity. Harmonic sums have a logarithmic derivative interpretation, but a derivative of a p-adic analytic object is not automatically a low-complexity algebraic phase.
