# 3. Which Katz/Laumon theorem actually applies

There is no theorem in Katz which takes a recurrence-defined table on F_p^2 as input and outputs a bounded-conductor sheaf. The existing theorems begin after fixed algebraic data have been supplied.

## 3.1 Katz-Laumon 1985

The relevant source is:

- N. Katz and G. Laumon, "Transformation de Fourier et majoration de sommes exponentielles", Publ. Math. IHES 62 (1985), 145-202, DOI 10.1007/BF02698808; correction in Publ. Math. IHES 69 (1989), 233.

For fixed schemes, vector bundles, morphisms, and constructible complexes defined over a finitely generated base, their Theorem 4.1 gives a finite uniform stratification adapted to Fourier transform; Corollary 4.2 packages the resulting uniformity over finite fields. Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.2.1 give, in a convenient fixed finite-map setting, square-root exponential-sum bounds outside a fixed homogeneous exceptional locus, with constants expressed by topological Euler-characteristic data. Theorem 5.4 supplies the cohomological concentration/purity mechanism, and Theorems 5.7.0-5.7.1 treat singular variants.

The hypotheses relevant here are:

1. a fixed finite-type algebraic source and fixed morphisms after inverting finitely many primes;

1. a fixed constructible complex with controlled ramification;

1. a finite stratification on which the maps are locally acyclic/lisse in the needed sense;

1. removal of phase parameters for which the Artin-Schreier phase is geometrically trivial or has a positive-dimensional critical locus;

1. no surviving constant sheaf in the centered part if one wants square-root rather than main-term size.

The theorem controls the Fourier transform of fixed algebraic data. It does not supply the missing map d(r,h).

## 3.2 Laumon's local Fourier transform

- G. Laumon, "Transformation de Fourier, constantes d'equations fonctionnelles et conjecture de Weil", Publ. Math. IHES 65 (1987), 131-210, DOI 10.1007/BF02698937.

Laumon's local Fourier theory describes how local monodromy, slopes, and Swan conductors transform. It is the right tool once the input sheaf on the line is known. It does not turn a finite table into a sheaf.

Applying FKM to a phase of complexity Theta(p) produces a polynomial-in-p conductor bound, which is exactly too weak.

## 4.3 Why a p-adic interpolation/depth tower does not automatically solve this

A Dwork crystal or overconvergent F-isocrystal can encode Frobenius and congruences of the generating function. That is p-adic geometry in the family/generating variable. The desired Artin-Schreier object is an l-adic sheaf in the additive coefficient-index variables (r,h).

To enter the Grothendieck trace formula one needs, at minimum:

1. a finite-type algebraic object over F_p;

1. canonical trace values at all extension fields F_{p^m}, compatible with Frobenius;

1. uniformly bounded ranks and local breaks.

A table known only on F_p, or a congruence tower modulo p^k, provides none of these extension-field data automatically. Artin-Schreier-Witt sheaves can encode higher p-adic digits once an algebraic Witt-vector phase is given, but they do not algebraize the iteration index and their Swan complexity generally records the depth.

Thus "D is not a polynomial" is fatal to the direct use of existing Katz/FKM theorems, but it is not a proof that no hidden algebraization exists. A modular, constant-term, or Mellin representation might move the geometry to a different parameter space.

## 7.3 Minimal code for the control

```python
from cmath import exp, pi


def central_binom_table(p):
    a = [0] * p
    a[0] = 1
    for r in range(p - 1):
        a[r + 1] = (
            a[r]
            * 2
            * (2 * r + 1)
            * pow(r + 1, -1, p)
        ) % p
    return a


def F_bin(p, a, t, xi):
    zeta = exp(2j * pi / p)
    S = sum(
        zeta ** ((t * a[r] - xi * r) % p)
        for r in range(p)
    )
    return abs(S) ** 2
```

def F_bin_grid(p, a):

# 9. Direct answers

## 9.1 What controls Betti numbers/conductor?

Once a fixed algebraic d(r,h) exists, the controls are:

- degree and singularity data of a projective compactification of Phi=(d,h);

- dimensions and degrees of critical loci and bad fibers;

- ranks, singularity counts, drops, and Swan conductors of the input complex;

- Katz-Laumon finite stratification and weight theory;

- Laumon local Fourier transform for local breaks;

- FKM Theorems 2.3 and 2.5 for polynomial conductor/Betti bounds under Artin-Schreier cohomological transforms.

The recurrence-defined nature of D is fatal to direct application because no fixed algebraic phase or bounded-conductor compatible system is currently known. A p-adic depth tower is not a substitute until it supplies an algebraic l-adic trace object in the index variables with extension-field compatibility.

No. Formula (5.1) is an explicit counterexample to any such inverse principle. Bounded spectrum is only forward-model evidence. There is no inverse Riemann existence theorem from one base-field Fourier bound.

# 10. Confidence audit

## (a) Least certain step

The least certain step is the negative structural assertion that the Dwork/K3/modular depth tower cannot be reorganized into a fixed algebraic object in a different dual parameter space. It certainly does not provide the required additive-index l-adic sheaf by any standard theorem, but a non-obvious Mellin or character-space construction could bypass the interpolation degree entirely. I am confident about the forward sheaf formalism and the inverse counterexample; I am less confident that the currently visible additive index plane is the only viable algebraic home.

## (b) Possible shared blind spot

There are two closely related blind spots.

First, we may be measuring the wrong stage. The raw maximum max |F|/p tests R_p, whereas GPRV lives after the t pushforward J_p. A flat raw spectrum can coexist with complete coherence in t.

Second, we may be insisting that the index r itself be an affine algebraic coordinate. The fixed geometry might instead live on a Mellin/character stack, a modular curve, or a constant-term torus. In that dual space, high-order Kummer characters still have bounded local conductor, and the apparent degree Theta(p) in the additive interpolation could be an artifact of Fourier inversion. The real breakthrough would be a fixed dual-space object whose collision/zero pushforward descends to (2.10) with bounded conductor.

The immediate experimental blind spot to eliminate is simpler: a nonwrapping triangular mask is not algebraic and can manufacture exactly the kind of spectral growth being used as a death test. The cyclic and masked plots must be kept separate.
