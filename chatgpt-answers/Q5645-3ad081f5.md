# Average zero fibers of the Apéry numbers: an unconditional \(x^{5/3}/\log x\) bound and the exact arithmetic barrier

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
b_r=\sum_{k=0}^r\binom{r}{k}^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2,
\qquad
Z_p=\{0\le r<p:p\mid b_r\},
\qquad
z_p=|Z_p|.
\]

I do **not** obtain the desired estimate

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll \pi(x).
\tag{T}
\]

The strongest unconditional estimate supplied by the current arithmetic inputs is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll \frac{x^{5/3}}{\log x}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, with logarithmic prime weight,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\log p\ll x^{5/3}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

This is a genuine power saving over the completely trivial
\(O(x^2/\log x)\) bound. It follows immediately from the proved vertical theorem
\(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\), but it can also be rederived directly by averaging the exact gap-continuant argument. The second derivation is useful because it identifies the precise obstruction:

\[
z_p
\le
1+\frac pH+\sum_{2\le h\le H}\rho_h(p),
\qquad
\rho_h(p):=\#\{r\bmod p:N_h(r)=0\},
\tag{3}
\]

where

\[
N_0=0,\quad N_1=1,\quad
N_{h+1}(X)=P(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X),
\tag{4}
\]

\[
P(X)=34X^3+51X^2+27X+5,
\qquad
\deg N_h=3(h-1).
\tag{5}
\]

The current theorem gives only

\[
\rho_h(p)\le 3(h-1),
\tag{6}
\]

so (3) is

\[
z_p\ll \frac pH+H^2.
\tag{7}
\]

Both pointwise and after summing over primes, the optimum is
\(H\asymp p^{1/3}\), giving the exponent \(2/3\) vertically and \(5/3\) after prime averaging.

The requested \(O(\pi(x))\) estimate is therefore not blocked by a missing logarithm or a loose endpoint. It requires a new theorem that averages roots of the **growing family** \(N_h\) over primes, or some different arithmetic mechanism that bypasses the gap family. Product identities, height bounds, reflection, Brun--Selberg sieve, known large-prime-factor theorems, and the currently available modular-form factorization do not provide such a theorem.

A concrete intermediate target is the following uniform average-root estimate. For dyadic \(P\) and \(H\le P^{1/2}\), suppose one could prove

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{2\le h\le H}\rho_h(p)
\ll P^{o(1)}H\frac P{\log P}.
\tag{AR}
\]

Then (3), summed over \(P<p\le2P\) and optimized at \(H=P^{1/2}\), would give the meaningful improvement

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p
\ll \frac{P^{3/2+o(1)}}{\log P}.
\tag{8}
\]

For fixed \(h\), Chebotarev suggests an average of the order of the number of irreducible factors of \(N_h\), but the repository has neither the required irreducibility theorem nor effective uniformity when \(h\) grows like a power of \(P\). Fixed-\(h\) Chebotarev is therefore far too weak for (AR).

The exact conclusion is:

> The listed inputs prove (1), and their presently available quantitative consequences saturate at (1). Proving (T) requires genuinely new average root, cross-prime, or coefficient anti-concentration information for the actual Apéry sequence. No independence hypothesis is used in this assessment.

## 2. The unconditional average power saving

Define

\[
S(x):=\sum_{p\le x}z_p.
\tag{14}
\]

The proved vertical theorem gives, for all primes outside a fixed finite set,

\[
z_p\le C p^{2/3}
\tag{15}
\]

with an absolute constant \(C\). Hence

\[
S(x)\ll 1+\sum_{p\le x}p^{2/3}.
\tag{16}
\]

By partial summation and the Chebyshev bound
\(\pi(t)\ll t/\log t\),

\[
\sum_{p\le x}p^{2/3}
=x^{2/3}\pi(x)-\frac23\int_2^x t^{-1/3}\pi(t)\,dt
\ll \frac{x^{5/3}}{\log x}.
\tag{17}
\]

This proves (1). Similarly,

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\log p
\ll \sum_{p\le x}p^{2/3}\log p
\ll x^{5/3},
\tag{18}
\]

which proves (2).

This argument needs no prime number theorem; Chebyshev suffices. If one uses the prime number theorem, partial summation gives the more precise scale

\[
\sum_{p\le x}p^{2/3}
\sim \frac35\frac{x^{5/3}}{\log x},
\tag{19}
\]

but the asymptotic constant is irrelevant here.

### Comparison with the trivial bound

The trivial estimate \(z_p\le p\) gives

\[
S(x)\le\sum_{p\le x}p\asymp\frac{x^2}{2\log x}.
\tag{20}
\]

Thus (1) saves a factor \(x^{1/3}\). It remains larger than the desired
\(x/\log x\) by a factor \(x^{2/3}\).

## 3. Reversing the sums: the height bound is weaker

The proposed reversal is exact:

\[
S(x)
=
\sum_{0\le r<x}
\#\{p\text{ prime}:r<p\le x,\ p\mid b_r\}.
\tag{21}
\]

For \(r\ge2\), let

\[
k_r(x):=\#\{p:r<p\le x,\ p\mid b_r\}.
\tag{22}
\]

The distinct primes counted by \(k_r(x)\) have product dividing \(b_r\). Since every such prime exceeds \(r\),

\[
(r+1)^{k_r(x)}
\le
\prod_{\substack{p>r\\p\mid b_r}}p
\le b_r.
\tag{23}
\]

Therefore

\[
k_r(x)\le\frac{\log b_r}{\log(r+1)}.
\tag{24}
\]

The Apéry asymptotic, or just the binomial formula, gives

\[
\log b_r=O(r).
\tag{25}
\]

Consequently

\[
k_r(x)\ll\frac r{\log(r+1)}
\tag{26}
\]

and summing gives only

\[
S(x)\ll\sum_{r<x}\frac r{\log(r+1)}
\ll\frac{x^2}{\log x}.
\tag{27}
\]

Thus reversal plus the individual height of \(b_r\) does not even recover the \(x^{5/3}/\log x\) vertical bound.

The same fact can be written as the exact global product divisibility

\[
\prod_{p\le x}p^{z_p}
\mid
\prod_{0\le r<x}b_r,
\tag{28}
\]

hence

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\log p
\le
\sum_{r<x}\log b_r
=O(x^2).
\tag{29}
\]

Again, this is weaker than (18).

### Why average large-prime-factor theorems point the wrong way

Results proving that \(b_r\) often has many prime factors, or a large greatest prime factor, are lower-bound statements about the factorization of \(b_r\). The present problem needs an upper bound on the number of primes in the moving interval

\[
r<p\le x
\tag{30}
\]

that divide \(b_r\), summed over \(r\). A theorem asserting that \(b_r\) has a large prime divisor can increase the possible incidence count; it does not bound it from above.

The known arithmetic literature on Apéry numbers, including Luca--Shparlinski-type results, therefore does not supply the needed direction. Standard primitive-divisor theorems for constant-coefficient linear recurrences are also inapplicable: \(b_r\) is a polynomial-coefficient, order-two holonomic sequence, and the modulus and index move together in (21).

## 4. Reflection changes only constants

For odd \(p\), reflection gives

\[
r\in Z_p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p-1-r\in Z_p.
\tag{31}
\]

Let

\[
Z_p^-:=Z_p\cap\left[0,\frac{p-1}{2}\right),
\qquad z_p^-:=|Z_p^-|.
\tag{32}
\]

Then

\[
z_p\le2z_p^-+1.
\tag{33}
\]

After folding, reversal becomes

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p^-
=
\sum_{r<x/2}
\#\{p:2r+1<p\le x,\ p\mid b_r\}.
\tag{34}
\]

The height argument now gives

\[
\#\{p:2r+1<p\le x,\ p\mid b_r\}
\le
\frac{\log b_r}{\log(2r+1)}
\ll\frac r{\log r},
\tag{35}
\]

which still sums to \(O(x^2/\log x)\).

At the continuant level, reflection produces the exact forced factor (13). Removing that factor lowers the degree of \(N_h\) by exactly one when \(h\) is even. Summed over \(h\le H\), this saves only \(O(H)\) roots from an \(O(H^2)\) ledger. It does not change the optimizing exponent.

This is an exact reason, not a heuristic: reflection removes the universal reflected return, but the remaining possible short returns still have total degree quadratic in \(H\).

## 5. Averaging the continuant proof reproduces \(x^{5/3}/\log x\)

This section tracks all quantifiers in the gap argument.

Fix a prime \(p\ge7\), and list the zeros as

\[
0\le r_1<r_2<\cdots<r_{z_p}<p.
\tag{36}
\]

For \(1\le i<z_p\), put

\[
h_i=r_{i+1}-r_i.
\tag{37}
\]

Fix an integer \(H\) with \(2\le H<p\).

### Long gaps

The number of \(i\) for which \(h_i>H\) is at most

\[
\frac{p-1}{H+1}<\frac pH,
\tag{38}
\]

because the long gaps are disjoint subintervals of \([0,p-1]\).

### Short gaps

For a fixed \(2\le h\le H\), every \(i\) with \(h_i=h\) satisfies

\[
p\mid b_{r_i},\qquad p\mid b_{r_i+h},\qquad r_i+h<p.
\tag{39}
\]

The exact cleared recurrence gives

\[
N_h(r_i)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{40}
\]

The range-content theorem gives

\[
N_h\not\equiv0\pmod p
\qquad(h<p),
\tag{41}
\]

and

\[
\deg N_h=3(h-1).
\tag{42}
\]

Therefore there are at most \(3(h-1)\) possible bases \(r_i\) for this fixed gap \(h\). Summing over \(h\le H\),

\[
\#\{i:h_i\le H\}
\le3\sum_{h=2}^H(h-1)
=\frac32H(H-1).
\tag{43}
\]

Combining (38) and (43),

\[
z_p
\le
1+\frac pH+\frac32H(H-1).
\tag{44}
\]

This is the proof-grade vertical inequality.

### Dyadic prime average

Let \(P\) be large and sum over \(P<p\le2P\). Choose one integer

\[
H=\lfloor P^{1/3}\rfloor.
\tag{45}
\]

For large \(P\), \(2\le H<p\) for every prime in the block. By (44) and Chebyshev,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p
&\ll
\pi(2P)
+\frac1H\sum_{P<p\le2P}p
+H^2\pi(2P)\\
&\ll
\frac P{\log P}
+\frac{P^2}{H\log P}
+\frac{H^2P}{\log P}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{46}
\]

The two main terms balance at \(H=P^{1/3}\), giving

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p
\ll\frac{P^{5/3}}{\log P}.
\tag{47}
\]

Summing dyadic blocks proves (1).

This second proof matters because it shows exactly where a prime average would have to enter. The long-gap term is already averaged optimally. The only possible gain is to replace the pointwise degree sum

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{h\le H}\rho_h(p)
\le
3\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{h\le H}(h-1)
\ll H^2\frac P{\log P}
\tag{48}
\]

by a genuinely smaller average-root estimate.

## 6. The exact average-root interpolation

Define

\[
\rho_h(p):=\#\{a\in\mathbf F_p:N_h(a)=0\}.
\tag{49}
\]

The same proof gives, uniformly for \(2\le H<p\),

\[
z_p\le1+\frac pH+\sum_{2\le h\le H}\rho_h(p).
\tag{50}
\]

For a dyadic block, define

\[
\mathcal R(P,H)
:=
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\sum_{2\le h\le H}\rho_h(p).
\tag{51}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p
\ll
\frac P{\log P}
+\frac{P^2}{H\log P}
+\mathcal R(P,H).
\tag{52}
\]

The current degree theorem is exactly

\[
\mathcal R(P,H)\ll H^2\frac P{\log P},
\tag{53}
\]

and (52) becomes (46).

More generally, suppose one proves, for some fixed \(\delta>0\),

\[
\mathcal R(P,H)
\ll
P^{o(1)}H^{2-\delta}\frac P{\log P}
\tag{54}
\]

uniformly in the required range. Balancing the two main terms in (52) gives

\[
H=P^{1/(3-\delta)},
\tag{55}
\]

and hence

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p
\ll
\frac{P^{\,2-1/(3-\delta)+o(1)}}{\log P}.
\tag{56}
\]

Thus:

- \(\delta=0\) is the current exponent \(5/3\);
- \(\delta=1\) gives \(3/2\);
- any fixed \(\delta>0\) gives a genuine improvement over \(5/3\).

This is the cleanest arithmetic location for a power saving.

### What fixed-\(h\) Chebotarev would say

For one fixed separable polynomial \(f\in\mathbf Z[X]\), the prime average of the number of roots of \(f\bmod p\) is governed by Frobenius fixed points. In particular, after splitting \(f\) into irreducible factors over \(\mathbf Q\), Chebotarev gives an average root count equal to the number of irreducible factors, apart from finitely many bad primes.

Applied to one fixed \(N_h\), this is promising. The exact centered symmetry already gives one rational factor when \(h\) is even, and computation suggests that the remaining factor is often irreducible. But the project does **not** currently prove that irreducibility statement.

More importantly, (52) needs \(h\) growing with \(P\). Even the first new exponent \(3/2\) requires control through \(H\asymp P^{1/2}\). The degrees, discriminants, and splitting fields of \(N_h\) grow with \(h\); ordinary fixed-polynomial Chebotarev has constants depending on that field and gives no usable uniformity at this scale.

Therefore the sentence “apply Chebotarev to the continuants” is not a proof. The exact missing input is a growing-family theorem such as (AR) or (54), including all dependence on \(h\).

## 7. Product and resultant identities have no spare height

### 7.1 One zero row

The reverse product identity (28) spends the full available height

\[
\sum_{r<x}\log b_r=O(x^2).
\tag{57}
\]

There is no sublinear-height common product hidden in this argument. Every incidence contributes one factor \(p\), and the characteristic-zero product already has exactly the scale that permits \(x^2/\log x\) primes of size \(x\).

### 7.2 A same-prime zero pair

For \(r+h<p\), two zeros give

\[
p\mid N_h(r).
\tag{58}
\]

The exact Dirichlet determinant for \(N_h(r)\) has size \(h-1\). A Hadamard bound gives, uniformly for \(r+h\le x\),

\[
\log N_h(r)
\le 3(h-1)\log(Cx)+O(h)
\tag{59}
\]

for an absolute \(C\). Hence the number of primes \(p>r+h\) which can divide this one nonzero integer is at most

\[
\frac{\log N_h(r)}{\log(r+h+1)}
\le 3h+O(1).
\tag{60}
\]

This is useful and completely arithmetic, but it has the same linear-in-gap cost as the degree calculation. Summing over all potential bases and gaps loses at least the amount saved by (60).

### 7.3 Resultants

A resultant

\[
\operatorname{Res}(N_h,N_k)
\tag{61}
\]

can control primes for which the **same base** is a root for two distinct gaps. Such primes correspond to a triple zero configuration

\[
b_r\equiv b_{r+h}\equiv b_{r+k}\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{62}
\]

This is not the dominant configuration in the packing proof. A zero fiber may have adjacent gaps of different sizes whose bases are roots of different \(N_h\)'s, with no base repeated across two gap polynomials. Resultants detect repeated or clustered roots; they do not reduce the total number of isolated roots counted in (48).

The current repository identities make this precise. Addition, Casoratian, and bordered-determinant formulas control coincidences among continuants, but no theorem bounds

\[
\mathcal R(P,H)
\tag{63}
\]

below its total-degree scale. The obstruction is not the absence of exact identities; it is the absence of an average theorem for their isolated roots.

### 7.4 Reflection saturation

For a reflected pair, \(h=p-1-2r\) is even and

\[
2r+h+1=p.
\tag{64}
\]

Thus the factor (13) explains \(p\mid N_h(r)\) universally. Dividing it out is mandatory before treating a continuant as a selective second-zero certificate. This saves one degree for each even \(h\), hence \(O(H)\) total degree through \(H\). The unsaturated degree budget is \(\asymp H^2\), so the exponent is unchanged.

This is the exact sense in which the product/resultant route is saturated: every genuinely new short return costs degree and logarithmic height proportional to its gap, while the only free return is the reflected one, whose removal is lower order in the aggregate gap ledger.

## 8. Brun--Selberg sieve does not create the missing density

A sieve over \(r\) would need information about

\[
A_d(R):=\#\{0\le r<R:d\mid b_r\}
\tag{65}
\]

for many squarefree \(d\), with an approximation of the form

\[
A_d(R)=R\,g(d)+\text{controlled error}
\tag{66}
\]

uniformly through a useful level of distribution.

The one-prime zero count supplies only

\[
g(p)\approx \frac{z_p}{p}
\tag{67}
\]

over a complete period modulo \(p\). But the target sum is itself the first moment of these local densities in a triangular range. The current bound gives

\[
\sum_{p\le x}\frac{z_p}{p}
\ll
\sum_{p\le x}p^{-1/3}
\ll\frac{x^{2/3}}{\log x},
\tag{68}
\]

which grows polynomially. There is no bounded sieve dimension to exploit.

Moreover, in the reversed sum (21), the condition \(p>r\) means that for a fixed modulus \(p\), the relevant initial segment may contain fewer than one complete period. A Brun or Selberg upper-bound sieve cannot replace the unknown distribution of the marked residues \(Z_p\) inside that segment.

Passing to squarefree \(d\) does not solve this. Chinese remaindering identifies the simultaneous residue set modulo \(d\) only after one knows the reductions for every prime factor, but a useful sieve still requires uniform estimates for how the initial interval samples those CRT classes. No such level-of-distribution theorem follows from \(z_p\ll p^{2/3}\).

Thus a sieve reformulation either:

1. inserts \(z_p/p\) as an input, in which case it has merely renamed the desired average; or
2. asks for new multi-prime distribution estimates, which are stronger than the listed inputs.

No independence assumption is legitimate here, and none is used.

## 9. Modular-form inputs do not presently control coefficient zeros

The Apéry generating series has deep modular and Picard--Fuchs structure. The currently relevant consequences include Lucas congruences and the CFVZ square/quadratic-times-square factorization of the truncated generating polynomial modulo \(p\).

These results concern different objects from \(z_p\):

- Lucas congruence describes \(b_n\bmod p\) from the base-\(p\) digits of \(n\), but for \(0\le r<p\) it imposes no restriction on how many first digits satisfy \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\).
- The CFVZ factorization controls roots and multiplicities of
  \[
  A_p(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}b_rt^r
  \tag{69}
  \]
  as a polynomial in \(t\). The condition \(b_r=0\) is vanishing of one **coefficient**, not a root condition in \(t\).
- Writing \(A_p(t)\) as a square converts \(b_r\) into a convolution of square-root coefficients. The project audit in Q5547 shows that the available Christoffel--Darboux and differential-module identities do not telescope the reversed convolution needed for one coefficient.

Therefore the modular-form structure does not currently imply a coefficient anti-concentration estimate of the form

\[
\#\{0\le r<p:b_r=0\}\ll1
\tag{70}
\]

or its prime average. A new theorem explicitly bridging the generating-series Galois structure to coefficient zeros would be needed.

## 10. A precise saturation theorem for the listed ledgers

The following is the sharp methodological conclusion.

### Proposition 10.1 — vertical gap-ledger saturation

Suppose an argument uses the recurrence only through the facts that, for each \(2\le h<p\),

\[
\{r:b_r=b_{r+h}=0\pmod p\}
\subseteq
\{r:N_h(r)=0\pmod p\},
\tag{71}
\]

and that \(N_h\) is a nonzero polynomial of degree \(3(h-1)\). Suppose its cross-prime step is summation of the resulting root counts. Then its optimal bound is

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll\frac{x^{5/3}}{\log x}.
\tag{72}
\]

Reflection changes the quadratic short-gap term by only \(O(H)\) and cannot alter the exponent.

#### Proof

The assumptions give (44). Summing on a dyadic block gives (46). The expression

\[
\frac{P^2}{H\log P}+\frac{H^2P}{\log P}
\tag{73}
\]

is minimized at \(H\asymp P^{1/3}\), where both terms have size
\(P^{5/3}/\log P\). Removing one root for each even \(h\) changes the second numerator from \(O(H^2P)\) to \(O((H^2-H)P)\), which has the same minimum. ∎

### Proposition 10.2 — reverse-height saturation

Suppose an argument reverses the sums and uses each row \(r\) only through

\[
\prod_{p>r,\ p\mid b_r}p\le b_r,
\qquad
\log b_r=O(r).
\tag{74}
\]

Then it yields at best

\[
\sum_{r<x}\#\{p>r:p\mid b_r\}
\ll\frac{x^2}{\log x}.
\tag{75}
\]

Reflection replaces \(\log(r+1)\) by \(\log(2r+1)\), which changes only constants.

These are saturation statements about the **available inequalities**, not a logical counterexample to the actual Apéry sequence. The exact recurrence may contain further arithmetic coupling, but that coupling has not yet been converted into a prime-average theorem.

## 11. What theorem would actually move the exponent

There are three clean levels.

### Level 1: any power saving beyond \(5/3\)

Prove (54) for some fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly for

\[
H\le P^{1/(3-\delta)}.
\tag{76}
\]

Then (56) beats \(P^{5/3}/\log P\).

This is an average anti-splitting theorem for the growing continuant family.

### Level 2: the natural \(3/2\) target

Prove

\[
\mathcal R(P,H)
\ll
P^{o(1)}H\frac P{\log P}
\qquad(H\le P^{1/2}).
\tag{77}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p
\ll\frac{P^{3/2+o(1)}}{\log P}.
\tag{78}
\]

Heuristically, (77) is what one might expect if each saturated \(N_h\) had \(O(1)\) irreducible factors and Frobenius fixed points averaged uniformly. The required growing-\(h\) uniformity is the substantive theorem.

### Level 3: the requested bounded mean

The target itself is

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}z_p\ll\frac P{\log P}.
\tag{79}
\]

The adjacent-gap decomposition cannot reach this merely by taking \(H\) larger: the long-gap term requires \(H\asymp P\), while summing even an \(O(1)\) average root count for every \(h\le P\) already costs \(P^2/\log P\). To prove (79), one needs more global structure than independent control of every gap polynomial. Examples of genuinely sufficient new inputs would be:

1. a direct bounded-average theorem for the actual zero fibers \(Z_p\);
2. a global factorization or orbit theorem showing that all zeros in one fiber are generated by \(O(1)\) arithmetic data;
3. a coefficient anti-concentration theorem for the modular Apéry period;
4. a common cross-\(h\) algebraic constraint that compresses the whole zero set into a bounded-complexity object.

None is presently in the repository or in the cited modular-form results.

---

## 12. Final answer

The desired estimate

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll\pi(x)
\]

is not currently proved by a purely arithmetic argument from the listed inputs.

What is rigorously proved is the meaningful power saving

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll\frac{x^{5/3}}{\log x},
\qquad
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\log p\ll x^{5/3}.
}
\]

The reverse-height method gives only \(O(x^2/\log x)\). Reflection changes constants but not exponents. The exact continuant certificates, when averaged with their current degree bounds, reproduce \(x^{5/3}/\log x\) and no better. Product and resultant identities control repeated configurations but spend height proportional to the gap and do not suppress isolated roots. Brun--Selberg sieve lacks the necessary level of distribution, and the known modular-form factorization concerns polynomial roots in the generating variable rather than coefficient zeros.

The narrowest rigorous next attack is a uniform growing-family average-root theorem for the continuants, such as (54). Any fixed \(\delta>0\) there produces a new power saving; the linear-root-average form (77) yields the explicit \(x^{3/2+o(1)}/\log x\) bound. Reaching \(O(\pi(x))\) needs a still more global compression of an entire zero fiber, not a refinement of the current one-gap degree ledger.
