# All-height critical-value separation: exact cell WKB, the cancellation that is real, and the obstruction that remains

## Executive verdict

I do not obtain an all-height proof of `[CRIT-2H]`.

There is, however, a useful analytic advance. The exact two-block identity already present in the repository can be combined with a two-order Birkhoff expansion of the shifted Apéry recurrence. This gives an explicit limiting cell function and an explicit first and second finite-block correction.

The calculation shows:

1. The common symmetric correction of relative size `h^-2` really does cancel in an adjacent-cell difference: its adjacent variation is `O(h^-3)` in the bulk, and `O(h^-4)` for the center-nearest pair.
2. A different, antisymmetric correction survives. For an individual reflected critical-value branch it is proportional to

   ```text
   (1/r - 1/s) * xi,
   ```

   where `r+s=h-1` and `±xi` is a limiting critical-point pair. Its adjacent variation is generically `Theta(h^-2)`. Reflection kills its derivative only at the center, or after passing to a genuinely reflection-symmetric statistic such as a branch average. It does not kill it uniformly over all nonmirror cells.
3. Therefore the proposed global estimate

   ```text
   Delta_h = kappa_parity*h^-2 + O(h^(-2-delta))
   ```

   after subtracting only the leading limiting-cell difference is false in that generality. The first omitted term is already `h^-2 G'(alpha)`.
4. For the center-nearest reflection-symmetric comparison, the parity factor `2` is analytically forced. If `H=h-1` and `F(alpha)=F(1-alpha)` is the leading scaled value function, then

   ```text
   odd h:   Delta = (1/2) F''(1/2) H^-2 + O(H^-3),
   even h:  Delta =       F''(1/2) H^-2 + O(H^-3).
   ```

   This is a local center statement, not a uniform all-cell statement.
5. A Sturm or total-positivity proof on `J_h` is not presently available. The tridiagonal positivity argument that proves `N_h` squarefree does not transfer to the critical polynomial, and `J_h` is not a real-rooted orthogonal-polynomial family with a positive three-term recurrence.

The exact first unsupported estimate is identified in Section 7. It is not the existence of a formal WKB expansion; it is the claimed uniform cancellation of the `H^-1 G(alpha)` correction in adjacent differences.

## 1. Exact local recurrence and transfer matrix

The recurrence in the question is

```text
N_0(X)=0,
N_1(X)=1,
N_h(X)=A(X+h)N_(h-1)(X)-(X+h-1)^6 N_(h-2)(X),
A(t)=34t^3-51t^2+27t-5.
```

It is convenient to put

```text
P(t)=A(t+1)=34t^3+51t^2+27t+5.
```

For a complex shift `z`, every shifted solution satisfies

```text
(n+z+1)^3 Y_(n+1)(z)
  = P(n+z)Y_n(z)-(n+z)^3Y_(n-1)(z).                 (1.1)
```

The exact transfer matrix is

```text
[ Y_(n+1) ]   [ P(n+z)/(n+z+1)^3   -(n+z)^3/(n+z+1)^3 ] [ Y_n     ]
[ Y_n     ] = [ 1                    0                       ] [ Y_(n-1) ]. (1.2)
```

Its limiting matrix is

```text
T_infty = [[34,-1],[1,0]],
```

with distinct eigenvalues

```text
lambda = 17+12*sqrt(2),
mu     = 17-12*sqrt(2)=lambda^-1.                    (1.3)
```

Thus there is a uniform spectral gap on every fixed compact `z`-set.

Use the two canonical shifted solutions

```text
F_0(z)=1,
F_1(z)=P(z)/(z+1)^3,

G_0(z)=0,
G_1(z)=1/(z+1)^3,                                    (1.4)
```

both satisfying (1.1). Then

```text
F_n(z)=N_(n+1)(z-1)/prod_(a=1)^n(z+a)^3,
G_n(z)=N_n(z)/prod_(a=1)^n(z+a)^3,
F_n(0)=b_n.                                           (1.5)
```

Here `b_n` is the usual positive Apéry sequence.

## 2. Exact two-block cell identity

Fix a pole `X=-j`, and write

```text
r=j-1,
s=h-j,
r+s=h-1,
X=-j+z.                                               (2.1)
```

The continuant addition law gives the exact rational-function identity

```text
f_h(-j+z)
  = z^-3 F_r(-z)F_s(z) + z^3 G_r(-z)G_s(z),           (2.2)

f_h=N_h/q_h^3,
q_h=prod_(a=1)^h(X+a).
```

This is the correct local scaled equation. It is not merely a Laurent expansion at the pole; it contains the entire cell.

The leading pole scale is recovered by setting `z=0` in the two long blocks:

```text
f_h(-j+z)
  = b_r b_s z^-3
    * (1+(omega_s-omega_r)z+O(z^2)),                  (2.3)
```

where `omega_n=F_n'(0)/b_n`.

Equation (2.2), not (2.3), is the object to use for critical points, since the relevant critical points remain at cell scale `z=Theta(1)`.

## 3. Two-order Birkhoff expansion

### 3.1 Formal dominant solution

Insert

```text
Y_n(z)=C(z) lambda^n n^alpha
       * (1+c_1(z)/n+c_2(z)/n^2+c_3(z)/n^3+...).      (3.1)
```

into (1.1). Coefficient comparison gives

```text
alpha=-3/2,                                            (3.2)

c_1(z)=3*(-16+5*sqrt(2)-32z)/64,                      (3.3)

c_2(z)=
  [2057-1200*sqrt(2)+480z(16-5*sqrt(2)+16z)]/4096.    (3.4)
```

The `z`-dependence in `c_1` is especially important:

```text
c_1(z)-c_1(0)=-3z/2.                                  (3.5)
```

Let

```text
c10 = 3*(-16+5*sqrt(2))/64,
rho = 3*(16-5*sqrt(2))/64.                            (3.6)
```

After division by the Apéry solution at `z=0`, the universal normalized expansion is

```text
Y_n(z)/b_n
  = C_Y(z)
    * [1 - 3z/(2n)
         + (rho*z+15z^2/8)/n^2
         + O_K(n^-3)],                                (3.7)
```

for every solution having a nonzero dominant coefficient. The connection factor `C_Y(z)` depends on the solution, but the bracket in (3.7) does not.

Define the analytic dominant connection coefficients by

```text
phi(z)   = lim_(n->infinity) F_n(z)/b_n,
gamma(z) = lim_(n->infinity) G_n(z)/b_n.              (3.8)
```

Then, formally and on any compact where the dominant coefficients are controlled,

```text
F_n(z)/b_n
 = phi(z)[1-3z/(2n)+(rho*z+15z^2/8)/n^2+O_K(n^-3)],

G_n(z)/b_n
 = gamma(z)[1-3z/(2n)+(rho*z+15z^2/8)/n^2+O_K(n^-3)]. (3.9)
```

### 3.2 What is rigorous here

The coefficient identities (3.2)-(3.4) are exact algebraic consequences of the recurrence and admit a finite symbolic check.

A standard parameter-uniform discrete Levinson/Birkhoff argument should turn (3.9) into a theorem on each fixed compact `K` avoiding the shifted singular lattice. One diagonalizes `T_infty`, solves the first two homological conjugation equations using `lambda!=mu`, and estimates the resulting summable `O_K(n^-3)` matrix tail. The same construction gives `z`-derivatives through any fixed order.

What is not yet banked is the version needed by `[CRIT-2H]`: explicit constants, a compact set trapping all relevant finite-`h` critical branches, nonvanishing of the needed connection coefficients there, and a uniform lower bound on the limiting critical-point Hessians.

## 4. Limiting cell function and the common multiplier

Substitute (3.9) into the exact cell identity (2.2), and normalize by `b_r b_s`.

The limiting cell function is

```text
Phi(z)
  = z^-3 phi(-z)phi(z) + z^3 gamma(-z)gamma(z).        (4.1)
```

It is odd:

```text
Phi(-z)=-Phi(z).                                       (4.2)
```

Put

```text
d     = 1/r-1/s,
sigma = 1/r+1/s,
e     = 1/(rs).                                       (4.3)
```

The two shifted blocks have the same universal multiplier through second order. A direct multiplication of (3.9) gives

```text
f_h(-j+z)/(b_r b_s)
  = M_(r,s)(z) Phi(z) + R_(r,s)(z),                   (4.4)
```

where

```text
M_(r,s)(z)
 = 1
   + (3/2)d z
   + (3/2)e z^2
   + (15/8)d^2 z^2
   - rho*d*sigma*z,                                   (4.5)
```

and the formal third-order remainder is

```text
R_(r,s)=O_K((1/r+1/s)^3),                              (4.6)
```

with the same order after the first two `z`-derivatives, provided the uniform Birkhoff estimates are available.

This formula isolates the two different corrections:

```text
antisymmetric first correction:  (3/2)d z,
common symmetric h^-2 correction: (3/2)e z^2.          (4.7)
```

The `d^2` and `d*sigma` terms are also second order in the bulk.

## 5. Critical point and critical value expansion

Let `xi` be a nonzero simple critical point of the limiting cell function:

```text
Phi'(xi)=0,
Phi''(xi)!=0,
tau=Phi(xi)!=0.                                       (5.1)
```

Oddness gives the paired critical point `-xi`, with value `-tau`.

Solving the critical-point equation for the perturbed function `M_(r,s)Phi` gives

```text
z_(r,s,xi)
  = xi - (3/2)d*tau/Phi''(xi) + O((1/r+1/s)^2).       (5.2)
```

The corresponding critical value is

```text
t_(r,s,xi)/(b_r b_s tau)
 = 1
   + (3/2)xi*d
   + (3/2)xi^2*e
   + [(15/8)xi^2-(9/8)tau/Phi''(xi)]d^2
   - rho*xi*d*sigma
   + O((1/r+1/s)^3).                                  (5.3)
```

For the squared critical value `w=t^2`, the paired branches `±xi` satisfy

```text
w_(r,s,±)/(b_r^2 b_s^2 tau^2)
 = 1
   ± 3xi*d
   + 3xi^2*e
   + [6xi^2-(9/4)tau/Phi''(xi)]d^2
   ∓ 2rho*xi*d*sigma
   + O((1/r+1/s)^3).                                  (5.4)
```

Formula (5.4) is the main diagnostic.

The common term `3xi^2/(rs)` is exactly of relative order `h^-2` in the bulk. However, the two individual reflected branches carry the opposite correction `±3xi(1/r-1/s)`. Reflection exchanges the two signs; it does not erase them branchwise.

## 6. Macroscopic cell coordinate and the parity factor

Put

```text
H=h-1,
r+s=H,
alpha=r/H.                                            (6.1)
```

The Apéry asymptotic has the form

```text
b_n = K lambda^n n^-3/2
      * (1+c10/n+c20/n^2+O(n^-3)),                    (6.2)
```

with `K>0` and the exact `c10` in (3.6).

For an unsquared critical-value branch, the leading scaled cell function is

```text
F_xi(alpha)
  = tau [alpha(1-alpha)]^-3/2.                         (6.3)
```

For the squared value it is

```text
F_xi^sq(alpha)
  = tau^2 [alpha(1-alpha)]^-3.                         (6.4)
```

The first relative correction for the squared branches is explicitly

```text
G_±(alpha)
  = 2c10/[alpha(1-alpha)]
    ± 3xi(1-2alpha)/[alpha(1-alpha)].                  (6.5)
```

Thus a bulk expansion has the shape

```text
w_(H,r,±)
 = global_H * F_xi^sq(alpha)
   * [1+H^-1 G_±(alpha)+H^-2 K_±(alpha)+O(H^-3)].      (6.6)
```

Reflection acts by

```text
alpha -> 1-alpha,
+ branch -> - branch,                                 (6.7)
```

and indeed

```text
G_+(1-alpha)=G_-(alpha).                               (6.8)
```

It does not imply that `G_+'(alpha)` or `G_-'(alpha)` vanishes away from the center.

### 6.1 The cancellation that is valid

Suppose the quantity being compared is genuinely reflection-symmetric, so that its expansion can be written

```text
V_H(alpha)
 = F(alpha)+H^-1 G(alpha)+H^-2 K(alpha)+O(H^-3),       (6.9)

F(1-alpha)=F(alpha),
G(1-alpha)=G(alpha).                                   (6.10)
```

Then

```text
F'(1/2)=G'(1/2)=0.                                     (6.11)
```

For `h` odd, `H` is even. The center orbit is at `alpha=1/2`, and the next orbit is one lattice step away. Taylor expansion gives

```text
V_H(1/2+1/H)-V_H(1/2)
 = (1/2)F''(1/2)H^-2+O(H^-3).                         (6.12)
```

For `h` even, `H` is odd. The two nearest orbit representatives lie at distances `1/(2H)` and `3/(2H)` from the center. Hence

```text
V_H(1/2+3/(2H))-V_H(1/2+1/(2H))
 = F''(1/2)H^-2+O(H^-3).                              (6.13)
```

Therefore

```text
kappa_even = 2*kappa_odd                              (6.14)
```

for this center-nearest reflection-symmetric comparison.

The `H^-2 K(alpha)` correction contributes only

```text
H^-2 [K(alpha+1/H)-K(alpha)] = O(H^-3),                (6.15)
```

provided `K` has a uniform `C^1` bound. This is the precise sense in which the common individual `h^-2` correction cancels in a difference.

### 6.2 The cancellation that is not valid

For a general adjacent pair,

```text
H^-1[G(alpha+1/H)-G(alpha)]
 = H^-2 G'(alpha)+O(H^-3).                             (6.16)
```

There is no reflection reason for `G'(alpha)` to vanish unless `alpha=1/2`, and for an individual branch (6.5) it is generically nonzero even in the limiting formula.

Consequently, after subtracting only the `F`-difference, the residual is generically still `Theta(H^-2)`. A global estimate `O(H^(-2-delta))` is false without an additional branch identity forcing `G'` to vanish or explicitly incorporating the `G`-difference into the leading coefficient.

This agrees with the earlier audit: the parity factor is a center-lattice effect; it is not a global all-cell cancellation theorem.

## 7. The first uniform estimate that fails

The first false or unsupported line in the proposed proof is the replacement

```text
H^-1 [G(alpha+1/H)-G(alpha)] = O(H^(-2-delta))         (7.1)
```

uniformly over nonmirror adjacent cells.

The correct generic estimate is

```text
H^-1 [G(alpha+1/H)-G(alpha)]
  = H^-2 G'(alpha)+O(H^-3).                            (7.2)
```

Thus one has only two legitimate options:

```text
(a) include G'(alpha) in the explicit leading adjacent-gap coefficient; or
(b) restrict to a reflection-symmetric center comparison, where G'(1/2)=0.
```

Even option (b) still needs a quantitative theorem with explicit constants:

```text
sup_K |partial_z^ell R_(r,s)(z)|
  <= C_K (1/r+1/s)^3,        ell=0,1,2,               (7.3)

|tau| >= c0,
|Phi''(xi)| >= c0,                                      (7.4)

uniform C^1 bounds for K(alpha),                        (7.5)
```

on all limiting critical branches used by the cell-squeeze argument.

The present repository has an `O(1/h)` conjugation-level control, while the observed margin is `Theta(h^-2)`. That control cannot prove the tail. The two-order expansion above identifies the terms that must be subtracted, but no certified constants for (7.3)-(7.5) are currently banked.

There is also an edge issue. The two-long-block expansion is uniform only when `r,s` both tend to infinity. Cells with `min(r,s)=O(1)` require a one-sided version of (2.2), keeping the short block exact and expanding only the long block. Their leading scales are much more widely separated, so they are not expected to be the bottleneck, but an all-height proof must state and prove this split rather than use the bulk expansion at the edge.

No rigorous numerical threshold `h0` follows until the relevant `F''(1/2)`, the `C^3` norm of `F`, the `C^2` norm of `G`, and the constant in (7.3) are interval-certified. The finite Arb verification through `h=60` does not supply those tail constants.

## 8. Why Sturm or total positivity does not presently close the problem

There is a successful tridiagonal argument for `N_h` itself. Writing `N_h` as the determinant of a real symmetric tridiagonal pencil, the derivative pencil is positive definite on the real axis. This proves every real zero simple; the three-roots-per-strip count then excludes repeated nonreal roots.

The same mechanism does not apply to the reflected critical polynomial `J_h(u)`.

1. `J_h` is obtained after differentiating a determinant ratio and eliminating the reflection variable. It is not itself the determinant of the same monotone Hermitian pencil.
2. The critical equation can be written schematically as

   ```text
   tr(M_h(X)^-1 M_h'(X)) = 3 q_h'(X)/q_h(X),           (8.1)
   ```

   but away from real zero-free intervals the resolvent is complex and has no fixed sign.
3. The roots relevant to `[CRIT-2H]` are not known all real, and the critical values are generally complex. Classical Sturm separation and oscillation count real zeros; they do not separate the images of complex critical points under `f_h`.
4. No scalar three-term recurrence

   ```text
   J_(h+1)(u)=(a_h u+b_h)J_h(u)-c_h J_(h-1)(u),
   c_h>0,                                               (8.2)
   ```

   is available. The exact propagation closes only in a growing or bounded-rank jet system before elimination, and the signs needed for a totally positive production matrix are lost under differentiation and reflection quotienting.
5. Even real-rootedness and interlacing of `J_h` would prove critical-point simplicity, not pairwise distinctness of squared critical values. The latter is a two-point image-separation statement.

A viable direct Sturm route would therefore require a genuinely new representation: either `J_h` as a characteristic polynomial of an oscillatory matrix, together with a monotone formula for the squared critical value along its eigenvalue branches, or a sign-regular two-point kernel. Neither is present in the current structure.

## 10. Crisp verdict

The WKB/Euler-Maclaurin route is viable only after a correction to the proposed claim.

The exact recurrence gives a universal limiting cell function

```text
Phi(z)=z^-3 phi(-z)phi(z)+z^3 gamma(-z)gamma(z),
```

and a universal two-order multiplier. The common symmetric relative `h^-2` term does cancel in adjacent differences. This rigorously explains why individual finite-height corrections of order `h^-2` need not destroy the center parity law.

But an antisymmetric first correction survives for individual branches, and its adjacent variation is itself `Theta(h^-2)`. Hence there is no global `O(h^(-2-delta))` residual over every nonmirror cell pair after subtracting only the limiting `F`-difference. Reflection yields the odd/even factor `2` only for the center-nearest reflection-symmetric comparison.

Therefore:

```text
ALL-HEIGHT [CRIT-2H]: NOT PROVED.
CENTER PARITY MECHANISM: ANALYTICALLY IDENTIFIED.
GLOBAL ADJACENT-CANCELLATION CLAIM: FALSE AS STATED.
FIRST MISSING THEOREM: explicit uniform C^2 transfer remainder through order 3,
plus nonvanishing of the corrected branchwise h^-2 coefficient.
STURM/TOTAL-POSITIVITY ALTERNATIVE: no viable representation currently known.
```
