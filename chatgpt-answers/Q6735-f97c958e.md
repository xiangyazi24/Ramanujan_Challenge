# q57 LASTSTAND: dyadic family root count

## Verdict

I do not obtain a proof of FR_eta from the currently banked arithmetic inputs. The strongest non-circular route is a bilinear addition-law plus a family-averaged cancellation statement. The missing statement is essentially the same quenched growing-gap anti-coexistence object identified by the terminal analyses.

The transpose duality and solution-space reformulation are consistent with the stated facts; neither is false, but neither alone gives the needed exponent saving.

## Attempt 1: addition law across dyadic blocks

Use the proved identity

N_{h+d}(x) = N_d(x+h) N_{h+1}(x) - (x+h+1)^6 N_{d-1}(x+h+1) N_h(x).

A root in two different heights gives a bilinear relation between four lower-height evaluations. The hope is that a large set of roots in many d in (D/2,D] would force too many simultaneous vanishing relations.

The obstruction is quantitative: the existing degree information only bounds each N_d separately by O(d). Eliminating x between two height equations produces resultants whose degree is O(D^2). The number of possible dyadic pairs is also O(D^2). Thus the resultant argument has no saving: it reproduces the degree-sum scale.

Minimal repair: prove a rank drop statement for the family of translated polynomials, for example that a positive proportion of pairs (d,e) have resultant degree/effective root intersection bounded by O(D^{1+theta}) with theta<1. This is exactly an arithmetic correlation statement absent from padded words.

## Attempt 2: h-recursion amplification

For fixed x, the N_h satisfy a nonautonomous three-term recursion in h. A Vandermonde idea would use many heights to force rigidity.

Failure point: the recursion controls a single trajectory in h, but FR_eta needs a statement after summing over x and over a dyadic interval of heights. Applying restart/zero counting in h gives O(D^(2/3)) per row, and summing over N rows gives N D^(2/3), much larger than D^2 in the relevant range.

Minimal repair: a horizontal large-sieve theorem for this nonautonomous recurrence:

number of pairs (x,h) with N_h(x)=0, D/2<h<=D is O(D^(2-eta)).

That theorem would directly be FR_eta; it cannot be obtained by one-row zero counting.

## Attempt 3: additive character sums

The per-height bound |S_h(t)| <= (4h-1)sqrt(p) is compatible with square-root cancellation for fixed h.

Failure point: summing absolute values over h<=D gives a loss of order D. There is no cancellation between heights in the supplied estimate. The naive bound is therefore roughly D^2 sqrt(p), far above the root-count scale.

Minimal repair: a mixed (x,h) estimate, not fixed-height estimates:

sum_{h~D} |S_h(t)|^2 << D p (or stronger with saving).

The h-recursion is the only plausible mechanism for such a bound.

## Attempt 4: run rigidity

The fresh observation is correct: if r and r+1 both lie in Z_d, then xi_r is constrained by an eigenvector condition involving M_r^(-1) M_{r+d}. A long run gives a chain of algebraic constraints.

This kills the interval padding mechanism. However, it does not kill the scattered adversary: one can distribute primitive pairs over many different d without creating long runs in any single Z_d.

Minimal repair: upgrade run rigidity to scattered rigidity, namely a theorem saying that a dyadic family of many first returns forces either a long run or an impossible algebraic subsystem.

## Sanity checks

The transpose duality claim is valid under the given hypotheses: for fixed r, Delta_{r,h} is a linear combination of two shifted recurrence solutions, so recurrence zero-counting applies in h. Its weakness is summation over r.

The solution-space reformulation is also valid: returns correspond to proportional distinguished solutions. The warning is also valid: the bad-set orbit reformulation is circular unless an independent sparsity/cancellation input is introduced.

## Proposed next theorem

The clean target is a quenched family theorem:

For D between sqrt(N) and N/2,

sum_{D/2<d<=D} |Z_d| << D^(2-eta).

A viable proof likely needs one of:

1. addition-law induced rank rigidity across many heights;

1. a horizontal large sieve for the h-recursion;

1. modular-form input from the unapplied quarter-value/self-twist/triple-pole package producing cancellation that padded words cannot imitate.

The honest triple poles are promising because they prevent fake poles and may make multi-height interpolation possible, but I do not see a complete inequality from them alone.

## Least confident step

The least confident step is the claim that a horizontal large-sieve inequality should follow from the h-recursion. The recursion gives structure, but converting that structure into family cancellation is the missing analytic theorem, not a formal consequence.

## Possible blind spot in the question

The blind spot may be assuming that scattered pairing is the final adversary after interval padding is removed. The actual arithmetic obstruction might be neither runs nor intervals: it may be a low-complexity but highly dispersed algebraic family of first returns. A theorem excluding that family is stronger than run rigidity and is probably the real endpoint.
