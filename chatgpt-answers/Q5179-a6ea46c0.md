# P2.7 Kummer twist: explicit recurrence and an exact no-intertwiner theorem

## Executive result

Let

\[
Q(n)=946n^2-731n+153,
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
M(n)={}&104060n^6+127710n^5+12788n^4-34525n^3\\
&-8482n^2+3298n+1071,
\end{aligned}
\]

and

\[
N(n)=3784n^5-1032n^4-1925n^3+853n^2+328n-184.
\]

For Zudilin's sequence \(b_n\), put

\[
k_n=64^{-n}\frac{(5/2)_n}{n!},
\qquad
h_n=k_nb_n.
\]

Then the exact twisted recurrence is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&2^{22}Q(n)(2n+1)(n+1)^4h_{n+1}\\
&\quad-2^{15}(2n+5)M(n)h_n\\
&\quad+2^8(2n+3)(2n+5)N(n)h_{n-1}\\
&\quad-Q(n+1)(n-1)^2(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+5)h_{n-2}=0,
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1}
\]

for \(n\ge2\).

Its characteristic polynomial is

\[
\rho^3-\frac{55}{64}\rho^2+rac1{2048}\rho-rac1{2^{20}}
=\frac{4(64\rho)^3-220(64\rho)^2+8(64\rho)-1}{2^{20}},
\tag{2}
\]

so its three Poincaré roots are exactly those of P2.7. Since

\[
k_n\sim \frac{64^{-n}n^{3/2}}{\Gamma(5/2)},
\]

the Zudilin power \(-3/2\) becomes \(0\). Thus the proposed twist really does remove the previously known root-and-power obstruction.

Nevertheless, the requested rational Ore intertwiner still does **not** exist. More strongly:

> **Theorem.** There are no nonzero \(U,V\in\mathbb Q(n)\langle S\rangle\), with \(\operatorname{ord}U<3\), such that
> \[
> L_{27}U=V L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}.
> \]
> Consequently, there are no rational functions \(u_0(n),u_1(n),u_2(n)\)—of degree at most \(10\), or of any degree—such that
> \[
> q_n=u_0(n)h_n+u_1(n)h_{n+1}+u_2(n)h_{n+2}
> \]
> for every \(n\ge0\).

The obstruction is the determinant line of the rank-three difference module. After the Kummer twist, the source and target have the same characteristic roots and the same Birkhoff powers, but their companion determinants differ by a rational function that is **not** a shift coboundary \(g(n+1)/g(n)\). Its divisor has total multiplicity \(+12\) on the integer shift orbit and \(-12\) on the half-integer shift orbit. A rational shift coboundary has total multiplicity zero on every orbit.

The complete Sage program in §6 derives (1), verifies the characteristic polynomial, computes the determinant obstruction exactly, and performs a direct degree-\(10\) polynomial-intertwiner nullspace check.

# 1. Derivation of the twisted recurrence

The original recurrence is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&2Q(n)(2n+1)(n+1)^3b_{n+1}-2M(n)b_n\\
&\quad+2nN(n)b_{n-1}
-Q(n+1)n(n-1)^3b_{n-2}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

The one-step ratio of the twisting factor is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_{n+1}}{k_n}
=\frac{2n+5}{128(n+1)}.}
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{k_{n+1}}{k_{n-1}}
=\frac{(2n+3)(2n+5)}{2^{14}n(n+1)},
\tag{7}
\]

and

\[
\frac{k_{n+1}}{k_{n-2}}
=\frac{(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+5)}
{2^{21}(n-1)n(n+1)}.
\tag{8}
\]

Substitute \(b_j=h_j/k_j\) into (5) and multiply by \(k_{n+1}\). This gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&2Q(n)(2n+1)(n+1)^3h_{n+1}\\
&\quad-\frac{(2n+5)M(n)}{64(n+1)}h_n\\
&\quad+\frac{(2n+3)(2n+5)N(n)}{2^{13}(n+1)}h_{n-1}\\
&\quad-\frac{Q(n+1)(n-1)^2(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+5)}
{2^{21}(n+1)}h_{n-2}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Multiplying by \(2^{21}(n+1)\) yields (1).

## 1.1 Poincaré polynomial

The four coefficients in (1) have degree \(7\). Their leading coefficients are, in the order \(h_{n+1},h_n,h_{n-1},h_{n-2}\),

\[
2^{23}\cdot946,\qquad
-2^{16}\cdot104060,\qquad
2^{10}\cdot3784,\qquad
-8\cdot946.
\]

Using

\[
104060=110\cdot946,
\qquad
3784=4\cdot946,
\]

and substituting \(h_n\sim\rho^n\), one obtains exactly (2).

## 1.2 Formal power

If a Zudilin mode has

\[
b_n^{(j)}\sim c_j\mu_j^n n^{-3/2},
\]

then

\[
h_n^{(j)}
\sim \frac{c_j}{\Gamma(5/2)}
\left(\frac{\mu_j}{64}\right)^n n^0.
\tag{10}
\]

Thus the twist aligns both formal invariants at infinity: the exponential roots and the powers.

# 2. The aligned twisted operator

Shift (1) by \(n\mapsto n+2\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
z_0(n)={}&-Q(n+3)(n+1)^2(2n+5)(2n+7)(2n+9),\\
z_1(n)={}&2^8(2n+7)(2n+9)N(n+2),\\
z_2(n)={}&-2^{15}(2n+9)M(n+2),\\
z_3(n)={}&2^{22}Q(n+2)(2n+5)(n+3)^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Then

\[
z_0(n)h_n+z_1(n)h_{n+1}+z_2(n)h_{n+2}+z_3(n)h_{n+3}=0,
\tag{12}
\]

and the monic positive-shift operator is

\[
\boxed{
L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}
=\frac{z_0(n)}{z_3(n)}
+\frac{z_1(n)}{z_3(n)}S
+\frac{z_2(n)}{z_3(n)}S^2+S^3.}
\tag{13}
\]

Any putative intertwiner may be reduced on the right modulo \(L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}\):

\[
U=H L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}+U_0,
\qquad \operatorname{ord}U_0<3.
\tag{14}
\]

The term \(H L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}\) acts as zero on every source solution. Hence an ansatz

\[
U=u_0(n)+u_1(n)S+u_2(n)S^2
\tag{15}
\]

is not a restriction; it is the canonical reduced form of every possible scalar Ore map.

# 3. Exact determinant obstruction

For a monic order-three operator

\[
L=\ell_0(n)+\ell_1(n)S+\ell_2(n)S^2+S^3,
\]

the companion system for

\[
X_n=(u_n,u_{n+1},u_{n+2})^T
\]

has determinant

\[
\det C_L(n)=-\ell_0(n).
\tag{16}
\]

## 3.1 Source determinant

From (11)–(13),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_Z(n):=\det C_Z(n)
=\frac{Q(n+3)(n+1)^2(2n+7)(2n+9)}
{2^{22}Q(n+2)(n+3)^4}.}
\tag{17}
\]

## 3.2 P2.7 determinant

Put

\[
R(n)=946n^2+4515n+5399.
\tag{18}
\]

The quadratic in \(A(n)\) is \(R(n+1)\), because

\[
946n^2+6407n+10860=R(n+1).
\]

Therefore (4) gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{27}(n):=\det C_{27}(n)
=\frac{(n+3)^4(n+4)^6R(n)}
{2^{10}(2n+5)^4(2n+7)^3(2n+9)^3R(n+1)}.}
\tag{19}
\]

## 3.3 Their quotient

Dividing (19) by (17), and writing the even linear factors as half-integer factors, gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta_{27}(n)}{\Delta_Z(n)}
={}&\frac{(n+3)^8(n+4)^6}
{(n+1)^2
 (n+\frac52)^4(n+\frac72)^4(n+\frac92)^4}\\
&\times\frac{R(n)}{R(n+1)}
\frac{Q(n+2)}{Q(n+3)}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{20}
\]

The two quadratic ratios are themselves shift coboundaries:

\[
\frac{R(n)}{R(n+1)}
=\frac{(1/R)(n+1)}{(1/R)(n)},
\qquad
\frac{Q(n+2)}{Q(n+3)}
=\frac{(1/Q(n+2))(n+1)}{1/Q(n+2)}.
\tag{21}
\]

The remaining linear divisor has:

- total multiplicity
  \[
  8+6-2=12
  \]
  on the integer shift orbit \(n+\mathbb Z\);
- total multiplicity
  \[
  -4-4-4=-12
  \]
  on the half-integer shift orbit \(n+\frac12+\mathbb Z\).

## Lemma — orbit-sum criterion

If \(g(n)\in\mathbb Q(n)^\times\), then the divisor of

\[
\frac{g(n+1)}{g(n)}
\]

has total multiplicity zero on every orbit of irreducible polynomials under integer translation \(f(n)\mapsto f(n+k)\).

### Proof

Write the valuation of \(g\) along the orbit as a finitely supported sequence \((v_k)_{k\in\mathbb Z}\). The valuation of \(g(n+1)/g(n)\) is \(v_{k-1}-v_k\). Summing over \(k\) telescopes to zero. ∎

By (20), the quotient \(\Delta_{27}/\Delta_Z\) violates this criterion. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta_{27}(n)}{\Delta_Z(n)}
\ne \frac{g(n+1)}{g(n)}
\quad\text{for every }g\in\mathbb Q(n)^\times.}
\tag{22}
\]

# 4. Why this proves that no intertwiner exists

Both operators have one horizontal Newton edge at \(n=\infty\), with edge polynomial

\[
4x^3-220x^2+8x-1.
\tag{23}
\]

This cubic is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) by the rational-root test. A nontrivial factorization of either order-three Ore operator over \(\mathbb Q(n)\) would factor its edge polynomial. Consequently both rank-three difference modules are irreducible.

Suppose that a reduced nonzero \(U\) satisfied

\[
L_{27}U=V L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}.
\tag{24}
\]

It would induce a nonzero morphism from the source rank-three difference module to the P2.7 rank-three module. Irreducibility makes that morphism an isomorphism. In companion bases there would then be a matrix

\[
G(n)\in\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb Q(n))
\]

such that

\[
G(n+1)C_Z(n)=C_{27}(n)G(n).
\tag{25}
\]

Taking determinants gives

\[
\frac{\Delta_{27}(n)}{\Delta_Z(n)}
=\frac{\det G(n+1)}{\det G(n)},
\tag{26}
\]

contradicting (22). Therefore \(U=0\) in reduced order.

This obstruction is strictly stronger than matching the Poincaré roots and Birkhoff powers. Those are local formal invariants at infinity; (20) is the global determinant-line class in

\[
\mathbb Q(n)^\times/
\left\{g(n+1)/g(n):g\in\mathbb Q(n)^\times\right\}.
\]

# 5. Consequence for the distinguished sequence \(q_n\)

The twisted Zudilin denominator begins from

\[
b_0=1,\qquad b_1=7,\qquad b_2=163.
\]

Its dominant ratio is a degree-three algebraic number. Therefore it cannot satisfy a rational recurrence of order less than three: the limiting characteristic polynomial of such a recurrence would have degree at most two over \(\mathbb Q\). Thus \(L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}\) is the minimal annihilator of \(h_n\).

If rational functions \(u_0,u_1,u_2\) gave

\[
q_n=u_0(n)h_n+u_1(n)h_{n+1}+u_2(n)h_{n+2}
\tag{27}
\]

for every \(n\), then \(L_{27}U\) would annihilate \(h\). Minimality would force \(L_{27}U\) to be right-divisible by \(L_Z^{\mathrm{tw}}\), contradicting the theorem above.

Hence the requested degree-\(10\) search has the exact answer

\[
\boxed{\text{no solution}.}
\]

An intertwiner with coefficients in a larger hypergeometric/Kummer extension could evade the rational determinant-class obstruction. A rational Ore intertwiner cannot.

def linear_orbit_sum(f, residue):
    """
    Total divisor multiplicity in the linear-factor orbit
    n + residue + Z.  residue is taken modulo Z.
    """
    f = K(f)
    total = ZZ(0)
    for sign, pol in [(1, Rn(f.numerator())), (-1, Rn(f.denominator()))]:

# 5. Small custom Ore engine and direct polynomial-U search

ZERO = K(0)


def trim(P):
    P = [K(c) for c in P]
    while len(P) > 1 and P[-1] == 0:
        P.pop()
    return P


def op_add(P, Q):
    m = max(len(P), len(Q))
    out = [ZERO]*m
    for i in range(m):
        if i < len(P):
            out[i] += P[i]
        if i < len(Q):
            out[i] += Q[i]
    return trim(out)


def op_neg(P):
    return trim([-c for c in P])


def op_sub(P, Q):
    return op_add(P, op_neg(Q))


def op_mul(P, Q):
    """Ore product with S*f(n)=f(n+1)*S."""
    P = trim(P)
    Q = trim(Q)
    out = [ZERO]*(len(P) + len(Q) - 1)
    for i, pi in enumerate(P):
        for j, qj in enumerate(Q):
            out[i + j] += pi*sh(qj, i)
    return trim(out)


def right_quo_rem(P, L):
    """P = Q*L + R, ord(R) < ord(L)."""
    P = trim(P)
    L = trim(L)
    r = len(L) - 1
    if L[-1] == 0:
        raise ZeroDivisionError("zero leading coefficient")
    Qop = [ZERO]*max(1, len(P) - r)
    while len(P) - 1 >= r and not (len(P) == 1 and P[0] == 0):
        d = (len(P) - 1) - r
        c = P[-1]/sh(L[-1], d)
        if d >= len(Qop):
            Qop += [ZERO]*(d + 1 - len(Qop))
        Qop[d] += c
        term = [ZERO]*d + [c]
        P = op_sub(P, op_mul(term, L))
    return trim(Qop), trim(P)


# Sanity check for right division.

assert trim(quo) == trim(L27)


def coefficient_matrix(columns, rem_order=3):
    """Convert rational-function remainder columns to a QQ matrix."""
    rows = []
    for j in range(rem_order):
        common = Rn(1)
        for col in columns:
            common = lcm(common, Rn(col[j].denominator()))

        polys = []
        for col in columns:
            val = K(common)*col[j]
            assert val.denominator().degree() == 0
            polys.append(Rn(val))

        maxdeg = max((p.degree() for p in polys if p != 0), default=-1)
        for e in range(maxdeg + 1):
            row = [QQ(p[e]) if e <= p.degree() else QQ(0) for p in polys]
            if any(c != 0 for c in row):
                rows.append(row)

    if not rows:
        return matrix(QQ, 0, len(columns))
    return matrix(QQ, rows)


def search_fixed_denominator(max_degree, denominator=1):
    """
    Search U=sum_{i=0}^2 P_i(n)/denominator(n) S^i,
    deg(P_i)<=max_degree.

    The all-rational no-go theorem above is stronger; this is a direct
    finite-dimensional verification for any chosen common denominator.
    """
    denominator = K(denominator)
    columns = []
    labels = []

    for i in range(3):
        for k in range(max_degree + 1):
            U = [ZERO]*i + [K(n**k)/denominator]
            product = op_mul(L27, U)
            _, remainder = right_quo_rem(product, LZ)
            remainder = trim(remainder)
            remainder += [ZERO]*(3 - len(remainder))
            columns.append(remainder[:3])
            labels.append((i, k))

    mat = coefficient_matrix(columns, rem_order=3)
    ker = mat.right_kernel()
    print(
        "fixed denominator =", denominator,
        "; numerator degree <=", max_degree,
        "; nullity =", ker.dimension()
    )

    if ker.dimension() != 0:
        v = ker.basis()[0]
        u = [Rn(0), Rn(0), Rn(0)]
        for coeff, (i, k) in zip(v, labels):
            u[i] += coeff*n**k
        print("candidate numerators:", u)
    return ker

# 7. What remains viable

The twist \(64^{-n}(5/2)_n/n!\) is mathematically meaningful: it aligns the complete formal data at infinity. The failure occurs at the next invariant, the determinant line.

A possible bridge would therefore have to leave the category requested in the question. Examples include:

1. an operator with hypergeometric rather than rational coefficients;
2. a nonlocal binomial, Mellin, or Cauchy transform;
3. a higher-rank matrix transform whose determinant contains an additional Kummer character;
4. a normal-function or Barnes-integral construction defined directly for the P2.7 initial vectors.

The present calculation shows exactly why matching roots and powers is necessary but not sufficient: the source and target still represent different rank-three difference modules.
