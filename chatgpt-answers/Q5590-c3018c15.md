# Arbitrary-order Apéry endpoint algebra: a fixed-order finite-generation theorem, an exact recurrence obstruction, and the first unseen primitive

## Executive verdict

Accept the audited grade-seven statements in the question.  For every prime
\(p\ge 11\), write

```text
Delta_p = b_(p-1)-1,                    v_p(Delta_p) >= 3,
H_p     = b_p-5+7 Delta_p,              v_p(H_p) >= 5.
```

At the next displayed layer the proved H6 identity gives

```text
p^6 W_p = (24*769/5) Delta_p^2          (mod p^7),
```

and the fixed direct/reflected elimination is

```text
(1680+2472 Delta_p^2) D_7 - 25 Z_7
     = 1655 (b_(p+r)/p)                 (mod p^7).
```

The correct conclusion is **positive, but weaker than bounded all-grade
closure**.

1. **Fixed-order finite generation is provable.**  For every fixed precision
   \(K\) and every fixed quotient range \(m\le M\), the binomial block
   expansion puts all endpoint values \(b_{mp},b_{mp-1}\), and all direct and
   reflected target rows modulo \(p^K\), in a finite filtered
   \(\mathbf Z_p\)-algebra generated by explicit complete-block harmonic
   coordinates and finitely many shifted-recurrence jets.  The generators,
   polynomials, and denominators depend on \(K,M\), but not on \(p\), outside
   a finite set of fixed primes.

2. **The number of generators is not bounded in \(K\) by the formal block
   algebra.**  A new complete-block logarithmic primitive can occur at every
   odd effective grade

   ```text
   3, 5, 7, 9, ... .
   ```

   In particular, modulo \(p^8\) the first previously invisible candidate is

   ```text
   xi_7(p) = (1/p) sum_(a=1)^(p-1) 1/a^6       (mod p).
   ```

   It occurs with a nonzero universal coefficient in a generic complete
   factorial block.  It is not a polynomial in the formal weight-three and
   weight-five power-sum coordinates.

3. **The grade-seven success is therefore not accidental, but it is not an
   induction step.**  A congruence modulo \(p^7\) sees effective grades at
   most six.  Grade six has the decomposable contribution \(3+3\), hence the
   observed square \(\Delta_p^2\).  The genuinely new grade-seven primitive
   is multiplied by \(p^7\) and is invisible modulo \(p^7\).  It first enters
   a calculation modulo \(p^8\).

4. **The Apéry recurrence alone cannot close the tower.**  At every crossing
   \(n=mp-1\), its leading coefficient is \((mp)^3\).  Modulo \(p^K\) the
   recurrence leaves three new \(p\)-adic digits of \(b_{mp}\) free.  The
   binomial/hypergeometric endpoint identities are exactly the extra input
   which selected the observed grade-three, grade-five, and grade-six
   coordinates.

5. **There is a rigorous induction criterion.**  At each grade one must pass
   to the indecomposable quotient of the filtered endpoint algebra and test
   whether the primitive part affecting the target lies in the module
   generated by the available endpoint observations.  Uniform elimination
   outside finitely many fixed primes requires a **constant Bezout/Fitting
   certificate**, not merely generic rank over a rational function field.
   Grade seven verifies this criterion at effective weight six.  The criterion
   has not been verified at effective weight seven.

6. **No failure of the grade-eight Apéry elimination is proved here.**  The
   new symbol \(\xi_7\) is a first formal obstruction, not yet an actual
   counterexample: the Apéry endpoint projection might annihilate it, or the
   direct and reflected rows might detect and eliminate its one-dimensional
   span.  That is the next exact computation.

7. **Even an all-grade local theorem would not prove P3.2 by itself.**  It
   could merely reconstruct the existing common cofactor in every local
   precision.  The global theorem still needs nonzero carriers

   ```text
   R_n^(e_K) | C_(n,K),
   log |C_(n,K)| <= A_K n + E_K log n,
   A_K/e_K -> 0,
   ```

   or an equivalent separation theorem.  Local reconstruction without such a
   carrier is compatible with arbitrary linear logarithmic mass.

The strongest safe research conclusion is therefore:

> The Apéry endpoint tower is finitely generated at each fixed precision in a
> growing filtered block-harmonic algebra.  The order-seven law proves the
> decomposable weight-six case.  An all-grade theorem is equivalent to a
> sequence of primitive-module/Fitting-ideal calculations plus uniform height
> control; it does not follow formally from the recurrence, the binomial block
> expansion, or H6.

# 1. Exact setup and grade convention

The Apéry numbers satisfy

\[
 (n+1)^3b_{n+1}=P(n)b_n-n^3b_{n-1},
 \qquad
 P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5,
\tag{1.1}
\]

and have summand

\[
 t(n,k)=\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{k}^2,
 \qquad b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n t(n,k).
\tag{1.2}
\]

There are two related indices which must not be conflated.

```text
precision K:   congruence modulo p^K;
effective grade w: coefficient multiplying p^w.
```

Thus a law modulo \(p^7\) determines grades \(0,1,\ldots,6\).  A new
coordinate whose first contribution is \(p^7u_7\) is absent from every
modulo-\(p^7\) formula.  This one-index shift is the central bookkeeping fact
behind the present audit.

For endpoint bookkeeping it is useful to normalize the first exposed
coordinates as

\[
 \delta_{3,p}=\frac{\Delta_p}{p^3}\in\mathbf Z_p,
 \qquad
 \eta_{5,p}=\frac{H_p}{p^5}\in\mathbf Z_p.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The H6 theorem says that the target-relevant grade-six residue is a fixed
quadratic polynomial in \(\delta_{3,p}\).  It does **not** say that the
primitive part at grade seven is generated by \(\delta_{3,p},\eta_{5,p}\).

# 2. A recurrence-only obstruction: three free digits at every block crossing

The following lemma is exact and shows why no induction can be extracted from
(1.1) alone.

## Lemma 2.1 — singular-crossing torsor

Fix integers \(M\ge1\), \(K\ge4\), a prime \(p>M\), and \(1\le m\le M\).
Consider solutions of the Apéry recurrence modulo \(p^K\).  Suppose the values
through \(u_{mp-1}\) satisfy the recurrence through the index \(mp-2\), and
suppose the compatibility numerator

\[
 P(mp-1)u_{mp-1}-(mp-1)^3u_{mp-2}
\]

is divisible by \(p^3\).  Then the equation at \(mp-1\) determines
\(u_{mp}\) only modulo \(p^{K-3}\).  More precisely, if \(u_{mp}\) is one
solution, then

\[
 u_{mp}'=u_{mp}+p^{K-3}c,
 \qquad c\in\mathbf Z/p^3\mathbf Z,
\tag{2.1}
\]

is another solution modulo \(p^K\).  Each choice propagates uniquely from
\(mp+1\) through \((m+1)p-1\).

### Proof

At the singular crossing, (1.1) is

\[
 (mp)^3u_{mp}
 =P(mp-1)u_{mp-1}-(mp-1)^3u_{mp-2}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Because \(p\nmid m\), the left coefficient has valuation exactly three.
Replacing \(u_{mp}\) by (2.1) changes the left side by

\[
 (mp)^3p^{K-3}c=m^3p^Kc,
\]

which is zero modulo \(p^K\).  For the subsequent indices
\(mp,mp+1,\ldots,(m+1)p-2\), the coefficients \((n+1)^3\) are \(p\)-adic
units, so forward propagation is unique.  ∎

## Consequence

The recurrence module has a three-digit extension class at every block
boundary.  Therefore none of the following can be a recurrence-formal theorem:

```text
all endpoint defects are functions of Delta alone;
all endpoint defects are functions of Delta and H;
a fixed finite set of endpoint scalars controls every p-adic order.
```

The actual Apéry sequence is one distinguished lift inside this torsor.  Its
binomial sum, Dwork congruences, H6, and related harmonic identities select
that lift.  Any all-grade proof must use this special arithmetic at every new
primitive grade.

# 3. Exact factorial-block expansion

The positive fixed-order theorem starts from an elementary identity.

For \(0\le c<p\), \(0\le s<p\), define

\[
 U_c(p)=\prod_{a=1}^{p-1}\left(1+\frac{cp}{a}\right),
 \qquad
 U_{c,s}(p)=\prod_{a=1}^{s}\left(1+\frac{cp}{a}\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
 (cp+s)!
 =p^c c!\,(p-1)!^c\,s!
   \left(\prod_{j=1}^{c-1}U_j(p)\right)U_{c,s}(p).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Indeed, separate the multiples \(p,2p,\ldots,cp\), the \(c\) complete
nonzero-residue blocks, and the final partial block.

For

\[
 H_s^{(j)}=\sum_{a=1}^s\frac1{a^j},
\]

and \(p>K\), the \(p\)-adic logarithm gives the exact truncation

\[
 \log U_{c,s}(p)
 \equiv
 \sum_{j=1}^{K-1}
   \frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{j}c^jp^jH_s^{(j)}
 \pmod {p^K}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The same formula with \(s=p-1\) gives \(U_c(p)\).  Exponentiating (3.3)
expresses every block factor modulo \(p^K\) as a weighted Bell polynomial in
harmonic sums of total expansion grade below \(K\).

Now write

```text
n = mp+epsilon,       epsilon in {0,-1,r},
k = ap+s,             0 <= s < p.
```

Assume \(m\le M\), \(p>2M+K\).  Every factorial in the summand (1.2) has
quotient digit at most \(2M+1<p\), so (3.2) applies and there is no \(p^2\)
multiple to track.  Legendre's formula first extracts an explicit carry power
\(p^{\nu(m,a,s,\epsilon)}\).  The remaining unit is a product of finitely many
factors (3.1) with fixed integral exponents.  Terms with \(\nu\ge K\) vanish
modulo \(p^K\); every other term has a finite expansion from (3.3).

This proves the following theorem.

## Theorem 3.1 — fixed-order block-algebra finite generation

Fix \(K,M\ge1\).  There are:

- a finite set of rational primes \(S_{K,M}\);
- a finite filtered algebra \(\mathscr E_{K,M}\) over
  \(\mathbf Z[S_{K,M}^{-1}]\);
- finitely many explicit universal polynomials
  \(E_{K,m}^{0},E_{K,m}^{-},T_{K,m}^{D},T_{K,m}^{R}\);

such that, for every prime \(p\notin S_{K,M}\), \(p>2M+K\), every
\(1\le m\le M\), and every admissible \(0\le r<p\), evaluation of the block
harmonic symbols gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b_{mp}     &\equiv \operatorname{ev}_{p}(E_{K,m}^{0})\pmod {p^K},\\
 b_{mp-1}   &\equiv \operatorname{ev}_{p}(E_{K,m}^{-})\pmod {p^K},\\
 D_K(p,r)   &\equiv \operatorname{ev}_{p,r}(T_{K,m}^{D})\pmod {p^K},\\
 Z_K(p,r)   &\equiv \operatorname{ev}_{p,r}(T_{K,m}^{R})\pmod {p^K}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Here \(\mathscr E_{K,M}\) may be generated by:

1. complete harmonic sums \(H_{p-1}(s_1,\ldots,s_d)\) of total weight below
   \(K\);
2. the finitely many partial/weighted sums produced by the low digit \(s\);
3. equivalently, after summation-by-parts and recurrence reduction, a finite
   list of direct/reflected shifted-recurrence jets of order below \(K\).

A crude uniform bound is

```text
number of formal block-harmonic generators <= C_M (2K)^K,
logarithmic height of universal rational coefficients = O_M(K log K),
```

and one may take a common denominator dividing a fixed power of

```text
(2M)! K! .
```

### Proof

Use (3.2) in every factorial of (1.2), extract the explicit carry power, use
(3.3), and truncate.  For fixed \(K,M\) there are only finitely many quotient
blocks, compositions of harmonic weight, and carry patterns.  Products of
harmonic sums are reduced to strict multiple harmonic sums by the finite
stuffle identities.  Summing the low digit \(s\) produces only the finitely
many weighted patterns already present in the truncated expansion.  The
coefficients arise from powers \(c^j\), the factors \(1/j\), and exponential
coefficients \(1/e!\), all with \(j,e<K\).  This gives the claimed denominator
and height bounds.  ∎

## What this theorem does and does not say

It is a genuine finite-generation theorem at every fixed order.  It does not
say that \(\mathscr E_{K,M}\) is generated by the image of
\(\mathscr E_{K-1,M}\), nor that the number of indecomposable generators stays
bounded as \(K\to\infty\).

It also does not prove a polynomial **Archimedean** height for every evaluated
jet.  A recurrence jet may have fixed-order polynomial coefficients while its
value still contains an Apéry quantity of size \(\exp(\Theta(r))\).  The
polynomial-size representatives proved for the particular \(D_7,Z_7\) law
must be re-established at each future grade; they are not a formal consequence
of Theorem 3.1.

# 4. Complete-block logarithmic primitives

The first unseen obstruction can be isolated without expanding the whole
Apéry sum.

Put

\[
 S_j(p)=\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\frac1{a^j}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

## Lemma 4.1 — parity valuations and pairing

For \(1\le j\le p-3\),

```text
j even:  S_j(p) is in p Z_p,
j odd:   S_j(p) is in p^2 Z_p.
```

Moreover, for \(h\ge1\) and \(p>2h+3\),

\[
 2S_{2h-1}(p)+(2h-1)pS_{2h}(p)\equiv0\pmod {p^3}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Modulo \(p\), \(S_j\) is a nontrivial power sum on
\(\mathbf F_p^\times\), hence zero.  Pair \(a\) with \(p-a\) and expand

\[
 (p-a)^{-j}=(-1)^ja^{-j}(1-p/a)^{-j}.
\]

For odd \(j\), the first surviving term is \(jp/a^{j+1}\); the even power sum
is itself zero modulo \(p\), giving the extra factor \(p\).  Keeping one more
term for \(j=2h-1\) gives (4.2); the omitted term contains
\(p^2S_{2h+1}\), which is divisible by \(p^4\).  ∎

Define the new odd-grade complete-block residue

\[
 \xi_{2h+1}(p)=\frac{S_{2h}(p)}p\pmod p.
\tag{4.3}
\]

## Lemma 4.2 — coefficient of the new primitive

For fixed \(c\in\mathbf Z\), \(p\nmid2h\), the contribution of
\(S_{2h-1},S_{2h}\) to the associated grade \(2h+1\) part of
\(\log U_c(p)\) is

\[
 -p^{2h+1}
   \frac{c^{2h-1}(c+h)}{2h}\,
   \xi_{2h+1}(p).
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

The two relevant terms of (3.3) are

\[
 \frac{c^{2h-1}p^{2h-1}}{2h-1}S_{2h-1}
 -\frac{c^{2h}p^{2h}}{2h}S_{2h}.
\]

By (4.2),

\[
 S_{2h-1}\equiv-\frac{2h-1}{2}pS_{2h}\pmod {p^3}.
\]

Substitution and \(S_{2h}=p\xi_{2h+1}\pmod {p^2}\) give (4.4).  ∎

The first three coefficients are

```text
grade 3:  -c(c+1)/2       * xi_3,
grade 5:  -c^3(c+2)/4     * xi_5,
grade 7:  -c^5(c+3)/6     * xi_7.
```

For every positive block index \(c\), the grade-seven coefficient is nonzero
outside the fixed primes dividing \(6c(c+3)\).

# 5. Why the grade-seven law closes, and why it does not imply grade eight

The logarithm/exponential distinction is essential.  If a normalized block
transport has

\[
 L_p=\log\Phi_p=p^3L_{3,p}+p^5L_{5,p}+p^7L_{7,p}+\cdots,
\tag{5.1}
\]

then

\[
 \Phi_p
 =I+p^3L_{3,p}+p^5L_{5,p}
   +\frac12p^6L_{3,p}^2
   +p^7L_{7,p}+\cdots.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Modulo \(p^7\), the term \(p^7L_{7,p}\) is absent.  The only new raw term
made solely from the first primitive is the decomposable square

\[
 \frac12p^6L_{3,p}^2.
\]

This is exactly the filtration pattern detected by H6:

```text
new raw grade 6 = polynomial in the exposed grade-3 coordinate.
```

The numerical coefficient \(24\cdot769/5\) is Apéry-specific; the fact that a
quadratic term is the only visible new logarithmic product at this precision
is formal.

At the next precision, modulo \(p^8\), (4.4) with \(h=3\) contributes

\[
 -p^7\frac{c^5(c+3)}6\xi_7(p).
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is a new logarithmic direction before any Apéry-specific cancellation is
used.

## Proposition 5.1 — no bounded-generation theorem from formal factorial blocks

In the universal factorial-block algebra, the grade-seven symbol
\(\xi_7\) is not a polynomial in the grade-three and grade-five power-sum
symbols.

### Proof

Before evaluation at a prime, complete-block logarithms live in the symmetric
function algebra over \(\mathbf Q\), which is freely generated by its power
sums.  The three residues above arise from the independent formal power sums

```text
p_2, p_4, p_6.
```

The element \(p_6\) is algebraically independent of \(p_2,p_4\).  Equivalently,
one may specialize \(p_2=p_4=0\), \(p_6=1\).  Every polynomial in the first
two vanishes under this specialization, while the grade-seven term (5.3) does
not.  ∎

This is a formal obstruction, not a claim of algebraic independence for the
actual numerical values \(\xi_j(p)\) as \(p\) varies.  An Apéry-specific
identity may annihilate or detect the \(p_6\) direction.  H6 is precisely the
kind of special identity which is invisible in the free symmetric-function
algebra.

The correct grade-eight question is therefore not “does another scalar
appear?”  It is:

```text
What is the image of the weight-seven primitive quotient under
(a) the direct endpoint rows,
(b) the reflected endpoint rows,
(c) the target quotient row?
```

# 6. The exact filtered induction criterion

Let \(\mathscr E\) be the universal block algebra, filtered by effective
\(p\)-adic grade.  Let

\[
 \operatorname{gr}_w\mathscr E=F^w\mathscr E/F^{w+1}\mathscr E,
\]

and define its indecomposable quotient

\[
 Q_w=
 \frac{\operatorname{gr}_w\mathscr E}
 {\sum_{i=1}^{w-1}
   \operatorname{gr}_i\mathscr E\,
   \operatorname{gr}_{w-i}\mathscr E}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

After all lower grades have been subtracted, let

```text
e_(w,1),...,e_(w,s) in Q_w
```

be the primitive classes seen by the available endpoint observations, and let
`t_w` be the primitive class affecting the desired target quotient.

## Theorem 6.1 — primitive-module lifting criterion

Fix a grade \(w\).  Suppose all lower-grade target formulas are already
constructed.  A fixed-coefficient elimination at grade \(w\), valid for all
primes outside a finite fixed set, follows if there are polynomials
\(f_1,\ldots,f_s\) in the previously exposed coordinates and a nonzero fixed
integer \(\delta_w\) such that

\[
 \delta_w t_w=\sum_{i=1}^s f_i e_{w,i}
 \qquad\text{in }Q_w.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For every prime \(p\nmid\delta_w\), the relation lifts through the filtration
to a nonlinear fixed-coefficient formula for the target modulo
\(p^{w+1}\).

### Proof

Subtract the known weighted-homogeneous polynomial in lower coordinates from
each row.  Equation (6.2) eliminates the remaining primitive class in the
associated graded.  The error moves to filtration \(w+1\), where the already
constructed lower-grade formulas and one further subtraction complete the
lift.  Division is only by the fixed integer \(\delta_w\), so the exceptional
set is independent of \(p\).  ∎

## Fitting-ideal formulation

Choose a basis of \(Q_w\) and put the endpoint coefficient vectors into a
matrix \(C_w(U)\), where \(U\) denotes lower coordinates.  Uniform elimination
requires the appropriate determinantal/Fitting ideal to contain a fixed
integer:

\[
 \operatorname{Fitt}(C_w)\cap\mathbf Z\ne(0).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Merely proving that \(C_w(U)\) has full rank over
\(\mathbf Q(U)\) is insufficient.  A nonzero determinant polynomial can
vanish after evaluation for infinitely many primes.  Likewise, dividing by an
endpoint coordinate such as \(\Delta_p\), \(H_p\), or a new primitive is
invalid because that coordinate may vanish modulo \(p\).

The grade-seven fixed law is unusually strong because its target coefficient
is the constant

```text
1655 = 5*331,
```

and the other divisions introduce only the fixed exceptional primes

```text
7, 331, 769.
```

No division by

```text
1680+2472 Delta_p^2
```

is needed.  This is exactly the model that an all-grade induction must
reproduce.

# 7. What can be asserted at all grades

Combining Theorems 3.1 and 6.1 gives a rigorous conditional induction.

## Corollary 7.1 — all-grade closure under primitive and unit-ideal hypotheses

Assume that, for every \(w\ge3\):

1. the target-relevant quotient \(Q_w^{\rm tar}\) is finite free over a fixed
   localization of \(\mathbf Z\);
2. the endpoint observation module contains the target class as in (6.2);
3. the corresponding fixed integers \(\delta_w\) and polynomial degrees have
   controlled growth;
4. the lifted rows admit the required polynomial-height representatives.

Then direct/reflected target elimination exists at every fixed precision.

This is an induction theorem.  It is not yet an unconditional theorem for the
Apéry tower because hypotheses 1--4 are not known beyond effective weight six.

## No bounded primitive set from the present methods

Lemma 4.2 exhibits a candidate \(\xi_{2h+1}\) at every odd grade.  Therefore a
bounded primitive set would require infinitely many Apéry-specific projection
identities.  Neither the order-two recurrence nor the formal factorial-block
algebra supplies them.  The strongest believable pattern is growing
finite-generation, for example

```text
primitive target rank 0 at some even weights,
primitive target rank <= 1 at odd weights,
```

but even this rank-one pattern is a conjecture.  The unrestricted finite
multiple-harmonic-sum algebra has many more primitive directions; any rank-one
result must come from the special Apéry endpoint projection.

# 8. Hostile audit of all divisions and uniformity claims

## 8.1 Fixed primes already present

The current grade-seven law uses inverses of fixed integers only.  The safe
exception list contains at least

```text
2, 3, 5, 7, 331, 769.
```

The stated theorem starts at \(p\ge11\) and separately removes
\(331,769\).  Their total contribution to any logarithmic target mass is
\(O(1)\).

## 8.2 Fixed-order block expansion

At precision \(K\) and quotient bound \(M\), the proof uses:

```text
1/j, 1/j!        for j<K,
1/c!             for c<=2M,
1/a              for 1<=a<p-1.
```

The last denominators are \(p\)-adic units.  The first two require only the
fixed exclusion \(p\le\max(K,2M)\).  No denominator depending on an unknown
endpoint coordinate is introduced.

## 8.3 Recurrence jets

Taylor expansion of a shifted recurrence row is legitimate only while every
shifted denominator is a \(p\)-adic unit.  For \(0\le r<p\) and a fixed jet
order \(K\), one must keep the stencil inside the first block or use a cleared
continuant lattice at the endpoint.  Crossing \(p\) without changing lattices
silently divides by \(p^3\) and loses exactly the three digits of Lemma 2.1.

## 8.4 Generic rank is not uniform rank

A determinant which is a nonzero polynomial in \(\delta_{3,p},\eta_{5,p}\)
is not automatically a unit.  Uniformity outside finitely many fixed primes
requires a Bezout relation producing a nonzero integer, as in (6.2)--(6.3).
This is the most important all-grade division audit.

## 8.5 Polynomial coefficient height versus value height

Fixed-order recurrence coefficients can have height \(n^{O_K(1)}\) while the
sequence values they multiply have height \(\exp(\Theta(n))\).  A claimed
polynomial representative must display the cancellation of all full-height
values.  The existence of a formula in a finite jet algebra does not prove
that cancellation.

# 9. Local algebra versus the global non-stabilization problem

Let

\[
 R_n=\prod_{\substack{n/2<p\le n\\p\mid b_n}}p.
\]

Suppose an order-\(K\) local tower determines the actual common cofactor
\(A_n=b_n/R_n\) modulo \(R_n^K\).  This is reconstruction, not a
contradiction.  If \(R_n^K>|A_n|\), the congruences simply recover the true
integer \(A_n\).

A useful global output must instead be a nonzero integer \(C_{n,K}\) satisfying

\[
 R_n^{e_K}\mid C_{n,K}
\tag{9.1}
\]

and

\[
 \log|C_{n,K}|\le A_Kn+E_K\log n.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Then

\[
 \log R_n\le\frac{A_K}{e_K}n+rac{E_K}{e_K}\log n.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Therefore P3.2 follows if arbitrarily large fixed grades give

\[
 \frac{A_K}{e_K}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9.4}
\]

This ledger gives two sharp warnings.

1. If a grade-\(K\) carrier contains \(\Theta(K)\) unrelated Apéry values of
   size \(\exp(\Theta(n))\), then typically \(A_K=\Theta(K)\) and (9.4)
   fails.
2. If a genuinely nonzero carrier had only polynomial height
   \(n^{O_K(1)}\), even one fixed grade would be extraordinarily strong.  Thus
   “\(D_K,Z_K\) have polynomial representatives” must mean that the final
   **selective nonzero integer**, not merely local coefficients, has that
   height.

The grade-seven law currently determines a local quotient digit and its CRT
class.  It does not yet construct the independent nonzero integer (9.1).

# 10. The concrete next exact computation: effective weight seven modulo \(p^8\)

The next computation should be symbolic before any prime fitting.

## 10.1 Formal coordinate ring

Work over

\[
 \mathbf Q[x_3,x_5,x_7,\mathcal D_7],
\]

where

```text
x_3 represents (1/p) H_(p-1)^(2),
x_5 represents (1/p) H_(p-1)^(4),
x_7 represents (1/p) H_(p-1)^(6),
D_7  denotes any remaining depth>1 weight-seven primitive classes.
```

Do not assume \(\mathcal D_7=0\).  Reduce only by proved stuffle, reversal,
H6, and lower-grade endpoint identities.

## 10.2 Expand the endpoints

For \(m=1,2,\ldots,M_0\), compute in exact rational arithmetic

\[
 b_{mp},\qquad b_{mp-1}\pmod {p^8},
\]

using (3.2)--(3.3).  Subtract the proved polynomials in
\(\delta_3,\eta_5,\delta_3^2\).  Record the primitive weight-seven
coefficient vectors

\[
 e_{7,m}^{0},\qquad e_{7,m}^{-}\in Q_7.
\]

The minimum useful \(M_0\) is not guessed in advance: increase it until the
row span stabilizes over \(\mathbf Q\).

## 10.3 Expand the target rows

Extend the audited direct and reflected constructions one digit:

```text
D_8 = direct row modulo p^8,
Z_8 = reflected row modulo p^8.
```

Subtract all lower-grade polynomials and extract

\[
 t_7^D,t_7^R,t_7^X\in Q_7,
\]

where \(t_7^X\) is the class multiplying \(b_{p+r}/p\).

## 10.4 Compute the fixed coefficient ideal

Form the exact matrix \(C_7\) of endpoint/target primitive coefficients.  The
pass criterion is not a numerical rank test.  Compute the saturated
integer/Fitting ideal and ask whether

\[
 \operatorname{Fitt}(C_7)\cap\mathbf Z=(\delta_7)
 \quad\text{with }\delta_7\ne0.
\tag{10.1}
\]

There are three possible outcomes.

```text
A. Q_7^tar = 0.
   The new primitive cancels; grade eight is another decomposable closure.

B. dim Q_7^tar = 1 and delta_7 != 0.
   Adjoin one new endpoint coordinate and eliminate it uniformly outside
   the fixed divisors of delta_7.

C. target primitive not in endpoint span, or Fitting ideal meets Z in 0.
   This is the first genuine all-grade obstruction.
```

# 11. Final answer to the exact question

## Is there an induction or finite-generation theorem?

**Yes at each fixed order, in a growing algebra:** Theorem 3.1 gives a
rigorous finite block-harmonic algebra, and Theorem 6.1 gives a rigorous
filtered induction once the primitive-module and fixed-unit-ideal conditions
are verified.

**No bounded all-grade theorem is presently justified:** the recurrence has
the exact three-digit singular-crossing freedom of Lemma 2.1, while the
factorial block logarithm has a new formal primitive \(\xi_{2h+1}\) at every
odd grade.  A bounded set of endpoint coordinates would require an infinite
family of special Apéry projection identities.

## Is order seven accidental?

Not in the numerical sense.  It is structurally the decomposable effective
weight-six layer, and H6 computes its Apéry-specific coefficient.  But its
success is **non-predictive for the next grade**, because effective weight
seven is invisible modulo \(p^7\) and first appears modulo \(p^8\).

## What is the first obstruction?

The first formal new direction is

\[
 \xi_7(p)=\frac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{-6}\pmod p,
\]

with complete-block logarithmic coefficient

\[
 -\frac{c^5(c+3)}6.
\]

The actual Apéry obstruction is the image of this symbol, together with any
weight-seven depth classes, in the target-relevant quotient \(Q_7^{\rm tar}\).
That image has not yet been computed.  It may cancel, be one-dimensional and
eliminable, or produce the first failure.

## What remains globally?

Even perfect local closure through all fixed grades must be converted into
nonzero controlled-height carriers with height-per-acquired-digit tending to
zero.  Otherwise the tower only reconstructs the pre-existing cofactor
\(b_n/R_n\) in its CRT coordinates.

This separates the two genuine open problems cleanly:

```text
LOCAL:
  compute Q_w^tar and a fixed Bezout/Fitting certificate at every grade;

GLOBAL:
  turn those local lifts into nonzero carriers C_(n,K) with
  log|C_(n,K)|/e_K = o(n).
```

The immediate proof-grade next step is the symbolic modulo-\(p^8\) computation
in Section 10.  It is the first calculation that actually tests a new
primitive rather than another decomposable consequence of the grade-three
endpoint defect.
