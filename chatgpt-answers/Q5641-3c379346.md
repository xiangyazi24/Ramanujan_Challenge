# First \(p\)-adic lift for the top-half Apéry channel: exact rank-one no-go

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
 b_m=\sum_{k=0}^m\binom{m}{k}^2\binom{m+k}{k}^2
\]

be the integral Apéry sequence, and let \(a_m\) be the rational companion solution of the same recurrence with

\[
 (b_0,b_1)=(1,5),\qquad (a_0,a_1)=(0,6).
\]

Fix a prime \(p\ge 5\) and a digit \(0\le r\le p-1\). Define the derivative Apéry sequence

\[
 D_r:=2\sum_{k=0}^r
 \binom{r}{k}^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2
 \bigl(H_{r+k}-H_{r-k}\bigr).
\tag{E1}
\]

Then \(D_r\in\mathbf Z_{(p)}\), and the exact first lifts are

\[
 \boxed{
 b_{p+r}\equiv 5\bigl(b_r+pD_r\bigr)\pmod{p^2},
 }
\tag{E2}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 p^3a_{p+r}\equiv 6\bigl(b_r+pD_r\bigr)\pmod{p^2}.
 }
\tag{E3}
\]

Formula (E2) is Gessel's published shifted congruence modulo \(p^2\), specialized to outer digit \(1\). Formula (E3) follows from the same differentiated Apéry recurrence, the exact Wronskian, and the endpoint supercongruences. No conjectural congruence is used.

Now assume \(n=p+r\), \(n/2<p\le n\), and \(p\ge7\). Put

\[
 G_n=\gcd(L_n^3a_n,L_n^3b_n),
 \qquad L_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n).
\]

Since \(n<2p\), write \(L_n=pU_n\) with \(U_n\in\mathbf Z_{(p)}^\times\). Then

\[
 L_n^3a_n=U_n^3\,p^3a_n,
 \qquad
 L_n^3b_n=p^3U_n^3b_n.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 p\mid G_{p+r}
 \Longleftrightarrow
 p\mid p^3a_{p+r}
 \Longleftrightarrow
 p\mid b_r
 \Longleftrightarrow
 p\mid b_{p+r}.
 }
\tag{E4}
\]

Thus the two normalized coordinates vanish modulo \(p\) for the same single reason.

More sharply, on a target write

\[
 b_r=pq_{p,r},\qquad q_{p,r}\in\mathbf Z_{(p)},
\]

and define

\[
 \xi_{p,r}:=q_{p,r}+D_r\pmod p.
\tag{E5}
\]

Dividing (E2)--(E3) by \(p\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \left(\frac{b_{p+r}}p,\frac{p^3a_{p+r}}p\right)
 \equiv
 \xi_{p,r}(5,6)\pmod p.
 }
\tag{E6}
\]

The first lift therefore remains rank one. Its only relation is

\[
 \boxed{6b_{p+r}-5p^3a_{p+r}\equiv0\pmod{p^2},}
\tag{E7}
\]

and (E7) is universal: it holds for every digit \(r\), not only when \(p\mid b_r\). It is not a second target-selective equation.

The first lift instead detects the next valuation depth:

\[
 \boxed{
 p\mid b_r
 \Longrightarrow
 \bigl(v_p(G_{p+r})\ge2\bigr)
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \xi_{p,r}=0
 \Longleftrightarrow
 p^2\mid b_{p+r}.
 }
\tag{E8}
\]

There is no implication \(\xi_{p,r}=0\) from targetness. An exact counterexample is

\[
 p=73,\qquad r=2,\qquad b_2=73,\qquad D_2=210.
\]

Here

\[
 \xi_{73,2}=1+210\equiv65\not\equiv0\pmod{73},
\]

so

\[
 \frac{b_{75}}{73}\equiv33\pmod{73},
 \qquad
 73^2a_{75}\equiv25\pmod{73},
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{v_{73}(G_{75})=1.}
\tag{E9}
\]

This proves outcome **(2)**. Modulo \(p^2\), the lift adds no target-selective equation for the support event \(v_p(G_n)\ge1\). It only separates exact depth one from depth at least two.

The remaining loophole is precise: one would need a new theorem special to the actual Apéry state which imposes an independent relation on

\[
 q_{p,r}=b_r/p\pmod p,
 \qquad D_r\pmod p,
\]

or on a genuinely new higher-lift coordinate, together with a nonzero cross-prime carrier of logarithmic height \(o(n)\). Such a relation does not follow from Gessel's first lift, the Wronskian, the recurrence, or the one target equation \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\).

## 2. Recurrence, Wronskian, and derivative sequence

Put

\[
 P(x)=34x^3+51x^2+27x+5.
\]

Both \(a_m\) and \(b_m\) satisfy

\[
 (m+1)^3u_{m+1}=P(m)u_m-m^3u_{m-1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Their exact Wronskian is

\[
 \boxed{
 a_m b_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m=\frac6{m^3}
 }
 \qquad(m\ge1).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Let

\[
 \mathcal B(z)=
 \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(-z)_k^2(z+1)_k^2}{(k!)^4}
\tag{2.3}
\]

be the entire Apéry interpolation. At a nonnegative integer \(r\), terms with \(k>r\) have a double zero, so differentiating termwise gives

\[
 \mathcal B'(r)=D_r
\]

with \(D_r\) as in (E1).

The interpolating recurrence has a defect proportional to

\[
 (2z+1)\sin^2(\pi z).
\]

Both the defect and its first derivative vanish at integers. Therefore differentiating (2.1) at \(r\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}\) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (r+1)^3D_{r+1}-P(r)D_r+r^3D_{r-1}
 &={P}'(r)b_r
   -3(r+1)^2b_{r+1}
   -3r^2b_{r-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Here

\[
 P'(r)=102r^2+102r+27.
\]

### \(p\)-integrality of \(D_r\)

For \(0\le r\le p-1\), the denominator of \(D_r\) is a \(p\)-unit.

If \(r+k<p\), every harmonic denominator in the \(k\)-summand is \(<p\). If \(r+k\ge p\), the binomial coefficient

\[
 \binom{r+k}{k}
\]

contains exactly one factor of \(p\), so its square contributes \(p^2\). The harmonic difference can contain at worst one factor \(1/p\). Hence that whole summand is still \(p\)-integral. This also follows directly from Gessel's theorem, which states that the denominator of \(D_r\) is not divisible by \(p\) for a base-\(p\) digit \(r\).

The initial derivative values needed below are

\[
 D_0=0,
 \qquad
 D_1=2\cdot4\cdot H_2=12.
\tag{2.5}
\]

## 3. The published shifted congruence for \(b_{p+r}\)

Gessel's Theorem 4 states that for a prime \(p\), a digit \(0\le d<p\), and an integer \(m\ge0\),

\[
 b_{d+pm}
 \equiv
 \bigl(b_d+pmD_d\bigr)b_m
 \pmod{p^2}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Taking \(d=r\) and \(m=1\), and using \(b_1=5\), gives (E2):

\[
 b_{p+r}\equiv5(b_r+pD_r)\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is a verified published formula, not a conjectural endpoint expansion.

For completeness, it can also be recovered from the recurrence. Define

\[
 X_r=b_r+pD_r.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Expanding modulo \(p^2\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (p+r+1)^3&\equiv(r+1)^3+3p(r+1)^2,\\
 P(p+r)&\equiv P(r)+pP'(r),\\
 (p+r)^3&\equiv r^3+3pr^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equations (2.1) and (2.4) imply

\[
 (p+r+1)^3X_{r+1}-P(p+r)X_r+(p+r)^3X_{r-1}
 \equiv0\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The sequence \(r\mapsto b_{p+r}\) satisfies the same shifted recurrence exactly.

Gessel's endpoint supercongruence gives

\[
 b_p\equiv b_1=5\pmod{p^3},
 \qquad
 b_{p-1}\equiv b_0=1\pmod{p^3}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus \(b_p\equiv5X_0\pmod{p^2}\). The recurrence at \(m=p\) gives

\[
 (p+1)^3b_{p+1}=P(p)b_p-p^3b_{p-1}.
\]

Since

\[
 P(p)\equiv5+27p,
 \qquad
 (p+1)^{-3}\equiv1-3p\pmod{p^2},
\]

we obtain

\[
 b_{p+1}\equiv25+60p=5(5+12p)=5X_1\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

For \(0\le r\le p-2\), the coefficient \(p+r+1\equiv r+1\not\equiv0\pmod p\). Hence the shifted recurrence propagates uniquely through \(r=p-1\), proving (3.2) for the full digit range.

## 4. The companion lift \(p^3a_{p+r}\pmod{p^2}\)

Define

\[
 C_r:=p^3a_{p+r}\in\mathbf Z_{(p)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The \(p\)-integrality follows because the denominator of \(a_{p+r}\) divides a cube of the lcm up to \(p+r<2p\), in which \(p\) occurs only once.

Multiplying the Apéry recurrence by \(p^3\) gives the exact shifted recurrence

\[
 (p+r+1)^3C_{r+1}-P(p+r)C_r+(p+r)^3C_{r-1}=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

We compare \(C_r\) with \(6X_r\).

### Initial value \(r=0\)

Use the Wronskian (2.2) at \(m=p\):

\[
 p^3a_p b_{p-1}-p^3a_{p-1}b_p=6.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The second term is divisible by \(p^3\) in \(\mathbf Z_{(p)}\), and \(b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^3}\). Therefore

\[
 C_0=p^3a_p\equiv6=6X_0\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Initial value \(r=1\)

At \(m=p\), recurrence (2.1) gives

\[
 (p+1)^3C_1=P(p)C_0-p^6a_{p-1}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The last term vanishes modulo \(p^2\). Using (4.4),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_1
 &\equiv (1-3p)(5+27p)\cdot6\\
 &\equiv 6(5+12p)\\
 &=6(b_1+pD_1)
 =6X_1
 \pmod{p^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Propagation

By (3.5), \(6X_r\) satisfies (4.2) modulo \(p^2\). The two sequences agree at \(r=0,1\), and the leading recurrence coefficient is a \(p\)-unit until the step after \(r=p-1\). Hence

\[
 C_r\equiv6X_r\pmod{p^2}
\]

for every \(0\le r\le p-1\). This proves (E3).

No formula of Liu or Sun is needed. In particular, no multiplicative-index congruence at \(Np^\ell\) has been repurposed as a shifted-index theorem.

## 5. Exact support law and exact depth-two law

Let \(n=p+r\) with \(n/2<p\le n\), \(p\ge7\). Since \(n<2p\),

\[
 L_n=pU_n,
 \qquad U_n\in\mathbf Z_{(p)}^\times.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
 v_p(G_n)
 =\min\bigl(v_p(C_r),\,3+v_p(b_{p+r})\bigr).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Reducing (E2)--(E3) modulo \(p\),

\[
 b_{p+r}\equiv5b_r,
 \qquad
 C_r\equiv6b_r
 \pmod p.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since \(5\) and \(6\) are units for \(p\ge7\), (E4) follows.

The endpoint residues are harmless:

- \(r=0\) is not a target because \(b_0=1\);
- \(r=p-1\) is not a target because \(b_{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p\);
- \(r=p-2\) is not a target because reflection gives
  \(b_{p-2}\equiv b_1=5\pmod p\).

Thus every target lies in

\[
 1\le r\le p-3,
\]

where every recurrence division used above is by a unit.

Now impose \(b_r=pq_{p,r}\). From (E2)--(E3),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b_{p+r}&\equiv5p(q_{p,r}+D_r)\pmod{p^2},\\
 C_r&\equiv6p(q_{p,r}+D_r)\pmod{p^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The second gcd coordinate in (5.2) then has valuation at least \(4\), because \(L_n^3\) already contributes \(p^3\) and \(b_{p+r}\) contributes another \(p\). Hence valuation depth two is controlled entirely by \(C_r\):

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_p(G_n)\ge2
 &\Longleftrightarrow p^2\mid C_r\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow q_{p,r}+D_r\equiv0\pmod p\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow p^2\mid b_{p+r}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the correct mathematical meaning of the first lift. It refines the valuation, but it does not refine the support set \(T_n\).

## 6. Formal rank-one no-go theorem

The absence of a second equation can be stated as an exact algebraic theorem.

### Theorem 6.1 — first-lift target algebra

Work over \(\mathbf F_p\) with \(p\ge7\). Introduce formal coordinates

\[
 q=\frac{b_r}{p}\pmod p,
 \qquad d=D_r\pmod p,
 \qquad \xi=q+d.
\]

Let \(X,Y\) denote the two divided lifted coordinates

\[
 X=\frac{b_{p+r}}p\pmod p,
 \qquad
 Y=\frac{p^3a_{p+r}}p\pmod p.
\]

The first-lift formulas define the homomorphism

\[
 \Phi:\mathbf F_p[X,Y]\longrightarrow\mathbf F_p[q,d],
 \qquad
 X\mapsto5(q+d),
 \quad
 Y\mapsto6(q+d).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{\ker\Phi=(6X-5Y).}
\tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

The image of \(\Phi\) is \(\mathbf F_p[\xi]\), because \(5\) is a unit and \(X\mapsto5\xi\). The map factors as the coordinate map of the affine line

\[
 \xi\longmapsto(5\xi,6\xi).
\]

The defining ideal of this line is the principal ideal \(6X-5Y\). Therefore (6.2) holds. \(\square\)

### Consequence

Any polynomial congruence among the two first-lift coordinates which is deduced only from

1. the target equation \(b_r=pq\), and
2. formulas (E2)--(E3),

is generated by the universal proportionality relation (E7). It cannot impose \(\xi=0\), nor any other restriction on the remaining scalar.

This is an arbitrary-degree statement for the two first-lift coordinates, not only a linear-rank count.

Its scope is deliberately local-formal. The actual Apéry values \(q_{p,r}\) and \(D_r\) are not arbitrary once the full distinguished sequence is imposed. A theorem discovering a new actual-sequence relation between them would lie outside Theorem 6.1 and is exactly the remaining loophole.

## 7. Exact depth-one counterexample

Take

\[
 p=73,
 \qquad r=2.
\]

The small Apéry value is

\[
 b_2=73,
\]

so \(p\mid b_r\) and \(p\in T_{75}\).

Compute \(D_2\) directly from (E1). The \(k=0\) term is zero. For \(k=1\),

\[
 \binom21^2\binom31^2=36,
 \qquad
 H_3-H_1=\frac56,
\]

so the contribution is

\[
 2\cdot36\cdot\frac56=60.
\]

For \(k=2\),

\[
 \binom22^2\binom42^2=36,
 \qquad
 H_4-H_0=\frac{25}{12},
\]

so the contribution is

\[
 2\cdot36\cdot\frac{25}{12}=150.
\]

Thus

\[
 D_2=210.
\]

Since \(b_2/73=1\),

\[
 \xi_{73,2}=1+210=211\equiv65\pmod{73}.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \frac{b_{75}}{73}
 \equiv5\cdot65
 \equiv33\pmod{73},
\]

and

\[
 73^2a_{75}
 =\frac{73^3a_{75}}{73}
 \equiv6\cdot65
 \equiv25\pmod{73}.
\]

Both quotients are nonzero. Hence \(v_{73}(p^3a_{75})=1\), and (5.2) gives

\[
 v_{73}(G_{75})=1.
\]

This is an exact symbolic counterexample, not a statistical inference. It rules out any proposed theorem asserting that targetness automatically forces the first quotient digit to vanish.

## 8. Why this does not produce a pointwise carrier

A second target-selective congruence would need to cut the line

\[
 (X,Y)=\xi(5,6)
\]

at a proper subset, ideally at \(\xi=0\) or at the zero set of another low-degree function. The first lift does not do this.

Taking a determinant of the two coordinates gives

\[
 6X-5Y=0,
\]

but this is true for every digit and every candidate prime. Its characteristic-zero lift is a universally \(p^2\)-divisible expression, not an integer whose divisibility selects \(T_n\).

Taking resultants, minors, exterior powers, or polynomial combinations of \(X,Y\) cannot create a second equation: after exact saturation their target algebra is still the line ideal (6.2). A product over candidate primes either contains a universal factor for every candidate or recovers the unknown radical, exactly as in Q5630/Q5638.

The deeper subset

\[
 T_n^{(2)}=
 \{p\in T_n:v_p(G_n)\ge2\}
\]

is characterized by \(\xi_{p,r}=0\), but controlling \(T_n^{(2)}\) does not control the exact-depth-one targets

\[
 T_n^{(1)}=
 \{p\in T_n:v_p(G_n)=1\}.
\]

The example \(p=73,r=2\) proves that \(T_n^{(1)}\) is nonempty. A pointwise proof for all of \(T_n\) therefore cannot discard this component.

## 10. Final theorem and remaining loophole

### Theorem

For every prime \(p\ge5\) and \(0\le r\le p-1\), put

\[
 X_{p,r}=b_r+pD_r.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 (b_{p+r},p^3a_{p+r})
 \equiv X_{p,r}(5,6)\pmod{p^2}.
 }
\]

For a top-half target \(p\ge7\), write \(b_r=pq_{p,r}\). Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \left(\frac{b_{p+r}}p,\frac{p^3a_{p+r}}p\right)
 \equiv(q_{p,r}+D_r)(5,6)\pmod p.
 }
\]

Therefore the first lift supplies one scalar coordinate and no second target-selective congruence for \(v_p(G_{p+r})\ge1\). It supplies only the deeper criterion

\[
 v_p(G_{p+r})\ge2
 \Longleftrightarrow
 q_{p,r}+D_r\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

### Remaining loophole

The no-go does **not** exclude an identity special to the distinguished Apéry sequence which proves, for every target prime, a new relation involving one or more of

\[
 q_{p,r}=b_r/p,
 \qquad D_r,
 \qquad \text{a genuinely independent second or higher Frobenius digit}.
\]

To solve P3.2, such an identity would still have to globalize pointwise: it must yield a nonzero integer defined without knowing \(T_n\), divisible by every target prime, with logarithmic height \(o(n)\). The first \(p\)-adic lift itself provides neither the second equation nor the height saving.
