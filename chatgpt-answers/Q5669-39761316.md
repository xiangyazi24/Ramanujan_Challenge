# Q5669 — finite-Racah cutoff comparison for adjacent Apéry target primes

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
\phi_m(X)=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(X-j(j+1))}{(m!)^2},
\qquad
H_q(X)=\sum_{m=0}^{(q-1)/2}\phi_m(X)^2
\quad(q\text{ odd prime}).
\]

For adjacent top-half target primes

\[
\ell=p+h,\qquad n=\ell+s=p+s+h,
\]

with

\[
0\le s<s+h<p<\ell\le n,
\qquad
p\mid b_{s+h},\quad \ell\mid b_s,
\]

the gap is automatically even:

\[
h=2d,\qquad d\ge1.
\]

Put

\[
M=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad L=\frac{\ell-1}{2}=M+d,
\]

and, after specializing the common integer

\[
X=n(n+1),
\]

put

\[
u_m:=\phi_m(n(n+1))^2,\qquad
S_K:=\sum_{m=0}^K u_m,\qquad
E:=S_L-S_M=\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{M+i}.
\]

The exact answer is negative for the natural bounded-order Racah/contiguous algebra, but the negative result comes with a sharp positive identity.

> **Cutoff-saturation theorem.**
> 
> 1. The two target conditions are exactly
>    \[
>    p\mid S_M,\qquad \ell\mid S_L.
>    \]
> 2. Every added boundary summand is universally divisible by \(p^2\):
>    \[
>    p^2\mid u_{M+i}\quad(1\le i\le d),
>    \qquad p^2\mid E.
>    \]
> 3. Consequently there are integers \(c,t\) such that
>    \[
>    \boxed{
>    S_M=p\ell t-p^2c,\qquad
>    E=p^2c,\qquad
>    S_L=p\ell t.}
>    \tag{V}
>    \]
>    In particular,
>    \[
>    \boxed{p\ell\mid S_L.}
>    \tag{C}
>    \]
> 4. The carrier \(S_L\) is exponentially large:
>    \[
>    \log S_L\ge \frac{n-1}{4}\log 36-O(1),
>    \]
>    so (C) gives only an \(O(n)\) height bound.
> 5. If one eliminates the shared prefix, the only linear boundary expression forced to be divisible by \(p\ell\) is an explicit multiple of
>    \[
>    \ell(S_L-S_M)=\ell E.
>    \]
>    Its \(p\)-divisibility is universal cutoff vanishing, and its \(\ell\)-divisibility is inserted by the coefficient \(\ell\). It contains no target selectivity.
> 6. After factoring the common boundary term, the remaining normalized \(d\)-term Racah quotient has logarithmic height \(O(d\log n)=O(h\log n)\), but neither target congruence forces it to vanish modulo \(\ell\). Thus the only small boundary object loses precisely the desired prime factor.

So there is no useful integer

\[
C_{n,p,h}
\]

of height \(O(h\log n)\), obtained from a bounded number of natural cutoff-contiguous Racah terms or a bounded-order local resultant, for which the two target congruences supply **selective** divisibility by \(p(p+h)\).

The exact saturation is:

```text
keep the target-dependent cutoff state  =>  p(p+h)-divisibility, but exponential height;
eliminate the cutoff state             =>  only universal boundary divisibility remains.
```

This does **not** prove that no new identity special to the distinguished Apéry initial state can exist. It proves that such an identity is not a consequence of the universal finite-Racah cutoff extension, its bounded contiguous relations, or a bounded-order resultant which sees only the local cutoff window. A successful identity would have to transport the initial condition through a growing prefix and then exhibit a new cancellation not present in the natural cutoff module.

## 2. Parity, ranges, and integer specialization

Both \(p\) and \(\ell\) are odd, so

\[
h=\ell-p
\]

is even. Write \(h=2d\). The exceptional case involving the prime \(2\) is outside the stated definition of \(H_p\) and is irrelevant to the asymptotic top-half problem.

Because \(p>n/2\) and \(\ell\le n\),

\[
\ell<2p,
\]

hence

\[
L=\frac{\ell-1}{2}<p.
\]

Therefore every factorial \(m!\) with \(m\le L\) is a unit modulo both \(p\) and \(\ell\). No denominator is being moved between characteristics.

At the common integer \(X=n(n+1)\), one has the exact characteristic-zero identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi_m(n(n+1))
&=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(n-j)(n+j+1)}{(m!)^2}\\
&=\binom nm\binom{n+m}{m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
u_m=\binom nm^2\binom{n+m}{m}^2\in\mathbb Z.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The two finite-field evaluations are reductions of the two honest integer prefixes

\[
H_p(n(n+1))\equiv S_M\pmod p,
\qquad
H_\ell(n(n+1))\equiv S_L\pmod\ell.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since

\[
n\equiv s+h\pmod p,
\qquad
n\equiv s\pmod\ell,
\]

the interpolation theorem gives

\[
S_M\equiv b_{s+h}\pmod p,
\qquad
S_L\equiv b_s\pmod\ell.
\tag{2.4}
\]

This proves the first assertion of the theorem without identifying the two residue fields.

## 3. Exact cutoff shift

The coefficients in the common portion of the two sums are literally the same rational numbers. The dependence on the prime is only the upper cutoff. Hence

\[
\boxed{
H_\ell(X)-H_p(X)
=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{M+i}(X)^2
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

as an identity over \(\mathbb Q[X]\), with denominators supported below \(L<p\).

After specializing \(X=n(n+1)\),

\[
\boxed{S_L-S_M=E=\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_{M+i}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is the exact contiguous identity. The question is whether the boundary sum can replace the exponentially large shared prefix while retaining both target primes.

It cannot.

## 4. The finite-field top-cutoff factor

There is a useful exact finite-field formula explaining why every added term vanishes modulo \(p\).

Let

\[
Y=4X+1,
\qquad M=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

The values \((2j+1)^2\), \(0\le j<M\), run through all nonzero quadratic residues modulo \(p\). Therefore

\[
\prod_{j=0}^{M-1}\bigl(Y-(2j+1)^2\bigr)
=Y^M-1
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p[Y].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since

\[
X-j(j+1)=\frac{Y-(2j+1)^2}{4}
\]

and \(4^M=1\pmod p\), one obtains, up to an irrelevant sign,

\[
\phi_M(X)\equiv \pm(Y^M-1)\pmod p.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover

\[
\frac{X-M(M+1)}{(M+1)^2}\equiv Y\pmod p,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\phi_{M+1}(X)\equiv \pm Y(Y^M-1)\pmod p.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

At the Casimir specialization

\[
Y=4n(n+1)+1=(2n+1)^2.
\]

If \(Y\ne0\pmod p\), Euler's criterion gives \(Y^M=1\). If \(Y=0\), the extra factor \(Y\) in (4.3) vanishes. Hence in all cases

\[
\phi_{M+1}(n(n+1))\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Every later \(\phi_{M+i}\) contains \(\phi_{M+1}\) as a multiplicative factor, and all its denominators remain \(p\)-units because \(M+i\le L<p\). Thus

\[
\boxed{
p\mid\phi_{M+i}(n(n+1)),
\qquad p^2\mid u_{M+i}
\quad(1\le i\le d).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{p^2\mid E.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the sole genuinely new divisibility supplied by extending the cutoff from \(p\) to \(p+h\). It is universal: it uses only that the evaluation is on the quadratic Casimir image modulo \(p\), not either target congruence.

An equivalent elementary proof chooses

\[
j_p=\min(s+h,p-1-s-h)\le M.
\]

For every \(m>M\), either \(n-j_p\) or \(n+j_p+1\) is divisible by \(p\), so the product defining \(\phi_m\) has a \(p\)-factor.

## 5. Exact target lattice

Let

\[
x=S_M,\qquad e=E,\qquad y=S_L=x+e.
\]

The target conditions and (4.6) say

\[
p\mid x,\qquad p^2\mid e,\qquad \ell\mid y.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Write

\[
x=pa,\qquad e=p^2c.
\]

Then

\[
y=p(a+pc).
\]

Since \((p,\ell)=1\) and \(\ell\mid y\), there is an integer \(t\) with

\[
a+pc=\ell t.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(x,e,y)
=t(p\ell,0,p\ell)+c(-p^2,p^2,0).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
S_M=p\ell t-p^2c,
\qquad E=p^2c,
\qquad S_L=p\ell t.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is the exact saturation ledger.

### 5.1 The positive carrier

Equation (5.3) gives

\[
\boxed{p\ell\mid S_L.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the two target congruences do produce a single common integer carrier: the larger Racah prefix.

But it is the wrong size; Section 6 proves that it has positive linear logarithmic height.

### 5.2 Linear-form Smith saturation

Consider an integer linear form

\[
C_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha S_M+\beta S_L.
\]

Using (5.3),

\[
C_{\alpha,\beta}
=p\ell(\alpha+\beta)t-\alpha p^2c.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
p\ell\mid C_{\alpha,\beta}
\quad\text{for every target-compatible local state }(t,c)
\]

if and only if

\[
\boxed{\ell\mid\alpha.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

To eliminate the shared prefix one must also impose

\[
\alpha+\beta=0.
\]

Combining with (5.6),

\[
(\alpha,\beta)=k(\ell,-\ell),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
C_{\alpha,\beta}
=-k\ell E.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The \(p\)-factor in (5.7) comes from the universal boundary vanishing (indeed \(p^2\mid E\)); the \(\ell\)-factor is inserted explicitly. Neither uses the \(\ell\)-target condition.

This is the exact linear saturation statement requested in the question.

### 5.3 The warned cross-weighted combination

The usual expression is

\[
\ell S_M-pS_L
=hS_M-pE.
\tag{5.8}
\]

It is divisible by \(p\ell\), but the coefficient of the shared prefix is \(h\ne0\). Thus it retains the exponentially large state. Formula (5.8) is not merely a poorly chosen combination: (5.7) proves that every exact prefix-canceling linear combination becomes nonselective.

## 6. The larger prefix has linear height

The positivity of the summands makes the Archimedean obstruction elementary.

Since \(p>n/2\),

\[
M=\frac{p-1}{2}\ge\frac{n-1}{4},
\qquad
M\le\frac n2.
\]

For \(m\le n/2\),

\[
\binom nm
=\prod_{i=1}^m\frac{n-m+i}{i}
\ge2^m,
\]

and

\[
\binom{n+m}{m}
=\prod_{i=1}^m\frac{n+i}{i}
\ge3^m.
\]

Hence

\[
u_m\ge36^m.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Since \(S_L\ge u_M\),

\[
\boxed{
\log S_L
\ge M\log36
\ge\frac{n-1}{4}\log36.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the exact carrier (5.4) has height \(\exp(\Theta(n))\). In particular it does not prove

\[
\log\prod_{p\in T_n}p=o(n).
\]

The same lower bound applies to every positive prefix containing \(u_M\). No cancellation is available inside \(S_L\), because all summands are positive integers.

## 7. Factoring the short boundary

The boundary does have a small normalized quotient. The one-step ratio is

\[
\rho_m(X)
:=\frac{\phi_{m+1}(X)^2}{\phi_m(X)^2}
=\frac{(X-m(m+1))^2}{(m+1)^4}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
E=\phi_{M+1}(X)^2Q_d(X),
\tag{7.2}
\]

where

\[
Q_d(X)
=1+\sum_{i=2}^{d}
 \prod_{t=1}^{i-1}
 \frac{(X-(M+t)(M+t+1))^2}{(M+t+1)^4}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

After setting \(X=n(n+1)\), a common denominator for \(Q_d\) is supported on the \(O(d)\) integers

\[
M+2,\ldots,M+d,
\]

and every numerator factor has size \(O(n^2)\). Hence the reduced rational height satisfies

\[
\boxed{h(Q_d(n(n+1)))=O(d\log n)=O(h\log n).}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is exactly the hoped-for height scale.

But the target divisibility sits in the discarded factor \(\phi_{M+1}^2\), not in \(Q_d\):

- modulo \(p\), \(\phi_{M+1}^2\) is universally zero by (4.4);
- modulo \(\ell\), the target equation is
  \[
  S_M+\phi_{M+1}^2Q_d=0,
  \tag{7.5}
  \]
  not \(Q_d=0\).

Thus dividing off the exponentially large common boundary term removes the only automatic \(p\)-factor, while the \(\ell\)-target continues to involve the old prefix.

### 7.1 Inactive and active \(\ell\)-boundary cases

Let

\[
j_\ell=\min(s,\ell-1-s)\le L.
\]

There are two cases.

#### Inactive boundary: \(j_\ell\le M\)

Every added term has index above the folded terminating index, so

\[
\ell^2\mid u_{M+i}\quad(1\le i\le d)
\]

universally. The boundary then carries \(p^2\ell^2\) for purely kinematic reasons. It contains no information that either Apéry value is zero.

The \(\ell\)-target is already visible in the smaller prefix:

\[
S_M\equiv S_L\equiv b_s\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

Thus \(S_M\) itself is a \(p\ell\)-carrier, but again has exponential height.

#### Active boundary: \(j_\ell>M\)

The first boundary term is an \(\ell\)-unit. The local boundary scale can vary freely, and (7.5) merely determines the additive prefix state modulo \(\ell\). It imposes no vanishing condition on \(Q_d\).

So in either case the short boundary fails:

```text
inactive: its ell-factor is universal, not target-selective;
active:   the ell-target fixes the old prefix, not the boundary quotient.
```

## 8. Why a bounded-order resultant cannot improve this ledger

The natural cutoff dynamics is the triangular first-order system

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
S_{m+1}\\ u_{m+1}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\rho_m\\0&\rho_m
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
S_m\\u_m
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

It has an exact translation symmetry:

\[
(S_m,u_m)\longmapsto(S_m+c,u_m).
\tag{8.2}
\]

The boundary terms determine the increment \(S_L-S_M\), but not the integration constant \(S_M\).

### 8.1 Local elimination ideal

Over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), the local equations are

\[
u_{m+1}=\rho_mu_m,
\qquad
S_L-S_M=E,
\qquad
S_L=0.
\]

For every boundary vector satisfying the universal ratio equations, one can choose

\[
S_M=-E.
\]

Therefore eliminating \(S_M,S_L\) produces **only** the universal ratio ideal among the \(u_{M+i}\). There is no extra polynomial relation caused by the target.

Over \(\mathbb F_p\), all the boundary terms already vanish at the Casimir specialization. The output of elimination is therefore a universal \(p\)-factor.

Combining the two fields by

\[
\mathbb Z/p\ell\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_\ell
\]

does not couple the two integration constants. Any bounded-order resultant which eliminates the cutoff state consequently returns one of:

1. a universal boundary relation, which evaluates to zero identically;
2. an explicit factor \(p\) or \(\ell\);
3. the old prefix state, and therefore exponential height.

It cannot produce a new selective low-height integer.

### 8.2 A precise polynomial saturation statement

The target lattice (5.3) projects onto

\[
E=p^2c,\qquad c\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Modulo \(\ell\), multiplication by \(p^2\) is a bijection. Hence, if

\[
F(T)\in\mathbb Z[T],\qquad \deg F<\ell,
\]

and \(\ell\mid F(E)\) for every target-compatible local state, then

\[
F(p^2c)=0\quad\text{for every }c\in\mathbb F_\ell.
\]

The degree bound gives

\[
F\equiv0\pmod\ell
\]

coefficientwise. Thus the \(\ell\)-factor was present before the target was imposed.

For a bounded-order construction, \(\deg F=O(h)\), and in the relevant range \(h=O(\log n)\ll\ell\). Therefore this applies directly.

For individual boundary coordinates, restrict a proposed polynomial to the one-dimensional hypergeometric line generated by \(u_{M+1}\). Either the restriction is identically zero because of the universal ratio relations, or the same degree argument forces an explicit \(\ell\)-factor. This is the bounded-resultant version of (5.7).

## 9. Why the distinguished initial state requires the exponential prefix

The translation symmetry is broken only by transporting the actual initial condition

\[
S_0=u_0=1
\]

through all earlier cutoffs. Multiplying the triangular matrices in (8.1) gives

\[
\prod_{m=0}^{K-1}
\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho_m\\0&\rho_m\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&S_K-1\\
0&u_K
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Thus the global connection coefficient that remembers the Apéry initial state is **exactly the full prefix** \(S_K-1\). The exponential term has not arisen from a bad estimate; it is the upper-right entry of the exact transfer product.

A Christoffel--Darboux or Gosper identity reducing \(S_K\) to a bounded window of boundary terms would split this triangular extension. No such fixed-width rational identity exists.

Indeed, suppose for a fixed \(J\) there were rational functions giving

\[
S_K(X)=R_0(X,K)+\sum_{|j|\le J}R_j(X,K)u_{K+j}(X)
\tag{9.2}
\]

uniformly in the cutoff. Specialize \(X=n(n+1)\) and \(K=n\). Every fixed backward shift \(u_{n-j}\) is a rational-function multiple of

\[
u_n=\binom{2n}{n}^2,
\]

and every forward shift vanishes. Hence (9.2) would imply

\[
b_n=R(n)+Q(n)\binom{2n}{n}^2.
\]

But

\[
\binom{2n}{n}^2=16^{n+o(n)},
\]

whereas

\[
b_n=(17+12\sqrt2)^{n+o(n)}.
\]

A rational function cannot change the exponential base. This contradiction rules out every fixed-width rational boundary telescoper.

The same dominant-exponential argument rules out a nonzero polynomial relation of fixed degree between the prefix and a bounded boundary window: after reducing all boundary shifts to \(u_n\), distinct monomials have bases

\[
(17+12\sqrt2)^a16^b.
\]

These bases are pairwise distinct, because no nonzero power of \(17+12\sqrt2\) is rational. A unique largest base cannot cancel.

This does not exclude a degree or width growing with \(n\). But such a construction is no longer a bounded-order contiguous/resultant identity, and its coefficient-height ledger must be audited separately. The existing Newton--Padé/Smith analyses show that the known growing constructions retain the target radical as their saturation index.

## 10. Same-characteristic resultants detect only an additional condition

Because \(p^2\mid E\), a \(p\)-target gives

\[
H_p(X)=H_\ell(X)=0\pmod p
\]

at the marked value. Thus a resultant of the two lifted cutoff polynomials can acquire a \(p\)-factor.

For \(\ell\), however, the target gives only

\[
H_p(X)+E(X)=H_\ell(X)=0\pmod\ell.
\]

To make the same resultant acquire \(\ell\), one additionally needs

\[
H_p(X)=E(X)=0\pmod\ell.
\]

That is a same-characteristic double-cutoff zero, not the pure-cross hypothesis. In the inactive case it is universal cutoff termination; in the active case it is an extra zero condition. Therefore ordinary resultants either detect a degenerate/additional event or fail to see \(\ell\).

This is exactly analogous to the continuant distinction in Q5545: low-height same-characteristic certificates exist, but pure-cross pairs are precisely the locus where the needed second zero is absent.

## 12. Dependency-free exact verifier

The following script checks, for all adjacent top-half target pairs up to a chosen limit:

- evenness of \(h\);
- the two original target congruences;
- the exact cutoff identity;
- \(p^2\mid E\);
- \(p\ell\mid S_L\);
- the lattice parametrization (5.3);
- inactive-boundary \(\ell^2\)-divisibility;
- pure-cross versus degenerate classification.

It uses exact integers only.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
from math import isqrt

LIMIT = 5000


def primes_upto(limit: int) -> list[int]:
    sieve = bytearray(b"\x01") * (limit + 1)
    sieve[:2] = b"\x00\x00"
    for q in range(2, isqrt(limit) + 1):
        if sieve[q]:
            sieve[q*q : limit + 1 : q] = b"\x00" * (
                (limit - q*q) // q + 1
            )
    return [q for q, flag in enumerate(sieve) if flag]


def apery_numbers(limit: int) -> list[int]:
    b = [0] * (limit + 1)
    b[0] = 1
    if limit >= 1:
        b[1] = 5
    for k in range(1, limit):
        middle = 34*k**3 + 51*k**2 + 27*k + 5
        numerator = middle*b[k] - k**3*b[k-1]
        denominator = (k+1)**3
        assert numerator % denominator == 0
        b[k+1] = numerator // denominator
    return b


def racah_prefixes(n: int, upper: int) -> list[int]:
    # u_m = binom(n,m)^2 binom(n+m,m)^2, updated exactly.
    u = 1
    total = 1
    result = [1]
    for m in range(upper):
        numerator = ((n-m)*(n+m+1))**2
        denominator = (m+1)**4
        assert (u*numerator) % denominator == 0
        u = u*numerator // denominator
        total += u
        result.append(total)
    return result


def main() -> None:
    primes = primes_upto(LIMIT)
    b = apery_numbers(LIMIT)

    pair_count = 0
    pure_cross_count = 0
    inactive_count = 0
    active_count = 0
    first_active_small_quotient_counterexample = None

    for n in range(2, LIMIT + 1):
        hits = [
            p for p in primes
            if n//2 < p <= n and p > 5 and b[n] % p == 0
        ]
        for p, ell in zip(hits, hits[1:]):
            h = ell - p
            assert h % 2 == 0
            d = h // 2
            s = n - ell
            assert 0 <= s < s+h < p < ell <= n

            # Original different-characteristic target conditions.
            assert b[s] % ell == 0
            assert b[s+h] % p == 0

            M = (p - 1)//2
            L = (ell - 1)//2
            assert L == M + d
            assert L < p

            prefixes = racah_prefixes(n, L)
            x = prefixes[M]
            y = prefixes[L]
            e = y - x

            assert x % p == 0
            assert y % ell == 0
            assert e % (p*p) == 0
            assert y % (p*ell) == 0

            c = e // (p*p)
            t = y // (p*ell)
            assert x == p*ell*t - p*p*c
            assert e == p*p*c
            assert y == p*ell*t

            j_ell = min(s, ell - 1 - s)
            if j_ell <= M:
                inactive_count += 1
                assert e % (ell*ell) == 0
            else:
                active_count += 1
                if c % ell != 0 and first_active_small_quotient_counterexample is None:
                    first_active_small_quotient_counterexample = (
                        n, p, ell, h, s, c % ell
                    )

            degenerate = (b[s] % p == 0) or (b[s+h] % ell == 0)
            if not degenerate:
                pure_cross_count += 1

            pair_count += 1

    print(f"PASS: {pair_count} adjacent target pairs")
    print(f"pure-cross: {pure_cross_count}")
    print(f"inactive ell-boundary: {inactive_count}")
    print(f"active ell-boundary: {active_count}")
```

## 13. Final conclusion

The universal finite-Racah cutoff dependence does provide one clean cross-prime identity:

\[
H_{p+h}(n(n+1))-H_p(n(n+1))
=\sum_{i=1}^{h/2}\phi_{(p-1)/2+i}(n(n+1))^2,
\]

and the right side has a universal \(p^2\)-factor. Combining this with the two targets yields

\[
p(p+h)\mid H_{p+h}(n(n+1))
\]

for the characteristic-zero integer lift of the larger prefix.

But this prefix has logarithmic height \(\Theta(n)\). The exact target lattice (5.3) proves that eliminating it leaves only the universal \(p^2\)-boundary direction. The \(p+h\) factor then has to be inserted explicitly or obtained from an extra same-characteristic zero. After normalizing the \(O(h)\) boundary terms to height \(O(h\log n)\), no target divisibility remains.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the natural finite-Racah cutoff comparison is exactly saturated and does not prove PCGC/PCLG.}
}
\]

A live reopening would require an identity that is simultaneously:

1. special to the fixed Apéry initial state \((1,5)\), not invariant under the cutoff translation \(S_m\mapsto S_m+c\);
2. global enough to transport that state to the half cutoff;
3. yet endowed with a new cancellation reducing the upper-right transfer entry from \(\exp(\Theta(n))\) to \(\exp(O(h\operatorname{polylog}n))\).

No standard contiguous, Gosper, Christoffel--Darboux, local resultant, or bounded-window Racah identity has those three properties.
