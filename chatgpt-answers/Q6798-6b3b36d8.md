# Q6798: average-over-primes route audit

## (i) Prime decomposition of the master sum

From

`M(n) = sum_{r<n/2} log rad_{>max(sqrt(n),r)} gcd(b_r,n-r)`

write `p` for a prime divisor of `n-r`. Since `r<n/2` and `r=n-p` in the relevant top window, the contribution of a prime is

`M_p(n)= log p * #{ r<n/2 : p|n-r and p|b_r, p>max(sqrt(n),r) }`.

Equivalently, putting `r=n-p` (so necessarily `p>n/2` for the single-prime top window),

`M_top(n)= sum_{n/2<p<=n, p|b_{n-p}} log p`.

The radical removes multiplicities: only one `log p` is charged. The valuation bound from the Casoratian argument is what controls the non-radical gcd part separately; it does not change this prime-counting expression.

More generally, for primes `p` in `(sqrt(n),n]`, define

`Z_p={r mod p : b_r=0 mod p}`.

Then

`M(n) <= sum_{sqrt(n)<p<=n} log p * 1_{Z_p intersects {r: r<n/2, r=n mod p}}`

with multiplicity coming only from the number of admissible residue representatives in the range.

## (ii) Exceptional prime tradeoff

Let `E(n)` be the exceptional primes. Split

`M=M_good+M_bad`.

For good primes,

`M_good <= sum_{p notin E, p<=n} log p * B(p)`

where `B(p)` is the allowed number of zero hits in the relevant residue slice.

For bad primes the worst case is the degree budget:

`M_bad <= sum_{p in E, p<=n} 3 p^(2/3) log p`

using the proved unconditional `|Z_p|<<p^(2/3)`; using the absolute trivial bound gives the weaker `p log p`.

Therefore a sufficient condition is

`sum_{p notin E, p<=n} B(p)log p + sum_{p in E,p<=n} p^(2/3)log p = o(n)`.

A stronger but cleaner sufficient condition is

`sum_{p in E,p<=n} p^(2/3)log p=o(n)`.

Since primes near `n` contribute about `n^(2/3)log n`, the exceptional set can contain up to roughly

`o(n^(1/3)/log n)`

worst-case primes near `n` under the unconditional bound. Thus a positive-density exceptional set in the interval `(sqrt(n),n]` is not affordable if those primes behave with the current worst-case bound. The tolerance is generous only if the exceptional primes themselves have much smaller zero sets.

## (iii) Average-over-p sufficient statements

The proposed average statement is essentially exactly the right regime. A sufficient theorem is:

`sum_{p<=X} |Z_p| log p = o(X)`.

Indeed insert `X=n` and restrict to the subset of primes selected by the congruence condition. The master sum is then bounded by the same weighted incidence count.

A second moment variant also suffices. For example, if

`sum_{p<=X} |Z_p|^2 log p << X^(1+epsilon)`

then Cauchy-Schwarz with the prime count in the relevant window gives enough savings after the Lucas reduction, provided the first moment is not simultaneously saturated.

The sharp target is therefore not a fixed-p theorem such as `|Z_p|<<p^theta`, but a weighted average incidence theorem:

`sum_{p<=X} log p * #{r<X : b_r=0 mod p} = o(X)`.

## (iv) What arithmetic input could prove this?

Available structure:

- Lucas/Gessel congruence reduces large indices to residue dynamics modulo p.
- The Apery sequence is modular: the weight-4 level-8 modular form gives Frobenius data.
- Chebotarev controls Frobenius classes of the attached representations.
- Sato-Tate type information controls trace distributions.

However the needed statistic is not just the distribution of Frobenius traces. It is a zero-hit incidence count for the moving orbit

`r -> [b_r:c_r]`

against a fixed line in `P^1(F_p)`.

Chebotarev alone gives averaged conjugacy information; it does not immediately count visits of a growing finite orbit segment. The missing bridge is an averaged orbit-incidence theorem.

A plausible exact research target is:

`sum_{p<=X} log p * |Z_p| <= X^(1-delta)`

for some fixed delta>0, or even the weaker

`=o(X)`.

No such theorem is currently established here. Existing modularity inputs appear compatible with it but do not automatically imply it.

## (v) Verdict

The master sum CAN tolerate a substantial exceptional set, but not arbitrary positive-density bad primes near size n under the current worst-case bounds. The correct route change is not to prove every prime has a small zero set. It is to prove a weighted average incidence estimate over primes.

The decisive reduction is:

`MAIN THEOREM follows from proving sum_{p<=X} log p |Z_p|=o(X)`.

This is a substantially different problem from the fixed-p quenched campaign. It opens large-sieve/Chebotarev-average style approaches, but a genuine orbit-incidence theorem is still required.

Least confident step: the implication from the full master sum with all residue representatives to the clean weighted incidence bound needs careful handling of middle-range primes and wrap classes; the top-window simplification is solid, the complete bookkeeping is the fragile point.

Possible blind spot in the question: it may overestimate what Chebotarev can provide. Frobenius equidistribution is an average over primes, but `p|b_r` is a condition involving a time evolution of length comparable to p; the missing object is dynamical, not merely Galois-theoretic.
