# Audit of the Apéry cross-prime gap-root lemma

## Executive verdict

The proposed inequality is correct, including its logarithm, and it can be sharpened in two useful ways.

First, one only needs roots in the **physical nonwrapping interval**

\[
0\le x\le p-1-h,
\]

not all roots of \(N_h\) in \(\mathbb F_p\). Thus, for integers \(P\ge 7\) and \(2\le H\le P\), define

\[
\rho_h^{\mathrm{phys}}(p)
 =\#\{x\in\{0,\ldots,p-1-h\}:N_h(x)\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|
 \ll \frac{P^2}{H\log P}
 +\sum_{2\le h<H}\sum_{P<p\le2P}
    \rho_h^{\mathrm{phys}}(p).
}
\tag{A}
\]

The range \(H\le P/2\) in the question is safe but unnecessarily restrictive; \(H\le P\) is enough.

Second, the roots omitted by this physical restriction, including the actual \(p=73\) common root

\[
\gcd_{\mathbb F_{73}}(N_3,N_4)=X+3,
\]

admit a strong unconditional aggregate bound. If

\[
\rho_h^{\mathrm{cut}}(p)
 =\#\{x\in\{p-h,\ldots,p-1\}:N_h(x)=0\},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{2\le h<H}\sum_{P<p\le2P}ho_h^{\mathrm{cut}}(p)
 \ll \frac{H^3}{\log P}.
}
\tag{B}
\]

Thus cut-edge pollution is not the uniformity barrier.

There is also a completely explicit bad-prime bound. Let \(\widetilde N_h\) be the primitive part of \(N_h\), and call \((h,p)\) ramified/bad if

\[
p\mid \operatorname{lc}(\widetilde N_h)\operatorname{Disc}(\widetilde N_h).
\]

The all-height squarefreeness theorem in the repository and elementary coefficient-height estimates give

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{2\le h<H}
 \sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\p\mid \operatorname{lc}(\widetilde N_h)\operatorname{Disc}(\widetilde N_h)}}
 \rho_h(p)
 \ll \frac{H^4\log(2H)}{\log P}.
}
\tag{C}
\]

At the proposed optimizing scale

\[
H=P^{1/(3-\delta)},
\]

(C) is smaller than \(PH^{2-\delta}/\log P\) for every fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\), while (B) is smaller for every fixed \(0<\delta<1\).

Consequently the exact remaining obstruction is the following unramified, noncentral, growing-family Frobenius estimate: for some fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\), prove uniformly at \(H=P^{1/(3-\delta)}\) that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{2\le h<H}
 \sum_{\substack{P<p\le2P\\p\nmid\operatorname{lc}(\widetilde N_h)\operatorname{Disc}(\widetilde N_h)}}
 \left(ho_h^{\mathrm{phys}}(p)-\mathbf 1_{2\mid h}\right)
 \ll \frac{P H^{2-\delta}}{\log P}.
}
\tag{UF}_{\delta}
\]

The subtraction is legitimate because every even \(h<p\) has the deterministic physical center root

\[
x=(p-1-h)/2.
\]

No currently banked theorem proves \((\mathrm{UF}_\delta)\). Fixed-\(h\) Chebotarev, finite-height Galois certificates, discriminants, renewal identities, pairwise resultants, polynomial large-sieve bounds, and aggregate products all stop before this first-moment statement.

Therefore:

```text
UNCONDITIONAL POWER IMPROVEMENT: not obtained.
VALIDITY OF THE PROPOSED LEMMA: confirmed.
NEW USEFUL REFINEMENT: physical-root version plus explicit cut/bad-prime disposal.
EXACT BARRIER: uniform Frobenius fixed-point control for h growing like a power of P.
```

The lemma is not a new numerical estimate: after dyadic summation it is the fixed-prime block/restart proof with the order of summation reversed. Its value is as a reduction, because it identifies exactly where a cross-prime theorem could beat the degree budget.

## 1. Normalization and banked inputs

Let

\[
Z_p=\{1\le r<p:p\mid b_r\},
\qquad
b_r=\sum_{k=0}^r\binom rk^2\binom{r+k}{k}^2.
\]

Since \(b_0=1\), this is the same zero count as the repository convention using \(0\le r<p\).

Write

\[
P_0(X)=34X^3+51X^2+27X+5.
\]

The gap continuants satisfy

\[
N_1(X)=1,
\qquad
N_2(X)=P_0(X+1),
\]

and

\[
N_{h+1}(X)
 =P_0(X+h)N_h(X)-(X+h)^6N_{h-1}(X).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The repository proves:

1. \(\deg N_h=3(h-1)\).
2. If \(p\ge7\), \(2\le h\le p\), \(0\le r<r+h<p\), and
   \(p\mid b_r,b_{r+h}\), then
   \(N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod p\).
3. For \(p\ge7\) and \(2\le h\le p\), \(N_h\) is not the zero polynomial modulo \(p\).
4. Two consecutive Apéry numbers cannot both vanish modulo \(p\) in the regular range.
5. Over \(\mathbb Q\), \(N_h\) is squarefree for every \(h\ge2\).
6. The reflection identity is

   \[
   N_h(-h-1-X)=(-1)^{h+1}N_h(X).
   \tag{1.2}
   \]

7. At the pole/cut locations, for \(1\le j\le h\),

   \[
   N_h(-j)
   =(-1)^{j-1}((j-1)!)^3((h-j)!)^3b_{j-1}b_{h-j}.
   \tag{1.3}
   \]

Only these proved inputs are used below.

## 2. Exact proof of the cross-prime inequality

For \(1\le h<p\), define the actual number of zero pairs of gap \(h\) by

\[
C_p(h)
 =\#\{r:1\le r<r+h<p,\ r,r+h\in Z_p\}.
\]

### Proposition 2.1: physical dyadic gap sieve

For \(P\ge7\) and every integer \(H\) with \(2\le H\le P\),

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|
\le
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\left\lceil\frac{p-1}{H}\right\rceil
+
\sum_{2\le h<H}\sum_{P<p\le2P}\rho_h^{\mathrm{phys}}(p).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|
 \ll \frac{P^2}{H\log P}
 +R_{\mathrm{phys}}(P,H),
\tag{2.2}
\]

where

\[
R_{\mathrm{phys}}(P,H)
 =\sum_{2\le h<H}\sum_{P<p\le2P}\rho_h^{\mathrm{phys}}(p).
\]

#### Proof

Fix \(p\in(P,2P]\). Partition the \(p-1\) indices

\[
1,2,\ldots,p-1
\]

into consecutive blocks, each of length at most \(H\). In every nonempty block containing zeros, mark its first zero. Every other zero in that block has an immediately preceding zero in the same block. The gap between those two zeros is an integer \(h\) satisfying

\[
1\le h<H.
\]

Gap \(h=1\) is impossible by the no-consecutive-zero theorem. Hence

\[
|Z_p|
 \le \left\lceil\frac{p-1}{H}\right\rceil
 +\sum_{2\le h<H}C_p(h).
\tag{2.3}
\]

For a pair counted by \(C_p(h)\), its starting index satisfies

\[
1\le r\le p-1-h.
\]

All recurrence denominators used in transporting from \(r\) to \(r+h\) are nonzero modulo \(p\), and the banked gap-continuant implication gives

\[
N_h(r)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Therefore

\[
C_p(h)\le\rho_h^{\mathrm{phys}}(p).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Substituting (2.4) into (2.3) and summing over \(p\) proves (2.1).

For the first term, Chebyshev's dyadic prime bound gives

\[
\pi(2P)-\pi(P)\ll\frac{P}{\log P}.
\]

Since \(p\le2P\),

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\left\lceil\frac{p-1}{H}\right\rceil
\le
\left(\frac{2P}{H}+1\right)(\pi(2P)-\pi(P)).
\]

Because \(H\le P\), the additive \(P/\log P\) term is absorbed by \(P^2/(H\log P)\). This proves (2.2). \(\square\)

### Endpoint audit

The proof requires only

\[
h<H\le P<p.
\]

Thus \(h<p\), the polynomial nonvanishing theorem applies, and all physical recurrence denominators are units modulo \(p\). The question's condition \(H\le P/2\) is more than enough.

If \(H\) is initially real, replace it by \(\lfloor H\rfloor\); this changes only absolute constants.

The finitely many dyadic blocks below \(P=7\) are harmless.

### Zero-polynomial and degree-drop audit

For every pair in the sum,

\[
2\le h<H\le P<p,
\]

so the repository's nonvanishing theorem proves that \(N_h\bmod p\) is not identically zero. A prime may annihilate the characteristic-zero leading coefficient, causing the degree to drop, but this can only decrease the number of distinct roots. Repeated roots likewise do not invalidate

\[
\rho_h(p)\le3(h-1).
\]

No discriminant restriction is needed for this elementary degree bound.

### Logarithm audit

Using the nominal degree bound,

\[
\begin{aligned}
R(P,H)
&\le \sum_{2\le h<H}3(h-1)(\pi(2P)-\pi(P))\\
&\ll \frac{P}{\log P}\sum_{h<H}h
 \ll \frac{P H^2}{\log P}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|
 \ll \frac{1}{\log P}
 \left(\frac{P^2}{H}+PH^2\right).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Balancing at \(H\asymp P^{1/3}\) gives

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|
 \ll \frac{P^{5/3}}{\log P}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Since the exponent is greater than one, ordinary dyadic summation yields

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|
 \ll \frac{X^{5/3}}{\log X}.
\]

There is no missing logarithm in this derivation.

## 3. The proposed power-saving implication

Assume that for a fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly in the required range,

\[
R(P,H)\ll\frac{P H^{2-\delta}}{\log P}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then (2.2) gives

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|
 \ll \frac1{\log P}
 \left(\frac{P^2}{H}+PH^{2-\delta}\right).
\]

The two terms balance when

\[
H^{3-\delta}=P,
\qquad
H=P^{1/(3-\delta)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|
 \ll
\frac{X^{\,2-1/(3-\delta)}}{\log X}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

For small \(\delta\),

\[
2-\frac1{3-\delta}
 =\frac53-\frac{\delta}{9}+O(\delta^2).
\]

This implication is correct.

There is, however, a deterministic lower floor. When \(h\) is even, (1.2) forces a root at the reflection center. Modulo an odd prime \(p>h\), its physical representative is

\[
x_{h,p}=\frac{p-1-h}{2},
\]

and

\[
0\le x_{h,p}<x_{h,p}+h<p.
\]

Hence

\[
R_{\mathrm{phys}}(P,H)
\ge
\left\lfloor\frac{H-2}{2}\right\rfloor
(\pi(2P)-\pi(P)).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the best conceivable power in (3.1) is \(2-\delta=1\), i.e. \(\delta\le1\). The expected optimal scale is \(R\asymp PH/\log P\), not smaller.

## 4. Cut-edge roots and the \(p=73\) warning

The distinction between all roots and physical roots is essential whenever resultants or common-root statements are used.

Represent every residue in \(\mathbb F_p\) by an integer in \([0,p-1]\). The nonphysical residues for gap \(h\) are exactly

\[
p-h,p-h+1,\ldots,p-1.
\]

Write such a residue as \(x=p-j\equiv-j\pmod p\), where \(1\le j\le h\). Since \(p>h\), the factorials in (1.3) are nonzero modulo \(p\). Therefore

\[
N_h(p-j)\equiv0\pmod p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\mid b_{j-1}b_{h-j}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This gives two bounds.

### 4.1 Fixed-prime cut bound

For fixed \(p\), each cut root is caused by a zero among

\[
b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_{h-1}.
\]

Each such zero can occur in at most two positions \(j\), so

\[
\rho_h^{\mathrm{cut}}(p)
\le 2\#\{0\le n<h:p\mid b_n\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The banked subinterval zero bound gives

\[
\rho_h^{\mathrm{cut}}(p)\ll h^{2/3}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Summing this estimate gives

\[
R_{\mathrm{cut}}(P,H)
\ll \frac{P H^{5/3}}{\log P}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### 4.2 Stronger cross-prime cut bound

Reversing the \((h,n,p)\) sums is better in the polynomial range. From (4.1),

\[
R_{\mathrm{cut}}(P,H)
\le
2\sum_{2\le h<H}
 \sum_{0\le n<h}
 \#\{P<p\le2P:p\mid b_n\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The elementary height estimate

\[
b_n\le(n+1)64^n
\]

implies

\[
\#\{P<p\le2P:p\mid b_n\}
\le\frac{\log b_n}{\log P}
\ll\frac{n}{\log P}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{cut}}(P,H)
&\ll \frac1{\log P}
 \sum_{n<H}(H-n)n\\
&\ll \frac{H^3}{\log P}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This proves (B).

At the target scale (3.2),

\[
\frac{H^3}{P H^{2-\delta}}
 =\frac{H^{1+\delta}}{P}
 =P^{(2\delta-2)/(3-\delta)}.
\]

It tends to zero for every fixed \(\delta<1\). Thus cut roots cannot obstruct any realistic power saving.

### 4.3 The prime \(73\)

At \(p=73\), one has \(b_2=73\), and

\[
\gcd_{\mathbb F_{73}}(N_3,N_4)=X+3.
\]

The common root is

\[
x=-3\equiv70\pmod{73}.
\]

For gap \(3\), the physical interval ends at \(69\); for gap \(4\), it ends at \(68\). Thus \(x=70\) is nonphysical for both gaps. Formula (4.1) explains it directly through \(p\mid b_2\).

This example invalidates any assertion that characteristic-zero coprimality transfers to raw modular coprimality for every prime. It does **not** invalidate Proposition 2.1, because that proposition uses only one polynomial at a time and only physical roots. It also does not invalidate a properly saturated common-root statement.

## 5. Ramified and degree-drop primes are quantitatively harmless

The all-height theorem \(\gcd(N_h,N_h')=1\) over \(\mathbb Q\) makes the discriminant nonzero. We now bound all primes where the reduction is not an etale polynomial of its nominal degree.

Let

\[
L_h=\|N_h\|_1
\]

be the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients. From (1.1),

\[
\|P_0(X+h)\|_1\ll(h+1)^3,
\qquad
\|(X+h)^6\|_1=(h+1)^6.
\]

Hence

\[
L_{h+1}
\le C(h+1)^6\max(L_h,L_{h-1})
\]

for an absolute \(C\). Iteration gives

\[
\log L_h\ll h\log(2h).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let \(\widetilde N_h\) be the primitive part and \(d_h=\deg\widetilde N_h\le3(h-1)\). A standard resultant/Hadamard bound for an integer polynomial gives

\[
\log\left|
\operatorname{lc}(\widetilde N_h)
\operatorname{Disc}(\widetilde N_h)
\right|
\ll d_h\log d_h+d_h\log L_h
\ll h^2\log(2h).
\tag{5.2}
\]

For \(p>P\ge H>h\), the repository's nonzero-polynomial theorem excludes \(p\) from the content of \(N_h\). The number of primes \(p\in(P,2P]\) dividing the integer in (5.2) is at most

\[
\ll\frac{h^2\log(2h)}{\log P}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

At each such prime, the crude root bound is \(\rho_h(p)\le3h\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{bad}}(P,H)
&\ll\sum_{h<H}
 h\cdot\frac{h^2\log(2h)}{\log P}\\
&\ll\frac{H^4\log(2H)}{\log P}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

At \(H=P^{1/(3-\delta)}\), the ratio of (5.4) to the desired scale \(PH^{2-\delta}/\log P\) is

\[
\frac{H^{2+\delta}\log(2H)}{P}
 =P^{(-1+2\delta)/(3-\delta)}\log(2H).
\]

This tends to zero for every fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\).

So neither discriminant primes nor leading-coefficient primes are the barrier to a small exponent gain.

## 6. Factor counts and fixed-height Chebotarev

Factor the primitive squarefree polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\):

\[
\widetilde N_h=f_{h,1}\cdots f_{h,\nu_h},
\]

where the \(f_{h,j}\) are distinct irreducibles. For every fixed \(h\), the Frobenius permutation on the complex roots has as many fixed points as \(N_h\) has roots modulo a good prime. Burnside's lemma, or equivalently the prime ideal theorem applied to the root fields, gives

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\rho_h(p)
 =\nu_h\bigl(\operatorname{Li}(2P)-\operatorname{Li}(P)\bigr)
 +o_h\left(\frac{P}{\log P}\right)
\tag{6.1}
\]

as \(P\to\infty\) with \(h\) fixed.

This explains the observed bounded mean. It does not prove the required growing-family estimate.

Two independent uniform statements would be needed:

\[
\sum_{h<H}\nu_h\ll H^{2-\delta}
\tag{6.2}
\]

and an error term in (6.1) summable for

\[
h<H=P^{1/(3-\delta)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Neither is banked.

### 6.1 Factor-count barrier

Reflection gives a forced linear factor for even \(h\) and a polynomial-in-a-square structure after centering. This reduces constants, not exponents. In the absence of an all-height irreducibility/factor-number theorem, the only unconditional bound remains

\[
\nu_h\le3(h-1),
\]

whose sum is \(O(H^2)\).

The repository has strong finite-height certificates and an all-height squarefreeness theorem, but squarefreeness does not limit the number of irreducible factors.

### 6.2 Chebotarev uniformity barrier

Even granting the conjectural all-height factor law \(\nu_h=O(1)\), fixed-field Chebotarev is not uniform at polynomial height.

The relevant root fields have total degree \(O(h)\), and (5.2) permits discriminants of exponential size in \(h^2\log h\). Standard unconditional effective Chebotarev and prime-ideal theorems have ranges and error terms depending on the degree and discriminant. Results of Lagarias--Odlyzko and modern refinements such as Thorner--Zaman do not furnish a main term uniformly when \(h=P^c\). They are effective for fixed fields, or for fields whose degree/conductor grows at most very slowly compared with \(\log P\), not for the present polynomial range.

The small-gap Chebotarev skeleton already in the repository reaches only a slowly growing cutoff of roughly square-root-logarithmic type. Such a cutoff cannot affect the power balance in (2.6): saving the first \((\log P)^A\) gaps is negligible compared with the \(H^2\) degree budget at \(H=P^{1/3+o(1)}\).

### 6.3 Diagnostic under GRH

This is not an unconditional result, but it quantifies the size of the barrier. Under GRH for the Dedekind zeta functions of all root fields, the prime-ideal theorem would give, schematically for one \(h\),

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\rho_h(p)
\ll \nu_h\frac{P}{\log P}
 +P^{1/2}\bigl(h^2\log(2h)+h\log P\bigr).
\tag{6.4}
\]

If one additionally had \(\nu_h=O(1)\) for all \(h\), summing (6.4) would yield

\[
R(P,H)
\ll \frac{PH}{\log P}
 +P^{1/2}\bigl(H^3\log(2H)+H^2\log P\bigr).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Balancing the block term against the dominant GRH error gives approximately

\[
H=P^{3/8+o(1)},
\qquad
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|\ll P^{13/8+o(1)}.
\]

Thus even the conditional route requires both an all-height factor theorem and analytic uniformity; GRH by itself is not the missing proof. Unconditionally, the available range is much shorter.

## 7. Why discriminants do not control distinct roots

A large value of \(\rho_h(p)\) means that Frobenius has many fixed points on the root set. It does **not** imply that two roots collide modulo \(p\).

The discriminant detects repeated roots. For a good prime one may have all \(3(h-1)\) roots distinct and rational over \(\mathbb F_p\), while the discriminant remains a unit. Hence a divisor count on \(\operatorname{Disc}(N_h)\) can dispose of ramification, as in Section 5, but cannot bound the unramified first moment.

Similarly,

\[
\operatorname{Res}(N_h,X^p-X)
\]

is tailored to the root event but depends on \(p\); it is not a fixed characteristic-zero integer whose prime divisors can be counted.

This is the precise category distinction:

```text
Discriminant/resultant between fixed polynomials: collision or ramification.
Number of distinct F_p-roots: Frobenius splitting statistic.
```

The desired estimate is of the second kind.

## 8. Renewal identities and saturated resultants

The continuants satisfy the exact renewal identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{m+g+1}(X)
={}&N_{m+1}(X)N_g(X+m+1)\\
&-(X+m+1)^6N_m(X)N_{g-1}(X+m+2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Together with adjacent coprimality, this converts a common root of two gap polynomials, after the necessary regularity/saturation conditions, into a shifted root of a shorter gap polynomial. This is valuable for second moments and common-root geometry.

It does not bound

\[
\sum_{h<H}\rho_h(p).
\]

Indeed, the first moment can be maximal at the degree scale while the root sets of the different \(N_h\) are pairwise disjoint. At the critical range \(H=P^{1/3+o(1)}\), the total degree is

\[
\sum_{h<H}3(h-1)\asymp H^2=o(P),
\]

so there is ample room in \(\mathbb F_p\) for such a scattered configuration. In that model every pairwise resultant/common-root statistic vanishes, yet the first moment is \(\asymp H^2\).

Therefore even a perfect theorem asserting saturated pairwise coprimality for all \(h\ne k\) would not improve \(R(P,H)\). It may actually make disjoint degree saturation easier, not harder.

The \(p=73\) example shows why the word **saturated** is mandatory, but saturation does not repair this first-moment obstruction.

## 9. Polynomial large sieve and determinant methods

### 9.1 Ordinary large sieve

For \(p\asymp P\), each residue class modulo \(p\) contains only \(O(1)\) integers in an interval of length \(O(P)\). A dual large-sieve setup for the varying root sets therefore has the usual \(P^2\) large-sieve constant. It does not produce a bound of size

\[
\frac{P H^{2-\delta}}{\log P}
\]

when \(H=P^{1/3+o(1)}\).

A large sieve for Frobenius can exploit a compatible bounded-conductor family. Here the degree, discriminant, and transfer length all grow with \(h\). No fixed-rank, bounded-conductor two-parameter sheaf in \((x,h)\) has been constructed. Applying a Frobenius large sieve separately to each \(h\) merely reintroduces degree/conductor dependence and does not survive summation to polynomial \(H\).

### 9.2 Determinant method

The congruence

\[
p\mid N_h(x)
\]

has polynomial degree \(3(h-1)\), which itself grows through the entire range being summed. A determinant method applied one \(h\) at a time therefore inherits a factor polynomial in \(h\), and summing returns the \(H^2\) budget.

The recurrence in \(h\) is a product of a growing number of transfer matrices; it is not presently encoded by a bounded-degree algebraic surface in the three variables \((x,h,p)\). Without such a fixed-complexity object, the determinant method has no uniformity lever.

## 10. Aggregate product arguments

Let

\[
F_H(X)=\prod_{2\le h<H}N_h(X).
\]

Then

\[
\deg F_H=\sum_{2\le h<H}3(h-1)\asymp H^2.
\]

For every prime,

\[
\#\{x:F_H(x)=0\}
\le\sum_{h<H}\rho_h(p),
\]

with equality if the root sets are disjoint. Thus the degree bound for \(F_H\) is exactly the existing \(H^2\) bound; taking the product does not compress the problem.

A product over integer values is also too large. From the recurrence and (5.1), for \(0\le x<2P\),

\[
\log|N_h(x)|\ll h\log(P+H).
\]

Consequently

\[
\log\prod_{0\le x<2P}|N_h(x)|
\ll Ph\log P.
\]

Counting prime divisors \(p\asymp P\) yields only

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\rho_h(p)\ll Ph,
\]

which is weaker by a factor \(\log P\) than the trivial degree-per-prime estimate

\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\rho_h(p)
\ll\frac{Ph}{\log P}.
\]

Summing over \(h\) therefore cannot improve the exponent.

## 11. Sharpest rigorous reduction after all disposals

Define

\[
\kappa_h=\mathbf1_{2\mid h}.
\]

Let \(R_{\mathrm{good}}^\circ(P,H)\) be the sum over \(2\le h<H\) and primes \(P<p\le2P\) satisfying

\[
p\nmid\operatorname{lc}(\widetilde N_h)\operatorname{Disc}(\widetilde N_h)
\]

of the number of **physical** roots other than the forced center root. Then Sections 2, 4, and 5 give the unconditional decomposition

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{P<p\le2P}|Z_p|
\ll{}&\frac{P^2}{H\log P}
 +\frac{PH}{\log P}\\
&+R_{\mathrm{good}}^\circ(P,H)
 +\frac{H^3}{\log P}
 +\frac{H^4\log(2H)}{\log P}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.1}
\]

The \(PH/\log P\) term is the forced center skeleton. The \(H^3/\log P\) term covers all cut-edge pollution. The \(H^4\log H/\log P\) term covers every degree-drop or ramified prime.

Fix \(0<\delta<1/2\) and take

\[
H=P^{1/(3-\delta)}.
\]

Then all displayed terms other than the block term and \(R_{\mathrm{good}}^\circ\) are

\[
o\left(\frac{P H^{2-\delta}}{\log P}\right).
\]

Therefore the following is a sufficient and essentially exact remaining statement:

\[
\boxed{
R_{\mathrm{good}}^\circ(P,H)
\ll\frac{P H^{2-\delta}}{\log P}
\quad
\text{for }H=P^{1/(3-\delta)},\quad0<\delta<1/2.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

This is a pure unramified Frobenius first-moment theorem for a polynomially growing continuant family. It contains none of the known saturation pathology.

---

## 12. Is the lemma new or merely a repackaging?

As an inequality, it is a repackaging.

For each fixed prime, the existing proof is

\[
|Z_p|
\le \frac{p}{H}+O(1)+\sum_{h<H}\rho_h(p).
\]

Summing this over \(P<p\le2P\) gives the proposed cross-prime lemma. No new cancellation occurs in that summation, and inserting the degree bound reproduces exactly the old \(p^{2/3}\) optimization.

It is nevertheless useful for three reasons.

1. It separates the block-combinatorial term from a single arithmetic remainder \(R(P,H)\).
2. It makes clear that fixed-\(h\) average root count is not enough; the needed theorem is uniform for \(h\) of polynomial size.
3. The physical-root refinement permits cut-edge and ramified-prime pollution to be removed rigorously, leaving the precise good-prime Frobenius statistic (11.2).

So the right description is:

```text
Not a new bound.
A useful and now sharpened reduction.
```

---

## Final verdict

The proposed block/pair inequality is valid, with the correct factor \(1/\log P\), and it holds in the stronger range \(2\le H\le P\). There is no zero-polynomial exception in this range. Degree drops and repeated roots are harmless for the trivial estimate.

Raw characteristic-zero coprimality must not be transferred modulo every prime. The \(p=73\) factor \(X+3\) is real, but it is a cut-edge root. The physical formulation removes it, and all cut-edge roots satisfy the aggregate bound \(O(H^3/\log P)\). All ramified and degree-drop pairs contribute only \(O(H^4\log H/\log P)\), which is lower order for any target saving \(0<\delta<1/2\).

No unconditional exponent improvement follows from the audited tools. The exact stopping point is not saturation, discriminants, or factor counting at fixed height. It is the absence of a uniform theorem controlling the number of Frobenius fixed points of \(N_h\) for

\[
h\le P^{1/(3-\delta)}.
\]

A proof of (11.2) for any fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\) would immediately give

\[
\sum_{p\le X}|Z_p|
\ll
\frac{X^{\,2-1/(3-\delta)}}{\log X},
\]

a genuine improvement over \(X^{5/3}/\log X\). At present, that growing-family Frobenius estimate is a new missing theorem, not a consequence of fixed-height Chebotarev or of the continuant identities already proved.
