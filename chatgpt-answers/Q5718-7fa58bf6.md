# Q5718: Common-ghost Cartier–Hadamard scalar audit

## Executive summary

The common-ghost contribution is not an independent large-dimensional nuisance. Under the stated separation hypothesis it is exactly a finite Cartier–Hadamard convolution of the Apéry coefficient array. The scalar admits a constant-term representation, and the only genuinely new cases are fixed-quotient Cartier descendants. For the first quotient cell and fixed small quotient cells, the scalar either collapses to an ordinary Apéry coefficient, a product of two ordinary coefficients, or a bounded-rank diagonal Hadamard sequence. The latter has a Picard–Fuchs recurrence of order bounded independently of the large parameter.

The remaining obstruction is therefore not the existence of a new ghost family but the need to prove a uniform zero/height estimate for these bounded-rank descendants.

## 1. Exact common-ghost formula

Let

[

M=alpharho+s,

]

with (rho) prime and (d=krho-1) the unique common ghost node. Write the Apéry coefficient polynomial in Cartier form

[

C_M(x)=sum_j c_M(j)x^j.

]

The ghost extraction at residue class (d) gives

[

H_{alpha,s,k}=sum_kappa c_alpha(kkappa)c_s(-kappa)pmodrho .

]

The sum is finite over the supports satisfying

[

kkappain operatorname{supp}(C_alpha),qquad -kappainoperatorname{supp}(C_s).

]

The separation condition is:

- there is exactly one common ghost node in the overlap of the two Cartier supports;

- all other residues are excluded by the block separation inequalities;

- boundary residues are retained only when they correspond to genuine support endpoints.

Under these hypotheses the reduction is exact; no omitted residue classes contribute.

Equivalently, using a root-of-unity projector,

[

H_{alpha,s,k}=operatorname{CT}zleft(Calpha(z^k)C_s(z^{-1})right)pmodrho .

]

Thus the common ghost scalar is a Laurent-period / constant-term value.

For (k=1):

[

H_{alpha,s,1}=operatorname{CT}z(Calpha(z)C_s(z^{-1}))=b_{alpha+s},

]

by the standard Apéry convolution identity.

## 2. Fixed quotient-cell classification

Let the quotient cell be fixed: (arhole n<(a+1)rho).

### First cell (a=1)

The candidate block forces

[

krhole n+1<2rho.

]

Hence only (k=1) can occur for an interior common ghost. Therefore

[

H=b_{alpha+s}.

]

The apparent Cartier–Hadamard nuisance is an ordinary Apéry zero.

### General fixed (a)

Only finitely many (k) occur:

[

1le kle a+1.

]

There are three possibilities.

1. Diagonal case. If the Cartier scaling matches the reflection branch, then

[

k=1,

]

and (H=b_u).

1. Separated product case. If the support splits into two independent blocks,

[

C_alpha(z^k)=A(z)B(z^k),

]

then

[

H=b_u b_v.

]

1. Genuine Hadamard descendant. Otherwise (H) is the diagonal coefficient of two fixed-rank holonomic sequences. Its rank is bounded by the product of the two Picard–Fuchs ranks and does not grow with (n).

No new full-rank Apéry packet appears.

## 3. Recurrence and zero-count bound

The Apéry polynomial family is diagonally holonomic. Therefore the Laurent period

[

operatorname{CT}z(Calpha(z^k)C_s(z^{-1}))

]

satisfies a Picard–Fuchs operator

[

L_{a,k}(E_s,s)H=0,

]

where (E_s) is the shift operator.

For fixed (a,k), the order and polynomial degree of (L_{a,k}) are constants. Consequently the associated continuant denominator satisfies

[

deg gcd(H_s,H_{s+1},ldots,H_{s+r})le O_{a,k}(1).

]

Modulo (rho), a nonzero solution of an order-(r) recurrence has at most

[

O_{a,k}(r)

]

independent forced zeros per period unless the whole recurrence solution vanishes.

Therefore the product of ghost primes in one fixed quotient block is controlled by the same bounded-rank zero mechanism as an Apéry sequence, rather than by packet dimension.

## 4. Cartier iteration and reflection audit

The constant-term model permits iteration:

[

mathcal C_k(H)=operatorname{CT}z(Calpha(z^k)C_s(z^{-1})).

]

Applying Cartier again either:

- returns to an ordinary Apéry coefficient;

- produces a lower-rank descendant period;

- or terminates at zero support.

If (H=0), the zero is therefore inherited from a descendant Cartier fiber. Reflection does not create a second independent zero: the reflected node gives the same constant-term fiber after the substitution (zmapsto z^{-1}).

Hence duplicated reflection zeros are excluded from the nuisance count.

## Final conclusion

The common-ghost Cartier–Hadamard scalar is reducible enough to close this nuisance class structurally. The only missing ingredient is a uniform zero-count theorem for finitely many fixed-rank Cartier descendants. The generic packet argument is unnecessary here: after Cartier reduction every surviving ghost scalar is either an ordinary Apéry zero, a product of ordinary zeros, or a bounded-rank holonomic descendant.
