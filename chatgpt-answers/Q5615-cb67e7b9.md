The exact obstruction has two equivalent forms.

1. In the canonical integral recurrence gauge, the \(2h\)-step transfer crosses the singular index \(2p\). Its localized Smith exponents at \(p\) are exactly
   
   
   \[
   (-3,+3).
   \]

There is no residual Smith factor \(p\), \(q\), or \(pq\) in the primitive elimination matrix. In particular, the short endpoint gap \(2h\) supplies no new cross-prime divisor.

This is a precise negative theorem for the proposed architecture. It does not rule out an Apéry-specific global identity using additional hypergeometric or \(G\)-function structure; it rules out the mechanism consisting only of the two fixed-order local quotient laws plus canonical recurrence transport.

## 3. Exact local Smith calculation

### 3.1 The \(p\)-adic calculation

Only the two consecutive matrices \(M_{2p-1}\) and \(M_{2p}\) are singular modulo \(p\). All other factors in \(B_{2p-1,2q-1}\) are invertible over \(\mathbf Z_p\), provided \(0<h<p/2\), which is automatic for two top-half primes of the same \(n\).

Their determinants satisfy

\[
v_p(\det M_{2p-1})=3,
\qquad
v_p(\det M_{2p})=3.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Moreover

\[
M_{2p-1}\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}
-5&1\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod p,
\qquad
M_{2p}\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}
5&0\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod p.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence

\[
M_{2p}M_{2p-1}\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}
-25&5\\
-5&1
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod p,
\tag{3.3}
\]

which has rank one and contains a unit entry. Therefore the product has first Smith invariant a \(p\)-adic unit, while its determinant has valuation six. Thus

\[
\operatorname{SNF}_{\mathbf Z_p}(B_{2p-1,2q-1})
 \sim \operatorname{diag}(1,p^6).
\tag{3.4}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
v_p(D_{2p-1,2q-1})=3,
\tag{3.5}
\]

because the denominator product contains exactly one multiple of \(p\), namely \(2p\), and it occurs cubed.

Consequently the rational transfer has Cartan/Smith exponents

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{SmithExp}_p(T_{p,q})=(-3,+3).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Equivalently, there exist \(U_p,V_p\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf Z_p)\) such that

\[
T_{p,q}=U_p
\begin{pmatrix}
p^{-3}&0\\
0&p^3
\end{pmatrix}
V_p.
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is the exact lattice distortion hidden by the unit determinant.

### 3.2 The \(q\)-adic calculation

The forward interval ends before the singular pair \(2q-1,2q\), so \(T_{p,q}\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf Z_q)\). But the inverse transport crosses that pair. Applying the same calculation to \(T_{q,p}=T_{p,q}^{-1}\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{SmithExp}_q(T_{p,q}^{-1})=(-3,+3).
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Equivalently, expressing the \(p\)-endpoint state integrally in terms of the \(q\)-endpoint state requires clearing \(q^3\).

### 3.3 Height

Every entry of the cleared matrix \(B_{2p-1,2q-1}\) has logarithmic height

\[
O(h\log n),
\tag{3.9}
\]

because it is a product of \(2h\) matrices whose entries are \(O(n^3)\). The same bound holds for \(D_{2p-1,2q-1}\).

Thus the short transfer itself has the desired gap-scale height. The failure is not an Archimedean explosion at this stage. The failure is that its primitive integral lattice content is trivial: the only moving prime powers forced into integral coefficients are the singularity-clearers \(p^3\) and, in the reverse direction, \(q^3\).

## 4. Primitive elimination of the endpoint state

Write the \(p\)-endpoint state as

\[
Y_p=\binom{b_{2p-1}}{b_{2p-2}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
Y_q=T_{p,q}Y_p,
\qquad
Y_q=\binom{b_{2q-1}}{b_{2q-2}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The \(p\)-local order-eight row can be written schematically as

\[
L_p(Y_p,\xi_p)-c\,\frac{b_n}{p}\equiv0\pmod{p^8},
\tag{4.3}
\]

and the \(q\)-local row as

\[
L_q(Y_q,\xi_q)-c\,\frac{b_n}{q}\equiv0\pmod{q^8},
\qquad c=830745,
\tag{4.4}
\]

where \(\xi_p,\xi_q\) collect the remaining locally reconstructed endpoint/jet data. Substituting (4.2) gives

\[
L_q(T_{p,q}Y_p,\xi_q)-c\,\frac{b_n}{q}\equiv0\pmod{q^8}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

There are two scalar congruences but the transported rank-two state still has two coordinates. The local Casoratian eliminated the companion coordinate internal to each characteristic; it did not turn the endpoint recurrence state into a one-dimensional object across characteristics.

Take any primitive \(2\times2\) coefficient minor used to eliminate \(Y_p\). Over \(\mathbf Z[1/pq]\), \(T_{p,q}\) is invertible and the two rows are independent only through their separate local right sides. Therefore the elimination ideal intersects the coefficient ring in the ideal generated by the two local moduli and by the diagonal compatibility

\[
q\left(\frac{b_n}{q}\right)-p\left(\frac{b_n}{p}\right)=0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Equation (4.6) is exactly the common-diagonal identity \(b_n-b_n=0\). It is the only cross-prime scalar relation supplied by the fact that both local laws reconstruct the same \(b_n\).

When one returns from \(\mathbf Z[1/pq]\) to \(\mathbf Z\), the Smith calculations show that the saturation quotient is killed only by the denominator clearers \(p^3\) and \(q^3\). Therefore every integral scalar consequence has the form

\[
C
 =A\,b_n+p^3C_p+q^3C_q,
\tag{4.7}
\]

with \(A,C_p,C_q\) integral expressions of logarithmic height \(O(h\log n)\) plus the heights of the local endpoint coordinates.

There is no primitive residual factor \(pq\). Hence:

- if \(A\ne0\), the certificate factors through \(b_n\), and its \(p,q\)-divisibility is the original diagonal divisibility;
- if \(A=0\), any \(p\)- or \(q\)-factor comes from the displayed coefficient factors \(p^3,q^3\), so it is tautological;
- if one divides out those moving coefficient factors, the primitive scalar consequence is a unit in both characteristics on the pure-cross locus.

This is the requested determinant/Smith obstruction.

## 5. Why pure cross is decisive

Section 12 proves that for a pure-cross pair the ordinary gap continuant is a unit in both residue fields:

\[
p\nmid N_h(t),
\qquad
q\nmid N_h(t).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus no same-prime singular direction is available at the short target-index gap \(h\). The endpoint transport considered here has gap \(2h\), but its only singular directions occur at the fixed recurrence singularities \(2p\) and \(2q\), not at the target zeros \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\) and \(b_t\equiv0\pmod q\).

Consequently the two sources of divisibility are disjoint:

- target-zero divisibility lives in different characteristics at indices \(r\) and \(t\);
- endpoint-transfer nonintegrality lives at the recurrence singular steps \(2p\) and \(2q\).

The order-eight laws convert each target zero into a local quotient coordinate, but the short transfer does not align those two characteristic-specific quotient lines. It transports the full two-dimensional endpoint state and preserves one free direction.

That free direction is exactly what allows the countermodel below.

## 6. Canonical-recurrence countermodel

The countermodel uses the exact canonical Apéry recurrence coefficients; it changes only the global initial state.

Fix distinct primes \(p<q=p+h\), a common outer index \(n\), and arbitrary prescribed local quotient residues

\[
\alpha\in\mathbf Z/p^8\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\beta\in\mathbf Z/q^8\mathbf Z.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Consider the rank-two solution module of (1.1) over

\[
R=\mathbf Z\left[\frac1{\prod_{k\le 2q}k}\right].
\tag{6.2}
\]

Evaluation at any nonsingular adjacent pair is an \(R\)-linear isomorphism from the solution module to \(R^2\). The order-eight \(p\)-law is one affine linear condition on this two-dimensional state modulo \(p^8\); the order-eight \(q\)-law, after applying \(T_{p,q}\), is one affine linear condition modulo \(q^8\).

Because \(p^8\) and \(q^8\) are coprime and the primitive row coefficients are units away from the fixed inversion exception, the two conditions can be imposed independently by CRT on the two coordinates of one initial state. Therefore there exists a canonical-recurrence solution \(u\) satisfying both local order-eight laws with

\[
\frac{u_n}{p}\equiv\alpha\pmod{p^8},
\qquad
\frac{u_n}{q}\equiv\beta\pmod{q^8},
\tag{6.3}
\]

subject only to the obvious compatibility that \(u_n\) is one scalar.

Equivalently, after choosing any integer \(X\) with

\[
X/p\equiv\alpha\pmod{p^8},
\qquad
X/q\equiv\beta\pmod{q^8},
\tag{6.4}
\]

one can choose the free initial state so that \(u_n=X\) and both endpoint identities hold. The short transfer is automatically satisfied because it is merely recurrence propagation of that same state.

Hence any universal scalar certificate obtained from:

1. the canonical recurrence,
2. the \(p\)-order-eight law,
3. the \(q\)-order-eight law, and
4. the \(2h\)-step endpoint transfer,

must vanish for every CRT-compatible \(X\). Therefore it is identically zero after saturation, or is a multiple of the diagonal compatibility relation (4.6), or contains the moduli \(p^8,q^8\) or the singularity-clearers \(p^3,q^3\) in its coefficients.

This countermodel is stronger than an abstract CRT no-go because it preserves the exact canonical cubic recurrence and the exact short endpoint transfer matrix. What it does not preserve is the distinguished Apéry initial state \((1,5)\) and its global hypergeometric realization. Therefore any successful horizontal theorem must use that distinguished global state in a way not reducible to fixed-order local reconstruction.

## 7. Exact no-go theorem

### Theorem

Let \(p<q=p+h\) be odd primes with \(q<n<2p\), and suppose \(p,q\mid b_n\) form a pure-cross target pair. Let \(\mathcal L_p,\mathcal L_q\) denote the denominator-free precision-eight target Casoratian laws of
`Q32_ORDER_EIGHT_TARGET_CASORATIAN_2026-07-29.md`, and let \(T_{p,q}\) be the canonical Apéry transfer from the state at \(2p-1\) to the state at \(2q-1\).

Then:

1. \(T_{p,q}\) has logarithmic coefficient height \(O(h\log n)\).
2. Its localized Smith exponents are
   
   \[
   \operatorname{SmithExp}_p(T_{p,q})=(-3,+3),
   \qquad
   \operatorname{SmithExp}_q(T_{p,q}^{-1})=(-3,+3).
   \]
3. After primitive saturation by \(p\) and \(q\), the scalar elimination ideal generated by \(\mathcal L_p,\mathcal L_q\), and the transfer equations contains no nonzero element forced to be divisible by \(pq\).
4. Before saturation, every such scalar element is a sum of a multiple of \(b_n\) and terms whose coefficients are divisible by \(p^3\) or \(q^3\).

Therefore this architecture cannot construct a nonzero selective certificate \(C_{n,p,q}\) satisfying

\[
pq\mid C_{n,p,q},
\qquad
\log|C_{n,p,q}|=O(h\operatorname{polylog} n),
\tag{7.1}
\]

unless the \(pq\)-divisibility is either inherited from \(b_n\) or inserted into the coefficients through the moving factors \(p,q\).

### Proof

Parts 1 and 2 are (3.1)--(3.9). Over \(\mathbf Z[1/pq]\), the transfer is invertible and the two local laws are affine reconstructions of the two local quotients of one scalar \(b_n\). Eliminating the endpoint state therefore leaves only the diagonal identity (4.6). Restoring integrality introduces precisely the Smith denominator clearers of Parts 2, namely \(p^3\) and \(q^3\). Pure-crossness rules out any additional continuant factor in either characteristic by (5.1). Hence the primitive elimination ideal has no \(p\)- or \(q\)-torsion, proving Parts 3 and 4 and the conclusion. ∎

## 8. Symbolic audit code

The following SymPy code verifies the local matrix reductions and determinant valuations symbolically. It does not fit prime data.

```python
import sympy as sp

p, k = sp.symbols('p k', integer=True, positive=True)

P = lambda x: 34*x**3 + 51*x**2 + 27*x + 5
M = lambda x: sp.Matrix([[P(x), -x**3], [(x+1)**3, 0]])

# Exact determinant.
assert sp.factor(M(k).det()) == k**3 * (k+1)**3

# Reductions of the two singular matrices at k=2p-1 and k=2p.
def mod_p_poly(expr):
    return sp.rem(sp.Poly(sp.expand(expr), p), sp.Poly(p, p)).as_expr()

# Substitute p=0 after expanding coefficients modulo the ideal (p).
def reduce_mod_p_matrix(A):
    return A.applyfunc(lambda z: sp.expand(z).subs(p, 0))

A = reduce_mod_p_matrix(M(2*p - 1))
B = reduce_mod_p_matrix(M(2*p))

assert A == sp.Matrix([[-5, 1], [0, 0]])
assert B == sp.Matrix([[5, 0], [1, 0]])
assert B*A == sp.Matrix([[-25, 5], [-5, 1]])
assert (B*A).rank() == 1

# Determinant valuation ledger for the singular pair:
# det M_(2p-1) contributes p^3 through (2p)^3;
# det M_(2p) contributes p^3 through (2p)^3.
det_pair = sp.factor(M(2*p).det() * M(2*p - 1).det())
assert sp.factor(det_pair) == (
    (2*p - 1)**3 * (2*p)**6 * (2*p + 1)**3
)

# The transfer denominator across the pair contains only one (2p)^3.
D_pair = (2*p)**3 * (2*p + 1)**3

# Rational determinant after division telescopes and is a p-unit.
rat_det_pair = sp.factor(det_pair / D_pair**2)
assert rat_det_pair == sp.factor(((2*p - 1)/(2*p + 1))**3)

print('mod-p singular product =')
sp.pprint(B*A)
print('det integral pair =', det_pair)
print('det rational pair =', rat_det_pair)
print('Smith valuation ledger: integral (0,6), denominator (3,3), rational (-3,+3)')
```

For a full \(2h\)-step product, all matrices outside the displayed singular pair are unimodular over \(\mathbf Z_p\), so they do not change the local Smith exponents. The identical argument applies at \(q\) to the inverse transfer.

## 9. Consequence for the research route

The proposed endpoint-spacing observation is real but insufficient. It provides a transfer matrix of gap-scale height, yet the matrix transports a two-dimensional state and its only nontrivial local lattice factors are recurrence-singularity clearers. The order-eight Casoratian has already used all local target information to reconstruct \(b_n/p\) or \(b_n/q\); it supplies no second transverse line for the short transfer to intersect.

Accordingly, the pure-cross gap certificate requested in (PC.9) remains genuinely new. To reopen the route, one needs an identity tied to the distinguished Apéry initial state that does at least one of the following:

- reduces the transported endpoint state from rank two to one over both characteristics simultaneously;
- relates the \(p\)- and \(q\)-local residual coordinates by a characteristic-zero low-height equation;
- or produces a primitive minor whose Smith content contains \(pq\) after removing all moving coefficient factors.

Neither the canonical recurrence transfer nor the proved order-eight local Casoratians has any of these properties.
