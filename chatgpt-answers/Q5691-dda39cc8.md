## 2. Power reduction is exact, but not uniform in the sheaf sense

The finite-field power reduction is completely uniform as an identity:

What cannot be done is to reduce the exponent while retaining a single fixed finite-rank sheaf or compatible system independent of q.

### 2.1 Keeping M fixed

The algebraic map u\mapsto\Lambda(u)^M is defined over \mathbb Z, but the field-valued function \Lambda(u)^M\in\mathbb F_q is not the complex or \ell-adic trace of a standard fixed sheaf. Étale trace functions take values in a fixed number field or its completions; there is no canonical characteristic-zero lift of the coordinate value t\in\mathbb F_q compatible for all q.

An Artin–Schreier sheaf attached to \psi(\Lambda^M) is a legitimate fixed-characteristic trace object, but it computes

not the ordinary field-valued moment \sum_u\Lambda(u)^M. Its Swan conductor along the pole divisor grows at least linearly with M, and general Betti-number bounds in three variables grow polynomially in M (typically on the scale M^3). This is the wrong observable and the wrong complexity.

### 2.2 Reducing to r=M\bmod(q-1)

Reduction to r allows a Kummer-character realization, but the character must have order dividing q-1 and generally of size comparable to q. The coefficient field becomes a cyclotomic field such as \mathbb Q(\zeta_m), where

Both m and the field vary with q. A fixed finite-order Kummer sheaf cannot recover the function t\mapsto t^r for unbounded q; a hypothetical universal Kummer object would be pro-finite/infinite-rank, outside the standard large-sieve framework.

Therefore:

There is no uniform reduction of M modulo q-1 that simultaneously keeps the sheaf finite-rank, the coefficient field fixed, and the trace equal to the ordinary moment.

## 4. Why the standard large-sieve theorems do not apply

### 4.1 Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel: trace functions over primes

Primary source: Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel, Algebraic trace functions over the primes, arXiv:1211.6043, especially Theorem 1.5 and Remark 1.6.

Their setup fixes one prime p, one sheaf \mathcal F/\mathbb F_p, and a trace function K:\mathbb F_p\to\mathbb C, then sums K(\ell) over rational primes \ell. The conductor must remain bounded; the theorem is nontrivial in a range roughly X>p^{3/4+\varepsilon} for general trace weights.

Here the summation prime q is itself the residue characteristic. There is no fixed ambient \mathbb F_p and no fixed function K whose argument is q. Replacing p by q changes the sheaf at every summand. In addition, the available interval has length H\le q^{1/2}, far below the general p^{3/4+\varepsilon} range.

### 4.2 Short sums of trace functions

Primary source: Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Raju–Rivat–Soundararajan, On short sums of trace functions, Ann. Inst. Fourier 67 (2017), especially Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.3, and equations (2.4)–(2.6).

for bounded-conductor trace functions on one fixed \mathbb F_p. Our block has H\le p^{1/2} even after pretending that a fixed trace function exists. Thus the geometry is at or below the square-root threshold before the more fundamental varying-characteristic obstruction is addressed.

### 4.3 Large sieve for Frobenius in compatible systems

Primary source: Perret-Gentil, Exponential sums over finite fields and the large sieve, arXiv:1703.06965, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.9.

A coherent family there has:

1. a fixed base characteristic p;

1. a fixed number field E;

1. compatibility as the auxiliary place \lambda varies;

1. a uniformly bounded conductor;

1. a parameter x\in U(\mathbb F_{p^e}) over which one averages.

Our problem has none of the required averaging geometry after the torus has already been summed out: there is one scalar per base prime q, and the base characteristic, character order, and coefficient field all vary. The theorem obtains density in the parameter x for one finite field, not density across changing residue characteristics.

### 4.4 Classical large sieve over characters or moduli

The multiplicative large sieve can average sums over many Dirichlet characters and moduli, but targetness is a q-adic divisibility condition on the algebraic sum J_{q,r}, not largeness or smallness of its complex absolute value. The Kummer bound above shows exactly why complex cancellation is orthogonal to the desired condition: an algebraic integer can be divisible by one prime above q while all complex conjugates satisfy square-root-type bounds.

## 6. Zeta-function and cohomological encodings

### 6.1 The ordinary moment is not a point count

### 6.2 Root-of-unity filter

The coefficient shell has the exact complex torsion-point formula

This is a trace on the group algebra of the d-torsion torus, but its natural rank is d^3. In the P3.2 range d=q-1\ge H^2, that rank is at least H^6. The shell formula compresses the number of selected coefficients to (2a+1)^3, but it does not produce a bounded-rank zeta object.

### 6.3 Kummer covers

The cover w^m=\Lambda has degree m=\operatorname{ord}(\chi_{q,r}), which varies and can be comparable to q. Using the rank-one Kummer sheaf keeps geometric divisor complexity bounded, but the cyclotomic coefficient field and local monodromy order vary. Using the cover keeps one coefficient field at a time but makes Betti numbers and discriminants grow with m.

### 6.4 Dwork and Hasse–Witt theory

Primary source: Mellit–Vlasenko, Dwork's congruences for the constant terms of powers of a Laurent polynomial, arXiv:1306.5811.

Dwork congruences explain excellent same-prime relations among constant terms \operatorname{CT}\Lambda^m and p-adic analytic continuation. They do not give horizontal anti-clustering as the residue characteristic varies. Hasse–Witt or Cartier interpretations are equal-characteristic objects: the matrix itself changes with q, and current theory provides no large sieve for the event that a moving Hasse coefficient vanishes in every adversarial interval of characteristics.

The useful open possibility is narrower: the Apéry Picard–Fuchs system has small fixed differential rank. If the whole shell packet C_M(d) could be projected to a fixed-rank distinguished unit-root quotient whose rank and integral height depend only polylogarithmically on n, that would be new and relevant. No such projection is currently known; the visible packet dimension is (2a+1)^3 and can be \asymp n.

For fixed M, the coefficient array c_M(\nu) is holonomic in its indices, and creative telescoping can in principle produce recurrences. This does not presently solve the problem for three reasons.

1. Complexity in M. Generic recurrences or certificates have order/height growing with the exponent or the relevant Newton-polytope volume.

1. Different moduli. A recurrence relating C_M(d) and C_M(d+h) does not by itself couple

## 9. A structural theorem that would make standard tools relevant

A more ambitious route would require all of the following.

1. A family \mathcal V_{n,a} of rank R=n^{o(1)} over \mathbb Q or an open of \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z.

1. Logarithmic conductor/discriminant \log D=n^{o(1)}.

1. A fixed coefficient field of degree n^{o(1)}.

1. For every block prime q, targetness implies a conjugacy condition

with density tending to zero.

1. A short-interval Chebotarev/large-sieve estimate uniform in n,a down to length H=n^{1/3}.

Items 1–4 are not currently constructed, and item 5 is beyond standard GRH Chebotarev at this scale. Thus “find a compatible system” alone is not a sufficient research target.

## 10. Recommended route selection

### Do not prioritize

- Generic \ell-adic large-sieve arguments for bounded-conductor trace functions.

- Standard Chebotarev, even under GRH.

- Replacing the moment by an additive exponential sum.

- A Kummer cover whose order is q-1 or \operatorname{ord}(\chi_{q,r}).

- Generic creative telescoping or generic resultants with height \Theta(M).

Each fails before reaching the required H=n^{1/3} scale.

### The two plausible checkpoints

1. Fixed-rank shell collapse. Determine whether the rank-three Apéry Picard–Fuchs structure compresses the shell packet

into a bounded-rank integral module with pair-elimination height o(H). A negative rank/height theorem would close this route cleanly.

1. Diagonal Kummer/Cartier pair sieve. Develop a new theorem directly controlling the simultaneous prime-ideal divisibilities of the Kummer lifts J_{q,r(q)} for nearby residue characteristics. This would be genuinely new cross-characteristic arithmetic, not an application of an existing large sieve.

The first checkpoint is more concrete and falsifiable. It should be tested before investing in a broad compatible-system program. The exact success criterion is not merely a recurrence: it is a recurrence or module whose rank and primitive height are independent of M at the required scale, and whose elimination respects the two distinct moduli q and q+h.

## 11. Bottom line

The new identity removes the moving exponent and places all targets in a block inside one characteristic-zero coefficient array. That is a real advance. It does not remove the diagonal-characteristic nature of the divisibility condition.

The strongest immediate theorem is the Kummer lift in Proposition 3.1, giving a bounded-geometry algebraic character sum and uniform complex bounds. Those bounds do not control divisibility by the base characteristic, and the character fields vary with q.

No standard cross-characteristic large sieve, compatible-system theorem, zeta-function theorem, moment-recurrence theorem, or resultant theorem currently yields adversarial anti-clustering in length-n^{1/3} blocks. The precise missing input is \mathrm{DCPS}_\Lambda, or a fixed-rank shell collapse strong enough to prove it.
