# Q5877 folded-boundary divisor attack

## 1. Exact folded map

For a moving prime `q` in the folded sector, write the residue index as `j` with

\[
q\mid b_j,
\qquad c=q-2j.
\]

Since the folded residue is in the interval `0\le j<q`, the slack `c` is odd and satisfies `-(q)<c<q`. The two branches are obtained by solving for `q` from the two affine factors.

### Branch I: `q | (2n+c)`

The factor identity gives

\[
2n+c=mq
\]

for an integer `m`. Substituting `q=2j+c` gives

\[
2n+c=m(2j+c),
\]

or

\[
2n=2mj+(m-1)c.
\]

Hence

\[
 c=\frac{2(n-mj)}{m-1}
\]

when `m>1`. The boundary case `m=1` forces `2n=2j`, hence `j=n`, which is the non-moving endpoint and must be removed separately.

### Branch II: `q | (2n+2-c)`

Similarly

\[
2n+2-c=mq=m(2j+c),
\]

so

\[
2n+2=2mj+(m+1)c,
\]

and therefore

\[
 c=\frac{2(n+1-mj)}{m+1}.
\]

The reflection involution sends these two branches into each other: replacing the folded coordinate by the reflected coordinate changes `c` to `-c+2` in the unshifted indexing convention, and the two divisors are exactly the reflected pair.

## 2. Divisor-sum partition by slack

The naive contribution is

\[
W(n)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal M_n}\log q,
\]

where every moving prime contributes a divisor of

\[
(2n+c)(2n+2-c).
\]

Partitioning by odd slack gives

\[
W(n)=\sum_c \sum_{q: q\mid b_{(q-c)/2}}\log q.
\]

The outer divisor information alone gives only

\[
\sum_{c}\log((2n+c)(2n+2-c))=O(n\log n),
\]

because there are `O(n)` possible slacks. This does not improve the target `o(n)`.

## 3. Dyadic slack ranges

Let

\[
2^r\le |c|<2^{r+1}.
\]

For fixed `r`, the possible carriers have height

\[
\prod_{|c|<2^{r+1}}(2n+c)(2n+2-c).
\]

The logarithm is bounded by

\[
O(2^r\log n).
\]

Summing over `r\le \log n` recovers `O(n\log n)`. Therefore dyadic decomposition only reorganizes the divisor sum; it does not create a smaller carrier.

## 4. Divisor switching obstruction

Using

\[
q=2j+c
\]

one may switch from prime divisors `q` to indices `j`. The condition becomes

\[
2j+c\mid b_j.
\]

The Apéry recurrence controls linear combinations of neighboring `b_j`, but no known pointwise theorem gives

\[
\prod_{2j+c\mid b_j}(2j+c)
\]

subexponential height uniformly in `c`.

The recurrence

\[
(j+1)^3b_{j+1}-(34j^3+51j^2+27j+5)b_j+j^3b_{j-1}=0
\]

only transfers divisibility between adjacent terms after paying coefficients of polynomial height. Those coefficients have logarithmic height `O(log j)` per step, so an interval of length proportional to `n` still gives an exponential-size carrier.

## 5. Second reflection does not shrink the carrier

Applying reflection again exchanges

\[
q\mid b_j
\]

with the equivalent folded condition at the reflected index. The resulting carrier is still generated by the same two linear factors. Algebraically the second reflection produces no new independent integer: the ideal generated by the two reflected factors is

\[
((2n+c)(2n+2-c)),
\]

up to factors dividing `2` and the fixed endpoints.

## 6. Sharp partial conclusion

The exact divisor partition proves:

\[
\prod_{q\in\mathcal M_n}q
\mid
\prod_{c\ {m odd}}(2n+c)(2n+2-c),
\]

but the right side has logarithmic height

\[
\Theta(n\log n),
\]

not `o(n)`.

The only genuinely smaller possible carrier would have to use the additional condition

\[
q\mid b_{(q-c)/2}
\]

in a way that removes almost all slack values or forces a sparse set of `c`. The current Apéry recurrence and reflection identities do not provide such a pointwise sparsity theorem.

Therefore:

* the folded-boundary divisor identity is exact;
* dyadic slack partitioning gives no asymptotic gain;
* divisor switching gives no smaller carrier without a new Apéry-specific divisibility theorem;
* the remaining missing ingredient is a pointwise bound on the number/weight of odd slacks `c` supporting primes with `q|b_{(q-c)/2}`.

No unconditional `o(n)` carrier follows from the folded divisor identity alone.
