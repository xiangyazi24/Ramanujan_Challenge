# Verdict

**No presently published Katz/Deligne theorem turns the fiberwise Hasse--Witt polynomial**

\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{0\le j<p} b_jt^j
\]

**into the desired average estimate**

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll \pi(x),\qquad z_p=\#\{0\le r<p:b_r\equiv0\pmod p\}.
\]

The coefficient-extraction identity is elementary Fourier inversion on \(\mathbf F_p^\times\). It is not, by itself, the trace formula of a bounded-conductor Mellin family whose Frobenius traces detect the congruences \(b_r\equiv0\pmod p\). The K3 geometry identifies \(A_p(t)\) as a fiberwise Hasse--Witt invariant in the **base parameter** \(t\); the desired zero count varies the **character/index parameter** \(r\). Passing from one to the other requires a new same-characteristic, character-twisted crystalline theorem which is not supplied by the cited geometry.

# 1. What coefficient extraction actually says

For any polynomial \(A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j\) over \(\mathbf F_p\), and \(1\le r\le p-1\),

\[
\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-r}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_j\sum_{t\ne0}t^{j-r}.
\]

The inner sum is \(-1\) when \(j\equiv r\pmod{p-1}\) and zero otherwise. Since \(0\le j<p\), this gives

\[
-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p^\times}A_p(t)t^{-r}
 =
\begin{cases}
 b_r,&1\le r\le p-2,\\
 b_0+b_{p-1},&r=p-1.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus the advertised formula is correct for interior indices. It is just the multiplicative discrete Fourier transform of the function \(t\mapsto A_p(t)\).

To turn this identity into an \(\ell\)-adic Mellin theorem one would need:

1. a fixed sheaf \(\mathcal F_p\) on \(\mathbf G_m/\mathbf F_p\) with trace function exactly \(A_p(t)\), or with a uniformly controlled normalization;
2. conductor bounded independently of \(p\);
3. a theorem identifying the Mellin sum against \(\omega^{-r}\) with a Frobenius trace in a family over the character parameter;
4. a mechanism converting the algebraic-integer trace into the residue class \(b_r\in\mathbf F_p\), and then controlling the event that this residue is zero.

The known K3 Hasse--Witt statement supplies neither items 2--4 in the required character-variable form.

# 2. The exact geometry that is known

The primary geometric source is:

- Jan Stienstra and Frits Beukers, **“On the Picard--Fuchs Equation and the Formal Brauer Group of Certain Elliptic K3-Surfaces,”** *Mathematische Annalen* **271** (1985), 269--304, DOI 10.1007/BF01455990.

Related background is:

- Frits Beukers and Chris A. M. Peters, **“A Family of K3 Surfaces and \(\zeta(3)\),”** *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik* **351** (1984), 42--54.

These works connect the Apéry Picard--Fuchs solution and the formal Brauer group of the K3 family. In characteristic \(p\), the truncation \(A_p(t)\) occurs as the Hasse--Witt/formal-group coefficient controlling ordinarity of the fiber at the base point \(t\).

That statement concerns the variation

\[
t\in U(\mathbf F_p)\longmapsto H^2_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_t/W(\mathbf F_p))
\]

or its Hasse--Witt scalar. It is a **fiberwise base-parameter** statement.

The desired quantity instead varies

\[
r\in\mathbf Z/(p-1)\mathbf Z,
\qquad
b_r=-\sum_{t\ne0}A_p(t)\omega(t)^{-r}.
\]

A geometric realization of this character variation would involve compactly supported cohomology of Kummer twists, schematically

\[
H^1_{c}(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},
\mathcal V\otimes\mathcal K_{\omega^{-r}}),
\]

or its rigid/crystalline analogue. This is a different global object from the fiber crystal at a single \(t\). No theorem in Stienstra--Beukers identifies the vanishing of the Mellin coefficient \(b_r\bmod p\) with a bounded-rank slope-zero condition in this twisted global cohomology.

# 3. Why Katz's finite-field Mellin equidistribution does not prove the claim

The relevant primary reference is:

- Nicholas M. Katz, **Convolution and Equidistribution: Sato--Tate Theorems for Finite-Field Mellin Transforms**, Annals of Mathematics Studies 180, Princeton University Press, 2012, DOI 10.23943/princeton/9780691153308.001.0001.

Katz studies Mellin transforms of trace functions arising from suitable perverse \(\ell\)-adic sheaves on \(\mathbf G_m\). The output is a family of Frobenius conjugacy classes, indexed by multiplicative characters, whose complex normalized traces equidistribute under geometric-monodromy hypotheses.

That theory does **not** directly imply the required average for four separate reasons.

## 3.1 No fixed bounded-conductor input sheaf has been identified

The function being transformed is the mod-\(p\) polynomial \(A_p(t)\). Its degree is \(p-1\), and the interpolation of

\[
r\longmapsto b_r
\]

on \(\mathbf F_p\) has degree about \(p-2\). Treating \(A_p\) merely as an Artin--Schreier trace function of a polynomial would therefore give conductor growing with \(p\), not the uniform complexity required for a constant-quality equidistribution theorem.

The bounded-conductor K3 sheaf \(R^2f_*\mathbf Q_\ell\) has a trace function related to point counts/Frobenius traces of fibers, not literally the characteristic-\(p\) Hasse--Witt polynomial value \(A_p(t)\) as an element of \(\mathbf F_p\). The latter is a mod-\(p\) first Frobenius/Hasse invariant. An equality of those two functions, with a fixed algebraic normalization and bounded conductor, is not available.

## 3.2 Equidistribution concerns normalized complex traces, not divisibility by \(p\)

Even if a Mellin transform were realized as an algebraic Frobenius trace \(T_{p,r}\), Katz's theorem controls the distribution of normalized embeddings of \(T_{p,r}\) in a compact group. The event

\[
b_r\equiv0\pmod p
\]

is a congruence in the same residue characteristic. It is not the event that a complex trace equals zero. A comparison theorem would have to show that \(b_r\) is the reduction of a distinguished integral Frobenius block and control exactly when that block is divisible by \(p\). No such passage follows from \(\ell\)-adic equidistribution with \(\ell\ne p\).

## 3.3 Square-root cancellation is too weak for \(O(1)\) zeros

For any function \(f:\mathbf F_p\to\mathbf F_p\),

\[
1_{f(r)=0}=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(uf(r)).
\]

Hence

\[
z_p=1+\frac1p\sum_{u\ne0}\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(ub_r).
\]

A uniform square-root estimate

\[
\left|\sum_re_p(ub_r)\right|\le C\sqrt p
\]

would give only

\[
z_p\le 1+C\sqrt p,
\]

not \(O(1)\). Therefore standard Deligne cancellation for a bounded-conductor additive trace function, even if it existed, would not prove the desired local bound.

## 3.4 Equidistribution across characters does not automatically average across primes

Katz's finite-field theorem is a statement inside one finite field, generally as the character varies and possibly in extension fields. The target theorem sums an arithmetic divisibility event over changing residue characteristics. A uniform family over \(\mathbf Z\), integral comparison at \(p\), and an effective prime-average theorem would still be required.

# 4. The double-moment calculation

Let

\[
z_p=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}1_{b_r\equiv0\,(p)}.
\]

Using additive characters,

\[
z_p=\frac1p\sum_{u\in\mathbf F_p}
\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(ub_r).
\]

For the second moment within one prime,

\[
z_p^2
=\frac1{p^2}\sum_{u,v\in\mathbf F_p}
\sum_{r,s\in\mathbf F_p}e_p(ub_r+vb_s).
\]

There is no useful orthogonality in \((r,s)\) unless one already controls the two-variable map \((r,s)\mapsto(b_r,b_s)\). The diagonal terms give the expected scale, but the off-diagonal terms are exactly a value-collision problem. A bounded-conductor description of the one-variable sequence would not by itself bound the two-variable conductor uniformly when the defining interpolation degree grows like \(p\).

There is also a multiplicative-Fourier Parseval identity. For interior coefficients,

\[
b_r=-\sum_{t\ne0}A_p(t)t^{-r}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_{r\bmod p-1}|b_r|_{\mathbf C}^2
=(p-1)\sum_{t\ne0}|A_p(t)|_{\mathbf C}^2
\]

after choosing complex lifts. This controls an analytic \(L^2\) norm of Fourier coefficients; it does not count which coefficients are zero modulo \(p\). Large complex size and residue divisibility are unrelated without a precise integral model.

Summing the additive expansion over primes gives

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p
=\pi(x)+
\sum_{p\le x}\frac1p\sum_{u\ne0}\sum_re_p(ub_r).
\]

Applying square-root cancellation prime by prime yields at best

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p
\ll \pi(x)+\sum_{p\le x}\sqrt p
\ll \frac{x^{3/2}}{\log x},
\]

which is worse than the available deterministic bound below. To reach \(O(\pi(x))\), one needs either \(z_p=O(1)\) for almost all \(p\), or cancellation in the prime variable for the entire family of same-characteristic exponential sums. No published K3/Katz theorem supplies that.

# 5. Precise obstruction

The missing object is not “monodromy” in the abstract. It is an **integral character-twisted Frobenius family of uniformly bounded complexity** with a theorem of the following form:

- for each \(p\), the quantities \(b_r\bmod p\) are the reductions of a specified matrix entry or Hasse invariant of
  \(H^1_{\mathrm{rig},c}(U,\mathcal V_{\mathrm{tr}}\otimes\mathcal K_{\omega^{-r}})\);
- the relevant crystal has rank and overconvergence conductor bounded independently of \(p,r\);
- the zero condition is equivalent to a bounded-degree Hasse polynomial in the tame-character parameter, or to failure of a unique-unit-root property;
- the number of exceptional characters is uniformly bounded, or at least has bounded prime average.

None of these conclusions follows from the fiberwise Hasse--Witt polynomial \(A_p(t)\). The raw character interpolation has degree \(p-2\), so any bounded-degree tame Hasse polynomial would require a genuinely new Gauss/Jacobi normalization or a structural collapse of the twisted Frobenius block.

This is the exact obstruction: **fiberwise ordinarity in the base variable does not control divisibility of Mellin coefficients in the character variable.**

# 6. Strongest unconditional average bound presently derivable

The repository's proved pointwise estimate is

\[
z_p\ll p^{2/3},
\]

obtained from the Apéry gap-polynomial/separated-block argument. Therefore

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p
\ll \sum_{p\le x}p^{2/3}
\ll x^{2/3}\pi(x)
\ll \frac{x^{5/3}}{\log x}.
\]

Using partial summation and the prime number theorem gives the same order, with a possible explicit leading comparison constant; no geometric input improves it unconditionally.

The trivial bound \(z_p\le p\) gives \(O(x^2/\log x)\), so the \(p^{2/3}\) theorem is the strongest established average consequence in the current project.

A bound

\[
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll\pi(x)
\]

remains conjectural. The K3 Hasse--Witt interpretation is relevant motivation for constructing the missing character-twisted crystal, but it is not a proof and should not be cited as one.

# Citation-level conclusion

- **Elementary Mellin coefficient extraction:** valid.
- **Known K3 Hasse--Witt interpretation of \(A_p(t)\):** valid, via Stienstra--Beukers, but it varies the base fiber \(t\).
- **Application of Katz finite-field Mellin equidistribution to deduce \(z_p=O(1)\) or \(\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll\pi(x)\):** invalid with the presently identified objects.
- **Published exact sheaf/crystal theorem proving the desired average:** none located.
- **Strongest unconditional result in the current state:**

\[
z_p\ll p^{2/3},\qquad
\sum_{p\le x}z_p\ll x^{5/3}/\log x.
\]
