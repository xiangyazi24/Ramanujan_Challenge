## (i) Local rigidity: what configuration first over-determines the orbit point?

I use the stated run-rigidity mechanism only as a template. The fresh stepped-pair statement itself is not assumed proved; it is a target derived from the same transfer formalism.

A single stepped pair

r in Z_d, r+1 in Z_d'

imposes

Delta_{r,d}(xi_r)=0 and Delta_{r+1,d'}(M_r xi_r)=0.

The unknown xi_r is one point of P^1, hence one parameter. The first equation says xi_r lies on the fibre line determined by the return condition; the second is another algebraic condition after pullback by M_r. Generically this gives finitely many possibilities only after projectivizing the full relation, but it does not force a unique explicit point in the way the same-gap consecutive run does. Therefore one stepped pair is not a rigidity theorem.

The smallest robust configuration is two independent stepped pairs whose endpoint sets overlap in a bounded window.

Take positions r and r+m with fixed m. Let

T_m(r)=M_{r+m-1}...M_r.

A stepped pair at offset m gives a pulled-back conic condition

Q_{m,d,d'}(xi_r)=0.

Because T_m is a bounded-length transfer product, its entries are rational functions of bounded degree when m is fixed. The condition is quadratic in homogeneous coordinates of xi_r, not a forced linear eigenvector condition. Two independent stepped pairs give two conics in P^1. Over an algebraic closure they intersect in at most four points. Hence:

For fixed m0, any two nondegenerate stepped pairs with all four endpoint conditions distinct restrict xi_r to at most four explicit algebraic points.

Eliminating xi_r gives an orbit-free algebraic identity in (r,gap variables).

Degree bookkeeping:

- A transfer product of length m0 has entry degree O(m0) after clearing denominators.

- Each Casoratian numerator has gap-degree 3(d-1).

- A pulled-back conic has coefficient degree O(m0+d+d').

- Two conics eliminate xi_r by a resultant of degree polynomial in the gap variables with exponent O(1) when m0 is fixed.

For a staircase triple (r,r+1,r+2) with gaps d,d',d'', there are three collision equations and one projective unknown. After eliminating xi_r, the remaining condition is a bounded-degree variety in (r,d,d',d''). It is stronger than a single stepped pair but weaker than the same-gap run because no single companion eigenvector chain appears.

The double-coverage configuration (two pairs at the same r with different gaps) is also sufficient: two independent return conditions constrain the same xi_r. Its elimination is lower dimensional and is the cleaner minimal test case.

## (ii) Packing consequence and the m0 obstruction

The proposed bounded-distance extension is valid in form:

xi_{r+m}=T_m xi_r

with T_m explicit. However the consequence depends on controlling the number of solutions of the eliminated varieties.

For fixed m0, the number of bad local configurations is at best a bounded-degree variety count. It gives something of size O(D^a) where the exponent a comes from the number of free gap variables. It does not by itself give a power saving in the primitive count.

A scattered construction can avoid consecutive coverage: covering every other position eliminates all adjacent covered pairs. Therefore any argument using only m=1 cannot force density below a constant. This agrees with the stated warning that consecutive mechanisms cap at density about 1/2.

For a block of length 2D with delta D covered positions, the number of forced staircase triples depends on the local density. There is no universal positive linear lower bound: an alternating pattern gives zero consecutive triples at density 1/2. More generally, spacing constructions show that one only obtains forced m0-patterns once the density exceeds the corresponding packing number.

Thus SPARSE-PAIRS with fixed m0 gives only

A(D) <= N/m0 + error,

not FR_eta.

The crossover computation is:

- If m0 is fixed, elimination degree is constant and counting may work, but the density saving is constant.

- If m0 grows, transfer degree becomes O(m0).

- The resultant degree grows rapidly (at least polynomially, and typically exponentially after repeated denominator clearing).

- A naive Bezout/Weil count then loses before m0 reaches any power of D.

Therefore the current conic-intersection route does not reach m0=D^c by direct counting.

To restore the route, one needs averaged cancellation over the family of eliminated varieties, not individual variety counts. The desired replacement is a family estimate of the form

sum over local configurations of the deviation from the expected count = o(number of configurations),

uniformly for m0 growing slowly. This is the missing quenched growing-gap cancellation object identified in the wall analysis.

## (iii) Target theorem and relation to FR_eta

The right target is:

[SPARSE-PAIRS]

There exist c>0 and m0 (possibly slowly growing) such that all but O(D^(2-eta')) starting positions r have at most one covered point in (r,r+m0].

Fixed m0 only yields a constant density reduction. To imply FR_eta one needs m0 growing with D.

The sharp target is therefore not merely SPARSE-PAIRS but:

SPARSE-PAIRS with m0(D) -> infinity and with an error term smaller than N/m0(D).

This would imply

A(D) << N/m0(D)+O(D^(2-eta')),

and choosing m0(D)=D^c would give the required power saving.

## Final answers

(i) Least confident step:

The least confident step is the proposed growth regime for m0. The local algebraic elimination is reliable for fixed m0, but I do not have a proof that the family of resultants retains enough cancellation when m0 grows. The degree explosion may kill the counting argument earlier than expected.

(ii) Blind spot in the question:

The possible blind spot is assuming that local rigidity configurations are the only obstruction. The padded adversary's surviving form is not necessarily long runs or nearby clusters; it is a high-entropy scattered matching with slowly varying gaps. Such a configuration may avoid every bounded-window rigidity test while still saturating primitive counts. A successful theorem likely needs a global ordered-clock invariant, not only local algebraic rigidity.
