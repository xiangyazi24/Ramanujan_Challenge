# 7. What this means for the AESZ #209 proof strategy

The current repository says that AESZ #209 reduces the proof to an Ore intertwiner plus a parametric deformation. The first half of that sentence is too strong for the two scalar operators presently displayed.

The AESZ identification remains useful, but the map cannot be a local finite-order operator in \mathbb Q(n)\langle S\rangle. A viable exact transformation must change more than the local cyclic coordinate. The main possibilities are:

1. A summation or binomial transform

1. A parameter derivative or normal-function extension of the AESZ period. The mixed constant \zeta(2)+\zeta(3) may live in an extension module rather than in the pure holomorphic period module.

1. A generating-function integral transform or algebraic pullback. Such a transform can alter local exponents in a way that a rational shift operator cannot.

1. A matrix-valued CMF/AZ certificate with an extra discrete variable. This would be an intertwiner in a larger holonomic category, not an element of the one-variable Ore ring \mathbb Q(n)\langle S\rangle.

The formal exponent calculation gives a useful acceptance test for future candidates: any proposed one-variable rational Ore equivalence between the displayed AESZ recurrence and L_{27} is impossible. The missing certificate must be nonlocal or must use a different source operator whose formal powers actually match.
