ANSWER Q6044 0b78a115

# Audit verdict

I found **no numerical or algebraic defect in the bound**

```text
|E_n| <= 2048 * 2^(-n)
```

once the Barnes representation is available. The midpoint product, its step ratio, the logarithmic-derivative estimate, the exponential majorant, and the final constant all check out.

There are, however, two implementation-critical gaps in Q6035:

1. **The Barnes equality was only sketched, not proved.** Zudilin prints the analogous identity (8) for the preceding construction and explicitly omits the two Section 2 integrals. Recovering them is mathematically correct, but it still requires a genuine contour argument.
2. **The contour integral was not connected rigorously to the recurrence-defined project sequence `m_n`.** The initial values alone do not identify a new analytic sequence with a third-order recurrence sequence. Either the local development already proves that the finite partial-fraction coefficients equal the recurrence-defined `b_n,m_n`, or one must add that exact bridge. Proving that the Barnes integrals themselves satisfy the recurrence would re-import the creative-telescoping theorem that the proposed route was meant to avoid.

Thus the estimate is not false; it is **conditional on an unproved load-bearing identity**. The right repair is to isolate one highly specialized Abel–Barnes residue theorem and one finite partial-fraction normalization lemma. Do not implement the broad generic residue lemma proposed in Q6035.

# 1. Normalization audit

Let

```text
R_n(t) = product_{j=1}^n (t-j)^3
         / ((n!)^2 * product_{j=0}^n (t+j)).
```

Zudilin's Section 2 uses

```text
r_n(z)              = sum_{nu>=1} z^nu R_n(nu),
tilde_r_n(z)        = -sum_{nu>=1} z^nu R_n'(nu),
double_tilde_r_n(z)= (1/2) sum_{nu>=1} z^nu R_n''(nu).
```

The coefficient of `Li_2` and `Li_3` is the same sequence `a_n`. The project normalization stated in Q6035 is correct:

```text
project b_n = paper a_n,
project m_n = paper tilde_b_n + paper double_tilde_b_n.
```

The initial data in Theorem 2 are

```text
a_0,a_1,a_2 = 1, 7, 163,
tilde_b_0,tilde_b_1,tilde_b_2 = 0, 23/2, 2145/8,
double_tilde_b_0,double_tilde_b_1,double_tilde_b_2
  = 0, 17/2, 3135/16.
```

Hence

```text
m_0,m_1,m_2 = 0, 20, 7425/16.
```

There is no index shift. The recurrence printed in Q6035 agrees with Theorem 2 and advances from indices `n,n-1,n-2` to `n+1` for `n>=2`.

## What the initial table does not prove

Suppose the Lean source defines `b,m` only by the recurrence and these three values. A separately defined Barnes integral sequence is not thereby equal to `((zeta2+zeta3)*b_n-m_n)`. To obtain equality, one needs one of:

```text
(A) a previously formalized theorem identifying recurrence b,m with the
    finite partial-fraction coefficients of R_n; or

(B) a direct finite formula for those coefficients and a proof at each n; or

(C) a proof that the Barnes-defined sequences satisfy the source recurrence.
```

Option (C) is essentially the omitted creative-telescoping input. The preferred bridge is (A), with (B) as fallback.

# 2. Residue, orientation, and factor audit

Put

```text
K(t) = (pi / sin(pi*t))^2.
```

At an integer `nu`, with `w=t-nu`,

```text
K(nu+w) = 1/w^2 + O(1).
```

The coefficient of `1/w` is zero. Thus for holomorphic `A`,

```text
Res_{t=nu} A(t) K(t) = A'(nu).
```

This is the correct local residue.

## Orientation

Take the vertical line from `C-iH` to `C+iH` and close it on the right. Traversing the left edge upward puts the interior on the right, so the closed contour is clockwise. Therefore

```text
vertical integral = -2*pi*i * sum of enclosed residues.
```

The minus sign in Q6035 is correct.

## The combined Abel identity

For `0<z<1`, define

```text
Phi_n(t) = R_n(t) - R_n'(t)/2,
A_n,z(t) = Phi_n(t) * exp(t*log z).
```

Then the exact residue identity is

```text
1/(2*pi*i) * integral_{C-i*infinity}^{C+i*infinity}
  Phi_n(t) K(t) z^t dt

= -sum_{nu>=1} z^nu *
    [R_n'(nu) - R_n''(nu)/2
     + log(z)*(R_n(nu)-R_n'(nu)/2)]

= [tilde_r_n(z)-log(z)*r_n(z)]
  + [double_tilde_r_n(z)-(log(z)/2)*tilde_r_n(z)].
```

Every sign in this formula checks. In particular, the second correction is

```text
-(log z)/2 * tilde_r_n(z),
```

not a plus sign.

## Why `C=n+1/2` is legal

Zudilin's equation (8) allows `0<C<n+1`. At each integer `1<=nu<=n`, `R_n` has a zero of order three. Consequently

```text
R_n(t) K(t)      is holomorphic at nu,
R_n'(t) K(t)     is holomorphic at nu.
```

So moving the line to

```text
C_n = n + 1/2
```

crosses only removable singularities. Closing to the right then encloses exactly the nonzero residue locations `nu>=n+1`.

# 3. Repaired fixed-line formulation

The shifted function is

```text
F_n(s) = R_n(s+n+1)
       = (s+1)_n^3 / ((n!)^2 * (s+n+1)_(n+1)).
```

Its zeros are at `s=-1,...,-n`, and its poles are at
`s=-n-1,...,-2n-1`. Thus the half-plane `Re(s)>=-1/2` contains neither zeros nor poles of `F_n`.

For the **Abel-stage** residue theorem, Q6035's fixed-line formula must retain the shifted power:

```text
z^t = z^(s+n+1).
```

Define

```text
Phi_n(s) = F_n(s) - F_n'(s)/2,
A_n,z(s) = Phi_n(s) * exp((s+n+1)*log z).
```

Then the narrow identity is

```text
1/(2*pi*i) * integral_{Re(s)=-1/2}
  A_n,z(s) * (pi/sin(pi*s))^2 ds

= -sum_{m>=0} A_n,z'(m).                              (BR)
```

The residues `m=0,1,...` correspond to the original indices
`nu=n+1+m`. Formula (BR) is the one theorem worth formalizing.

Only after taking `z -> 1-` does the integrand simplify to the fixed-contour formula printed in Q6035:

```text
E_n = 1/(2*pi*i) * integral_{Re(s)=-1/2}
        (F_n(s)-F_n'(s)/2) * (pi/sin(pi*s))^2 ds.
```

## Endpoint warning

The series on the right of (BR) is not to be evaluated termwise at `z=1`; `F_n` has degree `2n-1` at infinity. The correct sequence is:

```text
1. prove (BR) for 0<z<1;
2. prove the Abel-regularized right side tends to the finite source error;
3. use dominated convergence on the fixed vertical line to set z=1.
```

The dominated-convergence step for the integral is easy for fixed `n`. The source-normalization limit on the series side is a separate finite partial-fraction theorem and must not be silently folded into dominated convergence.

# 4. Real-line normalization

On the original midpoint line `t=n+1/2+i*y`,

```text
sin(pi*t) = (-1)^n cosh(pi*y).
```

Since `dt=i dy`,

```text
1/(2*pi*i) * pi^2 * i = pi/2.
```

Therefore

```text
E_n = (pi/2) * integral_{-infinity}^{infinity}
        [R_n(c_n+i*y)-R_n'(c_n+i*y)/2]
        / cosh(pi*y)^2 dy.
```

Conjugation sends the value at `y` to the value at `-y`, so the half-line real formula is

```text
E_n = pi * integral_0^infinity
        Re[R_n(c_n+i*y)-R_n'(c_n+i*y)/2]
        / cosh(pi*y)^2 dy.
```

The factors `pi/2`, `pi`, and the sign of `R_n'/2` are correct.

# 5. Midpoint product and ratio audit

Let

```text
q_n = R_n(n+1/2).
```

Then

```text
q_n = product_{k=0}^{n-1}(k+1/2)^3
      / ((n!)^2 * product_{k=n}^{2n}(k+1/2)),
q_0 = 2.
```

The exact ratio is

```text
q_(n+1)/q_n
 = (n+1/2)^4
   / ((2n+3/2)*(2n+5/2)*(n+1)^2).
```

The inequality `q_(n+1)/q_n <= 1/4` is equivalent to

```text
(4n+3)*(4n+5)*(n+1)^2 - (2n+1)^4 >= 0,
```

and the difference is

```text
32n^3 + 71n^2 + 54n + 14.
```

Hence

```text
0 < q_n <= 2*4^(-n).
```

All midpoint formulas in Q6035 are correct.

# 6. Uniform product bound audit

For `y>=0`,

```text
|R_n(n+1/2+i*y)| / q_n
 = product_{k=0}^{n-1}(1+y^2/(k+1/2)^2)^(3/2)
   / product_{k=n}^{2n}(1+y^2/(k+1/2)^2)^(1/2).
```

Dropping the denominator is legitimate.

Set

```text
f_y(x) = log(1+y^2/x^2).
```

Q6035 uses

```text
f_y(k+1/2) <= integral_{k-1/2}^{k+1/2} f_y(x) dx,
```

for `k>=1`. This does follow merely from monotonicity: `k+1/2` is the **right endpoint** of the integration interval, so every integrand value on the interval is at least `f_y(k+1/2)`. No convexity hypothesis is missing.

The resulting estimates

```text
P_n(y) <= (1+4y^2)*exp(pi*y),
P_n(y)^(3/2) <= (1+2y)^3*exp(3*pi*y/2)
```

are correct, assuming the elementary improper integral

```text
integral_0^infinity log(1+y^2/x^2) dx = pi*y.
```

Since

```text
cosh(pi*y)^(-2) <= 4*exp(-2*pi*y),
```

the kernel leaves the integrable envelope

```text
4*q_n*(1+2y)^3*exp(-pi*y/2).
```

The polynomial degree `2n-1` of `R_n` is therefore harmless in the **uniform midpoint estimate**; the finite-product comparison removes it.

# 7. Logarithmic derivative and zero/pole audit

On the midpoint line,

```text
R_n'(t)/R_n(t)
 = 3*sum_{j=1}^n 1/(t-j)
   -sum_{j=0}^n 1/(t+j).
```

Every factor is nonzero there:

```text
Re(t-j) >= 1/2,
Re(t+j) >= 1/2.
```

Thus

```text
|R_n'/R_n| <= 3*n*2 + (n+1)*2 = 8n+2,
|R_n-R_n'/2| <= (4n+2)|R_n|.
```

These estimates are correct.

## Necessary Lean repair

Do **not** define `RPrime` globally by

```text
RPrime(t) := R(t) * logarithmicDerivative(t).
```

At the zeros `1,...,n` and poles `0,-1,...,-n`, totalized complex inversion gives junk values, and the displayed expression is not globally the derivative. Define `RPrime` as the actual complex derivative of the finite rational expression. Prove the logarithmic-derivative identity only under a hypothesis that all factors are nonzero—specifically on the midpoint line or, after shifting, on `Re(s)>=-1/2`.

This is not a defect in the inequality, but it is a likely source of a false Lean lemma if Q6035's implementation suggestion is followed literally.

# 8. Constant audit

Let `alpha=pi/2` and

```text
M = 1/alpha + 6/alpha^2 + 24/alpha^3 + 48/alpha^4.
```

Then

```text
integral_0^infinity (1+2y)^3 exp(-alpha*y) dy = M.
```

The preceding bounds give

```text
|E_n| <= 4*pi*q_n*(4n+2)*M
      <= 16*pi*(2n+1)*M*4^(-n).
```

Using `2n+1 <= 2^(n+1)` gives

```text
|E_n| <= C*2^(-n),
C = 32*pi*M
  = 64 + 768/pi + 6144/pi^2 + 24576/pi^3.
```

From `pi>3`,

```text
C < 64 + 256 + 6144/9 + 24576/27
  = 17216/9
  < 2048.
```

So the advertised constant survives intact.

## Bottom line on the bound

There is **no fatal gap in the real-variable derivation of `2048*2^-n`**. The fatal gap is upstream: Q6035 did not prove the Barnes/source identity to which the estimate is applied.

# 9. Narrowest Lean theorem to prove

Avoid a generic theorem parameterized by an arbitrary rational function. Specialize to the fixed family `F_n` and the fixed line `Re(s)=-1/2`.

A suitable interface is:

```lean
def zF (n : Nat) (s : Complex) : Complex :=
  ((∏ k in Finset.range n, (s + (k+1))) ^ 3) /
    ((n.factorial : Complex)^2 *
      ∏ k in Finset.range (n+1), (s + (n+1+k)))

def zPhi (n : Nat) (s : Complex) : Complex :=
  zF n s - deriv (zF n) s / 2

def abelFactor (n : Nat) (z : Real) (s : Complex) : Complex :=
  zPhi n s *
    Complex.exp ((s + (n+1 : Real)) * Real.log z)

def sqSinKernel (s : Complex) : Complex :=
  ((Real.pi : Complex) / Complex.sin ((Real.pi : Complex) * s))^2
```

Using a real parameterization of the vertical line, define

```lean
def verticalIntegral (h : Complex -> Complex) : Complex :=
  ∫ y : Real, Complex.I * h ((-1/2 : Real) + y*Complex.I)
```

The specialized residue statement is then:

```lean
theorem zudilin_abel_barnes
    (n : Nat) {z : Real} (hz0 : 0 < z) (hz1 : z < 1) :
  ((2 * Real.pi * Complex.I : Complex)⁻¹) *
      verticalIntegral
        (fun s => abelFactor n z s * sqSinKernel s)
    = -∑' m : Nat, deriv (abelFactor n z) (m : Complex)
```

This theorem contains exactly the needed contour content and nothing about zeta values, recurrence sequences, or general meromorphic functions.

Then prove separately:

```lean
theorem zudilin_abel_limit_matches_source (n : Nat) :
  Tendsto
    (fun z : Real =>
      -∑' m : Nat, deriv (abelFactor n z) (m : Complex))
    (nhdsWithin 1 (Set.Iio 1))
    (nhds (((zeta2 + zeta3) * b n - m n : Real) : Complex))
```

and

```lean
theorem zudilin_vertical_tendsto_one (n : Nat) :
  Tendsto
    (fun z : Real =>
      ((2*pi*I)⁻¹) * verticalIntegral (... z ...))
    (nhdsWithin 1 (Set.Iio 1))
    (nhds (barnesAtOne n))
```

The first is the finite partial-fraction/source-normalization bridge. The second is dominated convergence on one line.

# 10. Realistic punctured-rectangle proof in Mathlib 4.29

Mathlib 4.29 has a rectangle Cauchy–Goursat theorem with the boundary already expanded into four interval integrals:

```lean
Complex.integral_boundary_rect_eq_zero_of_continuousOn_of_differentiableOn
```

and the more flexible countable-exception version

```lean
Complex.integral_boundary_rect_eq_zero_of_differentiable_on_off_countable
```

in [`CauchyIntegral.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/v4.29.0/Mathlib/Analysis/Complex/CauchyIntegral.lean).

There is no need to build a general residue theorem. Use square punctures, because the available Cauchy theorem is rectangular.

## 10.1 Finite geometry

For fixed `N,H` and `0<eps<1/4`, take the outer rectangle

```text
[-1/2, N+1/2] x [-H,H]
```

and remove the squares

```text
[m-eps,m+eps] x [-eps,eps],   m=0,...,N.
```

Partition the remainder into:

```text
one top rectangle,
one bottom rectangle,
N+2 middle gap rectangles.
```

Apply the rectangle Cauchy theorem to each piece and add the identities. Internal edge integrals cancel using

```lean
intervalIntegral.integral_add_adjacent_intervals
intervalIntegral.integral_symm
```

from the interval-integral API. The result is

```text
outer CCW boundary integral
  = sum_{m=0}^N inner-square CCW boundary integrals.
```

This finite cancellation lemma will be tedious, but it is elementary and reusable.

## 10.2 Local square residue

By integer periodicity after squaring,

```text
sqSinKernel(m+w) = sqSinKernel(w).
```

Define the removable kernel

```text
kappa(w) = (pi*w/sin(pi*w))^2,   kappa(0)=1.
```

One needs

```text
kappa is analytic near 0,
kappa(0)=1,
kappa'(0)=0.
```

For `A=abelFactor n z`, Taylor expansion then gives a bounded continuous remainder `H_m` with

```text
A(m+w)*sqSinKernel(m+w)
  = A(m)/w^2 + A'(m)/w + H_m(w).
```

On the CCW boundary of the square of radius `eps`, prove directly

```text
integral 1/w^2 dw = 0,
integral 1/w dw   = 2*pi*I,
|integral H_m(w) dw| <= 8*eps*sup|H_m| -> 0.
```

The simple-pole square integral can be reduced side-by-side to

```text
integral_{-1}^1 1/(1+x^2) dx = pi/2.
```

This direct square computation is more realistic than using circular holes: Mathlib has Cauchy's derivative formula for circles,

```lean
Complex.circleIntegral_one_div_sub_center_pow_smul_of_differentiable_on_off_countable
```

but it does not by itself identify a rectangle-with-circular-holes boundary. Square holes align with the existing rectangle theorem.

## 10.3 Limit order

Take limits in this order:

```text
1. eps -> 0+      (fixed N,H): recover the finite residue sum;
2. H -> infinity (fixed N):   horizontal edges vanish and vertical edges
                              become improper line integrals;
3. N -> infinity:             the right vertical edge vanishes because
                              z^N beats every fixed polynomial;
4. z -> 1-      (fixed n):    dominated convergence on the left line.
```

Do not combine these into a single three-parameter limit.

## 10.4 Bounds needed for the outer edges

For fixed source index `n`, establish:

```text
|F_n(x+i*y)| + |F_n'(x+i*y)|
  <= C_n * (1+x+|y|)^(d_n)
```

on `x>=-1/2` away from the integer kernel poles, for some explicit natural `d_n` (the sharp degree is unnecessary).

Also use

```text
|sin(pi*(x+i*y))|^2 = sin(pi*x)^2 + sinh(pi*y)^2.
```

Then:

```text
horizontal edges: polynomial(H) / sinh(pi*H)^2 -> 0;
right edge:       z^(N+1/2) * polynomial(N,|y|) / cosh(pi*y)^2
                  has integral -> 0.
```

This is where the polynomial degree of `R_n` enters the residue proof. It is harmless because `n` is fixed before `N -> infinity` and `0<z<1`.

# Final recommendation

Do not begin with the `2048` estimate; that part is sound and comparatively routine. The critical path is:

```text
fixed-line Abel–Barnes theorem (BR)
        |
finite partial-fraction source normalization
        |
exact sourceError_eq_barnesAtOne
        |
2048 * 2^(-n) bound
        |
Tendsto sourceError atTop (nhds 0)
```

The only repaired mathematical formulas are:

1. retain `z^(s+n+1)` in the shifted Abel-stage contour;
2. state the combined correction as
   `tilde_r - log(z) r + double_tilde_r - (log(z)/2) tilde_r`;
3. restrict the logarithmic-derivative identity to the nonzero midpoint/fixed-line region.

With those repairs, Q6035's claimed geometric constant remains valid.
