# Q6761: harmonic toy Heilbronn-class sum audit

## Verdict

The reduction to a harmonic Weyl sum is correct in spirit, but the requested p^(1-delta) estimate is NOT currently a theorem for the harmonic phase H_r. The fresh Bernoulli-polynomial reduction needs a regularization correction, and the direct Stepanov transfer from Heilbronn sums stops at a precise obstruction: the shift relation has high-degree inverse increments, not a low-degree algebraic relation of the type used in the known Heilbronn arguments.

The sharp partial result is the exact reduction: strip anti-concentration follows from a uniform bound

|Sigma(t,s)| = |sum_r e_p(t H_r + s r)| <= p^(1-delta)

for t != 0. Establishing this bound remains the missing input.

## Bernoulli reduction audit

The identity

H_r = sum_{n=1}^r n^(p-2) mod p

is valid by Fermat for r<p.

The naive formula

H_r = -(B_{p-1}(r+1)-B_{p-1}) mod p

must be interpreted carefully. B_{p-1} itself is not an ordinary element of F_p because of the von Staudt-Clausen denominator. The safe statement is the finite-difference one:

There is a unique polynomial f of degree at most p-1 with f(0)=0 and

f(x+1)-f(x)=x^(p-2)

on F_p minus the singular point. This polynomial satisfies f(r)=H_r for all r in the range.

Thus the phase is a degree-(p-1) polynomial with a rigid first-difference equation. The Bernoulli expression is a shorthand for this regularized object, not a literal substitution of B_{p-1} into F_p.

## What Heilbronn gives and what it does not

Classical Heilbronn sums concern phases with extra lifting structure, for example exponential sums arising from n^p modulo p^2. The key input is not merely high degree; it is a relation after lifting which produces low-complexity algebraic constraints.

Known Heath-Brown/Konyagin type estimates therefore do not immediately apply to

sum_r e_p(t H_r).

The harmonic polynomial has the shift law

f(x+1)-f(x)=x^(p-2),

but x^(p-2) behaves as 1/x on F_p^*. This is a rational increment with p-1 possible poles, not a bounded-degree polynomial increment.

Therefore the existing Heilbronn machinery does not provide the needed uniform power saving.

## Attempted Stepanov construction

The graph

G={(x,f(x))}

has the deterministic transition

(x,y) -> (x+1,y+x^(p-2)).

A Stepanov auxiliary polynomial would need a nonzero low-degree relation vanishing on many points of G and surviving finite-difference differentiation.

The obstruction is degree growth:

After k shifts the accumulated increment is

f(x+k)-f(x)=sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(x+j)^(p-2),

which is a rational function with k poles. For k comparable to the auxiliary multiplicity, the pole divisor grows at the same scale as the number of imposed zeros.

The usual Stepanov balance fails: multiplicity gain is paid back by pole/degree growth.

The exact obstruction is therefore:

No bounded-degree auxiliary relation is currently produced from the shift equation alone.

## Linear twist s

The additional factor

e_p(s r)

is harmless if the main construction exists. It only changes the phase by a linear polynomial. Standard Stepanov constructions tolerate additive linear twists because they do not increase the pole divisor or the finite-difference complexity.

The bottleneck is t H_r, not s r.

## Strip consequence

The Q6759 bilinear separation remains valid:

A dyadic strip count can be expressed through the squared harmonic Weyl sums after smoothing the distance constraint.

Hence a theorem of the form

max_{t!=0}|sum_r e_p(tH_r)| <= p^(1-delta)

would imply a power-saving distance anti-concentration theorem for the harmonic toy.

The same template on the Apery side would require an analogue

max_{t!=0}|sum_r e_p(t E_r)| <= p^(1-delta)

where E_r is the relevant Apery twisted walk phase. This is the exact Heilbronn-class statement needed.

## Final answers

(i) Least confident step:

The least confident step is the negative claim about transferring Heilbronn estimates. A new variant of the Heath-Brown auxiliary method could exploit the finite-difference equation in a way not captured by the standard degree count. The obstruction identified here is a failure of the known construction, not a proof of impossibility.

(ii) Blind spot in the question:

The question may be treating "low-complexity difference equation" as equivalent to "Heilbronn structure." They are not the same. The decisive feature of Heilbronn sums is a liftable relation with controlled algebraic degree; the harmonic walk has a simple recurrence but a maximal-pole rational increment. The missing theorem is therefore not just a better Stepanov argument, but a new finite-field exponential-sum mechanism for high-pole finite-difference systems.
