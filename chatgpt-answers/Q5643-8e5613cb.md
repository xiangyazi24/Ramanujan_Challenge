# Apéry companion numerator versus the integer Apéry number

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
a_n=\frac{A_n}{C_n},\qquad A_n\in\mathbf Z,\quad C_n>0,\quad
\gcd(A_n,C_n)=1,
\]

and put

\[
H_n:=\gcd(A_n,b_n).
\]

I do **not** obtain a proof that \(H_n=1\) for every \(n\), and I do not find a rigorous counterexample in the live repository or in the literature checked for this audit. The exact computation through \(n=10000\) is strong evidence, but it is not presently promoted to a theorem.

The strongest proof-grade conclusions I obtain are these.

1. **Exact Wronskian denominator-hole theorem.** For every \(n\ge2\),
   \[
   H_n\mid 6C_{n-1},\qquad H_n\mid 6C_{n+1}.
   \]
   In fact \(b_n\) is coprime to \(6\), so
   \[
   \boxed{H_n\mid \gcd(C_{n-1},C_{n+1}),\qquad
          \gcd(H_n,C_n)=1.}
   \tag{1}
   \]
   Equivalently, with
   \[
   \Delta_n:=
   \frac{\gcd(C_{n-1},C_{n+1})}
        {\gcd(C_{n-1},C_n,C_{n+1})},
   \]
   one has
   \[
   \boxed{H_n\mid\Delta_n.}
   \tag{2}
   \]
   Thus a common prime is necessarily an isolated prime missing from the denominator at the central index and present in both neighboring denominators.

2. **Best unconditional pointwise size bound justified by the current inputs.** Since Apéry integrality gives
   \[
   C_m\mid \operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,m)^3,
   \]
   (1) implies
   \[
   v_p(H_n)\le 3\lfloor\log_p(n-1)\rfloor
   \]
   and hence
   \[
   \boxed{
   \log H_n\le 3\psi(n-1)=3n+o(n).
   }
   \tag{3}
   \]
   I do **not** find a proof of the desired pointwise improvement \(\log H_n=o(n)\). The current project density-one theorem does transfer to \(H_n\), because \(H_n\le G_n\), but that is not an all-\(n\) result.

3. **Sharper top-half obstruction.** If \(p\ge7\), \(n/2<p<n\), and
   \[
   p\mid A_n,\qquad p\mid b_n,
   \]
   then
   \[
   \boxed{v_p(b_n)=3.}
   \tag{4}
   \]
   Thus a top-half counterexample cannot be an ordinary simple Apéry zero: it must be an exact cubic divisibility event together with an isolated companion-denominator hole.

4. **The Wronskian and generalized continued fraction do not by themselves prove coprimality.** After factorial scaling they give an exact determinant, but its unexplained row content is precisely \(H_n\). Moreover, replacing the companion by \(a_m+c b_m\) preserves both the recurrence and the Wronskian and can force an arbitrary chosen prime divisor of \(b_n\) into the numerator at index \(n\). Therefore any proof must use the distinguished initial condition globally, not merely nonvanishing of the Wronskian or the abstract recurrence.

The clear verdict is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(A_n,b_n)=1\text{ for every }n
\text{ is a plausible new theorem, but it is not proved by the current machinery.}
}
\]

The precise missing local statement is

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_n,\quad v_p(a_n)\ge0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_p(a_n)=0.
}
\tag{5}
\]

The explicit companion sum and the known block congruences determine the leading pole of \(a_n\), but at an Apéry zero that leading state vanishes; no current theorem proves that the surviving integral state in (5) is a unit.

## 2. Two elementary congruence lemmas for \(b_n\)

These remove the small primes from the Wronskian carrier.

### Lemma 2.1: \(b_n\equiv1\pmod4\)

For every \(n\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{b_n\equiv1\pmod4.}
\tag{7}
\]

#### Proof

Put

\[
R(n,k)=\binom nk\binom{n+k}{k}.
\]

For \(k>0\), the two binomial coefficients cannot both be odd. Indeed,

- \(\binom nk\) is odd only when every binary one of \(k\) is also a binary one of \(n\);
- \(\binom{n+k}{k}\) is odd only when adding \(n\) and \(k\) in base two has no carry, equivalently \(n\mathbin{\&}k=0\).

Together these conditions force \(k=0\). Hence \(R(n,k)\) is even for every \(1\le k\le n\). Therefore

\[
b_n=1+\sum_{k=1}^nR(n,k)^2\equiv1\pmod4.
\]

\(\square\)

### Lemma 2.2: \(b_n\equiv(-1)^n\pmod3\)

For every \(n\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{b_n\equiv(-1)^n\pmod3.}
\tag{8}
\]

#### Proof

Use the Gessel--Lucas congruence for the Apéry numbers. If

\[
n=\sum_i n_i3^i,\qquad n_i\in\{0,1,2\},
\]

then

\[
b_n\equiv\prod_i b_{n_i}\pmod3.
\]

Now

\[
b_0=1,\qquad b_1=5\equiv-1,\qquad b_2=73\equiv1\pmod3.
\]

Thus the product is \((-1)^t\), where \(t\) is the number of digits equal to one. Since every \(3^i\) is odd, \(t\equiv n\pmod2\). This proves (8). \(\square\)

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\gcd(b_n,6)=1.}
\tag{9}
\]

## 3. Exact Wronskian denominator-hole theorem

### Theorem 3.1

For every \(n\ge2\),

\[
H_n\mid6C_{n-1},
\qquad
H_n\mid6C_{n+1}.
\tag{10}
\]

#### Proof

The Wronskian at \(n\) is

\[
\frac{A_n}{C_n}b_{n-1}
-
\frac{A_{n-1}}{C_{n-1}}b_n
=
\frac6{n^3}.
\]

Clearing denominators gives

\[
n^3A_nC_{n-1}b_{n-1}
-
n^3A_{n-1}C_nb_n
=
6C_nC_{n-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

Both terms on the left are divisible by \(H_n=\gcd(A_n,b_n)\). Hence

\[
H_n\mid6C_nC_{n-1}.
\]

But \(H_n\mid A_n\) and \(\gcd(A_n,C_n)=1\), so \(\gcd(H_n,C_n)=1\). Euclid's lemma yields

\[
H_n\mid6C_{n-1}.
\]

Similarly, the next Wronskian

\[
a_{n+1}b_n-a_nb_{n+1}=\frac6{(n+1)^3}
\]

gives

\[
H_n\mid6C_{n+1}.
\]

\(\square\)

By (9), every prime divisor of \(H_n\) is coprime to six. Therefore (10) sharpens to

\[
H_n\mid\gcd(C_{n-1},C_{n+1}).
\tag{12}
\]

Since \(H_n\) is also coprime to \(C_n\), division by the common three-term denominator content proves (2).

### Corollary 3.2: support and pointwise height

Every prime divisor of \(H_n\) is at most \(n-1\), and

\[
v_p(H_n)\le3\lfloor\log_p(n-1)\rfloor.
\tag{13}
\]

Indeed, \(H_n\mid C_{n-1}\mid L_{n-1}^3\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log H_n
&\le3\sum_{p\le n-1}\lfloor\log_p(n-1)\rfloor\log p\\
&=3\psi(n-1)\\
&=3n+o(n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

This is linear. It does not prove the requested little-oh estimate.

## 4. A sharper necessary condition in the top half

The following is the strongest genuinely new local exclusion obtained in this audit.

### Proposition 4.1

Let \(p\ge7\) be prime and

\[
\frac n2<p<n.
\]

If \(p\mid A_n\) and \(p\mid b_n\), then

\[
v_p(b_n)=3.
\]

#### Proof

Write

\[
n=p+r,\qquad0<r<p.
\]

Because \(p\mid b_n\), Gessel--Lucas gives

\[
0\equiv b_n\equiv b_1b_r=5b_r\pmod p,
\]

so \(p\mid b_r\). For \(p\ge7\), the endpoint values and reflection exclude
\(r=0,1,p-2,p-1\). Thus

\[
2\le r\le p-3.
\]

The no-consecutive-zero lemma in the first \(p\)-block gives

\[
p\nmid b_{r-1}.
\]

Another Lucas reduction therefore gives

\[
p\nmid b_{n-1}=b_{p+r-1}.
\tag{15}
\]

Put

\[
e=v_p(b_n)\ge1.
\]

Since \(p\mid A_n\) and \(p\nmid C_n\),

\[
v_p(a_n)\ge1.
\]

The Wronskian

\[
a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=\frac6{n^3}
\]

has a \(p\)-adic unit on the right, because \(p\nmid n\). By (15), the first term has positive valuation. Hence the second term must have valuation zero:

\[
v_p(a_{n-1})+e=0.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus the state \((a_n,a_{n-1})\) has minimum valuation \(-e\).

For every index \(m\) with

\[
p<m<n,
\]

the recurrence transition

\[
\binom{a_{m+1}}{a_m}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
P(m)/(m+1)^3&-m^3/(m+1)^3\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{a_m}{a_{m-1}}
\tag{17}
\]

lies in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf Z_p)\), because its determinant
\(m^3/(m+1)^3\) is a \(p\)-adic unit. Therefore the minimum valuation of the two state coordinates is invariant while moving through the regular block. It follows that

\[
\min\{v_p(a_{p+1}),v_p(a_p)\}=-e.
\tag{18}
\]

The explicit Apéry companion sum, with the Kummer valuation audit already proved in the repository, gives

\[
p^3a_p\equiv6\pmod p.
\tag{19}
\]

Hence

\[
v_p(a_p)=-3.
\]

At the recurrence row \(p\),

\[
(p+1)^3a_{p+1}=P(p)a_p-p^3a_{p-1}.
\]

Here \(P(p)\equiv5\pmod p\), while \(a_{p-1}\) is \(p\)-integral because
\(C_{p-1}\mid L_{p-1}^3\). Therefore

\[
v_p(a_{p+1})=-3.
\]

Equation (18) now gives \(e=3\). \(\square\)

This proposition does not prove that no common prime exists. It says that any top-half common prime is a highly exceptional cubic Apéry divisor. No theorem in the current repository excludes all such divisors.

## 5. Why the nonzero Wronskian is not a coprimality proof

There is a clean structural counterexample to any argument that uses only the recurrence and the value of the Wronskian while ignoring the distinguished initial state.

Fix \(n\) and a prime \(p\mid b_n\). For any rational \(c\), define

\[
a_m^{(c)}:=a_m+c b_m.
\]

Then \(a^{(c)}\) satisfies the same recurrence and

\[
a_m^{(c)}b_{m-1}-a_{m-1}^{(c)}b_m
=
a_mb_{m-1}-a_{m-1}b_m
=
\frac6{m^3}.
\]

Choose

\[
c=\frac{p-a_n}{b_n}.
\]

Then

\[
a_n^{(c)}=p,
\]

so the reduced numerator at index \(n\) is divisible by \(p\), as is \(b_n\), while the recurrence and Wronskian are unchanged.

This does **not** give a counterexample to the actual Apéry companion, because it changes the initial condition. It proves exactly the required hostile point: nonzero Wronskian plus the abstract recurrence is logically insufficient. A proof must use the global connection from

\[
(a_0,a_1)=(0,6)
\]

to the row \(n\).

## 6. Factorial scaling and generalized continued fractions

Put

\[
X_n:=\frac{(n!)^3a_n}{6},
\qquad
Y_n:=(n!)^3b_n.
\]

The explicit companion formula implies \(X_n\in\mathbf Z\), and clearly
\(Y_n\in\mathbf Z\). Both satisfy

\[
U_{n+1}=P(n)U_n-n^6U_{n-1},
\qquad
P(n)=34n^3+51n^2+27n+5.
\tag{20}
\]

The Wronskian becomes

\[
\boxed{
X_{n+1}Y_n-X_nY_{n+1}=(n!)^6.
}
\tag{21}
\]

This is the canonical generalized-continued-fraction determinant. It is exact, but it does not prove the desired row is primitive.

Indeed, with \(F_n=(n!)^3\),

\[
6X_n=\frac{F_n}{C_n}A_n,
\qquad
Y_n=\frac{F_n}{C_n}(C_nb_n),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(6X_n,Y_n)
=
\frac{F_n}{C_n}\,H_n.
}
\tag{22}
\]

Thus the unexplained common content of the canonical convergent is exactly the same \(H_n\). Equation (21) bounds products of adjacent contents; it does not establish \(H_n=1\). Treating (21) as if it were the determinant-one identity for a simple continued fraction is the precise invalid shortcut.

## 7. What the explicit companion sum actually supplies

The live formula is

\[
a_N
=
\sum_{K=0}^N
\binom NK^2\binom{N+K}K^2
\left(
H_N^{(3)}+
\sum_{j=1}^K
\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}
{2j^3\binom Nj\binom{N+j}j}
\right).
\tag{23}
\]

Together with termwise Kummer bookkeeping, it proves, for \(p\ge7\),
\(n=qp+r<p^2\),

\[
\boxed{
p^3a_{qp+r}\equiv a_qb_r\pmod p.}
\tag{24}
\]

This is enough for the support law for \(G_n\), but not for numerator coprimality.

At a lower-channel zero \(p\mid b_r\), the right side of (24) vanishes. The conclusion is only

\[
v_p(a_{qp+r})>-3
\]

relative to the generic pole. It does not decide whether the surviving value has valuation

\[
-2,-1,0,1,\ldots.
\]

The desired coprimality statement is exactly the assertion that, whenever this cancellation reaches the integral range, it stops at valuation zero. The mod-\(p^2\) audit in `Q804.md` shows why the first refinement is insufficient: a bad fiber kills the zeroth Frobenius state, while the first defect/derivative state remains free and generally nonzero. It does not yield a second vanishing equation, nor does it prove the first surviving state is always a unit after all denominator cancellations.

Therefore the explicit sum is essential for Proposition 4.1 and for the block law, but no presently proved termwise identity closes (5).

## 8. Strongest unconditional conclusions on \(H_n\)

For every \(n\), the following are unconditional:

\[
H_n\mid b_n,
\qquad
H_n\mid\Delta_n,
\qquad
H_n\mid L_{n-1}^3,
\tag{25}
\]

and hence

\[
\log H_n
\le
\min\{\log b_n,3\psi(n-1)\}
=
3n+o(n).
\tag{26}
\]

Here

\[
\log b_n=n\log(17+12\sqrt2)+O(\log n),
\]

so the denominator-hole bound is the better universal coefficient.

The current accepted project theorem also gives

\[
\log H_n=o(n)
\]

for a set of integers of natural density one, because \(H_n\le G_n\). What remains unavailable is the pointwise statement for **every** \(n\).

A proof of either of the following would be genuinely new and sufficient:

1. the unit-survival theorem (5);
2. the denominator-hole exclusion
   \[
   \gcd(b_n,\Delta_n)=1;
   \]
3. a pointwise subexponential bound
   \[
   \log\gcd(b_n,\Delta_n)=o(n).
   \]

The second is stronger than the observed \(H_n=1\); the third would already give the requested \(\log H_n=o(n)\).

## 9. Exact implication for \(G_n\)

Let

\[
L_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),
\qquad
G_n=\gcd(L_n^3a_n,L_n^3b_n).
\]

Since \(C_n\mid L_n^3\),

\[
L_n^3a_n=\frac{L_n^3}{C_n}A_n,
\qquad
L_n^3b_n=\frac{L_n^3}{C_n}(C_nb_n).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_n
&=\frac{L_n^3}{C_n}\gcd(A_n,C_nb_n)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{L_n^3}{C_n}\gcd(A_n,b_n)},
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

because \(\gcd(A_n,C_n)=1\).

Thus the identity requested in the question is correct when its \(d_n\) denotes \(L_n\):

\[
\boxed{
G_n=\frac{d_n^3}{C_n}\gcd(A_n,b_n).
}
\]

In the repository convention where `d_n` already denotes \(L_n^3\), the same formula is instead

\[
G_n=\frac{d_n}{C_n}\gcd(A_n,b_n).
\]

Consequences:

- If the observed coprimality theorem were proved, then
  \[
  G_n=\frac{L_n^3}{C_n}
  \]
  exactly.
- In general,
  \[
  \log G_n
  =3\log L_n-\log C_n+\log H_n.
  \]
- Since \(L_n^3/C_n\) is a positive integer,
  \[
  H_n\mid G_n.
  \]
  Hence a pointwise proof of the full Problem 3.2 estimate \(\log G_n=o(n)\) would automatically imply \(\log H_n=o(n)\), but that full pointwise theorem is exactly the current open frontier.

## Final verdict

No counterexample is presently known, and the computation through \(10000\) is entirely consistent with

\[
\gcd(A_n,b_n)=1.
\]

However, neither the Wronskian, the explicit companion sum at its currently proved precision, the generalized continued fraction determinant, nor the local recurrence proves it.

The proof-grade replacement statement is the exact divisor theorem

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(A_n,b_n)\mid
\frac{\gcd(C_{n-1},C_{n+1})}
     {\gcd(C_{n-1},C_n,C_{n+1})},
}
\]

with the universal pointwise bound

\[
\boxed{
\log\gcd(A_n,b_n)\le3\psi(n-1)=3n+o(n).
}
\]

A full proof now requires a distinguished-initial-state, higher-precision \(p\)-adic theorem showing that an Apéry zero and an integral companion value cannot simultaneously vanish modulo the same prime. No such theorem is currently available in the audited project state.
