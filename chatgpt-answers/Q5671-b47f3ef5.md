# Finite Racah `H_p`: exact spectral object, and a no-go for elliptic/Apéry-K3 Hasse interpretations

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
\phi_m(X)=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(X-j(j+1))}{(m!)^2},
\qquad
H_p(X)=\sum_{m=0}^{(p-1)/2}\phi_m(X)^2\in\mathbf F_p[X].
\]

The concrete identification is **spectral, not geometric**.

1. Writing \(X=\nu(\nu+1)\), the characteristic-zero object is

   \[
   \mathcal G_\nu(t)
   ={}_4F_3\!\left(
   \begin{matrix}-\nu,-\nu,\nu+1,\nu+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};t
   \right),
   \]

   and \(H_p(X)\) is its mod-\(p\) half truncation at \(t=1\). Its differential equation is

   \[
   \boxed{\bigl[\theta^4-t(\theta^2+\theta-X)^2\bigr]\mathcal G_\nu(t)=0,}
   \qquad \theta=t\frac d{dt}.
   \]

   At \(t=1\), the full characteristic-zero value is the Apéry interpolation

   \[
   B(\nu)={}_4F_3(-\nu,-\nu,\nu+1,\nu+1;1,1,1;1),
   \]

   satisfying the Apéry **difference** equation in the spectral coordinate \(\nu\).

2. For

   \[
   F_p(Z)=H_p(Z(Z+1)),
   \]

   the exact finite-field difference equation is the inhomogeneous identity

   \[
   \boxed{
   (Z+1)^3F_p(Z+1)-P(Z)F_p(Z)+Z^3F_p(Z-1)
   =-16(2Z+1)(Z^p-Z)^2,
   }
   \]

   where

   \[
   P(Z)=34Z^3+51Z^2+27Z+5.
   \]

   Thus the finite polynomial is a degree-\(p-1\) Racah/Wilson spectral interpolation with one universal finite-field seam defect. It is not obtained by replacing the Apéry generating-function parameter \(t\) with \(X\).

3. There is a rigorous no-go theorem for the preferred geometric possibilities. No elliptic scheme over a fixed open subset of the \(X\)-line can have Hasse invariant equal to \(H_p(X)\), even only up to a legitimate nowhere-zero elementary factor. The same excludes a K3 family whose variable crystal is the symmetric square of such an elliptic family. The proof is the fixed-point stabilization

   \[
   \boxed{H_p(n(n+1))\equiv b_n\pmod p\qquad(p>2n+1),}
   \]

   combined with Elkies's theorem that every elliptic curve over \(\mathbf Q\) has infinitely many supersingular primes.

4. The known Apéry K3/Shioda--Inose variation is parametrized by the generating-function variable \(t\). Its Picard--Fuchs operator is

   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal L_t
   =\theta^3
   -t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
   +t^2(\theta+1)^3.
   }
   \]

   Its first Hasse/Dwork block is, up to a nonzero conventional scalar,

   \[
   A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j.
   \]

   The exact relation to the Racah polynomial is coefficient interpolation,

   \[
   \boxed{H_p(j(j+1))=[t^j]A_p(t),}
   \]

   not evaluation \(A_p(\psi(X))\). The order, local exponents, and small-prime polynomials are incompatible with a direct rational reparametrization into either the Apéry K3 operator or the Legendre/Deuring operator.

5. I do **not** claim an absolute nonexistence theorem for every exotic K3 or higher-dimensional family. The elliptic and elliptic-symmetric-square cases are ruled out. The natural hypergeometric and known Apéry K3 identifications are ruled out. For an arbitrary fixed K3 family, no analogue of Elkies's infinitude theorem is available in the needed generality, so a completely exotic realization is presently not disproved. No such realization is present in the repository or in the primary literature audited here.

This is therefore a negative answer for the tactically useful families, with a precise remaining logical gap only for exotic non-elliptic geometry.

# 2. Characteristic-zero hypergeometric object

Choose \(\nu\) with

\[
X=\nu(\nu+1).
\]

Then

\[
(-\nu)_m(\nu+1)_m
=(-1)^m\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(X-j(j+1)),
\]

so

\[
\phi_m(X)^2
=\frac{(-\nu)_m^2(\nu+1)_m^2}{(m!)^4}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G(X,t)
:=\sum_{m\ge0}\phi_m(X)^2t^m
={}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}-\nu,-\nu,\nu+1,\nu+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};t
\right).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The generalized hypergeometric equation gives

\[
\left[
\theta^4
-t(\theta-\nu)^2(\theta+\nu+1)^2
\right]\mathcal G=0.
\]

Because

\[
(\theta-\nu)(\theta+\nu+1)
=\theta^2+\theta-X,
\]

this becomes

\[
\boxed{
\left[\theta^4-t(\theta^2+\theta-X)^2\right]\mathcal G=0.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is the clean differential equation requested in part 1. Its differential variable is \(t\). The quantity \(X\) occurs in the exponents/upper parameters.

At \(t=1\),

\[
\mathcal G(X,1)=B(\nu)
={}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}-\nu,-\nu,\nu+1,\nu+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1
\right).
\tag{2.3}
\]

For \(\nu=r\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}\), the series terminates and equals

\[
B(r)=b_r.
\]

The exact characteristic-zero contiguous equation is

\[
\boxed{
(\nu+1)^3B(\nu+1)-P(\nu)B(\nu)+\nu^3B(\nu-1)=0.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus the natural equation in the requested \(X\)-direction is a quadratic-lattice difference equation. If

\[
s=2\nu+1,\qquad s^2=4X+1,
\]

then the shifts are

\[
(\nu+1)(\nu+2)=X+s+1,
\qquad
(\nu-1)\nu=X-s+1.
\]

Equation (2.4) is therefore a divided-difference equation on the two-sheeted quadratic lattice, not a Picard--Fuchs ODE on an \(X\)-moduli line.

## 2.1 Exact finite truncation as a terminating `6F5`

Put \(M=(p-1)/2\). In characteristic \(p\),

\[
-M\equiv M+1\equiv\frac12.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
H_p(\nu(\nu+1))
={}_6F_5\!\left(
\begin{matrix}
-M,M+1,-\nu,-\nu,\nu+1,\nu+1\\
\frac12,\frac12,1,1,1
\end{matrix};1
\right)
\pmod p.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

The factor \((-M)_m\) terminates the series at \(m=M\), and the quotient

\[
\frac{(-M)_m(M+1)_m}{(1/2)_m^2}
\]

is \(1\) modulo \(p\) throughout the truncation range. Formula (2.5) makes the obstruction visible: the characteristic-zero terminating parameter \(M=(p-1)/2\) itself grows with the residue characteristic.

# 3. Exact finite-field difference equation

Set

\[
F_p(Z)=H_p(Z(Z+1)).
\]

The node identity is

\[
F_p(r)=b_r\pmod p
\qquad(r\in\mathbf F_p).
\]

The polynomial recurrence residual is

\[
\mathcal R_p(Z)
=(Z+1)^3F_p(Z+1)-P(Z)F_p(Z)+Z^3F_p(Z-1).
\]

The exact symbolic identity established in the current project state is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_p(Z)
=-16(2Z+1)(Z^p-Z)^2.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is stronger than a nodewise recurrence. It says:

- the recurrence is exact at every field node;
- its first derivative is also compatible at every field node;
- the first global discrepancy is the universal second-order finite-field seam;
- the discrepancy is one next Newton--Racah basis vector, not a target selector.

Equation (3.1) is the most useful exact `X`-direction equation presently available.

# 4. Local exponents: natural spectral object versus K3 and Deuring

## 4.1 The natural `4F3` object

For (2.2), the Riemann exponents in the differential variable \(t\) are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 t & \text{exponents}\\ \hline
 0 & 0,0,0,0\\
 1 & 0,1,1,2\\
 \infty & -\nu,-\nu,\nu+1,\nu+1.
\end{array}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The monodromy eigenvalues at infinity depend on \(\nu\), hence on \(X=\nu(\nu+1)\). This is exactly what a spectral parameter does.

## 4.2 The Apéry K3 Picard--Fuchs operator

The third-order operator is

\[
\mathcal L_t
=\theta^3
-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Its singular points and exponents are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 t & \text{exponents}\\ \hline
 0 & 0,0,0\\
 17\pm12\sqrt2 & 0,\frac12,1\\
 \infty & 1,1,1.
\end{array}
\tag{4.3}
\]

It is the symmetric square of the rank-two operator

\[
\boxed{
(1-34t+t^2)\theta^2
-t(17-t)\theta
-\frac14t(10-t).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The rank-two exponents are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 t & \text{exponents}\\ \hline
 0 & 0,0\\
 17\pm12\sqrt2 & 0,\frac12\\
 \infty & \frac12,\frac12.
\end{array}
\tag{4.5}
\]

## 4.3 Deuring/Legendre

The Deuring polynomial is

\[
D_p(\lambda)
=\sum_{m=0}^{(p-1)/2}\binom{(p-1)/2}{m}^2\lambda^m.
\]

Modulo \(p\), it solves

\[
\boxed{
\lambda(1-\lambda)y''+(1-2\lambda)y'-\frac14y=0,
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

with exponents

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \lambda & \text{exponents}\\ \hline
 0 & 0,0\\
 1 & 0,0\\
 \infty & \frac12,\frac12.
\end{array}
\tag{4.7}
\]

## 4.4 Consequence

A rational pullback and scalar gauge do not change the differential rank. They multiply local exponent differences by ramification indices, but they cannot turn the \(X\)-dependent infinity exponents in (4.1) into one fixed exponent table such as (4.3) or (4.7) for all \(X\).

Therefore the natural characteristic-zero object behind \(H_p\) is not a rational pullback of the Apéry K3 period or of a Deuring/Legendre period. This is a rigorous obstruction to the direct hypergeometric identification. It is not, by itself, an obstruction to an entirely unrelated family having accidentally equal Hasse polynomials; the next section supplies the stronger elliptic no-go.

# 5. Fixed-point stabilization

## Theorem 5.1

For every integer \(n\ge0\) and every prime \(p>2n+1\),

\[
\boxed{H_p(n(n+1))\equiv b_n\pmod p.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

Since \((p-1)/2>n\), every term with \(m>n\) contains the factor

\[
n(n+1)-n(n+1)=0.
\]

For \(0\le m\le n\),

\[
\phi_m(n(n+1))
=\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)(n+1)(n+2)\cdots(n+m)}{(m!)^2}
=\binom nm\binom{n+m}{m}.
\]

Therefore the defining sum for \(H_p(n(n+1))\) is exactly the finite integer sum defining \(b_n\), reduced modulo \(p\). ∎

Hence, at every fixed integral spectral point \(X_n=n(n+1)\),

\[
\{p:H_p(X_n)=0\}
\]

is finite outside the fixed small-prime set: it is contained in the prime divisors of \(b_n\).

# 6. Rigorous elliptic and symmetric-square no-go

A legitimate change of Hasse-invariant trivialization multiplies the scalar by a nowhere-zero factor of the form

\[
c_p\,g(X)\,u(X)^{p-1},
\]

on a common open set, where \(g,u\) are fixed rational functions and \(c_p\ne0\). Such a factor does not change the zero locus at any fixed good rational point for all sufficiently large primes. Thus it suffices to compare zero loci.

## Theorem 6.1: no elliptic family

There is no nonempty open \(U\subset\mathbf A^1_X\) over \(\mathbf Q\) and elliptic scheme

\[
E\longrightarrow U
\]

such that, for all sufficiently large good primes and all points in the common good locus,

\[
H_p(x)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
E_x\text{ is supersingular}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

Choose \(n\) with \(X_n=n(n+1)\in U(\mathbf Q)\). This is possible because the complement of \(U\) is finite.

The fixed elliptic curve \(E_{X_n}/\mathbf Q\) has good reduction at all but finitely many primes. By Theorem 5.1, for every sufficiently large good prime,

\[
H_p(X_n)\equiv b_n\pmod p.
\]

Thus \(H_p(X_n)=0\) only at the finite set of prime divisors of \(b_n\), together with the fixed excluded primes. Equation (6.1) would imply that \(E_{X_n}\) has only finitely many supersingular primes.

This contradicts Elkies's theorem that every elliptic curve over \(\mathbf Q\) has infinitely many supersingular primes. ∎

This excludes all Legendre/Deuring pullbacks, all fixed elliptic pencils, and all claimed identities up to nonvanishing elementary factors.

## Corollary 6.2: no elliptic symmetric-square K3 family

Suppose the varying rank-three crystal of a K3 family is, up to a fixed Tate twist, \(\operatorname{Sym}^2H^1(E_X)\). At a good prime:

- an ordinary elliptic curve has slopes \(0,1\), hence symmetric-square slopes \(0,1,2\);
- a supersingular elliptic curve has slopes \(1/2,1/2\), hence symmetric-square slopes \(1,1,1\).

Therefore the K3 fiber is nonordinary exactly when the elliptic fiber is supersingular. Theorem 6.1 excludes \(H_p\) as its Hasse zero divisor.

This rules out the standard tactically useful Shioda--Inose interpretation: a fixed rational elliptic family, followed by its symmetric square/Kummer construction, cannot produce \(H_p(X)\).

## Exact boundary of the theorem

The proof does not exclude an exotic K3 family whose transcendental crystal is not controlled by an elliptic rank-two system. For arbitrary K3 surfaces there is no theorem asserting infinitely many nonordinary primes in the generality needed to repeat Elkies's argument. Therefore the universal statement

```text
no bounded-dimensional family of any kind can realize H_p
```

is not currently proved. The theorem-level negative result is exactly:

```text
no elliptic family;
no Legendre/Deuring pullback;
no elliptic symmetric-square K3 family;
no direct pullback of the natural 4F3 spectral equation to the Apéry K3 equation.
```

# 7. The known Apéry K3/Shioda--Inose family

A standard Apéry--Fermi K3 pencil is written

\[
x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}+z+z^{-1}=k.
\tag{7.1}
\]

After the usual compactification/resolution, the family is Shioda--Inose/Kummer-related to a product of isogenous elliptic curves. Its modular/Picard--Fuchs parameter is a generating-function parameter, not the spectral Casimir \(X\).

At the operator level, the verified Apéry variation is (4.2), with holomorphic period

\[
F(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n.
\]

Its first Hasse/Dwork polynomial is

\[
\boxed{A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j,}
\tag{7.2}
\]

up to a nonzero conventional scalar/Hodge trivialization.

The current repository also has the corrected integral constant-term identity

\[
\Lambda(x,y,z)
=
\frac{(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)\bigl((1+y)(1+z)+xyz\bigr)}{xyz},
\tag{7.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{CT}\Lambda^m=b_m.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Consequently, purely algebraically,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{CT}(1-t\Lambda)^{p-1}
\equiv\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}b_mt^m
=A_p(t)\pmod p.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Equation (7.5) proves the coefficient formula. It should not be overstated: a complete geometric identification of this particular toric model, compactification, residue form, and Hodge-line normalization is a separate check. The Picard--Fuchs K3 variation itself is already known independently.

The relation to \(H_p\) is

\[
\boxed{H_p(j(j+1))=b_j=[t^j]A_p(t).}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Thus \(H_p\) is the unique degree-\(p-1\) spectral polynomial built from the coefficient sequence on the folded quadratic lattice. It is not the Hasse invariant of the K3 fiber at \(t=X\), and no rational parameter change has been verified or is compatible with the preferred elliptic/symmetric-square interpretation.

# 8. Symbolic checks for `p=5,7,11,13`

Direct expansion of the defining product gives:

```text

H_7(X)  = 1 + 2 X^3 + X^4 + 5 X^5 + X^6.

H_11(X) = 1 + 6 X^3 + 4 X^4 + 3 X^5
            + 9 X^6 + 9 X^7 + 7 X^8 + 8 X^9 + X^10.

H_13(X) = 1 + 6 X^3 + 8 X^4 + 4 X^5
            + 2 X^6 + 6 X^7
            + 9 X^9 + 5 X^10 + 3 X^11 + X^12.
```

The missing \(X\) and \(X^2\) coefficients are not typographical errors. In particular,

\[
[X^2]H_p
=\sum_{m=1}^{(p-1)/2}\frac1{m^2}=0\pmod p.

The folded node checks are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}

  & H_p(X)=b_r\pmod p\\ \hline
5  & 0,2,1       & 1,0,3\\

13 & 0,2,6,12,7,4,3 & 1,5,8,2,7,5,9.

\tag{8.1}

For comparison, the genuine Apéry K3 Hasse/Dwork blocks are

```text
A_5(t)  = 1 + 3 t^2 + t^4.

A_7(t)  = 1 + 5 t + 3 t^2 + 3 t^3
            + 3 t^4 + 5 t^5 + t^6.

A_11(t) = 1 + 5 t + 7 t^2 + 4 t^3 + t^4
```

# 9. Correct saturated conclusion

The tactical classification is:

```text
PROVED:
  H_p is a half-truncated 4F3 spectral polynomial on X=nu(nu+1).
  Its exact finite difference residual is the universal double seam.
  It is not a direct pullback of the Apéry K3 or Deuring operator.
  It cannot be an elliptic Hasse invariant, even up to a nonzero
  elementary factor.
  It cannot be the Hasse invariant of an elliptic symmetric-square K3.
  The actual Apéry K3 Hasse block lives in t, and H_p interpolates its
  coefficients rather than its fiber values.

OPEN:
  an entirely different, non-elliptic K3 or higher-dimensional family
  over the X-line whose Hasse scalar happens to be H_p.

NOT SUFFICIENT:
  degree growth alone;
  the word "hypergeometric";
  the Shioda--Inose property of the known t-family;
  small-prime polynomial matching after an ad hoc p-dependent change.
```

A viable positive identification would now have to defeat Theorem 5.1. In particular, every fixed rational fiber \(X=n(n+1)\) would have to be ordinary at all but finitely many primes. That is impossible for elliptic and elliptic-symmetric-square fibers and is a highly restrictive arithmetic property for any proposed replacement.

Accordingly, `folded Hasse polynomial` should remain explicitly marked as project terminology. A safer theorem-level name is

```text
finite Racah spectral interpolation polynomial
```

or

```text
folded Apéry--Racah polynomial.
```
