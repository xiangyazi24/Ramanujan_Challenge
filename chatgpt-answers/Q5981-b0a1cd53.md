# Terminal exterior propagation: rank 8001, and the missing factor is observability rather than transfer

## Verdict

Let the fourteen signed-binomial ray blocks have exact orders

\[
r_1,\ldots,r_{14},\qquad \sum_{\nu=1}^{14}r_\nu=126.
\]

There is an exact common state of rank \(126\) for each coordinate of the \(Y/W\) packet.  Adjoining the cumulative coordinate raises the scalar state rank to

\[
R=126+1=127.
\]

The determinant

\[
e_L=\det(B_L,V_{L-1})
\]

therefore lives in the exterior square of that augmented state, of rank

\[
\boxed{K=\binom{127}{2}=8001.}
\]

This is the correct finite rank.  The naive augmented tensor state has rank \(127^2=16129\); the antisymmetry of the determinant and the fact that the two coordinates have the same transfer reduce it exactly to \(8001\).

A tail of \(8001\) consecutive zeros does propagate to every earlier \(e_L\), **provided two separate conditions hold**:

1. the fourteen block transfers are invertible modulo \(p\) on the interval in question; and
2. one terminal \(8001\times8001\) exterior observability determinant is a \(p\)-unit.

The first condition is exactly what the endpoint ledger addresses.  The second is not implied by it.  More precisely, if \(\Theta_T\) is the terminal observability determinant defined below, then

\[
 e_T=\cdots=e_{T+8000}=0\pmod p,\qquad
 p\nmid \Theta_T,
\]

together with transfer regularity, implies

\[
 e_L=0\pmod p
\]

for every \(L\), in particular for the fixed prefix \(e_1,\ldots,e_{8001}\).

The scalar order-\(8001\) cofactor recurrence has endpoint factors

\[
\boxed{
H_{8001}(T)=\pm\Theta_T,
\qquad
H_0(T)=\Theta_{T+1}\det(\Psi_T),
}
\]

where \(\Psi_T\) is the exterior transfer.  Thus the block endpoint factors occur only through \(\det(\Psi_T)\); the additional factors \(\Theta_T,\Theta_{T+1}\) are scalar-observability/Fitting factors.

Consequently the proposed propagation is **not proved by the two terminal reports as summarized in the question**.  Those reports prove finite transfer rank and target-specialized transfer regularity.  They do not prove that the determinant output is cyclic/observable after target specialization.  No heuristic is involved in this distinction: it is the exact cofactor formula above.

There is, however, a clean exact completion criterion.  On each finitely many terminal branches, reduce \(\Theta_T\) at the target relation modulo \(p\).  If the resulting fixed polynomial \(F_{\rm obs}(M)\) is nonzero, then every exceptional target prime divides the fixed polynomial

\[
F_{\rm end}(M)F_{\rm obs}(M),
\]

where \(F_{\rm end}\) is the product of the endpoint/boundary factors from the ledger.  The exceptional radical then has logarithmic height \(O(\log M)\).  If the reduced observability polynomial is identically zero, the exact next output must be a target-specialized vector in the right kernel of the observability matrix; that is the requested failure certificate.  Transfer endpoint coprimality alone cannot decide between these two alternatives.

## 1. The common rank-126 ray state

For ray block \(
u\), write its primitive recurrence as

\[
P_\nu(M,L,S)
 =a_{\nu,0}(M,L)+a_{\nu,1}(M,L)S+\cdots
   +a_{\nu,r_\nu}(M,L)S^{r_\nu}.
\]

The precise coefficient ring can be taken to be the polynomial ring in the global parameters, localized at the displayed leading coefficients.  Let

\[
x^c_{\nu,L}
 =\bigl(f^c_{\nu,L},f^c_{\nu,L+1},\ldots,
        f^c_{\nu,L+r_\nu-1}\bigr)^t,
\qquad c\in\{Y,W\},
\]

and let \(T_{\nu,L}\) be the usual companion transfer.  Then

\[
x^c_{\nu,L+1}=T_{\nu,L}x^c_{\nu,L},
\]

and

\[
\det T_{\nu,L}
 =(-1)^{r_\nu}
   \frac{a_{\nu,0}(M,L)}{a_{\nu,r_\nu}(M,L)}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Take the block direct sum

\[
E=\bigoplus_{\nu=1}^{14}E_\nu,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}E=126,
\]

\[
x_L^c=\bigoplus_\nu x^c_{\nu,L},
\qquad
T_L=\bigoplus_\nu T_{\nu,L}.
\]

The signed sum defining the high-difference packet is an exact row observation

\[
B_L^c=\beta_Lx_L^c.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The row \(eta_L\) is common to the two coordinates.  This common transfer is load-bearing: treating \(Y\) and \(W\) as unrelated rank-126 sequences would give only a tensor bound, while the common state gives the smaller exterior rank below.

Put

\[
A_-(M,L)=\prod_{\nu=1}^{14}a_{\nu,0}(M,L),
\qquad
A_+(M,L)=\prod_{\nu=1}^{14}a_{\nu,r_\nu}(M,L).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Then

\[
\det T_L=(-1)^{126}\frac{A_-(M,L)}{A_+(M,L)}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Thus transfer invertibility in both directions modulo \(p\) is exactly the assertion that the specializations of \(A_-\) and \(A_+\) are \(p\)-units.  The endpoint ledger may include additional denominator clearings or finitely many boundary factors, but algebraically they belong to this transfer part of the argument.

## 2. Adjoining the cumulative coordinate

For either coordinate \(c=Y,W\), define

\[
V_L^c=V_{L-1}^c+B_L^c.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Set

\[
\widetilde x_L^c=(x_L^c,V_{L-1}^c)^t\in \widetilde E:=E\oplus\mathbf 1.

Then \(\widetilde E\) has rank \(127\), and

\[
\widetilde x_{L+1}^c
 =\widetilde T_L\widetilde x_L^c,
\qquad
\widetilde T_L=
\begin{pmatrix}
T_L&0\\
\beta_L&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.2}

In particular,

\[
\det\widetilde T_L=\det T_L.
\tag{2.3}

Let \(q\) denote the last basis vector of \(\widetilde E\).  The determinant packet is the exterior observation

\[
\xi_L:=\widetilde x_L^Y\wedge\widetilde x_L^W
       \in\Lambda^2\widetilde E,
\tag{2.4}

e_L=(\beta_L\wedge q^*)(\xi_L).
\tag{2.5}

\operatorname{rank}\Lambda^2\widetilde E

\Psi_L:=\Lambda^2\widetilde T_L.

Using \(n=127\), (1.4), and (2.3),

\boxed{

## 3. The \(7875+126\) curvature decomposition

There is a useful equivalent form which makes the obstruction in Sections 68.9--68.10 visible inside the finite state.

Decompose

\[
\Lambda^2(E\oplus\mathbf 1)
 \simeq \Lambda^2E\oplus E,
\]

whose ranks are

\[
\binom{126}{2}=7875,
\qquad 126.
\]

Write

\[
\omega_L=x_L^Y\wedge x_L^W\in\Lambda^2E,
\]

\[
z_L=V_{L-1}^W x_L^Y-V_{L-1}^Y x_L^W\in E.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
\xi_L=(\omega_L,z_L),
\qquad e_L=\beta_Lz_L.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define the contraction

\[
\delta_L(u\wedge v)
 =\beta_L(v)T_Lu-\beta_L(u)T_Lv.
\tag{3.3}
\]

A direct expansion of (2.1) gives

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\omega_{L+1}\\z_{L+1}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Lambda^2T_L&0\\
\delta_L&T_L
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
\omega_L\\z_L
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

This displays the exact issue:

* the output \(e_L\) sees only the \(126\)-dimensional \(z_L\) component;
* the hidden \(7875\)-dimensional curvature state \(\omega_L\) drives \(z_L\) through \(\delta_L\);
* invertibility of \(T_L\), and hence of the full transfer, says nothing about whether repeated scalar observations \(eta_Lz_L\) recover that hidden state.

This is the finite-rank form of the curvature term in (68.99).  The old growing-rank universal curvature has not disappeared; it has been compressed into a fixed \(7875\)-dimensional hidden state.  Compression of transfer rank is not the same as observability of the scalar determinant.

## 4. Exact terminal observability matrix

Fix the first index \(T\) of a forced zero tail.  For \(j\ge0\), put

\[
\Phi_{T,0}=I,
\qquad
\Phi_{T,j}=\Psi_{T+j-1}\cdots\Psi_T
\quad(j\ge1),
\tag{4.1}
\]

and let

\[
\gamma_L=(0,\beta_L)
\]

be the row in the decomposition (3.2).  Define the transported observation rows

\[
R_{T,j}=\gamma_{T+j}\Phi_{T,j}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
e_{T+j}=R_{T,j}\xi_T.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The \(K\)-step observability matrix is

\[
\mathcal O_T=
\begin{pmatrix}
R_{T,0}\\R_{T,1}\\ \vdots\\R_{T,K-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad K=8001.
\tag{4.4}
\]

It is a square matrix over the rational-function coefficient field.  Let

\[
\Theta_T=\det\mathcal O_T.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For an integral certificate, multiply rows by the explicit products of the block leading coefficients required to clear the companion denominators, take the determinant, and then take its primitive numerator.  This produces a fixed polynomial, well-defined up to sign and multiplication by already-recorded endpoint factors.  Equivalently, \(\Theta_T\) is the top observability Fitting factor in the localized coefficient ring.

The following theorem is immediate but is the exact theorem required by the question.

### Theorem 4.1 — fixed-tail propagation

Suppose a prime \(p\) satisfies:

1. \(p\nmid A_-(M,L)A_+(M,L)\) for every transfer step between the fixed prefix and the terminal window, including the denominator-clearing factors from the endpoint ledger;
2. \(p\nmid\Theta_T\); and
3. the forced zero tail contains \(K=8001\) consecutive terms:
   
   \[
   e_T,e_{T+1},\ldots,e_{T+K-1}\equiv0\pmod p.
   \]

Then

\[
\xi_T=0\pmod p.
\]

Since every \(\Psi_L\) is invertible modulo \(p\), it follows that

\[
\xi_L=0\pmod p
\]

at every earlier and later index where the state is defined.  Hence

\[
\boxed{e_L\equiv0\pmod p\quad\text{for all }L.}
\]

In particular,

\[
p\mid e_1,\ldots,e_{8001}.
\]

#### Proof

Equations (4.3)--(4.4) turn the \(8001\) tail congruences into

\[
\mathcal O_T\xi_T=0\pmod p.
\]

The second assumption makes \(\mathcal O_T\) invertible, so \(\xi_T=0\).  The first assumption and (2.8) make every state transition invertible in both directions.  Therefore the zero state propagates through the whole interval.  Applying (3.2) gives the conclusion.  No estimate or genericity argument is used.

## 5. The scalar cofactor recurrence and its endpoint factors

The distinction between transfer and observability can also be read directly from the scalar recurrence.

Take the \(K+1\) rows

\[
R_{T,0},R_{T,1},\ldots,R_{T,K}.
\]

For \(0\le i\le K\), define the cofactor

\[
H_i(T)=(-1)^i
\det\bigl(R_{T,0},\ldots,\widehat{R_{T,i}},\ldots,R_{T,K}\bigr).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Laplace expansion gives the exact row identity

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{K}H_i(T)R_{T,i}=0,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=0}^{K}H_i(T)e_{T+i}=0.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The last endpoint is

\[
H_K(T)=(-1)^K\Theta_T.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the first endpoint, note that

\[
R_{T,i}=R_{T+1,i-1}\Psi_T\qquad(i\ge1).
\]

Hence the matrix with rows \(R_{T,1},\ldots,R_{T,K}\) is

\[
\mathcal O_{T+1}\Psi_T,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
H_0(T)=\Theta_{T+1}\det\Psi_T
      =\Theta_{T+1}
       \left((-1)^{126}\frac{A_-(M,T)}{A_+(M,T)}\right)^{126}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

After primitive denominator clearing, the two scalar endpoints contain exactly:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{scalar endpoint}&\text{factors}\\ \hline
H_K(T)&\text{terminal observability factor }\Theta_T,\\
H_0(T)&\text{shifted observability factor }\Theta_{T+1}
       \text{ and transfer determinant }(A_-/A_+)^{126}.
\end{array}
\]

This is the requested endpoint/Fitting ledger.  It proves formally that coprimality of all \(a_{\nu,0}\) and \(a_{\nu,r_\nu}\) at the target does **not** prove that either scalar endpoint in (5.2) is a \(p\)-unit.  One must also control \(\Theta_T\).

## 6. Fixed-polynomial exceptional factors on a terminal branch

The terminal geometry has only finitely many branches, such as left-active and right-active boundary branches.  On a fixed branch, the terminal start has a fixed affine or piecewise-affine form

\[
T=\tau_\sigma(M,p).
\]

Substitute this expression into a primitive numerator of \(\Theta_T\), and reduce modulo the target prime.  Since every occurrence of \(p\) itself vanishes modulo \(p\), one obtains a fixed polynomial in \(M\):

\[
F_{{\rm obs},\sigma}(M)
 :=\operatorname{prim}
   \left(\operatorname{Num}\Theta_{\tau_\sigma(M,p)}
   \bmod p\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Likewise let \(F_{{\rm end},\sigma}(M)\) be the product of the finitely many boundary/endpoint polynomials left by the terminal endpoint ledger after the same reduction.

### Corollary 6.1 — polynomial-height exceptional set

Assume

\[
F_{{\rm obs},\sigma}\ne0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then every target prime on branch \(\sigma\) for which Theorem 4.1 cannot be applied divides

\[
F_{{\rm end},\sigma}(M)F_{{\rm obs},\sigma}(M).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Consequently the product of all such exceptional primes divides the absolute value of the fixed polynomial in (6.3), and therefore

\[
\log\operatorname{rad}_{\rm exceptional}=O(\log M).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the finite-rank route would close exactly as hoped once (6.2) is certified on every terminal branch.

The nonvanishing in (6.2) is not a cosmetic detail.  It is precisely scalar observability.  If the reduced determinant vanishes identically, then no target-prime exclusion by a fixed nonzero polynomial follows from this \(8001\)-row matrix.

## 7. Exact Fitting descent if the top determinant vanishes

If \(\Theta_T=0\) identically, the correct next step is not to increase the tail numerically.  Compute the generic rank \(q<K\) of the row module generated by the transported observations.  Let

\[
I_q(T)
\]

be its \(q\)-th observability Fitting ideal, generated by the \(q\times q\) minors.  Choose a primitive nonzero generator/minor \(	heta_T\), quotient the exterior state by the common right kernel of all future observation rows, and repeat the argument in the observable quotient of rank \(q\).

There are two exact outcomes.

### Outcome A: observable quotient

A fixed finite set of future rows has a \(q\times q\) minor whose target reduction is a nonzero polynomial.  A forced tail covering those row positions determines the observable quotient state.  Transfer invertibility then propagates every scalar output.  The exceptional primes divide the corresponding fixed Fitting polynomial.

### Outcome B: target-specialized unobservable vector

There is a nonzero vector

\[
0\ne u_T\in\bigcap_{j\ge0}\ker R_{T,j}\pmod p.
\tag{7.1}
\]

To prove failure of backward propagation, one must also exhibit an earlier row with

\[
R_{s,T}^{\rm back}u_T\ne0\pmod p
\tag{7.2}
\]

for some \(s<T\).  Equations (7.1)--(7.2) are an exact target-specialized unobservable-vector certificate.  If the claim is meant for actual two-vector packets rather than the full linear exterior module, the vector should additionally be decomposable, equivalently satisfy the Pluecker equations, or it should be identified with the actual specialized \(Y/W\) exterior state.

This is the precise certificate the question requests in the failure case.  Merely finding a singular transfer matrix, a vanishing block endpoint, or a zero scalar recurrence coefficient is not an observability certificate.

## 8. What the current endpoint information proves, and what it does not

The reported facts imply:

1. the base direct-sum state has fixed rank \(126\);
2. the cumulative exterior state has fixed rank \(8001\);
3. the exterior transfer determinant is exactly (2.8);
4. outside the fixed endpoint/boundary factors in the ledger, transfer is regular at the target prime;
5. for a target sufficiently far above \((M+1)/2\), the terminal geometry supplies a zero tail whose length eventually exceeds \(8001\).

They do **not** imply:

1. \(\Theta_T\ne0\) over characteristic zero;
2. \(\Theta_T\not\equiv0\pmod p\) on the target branch;
3. a \(p\)-unit leading or trailing coefficient for the scalar recurrence of \(e_L\);
4. a backward implication from terminal scalar zeros to the prefix.

This is exactly analogous to an invertible state transfer with a non-observable output: the state moves regularly, but a nonzero state can remain invisible to every measured scalar.  In the present problem, the hidden state is not abstract; it is the \(7875\)-dimensional curvature component in (3.4).

Therefore the correct conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
A fixed-tail theorem exists with explicit rank \(K=8001\).  Its only new obstruction, beyond the already-audited block endpoints, is the terminal exterior observability/Fitting factor.  The terminal endpoint ledger proves transfer regularity, not scalar observability.  Hence it does not by itself force the target prime into the fixed prefix.  Computing the target reduction of the observability Fitting ideal is the exact remaining finite computation.
\end{minipage}}
\]

If its primitive target reduction is nonzero, the exceptional target radical has \(O(\log M)\) height and the propagation route succeeds.  If it vanishes, an exact right-kernel vector together with a nonzero backward observation proves failure.

## 9. Replay recipe

The computation needed to settle the dichotomy is finite and deterministic.

1. Read the fourteen exact recurrences and form their companion matrices \(T_{\nu,L}\).
2. Form \(T_L=\bigoplus T_{\nu,L}\) and the exact signed observation row \(eta_L\).
3. Build the block matrix (3.4) on
   
   \[
   \Lambda^2E\oplus E,
   \]
   
   using the ordered pair basis for \(\Lambda^2E\).
4. Build the rows \(R_{T,j}\) recursively:
   
   \[
   R_{T,0}=\gamma_T,
   \qquad
   R_{T,j+1}=\gamma_{T+j+1}\Psi_{T+j}\cdots\Psi_T.
   \]
5. Do fraction-free rank/Fitting elimination; do not expand a generic dense \(8001\times8001\) determinant first.
6. Specialize the terminal branch and reduce modulo the symbolic target relation.
7. If a maximal minor is nonzero, factor its primitive numerator into endpoint factors and a residual observability polynomial.
8. If the rank drops, output a right-kernel vector and test all backward prefix rows; impose the Pluecker equations if a decomposable packet counterexample is required.

Steps 5--8 distinguish the two logically different conclusions.  A successful transfer endpoint audit completes only Steps 1--3.
