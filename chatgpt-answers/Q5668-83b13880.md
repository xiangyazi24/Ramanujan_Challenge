# Apéry P3.2: the Racah/Hasse polynomial does **not** define a fixed elliptic fiber

## Executive verdict

Let

\[
\phi_m(X)=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(X-j(j+1))}{(m!)^2},
\qquad
H_p(X)=\sum_{m=0}^{(p-1)/2}\phi_m(X)^2\in\mathbf F_p[X].
\]

The identity

\[
H_p(r(r+1))\equiv b_r\pmod p
\]

is correct, and for a top-half prime `p` with `r=n-p` it gives

\[
p\mid b_{n-p}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H_p(n(n+1))=0.
\]

This is a useful exact reparametrization, but it is **not** presently a fixed-fiber geometric interpretation. The audit gives a stronger negative statement.

1. `H_p(X)` is a Racah/Wilson **spectral truncation**: after writing `X=nu(nu+1)`, it is a half-truncated balanced `4F3(1)` whose upper parameters are `-nu,-nu,nu+1,nu+1`. The variable `X` changes hypergeometric parameters; it is not the geometric argument of the Apéry Picard--Fuchs family.

2. For every fixed nonnegative integer `n`, there is the exact stabilization

   \[
   \boxed{H_p(n(n+1))\equiv b_n\pmod p\qquad(p>2n+1).}
   \]

   Hence the set of primes for which the putative fixed fiber at `X=n(n+1)` is nonordinary is eventually contained in the finite prime divisor set of the fixed integer `b_n`.

3. Consequently, under any `p`-independent normalization by a fixed rational unit, `H_p(X)` **cannot** be the Hasse invariant of an elliptic-curve family over `Q(X)`. Every elliptic curve over `Q` has infinitely many supersingular primes (Elkies); a CM elliptic curve has supersingular primes of density `1/2`. The same argument excludes an elliptic symmetric-square K3 family, because its rank-three crystal is nonordinary exactly when the underlying elliptic curve is supersingular.

4. I found no primary reference constructing a different K3 family over the `X`-line whose Hasse--Witt scalar is `H_p(X)`. Such an abstract K3 interpretation is not logically excluded by Elkies, because infinitude of nonordinary primes is not known for an arbitrary fixed K3 surface. It would nevertheless be a genuinely new geometric theorem, not a consequence of the known Apéry K3 pencil.

5. The actual Beukers--Peters/Stienstra--Beukers K3 family is parametrized by the generating-function variable `t`. Its Hasse/Dwork block is

   \[
   A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j,
   \]

   up to the harmless nonzero normalization used by Beukers--Tsai--Ye. The Racah polynomial satisfies

   \[
   H_p(j(j+1))=[t^j]A_p(t),
   \]

   so it interpolates **coefficients** of the genuine Hasse polynomial; it is not its evaluation at a characteristic-zero parameter.

6. Fixed-curve Lang--Trotter/supersingular-prime bounds would be more than strong enough if a fixed elliptic fiber existed, but their constants are for one fixed curve. No published theorem gives the needed estimate uniformly for every specialization of height about `n^2` at the simultaneous prime scale `p\asymp n`. Average theorems for parameter families do not control the single prescribed section `X_n=n(n+1)`.

The fixed-fiber idea therefore does not escape the known horizontal obstruction. It converts the moving node into one integer parameter, but the geometric object or truncation still changes with the residue characteristic.

## 2. Exact Racah normalization

### Proposition 2.1: hypergeometric form

Choose `nu` satisfying

\[
X=\nu(\nu+1).
\]

Then

\[
X-j(j+1)=(\nu-j)(\nu+j+1),
\]

and therefore

\[
\phi_m(X)
=(-1)^m\frac{(-\nu)_m(\nu+1)_m}{(m!)^2}.
\]

The sign disappears after squaring, so

\[
\boxed{
H_p(\nu(\nu+1))
=\sum_{m=0}^{(p-1)/2}
 \frac{(-\nu)_m^2(\nu+1)_m^2}{(m!)^4}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is the half truncation at `t=1` of

\[
{}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}-\nu,-\nu,\nu+1,\nu+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};t
\right).
\]

The quadratic lattice `nu(nu+1)` is the standard Casimir lattice of the dual-Hahn/Racah/Wilson class. Equation (2.1) is exact, but it does not make `X` a geometric modulus: `X` moves the **upper parameters** of the hypergeometric equation in `t`.

### Proposition 2.2: interpolation at the Apéry nodes

For an integer `r>=0` and `m<=r`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi_m(r(r+1))
&=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(r-j)(r+j+1)}{(m!)^2}\\
&=\frac{(r+m)!}{(r-m)!(m!)^2}\\
&=\binom rm\binom{r+m}{m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For `m>r`, the product contains the factor with `j=r`, hence vanishes. Thus, whenever `0<=r<=(p-1)/2`,

\[
H_p(r(r+1))
=\sum_{m=0}^{r}\binom rm^2\binom{r+m}{m}^2
=b_r
\pmod p.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For `r>(p-1)/2`, note that

\[
r(r+1)\equiv(p-1-r)(p-r)\pmod p,
\]

and use the first-block reflection `b_r\equiv b_{p-1-r}\pmod p`. This gives the stated identity for every residue `r`.

### Proposition 2.3: the top-half diagonal

If `n/2<p<=n` and `r=n-p`, then `0<=r<p` and

\[
r(r+1)=(n-p)(n-p+1)\equiv n(n+1)\pmod p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
p\mid b_{n-p}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H_p(X_n)=0,
\qquad X_n=n(n+1).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This part of the proposed reinterpretation is fully valid.

## 3. The stabilization theorem and its consequence

### Theorem 3.1: exact stabilization at a fixed characteristic-zero parameter

For every integer `n>=0` and every prime `p>2n+1`,

\[
\boxed{H_p(n(n+1))\equiv b_n\pmod p.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

When `p>2n+1`, the truncation index `h=(p-1)/2` satisfies `h>=n+1`. Formula (2.2) gives

\[
\phi_m(n(n+1))=\binom nm\binom{n+m}{m}
\quad(0\le m\le n),
\]

and every term with `m>n` vanishes because its product contains

\[
X_n-n(n+1)=0.
\]

Thus the sum defining `H_p(X_n)` is exactly the finite integer sum defining `b_n`, reduced modulo `p`. ∎

### Corollary 3.2

For fixed `n`,

\[
\{p>2n+1:H_p(X_n)=0\}
\subseteq\{p:p\mid b_n\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

In particular this set is finite, because `b_n>0`.

### Why this matters

The same integer `X_n` indeed fixes the Casimir parameter `nu=n`. But the characteristic-zero hypergeometric value is simply

\[
{}_4F_3\!\left(
\begin{matrix}-n,-n,n+1,n+1\\1,1,1\end{matrix};1
\right)=b_n.
\]

For `p>2n+1`, the mod-`p` truncation sees the complete terminating series. For `p\asymp n`, the boundary `(p-1)/2` cuts the series before its characteristic-zero endpoint, and the omitted part is exactly where the moving-characteristic phenomenon lives.

Thus the proposed fixed-fiber picture fixes a spectral parameter but not a fixed cohomological fiber. The `p`-dependent half truncation remains essential.

## 4. A rigorous no-go for elliptic and symmetric-square families

### Theorem 4.1: no fixed elliptic Hasse invariant

There do not exist

- a nonempty open `U` of the affine `X`-line over `Q`;
- an elliptic scheme `E -> U`;
- a fixed nonzero rational function `u(X) in Q(X)`;
- a finite set of excluded rational primes;

such that, for every remaining prime `p`, the reduction of

\[
u(X)H_p(X)
\]

is the Hasse invariant of `E/U` on the common good locus.

The same conclusion holds if the equality is allowed after multiplying by any fixed bounded-degree factor that is nonzero at the chosen fiber.

#### Proof

Choose an integer `n` for which

\[
X_n=n(n+1)\in U(\mathbf Q),
\]

and for which `u(X_n)` is finite and nonzero. All but finitely many primes are good for the fixed elliptic curve `E_{X_n}` and are units for `u(X_n)`.

For every sufficiently large such prime, Theorem 3.1 says

\[
H_p(X_n)\equiv b_n\pmod p.
\]

Hence the purported Hasse invariant vanishes only at the finite set of primes dividing the numerator of `u(X_n)b_n` (plus the fixed bad set). Therefore `E_{X_n}` would have only finitely many supersingular primes.

This contradicts Elkies's theorem that every elliptic curve over `Q` has infinitely many supersingular primes. In the CM case the contradiction is stronger: Deuring's theorem identifies the supersingular primes with the inert primes, up to finitely many exceptions, and they have density `1/2`. ∎

### Corollary 4.2: no elliptic symmetric-square K3 interpretation

Suppose the variable part of a K3 family were, after a fixed Tate twist, the symmetric square of `H^1(E_X)`. At a good prime:

- if `E_X` is ordinary, its slopes are `0,1`, and the symmetric-square slopes are `0,1,2`;
- if `E_X` is supersingular, its slopes are `1/2,1/2`, and the symmetric-square slopes are `1,1,1`.

Thus the symmetric-square K3 fiber is nonordinary exactly when `E_X` is supersingular. Theorem 4.1 again gives a contradiction.

This rules out precisely the most attractive possibility suggested by the fact that the Apéry third-order Picard--Fuchs operator is a symmetric square.

### What is not ruled out

The argument does **not** rule out an exotic K3 or higher-dimensional family whose fixed rational fibers can have only finitely many nonordinary primes. No general analogue of Elkies's infinitude theorem is known for arbitrary K3 surfaces.

However, I found no primary reference identifying `H_p(X)` with the Hasse--Witt scalar or determinant of such a family. Constructing one would require, at minimum, a fixed family over `Z[X]`, a fixed-rank relative crystalline/étale object, and a proof that its Hasse--Witt determinant is `H_p(X)` up to a fixed unit for every good prime. The formal hypergeometric identity (2.1) does not provide this.

A normalization or parameter map allowed to depend on `p` can evade Theorem 4.1, but then the object is not a characteristic-zero fixed family and effective Chebotarev cannot be applied to it.

## 5. The actual Apéry K3 family and the correct Hasse variable

Let

\[
F(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n.
\]

It satisfies the Apéry Picard--Fuchs operator

\[
\left[
\theta^3
-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)
+t^2(\theta+1)^3
\right]F=0,
\qquad \theta=t\frac d{dt}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Beukers--Peters construct a K3 pencil with this period, and Stienstra--Beukers identify its Picard--Fuchs equation and formal Brauer group. A standard level-six modular normalization is

\[
t(\tau)=
\left(
\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(6\tau)}
     {\eta(2\tau)\eta(3\tau)}
\right)^{12},
\tag{5.2}
\]

\[
Z(\tau)=
\frac{\eta(2\tau)^7\eta(3\tau)^7}
     {\eta(\tau)^5\eta(6\tau)^5},
\qquad
F(t(\tau))=Z(\tau).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Here `t` is the genuine geometric/modular parameter. The first Frobenius/Hasse block is

\[
A_p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b_jt^j.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The Lucas/Dwork identity is

\[
\frac{F(t)}{F(t^p)}\equiv A_p(t)\pmod p.
\tag{5.5}
\]

In the normalization of Beukers--Tsai--Ye, the modular Frobenius quotient is a polynomial `P_p(t)` with

\[
P_p(t)\equiv4A_p(t)\pmod p;
\tag{5.6}
\]

the scalar `4` is a unit for `p>=5`.

For a good geometric fiber `t=t_0`, vanishing of the appropriate Hasse--Witt scalar `A_p(t_0)` is a genuine statement about that fixed fiber's formal Brauer group/Frobenius slopes.

By contrast, the Racah identity is

\[
\boxed{
H_p(j(j+1))=[t^j]A_p(t).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

It changes coefficient extraction into polynomial interpolation on the quadratic lattice. No result of Beukers--Peters, Stienstra--Beukers, or Beukers--Tsai--Ye identifies this interpolation polynomial with the Hasse invariant of a new family over the `X`-line.

The distinction is therefore exact:

```text
known geometric object:
    vary t, evaluate A_p(t), fixed K3 pencil;

Racah object:
    vary spectral index j, extract [t^j]A_p(t),
    interpolate the coefficients by X=j(j+1).
```

## 6. What Frobenius statement does `H_p(X_n)=0` actually encode?

### 6.1 It is not presently a fixed-fiber trace equation

Because no fixed family over `Q(X)` has been identified, there is no justified formula of the form

\[
H_p(X_n)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a_p(\mathcal V_{X_n})=0
\]

for one characteristic-zero fiber `\mathcal V_{X_n}`.

The equality in (2.4) is a coefficient congruence, not a point-count formula for an `X`-fiber.

### 6.2 The valid Mellin/character interpretation

For each fixed `p`, the values `b_r mod p` can be realized as finite Mellin transforms of the Apéry/Beukers--Peters K3 trace function. Geometrically, twisting the rank-three weight-two K3 sheaf by the Kummer character indexed by `r` and taking middle cohomology produces a rank-two weight-three object. In the repository's normalization its determinant has weight `3`, and `p|b_r` is the nonordinary/no-unit-root event.

But the Kummer character has order

\[
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,r)},
\]

which generally grows with `p`; the coefficient field and the chosen character therefore change with the residue characteristic. Writing `X_n=n(n+1)` does not remove this dependence. It only labels the same character index through the Casimir map.

This is a `p`-dependent Mellin fiber, not a specialization of one fixed compatible system over `Q`.

### 6.3 The genuine central exception

At

\[
r=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
r(r+1)\equiv-\frac14\pmod p,
\]

the character is quadratic and the motive becomes fixed. Ahlgren--Ono prove the supercongruence relating the central Apéry number to the weight-four level-eight form

\[
f(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4
       =\sum_{m\ge1}a_m(f)q^m,
\]

in particular

\[
b_{(p-1)/2}\equiv a_p(f)\pmod p
\]

(and in the standard statement modulo `p^2`). This is a true fixed-motive slice, but for fixed outer `n` the central equation determines at most one prime. It cannot control the full moving diagonal.

## 7. Audit of existing prime-counting theorems

Assume hypothetically that a non-CM elliptic family `E_X` had survived the no-go theorem, and put

\[
\pi_{E,0}(x)=\#\{p\le x:p\nmid N_E,\ a_p(E)=0\}.
\]

For one **fixed** non-CM elliptic curve over `Q`, the known bounds are already sufficient in shape:

1. Murty's unconditional general Lang--Trotter bound, as summarized precisely by Zywina, is

   \[
   \pi_{E,a}(x)
   \ll_E
   x\frac{(\log\log x)^2}{(\log x)^2}.
   \tag{7.1}
   \]

2. For the supersingular case `a=0`, Elkies--Kaneko--Murty give the stronger

   \[
   \pi_{E,0}(x)\ll_E x^{3/4}.
   \tag{7.2}
   \]

3. Under GRH, the strongest standard Chebotarev/newform formulations give, for fixed `E`, bounds at the scale

   \[
   \pi_{E,0}(x)\ll_E
   \frac{x^{3/4}}{(\log x)^{1/2}}.
   \tag{7.3}
   \]

Multiplying by `log x`, any of (7.1)--(7.3) gives

\[
\sum_{x/2<p\le x,\ a_p(E)=0}\log p=o(x)
\]

for that fixed curve.

### The fatal uniformity mismatch

In the proposed application the curve would be

\[
E_{X_n},\qquad X_n=n(n+1),
\]

and the prime range is `p\asymp n`. The curve changes at exactly the same rate as the counting endpoint. The notation `\ll_E` in (7.1)--(7.3) is therefore load-bearing: neither the implied constant nor the threshold from which the estimate is valid is uniform in `n`.

The multiplicative height is

\[
H(X_n)=n(n+1)\asymp n^2,
\]

while the logarithmic height is

\[
h(X_n)=2\log n+O(1).
\]

For a fixed Weierstrass family of bounded degree one expects the discriminant and conductor of `E_{X_n}` to be at most `n^{O(1)}`, apart from cancellation. Thus `\log N_{E_{X_n}}=O(\log n)` would be favorable. But the published fixed-curve bounds do not state a version whose constants and starting point are controlled only by this logarithmic conductor bound.

Effective Chebotarev also requires control of the division fields used in the sieve: their discriminants, degrees, and Galois images depend on both the auxiliary prime and the specialization. A conductor estimate alone does not supply uniform open-image data for every specialization.

### Uniform Serre theorems

Cojocaru--Hall prove uniform forms of Serre's open-image theorem for nonconstant curves over function fields and for one-parameter families over `Q`. This is useful input for a family Lang--Trotter theorem, but it is not itself a bound for

\[
\pi_{E_u,0}(x)
\]

uniformly for **every** rational `u` of height `<=x^2`. Exceptional/CM/isotrivial specializations still have to be removed, and the Chebotarev optimization has to be uniform through a growing set of auxiliary primes.

### Average family theorems

Sha--Shparlinski and related work prove Lang--Trotter/Sato--Tate estimates averaged over integer or rational parameters of bounded height, including thin sets. Such results may show that almost every specialization is good. They do not bound the prescribed diagonal specialization

\[
u=X_n=n(n+1)
\]

for every `n`. One exceptional specialization at each height can survive every parameter average.

### Large sieve for Frobenius

The standard large-sieve frameworks have one of two arities:

```text
one finite field, many parameter points;
or
one fixed compatible system, many rational primes.
```

Here `H_p` itself changes with `p`, and no fixed `X`-family has been constructed. Even in the Mellin realization there is one character in each changing field, with unbounded character order. There is therefore no fixed finite quotient in which the event `H_p(X_n)=0` is one Chebotarev conjugacy condition.

Moreover, exact vanishing is reduction modulo the same prime that is being counted. Complex Sato--Tate or Deligne bounds for normalized traces do not detect this shrinking `p`-adic condition.

Thus neither effective Chebotarev nor a standard Frobenius large sieve currently applies with the required quantifiers.

## 8. CM and geometrically exceptional fibers

### 8.1 Elliptic CM would be fatal

For a fixed CM elliptic curve, Deuring's theorem gives

\[
\pi_{E,0}(x)\sim\frac12\pi(x),
\]

up to the finitely many ramified/bad primes. Hence

\[
\sum_{x/2<p\le x,\ a_p(E)=0}\log p
\sim\frac{x}{4},
\]

with the exact interval constant determined by Chebotarev. This is linear, not `o(x)`.

For a hypothetical nonisotrivial elliptic family over `Q(X)`, a rational CM specialization has rational CM `j`-invariant, hence comes from the finite class-number-one list of imaginary quadratic orders. One would have to solve the finitely many equations

\[
j(E_X)=j_D,
\qquad X=n(n+1),
\]

and separately exclude an isotrivial CM family. No such check can be made because the family does not exist; Theorem 4.1 already rules it out globally.

### 8.2 CM/singular K3 fibers are also dangerous

For singular K3 surfaces (Picard rank `20`), supersingular reduction occurs at inert primes in the associated imaginary quadratic field, subject to the standard good-reduction exclusions. Shimada studies the reduction lattices, and the inert-prime criterion is used explicitly in the singular-K3 literature. Ito gives the broader CM-K3 description of Picard number and formal Brauer height at good primes.

Thus a CM/singular K3 fiber can also have a positive-density nonordinary or supersingular set. Any proposed K3 family over the `X`-line would need an explicit classification of the values `X_n` landing on CM/singular fibers, not merely generic monodromy.

### 8.3 Singular geometric fibers

If a fixed family had discriminant `Delta(X)`, then bad specialization at `X_n` would satisfy

\[
\Delta(n(n+1))=0.
\]

For nonzero `Delta` this has only finitely many integer solutions. This part would be easy; the CM/nonordinary issue is the serious one.

## 9. The strongest useful new lemmas

### 9.1 What would be sufficient after a genuine fixed family was constructed

For an elliptic family, the exact uniform theorem needed is much weaker than a full uniform Lang--Trotter asymptotic.

#### Uniform diagonal supersingular lemma

For a fixed nonisotrivial family `E -> U`, prove

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{u\in U(\mathbf Q),\ H(u)\le x^2\\
                  E_u\ \mathrm{nonCM}}}
\#\{x/2<p\le x:a_p(E_u)=0\}
=o\!\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right).
}
\tag{UDS}
\]

A quantitative version such as

\[
\ll
\frac{x(\log\log x)^A}{(\log x)^{2+\delta}}
\]

with constants depending only on the family would suffice. Multiplication by `log x` would give the desired weighted `o(x)` estimate.

For a rank-three K3/transcendental system, replace `a_p(E_u)=0` by “the fiber is nonordinary.” No theorem of this uniform every-specialization form is currently available.

But `(UDS)` cannot presently be applied here, because the prerequisite fixed family is absent and is impossible in the elliptic/symmetric-square cases.

### 9.2 The actual two-characteristic lemma for the Racah/Mellin object

Put

\[
T_n=\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ H_p(X_n)=0\}
    =\{p:n/2<p\le n,\ p\mid b_{n-p}\},
\]

and `K(n)=|T_n|`. The exact pair-energy statement

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{N<n\le2N}(K(n))_2
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right)
}
\tag{PE}
\]

would imply

\[
\sup_{N<n\le2N}K(n)=o(N/\log N),
\]

because a single spike of size `epsilon N/log N` contributes a positive constant times `N^2/log^2N` to the left side.

Written with all characteristics visible, `(PE)` is

\[
\sum_{\substack{p\ne q\\N/2<p,q\le2N}}
\#\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
N<n\le2N,\quad n/2<p,q\le n,\\
H_p(n(n+1))=0,\\
H_q(n(n+1))=0
\end{array}
\right\}
=o\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right).
\tag{9.1}
\]

This is the exact two-characteristic large-sieve target. A theorem for one `p`, even a bounded zero-fiber theorem, does not imply it.

The repository has a still smaller sufficient interface. Order the hit primes at one `n`, retain only adjacent pairs of gap at most `A log N`, and discard the degenerate pairs for which one characteristic sees both endpoint indices; those degenerate pairs are already controlled by the Apéry continuant. It is enough to prove, for every fixed `A>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{N<n\le2N}
A^{\times}_{A\log N}(n)
=o_A(N/\log N),
}
\tag{PC-LG}
\]

where `A^times_H(n)` counts adjacent **pure-cross** hit pairs of gap at most `H`.

This is strictly weaker than `(PE)`: it ignores nonadjacent pairs, large gaps, and all same-characteristic/degenerate collisions. It is the smallest concrete new horizontal lemma presently isolated.

The fixed integer `X_n` may be useful in constructing a two-prime correspondence for `(PC-LG)`, but by itself it supplies no common sheaf over `F_p` and `F_q`. A successful theorem must build actual cross-characteristic arithmetic of the distinguished Apéry state.

## 10. Strategic conclusion

The proposed identity is exact but its interpretation must be corrected.

```text
What is gained:
  all top-half hits at one n are evaluations at one integer X_n;
  the Racah interpolation is explicit and characteristic-zero normalized;
  the central quadratic-character fiber is genuinely modular.

What is not gained:
  a fixed elliptic/K3 fiber over Q at X_n;
  a fixed compatible Galois representation as p varies;
  uniform Chebotarev constants;
  a two-characteristic correlation theorem.
```

The stabilization

\[
H_p(X_n)=b_n\pmod p\qquad(p>2n+1)
\]

is the decisive audit test. It proves that `H_p` cannot be the Hasse invariant of a fixed elliptic family or of an elliptic symmetric-square K3 family. The known Apéry K3 Hasse polynomial lives in `t`, while `H_p(X)` is a spectral interpolation of its coefficients.

Therefore the fixed-fiber reinterpretation does **not** reopen ordinary supersingular-prime technology. The smallest credible new input remains the two-characteristic pure-cross logarithmic-gap lemma `(PC-LG)`, or, more ambitiously, the shell pair-energy theorem `(PE)`.
