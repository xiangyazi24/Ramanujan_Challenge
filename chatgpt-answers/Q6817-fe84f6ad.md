# AVG-ZERO via Christol audit

## Verdict

The Christol/Furstenberg route gives a useful reformulation but does not currently prove `sum_{p<=X} |Z_p| = o(X)`. The decisive obstruction is quantitative: the algebraic degree/automaton size over F_p is not bounded independently of p. General zero-count theorems for automatic/algebraic sequences do not give a sublogarithmic bound for the first p coefficients. An Apery-specific input is still required.

## (i) Algebraicity of the Apery generating series

The diagonal premise is valid in the usual sense: Apery numbers are diagonals of rational functions, hence their reductions modulo p have algebraic generating series by Furstenberg's theorem on diagonals in characteristic p.

The important quantitative point is that Furstenberg's construction is not a bounded-degree theorem. For a rational function in d variables, the Cartier-operator orbit gives a finite-dimensional algebraic closure. General bounds are of size roughly p^d for the Cartier kernel, and the resulting algebraic equation has degree bounded by that orbit size. For a fixed multivariate rational presentation this is polynomial in p with exponent depending on the number of variables, not a constant independent of p.

Adamczewski-Bell type effective results similarly give effective algebraicity bounds for diagonals, but the characteristic-p specialization does not remove the p dependence. There is no known small-degree Apery equation over F_p(t) with degree O(1).

The Lucas congruence is a stronger digit statement:

b_{mp+r}=b_m b_r mod p.

Equivalently, if B_0(t)=sum_{r=0}^{p-1} b_r t^r, then formally

B(t)=B_0(t) B(t^p).

Iterating gives the digit product expansion. However, in the window r<p, this says only that the first factor B_0 is the entire truncated series. It does not compress the zero count; determining the zeros of B_0 is exactly the original problem.

## (ii) Zero coefficients of algebraic series

For a fixed algebraic series over F_p, Christol implies p-automaticity. The zero set is therefore p-automatic and its density is computable from the transition matrices of the automaton.

But the automaton can have size growing rapidly with p. There is no theorem of the form

number of zeros among first p coefficients <= poly(degree,height)

uniformly over all algebraic series over F_p.

The reason is simple: algebraicity over F_p(t) permits algebraic functions whose Cartier kernels have dimension growing with p. The automatic representation is allowed to have p-dependent complexity.

Therefore the desired implication

bounded degree + bounded height => |Z_p|=O(1)

would be powerful, but it is not available in this setting and the premise (bounded degree/height) is false for the known diagonal constructions.

## (iii) If degree grows with p

A general Cartier bound gives automaton size at least potentially p^c for some c depending on the rational presentation. Automatic zero-count bounds then give only bounds comparable to that state complexity. Such estimates are far too weak for AVG-ZERO.

The target requires approximately

average |Z_p| = o(log p)

or stronger. A bound p^alpha with alpha>0 gives no information because

sum_{p<=X} p^alpha

is much larger than X.

Thus the general algebraicity machinery is not quantitatively close enough.

## (iv) General counterexamples

The hoped-for zero theorem is false for arbitrary algebraic series. For example, over F_p one can take algebraic rational functions whose coefficient sequence is periodic with large zero blocks, such as

A(t)=1/(1-t^m)

or simple variants with numerator chosen to force many coefficients to vanish in the first period. The corresponding automatic automaton has size comparable to the period. More elaborate algebraic examples can realize large zero densities.

The feature that may exclude this for Apery is not algebraicity alone. Possible excluding structures are:

1. the specific Lucas multiplicativity;
2. the reflection symmetry b_{p-1-r}=b_r;
3. the modular-form interpretation of the midpoint;
4. the recurrence's constrained monodromy.

None of these is currently converted into a zero-density theorem.

## (v) Reflection and digit structure

The reflection pairing reduces the effective object slightly. The zero set is symmetric under r -> p-1-r, except for the midpoint exception. Therefore zero counts are naturally counts of pairs plus possibly one central point.

This does not reduce automatic complexity: the paired set is still a p-automatic subset of [0,p), and the symmetry is a final constraint rather than a smaller automaton construction.

The Lucas digit structure also says the entire infinite zero set is determined by the seed digits Z_p. This is important conceptually: there is no hidden higher-digit randomness. But it makes |Z_p| the fundamental quantity rather than solving it.

## Terminal assessment

The Christol route changes the question from projective-orbit zeros to zero coefficients of an algebraic series, but the available theory stops exactly before the needed scale.

What would close AVG-ZERO is a new Apery-specific lemma of one of the following forms:

- a uniform bound on the Cartier orbit dimension of the Apery diagonal modulo p;
- a proof that the Apery p-automaton has only O(1) effective zero-producing states;
- a modular-form/monodromy argument showing the zero state has stationary measure o(1/log p);
- direct cancellation in the distribution of the digit seed set Z_p.

The current general algebraic/automatic theory supplies none of these.

Least confident step: the sharp size of the minimal Cartier orbit for this particular diagonal. General upper bounds are clear, but a much smaller hidden Apery-specific automaton is not ruled out.

Possible blind spot: the question assumes algebraic degree is the right complexity parameter. For automatic sequences, the relevant quantity is the Cartier orbit/state graph, and a small algebraic equation can still hide complicated coefficient behavior after reduction modulo varying primes.
