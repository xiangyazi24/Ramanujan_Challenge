# Audit of the diagonal-margin family

## Conclusion

The diagonal-margin determinants H_m are not algebraically new carriers. The two-parameter Pascal identities imply that the family is generated by the same exterior-content module as the fixed-length adjacent minors E_{d,L}. The observed small gcd between neighboring margins is therefore evidence for a stronger content statement, but not a new independent mechanism.

## Pascal transport identity

Write

G_{d,L}(F)=\sum_{i=0}^{L}(-1)^i {d-1+i\choose i}{d+L\choose L-i}F_{d-1+i}.

The two elementary identities

G_{d+1,L-1}=G_{d,L}-\lambda_{d,L}G_{d+1,L-1}^{\mathrm{shift}},

and

\binom{x+1}{r}=\binom{x}{r}+\binom{x}{r-1}

show that changing the margin by one is a unimodular Pascal row operation plus a content multiplier. Hence every diagonal carrier satisfies

\alpha_m H_m\in (E_{d_0,L_M},\ldots,E_{D-1,L_M}),

where

\alpha_m=\prod_{s=0}^{m-2}\frac{g_{s+1}}{g_s}

is exactly the saturation factor accumulated from the removed binomial contents.

Conversely, reversing the Pascal operations gives

\beta_d E_{d,L_M}\in (H_2,\ldots,H_M),

with the same factors. Thus the ideals coincide after saturation.

## Saturation multipliers

For the common target prime p, valuation is determined only by the Pascal content:

v_p(g_m)=\min_{0\le s<m-1}v_p\binom{d_m+s+L_m+1}{L_m}.

The transport multiplier contributes

v_p(\alpha_m)=v_p(g_{m-1})-v_p(g_m).

Therefore the diagonal family introduces no new target divisibility. If p is not a binomial-content prime, the transport is a unit modulo p; if it is, the loss is exactly the already known exterior-content obstruction.

## Endpoint minors

At maximal margin, one boundary is forced:

d_M-1=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor

or

D+L_M=n-1.

The Pascal transport reduces the endpoint determinant to the two endpoint minors plus content factors. The remaining obstruction is precisely the exterior-content lemma:

\gcd(E_{\mathrm{left}},E_{\mathrm{right}}) / \operatorname{content}=O(e^{o(n)}).

No new determinant appears.
